A tensor category construction of the W p , q W_{p,q} triplet vertex operator algebra and applications
Robert McRae and Valerii Sopin

Abstract
. For coprime p , q ∈ ℤ ≥ 2 p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2} , the triplet vertex operator algebra W p , q W_{p,q} is a non-simple extension of the universal Virasoro vertex operator algebra of central charge c p , q = 1 − 6 ​ ( p − q ) 2 p ​ q c_{p,q}=1-\frac{6(p-q)^{2}}{pq} , and it is a basic example of a vertex operator algebra appearing in logarithmic conformal field theory. Here, we give a new construction of W p , q W_{p,q} different from the original screening operator definition of Feigin-Gainutdinov-Semikhatov-Tipunin. Using our earlier work on the tensor category structure of modules for the Virasoro algebra at central charge c p , q c_{p,q} , we show that the simple modules appearing in the decomposition of W p , q W_{p,q} as a module for the Virasoro algebra have P ​ S ​ L 2 PSL_{2} -fusion rules and generate a symmetric tensor category equivalent to Rep ​ P ​ S ​ L 2 \mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2} . Then we use the theory of commutative algebras in braided tensor categories to construct W p , q W_{p,q} as an appropriate non-simple modification of the canonical algebra in the Deligne tensor product of Rep ​ P ​ S ​ L 2 \mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2} with this Virasoro subcategory. As a consequence, we show that the automorphism group of W p , q W_{p,q} is P ​ S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℂ ) PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C}) . We also define a braided tensor category 𝒪 c p , q 0 \mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0} consisting of modules for the Virasoro algebra at central charge c p , q c_{p,q} that induce to untwisted modules of W p , q W_{p,q} . We show that 𝒪 c p , q 0 \mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0} tensor embeds into the P ​ S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℂ ) PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C}) -equivariantization of the category of W p , q W_{p,q} -modules and is closed under contragredient modules. We conjecture that 𝒪 c p , q 0 \mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0} has enough projective objects and is the correct category of Virasoro modules for constructing logarithmic minimal models in conformal field theory.



1.
Introduction

The triplet
W
-algebras
W_{p,q}
for coprime
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
are fundamental examples of vertex operator algebras (VOAs) with finite but non-semisimple representation theory. When
q=1
,
W_{p,1}
is a simple and
C_{2}
-cofinite VOA with
2p
simple modules
[
3
]
, automorphism group
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})

[
2
]
, and a non-semisimple modular tensor category of representations
[
82
,
44
]
. Moreover, as conjectured in
[
34
]
and proved recently in
[
44
,
19
,
20
]
, this modular tensor category is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional representations of a quasi-Hopf modification of the restricted quantum group of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
at the root of unity
e^{\pi i/p}
. When
q\geq 2
,
W_{p,q}
is no longer simple but is still
C_{2}
-cofinite
[
5
,
83
]
. These more complicated VOAs are the subject of this paper. Our main results are a new construction of
W_{p,q}
for
q\geq 2
using tensor category methods and a proof that, as in the
q=1
case, the automorphism group of
W_{p,q}
is
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

For
q\geq 2
,
W_{p,q}
was constructed in
[
35
]
as the intersection of the kernels of two screening operators acting on the lattice VOA
V_{\sqrt{2pq}\mathbb{Z}}
. The triplet algebra
W_{p,q}
has central charge
c_{p,q}=1-\frac{6(p-q)^{2}}{pq}
, contains the universal Virasoro VOA of central charge
c_{p,q}
as a subalgebra, and is non-simple with the rational simple Virasoro VOA
L_{c_{p,q}}
of central charge
c_{p,q}
as its unique simple quotient. Just as
L_{c_{p,q}}
is the VOA of minimal models in rational conformal field theory in physics,
W_{p,q}
is the VOA of
W
-extended logarithmic minimal models in logarithmic conformal field theory
[
74
,
72
,
73
,
71
,
42
,
43
]
. Such logarithmic conformal field theories sometimes arise in the analysis of statistical models at critical points; in particular the
(p,q)=(2,3)
case is relevant for critical percolation (see for example
[
26
]
and references therein).

The VOA structure and representation theory of
W_{p,q}
for
q\geq 2
are not yet as well understood as in the
q=1
case. However,
W_{p,q}
is
C_{2}
-cofinite (see
[
5
]
for the
q=2
case and
[
83
]
in general), which thanks to
[
46
]
implies that its representation category is a finite abelian braided monoidal category. Simple
W_{p,q}
-modules have been classified in
[
6
]
for the
q=2
case and
[
83
]
in general, while logarithmic indecomposable
W_{p,q}
-modules have been constructed in
[
7
,
69
]
. The monoidal structure on the category of
W_{p,q}
-modules is not rigid, essentially because
W_{p,q}
is not simple or self-contragredient when
q\geq 2
, but fusion rules have been obtained by various methods in, for example,
[
73
,
86
,
77
,
70
]
. In
[
36
]
, a relation (though not quite an equivalence) was conjectured between the monoidal categories of modules for
W_{p,q}
and for a certain “Kazhdan-Lusztig dual” quantum group, generalizing the previously-mentioned equivalence between the categories of modules for
W_{p,1}
and for the restricted quantum group of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Proving a precise version of this conjecture remains one of the most significant open problems pertaining to the
W_{p,q}
triplet algebra.

Another problem is determining the automorphism group of
W_{p,q}
, which is
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
in the
q=1
case
[
2
]
. It was suggested in
[
35
]
that
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
should also act by VOA automorphisms of
W_{p,q}
in the
q\geq 2
case. However, although two derivations of
W_{p,q}
labeled
E
and
F
were constructed in
[
83
]
, it was not checked there whether the exponentials of
E
and
F
actually generate an action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by automorphisms. In the present paper, we solve this problem, showing that indeed the automorphism group of
W_{p,q}
is
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, by giving a new tensor category construction of
W_{p,q}
that does not require analysis of screening operators.

Our starting point is our previous paper
[
63
]
where we detailed some of the tensor structure of the category
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
of
C_{1}
-cofinite modules for the universal Virasoro VOA
V_{c_{p,q}}
of central charge
c_{p,q}
. This is the same as the category of finite-length modules for the Virasoro algebra
\mathcal{V}ir
of central charge
c_{p,q}
whose composition factors are irreducible quotients of reducible Verma modules, and it is a non-rigid braided tensor category
[
16
]
.
It turns out that
W_{p,q}
is an infinite direct sum of modules in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, which by
[
47
,
23
]
implies that
W_{p,q}
has the structure of a commutative algebra in the ind-completion (or direct limit completion)
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}})
of the braided tensor category
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
.
Specifically, as a
\mathcal{V}ir
-submodule of the lattice VOA
V_{\sqrt{2pq}\mathbb{Z}}
,
W_{p,q}
is generated by the socles of the Feigin-Fuchs
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules that make up
V_{\sqrt{2pq}\mathbb{Z}}
, together with the vacuum (see for example
[
35
,
5
,
8
,
83
]
), and as a result,

(1.1)

W_{p,q}=V_{c_{p,q}}\oplus\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(2n-1)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2np-1,1},

where
\mathcal{L}_{2np-1,1}
is the simple
\mathcal{V}ir
-module of central charge
c_{p,q}
and lowest conformal weight
(np-1)(nq-1)
.

Our goal is to use the tensor structure of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
to construct a commutative algebra structure with
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
automorphism group on the Virasoro direct sum in (
1.1
), and then show that the resulting VOA structure (given by
[
47
]
) on this Virasoro direct sum is isomorphic to
W_{p,q}
using a suitable uniqueness result. To achieve this, we first show in Section
2
that the
\mathcal{V}ir
-module direct summands in (
1.1
), but with the non-simple VOA
V_{c_{p,q}}
replaced by its contragredient module
V_{c_{p,q}}^{\prime}
, are closed under the fusion tensor product of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
. Moreover, we show that these summands have the same fusion rules as the simple modules in the category
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
of finite-dimensional continuous
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-modules.

Motivated by these Virasoro fusion rules, we then in Section
3
define
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
to be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
consisting of finite direct sums of the modules
V_{c_{p,q}}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2np-1,1}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. We show that the fusion tensor product on
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
gives
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
the structure of a rigid symmetric tensor category with new unit object
V_{c_{p,q}}^{\prime}
rather than
V_{c_{p,q}}
, which is not an object of
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
. Moreover, we show that
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
is symmetric tensor equivalent to
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
. The most interesting part of the proof is showing that
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
is rigid, since its objects are not rigid as objects of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
(because the unit objects in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
are different). We prove that
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
is rigid by showing that a certain
F
-matrix entry (or
6j
-symbol) associated to a suitable associativity isomorphism is non-zero, and we show this using constraints on the
F
-matrix coming from the hexagon axiom for braided tensor categories. This is partially related to (but in this specific case simpler than) Huang’s proof of rigidity for the module category of a rational
C_{2}
-cofinite VOA
[
45
]
, where relevant
F
-matrix entries were shown to be non-zero using information coming from modular character transformations.

Alternatively, one could use the
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
fusion rules of
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
together with the recent paper
[
32
]
to show that
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
is rigid. However,
we believe that constraining
F
-matrices using the hexagon axiom may be useful for proving rigidity in further examples of vertex algebraic tensor categories where the fusion rules are not fully known. We also remark that calculating
F
-matrices (or
6j
-symbols) is an interesting problem in its own right since
F
-matrices appear directly in rational conformal field theory
[
67
]
and state-sum invariants of
3
-manifolds such as Reshetikhin-Turaev
[
76
]
and Turaev-Viro
[
84
]
invariants, and they are also related to Kashaev’s volume conjecture for hyperbolic knots
[
56
]
. They are also relevant in the classification of semisimple tensor categories with given fusion rules
[
78
]
, since the information in the
6j
-symbols is precisely what is forgotten in passing from a semisimple tensor category to its Grothendieck ring.

In Section
4
, we complete our tensor-categorical construction of
W_{p,q}
. First, since
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
and
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
are symmetric tensor equivalent, the canonical algebra of
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
induces a simple commutative algebra
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
in the Deligne product category
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
with decomposition

(1.2)

W_{p,q}^{\prime}=V_{c_{p,q}}^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=2}^{\infty}V_{2n-2}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2np-1,1},

where
V_{2n-2}
is the
(2n-1)
-dimensional simple continuous
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-module. For information about canonical algebras, see for example
[
30
]
, or
[
18
]
for a more detailed exposition, where canonical algebras are constructed by “gluing” braid-reversed equivalent braided tensor categories. We then show that applying the forgetful fiber functor
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}ec
to (
1.2
) yields a simple commutative algebra in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
with automorphism group
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and with the same
\mathcal{V}ir
-module decomposition as (
1.1
), except that
V_{c_{p,q}}
is replaced by
V_{c_{p,q}}^{\prime}
.
Next, we explain how to use the unique (up to scaling) non-zero
\mathcal{V}ir
-homomorphism
V_{c_{p,q}}^{\prime}\rightarrow V_{c_{p,q}}
to turn the simple commutative algebra structure on
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
into a commutative algebra structure on the direct sum (
1.1
) with a unique simple ideal and simple quotient.

We then have to show that the commutative algebra structure we have obtained on (
1.1
) yields the VOA
W_{p,q}
under the correspondence between commutative algebras and VOAs from
[
47
]
. Thus we show that
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
is the unique simple commutative algebra structure on the direct sum in (
1.2
) and use this to prove that the algebra structure on (
1.1
) is also unique if it has a suitable simple ideal. Since the triplet algebra
W_{p,q}
has such a simple ideal, it follows that the algebra we have constructed is indeed the triplet algebra, and moreover, we can show that the automorphism group of
W_{p,q}
is
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by transfer from
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
. These results are formally stated in Theorem
4.6
and Corollary
4.7
.

Finally, in Section
5
, we use the VOA extension theory of
[
47
,
17
,
23
]
to discuss tensor-categorical relations between the Virasoro category
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
and the triplet category
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
. Using the induction tensor functor
F
from
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
to the category of “non-local,” or “twisted,”
W_{p,q}
-modules, we define the category
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
to be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
consisting of modules which induce to ordinary modules for the triplet algebra, that is, objects of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
. We then show that when restricted to this subcategory,
F
defines a fully faithful tensor functor from
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
to the
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariantization of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
. However, unlike in the
q=1
case considered in
[
64
, Section 7]
, induction does not give an equivalence between
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
and the equivariantization
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
because
W_{p,q}
is not simple.

The main result of Section
5
is that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
contains all simple objects of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
and is a ribbon Grothendieck-Verdier category in the sense of
[
14
]
, where the Grothendieck-Verdier duality (a weaker duality structure than rigidity) is given by contragredient modules (see
[
9
]
). The main difficulty is to show that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is actually closed under taking contragredients. We conjecture that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
, unlike the larger category
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, has enough projective objects. If so, then it is natural to conjecture that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is the correct category of
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules to use to build a full logarithmic minimal model conformal field theory. For now, however, we leave the further study of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
to future work.

2.
Some Virasoro fusion rules

To give a novel construction of the
W_{p,q}
triplet VOA, without using screening operators
[
35
,
36
]
, we first need to calculate the fusion rules for the Virasoro modules which appear in the decomposition of
W_{p,q}
as a Virasoro module. Thus let
\mathcal{V}ir
be the Virasoro Lie algebra with basis
\{L_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{\mathbf{c}\}
where
\mathbf{c}
is central and

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\delta_{m+n,0}\mathbf{c}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. In this paper, we will only consider
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules on which
\mathbf{c}
acts by the central charge

c_{p,q}=1-\frac{6(p-q)^{2}}{pq}

for some coprime
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
.

Let
V_{c_{p,q}}
be the universal Virasoro VOA of central charge
c_{p,q}

[
40
]
. By
[
16
]
, the category
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
of
C_{1}
-cofinite
V_{c_{p,q}}
-modules admits the vertex algebraic braided tensor category structure of
[
48
]
-
[
55
]
. More specifically,
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
is the category of finite-length
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules of central charge
c_{p,q}
whose composition factors are of the form
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
for some
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Here,
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
is the simple highest-weight
\mathcal{V}ir
-module of central charge
c_{p,q}
whose lowest
L_{0}
-eigenvalue is

h_{r,s}=\frac{r^{2}-1}{4}\cdot\frac{q}{p}-\frac{rs-1}{2}+\frac{s^{2}-1}{4}\cdot\frac{p}{q}.

Note the symmetries
h_{r,s}=h_{r+p,s+q}
and
h_{r,s}=h_{-r-s}
, which imply that each simple object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
is isomorphic to a unique
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
such that
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
1\leq s\leq q
, and
qr\geq ps
.

In
[
63
]
, we determined some of the braided tensor category structure of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, focusing mainly on the Virasoro Kac modules
\mathcal{K}_{r,s}
defined in
[
68
]
. For each
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
\mathcal{K}_{r,s}
is the submodule of a Feigin-Fuchs module
[
33
]
(of lowest
L_{0}
-eigenvalue
h_{r,s}
) generated by all vectors with
L_{0}
-eigenvalue strictly less than
h_{r,s}+rs
. Thus unlike the simple modules
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
, we do not necessarily have
\mathcal{K}_{r,s}=\mathcal{K}_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
if
h_{r,s}=h_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
; instead,
\mathcal{K}_{r,s}
is a proper submodule of
\mathcal{K}_{r+p,s+q}
, for example.
As special cases, there are non-split short exact sequences

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{(m+2)p-r,1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{mp+r,1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp+r,1}\longrightarrow 0\\
0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,(n+2)q-s}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,nq+s}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,nq+s}\longrightarrow 0\end{matrix}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
1\leq r\leq p-1
, and
1\leq s\leq q-1
.
As a
\mathcal{V}ir
-module, the universal Virasoro VOA
V_{c_{p,q}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}
.
In particular, by (
2.1
), the unique simple
\mathcal{V}ir
-module quotient of
V_{c_{p,q}}
is
\mathcal{L}_{1,1}
, and the unique simple
\mathcal{V}ir
-submodule is
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}=\mathcal{L}_{1,2q-1}
.

In
[
63
]
, we completely determined the fusion products
\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{r,s}
and
\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. In particular, there is a short exact sequence

(2.2)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{r,s-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{r,s}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{r,s+1}\longrightarrow 0

which splits if and only if
q\nmid s
.
We also showed that
\mathcal{K}_{1,2}
is rigid and self-dual, which means in particular that the tensoring functors
\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes\bullet
and
\bullet\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,2}
are exact (see for example
[
30
, Proposition 4.2.1]
), and that

(2.3)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{V}ir}(\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes W_{1},W_{2})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{V}ir}(W_{1},\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes W_{2})

for any modules
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
(see for example
[
30
, Proposition 2.10.8]
). Due to the symmetry
c_{p,q}=c_{q,p}
, the same results hold for
\mathcal{K}_{2,1}
. We also showed that

(2.4)

\mathcal{K}_{r,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,s}\cong\mathcal{K}_{r,s}

for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

[
63
, Theorem 6.7]
.

As we will discuss in more detail later, the simple
\mathcal{V}ir
-submodules of the triplet vertex algebra
W_{p,q}
will come from among the modules
\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}=\mathcal{L}_{1,nq-1}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
(actually, for the
\mathcal{V}ir
-submodules of
W_{p,q}
, we will only need
n
even). At irrational central charges, the fusion rules for such simple modules were determined in
[
39
]
using Zhu algebra methods, while at central charge
c_{1,q}
, their fusion rules were determined in
[
64
]
using the action of
SU(2)
by automorphisms on the doublet algebra, an abelian intertwining algebra extension of the triplet algebra
W_{1,q}
. But at central charge
c_{p,q}
with
p\geq 2
, Zhu algebra computations seem to be difficult, and it is not
a priori
clear that
SU(2)
acts on the triplet or doublet algebra. So instead, we prove the fusion rules using properties of Virasoro Kac modules derived in
[
63
]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

In the tensor category
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
for coprime
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
,

\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=|m-n|+2\\
i+m+n\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2\\
\end{subarray}}^{m+n-2}\mathcal{L}_{ip-1,1}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
such that
m\neq n
, while

\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\cong\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=2}^{n-1}\mathcal{L}_{2jp-1,1}

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, where
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}
is the contragredient dual of
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}
.

Proof.

Since the tensor product on
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
is commutative, and since the tensor product formulas in the theorem statement are symmetric in
m
and
n
, we assume throughout the proof that
m\geq n
. We will use the short exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp+1,1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,nq+1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,nq-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,nq-1}\longrightarrow 0,

which are special cases of (
2.1
). Equation (
2.1
) also gives surjections
\mathcal{K}_{mp+1,1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp+1,1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{1,nq+1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,nq+1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{mp+1,1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1,nq+1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,nq-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\longrightarrow 0.

We now show that the kernel
K
of the surjection
\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}
from
the right exact sequence (

(2.7)

\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-1}/M.

Indeed, by considering the composition

\mathcal{K}_{mp+1,nq-1}

\mathcal{L}_{(m-n+2)p+1,1},\mathcal{L}_{(m-n+4)p+1,1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{(m+n-2)p+1,1},

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{1,(m-n)q+1},\mathcal{L}_{1,(m-n+2)q+1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{1,(m+n-2)q+1}&\text{if}&m>n\\

Thus from (

\mathcal{K}_{mp+1,nq-1}

\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq+1}

\mathcal{L}_{1,1}

has no simple quotients. Thus

, that is,
K\subseteq M

2.7

We still need to show that (
2.7
) is an isomorphism, that is,
M=K
.
It is enough to show that
K
contains all composition factors in the middle socle layer of
\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-1}
, except for
\mathcal{L}_{1,1}
in the
m=n
case. Again, from (
2.6
), the composition factors of the middle socle layer are:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1,iq+1},\quad m-n\leq i\leq m+n-2,\quad i+m+n\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{jp+1,1},\quad m-n+2\leq j\leq m+n-2,\quad j+m+n\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2.

For any of these simple modules
W=\mathcal{L}_{1,iq+1}
or
\mathcal{L}_{jp+1,1}
, let
\widetilde{W}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-1}
be the submodule containing
\mathrm{Soc}(\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-1})
such that
\widetilde{W}/\mathrm{Soc}(\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-1})
is the direct sum of all composition factors of the middle socle layer except for
W
.
Thus if
W
occurs as a composition factor of
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}
, then
K\subseteq\widetilde{W}
, and hence there is a surjection

\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-1}/\widetilde{W}.

We denote the indecomposable summand of
\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-1}/\widetilde{W}
that contains
W
as a submodule by
Q_{i}
if
W=\mathcal{L}_{1,iq+1}
and by
P_{j}
if
W=\mathcal{L}_{jp+1,1}
. Thus to complete the proof of the theorem, it is enough to show that there is no surjection
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\twoheadrightarrow Q_{i}
or
P_{j}
for
m-n\leq i\leq m+n-2
,
i+m+n\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
or
m-n+2\leq j\leq m+n-2
,
j+m+n\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
, except in the case that
m=n
and
i=0
.

We first consider
Q_{m-n}
. By (
2.6
), there is a non-split exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,(m-n)q+1}\longrightarrow Q_{m-n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,(m-n+2)q-1}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
h_{1,(m-n)q+1}<h_{1,(m-n+2)q-1}
, the same argument as used previously in the
m=n
case, using (
2.1
), implies that
Q_{m-n}\cong\mathcal{K}_{1,(m-n)q+1}^{\prime}
. Thus using symmetries of vertex algebraic intertwining operators from
[
38
]
,

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1},Q_{m-n})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,(m-n)q+1},\mathcal{L}_{1,nq-1}).

So by (
2.4
) and (
2.1
), a surjection
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\twoheadrightarrow Q_{m-n}
would induce a surjection

\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,(m-n)q+1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,(m-n)q+1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,(m-n)q+1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,nq-1}.

Such a surjection follows from (

m=n
, but if

, then replacing

\mathcal{L}_{1,nq+1},\mathcal{L}_{1,(n+2)q+1},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{1,(2m-n-2)q+1}.

This set of modules does not include
\mathcal{L}_{1,nq-1}
, so

cannot be a simple quotient of
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}

.

We next consider
Q_{i}
for
m-n+2\leq i\leq m+n-2

. From (

Comparing with (

since both must be the same length
2
quotient of the Verma module of lowest conformal weight

. Then (
2.6
) again yields an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,iq-1}\longrightarrow Q_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,(i+2)q-1}\longrightarrow 0

when
m-n+2\leq i<m+n-2
, and
Q_{m+n-2}\cong\mathcal{K}_{1,(m+n-2)q-1}
. Now to show that
Q_{i}
is not a quotient of
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}
, it is enough to show that there is no surjection

\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes Q_{i},

since
\boxtimes
is right exact. Using
[
63

(see also (

)), there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,iq-2}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{1,iq}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes Q_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,(i+2)q-2}\longrightarrow 0

for

, while

\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes Q_{m+n-2}\cong\mathcal{K}_{1,(m+n-2)q-2}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{1,(m+n-2)q}.

In either case,
\mathcal{K}_{1,iq}
is a direct summand of
\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes Q_{i}
since
h_{1,iq}\neq h_{1,iq-2},h_{1,(i+2)q-2}\,\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbb{Z}
. Thus a surjection
\mathcal{L}_{1,mp-1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1,np-1}\twoheadrightarrow Q_{i}
would induce a surjection

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,nq-2}

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1,nq-2}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1,nq-1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,2}\boxtimes Q_{i}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,iq}.

From the diagrams in
[
63
, Section 2.3]
, it is clear that
\mathcal{K}_{1,iq}\cong\mathcal{L}_{1,iq}
, but that
\mathcal{K}_{mp-1,nq-2}
has no simple quotient of the form
\mathcal{L}_{1,iq}
if

. For the remaining

has simple quotients

\mathcal{L}_{1,(m-n+1)q},\mathcal{L}_{1,(m-n+3)q},\ldots,\mathcal{L}_{1,(m+n-3)q},

and these do not include

is not a quotient of
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}
, and therefore
\mathcal{L}_{1,iq+1}
is not a composition factor of
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}
.

Finally, the case of

for
m-n+2\leq j\leq m+n-2

is similar by

symmetry. In this case, (
2.6
) and (
2.1
) yield a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{jp-1,1}\longrightarrow P_{j}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{(j+2)p-1,1}\longrightarrow 0

when

, and

. Then similar to the

case, a surjection
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\twoheadrightarrow P_{j}
would induce a surjection

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{mp-2,nq-1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{K}_{mp-2,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,nq-1}

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{mp-2,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1,nq-1}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{K}_{2,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{2,1}\boxtimes P_{j}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{jp,1}.

But
\mathcal{K}_{jp,1}\cong\mathcal{L}_{jp,1}
, and the diagrams in
[
63
, Section 2.3]
show that
\mathcal{L}_{jp,1}
is not a simple quotient of
\mathcal{K}_{mp-2,nq-1}
(whether

or

). Thus there is no surjection
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\twoheadrightarrow P_{j}
, and therefore
\mathcal{L}_{jp+1,1}
is not a composition factor of
\mathcal{L}_{mp-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}
for

,

. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3.
An
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-type tensor category

The
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-like fusion rules of Theorem
2.1
suggest that we consider the additive full subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
such that

(3.1)

\{\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\}\cup\{\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 3}\}

is a complete list of representatives of the isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
. That is, every object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of objects from (
3.1
). In particular,
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}
are not objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
. In this section, we will show that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is a tensor subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
with different unit object
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}
, and that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is rigid and tensor equivalent to some
3
-cocycle twist of the category
\mathrm{Rep}\,\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
of finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-modules.

In view of Theorem
2.1
, to show that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is closed under the tensor product
\boxtimes
on
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, it only remains to show that if
\mathcal{W}
is an object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
, then
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\boxtimes W
is also an object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
. In fact, since we want
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}
to be the unit object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
, we want
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\boxtimes W\cong W
. To prove this, we need the following lemma (compare with
[
66
, Lemma 2.19]
):

Lemma 3.1
.

If
W
is an object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\boxtimes W=0
.

Proof.

The simple VOA quotient
L_{c_{p,q}}
of
V_{c_{p,q}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}_{1,1}
as a
\mathcal{V}ir
-module. Also,
L_{c_{p,q}}
is a rational VOA, and every simple
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
for some
1\leq r\leq p-1
and
1\leq s\leq q-1

[
85
]
.
If
W
is any object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, then
\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\boxtimes W
is an
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module by
[
63
, Lemma 5.11]
. On the other hand, the quotient map
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,1}
induces a surjection

W\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\boxtimes W\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\boxtimes W.

Thus
\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\boxtimes W
is an
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module which is a quotient of
W
. Then if
W
is an object of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
the only such quotient of
W
is
0
, since any simple quotient of
W
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}
for some
n\geq 2
(note from (
2.1
) that
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}
is the unique simple quotient of
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}
).
∎

We now define left and right unit isomorphism candidates in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.
First, it follows from (
2.1
), the exactness of the contragredient functor, and the fact that simple
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules are self-contragredient, that we have exact sequences

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}&0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\xrightarrow{\,\pi\,}\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\longrightarrow 0,\\
&0\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\xrightarrow{\,\eta\,}\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\longrightarrow 0.\\
\end{matrix}

We fix
\varphi=\eta\circ\pi:\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,1}
, and then for any object
W
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
, we define homomorphisms

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}&l_{W}^{\prime}:\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\boxtimes W\xrightarrow{\varphi\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}}\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\boxtimes W\xrightarrow{l_{W}}W\\
&r_{W}^{\prime}:W\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{W}\boxtimes\varphi}W\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\xrightarrow{r_{W}}W\end{matrix}

where
l_{W}
and
r_{W}
are the left and right unit isomorphisms in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, respectively.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any object
W
in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
,
l_{W}^{\prime}
and
r_{W}^{\prime}
are isomorphisms.

Proof.

By elementary properties of braided tensor categories,
r_{W}=l_{W}\circ\mathcal{R}_{W,\mathcal{K}_{1,1}}
, where
\mathcal{R}
is the natural braiding isomorphism in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
. Thus naturality implies
r_{W}^{\prime}=l_{W}^{\prime}\circ\mathcal{R}_{W,\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}}
, and hence
l_{W}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism if and only if
r_{W}^{\prime}
is.

To show
l_{W}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism, it is enough to show that
\varphi\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}
is an isomorphism, and for this it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\pi\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}:\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\boxtimes W\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes W

\displaystyle\eta\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}:\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes W\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\boxtimes W

are both isomorphisms. By (
3.2
) and right exactness of
\boxtimes
, we have right exact sequences

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\boxtimes W\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\boxtimes W\xrightarrow{\pi\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}}\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes W\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes W\xrightarrow{\eta\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}}\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\boxtimes W\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\boxtimes W\longrightarrow 0.

It then follows from Lemma
3.1
that
\pi\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}
is an isomorphism and
\eta\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}
is surjective.

To show that
\eta\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}
is also injective and thus an isomorphism, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes W\cong W
, since then the finite-dimensional weight spaces of
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes W
and
\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\boxtimes W\cong W
will have the same dimension. To prove
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes W\cong W
, we may assume
W
is indecomposable, and the case
W=\mathcal{L}_{np-1,1}
for
n\geq 3
is covered by Theorem
2.1
. For the case
W=\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}
, (
3.2
) and right exactness of
\boxtimes
yield a right exact sequence

\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1,1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1,1}=0
by the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, and since
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\cong\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}
by Theorem
2.1
, we get
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}
, as required.
∎

Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3.2
now show in particular that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is closed under the tensor product on
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
.
To write the tensor products of indecomposable objects in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}

Proposition

Theorem 3.3

(\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}},\boxtimes,\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime},\mathcal{A},l^{\prime},r^{\prime},\mathcal{R})

Proof.

To show that

To show that

is a braided tensor category, it remains to prove the triangle axiom for

,
r^{\prime}
, and

\displaystyle(r^{\prime}_{W_{1}}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}})\circ\mathcal{A}_{W_{1},\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime},W_{2}}

\displaystyle=(r_{W_{1}}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}})\circ((\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}}\boxtimes\varphi)\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}})\circ\mathcal{A}_{W_{1},\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime},W_{2}}

,

in

∎

We will need the case of

Theorem 3.4
.

The tensor category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}

Proof.

Since

Similar to

\mathcal{L}_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{L}_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{0}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\boxtimes i^{0}_{11}}\mathcal{L}_{1}\boxtimes(\mathcal{L}_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1})\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathcal{L}_{1}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1})\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1}\xrightarrow{\pi^{0}_{11}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}}\mathcal{L}_{0}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{L}_{1}

is a non-zero scalar multiple of
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}
. From the definitions and (

), this composition equals

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}_{0}\circ\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1}}

\displaystyle\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\boxtimes i^{0}_{11})\circ(r_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}^{\prime})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0,2}F_{0k}\cdot\Pi_{k}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\boxtimes i^{0}_{11})\circ i^{1}_{10}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0,2}F_{0k}\cdot\pi^{1}_{1k}\circ(\mathrm{Id}\boxtimes\pi^{k}_{11})\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\boxtimes i^{0}_{11})\circ i^{1}_{10}=F_{00}\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}.

Thus if

\mathcal{L}_{1}
is rigid with evaluation

are equal; here we have dropped labels on morphisms for brevity. Composing these two compositions with
\widetilde{\Pi}_{k}
for
m=0,2
yields constraints on the
F
-matrix (see for example
[
1

R_{1}^{k}F_{kl}R_{1}^{m}=\sum_{m=0,2}F_{km}R_{m1}^{1}F_{ml},

for

,
where

is defined by
\pi_{1m}^{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{L}_{m},\mathcal{L}_{1}}=R_{m1}^{1}\cdot\pi_{m1}^{1}

The equation for the upper left entry yields

\displaystyle\det(F)=F_{00}F_{22}-F_{02}F_{20}=F_{00}F_{22}-(-1)^{pq}F_{00}+F_{00}^{2}=F_{00}(F_{22}-(-1)^{pq}+F_{00}).

Remark 3.7
.

As far as we are aware, our proof of Theorem
3.4
is the first rigidity proof for VOAs that uses explicit calculation of

-matrices via general categorical principles. In previous rigidity proofs for VOAs, such as
[
45
,
82
,
22
,
64
,
63
]
,

-matrix entries have been calculated or constrained by analytic methods, such as by solving regular singular point differential equations.

In principal, it might be possible to prove

is rigid in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
by such analytic methods, using BPZ partial differential equations derived from explicit expressions for singular vectors given by the Benoit–Saint-Aubin formula
[
11
]
. But although these differential equations are explicit
[
59
, Section 5.3]
, they have rather high order and do not seem particularly easy to solve explicitly.
Thus in Theorem
3.4
we have used the hexagon axiom to constrain

instead.

Alternatively, as we remarked above, we could use
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
to prove
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is rigid in

. This would require showing that

for some

, and this is rather easy from the fusion rules (

). In examples of vertex algebraic tensor categories where the fusion rules are not fully known, the methods from our proof of Theorem
3.4
may be more useful.

By (
3.5
) and Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is a rigid semisimple tensor category with the same fusion rules as the category
\mathrm{Rep}\,\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
of finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-modules. Such categories were classified up to tensor equivalence in
[
58
]
, so we could use this classification to identify
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
.
However, here we will mainly focus on the full tensor subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
whose objects are isomorphic to finite direct sums of the modules
\mathcal{L}_{2n}
,

. This subcategory has the same fusion rules as the category
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
of finite-dimensional modules for the algebraic group

.

Theorem 3.8
.

The category
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
is braided tensor equivalent to
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
.

Proof.

In view of (
3.5
) and Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
, it follows from
[

]
that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
is tensor equivalent to some

-cocycle twist of the category
\mathrm{Rep}\,U_{\zeta}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
of finite-dimensional modules for the quantum group of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
at
\zeta=\pm 1
or
\zeta
not a root of unity. Here
\zeta
denotes a square root of the parameter denoted
q
in
[
58
]
. The only difference between
\mathrm{Rep}\,U_{\zeta}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
and its non-trivial
3
-cocycle twist is that the

-cocycle twist has a new associativity isomorphism

characterized by

(3.9)

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{V_{n_{1}},V_{n_{2}},V_{n_{3}}}=(-1)^{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}\mathcal{A}_{V_{n_{1}},V_{n_{2}},V_{n_{3}}},

where

is the
(n+1)
-dimensional simple object of
\mathrm{Rep}\,U_{\zeta}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. It is not difficult to use
[
58
, Theorem

]
to see that the non-trivial

\mathrm{Rep}\,U_{\zeta}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is tensor equivalent to
\mathrm{Rep}\,U_{-\zeta}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}

\mathrm{Rep}\,U_{\zeta}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})

From
[

, the tensor category

Thus
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}

\mathcal{R}

\mathrm{Rep}\,\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}

\mathrm{Rep}\,\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

4.
Commutative algebras and vertex operator algebras

Let
(\mathcal{C},\boxtimes,\mathbf{1},\mathcal{A},l,r,\mathcal{R})
be a braided tensor category. A
commutative algebra

(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
in
\mathcal{C}
is an object
A
equipped with morphisms

\mu_{A}:A\boxtimes A\longrightarrow A,\qquad\iota_{A}:\mathbf{1}\longrightarrow A

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

Unitality
:
\mu_{A}\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A})=l_{A}
and
\mu_{A}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\iota_{A})=r_{A}
.

(2)

Associativity
:
\mu_{A}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\mu_{A})=\mu_{A}\circ(\mu_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A})\circ\mathcal{A}_{A,A,A}
.

(3)

Commutativity
:
\mu_{A}=\mu_{A}\circ\mathcal{R}_{A,A}
.

Since
A
is commutative, ideals in
A
are the same as left ideals, and a left ideal is a subobject
I\subseteq A
such that
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{A\boxtimes I}\subseteq I
. A commutative algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}
is
simple
if its only (left) ideals are
0
and
A
.

If
(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
and

are two commutative algebras in
\mathcal{C}
, then an algebra isomorphism
(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})\rightarrow(B,\mu_{B},\iota_{B})

\mathcal{C}
-isomorphism

such that

g\circ\iota_{A}=\iota_{B},\qquad g\circ\mu_{A}=\mu_{B}\circ(g\boxtimes g).

Let
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
be the group of automorphisms of the commutative algebra
(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
in
\mathcal{C}
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a braided tensor category of modules for a VOA

in
\mathcal{C}

is the same thing as a VOA

is

\mu_{A}\circ\mathcal{Y}_{\boxtimes}=Y_{A},

where
\mathcal{Y}_{\boxtimes}
is the tensor product intertwining operator of type
\binom{A}{A\,A}
.

In this paper, we are particularly concerned with the VOA extension

(4.1)

-modules

Here

[

]
.

In the rest of this section, we will give a new tensor-categorical construction of

, we will first use Theorem

to obtain a simple commutative algebra

in
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
on which

acts by automorphisms, such that

(4.3)

W_{p,q}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=2}^{\infty}V_{2n-2}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2np-1,1}\cong\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}

as a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathcal{V}ir
-module; here as before,

denotes the

-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module, which is a

-module if and only if

is even. Then we will use the non-zero
\mathcal{V}ir
-homomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{1,1}
to transfer this simple algebra structure on

to a non-simple algebra structure on the

of (

-submodule

from (

Proposition 4.1
.

There is a unique (up to isomorphism) simple commutative algebra structure on the object

(4.4)

W_{p,q}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}(2n+1)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2n}

of
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})}(W_{p,q}^{\prime})\cong PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
has the decomposition (
4.3
) as a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathcal{V}ir
-module.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
is a rigid symmetric tensor category equivalent to
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
by Theorem
3.8
, we can “glue”
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
and
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
as in
[
18
, Main Theorem 1]
to obtain a simple commutative algebra in the ind-category of the Deligne tensor product category
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
with the decomposition (
4.3
). This algebra is essentially the canonical algebra of

\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
; see for example
[
30
, Section 7.9]
, and compare also with the Peter-Weyl Theorem for the compact real form
SO_{3}(\mathbb{R})
of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
Applying the (forgetful) fiber functor from
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
to the category
\mathcal{V}ec
of finite-dimensional vector spaces and observing that
\mathcal{V}ec\otimes\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}\cong\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
as symmetric tensor categories, we get a commutative algebra
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
with the decomposition (
4.4
); see for example
[
65
, Appendix A]
.

Since
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
is obtained from a commutative algebra in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
, the algebra multiplication
\mu_{W_{p,q}^{\prime}}:W_{p,q}^{\prime}\boxtimes W_{p,q}^{\prime}\rightarrow W_{p,q}^{\prime}
is a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-module homomorphism if we give the
(2n+1)
-dimensional multiplicity space of each
\mathcal{L}_{2n}
in
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
the structure of the
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-module
V_{2n}
as in (
4.3
). This is equivalent to saying that
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
acts on
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
by algebra automorphisms. Moreover, since the original commutative algebra in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
is simple,
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
has no non-zero proper
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant ideals. We claim that this implies
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
has no non-zero proper ideals and thus is simple as an algebra in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
.

The proof of the claim is similar to part of the proof of
[
65
, Proposition C.1]
. First, since
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
is semisimple, any non-zero ideal of
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
contains a copy of
\mathcal{L}_{2n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Thus it is enough to show that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and non-zero
v\in V_{2n}
, the ideal generated by
v\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}\subseteq W_{p,q}^{\prime}
contains
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, since
\mu_{W_{p,q}^{\prime}}|_{W_{p,q}^{\prime}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{0}}=r_{W_{p,q}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is surjective.
To prove this, let

i_{m}:V_{2m}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2m}\longrightarrow W_{p,q}^{\prime},\qquad\pi_{m}:W_{p,q}^{\prime}\longrightarrow V_{2m}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2m}

for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be the obvious inclusion and projection morphisms in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
. Since
V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}
is
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant, it generates a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant ideal which must be all of
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
. This implies in particular that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\pi_{0}\circ\mu_{W_{p,q}^{\prime}}\circ(i_{m}\boxtimes i_{n})\neq 0

for some
m
, and in fact
m=n
since
\mathcal{L}_{m}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{n}
contains
\mathcal{L}_{0}
only if
m=n
. Then since
\mu_{W_{p,q}^{\prime}}
is a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-homomorphism,

(4.5)

\pi_{0}\circ\mu_{W_{p,q}^{\prime}}\circ(i_{m}\boxtimes i_{n})=(\cdot,\cdot)_{2n}\otimes\pi_{2n,2n}^{0},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{2n}
is the unique (up to scaling) non-degenerate
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant bilinear form on
V_{2n}
and
\pi_{2n,2n}^{0}:\mathcal{L}_{2n}\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{0}
is as in (
3.6
) (and is surjective). Now we want the ideal generated by
v\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}
to contain
\mathcal{L}_{0}
for any non-zero
v\in V_{2n}
. In fact, taking
v^{\prime}\in V_{2n}
such that
(v^{\prime},v)_{2n}\neq 0
, (
4.5
) implies that

\mathrm{Im}\,\pi_{0}\circ\mu_{W_{p,q}^{\prime}}|_{(v^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n})\boxtimes(v\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n})}=\mathcal{L}_{0}.

Since the ideal generated by
v\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}
is semisimple, it thus contains
\mathcal{L}_{0}
as required. This completes the proof that
W_{p,q}^{\prime}
is a simple algebra in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
.

Finally, to show that the simple commutative algebra structure on (
4.4
) is unique up to isomorphism and that

is the full automorphism group of

, we may replace
\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}}
with any symmetric tensor category
\mathcal{C}
equivalent to
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
and then replace

with the corresponding simple algebra in

. That is, it is enough to find
\mathcal{C}
such that any simple commutative algebra in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})
of the form

where

is the image of
V_{2n}
under a symmetric tensor equivalence

, is unique up to isomorphism and has automorphism group

. See for example
[
65
, Appendix A]
and the last paragraph in the proof of
[
65
, Theorem 7.1]
for why this is sufficient.

One possibility for
\mathcal{C}
is a subcategory of the braided tensor category
\mathcal{O}_{1}
of
C_{1}
-cofinite modules for the Virasoro VOA of central charge
1
. Namely, we take
\mathcal{C}
to be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{1}
whose objects are isomorphic to finite direct sums of simple
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules

of central charge

and lowest conformal weight

. It is shown that
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
as symmetric tensor categories in
[

, Example 4.12]
. Moreover,
[
65
, Theorem B.1]
shows that any simple VOA, equivalently simple commutative algebra,

in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C})
such that

as a
\mathcal{V}ir
-module is isomorphic to the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-root lattice VOA
V_{\sqrt{2}\mathbb{Z}}
. Now, the group of algebra automorphisms of

is the group of VOA automorphisms that fix

; but this is the group of all VOA automorphisms since

is the Virasoro vertex operator subalgebra of

and hence is fixed by any automorphism. Then since

. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Now we need to adjust the simple commutative algebra structure on

in
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
to get a non-simple commutative algebra structure on the
\mathcal{V}ir
-module
W_{p,q}
in (
4.1
), which is an object of
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}})
. For future applications, we work in a general situation. Let
(\mathcal{C},\boxtimes,\mathbf{1},\mathcal{A},l,r,\mathcal{R})
be a braided tensor category, and let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be a full subcategory which is closed under
\boxtimes
. We assume
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
has an object
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
together with a morphism
\varphi:\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbf{1}
in
\mathcal{C}
such that

(4.6)

l_{X}^{\prime}=l_{X}\circ(\varphi\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{X}),\qquad r_{X}^{\prime}=r_{X}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{X}\boxtimes\varphi)

are isomorphisms for any object

in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
(like in (
3.3
)), and such that

(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\boxtimes,\mathcal{A},l^{\prime},r^{\prime},\mathcal{R})
is a braided tensor category. For example, we could take
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}})
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})
.

Now suppose we have objects
A
and
A^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, respectively, equipped with a morphism
\Phi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
. For example, if
A=\mathbf{1}\oplus J
and
A^{\prime}=\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\oplus J
for some object

in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, then we could take
\Phi=\varphi\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{J}
. We also assume that the maps

(4.7)

\Phi\circ-:\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},A),\qquad-\circ\varphi:\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1},A)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},A)

and

(4.8)

\Phi\circ-:\mathrm{Hom}((A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes n},A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}((A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes n},A),\quad-\circ\Phi^{\boxtimes n}:\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\boxtimes n},A)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}((A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes n},A)

for

are isomorphisms. Note that these maps would obviously be isomorphisms if
\Phi
and
\varphi
were isomorphisms in
\mathcal{C}
, but we are
not
assuming this. Note also that (
4.7
) and the

4.8
) yield isomorphisms

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},A^{\prime})&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1},A)\\
\iota_{A^{\prime}}&\longmapsto\iota_{A},\\
\end{array},\qquad\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\prime}\boxtimes A^{\prime},A^{\prime})&\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A\boxtimes A,A)\\
\mu_{A^{\prime}}&\longmapsto\mu_{A},\\
\end{array}

such that

(4.9)

\Phi\circ\iota_{A^{\prime}}=\iota_{A}\circ\varphi,\qquad\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}=\mu_{A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi).

Similarly, the
n=1
case of (
4.8
) yields an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\prime},A^{\prime})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}

\displaystyle\longmapsto g

such that

\Phi\circ g^{\prime}=g\circ\Phi.

With this setup, we now prove:

Theorem 4.2
.

In the setting of the previous paragraph:

(1)

(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}},\iota_{A^{\prime}})
is a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
if and only if
(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
is a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}
, where
(\iota_{A^{\prime}},\mu_{A^{\prime}})
and
(\iota_{A},\mu_{A})
are related by (
4.9
).

(2)

A
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
-morphism
g^{\prime}:A^{\prime}\rightarrow A^{\prime}
is an algebra isomorphism between two commutative algebra structures
(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}}^{(1)},\iota_{A^{\prime}}^{(1)})
and
(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}}^{(2)},\iota_{A^{\prime}}^{(2)})
if and only if
g:A\rightarrow A
defined by (
4.10
) is an algebra isomorphism between
(A,\mu_{A}^{(1)},\iota_{A}^{(1)})
and
(A,\mu_{A}^{(2)},\iota_{A}^{(2)})
, where
(\mu_{A}^{(i)},\iota_{A}^{(i)})
for

Proof.

(1) For the left unit property of a commutative algebra, we calculate

\displaystyle\mu_{A}\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A})\circ l_{A}^{-1}\circ\Phi

\displaystyle=\mu_{A}\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A})\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{1}}\boxtimes\Phi)\circ l_{A^{\prime}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mu_{A}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\Phi)\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}})\circ(\varphi\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}})\circ(l_{A^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mu_{A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)\circ(\iota_{A^{\prime}}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}})\circ(l_{A^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}\circ(\iota_{A^{\prime}}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}})\circ(l_{A^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{-1}

using the naturality of the left unit isomorphisms in
\mathcal{C}
, (
4.6
), and (
4.9
).
Since the two maps in (
4.8
) are isomorphisms in the
n=1

\mu_{A}\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A})\circ l_{A}^{-1}=\mathrm{Id}_{A}\longleftrightarrow\mu_{A^{\prime}}\circ(\iota_{A^{\prime}}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}})\circ(l_{A^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{-1}=\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}}.

Thus the left unit property holds for
(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}},\iota_{A^{\prime}})
if and only if it holds for
(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
.

It follows similarly that the right unit property for
(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}},\iota_{A^{\prime}})
is equivalent to the right unit property for
(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
. Alternatively, this follows from the left unit property and the equivalence of the commutativity of
\mu_{A^{\prime}}
and
\mu_{A}
, which we prove next. Since

\displaystyle\mu_{A}\circ\mathcal{R}_{A,A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)=\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{R}_{A^{\prime},A^{\prime}},\qquad\mu_{A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)=\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}

by (
4.9
) and the naturality of the braiding isomorphisms, the assumption that the two maps in (
4.8
) are isomorphisms in the
n=2
case implies that
\mu_{A}\circ\mathcal{R}_{A,A}=\mu_{A}
if and only if
\mu_{A^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{R}_{A^{\prime},A^{\prime}}=\mu_{A^{\prime}}
. Thus
\mu_{A}
is commutative if and only if
\mu_{A^{\prime}}
is. Similarly,
\mu_{A}
is associative if and only if
\mu_{A^{\prime}}
is because

\displaystyle\mu_{A}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\mu_{A})\circ(\Phi\boxtimes(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi))=\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}}\boxtimes\mu_{A^{\prime}})

and

\mu_{A}\circ(\mu_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A})\circ\mathcal{A}_{A,A,A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi))=\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}\circ(\mu_{A^{\prime}}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}})\circ\mathcal{A}_{A^{\prime},A^{\prime},A^{\prime}}

by (
4.9
) and naturality of the associativity isomorphisms, and because the two maps in (
4.8
) are isomorphisms in the

This proves the first statement of the theorem.

(2) Now suppose
g^{\prime}:A^{\prime}\rightarrow A^{\prime}
and
g:A\rightarrow A
are two morphisms related by (
4.10
). If
g
is a
\mathcal{C}
-isomorphism with inverse
g^{-1}
, then
g^{\prime}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
-isomorphism with inverse
(g^{-1})^{\prime}
characterized by
\Phi\circ(g^{-1})^{\prime}=g^{-1}\circ\Phi
as in (
4.10
). Indeed,

\Phi\circ g^{\prime}\circ(g^{-1})^{\prime}=g\circ g^{-1}\circ\Phi=\Phi

by (
4.10
), and therefore
g^{\prime}\circ(g^{-1})^{\prime}=\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}}
since the first map of (
4.8
) is an isomorphism in the
n=1
case. Similarly,
(g^{-1})^{\prime}\circ g^{\prime}=\mathrm{Id}_{A^{\prime}}
, and similarly
g
is a
\mathcal{C}
-isomorphism if
g^{\prime}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
-isomorphism.

Now we consider how
g
and
g^{\prime}
relate to different algebra structures on
A
and
A^{\prime}
. We have

g\circ\iota^{(1)}_{A}\circ\varphi=\Phi\circ g^{\prime}\circ\iota_{A^{\prime}}^{(1)},\qquad\iota_{A}^{(2)}\circ\varphi=\Phi\circ\iota_{A^{\prime}}^{(2)}

and

g\circ\mu_{A}^{(1)}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)=\Phi\circ g^{\prime}\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}^{(1)},\qquad\mu_{A}^{(2)}\circ(g\boxtimes g)\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)=\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}^{(2)}\circ(g^{\prime}\boxtimes g^{\prime})

by (

because the maps in (

g\circ\mu_{A}^{(1)}=\mu_{A}^{(2)}\circ(g\boxtimes g)\longleftrightarrow g^{\prime}\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}^{(1)}=\mu_{A^{\prime}}^{(2)}\circ(g^{\prime}\boxtimes g^{\prime})

because the maps in (
4.8
) are isomorphisms in the

g^{\prime}:(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}}^{(1)},\iota_{A^{\prime}}^{(1)})\rightarrow(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}}^{(2)},\iota_{A^{\prime}}^{(2)})
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
-algebras.
∎

Taking
\mu_{A}^{(1)}=\mu_{A}^{(2)}

Corollary 4.3
.

In the setting of Theorem

, suppose
(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})

\mathcal{C}

(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}},\iota_{A^{\prime}})

(\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
and
(\mu_{A^{\prime}},\iota_{A^{\prime}})
are related by (
4.9
). Then
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)\cong\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(A^{\prime})
, with isomorphism
g\mapsto g^{\prime}
given by (
4.10

Next, still in the setting of Theorem
4.2
, we consider the relation between simplicity of the
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
-algebra

and ideals of the
\mathcal{C}
-algebra
A
. Since
\Phi
need not be a
\mathcal{C}
-isomorphism,
A
need not be simple if
A^{\prime}
is, but it will be almost simple under mild conditions. First we prove:

Lemma 4.4
.

In the setting of Theorem
4.2
, suppose
(A,\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
is a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}
and
(A^{\prime},\mu_{A^{\prime}},\iota_{A^{\prime}})
is a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
such that
(\mu_{A},\iota_{A})
and
(\mu_{A^{\prime}},\iota_{A^{\prime}})
are related by (
4.9
), and assume that
A=\mathbf{1}\oplus J
and
A^{\prime}=\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\oplus J
for some object
J
in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Assume also that
\iota_{A}
and
\iota_{A^{\prime}}
are the inclusions of
\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
into the direct sums
\mathbf{1}\oplus J
and
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\oplus J
, respectively, and that
\Phi=\varphi\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{J}
. Then
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi=\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi\oplus J
is an ideal of
A
.

Proof.

Since
A=\mathbf{1}\oplus J
, we need to show
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi}\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
and
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{J\boxtimes\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi}\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
. The first inclusion holds because
\mu_{A}|_{\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi}=l_{\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi}
, and the second holds because

\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{J\boxtimes\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi}=\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)|_{J\boxtimes A^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{J\boxtimes A^{\prime}},

using (
4.9
).
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

In the setting of Lemma
4.4
, assume also that any subobject
I\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
decomposes as a direct sum
I=I_{\mathbf{1}}\oplus I_{J}
in
\mathcal{C}
with
I_{\mathbf{1}}\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi
and
I_{J}\subseteq J
, and that any subobject
I^{\prime}\subseteq A^{\prime}
decomposes as a direct sum
I^{\prime}=I_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}\oplus I_{J}
in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with
I_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
and
I_{J}\subseteq J
.

(1)

If
A^{\prime}
is a simple commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi
is a simple object of
\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
is a simple ideal of
A
.

(2)

Conversely, if
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
is a simple ideal of
A
and
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
is a simple object of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, then
A^{\prime}
is a simple commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(1) To show that
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi=\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi\oplus J
is a simple ideal of
A
, let
I\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
be any non-zero ideal. By assumption,
I=I_{\mathbf{1}}\oplus I_{J}
for
\mathcal{C}
-subobjects
I_{\mathbf{1}}\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi
and
I_{J}\subseteq J
, with either
I_{\mathbf{1}}\neq 0
or
I_{J}\neq 0
. In the first case,
I_{\mathbf{1}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi
since
\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi
is simple, so

I\supseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{A\boxtimes\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi}\supseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)|_{A^{\prime}\boxtimes\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{A^{\prime}\boxtimes\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi\circ r_{A^{\prime}}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi.

Thus
I=\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
, proving that
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
is a simple ideal if
I_{\mathbf{1}}\neq 0
.

In the second case, that
I_{J}\neq 0
, the ideal
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{A^{\prime}\boxtimes I_{J}}
of
A^{\prime}
generated by
I_{J}
is equal to
A^{\prime}
because
A^{\prime}
is simple. Thus since
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\boxtimes I_{J}}=I_{J}\subseteq J
, and since
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}
decomposes as the direct sum of subobjects of
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
and
J
by assumption, we get
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}
. Then

I\supseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}\supseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi|_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi.

Thus again
I=\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
by the argument of the preceding paragraph. This proves the first part of the proposition.

(2) Conversely, to show that
A^{\prime}
is a simple algebra, let
I^{\prime}
be a non-zero ideal of
A^{\prime}
. By assumption,
I^{\prime}=I_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}\oplus I_{J}
for some
I_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
and
I_{J}\subseteq J
, with either
I_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}\neq 0
or
I_{J}\neq 0
. In the first case,
I_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}=\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
since we assume
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
is simple in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Then
I^{\prime}=A^{\prime}
because
\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{A^{\prime}\boxtimes\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}=r_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is surjective. Thus
A^{\prime}
is simple if
I_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}\neq 0
.

In the second case, that
I_{J}\neq 0
, the ideal
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{A\boxtimes I_{J}}
of
A
generated by
I_{J}
is equal to
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
because
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
is a simple ideal. Thus since
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{\mathbf{1}\boxtimes I_{J}}=I_{J}\subseteq J
and since
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}
decomposes as a direct sum of subobjects of
\mathbf{1}
and
J
by assumption, we get

\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi\subseteq\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A}\circ(\Phi\boxtimes\Phi)|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}=\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi\circ\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}.

Since
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi|_{J}=J
, it follows that
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}
is not contained in
J
. Since by assumption
\mathrm{Im}\,\mu_{A^{\prime}}|_{J\boxtimes I_{J}}
decomposes as a direct sum of subobjects of
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
and
J
, this forces
I_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}\neq 0
, and we get
I=A^{\prime}
as in the preceding paragraph.
∎

We can now apply the preceding general results to the case
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}})
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{PSL_{2}})

\mathbf{1}=\mathcal{K}_{1,1}

) holds in this setting.
Also,

, while

is simple in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, as required in Proposition
4.5
.
Recalling (

) and (

), we set

and
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\oplus J
, where

, and then we define

, as required in Lemma
4.4
. Note that as required in Proposition

, any
\mathcal{C}
-subobject

decomposes as a direct sum of subobjects of
\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi\cong\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}
and

because
\mathrm{Im}\,\Phi
is semisimple and
\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}

. Similarly, any

-subobject of

We can now prove the main theorem of this paper:

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
be coprime.

(1)

There is a unique up to isomorphism VOA
W_{p,q}
of central charge
c_{p,q}
such that

W_{p,q}\cong\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=2}^{\infty}(2n-1)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2np-1,1}

as a
\mathcal{V}ir
-module and such that
I_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}(2n-1)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2np-1,1}
is a simple ideal.

(2)

The VOA automorphism group of

is isomorphic to
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and

(4.11)

W_{p,q}\cong(V_{0}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{1,1})\oplus\bigoplus_{n=2}^{\infty}V_{2n-2}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2np-1,1}

as a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathcal{V}ir
-module.

Proof.

(1) By
[
47
]
, and using the notation in the paragraph before the theorem statement, it is equivalent to show that there is a unique commutative algebra structure on
A=\mathbf{1}\oplus J
such that
\mathrm{Im}\,\varphi\oplus J
is a simple ideal. To apply the preceding results, we still need to check that the linear maps in (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) are isomorphisms. For more uniform notation, we set
A^{\boxtimes 0}=\mathbf{1}
,
(A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes 0}=\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
and
\Phi^{\boxtimes 0}=\varphi
, so that we need to show

\Phi\circ-:\mathrm{Hom}((A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes n},A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}((A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes n},A),\quad-\circ\Phi^{\boxtimes n}:\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\boxtimes n},A)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}((A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes n},A)

are isomorphisms for
n=0,1,2,3
.

To show
\Phi\circ-
is an isomorphism, we write

\displaystyle(A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes n}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}=0}^{\infty}(2m_{1}+1)\cdots(2m_{n}+1)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2m_{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{L}_{2m_{n}}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}=0}^{\infty}(2m_{1}+1)\cdots(2m_{n}+1)\cdot(N_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\oplus J_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}),

where
N_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
is the multiplicity of
\mathbf{1}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{L}_{2m_{1}}\boxtimes\cdots\mathcal{L}_{2m_{n}}
and
J_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}
is a finite direct sum of objects
\mathcal{L}_{2m}
for

. Under this identification, and observing that

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},J)=\mathrm{Hom}(J_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}},\mathbf{1}^{\prime})=\mathrm{Hom}(J_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}},\mathbf{1})=0,

the map
\Phi\circ-
induces a map

\displaystyle\prod_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle(2m_{1}+1)\cdots(2m_{n}+1)\cdot(N_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}\cdot\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{1}^{\prime})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(J_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}},J))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\prod_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}=0}^{\infty}(2m_{1}+1)\cdots(2m_{n}+1)\cdot(N_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}\cdot\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{1})\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(J_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}},J))

which we need to show is an isomorphism. In fact, since

, the induced map is

\prod_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}=0}^{\infty}(2m_{1}+1)\cdots(2m_{n}+1)\cdot(N_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}\cdot(\varphi\circ-)\oplus\mathrm{Id}_{\mathrm{Hom}(J_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}},J)}),

and this is an isomorphism because

is: it sends the basis endomorphism
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{1}^{\prime}}
to the basis homomorphism
\varphi
. This proves that

is an isomorphism for

(and in fact for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
).

To show

is an isomorphism, the direct sum decompositions

and

(A^{\prime})^{\boxtimes n}\cong\mathbf{1}^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{m=1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\cdot J^{\boxtimes m},\qquad A^{\boxtimes n}\cong\mathbf{1}\oplus\bigoplus_{m=1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\cdot J^{\boxtimes m}.

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1},A)\oplus\bigoplus_{m=1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\cdot\mathrm{Hom}(J^{\boxtimes m},A)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},A)\oplus\bigoplus_{m=1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\cdot\mathrm{Hom}(J^{\boxtimes m},A)

Now,

is an isomorphism because under the identifications

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1},A)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}),\qquad\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},A)\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{1}),

it sends the basis endomorphism
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{1}}
to the basis homomorphism

. Thus

is an isomorphism for

(and in fact for all

).

We can now use Proposition
4.1
, Theorem

(1), and Proposition

(1) to show that

has a commutative

. Thus the two commutative

-algebra structures on

are isomorphic by Theorem

(2).

(2) By Proposition
4.1
and Corollary
4.3
, the automorphism group of the commutative algebra structure on

from the proof of part (1) of the theorem is isomorphic to

. Moreover, for

as a

Finally, to complete the proof of the theorem, we just need to observe that the automorphisms of

∎

5.

As in

Let

It is a finite abelian category and a braided tensor category

Now as discussed in the previous section

has the structure of a commutative algebra in the braided tensor category

G:=PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
acts on

by algebra automorphisms. Let
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
be the category of modules for the commutative algebra

in
\mathcal{C}

is an object

\mathcal{C}

\mu_{X}\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{X})=l_{X},\qquad\mu_{X}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{X}\boxtimes\mu_{X})=\mu_{X}\circ(\mu_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{X})\circ\mathcal{A}_{A,A,X}.

A morphism from

to

in

is a
\mathcal{C}
-morphism

such that

Let
\mathrm{Mod}^{0}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
be the category of local
A
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
, which consists of
A
-modules
(X,\mu_{X})

\mu_{X}\circ\mathcal{R}_{X,A}\circ\mathcal{R}_{A,X}=\mu_{X}.

Then
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
is a tensor category, and its subcategory
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(A)
is a braided tensor category.

By Proposition 4.14 and Theorem 5.13 of
[
83
]
, all simple objects of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
are (possibly infinite) direct sums of simple
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
. Thus by the same argument as in the proof of
[

, Proposition 3.1.3]
, every object of

is an object of
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}})
when considered as a
\mathcal{V}ir

So by
[

and
[

There is a monoidal tensor functor of induction

for objects

in
\mathcal{C}
. Induction is right exact because the tensor product
\boxtimes
on

is right exact. Induction also satisfies Frobenius reciprocity, that is, there is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(W,X)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\,\,\sim\,\,}\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(F(W),(X,\mu_{X}))

(5.1)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mu_{X}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes f)

for all objects
W
in
\mathcal{C}
and
(X,\mu_{X})
in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
consisting of objects

such that

is an object of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
.

Using
[

for example,
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is equivalently the full subcategory of objects

Because

\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is a monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, and by

Proposition 5.2

The braided monoidal category

for

Proof.

First,
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is closed under quotients because

and

are closed under quotients, and because

Next, to show that

is an object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
, we note from (

) and
[

as a
\mathcal{V}ir
-module. Since the conformal weights satisfy

h_{1,2nq-2}=pqn^{2}-(2p+q)n+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{3p}{4q}=h_{1,2}+(np-1)(nq-2),

the conformal weight spaces of
F(\mathcal{K}_{1,2})
are finite dimensional, and the conformal weights of
F(\mathcal{K}_{1,2})
have a lower bound and are all congruent to
h_{1,2}
mod
\mathbb{Z}
. The balancing equation for the double braiding then implies

\mathcal{R}^{2}_{W_{p,q},\mathcal{K}_{1,2}}=e^{2\pi iL_{0}}\circ(e^{-2\pi iL_{0}}\boxtimes e^{-2\pi iL_{0}})=e^{2\pi i(h_{1,2}-0-h_{1,2})}=\mathrm{Id}_{W_{p,q},\mathcal{K}_{1,2}},

so
\mathcal{K}_{1,2}
is an object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
. By
c_{p,q}=c_{q,p}
symmetry,
\mathcal{K}_{2,1}
is also an object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
.

For the remaining Kac modules in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, it follows from
[
63
, Theorem 4.7]
that for
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, there is a surjection
\mathcal{K}_{2,1}^{\boxtimes(r-1)}\boxtimes\mathcal{K}_{1,2}^{(s-1)}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{r,s}
. So because
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is a monoidal subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
which is closed under quotients,
\mathcal{K}_{r,s}
is an object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Finally, the simple objects of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
can be parametrized by
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
for
1\leq r\leq p
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
ps\geq qr
. For all such
(r,s)
, the diagrams in
[
63
, Section 2.3]
show that
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
is a quotient of
\mathcal{K}_{r,s}
, so all
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
are objects of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
.
∎

Because
G=PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
acts on
W_{p,q}
by automorphisms, the category
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
has a
G
-equivariantization
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
as defined in
[
30
, Section 2.7]
for example. Concretely, this is the category consisting of objects
(X,\mu_{X})
of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
equipped with a continuous representation
\varphi_{X}:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
such that

(5.2)

\varphi_{X}(g)\circ\mu_{X}=\mu_{X}\circ(g\boxtimes\varphi_{X}(g))

for all
g\in G
. Morphisms from
(X_{1},\mu_{X_{1}},\varphi_{X_{1}})
to
(X_{2},\mu_{X_{2}},\varphi_{X_{2}})
in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
consist of all morphisms
f:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
such that

\varphi_{X_{2}}(g)\circ f=f\circ\varphi_{X_{1}}(g)

for all
g\in G
. Note that the
A
-action
\mu_{X}
of an object of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
is equivalent to a
V_{c_{p,q}}
-module intertwining operator
Y_{X}:W_{p,q}\otimes X\rightarrow X[\log x]\{x\}
such that
Y_{X}=\mu_{X}\circ\mathcal{Y}_{\boxtimes}
, where
\mathcal{Y}_{\boxtimes}
is the canonical tensor product
V_{c_{p,q}}
-module intertwining operator of type
\binom{X}{W_{p,q}\,X}
in
\mathcal{C}
.
Thus in vertex algebraic terms, the
G
-representation
\varphi_{X}
of an object of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
satisfies

(5.3)

\varphi_{X}(g)(Y_{X}(a,x)b)=Y_{X}(g\cdot a,x)\varphi_{X}(b)

for all
g\in G
,
a\in W_{p,q}
, and
b\in X
.

The
G
-equivariantization
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
is a braided tensor category (see for example
[
30
]
or the discussion in
[
64
, Section 7]
),
and as in
[
64
, Lemma 7.13]
,
induction defines a braided monoidal functor
F:\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
(though we cannot say that
F
is exact because
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
is not rigid). For an object
W
in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
, the representation
\varphi_{F(W)}
is defined by
\varphi_{F(W)}(g)=g\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}
for all
g\in G
. Note that the subcategory
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})\subseteq\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
also has a
G
-equivariantization which is a braided tensor subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
, and thus
F
restricts to a braided tensor functor from
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
to
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

The braided monoidal induction functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
is fully faithful. In particular,
F|_{\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}}:\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}\rightarrow\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
is fully faithful.

Proof.

We continue to use the notation
A
for
W_{p,q}
considered as a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}})
, and we continue to set
G=PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. We need to show that for any objects
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
in
\mathcal{C}
, the map

\displaystyle F:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(W_{1},W_{2})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{G\times A}(F(W_{1}),F(W_{2}))

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes f

is an isomorphism. Since
A=\mathbf{1}\oplus J
as an object of
\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathbf{1}=\mathcal{K}_{1,1}
and
J=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}
as a
G\times\mathcal{V}ir
-module, we can define a projection
\pi_{A}:A\rightarrow\mathbf{1}
such that
\pi_{A}\circ\iota_{A}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{1}}
. This allows us to define

\widetilde{F}:\mathrm{Hom}_{G\times A}(F(W_{1}),F(W_{2}))\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(W_{1},W_{2})

such that for
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{G\times A}(F(W_{1}),F(W_{2}))
,
\widetilde{F}(\Gamma)
is the composition

W_{1}\xrightarrow{l_{W_{1}}^{-1}}\mathbf{1}\boxtimes W_{1}\xrightarrow{\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}}}A\boxtimes W_{1}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}A\boxtimes W_{2}\xrightarrow{\pi_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}}}\mathbf{1}\boxtimes W_{2}\xrightarrow{l_{W_{2}}}W_{2}.

Since
\pi_{A}\circ\iota_{A}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{1}}
, it is clear that
\widetilde{F}(F(f))=f
for all
\mathcal{C}
-morphisms
f:W_{1}\rightarrow W_{2}
.

To show that
F(\widetilde{F}(\Gamma))=\Gamma
as well, note that if
\Gamma:F(W_{1})\rightarrow F(W_{2})
is a morphism in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
, then for all
g\in G
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{F(W_{2})}(g)\circ\Gamma\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}})

\displaystyle=\Gamma\circ\varphi_{F(W_{1})}(g)\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}})

\displaystyle=\Gamma\circ(g\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}})\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}})=\Gamma\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}}).

That is,
\mathrm{Im}\,\Gamma\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}})
is contained in the subspace of
G
-invariants of
F(W_{2})
. In fact, this subspace of
G
-invariants is precisely
\mathrm{Im}\,\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}}
since
J\boxtimes W_{2}\cong\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes(\mathcal{L}_{2n}\boxtimes W_{2})
is a direct sum of non-trivial simple
G
-modules. It follows that

\Gamma\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}})=(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}})\circ(\pi_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}})\circ\Gamma\circ(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}}).

Using this identity along with the triangle axiom of a tensor category the right unit property of the algebra multiplication
\mu_{A}
, and naturality of the associativity isomorphisms the morphism
F(\widetilde{F}(\Gamma))=\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\widetilde{F}(\Gamma)
reduces to the composition

\displaystyle A\boxtimes W_{1}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes l_{W_{1}}^{-1}}A\boxtimes(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes W_{1})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}})}A\boxtimes(A\boxtimes W_{1})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\Gamma}A\boxtimes(A\boxtimes W_{2})\xrightarrow{\mathcal{A}_{A,A,W_{2}}}(A\boxtimes A)\boxtimes W_{2}\xrightarrow{\mu_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{2}}}A\boxtimes W_{2}.

Since
\Gamma
is in particular a morphism in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
, this composition equals

\displaystyle A\boxtimes W_{1}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes l_{W_{1}}^{-1}}A\boxtimes(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes W_{1})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes(\iota_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}})}A\boxtimes(A\boxtimes W_{1})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mathcal{A}_{A,A,W_{1}}}(A\boxtimes A)\boxtimes W_{1}\xrightarrow{\mu_{A}\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{W_{1}}}A\boxtimes W_{1}\xrightarrow{\Gamma}A\boxtimes W_{2}.

But this is just
\Gamma
by naturality of the associativity isomorphisms, the triangle axiom, and the right unit property of
\mu_{A}
. This completes the proof that the induction functor
F
is an isomorphism on morphisms.
∎

Unlike in
[
64
, Theorem 7.14]
, where it was shown that the
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariantization of the representation category of the
W_{p,1}
triplet VOA is braided tensor equivalent to a representation category of the Virasoro algebra at central charge
c_{p,1}
, it is
not
true that induction at
c_{p,q}
central charge for coprime
p,q\geq 2
gives an equivalence between
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
and
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
. The problem is that unlike in the
q=1
case,
W_{p,q}
is not a simple VOA but rather has the simple Virasoro VOA
L_{c_{p,q}}
as its non-trivial simple quotient. This allows us to construct objects of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
which are not in the essential image of the induction functor:

Example 5.4
.

Let
X=V\otimes W
where
V
is any finite-dimensional continuous
G=PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-module and
(W,Y_{W})
is any
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module with finite-dimensional
L_{0}
-eigenspaces. Then
X
admits the
G
-representation
\varphi_{X}(g)=g\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{W}
, and
X
is also a
W_{p,q}
-module with vertex operator
Y_{X}=\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes Y_{W}(\pi(-),x)
, where
\pi:W_{p,q}\rightarrow L_{c_{p,q}}
is the surjective VOA homomorphism. Further, (
5.3
) holds because
\pi(g\cdot a)=\pi(a)
for all
a\in W_{p,q}
and
g\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, so
(X,Y_{X},\varphi_{X})
is an object of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
However, if
V
does not contain the trivial
G
-module
V_{0}
as a direct summand, then
X
is not in the essential image of the induction functor
F
, because any non-zero induced module always contains a non-zero
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant subspace.

Our next result will show that the above examples are typical. We continue to use the notation
A
for
W_{p,q}
considered as a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}})
, and we continue to set
G=PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Since
G
acts continuously on any object
X
of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
, we have
X=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes X_{2n}
, where
X_{2n}=\mathrm{Hom}_{G}(V_{2n},X)
is a
\mathcal{V}ir
-module which is an object of
\mathcal{C}
. We use
X^{G}
to denote the set of
G
-invariants in
X
, that is,
X^{G}=V_{0}\otimes X_{0}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

If
(X,Y_{X},\varphi_{X})
is an object of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
such that
X^{G}=0
, then as a
G\times\mathcal{V}ir
-module,
X=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes X_{2n}
where each
X_{2n}
is an
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module.

Proof.

By assumption,
X=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes X_{2n}
where each
X_{2n}
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
. To show that each
X_{2n}
is actually an
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module, recall the simple ideal
I_{p,q}=\mathcal{L}_{2p-1,1}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}
from (
4.2
).

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, there is nothing to prove if
X_{2n}=0
, so we assume
X_{2n}\neq 0
and take an arbitrary non-zero
b\in X_{2n}
. Then
V_{2n}\otimes b
is a non-zero
G
-submodule of
X
with basis
\{v^{(i)}\otimes b\}_{i=1}^{2n+1}
, where
\{v^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{2n+1}
is an orthonormal basis with respect to a non-degenerate
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-invariant bilinear form on
V_{2n}
. Now take some non-zero
a\in\mathcal{L}_{2n}
, so that
V_{2n}\otimes a
is a non-zero
G
-submodule of
I_{p,q}
with basis
\{v^{(i)}\otimes a\}_{i=1}^{2n+1}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{2n+1}Y_{X}(v^{(i)}\otimes a,x)(v^{(i)}\otimes b)\in X^{G}[\log x]\{x\}=0,

so because
I_{p,q}
is a simple
W_{p,q}
-module, the argument in the proof of
[
61
, Lemma 4.18]
using the analytic associativity of
Y_{X}
and the Jacobson Density Theorem (see also
[
29
, Lemma 3.1]
) shows that
Y_{X}(v^{(i)}\otimes a,x)(v^{(i)}\otimes b)=0
for all
i
. In particular, the annihilator ideal

\mathrm{Ann}_{W_{p,q}}(v^{(i)}\otimes b)=\{w\in W_{p,q}\mid Y_{X}(w,x)(v^{(i)}\otimes b)=0\}

is non-zero and thus contains
I_{p,q}
for all
i
and all
b\in\mathcal{L}_{2n}
.

Since
X
is spanned by the vectors
v^{(i)}\otimes b
for non-zero
b\in X_{2n}
,
n\geq 1
, and
1\leq i\leq 2n+1
, we have now shown that
Y_{X}(a,x)b=0
for all
a\in I_{p,q}
and
b\in X
. Thus
(X,\overline{Y}_{X})
is a well-defined
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module, where
\overline{Y}_{X}=Y_{X}(\pi(-),x)
and
\pi:W_{p,q}\rightarrow L_{c_{p,q}}
is the quotient VOA homomorphism. In particular, each
X_{2n}
is a (not necessarily grading restricted)
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module.
∎

As a first application, Lemma
5.5
gives some information about the relation between arbitrary objects of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
and the essential image of the induction functor.
Indeed, by Frobenius reciprocity (
5
), the inclusion
\iota_{X}:X^{G}\hookrightarrow X
induces the
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)
-morphism

f_{X}=\mu_{X}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\iota_{X}):F(X^{G})\longrightarrow X,

and
f_{X}
is further a morphism in
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
because

\displaystyle\varphi_{X}(g)\circ f_{X}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{X}(g)\circ\mu_{X}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\iota_{X})

\displaystyle=\mu_{X}\circ(g\boxtimes\varphi_{X}(g))\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\iota_{X})

\displaystyle=\mu_{X}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{A}\boxtimes\iota_{X})\circ(g\boxtimes\mathrm{Id}_{X^{G}})=f_{X}\circ\varphi_{F(X^{G})}(g)

for all
g\in G
. Although we cannot say that
f_{X}
is an isomorphism in general (in contrast with
[
64
, Theorem 7.14]
on the relation between the Virasoro and triplet algebras at
c_{p,1}
central charge), the restriction of
f_{X}
to
G
-invariants is an isomorphism. So the kernel and cokernel of
f_{X}
have no
G
-invariants, and Lemma
5.5
immediately yields:

Corollary 5.6
.

For any object
(X,Y_{X};\varphi_{X})
of
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(A)^{G}
, there is a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times W_{p,q}
-module exact sequence

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow F(X^{G})\xrightarrow{\,\,f_{X}\,\,}X\longrightarrow\widetilde{L}\longrightarrow 0

where
L
and
\widetilde{L}
are
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times L_{c_{p,q}}
-modules on which
W_{p,q}
acts through the quotient map
W_{p,q}\twoheadrightarrow L_{c_{p,q}}
.

We can also use Lemma
5.5
to show that the subcategory
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
defined previously is closed under contragredient modules:

Proposition 5.7
.

If
W
is an object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
, then so is its contragredient
W^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By assumption, the induced module
F(W)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes(\mathcal{L}_{2n}\boxtimes W)
is an object of the category
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
of grading-restricted generalized
W_{p,q}
-modules. We need to show the same for
F(W^{\prime})
. First, since
W^{\prime}
has finite length and
F
is right exact, induction on the length shows that
F(W^{\prime})
has finite-dimensional conformal weight spaces and a lower bound on conformal weights, provided the same holds for
F(L)
whenever
L
is a simple object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
. Indeed, this holds for
F(L)
by Proposition
5.2
. It remains to show that
\mu_{F(W^{\prime})}\circ\mathcal{R}_{W_{p,q},F(W^{\prime})}^{2}=\mu_{F(W^{\prime})}
, or equivalently, the vertex operator

Y_{F(W^{\prime})}=\mu_{F(W^{\prime})}\circ\mathcal{Y}_{\boxtimes}:W_{p,q}\otimes F(W^{\prime})\longrightarrow F(W^{\prime})[\log x]\{x\}

involves only integral powers of the formal variable
x
.

To do so, we consider the
W_{p,q}
-module contragredient
F(W)^{\prime}
, which like
F(W)
is an object of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
, with vertex operator defined by

\langle Y_{F(W)^{\prime}}(v,x)w^{\prime},w\rangle=\langle w^{\prime},Y_{F(W)}(e^{xL_{1}}(-x^{-2})^{L_{0}}v,x^{-1})w\rangle

for
v\in W_{p,q}
,
w^{\prime}\in F(W)^{\prime}
, and
w\in F(W)

[
38
]
. Using this relation and (
5.3
), it is easy to see that
F(W)^{\prime}
is also an object of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
, with

\langle\varphi_{F(W)^{\prime}}(g)\cdot w^{\prime},w\rangle=\langle w^{\prime},\varphi_{F(W)}(g)^{-1}\cdot w\rangle

for all
g\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
w^{\prime}\in F(W)^{\prime}
, and
w\in F(W)
. In particular,
F(W)^{\prime}
is the (graded) dual of
F(W)
as both
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
- and
\mathcal{V}ir
-module, that is,

(5.4)

F(W)^{\prime}\cong\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V_{2n}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{L}_{2n}\boxtimes W)^{\prime}\cong W^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes(\mathcal{L}_{2n}\boxtimes W)^{\prime}

as a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\mathcal{V}ir
-module.

The decomposition (
5.4
) shows that
F(W^{\prime})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}\cong(F(W)^{\prime})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})}\cong W^{\prime}
, so we get a
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times W_{p,q}
-module homomorphism

f:F(W^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\,\,\sim\,\,}F((F(W)^{\prime})^{PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})})\xrightarrow{f_{F(W)^{\prime}}}F(W)^{\prime}

which is an isomorphism on
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant subspaces.
Thus we have an exact sequence

(5.5)

0\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ker}\,f\longrightarrow F(W^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathrm{Im}\,f\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathrm{Im}\,f
is an object of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
since it is a submodule of
F(W)^{\prime}
, and
\mathrm{Ker}\,f
has no
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariants. Thus by Lemma
5.5
,
\mathrm{Ker}\,f
is an
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module, that is, a direct sum of modules
\mathcal{L}_{r,s}
for
1\leq r\leq p-1
,
1\leq s\leq q-1
, and
W_{p,q}
acts on
\mathrm{Ker}\,f
through the quotient map
W_{p,q}\twoheadrightarrow L_{c_{p,q}}
. Note that
\mathrm{Ker}\,f
is a grading-restricted
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module since
F(W^{\prime})
has finite-dimensional conformal weight spaces.

We now write the vertex operator
Y_{F(W^{\prime})}
as

Y_{F(W^{\prime})}=\sum_{\lambda+\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,k}\,x^{\lambda}(\log x)^{k},

where
\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,k}:W_{p,q}\otimes F(W^{\prime})\longrightarrow F(W^{\prime})((x))
is a Laurent series and
K
is related to the maximum Jordan block size of
L_{0}
acting on
F(W^{\prime})
(see
[
49
, Proposition 3.20(c)]
). Note that
K
is finite by (
5.5
), since
L_{0}
acts semisimply on
\mathrm{Ker}\,f
and the maximum Jordan block size for
L_{0}
acting on the object
\mathrm{Im}\,f
of
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
is finite. For any
\lambda+\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}
,
x^{\lambda}\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}
is a
V_{c_{p,q}}
-module intertwining operator of type
\binom{F(W^{\prime})}{W_{p,q}\,F(W^{\prime})}
. It suffices to show that
\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}=0
if either
\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z}
or
K>0
.

Indeed, if
\lambda\notin\mathbb{Z}
or
K>0
, then
\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}(a,x)b\in(\mathrm{Ker}\,f)((x))
for all
a\in W_{p,q}
,
b\in F(W^{\prime})
because
\mathrm{Im}\,f\subseteq F(W)^{\prime}
is local and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda+\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k=0}^{K}f(\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}(a,x)b)\,x^{\lambda}(\log\,x)^{k}=f(Y_{F(W^{\prime})}(a,x)b)=Y_{F(W)^{\prime}}(a,x)f(b)\in F(W)^{\prime}((x)).

Thus
x^{\lambda}\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}
is an intertwining operator of type
\binom{\mathrm{Ker}\,f}{W_{p,q}\,F(W^{\prime})}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}|_{\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\otimes F(W^{\prime})}=0
since as a
V_{c_{p,q}}
-module,
F(W^{\prime})
is local with vertex operator
Y_{F(W^{\prime})}|_{\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\otimes F(W^{\prime})}
. Since
W_{p,q}=\mathcal{K}_{1,1}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n}
, it remains to show that
\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}|_{(V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n})\otimes F(W^{\prime})}=0
. In fact, by symmetries of intertwining operators,
x^{\lambda}\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}
induces a
V_{c_{p,q}}
-module intertwining operator

\mathcal{Y}:(\mathrm{Ker}\,f)^{\prime}\otimes F(W^{\prime})\longrightarrow(V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n})((x))

which equals
0
if and only if
\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}|_{(V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n})\otimes F(W^{\prime})}=0
. By
[
63
, Lemma 5.11]
, the image of
\mathcal{Y}
is an
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module. Thus because
\mathcal{L}_{2n}=\mathcal{L}_{2(n+1)p-1,1}
is not an
L_{c_{p,q}}
-module,
\mathcal{Y}=0
and then so is
\mathcal{Y}_{\lambda,K}|_{(V_{2n}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2n})\otimes F(W^{\prime})}
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

It is almost immediate from Propositions
5.2
and
5.7
that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is a locally finite abelian category. Indeed, since it is a full subcategory of the locally finite abelian category
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
which contains
0
and is closed under finite direct sums and quotients, all that remains is to show that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is closed under submodules. But if
\widetilde{W}
is a submodule of an object
W
of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
, then
\widetilde{W}^{\prime}
is a quotient of
W^{\prime}
and thus is an object of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
, and then so is
\widetilde{W}\cong(\widetilde{W}^{\prime})^{\prime}
. Moreover, by
[
9
]
, contragredient modules give
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
the structure of a ribbon Grothendieck-Verdier category, which means that taking contragredients is a contravariant auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
and that there is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}(W\boxtimes X,\mathcal{K}_{1,1}^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(W,X^{\prime})

for all objects
W
and
X
in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
:

Theorem 5.8
.

\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is a locally finite abelian ribbon Grothendieck-Verdier category.

In
[
63
, Section 7]
, we conjectured that there should be a suitable tensor subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
that contains all simple objects of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
and has enough projectives, and that such a subcategory should be the right category for constructing a full (bulk) logarithmic conformal field theory based on the Virasoro algebra, that is, a logarithmic minimal module at
c_{p,q}
central charge. We now conjecture that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is the appropriate subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
for logarithmic conformal field theory. Although we have not yet shown that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
has enough projectives, it may be possible to prove this using the existence and structure of projective objects in
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})

[
69
]
. We leave the problem of obtaining projective objects in
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
to future work.

6.
Conclusion and outlook

In this paper, we have given a new tensor-categorical construction of the triplet
W
-algebra
W_{p,q}
for coprime
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
. Specifically, we have “glued”
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
with a subcategory of
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules at central charge
c_{p,q}
having
PSL_{2}
-fusion rules, and we have then appropriately modified to obtain the non-simple VOA
W_{p,q}
. Due to the involvement of
\mathrm{Rep}\,PSL_{2}
in the construction, a major corollary is that the automorphism group of
W_{p,q}
is
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, with no need to use complicated analysis of screening operators to give an explicit action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. It would be interesting to explore whether the
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-action on
W_{p,q}
could be exploited to simplify the proofs from
[
83
]
of important properties of
W_{p,q}
such as
C_{2}
-cofiniteness and the classification of its simple modules.

We have also defined a tensor subcategory
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
of
\mathcal{V}ir
-modules at central charge
c_{p,q}
that induce to ordinary modules for
W_{p,q}
, and we have shown that it contains all simple objects of
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}
and is closed under contragredients and thus is a ribbon Grothendieck-Verdier category. The main remaining open problems for
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
are to show that it has enough projective objects, and to explore its applications in logarithmic conformal field theory. For the latter problem, our result that
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
is a Grothendieck-Verdier category will be key since there is now a theory of module categories and Frobenius algebras for Grothendieck-Verdier categories under development
[
41
]
, and these structures are important in constructions of full conformal field theories
[
79
,
80
]
.

Beyond conformal field theory, topological quantum field theories and invariants of low-dimensional manifolds can be constructed from braided tensor categories that are not necessarily semisimple (see for example
[
13
,
15
,
28
,
12
]
for some recent results), which is significant since for example
semisimple
4
-dimensional topological field theories cannot detect exotic smooth structures
[
75
]
. While most results in this direction assume rigidity for the braided tensor categories under consideration, it would also be interesting to explore how much these results generalize to non-rigid categories such as
\mathrm{Rep}(W_{p,q})
and
\mathcal{O}_{c_{p,q}}^{0}
, or to the module categories for universal affine
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
VOAs studied in
[
66
]
.

The triplet algebras
W_{p,q}
are special cases of a large class of VOAs sometimes called Feigin-Tipunin algebras
[
37
,
81
]
. In general, these VOAs are (or are expected to be) large extensions of an affine
W
-algebra associated to a simple Lie algebra at a given level, such that the affine
W
-subalgebra is the fixed points of a corresponding Lie group of automorphisms. Thus
W_{p,q}
is the Feigin-Tipunin algebra associated to the principal affine
W
-algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
at level
-2+\frac{p}{q}
. It would be interesting to explore whether the methods of this paper could be used to study Feigin-Tipunin algebras beyond
W_{p,q}
. In fact, we expect that the methods of Section
4
can be combined with the results of
[
66
]
to give a tensor-categorical construction of the Feigin-Tipunin algebra associated to the universal affine VOA of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
(the affine
W
-algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
for the trivial nilpotent element) at level
-2+\frac{p}{q}
for coprime
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
; when
p=1
, these algebras have been recently studied in
[
20
]
.

Finally, we note that there are vertex operator superalgebra analogues of the triplet
W
-algebras which are extensions of the
N=1
super Virasoro vertex operator superalgebra
[
4
]
. We expect that our methods could also apply to give tensor-categorical constructions of these algebras, using the
N=1
super Virasoro tensor categories recently constructed in
[
21
]
.

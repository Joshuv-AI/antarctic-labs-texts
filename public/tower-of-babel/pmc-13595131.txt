Zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy versus immune checkpoint inhibitor regimens by programmed death-ligand 1 combined positive score in locally advanced or metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma: a Bayesian network meta-analysis
Authors: K. Shitara, S.Y. Rha, S.J. Klempner, F. Lordick, R. Ranganath, M. Oh, R.H. Getzenberg, G. Gourgioti, X. Chai, H. Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn this network meta-analysis (NMA), efficacy of first-line (1L) zolbetuximab and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) was compared across programmed death-ligand 1 [PD-(L)1] combined positive score (CPS) levels in patients with locally advanced (LA) unresectable or metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal junction (mG/GEJ) adenocarcinoma in global trials.Materials and methodsStudies evaluating zolbetuximab or ICIs plus chemotherapy as 1L treatments among adults with LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma were included. A Bayesian fixed-effects NMA compared overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) across treatments in PD-(L)1 CPS subgroups (CPS ≥1 to <5; ≥5 to <10; or ≥10). Because PD-(L)1 CPS is a biologically and clinically validated treatment effect modifier for ICIs, CPS-specific hazard ratios were used for ICIs but not zolbetuximab, which targets claudin 18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2). Sensitivity analyses of PD-(L)1 CPS ≥10 leveraged patients with optimal zolbetuximab exposure—those who did not experience nausea/vomiting leading to inadequate dose exposure or discontinuation.ResultsFive regimens (zolbetuximab, pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, or nivolumab plus chemotherapy, and chemotherapy alone) were included. In PD-(L)1 CPS ≥1 to <5, zolbetuximab showed similar or numerically favorable OS and PFS versus ICIs. In PD-(L)1 CPS ≥5 to <10, zolbetuximab had numerically favorable OS and PFS versus ICIs. In PD-(L)1 CPS ≥10, ICIs had numerically favorable OS and PFS versus zolbetuximab. However, in sensitivity analyses, among patients with optimal zolbetuximab exposure, zolbetuximab showed similar OS and PFS to ICIs.ConclusionsThese findings support a biomarker-informed treatment approach in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, CLDN18.2-positive advanced gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma. In PD-(L)1 CPS ≥1 to <10, zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy demonstrated consistent efficacy and may represent a relevant option beyond PD-(L)1–driven selection. In CPS ≥10, zolbetuximab remained clinically meaningful, with efficacy comparable to select ICI-based regimens when exposure is optimized. Results should be interpreted cautiously given these are indirect comparisons and warrant confirmation in prospective studies.

Introduction

Gastric or gastroesophageal junction (G/GEJ) cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide,1,2 with limited treatment options for patients with locally advanced (LA) or metastatic disease. First-line (1L) treatments typically involve chemotherapy, sometimes combined with targeted therapies or immunotherapies. For patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, LA unresectable or metastatic G/GEJ (mG/GEJ) adenocarcinoma, who make up a substantial proportion of patients and often have poor prognoses, treatment has shifted from chemotherapy-only regimens to chemotherapy plus immunotherapies, including pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, and nivolumab, or to chemotherapy plus targeted therapies, including zolbetuximab.3,4

Programmed death-ligand 1 [PD-(L)1] expressed by tumor cells and adjacent immune cells is a targetable biomarker for immunotherapies such as pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, and nivolumab, with a higher combined positive score (CPS) generally associated with better patient responses.5, 6, 7 However, as summarized by the United States Food and Drug Administration’s Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee (ODAC), efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) hinges on expression levels: ICI treatment generally prolongs overall survival (OS) in patients with tumor PD-(L)1 CPS ≥10, has variable effects with CPS ≥1 to <10, and does not show clinically meaningful OS benefits with CPS ≤1.8,9 Although ODAC continues to recommend ICI treatment in patients with PD-(L)1 CPS ≥1,9 further clarity on clinical benefit is needed for patients with CPS <10.

Another cornerstone of treatment for patients with mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma is zolbetuximab, a monoclonal antibody targeting claudin 18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2).10, 11, 12 Zolbetuximab is currently indicated as 1L treatment for patients with CLDN18.2-positive and HER2-negative LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma based on the phase 3 SPOTLIGHT and GLOW trials.10, 11, 12 PD-(L)1 expression in tumors does not preclude the indication for zolbetuximab.13 Yet, in patients whose tumors coexpress PD-(L)1 and CLDN18.2, selecting the most effective treatment option can be challenging. In SPOTLIGHT and GLOW, PD-(L)1 status was neither an enrollment criterion nor a stratification factor and eligibility for zolbetuximab administration was not contingent on PD-(L)1 CPS expression.10, 11, 12 Conversely, in trials for pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, or nivolumab, CLDN18.2 status was neither evaluated nor used as a stratification factor.5, 6, 7 To date, no head-to-head clinical studies have directly compared the efficacy of 1L zolbetuximab versus ICIs in patients with CLDN18.2-positive and/or PD-(L)1-expressing advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

When direct comparisons from phase 3 trials are lacking, network meta-analysis (NMA) bridges evidence gaps by integrating data from multiple randomized controlled trials (RCTs). NMA enables indirect comparisons of treatment regimens through common comparators, thus providing a comprehensive assessment of relative efficacy that can be used to support clinical decision-making.14,15 In a previous NMA comparing zolbetuximab and other 1L treatments for patients with LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma in global trials, zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy generally had a higher likelihood of treatment superiority in progression-free survival (PFS) or OS compared with other therapies, including pembrolizumab or nivolumab plus chemotherapy.16

To facilitate clinical decision-making in patients with CLDN18.2-positive and/or PD-(L)1–expressing advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, we present the findings of an NMA of global trials evaluating comparative efficacy of 1L zolbetuximab and ICI treatments across CPS level subgroups in patients with LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study followed guidelines for conducting NMAs outlined by the United Kingdom National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) Decision Support Unit and the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR).17,18 The analysis was based on data from RCTs identified through a systematic literature review.

This study used an NMA to indirectly compare the efficacy of 1L ICI treatments with that of 1L zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy for patients with HER2-negative or unknown HER2 status, LA unresectable, or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma. The analysis focused on primary outcomes of PFS and OS, stratified by CPS level. Hazard ratios (HRs) were used to measure the treatment effect for both outcomes.

Search strategy

A systematic literature review of English-language articles published in the period from January 2000 to January 2026 was conducted to identify eligible RCTs of 1L ICI-based treatment regimens in patients with LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma. These trials were further screened for inclusion in the NMA using predefined eligibility criteria based on the population, intervention, comparison, outcome, and study design framework (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108530), as recommended by Centre for Reviews and Dissemination guidelines.19

Data extraction

Data on treatment arms, dosing, and outcomes were extracted from the included trials. For trials with blended chemotherapy backbones, HRs were extracted from Kaplan–Meier (KM) curves.

Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

Similar to the PD-(L)1 segmentation used by ODAC, we used the following key CPS subgroups: ≥1 to <5, ≥5 to <10, and ≥10, to determine whether CPS influences the relative efficacy of zolbetuximab compared with other treatments.

Two sensitivity analyses of patients treated with zolbetuximab were conducted. The first leveraged those with optimal zolbetuximab exposure; that is, patients who did not experience nausea or vomiting leading to inadequate dose exposure or discontinuation.20 Median OS [17.9 months; 95% confidence interval (CI) 16.43, 19.78 months] and median PFS (10.38 months; 95% CI 8.71, 12.22 months) as reported in the zolbetuximab European Public Assessment Report (EPAR),20,21 based on patients with optimal zolbetuximab exposure, were extracted and used as EPAR-derived inputs for the sensitivity analysis.

The second sensitivity analysis used results from the SPOTLIGHT trial only, in which patients received zolbetuximab plus folinic acid plus 5-fluorouracil and oxaliplatin (FOLFOX), because the FOLFOX backbone was used more often in non-Asian populations than the capecitabine plus oxaliplatin (CAPOX) backbone.10,11

In this NMA, CPS-specific HRs were used for pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, and nivolumab. PD-(L)1 CPS was considered a biologically and clinically validated treatment effect modifier for ICIs.5, 6, 7 Zolbetuximab targets CLDN18.2 rather than PD-(L)1,22,23 and PD-(L)1 CPS was not considered a treatment effect modifier for zolbetuximab.24 For tislelizumab, efficacy results were analyzed using tumor area positivity (TAP) scores, the predefined biomarker testing criterion in the included RATIONALE-305 trial.7 These scores were chosen over CPSs to match the biomarker testing protocol of RATIONALE-305. Aligned with this approach, a 2025 NMA comparing tislelizumab and other ICIs in 1L G/GEJ cancer-treated TAP and CPS as interchangeable biomarkers in its analyses.25

Statistical analysis

From the included studies, HRs and 95% CIs for OS and PFS were retrieved from KM curves. The Bayesian NMA conducted 50 000 burn-in iterations to estimate treatment effects relative to a comparator. Pairwise meta-analyses were carried out to compare treatment efficacy and the probability of treatment superiority was evaluated using pairwise comparisons. A fixed-effects model was fitted based on model stability and goodness of fit. Comparative estimates were summarized as median HRs with 95% credible intervals (CrIs). Network plots were used to illustrate the connectivity of the treatment networks. Treatment efficacy ranking probabilities were estimated using surface under the cumulative ranking (SUCRA) curves. SUCRA values represent the probability that a treatment ranks among the most effective options, with higher values representing a greater probability of being the superior treatment.30

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.3.0, Vienna, Austria) and Just Another Gibbs Sampler. Statistical methods adhered to the guidelines for conducting NMAs provided by the NICE Decision Support Unit and ISPOR.17,18

Results

In total, 71 RCTs were identified. After applying inclusion and exclusion criteria, five trials were included in the primary NMA, representing five unique regimens (zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy, pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, nivolumab plus chemotherapy, and chemotherapy alone; Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108530). Comprehensive evidence networks for OS and PFS were constructed using these trials. Baseline characteristics across trials are reported in Supplementary Results and Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.108530.

Patients who received 1L ICIs in combination with CAPOX/FOLFOX/CF/CX (henceforth simplified to ‘chemotherapy’) had a significantly improved or numerically favorable OS and PFS compared with chemotherapy alone. Subgroup analysis: CPS ≥1 to <5, PFS

Compared with chemotherapy alone, the hazard of disease progression or death was significantly lower with zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.71, 95% CrI 0.61, 0.83) or pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.79, 95% CrI 0.64, 0.97). Tislelizumab plus chemotherapy and nivolumab plus chemotherapy each showed no significant survival difference compared with chemotherapy alone (Figure 2A). Zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy had the highest SUCRA for PFS (93.2%) (Table 1).

Subgroup analysis: CPS ≥5 to <10, OS

Zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy showed a significantly lower hazard of death compared with chemotherapy alone (HR 0.77, 95% CrI 0.67, 0.89). Pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy, tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, and nivolumab plus chemotherapy each exhibited a similar hazard of death to that with chemotherapy alone (Figure 2B). Zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy had the highest SUCRA for OS (89.7%) (Table 1).

Subgroup analysis: CPS ≥5 to <10, PFS

The hazard of disease progression or death was significantly lower with zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy than with chemotherapy alone (HR 0.71, 95% CrI 0.61, 0.83). In contrast, it was similar between pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone (HR 0.95, 95% CrI 0.70, 1.29; Figure 2B). Zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy had a numerically lower hazard of disease progression or death than pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.75, 95% CrI 0.53, 1.06) but this difference was not statistically significant, with overlapping CrIs. Zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy had the highest SUCRA for PFS (91.0%) (Table 1).

Subgroup analysis: CPS ≥10, OS

Compared with chemotherapy alone, the hazard of death was significantly reduced with zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.77, 95% CrI 0.67, 0.89), pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.67, 95% CrI 0.57, 0.79), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.57, 95% CrI 0.43, 0.76), and nivolumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.68, 95% CrI 0.58, 0.79; Figure 2C). Tislelizumab plus chemotherapy had the highest SUCRA for OS (92.2%) (Table 1).

Subgroup analysis: CPS ≥10, PFS

Compared with chemotherapy alone, the hazard of disease progression or death was significantly lower with zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.71, 95% CrI 0.61, 0.83), pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.65, 95% CrI 0.55, 0.77), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.56, 95% CrI 0.42, 0.74), and nivolumab plus chemotherapy (HR 0.68, 95% CrI 0.58, 0.80; Figure 2C). Tislelizumab plus chemotherapy had the highest SUCRA for PFS (91.2%) (Table 1).

Discussion

ICIs and targeted antibody therapies have led to several approved 1L treatments for patients with LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma. In this NMA, the benefits of the CLDN18.2-targeting therapy zolbetuximab and programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/PD-(L)1 inhibitors were compared when combined with chemotherapy. Zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy demonstrated similar or numerically favorable OS and PFS benefits versus those observed with pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, or nivolumab plus chemotherapy in patients with PD-(L)1 CPS <10. Furthermore, zolbetuximab demonstrated consistent ranking superiority across PD-(L)1 CPS ≥1 to <10 subgroups, a setting in which the benefit of ICIs remains uncertain.8,9 Although ICIs demonstrated numerically favorable relative efficacy in patients with PD-(L)1 CPS ≥10, these differences were attenuated in analyses of patients with optimal zolbetuximab exposure.20,21 To our knowledge, this is the first indirect comparison of 1L zolbetuximab with PD-1/PD-(L)1 inhibitors by CPS level in patients with LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

Our previous NMA compared relative efficacy of zolbetuximab and other select 1L treatments in patients with LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma in the overall population regardless of CPS cut-offs. In that prior analysis, zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy showed OS and PFS benefits similar to those observed with pembrolizumab or nivolumab in combination with chemotherapy.16 In the current NMA of patients with PD-(L)1 CPS <10, zolbetuximab combined with chemotherapy showed OS and PFS benefits similar or numerically favorable versus those of PD-1/PD-(L)1 inhibitors. Although this NMA pooled CAPOX and FOLFOX regimens to facilitate the comparison, zolbetuximab has demonstrated efficacy in combination with either CAPOX or FOLFOX in independent trials.10,11

The efficacy of PD-1/PD-(L)1 inhibitors, including pembrolizumab, tislelizumab, and nivolumab, depends on PD-(L)1 expression level.8,31 Data from the ODAC meeting and phase 3 trials indicate that ICIs primarily benefit patients with HER2-negative mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma with high PD-(L)1 expression, particularly when CPS is ≥10.6,8,9,12 In patients whose tumors had PD-(L)1 CPS <10, ICIs offered little or no OS benefit, including in those with CPS <1, with HRs being close to 1.8 A meta-analysis published in 2024 reported that the benefit of PD-(L)1 inhibitors is less clear in patients with CPS <5 than in those with CPS ≥5.31 Overall, for HER2-negative G/GEJ adenocarcinoma, these findings suggest that PD-1/PD-(L)1 inhibitors are most effective when PD-(L)1 expression is high (CPS ≥10) but the extent of benefit in patients with CPS 1 to <10 remains unclear.

For anti-PD-1 trials in our study, patients with microsatellite-instability-high (MSI-H) tumors were included. Because MSI-H tumors accumulate genetically damaged cells that are particularly susceptible to immune-mediated elimination by ICIs,32 exclusion of patients with MSI-H tumors may have demonstrated a greater benefit for zolbetuximab than ICIs across PD-(L)1 CPS subgroups due to no longer favoring that mechanism of action for ICIs. Additionally, this NMA is based on the assumption that ICI efficacy is independent of CLDN18.2 positivity. However, emerging evidence suggests that outcomes with ICIs may be impacted among patients whose tumors are CLDN18.2-positive (defined as ≥75% of tumor cells demonstrating moderate to strong membranous CLDN18 staining as determined by central immunohistochemistry),33 although these data remain limited and should be interpreted cautiously. Thus, the estimated relative treatment effects of zolbetuximab-based therapy may be conservative in this analysis.

CLDN18.2 immunohistochemistry has been standardized using the VENTANA CLDN18 (43-14A) RxDx Assay, with clearly defined membranous staining and tumor cell percentage levels, demonstrating robust analytical and clinical performance.34 Additionally, CLDN18.2 is highly prevalent in patients with advanced G/GEJ cancer, with 73.8% of patients expressing some level of CLDN18.2.35 In patients screened for CLDN18.2 tumor status in SPOTLIGHT and GLOW, 38.4% (1730/4507) had ≥75% of tumor cells demonstrating moderate to strong membranous CLDN18 staining as determined by central immunohistochemistry.36

CLDN18.2 and PD-(L)1 represent biologically distinct and potentially complementary pathways, reflecting tumor epithelial phenotype and immune activation, respectively.22,29 This distinction may be particularly relevant for patients whose tumors co-express both biomarkers. The optimal treatment approach remains uncertain in this patient population because no head-to-head trials have directly compared zolbetuximab-based therapy with PD-1 inhibitors. In this setting, the combination of PD-1/PD-(L)1 inhibitors, anti-CLDN18.2 antibodies, and chemotherapy is being investigated in the phase 2 multicohort ILUSTRO study (NCT03505320), which was designed to assess the efficacy and safety of zolbetuximab as monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy and/or PD-1 inhibition.37,38 In ILUSTRO, the median PFS was 23.6 months in patients with high CLDN18.2 positivity and PD-(L)1 CPS ≥1.38 Findings from ILUSTRO are informative, as they suggest that combining anti-CLDN18.2 therapy with PD-1 inhibition and chemotherapy may be a promising strategy in patients whose tumors are both CLDN18.2-positive and have PD-(L)1 CPS ≥1. This strategy will be assessed in the ongoing phase 3, double-blind, randomized LUCERNA study (NCT06901531), in which zolbetuximab plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy is being evaluated versus placebo plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy as 1L treatment for patients with CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative, PD-(L)1-positive advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma.39 Together, these considerations support cautious interpretation of cross-trial comparisons involving ICIs while also highlighting the rationale for ongoing prospective evaluation of triplet approaches, including in LUCERNA.

The most common treatment emergent adverse events in patients treated with zolbetuximab were nausea and vomiting,10, 11, 12 which were most common on the first infusion day.20 These effects are generally manageable with a minimum of a three-drug antiemetic prophylaxis and infusion modifications.20,40,41 With effective antiemetic prophylaxis and subsequently maintained treatment exposure, a recent ad hoc analysis reported zolbetuximab can achieve numerically improved outcomes beyond those observed in SPOTLIGHT and GLOW.20 With optimal zolbetuximab exposure,20,21 the sensitivity analysis of patients with PD-(L)1 CPS ≥10 suggested that the efficacy of zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy may be similar to that of ICIs plus chemotherapy; however, these findings should be interpreted with caution given the indirect nature of the comparisons and the potential for selection bias.

This study has several limitations. Safety comparisons of the therapeutic regimens were beyond the scope of this work. Trials enrolled in Asia-only populations were not included. Across global phase 3 trials, Asian subgroups consistently demonstrated numerically stronger outcomes compared with the overall ITT populations;7,10,11 thus, including Asia-only studies while using ITT results from global trials could introduce imbalance and limit cross-trial comparability. Additionally, the indirect comparisons of treatment efficacy do not equate to a head-to-head comparison. The NMA extracted data from published RCTs and thus may be subject to publication bias. Most network links were based on a single trial; owing to limited data, meta-regression was not carried out to adjust for trial heterogeneity. Cross-trial heterogeneity may have been introduced by variability in biomarker prevalence and differences in study design, including stratification factors, the use of prespecified versus nonprespecified subgroup analyses, and variations in enrolled populations (e.g. biomarker-selected versus all-comer cohorts). For anti-PD-1 trials, outcome estimates may have been influenced by the inclusion of patients with MSI-H tumors, who derive disproportionately high benefits from ICIs. However, MSI status was not consistently reported across studies or stratified by PD-(L)1 CPS subgroups in the studies included in this NMA, precluding a sensitivity analysis restricted to microsatellite stable/proficient mismatch repair populations. Given the low prevalence of MSI-H status in patients with advanced or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma (∼3%-5%),42 its impact on overall estimates is likely limited. As MSI-H tumors respond favorably to ICIs, their inclusion may inflate ICI effects; exclusion of MSI-H tumors may further favor zolbetuximab and is unlikely to change conclusions. Another limitation is the inclusion of KEYNOTE-062, in which early crossing of survival curves may indicate potential violation of the proportional hazards assumption; however, KEYNOTE-062 was only included in the PD-(L)1 CPS ≥10 subgroup and its impact on OS estimates may be limited due to the small sample size. In RATIONALE-305, PD-(L)1 expression was assessed using the TAP rather than CPS. Although TAP and CPS have been considered broadly comparable, differences in antibodies, scoring algorithms, and thresholds may introduce variability, particularly at low PD-(L)1 expression levels. These methodological differences may limit the comparability of subgroup analyses and contribute to observed differences in relative efficacy across ICIs, including the numerically favorable outcomes observed with tislelizumab in the PD-(L)1 CPS ≥10 subgroup, possibly reflecting a challenge inherent to analyses synthesizing data across trials conducted in different settings. The usage of ICIs may also differ across regions and may have influenced the OS and PFS results. One key limitation of the analysis was that CLDN18.2 status was not evaluated in most trials, and comparisons relied on the assumption that CLDN18.2 is not an effect modifier of PD-1.24 Possible nonproportional hazards for ICI regimens (i.e. late separation of KM curves) suggest that HRs may underestimate or overestimate time-varying effects. Median follow-up varied across trials, and with long-term survival data from SPOTLIGHT and GLOW still maturing,10, 11, 12 cross-trial comparisons of long-term outcomes should be interpreted with caution; longer follow-up is needed to fully characterize the durability of treatment effects of zolbetuximab. Lastly, CAPOX and FOLFOX were assumed to be equally efficacious,26, 27, 28 similar to the approach taken in an NMA presented in 2025, in which tislelizumab and other ICIs were compared in 1L G/GEJ cancer.25

Conclusions

Findings from this NMA suggest that zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy provides a consistent and clinically relevant treatment option across PD-(L)1 CPS subgroups in patients with CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma. In patients with CPS ≥1 to <10, zolbetuximab demonstrated a favorable efficacy profile relative to ICI-based regimens and may represent a relevant option beyond PD-(L)1–guided selection. In CPS ≥10, zolbetuximab remained a clinically meaningful option, with comparable efficacy to ICI-based regimens when zolbetuximab treatment exposure was optimized. These findings highlight the potential value of biomarker-informed treatment strategies that integrate both PD-(L)1 and CLDN18.2 while recognizing the need for prospective validation.

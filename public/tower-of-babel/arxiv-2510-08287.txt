On the Cahn-Hilliard equation with nonlinear diffusion: the non-convex case
Monica Conti, Stefania Gatti, Andrea Giorgini & Giulio Schimperna

Abstract
. We investigate the Cahn-Hilliard equation with nonlinear diffusion and non-degenerate mobility modeling phase separation phenomena in complex systems (e.g., crystals and polymers). Previous results in the literature on this model relied on the strong convexity assumption of the gradient part of the energy, which excludes relevant cases. In this work, we remove the convexity condition and establish new qualitative properties of solutions under general assumptions on the diffusion and mobility functions. In two spatial dimensions, we prove uniqueness of weak solutions, their smoothing effect for positive times, and convergence to equilibrium as time tends to infinity. In three dimensions, we show local well-posedness of strong solutions for arbitrary initial data and global existence for data close to energy minimizers, yielding a Lyapunov stability principle. A key ingredient of our analysis is a Lojasiewicz-Simon inequality tailored to the nonlinear diffusion case, which enables us to characterize the longtime dynamics. Keywords: Cahn-Hilliard equation, non-degenerate mobility, nonlinear diffusion, Lojasiewicz–Simon inequality, convergence to equilibrium MSC 2010: 35K55, 35A02, 35B65, 35B40, 35Q82



1.
Introduction

We study the Cahn-Hilliard equation with nonlinear diffusion

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}(b(\phi)\nabla\mu),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}(a(\phi)\nabla\phi)+a^{\prime}(\phi)\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(\phi).

The initial-boundary value problem related to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is considered in
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
, where
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d=2
and
d=3
. The system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is equipped with the following homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions

(1.3)

\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}\phi=b(\phi)\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}\mu=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),

and initial condition

(1.4)

\phi|_{t=0}=\phi_{0}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

Here,
n
is the unit outward normal vector on
\partial\Omega
, and

\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}
denotes the outer normal derivative on
\partial\Omega
.
The state variable of the system is the difference of two fluid concentrations
\phi\colon\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to[-1,1]
(order parameter).
The function
a\colon[-1,1]\to[0,\infty)
represents nonlinear diffusion under the assumptions

(1.5)

a\in C^{2}([-1,1]):0<a_{m}\leq a(s)\leq a_{M},\ s\in[-1,1].

The function
b\colon[-1,1]\to[0,\infty)
is the non-degenerate Onsager mobility, which satisfies

(1.6)

b\in C^{1}([-1,1]):0<b_{m}\leq b(s)\leq b_{M},\ s\in[-1,1].

The homogeneous free energy density
\Psi
is the Flory-Huggins potential

(1.7)

\Psi(s)=F(s)-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}s^{2}=\frac{\theta}{2}\bigg[(1+s)\log(1+s)+(1-s)\log(1-s)\bigg]-\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}s^{2},\quad s\in[-1,1],

where
\theta<\theta_{0}
are constant positive parameters. The system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) is a generalization of the classical Cahn-Hilliard equation, which is recovered when
a\equiv 1
.
The associated Ginzburg-Landau free energy is

(1.8)

E(\phi)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{a(\phi)}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\Psi(\phi)\,{\rm d}x,

and (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), together with (
1.3
), is the resulting mass conserving gradient flow with respect to the metric of the dual space of
H^{1}(\Omega)
. The importance of including a nonlinear coefficient in the interfacial free energy density, depending on the order parameter, stems from the modeling of complex systems such as crystals, polymers and hydrogels (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
).
Different forms of
a
significantly affect the free energy costs of interfaces between components. For instance, specific approximations arising from the description of phase separation include polynomials such as

a(u)=a_{0}+a_{1}u+a_{2}u^{2},

where
a_{0}>0
(cf.
[
26
]
), or even singular coefficients of de Gennes type as in
[
16
]
. We also mention that more general Ginzburg-Landau free energy (w.r.t. (
1.8
)) are employed in amphiphilic systems (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
)

The mathematical analysis of system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) has been intensively studied over the last decades in the constant diffusion case, namely
a\equiv 1
. For the interested reader, we mention the works with constant mobility
[
1
,
5
,
15
,
17
,
20
,
29
]
,
non-degenerate mobility
[
7
,
12
,
31
,
33
]
, as well as degenerate mobility
[
10
,
18
,
14
,
27
]
,
and the references therein.
On the other hand, the nonlinear diffusion case
a(\phi)
has only been partially developed so far. The main contribution in the literature is the work by the fourth author and Pawlow
[
32
]
(see, in particular, Sections 5 and 6) referring to the case with constant mobility. Therein, the authors first showed the existence of global weak solutions under the assumption (
1.5
). The uniqueness of weak solutions and their regularization properties for positive times were achieved under the assumptions

(1.9)

a^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq 0,\quad\left(\frac{1}{a}\right)^{\prime\prime}(s)<-\kappa,\quad\forall\,s\in[-1,1],

where
\kappa>0
. As observed in
[
32
]
, the above conditions entails that the gradient part of the energy

J(\phi)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{a(\phi)}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\quad\text{is {strictly convex}}.

Concurrently, a Navier-Stokes/Cahn-Hilliard system, including (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with non-degenerate or degenerate mobility, together with nonlinear diffusion, was studied by Abels, Garcke and Depner in
[
2
,
3
]
, where they proved the existence of global weak solutions. Therefore, several mathematical questions on the model in presence of a
non-convex
gradient energy part remains largely unexplored.

In this work, we investigate fine qualitative properties of the solutions to the Cahn-Hilliard system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) without imposing the convexity assumption (
1.9
). Our aim is twofold: to address uniqueness and regularity of solutions in the general setting (
1.5
); to characterize the longtime behavior of solutions, a question that has not been explored so far, even in the
convex
case (
1.9
).
Under the general setting (
1.5
), our main results are summarized here below:

•

Theorem
1.2
:
In two space dimensions, the global weak solutions are unique, propagate regularity for positive times, and converge to an equilibrium point of the free energy
E(\phi)
as
t\to\infty
.

•

Theorem
1.3
: In three space dimensions, strong solutions exist locally in time for arbitrarily large initial data, and globally in time for initial data sufficiently close to energy minimizers. The latter entails a Lyapunov stability principle. Furthermore, such global strong solutions converge to an equilibrium point of the free energy
E(\phi)
as
t\to\infty
.

Before detailing our main results, let us point out some novelties of this work.
First of all, we show that any weak solution to the system exhibits enhanced regularity properties.
A key tool at this stage is a tailored regularity theory
for the associated stationary problem with nonlinear diffusion that we derive in Section
3
based on a suitable reformulation of the
chemical potential borrowed from
[
2
]
.
In particular, obtaining that any weak solution belongs to
L^{4}(0,\infty;H^{2}(\Omega))
enables us to extend the techniques of
[
12
]

to address the combined effects of the nonlinear diffusion
a
and the variable mobility
b
.
This leads to uniqueness and global regularity results in two spatial dimensions.
Furthermore, based on the improved regularity of weak solution at any positive time, we resolve an open question from
[
32
, Remark 6.4]
regarding the validity of the energy equality for
E
in the non-convex setting. In summary, our analysis goes beyond the classical Cahn–Hilliard equation with constant coefficients,
providing a comprehensive well-posedness framework for the general nonlinear, non-degenerate system in two dimensions.

Secondly, we establish a crucial tool for analyzing the long-time behavior of solutions, as well as proving Lyapunov stability
in the spirit of Simon
[
34
]
: the Lojasiewicz–Simon inequality. For the classical Cahn–Hilliard energy with constant coefficient,
this inequality was first derived in
[
25
]
and later refined in
[
5
]
. However, such a tool has not yet been developed in the setting of nonlinear diffusion.
In order to present the new Lojasiewicz-Simon inequality related to the free energy (
1.8
) with nonlinear coefficient
a
, we recall the stationary problem corresponding to the evolution system
(
1.1
)-(
1.4
):

(1.10)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(a(u)\nabla u)+\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=\overline{\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)}\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u

\displaystyle=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

together with

\overline{u}=\overline{\phi_{0}},

where the notation
\overline{f}:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}f\,{\rm d}x
is the spatial average.
For
m\in(-1,1)
, we denote the set of the stationary states as

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{m}=

\displaystyle\big\{u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\text{ with }\Psi^{\prime}(u)\in L^{2}(\Omega):\ u\text{ solves }\eqref{SSS-i}-\eqref{SSS-ii}\text{ and }\overline{u}=m\big\}.

We show that

Theorem 1.1

(Lojasiewicz–Simon inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, be a bounded smooth domain. Assume that
a
is real analytic on the open interval
(-1,1)
. Then, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
, there exist
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
C_{L}>0
and

\beta>0
such that the following inequality holds

(1.13)

\displaystyle|E(u)-E(\psi)|^{1-\theta}\leq C_{L}\left\|-\mathrm{div}(a(u)\nabla u)+\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)-\overline{\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

for all
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
such that
\overline{u}=m
,
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
\|u-\psi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\beta
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the approach proposed in
[
30
]
, where the stationary points are interpreted as constrained critical points of the free energy on a manifold
\mathcal{M}
which accounts for the conservation of mass. It is important to point out that, on the contrary to the classical case with constant diffusion, it is not possible to work in the energy space
H^{1}(\Omega)
, but we rather need to consider the smaller space
H^{2}(\Omega)
, which requires an assumption on the vicinity of
u
to
\psi
in
H^{2}(\Omega)
. This is due to the fact that, in the
H^{1}(\Omega)
framework, the Frechet derivative
J^{\prime}
is not well defined due to the quadratic term arising from
a(\phi)
.

1.1.
Main results

For
m\in(-1,1)
, we introduce the space of admissible finite energy data

\mathcal{V}_{m}=\Big\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega):\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1\text{ and }\overline{f}=m\Big\},

and we study the evolution system (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) with initial datum in

In the two dimensional case, our results are collected in the following (some notation used in the statement will be specified in Subsection
2.1
below):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Assume that
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
.

(1)

Global weak solutions
: There exists a global weak solution
\phi
to (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) in the following sense:

(1.14)

\displaystyle\phi\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\mathcal{V}_{m})\cap L_{\operatorname{uloc}}^{4}([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)),

(1.15)

\displaystyle\phi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))\text{ such that }|\phi(x,t)|<1\ \text{a.e. in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi\in L^{2}(0,\infty;H_{(0)}^{-1}(\Omega)),

(1.17)

\displaystyle\mu\in L_{\operatorname{uloc}}^{2}([0,\infty);H^{1}(\Omega)),

and, for any

,

(1.18)

\phi\in L_{\operatorname{uloc}}^{2}([0,\infty);W^{2,p}(\Omega))\quad\text{and}\quad F^{\prime}(\phi)\in L_{\operatorname{uloc}}^{2}([0,\infty);L^{p}(\Omega)),

(1.19)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\phi,v\rangle+(b(\phi)\nabla\mu,\nabla v)=0,\quad\forall\,v\in H^{1}(\Omega),\ \text{a.e. in }(0,\infty),

Moreover,

almost everywhere

\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)
, and

\phi(\cdot,0)=\phi_{0}
in
\Omega
.

(2)

Uniqueness
: Assume that

. Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
be two weak solutions starting from

Propagation of regularity
: For any

2.
Mathematical setting

2.1.
Function spaces

For
X
Banach space, the set
X^{\prime}
denotes the dual space and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X^{\prime},X}
the duality product. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and an interval
I\subseteq[0,\infty)
,
L^{p}(I;X)
is the space of all Bochner
p-
integrable functions defined from
I
into
X
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the set
L^{p}_{\rm uloc}([0,\infty),X)
denotes the space of functions
f\in L^{p}(0,T;X)
for any
T>0
such that there exists
C>0
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}_{\rm uloc}([0,\infty),X)}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{t}^{t+1}\left\|f(s)\right\|_{X}^{p}\,{\rm d}s<\infty.

The set of continuous functions
f:I\to X
is denoted by
C(I;X)
.
The space
BC_{\rm w}(I;X)
consists of the topological vector space of
all
X
-valued bounded and weakly continuous functions (i.e., the map
t\in I\mapsto\langle\phi,f(t)\rangle_{X^{*},X}
is continuous for all
\phi\in X^{*}
).
Finally, the set
BUC(I;X)
is the subspace of all
X
-valued bounded and uniformly continuous functions with respect to the supremum norm.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
. For any positive real
k
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we denote by
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
the Sobolev space of function in
L^{p}(\Omega)
such that the distributional derivative of order up to
k
is an element of
L^{p}(\Omega)
. We use the notation
H^{k}(\Omega)
for the Hilbert space
W^{k,2}(\Omega)
with norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{k}(\Omega)}
. In particular, for
k=1
, we denote the duality product between
H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime}
and
H^{1}(\Omega)
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Given the definition of spatial average

\overline{f}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,{\rm d}x\quad\text{if }f\in L^{1}(\Omega),

for any
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we introduce the function space

H^{1}_{(m)}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega):\ \overline{f}=m\right\}\!.

If
m=0
, we recall the Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality

(2.1)

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{P}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\!,\quad\forall\,f\in H_{(0)}^{1}(\Omega),

where
C_{P}
only depends on
\Omega
. Thus,

H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space endowed with inner product
(f,g)_{H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}=(\nabla f,\nabla g)
and corresponding norm
\|f\|_{H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
Extending the definition of total mass as

\overline{f}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\langle f,1\rangle\quad\text{if }f\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime},

we define the Hilbert space

(2.2)

H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime}:\ \overline{f}=0\right\}\!,

with corresponding norm defined as
\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}:=\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime}}
.

We also report the generalized Poincaré inequality (see
[
35
, Chapter II, Section 1.4]
):
there exists a positive constant
C=C(\Omega,\eta_{\ast},\eta^{\ast})
such that

(2.3)

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\Big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\Big|\int_{\Omega}\eta u\,{\rm d}x\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where
\eta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is such that
0<\eta_{\ast}\leq\eta(x)\leq\eta^{\ast}
for almost every
x\in\Omega
.

Furthermore, the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities hold:

(2.4)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall\,u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega),\ 2\leq p\leq\infty,

which, in particular, gives

(2.5)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}},

Lastly, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad d=2,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad d=3.

2.2.
Elliptic problems.

We introduce the solution operator of the Laplace problem with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition
\mathcal{G}:H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
such that, for every
f\in H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{G}f\in H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
satisfies

(2.8)

(\nabla\mathcal{G}f,\nabla v)=\langle f,v\rangle,\quad\forall\,v\in H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega).

For any
f\in H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
,
\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is a norm on
H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
, that is equivalent to
\|f\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}
. Furthermore,

(2.9)

|\langle f,v\rangle|\leq\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\,f\in H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega),v\in H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega).

Next, assuming (
1.6
) and given a measurable function
q:\Omega\to[-1,1]
, we introduce the following elliptic problem

(2.10)

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(b(q)\nabla u)=f\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega\\
b(q)\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0\quad&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Similarly to the definition of
\mathcal{G}
in (
2.8
), we define the solution operator
\mathcal{G}_{q}:H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
as follows:
for every
f\in H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{G}_{q}f\in H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
such that

(2.11)

(b(q)\nabla\mathcal{G}_{q}f,\nabla v)=\langle f,v\rangle,\quad\forall\,v\in H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega).

It is immediate to see that

(2.12)

\sqrt{b_{m}}\left\|\sqrt{b(q)}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{q}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{b_{M}}\left\|\sqrt{b(q)}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{q}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\!,\quad\forall\,f\in H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega),

from which it follows that
\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{q}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is a norm on
H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
that is equivalent to
\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.

We now report some elliptic estimates related to problem (
2.10
). Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
. Assuming that
b\in C^{1}([-1,1])
and
q\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{q}f\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|\nabla q\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|q\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{q}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),\quad d=2.

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{q}f\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|q\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{q}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),\quad d=3.

More properties of
\mathcal{G}_{q}
can be found in
[
7
]
and
[
12
]
.

The following is a classical result in the theory of elliptic systems (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Lemma 4]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
d=2,3
. Assume that
K\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

0<\underline{K}\leq K(s)\leq\overline{K}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and let

\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function.
Consider the Neumann problem

(2.15)

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\,(K(\varphi)\nabla u)+u=f,\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0,\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If
\varphi\in W^{1,r}(\Omega)
, with
d<r\leq\infty
, and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then,
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
and
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Moreover, there exists a positive increasing function
Q
depending on
\underline{K}
,
\overline{K}
and
r
such that,

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Q(R)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,r}(\Omega)}\leq R
.

3.
The stationary system

Let
d=2,3
and let
m\in(-1,1)
be given. In this section we provide some regularity properties for the elements of
\mathcal{S}_{m}
.
To this aim, following
[
2
]
, it is convenient to introduce the integral function
A:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

(3.1)

A(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\sqrt{a(\tau)}\,{\rm d}\tau.

Since
\nabla A(u)=A^{\prime}(u)\nabla u=\sqrt{a(u)}\nabla u
, we notice that, for
u\in\mathcal{V}_{m}\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
,

(3.2)

-\sqrt{a(u)}\Delta A(u)=-\mathrm{div}(a(u)\nabla u)+a^{\prime}(u)\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{2},

and

(3.3)

\Delta u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(u)}}\Delta A(u)-\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2a^{2}(u)}|\nabla A(u)|^{2}.

In addition, we have

\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}A(u)=\sqrt{a(u)}\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty).

Accordingly, if
u\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
, then
u
solves the following quasilinear elliptic problem with logarithmic nonlinear term

(3.4)

\begin{cases}-\sqrt{a(u)}\Delta A(u)+F^{\prime}(u)=f,\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}A(u)=0,\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
f
is given by

f=\overline{\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)}+\theta_{0}u.

In the following, let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a generic function and assume that
u
is any solution to (
3.4
) in the following sense:
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
with
F^{\prime}(u)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, which satisfies (
3.4
) almost everywhere. In particular, we observe that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
. Then, the following result holds true.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d=2,3
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
2\leq p\leq\infty
.
Then, we have

\|F^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

for some positive constant
C
independent of
p
.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let us define the globally Lipschitz function
h_{k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(3.5)

h_{k}(s)=\begin{cases}-1+\frac{1}{k},&s<-1+\frac{1}{k},\\
s,&s\in[-1+\frac{1}{k},1-\frac{1}{k}],\\
1-\frac{1}{k},&s>1-\frac{1}{k},\end{cases}\quad\text{and }\quad u_{k}=h_{k}\circ u.

Since
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, the classical result on compositions in Sobolev spaces yields
u_{k}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and

\nabla u_{k}=\nabla u\cdot\chi_{[-1+\frac{1}{k},1-\frac{1}{k}]}(u)
, for any
k>0
.
Since
a(\cdot)>0
, we see from (
3.4
) that
u
satisfies

-\Delta A(u)+\frac{F^{\prime}(u)}{\sqrt{a(u)}}=\frac{f}{\sqrt{a(u)}}\quad\text{a.e in }\Omega,

where
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
by assumption.
Let
2\leq p<\infty
. Choosing as test function
|F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-2}F^{\prime}(u_{k})
, which belongs to
H^{1}(\Omega)
for any
k
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\Delta A(u)|F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-2}F^{\prime}(u_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{F^{\prime}(u)}{\sqrt{a(u)}}|F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-2}F^{\prime}(u_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f}{\sqrt{a(u)}}|F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-2}F^{\prime}(u_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
F^{\prime\prime}(u_{k})
is well-defined and positive, and
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}A(u)=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we learn that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}-\Delta A(u)|F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-2}F^{\prime}(u_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad=(p-1)\int_{\Omega}|F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-2}F^{\prime\prime}(u_{k})\nabla u\cdot\chi_{[-1+\frac{1}{k},1-\frac{1}{k}]}(u)\cdot\nabla A(u)\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

Then, recalling that
a_{m}\leq a(s)\leq a_{M}
for any
s\in[-1,1]
, we obtain

\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{M}}}\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime}(u)|F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-2}F^{\prime}(u_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{m}}}\int_{\Omega}|f||F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x.

By the Hölder inequality

\int_{\Omega}|f||F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|F^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

On the other side, by exploiting the monotonicity of
F^{\prime}
and the fact that
F^{\prime}(s)s\geq 0
, we have

\|F^{\prime}(u_{k})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime}(u)|F^{\prime}(u_{k})|^{p-2}F^{\prime}(u_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, we arrive at

\|F^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\sqrt{a_{M}}}{\sqrt{a_{m}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
, by Fatou’s lemma we end up with

\|F^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\sqrt{a_{M}}}{\sqrt{a_{m}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Finally, in the case when
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we infer from the above estimate that

\|F^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C,

where
C
is independent of
p
. Thus, passing to the limit as
p\to\infty
, the claim follows.
∎

We are now in the position to show the validity of the following crucial result.

Proposition 3.2
.

For each
u\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
, there exists a constant
\delta_{u}\in(0,1)
such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle|u(x)|\leq 1-\delta_{u},\quad\forall\,x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Moreover,
u\in H^{3}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We notice that
u
solves the elliptic problem (
3.4
) with
f
given by

f=\overline{\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)}+\theta_{0}u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).

Therefore, we learn by Lemma
3.1
that
F^{\prime}(u)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Since
F^{\prime}
diverges at
\pm 1
and

u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, we immediately deduce the existence of
\delta_{u}>0
such that

|u(x)|\leq 1-\delta_{u}
, for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

In order to establish further regularity, we rewrite (
3.4
) in the following form

(3.7)

\begin{cases}-\Delta A(u)+A(u)=g\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}A(u)=0\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where

g=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(u)}}(f-F^{\prime}(u))+A(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(u)}}\left(-\Psi^{\prime}(u)+\overline{\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)}\right)+A(u).

Since
u
is separated from
\pm 1
by (
3.6
), we learn that
\Psi^{\prime}(u)
and
\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)
are in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Therefore, since
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, we easily see that

g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Besides, since

\partial_{x_{i}}g=\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{a^{\frac{3}{2}}(u)}\left(\Psi^{\prime}(u)-\overline{\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)}\right)\partial_{x_{i}}u-\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(u)}}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\partial_{x_{i}}u+\sqrt{a(u)}\partial_{x_{i}}u,

we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{i}}g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{a^{\frac{3}{2}}(u)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(2\|\Psi^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2|\Omega|}\|a^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(u)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\Psi^{\prime\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\sqrt{a(u)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which entails that
g\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Hence, the classical elliptic regularity theory yields that
A(u)\in H^{3}(\Omega)
. Next,
on account of (
3.3
),
u
solves

(3.8)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+u=h\quad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0\quad&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where

h=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(u)}}\Delta A(u)+\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2a^{2}(u)}|\nabla A(u)|^{2}+u.

We notice that

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}h

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{a^{\frac{3}{2}}(u)}\Delta A(u)\partial_{x_{i}}u-\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(u)}}\partial_{x_{i}}\Delta A(u)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{a^{\prime\prime}(u)a(u)-2(a^{\prime}(u))^{2}}{a^{3}(u)}\right)\partial_{x_{i}}u|\nabla A(u)|^{2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{a^{2}(u)}\nabla A(u)\cdot\partial_{x_{i}}\nabla A(u)+\partial_{x_{i}}u.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{i}}h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2a^{\frac{3}{2}}(u)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\Delta A(u)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(u)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\Delta A(u)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\frac{a^{\prime\prime}(u)a(u)-2(a^{\prime}(u))^{2}}{2a^{3}(u)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla A(u)\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{a^{2}(u)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla A(u)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\nabla A(u)\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{x_{i}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{2a_{m}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|A(u)\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{m}}}\|A(u)\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{2a_{m}^{3}}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|A(u)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{C}{a_{m}^{2}}\|A(u)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|A(u)\|_{H^{3}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Since
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
and
A(u)\in H^{3}(\Omega)
, we obtain that
h\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, and thus
u\in H^{3}(\Omega)
as claimed.
∎

4.
A gradient Lojasiewicz-Simon inequality with nonlinear diffusion

In this section we demonstrate a gradient inequality of Lojasiewicz-Simon
type for the energy (
1.8
), namely we prove Theorem
1.1
. To this aim, we embed the problem in a more general setting as follows.

4.1.
A general result

We set
H=L^{2}(\Omega)
and we introduce the Hilbert space

V=\{u\in H^{2}(\Omega):\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}\subset H,

with continuous and dense embedding into
H
.
We consider the class of functionals
\mathcal{E}:V\to\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{E}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{a}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\widetilde{\Psi}(u)\,{\rm d}x,

where
\widetilde{a}
and
\widetilde{\Psi}
are two arbitrary regular functions such that

(4.1)

\widetilde{a},\widetilde{\Psi}\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R})\text{ are analytic on }\left(-1+\frac{\widetilde{\delta}}{2},1-\frac{\widetilde{\delta}}{2}\right)\!,\text{ for some $\widetilde{\delta}>0$.}

We preliminarily observe that, for any
u\in V
,

\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V}=\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{a}(u)\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\frac{\widetilde{a}^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}v+\widetilde{\Psi}^{\prime}(u)v\,{\rm d}x,\quad\forall\,v\in V.

Since
u\in V
, it is easily seen that

\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(u),v\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V}=\int_{\Omega}\left(-\mathrm{div}(\widetilde{a}(u)\nabla u)+\frac{\widetilde{a}^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\widetilde{\Psi}^{\prime}(u)\right)v\,{\rm d}x,\quad\forall\,v\in V.

Hence, for any
u\in V
,
\mathcal{E}
has an
H
-gradient (see, for instance,
[
30
, Definition 5.1]
) defined by

(4.2)

\nabla\mathcal{E}(u):=-\mathrm{div}(\widetilde{a}(u)\nabla u)+\frac{\widetilde{a}^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\widetilde{\Psi}^{\prime}(u)\in H.

Finally, we introduce the set

U=\left\{u\in V:-1+\widetilde{\delta}<u(x)<1-\widetilde{\delta},\quad\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}\right\}

which is an open subset of
V
, due to the continuous embedding
V\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
d=2,3
. Assume that (
4.1
) hold.
Let
u^{\star}\in U\cap H^{3}(\Omega)
with
\overline{u^{\star}}=m
with
m\in(-1,1)
such that

(4.3)

(\nabla\mathcal{E}(u^{\star}),v)=0,\quad\forall\,v\in H\text{ with }\overline{v}=0.

Then, there exist
\theta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
,
C>0
,
\beta>0
such that

(4.4)

|\mathcal{E}(u)-\mathcal{E}(u^{\star})|^{1-\theta}\leq C\left\|\nabla\mathcal{E}(u)-\overline{\nabla\mathcal{E}(u)}\right\|_{H}

for all
u\in U
such that
\overline{u}=m
and

\|u-u^{\star}\|_{V}\leq\beta
.

The strategy of the proof consists in interpreting
u^{\star}
as a constrained critical point of
\mathcal{E}
on a manifold
\mathcal{M}
defined as
the nodal set of a suitable map
\mathcal{G}:V\to\mathbb{R}
, see (
4.6
) below, accounting for the conservation of mass of the model. Accordingly,
hinging upon the abstract result
[
30
, Corollary 5.2]
(see also
[
9
]
), we will obtain a refined Lojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality, that will yield the claimed inequality (
4.4
).

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We proceed by recasting our problem in the framework of
[
30
, Corollary 5.2]
throughout the subsequent three steps.

Step 1. Analyticity of
\mathcal{E}
e
\nabla\mathcal{E}
on
U
.

We will make use of the following result on the analyticity of superposition functions.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
f:(-1+\frac{\widetilde{\delta}}{2},1-\frac{\widetilde{\delta}}{2})\to\mathbb{R}
is analytic, then the map

\mathcal{F}:U\to C(\overline{\Omega}),\qquad u\mapsto f(u)

is analytic.

Proof.

We argue as in the proof of
[
9
, Corollary 4.6]
. We preliminarily observe that
f
is uniformly analytic in
J=(-1+\widetilde{\delta},1-\widetilde{\delta})
. This means that there exists
0<\rho<\frac{\widetilde{\delta}}{2}
such that the power series expansion of
f
near any given
\widehat{s}\in J

(4.5)

f(s)=\sum_{n}\alpha_{n}(\widehat{s})(s-\widehat{s})^{n},\qquad\alpha_{n}(\widehat{s})\in\mathbb{R},

converges absolutely for every
s\in(-1,1):|s-\widehat{s}|<\rho
.
Let
\mathcal{U}:=\{u\in C(\overline{\Omega}):u(x)\in J,\,\forall\,x\in\overline{\Omega}\}
,
noticing that
\mathcal{U}
is an open set of
C(\overline{\Omega})
, and
fix
\widehat{u}\in\mathcal{U}
. Defining the ball
B_{\rho}(\widehat{u})=\{v\in C(\overline{\Omega}):\|v-\widehat{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\rho\}
, we learn from (
4.5
) that

f(u)=\sum_{n}a_{n}(u-\widehat{u})^{n}\quad\text{in}\quad C(\overline{\Omega}),

for any
u\in B_{\rho}(\widehat{u})
, where the multilinear forms
a_{n}:[C(\overline{\Omega})]^{n}\to C(\overline{\Omega})
and
a_{n}(u-\widehat{u})^{n}:=a_{n}(u-\widehat{u},\dots,u-\widehat{u})
by the rule
x\mapsto\alpha_{n}(\widehat{u}(x))(u(x)-\widehat{u}(x))^{n}
, for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Accordingly, since
\widehat{u}
is arbitrary in
\mathcal{U}
, the map

\mathcal{U}\to C(\overline{\Omega}),\qquad u\mapsto f(u)

is analytic.
In order to prove that
\mathcal{F}
is analytic as a map from
U
to
C(\overline{\Omega})
, we rely on the continuous embedding
U\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
. Since the inclusion
i:U\to C(\overline{\Omega})
is analytic,
i(U)\subset\mathcal{U}
and the composition of analytic maps is analytic (see e.g.
[
30
, Theorem 2.2]
), the conclusion immediately follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove the required analyticity of
\mathcal{E}
and
\nabla\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The functional
\mathcal{E}
is analytic on
U
.

Proof.

The following properties hold:

(M1)

the pointwise multiplications
C(\overline{\Omega})\times L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega):(f,g)\mapsto fg
is analytic;

(M2)

The map
L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}:f\mapsto\int_{\Omega}f\,{\rm d}x
is analytic;

(M3)

The map
H^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega):u\mapsto\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u
, for any
i,j
, is analytic;

(M4)

The map
H^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega):u\mapsto|\nabla u|^{2}
is analytic.

Indeed, (M1)-(M3) follow as the maps are (multi)linear and bounded (see e.g.
[
30
, Example 2.3]
). As to (M4), the map is analytic since it is the diagonal of the bounded bilinear map
a:H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
such that
a(u,v)=\nabla u\cdot\nabla v
.

Now, by (
4.1
) and Lemma
4.2
, the map
U\to C(\overline{\Omega}):u\mapsto\widetilde{a}(u)
is analytic.
Since the pointwise multiplication
C(\overline{\Omega})\times L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is analytic (see (M1)), the map
U\to L^{2}(\Omega):u\mapsto\frac{\widetilde{a}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}

is analytic in light of (M4). Then, as the composition of analytic maps is analytic, we deduce from (M2) that

U\to\mathbb{R}:\ u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{a}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\text{ is analytic.}

Analogously, the analyticity of the map
U\to\mathbb{R}:\ u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\Psi}(u)\,{\rm d}x
easily follows. Hence, we obtain the desired claim.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The map
\nabla\mathcal{E}:U\to L^{2}(\Omega)

is analytic.

Proof.

By standard computations and (
4.2
), we have

\nabla\mathcal{E}(u)=-\widetilde{a}(u)\Delta u-\frac{\widetilde{a}^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\widetilde{\Psi}^{\prime}(u)\in H.

Hence, by arguing as in Lemma
4.3
for the analyticity of
\mathcal{E}
, we immediately learn that the map

U\to L^{2}(\Omega):u\mapsto-\frac{\widetilde{a}^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\widetilde{\Psi}^{\prime}(u)

is analytic.
Finally,
the map
U\to L^{2}(\Omega):u\mapsto\widetilde{a}(u)\Delta u
is analytic by (M1) and (M3).
∎

Step 2.
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u^{\star})
is a Fredholm operator of index zero on
V
.

First of all, for
u\in U
, the second derivative
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u)=(\nabla\mathcal{E})^{\prime}(u)
is such that

\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}(u)v,z\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\widetilde{a}^{\prime\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}vz+\widetilde{a}^{\prime}(u)v\nabla u\cdot\nabla z+\widetilde{a}^{\prime}(u)z\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\widetilde{a}(u)\nabla v\cdot\nabla z+\widetilde{\Psi}^{\prime\prime}(u)vz\,{\rm d}x,

for any
v,z\in V
. We notice that

\int_{\Omega}|\Delta A(\phi)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq C\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C.

Since

\displaystyle\leq C\|A(\phi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

Finally, we conclude that

\phi\in L_{\rm uloc}^{4}([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega)).

-estimate of the potential.

6.
Uniqueness of weak solutions in two dimensions

We consider two weak solutions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) originating from two initial data
\phi_{1}^{0}
and
\phi_{2}^{0}
such that
\overline{\phi_{1}^{0}}=\overline{\phi_{2}^{0}}
.
Following the notation in
[
12
]
, we notice that (
1.19
) and (
1.20
) entail that

(6.1)

\mu_{i}-\overline{\mu_{i}}=-\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{i}}\partial_{t}\phi_{i},\quad\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\ i=1,2,

where
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are the chemical potentials corresponding to
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, respectively.
Setting
\Phi=\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}
, we find from (
1.2
) that

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\left(a(\phi_{1})\nabla\Phi\right)-\mathrm{div}\left((a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2}))\nabla\phi_{2}\right)+\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})}{2}\left(|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-a^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-\Psi^{\prime}(\phi_{2})=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2},\quad\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\times(0,\infty).

Multiplying the above equation by
\Phi
and integrating over
\Omega
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(\phi_{1})|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left(F^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\left(a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2})\right)\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})}{2}\left(|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-a^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

In light of (
6.1
), since
\overline{\Phi}\equiv 0
by the conservation of mass, the third term on the left-hand side is

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{2}}\partial_{t}\phi_{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\partial_{t}\Phi\,\Phi\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}-\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{2}}\right)\partial_{t}\phi_{2}\,\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

We know from
[
12
, eq. (3.16)]
that the following integration-by-parts formula holds in the class of weak solutions

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\partial_{t}\Phi\,\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathcal{G}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\cdot b^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{1})\nabla\phi_{1}\left|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(6.2)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathcal{G}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\cdot b^{\prime}(\phi_{1})\left(D^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right)\,{\rm d}x,

almost everywhere in
(0,\infty)
, where
D^{2}f
is the Hessian of
f
.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}-\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{2}}\right)\partial_{t}\phi_{2}\,\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{t}\phi_{2},\left(\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}-\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{2}}\right)\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}b(\phi_{2})\nabla\mu_{2}\cdot\nabla\left(\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}-\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{2}}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

(6.3)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}(b(\phi_{2})-b(\phi_{1}))\nabla\mu_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

Thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathcal{G}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\cdot b^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{1})\nabla\phi_{1}\left|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathcal{G}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\cdot b^{\prime}(\phi_{1})\left(D^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right)\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}(b(\phi_{2})-b(\phi_{1}))\nabla\mu_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

Therefore, we end up with the differential equality

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}a(\phi_{1})|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left(F^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\left(a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2})\right)\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})}{2}\left(|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-a^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathcal{G}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\cdot b^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{1})\nabla\phi_{1}\left|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathcal{G}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\cdot b^{\prime}(\phi_{1})\left(D^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right)\,{\rm d}x

(6.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}(b(\phi_{2})-b(\phi_{1}))\nabla\mu_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

Let us now proceed by estimating the terms on the right-hand side.
First, the first term on the right-hand side is simply estimated by

\theta_{0}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\left\|\nabla\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left\|\Phi\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}\!,

The terms containing the nonlinear diffusion
a
can be controlled as follows.
By (
1.5
), (
2.6
) and the Hilbert interpolation, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}\left(a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2})\right)\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2})\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\phi_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Phi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\phi_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{7}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})}{2}\left(|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})}{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\|\nabla\phi_{1}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\phi_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\right)\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Phi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}})\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{\frac{7}{4}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C(\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4})\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-a^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-a^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\phi_{2}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\Phi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Phi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The remaining terms

(6.5)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathcal{G}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\cdot b^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{1})\nabla\phi_{1}\left|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

(6.6)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathcal{G}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\cdot b^{\prime}(\phi_{1})\left(D^{2}\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right)\,{\rm d}x,

(6.7)

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(b(\phi_{1})-b(\phi_{2}))\nabla\mu_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x

require a careful control based on higher order estimates for the operator
\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi
. This has been done in
[
12
, Section 3]
based on suitable controls of

\|\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
and of
\|\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{W^{2,4}(\Omega)}
in terms of
\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}
,
\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
(that is equivalent to
\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}}
), coming from the elliptic regularity theory.
Based on
[
12
, Eqn. (3.46)]
, there holds

\|\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|\nabla\phi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\!.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}\left\|b^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{1})\nabla\phi_{1}|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\!

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\phi_{1}\right\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\phi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{3}}\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\right)\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}\!.

Concerning
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, we report the final results from
[
12
, Eqns. (3.50) and (3.51)]

|I_{2}|\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\left\|\nabla\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\right)\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}\!,

and

|I_{3}|\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(\left\|\nabla\mu_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\right)\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}\!.

We now collect all the estimates above in (
6.4
). Observing that
(F^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\phi_{2}),\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})\geq 0
and that
a(\phi_{1})\geq a_{m}>0
, recalling that

\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}
is equivalent to
\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, we arrive at the following differential inequality

(6.8)

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+a_{m}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq h(t)\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\!,

where

h(\cdot)=C\Big(1+\left\|\nabla\mu_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\phi_{1}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\|\phi_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\Big)\in L^{1}(0,T),\quad\forall\,T>0.

A straightforward application of the Gronwall lemma entails that

(6.9)

\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\left(\phi_{1}(t)-\phi_{2}(t)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\left(\phi_{1}^{0}-\phi_{2}^{0}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\int_{0}^{t}h(s)\,{\rm d}s},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0,

which implies (
1.21
) by the equivalence of the norms in
H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
. Thus, if
\phi_{1}^{0}=\phi_{2}^{0}
, the uniqueness of the weak solutions immediately follows. The proof of part (3) of Theorem
1.2
is done.

7.1.
Global regularity

Below, we carry out formal estimates, which can be easily performed in the approximation scheme exploited in
[
32
]
. Multiplying (
1.1
) by
\partial_{t}\mu
and integrating over

, we find

(7.1)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}b(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}b^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{t}\mu\,{\rm d}x=0.

By exploiting (
5.13
), we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{t}\mu\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\left(-\sqrt{a(\phi)}\Delta A(\phi)\right)\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\theta_{0}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\Delta A(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{a(\phi)}\Delta\partial_{t}A(\phi)\,\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\theta_{0}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\Delta A(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial_{t}A(\phi)\cdot\nabla\left(\sqrt{a(\phi)}\partial_{t}\phi\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\theta_{0}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\Delta A(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\left(\sqrt{a(\phi)}\partial_{t}\phi\right)\Big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\theta_{0}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

By a direct calculation, we notice that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\left(\sqrt{a(\phi)}\partial_{t}\phi\right)\Big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\nabla\phi\,\partial_{t}\phi+\sqrt{a(\phi)}\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right)\cdot\left(\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\nabla\phi\,\partial_{t}\phi+\sqrt{a(\phi)}\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a(\phi)|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(a^{\prime}(\phi))^{2}}{4a(\phi)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}a^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x.

Collecting the above calculations all together, we end up with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}b(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}a(\phi)|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(a^{\prime}(\phi))^{2}}{4a(\phi)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}b^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\Delta A(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(7.2)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}a^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x.

We proceed with the control of the nonlinear terms.
First, by interpolation in Hilbert spaces and (
5.11
), we know that

\theta_{0}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{a_{m}}{4}\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Reasoning as in
[
12
]
(see, in particular, Eqns. (4.26)-(4.27) and the subsequent estimates therein), we have

(7.3)

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}b^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right|&\leq\frac{a_{m}}{4}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.\end{split}

Next, we consider

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\Delta A(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\Delta A(\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta A(\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{8}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|A(\phi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Similarly, by (
2.6
) and (
5.7
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}a^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|a^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{7}{4}}\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{8}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Hence, recalling that (
5.19
) and (
5.21
), we arrive at

(7.4)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\int_{\Omega}b(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)+\frac{a_{m}}{4}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\left(1+\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\int_{\Omega}b(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

In order to derive a global control independent of time, we recall that, due to (
5.18
),

\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{t}^{t+1}\int_{\Omega}b(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}s\leq C_{0},\quad\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{t}^{t+1}C\left(1+\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}s\leq C_{1},

where
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
are two positive constant depending on
E(\phi_{0})
,
m
and the parameters of the system.
By applying the uniform Gronwall lemma (see
[
35
, Chapter III, Lemma
1.1]
),
we derive that

(7.5)

\sup_{t\geq\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mu(t)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq\frac{C_{0}}{b_{m}\tau}\,{\rm exp}(C_{1}).

In the sequel, we denote by
C(\tau)
a generic constant depending on the parameters of the system, the initial energy
E(\phi_{0})
, and
\tau>0
.
Thanks to (
5.17
), it immediately follows from (
7.5
) that

(7.6)

\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C(\tau).

As a consequence, we learn from (
5.11
) that

(7.7)

\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega))}\leq C(\tau).

Now, we integrate (
7.4
) on the time interval
[t,t+1]
with
t\geq\tau
. Thanks to (
5.8
) and (
7.6
), we infer that

(7.8)

\sup_{t\geq\tau}\int_{t}^{t+1}\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}s\leq C(\tau),

which, by the conservation of mass,
gives

\partial_{t}\phi\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{uloc}}([\tau,\infty);H^{1}_{(0)}(\Omega)).

By (
5.23
), (
5.26
) and (
5.27
), we learn that

(7.9)

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;W^{2,p}(\Omega))}+\|F^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;L^{p}(\Omega))}\leq C(\tau,p),\quad\forall\,p\in[2,\infty).

Next, we prove the separation property (
1.23
) following
[
13
]
and
[
24
]
. To this end, we first observe that
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;W^{2,6}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}_{\rm uloc}(\tau,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega}\times[\tau;T])
, for any
T\geq\tau
, by standard interpolation. We claim that

(7.10)

\|F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;L^{p}(\Omega))}\leq C\left(\tau,p\right)\!,\quad\forall\,p\in[2,\infty).

In fact, recalling that
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\sqrt{p}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
for
2\leq p<\infty
, we learn from (
5.23
) that

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\geq\tau}\|F^{\prime}(\phi(t))\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\sqrt{p}\|\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))}\right)\leq C(\tau)(1+\sqrt{p}),

where the constant
C(\tau)
is independent of
p
. Since
F^{\prime\prime}(s)\leq C\mathrm{e}^{C|F^{\prime}(s)|}
for all
s\in(-1,1)
, by arguing as in
[
19
, pg. 2279–2281]
, we obtain the desired conclusion (
7.10
).
Now, by
[
24
, Lemma 3.2]
,
\partial_{x_{i}}F^{\prime}(\phi)=F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\partial_{x_{i}}\phi
in the sense of distributions (and thus almost everywhere).
As an immediate consequence, we derive that

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\geq\tau}\|F^{\prime}(\phi(t))\|_{W^{1,3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|F^{\prime}(\phi(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;L^{3}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\quad+\|F^{\prime\prime}(\phi(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;L^{6}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\phi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;L^{6}(\Omega))}\leq C(\tau).

As a consequence, we obtain that
\|F^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(\tau,\infty))}\leq C(\tau)
.
Then, we deduce from
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\pm 1}|F^{\prime}(s)|=\infty
that there exists
\delta>0
such that (
1.23
) holds.

We now go back to the elliptic problem (
5.25
) for
A(\phi)
with right-hand side given by

f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\left(\mu-\Psi^{\prime}(\phi)\right)+A(\phi).

It is immediate to observe that
f\in L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
. Moreover, we have

\partial_{x_{i}}f=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{a^{\frac{3}{2}}(\phi)}\left(\mu-\Psi^{\prime}(\phi)\right)\partial_{x_{i}}\phi+\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\left(\partial_{x_{i}}\mu-\Psi^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\right)+\sqrt{a(\phi)}\partial_{x_{i}}\phi.

By exploiting (
1.5
), we immediately obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x_{i}}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2a^{\frac{3}{2}}(\phi)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mu-\Psi^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

(7.11)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\Psi^{\prime\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\sqrt{a(\phi)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence, by (
7.6
), (
7.9
) and (
7.10
), we deduce that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C(\tau),

and by the elliptic regularity theory for (
5.25
), we get

(7.12)

\|A(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;H^{3}(\Omega))}\leq C(\tau).

Finally, since
\phi
solves the elliptic problem (
3.8
) with
u=\phi
, we deduce from (
3.9
)-(
3.10
), (
7.9
) and (
7.12
) that

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;H^{3}(\Omega))}\leq C(\tau).

7.2.
Energy equality

Let
\phi
be the weak solution originating from
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
. For any
\tau>0
, we recall that
\phi
satisfies the properties (
1.22
)-(
1.24
). Thus, an application of
[
32
, Lemma 2.6]
entails that

(7.13)

E(\phi(t))+\int_{\tau}^{t}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi(s))}\nabla\mu(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}s=E(\phi(\tau)),\quad\forall\,t\geq\tau,

for any
\tau>0
. In order to prove the claimed energy equality (
1.25
), we are left to show the validity of (
7.13
) for
\tau=0
.
To this aim, we first observe that the gradient part of the energy can be rewritten in terms of
A
, namely, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{m}

(7.14)

E(\phi)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla A(\phi)|^{2}+\Psi(\phi)\,{\rm d}x.

Besides, since
\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi(s))}\nabla\mu(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
, we can pass to the limit as
\tau\to 0
in (
7.13
),
which leads to

(7.15)

E(\phi(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi(s))}\nabla\mu(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}s=\lim_{\tau\to 0}E(\phi(\tau)),\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Owing to the energy inequality (
5.5
), we thus deduce that

(7.16)

\lim_{\tau\to 0}E(\phi(\tau))\leq E(\phi_{0}).

On the other hand, it follows from (
1.14
) and (
1.16
)
that
\phi\in BC_{\rm w}([0,\infty);H^{1}(\Omega))\cap C([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
. Then, we have

(7.17)

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\|\phi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and

(7.18)

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\|A(\phi(\tau))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|A(\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

By the convexity of
F
, it also follows that

(7.19)

\liminf_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\Omega}F(\phi(\tau))\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}F(\phi_{0})\,{\rm d}x.

In addition, recalling that
A(\phi)\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))
, by the lower-semicontinuity of the norm and (
7.18
),
we derive that there exists a sequence
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
such that
\tau_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and

(7.20)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla A(\phi(\tau_{n}))|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla A(\phi_{0})|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

By collecting (
7.14
) and (
7.17
)-(
7.20
) together,
we obtain

(7.21)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}E(\phi(\tau_{n}))\geq E(\phi_{0}).

Thus, in light of (
7.16
) and (
7.21
), we conclude that

(7.22)

\lim_{\tau\to 0}E(\phi(\tau))=E(\phi_{0}),

which, by (
7.15
), proves the energy identity (
1.25
).

Let us now focus on the continuity properties of
\phi
with respect to time. By (
1.25
), we immediately have that
E(\phi(\cdot))\in AC([0,T])
, for any
T>0
. Hence, for any
t,s\in[0,T]
we find that

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to s}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla A(\phi(t))|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to s}\left(E(\phi(t))-\int\Psi(\phi(t))\,{\rm d}x\right)=E(\phi(s))-\liminf_{t\to s}\int\Psi(\phi(t))\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq E(\phi(s))-\int\Psi(\phi(s))\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla A(\phi(s))|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Therefore, since

\liminf_{t\to s}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla A(\phi(t))|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla A(\phi(s))|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

we conclude that

(7.23)

\|\nabla A(\phi(\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\in C([0,T]),\quad\forall\,T\geq 0.

By the equality

\displaystyle\nabla\phi(t)-\nabla\phi(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi(t))}}\nabla A(\phi(t))-\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi(s))}}\nabla A(\phi(s)),

adding and subtracting the term
\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi(t))}}\nabla A(\phi(s))
,
we infer that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\phi(t)-\nabla\phi(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{a_{m}}\left\|\nabla A(\phi(t))-\nabla A(\phi(s))\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi(t))}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi(s))}}\right)^{2}|\nabla A(\phi(s))|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Now notice that, if we take any sequence
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
such that
\tau_{n}\to s
as
n\to\infty
, by the dominated convergence theorem,
the second term on the right-hand
side converges to 0 as
n\to\infty
. Indeed, since
\phi\in C([0,\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))
, we have

f_{n}:=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi(\tau_{n}))}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{a(\phi(s))}}\right)^{2}|\nabla A(\phi(s))|^{2}\to 0\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega,

as
n\to\infty
, and
|f_{n}|\leq\frac{2}{a_{m}}|\nabla A(\phi(s))|^{2}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
At this point we easily conclude from (
7.23
) that

\lim_{t\to s}\left\|\nabla\phi(t)-\nabla\phi(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0,

yielding the conclusion
\phi\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}(\Omega))
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
, part (4), is now completed.

8.
Convergence to equilibrium in two dimensions

This section is devoted to characterizing the longtime behavior of any solution in two dimensions. Let
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
and let
\phi(t)
be the corresponding global weak solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.4
). Fixed
\tau>0
, since
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;H^{3}(\Omega))
and
\partial_{t}\phi\in L^{2}(0,\infty;H_{(0)}^{-1}(\Omega))
, it immediately follows that
\phi\in BC_{\rm w}([\tau,\infty);H^{3}(\Omega))\cap BUC([\tau,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega))
.
Then, the
\omega
-limit set, defined by

\omega(\phi_{0})=\Big\{\phi^{\prime}\in H^{3}(\Omega):\ \exists\,t_{n}\to\infty\text{ such that }\phi(t_{n})\to\phi^{\prime}\text{ in }H^{2}(\Omega)\Big\},

is non-empty and compact.

Lemma 8.1
.

For any
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
, we have
\omega(\phi_{0})\subset\mathcal{S}_{m}.

Proof.

We introduce some terminology from the theory of dynamical systems, see e.g.
[
11
, Chapter 9]
. According to Theorem
1.2
, we associate problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) with the solution map

S(t):\mathcal{V}_{m}\to\mathcal{V}_{m},\quad z_{0}\mapsto S(t)z_{0}=z(t),\forall\,t\geq 0,

where
z(t)
is the unique weak solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) departing from
z_{0}
. This is a one-parameter family of maps satisfying
S(0)z_{0}=z_{0}
for any
z_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
and the concatenation rule

S(t+s)=S(t)S(s)
, for every
t,s\geq 0
.
In addition,owing to the energy inequality (
5.5
), the following properties hold:

(i)

E(S(t)z_{0})\leq E(z_{0})
, for any
t\geq 0
,
\forall\,z_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
;

(ii)

E(S(t)z_{0})=E(z_{0})
for any
t\geq 0
implies that
z_{0}
is a stationary point of the map
S(t)
, namely
S(t)z_{0}=z_{0}
,
\forall\,t\geq 0
, which means
z\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
(see Section
3
).

Besides, the continuous dependence estimate (
1.21
) and the regularity
BC_{\rm w}([\tau,\infty);H^{3}(\Omega))
, for any
\tau>0
, entail by interpolation that
S(t)\in C(H^{2}(\Omega);H^{2}(\Omega))
, for any
t>0
.

We are now ready to prove the result. To this end, we observe that
\phi(t)=S(t)\phi_{0}
. Since the map
t\mapsto E(\phi(t))
is decreasing by (i) and bounded from below, there exists
E_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(8.1)

E_{\infty}=\lim_{t\to\infty}E(\phi(t)).

Next, we claim that

(8.2)

\quad E(\phi^{\prime})=E_{\infty},\ \forall\,\phi^{\prime}\in\omega(\phi_{0}).

Indeed, by the separation property (
1.23
) of the solution
\phi
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1-\delta,\quad\forall\,t\geq 1.

By the embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
, we deduce that

\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1-\delta,\quad\forall\,\phi^{\prime}\in\omega(\phi_{0}).

Therefore, for any
\phi^{\prime}\in\omega(\phi_{0})
and
t\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(\phi(t))-E(\phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}a(\phi(t))(|\nabla\phi(t)|^{2}-|\nabla\phi^{\prime}|^{2})\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(a(\phi(t))-a(\phi^{\prime}))|\nabla\phi^{\prime}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\Psi(\phi(t))-\Psi(\phi^{\prime})\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{M}}{2}\|\nabla(\phi(t)+\phi^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla(\phi(t)-\phi^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\max_{s\in[-1,1]}|a^{\prime}(s)|\|\phi(t)-\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\max_{s\in[-1+\delta,1-\delta]}|\Psi^{\prime}(s)|\|\phi(t)-\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi(t)-\phi^{\prime}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since
\phi^{\prime}\in\omega(\phi_{0})
, there exists
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
\phi(t_{n})\to\phi^{\prime}
in
H^{2}(\Omega)
. Thus, exploiting the above inequality, we have

0\leq E(\phi(t_{n}))-E(\phi^{\prime})\leq\gamma\|\phi(t_{n})-\phi^{\prime}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

which yields

E(\phi^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to\infty}E(\phi(t_{n})).

Hence, in light of (
8.1
), we finally derive that
E(\phi^{\prime})=E_{\infty}
, as claimed.

Let now
\phi^{\prime}\in\omega(\phi_{0})
be fixed and let

t_{n}\to\infty
be such that
S(t_{n})\phi_{0}\to\phi^{\prime}
in
H^{2}(\Omega)
.
Setting
\tau_{n}=t+t_{n}
, where
t>0
is arbitrarily fixed, by the concatenation rule and the
H^{2}
-continuity of the map
S(t)
,
we obtain

S(\tau_{n})\phi_{0}=S(t)S(t_{n})\phi_{0}\to S(t)\phi^{\prime}\quad\text{in }H^{2}(\Omega),

namely

S(t)\phi^{\prime}\in\omega(\phi_{0}),\quad\forall\,t>0.

Owing to (
8.2
), we thus learn that

E(S(t)\phi^{\prime})=E_{\infty}=E(\phi^{\prime}),\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

At this point, we exploit property (ii) to conclude that
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
. By the arbitrariness of
\phi^{\prime}\in\omega(\phi_{0})
, the proof is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove the following result, which implies the claim of part (5) of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 8.2
.

For any
\phi_{0}\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
, the set
\omega(\phi_{0})
is a singleton.

Proof.

Let
\phi^{\prime}\in\omega(\phi_{0})
. Since
\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
, the Lojasiewicz–Simon inequality in Theorem
1.1
holds true at
\phi^{\prime}
, yielding
constants
\theta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
C>0
and

\beta>0
such that

|E(u)-E(\phi^{\prime})|^{1-\theta}\leq C_{L}\left\|-\mathrm{div}(a(u)\nabla u)+\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)-\overline{\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

for all
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
such that
\overline{u}=m
,
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
\|u-\phi^{\prime}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\beta
.

Exploiting the fact that
\omega(\phi_{0})
is a compact subset of
H^{2}(\Omega)
, we can cover
\omega(\phi_{0})
by finitely many open balls of
H^{2}(\Omega)
, denoted by
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset H^{2}(\Omega)
, centered at
\phi^{\prime}_{i}\in\omega(\phi_{0})
and with radii
\beta_{i}
,
prescribed by Theorem
1.1
for
\phi^{\prime}_{i}
. Since
E(\phi^{\prime}_{i})=E_{\infty}
for any
i
,
we conclude that there exist universal constants
\widetilde{\theta}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
and
\widetilde{C}>0
such that

(8.3)

|E(u)-E_{\infty}|^{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\leq\widetilde{C}\left\|-\mathrm{div}(a(u)\nabla u)+\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)-\overline{\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(u)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

for all
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
such that
\overline{u}=m
,
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}u=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
u\in\mathcal{B}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B_{i}
.

Now, let
\phi(t)
be the solution to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
). Recalling that
\mathrm{d}_{H^{2}(\Omega)}(\phi(t),\omega(\phi_{0}))\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, there exists
t^{\star}
such that

\phi(t)\in\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{V}_{m}
for all
t\geq t^{\star}
. As a result, we can apply (
8.3
) with
u=\phi(t)
, for all
t\geq t^{\star}
. In which case, using the definition of
\mu
in (
1.2
)

\mu=-\mathrm{div}(a(\phi)\nabla\phi)+\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(\phi),

we obtain from (
8.3
) the following inequality

(8.4)

|E(\phi(t))-E_{\infty}|^{1-\widetilde{\theta}}\leq\widetilde{C}\left\|\mu(t)-\overline{\mu(t)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\!\!,\quad\forall\,t\geq t^{\star}.

Let us define the function
H:[t^{\star},\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

H(t):=\left(E(\phi(t))-E_{\infty}\right)^{\widetilde{\theta}}\!.

By the regularity of the solution (
1.22
)-(
1.24
), an application of
[
32
, Lemma 2.6]
, together with the standard chain rule in Bochner spaces, gives

(8.5)

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}E(\phi)+\int_{\Omega}b(\phi)|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=0,\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in(1,\infty).

By exploiting (
2.1
), (
8.4
) and (
8.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}H(t)

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{\theta}\left(E(\phi(t))-E_{\infty}\right)^{\widetilde{\theta}-1}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}E(\phi(t))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\widetilde{\theta}}{\widetilde{C}}\frac{\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi(t))}\nabla\mu(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{\left\|\mu(t)-\overline{\mu(t)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\geq\frac{\widetilde{\theta}}{C_{P}}\frac{b_{m}}{\widetilde{C}}\|\nabla\mu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\text{a.e. in }(t^{\star},\infty).

Now, the conclusion follows in a standard way. Integrating from
t^{\star}
to
\infty
, and observing that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}H(t)=0
, we infer that

\int_{t^{\star}}^{\infty}\|\nabla\mu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,{\rm d}t\leq\frac{C_{P}\,\widetilde{C}}{\widetilde{\theta}b_{m}}H(t^{\star}),

which gives that
\nabla\mu\in L^{1}(t^{\star},\infty;L^{2}(\Omega))
. Hence, by (
5.11
), we have that
\partial_{t}\phi\in L^{1}(t^{\star},\infty;H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega))
. Since

\phi(t)=\phi(t^{\star})+\int_{t^{\star}}^{t}\partial_{t}\phi(\tau)\,{\rm d}\tau,\quad\forall\,t\geq t^{\star}\!,

we conclude that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi(t)
exists in
H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)
.
Therefore,
\omega(\phi_{0})=\{\phi_{\infty}\}
, where
\phi_{\infty}=\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi(t)
.
∎

9.
Local strong solutions in three dimensions

In this section, we study the existence and uniqueness of local strong solutions in three dimensions. We will prove the following result, which provides part (2) of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
\phi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
be such that
\overline{\phi_{0}}=m\in(-1,1)
,
\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}\phi_{0}=0
on
\partial\Omega
and

(9.1)

and

to (

)-(

)-(

Furthermore, we have the following estimates

In addition, if

\widetilde{T_{M}}\in(0,T_{M}]

, but independent of the specific initial datum, such that

Proof.

First of all, we collect the basic energy estimates provided in Section
5
. Denoting by
C>0
a generic constant only depending on
E(\phi_{0})
and
m
, the following estimates hold true:

(9.7)

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C,\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla\mu(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\leq C.

Besides,

(9.8)

\|\mu\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C,

and

(9.9)

\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|A(\phi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C,

as well as

(9.10)

\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We now perform higher-order energy estimates by relying on the assumption (
9.1
). As in Section
7
, this argument can be rigorously justified within the approximation procedure devised in
[
32
]
(see also
[
21
, Section 4]
for the approximation of the initial datum).
By reasoning as in the two dimensional case (cf. (
7.1
)), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega}b(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}a(\phi)|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(a^{\prime}(\phi))^{2}}{4a(\phi)}|\nabla\phi|^{2}|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime\prime}(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}b^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\Delta A(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

(9.11)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}a^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x.

We control the last two terms on the right hand side. By exploiting (
2.7
), (
9.9
) and (
9.10
), we find

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\Delta A(\phi)|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi)}{2\sqrt{a(\phi)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\Delta A(\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Delta A(\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{7}{4}}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{12}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|A(\phi)\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{8}\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{12}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(1+\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\right)\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Similarly, we also obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}a^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\,\partial_{t}\phi\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|a^{\prime}(\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{7}{4}}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{12}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{8}\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{12}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(1+\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\right)\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We now control the nonlinear term containing
b^{\prime}
. By Lebesgue interpolation, noticing that
\frac{5}{12}=\frac{a}{2}+\frac{1-a}{6}
with
a=\frac{3}{4}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}b^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mu\right\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{24}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left\|\nabla\mu\right\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(\Omega)}^{4}

(9.12)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{24}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left\|\nabla\mu\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}\left\|\mu-\overline{\mu}\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Owing to
[
7
, eq. (2.26)]
and (
9.10
), we know that

\displaystyle\|\mu-\overline{\mu}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{G}_{\phi}\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi}\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\!.

Thus, we derive that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}b^{\prime}(\phi)\partial_{t}\phi|\nabla\mu|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{24}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mu\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+C\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{7}{2}}\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{12}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\nabla\mu\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}+C\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{14}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{12}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\left(1+\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\right)\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Lastly, we recall that

\theta_{0}\|\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{a_{m}}{4}\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\nabla\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

In light of (
1.6

9.11

(9.13)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{Y}(t)+a_{m}\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{0}\left(1+\mathcal{Y}^{2}(t)\right)\mathcal{Y}(t)

for some
C_{0}

E(\phi_{0})
,
m
and the parameters of the system.
By classical ODE’s comparison principles (see, e.g.,
[
8

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mu(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{b_{m}}\frac{\mathcal{Y}(0)}{\sqrt{\left(1+\mathcal{Y}^{2}(0)\right)\mathrm{e}^{-2C_{0}T}-\mathcal{Y}^{2}(0)}},

where

Since

\mathcal{Y}(0)\leq b_{M}\|\nabla\mu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq b_{M}M^{2}.

Then, integrating (

(9.17)

On the other hand, by (

Local separation property.

Next, we consider an initial datum

\displaystyle\|\phi(t_{1})-\phi(t_{2})\|_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)}

.

Uniqueness.
Let
\phi_{1}^{0}
and
\phi_{2}^{0}
be two initial data such that
\overline{\phi_{1}^{0}}=\overline{\phi_{2}^{0}}
. We consider two corresponding local strong solutions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) defined on a common interval
[0,T_{M}]
.
Setting
\Phi=\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}
, arguing as in Section
6
, we find the identity

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(\phi_{1})|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left(F^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\left(a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2})\right)\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})}{2}\left(|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-a^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

We observe that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}-\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{2}}\partial_{t}\phi_{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\partial_{t}\Phi\,\Phi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}(b(\phi_{2})-b(\phi_{1}))\nabla\mu_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

Next, thanks to the regularity properties of strong solutions, we notice (cf.
[
7
, Eqn. (2.22)]
) that the following chain rule holds true:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\partial_{t}\Phi\,\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}b^{\prime}(\phi_{1})\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

Therefore, we end up with the following differential equality

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}a(\phi_{1})|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left(F^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\left(a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2})\right)\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})}{2}\left(|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-a^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi\,{\rm d}x

(9.21)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}b^{\prime}(\phi_{1})\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}(b(\phi_{2})-b(\phi_{1}))\nabla\mu_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x.

Let us now proceed by estimating the terms on the right-hand side. It is useful to recall the following control from Proposition
2.1

\|\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By exploiting the interpolation in Lebesgue spaces, we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\phi_{1}b^{\prime}(\phi_{1})\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{L^{\frac{12}{5}}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Besides, since
\mu_{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{M};H^{1}(\Omega))
, the last term on the right-hand side of (
9.21
) is estimated as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(b(\phi_{2})-b(\phi_{1}))\nabla\mu_{2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla\mu_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|b(\phi_{1})-b(\phi_{2})\right\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

By exploiting (
2.7
) and the regularity
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{M};W^{2,6}(\Omega))
, we control the terms containing the nonlinear diffusion
a
as

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}\left(a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2})\right)\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\nabla\Phi\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|a(\phi_{1})-a(\phi_{2})\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\phi_{2}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\Phi\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{7}{4}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}\frac{a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})}{2}\left(|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2}-|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\right)\Phi\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\phi_{1}+\nabla\phi_{2}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\Phi\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{\frac{7}{4}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Besides, we have

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-a^{\prime}(\phi_{2})\right)|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\Phi^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\phi_{2}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\|\Phi\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{m}}{16}\|\nabla\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Collecting all the estimates above, observing that
(F^{\prime}(\phi_{1})-F^{\prime}(\phi_{2}),\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})\geq 0
,
a(\phi_{1})\geq a_{m}>0
, and recalling that

\|\Phi\|_{H^{-1}_{(0)}(\Omega)}
is equivalent to
\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, we deduce that

(9.22)

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq h(t)\left\|\sqrt{b(\phi_{1})}\nabla\mathcal{G}_{\phi_{1}}\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\!,

where

h(\cdot)=C\left(1+\|\partial_{t}\phi_{1}\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)\in L^{1}(0,T_{M}).

Therefore, the uniqueness follows by the Gronwall lemma.
∎

10.
Lyapunov Stability and Longtime Behavior in three dimensions

In this section we show that if the initial datum
\phi_{0}
is sufficiently close to a local minimizer of the energy functional
E
, then the local strong solution provided by Theorem
9.1
is indeed a global one, and
\phi
will stay close to that minimizer for all
t\geq 0
. Finally, we investigate the longtime behavior of such global solutions as
t\to+\infty
.
We start by providing a characterization of energy minimizers.

10.1.
Energy minimizers

In the sequel, let
m\in(-1,1)
be fixed. We consider local minimizers
\psi
of the total energy
E
subject to the mass constraint, namely

\psi\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
such that

E(\psi)\leq E(u),\quad\forall\,u\in\mathcal{V}_{m}\ :\ \|u-\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}<\nu,

for some
\nu>0
.
As a first result, we show the connection between local minimizers and the stationary system (
1.10
)-(
1.11
).

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{V}_{m}
be a local energy minimizer of
E
on
\mathcal{V}_{m}
. Then,
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and consider the viscous Cahn–Hilliard equation with nonlinear diffusion

(10.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi

\displaystyle=\Delta\mu,

(10.2)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{div}\left(a(\varphi)\nabla\varphi\right)+a^{\prime}(\varphi)\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{2}+\Psi^{\prime}(\varphi)+\varepsilon\partial_{t}\varphi

in
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
, equipped with the boundary and initial conditions

(10.3)

\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}\varphi=\partial_{\textbf{{n}}}\mu=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\qquad\varphi|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

It has been proved in
[
32
]
(see Theorem 5.1 and Proposition 6.3) that for both
d=2,3
and any
\varphi_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
\Psi(\varphi_{0})\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\overline{\varphi}_{0}\in(-1,1)
, the problem (
10.1
)-(
10.3
) admits a global weak solution
\varphi(t)
, which satisfies the energy equality

E(\varphi(t))=E(\varphi_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla\mu(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}\varphi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau.

In particular, this implies that the map
t\mapsto E(\varphi(t))
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
. At this point, it is immediate to deduce that
\varphi\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}(\Omega))
(see, e.g.,
[
1
, pg. 482]
). Now, let
\varphi_{0}=\psi
, where
\psi
is a local minimizer of
E
in
\mathcal{V}_{m}
and let
\varphi(t)
be a weak solution to the viscous problem (
10.1
)-(
10.3
) departing from
\psi
. Then, by the continuity of the trajectory, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\|\varphi(t)-\psi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}<\nu
whenever

.
Exploiting the energy equality and the fact that

on

E(\psi)\leq E(\varphi(t))=E(\psi)-\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla\mu(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}\varphi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau,

namely

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla\mu(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{t}\varphi(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\,{\rm d}\tau\leq 0,\quad\forall\,t\in[0,\delta).

This implies that
\partial_{t}\varphi=0
for a.e.

. That is,
\varphi(t)\equiv\psi
for any
t\in[0,\delta)
.
As a result, since
\varphi\in L^{2}([0,\infty);H^{2}(\Omega))

\psi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
. Besides,

a.e.

, meaning that
\mu
is constant. Therefore,
\psi
is a solution to the stationary system (
1.10
)-(
1.11
) and belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{m}
.
∎

Let

\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime}(r)<\Psi^{\prime}(s)\quad\forall\,r\in(-1,a),\quad\forall\,s\in(m,1).

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Dhanwanthararishtam is a classical Ayurvedic medicine, primarily used for postnatal care and rejuvenation.Aim:The present study evaluates the antioxidant properties of the Ayurvedic medicine Dhanwanthararishtam, traditionally used for postnatal care to rejuvenate and strengthen the mother.Methods:ABTS, DPPH, and FRAP, were the antibiotic assays carried out following standard protocols.Results:Among these assays, Dhanwanthararishtam exhibited exceptional results at very low concentrations in the FRAP assay, while the ABTS and DPPH assays showed moderate antioxidant activities.Conclusion:These findings clearly demonstrate the antioxidant potential of Dhanwanthararishtam, supporting its medicinal efficacy.

INTRODUCTION

Ayurvedic and other traditional medicines have not been studied for their mechanism of action in the body, and due to this lack of knowledge, they are not becoming popular globally. The present workers have reported some work in this direction for the last few years and it continues.[123456] Dhanwanthararishtam is a classical Ayurvedic medicine, primarily used for postnatal care and rejuvenation. It is traditionally formulated to aid in the recovery and strengthening of mothers after childbirth. This herbal preparation contains a blend of natural ingredients known for their therapeutic properties, including Dashamoola (a group of ten medicinal roots), herbs like Manjistha (Rubia cordifolia), and other Ayurvedic ingredients.[7] The use of Ayurvedic formulations is important due to their potential in managing chronic diseases and improving quality of life. These traditional remedies are being increasingly researched for their therapeutic properties, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory effects.[8910]

Research has demonstrated the significant antioxidant potential of Dhanwanthararishtam. In studies evaluating its antioxidant properties, Dhanwanthararishtam has shown remarkable activity in assays, such as FRAP, ABTS, and DPPH. The FRAP assay, in particular, indicated excellent results at low concentrations, suggesting strong reducing power, which is a key indicator of antioxidant capability. The moderate activity observed in ABTS and DPPH assays further supports its role in neutralizing free radicals and reducing oxidative stress. These antioxidant properties of Dhanwanthararishtam contribute to its overall medicinal efficacy, supporting its traditional use in Ayurveda to enhance postpartum recovery and general health. The scientific validation of its antioxidant potential underscores its value in modern therapeutic applications. Hence, the present work deals with the antioxidant assays of Dhanwanthararishtam.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Sample preparation

Dhanwanthararishtam was obtained by the standard Ayurvedic vendor at Chennai, India.

Antioxidant studies

ABTS (2, 2’- azino-bis (3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid)) radical scavenging assay

The antioxidant activity of Dhanwanthararishtam was assessed using the ABTS radical cation decolorization assay, which was adapted from a method described by Perumal et al. (2018).[8] To prepare the ABTS radical cations, a solution of ABTS (7 mM) in deionized water was combined with potassium persulfate (140 mM). Various concentrations of the test samples (ranging from 10 to 80 μg/ml) and a standard solution of ascorbic acid were then mixed with 3.9 ml of the ABTS working solution. After allowing the mixtures to stand at room temperature for 20 minutes, the absorbance was measured at 734 nm using a UV–visible spectrophotometer. DPPH (2, 2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl) radical scavenging assay:

The antioxidant properties of compounds were determined using the DPPH radical scavenging assay, adapted from the method by Perumal et al. (2018).[8] This assay evaluates the reduction of DPPH, a stable free radical, by antioxidants. When DPPH interacts with a hydrogen-donating antioxidant, it changes color from purple to light yellow, indicating the formation of diphenyl picryl hydrazine. For this experiment, a 0.135 mM solution of DPPH was prepared in methanol. The test samples of Dhanwanthararishtam and the standard ascorbic acid at varying concentrations (10–80 μg/ml) were each combined with 900 μl of the DPPH solution. The mixtures were vortexed and allowed to stand at room temperature for 30 minutes. Absorbance readings were taken at 517 nm using ascorbic acid as the reference standard. The DPPH radical scavenging ability of the plant extract and control was calculated using the following formula:

% DPPH inhibition = [(OD of control - OD of test)/(OD of control)] ×100

Ferric-reducing antioxidant potential assay

The reducing power of Dhanwanthararishtam was assessed using a modified version of the method described by Benzie and Strain et al. (1999).[9] This method measures the ability of the extract to reduce Fe3+ to Fe2+, with the transformation observed at 680 nm. Higher absorbance values indicate greater reducing power. For the assay, varying concentrations of the test samples (10–80 μg/ml) and the standard ascorbic acid were prepared. Each sample was dissolved in 2.5 ml of 0.2 M phosphate buffer (pH 6.6) and mixed with 2.5 ml of 1% potassium ferricyanide solution. The mixtures were then incubated at 50°C for 20 minutes. Following incubation, 2.5 ml of 10% trichloroacetic acid was added to each sample, and the mixtures were centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 10 minutes. From each centrifuged sample, 2.5 ml of the upper layer was taken and mixed with 2.5 ml of distilled water and 0.5 ml of 0.1% ferric chloride solution. The absorbance of the resulting solutions was measured at wavelengths ranging from 400 to 680 nm using a colorimeter.

The antioxidant capabilities of Dhanwanthararishtam are crucial because oxidative stress is implicated in numerous health issues, including inflammation and tissue damage, which are particularly relevant during the postpartum period. By mitigating oxidative stress, Dhanwanthararishtam may help improve overall recovery and health in new mothers, aligning with its traditional use in Ayurveda.[7] Hence, Ayurvedic formulations are considered vital for their holistic approach to health, focusing on the balance of mind, body, and spirit. They are often used to promote wellness and prevent illness by maintaining equilibrium within the body’s systems.[11]

CONCLUSION

The results clearly indicate that the antioxidant activity of Dhanwanthararishtam, as claimed in Ayurveda, could contribute to its medicinal properties and potential efficacy in treating various diseases.

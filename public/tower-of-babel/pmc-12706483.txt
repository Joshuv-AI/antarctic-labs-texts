Lymphocytic panhypophysitis with clival extension and positive anti-rabphilin-3A antibodies: illustrative case
Authors: Takenori Kato, Hiroshi Ochiai, Masayoshi Hasegawa, Naoko Iwata, Haruki Fujisawa, Atsushi Suzuki, Yoshihisa Sugimura, Toshinori Hasegawa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBACKGROUNDLymphocytic panhypophysitis (LPH) is a rare inflammatory disorder that primarily affects the pituitary gland. Extension into the cavernous sinus has been described, but clival involvement is exceedingly rare. To date, no cases of isolated clival extension have been documented. Lymphocytic hypophysitis (LH) is a rare, chronic inflammatory disorder characterized by lymphocytic infiltration of the pituitary gland. LH is classified into three main categories according to the anatomical site of inflammation: lymphocytic adenohypophysitis, lymphocytic infundibuloneurohypophysitis (LINH), and lymphocytic panhypophysitis (LPH), which affects both the anterior and posterior lobes.1 Although LH usually remains confined to the pituitary gland and stalk, extrapituitary extension is rare.

The pathogenesis of LH is believed to involve an autoimmune mechanism, supported by the presence of various autoantibodies, associations with other autoimmune diseases, and links to pregnancy and the postpartum period.1,2 Recently, anti-rabphilin-3A antibodies have been identified as useful diagnostic markers for LH.3

Here, we report the first case of LPH with isolated clival extension in an 81-year-old man who presented with multiple anterior pituitary hormone deficiencies and central diabetes insipidus (CDI). Notably, the patient tested positive for anti-rabphilin-3A antibodies, providing additional diagnostic support for this rare manifestation.

Illustrative Case

An 81-year-old man presented with a 3-month history of headache and a 2-week history of anorexia and generalized fatigue. His medical history included well-controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and dyslipidemia. There was no family history of endocrine or autoimmune disease. Physical examination revealed mild dehydration, without visual field defects. Initial laboratory evaluations revealed hyponatremia, an elevated C-reactive protein level, mild renal impairment, and slightly decreased urine osmolality. Comprehensive endocrine evaluation demonstrated panhypopituitarism with decreased levels of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), cortisol, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), free thyroxine (T4), luteinizing hormone, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), and total testosterone. Immunological markers, including IgG4, myeloperoxidase antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (MPO-ANCA), and proteinase 3–antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (PR3-ANCA), were within normal limits (Table 1). Pituitary MRI demonstrated homogeneous enlargement of the pituitary gland and stalk with continuous extension into the clivus, showing a hypointense signal on T1-weighted images and homogeneous enhancement after gadolinium administration (Fig. 1A–C). The posterior pituitary bright spot was absent. The patient was treated with hydrocortisone and levothyroxine replacement therapy. On hospital day 4, he developed CDI, presenting with polyuria (4 L/day), hypernatremia (153 mEq/L), low urine osmolality (219 mOsm/kg H2O), and decreased antidiuretic hormone (ADH; 0.9 pg/mL), which responded well to desmopressin acetate.

Although clinical improvement was observed following initiation of hormone replacement therapy, histopathological confirmation was deemed essential because LH with isolated clival extension has never been reported in the literature, and differentiation from neoplastic or infectious processes was crucial for appropriate management. After careful consideration of the patient’s age and overall condition, transsphenoidal biopsy was performed on hospital day 12. Intraoperative examination revealed thickened dura mater extending between the pituitary gland and clivus, with evidence of direct infiltration into the clival bone (Fig. 2). Histological examination revealed predominantly fibrous tissue with lymphocytic and plasma cell infiltration, including inflammatory infiltrates and fibrosis between bone trabeculae, confirming clival osseous involvement. Immunohistochemical analysis demonstrated that CD20-positive B cells outnumbered CD3-positive T cells, with rare IgG4-positive plasma cells and no granulomatous formation (Fig. 3). Serum anti-rabphilin-3A antibody testing subsequently yielded positive results (SST Life Science). These findings, combined with the histopathological evidence, clinical presentation, radiological features, and positive anti-rabphilin-3A antibody, confirmed the diagnosis of LPH.

The patient was discharged on hormone replacement therapy. Follow-up MRI at 3 months demonstrated marked improvement in the pituitary and clival lesions (Fig. 1D– F), with continued resolution observed at 6 months (Fig. 1G– I).

Discussion

Observations

This case highlights several distinctive features of LPH. We report a rare manifestation of LPH with clivus invasion in an 81-year-old man. Intraoperative endoscopic evaluations during transsphenoidal biopsy provided direct visualization of thickened abnormal tissue extending between the pituitary gland and clivus. The diagnosis was confirmed by histopathological findings and by detection of anti-rabphilin-3A antibodies, a recently proposed biomarker of LH. Notably, marked improvement of the mass-like lesion was observed on follow-up MRI after surgery, making this a valuable case that provides further insight into the clinical course and management of this rare condition. In this case, the diagnosis of LPH was established through comprehensive clinical, radiological, and histopathological evaluations. The patient met the diagnostic criteria for LPH as described in recent literature.1,4 The clinical presentation of panhypopituitarism with CDI, combined with characteristic MRI findings of pituitary enlargement and stalk thickening with homogeneous enhancement, strongly suggested an inflammatory etiology. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report to present sequential endoscopic images clearly demonstrating intraoperative findings of LPH extending into the clivus. These operative views provide direct visual evidence of clival involvement, which has rarely been reported, and may contribute to a better understanding of disease progression and guide surgical decision-making in such rare cases. Histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis, revealing lymphoplasmacytic infiltration, with CD20-positive B cells outnumbering CD3-positive T cells, consistent with LH. Neoplastic processes, including pituitary tumors and primary or metastatic clival tumors—the most important differential diagnoses for sellar-clival lesions—were excluded because no tumor-specific histological features were present.5 Granulomatous hypophysitis was ruled out by the absence of epithelioid cells and multinucleated giant cells.6 IgG4-related diseases were excluded based on normal serum IgG4 levels and the presence of only minimal IgG4-positive plasma cells in the tissue.7 Importantly, histopathological analysis of the biopsied lesions provided direct evidence of clival invasion, which has rarely been documented in previous reports of LH. Specifically, dense inflammatory cell infiltration and fibrosis within the bone trabeculae of the clivus confirmed that the lesion extended beyond the sellar region into the bone structure itself. This histological finding, in conjunction with preoperative imaging and intraoperative endoscopic observations, provides robust confirmation of clival involvement and represents a key feature of this case.

This case represents the first documented instance of LPH with isolated clival extension. Extrapituitary extension of LH is exceptionally uncommon, with cavernous sinus involvement reported in few isolated cases,8,9 but clival extension is an even rarer manifestation. Prior to our report, only one case of LPH with clival involvement combined with bilateral cavernous sinus extension had been described.10 Both our case and previous reports demonstrated panhypopituitarism, CDI, and similar MRI features, including pituitary enlargement, stalk thickening, and clival extension. However, notable differences included patient age (81 vs 42 years), with our case demonstrating neither visual symptoms nor the bilateral cavernous sinus involvement reported previously.10 Although our clival lesion was considerably larger and more extensive than that described in the previous report,10 it showed marked improvement on follow-up MRI after biopsy and hormonal replacement therapy. To the best of our knowledge, such significant regression of clival involvement has not been reported previously. These findings highlight not only the potential for extensive progression but also the possibility of spontaneous or treatment-induced regression, even in cases characterized by marked clival invasion.

Positive serum anti-rabphilin-3A antibody testing has proven valuable for diagnosis.3,11,12 A recent report summarized antibody positivity and negativity in histologically confirmed pituitary diseases from previous studies. According to this report, the sensitivity of the antibody was 100% for LINH and 80% for LPH. When both entities were considered together as LH associated with CDI, the sensitivity was 88.9%. The overall specificity for detecting sellar or suprasellar mass lesions was 97.4%.13 Therefore, antibody positivity provided crucial diagnostic support in our challenging case with atypical clival extension, helping to distinguish LH from the extensive differential diagnosis of clival lesions, including primary bone tumors, metastases, lymphoma, and infectious processes.3,11,12,14,15

In addition to its diagnostic utility, this case raises important questions regarding the underlying pathophysiology of extrapituitary extensions. The mechanism underlying clival extension in LH remains poorly understood. Anatomically, the clivus lies in close proximity to the sella turcica, and our intraoperative findings revealed thickened dura at the sellar floor. Histopathological analysis demonstrated inflammatory infiltration between clival bone trabeculae, suggesting that the inflammatory process may have extended from the enlarged pituitary gland to the clivus via dural involvement. The dural thickening observed during surgery thus provides direct evidence of a potential pathway for clival extension in this rare condition. Notably, even in experimental autoimmune hypophysitis models using rabphilin-3A, inflammatory cell infiltration has been confirmed in the neurohypophysis as well as in the hypothalamic supraoptic nucleus and paraventricular nucleus, demonstrating upward extension into hypothalamic structures.14 However, downward extension to the clivus has not been evaluated in these models, highlighting the exceptional rarity of this manifestation and the need for further investigation into the mechanisms of extrapituitary extension, particularly in the caudal direction.

Recognizing this rare variant has substantial clinical implications. LH should be considered in the differential diagnosis of clival lesions, particularly when accompanied by panhypopituitarism and CDI. Although histopathological confirmation remains the diagnostic gold standard, as demonstrated in our case, serum anti-rabphilin-3A antibody testing offers a valuable diagnostic tool for atypical presentations and serves as an alternative when biopsy is not feasible because of patient-related factors or technical challenges. Early recognition by either method is crucial, as prompt hormone replacement therapy can significantly improve patient outcomes and prevent life-threatening complications.1,4

This study has certain limitations. Long-term follow-up is limited, and the mechanisms underlying clival extension in LH remain unclear. Although serum anti-rabphilin-3A antibody testing proved valuable in our case and may aid diagnosis when biopsy is not feasible, this assay is not yet widely available or standardized in clinical practice. Additional case reports and the standardization of antibody testing will be essential to advance diagnostic strategies for this rare condition.

Lessons

Here, we report the first case of LPH with isolated clival extension by positive anti-rabphilin-3A antibody testing. Intraoperative findings of dural thickening provided valuable insights into the potential mechanism of clival extension through inflammatory spread along the dural surfaces. This case highlights the need to consider LH in the differential diagnosis of clival lesions, particularly when accompanied by panhypopituitarism and CDI. Early diagnosis and timely initiation of hormone replacement therapy are essential to optimize patient outcomes.

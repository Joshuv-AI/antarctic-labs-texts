Definitions and notation

All groups and incidence structures in this paper are finite. We denote by

\mathrm{S}_{n}

[
14
]
or
[
26

We use the standard notation as in
[
14
]
for finite simple groups. A
2
-design
\mathcal{D}
with parameters

is a pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B})
with a set

of
v

of blocks of size

k

and each pair of two distinct points is contained in
\lambda

is contained in exactly

blocks which is called the

In this paper, we refer to

as the parameter set of

. The design

is symmetric if

(or

), and it is nontrivial when

.
An

of a
2
-design
\mathcal{D}
is a permutation on the points mapping blocks to blocks. The full automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{D})
of
\mathcal{D}
is the group consisting of all automorphisms of
\mathcal{D}
. A

of
\mathcal{D}
is a point-block pair

. For

. Further notation and definitions in both design theory and group theory are standard and can be found, for example, in

q-1\mid k_{q/2}
. Then by (

for some

with

for

, and

. Hence,

. Thus

for

and

for each

\mathcal{D}_{0}
, we observe that each pair of points is incidence with

blocks, that is to say,
\lambda_{0}=q+1
. This completes the proof.

is isomorphic to a group of automorphisms of

. Firstly, note that

where

is a

contains a unique conjugate class of subgroups isomorphic to

, we may assume that

normalizes a copy of

, say

. In particular,

normalizes one of its

, and hence
\left\langle\phi\right\rangle
normalizes the unique subgroup of

isomorphic to

containing

by Lemma
2.2
applied to

. Clearly, there is
\gamma\in T
such that

,
and there is
\delta\in H_{0}
such that
Q^{\gamma\delta}=Q_{0}
.
Set
\vartheta:=\gamma\delta
. Then
H^{\vartheta}=H_{0}
and
Q^{\vartheta}=Q_{0}
, and hence
K^{\vartheta}=K_{0}
again by Lemma
2.2
. Now, we define
\bar{\vartheta}:\textsf{Cos}(T,H,K)\rightarrow\textsf{Cos}(T,H_{0},K_{0})
given by

(Hx,Ky)^{\bar{\vartheta}}=\left(H_{0}x^{\vartheta},K_{0}y^{\vartheta}\right)
. Then
\bar{\vartheta}

is a bijection such that
Hx\cap Ky\neq\varnothing
if
and only if
H_{0}x^{\vartheta}\cap K_{0}y^{\vartheta}=\left(Hx\cap Ky\right)^{\vartheta}\neq\varnothing
. Thus
\bar{\vartheta}
is an
isomorphism from
\textsf{Cos}(T,H,K)
onto
\textsf{Cos}(T,H_{0},K_{0})
.

Let
H_{0}x
and
H_{0}y
with

be two right cosets of
H_{0}
and
K_{0}
in
T
, respectively, such that
H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle x=H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle y
. Then

, and hence
H_{0}x=H_{0}y
. Therefore,
H_{0}x=H_{0}y
if and only if
H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle x=H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle y
for

. Further,

\left|T:H_{0}\right|=\left|T\left\langle\phi\right\rangle:H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle\right|=q^{3}(q^{3}-1)/2
. Hence
\left\{H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle x:x\in T\right\}
is the set of all right cosets of
H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle
in
G
. Similarly,
\left\{K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle y:y\in T\right\}
is the set of all right cosets of
K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle
in
G
.

H_{0}x\cap K_{0}y\neq\varnothing
with
x,y\in T
, then
xy^{-1}\in H_{0}K_{0}\subseteq H_{0}K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle=H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle
since

normalizes

, and hence
H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle x\cap K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle y\neq\varnothing
since also

is normalized by
\left\langle\phi\right\rangle
. Conversely,
H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle x\cap K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle y\neq\varnothing
with

implies
xy^{-1}\in H_{0}K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle\cap T=H_{0}K_{0}
and hence
H_{0}x\cap K_{0}y\neq\varnothing
. Therefore the map
\bar{\mu}:\textsf{Cos}(T,H_{0},K_{0})\rightarrow\textsf{Cos}(G,H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle,K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle)
given by

is an

onto
\textsf{Cos}(G,H_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle,K_{0}\left\langle\phi\right\rangle)
, and hence

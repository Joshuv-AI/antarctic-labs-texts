Calculating Gromov–Hausdorff distance by means of asymptotic dimension
Ivan N. Mikhailov

Abstract
In this paper, we apply the concept of asymptotic dimension to calculating Gromov–Hausdorff distances between some unbounded metric spaces. In particular, we show that the Gromov–Hausdorff between ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} with the Euclidean metric and ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} equals the Hausdorff distance between them: d G ​ H ​ ( ℝ 2 , ℤ 2 ) = d H ​ ( ℝ 2 , ℤ 2 ) d_{GH}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{Z}^{2})=d_{H}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{Z}^{2}) .

Preliminaries

A metric space
is an arbitrary pair
(X,\,\operatorname{d}_{X})
, where
X
is an arbitrary set,
\operatorname{d}_{X}\colon X\times X\to[0,\,\infty)
is some metric on it, that is, a nonnegative symmetric, positively definite function that satisfies the triangle inequality.

For convenience, if it is clear in which metric space we are working, we denote the distance between points
x
and
y
by
|xy|
. Suppose
X
is a metric space. By
U_{r}(a)=\{x\in X\colon|ax|<r\}
,
B_{r}(a)=\{x\in X\colon|ax|\leq r\}
we denote open and closed balls centered at point
a
of radius
r
in
X
. For an arbitrary subset
A\subset X
of a metric space
X
, let
U_{r}(A)=\cup_{a\in A}U_{r}(a)
be an open
r
-neighborhood of
A
. For non-empty subsets
A\subset X
,
B\subset X
we put
\operatorname{d}(A,\,B)=\inf\bigl\{|ab|:\,a\in A,\,b\in B\bigl\}
.

2.1
Hausdorff and Gromov–Hausdorff distances

Definition 1
.

Let
A
and
B
be non-empty subsets of a metric space.

The Hausdorff distance
between
A
and
B
is the quantity

\operatorname{d}_{H}(A,\,B)=\inf\bigl\{r>0\colon A\subset U_{r}(B),\,B\subset U_{r}(A)\bigr\}.

Definition 2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces. The triple
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},Z)
, consisting of a metric space
Z
and its two subsets
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
, isometric to
X
and
Y
respectively, is called
a realization of the pair

(X,Y)
.

Definition 3
.

The Gromov-Hausdorff distance

d_{GH}(X,Y)
between
X
and
Y
is the exact lower bound of the numbers
r\geq 0
for which there exists a realization
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime},Z)
of the pair
(X,Y)
such that
\operatorname{d}_{H}(X^{\prime},\,Y^{\prime})\leq r
.

Now let
X,\,Y
be non-empty sets.

Definition 4
.

Each
\sigma\subset X\times Y
is called a
relation
between
X
and
Y
.

By
\mathcal{P}_{0}(X,\,Y)
we denote the set of all non-empty relations between
X
and
Y
.

We put

\pi_{X}\colon X\times Y\rightarrow X,\;\pi_{X}(x,\,y)=x,

\pi_{Y}\colon X\times Y\rightarrow Y,\;\pi_{Y}(x,\,y)=y.

Definition 5
.

A relation
R\subset X\times Y
is called a
correspondence
, if restrictions
\pi_{X}|_{R}
and
\pi_{Y}|_{R}
are surjective.

Let
\mathcal{R}(X,\,Y)
be the set of all correspondences between
X
and
Y
.

Definition 6
.

Let
X,\,Y
be metric spaces,
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(X,\,Y)
. The
distortion
of
\sigma
is the quantity

\operatorname{dis}\sigma=\sup\Bigl\{\bigl||xx^{\prime}|-|yy^{\prime}|\bigr|\colon(x,\,y),\,(x^{\prime},\,y^{\prime})\in\sigma\Bigr\}.

Proposition 1

(
[
2
]
)
.

For arbitrary metric spaces
X
and
Y
, the following equality holds

2\operatorname{d}_{GH}(X,\,Y)=\inf\bigl\{\operatorname{dis}\,R\colon R\in\mathcal{R}(X,\,Y)\bigr\}.

Denote the set of all positive real numbers by
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Definition 7
.

Following
[
4
]
, we define a
stabilizer
of a metric space
X
as follows:

\operatorname{St}X=\bigl\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\colon\lambda X\;\text{is isometric to}\;X\bigr\}.

It is not difficult to show that
\operatorname{St}X
is a subgroup in a multiplicative group
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

2.2
Asymptotic dimension

Let
X
be an arbitrary metric space
X
.

Definition 8
.

It is said that the asymptotic dimension of
X
does not exceed
n
and written
\operatorname{asdim}X\leq n
iff for every uniformly bounded open cover
\mathcal{V}
of
X
there is a uniformly bounded open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
of multiplicity
\leq(n+1)
so that
\mathcal{V}
refines
\mathcal{U}
. Then
\operatorname{asdim}X=n
iff
\operatorname{asdim}X\leq n
and
\operatorname{asdim}X\,\cancel{\leq}\,n-1
.

We will need the equivalent definition of the asymptotic dimension
[
1
]
[Theorem 19 (2), p.7].

Definition 9
.

The family
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
of subsets of a metric space
X
is said to be
r
-disjoint iff
d(U_{\alpha},U_{\beta})>r
for all possible
\alpha\neq\beta
.

Definition 10
.

We have
\operatorname{asdim}X\leq n
iff, for every
r<\infty
, there exist
r
-disjoint families
\mathcal{U}^{0},\ldots\mathcal{U}^{n}
of uniformly bounded subsets of
X
such that
\cup_{i}\mathcal{U}^{i}
is a cover of
X
.

Theorem 1

(
[
3
]
)
.

The following equality holds:
\operatorname{asdim}\mathbb{R}^{n}=n
.

Since every two norms on a finite-dimensional linear space are equivalent to each other, we obtain the following enhancement:

Theorem 2
.

For an arbitrary
n
-dimensional normed space
X
, we have
\operatorname{asdim}X=n
.

3
Main theorem

Theorem 3
.

Let
X
be a metric space with
\operatorname{asdim}X\geq n
and
\operatorname{St}X\neq\{e\}
. Suppose in a metric space
A
there exist
r
-disjoint families
\mathcal{U}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}^{k}
,
1\leq k\leq n
of uniformly bounded subsets of
A
such that
A\subset\cup_{i}\mathcal{U}^{i}
. Then
d_{GH}(A,X)\geq\frac{r}{2}
.

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary correspondence
R\in\mathcal{R}(A,X)
. Suppose that
\operatorname{dis}R<r
. Then there exists some
\varepsilon>0
such that
\operatorname{dis}R<r-\varepsilon
.

Let
\mathcal{U}^{i}=\{U_{\alpha}^{i}\}_{\alpha}
. We put
V^{i}_{\alpha}=R(U^{i}_{\alpha})
and
\mathcal{V}^{i}=\{V^{i}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
.

Since the families
\mathcal{U}^{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
consist of uniformly bounded subsets of
A
, there exists
C>0
such that
\operatorname{diam}U^{i}_{\alpha}<C
for arbitrary
i
,
\alpha
. Since
\operatorname{dis}R<r-\varepsilon
, we obtain
\operatorname{diam}V^{i}_{\alpha}<C+r-\varepsilon
. Therefore, the families
\mathcal{V}^{i}
consist of uniformly bounded subsets of
X
.

By condition, for arbitrary possible
\alpha\neq\beta
and arbitrary
i
, we have
d(U^{i}_{\alpha},U^{i}_{\beta})>r
. Since
\operatorname{dis}R<r-\varepsilon
, we obtain
d(V^{i}_{\alpha},V^{i}_{\beta})>\varepsilon
. Therefore, the families
\mathcal{V}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{V}^{k}
are
\varepsilon
-disjoint in
X
.

Since
R
is a correspondence and
A=\cup_{i}\mathcal{U}^{i}
, we obtain
X=\cup_{i}\mathcal{V}^{i}
.

Since
\operatorname{St}X\neq\{e\}
, there exists
\lambda\in\operatorname{St}X
such that
\lambda>1
. Consider the families
\lambda^{n}\mathcal{V}^{1},\ldots\lambda^{n}\mathcal{V}^{k}
of subsets of
X
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. These families are
(\lambda^{n}\varepsilon)
-separated and consist of subsets whose diameters are bounded by
\lambda^{n}(C+r-\varepsilon)
from above.

Now we observe that due to the arbitrariness of
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have obtained

\operatorname{asdim}X\leq k-1<n

by the definition of the asymptotic dimension. This is a contradiction.
∎

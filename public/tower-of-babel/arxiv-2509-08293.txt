Tensorial representations of positive weakly ( q , r ) (q,r) -dominated multilinear operators
Abdelaziz Belaada, Adel Bounabab, Athmane Ferradi and Khalil Saadi

Abstract
. We introduce and study the class of positive weakly ( q , r ) (q,r) -dominated multilinear operators between Banach lattices. This notion extends classical domination and summability concepts to the positive multilinear setting and generates a new positive multi-ideal. A Pietsch domination theorem and a polynomial version are established. Finally, we provide a tensorial representation that yields an isometric identification with the dual of an appropriate completed tensor product..

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

The theory of absolutely summing operators, initiated by Pietsch in the
1960s, has played a central role in the development of operator ideals and
their applications to Banach space theory. Since then, several nonlinear
extensions have been studied, especially for multilinear operators,
homogeneous polynomials, and Lipschitz mappings, leading to a rich framework
that unifies summability, domination, and factorization properties. This
area of research has provided a unified approach to extending linear results
to nonlinear settings, with significant contributions from works of Pietsch

[
14
,
15
]
, Cohen
[
7
]
, Kwapień
[
10
,
11
]
, and others. In recent years, increasing attention has
focused on the positive versions of these operators. In fact, positive
operator theory, which uses the lattice structure of Banach spaces, has
become a powerful tool for strengthening and extending classical results. In

[
8
]
, the basic elements of positive linear and multilinear operator
ideals were established. Thereafter, in
[
4
]
, positive polynomial
ideals were introduced as a natural extension of the linear and multilinear
cases. These ideals not only encompass the corresponding positive classes,
which fail to satisfy the conditions of classical ideals, but also provide a
unifying framework for their study. A positive multilinear ideal
\mathcal{M}^{+}
(or polynomial ideal
\mathcal{P}^{+}
) is a class of multilinear
operators (or polynomials) between Banach lattices that is stable under
composition with positive linear operators. The theory of summability in the
positive setting not only produces sharper inequalities but also exposes
phenomena absent in the purely linear framework. The concept of absolutely
\left(p,q_{1},\ldots,q_{m},r\right)
-summing multilinear operators,
introduced by Achour
[
1
]
, provides a natural extension of the
classical absolutely
(p,q,r)
-summing operators of Pietsch
[
14
]
. When
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r},
this class is referred to as
the
\left(q,r\right)
-dominated operators. In this paper, we introduce
the class of positive weakly
(q,r)
-dominated multilinear
operators, which combines lattice positivity with weak absolute summability.
This class is stable under composition with positive operators and fits
naturally into the framework of positive multi-ideals. We also establish the
Pietsch domination theorem in this setting, characterizing these operators
through vector measures on the positive balls of the dual space. This result
extends the classical Pietsch theorem to the positive setting, based on
Banach lattice spaces. Furthermore, we also examine the polynomial version,
showing that the structure extends to
m
-homogeneous polynomials, giving
rise to positive polynomial ideals. Finally, we establish a tensorial
representation for positive weakly
(q,r)
-dominated multilinear
operators. By introducing a suitable tensor norm, we obtain an isometric
identification of these operators with the dual of a completed tensor
product, thereby providing the natural tensorial framework for the theory.
The same approach applies to polynomials, where a tensor norm is constructed
using
\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E,
the
m
-fold positive projective symmetric tensor product of
E
.

2.

-dominated multilinear operators

The notion of absolutely

-summing linear operators was first
introduced and studied by Pietsch
[
14
]

[

[

was proposed in
[

context. When

this notion is referred
to as
(q,r)
-dominated linear operators. In this section, we present
another version of positive multilinear operators. Let us start with de
definition in the positive linear case.

Definition 2.1

[

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}

(2.1)

The space consisting of all such mappings is denoted by

\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{\left(q,r\right)}}=\inf\{C>0:C\text{
{satisfies }}(\ref{2.1})\}.

Theorem 2.5

(Pietsch Domination Theorem)
.

Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}
. Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach lattices. The
following statements are equivalent.

1) The operator
T\in\mathcal{L}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m};F\right)
is
positive weakly
(q,r)
-dominated.

2) There is a constant
C>0
and Borel probability measures
\mu
on
B_{\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)^{\ast}}^{+}

and
\eta
on
B_{F^{\ast\ast}}^{+}
such that

\left|\langle T(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m}),y^{\ast}\rangle\right|\leq C\left(\int_{B_{\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)^{\ast}}^{+}}\varphi\left(\left|x_{i}^{1}\right|,\ldots,\left|x_{i}^{m}\right|\right)^{q}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}(\int_{B_{F^{\ast\ast}}^{+}}\langle\left|y^{\ast}\right|,y^{\ast\ast}\rangle^{r}d\eta)^{\frac{1}{r}},

(2.5)

for all
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m},y^{\ast})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}\times F^{\ast}.
Therefore, we have

d_{w,\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}(T)=\inf\{C>0:C\text{ {satisfies
inequality} }(\ref{123})\}.

3) There is a constant
C>0
and Borel probability measures
\mu
on
B_{\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)^{\ast}}^{+}

and
\eta
on
B_{F^{\ast\ast}}^{+}
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}&|\langle T(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m}),y^{\ast}\rangle|\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{B_{\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)^{\ast}}^{+}}\varphi\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m}\right)^{q}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{F^{\ast\ast}}^{+}}\langle y^{\ast},y^{\ast\ast}\rangle^{r}d\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},\end{array}

(2.6)

for all
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m},y^{\ast})\in E_{1}^{+}\times\cdots\times E_{m}^{+}\times F^{\ast+}.
Therefore, we have

d_{w,\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}(T)=\inf\{C>0:C\text{ {satisfies }}(\ref{def2sec4})\}.

Proof.

1)\Longleftrightarrow 2):
We will choose the parameters as specified in

[
13
, Theorem 4.6]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}S:\mathcal{L}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m};F\right)\times\left(E_{1}\times\ldots\times E_{m}\times F^{\ast}\right)\times\mathbb{R\times R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}:\\
S\left(T,\left(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m},y^{\ast}\right),\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)=\left|\lambda_{2}\right||\langle T(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m}),y^{\ast}\rangle|\\

These maps satisfy conditions
\left(1\right)
and
\left(2\right)
from

[
13

(q,r)

\displaystyle(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}S\left(T,\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m},y_{i}^{\ast}\right),\lambda_{i,1},\lambda_{i,2}\right)^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{\varphi\in B_{\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)^{\ast}}^{+}}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}R_{1}(x^{\ast},\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m},y_{i}^{\ast}\right),\lambda_{i,1})^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\times\sup_{y^{\ast\ast}\in B_{F^{\ast\ast}}^{+}}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}R_{2}(y^{\ast\ast},\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m},y_{i}^{\ast}\right),\lambda_{i,2})^{r})^{\frac{1}{r}},

i.e.,
T
is
R_{1},R_{2}
-
S
-abstract
(q,r)
-summing. As outlined in

is
R_{1},R_{2}
-
S
-abstract

C
and
probability measures
\mu
on
B_{\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)^{\ast}}^{+}
and
\eta
on
B_{F^{\ast\ast}}^{+}
,
such that

\displaystyle S\left(T,\left(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m},y^{\ast}\right),\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\int_{B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}}R_{1}(x^{\ast},\left(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m},y^{\ast}\right),\lambda_{1})^{q}d\mu)^{\frac{1}{q}}(\int_{B_{F^{\ast\ast}}^{+}}R_{2}(y^{\ast\ast},\left(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m},y^{\ast}\right),\lambda_{2})^{r}d\eta)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Consequently

\left|\langle T(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m}),y^{\ast}\rangle\right|\leq C\left(\int_{B_{\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)^{\ast}}^{+}}\varphi\left(\left|x^{1}\right|,\ldots,\left|x^{m}\right|\right)^{q}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}(\int_{B_{F^{\ast\ast}}^{+}}\langle\left|y^{\ast}\right|,y^{\ast\ast}\rangle^{r}d\eta)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

3.
Tensorial representation

Tensorial representation plays a fundamental role in the study of Banach
spaces. For the projective tensor product, it is well known that the space
of multilinear operators
\mathcal{L}\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m};Y\right)

coincides with the dual of
X_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}X_{m}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}Y^{\ast}
. Similarly,
replacing the projective norm with the injective norm
\varepsilon
yields
a coincidence with the space of integral multilinear operators. For this
reason, tensorial representation has become a standard objective in the
study of new classes of operators. It is therefore natural, when introducing
a new class, to seek an appropriate tensor norm that produces an analogous
identification. In the present section, we define a tensor norm on the
algebraic tensor product
E_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{m}\otimes F^{\ast}

and show that its topological dual is isometric to the space of positive
weakly
(q,r)
-dominated multilinear operators. This tensorial approach
provides a natural framework to identify such operators. In the polynomial
case, we similarly define a tensor norm on
\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\otimes F
and show that its
topological dual is isometric to the space of positive weakly
(q,r)
-dominated polynomials.

3.1.
Multilinear case

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}.
Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{m},F
be Banach lattices.
Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}

and

For simplify, we
denote by

\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}=E_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\left|\pi\right|}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{\left|\pi\right|}E_{m}.

Consider

\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}\left(u\right)=\inf\left\{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\left\|(x_{i}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}^{m})\right\|_{\ell_{q,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)}\left\|\left(y_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F\right)}\right\},

(3.1)

where the infimum is taken over all general representations of

Lemma 3.1

1)\Leftrightarrow 2):

This is immediate, since

.
∎

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}^{\ast}\in B_{E_{j}^{\ast}},y^{\ast}\in B_{F^{\ast}}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}\left\{\left|\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\lambda_{i}x_{1}^{\ast}\left(x_{i}^{1}\right)\cdots x_{m}^{\ast}\left(x_{i}^{m}\right)y^{\ast}\left(y_{i}\right)\right|\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{j}^{\ast}\in B_{E_{j}^{\ast}},y^{\ast}\in B_{F^{\ast}}\\
1\leq j\leq m\end{subarray}}\left\{\left|\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\lambda_{i}x_{1}^{\ast}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{m}^{\ast}\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m}\right)y^{\ast}\left(y_{i}\right)\right|\right\}.

Since
x_{1}^{\ast}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{m}^{\ast}\in B_{\mathcal{L}^{r}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\right)},
and taking absolute values inside,
we get

\varepsilon\left(u\right)\leq\sup_{\varphi\in B_{\mathcal{L}^{r}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\right)},y^{\ast}\in B_{F^{\ast}}}\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\left|\varphi\left(x_{1}^{i},\ldots,x_{m}^{m}\right)\right|\left|y^{\ast}\left(y_{i}\right)\right|\right\}.

Since
\left|\varphi\right|\in B_{\mathcal{L}^{r}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\right)}^{+}
and
\left|y^{\ast}\right|\in B_{F^{\ast}}^{+},
we further obtain

\varepsilon\left(u\right)\leq\sup_{\varphi\in B_{\mathcal{L}^{r}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\right)}^{+},y^{\ast}\in B_{F^{\ast+}}}\left\{\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\varphi\left(\left|x_{i}^{1}\right|,\ldots,\left|x_{i}^{m}\right|\right)y^{\ast}\left(\left|y_{i}\right|\right)\right\}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality

\varepsilon\left(u\right)\leq\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\sup_{\varphi\in B_{\mathcal{L}^{r}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m}\right)}^{+}}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\varphi\left(\left|x_{i}^{1}\right|,\ldots,\left|x_{i}^{m}\right|\right)^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\left(y_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F\right)}.

Finally, taking the infimum over all representations of
u
of the form
\left(\ref{3.3}\right)
yields

\varepsilon\left(u\right)\leq\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}\left(u\right).

∎

We denote by
E_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}E_{m}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}F
the completed of
E_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{m}\otimes F
for the norm
\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}
.
The main result of this section is the following identification.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}.
We have the following isometric identification

\mathcal{L}_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m};F\right)=(E_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}E_{m}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}F^{\ast})^{\ast}.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L}_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left(E,\ldots,E_{m};F\right).
We define a linear functional on
E_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{m}\otimes F^{\ast}
by

\Psi_{T}\left(u\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle T\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle,

where
u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}^{m}\otimes y_{i}^{\ast}
. Then, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Psi_{T}\left(u\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle T\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\left\langle T\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
T
is positive weakly
\left(q,r\right)
-domintaed, we get

\left|\Psi_{T}\left(u\right)\right|\leq d_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}(T)\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\left\|(x_{i}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}^{m})\right\|_{\ell_{q,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)}\left\|\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F^{\ast}\right)}.

Hence, as
u
is arbitrary,
\Psi_{T}
is
\mu_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}
-continuous on
E_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{m}\otimes F^{\ast},
and extends
continuously to the completed tensor product
E_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}E_{m}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}F^{\ast}
with

\left\|\Psi_{T}\right\|\leq d_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}(T).

Conversely, let
\Psi\in(E_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}E_{m}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}F^{\ast})^{\ast}
. We
consider the mapping
B(\Psi)
defined by

B(\Psi)\left(x^{1},\ldots,x^{m}\right)\left(y^{\ast}\right)=\Psi\left(x^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x^{m}\otimes y^{\ast}\right)

It is clear that
B(\Psi)\in\mathcal{L}\left(E_{1},\ldots,E_{m};F\right).

Let
\left(x_{i}^{j}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\subset E_{j},
(
j=1,\ldots,m
) and
y_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,y_{n_{1}}^{\ast}\in Y^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle B(\Psi)\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\Psi(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}^{m}\otimes y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi\right\|\mu_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}^{m}\otimes y_{i}^{\ast}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\left\langle B(\Psi)\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\leq 1}(\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle B(\Psi)\left(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{m}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\leq 1}(\left|\Psi(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}^{m}\otimes y_{i}^{\ast})\right|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\leq 1}\left\|\Psi\right\|\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n_{1}}}\left\|(x_{i}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}^{m})\right\|_{\ell_{q,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)}\left\|\left(y_{k}^{\ast}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F^{\ast}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi\right\|\left\|(x_{i}^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}^{m})\right\|_{\ell_{q,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(\widehat{E}_{\left|\pi\right|}\right)}\left\|\left(y_{k}^{\ast}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F^{\ast}\right)}.

Hence,
B(\Psi)
is positive weakly
\left(q,r\right)
-dominted and

d_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left(B(\Psi)\right)\leq\left\|\Psi\right\|.

∎

3.2.
Polynomial case

The polynomial case differs from the multilinear case in essential details;
it does not directly follow from the multilinear setting, and the proof
steps must be revisited to handle this situation. Let
E
and
F
be Banach
lattice. Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty.
Consider
u\in\left(\otimes_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\otimes F
of the form

u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\otimes\overset{\left(m\right)}{\cdots}\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i},

(3.4)

where
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R},x_{i}\in E
and
y_{i}\in F\left(1\leq i\leq n\right).
This representation of
u
can be considered a general
form, as any other representation can be rewritten in this way. Define

\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left(u\right)=\inf\left\{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\sup_{\phi\in B_{\mathcal{P}^{r}\left({}^{m}E\right)}^{+}}\left\|\left(\phi\left(\left|x_{i}\right|\right)\right)\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{n}}\left\|\left(y_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F\right)}\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all general representations of
u
of the
form
\left(\ref{1234}\right).

Proposition 3.5
.

For every
u
of the form
\left(\ref{1234}\right),
we have

\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left(u\right)=\mu_{\left(q,r\right)}^{1+}\left(u\right).

Proof.

Let
u
of the form
\left(\ref{1234}\right).
We have

\displaystyle\mu_{\left(q,r\right)}^{1+}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\left\|(x_{i}\otimes\overset{\left(m\right)}{\cdots}\otimes x_{i})\right\|_{\ell_{q,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)}\left\|\left(y_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F\right)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\sup_{\phi\in B_{\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)^{\ast}}^{+}}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\phi\left(\left|x_{i}\otimes\overset{\left(m\right)}{\cdots}\otimes x_{i}\right|\right)^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\|\left(y_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F\right)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\sup_{\phi\in B_{\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)^{\ast}}^{+}}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\phi\left(\left|x_{i}\right|\otimes\overset{\left(m\right)}{\cdots}\otimes\left|x_{i}\right|\right)^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\|\left(y_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F\right)}\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{P}^{r}\left({}^{m}E\right)=\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)^{\ast}
, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{\left(q;r\right)}^{1+}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\sup_{\phi\in B_{\mathcal{P}^{r}\left({}^{m}E\right)}^{+}}\left\|\left(\phi\left(\left|x_{i}\right|\right)\right)\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{n}}\left\|\left(y_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F\right)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left(u\right).

∎

From the above discussion on the tensor norm
\mu_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left(u\right)
, and the previous proposition, we obtain the
following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}.
Then
\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}
is a tensor norm on
\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\otimes F

and we have

\varepsilon\leq\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\leq\pi,

where
\varepsilon
and
\pi
denote the injective and
projective norms on
E\otimes F,
respectively.

We denote by
\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{\lambda_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}F
the
completed of
\left(\otimes_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\otimes F
for the norm
\lambda_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}
.

Now, the main result of this section is the following identification.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}.
We have the following isometric identification

\mathcal{P}_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left({}^{m}E;F\right)=(\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}}F^{\ast})^{\ast}.

Proof.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left({}^{m}E;F\right).
We
define a linear functional on
\left(\otimes_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\otimes F^{\ast}
by

\Psi_{P}\left(u\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle P\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle,

where
u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i}^{\ast}
. Then, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\left|\Psi_{P}\left(u\right)\right|=\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle P\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|\leq\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle P\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
P
is positive weakly
\left(q,r\right)
-dominated, we get

\left|\Psi_{P}\left(u\right)\right|\leq d_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}(P)\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\sup_{\phi\in B_{\mathcal{P}^{r}\left({}^{m}E\right)}^{+}}\left\|\left(\phi\left(x_{i}\right)\right)\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{n}}\left\|\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F^{\ast}\right)}.

Hence, as
u
is arbitrary,
\Psi_{P}
is
\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}
-continuous on
\left(\otimes_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\otimes F^{\ast},
and extends continuously to the completed
tensor product
\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}}F^{\ast}
with

\left\|\Psi_{P}\right\|\leq d_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}(P).

Conversely, let
\Psi\in(\left(\widehat{\otimes}_{s,\left|\pi\right|}^{m}E\right)\widehat{\otimes}_{\lambda_{\left(q;r\right)}^{m+}}F^{\ast})^{\ast}
. We consider the mapping
B(\Psi)
defined by

B(\Psi)\left(x\right)\left(y^{\ast}\right)=\Psi\left(x\otimes\overset{\left(m\right)}{\cdots}\otimes x\otimes y^{\ast}\right)

It is clear that
B(\Psi)\in\mathcal{P}\left({}^{m}E;F\right).
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E,
and
y_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,y_{n}^{\ast}\in F^{\ast}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle B(\Psi)\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\Psi(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes\overset{\left(m\right)}{\cdots}\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi\right\|\lambda_{\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes\overset{\left(m\right)}{\cdots}\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i}^{\ast}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\left\langle B(\Psi)\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}=\sup_{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\leq 1}(\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\left\langle B(\Psi)\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\leq 1}(\left|\Psi(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}\otimes\overset{\left(m\right)}{\cdots}\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i}^{\ast})\right|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\leq 1}\left\|\Psi\right\|\left\|\left(\lambda_{i}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\ast}}^{n}}\sup_{\phi\in B_{\mathcal{P}^{r}\left({}^{m}E\right)}^{+}}\left\|\left(\phi\left(x_{i}\right)\right)\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{n}}\left\|\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F^{\ast}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\Psi\right\|\sup_{\phi\in B_{\mathcal{P}^{r}\left({}^{m}E\right)}^{+}}\left\|\left(\phi\left(x_{i}\right)\right)\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{n}}\left\|\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{r,\left|w\right|}^{n}\left(F^{\ast}\right)}.

Hence,
B(\Psi)
is positive weakly
\left(q,r\right)
-dominted and

d_{w,\left(q,r\right)}^{m+}\left(B(\Psi)\right)\leq\left\|\Psi\right\|.

∎

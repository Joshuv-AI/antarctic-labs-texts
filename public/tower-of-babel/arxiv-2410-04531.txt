On the K-theory of magnetic algebras: Iwatsuka case
Giuseppe De Nittis, Jaime Gomez, Danilo polo Ojito

Abstract
. In the tight-binding approximation, an Iwatsuka magnetic field is modeled by a function on ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} with constant, but distinct values in the two parts of the lattice separated by a straight line of slope α ∈ [ − ∞ , ∞ ] \alpha\in[-\infty,\infty] . In this paper, the K K -theory of the magnetic C ∗ C^{*} -algebras generated by an Iwatsuka magnetic field for any possible α \alpha is computed. One interesting aspect concerns the analysis of the behavior of the system in the transition from rational to irrational α \alpha . It turns out that when α \alpha is irrational, the magnetic hull associated with the flux operator forms a Cantor set. On the other hand, for rational α \alpha this set coincides with the two-point compactification of ℤ \mathbb{Z} . This characterization, along with the use of the Pimsner-Voiculescu exact sequence, is the main ingredient for the computation of the K K -theory. Once the K K -theory is known, with the use of the index theory one can deduce the bulk-interface correspondence for tight-binding Hamiltonians subjected to an Iwatsuka magnetic field. Notably, it occurs that the topological quantization of the interface currents remains independent of the slope α \alpha . MSC 2010 : Primary: 81R60 ; Secondary: 46L80, 81R60, 19K56. Keywords : Magnetic algebras, Iwatsuka magnetic field, bulk-interface correspondence, K K -theory.



1.
Introduction

In the tight-binding approximation, a generalized Iwatsuka magnetic field on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is a function
B_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
which takes constant values
b_{\pm}
in the two regions separated by the
interface
described by the straight line
y=\alpha x
of
slope

\alpha
. The standard Iwatsuka model is recovered when
\alpha=\pm\infty,
meaning the dividing line is
x=0

[
IWA
]
. In this paper, we investigate the topological property of the Iwatsuka model for any possible
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=[-\infty,+\infty]

with a special interest in the analysis of the behavior of the system in the transition from rational to irrational
\alpha
.

The system of interest will be described in terms of the

C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}))
generated by magnetic translations
\mathfrak{s}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{2}
associated to the Iwatsuka magnetic field and subjected to the following commutation relation

\big(\mathfrak{s}_{1}\mathfrak{s}_{2}\mathfrak{s}_{1}^{*}\mathfrak{s}_{2}^{*}\psi\big)(n)\;=\;\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,B_{\alpha}(n)}\,\psi(n),\qquad\forall\,\psi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\;.

We will refer to
\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
as the
Iwatsuka algebra
for short.
In order to compute the
K
-groups of
\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
the first step consists is noticing that the algebra can be decomposed in terms of crossed products,
i. e.

\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}\;\simeq\;\big(C(\Omega_{\alpha})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}\big)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}

where
\Omega_{\alpha}
, the so called
magnetic hull

[
DG1
,
DAN
]
, is a suitable compact Hausdorff space equipped with a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action
\sigma^{*}
. Our first main result provides a precise description of the topology of
\Omega_{\alpha}
and all its invariant measures.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
the dynamical system
(\Omega_{\alpha},\sigma^{*},\mathbb{Z}^{2})
has only three invariant measures
\big\{\mu_{\alpha},\mathbb{P}_{+},\mathbb{P}_{-}\big\}
, up to a scaling factor. Moreover,

i.

\Omega_{\alpha}
is homeomorphic to the two-point compactification of
\mathbb{Z}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\pm\infty\}.

ii.

\Omega_{\alpha}
is a Cantor set for
\alpha
irrational.

Sections
3.2
and
3.3
are devoted to the proof of this result, where an explicit description of the elements of the magnetic hull and the measures is provided. More precisely the arguments for the proof of Theorem
1.1
are contained in Propositions
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.8
and
3.11
.

The main implication of Theorem
1.1
is the existence of a set of three invariant traces
\{\mathcal{T}_{\alpha},\mathcal{T}_{+},\mathcal{T}_{-}\}
on
\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
obtained by the composition of the invariant measures with the conditional expectation
E\colon\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}\to C(\Omega_{\alpha})
(
cf
.
[
DAV
, Chapter VIII]
). From the physical side,
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
is an infinite trace that provides the expectation value of extended observables supported near the interface. The other two traces
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
are finite traces per-unit-volume related with observables supported away from the interface in the two opposite directions.

The main ingredient in describing the topology of
\Omega_{\alpha}
is the observation that this space is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-homeomorphic to some
subshift space
of
{\bf\Omega}:=\{b_{+},b_{-}\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
. For
\alpha
irrational it holds true that
\Omega_{\alpha}
is a closed subset of
{\bf\Omega}
without isolated points. On the other hand, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
the space
\Omega_{\alpha}
is a countable set with two accumulation points. It is important to highlight that the characterization of the irrational case is the truly new result of this work since the rational can be inferred (with a little effort) from the previous work
[
DG1
]
. With this information in mind, the Pimsner-Voicolescu exact sequence
[
PIM
]
allows us to compute the
K
-theory
\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
. This is the second main result of this work:

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
the following holds

K_{0}(\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha})\;=\;\mathbb{Z}^{3}\;,\qquad K_{1}(\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha})\;=\;\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}^{3}&\text{if}\;\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\pm\infty\}\\
\mathbb{Z}^{2}&\text{\rm otherwise\;.}\end{cases}

The proof of this Theorem is given in Propositions
3.14
and
3.16
, where the generators of these groups are also included. It should be mentioned that the
K
-groups for the case
\alpha=\pm\infty
were already computed in
[
DG1
]
.

The
K
-theory of the magnetic
C^{*}
-algebras, like the Iwatsuka
C^{*}
-algebra, has been a widely studied topic in recent years due to its applications to topological effect in transport phenomena like the integer quantum Hall effect (IQHE)
[
SH1
,
KRS2
,
KRS1
]
and the quantized edge currents generated by magnetic interfaces
[
DAN
,
DG1
,
KSV
]
.
In this work, as a concrete application of Theorem
1.2
, we study the
topological quantization of the edge currents along the magnetic barriers induced by
B_{\alpha}
.
It is known that in the standard Iwatsuka model (
\alpha=\pm\infty
) when
b_{+}\neq b_{-}
may exist extended states localized near the interface carrying currents. Moreover, such currents are quantized, similar to the case of the IQHE, by an integer which is obtained as the difference of two Chern numbers associated with the constant magnetic fields
b_{\pm}
. It is worth observing that this is a purely magnetic phenomenon since the magnetic field is what generates the interface while the material remains the same (infinitely extended and without boundaries). From the mathematical point of view, there is quite an extensive literature where the localization, existence, and quantization of interface currents have been proven for the one-particle sector
[
DGR
,
KSV
,
HS
,
DG1
]
(see also
[
DAN
,
DZ1
,
DZ2
,
GV
]
for non-straight interfaces). By following the
K
-theoretic framework constructed in
[
DG1
]
, we prove the quantization of the interface currents for the generalized Iwatsuka magnetic field
B_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. We start by constructing an exact sequence of
C^{*}
-algebras

(1.1)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{I}_{\alpha}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\rm ev}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{A}_{{\rm bulk}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\;,}

where
\mathfrak{I}_{\alpha}
is the interface algebra describing the behavior near the interface, and the bulk algebra
\mathfrak{A}_{\rm bulk}:=\mathfrak{A}_{b_{+}}\oplus\mathfrak{A}_{b_{-}}
contains the information of the asymptotic magnetic field. The full Hamiltonian of the system is a selfadjoint element
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
, and the bulk Hamiltonian is described by
\mathfrak{h}:=(\mathfrak{h}_{+},\mathfrak{h}_{-})={\rm ev}(\hat{\mathfrak{h}})\in\mathfrak{A}_{\rm bulk}
. If we assume that there is a compact set
\Delta
contained in a non-trivial spectral gap of the bulk Hamiltonian
\mathfrak{h}
(
bulk gap assumption
), then for any
\mu\in\Delta
the
Fermi projection

\mathfrak{p}_{\mu}\;:=\;\big(\mathfrak{p}_{\mu_{+}},\mathfrak{p}_{\mu_{-}}\big)\;=\;\big(\,\chi_{(-\infty,\mu]}(\mathfrak{h}_{+})\,,\,\chi_{(-\infty,\mu]}(\mathfrak{h}_{-})\,\big)\;\in\;\mathfrak{A}_{\rm bulk}

defines an element in
[\mathfrak{p}_{\mu}]\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{A}_{\rm bulk})
. The bulk magnetic invariants of the system can be written in terms of the Chern numbers of the projections,
i. e.
\sigma_{b_{\pm}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mu}):=\frac{e^{2}}{h}{\rm Ch}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mu_{\pm}})\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
e>0
is the magnitude of the electron charge and
h
is the Planck’s constant.

Let
\sigma(\Delta)
be the conductance associated with the interface current carried by the extended states of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
with energy inside of
\Delta.
Our third main result states that the interface conductance can be written as a difference of the bulk magnetic invariants of the system.

Theorem 1.3

(Bulk-interface correspondence)
.

Let the bulk gap assumption be valid and
\mu\in\Delta
. The interface conductance
\sigma(\Delta)
carried by the states of energy inside
\Delta
is given by

\sigma(\Delta)\;=\;\sigma_{b_{+}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mu})-\sigma_{b_{-}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\mu})

The relevance of Theorem
1.3
is its independence of the values of
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. A precise statement of this result with a detailed proof is presented in Theorem
4.5
.

The result above offers a not-innocent generalization of the bulk-interface correspondence established in
[
DG1
,
DGR
,
KSV
]
. The major innovation here lies in the treatment of interface currents at irrational slope. In fact, in the irrational case, the loss of symmetry along the magnetic interface made complicated the definition of the trace per unit of surface of the interface, and in turn one ends in the challenge of providing a good calculation of the expected value of observables near the interface.
This fact was already noted in the context of geometric defects in materials in
[
GUO
, Section 5.1]
. The present work, not only establishes the existence of a good interface trace. It also shows the uniqueness of such trace (Proposition
3.13
). Furthermore, this trace provides the correct
1
-cocycle which
computes the interface currents when paired with a suitable
K
-class. This discovery, along with the techniques introduced here, paves the way for extending well-known results on bulk-edge correspondence to settings where symmetry along the defect is lost.

It is important to point out that there are a few works dealing with materials with irrational straight boundaries. For instance, in the monograph
[
SBT
]
is considered the so-called
smooth Toeplitz extension

[
LES
,
JI
,
JK
]
to deal with operators in half-plane spaces with an arbitrary angle. This “smooth approximation” means that the usual half-plane
C^{*}
-algebra is replaced by a new
C^{*}
-algebra obtained by a “smooth” approximation of the half-plane (Toeplitz) projection.
In this setting, the authors construct the trace as the dual of the bulk-trace, and the bulk-edge correspondence is obtained from the Connes-Tom isomorphism and the duality of Chern cocycles. However, without this smooth approximation, the trace in
[
SBT
]
cannot be defined in the same way since the duality is lost. It is worth pointing out that
our strategy is independent of such an approximation procedure. Moreover, it can be verified that our trace is directly related to the trace in
[
SBT
]
as shown in
Appendix
A
. Let us end by observing that significant contributions are also contained in
[
LT
]
where the coarse geometry is used to establish a version of the bulk-edge correspondence for a broad class of geometries, including those coarsely equivalent to straight-line irrational defects. However, it is difficult, or at least not immediately clear, how this approach could be used to derive the equivalent of Theorem
1.3
. One main problem is related to the fact that Roe
C*
-algebras are insensitive to the twists generated by magnetic fields (see
e. g.
[
EM
, Section 2.1]
). Moreover, the numerical invariants are not well defined in the Roe
C^{*}
-algebras in view of the lack of a nice trace.

Magnetic algebras

2.1.
Algebra of the magnetic translations

In this section, we introduce the magnetic algebras following the presentation in the previous works
[
DG1
,
DAN
]
.

For two-dimensional systems in the tight-binding approximation, the
magnetic field
orthogonal to the plane is represented by a function
B\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and the corresponding
vector potential
by a function
A_{B}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
whose circulation is equal to
B
. Namely,
B(n)=\mathfrak{C}[A_{B}](n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where

\begin{split}\mathfrak{C}[A_{B}](n)\;:=\;&A_{B}(n,n-e_{1})+A_{B}(n-e_{1},n-e_{1}-e_{2})\\
&+A_{B}(n-e_{1}-e_{2},n-e_{2})+A_{B}(n-e_{2},n)\;.\end{split}

Along this work
e_{1}:=(1,0)
and
e_{2}:=(0,1)
denote the canonical basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The
standard
vector potential associated with the magnetic field
B
is given by

(2.1)

A_{B}(n,n-e_{j})\;:=\;\delta_{j,1}\left(\delta_{n_{2}>0}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{2}}B(n_{1},m)-\delta_{n_{2}<0}\sum_{m=0}^{|n_{2}|-1}B(n_{1},-m)\right)\;,

where
n=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Remark 2.1
.

When the magnetic field is independent of the
y
-axis one obtains that

A_{B}(n,n-e_{j})\;=\;\delta_{j,1}n_{2}B(n)\;,

that is,

is a
Landau-type
vector potential
[

, Section 2.2]
.

The magnetic translations
\mathfrak{s}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{2}
associated to the magnetic potential

\begin{split}(\mathfrak{s}_{1}\psi)(n)\;&:=\;\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,A_{B}(n,n-e_{1})}\,\psi(n-e_{1})\;,\\
(\mathfrak{s}_{2}\psi)(n)\;&:=\;\psi(n-e_{2})\;.\end{split}

In this paper we will identify the group
\mathbb{U}(1)
of the complex numbers of modulus one with the one-dimensional torus

.
Let

be the function

(2.3)

f_{B}(n)\;:=\;\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,B(n)}\,\;,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},

which provides the exponential of the
magnetic flux
through the unit cell sited in

(\mathfrak{f}_{B}\psi)(n)\;:=\;f_{B}(n)\psi(n)\;,\qquad\psi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\;.

A straightforward computation shows that the magnetic translations
satisfy the condition

(2.4)

\mathfrak{s}_{1}\;\mathfrak{s}_{2}\;=\;\mathfrak{f}_{B}\;\mathfrak{s}_{2}\;\mathfrak{s}_{1}\;.

The

. There is also a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action given by

(2.14)

\sigma^{\prime}_{\gamma}(x)(n)\;:=\;x(n+\gamma)\;,\qquad\gamma,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

for all
x\in{\bf\Omega}
(see for instance
[
CC
]
).
Explicitly, it is possible to give a metric compatible with the topology as follows. For
x_{1},x_{2}\in{\bf\Omega}
one sets

(2.15)

\displaystyle d\big(x_{1},x_{2}\big)\;:=\;\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{s_{i}(x_{1},x_{2})}{2^{i+1}},\;\qquad\;s_{i}(x_{1},x_{2})\;=\;\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{ if }x_{1}|_{[-i,i]^{2}}=x_{2}|_{[-i,i]^{2}}\\
1,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
[-i,i]^{2}
is meant as the finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
made of points contained in the square centered at zero with sides
2i
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Notice that
f_{B}
, defined in (
2.3
), lies in
{\bf\Omega}
and hence one can consider the associated
sub-shift

\Omega^{\prime}_{B}\subset{\bf\Omega}
generated by
f_{B}
, that is,
\Omega^{\prime}_{B}:=\overline{\mathtt{Orb}(f_{B})}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
{\rm Im}(B)=\{b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{m}\}
then there exists a conjugacy between the dynamical systems
(\Omega_{B},\sigma^{*},\mathbb{Z}^{2})
and
(\Omega^{\prime}_{B},\sigma^{\prime},\mathbb{Z}^{2})
,
i. e.
, an homeomorphism
T:\Omega_{B}\to\Omega_{B}^{\prime}
such that
T(\sigma^{*}_{n}(\omega))=\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(T(\omega))
for each
\omega\in\Omega_{B}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let us choose an enumeration
\{\mathfrak{f}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of the set

\{\sigma_{n}(\mathfrak{f}_{B})\;|\;n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}
so that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
the subset

\{\sigma_{n}(\mathfrak{f}_{B})\mid n\in[-m,m]^{2}\}\;=\;\{\mathfrak{f}_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq(2m+1)^{2}\}

consists of the “first”
(2m+1)^{2}
elements.
Define the map
T\colon\Omega_{B}\to{\bf\Omega}
by
T(\omega)(n)=\omega(\sigma_{-n}(\mathfrak{f}_{B}))
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
\omega\in\Omega_{B}
. Notice that

(2.16)

T(\omega_{0})(n)\;=\;\omega_{0}(\sigma_{-n}(\mathfrak{f}_{B}))\;=\;f_{B}(n),

so in view of (
2.11
) one gets that
T
is well defined.
Let us show that
T
is continuous.
Let
\varepsilon>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\frac{1}{2^{m}}\leq\varepsilon
.
Consider
{\delta=\frac{1}{2^{(2m+1)^{2}}}}
.
If
\overline{d}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})<\delta
, we obtain that
\omega_{1}(\mathfrak{f}_{i})=\omega_{2}(\mathfrak{f}_{i})
for every
{1\leq i\leq(2m+1)^{2}}
and this leads

T(\omega_{1})(n)\;=\;\omega_{1}(\sigma_{-n}(\mathfrak{f}_{B}))\;=\;\omega_{2}(\sigma_{-n}(\mathfrak{f}_{B}))\;=\;T(\omega_{2})(n),

for every
n\in[-m,m]^{2}
. Consequently,
d\big(T(\omega_{1}),T(\omega_{2}))<\frac{1}{2^{m}}\leq\varepsilon
and one concludes that
T
is continuous.
The map
T
is injective since the relation
T(\omega_{1})=T(\omega_{2})
implies that
\omega_{1}(\sigma_{-n}(\mathfrak{f}_{B}))=\omega_{2}(\sigma_{-n}(\mathfrak{f}_{B}))
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Moreover, for
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
one has

\displaystyle T\big(\sigma^{*}_{n}(\omega)\big)(s)\;

\displaystyle=\;\omega\big(\sigma_{-n-s}(\mathfrak{f}_{B})\big)\;=\;T(\omega)(n+s)\;=\;\sigma^{\prime}_{n}(T(\omega))(s).

and in turn
T
is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-equivariant.
Since (
2.16
) says that
T(\omega_{0})=f_{B}
, one infers from the continuity and the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-equivariance that
T(\Omega_{B})=\Omega^{\prime}_{B}

i. e.
, that
T
is surjective.
Since
T
is a continuous bijection between compact and Hausdorff spaces, it turns out
that
T
is indeed a homeomorphism.
∎

3.
Structure and
K
-theory of the Iwatsuka algebras

This section is devoted to the study of magnetic algebras associated with Iwatsuka-like magnetic fields. We compute the magnetic hull and the K-theory for such
C^{*}
-algebras.

3.1.
Iwatsuka algebras

Let
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=[-\infty,+\infty]
be the extended real line (two points compactification) and
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. The
Iwatsuka magnetic
field
B_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(3.1)

{B_{\alpha}}(n)\;:=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}b_{+}&&-\alpha n_{1}+n_{2}>0\\
b_{-}&&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.\qquad{B_{\pm\infty}}(n)\;:=\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}b_{\mp}&&n_{1}>0\\
b_{\pm}&&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

where
b_{+},b_{-}\in\mathbb{R}
so that
b_{+}-b_{-}\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. The
Iwatsuka algebra

\mathfrak{A}_{\alpha}
is the
C^{*}
-algebra related to
B_{\alpha}
according to the Definition
2.2
. We shall use the notation
\mathfrak{f}_{\alpha}
,
\mathfrak{F}_{\alpha}
and
\Omega_{\alpha}
for all the objects associated to such
C^{*}
-algebra. Along this work, we say that
\alpha
is rational when
\alpha
is either a rational number or
\pm\infty
, and we say that
\alpha
is irrational otherwise.

3.2.
Iwatsuka magnetic hulls

In this section we characterize in detail the magnetic hull of the Iwatsuka
algebra with a special focus on the topology.

For every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
let us introduce the subgroup
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
of
\mathbb{R}
given by

\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}\;:=\;\left\{x^{\alpha}_{n}:=-\alpha n_{1}+n_{2}\;|\;n=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\right\}

The cases
\alpha=\pm\infty
are similar with
x^{\pm\infty}_{n}:=\mp n_{1}
. Observe that
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
labels the points of
\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{0})
via the map

(3.2)

\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{0})\ni\sigma_{n}^{*}(\omega_{0})\;\mapsto\;x_{n}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}

For
\alpha
rational this map is a homeomorphism since it is bijective and the group
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
and the set
\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{0})
are discrete spaces. Hence
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
determines the topology of
\Omega_{\alpha}
in view of (
2.11
). Nevertheless, for
\alpha
irrational this correspondence is no longer a homeomorphism although it remains continuous as we shall see in Proposition
3.6
.

Since
B_{\alpha}
meets the assumptions of Proposition
2.8
, we can use the identification
\omega_{0}\equiv f_{B}
in order to work inside the full shift space
{\bf\Omega}=\{\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,b_{+}}\,,\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,b_{-}}\,\}.
In the following, we pursue a detailed description of the elements and the topology of
\Omega_{\alpha}.
For that, consider
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and define the following elements in
{\bf\Omega}

(3.3)

\begin{split}\omega_{+}(n)\;&:=\;\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,b_{+}}\,,\qquad\omega_{x}(n)\;:=\;\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,b_{+}}\,,&\mbox{ if }\;x_{n}^{\alpha}-x\;>\;0,\\
\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,b_{-}}\,,&\mbox{ otherwise, }\end{cases}\\
\omega_{-}(n)\;&:=\;\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,b_{-}}\,,\qquad\omega_{\bullet}(n)\;:=\;\begin{cases}\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,b_{+}}\,,&\mbox{ if }\;x_{n}^{\alpha}\;\geq\;0,\\
\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,b_{-}}\,,&\mbox{ otherwise, }\end{cases}\end{split}

Remark 3.1
.

When
\alpha
is rational a simple exercise verifies that
\omega_{\bullet}
is actually an element in
\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{0})
. Indeed, if
\alpha=p/q
with
p
and
q
relatively prime with
q>0
and let
s
and
t
be integer numbers such that
1=-ps+qt.
It follows that
\omega_{0}\big(n+(s,t)\big)=\omega_{\bullet}(n)
because
(-p/q)(n_{1}+s)+n_{2}+t>0
if and only if
(-p/q)n_{1}+n_{2}\geq 0.
The case
\alpha=\pm\infty
is similar.
\blacktriangleleft

Proposition 3.2
.

It holds that
\omega_{\pm}\in\Omega_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Notice that for
\alpha\geq 0
the elements
\omega_{\pm}
can be obtained as a limit of the Cauchy sequences
\omega^{k}_{\pm}:=\sigma^{*}_{\pm ke_{2}}(\omega_{0})
, respectively. The case
\alpha<0
follows with the same argument by using the sequences
\omega^{k}_{\pm}:=\sigma^{*}_{\pm ke_{1}}(\omega_{0})
and so one gets that
\omega_{\pm}\in\Omega_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha
.
∎

Now we characterize the magnetic hull of the Iwatsuka algebra for any
\alpha
. We start with the rational case which is just an adaptation of
[
DG1
, Example 2.24]
which concerns the cases
\alpha=\pm\infty
. Let us point out that the symbol
\sqcup
is used in the following to denote the
disjoint
union. We will also use the short notation
\mathbb{Z}_{q}:=\mathbb{Z}/(q\mathbb{Z})
and the convention
\mathbb{Z}_{0}:=\{\ast\}
a singleton.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\alpha=p/q
with
p
and
q
relatively primes and
q\geq 0
. Then,
\Omega_{\alpha}
is a countable space and agrees with the two-point compactification of
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
i. e.

(3.4)

\Omega_{\alpha}\;=\;\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{\bullet})\sqcup\big\{\omega_{+},\omega_{-}\big\}\;\simeq\;(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q})\sqcup\big\{\pm\infty\big\}

Proof.

The map introduced in (
3.2
) and Remark
3.1
provide the following
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-equivariant homeomorphism

\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{\bullet})\;=\;\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{0})\;\simeq\;\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}\;.

For
\alpha=\pm\infty
or
\alpha=0
(in both cases one can assume
q=0
) one has that
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{Z}
and this case reduces to
[
DG1
, Example 2.24]
. If
\alpha=p/q
with
q>0
, then

\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}\simeq\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}
as a group via the map

\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}\ni-(p/q)n_{1}+n_{2}\;\mapsto\;\big(-pn_{1}^{\prime}+n_{2},[r]\big)

where
n_{1}=qn_{1}^{\prime}+r
with
0\leq r\leq q-1
.
Moreover,
the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action on
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
becomes the standard
\mathbb{Z}
-action by translation on the factor
\mathbb{Z}
. Thus one also concludes this case with
[
DG1
, Example 2.24]
and this yields that
\Omega_{\alpha}
is homeomorphic to the two-point compactification of
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
∎

Let us observe that
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}
becomes “larger” as
q
grows. This gives us a clue as to why
\Omega_{\alpha}
becomes uncountable for
\alpha
irrational as described in the next result.

Proposition 3.4
.

The compact Hausdorff space
\Omega_{\alpha}
is a Cantor set when
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
. An explicit description is

(3.5)

\Omega_{\alpha}\;=\;\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{\bullet})\sqcup\big\{\omega_{x}\big\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sqcup\big\{\omega_{+},\omega_{-}\big\}\;.

where the topology is the subspace topology induced from the full-shift space
{\bf\Omega}.

Proof.

Since
\Omega_{\alpha}
is a subset of the Cantor set
{\bf\Omega}
, then to conclude the proof it is enough to check the equality of sets and that all the elements in
\Omega_{\alpha}
are limit points. For that, let us first show that
\omega_{x}\in\Omega_{\alpha}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. If
x
lies in the dense subgroup
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
of
\mathbb{R}
it follows from its very definition that
x=x_{n}^{\alpha}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and this leads to
\omega_{x}=\sigma_{n}^{*}(\omega_{0})\in\Omega_{\alpha}
. Now if
x\notin\mathbb{X}_{\alpha},
there exists a sequence
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}x_{j}=x
. For
\epsilon>0
, consider
M\in\mathbb{N}
so that
\epsilon>2^{-(M+1)}
and define

(3.6)

\delta_{M}\;:=\;\min_{m\in[-M,M]^{2}}\big|x_{m}^{\alpha}-x\big|\;>\;0

One has
|x_{j}-x|<{\delta_{M}}
for
j
big enough.
Let
n(j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be such that
x_{j}\equiv x_{n(j)}^{\alpha}
.
We claim that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}_{n(j)}(\omega_{0})(m)\;=\;\omega_{x}(m)\;,\qquad\forall\,m\in[-M,M]^{2}

for
j
sufficiently large.
From its very definition, it follows that (
3.7
) is fulfilled whenever the numbers
x_{m-n(j)}^{\alpha}
and
x_{m}^{\alpha}-x
have the same sign. A direct computation provides

(3.8)

\displaystyle\big|x_{m-n(j)}^{\alpha}-(x_{m}^{\alpha}-x)\big|\;

\displaystyle=\;\big|x_{n(j)}^{\alpha}-x\big|\;=\;|x_{j}-x|\;<\;{\delta_{M}}\;

for
j
big enough and for every
m\in[-M,M]^{2}
. Let us now suppose that there exists a
m^{\prime}\in[-M,M]^{2}
such that
x_{m^{\prime}-n(j)}^{\alpha}
and
x_{m^{\prime}}^{\alpha}-x
have different signs. Then

\big|x_{m^{\prime}-n(j)}^{\alpha}-(x_{m^{\prime}}^{\alpha}-x)\big|\;=\;|x_{m^{\prime}-n(j)}^{\alpha}|+|x_{m^{\prime}}^{\alpha}-x|\;>\;\delta_{M}

which is in contradiction with (
3.8
). Therefore (
3.7
) is proved and one gets

d\big(\sigma^{*}_{n(j)}(\omega_{0}),\omega_{x}\big)\;<\;\frac{1}{2^{M+1}}\;<\;\epsilon

for
j
sufficiently large. This yields
\omega_{x}\in\overline{\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{0})}=\Omega_{\alpha}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. To verify that
\omega_{\bullet}\in\Omega_{\alpha}
, it is enough to check that the sequence
\{\omega_{-j^{-1}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Omega_{\alpha}
converges to
\omega_{\bullet}
when
j
goes to infinity. As a consequence, one also has that
\text{$\mathchar 525$}(\omega_{\bullet})\subset\Omega_{\alpha}
.
To sum up, one infers that
\{\omega_{x}\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\omega_{+},\omega_{-}\}\cup\text{$\mathchar 525$}(\omega_{\bullet})\subseteq\Omega_{\alpha}
.
Now let us verify the other inclusion. Let
\omega\in\Omega_{\alpha}\setminus\{\omega_{+},\omega_{-}\}
and choose a sequence
\{n(j)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
\{\sigma^{*}_{n(j)}(\omega_{0})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\omega
. Since
\omega
is not constant there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
so that
\omega(0,r+1)\neq\omega(0,r)
. Consider now
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M>r+1
, so there is
j_{M}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\omega|_{[-M,M]^{2}}=\sigma^{*}_{n(j)}(\omega_{0})|_{[-M,M]^{2}}
for
j\geq j_{M}
. Since
(0,r)
and
(0,r+1)
belong to
[-M,M]^{2}
then the definition of
\sigma^{*}_{n(j)}(\omega_{0})
with the above equality show that the numbers
x_{(0,r)-n(j)}^{\alpha}
and
x^{\alpha}_{(0,r+1)-n(j)}
have different signs, that is,
x_{(0,r+1)-n(j)}^{\alpha}>0
and
x_{(0,r)-n(j)}^{\alpha}\leq 0
.
Therefore one concludes that

x_{(0,r)}^{\alpha}\;\leq\;x_{n(j)}^{\alpha}\;<\;x_{(0,r+1)}^{\alpha}

So
\big\{x_{n(j)}^{\alpha}\big\}_{j\geq j_{M}}
is a bounded sequence in
\mathbb{R}
. By the Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem, we can assume that
x_{n(j)}^{\alpha}
is a monotone sequence that converges to some
x\in\mathbb{R}
as
j\to\infty
. Observe that when
x\notin\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
one can proceed as before and check that the sequence
\sigma_{n(j)}^{*}(\omega_{0})
converges to
\omega_{x}
. On the other hand, if
x=x_{n}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
and the sequence
x_{n(j)}^{\alpha}
is increasing or decreasing then
\sigma_{n(j)}^{*}(\omega_{0})
converges either to
\sigma_{n}^{*}(\omega_{0})
or
\sigma_{n}^{*}(\omega_{\bullet})
, respectively. By uniqueness of the limit
\omega\in\Omega_{\alpha}
in all the cases, one can conclude the other inclusion and (
3.5
). Finally,
\Omega_{\alpha}
is a Cantor set since it is a closed subset of
{\bf\Omega}
without isolated points. ∎

Remark 3.5
.

When
\alpha
is irrational a base for the topology of
\Omega_{\alpha}
is given by the collection
\mathfrak{B}
of sets of the form
Z(\beta)\cap\Omega_{\alpha}

where the

’s are defined in (

). In terms of the metric (

describes

and center in

.

\blacktriangleleft

3.3.
Invariant measures on the magnetic hulls

In this section we compute all the invariant measures of the magnetic hull of the Iwatsuka algebra.

There is a natural action of
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{R}
by translations:

(3.9)

\zeta_{\gamma}(x)\;:=\;x+\gamma\;,\qquad\gamma\in\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}

This action could be extended to
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
by letting the points
\pm\infty
invariants. It turns out that the map given in (
3.2
) can be continuously extend to a map
\pi:\Omega_{\alpha}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
which is equivariant with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-action
\sigma^{*}
on
\Omega_{\alpha}
and the
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
-action
\zeta
on
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. This fact is almost immediate for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
in view of the discreteness of
\Omega_{\alpha}
proved in Proposition
3.3
.
The irrational case, based on the description of
\Omega_{\alpha}
provided in Proposition
3.4
, is more technical:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
.
The map
\pi\colon\Omega_{\alpha}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by

(3.10)

\begin{split}\pi(\omega_{x})\;=\;x,\qquad\pi\big(\sigma^{*}_{n}(\omega_{\bullet})\big)\;=\;x_{n}^{\alpha},\qquad\pi(\omega_{\pm})\;=\;\pm\infty\ \end{split}

is continuous, surjective, and meets the following equivariance condition

(3.11)

\pi\big(\sigma_{n}^{*}(\omega)\big)\;=\;\zeta_{x_{n}^{\alpha}}\big(\pi(\omega)\big)\;=\;\pi(\omega)+x_{n}^{\alpha}\;,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\;\omega\in\Omega_{\alpha}\;.

Proof.

It is a matter of straightforward computations to check that
\pi
is continuous on
\omega_{\pm}
. So let us focus on the remaining points. The idea is to use sequential continuity which is equivalent on these spaces. Let us start with a convergent sequence of the form
\omega_{x_{j}}\to\omega
with
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\omega=\omega_{x}
for some
x\notin\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
consider
\delta_{M}>0
defined in (
3.6
) for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
. In view of
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}
one gets that
\delta_{M}\to 0
as
M
goes to infinity.
Moreover, by the definition of the convergence of
\omega_{x_{j}}
, for any
M
there exists
j
big enough such that the numbers
x_{n}^{\alpha}-x_{j}
and
x_{n}^{\alpha}-x
have the same sign for every
n
in the square
[-M,M]^{2}
.
As a consequence,
|x_{j}-x|<\delta_{M}
for
j
big enough and this leads to
\pi(\omega_{x_{j}})\to\pi(\omega_{x}).
Meanwhile if the limit point
\omega
is either
\omega_{x}
with
x=x_{m}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}
or
\omega=\sigma_{m}^{*}(\omega_{\bullet}),
we can proceed as before by taking in this case

\hat{\delta}_{M}\;=\;\min_{n\in[-M,M]^{2}\setminus\{m\}}\big|x_{n}^{\alpha}-x\big|\;>\;0

and arrives in
|x_{j}-x|<\hat{\delta}_{M}\to 0
.
Now if the sequence is contained in
\mathtt{Orb}(\omega_{\bullet})
, that is, a sequence of the form
\sigma_{n(j)}^{*}(\omega_{\bullet})
with
n(j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, then the same analysis also applies for this case.
To sum up, if
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is sequence in
\Omega_{\alpha}
that converges to
\omega
, then
\pi(\omega_{j})\to\pi(\omega)
as
j\to\infty.
This concludes the continuity of
\pi.

The surjectivity comes from the definition of
\pi.
Finally, one has the relation

\pi\big(\sigma_{n}^{*}(\omega_{x})\big))\;=\;\pi(\omega_{x+x_{n}^{\alpha}})\;=\;x+x_{n}^{\alpha}\;=\;\zeta_{x_{n}^{\alpha}}\big(\pi(\omega_{x})\big)

which implies the equivariance (
3.11
) since the other cases follow from its very definition.
∎

Let us define the
interface Hull
as the open invariant subset
\Omega_{\alpha}^{\circ}:=\Omega_{\alpha}\setminus\{\omega_{\pm}\}
. Proposition
3.3
implies that for

of

-algebras induced by (the inverse of) the map (

). When

is irrational Proposition

provides the following embedding of

-algebras

(3.12)

\textstyle{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hat{\pi}}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{C(\overline{\mathbb{R}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hat{\pi}}
\textstyle{C_{0}(\Omega_{\alpha}^{\circ})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

with

Remark 3.7
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}
the dynamical system
(\Omega_{\alpha},\mathbb{Z}^{2},\sigma^{*})
has only two ergodic probability measures
\mathbb{P}_{+}
and
\mathbb{P}_{-}
specified by
\mathbb{P}_{\pm}(\{\omega_{\pm}\})=1
.

Proof.

Since
\omega_{\pm}
are invariant points then it is not difficult to see that
\mathbb{P}_{\pm}
are ergodic measures on
\Omega_{\alpha}
. Now let us suppose that there exists another ergodic measure

On the other hand, for

, which is a probability measure on

∎

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
. Then,
\mu_{\alpha}
is a well-defined invariant regular measure on
\Omega_{\alpha}^{\circ}
.

Proof.

One has that

(3.14)

\mu_{\alpha}(\emptyset)\;=\;\lambda(\pi(\emptyset))\;=\;\lambda(\emptyset)\;=\;0\;.

From the definition of
\pi
, it follows

\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}\;=\;\left.\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;\right|\;\exists\;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega_{\alpha}^{\circ},\;\omega_{1}\neq\omega_{2}\;{\rm and}\;\pi(\omega_{1})=\pi(\omega_{2})=x\big\}

and moreover,

since this set is countable. Now let

The latter leads to
\mu_{\alpha}(A\cup B)=\mu_{\alpha}(A)+\mu_{\alpha}(B).
To verify the
\sigma
-additive, suppose
\{A_{i}\}
is a non-decreasing monotone family of Borel sets in
\Omega_{\alpha}^{\circ}.
Then
\{\pi(A_{i})\}
is a non-decreasing monotone family of Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}

\begin{split}\mu_{\alpha}\big(\bigcup_{i}A_{i}\big)\;&=\;\lambda\left(\pi\big(\bigcup_{i}A_{i}\big)\right)\;=\;\lambda\left(\bigcup_{i}\pi(A_{i})\right)\;=\;\lim_{i}\lambda\left(\pi(A_{i})\right)\\

is a Borel measure on
\Omega_{\alpha}^{\circ}
which is
\sigma^{*}
-invariant by Proposition
3.6

∎

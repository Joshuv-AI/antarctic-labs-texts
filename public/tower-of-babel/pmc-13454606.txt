Joint UAV trajectory and offloading optimization with robust secrecy for intelligent mining
Authors: Abdulbasit A Darem, Khalid Almalki, Abdulrahman Alzahrani, Khalid N R Alharbi, Asma A Alhashmi, Laith A Darem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntelligent mining demands real-time processing of UAV sensor streams under latency, safety, and energy constraints. We study a dual-edge architecture in which a ground base station (BS) and an aerial edge server (AES) collaboratively serve aerial users (FDMA), while a protective jammer UAV adapts its trajectory subject to speed/acceleration limits and rotary-wing propulsion. The system models 3GPP A2G channels (probabilistic LoS/NLoS and state-conditioned fading) and passive ground-eavesdroppers with bounded location uncertainty. We formulate a robust energy-efficiency maximization that epigraphs worst-case eavesdropper rates, introduces secrecy-QoS slack variables for feasibility, and enforces slot-level task causality. The fractional objective is handled via Dinkelbach, and three-block BCD solves the problem with conservative SCA surrogates; a micro-AO resolves the bi-convex throughput epigraph in the radio block, and the trajectory block uses affine secrecy bounds plus an SOC treatment of induced power in the rotary-wing model. Under 3GPP Urban Macro calibration, the proposed scheme attains 60.5 kbits/J at U=10, exceeding STRO by 22.2% and SHJ by 116.1%. With eavesdropper uncertainty m, energy efficiency degrades only 13.1%, whereas non-robust CENR collapses to 12.0 kbits/J (76.9% drop). The optimized jammer path is 1250 m (vs. 1131 m straight line; +10.5%) to secure stronger jamming geometry; propulsion dominates the energy budget at 4800 J (86.8% of total), while SLT despite saving 6.3% propulsion energy, incurs a 730.9% increase in RF jamming to 2742 J. The algorithm runs in 1.95 s/iteration on average (trajectory-frozen: 0.98 s/iteration), converges within 5–7 iterations, and captures 85% of its total gain in the first 3 iterations–validating near-real-time feasibility for secure, energy-efficient offloading in mining operations.Keywords: Aerial-edge computing, UAV trajectory optimization, Robust physical-layer secrecy, Cooperative jamming, Dinkelbach transform, Block coordinate descent, Successive convex approximation, Rotary-wing propulsion, FDMA, Intelligent miningSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The convergence of artificial intelligence, Mobile Edge Computing (MEC), and Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) is rapidly transforming industrial operations across multiple sectors1,2. Intelligent mining stands out as a particularly demanding application due to its reliance on continuous, high rate sensing and stringent latency requirements for safety critical decision making3. Aerial users in these environments generate computationally intensive tasks4, such as real-time geological analysis, hazard detection, and equipment monitoring, that must be executed under realistic Air-to-Ground (A2G) propagation conditions and limited onboard processing capabilities5,6. Specifically, these tasks involve heavy payloads such as high-resolution video streams, 3D LiDAR point clouds, and multispectral telemetry that require massive processing power (e.g., over 1000 Central Processing Unit (CPU) cycles/bit). Because mining UAVs have severely constrained batteries, and distant cloud servers violate the strict sub-second latency (Δ = 1 s) required for emergency hazard alerts, real-time edge offloading is functionally mandatory.

These operational demands introduce interconnected challenges that existing frameworks do not fully address. The radio environment follows complex 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP)- specified propagation characteristics, with probabilistic Line-of-Sight (LoS) and state-conditioned fading7,8. Furthermore, the wireless medium is vulnerable to passive ground eavesdroppers whose locations are only imperfectly known, directly threatening the confidentiality of offloaded operational data9,10. Simultaneously, UAV platforms must meticulously balance communication, computation, and mobility under strict aerodynamic propulsion limits and kinematic constraints11,12. While aerial edge computing offers a promising paradigm by offloading tasks to nearby ground-based and Aerial Edge Server (AES)13,14, no unified framework currently exists that jointly optimizes radio resource allocation, computation scheduling, UAV trajectory design15, and security against location uncertain eavesdroppers in a manner explicitly tailored to intelligent mining deployments.

Research questions

RQ1: Joint optimization. How can communication, computation, and UAV mobility be jointly optimized to maximize system Energy Efficiency (EE) under latency and processing limits?

RQ2: Robust secrecy. How can secure offloading be guaranteed against worst-case eavesdroppers with location uncertainty while maintaining optimization feasibility?

RQ3: Jammer trajectory design. How should a protective jammer UAV shape its trajectory to trade propulsion energy for improved jamming geometry and secrecy?

RQ4: Efficient solution method. How can the resulting highly non-convex fractional program coupling secrecy, power control, Frequency Division Multiple Access (FDMA) bandwidth, CPU scheduling, and UAV kinematics—be solved efficiently enough for near-real-time deployment?

RQ5: Architectural advantage. Under what conditions and workload regimes does a dual-edge architecture with both a ground Base Station (BS) and an AES outperform single-edge baselines in intelligent mining?

To answer these research questions, we develop a comprehensive optimization framework that captures the nonlinear interdependencies between secrecy, propulsion, radio resource allocation, and computation scheduling5,9. The proposed approach integrates a worst-case robust secrecy model with bounded eavesdropper uncertainty, enforces strict task causality constraints, and incorporates realistic UAV kinematics alongside rotary wing propulsion dynamics10,11. Through a novel combination of the Dinkelbach transformation, Block Coordinate Descent (BCD), and Successive Convex Approximation (SCA) with embedded micro Alternating Optimization (AO)14,16, we transform the intractable original problem into a sequence of efficiently solvable convex subproblems. Extensive simulations calibrated with 3GPP Urban Macro (UMa) parameters demonstrate that our framework achieves significant EE gains over multiple baselines, maintains robust performance under severe location uncertainty, and converges rapidly enough for near real-time implementation in dynamic mining environments17,18. The detailed literature review and technical contributions of this work are presented in the following sections.

Related work

The deployment of UAV in MEC networks has attracted significant research attention. Due to the inherent complexities of these networks, current literature primarily focuses on three intersecting domains: trajectory optimization and resource allocation, learning-based offloading frameworks, and Physical Layer Security (PLS) under eavesdropper uncertainty.

Trajectory optimization and task offloading in UAV-MEC

The joint optimization of UAV trajectories and computational offloading is fundamental to minimizing system latency and energy consumption. Early works formulated alternative optimization techniques for bit allocation and basic trajectory planning5. To handle broader spatial distributions, optimal transport theory19 and BCD paired with SCA18,20 have been widely applied to large-scale networks. Beyond traditional latency metrics, ensuring information freshness has driven the adoption of Age of Information (AoI) scheduling, which jointly optimizes UAV trajectories and localization accuracy using SCA-based methods21. Furthermore, for remote data collection lacking terrestrial infrastructure, satellite-assisted UAV frameworks have been formulated to maximize system energy efficiency by carefully balancing communication power allocation and trajectory tracking control22. Recent advances expand this paradigm by incorporating task caching and migration via Lyapunov optimization frameworks6, and adopting novel “computing while navigating” schemes leveraging particle swarm optimization to parallelize task execution12. Furthermore, in emergency scenarios, the introduction of Location Priority Levels (LPL) has enabled highly responsive bi-objective optimization for minimization of makespan and energy3.

To adapt to highly dynamic and non-convex MEC environments, researchers have increasingly adopted Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL). Standard DRL algorithms and Double Deep Q-learning (D-DQN) have been effectively utilized to schedule offloading ratios and design Three-Dimensional (3D) trajectories11,23, while Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) methods are applied to manage complex Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) task dependencies1. In hierarchical and multi-UAV edge systems, Optimistic Actor-Critic13 and multi-agent DRL17 networks address computation and communication uncertainties. To manage conflicting system objectives, evolutionary multi-objective reinforcement learning24–26 and game-theoretic models, such as Stackelberg games, have been proposed to optimize UAV utilities and dynamic pricing strategies2,27. As these highly dynamic UAV deployments scale to support the emerging low-altitude economy, researchers are increasingly proposing communication and control co-design strategies within three-layered resilient architectures to actively combat pervasive coverage holes, spectrum scarcity, and volatile wireless links28.

Physical layer security in the UAV communications

Due to the dominant LoS nature of the air-to-ground channels; the PLS has emerged as a crucial defense mechanism for the UAV networks7. The PLS has been analyzed under the various UAV mobility models; including the random waypoint model29, and extended to the integrated satellite-UAV networks30 as well as the authentication systems managing the jamming uncertainties31. To maximize the secrecy capacity; the modern PLS frameworks integrate the emerging physical layer technologies. These include the Intelligent Reflective Surfaces (IRS) to reconfigure the propagation environments32; the Non-Orthogonal Multiple Access (NOMA) for the Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (uRLLC)8,33,34, and the Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) structures utilizing the transmit antenna selection35. Additionally; the movable antenna technologies36 and the Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) architectures37 have been recently exploited to counter the eavesdroppers lacking the exact location information.

Secure UAV-MEC and the robust anti-eavesdropping

The intersection of the UAV-assisted MEC and the PLS introduces the severe optimization challenges; particularly when securing the computational offloading links against the malicious eavesdroppers. The cooperative jamming is a prevalent strategy wherein the base stations9,16 or the dedicated multi-role UAVs acting as the jammers14,38 emit the artificial noise (AN) to degrade the eavesdropper’s channel. Crucially; in the many practical scenarios, the exact locations of these eavesdroppers are unknown. Addressing this spatial uncertainty; the robust optimization formulations leveraging the worst-case bounds have been developed for the covert communications in the vehicular networks39,40; the multi-layer aerial-offshore networks utilizing the mobile jammer UAVs10, and the LEO satellite beamforming assisted by the UAV AN41. Despite these significant advancements; the existing literature lacks a unified analytical framework that jointly tackles the dynamic dual-edge MEC resource allocation; the robust worst-case secrecy constraints against the location-uncertain eavesdroppers, and the realistic aerodynamic UAV propulsion models. This paper bridges this gap by proposing a comprehensively coupled Dinkelbach-BCD optimization framework tailored for the highly secure; computation-intensive networks.

Motivation

As comprehensively detailed in Table 1, the existing literature exhibits several critical limitations that preclude the direct deployment in the highly secure and energy-constrained intelligent mining environments. First, the vast majority of the current MEC studies5,13,19 rely on the simplified kinetic models that fail to capture the highly nonlinear aerodynamic power consumption of the rotary wing UAVs. This fundamental oversight heavily skews the EE optimizations; because hovering and the forward flight require vastly different mechanical power expenditures. Second, while the PLS has gained significant traction; the prior frameworks9,14,16 overwhelmingly assume the perfect knowledge of the passive eavesdropper locations. In the practical mining sites, adversaries deliberately conceal their presence; making the certainty-equivalent network designs highly susceptible to the catastrophic information leaks.

The distinct absence of a unified mathematical framework that rigorously bounds the worst case secrecy while simultaneously optimizing the fractional EE under the strict rotary wing physics; serves as the primary motivation for this work. System model and problem formulation

We consider an offline setting where task parameters for all users and slots are known at the beginning of the horizon. Eavesdroppers are ground-based with zero altitude. The jammer is assumed to remain active throughout the slot. To ensure mathematical consistency with 3GPP standards, the small-scale fading is strictly state-conditioned (Rician for LoS and Rayleigh for Non-Line-of-Sight (NLoS)).

Conclusion

This work presented a realism-aware framework for secure aerial-edge computing in intelligent mining that jointly optimizes radio resources, computation scheduling, and a protective jammer UAV trajectory under 3GPP-calibrated A2G channels, bounded eavesdropper uncertainty, and rotary-wing propulsion physics. The robust secrecy formulation combines epigraphed worst-case eavesdropper rates with non-negative slack variables to preserve feasibility, while strict slot-level causality guarantees transmission-then-computation ordering. To solve the resulting fractional and highly coupled problem efficiently, we integrated Dinkelbach’s transform with a three-block BCD and conservative SCA surrogates, including a micro alternating-optimization within the FDMA radio block and an SOC representation for induced power in the aerodynamics model. Comprehensive evaluations in an Urban Macro setting demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms Straight-Line (SLT), Sequential (STRO), Non-Robust (CENR), and Static Hover (SHJ) baselines in system EE, maintains robustness against eavesdropper location uncertainty with modest degradation at m, and converges within practical computational budgets (sub-2 s average per iteration with rapid monotonic ascent), thereby supporting near-real-time deployment. Analyses further reveal that propulsion is the dominant energy component and show that geometry-aware jamming can suppress RF energy by positioning rather than solely by power. Future directions include: (i) online and learning-augmented variants for time-varying traffic and channels; (ii) multi-UAV cooperative jamming and distributed dual-edge orchestration; (iii) richer uncertainty sets (e.g., distributionally robust secrecy and partial Channel State Information (CSI)) and stochastic task arrivals; and (iv) integration with advanced physical-layer enablers (e.g., massive MIMO, IRS, or ISAC) and safety-critical field trials in operational mines. These avenues can further close the gap between theoretical guarantees and large-scale, secure, and energy-efficient industrial deployments.

Furthermore, while this paper establishes a theoretically rigorous mathematical optimization framework, exploring data-driven and learning-based techniques such as DRL to achieve faster, real-time trajectory and resource allocation in highly dynamic environments remains a highly promising direction for future work.

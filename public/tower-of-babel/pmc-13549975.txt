Introduction

Rare tumour risk syndromes (RTRS) are heritable conditions caused by known genetic defects, affecting 5 per 10,000 people or less, that confer a high lifetime risk of multiple, often early-onset cancers [1, 2]. Despite providing a tangible context for cancer prevention or early detection through intensive surveillance and prophylactic strategies [3], many healthcare systems opt for treatment of clinically expressed cancer, even when a positive genetic diagnosis is available [4]. Although national and international guidelines exist for carriers of RTRS-predisposing pathogenic variants (see glossary in Supplementary Information) [5], their impact on health outcomes and healthcare spending has not been systematically quantified [6, 7]. This gap reflects limited implementation of guideline-based care, lack of research on clinical outcomes, and lack of real-world cost-effectiveness evidence [3, 8].

The PREVENTABLE initiative (https://preventable.eu/; January 2023 to June 2026) was launched to address this gap. Funded by Horizon Europe (no. 101095483), it aims to generate robust, real-world evidence on the clinical, social and economic impact of preventing advanced cancer in RTRS patients across multiple European centres. Rare tumour risk syndromes

PREVENTABLE focuses on eight RTRS that, despite their differences, share common challenges (Fig. 2). Cancer may emerge in various organs, across the lifetime, recur in the same or other organs, and exhibit sex-specific patterns, complicating disease management. Consensus clinical guidelines and specialised RTRS clinical teams are therefore essential to ensure timely, effective, and personalised preventive care. The eight RTRS addressed in PREVENTABLE are: (i) Birt–Hogg–Dubé Syndrome (BHDS), (ii) Familial Malignant Melanoma (FMM), (iii) Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumour Syndrome (GIST), (iv) Hereditary Diffuse Gastric Cancer (HDGC), (v) Hereditary Leiomyomatosis and Renal Cell Carcinomas (HLRCC), (vi) Li-Fraumeni Syndrome (LFS), (vii) PTEN Hamartoma Tumour Syndrome (PHTS), and (viii) Peutz–Jeghers Syndrome (PJS). These RTRS fall within the remit of the European Reference Network (ERN) on Genetic Tumour Risk Syndromes (GENTURIS), granting access to the network’s clinical expertise and robust evidence. Shared features across PREVENTABLE RTRS

In all eight RTRS, pathogenic germline variants in specific genes increase the risk of developing cancer, and can be diagnosed through genetic testing, creating an opportunity for prevention and/or early detection and management [2, 3, 8]. These syndromes are all autosomal-dominant, resulting in familial clustering with a notable proportion of hereditary cases [2]. RTRS are characterised by earlier cancer onset compared to the sporadic cancer counterpart [8, 9]. Patients often face an increased risk of developing multiple primary cancers during their lifetime (e.g. LFS patients) [2, 8]. Each RTRS has specific cancer spectra, and some have histological specificities (e.g. DGC and LBC in HDGC) [10]. Some, like HLRCC, demonstrate high variability in disease severity and clinical outcomes, even within families [11]. Individuals with different RTRS but with high risk for the same cancer (e.g., female breast cancer in TP53, CDH1, PTEN, STK11 pathogenic variant carriers) may be offered the same risk-reduction/prophylactic surgery [12, 13]. By recognising shared features, healthcare teams and reference centres implement appropriate surveillance and management strategies, tailored to each individual’s genetic predisposition, ultimately improving outcomes and quality of life for individuals affected with RTRS [1, 2, 8].

Preventable: a study of cost-effectiveness based on real clinical data

Healthcare costs are a concern for health systems sustainability. Decision-makers must balance growing service demands with limited health budgets and users’ financial constraints [6]. As a result, an increasing number of public and private health systems use cost-effectiveness analysis to inform resource allocation decisions [14].

A limitation of published health-economics studies on RTRS is their failure to acknowledge the diversity of cancers and care pathways within and across patients with the same RTRS. Most studies address a specific cancer type [7, 15]. In addition, even among patients with the same syndrome, sex-specific differences exist, and the order or age of cancer onset can vary greatly, depending on the survival of prior cancers. To better understand real RTRS healthcare costs, PREVENTABLE leveraged the consortium’s clinical expertise to map individual care pathways covering virtually all RTRS patient trajectories, which may include the likelihood of several cancers affecting different organs across the lifetime of the same patient. These pathways were then refined with real clinical data from adult and paediatric patients, when applicable (Table 1). Data from cancer-free RTRS family members who tested negative were also included to assess the value of genetic testing to discharge individuals from lifelong surveillance. A dedicated IT tool (PREVENT-IT) was developed to systematically collect, standardise and register multicentric clinical data, following rigorous standards of data integrity and protection. PREVENT-IT ensures that retrospective data, collected from patients’ clinical records, are exclusively focused on procedures directly associated with their RTRS, and that all unrelated clinical procedures are excluded. This enables a highly accurate assessment of the clinical and financial impact of RTRS care pathways, framing the cost-effectiveness analysis through a patient-centred lens (Fig. 1). This technological innovation holds the potential to improve healthcare organisations not only for RTRS but also for other complex or rare diseases, benefitting from the analysis of standardised multicentric data. An intellectual property evaluation and an exploitation roadmap are in progress. Another limitation of current health economic studies is the use of single-source or single-country data, and direct medical costs obtained from the literature, i.e., predictive economic models [3, 7]. In contrast, PREVENTABLE uses data from multiple sources, including country-specific prices per syndrome, to enable direct comparisons of clinical care, rather than comparing asymmetric healthcare costs across countries. For that, PREVENT-IT automates the price indexing to clinical data, facilitating the comparison of prevention- and treatment-related care pathways, within each syndrome, and subsequent cost-effectiveness analysis.

Consortium synergy, multidisciplinary and integrated approach

PREVENTABLE fosters integrated collaboration among clinical, technological, economic and social-science teams. To ensure active participation from healthcare professionals — essential for collecting real-world clinical data — the project provides financial compensation tied to their involvement, acknowledging their contribution and lowering barriers to research engagement. IT teams also play a key role by developing and constantly adapting PREVENT-IT to the project’s evolving needs. The consortium unites experts from healthcare, biomedical research, health economics, social sciences, communication, IT, media, patient advocacy and branding. This multidisciplinary approach strengthens care pathways, supports evidence-based policymaking, and enhances patient visibility and outcomes across Europe.

Preventable impact and expected outputs

PREVENTABLE aims to fundamentally transform RTRS knowledge by offering innovative tools and approaches to collect clinical data and estimate real health costs with cancer prevention or early detection versus treatment. Central to this effort is the development of RTRS-specific pathways of care that reflect the diverse trajectories and clinical needs of each patient, and across patients. These pathways were validated and consolidated using a collaborative cohort of about 1000 RTRS families and 4000 relatives followed in nine European reference centres. This unprecedentedly large pan-European cohort was able to capture a broad and representative range of patient trajectories and obtain detailed knowledge on care pathways offered to RTRS patients in Europe. These data are now feeding novel cost-effectiveness models, providing invaluable resources for decision-makers and policymakers in healthcare.

Social and economic impact

According to the Global Cancer Observatory, about 4.5 million new cancers affect European adults and children per year. Ten per cent of all diagnosed cancers are predicted to be heritable, leading to healthcare spending of approximately €20 billion in Europe in 2018, only for hereditary cancer syndromes [2, 16]. Preventing cancer in relatives of RTRS carriers, identified through predictive genetic testing, can substantially reduce treatment costs [3]. Likewise, individuals testing negative can be safely discharged from lifelong surveillance, further decreasing unnecessary healthcare costs.

By prioritising surveillance, early intervention, and preventive care, PREVENTABLE aims to avert advanced cancers, cut treatment costs, and improve life expectancy and quality of life for at-risk individuals — thereby reducing long-term healthcare and economic burdens across Europe.

Cost-effectiveness models and healthcare system optimisation

Shifting from treatment to prevention in RTRS is expected to optimise resource allocation. Savings generated through regular surveillance of organs at risk, or timely risk-reduction measures, can be reinvested to improve healthcare infrastructures, enhance clinical outcomes, and ensure that healthcare professionals are better remunerated and incentivised. ERN GENTURIS centres, known for their specialisation in RTRS care, serve as key sites for implementation and knowledge transfer, ensuring that evidence generated in PREVENTABLE leads to improvements in clinical practices and policy decisions across Europe. Moreover, the project’s cost-effectiveness models will be directly applicable to healthcare systems, allowing stakeholders to make informed decisions on how to best allocate resources. By assessing healthcare spending differences between preventive and treatment measures, PREVENTABLE ambitions to present evidence-based recommendations that encourage sustainability while improving patient outcomes.

Communication guidelines for effective patient and professional engagement

A key component of PREVENTABLE is developing communication guidelines to support informed, person-centred decisions by clinicians and patients, and to improve communication within healthcare teams. This will ensure that both clinicians and patients understand the real impact of surveillance, early detection, risk reduction, prophylaxis, and personalised care for RTRS families. The project also analyses the factors influencing decision-making on cancer risk-reduction options by both healthcare professionals and RTRS patients.

A mixed-methods study (surveys and focus groups) is uncovering the barriers and facilitators to the referral and uptake of risk-reduction and care options, using a behavioural science approach. These activities count on the voluntary participation of healthcare professionals, adult patients and their social network. Hence, the guidelines for communication will take into account the recommendations of the clinical teams, patients’ preferences and perception of outcomes related to cancer risk.

Gender dimension

Sex and gender differences are relevant factors in RTRS prevention strategies. Biological sex influences cancer risk patterns and surveillance recommendations, while gender-related factors can affect access to and uptake of preventive care [17]. PREVENTABLE integrates a sex- and gender-sensitive approach, using inclusive pedigree nomenclature and exploring how these factors intersect with genetic risk and healthcare behaviours, ensuring that recommendations address the needs of all individuals.

Addressing the variety of stakeholders

For an effective dissemination, PREVENTABLE has identified eight primary target groups, including: (i) Patients and Their Families, (ii) Hospital Managers, (iii) Healthcare Professionals, (iv) Policymakers, (v) Healthcare Institutions, (vi) Patients’ Associations, (vii) Research and Clinical Team, and (viii) Health Economic Experts (Fig. 1).

By involving these groups, the project creates a platform for multidisciplinary collaboration, ensuring that all aspects of healthcare are addressed. Patients’ associations will play a crucial role in raising awareness, advocating for patients’ needs, and supporting the communication of prevention strategies to the public.

Conclusion and outlook

PREVENTABLE addresses a critical gap in the evidence base for preventive care in RTRS. By generating real-world data on clinical outcomes and costs, it supports a shift from treatment-focused to prevention-focused healthcare for hereditary cancer syndromes. The initiative is expected to inform European health policies, optimise resource allocation, and improve outcomes for patients and their families. PREVENTABLE illustrates how collaborative, multidisciplinary efforts can provide actionable knowledge for sustainable and equitable healthcare systems. It also serves as a blueprint for wider use in other inherited conditions, ultimately helping shift European healthcare from reactive treatment to proactive, people-centred prevention and improved health equity.

Upper and lower bounds on the subgeometric convergence of adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo
Austin Brown, Jeffrey S. Rosenthal

Abstract
We investigate lower bounds on the subgeometric convergence of adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo under any adaptation strategy. In particular, we prove general lower bounds in total variation and on the weak convergence rate under general adaptation plans. If the adaptation diminishes sufficiently fast, we also develop comparable convergence rate upper bounds that are capable of approximately matching the convergence rate in the weak subgeometric lower bound. These results provide insight into the optimal design of adaptation strategies and also limitations on the convergence behavior of adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo. Applications to an adaptive unadjusted Langevin algorithm as well as adaptive Metropolis-Hastings with independent proposals and random-walk proposals are explored.

1
Introduction

Let
\pi
be a Borel probability measure on a Polish space
\mathcal{X}
.
Adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo
[
9
,
17
]
is a widely successful framework to simulate realizations from
\pi
when optimal tuning parameters for the Markov chain are not readily available.
The adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
is constructed from a family of Markov kernels indexed by a set of potential tuning parameters.
The discrete-time adaptive process first updates the tuning parameter
\Gamma_{t}|(\Gamma_{s},X_{s})_{0\leq s\leq t-1}
with an adaptation strategy utilizing previous history and next, updates
X_{t}|\Gamma_{t},X_{t-1}
using a Markov transition kernel.
The goal is for the adaptive process to “learn” optimal tuning parameters so that the marginal distribution of the random variable
X_{t}
produces a close approximation to the measure
\pi
.

With a large option for adaptation strategies, theoretical convergence rates of adaptive algorithms are less understood than for non-adaptive Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) where fixed tuning parameters are chosen carefully beforehand.
In particular, a theoretical understanding of the rate of convergence is essential in applications as it helps to ensure a stable and reliable Monte Carlo simulation.
However, adaptive MCMC can exhibit empirical performance superseding the performance of standard MCMC even though much of the theoretical understanding is lacking.
For example, adaptive MCMC is widely used to automatically learn the covariance in random-walk Metropolis-Hastings
[
9
]
, which is often difficult or impossible to choose optimally with only fixed tuning parameter choices.

The main contributions of this paper develop general subgeometric lower bounds in total variation and the weak convergence rate of adaptive MCMC paired with upper bounds under strong conditions on the rate at which adaptation diminishes.
Applications of the theory are demonstrated on an adaptive unadjusted Langevin algorithm, Metropolis-Hastings independence sampler, and an adaptive Metropolis-Hastings random-walk.
The lower bounds for convergence hold under arbitrary adaptation plans and serve as a measurement of the optimal convergence behavior for adaptive MCMC.
The techniques for obtaining these lower bounds are based on finding large discrepancies between the tail probabilities of the marginal adaptive process and the target measure
\pi
.
Since the convergence rate is determined by tail properties, this may guide further theoretical understanding of some modern adaptation strategies that restrict adaptation to compact sets
[
16
]
.
Convergence rate lower bounds can also be of practical use in applications to determine if an appropriate rate is achievable so that central limit theorems may hold
[
2
,
15
]
.

One barrier in developing lower bounds for adaptive MCMC is due to the non-Markovian, non-reversible nature of these processes and spectral analysis for reversible Markov processes is not directly available.
To the best of our knowledge, the lower bounds for weak convergence developed here are novel, even when applied to non-adapted Markov chains, and general total variation lower bounds have not yet been explored for adaptive MCMC.
In specific situations, adaptive random-walk algorithms have been shown to improve “local” behavior but fail to adapt to “global” properties of the target measure, such as the tail probabilities, and proven to experience poor convergence properties
[
19
]
.
Related research develops general lower bounds in total variation for Markov processes
[
10
, Theorem 3.6, Corollary 3.7]
.
More recently, this technique has also been extended to polynomial rate lower bounds in unbounded Wasserstein distances for some Markov processes
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
.
When the tail decay of the target measure is unavailable, lower bounds for Markov processes in total variation have recently been developed, but a precise computation of the constants is not available
[
4
]
.

In addition to lower bounds, we develop explicit quantitative subgeometric upper bounds in total variation that can match the lower bound rate if the adaptation diminishes sufficiently fast.
The condition required on the adaptation is similar to the well-known diminishing adaptation condition
[
17
]
often used for the asymptotic convergence of adaptive MCMC.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first subgeometric upper bound to quantify the mixing for adaptive MCMC in total variation.
In comparison, existing convergence results are asymptotic
[
3
]
, require strong assumptions for adaptive MCMC
[
1
,
2
]
, or develop central limit theorems through Poisson’s equation
[
15
]
.

The organization of this article is as follows.
Some preliminary definitions are presented in Section
2
and Section
3
first develops lower bounds in total variation for large classes of adaptation strategies and then extends these lower bounds to weak convergence when the state space is Euclidean.
A lower bound is shown on a concrete example for the adapted unadjusted Langevin algorithm.
Section
4
proves comparable upper bounds under diminishing conditions on the adaptation plans that are capable of approximately matching the lower bound rates.
Section
5
illustrates the lower bounds on a toy example with an adaptive Metropolis-Hastings independence sampler, and Section
6
applies the lower bounds to the popular adaptive random-walk Metropolis-Hastings.
Section
7
provides a final discussion on the results and future research directions.

2
Preliminaries

For two Borel probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\mathcal{X}
, let
\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)
be the set of all couplings consisting of Borel probability measures on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
satisfying
\Gamma(\cdot\times\mathcal{X})=\mu
and
\Gamma(\mathcal{X}\times\cdot)=\nu
. Denote then the total variation distance between
\mu
and
\nu
as the best probability of the off-diagonal over all possible couplings, that is,

\left\lVert\mu-\nu\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)}\xi(\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}:x\not=y\}).

Denote the min and max of
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
by
a\wedge b
and
a\vee b
respectively.
On a Polish space
(\mathcal{X},d)
where
d:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
is a metric, we denote the Wasserstein distance that metrizes the weak convergence of probability measures
[
6
, Theorem 11.3.3]

\mathcal{W}_{d\wedge 1}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\left[d(x,y)\wedge 1\right]\xi(dx,dy).

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Polish space and
\mathcal{Y}
be a Borel measurable space equipped with their Borel sigma-algebras
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Y})
respectively where
\mathcal{X}
is the state space and
\mathcal{Y}
is the space for tuning parameters.
We now define the adaptive process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t=0}^{\infty}
on
\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X}
using the filtration
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\mathcal{B}(\Gamma_{s},X_{s},0\leq s\leq t)
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}
define an adaptation plan which denotes the map
t\mapsto\mathcal{Q}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
where
\mathcal{Q}_{t}:(\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X})^{t}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{Y})\to[0,1]
is a Borel probability kernel.
The kernels
\mathcal{Q}_{t}
act on Borel functions
g:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
and Borel measures
\nu
on
(\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X})^{t}
with

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}_{t}g)(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{t-1},x_{t-1})=\int_{\mathcal{X}}g(\gamma_{t})\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{t-1},x_{t-1},d\gamma_{t})

\displaystyle(\nu\mathcal{Q}_{t})(\cdot)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{t-1},x_{t-1},\cdot)\nu(d\gamma_{0},dx_{0},\ldots,d\gamma_{t-1},dx_{t-1})

for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\gamma_{0},x_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{t-1},x_{t-1}\in(\mathcal{Y}\times\mathcal{X})^{t}
.
Initialized at fixed
x_{0},\gamma_{0}\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, the discrete-time adaptive process first updates the tuning parameter

\Gamma_{t}|(\Gamma_{s},X_{s})_{0\leq s\leq t-1}\sim\mathcal{Q}_{t}((\Gamma_{s},X_{s})_{0\leq s\leq t-1},\cdot)

using an adaptation plan.
Let
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
be a family of Borel Markov kernels where
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})\to[0,1]
for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
and for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
x,\gamma\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot)
is Borel measurable.
The Markov family acts on Borel functions
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
and Borel measures
\mu
on
\mathcal{X}
with

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}f)(x)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}f(y)\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,dy)

\displaystyle(\mu\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})(\cdot)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot)\mu(dx)

for all
(x,\gamma)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.
The process then updates the state space given the updated tuning parameters

X_{t}|\Gamma_{t},X_{t-1}\sim\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(X_{t-1},\cdot)

for
t\geq 1
using the Markov kernel.

3
Lower bounds on the convergence of adaptive MCMC

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
denote the set of all possible adaptation plans
\mathcal{Q}
that define the Borel kernels
\mathcal{Q}_{t}
updating the tuning parameters at every iteration time
t
.
For a chosen adaptive strategy
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
, we denote the marginal of the adaptive process at iteration time
t
by
X_{t}\sim\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}((\gamma_{0},x_{0}),\cdot)
.
We will develop conditions to lower bound the total variation over all feasible adaptation strategies, that is, to lower bound

\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot)-\pi\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}

for
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

The main tool will be a function prescribing a subgeometric rate defined implicitly as an inverse which we now define.
For concave functions
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
and
w_{0}\in[1,\infty)
, define

\displaystyle H_{w_{0},\varphi}(w)=\int_{w_{0}}^{w}\frac{dv}{\varphi(v)}

(1)

for all
w\geq w_{0}
.
The assumptions on
\varphi
imply it is non-decreasing and
H_{w_{0},\varphi}(\cdot)
is strictly increasing as well as the inverse
H^{-1}_{w_{0},\varphi}(\cdot)
exists.
Depending on the form of
\varphi
, the inverse function
H^{-1}_{w_{0},\varphi}(\cdot)
defines a polynomial, subgeometric, or geometric function increasing to infinity.

The first lower bound in total variation uses a technique extended from
[
10
, Corollary 3.7]
to adaptive MCMC over all adaptive strategies.

Theorem 1
.

Assume there is a Borel function
W:\mathcal{X}\to[1,\infty)
and constants
C\in(0,1]
and
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
where

\displaystyle\pi(W\geq r)\geq Cr^{-\kappa}

(2)

holds for all
r\geq 1
and there is an
\alpha>\kappa
and a concave function
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}W^{\alpha})(x)-W(x)^{\alpha}\leq\varphi(W(x)^{\alpha})

(3)

holds for all
(x,\gamma)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.
Then for all
t
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot)-\pi\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}\geq\frac{M}{\left(H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)\right)^{\frac{\kappa}{\alpha-\kappa}}}

where

\displaystyle M=C^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-\kappa}}\left[(\kappa/\alpha)^{\frac{\kappa}{\alpha-\kappa}}-(\kappa/\alpha)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-\kappa}}\right].

(4)

Proof.

Let
V(x)=W^{\alpha}(x)
, and let
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, so then we have

\mathbb{E}\left(V(X_{t+1})|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right)-V(X_{t})\leq\varphi(V(X_{t})).

Since
\mathbb{E}\left[V(X_{1})\right]-V(x_{0})\leq\varphi(V(x_{0}))
, then assume by induction for all
1\leq k\leq t-1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[V(X_{k+1})\right]-\mathbb{E}[V(X_{k})]\leq\varphi(\mathbb{E}[V(X_{k})])

and
\mathbb{E}[V(X_{k})]<\infty
.
By the induction hypothesis and Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V(X_{t+1})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[V(X_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(V(X_{t+1})|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right)-V(X_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\varphi[V(X_{t})]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(\mathbb{E}\left[V(X_{t})\right]).

(5)

The inverse function theorem implies the derivative

\frac{d}{ds}H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(s)=\varphi(H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(s)).

Since
H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(0)\geq V(x_{0})
, assume by induction
H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(k)\geq\mathbb{E}[V(X_{k})]
for all
k\leq t
.
Since
\varphi
is non-decreasing combined with the fundamental theorem of calculus and (
5
),

\displaystyle H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(t+1)=H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(t)+\int_{t}^{t+1}\varphi(H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(s))ds

\displaystyle\geq H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(t)+\varphi(H^{-1}_{V(x_{0}),\varphi}(t))

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[V(X_{t})\right]+\varphi(\mathbb{E}\left[V(X_{t})\right])

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[V(X_{t+1})\right].

By Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}(W(X_{t})\geq r)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[W(X_{t})^{\alpha}\right]}{r^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)}{r^{\alpha}}.

We choose
r^{\alpha-\kappa}=(\alpha/\kappa)\cdot H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)/C
so that
r\geq 1
and gives the lower bound

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot)-\pi\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}

\displaystyle\geq\pi(W\geq r)-\mathbb{P}(W(X_{t})\geq r)\geq\frac{C}{r^{\kappa}}-\frac{H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)}{r^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{M}{\left(H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)\right)^{\frac{\kappa}{\alpha-\kappa}}}.

∎

Assumption (
3
) of Theorem
1
requires the Markov family
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
to satisfy a simultaneous growth condition for some concave function
\varphi
.
We look at some concrete examples of concave functions that lead to common subgeometric convergence rates that have been explored previously for upper bounds
[
5
]
.

Example 2
.

(Polynomial lower bounds)
Assume (
2
) holds with constants
C>0
and
\kappa=1
and additionally, (
3
) holds with function
W(\cdot)
,
\alpha=2
, and
\varphi(w)=cw^{\beta}
for some constants

and
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then a straight-forward calculation gives

H^{-1}_{W(x_{0})^{2},\varphi}(t)=\left((1-\beta)ct+W(x_{0})^{2(1-\beta)}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}

and Theorem

implies for all

,

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot)-\pi\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}\geq\frac{C^{2}}{4\left((1-\beta)ct+W(x_{0})^{2(1-\beta)}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}}.

Example 3
.

(Subgeometric lower bounds)
If (
2
) holds with constants
C>0
,
\beta\in(0,1)
, and
\kappa=1
and (
3
) holds with
W(\cdot)
,
\alpha=2
, and
\varphi(x)=c(x+K_{\beta})/\log(x+K_{\beta})^{\beta}
where
K_{\beta}=\exp(\beta+1)
, then

H^{-1}_{W(x_{0})^{2},\varphi}(t)\leq(W(x_{0})^{2}+K_{\beta})\exp\left([(1+\beta)ct]^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\right).

By Theorem

, then for all

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot)-\pi\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}\geq\frac{C^{2}}{4(W(x_{0})^{2}+K_{\beta})}\exp\left(-[(1+\beta)ct]^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\right).

Now we obtain a matching weak lower bound rate as total variation under additional conditions.

Theorem 4
.

Assume (
2
) holds with
C,\kappa
and (
3
) holds with
W(\cdot)
, and
\alpha
, and let
M
be defined as in (
4
).
Assume
\log(W)
is Lipschitz continuous.
Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot),\pi\right]}\xi(\{x,y\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}:d(x,y)>\delta_{\epsilon}\})\geq\frac{(1-\epsilon)M}{H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)^{\frac{\kappa}{\alpha-\kappa}}}

holds for some
\delta_{\epsilon}\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
r\geq 1
and let
T=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:W(x)\geq r\}
.
Since
\log(W)
is continuous, then
T
is closed and by Strassen’s theorem (
[
20
]
and
[
21
, Corollary 1.28]
), then for any
\delta>0
,

\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot),\pi\right]}\xi(\{x,y\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}:d(x,y)>\delta\})\geq\pi(T)-\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},T^{\delta})

where
T^{\delta}=\{y\in\mathcal{X}:\text{dist}(y,T)\leq\delta\}
and
\text{dist}(y,T)=\inf_{x\in T}d(x,y)
.
Thus, we will find a discrepancy between
\pi\left(\{W\geq r\}\right)
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}\left(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\{W\geq(1-\epsilon)r\}\right)
for small
\epsilon
and the intuition is illustrated in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:

The diagram illustrates intuition for a discrepancy between the set
\{W\geq r\}
for the adaptive process and the target measure and also
\{W\geq(1-\epsilon)r\}
for small
\epsilon
.

For
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, since
\log(W)
is Lipschitz continuous, then we can then choose
\delta_{\epsilon}
depending on
\epsilon
sufficiently small so that
W(x)\geq(1-\epsilon)r
if
\text{dist}(x,T)\leq\delta_{\epsilon}
and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in T^{\delta_{\epsilon}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(W(X_{t})\geq(1-\epsilon)r).

Markov’s inequality and (
3
) imply that

\mathbb{P}(W(X_{t})\geq(1-\epsilon)r)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[W^{\alpha}(X_{t})\right]}{(1-\epsilon)^{\alpha}r^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)}{(1-\epsilon)^{\alpha}r^{\alpha}}.

Optimizing, we get for
t
large enough so that

r=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\kappa C(1-\epsilon)^{\alpha}}H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-\kappa}}\geq 1

and this yields the lower bound

\displaystyle\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot),\pi\right]}\xi(\{x,y:d(x,y)>\delta_{\epsilon}\})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{r^{\kappa}}-\frac{H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)}{(1-\epsilon)^{\alpha}r^{\alpha}}.

\displaystyle\geq(1-\epsilon)^{\frac{\alpha\kappa}{\alpha-\kappa}}\frac{M}{H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)^{\frac{\kappa}{\alpha-\kappa}}}

where
M
is defined by (
4
).
The conclusion follows since we may choose
\epsilon
so that
(1-\epsilon)^{\frac{\alpha\kappa}{\alpha-\kappa}}\geq 1-a
for any
a\in(0,1)
and some
\delta_{a}
.
∎

An interpretation of Theorem
4
is the best possible rate of convergence for adaptive MCMC satisfying (
3
) for target measure satisfying (
2
).
As can be seen in the proof, the conclusion of Theorem
4
holds generally for functions
W
that are not necessarily log-Lipschitz but satisfy for any
\epsilon>0
and
r\geq 1
, if
d(x,\{W\geq r\})<\delta_{\epsilon}
for some
\delta_{\epsilon}
, then
W\geq(1-\epsilon)r
.
The assumption on the function
W
holds in many applications. However, there is a significant drawback to the Wasserstein lower bound being as the constant is non-explicit compared to the explicit lower bound in total variation.

What is surprising about the lower bounds in this section is the requirement only on the Markov family
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
to satisfy (
3
) and does not directly depend on an adaptation strategy.
For example, it is common scenario in adaptive MCMC for the parameter space
\mathcal{Y}
to be compact.
In this case, the simultaneous growth condition (

) often holds if a Markov kernel satisfies some mild regularity conditions and (

) holds with only fixed parameters.

Example 5
.

(Adaptive Unadjusted Langevin algorithm)
Consider the multivariate Student’s t-distribution
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
and
v>0
degrees of freedom. The Lebesgue density is defined by

D_{\pi}(x)=\frac{\Gamma((v+d)/2)}{\Gamma(v/2)(v\pi)^{d/2}}\exp(-U(x))

where
U(x)=\frac{v+d}{2}\log(1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}/v)
.
The adapted unadjusted Langevin process
(\Gamma_{t},X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by

X_{t+1}=X_{t}-\Gamma_{t+1}\nabla U(X_{t})+\sqrt{2\Gamma_{t+1}}Z_{t+1}

where
\Gamma_{t+1}\in(0,1)
and
Z_{t+1}
is an independent standard normal random vector.
Subgeometric drift conditions have been shown for unadjusted Langevin in the non-adaptive case for heavy tailed target measures
[
13
]
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
W(x)=(1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2})^{(v+d)/2}
.
By Ito’s formula, for large enough
\left\lVert x\right\rVert
, there is a constant
\epsilon>0
such that the second term is bounded using the moment generating function of non-central chi-square random variables by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[W^{\alpha}(X_{t+1})|\Gamma_{t+1}=\gamma,X_{t}=x\right]-W^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\gamma}\nabla W(x)^{\alpha}\cdot dX_{t}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{\gamma}\text{tr}\left(\nabla^{2}W(X_{t})^{\alpha}X_{t}\right)dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(v+d)\left[-\gamma_{*}(v+2)+\alpha(v+d)+\epsilon\right]\left(1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{\alpha(v+d)/2-1}.

It follows that for some constant
C_{\alpha}>0
and for all
x,\gamma
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[W^{\alpha}(X_{t+1})|\Gamma_{t+1}=\gamma,X_{t}=x\right]-W^{\alpha}(x)\leq C_{\alpha}W^{\alpha}(x)^{1-\frac{2}{\alpha(v+d)}}.

So then here

H^{-1}_{W(0)^{\alpha},\varphi}(t)=\left(\frac{2}{\alpha(v+d)}C_{\alpha}t+1\right)^{\alpha\frac{v+d}{2}}.

For
r\geq 1
, one has the lower bound for some constant
C>0

\pi(W\geq r)\geq\frac{C}{r^{v/(v+d)}}.

By Theorem
4
, there are constants
M>0
such that with
\alpha
chosen large enough

\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert\wedge 1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},0,\cdot),\pi)\geq\frac{M}{\left(\frac{2}{\alpha(v+d)}C_{\alpha}t+1\right)^{\frac{v}{\alpha-v/(v+d)}\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

Of particular interest is that the rate cannot be geometric even when considering weak convergence.

In certain situations, the tail probability decay on
\pi
in (
2
) may be difficult to establish.
In this case, we consider finding a function that is not integrable with respect to
\pi
, but this results in a trade-off of only having a lower bound for a subsequence.
An analogous result will also hold in total variation.

Theorem 6
.

Assume for some function
W:\mathcal{X}\to[1,\infty)
such that
\log(W)
is Lipschitz and
\int_{\mathcal{X}}Wd\pi=\infty
but also for some
\alpha>1
and some concave function
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}W^{\alpha})(x)-W(x)^{\alpha}\leq\varphi(W(x)^{\alpha})

(6)

holds for all
(x,\gamma)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.
Then for
\beta\in(0,1)
with
\alpha>1+\beta
, there is a constant
M_{*}>0
and a subsequence

increasing to infinity such that,

\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\mathcal{W}_{d\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t_{n})}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot),\pi\right)\geq\frac{M_{*}}{\left(H_{W^{\alpha}(x_{0}),\varphi}^{-1}(t_{n})\right)^{\frac{1+\beta}{\alpha-1-\beta}}}.

Proof.

The inverse function theorem implies the derivative

\frac{d}{ds}H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(s)=\varphi(H^{-1}_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}(s)).

Since
\varphi
has a linear upper bound,

\frac{d}{dt}\log(H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t))\leq m_{\alpha,x_{0}}<\infty
for some
m_{\alpha,x_{0}}>0
.
We then have

H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t+1)\leq\exp(m_{\alpha,x_{0}})H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t).

Since
\int_{\mathcal{X}}Wd\pi=\infty
, we can choose a constant
C
so that

C\exp(m_{\alpha,x_{0}})^{-\frac{1+\beta}{\alpha-1-\beta}}-(1-\epsilon)^{-\alpha}>0
and there is a sequence
(r_{n})_{n}
with
\lim_{n}r_{n}=\infty
such that with
T_{n}=\{x:W(x)\geq r_{n}\}
,

\pi(T_{n})\geq\frac{C}{r_{n}^{1+\beta}}.

For some
\epsilon,\delta_{\epsilon}\in(0,1)
as in Theorem
4

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{\epsilon}}\mathcal{W}_{d\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t_{n})}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot),\pi\right)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot),\pi\right]}\xi(\{x,y:d(x,y)>\delta_{\epsilon}\})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{r_{n}^{1+\beta}}-\frac{H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t)}{(1-\epsilon)^{\alpha}r_{n}^{\alpha}}.

holds for
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
We can construct a subsequence

using

.
Due to monotonicity, we can choose

so that

\displaystyle H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t_{n})^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1-\beta}}\leq r_{n}

\displaystyle\leq H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t_{n}+1)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1-\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(m_{\alpha,x_{0}})^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1-\beta}}H_{W(x_{0})^{\alpha},\varphi}^{-1}(t_{n})^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1-\beta}}.

Plugging in the bounds to

with the chosen

4
Subgeometric upper bounds for adaptive MCMC

This section is dedicated to studying conditions such that an upper bound convergence rate can be obtained for adaptive MCMC comparable to the lower bounds in the previous section.
We first consider a quantitative alternative to the widely-used diminishing adaptation condition
[
17
]
that is stronger in the sense that it requires a specified rate of decay.

Definition 7
.

An adaptive process satisfies
quantitative diminishing adaptation
with a function
G:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to(0,\infty)
strictly decreasing to 0 if for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there is a nonnegative integer
s_{\epsilon}
such that with probability at least
1-\epsilon
,

\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(x,\cdot)-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(x,\cdot)\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}\leq G(t)

(7)

holds for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
t\geq s_{\epsilon}
and this event is Borel measurable.

Since
\mathcal{X}
is separable, a sufficient condition for Borel measurability here is lower semicontinuity of the total variation as a function in
x
.
Next, we consider a simultaneous version of a subgeometric drift condition on the Markov family.

Definition 8
.

A Markov family
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
satisfies a
simultaneous subgeometric drift condition
if there is a Borel function
V:\mathcal{X}\to[1,\infty)
and a concave function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
strictly increasing to infinity with
\lim_{v\to\infty}\varphi(v)/v=0
and a constant
K\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V)(x)-V(x)\leq-\varphi(V(x))+K

(8)

holds for every
(x,\gamma)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.

Here we assume
\lim_{v\to\infty}\varphi(v)/v=0
to exclude the geometric case.
Subgeometric drift conditions for Markov chains has been studied previously
[
5
,
12
]
but we adjust the previous conditions to hold over feasible tuning parameters
\mathcal{Y}
. We now combine this drift condition with a simultaneous local contracting condition.

Definition 9
.

A Markov family
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
satisfies a
simultaneously locally contracting condition
on a set
S\subseteq\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
if there is a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
where

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot)-\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(y,\cdot)\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}\leq 1-\alpha

(9)

holds for all
(x,y)\in S
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Local coupling conditions have been studied in the subgeometric case for Markov chains
[
7
]
.
For example, a minorization condition can be used to verify the Markov family is simultaneously locally contracting (see
[
17
]
).
If the inverse is not well-defined, we will denote the generalized inverse of a real-valued function
F
by
F^{-1}(\cdot)=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq\cdot\}
.
Under these three conditions, we can establish an upper bound for the adaptation process.

Theorem 10
.

Assume the following assumptions hold for the Markov family
(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}}
:

1.

\pi\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}=\pi
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
.

2.

A simultaneously subgeometric drift condition (
8
) holds with a Borel function
V(\cdot)
such that
\int_{\mathcal{X}}Vd\pi<\infty
.

3.

A simultaneous locally contracting condition (
9
) holds on the set
S=\{x,y\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}:V(x)+V(y)\leq R\}
for
R=\varphi^{-1}[2K/(1-\delta)]
and some
\delta\in(0,1)
.

Additionally, assume the quantitative diminishing adaptation condition (
7
) holds.
Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and all
t
sufficiently large,

\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(T_{\epsilon,t}+t)}((\gamma_{0},x_{0}),\cdot)-\pi\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}\leq\frac{2+\left[r(1)/r(0)\right]\left(V(x_{0})+\int_{\mathcal{X}}Vd\pi+KT_{\epsilon,t}\right)+C}{H_{1,\delta\varphi}^{-1}\left(\frac{t}{-\log(H_{1,\delta\varphi}^{-1}(t))/\log(1-\alpha)+1}\right)}+2\epsilon

where
T_{\epsilon,t}=s_{\epsilon}+\lceil(1/G)^{-1}(t^{2}/\epsilon)\rceil
and

\displaystyle r(\cdot)=\delta\varphi(H_{1,\delta\varphi}^{-1}(\cdot)),

\displaystyle C=\frac{r(1)}{r(0)}\left\{R+\frac{2Kr(1)}{r(0)}\right\}.

There are many ways to verify quantitative diminishing adaptation (
7
) through concentration inequalities and almost sure convergence.
One specific way to verify quantitative diminishing adaptation (
7
) when
\mathcal{Y}
is a Euclidean space is through Lipschitz continuity properties of
\gamma\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
combined with choosing an adaptation strategy so that
(\Gamma_{t+1}-\Gamma_{t})/G(t)
convergences almost surely.
For example, if for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,
\gamma\mapsto\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,\cdot)
is Lipschitz with constant
\beta_{x}
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(x,\cdot)-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(x,\cdot)\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}\leq\left(\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\beta_{x}\right)\left\lVert\Gamma_{t+1}-\Gamma_{t}\right\rVert

holds for all

and (

The Lipschitz continuity used above can be verified under certain conditions when adapting the covariance in Gaussian random-walk proposals for Metropolis-Hastings
[

]
.
A concrete example to satisfy the almost sure convergence of

is the widely-used adaptation with stochastic approximation when

5
Example: adaptive Metropolis-Hastings independence sampler

In many cases, it is difficult to choose a proposal for Metropolis-Hastings that approximately matches the tail behavior of a complex target measure
[
19
]
and adaptive MCMC is often employed.
The point of this toy example is to concretely demonstrate this scenario.
We will use the upper and lower bounds on the convergence to investigate the sensitivity to different adaptation strategies.
Consider the target measure
\pi(dx)=\exp(-x)I_{[0,\infty)}(x)dx

Let
(\gamma_{*},\gamma^{*})
be the interval for some potential tuning parameters
1<\gamma_{*}<\gamma^{*}

\gamma\exp(-\gamma x)I_{[0,\infty)}(x)

and Markov kernel defined for
(x,\gamma)\in[0,\infty)\times(\gamma_{*},\gamma^{*})
and all Borel sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,A)=\int_{A}a_{\gamma}(x,y)\gamma\exp(-\gamma y)dy+\delta_{x}(A)R_{\gamma}(x)

where the acceptance function is
a_{\gamma}(x,y)=\exp\left[(\gamma-1)(y-x)\right]\wedge 1
and the rejection probability is
R_{\gamma}(x)=1-\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{\gamma}(x,y)\gamma\exp(-\gamma y)dy
.
Since we restrict
\gamma>1
, the tail probabilities of the proposal and the Metropolis-Hastings kernel are lighter than the target.
Due to this restriction, we will have a polynomial lower bound over any possible adaptation plan.

.
So then

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}W^{\alpha})(x)-W^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{\gamma}{\alpha-1}+\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}+\gamma-1\right\}\exp\left[-(\gamma-\alpha)x\right]-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha-1}\exp\left[-(\gamma-1)x\right]-\gamma W^{\alpha}(x)^{\frac{1+\alpha-\gamma}{\alpha}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}W^{\alpha})(x)-W^{\alpha}(x)

Example: Adaptive random walk Metropolis

Adaptive random-walk Metropolis is a popular simulation algorithm for Bayesian statistics
[
9
]
.
Let
U:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and consider the target measure with normalizing constant
Z=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp(-U(x))dx
defined by
\pi(dx)=Z^{-1}\exp(-U(x))dx
.
We make the following regularity assumptions on the target
\pi
which have been used previously to show convergence results in MCMC
[
5
]
.
Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{F}
denote the Frobenius norm.

Assumption 13
.

Suppose
U
is twice continuously differentiable and there exists a minimum such that
x_{*}
and
U(x_{*})=0
.
Let
m\in(0,1)
and assume there are constants
d_{k},D_{k},r>0
for
k=1,2,3
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\geq R
for some
R>0
,

\displaystyle d_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{m}\leq U(x)\leq D_{1}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{m},

(14)

\displaystyle d_{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{m-1}\leq\left\lVert\nabla U(x)\right\rVert\leq D_{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{m-1},

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla U(x)}{\left\lVert\nabla U(x)\right\rVert}\cdot\frac{x}{\left\lVert x\right\rVert}\geq r,

(15)

\displaystyle d_{3}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{m-2}\leq\left\lVert\nabla^{2}U(x)\right\rVert_{F}\leq D_{3}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{m-2}.

(16)

While Assumption (
13
) is strong,
U(\cdot)\propto\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert^{m}
and the Weibull distribution are examples (see
[
8
]
).
Let
g
be a standard Gaussian density.
For
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
, define the random-walk Metropolis Markov family for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and Borel
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}(x,A)=\int_{x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi\in A}a(x,x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)g(\xi)d\xi+\delta_{x}(A)R_{\gamma}(x)

(17)

with acceptance function
a(x,y)=\exp[U(x)-U(y)]\wedge 1
, and rejection probability
R_{\gamma}(x)=1-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(x,x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)g(\xi)d\xi
.
We define an adaptive random-walk Metropolis process by adapting the covariance of the proposal
[
9
]
with dynamics

\Gamma_{t}|(\Gamma_{s},X_{s})_{s\leq t-1}
first updating the covariance matrix, and then
X_{t}|\Gamma_{t},X_{t-1}
updating the current state with random-walk Metropolis.

For the tuning parameter set
\mathcal{Y}
, we consider the set of symmetric positive definite matrices on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the eigenvalues are bounded by constants
\lambda_{*},\lambda^{*}>0
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}=\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\lambda_{*}I\leq\gamma\leq\lambda^{*}I,\gamma^{T}=\gamma\right\}.

(18)

One example is to adapt a sample covariance matrix scaled by
h>0
using the following identity

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}=\frac{h}{t}\sum_{s=0}^{t}(X_{s}-\bar{X}_{t})(X_{s}-\bar{X}_{t})^{T}=\frac{t-1}{t}\Gamma_{t-1}+\frac{h}{t+1}(X_{t}-\bar{X}_{t-1})(X_{t}-\bar{X}_{t-1})^{T}

(19)

where
\bar{X}_{t}=(t+1)^{-1}\sum_{s=0}^{t}X_{s}

[
9
,
2
]
.
The set
\mathcal{Y}
is convex and one way to ensure the updates remain in
\mathcal{Y}
is to truncate the eigenvalues of (
19
).

Under Assumption (
13
), we first obtain a lower bound on the convergence rate for the adaptive random-walk Metropolis process.

Proposition 14
.

For
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot)
be the marginal of the adaptive random-walk Metropolis process initialized at

17
) and adaptation parameter set (
18
).
If Assumption (

) holds for
\pi
, then there are constants

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot),\pi\right)\geq M_{*}\exp\left(-c_{*}{t}^{\frac{m}{2-m}}\right).

In order to proceed, we will first establish a simultaneous growth condition on the Markov family.

Lemma 15
.

With
W(x)=\exp(U(x))
, there are constants

such that for any

and
(x,\gamma)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}W^{\alpha})(x)-W^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq M^{*}\alpha^{2/m-1}\left[\alpha-N\right]\varphi_{K}(W^{\alpha}(x))+L^{*}_{\alpha}

and if

is small enough, there are constants

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}W^{\alpha})(x)-W^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq M_{*}\alpha^{2/m-1}\left[\alpha-N\right]\varphi_{K}(W^{\alpha}(x))+L_{*,\alpha}

Proof.

Let

We will control

\displaystyle\frac{(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}W^{\alpha})(x)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}-1

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}-1\right]a(x,x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)g(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{x}}\left[\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}-1\right]a(x,x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)g(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle+\int_{C_{x}^{c}}\left[\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}-1\right]a(x,x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)g(\xi)d\xi.

The first term is controlled through Taylor expansions.

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in C_{r}}\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left\lVert\gamma\right\rVert\sup_{\xi\in C_{r},t\in[0,1]}\left\lVert\xi\right\rVert\sup_{\xi\in C_{r}}\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+t\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert x+t\gamma^{1/2}\xi\right\rVert^{m-1}dt.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{\left\lVert x\right\rVert\geq r}\sup_{\xi\in C_{r}}\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq 1.

Using the fundamental theorem of calculus twice with (
15
) and (
16
), there is a constant
M>0
such that for large enough

and all

\displaystyle\left|\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}-1-\alpha\gamma^{1/2}\nabla U(x)\cdot\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+t\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}\{\alpha^{2}[\gamma^{1/2}\nabla U(x+t\gamma^{1/2}\xi)\cdot\xi]^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\alpha\xi\cdot\gamma^{1/2}\nabla^{2}U(x+t\gamma^{1/2}\xi)\gamma^{1/2}\xi\}(1-t)dt

Here we used again that for

large enough and

After integrating, we have shown that there is a constant

\displaystyle\int_{C_{x}}\left[\frac{W^{\alpha}(x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)}{W^{\alpha}(x)}-1\right]a(x,x+\gamma^{1/2}\xi)g(\xi)d\xi

For large enough

Proof of Proposition

\displaystyle H_{W^{\alpha}(x_{0}),\varphi}(u)

\displaystyle H_{W^{\alpha}(x_{0}),\varphi}^{-1}(t)

We investigate now an upper bound with the quantitative diminishing adaptation condition (

The following upper and lower bounds show that the convergence of adaptive random-walk Metropolis in this situation is not geometric.
One drawback is that we do not obtain explicit constants in the upper and lower bounds.

Proposition 16
.

For
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(t)}(\gamma_{0},x_{0},\cdot)
be the marginal of an adaptive random-walk Metropolis process as in Proposition

.
Assume

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert\Gamma_{t+1}-\Gamma_{t}\right\rVert_{F}}{G(t)}=0

almost surely with
G(\cdot)
strictly decreasing to
0
.
Then there are constants
M^{*}
depending on
x_{0}
and
c^{*},J^{*},d^{*}>0
such that for all
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and all
t
sufficiently large,

\mathcal{W}_{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert\wedge 1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{(T_{\epsilon,t}+t)}((\gamma_{0},x_{0}),\cdot),\pi\right)\leq M^{*}T_{\epsilon,t}\exp\left[-c^{*}t^{\frac{m}{2-m}\left(1-\frac{m}{2-m}\right)}\right]+\epsilon

where
T_{\epsilon,t}=S_{\epsilon}+\lceil(1/G)^{-1}\left(J^{*}t^{2}/\epsilon\right)\rceil
for some
S_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

We will apply Theorem
10
to obtain the conclusion.
Choosing
\alpha<1
sufficiently small in the drift condition from Lemma
15
, gives the simultaneous drift condition ensures
\pi
-integrability of the drift function.
Using the compactness of the sublevel sets and continuity of the proposal density shows a simultaneous local minorization using Lebesgue measure holds.

It remains to verify quantitative diminishing adaptation (
7
).
For
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
, define

f_{\gamma}(y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}\det(\gamma)^{1/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}y^{T}{\gamma}^{-1}y\right).

Following
[
2
, Lemma 13]
, the mean value theorem gives the upper bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f_{\gamma^{\prime}}(y)-f_{\gamma}(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}f_{\gamma_{t}}(y)\left|\text{tr}\left(\gamma_{t}^{-1}(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)+\gamma_{t}^{-1}yy^{T}\gamma_{t}^{-1}(\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma)\right)\right|dtdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{\lambda_{*}}\left\lVert\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma\right\rVert_{F}.

Since the proposal is symmetric, then for Borel
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to[0,1]
, let
\psi(x,y)=(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))a(x,y)
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\gamma^{\prime}}\varphi(x)-\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x,y)f_{\gamma^{\prime}}(y)dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x,y)f_{\gamma}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f_{\gamma^{\prime}}(y)-f_{\gamma}(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{\lambda_{*}}\left\lVert\gamma^{\prime}-\gamma\right\rVert_{F}.

Taking the supremum over
\varphi
, we then have for each
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left\lVert\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t+1}}(x,\cdot)-\mathcal{P}_{\Gamma_{t}}(x,\cdot)\right\rVert_{\text{TV}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{\lambda_{*}}\left\lVert\Gamma_{t+1}-\Gamma_{t}\right\rVert_{F}.

∎

7
Final discussion

The general weak lower bound convergence rates developed here in combination with total variation upper bounds for adaptive MCMC can provide useful guidance in designing adaptation strategies in MCMC.
We showed that the lower bound for weak convergence in Theorem
4
can produce the same rate as the lower bound in total variation from Theorem
1
.
We also used a novel quantitative diminishing adaptation condition (
7
) to show these lower bounds can be accompanied by upper bounds with subgeometric convergence rates.
Our contributions are useful not only in understanding the convergence of adaptive MCMC, but also for gaining intuition for constructing adaptation strategies in practice.

Choosing an optimal adaptation strategy for an adaptive MCMC simulation remains a difficult task in general and our subgeometric upper bounds are limited by requiring the adaptation to diminish sufficiently fast according to (
7
).
While this is to be expected, some interesting future research directions could include finding more precise classes of adaptation strategies that are capable of achieving upper bounds that can approximately match the lower bound rate.
Another area of interest is studying requirements on the adaptation that result in geometric convergence rates for adaptive MCMC.

The following is a technical result to ensure Borel measurability of conditional Wasserstein distances used in adaptive MCMC.

Proposition 17
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be a Polish spaces.
Assume
(x,\gamma)\mapsto K_{x,\gamma}
is Borel measurable where
K_{x,\gamma}
is a Borel probability measure on
\mathcal{X}
and
x,\gamma\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.
Let
c:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to[0,\infty)
be a lower semicontinuous function and for each
x,\gamma,x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, let

\mathcal{W}_{c}\left(K_{x,\gamma},K_{x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}\right)=\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}\left(K_{x,\gamma},K_{x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}\right)}\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}c(u,v)\xi(du,dv)=\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}c(u,v)\xi^{*}_{x,\gamma,x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}(du,dv).

for an optimal coupling
\xi^{*}_{x,\gamma,x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
.
Then there is a Borel measurable choice of the the function
x,\gamma,x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\mapsto\xi^{*}_{x,\gamma,x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
T
be the set of Borel optimal couplings
\xi^{*}
on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
satisfying

\inf_{\xi\in\mathcal{C}\left(\mu,\nu\right)}\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}c(u,v)\xi(du,dv)=\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}c(u,v)\xi^{*}(du,dv)

for some Borel probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\mathcal{X}
.
Since
\mathcal{X}
is Polish, then
T
is a Polish space.
Let
M_{1}(\mathcal{X})
be the set of probability measures on
\mathcal{X}
.
Let
\Phi:T\to M_{1}(\mathcal{X})\times M_{1}(\mathcal{X})
be the function defined by
\Phi(\xi)=(\mu,\nu)
for the optimal couplings
\xi\in\mathcal{C}\left(\mu,\nu\right)
.
Then the pre-images
\Phi^{-1}(\mu,\nu)
are compact nonempty sets of optimal couplings
[
22
, Corollary 5.21]
.
By construction,
\Phi
is continuous with respect to the weak topology as it is the projection onto the first and second coordinates.
Since
\Phi
is continuous it is weakly Borel measurable so that
\{\Phi(\xi)\cap U\not=\varnothing\}
is Borel measurable for every open set
U
.
By Kuratowski and Ryll-Nardzewski
[
14
]
, there is a Borel measurable selection of the inverse
\Phi^{-1}
.
Now let
\psi:x,\gamma,x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\mapsto(K_{x,\gamma},K_{x^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}})
be Borel measurable and thus
\Phi^{-1}(\psi)
is Borel measurable completing the proof.
∎

The following provides useful properties for the function
H_{1,\varphi}
and
\varphi
defined in Section
3
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be concave and define for
w\geq 1
,

\displaystyle H_{1,\varphi}(w)=\int_{1}^{w}\frac{1}{\varphi(v)}dv.

(20)

Then
\varphi
is non-decreasing and
H_{1,\varphi}(\cdot)
is strictly increasing to
\infty
.

Proof.

The fundamental theorem of calculus implies
H_{1,\varphi}(\cdot)
is strictly increasing and then this implies that
H_{1,\varphi}^{-1}(\cdot)
exists.
Since
\varphi
is positive and concave, it is sublinear and so
H_{1,\varphi}(\cdot)
increases to
\infty
.
We need to show that
\varphi
is non-decreasing.
Suppose by contradiction that for some
u<v
that
\varphi(v)<\varphi(u)
.
Let
\partial\varphi
denote a subgradient of
\varphi
and then
\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)=\partial\varphi(\bar{u})(v-u)<0
for some
\bar{u}
in between
u,v
.
For any
v^{\prime}\geq v
,
\varphi(v^{\prime})-\varphi(v)\leq\partial\varphi(v)(v^{\prime}-v)\leq\partial\varphi(\bar{u})(v^{\prime}-v)
since
\varphi
is concave and its subgradients are not increasing.
For sufficiently large
v^{\prime}
, we would then have
\varphi(v^{\prime})-\varphi(v)\leq-\varphi(v)-1
which is a contradiction to
\varphi\geq 0
.
∎

The next simple lemma is used for drift conditions.

Lemma 19
.

Assume there is a Borel function
V:\mathcal{X}\to[1,\infty)
, an strictly increasing function
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, and a constant
K\in(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V)(x)-V(x)\leq-\varphi(V(x))+K

(21)

holds for every
(x,\gamma)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.
Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and
C_{\delta}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:V(x)\leq\varphi^{-1}(K/(1-\delta))\}
,

(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V)(x)-V(x)\leq-\delta\varphi(V(x))+KI_{C_{\delta}}(x)

holds for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
and all
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{X}
and if
\varphi(V(x))\geq R
with
R=K/(1-\delta)
, then by the drift condition,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}V)(x)-V(x)\leq\left(\frac{K}{R}-1\right)\varphi(V(x))\leq-\delta\varphi(V(x)).

If
\varphi(V(x))\leq R
, then the result follows from the assumed drift condition since
\delta<1
.
∎

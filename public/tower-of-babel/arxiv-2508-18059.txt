Automorphisms of punctual Hilbert schemes and symmetric powers of varieties
Ashima Bansal, Supravat Sarkar, Shivam Vats

Abstract
. We classify complex smooth projective surfaces whose punctual Hilbert scheme has a non-natural automorphism preserving the big diagonal. This completely answers a question raised by Belmans, Oberdieck and Rennemo, and extends previous works by Boissière-Sarti, Hayashi, Sasaki, Girardet and Wang. We reduce this to studying the existence of non-natural automorphisms of symmetric powers. We study this question for higher dimensional varieties too, giving some sufficient conditions guaranteeing every automorphism of a symmetric power to be natural. As a corollary, we characterize smooth projective surfaces of Kodaira dimension ≥ 1 \geq 1 whose punctual Hilbert scheme has a non-natural automorphism, this time not assuming the automorphism preserves the big diagonal. We also address the question, when a smooth projective variety is determined up to isomorphism by its punctual Hilbert scheme.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
\geq\,2
. Then any automorphism of
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\Omega_{X})
is over an automorphism of
X
, unless
X\,\cong\,\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose
X\,\not\cong\,\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Let
W\,=\,\mathbb{P}_{X}(\Omega_{X})
, and let

W\,\xrightarrow{\,\,\,p\,\,\,}\,X

be the natural projection. Suppose
W
admits an automorphism that is not over an automorphism of
X
. Then, one of the cases
(1)
,
(2)
or
(3)
in Lemma
3.4
must occur.

An examination of Chern classes shows that
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
is not isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{n}
or to
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, up to line bundle twist, if
n\,>\,2
. Therefore, cases
(1)
or
(2)
of Lemma
3.4
cannot occur. Thus, case
(3)
of Lemma
3.4
must occur. Hence
X\,=\,\mathbb{P}_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
for some rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{1}
over a curve
C
, and
\Omega_{X}\,\cong\,f^{\ast}\mathcal{E}_{2}
up to line bundle twist, where
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is another rank
n
vector bundle on
C
, and
f\,:\,\mathbb{P}_{C}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\,\longrightarrow\,C
is the projection. Now, restrict this isomorphism to a fibre of
f
, which is an isomorphism to
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. We obtain

\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\,\oplus\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\,\cong\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(r)^{\oplus n}\textrm{for some}\,\,r\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}.

Taking
h^{0}
, we get:

1\,=\,h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\,\oplus\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}})\,=\,h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(r)^{\oplus n})

\hskip 159.3356pt=\,n\,h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n-1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(r)),

which contradicts the assumptions
n\,\geq\,2
.
∎

Lemma 4.2

Let

Then the following holds:

(i)

\textnormal{N}_{G}(\textnormal{im}(f))=\textnormal{im}(f)\cdot\prod_{i}K_{i}.

\textnormal{N}_{G}(\textnormal{im}(f))=\textnormal{im}(h)\cdot\prod_{i}K_{i}.

Note that
\prod_{i}K_{i}
commutes with
\textnormal{im}(h)\,\supset\,\textnormal{im}(f),
so both products
\textnormal{im}(f)\cdot\prod_{i}K_{i}
and

and

are subgroups of

. Clearly,

\textnormal{im}(f)\cdot\prod_{i}K_{i}\subset\textnormal{N}_{G}(\textnormal{im}(f))\quad in\,\,(i),\quad\textrm{and}\quad\textnormal{im}(h)\cdot\prod_{i}K_{i}\subset\textnormal{N}_{G}(\textnormal{im}(f))\quad in\,\,(ii).

\underline{g}=(g_{i})_{i}\in\textnormal{N}_{G}(\textnormal{im}(f))
, where

for all

. Then for every

for all

and

(i)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

, the symmetric power

admits a non-natural automorphism.

Proof.

Since

m|(1\,-\,\alpha)
in
\textrm{End}(X)

The rest of the proof follows exactly as in
[
Sas24
]
. More explicitly, define

f\,=\,\frac{1+(m-1)\alpha}{m},\quad g\,=\,\frac{1-\alpha}{m}\,\in\,\textrm{End}(X).

Then,
f,g\neq 0.
Consider the automorphism
\Phi
of

given by the matrix

\begin{bmatrix}f&g&\cdots&g\\
g&f&\cdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
g&\cdots&g&f\end{bmatrix}.

.

∎

Lemma 5.2

\emph{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
commutative, and let

(\Leftarrow)

(\Rightarrow)

Proof of Theorem

:

Proof.

Let

. By
[

]
,
\phi
lifts to an automorphism
\Phi
of
X^{m}
. By composing
\phi
with a natural automorphism, we may assume that
\Phi|_{\Delta_{X}}=id
.

We have birational contraction

. Let

denote the fundamental locus of
f
, which is a finite nonempty set since

is not minimal. We have induced birational contraction

X^{m}\,\xlongrightarrow{f_{(m)}}\,Y^{m},

whose fundamental locus

has irreducible components

where

denotes the projection onto the

-th factor.

Since the fibres of

are rationally chain-connected and

\Phi
descends to an automorphism

of

Hence, there exists a permutation

such that for all

.

So, by precomposing

and

Let

be the projections, and

be the components of

. We have

factors through

for all

. As

. In other words,

(5.6)

\phi_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\,=\,\psi_{1}(p(x_{1}),x_{2})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\phi_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\,=\,\psi_{2}(x_{1},p(x_{2}))

(5.7)

for all

and

.

As
\phi
descends to

, we have:

\phi_{1}(x_{2},x_{1})\,=\,\phi_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}),

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\,\in\,X
. By
(

)
,
\psi_{1}(p(x_{1}),x_{2})\,=\,\psi_{2}(x_{2},p(x_{1}))

\psi_{1}(c,x)\,=\,\psi_{2}(x,c)

for all
c\,\in\,C
and
x\,\in\,X
. For

, so,
\psi_{c}(X)\,\subset\,X_{c}

By (

), we have:

\phi(x_{1},x_{2})\,=\,(\psi_{p(x_{1})}(x_{2}),\psi_{p(x_{2})}(x_{1}))

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\,\in\,X
. For

Now define

x\,\longmapsto\,(p(x),\psi_{c_{0}}(x))

Step 4
: We now complete the proof.

\phi

\psi_{11}\,:\,Z_{1}\,\longrightarrow\,X
the first component of

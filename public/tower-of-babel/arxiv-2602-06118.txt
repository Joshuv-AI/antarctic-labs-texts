Robust flat bands of the honeycomb wire network
Chunxiao Liu, Benoît Douçot, Jérôme Cayssol

Abstract
We show that periodic honeycomb networks of ballistic conducting channels generically host exact flat bands spanning the entire Brillouin zone. These flat bands are independent of microscopic vertex scattering, persist for any number of transverse modes, and occur in a universal 1 : 2 1\colon 2 ratio with dispersive bands. Their existence is enforced by local D 3 D_{3} vertex symmetry and lattice translations. We construct compact localized states obeying a Bohr–Sommerfeld-type quantization condition and demonstrate that flat bands survive in realistic antidot lattices, establishing honeycomb wire networks as a robust flat band platform relevant to gated high-mobility 2D electron gases and molecule-patterned metallic surfaces.

The single channel case in momentum space.
We study the Schrödinger differential operator
H=-\hbar^{2}/(2m)d^{2}/ds^{2}
along the HQG, where
s\in(0,a_{0})
denotes the continuous local coordinate defined on each bond, as the distance from
B
site. For the rest of the paper we set
E_{0}\equiv\hbar^{2}/(2m)=1
and
a_{0}=1
. We consider solely elastic scattering at the nodes described by 3 by 3 scattering matrices
S_{v}
, indexed by the sublattice index
v=A,B
. The bond index
{\boldsymbol{l}}=(m,n,i)
encapsulates the leg index
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, and the integer coordinates
(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
for the unit cell (Fig.
1
). The scalar wave function
\Psi_{\boldsymbol{l}}(s)
at energy
E
inside bond
{\boldsymbol{l}}=(m,n,i)
is a linear superposition of two counterpropagating plane waves :

\Psi_{\boldsymbol{l}}(s)=\alpha_{\boldsymbol{l}}\,\,e^{iqs}+\beta_{\boldsymbol{l}}\,\,e^{-iqs}\,\,,

(1)

where
q>0
is the longitudinal wave vector along the bond, and
E=q^{2}
. Taking advantage of the invariance under lattice translations, we further performed the Bloch reduction
(m,n)\rightarrow{\boldsymbol{k}}
by introducing a Bloch wave vector
{\boldsymbol{k}}

1
. The scattering conditions at vertices
A
(
s=1
) and
B
(
s=0
) read :

\begin{pmatrix}|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\\
|\beta_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&D^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S_{B}D_{\boldsymbol{k}}\\
e^{2iq}S_{A}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}|\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\\
|\beta_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\end{pmatrix}\,\,,

(2)

where the vector
\ket{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}}=(\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}},1},\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}},2},\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}},3})
represents the mode propagating from
B
to
A
, while
\ket{\beta_{\boldsymbol{k}}}=(\beta_{{\boldsymbol{k}},1},\beta_{{\boldsymbol{k}},2},\beta_{{\boldsymbol{k}},3})
represents the mode propagating from
A
to
B
. The diagonal matrix
D_{\boldsymbol{k}}
with diagonal entries
(1,e^{-i{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}_{1}},e^{-i{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{e}}_{2}})
accounts for the Bloch phase shifts between different unit cells. The condition for a non-trivial solution of Eq. (
2
) is expressed by the following eigenproblem :

e^{2iq(E)}D_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}S_{B}D_{\boldsymbol{k}}S_{A}\ket{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\ket{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\,,

(3)

where
q=q(E)=\sqrt{E}
. Solving the corresponding determinant secular equation provides the full band structure of HQG, which consists of 3 branches of
e^{iq}
vs
{\boldsymbol{k}}
, yielding an infinite spectrum of discrete levels
q
(or
E
) for each
{\boldsymbol{k}}
. Note that this quantum graph construction, and specifically Eq. (
3
), apply to any bipartite lattice.

We now explain that the particular
D_{3}
symmetry around each vertex of HQG implies that one of the 3 energy branches is always a FB. Indeed, the scattering matrix
S_{v}
is an element of the quotient group
U(3)/D_{3}
and has therefore the very particular spectral decomposition
1
1

1

Explicitly
S_{v}
consists in repeated identical reflection amplitude
r_{v}
on its diagonal, and a unique transmission amplitude
t_{v}
on non-diagonal entries. The complex transmission and reflection amplitudes are given respectively by
t_{v}=(e^{i\theta_{v}}-e^{i\phi_{v}})/3
and
r_{v}=(e^{i\theta_{v}}+2e^{i\phi_{v}})/3
as a function of the angular parameters
(\theta_{v},\phi_{v})
.
:

S_{v}=e^{i\phi_{v}}P_{\perp}+e^{i\theta_{v}}P_{\parallel}\,\,,

(4)

where the eigenvalue
e^{i\theta_{v}}
is associated with the rank-1 projector
P_{\parallel}
along the vector
\ket{e_{3}}=(1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}
, while eigenvalue
e^{i\phi_{v}}
is attached to the rank-2 projector
P_{\perp}=\mathbb{I}_{3}-P_{\parallel}
onto the subspace orthogonal to
\ket{e_{3}}
.

Obtaining FBs consists in finding, for each Bloch wave vector
{\boldsymbol{k}}
, a nontrivial vector
\ket{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}}
that solves the eigenproblem (
3
) with a value of
q

independent
of
{\boldsymbol{k}}
. The solution is not guaranteed to exist for arbitrary bipartite lattice because the scattering matrices do not commute with
D_{\boldsymbol{k}}
. Nevertheless,
a FB solution does exist for the HQG
due to the specific two-projector structure of Eq. (
4
). Indeed for any
{\boldsymbol{k}}
, one can always choose a vector
\ket{\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}
such that
\ket{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in\text{Im}P_{\perp}
and
D_{\boldsymbol{k}}\ket{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in\text{Im}P_{\perp}
. Then for such states, the matching Eq. (
3
) reduces to :

e^{i(2q+\phi_{A}+\phi_{B})}\ket{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\ket{\alpha_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\,

(5)

since
D_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}D_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\mathbb{I}_{3}
, and therefore

2q+\phi_{A}+\phi_{B}=2\pi\mathbb{Z}\,,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\text{BZ}\,.

(6)

The momentum
q
and the energy
E=q^{2}
are now
{\boldsymbol{k}}
-independent, hence the FB, although the eigenscattering vector
\ket{\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}
forms a texture winding as a function of
{\boldsymbol{k}}

1
. This leads to an infinite set of
exact flat bands extending within the whole Brillouin zone
. Hence, even for arbitrary values of
\theta_{v}
and
\phi_{v}
, Eq. (
6

\theta_{v}=\phi_{v}
and
S_{v}=e^{i\phi_{v}}\mathbb{I}_{3}

2q+\phi_{A}+\phi_{B}
is the phase accumulated along a closed semi-classical trajectory. Note that the energy of the FBs depends only on the angular parameters
\phi_{v}
(
v=A,B
), and not on
\theta_{v}
, which affect solely the dispersive bands. Previous works
35
treated the case where both wave functions and their derivatives are continuous at
A
and
B

(\theta_{v},\phi_{v})=(0,\pi)
for
v=A,B
. Throughout our analysis we allow for different scattering matrices,
S_{A}
and
S_{B}

A
-type and
B
-type vertices, which generalizes previous work assuming
S_{A}=S_{B}

Compact localized states.
Since the extended Bloch states forming the FB are degenerate in energy, their superpositions are still eigenstates and can have compact supports
6
;
51
;
27
;
24
. Here we build explicitly the CLS corresponding to the FBs Eq. (
6
) of the HQG by following three steps. First, we set all the amplitudes
\alpha_{m,n,i}
and
\beta_{m,n,i}
to zero everywhere over the HQG, except on a selected hexagon (Fig. (
2
)). Second, we choose the remaining amplitudes to follows the alternating pattern
(\alpha_{b},\beta_{b})=(\alpha,\beta)(-1)^{b}
, around the hexagon, where
b=0,..,5
is a bond index. As a result, any scattering in- or out- state at any corner of the hexagon looks like
\ket{\alpha}_{v}=\alpha(1,-1,0)
or a circular permutation, and similarly
\ket{\beta}_{v}=\beta(1,-1,0)
or a circular permutation. We observe that such vectors span the plane orthogonal to
\ket{e_{3}}=(1,1,1)/\sqrt{3}
. Therefore the action of the
S_{A,B}
matrices on such in- and out-scattering states is very simple, and leads to a single scalar equation
\beta=e^{2iq+i\phi_{A}}\alpha
at the 3
A
-corners of the selected hexagon, and
\alpha=e^{i\phi_{B}}\beta
at all the 3
B
-corners. Combining those two equations leads directly to Eq. (
6
) already obtained in the Bloch representation. Since this construction can be performed for any hexagon, it is an alternative proof of the existence of FBs ruled by the Bohr–Sommerfeld condition (
6
). For the particular case of Neumann boundary conditions
\phi_{A}=\phi_{B}=\pi
, we recover the Dirichlet eigenstates
35
where the CLS have nodes at vertices. More generally, one can follow the number and locations of the nodal points in the CLS, depending on the FB number
n
and on the parameters
(\phi_{A},\phi_{B})
. It is possible to go from CLS to Bloch scattering states by summing the CLS over unit cells with appropriate phase, and vice versa. It is interesting to note that both the FBs and the CLSs discussed above are robust to the introduction of a longitudinal localized potential
V(s)
acting on the bonds, thereby generalizing the results of
35
which considered symmetric
V(s)
with respect to the center of a bond.

Multichannel model with a sharp transverse confining potential.

Typical experimental settings of HQG involve multiple transverse channels due to the wire width
w
. To model such more realistic situations, we introduce local coordinates for each bond
{\boldsymbol{s}}=(s,s_{\perp})
that gather the longitudinal coordinate
s
and the transverse one
s_{\perp}\in[-w/2,w/2]
with respect to its axis. Using strict boundary conditions, corresponding to an infinite potential well in the
s_{\perp}
direction, the wave function
\Psi_{\boldsymbol{l}}{({\boldsymbol{s}})}=\Psi_{\boldsymbol{l}}{(s,s_{\perp})}
reads :

\Psi_{\boldsymbol{l}}({\boldsymbol{s}})=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N}\alpha_{\mu,{\boldsymbol{l}}}\,e^{iq_{\mu}s}\,\Phi_{\mu}(s_{\perp})+\beta_{\mu,{\boldsymbol{l}}}\,e^{-iq_{\mu}s}\,\Phi_{\mu}^{*}(s_{\perp})\,,

(7)

where the transverse envelope functions
\Phi_{\mu}(s_{\perp})
vanish at the lateral walls of each wire :
\Phi_{\mu}(w/2)=\Phi_{\mu}(-w/2)=0
. The sum is over the
N=1+Int[q(E)w/\pi]
transverse channels, labelled by the integer index
\mu
and carrying longitudinal momenta
q_{\mu}(E)=\sqrt{E-(\pi\mu/w)^{2}}
. Owing to mirror symmetry with respect to each wire axis,
(s,s_{\perp})\rightarrow(s,-s_{\perp})
, the transverse modes are classified in two categories according to their parity :
N_{+}
even modes and
N_{-}
odd modes, with
N=N_{+}+N_{-}
. At vertex
v=A,B
, the multichannel scattering matrix
S_{v}
belongs to
U(3N)/D_{3}
. Using group theory (see
1
for details) we obtain the tensor decomposition :

S_{v}=S_{v,0}\otimes P_{\perp}+\mathrm{diag}(S_{v,+},S_{v,-})\otimes P_{\parallel}\,\,.

(8)

Here the projectors
P_{\perp}
and
P_{\parallel}
(see Eq. (
4
)) act in the wire/leg space while
S_{v,0}\in U(N)
and
\mathrm{diag}(S_{v,+},S_{v,-})\in U(N_{+})\oplus U(N_{-})
act in the transverse channel space; the block-diagonal structure of
S_{v,\pm}
shows that mixing between even and odd subspaces are forbidden for the
P_{\parallel}
sector by the
D_{3}
symmetry.
The multichannel secular equation is a direct generalization of the single channel case Eq. (
3
) and reads

\det\left((e^{iQ(E)}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger})S_{B}(e^{iQ(E)}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}})S_{A}-\mathbb{I}_{3N}\right)=0\,,

(9)

where
Q=\mathrm{diag}(q_{1},q_{3},...,q_{2N_{+}-1},q_{2},q_{4},...,q_{2N_{-}})
collects the propagating wave vectors of the odd- and even-parity channels.

A natural question concerning FBs is whether they persist in the multichannel case. To answer this, we plot in Fig.
3
the low energy spectrum obtained from numerically solving the secular equation (
9
) for two representative cases
N_{\pm}=2
and
N_{\pm}=5
, with arbitrary scattering matrices chosen according to Eq. (
8
). Remarkably, we observe multiple FBs
in both cases; furthermore, they appear in a ratio of one FB for two dispersive ones, suggesting that FBs are ubiquitous and have a prescribed density in the spectrum. Indeed, we prove in
1
that the existence of FBs is a direct consequence of the
D_{3}
symmetry and the decomposition Eq. (
8
). Finally an argument using random matrix theory is used to characterize the density of the FBs. We expect all these results to hold for HQG with arbitrarily large number of channels, which is relevant for clean metallic wires.

Conclusion.
Using the honeycomb design, FBs can be obtained generically in clean 2DEG patterned by antidots or gating, or in metallic wire networks regardless of the details of the connections. We have also obtained similar FBs for the 3D diamond structure, being the 3D counterpart of the honeycomb structure.

In addition to gated high-mobility 2DEGs and molecule-patterned metallic surfaces, our proposed FBs can also be naturally realized in photonic and phononic crystals, microwave and optical waveguide networks, artificial graphene, engineered metamaterials. Concerning electronics, our model can be used to study correlation effects including superconductivity and spin liquids, and explore the interplay between flat bands and correlations in moiré graphene
12
, rhombohedral graphite
68
and kagome metals
45
. Another interesting perspective concerns the disorder effects on FBs, localization (or antilocalization) effects and quantum transport outside the ballistic regime.

Appendix B
Group theory derivation on the multichannel case

Here we present a detailed derivation of the multichannel
S
matrix for the 2D honeycomb and 3D diamond lattices
2
2

2

This construction applies to any “homogeneous” lattices, namely lattice graphs that are regular, and transforms transitively under the lattice symmetry group.
. Quite generally, we denote
Z
as the degree of (any) vertex,
N_{+}
(
N_{-}
) the number of parity-even (parity-odd) channels on each edge,
N=N_{+}+N_{-}
the total number of channels on each edge, and
G
the onsite symmetry group of the vertex. Then the
Z\,N
channels form a tensor product of two representations of the group
G

\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{Z}\cong(\mathbf{1}_{+}^{\otimes N_{+}}\oplus\mathbf{1}_{-}^{\otimes N_{-}})\otimes\mathbf{Perm},

(27)

where
\mathbf{Perm}
denotes the
Z
-dimensional representation formed by the edge basis
|x\rangle
,
x=1,2,...,Z
, on which all point group operation acts as certain permutation of those edges. The
\mathbf{Perm}
representation is generally reducible, as the vector
|e_{Z}\rangle=(1,...,1)^{T}/\sqrt{Z}
is obviously an invariant one-dimensional subspace of
G
. The task is then to first decompose the
Z
-dimensional
\mathbf{Perm}
into irreps, and then the
Z\,N
-dimensional representation
\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{Z}
into irreps; this gives the most general parameterization of the
S
matrix allowed by the symmetry
G
.

B.1
2D Honeycomb

The 2D honeycomb network has a site symmetry group of
D_{3}
, generated by a threefold rotation
C_{3}
and a mirror reflection
M
. (The order-2 element
M
can be equivalently interpreted as a twofold rotation
C^{\prime}_{2}
whose axis lies in the plane – These interpretations do not alter any analysis below.) The degree of vertex is

. The permutation representation
\mathbb{C}^{3}=\mathbf{Perm}
is defined in the basis of three edges
|x\rangle
,
x=1,2,3
, that
are permuted by
D_{3}
according to

O_{C_{3}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ O_{M}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(28)

We have

\mathbf{Perm}=\mathbf{1}_{+}\oplus\mathbf{2},

(29)

where the trivial representation
\mathbf{1}_{+}
is the invariant subspace
|e\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,1,1)^{T}
.
Plugging Eq. (

\mathbf{1}_{+}\otimes R=R

D_{3}

\mathbf{2}

To obtain a concrete decomposition of

\lambda

\lambda

(31)

where
d_{\lambda}

\chi_{\lambda}(g)

U_{C_{3}}=\mathbb{I}_{N}\otimes O_{C_{3}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ U_{M}=\text{diag}(\mathbb{I}_{N_{+}},-\mathbb{I}_{N_{-}})\otimes O_{M},

P_{\mathbf{1}_{+}}=P_{+}\otimes P,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ P_{\mathbf{1}_{-}}=P_{-}\otimes P,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ P_{\mathbf{2}}=\mathbb{I}_{N}\otimes(2\cdot\mathbb{I}_{3}-O_{C_{3}}-O_{C_{3}}^{2}),

(33)

which give a decomposition of identity
\mathbb{I}_{3N}=P_{+}\otimes P+P_{-}\otimes P+\mathbb{I}_{N}\otimes(\mathbb{I}_{3}-P)

To obtain a concrete parametrization of the scattering matrix
S
, notice that within each sector of irreducible subspaces with multiplicity, the degenerate subspaces can freely rotate among them. The subspaces of
\mathbf{1}_{+}
,
\mathbf{1}_{-}
, and
\mathbf{2}

,
N_{-}

Y_{+}\in U(N_{+}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ Y_{-}\in U(N_{-}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ X\in U(N).

\mathbb{C}^{3N}

(35)

Appendix C
Proof for the existence of flat bands in the multichannel case

Here we show one of the main results of this work, namely that flat bands persist in the multichannel case. We use the tensor decomposition of the
S_{v}
matrices given by Eq. (
8

S_{v}=S_{v,0}\otimes P_{\perp}+\mathrm{diag}(S_{v,+},S_{v,-})\otimes P_{\parallel}\,\,,

(40)

and a generalization of the type of linear algebra argument already presented for the single channel case in the main text. To this purpose, we look for eigenvectors
\ket{\phi}\otimes\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}
that solve the following eigenproblem with eigenvalue one :

(e^{iQ(E)}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger})S_{B}(e^{iQ(E)}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}})S_{A}[\ket{\phi}\otimes\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}]=\ket{\phi}\otimes\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\,,

such that

does not depends on Bloch momentum
{\boldsymbol{k}}
. The difficulty is that
D_{\boldsymbol{k}}
and

does depend on
{\boldsymbol{k}}
and do not commute with the projectors
P_{\perp}
and
P_{\parallel}
involved in the scattering matrices

with

As in the single channel case, the main idea is to choose a class of states such that

and

. For such states, it is easy to compute the action of such chain of matrices. First, we start by acting on the ket as :

(e^{iQ(E)}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}})S_{A}\,[\ket{\phi}\otimes\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}]=e^{iQ(E)}S_{A,0}\ket{\phi}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}}\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\,,

(42)

Then we apply the action of rest of the matrix chain to obtain :

(e^{iQ(E)}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger})S_{B}\,[e^{iQ(E)}S_{A,0}\ket{\phi}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}}\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}]\,=e^{iQ(E)}S_{B,0}e^{iQ(E)}S_{A,0}\ket{\phi}\otimes D_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\dagger}D_{\boldsymbol{k}}\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}},

(43)

So finally the eigenvalue problem reduces to, for the class of states we have selected :

e^{iQ(E)}S_{B,0}e^{iQ(E)}S_{A,0}\ket{\phi}\otimes\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\ket{\phi}\otimes\ket{\chi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\,,

(44)

since

and vector

\boxed{e^{iQ(E)}S_{B,0}e^{iQ(E)}S_{A,0}\ket{\phi}=\ket{\phi}}\,

\mathrm{det}(e^{iQ(E)}S_{B,0}e^{iQ(E)}S_{A,0}-\mathbb{I}_{N})\neq 0\text{ for any }E\in[(\pi N/w)^{2},\infty)

then we have (a proof is provided shortly):

\text{Any eigenvalue }\lambda_{\mu}(z):=e^{i\vartheta_{\mu}(z)}\text{ of }e^{iQ(z)}S_{B,0}e^{iQ(z)}S_{A,0}\text{ contributes a finite phase }\vartheta_{\mu}(z)\in[-\pi,\pi]\text{ to }f(z).

(51)

but this implies that
f(z)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{N}\vartheta_{\mu}(z)\in[-N\pi,N\pi]
is bounded, contradicting the unboundedness of
f(z)
on
z=E\in[(\pi N/w)^{2},\infty)
. This shows that the assumption (
50
) is incorrect, proving (
46
).

Below we provide a technical proof for the statement (
51
).
\mathrm{det}(e^{iQ(E)}S_{B,0}e^{iQ(E)}S_{A,0}-\mathbb{I}_{N})\neq 0
implies that the following Cayley transform is well-defined:

H(z)=i\frac{\mathbb{I}_{N}+e^{iQ(z)}S_{B,0}e^{iQ(z)}S_{A,0}}{\mathbb{I}_{N}-e^{iQ(z)}S_{B,0}e^{iQ(z)}S_{A,0}},

(52)

and the phase
\vartheta_{\mu}(z)
is related to the eigenvalue
h_{\mu}(z)
of
H(z)
by

\vartheta_{\mu}=\mathrm{arg}\left(\frac{\lambda_{\mu}-i}{\lambda_{\mu}+i}\right)=-2\arctan(1/h_{\mu})\in(-\pi,\pi),

(53)

proving (
51
).

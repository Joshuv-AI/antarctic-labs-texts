A virtual structure for symplectic Higgs bundles
Simon Schirren

Abstract




Abstract.
We propose a virtual structure for a moduli of symplectic Higgs sheaves
(E,\phi)
on projective surfaces
S
. Key to this is a minimality assumption on
\mathrm{ch}(E)
that forces all
E
to be locally free. This might have implications to define a virtual count and
Sp(r)
-Vafa-Witten invariants.

1.
Introduction

In
[
VW
]
, Vafa and Witten discuss solutions to the supersymmetric Yang-Mills equations and explain relations to the Euler characteristic of the instanton moduli space of sheaves on a real 4-manifold, underlying a smooth complex surface
S
. A mathematical definition for such a moduli space of
Higgs sheaves
on
S
and an enumerative geometry yielding
Vafa-Witten invariants
has been proposed by Tanaka and Thomas
[
TT1
]
for the gauge groups
U(r)
and
SU(r)
.

Fixing invariants
\mathrm{ch}(E)
, Higgs sheaves
(E,\phi)
- where
E
is a torsion-free sheaf on
S
of
\mathrm{rank}(E)=r
and
\phi\in\mathrm{Hom}(E,E\otimes K_{S})
- appear in the stable locus
\curly{N}\subset T^{*}[-1]\curly{M}
inside the
(-1)
-shifted cotangent bundle to the moduli stack of torsion-free sheaves
\curly{M}
.

Via spectral theory,
\curly{N}
can equivalently be described as a moduli space for stable torsion sheaves
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on
X=\mathrm{Tot}(K_{S})\xrightarrow{\pi}S
, a non-compact Calabi-Yau threefold.
\curly{N}
admits a
virtual cotangent bundle

V^{\bullet}=[V^{-1}\rightarrow V^{0}]
that admits a
(-1)
-
shifted symplectic structure

\theta:V^{\bullet}\rightarrow V^{\bullet,\vee}[1].

The corresponding Donaldson-Thomas type invariant comes from the associated symmetric
U(r)
-perfect obstruction theory on
\curly{N}
,

\psi:V^{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathbf{L}{N}.

In order to define a
virtual count
, one uses its natural
\textbf{C}^{\times}
-action

(E,\phi)\mapsto(E,\lambda\phi)

and does equivariant localization to define the
U(r)
-Vafa-Witten invariant
as in
[
TT1
, 3.18]
.

Unfortunately, this invariant turns out to be zero in many cases, so one has to modify the moduli space, which is roughly done as follows: Taking determinants

E\mapsto\det(E),

we get a map

(1.0.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\curly{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Pic}(S)\times\Gamma(K_{S})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\det\pi_{*},\mathrm{tr})}

given by

(E,\phi)\rightarrow(\det(E),\mathrm{tr}(\phi)).

As
\mathbf{Pic}(S)
has trivial tangent bundle and
H^{1}(\curly{O}_{S})^{*}\cong H^{1}(K_{S})
, one may think of
1.0.1
as an instance of a map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{T}^{*}[-1]\curly{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{*}[-1]\mathbf{Pic}(S),}

which, unfortunately, we have not yet succeeded in defining properly.

The sub-moduli space
\curly{N}_{SU(r)}\subset\curly{N}
relative
\mathbf{Pic}(S)\times\Gamma(K_{S})
admits again a symmetric obstruction theory and defines a better invariant via
\textbf{C}^{\times}
-localization, which gives access to more surfaces
S
, see
[
TT1
, 6]
. This invariant is given by

\mathsf{VW}_{\curly{N}_{SU(r)}}=\int_{[\curly{N}_{SU(r)}^{\textbf{C}^{\times}}]^{vir}}\frac{1}{e(\nu^{vir})}\in\mathbf{Q}

Here, the denominator is the Euler class of the virtual normal bundle
\nu^{vir}
to the fixed locus
\curly{N}^{\textbf{C}^{\times}}\subset\curly{N}
.

In other words, this is an enumerative theory for Higgs bundles of gauge group
SL(r)
, i.e. those pairs
(E,\phi)
of
\curly{N}
, where
\det(E)\cong\mathcal{L}
is fixed and
\mathrm{tr}(\phi)=0
. This has been computed by many authors, see e.g. the work of
[
GK
]
and
[
GKL
]
and for Enrique surfaces, recent work of
[
O
]
.

In view of
S
-duality, we interested in defining similar invariants for Higgs sheaves of other gauge groups, such as the orthosymplectic ones
O(r)
and
Sp(r)
.

1.1.
Outline of the paper

The idea is that bundles
E
of
\curly{M}
of gauge group
O(r)
or
Sp(r)
can be expressed as fixed points of an involution

E\mapsto E^{*}.

The involution gives an action on the deformation-obstruction complex at each point,

\mathbf{R}\Gamma(\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(E,E)[1])\rightarrow\mathbf{R}\Gamma(\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(E^{*},E^{*})[1]),

given by

\phi\mapsto-\phi^{*}.

Over
\curly{N}\subset T^{*}[-1]\curly{M}
, we get an action on stable sheaves given by

\iota:(E,\phi)\mapsto(E^{*},-\phi^{*}).

Fixed pairs of
\curly{N}^{\iota}\subset\curly{N}
under this action are given by triples
(E,\phi,f)
, with a non-degenerate pairing

f:E\xrightarrow{\sim}E^{*}

intertwining
\phi
with
-\phi^{*}
, i.e. there is a commutative square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\otimes K_{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{*}\otimes K_{S}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\phi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes 1}

It turns out (using that stable sheaves are simple) that
f
is
either
symmetric
f^{*}=f
and
E
is an
O(r)
-bundle together with a section
\phi
of
\mathfrak{so}(E)\otimes K_{S}

or

f^{*}=-f
is symplectic and
E
is an
Sp(r)
-bundle together with a section
\phi
of
\mathfrak{sp}(E)\otimes K_{S}
. Accordingly, in Sec.
4
we will show that we get under certain assumptions two components
\curly{N}_{O(r)}
and
\curly{N}_{Sp(r)}
of
\curly{N}^{\iota}
.

To address the virtual structure, we need to phrase this action in terms of spectral sheaves
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on
X
, which is done in Sec.
5
. The deformation-obstruction theory of spectral sheaves is
symmetric

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\phi},\mathcal{E}_{\phi})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\phi},\mathcal{E}_{\phi})^{*},

and we want to lift
\iota
to an action on the complex

\iota_{*}:\textbf{R}\Gamma(\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(\mathcal{E}_{\phi},\mathcal{E}_{\phi})[1])\rightarrow\textbf{R}\Gamma(\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(\mathcal{E}_{-\phi^{*}},\mathcal{E}_{-\phi^{*}})[1]).

This will be done directly in families of spectral families
\curly{E}
, where will get an action of
\iota
on

\mathbf{T}{N}^{vir}=\tau^{[0,1]}\mathbf{R}\curly{H}om_{p_{X}}(\curly{E},\curly{E})[1],

compatible with the natural action on
\mathbf{T}{N}=\mathbf{L}{N}^{\vee}
via the Atiyah class
\mathrm{At}_{\curly{E},\curly{N}}
, see Sec.
7
.

This is enough to meet the requirements of equivariant localization in the sense of
[
GP
]
, where we replace
\textbf{C}^{\times}
by
\langle\iota\rangle
. The final part is analogous to
[
Sch
]
, where we use the
U(r)
-obstruction theory of
[
TT1
]
, which is now
\iota
-equivariant and localize it to
\curly{N}_{Sp(r)}\subset\curly{N}^{\iota}
. This gives a perfect obstruction theory on
\curly{N}_{Sp(r)}
and endows it with a virtual cycle
[\curly{N}_{Sp(r)}]^{vir}
.

Key to all of this is an assumption on
c_{2}(E)
when fixing
\mathrm{ch}(E)
that assures that all pairs of
\curly{N}
are in fact locally free. Only this will make
(E,\phi)\mapsto(E^{*},-\phi^{*})
into a globally well-defined involution, see
2.7
.

1.2.
Outlook

We are interested in generalizations to arbitrary
\mathrm{ch}(E)
that would include torsion-free sheaves as well. A different approach that we would like to compare with involves replacing
\curly{N}
by a moduli space of complexes and a different stability condition; see recent work of
[
B
]
.

Furthermore, we would like to show that the virtual structure on
\curly{N}_{Sp(r)}
is again symmetric, so
[\curly{N}_{Sp(r)}]^{vir}
is degree
0
and we can set up new Vafa-Witten invariants using torus localization as in
[
TT1
]
.

Finally, we remark that although under certain assumptions both
O(r)
and
Sp(r)
appear in
\curly{N}
, we restrict to
Sp(r)
for this discussion. There are some stability issues with
O(r)
and as
Sp(2)\cong SL(2)
,
\curly{N}_{Sp(2)}
has already a virtual theory as discussed in
[
TT1
]
and
[
Sch
]
.

2.
Higgs pairs

2.1.
Higgs pairs

We fix a smooth projective surface
S
over
\mathbf{C}
with polarization
\curly{O}_{S}(1)
. A Higgs pair

(E,\phi)\;\text{on}\;S

is a coherent sheaf
E
together with a map

\phi\in\Gamma(\curly{E}\!\mathit{nd}(E)\otimes K_{S})=\mathrm{Hom}(E,E\otimes K_{S}).

If
X:=\mathrm{Tot}(K_{S})\xrightarrow{\pi}S
denotes the total space of the canonical bundle, the spectral correspondence

\mathbf{Higgs}(S)\cong\mathbf{Coh}_{c}(S),

identifies pairs
(E,\phi)
with their spectral sheaves

\mathcal{E}_{\phi}\;\text{on}\;X,

which are compactly supported sheaves on
X
.

Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
form an abelian category
\mathbf{Higgs}(S)
, where a morphism

f:(E,\phi)\rightarrow(E^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})

is a morphism

f:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}

that intertwines
\phi
with
\phi^{\prime}
, i.e.

f\phi=\phi^{\prime}f.

Given
(E,\phi)
, we get
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
as the sheaf of eigenspaces of
\phi
acting on
E
, supported on the corresponding eigenvalues in
K_{S}
.

And given
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
, we recover

(E,\phi)=(\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}_{\phi},\pi_{*}(\tau\cdot\mathrm{id})),

where
\tau\cdot\mathrm{id}
is the tautological endomorphism on
X
.

We refer to
[
TT1
, 2.2]
for the details of the spectral construction.

Resolutions

There is a natural resolution of
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
in terms of
(E,\phi)
, see
[
TT1
, 2.11.]
. Namely, if
\tau
denotes the tautological section of
\pi^{*}K_{S}
, this is given by

(2.2.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}E\otimes K_{S}^{-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{\phi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}\phi-\tau\cdot\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ev}

Remark 2.2.2
.

An important observation is that if
E
is a bundle,
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
has
projective dimension

\mathrm{pd}(\mathcal{E}_{\phi})
equal to
1
.

2.3.
Different gauge groups

We start with some preliminaries:

For a locally free sheaf
E
with dual
E^{*}=\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(E,\curly{O}_{S})
, we have a natural isomorphism
E^{*}\otimes E^{*}\cong\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(E,E^{*})
and taking duals
f\mapsto f^{*}
defines an involution with
\pm 1
eigensheaves

\curly{S}ym^{2}(E^{*})\oplus\wedge^{2}E^{*}.

Sections of the former are symmetric
f^{*}=f
and section of the latter skew
f^{*}=-f
.

If
E
is endowed with a non-degenerate pairing

f:E\xrightarrow{\sim}E^{*},

the triple
(E,f,\phi)
is an orthogonal Higgs bundle or
O(r)
-bundle, if
f
is symmetric and

\phi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{so}(E)\otimes K_{S})\subset\Gamma(\curly{E}\!\mathit{nd}(E)\otimes K_{S})

is a section of the
K_{S}
-twisted Lie algebra bundle of skew-symmetric endomorphisms.

We call the triple a symplectic Higgs bundle or
Sp(r)
-bundle if
f
is skew and

\phi\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{sp}(E)\otimes K_{S})\subset\Gamma(\curly{E}\!\mathit{nd}(E)\otimes K_{S})

is a section of the
K_{S}
-twisted Lie algebra bundle of symmetric endomorphisms.

We refer to App.
10.3
for some background.

2.4.
SO(r)

We call an
O(r)
-bundle
(E,f,\phi)
together with an
orientation

o:\curly{O}\xrightarrow{\sim}\wedge^{r}E
an
SO(r)
-bundle, see
[
OT
, Def.2.1]
. For example, every
O(2)
-bundle splits into its isotropic lines
E\cong L\oplus M
. In this simplest case, choosing an orientation is the same as fixing an isomorphism
M\xrightarrow{\sim}L^{*}
, such that
E\cong L\oplus L^{*}
and
\wedge^{2}E\cong L\otimes L^{*}
and the map

\curly{O}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}L\otimes L^{*}\xrightarrow{a\otimes b\mapsto-b\otimes a}L^{*}\otimes L\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev}}\curly{O}

is given by
1\mapsto-1
.

2.6.
Stability

Definition 2.6.1
.

Denoting
H:=c_{1}(\curly{O}_{S}(1))
on
S
, we define the slope of
E
to be

\mu(E):=\frac{\deg(E)}{\mathrm{rank}(E)},

where
\deg(E):=\int_{S}c_{1}(E).H
.

We call the pair
(E,\phi)
slope semi-stable or
\mu
-semi-stable, if

\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)

for all proper
\phi
-invariant subsheaves
F
on
S
and we call
(E,\phi)
slope stable, if the inequality is strict.

Definition 2.6.2
.

The reduced Hilbert polynomial of
E
is given by

p_{E}(n):=\frac{P_{E}(n)}{\mathrm{rank}(E)}

and we call
(E,\phi)
Gieseker semi-stable if we have

p_{F}(n)\leq p_{E}(n)\;\text{for}\;n\gg 0,

and we define Gieseker stability again by making the inequality strict.

Remark 2.6.3
.

If
c_{1}(E)=0
,
p_{E}
is given by

(2.6.4)

\displaystyle P_{E}(n)=r\chi(\curly{O}_{S})-c_{2}(E)+\frac{1}{2}(rnHc_{1}(S)+r(r+1)n^{2}H^{2}),

which follows from simplifying the general formula of App.
10.2.1
.

We recall the following standard fact, see
[
HL
, 1.2.7]
:

Lemma 2.6.5
.

Let
f:(E,\phi)\rightarrow(E^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
be a non-zero map of semi-stable Higgs pairs with
p_{E}=p_{E^{\prime}}
. Assume that
(E,\phi)
is stable. Then
f
is an isomorphism.

Claim 2.6.6
.

If
(E,\phi)
is stable, so is
(E^{**},\phi^{**})
.

Proof.

As
c_{1}(E)=c_{1}(E^{**})=0
, we have
\mu(E)=\mu(E^{**})=0
.

Assuming that
(F,\psi)\subset(E^{**},\phi^{**})
is a Higgs sub-sheaf, pulling back along
\mathrm{ev}
gives a Higgs sub-sheaf
(E\cap F,\psi|_{E\cap F})
of both
(F,\psi)
and
(E,\phi)
.

Thus, as
E^{**}/E
is supported on points, so is
E/(E\cap F)
: we have
c_{1}(F).H=c_{1}(E\cap F).H<0
, as
(E,\phi)
is stable, hence
\mu(F)<0
as desired.
∎

Remark 2.6.7
.

We further remark that slope stability implies Gieseker stability, which implies slope semi-stability. Thus, if the invariants
\mathrm{ch}(E)
are chosen such that slope stability agrees with slope semi-stability, we get that Gieseker stability = slope stability.

2.7.
Moduli spaces

Fixing invariants
\mathrm{ch}(E)
such that slope semi-stability implies stability, there is a quasi-projective moduli scheme

\curly{N}=\curly{N}_{\mathrm{ch}(E)}

whose closed points
\mathbf{Spec}(\textbf{C})\rightarrow\curly{N}
parametrize isomorphism classes of stable Higgs pairs
[(E,\phi)]
. As stable sheaves are simple, we have

\mathrm{Aut}(E,\phi)\cong\mathbf{C}^{\times}.

We fix
\mathrm{ch}(E)=(r,0,c_{2})
such that semi-stability implies stability.

Claim 2.7.1
.

Let
c_{2}(E)
be of minimal degree such that
\curly{N}
is non-empty. Then all pairs
(E,\phi)
are locally free.

Proof.

E
is torsion free, we have a canonical embedding
E\hookrightarrow E^{**}
, extending uniquely to a map
(E,\phi)\hookrightarrow(E^{**},\phi^{**})
by Cor.
2.5.3
, which is an isomorphism if and only if
E
is locally free by Lem.
2.5.2
.

Assuming that
E
is not locally free,
\mathrm{rank}(E)=\mathrm{rank}(E^{**})
implies that
P_{E}<P_{E^{**}}
by stability of
(E,\phi)
. By the above expression for Hilbert polynomials
2.6.4
, this implies
c_{2}(E)>c_{2}(E^{**})
, which contradicts minimality of
c_{2}(E)
.
∎

3.
Involutions

We want to express pairs
(E,\phi)
of
\curly{N}
of gauge group
Sp(r)
and
O(r)
as fixed points of an involution. To make this into a well-defined action on moduli spaces, we need to address stability. To keep it simple, the involution will be first defined for single sheaves, then in families.

3.1.
Single sheaves

Definition 3.1.1
.

Fixing a Chern character
\mathrm{ch}(E)=(r,0,c_{2})
with
r>0
and
c_{2}(E)
minimal as above, we define

(E,\phi)\mapsto(E^{*},-\phi^{*}),

preserving
\mathrm{ch}(E)
.

We claim that this preserves stability:

Claim 3.1.2
.

If
(E,\phi)
is
\mu
-stable, so is
(E^{*},-\phi^{*})
.

Proof.

Note that
\mu(E)=\mu(E^{*})=0
. If
(E,\phi)
is stable, let
(E^{*},-\phi^{*})\twoheadrightarrow(Q,\psi)
be a Higgs quotient, so we need to show that
\deg(Q)>0
. By
[
HL
, 1.2.6]
we may assume that
Q
is torsion free. As
E
is reflexive,
(Q^{*},-\psi^{*})\subset(E,\phi)
is a Higgs sub-sheaf, so
\deg(Q^{*})<0
by stability, thus
\deg(Q)=-\deg(Q^{*})>0
.
∎

3.2.
Universal families

We have a universal family of Higgs bundles

(\mathsf{E},\Phi)\;\text{on}\;S\times\curly{N},

flat over
\curly{N}
.
Equivalently, this is a
\curly{N}
-flat universal family of spectral sheaves

\curly{E}\;\text{on}\;X\times\curly{N}.

So in particular, for a closed point
n:\textbf{Spec}(\textbf{C})\rightarrow\curly{N}
, corresponding to
\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
on
X
, we have
\curly{E}|_{X_{n}}\cong\mathcal{E}_{\phi}
.

As
\mathsf{E}
is a bundle, there is a two-term resolution of locally frees (
[
TT1
, 2.11]
),

\curly{E}^{\bullet}=[\curly{E}^{-1}\xrightarrow{d}\curly{E}^{0}]\twoheadrightarrow\curly{E}

We further remark that universal families always exists locally and globally perhaps only as a
twisted
universal family.

Furthermore, the deformation-obstruction complex

\mathbf{R}\curly{H}om_{p_{X}}(\curly{E},\curly{E})[1]\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(\curly{N})

is always a well-defined object.

3.3.
Classifying maps

As stability of
(E,\phi)
implies stability of the pair
(E^{*},-\phi^{*})
,
the functor

\iota:(\mathsf{E},\Phi)\mapsto(\mathsf{E}^{*},-\Phi^{*}),

preserves flatness and induces a classifying map

\iota:\curly{N}\rightarrow\curly{N},

such that

(\mathrm{id}\times\iota)^{*}(\mathsf{E},\Phi)\cong(\mathsf{E}^{*},-\Phi^{*}).

Thus,
\iota
is point-wise given by

[(E,\phi)]\mapsto[(E^{*},-\phi^{*})].

As bundles are reflexive, we immediately see that

10.1.
Dual bundles

Let
A,B
be vector bundles over a variety
S
. Let
A^{*}=\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(A,\curly{O})
be the bundle of dual sections.
Under the natural isomorphism
A^{*}\otimes B\xrightarrow{\sim}\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(A,B)
, given by
\sigma\otimes b\mapsto\sigma\lrcorner\otimes b:a\mapsto\sigma(a)b
, taking duals
\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(A,B)\rightarrow\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(B^{*},A^{*}),\phi\mapsto\phi^{*}
is the same thing as swapping the factors
\sigma\otimes b\mapsto b\otimes\sigma
where a section
b
of
B
acts on a section
\tau
of
B^{*}
via
ev:B\xrightarrow{\sim}B^{**}
.

More precisely, the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{*}\otimes B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(A,B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\otimes A^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(B^{*},A^{*})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{swap}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\mapsto f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

commutes.

Proof.

Let
a,\tau
be sections of
A,B^{*}
respectively.

As discussed above, following
\sigma\otimes b
down the LHS gives the map

b\lrcorner\otimes\sigma:\tau\mapsto[a\mapsto\tau(b)\sigma(a)].

On the RHS, we get the map
\tau\mapsto(\sigma\lrcorner\otimes b)^{*}\tau
, given by

\tau\mapsto[a\mapsto\tau\circ(\sigma\lrcorner\otimes b)(a)=\tau(\sigma(a)b)=\sigma(a)\tau(b)].

∎

In the case that we have
B=A^{*}
, swapping the factors
\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2}\mapsto\sigma_{2}\otimes\sigma_{1}
defines an involution on
\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(A,A^{*})=A^{*}\otimes A^{*}
, splitting into
\pm 1
eigensheaves

\curly{S}ym^{2}(A^{*})\oplus\wedge^{2}A^{*}.

Sections of the former are symmetric (self-dual) maps
\sigma_{1}\lrcorner\otimes\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{2}\lrcorner\otimes\sigma_{1}
and sections of the latter anti-symmetric (skew)
\sigma_{1}\lrcorner\otimes\sigma_{2}=-\sigma_{2}\lrcorner\otimes\sigma_{1}
. We remark:

Remark 10.1.1
.

A map
\sigma_{1}\lrcorner\otimes\sigma_{2}:A\rightarrow A^{*}
is skew if and only if

\sigma_{1}(a)\sigma_{2}(b)=-\sigma_{1}(b)\sigma_{2}(a).

10.3.
Orthosymplectic bundles

Definition 10.3.1
.

We call a pair
(E,q)
consisting of a rank-
r
vector bundle
E
over
S
with a symmetric isomorphism

q:E\xrightarrow{\sim}E^{*}

an orthogonal bundle.

We call a pair
(E,\omega)
a symplectic bundle, if we replace
q
by a skew-symmetric isomorphism

\omega:E\xrightarrow{\sim}E^{*}.

We denote the associated pairing in both cases as
e\mapsto e^{*}=(e_{,}\_)
.

Remark 10.3.2
.

If
E=\Lambda\oplus\Lambda^{*}
where
\Lambda\subset E
is a
\mathrm{rank}
-n sub-bundle, then it admits both a natural symmetric
q
and skew pairing
\omega
.

Here, the symmetric pairing

q:\Lambda\oplus\Lambda^{*}\times\Lambda^{*}\oplus\Lambda\rightarrow\curly{O}

is given by

((a,\sigma),(\sigma^{\prime},b))\mapsto\sigma^{\prime}(a)+\sigma(b)

and we define
\omega
by replacing
"+"
by
"-"
on the RHS.

10.3.1.
Orthogonal is skew

We remark that we have a splitting

\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(E,E^{*})\cong\curly{S}ym^{2}(E^{*})\oplus\wedge^{2}E^{*}

into symmetric maps
f=f^{*}
and skew maps
f=-f^{*}
.

Definition 10.3.3
.

Fixing an orthogonal bundle
(E,q)
, we define the orthogonal group
O(E)\subset GL(E)
to be those invertible maps
f
such that

(f(a),f(b))=(a,b)

holds.

Accordingly, we define

\mathfrak{so}(E)\subset\curly{E}\!\mathit{nd}(E)

to be the bundle of Lie algebras consisting of all endomorphisms
\phi
with

(\phi(a),b)+(a,\phi(b))=0.

Writing
\phi=\sigma\lrcorner\otimes e
, this means that

(\phi(a),b)=\sigma(a)e^{*}(b)

is equal to

-(a,\phi(b))=-(\phi(b),a)=-\sigma(b)e^{*}(a).

Corollary 10.3.4
.

Let
(E,q)
be an orthogonal bundle. Then the isomorphism

q_{*}:\curly{E}\!\mathit{nd}(E)\xrightarrow{\sim}\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(E,E^{*}),

sending

\sigma\lrcorner\otimes e\mapsto\sigma\lrcorner\otimes e^{*},

identifies

\mathfrak{so}(E)\cong\wedge^{2}E^{*}.

Proof.

By the above observation and Rmk.
10.1.1
,
\sigma\lrcorner\otimes e
is a section of
\mathfrak{so}(E)
if and only if
\sigma\lrcorner\otimes e^{*}
is skew.
∎

10.3.2.
Symplectic is symmetric

Definition 10.3.5
.

Replacing the symmetric structure
q
by a skew symmetric one
\omega:E\xrightarrow{\sim}E^{*}
, we have for a symplectic bundle
(E,\omega)
the sub-sheaf of symplectic maps
{Sp}(E)\subset GL(E)
, i.e. those maps
f
preserving
\omega
:

(f(a),f(b))=(a,b).

We denote the corresponding bundle of Lie algebras as

\mathfrak{sp}(E)\subset\curly{E}\!\mathit{nd}(E)

satisfying again

(\phi(a),b)+(a,\phi(b))=0.

Corollary 10.3.6
.

The map

\omega_{*}:\curly{E}\!\mathit{nd}(E)\xrightarrow{\sim}\curly{H}\!\mathit{om}(E,E^{*})

identifies

\mathfrak{sp}(E)\cong\curly{S}ym^{2}(E^{*}).

Proof.

An endomorphism
\phi=\sigma\lrcorner\otimes e
is in
\mathfrak{sp}(E)
if

(\phi(a),b)=\sigma(a)e^{*}(b)

is equal to

-(a,\phi(b))=(\phi(b),a)=\sigma(b)e^{*}(a),

so
\sigma\lrcorner\otimes e
is in
\mathfrak{sp}(E)
if and only
\sigma\lrcorner\otimes e^{*}
is symmetric.
∎

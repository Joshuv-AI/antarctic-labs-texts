Non-commutative Law of iterated logarithm
Sourav Panja, Éric Ricard, Diptesh Saha

Abstract
. We prove optimal non-commutative analogues of the classical Law of Iterated Logarithm (LIL) for both martingales and sequences of independent (non-commutative) random variables. The classical martingale version was established by Stout [ Sto70b ] and the independent case by Hartman–Wintner [ HW41 ] . Our approach relies on a key exponential inequality essentially due to Randrianantoanina [ Ran24 ] that improves that from Junge and Zeng [ JZ15 ] . It allows to derive an optimal non-commutative Stout-type LIL just as in [ Zen15 ] ; from that martingale result we then deduce a non-commutative Hartman–Wintner-type LIL for independent sequences of random variables.

Primary 46L53, 46L55; Secondary 60F15.

1.
Introduction

Within probability theory, the
law of the iterated logarithm
(LIL) occupies a unique position among limit theorems: while the strong law of large numbers (LLN) describes the almost sure convergence of normalized sums to a constant, and the central limit theorem (CLT) characterizes the limiting distribution of these sums after normalization, the LIL precisely identifies the scale of the fluctuations that remain between these two extremes. The earliest contributions in the context of independent increments are due to Khintchine, Kolmogorov, and Hartman–Wintner; a historical overview can be found in
[
Bau96
]
. Stout subsequently extended the LIL of Kolmogorov and Hartman–Wintner to the martingale setting in
[
Sto70b
,
Sto70a
]
.

Beyond the scalar case, analogues of the LIL have been established for sums of independent random variables taking values in Banach spaces, due to Kuelbs, Ledoux, Talagrand, Pisier, and others (see
[
LT91
]
for detailed references).
In recent decades, non-commutative probability theory has emerged as a powerful generalization of classical probability, motivated by the study of operator algebras, quantum statistical mechanics, and free probability. In this setting, the role of the probability space is played by a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
equipped with a faithful, normal, tracial state
\tau
, and random variables correspond to
\tau
-measurable operators affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
. In this framework, convergence theorems for martingales and ergodic theorems have been studied extensively (cf.
[
Cuc71
]
,
[
Jun02
]
,
[
Lan76
]
,
[
JX07
]
and references therein). On the other hand, both the LLN and the CLT have also received considerable attention in the literature; see, for example,
[
Bat79
,
Łuc85
,
QSS17
,
Voi91
]
and the references cited there. However, the progress on the LIL has been more recent and relatively limited in the non-commutative setting. In particular, Konwerska
[
Kon08
]
obtained a version of the Hartman–Wintner type LIL for sequences of independent self-adjoint operators and Zeng
[
Zen15
]
an analogue of Stout’s result that we both aim to improve.

The martingale version of Kolmogorov’s LIL is due to Stout
[
Sto70b
]
. Next we recall this first. Let
(X_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a martingale with
\mathbb{E}[X_{n}]=0
and define the martingale differences
Y_{n}=X_{n}-X_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
, with
X_{0}=0
. Set

s_{n}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]

and for
x>0
, set

L(x)=\max\{1,\ln\ln x\}.

If
s_{n}^{2}\to\infty
a.s. and if for some sequence
(\alpha_{n})
of positive reals with
\alpha_{n}\to 0
,

(1.1)

|Y_{n}|\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}s_{n}}{\sqrt{L(s_{n}^{2})}}\quad\text{a.s.},

then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{X_{n}}{\sqrt{s_{n}^{2}L(s_{n}^{2})}}=\sqrt{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

In
[
Zen15
]
, the author extended stout’s result to non-commutative setup. In this setting, we assume that
\mathcal{M}
is a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful, normal (f.n. henceforth) tracial state
\tau
. Furthermore, let
(\mathcal{M}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of von Neumann subalgebras with the conditional expectations
(E_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
E_{k}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{k}
with the property
E_{n}\circ E_{m}=E_{\min\{m,n\}}
. Let
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-commutative martingales in
\mathcal{M}
and define
x_{0}=0
, and let
d_{k}:=x_{k}-x_{k-1}
be the associated martingale differences. Further, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
define,

s_{n}^{2}=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k-1}(d_{k}^{2})\right\|_{\infty},\quad u_{n}=\sqrt{L(s_{n}^{2})}.

Then, Zeng proved the following theorem. For other relevant definitions we refer to
Section

2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup{0}}
be a self-adjoint martingales in
\mathcal{M}
with
x_{0}=0
. Further suppose that
s_{n}^{2}\to\infty
and
\|d_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{n}s_{n}/u_{n}
for some sequence
(\alpha_{n})
of positive numbers with
\alpha_{n}\to 0
. Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{s_{n}u_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}2.

First, we observe that in this result the bound is
2
rather than

\sqrt{2}
, which seems inconsistent with the classical case. To
establish
1.1
, the author invokes an exponential
inequality from
[
JZ15
, Lemma 2.2]
. However,
its application to
[
Zen15
, Theorem 1]
is
slightly incorrect. To address this issue, we begin by
constructing an appropriate sequence in
3.2
. We
then refine the exponential inequality using results by
Randrianantoanina
[
Ran24
]
, and by employing
a standard technique we extend
1.1
to the
non-self-adjoint martingale case. This yields a version consistent with the classical setting. Our main result is stated
below.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a non-commutative martingale in
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
with
x_{0}=0
. Define

s_{n}^{2}=\max\left\{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(d_{i}^{*}d_{i})\right\|_{\infty},\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(d_{i}d_{i}^{*})\right\|_{\infty}\right\}\text{ and }u_{n}=L(s_{n}^{2})^{1/2}.

Suppose that
s_{n}^{2}\to\infty
and
\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}s_{n}}{u_{n}}
, where
(\alpha_{n})
is a sequence of positive numbers such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0
, then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{s_{n}u_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}.

As already pointed out in
[
Zen15
]
, one can not have equality for general non-commutative random variables because of the free CLT
[
Voi91
]
. In some sense, the commutative situation is the worst possible for the LIL.

Next, we use this martingale analogue of LIL to deduce a non-commutative analogue of independent random variable version due to Hartman and Wintner
[
HW41
]
. They stated that if
(y_{i})
is an i.i.d. sequence of real, centered, square-integrable random variables with variance
\sigma^{2}
, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{1}^{n}y_{i}}{\sqrt{nL(n)}}=\sqrt{2}\sigma\quad a.s.

A first non-commutative analogue of this result was established in
[
Kon08
, Theorem 3.23]
, where the author obtained a similar conclusion without really relying on martingales. We emphasize that the result there concerns bounds in the bilaterally almost uniform (b.a.u.) sense, whereas
[
Zen15
, Theorem 1]
provides bounds in the almost uniform (a.u.) sense. More recently,
[
HR24
]
exhibited examples of non-commutative probability spaces
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
and martingale sequences in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for
1\leq p<2
that fail to converge almost uniformly. This makes it natural to investigate a.u. bounds for the limits of such sequences. In this article, our approach (cf.
4.4
), in the spirit of
[
HW41
]
or
[
dA83
]
, employs martingale exponential inequalities, allowing us to derive a sharper bound in the almost uniform sense rather than in the bilaterally almost uniform one.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
2
reviews the necessary background on non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces, conditional expectations, and boundedness concepts used in our analysis. Section
3
presents the detailed proof of the main theorem. We conclude with an extension of our main result as in
[
Sto70b
]
. Next, in Section
4
, we establish the non-commutative LIL for independent random variables.

2.
preliminaries

Throughout this section, we assume that
\mathcal{M}(\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
is a finite von Neumann algebra with f.n. tracial state
\tau
, where
\mathcal{H}
is assumed to be a separable Hilbert space. A closed, densely defined operator
x:\mathcal{D}(x)\subseteq\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is called affiliated to
\mathcal{M}
if
xu\subset ux
for all unitaries in
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, the commutant of
\mathcal{M}
. An operator
x
, which is affiliated to
\mathcal{M}
is called
\tau
-measurable if for all
\epsilon>0
there exists a projection
e
in
\mathcal{M}
such that
\tau(1-e)<\epsilon
and
\left\|xe\right\|<\infty
. Since
\tau
is finite, it is observed that every closed, densely defined operator, which is affiliated to
\mathcal{M}
is
\tau
-measurable. The
*
-algebra of closed, densely defined operator, which are affiliated to
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. Throughout this article, the set of all projections will be denoted by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
K\geq 0
. We say
\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_{n}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}K
, that is,
(x_{n})_{n}
is almost uniformly (a.u.) bounded by
K
if for all
\epsilon,\delta>0
, there exists
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(1-p)<\epsilon
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}p\right\|\leq K+\delta.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. We say
(x_{n})_{n}
is almost uniformly (a.u.) convergent to
x
if for all
\epsilon>0
there exists
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(1-p)<\epsilon
and

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left\|(x_{n}-x)p\right\|=0.

Our next remark concludes that the definition of the almost uniform boundedness and the almost uniform convergence is independent of the ambient von Neumann algebra.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{N}
with
\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_{n}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}K
in
\mathcal{M}
for some constant
K>0
. Let
\epsilon,\delta>0
. We first choose
0<\eta<1
such that
(K+\frac{\delta}{2})/(1-\eta)\leq K+\delta
. Then, there exists
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\tau(1-p)<\eta\epsilon
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}p\right\|\leq K+\frac{\delta}{2}.

We claim that there exists
q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})
such that
\tau(1-q)<\epsilon
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}q\right\|\leq K+\delta.

Consider
p^{\prime}:=E(p)
, where
E
is the faithful, normal conditional expectation from
\mathcal{M}
onto
\mathcal{N}
. Now, consider
q:=1_{[1-\eta,1]}(p^{\prime})\in\mathcal{N}
. Next, observe that
1-p^{\prime}\geq\eta(1-q)
and

\eta\epsilon\geq\tau(1-p)=\tau(1-p^{\prime})\geq\eta\tau(1-q).

On the other hand, since
E(p)\geq(1-\eta)q
, we have

(1-\eta)\left\|x_{n}q\right\|^{2}=(1-\eta)\left\|x_{n}qx_{n}^{*}\right\|\leq\left\|x_{n}E(p)x_{n}^{*}\right\|=\left\|E(x_{n}px_{n}^{*})\right\|\leq\left\|x_{n}p\right\|^{2}.

Therefore, we obtain
\tau(1-q)\leq\epsilon
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}q\right\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\eta}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}p\right\|\leq K+\delta.

A similar argument also holds true for almost uniform convergence.

We state the following lemma, which will be used repeatedly in the subsequent results. However, the proof is straightforward and is left to the reader.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
be two sequences in a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
such that
\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}x_{n}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}K
and
y_{n}\xrightarrow{a.u}0
for some constant
K>0
, then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(x_{n}+y_{n}\right)\underset{a.u.}{\leq}K.

We will need a version of the Kronecker lemma for the a.u. convergence.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
,
(\alpha_{n})_{n}
an increasing sequence of positive real numbers, which diverges to
+\infty
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}}{\alpha_{i}}\text{ converges a.u. }\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\text{ converges a.u. to }0.

Proof.

Let
S_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}
and set
\beta_{k}=\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}
(
\alpha_{-1}=0)
. Then
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{k}=\alpha_{n}\to\infty
. We denote
y_{n}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{k}S_{k}}{\alpha_{n}}
.

Using an Abel transform, we have

\frac{1}{\alpha_{n+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}x_{k}=S_{n+1}+\frac{\alpha_{1}S_{1}}{\alpha_{n+1}}-y_{n}
. As
x_{1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
,
\frac{\alpha_{1}S_{1}}{\alpha_{n+1}}\to_{a.u.}0
, thus we only need to show that
\lim_{a.u.}y_{n}=\lim_{a.u.}S_{n}=S
.

Let
\epsilon>0
, then there is some projection
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
,
with
\tau(1-p)<\epsilon
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that

\lim_{n}\|(S-S_{n})p\|=0
. We may find
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for

n\geq n_{0}
,
\|(S-S_{n})p\|<\infty
. As

S,S_{1},...,S_{n_{0}}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
, there is another projection

q
with
\tau(1-q)<\epsilon
and
\|(S-S_{k})q\|<\infty
for
k\leq n_{0}
.
Set
p^{\prime}=p\wedge q
, so that
(S-S_{k})p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

\lim_{n}\|(S-S_{n})p^{\prime}\|=0
. By the Cesaro theorem in
\mathcal{M}
, we get

\lim_{n}\|(S-y_{n})p^{\prime}\|=0
and
\tau(1-p^{\prime})\leq 2\epsilon
. This gives the desired a.u. convergence.
∎

x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
and
t\in(0,1)
, then the generalised

\mu_{t}(x):=\inf\{s\geq 0:\tau(1_{(s,\infty)}(\left|x\right|))\leq t\}.

\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}

.

Definition 2.6
.

We say that
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are independent if

\tau(xy)=\tau(x)\tau(y)

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
y\in\mathcal{M}_{2}
. Moreover, a sequence of random variables
(x_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is said to be independent if for all

\mathcal{M}_{i}:=\{1,x_{1},...,x_{i}\}^{\prime\prime}

{x_{i+1}}^{\prime\prime}

Now let
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of von Neumann subalgebras of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{M}_{n}
is
w^{*}
-dense in
\mathcal{M}
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a normal conditional expectation
E_{n}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
, satisfying
E_{m}\circ E_{n}=E_{\min\{m,n\}}
for all

. A sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in

is called a martingale sequence with respect to

E_{n}(x_{n+1})=x_{n}
for all

. For a martingale sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

dx_{n}:=x_{n}-x_{n-1}
for all

(with
x_{-1}=0
). In this setting, without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathbb{C}

.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. The non-commutative
L_{p}
-space associated to
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is defined as

L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau):=\{x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}:\tau(\left|x\right|^{p})<\infty\}

with the Banach space norm
\left\|x\right\|_{p}:=\tau(\left|x\right|^{p})^{1/p}
.
The
\ell_{\infty}
-column valued non-commutative

-space is defined as

L_{p}(\ell_{\infty}^{c}):=\{(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau):\text{ there exist }a\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\text{ and }(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}\text{ such that }x_{n}=y_{n}a\}

with associated Banach space norm (when
p\geq 2
) defined by

\left\|(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\ell_{\infty}^{c})}:=\inf\{\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|y_{n}\right\|\left\|a\right\|_{p}\},

where the the infimum is taken over all such factorization as above.

The next statement is a non-commutative, asymmetric analogue of Doob’s maximal inequality, originally established by Junge in

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
4\leq p\leq\infty
. For every

-bounded martingale

x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
:

\left\|(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\right\|_{L_{p}(\ell_{\infty}^{c})}\leq 2^{2/p}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|x_{n}\|.

Let us recall the following Golden-Thompson inequalities and prove an exponential inequality which is crucial for our main result.

Lemma 2.8

(Golden–Thompson Inequality)
.

[
Rus72
]

Let
a
and
b
be self-adjoint operators that are bounded above, and assume
a+b
is essentially self-adjoint (that is, its closure is self-adjoint). Then the following inequality holds:

(2.1)

\tau(e^{a+b})\leq\tau(e^{a/2}e^{b}e^{a/2}).

Moreover, if either
\tau(e^{a})<\infty
or
\tau(e^{b})<\infty
, then:

(2.2)

\tau(e^{a+b})\leq\tau(e^{a}e^{b}).

Lemma 2.9

(Improved Golden–Thompson inequality)
.

[
Ran24
]

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a tracial state
\tau
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Assume that
a
,
b
, and
c
are self-adjoint
\tau
-measurable operators satisfying:
a\in\mathcal{M}
,
e^{b}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, and
e^{c}\in L_{q}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. The following inequality holds:

\tau(e^{a+b+c})\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau\left(e^{c/2}(e^{-a}+t1)^{-1}e^{b}(e^{-a}+t1)^{-1}e^{c/2}\right)dt.

The proof of the next result is very close to the
[
Ran24

This enhancement plays a central role in establishing our improved version of the law of the iterated logarithm, serving as a key technical component in the overall argument. In addition, the refined bound obtained here may be of independent interest for related problems in the non-commutative probability framework.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(x_{k})_{k=0}^{n}
be a finite self-adjoint martingale sequence with respect to the filtration
(\mathcal{M}_{k},E_{k})
, and let
d_{k}:=dx_{k}=x_{k}-x_{k-1}
represent the associated martingale differences. Let the following conditions hold:

(i)

x_{0}=0
,

(ii)

\|d_{k}\|\leq M
for all
1\leq k\leq n
,

(iii)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k-1}(d_{k}^{2})\leq D^{2}1
.

Then, the following hold:

(1)

For all
\lambda>0
,

\tau\left(e^{\lambda x_{n}}\right)\leq\exp\left[\frac{e^{\lambda M}-\lambda M-1}{M^{2}}D^{2}\right]

(2)

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1]
and
\lambda\in\left[0,\frac{3\epsilon}{M}\right]
,

\tau\left(e^{\lambda x_{n}}\right)\leq\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}(1+\epsilon)\lambda^{2}D^{2}\right].

Proof.

(1):
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let us first define the function

F(s):=e^{s}-s-1.

Note that
F(s)\leq F(\left|s\right|)
.
Now for
n\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
, let us define for
1\leq l\leq n

z_{l}:=\frac{F(\lambda M)}{M^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{l}E_{k-1}(d_{k}^{2}).

Then, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda>0
, by
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle\tau(e^{\lambda x_{n}})\leq\tau(e^{\lambda x_{n}-z_{n}}e^{z_{n}}).

Since for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq z_{n}\leq\frac{F(\lambda M)}{M^{2}}D^{2}\cdot 1
, we further obtain

\tau(e^{\lambda x_{n}})\leq\exp\left[\frac{F(\lambda M)}{M^{2}}D^{2}\right]\tau(e^{\lambda x_{n}-z_{n}}).

We claim that

(2.3)

\tau\left(e^{\lambda x_{l}-z_{l}}\right)\leq 1,\text{ for every }n\geq l\geq 1.

Now fix
l\geq 1
and write,

(2.4)

\lambda x_{l}-z_{l}=\alpha_{l}+\beta_{l}+\eta_{l},

where,

\alpha_{l}:=-\frac{F(\lambda M)}{M^{2}}E_{l-1}\left(d_{l}^{2}\right),~\beta_{l}:=\lambda d_{l},~\text{ and }\eta_{l}:=\lambda x_{l-1}-z_{l-1}.

Notice that,
\alpha_{l},\beta_{l}
and
\eta_{l}
are all bounded self-adjoint operators and
z_{0}=0
. Using
2.9
, we have:

\displaystyle\tau\left(\exp\left(\lambda x_{l}-z_{l}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\left(e^{\alpha_{l}+\beta_{l}+\eta_{l}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau\left(e^{\eta_{l}}(e^{-\alpha_{l}}+t\mathbf{1})^{-1}e^{\beta_{l}}(e^{-\alpha_{l}}+t\mathbf{1})^{-1}\right)dt.

Given that both
\alpha_{l}
and
\eta_{l}
lie in
\mathcal{M}_{l-1}
, the trace-preserving property of the conditional expectation
E_{l-1}
allows us to deduce that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\tau\left(\exp\left(\lambda x_{l}-z_{l}\right)\right)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau\left(e^{\eta_{l}}(e^{-\alpha_{l}}+t\mathbf{1})^{-1}E_{l-1}(e^{\beta_{l}})(e^{-\alpha_{l}}+t\mathbf{1})^{-1}\right)dt.

Consider a real valued function
g
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by:

g(s):=\frac{F(s)}{s^{2}}.

Now recall that, for
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+},
we have
s\leq e^{s-1}.
Using functional calculus, it yields that:

(2.6)

\displaystyle E_{l-1}(e^{\beta_{l}})=E_{l-1}(e^{\lambda d_{l}})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(E_{l-1}\left(e^{\lambda d_{l}}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(E_{l-1}\left(e^{\lambda d_{l}}-\lambda d_{l}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(E_{l-1}\left(F\left(\left|\lambda d_{l}\right|\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(E_{l-1}\left(\lambda^{2}d_{l}^{2}g(\lambda\left|d_{l}\right|)\right)\right).

Since the function
g(s)
is strictly increasing on the interval
(0,\infty)
, we must have
g(\lambda\left|d_{l}\right|)\leq g(\lambda M)
. Therefore,

(2.7)

E_{l-1}\left(\lambda^{2}d_{l}^{2}g\left(\lambda\left|d_{l}\right|\right)\right)\leq\frac{F(\lambda M)}{M^{2}}E_{l-1}\left(d_{l}^{2}\right).

Now combining
eq.

2.6
and
eq.

2.7
, we have:

(2.8)

E_{l-1}\left(e^{\beta_{l}}\right)\leq\exp\left(\frac{F(\lambda M)}{M^{2}}E_{l-1}\left(d_{l}^{2}\right)\right)=\exp\left(-\alpha_{l}\right).

By applying both
eq.

2.5
and
eq.

2.8
, we can deduce the following estimate:

\tau\left(\exp(\lambda x_{l}-z_{l})\right)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau\left(e^{\eta_{l}}(e^{-\alpha_{l}}+t\mathbf{1})^{-1}e^{-\alpha_{l}}(e^{-\alpha_{l}}+t\mathbf{1})^{-1}\right)dt.

Now since for all
s>0

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s}{(s+t)^{2}}\,dt=1,

using functional calculus, we obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\alpha_{l}}+t\mathbf{1})^{-1}e^{-\alpha_{l}}(e^{-\alpha_{l}}+t\mathbf{1})^{-1}dt=1.

Therefore, for all integers
l

n\geq l\geq 1

\tau\left(\exp(\lambda x_{l}-z_{l})\right)\leq\tau(e^{\eta_{l}})=\tau\left(\exp(\lambda x_{l-1}-z_{l-1})\right).

Hence, by induction, we get

\tau\left(\exp(\lambda x_{n}-z_{n})\right)\leq\tau\left(\exp(\lambda x_{0}-z_{0})\right)=1.

This proves the claim.

(2):
Observe that, for every

An essential component of the arguments involves a standard extension of the classical Borel–Cantelli lemma. To describe this, which introduces the column-wise tail probability for a sequence of random variables

3.
LIL for Martingales

This section is devoted to the proof of a non-commutative martingale analogue of the law of iterated logarithm. We begin with the precise description.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of martingales with respect to a filtration
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Furthermore, recall that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
dx_{n}:=x_{n}-x_{n-1}
denote the martingale differences. Then we will first prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0=x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots
be a sequence of self-adjoint martingales in the von Neumann algebra
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Let
s_{n}^{2}=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{k-1}(d_{k}^{2})\right\|
and
u_{n}=L(s_{n}^{2})^{1/2}
. Suppose that
s_{n}^{2}\to\infty
and
\left\|d_{n}\right\|\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}s_{n}}{u_{n}}
, where
(\alpha_{n})
is a sequence of positive numbers such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0
, then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{s_{n}u_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}.

In
[
Zen15
]
, the author proved this theorem with the bound of the limit
supremum equal to
2
. To establish this result, the martingale inequality

[
Zen15
, Lemma 3]
was employed. However, the application of the aforesaid
lemma in the proof of
[
Zen15
, Theorem 1]
is not totally correct, since for the chosen
real sequence
(\alpha_{n})
, the constant

M=\alpha_{k_{n+1}}\frac{s_{k_{n+1}}}{u_{k_{n+1}}}

does not necessarily bound
\|d_{k}\|
for all
1\leq k\leq n
. Here, we rectify this inaccuracy by constructing another real sequence
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime})
,
associated with the given sequence
(\alpha_{n})
in the following proposition, which satisfies
the aforementioned property. In the process, we also improve
[
Zen15
, Lemma 3]

(cf.
2.10
), and by using this improved version, we strengthen the bound to

\sqrt{2}
, which is consistent with the classical result of
[
Sto70a
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(\alpha_{n})
and
(\beta_{n})
be sequences of positive real numbers such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0
and
\beta_{n}\to\infty
, with
(\beta_{n})
being monotonically increasing. Then there exists a sequence
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime})
of positive real numbers satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\geq\alpha_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(2)

\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\downarrow 0
, that is,
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime})
is decreasing and converges to zero,

(3)

the sequence
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\beta_{n})
is non-decreasing; that is,
\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\beta_{n}\leq\alpha_{n+1}^{\prime}\beta_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By replacing the original sequence
(\alpha_{n})
with the sequence
\tilde{\alpha}_{n}:=\sup_{j\geq n}\alpha_{j}
, we may assume without loss of generality that
(\alpha_{n})
is decreasing and tends to zero. Since
(\beta_{n})
is an increasing sequence diverging to infinity as
n\to\infty
, we can express it in the form

\beta_{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k},

for some sequence
(\gamma_{k})
of real numbers satisfying
\gamma_{k}\geq 1
for all
k
. Moreover, the fact that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=\infty

guarantees the existence of an increasing sequence
(n_{k})
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\prod_{i=n_{k}+1}^{n_{k+1}}\gamma_{i}=\infty
.

We define the sequence
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime})
inductively in the following way:

\alpha_{1}^{\prime}:=\alpha_{1};~\alpha_{n+1}^{\prime}=\max\left\{\alpha_{n+1},\frac{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{n+1}}\right\}~\text{ for }n\geq 2.

First observe that
\alpha_{n}\leq\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\alpha_{n+1}\leq\alpha_{n}\leq\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
and
\frac{\alpha_{n}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{n+1}}\leq\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
, we have
\alpha_{n+1}^{\prime}\leq\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
. Moreover, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

\alpha_{n+1}^{\prime}\gamma_{n+1}=\max\left\{\alpha_{n+1}\gamma_{n+1},\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\right\}\geq\alpha_{n}^{\prime}.

Hence,
(3)
holds true.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. Consider the numbers
\{\alpha^{\prime}_{n_{k}},\ldots,\alpha^{\prime}_{n_{k+1}}\}
. Suppose, there exists
n_{k}\leq j\leq n_{k+1}
such that
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}=\alpha_{j}
. In this case, since both
(\alpha_{n})
and
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime})
are decreasing sequences, we obtain
\alpha_{n_{k+1}}^{\prime}\leq\alpha_{j}\leq\alpha_{n_{k}}
. Otherwise, if for all
n_{k}\leq j\leq n_{k+1}
,
\alpha_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha_{j-1}^{\prime}}{\gamma_{j}}
, then

\alpha_{n_{k+1}}^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha_{n_{k}}^{\prime}}{\prod_{i=n_{k}+1}^{n_{k+1}}\gamma_{i}}\leq\frac{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}{\prod_{i=n_{k}+1}^{n_{k+1}}\gamma_{i}}.

Therefore, we proved that

\alpha_{n_{k+1}}^{\prime}\leq\max\left\{\alpha_{n_{k}},\frac{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}{\prod_{i=n_{k}+1}^{n_{k+1}}\gamma_{i}}\right\}~\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\alpha_{n_{k+1}}^{\prime}=0
, and since
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime})
is a decreasing sequence, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}^{\prime}=0
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a non-commutative martingale in
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
with
x_{0}=0
. Define

t_{n}^{2}=\max\left\{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(d_{i}^{*}d_{i})\right\|,\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(d_{i}d_{i}^{*})\right\|\right\}
and
v_{n}=[L(t_{n}^{2})]^{1/2}
. Suppose that
t_{n}^{2}\to\infty
and
\left\|d_{n}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}t_{n}}{v_{n}}
, where
(\alpha_{n})
is a sequence of positive numbers such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0
, then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{t_{n}v_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}.

Proof.

Consider the von Neumann algebras

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}:=\mathcal{M}\otimes M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{k}:=\mathcal{M}_{k}\otimes M_{2}(\mathbb{C})

for each integer
k\geq 1
. Let
(\tilde{x}_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of elements in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
defined by

\tilde{x}_{n}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{n}\\
x_{n}^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad n\geq 0,

where
x_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
.

The sequence
(\tilde{x}_{n})_{n}
forms a self-adjoint martingale with respect to the increasing filtration
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{n},\mathcal{E}_{n})
, where the conditional expectation

\mathcal{E}_{n}:\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{n+1}\to\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{n}

satisfies

\mathcal{E}_{n}(\tilde{x}_{n+1}):=\begin{pmatrix}0&E_{n}(x_{n+1})\\
E_{n}(x_{n+1}^{*})&0\end{pmatrix},\quad n\geq 0.

Here,
E_{n}
denotes the conditional expectation from
\mathcal{M}_{n+1}
onto
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
Let us define for
n\geq 1
,

\tilde{d}_{n}:=\tilde{x}_{n}-\tilde{x}_{n-1}.

Observe that each
\tilde{d}_{n}
is self-adjoint for all
n\geq 0
. Moreover,

\tilde{d}_{n}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{n}\\
d_{n}^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{n}\\
d_{n}^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{n}d_{n}^{*}&0\\
0&d_{n}^{*}d_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Define, for each
n\geq 1
,

\tilde{t}_{n}^{2}:=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\left(\tilde{d}_{i}^{2}\right)\right\|.

It follows that
\tilde{t}_{n}=t_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Furthermore, for every
n\geq 1

\|\tilde{d}_{n}\|_{\infty}=\|d_{n}^{*}d_{n}\|^{1/2}=\|d_{n}\|.

Consequently,
(\tilde{x}_{n})_{n}
constitutes a sequence of self-adjoint martingales in the von Neumann algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tilde{t}_{n}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\|\tilde{d}_{n}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}\tilde{t}_{n}}{v_{n}},\quad\text{for all }n\geq 1.

Applying Theorem
3.1
, we deduce that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tilde{x}_{n}}{t_{n}v_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}.

1&0\end{pmatrix}

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\tilde{r}\tilde{x}_{n}}{t_{n}v_{n}}\underset{{a.u.}}{\leq}\sqrt{2}.

Since almost uniform boundedness does not depend on the ambient von Neumann algebra (cf. Remark

by

]
. We have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\frac{z_{n}}{s_{n}u_{n}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s_{n}^{2}u_{n}^{2}}\left\|{d_{n}^{\prime\prime}}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq C<\infty.

Next, to conclude, we claim that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}^{\prime}}{s_{n}u_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}.

Then this, together with Lemma
2.4
, proves the result.

We check that the martingale
(Z_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}z^{\prime}_{k})_{n}

satisfies the hypotheses of
3.1
.

First, let
s_{n}^{\prime 2}:=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(z_{i}^{\prime 2})\right\|
.
By
[
Jun02
]
, the quantity
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(h_{i}^{2})\right\|^{1/2}
is a norm on finite self-adjoint martingale differences
(h_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
. Thus,

|s_{n}-s^{\prime}_{n}|^{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(z_{i}^{2})\right\|\leq\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(d_{i}^{\prime\prime 2})\right\|.

But since
s_{i}/u_{i}^{\alpha/2}<As_{n}/u_{n}^{\alpha/2}
for
i<n
and some
A>1
, our assumption gives

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i-1}(d_{i}^{2}1_{(K_{i}s_{i}/u_{i},\infty)}(\left|d_{i}\right|))\right\|\leq CA^{2}s_{n}^{2}/u_{n}^{\alpha}.

Therefore, we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{s_{n}}{s_{n}^{\prime}}=1\qquad\textrm{ and also}\qquad\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{u_{n}}{u_{n}^{\prime}}=1.

Clearly, by definition, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left\|z_{n}^{\prime}\right\|\leq 2K_{n}\frac{s_{n}}{u_{n}}=2K_{n}\frac{s_{n}u_{n}^{\prime}}{s_{n}^{\prime}u_{n}}\cdot\frac{s_{n}^{\prime}}{u^{\prime}_{n}}=K_{n}^{\prime}\frac{s_{n}^{\prime}}{u_{n}^{\prime}}
with
K_{n}^{\prime}\to 0
. Obviously
z^{\prime}_{0}=0
and we conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}^{\prime}}{s_{n}^{\prime}u_{n}^{\prime}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}.

The proof of the claim is over as
\lim\frac{u_{n}s_{n}}{u^{\prime}_{n}s^{\prime}_{n}}=1
.
∎

4.
LIL for independent r.v.

This section is devoted to the study of the non-commutative LIL for independent random variables. The main result of this section (
4.4
) generalises the Hartman-Wintner law of iterated logarithm
[
HW41
]
to the non-commutative setting.

Throughout this section, we fix a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
with f.n. tracial state
\tau
. We say that a random variable
y\in\mathcal{M}
to have mean zero if
\tau(y)=0
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we also set the notation
u_{n}=L(n)^{1/2}
. Now we have the following crucial result.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\delta^{\prime}>0
, we can find some
e>0
such that any sequence
(y_{i})
of independent mean zero self-adjoint random variables such that

(1)

\left\|y_{i}\right\|\leq e\frac{\sqrt{i}}{u_{i}}
,

(2)

\left\|y_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq 1
,

we have, with
x_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{k}
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{n}u_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}(1+\delta^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}=\{1,y_{1},...,y_{n}\}^{\prime\prime}
. The sequence
(x_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{k})_{n\geq 0}
, with
x_{0}=0

is a self-adjoint martingale in the von Neumann algebra
(\mathcal{M},\tau)

with respect to the filtration
(\mathcal{M}_{n})
. Its difference sequence is given by
d_{n}:=dx_{n}=y_{n}
. Then, the proof is almost the same as
3.1
once
\delta^{\prime}
is fixed. First, one has
s_{n}^{2}\leq n
by independence and (
2
). Next, we used that the sequence
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime})
goes to 0 twice:

•

right before (
3.3
) to check the assumptions of

2.10
; this now is true by our assumption (
1
):
that is we take
M=\frac{e}{u^{2}_{k_{n+1}}}
because

l\mapsto\frac{\sqrt{l}}{u_{l}}
is increasing in
l
.

•

to have the first inequality in (
3.5
). Now, this is how we choose
e
, that is so that
0<e<\frac{3\epsilon(1+\epsilon)}{\sqrt{2}(1+\delta)}
.

The rest of the arguments is unchanged.
∎

For
z\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
, we denote
\mathring{z}=z-\tau(z)
. We will need the following estimate:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
e>0
and
(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a family of independent, identically distributed, mean zero,
self-adjoint random variables in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
, then with
z_{k}=y_{k}1_{\sqrt{k}\geq|y_{k}|>e\frac{\sqrt{k}}{u_{k}}}
,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}{u_{n}}}\mathring{z}_{n}
converges in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

Let
y(t)=\mu_{t}(y_{1})
defined on
(0,1)
.
By orthogonality, we have to show that
\sum_{n}\frac{1}{nu_{n}^{2}}\|\mathring{z}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}<\infty
. But by the hypotheses

\sum_{n}\frac{1}{nu_{n}^{2}}\|\mathring{z}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{n}\frac{1}{nu_{n}^{2}}\|z_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{n}\frac{1}{nu_{n}^{2}}\int_{\sqrt{n}\geq y>e\frac{\sqrt{n}}{u_{n}}}y^{2}=\int G(y)y^{2},

where for
t>0
,

G(t)=\sum_{\sqrt{n}\geq t>e\frac{\sqrt{n}}{u_{n}}}\frac{1}{nu_{n}^{2}}
. For every
t>0
, let
n_{t}
be the biggest integer so
that
u_{n_{t}}^{2}t^{2}\geq e^{2}n_{t}
. Note that one can easily find

a>1
and
b>0
(depending en
e
) so that
a\ln(\lceil t^{2}\rceil)+b\geq\ln(n_{t})
when

t>0
. It follows that
u_{n_{t}}=L(n_{t})^{1/2}\leq cu_{\lceil t^{2}\rceil}

for some
c>1
. We have the estimate

G(t)\leq\sum_{n=\lceil t^{2}\rceil}^{n_{t}}\frac{1}{nu_{n}^{2}}\leq\frac{n_{t}}{\lceil t^{2}\rceil u_{\lceil t^{2}\rceil}^{2}}\leq\frac{u_{n_{t}}^{2}\lceil t^{2}\rceil}{e^{2}\lceil t^{2}\rceil u_{\lceil t^{2}\rceil}^{2}}\leq\frac{c^{2}}{e^{2}}.

This means that
G
is bounded and this concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
e>0
and
(y_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be a family of independent, identically distributed mean zero,
self-adjoint random variables in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
, then with
w_{k}=y_{k}1_{|y_{k}|>\sqrt{k}}
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathring{w}_{n}
goes to 0 a.u..

Proof.

Let
p_{k}=1_{|y_{k}|>\sqrt{k}}
and
q_{k}=1_{y>\sqrt{k}}
with
y=\mu(y_{1})
, we have

\tau(p_{k})=\int q_{k}
. But
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int q_{k}\leq\int y^{2}<\infty
. Hence for any
\epsilon>0
, we may find
k_{0}
so that

p^{\prime}=\wedge_{k\geq k_{0}}(1-p_{k})
satisfies
\tau(1-p^{\prime})\leq\epsilon/2
. Taking
p=p^{\prime}\wedge q
where
q
is such that
\tau(1-q)<\epsilon/2
and
w_{k}q\in\mathcal{M}
for
k<k_{0}
. We have
\tau(1-p)\leq\epsilon
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}p=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}w_{k}p
, this goes to 0 in
\mathcal{M}
.

As
\mathring{w_{k}}=w_{k}-\tau(w_{k})
. It remains to check that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tau(w_{k})
goes to 0. By the Kronecker lemma, it suffices to prove that
\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\tau(|w_{n}|)<\infty
. Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor{y^{2}}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\leq{2}|y|
, one gets
\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\tau(|w_{n}|)<{2}\int|y|^{2}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(y_{i})
be a sequence of independent, identically distributed, mean zero
random variables with
\|y_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1
, then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{n}u_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}.

Proof.

By the trick of Theorem
3.3
, we can assume that
y_{k}
’s are self-adjoint. We want to show that for any
\delta^{\prime}>0
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}}{\sqrt{n}u_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}(1+\delta^{\prime})
.

Let
e\in(0,1)
be given by Proposition
4.1
. We decompose the martingale into three parts

(4.1)

y_{k}=\mathring{\overbrace{y_{k}1_{[0,\frac{e\sqrt{k}}{2u_{k}}]}(|y_{k}|)}}+\mathring{\overbrace{y_{k}1_{(\frac{e\sqrt{k}}{2u_{k}},\sqrt{k}]}(|y_{k}|)}}+\mathring{\overbrace{y_{k}1_{(\sqrt{k},\infty)}(|y_{k}|)}}=\mathring{y_{k}^{\prime}}+\mathring{z_{k}}+\mathring{w_{k}}.

The sequence
(\mathring{y_{k}^{\prime}})
satisfies all the hypotheses in Proposition

4.1
, so we get, with
x^{\prime}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathring{y_{k}^{\prime}}
:

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{x_{n}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n}u_{n}}\underset{a.u.}{\leq}\sqrt{2}(1+\delta^{\prime}).

Next, by Lemma
4.2
, we have that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}{u_{n}}}\mathring{z}_{n}\in L_{2}
. Thus, the associated martingale sequence

(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}{u_{k}}}\mathring{z}_{k})_{n}
converges a.u. by
[
Cuc71
]
. Then by Lemma
2.5
, we conclude that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}{u_{n}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathring{z}_{k}
converges a.u. to 0. Finally, Lemma
4.3
yields that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}{u_{n}}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathring{w}_{k}
converges a.u. to 0. One concludes with Lemma

2.4
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The martingale with differences
\mathring{z_{k}}+\mathring{w_{k}}
is easily seen to be in
L_{1}
(see Lemma 2.3 in
[
dA83
]
). This is a way to get a quick proof for the b.a.u. convergence or the commutative result. It seems simpler than in
[
dA83
]
as we do not use any maximal inequality that seems unavailable here.

It is also possible to relax the identically distributed assumption to a weaker domination property in distribution that is commonly used (see
[
HW41
]
and
[
Kon08
]
). We mean that
y=\sup_{i}\mu_{t}(y_{i})\in L_{2}(0,1)
. We briefly explain how. First, as a.u. convergence does not depend on the algebra, we can enlarge all the algebras so that
y_{k}
sits in a diffuse algebra that is independent from the previous ones. Then, one has to change the decomposition (
4.1
), taking
y^{\prime}_{k}=y_{k}a_{k}
,
z_{k}=y_{k}b_{k}
and
w_{k}=y_{k}{c_{k}}
in such a way that
a_{k},b_{k},c_{k}
are orthogonal projections summing up to 1 so that
\mu(y_{k}^{\prime})\leq\mu\big(y1_{[0,\frac{e\sqrt{k}}{2u_{k}}]}(y)\big)
,
\mu(z_{k})\leq\mu\big(y1_{(\frac{e\sqrt{k}}{2u_{k}},\sqrt{k}]}(y)\big)
,
\mu(w_{k})\leq\mu\big(y1_{(\sqrt{k},\infty)}(y)\big)
. This is possible as
\mu(y_{k})\leq y=\mu(y)
and the algebras are diffuse.

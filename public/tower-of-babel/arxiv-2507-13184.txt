Hydrodynamical transports in generic AdS Gauss-Bonnet-scalar Gravity
Chenwei Tong, Rohit Mishra, Yanqi Wang, and Song He

Abstract
The experimentally observed temperature-dependent shear and bulk viscosities of the quark-gluon plasma (QGP), along with its apparent violation of the Kovtun-Son-Starinets (KSS) bound η / s = 1 / ( 4 ​ π ) \eta/s=1/(4\pi) , necessitate a holographic description that incorporates higher-derivative corrections. We propose a five-dimensional Einstein-Scalar-Maxwell-Gauss-Bonnet model in which a scalar-Gauss-Bonnet coupling H ⁡ ( ϕ ) H(\phi) encodes leading curvature corrections. Although no closed-form black hole solution is available, we employ an entropy-production analysis at the event horizon to derive exact analytic formulas for the shear viscosity η \eta and bulk viscosity ζ \zeta . These expressions exhibit apparent deviation from the KSS bound and nontrivial temperature dependence. We then perform an independent computation via the retarded Green function (Kubo) method, finding perfect agreement for η \eta and isolating a single constant in ζ \zeta that requires numerical determination. Our dual derivation underscores the pivotal role of higher-derivative terms in realistic QGP modeling and demonstrates the efficacy of nonanalytic holographic backgrounds in capturing the dynamics of strongly coupled fluids.

One can verify that the equation of motion for the scalar field decouples from the other perturbations:

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle Z_{\phi}^{\prime\prime}+\left(\ln\frac{c_{1}c_{2}^{3}}{c_{3}}\right)^{\prime}Z_{\phi}^{\prime}+\frac{c_{3}^{2}w^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}}Z_{\phi}-Z_{\phi}\left(\frac{4c_{2}c_{3}^{2}c_{1}^{\prime}V}{3c_{1}c_{2}^{\prime}}+\frac{4}{3}c_{3}^{2}V+\frac{2c_{2}^{2}c_{3}^{4}V^{2}}{9c_{2}^{\prime 2}}+\frac{2c_{2}c_{3}^{2}\phi^{\prime}V^{\prime}}{3c_{2}^{\prime}}+c_{3}^{2}V^{\prime\prime}\right)

(57)

\displaystyle+Z_{\phi}\left(-\frac{2c_{2}^{2}c_{3}^{2}VZ\left(A^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{9c_{1}^{2}\left(c_{2}^{\prime}\right){}^{2}}+\frac{\left(A^{\prime}\right)^{2}Z^{\prime\prime}}{2c_{1}^{2}}-\frac{c_{2}^{2}Z^{2}\left(A^{\prime}\right)^{4}}{18c_{1}^{4}\left(c_{2}^{\prime}\right){}^{2}}+\frac{c_{2}\left(A^{\prime}\right)^{2}Z^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}}{3c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{\prime}}\right.

(58)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\left(A^{\prime}\right)^{2}\left(Z^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}Z}-\frac{2c_{2}Z\left(A^{\prime}\right)^{2}c_{1}^{\prime}}{3c_{1}^{3}c_{2}^{\prime}}-\frac{2Z\left(A^{\prime}\right)^{2}}{3c_{1}^{2}}\right)+\alpha\cdot[\cdots],

(59)

where
\alpha
-dependent terms are omitted for brevity. The metric perturbations remain coupled through the following equations:

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle H_{00}+\frac{2Z_{\phi}Z^{\prime}}{Z}+\frac{H\left(3c_{2}^{\prime}Z+c_{2}\phi^{\prime}Z^{\prime}\right)}{c_{2}c_{3}Z},

(60)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle H^{\prime}+\frac{c_{3}c_{2}}{3c_{2}^{\prime}}Z_{\phi}\phi^{\prime}+\alpha\cdot[\cdots].

(61)

For convenience, we rewrite the perturbations as

\displaystyle h_{tt}(t,r)=e^{-iwt}h_{00,w}(r),\quad h_{11}(t,r)=e^{-iwt}h_{11,w}(r),\quad\psi(t,r)=e^{-iwt}p_{w}(r).

(62)

Then, the Lagrangian is complexified via the replacement

\displaystyle\delta A\delta B\longrightarrow\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta A^{*}\delta B+\delta A\delta B^{*}\right),

(63)

where
\delta A
or
\delta B
denotes
h_{00,w}
,
h_{11,w}
,
p_{w}
, or their derivatives.
The on-shell action then becomes

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{C}}=16\pi G_{N}\cdot\left(h_{00,w}^{*}\cdot\frac{\delta S^{(2)}}{\delta h_{00,w}^{*}}+h_{11,w}^{*}\cdot\frac{\delta S^{(2)}}{\delta h_{11,w}^{*}}+p_{w}^{*}\cdot\frac{\delta S^{(2)}}{\delta p_{w}^{*}}\right)+\partial_{r}J_{w},

(64)

where
J_{w}
is a boundary current. The perturbations are decomposed into real and imaginary parts as

Z_{\phi}=\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\right)^{-i\mathfrak{w}}\left(z_{0}+i\mathfrak{w}z_{1}\right),\quad H=H_{0}+i\mathfrak{w}H_{1},\quad H_{00}=H_{00,0}+i\mathfrak{w}H_{00,1},

(65)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{w}=\frac{\omega}{2\pi T}.

(66)

The retarded Green’s function is obtained from the imaginary part of the current

\operatorname{Im}G_{R}(w)=\frac{1}{8\pi G_{N}}\lim_{r\rightarrow r_{h}}\operatorname{Im}J_{w}.

(67)

Decomposing the current into
J_{w}=J_{0}+i\mathfrak{w}J_{1}
yields

J_{1}\equiv F+\alpha\cdot\delta F,

(68)

with

F=\frac{c_{2}^{2}\left(c_{2}c_{1}^{\prime}-c_{2}^{\prime}c_{1}\right)}{2c_{3}}\left(z_{0}\right)^{2}-\frac{c_{2}^{3}c_{1}}{2c_{3}}\left(z_{1}^{\prime}z_{0}-z_{0}^{\prime}z_{1}\right),

(69)

and

\delta F=0.

(70)

Using (
52
), (
67
), and (
70
), the bulk viscosity simplifies to

\zeta=-\frac{4}{9}\frac{1}{8\pi G}\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\operatorname{Im}J_{w}=\frac{4}{9}\frac{1}{8\pi G}\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\cdot\frac{\mathfrak{w}}{\omega}j_{1}=\frac{4}{9}\frac{1}{8\pi G}\frac{1}{2\pi T}(F+\alpha\delta F)=\frac{s_{B}}{4\pi}\cdot\frac{4}{9}z_{0}^{2}.

(71)

The expression for the bulk viscosity depends on a constant
z_{0}
, which satisfies (
57
). Since this equation does not admit an analytic solution,
z_{0}
must be determined numerically. Future work will involve solving this equation to compare the result with the analytic expression derived in Section
6
.

5
Entropy production and viscous flow

It is well-established from
weinberg1972gravitation
;
batchelor1989fluid
;
bemfica2022first
in the context of relativistic hydrodynamics that the entropy production due to viscous flow in terms of the shear and scalar expansion is given by

T\Delta S=\int^{f}_{i}d\tau\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}\left(2\eta{\sigma^{(\tau)}}^{2}+\zeta\theta^{2}_{\tau}\right)+\Delta Q.

(72)

This relation can be applied to the membrane fluid on the event horizon of a black hole. As an example in this section, we take
S\equiv S_{BH}
as the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy in Einstein gravity and compute the shear and bulk viscosity using the variation of the entropy. We follow the approach given in
fairoos2018black
.

The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is given by

S=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{H}}dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h},

(73)

where
\partial\mathcal{H}
is the horizon cross section,
x^{a}
are the coordinates, and
h_{ab}
is the induced metric on
\partial\mathcal{H}
.
Taking the variation along the generator
k=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}
of the horizon
\mathcal{H}
, we get the first and second variations as

\displaystyle\frac{dS}{d\lambda}=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int dx^{D-2}\nabla_{k}{\sqrt{h}}=\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}\theta_{k},

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}S}{d\lambda^{2}}=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int dx^{D-2}\left(\nabla_{k}{\sqrt{h}}\theta_{k}+\sqrt{h}\frac{d\theta_{k}}{d\lambda}\right)=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}\left(\theta^{2}_{k}+\frac{d\theta_{k}}{d\lambda}\right).

(75)

It is useful to introduce the null Raychaudhuri equation as

\frac{d\theta_{k}}{d\lambda}=-R_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}-{\sigma^{k}}^{bd}\sigma^{k}_{bd}-\frac{\theta^{2}_{k}}{(D-2)}.

(76)

We consider the minimally coupled scalar field
\phi
, then the equation of motion will be

R_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}=\sum_{i}c_{i}(\nabla_{k}\phi)^{2}+8\pi G_{N}T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}.

(77)

Note that the cosmological constant term doesn’t contribute here because the generator is null
(g_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}=0)
, where
T_{\mu\nu}
is the energy-momentum stress tensor due to the matter contribution.

Substituting the null Raychaudhuri equation and the equation of motion for
\phi
into (
75
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}S}{d\lambda^{2}}=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}\left(\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}\theta^{2}_{k}-{\sigma^{k}}^{2}-\sum_{i}c_{i}(\nabla_{k}\phi)^{2}\right)-2\pi\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}.

Note that the first variation of the entropy is first order in
\theta_{k}
, so we have

\displaystyle\Delta S=\int^{f}_{i}\frac{dS}{d\lambda}d\lambda=-\int^{f}_{i}\frac{d^{2}S}{d\lambda^{2}}\lambda d\lambda,

(79)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int^{f}_{i}\lambda d\lambda\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}\left(\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}\theta^{2}_{k}-{\sigma^{k}}^{2}-\sum_{i}c_{i}(\nabla_{k}\phi_{i})^{2}\right),

(80)

\displaystyle-2\pi\int^{f}_{i}\lambda d\lambda\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}.

(81)

The boundary term vanishes because the initial bifurcation surface is set at
\lambda=0
, and the final stationary horizon is set at vanishing expansion
\theta_{k}
. Furthermore, we note that for the stationary solutions, we will consider the derivative of any scalar field along the null generator.
k
is given by

\nabla_{k}\phi_{i}=C~\phi_{i}^{\prime}(r)\theta_{k},

(82)

where
C
is a constant determined by the solution of the underlying theory. Further, we note that for a stationary spacetime, the event horizon is also a Killing horizon. The timelike Killing vector on the horizon cross section is proportional to the horizon generator
k=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}
, and is given as
\xi=\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}=\kappa\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}
.
\xi
is also geodesic on the horizon cross-section with the nonaffine parameter
\tau
, i.e.,
\xi^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\xi^{\nu}=\kappa\xi^{\nu}
. The evolution of the JM entropy along this nonaffine parameter matches the fluid entropy balance law. For ease of calculation, we take the variation with respect to the affinely parameterized null generator
k
and then recast the entropy relation with respect to the non-affinely parametrized Killing generator
\xi
. The non-affine parameter, expansion scalar and shear tensor
(\tau,\theta^{\tau},\sigma^{(\tau)}_{ab})
are related to the affine one by the following relation

\tau=\frac{\ln{\lambda}}{\kappa_{h}},~\theta_{k}=\frac{\theta_{t}}{\kappa_{h}\lambda},~\sigma^{(k)}_{ab}=\frac{\sigma^{(t)}_{ab}}{\kappa_{h}\lambda}.

(83)

Using this in the above expression (
79
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{h}}{2\pi}\Delta S=\int^{f}_{i}d\tau\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}\left(\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\left(-2\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}+2C^{2}\sum_{i}c_{i}\phi_{i}^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right)\theta^{2}_{\tau}+\frac{2}{16\pi G_{N}}{\sigma^{\tau}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\int^{f}_{i}d\tau\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}T(\xi,\xi).

Now, by comparing with the entropy balance law

\displaystyle T\Delta S=\int^{f}_{i}d\tau\int dx^{D-2}\sqrt{h}\left(2\eta{\sigma^{(\tau)}}^{2}+\zeta\theta^{2}_{\tau}\right)+\Delta Q,

we obtain the shear
(\eta)
, bulk viscosity
(\zeta)
and entropy density
(s)
as follows

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}},~~\zeta=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\left(-2\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}+2C^{2}\sum_{i}c_{i}\phi_{i}^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right),~~s=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}.

(84)

We can see that the correct value for
\frac{\eta}{s}=\frac{1}{4\pi}
is obtained. Note that Smarr’s formula for a non-rotating stationary black hole bears a resemblance to the first term in the bulk viscosity, namely

\frac{\kappa A}{8\pi G}=\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}M,

(85)

this term is the contribution coming from pure Einstein relativity. For
D=4
, it correctly reproduces the known result in Einstein gravity, namely
\eta=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}},~~\zeta=-\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}
.
We will then evaluate the bulk viscosity for two cases. First, we will consider the Sakai-Sugimoto model for which we have three scalar fields, and the stress-energy tensor is given by
benincasa2006hydrodynamics

T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{40}{3}(\nabla_{\mu}f)(\nabla_{\nu}f)+20(\nabla_{\mu}w)(\nabla_{\nu}w)+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\mu}\Phi)(\nabla_{\nu}\Phi)+\frac{1}{3}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{P}(f,w,\Phi).

(86)

The three constants in (
84
) are
c_{1}=\frac{40}{3},~c_{2}=20,~c_{3}=\frac{1}{2}
, and
C
can be calculated as
C=\frac{4}{25}r_{h}^{2}
(see Appendix
B
for details). Rewriting the expression for the bulk viscosity of the Sakai-Sugimoto model, we get

\displaystyle 16\pi G_{N}\zeta=16\pi G_{N}\zeta_{H}+16\pi G_{N}\zeta_{K}=-\frac{4}{3}+\frac{8}{25}r_{h}^{2}\left(\frac{40}{3}f^{\prime}(r_{h})^{2}+20w^{\prime}(r_{h})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime}(r_{h})^{2}\right).

Where
\zeta_{H}=-\frac{4}{3}\times\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}
. It is negative due to the future boundary conditions on the event horizon to ensure stability under perturbation
jacobson2017membrane
;
mishra2018physical
. From
benincasa2006hydrodynamics
, we know that the scalar fields are given by

w(r)=\frac{1}{10}\ln{r},~f(r)=\frac{13}{80}\ln{r},~\Phi(r)=\frac{3}{4}\ln{r}.

(87)

Using this we evaluate
\frac{\zeta_{K}}{\eta}
to get

\frac{\zeta_{K}}{\eta}=\frac{8}{25}r_{h}^{2}\left(\frac{169}{480}+\frac{1}{5}+\frac{9}{32}\right)=\frac{4}{15}.

(88)

One can verify that this matches the value obtained by using the Kubo formula
benincasa2006hydrodynamics
;
eling2011novel
. Another observation is that the value of
\zeta_{K}
will be zero if the matter field vanishes, but
\zeta_{H}
is non-zero, because it is a pure gravity contribution from Einstein gravity.

Our second example is the five-dimensional Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton (EMD) theory, the action for which is given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}_{N}}\int d^{5}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\mathcal{R}-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{\mu}\phi)(\nabla^{\nu}\phi)-V(\phi)-\frac{Z(\phi)}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right].

(89)

Since there is a single scalar
(\phi_{1}=\phi,c_{1}=\frac{1}{2})
, and from (
118
) we see that
C=\frac{r_{h}}{3}
. The value of
\eta
and
\zeta_{H}
remains the same as in Einstein gravity. However, the expression for
\zeta_{K}
is given by

\frac{\zeta_{K}}{\eta}=\frac{2r^{2}_{h}}{9}\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right)=\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{9}\phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}.

(90)

Here,
r_{h}
is the horizon radius. The discrepancy between the values obtained for the two viscosities was explained in detail in
gursoy2009thermal
and summarized in
eling2011novel
. The two also match for special cases as reported in
buchel2011eling
;
buchel2011holographic
.

Next, we will consider adding higher derivative corrections to the Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton theory. As we add higher derivative terms, the entropy expression (
73
) changes, whose variation will contain terms with curvature and its derivatives. Secondly, the equation of motion given in (
77
) will have extra terms due to higher derivative corrections. Lastly, the null Raychaudhuri equation will change due to the equation of motion. Now, one proceeds by expressing all the expressions in terms of shear and expansion along the null generator and the auxiliary null vector, respectively, under the approximation that we keep terms up to second order in
\theta_{k}
and
\sigma^{(k)}_{ab}
to extract the shear and bulk viscosity.

This method was used in
fairoos2018black
for Gauss-Bonnet gravity, and the result for shear viscosity exactly matched the one obtained in
brigante2008viscosity
using the Kubo formula. Now, adding the gauge field and the Gauss-Bonnet term to the theory gives the following action.

S=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{N}^{2}}\int d^{5}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\mathcal{R}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\mu}\phi-\frac{Z(\phi)}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-V(\phi)+\alpha\mathcal{R}_{GB}^{2}\right],

(91)

For this theory, the expression for shear and bulk viscosity with a minimally coupled scalar field for the metric ansatz in Appendix
C
is reproduced below

\eta=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\left(1-2\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{h})}{r_{h}}\right),~\zeta=-2\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}\eta+\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{9}\phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}.

(92)

Thus, we observe that the Einstein gravity contribution
\zeta_{H}
to the membrane bulk viscosity is the same, but the value of
\frac{\zeta_{K}}{\eta}
deviates from the Eling-Oz formula (
90
) as reported in
Buchel:2023fst
, namely

\frac{\zeta_{K}}{\eta}=\frac{r^{2}_{h}\phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}}{9\left(1-2\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{h})}{r_{h}}\right)}.

(93)

We see that although the form of expression for the bulk viscosity remains the same as in the case of Einstein gravity, introducing higher derivative corrections changes the shear viscosity and thereby changes the value of
\frac{\zeta_{K}}{\eta}
.

6
Coefficient of viscosity for Gauss-Bonnet scalar gravity

We now consider the Einstein-Scalar-Maxwell-Gauss-Bonnet theory with the Gauss-Bonnet term coupled to the scalar field
\phi
(
3
). As soon as we move away from Einstein gravity, the first change is to the entropy function itself. We adopt the Jacobson-Myers (JM) entropy
jacobson1993entropy
to overcome the ambiguities associated with the Wald entropy
jacobson1994black
;
jacobson1995black
. The following change is to the equations of motion, which include the higher derivative corrections.

The JM entropy functional for an initial stationary state is given by

S=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{H}}d^{(D-2)}x\sqrt{h}(1+2\alpha H(\phi)\bar{R}).

(94)

Here
\bar{R}
is the intrinsic Ricci scalar of the horizon cross-section. We are interested in finding the variation of (
94
) along the generator
(k=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda})
of the dynamical event horizon.

The first and second variations of the entropy functional along the null generator
k
read

\displaystyle\frac{dS}{d\lambda}=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int d^{(D-2)}x\sqrt{h}\tilde{K},

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}S}{d\lambda^{2}}=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int d^{(D-2)}x\sqrt{h}\left(\theta_{k}\tilde{K}+\nabla_{k}\tilde{K}\right),

(95)

where
\tilde{K}
is

\displaystyle\tilde{K}=

\displaystyle\theta_{k}\left(1+2\alpha H(\phi)\bar{R}-4\alpha\Delta^{(h)}H(\phi)\right)-2\alpha H(\phi){v^{(k)}}^{eb}\bar{R}_{eb}

(96)

\displaystyle+2\alpha(\nabla_{k}H(\phi))\bar{R}+2\alpha(D_{a}D_{c}H(\phi)){v^{(k)}}^{ac}.

(97)

Here we have defined
v^{(k)}_{ab}=\nabla_{k}h_{ab}
. The scalar expansion is given by
\theta_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\nabla_{k}\sqrt{h}
, and
D,\Delta^{(h)}H(\phi)
are the intrinsic covariant derivative and intrinsic Laplacian.

Further calculation shows

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\theta_{k}\tilde{K}+\nabla_{k}\tilde{K}\right)=4\alpha(D-4)\frac{\kappa_{h}\lambda e^{w(r_{h})/2}}{r_{h}}H(\phi_{h})\left({\sigma^{(k)}}^{2}-\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}\theta^{2}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle+4\alpha c\frac{\kappa_{h}e^{w(r_{h})/2}}{r_{h}}\left({\sigma^{(k)}}^{2}-3\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}\theta^{2}_{k}\right)\lambda+\lambda\left(\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}\theta^{2}_{k}-{\sigma^{(k)}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{2(D-2)^{2}}\phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}\theta^{2}_{k}+\frac{d}{d\lambda}(\lambda\theta_{n}A)+\frac{d}{d\lambda}(\lambda\theta_{n}B)-8\pi G_{N}\lambda T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}+\text{Total Divergence}.

See Appendix
E
for the details of the calculation.
Therefore, the change in the entropy calculated on a stationary horizon background is given below

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{h}}{2\pi}\Delta S=-\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int^{f}_{i}\kappa_{h}\lambda d\lambda\int d^{(D-2)}x\sqrt{h}\left(\theta_{k}\tilde{K}+\nabla_{k}\tilde{K}\right)\mid_{r=r_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int^{f}_{i}d\tau\int d^{(D-2)}x\sqrt{h}\Biggl(2\left(1-4\alpha\frac{\kappa_{h}e^{w(r_{h})/2}}{r_{h}}\left((D-4)H(\phi_{h})\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+r_{h}H^{\prime}(\phi_{h})\phi^{\prime}(r_{h})\right)\right){\sigma^{(t)}}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\frac{(D-3)}{(D-2)}\left(1-4\alpha\frac{\kappa_{h}\lambda e^{w(r_{h})/2}}{r_{h}}\left((D-4)H(\phi_{h})+3r_{h}H^{\prime}(\phi_{h})\phi^{\prime}(r_{h})\right)\right)\theta^{2}_{t}

\displaystyle+\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{2(D-2)^{2}}\phi^{\prime}(r)^{2}\theta^{2}_{t}\Biggr)

\displaystyle+\int^{f}_{i}d\tau\int d^{(D-2)}x\sqrt{h}~T^{\text{Gauge}}_{\mu\nu}\xi^{\mu}\xi^{\nu}.

(99)

Here we have used (
83
). Comparing this equation with the entropy balance law

T\Delta S=\int^{f}_{i}d\tau\left(2\eta\sigma^{2}+\zeta\theta^{2}\right)+\Delta Q,

(100)

and by using (
114
) for surface gravity
\kappa_{h}=\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime}(r)e^{-\frac{w(r)}{2}}
, we get the shear viscosity

\eta=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\left(1-2\alpha\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{h})}{r_{h}}\left(H(\phi_{h})+r_{h}H^{\prime}(\phi_{h})\phi^{\prime}(r_{h})\right)\right).

(101)

We can check that if we set the Gauss-Bonnet term to zero (
\alpha=0
), the KSS bound is restored (
\eta/s=1/4\pi
). This shear viscosity exactly matches the result obtained using the Kubo formula (
51
).
The expression for the bulk viscosity of the membrane fluid is given below

\zeta=-\frac{4}{3}\eta+\frac{\alpha}{3\pi G_{N}}f^{\prime}(r_{h})H^{\prime}(\phi_{h})\phi^{\prime}(r_{h})+\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{9}\phi^{\prime}(r_{h})^{2}.

(102)

We can see that the second term above represents the contribution from the Gauss-Bonnet term, and the third term is the contribution from the scalar field. If we set
\alpha=0
and
\phi=0
, this reduces to the result obtained in
Buchel:2023fst
.

7
Discussion and Outlook

Driven by the need to capture the quark–gluon plasma’s (QGP) experimentally observed, temperature-dependent viscosity and its violation of the Kovtun–Son–Starinets bound, we have constructed a five-dimensional Einstein–Scalar–Maxwell–Gauss–Bonnet holographic model with a scalar-coupled higher-derivative term
H(\phi)
. Despite the lack of an analytic black-brane solution, we derived exact, analytic expressions for the shear viscosity
\eta
and bulk viscosity
\zeta
via the entropy-production method at the event horizon (See equation (
101
) and (
102
)), revealing clear deviations from
\eta/s=1/(4\pi)
and nontrivial temperature scaling. We then cross-checked these results using the retarded Green’s function (Kubo) formalism (See equation (
51
) and (
71
)), finding perfect agreement for
\eta
and isolating a single undetermined constant in
\zeta
that awaits numerical resolution. This dual approach highlights the utility of higher-derivative corrections in modeling realistic QGP dynamics and underscores the importance of non-analytic backgrounds in holographic model building.

Looking forward, we identify several avenues to deepen and broaden this work:

1.

Data-Driven Model Calibration.

Integrate our theoretical framework with numerical methods and machine-learning techniques
Hashimoto:2018bnb
;
Cai:2024eqa
;
Aarts:2025gyp
to calibrate holographic transport coefficients against lattice QCD and heavy-ion collision data
Apostolidis:2025gnn
, thereby deriving the form of the metric components
c_{i}(r)
, the scalar coupling
H(\phi)
, and the gauge kinetic function
Z(\phi)
.

2.

Extended Entropy-Production Methods.

Generalize the horizon-based derivation to other first-order transport coefficients (e.g. electric and thermal conductivities
Iqbal:2008by
) and advance to second-order hydrodynamics
Wu:2023nlw
, extracting relaxation times and nonlinear fluid responses directly from higher-derivative gravity.

3.

Exploration of Alternative Higher-Curvature Terms.

Study the effects of other curvature invariants (e.g. cubic Lovelock
Edelstein:2014dje
,
f(R)
gravity
Sotiriou:2008rp
) on transport bounds, seeking novel mechanisms for bound violation independent of anisotropy or external fields.

4.

Anisotropy and External Fields.

Extend the isotropic setup to geometries with spatial anisotropy or background electromagnetic fields
Grozdanov:2016tdf
, probing their interplay with higher-derivative corrections and enriching the holographic description of realistic QGP environments.

By pursuing these directions, we aim to strengthen the connection between holographic theory and QGP phenomenology, advancing toward a predictive, data-anchored understanding of strongly coupled relativistic fluids.

Appendix B
Black brane solution in Sakai-Sugimoto model

We consider the 5-dimensional black brane solution in the Sakai-Sugimoto model

(110)

Here

(U,V)

\displaystyle U=-e^{-\kappa u},~~V=e^{\kappa v},~u=t-\int\frac{dr}{r^{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta(r)},~v=t+\int\frac{dr}{r^{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta(r)}.

This coordinate system is necessary for this analysis because in these coordinates,
V
acts as an affine parameter along the null generator. The metric becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=r^{\frac{5}{3}}\left(\frac{\Delta(r)}{\kappa^{2}UV}dUdV+dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}\right).

Here
r=r(U,V)
.
The reason for shifting to the Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates is that the null vector gets identified with the coordinate basis vectors, and the coordinate behaves as an affine parameter. The null generators are
k=\partial_{V}
and
n=-\frac{1}{g_{UV}}\partial_{U}
on this background.

The null expansion
\theta_{k}
on the event horizon corresponding to the auxiliary null vector is given by

\displaystyle\theta_{k}=\frac{5}{2r_{h}}\frac{\partial r}{\partial V}.

(111)

The rest of the null expansion and shear vanish on the stationary event horizon.

Further, for any scalar function, we have

\nabla_{k}\phi=\frac{2r_{h}\phi^{\prime}(r_{h})}{5}\theta_{k}.

(112)

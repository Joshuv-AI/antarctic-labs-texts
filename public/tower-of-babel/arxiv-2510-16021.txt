Feature-driven reinforcement learning for photovoltaic in continuous intraday trading
Arega Getaneh Abate, Xiufeng Liu, Ruyu Liu, and Xiaobing Zhang

Abstract
Photovoltaic (PV) operators face substantial uncertainty in generation and short-term electricity prices. Continuous intraday markets enable producers to adjust their positions in real time, potentially improving revenues and reducing imbalance costs. We propose a feature-driven reinforcement learning (RL) approach for PV intraday trading that integrates data-driven features into the state and learns bidding policies in a sequential decision framework. The problem is cast as a Markov Decision Process with a reward that balances trading profit and imbalance penalties , and is solved with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) using a predominantly linear, interpretable policy. Trained on historical market data and evaluated out-of-sample, the strategy consistently outperforms benchmark baselines across diverse scenarios. Extensive validation shows rapid convergence, real-time inference, and transparent decision rules. Learned weights highlight the central role of market microstructure and historical features. Taken together, these results indicate that feature-driven RL offers a practical, data-efficient, and operationally deployable pathway for active intraday participation by PV producers.

Short-term electricity markets are crucial for integrating variable renewable energy sources, such as solar photovoltaics (PV) and wind

Designing an effective intraday strategy for PV producers is nevertheless challenging.
First

Second
, intraday prices are volatile and exhibit strong within-session dynamics
[

.

[

The literature tackles some of the challenges from several angles. Stochastic programming captures uncertainty in generation and prices. The authors in

Empirical work using Danish and Nordic data demonstrates that renewable forecast errors drive systematic spreads between intraday and day-ahead prices

A third strand of research applies learning-based trading. Reinforcement learning (RL) policies, including Deep Q-networks and policy-gradient methods, have been explored for intraday trading

Despite this progress, methods centered on price prediction

This paper proposes an end-to-end framework for PV intraday trading that combines feature-driven forecasting and an RL algorithm that learns sequential bidding decisions in continuous intraday markets. The central premise of the paper is that augmenting RL with data-driven features about PV generation and market state yields better risk–return performance by directly managing imbalance penalties across intraday, and balancing stages. The main contributions of this work are:

i)

Formulating PV intraday trading as a multistage decision problem under uncertainty and developing a mathematical model that maximizes expected profit while explicitly penalizing imbalances.

ii)

Implementing a Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm with a predominantly linear policy structure that learns from historical data without requiring explicit probabilistic models of prices or generation.

iii)

Designing a real-time implementable strategy that iteratively updates intraday positions as new information arrives, respecting continuous-market constraints and gate-closure rules.

iv)

Validating learning dynamics, computational efficiency, and policy interpretability through systematic experiments: convergence analysis across multiple seeds, inference latency benchmarking on consumer hardware, and weight analysis revealing economic interpretability of learned trading rules.

v)

Evaluating with a case study using real data from the Danish electricity market (2023–2024), showing consistent profit gains across diverse scenarios, including liquidity constraints, imbalance price shocks, and feature ablations.

II-B

Feature-driven decision model

We now describe the feature-driven policy that generates the recommended intraday trading actions. Let
\mathbf{X}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the feature vector at time
t
, and let
\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the learned weight vector defining a linear policy. The policy’s
recommended
net intraday action is given by

a^{\mathrm{rec}}_{t}=q^{\mathrm{ask}}_{t}-q^{\mathrm{buy}}_{t}=\boldsymbol{q}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}.

(6)

This recommendation is incorporated into the objective via the quadratic penalty term with weight
\kappa
. The optimizer is incentivized to choose intraday trades such that the net executed volume is close to
\boldsymbol{q}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}
, but it retains the flexibility to deviate when necessary. In other words, the policy suggests an action based on features, and the MIQP can override or adjust it within the limits of the constraints and for economic gain.
In this work, we include five feature domains:

\mathbf{X}_{t}=\Big[(\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{temp}}_{t})^{\top},(\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{fcst}}_{t})^{\top},(\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t})^{\top},(\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{wx}}_{t})^{\top},(\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{t})^{\top},1\Big]^{\top}.

(7)

Temporal
features
\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{temp}}_{t}
encode time-of-delivery information;
forecast
features
\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{fcst}}_{t}
include the latest PV generation from hour
t-5
forecast
\hat{G}_{t}
and recent updates or errors in the forecast;
market
features
\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{mkt}}_{t}
include relevant price signals;
weather
features
\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{wx}}_{t}
capture meteorological signals such as solar irradiance, cloud cover, which influence PV output uncertainty; and
volatility
features
\mathbf{x}^{\mathrm{vol}}_{t}
quantify recent variability. We also include a constant 1 to allow an intercept term. All features are appropriately scaled for the learning algorithm.

The linear policy weight vector
\boldsymbol{q}
can be structured in different ways to capture heterogeneity across time or regimes.
In the
training phase
, the policy parameters
\boldsymbol{q}
are learned from historical data. This can be formulated as finding the
\boldsymbol{q}
that maximizes the total profit on the training dataset, using historical feature vectors
\mathbf{X}_{t}
and observed outcomes. In other words, we solve a learning problem to obtain
\boldsymbol{q}^{*}
that yields a high profit when the intraday optimization (
II-A
) is executed. In the
online phase
, we keep
\boldsymbol{q}
fixed to the learned values. At each decision time
t
, we compute the recommended action
a^{rec}_{t}=\boldsymbol{q}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}
from the latest features, and then solve the MIQP (
II-A
) to determine the actual intraday trade volumes
q^{ask}_{t}
and
q^{buy}_{t}
. This two-step procedure ensures that real-time trading decisions are both adaptive and feasible in terms of market and physical constraints.

For computational efficiency within the RL training loop, we substitute the closed-form execution surrogate for repeatedly solving the MIQP. The surrogate respects the same capacity and imbalance constraints and produces the quantities
q^{ask}_{t}
and
q^{buy}_{t}
that would be delivered to the market.

II-C

RL approach for policy training

We frame the intraday trading problem as a sequential decision-making task and employ RL to train the policy parameters
\boldsymbol{q}
. In this formulation, each delivery horizon can be viewed as an episode consisting of a sequence of states, actions, and rewards. The
state

s_{t}
at hour
t
is characterized by the feature vector
\mathbf{X}_{t}
. The
action

a_{t}
is the intraday trade decision, which in our policy class corresponds to the net volume
a_{t}=\boldsymbol{q}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}
. The immediate
reward

r_{t}
is the profit achieved for hour
t
as defined by the terms inside the summation of (
II-A
). The objective of the RL agent is to learn a policy
\pi_{\theta}
that maximizes the expected cumulative reward over the episode.

To learn
\boldsymbol{q}
, we adopt a policy-gradient RL algorithm, specifically a variant of
Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO), which iteratively improves the policy by simulating trading experiences and using the observed rewards as feedback. Conceptually, the training procedure works as follows: the agent simulates many episodes using historical data. In each episode, for each period
t
, the agent observes the current state
\mathbf{X}_{t}
, then samples an action
a_{t}
according to the current policy. The intraday trading optimization is executed with that action. The agent then observes the next state and repeats this process. After completing an episode, the policy parameters are updated to increase the probability of actions that yielded higher profits and decrease the probability of suboptimal actions. Over many iterations, this process converges to a policy that maximizes the expected profit.
Learning directly from data allows the agent to discover complex patterns or strategies that would be hard to derive from first principles.

The training procedure is given in Algorithm
1
. In implementation, we maintain a parameter vector
\boldsymbol{q}
which is updated by gradient ascent on the PPO objective. We also maintain a value function (critic) for variance reduction in estimating advantages. The
Executor
in the algorithm refers to solving the intraday optimization (
II-A
) given a tentative action
a_{t}
, returning the actual executed volumes and the resulting reward
r_{t}
. The training involves multiple epochs of simulated rollouts over the training dataset. After convergence, the learned
\boldsymbol{q}
is used for the online policy as described earlier.

III
Numerical experiments

III-A

Data and market context

We evaluate the proposed framework in the Danish electricity market, which features a high penetration of wind and solar generation, as well as well-defined intraday and balancing mechanisms. The case study simulates a PV owner/BRP that commits day-ahead volumes based on a day-ahead forecast, updates its position in the IDC market up to shortly before delivery as information improves, and settles any residual deviation in the balancing market.

We use hourly data from 2023 to 2024. The dataset includes day-ahead clearing prices
P^{\mathrm{DA}}_{t}
and committed volumes
G^{\mathrm{DA}}_{t}
, intraday continuous market prices
(P^{\mathrm{ID,bid}}_{t},P^{\mathrm{ID,ask}}_{t})
, imbalance prices
P^{\mathrm{IM}}_{t}
, system up/down regulation status, measured PV generation
G^{\mathrm{act}}_{t}
, and intraday PV production forecasts, including
t-5
hours lags. All time series are synchronized to hourly delivery periods for consistency, even though the IDC market supports sub-hourly trading. Day-ahead prices and PV-related time series are sourced from
[
24
]
. Continuous intraday trading records (Elbas) and depth snapshots (bid/ask volumes) are taken from
[
25
]
. Gas balancing prices are used as the basis for the imbalance penalty surrogate.
After processing all raw data through a single pipeline, the combined series yields 17,369 hourly observations spanning January 2023 through December 2024. Synthetic intraday bid/ask quotes and depth bounds are then derived from the day-ahead price spread and the observed countertrade volumes, providing the execution parameters used by the RL environment. The resulting panel is split chronologically: January-December 2023 is used for training, and January-December 2024 serves as the out-of-sample evaluation set.

Generation is represented by a 10 MW PV plant parameterized to Danish conditions (annual capacity factor
12.5\%
, typical for latitude
\sim 55.7^{\circ}
N). Forecast errors are calibrated to empirical magnitudes: day-ahead forecast standard deviation
\approx 15\%
of capacity and intraday updates
\approx 8\%
. Meteorological features are obtained from the Danish Meteorological Institute (DMI) at hourly resolution and spatially interpolated to the plant site
[
26
]
. We include global horizontal irradiance, satellite-derived cloud cover, and 2m air temperature, both directly and through engineered features that capture diurnal and seasonal structure, to inform forecasts and uncertainty.

For each delivery hour
t
, we construct a feature vector
\mathbf{X}_{t}
from information available up to a cutoff one hour before delivery. Specifically, by
t{-}1
h the BRP observes a final intraday forecast for
G^{\mathrm{act}}_{t}
, the current IDC quotes, and recent imbalance price dynamics.

To emulate continuous intraday trading, we simulate depth-aware execution against best quotes and a stylized order book. Market orders incur a spread-scaled linear cost plus a convex depth penalty. Limit orders placed at an offset
\delta_{t}
fill according to a calibrated probability model that depends on observed bid/ask volumes and recent order flow. Time steps are refined near gate closure to reflect increased trading intensity and more frequent forecast updates. This setup preserves tractability while capturing the essential maker-taker and liquidity trade-offs faced by PV traders in practice. Residual deviations at delivery are settled at
P^{\mathrm{IM}}_{t}
.

III-B

Model training and benchmarking

We train the feature-driven PPO agent on data from January 2023 to December 2023 and evaluate it out-of-sample on calendar year 2024. Training is conducted at the daily granularity: each episode consists of 24 intraday trading rounds, followed by a policy update. To reduce sensitivity to random initialization, we run five independent seeds (17, 29, 41, 53, 67); each seed executes six PPO epochs with 1,024 environment steps per epoch. The discount factor is set to
\gamma=0.98
, and the initial policy mean is zero so that exploration noise and gradient updates jointly shape the coefficients.

After training, the learned weights are frozen and deployed in 2024 without further adaptation. Every result below reports the mean across the five seeds together with the half-width of the 95% confidence interval (CI), thereby reflecting both seed-to-seed variability and stochasticity in the simulated execution model.

IV
Conclusion and future work

This paper presented a comprehensive study on strategic intraday trading for a PV power producer, aimed at maximizing profit while minimizing imbalance costs. We formulated the intraday trading problem as a sequential decision optimization and introduced a feature-driven reinforcement learning approach that leverages real-time data and domain features to make optimal trading decisions. The method enables a PV operator to continuously adjust its position in the intraday market as new information becomes available, thereby reducing its reliance on imperfect day-ahead forecasts.

Across various market conditions, the feature-driven policy consistently delivers a significant uplift over spot-only trading. CVaR remains tightly clustered, indicating that improvements are not obtained by loading additional tail risk.
Methodologically
, the controller is prescriptive and lightweight: it learns directly from realized intraday profit and imbalance penalties using familiar hourly forecasts and price feeds, without requiring calibrated probabilistic models. The predominantly linear policy remains interpretable; learned weights suggest market microstructure and weather signals dominate decisions.

Operationally
, the policy intervenes sparingly, yet residual imbalances decline versus the spot-only benchmark, consistent with targeted realignment rather than wholesale re-hedging. By learning adaptive policies on standard BRP data feeds, it narrows the gap between perfect-foresight benchmarks and real-world operations under uncertainty, supporting more efficient integration of variable renewables.
Limitations include a stylized execution layer calibrated to depth snapshots and spreads (abstracting strategic order-book interactions and venue heterogeneity), a one-year out-of-sample evaluation (2024) after training on 2023 data, hourly proxies for imbalance settlement, and a focus on a single 10 MW PV asset with exogenous day-ahead commitments. Performance under different weather regimes, regulatory changes, or structural shifts warrants further study.

Future work could include (
i
) portfolio-level control and aggregation across assets with explicit risk objectives (e.g., CVaR) in training, (
ii
) richer execution models and market-design analyses (gate-closure rules, liquidity incentives, imbalance pricing), (
iii
) multi-agent settings to capture strategic BRP interactions, and coupling with physical models such as hybrid PV–storage and degradation-aware dispatch to assess system-level benefits.

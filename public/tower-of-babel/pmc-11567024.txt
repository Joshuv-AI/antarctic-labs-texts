Protocol for manufacturing photosynthetic cyanobacterial cell-based living building materials
Authors: Yongjun Son, Jihyeon Min, Jiyoon Park, Chongku Yi, Woojun Park
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryCement-free and photosynthetic cyanobacterial cell-based living building materials (LBMs) can be manufactured using microbially induced calcium carbonate precipitation (MICP) technology. Here, we present a protocol for cultivating Leptolyngbya boryana GGD and manufacturing LBMs using a sand-gelatin solution. We describe steps for fabricating acrylic molds and mixing abiotic substances with cyanobacterial cells for constructing LBMs. We then detail the procedures for analyzing the compressive strengths of our LBMs with or without the cyanobacterial cells using a universal testing machine (UTM).For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Son et al.1Subject areas: Cell culture, Microbiology, Biotechnology and bioengineering, Material sciences

Before you begin

Cement-free and regenerative living building materials (LBMs) can be engineered by incorporating calcium carbonate precipitation (CCP)-capable cyanobacteria into an inert sand-gelatin scaffold.2,3 During photosynthetic growth, these cyanobacterial cells precipitate CaCO₃ in the presence of calcium ions. This biogenic CaCO₃ fills the voids within the porous sand-gelatin matrix, thereby enhancing the mechanical properties of the quasi-brittle LBMs. To apply this technology, we isolated the photosynthetic and axenic filamentous cyanobacterium Leptolyngbya boryana GGD, as previously described.1 Our isolated GGD strain precipitates CaCO₃ in BG11 medium supplemented with CaCl2, as confirmed by field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX), and X-ray diffractometry (XRD) analyses.4,5 We optimized the culture conditions for the GGD strain using genomic analyses and phenotypic assays, including growth, nitrogen availability, and biofilm formation, to maximize its CCP capability.6,7 This protocol outlines the preparation of L. boryana GGD cultures and sand-gelatin mixtures for LBM fabrication. Additionally, we detail the procedure for measuring the compressive strengths of LBMs in accordance with the American Society for Testing and Materials (ASTM) C346 standard method.

Expected outcomes

Compressive strengths of the cubic and cuboid LBMs without cyanobacterial cells were measured in 0.7 MPa ± 0.07 and 1.7 MPa ± 0.07, respectively (Figures 6A and 6C). Compressive strengths of the cubic and cuboid LBMs containing cyanobacterial cells were measured in 1.5 MPa ± 0.07 and 2.8 MPa ± 0.07, respectively (Figures 6B and 6D). To achieve the expected compressive stresses of LBMs harboring the cyanobacterial cells, 10% gelatin and 60 ± 2.3 mg CaCO3 are required at curing 14 days. Although cyanobacterial cell-based LBMs have lower compressive stress compared to cement-based materials, they can serve as alternatives to cementitious materials-based reusable photosynthetic exterior surface structures (e.g., exterior wall sheathing, gazebo roof panels, and patio walls).

Limitations

For manufacturing regenerative LBMs using old materials from the parent LBMs

Our protocol is suitable for manufacturing engineered LBM with compressive strengths, not for regenerative LBMs using old materials from parent LBMs.1 To utilize the old materials from the parent LBMs, we break them down into particles using a rubber mallet. However, the gelatin within the recycled sand fragments did not fully melt when heated to 40°C during the preparation of the mixture process (step 2a). This resulted in an uneven distribution of gelatin within manufacturing regenerated LBMs, leading to a decrease in their mechanical properties. Repeated experiments are important to overcome this limitation in our mixture process (step 2) using recycled materials from the parent LBMs, ensuring uniform gelatin distribution within the regenerated LBMs.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Low yield of cyanobacterial cells after 7 days.

Potential solution

Use inoculum with high cell density in fresh BG11 medium.

Problem 2

Is it mandatory to use standard ISO sand?

Potential solution

Standard ISO sand has a uniform particle size distribution and consistent composition, which enhances the reproducibility and reliability of experimental results. If non-standard sand is used for manufacturing LBMs, it is crucial to collect uniform sand particles using standard sieves to ensure reliable compressive strengths of LBMs.

Problem 3

The acrylic mold breaks when the mixtures for manufacturing LBMs are placed into the assembled mold.

Potential solution

Temporarily bind the side of the acrylic mold using tape.

Problem 4

When removing acrylic mold, some parts of the LBMs break away.

Potential solution

Place initially curing LBMs under a low-temperature incubator for 30 min.

Problem 5

When removing the acrylic mold, some parts of the LBMs adhere to it and break off.

Potential solution

Attach Teflon tape to the inside of the acrylic mold to prevent the LBMs from sticking and breaking off.

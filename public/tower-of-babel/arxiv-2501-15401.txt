Splitting Property of quasitriangular Hopf algebras Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Jinsong Wu, Kun Zhou*

Abstract
. We investigate the splitting property of quasitriangular Hopf algebras through the lens of twisted tensor products. Specifically, we demonstrate that an infinite-dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebra possesses the splitting property if it admits a factorizable quotient Hopf algebra. We also establish that the splitting property holds if there exists a full rank quotient Hopf algebra where the left and right coinvariants coincide. As a consequence, we obtain a new obstruction for quasitriangular structure in finite-dimensional Hopf algebras.

1.
Introduction

Quasitriangular Hopf algebras, introduced by Drinfeld
[
51
]
, provide solutions to the quantum Yang-Baxter equations, a cornerstone in the study of quantum integrable systems.
The small quantum group
u_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, with appropriate parameter
q
, is an example of a quasitriangular Hopf algebra
[
7
,
8
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
42
]
.
Representations of finite-dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebras give rise to braided finite tensor categories, which serve as a rich and significant source of such structures. Braided finite tensor categories play a central role in various fields of mathematics and theoretical physics due to their profound connections with quantum groups, knot theory, and topological quantum field theory
[
13
,
27
,
50
,
52
,
29
]
.

A natural question arises: can a quasitriangular Hopf algebra be expressed as a tensor product of two non-trivial quasitriangular Hopf algebras? The categorical analogue of this question asks whether a braided finite tensor category is braided equivalent to the Deligne tensor product of two braided finite tensor categories.
It is known that a semisimple braided finite tensor category is braided equivalent to the Deligne tensor product of a non-degenerate braided tensor subcategory and its relative Müger centralizer (See
[
33
]
for details).
In 2019, K. Shimizu
[
22
]
established dimension formulas for relative Müger centralizers in the non-semisimple case.
Building on these results, R. Laugwitz and C. Walton
[
40
]
extended the conclusion to non-semisimple settings in 2022.
Moreover, two representation categories of quasitriangular Hopf algebras are braided equivalent if and only if one quasitriangular Hopf algebra is Hopf isomorphic to a twist of the other.
As a result, any finite-dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebra with a factorizable quotient Hopf algebra can be expressed as a twisted tensor product of two quasitriangular Hopf algebras.
This leads us to modify the question: can a quasitriangular Hopf algebra be expressed as a twisted tensor product of two non-trivial quasitriangular Hopf algebras?
We say a quasitriangular Hopf algebra has splitting property if it is a twisted tensor product of two non-trivial quasitriangular Hopf algebras.

Their methods rely heavily on dimension formulas.
In contrast, we propose an alternative approach that allows us to eliminate the finiteness assumption.
In this paper, we establish a series of sufficient conditions for the splitting property of quasitriangular Hopf algebras.

Main Theorem 1.1

(Theorems
4.6
,
4.10
)
.

Suppose that
(H,R)
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra ( which may be infinite dimensional) and
(K,(\pi\otimes\pi)(R))
is a nontrivial quotient Hopf algebra of
(H,R)
.
Suppose that one of the following conditions holds

•

(K,(\pi\otimes\pi)(R))
is factorizable.

•

H^{co\pi}=\prescript{co\pi}{}{H}
and
(K,(\pi\otimes\pi)(R))
is full rank.

Then
(H,R)
has splitting property.

When the quasitriangular Hopf algebra
H
is an extension, we have the following results.

Main Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.2
)
.

Suppose that
(H,R)
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra (may infinite dimension) and one of the following conditions holds:

•

H
is an extension of a Hopf algebra
A
by
K
and
K
is factorizable.

•

H
is an extension of a Hopf algebra
A
by
K
and
K

Then

In general, identifying obstructions to the existence of quasitriangular structures in finite-dimensional Hopf algebras is challenging. However, leveraging the splitting property, we derive a concrete obstruction for such structures.

Main Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
6.4
)
.

Suppose
H
is a finite dimensional Hopf algebra.
Suppose
|G(H^{*})|\neq 1
,
G(H^{*})\cap Z(H^{*})=\{1\}
,
G(H)\cap Z(H)=\{1\}
, and there exists an odd prime factor
p
of
|G(H^{*})|
such that for any

with order

and any
\alpha\in G(H^{*})
with order

\alpha(g)\neq 1

Then
H
don’t have any universal
R

Notably, this criterion depends solely on the group parts of Hopf algebras.
We anticipate that this approach will provide a convenient method for excluding certain Hopf algebras without quasitriangular structures.

The existing criteria for determining whether a Hopf algebra
H
is quasitriangular are very few, such as checking if the representation ring of
H
, is commutative or if the antipode of
H
is inner (
S^{2}(h)=uhgu^{-1}
for some invertible element

or
S^{4}(h)=ghg^{-1}
for some

However, these criteria are often not sufficient.
For example the Taft algebra
H_{p^{2}}
(Example
6.5
) with dimension
p^{2}
for an odd prime
p
satisfies both the commutativity of its representation ring (
[
21
]
) and the inner property of its antipode, but it admits no quasitriangular structure, which can be deduced by our obstruction.

The remarkable theorem of S. Natale (
[
46
, Theorem 1.2]
) plays a pivotal role in addressing a question posed by N. Andruskiewitsch
[
31
, Question 6.5]
in the context of quasitriangular Hopf algebras.
The original proof of this theorem is intricate and profound.
Here, we aim to provide a shorter proof by leveraging our results.

This paper is organized as follows.
Section 2, we review the Hopf algebra in a braided monoidal category and the Cleft extension.
Section 3, we study the twisted tensor product and revisit the splitting property of finite dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebras.
Section 4, we study the splitting property for (possibly infinite dimensional) quasitriangular Hopf algebras and obtain a series sufficient conditions for the splitting property.
Section 5, we study the splitting property for extensions of Hopf algebras.
Section 6, we obtain an obstruction of quasitriangular structure in finite dimensional Hopf algebras.
Moreover, we present a short proof of Natale’s result in
[

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Quasitriangular Hopf Algebras

A
quasitriangular Hopf algebra
is a pair
(H,R)
, where
H\equiv(H,m,\eta,\Delta,\epsilon,S)
is a Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
and
\displaystyle R=\sum_{i}R_{i}\otimes R^{i}
is the
R
-matrix.
Precisely,
R
is an invertible element in
H\otimes H
such that

(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{Id})(R)=R_{13}R_{23},\;(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\Delta)(R)=R_{13}R_{12},\;,

and
\Delta^{op}(h)=R\Delta(h)R^{-1}
for all
h\in H
, where
\Delta^{op}=\tau\circ\Delta
, and
\tau
is the flip map.
Here,
\displaystyle R_{12}=\sum_{i}R_{i}\otimes R^{i}\otimes 1
, with similar definitions for
R_{13}
and
R_{23}
.

Given a Hopf algebra
(H,m,\eta,\Delta,\epsilon,S)
, we write
H^{cop}
(resp.
H^{op}
) as the Hopf algebra
(H,m,\eta,\tau\circ\Delta,\epsilon,S)
(resp.
(H,m\circ\tau,\eta,\Delta,\epsilon,S)
). And we will use Sweedler’s notation for its coproduct.

The finite dual
H^{o}
of
H
is defined by

\displaystyle H^{o}=\{f\in H^{*}|\;f(\mathcal{I}_{f})=0\text{ for some finite codimensional ideal }\mathcal{I}_{f}\text{ in }H\}.

The Hopf algebra structure on
H^{o}
is induced by
H
.(see
[
11
]
for example).

Suppose
(H_{1},R_{1}),(H_{2},R_{2})
are quasitriangular Hopf algebras.
We say
(H_{1},R_{1})
and
(H_{2},R_{2})
are isomorphic as quasitriangular Hopf algebras if there exists a Hopf isomorphism
\Psi:H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
that preserves their quasitriangular structures, meaning
(\Psi\otimes\Psi)(R_{1})=R_{2}
.

A quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(H,R)
is called
a ribbon Hopf algebra
if there exists a ribbon element in
H
, i.e. a central element
\theta
in
H
satisfying the relations:
\epsilon(\theta)=1
,
S(\theta)=\theta
and

\Delta(\theta)=(R_{21}\cdot R)^{-1}(\theta\otimes\theta),

where

\displaystyle R_{21}\cdot R=(m\otimes m)(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\tau\otimes\operatorname{Id})((\tau R)\otimes R).

Two ribbon Hopf algebras are said to be isomorphic as ribbon Hopf algebras if there exists a Hopf isomorphism between them that preserves both their quasitriangular structures and their ribbon elements.

We define the linear maps
\Phi_{R}:H^{o}\rightarrow H
and
\Phi_{\tau R}:H^{o}\rightarrow H
as follows:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{R}(f):

\displaystyle=(f\otimes\operatorname{Id})(R_{21}\cdot R),\;f\in H^{o}.

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau R}(f):

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{Id}\otimes f)(R_{21}\cdot R),\;f\in H^{o}.

A
factorizable Hopf algebra
is a finite dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(H,R)
for which
\Phi_{R}
or equivalently
\Phi_{\tau R}
, is a linear isomorphism.
Suppose
C\subseteq H^{o}
be a subcoalgebra.
Then
\Phi_{R}(C)\subseteq H
is a normal left coideal of
H
(See
[
46
, Lemma 4.1]
.)

Remark 2.1
.

It’s known that
\Phi_{R}(H^{o})
is a normal left coideal subalgebra of
H
(see
[
45
, Chapter 9]
).
More generally, if
B\subseteq H^{o}
is a Hopf subalgebra, then
\Phi_{R}(B)
is a normal left coideal subalgebra of
H
(see
[
46
, Section 3]
).

Suppose
(K,\pi)
is a quotient Hopf algebra of
(H,R)
, where
\pi:H\to K
is a surjective Hopf map.
Let

\displaystyle H^{co\pi}=

\displaystyle\{h\in H|\;(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta=h\otimes 1\},

\displaystyle\prescript{co\pi}{}{H}=

\displaystyle\{h\in H|\;(\pi\otimes\operatorname{Id})\circ\Delta=1\otimes h\}.

H^{co\pi}
(resp.
\prescript{co\pi}{}{H}
) is normal left (resp. right) coideal subalgebra of
H
.

For the quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(H,R)
, there are Hopf maps
l_{R}:(H^{o})^{cop}\rightarrow H
and
r_{R}:(H^{o})^{op}\rightarrow H
, given respectively by

l_{R}(f):=(f\otimes\operatorname{Id})(R),\;\;r_{R}(f):=(\operatorname{Id}\otimes f)(R),\;f\in H^{o}.

If the above map
l_{R}
is surjective, we say
(H,R)
is
full rank
.

\underline{S}(k):=\sum_{i}\pi(R^{i})S(\text{ad}_{\pi(R_{i})}(k)),\quad k\in\underline{K}.

The action of

\displaystyle h.k:=\text{ad}_{\pi(h)}(k),\quad h\in H,\ k\in\underline{K}.

Now we see that

Similarly, the finite dual

The action of

\displaystyle\langle h\triangleright f,k\rangle=\langle f,S(\pi(h_{(1)}))k\pi(h_{(2)})\rangle,\quad h\in H,k\in\underline{K},f\in\underline{K^{o}}.

Moreover, the multiplication and the antipode of

(2.8)

The linear map

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
(H,R)
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra and
(K,\pi)
a quotient Hopf algebra of
(H,R)
.
We have that
\underline{\pi}
is a Hopf map in
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
and
(\underline{H},(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\underline{\pi})\circ\underline{\Delta})
is a right
\underline{K}
-comodule algebra in
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
.

Proof.

Note that
\underline{H},\underline{K}\in\operatorname{Rep}(H)
and
\pi:H\to K
is a Hopf map.
We see that
\underline{\pi}
is a morphism in
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
and
\underline{\pi}
is an algebra map in
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
.
Now we will show that
\underline{\pi}
is a coalgebra map in
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
.
For any
h\in\underline{H}
, we have that

\displaystyle(\underline{\pi}\otimes\underline{\pi})\circ\underline{\Delta}(h)

\displaystyle=(\underline{\pi}\otimes\underline{\pi})(\sum_{i}h_{(1)}S(R^{i})\otimes\text{ad}_{R_{i}}(h_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\pi(h_{(1)})S(\pi(R^{i}))\otimes\pi[(R_{i})_{(1)})h_{(2)}S((R_{i})_{(2)})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\pi(h_{(1)})S(\pi(R^{i}))\otimes\pi((R_{i})_{(1)})\pi(h_{(2)})\pi(S((R_{i})_{(2)}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\pi(h_{(1)})S(\pi(R^{i}))\otimes\pi(R_{i})_{(1)})\pi(h_{(2)})S(\pi(R_{i})_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\pi(h)_{(1)}S(\pi(R^{i}))\otimes\pi(R_{i})_{(1)})\pi(h)_{(2)}S(\pi(R_{i})_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\pi(h)_{(1)}S(\pi(R^{i}))\otimes\text{ad}_{\pi(R_{i})}(\pi(h)_{(2)})

\displaystyle=\underline{\Delta}\circ\underline{\pi}(h).

This implies that
\underline{\pi}
is a coalgebra map in
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
.
To show that
(\underline{H},(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\underline{\pi})\circ\underline{\Delta})
is a right

-comodule algebra in

\displaystyle(\operatorname{Id}_{\underline{H}}\otimes\underline{\pi})\circ\Delta_{\underline{H}}\circ m_{\underline{H}}

\displaystyle=(m_{\underline{H}}\otimes m_{\underline{K}})\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{\underline{H}}\otimes c_{\underline{K},\underline{H}}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\underline{H}})\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{\underline{H}}\otimes\underline{\pi}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{\underline{H}}\otimes\underline{\pi})\circ(\Delta_{\underline{H}}\otimes\Delta_{\underline{H}}).

∎

Let
\underline{H^{co\pi}}=\{h\in\underline{H}|\;(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\underline{\pi})\circ\underline{\Delta}(h)=h\otimes 1\}

Lemma 2.4
.

We have
\underline{H^{co\pi}}=H^{co\pi}
as vector spaces.

Proof.

Let

We have
\displaystyle\sum_{i}h_{(1)}S(R^{i})\otimes\underline{\pi}(\text{ad}_{R_{i}}(h_{(2)}))=h\otimes 1
. Thus, we obtain

\sum_{i,j}h_{(1)}S(R^{i})S(R^{j})\otimes\text{ad}_{\pi\circ S(R_{j})}[\underline{\pi}(\text{ad}_{R_{i}}(h_{(2)}))]=h\otimes 1,

Combing this equation with

, we know
(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta(h)=h\otimes 1
, i.e.

. Thus,
\underline{H^{co\pi}}\subseteq H^{co\pi}
. Similarly, we get
H^{co\pi}\subseteq\underline{H^{co\pi}}
. Hence, we complete the proof.

2.3.
Cleft Extension

We recall cleft extensions in a braided monoidal category
\mathcal{D}
(see
[
4
]

Suppose
H
is a Hopf algebra in
\mathcal{D}
.

Let
A
be an algebra in
\mathcal{D}
and
\mu_{A}\in\mathcal{D}(H\otimes A,A)
.
The morphism
\mu_{A}
is an action of
H

(i)

\mu_{A}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{A})=\operatorname{Id}_{A}
;

(ii)

\mu_{A}\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{H}\otimes\eta_{A})=\epsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{A}

\mu_{A}\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{H}\otimes m_{A})=m_{A}\circ\mu_{A\otimes A}

\displaystyle\mu_{A\otimes A}=(\mu_{A}\otimes\mu_{A})\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{H}\otimes c_{H,A}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{A})\circ(\Delta_{H}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{A}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{A}).

For an action
\mu_{A}
and a morphism
\sigma\in\text{Reg}(H\otimes H,A)
, we formally define two morphisms
\eta_{A\#_{\sigma}H}
and
m_{A\#_{\sigma}H}

\displaystyle\eta_{A\#_{\sigma}H}:=\eta_{A}\otimes\eta_{H},

\displaystyle m_{A\#_{\sigma}H}:=

\displaystyle(m_{A}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{H})\circ(m_{A}\otimes\sigma\otimes m_{H})\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{A}\otimes m_{A}\otimes\Delta_{H\otimes H})

\displaystyle\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{A}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{H}\otimes c_{H,A}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{H})\circ(\operatorname{Id}_{A}\otimes\Delta_{H}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{A}\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{H}).

We say
(A\otimes H,m_{A\#_{\sigma}H},\eta_{A\#_{\sigma}H})
is a
crossed product
of
A
and
H
in
\mathcal{D}
, if it is an algebra in
\mathcal{D}
.

Now we recall
H
-cleft comodule algebra in a braided monoidal category
\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 2.5
.

[

in

(H,A)
.

a crossed product in

.

Moreover, all

3.
Twisted Tensor Product

In this section, we introduce the twisted tensor product of two quasitriangular Hopf algebras.

Recall that a normalized twist for a Hopf algebra
H
is an invertible element
J\in H\otimes H
which satisfying

\displaystyle(\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{Id})(J)=(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\epsilon)(J)=1

and

(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{Id})(J)(J\otimes 1)=(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\Delta)(J)(1\otimes J).

With this twist, we can define a twisted Hopf algebra
H^{J}
whose comultiplication
\Delta^{J}
is given by

\Delta^{J}(h)=J\Delta(h)J^{-1},\quad h\in H

while its algebra structure remains the same as that of
H
.

According to
[
43
, Theorem 2.2]
, two Hopf algebras
H
and
H^{\prime}
are gauge equivalent (which means their representation categories are equivalent as tensor categories)
if and only if there exists a twist
J
of
H
such that
H^{\prime}\cong H^{J}
as Hopf algebras.
Furthermore, if both
(H,R)
and
(H^{\prime},R^{\prime})
are quasitriangular Hopf algebras, then their representation categories are equivalent as braided tensor categories if and only if there exists a twist
J
of
H
such that

(H^{\prime},R^{\prime})\cong(H^{J},R^{J})\text{ as quasitriangular Hopf algebras},

where
R^{J}
is given by

R^{J}:=J_{21}RJ^{-1}.

Definition 3.1

(Twisted Tensor Product)
.

Suppose
(H,R)
,
(K_{1},R_{1})
,
(K_{2},R_{2})
are quasitriangular Hopf algebras.
We say
(H,R)
is a twisted tensor product of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
if there exists a twist
J
of
K_{1}\otimes K_{2}
such that

\displaystyle(H,R)\cong(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J},\widetilde{R}^{J})

as quasitriangular Hopf algebras,
where
\displaystyle\widetilde{R}=\sum_{i,j}[(R_{1})_{i}\otimes(R_{2})_{j}]\otimes[(R_{1})^{i}\otimes(R_{2})^{j}]
.

If the Hopf algebras
K_{1},K_{2}
above are not isomorphic to
\Bbbk
, we say that
(H,R)
is a
non-trivial
twisted tensor product of them. Note that the Hopf algebra
(H,R)
in this definition may be triangular, and we will consider some of these cases (eg. Theorem
4.10
).

Suppose
(H,R)
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra and
(K,\pi)
is a quotient Hopf algebra of
(H,R)
.
We will simply denote the corresponding braided monoidal category
(\operatorname{Rep}(H),\otimes,\Bbbk,c)
as
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
, i.e. the representation category of
H
with the braiding structure
\tau\circ R
, where
\tau
is the flip map. Denote by
\operatorname{Rep}(K)
the representation category of the quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(K,(\pi\otimes\pi)(R))
.
Through the surjective Hopf map
\pi
, we shall view
\operatorname{Rep}(K)
as a natural braided monoidal subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
.

Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle L=\Phi_{R}(K^{*})=\{((f\circ\pi)\otimes\operatorname{Id})(R_{21}\cdot R)|\;f\in K^{*}\}.

By Remark
2.1
, we see that
L
is a normal left coideal subalgebra of
H
.
Therefore,
HL^{+}
is a Hopf ideal and
H/HL^{+}(=K^{\prime})
is a quotient Hopf algebra, where

L^{+}=\{h\in L|\;\epsilon(h)=0\}=L\cap\operatorname{Ker}\epsilon.

Let
\pi^{\prime}:H\to K^{\prime}
be the natural quotient map.
Then
(K^{\prime},(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\pi^{\prime})(R))
is also a quasitriangular Hopf algebra, leading to a corresponding braided monoidal category denoted by
\operatorname{Rep}(K^{\prime})
.
Similarly, we view
\operatorname{Rep}(K^{\prime})
as a braided monoidal subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}(H)
through the quotient map
\pi^{\prime}
.

Let
R_{K}:=(\pi\otimes\pi)(R)
and
R_{K^{\prime}}:=(\pi^{\prime}\otimes\pi^{\prime})(R)
.
Then, there is a natural quasitriangular structure on
K\otimes K^{\prime}
induced by
R_{K},R_{K^{\prime}}
as follows:

(3.2)

R_{K\otimes K^{\prime}}:=\sum_{i,j}[(R_{K})_{i}\otimes(R_{K^{\prime}})_{j}]\otimes[(R_{K})^{i}\otimes(R_{K^{\prime}})^{j}],

where
\displaystyle R_{K}=\sum_{i}(R_{K})_{i}\otimes(R_{K})^{i}
and
\displaystyle R_{K^{\prime}}=\sum_{j}(R_{K^{\prime}})_{j}\otimes(R_{K^{\prime}})^{j}
.

A finite tensor category
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathds{1})

[
33
]
is defined as a rigid monoidal category where
\mathcal{C}
is a finite abelian category.
Here “finite” means that
\mathcal{C}(X,Y)
are finite dimensional and
X
has finite length for all
X,Y\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
, and every simple object of
\mathcal{C}
has a projective cover, the number of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
is finite. The tensor product of
\mathcal{C}
is
\Bbbk
-linear in each variable,
and the unit object of
\mathcal{C}
is a simple object.
If
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathds{1})
is equipped with a braiding
c
,
then it is termed a braided finite tensor category
[
33
]
. Denote
\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(H)
as the braided tensor category consisting of finite dimensional representation of
H
and the braiding structure is
\tau\circ R
.

Recall that a full subcategory of an abelian category is called
topologizing subcategory

[
5
,
22
]
if it is closed under finite direct sums and subquotients. By a
braided tensor subcategory
of a braided tensor category
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathds{1},c)
we mean a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
containing the unit object of
\mathcal{C}
, closed under the tensor product of
\mathcal{C}
, and containing the braiding isomorphisms.

We shall recall that a finite braided tensor category
\mathcal{D}
can factor through the relative Müger centralizer as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
40
, Theorem 4.17]

Let
(\mathcal{D},\otimes,\mathds{1},c)
be a finite braided tensor category, let
\mathcal{E}
be a topologizing non-degenerate braided tensor subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
. Then there is an equivalence of braided tensor categories:

\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathcal{E}\boxtimes C_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{E}),

where

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ob}(C_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{E})):=\{Y\in\mathcal{D}|\;c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=\operatorname{Id}_{X\otimes Y}\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{E}\}

is the relative Müger centralizer.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}
is a ribbon category, then the above equivalence can be an equivalence of ribbon categories.

Note that semisimple case of this theorem is obtained by many authors (
[
33
, Theorem 8.21.4]
).
Then, we have

Lemma 3.3
.

With the notations above and assume that
H
is finite dimensional, the braided tensor subcategory
\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(K^{\prime})
is the Müger centralizer of
\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(K)
in
\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
X\in\text{Ob}(\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(K)),Y\in\text{Ob}(\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(H))
such that
c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=\operatorname{Id}_{X\otimes Y}
.
Then we have
(R_{21}\cdot R)|_{X\otimes Y}=\operatorname{Id}_{X\otimes Y}
.
It’s not difficult to see that
c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}=\operatorname{Id}_{X\otimes Y}
for all
X\in\text{Ob}(\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(K))
if and only if
(R_{21}\cdot R)|_{K\otimes Y}=\operatorname{Id}_{K\otimes Y}
, where
K
is the left regular represenation in
\text{Ob}(\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(K))
.
And the last equation is equivalent to
[(f\circ\pi\otimes\operatorname{Id})(R_{21}\cdot R)]|_{Y}=\epsilon(f)\operatorname{Id}_{Y}
for all
f\in K^{*}
.
Next, we only need to show that
(f\circ\pi\otimes\operatorname{Id})(R_{21}\cdot R)]|_{Y}=\epsilon(f)\operatorname{Id}_{Y}
if and only if
Y\in\text{Ob}(\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(K^{\prime}))
.

Recall that
L=\Phi_{R}(K^{*})
.
Then the equation
[(f\circ\pi\otimes\operatorname{Id})(R_{21}\cdot R)]|_{Y}=\epsilon(f)\operatorname{Id}
for all
f\in K^{*}
is equivalent to
(HL^{+}).Y=0
, i.e.
Y\in\text{Ob}(\operatorname{Rep}_{f}(K^{\prime}))
.
Thus, we achieve the desired conclusion.
∎

4.
Main Results

In this section, we introduce a novel approach to studying the splitting property of quasitriangular Hopf algebras.
This method allows us to eliminate the restriction of finiteness, extending the applicability of the results.
We start by defining the concept of exact factorization in Hopf algebras.

Definition 4.1

(Exact Factorization)
.

Suppose
H
is a Hopf algebra. Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be left coideal subalgebras of
H
.
We say
H=L_{1}L_{2}
is an exact factorization if the linear map
\varphi:L_{1}\otimes L_{2}\to H
defined by
\varphi(u\otimes v)=uv
is bijective, where
u\in L_{1}
,
v\in L_{2}
. Moreover, if
L_{1},L_{2}
normal, then we call this exact factorization is normal.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
(H,R)
is a quasitriangular (not necessarily finite-dimensional) Hopf algebra.
The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

There exist quotient Hopf algebras
(K_{1},\pi_{1})
,
(K_{2},\pi_{2})
of
(H,R)
such that
(H,R)
is a twisted tensor product of
(K_{1},\pi_{1})
and
(K_{2},\pi_{2})
with twist
J
:

J=\sum_{i}(1\otimes\pi_{2}(S(R_{i})))\otimes(\pi_{1}(R^{i})\otimes 1).

(2)

There exist normal left coideal subalgebras
L_{1},L_{2}
of
(H,R)
such that

•

H=L_{1}L_{2}
is an exact factorization;

•

(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})(R_{21}R)=1\otimes 1
, where
\pi_{1}
(resp.
\pi_{2}
) denote the associated natural quotient map with
H/HL_{1}^{+}
(resp.
H/HL_{2}^{+}
);

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2):
We assume that
H=((K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J},R_{K_{1}\otimes K_{2}}^{J})
.
Let
L_{1}=K_{1}\otimes 1\subseteq H
and
L_{2}=S(1\otimes K_{2})\subseteq H
.
It is clear that
K_{1}\otimes 1
is normal left coideal subalgebra and
1\otimes K_{2}
is normal right coideal subalgebra of
(H,R)
, i.e. a right coideal subalgebra such that
S(h_{1})kh_{2}\in K_{2}
for all
h\in H,k\in K_{2}
.
This implies that
L_{1},L_{2}
are normal left coideal subalgebras of
(H,R)
. Moreover,
(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})(R_{21}R)=1\otimes 1
in this case. Now we see that the map
\varphi
is bijective.
Thus, the normal left coideal subalgebras
L_{1},L_{2}
are obtained as required.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1):
Let
K_{1}=H/HL_{1}^{+}
and
K_{2}=H/HL_{2}^{+}
and denote the associated natural quotient map by
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
.
Let

\displaystyle J:=\sum_{i}(1\otimes\pi_{2}(S(R_{i})))\otimes(\pi_{1}(R^{i})\otimes 1)\in K_{1}\otimes K_{2}.

By
[
20
, Lemma 4.2]
, we see that
J
is a twist for the quasitriangular Hopf algebra
K_{1}\otimes K_{2}
.
We define
F:H\to K_{1}\otimes K_{2}
as
F=(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})\circ\Delta
.
Then
F
is a Hopf map.
Moreover,
(F\otimes F)(R)=R_{K_{1}\otimes K_{2}}^{J}
by using
(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})(R_{21}R)=1\otimes 1
, where

\displaystyle R_{K_{1}\otimes K_{2}}^{J}=J_{21}R_{K_{1}\otimes K_{2}}J^{-1}.

Next, we show
F
is surjective.
Since
L_{1}
is left coideal and
\pi_{1}(a)=\epsilon(a)1
for
a\in L_{1}
, we have that
a\otimes 1=a_{(1)}\otimes\pi_{1}(a_{(2)})
for all
a\in L_{1}
.
This implies for all
a\in L_{1}
,

\displaystyle 1\otimes S^{-1}(a)=\pi_{1}\big(S^{-1}(a)_{(1)}\big)\otimes S^{-1}(a)_{(2)}.

Applying the Hopf map
F
, we see that for
a\in L_{1}
,

\displaystyle F(S^{-1}(a))=1\otimes S_{K_{2}}^{-1}\circ\pi_{2}(a).

Therefore
S_{(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}}(1\otimes\pi_{2}(L_{1}))\subseteq\operatorname{im}(F)
.
By the fact that
\varphi
is bijective, we have that
\pi_{2}|_{L_{1}}
,
\pi_{1}|_{L_{2}}
are bijective.
Thus,
S_{(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}}(1\otimes K_{2})\in\operatorname{im}(F)
.
Similarly we have
F(b)=\pi_{1}(b)\otimes 1
for
b\in L_{2}
.
Thus, we obtain that
K_{1}\otimes 1\in\operatorname{im}(F)
.
By the fact that
F
is Hopf map and
(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}=(K_{1}\otimes 1)S_{(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}}(1\otimes\pi_{2}(L_{1}))
, we see that
F
is surjective.

Now we show that
F
is injective.
Let
\{x_{i}|i\in I\}
be a linear basis of
L_{1}
and
\{y_{j}|j\in I^{\prime}\}
a linear basis of
L_{2}
.
Note that
\pi_{2}|_{L_{1}},\pi_{1}|_{L_{2}}
are bijective.
We see that
\{S_{K_{2}}^{-1}\circ\pi_{2}(x_{i})|i\in I\}
is a linear basis of
1\otimes K_{2}
and
\{\pi_{1}(y_{j})|j\in I^{\prime}\}
is a linear basis of
K_{1}\otimes 1
.
Let
h\in H
such that
F(h)=0
.
Since
L_{1}
is normal, we obtain
H=L_{2}L_{1}
.
Thus, we write
\displaystyle h=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\lambda_{ij}y_{j}x_{i}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
\lambda_{ij}\in\Bbbk
.
Note that
F(a)=S_{(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}}(1\otimes S_{K_{2}}^{-1}\circ\pi_{2}(a))
for
a\in L_{1}
and
F(b)=\pi_{1}(b)\otimes 1
for
b\in L_{2}
, we obtain

F\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\lambda_{ij}y_{j}x_{i}\right)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\lambda_{ij}[\pi_{1}(y_{j})\otimes 1][S_{(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}}(1\otimes S_{K_{2}}^{-1}\circ\pi_{2}(x_{i}))].

L_{1}^{\prime}=K_{1}\otimes 1\subseteq(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}
and let
L_{2}^{\prime}=S_{(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}}(1\otimes K_{2})\subseteq(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}
.
By statement (1), we have that the following map is bijective

\begin{matrix}\varphi^{\prime}:&L_{1}^{\prime}\otimes L_{2}^{\prime}&\rightarrow&(K_{1}\otimes K_{2})^{J}\\
\;&u\otimes v&\mapsto&uv\end{matrix}

Combing this with the fact that
\{1\otimes S_{K_{2}}^{-1}\circ\pi_{2}(x_{i})|i\in I\}
is a linear basis of
1\otimes K_{2}
and
\{\pi_{1}(y_{j})\otimes 1|j\in I^{\prime}\}
is a linear basis of
K_{1}\otimes 1
, we get that
\lambda_{ij}=0
for all
i,j=1,\ldots,n
.
This implies
h=0
, and hence
F
is injective.
∎

(4.2)

\textstyle{\underline{H}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\underline{K}}
\textstyle{\underline{H^{o}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\underline{K^{*}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Definition 5.1

\;\;A\xrightarrow{\iota}H\xrightarrow{\pi}K

\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)=HA^{+}

where

An extension

of

is abelian if

is commutative and

is cocommutative.

For an exact sequence (

), we say that
A
is a normal Hopf
subalgebra of

, i.e.
h_{(1)}kS(h_{(2)})\in K
for all

.

When the extension

is abelian,

is finite dimensional and

is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, we see that there exist finite groups

,

\;\;\Bbbk^{G}\xrightarrow{\iota}H\xrightarrow{\pi}\Bbbk F,

Theorem 5.2

Suppose

is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra satisfying the following extension:

6.
Criterion for Quasitriangular Hopf Algebras

In this section, we will provide a criterion for finite dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebras.

Let
f\in H^{*}
and let
h\in H
, define the actions as follows:

f\rightharpoonup h:=h_{(1)}f(h_{(2)}),\;h\leftharpoonup f:=f(h_{(1)})h_{(2)}.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
11

Suppose that
(H,R)
is a finite dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebra. Then, for all
h\in H
and
f\in H^{*}
, we have

(f_{(1)}\rightharpoonup h)l_{R}(f_{(2)})=l_{R}(f_{(1)})(h\leftharpoonup f_{(2)}).

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose
(H,R)
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra.
Then
H
must satisfies one of following conditions:

(i)

|G(H^{o})|=1
;

(ii)

|G(H^{o})|\neq 1
and
G(H^{o})\cap Z(H^{o})\neq\{1\}
;

(iii)

|G(H^{o})|\neq 1
and
G(H^{o})\cap Z(H^{o})=\{1\}
and
Z(G(H))\neq\{1\}
;

where
Z(H)
(resp.
Z(H^{o})
) is the center of
H
(resp.
H^{o}
).

Proof.

Suppose
|G(H^{o})|\neq 1
. Let
1\neq\alpha\in G(H^{o})
. If
l_{R}(\alpha)=1
, using Lemma
6.1
by letting
f=\alpha
, we conclude that
\alpha\rightharpoonup h=h\leftharpoonup\alpha
for all

. From this, we know
\alpha\in G(H^{o})\cap Z(H^{o})
, i.e. (ii) holds. If
l_{R}(\alpha)\neq 1
, then
l_{R}(\alpha)\in G(H)
and it belongs to the center of

, which implies (iii).
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Suppose
(H,R)
is a finite dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebra. Then

(ii)

Then we have

\alpha\in G(H^{*})
with order
p
such that
\alpha(g)=1
; In particular,
p^{2}|\operatorname{dim}(H)
.

Proof.

Since
H
is simple Hopf algebra with finite dimension , we know that
G(H^{*})\cap Z(H^{*})=\{1\}
and
G(H)\cap Z(H)=\{1\}
.
Now applying Proposition
6.3
, we see the corollary is true.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

Suppose
H
is a self-dual non-semisimple simple Hopf algebra with odd dimension.
If there exists an odd prime factor
p
of
|G(H^{*})|
such that for any
g\in G(H)
with order
p
and any
\alpha\in G(H^{*})
with order
p
,
\alpha(g)\neq 1
, then
H
admits no universal
R
-matrix.

Proof.

Since
H
is non-semisimple Hopf algebra with odd dimension, we know
\text{Tr}|_{S^{2}}
is zero.
This implies the distinguished group-like element of
H^{*}
is not
1
by Radford’s fourth power formula, i.e.
|G(H^{*})|\neq 1
.
Thus, we can apply Corollary
6.6
to complete the proof.
∎

In the end of the section, we will apply Corollary
6.6
to obtain a short proof of a result of Natale.
For readers’ convenience, we state her result as follows:

Theorem 6.8
.

[
46
, Theorem 1.2]

Let
(H,R)
be a finite dimensional quasitriangular Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
Assume that
\operatorname{dim}(H)
is odd and square-free.
Then
H
is semisimple.

Proof of Theorem
6.8
.

Let
n
be the number of prime factors of
\operatorname{dim}(H)
.
We will proceed by induction on
n
.
For
n=1
, by
[
56
]
,
H
is a group algebra, and therefore
H
is semisimple.

Now, assume
H
is semisimple for
n\leq k
.
We need to prove that
H
is also semisimple for
n=k+1
.
Let
H_{l}:=l_{R}(H^{*})=\{l_{R}(f)|\;f\in H^{*}\}
. If
\operatorname{dim}(H_{l})\neq\operatorname{dim}(H)
.
Assume that
p
is an odd prime factor of
\operatorname{dim}(H)
such that
p\nmid\operatorname{dim}(H_{l})
.
This implies that
p\nmid\operatorname{dim}(D(H_{l}))
.
Note that
H_{R}
is a quotient Hopf algebra of
D(H_{l})
.
Hence,
p\nmid\operatorname{dim}(H_{R})
.
This implies that
H_{R}
is a proper Hopf subalgebra of
H
, i.e. the number of prime factors of
\operatorname{dim}(H_{R})
is less than
k+1
.
By the inductive hypothesis,
H_{R}
is semisimple. Combining this with the assumption that
(H,R)
is quasitriangular with odd dimension, we obtain that
H
is also semisimple.

If
\operatorname{dim}(H_{l})=\operatorname{dim}(H)
, then
l_{R}
is an isomorphism.
In this case, if
H
is not simple Hopf algebra, we can apply induction to show that
H
must be an extension of semisimple Hopf algebras, implying
H
is semisimple (
[
38
]
).

The remaining case is when
H
is a simple Hopf algebra. By Corollary
6.6
and dimensional arguments, we know that
|G(H^{*})|=1
. Since
H\cong(H^{*})^{cop}
, we also have
|G(H)|=1
, which implies
S^{4}=\operatorname{Id}
. As
\operatorname{dim}(H)
is odd, we know that
\text{Tr}(S^{2})\neq 0
. Therefore, by
[
11
, Proposition 10.4.2]
,
H
is also semisimple.
∎

Remark 6.9
.

In S. Natale’s proof in
[
46
]
, she firstly studied the conormality of the map
\pi:H\rightarrow H/HL^{+}
, where
\pi
is the natural quotient map and
L=\Phi_{R}(H^{*})
.
Under these conditions, along with the classification of triangular Hopf algebras from
[
36
]
, she proved Theorem
6.8
.
However, our proof primarily relies on Corollary
6.6
, which is a byproduct of Theorem
4.6
.

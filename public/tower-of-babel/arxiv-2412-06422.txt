Universal 𝐂 ∗ \bm{{\mathrm{C}^{\ast}}} -algebras generated by doubly non-commuting isometries
Marcel de Jeu, Alexey Kuzmin, Paulo R. Pinto

Abstract
. We give an explicit injective representation of the universal C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -algebra that is generated by doubly non-commuting isometries. This injectivity allows us to prove that such universal algebras embed naturally into each other and also, when combined with Rieffel’s theory of deformation, to show that they are nuclear and to compute their K-theory.

1.
Introduction and overview

In this paper, we study the universal
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
​-algebras in the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
n\geq 1
is an integer and that, for all
i\neq j
with
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
z_{ij}\in{\mdmathbb T}
are given such that
z_{ij}={\overline{z}}_{ij}

denote the universal unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra that is generated by

such that

(1.1)

s_{i}^{\ast}s_{j}^{\phantom{\ast}}={\overline{z}}_{ij}^{\phantom{\ast}}s_{j}^{\phantom{\ast}}s_{i}^{\ast}

and

(1.2)

s_{i}^{\phantom{\ast}}s_{j}^{\phantom{\ast}}=z_{ij}^{\phantom{\ast}}s_{j}s_{i}

for all

with
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

(1.3)

s_{i}^{\ast}s_{i}^{\phantom{\ast}}=1

(1.4)

s_{i}^{\ast}s_{i}^{\phantom{\ast}}=s_{i}^{\phantom{\ast}}s_{i}^{\ast}=1

for

In view of

1.2

.

[
5

{\mdmathbb N}_{0}\coloneqq\{0,1,2,\dotsc\}

Set

(1.5)

H_{l,n-l}\coloneqq\ell^{2}({\mdmathbb N}_{0})^{\otimes l}\otimes\ell^{2}({\mdmathbb Z})^{\otimes(n-l)},

The main result of the present paper is the injectivity of the standard representation

When

This paper is organised as follows.

In
Section

2
, we show in a number of steps that the standard representation is injective.

One can select a subset of the generators of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
and retain the corresponding structure constants
z_{ij}
. This yields natural unital
\ast
-homomorphisms from ‘smaller’ universal
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
​-algebras into ‘larger’ ones. In
Section

3
, it is shown that ‘smaller’ and ‘larger’ are, indeed, correct to speak of: these natural unital
\ast
-homomorphisms are injective. The proof of this fact uses the injectivity of the standard representation.

In
Section

4
, Rieffel’s theory of deformation is applied. Using the fact that the standard representations are injective, it is shown that, for fixed
n
and
l
, the algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
are Rieffel deformations of each other for all choices of the
z_{ij}
. Taking the
z_{ij}
equal to 1, it is then immediate that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
is nuclear, a fact which is also established in
[
8
, Theorem 6.2]
for a larger class of algebras using a theorem of Rosenberg’s (see
[
12
, Theorem 3]
). Since K-theory is stable under Rieffel deformation, it is then straightforward to compute the K-groups of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
. Using more elaborate methods, these are also determined in
[
1
]
.

2.
Injectivity of the standard representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}

In this section, we show that the standard representation
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
on
H_{l,n-l}
is injective. As explained in
Section

1
, this is done by finding a faithful unital completely positive map
\phi
from
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
into the bounded operators on a Hilbert space, and showing that the injective corresponding Stinespring representation is essentially an inflation of the standard representation
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
. Hence
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}
must itself be injective.

This programme is completed in a number of steps.

2.1.
The involutive algebra underlying
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}

We let
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
denote the universal unital involutive algebra generated by elements
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
satisfying the relations in
equations

1.1
,
1.2
,
1.3
, and
1.4
.
For
x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
, we set

{\lVert x\rVert}\coloneqq\sup{\left\{{\lVert\pi(x)\rVert}:\pi\text{ is a unital $\ast$-representation of }{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}\right\}}.

This supremum is finite since the
\pi(s_{i})
are always isometries, and
{\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert}
is then a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-seminorm on
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
. If we let
I
denote its kernel, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
is the completion of
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}/I
in the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-norm that
{\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert}
induces on this quotient; see, e.g.,
[
2
, II.8.3.1]
for the construction of universal
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras.

The first step in our programme is to show that
{\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert}
is actually a norm on
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
, so that
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
can be identified with a dense subalgebra of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
. For this, we again take
H_{l,n-l}
and
S_{1},\dotsc,S_{n}
as in
equations

1.5
and
1.6
, respectively, and we define—in an anticipating terminology and notation—the standard representation

\pi_{\mathrm{s}}:{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}\to\mathrm{B}(H_{l,n-l})

of
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
on
H_{l,n-l}
by requiring that
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{i})=S_{i}
for
i=1,\dotsc,n
. We shall show that the standard representation of
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
is injective, which shows that
{\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert}
is a norm.
We shall prove at the same time that a certain canonical spanning set for
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
is, in fact, a basis of
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}

For this, we start by collecting a few algebraic results which will be used throughout the paper. The proofs are elementary and left to the reader.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
n\geq 1
is an integer and that, for all
i\neq j
with
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

are given such that

Let

are elements of

1.2

Then:

(1)

s_{j}^{\ast}s_{i}^{\phantom{\ast}}=z_{ij}^{\phantom{\ast}}s_{i}^{\phantom{\ast}}s_{j}^{\ast}
for all

with

(2)

(3)

for all

and all

;

(4)

s_{i}^{k}s_{i}^{\ast k}
is a selfadjoint idempotent for all

with
1\leq i\leq n
and all
k\geq 0
.

(5)

for all

1\leq i\leq n
and for all

\left(s_{i}^{k}s_{i}^{\ast k}\right)\left(s_{i}^{l}s_{i}^{\ast l}\right)=\left(s_{i}^{l}s_{i}^{\ast l}\right)\left(s_{i}^{k}s_{i}^{\ast k}\right)=s_{i}^{l}s_{i}^{\ast l};

(6)

s_{i}^{\phantom{\ast}}
and
s_{i}^{\ast}
commute with
s_{j}^{k}s_{j}^{\ast k}
for all
i\neq j
with
1\leq i,j\leq n
and all

.

We can now describe a spanning set of
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
that will turn out to be a basis. First of all, as a consequence of

1.1
and
1.2
and

and

and
s_{1}^{\ast}
, then a product in
s_{2}
and
s_{2}^{\ast}
, etc. For

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
be written as finite linear combination

x=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{e_1,f_1,\dotsc,e_l,f_l\geq 0}{ g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_n\in\ZZ}}\lambda_{e_{1},f_{1},\dotsc,e_{l},f_{l},g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}}s_{1}^{e_{1}}s_{1}^{\ast f_{1}}s_{1}^{e_{1}}s_{1}^{\ast f_{1}}\dotsm s_{l}^{e_{l}}s_{l}^{\ast f_{l}}s_{l+1}^{g_{l+1}}\dotsm s_{n}^{g_{n}}

for some complex constants
\lambda_{e_{1},f_{1},\dotsc,e_{l},f_{l},g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}}
.
Suppose that
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(x)=0
. Then
\lambda_{e_{1},f_{1},\dotsc,e_{l},f_{l},g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}}=0
for all
e_{i},f_{i}\geq 0
and all
g_{j}\in{\mdmathbb Z}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
l=0
. Since, in particular,
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(x)\epsilon_{0,\dotsc,0}^{\!\!\!\!\phantom{k_{i}}}=0
, and since
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{1}^{g_{1}}\dotsm s_{n}^{g_{n}})\epsilon_{0,\dotsc,0}^{\!\!\!\!\phantom{k_{i}}}=\epsilon_{g_{1},\dotsc,g_{n}}
for all
g_{1},\dotsc,g_{n}\in{\mdmathbb Z}
, the conclusion in the statement is immediate.

Suppose that
l
is such that
1\leq l\leq n
. It is then sufficient to prove the following:
Let
p\geq 0
, and suppose that
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{n}\geq 0
are such that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}f_{i}=p
. Then
\lambda_{e_{1},f_{1},\dotsc,e_{l},f_{l},g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}}=0
for all
e_{1},\dotsc,e_{l}\geq 0
and all
g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}\in{\mdmathbb Z}
. We prove this statement by induction on

. For
p=0
, the argument is as follows.

Write
x

x=\sum_{p\geq 0}\!\!\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{f_1,\dotsc,f_l\geq 0}{f_1+\dotsb+f_l=p}}\!\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{e_1,\dotsc,e_l\geq 0}{ g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_n\in\ZZ}}\!\!\lambda_{e_{1},f_{1},\dotsc,e_{l},f_{l},g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}}s_{1}^{e_{1}}s_{1}^{\ast f_{1}}\dotsm s_{l}^{e_{l}}s_{l}^{\ast f_{l}}s_{l+1}^{g_{l+1}}\dotsm s_{n}^{g_{n}}.

We now let
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(x
) act on
\epsilon_{0,\dotsc,0}^{\!\!\!\!\phantom{k_{i}}}
. This is annihilated by
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{1}^{\ast}),\dotsc,\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{l}^{\ast})
, so that

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{e_1,\dotsc,e_j\geq 0}{ g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_n\in\ZZ}}\lambda_{e_{1},0,\dotsc,e_{l},0,g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}}\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{1}^{e_{1}}\dotsm s_{l}^{e_{l}}s_{l+1}^{g_{l+1}}\dotsm s_{n}^{g_{n}})\epsilon_{0,\dotsc,0}^{\!\!\!\!\phantom{k_{i}}}=0.

Since

(2.1)

\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{1}^{e_{1}}\dotsm s_{l}^{e_{l}}s_{l+1}^{g_{l+1}}\dotsm s_{n}^{g_{n}})\epsilon_{0,\dotsc,0}^{\!\!\!\!\phantom{k_{i}}}=\epsilon_{e_{1},\dotsc,e_{l},g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}},

and all

.
This proves the statement for

Assuming the statement for

, we have

x=\sum_{p\geq p_{0}+1}\!\!\!\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{f_1,\dotsc,f_l\geq 0}{f_1+\dotsb+f_l=p}}\!\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{e_1,\dotsc,e_l\geq 0}{ g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_n\in\ZZ}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\lambda_{e_{1},f_{1},\dotsc,e_{l},f_{l},g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}}s_{1}^{e_{1}}s_{1}^{\ast f_{1}}\dotsm s_{l}^{e_{l}}s_{l}^{\ast f_{l}}s_{l+1}^{g_{l+1}}\dotsm s_{n}^{g_{n}}.

Fix

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\tilde{f}_{i}=p_{0}+1
, and consider the action of
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(x)
on
\epsilon_{\tilde{f}_{1},\dotsc,\tilde{f}_{l},0,\dotsc,0}
.
If
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{l}\geq 0
are such that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}f_{i}>p_{0}+1
, then there exists
i_{0}
with

\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{i_{0}}^{\ast f_{i_{0}}})

\epsilon_{\tilde{f}_{1},\dotsm\tilde{f}_{l},0,\dotsc,0}

If

Lemma 2.5
.

(1)

\displaystyle=[yx\otimes h]

For

2.14

Remark 2.19
.

In view of
equation

, the injectivity of the standard representation has the following two special cases:

(1)

The universal
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
​-algebra that is generated by a unitary is the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra of

that is generated by the bilateral shift on

is the universal

​-algebra that is generated by a unitary, but in the light of the general picture in

(2)

-subalgebra of

that is generated by the unilateral shift, i.e., the Toeplitz algebra. The proofs in the literature of this well-known result (due to Coburn; see
[
4
]
) that we are aware of all use the Wold decomposition for one isometry. Although it was the simultaneous Wold decomposition that led to the definition of the standard representation in

1.6

used in the present paper.

3.
Embeddings between universal algebras

In the previous sections, it has become clear that the standard representation is (most) easily described using the words
s_{1}^{e_{1}}\dotsm s_{l}^{e_{l}}s_{l+1}^{g_{l+1}}\dotsm s_{n}^{g_{n}}
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{0}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
. The action of a generator
s_{i}
in this representation is found by ‘moving
s_{i}
to its proper spot’ in all products
s_{i}\cdot s_{1}^{e_{1}}\dotsm s_{l}^{e_{l}}s_{l+1}^{g_{l+1}}\dotsm s_{n}^{g_{n}}
; see
Remark

1.4
. For each enumeration
e_{1},\dotsc,e_{l},g_{l+1},\dotsc,g_{n}
one can also choose and fix
any
monomial containing precisely that many factors of the
s_{i}
, and then (attempt to) define a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
in an analogous manner. We shall now show that this
does
yield a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
, and that this representation is always unitarily equivalent to the standard representation. This fact will then help us to show that the natural unital
\ast
-homomorphisms between various algebras
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
are, in, fact, injective; see
Theorem

4.
Rieffel deformation

In this section, we show that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
is isomorphic to a Rieffel deformation of

is nuclear, which is a particular case of
[

[
1
]

[
1
]

We start with a brief review.
Let
A
be a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
​-algebra, supplied with a strongly continuous action
\alpha
of
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}
, and denote by
A^{\infty}
the set of

such that

is a
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}
​-function. It is a dense
\ast
-subalgebra of
A
which is invariant under
\alpha
. Let
\Theta
be a real skew-symmetric
n\times n
matrix. In Rieffel’s deformation theory (see
[
11
]
), using oscillatory integrals, one introduces a product
(a,b)\mapsto a\cdot_{\Theta}b
on
A^{\infty}

such that, with the original involution,
A^{\infty}
is a
\ast
-algebra. The
\ast
-algebra
(A^{\infty},\cdot_{\Theta})
admits a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-completion
A_{\Theta}
in a
{\mathrm{C}}^{\ast}
-norm
{\lVert\,\cdot\,\rVert}_{\Theta}
, defined by Hilbert module techniques, such that the action on
A^{\infty}
extends to a strongly continuous action of
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}
on the
{\mathrm{C}}^{\ast}
-algebra
A_{\Theta}
with

.

In our case, we are interested in a periodic action of
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}
, i.e., an action
\alpha
of
{\mdmathbb T}^{n}
. We identify
\widehat{{\mdmathbb T}}^{n}
and

(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n})\in{\mdmathbb T}^{n}
. We let
e_{1},\dotsc,e_{n}
denote the canonical generators of
{\mdmathbb Z}^{n}
. For

, set

Clearly,
A_{p}\subset A^{\infty}
. As

, we have

for

for

for

are the homogeneous components of a

{\mdmathbb Z}^{n}
-grading on the
\ast
-subalgebra of

that they span. The general theory of representations of compact groups on Banach spaces implies that

(4.1)

A=\overline{\bigoplus_{p\in{\mdmathbb Z}^{n}}A_{p}}.

From
[

denotes the usual inner product on

Let

be a

-algebra with a

-action. For

We can conclude from this that

Furthermore, combining

3

Proposition 4.2

Let

-algebra with a

\alpha

, implying that
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}(\alpha_{t}(a))=\rho_{t}\pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime}(a)\rho_{t}^{\ast}
for
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\{1\};l,n-l}
and
t\in{\mdmathbb T}^{n}
.
Hence
Proposition

4.2
yields a representation
(\pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime})_{\Theta}
of
(\mathcal{A}_{\{1\};l,n-l})_{\Theta}
on
H_{l,n-l}
.

As a second preparation, we define a unitary operator
T\colon H_{l,n-l}\to H_{l,n-l}
, as follows. For
i,j=1,\dotsc,n
, set

w_{i,j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{ when }i=j;\\
e^{\pi{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{ij}}&\text{ when }i<j;\\
e^{-\pi{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{ji}}&\text{ when }i>j.\end{cases}

Then
w_{i,j}=\overline{w}_{j,i}
for
i,j=1,\dotsc,n
and
w_{i,j}^{2}=z_{i,j}
when
i\neq j
. The diagonal unitary operator
T
on
H_{l,n-l}
is then defined by setting

T{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{n}}\coloneqq\prod_{p>q}w_{p,q}^{k_{p}k_{q}}{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{n}}.

The sought relation between the injective representation
\pi_{\mathrm{s}}
and the
\ast
-homomorphism
\Psi
is as follows.

Proposition 4.5
.

For
a\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
, we have

(4.3)

\left[(\pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime})_{\Theta}\circ\Psi\right](a)=T^{\ast}\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(a)T.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove
equation

4.3
when
a
is one of the generators
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
.

Note that
{\mdmathbb C}\cdot{\epsilon}_{k_{1}\dotsc k_{n}}
is the
(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{n})
-homogeneous component of
H_{l,n-l}
for the representation
\rho
of
{\mdmathbb T}^{n}
on this space. Using
Proposition

4.2
, we then have that

\begin{split}\big[(\pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime})_{\Theta}\circ\Psi\big](s_{i})&{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{n}}=\left[(\pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime})_{\Theta}(s_{i}^{\prime})\right]{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{n}}\\
&=e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\langle\Theta(e_{i})\,,\,(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{n})\rangle}{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1,k_{i+1},\dotsc,k_{n}}\\
&=e^{\pi{\mathrm{i}}(-k_{1}\varphi_{1i}-\,\dotsb\,-k_{i-1}\varphi_{i-1,i}+k_{i+1}\varphi_{i,i+1}+\,\dotsb\,+k_{n}\varphi_{in})}{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1,k_{i+1},\dotsc,k_{n}}\\
&=w_{i,1}^{k_{1}}\dotsb w_{i,n}^{k_{n}}{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1,k_{i+1},\dotsc,k_{n}}.\end{split}

Then

\begin{split}T&\big[\big[(\pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime})_{\Theta}\circ\Psi\big](s_{i}){\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{n}}\big]=w_{i,1}^{k_{1}}\dotsb w_{i,n}^{k_{n}}T{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1,k_{i+1}\dotsc,k_{n}}\\
&=w_{i,1}^{k_{1}}\dotsb w_{i,n}^{k_{n}}\prod_{{\scriptsize\begin{matrix}p>q\\
p,q\not=i\end{matrix}}}w_{p,q}^{k_{p}k_{q}}\prod_{q<i}w_{i,q}^{(k_{i}+1)k_{q}}\prod_{p>i}w_{p,i}^{k_{p}(k_{i}+1)}{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1,k_{i+1},\dotsc,k_{n}}\\
&=z_{i,1}^{k_{1}}\dotsb z_{i,i-1}^{k_{i-1}}\prod_{p>q}w_{p,q}^{k_{p}k_{q}}{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1,k_{i+1},\dotsc,k_{n}}\\
&=\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{i})(T{\epsilon}_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{n}}).\end{split}

Here the third equality follows by bringing the first part of

w_{i,1}^{k_{1}}\dotsb w_{i,n}^{k_{n}}=w_{i,1}^{k_{1}}\dotsb w_{i,i-1}^{k_{i-1}}\,\cdot\,w_{i,i+1}^{k_{i+1}}\dotsb w_{i,n}^{k_{n}}

into the second product, the second part of it into the third product, and then using that

\displaystyle w_{i,q}^{k_{q}}w_{i,q}^{(k_{i}+1)k_{q}}

\displaystyle=w_{i,q}^{2k_{q}+k_{i}k_{q}}=z_{i,q}^{k_{q}}w_{i,q}^{k_{i}k_{r}}

for
q<i
, and that

\displaystyle w_{i,p}^{k_{p}}w_{p,i}^{k_{p}(k_{i}+1)}

\displaystyle=w_{p,i}^{-k_{p}}w_{p,i}^{k_{p}(k_{i}+1)}=w_{p,i}^{k_{p}k_{i}}

for
p>i
.

We conclude that

T\,\big[(\pi_{\mathrm{s}}^{\prime})_{\Theta}\circ\Psi\big](s_{i})=\pi_{\mathrm{s}}(s_{i})\,T.

As
T
is unitary, this implies the validity of
equation

4.3
for the generators
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\{z_{ij}\};l,n-l}
, as desired.

∎

Since

Theorem 4.8
.

The K-groups of

\bullet

Proof.

[

]

\mathrm{C}^{\ast}

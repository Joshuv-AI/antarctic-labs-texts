Finding b b -colorings Using Feedback Edges
Jakub Balabán

Abstract
A b b -coloring of a graph is a proper vertex coloring such that each color class contains a vertex that sees all other colors in its neighborhood. The b b - coloring problem, in which the task is to decide whether a graph admits a b b -coloring with k k colors, is NP -complete in general but polytime solvable on trees. Moreover, it is known that b b - coloring is in XP but W[ t t ] -hard for all t ∈ ℕ t\in\mathbb{N} when parameterized by tree-width. In fact, only very few parameters, such as the vertex cover number, were known to admit an FPT algorithm for b b - coloring . In this paper, we consider a more restrictive parameter measuring similarity to trees than tree-width, namely the feedback edge number, and show that b b - coloring is fixed-parameter tractable under this parameterization. Our algorithm combines standard techniques used in parameterized algorithmics with the problem-specific ideas used in the polytime algorithm for trees. In addition, we present an FPT algorithm for b b - coloring parameterized by distance to co-cluster, which is a parameter measuring similarity to complete multipartite graphs. Finally, we make several observations based on known results, including that b b - coloring is W[ 1 1 ] -hard when parameterized by tree-depth.

Introduction

A
proper coloring
of a graph is an assignment of colors to its vertices such that no two adjacent vertices receive the same color.
The
Chromatic Number
problem, in which the task is to decide whether a graph admits a proper coloring with
k
colors, is one of the famous 21
NP
-complete problems listed by Karp
[
27
]
.
This problem is
NP
-complete even when
k
is any fixed constant higher than 2.
Consequently, various heuristics must be employed in practice.
One simple heuristic works as follows.
We start with an arbitrary proper coloring and, in each iteration, we try to decrease the number of colors by one.
We search for a color
c
such that for each vertex
u
colored with
c
, there is a color
c_{u}\neq c
that is not present in the neighborhood of
u
.
If we can find such a color
c
, then we assign the color
c_{u}
to each vertex
u
currently colored with
c
, and the obtained coloring remains proper.
Otherwise, the heuristic terminates.

Irving and Manlove
[
21
]
initiated the theoretical study of this heuristic in 1999 and defined a proper coloring to be a
b
-coloring
if each color class contains a vertex, a so-called
b
-vertex
, that sees all other colors in its neighborhood.
It is easy to see that
b
-colorings are exactly those colorings that cannot be improved by the heuristic.
In addition, they defined the
b
-chromatic number
of a graph

such that

admits a

-coloring with

Informally, our algorithm works as follows.
Let
G
be the input graph.
First, we find a small set
S
of vertices such that
G-S
is a complete multipartite graph (such a set exists by definition of the parameter), and we guess a proper coloring
\chi
of
S
.
Second, we partition the maximal independent sets of
G-S
into “types” based on their connection to
S
.
Crucially, the number of types will be small.
Third, for each color
c
used by
\chi
, we guess whether some vertex of
G-S
should be colored with
c
, and if yes, we guess the type of the independent set that should contain such a vertex.
The key technical lemma states that two sets with the same type are interchangeable, i.e., if a color
c
should have a vertex in a set of type
t
, then it does not matter which one of such sets we choose.
Finally, we must also handle the colors
not
used by
\chi
: there may be many such colors but their treatment is relatively straightforward.

In Section
4
, we show an
FPT
algorithm for
b
-
coloring
parameterized by the feedback edge number, which is the smallest number of edges that need to be deleted to obtain an acyclic graph; this parameter has also been used in several
FPT

[
2
,
36
,
3
,
18

.
This algorithm is the main contribution of this paper.
We combine standard techniques used in parameterized algorithms with the concept of a

, which was introduced in the polytime algorithm for
b
-Chromatic Number
on trees
[
21
]
.
However, our definition of a pivot will be more technical because it will be “parameterized” by a partial coloring of the input graph.
In order to smoothly introduce pivots to the reader, we now briefly describe the original algorithm.

Algorithm for trees.

Let
T
be a tree and let
k
be the
m
-degree of
T
; recall that
k
is an upper bound on the
b
-chromatic number of
T
, which we denote by
\chi_{b}(T)
.
Informally, this upper bound can be achieved “almost always”: the only exception are so-called
pivoted
trees, which can be recognized in linear time
[
21
]
.
More precisely,
\chi_{b}(T)=k-1
if
T
is pivoted, and
\chi_{b}(T)=k
otherwise.
Let us call vertices of degree at least
k-1

candidates
; notice that candidates are those vertices that may serve as
b
-vertices in a
k
-coloring of
T
.
We say that
T
is
pivoted
if there are exactly
k
candidates and there is a vertex

, a so-called
pivot
, such that:

1.

u
is not a candidate;

2.

each candidate is adjacent to
u
or to another candidate that is adjacent to
u
;

3.

each candidate that is adjacent to
u
and to another candidate has degree
k-1
.

It is not hard to see that a pivoted tree does not admit a
b
-coloring with
k
colors: each candidate would have to be a
b
-vertex and a vertex of degree
k-1
can be a
b
-vertex only if it sees no color twice, which means that no color may be assigned to the pivot
u
.

We have already mentioned that several polytime algorithms for
b
-Chromatic Number
on tree-like graphs have been developed
[
6
,
10
,
9
]
.
These algorithms build on the concept of a pivoted tree.
For example, the concept of a
pivoted cactus
has been introduced
[
10
]
.

Let

The next step is to choose

[b+1,k]
.
What needs to be carefully handled are the colors in
[b+1,p]
because we need to ensure that

will be proper and that vertices in

For example, if a vertex

We begin by guessing a so-called

[b+1,p]

Crucially, the number of color plans will be small.
The next step is to compute a so-called

[b+1,k]
to

We show that using a “valid” color plan, we can compute a color realization such that each chosen candidate can indeed become a

More precisely, each vertex in

will be

\chi_{\rho}=\chi\cup\rho^{-1}
is the coloring obtained by “extending”

Now it may occur that the coloring
\chi_{\rho}
we have constructed has a “pivot”, i.e., a vertex that cannot be assigned any color, see the above described algorithm for

-Chromatic Number
on trees for an idea what a pivot is.
Sometimes, the realization

However, this is not straightforward because we must ensure that each vertex in

is a

-candidate, where

is the new realization.
On the other hand, sometimes the pivot is “unavoidable”, and we must try a different pair
(\chi,B)

In fact, there are more properties we need

to have but achieving “pivot-freeness” will be the most challenging.

Once we have a “feasible” color realization

\chi_{\rho}
into a

-coloring of
G
.
First, we color the neighborhoods of all chosen candidates.
Roughly speaking, we proceed in a BFS-order from
S
, but there may be an “exceptional” candidate that needs to be handled first.
Finally, we color the remainder of the graph.
This last step is also non-trivial because there may be a vertex which already sees all colors.
However, one property we require from
\rho
will ensure that this cannot happen.

The

For

If

For

Instead of

A

Given a graph class

A

is a disjoint union of cliques and a

(

Given an

An edge set

A

in

in

Lemma 2
.

If
G
is a connected graph with feedback edge number

that contains no dangling trees,
then there is a set

such that
|S|\leq 4p
, and if
\mathcal{P}
is the set of connected components of

, then
|\mathcal{P}|\leq 4p
and each

is a dangling path in

.
Moreover,

Proof.

Let
F\subseteq E(G)
be a minimum feedback edge set in
G
,
let
S_{0}=\{u\in V(G)\;|\;u
is incident to an edge of

in

S_{1}=\{u\in V(G)\;|\;u
has degree higher than
2

, and

.
Since

can be found in linear time using the DFS algorithm,

can be efficiently computed as well.
It is easy to see that
|S_{0}|\leq 2p
. Let

; clearly,

is a tree. Observe that all leaves of

belong to

(a leaf not in

would be single-vertex dangling tree), which means that

has at most

has degree higher than

.

Next, observe that each vertex

has degree exactly

in

is acyclic, each

Finally, we prove that

to be the root of

, let

that are adjacent to a vertex of

Before we continue, we prove a simple lemma concerning two

Lemma 17

Let

Definition 18

Let

Now let

3.4
Finishing the proof

Using Lemma

Proof of Theorem
8
.

We need to design an algorithm that given an integer

and a graph
G
with distance to co-cluster
p_{0}
decides whether there is a
k
-
b
-coloring of
G
. Let
p=\min(p_{0},k)
.
First, we apply Theorem
1
to the complement of

to compute a minimum co-cluster-modulator

of
G
. Second, we try all signatures
\sigma
(recall that the definition of a signature depends on

, see Definition
10
), and we find a minimal
\sigma
-coloring
\chi
of
G
using Lemma
13
.
If
\chi
is not proper, we discard it and continue with the next signature. Otherwise, we try all
\chi
-candidate subsets

, and we compute for each set

, whether it is
\chi
-flexible or a
(\chi,B)
-candidate.
If there is a set

that is neither
\chi
-flexible nor a
(\chi,B)
-candidate, we reject
B
. Otherwise, we compute the sets
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{U\in\mathcal{U}\;|\;U
is a
(\chi,B)
-candidate but not

-flexible
\}
and
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\{U\in\mathcal{U}\;|\;U
is a
\chi
-flexible

-candidate
\}
.
If
|\mathcal{C}_{0}|>k-p
or
|\mathcal{C}_{0}|+|\mathcal{C}_{1}|<k-p
, we reject

. Otherwise, we can find a set
\mathcal{C}
such that
|\mathcal{C}|=k-p
and
\mathcal{C}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{0}\cup\mathcal{C}_{1}
, which allows us to compute a
b
-coloring of
G
by Lemma
19
. If we do not find a
b
-coloring for any

and

, we reject
(G,k)
.

Let us prove that this algorithm is correct.
By Lemma
19
, if we output a coloring, then it is a
k
-
b
-coloring of
G
.
In the other direction, suppose that
(G,k)
is a YES instance. If
\psi
is a
k
-

-coloring of
G
, then by Lemma

, there is a signature

such that
\psi
is a
\sigma
-coloring. By Lemma

, any minimal
\sigma
-coloring

can be extended into a

-coloring

of

.
By Lemma

, the existence of

implies the existence of

and

with the required properties.
Moreover, the existence of

implies that all sets

are
\chi
-flexible or

-candidates, and

implies

and

.
Therefore, the algorithm finds

and
\mathcal{C}
, and based on them, it finds a

-coloring of

.

Finally, observe that the desired running time is achieved by definition of

, Theorem

, Observation

, Lemma

, and Lemma

.
In particular, it is safe to try each
\chi
-candidate subset

because the number of vertices colored by a minimal

-coloring is

by Lemma

.
∎

4
Feedback Edge Number

In this section, we prove the following theorem. For a high-level overview of its proof, see the end of Section
1
.

Theorem 20
.

The
b
-Coloring
problem can be solved in time
2^{\mathcal{O}(p^{2})}\cdot n^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
, where
p
is the feedback edge number of the input
n
-vertex graph.

In Section
4.1
, we define key concepts, such as the fen-core
S
and the
S
-profile
(\chi,B)
, and we prove a few simple lemmas.
In Section
4.2
, we define color plans and color realizations, and describe their properties.
A very technical part of the proof is making the color realization pivot-free; this is done in Section
4.3
.

The purpose of Sections
4.2
and
4.3
is to find a feasible color realization
\rho
(it exists if and only if the desired
b
-coloring exists).
This realization
\rho
chooses vertices that will become
b
-vertices.
In Section
4.4
, we color the neighbors of the vertices chosen by
\rho
, and obtain a partial
b
-coloring.
Finally, in Section
4.5
, we put everything together, and prove Theorem
20
.

4.1
Initial setup

Let us fix an instance
(G,k)
of the
b
-Coloring
problem, and let

be the feedback edge number of

.
We will assume that

since otherwise we could simply use Proposition

We begin by defining a small vertex set

Definition 21

Let

Lemma 22
.

A fen-core exists and it can be found in polynomial time.

Proof.

Let

be the set of vertices contained in dangling trees in

, and

be the set of connected components of

Since

Since each path

∎

Let us fix a fen-core

, and let

.
From now on, we will view

.
Note that

.

Since

.

Observation 23
.

If

.

Let

.
If

Observation 24
.

For each vertex

4.1.1
General definition of pivots

As discussed in Section
1
, a key role in our algorithm will be played by vertices called
pivots
.
Later, the definition of a pivot will depend on some partial coloring of
G
.
However, it will be useful to first define pivots generally, without referring to any coloring.

Definition 25
.

Let
D\subseteq V(G)
be a set. We say that a vertex
u\in\overline{S\cup D}
is a
D
-pivot
if for each
v\in D
, either
uv\in E(G)
or there is a vertex
w\in N(u)\cap N(v)\cap D
.
The vertices in
N(u)\cap N(D)\cap D
will be called
(u,D)
-links
.
A vertex
v\in D
is called
S
-influenced
if
N[v]\cap N[S]\nsubseteq\{u\}
, i.e., there is a
v
-
S
path of length at most 2 not containing

.

We will sometimes encounter a situation in which a vertex of
D
has a neighbor in
S\setminus D
or in
N(S)\setminus(D\cup\{u\})
; this motivates our definition of being
S
-influenced.
Observe that the definition depends on
u
; when we say that a vertex is
S
-influenced, the pivot
u
will always be clear from context.

The following lemma describes how a “pivoted” set
D
can interact with
S
, and its proof follows easily from Definitions
21
and
25
.

Lemma 26
.

Let
D\subseteq V(G)
be a set, let
u\in\overline{S\cup D}
be a
D
-pivot, and let
D^{+}=D\cup\{u\}
.

[(a)]

1.

If
C
is a connected component of
G[D]
, then there is a vertex
v_{C}\in C
such that
N(u)\cap C=\{v_{C}\}
and
C\subseteq N[v_{C}]
.

2.

If
v\in D\setminus N(u)
and
w\in D\cap N(v)
, then either
w\in N(u)
or
v,w\in S
.

3.

There is a connected component
C
of
G[D]
such that all
S
-influenced vertices are in
C
.

4.

If
v_{1},v_{2}\in D\cap S
are distinct vertices, then there is a
(u,D)
-link
w\in S\cap N[v_{1}]\cap N[v_{2}]
.

5.

Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in D
be distinct vertices and let

be such that
\{v_{1},v_{2},w\}\nsubseteq S
. If
w\in\overline{D}
or
v_{1}\in N(u)
, then
w=u
.

6.

If
v_{1}\in D\setminus S
is such that
N(v_{1})\cap\overline{D^{+}}\cap N[S]\neq\emptyset
and
v_{2}\in D\setminus\{v_{1}\}
, then

. In particular,

, all
S
-influenced vertices are in

, and if
v_{1}\notin N(S)
, then
v_{1}
is the only

-influenced vertex.

Proof.

For (
1
), let
C
be a component of
G[D]
.
By Definition
25
, for each
v\in C
, there is a vertex
w_{v}
such that
w_{v}\in N(u)\cap N[v]\cap D
.
Observe that if
v\neq w_{v}
, then

; otherwise

would be a cycle containing

, contradicting Observation
23
.
Suppose that there are vertices

such that
w_{v}\neq w_{x}
; we may choose

and

so that

.
Now

is a cycle, which again contradicts Observation
23
.
Hence, we may define

to be

for any

.

For (
2
), let
v,w\in D
be two adjacent vertices, and suppose that

.
By (
1
), there is a vertex
x\in N(u)\cap N(v)\cap N(w)
.
Since

is a cycle, we obtain

by Observation

, which concludes the proof.

For (
3
), suppose for contradiction that there are two
S
-influenced vertices

in different components of

.
For
i\in[2]
, if
v_{i}\in N[S]
, then we set
w_{i}:=v_{i}
, and otherwise we define

to be a vertex in

such that

; note that

exists by definition of being

-influenced.
Let

be a
v_{1}
-
v_{2}

.
Since

and

are in different components of

, we have

.
Let

be the path obtained from

by adding the edge

w_{1}\notin V(P)
and the edge
v_{2}w_{2}
if
w_{2}\notin V(P)
.
Now observe that a subpath of

containing

is an outer path of length at most 6, which contradicts Definition

.

For (
4
), let
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
be as in the statement.
By (
3
), there is a connected component
C
of
G[D]
such that
v_{1},v_{2}\in C

By (

), there is a vertex

such that

.
Without loss of generality,

is a

-link.
If

For (

), let

If

, then

, and

.
By (

),

Suppose that

and let

be the

For (

Let

4.1.2
S
-profiles

Now we present a key definition.
Informally, a vertex is a
\chi
-candidate if it may become a
b
-vertex in a coloring of
G
that extends a partial coloring
\chi
.
We also define an
S
-profile
, which consists of a coloring of
S
and a set
B\subseteq S
of vertices that should become
b
-vertices.

Definition 27
.

Let
U\subseteq V(G)
and let
\chi
be a proper coloring of
G[U]
.
The
\chi
-redundancy
of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is the integer
\textsf{red}_{\chi}(v)=|N[v]\setminus U|+|\chi(N[v])|-k
.
If
\textsf{red}_{\chi}(u)\geq 0
, we say that
u
is a
\chi
-candidate.

If
\textsf{red}_{\chi}(u)=0
, we say that
u
is
\chi
-tight.

Let
\chi\colon S\rightarrow[p]
be a proper coloring of
G[S]
, and let
B\subseteq S
be a set such that each vertex in
B
is a
\chi
-candidate and
\chi(B)=[b]
, where
b=|B|
.
We say that
(\chi,B)
is an
S
-profile
.
We call a
k
-
b
-coloring
\psi
of
G

described
by
(\chi,B)
if

\chi=\psi\upharpoonright S
, all vertices in
B
are
b
-vertices in
\psi
, and no color in
[b+1,p]
has a
b
-vertex in
S
.

Let us fix an
S
-profile
(\chi,B)
. Recall that the next step of the algorithm after computing
S
is trying all
S
-profiles, see Figure
2
.

Let
K=\{u\in\overline{S}\;|\;\textsf{red}_{\chi}(u)\geq 0\}
and
K^{+}=K\cup B
.
The reader should think of
K^{+}
as the set of our candidates for
b
-vertices.
However,
K^{+}
is
not
the set of all
\chi
-candidates because we do not care about vertices in
S\setminus B
.
Indeed, when such a vertex is a
b
-vertex for some color
c\in[p]
, then
c\leq b
and
c
has another
b
-vertex in
B
.
The following observation formalizes this idea.

Observation 28
.

If
\psi
is a
k
-
b
-coloring of
G
described by
(\chi,B)
, then each color
c\in[k]
has a
b
-vertex
u\in K^{+}
in
\psi
.
Moreover, if
|K^{+}|=k
, then all vertices in
K^{+}
are
b
-vertices in
\psi
.

Proof.

Let
c\in[k]
be a color and let
u\in V(G)
be a
b
-vertex for
c
in
\psi
. If
u\in S\setminus B
, then by Definition
27
,
c\in[b]
and there is another
b
-vertex for
c
in
\psi
that is in
B
. Hence, we may assume
u\notin S\setminus B
. By definition of a
b
-vertex, we know that
|\psi(N[u])|=k
. Since
|N[u]\setminus S|+|\chi(N[u])|\geq|\psi(N[u])|
, we deduce that the
\chi
-redundancy of
u
is non-negative, which implies that
u\in K^{+}
. The second part of the statement follows by the pigeonhole principle.
∎

Now we define a
link
, which is a vertex that cannot become a
b
-vertex if the two linked vertices have the same color.
We also specify Definition
25
and present a more concrete definition of a pivot.

Definition 29
.

Let
\psi
be a partial coloring of
G
such that
\chi\subseteq\psi
.
If
u,v\in V(G)
are distinct vertices, then we say that
u
and
v
are
\psi
-linked
if there is a
\psi
-tight vertex
w\in N(u)\cap N(v)\cap K^{+}
.
We also say that
u
and
v
are
\psi
-linked via
w

and that
w
is a
(\psi,u,v)
-link.

Let
D\subseteq V(G)
be a set.
We say that
u\in\overline{D\cup S}
is an
(\psi,D)
-
pivot
if
u
is a
D
-pivot and each
(u,D)
-link is a
(\psi,u,v)
-link for some
v\in D
.

Let us remark that there can be at most one
(\psi,u,v)
-link unless
u,v\in S
.
However, the definition will not be used for
u,v\in S
, so we may speak of
the

(\psi,u,v)
-link.

Now we observe that the
(\chi,u,v)
-link cannot be a
b
-vertex in a coloring described by
(\chi,B)
if
u
and
v
have the same color.

Observation 30
.

If
\psi
is a
k
-
b
-coloring of
G
described by
(\chi,B)
,
u\in V(G)
,
v\in\overline{S}
, and
w\in K^{+}
is the
(\chi,u,v)
-link and also a

-vertex in
\psi
, then
\psi(u)\neq\psi(v)
.

Proof.

Let
u,v,w
, and
\psi
be as in the statement.
Let

be a function such that for

, we have

, and otherwise

is a vertex of

by

.
Since

is a

,

is indeed a well-defined function.
By Definition

,

is

and that

is a bijection.
Suppose for contradiction that

If

).

4.2
Color plans and realizations

As in the previous section, let
S
be a fen-core,
(\chi,B)
be an
S
-profile,
K=\{u\in\overline{S}\;|\;\textsf{red}_{\chi}(u)\geq 0\}
, and
K^{+}=K\cup B
.
Recall that
\chi(S)\subseteq[p]
and
\chi(B)=[b]
.
In addition, we define
K^{*}=K\setminus N(S)
.
Let us also fix an extension
S^{+}
of
S
, see Definition
21
.

Now we define a
color plan
, which is a function deciding, for each color
c\in[b+1,p]
, whether the
b
-vertex for
c
should be in
N(S)
, and if yes, which vertex of
S
it should be adjacent to (it may be adjacent to at most one vertex of
S
by Definition
21
).
The
b
-vertices that should not be in
N(S)
must be in
K^{*}
, and the color plan assigns
*
to their colors.
Only some color plans may be used to produce a
b
-coloring: we call such plans
valid
.

Definition 31
.

A function
\pi\colon[b+1,p]\rightarrow S\sqcup\{*\}
is called a
color plan
of
(\chi,B)
(or simply a color plan when
(\chi,B)
is clear from context).
If
c\in[b+1,p]
is a color, then
\pi
is
c
-critical
if
\pi(c)=*
and each vertex in
K^{*}
is
\chi
-linked to a vertex in
\chi^{-1}(c)
.
We say that
\pi
is
critical
if it is
c
-critical for some
c\in[b+1,p]
.

We say that
\pi
is
valid
if it satisfies the following properties:

1.

|\pi^{-1}(*)|\leq|K^{*}|
;

2.

for each
u\in S
, we have
|\pi^{-1}(u)|\leq|N(u)\cap K|
;

3.

for each
u\in S
, if
\chi(u)\in[b+1,p]
, then
\pi(\chi(u))\neq u
;

4.

for each
u\in B
, we have
|\pi^{-1}(u)\cap\chi(N(u))|\leq\textsf{red}_{\chi}(u)
;

5.

if
\pi
is critical, then
|K^{+}|>k
and there is a vertex
u\in S
, called a
\pi
-anchor
, such that
|\pi^{-1}(u)|<|N(u)\cap K|
and there is a vertex in
K^{*}

\chi
-linked to
u
.

Let us present an intuition behind the five properties of a valid color plan.
First, Properties
1
and
2
ensure that there are enough vertices for the plan to be realized.
Second, a violation of Property
3
would mean that the obtained coloring cannot be proper.
Third, Property
4
ensures that each vertex in
B
can still become a
b
-vertex.
Finally, if
\pi
is
c
-critical, then the
b
-vertex for
c
must be linked to a vertex in
S
colored with
c
, which means that the link cannot be a
b
-vertex.
Property
5
ensures that there exists such a link that does not have to be a
b
-vertex, which will enable us to find a
b
-vertex for
c
.

Now we define a
color realization

\rho
, which is a function selecting concrete vertices as
b
-vertices for colors in
[b+1,k]
, based on some color plan.
We also define two properties of
\rho
:
damage-freeness
, which means that the chosen vertices do not interfere with each other, and a technical property called
safeness
, which will enable us to color the entire graph at the very last step of the algorithm, see Figure
2
.

Definition 32
.

An injective function
\rho\colon[b+1,k]\rightarrow K
is called a
color realization
.
Let
B_{\rho}:=B\cup\range(\rho)
,
S_{\rho}:=S\cup B_{\rho}
, and
\chi_{\rho}:=\chi\cup\rho^{-1}
; note that
\chi_{\rho}
is a
k
-coloring of
G[S_{\rho}]
.
We say that
\rho

realizes
a color plan
\pi
(or is a color realization of
\pi
) if for each
c\in[b+1,p]
:

[(a)]

1.

\pi(c)=*
if and only if
\rho(c)\in K^{*}
;

2.

for each
u\in S
, it holds that
\pi(c)=u
if and only if
\rho(c)\in N(u)
.

A vertex
u
is
damaged
in
\rho
if there is a color
c\in[b+1,p]
and a vertex
v\in\chi^{-1}(c)
such that
\rho(c)\in K^{*}
and
v
and
\rho(c)
are
\chi
-linked via
u
.
We say that
\rho
is
damage-free
if no vertex in
\range(\rho)
is damaged in
\rho
.

A vertex
u\in V(G)
is
\ell
-safe
in
\rho
if

The goal of this section is to show how a pivot-free color realization can be computed, see Definition

be an extension of

5
Conclusion

Even though the feedback edge number is a restrictive parameter, generalizing the polynomial-time algorithm for the
b
-Chromatic Number
problem on trees into an
FPT
algorithm for
b
-
coloring
under this parameterization turned out highly non-trivial.
The natural open question is whether our algorithm can be further generalized into an
FPT
algorithm parameterized by the feedback
vertex
number or whether this generalization is
W[1]
-hard, see Figure
1
.
Recall that our algorithm is based on certain locality: the complexity of the graph is contained in the fen-core
S
, and the rest of the graph is tree-like.
Such a locality does not hold when parameterized by the feedback vertex number because the feedback vertices (whose removal yields a forest) may have neighbors everywhere in the graph.
For this reason, we would not be surprised if this problem was
W[1]
-hard.

Another natural question arising from our results is whether
b
-
coloring
is
FPT
when parameterized by the distance to cluster (instead of the distance to co-cluster we have considered), see Figure
1
. Recall that bounded distance to cluster means that there is a small set whose removal yields a disjoint union of cliques.
Let us remark that a closely related and more restrictive parameter, namely the twin-cover number, was shown to be sufficient for an
FPT
algorithm
[
23
]
.
This parameter provides an additional condition, which guarantees that the vertices contained in the same clique have the same neighborhood in the cluster-modulator.
Is this additional condition necessary or is
b
-
coloring
parameterized by the distance to cluster in
FPT
?

Finally, another open problem asked by
[
23
]
is the complexity of
b
-
coloring
when parameterized by the modular width of the graph, which is another parameter generalizing the twin-cover number.

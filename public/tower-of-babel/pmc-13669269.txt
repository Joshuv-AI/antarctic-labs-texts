Aligning Out-of-Plane Polarization in 2D Ferroelectric Perovskite Wafer Boosts Interlayer Carrier Transport for Efficient X-Ray Detection
Authors: Chenhao Deng, Shihai You, Haiqing Zhong, Xiankan Zeng, Xiaolong Hao, Jiasheng Xie, Zheng Zhou, Zhangjian Jin, Huang Ye, Lijun Xu, Jianbo Wu, Panpan Dong, Wen Li, Xingxing Jiao, Weiqing Yang

ABSTRACT
Hightlights High-quality, centimeter-sized polycrystalline wafers of a 2D ferroelectric perovskite are fabricated via a facile hot-pressing method, circumventing the challenge of growing large, thick single crystals of 2D perovskites.The preferred horizontal orientation of grains, combined with electric poling, creates macroscopic out-of-plane polarization, generating a strong built-in electric field that boosts interlayer carrier transport.The poled wafer not only enables self-powered X-ray detection but also achieves a high sensitivity (2285 μC Gy−1 cm−2) and an ultralow detection limit (7.2 nGy s−1) under a small bias, along with exceptional operational stability and high-contrast X-ray imaging. Introduction

Metal halide perovskites (MHPs) have emerged as a star class of optoelectronic semiconductors for direct X-ray detection, owing to their strong X-ray attenuation, high carrier mobility-lifetime product (μτ), and facile solution-processed synthesis [1–7]. Significant progress has been achieved in the past decade. For instance, a high sensitivity of 2.16×105 μC Gy−1 cm−2 and an ultralow detection limit of 2 nGy s−1 have been reported in a 3D FAPbI3 (FA = formamidine) single-crystal X-ray detector [8], far surpassing those of commercial amorphous selenium (α-Se) film detectors (~25 μC Gy−1 cm−2 and 5500 nGy s−1) by several orders of magnitude. However, the practical deployment of 3D MHPs is hindered by intrinsic structural instability and severe ion migration, which compromises their long-term working stability [9–11]. In this context, 2D MHPs, naturally adopting a motif of alternating organic layers and inorganic slabs, offer enhanced environmental stability, suppressed ion migration, and reduced leakage currents, making them an ideal subclass for direct X-ray detection [12–16]. Recently, a series of 2D MHPs including Ruddlesden–Popper (RP) and Dion–Jacobson (DJ) phases, such as (F-PEA)2PbI4 (F-PEA = fluorophenethylammonium) and (BDA)PbI4 (BDA = 1,4-butanediamine), have been developed and indeed exhibited superior working stability in direct X-ray detectors [17–25]. However, the pronounced dielectric contrast between the organic spacer and the inorganic metal halide layers creates a multi-quantum-well electronic structure in 2D MHPs, thus leading to strong quantum and dielectric confinement effects. This inherent property severely limits the efficient transport of carriers especially the interlayer (out-of-plane) transport between inorganic layers [12, 26–28], resulting in generally inferior detection sensitivity than their 3D counterparts. Therefore, enhancing interlayer carrier transport in 2D MHPs is crucial for advancing their performance in practical X-ray detection applications.

In recent years, the intrinsic spontaneous polarization in ferroelectric or polar MHPs has attracted considerable interest in optoelectronics [29–31]. This polarization generates a built-in electric field that drives the spontaneous separation and transport of photogenerated electrons and holes, leading to a stable bulk photovoltaic effect (BPVE) under light irradiation [32–35]. Leveraging this effect has significantly improved the performance of various MHP-based optoelectronic devices, including solar cells and photodetectors [26, 36]. For instance, Wang et al. utilized the 2D ferroelectric MHP (DFPD)2PbI4 (DFPD = 4,4-difluoropiperidinium) as the absorbing layer in a film solar cell, where the polarization-induced built-in electric field enhanced out-of-plane carrier transport, breaking the limit of multi-quantum-well electronic structure and achieving a high efficiency of 3.71% among 2D RP MHPs [26]. Similarly, Luo’s group developed a series of polar MHPs whose spontaneous polarization enabled pronounced BPVE under X-ray irradiation, facilitating the creation of high-performance, self-powered single-crystal X-ray detectors [23, 30, 32, 37–41]. These works reveal that introducing polarization is a promising strategy to boost the performance of 2D MHP-based X-ray detectors.

However, two major challenges impede the application of this strategy in practical X-ray detection. First, in most reported 2D polar MHPs, the polarization axis lies parallel to the inorganic layers (e.g., in-plane polarization) [42, 43]. This does not enhance the critical interlayer (out-of-plane) carrier transport, rendering it incompatible with the standard vertical architecture of commercial X-ray detectors. Second, practical devices require X-ray detection materials with scalable area and sufficient thickness for effective X-ray attenuation and imaging. Unfortunately, the growth of large, thick single crystals of 2D MHPs remains challenging and time-consuming, primarily owing to the complex molecular configuration of large organic cations which hinders ordered crystal growth along the vertical direction [44–46]. In this context, fabricating large-area 2D MHPs that simultaneously possess out-of-plane polarization is of great significance for advancing high-performance X-ray detection.

In this work, we address these challenges through fabricating centimeter-sized polycrystalline wafers of the 2D ferroelectric MHP, (Mpda)PbBr4 (Mpda = N-methyl-1,3-propanediamine), via a facile hot-pressing method. This process induces a horizontal orientation of micrograins, enabling the vertical alignment of their intrinsic polarization axes and resulting in macroscopic out-of-plane polarization across the entire wafer. This unified polarization creates a robust built-in electric field that efficiently drives the separation and interlayer transport of photogenerated carriers, exhibiting a strong, switchable BPVE (~ 2 V) under X-ray irradiation. Constructing vertical X-ray detectors based on high-quality (Mpda)PbBr4 wafers achieves a high sensitivity of 2285 μC Gy−1 cm−2 and an ultralow detection limit of 7.2 nGy s−1 even under a small external bias. Moreover, this device also demonstrates excellent operational stability as well as high-resolution imaging capability. This study successfully integrates inherent out-of-plane polarization into scalable 2D MHP polycrystalline wafers, providing new insights into the design and fabrication of high-performance X-ray detectors.

Experimental Section

2.1 Materials

Starting materials, lead acetate trihydrate (Pb(CH3COO)2·3H2O, 99.5%, Aladdin), hydrobromic acid (HBr, 48 wt% in water, Aladdin), N-methyl-1,3-propanediamine (Mpda, 98%, Aladdin), are commercially available and used without further purification.

Preparation and Characterization

Synthesis of (Mpda)PbBr4 and Wafer Fabrication

(Mpda)PbBr4 microcrystals were synthesized by dissolving Pb(CH3COO)2·3H2O (1 mmol, 379 mg) and Mpda (1 mmol, 88 mg) into 5 mL HBr solution through heating (100 °C) and constant magnetic stirring. After a clear solution was obtained, stop heating and stirring, and the mixture solution was left on the hotplate to cool to room temperature. Finally, light-yellow microcrystals were precipitated.

The microcrystalline powders (e.g., 0.5 g) of (Mpda)PbBr4 were ground for 20 min to ensure uniformity and then were transferred into a stainless-steel hot-pressing mold. First, the mold was heated to the setting temperature (e.g., 170 °C) and preheated for 10 min to soften these powders. Uniaxial pressure was applied at a rate of 30 MPa min−1 to the setting pressure (e.g., 300 MPa) and held for 60 min under continuous heating. The pressure was manually adjusted during hot-pressing to maintain the fluctuation within 10 MPa and ensure stability. After cooling to room temperature, the pressure was released manually and a high-quality wafer was demolded.

Characterization

The X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were acquired on a powder X-ray diffractometer (Empyrean, Malvern Panalytical) with Cu Kα1 radiation (λ = 0.154 nm). Second harmonic generation (SHG) spectra were carried out on polycrystals using a Nd:YAG laser with the wavelength of 1064 nm, pulse duration of 5 ns, peak power of ~ 1.6 MW, and repetition rate of 10 Hz. The thermogravimetry (TG) curve was measured on a simultaneous thermal analyzer (STA 449 F3 Jupiter, Netzsch) in a N2 atmosphere from room temperature to 700 °C with a temperature increasing rate of 10 °C min−1. The absorption spectra were recorded on an ultraviolet–visible (UV–Vis) spectrometer (Lambda 950, PerkinElmer) from 200 to 800 nm. The ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) spectrum was performed on film using an ultraviolet photoelectron spectrometer (QXi, ECSALAB) with He I as the radiation source. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectrum was collected on an X-ray photoelectron spectrometer (K-Alpha, Thermo Scientific). Scanning electron microscope (SEM) images and corresponding energy-dispersive spectrometer (EDS) mappings were obtained by a field emission scanning electron microscope (JSM7800-F, JEOL) equipped with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscope (X-Max 80, Oxford). The atomic force microscope (AFM) image was measured using an atomic force microscope (Dimension ICON, Bruker). The piezoresponse force microscopy (PFM) measurement of the spin-coated (Mpda)PbBr4 film was carried out using a resonant-enhanced piezoresponse force microscope (MFP-3D, Asylum Research) equipped with conductive Pt/Ir-coated silicon tips (EFM-50, Nanoworld). The film was coated on an indium tin oxide (ITO) substrate. The electrical properties, e.g., current–voltage (I-V) curve, resistivity, temperature-dependent conductivity, were tested using a high-precision electrometer (6517B, Keithley).

Device Fabrication and X-Ray Detection

The vertical X-ray detectors were fabricated by coating silver conductive paste (SPI) on the top and bottom sides of diced (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer, and the electrode area is about 1 mm2 and the wafer thickness is 0.6 mm. A commercial X-ray tube with tungsten target (Magpro TUB155, Moxtek) operated at 50 kV tube voltage was used as the X-ray source, and aluminum plate was used as the attenuator. The X-ray dose rate was tuned by changing its tube current and measured using an X-ray dosimeter (Accu-Gold, Radcal) with ion chamber (10×6−180 model). The I-V, voltage–time (V-t), and current–time (I-t) curves were recorded on the high-precision electrometer (6517B, Keithley). The X-ray imaging was carried out by placing the object (a metal nut) on an electric X-Y displacement stage and collecting the photocurrent at each position. The scanning was performed over an area of 10 mm × 10 mm with a step size of 0.5 mm, yielding a pixel matrix of 20 × 20 (400 pixels in total). The X-ray dose rate and bias voltage for imaging were 60 µGy s−1 and 20 V, respectively. The acquired photocurrent values were then mapped into a matrix to reconstruct the X-ray image.

Computation

The density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed on the CASTEP module in Materials Studio. The PBE function in the framework of generalized gradient approximation (GGA) was used to deal with the exchange–correlation effects. An energy cutoff of 830 eV was used, and the k-space integration was carried out with a Monkhorst–Pack grid of 2×1×1. The interactions between the ionic cores and electrons were described using the norm-conserving pseudopotential. The orbital electrons of C (2s22p2), N (2s22p3), H (1s1), Pb (5d106s26p2), and Br (4s24p5) were regarded as valence electrons.

Results and Discussion

Crystal Structure and Semiconducting Property

(Mpda)PbBr4 is a 2D perovskite ferroelectric induced by the temperature-dependent order–disorder transition of Mpda cations and the lattice expansion of inorganic skeleton [43]. At room temperature, it crystallizes in a polar space group Pn (no. 7) and adopts a typical 2D DJ motif with alternating Mpda organic layers and [PbBr4]∞ inorganic slabs, as shown in Fig. 1a. This non-centrosymmetric structure is further corroborated by a pronounced SHG signal, with intensity comparable to the reference KH2PO4 (KDP), as plotted in Fig. 1b. The organic diamine cations are vertically anchored to two adjacent inorganic slabs through strong N-H···Br hydrogen bonds, which constitute 77.3% of all intermolecular forces as visualized by the Hirshfeld surface analysis (Fig. 1c, d) [47]. These strong interactions not only enable Mpda to be asymmetrically aligned in the interlayer space but also induce large distortion within the inorganic octahedral layer (Fig. S1), synergistically displacing the positive and negative charge centers and thus generating out-of-plane polarization along the c-axis in (Mpda)PbBr4. The spontaneous polarization strength is calculated to be 51.99 μC cm−2 based on the point charge model, as listed in Table S1. This strong out-of-plane polarization can establish a robust built-in electric field along this direction, suggesting its great potential for constructing vertical optoelectronic devices.

Light-yellow microcrystals of (Mpda)PbBr4 were grown from the hot HBr solution containing stoichiometric lead acetate trihydrate and Mpda via a slow cooling process. Their phase purity was confirmed by powder XRD patterns (Fig. S2). The TG curve of (Mpda)PbBr4 powders shows a high decomposition temperature of 598 K (Fig. S3), revealing its excellent thermal stability and great suitability for hot-pressing process. Figure 1e shows the UV–Vis absorption spectrum of (Mpda)PbBr4, in which a sharp cutoff at 434 nm is observed. According to the Tauc equation, its optical bandgap (Eg) can be determined to be 2.88 eV [48]. To have a deeper understanding of its electronic structure, DFT calculations were performed. As shown in Fig. 1f, (Mpda)PbBr4 exhibits a direct bandgap featuring the conduction band minimum (CBM) and valence band maximum (VBM) located at the same point of G. The calculated bandgap is 2.35 eV, slightly smaller than the experimental result, which can be ascribed to the inherent drawback of Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) exchange and correlation functional [49]. The partial density of states (DOS) profiles suggest that the VBM is primarily governed by Br 4p orbitals, while the CBM is mainly stemming from Pb 6p orbitals, indicating that the bandgap and electronic structure are chiefly determined by the lead bromide inorganic framework. The UPS spectrum was measured to determine the energy levels of (Mpda)PbBr4, as presented in Fig. 1g. From the secondary electron cutoff edge of this spectrum, its work function is 3.82 eV, and the valence band is located at 2.01 eV below the Fermi level (EF). Combined with the optical bandgap, the energy-level diagram displays that the CBM, VBM, and EF are located at −2.95, −5.83, and −3.82 eV (Fig. 1h). The EF value is 0.57 eV higher than middle position of its forbidden band (Ei = −4.39 eV), indicative of n-type electronic property of (Mpda)PbBr4. This energy-level structure can provide guidance for preparing (Mpda)PbBr4 semiconductor device and optimizing its device structure.

High-Quality Wafer Fabrication and Ferroelectricity

To circumvent the challenges and long period associated with growing large-sized single crystals of 2D MHPs, here we turn to fabricate high-quality polycrystalline wafers via a scalable hot-pressing method [44, 50]. Figure 2a schematically illustrates the process of wafer tableting. Well-ground (Mpda)PbBr4 microcrystal powders were used as the precursor to achieve high phase purity. Under simultaneous heating and uniaxial pressure, the powders undergo a dynamic process of rearrangement, pre-melting, fusion, and grain growth, finally forming densified, pinhole-free wafers. In this process, three parameters, e.g., temperature, pressure, and time, were systematically optimized (Fig. S4) to prevent decomposition and ensure high quality and preferred orientation texture, yielding an optimal combination of temperature at 170 °C, pressure at 300 MPa, and holding time for 60 min. As shown in Fig. 2b, a dark yellow wafer with a diameter of 10 mm and thickness of 0.6 mm was successfully fabricated, which exhibits a highly smooth and mirror-like surface, indicating that it is significantly densified with high quality. The micromorphology of (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer was studied using SEM, as shown in Fig. 2c. The surface image reveals a dense morphology constructed by well-defined large grains (1~2 μm) without visible voids, while the cross-sectional SEM image displays a distinct layered texture, suggesting a preferred orientation during hot-pressing [44]. Corresponding EDS mappings show that all elements such as Pb, Br, C, and N are uniformly distributed within this wafer (Fig. 2d). Its surface roughness was characterized by AFM, with a root-mean-square average (RMS) of only 7.85 nm, further verifying the high quality of (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer (Fig. 2e). XPS survey of (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer clearly shows the containing of Pb, Br, C, and N peaks (Fig. S5). High-resolution XPS spectra for Pb 4f and Br 3d are plotted in Fig. 2f, in which these detected peaks can be attributed to characteristic signals of Pb2+ and Br− species, respectively. XRD analysis of (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer shows the characteristic peaks consistent with the standard pattern (CCDC 2277171) [43], confirming its phase purity. Comparing to the XRD pattern of (Mpda)PbBr4 powders, it is intriguing that diffraction peaks of (00l) planes are significantly intensified in wafer, revealing that an obvious horizontal orientation occurred in the rearrangement and growth of micrograins during hot-pressing tableting. As is well known, the 2D MHP cell occupies a minimum volume when all planes are parallel to the horizontal level. Consequently, this phenomenon can be ascribed to more (00l) planes becoming parallel to the substrate, thereby minimizing the occupied volume upon external pressure [44–46]. Such a strongly preferred (00l) orientation indicates that the intrinsic, out-of-plane polar c-axes of the vast majority of (Mpda)PbBr4 micrograins are aligned along the vertical direction, thereby providing a critical structural basis for acquiring a net macroscopic polarization in the polycrystalline wafer. The trap-state density of (Mpda)PbBr4 polycrystalline wafer was assessed using the space-charge-limited-current (SCLC) test on an Ag/(Mpda)PbBr4/Ag vertical device. As plotted in Fig. 2h, the logarithmic I-V curve exhibits distinct Ohmic (n = 1) and trap-filling limit (TFL, n > 3) regions, yielding a TFL threshold voltage (VTFL) of 8.10 V. To investigate the ferroelectricity of (Mpda)PbBr4 in a polycrystalline form, the PFM measurement was performed on the spin-coated film at room temperature [55, 56]. The surface topography of (Mpda)PbBr4 film is shown in Fig. S7, and corresponding vertical (out-of-plane) PFM phase and amplitude images are demonstrated in Fig. 2i, j. Clearly, the observed phase signals with yellow and purple colors have a contrast of about 180°, which correspond to different domains with upward and downward polarization directions in the film [57]. Simultaneously, the PFM amplitude image indicates that these domains have different piezoresponse. Further, the local PFM spectroscopic measurements were carried out to confirm that the polarization is switchable. As shown in Fig. 2k, the typical phase–voltage hysteresis loop and the butterfly-shaped amplitude–voltage curve suggest that the ferroelectric polarization is electrically switchable, confirming the ferroelectric nature of (Mpda)PbBr4 in a polycrystalline state.

Subsequently, the electrical properties of (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer along the out-of-plane and in-plane directions were systematically evaluated. It should be noted that the measured in-plane and out-of-plane electric properties here refer to the lateral and vertical directions of the (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer, respectively. Owing to the preferred grain orientation, these properties are dominated by the intrinsic transport along the crystallographic ab-plane and c-axis. Therefore, to connect the macroscopic properties of (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer with its intrinsic crystal structure, we also used the terms “in-plane” and “out-of-plane” for the wafer device. Figure S8 schematically illustrates the corresponding device configurations. Figure 3a presents the bulk resistivity (ρ) of (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer along the in-plane and out-of-plane directions, respectively. Obviously, a higher ρ value (1.3×109 Ω cm) is obtained in the out-of-plane direction, further supporting the preferred horizontal orientation of micrograins. Macroscopic Polarization and Built-In Electric Field

To further study the influence of macroscopic out-of-plane polarization on the photoresponse, a vertical device of Ag/(Mpda)PbBr4 wafer/Ag was fabricated by depositing Ag paste on its top and bottom sides (Figs. 4a and S10). Prior to measurement, the wafer was subjected to a poling process (±200 V for 5 min) to align the polarized domains. After positive poling, a large open-circuit voltage (Voc) value of −2 V and a short-circuit current density (Jsc) value of 33.01 nA cm−2 were observed in the current density–voltage (J-V) curve of (Mpda)PbBr4 device under X-ray irradiation (60.10 μGy s−1), as shown in Fig. 4b. Reversing the poling electric field (negative poling) switches the sign of both Voc and Jsc accordingly (2 V and −25.17 nA cm−2), demonstrating a typical and electrically switchable BPVE characteristic [43, 45, 57]. The stability of this bulk photovoltage is shown in the V-t curves under alternating poling states (Fig. 4c). This phenomenon can be ascribed to the alignment of initially randomized polarization directions within individual micrograins with the assistance of external electric field, as shown in Fig. 4d. As the wafer is fabricated, even though the c-axes (intrinsic polar axis) of most micrograins are aligned along the vertical direction, their spontaneous polarization directions can be upward or downward, thus resulting in negligible net macroscopic polarization. After positive or negative poling, the polarization directions of most textured, individual grains are oriented consistently along the external electric field, hence establishing a significant net out-of-plane macroscopic polarization across the wafer. This aligned polarization further creates a strong built-in electric field that can efficiently separate X-ray-generated carriers and drive their out-of-plane transport (Fig. 4e) [26, 36], thereby exhibiting a stable bulk photovoltage. Benefiting from this intriguing built-in electric field, the Ag/(Mpda)PbBr4 wafer/Ag detector can operate in self-powered mode without external bias. As shown in Fig. 4f, both poling states yield clear X-ray photocurrent responses at zero bias. The magnitude and direction of the photocurrent density depend on the X-ray dose rate and applied direction of the poling electric field. With the X-ray dose rate gradually increasing from 0.1 to 28.0 μGy s−1, the corresponding photocurrent density increases from 15.80 to 32.50 nA cm−2, showing a good linear response characteristic. X-Ray Detection and Imaging

We further evaluated the systematic X-ray detection performance of the macroscopically polarized 2D MHP wafer. Upon applying a small bias (1 V), the J-t curve of the poled (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer shows a significantly enhanced photocurrent response compared to the as-fabricated one (Fig. S11), confirming that the built-in electric field, arising from aligned polarization, synergistically reinforces carrier extraction under an external electric field. Further, J-t curves of the polarized wafer under different external bias voltages (e.g., 2, 4, 8, and 16 V) were measured (Figs. S12, S13, and 5a). As shown in Fig. 5a, this device exhibits a linear and sensitive photocurrent increase across a broad range of X-ray dose rates, with a clear response even at a low dose rate of 100 nGy s−1, confirming its excellent X-ray detection performance under external bias operation. Figure 5b shows the change of X-ray-induced current densities with increasing X-ray dose rates, from which the sensitivities under different external bias can be derived. The sensitivity gradually grows with the increasing bias voltages due to the more efficient charge collection under larger electric field [13, 40], reaching 2285 μC Gy−1 cm−2 at 16 V bias (Fig. 5c). Figure 5d shows SNRs under various X-ray dose rates at 16 V bias. Notably, a high SNR of 19.66 is obtained even at a low dose rate of 100 nGy s−1. Fitting the relationship between SNR and dose rate gives an ultralow detection limit of 7.2 nGy s−1, which is about 763-fold lower than that required for regular medical imaging (5500 nGy s−1). Furthermore, such a low detection limit can be achieved at different applied biases (Fig. S14), indicating its suitability for low-dose applications. A comprehensive comparison with many recently reported 2D MHP X-ray detectors (Table S2 and Fig. 5e) highlights that the (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer achieves not only high sensitivity but also an ultralow detection limit, revealing its strong potential for use in high-performance X-ray detection and imaging.

As seen, a stable and repeatable X-ray response can be acquired even after long-term operation, indicating its great potential for practical application. Finally, we evaluated the imaging capability of (Mpda)PbBr4 wafer detector in a single-pixel imaging mode, as illustrated in Fig. 5h (left). In this work, a metal nut was selected as the object for imaging, and the X-ray dose rate and operational voltage were set to 60.1 μGy s−1 and 20 V, respectively. As shown in Fig. 5h, a well-defined, high-contrast image of this nut was successfully reconstructed by mapping position-dependent response current densities with color scales. This result suggests that the 2D (Mpda)PbBr4 polycrystalline wafer is suitable for X-ray imaging and exhibits the potential for use in areas like medical imaging or nondestructive testing.

Conclusions

In summary, we successfully achieved macroscopic out-of-plane polarization in a centimeter-sized 2D ferroelectric perovskite polycrystalline wafer, (Mpda)PbBr4, via a facile hot-pressing strategy, which circumvents the difficulty of large-size crystal growth and enhances the interlayer carrier transport, thus achieving efficient X-ray detection. During processing, (Mpda)PbBr4 micrograins adopt a horizontal orientation, enabling the vertical alignment of their polarization axes. Subsequent electrical poling yields a macroscopic polarization within the wafer, thus generating a robust, switchable built-in electric field. This field drives the efficient separation and interlayer transport of photogenerated carriers, manifesting as a pronounced bulk photovoltaic effect (~ 2 V). As a result, the vertical X-ray detector based on poled wafer operates effectively in a self-powered mode, achieving a sensitivity exceeding 448 μC Gy−1 cm−2. The synergy between the built-in and an external electric field (16 V) further enhances the detection performance, delivering a high sensitivity of 2285 μC Gy−1 cm−2 and an ultralow detection limit of 7.2 nGy s−1, which surpasses the performance of many reported 2D perovskite-based detectors. The device also demonstrates excellent operational stability, with a minimal baseline drift of 5.04×10−6 nA cm−1 V−1 s−1, and showcases high-resolution imaging capability. This study establishes that engineering macroscopic out-of-plane polarization in polycrystalline wafer is a promising pathway to overcome inefficient interlayer carrier transport, providing insights into future design and fabrication of high-performance, flat-panel X-ray detectors based on 2D hybrid perovskites.

Classification of invariant tight contact structures on the 3-space, -ball and -sphere
Mirko Torresani

Abstract
. We prove some classification results for tight contact structure in the 3-space, -ball and -sphere that are invariant with respect to some arbitrary involution, that is conjugated to the standard rotation around the x-axis. Unlike the classical scenario, a new integral torsion appears, dictating a splitting between equivalence classes. These tools could be useful fur future classification results regarding strongly invertible Legendrian knots.

1.
Introduction

In recent year the study of strongly invertible Legendrian knots has received considerable attention. The aim of this paper is to build some basic classification results for tight contact structures in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
S^{3}
and
B^{3}_{\text{std}}
that are invariant with respect to some
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-action on such spaces.

Recall that a contact structure
\xi
on an oriented three manifold
M
is a plane distribution such that, if locally we fix a form
\alpha
whose Kernel is
\xi
, then
\alpha\wedge d\alpha
is a non-vanishing 3-form. The structure
\xi
is
positively cooriented
if it coincides with
\Ker(\alpha)
for some global 1-form
\alpha
, such that
\alpha\wedge d\alpha
is positive with respect of the orientation on
M
. From now we will always suppose such condition to hold.

A contact structure
\xi
is
overtwisted
if there exists an embedded disk
D
with
T_{p}D
intersecting
\xi_{p}
only at
T_{p}\partial D
for every
p\in\partial D
. If
\xi
is not overtwisted, then it is
tight
.

Furthermore, an oriented surface
S
, in a neighborhood
S\times\mathbb{R}
the contact form can be written as
\beta_{z}+w_{z}\,dz
, with
w_{z}\colon S\to\mathbb{R}
a smooth function and
\beta_{z}
identifiable a 1-form
S
. The contact condition is given by

(1)

w_{z}\,d\beta_{z}+\beta_{z}\wedge(dw_{z}-\dot{\beta}_{z})>0\,.

Moreover, if we equip
S
of an area form
\Omega
, then we define the
characteristic foliation

S_{\xi}
to be the unique vector field
X
such that
i_{X}\Omega=\beta_{0}
, where
i_{X}
is the contraction given by substituting
X
in the
first entry
. The singularity of
X
are the points where the planes
\xi_{p}
and
T_{p}S
coincide. we give signs to such singularities:
positive
if such planes have same orientation,
negative
otherwise.

Finally, given a global contact form
\alpha
, the
Reeb vector field
is the unique vector field
R_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

\begin{split}d\alpha(R_{\alpha},\cdot)&\equiv 0\\
\alpha(R_{\alpha})&\equiv 1.\end{split}

Let’s now introduce the involution we will be working with. A priori we want to fix a generic involution
u
, acting on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with the following properties:

1)

it extends to an infinity-preserving involution of
S^{3}
;

2)

the fixed point set is non-empty, at every fixed point the differential, viewed as an endomorphism of the tangent space, is diagonalizable as
[1]\oplus[-1]\oplus[-1]
.

Thanks to Waldhausen’s results, such
u
is conjugated to a standard rotation. However, it will be more useful to abstract the reasoning up to the last section.

We say that a contact structure
\xi
is
invariant
if
u^{*}\xi=\xi
and
u^{*}\alpha=-\alpha
. The aim is to classify such objects up to
equivariant isotopy
, i.e. up to isotopies
\psi_{t}
of the space such that
\psi_{t}\circ u
coincides with
u\circ\psi_{t}
. The three main theorems are then the following. To future reference our isotopies will always start at the identity.

Theorem 1
.

The set of tight contact structures on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
up to equivariant isotopy is trivial.

Theorem 2
.

If such action
u
preserves the complementary of the standard
3
-ball, then the set of invariant tight contact structures on
(B^{3}_{\text{std}})^{c}
, inducing a fixed characteristic foliation on
S^{3}=\partial B^{3}_{\text{std}}
, up to equivariant isotopy is trivial.

Theorem 3
.

If such action
u
preserves the standard
3
-ball, then the set of invariant tight contact structures on
B^{3}_{\text{std}}
, inducing a fixed characteristic foliation on
S^{3}=\partial B^{3}_{\text{std}}
, up to equivariant isotopy is a
\mathbb{Z}
-torsor.

Theorem 4
.

The set of invariant tight contact structures on
S^{3}
, up to equivariant isotopy, is a
\mathbb{Z}
-torsor.

Before passing to the next section, let’s briefly define the surfaces, and vector fields, we will work with. All the surfaces will be oriented and connected.

Definition
.

A surface
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
preserved by
u
is said to be
u
-oriented if it is equipped with an area form
\Omega\in\Lambda^{2}S
such that
u^{*}\Omega=\Omega
.

Definition
.

Given an
u
-oriented surface
S
, an anti-invariant singular foliation on
S
is an element of

\{X\in\mathfrak{X}(S)\mid u^{*}X=-X\}/\sim\,,

where

1)

u^{*}X
at a point
p
is defined as the pull-back, via the differential, of
X(u(p))
;

2)

X
is equivalent to
X^{\prime}
if there exists a smooth function
f\colon Z\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
f\circ u=f
and
X^{\prime}=f\,X
.

The equivalence class of the characteristic foliation on a
u
-oriented surface induced by an invariant contact structure is an example of such foliation.

For now on the term
anti-invariant
will be omitted, and we will refer to the previous vector fields simply as
singular foliation
.

The paper will be structured as follows. In
Section

2
we state and prove equivariant versions of classical theorems in contact topology;
Section

3
is dedicated to expand on this theorem in order to set foundation for a basic equivariant convex theory. Although the setting is new, most of the work consists in checking that the
u
-action respects the quantity at play. The last preparations are done in
Section

4
, in which a classification theorem for invariant tight contact structure in
S^{2}\times[-1,1]
is proven. Here is where the equivariant scenario diverges from the classical one: a new
\mathbb{Z}
-torsor appears, dictating a splitting between equivalence classes. Finally
Sections

5
and
6
are devoted to proving the main results.

2.
Equivariant Giroux and Darboux’ Theorems

The aim of this section is to state and prove equivariant versions of classical results in contact topology: Gray, Giroux and Darboux’ theorems. Such restatements are given by
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.5
respectively.

Let’s introduce the following notation: given a surface
S
, we will extend the action on
S\times\mathbb{R}
as
u(x,y)=(u(x),y)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Each
u
-oriented surface admits a tubular neighborhood diffeomorphic
S\times\mathbb{R}
, which is
u
-equivariant, and such that the action is given by the convention above.

Proof.

Since
u
is an involution, it’s action can be viewed as a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, which is a compact group. Hence we can fix an invariant Riemannian metric
g
on
S
. By Bredon
[
3
, Thm. 2.2]
there exists small enough invariant neighborhood
U
of
S
inside the normal bundle
N(S)=TS^{\bot}\subseteq T\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that the exponential map
\exp\colon U\to M
is an embedding. Let’s observe that the exponential map is equivariant, with the action on the normal bundle given by the differential.

Then the image
\exp(U)
is the tubular neighborhood we searched for. Moreover, since
N(S)
is an oriented 1-bundle, it is isomorphic to
S\times\mathbb{R}
as vector bundle. By taking at each fiber the positively oriented vector of
g
-norm 1, one can impose a canonical isomorphism.

Finally, since
u
is a
g
-isometry, it must send
(x,y)
into
(u(x),\pm y)
. Since
u
preserves the orientation both of
S
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such sign must be negative.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\xi_{t}
,
t\in[0,1]
, be a smooth family of invariant contact structures on
S^{3}
. Then there exist an equivariant isotopy
\psi_{t}
such that
T\psi_{t}(\xi_{0})=\xi_{t}
.

Proof.

The equation we have to solve is

\psi_{t}^{*}\alpha_{t}=\lambda_{t}\alpha_{0}

for some family of functions
\lambda_{t}\colon S^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
. By taking derivatives in the previous equation we get

\psi^{*}_{t}(\dot{\alpha}_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}}\alpha_{t})=\dot{\lambda}_{t}\alpha_{0}=\frac{\dot{\lambda}_{t}}{\lambda_{t}}\psi_{t}^{*}\alpha_{t}\,,

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to
t

[
7
, Lemma 2.2.1]
.

Let’s set
\mu_{t}\coloneqq\tfrac{d}{dt}(\log\lambda_{t})\circ\psi_{t}^{-1}
, and let’s use the Cartan’s formula
1
1

1

which holds also for time-dependent vector field, see
[
7
, App. B]
.
to get

\psi^{*}_{t}(\dot{\alpha}_{t}+d(\alpha_{t}(X_{t}))+\iota_{X_{t}}\alpha_{t})=\dot{\lambda}_{t}\alpha_{0}=\psi_{t}^{*}(\mu_{t}\alpha_{t})\,.

By making the Ansatz
X_{t}\in\xi_{t}
, the previous equation implies

(2)

\begin{cases}\dot{\alpha}_{t}+i_{X_{t}}d\alpha_{t}=\mu_{t}\alpha_{t}\\
\dot{\alpha}_{t}(R_{t})=\mu_{t}\end{cases}\,,

where
R_{t}
is the Reeb’s vector field of
\alpha_{t}
.

Finally, having defined
\mu_{t}
, we can choose a unique element
X_{t}\in\xi_{t}
satisfying Equations (
2
): restricting the 1-forms to
\xi_{t}
allows us to pinpoint a precise
X_{t}
, thanks to the non-degeneracy of
d\alpha_{t}
, and with such choice Equations (
2
) holds, since
R_{t}\in\Ker(\mu_{t}\alpha_{t}-\dot{\alpha}_{t})
.

Moreover

\mu_{t}\circ u=u^{*}\mu_{t}=u^{*}[\dot{\alpha}_{t}(R_{t})]=-\dot{\alpha}_{t}(-R_{t})=\mu_{t}\,.

Hence
u^{*}X_{t}
satisfies the following equality:

u^{*}\dot{\alpha}_{t}+i_{u^{*}X_{t}}du^{*}\alpha_{t}=\mu_{t}u^{*}\alpha_{t}\,,

re-writable as

-\dot{\alpha}_{t}-i_{u^{*}X_{t}}d\alpha_{t}=-\mu_{t}\alpha_{t}\,.

Thus
u^{*}X_{t}
satisfies the same equation as
X_{t}
, and hence they must coincide. By integrating
X_{t}
we obtain an equivariant isotopy
\psi_{t}
with the desired properties.
∎

The previous discussion also holds for contact structures in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. However, the last step is no longer true, and we can integrate
X_{t}
on
[0,1]
only over a compact set, or on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{3}
if
X_{t}
vanishes away of such set.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
two
u
-oriented compact surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and let
\phi\colon S_{0}\to S_{1}
an equivariant diffeomorphism, such that
\phi(S_{0,\xi_{0}})=S_{1,\xi_{1}}
singular foliations, for two invariant contact structures. Then there exist appropriate invariant neighborhoods and an equivariant contactomorphism
\psi\colon\mathcal{N}(S_{0})\to\mathcal{N}(S_{1})
such that
\psi|_{S_{0}}
is isotopic to
\psi
via an isotopy preserving the characteristic foliation.

Proof.

On invariant tubular neighborhoods of
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
, diffeomorphic to
S_{i}\times\mathbb{R}
, the contact forms can be written as
\beta_{z}^{i}+w_{z}^{i}dz
, with
\beta_{z}^{i}
and
u_{z}^{i}
that are anti-invariant.

By hypothesis there exists a function
f\colon S\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\beta_{0}^{0}
coincides with
f\phi^{*}\beta_{0}^{1}
, with
f
being constantly positive or negative. The sign depends on the fact that
\phi
does or does not respects the orientations of
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
. Moreover,
f
is invariant with respect to the the
u
-action.

Secondly, let’s consider the natural extension of
\phi
from
S_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
to
S_{1}\times\mathbb{R}
, which we identify with invariant neighborhoods, such that the contact form
f\,\phi^{*}\alpha_{1}
is positively cooriented in
S_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Let’s define the family of forms
\alpha_{t}
on
\mathcal{N}(S_{0})\cong S_{0}\times\mathbb{R}
as

\alpha_{t}\coloneqq(1-t)\alpha_{0}+tf\phi^{*}\alpha_{1}\,.

Equation (
1
) is simultaneously linear in
\dot{\beta}_{z}
and
w_{z}
, and
\alpha_{t}
induce the same form
\beta_{0}
on
S\times\{0\}
. Hence the contact condition is satisfied on
S\times\{0\}
, and it will be satisfied in a small neighborhood
\lvert z\rvert<1
. Moreover
\alpha_{t}
is always anti-invariant.

By
2.2
we can construct an equivariant contact isotopy in a small neighborhood
\mathcal{N}(S_{0})
of
S_{0}
, keeping
S_{0}
fixed. Let’s call
X_{t}
the vector field
\tfrac{d}{dt}\psi_{t}
.

On the space
TS_{0}
we have that
\dot{\alpha}_{t}\equiv 0
, since
\alpha_{0}
and
f\phi^{*}\alpha_{1}
induce the same form
\beta_{0}
. Hence, for every
v
tangent to
(S_{0})_{\xi_{0}}
we have that

\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}}\alpha_{t}(v)\overset{(\triangle)}{=}i_{X_{t}}d\alpha_{t}(v)+di_{X_{t}}\alpha_{t}(v)\overset{X_{t}\in\xi_{t}}{=}i_{X_{t}}d\alpha_{t}(v)\overset{(\ast)}{=}-\dot{\alpha}_{t}(v)=0\,,

where equality
(\triangle)
is justified by Cartan’s formula, and equality

by Equations (
2
), present in the proof of
2.2
. As a consequence, the flow of
X_{t}
must keep
\beta_{0}
fixed, and hence it preserves the characteristic foliation.

Finally, by setting
\psi\coloneqq\phi\circ\psi_{1}
, the distribution
T\psi(\xi_{0})
coincides with
\xi_{1}
, and
\psi
is the required diffeomorphism.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

With the assumption of the preceding theorem we can find a stronger
\psi
such that
\psi|_{S_{0}}=\phi
.

Proof.

We want to reuse the proof of
2.2
, without forcing
X_{t}
to belong to
\xi_{t}
.

As the previous theorem, we know that
\alpha_{0}
and
f\phi^{*}\alpha_{1}
induce the same form
\beta_{0}
on
S_{0}=S_{0}\times\{0\}
for some function
f
; in particular
\dot{\alpha}_{t}|_{TS_{0}}\equiv 0
. By no longer requiring that
X_{t}
vanishes on
\alpha_{t}
, the desired condition becomes

\dot{\alpha}_{t}+d(\alpha_{t}(X_{t}))+i_{X_{t}}d\alpha_{t}=\mu_{t}\alpha_{t}\,,

where

\mu_{t}=\frac{d}{dt}\,\log\lambda_{t}\circ\psi_{t}^{-1}\,.

Let’s write
X_{t}=H_{t}R_{t}+Y_{t}
, with
Y_{t}\in\Ker\alpha_{t}
. Then the previous condition becomes

(3)

\dot{\alpha}_{t}+dH_{t}+i_{Y_{t}}d\alpha_{t}=\mu_{t}\alpha_{t}\,.

The Equation (
3
) completely determines
\mu_{t}
via
H_{t}
once we evaluate it on the Reeb vector field. Once this is done,
Y_{t}
is determined by the non-degeneracy
d\alpha_{t}|_{\xi_{t}}
.

The function
H_{t}
is determined via the following system, whose equations are compatible thanks to the condition
\dot{\alpha}_{t}|_{TS_{0}}\equiv 0
:

(4)

\begin{cases}\dot{\alpha}_{t}+dH_{t}=0&\text{on $S_{0}$}\\
H_{t}\equiv 0&\text{on $S_{0}$}\end{cases}\,.

At this point the vector field
X_{t}
identifies a flow
\psi_{t}
fixing
S_{0}
pointwise.

Moreover, let’s show that
u^{*}X_{t}=X_{t}
holds. As a start, from Equations (
4
) we get

u^{*}dH_{t}=-u^{*}\dot{\alpha}_{t}=\dot{\alpha}_{t}=-dH_{t}\,.

As a consequence, Equation (
3
) implies that
u^{*}Y_{t}=Y_{t}
. Hence
u^{*}
preserves also
X_{t}=H_{t}R_{t}+Y_{t}
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
u
is given by
(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,-y-z)
. Given an invariant contact structure in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(or
S^{3}
), we can find a global equivariant isotopy such that
\xi
coincides with
\Ker(dz-y\,dx)
in a small neighborhood of the origin.

Proof.

We will build a deformation of the contact structure, and realize it via
2.2
.

Firstly, let’s fix
v\in T_{\mathbf{0}}\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\alpha(\mathbf{0})
coincides with
\langle v,\cdot\rangle
. Since
u^{*}\alpha
equals
-\alpha
, then
d_{p}u(v)
coincides with
-v
.

Thus the vector
v
belongs to the
yz
-plane. Moreover, up an isotopy of the form

\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\cos t&-\sin t\\
0&\sin t&\cos t\end{bmatrix}

we can suppose
v
to be a positive multiple of the vector
\partial_{z}
. Consequently,
\xi(\mathbf{0})
is set to the
xy
-plane. Moreover, without loss of generality, we can suppose that we have chosen the contact form such that
v
is the vector
\partial_{z}
. Moreover,
d\alpha(\mathbf{0})
is a symplectic 2-form, positively oriented, on
\xi(\mathbf{0})
. Hence
d\alpha|_{\xi(\mathbf{0})}
is equal to
\lambda\,dx\wedge dy
for some positive
\lambda
.

Thus, up to rescale the

- and

-axis with an isotopy of the form

\psi_{t}\colon\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{bmatrix}\mapsto(1-t)\begin{bmatrix}x\\[2.15277pt]
y\\[2.15277pt]
z\end{bmatrix}+t\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{\lambda}\,x\ \\[2.15277pt]
\sqrt{\lambda}\,y\\[2.15277pt]
z\end{bmatrix}

and substitute
\xi

T\psi_{1}^{-1}(\xi)
, we can actually suppose that

coincides with

.

Now we want to adjust the two-from
d\alpha
on the whole tangent space

. Firstly, since it is anti-invariant the equality

d\alpha(\mathbf{0})=dx\wedge dy+\mu\,dx\wedge dz\,.

is forced to hold. Let’s then replace

with a new contact form

, for the same structure, given by

Then

and

Finally, just set

and interpolate it with

. Since

at the origin are constantly
\alpha_{1}
and

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose that
u
is given by
(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,-y,-z)
. Up to equivariant isotopy, any two invariant contact structures on

can be made be equal in a small neighborhood of the origin.

Let’s end the section with a last lemma, that allows us to reconstruct contact structures from singular foliations.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
S
be an
u
-oriented compact surface embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. A vector field
X
on
S
represents the characteristic foliation of some invariant contact structure defined near
S
if and only if it satisfies the condition

\diver_{\Omega}(X)(p)\neq 0\text{ if }X(p)=0\,.

Proof.

Let’s prove one implication, by fixing an invariant contact structure
\xi
near
S
and a vector field
X
defining the characteristic foliation. Locally the contact form can be written as
\beta_{z}+w_{z}dz
, with
\beta\coloneqq\beta_{0}
. If
p
is a singularity of
X
we know that the planes
T_{p}S
and
\xi_{p}
coincide. Hence

\diver_{\Omega}(X)(p)\,\Omega_{p}=(d(i_{X}\Omega))_{p}=(d\beta)_{p}=d\alpha|_{T_{p}S}=d\alpha|_{\xi_{p}}\neq 0\,.

Hence
\diver_{\Omega}(X)
cannot vanish on any singular point of
X
.

On the other hand, let’s fix a vector field
X
on
S
satisfying the divergence condition, and set
w\colon S\to\mathbb{R}
such that
d\beta=w\Omega
, where
\beta\coloneqq i_{X}\Omega
. By construction
u^{*}w=-w
.

Observe that the vanishing set of
\beta
and
X
coincide. And if
p
such of a point, then

0\neq\diver_{\Omega}(X)(p)\,\Omega_{p}=(d(i_{X}\Omega))_{p}=(d\beta)_{p}\,.

Hence
w(p)
cannot vanish on such points.

Secondly, fix an invariant Riemannian metric
g
on
S
. Since
S
is oriented, we know that there is a unique
\gamma\in\Lambda^{1}S
such that

\begin{cases}\lVert\gamma\rVert_{g}=\lVert\beta\rVert_{g}\\
\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle_{g}=0\\
(\beta\wedge\gamma)/\Omega\geq 0\\
(\beta\wedge\gamma)/\Omega>0&\text{if $\beta(p)\neq 0$}\end{cases}

Moreover, we know that

\begin{cases}\lVert-u^{*}\gamma\rVert_{g}=\lVert\gamma\rVert_{g}=\lVert\beta\rVert_{g}\\
\langle\beta,-u^{*}\gamma\rangle_{g}=\langle u^{*}\beta,u^{*}\gamma\rangle_{g}=\langle\beta,\gamma\rangle_{g}=0\\
(\beta\wedge-u^{*}\gamma)/\Omega=u^{*}[(\beta\wedge\gamma)/\Omega]\geq 0\end{cases}\,,

and hence
u^{*}\gamma
coincides with
-\gamma
.

Now set

\beta_{z}\coloneqq\beta+z(dw-\gamma)

and

\alpha^{\prime}\coloneqq\beta_{z}+w\,dz\,.

Then
u^{*}\alpha^{\prime}=-\alpha^{\prime}
, and

w\,d\beta_{0}+\beta_{0}\wedge(dw-\dot{\beta}_{z}|_{z=0})=w^{2}\Omega+\beta\wedge\gamma>0,.

Thus,
\alpha^{\prime}
defines a invariant contact structure near
S
.
∎

3.
Equivariant Convex Theory

The goal of this section is to build the fundamental tools necessary to adapt basic convex theory to our equivariant scenario.

Definition 3.1
.

An action of a group
G

X

g\cdot x=x
, for some
g\in G

g=\text{id}_{X}

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix a finite group

,

-invariant positive continuous function

, there exists a smooth
G
-map

satisfying the following conditions:

is transversal on
M
to
Y
in
N
;

2)

Proof.

See
[

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S
be a closed surface, equipped with a involution that is free outside a finite number of points, and with an anti-invariant vector field with no closed orbits, that does not vanish on such points. Then it can be
C^{\infty}
-approximated by an anti-invariant vector field having a finite number of singularities, all non-degenerate (i.e. transverse to the zero-section).

Proof.

Set
G\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Let’s consider the
G
-spaces
M\coloneqq S
and
N\coloneqq TS
, with the action on the second space being

g\cdot(p,v)=(g\cdot p,-d_{p}g[v])\,.

The anti-invariant vector field are precisely the

Recall that given a vector field

Definition 3.4

We are not going to specify the meaning of the last requirement, since we will avoid close orbit all together.

Equivariant Tomography Theory

In this section we will look at the

S^{2}\times[-1,1]
via the characteristic foliation on the slices

We suppose the product

We will call such scenario to be an

With such setting in mind, the aim of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 5

Let

an equivariant tomography. The set of invariant tight contact structure on

-torsor.

Consider an arbitrary map

is a smooth isotopy of embeddings from

to

\phi_{u,t}(N)

\sigma_{u,0}
coincides with
\tau_{u}
.¨

For every point

By fixing a Riemannian metric on

of

\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{u,t}=\tilde{X}_{u,t}\end{cases}\,.

Since

extended

the equality

Palais’ argument is similar, and quite easy to understand. He uses vector fields and exponential maps to find local section of the map he claims to be locally trivial. Observe that the above reasoning is not complete, since

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

4.1.
Reconstruction and Uniqueness Lemmas

Before dwelling into the proof of
5
, let’s state a couple of lemmas, that will indicate the direction we are trying to follow.

Lemma 4.4

(Reconstruction Lemma)
.

Let
S\times[-1,1]
be an equivariant tomography, equipped with two invariant contact structures
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{1}
, such that the foliations
S^{z}_{\xi_{i}}
coincide for each
z\in[-1,1]
. Then
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{1}
are equivariantly isotopic rel boundary.

Proof.

The assumption on the characteristic foliations implies that we can write

\alpha_{i}=\beta_{z}+w^{i}_{z}\,dz\,,

with
\beta_{z}
independent of
i=0,1
. Moreover, the condition

w_{z}^{i}d\beta_{z}+\beta_{z}\wedge(dw_{z}^{i}-\dot{\beta}_{z})>0

is convex if
\beta_{z}
is fixed. Hence

\alpha_{t}\coloneqq t\alpha_{1}+(1-t)\alpha_{0}

is a contact form. It then follows from
2.2
that
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{1}
are equivariantly isotopic; an isotopy rel boundary can be build as in
2.4
, since the homotopy of contact forms is stationary on
TS^{\pm 1}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5

(Uniqueness Lemma)
.

Let
S\times[-1,1]
be an equivariant tomography, equipped with two invariant contact structures
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{1}
with the following properties:

1)

the characteristic foliations
S^{\pm 1}_{\xi_{i}}
on the boundary of
S\times[-1,1]
coincide for
i=0,1
;

2)

each surface
S^{z}
,
z\in[-1,1]
, is convex for either contact structure, and there is a smoothly varying family of multi-curves
\Gamma_{z}
dividing both
S^{z}_{\xi_{0}}
and
S^{z}_{\xi_{1}}
.

Then
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{1}
equivariantly are isotopic rel boundary

Proof.

Choose contact forms

\alpha^{i}=\beta^{i}_{z}+w^{i}_{z}\,dz,\quad i=0,1,

defining
\xi_{i}=\Ker\alpha^{i}
, with
\beta^{0}_{\pm 1}=\beta^{1}_{\pm 1}
. Recall the contact condition

A^{i}\coloneqq w^{i}_{z}\,d\beta^{i}_{z}+\beta^{i}_{z}\wedge(dw^{i}_{z}-\dot{\beta}^{i}_{z})>0,\quad i=0,1,

with the dot denoting the derivative with respect to
z
. The function
A^{i}
is invariant.

The convexity assumption allows us to choose, for each
z\in[-1,1]
, functions
v^{0}_{z}
and
v^{1}_{z}
on
S
that vanish exactly on
\Gamma_{z}
, only to first order, and that satisfy the contact condition

B^{i}\coloneqq v^{i}_{z}\,d\beta^{i}_{z}+\beta^{i}_{z}\wedge dv^{i}_{z}>0,\quad i=0,1.

The function
B^{i}
is invariant.

The construction in the proof of Theorem 4.8.5 gives us two families of functions
v^{i}_{z}
varying smoothly with
z
, and with
v^{0}_{\pm 1}=v^{1}_{\pm 1}
. Notice that for each
z
the function
v^{0}_{z}/v^{1}_{z}
, defined a priori on
S\setminus\Gamma_{z}
only, extends unambiguously to a positive function on all of
S
. The computation

\begin{split}v^{0}\,d\left(\frac{v^{0}}{v^{1}}\beta\right)+\left(\frac{v^{0}}{v^{1}}\beta\right)\wedge dv^{0}&=\left(\frac{v^{0}}{v^{1}}\right)^{2}v^{1}d\beta+\frac{v^{0}}{v^{1}}dv^{0}\wedge\beta\\
&\quad-\left(\frac{v^{0}}{v^{1}}\right)^{2}dv^{1}\wedge\beta+\frac{v^{0}}{v^{1}}\beta\wedge dv^{0}\\
&=\left(\frac{v^{0}}{v^{1}}\right)^{2}(v^{1}d\beta+\beta\wedge dv^{1})\end{split}

shows that if we replace
\beta^{1}_{z}
by
(v^{0}_{z}/v^{1}_{z})\cdot\beta^{1}_{z}
we may take
v^{1}_{z}=v^{0}_{z}\eqqcolon v_{z}
. Observe that after this replacement we still have
\beta^{0}_{\pm 1}=\beta^{1}_{\pm 1}
.

With
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
set the anti-invariant form

\alpha^{i}_{t}\coloneqq\beta^{i}_{z}+(1-t)w^{i}_{z}+t\,\lambda\,v_{z}\,dz,\quad i=0,1,\quad t\in[0,1]\,.

Then

\alpha^{i}_{t}\wedge d\alpha^{i}_{t}=((1-t)A^{i}-t\beta^{i}_{z}\wedge\dot{\beta}^{i}_{z}+t\lambda B^{i})\wedge dz\,,

which is positive for all
t\in[0,1]
if
\lambda
is chosen sufficiently large. Finally, set the family of anti-invariant forms
\alpha_{s}
as

\alpha_{s}\coloneqq(1-s)\alpha^{0}_{1}+s\alpha^{1}_{1}=(1-s)\beta^{0}_{z}+s\beta^{1}_{z}+\lambda\,v_{z}\,dz,\quad s\in[0,1]\,.

Then

\alpha_{s}\wedge d\alpha_{s}=\lambda((1-s)B^{0}+sB^{1})\wedge dz+O_{\lambda}(1)\,,

which is likewise positive for all
s\in[0,1]
, provided
\lambda
is sufficiently large.
Then

\begin{cases}\alpha^{0}_{3t},&0\leq t\leq 1/3,\\
\alpha_{3t-1},&1/3\leq t\leq 2/3,\\
\alpha^{1}_{3-3t},&2/3\leq t\leq 1,\end{cases}

defines a homotopy via contact forms from
\alpha^{0}
to
\alpha^{1}
that is stationary on
TS^{\pm 1}
. Now the proof concludes as in the reconstruction lemma.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

If in either of the two preceding Lemmas the contact structure
\xi_{0}
,
\xi_{1}
coincide near the boundary, the proofs give isotopies that are stationary near the boundary.

Moreover, from the proof of the Reconstruction and Uniqueness Lemmas we can get the following result.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\xi
be an invariant tight contact structure on an equivariant tomography
S^{2}\times[-1,1]
, such that
S^{z}
are
\xi
-convex. Then a smooth family of dividing circles
\Gamma_{z}
, coinciding near the boundary, is determined up to level-preserving isotopy

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{z}^{0}
and
\Gamma_{z}^{1}
be two families of dividing circles.

Similarly to the proof of the Uniqueness Lemma, we can write

as

\alpha=\begin{cases}\beta_{z}+v^{0}_{z}\,dz&\text{using the circle $\Gamma^{0}_{z}$}\\
\beta_{z}+v^{1}_{z}\,dz&\text{using the circle $\Gamma^{1}_{z}$}\end{cases}\,.

Hence we can define the family

\alpha_{t}\coloneqq\beta_{z}+((1-t)v^{0}_{z}+tv^{1}_{z})\,dz\,,

4.2.
Defining the twisting torsor

The aim of this subsection is to introduce define the correct twisting invariant appearing in the statement of
5

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
F
be a line in a 3-manifold
S\times[-1,1]
, intersecting each

at a single point. Then the set of hyperplanes distribution
\xi_{p}\subseteq T_{p}M
tangent to

, with fixed endpoints, up to isotopy rel boundary, is a

-torsor.

Proof.

The above equivalence classes are in bijection with the set of trivializations of normal bundle

up to isotopy of
S\times[-1,1]
rel boundary. It is then a standard fact in differential geometry that such object is a
\mathbb{Z}
-torsor.
∎

Let’s now consider an arbitrary invariant tight contact structure
\xi
on an equivariant tomography
S^{2}\times[-1,1]
, and let’s pick an arbitrary line
F=\{s\}\times[-1,1]
of fixed points. For every point

on

, the plane
\xi_{p}
is preserved by

. Since

is diagonalizable as
[1]\oplus[-1]\oplus[-1]

. Finally, since

reverses the orientation of the contact planes,

must necessarily be writable as

, and hence

must be tangent to
\xi_{p}
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
be two invariant tight contact structures on

coinciding at the boundary. Then, having chosen a line of fixed points

is the

-difference of

4.2
computed using
F
.

The previous definition seems dependent on the line of fixed point we choose. A way to resolve that is to notice that in the case of a film of convex surfaces the quantity becomes independent. This is what we shall do.

Firstly, let’s define the
equivariant unparameterized embedded loop space
as

\Loop^{eq}(S^{2})\coloneqq\Emb^{eq}(S^{1},S^{2})/\Diff^{eq,+}(S^{1})\,,

with the latter diffeomorphism group acting on the right. In the next

.

The group
\Loop^{eq}(S^{2})
is weakly homotopically equivalent to

Proof.

By quotenting
S^{2}
by

we obtain a double cover

, branched over two points

,
b
. Hence the space we are looking to can be viewed as the space

, i.e. the space of embedded paths from

to

.

Let’s fix an element

. Firstly we observe that there exists a map

\begin{split}\phi\colon G\coloneqq\Diff^{+}(S^{2},a,b)&\to\Omega^{\text{emb}}_{a,b}S^{2}\\
\phi&\mapsto\phi\circ e_{0}\end{split}

with the former being the space of orientation-preserving diffeomorphism fixing the points

and

so must be the space

By cutting along

of orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of the disk preserving the boundary. By Smale

Let’s consider the following Serre fibration, by

.

Finally, by valuating at

It sits in the following commutative diagram of fibrations:

.

Let

4.3.
Proof of Theorem 0

Let’s start from an invariant tight contact structure
\xi
on an equivariant tomography
S\times[-1,1]
,
S=S^{2}
.

Supposing
S^{z}
to be Morse–Smale, and hence convex, in a neighborhoods of
z=0,1
, we want to show how to manipulate the characteristic foliations
S_{\xi}^{z}
by an isotopy rel boundary of
\xi
until we arrive at a contact structure for which each
S^{z}
is a convex surface.

We have seen in
3.7
that, given an adequate surface
u
-oriented in a contact 3-manifold, by perturbing the contact structure we can arrange the surface to have a characteristic foliation of Morse–Smale type, so that the surface is convex by
3.16
. Now, however, we are dealing with a 1-parametric family of surfaces, and the Morse–Smale condition is not generic for 1-parametric families of singular foliations. Nonetheless, there are some simple “genericity” assumptions that we may impose. The key to this are the fact that the contact condition is open in the
C^{1}
-topology, and equivariant Gray stability for invariant contact structures. Therefore, any property that we may impose on a film of singular foliations by a
C^{1}
-small perturbation can be achieved by a corresponding isotopy of contact structures.

Let’s start with a simple lemma, that allows us to use some extra information given by the contact structure.

Lemma 4.13
.

There can be no accumulation point, in terms of the parameter
z
, of retrograde hyperbolic-hyperbolic connections, i.e. connections from a negative to a positive hyperbolic point.

Proof.

Since the statement already holds without any group action, we can ignore the latter for this proof.

Arguing by contradiction, assume that we had such an accumulation point at
z=0
.

Let
z_{\nu}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, be a sequence of points
z_{\nu}
with
\lim_{\nu\to\infty}z_{\nu}=0
such that each
S_{\xi}
contains a retrograde connection, accumulating at such a connection for
\nu\to\infty
. We may choose local coordinates in such a way that we are dealing with a contact structure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[-1,1]
, with a positive (respectively, negative) hyperbolic point at
(\pm 1,0,z_{\nu})
, including
z_{\infty}=0
, and with the line segment between these to points (for each
\nu
) along the
x
-axis being a retrograde connection.

On each of these slices
S^{z_{\nu}}
, the vector field defining the characteristic foliation is a positive multiple of

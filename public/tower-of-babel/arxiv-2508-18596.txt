Linear Trading Position with Sparse Spectrum
Zhao-Rong Lai, Haisheng Yang

Abstract
The principal portfolio approach is an emerging method in signal-based trading. However, these principal portfolios may not be diversified to explore the key features of the prediction matrix or robust to different situations. To address this problem, we propose a novel linear trading position with sparse spectrum that can explore a larger spectral region of the prediction matrix. We also develop a Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann fixed-point algorithm to optimize this trading position, which possesses the descent property and achieves a linear convergence rate in the objective value. This is a new theoretical result for this type of algorithms. Extensive experiments show that the proposed method achieves good and robust performance in various situations.

In general, asset pricing starts with finding some current signals
\bm{S}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
as proxies for the conditional expected returns
\bm{R}_{t+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
at the next time, which gives rise to signal-based trading
Gibbons et al. 1989
. Since asset correlation exists in most cases, linear trading strategies are proposed to exploit cross-predictability (see Figure
1
)
Gârleanu and Pedersen 2013
;
Collin-Dufresne et al. 2020
;
Lai et al. 2024
;
Lin et al. 2024b
;
Lin et al. 2024a
. Recently,
Kelly et al. 2023a
propose to directly combine
signals
and
returns
into a
bi-linear
form with a “prediction matrix”, and extract several principal portfolios (PP) of this prediction matrix to form a linear trading position (LTP).
Kelly et al. 2023b
further propose a conditional factor model for individual corporate bond based on instrumented principal components analysis (IPCA,
Kelly et al. 2019
). This approach exploits the predictability from both own-asset signal and other-asset signals, while keeping a closed-form LTP. It not only keeps a concise portfolio but also improves interpretability of LTP in finance, which leads a new direction in future research.

However, LTP composed by several PPs may not be diversified to explore the key features of the prediction matrix. First, it is recognized in
Kelly et al. 2023a
that including more PPs into the LTP does not necessarily lead to better performance. Second, the spectral energy of each PP is either
1
(selected) or
0
(non-selected), and cannot be adjusted in the current framework (green or blue bars in Figure

). Third, the choice of PPs relies on empirical experience.

suggest empirically using the first three PPs (green bars in Figure
1
) as they possess the most proportion of singular value magnitude, but it may not be robust to different situations (see Supplementary
A.5.2
).

Importance of diversification:
given a simple economic system with
10
states and
10
assets. On each trading period, only one state can occur, and each state occurs with
10\%
probability. If state
i
occurs, the price of Asset
i
will increase by
10\%
, while those of others will decrease by
1\%
. Then if an investor invests all his/her wealth in only one asset, he/she will have only
10\%
probability to gain
10\%
return, but
90\%
probability to lose
1\%
. However, if he/she diversifies the wealth equally in
10
assets (each with
10\%
of the position), he/she will have
100\%
probability to gain
1\%
return without taking the risk of losing wealth.

The same law also holds for PPs, where each PP can be considered as an asset in the above example. Then the
diversification over PPs leads to the sparse spectrum concept
that has been widely used in various machine learning scenarios, such as matrix completion
Cai et al. 2010
;
Candès and Recht 2012
, knowledge base completion
Lacroix et al. 2018
, graph representation learning
You et al. 2020
, and insufficient-label recognition
Cui et al. 2020
. We aim to employ this concept in the LTP framework to extract the key features of the prediction matrix (red bars in Figure
1
). There are some
main difficulties: 1.
This problem has a three-part complicated non-differentiable geometrical structure with the Frobenius, nuclear, and spectral norms. A common solver is the semi-definite programming (SDP) with a conic reformulation, which is a surrogate model that cannot achieve full-reinvestment and thus result in poor performance.
2.
The projection onto the self-financing constraint
Lai et al. 2020
;
Lai and Yang 2023
is nonlinear and non-orthogonal, which rules out most projected subgradient approaches that require orthogonality and the inner product property of the Hilbert space.
3.
The descent property and the convergence rate of the solving algorithm are difficult to established.

To address the above challenges, we mainly offer the following contributions.
1.
We propose a Linear Trading Position with Sparse Spectrum (LTPSS) that can explore a larger spectral region of the observation matrix, while keeping a sparse and concise representation.
2.
We prove that the nonlinear and non-orthogonal projection onto the feasible set is non-expansive, which is crucial to the convergence of the whole solving algorithm.
3.
We develop a Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann (KM) fixed-point algorithm to solve LTPSS. It possesses the descent property and achieves a linear convergence rate in the objective value. To the best of our knowledge, this is a new theoretical result for KM algorithms, as the current best result is
{o}\left(\frac{1}{{k}}\right)
in the fixed point iteration gap
Boţ and Nguyen 2023
, but not in the objective value. This new finding may reveal greater breakthroughs for KM algorithms.

2
Preliminaries and Related Works

2.1
Linear Trading Position with Principal Portfolios

We start with introducing the concepts of LTP and PP. Let
\bm{S}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\bm{R}_{t+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the signals at time
t
and returns at time
(t+1)
for the
N
assets in a financial market, respectively. The portfolio optimization task is to find an LTP
\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
such that

\max\mathbb{E}[\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{R}_{t+1}].

(1)

In finance, a self-financing constraint is usually imposed to ensure a realistic and feasible trading position, which means that no external money can be added to the position once the trading strategy starts
Lai et al. 2020
;
Lai and Yang 2023
. In the framework of
Kelly et al. 2023a
;
Kelly et al. 2023b
, the following self-financing constraint is used

\Omega:=\{\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}:\|\bm{L}\|_{2}\leqslant 1\},

(2)

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the
\ell_{2}
norm (spectral norm) of a matrix. A trivial strategy satisfying this constraint is the simple factor:

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{L}}_{SF}:=\bm{I}_{(N)},

(LTP-SF)

where
\bm{I}_{(N)}
denotes the identity matrix of
N
dimensions. It only uses the own signal of each asset to determine its position. The original LTP model can be formulated as:

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{L}\in\Omega}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{L}\bm{\Pi}),\quad\bm{\Pi}:=\mathbb{E}[\bm{R}_{t+1}\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}],

(LTP)

where
\mathrm{tr}(\cdot)
denotes the trace operator of a matrix. This formulation exploits the commutative law and the linearity of the trace operator:

\mathbb{E}[\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{L}\bm{R}_{t+1}]=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{tr}(\bm{L}\bm{R}_{t+1}\bm{S}_{t}^{\top})]=\mathrm{tr}(\bm{L}\mathbb{E}[\bm{R}_{t+1}\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}]).

\bm{\Pi}
is called a
prediction matrix
, which contains all the information that can be exploited to determine
\bm{L}
.
Kelly et al. 2023a
propose to estimate it by the empirical estimator:

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\Pi}}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{\tau=t-T}^{t-1}\bm{R}_{\tau+1}\bm{S}_{\tau}^{\top}.

(3)

Since the matrix
\ell_{2}
norm is equivalent to the Schatten
\infty
-norm, it follows from the Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\bm{L}\hat{\bm{\Pi}})\leqslant\|\bm{L}\|_{2}\|\hat{\bm{\Pi}}\|_{*},

(4)

where
\|\cdot\|_{*}
denotes the nuclear norm of the matrix. The equality holds if and only if
\bm{L}
is linear to
(\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{\Pi}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}
. Since
\|\bm{L}\|_{2}\leqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{L}}_{CF}:=(\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}\hat{\bm{\Pi}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}

(LTP-CF)

is exactly the closed-form solution to (
LTP
) with the empirical estimator
\hat{\bm{\Pi}}
.

Instead of
\hat{\bm{L}}_{CF}
,
Kelly et al. 2023a
propose to use the PPs of
\hat{\bm{\Pi}}
to develop the LTP
\bm{L}
. To do this, the first step is to conduct the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}{:=}\bm{U}{\bm{\Sigma}}\bm{V}^{\top},\bm{\Sigma}{:=}\mathrm{diag}(\{{\sigma}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}),{\sigma}_{1}{\geqslant}{\sigma}_{2}{\geqslant}\cdots{\geqslant}{\sigma}_{N}{\geqslant}0,

(5)

where
\bm{U},\bm{V},\bm{U}^{\top},\bm{V}^{\top}
are orthonormal bases of
\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
. Then the principal components
\{\bm{u}_{n}\bm{v}_{n}^{\top}\}_{n=1}^{N}
are defined as the PPs of the prediction matrix
\hat{\bm{\Pi}}
, where
\bm{u}_{n}
and
\bm{v}_{n}
are the
n
-th columns of the matrices
\bm{U}
and
\bm{V}
, respectively.
Kelly et al. 2023a
propose to use the first several PPs to construct an LTP:

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{L}}_{PP}:=\sum_{n=1}^{l}\bm{u}_{n}\bm{v}_{n}^{\top},

(LTP-PP)

where
l
denotes the number of selected PPs. This LTP has a simple form, and is highly interpretable in the literature of finance. However, it may not be diversified to capture the key features of
\hat{\bm{\Pi}}
. First, the setting of
l
relies on empirical experience. As indicated by
Kelly et al. 2023a
, a larger
l
does not necessarily lead to better results. Second, (
LTP-PP
) indicates that the spectral energy of a selected PP is fixed as
1
, which may not be adaptive to different situations.

Surrogate Model

There are some surrogate models for the proposed (
LTPSS
) model in Section
3.1
. One main approach is to reformulate (

) as a semi-definite conic programming

https://www.seas.ucla.edu/~vandenbe/236C/lectures/conic.pdf
(SDCP). Specifically,

\displaystyle\|\bm{L}\|_{2}\leqslant 1\Leftrightarrow\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{(N)}&\bm{L}\\
\bm{L}^{\top}&\bm{I}_{(N)}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,

(6)

\displaystyle\|\bm{L}\|_{*}\leqslant\mathfrak{l}\Leftrightarrow\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}&\bm{L}\\
\bm{L}^{\top}&\bm{V}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0,\ \text{s.t.}\ \frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{tr}{\bm{U}}+\mathrm{tr}{\bm{V}})\leqslant\mathfrak{l},

(7)

where

,

, and

are auxiliary arguments that need to be optimized simultaneously with

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{L},\bm{U},\bm{V},\mathfrak{l}}-\mathrm{tr}(\bm{L}\hat{\bm{\Pi}})+\eta\mathfrak{l},

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{(N)}&\bm{L}\\

\bm{L}^{\top}&\bm{V}\end{bmatrix}{\succeq}0,\ \frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{tr}{\bm{U}}{+}\mathrm{tr}{\bm{V}})\leqslant\mathfrak{l}.

(SDCP)

This model is not equivalent to (
LTPSS
).
It directly replaces
\|\bm{L}\|_{*}
in (
LTPSS
) by

, which is only an upper bound of
\|\bm{L}\|_{*}
. Besides, the mainstream solvers for (
2.2
) are based on interior-point primal-dual algorithms, which
cannot achieve the equality

\|L\|_{2}=1

Worse still, it significantly increases computational complexity due to the auxiliary arguments

\mathfrak{l}

) quadruples that of (
LTPSS
).

3
Methodology

To address the above problems, we propose to expand the spectral region of LTP. First, we consider all the PPs to construct the LTP. Second, we allow for flexible spectral energies in
[0,1]
. Third, we impose sparse spectrum on the LTP to extract the key features of the prediction matrix
\hat{\bm{\Pi}}
. Fourth, we develop a KM fixed-point algorithm to directly solve (
LTPSS
) instead of using the defective and deficient surrogate model (
2.2
).

3.1
Linear Trading Position with Sparse Spectrum

Since an LTP
\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, our framework is developed on the linear space
\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, which is more complicated than
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. First of all, we consider
\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
as a Hilbert space, then the trace operator
\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{B})=:\langle\bm{A},\bm{B}\rangle
is the inner product of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{B}
, for any
\bm{A},\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
. Moreover,
\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A})}=\|\bm{A}\|_{F}
and thus the Frobenius norm is the induced norm from the trace operator for
\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
. First, we give some properties of the constraint set
\Omega
defined in (
2
).

Proposition 1
.

\Omega
is a convex, closed, and bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
.

Proof.

\forall\bm{A},\bm{B}\in\Omega
and
\forall\theta\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|\theta\bm{A}{+}(1{-}\theta)\bm{B}\|_{2}{\leqslant}\theta\|\bm{A}\|_{2}{+}(1{-}\theta)\|\bm{B}\|_{2}{\leqslant}\theta{+}(1{-}\theta){=}1.

(8)

Hence
\|\theta\bm{A}+(1-\theta)\bm{B}\|_{2}\in\Omega
and
\Omega
is convex. Since
\Omega
is a sub-level-set of the continuous function
\|\cdot\|_{2}
, it is closed
Rockafellar and Wets 2009
.
\|\bm{L}\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}^{2}}
, where
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are the singular values of
\bm{L}
(singular values are non-negative). (
2
) indicates that
(\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}\lambda_{i})\leqslant 1
. Hence
\|\bm{L}\|_{F}\leqslant\sqrt{N}
for any
\bm{L}\in\Omega
, which proves that
\Omega
is bounded.
∎

Next, we propose the following LTPSS model:

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{L}\in\Omega}F(\bm{L}):=f(\bm{L})+g(\bm{L}):=-\mathrm{tr}(\bm{L}\hat{\bm{\Pi}})+\eta\|\bm{L}\|_{*},

(LTPSS)

where
\eta\geqslant 0
is a regularization parameter.
f(\bm{L})
has the gradient
\nabla f(\bm{L})=-\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}
, while
g(\bm{L})
is non-differentiable. Furthermore, the constraint
\bm{L}\in\Omega
is also a non-differentiable structure. In summary,
(
LTPSS
) has three parts with the Frobenius, nuclear, and spectral norms, where the latter two are non-differentiable. By dropping either
g(\bm{L})
or
\bm{L}\in\Omega
, it can be reduced to a common problem that can be solved by either projected gradient or proximal gradient methods, respectively. However, with both non-differentiable parts present, (
LTPSS
) cannot be effectively and efficiently solved via common approaches.

Fact:
there exists at least one solution to (
LTPSS
). Since
F(\bm{L})
is a continuous function on
\bm{L}
and
\Omega
is a convex and compact set, this fact follows from the Weierstrass extreme value theorem.

3.2
Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann Fixed-point Algorithm

To address the above difficulty, we develop a KM fixed-point scheme to solve (
LTPSS
), which alternately minimizes
F(\bm{L})
and projects
\bm{L}
onto
\Omega
. Denote the
k
-th iterate of
\bm{L}
by
\bm{L}^{(k)}
. Then in the next iteration, we can use a quadratic approximation to
F(\bm{L})
:

\displaystyle Q(\bm{L},\bm{L}^{(k)})=-\mathrm{tr}(\bm{L}^{(k)}\hat{\bm{\Pi}})-\frac{\beta}{2}\|\hat{\bm{\Pi}}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\bm{L}-\bm{L}^{(k)}-\beta\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}\|_{F}^{2}+\eta\|\bm{L}\|_{*},

(9)

where
\beta>0
is the step size parameter (which can be seen later). By ignoring the constant terms with respect to (w.r.t.) the argument
\bm{L}
and temporarily relaxing the constraint
\bm{L}\in\Omega
, we minimize
Q(\bm{L},\bm{L}^{(k)})
:

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}}Q(\bm{L},\bm{L}^{(k)})\quad\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\argmin_{\bm{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\beta}\|\bm{L}-(\bm{L}^{(k)}+\beta\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top})\|_{F}^{2}+\eta\|\bm{L}\|_{*}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=:\mathrm{prox}_{\beta\eta\|\cdot\|_{*}}(\bm{L}^{(k)}+\beta\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}),

(10)

which is the proximal mapping of
(\bm{L}^{(k)}+\beta\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top})
w.r.t. the function
\beta\eta\|\cdot\|_{*}
. It has a closed form solution
Cai et al. 2010
. Conduct the SVD of
(\bm{L}^{(k)}+\beta\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top})
as

\displaystyle\bm{L}^{(k)}+\beta\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}=\tilde{\bm{U}}\bm{\Lambda}\tilde{\bm{V}}^{\top},\ \bm{\Lambda}:=\mathrm{diag}(\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}).

(11)

Then the singular value thresholding of
\bm{\Lambda}
is

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\Lambda}}:=\mathrm{diag}(\{\mathrm{sign}(\lambda_{i})\cdot\max\{|\lambda_{i}|-\beta\eta,0\}\}_{i=1}^{N}).

(12)

To be intuitive,
\tilde{\bm{\Lambda}}
drags each singular value of
\bm{\Lambda}
towards
0
by a step
\beta\eta
. The closed form solution to (
3.2
) is

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}(\bm{L})

\displaystyle{:=}\bm{L}{+}\beta\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top},\tilde{\bm{L}}^{(k)}{:=}\mathrm{prox}_{\beta\eta\|\cdot\|_{*}}(\mathscr{G}(\bm{L}^{(k)})){=}\tilde{\bm{U}}\tilde{\bm{\Lambda}}\tilde{\bm{V}}^{\top},

(13)

which is actually a proximal-gradient operator. Next, we need to design a feasible projection operator to project
\tilde{\bm{L}}^{(k)}
onto the constraint set
\Omega
.

Definition 2
.

Recall the singular vectors of
\hat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}
defined in (
5
) as
\bm{U}
and
\bm{V}
. Given any matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, let
{\bm{\Gamma}}:=\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{V}
and
{\gamma}_{ii}
be the
i

{\bm{\Gamma}}
. Define

\displaystyle\check{\bm{\Gamma}}:=\mathrm{diag}(\{\check{\gamma}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}),\ \check{\gamma}_{i}:=\begin{cases}\mathrm{sign}{({\gamma}_{ii})},&\text{if}\ |{\gamma}_{ii}|>1;\\
{\gamma}_{ii},&\text{if}\ |{\gamma}_{ii}|\leqslant 1,\end{cases},

\displaystyle\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\bm{A}):=\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top}.

(14)

Note that
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
is a nonlinear and non-orthogonal projection, but it is a non-expansive operator, which is crucial to the convergence of the whole algorithm.

Theorem 3
.

\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
defined in (
2
) is non-expansive.

Proof.

First, it can be easily observed from the definition of
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
that
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}\circ\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}=\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
: the first
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
makes every
\check{\gamma}_{i}
lie in
[-1,1]
, then the second
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
will not change
\check{\gamma}_{i}
any more. Thus
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
is a projection (not necessarily linear or orthogonal) by definition. Second, to prove
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
is non-expansive, we need to verify that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\bm{A}){-}\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\bm{B})\|_{F}{\leqslant}\|\bm{A}{-}\bm{B}\|_{F},\ \forall\bm{A},\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}.

(15)

Let
\bm{A}=\bm{U}(\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{V})\bm{V}^{\top}:=\bm{U}\bm{\Gamma}\bm{V}^{\top}
and
\bm{B}=\bm{U}(\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{B}\bm{V})\bm{V}^{\top}:=\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{V}^{\top}
. Then
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\bm{A})=\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top}
and
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\bm{B})=\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Lambda}}\bm{V}^{\top}
. (
15
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\|\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top}-\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Lambda}}\bm{V}^{\top}\|_{F}^{2}{\leqslant}\|\bm{U}\bm{\Gamma}\bm{V}^{\top}{-}\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{V}^{\top}\|_{F}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[(\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top}{-}\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Lambda}}\bm{V}^{\top})^{\top}(\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top}{-}\bm{U}\check{\bm{\Lambda}}\bm{V}^{\top})]

\displaystyle{\leqslant}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[(\bm{U}{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top}{-}\bm{U}{\bm{\Lambda}}\bm{V}^{\top})^{\top}(\bm{U}{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top}{-}\bm{U}{\bm{\Lambda}}\bm{V}^{\top})].\qquad\quad

(16)

Since
\bm{U}
and
\bm{V}
are orthonormal bases of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle\|\bm{U}{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top}\|_{F}^{2}=\mathrm{tr}(\bm{V}{\bm{\Gamma}}^{\top}\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{U}{\bm{\Gamma}}\bm{V}^{\top})=\mathrm{tr}(\bm{V}{\bm{\Gamma}}^{\top}\bm{\Gamma}\bm{V}^{\top})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\bm{V}^{\top}\bm{V}{\bm{\Gamma}}^{\top}\bm{\Gamma})=\mathrm{tr}({\bm{\Gamma}}^{\top}\bm{\Gamma})=\|\bm{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}.\qquad\quad

(17)

By similar transformations, (
3.2

\displaystyle\|\check{\bm{\Gamma}}\|_{F}^{2}{+}\|\check{\bm{\Lambda}}\|_{F}^{2}{-}2\mathrm{tr}(\check{\bm{\Lambda}}\check{\bm{\Gamma}}){\leqslant}\|{\bm{\Gamma}}\|_{F}^{2}{+}\|{\bm{\Lambda}}\|_{F}^{2}{-}2\mathrm{tr}({\bm{\Lambda}}^{\top}{\bm{\Gamma}})

(18)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}(\check{\gamma_{i}}-\check{\lambda_{i}})^{2}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\gamma_{ij}-\lambda_{ij})^{2}.

(19)

(
19
) will hold if

\displaystyle|\check{\gamma_{i}}-\check{\lambda_{i}}|\leqslant|\gamma_{ii}-\lambda_{ii}|,\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

(20)

(
2
) indicates that
\check{\gamma_{i}}
and
\check{\lambda_{i}}
are one-dimensional projections of
\gamma_{ii}
and
\lambda_{ii}
onto the compact interval

, respectively. Such one-dimensional projection is non-expansive, which means that (

) holds. Back from (

) to (

),
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
is non-expansive.
∎

Now
\tilde{\bm{L}}^{k}
can be projected onto
\Omega
by

\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}(\tilde{\bm{L}}^{(k)})=\bm{U}\hat{\bm{\Lambda}}\bm{V}^{\top}.

(21)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)}):=\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}\circ\mathrm{prox}_{\beta\eta\|\cdot\|_{*}}\circ\mathscr{G}(\bm{L}^{(k)}).

.

\mathscr{T}:\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

The proof is put in Supplementary

Proposition 5
.

There exists a fixed-point in
\Omega
for
\mathscr{T}
.

The proof is put in Supplementary
A.2
. Denote
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}:=\{\bm{A}\in\Omega:\mathscr{T}(\bm{A})=\bm{A}\}\neq\emptyset
as the fixed point set of
\mathscr{T}
on

. The next step is to develop a convergent algorithm with

. Given any initial point

, let
\bm{L}^{(1)}:=\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(0)})

. We develop a KM iteration with

\displaystyle\bm{L}^{(k+1)}:=(1-\theta)\bm{L}^{(k)}+\theta\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)}),\quad k=1,2,\cdots

(23)

Since

,

, and

) implies

4.2
Sharpe Ratio

The Sharpe ratio (SR,
Sharpe 1966
) is a widely-used evaluating metric for the risk-adjusted return of a trading strategy. It can be calculated as follows in the framework of this paper:
\mathrm{SR}:=\frac{\mathrm{MR}-{r}_{\mathfrak{f}}}{\hat{s}({r}_{t})}
, where
{r}_{\mathfrak{f}}
is the average risk-free return during the investment, and
\hat{s}(\cdot)
is the sample standard deviation operator. The SR need not be annualized and this does not affect the assessment.
{r}_{\mathfrak{f}}
is given on the FF25 data sets but not given on MSCI, Stoxx50, FOF, and FTSE100. Hence we set
{r}_{\mathfrak{f}}=0
for the latter
4
data sets. The SRs of different trading strategies are shown in Table
1
. Again, SDCP, LTP-SF, and mSSRM-PGA have similar bad performance due to ineffective investing schemes. LTP-PP outperforms LTP-CF on FF25BM and FF25MEINV, but it still performs badly on Stoxx50 and FTSE100. It indicates that the first few PPs may not be robust enough to different financial circumstances.

LTPSS achieves the best SRs on all the data sets, especially on Stoxx50, FOF and FTSE100 where it keeps up robust performance. The advantage of LTPSS over LTP-CF and LTP-PP is large on Stoxx50. These results indicate that LTPSS is effective and robust in balancing investing return and risk.

4.3
Information Ratio

In finance, it is conventional to factorize the return of a trading strategy for in-depth analysis of its composition. Following
Kelly et al. 2023a
, we adopt the following Fama-French five-factor model:

\displaystyle{r}_{t}=

\displaystyle\alpha+\zeta_{0}{r}_{SF,t}+\zeta_{1}MKT_{t}+\zeta_{2}SMB_{t}+\zeta_{3}HML_{t}

\displaystyle+\zeta_{4}RMW_{t}+\zeta_{5}CMA_{t}+\varepsilon_{t},

(34)

where
{r}_{t}
and
{r}_{SF,t}
denote the returns on the
t
-th trading time of a trading strategy and the LTP-SF strategy, respectively. MKT (market), SMB (size), HML (value), RMW (profitability), and CMA (investment) denote the Fama-French five factors.
\zeta_{0}\sim\zeta_{5}
are the coefficients of the corresponding factors.
\varepsilon_{t}
is the residue for this regression model.
\alpha

Lintner 1965
can be seen as the pure return of the trading strategy excluding all the above-mentioned factors, which will be further investigated in Section
4.4
. After conducting a regression for (
4.3
), estimations for
\hat{\alpha}
and
\hat{s}(\varepsilon_{t})
can be obtained. The information ratio (IR,
Treynor and Black 1973
) is another widely-used
risk-adjusted return:
\mathrm{IR}:=\frac{\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{s}(\varepsilon_{t})}
. It can be seen as the pure SR hedging out all the above-mentioned factors. Note that the return of LTP-SF has also been hedged out in (
4.3
), thus LTP-SF does not have IRs itself. As for other trading strategies, a positive IR can be roughly considered as a better performance than LTP-SF. Table
1
shows the IRs of different trading strategies. LTP-PP performs well on the FF25 data sets but deteriorates on MSCI, Stoxx50, and FTSE100. In comparison, LTPSS outperforms LTP-CF and LTP-PP on all the data sets, especially on MSCI and Stoxx50 where it achieves effective and robust performance. These results also show its good ability in balancing return and risk.

4.4
\alpha
Factor

\alpha
represents the pure return of a trading strategy based on the market-neutral perspective, since it hedges out all the market-related factors. It measures the intrinsic return that is brought by the traded assets themselves instead of the market volatility. It is widely employed to evaluate the effectiveness of a trading strategy despite the market effect. Along with the regression for (
4.3
), a left-tailed t-test can be conducted to obtain the p-value for the corresponding
\alpha
. Both
\alpha
s and p-values of different trading strategies are presented in Table
1
. SDCP and mSSRM-PGA perform badly on all the data sets. Similar to the results of IR, LTP-PP performs badly on MSCI, Stoxx50, and FTSE100. In comparison, LTPSS achieves the best performance on all the data sets. Hence LTPSS is effective in achieving pure returns.

4.5
Maximum Drawdown

The maximum drawdown (MDD,
Magdon-Ismail and Atiya 2004
) measures the maximum percentage loss of wealth from a past peak to a past valley for a PO method, which corresponds to the worst case in the investment. It is mainly treated by a back-end risk control mechanism, such as setting stop loss orders. For a reference purpose, we present MDDs of different trading strategies in Table
1
. LTPSS achieves competitive MDDs, roughly between those of LTP-PP and LTP-CF. Since LTPSS achieves the best performance in most cases with other evaluating metrics, it is an effective method considering both return and risk.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we propose a linear trading position with sparse spectrum (LTPSS) that can explore a larger spectral region of the prediction matrix and extract key features from it. Our approach improves on the existing principal portfolio (PP) strategy that it allows for all the PPs with flexible spectral energies. We develop a Krasnosel’skiĭ-Mann fixed-point algorithm to solve the proposed optimization model, which is able to handle the complicated geometric structure of the constraint and the nuclear norm regularization. It also guarantees a linear convergence and the descent property of the objective values. Experimental results show that LTPSS achieves competitive and robust investing performance in diverse financial circumstances according to some common evaluating metrics.

A direct impact of LTPSS is to provide a novel trading strategy for the interdiscipline of machine learning and financial technology. It is highly interpretable and understandable in the general framework of finance, as well as tractable and reliable with theoretical guarantees. It can enrich the factor library for quantitative research, or reveal some intrinsic patterns of the asset pool. A possible limitation is that LTPSS may not be applied to nonlinear trading frameworks, which is partly due to the lack of such trading frameworks in finance. As for future works, we can extend LTPSS to more general factor trading architectures, or develop a nonlinear trading position that has a stronger signal processing ability. Exploring the relationship between PPs and conventional financial factors is also an interesting topic.

Proof.

We need to prove that
\forall\bm{A},\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
\|\mathscr{T}(\bm{A})-\mathscr{T}(\bm{B})\|_{F}\leqslant\|\bm{A}-\bm{B}\|_{F}

\mathscr{G}

\mathrm{prox}_{\beta\eta\|\cdot\|_{*}}
, it is obvious that
\beta\eta\|\cdot\|_{*}
is a convex function because

\displaystyle\|\theta\bm{A}+(1-\theta)\bm{B}\|_{*}\leqslant\theta\|\bm{A}\|_{*}+(1-\theta)\|\bm{B}\|_{*},\qquad\forall\bm{A},\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N},\ \forall\theta\in[0,1].

Then according to
Moreau 1965
,
\mathrm{prox}_{\beta\eta\|\cdot\|_{*}}
is a firmly non-expansive operator, which is also non-expansive. Next, Theorem
3
indicates that
\mathrm{proj}_{\Omega}
is also a non-expansive operator. Finally,
\mathscr{T}
is non-expansive as composed by three non-expansive operators.
∎

\Omega

\mathscr{T}

A.3
Proof of Theorem
6

The general framework for the convergence of KM algorithms originates from
Krasnosel’skiĭ 1955

and
Mann 1953
. We follow their framework in our special problem. First, we take any fixed point
\bm{L}^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
and examine its distances to two successive iterates
\bm{L}^{(k)}
and
\bm{L}^{(k+1)}
. To do this, we need the following equality for the inner product:

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{tr}((\bm{A}-\bm{B})^{\top}(\bm{B}-\bm{C}))=\|\bm{A}-\bm{C}\|_{F}^{2}-\|\bm{A}-\bm{B}\|_{F}^{2}-\|\bm{B}-\bm{C}\|_{F}^{2},\quad\forall\bm{A},\bm{B},\bm{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N},

(35)

which is easy to verify. Then,

\displaystyle\|\bm{L}^{(k+1)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(1-\theta)\bm{L}^{(k)}+\theta\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})-((1-\theta)+\theta)\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(1-\theta)(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet})+\theta(\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})-\bm{L}^{\bullet})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(1-\theta)(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet})\|_{F}^{2}+\|\theta(\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})-\bm{L}^{\bullet})\|_{F}^{2}+2(1-\theta)\theta\mathrm{tr}((\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet})^{\top}(\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})-\bm{L}^{\bullet}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(1-\theta)(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet})\|_{F}^{2}+\|\theta(\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})-\bm{L}^{\bullet})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle-(1-\theta)\theta(\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}-\|\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}-\|\bm{L}^{\bullet}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\|\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}+\theta\|\bm{L}^{\bullet}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}-(1-\theta)\theta\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\|\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}+\theta\|\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{\bullet})-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}-(1-\theta)\theta\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\|\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}+\theta\|\bm{L}^{\bullet}-\bm{L}^{(k)}\|_{F}^{2}-(1-\theta)\theta\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}-(1-\theta)\theta\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}.

The inequality holds since
\mathscr{T}
is non-expansive. Since
\theta\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\bm{L}^{(k+1)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}\leqslant\|\bm{L}^{(k)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2},\quad\forall k\geqslant 1.

(37)

Moreover,
\bm{L}^{\bullet}
is arbitrary in
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
. Hence
\{\bm{L}^{(k)}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
is called a
Fejér monotone
sequence
Bauschke and
Combettes 2017
w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
. It has a useful property that
\bm{L}^{(k+1)}
will not get strictly farther than
\bm{L}^{(k)}
from any point in
\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
. Therefore,
\{\bm{L}^{(k)}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
is a bounded sequence.

Summing up both sides of (
A.3
) from
k=1
to
k=m
yields

\displaystyle\|\bm{L}^{(m+1)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}\leqslant\|\bm{L}^{(1)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}-(1-\theta)\theta\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2},\quad\forall m\geqslant 1.

(38)

Hence,

\displaystyle(1-\theta)\theta\sum_{k=1}^{m}\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}\leqslant\|\bm{L}^{(1)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2},\quad\forall m\geqslant 1.

(39)

Letting

yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{(1-\theta)\theta}\|\bm{L}^{(1)}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}<\infty.

(40)

Therefore,
\|(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)})\|_{F}\rightarrow 0
as

. In other words,
(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)}))
converges to
\mathbf{0}_{N\times N}
.

Since
\{\bm{L}^{(k)}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
is a bounded sequence, it has at least one cluster point
\bm{L}^{*}
. Then there exists a subsequence
\{\bm{L}^{(k_{j})}\}
that converges to
\bm{L}^{*}
. Since
\mathscr{T}
is continuous,

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k_{j})})=\mathscr{T}(\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bm{L}^{(k_{j})})=\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{*}).

(41)

Since
(\bm{L}^{(k)}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k)}))
converges to
\mathbf{0}_{N\times N}

(\bm{L}^{(k_{j})}-\mathscr{T}(\bm{L}^{(k_{j})}))

\mathbf{0}_{N\times N}

\bm{L}^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}

\bm{L}^{*}
is arbitrary, thus the above deduction implies that every cluster point
\bm{L}^{*}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}
.

The last step is to prove that

can have at most one cluster point. If not, denote

and
\bm{L}^{\bullet}
as two distinct cluster points of

. Then for any

\bm{L}^{*},\bm{L}^{\bullet}
or others),

is a monotonically non-increasing sequence with a lower bound
0
based on (
37

converges to a non-negative real number, and so does its subsequences:

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|\bm{L}^{(k_{j})}-\bm{L}^{\circ}\|_{F}^{2}=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\bm{L}^{(k_{m})}-\bm{L}^{\circ}\|_{F}^{2},\quad\forall\bm{L}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}.

(43)

Expanding both sides of (

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\|\bm{L}^{(k_{j})}\|_{F}^{2}-\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\|\bm{L}^{(k_{m})}\|_{F}^{2}=2\mathrm{tr}((\bm{L}^{*}-\bm{L}^{\bullet})^{\top}\bm{L}^{\circ}),\quad\forall\bm{L}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Omega}.

The left side of (

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{tr}((\bm{L}^{*}-\bm{L}^{\bullet})^{\top}\bm{L}^{*})=2\mathrm{tr}((\bm{L}^{*}-\bm{L}^{\bullet})^{\top}\bm{L}^{\bullet})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\|\bm{L}^{*}-\bm{L}^{\bullet}\|_{F}^{2}=0.

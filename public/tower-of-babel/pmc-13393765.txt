Impaired left atrial function increases the risk of incident atrial fibrillation and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction in hypertensive patients
Authors: Yin Ai, Songbing Long, lin He, Jiang Li, Wang Xu, Jianbo Yue, Jin Luo, Jian Yang, Xiangjiang Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeLeft atrial (LA) function has emerged as an important marker of LA compliance and has been associated with adverse outcomes in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). This study aimed to evaluate the association between impaired LA function and the risk of incident atrial fibrillation (AF) and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF).HypothesisImpaired LA function is associated with an increased risk of incident AF and HFpEF.MethodsA total of 268 patients without a prior diagnosis of heart failure or atrial fibrillation were prospectively enrolled according to predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria.ResultsDuring a mean follow-up of 36 ± 3 months, 79 patients (29.48%) developed AF and 69 patients (25.75%) developed HFpEF, including 23 patients (8.6%) who developed both conditions. Compared with patients who remained free of both conditions, those who developed AF and/or HFpEF exhibited impaired LA function, reflected by lower LATEF, LAPEF, LAAEF, and LAEI, as well as a higher E/e′ ratio (all P < 0.05).In multivariable Cox regression analyses, LAD, E/e′ ratio, interventricular septal thickness (IVS), and LATEF were independently associated with incident AF. Similarly, E/e′ ratio, LATEF, LAAEF, and IVS were independently associated with incident HFpEF.Patients with lower LATEF had higher risks of all-cause mortality and stroke during follow-up.ConclusionImpaired LA function is associated with an increased risk of AF and HFpEF. Reduced LATEF is independently associated with these outcomes and is further associated with adverse clinical events.Keywords: Left atrial function, Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, Atrial fibrillation, Left atrial emptying fraction, Long-term follow-up

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia and represents a major contributor to cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) accounts for approximately half of all heart failure cases [1], yet effective diagnostic and therapeutic strategies remain limited. Increasing evidence suggests that left atrial (LA) dysfunction plays a central role in the pathophysiology of HFpEF. In addition, AF—one of the most common comorbidities in HFpEF—is closely linked to LA structural and functional remodeling. Traditionally, HFpEF has been attributed to left ventricular diastolic dysfunction and increased myocardial stiffness. However, this paradigm has evolved to incorporate the role of the left atrium as a key mediator of disease progression [2–3]. Studies have shown that left atrial cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and microvascular dysfunction are already present in the early stages of HFpEF, supporting the concept of a global atrial remodeling model.

In recent years, the concept of atrial cardiomyopathy (atrial myopathy) has emerged as a unifying framework linking atrial structural, functional, and electrophysiological remodeling with the development of both AF and HFpEF. From this perspective, impaired LA function may represent an early marker of atrial disease rather than merely a consequence of ventricular dysfunction [4]. Although LA functional parameters have been associated with adverse outcomes in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction [5], prospective data evaluating their role in predicting incident AF and HFpEF, particularly in hypertensive populations, remain limited. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the association between LA functional parameters and the risk of incident AF and HFpEF in a prospective cohort of patients with hypertension.

Methods

Patient population

This study was based on a single-center prospective cohort derived from the “Pharmaco-omics-Based Individualized Treatment of Hypertension” study conducted at Shaoyang Central Hospital between July 2021 and March 2022. The study flowchart is presented in Fig. 1. Clinical trial number: not applicable, as this study is an observational cohort study. A total of 688 participants were initially screened. Patients with established HFpEF at baseline (n = 266) were excluded according to the 2016 European Society of Cardiology guidelines. Among the remaining patients, additional exclusion criteria included severe valvular heart disease (n = 6), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (n = 9), permanent pacemaker implantation (n = 7), inadequate echocardiographic image quality (n = 46), and documented atrial fibrillation (n = 86). After applying predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 268 patients without prior heart failure or atrial fibrillation were included in the final analysis. A consecutive enrollment strategy was adopted, and no formal sample size calculation was performed. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shaoyang Central Hospital, and all participants provided written informed consent. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Flowchart of patient selection. A total of 688 patients with hypertension were screened between 2021 and 2022. Patients with HFpEF at baseline were excluded (n = 266). Among the remaining non-HFpEF patients, additional exclusions were applied due to severe valvular heart disease (n = 6), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (n = 9), permanent pacemaker implantation (n = 7), inadequate echocardiographic image quality (n = 46), and atrial fibrillation at baseline (n = 86). The final study population consisted of 268 patients, who were followed for a mean of 36 ± 2 months for incident AF and/or HFpEF

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was a composite of incident AF and/or HFpEF during follow-up. AF and HFpEF were also analyzed separately as secondary endpoints. The composite endpoint was defined a priori based on shared pathophysiological mechanisms related to left atrial dysfunction. Secondary endpoints included all-cause mortality and incident stroke.

Clinical data collection

Baseline data included demographic characteristics, comorbidities, medication use, vital signs, and laboratory parameters. Laboratory measurements included serum creatinine, B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP), N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), lipid profile, and fasting glucose. All participants underwent standard electrocardiography at baseline.

Echocardiographic assessment

Transthoracic echocardiography was performed using a commercially available ultrasound system (EPIC 7 C, Philips Medical Systems, Andover, MA, USA). All images were analyzed according to the recommendations of the American Society of Echocardiography [6].

Standard measurements included left ventricular (LV) volumes, LV ejection fraction (LVEF), mitral inflow velocities, and mitral annular early diastolic velocity (e′). LVEF was calculated using the biplane Simpson method.

Left atrial (LA) function was assessed using volumetric parameters. LA reservoir function was evaluated by left atrial total emptying fraction (LATEF), conduit function by passive emptying fraction (LAPEF), and pump function by active emptying fraction (LAAEF). The left atrial expansion index (LAEI) was calculated as (LAVmax − LAVmin) / LAVmin.

Outcome and follow-up

HFpEF was defined according to the 2016 European Society of Cardiology guidelines, including symptoms and/or signs of heart failure, elevated natriuretic peptides (BNP > 35 pg/mL or NT-proBNP > 125 pg/mL), and preserved LVEF (≥ 50%) with evidence of diastolic dysfunction—defined as a ratio of early transmitral flow velocity to early diastolic mitral annular velocity (E/e’) > 15—and/or left atrial enlargement, indicated by a left atrial volume index (LAVI) > 34 mL/m².

Participants were followed at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months after enrollment and annually thereafter. Clinical evaluation included physical examination, laboratory testing, and electrocardiography.

AF was diagnosed based on ECG or Holter monitoring demonstrating typical features of atrial fibrillation. Although multiple surveillance methods were used, AF detection relied on intermittent monitoring rather than continuous rhythm surveillance. Patients presenting with AF-related symptoms, such as palpitations, were advised to undergo prompt evaluation with a standard ECG or 24-hour Holter monitoring at a nearby medical facility. Asymptomatic patients underwent auscultation by outpatient physicians at the 6-, 12-, 24-, and 36-month follow-up visits.

Medical therapy

Antihypertensive medications were adjusted based on the patient’s blood pressure control, with additional diagnostic evaluations and treatment modifications made as clinically indicated. HFpEF management and treatment strategies were implemented in accordance with the prevailing Chinese HF guideline recommendations at the time (2018 editions) [7]. These strategies primarily involved the use of diuretics for patients exhibiting signs of fluid retention, as well as targeted interventions for underlying comorbidities and complications. Overall, these approaches were largely aligned with the contemporaneous recommendations of the European Society of Cardiology HF guidelines [8].

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate, and categorical variables as percentages. Group comparisons were performed using the independent t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. The associations between clinical variables and outcomes were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression models. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Separate models were constructed for the composite endpoint, AF, and HFpEF. Variables included in multivariable models were selected based on clinical relevance and statistical significance in univariable analyses (P < 0.05). Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIFs), with values < 10 considered indicative of no significant multicollinearity.Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated and compared using the log-rank test. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 24.0, GraphPad Prism version 9.0, and R software (version 4.3.0). A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics and demographics

A total of 268 hypertensive patients were included in the final analysis. During a median follow-up of 36 months (interquartile range: 32–37 months), 79 patients (29.48%) developed AF, and 69 patients (25.75%) developed HFpEF, including 23 patients (8.6%) who developed both conditions, they were classified into the AF-HFpEF group. The remaining patients who did not develop either AF or HFpEF were classified into the NAF-HFpEF group. The AF-HFpEF group exhibited a significantly higher median age compared with the NAF-HFpEF group (64.20 ± 7.23 years vs. 62.24 ± 7.34 years, P = 0.03). Additionally, the duration of hypertension was significantly longer in the AF-HFpEF group (41.27 ± 12.30 months vs. 37.46 ± 17.19 months, P = 0.04). No significant differences were observed between the two groups in other baseline characteristics, including the gender, body mass index (BMI), prevalence of diabetes mellitus, resting heart rate, prevalence of sleep apnea, presence of carotid plaques, or estimated glomerular filtration rate.

Furthermore, there were no significant differences between the groups regarding medication history. Similarly, no statistically significant differences were found in the prevalence of underlying cardiac conditions, including hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, dilated cardiomyopathy, and coronary artery disease.

In terms of cardiac function, patients in the AF-HFpEF group exhibited impaired atrial function compared to those in the NAF-HFpEF group, as reflected by significantly lower values of LATEF (60.28 ± 3.40 vs. 62.94 ± 3.33, P < 0.001), LAPEF (40.48 ± 1.99 vs. 41.11 ± 1.88, P = 0.008), LAAEF (43.74 ± 2.25 vs. 44.75 ± 2.03, P < 0.001), and LAEI (202.32 ± 5.99 vs. 205.36 ± 6.12, P < 0.001). In addition, the AF-HFpEF group had a significantly higher E/e′ ratio (9.89 ± 2.15 vs. 8.64 ± 1.27, P < 0.001), indicating elevated left ventricular filling pressures. With respect to cardiac structure, the AF-HFpEF group had significantly larger left atrial diameter (LAD) (35.62 ± 2.14 mm vs. 34.86 ± 1.66 mm, P = 0.001) and left atrial volume index (LAVI) (30.56 ± 1.71 mL/m² vs. 29.99 ± 1.45 mL/m², P = 0.004) compared to the NAF-HFpEF group. Furthermore, interventricular septal thickness was also significantly greater in the AF-HFpEF group (10.77 ± 1.64 mm vs. 9.50 ± 1.27 mm, P < 0.001). However, no significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of LVEF or left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD).

Multivariable cox regression analyses

Multivariable Cox regression analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of AF and HFpEF. Prior to model construction, multicollinearity among candidate variables was assessed using variance inflation factors, and no significant collinearity was observed (all VIFs < 10). As shown in Fig. 2A, Cox regression analysis identified left atrial diameter (LAD) (HR = 1.32; 95% CI: 1.01–1.72; P = 0.04), E/e′ ratio (HR = 1.16; 95% CI: 1.01–1.33; P = 0.035), interventricular septal thickness (IVS) (HR = 1.25; 95% CI: 1.08–1.46; P < 0.001), and LATEF (HR = 0.86; 95% CI: 0.78–0.95; P = 0.004) as independent predictors of AF occurrence. Similarly, as illustrated in Fig. 2B, E/e′ ratio (HR = 1.18; 95% CI: 1.03–1.35; P = 0.014), LATEF (HR = 0.88; 95% CI: 0.78–0.98; P = 0.025), LAAEF (HR = 0.72; 95% CI: 0.55–0.93; P = 0.011), and interventricular septal thickness (IVS) (HR = 1.42; 95% CI: 1.20–1.67; P < 0.001) were independently associated with the development of HFpEF. Notably, the associations between left atrial functional parameters and outcomes were consistent across analyses of the composite endpoint and the individual endpoints.

LATEF and clinical outcomes

Patients were stratified according to LATEF levels. Those with lower LATEF had a higher incidence of all-cause mortality (4/126 vs. 13/142, P = 0.049) (Fig. 4A) and stroke (4/126 vs. 19/142, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

Main findings

In this prospective cohort study, impaired LA function was associated with an increased risk of incident atrial AF and HFpEF in hypertensive patients. Reduced LATEF was independently associated with both outcomes and was further associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality and stroke. In addition, interventricular septal thickness was independently associated with the development of both AF and HFpEF.

LA function and HFpEF

The left atrium plays a critical role in maintaining ventricular filling and overall cardiovascular performance through its reservoir, conduit, and booster pump functions [9]. Impairment of these phasic functions may contribute to the development and progression of HFpEF [10].

Consistent with previous studies [11–14], our findings suggest that impaired LA function is associated with an increased risk of incident HFpEF in hypertensive individuals. After adjustment for potential confounders, both LATEF and LAAEF remained independently associated with HFpEF, supporting the importance of LA reservoir and pump function in disease development. Specifically, each 1% decrease in LATEF was associated with a 12% higher risk of developing HFpEF, while each 1% decrease in LAAEF corresponded to a 28% increase in HFpEF risk.

The relatively high incidence of HFpEF observed in this study may be explained by the characteristics of the study population, which consisted predominantly of patients with hypertension, Hypertension is present in approximately 60% to 89% of patients with HFpEF [15],for every 10-mmHg increase in systolic blood pressure above normal levels, the risk of developing HFpEF rises by a factor of 1.13 (HR = 1.13, 95% CI: 1.04–1.22), which is notably higher than the corresponding risk for HFrEF [16].many of whom had long-standing disease and additional cardiovascular risk factors. In addition, the use of comprehensive diagnostic assessments may have facilitated the detection of HFpEF, including at earlier or subclinical stages. These factors may limit the generalizability of the findings to broader populations.

LA function, AF, and atrial myopathy

Hypertension is a well-established risk factor for atrial fibrillation, and a substantial proportion of patients with AF have underlying hypertensive heart disease. A Previous study has shown that with diagnosis of hypertension or taking antihypertensive medication increased the likelihood of AF by 73% (OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.31 to 2.28) [17]. Conversely, hypertension was present in > 70% of AF patients in epidemiological studies [18] and recent AF real-world registries [19], and in 49–90% of patients in randomized AF trials [20]. These findings indicate that individuals with hypertension are at a substantially elevated risk of developing AF. In the present study, the incidence of AF was relatively high, which may be attributable to the high-risk profile of the study population, the duration of follow-up, and the use of multiple surveillance methods that enhanced event detection.

In this context, the observed structural and functional alterations of the left atrium may be interpreted within the framework of atrial cardiomyopathy (atrial myopathy), a condition characterized by structural, functional, and electrophysiological remodeling of the atrium [21]. Atrial myopathy may precede the onset of AF and provide a substrate for its initiation, while AF itself may further promote atrial remodeling, resulting in a self-perpetuating cycle. Accordingly, LA functional parameters assessed in this study may serve as noninvasive markers of underlying atrial myopathy, providing a mechanistic link between hypertension, AF, and HFpEF. While conventional volumetric parameters were used in the present study, deformation-based indices such as LA strain may offer additional sensitivity in detecting early atrial dysfunction. Future studies incorporating advanced imaging techniques are warranted to further refine risk stratification.

LA remodeling, valvular function, and broader implications

Beyond their association with AF and HFpEF, atrial structural and functional alterations may also contribute to valvular dysfunction. Emerging evidence suggests that hypertension and AF are associated with increased valvular disease severity even in patients with so-called non-valvular HFpEF [22]. This may be related to atrial remodeling and annular dilation, which can impair valvular coaptation and lead to functional regurgitation.

In this context, atrial myopathy may represent a unifying mechanism linking LA dysfunction, AF, HFpEF, and valvular abnormalities. The LA functional parameters assessed in this study may therefore reflect a broader spectrum of atrial-related cardiovascular remodeling.

Interpretation of effect size

Multiple studies have demonstrated that echocardiographic parameters—including left atrial anteroposterior diameter, maximal and minimal left atrial volumes, and left atrial emptying fraction—are significantly associated with the incidence of atrial fibrillation in community-based populations [23–25]. Our study demonstrated that the risk of incident atrial fibrillation increased significantly when the left atrial diameter exceeded 35 mm. Specifically, each 1-mm increase in left atrial diameter was associated with a 32% higher risk of atrial fibrillation. Notably, the observed magnitude of risk increase per millimeter was relatively high and should be interpreted with caution. This may in part reflect the high-risk characteristics of our predominantly hypertensive study population, as well as potential residual confounding. In addition, effect estimates expressed per unit increase may appear numerically large depending on the scale of the variable. Therefore, the present findings should be interpreted primarily in terms of the direction and consistency of the associations rather than the absolute magnitude of effect. In participants of the community-based Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA), a higher maximum left atrial volume index measured by cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (HR = 1.38; P = 0.042) and a lower left atrial emptying fraction (HR = 0.70; P = 0.020) were both significantly associated with an increased risk of incident atrial fibrillation. These associations remained statistically significant after adjustment for clinical risk factors and left ventricular mass [26]. Our findings further demonstrated that a reduction in LATEF was associated with an increased risk of incident AF, with each 1% decrease in LATEF corresponding to a 14% increase in AF risk. Although left atrial size has been consistently associated with the development of AF, the inclusion of LAD in clinical risk models has not been shown to significantly improve discriminatory performance. In contrast, the incremental predictive value of incorporating maximal LAVI or left atrial emptying fraction into established clinical risk scores has not yet been well established [26, 27]. In our study, univariable analysis revealed a modest association between LAVI and incident AF. However, after adjustment for potential confounders, LAVI was no longer independently associated with AF. This finding may be attributable to the fact that all enrolled participants had hypertension, with varying degrees of left atrial enlargement, thereby limiting the ability of LAVI to discriminate AF risk within this relatively homogeneous population.

LATEF and clinical outcomes

Left atrial reservoir function has been increasingly recognized as an important prognostic marker across various cardiovascular conditions [28–30]. In the present study, reduced LATEF was associated with a higher risk of all-cause mortality and stroke, suggesting its potential role as an early indicator of adverse outcomes in hypertensive patients.

The underlying mechanisms may involve progressive impairment of atrial compliance and elevated filling pressures, which can contribute to pulmonary congestion and hemodynamic instability. These findings further support the clinical relevance of LA functional assessment beyond arrhythmia and heart failure risk.

Interventricular Septal Hypertrophy and AF and HFpEF

Several studies have reported an association between interventricular septal hypertrophy and an increased risk of adverse outcomes [31–34]. Consistent with these prior findings, our study shows that in hypertensive patients, interventricular septal hypertrophy significantly increases the risk of AF and HFpEF, with each 1 mm increment in septal thickness corresponding to a 25% higher risk of AF and a 42% higher risk of HFpEF. The strong association between septal hypertrophy and adverse outcomes observed across multiple studies underscores the clinical significance of septal thickness measured by echocardiography as a key risk factor, emphasizing its utility for improved risk stratification and prognostication.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center observational study with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, the study population consisted exclusively of hypertensive patients, and caution is warranted when extrapolating the results to other populations.

Third, although multiple surveillance methods were used, AF detection relied on intermittent monitoring rather than continuous rhythm surveillance. As a result, paroxysmal or asymptomatic AF episodes may have been missed, potentially leading to underestimation of AF incidence. Conversely, enhanced surveillance may also have increased detection rates, resulting in potential bidirectional bias.

Fourth, no formal sample size calculation was performed, and the number of events relative to the number of covariates may introduce a risk of overfitting. Finally, LA functional parameters were assessed using echocardiography without validation by other imaging modalities, and residual confounding cannot be entirely excluded. Although multicollinearity was assessed, the potential interdependence among echocardiographic parameters cannot be entirely excluded. Future studies could consider integrating additional imaging modalities and providing more visual examples to enhance interpretability and generalizability. Finally, as an observational study, causal relationships cannot be confirmed, and the conclusions drawn herein require validation in multicenter studies with larger cohorts and longer follow-up periods.

Conclusions

Impaired left atrial function is associated with an increased risk of atrial fibrillation and HFpEF in hypertensive patients. Reduced LATEF may serve as a noninvasive marker of atrial dysfunction and is associated with adverse clinical outcomes.

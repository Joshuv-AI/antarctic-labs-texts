Extreme non-differentiability of typical Lipschitz mappings
Michael Dymond, Olga Maleva

Abstract
. We show that no matter what subset of a normed space is given, a typical 1-Lipschitz mapping into a Banach space is non-differentiable at a typical point of the set in a very strong sense: the derivative ratio approximates, on arbitrary small scales, every linear operator of norm at most 1. For subsets of finite-dimensional normed spaces which can be covered by a countable union of closed purely unrectifiable sets this extreme non-differentiability holds for a typical Lipschitz mapping at every point. Both results are new even for Lipschitz mappings with a finite-dimensional co-domain.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to present a striking (non-)differentiability property of typical Lipschitz mappings. We show that a typical, in Baire category sense,
1
-Lipschitz mapping between a normed space
X
and a Banach space
Y
is not differentiable at a typical point of a given set
E\subseteq X
in a most extreme way: its derivative ratios can approximate all linear operators
X\to Y
of norm at most
1
. Moreover, if the dimension of
X
is finite and
E
is
F_{\sigma}
purely unrectifiable, the above holds for all (not just residually many) points of
E
.

Differentiability of Lipschitz mappings is the focus of mathematical research in an array of settings including Euclidean spaces (see e.g.
[
12
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
), Hilbert and Banach spaces (see e.g.
[
3
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
6
]
) and geodesic metric spaces (see e.g.
[
15
]
,
[
20
]
). A starting point for these investigations is Rademacher’s theorem, which guarantees that the set of non-differentiability points of
a Lipschitz mapping
\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
is of Lebesgue measure zero. Versions of Rademacher’s Theorem are also available beyond finite-dimensional spaces: under reasonable assumptions on Banach spaces
X,Y
, a Lipschitz mapping
X\to Y
is Gâteaux differentiable everywhere except an Aronszajn null set, see
[
3
, Theorem 6.42]
. Furthermore, a celebrated result by Preiss
[
21
]
says that a Lipschitz function defined on a Banach space
X
which is Asplund (i.e. every separable subspace has a separable dual) is differentiable on a dense subset of
X
. Thus, in many settings, Lipschitz mappings constitute a class of mappings which on the whole have good differentiability properties, but crucially have the flexibility of pathological behaviour on a null set. There are various notions of null or exceptional sets, to which this may refer, but even Lebesgue null sets in Euclidean spaces are a diverse class with important tools such as category and fractal dimension which distinguish between them.

Typical differentiability as an object of interest dates back to Banach’s famous 1931 result
[
2
, Satz 1]
that a typical continuous function on an interval is nowhere differentiable.
Such a result would be impossible for a typical Lipschitz mapping between Euclidean spaces, by Rademacher’s theorem. The extent to which a typical Lipschitz mapping is differentiable has been investigated recently in
[
22
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
19
]
.
Recall also
[
21
,
5
,
6
,
9
]
that Banach spaces with separable dual of dimension
2
or more have
universal differentiability sets
(UDS) which are “very small” but contain a point of differentiability of every
\mathbb{R}
-valued Lipschitz function.
One naturally asks how “big” is the set of points where a typical Lipschitz function is not differentiable.
In a sense, an opposite of UDS are sets where a typical
1
-Lipschitz
\mathbb{R}
-valued function is nowhere differentiable.
In
[
22
]
, the class of analytic subsets of
[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with this property has been shown to coincide with the
F_{\sigma}
null sets, and
[
10
]
extended this to the case of
[0,1]^{d}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
by showing that the relevant condition must be “
F_{\sigma}
purely unrectifiable.”
Against this background, the present paper achieves the following significant advances:

•

In Theorem
1.1
, we establish that inside any given set
S
the set of non-differentiability points of a typical Lipschitz mapping is a residual subset of
S
, no matter on what (bounded, normed) domain (containing
S
) the space of Lipschitz mappings is considered.
This holds for vector-valued (
Y
-valued) mappings. The norms on
X
and
Y
are arbitrary, as long as
Y
is Banach.
Non-differentiability can be strengthened to extreme non-differentiability which still holds at residually many points.

•

In Theorem
1.2
we determine that inside any given
F_{\sigma}
purely unrectifiable set, a typical
Y
-valued Lipschitz mapping is nowhere differentiable in the extreme sense.

Versions of these two results were known only in the special case of functions from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}
, see
[
17
, Theorem 4]
and
[
10
]
. We point out straight away that typical behaviour of scalar-valued Lipschitz functions has no direct implications for vector-valued mappings, even from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{l}
when
l>1
; see
[
10
, Theorem 6.1]
.
The results of the present paper also go significantly beyond the premises of
[
10
]
by
allowing arbitrary norms into consideration and replacing directional non-differentiability by extreme non-differentiability.

This article further acts as an Erratum to
[
10
, Remarks 2.9 and 3.18]
. The authors hereby retract these two remarks, which are shown to be invalid by the present article; see Corollary
2.2
. We further note that the content of those two remarks does not affect any of the results or indeed anything at all in the rest of the paper
[
10
]
.

1.1.
Main results.

Given a topological space
Z
, we say that a typical element of
Z
possesses a certain property if the collection of those elements of
Z
having that property forms a residual subset of
Z
. If
Z
is a complete metric space, then its residual subsets are dense in
Z
, by the Baire Category theorem, hence a condition satisfied by a typical element is satisfied by elements of a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of the space.

It is therefore important to note that the meaning of ‘typical’ depends on the ambient space. We thus need to clarify, as we do in Section
2.3
, whether a typical Lipschitz mapping is to be understood relative to the space of all Lipschitz mappings, or only those with Lipschitz constant bounded by
L
for certain
L>0
; whether the mappings are defined on the whole space or on a certain subset; and what topology (or metric) is used on the space of Lipschitz mappings.

We presently state the first main result of this paper; see Sections
2.1
and
2.3
for detailed explanation of the notation involved.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a normed space,
Y
be a Banach space,
W
be a separable subspace of
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
Q
be a bounded subset of
X
and
E\subseteq\operatorname{Int}Q
. Then there is a residual subset
\mathcal{F}
of
(\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty})
such that for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
the set

\mathcal{N}_{f,W}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in E\colon\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})\supseteq\mathbb{B}_{W}\right\}

is residual in
E
.

Here
\mathbb{B}_{W}
is the closed unit ball of
W
,
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is the space of bounded linear operators
X\to Y
and
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
is the collection of those operators which, in a specific sense, behave like a derivative of
f
at
\mathbf{x}
; for the precise definition of
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
see (
2.1
) below. The inclusion
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})\supseteq\mathbb{B}_{W}
for non-trivial
W
and points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{N}_{f,W}
in Theorem
1.1
implies, in particular, that
\mathcal{N}_{f,W}
is contained in the set of Gâteaux non-differentiability points of
f
. However, this condition should be interpreted as a very strong form of non-differentiability. We elaborate on this presently.

A particularly strong failure of differentiability of a mapping at a point, considered in
[
18
, Theorem 1.9]
, happens when many different linear mappings simultaneously behave like a derivative of the mapping. Note that if
f
is Gâteaux differentiable then either
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
is empty, or it is the singleton set containing only the Gâteaux derivative of
f
; if
f
is
Fréchet differentiable at
\mathbf{x}
, then
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})=\left\{Df(\mathbf{x})\right\}
.
Accordingly, the size of the set
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
is a measure of the severity of non-differentiability of
f
at
\mathbf{x}
. In the case when
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is separable (for example, when
X
is finite-dimensional and
Y
is separable), the most extreme form of non-differentiability of a
1
-Lipschitz
f
at
\mathbf{x}
occurs if
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}
, the closed unit ball of
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
.

When
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is non-separable, it is impossible to achieve
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}
, as we show, in Lemma
2.2
below, that
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
is always separable. Qualitatively, the strongest form of non-differentiability of a
1
-Lipschitz
f
that may hold in such case is
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})\supseteq\mathbb{B}_{W}
for an infinite-dimensional, separable subspace
W
of
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
. For any such
W
,
this is what we achieve for a typical
1
-Lipschitz
f
.

In light of
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
, concerning non-differentiability of a typical (real-valued) Lipschitz function at every point of an arbitrary fixed
F_{\sigma}
purely unrectifiable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, one asks if the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
, where the settings are much more general, can be strengthened for such sets. We answer this for finite-dimensional domains in the affirmative in our second main result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a finite-dimensional normed space,
Y
be a Banach space,
W
be a separable subspace of
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
Q\subseteq X
be bounded and
E\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(Q)
be an
F_{\sigma}
purely unrectifiable set.
Then
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})\supseteq\mathbb{B}_{W}
for a typical
f\in(\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty})
and every
\mathbf{x}\in E
.

Theorem
1.2
also strengthens previous results in this direction obtained in
[
10
, Theorem 2.7]
and in
[
19
]
: significant gains being that it caters for infinite-dimensional spaces
Y
, any norms on
X
and
Y
, as long as
Y
is Banach, and the derivative ratios ‘see’ all possible linear operators
T\in W
on arbitrarily small scales.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation.

2.1.
General notation and differentiability notions.

Given a normed vector space
X
, we let
\mathbb{B}_{X}
denote its closed unit ball and
\mathbb{S}_{X}
its unit sphere. An open ball in
X
with centre
\mathbf{x}
and radius
r
will be written as
B_{X}(\mathbf{x},r)
and for closed balls we write
\overline{B}_{X}
instead of
B_{X}
. The origin in
X
will be denoted by
\mathbf{0}_{X}
. If
Y
is an additional normed vector space, we let
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
denote the space of bounded linear operators
X\to Y
. The operator norm on
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is denoted by
\left\|-\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}
. For a subset
Q
of a topological space, we let
\operatorname{Int}Q
denote the interior of
Q
.
For a mapping
f\colon Q\subseteq X\to Y
and
\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{Int}Q
we let

(2.1)

\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x}):=\left\{T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)\colon\liminf_{r\to 0+}\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in\overline{B}(\mathbf{0}_{X},r)}\frac{\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-T\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}}{r}=0\right\}.

Observe that if
f
is
1
-Lipschitz, we have
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{Int}Q
.

Finally, we will refer to a subset
\Gamma
of a metric space
(M,d)
as
uniformly separated
if
\inf\left\{d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\colon\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma,\,\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\right\}>0
. For such a set
\Gamma
and
s>0
, we call
\Gamma

s
-separated
if
\inf\left\{d(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\colon\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma,\,\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\right\}\geq s
.

2.2.
Optimality of Theorem
1.1
and comparison with previous results.

Taking
W
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
as any non-trivial, separable subspace of
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
ensures that the set
\mathcal{N}_{f,W}
is contained in the set of points of Gâteaux non-differentiability of
f
. Moreover, when
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is itself separable, we may take
W=\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
. In this case we get that for a typical
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y)
the set
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
is maximum possible, as it is equal to
\mathbb{B}_{W}=\mathbb{B}_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}
, at a typical point
\mathbf{x}
of
E
(and at every
\mathbf{x}\in E
in the premises of Theorem
1.2
). In the following lemma we show that it is not possible to omit the separability condition on
W
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Lemma 2.0
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normed spaces,
Q\subseteq X
,
\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{Int}Q
and
f\colon Q\to Y
be a mapping. Then the set
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
is separable and closed in
(\mathcal{L}(X,Y),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\operatorname{op}})
.

Proof.

Let
r>0
be small enough so that
B_{X}(\mathbf{x},r)\subseteq Q
. For each rational
q\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,r)
and each
n\in\mathbb{N}
choose
{T}_{q,n}\in{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}
such that

\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in\overline{B}_{X}(\mathbf{0}_{X},q)}\frac{\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-{T}_{q,n}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}}{q}\\
\leq\inf_{{T}\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in\overline{B}_{X}(\mathbf{0}_{X},q)}\frac{\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-{T}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}}{q}+\frac{1}{n}.

We show that
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})\subseteq\overline{\left\{T_{q,n}\colon q\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,r),\,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}}
, where the closure is taken with respect to the operator norm.

Indeed, consider arbitrary
{T}_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
n>3/\varepsilon
and choose
q\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,r)
so that

\sup_{\mathbf{u}\in\overline{B}_{X}(\mathbf{0}_{X},q)}\frac{\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-{T}_{0}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}}{q}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Then, for every
\mathbf{u}\in\overline{B}_{X}(\mathbf{0}_{X},q)
we have

\displaystyle\left\|({T}_{q,n}-{T}_{0})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{T}_{q,n}\mathbf{u}+f(\mathbf{x})-f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-{T}_{0}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon q}{3}+\frac{q}{n}+\frac{\varepsilon q}{3}\leq\varepsilon q,

which implies
\left\|{T}_{q,n}-{T}_{0}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\varepsilon
.

To show that
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
is closed, assume
T_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
converge in the operator norm to
T_{0}
. Fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
and choose
n\geq 1
and
0<\rho<\varepsilon
such that
\left\|T_{n}-T_{0}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}<\varepsilon/2
and
\frac{1}{\rho}\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-T_{n}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}<\varepsilon/2
whenever
\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{X}\leq\rho
. Then for all
\mathbf{u}\in\overline{B}_{X}(\mathbf{0}_{X},\rho)

\tfrac{1}{\rho}\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-T_{0}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}\leq\tfrac{1}{\rho}\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-T_{n}\mathbf{u}\right\|_{Y}+\left\|T_{n}-T_{0}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\tfrac{\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{X}}{\rho}<\varepsilon.

From the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
we conclude
T_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
. ∎

Let us record a simple comparison, in the case of real-valued
f
, of the set
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
with the Dini subgradient
\hat{\partial}f(\mathbf{x})
of
f
at
\mathbf{x}
, considered in
[
17
]
. Let
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a function,
\mathbf{x}\in X
and for each
\mathbf{v}\in X
consider the lower Dini directional derivative

f_{+}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{v}):=\liminf_{t\to 0+}\frac{f(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{v})-f(\mathbf{x})}{t}.

The Dini subgradient of
f
at
\mathbf{x}
is then defined by

\hat{\partial}f(\mathbf{x}):=\left\{\mathbf{x}^{*}\in X^{*}\colon f_{+}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{v})\geq\langle\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{v}\rangle\,\forall\mathbf{v}\in X\right\}.

Lemma 2.0
.

Let
X
be a normed space,
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-Lipschitz function,
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}\in X
and
\mathbf{y}^{*},\mathbf{z}^{*}\in\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})

be such that

\mathbf{z}^{*}(\mathbf{v})<0<\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{v})
. Then
\hat{\partial}f(\mathbf{x})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathbf{z}^{*}\in\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbf{z}^{*}(\mathbf{v})<0
implies
f_{+}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{v})\leq\mathbf{z}^{*}(\mathbf{v})<0
. Similarly
\mathbf{y}^{*}\in\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
and
\mathbf{y}^{*}(-\mathbf{v})<0
implies
f_{+}(\mathbf{x};-\mathbf{v})<0
. Thus, we have
f_{+}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{v})<0
and
f_{+}(\mathbf{x};-\mathbf{v})<0
, which implies
\hat{\partial}f(\mathbf{x})=\emptyset
.
∎

Remark 2.0
.

Note that, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, given any
\mathbf{v}\in X\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}_{X}\right\}
, we may choose the functionals
\mathbf{y}^{*},\mathbf{z}^{*}
of norm
1
such that
\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{v})=\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{X}
and
\mathbf{z}^{*}(\mathbf{v})=-\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{X}
. Therefore, Lemma
2.2
may be applied whenever
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})\supseteq\mathbb{S}_{X^{*}}
, the unit sphere of
X^{*}
.

It can also be easily seen from the example of
f(\mathbf{x})=-\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
that a
1
-Lipschitz function may have

\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{0}_{X})=\hat{\partial}f(\mathbf{0}_{X})=\emptyset
. Hence, there is no reverse implication to the statement of Lemma
2.2
: emptiness of the Dini subgradient
\hat{\partial}f(\mathbf{x})
does not imply any type of largeness of the set
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})
.

Thus Theorem
1.1
, which proves
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\mathbf{x})\supseteq\mathbb{B}_{X^{*}}\supseteq\mathbb{S}_{X^{*}}
is stronger than
[
17
, Theorem 4]
even in the case when
X
is finite-dimensional and
Y=\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.0
.

We also note that Theorem
1.1
is stronger than results obtained in
[
17
]
in another aspect. The paper
[
17
]

requires
E
to be equal to the whole space
X
while Theorem
1.1
allows any
E\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(Q)
. This is crucial in order to refute
[
10
, Remarks 2.9 and 3.18]
discussed in the Introduction.
We do that in the next corollary.

Corollary 2.0
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
S\subseteq(0,1)^{d}
be arbitrary.
Then there is a residual subset
\mathcal{F}
of
\operatorname{Lip}_{1}([0,1]^{d},\mathbb{R})
such that for every
f\in\mathcal{F}
the set
of non-differentiability points of
f
in
S
is residual in
S
.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
1.1
to
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the Euclidean norm,
Y=\mathbb{R}
,
W=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
,
Q=[0,1]^{d}
and
E=S
.
∎

2.3.
Lipschitz mappings.

Given a metric space
Q
and a Banach space
Y
, we denote by
\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y)
the set of Lipschitz mappings
f\colon Q\to Y
with
\operatorname{Lip}(f)\leq 1
.
If
Q
is a bounded metric space, then
\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y)
is a closed subset of the Banach space
C_{b}(Q,Y)
of
Y
-valued continuous bounded functions on
Q
, with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\sup\left\{\left\|f(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\colon\mathbf{x}\in Q\right\}.

We require completeness of
(\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty})
in order for residual subsets of
\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y)
to be dense in
\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y)
, see Section
1.1
.

Note that if
X
is a normed space and
Q\subseteq X
is not bounded, one could still consider the space
\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y)

as a complete metric space with metric

\rho(f,g)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\min\left\{1,\left\|f|_{Q\cap n\mathbb{B}_{X}}-g|_{Q\cap n\mathbb{B}_{X}}\right\|_{\infty}\right\}.

This is the approach chosen in
[
17
]
. In the present work, we elect to work only with bounded
Q
in order to be consistent with the papers
[
22
]
and
[
10
]
. However, we note that
the proofs given in the present paper may be easily
modified to obtain the same results for
\operatorname{Lip}_{1}(Q,Y)
in the case
Q
is unbounded.

2.4.
The Banach-Mazur Game

To prove that a set is residual, i.e. a complement of the set of first Baire category, we will make use of the Banach-Mazur game, see
[
14
, 8.H]
.

Given a topological space
Z
and its subset
H\subseteq Z
, the Banach-Mazur game in
Z
with target
H
is played by two players, Player I and Player II, as follows: The game starts by Player I selecting a non-empty open subset
U_{1}
of
Z
. Player II must then respond by nominating a non-empty open subset
V_{1}
of
Z
with
V_{1}\subseteq U_{1}
. In the
k
-th round of the game, with
k\geq 2
, Player I chooses a non-empty open set
U_{k}\subseteq V_{k-1}
and Player II returns a non-empty open set
V_{k}\subseteq U_{k}
. Thus, a run of the game is described by an infinite sequence of open sets

U_{1}\supseteq V_{1}\supseteq U_{2}\supseteq V_{2}\supseteq\ldots\supseteq U_{k}\supseteq V_{k}\supseteq\ldots,

where the sets
U_{k}
are the choices of Player I and the sets
V_{k}
are those of Player II. Player II wins the game if

\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}V_{k}\subseteq H.

Otherwise Player I wins.

The Banach-Mazur game can be used to determine whether a subset of a topological space is residual. More precisely, for any non-empty topological space
Z
and any subset
H
of
Z
it holds that
H
is a residual subset of
Z
if and only if Player II has a winning strategy in the Banach Mazur game in
Z
with target set
H
; see
[
14
, Theorem 8.33]
.

In the case that
Z
is a metric space (as will be the case in our setting), open balls may be used in place of the open sets
U_{k}
and
V_{k}
above, see also
[
10
, Theorem 3.16]
. Thus, the moves of Player I and Player II effectively become a choice of pairs
(\mathbf{x},r)
where
\mathbf{x}\in Z
prescribes the centre of the ball and
r>0
the radius. In the special case when Player II is always able to ensure that the intersection
\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}V_{k}=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}B(y_{k},s_{k})
of their choices is a singleton
y_{0}\in Z

3.
A typical Lipschitz mapping is extremely non-differentiable at a typical point of a set

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
. For the proof of subsequent auxiliary lemmata we follow the convention that the infimum of the empty set is
+\infty
. We also note that in any normed space a bounded non-empty subset has a non-empty boundary.

The next lemma is a generalisation of
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
for normed spaces instead of convex sets.

Lemma 3.0
.

Let
X
and
Z
be normed spaces,
0<a<b
, and let
f_{1},f_{2}\colon X\to Z
be Lipschitz mappings such that
\operatorname{Lip}(f_{1})+\operatorname{Lip}(f_{2})\leq 1
and
f_{1}(\mathbf{0}_{X})=f_{2}(\mathbf{0}_{X})=\mathbf{0}_{Z}
.
Then there exists a Lipschitz mapping
\Phi=\Phi(a,b,f_{1},f_{2})\colon X\to Z
such that

(i)

\Phi(\mathbf{x})=f_{1}(\mathbf{x})
whenever
\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}\leq a
.

(ii)

\Phi(\mathbf{x})=f_{2}(\mathbf{x})
whenever
\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}\geq b
.

(iii)

\operatorname{Lip}(\Phi)\leq 1+\frac{a}{b-a}
.

(iv)

If
f_{1}=\mathbf{0}_{Z}
is the constant
\mathbf{0}_{Z}
mapping, then

\left\|\Phi(\mathbf{x})-f_{2}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}\leq a\operatorname{Lip}(f_{2})
for all
\mathbf{x}\in X
.

(v)

If
f_{2}=\mathbf{0}_{Z}
is the constant
\mathbf{0}_{Z}
mapping, then

\left\|\Phi(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}\leq b\operatorname{Lip}(f_{1})
for all
\mathbf{x}\in X
.

Proof.

Define
\Phi\colon X\to Z
by

\Phi(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}f_{1}(\mathbf{x})&\text{ if }\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}\leq a,\\
\displaystyle\frac{b-\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}f_{1}(\mathbf{x})+\frac{b\bigl(\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}-a\bigr)}{\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}(b-a)}f_{2}(\mathbf{x})&\text{ if }a<\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}<b,\\
f_{2}(\mathbf{x})&\text{ if }\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}\geq b.\end{cases}

Clearly,
\Phi
satisfies (
i
) and (
ii
).
Observe that
\Phi
is a continuous mapping
X\to Z
.
Moreover, since
f_{1},f_{2}\in\operatorname{Lip}_{1}
, in order to show that
\Phi
is Lipschitz and to check (
iii
), it is enough to verify

(3.1)

\left\|\Phi(\mathbf{y})-\Phi(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}\leq{\left(1+\frac{a}{b-a}\right)}\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}

whenever
\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X},\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\in(a,b)
. To show this, fix such
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in X
and note first that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi(\mathbf{y})-\Phi(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\tfrac{b-\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}f_{1}(\mathbf{y})-\tfrac{b-\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}

\displaystyle+\left\|\tfrac{b(\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}-a)}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}(b-a)}f_{2}(\mathbf{y})-\tfrac{b(\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}-a)}{\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}(b-a)}f_{2}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}.

In several estimates that follow we will use that

(3.3)

\left\|f_{i}(\mathbf{u})\right\|_{Z}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f_{i})\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{X}\qquad\text{for all $\mathbf{u}\in X$ and $i=1,2$.}

This holds due to the condition
f_{i}(\mathbf{0}_{X})=\mathbf{0}_{Z}
for
i=1,2
. Assuming without loss of generality that
\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\geq\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}
, the first term of (
3.2
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\frac{b-\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}\left\|f_{1}(\mathbf{y})-f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}+\left|\frac{b-\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}-\frac{b-\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}\right|\left\|f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{b-\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}\operatorname{Lip}(f_{1})\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}+\frac{\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}\operatorname{Lip}(f_{1})\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b-\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}+\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}\operatorname{Lip}(f_{1})\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}\leq\left(1+\frac{a}{b-a}\right)\operatorname{Lip}(f_{1})\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}.

The second term of (
3.2
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\frac{b\bigl(\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}-a\bigr)}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}(b-a)}\left\|f_{2}(\mathbf{y})-f_{2}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}+\left|\frac{b\bigl(\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}-a\bigr)}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}(b-a)}-\frac{b\bigl(\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}-a\bigr)}{\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}(b-a)}\right|\left\|f_{2}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{b\bigl(\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}-a\bigr)}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}(b-a)}\operatorname{Lip}(f_{2})\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}+\frac{ab}{\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}(b-a)}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\operatorname{Lip}(f_{2})\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{a}{b-a}\right)\operatorname{Lip}(f_{2})\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}.

Summing the derived upper bounds for the two terms of (
3.2
) establishes (
3.1
).

Finally,
if
f_{1}=\mathbf{0}_{Z}
, then

\left\|\Phi(\mathbf{x})-f_{2}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}=\begin{cases}\left\|f_{2}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}&\text{if $\left\|x\right\|_{X}\leq a$},\\
\frac{a(b-\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X})}{(b-a)\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}}\left\|f_{2}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}&\text{if $a<\left\|x\right\|_{X}<b$},\\
0&\text{if $\left\|x\right\|_{X}\geq b$},\end{cases}

and if
f_{2}=\mathbf{0}_{Z}
, then

\left\|\Phi(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}=\begin{cases}\left\|f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}&\text{if $\left\|x\right\|_{X}\leq a$},\\
\frac{b-\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}}{b-a}\left\|f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Z}&\text{if $a<\left\|x\right\|_{X}<b$},\\
0&\text{if $\left\|x\right\|_{X}\geq b$}.\end{cases}

Applying (
3.3
) to these formulae, we verify (
iv
) and (
v
).
∎

Lemma 3.0
.

The approach we take to modify the mapping

The conclusion of this lemma is valid for

\mathbf{0}_{Y}\in f(\Gamma)

3

,

,

,

Further, we note that Lemma

(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

Lemma 4.0

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

Proof.

Observe that if

The next lemma says that for a given compact purely unrectifiable subset
E
of a finite-dimensional normed space
X
, and a given bounded linear operator
T
on
X
there exists a Lipschitz
g\colon X\to T(X)
which, on a neighbourhood of the set
E
, has derivative approximately equal to
T
and everywhere outside this neighbourhood has derivative zero. In other words, there are Lipschitz mappings which are constant except on a small neighbourhood of the given compact purely unrectifiable set, where their derivative is approximately whatever bounded linear operator you wish. It is natural to ask what is the optimal Lipschitz constant with which such a mapping
g\colon X\to T(X)
can be found. The optimal result that could be hoped for is clearly
\operatorname{Lip}(g)\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}+\theta
for an arbitrarily small error term
\theta>0
and indeed, in all ‘classical’ settings (for example, when the norm on
T(X)
is Euclidean), this is what Lemma
4
achieves; see Remark
4
. However, in the general setting, we identify a constant
\mathfrak{C}(T)\geq\left\|T\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}
, so that the Lipschitz constant of
g
may be bounded above by
\mathfrak{C}(T)+\theta
. It would be of interest to determine whether this upper bound is optimal.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normed vector spaces and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
be a nonzero bounded linear operator of finite rank
l
. We associate to
T
the constant

\mathfrak{C}(T):=\min\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq l}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}\circ T(\cdot)\,\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\colon\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}\text{ is a basis of $T(X)$}\right\}.

In the above
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}^{*}\in T(X)^{*}
is the basis of
T(X)^{*}
dual to the basis
\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}
of
T(X)
so that, in particular,
T=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}\circ T(\cdot)\,\mathbf{w}_{i}
. We also define
\mathfrak{C}(\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)})=0
.

Remark 4.1
.

(i)

The constant
\mathfrak{C}(T)
is well-defined, that is, the minimum of the above set exists, due to Lemma
C
.

(ii)

The identity
T=\sum_{i=1}^{l}(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}\circ T)(\cdot)\,\mathbf{w}_{i}
for any choice of
\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}
and
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}^{*}
as above implies
\mathfrak{C}(T)\geq\left\|T\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}
.

(iii)

For any normed space
X
and
(Y,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y})=(\ell_{p},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{q})
,
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
(in particular, Hilbert and finite-dimensional Euclidean), and for every
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, one has

\mathfrak{C}(T)=\|T\|
. However for any
Y
of dimension at least
3
there is a norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y}
so that for
X=Y
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y}
one has
\mathfrak{C}(\operatorname{Id}_{X})>1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

and

In the following lemma we will consider mappings defined on
Q\subseteq X
. Note that although our final aim, Theorem
1.2
, is to prove typical non-differentiability within the space of

-Lipschitz mappings defined on a bounded subset

of

, Lemma
4
works for any

, in particular,

. Also, in the proof of Lemma

we will require a familiar type of smooth approximation result for Lipschitz mappings; the formal statement and proof of this result appears later on, in Section
5
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normed spaces, where
X
is finite-dimensional. Let a closed subset
Q\subseteq X
contain an open set
V
,
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
g\colon Q\to W
be a Lipschitz mapping, where
W\supseteq T(X)
is a finite-dimensional subspace of
Y
, functions
\psi\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\colon V\to[0,\infty)
be continuous and bounded on
V
, and
\theta>0
. Assume further that

(4.20)

\left\|Dg(\mathbf{x})-\psi(\mathbf{x})T\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\xi(\mathbf{x})

for almost all
\mathbf{x}\in V
. Then there exists a
Lipschitz mapping
f\colon Q\to W
such that

(a)

f(\mathbf{x})=g(\mathbf{x})
whenever
\mathbf{x}\in Q\setminus\{\mathbf{y}\in V\colon\xi(\mathbf{y})>0\}
.

(b)

\left\|f(\mathbf{x})-g(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\theta
for all
\mathbf{x}\in Q
.

(c)

f\in C^{1}(V,Y)
.

(d)

\left\|Df(\mathbf{x})-\psi(\mathbf{x})T\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\xi(\mathbf{x})(1+\theta)
for all
\mathbf{x}\in V
.

Proof.

The statement of the lemma is trivial if
W=\{\mathbf{0}_{Y}\}
. Also, if the open set
U:=\{\mathbf{x}\in V\colon\xi(\mathbf{x})>0\}
is empty, then
Dg
coincides, almost everywhere on
V
, with a continuous mapping
\psi T
by (
4.20
). Therefore

g\in C^{1}(V,Y)
and
Dg(\mathbf{x})=\psi(\mathbf{x})T
for all
\mathbf{x}\in V
(see Theorem
B.2
), so
f:=g
satisfies conditions (
a
)–(
d
). Hence assume, without loss of generality, that
W\neq\{\mathbf{0}_{Y}\}
and
U\neq\emptyset
.

Let
\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}\in Y
be a basis of
W
, such that
\left\|\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\|_{Y}=1
for all
1\leq i\leq l
and
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}^{*}\in W^{*}
be the corresponding biorthogonal functionals; let
g_{i}=\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}\circ g
, so that
g(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{l}g_{i}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{w}_{i}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in Q
. We also fix a basis of
X
and use this to identify
X
with
\mathbb{R}^{\dim X}
in the standard way. This identification allows us to define the Lebesgue measure on
X
. Accordingly all integrals on subsets of
X
which appear in this proof should be understood via this identification. Let also
C>0
be a constant of equivalence between the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}
and the Euclidean norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\text{E}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{\dim X}=X
so that for all
\mathbf{z}\in X

(4.21)

\frac{1}{C}\left\|\mathbf{z}\right\|_{\text{E}}\leq\left\|\mathbf{z}\right\|_{X}\leq C\left\|\mathbf{z}\right\|_{\text{E}}.

Let
\rho\colon X\to[0,\infty)
denote the standard smooth (Euclidean) mollifier in
\mathbb{R}^{\dim X}=X
and for
\varepsilon>0
let
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\coloneq\varepsilon^{-\dim X}\rho(\mathbf{x}/\varepsilon)
. In what follows we consider, for each
\mathbf{x}\in U
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon(\mathbf{x}))
, where
\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})>0
is such that
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}\in U
for any
\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\text{E}}\leq\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})
, the convolution

g*\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{l}(g_{i}*\rho_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{w}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\int_{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\text{E}}\leq\varepsilon}g_{i}(\mathbf{x-y})\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{y}\right)\mathbf{w}_{i},

where the integration is with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Note that
g*\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\in W
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon(\mathbf{x}))
. We also let
{U}_{\varepsilon}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in U\colon\operatorname{dist}_{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\text{E}}}(\mathbf{x},X\setminus U)>\varepsilon\right\}
, for
\varepsilon>0
.

Note that for any
\varepsilon>0
the convolution
g*\rho_{\varepsilon}
is defined on
{U}_{\varepsilon}
, belongs to
\operatorname{Lip}({U}_{\varepsilon},Y)\cap C^{1}({U}_{\varepsilon},Y)
and approximates
g
well: for any
\mathbf{x}\in{U}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\left\|g*\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})-g(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{l}(g_{i}*\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})-g_{i}(\mathbf{x}))\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\int_{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\text{E}}\leq\varepsilon}\left(g_{i}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})-g_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right)\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{y}\right)\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\|_{Y}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\int_{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\text{E}}\leq\varepsilon}\left|g_{i}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})-g_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right|\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{y}\leq l\max_{1\leq i\leq l}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}\right\|_{X^{*}}C\operatorname{Lip}(g)\varepsilon.

We note, for future reference, that using
D(g_{i}*\rho_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{x})=(Dg_{i}*\rho_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{x})
and (
4.20
), we have for all
\varepsilon>0
,
\mathbf{x}\in{U}_{\varepsilon}
and
\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{X}\leq 1

(4.23)

\displaystyle{D(g*\rho_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{v})-\psi(\mathbf{x})T(\mathbf{v})}={\sum_{i=1}^{l}D(g_{i}*\rho_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{w}_{i}-\psi(\mathbf{x})T(\mathbf{v})}

\displaystyle={\sum_{i=1}^{l}(Dg_{i}*\rho_{\varepsilon})(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{w}_{i}-\psi(\mathbf{x})T(\mathbf{v})}={\int\limits_{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{\text{E}}\leq\varepsilon}\left(Dg(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})(\mathbf{v})-\psi(\mathbf{x})T(\mathbf{v})\right)\rho_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{y})\,d\mathbf{y}}.

We now use (
4.20
) to estimate the norm of (
4.23
) from above as

\tilde{h}\in C(Q,Y)\cap C^{1}(U,Y)
and
\left\|\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\theta
for all
\mathbf{x}\in Q
, and

\operatorname{Lip}(\tilde{h})\leq\max(\operatorname{Lip}(h),\theta+\sup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Lip}(h_{k}|_{A_{k}}))
.
In particular, if
\sup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Lip}(h_{k}|_{A_{k}})
is finite, then
\tilde{h}\in\operatorname{Lip}(Q,Y)
.

(ii)

If, additionally, for each
\mathbf{x}\in U
there is
P(\mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
and
\eta(\mathbf{x})>0
such that

\left\|Dh_{k}(\mathbf{x})-P(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\eta(\mathbf{x})\qquad\text{for all }\mathbf{x}\in A_{k}\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N},

then

\left\|D\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-P(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\eta(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Lip}(\varphi_{k})\mathbbm{1}_{\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{k})}(\mathbf{x})\theta_{k}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in U
.

Proof.

Observe that due to the local finiteness property of the partition of unity, for each point
\mathbf{x}\in U
there is an open neighbourhood
V(\mathbf{x})\subseteq U
such that
the set

(5.2)

I(\mathbf{x})=\left\{k\geq 1\colon V(\mathbf{x})\cap\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{k})\neq\emptyset\right\}

is finite. Hence for any
\mathbf{x}\in U

(5.3)

\tilde{h}(\mathbf{y})=\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{y})h_{k}(\mathbf{y})\quad\text{for all}\quad\mathbf{y}\in V(\mathbf{x}),

and thus
\tilde{h}|_{V(\mathbf{x})}
is a finite sum of mappings
C^{1}
-smooth on
V(\mathbf{x})
.
Hence by the arbitrariness of
\mathbf{x}\in U
we conclude
\tilde{h}\in C^{1}(U,Y)
.
We now show that
\tilde{h}
is continuous on
Q
. The only points of the domain at which continuity of
\tilde{h}
is unclear are those in
\partial U
. Let
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\partial U\cap Q
and let
\theta>0
be arbitrary. There exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that

(5.4)

\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}<\theta/2.

Next, choose

(5.5)

0<\eta<\frac{\theta}{2\left(\operatorname{Lip}(h)+1\right)}

sufficiently small so that

(5.6)

B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\eta)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{i})=\emptyset\qquad\text{for $i=1,\ldots,N$,}

which is possible because each
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{i})
is contained in the open set
U
, whilst
\mathbf{x}_{0}\notin U
, so
\mathbf{x}_{0}\notin\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{i})
. Let
\mathbf{x}\in B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\eta)\cap Q
be arbitrary.

If
\mathbf{x}\in Q\setminus U
we have
\left\|\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\|_{Y}=\left\|h(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\|_{Y}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(h)\eta\leq\theta
. Now assume that
\mathbf{x}\in U
. Then, using (
5.6
) and that
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are a partition of unity on
U
, we get

\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})(h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x}_{0}))=\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})(h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x}_{0}))\\
=\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})(h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x}))+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})(h(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x}_{0})).

Using this with (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), as well as
h_{k}\in\operatorname{SLA}(h,A_{k},Y,\theta_{k})
which implies
\left\|h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\theta_{k}
for
\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{k})\subseteq A_{k}
, we derive

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\left\|h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\left\|h(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}+\operatorname{Lip}(h)\eta\leq\theta.

Thus, we have established
\left\|\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\|_{Y}\leq\theta
for all
\mathbf{x}\in B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{0},\eta)\cap Q
. Since
\theta>0
was arbitrary, this verifies the continuity of
\tilde{h}
at
\mathbf{x}_{0}
. This completes the proof that
\tilde{h}
is continuous on
Q
.

For each
\mathbf{x}\in Q\setminus U
we have
\left\|\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}=0
and for each
\mathbf{x}\in U
we have, using that
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a partition of unity on
U
,
\left\|h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\theta_{k}
for
\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{k})
and
\sum\theta_{k}\leq\theta
,

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})(h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x}))\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\left\|h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\theta_{k}\leq\theta.

Therefore
\left\|\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\theta
for all
\mathbf{x}\in Q
.

It only remains to verify the desired bound on the Lipschitz constant of
\tilde{h}
. Consider first
\mathbf{x}\in U
. Using that
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a locally finite smooth partition of unity, so
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\varphi_{k}
is identically
1
on
U
, we conclude
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}D\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})=0
. However
D\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})=0
for each
k\notin I(\mathbf{x})
, see (
5.2
), so we also conclude
\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}D\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})=0
.
Recall that
\tilde{h}
can be written as a finite sum of
C^{1}
-smooth terms in an open neighbourhood of
\mathbf{x}
, see (
5.3
); thus

(5.7)

D\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})Dh_{k}(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}D\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})h_{k}(\mathbf{x})\\
=\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})Dh_{k}(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}D\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}D\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})h(\mathbf{x}).

We deduce, using the properties of
h_{k}\in\operatorname{SLA}(h,A_{k},Y,\theta_{k})
for each
k\in I(\mathbf{x})
, that

\displaystyle\left\|D\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\left\|Dh_{k}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}+\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\left\|D\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{X^{*}}\left\|h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\operatorname{Lip}(h_{k}|_{A_{k}})+\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\operatorname{Lip}(\varphi_{k})\theta_{k}\leq\sup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Lip}(h_{k}|_{A_{k}})+\theta.

Since this inequality has been established for an arbitrary point
\mathbf{x}
in the open set
U
, we conclude that
\tilde{h}
is locally
\left(\sup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Lip}(h_{k}|_{A_{k}})+\theta\right)
-Lipschitz on
U
, if this constant is finite. In summary, we have now established that
\tilde{h}
is continuous,
\tilde{h}
is locally
\left(\sup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Lip}(h_{k}|_{A_{k}})+\theta\right)
-Lipschitz on
U
, whilst by definition of
\tilde{h}
,
\operatorname{Lip}(\tilde{h}|_{Q\setminus U})\leq\operatorname{Lip}(h)
. Therefore,
\tilde{h}
is Lipschitz with
\operatorname{Lip}(\tilde{h})\leq\max(\operatorname{Lip}(h),\sup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Lip}(h_{k}|_{A_{k}})+\theta)
; see Lemma
A
. This finishes the proof of (
i
).

To prove (
ii
), we use again (
5.7
) to get, for every
\mathbf{x}\in U
,

\displaystyle\left\|D\tilde{h}(\mathbf{x})-P(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\left\|Dh_{k}(\mathbf{x})-P(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}+\sum_{k\in I(\mathbf{x})}\left\|D\varphi_{k}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{X^{*}}\left\|h_{k}(\mathbf{x})-h(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\eta(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Lip}(\varphi_{k})\mathbbm{1}_{\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{k})}(\mathbf{x})\theta_{k}.\qed

Definition 5.2
.

If
X
and
Y
are normed spaces,
U\subseteq X
is open and
f\colon U\to Y
is Gâteaux differentiable at every point of
U
, we say that
f
is uniformly Gâteaux differentiable on
U
if for each pair of
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta=\delta(\mathbf{h},\varepsilon)>0
such that

(5.8)

\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{h})-f(\mathbf{x})-tD_{G}f(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{h})\right\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon\left|t\right|

whenever
\mathbf{x}\in U
and
\left|t\right|\leq\min\left\{\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},X\setminus U),\delta\right\}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces,
U\subseteq X
be open and
f\colon U\to Y
be uniformly Gâteaux differentiable. Then
f\in C^{1}(U,Y)
.

Proof.

Fix
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in U
and choose
r>0
so that
B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{0},r)\subseteq U
. We verify continuity of the Gâteaux derivative
D_{G}f
of
f
at
\mathbf{x}_{0}
. By
[
3
, Prop 4.2]
, this will imply Fréchet differentiability of
f
on
U
and therefore that
f\in C^{1}(U)
. Given
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, exploit the uniform Gâteaux differentiability of
f
on
U
, see (
5.8
) and let
\delta=\min(\delta(\mathbf{u},\varepsilon/4),r/2)

Then for all
\mathbf{x}\in B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{0},r/2)
and
t\in[-\delta,\delta]
,

\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})-tD_{G}f(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{u})\right\|_{Y}\leq\frac{\varepsilon\left|t\right|}{4}

Then for every
\mathbf{x}\in U
with
\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\right\|_{X}\leq\frac{\varepsilon\delta}{4\left(\operatorname{Lip}(f)+1\right)}
we use the above inequality with
t=\delta
to get

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\left\|\left(D_{G}f(\mathbf{x})-D_{G}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right)(\mathbf{u})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\delta D_{G}f(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{u})-\left(f(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x})\right)\right\|_{Y}+\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\delta\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\delta\mathbf{u})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle+\left\|f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-f(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|f(\mathbf{x}_{0}+\delta\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{x}_{0})-\delta D_{G}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})(\mathbf{u})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\frac{\varepsilon\delta}{4}+2\operatorname{Lip}(f)\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0}\right\|_{X}\leq\varepsilon\delta.

Since
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}
was arbitrary, this shows
\left\|D_{G}f(\mathbf{x})-D_{G}f(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\varepsilon
.
∎

The next theorem is a generalisation of a smooth approximation result of Johanis
[
13
]
. The difference to
[
13
]
is that the following statement treats Lipschitz mappings defined only on a subset of a Banach space, whereas
[
13
]
provides smooth approximations only for mappings defined on the whole space
X
, and Lipschitz mappings defined on subsets of Banach spaces may not necessarily be extended to the whole space. In other words, the result of
[
13
]
is the special case of the following theorem, where we set
Q=X
. This local version of Johanis’s smooth approximation is a useful statement, independent of the present paper. The proof is an adaptation of the argument of Johanis
[
13
]
, see also
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
, but for completeness we include the full argument.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces, where
X
is separable,
Q\subseteq X
and
\varepsilon>0
be such that

(5.9)

\emptyset\neq{Q}_{\varepsilon}\coloneq\left\{\mathbf{x}\in Q\colon\operatorname{dist}_{\left\|-\right\|_{X}}(\mathbf{x},X\setminus Q)>\varepsilon\right\},

and let
f\colon Q\to Y
be a Lipschitz mapping.
Then there exists
g\in\operatorname{Lip}(Q_{\varepsilon},Y)\cap C^{1}(Q_{\varepsilon},Y)
such that

g
is uniformly Gâteaux differentiable on
{Q}_{\varepsilon}
,

\operatorname{Lip}(g)\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)
and
\left\|g(\mathbf{x})-f(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon
for all
\mathbf{x}\in Q_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

We follow the argument of
[
13
]

and make only small adjustments. Let
(\mathbf{h}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable dense subset of
\mathbb{S}_{X}
; for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
\varphi_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\varphi_{i}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{i}(t)\,dt=1
and

\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{i}\subseteq J_{i}:=\left[\frac{-\varepsilon}{2\left(\operatorname{Lip}(f)+1\right)2^{i}},\frac{\varepsilon}{2\left(\operatorname{Lip}(f)+1\right)2^{i}}\right].

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let
P_{n}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}J_{i}

and observe that
\mathbf{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\in Q
whenever
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in P_{n}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define mappings
g_{n}\colon{Q}_{\varepsilon}\to Y
by

g_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{P_{n}}f\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\bigr)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{i}(t_{i})\,d\lambda_{n},

where the integral is the Bochner integral and
\lambda_{n}
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\supseteq P_{n}
. We note, for further reference, that for any
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and
m\geq n

(5.10)

g_{n}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{P_{m}}f\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\bigr)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}(t_{i})\,d\lambda_{m}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\bigr)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}(t_{i})\,d\lambda_{m}.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following inequalities show that
g_{n}\colon{Q}_{\varepsilon}\to Y
is Lipschitz with
\operatorname{Lip}(g_{n})\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)
: whenever
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\left\|g_{n}(\mathbf{x})-g_{n}(\mathbf{y})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{P_{n}}\left\|f\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\bigr)-f\bigl(\mathbf{y}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\bigr)\right\|_{Y}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{i}(t_{i})\,d\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}\int_{P_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{i}(t_{i})\,d\lambda_{n}=\operatorname{Lip}(f)\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}.

For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>n
and any
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
we observe, using (
5.10
),

\left\|g_{m}(\mathbf{x})-g_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\int_{P_{m}}\left\|f\left(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\right)\right\|_{Y}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}(t_{i})\,d\lambda_{m}\\
\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\int_{P_{m}}\left\|\sum_{i=n+1}^{m}t_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}\right\|_{X}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}(t_{i})\,d\lambda_{m}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\sum_{i=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{2\left(\operatorname{Lip}(f)+1\right)2^{i}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{n}},

and conclude from this that the sequence of Lipschitz mappings
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\operatorname{Lip}(g_{n})\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)
converges uniformly on
{Q}_{\varepsilon}
to a Lipschitz mapping
g\colon{Q}_{\varepsilon}\to Y
with
\operatorname{Lip}(g)\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)
and such that
\left\|g_{n}(\mathbf{y})-g(\mathbf{y})\right\|_{Y}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{n}}
for each
\mathbf{y}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. To see that
g
is a good approximation of
f
, observe for
\mathbf{x}\in Q_{\varepsilon}
that

\left\|f(\mathbf{x})-g(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|f(\mathbf{x})-g_{1}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|g_{1}(\mathbf{x})-g(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\\
\leq\int_{P_{1}}\left\|f(\mathbf{x})-f\left(\mathbf{x}-t_{1}\mathbf{h}_{1}\right)\right\|_{Y}\varphi_{1}(t_{1})\,d\lambda_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\int_{P_{1}}\left|t_{1}\right|\varphi_{1}(t_{1})\,d\lambda_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\varepsilon.

We are now only left to check that
g\in C^{1}({Q}_{\varepsilon},Y)
and
g
is uniformly Gâteaux differentiable on
{Q}_{\varepsilon}
. Note that by Lemma
5
, the latter implies the former. We first show that
g
is Gâteaux differentiable at every
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
, and then verify that condition (
5.8
) of Definition
5.2
is satisfied.

Let us start by using (
5.10
) to compute the directional derivatives of
g_{n}
at
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
in the direction of the vectors
\mathbf{h}_{i}
for
i,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq i
as follows:

(5.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})&=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\frac{g_{n}(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g_{n}(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}\\
&=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Bigl(f\bigl((\mathbf{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j})+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i}\bigr)-f\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j}\bigr)\Bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}(t_{j})\,d\lambda_{n}\\
&=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j}\bigr)\cdot\frac{\varphi_{i}(t_{i}+\tau)-\varphi_{i}(t_{i})}{\tau}\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n}\varphi_{j}(t_{j})\,d\lambda_{n}\\
&=\int_{P_{n}}f\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j}\bigr)\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(t_{i})\prod_{1\leq j\neq i\leq n}\varphi_{j}(t_{j})\,d\lambda_{n},\end{split}

The penultimate equality is a standard application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem for the Bochner integral, and the last equality follows from
\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq J_{i}
. It is also important to observe that for a fixed pair of
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and
i\geq 1
the limits in (
5.11
) are uniform with respect to
n
: this may be verified by applying the Mean Value Theorem to
\varphi_{i}
and recalling that
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with bounded support and is therefore Lipschitz. For any
\theta>0
and
\mathbf{x},i
as above we let
\tau_{0}=\tau_{0}(\theta,\mathbf{x},i)>0
be such that
B_{X}(\mathbf{x},\tau_{0})\subseteq{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and

(5.12)

\left\|g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})-\frac{{g_{n}(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g_{n}(\mathbf{x})}}{\tau}\right\|_{Y}\leq\theta\qquad\text{whenever $n\geq i$ and $0<\left|\tau\right|<\tau_{0}$.}

We will now argue that for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
, the sequence of directional derivatives
g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})
,
n\geq i
, computed above, converges to the directional derivative
g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})
. To this end, let
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and observe, using (
5.10
), for
m>n\geq i

\displaystyle\left\|g_{m}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})-g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})\right\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{P_{m}}\left\|f\left(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j}\right)-f\left(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j}\right)\right\|_{Y}\left|\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(t_{i})\right|\prod_{1\leq j\neq i\leq m}\varphi_{j}(t_{j})\,d\lambda_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\int_{P_{m}}\sum_{j=n+1}^{m}\left|t_{j}\right|\left|\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(t_{i})\right|\prod_{1\leq j\neq i\leq m}\varphi_{j}(t_{j})\,d\lambda_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\sum_{j=n+1}^{m}\frac{\varepsilon}{2\left(\operatorname{Lip}(f)+1\right)2^{j}}\int_{J_{i}}\left|\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(t_{i})\right|\,d\lambda_{1}(t_{i})\leq\frac{\operatorname{Lip}(\varphi_{i})\varepsilon}{2^{n}}.

Hence,
(g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
(Y,\left\|-\right\|_{Y})
; let
D_{i}(\mathbf{x})\coloneq\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})
. Fix an arbitrary
\eta>0
, let
0<\left|\tau\right|<\tau_{0}(\eta/3,\mathbf{x},i)
and choose
N\geq i
large enough such that

(5.13)

\left\|g_{N}(\mathbf{y})-g(\mathbf{y})\right\|_{Y}\leq\frac{\eta\tau}{6}\text{ for all }\mathbf{y}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\left\|g_{N}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})-D_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\frac{\eta}{3}.

Then we may combine (
5.12
) and (
5.13
) to deduce

(5.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\|\tfrac{g(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}-D_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|\tfrac{g(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}-\tfrac{g_{N}(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g_{N}(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}\right\|_{Y}\\
&+\left\|\tfrac{g_{N}(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g_{N}(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}-g_{N}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|g_{N}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})-D_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{Y}\\
&\leq\frac{\eta}{3}+\frac{\eta}{3}+\frac{\eta}{3}=\eta.\end{split}

This establishes that
g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})
exists and equals
D_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})
for all
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and
i\geq 1
, and so inequality (
5.12
) also holds with
g_{n}
replaced by
g
, for all
0<\left|\tau\right|<\tau_{0}(\theta,\mathbf{x},i)
.

Let an arbitrary
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
be fixed. Since
g\in\operatorname{Lip}({Q}_{\varepsilon})
, the mapping
g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\cdot)
is Lipschitz on
\{\mathbf{h}_{i}\colon i\geq 1\}
, as for any
\mathbf{h}_{i}\neq\mathbf{h}_{j}
and any
\eta>0

\left\|g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{j})-g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})\right\|_{Y}\leq 2\eta\left\|\mathbf{h}_{j}-\mathbf{h}_{i}\right\|_{X}+\operatorname{Lip}(g)\left\|\mathbf{h}_{j}-\mathbf{h}_{i}\right\|_{X}

using (
5.12
) with
0<\left|\tau\right|<\tau_{0}(\eta\left\|\mathbf{h}_{j}-\mathbf{h}_{i}\right\|_{X},\mathbf{x},i)
and
g
instead of
g_{n}
.

Let
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}\colon\mathbb{S}_{X}\to Y
denote the unique Lipschitz extension to
\mathbb{S}_{X}
of the mapping
g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\cdot)\colon\left\{\mathbf{h}_{i}\colon i\geq 1\right\}\to Y
. We now verify that the directional derivative
g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h})
exists and equals
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{h})
for all
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}
and
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
. Indeed, given
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}
and
\eta>0
, choose
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left\|\mathbf{h}_{i}-\mathbf{h}\right\|_{X}\leq\eta/3\left(\operatorname{Lip}(\Phi_{\mathbf{x}})+\operatorname{Lip}(g)+1\right)
. Then

(5.15)

\left\|\frac{g(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h})-g(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}-\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{h})\right\|_{Y}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(g)\left\|\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{h}_{i}\right\|_{X}+\frac{\eta}{3}+\operatorname{Lip}(\Phi_{\mathbf{x}})\left\|\mathbf{h}_{i}-\mathbf{h}\right\|_{X}\leq\eta,

whenever
0<\left|\tau\right|<\tau_{0}(\eta/3,\mathbf{x},i)
, using (
5.12
) for
g
instead of
g_{n}
and
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{h}_{i})=g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})
. Extending
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}
now to the whole of
X
via the formula
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(t\mathbf{h})=t\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{h})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}
, it is readily verified that
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}
remains Lipschitz and we get

(5.16)

g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{v})=\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{v})\qquad\text{for all }\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}\in X.

We finally verify that
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}
is a linear operator for each
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
. Together with (
5.16
) and Lipschitzness of
g
this will establish that
g
is Gâteaux differentiable on
{Q}_{\varepsilon}
, with Gâteaux derivative
Dg(\mathbf{x})=\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}
of norm
\left\|\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(g)
at every
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
. To show that
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}
is a linear operator, it is enough to check linearity of
\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}
on
\{\mathbf{h}_{i}\colon i\geq 1\}
. For this, note that a
calculation similar to (
5.11
) shows, for
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, that for
n\geq i,j
the directional derivative
g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};{\alpha_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}+\alpha_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j}})
exists and equals

\int_{P_{n}}f\bigl(\mathbf{x}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j}\bigr)\Bigl(\alpha_{i}\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(t_{i})\prod_{k\neq i}\varphi_{k}(t_{k})+\alpha_{j}\varphi_{j}^{\prime}(t_{j})\prod_{k\neq j}\varphi_{k}(t_{k})\Bigr)\,d\lambda_{n},

and so

g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};{\alpha_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}+\alpha_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j}})=\alpha_{i}g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};{\mathbf{h}_{i}})+\alpha_{j}g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};{\mathbf{h}_{j}})
.
Taking limits in this identity as
n\to\infty
gives, similarly to (
5.14
),

\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\alpha_{i}\mathbf{h}_{i}+\alpha_{j}\mathbf{h}_{j})=\alpha_{i}\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{h}_{i})+\alpha_{j}\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{h}_{j}).

The only thing left is to check that
g
is uniformly Gâteaux differentiable on
{Q}_{\varepsilon}
.
Assume that
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
are fixed. Choose
i\geq 1
such that
\left\|\mathbf{h}_{i}-\mathbf{h}\right\|_{X}<\varepsilon/(4\operatorname{Lip}(g)+1)
.

Then, for each
n\geq i
and all
\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
we can use (
5.11
) to derive

\left\|g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{y}_{1};\mathbf{h}_{i})-g_{n}^{\prime}(\mathbf{y}_{2};\mathbf{h}_{i})\right\|_{Y}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\int_{J_{i}}\left|\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(t)\right|\,d\lambda_{1}(t)\cdot\left\|\mathbf{y}_{1}-\mathbf{y}_{2}\right\|_{X}=:L_{i}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{1}-\mathbf{y}_{2}\right\|_{X}.

Taking a limit of the above inequality as
n\to\infty
we obtain, for any
\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
,

\left\|g^{\prime}(\mathbf{y}_{1};\mathbf{h}_{i})-g^{\prime}(\mathbf{y}_{2};\mathbf{h}_{i})\right\|_{Y}\leq L_{i}\left\|\mathbf{y}_{1}-\mathbf{y}_{2}\right\|_{X},

which implies for every
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and any
0<\left|\tau\right|<\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},X\setminus{Q}_{\varepsilon})
,

\left\|\frac{g(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}-g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};{\mathbf{h}_{i}})\right\|_{Y}=\frac{1}{\left|\tau\right|}\left\|\int_{(0,\tau)}g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{h}_{i};{\mathbf{h}_{i}})-g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};{\mathbf{h}_{i}})\,d\lambda_{1}(t)\right\|_{Y}\\
\leq\frac{L_{i}}{\left|\tau\right|}\int_{(0,\tau)}\left|t\right|\,d\lambda_{1}(t)=L_{i}\left|\tau\right|/2.

Let
\delta=\varepsilon/(L_{i}+1)
; consider any
\mathbf{x}\in{Q}_{\varepsilon}
and
0<\left|\tau\right|<\min(\delta,\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},X\setminus{Q}_{\varepsilon}))
. We now verify that condition (
5.8
) of Definition
5.2
is satisfied. Indeed, we readily have
\left\|\frac{g(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}-g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};{\mathbf{h}_{i}})\right\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon/2
from above,

\left\|g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h})-g^{\prime}(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{h}_{i})\right\|_{Y}=\left\|\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{h}_{i})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|\Phi_{\mathbf{x}}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\left\|\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{h}_{i}\right\|_{X}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(g)\left\|\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{h}_{i}\right\|_{X}<\varepsilon/4

and

\left\|\frac{g(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h})-g(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}-\frac{g(\mathbf{x}+\tau\mathbf{h}_{i})-g(\mathbf{x})}{\tau}\right\|_{Y}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(g)\left\|\mathbf{h}-\mathbf{h}_{i}\right\|_{X}<\varepsilon/4.

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

and

is separable. Let

,
\emptyset\neq E\subseteq X
and

-smooth partition of unity with supports in

. Let

and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a mapping

such that
g|_{Q\setminus H}=f|_{Q\setminus H}
,
\left\|g(\mathbf{y})-f(\mathbf{y})\right\|_{Y}\leq\varepsilon

\mathbf{y}\in Q

\operatorname{Lip}(g)\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)+\varepsilon
.

is Banach, we may assume without loss of generality that

5.3
. Let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

for each

. Choose any

.

5.3

for each

. To complete the proof, we let

,

.

Appendix A
Local to global Lipschitz estimates.

Lemma A.0
.

Let
X,Y
be normed spaces,
F\subseteq U\subseteq X
where
U
is open and convex, and suppose that for any
\varepsilon>0
and any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in U
there exist
\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in U
such that
\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right\|_{X},\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\|_{X}<\varepsilon
and
[\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}]\cap F
has
1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
0
.
Let

g\colon U\to Y
be locally Lipschitz on
U
and suppose that
g
has at least one of the following properties:

(i)

g
is locally
L
-Lipschitz on
U\setminus F
.

(ii)

for every
\mathbf{x}\in U\setminus F
, the derivative
Dg(\mathbf{x})
exists and satisfies

\left\|Dg(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq L
.

Then
g\colon U\to Y
is
L
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

In order to show that
g
is
L
-Lipschitz, we fix an arbitrary
\mathbf{w}^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{Y^{*}}
and show that the function
g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}=\mathbf{w}^{*}\circ g\colon U\to\mathbb{R}
is
L
-Lipschitz.
Let
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in U
be any pair of distinct points. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}/2)
be arbitrary; find
\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in U
as guaranteed by the hypothesis of the lemma. Then, since
[\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}]\subseteq U
and
g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}|_{[\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}]}
is locally Lipschitz as a mapping
[\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}]\to Y
,

g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}(\mathbf{y}^{\prime})-g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{\left\|\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right\|_{X}}g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+t\mathbf{v};\mathbf{v})\,dt,

where
\mathbf{v}=\frac{\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}{\left\|\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right\|_{X}}
,
g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+t\mathbf{v};\mathbf{v})
is the directional derivative of
g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}
at
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+t\mathbf{v}
in the direction of
\mathbf{v}
which exists for Lebesgue almost all
t\in[0,\left\|\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}]
.
Recall that
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-almost every point of
[\mathbf{x}^{\prime},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}]
belongs to
U\setminus F
, hence
|g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+t\mathbf{v};\mathbf{v})|\leq L
for almost all
t\in[0,\left\|\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right\|_{X}]
, implying
\left|g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}(\mathbf{y}^{\prime})-g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\right|\leq L\left\|\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\right\|_{X}
.
Passing to a limit when
\varepsilon\to 0
gives
\left|g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}(\mathbf{y})-g_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}(\mathbf{x})\right|\leq L\left\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}
which, in turn, due to arbitrariness of
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in U
and
\mathbf{w}^{*}\in\mathbb{S}_{Y^{*}}
implies the statement.
∎

Corollary A.0
.

,

.

Defining
F
as a Borel Lebesgue null set containing the Lebesgue null set

, where

Lemma A.0
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normed spaces,
U\subseteq Q\subseteq X
be sets where
U
is open and
Q
is closed, let
f\colon Q\to Y
be a continuous function, which is locally
L
-Lipschitz on
U
and is Lipschitz on
Q\setminus U
. Then
f\in\operatorname{Lip}(Q,Y)
and

\operatorname{Lip}(f)\leq\max\left\{L,\operatorname{Lip}(f|_{Q\setminus U})\right\}.

Proof.

Fix distinct points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in Q
and set
L_{1}:=\max\left\{L,\operatorname{Lip}(f|_{Q\setminus U})\right\}
. We show that

(A.1)

\left\|f(\mathbf{x}_{2})-f(\mathbf{x}_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq L_{1}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{2}-\mathbf{x}_{1}\right\|_{X}.

This inequality is clear if both
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in Q\setminus U
. Assume without loss of generality
\mathbf{x}_{1}\in U
.
Let
\mathbf{e}:=\mathbf{x}_{2}-\mathbf{x}_{1}
,
U_{1}=U\cap\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{e}\right)
and
Q_{1}=Q\cap\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{e}\right)
. Then
\mathbf{x}_{1}\in U_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}\subseteq\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{e}\right)
and
U_{1}
is a relatively open subset of the line
\left(\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{e}\right)
, hence can be written as a disjoint union of open intervals. Let
I
be the open interval containing
\mathbf{x}_{1}
. If
\mathbf{x}_{2}\in I\subseteq U_{1}
, then (
A.1
) is trivially satisfied, even with
L
instead of
L_{1}
in the right-hand side. Hence assume
I
has a right endpoint
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{e}
lying between
\mathbf{x}_{1}
and
\mathbf{x}_{2}
, implying
\left\|\mathbf{x}_{2}-\mathbf{b}\right\|_{X}+\left\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{x}_{1}\right\|_{X}=\left\|\mathbf{x}_{2}-\mathbf{x}_{1}\right\|_{X}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\overline{U_{1}}\setminus U_{1}\subseteq Q_{1}\setminus U_{1}
. If
\mathbf{x}_{2}\notin U_{1}
, then (
A.1
) follows from

\left\|f(\mathbf{x}_{2})-f(\mathbf{b})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|f(\mathbf{b})-f(\mathbf{x}_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f|_{Q\setminus U})\left\|\mathbf{x}_{2}-\mathbf{b}\right\|_{X}+L\left\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{x}_{1}\right\|_{X},

establishing the
L_{1}
-Lipschitzness of
f
between points from
U_{1}
and
Q_{1}\setminus U_{1}
.
Therefore if

\mathbf{x}_{2}\in U_{1}\setminus I
,
then (
A.1
) follows from

\left\|f(\mathbf{x}_{2})-f(\mathbf{b})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|f(\mathbf{b})-f(\mathbf{x}_{1})\right\|_{Y}\leq L_{1}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{2}-\mathbf{b}\right\|_{X}+L\left\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{x}_{1}\right\|_{X},

which
holds due to
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in U_{1}
,
\mathbf{b}\in Q_{1}\setminus U_{1}
.
∎

Appendix B
Derivatives of Lipschitz mappings.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
(X,\left\|-\right\|_{X})
and
(Y,\left\|-\right\|_{Y})
be normed spaces, where
X
is finite-dimensional,
H\subseteq X
be open,
f,g\colon H\to Y
be locally Lipschitz mappings and
A=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in H\colon f(\mathbf{x})=g(\mathbf{x})\right\}
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

At each Lebesgue density point
\mathbf{x}
of
A
the mapping
f
is Fréchet differentiable if and only if the mapping
g
is Fréchet differentiable and
Df(\mathbf{x})=Dg(\mathbf{x})
.

(ii)

If
Y
is finite-dimensional then
Df(\mathbf{x})
and
Dg(\mathbf{x})
exist and are equal for Lebesgue almost every
\mathbf{x}\in A
.

Proof.

Statement (
ii
) is a consequence of (
i
), Stepanov’s Theorem and the Lebesgue Density Theorem. Indeed, by (
i
), we have
Df(\mathbf{x})=Dg(\mathbf{x})
everywhere in the set

\left\{\mathbf{x}\in A\colon\text{$\mathbf{x}$ is a Lebesgue denisty point of $A$}\right\}\cap\left\{\mathbf{x}\in A\colon\text{$f$ is Fr\'{e}chet differentiable at $\mathbf{x}$}\right\}.

The first set in this intersection has full Lebesgue measure in
A
by the Lebesgue Density Theorem and, for finite-dimensional
Y
, the second set in the intersection is also of full Lebesgue measure in
A
, by Stepanov’s Theorem.

We now prove (
i
): Let
\mathbf{x}
be a Lebesgue density point of
A
, choose
r>0
such that
B:=B_{X}(\mathbf{x},r)\subseteq H
and
f|_{B}
and
g|_{B}
are Lipschitz and assume that
f
is differentiable at
\mathbf{x}
. Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Let
d:=\dim(X)
, set

(B.1)

\eta=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{16\left(\operatorname{Lip}(f|_{B})+\operatorname{Lip}(g|_{B})+1\right)}\right)^{d}

and choose
\delta\in(0,r)
so that

(B.2)

\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})-f(\mathbf{x})-Df(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{y})\right\|_{Y}\leq\eta\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\qquad\text{for all $\mathbf{y}\in\overline{B}_{X}(\mathbf{0},\delta)$,}

and

(B.3)

\mathcal{L}_{X}\left(B_{X}(\mathbf{x},t)\cap A\right)\geq(1-\eta)\mathcal{L}_{X}\left(B_{X}(\mathbf{x},t)\right)\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,\delta]$.}

Let
\mathbf{y}\in B_{X}(\mathbf{0},\delta/2)\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}
and set
t=2\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}
. Observe, using
\eta<2^{-d-1}
and
\delta<r
, that

B_{X}(\mathbf{x+\mathbf{y}},2^{1/d}\eta^{1/d}t)\subseteq B_{X}(\mathbf{x},t)\subseteq B\subseteq H\quad\text{and}\\
\mathcal{L}_{X}\left(B_{X}(\mathbf{x+\mathbf{y}},2^{1/d}\eta^{1/d}t)\right)=2\eta\mathcal{L}\left(B_{X}(\mathbf{x},t)\right).

Therefore, by (
B.3
) we have that
B_{X}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y},2^{1/d}\eta^{1/d}t)\cap A\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Let
\mathbf{z}\in B_{X}(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y},2^{1/d}\eta^{1/d}t)\cap A\cap B
and set
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathbf{z}-(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})\right\|_{X}\leq 2^{1/d}\eta^{1/d}t=2^{1+\frac{1}{d}}\eta^{1/d}\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\leq 4\eta^{1/d}\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\quad\text{so that}

(B.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{y^{\prime}}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+4\eta^{1/d}\right)\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\leq 2\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}<\delta.

We may now use the hypothesis
f|_{A}=g|_{A}
, (
B.4
), (
B.2
) and
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x}\in A\cap B
,
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}\in B
to write

\left\|g(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})-g(\mathbf{x})-Df(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{y})\right\|_{Y}\leq\\
\left\|g(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})-g(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|f(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}^{\prime})-f(\mathbf{x})-Df(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\right\|_{Y}+\left\|Df(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{y})\right\|_{Y}\\
\leq\operatorname{Lip}(g|_{B})4\eta^{1/d}\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}+\eta\left(1+4\eta^{1/d}\right)\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}+\operatorname{Lip}(f|_{B})4\eta^{1/d}\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\leq\varepsilon\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X},

where we apply (
B.1
) to get the final inequality (bound the three coefficients in order by
1/4
,
1/2
and
1/4
). Since
\varepsilon>0
and
\mathbf{y}\in B_{X}(\mathbf{0},\delta/2)\setminus\left\{\mathbf{0}\right\}
were arbitrary, this establishes the Fréchet differentiability of
g
at
\mathbf{x}
with
Dg(\mathbf{x})=Df(\mathbf{x})
. Since the roles of
f
and
g
in the above argument are symmetric, this proves the if and only if statement of (
i
).
∎

Lemma B.1
.

Let
(X,\left\|-\right\|_{X})
,
(Y,\left\|-\right\|_{Y})
be normed spaces,
H\subseteq X
be open,
L\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
f\colon H\to Y
be a Lipschitz mapping and
\mathbf{z}\in H
.

Then the set
D:=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}\colon f^{\prime}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u})\text{ exists and equals }L(\mathbf{u})\right\}
is closed.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbf{u}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
D
with limit
\mathbf{u}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathbf{u}_{j}\in X
. We show that
\mathbf{u}\in D
. Given
\varepsilon>0
we choose choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that

\left\|\mathbf{u}_{k}-\mathbf{u}\right\|_{X}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3\left(\operatorname{Lip}(f)+\left\|L\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}+1\right)}.

Next choose
\delta>0
small enough so that

\left\|f(\mathbf{z}+t\mathbf{u}_{k})-f(\mathbf{z})-tL(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right\|_{Y}\leq\frac{\varepsilon\left|t\right|}{3}\qquad\text{ for all $t\in(0,\delta)$.}

Then, for all
t\in(0,\delta)
we have

\left\|f(\mathbf{z}+t\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{z})-tL(\mathbf{u})\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|f(\mathbf{z}+t\mathbf{u})-f(\mathbf{z}+t\mathbf{u}_{k})\right\|_{Y}\\
+\left\|f(\mathbf{z}+t\mathbf{u}_{k})-f(\mathbf{z})-tL(\mathbf{u}_{k})\right\|_{Y}+\left|t\right|\left\|L(\mathbf{u}_{k})-L(\mathbf{u})\right\|_{Y}\\
\leq\frac{\varepsilon\left|t\right|}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon\left|t\right|}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon\left|t\right|}{3}=\varepsilon\left|t\right|.

We conclude that
\mathbf{u}\in D
.
∎

Theorem B.2
.

Let
(X,\left\|-\right\|_{X})
,
(Y,\left\|-\right\|_{Y})
be normed spaces, where
X
is finite-dimensional,
H\subseteq X
be open,
\Phi\colon H\to\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
be continuous and
f\colon H\to Y
be a Lipschitz mapping such that
Df(\mathbf{x})=\Phi(\mathbf{x})
for Lebesgue almost every
\mathbf{x}\in H
. Then
f\in C^{1}(H,Y)
and
Df(\mathbf{x})=\Phi(\mathbf{x})
for every
\mathbf{x}\in H
.

Proof.

The proof goes by induction on
d=\dim X
. For
d=1
it suffices to observe that

f(y+t)=f(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(y+s)(1)\,ds

for all
y\in H
and
t\in(0,\operatorname{dist}(y,X\setminus H))
.

Assume now that
d\geq 2
and the theorem is valid whenever the domain space has dimension less than
d
. Suppose
\dim X=d
, fix
\mathbf{x}\in H
and
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{S}_{X}
. We complete the proof by showing that the directional derivative
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v})
exists and equals
\Phi(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{v})
. Given any
(d-1)
-dimensional subspace
U
of
X
, not containing
\mathbf{v}
we have, by Fubini’s Theorem, that
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
\mathbf{z}\in\left(\mathbf{x}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{v}\right)\cap H
has the property that for
\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
-a.e.
\mathbf{y}\in\left(\mathbf{z}+U\right)\cap H
the mapping
f
is differentiable at
\mathbf{y}
and
Df(\mathbf{y})=\Phi(\mathbf{y})
. By the induction hypothesis, we get that
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
\mathbf{z}\in\left(\mathbf{x}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{v}\right)\cap H
is such that the directional derivatives
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{u})
exist and equals
\Phi(\mathbf{y})(\mathbf{u})
for every
\mathbf{y}\in\left(\mathbf{z}+U\right)\cap H
and every
\mathbf{u}\in U
, in particular for
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{z}
. We conclude, by applying this argument to each
U
from a countable dense subset of the set of
(d-1)
-dimensional subspaces of
X
not containing
\mathbf{v}
, that
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
\mathbf{z}\in\left(\mathbf{x}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{v}\right)\cap H
has the property that all directional derivatives
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u}_{j})
for a dense sequence

(\mathbf{u}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{S}_{X}\setminus\left\{\mathbf{v},-\mathbf{v}\right\}
exist and are given by the formula
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{u}_{j})=\Phi(\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{u}_{j})
. By Lemma
B
this formula extends to all
\mathbf{u}\in\overline{\left\{\mathbf{u}_{j}\colon j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}}=\mathbb{S}_{X}
. In particular, it extends to
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{v}
, giving
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})=\Phi(\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{v})
for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
\mathbf{z}\in\left(\mathbf{x}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{v}\right)\cap H
. Finally, by the induction hypothesis, this implies
f^{\prime}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{v})=\Phi(\mathbf{z})(\mathbf{v})
for all
\mathbf{z}\in\left(\mathbf{x}+\mathbb{R}\mathbf{v}\right)\cap H
, in particular for
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{x}
.
∎

The following lemma verifies that the minimum in the definition of
\mathfrak{C}(T)
, see Definition
4.1
, is attained.

Lemma C.0
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normed vector spaces and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{\mathcal{L}(X,Y)}\}
be of finite rank
l
. Then the infimum

\mathfrak{C}(T):=\inf\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq l}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}\circ T(\cdot)\,\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\colon\begin{array}[]{c}W=(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l})\text{ is a basis of $T(X)$},\\
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}^{*}\in T(X)^{*}\text{ is dual to }W\end{array}\right\}

is attained, so it is in fact a minimum.

Proof.

Whenever
(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l})
and
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{*},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l}^{*}
contribute to the set over which the infimum defining
\mathfrak{C}(T)
is defined, the operators
\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}\circ T(\cdot)\mathbf{w}_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, for
1\leq i\leq l
, are invariant under rescaling of
\mathbf{w}_{i}
. Therefore, the set in the definition of
\mathfrak{C}(T)
is unchanged if we only allow contributions from bases
W
with all vectors
\mathbf{w}_{i}
of norm
1
for all
1\leq i\leq l
. We will work with this equivalent definition of
\mathfrak{C}(T)
in the present proof.
Let
Z:=T(X)\subseteq Y
and
\mathcal{W}\subseteq(\mathbb{S}_{Z})^{l}
be the collection of ordered bases of
Z
, consisting of vectors of norm
1
.
For each
1\leq s\leq l
and

W=(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{l})\in\mathcal{W}
, let

\mathfrak{C}(T,W,s)=\max_{1\leq j\leq s}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{*}\circ T(\cdot)\,\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}.

Then
\mathfrak{C}(T)=\inf_{W\in\mathcal{W}}\mathfrak{C}(T,W,l)
.
Let
W_{n}\in\mathcal{W}
be such that
\mathfrak{C}(T,W_{n},l)\to\mathfrak{C}(T)
. Let
W=(\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{l})
be the limit, in
{(\mathbb{S}_{Z})}^{l}
, of a convergent subsequence of
W_{n}
.
Assume
W\notin\mathcal{W}
, i.e. the vectors are linearly dependent. Let
k\leq l
be the smallest index such that
\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{k}
are linearly dependent,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\mathbf{u}_{k}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k-1}a_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}
and denote
A=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k-1}|a_{i}|
. Note that
k\geq 2
as
\left\|\mathbf{u}_{1}\right\|_{Y}=1
.
For each
\alpha>0
, let
W^{(\alpha)}=(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(\alpha)})\in\mathcal{W}
be such that

(C.1)

\mathfrak{C}(T,W^{(\alpha)},l)<\mathfrak{C}(T)+\alpha

and

\left\|\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(\alpha)}-\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\|_{Y}<\alpha
for all
1\leq i\leq l
. Note that each
W^{(\alpha)}
may be chosen from the sequence
(W_{n})
. Letting
C_{\alpha}=\left\|(\mathbf{w}_{k}^{(\alpha)})^{*}\right\|_{Z^{*}}
, we get

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{w}_{k}^{(\alpha)})^{*}(\mathbf{w}_{k}^{(\alpha)})\leq(\mathbf{w}_{k}^{(\alpha)})^{*}(\mathbf{u}_{k})+\alpha C_{\alpha}=(\mathbf{w}_{k}^{(\alpha)})^{*}(\sum_{1\leq i\leq k-1}a_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i})+\alpha C_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathbf{w}_{k}^{(\alpha)})^{*}(\sum_{1\leq i\leq k-1}a_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(\alpha)})+\alpha AC_{\alpha}+\alpha C_{\alpha}=0+\alpha(A+1)C_{\alpha}=\alpha(A+1)C_{\alpha}.

Hence
\left\|(\mathbf{w}_{k}^{(\alpha)})^{*}\right\|_{Z^{*}}=C_{\alpha}\to\infty
as
\alpha\to 0
.

Fix a null sequence
\alpha_{n}\in(0,1)
, let
s\leq k
be the smallest index such that
\left\|(\mathbf{w}_{s}^{(\alpha_{n})})^{*}\right\|_{Z^{*}}
is unbounded and let
\beta_{n}\to 0
be a subsequence of
(\alpha_{n})
such that
\left\|(\mathbf{w}_{s}^{(\beta_{n})})^{*}\right\|_{Z^{*}}\to\infty
. Then
\left\|{(\mathbf{w}_{s}^{(\beta_{n})})^{*}}\circ T(\cdot)\mathbf{u}_{s}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}=\left\|(\mathbf{w}_{s}^{(\beta_{n})})^{*}\circ T(\cdot)\right\|_{X^{*}}\to\infty
, while
\left\|{(\mathbf{w}_{i}^{(\beta_{n})})^{*}}\circ T(\cdot)\mathbf{u}_{i}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}
are bounded for each
1\leq i\leq s-1
, implying
\mathfrak{C}(T,W^{(\beta_{n})},s)\to\infty
. Thus, by (
C.1
),

\mathfrak{C}(T)\geq\mathfrak{C}(T,W^{(\beta_{n})},l)-\beta_{n}\geq\mathfrak{C}(T,W^{(\beta_{n})},s)-1\to\infty

a contradiction.
∎

The following theorem is a version of an observation in

Theorem C.1
.

Let
X
be a finite-dimensional normed space,
E\subseteq X
be a compact purely unrectifiable set and
T\in X^{*}\setminus\left\{0\right\}

\varepsilon>0

\sup\left\{\mathcal{H}^{1}(G\cap\gamma(\mathbb{R}))\colon\gamma\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathbb{R},X),\right.\\
\left.T(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\geq\varepsilon\left\|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right\|_{X}\left\|T\right\|_{X^{*}}\text{for Lebesgue a.e.\ $t\in\mathbb{R}$}\right\}\leq\varepsilon.

The statement is obtained by applying

denote the Euclidean norm on

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{X}\leq M\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{E},\qquad\text{for all $\mathbf{x}\in X$, and}

Lemma C.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normed spaces,
E\subseteq U\subseteq X
be sets where
E
is compact and
U
is open, and let
g\in C^{1}(U,Y)
and
\theta>0
. Then there exists
\delta\in(0,\theta)
such that for every
\mathbf{x}\in E
and every
\mathbf{y}\in X
with
\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\leq\delta
we have

\left\|g(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})-g(\mathbf{x})-Dg(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{y})\right\|_{Y}\leq\theta\left\|y\right\|_{X}.

Proof.

For each
\mathbf{x}\in E
choose
\delta_{\mathbf{x}}>0
small enough so that

\left\|Dg(\mathbf{z})-Dg(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq\theta\qquad\text{for all }\mathbf{z}\in B_{X}(\mathbf{x},2\delta_{\mathbf{x}})\subseteq U.

The collection of sets
(B_{X}(\mathbf{x},\delta_{\mathbf{x}}))_{\mathbf{x}\in E}
is an open cover of the compact set
E
; it therefore admits a finite subcover
B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\delta_{1}),B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{2},\delta_{2}),\ldots,B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{N},\delta_{N})
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, where for
j=1,\ldots,N
we relabel
\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{j}}
as
\delta_{j}
.

Let
\delta:=\min\left\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{N}\right\}>0
,
\mathbf{x}\in E
and
\mathbf{y}\in X
with
0<\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}\leq\delta
. Then there exists
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
such that
\mathbf{x}\in B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{i},\delta_{i})
and so
[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}]\subseteq B_{X}(\mathbf{x}_{i},2\delta_{i})\subseteq U
. Set
\mathbf{e}:=\frac{\mathbf{y}}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}}
and let
\varphi\in Y^{*}
be a functional with
\left\|\varphi\right\|_{Y^{*}}=1
. Then

\left|\varphi\Bigl(g(\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y})-g(\mathbf{x})-Dg(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{y})\Bigr)\right|\\
=\left|\int_{0}^{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}}D(\varphi\circ g)(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{e})(\mathbf{e})-D(\varphi\circ g)(\mathbf{x})(\mathbf{e})\,dt\right|\\
\leq\int_{0}^{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}}\left\|Dg(\mathbf{x}+t\mathbf{e})-Dg(\mathbf{x})\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\,dt\leq\theta\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|_{X}.

Taking supremums in the above inequality over all
\varphi\in Y^{*}
completes the proof.
∎

Remark C.1
.

If
X
is a finite-dimensional normed space,
E\subseteq U\subseteq X
are such that
E
is compact and
U
is open, then then there exists an open
U_{0}
such that
E\subseteq U_{0}\subseteq\overline{U_{0}}\subseteq U
and the Lebesgue measure of
\partial U_{0}
is zero. The latter could be obtained by choosing
U_{0}
in the form of a finite union of open balls with centres in
E
.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
X
be a normed space,
E\subseteq V\subseteq X
be sets where
E
is compact and
V
is open,
(G_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of open subsets of
X
which contain
E
and let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a smooth, locally finite partition of unity with supports contained in
V
. Then there is a number
K\in\mathbb{N}
and an open set
U\subseteq X
such that

E\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{U}\subseteq V\cap\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}G_{k}

and

\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{k})\cap U=\emptyset\quad\text{for all}\quad k>K.

In the case that
X
is finite-dimensional,
U
may be chosen so that additionally
\partial U
has Lebesgue measure zero.

Proof.

For each point
\mathbf{p}\in E
we may choose a radius
r_{\mathbf{p}}>0
such that
\overline{B}_{X}(\mathbf{p},r_{\mathbf{p}})\subseteq V
and the set

M_{\mathbf{p}}:=\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}\colon\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{k}\cap B_{X}(\mathbf{p},r_{\mathbf{p}})\neq\emptyset\right\}

is finite. The collection of balls
(B_{X}(\mathbf{p},r_{\mathbf{p}}))_{\mathbf{p}\in E}
is then an open cover of the compact set
E
. Accordingly, it has a finite subcover. In other words, there exists a finite subset
F
of
E
such that

E\subseteq\bigcup_{\mathbf{p}\in F}B_{X}(\mathbf{p},r_{\mathbf{p}}).

Let

K:=\max\bigcup_{\mathbf{p}\in F}M_{\mathbf{p}}

and

V^{\prime}:=\left(\bigcup_{\mathbf{p}\in F}B_{X}(\mathbf{p},r_{\mathbf{p}})\right)\cap\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}G_{k}.

Finally, we apply Remark
C
to define
U\subseteq V^{\prime}
with the required properties. The assertions of the lemma for
K
and
U
are now readily verified.
∎

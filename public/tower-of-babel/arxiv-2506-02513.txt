Characterization of the D’Alembertian by the Poincaré Invariance Dedicated to Professor Takayoshi Ogawa on the occasion of his sixtieth birthday
Hiromichi Nakazato, Tohru Ozawa

Abstract
Many physical models are described by partial differential equations and the most important mathematical structure of the equations is governed by the corresponding linear partial differential operators. Those linear partial differential operators are sometimes determined by the symmetry under the group of motion. In this paper, the d’Alembertian is shown to be characterized as the only linear partial differential operator of the second order that is invariant under the Poincaré group and dilations in the Minkowski space-time ℝ × ℝ n \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n} . The method of proof depends on the analysis of the invariance of the corresponding polynomial in space-time under the time reflections and space rotations. Keywords: D’Alembertian, Poincaré Invariance, linear partial differential operators, group of motion, Lorentz transformation. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010): 35Q75, 35L55, 35E20, 35Q60.

Introduction

Many physical models are described by partial differential equations and the most important mathematical structure of the equations is governed by the corresponding linear partial differential operators. Those linear partial differential operators are sometimes determined by the symmetry under the group of motion.

Symmetry is a decisive perspective in both mathematics and physics.
In most cases, symmetry is recognized as invariance under specific operations such as permutations, translations, rotations, reflections, dilations and so on.
Symmetry based on the invariance under transformations forms an
essential concept in the Galois theory, crystallography, Roger Penrose’s quasicrystals, Felix Klein’s “Erlangen Program” of geometry, the Cartan geometry as a natural generalization of “Erlangen Program”
Sharpe
, Emmy Noether’s principle as well as the notion of gauge invariance introduced by Hermann Weyl in classical and quantum field theories
FariaMelo
;
Scheck
;
Zeidler1
;
Zeidler2
;
Zeidler3
, and in the classification of elementary particles of the standard model
FariaMelo
;
Zeidler1
;
Zeidler2
;
Zeidler3
.

Among others, the Lorentz transformation plays a central role to support the concept of symmetry in relativistic physics.
It is well known that Hendrik Lorentz introduced the transformation to explain the experimental result testifying the invariance of the speed of light, that Henri Poincaré proved the invariance of the Maxwell equations under the Lorentz transformation, and that Albert Einstein derived the Lorentz transformation from the principle of the invariance of the speed of light and of the special relativity
Jackson
;
Scheck
;
Zeidler1
;
Zeidler2
;
Zeidler3
.
These facts made a clear account of why natural laws in relativity must obey the Lorentz invariance.

In this paper, we make a clear account of how the Lorentz invariance determines the structure of partial differential operators in space-time with constant coefficients.
To be more specific, we study the symmetry of partial differential operators in space-time from the viewpoint of the invariance under the Lorentz transformations with translations and dilations.
As a corollary, the d’Alemertian is shown to be characterized as the only second-order partial differential operator with constant coefficients that is invariant under Lorentz transformations and dilations.
This reveals a special status of the d’Alembertian in the vector spaces consisting of all partial differential operators in space-time on the basis of the symmetry associated with the Poincaré group (generated by Lorentz transformations and translations) and dilations.

The proof of the main result depends on the reduction of the problem to the analysis of the corresponding polynomial in space-time, where the correspondence is given by exponential functions as in the theory of linear partial differential operators through the Fourier (or Laplace) transform
Hormander
, thereby leading us to characterization problem of the polynomial by the Lorentz transformation.
Then we make a separation of the space and time variables and show the invariance of the polynomial under time reflections and space rotations.
Finally, we use the characterization of rotation-invariant polynomials in space variables.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce basic notation in the Minkowski space-time and state our main results.
In Section
3
, we give proofs of the main results.
Section
4
is the Appendix, where the characterization of rotation invariant polynomials as well as of the Laplacian is given in the Euclidean space. In Section
5
, we discuss the corresponding problem in the quantum mechanics.

We now state our main results.

Theorem 1
.

For any partial differential operator in space-time
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
of order
m
of the form

L=\sum_{j+|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{j\bm{\alpha}}\partial_{t}^{j}\bm{\partial}^{\bm{\alpha}},

with
a_{j\bm{\alpha}}\in{\rm C}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
and
\sum\limits_{j+|\bm{\alpha}|=m}|a_{j\bm{\alpha}}|\not=0
, the following three statements are equivalent.

(1)

L
is Poincaré invariant.
Namely,

(i)

For any
y\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
,
T_{y}^{*}Lu=LT_{y}^{*}u
.

(ii)

For any
\Lambda\in{\rm O}(1,n)
and any
u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
,
\Lambda^{*}Lu=L\Lambda^{*}u
.

(2)

All coefficients of
L
are constants and
L
is Lorentz invariant.
Namely,

(i)

For any
(j,\bm{\alpha})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
j+|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m
and any

a_{j\bm{\alpha}}(x)=a_{j\bm{\alpha}}(0)
.

(ii)

For any
\Lambda\in{\rm O}(1,n)
and any
u\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{C})
,
\Lambda^{*}Lu=L\Lambda^{*}u
.

(3)

L
is given by a polynomial in
\square
of order
[m/2]\coloneqq{\rm max}\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0};k\leq m/2\}
.
Namely, there exists
(b_{j};0\leq j\leq[m/2])\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
b_{[m/2]}\not=0
and
L=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{[m/2]}b_{j}\square^{j}
.

Corollary 1
.

Any Poincaré invariant partial differential operator of the second order in space-time
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
is represented as
L=\alpha\square+\beta
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
.

Corollary 2
.

Any Poincaré and dilation invariant partial differential operator of the second order in space-time
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
is represented as
L=\alpha\square
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
.

Theorem
1
shows that the Poincaré invariance determines the structure of the space of partial differential operators that is exclusively generated by the d’Alembertian and that the Lorentz invariance determines the structure of the space of partial differential operators with constant coefficients that is exclusively generated by the d’Alembertian as well.
Conversely, Corollary

characterizes the d’Alembertian up to mass term in terms of the Poincaré [resp. Lorentz] invariance of partial differential operators [resp. constant coefficients] on space-time.
Corollary

shows that the mass term can be deleted away under the dilation invariance.

e_{\bm{\xi}}\in{\rm C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
by

e_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})=\exp(\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{x})\quad\hbox{\rm for $\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

(4.2)

Then we see that
e_{\bm{\xi}}(0)=1
and for any
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
,

\bm{\partial}^{\bm{\alpha}}e_{\bm{\xi}}=\bm{\xi}^{\bm{\alpha}}e_{\bm{\xi}}.

(4.3)

For
\bm{x},\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

p(\bm{x},\bm{\xi})=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x})\bm{\xi}^{\bm{\alpha}}.

(4.4)

By (
4.2
), (
4.3
), and (
4.4
), we have

(Le_{\bm{\xi}})(\bm{x})=p(\bm{x},\bm{\xi})e_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})

(4.5)

for any
\bm{x},\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We use the equality (
4.5
) in the subsequent argument.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: It suffices to prove (1)(i)
\Rightarrow
(2)(i).
The translation invariance described in (1)(i) with
u=e_{\bm{\xi}}
implies

T_{\bm{y}}^{*}Le_{\bm{\xi}}=LT_{\bm{y}}^{*}e_{\bm{\xi}}\quad\hbox{\rm for any}\quad\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(4.6)

The LHS of (
4.6
) at
\bm{x}
is given by

(T_{\bm{y}}^{*}Le_{\bm{\xi}})(\bm{x})=(Le_{\bm{\xi}})(\bm{x}-\bm{y})=p(\bm{x}-\bm{y},\bm{\xi})e_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x}-\bm{y}),

(4.7)

where we have used (
4.5
), while the RHS of (
4.6
) is given by

\displaystyle(LT_{\bm{y}}^{*}e_{\bm{\xi}})(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x})(\bm{\partial}^{\bm{\alpha}}T_{\bm{y}}^{*}e_{\bm{\xi}})(\bm{x})=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x})(T_{\bm{y}}^{*}\bm{\partial}^{\bm{\alpha}}e_{\bm{\xi}})(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x})\bm{\xi}^{\bm{\alpha}}e_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x}-\bm{y})=p(\bm{x},\bm{\xi})e_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x}-\bm{y}),

(4.8)

where we have used (
4.3
) and (
4.4
).
By (
4.6
), (
4.7
), and (
4.8
), with
\bm{y}=\bm{x}
, we have

p(\bm{0},\bm{\xi})=p(\bm{x},\bm{\xi})

(4.9)

since
e_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{0})=1
.
Since (
4.9
) holds for any
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x})=a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{0})

for any
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m
, as required.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: We define the following polynomial
p
in
\bm{\xi}
of order
m
by

p(\bm{\xi})=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(0)\bm{\xi}^{\bm{\alpha}}

(4.10)

and for
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
, we define the following homogeneous polynomial
p_{j}
of order
j
by

p_{j}(\bm{\xi})=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|=j}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(0)\bm{\xi}^{\bm{\alpha}}.

(4.11)

Then
p
and
L
are represented as
p=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{m}p_{j}
and

L=p(\bm{\partial})=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{0})\bm{\partial}^{\bm{\alpha}}.

(4.12)

By (
4.3
), (
4.10
), and (
4.12
), we obtain

Le_{\bm{\xi}}=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{0})\bm{\partial}^{\bm{\alpha}}e_{\bm{\xi}}=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|\leq m}a_{\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{0})\bm{\xi}^{\bm{\alpha}}e_{\bm{\xi}}=p(\bm{\xi})e_{\bm{\xi}}.

(4.13)

For any
R\in{\rm O}(n)
, we apply (
4.13
) with
\bm{\xi}\mapsto{}^{t}\!R\bm{\xi}
to obtain

p(^{t}\!R\bm{\xi})e_{{}^{t}\!R\bm{\xi}}=Le_{{}^{t}\!R\bm{\xi}}=LR^{*}e_{\bm{\xi}},

(4.14)

where we have used

(R^{*}e_{\bm{\xi}})(\bm{x})=e_{\bm{\xi}}(R\bm{x})=\exp(\bm{\xi}\cdot R\bm{x})=\exp(^{t}\!R\bm{\xi}\cdot\bm{x})=e_{{}^{t}\!R\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x}).

(4.15)

By assumption (2)(ii), we have

R^{*}Le_{\bm{\xi}}=LR^{*}e_{\bm{\xi}},

(4.16)

for any
R\in{\rm O}(n)
and
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The RHS of (
4.16
) is given by (
4.14
), while the LHS of (
4.16
) is computed as

R^{*}Le_{\bm{\xi}}=R^{*}p(\bm{\xi})e_{\bm{\xi}}=p(\bm{\xi})R^{*}e_{\bm{\xi}}=p(\bm{\xi})e_{{}^{t}\!R\bm{\xi}},

(4.17)

where we have used (
4.5
), (
4.10
), and (
4.15
).
Therefore, it follows from (
4.16
), (
4.14
), and (
4.17
), that

p(^{t}\!R\bm{\xi})e_{{}^{t}\!R\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})=p(\bm{\xi})e_{{}^{t}\!R\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})

(4.18)

for any
\bm{x},\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
R\in{\rm O}(n)
.
Evaluating (
4.18
) at
\bm{x}=\bm{0}
, we have

p(^{t}\!R\bm{\xi})=p(\bm{\xi})

(4.19)

for any
R\in{\rm O}(n)
and
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Applying (

p(R\bm{\xi})=p(\bm{\xi})

(4.20)

for any
R\in{\rm O}(n)
and
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By homogeneity, for any

p(r\bm{\xi})=\sum_{j=0}^{m}p_{j}(\bm{\xi})r^{j}.

(4.21)

By (
4.19
) and (

\sum_{j=0}^{m}p_{j}(R\bm{\xi})r^{j}=p(rR\bm{\xi})=p(R(r\bm{\xi}))=p(r\bm{\xi})=\sum_{j=0}^{m}p_{j}(\bm{\xi})r^{j}

(4.22)

holds for any

,
R\in{\rm O}(n)
and
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
It follows from (

R^{*}p_{j}=p_{j}

(4.23)

holds for any

and

By Proposition
1

for

Therefore

is represented as

We notice that

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
: It suffices to prove the Euclidean motion invariance of the Laplacian
\triangle
.
The commutativity between space derivatives and translations implies the invariance of
\triangle
under space translations.
For

, we write
R=(R^{j}\,_{k})
.
As in (
3.41
), we have

\partial_{k}R^{*}=R^{j}\,_{k}R^{*}\partial_{j}

which in turn implies

\displaystyle\triangle R^{*}

\displaystyle=\delta^{jk}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}R^{*}=\delta^{jk}\partial_{j}(R^{\ell}\,_{k}R^{*}\partial_{\ell})=\delta^{jk}R^{\ell}\,_{k}(\partial_{j}R^{*})\partial_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\delta^{jk}R^{\ell}\,_{k}(R^{i}\,_{j}R^{*}\partial_{i})\partial_{\ell}=\delta^{jk}R^{\ell}\,_{k}R^{i}\,_{j}R^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{\ell}=\delta^{\ell i}R^{*}\partial_{i}\partial_{\ell}

\displaystyle=R^{*}\triangle,

as required.

Zariski density of modular points in the Eisenstein case
Xinyao Zhang

Abstract
. In this article, we study the Zariski closure of modular points in the two-dimensional universal deformation space when the residual Galois representation is reducible. Unlike the previous approaches in the residually irreducible case from Gouvêa-Mazur, Böckle and Allen, our method relies on local-global compatibility results, potential pro-modularity arguments and a non-ordinary finiteness result between the local deformation ring at p p and the global deformation ring. This allows us to construct sufficiently many non-ordinary regular de Rham points whose modularity is guaranteed by the recent progress on the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture. Also, we will discuss some applications of our main results, including the equidimensionality of certain big Hecke algebras and big R = 𝕋 R=\mathbb{T} theorems in the residually reducible case.



1.
Introduction

Let
E
be a number field and
p
be a prime. Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of finite places of
E
containing all those lying above
p
. Let
\bar{\rho}:\Gal(\overline{E}/E)\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}})
be a continuous Galois representation unramified outside
\Sigma
. By classical Galois deformation theory (see
[
BBD+13
, Proposition 2.3.1, page 42]
), there exists a universal pseudo-deformation ring
R_{E}^{\mathrm{ps}}
parametrizing all pseudo-representations of
\mathrm{tr}\bar{\rho}
(for the profinite group
G_{E,\Sigma}
). The geometry of the set of modular/automorphic points in the universal deformation space
\Spec R_{E}^{\mathrm{ps}}
has attracted much interest among number theorists. For example, we wonder whether it is Zariski dense.

1.1.
The previous work

We first recall some previous progress in the residually irreducible case. Note that when
\bar{\rho}
is irreducible,
R_{E}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is just the universal deformation ring of
\bar{\rho}
(see
[
BBD+13
, Theorem 2.4.1, page 44]
).

In
[
GM98
]
, Gouvêa and Mazur first studied this question in the case
E=\mathbb{Q}
,
\Sigma=\{p\}
, and
\bar{\rho}
is irreducible, unobstructed and modular (it is known by Serre’s modularity conjecture in
[
KW09a
]
and
[
KW09b
]
at present). Under these settings, they showed that modular points are Zariski dense in
\Spec R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}
, and
R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is isomorphic to some big Hecke algebra, using the theory of eigencurves (the so-called
infinite fern
).

Later in
[
Böc01
]
, Böckle refined Gouvêa-Mazur’s strategy, allowing a much broader generalization. He first showed that each irreducible component of
\Spec R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}
contains a smooth modular point under some technical assumptions, using explicit calculations of some local deformation rings (in the Fontaine-Laffaille case or the ordinary case) and small
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem in the sense of Taylor-Wiles. The existence of a smooth point implies that the irreducible component has the correct Krull dimension. Then combining the infinite fern arguments, we can show that modular points are Zariski dense in each irreducible component and hence in
\Spec R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}
. In
[
Eme11a
]
, Emerton generalized Böckle’s result to obtain a big
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem (see
[
Eme11a
, Theorem 1.2.3]
) and further proved the two-dimensional Fontaine-Mazur conjecture in the non-ordinary case under the Taylor–Wiles hypothesis.

Following Böckle’s strategy, Allen proved similar results in the context of polarized Galois representations for CM fields and in the Hilbert modular case for some totally real fields in
[
All19
]
. The main new idea there is to use the finiteness of the universal global (polarized) deformation ring
R^{\operatorname{pol}}
over the local framed deformation ring
R^{\operatorname{loc}}
at
p
(see
[
AC14
]
). After assuming some local conditions on
\bar{\rho}|_{G_{E_{v}}}
for
v|p
, the local framed deformation ring will be a regular local ring. Then by intersection theory (see
[
All19
, Lemma 1.1.2]
), Allen showed that each irreducible component of
\Spec R^{\operatorname{pol}}
contains a modular point with prescribed
p
-adic Hodge theoretic type, which is also smooth by using an argument of the vanishing of the geometric Bloch-Kato Selmer group (see
[
All16
]
). The Zariski density follows from Chenevier’s generalization of the infinite fern result
[
Che11
]
.

Recently in
[
Deo23
]
, Deo proved some big
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem for
E=\mathbb{Q}
in the residually reducible case under some technical assumptions, especially the cyclicity of certain cohomology group. He also followed Böckle’s strategy and used Pan’s ordinary finiteness result
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 5.1.1]
instead of small
R=\mathbb{T}
theorems to find a smooth point in each irreducible component of
\Spec R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}
.

We emphasize here that in each work mentioned above, an infinite fern argument is necessary. One can see
[
GM98
]
,
[
Che11
]
,
[
Eme11b
]
,
[
HMS22
]
and
[
HS22
]
for more discussions on this topic.

1.2.
Main results

Now we state our main results in this paper.

We first fix some notations. Let
p
be an odd prime. Let
K
be a
p
-adic local field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and the residue field
\mathbb{F}
.

Let
F
be an abelian totally real field in which
p
splits completely. Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of finite places of
F
containing
\Sigma_{p}
, the set of all places lying above
p
. Let
\bar{\chi}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be a continuous, totally odd character and assume that
\bar{\chi}
can be extended to a character of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
\chi:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a continuous character lifting
\bar{\chi}
such that
\chi|_{G_{F_{v}}}
is de Rham at each place
v|p
. Write
\omega
for the mod
p
cyclotomic character.

Note that
\bar{T}=1+\bar{\chi}
is a pseudo-representation of
G_{F,\Sigma}
. Let
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring of
\bar{T}
, and let
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring of the pseudo-representation
\bar{T}
with determinant
\chi
. We say that a point of
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}
(or
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
) is
modular
if it corresponds to a Galois representation arising from a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. We say a modular point is
global crystalline
(or just
crystalline
) if the corresponding Galois representation is crystalline at each place
v|p
.

Theorem 1.2.1

(Theorem
5.3.7
& Theorem
5.3.8
)
.

Assume that
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\neq\omega
for any
v|p
if
p=3
.

1) The Zariski closure of the set of all modular points in
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is the union of all irreducible components of dimension
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. If further
\chi
is crystalline at each
v|p
, then we can replace modular points by crystalline points in the previous sentence.

2) The Zariski closure of the set of all crystalline points in
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is the union of all irreducible components of dimension
2+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Before explaining the strategy of the proof of our main theorems, we indicate some obstructions to the question in the residually reducible case.

First, the universal pseudo-deformation ring in the residually reducible case is much more complicated and mysterious than that in the residually irreducible case, or equivalently, the universal deformation ring. We do not know how to use Galois cohomology to describe the dimension of the tangent space and the minimal number of quotient relations as in
[
BBD+13
, Proposition 1.5.1, page 30]
. In general, the universal pseudo-deformation ring is not a complete intersection ring. See
[
Deo22
]
for some calculations.

Second, we have hardly any small
R=\mathbb{T}
theorems in the residually reducible case so far. The patching results in
[
SW99
]
,
[
Pan22a
]
are established only for certain special primes (called “nice primes”) under restrictive conditions, but these suffice to deduce the automorphy via soluble base change. Also see
[
Tho14
]
and
[
ANT20
]
in the higher dimensional case. However, we do not know how to find such a special prime in general.

In addition, although the arguments of the vanishing of the Bloch-Kato Selmer group and infinite fern are likely available in the residually reducible case for most cases (see
[
NT23
]
and
[
HS22
]
respectively), we hope to add fewer assumptions in our context.

To avoid these difficulties, we adopt a new strategy instead of Böckle’s. Our main idea is to find sufficiently many non-ordinary regular de Rham points in the universal pseudo-deformation space. Unlike the Galois theoretic method used in
[
FKP22
]
, ours is based on the proof of the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture (or automorphy lifting theorems in general). Here we explain the main steps of the proof of Theorem
1.2.1
.

Step 1. Local-global compatibility results

Write
\mathbb{T}
for the big Hecke algebra for completed cohomology of quaternionic forms and let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}
. Then it is classical that there exists a pseudo-representation
T_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
sending
\Frob_{v}
to the Hecke operator
T_{v}
for
v\notin\Sigma
. Write
\overline{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}/\mathfrak{m}=\mathbb{F}
for the residual pseudo-representation. For each place
v|p
, let
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring of the pseudo-representation
\overline{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}|_{G_{F_{v}}}
for
G_{F_{v}}
with a fixed determinant. Recall that by the local-global compatibility arguments, we can show that the natural map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
induced by the universal property is finite (see
[
Pan22a
, Corollary 3.5.8]
and
[
Zha24a
, Corollary 3.3.7]
). In this part, we will prove a stronger finiteness result.

Consider the map
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
and let
I_{v}
be its kernel. Recall that the local-global compatibility argument (see
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 3.5.3]
and
[
Zha24a
, Theorem 3.3.3]
) shows that the two actions of
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
on the Hecke module
\mathrm{m}_{v}
, the Galois side
\tau_{\Gal}
and the automorphic side
\tau_{\mathrm{Aut}}
, are actually the same. Reviewing this proof, we can also conclude that the ideal
I_{v}
is the intersection of all
global regular de Rham
primes. Here for a global regular de Rham prime, we mean that it is the inverse image of a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p]
via the composite map
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p]/\mathfrak{p}
, satisfying that the corresponding Galois representation
\rho(\mathfrak{p})|_{G_{F_{v}}}
is de Rham of distinct Hodge-Tate weights. We emphasize that a global regular de Rham prime of
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
not only carries the information of
p
-adic Hodge theoretic type, but also comes from a restriction of a global Galois representation.

Write
R_{v}:=R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}/I_{v}
. The ring
R_{v}
was first studied by Paškūnas in
[
Paš21
]
. (Here we point out that
R_{v}
is not Kisin’s potential crystalline/semi-stable deformation ring, but it might be interesting to compare
R_{v}
with those objects defined in
[
Kis08
]
. See Remark
3.3.8
for more discussions.) If
\overline{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\neq\eta+\eta\omega
for any character
\eta
when
p=3
, Paškūnas showed that the Krull dimension of
R_{v}
is at most
3
(see
[
Paš21
, Corollary 5.13]
). Note that the map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
factors through
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}
. Then we get a finite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
, which also implies that the Krull dimension of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is bounded by
\dim\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}\leq 1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. Combining
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 3.6.1]
, we obtain the following byproduct, which may give a positive answer to the question in
[
CE12
, page 255-256]
in our setting.

Theorem 1.2.2

(Theorem
3.3.9
)
.

Assume that
\overline{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\neq\eta+\eta\omega
for any character
\eta
for any
v|p
when
p=3
. Then the big Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is equidimensional of dimension
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. In particular,
\mathbb{T}
is equidimensional of Krull dimension
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
when
p\geq 5
.

Step 2. Potential pro-modularity

We say that a prime of
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is
pro-modular
if it comes from a prime of some big Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
via the natural map
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. As we mentioned before, the patching arguments in the residually reducible case are established only for some nice primes, which are difficult to find in general. In the author’s previous work
[
Zha24b
]
and
[
Zha24a
]
, we show that there is a practicable way to find a nice prime in each irreducible component of
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
of dimension at least
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
when some assumptions on
F,\Sigma
and
\chi
hold. Combining Pan’s patching argument
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 4.1.4]
(also
[
Zha24a
, Theorem 4.1.4]
) and Grunwald-Wang’s theorem, for the totally real field
F
, we can find a finite abelian extension
F^{1}/F
such that
p
splits completely in
F^{1}
and for any irreducible component of
\Spec R_{F^{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
of dimension at least
1+2[F^{1}:\mathbb{Q}]
, it is pro-modular. Write
\Spec R_{F^{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
for the union of all such irreducible components with reduced scheme structure. Then by
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 3.6.1]
and
[
Zha24a
, Corollary 3.3.10]
, we are able to prove a
potential pro-modularity
argument, i.e.,
\Spec R_{F^{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}=\Spec\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
for the number field
F^{1}
.

Combining the argument in
Step 1
, for the number field
F^{1}
, we can show that the composite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to R_{F^{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\twoheadrightarrow R_{F^{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
factors through
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}
. In other words, there exists a well-defined finite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}\to R_{F^{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. We emphasize that it is unclear that the map
R_{v}\to R_{F^{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is well-defined without our potential pro-modularity argument.

Step 3. A non-ordinary finiteness result

Recall that in the ordinary case, Pan showed that there exists a finite map from the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{F}
to the universal ordinary pseudo-deformation ring
R^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
(see
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 5.1.1]
and
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 6.1.1]
for the precise statements). In this step, we prove a non-ordinary analogue to Pan’s finiteness results. More precisely, we show that there exists a finite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}\to R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, where
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is the maximal reduced quotient of
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
such that each irreducible component of it has dimension at least
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Note that we have got a well-defined finite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}\to R_{F^{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
for the number field
F^{1}
. This implies that there also exists a well-defined finite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}\to R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, if we argue in a similar way as the proof of
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 5.1.1]
. Here the ring
\widehat{\otimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}
plays a similar role as the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{F}
in the ordinary case and it may depend on the choice of
F^{1}
.

As the Krull dimension of
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}
is at most
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
, our non-ordinary finiteness result gives an upper bound of the Krull dimension of the universal pseudo-deformation ring.

Theorem 1.2.3

(Proposition
5.2.6
)
.

Assume that
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\neq\omega
for any
v\in\Sigma_{p}
if
p=3
.

1) The ring
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is equidimensional of dimension
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. In particular,
\dim R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}=1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

2) We have
\dim R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}=2+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Remark 1.2.4
.

We can further show that for each minimal prime of
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, it is of characteristic zero and non-ordinary at each
v|p
. See Lemma
5.3.4
and Lemma
5.3.5
.

In addition, we can show that for each irreducible component of
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, either it is of dimension
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
or it is contained in its reducible locus, which is of dimension at most
2
by Leopoldt’s conjecture. This can be proved by the classical Galois deformation theory and a trick of connectedness dimension (see
[
BS13
, Chapter 19]
for details). In some cases (see
[
Deo22
]
and
[
Deo23
]
),
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is equidimensional, but such a result is unknown in general (at least to the author).

As the Krull dimension of the big Hecke algebra is bounded by the Krull dimension of the universal pseudo-deformation ring, our result also implies Emerton’s conjecture
[
Eme11b
, Conjecture 2.9]
in most cases. (See Subsection
6.1
for the definition of Emerton’s big Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
.)

Theorem 1.2.5

(Theorem
6.1.4
)
.

The big Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
(in the sense of Emerton) is equidimensional of Krull dimension
4
when
p\geq 5
.

Remark 1.2.6
.

1) Unlike Theorem
3.3.9
, the lower bound of
\dim\mathbb{T}
in this result is proved by Gouvêa-Mazur’s infinite fern argument rather than the local-global compatibility results.

2) Actually, our arguments in these three steps are also valid in the residually irreducible case. In particular, the potential pro-modularity argument based on
[
Pan22a
, Section 9]
allows us to relax the Taylor-Wiles hypothesis in the residually irreducible case. See Appendix
A
for the discussions.

Step 4. Going-up

Note that we have obtained a well-defined finite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}\to R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, and both have the same Krull dimension. From the local-global compatibility results in
Step 1
, global non-ordinary regular de Rham primes of
\widehat{\otimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}
are Zariski dense. Here for a global non-ordinary regular de Rham prime of
\widehat{\otimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}
, we mean that its inverse image via the composite map
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}\twoheadrightarrow R_{v}\to\widehat{\otimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}
is a global non-ordinary regular de Rham prime for each
v\in\Sigma_{p}
. By the going-up property, we can prove the Zariski density of non-ordinary regular de Rham liftings in
\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, which implies our main result by using the Brauer-Nesbitt theorem and the non-ordinary Fontaine-Mazur conjecture (see
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 7.1.1]
and
[
Zha24a
, Theorem 5.1.1]
).

Remark 1.2.7
.

In
[
AC14
]
, Calegari and Allen proved a finiteness result between the local framed deformation ring at
p
and the global universal deformation ring in the residually irreducible case. Such a result is used in Allen’s work
[
All19
]
, provided that the local framed deformation ring at
p
is regular. Following Calegari-Allen’s method, we also show a finiteness result from the local pseudo-deformation ring at
p
and the global pseudo-deformation ring in the residually reducible case with a wider range of applications than our finiteness result in
Step 3
. See Proposition
5.1.1
. However, as the local pseudo-deformation ring at
p
has Krull dimension
1+3[F:\mathbb{Q}]>\dim R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, we cannot use it for
Step 4
.

From our strategy, it is not necessary to know more properties of
R_{v}
and
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. Some bounds of their Krull dimensions are sufficient for our purpose in
Step 4
. Hence, we do not need more local or global conditions in our setup.

In addition, we do not use the arguments of infinite fern and the vanishing of Bloch-Kato Selmer group in our four steps.

As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.2.1
, we can prove some big
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem in the residually reducible case. Here we give a sample result as an analogue to
[
Eme11a
, Theorem 1.2.3]
in the Eisenstein case.

Theorem 1.2.8

(Theorem
6.2.1
)
.

Assume that
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\neq\omega
if
p=3
. For a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}
, if
\mathfrak{p}
is contained in an irreducible component of Krull dimension
4
, then it is pro-modular, i.e., it comes from a prime of the big Hecke algebra of the completed cohomology of modular curves.

Remark 1.2.9
.

Here we explain the assumptions in our setting.

The most crucial one is that
p
splits completely in
F
. This is because Paškūnas theory (
p
-adic Langlands correspondence) is only established for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. In our strategy of the proof, Paškūnas’ results in
[
Paš13
]
,
[
PT21
]
and
[
Paš21
]
are used in each step.

The assumption that
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\neq\omega
for any
v|p
if
p=3
is only prepared for the upper bound of
\dim R_{v}
, coming from
[
Paš21
, Corollary 5.13]
. The proof of Paškūnas’ result relies on the explicit description of the ring
E_{\mathfrak{B}}
for the block
\mathfrak{B}
calculated in
[
Paš13
]
(see Subsection
3.2
for precise definitions of
E_{\mathfrak{B}}
and
\mathfrak{B}
). In the exceptional case, the behavior of the block
\mathfrak{B}
is quite different (see
[
Zha24a
, Appendix A.2]
for the list of
\mathfrak{B}
) so that the computation of
E_{\mathfrak{B}}
is much more involved. Once Paškūnas’ result can be proved in that case, the assumption can be removed directly based on the author’s previous work
[
Zha24a
]
.

The other assumptions on
F
and
\bar{\chi}
come from the known case of Fontaine-Mazur conjecture for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, particularly the work
[
SW99
]
and
[
Pan22a
]
. Also, it is sometimes convenient to use Leopoldt’s conjecture for abelian totally real fields.

In addition, our strategy is also valid in the residually irreducible case. See some discussions in Appendix
A
. If one can prove analogous finiteness results to
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 5.1.1 & Theorem 6.1.1]
in the residually irreducible case, following the strategy in this paper, we may obtain similar results without Taylor-Wiles hypothesis. See Remark
A.0.7
.

The author expects to see more arithmetic applications of the main results in this paper, especially the residually reducible analogue to
[
Nak23
]
and
[
FW21
]
in Iwasawa theory.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prepare some results for the universal pseudo-deformation rings. In Section 3, we recall Paškūnas theory in
[
Paš13
]
,
[
PT21
]
and
[
Paš21
]
, and give some corollaries based on the local-global compatibility results at
p
. In Section 4, we recall the known cases of Fontaine-Mazur conjecture for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and potential pro-modularity arguments in
[
Zha24b
]
and
[
Zha24a
]
. In Section 5, we give some non-ordinary finiteness results and further prove our main theorem. In Section 6, we present some applications of our results, such as the equidimensionality of some big Hecke algebras and big
R=\mathbb{T}
theorems. Furthermore, we conjecture the Zariski density of modular points in some irreducible components of the universal deformation ring in the residually reducible case there. In Appendix A, we discuss
Step 2
and
Step 3
in the residually irreducible case, which is prepared for the proof of Emerton’s conjecture.

1.3.
Notations

Let
p
be a prime. We always denote a
p
-adic local field by
K
. We also denote its uniformizer by
\varpi
, its ring of integers by
\mathcal{O}
and the residue field by
\mathbb{F}
. We fix an embedding of

into
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}_{p}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, some algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and an isomorphism
\iota_{p}:\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}_{p}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\simeq\mathbb{C}

We use

\mathfrak{m}_{R}

to denote the maximal reduced quotient of the ring

Suppose

(resp.

2.
Galois deformation theory

In this section, we collect some results about Galois deformation theory.

2.1.
Preliminaries in commutative algebra

In this subsection, let
p
be an odd prime and
K
be a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

[
BS13
, Remark 19.2.5]

For a positive integer
r
, denote by
S(r)
the set of all ordered pairs
(A,B)
of non-empty subsets of
\{1,...,r\}
for which
A\amalg B=\{1,...,r\}
. Let
R
be a Noetherian ring with minimal primes
\mathfrak{p}_{1},...,\mathfrak{p}_{r}
. The
connectedness dimension

c(R)
of
R
is defined to be

c(R)=\min_{(A,B)\in S(r)}\{\dim R/((\cap_{i\in A}\mathfrak{p}_{i})+(\cap_{j\in B}\mathfrak{p}_{j}))\}.

It is easy to see that the connectedness dimension can also be defined as

c(R)=\min_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}\{\dim(Z_{1}\cap Z_{2})\},

where
Z_{1},Z_{2}
are non-empty unions of irreducible components of
\Spec R
such that
Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}=\Spec R
.

Proposition 2.1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m}_{R})
be a Noetherian local ring, and let
\widehat{R}
be its
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
-adic completion. Then we have
c(R)\geq c(\widehat{R})
.

Proof.

See
[
BS13
, Lemma 19.3.1]
.
∎

Write
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
for the category of CNL
\mathcal{O}
-algebras with residue field
\mathbb{F}
. The following result collects some useful facts about the completed tensor product of finitely many objects.

Proposition 2.1.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. For any
1\leq i\leq n
, assume that
R_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
is
\mathcal{O}
-flat. (Here we say that an ideal
I
of
R_{i}
is
\mathcal{O}
-flat if the ring
R_{i}/I
is
\mathcal{O}
-flat.)

1) The completed tensor product
{\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}}R_{i}
is
\mathcal{O}
-flat.

2) If all
R_{i}
are
\mathcal{O}
-flat domains,
1\leq i\leq n
, then
\dim{\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}}R_{i}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\dim R_{i}-1)
.

3) For any ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
of
R_{i}
such that
R_{i}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is
\mathcal{O}
-flat, we have

\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}(R_{i}/\mathfrak{p}_{i})=(\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{i})/(\mathfrak{p}_{1},...,\mathfrak{p}_{n}).

4) For each
i
, assume that
R_{i}
is a domain of dimension
1
. Then
{\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}}R_{i}
is reduced.

5) Let
\{\mathfrak{q}_{i}\}
be a set (possibly infinite) of
\mathcal{O}
-flat primes of
R_{1}
. Then we have
(\cap\mathfrak{q}_{i})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}=\cap(\mathfrak{q}_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2})
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from
[
Tho14
, Lemma 1.3]
. The second one follows from 1) and
[
Tho14
, Lemma 1.4]
. The third one follows from 1) and
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 4.2.4]
.

Here we prove the fourth one. As
R_{i}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-flat CNL domain of dimension
1
, it is finite over
\mathcal{O}
. The natural map
R_{i}\to R_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
is an injection, where
K
is the fraction field of
\mathcal{O}
. Hence,
R_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
is a finite separable extension of
K
.

By the first assertion, to show our claim, we only need to show that
({\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}}R_{i})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K=({\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{i})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K=\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(R_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K)
is reduced. As each
R_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
is finite separable over
K
,
\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}(R_{i}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K)
is a finite étale
K
-algebra, and hence a product of fields of characteristic
0
. This shows the result.

For the last one, we write
A_{i}=R_{1}/\mathfrak{q}_{i}
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
. As for any integer
k>0
, both
A_{i}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}^{k}
and
R_{2}/\mathfrak{m}_{R_{2}}^{k}
are of finite length (hence finitely presented), and by
[
Sta25
, Lemma 10.89.3]
, we have
(\prod A_{i}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}^{k})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}/\mathfrak{m}_{R_{2}}^{k}\cong\prod(A_{i}/\mathfrak{m}_{i}^{k}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}/\mathfrak{m}_{R_{2}}^{k})
. Taking the inverse limit, we conclude that
(\prod A_{i})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}=\prod(A_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2})
(using
[
Sta25
, Lemma 4.14.10]
). Consider the exact sequence

0\to\cap\mathfrak{q}_{i}\to R_{1}\to\prod A_{i},

and we tensor it with
R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}
over
R_{1}
.
Combining the third assertion and
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 4.2.4 (1) & (2)]
, we get the exact sequence

0\to\cap(\mathfrak{q}_{i})\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}\to R_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}\to\prod(A_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{2}).

This shows our result.
∎

2.2.
Pseudo-representations

In this subsection, we recall the definition of two-dimensional pseudo-representations. For a general theory, one can see
[
BC09
]
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

For a profinite group
G
and a topological commutative ring
R
in which
2
is invertible, a two-dimensional
pseudo-representation
is a continuous function
T:G\to R
such that

1)
T(1)=2
,

2)
T(\sigma\tau)=T(\tau\sigma)
for all
\sigma,\tau\in G
,

3)
T(\gamma\delta\eta)+T(\gamma\eta\delta)-T(\gamma\eta)T(\delta)-T(\eta\delta)T(\gamma)-T(\delta\gamma)T(\eta)+T(\gamma)T(\delta)T(\eta)=0,

for any
\delta,\gamma,\eta\in G
.

The
determinant

\operatorname{det}(T)
of a pseudo-representation
T
is a character of
G
defined by

\operatorname{det}(T):G\to R^{\times},~\operatorname{det}(T)(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}(T(\sigma)^{2}-T(\sigma^{2})),\sigma\in G.

Sometimes, we may take
G
as some Galois group of a totally real number field. In this case, pseudo-representations will have more properties as a complex conjugation exists in
G
. We make the following assumption:

Assumption 2.2.2
.

There exists an order
2
element
c\in G
such that
T(c)=0
.

Definition 2.2.3
.

Suppose Assumption
2.2.2
holds for
G
. For a pseudo-representation
T
and
\sigma,\tau\in G
, we define:

1)
a(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}(T(c\sigma)+T(\sigma))
,

2)
d(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}(-T(c\sigma)+T(\sigma))
,

3)
y(\sigma,\tau)=a(\sigma\tau)-a(\sigma)a(\tau).

We can check that this definition is equivalent to the one in
[
SW99
, Section 2.4]
.

Definition 2.2.4
.

[
Pan22a
, 2.1.5]

Suppose Assumption
2.2.2
holds for
G
. Assume that
R
is either a field or a DVR. For a pseudo-representation
T:G\to R
, a representation
\rho
associated to
T
is in the following form.

1)
\rho(\sigma)=\begin{pmatrix}a(\sigma)&~\\
~&d(\sigma)\end{pmatrix}
, if all
y(\sigma,\tau)=0
. We call this case
reducible
.

2) If
y(\sigma,\tau)\neq 0
for some
\sigma,\tau\in G
, choose
\sigma_{0},\tau_{0}
such that
\frac{y(\sigma,\tau)}{y(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})}\in R
for any
\sigma,\tau\in G
. Define
\rho(\sigma)=\begin{pmatrix}a(\sigma)&\frac{y(\sigma,\tau_{0})}{y(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})}\\
y(\sigma_{0},\sigma)&d(\sigma)\end{pmatrix}
. We call this case
irreducible
.

Assume the profinite group
G
satisfies Mazur’s finiteness condition
\Phi_{p}
(see
[
Pan22a
, 2.1.2 Assumption 1]
). Let
\mathcal{O}
be a complete DVR of characteristic
0
with residue field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p>2
. Let
T_{\mathbb{F}}:G\to\mathbb{F}
be a two-dimensional pseudo-representation. In our case, the functor sending each object
R
of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
to the set of pseudo-representations
T:G\to R
which lift
T_{\mathbb{F}}
is pro-represented by a CNL
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{T_{\mathbb{F}}}
. This is the universal pseudo-deformation ring of the residual pseudo-representation
T_{\mathbb{F}}
.

2.3.
Local deformation rings

In this subsection, we study certain local universal pseudo-deformation rings.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
K
be a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\chi:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a continuous character with its residual character
\bar{\chi}
.

Let
T:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\mathbb{F}
be a two-dimensional pseudo-representation with determinant
\bar{\chi}
. As the profinite group
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
satisfies Mazur’s finiteness condition
\Phi_{p}
(for example, see
[
NSW08
, Theorem 7.5.12]
), there exists a universal pseudo-deformation ring
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
parametrizing all the pseudo-deformations of
T
with determinant
\chi
. The ring
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is a CNL
\mathcal{O}
-algebra.

Proposition 2.3.1
.

The ring
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-flat integral domain of Krull dimension
4
.

Proof.

If
T=\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}
is the sum of two trivial characters, then by
[
Paš13
, Corollary 9.13]
,
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is a power series ring over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
3
. Thus, our result holds in this case.

Now we assume
T
is multiplicity-free (in the sense of
[
BC09
, Definition 1.4.1]
). By
[
BIP23
, Corollary 5.11]
,
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-flat integral domain. We only need to show that
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is of Krull dimension
4
.

If
T
is reducible, then
\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathbb{F}[G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}]}^{1}(\bar{\chi},\mathbf{1})
is non-trivial (for example, see
[
NSW08
, Theorem 7.3.2]
). We can choose a non-split extension
0\to\mathbf{1}\to\bar{\rho}\to\bar{\chi}\to 0
. If
T
is absolutely irreducible, it defines an absolutely irreducible Galois representation
\bar{\rho}
of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
. In each case, there exists a universal deformation ring
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\chi}
parametrizing all deformations of
\bar{\rho}
with determinant
\chi
. Combining
[
BIP23
, Theorem 1.5]
and
[
KW09b
, Proposition 2.1]
, we know that
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\chi}
is
\mathcal{O}
-flat of Krull dimension
4
.

Choose an
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-irreducible lifting
\rho
of
\bar{\rho}
, where
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is some finite normal extension of
\mathcal{O}
with fraction field
K^{\prime}
. Then we have natural maps
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\chi}\to\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, and suppose
\rho
defines the prime
\mathfrak{p}
(resp.
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
) of
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
(resp.
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\chi}
). Consider the universal deformation ring
R_{\rho}
of the representation
\rho\otimes K^{\prime}
with determinant
\chi
, and by
[
Pan22a
, Corollary 2.2.2]
,
R_{\rho}
is isomorphic to
\widehat{(R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}})_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. By
[
Kis03
, Proposition 9.5]
,
R_{\rho}
is isomorphic to
\widehat{(R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\chi}[\frac{1}{p}])_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}}\otimes K^{\prime}
, which is of Krull dimension
3
. As
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-flat integral domain (hence irreducible), we can conclude that
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is of Krull dimension
4
.
∎

Remark 2.3.2
.

In this article, we are concerned with the case
T
is reducible. If we are not in a special case
\bar{\chi}=\omega
and
p=3
, the ring
R_{p,\chi}^{\mathrm{ps}}
has an explicit description. See
[
Paš13
, Corollary 9.13, B.5, Proposition B.17 and Remark B.28]
for more details.

2.4.
Global deformation rings

In this subsection, we collect some results about global pseudo-deformation rings.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
K
be a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
.

Let
F
be an abelian totally real field. For simplicity, we suppose that
p
splits completely in
F
(but it may not be necessary in this subsection). Let
\Sigma_{p}
be the set of places of
F
lying above
p
and let
\Sigma
be a finite set of finite places of
F
containing
\Sigma_{p}
. Let
\chi:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a continuous totally odd character with its residual character
\bar{\chi}
.

Let
T
be the pseudo-representation
1+\bar{\chi}
. As the profinite group
G_{F,\Sigma}
satisfies Assumption
2.2.2
and Mazur’s finiteness assumption (see
[
NSW08
, Corollary 10.11.15]
), there exists a universal pseudo-deformation
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}
parametrizing all the pseudo-deformations of
T
. Let
R_{\bar{\chi}}
be the universal deformation ring parametrizing all the liftings of
\bar{\chi}
. Write
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}=R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}\widehat{\otimes}_{R_{\bar{\chi}},\chi}\mathcal{O}
, where the map
R_{\bar{\chi}}\to\mathcal{O}
is given by
\chi
. Then
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is the universal pseudo-deformation ring parametrizing all the pseudo-deformations of
T
with determinant
\chi
.

Proposition 2.4.1
.

1) Let
\Gamma
be the maximal pro-
p
abelian quotient of
G_{F,\Sigma}
. Then there exists a CNL
\mathcal{O}
-algebra isomorphism
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma]]
.

2) Let
\chi^{\prime}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a character lifting
\bar{\chi}
. Let
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring parametrizing all the pseudo-deformations of
T
with determinant
\chi^{\prime}
.Then there exists a CNL
\mathcal{O}
-algebra isomorphism
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

This is an analogue to
[
All19
, Lemma 6.1.2]
. We sketch the proof here.

Let
T^{\chi}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
be the universal pseudo-deformation of
T
with determinant
\chi
and let
D:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma]]^{\times}
be the universal deformation of the trivial character
\mathbf{1}
. As
p
is odd, there exists a unique character
\tilde{\chi}
of
G_{F,\Sigma}
lifting
\mathbf{1}
satisfying
(\tilde{\chi})^{2}=\chi\tilde{\bar{\chi}}^{-1}
, where
\tilde{\bar{\chi}}
is the Teichmüller lifting of
\bar{\chi}
. Then
T^{\chi}\otimes(\tilde{\chi}D):G_{F,\Sigma}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma]]
defines a pseudo-deformation of
T
.

For a pseudo-deformation
T_{A}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to A
lifting
T
,
A\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
, there exists a unique character
d_{A}
of
G_{F,\Sigma}
(lifting
\mathbf{1}
) satisfying
(d_{A})^{2}=\det(T_{A})\chi^{-1}
. Then
T_{A}\otimes(d_{A})^{-1}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to A
defines a pseudo-deformation of
T
with determinant
\chi
. Thus, we get natural maps
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to A
and
\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma]]\to A
(induced by
\tilde{\chi}^{-1}d_{A}
) by the universal property. By the uniqueness of the universal object, we have
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}\cong R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}[[\Gamma]]
. This shows the first claim.

For the second one, we may consider the pseudo-representation
T^{\chi}\otimes\mu:G_{F,\Sigma}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, where
\mu:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
is the unique character lifting
\mathbf{1}
satisfying
\mu^{2}=\chi^{\prime}\chi^{-1}
. This defines a pseudo-deformation of
T
with determinant
\chi^{\prime}
, and we get the map
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
by the universal property. Similarly, we can also obtain the map
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}
. The composite maps
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}
and
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
are both identities. This shows the second claim.

∎

The following result is crucial to our main results.

Proposition 2.4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a one-dimensional prime of
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
such that the corresponding Galois representation is absolutely irreducible in the sense of Definition
2.2.4
. Here we do not require
\varpi\notin\mathfrak{p}
.

1) We have
c((R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi})_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq 2[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1
.

2) For any irreducible component
C
of
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
containing
\mathfrak{p}
, we have
\dim C\geq 2[F:\mathbb{Q}]+1
.

Proof.

For the first assertion, it is proved in
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 7.4.6]
. Here we only point out that the inequality
c((R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi})_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq c(\widehat{(R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi})_{\mathfrak{p}}})
follows from Proposition
2.1.2
.

For the second assertion, it is a direct consequence of the first one. See
[
Zha24b
, Remark 3.2.8]
.
∎

Corollary 2.4.3
.

For any irreducible component
C
of
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
,
C
is either of dimension at least
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
or at most
2
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4.2
, if
C
is of dimension less than
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
, then
C
only contains reducible pseudo-deformations. Let
R_{F}^{\operatorname{ps,red}}
be the reducible locus of
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. Then there exists a natural surjection
\mathcal{O}[[G_{F,\Sigma}^{\operatorname{ab}}(p)]]\twoheadrightarrow R_{F}^{\operatorname{ps,red}}
. As
F
is abelian, by Leopoldt’s conjecture, we know that
R_{F}^{\operatorname{ps,red}}
is of dimension at most
2
, and so is
C
. This proves the result.
∎

Assume that there exists a one-dimensional irreducible pseudo-deformation of
T
with determinant
\chi
. Let
I_{F}
be the intersection of all minimal primes of
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
of dimension at least
2[F:\mathbb{Q}]+1
, and write
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}:=R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/I_{F}
. Then from our definition, we can conclude the following properties of
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
directly.

Proposition 2.4.4
.

1)
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is reduced. Each irreducible component of
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is of Krull dimension at least
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

2) For any irreducible prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, the natural surjection
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\twoheadrightarrow R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/\mathfrak{p}
factors through
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
.

Now let
F_{1}/F
be a totally real extension and suppose
F_{1}
is also abelian. Let
\Sigma_{1}
be the set of primes of
F_{1}
lying above
\Sigma
. Let
R_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is the universal pseudo-deformation ring parametrizing all the pseudo-deformations of
T|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}
with determinant
\chi|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}
. By the universal property, there is a natural map
R_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
.

Proposition 2.4.5
.

The natural map
R_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is finite.

Proof.

Write
\mathfrak{m}_{1}
for the maximal ideal of
R_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. We only need to show that
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/(\mathfrak{m}_{1})
is an Artinian ring. If not, we can find a one-dimensional prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/(\mathfrak{m}_{1})
. Consider the Galois representation
\rho(\mathfrak{q}):G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k(\mathfrak{q}))
in the sense of Definition
2.2.4
(here we fix
\sigma_{0},\tau_{0}\in G_{F,\Sigma}
).

We claim that
\rho(\mathfrak{q})|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}
is either upper triangular or lower triangular. From our construction, we have
y(\alpha,\beta)=0
,
\alpha,\beta\in G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}
. If it is not lower triangular, i.e.,
y(\alpha,\tau_{0})\neq 0
for some
\alpha\in G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}
, then
0=y(\alpha,\beta)y(\sigma_{0},\tau_{0})=y(\alpha,\tau_{0})y(\sigma_{0},\beta)
(by
[
Pan22a
, 2.1.4]
), which implies
y(\sigma_{0},\beta)=0
. Then by Definition
2.2.4
,
\rho(\mathfrak{q})|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}
is
upper triangular.

We assume
\rho(\mathfrak{q})|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}
is upper triangular (the other case will be similar). Note that
\bar{\chi}
is totally odd. For any complex conjugation
c\in G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}
, by our definition, we have
\rho(\mathfrak{q})(c)=\begin{pmatrix}1&*\\
~&-1\end{pmatrix}
(as
a|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}=\mathbf{1},d|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}=\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}
for functions
a,d
in Definition
2.2.3
). For any
x\in G_{F,\Sigma}
, we have
\rho(\mathfrak{q})(xcx^{-1})=\rho(\mathfrak{q})(c^{\prime})
, where
c,c^{\prime}
are complex conjugations of
G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}
. Then by an easy calculation, we conclude that
\rho(\mathfrak{q})(x)
is upper-triangular. This shows that
\rho(\mathfrak{q})
is reducible. Consequently, we know that
\rho(\mathfrak{q})
is a direct sum of two characters. Here we write
\rho(\mathfrak{q})=\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{2}
, where
\psi_{1}
is a lifting of
\mathbf{1}
. By the definition of
\mathfrak{q}
, we know that
\psi_{1}|_{G_{F_{1},\Sigma_{1}}}=\mathbf{1}
. As
F_{1}/F
is finite, we have
\psi_{1}=\mathbf{1}
and
\psi_{2}=\bar{\chi}
as
\psi_{1}\psi_{2}=\bar{\chi}
. It is contrary to the assumption that
\mathfrak{q}
is of dimension one. This proves our result.
∎

Corollary 2.4.6
.

Assume that
\bar{\chi}
can be extended to a character of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then we have
\dim R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\geq 1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. In other words,
\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is not empty.

Proof.

Note that
\bar{\chi}
can be extended to a character of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. By the arguments in
[
FKP22
, Lemma 7.3]
, the pseudo-deformation space
\Spec R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}
(of the pseudo-representation
1+\bar{\chi}
with determinant
\chi^{\prime}
) has a one-dimensional irreducible point associated to some cusp eigenform
f
of tame level
N_{0}
(the Artin conductor of the representation
1\oplus\bar{\chi}
), where
\chi^{\prime}
is the corresponding determinant lifting
\bar{\chi}
. Then by Proposition
2.4.2
, we have
\dim R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}\geq 3
. Consider the natural map
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}\to R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}
. By Proposition
2.4.5
, we have
\dim R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}\geq\dim R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}>2
, and hence
\dim R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi^{\prime}}\geq 1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
by Corollary
2.4.3
. Using 2) of Proposition
2.4.1
, we get
\dim R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\geq 1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. In particular, we have
\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is not empty.
∎

Similarly to
R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, we can also define
R_{F_{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}:=R_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/I_{F_{1}}
as the union of all irreducible components of
R_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
dimension at least
1+2[F_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Corollary 2.4.7
.

The composite map
R_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\twoheadrightarrow R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
factors through
R_{F_{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. In other words, there exists a well-defined and finite map
R_{F_{1},0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
induced by the universal property.

Proof.

For the first assertion, we need to show that the kernel
I:=\operatorname{ker}\{R_{F_{1}}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\}
contains
I_{F_{1}}
. Combining Proposition
2.4.4
and Proposition
2.4.5
, we know that each minimal prime of
I
is of dimension at least
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]>2
. By Corollary
2.4.3
, we have
I_{F_{1}}\subset I
. The second one follows from Proposition
2.4.5
.
∎

In this section, we recall some local-global compatibility results and deduce some corollaries. Our references are
[
Paš13
]
,
[
Paš21
]
,
[
PT21
]
and
[
Pan22a
]
.

Throughout this section, we fix
p
an odd prime, and
F
a totally real field in which
p
splits completely. We denote by
K
a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
.

3.1.
Completed cohomology and Hecke algebra

In this subsection, we recall some definitions about the completed cohomology for quaternionic forms and the
p
-adic big Hecke algebra. Our reference is
[
Pan22a
, Section 3]
.

Let
D
be a quaternion algebra with centre
F
such that
D
is ramified at all infinite places of
F
and unramified at all places lying above
p
.

Let
A
be a topological
\mathcal{O}
-algebra and
U=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}U_{v}
be an open compact subgroup of
(D\otimes_{F}\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})^{\times}
such that
U_{v}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})
for any
v\mid p
. We write
U^{p}=\prod_{v\nmid p}U_{v}
(the tame level) and
U_{p}=\prod_{v\mid p}U_{v}
. Let
\psi:(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})^{\times}/F_{+}^{\times}\to A^{\times}
be a continuous character, where
F_{+}
is the set of totally positive elements in
F
.

Let
S_{\psi}(U,A)
be the space of continuous functions:

f:D^{\times}\setminus(D\otimes_{F}\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})^{\times}\to A

such that for any
g\in(D\otimes_{F}\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})^{\times},u\in U,z\in(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})^{\times}
, we have

•

f(gu)=f(g)
,

•

f(gz)=\psi(z)f(g)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of places of
F
containing all those lying above
p
and places
v
where either
D
is ramified or
U_{v}
is not a maximal open subgroup. For any
v\notin\Sigma
, we define the Hecke operator
T_{v}\in\operatorname{End}(S_{\psi}(U,A))
to be the double coset action
[U_{v}\begin{pmatrix}\varpi_{v}&~\\
~&1\end{pmatrix}U_{v}]
. Precisely, if we write
U_{v}\begin{pmatrix}\varpi_{v}&~\\
~&1\end{pmatrix}U_{v}=\coprod_{i}\gamma_{i}U_{v}
, then

(T_{v}\cdot f)(g)=\sum_{i}f(g\gamma_{i}),~~f\in S_{\psi}(U,A).

We define the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}^{\Sigma}(U,A)\subseteq\operatorname{End}(S_{\psi}(U,A))
as the
A
-subalgebra generated by all Hecke operators
T_{v},v\notin\Sigma
. In the case that
A=\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}
,
U
is sufficiently small (in the sense of
[
Pan22a
, page 1046]
) and
\psi\mid_{U\cap(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})^{\times}}
is trivial modulo
\varpi^{n}
, we can check that the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}^{\Sigma}(U,\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n})
is independent of
\Sigma
, and we drop
\Sigma
afterwards.

For a tame level
U^{p}
, we write
S_{\psi}(U^{p},A):=\varinjlim_{U_{p}}S_{\psi}(U^{p}U_{p},A)
with discrete topology, where
U_{p}=\prod_{v|p}U_{v}
ranges over all open compact subgroups
U_{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v})
.

Definition 3.1.1
.

The
completed cohomology
of level
U^{p}
is defined to be

S_{\psi}(U^{p}):=\Hom_{\mathcal{O}}(K/\mathcal{O},S_{\psi}(U^{p},K/\mathcal{O}))

equipped with
\varpi
-adic topology, and the
completed homology
is defined to be

M_{\psi}(U^{p}):=S_{\psi}(U^{p},K/\mathcal{O})^{\vee}=\Hom_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(S_{\psi}(U^{p},K/\mathcal{O}),K/\mathcal{O})

equipped with compact-open topology.

The following proposition is clear from the definition.

Proposition 3.1.2
.

1)
S_{\psi}(U^{p})
is
\varpi
-torsion free.

2)
S_{\psi}(U^{p})\cong\Hom^{\mathrm{cont}}_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{\psi}(U^{p}),\mathcal{O})
.

Definition 3.1.3
.

Let
U^{p}
be a tame level and
\psi:(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})^{\times}/(U^{p}\cap(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty})^{\times})F_{+}^{\times}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a continuous character. Define the
big Hecke algebra

\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})=\varprojlim_{(n,U_{p})\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p}U_{p},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}),

where
\mathcal{I}
is the set of pairs
(n,U_{p})
with
U_{p}\subseteq K_{p}:=\prod_{v\mid p}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})
and
n
a positive integer such that
\psi\mid_{U_{p}\cap\mathcal{O}_{F,p}^{\times}}\equiv 1~\operatorname{mod}~\varpi^{n}
.

The following result is classical.

Proposition 3.1.4
.

1)
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})
is a semi-local ring. Write
\mathfrak{m}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{m}_{r}
for all the maximal ideals of
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})
. Then we have
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})\cong\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}_{r}}
and each
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
is
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
-adically complete and separated.

2) For each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{r}
, there exists a two-dimensional pseudo-representation
T_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
sending
\Frob_{v}
to
T_{v}
for any
v\notin\Sigma
with determinant
\psi\varepsilon^{-1}
.

3.2.
Paškūnas theory

In this subsection, we summarize some basic definitions and results of Paskunas theory for the
p
-adic analytic group
D_{p}^{\times}:=\prod_{v|p}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v})
. For more details and precise definitions, one can see
[
Paš13
]
,
[
PT21
]
or a summary in
[
Zha24a
, Appendix A]
for the case
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. We also keep the notations and terminologies there.

Let
G=\prod_{v|p}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v})\cong\prod_{v|p}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
Z(G)\simeq\prod_{v|p}F_{v}^{\times}
be its centre. Let
\zeta:Z(G)\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a continuous character.

Let
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{\mathcal{O}})
be the category of smooth
representations of
G
on
\mathcal{O}
-torsion modules with central character
\zeta
, i.e. the centre
Z
acts via
\zeta
. Let
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,fin}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
be the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}_{G,\zeta}^{\mathrm{sm}}(\mathcal{O})
consisting of representations locally of finite length and
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
the full subcategory consisting of locally admissible representations. By
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 3.4.1]
,
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,fin}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
are actually the same.

Let
H\subset G
be an open compact subgroup and
\mathcal{O}[[H]]
be the completed group algebra of
H
over
\mathcal{O}
. Let
\mathrm{Mod}_{G,\zeta}^{\mathrm{pro\,aug}}(\mathcal{O})
be the category of profinite linearly topological
\mathcal{O}[[H]]
-modules with action of
\mathcal{O}[G]
and central character
\zeta
such that both actions agree on
\mathcal{O}[H]
, with morphisms given by
G
-equivariant continuous homomorphisms of topological

\mathcal{O}[[H]]
-modules. This category is independent of the subgroup
H
.

For an object
M
of
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{\mathcal{O}})
, the Pontryagin dual
M\mapsto M^{\vee}
induces an anti-equivalence between categories
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{\mathcal{O}})
and
\mathrm{Mod}_{G,\zeta}^{\mathrm{pro\,aug}}(\mathcal{O})
. Write
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
for the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}_{G,\zeta}^{\mathrm{pro\,aug}}(\mathcal{O})
with objects isomorphic to
\pi^{\vee}
for some object
\pi
in
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
.

Let
\mathrm{Irr}_{G,\zeta}
be the set of irreducible representations in
\mathrm{Mod}_{G,\zeta}^{\mathrm{sm}}(\mathcal{O})
. Recall that a
block
is an equivalence class of the relation
\sim
, where
\pi\sim\pi^{\prime}
if there exists
\pi_{1},\cdots,\pi_{n}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{G,\zeta}
such that
\pi\cong\pi_{1}
,
\pi^{\prime}\cong\pi_{n}
, and for
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
\pi_{i}\cong\pi_{i+1}
or
\Ext^{1}_{G}(\pi_{i},\pi_{i+1})\neq 0
or
\Ext^{1}_{G}(\pi_{i+1},\pi_{i})\neq 0
.
Under this equivalence relation, we have a decomposition of the category
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})\cong\prod_{\mathfrak{B}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{G,\zeta}/\sim}\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}},

where
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}}
is the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
consisting of representations with all irreducible subquotients in the block
\mathfrak{B}
. Write
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}}
for the full subcategory of
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
anti-equivalent to
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}}
for the block
\mathfrak{B}
via the Pontryagin dual. Then we also have a natural decomposition

\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})\cong\prod_{\mathfrak{B}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{G,\zeta}/\sim}\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}}.

In our case, the blocks containing an absolutely irreducible representation have been well-understood. See the list in
[
Zha24a
, Appendix A.2]
(containing the case
p=2
) for the case
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and combine
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 3.4.7]
. For each
v|p
and a block
\mathfrak{B}
, we can attach a semi-simple two-dimensional representation
(\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}})_{v}
over
\mathbb{F}
with determinant
\varepsilon\zeta|_{F_{v}}\modd\varpi
. Here we view
\zeta|_{F_{v}}
as a character of
G_{F_{v}}
by local class field theory.

Now fix a block
\mathfrak{B}
, and write
\pi_{\mathfrak{B}}=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\mathfrak{B}_{i}}\pi
, where
\mathfrak{B}_{i}
is the set of isomorphism classes of elements of
\mathfrak{B}
. This is a finite set by the discussion in the previous paragraph. Let
\pi_{\mathfrak{B}}\hookrightarrow J_{\mathfrak{B}}
be an injective envelope of
\pi_{\mathfrak{B}}
in
\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,adm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
. Then its Pontryagin dual
P_{\mathfrak{B}}:=J_{\mathfrak{B}}^{\vee}
is a projective envelope of
\pi^{\vee}\cong\bigoplus_{\pi\in\mathfrak{B}_{i}}\pi^{\vee}
in
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
, called a
projective generator
of
\mathfrak{B}
. Write

E_{\mathfrak{B}}:=\End_{\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})}(P_{\mathfrak{B}})\cong\End_{G}(J_{\mathfrak{B}}).

This is a pseudo-compact ring (not necessarily commutative). Let
Z_{\mathfrak{B}}
be the centre of
E_{\mathfrak{B}}
.

Theorem 3.2.1
.

Write
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
for the universal pseudo-deformation ring of
(\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}})_{v}
with determinant
\varepsilon\zeta|_{F_{v}}
(via class field theory).

1) There exists a natural finite map
\widehat{\bigotimes}_{v|p}R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{v}\to E_{\mathfrak{B}}
. In particular,
E_{\mathfrak{B}}
is Noetherian.

2)
E_{\mathfrak{B}}
is a finitely generated module over
Z_{\mathfrak{B}}
.

Proof.

See
[
Pan22a
, Corollary 3.4.8]
and
[
Zha24a
, Proposition 3.3.2]
.
∎

The functor sending an object
N
of the category
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
to
\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})}(P_{\mathfrak{B}},N)
induces an anti-equivalence between
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})^{\mathfrak{B}}
and the category of right pseudo-compact
E_{\mathfrak{B}}
-modules. The inverse functor is given by
\mathrm{m}\mapsto\mathrm{m}\widehat{\otimes}_{E_{\mathfrak{B}}}P_{\mathfrak{B}}
.

Let
\Pi
be a unitary
K
-Banach space representation of
G
and
\Theta
an open bounded
G
-invariant lattice in
\Pi
. By
[
Paš13
, Lemma 4.4]
, its Schikhof dual

\Theta^{d}:=\Hom_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\Theta,\mathcal{O})
is an object of
\mathrm{Mod}_{G,\zeta}^{\mathrm{pro\,aug}}(\mathcal{O})
.

Remark 3.2.2
.

Fix a tame level
U^{p}
and a character
\psi
as in the previous subsection. Take
\zeta=\psi
. We can conclude the following facts:

1)
S_{\psi}(U^{p},K/\mathcal{O})
is a smooth admissible
\mathcal{O}
-representation of
D_{p}^{\times}
.

2) The complete homology
M_{\psi}(U^{p})
is an object in
\mathfrak{C}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
. Here we view
\psi
as a character of
Z(G)
.

3) Write
S_{\psi}(U^{p})_{K}:=S_{\psi}(U^{p})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
. Then
S_{\psi}(U^{p})_{K}
is a
K
-Banach space representation with open bounded
G
-invariant
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
S_{\psi}(U^{p})
.

3.3.
Local-global compatibility

In this subsection, we collect some local-global compatibility results.

We fix a tame level
U^{p}
and character
\psi
. Recall that
F
is a totally real field in which
p
splits completely. Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of places of
F
containing all those lying above
p
and places
v
where either
D
is ramified or
U_{v}
is not a maximal open subgroup. Under this setting, we can define a big Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})
. Then by Proposition
3.1.4
, we get a two-dimensional pseudo-representation
T_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
with determinant
\psi\varepsilon^{-1}
and its residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
in the sense of

2.2.4
.

For each
v|p
, we write
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m},v}:=\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m}}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\otimes\varepsilon
. Assume that
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m},v}
is semisimple for each
v|p
. We can define a block
\mathfrak{B}_{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v})
corresponding to
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m},v}
(see the list in
[
Zha24a
, Appendix A.2]
) for each
v|p
. Then by
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 3.4.7]
,
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\otimes_{v|p}\mathfrak{B}_{v}
defines a block of
D_{p}^{\times}=\prod_{v|p}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v})
.

Let
P_{\mathfrak{B}_{v}}
be a projective generator of
\mathfrak{B}_{v}
for each
v|p
. Write
E_{\mathfrak{B}_{v}}:=\End_{\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v}),\psi}(\mathcal{O})}(P_{\mathfrak{B}_{v}})
. Then by
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 3.4.5]
,
P_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}}:=\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}P_{\mathfrak{B}_{v}}
defines a projective generator of the block
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Also, by
[
Zha24a
, Proposition 3.3.2]
, we have
E_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}}:=\End_{\mathfrak{C}_{D_{p}^{\times},\psi}(\mathcal{O})}(P_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}})\cong\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}E_{\mathfrak{B}_{v}}
.

Write

\mathrm{m}_{v}:=\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v}),\psi}(\mathcal{O})}(P_{\mathfrak{B}_{v}},M_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}),~~~v|p,

\mathrm{m}:=\Hom_{\mathfrak{C}_{D_{p}^{\times},\psi}(\mathcal{O})}(P_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}},M_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}).

Write
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
for the universal pseudo-deformation ring of the pseudo-representation
\mathrm{tr}\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{m},v}
with determinant
\psi\varepsilon^{-1}
, and write
R_{F,p}^{\mathrm{ps}}:=\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
. Then by the universal property, there is a natural map
R_{F,p}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
induced by
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

We can define two actions of
R_{F,p}^{\mathrm{ps}}
on
\mathrm{m}
.

(1)

the Galois side

\tau_{\Gal}
: which comes from the action of
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
on
M_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
and the natural map
R_{F,p}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

(2)

the automorphic side

\tau_{\mathrm{Aut}}
: which comes from the action of
R_{F,p}^{\mathrm{ps}}
(after twisting
\varepsilon^{-1}
) on
P_{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}}
via

Remark 5.2.7
.

So far, we have proved two independent but similar finiteness results between the completed tensor product of all local pseudo-deformation rings at
v|p
and the global deformation ring, using different methods.
Both of them are analogous to Pan’s ordinary finiteness results (Theorem
4.1.1
and Theorem
4.1.2
) in the non-ordinary case, substituting the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{F}
with some suitable local pseudo-deformation ring. Although Proposition
5.1.1
applies for more totally real fields, Proposition
5.2.5
is much stronger so that we can control the Krull dimension of the global pseudo-deformation ring (as in Proposition
5.2.6
) and further use the going-up property to find modular points in the global deformation space (see the following subsection). Note that by Proposition
2.3.1
,
\widehat{\otimes}_{v\in\Sigma_{p}}R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
has Krull dimension
1+3[F:\mathbb{Q}]
, which is much larger than the Krull dimension of
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
(expected to be
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
). Thus, Proposition
5.1.1
is insufficient for our purposes.

More precisely, we actually find a non-trivial local deformation problem defined by
I_{v}
such that the natural map
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
factors through
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}/I_{v}
by using local-global compatibility results at
p
and potential pro-modularity. In general, it is unclear whether the map
R_{v}\to R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is well-defined. The ring
R_{v}
seems to be quite mysterious, and we do not know whether it is equidimensional. Although the definition of
R_{v}
comes from the big Hecke algebra for the totally real field
F^{1}
in our context, we do not know whether it is actually independent of the choice of such abelian base change.

In addition, if Paškūnas’ result (
[
Paš21
, Corollary 5.13]
) holds in the exceptional case, then by our proof, Proposition
5.2.6
also holds in that case without more arguments.

Remark 5.3.2
.

The proof of this lemma relies on the explicit calculation of the local pseudo-deformation ring

, but such a result is not known in the exceptional case. However, we can also prove this result in that case in the following way.

Let
(R_{1},\psi_{1}^{\operatorname{univ}})
and
(R_{\bar{\chi}},\psi_{\bar{\chi}}^{\operatorname{univ}})
be the universal deformation of the
1
-dimensional character
\mathbf{1}
and

respectively. As

, it is well known that both

and

are power series rings over

of relative dimension

. On the one hand, by the universal property, we have natural maps

is isomorphic to

of relative dimension

.

of

X_{v^{\prime}}
corresponds to the generator of
\mathcal{O}_{F_{v^{\prime}}}^{\times}(p)
. In other words, we get a finite map
\mathbb{F}[[X_{v}]]\to R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
. We get a contradiction as
\dim\mathbb{F}[[X_{v}]]=1
and
\dim R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}=\dim R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}/(\varpi)=2
.

Now consider the case
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}=\mathbf{1}
for any
v|p
. By Definition
2.2.4
, the pseudo-deformation
T(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})\otimes k(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})
defines a Galois representation
\rho(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}):G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}))
. Combining
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 5.3.2]
, we get a surjection
R_{1}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}\twoheadrightarrow R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
, where
R_{1}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
is the universal ordinary pseudo-deformation ring defined in Subsection
4.1
. Then we can use the same arguments as in the previous paragraph and get a contradiction as well, replacing Theorem
4.1.1
by Theorem
4.1.2
.
∎

We say that a one-dimensional prime
\mathfrak{r}
of
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}/\mathfrak{p}_{v}
is
non-ordinary regular de Rham
, if for any
v|p
, the inverse image of
\mathfrak{r}
via the composite map
R_{v}\to\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}/\mathfrak{p}_{v}\twoheadrightarrow(\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}/\mathfrak{p}_{v})/\mathfrak{r}
is non-ordinary and regular de Rham.

Let
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
be the inverse image of the prime
\mathfrak{p}
via the finite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}\to R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. Then
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
is a minimal prime of
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}
, and also a minimal prime of the ideal
(\mathfrak{p}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{p}_{v_{d}})
, where
\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}=\Sigma_{p}
and
d=[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Lemma 5.3.6
.

The prime
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
is the intersection of all non-ordinary regular de Rham primes containing
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Write
\sqrt{(\mathfrak{p}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{p}_{v_{d}})}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{\prime}\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{\prime}
, and we prove that for each
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{\prime},1\leq i\leq s
, it is the intersection of all non-ordinary regular de Rham primes containing itself.

By Lemma
5.3.3
and Lemma
5.3.5
, we know that for each
\mathfrak{p}_{v_{i}}
, it is the intersection of all non-ordinary regular de Rham primes containing
\mathfrak{p}_{v_{i}}
. Then by 3) and 5) of Proposition
2.1.3
, we have

(\mathfrak{p}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{p}_{v_{d}})=\cap(\mathfrak{q}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{q}_{v_{d}}),

where
\mathfrak{q}_{v_{i}}
ranges over all non-ordinary regular de Rham primes containing
\mathfrak{p}_{v_{i}}
.

As
R_{v}/\mathfrak{q}_{v}
is
\mathcal{O}
-flat and one-dimensional, by 4) of Proposition
2.1.3
, we know that the ideal
(\mathfrak{q}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{q}_{v_{d}})
is radical. If we write
(\mathfrak{q}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{q}_{v_{d}})=\sqrt{(\mathfrak{q}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{q}_{v_{d}})}=\mathfrak{r}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak{r}_{t}
for the primary decomposition, then by our definition, each
\mathfrak{r}_{i}
is a non-ordinary regular de Rham prime. Then we have the following relations.

\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{\prime}\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{\prime}=\sqrt{(\mathfrak{p}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{p}_{v_{d}})}=\sqrt{\cap(\mathfrak{q}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{q}_{v_{d}})}=\sqrt{\cap\mathfrak{r}}=\cap\mathfrak{r},

where
\mathfrak{r}
ranges over all non-ordinary regular de Rham primes containing the ideal
(\mathfrak{p}_{v_{1}},\cdots,\mathfrak{p}_{v_{d}})
.

Write
\mathfrak{a}_{i},1\leq i\leq s
for the intersection of all non-ordinary regular de Rham primes containing the minimal prime
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{\prime}
. Then by
[
Sta25
, Lemma 10.15.1]
, we know that there exists
j,1\leq j\leq s
, such that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{\prime}\supset\mathfrak{a}_{j}\supset\mathfrak{p}_{j}^{\prime}
. Therefore, we know that
i=j
, and
\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}_{i}
. This shows the result.
∎

By a
modular point
in the deformation space
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, we mean that the corresponding Galois representation arises from a twist of a Hilbert modular form, or equivalently, a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.

Theorem 5.3.7
.

The Zariski closure of the set of all modular points in the deformation space
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is
\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
, i.e. the union of all irreducible components of dimension
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. If further
\chi
is crystalline at each
v|p
, then the Zariski closure of all modular points which are crystalline at each
v|p
in the deformation space
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
is
\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}\subset\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
be the set consisting of all modular points and
\overline{\mathcal{X}}
be its Zariski closure in
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
.

Let
C
be an irreducible component of
\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
and let
\mathfrak{p}
be the corresponding generic point. Let
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
be the inverse image of
\mathfrak{p}
via the finite map
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}\to R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. It is a minimal prime of
\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v}
, and we get a finite injection
(\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v})/\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/\mathfrak{p}
. By Lemma
5.3.6
,
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
is the intersection of all non-ordinary regular de Rham primes containing
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
. For any non-ordinary regular de Rham prime
\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}\supset\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
, by the going-up property, we can find a prime

\mathfrak{r}\supset\mathfrak{p}
lying above
\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}
.

We show that the Galois representation
\rho(\mathfrak{r})
corresponding to
\mathfrak{r}
(in the sense of Definition
2.2.4
) arises from a twist of a Hilbert modular form. First, from our construction on
\mathfrak{r}
, we know that
\rho(\mathfrak{r})|_{G_{F_{v}}}
is absolutely irreducible for each
v|p
. In particular,
\rho(\mathfrak{r})
is absolutely irreducible. Second, for each
v\in\Sigma_{p}
, by our definition of regular de Rham prime (see Definition
3.3.3
), we know that there exists a big Hecke algebra (for some abelian extension
F^{1}
of
F
)
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
and a maximal prime
\mathfrak{t}
of
\mathbb{T}_{\psi}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p]
such that the Galois representation
\rho(\mathfrak{t})|_{G_{F^{1}_{v^{\prime}}}}
for some
v^{\prime}
lying above
v
is absolutely irreducible regular de Rham and
\mathrm{tr}\rho(\mathfrak{t})|_{G_{F^{1}_{v^{\prime}}}}=\mathrm{tr}\rho(\mathfrak{r})|_{G_{F_{v}}}
via the isomorphism
F^{1}_{v^{\prime}}\cong F_{v}
. By Brauer-Nesbitt theorem (see
[
Gee22
, Fact 2.5]
) for example), we know that for each
v\in\Sigma_{p}
,
\rho(\mathfrak{r})|_{G_{F_{v}}}
is absolutely irreducible and de Rham with distinct Hodge-Tate weights. Then by the non-ordinary Fontaine-Mazur conjecture (Theorem
4.1.3
),
\mathfrak{r}
is a modular point.

Write
C^{\operatorname{non-ord}}\subset C
for the set of all non-ordinary modular points obtained in the above way contained in
C
. We claim that
C^{\operatorname{non-ord}}
is Zariski dense in
C
. Write
\mathfrak{I}=\cap\mathfrak{r}
, where
\mathfrak{r}
ranges over all points in
C
that we obtain in the above way. By Lemma
5.3.6
, the inverse image of
\mathfrak{I}
via the finite injection
f:(\widehat{\otimes}_{v|p}R_{v})/\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}/\mathfrak{p}
is
0
. Write
\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak{p}_{c}
as its primary decomposition, and we have
0=f^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\cap\cdots\cap f^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{c})
. It implies
f^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=0
for some
1\leq i\leq c
. By the incomparability property (see
[
Sta25
, Lemma 10.36.20]
), we have
\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{p}_{i}=\mathfrak{p}
. This proves the claim.

In conclusion, we know that the Zariski closure of
\cup C^{\operatorname{non-ord}}
contains
\cup C=\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. In particular, we have
\overline{\mathcal{X}}\supset\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. On the other hand, as a modular point defines an irreducible Galois representation, by Proposition
2.4.4
, we have
\mathcal{X}\subset\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
. Consequently, we have
\overline{\mathcal{X}}=\Spec R_{F,0}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
.

If further
\chi
is crystalline at each
v|p
, then combining Lemma
3.3.4
, we can use the same proof.
∎

Let
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring of the pseudo-representation
1+\bar{\chi}
for
G_{F,\Sigma}
(without fixed determinant). We can also study the Zariski closure of modular points in the deformation space
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}
.

Theorem 5.3.8
.

The Zariski closure of the set of all modular points which are crystalline at each
v|p
in the deformation space
\Spec R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is the union of all irreducible components of Krull dimension
2+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Proof.

As
\bar{\chi}
can be viewed as a character of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
F
is an abelian totally real field in which
p
splits completely, we know that the set of crystalline liftings of
\bar{\chi}
is Zariski dense in its Galois deformation space
\Spec\mathcal{O}[[G_{F,\Sigma}^{\operatorname{ab}}(p)]]
. Then our result follows from Proposition
2.4.1
and Theorem
5.3.7
.
∎

We observe that if Paškūnas’ result (

6.
Applications

In this section, we study some applications of our results.

6.1.
Emerton’s conjecture

In this subsection, we prove Emerton’s conjecture about the Krull dimension of the big Hecke algebra for
p\geq 5
. Our main reference here is
[
Eme11b
]
.

For notations, let
p\geq 5
be an odd prime. We denote by
K
a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
. We fix a positive integer
N\geq 1
such that
p
does not divide
N
. Write
\Sigma
for the set of primes of
\mathbb{Q}
dividing
Np
.

For an integer
k\geq 1
, write
\mathcal{M}_{k}(N)
for the space of modular forms of weight
k
and level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
(or level
N
for simplicity). Recall that the map
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\mapsto d~\modd N
induces an isomorphism
\Gamma_{0}(N)/\Gamma_{1}(N)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. If
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, this map induces an automorphism of
\mathcal{M}_{k}(N)
by
\langle d\rangle
, the
diamond operator
.

Let
l
be a prime not dividing
N
. We define the automorphism
S_{l}
of
\mathcal{M}_{k}(N)
via the formula
S_{l}=\langle l\rangle l^{k-2}
. We also define the endomorphism
T_{l}
of
\mathcal{M}_{k}(N)
via the formula

(T_{l}f)(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{nl}(f)q^{n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}lc_{n}(S_{l}f)q^{nl},~~\tau\in\mathcal{H},~~q=\exp(2\pi i\tau),

where
c_{n}(f)
is the
n
-th Fourier coefficient of the modular form
f
.

The
Hecke algebra

\mathbb{T}_{k}(N)
(or just
\mathbb{T}_{k}
) is defined to be the
\mathbb{Z}
-subalgebra of
\End(\mathcal{M}_{k}(N))
generated by the Hecke operators
lS_{l}
and
T_{l}
as
l
ranges over primes not dividing
N
.

Let
\mathbb{T}_{\leq k}^{(p)}(N)
(or just
\mathbb{T}_{\leq k}^{(p)}
) be the
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra of endomorphisms of
\oplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{M}_{i}(N)
generated by the operators
lS_{l}
and
T_{l}
as
l
ranges over primes not dividing
Np
. From the definition, we can find that if
k^{\prime}>k
, then there is a surjection
\mathbb{T}_{\leq k^{\prime}}^{(p)}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\leq k}^{(p)}
. The
p
-adic Hecke algebra

\mathbb{T}(N)
(or just
\mathbb{T}
) is defined to be the projective limit

\mathbb{T}:=\varprojlim_{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{T}_{\leq k}^{(p)}

.

Theorem 6.1.1
.

The ring
\mathbb{T}
is
p
-adically complete, Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra, and is the product of finitely many noetherian local
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebras.

Proof.

See
[
Eme11b
, Theorem 2.7]
.
∎

Conjecture 6.1.2
.

[
Eme11b
, Conjecture 2.9]

The ring
\mathbb{T}
is equidimensional of Krull dimension
4
.

Using the theory of eigencurves, we can obtain the lower bound of
\dim\mathbb{T}
.

Proposition 6.1.3
.

The Krull dimension of each irreducible component of
\Spec\mathbb{T}
is at least
4
.

Proof.

See
[
Eme11b
, Corollary 2.28]
.
∎

If we replace
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by
\mathcal{O}
in all definitions above, we can define a
p
-adic Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}^{\prime}
over
\mathcal{O}
. It is clear that we have
\mathbb{T}^{\prime}=\mathbb{T}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathcal{O}
. From now on, we assume that our Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}
is defined over
\mathcal{O}
.

By Theorem
6.1.1
, we know that
\mathbb{T}
is semi-local. If we assume
\mathfrak{m}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{m}_{r}
are all maximal ideals of
\mathbb{T}
, combining
[
Mat80
, 24.C]
, we have

\mathbb{T}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{r}},

where each
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
is
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
-adically complete.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}
. It is classical that there exists a pseudo-representation
T_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{\mathbb{Q},\Sigma}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
such that
T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Frob_{l})=T_{l}
and
\det(\Frob_{l})=lS_{l}
(see
[
Deo23
, Lemma 4.1]
for example). Let
R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring of
T_{\mathfrak{m}}\modd\mathfrak{m}
for
G_{\mathbb{Q},\Sigma}
. Then by the universal property, we get a surjection
R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Theorem 6.1.4
.

Conjecture
6.1.2
holds for
p\geq 5
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a maximal ideal of
\mathbb{T}
. Combining Proposition
6.1.3
, we only need to show that
\dim\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}=4
. By Definition
2.2.4
, we can divide the proof into two cases, depending on the reducibility of
T_{\mathfrak{m}}\modd\mathfrak{m}
.

If
T_{\mathfrak{m}}\modd\mathfrak{m}
is reducible (
\mathfrak{m}
is Eisenstein), combining Proposition
5.2.6
and Proposition
6.1.3
(after a finite twist), we have
4\leq\dim\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}\leq\dim R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}=4
.

If
T_{\mathfrak{m}}\modd\mathfrak{m}
is irreducible (
\mathfrak{m}
is non-Eisenstein), then the universal deformation ring and the universal pseudo-deformation ring are the same (see
[
BBD+13
, Theorem 2.4.1, page 44]
). Combining Proposition
6.1.3
and Corollary
A.0.6
, we have
4\leq\dim\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}\leq\dim R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}=4
.
∎

6.2.
Big
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem (I)

In this subsection, we prove a big
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem in the residually reducible case in the setting of completed cohomology of modular curves.

For notations, let
p\geq 3
be an odd prime. We denote by
K
a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
. Write
\Sigma
for a set of primes of
\mathbb{Q}
containing
p
and write
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}\amalg\{p\}
. Let
\bar{\chi}:G_{\mathbb{Q},\Sigma}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be a continuous, odd character. Assume that
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\neq\omega
if
p=3
.

We first recall the definition of completed cohomology of modular curves. Our main references are
[
Eme11a
, Section 5]
and
[
Nak23
, Section 2]
.

For any compact open subgroup
K_{f}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty})
, we let
Y(K_{f})
denote the adelic modular curve

Y(K_{f}):=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\setminus((\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R})\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\infty}))/K_{f}.

We may also view
Y(K_{f})
as an algebraic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. We write
H^{1}(K_{f})_{A}:=H_{\operatorname{et}}^{1}(Y(K_{f})_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}},A)
, where
A
denotes one of
K,\mathcal{O},
or
\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{s}\mathcal{O}
for some
s>0
.

If
K^{p}
is some fixed compact open subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
, where
\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}=\mathbb{Q}\otimes\prod_{l\neq p}\mathbb{Z}_{l}
, we write
H^{1}(K^{p})_{A}:=\varinjlim_{K_{p}}H^{1}(K_{p}K^{p})_{A}
. Here the direct limit is taken over all compact open subgroups
K_{p}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Write
\widehat{H}^{1}(K^{p})_{\mathcal{O}}:=\varprojlim_{s}H^{1}(K^{p})_{\mathcal{O}}/\varpi^{s}H^{1}(K^{p})_{\mathcal{O}}
for the
\varpi
-adic completion of
H^{1}(K^{p})_{\mathcal{O}}
. Write
\widehat{H}^{1}(K^{p})_{K}:=\widehat{H}^{1}(K^{p})_{\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
.

Write
G_{\Sigma_{0}}:=\prod_{l\in\Sigma_{0}}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{l})
and
K_{0}^{\Sigma}:=\prod_{l\notin\Sigma}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{l})
. If
K_{\Sigma_{0}}
is an open subgroup of
G_{\Sigma_{0}}
, we write
\widehat{H}^{1}(K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{A}:=\widehat{H}^{1}(K_{\Sigma_{0}}K_{0}^{\Sigma})_{A}
for
A=K
or
\mathcal{O}
. Also write
\widehat{H}^{1}_{A,\Sigma}:=\varinjlim_{K_{\Sigma_{0}}}\widehat{H}^{1}(K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{A}
. It is well-known that
\widehat{H}^{1}_{A,\Sigma}
are equipped with a continuous action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\times G_{\Sigma_{0}}
.

Let
K_{\Sigma}
be a compact open subgroup of
G_{\Sigma}=\prod_{l\in\Sigma}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{l})
. For any prime
l\notin\Sigma
, we write
S_{l}
and
T_{l}
for the Hecke operators acting on
H^{1}(K_{\Sigma}K_{0}^{\Sigma})_{\mathcal{O}}
corresponding to the double cosets of
K_{l}
in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{l})
represented by
\begin{pmatrix}l&0\\
0&l\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}l&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
respectively. We let
\mathbb{T}(K_{\Sigma})_{\mathcal{O}}
denote the
\mathcal{O}
-subalgebra of
\End_{\mathcal{O}}(H^{1}(K_{\Sigma}K_{0}^{\Sigma})_{\mathcal{O}})
generated by
S_{l}
and
T_{l}
for all
\ \notin\Sigma
. If
K_{\Sigma_{0}}
is an open subgroup of
G_{\Sigma_{0}}
, and
K_{p}^{\prime}\subset K_{p}
are compact open subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, then there is a natural surjection
\mathbb{T}(K_{p}^{\prime}K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{\mathcal{O}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}(K_{p}K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{\mathcal{O}}
. Define
\mathbb{T}(K_{\Sigma_{0}})=\varprojlim_{K_{p}}\mathbb{T}(K_{p}K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{\mathcal{O}}
, which is an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra topologically generated by the Hecke operators
S_{l}
and
T_{l}
for all
l\notin\Sigma
and acts faithfully on
\widehat{H}^{1}(K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{\mathcal{O}}
.

We consider the pseudo-representation
\bar{T}=\bar{\chi}_{1}+\bar{\chi}_{2}:G_{\mathbb{Q},\Sigma}\to\mathbb{F}
, where
\bar{\chi}_{1},\bar{\chi}_{2}:G_{\mathbb{Q},\Sigma}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
are continuous characters satisfying
\bar{\chi}_{1}\bar{\chi}_{2}^{-1}=\bar{\chi}
. It is classical that
\bar{T}
is modular (see
[
Deo23
, Lemma 2.23]
or
[
FKP22
, Lemma 7.3]
for example). In other words, there exist a compact open subgroup
K_{\Sigma_{0}}\subset G_{\Sigma_{0}}
and a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathbb{T}(K_{\Sigma_{0}})
satisfying

\bar{T}(\Frob_{l})\equiv T_{l}\modd\mathfrak{m},~~\det(\bar{T}(\Frob_{l}))\equiv lS_{l}\modd\mathfrak{m}

for all
l\notin\Sigma
. We say such
K_{\Sigma_{0}}
is an
allowable level
for the pseudo-representation
\bar{T}
.

For an allowable level
K_{\Sigma_{0}}
, there exists a pseudo-deformation
T_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{\mathbb{Q},\Sigma}\to\mathbb{T}(K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
satisfying

T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Frob_{l})=T_{l},~~\det(T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\Frob_{l}))=lS_{l}

for all
l\notin\Sigma
. If
K_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\prime}\subset K_{\Sigma_{0}}
is an inclusion of allowable levels, then
\mathbb{T}(K_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\prime})_{\mathfrak{m}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}(K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a surjection. Define
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\varprojlim_{K_{\Sigma_{0}}}\mathbb{T}(K_{\Sigma_{0}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
, where
K_{\Sigma_{0}}
ranges over all allowable levels.

Let
R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring of
\bar{T}
. Combining Proposition
2.4.1
and Proposition
5.2.6
(after a finite twist), we can define
R_{\mathbb{Q},0}^{\mathrm{ps}}:=R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}/I_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
I_{\mathbb{Q}}
is the intersection of all minimal prime ideals of dimension
4
. Hence,
R_{\mathbb{Q},0}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is equidimensional.

Note that by the universal property, we get a natural surjection
R_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ps}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
. The following result is an analogue to the big
R=\mathbb{T}
theorem in the residually irreducible case proved by Böckle and Emerton (see

6.4.
Serre’s modularity conjecture in the residually reducible case

In this subsection, we discuss the relation between our previous results and Serre’s modularity conjecture in the residually reducible case.

Serre’s modularity conjecture states that for an odd, irreducible, continuous Galois representation
\bar{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
with a finite field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
(including the case
p=2
), it arises from a newform. It has been proved in
[
KW09a
]
and
[
KW09b
]
. If
\bar{\rho}
is reducible, then we say it is
modular
if there exist a Galois representation
\rho_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{f})
associated to some newform
f
and a suitable choice of lattice over
\mathcal{O}_{f}
such that
\bar{\rho}\cong\rho_{f}\modd\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{f}}
.

To state it more precisely, we give some notations first. Let
p\geq 5
be an odd prime, and let
F
be an abelian totally real field in which
p
splits completely. Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of finite places containing
\Sigma_{p}
, the set of places
v
of
F
lying above
p
. We denote by
K
a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\bar{\chi}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be a continuous, totally odd character, and suppose that
\bar{\chi}
can be extended to a character of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let
x\in H^{1}(F_{\Sigma}/F,\mathbb{F}(\bar{\chi}^{-1}))
be a non-zero cohomology class. It is easy to see that
x
defines a two-dimensional representation (up to equivalence)

\bar{\rho}_{x}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}),~~g\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&x(g)\bar{\chi}(g)\\
0&\bar{\chi}(g)\end{pmatrix}.

For simplicity, we choose a complex conjugation
c\in G_{F,\Sigma}
and an element
\sigma\in G_{F,\Sigma}
, and assume
x(c)=0,x(\sigma)=1
for the definition of
\bar{\rho}_{x}
. Then it is conjectured that the Galois representation
\bar{\rho}_{x}
arises from some newform
f
. In
[
FKP22
]
, they solved most cases when
F=\mathbb{Q}
(also including
x=0
) by a Galois theoretic method.

Let
\chi:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a de Rham lifting of
\bar{\chi}
. As

, we can define the universal deformation ring

of the non-split Galois representation

with determinant
\chi
. Let
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring parametrizing all pseudo-deformations of

with determinant
\chi
. By the universal property, we have a natural map
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{x}^{\chi}
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a one-dimensional prime of

such that the Galois representation

associated to

is irreducible. Here we do not require

.

Proposition 6.4.1
.

For any irreducible component of
\Spec R_{x}^{\chi}
containing
\mathfrak{p}
, it is of Krull dimension
1+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Proof.

Let
C
be an irreducible component of
\Spec R_{x}^{\chi}
with generic point
\mathfrak{q}
. Let
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
be the kernel of the composite map
R_{F}^{\mathrm{ps},\chi}\to R_{x}^{\chi}\twoheadrightarrow R_{x}^{\chi}/\mathfrak{q}
. We show that
C

is of Krull dimension

.

By the global characteristic formula (see
[
Zha24b
, Proposition 3.1.3]
), we know that each irreducible component of

has Krull dimension at least

.

Note that
\rho(\mathfrak{p})
is irreducible, which implies that the Galois representation

associated to
\mathfrak{p}
is also irreducible. By

, Corollary 2.3.6 (2)]

∎

Keep notations in the proof of Proposition
6.4.1
. Combining Proposition
5.2.6
, we can find that
\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
defines an irreducible component

of

is Zariski dense. Then it is natural to pose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 6.4.2
.

For an irreducible component
C
of
\Spec R_{x}^{\chi}

In general, we do not know whether there exists an irreducible lifting of

unramified outside

]
, their method first requires to enlarge the finite set

Remark 6.4.3
.

We may prove Conjecture

under the assumption

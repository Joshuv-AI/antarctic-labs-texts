Outer length billiards on a large scale
Peter Albers, Lael Edwards-Costa, Serge Tabachnikov

Introduction

The billiard dynamical system describes the motion of a point mass confined in a domain and experiencing elastic collision with its boundary.
We consider the case when the billiard table is bounded by a smooth closed quadratically convex planar curve (an oval).

The billiard reflection law, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, has a variational origin. Consider the left Figure
1
. Fix points
x
and
z

vary on the curve
{\gamma}

|xy|+|yz|
is extremal as a function of

, see, e.g.,
[
23

.
Thus one may describe this system as the “inner length billiard”.

The goal of this paper is two-fold. Section

The second goal of the paper is motivated by our recent study of the dynamical behavior of the outer symplectic billiard map “at infinity”, that is, very far away from the billiard table, in

Take a point very far away from the billiard table, the oval
{\gamma}

Generating function and invariant area form

Let
{\gamma}(x)
be arc length parameterization of the oval.
Recall from
[
3
]
that a generating function for the map
F
is
H(x,x^{\prime})=|xA|+|Ax^{\prime}|
: if points
x
and
x^{\prime\prime}
are fixed and
x^{\prime}\in{\gamma}
varies, then
x^{\prime}
is a critical point of the function
H(x,x^{\prime})+H(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})
if and only if
x^{\prime}
is the tangency point of a circle that is tangent to the positive tangent ray to
{\gamma}
at
x
, the negative tangent ray at
x^{\prime\prime}
, and
{\gamma}
, as in Figure
2
. In formulas,

F(x,x^{\prime})=(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})
if and only if

\frac{\partial(H(x,x^{\prime})+H(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}))}{\partial x^{\prime}}=0.

(1)

Since the results of the next two lemmas were stated in
[
3
]
without proofs, we present proofs here.

Lemma 2.1

The partial derivatives if the generating function are as follows:

\begin{split}\frac{\partial H(x,x^{\prime})}{\partial x}=-&k(x)|Ax|\cot\frac{\varphi}{2}-1,\ \frac{\partial H(x,x^{\prime})}{\partial x^{\prime}}=k(x^{\prime})|Ax^{\prime}|\cot\frac{\varphi}{2}+1,\\
&\frac{\partial^{2}H(x,x^{\prime})}{\partial x\partial x^{\prime}}=-\frac{k(x)k(x^{\prime})(|Ax|+|Ax^{\prime}|)}{2\sin^{2}\frac{\varphi}{2}},\end{split}

(2)

where
k
is the curvature function of
{\gamma}
, and
\varphi
is the angle between the tangent segments
Ax
and
Ax^{\prime}
.

Figure 5:
To Lemma
2.1
.

Proof.

Referring to Figure
5
, choose the coordinate system so that point
x^{\prime}
is the origin and
x^{\prime}A
is the positive horizontal coordinate axis. Assume that the curve near point
x
is arc length parameterized and its components are functions
a(x)
and
b(x)
. Then

a^{\prime}=\cos\phi,b^{\prime}=\sin\phi,a^{\prime\prime}=-k(x)\sin\phi,b^{\prime\prime}=k(x)\cos\phi,

where
k
is the curvature and prime means
d/dx
.

Let
|xA|=\lambda
. We have

a+\lambda a^{\prime}=A,\ b+\lambda b^{\prime}=0.

Differentiating the second equation, we get

1+\lambda^{\prime}=-\frac{\lambda k\cos\phi}{\sin\phi},

hence

\lambda^{\prime}=-\frac{\lambda k\cos\phi}{\sin\phi}-1.

Differentiating the first equation, yields

A^{\prime}=(1+\lambda^{\prime})\cos\phi-\lambda k\sin\phi=-\frac{\lambda k}{\sin\phi}.

Hence

\frac{dH}{dx}=-|Ax|k(x)\cot(\phi/2)-1,\ \frac{dH}{dx^{\prime}}=|Ax^{\prime}|k(x^{\prime})\cot(\phi/2)+1,

where the second equation is obtained the same way as the first one.

Differentiating the second equation with respect to
x
, using the fact that
d\varphi/dx=k(x)
and some trigonometry, yields the last formula in (
2
).

\Box

Equation (
2
) implies that

\frac{\partial^{2}H(x,x^{\prime})}{\partial x\partial x^{\prime}}<0,

the monotone twist condition for the map
F
. The map
F
preserves the area 2-form

\omega=-\frac{\partial^{2}H(x,x^{\prime})}{\partial x\partial x^{\prime}}\ dx\wedge dx^{\prime}.

Remark 2.2

Along with the arc length parameterization of the curve
\gamma
, one can parameterize it by the direction of the outward normal. Let
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
be these directions at points
\gamma(x)
and
\gamma(x^{\prime})
.
Then
0<\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha<\pi
.
Since
k(x)=d\alpha/dx
and likewise for
x^{\prime}
, and since
\varphi=\pi-(\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha)
,
the last formula in (
2
) implies:

\omega=-\frac{(|Ax|+|Ax^{\prime}|)}{2\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha}{2}\right)}\ d\alpha\wedge d\alpha^{\prime}.

The form
\omega
is a multiple of the standard area form
\omega_{0}
.

Lemma 2.3

One has

\omega_{A}=\cot\left(\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Ax|}+\frac{1}{|Ax^{\prime}|}\right)\omega_{0}.

Proof.

This follows from Crofton’s formula, see formula (2.8) in
[
19
]
:

\omega_{0}=\frac{|Ax||Ax^{\prime}|k(x)k(x^{\prime})}{\sin\varphi}dx\wedge dx^{\prime}

and the last formula in (
2
).

\Box

Remark 2.4

The invariant area forms of the conventional, inner, and of the outer billiard maps are independent of the shape of the billiard table: in the former case, this is the canonical symplectic form on the space of oriented lines, and in the latter it is the standard area form in the plane. Thus in these cases the area form and the map are decoupled.

For the conventional billiards this fact implies that the billiard maps with respect to confocal ellipses commute, see, e.g.,
[
23
]
. This is a non-trivial configuration theorem, see Figure
6
. The idea of the proof is that a confocal family of ellipses define a foliation of the space of oriented lines, inducing the Arnold-Liouville coordinates on the leaves. The billiard maps are parallel translations in these coordinates, and parallel translations commute.

Figure 6:
Left: the billiard maps with respect to confocal ellipses commute. Right: the outer length billiard maps about confocal ellipses do not commute.

In contrast, for outer length billiards, as well as for symplectic billiards (see
[
2
]
), the invariant form depends on the billiard table. Although the outer length billiard map about an ellipse is integrable – the invariant curves again are confocal ellipses (see the next section) – the respective maps do not commute.

Next we determine the behavior of the area form
\omega
at infinity.
To do that we work in polar coordinates and let the points go to infinity radially. In the notations of Lemma
2.3
, we write
A=(r,\psi)
and we consider the limit of the factor

C_{A}(\psi):=\cot\left(\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Ax|}+\frac{1}{|Ax^{\prime}|}\right)

as
r\to\infty
.

Let
{\gamma}
be an oval and
\psi
a direction. There are two support lines of
{\gamma}
parallel to
\psi
. The distance between them is the
width

w_{\gamma}(\psi)
.

Lemma 2.5

One has

\lim_{r\to\infty}C_{A}(\psi)=\frac{4}{w_{\gamma}(\psi)}.

Figure 7:
To Lemma
2.5
.

Proof.

Consider Figure
7
left. The segments
xy
and
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
are orthogonal to the tangents
Ax
and
Ax^{\prime}
, the points
y
and
y^{\prime}
lie on the bisector of the angle

. Hence

\cot\left(\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)\frac{1}{|Ax|}=\frac{1}{|xy|},\ \ \cot\left(\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)\frac{1}{|Ax^{\prime}|}=\frac{1}{|x^{\prime}y^{\prime}|}.

As

, the lines

become parallel and the bisector becomes the middle line of the strip made by these lines, see Figure

Remark 2.6

One can analyze the limiting behavior of the form
\omega_{A}
as the point tends to the curve
{\gamma}

Similarly to the conventional billiard, the outer length billiard map

is integrable if the table
{\gamma}
is an ellipse. The next result was proved in
[
9

Confocal ellipses are invariant curves of the outer length billiard about an ellipse.

Theorem 1

If
{\gamma}
is an ellipse then the confocal ellipses are invariant curves of the outer length billiard map
F
, see Figure
8
.

In this section, we put this result into historical context and make some related comments.

(1) Figure
8
is a limiting case of the Chasles-Reye theorem depicted in Figure
9
. Let us recall this theorem.

Let
{\gamma}
be an ellipse and
C
and
D
be two points on a confocal hyperbola. Draw tangent lines to
{\gamma}
from points
C
and
D
. These four lines form a quadrilateral
ACBD
. The claim of the theorem is that points
A
and
B
lie on a confocal ellipse, and the quadrilateral
ACBD
is circumscribed about a circle. See
[
1
,
15
]
for a modern take on this result, its generalizations, and the relation to billiards in ellipses.

If point
C
is chosen to be on
{\gamma}
, the quadrilateral
ACBD
degenerates to a triangle, and the circle that is tangent to the sides of this triangle is tangent to the side
AB
at point
C
, as needed in the definition of the outer length billiard.

Figure 9:
Chasles-Reye theorem and the Poncelet grid.

(2) Let
E_{1}\subset E_{2}
be nested confocal ellipses. Assume that there exists
a polygon inscribed into
E_{2}
and circumscribed about
E_{1}
, that is, a periodic orbit of the conventional (inner) billiard in
E_{2}
and, at the same time, a periodic orbit of the outer length billiard about

The extensions of the sides of such a Poncelet polygon form the Poncelet grid, see
[

20

. The Chasles-Reye theorem implies that the quadrilaterals of this grid are circumscribed, see Figure

(3) The observation that the vertices of the polygons of minimal perimeter circumscribed about an ellipse lie on a confocal ellipse is due to Steiner (Werke II. pp. 618--20).

(4) Consider again nested confocal ellipses
E_{1}\subset E_{2}
. Then
E_{2}
is an invariant curve of the outer length billiard map
F
with respect to
E_{1}
. Since

is completely integrable, the Arnold-Liouville theorem implies the existence of an

E_{2}
. The description of this measure goes back to Bertrand’s proof of the Poncelet porism ; see, e.g.,
[
5
]
for a recent treatment.

We present an explicit formula, a particular case of Theorem 1.5 in
[
5
]
when the ellipses are confocal. Let

\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}=1\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ \frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}+\lambda}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}+\lambda}=1,\ \lambda>0

be the equations of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
respectively, and let
s
be the arc length parameter on
E_{2}
. Then the
F
-invariant measure
\mu
on
E_{2}
is given by
ds/G(x,y)
where

G(x,y)={\sqrt{\left[\frac{x^{2}}{(a^{2}+\lambda)^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{(b^{2}+\lambda)^{2}}\right]\left[\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}-1\right]}}.

In particular, consider the case of
\lambda\to\infty
. In this limit,
E_{2}
is a circle parameterized as
\sqrt{\lambda}(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)
. The (rescaled) invariant measure is then given by

\mu=\frac{d\alpha}{\sqrt{b^{2}\cos^{2}\alpha+a^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha}}.

We note that the denominator is the support function of the ellipse
E_{1}
,
see, e.g., Lemma 3.1 of
[
12

. This is half the width of
E_{1}
in the direction
\alpha
, that is,

\mu=2\frac{d\alpha}{w_{E_{1}}(\alpha)}.

Let the ellipse degenerate to a segment of length
2a
, that is, let

(cf. Figure
3

\mu=\frac{d\alpha}{a\sin\alpha},

where the denominator is half the length of the projection of the segment along the direction
\alpha
.
This measure has singularities at
\alpha=0,\pi
, corresponding to the fact that the billiard trajectory in an ellipse that passes through its foci converges to the major axis, traversed back and forth.
The vector field dual to
\mu
, namely,

4
The second iteration of the outer length billiard map at infinity

As stated in the introduction, the second iteration of the outer length billiard map at infinity is approximated by the flow of a Hamiltonian vector field whose Hamiltonian function is the distance from the origin with respect to the area form, invariant under the map. In this section we explain and quantify this phenomenon.

Let
(\alpha,r)
be the polar coordinates; since
{\gamma}
is fixed, we shorthand
w_{\gamma}(\alpha)
to
w(\alpha)
. The standard area form is
rdr\wedge d\alpha
. Consider the area form

\Omega=\frac{rdr\wedge d\alpha}{w(\alpha)}.

This is, up to a factor, the invariant area form from Lemma
2.5
, extended to the whole plane.

Consider a Hamiltonian function
H(\alpha,r)=2r
, and let

X=-\frac{2w(\alpha)}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}

be its Hamiltonian vector field. This field is homogeneous of degree zero and its trajectories are the origin centered circles. Denote by
\Phi
the time-
1
flow of
X
.

As stated above, the flow of
X
approximates the second iteration of the outer length billiard map at infinity. More precisely, one has

Theorem 2

There is a constant
C
, depending only on the oval
{\gamma}
, such that

|F^{2}(x)-\Phi(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|}.

The aim of this section is to prove this theorem. Before immersing in rather tedious estimates, we describe a “blueprint” of the proof.

Figure 10:
Two ellipses.

Consider Figure
10
where
y=F(x),z=F(y)
. Since the two tangents to a (brown) circle from the same point have equal lengths, we have
|xb|+|xd|=|yb|+|yd|
, that is, points
x
and
y
lie on the (red) ellipse with foci
b
and
d
. Likewise, points
y
and
z
lie on the (purple) ellipse with foci
a
and
c
.

What happens when point
x
goes to infinity in direction
\alpha
?
The lines
xy
and
zy
become nearly parallel. Points
a
and
b
merge together, and so do
c
and
d
; the two resulting “double points”, say,
p
and

, are the tangency points of the support lines of
{\gamma}
in the direction
\alpha
. The two ellipses also merge together; they have foci
p
and
q
. Thus the outer length billiard map on the oval
{\gamma}
is approximated by that on the segment
pq
.

Furthermore, the large ellipses with fixed foci become nearly circular. Thus, in the limit, we obtain a circle with a segment
pq
inside it and a Poncelet-like circle map using, alternating, the support lines of the segment at its end points. This map can be explicitly analyzed: it is approximated by the time-1 flow of the vector field that is the limit of the field
X
as
{\gamma}
tends to a segment.

Now we get to the estimates needed for the proof of Theorem
2

{\gamma}
and (in some case) on the choice of the origin inside it.

To keep the size of the paper reasonable, occasionally we use the big O notation.
Recall the definition. Given two functions,

for all

f(x)=O(g(x))\ \ {\rm as}\ \ x\to 0

if there exist constants

|f(x)|\leq C|g(x)|
for all

.

depends on the choice of the origin, and we start by describing its dependence on this choice. The next lemma makes it possible to choose the origin in a convenient way in what follows.

Recall two notions from convex geometry.

Let

Lemma 5.1

Let

Proof.

The (co)vector

Rescaling by dividing by

To find the speed of the limiting continuous motion of the point
C
, let
t
be the time parameter of the Hamiltonian flow that approximates the motion of point
x

F^{2}
. Denote the time derivative by dot. The sectorial area rate of change is
\frac{1}{2}R^{2}\dot{\beta}

\beta=\alpha+\frac{\pi}{2},\ R=\frac{2r^{2}}{w(\alpha)},

one has

\frac{1}{2}R^{2}\dot{\beta}=\frac{2r^{3}}{w(\alpha)}.

That is, the rate of change of the sectorial area is inverse proportional to the widths of the oval
{\gamma}

Gravitomagnetism from Temporal Dimensional Reduction
Mehran Z-Abyaneh and Mehrdad Farhoudi

Abstract
We propose that the Taub-NUT metric can be envisaged as a (3+1) dimensional analog of the Kaluza-Klein (4+1) dimensional metric. After dimensional reduction of the Taub-NUT metric to (3) spatial dimensions, by treating time as the extra curled dimension (since the closed time-like curves can exist in the Taub-NUT framework, such a dimensional reduction is justified), we end up with three dimensional Einstein field equations plus the Maxwell equations for the gravitomagnetic field, which also acts as a source to Einstein field equations. Hence, the Taub-NUT metric unifies gravity and the NUT charge related gavitomagnetism in four dimensions, at the same footing the Kaluza-Klein metric unifies gravity and electromagnetism in five dimensions. We also find an interesting relation between the four dimensional gravitational constant and the Taub-NUT charge. The results are derived from classical field equations in Lorentzian signature.



Keywords:
Kaluza-Klein theory, Taub-NUT metric, Gravitomagnetism, Closed time-like curves.

Introduction

It is known that gravity and electromagnetism can be
unified in the framework of the (4+1) dimensional Kaluza-Klein (KK)
theory, which reduces to the Einstein equations of general
relativity and Maxwell equations in (3+1) dimensions, after the
extra dimension is compactified.

adding an extra dimension. Kaluza did the calculations by taking

fifth dimension exists but that no physical quantities depend upon
it. Another approach to explain the apparently four-dimensional
nature of the world was introduced by Oskar

dependence on the extra dimension is because it is compactified to

relativity. The theory proposes that the electromagnetic field
arises from the curvature of the extra dimension in the same way
that gravity arises from the curvature of the four dimensions of
spacetime.

KK theory was an important precursor to modern theories of
particle physics, such as string theory and
supersymmetry
[

. While the theory has not been fully
validated by experimental evidence, it continues to be an area of
active research, as it provides a framework for exploring the

theories of particle physics, such as string theory, brane theory

paradoxical behavior implies that the usual interpretation of

CTCs are not unique to the Taub-NUT metric though. They arise in
several solutions of Einstein’s field equations, particularly in
spacetimes with unusual topology or specific symmetry properties.
Notable examples include: The Gödel solution as a rotating
cosmological model that admits CTCs

and violates

event horizon) of a rotating black hole, which is represented by

[

The existence of CTCs in these solutions has sparked significant
debate and research. The causality violations are often considered
artifacts of the mathematical structure rather than physically
realizable scenarios and several approaches have been adapted in
the literature to modify spacetime metrics and eliminate CTCs.
Some treatments suggest removing or modifying the problematic
regions of spacetime, such as cutting out the regions responsible
for CTCs
[
18
]
. Also, Stephen Hawking proposed the
Chronology Protection Conjecture, hypothesizing that quantum
effects prevent the formation of CTCs in physically realistic
spacetimes
[
28

that there is a Chronology Protection Agency which prevents the
appearance of closed time-like curves and so makes the universe
safe for historians.” Also
[

adjusting the

Nonetheless, such approaches to eliminate CTCs in the 4D Taub-NUT
metric come with trade-offs, such as restricting the spatial
coordinates.

In this work, we study the Lorentzian Taub-NUT solution, retaining its CTCs and associated causal structure. Our analysis
suggest that, when time is not compactified, the Taub-NUT metric unifies gravitomagnetism and gravity in
(3+1) dimensions. On the other hand, when the time
coordinate is considered to be curled to a non-observable
size, similar to the fifth dimension of the KK metric, gravity and the NUT charge related gravitomagnetism
appear as two distinct fields. We stress that when only the ordinary mass is
involved and the NUT charge is zero, such a compactification
does not work. Also, by analyzing its geometric properties, we uncover a novel relation between the NUT charge and Newton’s constant.
Although the results are derived from classical field equations in Lorentzian signature, they may admit a Euclidean counterpart, subject to appropriate analytic continuation and boundary conditions, where the Taub-NUT charge can be interpreted as a quantized topological charge, arising from the non-trivial fiber bundle structure and regularity conditions in Euclidean gravity
[
35

the summary of the results in conclusions.

3
The Taub-NUT Solution

The Taub-NUT solution is an important exact solution to Einstein’s field equations. It represents a class of
spacetimes that generalize the Schwarzschild solution by
incorporating additional parameters associated with gravitational
monopole and dipole moments. The analog of this solution in five
dimensional space-time is called the Gross-Perry
solution
[
39
]
. The Taub-NUT solution, which is a vacuum solution that exhibits
unique topological and geometrical properties is expressed as

ds^{2}=-\Phi^{2}(r)\left(dt+2l\cos{\theta}d{\varphi}\right)^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\Phi^{2}(r)}+(r^{2}+l^{2})\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}{\theta}d\varphi^{2}\right),

(9)

where the metric function can be written as
[
40
]

\Phi(r)=\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}-2Mr-l^{2}}{r^{2}+l^{2}}}.

(10)

Here,
M
represents the gravitational mass, and
l
is the NUT
charge. The parameter distinguishes the Taub-NUT solution from
the Schwarzschild metric, which corresponds to the case
l=0
. The
NUT parameter introduces a non-trivial structure in the spacetime,
often interpreted as a gravitomagnetic monopole moment. The
presence of the term
2l\cos{\theta}
in the metric indicates a
coordinate singularity along the
z
axis, known as Misner
strings
[
27
]
, which is a semi-infinite (quasiregular)
singularity along the half-axis
\theta=\pi
. These strings are
associated with non-trivial topology and are analogous to the
Dirac string in the theory of magnetic monopoles
[
26
]
.
Unlike the Schwarzschild solution, the Taub-NUT spacetime is not
asymptotically flat in the usual sense. Instead, due to the
parameter
l
, it exhibits a more complex asymptotic behavior.

The main signature of Taub-NUT solutions is the presence of the
Misner string either at
\theta=0
or
\theta=\pi
making the
1-form
d\varphi
ill-defined at either of these points. In order
to get rid of the singularity at
\theta=0
, one can shift the
time coordinate such that
t^{\prime}=t+2n\varphi
. For the
\theta=\pi

one can take
t^{\prime\prime}=t-2n\varphi
. Since
\varphi
is periodic by

2\pi
one has to identify
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
modulo
8\pi n
, which
boils down to imposing the periodicity on the time coordinate by

t\rightarrow t+8\pi n
. Such a result makes the one form

d\varphi
well defined but leads to CTCs in the Lorentzian
metric. Importantly, eliminating the Misner string also leads to a quantization condition,
namely quantization the NUT charge
[
41
]
,
which also acts as a topological charge by determining the topological twisting of the spacetime
[
42
]
.

5
Conclusions

The KK metric unifies gravity and electromagnetism by
adding an extra dimension, while assuming that the extra dimension
is curled to a very small scale, i. e., the Planck scale. After
the compactification to four dimensions the KK metric leads to
Einstein equations and Maxwell equations of electromagnetism in
four dimensions.

On the other hand, the Lorentzian Taub-NUT metric, as a peculiar solution to
Einstein’s field equations of general relativity in (3+1)
dimensions, introduces a parameter known as the NUT charge that leads to the gravitomagnetic field, which is different from the one that arises in the weak-field slow-motion limit of general relativity and is related to the
gravitational magnetic monopole. Such a monopole exhibits a feature called Misner string, which is a singularity analogous to the Dirac string in
electromagnetism. One way to avoid the Misner string is to make time periodic, but by doing so one automatically makes CTCs.
However, the existence of CTCs challenges the causality principle and has led to the idea that Taub-NUT is more of a gravitational toy
model rather than a physically realistic solution.

In this work, we study the Lorentzian Taub-NUT solution, retaining its CTCs and associated causal structure.
By analyzing its geometric properties and exploiting the formal similarity of the metric and the
KK metric we show that, when
l\neq 0
we can deal with the time as an extra dimension.
After such an extra dimension is curled, that is by reduction of (3+1) dimensions to (3) spatial dimensions,
the vacuum Einstein equations in (3+1) dimensions lead to the Einstein field equations in (3) spatial dimensions
plus the three dimensional gravitomagnetic Maxwell equations. Such a gravitomagnetic filed also acts as a source to the three dimensional Einstein equations, similar to
the KK reduction, where the electromagnetic field acts as a source to (3+1) dimensional Einstein equations.
It is specifically
interesting that in Lorentzian Taub-NUT solution such a dimensional reduction scheme goes hand in
hand with the existence of CTCs because it only works if time is curled up. One way to avoid problems with causality is to assume
that they are confined to Planck-scale.
Such a compactification only occurs when
l\neq 0
and for
l=0
, i. e., the Schwarzschild limit, such a compactification
does not occur and time remains as a non-compact coordinate.

We emphasize that the reduction of the (3+1) dimensions to only three spatial
dimensions leads the gravity and the NUT charge related gravitomagnetism, to appear as two distinct fields. We also find an interesting relation between the four dimensional gravitational constant, i. e., the Newton’s constant, and the Taub-NUT charge in Lorentzian signature. Provided appropriate analytic continuation and boundary conditions are met, such a relation may admit a Euclidean counterpart, where the Taub-NUT charge may be interpreted as a quantized topological charge, and the relation can have implications for quantum gravity and string theory.

Moreover, such a compactification leads to a specific form of the
dilaton field that can have interesting effects. Also, such an approach would potentially serve
as a template for addressing CTC issues in other spacetimes and might strengthen our confidence in the consistency of
general relativity and causality.

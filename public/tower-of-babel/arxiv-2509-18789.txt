A Weyl equidistribution theorem over function fields
Ethan Ackelsberg

Abstract
. A classical theorem of Weyl states that any polynomial with an irrational coefficient other than the constant term is uniformly distributed mod 1. We prove a new function field analogue of this statement, confirming a conjecture of Lê, Liu, and Wooley.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this short note is to provide a positive resolution to a conjecture of Lê, Liu, and Wooley (
[
LLW23
, Conjecture 1.3]
) concerning equidistribution of polynomial sequences over function fields.

We briefly review Weyl’s classical equidistribution theorem as motivation for the function field analogue.
A sequence of real numbers
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
uniformly distributed mod 1
if for every interval
(a,b)\subseteq[0,1]
,

\frac{\left|\left\{1\leq n\leq N:\{x_{n}\}\in(a,b)\right\}\right|}{N}\to b-a,

where
\{x_{n}\}
denotes the fractional part of
x_{n}
.
In other words, the amount of time the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
spends in any given interval is proportional to the length of the interval.
The study of uniform distribution was initiated by Weyl
[
W16
]
, who proved the following remarkable theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
W16
, Satz 9]
)
.

Let
P(n)=\alpha_{d}n^{d}+\ldots+\alpha_{1}n+\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}[n]
.
If at least one of
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
is irrational, then
(P(n))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly distributed mod 1.

Let us now set our notation and terminology for the function field setting.
Fix a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
p
.
We introduce the suggestive notation
\mathcal{Z}
for the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
and
\mathcal{Q}
for the field of rational functions
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
.
The field
\mathcal{Q}
has an absolute value
\left|\frac{u}{v}\right|=q^{\deg{u}-\deg{v}}
with the convention that
\deg{0}=-\infty
.
The completion of
\mathcal{Q}
with respect to this absolute value is (isomorphic to) the field of formal Laurent series

\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}_{q}((t^{-1}))=\left\{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N}c_{n}t^{n}:N\in\mathbb{Z},c_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

The quotient space
\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{Z}
is a compact group, which we denote by
\mathcal{T}
.
In analogy with the well-known isomorphism
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, there is an isomorphism between the dual group
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}
and the group
\mathcal{T}
.
In particular, every character on
\mathcal{Z}
is of the form
u\mapsto e(u\alpha)
for some
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}
, where

e\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N}c_{n}t^{n}\right)=\textup{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(c_{-1}).

In order to define a notion of uniform distribution in this context, we need an averaging method to replace averaging the first
N
terms of a sequence and an analogue of subintervals of
[0,1]
.
The averaging method comes through the notion of a Følner sequence.
A
Følner sequence
in
\mathcal{Z}
is a sequence
(F_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
of finite subsets of
\mathcal{Z}
such that

\frac{|F_{N}\cap(F_{N}+u)|}{|F_{N}|}\to 1

as
N\to\infty
.
The topology on
\mathcal{T}
is generated by cylinder sets

C(M;a_{1},\ldots,a_{M})=\left\{\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}c_{n}t^{n}:c_{-1}=a_{1},\ldots,c_{-M}=a_{M}\right\}

for
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and these will play the role of intervals.
Hence, we say that a
\mathcal{Z}
-sequence
(a(u))_{u\in\mathcal{Z}}
is
uniformly distributed
in
\mathcal{T}
with respect to a Følner sequence
(F_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
if for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\frac{\left|\left\{u\in F_{N}:a(u)\in C(M;a_{1},\ldots,a_{M})\right\}\right|}{|F_{N}|}\to q^{-M}.

We say that
(a(u))_{u\in\mathcal{Z}}
is
well-distributed
if it is uniformly distributed with respect to every Følner sequence.

Contrasting with the situation in characteristic zero, there are polynomial sequences in the function field setting that have irrational coefficients but fail to be well-distributed.
For example, if
\alpha=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}t^{-pn}
with
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots)
not eventually periodic, then
\alpha\notin\mathcal{Q}
, but for any
u=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}t^{n}\in\mathcal{Z}
,

\alpha u^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}t^{-pn}\cdot\sum_{m=0}^{N}c_{m}^{p}t^{pm}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{N}\alpha_{n+m}c_{m}^{p}\right)t^{-pn}+\underbrace{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left(\sum_{m=n+1}^{N}\alpha_{m-n}c_{m}^{p}\right)t^{pn}}_{\in\mathcal{Z}}

so
\alpha u^{p}
is never contained in the cylinder set
C(1;1)
.
Similar examples can be constructed for polynomials of the form
\alpha u^{p}+\beta u
, for example, using the fact that both
u
and
u^{p}
are additive homomorphisms; see
[
Ca52
, Equation (6.9)]
.

Lê, Liu, and Wooley conjectured that the above examples are representative of the general situation in the sense that the only obstacles to equidistribution are exponents divisible by
p
(creating obstructions because the Frobenius map is a homomorphism) and “interference” between exponents when one is a multiple of the other by a power of
p
; see
[
LLW23
, Conjecture 1.3]
.

The main result of this paper is the following function field analogue of Theorem
1.1
, confirming
[
LLW23
, Conjecture 1.3]

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is a general equidistribution theorem of Bergelson and Leibman that we reproduce below in Theorem
2.1
using the notation established in the introduction.
Before stating their theorem, we need a few more definitions.

We say that a polynomial
\eta(u)\in\mathcal{R}[u]
is
additive
if
\eta(u+v)=\eta(u)+\eta(v)
for
u,v\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Additive polynomials are precisely the polynomials of the form
\eta(u)=\alpha_{0}u+\alpha_{1}u^{p}+\ldots+\alpha_{s}u^{p^{s}}
for
s\geq 0
and
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s}\in\mathcal{R}
.
We say that a monomial
u^{r}
is
separable
if
p\nmid r
and a polynomial
P(u)\in\mathcal{R}[u]
is
separable
if it is a linear combination of separable monomials.
By dividing out powers of
p
in each term, every polynomial
P(u)\in\mathcal{R}[u]
can be uniquely expressed in the form
P(u)=\alpha_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{i}(u^{r_{i}})
, where
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{d}
are additive and
u^{r_{1}},\ldots,u^{r_{d}}
are distinct and separable.
The equidistribution theorem of Bergelson and Leibman says that the distributional behavior of
P(u)
is controlled by the additive polynomials
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{d}
involved in this decomposition.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
BL16
]
, Theorem 0.3)
.

Any additive polynomial
\mathcal{Z}
-sequence
(\eta(u))_{u\in\mathcal{Z}}
in
\mathcal{T}
is well-distributed in a set of the form
\mathcal{F}(\eta)+\eta(K)
, where
\mathcal{F}(\eta)
is a
\Phi
-subtorus

\mathcal{T}

For any polynomial

where
\alpha_{0}\in\mathcal{T}

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The assumption
p\nmid k
means that

is a separable monomial.
The additional assumption

\displaystyle P(u)=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{k}u^{k}+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}{\eta_{s}\left(u^{s}\right)},

\alpha_{k}

A consequence of Theorem
3.1
is an equidistribution theorem for polynomial sequences in the dual group of the ring of integers of an arbitrary global function field.
In this abstract setting, we use the following notation.
We use
\mathbb{K}
to denote a global function field (i.e., a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
for some
q
) and denote its ring of integers (the integral closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
) by
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}
.
We will write
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}
for the dual group of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}
.
For us, polynomial sequences in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}
will be
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}
-sequences of the form
P(u)=\prod_{j=0}^{d}\alpha_{j}^{u^{j}}
, where
\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}
, and
\alpha^{u}
is the character defined by
\alpha^{u}:v\mapsto\alpha(uv)
.
When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, this agrees with the notion of polynomial sequences dealt with in Theorem
1.2
up to notational changes.

The appropriate notion of irrationality for “coefficients”
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}
is defined as follows: we say that
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}
is
rational
if
\alpha^{u}=1
for some
u\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}
and
irrational
otherwise.

.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a global function field, and let
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}

\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}

\mathcal{K}

Suppose
\alpha_{r}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}

, and let

P(u)=\prod_{r\in\mathcal{K}\cup\{0\}}\alpha_{r}^{u^{r}}.

Suppose that for some
k\in\mathcal{K}
,

•

p\nmid k
,

•

,
p^{v}k\notin\mathcal{K}
, and

•

\alpha_{k}

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbb{K}
is a degree

\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)

Let

That is,

is a basis for
\mathbb{K}

\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}u_{j}b_{j}:u_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\right\}.

For a proof of existence of such a basis, see
[
Ch63

The integral basis

establishes an isomorphism
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}\cong\mathcal{Z}^{d}
, so
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{T}^{d}
.
In particular, for each
r\in\mathcal{K}\cup\{0\}
, there exist
\alpha_{r,1},\ldots,\alpha_{r,d}\in\mathcal{T}
such that

\alpha_{r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}u_{j}b_{j}\right)=e\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{r,j}u_{j}\right)

for
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})\in\mathcal{Z}^{d}
.

For
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, and

, let

such that
\prod_{i=1}^{s}b_{j_{i}}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{s},l}b_{l}
.
Then given
u=\sum_{j=1}^{d}u_{j}b_{j}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}
and

u^{r}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}u_{j}b_{j}\right)^{r}=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}=1}^{d}u_{j_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot u_{j_{r}}b_{j_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot b_{j_{r}}=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r},l=1}^{d}c_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r},l}u_{j_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot u_{j_{r}}b_{l}.

Let
P_{r,l}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}=1}^{d}c_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r},l}u_{j_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot u_{j_{r}}\in\mathcal{Z}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}]
so that

u^{r}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}P_{r,l}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})b_{l}.

Note that
P_{r,l}
is homogeneous of degree
r
.
Moreover, if
p\nmid r
, then the polynomials

are linearly independent (in fact, algebraically independent) over
\mathcal{Q}
by

In order to establish well-distribution of
(P(u))_{u\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}}
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}
, it suffices by Weyl’s criterion to prove that
(\chi(P(u)))_{u\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathbb{K}}}
is well-distributed in the group of
p

\chi:\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}\to S^{1}

Fix a nontrivial character
\chi:\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{K}}\to S^{1}

Then there exists a nonzero

such that for all

\chi(\alpha_{r})=e\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{r,j}v_{j}\right).

We then have

\chi(P(u))=\chi\left(\prod_{r\in\mathcal{K}\cup\{0\}}\alpha_{r}^{u^{r}}\right)=e\left(\sum_{r\in\mathcal{K}\cup\{0\}}\sum_{j,l,m=1}^{d}c_{j,l,m}\alpha_{r,j}P_{r,l}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})v_{m}\right)

\mathcal{T}
.
For
r\in\mathcal{K}\cup\{0\}

Note that
Q_{r,j}
is homogeneous of degree

We claim that
Q_{k,1},\ldots,Q_{k,d}

Indeed, suppose for contradiction that
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})\in\mathcal{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}

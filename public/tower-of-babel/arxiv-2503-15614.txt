Graded Frobenius algebras from tensor algebras of bimodules
Sorin Dăscălescu 1 , Constantin Năstăsescu 2 and Laura Năstăsescu 2

Abstract
. We consider certain quotient algebras of tensor algebras of bimodules M M over a finite-dimensional algebra R R , and we investigate Frobenius type properties of such algebras. Our main interest is in the case where M = R ∗ M=R^{*} , the linear dual of R R . We obtain a large class of Frobenius or symmetric algebras, which are also equipped with a finite grading. 2020 MSC: 16D20, 16D50, 16L60, 16W50. Key words: Frobenius algebra, quasi-Frobenius algebra, symmetric algebra, graded algebra, invertible bimodule, trivial extension.



1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Trivial extensions associated with bimodules over a ring have
occurred as relevant objects in several branches of algebra: in
homological algebra (Hochschild’s study of the cohomology of a ring
with coefficients in a bimodule, Quillen’s cohomological
investigation of the category of rings over a given ring), in
algebraic geometry (the ring of dual numbers and its usefulness to
schemes), in commutative rings (Nagata’s principle of idealization,
the theory of Gorenstein modules), in representation theory (the
trivial extension of an algebra); see
[
4
]
for details. If
M

is a bimodule over a ring
R
, the trivial extension
R\mathrel{\rhd}\joinrel\mathrel{<}M

is the additive group
R\oplus M
with a multiplication defined by

(r_{1},m_{1})\cdot(r_{2},m_{2})=(r_{1}r_{2},r_{1}m_{2}+m_{1}r_{2})
, which makes it a
ring.

A striking example in representation theory, due to Tachikawa, shows
that if
R
is an arbitrary finite-dimensional algebra, and
R^{*}
is
its linear dual, endowed with the usual
R
-bimodule structure, then
the trivial extension
R\mathrel{\rhd}\joinrel\mathrel{<}R^{*}
is a symmetric algebra, see

[
6
, 16.60]
; consequently, any finite dimensional algebra is
isomorphic to a factor algebra of a symmetric algebra. We aim to
uncover what is behind this example.

A more general construction is the semitrivial extension

R\mathrel{\rhd}\joinrel\mathrel{<}_{\varphi}M
associated with an
R
-bimodule
M
and a
morphism of bimodules
\varphi:M\otimes_{R}M\rightarrow R
which is associative,
i.e., it satisfies
\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}m_{2})m_{3}=m_{1}\varphi(m_{2}\otimes_{R}m_{3})

for any
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
. The multiplication is defined in this case by

(r_{1},m_{1})\cdot(r_{2},m_{2})=(r_{1}r_{2}+\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}m_{2}),r_{1}m_{2}+m_{1}r_{2})
, see
[
9
]
. It was proved in
[
3
]

that if
R
is a finite-dimensional algebra, then any semitrivial
extension
R\mathrel{\rhd}\joinrel\mathrel{<}_{\varphi}R^{*}
is a symmetric algebra.

In this paper we develop a construction that extends widely the
semitrivial extension. Let
M
be a bimodule over an algebra
R
.
For any
i\geq 2
, denote by
M^{\otimes_{R}\,i}
the
i
-fold tensor
product
M\otimes_{R}M\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}M
; also denote
M^{\otimes_{R}\,0}=R

and
M^{\otimes_{R}\,1}=M
. The tensor algebra of the bimodule
M
is

T_{R}(M)=\oplus_{i\geq 0}M^{\otimes_{R}\,i}
, with multiplication induced
by tensor concatenation. Let
n\geq 2
be an integer, and let

\varphi:M^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R
be a morphism of
R
-bimodules which is
associative, in the sense that
\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n})m_{n+1}=m_{1}\varphi(m_{2}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n+1})
for any

m_{1},\ldots,m_{n+1}\in M
. We consider the factor algebra

A(R,M,\varphi)
of
T_{R}(M)
by the two-sided ideal generated by all
elements of the form

m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}-\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n})
. We show
that
A(R,M,\varphi)
can be identified with
R\oplus M\oplus\ldots\oplus M^{\otimes_{R}\,(n-1)}
, with multiplication induced
by tensor concatenation for tensor monomials whose sum of length is
less than
n
, respectively by tensor concatenation followed by
applying
\varphi
to a (in fact to any) successive tensor sequence
of length
n
. It follows that
A(R,M,\varphi)
has a

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-graded algebra structure.

We investigate Frobenius type properties of algebras obtained by
this construction. Frobenius algebras and their variants
(quasi-Frobenius algebras, symmetric algebras) form a very
interesting class of algebras, occurring in algebra, geometry and
topology. A finite-dimensional algebra
R
is called quasi-Frobenius
if
R
is an injective left
R
-module, and is called Frobenius if

R^{*}
and
R
are isomorphic as left
R
-modules; if the isomorphism
is as bimodules, then
R
is called symmetric. Graded versions of
these concepts exist, in particular an algebra
R
graded by a group

G
is called
\sigma
-graded Frobenius, where
\sigma\in G
, if

R^{*}
is isomorphic as a graded left
R
-module with the shift

R(\sigma)
of
R
. Such objects occur in non-commutative geometry,
for example Koszul duals of certain connected Noetherian graded
algebras
[
11
]
, and certain algebras constructed from twisted
superpotentials, related to Calabi-Yau algebras
[
5
]
.

All algebras, spaces and linear duals will be over a field
K
. We prove the following.

Proposition A.

Let
R
be a finite-dimensional algebra,

M
a finite-dimensional
R
-bimodule, and let
\varphi:M^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R
be an associative morphism of

R
-bimodules, where
n\geq 2
. The following assertions hold.

(i)
If
M
is an invertible
R
-bimodule, then

A(R,M,\varphi)
is
\widehat{n-1}
-faithful. If moreover,

M^{\otimes_{R}\,(n-1)}\simeq R^{*}
as left
R
-modules, then

A(R,M,\varphi)
is
\widehat{n-1}
-graded
Frobenius, in particular it is Frobenius.

(ii)
If
M
is invertible, then

A(R,M,\varphi)
is quasi-Frobenius if and only if
R
is quasi-Frobenius.

(iii)
If
\varphi
is an isomorphism, then the

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-graded
algebra
A(R,M,\varphi)
is strongly graded.

(iv)
If
\varphi
is an isomorphism and there exists
0\leq i\leq n-1
such that
M^{\otimes_{R}\,i}\simeq R^{*}
as left
R
-modules,
then
A(R,M,\varphi)
is
\hat{i}
-graded Frobenius, in
particular it is Frobenius.

We are interested in the special case where
M=R^{*}
. If
n\geq 2

and
\varphi:(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R
is the zero morphism, we denote

A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
by
A(R,n)
; the subclass consisting of such
algebras is of particular interest. An important fact for our
further considerations is that a finite-dimensional algebra
R
is
quasi-Frobenius if and only if
R^{*}
is an invertible
R
-bimodule.
In the case where
R
is a Frobenius algebra and
n\geq 2
, we
determine all bimodule morphisms
\varphi:R^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R
that
are associative; wee see that some bimodule morphisms may not be
associative. However, even when
R
is only quasi-Frobenius, if the
order of the class of
R^{*}
in the Picard
group of
R
is a divisor of
n
, which means that
(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\simeq R
as
R
-bimodules, we prove the following.

Theorem B.

Let
R
be a finite-dimensional algebra such
that
(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}n}\simeq R
as
R
-bimodules for some
n\geq 2

(note that
R
is necessarily quasi-Frobenius).
Then any morphism of
R
-bimodules
\varphi:(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}n}\rightarrow R
is associative.

We first prove Theorem B in the case where
R
is Frobenius, by
investigating the Nakayama automorphism of
R
. In the case where

R
is quasi-Frobenius, we consider a basic algebra
S
of
R
,
which is Frobenius and Morita equivalent to
R
. Then there is a
monoidal equivalence between the categories of bimodules over
R

and
S
, which we use to transfer the property to be proved in
Theorem B from
S
to
R
.

In the situation where
\varphi:(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}n}\rightarrow R
is an
isomorphism of bimodules, thus associative by Theorem B, we show
that the strongly graded algebra
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
enjoys good Frobenius properties.

Theorem C.

Let
R
be a finite-dimensional algebra such
that there is an isomorphism of
R
-bimodules

\varphi:(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R
for some
n\geq 2
. Then

(i)

A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
is
\hat{1}
-graded Frobenius, and it
is a
symmetric algebra.

(ii)
If moreover,
R
is Frobenius, then
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
is

\hat{i}
-graded Frobenius for any
0\leq i<n
.

We also investigate Frobenius properties of
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
in
the case where
\varphi
is not necessarily an isomorphism, provided that some additional properties are satisfied.

Theorem D.

Let
R
be a finite-dimensional algebra, and
let
\varphi:(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R
be an associative morphism of
bimodules, where
n\geq 2
. The following hold.

(i)
If

(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,(n-2)}\simeq R
as bimodules, then

A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
is a symmetric algebra.

(ii)
If
R
is a Frobenius algebra, then
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
is

\widehat{n-1}
-graded Frobenius, in particular it is a Frobenius
algebra. Moreover, if
\lambda
is a Frobenius form on
R
, with
associated Nakayama automorphism
\nu
, then the linear map

\Lambda:A(R,R^{*},\varphi)\rightarrow K
such that

\Lambda((r_{1}\rightharpoonup\lambda)\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}(r_{n-1}\rightharpoonup\lambda))=\lambda(r_{1}\nu(r_{2})\cdots\nu^{n-2}(r_{n-1}))

for any

r_{1},\ldots,r_{n-1}\in R
, and
\Lambda
vanishes on any homogeneous
component of degree
\neq\widehat{n-1}
of
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
, is a
Frobenius form on
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
, and the Nakayama automorphism

\mathcal{N}
of
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
associated with
\Lambda
is
given by

\mathcal{N}((r_{1}\rightharpoonup\lambda)\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}(r_{i}\rightharpoonup\lambda))=(\nu^{2-n}(r_{1})\rightharpoonup\lambda)\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}(\nu^{2-n}(r_{i})\rightharpoonup\lambda)

for any

1\leq i\leq n-1
, and any
r_{1},\ldots,r_{i}\in R
, and

\mathcal{N}(r)=\nu^{2-n}(r)
for any
r\in R
.

On the other hand, If
R
is
Frobenius, we use Theorem D (ii) for obtaining a criterion for

A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
to be a symmetric algebra. As a consequence, we
show the following.

Corollary E.

Let
R
be a Frobenius algebra,
n\geq 2
,
and let
\varphi:(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R
be an associative morphism
of bimodules. Then

(i)
If
R
is a local algebra, then
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
is a
symmetric algebra if and only if either
(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,(n-2)}\simeq R
as
R
-bimodules, or
\varphi
is an isomorphism.

(ii)

A(R,n)
is a symmetric algebra
if and only if
(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,(n-2)}\simeq R
as
R
-bimodules.

We give an example to show that the condition that
R
is local
cannot be omitted in Corollary E (i).

If
R
is quasi-Frobenius, then an algebra of type

A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
is quasi-Frobenius by Proposition A (ii), however
we show that it might not be Frobenius. Let
R
be a finite
dimensional quasi-Frobenius algebra and
e_{1},\ldots,e_{q}
be
idempotents of
R
such that
Re_{1},\ldots,Re_{q}
is a system of
representatives for the isomorphism types of principal
indecomposable
R
-modules; let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{q}
be their
multiplicities in
R
. Then there is a permutation
\pi
of

1,\ldots,q
, called the Nakayama permutation, such that
{\rm top}(Re_{i})\simeq{\rm soc}(Re_{\pi(i)})
for any
1\leq i\leq q
,
where for an
R
-module
M
we denote by
{\rm soc}(M)
its socle
and by
{\rm top}(M)=M/J(R)M
. With these notations, we prove the following.

Theorem F.

Let
R
be quasi-Frobenius and
n\geq 2
.
Then
A(R,n)
is Frobenius if and only if
m_{i}=m_{\pi^{n-2}(i)}
for any
1\leq i\leq q
.

We mentioned that
A(R,2)
, the trivial extension of
R^{*}
by
R
,
is a symmetric algebra for any finite-dimensional algebra
R
. We
give examples showing that if
n\geq 3
,
A(R,n)
may be not even
quasi-Frobenius (for certain
R
which is not quasi-Frobenius). On
the other hand, we give examples of
R
which is not
quasi-Frobenius, while
A(n,R)
is symmetric. Thus the case
n=2
is
an exceptional situation.

[

]

]
.

2.
Graded Frobenius algebras and graded algebras from
bimodules

(Quasi-)Frobenius algebras and symmetric algebras.

Let
R
be a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra. Denote by
\rightharpoonup
and

\leftharpoonup
the usual left and right actions of
R
on the dual space

R^{*}
, which make
R^{*}
an
R
-bimodule; thus
(r\rightharpoonup r^{*})(s)=r^{*}(sr)
and
(r^{*}\leftharpoonup r)(s)=r^{*}(rs)
for any
r^{*}\in R^{*}

and
r,s\in R
.
R
is called Frobenius if
R^{*}
is isomorphic to

R
as a left
R
-module (or equivalently, as a right
R
-module).
If
R^{*}
is isomorphic to
R
as an
R
-bimodule, then
R
is called
symmetric.

A basic fact is that
R
is Frobenius if and only if there exists

\lambda\in R^{*}
such that
R\rightharpoonup\lambda=R^{*}
; such a
\lambda
is
called a Frobenius form on
R
, and it also satisfies
\lambda\leftharpoonup R=R^{*}
. The Nakayama automorphism
\nu:R\rightarrow R
associated with

\lambda
is the unique map such that
\lambda\leftharpoonup r=\nu(r)\rightharpoonup\lambda
for any
r\in R
;
\nu
is an algebra automorphism, and

R^{*}
is isomorphic as an
R
-bimodule to
{}_{1}R_{\nu}
, where for an

R
-bimodule
M
and two automorphisms
\alpha
and
\beta
of
R
,
we denote by
{}_{\alpha}M_{\beta}
the
R
-bimodule with underlying
space
M
, left action
r*m=\alpha(r)m
, and right action

m*r=m\beta(r)
.

An algebra
R
is symmetric if and only if there is a Frobenius form

\lambda
such that
\lambda(rs)=\lambda(sr)
for any
r,s\in R
;
in this situation, the associated Nakayama automorphism is inner.

A finite-dimensional algebra
R
is called quasi-Frobenius if it is
self-injective. By
[
3
, Corollary 2.3]
, this is equivalent to

R^{*}
being an invertible
R
-bimodule. We recall that an

R
-bimodule is called invertible
if there is a bimodule
N
such that
M\otimes_{R}N\simeq N\otimes_{R}M\simeq R
as
R
-bimodules.

Graded algebras and
\sigma
-graded Frobenius
conditions.

Let
G
be a group with identity element
e
. A
G
-graded algebra
is an algebra
A
with a linear decomposition
A=\oplus_{g\in G}A_{g}
, such that
A_{g}A_{h}\subset A_{gh}
for any
g,h\in G
. If

\sigma\in G
,
A
is called
\sigma
-faithful if for any
g\in G

and any
a_{g}\in A_{g}
such that
A_{\sigma g^{-1}}a_{g}=0
, we have
that
a_{g}=0
.

A
G
-graded algebra
A
is strongly graded if
A_{g}A_{h}=A_{gh}
for
any
g,h\in G
, or equivalently, if
1\in A_{g}A_{g^{-1}}
for any

g\in G
. A strongly graded algebra is
\sigma
-faithful for any

\sigma\in G
.

If
A
is a finite-dimensional
G
-graded algebra, then
A^{*}
is a
graded
A
-bimodule, with the grading given by

(A^{*})_{g}=\{a^{*}\in A^{*}\;|\;a^{*}(A_{h})=0\mbox{ for any }h\neq g^{-1}\}.

If
\sigma\in G
, denote by
A(\sigma)
the graded left

A
-module which is just
A
as a left
A
-module, and has a grading
shifted by
\sigma
, i.e.,
A(\sigma)_{g}=A_{g\sigma}
for any
g\in G
. Then
A
is called
\sigma
-graded Frobenius if
A^{*}\simeq A(\sigma)
as graded left
A
-modules. Clearly, if
A
is

\sigma
-graded Frobenius, then
A
is a Frobenius algebra. We will
need the following characterization of
\sigma
-graded Frobenius
algebras.

Proposition 2.1
.

A finite-dimensional graded algebra
A
is
\sigma
-graded Frobenius
if and only if it has a Frobenius form
\lambda
of degree

\sigma^{-1}
in
A^{*}
.

Proof.

If
\varphi:A(\sigma)\rightarrow A^{*}
is an isomorphism of graded left

A
-modules, then
\lambda=\varphi(1)
is a Frobenius form of degree

\sigma^{-1}
. Conversely, if
\lambda
is such a form, then the map

\varphi:A(\sigma)\rightarrow A^{*}
,
\varphi(r)=r\rightharpoonup\lambda
, is an
isomorphism of graded left
A
-modules.
∎

Note that if
A
is
\sigma
-graded Frobenius and
\sigma
lies in
the centre of
G
, then the associated Nakayama automorphism
\nu

preserves degree.

We recall from
[
1
, Theorem 4.3]
that a
finite-dimensional
G
-graded algebra
A
is
\sigma
-graded
Frobenius if and only if it is
\sigma
-faithful and
A_{\sigma}\simeq A_{e}^{*}
as left
A_{e}
-modules. For such an
A
, a Frobenius form

\lambda\in A^{*}
of degree
\sigma^{-1}
can be obtained by taking
an isomorphism
\theta:A_{\sigma}\rightarrow A_{e}^{*}
of left
A_{e}
-modules,
and defining
\lambda(x)=\theta(x)(1)
for any
x\in A_{\sigma}
, and
\lambda(x)=0
for any homogeneous
x
of degree
\neq\sigma
.

Graded algebras from bimodules

Let
R
be an algebra and let
M
be an
R
-bimodule. The tensor
algebra of the bimodule
M
is the space
T_{R}(M)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}M^{\otimes_{R}\,n}
, with multiplication (which we occasionally denote
by
\cdot
) induced by tensor concatenation and the left and right
actions of
R
on
M
; this is a positively graded algebra with
homogeneous component of degree
n
being
T_{R}(M)_{n}=M^{\otimes_{R}\,n}

for any
n\geq 0
.

If
n\geq 2
, a bimodule morphism
\varphi:M^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R
is
called associative if

(1)

\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n})m_{n+1}=m_{1}\varphi(m_{2}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n+1})

for any
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n+1}\in M
. If
\varphi
is associative, it can be easily
proved by induction on
p
that for any
r\geq n+1
, any
1\leq p\leq r-n
and any
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\in M
we have

(2)

\displaystyle m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{p-1}\otimes_{R}\varphi(m_{p}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{p+n-1})m_{p+n}\otimes_{R}m_{p+n+1}\otimes_{R}\ldots m_{r}=

\displaystyle\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n})m_{n+1}\otimes m_{n+2}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{r}.

If
\varphi:M^{\otimes_{R}n}\rightarrow R
is associative, then we have a Morita
context
(R,R,M,M^{\otimes_{R}(n-1)},[,],(,))
connecting the rings
R

and
R
, where both Morita maps
[,]
and
(,)
are induced by

\varphi
in the obvious way. As a consequence, if
\varphi
is
surjective, then it is an isomorphism of
R
-bimodules, in
particular
M
is an invertible bimodule.

Now let
M
be an
R
-bimodule, and let
\varphi:M^{\otimes_{R}\,n}\rightarrow R

be an associative morphism of bimodules, where
n\geq 2
. Let
I
be
the two-sided ideal of
T_{R}(M)
generated by all elements of the
form
m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}-\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n})
, with

m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in M
. We consider the factor algebra
T_{R}(M)/I
,
which we denote by
A(R,M,\varphi)
. In order to describe this
factor algebra, we need the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

(i)

I
is the left ideal generated by all elements of the
form
m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}-\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n})
, with

m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in M
.

(ii)

I\cap(T_{R}(M)_{0}+T_{R}(M)_{1}+\ldots+T_{R}(M)_{n-1})=0
.

(iii)
For any
u\in T_{R}(M)
there exists
u^{\prime}\in I
such that

u-u^{\prime}\in T_{R}(M)_{0}+T_{R}(M)_{1}+\ldots+T_{R}(M)_{n-1}
.

Proof.

(i) Let
z=m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}-\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n})

and
m\in M
. The statement follows from

\displaystyle z\cdot m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}\otimes_{R}m-\varphi(m_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n})m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{1}\cdot(m_{2}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}\otimes_{R}m)-m_{1}\varphi(m_{2}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}\otimes_{R}m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{1}\cdot(m_{2}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}\otimes_{R}m-\varphi(m_{2}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{n}\otimes_{R}m))

and the fact that

T_{R}(M)
is generated by
T_{R}(M)_{1}=M
.

(ii) Let

y=\sum_{j\in J}z_{j}\cdot(m_{j1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{jn}-\varphi(m_{j1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}m_{jn}))\in T_{R}(M)_{0}+T_{R}(M)_{1}+\ldots+T_{R}(M)_{n-1},

where the
m_{ji}
’s lie in
M
and
(z_{j})_{j\in J}
is a family of elements of
T_{R}(M)
; we may assume that each

z_{j}
is a homogeneous element. For any
d
, let
J_{d}
be the set of
all
j
such that
z_{j}
has degree

, and denote

3.
Associative bimodule morphisms

If
R
is an algebra and
\alpha,\beta
are algebra automorphisms of

R
, then there is a bimodule isomorphism
F:{{}_{1}R_{\alpha}}\otimes{{}_{1}R_{\beta}}\rightarrow{{}_{1}R_{\alpha\beta}}
given by

F(r\otimes_{R}s)=r\alpha(s)
; its inverse is

.
Extending this, if

and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}
are
automorphisms of

, there is an isomorphism of bimodules

F:{{}_{1}R_{\alpha_{1}}}\otimes_{R}{\;{}_{1}R_{\alpha_{2}}}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}{\;{}_{1}R_{\alpha_{p}}}\rightarrow{{}_{1}R_{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{p}}},

F(r_{1}\otimes_{R}r_{2}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}r_{p})=r_{1}\alpha_{1}(r_{2})\cdots(\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{p-1})(r_{p}),

whose inverse is given by

.

In the case where
R
is a Frobenius algebra with a Frobenius form

\lambda
and associated Nakayama automorphism
\nu
, since there is
an
R
-bimodule isomorphism
{{}_{1}R_{\nu}}\rightarrow R^{*}
which takes

to

, we obtain a bimodule isomorphism

\theta:(R^{*})^{\otimes_{R}\,p}\rightarrow{{}_{1}R_{\nu^{p}}},\theta((r_{1}\rightharpoonup\lambda)\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}(r_{p}\rightharpoonup\lambda))=r_{1}\nu(r_{2})\cdots\nu^{p-1}(r_{p}).

On the other hand, if
\alpha
is an automorphism of
R
, then a
bimodule morphism
\gamma:{{}_{1}R_{\alpha}}\rightarrow R
is of the form

\gamma(r)=rc
, where
c
is an element of
R
such that

\alpha(r)c=cr
for any
r\in R
. Combining with the considerations
above, if
R
is Frobenius and

, we obtain that a bimodule

is of the form

\varphi((r_{1}\rightharpoonup\lambda)\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}(r_{p}\rightharpoonup\lambda))=r_{1}\nu(r_{2})\cdots\nu^{p-1}(r_{p})c
, where

such

\nu^{p}(r)c=cr

\varphi((r_{1}\rightharpoonup\lambda)\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}(r_{p}\rightharpoonup\lambda))\rightharpoonup(r_{p+1}\rightharpoonup\lambda)=(r_{1}\nu(r_{2})\cdots\nu^{p-1}(r_{p})cr_{p+1})\rightharpoonup\lambda,

\displaystyle(r_{1}\rightharpoonup\lambda)\leftharpoonup\varphi((r_{2}\rightharpoonup\lambda)\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}(r_{p+1}\rightharpoonup\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r_{1}\rightharpoonup\lambda)\leftharpoonup(r_{2}\nu(r_{3})\cdots\nu^{p-1}(r_{p+1})c)

\displaystyle(r_{1}\nu(r_{2}\nu(r_{3})\cdots\nu^{p-1}(r_{p+1})c))\rightharpoonup\lambda

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
R
be a Frobenius algebra with a Frobenius form
\lambda
and
associated Nakayama automorphism
\nu
,
n\geq 2
an integer, and

c\in R
such that
\nu(c)=c
and
\nu^{n}(r)c=cr
for any

\rm{(I)}

\rm{(II)}

\rm{(III)}
The following equations are satisfied

\begin{array}[]{lllllllllll}r_{0}s_{0}&+&r_{1}\nu(s_{n-1})c&+&r_{2}\nu^{2}(s_{n-2})c&+&\ldots&+&r_{n-1}\nu^{n-1}(s_{1})c&=1\\

\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
r_{0}s_{n-2}&+&r_{1}\nu(s_{n-3})&+&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots+r_{n-2}\nu^{n-2}(s_{0})&+&r_{n-1}\nu^{n-1}(s_{n-1})c&=0\\
r_{0}s_{n-1}&+&r_{1}\nu(s_{n-2})&+&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&+&r_{n-1}\nu^{n-1}(s_{0})&=0\end{array}

Proof.

Denote
A(R,R^{*},\varphi)
by
A
. It is
a Frobenius algebra, with a Frobenius form
\Lambda
, and associated Nakayama
automorphism
\mathcal{N}
. Then

is symmetric if and only if

\mathcal{N}
is an inner automorphism, i.e., there exists an

\mathcal{N}(a)=U^{-1}aU
, or

, for any
a\in A
. Since

\mathcal{N}
preserves the degree, this is further equivalent to
the fact that each homogeneous component

of

, for any
a\in A
. As in

4.1
, for any

. We seek

such that

for any

a\in A
. For

of degree
\hat{i}

(
i

(4)

Some examples

We have seen that

is symmetric for any

Example 5.1
.

Let

\displaystyle F_{rt}F_{tu}=F_{ru}

\displaystyle E_{ij}X_{jr}=X_{ir},

\displaystyle X_{ir}F_{rt}=X_{it}

\displaystyle Y_{ri}E_{ij}=Y_{rj}

is the transposition

By Proposition A (ii),

is quasi-Frobenius. As

is

, and it is

[

Before going to the next example, we note that by the tensor-Hom
adjunction, for any finite-dimensional

-modules

and

-bimodules

Example 5.2
.

Let
R=\small{\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}K&K\\

Example 5.5
.

Let
H
be a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra with antipode
S
.
Let
[S^{2}]
be the class of
S^{2}
in
{\rm Out}(H)={\rm Aut}(H)/{\rm Inn}(H)
, the group of outer automorphisms of the algebra
H
. The
order
o([S^{2}])
of
[S^{2}]
in this group is the least positive
integer
\ell
such that
S^{2\ell}
is inner. Let also

\mathcal{G}
be the distinguished grouplike element in
H^{*}
, and
denote by
o(\mathcal{G})
its order in the group of grouplike
elements of
H^{*}
. It was proved in
[
3
, Theorem 2.9]
that
the order of the class of the
H
-bimodule
H^{*}
in the Picard group
of
H
is the least common multiple of
o([S^{2}])
and

o(\mathcal{G})
.

For any integer
n\geq 2
,
A(H,n)
is a Frobenius algebra by
Theorem D (ii). By Corollary E, it is a symmetric algebra if and
only if both
o([S^{2}])
and
o(\mathcal{G})
divide
n-2
.

Upper Bounds for Logarithmic Gromov–Witten Invariants of Projective Space
Dan Simms

Abstract
. We provide an upper bound for the genus zero logarithmic Gromov–Witten invariants of projective space relative to its toric boundary. The upper bound is polynomial in the contact orders, with degree depending on the number of marked points. The result hinges on positivity of intersections for projective spaces.

1.
Introduction

Consider the space
\mathcal{M}_{g,\upalpha}(X,D)
of maps

(C,p_{1}+\cdots+p_{n})\to(X,D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k})

from a smooth genus
g
curve
C
to a smooth projective variety

X
with simple normal crossings divisor
D=D_{1}+\cdots+D_{k},
and fixed tangency orders
\upalpha_{ij}
between the
marked point
p_{i}
and the component
D_{j}
of
D
.
Logarithmic Gromov–Witten theory, developed in
[
9
,
4
,
1
]
,
enumerates these maps by compactifying

\mathcal{M}_{g,\upalpha}(X,D)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\upalpha}(X,D)

to the space of logarithmic stable maps to
X
, and
performing intersections against a virtual fundamental class.
Concretely we use the evaluation maps,

\ev_{i}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\upalpha}(X,D)\to X,

to define

\langle\upgamma_{1},\dots,\upgamma_{n}\rangle^{X|D}_{g,\upalpha}\coloneq\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,\upalpha}(X,D)]^{\operatorname{vir}}}\prod_{i}\ev_{i}^{*}(\upgamma_{i}),

for
\upgamma_{i}\in H^{*}(X).

In this article, we provide an upper bound for the genus 0
primary invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
relative its toric boundary
by alternatively compactifying

\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}.

Here one makes use of an isomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})\cong\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times T,
where
T\subset\mathbb{P}^{k}
is the dense torus.
The invariants can be expressed as a transverse intersection
in the interior, while the extra contributions from the
boundary contribute positively because the compactification
is a product of projective spaces. What remains is to perform
a calculation on this product, whose intersection theory is simple.

1.1.
Main Result

Fix a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{k}
, some number
n
of marked points, and
natural numbers
\upalpha_{ij}
representing the contact order between
the
i^{th}
marked point and the
j^{th}
component of the toric
boundary of
\mathbb{P}^{k},
and suppose further that some marked point is
mapped into the torus of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
. That is, there exists
i
such that
all
\upalpha_{ij}=0.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each marking
i
, let

\upalpha^{\max}_{i}=\max_{j}\upalpha_{ij}.
Then

\langle d_{1}H^{\upnu_{1}},\dots,d_{n}H^{\upnu_{n}}\rangle^{\mathbb{P}^{k}|\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}}_{0,\upalpha}\leq d_{1}\cdots d_{n}\binom{n-3+k-\upnu}{n-3}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\upalpha^{\max}_{i}\right)^{n-3},

where
\upnu
is the maximal codimension of an insertion at a
marked point with no tangency to the boundary. In particular,
if one imposes a point constraint at such a point this binomial
coefficient is 1.

We illustrate the types of upper bounds one obtains with a
couple of concrete examples.

•

Maximal Contact.
Suppose we count curves which are
maximally tangent to the toric boundary of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
, passing through
2 general points. That is, we count
curves whose intersection with the
i^{th}
coordinate hyperplane

H_{i}
consists of a single point, necessarily tangent to order

d
by Bézout’s theorem. Theorem
1.1
gives an upper bound
for the number of such curves as

\left((k+1)d\right)^{k}.

•

Characteristic Numbers.
Let
N_{d}
denote
the number of rational degree
d
plane curves passing
through
3d-1
general points. These numbers have a storied
history, and can be computed recursively
[
11
]
.
It is possible to express
N_{d}
as a logarithmic
Gromov–Witten invariant as follows. Let
\upalpha=(\upalpha_{ij})_{ij}

denote the following tangencies.

\,H_{1}\,

\,H_{2}\,

\,H_{3}\,

x_{1},\dots,x_{d}

1

0

0

y_{1},\dots,y_{d}

0

1

0

z_{1},\dots,z_{d}

0

0

1

p_{1},\dots,p_{3d-1}

0

0

0

The
p_{i}
are where the point constraints are imposed. Since they
are generic points they can be chosen away from the boundary,
hence the contact order is zero.
Meanwhile,
\{x_{i},y_{i},z_{i}\}
represent the intersection points
between a generic curve as above with the toric boundary, with
generic tangency. The choice of labelling of these
points introduces a factor of proportionality between the
logarithmic Gromov–Witten invariants and the classical count:

\langle 1,\dots,1,H^{2},\dots,H^{2}\rangle^{\mathbb{P}^{k}|\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}}_{0,\upalpha}=(d!)^{3}N_{d}.

From this, Theorem
1.1
yields

N_{d}\leq\frac{(3d)^{6d-4}}{(d!)^{3}}.

1.2.
Proof Outline

The main point of the proof is that
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
is isomorphic to
an open inside a product of projective spaces, where all
contributions to intersection products are positive. The following
example carries all the information one needs to concretely
understand the interior
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}).

Example 1.2
.

Suppose
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a linear embedding
meeting
\partial\mathbb{P}^{2}
transversely at three points. Up to
automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we can take the three points to be
[0:1],[1:1],[1:0].
Then necessarily
f
is of the form

[X:Y]\mapsto[\uplambda_{0}X:\uplambda_{1}(X-Y):\uplambda_{2}Y],

with
\uplambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

The space of all such
f
is therefore abstractly isomorphic to

\mathcal{M}_{0,3}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}.
We will see later that we can make
this isomorphism explicit if there is a marked point mapping into the
torus of
\mathbb{P}^{2}.

Note that both
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
and
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{k}
are each isomorphic to open
subsets of a projective space,
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
and
\mathbb{P}^{k}
respectively.

•

Step 1.
Embed

\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
rather than

\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}).

Since we work in genus 0, there are no virtual considerations necessary.
The invariants are a transverse intersection in
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
. That is,
we can express the invariant as
\deg\prod_{i}A_{i}
for some transverse
cycles
A_{i}
on
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}).
One can then take closures
\overline{A_{i}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}.
The product
\prod_{i}\overline{A_{i}}

decomposes as the transverse intersection in
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
, whose degree
is the invariant as mentioned, as well as extra components in the
boundary.

•

Step 2.
Positivity of projective space ensures the extra contributions from the boundary cannot
be negative. Therefore one obtains

\langle\upgamma_{1},\dots,\upgamma_{n}\rangle_{0,\upalpha}^{\mathbb{P}^{k}|\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}}\leq\deg\prod_{i}\overline{A_{i}}.

•

Step 3.
Perform a calculation on projective space to
determine an upper bound for the degree of
\prod_{i}\overline{A}_{i}.

Remark 1.3
.

Projective space appears multiple times in this plan, for two
different reasons. The choice to compactify
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
with a
product of projective spaces is to leverage the positivity of its
tangent bundle. On the other hand, we consider maps to projective
space because their simple cohomology greatly restricts the possible
insertions, thereby making explicit calculations feasible.

Remark 1.4
.

The idea to use positive compactifications to prove bounds on
interesting algebro-geometric quantities also appears in
contemporaneous work
[
6
,
3
]
, the former
being the main inspiration behind this paper.

1.3.
Future Work

The methods in this paper apply directly to
any situation where one performs intersections on rational moduli spaces, or more generally on moduli spaces birational to a variety
with globally generated tangent bundle. In particular, this method
will certainly give upper bounds for genus zero logarithmic
Gromov–Witten invariants of all smooth toric varieties. One expects
that these bounds can be made explicit in nice cases, such as
products of projective spaces.

2.
Background

2.1.
Logarithmic Gromov–Witten Theory of Toric Varieties

Throughout this section let
(X,D)
be a smooth projective toric pair, with torus
T\subset X
.
Let
k=\dim X.
As usual, we write

N=\Hom(\mathbb{G}_{\operatorname{m}},T)
for the lattice in which the fan
\Upsigma
associated to
X
lives, and

M=\Hom(T,\mathbb{G}_{\operatorname{m}}).

Definition 2.1
.

A choice of
numerical data

\uplambda
is a tuple
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in N^{n}
satisfying

\sum_{i}v_{i}=0.

This constraint is often called the
balancing condition
.

Much information can be extracted from a choice of numerical data

\uplambda.

•

Number of marked points.
Take
n=\lvert\uplambda\rvert.

•

Tangency orders.
Since
X
is smooth and proper, we can write each
v\in N
uniquely as

v=\sum_{v_{\uprho}\in\Upsigma(1)}\upalpha_{\uprho}v_{\uprho}

with
\upalpha_{\uprho}\in\mathbb{N}
and
v_{\rho}
primitive.
Note that
\upalpha_{\uprho}\neq 0
if and only if
\uprho
is a
generator for the minimal cone of
\Upsigma
containing
v.

Each
v_{\rho}
corresponds to a component of the toric boundary

D
. Decomposing
v_{i}
in this way produces a tuple
\upalpha_{ij},

which we take to be tangency orders between the
i^{th}
marking and

D_{j}
.

•

Curve class.
Denote by
\upbeta
the curve class of a map

[f:C\to X].
An operational Chow class on a toric variety is determined
by its pairings
\upbeta\cdot D_{j}
with the components of the boundary via the theory of Minkowski weights

[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
. The balancing condition ensures
that there is a curve class
\upbeta
with
\upbeta\cdot D_{j}=\sum_{i}\upalpha_{ij}
for all

j
. This statement is simply the exactness of

0\to A_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\Upsigma(1)}\to N\to 0,

dual to the familiar

0\to M\to\mathbb{Z}^{\Upsigma(1)}\to\mathrm{Cl}(X)\to 0.

Remark 2.2
.

One advantage of working with tangencies as elements of
N
is the
independence of the choice of toric variety compactifying the torus.
This is beneficial, for example, if one obtains
X^{\prime}
from
X
by
subdividing its fan. Then a choice of numerical data for
X
as we
define it immediately lifts along the natural map
X^{\prime}\to X
to give
valid numerical data for a map to
X^{\prime}
. This is much easier than
choosing a lift of a curve class and checking compatibilities.

The only other invariant of stable maps constant in flat families is the
genus. We record this separately.

Definition 2.3
.

Denote by

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(X,D)

the space of genus zero logarithmic stable
maps to
(X,D)
with tangency orders
\upalpha

[
9
,
4
,
1
]
. This carries a
natural logarithmic structure; denote by

\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(X,D)

the locus where the log structure is trivial. This
parametrises the maps from smooth curves, whose image
meets
D
precisely at the points
p_{i}
with the prescribed
tangencies.

Proposition 2.4
.

(
[
13
, Proposition 3.3.3]
)

Fix numerical data
\upalpha=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}),
with
v_{i}=0
.
Then the map

\mathrm{fgt}\times\ev_{i}:\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(X,D)\to\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times T

is an isomorphism, where

\mathrm{fgt}:\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(X,D)\to\mathcal{M}_{0,n}

is the forgetful map sending a map to its, necessarily
smooth, marked source curve.

The proof follows exactly as Example
1.2
, appealing in
general to the homogeneous coordinate ring on a smooth toric variety

[
5
]
.
As noted in
[
13
]
, there is an abstract isomorphism
even without the assumption that some
v_{i}=0,
but we rely on an
explicit construction of the isomorphism.

While virtual machinery is in general
necessary to compute invariants, the case of genus zero maps to a toric
variety is simpler.

Theorem 2.5
.

The space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
is irreducible of the expected
dimension. Moreover,

[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})]^{\operatorname{vir}}=[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})].

Proof.

The irreducibility of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
is
[
13
, Proposition 3.3.5]
.
A relative perfect obstruction theory for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})\to\mathfrak{M},
where
\mathfrak{M}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\alpha}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{P}^{k}|\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}})
is the space of
maps to the Artin fan of
\mathbb{P}^{k},
is
constructed in
[
2
]
as follows.
Denote by
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
the universal curve,
and
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{P}^{k}
the universal map. Then

E^{\bullet}=\mathrm{R}\pi_{*}f^{*}T^{\log}_{\mathbb{P}^{k}|\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}}\to\mathbb{L}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})/\mathfrak{M}}

is a relative perfect obstruction theory.
But
T^{\log}_{\mathbb{P}^{k}|\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{k}}^{\oplus k},
so in genus zero there are no obstructions. Therefore

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
is smooth over
\mathfrak{M},
and

[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})]^{\text{\rm vir}}=[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})].

∎

Given cohomology classes
\upgamma_{1},\dots,\upgamma_{n}\in H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{k}),
one defines logarithmic Gromov–Witten invariants

\langle\upgamma_{1},\dots,\upgamma_{n}\rangle^{\mathbb{P}^{k}|\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}}_{0,\alpha}\coloneq\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})]}\prod_{i}\ev_{i}^{*}\upgamma_{i}.

2.2.
Positivity

The other key ingredient we need is the following theorem on positivity
of intersections.

Theorem 2.6
.

(
[
8
, Corollary 12.2]
)

Let
X
be a nonsingular variety with globally generated tangent
bundle, and let
V_{1},\dots,V_{k}
be subvarieties with
\sum\mathrm{codim}(V_{i}/X)=\dim X.
Let
Z_{i}
be the
distinguished varieties of the scheme-theoretic
intersection
\bigcap V_{i}
, and
\upzeta_{i}
be the component of

\prod_{j}[V_{j}]
in
A_{0}(Z_{i}).
Then
\deg\upzeta_{i}\geq 0
for
all
i
.

While transverse intersections are positive in complex geometry, due to
canonical orientations, negativity can be introduced from excess
intersections. Intuitively, this arises from negativity of a
normal bundle. But all such arise as quotients of the ambient
tangent bundle, so a positivity assumption on the tangent
bundle provides a uniform way of obstructing
negative contributions.

In our applications
X
will be a product of projective spaces,
which satisfies the hypothesis because of the Euler sequence.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We consider genus zero logarithmic maps to
\mathbb{P}^{k}
relative to its
toric boundary, and insist that at least one marked point is mapped into
the torus
T\subset\mathbb{P}^{k}
. Throughout this text,
we will fix a number
n
of marked points, labelled
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}.
Fix also a labelling
H_{0},\dots,H_{k}
of the boundary components, and tangency data
\upalpha_{ij}

between the marked point
p_{i}
and the boundary component
H_{j}.

We assume that at least one marked point
is not tangent to any component of
\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}.
Any map
with these tangency data is necessarily of degree

for any

.

Note that we can use multilinearity of Gromov–Witten invariants to
restrict to linear insertions. It is therefore more convenient to
work with the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix a partition
\upnu=(\upnu_{1},\dots,\upnu_{n})
of

. Define

N_{\upalpha,\upnu}\coloneq\langle H^{\upnu_{1}},\dots,H^{\upnu_{n}}\rangle^{\mathbb{P}^{k}|\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}}_{0,\upalpha},

where

is the hyperplane class.

Transversality

In Sections
3.1
and
3.2
we establish the theoretical underpinnings of the upper
bound, before moving onto explicit calculations in Section

3.3
. We express
N_{\upalpha,\upnu}
as a transverse intersection in
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}).

.

Fix tangency orders
\upalpha
and insertions
\upnu.

Then choosing generic linear subspaces

in
\mathbb{P}^{k},

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ev_{i}^{-1}V_{i}

consists of

many reduced points contained

This follows from Kleiman’s theorem
[

]
. Indeed,

\prod_{i}\operatorname{PGL}_{k+1}
acts transitively on

Having chosen

generically, define

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}

3.2.
Alternative Compactification

We know from Proposition
2.4
that a choice of
marked point with no tangency to
\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}
gives an
explicit isomorphism
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})\cong\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times T.
Moreover, a choice of any three
marked points rigidifies the action of
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and gives an isomorphism between

\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
and an open subset of
\mathbb{A}^{n-3}.
We make
these choices now.

Fix a labelling
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
of the marked points, and send
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\mapsto(0,1,\infty).
We will also
denote by

p=p_{i_{0}}\in\{p_{4},\dots,p_{n}\}

a fixed choice of marked point with no tangency to the
boundary. In homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{1},
write
p_{i}=[S_{i}:T_{i}].

Our rigidification of the
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}
action was achieved by declaring
p_{1}=[0:1],p_{2}=[1:1],p_{3}=[1:0].

We obtain the following affine coordinates on
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\subset\mathbb{A}^{n-3}

x_{4}=\frac{S_{4}}{T_{4}},\dots,x_{n}=\frac{S_{n}}{T_{n}}.

Compactifying to
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}
gives homogeneous coordinates
X_{0},X_{4},\dots,X_{n}
with

(1)

\frac{X_{i}}{X_{0}}=x_{i}=\frac{S_{i}}{T_{i}}.

Also naturally

say with coordinates

where
A_{0},\dots,A_{k}
are homogeneous
coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{k}.
Now
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
is isomorphic to an open subset of
\mathbb{A}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{A}^{k},
or equivalently an open

\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}.

Section

relates
\bigcap_{i}\ev_{i}^{-1}V_{i},
for generic linear subspaces

of

to logarithmic Gromov–Witten invariants. The positivity considerations of Section

lead us to consider the intersection of the closures

in
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}.

.

Denoting by

for

\mathbb{P}^{k},

we have an upper bound

N_{\upalpha,\upnu}\leq\deg\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{\upnu_{i}}[\overline{\ev_{i}^{-1}H_{ij}}].

Proof.

Consider the intersection

in
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k},
with

\mathbb{P}^{k}.

Denote by

the points of the intersection

and by

Write

.

Remark 3.4
.

From this result, we see that the intersections we
perform on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
can be arranged to avoid the boundary.
The fact that the alternative compactification

\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
gives us
an
upper bound
and not an exact calculation of the
invariants tells us that here, intersections

cannot
be made to avoid the boundary. The reason
for this is that the evaluation map
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}\mathrel{-\,}\rightarrow\prod_{i}\mathbb{P}^{k}
is only a rational map.
Intersections can therefore be made to avoid the boundary
of a blowup, but this will in general not have the
positivity we need.

3.3.
Calculations

We turn to direct computations. The reader may benefit from
bearing in mind that, since we want to find an upper bound
for the degree of a divisor in a product of projective
spaces, it is enough to find any polynomial vanishing
along the divisor and read off its bidegree.
In the remainder of this Section,
H
will denote a generic
hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{k}
and we will write

A_{n+k-4}(\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k})=\mathbb{Z}H_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}H_{2}.

We first deal with the easy case of evaluation at
p=p_{i_{0}}.

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

[\overline{\ev_{i_{0}}^{-1}H}]=H_{2}.

Proof.

Under our chosen isomorphism
\operatorname{fgt}\times\ev_{i_{0}}:\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})\to\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times T,
the map
\ev_{i_{0}}
is identified with
projection onto the second factor.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

For
i\neq i_{0}
, one has
[\overline{\ev_{i}^{-1}H}]=\upchi H_{1}+H_{2}
with

\upchi\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\upalpha^{\max}_{i},

where

\upalpha^{\max}_{i}=\max_{j}\upalpha_{ij}.

Proof.

Suppose for now that
i\neq 1,2,3,
and that
p_{i}
has no
tangency to
\partial\mathbb{P}^{k}
. This way we are not
evaluating at a marked point whose position in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
fixed, and the evaluation map factors as

\ev_{i}:\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times T\to T\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{k}.

As discussed in Section
2.1
, any
f\in\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})

is determined up to units by

(2)

f_{j}\colon[X:Y]\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{n}(X\,T_{i}-Y\,S_{i})^{\upalpha_{ij}},\quad j=0,\dots,k,

recalling that
[S_{1}:T_{1}],[S_{2}:T_{2}],[S_{3}:T_{3}]
have fixed
values. In summary,

f\colon[X:Y]\mapsto\big[\uplambda_{0}f_{0}\left([X:Y]\right):\uplambda_{1}f_{1}\left([X:Y]\right):\cdots:\uplambda_{k}f_{k}\left([X:Y]\right)\big],

for some
\uplambda_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

Because
p
has zero contact with the boundary, the value of
f(p)
and the tuple
(\uplambda_{j})
are equivalent data.
Computing the other evaluation maps is achieved by passing back and forth between this equivalence. Precisely, a point

(\{[S_{i}:T_{i}]\}_{i},[A_{0}:\dots:A_{k}])\in\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times T
has

[A_{0}:\dots:A_{k}]=\left[\uplambda_{0}f_{0}(p):\uplambda_{1}f_{1}(p):\dots:\uplambda_{k}f_{k}(p)\right],

so

\frac{\uplambda_{j}}{\uplambda_{0}}=\frac{A_{j}}{A_{0}}\frac{f_{0}(p)}{f_{j}(p)}.

We deduce that
\ev_{i}:\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times T\longrightarrow T
sends

\left(\{[S_{i}:T_{i}]\}_{i},[A_{0}:\cdots A_{k}]\right)\mapsto\left[f_{0}(p_{i}):\frac{A_{1}}{A_{0}}\frac{f_{0}(p)}{f_{1}(p)}f_{1}(p_{i}):\dots:\frac{A_{k}}{A_{0}}\frac{f_{0}(p)}{f_{k}(p)}f_{k}(p_{i})\right].

While the
T
in the domain and codomain are the same torus, it
will help to give them distinct coordinates. On the target
torus
T,
let
B_{0},\dots,B_{k}

be the restriction of the homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{k}.

We can then pick a generic hyperplane

H\subset T
defined by

H=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{k}\upmu_{j}B_{j}=0\right\}.

We have

\ev_{i}^{-1}H=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{k}\upmu_{j}A_{j}\frac{f_{0}(p)}{f_{j}(p)}f_{j}(p_{i})=0\right\}.

Clearing denominators expresses
\ev_{i}^{-1}H
as

\left\{F_{i}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\coloneq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\upmu_{j}A_{j}f_{j}(p_{i})\prod_{l\neq j}f_{l}(p)\right)=0\right\}.

From (
2
) and our choices of coordinates for
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},
we see that

f_{j}(p_{i})=S_{i}^{\upalpha_{1j}}(S_{i}-T_{i})^{\upalpha_{2j}}T_{i}^{\upalpha_{3j}}\prod_{u=4}^{n}(S_{i}T_{u}-T_{i}S_{u})^{\upalpha_{uj}},

and

f_{l}(p)=S_{i_{0}}^{\upalpha_{1l}}(S_{i_{0}}-T_{i_{0}})^{\upalpha_{2l}}T_{i_{0}}^{\upalpha_{3l}}\prod_{u=4}^{n}(S_{i_{0}}T_{u}-T_{i_{0}}S_{u})^{\upalpha_{ul}}.

In terms of the affine coordinates (
1
) on
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times T
we have:

\ev_{i}^{-1}H=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{k}\upmu_{j}a_{j}\left(\uptheta_{ij}\prod_{u=4}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{u})^{\upalpha_{uj}}\right)\left(\upphi_{ij}\prod_{v=4}^{n}(x_{i_{0}}-x_{v})^{\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{vl}}\right)=0\right\},

where

\uptheta_{ij}=x_{i}^{\upalpha_{1j}}(x_{i}-1)^{\upalpha_{2j}}(-1)^{\upalpha_{3j}},\quad\upphi_{ij}=x_{i_{0}}^{\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{1l}}(x_{i_{0}}-1)^{\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{2l}}(-1)^{\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{3l}},

and
a_{0}=1.

Note that
F_{i}
is
not
the zero
polynomial since
x_{i}
and
x_{i_{0}}
were assumed not to meet
the boundary
\partial\mathbb{P}^{k},
so

\upalpha_{ij},\upalpha_{i_{0}j}=0
for all
j.

The equation defining
\overline{\ev_{i}^{-1}H}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
certainly divides

\tilde{F}_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\upmu_{j}A_{j}\left(\Uptheta_{ij}\prod_{u=4}^{n}(X_{i}-X_{u})^{\upalpha_{uj}}\right)\left(\Upphi_{ij}\prod_{v=4}^{n}(X_{i_{0}}-X_{v})^{\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{vl}}\right),

where

\Uptheta_{ij}=X_{i}^{\upalpha_{1j}}(X_{i}-X_{0})^{\upalpha_{2j}}(-X_{0})^{\upalpha_{3j}},\quad\Upphi_{ij}=X_{i_{0}}^{\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{1l}}(X_{i_{0}}-X_{0})^{\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{2j}}(-X_{0})^{\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{3j}}.

The balancing condition shows that
\tilde{F}_{i}
is of
bidegree
(dn,1).
Indeed

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\upalpha_{ij}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{il}=\sum_{i,j}\upalpha_{ij}=dn.

But
\tilde{F}_{j}
is
reducible, with some factors cutting out parts
of the boundary of
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
inside
\mathbb{P}^{n-3}\times\mathbb{P}^{k}.
Our previous estimate of the bidegree of
[\overline{\ev_{i}^{-1}H}],
namely
(1,dn),
can therefore be
improved upon.

Since no marked point
can be tangent to every component of the boundary we will not
find common factors from the first bracketed factor. However,
removing common factors from the second set of brackets
leaves a polynomial of degree

dn-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min_{j}\left(\sum_{l\neq j}\upalpha_{il}\right)=dn-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\upalpha_{ij}-\upalpha_{i}^{\max}\right)=\sum_{i}\upalpha_{i}^{\max}

in the
X_{i}
as claimed. Note that the factors we cancel
are all invertible on
\mathcal{M}_{0,\upalpha}(\mathbb{P}^{k},\partial\mathbb{P}^{k})
where marked points
cannot collide, so what remains still contains
\ev_{i}^{-1}H.

We conclude by explaining how to do away with the
simplifying assumptions made in the first line of the proof.
In the case of evaluating at
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},
the

\uptheta_{ij}
which were previously of positive degree
become constant. The bound is less sharp in these cases,
but nevertheless true. Meanwhile, if
p_{i}
has tangency
to the boundary, then
\ev_{i}
instead has image in the dense
torus of the stratum to which
p_{i}
maps. This is a quotient
of the torus
T,
so the same argument goes through with
fewer terms in
F_{i}
. Again this introduces some
inefficiency but the bound holds nonetheless.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma
3.5
and Propositions
3.3
and

3.6
combine to give

N_{\upalpha,\upnu}\leq\deg H_{2}^{\upnu_{i_{0}}}\prod_{i\neq i_{0}}\left(\left(\sum_{i}\upalpha_{i}^{\max}\right)H_{1}+H_{2}\right)^{\upnu_{i}}.

Extracting the coefficient of
H_{1}^{n-3}H_{2}^{k}
in the above yields

N_{\upalpha,\upnu}\leq\binom{k-\upnu_{i_{0}}+n-3}{n-3}\left(\sum_{i}\upalpha_{i}^{\max}\right)^{n-3},

recalling that
\sum_{i}\upnu_{i}=k+n-3.
Minimising over the possible

i_{0}
gives the result.
∎

4.
Examples

We conclude with some comparisons between our upper bound
and known invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The most salient feature is that the
bound becomes less sharp as the number of marked points increases. All of
our exact calculations use Mikhalkin’s tropical correspondence
theorem
[
12
]
.
We assume some familiarity with tropical curves.

In all of our examples, we display the markings with nonzero
tangencies to the boundary
\partial\mathbb{P}^{2}.
At each of the
remaining markings we impose a point constraint. A virtual
dimension check shows that, if we have
n^{\prime}
markings with
positive tangency, there should be
n^{\prime}-1
markings with no
tangencies carrying point constraints. Therefore there are

n=2n^{\prime}-1
markings in total here.

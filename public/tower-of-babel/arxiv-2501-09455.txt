Abstract
. Building on results of Medvedev, we construct a 𝖹𝖥𝖢 \mathsf{ZFC} example of a non-Polish topological group that is countable dense homogeneous. Our example is a dense subgroup of ℤ ω \mathbb{Z}^{\omega} of size 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} that is a λ \lambda -set. We also conjecture that every countable dense homogenous Baire topological group with no isolated points contains a copy of the Cantor set, and give a proof in a very special case.

1.
Introduction

As is common in the literature about countable dense homogeneity, all spaces (including groups) are assumed to be separable and metrizable. By
group
we mean topological group. By
countable
we mean at most countable. Our reference for general topology is
[
En
]
. Our reference for groups is
[
AT
]
. Our reference for descriptive set theory is
[
Ke
]
. For all other set-theoretic notions, we refer to
[
Ku
]
. A space
X
is
countable dense homogeneous
(
\mathsf{CDH}
) if for every pair
(D,E)
of countable dense subsets of
X
there exists a homeomorphism
h:X\longrightarrow X
such that
h[D]=E
.

The fundamental positive result in the theory of
\mathsf{CDH}
spaces is due to Anderson, Curtis and van Mill
[
ACvM
, Theorem 5.2]
, and it states that every strongly locally homogeneous Polish space is
\mathsf{CDH}
.

Recall that a space
X
is
strongly locally homogeneous
if there exists a base
\mathcal{B}
for
X
such that for every
U\in\mathcal{B}
and
(x,y)\in U\times U
there exists a homeomorphism
h:X\longrightarrow X
such that
h(x)=y
and
h(z)=z
for every
z\in X\setminus U
.
In particular, familiar spaces like the Cantor set
2^{\omega}
, the Baire space
\omega^{\omega}
, the Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the spheres
\mathsf{S}^{n}
and the Hilbert cube
[0,1]^{\omega}
are all examples of
\mathsf{CDH}
spaces. See
[
AvM
, Sections 14-16]
for much more on this topic.

Fitzpatrick and Zhou
[
FZ1
, Problem 6]
asked for non-Polish examples of
\mathsf{CDH}
spaces (this is Problem 389 from the book “Open problems in topology”), and the present article belongs to this line of research. Consistent examples were constructed in
[
FZ1
, Theorem 4.6]
and
[
BB
, Theorem 3.5]
. The first
\mathsf{ZFC}
example was given in
[
FHMR
]
, using metamathematical methods. Another breakthrough was obtained in
[
HGHvM
]
, where the authors gave a more direct, “down-to-earth”
\mathsf{ZFC}
construction. This approach was further developed in
[
Me1
]
,
[
Mv2
]
and
[
Mv3
]
. In particular, one of these results of Medvedev (namely, Theorem
3.2
) will be a fundamental ingredient in obtaining our main result.

A more recent source of inspiration was the following interesting open question
[
DKM
, Question 1.2]
. We remark that Hernández-Gutiérrez
[
HG
, Corollary 3.8]
showed that, consistently, there exists a non-Polish
\mathsf{C}_{p}
-space that is
\mathsf{CDH}
.

Question 1.1

(Dobrowolski, Krupski, Marciszewski)
.

Is there a
\mathsf{ZFC}

\mathsf{CDH}
topological vector space?

\mathsf{P}

\mathsf{ZFC}

2.
More preliminaries and terminology

Given a space
Z
, we will say that a subspace
X
of
Z
is a
copy
of a space
Y
if
X
is homeomorphic to
Y
. A space is
crowded
if it is non-empty and it has no isolated points. A subset
S
of a space
X
is
meager
in
X
if there exist closed nowhere dense subsets
C_{n}
of
X
for
n\in\omega
such that
S\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}C_{n}
. A space
X
is
meager
if
X
is a meager subset of
X
. A space
X
is
Baire
if no non-empty open subset of
X
is meager in
X
.

A space
X
is
homogeneous
if for every
(x,y)\in X\times X
there exists a homeomorphism
h:X\longrightarrow X
such that
h(x)=y
. Using translations, one sees that every group is homogeneous. A space
X
is
h-homogeneous
if every non-empty clopen subspace of
X
is homeomorphic to
X
.

A subset
S
of a group
X
is
precompact
if for every neighborhood
U
of the identity in
X
there exists a finite
F\subseteq X
such that
S\subseteq FU\cap UF
. A group
X
is
locally precompact
if there exists a precompact neighborhood of the identity in
X
. The fact that
\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
is not locally precompact (which is a simple exercise) will be crucial in the proof of Corollary
3.4
. Here, we use
\mathbb{Z}
to denote the integers with the discrete topology and the standard operation of addition. Recall that every group
X
is a dense subgroup of a Polish group
\varrho X
, known as the
Raĭkov completion
of
X
(see
[
AT
, Theorem 3.6.10, Proposition 3.6.20 and Proposition 4.3.8]
).

Given
f,g\in\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
, we will write
f\leq^{\ast}g
(respectively
f<^{\ast}g
or
f=^{\ast}g
) to mean that
f(n)\leq g(n)
(respectively
f(n)<g(n)
or
f(n)=g(n)
) for all but finitely many
n\in\omega
. We will say that
S\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
is
bounded
if there exists
g\in\omega^{\omega}
such that
f\leq^{\ast}g
for every
f\in S
. Recall that

\mathfrak{b}=\mathsf{min}\{|S|:S\text{ is an unbounded subset of }\omega^{\omega}\}.

A space
X
is a
\lambda
-set
if every countable subset of
X
is a
\mathsf{G_{\delta}}
-subset of
X
. It is easy to realize that every crowded
\lambda
-set is meager, hence non-Polish. The only other fact concerning
\lambda
-sets that we will need is the following classical result
[
Ro
, Théorème 1]
. See
[
HGHvM
, Section 2]
and
[
Mi
, Section 5]
for much more on this topic.

Lemma 2.1

(Rothberger)
.

Let
X
be a space such that
|X|<\mathfrak{b}
. Then
X
is a
\lambda
-set.

A subspace
X
of
2^{\omega}
is a
Bernstein set
if
X\cap K\neq\varnothing
and
(2^{\omega}\setminus X)\cap K\neq\varnothing
for every copy
K
of
2^{\omega}
in
2^{\omega}
. It is easy to see that Bernstein sets exist in
\mathsf{ZFC}
, and that they are Baire spaces (in fact, using
[
vM
, Corollary 1.9.13]
, one can show that every closed subspace of a Bernstein set is a Baire space).

3.
The main result

In this section, we will obtain our main result (see Corollary
3.4
), although Theorem
3.3
seems to be of independent interest. We begin by stating two theorems of Medvedev that will be our fundamental tools.

The following result shows that, in the context of zero-dimensional groups, there is a particularly simple sufficient condition for h-homogeneity. For a proof, see
[
Mv1
, Theorem 8]
.
3
3

3

We remark that in
[
Mv1
]
the assumption “zero-dimensional paracompact” often appears, while the stronger assumption “ultraparacompact” is in fact used. (Recall that a space
X
is
ultraparacompact
if every open cover of
X
can be refined by a clopen partition of
X
.) This does not affect us, since zero-dimensionality easily implies ultraparacompactness in the separable metrizable realm.

Theorem 3.1

(Medvedev)
.

Let
X
be a zero-dimensional group. If
X
is not locally precompact then
X
is h-homogeneous.

The following is
[
Mv2
, Corollary 6]
, and it can be viewed as the culmination of the technique introduced in
[
HGHvM
]
. The fact that every meager
\mathsf{CDH}
space is a
\lambda
-set is due to Fitzpatrick and Zhou
[
FZ2
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Medvedev)
.

Let
X
be an uncountable zero-dimensional h-homogeneous space. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

•

X
is a meager
\mathsf{CDH}
space,

•

X
is a
\lambda
-set.

Theorem 3.3
.

There exists a dense subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
of size
\mathfrak{b}
that is a
\lambda
-set.

Proof.

Given
f\in\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
, we will denote by
|f|
the function defined by
|f|(n)=|f(n)|
for
n\in\omega
. We will denote by
\vec{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
the constant function with value
0
. Given
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
, we will denote by
\langle S\rangle
the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
generated by
S
.

Using transfinite recursion, it is straightforward to construct
f_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
for
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

f_{\alpha}(n)>0
for each
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}
and
n<\omega
,

(2)

f_{\alpha}>^{\ast}k_{0}\cdot f_{\beta_{0}}+\cdots+k_{n}\cdot f_{\beta_{n}}
whenever
n<\omega
,
k_{0},\ldots,k_{n}\in\omega
, and
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{n}<\alpha<\mathfrak{b}
.

Observe that
f_{\alpha}\neq f_{\beta}
whenever
\alpha\neq\beta
. Set
Q=\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}:z=^{\ast}\vec{0}\}
and

X=\langle\{f_{\alpha}:\alpha<\mathfrak{b}\}\cup Q\rangle.

It is clear that
X
is dense in
\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
and that
|X|=\mathfrak{b}
.

It remains to show that
X
is a
\lambda
-set. For any given
z\in X\setminus Q
, fix
k(z)\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha(z)<\mathfrak{b}
such that

z=^{\ast}k(z)\cdot f_{\alpha(z)}+z^{\prime}

for some
z^{\prime}\in\langle\{f_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha(z)\}\rangle
. Now pick a countable
C\subseteq X
. Since
\mathfrak{b}
has uncountable cofinality, it is possible to fix
\delta<\mathfrak{b}
such that
\delta>\mathsf{sup}\{\alpha(z):z\in C\}
.

Claim.
Let
x\in C
, and let
y\in X\setminus Q
. Assume that
\alpha(y)>\delta
. Then

|x|\leq^{\ast}f_{\delta}<^{\ast}|y|.

Proof.
The first inequality follows easily from our choice of
\delta
, so we will only prove the second one. Pick
n\in\omega
,
k_{0},\ldots,k_{n}=k(y)\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha_{0}<\cdots<\alpha_{n}=\alpha(y)<\mathfrak{b}
such that

y=^{\ast}k_{0}\cdot f_{\alpha_{0}}+\cdots+k_{n}\cdot f_{\alpha_{n}}.

Set

y^{\prime}=f_{\alpha(y)}-\sum_{i<n}|k_{i}|\cdot f_{\alpha_{i}},

and observe that
y^{\prime}\geq^{\ast}\vec{0}
by condition
(2)
.

Next, we will prove that
|y|\geq^{\ast}y^{\prime}
. First assume that
k(b)>0
. In this case, it is easy to realize that
y\geq^{\ast}y^{\prime}\geq^{\ast}\vec{0}
, hence
|y|=^{\ast}y\geq^{\ast}y^{\prime}
. Now assume that
k(b)<0
. In this case, one sees that

-y=^{\ast}(-k(y))\cdot f_{\alpha(y)}-\sum_{i<n}k_{i}\cdot f_{\alpha_{i}}\geq f_{\alpha(y)}-\sum_{i<n}|k_{i}|\cdot f_{\alpha_{i}}=y^{\prime}\geq^{\ast}\vec{0},

so
|y|=^{\ast}-y\geq^{\ast}y^{\prime}
. In conclusion, we must have

|y|\geq^{\ast}y^{\prime}=f_{\alpha(y)}-\sum_{i<n}|k_{i}|\cdot f_{\alpha_{i}}>^{\ast}f_{\delta},

where the last inequality follows from the assumption that
\alpha(y)\geq\delta
.
\blacksquare

Finally, define

G=\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}:|z(n)|\leq f_{\delta}(n)\text{ for infinitely many }n\in\mathbb{Z}\},

and observe that
G
is a
\mathsf{G_{\delta}}
-subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
. Furthermore, as a consequence of the above Claim, it is easy to realize that
C\subseteq X\cap G
and
|X\cap G|<\mathfrak{b}
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
, there exists a
\mathsf{G_{\delta}}
-subset
G^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
such that
X\cap G\cap G^{\prime}=C
. This clearly shows that
C
is a
\mathsf{G_{\delta}}
-subset of
X
, as desired.
∎

We remark that the above theorem (to the best of our knowledge) gives the first
\mathsf{ZFC}
example of an uncountable group that is a
\lambda
-set.

Rather unsurprisingly, we suggest the terminology
\lambda
-group
.
(For a consistent example, under the assumption that
\omega_{1}<\mathfrak{b}
, consider an arbitrary group of size
\omega_{1}
and apply Lemma

.)

Corollary 3.4
.

There exists a non-Polish
\mathsf{CDH}
group.

Proof.

Let
X
be the group given by Theorem
3.3
. By Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, in order to conclude the proof, it will be enough to show that
X
is not locally precompact. Assume, in order to get a contraction, that
X
is locally precompact. Using
[
AT
, Lemma 3.7.5]
, one can easily show that every group containing a locally precompact dense subgroup is locally precompact. Since
\mathbb{Z}^{\omega}
is not locally precompact, we have reached a contradiction.
∎

Given that our example is meager, the following question is still open (as we mentioned in Section
1
, non-meager
\mathsf{P}
-filters give consistent examples).

Question 3.5
.

Is there a
\mathsf{ZFC}
example of a non-Polish Baire
\mathsf{CDH}
group?

Since every
\mathsf{CDH}
topological vector space is Baire (see the remarks that follow Conjecture
4.2
), an affirmative answer to Question
3.5
could be viewed as a further “baby-step” towards answering Question
1.1
.

4.
A conjecture about Baire
\mathsf{CDH}
groups

This section is motivated by the following open problem
[
HGHvM
, Question 4.7]
.

Question 4.1

(Hernández-Gutiérrez, Hrušák, van Mill)
.

Is it consistent with
\mathsf{ZFC}
to have a crowded Baire
\mathsf{CDH}
space of size less than
\mathfrak{c}
?

Assuming Martin’s Axiom for
\sigma
-centered posets, Baldwin and Beaudoin
[
BB
, Theorem 3.5]
constructed a
\mathsf{CDH}
Bernstein set in
2^{\omega}
(see also
[
Me2
]
). In an attempt to better understand
\mathsf{CDH}
groups, we tried to modify this construction in order to obtain a subgroup of
2^{\omega}
. However, we ran into serious difficulties. This lead us to the following conjecture, which would resolve Question
4.1
in the realm of groups.

Conjecture 4.2
.

Every crowded Baire
\mathsf{CDH}
group contains a copy of
2^{\omega}
.

Another interesting consequence of Conjecture
4.2
would be the equivalence of the following conditions for every crowded
\mathsf{CDH}
group
X
:

•

X
is Baire,

•

X
contains a copy of
2^{\omega}
.

In fact, Dobrowolski, Krupski and Marciszewski
[
DKM
, Theorem 3.1]
proved that every homogeneous
\mathsf{CDH}
space containing a copy of
2^{\omega}
is Baire. Notice however that mere homogeneity would not be sufficient for this equivalence, since the
\mathsf{CDH}
Bernstein set mentioned above can be easily made homogeneous as well.

We conclude this article by proving Conjecture
4.2
in the very special case of groups that have index
2
in their completion (see Theorem
4.4
). In the following results, although the group
X
is not assumed to be commutative, we will still use the additive notation for the sake of readability. In particular, given
f,g:X\longrightarrow X
, we will use
f-g
to denote the function obtained by setting
(f-g)(x)=f(x)-g(x)
. We will use
\mathsf{id}_{X}
to denote the identity function on a set
X
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be a crowded Baire
\mathsf{CDH}
group. Then there exists a homeomorphism
h:X\longrightarrow X
such that
(h-\mathsf{id}_{X})[X]
is uncountable.

Proof.

Fix a countable dense subgroup
D
of
X
. Notice that
X
must be uncountable, because it is a crowded
\mathsf{CDH}
space. Therefore, we can fix
x_{0}\in X\setminus D
. Set
E=x_{0}+D
, and observe that
D\cap E=\varnothing
. Let
h:X\longrightarrow X
be a homeomorphism such that
h[D\cup E]=D
. Set
f=h-\mathsf{id}_{X}
.

Assume, in order to get a contradiction, that
f[X]
is countable. Then, since
X
is Baire and

X=\bigcup_{y\in f[X]}f^{-1}(y),

there must be a non-empty open subset
U
of
X
such that
f\upharpoonright U
is constant. In other words, there exists
x_{1}\in X
such that
h(z)=x_{1}+z
for every
z\in U
. Fix
x\in D\cap U
and
y\in E\cap U
. Notice that
x_{1}\in D
because
x_{1}+x=h(x)\in D
and
D
is a subgroup of
X
. Since
x_{1}+y=h(y)\in D
, it follows that
y\in D
, a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a crowded Baire
\mathsf{CDH}
group. Assume that
X
has index
2
in its Raĭkov completion
\varrho X
. Then
X
contains a copy of
2^{\omega}
.

Proof.

Let
h
denote the homeomorphism given by Lemma
4.3
. By
[
Ke
, Exercise 3.10]
, we can fix a
\mathsf{G_{\delta}}
-subset
G
of
\varrho X
and a homeomorphism
\widetilde{h}:G\longrightarrow G
such that
h\subseteq\widetilde{h}
. Notice that
G
is a Polish space by
[
Ke
, Theorem 3.11]
, hence
(\widetilde{h}-\mathsf{id}_{G})[G]
is analytic. Furthermore, Lemma
4.3
guarantees that
(\widetilde{h}-\mathsf{id}_{G})[G]
is uncountable. Since analytic sets have the perfect set property (see
[
Ke
, Theorem 29.1]
), in order to conclude the proof, it will be enough to show that
(\widetilde{h}-\mathsf{id}_{G})[G]\subseteq X
.

Since
X
has index
2
in
\varrho X
, there exists
z_{0}\in\varrho X
such that
z_{0}+X=\varrho X\setminus X
. Pick
z\in G
. If
z\in X
then clearly
(\widetilde{h}-\mathsf{id}_{G})(z)=h(z)-z\in X
, so assume that
z\in G\setminus X
. Then
z=z_{0}+x
and
\widetilde{h}(z)=z_{0}+y
for suitable
x,y\in X
. Hence

(\widetilde{h}-\mathsf{id}_{G})(z)=(z_{0}+y)-(z_{0}+x)=z_{0}+(y-x)-z_{0}\in z_{0}+X-z_{0}=X,

as desired, where the last equality follows from the well-known (and easy to prove) fact that subgroups of index two are normal.
∎

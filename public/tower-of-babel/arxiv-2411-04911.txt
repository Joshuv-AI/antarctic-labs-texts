When is a CPD weighted shift similar to a subnormal operator? Abstract
. We prove that a CPD unilateral weighted shift W 𝝀 W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}} of type III is a quasi-affine transform of the operator M z M_{z} of multiplication by the independent variable on the L 2 ​ ( ρ ) L^{2}(\rho) -closure of analytic complex polynomials on the complex plane, where ρ \rho is a measure precisely determined by W 𝝀 W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}} . By using this model, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for similarity of W 𝝀 W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}} to M z M_{z} . Necessary conditions for a CPD operator to be similar to a subnormal one are given. A variety of concrete classes of non-subnormal CPD unilateral weighted shifts similar to subnormal operators are established.

1.1.
Introduction

The similarity problems for Hilbert space operators
have attracted the attention of many researchers over
many decades and have resulted in many non-trivial
theorems, not to mention one of the earliest, namely
the Sz.-Nagy similarity theorem
[
28
]
, saying
that an invertible operator is similar to a unitary
operator if and only if both the operator itself and
its inverse are power bounded. Another famous
similarity problem, posed by Halmos

[
11
, Problem 6]
and solved negatively by
Pisier
[
19
]
, concerns whether a polynomially
bounded operator is similar to a contraction. Yet
another problem, called the Kadison similarity problem

[
17
]
, asking whether a bounded homomorphism of
a
C^{*}
-algebra into the algebra of operators on a
Hilbert space is similar to a
*
-homomorphism, has
remained unsolved for almost
70
years.

The aforementioned similarity problems do indeed apply
to operator-valued functions on algebraic structures.
The two questions described below can be seen more as
similarity problems for single operators. The first,
stated by Halmos
[
11
, Problem 2]
, asks whether
every part of a weighted shift is similar to a
weighted shift. The second, in the spirit of our
paper, is whether similarity of two normal operators
implies their unitary equivalence. An affirmative
solution was given by Putnam
[
20
, Theorem I]
.
D. E. Sarason showed that this is no longer true for
subnormal operators, namely he constructed an explicit
example of two similar subnormal unilateral weighted
shifts that are not unitary equivalent (see

[
12
, Problem 199]
).

Every (injective) unilateral weighted shift
is unitarily equivalent to the operator of
multiplication by the independent variable
on some Hilbert space of formal series (see

[
23
, Sec. 3]
). It is known that
every subnormal unilateral weighted shift is
unitarily equivalent to the operator
M_{z}

of multiplication by the independent
variable
z
on
H^{2}(\mu)
, where
\mu
is
a finite Borel measure on a compact subset
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and
H^{2}(\mu)

is the
L^{2}(\mu)
-closure of analytic
complex polynomials (see

[
8
, Theorem II.6.10]
). This
together with
[
25
, Theorem 4.1]

implies that every contractive conditionally
positive definite (CPD) unilateral weighted
shift is unitarily equivalent to
M_{z}
on

H^{2}(\mu)
. In general, a CPD unilateral
weighted shift may not be unitarily
equivalent to
M_{z}
on
H^{2}(\mu)
. Worse,
it may not even be similar to such an
operator (see, e.g., Theorem
2.1.3
and
Proposition
2.2.3
). A natural
question then arises: when is a CPD
unilateral weighted shift similar to the
operator of multiplication by
z
on some

H^{2}(\mu)
? In fact, according to
Lemma
2.3.1
, this question is
equivalent to the following problem:

Problem 1.1.1
.

When is a CPD unilateral weighted shift
similar to a subnormal operator?

In this paper, we will analyze
Problem
1.1.1
, namely, we will look for
necessary and/or sufficient conditions,
written in terms of the scalar representing
triplet, for a CPD unilateral weighted shift
to be similar to a subnormal operator. It
can be seen that CPD operators are cousins
of subnormal operators in the sense of
harmonic analysis on the additive semigroup

\mathbb{Z}_{+}
of all nonnegative integers, that
is, a Hilbert space operator
T
is
subnormal (resp., CPD) if and only if the
sequence
\{\|T^{n}h\|^{2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
positive definite (resp., conditionally
positive definite) on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
for every
vector
h
(see
[
8
,
14
]
). To
better understand the similarity problem, we
divide the class of all CPD unilateral
weighted shifts into three disjoint classes
the members of which are called of types I,
II and III, respectively (see
Definition
2.2.2
). CPD unilateral
weighted shifts of class I are exactly

2
-isometries.

The following theorem is the most general
result obtained by the authors in this area.
On this occasion, we refer the reader to
(
1.3.1
), (
1.4.1
) and
Theorem
1.4.1
for the definitions of

\mathscr{B}_{2}(\cdot)
,
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and “Berger
measure”, respectively. Recall that an
operator from a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
into a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
is called
quasi-invertible
if it is injective and has
dense range.

Theorem 1.1.2
.

Let
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
be a CPD
unilateral weighted shift with weights

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
that
is not of type I. Then there exists a
nonzero, compactly supported, finite,
rotation-invariant Borel measure
\rho
on

\mathbb{C}
and an operator
X\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2},H^{2}(\rho))
with dense range such that

(i)

XW_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=M_{z}X
,

(ii)

X^{*}X=\mathscr{B}_{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
,

(iii)

X
is
quasi-invertible if and only if
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is
of type III,

(iv)

if
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is of type III, then
M_{z}\in\boldsymbol{B}(H^{2}(\rho))
is unitarily equivalent
to a subnormal unilateral weighted shift
with the Berger measure

\frac{1}{\rho(\mathbb{C})}\rho\circ\phi^{-1}
,
where
\phi(z)=|z|^{2}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
and

\rho\circ\phi^{-1}(\varDelta)=\rho(\phi^{-1}(\varDelta))

for any Borel subset
\varDelta
of the
closed half-line
[0,\infty)
,

(v)

X
has a bounded inverse if
and only if there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathscr{B}_{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\geqslant\varepsilon I
, which is
equivalent to

\displaystyle\beta_{n}=\beta_{n}(W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}):=1-2\lambda_{n}^{2}+\lambda_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n+1}^{2}\geqslant\varepsilon,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+};

(1.1.1)

if this is the case, then
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is of type III and
is similar to a subnormal unilateral weighted shift
with the Berger measure
\frac{1}{\rho(\mathbb{C})}\rho\circ\phi^{-1}
.

Observe that the condition
\mathscr{B}_{2}(T)\geqslant\varepsilon I
is not necessary for a CPD
unilateral weighted shift
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
to be
similar to a subnormal operator, e.g., an
isometry
T
being subnormal is CPD and

\mathscr{B}_{2}(T)=0
. Necessary conditions for a
CPD operator are given in
Subsection
2.1
(see
Theorem
2.1.3
). As a consequence, it
is proved that a non-isometric
3
-isometry
is not similar to any subnormal operator
(see Corollary
2.1.6
). The proof of
Theorem
1.1.2
is provided in
Subsection
2.2
. In this subsection
we also show that non-subnormal CPD weighted
shifts of types I and II are not similar to
subnormal operators (see
Proposition
2.2.3
). In fact, CPD
unilateral weighted shifts of types I and II
satisfy the following dichotomy property:
they are either subnormal or not similar to
a subnormal operator. This dichotomy is no
longer valid for unilateral weighted shifts
of type III (see Theorems
3.1.2
and

3.2.2
). As shown in
Proposition
2.3.2
, in contrast to
Theorem
1.1.2
, a single non-subnormal
CPD unilateral weighted shift is a
quasi-affine transform of a huge number of
subnormal unilateral weighted shifts.
Subsection
2.4
provides criteria
for a CPD unilateral weighted shift to be of
type III. In fact, we give a model for CPD
unilateral weighted shifts of types I and II
(see Theorem
2.4.1
). Subnormality of
the model unilateral weighted shift is
characterized in Corollary
2.4.3
. In
Subsections
3.1
and
3.2

we present several concrete classes of
non-subnormal CPD unilateral weighted shifts
that are similar to subnormal operators (see
Theorems
3.1.2
and
3.2.2
). The
paper concludes with an appendix
characterizing when one unilateral weighted
shift is a quasi-affine transform of
another, and showing that the
quasi-similarity and similarity relations
coincide for unilateral weighted shifts.

1.2.
A little harmonic analysis

Let
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
stand for the fields of real and
complex numbers, respectively, and let
\mathbb{R}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\colon x\geqslant 0\}
,
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|<1\}
and
{\mathbb{T}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|=1\}
. Denote
by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\mathbb{N}
the sets of nonnegative and
positive integers respectively. We write

{\mathfrak{B}}(\varOmega)
for the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel
subsets of a topological space
\varOmega
. Unless
otherwise stated, all scalar measures we consider in
this paper are assumed to be positive. We call a
finite Borel measure
\rho
on
\mathbb{C}

rotation-invariant
if
\rho(\E^{{\mathrm{i}\hskip 0.3014pt}\varphi}\varDelta)=\rho(\varDelta)
for every
\varDelta\in{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbb{C})
and every
\varphi\in[0,2\pi)
. The
closed support of a finite Borel measure
\mu
on

\mathbb{C}
is denoted by
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
. We write
\delta_{t}

for the Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}

concentrated at the point
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\mathbb{C}[z]

stand for the ring of all polynomials in complex
variable
z
with complex coefficients. We regard them
as complex functions on
\mathbb{C}
.

We now recall some basic facts from harmonic analysis
on semigroups (see
[
5
]
; see also

[
14
]
). Let
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of real
numbers. We say that
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
is
positive
definite
(
PD
for brevity) if

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{k}\gamma_{i+j}\lambda_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{j}\geqslant 0,

(1.2.1)

for all finite sequences
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
. If the inequality (
1.2.1
) holds for
all finite sequences
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\sum_{j=0}^{k}\lambda_{j}=0
, then we
call
\boldsymbol{\gamma}

conditionally positive definite

(
CPD
for brevity). Obviously, PD sequences are
CPD but not conversely. The discrete differentiation
transformation
\triangle\colon\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
is given by

\displaystyle(\triangle\boldsymbol{\gamma})_{n}=\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}.

We denote by
\triangle^{k}
the
k
th composition power
of
\triangle
. Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we define the
polynomial
Q_{n}
in real variable
x
by

\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }n=0,1,\\
\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(n-j-1)x^{j}&\text{if }n\geqslant 2,\end{cases}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.2.2)

Then
Q_{n}
takes the following explicit form:

\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{n}-1-n(x-1)}{(x-1)^{2}},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1\}.

(1.2.3)

The polynomials
Q_{n}
have the following properties:

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle=xQ_{n}(x)+n,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.2.4)

\displaystyle(\triangle^{2}Q_{(\cdot)}(x))_{n}

\displaystyle=x^{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.2.5)

where
\triangle^{j}Q_{(\cdot)}(x)
denotes the action
of the transformation
\triangle^{j}
on the sequence

\{Q_{n}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

j\in\mathbb{N}
.

CPD sequences of (at most) exponential growth can be
characterized as follows. The formula (
1.2.6
) is
derived from the Lévy-Khinchin formula for
negative definite functions on
*
-semigroups (see

[
5
, Theorems 4.3.19 and 6.2.6]
).

Theorem 1.2.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 2.2.5]
)
.

A sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of real
numbers is CPD and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\gamma_{n}|^{1/n}<\infty
if and only if there
exist
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and a finite
compactly supported Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
such
that
\nu(\{1\})=0
and

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{0}+bn+cn^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\nu(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(1.2.6)

Such a triplet
(b,c,\nu)
is unique.

1.3.
A little operator theory

Given (complex) Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and

\mathcal{K}
, we denote by
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
the
Banach space of all bounded linear operators
from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}
. We abbreviate

\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
to
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
and regard

\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
as a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with
the identity operator
I
on
\mathcal{H}
as the
unit. The kernel and the range of
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
are denoted by
\mathscr{N}(T)
and

{\mathscr{R}}(T)
, respectively. Two operators
A\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
B\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
are said to
be
similar
if there exists a bijective
operator
X\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
such that

XA=BX
. By the inverse mapping theorem,

X^{-1}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H})
. We say that an
operator
X\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
is
quasi-invertible
, or that
X
is a
quasi-affinity
, if
X
is injective and has
dense range. Following
[
29
]
we
say that the operator
A
is a
quasi-affine transform
of the operator
B

if there exists a quasi-invertible operator

X\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
such that
XA=BX
. The
operators
A
and
B
are called
quasi-similar
if they are quasi-affine
transforms of each another. Obviously,
similarity implies quasi-similarity (see

[
29
]
for more information).

An operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be

power bounded
if
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\|T^{n}\|<\infty
. By the uniform boundedness
principle, an operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is
power bounded if and only if
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}h\|<\infty
for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Given an operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
an integer
m\geqslant 1
, we set

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{m}(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}T^{*k}T^{k}.

(1.3.1)

We say that
T
is am
m
-isometry if
\mathscr{B}_{m}(T)=0
.
We refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
for
the basics of the theory of
m
-isometries.

An operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
subnormal
if there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
and a
normal operator
N\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
such that

\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
(isometric embedding) and
Th=Nh

for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{K}
has no proper closed
subspace that reduces
N
and contains
\mathcal{H}
, then
N

is called a
minimal normal extension
of
T
.
According to Lambert’s theorem
T
is subnormal if and
only if for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, the sequence
\{\|T^{n}h\|^{2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is positive definite (see

[
18
]
and
[
14
, Theorem 1.1.1]
).
Following
[
14
]
, we say that an operator

T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is
conditionally positive
definite
(
CPD
for brevity) if for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, the sequence
\{\|T^{n}h\|^{2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
conditionally positive definite. The class of CPD
operators contains in particular subnormal operators

[
10
,
8
]
, complete hypercontractions of
order
2

[
6
]
,
3
-isometries

[
1
,
2
,
3
]
and many others.

Let
F\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
semispectral measure
on a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
of
subsets of a set
\varOmega
, i.e.,
F
is

\sigma
-additive in the weak operator topology and

F(\varDelta)\geqslant 0
for every
\varDelta\in\mathscr{A}
. We

do not assume
that
F(\varOmega)=I
. Denote by

L^{1}(F)
the linear space of all complex

\mathscr{A}
-measurable functions
\zeta
on
\varOmega

such that
\int_{\varOmega}|\zeta(x)|\langle F(\mathrm{d}x)h,h\rangle<\infty
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Then for every
\zeta\in L^{1}(F)
, there exists a unique operator

\int_{\varOmega}\zeta\mathrm{d}F\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that
(see e.g.,
[
26
, Appendix]
)

\displaystyle\Big\langle\int_{\varOmega}\zeta\mathrm{d}Fh,h\Big\rangle=\int_{\varOmega}\zeta(x)\langle F(\mathrm{d}x)h,h\rangle,\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

If
\varOmega=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
and
F\colon{\mathfrak{B}}(\varOmega)\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a semispectral
measure, then its closed support is denoted by

\mathrm{supp}(F)
(recall that such
F
is automatically
regular so
\mathrm{supp}(F)
exists). By a
semispectral
measure
of a subnormal operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
we
mean a compactly supported semispectral measure

G\colon{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbb{C})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
defined by

G(\varDelta)=PE(\varDelta)|_{\mathcal{H}}
for
\varDelta\in{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbb{C})
, where
E\colon{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbb{C})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
is the spectral measure of a minimal normal
extension
N\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
of
T
and
P\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})

is the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{K}
onto
\mathcal{H}
.
Clearly
F
is normalized, i.e.,
F(\mathbb{C})=I
. It
follows from
[
16
, Proposition 5]
and

[
8
, Proposition II.2.5]
that a subnormal
operator has exactly one semispectral measure. It is
also easily seen that (see, e.g.,

[
16
, Proposition 3]
)

\displaystyle T^{*m}T^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\bar{z}^{m}z^{n}G(\mathrm{d}z),\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(1.3.2)

We refer the reader to
[
8
]
for the
foundations of the theory of subnormal operators.

The CPD operators can be characterized as follows.

Theorem 1.3.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 3.1.1]
)
.

An operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is CPD if
and only if there exist operators
B,C\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

and a compactly supported semispectral measure

F\colon{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

B=B^{*}
,
C\geqslant 0
,
F(\{1\})=0
and

\displaystyle T^{*n}T^{n}=I+nB+n^{2}C+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}(x)F(\mathrm{d}x),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(1.3.3)

Moreover, such a triplet
(B,C,F)
is unique.

Call
(B,C,F)
appearing in Theorem
1.3.1
the

representing triplet
of
T
.

1.4.
CPD weighted shifts

Let
\ell^{2}
stand for the Hilbert space of square
summable complex sequences

\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and let

\{e_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be the standard orthonormal
basis of
\ell^{2}
, i.e.,

e_{n}=\{\delta_{n,k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
, where

\delta_{n,k}
is the Kronecker delta. For any bounded
sequence
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subseteq(0,\infty)
, there exits a unique operator

W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
such that

\displaystyle W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{n}=\lambda_{n}e_{n+1},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(1.4.1)

We call
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
a
unilateral weighted
shift
with weights
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
. Clearly,
such
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is always injective. If

\lambda_{n}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, then
we call
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
the
unilateral shift
.
In this paper, we only consider bounded
unilateral weighted shifts with positive
real weights. We refer the reader to the
classical treatise
[
23
]
for the
fundamentals of the theory o weighted
shifts.

If
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
is a unilateral weighted
shift, then the sequence

\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\{\hat{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

associated to
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{n}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n=0,\\
\lambda_{0}^{2}\cdots\lambda_{n-1}^{2}&\text{if }n\geqslant 1,\end{cases}\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(1.4.2)

Call the sequence
\hat{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
the
formal
moment sequence
of
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
. The famous Berger
theorem, which characterizes the subnormality of
unilateral weighted shifts, can be stated as
follows.

Theorem 1.4.1

(
[
9
,
11
]
;

[
23
, Proposition 25]
)
.

W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is subnormal if and
only if there exists a
(
unique
)

compactly supported Borel probability
measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}x^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(1.4.3)

Moreover
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq[0,\|W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\|^{2}]
. The measure
\mu
is
called the Berger measure of
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
.

CPD unilateral weighted shifts can be characterized as
follows.

Theorem 1.4.2

(
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

A unilateral weighted shift

W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is CPD if and only if there exist

\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and a
compactly supported finite Borel measure

\nu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\nu(\{1\})=0

and

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}_{n}=1+\mathfrak{b}n+\mathfrak{c}n^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

(1.4.4)

In this subsection, we provide some

Lemma 2.1.1
.

Let
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a CPD operator with the
representing triplet
(B,C,F)
such that
\mathrm{supp}(F)\subseteq[0,1]
. Suppose that
T
is is similar to a
subnormal operator. Then
T
is power bounded.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a subnormal operator
S\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
and an invertible operator
X\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{H})
such that
XS=TX
. Then

\displaystyle XS^{n}=T^{n}X,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Using (
1.3.3
) and the fact that
X
is
invertible, we get

\displaystyle\|X\|^{-2}\varLambda(n;h)

\displaystyle=\|X\|^{-2}\|T^{n}Xh\|^{2}=\|X\|^{-2}\|XS^{n}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|S^{n}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|X^{-1}\|^{2}\|XS^{n}h\|^{2}=\|X^{-1}\|^{2}\varLambda(n;h),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,h\in\mathcal{H},

(2.1.1)

where
\varLambda(n;h)
is defined for
h\in\mathcal{H}
and

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
as follows

\displaystyle\varLambda(n;h):=\|Xh\|^{2}+n\langle BXh,Xh\rangle+n^{2}\langle CXh,Xh\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}(x)\langle F(\mathrm{d}x)Xh,Xh\rangle.

Let
G
be the semispectral measure of the subnormal
operator
S
. Then, by (
1.3.2
) and
(
2.1.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|X\|^{-2}\varLambda(n;h)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2n}\langle G(\mathrm{d}z)h,h\rangle\leqslant\|X^{-1}\|^{2}\varLambda(n;h),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,h\in\mathcal{H}.

(2.1.2)

Since, by (
1.2.2
),
Q_{n}(x)\leqslant\frac{(n-1)n}{2}

for all
x\in[0,1]
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\varLambda(n;h)\leqslant\|Xh\|^{2}+n\langle BXh,Xh\rangle+n^{2}\langle CXh,Xh\rangle+\frac{(n-1)n}{2}\langle F(\mathbb{R}_{+})Xh,Xh\rangle

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. This and the
right inequality in (
2.1.2
) imply that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2n}\langle G(\mathrm{d}z)h,h\rangle\Big)^{1/n}\leqslant 1,\quad h\in\mathcal{H},

so, by
[
21
, Ex. 4(e), p. 71]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(\langle G(\cdot)h,h\rangle)\subseteq\bar{\mathbb{D}},\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

(2.1.3)

Using the left inequality in (
2.1.2
), together
with (
2.1.3
) and Lebesgue’s dominated
convergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\|X\|^{-2}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varLambda(n;h)\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2n}\langle G(\mathrm{d}z)h,h\rangle=\langle G({\mathbb{T}})h,h\rangle,\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

Hence
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}h\|^{2}\overset{\eqref{tynst}}{=}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varLambda(n;h)<\infty
for
every
h\in\mathcal{H}
. By the uniform boundedness
principle,
T
is power bounded.
∎

Theorem 2.1.3
.

Let
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a CPD operator with the
representing triplet
(B,C,F)
such that
\mathrm{supp}(F)\subseteq[0,1]
. Suppose that
T
is is similar to a
subnormal operator. Then the following conditions

(i)

,

(ii)

,

(iii)

(i) Suppose, to the contrary, that

,

2.1.1
.

(ii) Suppose, to the contrary, that

\displaystyle=(\|h_{0}\|^{2}-\langle F(\mathbb{R}_{+})h_{0},h_{0}\rangle)+n\alpha,\quad n\geqslant 1.

2.1.1

(iii) Suppose, to the contrary, that

\mathrm{supp}(F)\subseteq\{0\}

C=0
.
According to (

\displaystyle=\|h\|^{2}+n\langle Bh,h\rangle+(n-1)\langle F(\{0\})h,h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I-F(\mathbb{R}_{+}))h,h\rangle)+n\langle(B+F(\mathbb{R}_{+})h,h\rangle,\quad h\in\mathcal{H},\,n\geqslant 1.

This implies that

(iv) Suppose, to the contrary, that

. Let

\displaystyle\geqslant\|h_{0}\|^{2}+n\langle Bh_{0},h_{0}\rangle+\int_{J_{\varepsilon}}Q_{n}(x)\langle F(\mathrm{d}x)h_{0},h_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-4.30554pt\overset{\eqref{rnx-1}}{\geqslant}\frac{n(1-(\theta+\varepsilon))-1}{(1-(\theta-\varepsilon))^{2}}\,\langle F(J_{\varepsilon})h_{0},h_{0}\rangle,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

.
∎

.

It follows from Definition

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\hat{\lambda}_{m}\hat{\lambda}_{n}}\;\langle\mathscr{B}_{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})e_{m},e_{n}\rangle

This yields

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{B}_{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{m}e_{0},W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{0}\rangle=0,\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\,m\neq n.

(2.2.5)

Denote by

the nonzero finite Borel measure on

(2.2.6)

for

\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\rho(\varDelta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\chi_{\varDelta}(t\E^{{\mathrm{i}\hskip 0.3014pt}\theta})\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(t),\quad\varDelta\in{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbb{C}),

(2.2.7)

\varDelta

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\mathrm{d}\rho=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(t\E^{{\mathrm{i}\hskip 0.3014pt}\theta})\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(t).

It follows from (

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{B}_{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{m}e_{0},W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}t^{m+n}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(t)\delta_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}t^{m+n}\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(t)\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\E^{{\mathrm{i}\hskip 0.3014pt}(m-n)\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\hskip-7.74998pt\overset{\eqref{rygtro}}{=}\int_{\mathbb{C}}z^{m}\bar{z}^{n}\mathrm{d}\rho(z),\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Summarizing, we have proved that

\displaystyle\langle\mathscr{B}_{2}(W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{m}e_{0},W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{0}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}z^{m}\bar{z}^{n}\mathrm{d}\rho(z),\quad m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Let

2.3.
Quasi-affine transform

According to the main result of this paper,
Theorem
1.1.2
, any CPD unilateral
weighted shift of type III (see
Definition
2.2.2
) is a quasi-affine
transform of a subnormal unilateral weighted
shift. However, this result does not answer
the question of whether a CPD unilateral
weighted shift, which is a quasi-affine
transform of a subnormal unilateral weighted
shift, is of type III. In this subsection,
we will answer this question in the
negative. Namely, we will prove, among other
things, that a non-subnormal CPD unilateral
weighted shift of type I or type II is a
quasi-affine transform of any contractive
subnormal unilateral weighted shift (see
Proposition
2.3.2
). Summarizing, this
means that any CPD unilateral weighted shift
is a quasi-affine transform of a subnormal
unilateral weighted shift.

We begin by showing that a CPD unilateral
weighted shift which is a quasi-affine
transform of a subnormal operator must be a
quasi-affine transform of a multiplication
operator by the independent variable on some

H^{2}(\tau)
. However, we have no guarantee
that such a measure
\tau
is
rotation-invariant (cf. Theorem
1.1.2
).

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Let
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
be a CPD
unilateral weighted shift,
S\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

be a subnormal operator,
X\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2},\mathcal{H})
be an operator with dense range such
that
XW_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=SX
. Then there exist a
compactly supported finite Borel measure

\tau
on
\mathbb{C}
and an operator
\widehat{X}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2},H^{2}(\tau))
with dense
range such that
\widehat{X}W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=M_{z}\widehat{X}
and
X^{*}X=\widehat{X}^{*}\widehat{X}
. In particular, if
X
is
quasi-invertible
(
resp. invertible
)
,
then so is
\widehat{X}
.

Proof.

Clearly
XW_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}^{n}e_{0}=S^{n}Xe_{0}
for all

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Thus we can assume that

f_{0}:=Xe_{0}\neq 0
. Since
X
has dense
range and
e_{0}
is cyclic for
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
,
f_{0}

is cyclic for
S
. The following argument is
well know (see e.g.,
[
27
, Subsections 9 and
14]
). If
N\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
is a
normal extension of
S
and
E
is its
spectral measure, then

\displaystyle\langle S^{n}f_{0},S^{m}f_{0}\rangle=\langle N^{n}f_{0},N^{m}f_{0}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\bar{z}^{m}z^{n}\mathrm{d}\tau(z),\quad m,n\geqslant 0,

where

\tau(\varDelta)=\langle E(\varDelta)f_{0},f_{0}\rangle

for
\varDelta\in{\mathfrak{B}}(\mathbb{C})
. Thus there
exists a unique unitary isomorphism
U\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},H^{2}(\tau))
such that
U(p(S)f_{0})=p
a.e.
[\tau]
for every
p\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. As in the proof of
Theorem
1.1.2
, we get
US=M_{z}U
. Set

\widehat{X}=UX\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2},H^{2}(\tau))
.
Then
\widehat{X}
has dense range,
\widehat{X}W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=M_{z}\widehat{X}
and
\widehat{X}^{*}\widehat{X}=X^{*}U^{*}UX=X^{*}X
.
∎

In contrast to Theorem
1.1.2
, the
following result shows that a single
non-subnormal CPD unilateral weighted shift
is a quasi-affine transform of a huge number
of subnormal unilateral weighted shifts.

Proposition 2.3.2
.

A non-subnormal CPD unilateral weighted
shift is a quasi-affine transform of any
contractive subnormal unilateral weighted
shift
(
including the unilateral
shift
)
. Moreover, a subnormal unilateral
weighted shift of type I or type II has norm

1
.

Proof.

Let
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
be a CPD
unilateral weighted shift with the
representing triplet
(\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{c},\nu)
.

Let us first consider the case where
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}

is not subnormal. Set

\displaystyle\varOmega

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\Big\{t\in\mathbb{R}\colon 1+tn+\mathfrak{c}n^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q_{n}\mathrm{d}\nu>0\Big\},

\displaystyle\underline{\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle=\inf\varOmega,

\displaystyle\vartheta

\displaystyle=\sup{\mathrm{supp}(\nu)},

\displaystyle\varGamma_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{(1-x)^{j}}\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\text{ (provided
the integral exists)},\quad j=1,2.

Recall that by
[
14
, Sec. 5]
,

3.2.2

Appendix A
Quasi-similarity versus similarity

The following lemma characterizes when,
given two unilateral weighted shifts, one of
them is a quasi-affine transform of the
other. For the sake of completeness, we will
sketch the proof (see

[
12
, Problem 90]
for the case of
similarity).

Lemma A.1
.

Let
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
and

W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
be unilateral
weighted shifts. Then the following
conditions are equivalent:

(i)

there exits
X\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})

such that
\overline{{\mathscr{R}}(X)}=\ell^{2}
and

XW_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}X
,

(ii)

W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is a quasi-affine transform of

W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
,

(iii)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\hat{\omega}_{n}}{\hat{\lambda}_{n}}<\infty
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Setting

a_{i,j}=\langle Xe_{j},e_{i}\rangle
for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}a_{i+1,j+1}=\langle XW_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}e_{j},e_{i+1}\rangle=\langle W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}Xe_{j},e_{i+1}\rangle=\omega_{i}a_{i,j},\quad i,j\geqslant 0.

(A.1)

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}a_{0,j+1}=0,\quad j\geqslant 0.

(A.2)

Using an induction argument, we infer from
(
A.1
) that

\displaystyle a_{n,n}=\sqrt{\frac{\hat{\omega}_{n}}{\hat{\lambda}_{n}}}a_{0,0},\quad n\geqslant 0.

(A.3)

Note that
a_{0,0}\neq 0
(because
otherwise, by (
A.2
),

\langle Xe_{j},e_{0}\rangle=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, so

e_{0}\perp\overline{{\mathscr{R}}(X)}=\ell^{2}
, a
contradiction). Then (
A.3
) yields

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\hat{\omega}_{n}}{\hat{\lambda}_{n}}\leqslant\frac{\|X\|^{2}}{|a_{0,0}|^{2}}
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) There exists a unique

X\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
such that

Xe_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{\hat{\omega}_{n}}{\hat{\lambda}_{n}}}e_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Clearly,

\mathscr{N}(X)=\{0\}
and

\overline{{\mathscr{R}}(X)}=\ell^{2}
. It is easy to
see that
XW_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}X
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Trivial.
∎

Now we show that for unilateral weighted
shifts the quasi-similarity and similarity

Theorem A.2
.

Let
W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
and

W_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})
. Then the

(i)

there exit operators
X,Y\in\boldsymbol{B}(\ell^{2})

W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and

(iii)

W_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and

Proof.

Implications (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) and
(ii)

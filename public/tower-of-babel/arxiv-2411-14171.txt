A rigorous Peierls-Onsager effective dynamics for semimetals in long-range magnetic fields.
Horia D. Cornean, Bernard Helffer, Radu Purice

Abstract
We consider periodic (pseudo)differential elliptic operators of Schrödinger type perturbed by weak magnetic fields not vanishing at infinity, and extend our previous analysis in [ 15 , 12 , 13 ] to the case of a semimetal having a finite family of Bloch eigenvalues whose range may overlap with the other Bloch bands but remains isolated at each fixed quasi-momentum. We do not make any assumption of triviality for the associated Bloch bundle. In this setting, we formulate a general form of the Peierls-Onsager substitution via strongly localized tight-frames and magnetic matrices. We also prove the existence of an approximate time evolution for initial states supported inside the range of the isolated Bloch family, with a precise error control.

1.1
Informal brief overview.

Let us explain in an informal way the results of this paper. The important feature shared by quantum periodic Hamiltonians
H
on a
d
-dimensional space is their Bloch structure. This means that they may be decomposed as a smooth family of self-adjoint operators (which we call fibre operators) acting on square integrable functions on the
d
-dimensional torus (
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) and indexed by the dual torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
(see the paragraph
2.1.1
). Moreover, each fibre operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
has a compact resolvent and thus an unbounded discrete spectrum
\big\{\lambda_{k}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,,\,k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\},\,\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}\big\}
. These
\lambda_{k}
, called the
Bloch levels
, are continuous functions on the dual torus whose graphs might cross among themselves and these crossings introduce singularities that may obscure the role of each individual level in the dynamics generated by the Hamiltonian
H
.

Nevertheless, in many situations there exists a group of Bloch levels which, although possibly crossing among themselves, stay well separated from the remaining infinite family of Bloch levels. We say in this case that we have an
isolated Bloch family
and we can associate with it a closed invariant subspace. We call it a
strictly isolated Bloch family
when its range is separated from the rest of the spectrum by some spectral gaps of the full operator
H
(the so called
gap condition
).

While the methods which we will develop in this paper are also capable of dealing with the gapped case, we are mostly interested in the more complicated situation in which the Bloch family stays isolated at each fixed quasi-momentum
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}
, but its range overlaps with the other bands, preventing
H
from developing a spectral gap, a case which is typical for metals and semimetals, see Figure
2.1.3
.

Physicists have inferred that for such an isolated Bloch family, the influence of a constant magnetic field on their dynamics, in a narrow energy window, could be simply described by applying the minimal coupling procedure to a corresponding Bloch level. Let us recall that the minimal coupling consists in considering that for a system described by a classical Hamiltonian
h(x,\xi)
with
x
the position variable and
\xi
the momentum variable, the influence of a magnetic field
B=dA
, with
A
its vector potential, may be described by replacing
\xi
with
\xi-A(x)
in the functional expression of the Hamiltonian
h(x,\xi)
and considering the dynamics generated by
h(x,\xi-A(x))
.
For a
simple isolated Bloch family
, i.e. having only one Bloch level, say
\lambda_{k_{0}}(\theta)
with
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, the above assumption leads to considering the dynamics associated with the Hamiltonian
\lambda_{k_{0}}(\xi-A(x))
, and this is the so-called Peierls-Onsager substitution for a non-degenerate, isolated Bloch level. Transforming this heuristic construction into a rigorous mathematical statement is a rather challenging problem with a long history. We will briefly comment on it in what follows, in order to better highlight the improvements added by our results.

The first main breakthrough in formulating a rigorous mathematical statement for the Peierls-Onsager substitution can be traced back at least to the papers by Helffer and Sjöstrand (
[
31
]
) and Nenciu (
[
48
]
) that have treated the case of one strictly isolated Bloch level with an associated trivial Bloch bundle, perturbed by a weak constant external magnetic field. Several natural questions remained:

•

How to consider isolated Bloch bands whose range overlap with others, hence when the gap is only ”local”
[
53
,
24
,
28
]
; in this case the Hamiltonian decomposition induced by the presence of the isolated gap is not associated to a spectral decomposition and the familiar Riesz projection construction cannot be used.

•

How to formulate a precise Peierls-Onsager substitution statement for an isolated multi-level Bloch band. Several possibilities may be imagined: to obtain a pure spectral result based on a kind of Grushin problem (see
[
29
,
39
]
), or to define an effective perturbed band Hamiltonian and even more, to try to replace the case with one level function by a matrix-valued effective Hamiltonian, even in the absence of rapidly decaying composite Wannier functions.

•

Another difficulty associated with the above remark comes from the possible non-triviality of the Bloch bundle associated to the isolated family, i.e. the non-existence of well localized composite Wannier functions. Conditions for the triviality of the Bloch bundle associated to an isolated Bloch family have been given in
[
27
,
16
,
51
,
35
]
, putting into evidence that non-triviality is related with the presence of periodic magnetic fields.

•

We note that a constant magnetic field perturbation has the important feature that the perturbed Hamiltonian commutes with a ’projective representation’ of the discrete translations (see
[
48
,
49
,
14
]
). In the presence of strongly localized composite Wannier functions for the unperturbed system, this projective representation allows one to introduce an algebra of infinite magnetic matrices (as in
[
48
,
49
]
) and even more, to associate a pseudo-differential calculus as in
[
31
,
55
]
(see also
[
4
]
for a
C^{*}
-algebraic point of view). When either the magnetic field perturbation is not constant, and/or strongly localized Wannier functions do not exist, these approaches do not work.

•

The case in which this magnetic
field
perturbation is generated by a smooth, bounded and slowly varying magnetic
potential
can be treated with space adiabatic perturbation theory as in
[
53
,
25
,
28
]
, up to any order in the perturbation.

In this paper we use the magnetic pseudo-differential calculus and the theory of strongly localized Parseval frames in order to obtain a general variant of the Peierls-Onsager substitution (see Theorem
2.14
and formula (
2.22
)) giving an answer to all the above mentioned problems. More precisely:

•

We allow the range of the isolated Bloch bands to overlap with the others, just like in
[
53
,
28
]
.

•

No ’slowly varying’ hypothesis is imposed to the perturbing magnetic field. Let us mention that under these general circumstances (no slow variation and no spectral gap), the
\epsilon^{2}
error in our Theorem
2.14
is a significant improvement of the existing results.

•

We do not ask the vector potential of the non-constant part of the magnetic field perturbation to be bounded.

•

A quite new aspect of our analysis is the use of strongly spatially localized Parseval frames in order to treat the non-trivial Bloch bundle case, when no rapidly decaying composite Wannier functions are present. In this sense, our approach is closer in spirit to the original Peierls-Onsager substitution
[
54
]
, providing a tight-binding Hofstadter-like matrix living in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{N}
for our effective magnetic band Hamiltonian. This result - to the best of our knowledge - has not been previously obtained for Bloch bundles with non-zero Chern numbers, where strongly localized Wannier bases do not exist. Moreover, the tight (Parseval) frame we construct is a result of independent interest.

•

Finally, let us mention that we could have also allowed a background constant magnetic field with a certain rational flux condition, but we decided not to, in order to simplify the already complicated setting and notation. We stress though that we work with a large class of elliptic (pseudo)differential symbols that may have topologically non-trivial projections.

2.3
The main result.

Our interest in this paper is to try to understand the behaviour of the restricted dynamics associated with an isolated Bloch family under the action of a weak long-range magnetic field.

In a first step, in Section
3
, using an argument based on the Feshbach-Schur inversion procedure, we identify a quasi-invariant subspace for
H^{\epsilon,\mathcal{c}}
associated with the energy window
J_{\mathfrak{B}}
in Hypothesis
2.8
and we construct an effective Hamiltonian that leaves this subspace invariant. An important fact is that this effective Hamiltonian approximates the spectrum of
H^{\epsilon,\mathcal{c}}
and its unitary time evolution in the quasi-invariant subspace up to an error of order
\epsilon^{2}
.
We emphasise that these results are proved without the use of Wannier functions, thus without a triviality condition on the sub-bundle defined by the isolated Bloch family
\mathfrak{B}
.

In order to obtain a matricial version of these results, even in the absence of exponentially localized Wannier functions, we shall construct strongly localized Parseval tight-frames instead, consisting of Schwartz functions, see Section
4
.

In fact, we would like to obtain a more refined result, by highlighting the effective Hamiltonian associated with the constant part
\epsilon\,B^{\bullet}
of the perturbing magnetic field, in a way extending to our situation the well-known Peierls-Onsager formula obtained by
[
49
,
31
]
for the case
N=0
with spectral gap, and in a matrix form by
[
15
]
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
with spectral gap and triviality of the Bloch bundle associated with the isolated Bloch family.

Our construction of the strongly localized Parseval frame starts from the following fact that we shall discuss in Subsection

(see Propositions

and
4.4

The Bloch-Floquet vector bundle.

Firstly, we notice that the Bloch-Floquet unitary transformation takes the canonical unitary action of
\mathcal{X}
by translations on
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
into the canonical unitary action by translations on each space
\mathscr{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}
defined in point (1) of Theorem
2.5
.

Secondly, let us identify the direct integral
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}d\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}\,\mathscr{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}
with a subspace of
L^{2}_{\text{\tt loc}}\big(\mathcal{X}^{*};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\big)
after multiplication with the smooth function
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}\times\mathcal{X}\ni(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*},x)\mapsto\,e^{-i<\mathfrak{s}_{*}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}),x>}\in\mathbb{S}
and of periodic functions, on
\mathcal{X}
or
\mathcal{X}^{*}
, with functions on the corresponding torus. One obtains a unitary equivalence of
\mathscr{F}
with the Hilbert space of functions

\mathscr{G}:=\big\{f\in L^{2}_{\text{\tt loc}}\big(\mathcal{X}^{*};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\big),\ f(\xi+\gamma^{*})=e^{-i<\gamma^{*},\cdot>}f(\xi)\big\}

endowed with the scalar product

\big(f\,,\,g\big)_{\mathscr{G}}:=\int_{\mathcal{B}}d\hat{\xi}\,\big(f(\hat{\xi})\,,\,g(\hat{\xi})\big)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,.

Under the above unitary transformation and after using on
\mathcal{X}^{*}=\Gamma^{*}\times\mathcal{B}
a similar decomposition to (
2.6
), the canonical representation of
\mathcal{X}^{*}
on
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
becomes:

\mathcal{X}^{*}\ni\xi\mapsto\hat{V}(\xi)\in\mathbb{U}(\mathscr{G}),\quad\big(\hat{V}(\xi)f\big)(\zeta,\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}):=e^{-i<\iota(\xi),\mathfrak{s}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}})>}\,f(\hat{\xi}+\zeta,\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}})

(4.1)

that is exactly
the equivariant representation
of
\mathcal{X}^{*}
induced by the following representation of its sub-group
\Gamma*\subset\mathcal{X}^{*}
,
[
42
]
:

\Gamma_{*}\ni\gamma^{*}\mapsto\hat{V}_{\circ}(\gamma^{*})\in\mathbb{U}\big(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\big),\quad\big(\hat{V}_{\circ}(\gamma^{*})f\big)(\zeta,\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}):=e^{-i<\gamma^{*},\mathfrak{s}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}})>}\,f(\zeta,\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}).

(4.2)

Thirdly, it is known that this equivariant representation may be identified with the representation by translations on sections of the vector bundle
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}:\mathfrak{F}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
associated with the principal bundle
\mathcal{X}^{*}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
with fibre isomorphic to
\Gamma_{*}
by its representation (
4.2
),
[
5
,
52
]
. Let us recall that the fibre of this representation may have the following identifications (by the above unitaries):

\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,\cong\,\mathscr{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\,\cong\,L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\,\cong\,L^{2}(\mathcal{E})

and let us denote by
\big(\cdot,\cdot\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}
the scalar product on this fibre.
Smoothness of sections with respect to the representation (
4.1
) of
\mathcal{X}^{*}
is equivalent to smoothness for the differentiable structure of the vector bundle
\mathfrak{F}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
.

Within the just introduced context, the family of projections
\big\{\widehat{P}_{\mathfrak{B}}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big\}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}
which builds up

in (

) defines a smooth sub-bundle
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}}\overset{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}}{\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow}\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
of finite rank

.
Let us denote by
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}};O)
the complex linear space of smooth sections of the vector bundle

over the open set

. We also introduce the notation

for the square integrable measurable sections of
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
over the open subset

.

We call the sub-bundle
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
trivializable if it exists an identification

For the case of a non-trivializable sub-bundle

(for example for a sub-bundle having a non-zero Chern class), we shall develop in what follows a technique based on tight-frames which are no longer orthonormal, but which are still smooth and form an over-complete system of generators (see

The main property of fibre bundles that we shall need in our construction is the following statement (Theorem 7.2 in Chapter 8 of

Theorem 4.1

(Embedding Theorem)
.

Given a smooth vector bundle
\mathfrak{B}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow M
of rank
n
over a smooth real manifold
M
of dimension
d
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
with

0\leq m\leq\,m_{d}:=\inf\{k\in\mathbb{N},\ k\geq(d/2)\}

and a smooth bundle homomorphism
\mathfrak{I}:\mathfrak{B}\rightarrow M\times\mathbb{C}^{n+m}
.

Applying this to our case where
n=N+1
and
M=\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
we have:

Corollary 4.2
.

There exist some
n_{\mathfrak{B}}\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}
with
N+1\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}\leq\,{N+1+m}
and a smooth bundle homomorphism
\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}
from
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}}
into
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}
that is isometric on each fibre.

Proposition 4.3
.

The application
\sigma\mapsto\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}\circ\sigma
defines an isometric map from
\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}},\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*})
into
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*};\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}})
and a continuous linear embedding
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}},\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*})\hookrightarrow\big[C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*})\big]^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}
.

Let us mention that explicit constructions of a smooth vector bundle homomorphism

with

may be given. The most elementary one is by using the usual covering of

with

open charts; this unfortunately has

which can be much larger then the optimal value

with

given by the above Embedding Theorem. In the non-trivializable case, where

and

,

4.2
The unperturbed projection

We have the following consequence of the abstract statement in Proposition
4.3
:

4.2.1
A strongly localized Parseval tight-frame for the range of
P_{\mathfrak{B}}

Proposition 4.4
.

There exists a family of
n_{\mathfrak{B}}
smooth global sections
\{\hat{\psi}_{p}\}_{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}
in the Bloch bundle
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}}\rightY\hskip-4.0pt\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
such that the closed complex linear space they generate is equal to
\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}},\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*})
and for any section
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}},\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*})
the following equalities hold:

\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,=\,\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\big(\hat{\psi}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,,\,\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}\hat{\psi}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}),\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*},

with the identity

\big\|\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big\|_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}^{2}\,=\,\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\big|\big(\hat{\psi}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,,\,\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}\big|^{2},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}.

Proof.

We notice that the bundle homomorphism
\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B}}:\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}
is in fact a smooth family of linear isometries
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}\ni\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}\mapsto\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\in\mathcal{L}\big(\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}),\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}\big)
.
For any
{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}
let:

\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{B}}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}):=\big\{\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\mathfrak{v}\,,\,\mathfrak{v}\in\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big\}\,\subset\,\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}},

(4.3)

so that
\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}:\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{B}}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})
is invertible and let
Q_{\mathfrak{B}}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}):\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{B}}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})
be the canonical orthogonal projection defined by this subspace.
If
\big\{\mathcal{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{e}_{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}\big\}
is the canonical orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}
, we can define the following global sections:

\hat{\psi}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}):=\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}^{-1}\big(Q_{\mathfrak{B}}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\mathcal{e}_{p}\big),\quad\forall(p,{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})\in\underline{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}.

(4.4)

Given any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}},\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*})
we can write that:

\displaystyle\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}^{-1}\big(Q_{\mathfrak{B}}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big)=\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}^{-1}\Big(Q_{\mathfrak{B}}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\Big(\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\big(\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,,\,\mathcal{e}_{p}\big)_{\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}}\,\mathcal{e}_{p}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\big(\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,,\,\mathcal{e}_{p}\big)_{\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}}\,\hat{\psi}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\big(Q_{\mathfrak{B}}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,,\,Q_{\mathfrak{B}}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\mathcal{e}_{p}\big)_{\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}}\,\hat{\psi}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})

\displaystyle=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\big(\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\,,\,\hat{\psi}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}\,\hat{\psi}_{p}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}).

In a similar way we notice that:

\displaystyle\big\|\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big\|_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\|\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}}^{2}=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\big|\big(Q_{\mathfrak{B}}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})\mathfrak{I}_{\mathfrak{B},\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}),\mathcal{e}_{p}\big)_{\mathbb{C}^{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\big|\big(\sigma(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}),\hat{\psi}_{p}\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}\big|^{2}.

∎

Let us map our
n_{\mathfrak{B}}
global smooth sections obtained in Proposition
4.4
into
n_{\mathfrak{B}}
rapidly decaying smooth functions in
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
by the inverse Bloch-Floquet transform (
2.8
):

\psi_{p}(\hat{x}+\gamma)\,:=\,(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}d{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\,e^{i<{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}},\gamma>}\,\hat{\psi}_{p}(\hat{x};{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}).

(4.5)

They define a well-localized finite-dimensional complex subspace of the isolated Bloch family subspace
P_{\mathfrak{B}}\,L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
. Moreover, for any
\alpha\in\Gamma
, the translated functions
\tau_{\alpha}\psi_{p}
verify the equalities:

\big(\mathfrak{U}_{F\Gamma}\tau_{\alpha}\psi_{p}\big)(\hat{x};{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}e^{-i<{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}},\gamma>}\,\psi_{p}(\hat{x}+\gamma+\alpha)=e^{i<{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}},\alpha>}\hat{\psi}_{p}(\hat{x};{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})\in\big[\widehat{P}_{\mathfrak{B}}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})\,\mathscr{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\big]

and we conclude that:

\Big\{\tau_{\alpha}\,\psi_{p},\ \alpha\in\Gamma,\ 1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}\Big\}\,\subset\,P_{\mathfrak{B}}\,L^{2}(\mathcal{X})\,.

Proposition 4.5
.

The family
\Big\{\tau_{\alpha}\,\psi_{p},\ \alpha\in\Gamma,\ 1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}\Big\}
defines a Parseval tight-frame (see Definition
B.1
) for the subspace
P_{\mathfrak{B}}\,L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
associated with the isolated Bloch family
\mathfrak{B}
.

Proof.

First let us prove that
P_{\mathfrak{B}}\,L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
is the linear span of the family
\Big\{\tau_{\alpha}\,\psi_{p},\ \alpha\in\Gamma,\ 1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}\Big\}
over
\mathbb{C}
. Thus let us choose any
f\in P_{\mathfrak{B}}\,L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
and consider its Bloch-Floquet transform:

\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}(\hat{x}):=\big(\mathfrak{U}_{F\Gamma}f\big)(\hat{x},{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})=\big(\mathfrak{U}_{F\Gamma}P_{\mathfrak{B}}f\big)(\hat{x},{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})=\hat{P}_{\mathfrak{B}}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})\big[\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}(\hat{x})\big]

that defines a bounded measurable global section
\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}\ni\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}\mapsto\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{E})\simeq\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})
and thus an element in
\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{F}_{\mathfrak{B}};\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*})
.
Using Proposition
4.4
we can write that:

\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\big(\hat{\psi}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})\,,\,\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\big)_{\mathscr{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}}\hat{\psi}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})

and consequently:

\displaystyle f(\hat{x}+\gamma)\,

\displaystyle=\,\big(\mathfrak{U}_{F\Gamma}^{-1}\hat{f}\big)(\hat{x}+\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}d{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\,e^{i<{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}},\gamma>}\,\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}d{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\,e^{i<{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}},\gamma>}\,\big(\hat{\psi}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})\,,\,\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}\hat{\psi}_{p}(\hat{x},{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})

\displaystyle=\,\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\underset{\alpha\in\Gamma}{\sum}\,\big[\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}\big(\hat{\psi}_{p}(\cdot)\,,\,\hat{f}\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\cdot)}\big](\alpha)\,\psi_{p}(\hat{x}+\gamma-\alpha).

For
(\alpha,p)\in\Gamma\times\underline{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}
we introduce the notation:

\mathbf{\Phi}_{\mathfrak{B}}(f)_{\alpha,p}:=\big[\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}\big(\hat{\psi}_{p}(\cdot)\,,\,\hat{f}\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\cdot)}\big](\alpha)

and conclude that:

f=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\underset{\alpha\in\Gamma}{\sum}\,\mathbf{\Phi}_{\mathfrak{B}}(f)_{\alpha,p}\,[\tau_{-\alpha}\psi_{p}].

In order to prove that it is a Parseval frame let us compute now the scalar products:
\big(\tau_{-\alpha}\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{X})}
for some
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
and some indices
(\alpha,p)\in\Gamma\times\underline{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}
:

\displaystyle\big(\tau_{-\alpha}\psi_{p},f\big)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}\int_{\mathcal{E}}d\hat{x}\,f(\hat{x}+\gamma)\,\overline{\psi_{p}(\hat{x}+\gamma-\alpha)}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}d{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\,e^{i<{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}},\alpha>}\int_{\mathcal{E}}d\hat{x}\,\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}(\hat{x})\,\overline{\hat{\psi}_{p}(\hat{x},{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}d{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\,e^{i<{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}},\alpha>}\,\big(\hat{\psi}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})\,,\,\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\big)_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}_{*}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*})}=\mathbf{\Phi}_{\mathfrak{B}}(f)_{\alpha,p}.

Using once again Proposition
4.4
we obtain finally:

\displaystyle\big\|f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\sum}\int_{\mathcal{E}}d\hat{x}\,\big|f(\hat{x}+\gamma)\big|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}d{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\,\big\|\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\big\|_{\mathscr{F}_{{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}_{*}}d{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\Big(\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\big|\big(\hat{\psi}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}})\,,\,\hat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}\big)_{\mathscr{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{z}}^{*}}}\big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\underset{\alpha\in\Gamma}{\sum}\Big|\mathbf{\Phi}_{\mathfrak{B}}(f)_{\alpha,p}\Big|^{2}=\underset{1\leq p\leq n_{\mathfrak{B}}}{\sum}\,\underset{\alpha\in\Gamma}{\sum}\Big|\big(\tau_{-\alpha}\psi_{p},f\big)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{X})}\Big|^{2}

and we notice that our map
\mathbf{\Phi}
is in fact the coordinate map associated with the Parseval frame
\{\tau_{-\alpha}\psi_{p}\}_{(\alpha,p)\in\Gamma\times\underline{n_{\mathfrak{B}}}}
as recalled in (
B.3
) in Appendix
B
.
∎

Remark 4.10

Appendix B
Some basic properties of frames on Hilbert spaces

In the whole section,
{\cal H}
denotes a complex Hilbert space.

Definition B.1
.

1.

We call frame of
{\cal H}
, an at most countable family of vectors
\big\{\psi_{p}\,,\,p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}\big\}\subset{\cal H}
such that there exists two positive constants
0<A\leq B
satisfying:

A\big\|f\|_{{\cal H}}^{2}\,\leq\,\underset{M\nearrow\infty}{\lim}\underset{1\leq p\leq M}{\sum}\big|\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)_{{\cal H}}\big|^{2}\,\leq\,B\big\|f\|_{{\cal H}}^{2},\quad\forall f\in{\cal H}.

(B.1)

2.

A frame of
{\cal H}
is called a Parseval frame, when the following equality is verified for any
f\in{\cal H}
:

\big\|f\big\|^{2}_{{\cal H}}=\underset{p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}{\sum}\,\big|\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)_{{\cal H}}\big|^{2}\,,

(B.2)

with the series converging in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{\bullet})
.

3.

Given a frame
\big\{\psi_{p}\,,\,p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}\big\}\subset{\cal H}
we introduce its coordinate map
\mathfrak{C}
as:

{\cal H}\ni f\mapsto\big(\mathfrak{C}f):=\{\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)_{{\cal H}}\big\}_{p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}).

(B.3)

Lemma B.2
.

Given a Parseval frame in
{\cal H}
, the following identities are valid, for
(f,g)\in{\cal H}\times{\cal H}
:

\big(f\,,\,g\big)_{{\cal H}}=\underset{p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}{\sum}\,\big(f,\psi_{p}\big)_{{\cal H}}\big(\psi_{p},g\big)_{{\cal H}},\qquad f=\underset{p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}{\sum}\,\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)_{{\cal H}}\,\psi_{p}\,,

(B.4)

with the series converging in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{\bullet})
and resp. in the strong topology of
{\cal H}
and the coordinate map
\mathfrak{C}:{\cal H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{\bullet})
is an isometry that may not be surjective.

Proof.

Let us write the polarization identity in
{\cal H}
:

\big(f\,,\,g\big)_{{\cal H}}=(1/4)\underset{0\leq k\leq 3}{\sum}i^{k}\big\|f+(-i)^{k}g\big\|^{2}_{{\cal H}}\,,

and use (
B.2
) to obtain:

\displaystyle\big(f\,,\,g\big)_{{\cal H}}

\displaystyle=(1/4)\underset{0\leq k\leq 3}{\sum}i^{k}\big\|f+(-i)^{k}g\big\|^{2}_{{\cal H}}=(1/4)\underset{0\leq k\leq 3}{\sum}i^{k}\underset{p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}{\sum}\,\big|\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f+(-i)^{k}g\big)_{{\cal H}}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\underset{p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}{\sum}\,(2/4)\Big(2\Re\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)_{{\cal H}}\overline{\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,g\big)}_{{\cal H}}-2\Im\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)_{{\cal H}}\overline{\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,g\big)}_{{\cal H}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\underset{p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}{\sum}\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,g\big)_{{\cal H}}\overline{\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)}_{{\cal H}}.

For the second identity one proves using the first identity that
\sum\limits_{p=1}^{M}\,\big(\psi_{p}\,,\,f\big)_{{\cal H}}\psi_{p}
is a sequence converging weakly to
f
and being Cauchy for the strong topology, due to the Parseval condition.
∎

We also recall the following theorem in
[
7
]
(Theorem 5.5.1.)

Theorem B.3
.

A sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
{\cal H}
is a frame for
{\cal H}
if and only if the application:

\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\mapsto{\text{\tt{\bf F}}}\big(\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\big)\,:=\,\underset{k\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}{\sum}c_{\ell}\,f_{\ell}

is a well defined surjective map from
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{\bullet})
onto
{\cal H}
.

Remark B.4
.

If we denote by
\{\mathcal{e}_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
the canonical orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{\bullet})
and use the notation introduced in the begining of section
4
we have the identities:

\mathfrak{C}=\underset{p\in\mathbb{N}_{\bullet}}{\sum}\mathcal{e}_{\mathfrak{p}}\bowtie\psi_{p},\qquad\text{I}\hskip-2.0pt\text{I{\sf d}}_{{\cal H}}=\mathfrak{C}^{*}\mathfrak{C}.

(B.5)

and for any linear bounded operator

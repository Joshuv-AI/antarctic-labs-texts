SBH-ellipticity of the relaxed interfacial energy density in the context of second-order structured deformations
Ana Cristina Barroso, José Matias, Elvira Zappale

Abstract
. Starting from an energy comprised of both a bulk term and a surface term, set in the space of special functions of bounded hessian, S ​ B ​ H SBH , a relaxation problem in the context of second-order structured deformations was studied in [ 15 ] . It was shown, via the global method for relaxation, that the relaxed functional admits an integral representation and the relaxed energy densities were identified. In this paper we show that, under certain hypotheses on the original densities, the corresponding relaxed energy densities verify the same type of growth conditions and the surface energy density satisfies a specific “convexity-type” property, i.e. it is S ​ B ​ H SBH -elliptic. MSC (2020): 26B25, 49J45 Keywords: global method for relaxation, second-order structured deformations, relaxed surface energy density, S ​ B ​ H SBH -ellipticity



1.
Introduction

Structured deformations were introduced by Del Piero and Owen in
[
10
]
in order to provide a multi-scale geometrical setting to describe the fields occurring in continuum mechanics, decomposing them into two parts: one arising from smooth changes at smaller length scales and another part due to disarrangements (such as slips and separations) occurring at smaller length scales. The portion generated by the part of deformation without disarrangements is computed as a limit (in an appropriate sense) of gradients of approximating deformations, and the portion generated by disarrangements corresponds to the effects of jumps in the approximating deformations. Adopting the same point of view as in
[
10
]
, Owen and Paroni in
[
17
]
extended the principal concepts and results of the above theory to include the effects of limits of second gradients and jumps in the first gradients of approximating deformations. In particular, this enriched theory allows to take into account bending and torsion effects.

In
[
15
]
,
the authors address second-order structured deformations, in the context of special functions of bounded Hessian,
SBH
, with the main aim of describing their energetic behaviour (we refer to Section
2
for more details and for the rigorous framework of first-order structured deformations and their second-order variants). In particular, in
[
15
]
the energy assigned to a second-order structured deformation was defined and computed, in terms of relaxation, departing from a hyper-elastic energy that also depends on second-order distributional gradients.
Indeed, an integral representation result for a quite general class of energy functionals satisfying standard properties (see Section
3
for more details) was derived, in the spirit of the global method for relaxation, introduced by Bouchitté, Fonseca and Mascarenhas in
[
6
]
, and later extended in
[
7
]
.
The limiting energy, obtained via the above-mentioned relaxation procedure, is described in terms of suitable densities, given by means of certain formulas (see Section
3
for the precise expressions), but no properties enjoyed by these densities have been discussed.

In this work, departing from a specific model, as a particular case of some applications considered in
[
15
]
, and having in mind the characterisation of the relaxed surface energy in the context of (first-order) structured deformations obtained by Šilhavý in
[
19
]
,
we prove the
SBH
-ellipticity of the corresponding relaxed surface energy density.

More precisely, we consider, over an open and bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the family of second-order structured deformations

SD_{2}(\Omega):=SBH(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}_{sym}^{d\times N\times N})

where
\mathbb{R}_{sym}^{d\times N\times N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times N\times N}
denotes the set of tensors

(M_{ijk}),i\in\{1,\ldots d\},j,k,\in\{1,\ldots N\},
such that

M_{ijk}=M_{ikj}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots d\},j,k\in\{1,\dots N\}.

Let
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
denote the family of open subsets of
\Omega
and let

be the functional defined for each

by

F_{0}(u;A):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{A}f_{0}(\nabla^{2}u(x))\,dx+\int_{A}f_{1}(x,u(x),\nabla u(x))\,dx&\\
\hskip 42.67912pt+\displaystyle\int_{S(\nabla u)\cap A}g_{0}([\nabla u](x),\nu_{\nabla u}(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x),&\mbox{if }u\in SBH(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the densities

satisfy the following hypotheses:

is continuous and satisfies

for all

\mathcal{F}(u,U;A):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}F_{0}(u_{n};A):u_{n}\to u\;\text{in}\;W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nabla^{2}u_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}U\;\text{in}\;\mathcal{M}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N\times N}_{s})\right\}.

An integral representation for

Fields of Bounded Hessian

The space of fields with bounded hessian, introduced in

13

, is defined as

\displaystyle BH(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):D(\nabla u)\hbox{ is a bounded Radon measure }\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}):Du\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})\right\},

.
The space

is endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{BH(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\|u\|_{W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|D^{2}u|(\Omega),

Given

,
taking into account that

D(\nabla u)=\nabla^{2}u\mathcal{L}^{N}+D^{s}(\nabla u)=\nabla^{2}u\mathcal{L}^{N}+[\nabla u]\otimes\nu_{\nabla u}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\lfloor S_{\nabla u}+D^{c}(\nabla u).

Due to the symmetry of the distribution

, in the previous expression,
\nabla^{2}u\mathcal{L}^{N}
is a vector-valued measure, in the space of

, that is absolutely continuous with respect to

and with

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}u=\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}=\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{j}},\;1\leqslant i\leqslant d,\;1\leqslant j,k\leqslant N,

S_{\nabla u}
denotes the singular set of

,

Again by the symmetry of

The space

.

2.4.
Structured Deformations

First-order structured deformations were introduced by Del Piero and Owen
[
10
]
in order to provide a mathematical framework that captures the effects at the macroscopic level of smooth deformations and of non-smooth deformations (disarrangements) at one sub-macroscopic level.
In the classical theory of mechanics, the deformation of the body is characterised exclusively by the macroscopic deformation field
g
and its gradient
\nabla g
.
In the framework of structured deformations, an additional geometrical field
G
is introduced with the intent to capture the contribution at the macroscopic scale of smooth sub-macroscopic changes, while the difference
\nabla g-G
captures the contribution at the macroscopic scale of non-smooth sub-macroscopic changes, such as slips and separations (referred to as
disarrangements
in
[
11
]
). The field
G
is called the deformation without disarrangements, and, heuristically, the disarrangement tensor
M\coloneqq\nabla g-G
is an indication of how “non-classical” a structured deformation is.
This broad theory is rich enough to address mechanical phenomena such as elasticity, plasticity and the behaviour of crystals with defects.

The variational formulation for first-order structured deformations in the
SBV
setting was first addressed by Choksi and Fonseca
[
8
]
where a (first-order) structured deformation is defined to be a pair

(g,G)\in SBV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}),\;p\geqslant 1.

Departing from
a functional which associates to any deformation
u
of the body an energy featuring a bulk contribution, which measures the deformation (gradient) throughout the whole body, and an interfacial contribution, accounting for the energy needed to fracture the body, an integral representation for the “most economical way” to approach a given structured deformation was derived.
The theory of first-order structured deformations was broadened by Owen and Paroni
[
17
]
to second-order structured deformations, which also account for other geometrical changes, such as curvature, at one sub-macroscopic level. The variational formulation in the
SBV^{2}
setting for second-order structured deformations was carried out by Barroso, Matias, Morandotti and Owen
[
3
]
. This formulation allows for jumps on both the approximating fields, as well as on their gradients. A formulation in
BH
, allowing for jumps only in the gradients was carried out by Fonseca, Hagerty and Paroni
[
15
]
.

for every

,
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
and for every piecewise constant function

such that

in

\partial Q_{\nu}\cap\{x\cdot\nu>0\}

\partial Q_{\nu}\cap\{x\cdot\nu<0\}
.

Observe that when

, then Definition

On the other hand, when dealing with fields which are a single pure jump,

Remark 2.3

In view of

A function

\Phi(a,b,\nu)\leqslant\liminf_{n}\int_{Q_{\nu}\cap S_{w_{n}}}\Phi(w_{n}^{+},w_{n}^{-},\nu_{w_{n}})\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1},

\partial Q_{\nu}\cap\{x\cdot\nu>0\}

\partial Q_{\nu}\cap\{x\cdot\nu<0\}

Definition 2.4

Let
\mathcal{P}:=\left\{(\xi,\eta,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}:\xi-\eta=\zeta\otimes\nu,\mbox{ for some }\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

A continuous function

\varphi(\xi,\eta,\nu)\leqslant\liminf_{n}\int_{Q_{\nu}\cap S_{\nabla u_{n}}}\varphi((\nabla u_{n})^{+},(\nabla u_{n})^{-},\nu_{\nabla u_{n}})\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1},

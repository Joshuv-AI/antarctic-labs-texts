Determining the usage preferences and expectations of postpartum women regarding mobile applications developed for the postpartum period
Authors: Burcu Dişli Oktar, Nazire Bahar, Ümran Oskay
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe postpartum period is a critical time when women need information and support for both their own health and newborn care. Mobile health applications can significantly improve access to information during the postpartum period. However, the effectiveness of such applications depends on understanding postpartum women’s usage preferences and expectations. Existing evidence on postpartum mobile health applications is largely derived from studies conducted in South Asian and East African contexts, highlighting the need for context-specific data from Türkiye. This study aimed to determine the usage preferences and expectations of postpartum women regarding mobile applications developed for the postpartum period.MethodsThis descriptive study was conducted with 385 postpartum women in Istanbul, Türkiye. Data were collected between October 2024 and February 2025 using a structured Descriptive Information Form prepared by the researchers. The form included items related to sociodemographic characteristics, obstetric characteristics, and opinions regarding the use of mobile applications during the postpartum period. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, chi-square and multivariate logistic regression analysis.ResultsWillingness to use mobile applications was significantly associated with age, educational level, income status, parity, and mode of birth. The most frequently requested application content included newborn care (45.5%), common newborn problems and their management (42.9%), and emergency situations related to maternal and newborn health (40.5%). Participants also emphasized the importance of applications providing reliable, clear, and up-to-date information.ConclusionPostpartum women have specific preferences and expectations regarding mobile health applications designed for the postpartum period. Developing user-centered mobile applications that prioritize newborn care and emergency-related content may support postpartum health services and enhance maternal and newborn health outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-09117-x.

Introduction

Mobile health applications have become effective tools for supporting maternal and infant health. They provide individualized education and counseling during the postpartum period and improve access to healthcare services in a cost-efficient manner [1]. These applications also have the potential to address the physical, emotional, and social needs women experience during the postpartum period. Accordingly, numerous mobile health applications have been developed across diverse cultural contexts to promote maternal and newborn health [2, 3]. However, despite the availability of these applications, studies examining women’s perceptions, usage preferences, and expectations are still limited [4]. In addition, a substantial proportion of the existing evidence in this field has been derived from studies conducted among women living in South Asian and East African countries [2]. Considering that women’s postpartum needs and expectations from mobile applications may vary across cultural and social contexts, context-specific evidence from Türkiye is essential. Such evidence helps expand the existing literature, which is currently largely based on South Asian and East African samples.

The present study examines the experiences and expectations of women living in a specific region of Türkiye regarding mobile health applications during the postpartum period. The findings may contribute to a better understanding of postpartum women’s needs, expectations, and preferences regarding mobile health applications within the sociocultural context of Türkiye. In this respect, the study provides context-specific evidence to support the development of user-centered and accessible mobile health applications for postpartum women.

Background

The postpartum period refers to the first six weeks following childbirth. It represents a multidimensional transitional phase that involves not only the woman’s physical recovery but also her psychological, social, and emotional adjustment [5]. During this period, postpartum women face various stressors such as hormonal changes, sleep disturbances, breastfeeding difficulties, social isolation, and increased caregiving responsibilities [6]. In first-time mothers, lack of knowledge and the process of adapting to the parenting role increase the need for psychological support [7].

Despite the increasing need for support during the postpartum period, short hospital stays after birth and time constraints during postpartum follow-up services provided at family health centres restrict the scope of care. In addition, difficulties in accessing healthcare professionals in rural areas and the standardized structure of counselling services may further limit women’s access to the support they need. Some studies have reported that postpartum mothers perceive the support systems available to them as inadequate and state that they do not receive sufficient education and counselling. They have also experienced difficulties in accessing the information they need in a timely manner [8]. In this context, it has been highlighted in the literature that women increasingly seek information through digital platforms. However, the accuracy of much of this information remains questionable [9–11].

Recent advances in digital health technologies, particularly the use of mobile applications in healthcare, have made postpartum care more accessible and personalised. Mobile applications enhance women’s health literacy. They also support women’s active engagement in care processes. These applications offer multiple functions, such as rapid access to information, symptom tracking, psychological support, breastfeeding guidance, and opportunities for social interaction [3]. Although digital health platforms have become widespread in Türkiye, the number of women-centered mobile applications specifically designed for the postpartum period remains quite limited [12].

Studies in the literature indicate that mobile applications may positively influence parental self-efficacy, perceived social support, parenting satisfaction, breastfeeding self-efficacy, sleep quality during the postpartum period, and intention to use postpartum contraception. They may also be effective in preventing postpartum constipation, prolonged lochia, and reducing depressive symptoms [13–17]. However, the effectiveness of postpartum mobile applications varies depending on their content, user-friendly design, and cultural appropriateness [6, 18]. Especially in low and middle-income countries, the accessibility, sustainability, and alignment of these applications with user expectations remain subjects of ongoing debate [19]. In addition, a substantial proportion of the existing evidence in this field appears to be based on studies conducted among women in South Asian and East African countries [2]. This may limit the direct generalizability of these findings to different sociocultural contexts, such as Türkiye. Furthermore, most existing applications feature standardized content. Therefore, they may not adequately address the individual needs and cultural contexts of postpartum women [20].

The Health Belief Model suggests that health-related actions are influenced by individuals’ personal perceptions of the risks associated with a health problem and the benefits of avoiding those risks. Identifying these perceptions can increase the likelihood that individuals engage in health-promoting and health-maintaining behaviors. In this context, particularly during the postpartum period, women’s need for information and support regarding maternal and newborn health may shape their preferences for using mobile applications designed to promote and sustain these behaviors. According to the Health Belief Model, women have varying information needs regarding maternal and newborn health during the postpartum period [21]. Their preferences for using mobile applications and their expectations from these applications may affect whether and how they use postpartum-related mobile health tools. Perceptions such as believing that applications provide rapid access to information or can be personalized may be considered perceived benefits [3]. In contrast, perceptions such as considering the application content insufficient, finding it difficult to use, or believing that it is not economically accessible may act as perceived barriers [19]. Therefore, examining postpartum women’s preferences and expectations regarding mobile health applications is important for understanding the perceptions and behaviors that shape their use. However, despite this theoretical foundation, the literature reveals a notable lack of studies that systematically evaluate user expectations and collect data that would inform content design [15, 22]. Particularly in the context of Türkiye, comprehensive research examining postpartum women’s mobile health application usage habits, information-seeking behaviors, and content preferences is limited. This gap represents an important research need for both academic knowledge and healthcare practice [23, 24].

The primary aim of this study is to comprehensively examine postpartum women’s usage preferences for mobile health applications developed specifically for the postpartum period, as well as their expectations from these applications. Within this scope, the study will assess postpartum women’s preferred information sources and the factors influencing these preferences. It will also evaluate the use and willingness to use postpartum-specific mobile applications, as well as the reasons and purposes for using such applications.

Methods

Study design and participants

This descriptive cross-sectional study was planned and reported in accordance with the STROBE Statement. The study population consisted of postpartum women aged 18 and over, literate, voluntarily willing to participate, and hospitalized in the obstetrics ward of Istanbul Sancaktepe Şehit Prof. Dr. İlhan Varank Training and Research Hospital in Istanbul, Türkiye, between October 2024 and February 2025. Women with mental or psychological disorders and those beyond six weeks postpartum were excluded from the study. The sample size was determined using a known population sample size calculation formula. In this formula, the population size (N) was set at 4128, which corresponds to the number of births that occurred in the hospital over the course of one year; the z-value (t) was 1.96 for a 95% confidence level; the probability of the event occurring (p) was 0.50; the probability of the event not occurring (q) was 0.50; and the acceptable margin of error (d) was 0.05. According to these parameters, the minimum required sample size was calculated as 352. Considering that data loss might occur during the data collection process, the sample size was increased by 10%, and the final sample size was determined as 387. During the data collection process, 401 postpartum women were interviewed. Fourteen postpartum women were not included in the study because they did not wish to participate. Of the 387 postpartum women included in the study, two were excluded because they discontinued the interview, and the study was completed with 385 postpartum women. The sample was selected using a non-probability convenience sampling method. Within this scope, postpartum women were identified according to the inpatient list of the ward on the days of data collection, and those who met the inclusion criteria and volunteered to participate in the study were included in the sample sequentially.

Data collection tools

Descriptive information form

The form was prepared by the researchers for this study based on the literature and consists of two sections: the Descriptive Section and the Mobile Application Section. The first section, comprising 36 questions, includes demographic data, obstetric information, the newborn’s health status, and the postpartum knowledge level. The second section, consisting of 19 questions, addresses postpartum women’s mobile application usage, their willingness or unwillingness to use postpartum mobile applications, and the information they expect to find within these applications [23, 25] (see Additional file 1).

Data collection

Written and verbal informed consent was obtained from postpartum women who agreed to participate in the study. Subsequently, data were collected through face-to-face interviews using the “Descriptive Information Form” in a designated private room, with all interviews conducted during the participants’ postpartum hospitalization, each lasting approximately 20 min. To ensure standardization during data collection, a researcher specialized in women’s health nursing was assigned.

Variables

The variables included in the study were grouped under three main categories: demographic and obstetric characteristics, postpartum preferences for receiving information, and postpartum mobile application use and preferences. The dependent variables were defined as perceived level of postpartum knowledge, mobile application usage status, willingness to use a mobile application, and preferences for receiving postpartum information. Perceived knowledge level, mobile application usage, and willingness to use were measured with “Yes” or “No” responses. Preferences for receiving information were obtained by selecting one or more options such as face-to-face, mobile application, website, and booklet.

Independent variables included age, educational level, income level, parity, mode of the last birth, and newborn health status at birth. Age (< 30 years/≥30 years), educational level (primary school, secondary school, high school, undergraduate), income level (low, middle, high), parity (primiparous/multiparous), mode of the last birth (vaginal/cesarean), and newborn health status at birth (healthy/unhealthy) were evaluated as categorical variables.

Data analysis

Since the research data were collected through face-to-face interviews, there were no missing data. The obtained data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics Version 29.0. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables were presented as frequency and percentage and analyzed using chi-square and Fisher’s exact tests. For significant chi-square results, the estimation of effect size was evaluated using the Phi coefficient (φ) for 2 × 2 tables. Multivariate logistic regression analysis using the enter method was performed to examine the effects of all independent variables on the willingness to use postpartum mobile applications. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Bias

This study was conducted at a single centre in Istanbul using a convenience sampling method based on voluntary participation. This may have led to selection bias by including participants with specific socioeconomic, cultural, or geographical characteristics, and it may also limit the generalisability of the findings. To minimise selection bias, all eligible postpartum women hospitalised in the maternity ward on the days of data collection were informed about the study, and those who agreed to participate were included voluntarily. In addition, as the data were collected through face-to-face interviews, there is a potential risk of reporting bias due to social desirability, as participants may have tended to provide socially acceptable responses. To reduce this, a structured questionnaire was used, and participants were assured that their responses would remain confidential and would not affect the care they received.

Ethical considerations

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Istanbul University-Cerrahpaşa Social and Human Sciences Research Ethics Committee (Date: 02.01.2024, No: 2024/34). Institutional permission was obtained from the Provincial Health Directorate to conduct the study in the affiliated hospital (Date: 13.09.2024, No: 2024/14). Informed consent was obtained from all participating postpartum women.

Results

Descriptive characteristics of the participants

The mean age of the postpartum women who participated in the study was 27.04 ± 4.78. The mean postpartum day of the participants was 1.81 ± 0.67. Most participants were married (99.2%), had a high school education or above (69.4%), had a middle or high income level (77.7%), were primiparous (54.5%), gave birth at term (97.7%), had a vaginal delivery (61.6%), and had a healthy newborn at birth (92.2%) (Table 1).

Information receiving status and preferences regarding the postpartum period

A majority of postpartum women (84.9%) reported receiving information specifically related to postpartum maternal and newborn care. This information was most frequently obtained from close friends and relatives (57.1%) and healthcare professionals (39.5%), while digital sources, including mobile applications (14.5%) and websites (13.5%), were used less often. Consistent with this, women most preferred to receive information face-to-face (59%), with mobile applications (48.1%) being the second most preferred method, regarding postpartum maternal and newborn care (Table 2).

Preferences for receiving information regarding the postpartum period and associated factors

Significant but weak associations were found between the preference for receiving information face-to-face and age (p < 0.05, φ = 0.121) and education level (p < 0.05, φ=-0.165). Preferences for receiving information via mobile applications or websites were significantly associated with age, education level, parity, and mode of the last birth. Most associations were weak (p < 0.05, φ range = -0.195 to 0.216), while a moderate association was observed between website preference and parity. The preference for receiving information via booklets was significantly associated with education level (p < 0.05, φ=-0.120). Women under the age of 30 and those with a high school education or above were less likely to prefer face-to-face information and more likely to prefer mobile applications or websites. Similarly, primiparous women and those who had a vaginal birth were more likely to prefer it via mobile applications or websites (p < 0.05) (Table 3).

Perceived knowledge level regarding the postpartum period, preferences for mobile application use, and associated factors

Among the postpartum women in the study, 14.8% reported using a mobile application related to the postpartum period, and 88.3% expressed a willingness to use one. Perceiving their knowledge of postpartum maternal and newborn care as sufficient was significantly associated with age, education level, parity, and newborn health status at birth. The associations were moderate for age and parity, and weak for education level and newborn health status (p < 0.05, φ range =-0.264 to 0.244). The use of a mobile application during the postpartum period was significantly associated with age (p < 0.05, φ=-0.125) and parity (p < 0.05, φ = 0.101), with weak associations. Willingness to use a postpartum mobile application was associated with age, education level, income level, parity, and mode of the last birth. These associations were moderate for income level and parity, and weak for the other variables (p < 0.05, φ range =-0.172 to 0.220). Women aged 30 and over, those with less than a high school education, multiparous women, and those who had a healthy newborn at birth were more likely to perceive their knowledge of postpartum maternal and newborn care as sufficient. Primiparous women were more likely to use postpartum mobile applications, whereas women under the age of 30, those with high school education or above, primiparous women, and those who had a vaginal birth were more willing to use such applications (p < 0.05) (Table 4).

Factors influencing the willingness to use mobile applications: regression results

According to the results of the multivariate logistic regression analysis, certain variables significantly influenced the willingness to use mobile applications related to postpartum maternal and newborn care. Regarding income level, individuals in the middle and high income groups were found to be 2.905 times more likely to be willing to use mobile applications compared to those in the low income group (β = 1.066, p < 0.05). In terms of parity, primiparous individuals were 3.033 times more likely to express a willingness to use mobile applications compared to multiparous individuals (β = 1.110, p < 0.05) (Table 5).

Reasons for willingness or unwillingness to use mobile applications and expected content

When asked about the reasons for willingness or unwillingness to use mobile applications related to maternal and newborn care in the postpartum period, the most common reasons for willingness were monitoring maternal and newborn health (52.5%), organizing maternal and newborn care (30.4%), consulting healthcare professionals (29.9%). Conversely, the most frequently reported reasons for unwillingness were perceiving no need for a mobile application (7.3%) and lack of time (4.2%). The topics that postpartum women most frequently wished to see included in such applications were newborn care (45.5%), common newborn problems (42.9%), and emergencies related to the mother and newborn (40.5%) (Table 6).

Discussion

Many mobile applications related to the postpartum period have been developed and continue to be developed [26, 27]. However, these applications mostly contain standardised content and do not adequately focus on the individual expectations and needs of postpartum women [17]. In this context, this study revealed postpartum women’s preferences for receiving information related to maternal and newborn care, the factors associated with these preferences, their preferences for using mobile applications, and their expectations regarding such applications.

Most postpartum women receive information specific to the postpartum period and prefer different sources for obtaining this information [28, 29]. Among the most frequently preferred sources of information are healthcare professionals, close friends and relatives, and the internet [30, 31]. Additionally, the information sources most valued for their reliability are the internet, relatives/friends, parenting websites, and healthcare professionals [29]. Although postpartum women value the opinions of healthcare professionals and trusted relatives and close friends, the internet and digital resources are increasingly becoming important sources of information due to their rapid accessibility [28]. However, it has been reported that women are unable to continue using mobile applications during the postpartum period due to the lack of free, user-friendly, and reliable apps that meet their needs [32]. Similarly, in this study, it was found that most postpartum women had knowledge related to the postpartum period, accessed this information from sources reported in the literature, and that half of them expressed willingness to use mobile applications.

In a study reported in the literature, it was found that more than half of women in the postpartum period had insufficient knowledge about postpartum care [33]. In another study, it was determined that postpartum women’s knowledge levels about postpartum care varied according to age groups, education levels, and parity. Women over the age of 25, with at least a secondary school education, and multiparous women were found to have higher knowledge levels [34]. Consistent with the literature, this study observed a moderate association between perceiving their knowledge of maternal and newborn care as sufficient and both age and parity. Additionally, a weak association was found between perceiving their knowledge as sufficient and both education level and newborn health status at birth. Furthermore, women under 30 years of age, those with high school and above education, primiparous women, and having an unhealthy newborn at birth were found to perceive their knowledge as more insufficient. Within the framework of the Health Belief Model, age, education, parity, and newborn health status can create barriers that lead postpartum women to perceive their knowledge of maternal and newborn care as insufficient. Removing these barriers may positively influence their health behaviors. Educational programs and tools, such as mobile applications, can be developed to support the knowledge gaps of women with these sociodemographic characteristics. This approach reduces perceived barriers and increases perceived benefits. According to the Health Belief Model, this can support the adoption of health-promoting behaviors.

The literature indicates that postpartum women frequently use the internet and digital technologies to obtain health-related information [32, 35, 36]. However, certain sociodemographic characteristics influence this usage. One study found that the willingness to use SMS-based mobile health applications was higher among younger age groups and those with secondary education or above [37]. In a study conducted among pregnant women, the use of mobile applications for pregnancy was found to be associated with maternal age and parity. Nulliparous women were reported to use such mobile applications three times more frequently [38]. In this study, although weak, age and parity were also found to be associated with the willingness to use mobile applications related to maternal and newborn care. Younger women and primiparous women were found to use such applications more frequently during the postpartum period. In addition, a moderate association was observed between willingness to use such mobile applications and income level and parity. Furthermore, a weak association was identified between willingness to use postpartum mobile applications and age, education level, and mode of the last birth. The study results revealed that women aged 30 years and above, those with below high school education, those with low income, multiparous women, and those who had a Caesarean section were less willing to use postpartum mobile applications. Furthermore, the multivariate logistic regression analysis supported these findings, demonstrating that income level and parity were significant predictors of willingness to use mobile applications related to postpartum maternal and newborn care. In this context, when developing mobile applications to meet the needs of women during the postpartum period, it is important to develop content that addresses individuals with lower education levels and is economically accessible. In addition, including content that can contribute more to the existing postpartum knowledge level of multiparous women, beyond their previous experiences, may be beneficial. Moreover, including content addressing the specific care needs of women who have had a Caesarean section may encourage more postpartum women to use such applications.

During the postpartum period, women tend to seek more information about the care needs of their newborn than their own self-care needs [28, 32, 39]. A study found that postpartum women primarily sought information about newborn care during the postpartum period, while they conducted less research on topics related to maternal self-care [40]. This has been attributed to the perception among postpartum women that care during this period primarily refers to the care of the newborn [39]. The literature indicates that primiparous postpartum women often have numerous questions related to their newborns. Therefore, the ability to access unlimited information easily and anonymously is considered highly valuable [41]. It has also been reported that approximately half of women in the postpartum period seek information about the care of both themselves and their newborn during this period [42]. Similarly, this study found that postpartum women were most willing to have topics related to newborn care included in mobile applications developed for the postpartum period. Additionally, they reported that they would primarily use such an application to monitor and organise their own health and their newborn’s health, and to access information quickly in emergency situations. Within the framework of the Health Belief Model, postpartum women prioritizing their newborn’s health over their own can be explained through the concepts of perceived susceptibility, perceived severity, and perceived benefit [21]. Developing mobile applications that include such content may increase perceived benefits. These applications should also emphasize the importance of maternal care, not only newborn care, to support women’s health behaviors related to both their own and their newborn’s care. Furthermore, presenting informative content that highlights the importance of topics that women reported as less preferred in mobile applications may contribute to reducing maternal mortality and postpartum health problems.

Conclusion

A large proportion of postpartum women are willing to use mobile applications specifically designed for the postpartum period. They particularly expect such applications to include features for monitoring and organising maternal and newborn health, as well as accessing information related to emergency situations. Preferences for using postpartum-specific mobile applications are associated with factors such as age, educational level, income level, parity, and mode of the last birth. Within the framework of the Health Belief Model, addressing these perceived barriers specific to these variables and enhancing perceived benefits through carefully designed applications may promote health-supportive behaviors among postpartum women. Therefore, mobile applications developed by considering the expectations and preferences of postpartum women may play a key role in improving the quality of care during this period. It is recommended that future studies with larger and multicentre samples be conducted to gain a better understanding of the individual expectations and preferences of postpartum women.

Limitations

The fact that the study was conducted at a single centre in Istanbul, Türkiye, led to the inclusion of a population with specific demographic characteristics. Accordingly, the majority of postpartum women in the study were under 30 years old (68.6%), had a high school level education (61.6%), and had a middle income level (76.4%). This limits the generalisability of the findings. The study was conducted in a single centre using convenience sampling. Participants were selected from a single hospital, and their inclusion depended on voluntary consent on the days of data collection among those who met the inclusion criteria. This may have limited the generalizability of the sample and increased the risk of selection bias. Data were collected through face-to-face interviews and self-reports. This may have increased the risk of reporting bias, as participants could have under- or over-reported their responses. The cross-sectional design of the study did not allow for the evaluation of changes in postpartum women’s preferences and expectations regarding mobile application use over time. No longitudinal follow-up was conducted. Despite these limitations, this study provides valuable insights into postpartum women’s preferences and expectations regarding mobile application use in populations with similar characteristics. Future studies should use multicentre study designs and probability-based sampling methods. Including larger sample sizes and longitudinal follow-up could provide more reliable and generalizable evidence regarding postpartum women’s preferences and expectations for mobile application use.

First report of the complete mitochondrial genome of Microtendipes famiefeus Sasa, 1996 (Diptera: Chironomidae)
Authors: Yongwei Jiang, Haifeng Xu, Weifeng Yue, Xiaolong Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe complete mitochondrial genome of Microtendipes famiefeus was sequenced in this study. The circular mitogenome is 16,034 bp and comprises 13 protein-coding genes, 22 tRNAs, two rRNAs, and one control region. The mitogenome exhibits a strong A + T bias (76.4%) and a conserved gene arrangement of Chironomidae. Sequencing depth analysis indicated high and uniform coverage across most regions, supporting assembly reliability. Phylogenetic reconstruction based on amino acid sequences robustly placed M. famiefeus within the Microtendipes (BS = 100), with M. tuberosus forming a basal clade to Microtendipes. This study enriches available mitogenome for Chironomidae and provides molecular data for future analyses.

Introduction

Chironomidae (Diptera), commonly known as non-biting midges, represent one of the most diverse groups of aquatic insects and play an important role in freshwater ecosystems. Their larvae occur in a wide range of habitats and respond sensitively to environmental changes, making them valuable organisms in ecological monitoring programs (Ferrington 2008). However, reliable species identification within Chironomidae is often challenging because of subtle morphological differences, intraspecific variation, and the existence of species complexes. These difficulties have driven an increasing reliance on molecular data to complement traditional taxonomy and to clarify evolutionary relationships (Ekrem et al. 2007; Lin et al. 2015; Ekrem et al. 2018; Lin et al. 2020; 2022a; 2022b).

Mitochondrial genomes have become an important source of molecular evidence in insect systematics. The typical insect mitogenome contains 37 genes arranged in a generally conserved order, together with a non-coding control region (Wolstenholme 1992; Boore 1999). Features such as relatively rapid substitution rates, absence of recombination, and maternal inheritance make mitochondrial DNA particularly informative for phylogenetic reconstruction and comparative genomic analyses (Brown et al. 1979; Curole and Kocher 1999). The application of next-generation sequencing technologies has greatly accelerated the accumulation of complete mitogenomes in Chironomidae, facilitating structural comparisons and improving our understanding of relationships within the family (Fang et al. 2022; Jiang et al. 2022; Lin et al. 2022b; Zhang et al. 2023).

Microtendipes Kieffer, 1915, a genus within Chironomini, is distributed worldwide in lentic and lotic freshwater systems, comprising more than 63 described species worldwide (Qi et al. 2012; Song et al. 2023; 2025). Although DNA barcoding has revealed substantial genetic divergence within certain taxa, recent comparative mitogenomic analyses indicated that reliance on single-gene markers may obscure species boundaries and phylogenetic placement in this genus (Song et al. 2025). Nevertheless, the number of available mitogenomes for Microtendipes remains limited, with only nine complete mitogenome sequences of species available prior to the present study.

In this study, we sequenced and characterized the complete mitochondrial genome of Microtendipes famiefeus. Mitogenome structure, nucleotide composition, and codon usage were examined, and phylogenetic analyses were conducted to the phylogenetic position of M. famiefeus. The newly obtained mitogenome enriches genomic resources for Microtendipes and contributes to future systematic and evolutionary investigations of the group.

Materials and methods

Collection, DNA extraction and sequencing

A larval specimen of M. famiefeus (Figure 1) was collected on March 30, 2019, from Wenzhou, Zhejiang Province, China (28.2998°N, 120.9914°E). The specimen was immediately preserved in 85% ethanol after collection. It has been deposited at the College of Fisheries and Life Science, Shanghai Ocean University, China (Xiao-Long Lin, lin880224@gmail.com), under voucher number ZJ929.

A photo of the Microtendipes famiefeus. Photograph was taken by Xiaolong Lin.

Total genomic DNA was extracted from the thoracic tissue (with the gut removed) using the Universal Genomic DNA Kit (CWBIO, China). A paired-end sequencing library with an insert size of 350 bp was constructed and subsequently sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq Xplus platform (PE150) by Novogene Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). After quality filtering with Trimmomatic v0.32 (Bolger et al., 2014), 2.34 Gb of clean reads were retained for downstream analyses.

Species identification of the larva was confirmed using DNA barcoding through comparison with reference sequences in the Barcode of Life Data System (BOLD) (Ratnasingham and Hebert 2007).

Assembly, annotation, and structural analysis

The mitochondrial genome was assembled using NOVOPlasty v4.3.5 (Dierckxsens et al. 2017). Mitogenome annotation was performed with MITOS2 v2.1.7 (Bernt et al. 2013). The annotated sequence was subsequently imported into Geneious Prime v2024.0.5 (Biomatters, New Zealand) to generate the annotation table. This table was then submitted to the CGView Server v1.0 (Grant et al. 2023) to produce the circular mitochondrial genome map (Figure 2).

To evaluate sequencing depth across the mitogenome, read coverage was calculated and visualized in R v4.3.2 (Team RC 2024). The assembled mitogenome was first indexed using minimap2 v2.28 (Li 2018), after which clean sequencing reads were aligned to the reference assembly. Coverage depth was computed with samtools v1.10 (Li et al. 2009). The resulting depth file (alin.sort.depth) was plotted using the ggplot2 package v3.5.1 (Wickham and Sievert 2009) in R.

Phylogenetic analysis

Based on the framework proposed by Song et al. (2025), ten species of Microtendipes were selected as the ingroup, with two Polypedilum species designated as the outgroup (Table 1). Following previous mitogenomic studies on Chironomidae (Jiang et al. 2022; Lin et al. 2022b; Li et al. 2024), phylogenetic reconstruction was performed using amino acid sequences translated from the 13 mitochondrial protein-coding genes (PCGs) with Maximum likelihood (ML) analysis.

Multiple sequence alignments were carried out using MAFFT v7.526 (Katoh and Standley 2013), and poorly aligned regions were subsequently removed with trimAl v1.4 (Capella-Gutiérrez et al. 2009). The optimal substitution model was determined using ModelFinder (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017) implemented in IQ-TREE2 v2.3.6 (Minh et al. 2020). The mtART + F + I + G4 model was selected as the best-fit model, and node support was assessed with 1,000 bootstrap replicates.

Results

The complete mitochondrial genome of Microtendipes famiefeus is a circular double-stranded molecule measuring 16,034 bp in length. No cis-splicing or trans-splicing genes are identified. The nucleotide composition is strongly biased toward A and T, with 38.2% A, 38.2% T, 8.8% C, and 14.7% G, resulting in an overall A + T content of 76.4%.

The 16S and 12S rRNA genes are 1,442 bp and 846 bp in length, respectively. The 22 tRNA genes range in size from 65 to 72 bp. The control region is notably short, measuring only 31 bp. Among the 13 PCGs, ATG serves as the initiation codon for COX2, COX3, ATP6, ND4, CYTB, and ND4L, whereas ATT is used by ND1, ND2, ND3, ND5, and ND6. Alternative start codons were observed in COX1 (TTG) and ATP8 (ATA). All PCGs terminate with the standard stop codon TAA.

The coverage depth distribution of M. famiefeus is presented in Supplementary Figure S1, showing high and relatively uniform sequencing coverage across the 16,034 bp mitochondrial genome, with an average depth of 264.89× and a maximum depth of 400×.

The phylogenetic tree (Figure 3) indicates that M. famiefeus is firmly nested within the Microtendipes. It clustered with other species of Microtendipes with good bootstrap support, while M. tuberosus forms a separate lineage.

Discussion

In this study, we generated the first complete mitochondrial genome of M. famiefeus, providing new molecular evidence for this species and genus. The mitogenome shows the conserved organization and nucleotide composition commonly observed in Chironomidae, consistent with previously reported chironomid mitochondrial genomes (Cameron 2014; Fang et al. 2023).

Phylogenetic analysis further supports the placement of M. famiefeus within Microtendipes, contributing additional data for resolving relationships within Chironomini. The newly obtained mitogenome expands available genetic resources and can facilitate molecular identification, DNA barcode reference database construction, and comparative mitochondrial genomic studies. Future research integrating broader taxon sampling and nuclear genomic data will help clarify evolutionary relationships and diversification patterns within Microtendipes and related taxa.

Conclusion

In this study, the mitochondrial genome of Microtendipes famiefeus was sequenced and characterized. The mitogenome exhibits the typical gene composition and strong A + T bias. Phylogenetic analysis places M. famiefeus within the Microtendipes clade with good bootstrap support. These results provide additional mitochondrial genomic data for the genus and contribute to future phylogenetic studies of Chironomini.

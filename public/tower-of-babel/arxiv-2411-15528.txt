On the wave equation with variable exponent nonlinearity and distributive delay
Mohammad Kafini

Abstract
In this work, we are concerned with a nonlinear wave equation with variable exponents. A distributive delay is imposed into the damping term with variable exponents nonlinearity. Firstly, we show that the global nonexistence time can be dominated. Secondly, global existence of solutions is shown under some suitable conditions on the initial data. Finally, the decay rates of that solutions are established as well. Keywords and phrases: Global existence, Global nonexistence, Nonlinearly damped, Distributive Delay, Variable exponents. AMS Classification : 35B05, 35L05, 35L15, 35L70

Introduction

In this work, we are concerned with the following delayed nonlinear
wave problem

\left\{\begin{tabular}[c]{lll}$u_{tt}-\Delta u+\mu_{1}u_{t}(x,t)|u_{t}|^{m(x)-2}(x,t)$&&\\
$+\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)u_{t}(x,t-\tau)|u_{t}|^{m(x)-2}(x,t-\tau)d\tau=u\left|u\right|^{p(x)-2},$&in&$\Omega\times(0,\infty)$\\
$u(x,t)=0,$&in&$\partial\Omega\times[0,\infty)$\\
$u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\emph{\quad}u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),$&in&$\Omega$\\
$u_{t}\left(x,t-\tau\right)=f_{0}\left(x,t-\tau\right),$&in&$\Omega\times(0,\tau_{2}),$\end{tabular}\ \right.

(1)

The domain
\Omega

2\leq m_{1}\leq m(x)\leq m_{2}<p_{1}\leq p(x)\leq p_{2}\leq\frac{2\left(n-1\right)}{n-2},\text{ \ \ }n\geq 3,

(2)

where

\displaystyle m_{1}

\displaystyle:=\text{ess inf}_{x\in\Omega}m(x),\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }m_{2}:=\text{ess sup}_{x\in\Omega}m(x),

\displaystyle p_{1}

\displaystyle:=\text{ess inf}_{x\in\Omega}p(x)\text{ \ \ \ and \ \ \ \ }p_{2}:=\text{ess sup}_{x\in\Omega}p(x)

and the log-Hölder continuity condition:

Considering distributive delays, we mention the work of Mustafa
et al

[
15

distributed delay. They use the energy method to prove, under a suitable
assumption on the weight of the delay, that the damping effect through heat
conduction is strong enough to uniformly stabilize the system even in the
presence of time delay.

Recently, several physical phenomena such as flows of electro-rheological
fluids or fluids with temperature-dependent viscosity, nonlinear
viscoelasticity, filtration processes through a porous media and image
processing are modelled by equations with variable exponents of nonlinearity.
More details on these problems can be found in
\left(\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{16}{}{}]}-\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{18}{}{}]}\right)

In our work, we aim to study the problem
\left(\ref{P}\right)
where the
distributive delay acting in the variable-exponent nonlinearly damping term.
Precisely, we establish a global nonexistence and existence results under
sufficient conditions on
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},m,p
and the initial data.
Consequantely, we obtain blow-up and decay results. To the best of our
knowledge, there is no work which dealt with the distibutive delay in the
variable-exponent nonlinearity. This paper consists of three sections, in
addition to the introduction. In Section 2, we recall the definitions of the
variable exponent Lebesgue spaces

the Sobolev spaces

we transform our problem by introducing a new functional together with its
corresponding energy. In Section 4, we prove a global nonexistence result and
establish a lower bound for the nonexistence time. Section 5 is devoted to the
global existence and the decay result.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some materials needed for the statement and the
proof of our results. In what follows, we give definitions and properties
related to Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces with variable exponents.

Let
\Omega
be a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and
p:\Omega\longrightarrow[1,\infty)
be a measurable function (here
p(\cdot)

has nothing to do with that we used in our problem). The Lebesgue space

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
with a variable exponent
p(\cdot)
is defined by

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)=\left\{u:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R};\text{
measurable in }\Omega\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}\left|\lambda u(x)\right|^{p(x)}dx<+\infty\right\},

for some
\lambda>0.
The Luxembourg-type norm is given by

\left\|u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{u(x)}{\lambda}\right|^{p(x)}dx\leq 1\right\}.

The space
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega),
equipped with this norm, is a Banach space
(see
[
21
]
). The variable-exponent Sobolev space
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is defined as

W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)\text{ such that
}\nabla u\text{ exists and }\left|\nabla u\right|\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)\right\}.

This space is a Banach space with respect to the norm

\left\|u\right\|_{W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\left\|u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}.

The space
W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is defined to be the closure of

C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega).
The definition of the
space
W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is usually different from the constant
exponent case. However, under condition
\left(\ref{eq2}\right)
both
definitions coincide (see
[
27
]
). The dual space of
W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is
W_{0}^{-1,p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\Omega)
defined in the same way
as in the classical Sobolev spaces, where

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}=1.

Lemma 1
(Poincaré’s inequality
[
22
]
)

Let
\Omega
be
a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
p(\cdot)
satisfies

\left(\ref{eq1}\right)
, then

\left\|u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq C\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p(\cdot)},\text{ for all }u\in W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega),

where the positive constant
C
depends on
p(\cdot)
and
\Omega.
In particular, the space
W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
has an equivalent norm given by

\left\|u\right\|_{W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}.

Lemma 2
(
[
20
]
)

If
p:\overline{\Omega}\longrightarrow[1,\infty)
is continuous and

2\leq p_{1}\leq p(x)\leq p_{2}\leq\frac{2n}{n-2},\text{ \ \ }n\geq 3,

then the embedding
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is continuous.

Lemma 3
(
[
19
]
)

If
p:\Omega\longrightarrow[1,\infty)
is a measurable function and

p_{2}<\infty,

then

C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)

is dense in

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega).

Lemma 4
(Hölder’s inequality
[
19
]
)

Let
p,q,s\geq 1
be measurable functions defined on
\Omega
such
that

\frac{1}{s(y)}=\frac{1}{p(y)}+\frac{1}{q(y)},\text{ \ \ for a.e. }y\in\Omega.

If
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{q(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, then
fg\in L^{s(\cdot)}(\Omega)
and

\left\|fg\right\|_{s(\cdot)}\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{p(\cdot)}\left\|g\right\|_{q(\cdot)}\emph{.}

Lemma 5
(Unit Ball Property
[
19
]
)

Let
p
be a measurable
function on
\Omega
. Then

\left\|f\right\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq 1\text{ \ if and only if }\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f)\leq 1,

where

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f)=\int_{\Omega}\left|f(x)\right|^{p(x)}dx.

Lemma 6
(
[
20
]
)

If
p
is a measurable function on
\Omega
satisfying
\left(\ref{eq1}\right)
, then

\min\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p_{1}},\left\|u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p_{2}}\right\}\leq\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(u)\leq\max\left\{\left\|u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p_{1}},\left\|u\right\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p_{2}}\right\},

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for any
u\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega).

,
adding and subtracting some term and using the relation

\frac{d}{dt}\left(E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx\right)=qE^{q-1}(t)E^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx+E^{q}(t)\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx,

Equation
\left(\ref{eq30}\right)
becomes

\displaystyle 2\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{s}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}\left(E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx\right)+q\int_{s}^{T}E^{q-1}(t)E^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dxdt

\displaystyle+2\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx-\mu_{1}\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}\left|u_{t}\right|^{m(x)-2}dxdt

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}u\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)z(1)\left|z(1)\right|^{m(x)-2}d\tau dxdt

(36)

\displaystyle+2\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\frac{\tau\left(\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)+\xi(x)\right)}{m(x)}\left|z(x,\rho,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}d\tau dxd\rho dt

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p(x)}dxdt-2\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u\right|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}dxdt.

The first term in the right hand side of
\left(\ref{eq31}\right)
is
estimated as follows.

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{s}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}\left(E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|E^{q}(s)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}(x,s)dx-E^{q}(T)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}(x,T)dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}E^{q}(s)\left[\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x,s)dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}(x,s)dx\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}E^{q}(T)\left[\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x,T)dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}(x,T)dx\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}E^{q}(s)\left[C_{p}\left\|\nabla u(s)\right\|_{2}^{2}+2E(s)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}E^{q}(T)\left[C_{p}\left\|\nabla u(T)\right\|_{2}^{2}+2E(T)\right]

\displaystyle\leq E^{q}(s)\left[C_{P}E(s)+E(s)\right]+E^{q}(T)\left[C_{P}E(T)+E(T)\right],

where
C_{P}
is the Poincaré constant. Using the fact that
E(t)
is
decreasing, we deduce that

\left|-\int_{s}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}\left(E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dx\right)\right|\leq cE^{q+1}(s)\leq cE^{q}(0)E(s)\leq cE(s).

(37)

Similarly, we treat the second term:

\displaystyle\left|q\int_{s}^{T}E^{q-1}(t)E^{\prime}(t)\int_{\Omega}uu_{t}dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq-q\int_{s}^{T}E^{q-1}(t)E^{\prime}(t)\left[C_{p}E(t)+2E(t)\right]

(38)

\displaystyle\leq-c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)E^{\prime}(t)dt\leq cE^{q+1}(s)\leq cE(s).

To handle the third term, we set

\Omega_{+}=\left\{x\in\Omega\text{ }|\text{ }\left|u_{t}(x,t)\right|\geq 1\right\}\text{ \ and \ }\Omega_{-}=\left\{x\in\Omega\text{ }|\text{ }\left|u_{t}(x,t)\right|<1\right\}

and use Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, to get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left[\int_{\Omega_{+}}u_{t}^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega_{-}}u_{t}^{2}dx\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left[\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}}\left|u_{t}\right|^{m_{1}}dx\right)^{2/m_{1}}+\left(\int_{\Omega_{-}}\left|u_{t}\right|^{m_{2}}dx\right)^{2/m_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{t}\right|^{m(x)}dx\right)^{2/m_{1}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{t}\right|^{m(x)}dx\right)^{2/m_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left[\left(-E^{\prime}(t)\right)^{2/m_{1}}+\left(-E^{\prime}(t)\right)^{2/m_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}\left[E(t)\right]^{qm_{1}/\left(m_{1}-2\right)}dt+c(\varepsilon)\int_{s}^{T}\left(-E^{\prime}(t)\right)dt

\displaystyle+c\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt+c(\varepsilon)\int_{s}^{T}\left(-E^{\prime}(t)\right)^{2(q+1)/m_{2}}dt.

For
m_{1}>2,
the choice of
q=\frac{m_{2}}{2}-1
will make
\frac{qm_{1}}{m_{1}-2}=q+1+\frac{m_{2}-m_{1}}{m_{1}-2}.
Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}u_{t}^{2}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt+c\varepsilon\left[E(0)\right]^{\frac{m_{2}-m_{1}}{m_{1}-2}}\int_{s}^{T}\left[E(t)\right]^{q+1}dt+c(\varepsilon)E(s)

\displaystyle\leq c\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt+c(\varepsilon)E(s).

(39)

For the case
m_{1}=2,
the choice of
q=\frac{m_{2}}{2}-1,
will give a
similar result.

For the fourth term, we use Young’s inequality. So, for a.e
x\in\Omega,
we
have

\displaystyle\left|-\mu_{1}\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}u\left|u_{t}\right|^{m(x)-1}dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\left|u(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt+c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\left|u_{t}(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left[\int_{\Omega_{+}}\left|u(t)\right|^{m_{1}}dxdt+\int_{\Omega_{-}}\left|u(t)\right|^{m_{2}}dxdt\right]

\displaystyle+c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\left|u_{t}(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt,

where we used Young’s inequality with

p(x)=\frac{m(x)}{m(x)-1}\text{ \ \ and \ \ }p^{\prime}(x)=m(x)

and, hence,

c_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{1-m(x)}\left(m(x)^{-m(x)}\left(m(x)-1\right)\right)^{m(x)-1}.

Therefore, using the embedding of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{m_{1}}(\Omega)
and
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{m_{2}}(\Omega)
, we
arrive at

\displaystyle\left|-\mu_{1}\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}u\left|u_{t}\right|^{m(x)-1}dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left[c\left\|\nabla u(s)\right\|_{2}^{m_{1}}+c\left\|\nabla u(s)\right\|_{2}^{m_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\left|u_{t}(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt

(40)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left[cE^{\frac{m_{1}-2}{2}}(0)E(t)+cE^{\frac{m_{2}-2}{2}}(0)E(t)\right]

\displaystyle+c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\left|u_{t}(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq c\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)+\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\left|u_{t}(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt.

The fifth term of
\left(\ref{eq31}\right)
can be estimated in a similar
manner to reach

\displaystyle\left|\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}u\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)\left|z(x,1,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)-1}d\tau dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left[c\left\|\nabla u(s)\right\|_{2}^{m_{1}}+c\left\|\nabla u(s)\right\|_{2}^{m_{2}}\right]

(41)

\displaystyle+c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\left|z(x,1,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}d\tau dxdt

\displaystyle\leq c\varepsilon\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt+\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\left|z(x,1,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}d\tau dxdt.

The sixth term can be estimated, using Lemma 5.4, as follows,

\displaystyle 2\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\tau\left(\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)+\xi(x)\right)\frac{\left|z(x,\rho,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}}{m(x)}d\tau dxd\rho dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\tau_{2}}{m_{1}}\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\left(\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)+\xi(x)\right)\left|z(x,\rho,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}d\tau dxd\rho dt

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\tau_{2}}{m_{1}\alpha_{2}}\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}e^{-\rho\tau}\tau\left(\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)+\xi(x)\right)|z|^{m(x)}d\tau dxd\rho\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\tau_{2}\alpha_{1}}{m_{1}\alpha_{2}}\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}|^{m(x)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\tau_{2}}{m_{1}\alpha_{2}}\left[E^{q}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}e^{-\rho\tau}\tau\left(\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)+\xi(x)\right)|z|^{m(x)}d\tau dxd\rho\right]_{t=s}^{t=T}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\tau_{2}\alpha_{1}}{m_{1}\alpha_{2}}\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}|^{m(x)}dxdt.

As
\xi\left(x\right),\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right),\tau
and
\rho
are
bounded and using
\left(\ref{eq4}\right)
, we obtain, for
c>0,

\displaystyle 2\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\tau\left(\mu_{2}\left(\tau\right)+\xi(x)\right)\frac{\left|z(x,\rho,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}}{m(x)}d\tau dxd\rho dt

\displaystyle\leq cE^{q}(s)E(s)+cE^{q+1}(T)

(42)

\displaystyle\leq cE^{q}(0)E(s)+cE^{q}(T)E(s)

\displaystyle\leq cE(s).

With the aid of
\left(\ref{eq26}\right)
, we can handle the last two
terms as follows.

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p(x)}dxdt-2\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|u\right|^{p(x)}}{p(x)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p(x)}dxdt-\frac{2}{p_{2}}\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p(x)}dxdt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p_{2}-2}{p_{2}}\right)\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p(x)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{2}-2}{p_{2}}\right)\beta\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\left\|\nabla u(t)\right\|_{2}^{2}dt

(43)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{2}-2}{p_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{2p_{1}}{p_{1}-2}\right)\beta\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{2}-2}{p_{2}}\right)\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt

Combining
\left(\ref{eq31}\right)-\left(\ref{eq39}\right)
and for
some
c>0
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left(2-\frac{p_{2}-2}{p_{2}}-\varepsilon\right)\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt

(44)

\displaystyle\leq cE(s)+\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\left|u_{t}(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\left|z(x,1,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}d\tau dxdt.

As
\frac{p_{2}-2}{p_{2}}<1,
then the choice of
\varepsilon
small enough
gives

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq cE(s)+c\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\left|u_{t}(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt

(45)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}c_{\varepsilon}(x)\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\left|z(x,1,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}d\tau dxdt.

Once
\varepsilon
is fixed, then
c_{\varepsilon}(x)
becomes bounded (i.e.

c_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq M
) as
m(x)
is bounded. So,
\left(\ref{eq41}\right)
yields, for
c>0,

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{T}E^{q+1}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq cE(s)+M\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{t}(t)\right|^{m(x)}dxdt

\displaystyle+M\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\left|z(x,1,t,\tau)\right|^{m(x)}d\tau dxdt.

\displaystyle\leq cE(s)-C_{0}M\int_{s}^{T}E^{q}(t)E^{\prime}(t)dt

(46)

\displaystyle\leq cE(s)+\frac{C_{0}M}{q+1}\left[E^{q+1}(s)-E^{q+1}(T)\right]\leq cE(s).

As
T\longrightarrow\infty
, from
\left(\ref{eq42}\right)
, we
get

\int_{s}^{\infty}E^{q+1}(t)dt\leq cE(s).

Therefore, Komornik’s Lemma is satisfied with
\sigma=q=\frac{m_{2}}{2}-1

which implies the desired result.

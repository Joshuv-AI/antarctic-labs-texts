Removal of singularities in Morse-Bott symplectizations
Manav Gaddam and Sushmita Venugopalan

Abstract
. Hofer-Wysocki-Zehnder [ 3 ] and Bourgeois [ 2 ] proved that a finite energy punctured pseudoholomorphic curve in the symplectization of a Morse-Bott contact manifold either has a removable singularity or asymptotes to a Reeb orbit. We give an alternate proof of this result.



On compact symplectic manifolds, singularities on finite energy
pseudoholomorphic maps are removable. We consider symplectizations,
which are cylindrical symplectic manifolds, each of which is a product
of
\mathbb{R}
and a Morse-Bott contact manifold. A contact manifold is
Morse-Bott
if periodic orbits of the same period form a closed
submanifold, and the contact form is non-degenerate in the direction
normal to the submanifold. In such symplectizations, a singularity in
a finite energy pseudoholomorphic map may either be removable, or the
puncture may asymptote to a Reeb orbit. In the special case where all
Reeb orbits of the contact form are non-degenerate (and consequently
isolated) the result was proved by Hofer-Wysocki-Zehnder

[
4
]
. The proof for the general Morse-Bott case was
carried out by Hofer-Wysocki-Zehnder
[
3
]
and Bourgeois

[
2
]
, and is stated below. In this paper, we give an
alternate simpler proof of this result. We include proofs of adaptations
of standard results in an attempt to make the presentation as complete
as possible.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M,\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M))
be a compact contact manifold
whose Reeb orbits form Morse-Bott families, and let
J
be a
cylindrical almost complex structure on
W:=\mathbb{R}\times M
satisfying a
taming condition from Definition
6
. Let

u:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times S^{1}\to(W,J)

be a
J
-holomorphic map with finite Hofer energy. Then,
u
either
has a removable singularity at
\infty
or there exist constants

c
,
\delta>0
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, a period
T\in\mathbb{R}
and a periodic Reeb
orbit
\gamma:\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}\to M
such that

(1)

d_{\operatorname{cyl}}(u(s,t),\overline{\gamma}(s,t))\leq ce^{-\delta s},

where

\overline{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to W,\quad(s,t)\mapsto(a+Ts,\gamma(Tt))

is a Reeb cylinder that lifts
\gamma
, and
d_{\operatorname{cyl}}
is a product
metric on
W=\mathbb{R}\times M
.

All the terms occurring in Theorem
1
are defined in Section
1
. The above result does not hold if the Morse-Bott assumption is dropped, as shown by Siefring
[
9
]
.

We prove Theorem
1

by transforming the map
u
to a map with a removable singularity. To explain the main idea,
consider first the case when
M
is fibered by Reeb orbits of length
1
. Then,

W
is a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
bundle. Suppose the map
u
in Theorem
1
converges to an
N
-cover of a Reeb orbit as
s\to\infty
.
Then the
twisted
map defined as
u_{\operatorname{tw}}(s,t):=e^{-N(s+it)}u(s,t)
has a removable singularity at the infinite end. Exponential convergence of
u
to the Reeb cylinder follows from exponential convergence of
u_{\operatorname{tw}}
to
u_{\operatorname{tw}}(\infty)
. Thus twisting the map
u
by the right amount transforms the problem into one of removable singularity. The proof of Theorem
1
in this special case is carried out in Section
2.3
.

Now consider the general case, where Reeb orbits occur in Morse-Bott families, and suppose the map
u=(u_{\mathbb{R}},u_{M})
converges to a Reeb orbit of period
T
as
s\to\infty
. Then, the
analogue of the twisting step produces a map

u_{\operatorname{tw}}(s,t):=(u_{\mathbb{R}}-sT,\psi_{-tT}\circ u_{M}):\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times[0,1]\to W,

where for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
\psi_{\tau}:M\to M
is the Reeb flow by time
\tau
.
The pseudoholomorphic map
u_{\operatorname{tw}}
on a strip
converges to a constant as
s\to\infty
, but it
does not close up in the sense that
u_{\operatorname{tw}}(s,0)\neq u_{\operatorname{tw}}(s,1)
. To get a map with good boundary behaviour, we
double
the strip and define

v(s,t):=(u(s,t),u(s,1-t)):\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times[0,{\tfrac{1}{2}}]\to W^{2}.

The doubled strip boundary maps to a pair of Lagrangians, namely the
diagonal
\Delta
in
W^{2}
, and a shifted diagonal
\Delta_{T}

consisting of points
((a,m),(a,\psi_{T}(m))
. The Lagrangians intersect cleanly because
the family of Reeb orbits is Morse-Bott. The doubled strip
v

converges to a point in the Lagrangian intersection. The problem is
thus transformed, via twisting and doubling, to one on removal of
singularity for strips in a compact manifold. The proof of Theorem

1
in the general case is carried out in Section
2.4
.

1.
Background

We define the various terms that occur in the statement of Theorem

1
. The proof of the theorem is carried out in the
setting of
stable Hamiltonian structures
which generalize
contact manifolds, and are a natural setting for proving compactness
results in symplectic field theory. See
[
1
]
and
[
11
, Section 6.1]
.

Definition 2
.

(a)

(Stable Hamiltonian structure) A
stable Hamiltonian
structure
is a triple
(M,\alpha,\Omega)
consisting of an
odd-dimensional manifold
M
, a non-vanishing one-form

\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and a closed two-form
\Omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
that
is non-degenerate on the tangent sub-bundle

\ker(\alpha)\subset TM
and satisfies
\ker\Omega\subseteq\ker(d\alpha)
.

(b)

(Reeb vector field) Given a stable
Hamiltonian structure
(M,\alpha,\omega)
, the
Reeb vector field

R\in\operatorname{Vect}(M)
on

M
is defined by the conditions

R_{\alpha}\in\ker\Omega,\quad\alpha(R_{\alpha})=1.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
we denote by
\psi_{t}:M\to M
the time
t
flow
by the Reeb vector field.

Example 3
.

(a)

(Contact case) A contact manifold
(M,\alpha)
is a stable Hamiltonian structure with
\Omega:=d\alpha
.

(b)

(Symplectic case) A special case of a stable Hamiltonian
structure is when the Reeb vector field generates a free

S^{1}
-action on
M
, and consequently
\Omega
is a pullback of a
symplectic form on the quotient
M/S^{1}
.

Motivated by these examples, in a stable Hamiltonian structure

(M,\alpha,\Omega)
, we refer to the one-form
\alpha
as the
contact
form
and
\Omega
as the
horizontal symplectic form
.

Definition 4
.

(Symplectization)
The
symplectization
of a
stable Hamiltonian structure
(M,\alpha,\Omega)
is the product

W:=\mathbb{R}\times M.

Translation
by
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
on
W
is denoted by

e^{\tau}:W\to W,\quad(a,m)\mapsto(a+\tau,m).

The term ‘symplectization’ comes from the fact that in the contact case,
W=\mathbb{R}\times M
has a symplectic form given by
d(e^{t}\alpha)
, but there is no globally defined symplectic form for a general stable Hamiltonian structure. We will not use this form for defining the area of pseudoholomorphic curves.

Asymptotic decay results require us to restrict our attention to contact
forms where Reeb orbits are either isolated (non-degenerate case), or
occur in a family with a non-degeneracy condition in the normal
direction (Morse-Bott case).

Definition 5
.

(a)

(Non-degeneracy) For any
T>0
, a
T
-periodic orbit

\gamma:\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}\to M
of the Reeb vector field is
non-degenerate
if the derivative of the return map does not
have
1
as an eigenvalue in the transversal of the Reeb
orbit, that is,

d\psi_{T}(\gamma(0))-\operatorname{Id}:T_{\gamma(0)}M/T\gamma\to T_{\gamma(0)}M/T\gamma

is non-singular. The contact form
\alpha

is non-degenerate if all its Reeb orbits are non-degenerate.

(b)

(Morse-Bott) A contact form
\alpha
is
Morse-Bott

if for any period
T>0
,

N_{T}:=\{x\in M:\psi_{T}(x)=x,\psi_{t}(x)\neq x\,\forall t\in(0,T)\}

is a submanifold of
M
and
T_{x}N_{T}=\ker(d\psi_{T}(x)-\operatorname{Id})
.

Definition 6
.

(Almost complex structures)
Let
(M,\alpha,\Omega)
be a stable Hamiltonian structure. Let
W:=\mathbb{R}\times M
.

(a)

(Cylindrical) An almost complex structure
J
on
W
is cylindrical if

•

it is invariant under
\mathbb{R}
-translations,

•

J(\partial_{r})=R_{\alpha}
where
r
is the
\mathbb{R}
-coordinate, and
R_{\alpha}
is the Reeb vector field,

•

and the sub-bundle
\ker(\alpha)\subset TM\subset TW
is
J
-invariant.

The conditions imply that
J
descends to an almost complex structure on the distribution
\ker(\alpha)\subset TM
, which we denote by
J|\ker(\alpha)
.

(b)

(Horizontal tamedness) A cylindrical almost complex structure
J
is
horizontally tamed
if the restriction
J|\ker(\alpha)
is tamed by the two-form
\Omega+rd\alpha
for all
r\in[-\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}}]
.

To define the notion of Hofer energy of curves in a
symplectization, we consider a family of embeddings of the
symplectization into a compact symplectic manifold, and take the
supremum of the symplectic area of the curve over all such
embeddings. (This quantity is called “energy” in
[
5
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
11
, Section 9.2]
.)
Finiteness of Hofer energy is a necessary condition for
punctured pseudoholomorphic maps to have good asymptotic behavior.

Definition 7
.

(Hofer Energy) Let
(M,\alpha,\Omega)
be a stable Hamiltonian structure.

(a)

(Embeddings into a compact symplectic manifold)
Let
\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}}>0
be a small constant so that

(2)

(W_{\operatorname{cpt}},\omega_{\operatorname{cpt}}):=([-\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}}]\times M,\Omega+d(\pi_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha)),

is a symplectic manifold. The constant
\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}}
is assumed to be a fixed
constant for a given
M
.
Let

\mathcal{T}:=\{\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to(-\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}}):\varphi\text{ is an increasing diffeomorphism}\}.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
, define an embedding

(\varphi\times\operatorname{Id}_{M}):W\to W_{\operatorname{cpt}}.

(b)

(Hofer energy)
Let
C
be a Riemann surface.
The
Hofer energy
of a map

u:C\to\mathbb{R}\times M
is

E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{C}u^{*}\omega_{\varphi},

where

\omega_{\varphi}:=(\varphi\times\operatorname{Id}_{M})^{*}\omega_{\operatorname{cpt}}=\Omega+d(\varphi\alpha).

The
horizontal energy
of
u
is defined as

E_{\operatorname{hor}}(u):=\int_{C}u^{*}\Omega.

A horizontally tamed cylindrical almost complex structure
J
on

\mathbb{R}\times M
is
\omega_{\varphi}
-tamed for any increasing diffeomorphism

\varphi\in\mathcal{T}
. Therefore, for a non-constant
J
-holomorphic curve

u
, the
\omega_{\varphi}
-area is positive, and

E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u)\geq 0
.
For such a
J
-holomorphic curve
u
, the horizontal energy is bounded by Hofer energy

(3)

E_{\operatorname{hor}}(u)\leq E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u).

This is a consequence of
[
11
, Lemma 9.11]
.

Assumption 8
.

(Metric)
We assume that both
W=\mathbb{R}\times M
and
W_{\operatorname{cpt}}=[-\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}}]\times M
are equipped with a product metric
g_{\operatorname{cyl}}=dt^{2}\oplus g_{M}
, and
d_{\operatorname{cyl}}
is the distance function with respect to
g_{\operatorname{cyl}}
.

2.
Proof of the theorem

We prove Theorem
1
in this section.
Throughout the section,
M
has a stable Hamiltonian structure,
J
is a
horizontally tamed cylindrical almost complex structure on

W:=\mathbb{R}\times M
and

u=(u_{\mathbb{R}},u_{M}):\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\times M=W

is a
J
-holomorphic map.

2.1.
A preliminary limit

For a punctured pseudoholomorphic map with finite Hofer energy, the punctured end approaches a
‘preliminary limit’
in the following sense.

Proposition 9
.

Suppose
E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u)<\infty
. Then,

(a)

(
[
11
, Lemma 9.3]
)

|du|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
,

(b)

(
[
11
, p165]
)

and there are sequences

\tau_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
,
s_{k}\to\infty
such that the sequence of maps

(s,t)\mapsto e^{\tau_{k}}u(s+s_{k},t))

converges uniformly on compact subsets to a Reeb cylinder

\overline{\gamma}_{\operatorname{pre}}:\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}\to W,\quad(s,t)\mapsto(Ts,\gamma_{\operatorname{pre}}(Tt)),

where
\gamma_{\operatorname{pre}}:\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}\to M
is a
T
-periodic Reeb orbit for
some
T\in\mathbb{R}
. In case
T=0
,
\gamma_{\operatorname{pre}}
is a constant point
in
M
.

We call
\gamma_{pre}
the
preliminary limit
of the cylinder
u
.

2.2.
The case of a removable singularity

We show that in the special case where the preliminary limit obtained in Proposition
9
is a constant, the map
u
has a removable singularity at the puncture
s=\infty
.

Proposition 10
.

(Removable singularity)

Suppose
u
has finite horizontal energy
\int_{[0,\infty)\times S^{1}}u^{*}\Omega
,
and there is a sequence
s_{k}\to\infty
for which the maps

u_{M}(s_{k},\cdot):S^{1}\to M

C^{1}
-converge to a constant map. Then,
there is a point
w\in\mathbb{R}\times M
and constants
s_{0}>0
,
c_{u}>0
such that

(4)

d_{\operatorname{cyl}}(u(s,t),w)\leq c_{u}e^{-s}\quad\forall s\geq s_{0},t\in S^{1}.

We first prove an intermediate result on Hofer energy decaying to zero on the end of the domain cylinder.

Lemma 11
.

Let
u
be a map satisfying the hypothesis of Proposition
10
. Then,

(5)

\lim_{s\to\infty}E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u,[s,\infty)\times S^{1})=0.

Proof.

Recall that
E_{\operatorname{Hof}}
is the supremum of the
\omega_{\varphi}
-area of
u
where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to(-\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}})
ranges over increasing diffeomorphisms and
\omega_{\varphi}=\Omega+d(\varphi\alpha)
.
We focus on the term
d(\varphi\alpha)
since
\int_{[0,\infty)\times S^{1}}u^{*}\Omega
is finite.
For any
\epsilon
, there exists
k
such that for
any
l\geq k
and any increasing diffeomorphism
\varphi
,

\int_{[s_{k},s_{l}]\times S^{1}}u^{*}d(\varphi\alpha)=\int_{\partial[s_{k},s_{l}]\times S^{1}}(\varphi\circ u_{\mathbb{R}})(u_{M}^{*}\alpha)<\epsilon.

Indeed
\varphi\circ u_{\mathbb{R}}
is uniformly bounded, and since the sequence
u_{M}(s_{k},\cdot)
converges to a constant,

|u_{M}^{*}\alpha|\to 0
uniformly on
\{s_{k}\}\times S^{1}
as

k\to\infty
. Consequently
\int_{[s_{k},\infty)\times S^{1}}u^{*}d(\varphi\alpha)\leq\epsilon
, and (
5
) follows.
∎

The main ingredient in the proof of Proposition
10
is Gromov’s monotonicity result for pseudoholomorphic curves (see for example
[
10
, Proposition 4.3.1]
), which we now state.

Proposition 12
.

(Monotonicity)
Let

(X,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold with a Riemannian metric
g
, and let
U_{\mathcal{J}}
be a

C^{0}
-neighborhood on the space of
\omega
-tamed almost complex structures.
There exist constants
c_{{\operatorname{monot}}},r_{\operatorname{monot}}>0
such that for any
x\in X
,

0<r\leq r_{\operatorname{monot}}
, a Riemann surface
C
with boundary
\partial C

and a pseudoholomorphic map
u:C\to X
with respect to a
domain-dependent almost complex structure
J:C\to U_{\mathcal{J}}
whose image
contains
x
and
u(\partial C)\subset\partial B_{g}(x,r)
,

\int_{C}u^{*}\omega\geq c_{{\operatorname{monot}}}r^{2}.

Proof of Proposition
10
.

We first show that the image of
u
is bounded; the proof is via the monotonicity result which says that
the image can not “wander too much”, since the Hofer energy decays to zero near the puncture.
Let
c_{\operatorname{monot}},r_{\operatorname{monot}}>0
be the constants
obtained by applying the monotonicity result to
the compact symplectic manifold

(W_{\operatorname{cpt}},\omega_{\operatorname{cpt}})=([-\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}}]\times M,\Omega+d(\pi_{\mathbb{R}}\alpha),J).

Choose a small
\epsilon<\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},r_{\operatorname{monot}}
. Since the maps

u_{M}(s_{k},\cdot)
converge to a constant in
M
, Lemma
11

says that
E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u,[s,\infty)\times S^{1})
goes to zero as
s\to\infty
.
Let
\sigma_{0}
be such that

(6)

E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u,[\sigma_{0},\infty)\times S^{1})<c_{{\operatorname{monot}}}\epsilon^{2}.

Next consider a domain point
z_{0}\in[\sigma_{0},\infty)\times S^{1}
. Let

a:=u_{\mathbb{R}}(z_{0}),\quad m:=u_{M}(z_{0}).

Choose an increasing diffeomorphism
\varphi_{a}:\mathbb{R}\to(-\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}})
that satisfies

(7)

\varphi_{a}(s)=s-a\quad\text{on $(a-\epsilon,a+\epsilon)$}.

Then
\Phi_{a}:=\varphi_{a}\times\operatorname{Id}_{M}:W\to W_{\operatorname{cpt}}
is an isometry on
B_{\epsilon}(a,m)
, where
both
W
and
W_{\operatorname{cpt}}
are equipped with the cylindrical metric (Assumption
8
).
Let
C_{z_{0}}\subset(\Phi_{a}\circ u)^{-1}(B_{\epsilon}(0,m))

be the connected component containing
z_{0}
.
If
C_{z_{0}}
is compact, then
C_{z_{0}}
intersects
\{\sigma_{0}\}\times S^{1}
. Indeed, otherwise, the monotonicity result (Proposition
12
) applied to the map

\Phi_{a}\circ u
and the ball
B_{\epsilon}(0,m)
implies

c_{\operatorname{monot}}\epsilon^{2}<\int_{C_{z_{0}}}(\Phi_{a}\circ u)^{*}\omega_{\operatorname{cpt}}=\int_{C_{z_{0}}}u^{*}\omega_{\varphi_{a}}\leq E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u,C_{z_{0}}),

which contradicts (
6
). Therefore, one of the following two cases occur:

Case 1:

For some
z_{0}\in[\sigma_{0},\infty)\times S^{1}
,

C_{z_{0}}
is non-compact. In this case,
C_{z_{0}}
intersects

\{s\}\times S^{1}
for all
s\geq s(z_{0})
. Since
|du|\leq c_{0}
for some constant
c_{0}
,
we conclude that the image
u([s(z_{0}),\infty)\times S^{1})
lies
within a
c_{0}
-radius of the neighborhood
B_{\epsilon}(a,m)
, and
therefore the image of
u
is bounded.

Case 2:

The set
C_{z_{0}}
is compact for all
z_{0}\in[\sigma_{0},\infty)\times S^{1}
.
In this case, for
any such
z_{0}
,
C_{z_{0}}
intersects
\{\sigma_{0}\}\times S^{1}
. Consequently,

d_{{\operatorname{cyl}}}(u(z_{0}),u(\{\sigma_{0}\}\times S^{1}))<\epsilon\quad\forall z_{0}\in[\sigma_{0},\infty)\times S^{1},

and therefore, the image of
u

is bounded in
\mathbb{R}\times M
.

We now finish the proof of the proposition. Since the image of
u

is bounded, there is an interval
[a_{0},a_{1}]\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
the image of
u
is contained in
[a_{0},a_{1}]\times M
. For any
increasing diffeomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to(-\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}},\epsilon_{\operatorname{cpt}})
,

\omega_{\varphi}
is a taming symplectic form on the compact manifold

[a_{0},a_{1}]\times M
. The area

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times S^{1}}u^{*}\omega_{\varphi}
is bounded by

E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u)
, and is therefore finite. We apply the removal of
singularities theorem for compact manifolds
[
7
, Theorem 4.1.2]
on a reparametrization
of
u
, namely

v:B_{1}\backslash\{0\}\to[a_{0},a_{1}]\times M,\quad v(re^{i\theta}):=u(-\ln r,-\theta),

where
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
is the unit ball, and conclude that
v

extends holomorphically over

. The differentiability of
v

implies that
d_{\operatorname{cyl}}(v(z),v(0))\leq c|z|
for some constant

c
. Changing to cylindrical coordinates, we obtain the exponential
decay (
4
) stated in the proposition.
∎

2.3.
The symplectic case

In this section, we state and prove the analog of Theorem

1
in the symplectic case, which will motivate our proof
in the general case. We recall that a stable Hamiltonian structure
M

is symplectic if the Reeb vector field generates a free
S^{1}
-action
on
M
. The
S^{1}
-action extends to a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
W
given
by

\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times W\to W,\quad(re^{i\theta},(a,m))\mapsto(a+r,e^{i\theta}m).

The
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action is holomorphic if we assume that
W
is equipped
with a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-invariant cylindrical almost complex
structure. Note that this is a stronger condition than the cylindricity
of
J
, because in general, a cylindrical almost complex structure is
not invariant under Reeb flow. Thus, in this case a cover of a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-orbit

\mathbb{R}\times S^{1}\ni(s,t)\mapsto e^{T(s+it)}w,\quad w\in W,T\in\mathbb{Z}

is a Reeb cylinder.

Proposition 13
.

(Symplectic analog of Theorem
1
)
Suppose the
stable Hamiltonian structure
(M,\alpha,\Omega)
is symplectic, and
suppose the almost complex structure
J
on
W
is

\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-invariant. Let

u=(u_{\mathbb{R}},u_{M}):\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times S^{1}\to(W,J)

be a
J
-holomorphic map
with finite Hofer energy. Then, there exist constants
T\in\mathbb{Z}
,
c_{u}>0
, and a point
w\in W
such that

(8)

d_{\operatorname{cyl}}(u(s,t),e^{T(s+it)}w)\leq c_{u}e^{-s}.

Proof.

We prove the result by transforming the given map
u
to a map with a removable singularity.
First, we observe that
by Proposition
9
, there is a sequence
s_{k}\to\infty

such that the sequence

u_{M}(s_{k},\cdot):\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to M

converges to a cover of a Reeb orbit, which in this case, is an
S^{1}
-orbit. Therefore,
the sequence
u_{M}(s_{k},\cdot)
converges to
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\ni t\mapsto e^{iTt}m
for some
T\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in M
.
We claim that the twisted
cylinder

(9)

u_{\operatorname{tw}}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\times M,\quad(s,t)\mapsto e^{-T(s+it)}u(s,t)

has a removable singularity at the infinite end of the cylinder.
Indeed, Proposition
10

is applicable on
u_{\operatorname{tw}}
since it is
J
-holomorphic, the sequence
of maps
\pi_{M}\circ u_{\operatorname{tw}}(s_{k},\cdot)
converges to a constant

m\in M
, and
u_{\operatorname{tw}}
has finite horizontal energy since

E_{\operatorname{hor}}(u_{\operatorname{tw}})=E_{\operatorname{hor}}(u)\leq E_{\operatorname{Hof}}(u).

The removable singularity result (Proposition
10
) implies
that
u_{\operatorname{tw}}(\infty)=w\in W
for some
w\in W
and there exists a constant
c_{u}

such that for all domain points
(s,t)
,

(10)

d_{\operatorname{cyl}}(u_{\operatorname{tw}}(s,t),w)\leq c_{u}e^{-s},

see (
4
). Since
u_{\operatorname{tw}}
is the
e^{-T(s+it)}
-twist
of
u
, the estimate (
8
) follows from (
10
),
finishing the proof of Proposition
13
.
∎

2.4.
The contact case

In this section, we prove Theorem
1
in the general case. Although the result is stated for contact manifolds, our proof applies to any stable Hamiltonian structure.

Proof of Theorem
1

3.
A monotonicity result for strips

In this section, we state and prove an analogue of Gromov’s
monotonicity result for pseudoholomorphic maps on strips. The result applies
to neighborhoods of intersections of Lagrangians, without requiring
that the image of the map contain a Lagrangian intersection point. The
domains of the maps occurring in this result are subsets
C
of the
strip
\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]
. In particular, we assume that

C\subset\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]
is a manifold with corners whose
codimension one boundary is a union of two kinds of strata, namely the

strip boundary
and the
non-strip boundary
defined as

\partial_{{\operatorname{st}}}C:=C\cap(\mathbb{R}\times\{0,1\}),\quad\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C:=\overline{\partial C\cap(\mathbb{R}\times(0,1))}

respectively,
and the codimension two boundary consists of points in
\partial_{\operatorname{st}}C\cap\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C
.

Theorem 14
.

(Monotonicity for strips)

Let
L_{0},L_{1}
be cleanly intersecting Lagrangian submanifolds in a
symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
equipped with a Riemannian metric
g
, and let
U_{\mathcal{J}}
be a

C^{0}
-neighborhood on the space of tamed almost complex structures.
Let
p\in L_{0}\cap L_{1}
. There are constants
R>0
,

c_{\operatorname{monot}}>0
such that

(a)

for any point
x
and
r>0
such that
B_{r}(x)\subset B_{R}(p)
,

(b)

a compact surface with corners
C\subset\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]
,

(c)

and a map
u:C\to X
that is pseudoholomorphic with respect to a
domain-dependent almost complex structure
J:C\to U_{\mathcal{J}}
, and which
satisfies the Lagrangian boundary conditions

u(C\cap(\{i\}\times\mathbb{R}))\subset L_{i}\quad\text{for
$i=0,1$ and}\quad u(\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C)\subset\partial B_{r}(x),

and
x\in u(C)
,

the symplectic area of
u
is bounded as

\int_{C}u^{*}\omega\geq c_{\operatorname{monot}}r^{2}.

For pseudoholomorphic curves, the symplectic area is equal to metric area.
For use in the proof of the result, we recall
the notion of metric area
from
[
7
, Section 2.2]
.
For a tamed almost complex structure
J
on a symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
, and a map
u:C\to X

on a Riemann surface
C
, the metric area is

(20)

E_{\omega}(u,C):={\tfrac{1}{2}}\int_{C}|du|_{J}^{2}\omega_{C},

where
\omega_{C}\in\Omega^{2}(C)
is an area form on
C
, and for any
z\in C
,

(21)

|du_{z}|_{J}^{2}:=\tfrac{1}{|v|^{2}_{C}}(|du(v)|_{J}^{2}+|du(j_{C}v)|_{J}^{2}),

where
v\in T_{z}C
is a non-zero vector, and
in the right hand side
|\cdot|_{J}
is the norm
(\omega(\cdot,J\cdot))^{1/2}

on
TX
,
and
|\cdot|_{C}
is the norm
\omega_{C}(\cdot,j_{C}\cdot)
on
TC
.
The quantity
|du|_{J}^{2}\omega_{C}
is independent of
\omega_{C}
and
v
. Given conformal coordinates
(s+it)
on
C
, we have

E_{\omega}(u,C)={\tfrac{1}{2}}\int_{C}(|\partial_{s}u|_{J}^{2}+|\partial_{t}u|_{J}^{2})ds\wedge dt.

The almost complex structure

is allowed to be domain-dependent, that is,

is a map from
C
to the space of tamed almost complex structures, in which case, the metric

is also domain-dependent. If the map

is

\int_{C}u^{*}\omega=E_{\omega}(u,C).

The following is frequently used.

Observation
.

(Uniform equivalence of metrics)

(X,\omega)
be a compact symplectic manifold with a Riemannian metric

.
Let

C_{r}:=\{d_{x}\leq r\}=u^{-1}(B_{r}(x)),\quad E(r):=\int_{C_{r}}u^{*}\omega.

In the rest of this proof
c
denotes a
u
-independent constant, whose value changes across instances.

Claim
.

There is a
u
-independent constant
c>0
such that for any regular value
r_{0}
of
d_{x}
,

(26)

{\tfrac{{\operatorname{d}}}{{\operatorname{d}}r}}E(r_{0})\geq c\ell_{g}(\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r_{0}}),

where
\ell_{g}
is length with respect to the metric
g
.

Proof of Claim.

On a small enough neighborhood
U\subset C
of

\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r_{0}}
, there are coordinates
(r,\tau)
such that

r=d_{x},\quad d\tau=f(r,\tau)dr\circ j_{C}

where
f:U\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a map satisfying
f\equiv 1
on

\{r=r_{0}\}
. Indeed, we first fix
\tau
on
\{r=r_{0}\}
by the
condition
d\tau=dr\circ j_{C}
; and in a neighborhood of

\{r=r_{0}\}
we define
\tau
to be constant on the integral curves
of the line field
\ker(dr\circ j_{C})
.
Let
r_{1}<r
be such that
C_{r}\backslash C_{r_{1}}\subset U
.
By the pseudoholomorphicity of

u
, and (
22
), (
21
),

E(r)-E(r_{1})=E_{\omega}(u,C_{r}\backslash C_{r_{1}})={\tfrac{1}{2}}\int_{C_{r}\backslash C_{r_{1}}}(\tfrac{1}{f}|\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial r}|^{2}_{{}_{J}}+f|\tfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}|^{2}_{J})dr\wedge d\tau.

The above expression may be obtained by taking, for example,
\omega_{C}:=dr\wedge d\tau

and
v:={\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}
in (
20
), (
21
) and observing that
j_{C}v=f{\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}}
.
Therefore,

{\tfrac{{\operatorname{d}}}{{\operatorname{d}}r}}E(r_{0})={\tfrac{1}{2}}\int_{\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r_{0}}}(|\partial_{r}u|^{2}_{{}_{J}}+|\partial_{\tau}u|^{2}_{J})d\tau\geq\int_{\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r_{0}}}(|\partial_{r}u|_{{}_{J}}|\partial_{\tau}u|_{J})d\tau\\
\geq c\int_{\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r_{0}}}(|\partial_{r}u|_{{}_{g}}|\partial_{\tau}u|_{g})d\tau\geq c\int_{\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r_{0}}}|\partial_{\tau}u|_{g}d\tau=c\ell_{g}(\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r_{0}}).

The second to last estimate follows from the uniform equivalence (
23
) of the
g
and
J
metrics, which holds because the image of
u
lies in the compact set
\overline{B_{R}(p)}
.
The last estimate follows from
|\partial_{r}u|_{g}\geq 1
, which is
a consequence of the definition of the coordinate
r
.
∎

We finish the proof of the theorem by using the isoperimetric
inequality. Recall that
u
maps to a Darboux neighborhood where

\omega
has a primitive
\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(X)
that vanishes on
L_{0}
,

L_{1}
. Therefore, for any
r
,

(27)

E(r)=\int_{C_{r}}u^{*}\omega=\int_{\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r}}u^{*}\lambda\leq c\ell_{g}(\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r})^{2}.

The last inequality is a consequence of
the isoperimetric inequality (
25
) applied to each connected component of
the non-strip boundary
\partial_{\operatorname{ns}}C_{r}
.
By (
27
) and (
26
),
\sqrt{E(r)}\leq c{\tfrac{{\operatorname{d}}}{{\operatorname{d}}r}}E(r)
for almost every
r
. Integrating this inequality, we obtain
E(r)\geq cr^{2}
.
∎

3.3.
Exponential convergence for strips

We continue with the setting of a pair of cleanly intersecting Lagrangians
and prove a result that is an application of the isoperimetric
inequality (Proposition
18
). We show that if a
pseudoholomorphic strip uniformly converges to a point at infinity,
then the rate of convergence is in fact exponential. The techniques are adapted from

[
7
]
and the proof is given here for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 19
.

(Exponential convergence)

Let
(X,\omega)
,
L_{0}
,
L_{1}
,
g
and the Darboux ball
U\subset X
centered at
p\in L_{0}\cap L_{1}
be as in the hypothesis of Proposition
18
. Let
u:C\to X
be a
map on
C:=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times[0,1]
that is pseudoholomorphic
with respect to a
[0,1]
-dependent
tamed
almost complex structure
J=(J_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
, which
satisfies the Lagrangian boundary conditions

u(C\cap(\{i\}\times\mathbb{R}))\subset L_{i}\quad\text{for
$i=0,1$},

and whose
\omega
-area
\int_{C}u^{*}\omega
is finite.
Suppose further that the maps
u(s,\cdot):[0,1]\to U
converge uniformly to a constant map mapping to
p
as
s\to\infty
. Then, there are constants
c_{u},\gamma>0
such that for all
s\geq 0
,

d(u(s,t),p)\leq c_{u}e^{-\gamma s},

where
d
is the distance on
X
with respect to the Riemannian metric
g
.

Proof.

First, we show that the
\omega
-area of the strip decays exponentially
towards the end. In this proof,
c
,
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
,
\gamma
denote

u
-independent constants, and the constant
c
changes across
instances. Denote the
\omega
-area on the truncated strip by

E(s_{0}):={\tfrac{1}{2}}\int_{C\cap\{s\geq s_{0}\}}u^{*}\omega=\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}_{J}dtds,

where the second equality follows from (
22
), and
since
\partial_{s}u=-J\partial_{t}u
,
|\partial_{t}u|_{J}=|J\partial_{t}u|_{J}
.
We have

-{\tfrac{{\operatorname{d}}}{{\operatorname{d}}s}}E(s_{0})=\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{t}u(s_{0},t)|^{2}_{J_{t}}dt\geq\left(\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{t}u(s_{0},t)|_{J_{t}}dt\right)^{2}\geq c_{0}\ell_{g}(s_{0})^{2},

where the second to last estimate follows from Hölder’s inequality, and the last estimate uses the uniform equivalence between the metrics
|\cdot|_{g}
and
|\cdot|_{J_{t}}
(see (
23
)).
The
isoperimetric inequality (
25
) implies
E(s_{0})\leq c_{1}\ell_{g}(s_{0})^{2}
, and therefore,

-{\tfrac{{\operatorname{d}}}{{\operatorname{d}}s}}E(s_{0})\geq 2\gamma E(s_{0}),\quad\text{ where }\gamma={\tfrac{1}{2}}c_{0}c_{1}^{-1}.

Integrating, we conclude that

(28)

E(s)\leq E(0)e^{-2\gamma s}.

We complete the proof using the mean value inequality for
pseudoholomorphic curves. Choose
0<r\ll 1
. By the mean value
inequality
[
7
, Lemma 4.3.1 (ii)]
, for any
z=(s,t)\in C
,

s>2r
,

|du(z)|_{g}^{2}\leq\tfrac{8}{\pi r^{2}}\int_{B_{2r}(z)\cap C}|du|_{g}^{2}.

By the uniform equivalence of the
J
and
g
metrics, there is a constant
c>0
such that for all
z=(s,t)
,

|du(z)|_{g}\leq c\left(\int_{B_{2r}(z)\cap C}|du|_{J}^{2}\right)^{1/2}=cE(u,B_{2r}(z))^{1/2}\leq cE(0)^{1/2}e^{-\gamma s}.

Integrating we obtain

d_{\operatorname{cyl}}(u(s,t),p)\leq\int_{s}^{\infty}|\partial_{s}u|ds\leq cE(0)^{1/2}e^{-\gamma s}.

The proposition follows by taking
c_{u}:=cE(0)^{1/2}
.
∎

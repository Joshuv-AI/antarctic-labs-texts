Refined Analysis of the Crystal Structure, Thermodynamic Property, and Structural Geometry of Perovskite (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4
Authors: Ae Ran Lim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Organic–inorganic hybrid single crystals of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 were grown by the aqueous solution technique, and their structure, phase transition temperature, and thermal stability were discussed. The crystals were determined to have a monoclinic structure using X-ray diffraction on single crystals, and a transition temperature of 458 K was confirmed from the differential scanning calorimetry and X-ray diffraction analyses on powders. Moreover, the compound exhibited excellent thermal stabilities up to 550 K. The 1H and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance chemical shifts of CH3 shifted in the positive direction as the temperature increased, while the 1H chemical shift of NH2 exhibited minor variations in N–H···Br hydrogen bonding, which was influenced by minor changes in the bromide ions surrounding Zn in the ZnBr4 anion. The spin–lattice relaxation time, which indicates the energy transfer near 1H and 13C, exhibited similar values, which is because 1H and 13C are bonded to each other in the CH3NH3 cation. This compound exhibits high structural and thermal stabilities and is expected to pave the way for future applications.

Introduction

Hybrid organic–inorganic compounds have recently attracted attention because of their potential as substitute materials for perovskites.1−4 They include organic–inorganic hybrids derived from divalent metal halides, whose physical and chemical properties are pertinent to emerging applications such as optical devices, optoelectronics, photovoltaics, photocatalysts, solar cells, and thin-film transistors.5−9 CH3NH3PbX3 (where X = Cl, Br, and I) thin-film photovoltaic devices were utilized as solar cells.10−14 However, concerns over the toxicity and thermal instability associated with certain types of perovskites are raising issues regarding their applications. Therefore, the pursuit of stable and nontoxic materials is an essential research objective. This diverse array of compounds enables the integration of the properties of both organic and inorganic components within a single structure.15,16 The structural flexibility and optical properties of these perovskites are attributed to the organic component, while their thermal and mechanical properties stem from the inorganic component. For example, metal complexes with the formula (CH3NH3)2MeX4 (where Me = Mn, Co, Cu, Zn, and Cd; X = Cl and Br) can be classified into two groups based on their crystal structures.17−24 The first one, with (CH3NH3)2MeX4 (where Me = Mn, Cu, or Cd), includes a perovskite-type layered structure composed of alternating layers of cations and corner-sharing halide octahedra with a divalent metal ion positioned at the center. These compounds are distinguished by a two-dimensional metal–halide network that is separated by methylammonium groups. The metal ions are encased in slightly distorted octahedra, MeX6.25−28 The second group, which includes (CH3NH3)2MeX4 (where Me = Co and Zn), consists of discrete CH3NH3+ and MeX42– ions arranged in a lattice and zero-dimensional structure. Unassociated MeX4 tetrahedra are present in these crystals instead of corner-sharing layers of octahedra.29,30 Hydrogen bonding occurs between the hydrogen atoms of the CH3NH3 cations and MeX4 anions, with various possible configurations facilitating structural phase transitions. The dynamics of cations and interionic interactions by hydrogen bonding are closely linked to the physical properties and mechanisms underlying these structural phase transitions.

(CH3NH3)2MeX4, where Me = Zn and X = Br, exhibits a phase transition at a temperature (TC) of 456 K, as confirmed from differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) measurements.31 At room temperature, the crystal structure of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 adopts a monoclinic form, belonging to the P21/c space group, and it is characterized by lattice constants values of a = 8.14 Å, b = 13.19 Å, c = 11.65 Å, and β = 97.30°.32 Previous NMR investigations revealed the motion of methylammonium ions in (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4, with CH3 and NH3 groups undergoing reorientational jumps around the C–N bond axis.31 Recently, theoretical insights into this compound were obtained via time-dependent density functional theory, where the interactions between the organic and inorganic components were explored through Hirshfeld surface analysis, atom-in-molecule, and reduced density gradient methods.33 In addition, they reported the semiconductor nature with a band gap of about 2.87 eV by an optical absorption measurement via UV–vis absorption. Despite active research on A2MeX4-type perovskites, studies on (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 are relatively limited.

Here, single crystals of the organic–inorganic hybrid compound (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 were synthesized using an aqueous solution method, and its structural characteristics, phase transition temperature, and thermal properties were thoroughly examined. Specifically, NMR was employed to analyze the 1H and 13C chemical shifts of atoms within the cations in the temperature range of 180–420 K for elucidating the structural changes. In addition, the spin–lattice relaxation time T1ρ was discussed to understand the energy transfer around the 1H and 13C atoms. The organic (CH3NH3) cations play a crucial role in determining the physical, thermodynamic, and structural properties of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4. This investigation aimed to enhance our understanding of these properties in the organic–inorganic hybrid (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4, potentially paving the way for future applications of these materials.

Results and Discussion

Single-Crystal X-ray Diffraction

Single-crystal X-ray diffraction (SCXRD) experiments were conducted for the (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 single crystals at 300 K, which revealed a monoclinic structure with the P21/c space group and specific lattice parameters: a = 7.9967 Å, b = 13.2027 Å, c = 11.2708 Å, β = 96.3970°, and Z = 4. Within the unit cell, two distinct sets of CH3NH3 ions and one ZnBr4 ion exist. Methylammonium groups are located between layers, forming hydrogen bonds with the Br– ions. These interactions involve N–H···Br hydrogen bonding, where two N–H bonds coordinate with the Br atoms within the ZnBr4 tetrahedron. Phase Transition Temperature

DSC analysis of the (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 crystals was conducted at a controlled heating and cooling speed of 10 °C/min, with a sample weight of 6.5 mg. The resulting DSC curves exhibited a distinct endothermic peak at approximately 458 K with an enthalpy of 8.08 kJ/mol during heating and two minor exothermic peaks at approximately 357 and 413 K with enthalpies of 1.51 and 2.77 kJ/mol during cooling, respectively, as shown in Figure 2. The phase transitions observed during the heating and cooling were irreversible. The endothermic peak at 458 K signifies a phase transition from monoclinic to another structure. The optical polarizing microscopy analysis revealed that the crystals maintained their form until the temperature reached 573 K. The morphological changes at temperatures of 300, 380, 480, 520, and 570 K are shown in the inset of Figure 2.

DSC curves of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 measured at heating and cooling rates of 10 °C/min [inset: morphology changes in the single crystal at several temperatures: (a) 300 K, (b) 380 K, (c) 480 K, (d) 520 K, and (e) 570 K].

Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) results were measured above 300 K in the 2θ range of 8–50° (Figure 3) using powder samples prepared by grinding the single crystals. The PXRD patterns at 300 (blue), 350 (red), and 400 K (olive) showed no significant differences. However, the PXRD patterns obtained at 460 K (black) and 500 K (violet) differed notably from those obtained below 400 K. This difference correlated well with the endothermic peak observed at 458 K in the DSC results. The PXRD pattern obtained at 300 K (yellow) after cooling from 500 to 300 K did not match the pattern obtained at 300 K (blue) before heating. The irreversibility of the phase transition temperature, as indicated by the DSC curve, was consistent with the PXRD findings, which confirms the irreversible nature of the phase transition. A simulated XRD pattern based on the CIFs of the crystal structure obtained at 300 K is also shown in Figure 3, which demonstrates the excellent agreement with the experimental patterns. The peaks in the diffractograms were indexed by using the Mercury program. In particular, the intense 2θ peak at approximately 14.54° in the PXRD and simulated patterns obtained at 300 K relate to the (11-1) reflection. Overall, the endothermic peak at 458 K serves as a clear indicator of the phase transition temperature observed in both the DSC and the PXRD experiments.

Thermodynamic Properties

To evaluate the thermodynamic stability of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4, simultaneous thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential thermal analysis (DTA) were performed in the temperature range of 300–900 K, as shown in Figure 4. For the TGA result, a sample weighing 7.82 mg and a heating speed of 10 °C/min were employed, which is consistent with the DSC results. The TGA curve indicated that the partial decomposition temperature (Td), associated with a 2% weight loss,34 was determined to be approximately 554 K, which suggested that the material exhibits thermal stability up to this temperature. The DTA curve exhibited an endothermic peak at approximately 456 K, which is in agreement with the phase transition temperature obtained from the DSC and PXRD results. On further increasing the temperature, the (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 crystals exhibited a rapid decrease in weight. Furthermore, the inflection point observed at 687 K indicates a 50% reduction in the molecular weight derived from the total molecular weight, which was associated with the partial decomposition of 2(CH3NH3Br). Complete weight loss occurred at temperatures above 750 K. These analyses provide insight into the thermal behavior and decomposition characteristics of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 under controlled heating conditions.

1H and 13C Magic-Angle Scanning NMR Chemical Shifts

Structural analysis of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 was conducted using magic-angle scanning (MAS) NMR spectroscopy of the 1H nuclei at varying temperatures. The 1H NMR signals of NH3 and CH3 in the (CH3NH3) cation were recorded at a Larmor frequency of 400.13 MHz. Distinct peaks for the CH3 and NH3 protons are evidently discernible in the 1H NMR spectrum. The lower chemical shift peak corresponds to CH3 protons, whereas the higher chemical shift peak corresponds to NH3 protons. As shown in Figure 5, the chemical shifts of NH2 and CH3 protons were observed at 6.57 and 2.90 ppm, respectively, at 300 K. Figure 5 shows that the resonance signal of the NH3 protons shifted slightly in the negative direction with rising temperature, while that of the CH3 protons displayed a slight shift in the positive direction as the temperature rose.

1H NMR chemical shifts of NH3 and CH3 in (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 with increasing temperatures at a Larmor frequency of 400.13 MHz (inset: 1H NMR spectrum at 300 K).

Figure 6 presents the 13C NMR chemical shifts and line widths for (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 as a function of temperature at a Larmor frequency of 100.61 MHz. At 300 K, its 13C MAS NMR spectrum displayed a resonance signal with a chemical shift of 29.04 ppm, which was attributed to CH3. As the temperature increased, the 13C NMR chemical shifts shifted in the positive direction, as indicated by the dotted lines. This continuous shift in the chemical shifts suggests fluctuations in the local magnetic field surrounding the 13C nuclei, thereby reflecting changes in the crystallographic geometry. The positive chemical shift observed in the case of the 13C resonance signal of CH3 mirrors that of the 1H resonance signal of CH3, indicating similar interactions with the surrounding atoms and ions. In addition, the line widths narrowed from 3 to 1 ppm with increasing temperature, indicating increased molecular motion of 13C in CH3. These findings highlight the sensitivity of NMR spectroscopy to the structural changes and molecular dynamics of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 at various temperatures, thereby providing insight into its structural behavior.

. 1H and 13C Spin–Lattice Relaxation Times of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4

The spin–lattice relaxation time (T1ρ) values of 1H and 13C in (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 at 300 K were determined at Larmor frequencies of ωo/2π = 500.13 and 125.76 MHz, respectively. The inversion recovery curves for the protons and carbons followed a single exponential decay described by the function1where S(t) is the magnetization at delay time t and S0 is the total nuclear magnetization at thermal equilibrium.35−38Figure 7 shows the recovery curves for 1H at delay times ranging from 10 ms to 60 s, demonstrating a clear single exponential decay behavior. Based on these results, the T1ρ values for 1H were determined to be 12.5 ± 0.5 s for NH2 and 11.3 ± 2.2 s for CH3, showing consistency within the error range. Similarly, the T1ρ value for the 13C signal in CH3 was found to be 16.5 ± 5.0 s. CH3 and NH3 are bonded within the (CH3NH3) group reflecting their comparable relaxation dynamics; hence, the T1ρ values for 1H and 13C were similar.

Conclusions

Single crystals of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 were grown by using the aqueous solution method, which was determined to possess a monoclinic structure by using SCXRD analysis. A transition temperature of 458 K was determined from the DSC and PXRD results. Moreover, the compound exhibited excellent thermal stabilities up to 550 K. The 1H and 13C NMR chemical shifts of CH3 shifted in the positive direction as the temperature increased, while the 1H NMR chemical shift of NH2 exhibited slight changes due to minor variations in the N–H···Br hydrogen bonding, which were influenced by the bromide ions surrounding Zn in the ZnBr4 anion. Changes in the bromide coordination around Zn in ZnBr4 affected the N–H···Br hydrogen bonds, thereby influencing the environment around the hydrogen atoms bonded to bromine atoms. The molecular motions of 1H and 13C are analogous because of the connectivity between the CH3 and NH3 ions in the (CH3NH3) groups, indicating similar dynamics for both the nuclei. Compared to CH3NH3PbBr3, which is used in solar cells, the (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 compound is relatively excellent in thermal stability and is expected to be applied to solar cells etc., an eco-friendly substance. Consequently, this compound exhibits high structural and thermal stabilities and is expected to be applied in various fields. Based on the current research results, future research will be conducted on the optical properties of the (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 type.

Experimental Methods

Crystal Growth

Single crystals of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4, which is also known as bis(methylammonium) tetrabromozincate, were grown by slow evaporation from an aqueous solution containing ZnBr2 (98% purity, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) and (CH3NH3)Br (99% purity, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) in stoichiometric proportions. Initially, both compounds were completely melted in distilled water at elevated temperature to achieve supersaturation at a ratio of 2:1. Several single crystals were grown from the homogeneous solution by slow evaporation in a thermostatic bath at 293 K. The resulting (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 crystals were colorless and transparent with dimensions of 5 mm × 4 mm × 2 mm.

Characterization

The thermodynamic properties of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 were investigated via a series of experiments using DSC, TGA, and DTA conducted with TA Instruments. These measurements were performed under a dry nitrogen gas atmosphere within the temperature range from 200 to 900 K. In addition, changes in the crystal morphology resulting from temperature variations were observed using an optical polarizing microscope with a hot stage (THMS 600, Linkam Scientific Instruments Ltd., UK).39 This allowed us to examine how the crystal structure of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 responds to different temperatures.

The crystal structure of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 at 300 K was elucidated through SCXRD at the Korea Basic Science Institute (Seoul Western Center, Korea). A D8 Venture PHOTON III M14 diffractometer (Bruker, Germany) equipped with a graphite-monochromated Mo Kα radiation source and a Cryocool NeverIce low-temperature device were utilized. Data collection was carried out using the SMART APEX3 and SAINT software, with absorption corrections performed via the multiscan method (SADABS).40 The analysis of the single-crystal structure was conducted using direct methods, and refinement was performed through least-squares on F2 with the SHELXTL program.41 Anisotropic refinement was applied to all non-hydrogen atoms, whereas hydrogen atoms were assigned to their geometrically ideal positions. Additionally, PXRD patterns were recorded at several temperatures (300, 350, 400, 450, and 500 K) by using an X-ray diffractometer with a Mo Kα radiation source. These measurements provide insights into the evolution of the crystal structure of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 with temperature.

The NMR spectra of (CH3NH3)2ZnBr4 were obtained using a 400 MHz NMR spectrometer (Bruker, Germany) at KBSI and a 500 MHz NMR spectrometer (Bruker, Germany) at the Laboratory of NMR, NCIRF, Seoul National University (Seoul, Korea). 1H and 13C MAS NMR experiments were measured at Larmor frequencies of ωo/2π = 400.13 and 500.13 and 100.61 and 125.76 MHz, respectively. To minimize the spinning sidebands, the MAS speed for both 1H and 13C was set between 5 and 10 kHz. The precise chemical shifts were based on tetramethylsilane (TMS) as a reference.34. One-dimensional (1D) NMR spectra were acquired for the 1H and 13C nuclei. Spin–lattice relaxation times (T1ρ) were determined by analyzing the intensity changes at varying delay times. Temperature-dependent NMR spectra were collected in the temperature range of 180–430 K, with precise temperature control achieved via the regulation of N2 gas flow and heater current, ensuring an accuracy within ±0.5 K.

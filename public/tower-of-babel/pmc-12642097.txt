Mother-to-child transmission of hepatitis B virus, its associated risk factors, and the adoption of maternal antiviral therapy in eastern China: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Mengting Sun, Xinning Chen, Yinwei Chen, Xiaohui Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatitis B virus (HBV) mother-to-child transmission (MTCT) remains a major global public health challenge. While the World Health Organization has set ambitious targets for elimination of MTCT, achieving these goals in high-burden countries like China requires robust, real-world evaluation of preventive strategies. Our study aims to analyze the effectiveness of preventing MTCT of HBV and the associated risk factors.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study comprised 16,223 pairs of hepatitis B surface antigen-positive mothers and infants from 2021 to 2023 in geographical regions replete with coverage by preventive measures. We calculated MTCT rates of HBV in different maternal-infant groups and analyzed the risk factors associated with MTCT of HBV.ResultsThe mother-infant pairs in our study exhibited a 99.46% compliance rate for hepatitis B immunoglobulin and 98.87% for the birth-dose vaccine. The overall HBV MTCT rate was 0.60% (97/16,223). When hepatitis B viral protein (HBeAg)-positive women received timely antiviral intervention, MTCT rates were reduced to 0.48%. Multivariate analysis confirmed that HBeAg-positive (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 132.03 [95% confidence interval {CI}, 18.26–954.41]) and HBV DNA viral load ≥ 2 × 105 IU/ml (aOR, 2.05 [95% CI, 1.30–3.22]) were associated with an increased risk for MTCT. Maternal antiviral therapy (aOR, 0.17 [95% CI, 0.10–0.28]) and infants receiving three doses of hepatitis B vaccine (aOR, 0.07 [95% CI, 0.01–0.76]) were found to be the protective factors.ConclusionsThis study provides important insights into the factors contributing to HBV MTCT. However, gaps remain with respect to adherence to antiviral therapy, the timeliness of the full-three-dose vaccination series in high-risk infants, and the standardization of treatment protocols.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-12015-w.

Background

Mother-to-child transmission (MTCT) of hepatitis B virus (HBV) remains a worldwide public health issue. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that over 90% of infants infected with HBV through MTCT and young children with HBV infection are at an increased risk of developing chronic liver disease later in life [1]. In 2016, the WHO established the goal of eliminating viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030 [2]. Specific targets included achieving ≥ 90% coverage of timely hepatitis B vaccine birth dose (HepB-BD) to reduce MTCT, achieving less than 0.1% hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) seroprevalence among children aged ≤ 5 years or an MTCT rate of less than 2%. In 2021, updated guidelines provided a framework to help countries achieve these targets [3, 4]. Most countries have in recent years prioritized the elimination of MTCT (EMTCT) [1, 5], and by December of 2021, 11 countries met the WHO’s goal of EMTCT, demonstrating an HBsAg prevalence among children at or below 0.1% [5]. The global HBV prevalence among children aged 5 years or younger was estimated to be 0.7% in 2022 [6].

China harbors the highest number of individuals living with HBV of any country [6]. The latest national serological survey conducted in 2020 reported that HBsAg seroprevalence among women aged 15–49 years in China was 5.87%, surpassing the global average for HBV prevalence of 3.2% in the general population [6, 7]. In response to the tremendous burden of HBV, hepatitis B vaccine (HepB) vaccination has been incorporated into China’s National Immunization Program (NIP) since 1992, and the Prevention of HIV, Syphilis and Hepatitis B MTCT Programme was integrated in 2010 [8]. In moving toward WHO targets, a pilot HBV, HIV, and syphilis EMTCT project commenced in 2017 and expanded nationwide in 2022 [9]. A recently generated estimate of an overall MTCT rate of 0.23% in China was based on a multicenter study that involved 178 hospitals and an intense intervention package [10]. As a country with a diverse health setting and high birth rates, ongoing efforts to reduce MTCT in infants born to HBsAg-positive mothers remain of critical importance to China.

Zhejiang Province is located in eastern China and reflects a well-developed economy [11]. In 2017, Zhejiang, Guangdong, and Yunnan provinces initiated an EMTCT pilot program in China [12]. Although the HBV prevalence among pregnant women in Zhejiang Province had declined over time, the prevalence still remained high at 5.15% in 2020 [13], and the government of Zhejiang has thus exhibited strong policy support for EMTCT initiatives [12]. In this region, free HBV screening is provided to women during their first antenatal care (ANC) visit, and antiviral therapy is recommended for HBsAg-positive women with high viral loads. Infants exposed to HBV are required to receive timely administration of hepatitis B immunoglobulin (HBIG) and HepB-BD within 12 h after birth. During the period 2018–2019, we estimated that the rate of HBV MTCT was 1.35% in a cohort of 1849 children aged between 7 and 24 months [14]. With these efforts as a framework, we herein aimed to update the MTCT rate in Zhejiang Province and investigated the associated risk factors using high-quality provincial data in a setting with high-coverage EMTCT services. We anticipate that our findings will provide valuable insights for developing effective strategies for the EMTCT of HBV.

Methods

Study population and design

This was a retrospective cohort study using prospectively collected data from the Zhejiang provincial EMTCT Information Network, a mandatory case-reporting system that covers all delivery hospitals in the province. We recruited 20,162 pregnant women infected with HBV who delivered live-born infants between January 2021 and December 2023 in Zhejiang Province (Fig. 1), and we followed the infants born to these women for up to 12 months after birth. Women and infants with incomplete data or women with multiple gestations were excluded, and children who were older than the recommended age for MTCT assessment (7–12 months) were also excluded.

Enrollment flowchart of HBsAg-positive mother-infant pairs included in this study

A structured questionnaire was used to collect maternal information that included demographics (age, educational level, employment status, ethnicity, and marital status), obstetric history (gravidity, parity, number of living children, timing of first antenatal care, and mode of delivery), HBV status (serological markers, viral load levels, and antiviral therapy), and child-related information (sex, birth weight, age, HBIG and HepB-BD administration, and HBsAg test results). The questionnaire was based on a nationally unified questionnaire registration form for EMTCT system and has been translated into English for this study (Supplementary File 1). These data were collected at different stages by designated trained medical staff. At the first antenatal care visit, information of sociodemographic characteristics and HBV screening was obtained by gynecologists or nurses. During delivery hospitalization, midwife collected birth information, such as delivery mode, birth weight and birth weeks, the administration of HBIG and HepB-BD et al. During child healthcare follow-up visits, pediatricians gathered infant-related information. All collected data were then entered into a web-based information system for integrated management and quality control. Based on the previously reported rate of MTCT among HBV-exposed infants in Zhejiang, p was set at 1.35% with an allowable bias δ = 0.15 and a significance level of α = 0.05 [14]. Based on these parameters, we calculated a minimal sample size of 12,463 mother-infant pairs for our analysis.

EMTCT of HBV

As shown in Fig. 2, HBsAg screening in Zhejiang is provided free for pregnant women during their first ANC visit before 13 gestational weeks. When testing was available, pregnant women were recommended to undergo evaluation for HBV DNA viral load, particularly those women who were hepatitis B viral protein (HBeAg)-positive. According to Chinese guidelines, antiviral therapy with tenofovir (TDF) is recommended for pregnant women with HBV DNA viral loads exceeding 200,000 IU/mL or who are HBeAg-positive, and this treatment is to be initiated at 24–28 weeks of gestation [15]. According to the National Immunization Program (NIP), HepB-BD and HBIG are to be administered in a delivery hospital within 12 h after birth or as early as possible in Zhejiang [16]. All infants should complete the standard “0–1–6 month” vaccination schedule to ensure adequate protection. In principle, timely HBIG administration are provided. However, for newborns with severe congenital anomalies, extremely low birth weight (< 1500 g), severe asphyxia, or respiratory distress syndrome leading to unstable vital signs, the administration of HepB-BD may be delayed until the infant’s condition stabilizes. For premature infants and those with low or very low birth weight, if vital signs are stable, both HepB-BD and HBIG should be administered as early as possible within 12 h after birth. In addition, infants with low birth weight should follow an enhanced four-dose schedule (at birth, 1, 2, and 7 months) to ensure adequate immunogenicity and protection. One or two months after the last dose of HepB, the efficiency of HBV MTCT prevention was evaluated by testing infant serological markers (HBsAg and anti-HBs levels) at maternal and child healthcare facilities. Infants who tested negative for HBsAg and had anti-HBs levels ≥ 10 mIU/mL were considered to have successful immunoprophylaxis. Infants who were HBsAg-negative but had anti-HBs < 10 mIU/mL were considered vaccine non-responders and recommended for revaccination. Data used for efficiency estimation were also obtained from the Zhejiang provincial EMTCT Information Network. Routine data quality control was conducted by local and provincial specialists to ensure accurate classification and reporting.

Definition of variables

A high HBV DNA viral load was designated as ≥ 200,000 IU/mL in the present study, and timely vaccination of HBIG or HepB-BD was defined as infants receiving an injection within 12 h after birth. We described integrated EMTCT services as a package, since pregnant women with a high HBV DNA viral load or who were HBeAg-positive received antiviral therapy; and their infants underwent timely vaccination with HBIG and three doses of HepB. Births of infants before 37 gestational weeks were classified as premature, while those neonates with a birth weight under 2500 g were categorized as having low birth weight.

In this study, the MTCT rate was estimated as the proportion of infants who tested positive for HBsAg within 12 months after birth, calculated as (number of HBsAg-positive infants / number of live-born infants) × 100%. The timely HepB-BD vaccination rate was defined as (number of infants receiving HepB-BD within 12 h / number of live-born infants) × 100%. The timely HBIG injection rate was defined as (number of infants receiving HBIG within 12 h / number of live-born infants) × 100%. Infants with incomplete or delayed vaccination records (e.g., missing timely HepB-BD or HBIG) were included and analyzed as separate groups. Only infants who lacked all serological testing and vaccination records were excluded from the analyses to ensure accurate estimation of MTCT and vaccination coverage rates.

Statistic analysis

Continuous data are presented as median values (range) and categorical data as counts and percentages. Pearson’s Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test was used to compare categorical data between groups. Multivariate logistic regression models were exploited to estimate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) of the associations for HBV MTCT. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. We conducted a statistical analysis using R software (version 4.4.1).

Results

Characteristics of the study population and risk factors for MTCT

A total of 16,223 infants born to HBV-positive women who possessed complete data were ultimately enrolled during the study period, comprising 97 HBsAg-positive infants and 16,126 HBsAg-negative infants; the median age of the mothers was 31.0 years. A majority of the women were between 20 and 34 years of age (11,909/16,223; 73.41%), achieved an educational level of secondary school or below (9117/16,223; 56.20%), were employed (8843/16,223; 54.51%), were of Han ethnicity (15,305/16,223; 94.34%), were married (15,431/16,223; 95.12%), experienced ≥ 2 pregnancies (12,115/16,223; 74.68%), were multiparous (12,846/16,223; 79.18%), had one child (10,552/16,223; 65.04%), had their first ANC visit within 13 weeks of gestation (12,324/16,223; 75.97%), and delivered vaginally (9417/16,223; 58.05%). Of these women, 52.65% (8542/16,223) were HBeAg-positive, and 96.40% (15,639/16,223) underwent HBV DNA viral load testing. Among 15,639 women who underwent an HBV DNA viral load test, 29.71% (4647/15,639) reported an HBV DNA viral load at or beyond 2 × 105 IU/ml. Notably, 24.29% (3940/16,223) of the total women were both HBeAg-positive and had a high viral load, indicating a substantial overlap between these two treatment criteria.

Regarding the infants, 52.64% (8540/16,223) were boys, 6.24% (1013/16,223) were preterm at birth, and 3.83% (622/16,223) were born with low birth weight. The vaccination coverage rates for HBIG and HepB-BD administered within 12 h after birth among infants were 99.46% (16,135/16,223) and 98.87% (16,040/16,223), respectively. Virtually all (99.54%; 16,149/16,223) infants received all of the recommended doses of HepB vaccine, 99.88% (16,204/16,223) received both HepB-BD and HBIG at birth, and 98.32% (15,950/16,223) completed three doses of HepB and HBIG at birth. We noted that 0.28% (46/16,223) of infants received HepB-BD and 0.14% (24/16,223) received HBIG beyond 24 h post-delivery.

Univariate regression analysis revealed that the risk of HBV MTCT was significantly associated with maternal HBeAg status, maternal HBV DNA viral load, maternal antiviral therapy, administration of three doses of HepB vaccination to infants, and injections of HBIG and HepB-BD. Using multivariate analysis, we subsequently determined that only maternal HBeAg-positivity (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 132.03 [95% CI, 18.26–954.41]) and a high HBV DNA viral load of ≥ 2 × 105 IU/ml (aOR, 2.05 [95% CI, 1.30–3.22]) were significant risk factors for MTCT, whereas maternal antiviral therapy (aOR, 0.17 [95% CI, 0.10–0.28]) and infant vaccination of three doses of HepB (aOR, 0.08 [95% CI, 0.01–0.82]) were protective factors against MTCT. HBV MTCT rate by subgroup

As shown in Table 2, a total of 97 infants contracted HBV infection within 12 months after birth, providing an overall HBV MTCT rate of 0.60%. Among all HBsAg-positive infants, 98.97% (96/97) were born to HBeAg-positive women, with the MTCT rate highest in those infants born to HBeAg-positive women at 1.12% (96/8542). In cases where HBeAg-positive women received antiviral therapy, the infection rate decreased to only 0.48% (25/5203). Infants born to women with an HBV DNA viral load lower than 2 × 105 IU/ml reflected the lowest MTCT rate at 0.45% (49/10,992), while infants with complete HBIG and HepB vaccinations demonstrated an MTCT rate of 0.58% (93/15,950).

With respect to the 8542 infants born to women who were HBeAg-positive, a maternal HBV DNA viral load of ≥ 2 × 105 IU/ml (aOR, 2.05 [95% CI, 1.30–3.22]) was the only risk factor for MTCT, while those infants born to women who underwent antiviral therapy (aOR, 0.17 [95% CI, 0.10– 0.27]) and those infants who were injected with three doses of HepB (aOR, 0.13 [95% CI, 0.03–0.54]) exhibited a lower risk of contracting HBV via MTCT (Supplementary Materials Table S1).

HBV mother-to-child transmission rate

Maternal antiviral therapy

Over one-third of the women (37.93%; 6154/16,223) received antiviral therapy during pregnancy, as detailed in Table 3. With respect to HBeAg-positive women, 60.91% (5203/8542) underwent therapy, and 51.85% (2698/5203) were treated between 24 and 28 gestational weeks; 81.15% (3771/4647) of women with an HBV DNA viral load of ≥ 2 × 105 IU/ml were treated, and 58.63% (2211/3771) initiated treatment at 24–28 weeks of pregnancy. Nevertheless, there were 17.08% (644/3771) of women with an HBV DNA viral load of ≥ 2 × 105 IU/ml who commenced antiviral therapy at or beyond 29 weeks of pregnancy.

Among the 6154 women who received antiviral therapy, most met either the criterion of HBeAg positivity or high HBV DNA viral load. Specifically, 1939 (31.51%) were HBeAg-positive only, 507 (8.24%) had a high viral load only, and 3264 (53.01%) met both criteria. The remaining 444 women (7.22%) received therapy based on other clinical or laboratory indications.

Of the 97 HBsAg-positive infants, 49 were born to mothers with low HBV DNA viral loads, with all being HBeAg-positive (Table 4). Discussion

We observed a markedly low MTCT rate in infants under 12 months in a region of high coverage of EMTCT services, congruent with WHO validation targets [2]. Our findings also revealed that maternal HBeAg-positivity and a high HBV DNA viral load increased the risk of MTCT of HBV. A small proportion of women additionally experienced delays or failures in antiviral treatment, and some infants did not receive timely or complete combined immunoprophylaxis; in fact, a few infants born to low-risk women still contracted HBV infection. Therefore, further efforts are warranted to sustain the achievements of low MTCT and to ensure that all eligible children remain protected from infection.

The overall HBV MTCT rate in this study was lower than our previous reports as 1.35%, and also less than rates in most other studies [14, 17, 18]. We observed 0.60% infants acquired HBV, which was slightly lower than the estimated global prevalence of 0.70% among children under 5 years of age across 170 countries in 2022 [6, 8]. The achievements should contribute to the strengthened measures in Zhejiang. For example, we require all newborns with HBIG and the first HepB-BD injection in delivery room to improve early injection, except of those with unstable vital signs. A web-based Maternal and Child Health Service Network improved follow up of children and complete HBV vaccination. However, according to the global report, only 46% of infants received a timely birth dose and 85% of infants completed the three-dose HBV vaccination, and only 14% received both HBIG and the complete vaccination regimen [6]. Our combined immunoprophylaxis achieved 98.32% coverage of timely HBIG and the three-dose HepB vaccination, which was higher than the coverage levels reported in Jiangsu Province(93.2–97.00%), China [19].

Our findings evidenced the effectiveness of antiretroviral therapy for prevention HBV MTCT. Even in infants born to HBeAg Positive women, HBV MTCT rate would reduce when women had antiretroviral therapy. Notably, the antiviral treatment rate for HBsAg-positive women who initiated treatment between 24 and 28 gestational weeks attained 50.41% in our study, significantly exceeding the standard treatment coverage reported by a multicenter study in southeast China (12.22%) [18]. The MTCT rate achieved by the Chinese nationwide SHIELD program (2022–2024) was 0.23%, and adherence to antiretroviral therapy during pregnancy was 83.2% among mothers with high viral loads [10]; however, in Zhejiang, we also noticed 18.85% of women with high viral load and 39.09% of HBeAg-positive women missed treatment. Women at high risk of MTCT have missed complete antiviral therapy, and this is prevalent in low- and middle-income countries [20]. In Zhejiang province, referral may lead to delay or missing treatment in women because HBV DNA testing and antiviral therapy are not available in some community hospitals. Globally, the antiviral therapy rates in high-risk women have ranged from 33.00% to 90.04% [21, 22]. In Baoan, Shenzhen pilot district of China, the MTCT rate was observed to be as low as 0.18% with a hospital-community-integrated strategy from 2018 to 2022, and this might have contributed to the one-stop, free-of-charge services for HBV-infected pregnant women [22]. Therefore, optimizing EMTCT service delivery mode is of great significance. In addition, to improve women’s awareness is also helpful. Women at a lower educational level who initiated their first ANC visit late might also face barriers to accessing EMTCT service [22].

Our findings indicated that the risk of MTCT increased with HBeAg-positivity and high viral load. HBeAg-positive women reflected a 9.29–18.89-fold enhanced risk of MTCT [10, 22, 23], and that a high HBV DNA viral load (≥ 2 × 105 IU/mL) was associated with ORs ranging from 2.77 to 10.4 [10, 22–24]. We demonstrated that over 50% of women in our study were HBeAg-positive and that nearly one-third of women exhibited high HBV DNA viral loads; this substantial proportion of women at high risk necessitates attention. A global systematic review and meta-analysis of 71,712 HBsAg-positive, pregnant women highlighted significant regional disparities. For example, the proportion of women with high viral loads was reported to be 31.56% in the western Pacific region, but only 9.98% in Europe and 7.81% in the eastern Mediterranean region. Similarly, HBeAg-positivity varied widely, reaching 34.53% in the western Pacific region compared with 15.09% in Europe and 8.95% in the eastern Mediterranean region [20]. The authors of a separate systematic review and meta-analysis in China ascertained that the proportion of HBeAg-positive women was comparable to that of women with high viral loads, reinforcing the close relationship between antigen expression and virologic activity [25]. These regional differences in HBV epidemiology suggest that the risk of MTCT varies substantially across populations and highlight the necessity for tailored prevention strategies in high-prevalence areas.

For HBV-exposed infants, vaccination with all three HepB doses was the protective factor in our investigation. Although the majority of previous studies had estimated vaccination rates of HepB-BD and HBIG using the benchmark of 24-hour post-delivery, the high coverage within 12 h in our study may have contributed to improved prophylactic effectiveness as early vaccination was critical in preventing MTCT. The small proportion of infants without prompt immunization in some studies might be due to factors such as prematurity, low birth weight, congenital anomalies or severe diseases as neonatal asphyxia et al. that disallow immunization in a timely fashion according to China’s immunization program [16].

Previous studies showed some maternal sociodemographics as indicators for HBV MTCT. We did not find an increased risk of HBV MTCT in younger mothers (< 20 years), and this might have been due to the typically very small proportion of younger women [26]. Additionally, while some studies have suggested that cesarean delivery contributes to a reduction in HBV MTCT [27], in China HBV infection alone is not considered an indicator for cesarean delivery. A study in Shenzhen revealed an increased risk of MTCT in mothers with lower educational levels, but this differed from our study [16, 22]. These discrepancies may reflect the homogenizing effects of China’s National Free Prenatal Checkup Policy that ensures standardized HBV serological testing and antiviral treatment initiation for all pregnant women regardless of educational background. Importantly, residual risk of vertical transmission among low risk infants also warrants attention, which suggests further research on the pathogenesis mechanism.

The strength of the study was that large sample size and comprehensive data set enabled a robust analysis of the real-world effects of EMTCT. Through subgroup analyses, we quantified the differential MTCT rates among high-risk populations and focused specifically on HBeAg-positive women and those with high viral load and undergoing maternal antiviral therapy. Next, the authors of a majority of studies assessed the effectiveness of HepB-BD and HBIG administration within 24 h of birth, and we also demonstrated the effectiveness of the full immunization schedule in early childhood. This provided additional evidence for long-term protection against HBV infection.

There were some limitations to our study. First, multiple births were excluded from this study, and this might have affected the exact estimation of MTCT in the general HBsAg-positive population. Second, 3.6% of the population were not tested for viral load, which may have affected the accuracy of viral load in estimating the risk of MTCT. Third, infants without documented serological marker testing or complete vaccination records were excluded from the analysis. However, this is unlikely to substantially bias our findings given the very high coverage of standard HBV MTCT prevention measures in China (three-dose HepB coverage rate of 99.6% [28]). Finally, there are many other factors associated with MTCT, including amniocentesis, co-infection with HIV, and specific genetic markers [29, 30]; but due to data limitations, we did not include these factors in our analysis.

Conclusions

This study has provided important insights into the factors associated with MTCT of HBV, particularly focusing on maternal HBeAg status, antiviral therapy, and immunization strategies. While our results revealed the effectiveness of current preventive measures, gaps remain in adherence to antiviral therapy, the timely administration of vaccination to high-risk infants, and the standardization of treatment protocols. We recommend that public health efforts continue to enhance maternal awareness and education, optimize compliance with antiviral treatments, and address the needs of vulnerable populations. Further research into the mechanisms behind breakthrough infections and other risk factors will be essential in refining our strategies for eliminating MTCT of HBV.

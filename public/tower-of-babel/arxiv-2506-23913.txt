On morphisms of topological quivers
Mariusz Tobolski

Abstract
We introduce regular morphisms of topological quivers and show that they give rise to a subcategory of the category of topological quivers and quiver morphisms. Our regularity conditions render the topological quiver C*-algebra construction a contravariant functor from the category of topological quivers and regular morphisms into the category of C*-algebras and ∗ * -homomorphisms.



Key words: topological quiver, Cuntz–Pimsner algebra, C*-correspondence, graph C*-algebra, factor map, morphism of quivers

MSC: 46L55, 46L08

1
Introduction

Directed graphs and their topological generalizations provide a rich framework for constructing operator algebras with deep connections to dynamics, geometry, and representation theory. Classical graph C*-algebras, first introduced in the context of discrete graphs by Kumjian, Pask, Raeburn, and Renault in
[
10
]
, have been extensively studied and classified in terms of graph-theoretic data (see, e.g.,
[
15
]
). The notion of a
topological quiver
, pioneered by Muhly and Tomforde
[
12
]
, extends this theory by allowing vertex and edge spaces to carry locally compact Hausdorff topologies, with source and range maps continuous and structure maps given by a continuous family of measures.

While the construction and analysis of C*-algebras associated to topological quivers have been developed in recent years, the study of
morphisms
between topological quivers has received less attention. Morphisms provide functoriality, enabling one to compare and relate the C*-algebras of different quivers, to construct equivalences and dualities, and to transfer structural properties across categories. This paper initiates a systematic exploration of morphisms of topological quivers, aiming to identify the right notion. Appropriate morphisms of topological graphs of Katsura
[
6
]
, which are special kinds of topological quivers, were defined in
[
8
]
. The special case of an isomorphism of topological quivers was considered in
[
5
]
.

Our main contributions are as follows:

(1)

We introduce the notion of a
regular quiver morphism
between topological quivers, requiring compatible continuous maps on vertex and edge spaces that intertwine the source, range, and measure data (Definition
3.2
).

(2)

We show that regular quiver morphisms compose naturally and form a category
\mathbf{Q}_{\rm reg}
(Proposition
3.4
). We also prove that our regular quiver morphisms generalize regular factor maps of Katsura (Section
3.2
).

(3)

Finally, we demonstrate that regular quiver morphism induce
*
-homomorphisms between the associated C*-algebras (Theorem
3.12
).

Throughout, all topological spaces are assumed to be locally compact, Hausdorff, and second countable. We denote by
C_{0}(X)
the algebra of continuous complex-valued functions vanishing at infinity on a space
X
.

2
Preliminaries

We refer the reader to
[
13
]
for an overview of C*-algebra theory. In what follows, we make use of the following categories:

{\bf C}^{*}:~\text{C*-algebras and $*$-homomorphisms},

{\bf C}^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}:~\text{$\mathbb{T}$-C*-algebras and $\mathbb{T}$-equivariant $*$-homomorphisms}.

Here a
\mathbb{T}
-C*-algebra is a C*-algebra
A
equipped with a circle (continuous) action
\alpha:\mathbb{T}\to{\rm Aut}(A)
. A
*
-homomorphism between
\mathbb{T}
-C*-algebras is
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant if it intertwines the respective actions.

2.1
C*-correspondences and Cuntz–Pimsner algebras

The general theory of Hilbert modules and C*-correspondences is presented in detail in
[
11
]
. The foundational idea of associating C*-algebras to C*-correspondences originates from Pimsner’s work
[
14
]
. In this paper, we follow the approach developed in
[
7
]
and
[
17
]
.

A
C^{*}
-correspondence
over a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
consists of a (right) Hilbert
A
-module
X
equipped with a nondegenerate left action of
A
, namely, a
*
-homomorphism
\varphi_{X}:A\to\mathcal{L}(X)
, where
\mathcal{L}(X)
denotes the algebra of adjointable operators on
X
. We often write
(X,A)
to indicate that
X
is a correspondence over
A
, and denote the left action of
a\in A
on
\xi\in X
by
a\cdot\xi:=\varphi_{X}(a)(\xi)
.

The C*-algebra
\mathcal{K}(X)
of
compact operators on
X
is defined as the closed linear span of rank-one operators
\theta_{\xi,\eta}
given by

\theta_{\xi,\eta}(\zeta):=\xi\cdot\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle,\qquad\zeta\in X,\quad\xi,\eta\in X.

This forms a closed two-sided ideal in
\mathcal{L}(X)
. An important ideal associated to a
C^{*}
-correspondence
X
is

J_{X}:=\left\{a\in A:\varphi_{X}(a)\in\mathcal{K}(X)\ \text{and}\ ab=0\ \text{for all}\ b\in\ker\varphi_{X}\right\},

which plays a key role in the construction of the associated Cuntz–Pimsner algebra.

Finally, let
X
and
Y
be
C^{*}
-correspondences over
C^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
, respectively, and suppose that
\psi:X\to Y
is a continuous linear map. Then one can define a
*
-homomorphism
\psi^{(1)}:\mathcal{K}(X)\to\mathcal{K}(Y)
on rank-one operators by

\psi^{(1)}(\theta_{\xi,\eta}):=\theta_{\psi(\xi),\psi(\eta)}.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
17
, Definition 2.3]
, cf.
[
9
, Definition 2.3]
)
Let
(X,A)
and
(Y,B)
be C*-correspondences. A pair
(\psi,\pi)
consisting of a linear map
\psi:X\to Y
and a
*
-homomorphism
\pi:A\to B
is a
correspondence morphism
from
X
to
Y
if

(C1)

\psi(a\cdot\xi)=\pi(a)\cdot\psi(\xi)
for all
a\in A
and
\xi\in X
,

(C2)

\pi(\langle\xi,\eta\rangle)=\langle\psi(\xi),\psi(\eta)\rangle
for all
\xi,\eta\in X
.

A correspondence morphism
(\psi,\pi)
is called
covariant
if

(C3)

\pi(J_{X})\subseteq J_{Y}
, and

(C4)

\varphi_{Y}(\pi(a))=\psi^{(1)}(\varphi_{X}(a))
for all
a\in J_{X}
.

\blacklozenge

We obtain the following categories:

{\bf Corr}^{*}:\text{C*-correspondences and correspondence morphisms},

{\bf Corr}^{*}_{\rm cov}:\text{C*-correspondences and covariant correspondence morphisms}.

Every C*-algebra
A
can be viewed as a C*-correspondence over itself with the inner product defined by the formula
\langle a,b\rangle:=a^{*}b
and the left and right actions given by the left and right multiplication on
A
, respectively. Furthermore,
\mathcal{K}(A)\cong A
via a canonical isomorphism. Hence, the category
{\bf C}^{*}
can be viewed as a subcategory of both
{\bf Corr}^{*}
and
{\bf Corr}^{*}_{\rm cov}
.

A
(covariant) representation
of a C*-correspondence
X
over
A
on a C*-algebra
B
is a (covariant) correspondence morphism from
X
to a C*-algebra
B
viewed as a C*-correspondence over itself.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
7
, Definition 3.5]
, cf.
[
14
]
)
The
Toeplitz–Pimsner algebra

\mathcal{T}_{X}
associated to a correspondence
X
over
A
is the C*-algebra
C^{*}(t_{X},t_{A})
generated by the universal representation
(t_{X},t_{A})
. The
Cuntz–Pimsner algebra

\mathcal{O}_{X}
associated to a correspondence
X
over
A
is the C*-algebra
C^{*}(u_{X},u_{A})
generated by the universal covariant representation
(u_{X},u_{A})
of
X
.

\blacklozenge

By universality, for every covariant representation
(\psi,\pi)
of
X
on
B
, there is a
*
-homomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{\psi,\pi}:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to B
onto
C^{*}(\psi,\pi)
such that
\mathcal{O}_{\psi,\pi}\circ u_{X}=\psi
and
\mathcal{O}_{\psi,\pi}\circ u_{A}=\pi
. The formulas

\alpha_{z}(u_{A}(a))=u_{A}(a),\qquad\alpha_{z}(u_{X}(\xi))=zu_{X}(\xi),\qquad z\in\mathbb{T},\quad a\in A,\quad\xi\in X,

(1)

give rise to a circle action
\alpha:\mathbb{T}\to{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
on the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra of a C*-correspondence
X
over
A
, called the
gauge action
.

Proposition 2.3
.

(
[
17
, Proposition 2.9 and 2.10]
)

Let
(\psi,\pi)
be a covariant correspondence morphism from
(X,A)
to
(Y,B)
. Then, there exists a unique gauge-equivariant
*
-homomorphism
\Psi:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}
such that

\Psi(u_{X}(\xi))=u_{Y}(\psi(\xi)),\quad\xi\in X,\qquad\Psi(u_{A}(a))=u_{B}(\pi(a)),\qquad a\in A.

Furthermore, the assignments
(X,A)\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
(\psi,\pi)\mapsto\Psi
give rise to a covariant functor from the category
{\bf Corr}^{*}_{\rm cov}
to the category
{\bf C}^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}
.

2.2
Topological quivers

We refer the reader to
[
12
]
for an extensive study of topological quivers and their C*-algebras. A
topological quiver

E
is a quintuple
(E^{0},E^{1},s_{E},r_{E},\lambda_{E})
, where
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are second-countable locally compact Hausdorff spaces,
s_{E},r_{E}:E^{1}\to E^{0}
are continuous maps such that
r_{E}
is open, and
\lambda_{E}=\{\lambda_{E}^{v}\}_{v\in E^{0}}
is a family of Radon measures on
E^{1}
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

{\rm supp}\,\lambda_{E}^{v}=r_{E}^{-1}(v)
for all
v\in E^{0}
,

2.

the function

v\longmapsto\int_{r_{E}^{-1}(v)}\xi(e)d\lambda^{v}_{E}(e)

belongs to
C_{c}(E^{0})
for all
\xi\in C_{c}(E^{1})
.

We call
E^{0}
the
vertex space
,
E^{1}
the
edge space
, and
s_{E}
and
r_{E}
the
source
and the
range
map, respectively. Since
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are second-countable locally compact Hausdorff spaces and
r_{E}
is an open map, a system of measures as above (often called an
r_{E}
-system) always exists by
[
18
, Corollary B.18]
but it is not unique. In fact, one may chose the measures
\lambda^{v}_{E}
to be probability measures. However, it is not always desirable to do this, for instance, when dealing with counting measures. Examples of topological quivers include directed graphs, topological graphs
[
6
]
, locally compact groupoids
[
16
]
, branched coverings
[
3
]
, and topological relations
[
2
]
.

One defines the following subsets of
E^{0}
:

\displaystyle E_{\rm sink}^{0}=

\displaystyle E^{0}\setminus\overline{s_{E}(E^{1})},

\displaystyle E_{\rm fin}^{0}=

\displaystyle\{v\in E^{0}~:~\text{there is a precompact neighborhood $V$ of $v$ such that}

\displaystyle~~s_{E}^{-1}(\overline{V})\text{ is compact and $r_{E}|_{V}$ is a local homeomorphism}\},

\displaystyle E_{\rm reg}^{0}=

\displaystyle E^{0}_{\rm fin}\setminus\overline{E^{0}_{\rm sink}},\qquad E_{\rm sing}^{0}=E^{0}\setminus E^{0}_{\rm reg}.

The elements of
E_{\rm sink}^{0}
are called
sinks
, the elements of
E_{\rm reg}^{0}
are called
regular
, and those of
E^{0}_{\rm sing}
are called
singular
.

Topological quivers give rise to C*-correspondences as follows. Let
A_{E}:=C_{0}(E^{0})
and define an
A
-valued inner product on
C_{c}(E^{1})
by the formula

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle(v):=\int_{r_{E}^{-1}(v)}\overline{\xi(e)}\eta(e)d\lambda^{v}_{E}(e).

Next, let
X_{E}
denote the closure of
C_{c}(E^{1})
with respect to the norm
\|\xi\|:=\sup_{v\in E^{0}}\sqrt{\langle\xi,\xi\rangle(v)}
. Define the left and right actions of
A_{E}
on
X_{E}
by setting

(\xi\cdot f)(e):=\xi(e)f(r_{E}(e)),\qquad e\in E^{1},\quad\xi\in C_{c}(E^{1}),\quad f\in C_{0}(E^{0}),

(\varphi_{E}(f)\xi)(e)=(f\cdot\xi)(e):=f(s_{E}(e))\xi(e),\qquad e\in E^{1},\quad\xi\in C_{c}(E^{1}),\quad f\in C_{0}(E^{0}),

and extending it to
X_{E}
. The pair
(X_{E},A_{E})
is the
quiver correspondence
associated with the topological quiver
E
. The
topological quiver C*-algebra

C^{*}(E)
is the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra associated to the C*-correspondence
(X_{E},A_{E})
.

3
Functoriality of C*-algebras of topological quivers

3.1
Morphisms of topological quivers

The notion of a graph homomorphism can be generalized to topological quivers almost verbatim.

Definition 3.1
.

A
quiver morphism

m=(m^{1},m^{0})
between topological quivers
E
and
F
consists of continuous maps
m^{1}:E^{1}\to F^{1}
and
m^{0}:E^{0}\to F^{0}
making the diagrams

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m^{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m^{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s_{F}}

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m^{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces m^{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{F}}

(2)

commute.

\blacklozenge

It is clear that we obtain a category:

{\bf Q}:\text{topological quivers and quiver morphisms}.

To define a special type of quiver morphisms generalizing admissible graph homomorphisms
[
4
, Section 2]
and factor maps
[
8
, Definition 2.6]
, we need to recall some definitions and notation:

•

A continuous map
f:X\to Y
between locally compact spaces is
proper
if
f^{-1}(K)
is compact for every compact
K\subseteq Y
.

•

If
f:X\to Y
is a measurable map between measurable spaces, then let
f_{*}(\mu)
denote the pushforward of a measure
\mu
on
X
along the map
f
.

•

Let
f:X\to Z
and
g:Y\to Z
be continuous maps. Then the space

X{}_{f}\times_{g}Y:=\{(x,y)\in X\times Y~:~f(x)=g(y)\}\subseteq X\times Y,

together with the maps
(x,y)\mapsto x
and
(x,y)\mapsto y
is the pullback of
f
and
g
in the category of topological spaces.

We are now ready to introduce regular morphisms.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
m:E\to F
be a quiver morphism. We say that
m
is
regular
if

(A1)

m^{1}
and
m^{0}
are proper,

(A2)

m^{1}|_{r_{E}^{-1}(v)}
is injective and
\lambda^{m^{0}(v)}_{F}=m_{*}^{1}(\lambda_{E}^{v})
for all
v\in E^{0}
,

(A3)

(m^{0})^{-1}(F^{0}_{\rm reg})\subseteq E^{0}_{\rm reg}
.

\blacklozenge

Due to the condition (A2), we have the following equality

\int_{r_{F}^{-1}(m^{0}(v))}\xi(x)d\lambda^{m^{0}(v)}_{F}(x)=\int_{r_{E}^{-1}(v)}\xi(m^{1}(e))d\lambda_{E}^{v}(e)

(3)

for every
v\in E^{0}
and
\xi\in C_{c}(F^{1})
, which we will use throughout the paper.

Remark 3.3
.

Our notion of a regular morphism generalizes regular factor maps of Katsura
[
8
, Definition 2.6]
and admissible graph homomorphism in the sense of
[
4
, Section 2]
. See Section
3.2
for details. We also note that the second part of the condition (A2) is analogous to
[
1
, Definition 3.1]
introduced in the context of locally compact groupoids.

\blacklozenge

Proposition 3.4
.

Topological quivers together with regular quiver morphisms form a subcategory of the category
{\bf Q}
.

Proof
.

Clearly,
{\rm id}_{E}=({\rm id}_{E^{0}},{\rm id}_{E^{1}})
is a regular quiver morphism.
Next, let
m:E\to F
and
n:F\to G
be regular quiver morphisms. We have to prove that
n\circ m
is also a regular quiver morphism.
Since composition of proper maps is again proper, we only need to check the conditions (A2) and (A3) of Definition
3.2
.

To prove the first part of the condition (A2), take
v\in E^{1}
and suppose that
n^{1}(m^{1}(e))=n^{1}(m^{1}(e^{\prime}))
for some
e,e^{\prime}\in r_{E}^{-1}(v)
. Since
m^{1}(e),m^{1}(e^{\prime})\in r_{F}^{-1}(m^{0}(v))
, the first part of (A2) for
n^{1}
implies that
m^{1}(e)=m^{1}(e^{\prime})
. Hence, the result follows from the same condition for
m^{1}
. Note that the second part of the condition (A2) follows from a basic property of the pushforward of a measure along a composition of maps, namely

(n^{1}\circ m^{1})_{*}(\lambda_{E}^{v})=n^{1}_{*}(m^{1}_{*}(\lambda_{E}^{v}))=n^{1}_{*}(\lambda_{F}^{m^{0}(v)})=\lambda_{G}^{(n^{0}\circ\,m^{0})(v)},\qquad v\in E^{0}.

Proving (A3) is also straightforward:

(m^{0})^{-1}((n^{0})^{-1}(G^{0}_{\rm reg}))\subseteq(m^{0})^{-1}(F^{0}_{\rm reg})\subseteq E^{0}_{\rm reg}.

\blacksquare

We introduce the following notation:

{\bf Q}_{\rm reg}:~\text{topological quivers and regular quiver morphisms}.

3.2
Relation to factor maps and admissible graph homomorphisms

Let us recall the notion of a topological graph introduced by Katsura in
[
6
]
(note that we use different convention, i.e. the source and range maps are interchanged). A
topological graph

E
is a quadruple
(E^{1},E^{1},s_{E},r_{E})
, where
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are locally compact topological spaces,
r_{E}:E^{1}\to E^{0}
is a local homeomorphism and
s_{E}:E^{1}\to E^{0}
is a continuous map.

Next, we discuss the relation between topological graphs and topological quivers. Let
E^{1}
and
E^{0}
be second countable. For each
v\in E^{0}
and every Borel set
B
in
E^{1}
, we consider the counting measures

\lambda^{v}_{E}(B):=\begin{cases}|B\cap r_{E}^{-1}(v)|&\text{if }B\cap r_{E}^{-1}(v)\text{ is finite}\\
\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since
r_{E}^{-1}(v)
is countable, the above formula gives rise to a Radon measure on
E^{1}
with support
r_{E}^{-1}(v)
. Then
(E^{1},E^{0},s_{E},r_{E},\lambda_{E})
, where
\lambda_{E}:=\{\lambda^{v}_{E}\}_{v\in E^{0}}
, defines a topological quiver. Let us also mention that Katsura‘s definition of the set of regular vertices
E^{0}_{\rm reg}
coincides with the same notion for topological quivers in the case of topological graphs.

Let
\widetilde{X}:=X\cup\{\infty\}
denotes the one-point compactification of a locally compact space
X
.

Definition 3.5
.

[
8
, Definition 2.1]

A
factor map

\widetilde{m}
from a topological graph
E
to a topological graph
F
is a pair
(\widetilde{m}^{1},\widetilde{m}^{0})
consisting of continuous maps
\widetilde{m}^{0}:\widetilde{E}^{0}\to\widetilde{F}^{0}
and
\widetilde{m}^{1}:\widetilde{E}^{1}\to\widetilde{F}^{1}
sending
\infty
to
\infty
and such that

(F1)

For every
e\in E^{1}
with
\widetilde{m}^{1}(e)\in F^{1}
, we have that
s_{F}(\widetilde{m}^{1}(e))=\widetilde{m}^{0}(s_{E}(e))
and
r_{F}(\widetilde{m}^{1}(e))=\widetilde{m}^{0}(r_{E}(e))
.

(F2)

If
x\in F^{1}
and
v\in E^{0}
satisfies
r_{F}(x)=\widetilde{m}^{0}(v)
, then there exists a unique element
e\in E^{1}
such that
\widetilde{m}^{1}(e)=x
and
r_{E}(e)=v
.

\blacklozenge

Definition 3.6
.

[
8
, Definition 2.6]

A factor map
\widetilde{m}:E\to F
is called
regular
if
s_{E}^{-1}(v)
is non-empty and contained in
(\widetilde{m}^{1})^{-1}(F^{1})
for every
v\in E^{0}
with
\widetilde{m}^{0}(v)\in F^{0}_{\rm reg}
.

\blacklozenge

Let us prove the equivalence of the notion of a regular factor map and a regular quiver morphism in the setting of topological graphs.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
E
and
F
be topological graphs. A pair
(\widetilde{m}^{1},\widetilde{m}^{0})
is a regular factor map if and only if
(m^{1},m^{0})
is a regular quiver morphism.

Proof
.

First, note that the condition (F1) of Definition
3.5
for
(\widetilde{m}^{1},\widetilde{m}^{0})
is equivalent to
(m^{1},m^{0})
being a quiver morphism. Next, for locally compact Hausdorff spaces
X
and
Y
, it is well-known that if
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{Y}
is a continuous map sending
\infty
to
\infty
, then
f:=\widetilde{f}|_{X}
is a proper continuous map from
X
to
Y
. Conversely, if
f:X\to Y
is a proper continuous map, then extending
f
to
\widetilde{f}
by putting
\widetilde{f}(\infty)=\infty
gives rise to a continuous map from
\widetilde{X}
to
\widetilde{Y}
. Hence,
(\widetilde{m}^{1},\widetilde{m}^{0})
is a pair of continuous maps sending
\infty
to
\infty
if and only if
(m^{1},m^{0})
is a pair of proper continuous maps, i.e. satisfying the condition (A1) of Definition
3.2
.

Next, assume the condition (F2) of Definition
3.5
. Then, clearly
m^{1}
restricted to
{r_{E}^{-1}(v)}
is injective for every
v\in E^{0}
. We need to prove that
\lambda_{F}^{m^{0}(v)}=m^{1}_{*}(\lambda_{E}^{v})
for every
v\in E^{0}
. Recall that
m^{1}_{*}(\lambda^{v}_{E})(B)=\lambda_{E}^{v}((m^{1})^{-1}(B))
for every Borel set
B\subseteq E^{1}
. Due to the condition (F2), we have that
|B\cap r_{F}^{-1}(m^{0}(v))|=|(m^{1})^{-1}(B)\cap r^{-1}_{E}(v)|
so the claim follows. Now, assume the condition (A2) of Definition
3.2
. Take any
x\in F^{1}
and
v\in E^{0}
such that
r_{F}(x)=m^{0}(v)
. Then

1=\lambda_{F}^{m^{0}(v)}(x)=m^{1}_{*}(\lambda_{E}^{v})(x)=\lambda_{E}^{v}((m^{1})^{-1}(x))=|(m^{1})^{-1}(x)\cap r_{E}^{-1}(v)|.

Hence, there exists a unique
e\in E^{1}
such that
m^{1}(e)=x
and
r_{E}(e)=v
.

Finally, let the factor map
(\widetilde{m}^{1},\widetilde{m}^{0})
be regular. Then
(m^{1},m^{0})
satisfies the condition (A3) due to
[
8
, Lemma 2.7]
. Conversely, the condition (A3) of Definition
3.2
clearly implies regularity of
(\widetilde{m}^{1},\widetilde{m}^{0})
.

\blacksquare

3.3
Contravariant functor from quivers to correspondences

In this section, we show that regular admissible quiver morphisms between topological quivers lead to
*
-homomorphisms between their quiver C*-algebras. First, we need some preliminary results.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
m:E\to F
be an regular quiver morphism. Then the assignments

\mu^{0}:A_{F}\longrightarrow A_{E},\qquad f\longmapsto f\circ m^{0},

\mu^{1}:C_{c}(F^{1})\longrightarrow C_{c}(E^{1}),\qquad\xi\longmapsto\xi\circ m^{1},

give rise to a correspondence morphism
(\mu^{1},\mu^{0}):(X_{F},A_{F})\to(X_{E},A_{E})
.

Proof
.

Since
m^{1}
and
m^{0}
are proper,
\mu^{1}
and
\mu^{0}
are well defined. Routine calculations show that
\mu^{1}
is linear and that
\mu^{0}
is a
*
-homomorphism.
The equation (
3
) implies that

\displaystyle\langle\mu^{1}(\xi),\mu^{1}(\eta)\rangle(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{E}^{-1}(v)}\overline{\xi(m^{1}(e))}\eta(m^{1}(e))d\lambda_{E}^{v}(e)=\int_{r_{F}^{-1}(m^{0}(v))}\overline{\xi(x)}\eta(x)d\lambda_{F}^{m^{0}(v)}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle(m^{0}(v))=\mu^{0}(\langle\xi,\eta\rangle)(v).

(4)

We infer that

\|\mu^{1}(\xi)\|^{2}=\sup_{v\in E^{0}}\langle\mu^{1}(\xi),\mu^{1}(\xi)\rangle(v)=\sup_{v\in E^{0}}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle(m^{0}(v))=\sup_{w\in m^{0}(E^{0})}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle(w)\leq\|\xi\|^{2}.

Hence,
\mu^{1}
is continuous and extends uniquely to a continuous linear map
X_{F}\to X_{E}
which we denote by the same symbol. Due to continuity of
\mu^{1}
and the inner product, (
4
) also shows that the pair
(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})
satisfies the condition (C2) of Definition
2.1
. Next, we check the condition (C1):

\displaystyle\mu^{1}(f\cdot\xi)(e)

\displaystyle=(f\cdot\xi)(m^{1}(e))=f(s_{F}(m^{1}(e)))\xi(m^{1}(e))

\displaystyle=f(m^{0}(s_{E}(e))\xi(m^{1}(e))=(\mu^{0}(f)\cdot\mu^{1}(\xi))(e),

where
e\in E^{1}
,
f\in C_{0}(F^{0})
, and
\xi\in C_{c}(F^{1})
. By the continuity of
\mu^{1}
and the left action, we conclude that (C1) holds for all elements of
X_{F}
.

\blacksquare

.

Note that we did not need to use the condition (A3) of Definition
3.2
to prove the above lemma.

The next lemma is a technical result needed for proving that the correspondence morphism defined in Lemma

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
m:E\to F
be an regular quiver morphism and let
(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})
be its associated correspondence morphism. If
g\in C_{0}(F^{1})
is such that for every
x\in{\rm osupp}\,g
there is an open neighborhood
U_{x}
of
x
such that
r_{F}|_{U_{x}}:U_{x}\to r_{F}(U_{x})
is a homeomorphism, then

(\mu^{1})^{(1)}(\sigma_{F}(g))=\sigma_{E}(\mu^{1}(g)).

Here
\sigma_{E}
and
\sigma_{F}
are the
*
-homomorphisms defined in
[
12
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Proof
.

First, recall that for every
g\in C_{0}(F^{1})
and
\varepsilon>0
there is a function
\zeta\in C_{c}(F^{1})
which is
\varepsilon
-close to
g
in sup-norm and such that
{\rm osupp}\,\zeta\subseteq{\rm osupp}\,g
(see, e.g.
[
12
, Lemma 3.10]
). Since
(\mu^{1})^{(1)}\circ\sigma_{F}
and
\sigma_{E}\circ\mu^{1}
are continuous, it suffices to prove the claim for
\zeta
as above.
Using our assumptions on
g
and arguing as in the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 3.11]
(see also
[
6
, Lemma 1.15 and 1.16]
), we conclude that
\sigma_{F}(\zeta)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{\xi_{i},\eta_{i}}
for
\xi_{i},\eta_{i}\in C_{c}(F^{1})
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, defined as follows:

•

Since
\zeta
is compactly supported, there exist finitely many
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in F^{1}
and their open neighborhoods
U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}
such that
r_{F}
is injective when restricted to them and such that
{\rm supp}\,\zeta\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}
.

•

Next, let
\chi_{i}
be the compactly supported partition of unity associated to the open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
.

•

Finally, put
\xi_{i}(x):=\zeta(x)\sqrt{\chi_{i}(x)}
and
\eta_{i}(x):=\sqrt{\chi_{i}(x)}\lambda^{r_{F}(x)}_{F}(\{x\})^{-1}
for all
x\in F^{1}
and
i=1,\ldots,m
. One can prove that
\xi_{i},\eta_{i}\in C_{c}(F^{1})
and that
{\rm supp}\,\xi_{i}={\rm supp}\,\eta_{i}\subseteq U_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
. Furthermore, we have that

\xi_{i}(x)\int_{r_{F}^{-1}(r_{F}(x))}\overline{\eta_{i}(x^{\prime})}h(x^{\prime})d\lambda_{F}^{r_{F}(x)}(x^{\prime})=\xi_{i}(x)\sqrt{\chi_{i}(x)}h(x)

(5)

for every
x\in F^{1}
and
h\in C_{c}(F^{1})
.

Note that
r_{E}^{-1}(r_{E}(e))\subseteq E^{1}
is closed for every
e\in E^{1}
and that
m^{1}
restricted to
r_{E}^{-1}(r_{E}(e))
is injective by the condition (A2). Take any
f\in C_{c}(E^{1})
and
e\in E^{1}
. Then, by Tietze extension theorem, there is
h_{e}\in C_{c}(F^{1})
such that
f=h_{e}\circ m^{1}
on
r_{E}^{-1}(r_{E}(e))
. Therefore, for any
f\in C_{c}(E^{1})
, we have that

\displaystyle\left((\mu^{1})^{(1)}(\sigma_{F}(\zeta))f\right)(e)

\displaystyle=\left((\mu^{1})^{(1)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{\xi_{i},\eta_{i}}\right)f\right)(e)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{\mu^{1}(\xi_{i}),\mu^{1}(\eta_{i})}f\right)(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mu^{1}(\xi_{i}))(e)\langle\mu^{1}(\eta_{i}),f\rangle(r_{E}(e))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}(m^{1}(e))\int_{r_{E}^{-1}(r_{E}(e))}\overline{\eta_{i}(m^{1}(e^{\prime}))}h_{e}(m^{1}(e^{\prime}))d\lambda^{r_{E}(e)}_{E}(e^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}(m^{1}(e))\int_{r_{F}^{-1}(r_{F}(m^{1}(e)))}\overline{\eta_{i}(x)}h_{e}(x)d\lambda^{r_{F}(m^{1}(e))}_{F}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i}(m^{1}(e))\sqrt{\chi_{i}(m^{1}(e))}f(e)=\zeta(m^{1}(e))f(e)=(\sigma_{E}(\mu^{1}(\zeta))f)(e).

Here we used (
3
) and (
5
).

\blacksquare

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
m:E\to F
be a regular quiver morphism. Then the correspondence morphism
(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})
defined in Lemma
3.8
is covariant.

Proof
.

To prove the condition (C3) of Definition
2.1
, recall that
J_{X_{E}}=C_{0}(E^{0}_{\rm reg})
for any quiver correspondence
(X_{E},A_{E})
. Hence, (C3) follows directly from the condition (A3) of Definition
3.2
. Indeed, if
f\in C_{0}(F^{0}_{\rm reg})
then
f(w)=0
for all
w\notin F^{0}_{\rm reg}
. Now, the condition (A3) implies that
m^{0}(v)\notin F^{0}_{\rm reg}
for any
v\notin E^{0}_{\rm reg}
. Hence,
\mu^{0}(f)(v)=f(m^{0}(v))=0
for any
v\notin E^{0}_{\rm reg}
, which implies that
\mu^{0}(f)\in C_{0}(E^{0}_{\rm reg})
for all
f\in C_{0}(F^{0}_{\rm reg})
. Finally, we prove the condition (C4). Due to
[
12
, Theorem 3.11]
, Lemma
3.10
, and the fact that
\varphi_{E}(f)=\sigma_{E}(f\circ s_{E})
, it suffices to prove that

\mu^{1}(f\circ s_{F})=\mu^{0}(f)\circ s_{E}

for all
f\in C_{0}(E^{0}_{\rm reg})
. If
s_{E}(e)\notin E^{0}_{\rm reg}
, then
m^{0}(s_{E}(e))\notin F^{0}_{\rm reg}
, so that
\mu^{0}(f)(s_{E}(e))=0=f(m^{0}(s_{E}(e)))
. Therefore, we have that

\mu^{1}(f\circ s_{F})(e)=f(s_{F}(m^{1}(e)))=f(m^{0}(s_{E}(e)))=\mu^{0}(f)(s_{E}(e)),\qquad e\in E^{1}.

\blacksquare

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
m:E\to F
be an regular quiver morphism. Then there exists a gauge-equivariant
*
-homomorphism
\mu:\mathcal{O}_{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{E}
such that the assignments

E\longmapsto\mathcal{O}_{E},\qquad m\longmapsto\mu,

give rise to a contravariant functor from the category
{\bf Q}_{\rm reg}
to the category
{\bf C}^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}
of
\mathbb{T}
-C*-algebras and
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant
*
-homomorphisms. If
E^{0}
and
F^{0}
are compact, then
\mu
is unital.

Proof
.

First, note that the assignments
E\mapsto(X_{E},A_{E})
and
(m^{1},m^{0})\mapsto(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})
define a contravariant functor from
{\bf Q}_{\rm reg}
to
{\bf Corr}^{*}_{\rm cov}
. Indeed, combining Lemma
3.8
and Lemma
3.11
, we have that
(\mu^{1},\mu^{0})

gives a contravariant functor is straightforward. Finally, we compose the above functor with the covariant functor from the category
{\bf Corr}_{\rm cov}^{*}
to the category
{\bf C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{*}
described in Proposition
2.3
to obtain the desired contravariant functor from
\bf{Q}_{\rm reg}
to

.

Integrating multimodal data for precise subtyping and prognostication in patients with pulmonary mucinous adenocarcinoma
Authors: Weiwei Yang, Chen Chen, Lue Li, Zehao Chen, Ziyi Chen, Hanbo Le, Yongkui Zhang, Wangyu Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPulmonary mucinous adenocarcinoma (PMA) represents a rare lung adenocarcinoma subtype, characterized by a lack of comprehensive pathological classification and prognostic factors. In this study, we propose a multimodal machine learning (ML) framework that integrates multiple data modalities to enhance PMA subtype classification and prognostic prediction.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled 175 primary PMA cases with details of demographic, histopathological, computed tomography (CT) imaging, and epithelial growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation data. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regularized logistic regression model was utilized for histological classification, and Cox proportional hazards model was employed for survival prediction with internal validation.ResultsPure mucinous adenocarcinoma presented a higher prevalence of smaller radiographic or pathological tumor size, lower lobe localization, lower rates of lymph node metastasis and spread through air spaces (STAS), early pathological stage, carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) ≤5 ng/mL, spiculated margin, Ki-67 low expression and lower frequency of EGFR exon 19 deletion (P=0.002, 0.003, <0.001, <0.001, 0.001, <0.001, 0.002, 0.03, 0.02, 0.001, respectively) compared to mix mucinous and mucin-secreting adenocarcinoma. The ML-derived nomogram achieved discriminative ability, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.792 in the training set and 0.687 in the validation set, and exhibited satisfactory calibration for the preoperative diagnosis of pure mucinous adenocarcinoma. Multivariate Cox modeling identified higher CEA levels, and EGFR exon 19 deletion as independent prognostic factors in PMA (P=0.04, 0.049, respectively). Moreover, Kaplan-Meier curves revealed significantly different outcomes between low- and high-risk groups stratified by the nomogram score (P<0.001).ConclusionsPulmonary pure mucinous adenocarcinoma exhibited lower malignancy compared to the mixed mucinous and mucin-secreting type. The nomogram model developed and validated in this study exhibited promising efficacy in predicting histological subtype preoperatively and survival in patients with PMA, offering guidance for clinicians in diagnosis and prognosis prediction.Keywords: Histological characteristics, machine learning (ML), nomogram, prognosis, pulmonary mucinous adenocarcinoma (PMA)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Pulmonary pure mucinous adenocarcinoma (PMA) exhibited lower malignancy compared with the mixed mucinous and mucin-secreting type. The developed nomogram model demonstrated exceptional efficacy in predicting the histological subtype of PMA. The prognostic nomogram model effectively predicted PMA survival.

What is known and what is new?

• PMA represents a rare subtype of lung adenocarcinoma and is a heterogeneous entity. However, its malignant potential and optimal pathological classification remain incompletely defined, and practical tools for predicting histological subtype and prognosis are lacking.

• This study provides evidence that PMA represents a distinct entity with reduced malignancy compared with the mixed mucinous and mucin-secreting type. It further introduces two practical predictive tools: a preoperative nomogram for predicting the histological subtype of PMA and a prognostic nomogram for predicting PMA survival. In addition, independent prognostic factors for PMA were identified.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• These findings support the potential pathological classification of PMA, with the pure mucinous type recognized as a distinct entity with lower malignancy. The diagnostic and prognostic nomograms offer practical tools that can aid clinical decision-making and risk stratification for patients with PMA. Further validation in multicenter cohorts is warranted before these models are adopted into routine practice.

Introduction

Lung cancer poses a significant global health burden, being the most prevalent cancer and the primary cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide (1,2). Pulmonary mucinous adenocarcinoma (PMA), a rare subtype comprising less than 1% of non-small cell lung carcinomas (NSCLC), is characterized by the presence of malignant epithelial cells with extracellular mucin pools. Analysis of 2018 U.S. cancer registry data at the population level revealed that PMA constituted 0.15% of lung cancer diagnoses, underscoring its epidemiological rarity (3). PMA typically presents with nonspecific clinical symptoms that can overlap with those of inflammatory and metastatic conditions, leading to diagnostic complexities. While population-based studies have indicated similar survival outcomes between PMA and non-mucinous adenocarcinomas, emerging data suggest potential variations in treatment responses and recurrence patterns, highlighting the need for further molecular stratification (4,5).

Specifically, the three-dimensional distribution patterns of acinar/papillary components and mucin deposition density are associated with the risk of lymphovascular invasion and chemotherapy response rates in PMA. These findings highlight the importance of incorporating assessments of tumor heterogeneity into PMA prognostic frameworks and therapeutic decision-making algorithms (6). Furthermore, different pathological variants of PMA may exhibit distinct associations with various clinical characteristics. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis of factors across the pathological subgroups of PMA is crucial for optimizing both diagnosis and treatment strategies.

In 2015, the World Health Organization (WHO) established a classification system for mucinous adenocarcinoma of the lung, which includes mucinous adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), mucinous minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma (IMA), mixed invasive mucinous and non-mucinous adenocarcinoma, and colloid adenocarcinoma, albeit with a weak recommendation and low-quality evidence (7,8). A detailed pathological subtype classification for PMA is currently lacking. Our previous research has delineated the pathological features of PMA based on the WHO criteria, enabling a more refined classification into pure mucinous, mixed mucinous, and mucin-secreting subtypes (9). Moreover, the correlation between PMA subtypes and clinical characteristics remains uncertain, and there is a need to identify further diagnostic and prognostic factors for PMA patients. Therefore, identification of predictive factors for PMA holds important clinical implications for optimizing patient management.

Recently, machine learning (ML), a subset of artificial intelligence (AI), has been employed to extract valuable information and identify elusive risk factors to enhance prediction accuracy by improving the handling of missing data and nonlinear parameters in cancer research (10,11). Numerous studies have demonstrated the benefits of ML in diagnosis and predicting prognosis in various types of lung cancers: Gould et al. constructed an ML based model to enhance the accuracy of NSCLC diagnosis using clinical and laboratory parameters (12); Zeng et al. developed a nomogram model for predicting survival in surgically treated limited-stage small cell lung cancer patients by incorporating demographic and clinical variables (10); and Yang et al. identified risk factors derived from genomic features, clinical profiles, and demographics using ML models, offering valuable insights to aid clinicians in devising personalized treatment strategies (13).

While an increasing number of studies have integrated pathology, radiology and genomics to developed a predictive model in types of lung cancer, research on prediction model for PMA remains limited. This study employs ML techniques to analyze 175 cases of primary PMA, aiming to explore the relationship between PMA subtypes and clinical characteristics, develop predictive nomogram models for the diagnosis and prognosis of PMA patients, and validate the effectiveness of the predictive models using internal data. Thus, specific clinical features can serve as predictive markers for determining potential pathological subtypes and estimating survival in PMA patients in clinical practice. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1241/rc).

Methods

Patients

The study cohort was derived from the prospectively maintained electronic medical record (EMR) system of Zhoushan Hospital, Zhejiang Province, China. This retrospective analysis enrolled 175 patients diagnosed with PMA who underwent surgical resection at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Zhoushan Hospital, between January 2012 and December 2023. Demographic parameters consisted of age at diagnosis, sex, and smoking history. Patients underwent either lobectomy or segmentectomy with systematic of selective lymph node dissection, or wedge resection with lymph node sampling, depending on tumor location and invasiveness. Preoperative serum levels of carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) were also collected using a Cobas e602 automated chemiluminescence analyzer (Roche, Mannheim, Germany). The pathological specimens were examined by two senior pathologists with expertise in lung cancer. The inclusion criteria consisted of: (I) histologically verified PMA; (II) prior surgical interventions; (III) comprehensive tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging data; (IV) follow-up information on mortality. Exclusion criteria included: (I) neoadjuvant treatment prior to surgery; (II) other histological subtypes of lung cancer; (III) multiple nodules. Ultimately, 175 cases were included, comprising 25 deaths (14.3%) and 150 survivors (85.7%). The patients with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation did not receive EGFR inhibitor therapy except one patient with brain metastasis. To ensure robust model evaluation and maximize data utility, the complete data set was randomly partitioned into a training set (70%) and an independent internal validation set (30%). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Zhoushan Hospital (Approval No.: Zhoushan Hospital Ethical Review 2025-052-01) with informed consent waived due to its retrospective nature.

Pathological evaluation

Surgically resected tissue specimens were immediately fixed in 10% neutral formalin and then sectioned sequentially at 5 mm intervals to allow for thorough histological assessment. Full-thickness tumor sampling was performed to ensure complete histomorphological analysis. Two board-certified pathologists conducted independent blinded evaluation of hematoxylin and eosin-stained sections. Histopathological assessment focused on quantitatively evaluating predominant architectural patterns, including papillary, micropapillary, acinar, and solid subtypes. Each component was proportionally quantified as a percentage of the total tumor area, and the presence of spread through air spaces (STAS) was also assessed. The histopathological parameters encompassed PMA subclassification: the pure mucinous type, characterized by tumors composed entirely of mucinous cells; the mixed mucinous type, defined by tumors containing >10% mucinous cells along with other histological components (e.g., lepidic, papillary, acinar, or solid patterns); and the mucin-secreting type, referring to tumors with >10% extracellular mucin deposition (9), as shown in Figure 1.

Computed tomography (CT) examination and assessment

High-resolution CT (HRCT) scan was conducted preoperatively by using a 16-row multislice CT scanner with the lung window settings as follows: level =−500/width =1,500 HU, and two board-certified radiologists independently interpreted the images under blinded conditions. The CT characteristics of pulmonary lesions evaluated comprised tumor size, lobulation, spiculated edges, vacuole signs, pleural traction, alveolar collapse, margin clarity, mixed density, air bronchogram, and vascular convergence signs. The CT features specific to PMA subtypes were detailed in Figure 1.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical staining was performed using 3,3'-diaminobenzidine chromogen. Two independent pathologists conducted blinded semi-quantitative assessments using microscopy (Olympus BX53, Japan), recording both the intensity and spatial distribution patterns of immunoreactivity. Staining intensity was scored as follows: 0= negative, 1= weak, 2= moderate, and 3= strong. The percentage of positive cells was scored as: 0 (≤10%), 1 (10–39%), 2 (40–69%), and 3 (≥70%). A total score was calculated as the product of the intensity and extent scores. Cases with a total score ≤2 (weak or negative staining) were categorized as low expression, whereas those with a score >2 (strong staining) were categorized as high expression. The antibodies included P63 (Maixin Biotech, Fuzhou, China), cytokeratin 7 (CK7) (Maixin Biotech, China), Ki-67 (ZSBio, Beijing, China), Napsin A (Maixin Biotech, China), and thyroid transcription factor-1 (TTF-1) (Maixin Biotech, China).

EGFR mutations

Paraffin-embedded tissues were employed for the detection of EGFR mutations in histologically confirmed patients. Nucleic acid was extracted from 2 to 5 sections by using QIAamp DNA FFPE tissue Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany), followed by spectrophotometric quantification of DNA concentration and purity (A260/A280 ratio ≥1.8) using the Quawell Q3000 spectrophotometer (Quawell Technology, San Jose, CA, USA). EGFR mutations were detected on ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR system (ABI, Foster City, CA, USA) utilizing a commercial kit based on the amplification refractory mutation system (Yuanqi Diagnostics, Shanghai, China), following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Follow-up and death ascertainment

Disease progression data were collected through semi-annual structured telephone interviews, medical records, or the regional population death registry maintained by Zhoushan Municipal Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Follow-up was calculated from the date of curative resection to the time of death or the last follow-up on September 30, 2024. Overall survival (OS) served as the key outcome. The cohort achieved 100% follow-up completeness with no patients lost to follow-up. The adjudicators of the death outcome were blinded to the predictors.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R 4.4.1 (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). The data set contained 8.3% missing data thus complete-case analysis was adopted. Prior to modeling, all predictor variables were standardized. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. To ensure robust model evaluation and maximize data utility, the complete data set was randomly partitioned into a training set (70%) and an internal validation set (30%). The randomization was performed using a computer-generated algorithm with a fixed seed (seed =123) to guarantee reproducibility. The training set was applied to the validation set to calculate the predicted risk for each patient. Additionally, stratified sampling approach was applied based on the outcome variable to preserve the distribution of event rates between the two sets.

Predictor selection, hyperparameter tuning, and internal validation via bootstrapping, were conducted exclusively on the training set to develop the model. The internal validation set was held out entirely during this phase and was used only for the final, single assessment of the model’s discrimination, calibration, and overall clinical utility. For the diagnostic model, independent predictors of pathological type were identified using binary logistic regression via the base glm() function (binomial family), indicating odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression (glmnet package) for feature selection of pathological subtypes, followed by predictive nomogram model construction. Model discrimination was assessed by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), while clinical utility was assessed via decision curve analysis (DCA) (dca.r package). For the prognostic model, survival outcomes were analyzed using Cox proportional hazards regression [coxph() in the survival package]. The proportional hazards assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residuals. Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs were demonstrated, and the Cox model was used to plot the nomogram. Model calibration was verified using bootstrap-corrected calibration curves (rms package). Survival probabilities were estimated by the Kaplan-Meier method, and intergroup differences were compared using the log-rank test. To stratify patients into low- and high-risk categories, the median of the nomogram-derived risk scores was employed as the primary cutoff, ensuring balanced group sizes and statistical power. Additionally, to evaluate the robustness of this cutoff, an exploratory analysis was performed to use X-tile software (version 3.6.1, Yale University, USA). All analyses adhered to two-tailed testing principles, with statistical significance defined as P<0.05.

Results

Characteristics of PMA and development and validation of a predictive model

Among the 175 patients diagnosed with PMA, 113 (64.6%) cases were classified as pure mucinous type, 36 (20.6%) as mixed mucinous type, and 26 (14.8%) cases with mucin-secreting type. Nevertheless, pure mucinous adenocarcinoma was notably associated with smaller radiographic or pathological tumor size, lower lobe localization, lower rates of lymph node metastasis and STAS, early pathological stage, CEA ≤5 ng/mL, spiculated margin, Ki-67 low expression and lower frequency of EGFR exon 19 deletion (P=0.002, 0.003, <0.001, <0.001, 0.001, <0.001, 0.002, 0.03, 0.02, 0.001, respectively, Table 1). In the CT imaging analysis, pure mucinous type demonstrated a significantly higher prevalence of pleural traction and spiculated margins compared to mixed mucinous type (P=0.02, 0.01, respectively). However, no statistically significant differences were observed between pure mucinous type and mucin-secreting type for these two imaging biomarkers (P=0.22, 0.57, respectively). Additionally, comparative analysis revealed no statistically significant difference between mixed mucinous and mucin-secreting subtypes in tumor size, tumor localization, lymph node metastasis, STAS, pathological stage, and CEA level (P all >0.05). Moreover, expression level of P63, CK7, Napsin A, and TTF-1 did not exhibit significant variations among different subtypes of PMA (P all >0.05, Table 1).

LASSO regression was then applied to identify parameters associated with different subtypes of PMA (Figure 2A,2B), followed by the development of a nomogram based on the four selected parameters (Figure 2C). The nomogram is primarily intended for preoperative stratification, with non-lower lobe tumor location, elevated serum CEA, radiographic tumor size, and mixed density retained as key predictive covariates for identifying the non-pure subtype. According to the nomogram scoring, tumor location contributed most strongly to the total score. The model achieved an AUC of 0.792 in the training set (Figure 2D) and 0.687 in the validation set (Figure 2E), indicating satisfactory discriminative performance. The calibration plot demonstrated good agreement [mean absolute error (MAE) =0.041], with some deviations observed within the intermediate probability ranges (Figure 3A). The DCA showed that the nomogram model provided greater net benefit across a wide range of high-risk thresholds compared to dichotomous risk classification strategies. A sharp decline in the nomogram curve was observed around a threshold of 0.2 (Figure 3B). This decline implied that the model’s false-negative or false-positive rate increased within this range, which reduced its net benefit.

Analysis of prognostic factors and predictive modeling for PMA

Univariate Cox proportional hazards analysis revealed larger tumor size, lymph node metastasis, mixed mucinous and mucin-secreting type, advanced pathological stage, higher CEA levels, indistinct margin, Ki-67 high expression and EGFR exon 19 deletion were distinctly correlated with poor prognosis (P=0.002, <0.001, 0.008, <0.001, <0.001, 0.003, 0.01, 0.02, respectively, Table 3). Subsequently, variables showing univariate significance were included in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression. Higher CEA levels (HR =4.759, 95% CI: 1.094–20.695, P=0.04), and EGFR exon 19 deletion (HR =4.728, 95% CI: 1.005–22.246, P=0.049), were identified as independent prognostic factors in PMA (Table 3).

LASSO regression analysis identified CEA levels, Ki-67 expression, EGFR exon 19 deletion, tumor size, lymph node metastasis, histological type, indistinct margin, and stage as factors associated with postoperative survival in patients with PMA (Figure 4A,4B). These parameters emerged as significant indicators of adverse survival, highlighting their clinical relevance in risk stratification. The prognostic model integrated critical determinants including pathological tumor size >2.0 cm, lymph node metastasis, advanced pathological stage, non-pure histology, CEA levels >5 ng/mL, indistinct margins, Ki-67 high expression and EGFR exon 19 deletion. Based on these predictive factors, a nomogram was developed to predict 3- and 5-year OS, and among which CEA level contributed most strongly to the nomogram score (Figure 4C). In the integrated prognostic model, the Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) was 0.888 (95% CI: 0.808–0.896) in the training set and 0.770 (95% CI: 0.606–0.934) in the validation set. Calibration curves (Figure 5) for 3- and 5-year survival yielded MAE of 0.038 and 0.047 in the training set (Figure 5A,5C), and 0.041 and 0.056 in the validation set (Figure 5B,5D), respectively. By contrast, a benchmark model constructed using pathological stage alone exhibited notably inferior discrimination, with C-indices of 0.738 (95% CI: 0.603–0.873) and 0.583 (95% CI: 0.432–0.734) across the two cohorts, and corresponding calibration MAEs of 0.023/0.021 and 0.039/0.045 for 3- and 5-year predictions (Figure S1A-S1D). These data confirm that our model provides incremental prognostic accuracy beyond conventional staging. Furthermore, we performed an ablation analysis to evaluate the models independence on established clinicopathological variables. Upon systematic exclusion of canonical predictors, including pathological stage, tumor size, lymph node status, STAS, and Ki-67 expression, the model retained robust discriminative capacity, achieving a C-index of 0.840 (95% CI: 0.772–0.958) in the training set and 0.668 (95% CI: 0.493–0.843) in the validation set, with calibration MAEs of 0.027/0.029 and 0.038/0.049 for 3- and 5-year endpoints, respectively (Figure S1E-S1H). This demonstrates that the model maintains substantial predictive value independent of well-characterized prognostic covariates. In addition, we constructed a series of stepwise models to evaluate the incremental value of multimodal integration: a clinical characters model, a model combining clinical features with CT findings, a model combining clinical features with CT findings and serum CEA, and the full multimodal model. The corresponding C-indices were 0.838 (95% CI: 0.747–0.929), 0.841 (95% CI: 0.751–0.931), 0.873 (95% CI: 0.784–0.962), and 0.875 (95% CI: 0.781–0.991), respectively. The stepwise addition of CT features and serum CEA progressively improved model discrimination, confirming the incremental benefit of multimodal integration.

By calculating the nomogram-derived risk scores (range: 0–375 points) for each patient, we determined the statistically optimal cutoff value through survival-based cutoff point analysis. A threshold of 34 points was established to stratify patients into different prognostic groups: individuals with scores ≤34 points formed the low-risk group (n=119), while those with scores >34 points constituted the high-risk group (n=56). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed significantly distinct clinical outcomes between the two risk groups, as evidenced by a remarkable separation in the OS curves (P<0.001, Figure 5E). Moreover, although histological subtype of PMA was not identified as an independent factor for the outcome of patients with PMA, Kaplan-Meier curve displayed that cases with pure mucinous type had longer OS than those with mixed mucinous type and mucin-secreting type (P=0.03, 0.01, respectively, Figure 5F).

Discussion

PMA represents a rare subtype of lung adenocarcinoma, characterized by its low incidence rate yet sharing a similar epidemiological profile with other lung adenocarcinoma subtypes (14). Noteworthy distinctions in clinical, pathological, genetic, and CT characteristics set PMA apart from non-mucinous lung adenocarcinomas (15-17). Notably, our previous research identified heterogeneous PMA subtypes based on variations in mucinous cellularity and extracellular mucinous matrix composition (9). Despite these advancements, inconsistent survival persistently exists, likely due to limited sample sizes and diverse analytical approaches. A key challenge lies in integrating multifaceted data including clincopathological, genetic and CT imaging features, to accurately predict prognosis. ML-based nomogram models offer a promising tool for distinguishing PMA from non-mucinous adenocarcinomas by leveraging high-dimensional data to capture nonlinear relationships and enhance predictive accuracy (18). However, the systematic application of nomogram models to assess histological subtype and survival outcomes in PMA, remains scarce, highlighting an understudied area. Addressing this gap could refine risk stratification and guide personalized therapeutic interventions for this rare yet clinically complex malignancy.

Our study reaffirmed previous observations that PMA is predominantly associated with the lower lobe of the lung, T1 stage, N0 stage, and pathological stage I (19). Notably, we found that the pure mucinous type of PMA exhibited less aggressive behavior compared with the mixed mucinous and mucin-secreting types, as characterized by smaller tumor size, earlier pathological stage, CEA level ≤5 ng/mL, lower rates of lymph node metastasis and STAS, low Ki-67 expression, and a lower frequency of EGFR exon 19 deletion. Hwang et al. classified the malignant degree of PMA based on the extent of lepidic and non-lepidic invasive patterns, noting that invasive patterns were least common in small tumors and progressively increased in larger tumors, suggesting a correlation between histological subtype of PMA and tumor progression (20). Moreover, consistent with prior reports, EGFR mutation rates in PMA vary significantly across populations (0–33.3%) due to racial and technical disparities (21-23). A subsequent investigation revealed EGFR mutations in 3 out of 41 metastatic PMA cases, which showed favorable clinical responses to EGFR-targeted therapy (24). Our results indicated EGFRexon 19 and 21 mutations accounted for 4.8% and 2.4%, respectively. Crucially, mucin-secreting type exhibited a higher frequency of EGFR mutation compared to pure mucinous type, consistent with another Chinese cohort (23). Additionally, in CT imaging features, the pure mucinous type showed a notably higher prevalence of pleural traction and spiculated margins compared to mixed mucinous type, while being similar to mucin-secreting type. Compared to non-mucinous adenocarcinoma, mucinous adenocarcinoma were more likely to exhibit indistinct margins and pleural retraction (18,25,26), and the spiculated margins being more common in pure mucinous adenocarcinoma than in mixed mucinous adenocarcinoma (17).

Significantly, we have pioneered the development and validation of a predictive model for distinguishing pure PMA utilizing logistic and LASSO regression analyses within an ML framework. According to the nomogram scoring, tumor location in the lower lobe contributed most strongly to the identification of pure PMA, which reflects the distinctive anatomical predilection of PMA. The underlying mechanisms may involve gravity-dependent mucus clearance and region-specific inflammatory microenvironments. The preoperative model demonstrated robust performance with relatively high AUC values in both the training and validation sets, along with well-calibrated curves. This predictive model offers a promising approach for accurately identifying pure PMA, providing a tool for facilitating more precise therapeutic interventions for patients.

To enhance the accuracy of prognostic modeling for PMA, we initially employed Cox regression analysis to identify prognostic factors of PMA, identifying higher CEA level and EGFR exon 19 deletion as independent prognostic factors for PMA patients. CEA level also made the strongest contribution to the nomogram score. CEA has been established as a clinically significant tumor biomarker known to facilitate malignant progression in lung cancer (27). Our previous cohort study revealed a correlation between elevated serum CEA level and adverse clinical outcomes (28). Notably, downstream genes activation by EGFR mutants has been linked to anti-apoptosis, pyroptosis, metastasis, and immune evasion (29-31). While specific subtypes of EGFR mutations are rarely used as distinguishing factors in prognostic assessments for PMA (23), our previous findings indicated that EGFRexon 19 deletion was an unfavourable prognostic marker in lung adenocarcinoma patients, with worse outcomes compared to EGFRexon 21 mutation cases (28). Moreover, Deng et al. demonstrated EGFR mutation was a poor prognostic factor in PMA and advanced stage lung adenocarcinoma (32), which was correlated with our study. One reason is that, except for one patient who received EGFR inhibitor therapy when brain metastasis occurred three years later, none of the EGFR mutation cases in our cohort received EGFR inhibitor therapy. Therefore, our results differ from those of some studies showing that EGFR mutation is a favorable prognostic predictor for NSCLC patients who received EGFR inhibitor therapy (33-36).

LASSO regression technique from ML was utilized to incorporate variables in the development of a nomogram with the calibration curves demonstrated strong consistency. Our stepwise model comparison demonstrated the incremental value of multimodal integration, with the full model achieving a significantly higher C-index than the clinical-only model. The ablation model also retained a relatively high C-index. Despite not achieving high C-index, the survival curve displayed significant differentiation between the two stratified groups, indicating the effectively discrimination ability of the nomogram and its potential utility in clinical prediction. Consistent with prior studies that had also developed satisfactory nomogram model despite moderate C-index values (10), our findings underscored the importance of histopathological classification in PMA by demonstrating that pure mucinous adenocarcinoma exhibited significantly superior clinical outcome compared to other PMA subtype, validating the prognostic relevance of histopathological classification in PMA. Additionally, our findings indicated that lobectomy for PMA provided comparable OS to sublobectomy, and that pure mucinous adenocarcinoma exhibited lower aggressiveness than the mixed mucinous and mucin-secreting type. Based on these observations, we suggest that segmentectomy or wedge resection may be sufficient for patients with pure mucinous adenocarcinoma, and that extending the resection to lobectomy is not routinely necessary. Similarly, Yang and colleagues reported that sublobectomy yielded OS comparable to lobectomy in small-sized PMA (37). Therefore, preoperative prediction of histologic subtype is clinically important to guide the surgical decision-making.

This study exhibits notable strengths and limitations. Initially, the data collection was confined to a single center, potentially introducing bias, and further multicenter cohort studies are warranted to enhance the dataset’s breadth and credibility. Secondly, the restricted patient population with PMA and the frequency of fatalities might result in data bias when partitioning patients into training and validation sets. The limited sample size and reduced event rate could lead to increased variability in calibration assessments. Thirdly, although the proportion of missing data in this study was relatively low, multiple imputation was not performed, which may have introduced some degree of bias into the results. Additionally, due to constraints in laboratory infrastructure in the archipelago setting, KRAS mutations were not identified in the patient cohort. Consequently, expanding the sample size, refining grouping strategies, or employing advanced corrective methods is recommended. Furthermore, it is crucial to validate the model’s performance across multiple datasets to ensure its generalizability.

Furthermore, our findings suggested that the models captureed both established and novel signals. The diagnostic model identified novel multimodal patterns, particularly the combination of CT-derived features and serum markers, which together improved subtype prediction beyond CT characters alone preoperatively. The prognostic model confirmed expected associations, such as smaller tumors, negative lymph node status, early stage, lower CEA, and less aggressive histology, as predictors of better outcomes. Additionally, we highlighted that EGFR exon 19 deletion were identified as a novel predictor of postoperative survival, adding new insights beyond established risk factors. These findings suggest that the models are not merely recapitulating known clinical knowledge but are extracting complementary information from multimodal inputs, supporting the incremental value of our approach.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we have established a refined histopathological classification approach for PMA with our validation study revealing distinctive clinicopathological characteristics indicating lower aggressiveness in pure mucinous adenocarcinoma compared to mixed mucinous and mucin-secreting types. Moreover, our developed nomogram model exhibited performance in predicting histological subtype of PMA preoperatively. Importantly, higher CEA level, and EGFR exon 19 deletion were confirmed as independent prognostic factors for PMA. These findings offer valuable insights for histopathological diagnosis and prognostic evaluation in the context of PMA.

17β-Estradiol Ameliorates Postoperative Cognitive Dysfunction in Aged Mice via miR-138-5p/SIRT1/HMGB1 Pathway
Authors: Ying Zhang, Meinv Liu, Dongdong Yu, Jing Wang, Jianli Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) is a common neurological complication in older patients and correlated with adverse outcomes. 17β-estradiol treatment was reported to provide neuroprotective protection in various neurologic disorders, but whether it attenuated POCD was unknown. The purpose of this study was to explore the effects of 17β-estradiol treatment on POCD and its mechanisms.MethodsWe generated a POCD model in 15-month-old mice via laparotomy, followed by subcutaneous injection of 17β-estradiol, intraperitoneal injection of EX527 (a Sirtuin 1 [SIRT1] inhibitor), or bilateral hippocampal injection of miR-138-5p-agomir. Morris water maze test and open field test were applied to evaluate the cognitive function. The neuronal apoptosis in the hippocampus was detected using the terminal transferase dUTP nick end labeling assay. Meanwhile, the levels of interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and microglia activation were measured by enzymelinked immunosorbent assay and immunofluorescence, respectively. Western blot was utilized to assess the expression of SIRT1 and high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) protein, and gene expression of miR-138-5p was determined through quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction.ResultsBehavioral tests showed that 17β-estradiol treatment improved cognitive function in aged POCD mice. In addition, 17β-estradiol attenuated neuronal apoptosis and microglia activation as well as IL-1β expression in the hippocampus. Nonetheless, injection with EX527 abolished the beneficial impacts of 17β-estradiol against POCD. Furthermore, miR-138-5p was verified to bind with SIRT1, which regulated the expression of HMGB1. After treatment with 17β-estradiol, miR-138-5p expression was reduced in the hippocampus, and the neuroprotective influence of 17β-estradiol on aged POCD mice was reversed after administration of miR-138-5p-agomir.Conclusions17β-estradiol treatment exerted neuroprotection effects on POCD in aged mice, which might be relevant to alleviating neuroinflammation via miR-138-5p/SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway.

Significant Statement

As the aging of the world’s population, a growing number of older persons are undergoing surgery. Postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) is a common postoperative complication in older patients, contributing not only to prolonged hospital stays and increased healthcare costs but also to enhanced mortality. However, there is no definitive treatment for POCD currently. 17β-estradiol is an endogenous neuroactive steroid that is produced in the brain. Many studies revealed the neuroprotective effects of 17β-estradiol, but whether it attenuated POCD was still unclear. In this study, we found that 17β-estradiol significantly enhanced cognitive function in aged mice suffering from POCD, which might be linked to the suppression of microglia-induced neuroinflammation and neuronal apoptosis. Furthermore, our research indicated that the neuroprotective effects of 17β-estradiol were associated with the miR-138-5p/SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway. The findings of this study might provide significant suggestions for the treatment of POCD in older patients.

INTRODUCTION

Postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) is a debilitating neurological complication that usually manifests as impaired memory and executive function deficits (Yang et al., 2022a). It affects approximately 26% of patients aged 60 years or older following surgical procedures, leading to numerous adverse consequences, such as prolonged hospitalization and increased mortality (Arefayne et al., 2023). The pathogenesis of POCD is multifaceted, involving blood–brain barrier disruption, oxidative stress, and release of inflammatory mediators (Zhou et al., 2023). Notably, evidence from animal and clinical research suggested that surgery/anesthesia-induced neuroinflammation was a primary factor in the development of POCD (Li et al., 2022). Therefore, searching for adjunctive neuroprotective strategies that inhibit neuroinflammation might be critical in combating POCD.

17β-estradiol, an endogenous neuroactive steroid, performs a variety of biological activities in the central nervous system (CNS) (Kwakowsky et al., 2016). Recent systematic reviews underscored the anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective properties of 17β-estradiol (Acosta-Martínez, 2020; Brann et al., 2022a). Furthermore, 17β-estradiol treatment could inhibit neuroinflammation to ameliorate cognitive impairment by increasing the expression of SIRT1 (Khan et al., 2019). Sirtuin 1 (SIRT1), a crucial member of the sirtuin family, modulates transcription factor activity through deacetylation and exerts a key role in the regulation of neuroinflammation (Yan et al., 2019). High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1), a substrate for SIRT1-mediated deacetylation, is known to stimulate the release of numerous pro-inflammatory cytokines (Lee et al., 2020). A recent study verified the significant role of the SRIT1/HMGB1 pathway in attenuating neuroinflammation to enhance cognitive function (Li et al., 2020). Nevertheless, whether 17β-estradiol ameliorates POCD by regulating the SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway remains to be elucidated.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a group of non-coding RNAs that regulate protein expression, primarily through the degradation of target messenger RNAs and inhibition of translation (Nemeth et al., 2024). It was confirmed that miRNAs were related to learning and memory functions, among which miR-138-5p might participate in the pathogenesis of various neurological diseases (Mao et al., 2022). The overexpression of miR-138-5p could exacerbate inflammation, oxidative stress, and neuronal apoptosis (Yan et al., 2021). Accordingly, miR-138-5p might play a role in the development of POCD. Furthermore, bioinformatics analysis based on the Targetscan database revealed the targeting relationship between SIRT1 and mmu-miR-138-5p (“mmu” is an abbreviation for “mus musculus,” denoting the mouse-specific miRNA). Given the aforementioned evidence, we proposed a hypothesis that 17β-estradiol might exert neuroprotective effects on POCD by modulating the miR-138-5p/SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway.

Therefore, we performed this study to explore the effect of 17β-estradiol in aged POCD mice and the underlying molecular mechanisms, hoping to offer supporting evidence for the clinical application of 17β-estradiol in the treatment of POCD.

METHODS

Animals

Healthy C57BL/6 elderly male mice (15 months, 30 ± 5 g) that obtained from SiPeiFu Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Beijing, China) were kept in standard humidity, temperature, and light/dark cycle situation with free access to food and water. The experiment protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Hebei General Hospital (Approval No.: 202330) and performed in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. All mice were adapted to the laboratory environment prior to the experiment for 7 days. Efforts were made to minimize the number of mice and their suffering.

POCD Model

Laparotomy was performed on aged mice to construct a POCD model (Chen et al., 2023a). Briefly, general anesthesia was induced and maintained at 3% sevoflurane with 100% oxygen (oxygen flow rate 1 L/min) with a rodent inhalation anesthesia device (RWD Life Science, China). Then, a 1.5-cm vertical incision was created in the abdominal midline to expose 2 cm of the intestine, which was rubbed vigorously for 2 minutes and replaced with the abdominal cavity after an interval of 1 minute. Finally, sterile 5-0- and 4-0-gauge silk were used to suture the muscles and skins. Mice were maintained at normal body temperature with a heating pad, and surgery-associated pain was alleviated with lidocaine.

Intrahippocampal Injection

At 24 hour postoperatively, mice were anesthetized with 3%–4% sevoflurane and immobilized in a stereotactic device. After exposing the skull surface of mice, a 0.05 mm diameter hole was drilled at the following coordinates with bregma as the zero point: anteroposterior (AP) −2.0 mm, mediolateral ±1.5 mm, and dorsoventral −1.5 mm. Then, miR-138-5p-agomir (Guangzhou RiboBio Co., Ltd., Guangzhou, China) or agomir-NC (Guangzhou RiboBio Co., Ltd., Guangzhou, China) was slowly injected into the bilateral hippocampus once a day for 3 days. The reagents (2 μL, 0.25 nmol/μL) were injected into the hippocampus at a rate of 0.2 μL/min (Chen et al., 2019). Then the needle was retained for 5 minutes and withdrawn slowly. The whole procedure was controlled for 30 minutes a day for each mouse.

Grouping

Eighty-four mice were randomly assigned into 7 groups with 12 animals in each group: control (group C), 17β-estradiol (group E), POCD (group P), POCD + 17β-estradiol (group EP), POCD + 17β-estradiol + EX527 (group EX), POCD + 17β-estradiol + miR-138-5p-agomir (group AG), and POCD + 17β-estradiol + agomir-NC (group AG-NC). All mice except for group C and group E received laparotomy as described above. 17β-estradiol (E2758, Sigma, USA) was diluted in 2% ethanol and injected subcutaneously with 0.1 mg/kg once a day for 7 days postoperatively (Zhong et al., 2020). Mice in the group EX were injected intraperitoneally with 5 mg/kg EX527 (S154104, Selleck, USA) every 2 days for a total of 4 times (Chen et al., 2023b). Moreover, mice in group AG and group AG-NC experienced the intrahippocampal injection mentioned above. The treatment details for different groups are available in Supplementary Tables 1 and 2.

Morris Water Maze Test

The classic Morris water maze (MWM) test included a learning phase and probe trial for evaluating the ability of learning and memory functions of the mice (Sun et al., 2022). In the learning phase, a white platform was placed in the third quadrant of the pool. The pool was filled with water until it was 1 cm higher than the platform. Then, a small amount of skim milk powder was sprinkled into the water to make the platform invisible. Four visual objects were placed on the pool wall to help the mice identify directions. The temperature of the water was kept at 22 °C. A researcher placed the mice into the water with their heads facing the wall of the pool. The time searching for the hidden platform was defined as escape latency and recorded using Any-Maze software (Xinruan Information Technology Co. Ltd., China). Mice that did not seek out the platform were directed to the platform and stayed for 10 seconds. This phase was repeated 4 times a day for 5 days, starting on postoperative day 7 to train the mice to find the underwater platform. Then, the probe trial was performed on postoperative day 12. After removing the platform, mice were released from the first quadrant of the pool. The times of platform crossing within 60 seconds and a percentage time staying in the target quadrant were documented to evaluate the memory ability of the mice.

Open Field Test

On postoperative day 7, the open field test (OFT) was performed to assess both the locomotor capabilities and anxiety levels of the mice (Wang et al., 2022). Briefly, mice were transported to the testing room and acclimatized to the environment for 1 hour before the experiment. Each mouse was then placed into the open field box and left to explore freely for 5 minutes. Finally, the movements of mice were automatically recorded by Any-Maze software for further analysis. The traveling distance reflected locomotor ability, and the total time spent in the central arena indicated the anxiety levels of mice. After each mouse finished the experiment, the arena was washed with 75% alcohol to avoid the influence of smell.

The behavioral experiments were conducted during the daytime from 9:00 to 17:00 (Liu et al., 2021a). Furthermore, the same mice were not repeated in the above 2 tests.

Tissue Preparation

After finishing the behavioral experiments (postoperative day 13), 3 mice were intracardially perfused with 4% paraformaldehyde, and the brain tissues were separated cautiously and put into the same fixative for 12 hours. Then the right and left brains were embedded in paraffin for terminal transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) and immunofluorescence assay. Meanwhile, 9 mice were euthanized with an overdose of pentobarbital sodium, and the hippocampi were removed expeditiously for enzymelinked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), western blot, and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) analysis.

TUNEL Assay

In accordance with the instructions of a commercial kit (KTA2010, Abbkine, USA), TUNEL was performed to visualize neuronal apoptosis. The paraffin sections were first permeabilized with proteinases K for 15 minutes. Subsequently, sections of the hippocampus were incubated with terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase (TDT) enzyme containing fluorescent labeling solution for 1 hour at 37 °C. Images of hippocampal cornu amoni 1 (CA1), cornu amoni 1 (CA3), and dentate gyrus (DG) regions were taken using fluorescence microscopy. The TUNEL-positive cells, exhibiting green fluorescence, were identified as apoptotic cells. For quantitative analysis, we employed ImageJ software to determine the rate of TUNEL/4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI)-positive cells, which was calculated by dividing the integrated density of TUNEL-positive cells by the area occupied by DAPI-stained nuclei.

Immunofluorescence for Iba1

Immunofluorescence analysis was conducted to assess the expression of Iba1, a marker of microglial activation (Green et al., 2022). After deparaffinization and hydration procedures, the slices were permeabilized by 0.2% Triton X-100 (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The citric acid buffer was used for antigen repair at 90 °C for 15 minutes, and nonspecific binding was blocked with 2% bovine serum albumin (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The sections were then incubated with a rabbit monoclonal anti-Iba1 (ab178846, 1:500, Abcam, UK) at 4 °C for 12 hours. Afterward, sections were incubated with FITC-conjugated anti-rabbit IgG (A23220, 1:1000, Abbkine, USA) for 2 hours at room temperature. Finally, the sections were stained with DAPI for 5 minutes. A fluorescence microscope was employed to capture images in the hippocampal CA1, CA3, and DG regions. ImageJ software was utilized to count the number of Iba1-positive cells.

Enzymelinked Immunosorbent Assay

An ELISA Kit (SEA563Mu, Cloud-Clone Crop, China) was adopted to assay interleukin-1β (IL-1β) levels in the hippocampi. All procedures were carried out in line with the instructions of the manufacturer. About 20 mg of hippocampal tissue was taken from each mouse and homogenized them with 1 mL of fresh lysis buffer. Then the resulting suspension was sonicated till the solution was clarified. Finally, the homogenates were centrifuged for 5 minutes at 10 000 × g, and the supernatant fluid was collected for analysis. The absorbance was evaluated at a wavelength of 450 nm, and the optical density (OD) reading was converted using the standard curve to calculate the final concentration of IL-1β.

Western Blot Analysis

Proteins were extracted from the hippocampi using RIPA buffer (R0010, Solarbio, China), complemented with Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail (P6730, Solarbio, China). Then, a Protein Quantification Kit (Abbkine, USA) was utilized to evaluate the protein concentrations. The protein samples were subsequently isolated via sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) electrophoresis and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. After blocking with 5% skimmed milk, the membrane was incubated with primary antibodies: rabbit monoclonal anti-SIRT1 (R25721, 1:700, ZEN-BIOSCIENCE, China), rabbit polyclonal anti-HMGB1 (10829-1-AP, 1:1000, Proteintech, China), or rabbit polyclonal anti-glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) (10494-1-AP, 1:15 000, Proteintech, China) at 4 °C for 12 hours. After being washed with Tris-buffered saline with Tween-20 (TBST) for 30 minutes, the PVDF membranes were incubated with an horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (511203, 1:12 000, ZEN-BIOSCIENCE, China) for 1 hour at room temperature. Ultimately, protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence detection system, and the grayscale values of these bands were quantified through ImageJ software.

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay

We utilized the TargetScan8.0 website (TargetScanMouse 8.0) to predict and identify SIRT1 as a putative target gene of mmu-miR-138-5p. Subsequently, the psiCHECK2.0 vector (Promega, USA) was used to perform a dual-luciferase reporter assay, which contained either the wild-type (SIRT1-WT) or mutant (SIRT1-MUT) forms of the SIRT1 gene. 293T cells were co-transfected with luciferase reporter plasmids along with either the miR-138-5p mimic or a negative control (NC). After incubating for 48 hours, the luciferase activity was detected with a Dual-luciferase Reporter Kit (Promega, USA) followed by calculating the proportion of renilla activity to firefly activity.

Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction

miRNAs were extracted through a miRcute miRNA Isolation Kit (TIANGEN, China), and the concentration and purity were checked using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The cDNA was synthesized with FastKing RT Kit (TIANGEN, China) and followed by qRT-PCR analysis through the SuperReal PreMx Plus reagent (TIANGEN, China). The thermal cycling protocol for qRT-PCR was performed in accordance with the manufacturer’s guidelines: initial pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 15 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 10 seconds at 95 °C for denaturation, 20 seconds at 60 °C for annealing, and 32 seconds at 72 °C for extension. Finally, the relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, a standard approach for quantifying gene expression. The specific primers of miR-138-5p and U6 were sourced from RiboBio Corporation (China).

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 25.0 software, while GraphPad Prism was employed to generate the graphical representations. Prior to the statistical evaluation, the data were analyzed for normal distribution through the Shapiro–Wilk normality test. Normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Student’s t-test was used to compare between 2 groups, while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare among 3 or more groups, complemented by the least significant difference (LSD) post hoc test for multiple comparisons. The mean escape latency, as determined by the MWM test, was analyzed by 2-way repeated measures ANOVA. For non-normally distributed data such as the time spent in the center assessed by OFT, the Kruskal–Wallis H test was utilized. Statistical significance was set at a threshold of P < .05.

RESULTS

17β-Estradiol Improved the Cognitive Function of Aged POCD Mice

To investigate whether 17β-estradiol treatment could ameliorate cognitive performance in aged POCD mice, we performed an MWM test to assess learning and memory functions. During the latency escape trials, a progressive reduction in escape times was observed across all experimental groups. However, the aged POCD mice demonstrated significantly longer latencies compared to the control group (F [6, 35] = 74.28, P < .05; Figure 1B). Furthermore, the results of one-way ANOVA showed significant differences among the groups in the number of platform-across times (F [6, 35] = 12.50, P < .001) and the time spent in the target quadrant (F [6, 35] = 14.40, P < .001). Post hoc analyses suggested that mice in group P traversed the platform less frequently and stayed in the target quadrant for a shorter time than group C (P < .01; Figure 1C and D). In contrast, aged POCD mice that were treated with 17β-estradiol displayed shorter escape latencies, increased platform crossings, and spent more time in the target quadrant (Figure 1B–D). The results of these tests did not demonstrate any significant differences between group C and group E, indicating that the sole administration of 17β-estradiol did not exert a substantial impact on cognitive function in aged mice. Collectively, the mice in group EP showed better performance compared with group P, suggesting that 17β-estradiol significantly ameliorated cognitive function in aged POCD mice.

The results of OFT showed that there was no significant discrepancy in the total moving distance among the groups (F [6, 35] = 2.21, P = .076; Figure 1E), indicating that the locomotor capacity of mice was not influenced by 17β-estradiol treatment. Moreover, mice in group P spent significantly less time in the central arena compared to group C (H [6] = 14.81, P = .020; Figure 1F), which might indicate the presence of anxiety in aged POCD mice. However, the comparison between group EP and group P showed no significant difference in this behavior (P = .078), implying that 17β-estradiol treatment did not exert a remarkable effect on the anxiety level of the mice.

17β-Estradiol Protected Hippocampal Neurons of Aged POCD Mice

Neuronal apoptosis in the hippocampus was detected by TUNEL assay. The one-way ANOVA findings indicated statistical differences in the intergroup analysis of TUNEL/DAPI-positive cell rates in the CA1 (F [6, 14] = 15.29, P < .001), CA3 (F [6, 14] = 19.61, P < .001), and DG (F [6, 14] = 12.51, P < .001) regions. Subsequently, the multiple comparisons presented a higher ratio of TUNEL-positive cells in these 3 hippocampal regions of aged POCD mice compared to the mice in group C (P < .01, Figure 2). Notably, the ratio of TUNEL-positive cells was substantially reduced following 17β-estradiol treatment (P < .01), confirming the neuroprotective effect of 17β-estradiol by attenuating hippocampal neuronal apoptosis in aged POCD mice. Additionally, a comparative analysis of neuronal apoptosis across various hippocampal subregions was conducted. As depicted in Figure 2G, the CA3 region exhibited a higher incidence of neuronal apoptosis; however, no statistically significant differences were observed among the group comparisons for the 3 hippocampal regions (P = .142).

17β-Estradiol Inhibited Microglia-Induced Neuroinflammation of Aged POCD Mice

Persistent neuroinflammation accelerates neuronal apoptosis to promote the occurrence of POCD (Yang et al., 2022b). To further assess the effects of 17β-estradiol on neuroinflammation in aged POCD mice, we conducted a quantitative analysis of IL-1β protein levels within the hippocampus. Our findings indicated significant intergroup differences (F [6, 35] = 13.42, P < .001). Furthermore, post hoc multiple comparison analyses disclosed a marked upregulation of IL-1β in the POCD group (P < .01, Figure 3G), which was followed by a notable downregulation after 17β-estradiol treatment (P < .01). Given that activated microglia were a major source of pro-inflammatory cytokines, the suppression of microglia activation could contribute to a decrease in pro-inflammatory cytokines and alleviate neuroinflammation (Xu et al., 2023). Next, we utilized immunofluorescence to evaluate the expression of Iba1, a classic biomarker of microglia activation. Similarly, one-way ANOVA revealed remarkable intergroup differences in hippocampal CA1 (F [6, 14] = 47.07, P < .001), CA3 (F [6, 14] = 24.67, P < .001), and DG (F [6, 14] = 13.91, P < .001) regions. Results of post hoc comparative analyses showed that the expressions of Iba1 in the 3 hippocampal regions were obviously enhanced in group P (P < .01, Figure 3D–F), while it declined in group EP. Collectively, these results supported the strikingly inhibitory effect of 17β-estradiol on microglia-induced neuroinflammation in aged POCD mice.

17β-Estradiol Treatment Exerted Anti-Inflammatory Effect on POCD via Regulating SIRT1/HMGB1 Pathway

Previous research highlighted the pivotal role of the SIRT1/HMGB1 signaling pathway in suppressing neuroinflammation to improve cognitive function (Li et al., 2020). Our aforementioned findings clearly demonstrated that 17β-estradiol treatment exerted beneficial effects on POCD; however, whether it was associated with the SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway still needed investigation. Thus, we quantified the expression of SIRT1 and HMGB1 in the hippocampus. Our analysis revealed statistically significant differences among the groups in the protein expression of SIRT1 (F [6, 35] = 8.25, P < .001) and HMGB1 (F [6, 35] = 4.40, P = .002). Subsequent post hoc test results indicated that SIRT1 expression was significantly decreased, and HMGB1 expression was enhanced in aged POCD mice compared to the control group (P < .01; Figure 4). Nevertheless, 17β-estradiol treatment was found to upregulate SIRT1 (P < .01) and downregulate HMGB1 (P = .042) expression in aged POCD mice. Notably, these effects of 17β-estradiol were reversed after administration of EX527, as evidenced by a significant reduction in SIRT1 expression (P < .01) and an elevation in HMGB1 expression (P = .027) within the hippocampus of mice in group EX compared to group EP. In addition, data from the MWM test clearly showed that EX527 attenuated the neuroprotective effects of 17β-estradiol (P < .05, Figure 1A–D). Additional results presented in Figures 2 and 3 demonstrated that EX527 markedly undermined the efﬁcacy of 17β-estradiol on neuronal apoptosis (P < .01) and neuroinflammation (P < .01). Overall, the results provide evidence that 17β-estradiol treatment improved POCD in aged mice by modulating the SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway to suppress microglia-induced neuroinflammation.

SIRT1 Was a Regulatory Target of miR-138-5p

Based on the above findings, we identified that SIRT1 was a significant regulator of 17β-estradiol in improving POCD. What’s more, there were interaction sites between mmu-miR-138-5p with SIRT1 3ʹ-untranslated region (UTR) in accordance with the TargetScan database (Figure 5A), which was further validated by a dual-luciferase reporter assay. As shown in Figure 5B, miR-138-5p bounded to the SIRT1-WT-3ʹ-UTR but not the SIRT1-MUT-3ʹ-UTR, strongly validating that SIRT1 was a regulatory target of miR-138-5p.

To further determine the role of miR-138-5p in the therapeutic benefits of 17β-estradiol for alleviating POCD, miR-138-5p-agomir or agomir-NC was administered in the bilateral hippocampus of mice. Group AG-NC was served as a NC group to exclude the false-positive results of group AG due to anesthesia exposure and intrahippocampal injection. Results of behavioral tests revealed that the beneficial effects of 17β-estradiol on learning and memory function were diminished by the injection of miR-138-5p-agomir (P < .05, in comparison with group AG-NC, Figure 1). Additionally, Figures 2 and 3 illustrated that miR-138-5p-agomir obviously counteracted the neuroprotective influence of 17β-estradiol on hippocampal neuronal apoptosis, microglia activation, and IL-1β expression (P < .01, in comparison with group AG-NC). Furthermore, the administration of miR-138-5p-agomir led to a decrease in SIRT1 expression and an increase in HMGB1 expression within the hippocampus (P < .05, in comparison with group AG-NC, Figure 4). Overall, these results indicated that 17β-estradiol ameliorated POCD in aged mice through modulating the miR-138-5p/SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway.

DISCUSSION

The present study was designed to investigate the neuroprotective properties of 17β-estradiol treatment in aged POCD mice and the specific mechanisms. Our research revealed that 17β-estradiol treatment effectively mitigated neuroinflammation, thereby ameliorating POCD in aged mice through miR-138-5p/SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway. These findings not only confirmed the neuroprotective effect of 17β-estradiol treatment on POCD for the first time but also provided a reliable theoretical basis for the clinical application of 17β-estradiol to improve POCD.

POCD is a prevalent neurological complication following surgery, which significantly diminishes the quality of life for patients, particularly among the elderly population (Lin et al., 2020). However, there was still no recognized prevention or treatment strategies for POCD due to its complex pathogenesis. Therefore, exploring effective measures to alleviate POCD remained a great challenge for clinicians. In recent years, there has been widespread interest in the neuroprotective role of 17β-estradiol in various neurological disorders (Brann et al., 2022a). 17β-estradiol is a neuroactive steroid that is produced in the brain of many species and participates in regulating synaptic plasticity and enhancing memory function (Finney et al., 2020). Available evidences demonstrated that treatment with 17β-estradiol could improve cognitive function in mouse models of both Alzheimer’s disease and cuprizone-induced demyelination (Acosta-Martínez, 2020; Aryanpour et al., 2021). Therefore, we hypothesized that 17β-estradiol treatment could offer neuroprotection against POCD. The results from this study, as evaluated by the MWM test and TUNEL assay, indicated that 17β-estradiol treatment protected hippocampal neurons and thus improved cognitive function in aged POCD mice. Furthermore, the total distance assessed by OFT was utilized to evaluate the motor function of mice, revealing no significant difference among the groups, which suggested that laparotomy did not substantially affect the autonomous locomotor ability of mice. Interestingly, although anxiety was observed in aged POCD mice, 17β-estradiol treatment exerted no significant effect on anxiety levels. These findings revealed that the beneficial effect of 17β-estradiol on memory function was not due to differences in locomotor function and anxiety levels, which were consistent with a previous finding (Lu et al., 2019).

Neuroinflammation was recognized as the primary contributor to the development of POCD (Granger and Barnett, 2021). Microglia, the intrinsic immunocytes of the CNS, played a pivotal role in neuroinflammatory processes (Liu et al., 2023). The overactivation of microglia could accelerate the expressions of pro-inflammatory cytokines to aggravate neuroinflammation and subsequently inducing cognitive impairment (Duggan and Parikh, 2021). Currently, many therapeutic strategies have been reported to enhance cognitive function in POCD patients by modulating microglia activation, such as electroacupuncture, dexmedetomidine, and rutin (a natural flavonoid compound) (Wu et al., 2022; Xin et al., 2022; Ji et al., 2024). In addition, 17β-estradiol could protect neurological function in ischemic encephalopathy and traumatic brain injury mice by inhibiting microglia activation (Wang et al., 2021). Therefore, it was reasonable to hypothesize that 17β-estradiol treatment might offer neuroprotection against POCD by suppressing the activation of microglia. To test this hypothesis, we investigated the role of 17β-estradiol on the expression of Iba1, a marker of microglia activation. The immunofluorescence results showed significant microglial activation in the hippocampi of aged POCD mice, as evidenced by an upregulation of Iba1 expression, which was consistent with previous findings (Luo et al., 2022; Qiao et al., 2023). Moreover, administration with 17β-estradiol efficiently suppressed the expression of Iba1 and IL-1β in aged POCD mice, strongly confirming that 17β-estradiol treatment attenuated microglia-mediated neuroinflammation in aged POCD mice.

We further investigated the mechanisms underlying the inhibitory effects of 17β-estradiol on microglial activation. SIRT1 is a deacetylase with dual anti-inflammation and anti-oxidant effects (Zhang et al., 2020). HMGB1, a protein substrate deacetylated by SIRT1, serves as an important biomarker of neuroinflammation (Lei et al., 2022). Previous studies suggested that SIRT1 inhibited the expression of HMGB1 to attenuate neuroinflammation induced by microglia activation (Chibaatar et al., 2021). In addition, researchers found that resveratrol improved cognitive function by reducing microglia activation via SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway (Wu et al., 2023). Collectively, SIRT1/HMGB1 signaling pathway might be an essential pathway to regulate microglial activation in POCD. To confirm whether the role of 17β-estradiol on microglia activation was related to SIRT1/HMGB1 pathways, we detected the protein expressions of SIRT1 and HMGB1. The results indicated a decrease in SIRT1 expression and an increase in HMGB1 expression in aged POCD mice, but 17β-estradiol treatment reversed these changes. Furthermore, administration with the SIRT1 inhibitor EX527 abolished the effects of 17β-estradiol on microglia, as well as the protein levels of SIRT1 and HMGB1. Overall, our results confirmed that 17β-estradiol inhibited microglia activation to exert anti-inflammatory effect via regulating SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway.

It was widely reported that miRNAs were linked to the pathogenesis of POCD through involvement in hippocampal neuroinflammation (Yazit et al., 2020). Therefore, investigating the potential involvement of miRNAs in the neuroprotective mechanisms of 17β-estradiol on POCD is of great significance. Our above results suggested that SIRT1 serves as a crucial target through which 17β-estradiol exerts its beneficial effects on POCD. Notably, bioinformatics analysis on the basis of the base-pairing rules further verified the existence of interaction sites between mmu-miR-138-5p and 3ʹ-UTR of SIRT1. In a Parkinson’s disease cell model, the downregulation of miR-138-5p could modulate SIRT1 to suppress inflammation and neuronal apoptosis (Yan et al., 2021). Additionally, miR-138-5p reduced synaptic plasticity and cognitive function by suppressing SIRT1 expression (Liu et al., 2021b). Our results elucidated that upregulation of miR-138-5p could downregulate the SRIT1/HMGB1 pathway, leading to hippocampal neuroinflammation. Furthermore, we demonstrated that miR-138-5p reversed the neuroprotective effects of 17β-estradiol on POCD, as evidenced by the enhanced neuroinflammation and neuronal apoptosis, along with impaired learning and memory functions in mice.

In vivo, 17β-estradiol is synthesized by astrocytes and neurons in the brains of both males and females. In addition, the ovaries in females are recognized as significant sources of 17β-estradiol production (Huang et al., 2023). Aged female mice inevitably experience altered endocrine status, resulting in an inability to guarantee stable 17β-estradiol levels (Khan et al., 2019). Therefore, we excluded aged female mice from this study to eliminate the effects of different levels of endogenous 17β-estradiol levels. However, it was worth mentioning that there did not appear a sex-related difference in the neuroprotective effects of 17β-estradiol (Marbouti et al., 2020). Although most studies focused on the neuroprotective effect of 17β-estradiol in male animals, a few studies utilized female animals and reported comparable neuroprotective benefits of 17β-estradiol to those observed in males (Brann et al., 2022b). Future research that delves into the detailed characterization of behaviors in aged female mice would provide valuable information.

Some limitations in our study also deserved discussion. First, the sample size of 6 mice per group in each behavioral experiment was relatively low. Given factors such as genetic differences, environmental influences, and individual characteristics, the behavior of rodents could be highly variable. Therefore, it was necessary to employ a more significant number of animals in the future to account for such variability and enhance the reliability of our findings. Second, although our study demonstrated that 17β-estradiol ameliorated POCD in aged mice by suppressing neuroinflammation, the potential role of other mechanisms, such as oxidative stress, required further investigation. Third, the current research centered on relatively short-term cognitive behaviors (7 days) following surgery and anesthesia, leaving the long-term cognitive effects unclear. Finally, although we confirmed the neuroprotective effects of 17β-estradiol in aged POCD mice, the translation of these findings to human therapeutics necessitated clinical validation.

In summary, the present study was the first to report that 17β-estradiol treatment alleviated neuroinflammation to improve POCD in aged mice through miR-138-5p/SIRT1/HMGB1 pathway (Figure 7). This discovery might pave the way for the potential clinical application of 17β-estradiol therapy for patients suffering from POCD.

Enhanced vertebrae localization in CT volumes: a two-stage deep learning framework
Authors: Hongzhi Liu, Junyang Han, Lingyue Jiang, Yuanxiu Zhang, Xu Wen, Yan Xi, Yudong Zhang, Chunfeng Yang, Rongjun Ge, Hui Tang, Shuai Wang, Qianjin Feng, Yang Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVertebral landmark localization in computed tomography (CT) volumes is crucial for spinal pathological diagnosis, postoperative assessment and surgical planning. However, vertebral localization within high-resolution three-dimensional CT volumes still poses prominent challenges.MethodsThis study introduces a novel two-stage deep learning framework that effectively addresses challenges such as large spatial spans and high morphological similarity of vertebrae. The first stage employs the improved squeeze and excitation V-Net (ISE-VNet) for coarse segmentation of the spinal column. The second stage utilizes a 3D spatial-generalized differential spatial-to-numerical transform (DSNT) module for precise localization of individual vertebrae.ResultsOur framework significantly improves identification rates from 81.15% to 96.32% and reduces localization errors from 7.6 mm to 2.1 mm, outperforming state-of-the-art methods.ConclusionThis approach provides a robust solution for clinical vertebral landmark localization in 3D medical images. This design effectively enables efficient and focused analysis of vertebral local regions and it holds significant value in both scientific research and clinical practice.Keywords: Computed tomography, Deep learning, Vertebral centroids, Landmarks localization

Introduction

As a vital anatomical structure, the spine plays a central role in human motor function, and its condition has a significant impact on overall health. Precise automatic localization and identification of vertebrae are essential in clinical diagnosis and treatment workflows, supporting key procedures such as 3D image-guided spinal disease diagnosis [1], preoperative planning [2, 3], and postoperative evaluation [4]. Moreover, accurate vertebral identification is a prerequisite for a variety of downstream tasks, including spinal deformity analysis, image-guided interventions, and disease monitoring [1, 5–7].

Similar to other image-guided diagnostic approaches in medical imaging, computer-aided diagnosis systems can streamline and enhance vertebral localization. This is particularly valuable as manually annotating vertebrae in 3D medical scans is not only tedious and time-intensive but also prone to subjective variability in human interpretation [8]. Nevertheless, as shown in Fig. 1, the high morphological similarity among vertebrae, coupled with complicating factors such as metal insertions, low image quality and vertebral deformities, poses a significant challenge to achieving the level of precision necessary for clinical applications [9–11].

Challenging cases in vertebral landmarks localization. (a) metal insertions, (b) low image quality, (c) vertebral deformities, (d) high morphological similarity among adjacent sides of vertebrae

Over the past decade, numerous methods have been developed to tackle the challenging task of vertebrae identification and localization. Early methods [12–15] relied heavily on prior anatomical knowledge or imposed specific assumptions about the content of spinal images. As a result, their performance tended to degrade when applied to more diverse or complex real-world spinal imaging scenarios, limiting their generalizability and robustness [10]. More recently, deep learning-based algorithms [9, 10, 16–27] have gained prominence by leveraging large datasets and powerful feature learning, achieving superior accuracy and robustness in complex scenarios. The context feature extraction network CFSeg-Net [28] enhances medical image segmentation performance by effectively modeling and integrating multi-scale contextual feature, while D2U-Net [29], a dual-path hybrid UNet architecture, achieves precise segmentation via feature representations from two complementary network paths. To reduce reliance on post-processing and improve generalization, Sekuboyina et al. [16] introduced a pseudo-3D butterfly-shaped network using two perspective slices of the spine and adversarial training based on anatomical priors. Then Payer et al. [17] extended U-Net [30] into the SCN architecture, combining local and global context to mitigate false positives. To improve the performance, two or multi-stage methods [9, 18–22, 26, 27], that first localize the vertebra and further classify the detected vertebra patches, are proposed to realize the state-of-the-art performance. Payer et al. [21] enhanced their SCN architecture for 3D vertebrae landmark detection with a two-stage strategy. The spine’s centerline is estimated to guide the generation of sliding windows, and then a 3D extension of SCN is applied within these windows to accurately localize landmarks. Chen et al. [9] proposed a three-stage architecture consisting of a random forest for coarse localization, a shape model for refining landmark coordinates using adjacent landmarks, and a CNN for landmark classification. Tao et al. [27] proposed Spine-Transformer, a two-stage framework for automatic vertebrae localization in spine CT images with arbitrary fields of view (FOV). Vertebral 3D CT images are characterized by large volumes and variable resolutions, while current methods suffer from excessive computational consumption and inadequate suppression of false positives induced by incorrect coordinates when predicting keypoints in high-resolution images, which renders accurate vertebral landmark localization extremely challenging.

In this paper, we propose a novel two-stage framework to improve vertebrae localization in large 3D medical images such as CT scans. In the first stage, we present the ISE-VNet, a V-Net variant designed to segment the entire spine from low-resolution CT volumes. Based on the segmentation results, the CT volume is then partitioned into multiple regions using a sliding window approach for further processing. This design effectively mitigates the challenges posed by the large size and variable resolution of 3D CT data, enabling efficient and focused analysis of localized regions. In the second stage, vertebrae landmarks are detected within higher-resolution sliding windows, and the results are aggregated back into the original CT volume. To address the challenges of broad spatial extent and high anatomical similarity between vertebrae, we propose a spatial-generalized module designed to improve landmark localization performance. This module learns the spatial masks from the low-resolution feature maps and then applies the masks point-wise to the high-resolution Gaussian heatmaps generated by the feature extraction network, which compensates for the deficiency of current methods in suppressing false-positive responses in local heatmaps. Rather than using direct coordinate regression, we introduce the refined differential spatial-to-numerical transform [31] as the 3D DSNT module for converting heatmaps to coordinates in an end-to-end manner. This component regularizes the spatial distribution of feature maps and transforms the heatmaps into precise landmark coordinates through fully differentiable operations, yielding continuous-valued outputs and solving the limitations of conventional methods with lower computational cost.

Methods

Fig. 2 illustrates the framework of the proposed two-stage vertebral landmark localization method. In the first stage, an improved V-Net variant, designated as ISE-VNet, is employed to segment the spinal column from low-resolution CT images. Subsequently, a sliding window strategy is applied to crop the original high-resolution CT volume into multiple spine-specific sub-volumes. In the second stage, these spine sub-volumes are processed under high-resolution settings: the spatial-generalized DSNT module is utilized to detect the centroids of individual vertebrae, and the resultant localization results are further fused to generate consistent outputs.

Overview of our proposed two-stage vertebral landmark localization network with the first stage to perform coarse segmentation and the second stage to realize the high-resolution vertebrae landmarks localization

Improved squeeze and excitation V-Net in the segmentation stage

Since the input is a full 3D CT image, which contains a significant amount of unnecessary information for spine landmark prediction, the first step is to perform coarse segmentation to separate the relevant areas.

The segmentation is performed using proposed ISE-VNet, a variant of the V-Net architecture. To adapt the network dimensions to the algorithm requirements in this chapter, we also enhance the use of spatial and channel attention mechanisms. We analyze the characteristics of both attention mechanisms and introduce them at optimal points in the network. This approach enhances segmentation performance by incorporating attention mechanisms at strategic locations without significantly increasing the network parameters.

In this paper, these two mechanisms are encapsulated into two modules, namely, the spatial Squeeze & Excitation (sSE) module and the channel Squeeze & Excitation (cSE) module, as shown in Fig. 3, where the input feature map is , the output of the spatial attention module is , and the output of the channel attention module is . The input and output dimensions remain identical before and after passing through the modules, ensuring that they do not alter the size of the original input. These modules can theoretically be inserted freely before or after the convolutional layers in the network, and they are strategically placed according to the respective characteristics of the spatial and channel attention mechanisms.

(b) spatial Squeeze & Excitation (sSE) module and channel Squeeze & Excitation (cSE) module

The spatial Squeeze and Excitation (sSE) module enables the network to focus on most important regions in the spatial dimension, such as fine edge details of vertebrae in spine segmentation task. The core of the spatial attention mechanism involves compressing the input feature map along the channel dimension using a 1×1×1 convolution kernel, resulting in a feature map of the same spatial size but with the number of channels reduced to one. The resulting feature map is passed through a sigmoid activation function to produce a spatial probability map, which reflects varying levels of spatial attention. The channel Squeeze and Excitation (cSE) module allows the network to focus on the most relevant channels across multiple feature channels, enhancing the network’s semantic understanding. First, global average pooling is applied to the input feature map along the spatial dimensions. The resulting feature vector is then processed through a convolution with halved channel size, followed by another convolution to restore the original channel size. Then the feature vector is activated to reflect different level of channel attention. In this paper, the sSE and cSE modules are inserted into the top and bottom layers of the V-Net respectively.

Spatial-generalized DSNT in the localization stage

Most current approaches to vertebrae localization are heatmap-based, where the network outputs a gaussian heatmap that is later converted into coordinates. The brightest point on the heatmap indicates the landmark’s location. However, operating directly on heatmaps presents several limitations. First, heatmap-based methods suffer from a theoretical lower bound on localization accuracy. Since heatmaps are typically downsampled to of the original CT resolution, their coordinates are constrained to integers. When these are mapped back to the original resolution, a quantization error is introduced that cannot be eliminated through optimization. Moreover, heatmap-based algorithms exhibit two inherent theoretical issues. On one hand, the loss can remain high even when the predicted landmarks are accurate; on the other hand, a low loss does not necessarily indicate precise localization.

Finally, these methods are not fully end-to-end due to the non-differentiability of the argmax operation, which prevents its integration into neural network training. As a result, the entire landmark detection pipeline cannot be seamlessly embedded into broader deep learning frameworks.

In this paper, we propose the spatial-generalized DSNT network for detecting the coordinates of all vertebral centroids. The model consists of two key modules: the spatial-generalized module and the 3D DSNT module. The spatial-generalized module generates heatmaps, while the 3D DSNT module converts these heatmaps into landmark coordinates using differentiable mathematical methods. Fig. 4 illustrates the complete architecture of our model, which includes the 3D U-Net for heatmap generation, the spatial-generalized module for reducing false negatives, and the 3D DSNT module for converting heatmaps to coordinates, thereby improving accuracy on high-resolution CT images.

Spatial-generalized module

For feature extraction, this paper employs a 3D U-Net to perform the initial extraction of spatial features. Following this, the spatial-generalized module is introduced to refine the feature map by filtering out artifacts and irrelevant landmarks that may have been generated by the 3D U-Net. This helps to focus the network’s attention on the most relevant anatomical structures, improving the overall accuracy of the model. As shown in the upper half of Fig. 4, the architecture of the spatial-generalized DSNT combines the strengths of the 3D U-Net for robust feature extraction with the filtering capabilities of the spatial-generalized module, ensuring cleaner and more accurate landmark detection.

In this paper, the gaussian peaks of landmarks other than the landmark corresponding to the channel appearing in the gaussian heatmap are called artifacts. As the perceptual field of 3D U-net is small relative to the height of the entire spine, the network sometimes has difficulty distinguishing the class of the landmarks even though it can accurately predict the coordinates of the landmarks based on the shape of the vertebrae. As shown in Fig. 5, when there are multiple gaussian peaks with sign points in a gaussian heatmap, only one is correct, and the others are artifacts that need to be eliminated. Spatial-generalized module is used to generate masks for the output of 3D U-net to eliminate artifacts. Fig. 5 shows the details of spatial-generalized module in one of the 25 output channels. Experiment and results

Dataset

The dataset in this paper are adopted from the Verse competition dataset, which contains the competition data of VerSe2020 [9]. The VerSe2020 dataset contains 300 patients with 319 sets of 3D CT data, including 113 sets in the training set, 103 sets in the test set, and 103 sets in the validation set. These data have voxel sizes of 1 mm in both x-axis and y-axis, and 1 mm-3 mm in z-axis. since the method in this paper can accommodate CT data with higher resolution (0.5 mm voxels), the data are all resampled to 0.5 mm voxels.

Implementation details

We used an Intel Xeon Platinum 8153 CPU with 64 physical cores, an NVIDIA 3090 GPU with 24 GB of video memory, 64 GB of RAM, and a Linux operating system. During inference, the average processing time is approximately 50 ms per case using GPU, and 200 ms per case using CPU. For the coarse segmentation, input sizes varied but were standardized by rotating to the RAS orientation, uniformly scaling, and padding or cropping to [768,768,2048] for 0.5 mm voxels, then downsampling to 8 mm voxels and resizing to [64,64,192] for input. During training, data augmentation included random left-right, up-down, and front-back flips, as well as rotations of around an axis. Training was performed with a batch size of 6 and a learning rate of 1e-4, halving every 5 epochs. Significant convergence was achieved by around 10 epochs. In the inference phase, dataset augmentation was unnecessary, but sliding-window partitioning of the spine CT into multiple blocks remained essential for landmark detection.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel all-3D, two-stage coordinate-based framework to localize vertebrae landmarks in high-resolution 3D CT volumes. Our approach consists of two stages: the first performs coarse segmentation, and the second localizes the vertebrae landmarks. Specifically, our main innovations include the spatial-generalized module, which eliminates artifacts, and the 3D DSNT module, which converts feature maps into precise landmark coordinates. Our method outperforms other approaches on the Verse2020 dataset and demonstrates an even greater advantage on high-resolution CT images, such as those with 0.5 mm voxel resolution, as the DSNT module supports exact fractional coordinate outputs. In future work, we plan to conduct external validation in cooperation with hospitals to verify the performance of the framework in more clinical situation.

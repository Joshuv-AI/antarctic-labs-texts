Basic Albanese maps of regular Riemannian foliations
Kinga Słowik 1,2, Robert Wolak 1

Abstract
. In the paper we introduce the notion of basic Albanese map which we define for foliated Riemannian manifolds using basic 1-forms. We relate this mapping to the classical Albanese map for the ambient manifold. The study of general properties is supplemented with the description of several important examples.

1.
Introduction

The Albanese map, also called the Abel–Jacobi map, was primarily introduced to study algebraic varieties, in particular complex surfaces. Although this tool originates from algebraic geometry, the Albanese map was also used in differential geometry, for example by Nagano and Smyth
[
14
]
to study minimal submanifolds of tori, as well as by Kotschick
[
9
]
and Nagy and Vernicos
[
15
]
to study geometrically formal manifolds, i.e. Riemannian manifolds such that the wedge product of differential forms preserves harmonicity. We investigate the Albanese map in the context of Riemannian foliated manifolds and its foliated counterpart. To this end, we need to consider the first basic cohomology which is rational or integer. The integer basic cohomology itself can be an interesting tool to investigate foliated manifolds, for example in
[
8
]
the assumption that
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})\cap H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
forms a lattice in
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})
is used to introduce basic Seiberg–Witten invariants for taut Riemannian foliations of codimension
4
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall, for the reader’s convenience, some information and theorems on Riemannian manifolds, Riemannian foliations, Albanese map, harmonic maps to flat tori and stratified spaces. Moreover, we fix assumptions, definitions and notations used further in the paper. Section
3
is devoted to computing the rank of
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Z})
(or equivalently computing
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})
) in case when
\mathcal{F}
is a Riemannian foliation. In our considerations we depend on the Molino structure theorem. Firstly we assume that the foliation is transversely parallelizable and then use these results in the general case. Proposition
3.5
, an important auxiliary result, says that
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})=\dim H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{b})
for a transversely parallelizable foliation
\mathcal{F}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{b}
is the basic foliation associated to
\mathcal{F}
. Next, we formulate and prove the corresponding result for general Riemannian foliations.

In Section
4
we introduce the basic Albanese map and prove some of its properties. More precisely,
4.1
is devoted to the construction of the basic Albanese map using given generators of
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})
, its relationship with classical Albanese map, and the universal property
4.6
.
In
4.2
the case of the basic Albanese map being a submersion is examined. Then it is a fiber bundle whose fibers are foliated by leaves of the foliation. The first result is Lemma
4.10
saying that if the basic Albanese torus is non-trivial then
\mathcal{F}
does not have dense leaves and an example that this implication can not be reversed is provided. Next, we prove Lemma
4.13
which says that the restriction of a Riemannian foliation is Riemannian – more precisely, if the foliation is Riemannian with bundle-like metric
g
, then the restricted metric on fiber is bundle-like with respect to the restricted foliation. Then we provide an example of a foliation which is transversely parallelizable, but its restriction to a fiber of basic Albanese map is not. Next we consider a foliation on a compact Lie group
G
induced by its dense compact subgroup
W
with closure
H
and show that the basic Albanese torus of
(G,\mathcal{F}_{W})
and the Albanese torus of
G/H
are isomorphic. We then pass to subsection
4.3
about some regular foliations on nilmanifolds defined using the construction presented in
[
19
]
. This subsection is completed by working out an example of a
9
-dimensional compact nilmanifold with a foliation constructed using the Iwasawa manifold.

Finally, in Section
5
we consider singular Riemannian foliations obtained by taking the leaf closures and we show Proposition
5.1
which says that if
\dim H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})=k
, then the foliation restricted to each stratum of the stratification induced by the foliation has codimension at least
k
and has no isolated compact leaves if
k\neq 0
.

2.
Preliminaries

In the paper we assume that all manifolds are smooth, connected and without boundary. For such a manifold
M
the Lie algebra of global vector fields is denoted
\Gamma(M)
. For a vector field
X
and a tensor field
T
on
M

\mathcal{L}_{X}T
is the Lie derivative of
T
along
X
. If
M
has local coordinates
x_{1},...,x_{n}
, we denote a canonical local vector field obtained from
x_{i}
coordinate by
\partial_{x_{i}}
.

The space of differential
k
-forms
\Omega^{k}(M)
with the exterior differential operator
d:\Omega^{*}(M)\to\Omega^{*+1}(M)
forms a cochain complex. We use the following convention: for
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)
and for
V_{1},...,V_{k}\in\Gamma(M)
we have

\begin{split}d\omega(V_{0},V_{1},...,V_{k})=&\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}V_{i}(\omega(V_{0},...,V_{i-1},V_{i+1},...,V_{k}))\\
&+\sum_{i<j}\omega([V_{i},V_{j}],V_{0},...,V_{i-1},V_{i+1},...,V_{k}).\end{split}

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
. The metric tensor
g
defines the isomorphism
\flat\colon TM\rightarrow T^{*}M
by

v\mapsto v^{\flat}=g(v,\cdot).

Its inverse is denoted by
\sharp\colon T^{*}M\rightarrow TM
and is explicitly given by

\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\sharp},\ \alpha(v)=g(\alpha^{\sharp},v).

Then
g
induces a Riemannian metric tensor
\tilde{g}
on
T^{*}M
defined by
\tilde{g}(\alpha,\beta)=g(\alpha^{\sharp},\beta^{\sharp})
.

Unless stated otherwise, by a foliation on a manifold we always mean a regular foliation, i.e. whose leaves are smooth and equidimensional. A pair
(M,\mathcal{F})
is called a foliated manifold.

Now consider a manifold
N_{0}
of dimension
q
, an open covering
\left\{U_{i}\right\}
of
M
and submersions with connected fibers
f_{i}:U_{i}\to N_{0}
satisfying
g_{ij}\circ f_{j}=f_{i}
on
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
, where
g_{ij}:f_{j}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})\to f_{i}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
are local diffeomorphisms. Then we have a cocycle
\mathcal{U}=\left\{U_{i},f_{i},g_{ij}\right\}_{I}
and we say that a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
M
is modelled on
N_{0}
if it is given by
\mathcal{U}
. We denote
N_{i}=f_{i}(U_{i})
and we call
N=\coprod_{I}N_{i}
the transverse manifold associated to cocycle
\mathcal{U}
. Moreover,
\left\{g_{ij}\right\}
generate a pseudogroup of local diffeomorphisms of
N
, called the holonomy pseudogroup. For a more detailed description refer to
[
19
]
.

Let
T\mathcal{F}
be a subbundle of
TM
consisting of vectors which are tangent to leaves of
\mathcal{F}
. Denote by
\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
the vector fields on
M
tangent to leaves of
\mathcal{F}
. We call a function

basic if
Xf=0
for any
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
and we denote the space of basic functions by
\Omega^{0}(M,\mathcal{F})
. A vector field
Y\in\Gamma(M)
is called
basic
,
foliate
or
projectable
if
[\Gamma(\mathcal{F}),Y]\subset\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
. We denote by
L(M,\mathcal{F})
the Lie algebra of foliate vector fields for
(M,\mathcal{F})
. We have the following characterization of foliate vector fields
[
13
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a vector field on
M
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

•

X
is foliate;

•

the flow
\phi_{X}
associated to
X
satisfies the condition that each
\phi_{X}^{t}:=\phi_{X}(t,\cdot)
is a foliate diffeomorphism for
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
and
(N,\mathcal{G})
be two foliated manifolds. We say that a smooth map
f:M\to N
is
foliate
if each leaf of
\mathcal{F}
is mapped to a leaf of
\mathcal{G}
.

Moreover, we consider the quotient bundle
N\mathcal{F}:=TM/T\mathcal{F}
, called the
normal bundle
of
\mathcal{F}
. It is a vector bundle of rank
q=\codim\mathcal{F}
.

The foliation
\mathcal{F}
is called
transversely orientable
if the bundle
N\mathcal{F}
is orientable. For a foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F})
we can form its transverse orientation covering
\mathscr{M}
, i.e. a covering of
M
such that
\mathcal{F}
lifts to a foliation
\mathscr{F}
on
\mathscr{M}
such that
\mathscr{F}
is transversely orientable.

We say that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is
homogeneous
when for any
x,y\in M
there exists a foliate automorphism of
(M,\mathcal{F})
which maps
x
to
y
.

Now we define a transversely parallelizable foliation. Since
\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
is a Lie ideal in
L(M,\mathcal{F})
, we can define
l(M,\mathcal{F}):=L(M,\mathcal{F})/\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
. We say that a foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F})
is
transversely parallelizable
if there exist
\tilde{Y}_{1},...,\tilde{Y}_{q}\in l(M,\mathcal{F})
forming a global frame for
N\mathcal{F}
. Transversely parallelizable foliations are important examples of homogeneous foliations.

Given a foliated compact connected manifold
M
with a homogeneous foliation
\mathcal{F}
of codimension
q
, one can define a codimension
q_{b}
foliation
\mathcal{F}_{b}
on
M
by vector fields
X\in\Gamma(M)
satisfying
Xf=0
for any
f\in\Omega^{0}(M,\mathcal{F})
.

Definition 2.2
.

The foliation
\mathcal{F}_{b}
defined as above is called the
basic foliation
for
(M,\mathcal{F})
.

We have the following

Theorem 2.3
.

[
12
,
13
]

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a homogeneous foliation. Let
\mathcal{F}_{b}
be the associated basic foliation. Then
W:=M/\mathcal{F}_{b}
admits a manifold structure and there exists a fiber bundle
p_{b}:M\to W
such that every leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{b}
is a fiber of
p_{b}
.

Moreover,

(1)

if
\mathcal{F}
is transversely parallelizable, then on each fiber
L

the foliation
\mathcal{F}_{L}=\mathcal{F}\cap L
is dense and transversely parallelizable;

(2)

if the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is given by fibers of a submersion, then the foliations
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}_{b}
are equal.

The manifold
W=M/\mathcal{F}_{b}
is called the basic manifold for
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated manifold with a Riemannian metric
g
. Denote the metric induced by

on

by
g_{N\mathcal{F}}
. Then
g
is called
bundle-like
for
\mathcal{F}
if
\mathcal{L}_{X}g_{N\mathcal{F}}=0
for every
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
. A foliation
\mathcal{F}
is called
Riemannian
if there exists a Riemannian metric on
M
which is bundle-like for
\mathcal{F}
. Equivalently, if the foliation is given by a cocycle
\left\{U_{i},f_{i},g_{ij}\right\}
we require
g_{ij}
to be local isometries. Whenever we consider a foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F})
with a metric
g
we assume that the metric is bundle-like for
\mathcal{F}
. In such situation we will use notation
(M,g,\mathcal{F})
and call such triple
Riemannian foliated manifold
.

Let
(M,g,\mathcal{F})
be a closed Riemannian foliated manifold. We construct, as in
[
13
]
, the bundle of orthonormal frames of the normal bundle of
\mathcal{F}
(so a
O(q)
-bundle over
M
, or
SO(q)
-bundle in case when
\mathcal{F}
is transversely orientable). We denote this bundle by
\widehat{M}
. Moreover, we lift the foliation
\mathcal{F}
to
\widehat{M}
and the lifted foliation is denoted by
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
. We have the following important

Theorem 2.4

(Molino structure theorem,
[
13
,
18
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a transversely orientable Riemannian foliation on a closed manifold
(M,g)
. Let
\widehat{M}
be the orthonormal frame bundle of
(M,\mathcal{F})
and let
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
be the lift of
\mathcal{F}
to
\widehat{M}
. Then
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies the following properties:

•

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is a transversely parallelizable foliation;

•

there is a submersion
p:\widehat{M}\to W
to a closed manifold

such that the fibers of

are precisely the closures of leaves of
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
;

•

for a fiber
F
of

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}_{F}=\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\cap F
is dense and transversely parallelizable.

Actually more properties than mentioned in
2.3
and
2.4
hold, but we only included statements we will use in our paper.

For a foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F})
we define the complex of basic differential forms
\Omega^{*}(M,\mathcal{F})
as

\Omega^{k}(M,\mathcal{F})=\{\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(M):\forall X\in T\mathcal{F}\ i_{X}\alpha=i_{X}d\alpha=0\}.

Since
d
maps basic forms to basic forms,
(\Omega^{*}(M,\mathcal{F}),d)
forms a subcomplex of the de Rham complex. Its cohomology is denoted
H^{*}(M/\mathcal{F})
and called basic cohomology
[
18
]
. In degree 1 the inclusion of
\Omega^{1}(M,\mathcal{F})
in
\Omega^{1}(M)
induces an injective map
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})\hookrightarrow H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
. We denote
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Z}):=H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})\cap H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, where
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
denotes de Rham cohomology classes whose integrals over elements of
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
are integers. The elements of
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Z})
are called integer first basic cohomology classes of
(M,\mathcal{F})
. Similarly we define rational first basic cohomology classes
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q}):=H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})\cap H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})
.

For
k>1
and for a foliation
\mathcal{F}
the basic cohomology groups
H^{k}(M/\mathcal{F})
may be infinite-dimensional. However, if we assume that
\mathcal{F}
is Riemannian,
\dim H^{k}(M/\mathcal{F})<\infty
for any
k
.

To a Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
on a manifold
M
we can associate its mean curvature form
\kappa_{\mathcal{F}}
which is an element of
\Omega^{1}(M)
. As described e.g. in
[
18
]
, we can modify the bundle-like metric for
(M,\mathcal{F})
so that
\kappa_{\mathcal{F}}
is a basic
1
-form. Then the 1-form
\kappa_{\mathcal{F}}
is closed and we can define a basic cohomology class
[\kappa_{\mathcal{F}}]\in H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})
, called the Alvarez class.
We say that a Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
is taut if, for the metric and
\kappa_{\mathcal{F}}
modified as above,
[\kappa_{\mathcal{F}}]=0
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{F}
is taut;

(2)

we can choose a bundle-like metric on
\mathcal{F}
such that the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are minimal submanifolds;

(3)

H^{q}(M/\mathcal{F})\cong\mathbb{R}
, where
q=\codim\mathcal{F}
;

(4)

Poincaré duality holds for
H^{*}(M/\mathcal{F})
.

For information on foliations, in particular proofs of the aforementioned statements and information on Riemannian foliations we refer the reader to
[

,

,

]

Denote by
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
the space of
1
-forms on

which are harmonic with respect to a Riemannian metric

. Recall that if

is compact and orientable, then we have the

-vector space isomorphism

given by taking cohomology class of harmonic forms. This isomorphism is called the Hodge isomorphism. Provided that the foliation
\mathcal{F}
is Riemannian (with a bundle-like metric

) and transversely oriented, the situation can be generalized to basic harmonic forms
[
18
,
6
]
. Namely, under such assumptions denote by
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},g)
the space of differential forms which are basic harmonic for the foliation
\mathcal{F}
. The generalized Hodge isomorphism also holds, giving
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},g)\cong H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})
. However, note that basic harmonic forms need not be harmonic for the same metric.

The following proposition will be used (
[

]
, Corollary

)

Proposition 2.5
.

If
T
is a flat torus and
(M,g)
is a compact oriented Riemannian manifold, then a smooth mapping
f:M\to T
is harmonic if and only if

maps harmonic
1
-forms on

to harmonic

-forms on

.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact oriented Riemannian manifold such that

. Fix harmonic forms
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
which form a basis of
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M,g)
. Denote by
\tilde{M}
the universal covering of

and consider a point

. The vectors in

of the form

to

3.
Integer and rational basic cohomology for Riemannian foliations

The section is dedicated to the investigation of the integer first basic cohomology classes of a foliated Riemannian manifold. Firstly we consider transversely parallelizable foliations. Combining the results for them and the Molino structure theorem we get interesting results for general compact Riemannian foliated manifolds.

3.1.
The case of transversely parallelizable foliations

In this subsection we assume that
M
is a compact connected manifold and
\mathcal{F}
is a transversely paralellizable foliation on
M
of codimension
q
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are dense. Let
\alpha
be a closed form in
\Omega^{1}(M,\mathcal{F})
. Then
\alpha
is exact if and only if
\alpha
is closed and non-singular. Moreover,
[\alpha]=0
implies that
\alpha=0
.

Proof.

Assume
[\alpha]\neq 0
. For a basic (foliate) vector field
Y
, the function
\alpha(Y)
is basic and therefore constant.
If
[\alpha]\neq 0
, then there exists
x\in M
such that
\alpha_{x}\neq 0
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is transversely parallelizable, non-zero foliate vector fields generate
\Gamma(M)
, thus
\alpha_{x}(Y_{x})\neq 0
for some foliate vector field
Y
. It means that the function
\alpha(Y)
is constant and non-zero, hence
\alpha
is non-singular.

Now suppose
[\alpha]=0
. It means that
\alpha=df
for some basic function
f
. As basic functions for dense foliations are constant,
\alpha=0
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

If
\mathcal{F}
has dense leaves, then
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})=0
.

Proof.

Suppose the contrary. Let
0\neq[\alpha]\in H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})
. Then the map
p:M\to S^{1}
, induced by integration of
\alpha
along homotopy classes of paths, is a surjective submersion. Namely, we integrate
\alpha
over elements of
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\cong\pi_{1}(M)/[\pi_{1}(M),\pi_{1}(M)]
. Since the integrals are rational numbers, they give a discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
so a lattice
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}
. The map induced by integration
\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is
(\pi_{1}(M),\Lambda)
-equivariant so we obtain a map
p:M\to\mathbb{T}^{1}=\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Moreover, the fibers of
p
form a codimension
1
foliation on
M
such that each leaf of the foliation is closed and contains a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
, since integrating
\alpha
along a leaf gives
0
. However, since leaves of
\mathcal{F}
are dense, the fiber of
p
equals
M
, which contradicts surjectivity.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

A similar reasoning will be used in
4.1
to define the basic Albanese map.

Remark 3.4
.

In the above case, there may exist non-rational basic forms. For example, consider
\mathbb{T}^{2}
with
\mathcal{F}
the Kronecker foliation (i.e. a foliation obtained by suspending an irrational rotation of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
). Then the leaves are dense, but
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{2}/\mathcal{F})=\mathbb{R}
.

We have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(M,\mathcal{F})
be a foliated compact connected manifold with
\mathcal{F}
transversally parallelizable and of codimension
q
. Then
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})=H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{b},\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

Note that we have a monomorphism
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{b})\hookrightarrow H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})
induced by
\Omega^{*}(M,\mathcal{F}_{b})\hookrightarrow\Omega^{*}(M,\mathcal{F})
. In particular, it means that
H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{b},\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})
. By
2.3
the quotient space
M/\mathcal{F}_{b}
is a compact manifold, so
\dim H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{b},\mathbb{Q})=\dim H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{b})
.

Now we show that if there exists a closed form
\alpha
representing a class
[\alpha]\in H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q})\setminus H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{b})
then for
i:L\to M
the inclusion of the fiber of
p_{b}
(where
p_{b}
is as in Theorem
2.3
),
[i^{*}\alpha]\in H^{1}(L,\mathcal{F}_{L})
is non-zero and rational, contradicting
3.2
.

Because
p_{b}
is a locally trivial bundle,
M
is locally of the form
L\times U
for
U
an open subset of
W
, so we can consider foliate atlases for
\mathcal{F}_{b}
with

•

coordinates
x_{1},...,x_{n-q}
on
V\subset L
which are leaf coordinates for
\mathcal{F}
;

•

coordinates
y_{1},...,y_{q_{b}}
on
U
which are transversal to leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{b}
;

•

coordinates
y_{q_{b}+1},...,y_{q}
on
V\subset L
which are the remaining leaf coordinates for
\mathcal{F}_{b}
.

In those local coordinates, we have

\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^{q}f_{j}dy_{j},\ i^{*}\alpha=\sum_{i=q_{b}+1}^{q}f_{i}|_{L}dy_{i}

for
f_{i}\in\Omega^{0}(L\times U,\mathcal{F})
for all

,

depends only on the coordinates on

are constants for

since

Let

be a closed basic

-form such that

Suppose

is exact, then by

. Locally on

, it means that for

we have

, so

. Therefore on

we get

and those

do not depend on leaf coordinates for

. Therefore

is an element of

, which is a contradiction.
∎

3.2.
General case

Firstly we consider a more general situation. Let
M\xrightarrow{\pi}B
be a fiber bundle with connected fiber
F
. Denote the inclusion by
i:F\hookrightarrow M
. Suppose all spaces are compact and connected. Then we have the following subsequence of the long exact sequence for fibrations

\pi_{1}(F)\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\pi_{1}(M)\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}\pi_{1}(B)\to 0.

By Hurewicz isomorphism, which is functorial and is abelianization for first homotopy groups (and abelianization is right exact) we obtain exact sequence of singular homology

H_{1}(F,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{i_{*}}H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}H_{1}(B,\mathbb{Z})\to 0,

and moreover, by taking tensor product with
\mathbb{Q}
over
\mathbb{Z}
(which is also a right exact functor) we obtain the exact sequence

H_{1}(F,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{i_{*}}H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\pi_{*}}H_{1}(B,\mathbb{Q})\to 0.

Then by dualizing we obtain exact sequences

0\to H^{1}(B,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}}H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{i^{*}}H^{1}(F,\mathbb{Z}),

0\to H^{1}(B,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}}H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{i^{*}}H^{1}(F,\mathbb{Q})

and we can consider the latter as an exact sequence in de Rham cohomology.
Therefore we have a short exact sequence

0\to H^{1}(B,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}}H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{i^{*}}i^{*}(H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Q}))\to 0.

(1)

Note that
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})=H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, the exact sequence (

) splits as an exact sequence of vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{Q}
.

In the remaining part of the subsection we assume that
M\xrightarrow{\pi}B
is a principal
G
-bundle for a compact and connected Lie group
G
. Then the tangent bundle

splits as
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}
, where the vertical bundle
\mathcal{V}
is formed by
\ker\pi_{*}
and the horizontal bundle
\mathcal{H}
is
G
-invariant. Then
\pi_{*}:\mathcal{H}\to TB
is an isomorphism fiber-wise and any vector field on

lifts uniquely to a
G
-invariant section of
\mathcal{H}
.

Now suppose that we have foliations
\mathcal{F}_{G},\mathcal{F}_{M},\mathcal{F}_{B}
on

respectively and the foliations are such that the maps

are foliate. Moreover, we assume that the lift of
\mathcal{F}_{B}
is contained in
\mathcal{F}_{M}
– as in Molino bundle (it follows by construction and Lemma 3.4 in

) or Example

(where we consider foliation by points on

For basic cohomology, consider the restrictions

We want to ask whether in this case

. By definition,
\ker i_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}=\ker i^{*}\cap H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{M},\mathbb{Q})
(the latter taken as de Rham cohomology classes with rational integrals and here

is considered as a map in de Rham cohomology). Therefore we need to check that

. It translates to the condition that if a class

satisfies
\pi^{*}\alpha=\omega
for some
\alpha\in H^{1}(B,\mathbb{Q})
then
\alpha\in H^{1}(B/\mathcal{F}_{B},\mathbb{Q})
. However, by the assumption that the lift of
\mathcal{F}_{B}
via

is contained in

, a vector field

is lifted to a vector field

and thus we have

. Since it works for any

, we have that

is basic.

As a corollary we obtain that over

we have

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H^{1}(M/\mathcal{F}_{M},\mathbb{Q})=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}H^{1}(B/\mathcal{F}_{B},\mathbb{Q})+\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}W

with

.

Now we return to situation and assumptions as in Molino structure theorem

. Firstly we assume that
\mathcal{F}
is transversely parallelizable, since then as

we can take an

-bundle. By the considerations above, it means we have

, where

is a submodule of

. The latter considerations depend on

.

If

then

and therefore

equals

.

If

then

is a

where

is the Euler class of the circle bundle and

•

for

Now consider the situation when

is not transversely orientable. Let

be the orientation covering of foliated manifold

and let

be the lift of

to

. We can act on

by

the 2-element group which corresponds to an involution. Denote the involution by

. Then

is a twofold covering of

,

is compact and

is transversely orientable.

The map

from which we obtain

It can be shown that Tischler’s theorem

4.
Basic Albanese map

We begin this section by defining the basic Albanese map and deriving some basic properties. Then we consider some topological and geometric implications of the assumption that the basic Albanese map is a submersion. Finally, we examine the basic Albanese maps for nilmanifolds with a particular type of a foliation.

4.1.
Construction and basic properties

The presented construction is a generalization of the classical Albanese map, also called Abel–Jacobi map.

Suppose we have a Riemannian foliated manifold
(M,\mathcal{F},g)
. Let
[\omega_{1}],...,[\omega_{b}]
be a basis of
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})
such that
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
are generators of
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M/\mathcal{F})
. Denote by
\tilde{M}
the universal covering of
M
. Similarly as in the classical case, we define a map

\tilde{A}_{\mathcal{F}}:\tilde{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{k},\ [\gamma]\mapsto\left[\int_{\gamma}\omega_{i}\right]_{i=1,...,k}.

Then the subgroup
\Lambda_{\mathcal{F}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
generated by
\left[\int_{\sigma}\omega_{i}\right]_{i=1,...,k}
, where
\sigma
are elements of the free part of
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, is a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Indeed, it is an Abelian subgroup of
\mathbb{Q}^{k}
generated by
k
independent elements, so it is a discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
The map
\tilde{A}_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\left(\pi_{1}(M),\Lambda_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
-equivariant so it factorizes to a map
A_{\mathcal{F}}:M\to\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The map
A_{\mathcal{F}}
defined above is called the
basic Albanese
map for
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
and the torus
Alb(M,\mathcal{F}):=\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
is called the
basic Albanese torus
for
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
.

The following properties of this basic Albanese map generalize some properties of classical Albanese map and are easy to prove.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A_{\mathcal{F}}
be the basic Albanese map for
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
. Then

•

A_{\mathcal{F}}
is smooth;

•

the differential
dA_{\mathcal{F}}:TM\to TAlb(M,\mathcal{F})
is given explicitly by
dA_{\mathcal{F}}=(\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k})
, in particular
A_{\mathcal{F}}
is a submersion if and only if all forms
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
are linearly independent, i.e. the form
\omega_{1}\wedge...\wedge\omega_{k}
is non-singular;

•

every leaf of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
(and therefore its closure) is contained in some fiber of
A_{\mathcal{F}}
.

We consider the basic Albanese torus with flat metric.

Basic Albanese map for given forms may not be harmonic in general. However, this is the case when
\kappa_{\mathcal{F}}
is basic, since then basic harmonic forms are harmonic (Lemma 2.4 in
[
5
]
).

Remark 4.3
.

Assume that
\kappa_{\mathcal{F}}
is basic. The basic Albanese map is related to the classical Albanese map in the following way. Complete
[\omega_{1}],...,[\omega_{k}]
to a basis of

and

being generators of torsionless part of

is again a lattice, so
\pi
induces a map

which we will denote by the same symbol. Then
\pi
maps
\Lambda
to
\Lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
and we have a commutative diagram

.

Let

be basic. Then the map

is a harmonic map.

Proof.

By

maps harmonic 1-forms on

to harmonic 1-forms on

By

Proposition 4.5
.

The map

Proof.

The forms

.

Let

be basic. Then for any flat torus

Example 4.8

Recall

Suppose that the basic Albanese map is a submersion. Then

If we assume

is Riemannian, it is natural to ask whether the restricted metric
g_{F}
is bundle-like for
\mathcal{F}|_{F}
. Indeed, we have

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric on
M
which is bundle-like for
\mathcal{F}
. Then the restricted metric
g_{F}
on
F
is bundle-like for
\mathcal{F}|_{F}
.

Proof.

By
[
4
]
the claim that
g_{F}
is bundle-like for
\mathcal{F}|_{F}
is equivalent to the claim that
g_{F}(\nabla_{X}^{F}V,Y)+g_{F}(X,\nabla_{Y}^{F}V)=0
for any vector field
V\in\Gamma(F)
which is tangent to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}|_{F}
and any vector fields
X,Y\in\Gamma(F)
which are orthogonal to leaves of
\mathcal{F}|_{F}
and
\nabla^{F}
is the Levi-Civita connection for
(F,g_{F})
.

Choose
X,Y\in\Gamma(F)
and extend them to vector fields on
M
and denote the extensions by the same symbol, then by Gauss formula

\nabla_{X}^{M}Y=\nabla_{X}^{F}Y+\alpha(X,Y)

where
\alpha:\Gamma(F)\times\Gamma(F)\to\Gamma(F)^{\perp}
is the second fundamental form.

We can extend vector fields on
F
which are foliate with respect to
\mathcal{F}|_{F}
to vector fields on
M
so that they are foliate with respect to
\mathcal{F}
and, when we use local trivialization for fiber bundle and consider a trivialized neighbourhood
U\times F
, in which we identify initial fiber
F
with
\left\{x\right\}\times F
, the vector fields are of the form
\sum f_{i}X_{i}
on
U\times F
, where each
X_{i}
is a vector field of
F
and each
f_{i}\in C^{\infty}(U)
is such that there exist neighbourhoods
x\in V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subsetneq U
satisfying
f_{i}|_{V_{1}}\equiv 1
and
f_{i}|_{U\setminus V_{2}}=0
, and the vector fields are extended to
0
on
M
without the trivialized neighbourhood.
Denoting the extended vector fields by the same symbols, we have

\begin{split}0&=g(\nabla_{X}^{M}V,Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}^{M}V)\\
&=g(\nabla_{X}^{F}V+\alpha(X,V),Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}^{F}V+\alpha(Y,V))\\
&=g(\nabla_{X}^{F}V,Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}^{F}V)\end{split}

which proves the initial claim.
∎

Remark 4.14

5.
Some applications to singular Riemannian foliations

In this section we use the basic Albanese map to obtain some information on the singular Riemannian foliation obtained from a Riemannian foliation by taking leaf closures.

Let
(M,g,{\mathcal{F}})
be a compact foliated Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with a regular Riemannian foliation
\mathcal{F}
of codimension
q
for which
g
is bundle-like.

If
\alpha
is 1-basic form, then
\alpha^{\sharp}
is an
\mathcal{F}
-orthogonal foliate vector field. Indeed,

•

X\in T{\mathcal{F}}
implies
g(\alpha^{\sharp},X)=\alpha(X)=0
so
\alpha^{\sharp}
is
\mathcal{F}
-orthogonal;

•

since the metric
g
is bundle-like and the form
\alpha
is basic, it projects to a 1-form
\bar{\alpha}
on the transverse manifold
N
(i.e. the manifold
N=\coprod_{i}N_{i}
where
f_{i}:U_{i}\to N_{i}
are local submersions defining the foliation; we define
\bar{\alpha}
by
\bar{\alpha}|_{N_{i}}:=f_{i}^{*}\alpha|_{U_{i}}
), and the vector field
\alpha^{\sharp}
corresponds to the holonomy invariant vector field,
\bar{\alpha}^{\sharp}
of
N
, therefore it is a foliate vector field of
(M,{\mathcal{F}})
.

Moreover, global foliate vector fields are tangent to the strata of
(M,g,{\mathcal{F}})
. More precisely, consider the stratification of
M
by closures of leaves of
\mathcal{F}
. Let
X
be a foliate vector field of
\mathcal{F}
and consider the associated global flow
\phi_{X}:M\times\mathbb{R}\to M
. Then
\phi_{X}^{t}:M\to M
is a diffeomorphism, by
2.1
it maps diffeomorphically a leaf
L
to another leaf
L^{\prime}
, and therefore is a diffeomorphism of closures of
L
and
L^{\prime}
. In particular, each
\phi_{X}(t)
preserves the stratification.
Now if we take the flow on each stratum, then we obtain that
X
corresponds to a vector field tangent to the stratum. Therefore, we have the following

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(M,g,{\mathcal{F}})
be a Riemannian foliated manifold such that
\dim H^{1}(M/{\mathcal{F}})=k
and the basic Albanese map is a submersion. Then the foliation
\mathcal{F}
restricted to each stratum has codimension at least
k
. In particular, if
k>0
, the foliation
\mathcal{F}
has no isolated compact leaves.

Proof.

By the assumption that the basic Albanese map is a submersion, the 1-forms which are representatives of non-zero classes of
H^{1}(M,\mathcal{F})
cannot vanish at any point.

It follows that we have basic
1
-forms
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k}
which are orthogonal
with respect to the metric
\tilde{g}
induced by
g
. Then
\alpha_{1}^{\sharp},...,\alpha_{k}^{\sharp}
are non-vanishing and orthogonal with respect to
g
. Moreover, they are also tangent to strata of the stratification and orthogonal to leaves of
\mathcal{F}
, therefore the codimension of
\mathcal{F}
on each stratum is at least
k
.

If
\mathcal{F}
has an isolated compact leaf then any basic 1-form vanishes along this compact leaf, therefore the leaf itself forms a stratum of the stratification.
∎

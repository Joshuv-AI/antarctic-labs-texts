Spectral and Dynamical Analysis of Fractional Discrete Laplacians on the Half-Lattice
Nassim Athmouni

Abstract
. We investigate discrete fractional Laplacians defined on the half-lattice in several dimensions, allowing possibly different fractional orders along each coordinate direction. By expressing the half-lattice operator as a boundary restriction of the full-lattice one plus a bounded correction that is relatively compact with respect to it, we show that both operators share the same essential spectrum and the same interior threshold structure. For perturbations by a decaying potential, the conjugate-operator method provides a strict Mourre estimate on any compact energy window inside the continuous spectrum, excluding threshold points. As a consequence, a localized Limiting Absorption Principle holds, ensuring the absence of singular continuous spectrum, the finiteness of eigenvalues, and weighted propagation (transport) bounds. The form-theoretic construction also extends naturally to negative fractional orders. Overall, the relative compactness of the boundary correction guarantees that the interior-energy spectral and dynamical results obtained on the full lattice remain valid on the half-lattice without modification.

2.1.1.
Topology and the boundary of

We equip

Definition 2.1

For

For

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

The embedding

2.1.2.
Restriction, extension, and projection

Define the restriction and extension-by-zero operators:

(\mathfrak{R}_{+}g)(n)=g(n),\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d},\qquad(\mathfrak{J}_{+}f)(n)=\begin{cases}f(n),&n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d},\\

Both are contractions of norm

Lemma 2.4
.

\mathfrak{R}_{+}^{*}=\mathfrak{J}_{+}
.

is self-adjoint and idempotent, hence the orthogonal projection onto

. Moreover,

\mathfrak{R}_{+}P_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}=\mathfrak{R}_{+},\qquad P_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}\mathfrak{J}_{+}=\mathfrak{J}_{+}.

Proof.

For

,

\|\mathfrak{R}_{+}g\|^{2}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}|g(n)|^{2}\leq\|g\|^{2},

so

, and equality holds for

supported in
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
. For

\|\mathfrak{J}_{+}f\|^{2}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|(\mathfrak{J}_{+}f)(n)|^{2}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}|f(n)|^{2}=\|f\|^{2},

so
\mathfrak{J}_{+}
is an isometry.

Adjointness: for

and
g\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
,

\langle\mathfrak{R}_{+}g,f\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}^{d})}=\langle g,\mathfrak{J}_{+}f\rangle_{\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}.

Thus
\mathfrak{R}_{+}^{*}=\mathfrak{J}_{+}
.

Self-adjointness and idempotence of

follow by direct computation. Finally,

\mathfrak{R}_{+}P_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}=\mathfrak{R}_{+},\qquad P_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}\mathfrak{J}_{+}=\mathfrak{J}_{+},

) to get

(2.4)

L_{j,+}^{r}-PL_{j}^{r}P=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}f_{r}(z)\,(z-L_{j,+})^{-1}\,B_{j}\,(z-L_{j})^{-1}P\ +\ \big[(\cdot)\big]^{*}\;dz.

Define
K_{j,r}
by the right-hand side of (
2.4
) (identified on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}^{d})
via
\mathrm{Ran}P\simeq\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}^{d})
). This gives (
2.2
).

Step 3: Boundary localization (collar support).

Let
\Pi_{R}
be multiplication by
\mathbf{1}_{\partial_{R}{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}
. Since
B_{j}
acts only across the boundary,
each integrand in (
2.4
) factors through the first boundary layer, and there exists
R\in{\mathbb{N}}
(one can take
R=1
) such that

K_{j,r}=\Pi_{R}\,K_{j,r}\,\Pi_{R}.

Thus
K_{j,r}
is supported in the collar
\partial_{R}{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
on both sides.

Step 4: Compactness.

Fix
z\in\Gamma
. The resolvents
(z-L_{j})^{-1}
and
(z-L_{j,+})^{-1}
are bounded and enjoy Combes–Thomas off–diagonal exponential decay
[
CoTo
]
. Since
B_{j}
has finite range and is supported on the first boundary layer, the operators

\Pi_{R}\,(z-L_{j,+})^{-1}B_{j},\qquad B_{j}\,(z-L_{j})^{-1}\,\Pi_{R}

are Hilbert–Schmidt. Hence each integrand in (
2.4
) is compact, and the contour integral converges in operator norm, proving that
K_{j,r}
is compact. Summation over
j
preserves compactness and collar support.

Collecting the steps, with
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}K_{j,r_{j}}
, we obtain

\Delta_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\;=\;\mathfrak{R}_{+}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\,\mathfrak{J}_{+}\;+\;K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}},\qquad K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}=\Pi_{R}\,K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\,\Pi_{R}\in\mathcal{K}\big(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}^{d})\big),

which completes the proof.
∎

3.
Extension of the Lattice Laplacian to the hypercubic lattice quadrant

3.1.
Fractional Laplacians on the Discrete Half-line

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T,S
be bounded linear operators on a Banach space, and suppose
ST=\nu\,\mathrm{id}
with
\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\ast}
. Then for all
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(T+S)^{h}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}\alpha_{h,k}\,\nu^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{h-2k}T^{l}S^{\,h-2k-l},\qquad\alpha_{h,k}:=\binom{h}{k}-\binom{h}{k-1},

with the convention
\binom{h}{-1}=0
and
\alpha_{0,0}=1
.

Proof.

By induction on
h
. The heart of the proof is the Pascal-type recursion

\alpha_{h+1,k}=\alpha_{h,k}+\alpha_{h,k-1}
. The cases
h=0,1
are immediate.

Assume the formula holds for some
h\geq 1
. With
m:=h-2k
,

(T+S)^{h+1}=(T+S)\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}\alpha_{h,k}\,\nu^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m}T^{l}S^{\,m-l}.

(1)
T
-part.
Multiplying on the left by
T
,

T\sum_{l=0}^{m}T^{l}S^{\,m-l}=\sum_{l=0}^{m}T^{l+1}S^{\,m-l}=\sum_{l=1}^{m+1}T^{l}S^{\,m+1-l},

so the contribution is
\sum_{k}\alpha_{h,k}\nu^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{h+1-2k}T^{l}S^{\,h+1-2k-l}
.

(2)
S
-part.
Using
ST^{l}=\nu T^{l-1}
for
l\geq 1
and
ST^{0}=S
,

S\sum_{l=0}^{m}T^{l}S^{\,m-l}=S^{\,m+1}+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\nu\,T^{l-1}S^{\,m+1-l}=\sum_{l=0}^{m+1}\gamma_{l}\,T^{l}S^{\,m+1-l},

with
\gamma_{0}=1
and
\gamma_{l}=\nu
for
1\leq l\leq m+1
. Thus the
S
-part contributes

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}\alpha_{h,k}\,\nu^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{h+1-2k}\gamma_{l}\,T^{l}S^{\,h+1-2k-l}.

(3) Collecting coefficients.
For fixed
k
and
l\geq 1
the total coefficient of
T^{l}S^{\,h+1-2k-l}

is
\alpha_{h,k}\nu^{k}+\alpha_{h,k-1}\nu^{k}=(\alpha_{h,k}+\alpha_{h,k-1})\nu^{k}
(set
\alpha_{h,-1}=0
to also cover
l=0
).
Hence

(T+S)^{h+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(h+1)/2\rfloor}(\alpha_{h,k}+\alpha_{h,k-1})\,\nu^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{h+1-2k}T^{l}S^{\,h+1-2k-l}.

Finally,
\alpha_{h+1,k}=\binom{h+1}{k}-\binom{h+1}{k-1}=(\binom{h}{k}-\binom{h}{k-1})+(\binom{h}{k-1}-\binom{h}{k-2})=\alpha_{h,k}+\alpha_{h,k-1}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Expanding
(T+S)^{h}
as words on
\{T,S\}
and reducing each occurrence of
ST
to
\nu
yields the normal-ordered monomials
T^{\ell}S^{\,h-2k-\ell}
with multiplicity

\alpha_{h,k}=\binom{h}{k}-\binom{h}{k-1}
, independent of
\ell
.

Corollary 3.3
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
,

(U+U^{*})^{h}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}\alpha_{h,k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,m\geq 0\\
\ell+m=h-2k\end{subarray}}U^{\,m}(U^{*})^{\,\ell},\qquad\alpha_{h,k}:=\binom{h}{k}-\binom{h}{k-1}.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

D_{h}\;:=\;(U_{+}+U_{+}^{*})^{h}\;-\;\mathfrak{R}_{+}\,(U+U^{*})^{h}\,\mathfrak{J}_{+}\qquad(h\in{\mathbb{N}}).

For
h=0,1
one has
D_{0}=D_{1}=0
. For
h\geq 2
, set

\beta_{h,p}\;:=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{h-2-p}{2}\right\rfloor}\alpha_{h,k}\qquad\Bigl(\alpha_{h,k}=\tbinom{h}{k}-\tbinom{h}{k-1},\ \tbinom{h}{-1}:=0\Bigr).

Then the exact factorization holds:

(3.1)

D_{h}\;=\;\sum_{p=0}^{h-2}\beta_{h,p}\,U_{+}^{\,p}\,P_{0}\,(U_{+}^{*})^{\,h-2-p},

where
P_{0}
is the rank-one projection onto
{\mathbb{C}}\,\delta_{0}
. In particular
\mathrm{rank}\,D_{h}\leq h-1
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.3
,

(U+U^{*})^{h}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}\alpha_{h,k}\!\!\sum_{\ell+m=h-2k}\!\!U^{m}(U^{*})^{\ell},\quad(U_{+}+U_{+}^{*})^{h}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}\alpha_{h,k}\!\!\sum_{\ell+m=h-2k}\!\!U_{+}^{m}(U_{+}^{*})^{\ell}.

Using (for
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
)

\bigl(\mathfrak{R}_{+}U^{m}(U^{*})^{\ell}\mathfrak{J}_{+}\,f\bigr)(n)=\mathbf{1}_{\{n+m-\ell\geq 0\}}\,f(n+m-\ell),\quad\bigl(U_{+}^{m}(U_{+}^{*})^{\ell}f\bigr)(n)=\mathbf{1}_{\{n\geq\ell\}}\,f(n+m-\ell),

we get

(D_{h}f)(n)=\sum_{k}\alpha_{h,k}\!\!\sum_{\ell+m=h-2k}\!\!\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\{n\geq\ell\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{n+m-\ell\geq 0\}}\bigr)\,f(n+m-\ell).

The bracket equals
-1
exactly when
n<\ell\leq n+m
.
Writing
\ell=n+s
with
s\in\{1,\dots,m\}
gives
p:=m-s\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
and
n+m-\ell=p
.
Reindexing yields (
3.1
) with
\beta_{h,p}=\sum_{k\leq\lfloor(h-2-p)/2\rfloor}\alpha_{h,k}
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

For
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
define

K_{r}:=\Delta_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\,r}-\mathfrak{R}_{+}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\,r}\,\mathfrak{J}_{+}.

Then

(3.2)

K_{r}=-\sum_{h=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{h}\binom{r}{h}\,2^{\,r-h}\,D_{h},

with
D_{h}
as in (
3.1
). Moreover:

(a)

If
r>0
, the series (
3.2
) converges in operator norm; hence
K_{r}
is compact (norm-limit of finite-rank operators).

(b)

If
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the sum (
3.2
) is finite and

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}\,K_{r}\leq\sum_{h=2}^{r}(h-1)=\frac{r(r-1)}{2}
.

(c)

If
r<0
, the series (
3.2
) converges strongly on
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
,
defines a densely defined closable operator; we denote its closure again by
K_{r}
,
and this closure equals
\Delta_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\,r}-\mathfrak{R}_{+}\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\,r}\,\mathfrak{J}_{+}
.

Proof.

Write
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}=2\bigl(I-\tfrac{1}{2}(U+U^{*})\bigr)
and
\Delta_{{\mathbb{N}}}=2\bigl(I-\tfrac{1}{2}(U_{+}+U_{+}^{*})\bigr)
.
By spectral calculus,

\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{\,r}=\sum_{h\geq 0}(-1)^{h}\binom{r}{h}\,2^{\,r-h}(U+U^{*})^{h},\qquad\Delta_{{\mathbb{N}}}^{\,r}=\sum_{h\geq 0}(-1)^{h}\binom{r}{h}\,2^{\,r-h}(U_{+}+U_{+}^{*})^{h},

with norm convergence if
r>0
and strong convergence on
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
for all
r
.
Subtract and use
D_{0}=D_{1}=0
to get (
3.2
).
For (a), combine
\mathrm{rank}\,D_{h}\leq h-1
with
|\binom{r}{h}|\sim C_{r}\,h^{-1-r}
and the extra
2^{-h}
to obtain absolute norm convergence.
For (b),
\binom{r}{h}=0
for
h>r
.
For (c), use strong convergence on the core
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{N}})
and the definition of
K_{r}
.
∎

3.2.
Heat kernels and method of images on
\mathbb{N}^{d}

We collect here standard kernel identities and notations used later
(in particular for Lemma
3.7
), without reproving
results that can be found in the literature
(see e.g.
[
Sp
,
Law
,
Wo
,
Car
,
De
,
Dav1
,
Cip
,
Len
]
).

Kernel on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

For the discrete Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z},j}

with
(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z},j}u)(n)=2u(n)-u(n+e_{j})-u(n-e_{j})
, the heat semigroup
has a product kernel (see e.g.
[
Sp
, Sec. I.9]
,
[
Law
]
)

(e^{-t\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}})(n,m)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}p_{t}(n_{j}-m_{j}),\qquad p_{t}(k)=e^{-2t}\,I_{|k|}(2t),

where
I_{\nu}
is the modified Bessel function given by

I_{\nu}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!\,\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+\nu},

for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.In particular,

p_{t}(k)=p_{t}(-k)\geq 0
,
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{t}(k)=1
(see
[
Wo
]
),
and the Gaussian bound

(3.3)

|p_{t}(k)|\ \leq\ C\,t^{-1/2}\exp\!\big(-c\,k^{2}/t\big),\qquad t>0,\ k\in\mathbb{Z},

holds for some
C,c>0
(see
[
Car
,
De
]
).
Consequently,

\big|(e^{-t\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}})(n,m)\big|\ \leq\ C\,t^{-d/2}\exp\!\big(-c\,|n-m|_{1}^{2}/t\big).

Method of images on
\mathbb{N}
.

On the half–line
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
with Dirichlet at
0
, the heat
kernel is obtained by reflection across the barrier between
-1
and
0

(see
[
Dav1
, Sec. 3.3]
,
[
Cip
, Prop. 2.2]
):

(3.4)

(e^{-t\Delta_{\mathbb{N}}})(n,m)\ =\ p_{t}(n-m)\ -\ p_{t}(n+m+2),\qquad n,m\geq 0.

Here the second term corresponds to the image point
-m-2
and enforces the
boundary cancellation.

Operator–kernel dictionary.

For
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
,

\big(e^{-t\Delta_{\mathbb{N}}}f\big)(n)=\sum_{m\geq 0}(e^{-t\Delta_{\mathbb{N}}})(n,m)\,f(m)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\big[p_{t}(n-m)-p_{t}(n+m+2)\big]\,f(m).

This is a standard convolution–reflection representation
(cf.
[
Wo
,
Law
]
).

Inclusion–exclusion on
\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

With Dirichlet on each coordinate face, the half–space kernel results from
coordinate–wise reflections (method of images, see
[
Dav1
, Ch. 3]
,

[
Len
]
):

(3.5)

(e^{-t\Delta_{\mathbb{N}^{d}}})(n,m)\ =\ \sum_{J\subset\{1,\dots,d\}}(-1)^{|J|}\,p_{t}\!\big(n-R_{J}m\big),

where
R_{J}
reflects the coordinates indexed by
J
(and leaves the others
unchanged), and
p_{t}(\cdot)
denotes the full-space kernel above. A simple
geometry gives, for every nonempty
J
(see
[
De
]
),

(3.6)

|n-R_{J}m|_{1}^{2}\ \geq\ |n-m|_{1}^{2}\ +\ \mathrm{dist}_{1}(n,\partial\mathbb{N}^{d})^{2}\ +\ \mathrm{dist}_{1}(m,\partial\mathbb{N}^{d})^{2}.

Spectral analysis and Mourre estimate

4.1.
First spectral properties

In this section we will denote by

(4.1)

H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}:=\big(\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\big)\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}

and

(4.2)

H_{0,{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}:=\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}=H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}+K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}},

as given by Proposition
4.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}:=\mathfrak{R}_{+}\,\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\,\mathfrak{J}_{+}
.
Then

\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}=\big(\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\big)\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}+K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}},

where the correction
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
satisfies:

(i)

If all
r_{j}>0
, then
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
is bounded and compact.

(ii)

If some
r_{j}<0
, then
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
is unbounded but relatively compact with respect to
\big(\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\big)\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}
.

Proof.

By tensor structure,

\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(d-j)},

where each
\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}},j}^{\,r_{j}}
acts in the
j
-th coordinate. Passing from
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
to
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
via

\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}=\mathfrak{R}_{+}\,\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\,\mathfrak{J}_{+}

replaces in direction
j
the bilateral shift by a unilateral one and produces a one–dimensional boundary
correction
K_{r_{j}}^{(j)}
supported on the face
\{n_{j}=0\}
. Therefore

\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}-\big(\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\big)\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}\;=\;K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Big(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(j-1)}\otimes K_{r_{j}}\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes(d-j)}\Big),

plus lower–dimensional terms supported on intersections of faces (finite sums of tensor products of the 1D corrections).

If all
r_{j}>0
, each
K_{r_{j}}
is compact on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})
(and of finite rank if
r_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
) by the 1D analysis; tensoring with bounded identities and summing finitely many such terms gives a compact
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
.

If some
r_{j}<0
, set
H_{0}:=\big(\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\big)\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}
. Each elementary correction term is
H_{0}
-compact by the one–dimensional argument in the
j
-th coordinate and bounded tensoring in the others; hence
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
is
H_{0}
-compact. Since
\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}=H_{0}+K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
, the resolvent identity yields relative compactness of
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
with respect to
H_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Compact resolvent difference)
.

For any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
, one has

(H_{0,{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}-z)^{-1}-\big(H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}-z\big)^{-1}\ \in\ \mathcal{K}\big(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}^{d})\big).

Proof.

By the resolvent identity,

(H_{0,{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}-z)^{-1}-\big(H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}-z\big)^{-1}=-(H_{0,{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}-z)^{-1}\,K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\,\big(H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}-z\big)^{-1}.

If all
r_{j}>0
,
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
is compact; (it is composed of bounded-compact-bounded).
If some
r_{j}<0
,
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\big(H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}-\overline{z}\big)^{-1}
is compact (relative compactness), hence the right hand side is compact.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

With the same notation, the essential spectra coincide:

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}\!\big(\Delta^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}\big)=\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}\!\big(H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\!\restriction_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}\big).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
the resolvent difference is compact for some (hence all)

. Apply Weyl’s theorem (e.g.
[
ReSi
, Theorem. XIII.14]
,
[
AmBoGe
, Proposition. 4.5.3]
).

4.2.
Conjugate operator and commutator estimates

In this section we establish a Mourre estimate for the operator

\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
.
Since fractional powers
\Delta^{r_{j}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
may be unbounded
(precisely when some
r_{j}<0
), the commutator

[\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}},\,\mathrm{i}A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}\,]
—with

A_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
the conjugate operator defined in
[
At
]

does not in general yield a bounded operator.
The correct framework is therefore to interpret commutators
in the sense of quadratic forms.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
H
and
A
be self-adjoint operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
The
form commutator
of
H
with
A
is the sesquilinear form

\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}(f,g)\;:=\;\langle Hf,\,\mathrm{i}Ag\rangle-\langle\mathrm{i}Af,\,Hg\rangle,\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{D}(H)\cap\mathcal{D}(A).

We say that
[H,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}
exists on a reducing subspace

Mc\subset\mathcal{H}
if
\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}
extends by continuity to a bounded form
on
Mc\times Mc
. In this case there is a unique bounded operator

B\in\mathcal{B}(Mc)
such that

\langle f,\,Bg\rangle=\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}(f,g),\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{D}(H)\cap\mathcal{D}(A)\cap Mc,

and we define
B:=[H,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}
on
Mc
.
In applications below we take
Mc=E_{I}(H)\mathcal{H}
for compact interior windows

I\Subset\sigma(H)^{\circ}
and write

E_{I}(H)\,[H,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}\,E_{I}(H)

for the resulting bounded operator on
E_{I}(H)\mathcal{H}
.

Localized regularity and Mourre estimate

In what follows we work exclusively on compact interior spectral windows

I\Subset\sigma(H)^{\circ}
and use only the
localized
regularity classes

\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
and
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
.

Definition 4.5

(Localized regularity)
.

Let
H
be self-adjoint and
I\Subset\sigma(H)^{\circ}
.
We say that
H\in\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
on
I
if, for every

\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
, the bounded operator
\varphi(H)

belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{k}(A)
.
Similarly,
H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
on
I

if
\varphi(H)\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}(A)
for all

\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
.

Equivalently,
H\in\mathcal{C}^{k}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
on
I

if and only if

E_{I}(H)\,(H-\mathrm{i})^{-1}\in\mathcal{C}^{k}(A),

and likewise for the class
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
.

Remark 4.6
.

For
H=\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
and the conjugate operator
A=A_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
constructed above,
the form
\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}
is well-defined on the core
\mathcal{C}_{c}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
even when some
r_{j}<0
(unbounded from below).
By the localized
\mathcal{C}^{2}
–regularity established earlier and the interior Mourre framework,

\mathfrak{q}_{A}^{H}
extends to a bounded form on
E_{I}(H)\mathcal{H}
; hence
E_{I}(H)[H,\mathrm{i}A]_{\circ}E_{I}(H)\in\mathcal{B}(E_{I}(H)\mathcal{H})
.
This is the object that appears in the Mourre estimate on
I
.

Conjugate operator on the half–space.

We adjust a conjugate operator
A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}
on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}^{d})
by summing the one-dimensional generators:

(4.3)

A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j,+},\quad\text{with}\quad A_{j,+}:=-\frac{\mathrm{i}\cdot\mathrm{sign}(r_{j})}{2}\left(U_{j,+}(Q_{j}+\tfrac{1}{2})-(Q_{j}+\tfrac{1}{2})U_{j,+}^{*}\right),

where
Q_{j}
is the position operator and
U_{j,+}
the unilateral shift along direction
j
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}})
yields

\mathrm{i}[H,F_{R}]=\tfrac{1}{R}\,\Phi^{\prime}(A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}/R)\,\mathrm{i}[H,A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}]+\mathscr{O}(R^{-2})

in
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2})
, uniformly on
\mathcal{I}
. Hence

\frac{d}{dt}\langle u(t),F_{R}u(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{R}\langle u(t),\Phi^{\prime}(A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}/R)\,\mathrm{i}[H,A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}]\,u(t)\rangle+\mathscr{O}(R^{-2})\|u\|^{2}.

Inserting
\chi(H)
on both sides of
\mathrm{i}[H,A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}]
and using the localized Mourre estimate

\chi(H)\,\mathrm{i}[H,A_{{\mathbb{N}}^{d}}]\,\chi(H)\geq c_{\mathcal{I}}\,\chi(H)^{2}-\chi(H)K_{\mathcal{I}}\chi(H)
with compact
K_{\mathcal{I}}
,
one integrates from
0
to
\mathcal{T}
and uses Kato smoothness (from the LAP) to control the compact term.
Choosing
R=\alpha\mathcal{T}
and optimizing logarithmically as in
[
AmBoGe
, § 7.4]
gives the stated bound.
∎

6.2.3.
Half-space fiber scattering

Setup 6.3

(Partial fiber decomposition)
.

Write
n=(n_{\parallel},n_{\perp})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{N}}
and let
\mathcal{F}_{\parallel}
be the Fourier transform in
n_{\parallel}
.
Then

\mathcal{F}_{\parallel}H_{0}\mathcal{F}_{\parallel}^{-1}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}}^{\oplus}H_{0}(\kappa)\,d\kappa,\qquad\mathcal{F}_{\parallel}H\mathcal{F}_{\parallel}^{-1}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}}^{\oplus}H(\kappa)\,d\kappa,

with
H(\kappa)=H_{0}(\kappa)+W_{\kappa}
acting on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}})
and
H_{0}(\kappa)
a one-dimensional fractional difference
operator in
n_{\perp}
plus a finite-range Hankel-type correction at the boundary.

Theorem 6.4
.

There exists a full-measure subset
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}
such that, for all
\kappa\in\Omega
,

H(\kappa)\in\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{\perp})
and the localized LAP holds on
\mathcal{I}
with constants uniform on compact
subsets of
\Omega
. Consequently, the fiber scattering matrix
S(\lambda,\kappa)
is unitary for a.e.

(\lambda,\kappa)\in\mathcal{I}\times\Omega
and depends continuously on
(\lambda,\kappa)
.

Proof of Theorem
6.4
.

Applying
\mathcal{F}_{\parallel}
yields the direct integral decomposition above.
The commutator with
A_{\perp}
coincides with the bulk commutator in the perpendicular direction, with coefficients continuous in
\kappa
and uniformly bounded on
{\mathbb{T}}^{d-1}
.
Thus
H_{0}(\kappa)\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{\perp})
, and away from thresholds (which form a zero-measure set in
\kappa
for fixed interior
\mathcal{I}
) the Mourre constant
c_{\mathcal{I}}(\kappa)
depends continuously on
\kappa
.
Hence there exists a full-measure
\Omega
with
c_{\mathcal{I}}(\kappa)\geq c_{\mathcal{I}}>0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
.
The perturbations
W_{\kappa}
inherit
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A_{\perp})
from
(H
0
)
–
(H
1
)
.
The localized LAP follows from
[
AmBoGe
, Thm. 7.4.1]
, and the unitarity and continuity of
S(\lambda,\kappa)
follow from the stationary construction fiberwise together with dominated convergence in
\kappa
.
∎

6.2.4.
Continuity in the fractional exponents

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subset\{\vec{r}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\ \min_{j}r_{j}>r_{*}>-1\}
be a compact set.
Then, for every
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}
, the map

\vec{r}\ \longmapsto\ (H_{0}(\vec{r})-z)^{-1}

is norm–continuous as an operator on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{N}}^{d})
.

Moreover, assume that

\mathcal{I}\Subset\bigcap_{\vec{r}\in\mathcal{R}}\Big(\sigma(H_{0}(\vec{r}))^{\circ}\setminus\operatorname{Thr}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{\vec{r}})\Big).

Then the Mourre and limiting–absorption–principle constants can be chosen

uniformly
in
\vec{r}\in\mathcal{R}
,
and the scattering matrix

\vec{r}\mapsto S_{\vec{r}}(\lambda)

is strongly continuous on
\mathcal{I}

for almost every
\lambda
.

Proof.

In Fourier variables on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
,
H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}(\vec{\mathrm{r}})
acts as multiplication by
h_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(k)=\sum_{j}(2-2\cos k_{j})^{r_{j}}
, continuous in
(\vec{\mathrm{r}},k)
on
[r_{*},R]^{d}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.
Hence
\vec{\mathrm{r}}\mapsto H_{0,{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}(\vec{\mathrm{r}})
and its resolvent are norm-continuous.
The half-space operator is a compression plus a compact boundary correction
K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
supported in a fixed collar;
\vec{\mathrm{r}}\mapsto K_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}
is norm-continuous as a finite linear combination of difference operators with continuous coefficients.
Thus
\vec{\mathrm{r}}\mapsto(H_{0}(\vec{\mathrm{r}})-z)^{-1}
is norm-continuous.
Uniform Mourre/LAP on
\mathcal{I}
follows from uniform avoidance of thresholds and compactness of
\mathcal{R}
.
The stationary formula (
6.4
) then yields strong continuity of
S_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\lambda)
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

The norm-resolvent continuity in
\vec{\mathrm{r}}
implies inner–outer continuity of the spectra.
This is in the spirit of results on magnetic families on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[
PR16
]

and of results for magnetic pseudodifferential families in the continuum
[
AMP10
]
.
In particular, a Mourre estimate uniform on
\mathcal{I}
yields a uniform LAP and the strong continuity of
the scattering matrix
\lambda\mapsto S_{\vec{\mathrm{r}}}(\lambda)
on
\mathcal{I}
.

Proposition 6.7
.

If
W\in\ell^{1}(\langle n\rangle^{1+\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
, then
the point spectrum of
H
inside
\mathcal{I}
is finite (counting multiplicities):

\#\big(\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(H)\cap\mathcal{I}\big)<\infty.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
. By Theorem
6.1
,

\chi(H)-\chi(H_{0})\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
. By the Helffer–Sjöstrand functional calculus, this implies

E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)-E_{\mathcal{I}}(H_{0})\in\mathfrak{S}_{1}
. On the interior window
\mathcal{I}
, the free operator
H_{0}
is purely absolutely continuous, hence
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H_{0})
has no eigenvalues. Thus
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H)
differs from
E_{\mathcal{I}}(H_{0})
by a trace-class operator, so its range is finite dimensional. Therefore the point spectrum of
H
inside
\mathcal{I}
consists of finitely many eigenvalues with finite multiplicity.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

The fact that
\#\bigl(\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(H)\cap\mathcal{I}\bigr)<\infty
follows from the uniform Mourre estimate on
\mathcal{I}
combined with the limiting absorption principle (LAP). These two ingredients yield compactness of the localized resolvent
\chi(H)(H-\lambda\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}\chi(H)
for
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{I})
, which in turn implies that
H
has only finitely many eigenvalues (each of finite multiplicity) in the interval
\mathcal{I}
.

In the continuous magnetic setting, similar finiteness results have been derived using Schatten–von Neumann criteria. For instance, Athmouni and Purice
[
AP18
]
exploit the framework of magnetic Weyl calculus to place Birman–Schwinger-type operators in trace ideals
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
and deduce spectral finiteness.

For a general background on trace ideals, Birman–Schwinger principles, and the Mourre theory, we refer the reader to
[
Sim05
]
and
[
ReSi
, Vol. IV]
.

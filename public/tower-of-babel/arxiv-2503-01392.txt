Dirac operators twisted by ramified Euclidean line bundles
Gorapada Bera, Thomas Walpuski

Abstract
This article is concerned with the analysis of Dirac operators D D twisted by ramified Euclidean line bundles ( Z , 𝔩 ) (Z,{\mathfrak{l}}) —motivated by their relation with 𝐙 / 2 ​ 𝐙 \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z} harmonic spinors, which have appeared in various contexts in gauge theory and calibrated geometry. The closed extensions of D D are described in terms of the Gelfand–Robbin quotient 𝐇 ˇ \check{\mathbf{H}} . Assuming that the branching locus Z Z is a closed codimension two submanifold, a geometric realisation of 𝐇 ˇ \check{\mathbf{H}} is constructed. This, in turn, leads to an L 2 L^{2} regularity theory.

2.1
The minimal and maximal extension

Proposition 2.3
.

The bounded operator

D\mskip 0.5mu\colon\thinspace H^{1}\Gamma\lparen X{\backslash}Z,S\otimes{\mathfrak{l}}\rparen\to L^{2}\Gamma\lparen X{\backslash}Z,S\otimes{\mathfrak{l}}\rparen

The proof relies on the following consequences of the borderline Hardy inequality.

Lemma 2.4
.

The following hold:

(1)

H^{1}\Gamma\lparen X{\backslash}Z,S\otimes{\mathfrak{l}}\rparen=H_{0}^{1}\Gamma\lparen X{\backslash}Z,S\otimes{\mathfrak{l}}\rparen

(2)

The inclusion

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0

\chi_{\varepsilon}\coloneq\chi(r/\varepsilon)
.
Let
\phi\in H^{1}\Gamma\lparen X{\backslash}Z,S\otimes{\mathfrak{l}}\rparen

Since
\lvert r{\rm d}\chi_{\varepsilon}\rvert\lesssim 1

\|\nabla(\chi_{\varepsilon}\phi)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|(r{\rm d}\chi_{\varepsilon})r^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\chi_{\varepsilon}\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\lparen\int_{\supp({\rm d}\chi_{\varepsilon})}\lvert r^{-1}\phi\rvert^{2}+\lvert\nabla\phi\rvert^{2}\right\rparen^{1/2}.

Therefore, by
2.1
and monotone convergence,

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\|\nabla(\chi_{\varepsilon}\phi)\|_{L^{2}}=0.

This implies
(1)
.

Let
(\phi_{n})\in H^{1}\Gamma\lparen X{\backslash}Z,S\otimes{\mathfrak{l}}\rparen^{\mathbf{N}}
with
\|\phi_{n}\|_{H^{1}}=1
for every

For every
\varepsilon>0

\|\chi_{\varepsilon}\phi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Therefore,
(2)

∎

Proposition 2.5

(cf.

(1)

Here, and throughout this article,

denotes a local orthonormal frame; and

(2)

The following argument can be found in
[

By direct computation,

\operatorname{div}v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{L}_{e_{i}}\langle v,e_{i}\rangle=\langle D\phi,\psi\rangle-\langle\phi,D\psi\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\gamma\lparen\nabla_{e_{i}}^{T}e_{i}\rparen\phi,\psi\rangle

(1)

By direct computation,

\displaystyle D^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\gamma(e_{i}){\nabla}_{e_{i}}\gamma(e_{j}){\nabla}_{e_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\gamma(e_{j})\gamma(e_{j}){\nabla}_{e_{i}}{\nabla}_{e_{j}}+\gamma(e_{i})\gamma({\nabla}_{e_{i}}^{T}e_{j}){\nabla}_{e_{j}}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{*}\nabla+\gamma(F_{\nabla})+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\gamma(e_{i})\gamma({\nabla}_{e_{i}}^{T}e_{j}){\nabla}_{e_{j}}

\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}^{T}e_{j},e_{k}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\Tor(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k}).

(2)

2.2
Closed extensions and residue conditions

The closed extensions of
D_{\mathrm{min}}
can be systematically understood as follows.

Definition 2.11
.

The
Gelfand–Robbin quotient
is the Hilbert space

\check{\mathbf{H}}\coloneq\frac{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}})}{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})}.

Since
D_{\mathrm{min}}
is closed,

\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})\subset\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}})
is a
\|-\|_{D}
–closed subspace.
Denote the canonical projection map by

[-]\mskip 0.5mu\colon\thinspace\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}})\to\check{\mathbf{H}}.

2.12
.

\check{\mathbf{H}}
is localised on
Z
in the following sense:

[\phi]=[\chi(r/\varepsilon)\phi]
for every
\varepsilon>0
and
\phi\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}})
.

Definition 2.13
.

A
residue condition
is a closed subspace

R\subset\check{\mathbf{H}}.

Proposition 2.14

(closed extension=residue condition; cf.
[
7
, Lemma 3.3 (a)]
)
.

If
R\subset\check{\mathbf{H}}
is a residue condition,
then

D_{R}\coloneq D_{\mathrm{max}}|_{\mathrm{dom}(D_{R})}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathrm{dom}(D_{R})\coloneq[-]^{-1}(R)

is a closed extension of
D_{\mathrm{min}}
.
Moreover,
every closed extension of
D_{\mathrm{min}}
is of this form.

Proof.

Let
R\subset\check{\mathbf{H}}
be a residue condition.
The canonical projection
[-]\mskip 0.5mu\colon\thinspace\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}})\to\check{\mathbf{H}}
is bounded.
Therefore,
\mathrm{dom}(D_{R})\coloneq[-]^{-1}(R)\subset\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}})
is a
\|-\|_{D}
–closed subspace;
hence:
D_{R}
is closed.

Let
\bar{D}
be a closed extension of
D_{\mathrm{min}}
.
Since
\mathrm{dom}(\bar{D})\subset\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}})
is a
\|-\|_{D}
–closed subspace,

R\coloneq[\mathrm{dom}(\bar{D})]=\frac{\mathrm{dom}(\bar{D})}{\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})}\subset\check{\mathbf{H}}
is a closed subspace.
Since
\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})\subset\mathrm{dom}(\bar{D})
,

\mathrm{dom}(\bar{D})=[-]^{-1}(R)
;
hence:
\bar{D}=D_{R}
.
∎

2.3
The Green’s form and adjoint extensions

The Gelfand–Robbin quotient carries a symplectic structure related to the construction of adjoint extensions.

Definition 2.15
.

The
Green’s form

G\in\Hom\lparen\Lambda^{2}\check{\mathbf{H}},\mathbf{R}\rparen

G([\phi]\wedge[\psi])\coloneq\langle D\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle\phi,D\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Proposition 2.16

(cf.
[
7
,
32
]
)
.

G
is symplectic;
that is:
it induces a Hilbert space isomorphism

\displaystyle J\mskip 0.5mu\colon\thinspace\check{\mathbf{H}}

\displaystyle\to\hat{\mathbf{H}}\coloneq{L}(\check{\mathbf{H}},\mathbf{R})

\displaystyle[\phi]

\displaystyle\mapsto G([\phi]\wedge-).

Moreover:
if
\sharp\mskip 0.5mu\colon\thinspace\hat{\mathbf{H}}\to\check{\mathbf{H}}
denotes the isomorphism induced by the inner product,
then
\sharp\circ J
is an isometric complex structure on
\check{\mathbf{H}}

Proof.

The canonical projection induces an isometry

[-]\mskip 0.5mu\colon\thinspace\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})^{\perp_{D}}\cong\check{\mathbf{H}}
.
Here

indicates the orthogonal complement with respect to the graph inner product

\langle-,-\rangle_{D}\coloneq\langle-,-\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle D-,D-\rangle_{L^{2}}.

By direct inspection,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})^{\perp_{D}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\phi\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}}):\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle D\phi,D\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\penalty\ \textnormal{for every}\penalty\ \psi\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\phi\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}}):D\phi\in\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{max}})\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ D^{2}\phi=-\phi\right\}.

Therefore,

D

D\mskip 0.5mu\colon\thinspace\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})^{\perp_{D}}\to\mathrm{dom}(D_{\mathrm{min}})^{\perp_{D}}
.

The diagram

Lemma 3.30

(Leading order terms; cf.
[
9
,
11
]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbf{R}
.
Let
\phi\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}((0,1))
with
\phi,\lparen\partial_{r}-\lambda/r\rparen\phi\in L^{2}((0,1),r{\rm d}r)
.
The following hold:

(1)

If
\lambda\in(-1,0)
,
then there is a unique
a\in\mathbf{R}
such that

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\phi(r)-ar^{\lambda}=0
.

(2)

If
\lambda=0
,
then
\lvert\phi(r)\rvert\lesssim_{\phi}\lvert\log(r)\rvert^{1/2}
.

(3)

If
\lambda\notin(-1,0]
,
then
\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\phi(r)=0
.

(4)

If
\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\phi(r)=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\phi(r)=0
,
then

\int_{0}^{1}\left\lparen\lvert\partial_{r}\phi\rvert^{2}+\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{r^{2}}\lvert\phi\rvert^{2}\right\rparen\,r{\rm d}r=\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\big\lparen\partial_{r}-\tfrac{\lambda}{r}\big\rparen\phi\right\rvert^{2}r{\rm d}r.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to that of
[
11
, Lemma 3.50]
and is repeated here only for the reader’s convenience.

Evidently,

(\partial_{r}-\lambda/r)r^{\lambda}=0
and
r^{\lambda}\in L^{2}\lparen(0,1),r{\rm d}r\rparen
if and only if
\lambda>-1
.
Set
\psi\coloneq\lparen\partial_{r}-\lambda/r\rparen\phi
.
By variation of parameters,
there is a unique
a\in\mathbf{R}
such that

\tilde{\phi}(r)\coloneq\phi(r)-ar^{\lambda}=\begin{cases}r^{\lambda}\int_{0}^{r}s^{-(\lambda+1)}\psi(s)\,s{\rm d}s&\text{if}\penalty\ \lambda<0\\
-r^{\lambda}\int_{r}^{1}s^{-(\lambda+1)}\psi(s)\,s{\rm d}s&\text{if}\penalty\ \lambda\geqslant 0.\end{cases}

Of course, if
\lambda\leqslant-1
, then
a=0
.

If
\lambda<0
,
then, by Cauchy–Schwarz and monotone convergence,

\lvert\tilde{\phi}(r)\rvert^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2\lvert\lambda\rvert}\int_{0}^{r}\lvert\psi(s)\rvert^{2}\,s{\rm d}s=o(1)\quad\textnormal{as}\quad r\downarrow 0.

If
\lambda=0
,
then

\lvert\tilde{\phi}(r)\rvert^{2}\leqslant\lvert\log(r)\rvert\int_{r}^{1}\lvert\psi(s)\rvert^{2}\,s{\rm d}s=O(\lvert\log(r)\rvert)\quad\textnormal{as}\quad r\downarrow 0.

If
\lambda>0
, then,
by Cauchy–Schwarz,
for
r\leqslant\varepsilon\leqslant 1

\lvert\tilde{\phi}(r)\rvert^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\lvert\psi(s)\rvert^{2}\,s{\rm d}s+\frac{(r/\varepsilon)^{2\lambda}}{\lambda}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\lvert\psi(s)\rvert^{2}\,s{\rm d}s\eqcolon{\rm I}(\varepsilon)+{\rm II}(r,\varepsilon).

By monotone convergence,

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}{\rm I}(\varepsilon)=0
.
Evidently,

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}{\rm II}(r,\varepsilon)=0
.
Therefore,
\tilde{\phi}(r)=o(1)
as
r\downarrow 0
.
These observations imply
(1)
,
(2)
, and
(3)
.

(4)
is a consequence of

\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert big\lparen\partial_{r}-\tfrac{\lambda}{r}big\rparen\phi\right\rvert^{2}r{\rm d}r=\int_{0}^{1}\left\lparen\lvert\partial_{r}\phi\rvert^{2}+\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{r^{2}}\lvert\phi\rvert^{2}\right\rparen\,r{\rm d}r-\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{r}\lvert\phi\rvert^{2}{\rm d}r.\qed

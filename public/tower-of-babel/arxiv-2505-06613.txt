Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities and ground states of Fermions in relativistic Hartree-Fock model
Yuan-da Wu Xiaoyu Zeng Yimin Zhang

Abstract
This paper presents a rigorous mathematical analysis of the relativistic Hartree-Fock model for finite Fermi systems. We first establish an optimal Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev (GNS) inequality with Hartree-type nonlinearities for orthonormal systems and characterize the qualitative properties of its optimizers. Furthermore, we derive a finite-rank Lieb-Thirring inequality involving convolution terms and show that it is the duality of the GNS-inequality-a result that, to our knowledge, has not previously appeared in the literature. For the relativistic Hartree-Fock model, we prove that ground states exist if and only if the coupling parameter K < 𝒦 ∞ ( N ) K<\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{(N)} , where 𝒦 ∞ ( N ) \mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{(N)} is the optimal constant in the GNS-inequality. Finally, under suitable assumptions on the external potentials, we calculate the precisely asymptotic behavior of ground states as K ↗ 𝒦 ∞ ( N ) K\nearrow\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{(N)} .

Introduction

The relativistic Fermi systems subject to the gravitational interactions, such as neutron stars and white dwarfs, could be described by the following relativistic Hartree-Fock energy functional

H_{e}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big[(p_{i}^{2}+m_{i}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}-m_{i}\big]+\kappa\sum_{i>j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-1},

(1.1)

where the first term describes the relativistic kinetic energy and the last term describes gravitational (
\kappa<0
) or coulomb potential (
\kappa>0
).
In
[
37
]
, Lieb and Thirring studied the stability of Fermi systems under gravitational interactions. Lieb and Yau in
[
38
]
further considered the stability of (
1.1
) with
m_{i}=m\not=0
for all
i
and
\kappa<0
. they revealed the relation between the critical constant
\kappa
of stability and the Chandrasekhar limit. Later, in
[
39
]
, they extended this framework by introducing nuclear-electron correlation effects, thereby providing rigorous mathematical foundations for various stability criteria of ground state. Recently, Lenzmann and Lewin
[
30
]
studied the existence of minimizer in Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov model, which provides a reliable description of unstable nuclei, introduces a pairing density matrix to describe the phenomenon of “Cooper pairing”. Based on this work, Nguyen in
[
42
]
analyzed the asymptotic behavior and showed that when
N
large enough, up to scaling, the solution of the limiting mean field equation corresponds to the unique optimizer of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality. Very recently, Chen, Guo, Nam and Ou Yang
[
9
]
generalized the existence results of
[
30
]
to the critical mass case. For more related studies, one can refer to
[
3
,
15
,
41
,
19
,
10
,
7
,
8
,
34
]
and references therein.

Under suitable simplifying assumptions, the Hartree-Fock functional can be expressed as
[
14
,
42
]
:

\mathcal{E}_{K}(\gamma):={\rm Tr}\big((\sqrt{-\Delta+m^{2}}+V(x))\gamma\big)-K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-1})\rho_{\gamma}dx,

(1.2)

where
0\leq\gamma=\gamma^{*}
is a compact operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})
with
{\rm rank~}\gamma\leq N
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\rm Tr}(\gamma)<\infty
.
\rho_{\gamma}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the density associated with
\gamma
.

V(x)\geq 0
is a trapping potential and the pseudo-differential operator
\sqrt{-\Delta+m^{2}}
describes the kinetic energy of a fermion with mass
m>0
. In general, the ground states of the relativistic Hartree-Fock energy functional can be obtained by solving the following constrained minimization problem:

\bar{E}_{K}(N)=\inf\Big\{\mathcal{E}_{K}(\gamma)\big|\ 0\leq\gamma=\gamma^{*}\leq 1,\ {\rm Tr}(\gamma)=N\Big\}.

(1.3)

By the spectral theory a positive compact operator
\gamma
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})
can be diagonalized by

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}n_{i}|u_{i}\rangle\langle u_{i}|,

where
n_{i}
are non-negative constants and
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is the orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C})
. As a consequence,

\text{Tr}(\sqrt{-\Delta+m^{2}}\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}n_{i}\|(-\Delta+m^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{i}\|_{2}^{2},\ \ \rho_{\gamma}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}n_{i}|u_{i}|^{2}\text{ and }{\rm Tr}(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\gamma}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}n_{i}.

We say that a compact self-adjoint operator
\gamma\geq 0
belongs to the
q
-trace class
\mathcal{S}^{q}
(
1\leq q\leq\infty
), provided that

\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{S}^{q}}:=\begin{cases}(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}n_{i}^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,\quad&\text{ if }1\leq q<\infty,\\
\max_{i}n_{i}<\infty,\quad&\text{ if }q=\infty.\end{cases}

In particular, when
q=\infty
, we would denote
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}}
as
\|\cdot\|
for brevity in the whole paper.

To rigorously investigate minimization problems of the form (
1.3
) and related topics, it is essential to balance the kinetic energy
{\rm Tr}(\gamma)
and the interaction energy
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-1})\rho_{\gamma}dx
. For this purpose, we generalize the problem by replacing the gravitational interaction
|x|^{-1}
with a Riesz potential
|x|^{-\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in(0,2)
. We then study the following optimal Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev (GNS) inequality for
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}^{q}
:

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\rm rank~}\gamma\in[1,N],\gamma\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{S}^{q}}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}}{\rm Tr}(\sqrt{-\Delta}\gamma)}{\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\gamma}(|x|^{-\alpha}*\rho_{\gamma})dx\big)^{1/\alpha}}.

(1.4)

We first remark that by applying the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and the Daubechies inequality
[
33
]
, which states that there exists
C>0
such that

\text{Tr}((-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma)\geq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\gamma}^{\frac{4}{3}}dx,

one can deduce that
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)}>0
is well-defined for each
N
. It is worth pointing out that some similar GNS-inequalities in a local setting have been established in two celebrated papers
[
18
,
14
]
. Gontier, Lewin and Nazar in
[
18
]
studied the following minimization problem in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for dimensions
d\geq 1
:

J(N)=\inf\Big\{{\rm Tr}(-\Delta\gamma)-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\gamma}^{p}dx,\ 0\leq\gamma=\gamma^{*}\leq 1,\ {\rm Tr}(\gamma)=N\Big\},

where
1<p<\min\{2,1+2/d\}
is a mass-subcritical exponent. By applying a modified concentration compactness principle together with some refined estimates, they proved that there exists an increasing sequence of integers

N_{1}=1<N_{2}=2<N_{3}<\ldots<N_{j}<\ldots
,
for which the
binding inequality
(or strict subadditivity condition) holds for
J(N)
with
N=N_{j}
. Consequently,
J(N)
admits a minimizer of the form
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|u_{i}\rangle\langle u_{i}|
. In particular, they proved that
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
solves the following fermionic Nonlinear Schrödinger equations under
orthonormal conditions

\Big[-\Delta-\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|u_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{p-1}\Big]u_{i}=\mu_{i}u_{i},\quad(u_{i},u_{j})_{L^{2}}=\delta_{ij},\quad i,j=1,2,...,N,

(1.5)

and
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
is also an optimizer for the following GNS-inequality with orthonormal conditions:

\mathcal{C}_{p,d}^{(N)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|u_{n}(x)|^{2}\Big)^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{2}{d(p-1)}}\leq N^{\frac{2}{d(p-1)-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}dx,\ \forall u_{i}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ (u_{i},u_{j})_{L^{2}}=\delta_{ij}.

Subsequently, Frank, Gontier and Lewin in
[
14
]
further explored some similar problem, where the mass-critical exponent
p=1+2/d
is particularly involved. They proved that the best constant
\mathcal{G}_{p,d}^{(N)}
for the following GNS-inequality

\mathcal{G}_{p,d}^{(N)}\|\rho_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{2p}{d(p-1)}}\leq\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{S}^{q}}^{\frac{p(2-d)+d}{d(p-1)}}{\rm Tr}(-\Delta\gamma),\ \ \forall\ 0<\gamma=\gamma^{*}\text{ and }{\rm rank~}\gamma\leq N

(1.6)

is attained by some
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{R}n_{i}|u_{i}\rangle\langle u_{i}|
with
R\leq N
, where

\begin{split}1\leq p\leq 1+\frac{2}{d},\ \text{ and }\ q:=\begin{cases}\frac{2p+d-pd}{2+d-pd},&\text{ if }1\leq p<1+\frac{2}{d},\\
+\infty,&\text{ if }p=1+\frac{2}{d}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(1.7)

Moreover,
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
solves an orthonormal system similar to (
1.5
). Specially, it has been proved in
[
14
]
that the inequality (
1.6
) is indeed dual to the well-known finite rank

Lieb-Thirring inequality
and the quantitative estimates were obtained:

\mathcal{G}_{p,d}^{(N)}(\mathcal{T}_{\kappa,d}^{(N)})^{\frac{2}{d}}=\Big(\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\frac{d}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{2\kappa}{d}}\Big(\frac{d}{2\kappa+d}\Big)\text{ with }\kappa:=\frac{p}{p-1}-\frac{d}{2},

where
\mathcal{T}_{\kappa,d}^{(N)}>0
is the best constant for the following finite rank Lieb-Thirring inequality

\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\lambda_{n}(-\Delta+V)|^{\kappa}\leq\mathcal{T}_{\kappa,d}^{(N)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)_{-}^{\kappa+\frac{d}{2}}dx,\ \text{for all }V\in L^{\kappa+\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Here,
a_{-}=\max\{0,-a\}
and
\lambda_{n}(-\Delta+V)<0
denotes the
n
-th negative eigenvalue of
-\Delta+V
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The Lieb-Thirring inequality is a famous inequality in literature of mathematical physics, which has been extensively studied. Lieb and Thirring
[
35
,
36
]
proved that if
\kappa>\frac{1}{2}
in
d=1
, or
\kappa>0
in
d\geq 2
, then there holds that

\mathcal{T}_{\kappa,d}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{T}_{\kappa,d}^{(N)}<\infty.

This estimate were further obtained in the critical cases for
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}
in
d=1
, and
\kappa=0
in
d\geq 3
by
[
11
,
32
,
44
,
47
]
, respectively. Moreover, to determine the precise value of
\mathcal{T}_{\kappa,d}
is a central issue in Density Functional Theory. One can refer to
[
14
,
12
,
28
]
for the recent progress on this aspect.
Very recently, Ilyin, Laptev and Zelik
[
27
]
generalized this inequality to the bounded domain and gave the sharp constant when the domain is sphere or torus.

Motivated by the heuristic work of
[
18
,
14
]
, in this manuscript, we investigate the relativistic Hartree-type GNS-equality (
1.4
). Specifically, we show that the
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)}
can be attained for all
q\in[1,+\infty]
and
\alpha
is even allowed to belong to
mass-superciritical
regime. More importantly, Through some variational arguments, we derive a Lieb-Thirring type inequality involving convolution potentials, which has not been observed in the literature, to the best of our knowledge. Our first main result addresses the achievement of equality (
1.4
) as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq N<\infty
,
0<\alpha<2
and
1\leq q\leq\frac{2-\alpha}{(1-\alpha)_{+}}
, where

\frac{2-\alpha}{(1-\alpha)_{+}}:=\begin{cases}\frac{2-\alpha}{1-\alpha},&\rm{for}\ \ 0<\alpha<1,\\
\infty,&\rm{for}\ \ 1\leq\alpha<2.\end{cases}

Then, the best constant
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)}>0
in (
1.4
)
can be attained by some positive operator
\gamma
with
1\leq{\rm rank~}\gamma\leq N
. Moreover, we have

(i)

When
1\leq q<\frac{2-\alpha}{(1-\alpha)_{+}}
, then up to scaling, every minimizing sequence for (
1.4
) is compact.

(ii)

Let
H_{\alpha,\gamma}:=\sqrt{-\Delta}-\frac{2}{\alpha}\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-\alpha}
, then
\gamma
has the following explicit expression

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{R}k_{i}|u_{n_{i}}\rangle\langle u_{n_{i}}|,\ \ \text{for}\ k_{i}>0,\quad i=1,2,...,R\leq N,

where
(\mu_{n_{i}},u_{n_{i}})
with
\mu_{n_{i}}\leq 0
and
(u_{n_{i}},u_{n_{j}})_{L^{2}}=\delta_{ij}\ (i,j=1,\cdots R)
are eigen-pairs of
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
. Especially, if
R<N
, then
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
has at most
R
negative eigenvalues.

(iii)

When
1<q<\frac{2-\alpha}{(1-\alpha)_{+}}
or
\alpha=1
and
q=\infty
, then
\mu_{n_{i}}<0
for all
1\leq i\leq R
, and
k_{i}
can be expressed as

k_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}(\sum_{k=1}^{R}|\mu_{n_{k}}|^{\frac{q}{q-1}})^{-1}|\mu_{n_{i}}|^{\frac{1}{q-1}},&0<\alpha<2,\ 1<q<\frac{2-\alpha}{(1-\alpha)_{+}},\\
\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}(\sum_{k=1}^{R}|\mu_{n_{k}}|)^{-1},&\alpha=1,\ q=\infty.\end{cases}

In particular,
\mu_{n_{i}}(1\leq i\leq R)
are the first
R
negative eigenvalues of
H_{\alpha,\gamma}
provided that either
0<\alpha\leq 1
, or
1<\alpha<2
and
{\rm rank~}\gamma<N
.

(iv)

If
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
q
is close to
\frac{2-\alpha}{(1-\alpha)_{+}}
enough, there exists a sequence
\{N_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
with
\lim_{i\to\infty}N_{i}=\infty
, such that

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N_{i})}<\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N_{i}-1)},\quad\forall\ i\geq 1.

Especially, there holds that

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(2N)}<\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)},\ \ \forall\ N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

(1.8)

The proof of the above theorem is primarily inspired by the arguments presented in
[
14
]
. However, in our setting, the inclusion of the nonlocal operator
\sqrt{-\Delta}
and Hartree nonlinearity introduces substantial difficulties in deriving the modified concentration-compactness principle. Another challenge in addressing inequality (
1.4
) arises from the regularity theory for the fractional Laplacian. In general,
(-\Delta)^{s}
with

is called ultra-relativistic Schrödinger operator, which is a threshold from the aspect of regularity theory. That is, for a linear equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u+Vu=0,\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\ d\geq 1,

suppose
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
V(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
one can show that
u\in C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with some
\beta\in(0,1)
. However, if
s\leq\frac{1}{2}
, to obtain similar estimates one needs to further assume that
V(x)\in C^{0,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for some
\gamma>1-2s
. We also emphasize that in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\alpha=1
(
\alpha=2
) corresponds to the mass-critical (Sobolev) exponent, therefore, our GNS-inequalities are very general and the parameters
q
is flexible. Specifically, our results cover the range
q\in[1,\frac{2-\alpha}{(1-\alpha)_{+}}]
for all
0<\alpha<2
, whereas in
[
14
]
, the paramter
p
is assumed to at most equal to the the mass critical exponent
1+\frac{2}{d}
, and
q
takes the specific values in (
1.7
).

Remark 1.1
.

(a)

Since
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)}
is non-increasing w.r.t.
N
, it then follows from the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev-inequality and Daubechies inequality that for the special case of
q=1
, there holds

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,1}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,1}^{(N)}=\inf_{N\geq 1}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,1}^{(N)}>0.

Indeed, one can show that
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,1}
is actually the best constant of the inequality

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,1}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}({|x|^{-\alpha}}*\rho_{\gamma})\rho_{\gamma}dx\Big)^{1/\alpha}\leq\|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{S}^{1}}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}}\rm Tr(\sqrt{-\Delta}\gamma)\ \text{ for all }0\leq\gamma\in\mathcal{S}^{1}.

(b)

When
N=1
, (
1.4
) degenerates to the following classical Hartree-type Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(1)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}({|x|^{-\alpha}}*|u|^{2})|u|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/\alpha}\leq\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{4-2\alpha}{\alpha}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u\|_{2}^{2},\ \forall\ u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We next establish a type of finite rank Lieb-Thirring inequality containing convolutions, which is dual to the GNS-inequality (
1.4
). Our result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Duality).
Let
1\leq N<\infty
,
0<\alpha<2
and
1<q\leq\infty
, then
we have the following optimal Hartree type Lieb-Thirring inequality:

\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\lambda_{n}(\sqrt{-\Delta}+V(x)*|x|^{-\alpha})|^{q^{\prime}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\alpha,q^{\prime}}^{(N)}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(V_{-}(x)*|x|^{-\alpha})V_{-}(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{2-\alpha}}

(1.9)

holds for all
V(x)
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|V(x)|*|x|^{-\alpha})|V(x)|dx<\infty
, where
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha,q^{\prime}}^{(N)}<\infty
is the best constant, and
\lambda_{n}(\sqrt{-\Delta}+V(x)*|x|^{-\alpha})
is the
n
-th negative eigenvalue of
\sqrt{-\Delta}+V(x)*|x|^{-\alpha}
when it exists and
0
otherwise.
Moreover, the following identity holds

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)}(\mathcal{L}_{\alpha,q^{\prime}}^{(N)})^{\frac{(2-\alpha)(q-1)}{\alpha q}}=\frac{\alpha}{2}\Big(\frac{2-\alpha}{2}\Big)^{\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}},

where
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)}
is the best constant in (
1.4
).

Remark 1.2
.

The classical Lieb-Thirring inequality for
\sqrt{-\Delta}
states that for
V(x)\in L^{q^{\prime}+3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then

\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\lambda_{n}(\sqrt{-\Delta}+V)|^{q^{\prime}}\leq\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,q^{\prime}}^{(N)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)_{-}^{q^{\prime}+3}dx.

(1.10)

If substituting
V(x)*|x|^{-\alpha}
into (
1.10
), we arrive at

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big|\lambda_{n}(\sqrt{-\Delta}+V(x)*|x|^{-\alpha})\Big|^{q^{\prime}}\leq\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha,q^{\prime}}^{(N)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(V(x)_{-}*|x|^{-\alpha}\Big)^{q^{\prime}+3}dx.

(1.11)

Unfortunately, we cannot finger out whether the inequality (
1.9
) or (
1.11
) is superior.

In what follows, we are concerned with the existence and quantitative properties of minimizers for the relativistic Hartree-Fock energy functional.
Instead of studying (
1.3
) directly, we restrict ourself to the case of finite particles for simplicity. We consider the following constrained minimizing problem

E_{K}(N)=\inf\Big\{\mathcal{E}_{K}(\gamma)\big|\gamma\in\Gamma\Big\},

(1.12)

where the constrained manifold
\Gamma
is defined as

\Gamma:=\Big\{\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|u_{i}\rangle\langle u_{i}|,\ \forall u_{i}\in\mathcal{H},\ (u_{i},u_{j})_{L^{2}}=\delta_{ij},\ \forall 1\leq i,j\leq r,0<{\rm rank~}\gamma=r\leq N\Big\}.

Here to ensure all terms in (
1.2
) make sense for

\mathcal{H}:=\Big\{u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)|u|^{2}dx<\infty\Big\}.

Before stating our main results, we first define ground states of a Fermi system with potentials according to the Aufbau principle.

Definition 1.1
.

(Ground state) In a
L
-Fermi system,
(u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{L})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{C}^{L})
is called a ground state of the the following system, if
(u_{i},u_{j})_{L^{2}}=\delta_{ij}
for all
i,j=1,2,\cdots,L
and

H_{V}u_{i}:=\Big(\sqrt{-\Delta+m^{2}}+V(x)-2K\big(\sum_{k=1}^{L}u_{k}^{2}\big)*|x|^{-1}\Big)u_{i}=\mu_{i}u_{i},\ i=1,2,\cdots,L,

(1.13)

where
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leq...\leq\mu_{L}
are the first-
L
eigenvalues of the operator
H_{V}
.

We now state our results on the minimization problem (
1.12
), and show that there exists a threshold for the existence of minimizers, which is indeed the ground states of the system (
1.13
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
be fixed and
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{(N)}:=\mathcal{K}_{1,\infty}^{(N)}
be the best constant given by (
1.4
). Assume that
V(x)
is a trapping potential satisfying

0\leq V(x)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=\infty\ \text{ and }\ \inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)=0.

(1.14)

Then

(i)

For
K\in\big(0,\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{(N)}\big)
, problem (
1.12
) has at least one minimizer
\gamma_{K}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|u_{i}\rangle\langle u_{i}|
, where
(u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{r})
is a ground state of
r
-Fermi system (
1.13
).

(ii)

When
K
is close to
0^{+}
, then (
1.12
) has a unique minimizer
\gamma_{K}
and
{\rm rank~}\gamma_{K}=1
; when

is close to
(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{(N)})^{-}
, then any minimizer

for (
1.12
) satisfies

.

(iii)

For

Based on the existence and non-existence results in Theorem

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that

satisfies (
1.14

\Lambda:=\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:V(x)=0\big\}.

(1.15)

Let

\gamma_{k}:=\gamma_{K_{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|u_{i}^{k}\rangle\langle u_{i}^{k}|
be a minimizer of (
1.12
) for each
K_{k}\nearrow\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{(N)}
, as

, where
[\frac{N}{2}]+1\leq r\leq N
is an integer. Then,

\varepsilon_{k}:=[{\rm Tr}(\sqrt{-\Delta}\gamma_{k})]^{-1}\to 0^{+}\ \text{ as }K_{k}\nearrow\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{(N)}

II. Explicit expression of optimizers.
In this part, we will reveal the explicit expression of the optimizer
\gamma

\gamma

\text{Tr}(\sqrt{-\Delta}\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-\alpha})\rho_{\gamma}dx=1\ \text{ and }\ \|\gamma\|_{\mathcal{S}^{q}}=(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)})^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}.

(2.26)

The proof is divided into two cases: case (A):

and

, and case (B):

Case (A)

1\leq q<\frac{2-\alpha}{(1-\alpha)_{+}}

Choosing a smooth curve of operators

0\leq\gamma^{*}(t)=\gamma(t)=\gamma+t\delta+o(t),\quad{\rm rank~}\gamma(t)\leq N

and substituting

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)})^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\big[\text{Tr}(\gamma^{q}(t))\big]^{\frac{2-\alpha}{q}}[{\rm Tr}(\sqrt{-\Delta}\gamma(t))]^{\alpha}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(\rho_{\gamma(t)}*|x|^{-\alpha}\big)\rho_{\gamma(t)}dx}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\big[{\rm Tr}(\gamma^{q})+qt\text{Tr}(\delta\gamma^{q-1})+o(t)\big]^{\frac{2-\alpha}{q}}[\text{Tr}(\sqrt{-\Delta}\gamma)+t\text{Tr}(\sqrt{-\Delta}\delta)+o(t)]^{\alpha}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[\rho_{\gamma}+t\rho_{\delta}+o(t)](x)[\rho_{\gamma}+t\rho_{\delta}+o(t)](y)|x-y|^{-\alpha}dxdy}

\displaystyle=[{\rm Tr}(\gamma^{q})]^{\frac{2-\alpha}{q}}\frac{[1+qt\frac{\text{Tr}(\delta\gamma^{q-1})}{{\rm Tr}(\gamma^{q})}+o(t)]^{\frac{2-\alpha}{q}}[1+t{\rm Tr}(\sqrt{-\Delta}\delta)+o(t)]^{\alpha}}{1+2t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\gamma}(x)\rho_{\delta}(y)|x-y|^{-\alpha}dxdy+o(t)}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,q}^{(N)})^{\alpha}\Big[1+t{\rm Tr}\Big(\delta\big[\alpha\sqrt{-\Delta}-2\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-\alpha}+(2-\alpha)\frac{\gamma^{q-1}}{\text{Tr}(\gamma^{q})}\big]\Big)+o(t)\Big],

and here

in integral is a function with respect to

or

1\leq 1+t\text{Tr}\Big[\delta\Big(\alpha\sqrt{-\Delta}-2\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-\alpha}+(2-\alpha)\frac{\gamma^{q-1}}{\text{Tr}(\gamma^{q})}\Big)\Big]+o(t),\quad\text{for $|t|$ small\ enough}.

, for some self-adjoint (smooth and finite rank) operator

,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\Big([H,\gamma]\Big(\alpha\sqrt{-\Delta}-2\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-\alpha}+(2-\alpha)\frac{\gamma^{q-1}}{\text{Tr}(\gamma^{q})}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}\Big(H\Big[\gamma,\alpha\sqrt{-\Delta}-2\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-\alpha}+(2-\alpha)\frac{\gamma^{q-1}}{\text{Tr}(\gamma^{q})}\Big]\Big),

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is Lie bracket and
[a,b]=ab-ba
. It is apparent from the arbitrariness of
H
that

\Big[\gamma,\alpha\sqrt{-\Delta}-2\rho_{\gamma}*|x|^{-\alpha}+(2-\alpha)\frac{\gamma^{q-1}}{\text{Tr}(\gamma^{q})}\Big]=0.

Therefore, we obtain the explicit expression of
\gamma
, which formed by some eigenfunctions of

assuming

R

It follows from selecting
\delta

\mu_{n_{i}}=-\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}\frac{k_{i}^{q-1}}{{\rm Tr}(\gamma^{q})}<0\text{\ and thus \ }k_{i}=\big[\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}{\rm Tr}\big(\gamma^{q})\big]^{\frac{1}{q-1}}|\mu_{n_{i}}|^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

Then plugging the expression of

(2.29)

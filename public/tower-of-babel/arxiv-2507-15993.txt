On prime coprime graphs of certain finite groups
Ravi Ranjan, Shubh N. Singh

Abstract
. The prime coprime graph Θ ⁡ ( G ) \Theta(G) of a finite group G G is the graph whose vertex set is G G and any two distinct vertices are adjacent if the greatest common divisor of their orders is either 1 1 or a prime. In this paper, we investigate Hamiltonicity, clique number, and vertex degree of Θ ⁡ ( G ) \Theta(G) for cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic groups G G . We establish that Θ ⁡ ( G ) \Theta(G) admits a ( k , 1 ) (k,1) -partition for cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic groups G G of specified orders.

1.
Introduction

The study of graphs associated with groups has garnered significant interest in the last two decades; see
[
4
]
. Adhikari and Banerjee
[
1
]
recently introduced the
prime coprime graph

\Theta(G)
of a finite group
G
as the graph whose vertex set is
G
and any two distinct vertices are adjacent if and only if the greatest common divisor (gcd) of their orders is either
1
or a prime. This graph is alternatively known as the
co-prime order graph
in some literature; see
[
8
,
10
]
. The
coprime graph
of a finite group
G
, introduced in
[
15
]
as the
order prime graph
, naturally forms a spanning subgraph of
\Theta(G)
.

Adhikari and Banerjee
[
1
]
characterized the completeness and Eulerian properties of
\Theta(G)
for all finite groups
G
. Furthermore, they determined the domination number, diameter, and girth of
\Theta(G)
for all finite groups
G
. In
[
1
]
, Hamiltonicity, planarity, vertex connectivity, and signless Laplacian spectra of
\Theta(G)
were also investigated for specific finite groups
G
. Hao et al.
[
8
]
classified all finite groups
G
whose
\Theta(G)
are planar. Furthermore, the vertex connectivity of
\Theta(G)

was determined for finite cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic groups
G

[
8
]
. For a fixed integer
k\geq 1
, Li et al.
[
10
]
proved that there are finitely many finite groups
G
whose
\Theta(G)
have (non)orientable genus
k
. Many other interesting results on prime coprime graphs have been obtained in the literature; see
[
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
]
.

The subsequent sections of this paper are organized as follows. In Section 2, we establish some notation and define key terminology related to graphs and groups.
In Section 3, We establish that
\Theta(G)
admits a
(k,1)
-partition for cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic groups
G
of specified orders. In Section 4, we investigate Hamiltonicity of
\Theta(G)
for every finite cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic group
G
. In Sections 5 and 6, we determine the clique number and vertex degree of
\Theta(G)
, respectively, for groups
G
belonging to the cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic classes.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

Let

denote the set of all positive integers. Unless stated otherwise, assume

, and let

All graphs considered in this paper are finite, undirected, and simple. We denote by

, and the minimum degree of

is denoted by

. The number of connected components of
\Gamma
is denoted by

. For a nonempty subset

, the subgraph of

induced by

is denoted by

. The clique number and vertex connectivity of

and

. Let

All groups considered in this paper are finite. The cyclic group of order

is denoted by
\Z_{n}=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}

. We use

to denote that

. Let

[

Let

.

[

3.
(k,1)
-graphs

For any two nonnegative integers
k,\ell
with
k+\ell>0
, recall that a graph is a
(k,\ell)
-graph
if its vertex set admits a partition into
k
independent sets and
\ell
cliques; see
[
2
, p. 48]
. Such a partition is called a
(k,\ell)
-partition
of the graph. In particular, a
(1,1)
-graph is known as a
split graph
; see
[
6
, p. 667]
.

This section comprises three subsections, each proving that the graph
\Theta(G)
admits a
(k,1)
-partition for specific cyclic, dihedral, and dicyclic groups
G
. Before that, we recall the definition of
H
-join of vertex-disjoint graphs, as delineated in
[
5
]
. Let
H
be a graph with
V(H)=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, where
k\geq 2
, and let
\{\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\ldots,\Gamma_{k}\}
be a family of pairwise vertex-disjoint graphs. The
H
-join
of
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\ldots,\Gamma_{k}
, denoted by
H[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\ldots,\Gamma_{k}]
, is the graph whose vertex and edge sets are

\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(\Gamma_{i})\quad\text{ and }\quad\Big(\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}E(\Gamma_{i})\Big)\cup\Big(\displaystyle\bigcup_{ij\in E(H)}\{uv\colon u\in V(\Gamma_{i}),v\in V(\Gamma_{j})\}\Big),

respectively. It is clear that
\Gamma_{1}\vee\Gamma_{2}=P_{2}[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}]
.

By Theorem
2.3
, the graph
\Theta(G)
admits a
(0,1)
-partition for every group
G
of prime order.

In the following proposition, we establish that the graph
\Theta(G)
of any group
G
of order
pq
admits either a
(1,1)
-partition or a
(0,1)
-partition depending on
G
is cyclic or noncyclic, respectively.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
G
is a group of order
pq
, then

\Theta(G)\cong\begin{cases}K_{p+q-1}\vee E_{(p-1)(q-1)}&\text{if $G$ is cyclic},\\
K_{pq}&\text{if $G$ is noncyclic}.\end{cases}

Proof
.

Assume that
G
is a group of order
pq
. If
G
is noncyclic, then
G
is an EPO-group. Therefore
\Theta(G)\cong K_{pq}
by Theorem
2.4
. So, assume further that
G
is cyclic. By combining Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we obtain
|S(G)|=p+q-1
and
|T(G)|=(p-1)(q-1)
. Now observe that the gcd of the orders of any two distinct elements in
T(G)
is a composite number, and so
\Theta(G)[T(G)]\cong E_{(p-1)(q-1)}
. By Theorem
2.3
, we thus obtain
\Theta(G)[S(G)]\cong K_{p+q-1}
and
\Theta(G)[S(G)]\sim\Theta(G)[T(G)]
. Hence
\Theta(G)\cong K_{p+q-1}\vee E_{(p-1)(q-1)}
.
∎

3.2.

D_{n}=\langle a,b\colon a^{n}=b^{2}=1,\ b^{-1}ab=a^{-1}\rangle,

.
∎

4.
Hamiltonian Graphs

For distinct primes
p,q
with
p<q
, Adhikari and Banerjee
[
1
, Theorem 3.10]
proved that
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{pq})
is Hamiltonian if and only if
p=2
. Motivated by this fact, we determine when the graph
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is Hamiltonian. Furthermore, we determine when each of the graphs
\Theta(D_{n})
and
\Theta(Q_{n})
is Hamiltonian. We begin by recalling two fundamental properties of Hamiltonian graphs.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
17
, Proposition 7.2.3]

If
\Gamma
is Hamiltonian graph, then
c(\Gamma-S)\leq|S|
for all nonempty proper subsets
S
of
V(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
17
, Theorem 7.2.8]

If
\Gamma
is a graph of order
n\geq 3
with minimum degree
\delta(\Gamma)\geq n/2
, then
\Gamma
is Hamiltonian.

The following theorem characterizes when the graph
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is Hamiltonian.

Theorem 4.3
.

The graph
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is Hamiltonian if and only if
n\in\{4,p,2p\}
, where
p
is an odd prime.

Proof
.

Assume that
n\in\{4,p,2p\}
, where
p
is an odd prime. If
n=4
, then
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\cong K_{2}\vee E_{2}
. Therefore
\delta\big(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\big)=n/2
, and hence
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is Hamiltonian by Theorem
4.2
. If
n=p
, then
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\cong K_{n}
by Theorem
2.4
. Since every complete graph with at least three vertices is Hamiltonian, it follows that
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is Hamiltonian. If
n=2p
, then
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\cong K_{p+1}\vee E_{p-1}
by Proposition
3.1
. Therefore
\delta\big(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\big)=p+1>n/2
, and hence
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is Hamiltonian by Theorem
4.2
.

To prove the converse, we will show that its contrapositive holds true. Assume that
n\notin\{4,p,2p\}
, where
p
is an odd prime. The graph
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is non-Hamiltonian for
n=1
and
n=2
. If
n=12
, then
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\cong H[K_{4},E_{2},E_{2},E_{4}]
by Proposition
3.3
, where
H
is given in Figure
1
. Since
c\big(H[K_{4},E_{2},E_{2},E_{4}]-V(K_{4})\big)>|V(K_{4})|
, it follows from Theorem
4.2
that
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is non-Hamiltonian. Next if

n=30
, then
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\cong H[K_{8},E_{2},E_{8},E_{4},E_{8}]
by Proposition
3.5
, where
H
is given in Figure
3
. Since
c\big(H[K_{8},E_{2},E_{8},E_{4},E_{8}]-V(K_{8})\big)>|V(K_{8})|
, it follows from Theorem
4.2
that

\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is non-Hamiltonian.

So, assume further that
n
is distinct from
12
and
30
. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}
be all distinct prime factors of
n
, and let
S_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{g\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\colon|g|=n\}
. By combining Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we obtain
|S(\Z_{n})|=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(p_{i})
and
|S_{n}|=\varphi(n)
. Moreover, observe that
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{n})[S_{n}]\cong E_{\varphi(n)}
and the subgraph
\Theta(\Z_{n})-S(\Z_{n})
is independent of the subgraph
\Theta(\Z_{n})[S_{n}]
. This implies that
c\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})-S(\Z_{n})\big)\geq\varphi(n)
. By combining Propositions 5 and 6 from
[
11
]
, we have
\varphi(n)>1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(p_{i})
. Therefore, since
|S(\Z_{n})|=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(p_{i})
, we obtain
c\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})-S(\Z_{n})\big)>|S(\Z_{n})|
. Hence
\Theta(\Z_{n})
is non-Hamiltonian by Proposition
4.1
. ∎

The theorem below asserts that the graph
\Theta(D_{n})
is Hamiltonian for all
n
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The graph
\Theta(D_{n})
is Hamiltonian.

Proof
.

Note that
D_{n}
contains at least
n
involutions, and so
|S(D_{n})|\geq n+1
. Therefore, by Theorem
2.3
we obtain
\delta\big(\Theta(D_{n})\big)\geq n+1
. Hence, since
|D_{n}|=2n
, the graph
\Theta(D_{n})
is Hamiltonian by Theorem
4.2
.
∎

The theorem below characterizes when the graph
\Theta(Q_{n})
is Hamiltonian.

Theorem 4.5
.

The graph
\Theta(Q_{n})
is Hamiltonian if and only if
n
is odd.

Proof
.

Assume that

is odd. Note that
\langle a\rangle
is a cyclic subgroup of order
2n
in
Q_{n}
. Therefore
\Theta(Q_{n})[\langle a\rangle]\cong\Theta(\Z_{2n})
.
Furthermore, since
|Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle|=2n
and each element in
Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle
has order
4
, we obtain
\Theta(Q_{n})[Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle]\cong E_{2n}
. Since
n
is odd, we obtain
\gcd(4,d)\in\{1,2\}
for each positive divisor
d
of
2n
. Therefore
\Theta(Q_{n})[Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle]\sim\Theta(Q_{n})[\langle a\rangle]
, and so
\Theta(Q_{n})\cong\Theta(\Z_{2n})\vee E_{2n}
. This implies that
\delta\big(\Theta(Q_{n})\big)=2n
. Hence, since
|Q_{n}|=4n
, the graph
\Theta(Q_{n})
is Hamiltonian by Theorem
4.2
.

To prove the converse part, we will show that its contrapositive holds true. Assume that
n
is even. If
n=2
, then
\Theta(Q_{2})\cong K_{2}\vee E_{6}
by Proposition
3.15
. Since

c\big((K_{2}\vee E_{6})-V(K_{2})\big)=6>|V(K_{2})|
, it follows from Proposition
4.1
that
\Theta(Q_{n})
is non-Hamiltonian. Next if
n=6
, then
\Theta(Q_{6})\cong H[K_{4},E_{2},E_{4},E_{14}]
by Proposition
3.13
, where
H
is given in Figure
4
. Since

c\big(H[K_{4},E_{2},E_{4},E_{14}]-V(K_{4})\big)=5>|V(K_{4})|
, it follows from Proposition
4.1
that
\Theta(Q_{n})
is non-Hamiltonian.

So, assume further that
n
is distinct from
2
and
6
. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}
be all distinct prime factors of
n
, and let
S_{2n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{g\in Q_{n}\colon|g|=2n\}
. By combining Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we obtain
|S(Q_{n})|=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(p_{i})
and
|S_{2n}|=\varphi(2n)
. Furthermore, observe that
\Theta(Q_{n})[S_{2n}]\cong E_{\varphi(2n)}
and the subgraph
\Theta(Q_{n})-S(Q_{n})
is independent of the subgraph
\Theta(Q_{n})[S_{2n}]
. Therefore
c\big(\Theta(Q_{n})-S(Q_{n})\big)\geq\varphi(2n)
. By
[

, Proposition 6]
, we have
\varphi(2n)>1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(p_{i})
. Thus, since
|S(Q_{n})|=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(p_{i})
, we obtain
c\big(\Theta(Q_{n})-S(Q_{n})\big)>|S(Q_{n})|
. Hence
\Theta(Q_{n})
is non-Hamiltonian by Proposition
4.1
.
∎

5.
Clique Number

In this section, we determine the clique number of each of the graphs
\Theta(\Z_{n})
,
\Theta(D_{n})
, and
\Theta(Q_{n})
. Recall that the
clique number

\omega(\Gamma)
of a graph
\Gamma
is the cardinality of its maximum clique.

In the following theorem, we determines the clique number of the graph
\Theta(\Z_{n})
for all
n>1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
n>1
be an integer with canonical factorization
n=p_{1}^{r_{1}}p_{2}^{r_{2}}\cdots p_{k}^{r_{k}}
. Then

\omega\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})\big)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(p_{i}-1)+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
p_{i}^{2}|n\end{subarray}}^{k}1+\frac{k(k-1)}{2}.

Proof
.

The result follows directly from Theorem
2.4
when
n
is prime. So, assume further that
n
is composite. Let
W
be a maximum clique of
\Theta(\Z_{n})
. By Theorem
2.3
, it follows that
S(\Z_{n})\subseteq W
. For each composite divisor
d
of
n
, let
S_{d}
denote the set of all elements of order
d
in
\Z_{n}
. By Theorem
2.1
, we have
|S_{d}|=\varphi(d)
, and consequently,
\Theta(\Z_{n})[S_{d}]\cong E_{\varphi(d)}
. Therefore
|W\cap S_{d}|\leq 1
. Furthermore, observe that the gcd of any two distinct products, each of them formed by multiplying two primes, is either one or a prime. Therefore, for all distinct indices
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq k
, it follows that
|W\cap S_{p_{i}p_{j}}|=1
, and
|W\cap S_{p_{i}^{2}}|=1
whenever
p_{i}^{2}\mid n
.

We now proceed to show that
W\cap S_{d}=\varnothing
whenever
d
is neither the square of a prime nor the product of two distinct primes. We consider the following three distinct cases:

Case 1:

p_{i}^{3}\mid d
for some prime
p_{i}
. Then
\gcd(d,p_{i}^{2})=p_{i}^{2}
, and consequently no vertex of
\Theta(\Z_{n})[S_{d}]
is adjacent to the vertex in
W
corresponding to an element of order
p_{i}^{2}
. Therefore
W\cap S_{d}=\varnothing
.

Case 2:

p_{i}^{a}p_{j}^{b}\mid d
for some distinct primes
p_{i},p_{j}
, where
a,b\in\N
with
a+b=3
. Then
\gcd(d,p_{i}p_{j})=p_{i}p_{j}
, and consequently no vertex of
\Theta(\Z_{n})[S_{d}]
is adjacent to the vertex in
W
corresponding to an element of order
p_{i}p_{j}
. Therefore
W\cap S_{d}=\varnothing
.

Case 3:

p_{i}p_{j}p_{\ell}\mid d
for some distinct primes
p_{i},p_{j},p_{\ell}
. Then
\gcd(d,p_{i}p_{j})=p_{i}p_{j}
, and consequently no vertex of
\Theta(\Z_{n})[S_{d}]
is adjacent to the vertex in
W
corresponding to an element of order
p_{i}p_{j}
. Therefore
W\cap S_{d}=\varnothing
.

Thus

W=S(\Z_{n})\cup\bigg(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
p_{i}^{2}|n\end{subarray}}^{k}\big(W\cap S_{p_{i}^{2}}\big)\bigg)\cup\bigg(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{l}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{k}\big(W\cap S_{p_{i}p_{j}}\big)\bigg).

Moreover, observe that

\big|\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{l}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{k}\big(W\cap S_{p_{i}p_{j}}\big)\big|=\frac{k(k-1)}{2}
. Also, we obtain
|S(\Z_{n})|=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(p_{i}-1)
by combining Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
. Hence

\omega\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})\big)=|W|=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(p_{i}-1)+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
p_{i}^{2}|n\end{subarray}}^{k}1+\frac{k(k-1)}{2}.

∎

In order to determine the clique number of the graph
\Theta(D_{n})
, we first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have
\Theta(D_{n})\cong\Theta(\Z_{n})\vee K_{n}
.

Proof
.

Note that
\langle a\rangle
is a cyclic subgroup of order
n
in
D_{n}
. Therefore
\Theta(D_{n})[\langle a\rangle]\cong\Theta(\Z_{n})
. Furthermore, since
|D_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle|=n
and each element in
D_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle
has order
2
, we obtain
\Theta(D_{n})[D_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle]\cong K_{n}
and
\Theta(D_{n})[D_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle]\sim\Theta(D_{n})[\langle a\rangle]
. Hence
\Theta(D_{n})\cong\Theta(\Z_{n})\vee K_{n}
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The clique number of the graph
\Theta(D_{n})
is given by

\omega\big(\Theta(D_{n})\big)=n+\omega\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})\big).

Proof
.

By Lemma
5.2
, the graph
\Theta(D_{n})
is isomorphic to
\Theta(\Z_{n})\vee K_{n}
. Therefore

\omega\big(\Theta(D_{n})\big)=\omega\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})\vee K_{n}\big)=\omega\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})\big)+\omega(K_{n})=\omega\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})\big)+n.

∎

The following proposition expresses the clique number of the graph
\Theta(Q_{n})
in terms of the clique number of
\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})
.

Proposition 5.4
.

The clique number of the graph
\Theta(Q_{n})
is given by

\omega\bigl(\Theta(Q_{n})\bigr)=\begin{cases}\omega\bigl(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})\bigr)&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\
1+\omega\bigl(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})\bigr)&\text{if }n\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof
.

Note that
\langle a\rangle
is a cyclic subgroup of order
2n
in
Q_{n}
. Therefore
\Theta(Q_{n})[\langle a\rangle]\cong\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})
, and so
\omega\big(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})\big)\leq\omega\big(\Theta(Q_{n})\big)
. Furthermore, since
|Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle|=2n
and each element in
Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle
has order
4
, we obtain
\Theta(Q_{n})[Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle]\cong E_{2n}
. We now proceed by considering two distinct cases:

Case 1:

n
is odd. Then for each positive divisor
d
of
2n
, we have
\gcd(4,d)\in\{1,2\}
. Since each element in
Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle
has order
4
, it follows that
\Theta(Q_{n})[\langle a\rangle]\sim\Theta(Q_{n})[Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle]
. Therefore
\Theta(Q_{n})\cong\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})\vee E_{2n}
. Hence

\omega\big(\Theta(Q_{n})\big)=\omega\big(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})\big)+\omega(E_{2n})=\omega\big(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})\big)+1
.

Case 2:

n
is even. Then
4\mid 2n
, and so
\langle a\rangle
contains
\varphi(4)=2
elements of order
4
by Theorem
2.1
. Let
W
be a maximum clique of the subgraph
\Theta(Q_{n})[\langle a\rangle]
. By Theorem
5.1
, the clique
W
contains exactly one vertex
v
corresponding to an element of order
4
in
\langle a\rangle
. Furthermore, since each element in
Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle
has order
4
, the vertex
v
is non-adjacent to every vertex of the subgraph
\Theta(Q_{n})[Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle]
. Therefore
W
is a maximum clique of
\Theta(Q_{n})
. Hence
\omega\bigl(\Theta(Q_{n})\bigr)=|W|=\omega\bigl(\Theta(Q_{n})[\langle a\rangle]\bigr)=\omega\bigl(\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})\bigr)
.
∎

6.
Vertex Degree

By Theorem
2.3
, every element
g
of the subset
S(G)
of a group
G
has degree
\deg_{\Theta(G)}(x)=|G|-1
in the graph
\Theta(G)
.

In this section, we determine the degrees of
composite-order vertices
of each of the graphs
\Theta(\Z_{n})
,
\Theta(D_{n})
, and
\Theta(Q_{n})
, where a
composite-order vertex
of the graph
\Theta(G)
is one that represents
an element of composite order in the group
G
.

In order to determine the degree of any vertex of the graph
\Theta(\Z_{n})
, we require the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
n>1
be an integer with canonical factorization
n=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
1\leq i\leq k\end{subarray}}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{k}(p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}-1)^{a_{i}}\bigg),

where
d
runs through all positive divisors of
n
.

Proof
.

Note that each positive divisor
d
of
n
admits a representation of the form

d=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{a_{i}\beta_{i}}
, where
a_{i}\in\{0,1\}
and
1\leq\beta_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
(cf.
[
3
, Theorem 6.1]
). By Theorem
2.2
, we therefore obtain

\varphi(d)=\varphi\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{a_{i}\beta_{i}}\bigg)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\varphi\Big(p_{i}^{a_{i}\beta_{i}}\Big)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\varphi\big(p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\big)\Big)^{a_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Big(p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}-p_{i}^{\beta_{i}-1}\Big)^{a_{i}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
1\leq i\leq k\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\beta_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}\\
1\leq i\leq k\end{subarray}}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\big(p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}-p_{i}^{\beta_{i}-1}\big)^{a_{i}}\bigg)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
1\leq i\leq k\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\bigg(\sum_{1\leq\beta_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}}\big(p_{i}^{\beta_{i}}-p_{i}^{\beta_{i}-1}\big)^{a_{i}}\bigg)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
1\leq i\leq k\end{subarray}}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Big(p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}-1\Big)^{a_{i}}\bigg).

∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
n
be a composite number with canonical factorization
n=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
, and let
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}

with composite order
\prod_{i=1}^{k}{p_{i}}^{\beta_{i}}
. Then

\deg_{\Theta(\Z_{n})}(x)=\sum_{\Delta}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(p_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}-1)^{a_{i}},\vskip-11.38109pt

where
\gamma_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\beta_{i}\geq 2,\\
\alpha_{i}&\text{if }\beta_{i}\leq 1,\end{cases}

and
\Delta
ranges over all choices of
a_{i}\in\{0,1\}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
\beta_{j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j}\leq 1
.

Proof
.

Clearly
0\leq\beta_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
. Observe for each vertex
y
of
\Theta(\Z_{n})
that
xy\in E\big(\Theta(\Z_{n})\big)
if and only if the orders
|x|
and
|y|
share at most one prime factor with exponent 1. Therefore, the vertex
x
is adjacent to each vertex of
\Theta(\Z_{n})
whose order divides
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{a_{i}\gamma_{i}}
, where

\gamma_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\beta_{i}\geq 2,\\
\alpha_{i}&\text{if }\beta_{i}\leq 1,\end{cases}
and
a_{i}\in\{0,1\}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
\beta_{j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j}\leq 1
. By Theorem
2.1
, the group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
contains
\varphi(d^{\prime})
elements of order
d^{\prime}
, for each positive divisor
d^{\prime}
of
n
. Therefore
\deg_{\Theta(\Z_{n})}(x)=\sum_{d^{\prime}\mid d}\varphi(d^{\prime})
. Hence, by Lemma
6.1
we obtain

\deg_{\Theta(\Z_{n})}(x)=\sum_{\Delta}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(p_{i}^{\gamma_{i}}-1)^{a_{i}},\vskip-11.38109pt

where
\gamma_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\beta_{i}\geq 2,\\
\alpha_{i}&\text{if }\beta_{i}\leq 1,\end{cases}

and
\Delta
ranges over all choices of
a_{i}\in\{0,1\}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq k\\
\beta_{j}\geq 1\end{subarray}}a_{j}\leq 1
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

If
x
is an element of composite order in the group
D_{n}
, then

\deg_{\Theta(D_{n})}(x)=n+\deg_{\Theta(\Z_{n})}(x)
.

Proof
.

It follows directly from Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
x
be an element of composite order
d
in the group
Q_{n}
. Then

\deg_{\Theta(Q_{n})}(x)=\begin{cases}2n+\deg_{\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})}(x)&\text{if }4\nmid d,\\
\deg_{\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})}(x)&\text{if }4\mid d.\end{cases}

Proof
.

Note that
\langle a\rangle
is a cyclic subgroup of order
2n
in
Q_{n}
. Therefore
\Theta(Q_{n})[\langle a\rangle]\cong\Theta(\Z_{2n})
.
Furthermore, since
|Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle|=2n
and each element of
Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle
has order
4
, we obtain
\Theta(Q_{n})[Q_{n}\setminus\langle a\rangle]\cong E_{2n}
. If
4\mid d
, then
\gcd(4,d)=4
. Therefore, the vertex
x
is non-adjacent to all vertices of
\Theta(Q_{n})
corresponding to elements of order

in

. Hence
\deg_{\Theta(Q_{n})}(x)=\deg_{\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})}(x)
. So, assume that

. Then
\gcd(4,d)\in\{1,2\}
. Therefore, the vertex

is adjacent to all vertices of
\Theta(Q_{n})
corresponding to elements of order

in

. Hence
\deg_{\Theta(Q_{n})}(x)=2n+\deg_{\Theta(\mathbb{Z}_{2n})}(x)
. ∎

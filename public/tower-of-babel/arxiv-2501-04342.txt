Abstract
. For (potentially infinite) matroids M M and N N , an ( M , N ) (M,N) -hindrance is a set H H that is independent but not spanning in N . 𝗌𝗉𝖺𝗇 M ​ ( H ) N.\mathsf{span}_{M}(H) . This concept was introduced by Aharoni and Ziv in the very first paper investigating Nash-Williams’ Matroid Intersection Conjecture. They proved that the conjecture is equivalent to the statement that the non-existence of hindrances implies the existence of an M M -independent spanning set of N N . In this paper we present a breakthrough towards the Matroid Intersection Conjecture. Namely, we found a matroidal generalization of the ‘popular vertex’ approach applied in the proof of the infinite version of König’s theorem. The main result of this paper is an application of this new approach to show that if M M and N N admit a common independent set I I that is “wasteful” in the sense that r ⁡ ( M / I ) < r ⁡ ( N / I ) r(M/I)<r(N/I) , then there exists an ( M , N ) (M,N) -hindrance.

The infinite version of König’s Duality Theorem

: If

In the proof of the infinite version of König’s Duality Theorem, the starting point is an asymmetric reformulation. A bipartite

[

context of the Matroid Intersection Conjecture as

which is independent but not
spanning in
N.\mathsf{span}_{M}(H)
.
The following statement is known to be equivalent to the Matroid Intersection Conjecture and it is usually the preferred angle
to approach it:

(
[

A key ingredient of Aharoni’s proof of König’s Duality Theorem for uncountable graphs is the concept of ‘popular vertices’.
Roughly speaking, a
vertex
v
is popular with respect to a matching if there are “a lot of” augmenting paths that terminate at
v
and are disjoint apart from
v
.
The
simplest example where the ‘popular vertex’ method can be applied is the proof that
G=(S,T,E)
is hindered under the assumption that there is a
matching
F
that is ‘‘wasteful’’
2
2

2

Wasteful from the perspective of searching for a matching that covers
T
.
in the sense that more
vertices in
T
are
uncovered by
F
than in
S
. We gave a direct proof for this in

[
4
]
in a
more general setting.

After
the Matroid Intersection
Conjecture was settled for countable matroids in
[
5
]
, we were searching for an adequate matroidal
generalization of Aharoni’s
‘popular
vertex’ technique. These efforts were eventually successful, and the resulting methods are explained in this paper by proving
the matroidal generalization of creating hindrances from wasteful matchings:

Theorem 1.2
.

If
M
and
N
are finitary matroids on a common ground set and there exists an
I\in\mathcal{I}(M)\cap\mathcal{I}(N)
with
r(M/I)<r(N/I)
, then
(M,N)
is hindered.

The setting of König’s Duality Theorem
corresponds to the special case of the Matroid Intersection
Conjecture where in each matroid every circuit has size two. The main difficulty is when we can have arbitrarily large circuits
that we might need multiple edges to span an edge and hence a single augmenting path is insufficient. Instead of popular vertices (which are
meaningless in the matroidal context for obvious reasons) we define something that we call the ‘popularity matroid’, which serves as a key concept
in the proofs.

The paper is organized as follows. In the following section we introduce our notation and provide a survey of the set-theoretic and matroidal
facts we need later. Then, in Section
3

1.2
, is
proved in Section
4
.

Lemma 3.1

Suppose that

Proof.

The existence of a circuit

is as well.

and hence it is the fundamental circuit of

on
I\setminus\{f\}\cup\{e_{1}\}
.

It remains to show

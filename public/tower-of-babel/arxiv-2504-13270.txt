Diameter and focal radius of submanifolds
Ricardo A. E. Mendes

Abstract
. In this note, we give a characterization of immersed submanifolds of simply-connected space forms for which the quotient of the extrinsic diameter by the focal radius achieves the minimum possible value of 2 2 . They are essentially round spheres, or the “Veronese” embeddings of projective spaces. The proof combines the classification of submanifolds with planar geodesics due to K. Sakamoto with a version of A. Schur’s Bow Lemma for space curves. Open problems and the relation to recent work by M. Gromov and A. Petrunin are discussed.

1.
Introduction

Denote by
M^{n}(\kappa)
the complete simply-connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant curvature
\kappa
. For simplicity we will assume
\kappa=0,1,-1
, so that
M^{n}(\kappa)
is the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the unit sphere
S^{n}
, or the hyperbolic
n
-space, respectively.

Let
M
be a closed (that is, compact without boundary), smooth, connected manifold, immersed in
M^{n}(\kappa)
. The
extrinsic diameter
of
M
is defined as the maximum distance, measured in
M^{n}(\kappa)
, of any pair
x,y\in M
. The
focal radius
of
M
is defined as the supremum of
r>0
such that the normal exponential map
\nu^{r}(M)\to M^{n}(\kappa)
is a local diffeomorphism when restricted to the set
\nu^{r}(M)
of normal vectors of length less than
r
.

The goal of this note is to prove the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a closed, smooth, connected, immersed submanifold of
M^{n}(\kappa)
. Then the extrinsic diameter of
M
is at least
2
times its focal radius.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if:
M
is a round sphere, and the immersion is a totally umbilical embedding; or
M
is a (real, complex, quaternionic, or octonionic) projective space, and the immersion is a “Veronese” embedding; or
M
is a round sphere, and the immersion is the composition of the covering map to the round real projective space with a Veronese embedding.

We remind the reader that totally umbilical spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are distance spheres in affine subspaces. In
S^{n}
they are intersections of
S^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and similarly for hyperbolic space, via its natural embedding into Minkowski space as a hyperboloid, see
[
Sak77
, page 28]
for more details. Veronese embeddings of (real, complex, quaternionic, or octonionic) projective spaces into spheres are defined using certain spaces of matrices. More generally, one obtains embeddings into
M^{n}(\kappa)
by composing these with totally umbilical embeddings of spheres into
M^{n}(\kappa)
, see Section
2.3
for more details.

Theorem
A
is related to some recent results in
[
Gro22b
,
Gro22a
,
Pet24a
,
Pet24b
]
. See Section
4
for more details, and a discussion of open problems.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Bow Lemma

For a unit-speed smooth curve
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M^{n}(\kappa)
(or any Riemannian manifold), the
(geodesic) curvature
at
\gamma(t)
is defined as
\|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)\|
.

A (geodesic) circle, that is, the set of points in
M^{2}(\kappa)
at a certain constant distance from a point, is a curve of constant geodesic curvature. Composing such a curve with a totally geodesic embedding of
M^{2}(\kappa)
in
M^{n}(\kappa)
yields what we will refer to as
planar circles
.

We will need the following consequence of
[
AB96
, Theorem 1.1]
:

Lemma 1
.

Let
\eta\colon[0,\ell/2]\to M^{2}(\kappa)
be a constant-curvature unit-speed parametrization of a half-circle of radius
r
. Let
\gamma\colon[0,\ell/2]\to M^{n}(\kappa)
be a smooth unit-speed curve with geodesic curvature at most that of
\eta
. Then the chord length of
\gamma
is no smaller than the chord length of
\eta
, that is:

d_{M^{n}(\kappa)}(\gamma(0),\gamma(\ell/2))\geq 2r.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
\gamma
is contained in a planar circle.

Proof.

Let
c
denote the curvature of
\eta
. In the case
\kappa=1
, note that
\ell/2=\pi\sin(r)
, so that the length of
\gamma
plus its chord length is at most
2\pi
, with equality only when
\gamma
is a geodesic between a pair of antipodal points in
M^{n}(\kappa)=S^{n}
, in which case the conclusions of the Lemma are clearly satisfied. Thus we may assume that the length of
\gamma
plus its chord length is strictly less than
2\pi
. This means that, for any
\kappa
, we can apply
[
AB96
, Theorem 1.1]
, and conclude that there exists a unit-speed curve
\eta^{\prime}\colon[0,\ell/2]\to M^{2}(\kappa)
with constant curvature
c^{\prime}\leq c
, and the same chord length as
\gamma
. By elementary geometry in
M^{2}(\kappa)
, for minor arcs of a fixed length, the chord length is strictly decreasing as a function of the geodesic curvature (see, for example,
[
AB96
, Appendix]
). Thus
d_{M^{n}(\kappa)}(\gamma(0),\gamma(\ell/2))\geq 2r
, and, if equality holds, we must have
c^{\prime}=c
. Thus the last statement follows from the rigidity statement in
[
AB96
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

1

\kappa=0
), this is a classical result due to A. Schur, see
[

, or
[
Hop89
, Theorem A, page 31]

2.2.
Focal radius and normal curvatures

Let
M
be an immersed submanifold of
M^{n}(\kappa)
, let
p\in M
, and
u\in T_{p}M
be a unit vector. The
normal curvature
of
M
at
p
in the direction of
u
is defined as the geodesic curvature at
t=0
of the unit-speed geodesic starting at
p
with initial velocity
u
, or, equivalently,
\|I\!I(u,u)\|
, where
I\!I
denotes the second fundamental form of
M
in
M^{n}(\kappa)
.

Given a unit normal vector
\xi\in\nu_{p}M
, the
focal distances
in the direction of
\xi
are the values of
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that the differential of the normal exponential map at
t\xi
has positive nullity. Equivalently,
t
is a focal distance in the direction of
\xi
if and only if the shape operator
A_{\xi}
has an eigenvalue
\lambda
of the form
\lambda=1/t,\cot(t),\coth(t)
, for
\kappa=0,1,-1
, respectively, see e.g.
[
CR78
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Note that, for fixed
p\in M
, the minimum (positive) focal distance
t_{\min}
over all unit
\xi\in\nu_{p}M
and the maximum normal curvature
c_{\max}
over all unit
u\in T_{p}M
satisfy
c_{\max}=1/t_{\min},\cot(t_{\min}),\coth(t_{\min})
, for
\kappa=0,1,-1
, respectively.

In particular, the
focal radius of
M
, which is equal to the infimum, over all
p\in M
and all unit
\xi\in\nu_{p}M
, of the smallest positive focal distance in the direction of
\xi
, is related by analogous formulas to the supremum, over all
p\in M
and all unit
u\in T_{p}M
, of the normal curvature.

2.3.
Veronese embeddings of projective spaces

We will use notations from
[
Lit76
]
, to which we refer the reader for more details.

Let
\mathbb{F}
denote either the reals
\mathbb{R}
, the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
, the quaternions
\mathbb{H}
, or the octonions
\mathbb{O}
. Let
M^{l+1}(\mathbb{F})
denote the real vector space of all
(l+1)\times(l+1)
-matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}
, where
l\in\mathbb{N}
, except for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{O}
, for which
l\in\{1,2\}
. The space
M^{l+1}(\mathbb{F})
is endowed with the inner product

M_{1}\cdot M_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}\left(M_{1}\overline{M_{2}^{t}}+M_{2}\overline{M_{1}^{t}}\right).

Let

\mathbb{F}P^{l}=\{M\in M^{l+1}(\mathbb{F})\mid M=\overline{M^{t}},\ M=M^{2},\ \operatorname{rank}(M)=1\}.

The subset
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
of the Euclidean vector space
M^{l+1}(\mathbb{F})
is a connected, closed, smooth, embedded submanifold, which is isometric to the corresponding projective space with the (appropriately rescaled) Fubini–Study metric.

Geometrically, the points of
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
are subspaces of
\mathbb{F}^{l+1}
with dimension
1
over
\mathbb{F}
, and they are identified via the Veronese embedding with the corresponding orthogonal projection matrix. The spaces
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
(together with round spheres) also coincide with the class of compact symmetric spaces of rank one.

Note that
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
is contained in the sphere of radius
\sqrt{l/(l+1)}
in the affine subspace of Hermitian matrices with trace
1
, with center
\operatorname{Id}/(l+1)
.

By rescaling, one thus obtains isometric embeddings of (appropriately rescaled)
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
into spheres of any radius. The compositions of these embeddings with isometric embeddings of spheres into
M^{n}(\kappa)
as totally geodesic or totally umbilical submanifolds (see
[
Sak77
, page 28]
for more details) will be referred to as the
Veronese embeddings
of
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
.

Theorem 3
.

[
Sak77
, Theorems 1, 2, and their Corollaries]

Under a given Veronese embedding, all geodesics of
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
are mapped to planar circles of the same length.

3.
Proof of Theorem
A

Let
r
denote the focal radius of
M
. Pick any unit-speed geodesic
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
. Then the geodesic curvature of
\gamma
in
M^{n}(\kappa)
is at
most that of a circle of radius
r
in
M^{2}(\kappa)
(see Section
2.2
). Let
\ell
denote the length of this circle.
It follows from Lemma
1
that the chord length of
\gamma_{[0,\ell/2]}
is at least
2r
. In particular, the extrinsic diameter of
M
is at least
2r
.

Suppose the extrinsic diameter of
M
is equal to
2r
. Then the rigidity statement in Lemma
1
implies that, for every geodesic
\gamma
, the set
\gamma([0,\ell/2])
is contained in a totally geodesic copy of
M^{2}(\kappa)
. This implies
M
has planar geodesics. By
[
Sak77
, Theorem 3]
,
M
is either a totally umbilical sphere, or a Veronese embedding of some
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
, or the composition of the covering map
S^{l}\to\mathbb{R}P^{l}
with a Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{R}P^{l}
.

Conversely, assume
M
is either a totally umbilical sphere, or a Veronese embedding of some
\mathbb{F}P^{l}
. In either case, every geodesic is a planar circle of the same radius
r
(see Theorem
3
). Thus the focal radius of
M
is
r
, and, since every two points of
M
can be joined by a geodesic, the extrinsic diameter of
M
is
2r
.

4.
Remarks and open questions

In this section we consider only the case
\kappa=0
, that is, we consider immersions into Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In view of Theorem
A
, it is natural to ask:

Question 4
.

Fix a connected, closed, smooth manifold
M
. What is the infimum of the quantity (extrinsic diameter)
/
(focal radius) over all smooth immersions of
M
into Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(for all
n
)? Is the infimum achieved? If so, classify the immersions that achieve the infimum.

Note that Theorem
A
completely solves Question
4
when
M
is diffeomorphic to a compact rank-one symmetric space.

Another natural question is obtained by replacing “extrinsic diameter” with “circumradius” in Question
4
. By circumradius we mean the smallest radius of a closed ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that contains the image of
M
under the immersion. After rescaling and translating, one may assume
M
has circumradius
1
and is contained in the closed unit ball
B^{n}
centered at the origin. Noting that the focal radius is the reciprocal of the maximum normal curvature (see Section
2.2
), one arrives at:

Question 5
.

Fix a connected, closed, smooth manifold
M
. What is the infimum of the maximum normal curvature over all smooth immersions of
M
into
B^{n}
(for all
n
)? Is the infimum achieved? If so, classify the immersions that achieve the infimum.

We first note that any immersion of any closed manifold
M
into
B^{n}
must have maximum normal curvature at least
1
. Moreover, if equality holds, then
M
must be diffeomorphic to a sphere, embedded as a totally geodesic subsphere of
\partial B^{n}=S^{n-1}
. Indeed, using the Bow Lemma, all geodesics of
M
must be mapped to great circles in
S^{n-1}
.

Question
5
has received much attention recently. In particular:

•

[
Gro22b
, 1.1.C]
For every
M
, the infimum in Question
5
is strictly less than
\sqrt{3}
.

•

[
Gro22a
,
Pet24a
]
For
M
diffeomorphic to the torus
T^{m}
, the infimum in Question
5
is
\sqrt{3m/(m+2)}
, and it is achieved.

•

[
Pet24b
]
For
M
diffeomorphic to some projective plane
\mathbb{F}P^{2}
, the infimum in Question
5
is
2/\sqrt{3}
, achieved precisely by the Veronese embeddings.

By Jung’s Theorem, for any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
one has:

\sqrt{2}\text{ circumradius}<\text{extrinsic diameter}\leq 2\text{ circumradius}.

Together with the results mentioned above, one obtains:

•

For every
M
, the infimum in Question
4
is strictly less than
2\sqrt{3}
.

•

For
M
diffeomorphic to the torus
T^{m}
, the infimum in Question
4
is between
\sqrt{2}\sqrt{3m/(m+2)}
and
2\sqrt{3m/(m+2)}
. Note that, for
m\geq 5
, the lower bound is strictly larger than
2
.

The upper bound for the torus obtained above is not sharp:

Example 6
.

Define
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{6}
by
\phi(x,y,z)=

\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(\cos(\sqrt{3}x),\sin(\sqrt{3}x),\cos(\sqrt{3}y),\sin(\sqrt{3}y),\cos(\sqrt{3}z),\sin(\sqrt{3}z)\right)

Note that the image of
\phi
is the Clifford torus contained in the unit sphere
S^{5}
. Let
X
be the plane in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined by the equation
x+y+z=0
. The restriction of
\phi
to
X
induces an isometric immersion of a flat
2
-torus into
\mathbb{R}^{6}
, all of whose normal curvatures are
\sqrt{3m/(m+2)}=\sqrt{3/2}
, see
[
Gro22a
, page 13]
. Solving a simple optimization problem yields that the extrinsic diameter of this
2
-torus is
\sqrt{3}
.

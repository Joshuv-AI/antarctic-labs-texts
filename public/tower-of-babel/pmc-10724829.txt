Pelvic intravenous leiomyomatosis
Authors: Chih-Yu Chou, Chi-Hau Chen
Published: 2023

A 42-year-old woman, gravida 2, parous 2, presented with a 12-month history of abdominal distention but no history of bowel habit change or abnormality in menstruation. Abdominal ultrasonography detected a uterine tumor with suspected endometrial thickening. Endometrial biopsy yielded an endometrial polyp. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed a large lobulated heterogeneous mass at the uterine fundus and lobulated tumors extending to the bilateral adnexa, parametria, and cul-de-sac (Figure 1). The tumors were heterogeneous enhancement on postcontrast T1-weighted imaging and heterogeneously hyperintense on T2-weighted imaging. These appearances raised suspicion of uterine malignancy. A total abdominal hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy was performed. During surgery, several soft reddish tumors bulging from the paratubal and parametrial areas were noted (Figure 2). On gross examination, myomalike tumor extension was found on each side of the ovarian vein (Figure 3). Upon sectioning the posterior wall of the uterus, one 8-cm hypervascularized leiomyoma at the fundus and multiple polypoid or wormlike tumoral growths that emerged from the cut surface were noted (Figure 4). Histological findings revealed leiomyomas with extensive intravenous leiomyomatosis in the uterus, bilateral paratubal soft tissue, and bilateral parametrium (Figure 5). No significant cytologic atypia, tumor necrosis, or increased mitotic activity was seen. The patient recovered well postoperatively, and at a follow-up visit 12 months later, the patient remained well with no further symptoms. Intravenous leiomyomatosis is a rare neoplasm characterized by a benign smooth muscle cell tumor growing along the uterus, the extrauterine venous system, or both.

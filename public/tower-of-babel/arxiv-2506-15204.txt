Cutting and pasting pairs of manifolds with tangential structures
R.A. Vlierhuis

Abstract
. This paper studies cutting and pasting groups (SK-groups) of pairs of manifolds. By a pair of manifolds we mean a manifold with a submanifold of strictly smaller dimension. Existing results in the unoriented category by Komiya are generalized to manifolds with certain tangential structures. In this way multiple new splitting results for SK of pairs are obtained, in particular for SK of pairs of manifolds with a map to a reference space. We also prove that SK of manifolds with a map into X × Y X\times Y with Y Y simply connected is the same as SK of manifolds with map into X X . This generalizes the result of Neumann that SK of manifolds with a map into a simply connected space is trivial.



1.
Introduction

\operatorname{SK}
-groups were introduced by Karras, Kreck, Neumann and Ossa in their 1973 book
[
KKNO73
]
. Given a manifold
M
, choosing a codimension one splitting submanifold with trivial normal bundle yields a splitting
M=M_{1}\cup M_{2}
, where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are manifolds with boundary. If one now takes a diffeomorphism
\varphi:\partial M_{1}\cong\partial M_{2}
, the pieces can be glued back along
\varphi
to get a manifold potentially different from
M
. The
\operatorname{SK}
-groups measure how many different classes of manifolds there are under this cutting and pasting relation. Recently, there has been renewed interest in these SK-groups and their generalizations, see for example
[
HMM+22
]
and the preprints
[
HSV25
]
and
[
MNS+25
]
.

The present paper aims to extend cutting and pasting of pairs. A pair of manifolds is a manifold with a submanifold of strictly smaller dimension. This generalization of SK was considered first by Komiya
[
Kom86
]
, who proved in the unoriented case that cutting and pasting of pairs does not introduce any new obstructions. Informally, “cutting and pasting a pair is the same as cutting and pasting the big manifold and the small manifold individually”. We shall extend his results from unoriented manifolds to manifolds endowed with a sufficiently nice tangential
B
-structure. Our statement is the following.

Theorem A

(
3.1
)
.

Let
B\to BO_{k}
be a strongly multiplicative structure, with
k\geq m>n
. There is a split short exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{SK}^{B}_{m}\xrightarrow{i}\operatorname{SK}^{B}_{m,n}\xrightarrow{j}\operatorname{SK}^{B}_{n}(B_{m-n})\to 0,

with
i([M])=[M,\emptyset]
and
j([M,N])=[N,\nu]
where
\nu:N\to B_{m-n}
is the
B
-structure for the normal bundle of
N
in
M
.

This result enables many new splitting results. For example, we present one for unoriented SK with a map to a reference space
X
(
3.2
), and an analogous result in the oriented case (
3.3
). These results may be phrased as saying that “cutting and pasting a pair of maps is the same as cutting and pasting the maps individually”. While proving the statement in the oriented case we will also show the following, which generalizes Theorem 1 from
[
Neu75
]
.

Theorem B

(
6.1
)
.

Let
Y
be a simply connected pointed space, and suppose
\pi_{1}(X)
is finitely presented. Then the projection
p_{*}:\operatorname{SK}_{n}(X\times Y)\to\operatorname{SK}_{n}(X)
is an isomorphism for all
n
.

Conventions

In this text, a manifold is smooth and closed, not necessarily oriented. The reference space
X
is assumed to be pointed and path connected. A star
*
will denote the appropriate constant map.

Our results about cutting and pasting of pairs will apply to structures that satisfy a two-out-of-three theorem. For example, if
E\to M
is a vector bundle, then two of the following being true implies the third:

(1)

M
is orientable

(2)

E
is orientable (as a manifold)

(3)

The bundle
E\to M
is orientable.

We formalize this for a general tangential structure as follows.

Definition 2.5

(Strongly multiplicative)
.

A structure
B\to BO_{n}
is called strongly multiplicative if and only if for every
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
with

there exists a map
\psi_{r,s}:B_{r}\times B_{s}\to B_{r+s}
such that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{r}\times B_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{r}\times B_{r+s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BO_{r}\times BO_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BO_{r}\times BO_{r+s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}\times\psi_{r,s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}\times\phi_{r,s}}

is a pullback square, where the vertical maps are the obvious ones and
\phi_{r,s}:BO_{r}\times BO_{s}\to BO_{r+s}
is the natural embedding.

This condition means that we choose a way to combine
B
-structures on
\xi^{r}
and
\eta^{s}
to a
B
-structure on
\xi^{r}\oplus\eta^{s}
, ensuring that
B
-structures on
\eta
and
\xi\oplus\eta
give rise to a
unique

B
-structure on
\xi
.

Remark
.

The terminology ‘strongly multiplicative’ is meant to reflect that we are demanding this two-out-of-three rule, and not just the weaker statement that a product of two
B
-bundles again has a
B
-structure.

Example 2.6
.

(1)

The
k
-connected covers of
BO_{n}
define strongly multiplicative structures. Indeed, one can check this by using the long exact sequence for the homotopy groups in a pullback square of fibrations. In particular, orientations and spin structures form strongly multiplicative structures, which can also be proven in a concrete manner.

(2)

Another example is given by the
k
-orientability of Hoekzema
[
Hoe18
]
. We call a manifold
k
-orientable if its Stiefel-Whitney classes up to dimension
2^{k}
vanish. It is easy to show that the corresponding structure satisfies the strongly multiplicative property.

(3)

If
B\to BO_{n}
is any strongly multiplicative structure and
X
is a space (we will assume it to be pointed and path connected), then another strongly multiplicative structure is given by the map
B\times X\to BO_{n}
which first projects onto
B
and then applies the initial fibration. Note that all pullbacks are of the form
B_{m}\times X\to BO_{m}
. That is, this structure represents a map from the
B
-manifold into
X
, with no further restrictions.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\xi:E\to M
be a vector bundle and
B
a strongly multiplicative structure. If two of the following conditions hold, so does the third.

(1)

M
is a
B
-manifold

(2)

E
is a
B
-manifold

(3)

\xi
is a
B
-bundle.

Proof.

Choose a connection on
\xi
. This is equivalent to a choice of splitting
TE=V\oplus H
where
V=\ker d\xi
and
H\cong\xi^{*}TM
. Additionally we can identify
V
with
\xi^{*}E
. Suppose (1) and (2) hold. Then
TM
is a
B
-bundle, hence so is
H
, thus by strong multiplicativity so is
V
. Pulling back
V
along the zero section
M\to E
we recover
\xi:E\to M
, which is thus a
B
-bundle.

Now if (2) and (3) hold,
TE
and
V
are
B
-bundles, hence so is
H
by strong multiplicativity. Again we pull back along the zero section, now to obtain
TM
. Finally, if (1) and (3) hold, then
V
and
H
are
B
-bundles, hence so is
TE
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle whose total space is a
B
-manifold, with
B
strongly multiplicative. Then the total space of the sphere bundle
S(E)
is a
B
-manifold.

Proof.

This will follow at once from the fact that the normal bundle of
S(E)
inside
E
is trivial. Indeed, this normal bundle is clearly a line bundle, and a nonvanishing global section can be found by considering at every point
(x,v)
in the sphere bundle the tangent vector given by the straight line to
(x,0)
.
∎

Definition 2.9

(Pairs of manifolds)
.

A pair of
B
-manifolds is a
B
-manifold
M
with a submanifold
N
of strictly smaller dimension that is also a
B
-manifold.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
M
and
N
be a pair of
B
-manifolds. Then the normal bundle of
N
in
M
, denoted
\nu(N,M)
, is a
B
-bundle.

Proof.

Let
\iota:N\to M
be the inclusion and consider
\iota^{*}TM
, a bundle over
N
. There is a natural splitting
\iota^{*}TM=TN\oplus\nu(N,M)
and so
\nu(N,M)
has a
B
-structure by strong multiplicativity.
∎

We shall apply
2.8
to the bundle
S:=S(\nu(N,M)\oplus\underline{\mathbb{R}})
. We will have that there is a tubular neighborhood of
N
in
M
that is
B
-diffeomorphic to a tubular neighborhood of
N
in
S
, since the
B
-structures are both induced from the ones on
N
and
M
.

We are now ready to define
\operatorname{SK}
of pairs of
B
-manifolds following Komiya
[
Kom86
]
. Note that a
B
-diffeomorphism is defined exactly so that we can glue two
B
-manifolds with boundary along a
B
-diffeomorphism to get another
B
-manifold. This ensures that the following constructions are well-defined.

Definition 2.11
.

Consider the monoid of
n
-dimensional manifolds with
B
-structure and operation disjoint union, denoted
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{B}
. We have an equivalence relation generated by
M_{1}\cup_{\phi}M_{2}\sim M_{1}\cup_{\psi}M_{2}
, where
\phi,\psi:\partial M_{1}\to\partial M_{2}
are
B
-diffeomorphisms. We get a cancellative semigroup given by
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{B}
modulo this relation. Its Grothendieck group is the
\operatorname{SK}
-group with
B
-structure,
\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{B}
.

Definition 2.12
.

Consider the monoid of
(m,n)
-pairs of
B
-manifolds, denoted
\mathcal{M}_{m,n}^{B}
. We have an equivalence relation generated by
(M_{1},N_{1})\cup_{\phi}(M_{2},N_{2})\sim(M_{1},N_{1})\cup_{\psi}(M_{2},N_{2})
, where
(M_{i},N_{i})
are pairs of manifolds with boundary and
\phi,\psi:\partial M_{1}\to\partial M_{2}
are
B
-diffeomorphisms such that
\phi|_{\partial N_{1}}
and
\psi|_{\partial N_{1}}
are
B
-diffeomorphisms onto
\partial N_{2}
. We get a cancellative semigroup given by
\mathcal{M}_{m,n}^{B}
modulo this relation and call its Grothendieck group
\operatorname{SK}_{m,n}^{B}
.

Remark
.

We will adopt a few shorthand notations for the
\operatorname{SK}
-groups that will be discussed in more detail in the rest of the paper. Unoriented
\operatorname{SK}
is given by the structure that is the identity on
BO_{n}
and will be denoted
\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{O}
. Oriented
\operatorname{SK}
is given by the 1-connected cover
BSO_{n}\to BO_{n}
and will be denoted simply
\operatorname{SK}_{n}
.
\operatorname{SK}
of
B
-manifolds with a map to
X
, given by the structure in
2.6
(3), will be denoted
\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{B}(X)
. We will use similar conventions for
\operatorname{SK}
of pairs.

3.
Splitting results for SK of pairs

Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
B\to BO_{k}
be a strongly multiplicative structure, with
k\geq m>n
. There is a split short exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{SK}^{B}_{m}\xrightarrow{i}\operatorname{SK}^{B}_{m,n}\xrightarrow{j}\operatorname{SK}^{B}_{n}(B_{m-n})\to 0,

with
i([M])=[M,\emptyset]
and
j([M,N])=[N,\nu]
where
\nu:N\to B_{m-n}
is the
B
-structure for the normal bundle of
N
in
M
.

Proof.

Now that we’ve set up the language of strongly multiplicative
B
-structures, the proof is essentially the same as Komiya’s proof in the unoriented case. Firstly, note that
j
is well-defined because the normal bundle of
N
in
M
has a canonical
B
-structure by strong multiplicativity.

It is clear that
i
is injective and that
j\circ i=0
. To see that
j
is surjective, let
[N,\nu]\in\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{B}(B_{m-n})
. Let
E
be the pullback of the universal bundle over
B_{m-n}
by
\nu
and consider the sphere bundle
S:=S(E\oplus\underline{\mathbb{R}})
.
E
is a
B
-bundle by definition, so by
2.8

S
is a
B
-manifold. We have a projection
\pi:S\to N
and additionally we can view
N
as a submanifold of
S
by considering the north poles of all the spheres. The normal bundle of
N
in
S
is now clearly classified by
\nu
, so
j[S,N]=[N,\nu]
.

The sequence splits since we can define
k:\operatorname{SK}_{m,n}(X)\to\operatorname{SK}_{m}(X)
mapping
[M,N]\mapsto[M]
. It just remains to show that
\ker j\subset\operatorname{im}i
.

To show that
\ker j\subset\operatorname{im}i
, let
[M,N]\in\ker j
. We can write
[M,N]=[M_{1},N_{1}]-[M_{2},N_{2}]
in
\operatorname{SK}_{m,n}^{B}
. Since
[M,N]
was in the kernel of
j
, it follows that
[N_{1},\nu_{1}]=[N_{2},\nu_{2}]
in
\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{B}(B_{m-n})
, where
\nu_{i}:N_{i}\to B_{m-n}
are the
B
-structures on the normal bundles of
N_{i}
in
M_{i}
for
i=1,2
. We can define
B
-manifolds
S_{i}
for
i=1,2
analogously to
S
above.

Let us find a closed neighborhood
T_{i}^{\prime}
of
N_{i}
in
S_{i}
that is
B
-diffeomorphic to a neighborhood
T_{i}
of
N_{i}
in
M_{i}
, using the tubular neighborhood theorem. We denote the interiors of the
T_{i}
and
T_{i}^{\prime}
by
\mathring{T_{i}^{\prime}}
and
\mathring{T_{i}}
, respectively.

We can split the pair
(M_{i},N_{i})
into
(M_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}},\emptyset)\cup_{\text{id}}\overline{(T_{i},N_{i})}
. Make the following definitions:

\displaystyle K_{i}

\displaystyle:=(S_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}^{\prime}})\cup\overline{(M_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}})}

\displaystyle K_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=(M_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}})\cup\overline{(M_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}})}.

In
\operatorname{SK}_{m,n}^{B}
we can now write

\displaystyle[M_{i},N_{i}]+[K_{i},\emptyset]=

\displaystyle[(M_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}},\emptyset)\cup\overline{(T_{i},N_{i})}]+[(S_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}^{\prime}},\emptyset)\cup\overline{(M_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}},\emptyset)}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(M_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}},\emptyset)\cup\overline{(M_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}},\emptyset)}]+[(S_{i}-\mathring{T_{i}^{\prime}},\emptyset)\cup\overline{(T_{i},N_{i})}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[K_{i}^{\prime},\emptyset]+[S_{i},N_{i}].

However, notice that
[S_{1},N_{1}]=[S_{2},N_{2}]
, so now computing
[M_{1},N_{1}]-[M_{2},N_{2}]
will yield

[M_{1},N_{1}]-[M_{2},N_{2}]=[K_{1}^{\prime},\emptyset]+[K_{2},\emptyset]-[K_{1},\emptyset]-[K_{2}^{\prime},\emptyset],

proving that
[M_{1},N_{1}]-[M_{2},N_{2}]\in\operatorname{im}(i)
.
∎

In
[
Kom86
]
, the theorem is then strengthened by simplifying the right term in the sequence. There it is used that the natural map
\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{O}(BO(k))\to\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{O}
is an isomorphism. Such vanishing results do not hold in the generality of
3.1
. However, there is a few interesting cases where results can be proved, and the rest of this paper will be dedicated to this theory. In particular we will show the following two theorems.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a pointed path connected space and
n<m
. There is a split short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{SK}_{m}^{O}(X)\xrightarrow{i}\operatorname{SK}_{m,n}^{O}(X)\xrightarrow{j}\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{O}(X\times X)\longrightarrow 0,

with
i([M,f])=[(M,\emptyset),(f,\emptyset)]
and
j([(M,N),(f,g)]=[N,f|_{N}\times g]
.

Proof.

First apply
3.1
with the structure
BO\times X\to BO
given by projection onto the first coordinate. The natural projection
\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{O}(X\times BO(m-n)\times X)\to\operatorname{SK}_{n}^{O}(X\times X)
is an isomorphism, which will be proved in
Section

5
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a pointed path connected space and
n<m
. There is a split short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{SK}_{m}(X)\xrightarrow{i}\operatorname{SK}_{m,n}(X)\xrightarrow{j}\operatorname{SK}_{n}(X\times X)\longrightarrow 0,

with
i([M,f])=[(M,\emptyset),(f,\emptyset)]
and
j([(M,N),(f,g)]=[N,f|_{N}\times g]
.

Proof.

First apply
3.1
with the structure
BSO\times X\to BO
given by projection onto the first coordinate and applying the natural map
BSO\to BO
. The natural projection
\operatorname{SK}_{n}(X\times BO(m-n)\times X)\to\operatorname{SK}_{n}(X\times X)
is an isomorphism, which will be proved in
Section

6
.
∎

5.
The unoriented case

We now move to proving the claims made in
Section

3
. In the unoriented case, we make use of the fact that there is a Künneth isomorphism for the unoriented bordism groups.

Proposition 5.1
.

The map

\Omega_{*}^{O}(X)\otimes_{\Omega_{*}^{O}}\Omega_{*}^{O}(Y)\to\Omega_{*}^{O}(X\times Y)

given by product of singular manifolds is an isomorphism.

Proof.

This can be shown using the fact that there is an isomorphism
\Omega_{*}^{O}(X)\cong H_{*}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\Omega_{*}^{O}
(see for example
[
CF62
]
) and the usual Künneth formula.
∎

The claim made in
3.2
now follows by applying the following proposition to
Y=BO(m-n)
. Indeed
\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}(BO(k))\to\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}
is an isomorphism. This is because generators for the homology of

come from maps
\mathbb{R}P^{i}\to BO
that classify the tautological line bundle over
\mathbb{R}P^{i}
. However, in
\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}(X)
it is true that
[\mathbb{R}P^{i},f]=[\mathbb{R}P^{i},*]
for all maps
f:\mathbb{R}P^{i}\to X
. For more details, see
[
KKNO73
, Thm 2.11]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
Y
be a pointed path connected space such that the forgetful map
\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}(Y)\to\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}
is an isomorphism. Then
p_{*}:\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}(X\times Y)\to\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}(X)
induced by the projection
p:X\times Y\to X
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

This map
p_{*}
is trivially surjective. Let
M
be a manifold,
f:M\to X\times Y
a map and consider
[M,f]-[M,pf]\in\Omega_{*}^{O}(X\times Y)
, where we use the basepoint of
Y
to embed
\Omega_{*}^{O}(X)
into
\Omega_{*}^{O}(X\times Y)
. By
5.1
, we can write
[M,f]
as a sum of elements of the form
[M_{1}\times M_{2},f_{1}\times f_{2}]
, with
f_{1}:M_{1}\to X
,
f_{2}:M_{2}\to Y
. Then clearly
[M,pf]
can be written as the sum of the elements
[M_{1}\times M_{2},f_{1}\times*]
. We have that
[M,f]-[M,pf]
is a sum of the elements

[M_{1}\times M_{2},f_{1}\times f_{2}]-[M_{1}\times M_{2},f_{1}\times*]=[M_{1},f_{1}]([M_{2},f_{2}]-[M_{2},*]).

But recall that
[M_{2},f_{2}]-[M_{2},*]
is zero in SK because
\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}(Y)\cong\operatorname{SK}_{*}^{O}
. So
[M,f]-[M,pf]
is zero in
\overline{\operatorname{SK}}_{*}^{O}(X\times Y)
, and thus
p_{*}:\overline{\operatorname{SK}}_{*}^{O}(X\times Y)\to\overline{\operatorname{SK}}_{*}^{O}(X)
is injective since the map
\overline{\operatorname{SK}}_{*}^{O}(X)\to\overline{\operatorname{SK}}_{*}^{O}(X\times Y)
given by including
X
into

using the basepoint is a left inverse. Therefore it is an isomorphism. A simple application of the five lemma now shows that

is an isomorphism, as claimed.
∎

Remark
.

To the author’s knowledge, there is no example in the literature that shows that unoriented SK of maps is nontrivial, i.e. a space

such that

. Neumann
[
Neu75

gives a way to compute

using the fundamental group of

and its abelian subgroups. This is then applied with

to show that

6.
The oriented case

The situation in the oriented case is more complicated, and we shall require powerful theory. However, we will eventually be able to prove

Theorem 6.1
.

Let

The proof given here is inspired by Neumann’s proof of

-groups vanish, so there is nothing to prove. In dimension 2, Neumann
[
Neu75

proves that the group
\operatorname{SK}_{2}(X)
depends only on

, and thus
\operatorname{SK}_{2}(X\times Y)\to\operatorname{SK}_{2}(X)
must be an isomorphism. The case

is done by studying the asymmetric signature of Ranicki
[
Ran13
]
and concluding that this does not depend on the map to

. This generalizes Neumann’s argument which relied on the Open Book Decomposition Theorem of Winkelnkemper
[
Win73
]
. Finally, in the case

Neumann uses the classification of simply connected 4-manifolds up to stable diffeomorphism. We need to consider non-simply connected manifolds and use the theory due to Kreck
[
Kre99
]

Proof.

of the high-dimensional case
n\geq 5
. We need to consider the asymmetric signature, which is an invariant of manifolds that have an open book decomposition on the boundary. Let us consider the bounded book bordism groups
BB_{n}(X)
of Ranicki. The group

consists of
(n+2)
-manifolds with boundary that have a book decomposition on the boundary together with a map to
X
, modulo bordisms of manifolds with boundary that extend the book decomposition on the boundary and the map to
X

[M,f]
. The class of a manifold with boundary is zero when the book decomposition on the boundary extends to the entire manifold.

Ranicki now defines the asymmetric signature by constructing some asymmetric Poincaré complex of

-modules from the
(n+2)
-manifold with boundary

and taking its cobordism class in the asymmetric

\operatorname{LAsy}_{h}^{n+2}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(M)])
. We extend this to an invariant of

by pulling back the coefficients along the map

to get a Poincaré complex of
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]
-modules, so that we get a map
\sigma*:BB_{n}(X)\to\operatorname{LAsy}_{h}^{n+2}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])

[

, so if the book decomposition on the boundary extends, then
\sigma^{*}(M,f)=0
. The crucial point is that the asymmetric signature is the only obstruction, that is, when

, the decomposition on the boundary extends [loc. cit., Thm 29.17], when

Consider

that is zero in

equals

because

is bordant to a manifold that has an open book decomposition. Hence the map

∎

To prove the claim for

Theorem 6.2

Two closed 4-dimensional manifolds with the same normal 1-type
\xi:B\to BSO

This is essentially Theorem C in
[

We need the following lemma that tells us what the normal 1-type of a manifold is. A proof can be found in

The way we will apply this is to start with

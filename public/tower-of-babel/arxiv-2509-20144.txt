A Hilbert 90 Property for S-Class Groups and Applications to the Gross-Kuz’min Conjecture
Julian Feuerpfeil

Abstract
. Let L / K L/K be a cyclic extension of number fields, and let S S be a finite set of places of K K containing the ramified and Archimedean ones. We say that L / K L/K has the 𝐜𝐥 S \mathbf{cl}^{S} -Hilbert 90 property if, for any generator σ ∈ Gal ⁡ ( L / K ) \sigma\in\Gal(L/K) , the kernel of the arithmetic norm map cl S ⁡ ( L ) → cl S ⁡ ( K ) \cl^{S}(L)\to\cl^{S}(K) coincides with ( 1 − σ ) ​ cl S ​ ( L ) (1-\sigma)\cl^{S}(L) . We establish an effective criterion for the 𝐜𝐥 S \mathbf{cl}^{S} -Hilbert 90 property and investigate its connections with Iwasawa theory, in particular its relation to the Gross–Kuz’min conjecture. This yields a new method for verifying the property by explicit computation, with further links to the theory of spins of prime ideals. 1.
Introduction

Hilbert’s Theorem 90 is arguably one of the most important and fundamental results in algebraic number theory. In its original form, it can be stated as follows (cf.
[
13
]
*Satz 90):

Theorem 1.1

(Hilbert 90)
.

Let
L/K
be a cyclic Galois extension and
\sigma
be a generator of
G:=\Gal(L/K)
. Then any element
x\in L^{\times}
with
\smash{N_{L/K}(x)=1}
can be written as
x=\sigma(y)y^{-1}
for some
y\in L^{\times}
.

In more sophisticated terms, we have
\widehat{H}^{-1}(G,L^{\times})=0
, were
\widehat{H}^{i}
denotes the
i^{\text{th}}
Tate-cohomology group. Noether generalized this theorem to arbitrary Galois extensions in the sense that
H^{1}(G,L^{\times})=0

[
28
]
.

In
[
30
]
Quadrelli and Weigel introduced a Hilbert 90 property for cohomological Mackey functors, which provides a generalized framework for statements of similar type and refines the cohomological interpretation of the classical Hilbert 90 theorem. For a finite group
G
, a (
G^{\circ}
-)
cohomological Mackey functor

\mathbf{X}
is the datum of a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{X}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{X}_{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{\mathbf{X}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{\mathbf{X}}}

where
\mathbf{X}_{1}
is a
G
-module and
\mathbf{X}_{G}
an abelian group considered as trivial
G
-module, and
N_{\mathbf{X}}
and
I_{\mathbf{X}}
are morphisms of
G
-modules, such that

\displaystyle(I_{\mathbf{X}}\circ N_{\mathbf{X}})(x)=\sum_{g\in G}gx\quad\text{and}\quad(N_{\mathbf{X}}\circ I_{\mathbf{X}})(x)=|G|\cdot x.

For a more precise definition, see Definition
2.1
. To a cohomological Mackey functor, one can associate certain abelian groups, called
section cohomology groups
(see Section
2.2
). One of those is
c_{1}(G,\mathbf{X})=\ker(N_{\mathbf{X}})/\omega_{G}\mathbf{X}_{1}

where
\omega_{G}=(1-g:g\in G)
is the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{Z}[G]
. We say that
G
has the
\mathbf{X}
-Hilbert 90 property if
c_{1}(G,\mathbf{X})=0
. If
G
is cyclic, this can be reformulated as
\ker N_{\mathbf{X}}=(1-\sigma)\mathbf{X}_{1}
, where
\sigma
is a generator of
G
. In Section
2
we present the general theory of cohomological Mackey functors and their cohomology.

In
[
30
]
Quadrelli and Weigel studied the Hilbert 90 property of the cohomological Mackey functor
\mathbf{Ab}
, whose definition is purely group-theoretical (see Example
2.2
(
2
)). This result also has applications in algebraic number theory:

Let
L/K
be a cyclic unramified extension and
S
a finite set of places containing all the Archimedean ones such that all the places of
S
are completely split (we say that an infinite place is completely split if it is complex or any place above it is real). Then we consider the cohomological Mackey functor
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
given by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cl^{S}(L)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cl^{S}(K).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}

Here
N_{I}
is the arithmetic norm, and
I
is given by
[\mathfrak{a}]\mapsto[\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{L}]
. By class field theory
\mathbf{cl}^{S}\cong\mathbf{Ab}
and by the results of Quadrelli and Weigel
\Gal(L/K)
has the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
-Hilbert 90 property.

The above definition also produces a cohomological Mackey functor even if the extension
L/K
is not unramified or
S
does not contain only split places. In this case, we also require that
S
contains all ramified and Archimedean places of the extension
L/K
. Here, the previously used method is no longer applicable. However, it is natural to ask the following questions:

(1)

What conditions on
L
,
K
and
S
are sufficient to conclude that
\Gal(L/K)
has the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
-Hilbert 90 property?

(2)

Is the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
-Hilbert 90 property a “rare”  property?

The objective of section
4
is to partially answer these questions. W break up the problem locally at primes by decomposing the class groups and therefore also the corresponding Mackey functors into their
p
-parts. For a torsion abelian group
A
and prime
p
, we write
A_{(p)}
for the
p
-primary part of
A
.

From Theorem
2.11
we deduce the following theorem:

Theorem

(See Corollary
4.2
)
.

The extension
L/K
has the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}_{(p)}
-Hilbert 90 property if and only if the following sequence is exact:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}(K^{\times}_{v}/N_{v}(L_{v}^{\times}))_{(p)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}

Using this result and some elementary class field theory, we prove Theorem
4.5
, which yields a more explicit criterion for an extension
L/K
to have the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
-Hilbert 90 property. One of the most important aspects of this criterion is that it does not require knowledge about the
S
-class group of
L
, which has the advantage of being easier to verify with a computer, since it only requires the computation of a ray class group and the classical class group of
K
. However, it only poses a sufficient but not necessary condition to check the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
-Hilbert 90 property, as Example
4.7
shows.

Using this criterion, we show in Proposition
4.11
that there are cyclic extensions
L/K
of arbitrarily high degree totally ramified at exactly two tame places
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}
that satisfy the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
-Hilbert 90 property for
S=\{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\}\cup S_{\infty}
.

In Section
5
we study the behavior of the
\smash{\mathbf{cl}^{S_{p}}_{(p)}}
-Hilbert 90 property along a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
extension
K_{\infty}/K
and connections with the coinvariants of the Kuz’min-Tate module
T_{p}(K_{\infty}/K)
associated to
K_{\infty}/K
, which is defined to be

\displaystyle T_{p}(K_{\infty}/K):=\varprojlim_{n}\cl^{S_{p}}(K_{n})_{(p)}.

Let
K^{cycl}_{\infty}/K
be the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
extension of
K
, then we write
T_{p}^{\text{cycl}}(K)
for its Kuz’min-Tate module. Denote by
\Gamma:=\Gal(K^{cycl}_{\infty}/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. One of the formulations of the celebrated Gross-Kuz’min Conjecture is the following:

Conjecture
5.4

(Gross-Kuz’min Conjecture)
.

The group
T_{p}^{\text{cycl}}(K)_{\Gamma}
is finite.

An important consequence of Proposition
5.6
is the following corollary which together with
4.5
gives an explicit computational tool to check the validity of Conjecture
Conjecture
:

Theorem

(See Corollary
5.7
)
.

Let
K_{\infty}/K
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
extension of a number field such that
n_{0}=0
, e.g., if
K_{\infty}/K
is totally ramified at all primes lying above
p
. If
K_{1}/K
has the
\mathbf{cl}^{S_{p}}_{(p)}
-Hilbert 90 property, then
T_{p}(K_{\infty}/K)_{\Gamma}
is finite.

The idea of studying the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture based on a version of the Hilbert 90 theorem is not new. It was essentially already known to Iwasawa
[
14
]
that the vanishing of
H^{1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{O}_{K^{cycl}_{\infty}}[1/p]))
is equivalent to the Gross-Kuz’min conjecture. Based on this approach Seo presented in
[
31
]
a connection between the Gross-Kuz’min and Leopoldt conjecture in conjunction with a Hilbert 90 property for the compact
\Gamma
module
\overline{\Delta(\mathcal{O}_{K,S_{p}}^{\times})}
, where
\Delta
is the map

\displaystyle K^{\times}\to\prod_{v\mid p}\widehat{K_{v}^{\times}},\qquad x\mapsto(x,...,x)

with
\widehat{K_{v}^{\times}}=\varprojlim K_{v}^{\times}\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. The Leopoldt conjecture for
K
and
p
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{K,S_{p}}^{\times}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cong\overline{\Delta(\mathcal{O}_{K,S_{p}}^{\times})}.

Without the Leopoldt conjecture, we are currently not able to find a direct connection between our approach and the one by Seo, but if we assume the Leopoldt conjecture to hold, his Hilbert 90 property implies that

\displaystyle\varprojlim c_{1}(\Gamma/\Gamma_{n},\mathbf{cl}^{S_{p}}_{(p)})\cong H^{1}(\Gamma,\varprojlim\cl^{S_{p}}(K_{n})_{(p)}).

Thus, it yields an equality between the section cohomology and the cohomology of the
S_{p}
-class group in the limit. It would be very interesting to generalize our approach and especially the definition of cohomological Mackey functors and their section cohomology to infinite profinite groups to get more precise statements in the limit. The benefit of our approach is that we do not need such limit arguments, but information about finite layers suffices for our conclusions. The disadvantage is that we have to restrict ourselves to more specific classes of examples to make our methods applicable.

Section
6.1
is devoted to finding a more feasible approach to check Corollary
5.7
by means of the “weak form”  of the Gras theorem (Theorem
3.10
). This leads to the following theorem:

Theorem
6.3
.

Let
K
be a number field and
p
and odd prime that is unramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Let
K_{\infty}/K
be a
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
extension, which is totally ramified over all places
\mathfrak{p}
above
p
. Let
T_{p}(K_{\infty}/K)
be its associated Kuz’min-Tate module.

If there exists
\mathfrak{p}\mid p
such that the map

\displaystyle\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}:E^{\mathfrak{p}}\to\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}\mid p}\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{q}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p},\quad\alpha\mapsto\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{q}\mid p}\alpha_{\mathfrak{q}}\otimes 1

is surjective, then
T_{p}(K_{\infty}/K)_{\Gamma}
is finite. In particular, if
K_{\infty}/K
is the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
extension, then
GKC
is true for
K
and
p
.

The homomorphism
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
factors through
E^{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
and using the Dirichlet unit theorem for
S
-units it is easy to determine the dimension of
E^{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
. If
p
is odd, our assumptions imply that it is
r_{1}+r_{2}
, where
(r_{1},r_{2})
is the signature of
K
. Modeling
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
as a random matrix leads to Heuristic
7.3
, which turns out not to be valid in all cases (see Section
7.2
), but seems to hold true at least in the totally real case, based numerical experiments. In Section
7.1
we present some of the data generated. For this matter, we used the computer algebra system OSCAR (see
[
29
]
). The source code can be found on GitHub (
https://github.com/Firebolt2222/Hilbert-90-for-S-Class-Groups-and-the-Gross-Kuz-min-Conjecture
).

The numerical results and Heuristic
7.3
together with known results about spin symbols, which have a remarkable similarity with
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
, lead us to the following Conjecture:

Conjecture
7.6
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field of degree
n
. There exist at most finitely many primes
p
, unramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that for no place
\mathfrak{p}
above
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
is surjective.

The first example we consider is Galois cohomology of number fields. Let

In the definition of

it is not strictly necessary to assume that

contains all ramified primes, but in general the straightforward definition of maps does not result in a cohomological Mackey functor. Nevertheless,
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
can be a cohomological Mackey functor if

does not contain all ramified primes for trivial reasons, e.g. if
\cl^{S}(L)\cong\cl^{S}(K)\cong 0
.

(2)

Then the exponential valuations define a morphism of cohomological Mackey functors

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{\times}\to\bigoplus_{v\not\in S}\mathbf{H}^{0}_{v}(\underline{\phantom{a}},\mathbb{Z}).

Now
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
is the cokernel of this morphism in the category of cohomological Mackey functors.

(3)

The Mackey functor
\mathbf{cl}^{S}
agrees with
\mathbf{Pic}
for the étale cover
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{L,S})\to\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{K,S})
.

2.1.2.
Selmer groups

Another example of can be constructed from Selmer groups of elliptic curves over number fields. Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field

. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the
m
-Selmer group of

is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sel}^{(m)}(E/K)=\ker\left(H^{1}(G_{K},E[m])\to{\bigoplus}_{v}H^{1}(G_{K_{v}},E)[m]\right)

An equivalent definition can be found in
[

]
*Chapter X.4.

If
L/K
is a finite Galois extension of

and

the base extension of
E
to

. Then for every place
v
of
K
we get a cohomological Mackey functor
\mathbf{H}^{1}_{v}(\underline{\phantom{a}},E)[m]
defined as the kernel of the multiplication by
m
in
\mathbf{H}^{1}_{v}(\underline{\phantom{a}},E)
. Now we set
\mathbf{Sel}^{(m)}(E/\underline{\phantom{a}})
to be the kernel of

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{1}(\underline{\phantom{a}},E[m])\to\bigoplus_{v}\mathbf{H}^{1}_{v}(\underline{\phantom{a}},E)[m].

Its components agree with
\mathrm{Sel}^{(m)}(E/K)
and
\mathrm{Sel}^{(m)}(E_{L}/L)
. Similarly, it is possible to turn the definition of the Tate-Shafarevich group of

into a cohomological Mackey functor. In fact, using the standard exact sequence for the Selmer and Tate-Shafarevich groups, it is possible to define it as the quotient of
\mathbf{Sel}^{(m)}(E/\underline{\phantom{a}})
by a cohomological Mackey functor representing the weak Mordell-Weil group

.

The following

\displaystyle c_{j}(G,\mathbf{X})=\bigoplus_{i\in I}c_{j}(G,\mathbf{X}_{i})\qquad\text{and}\qquad k^{j}(G,\mathbf{X})=\bigoplus_{i\in I}k^{j}(G,\mathbf{X}_{i})

and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbf{X}_{1})_{(p)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbf{X}_{G})_{(p)}}

and

\displaystyle c_{j}(G,\mathbf{X}_{(p)})\cong c_{j}(G,\mathbf{X})_{(p)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad k^{j}(G,\mathbf{X}_{(p)})\cong k^{j}(G,\mathbf{X})_{(p)}.

. If

2.3.
Closing the hexagon for
\mathbf{Pic}

For this section, we fix a cyclic Galois covering of schemes

and let
G
be its Galois group. Then we get a Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence in étale cohomology (see
[
23
]
*§2 Theorem 2.20):

(3)

\displaystyle E^{p,q}_{2}=H^{p}(G,H^{q}_{\et}(Y,\mathbb{G}_{m}))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{\et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})

From this spectral sequence one can deduce the following seven-term sequence:

(4)

\begin{split}0\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{G}_{m}(Y))&\to\Pic(X)\to\Pic(Y)^{G}\to H^{2}(G,\mathbb{G}_{m}(Y))\to\phantom{ab}...\\
...\to&\Br(Y/X)\to H^{1}(G,\Pic(Y))\to H^{3}(G,\mathbb{G}_{m}(Y))\end{split}

Here we write
\Br(Y/X)
for the relative cohomological Brauer group:

\displaystyle\Br(Y/X):=\ker(H^{2}_{\et}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})\overset{f_{*}}{\to}H^{2}_{\et}(Y,\mathbb{G}_{m})).

This group coincides with the Brauer group defined via Azumaya algebras in various cases by Gabber’s theorem (notice that

is always contained in
\Br(X)_{\text{torsion}}
). The seven-term sequence (

) was independently discovered for Galois extensions of rings by Chase, Harrison, and Rosenberg in
[

]
*Corollary 5.5. Their methods were based on Amitsur cohomology. A modern exposition can be found in
[
4
]
.

We use this sequence to deduce some properties about the cohomological Mackey functor

. One immediately observes that

(5)

\displaystyle k^{0}(G,\mathbf{Pic})

\displaystyle\cong H^{1}(G,\mathbb{G}_{m}(Y))\quad\text{and}

(6)

\displaystyle k^{1}(G,\mathbf{Pic})

\displaystyle\cong\ker(H^{2}(G,\mathbb{G}_{m}(Y))\to\Br(Y/X)).

Since we assume that
G
is cyclic, we can identify

with

for any

. Such an isomorphism depends on the choice of a generator

of

.

.

Assume that

and

are locally factorial, regular, and integral. Then the following diagram is (for any choice of generator of

Remarks 2.12

(1)

For the case where

is a cyclic extension of number fields and

3.
Some results from Class field theory

3.1.
Notations and basic results

The results presented in this section are basic and can be found in almost any standard textbook on algebraic number theory covering class field theory (e.g.,
[
9
,
27
,
24
]
). Since conventions vary in the vast literature, the primary goal of this section is to fix the notation, which we will use later.

We chose the ideal-theoretic view on class field theory in this article, as it is more suitable for explicit computations.

Let
K
be a number field and
v
a place of
K
. We denote by
K_{v}
the completion of
K
with respect to the norm induced by the place

. In particular, we have

if

is a real place and

if

is complex. If

is a finite place, we denote the prime ideal of

associated to

by
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
, then
K_{v}
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for a unique prime number
p
. For a finite place
v
, we write

for the normalized discrete valuation associated to
\mathfrak{p}_{v}
. If
v
is a place of
K
and

, then we denote by

the image of

under the embedding

.

Let

be a finite extension of number fields. For a place
v
of
K
and
w
of
L
lying above
v
, we denote by

the degree of the local extension

, by

the inertia degree, and

the respective ramification index. If these numbers are independent of the chosen place

(for example if

is Galois), we simply write

,

, and

.

A

is a formal product

with

and

. The support of a modulus

is the set of places

consists only of finite places and can therefore be identified with an ideal in

, and the support of

consists only of real places of

Let

if for all

\displaystyle\text{if }v_{i}\text{ is finite and}

\displaystyle\alpha_{v_{i}}>0\quad

\displaystyle\text{if }v_{i}\text{ is real.}

Set

. The following definition is a mere reformulation of

4.

\mathbf{cl}^{S}
-Hilbert 90 property

For this section, fix a cyclic extension

of number fields and a set of primes

. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
resp.
\mathcal{O}_{L}
the rings of integers of

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
resp.
\mathcal{O}_{L,S}
be the ring of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Br(\mathcal{O}_{L,S}/\mathcal{O}_{K,S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{v\in S}\Br(L_{v}/K_{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum\inv_{v}}

Proof.

It is an easy exercise in étale cohomology to prove the exactness of the following sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Br(\mathcal{O}_{K,S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Br(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{v\not\in S}\Br(K_{v})}

Now one applies the Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether Theorem to replace

and get the sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Br(\mathcal{O}_{K,S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{v\in S}\Br(K_{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sum\inv_{v}}

The right most arrow is surjective if

contains at least one finite place.
The relative version comes from the first terms of an application of the snake lemma.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

The extension

has the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}_{(p)}
-Hilbert 90 property if and only if the canonical map
H^{2}(G,\mathcal{O}_{L,S}^{\times})_{(p)}\to\bigoplus_{v\in S}H^{2}(G_{v},L^{\times}_{v})_{(p)}
surjects onto the kernel of the sum of the invariant maps
\inv_{v}:H^{2}(G_{v},L^{\times}_{v})_{(p)}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Equivalently, the following sequence is exact

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}(K^{\times}_{v}/N_{v}(L_{v}^{\times}))_{(p)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}

where the first map is induced by

.

\mathbf{cl}^{S}_{(p)}
-Hilbert 90 property between two different extensions:

.

be a cyclic extension having the

. Let

The assumption that

4.1.
An alternative proof of Corollary
4.2

It is possible to prove a special case of Corollary
4.2
without the use of étale cohomology and Brauer groups simply by using elementary results from algebaric number theory, such as the Herbrand-Chevalley formula. I would like to thank Georges Gras for bringing this approach to my attention.

The following ambiguous
S
-class formula can be found in
[
15
]
*Théorème III.1.9:

\displaystyle|\cl^{S}(L)^{G}|=|\cl^{S}(K)|\frac{\prod_{v\in S}n_{v}}{n[\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}:(\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}\cap N(L^{\times}))]}

Let
L/K
be a cyclic extension of number fields and
S
be a finite set of places containing the Archimedean and ramified ones. In addition to the usual assumptions, we also suppose that for one place
v\in S
, we have
n_{v}=[L:K]
. Then the arithmetic norm
\cl^{S}(L)\to\cl^{S}(K)
is surjective. Then we have

\displaystyle|c_{1}(L/K,\mathbf{cl}^{S})|=\frac{|\cl^{S}(L)^{G}|}{|\cl^{S}(K)|}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\prod_{v\in S}n_{v}\right)[\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}:(\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}\cap N(L^{\times}))]^{-1}.

Under our stronger assumptions, we also get the exactness of the following sequence:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}\overset{\alpha}{\longrightarrow}\bigoplus_{v\in S}K_{v}^{\times}/N_{L_{v}/K_{v}}(L_{v}^{\times})\overset{\sum\inv_{v}}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow 0

Since
n_{v}=|K_{v}^{\times}/N_{L_{v}/K_{v}}(L_{v}^{\times})|
, the kernel of
\sum\inv_{v}
has order
\frac{1}{n}\prod_{v\in S}n_{v}
. Furthermore, the kernel of
\alpha
is
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}\cap N(L^{\times})
by the Hasse norm theorem. Thus,
|c_{1}(L/K,\mathbf{cl}^{S})|=1
if and only if
\im\alpha=\ker\sum\inv_{v}
. By comparing prime factorizations on both sides of the equation, it is also possible to deduce the statement for the
\mathbf{cl}^{S}_{(p)}
-Hilbert 90 property.

Remark 4.4
.

Maire performed similar computations in
[
21
]
*Section 5 while studying capitulation of ray class groups in certain extensions and determined in our language
|k^{1}(L/K,\mathbf{cl}^{S}_{\mathfrak{m}})|
, where
L/K
is a cyclic extension, tamely ramified at a set of places
T
and
\mathfrak{m}=\prod_{v\in T}v
. These computations suggest, that in this case

\displaystyle k^{1}(L/K),\mathbf{cl}^{S}_{\mathfrak{m}})\cong\ker(E_{K,\mathfrak{m}}^{S}\to\Br(\mathcal{O}_{L,S}/\mathcal{O}_{K,S})).

We suspect that it should also be true that

is isomorphic to the cokernel of the above map, as is the case for
\mathfrak{m}=1
by Theorem
2.11
, but a proof must follow different lines than the one of Theorem
2.11
.

Remarks 5.2

(1)

(2)

5.1.

We define the cyclotomic

6.2.
Connection to the Spin symbol

In 2013 Friedlander, Iwaniec, Mazur, and Rubin defined the so-called
spin symbol
for ideals (see
[
5
]
). We quickly recall their definition: Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a totally real cyclic Galois extension of degree
\geq 3
and
\sigma
a generator of
\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
. Assume that every totally positive unit
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
(that is,
\varepsilon_{v}>0
for all real places
v
of
K
) is a square, i.e.
\varepsilon=\eta^{2}
for another
\eta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. For an odd principal ideal
\mathfrak{a}
pick a totally positive generator
\alpha
, and define

\displaystyle\mathrm{spin}(\mathfrak{a}):=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{a}^{\sigma}}\right)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\gcd(\alpha,\alpha^{\sigma})\neq 1,\\
1&\text{if }\alpha\text{ is a square }\pmod{\mathfrak{a}^{\sigma}}\text{ and}\\
-1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that the assumptions on the units guarantee that
\mathrm{spin}(\mathfrak{a})
is well defined. The main result of
[
5
]
is the following theorem:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
K
be as before and
n=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. Assume Conjecture
C_{n}
(
[
5
]
*Section 9) is true with exponent
\delta\leq 2/n
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\text{ principal, odd}\\
N\mathfrak{p}\leq x\end{subarray}}\mathrm{spin}(\mathfrak{p})\ll x^{1-\nu+\varepsilon}

where
\nu=\delta/(2n(12n+1))
.

Conjecture
C_{n}
is known to be true, at least in the case
n=3
for
\delta=1/48
. So, it holds unconditionally for cyclic cubic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
. The theorem essentially says that spin takes the values
+1
and
-1
asymptotically equally often.

Let
p
be an odd prime number and assume that there are two prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
above
p
, which are also principal. Then we get a homomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi:E^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\to\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}/\mathcal{U}_{p_{2}}^{2}\cong\{\pm 1\}

similar to before. If we choose a totally positive generator
\pi_{1}
of
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
, then
\mathrm{spin}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})
agrees with the image of
\pi_{1}
under the previous homomorphism. This homomorphism resembles the map
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
defined in Proposition
6.1
. As
\pi_{1}\in E^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
, the map
\varphi
is surjective if
\mathrm{spin}(\pi_{1})=-1
, but this is not necessary, as there could be a (not totally positive) unit
\varepsilon\in E
such that
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}(\varepsilon)=-1
in case
\mathrm{spin}(\pi_{1})=1
.

In fact, this is one crucial point, where our setup differs from that given by the spin: The goal of the assumptions made is to eliminate the contribution from the unit group, whereas the conditions of Theorem
6.3
are in general not satisfiable if we would exclude contributions by units (as they contribute to a
r_{1}+r_{2}-1
dimensional subspace to the domain of
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
).

Furthermore, the “degree”  of the spin symbol is fixed to be
2
. There have been attempts to generalize to higher spin symbols, e.g., in
[
18
]
for imaginary quadratic fields. But also in this case one is limited to “tame”  symbols, i.e. where the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
and the degree
m
are coprime. We are interested in the complementary case, that is, where
m=p
is the rational prime below the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. However, it is natural to ask the following question:

Question 6.7
.

Is it possible to apply the analytic methods used for the usual spin symbol to study the map
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
?

7.

7.1.
Statistical Methods on ranks of random matrices

It seems very hard to determine the map
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
and its image in general, as computing the unit group is already a difficult task. Our goal is to model the linear map
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-f_{\mathfrak{p}}}
of Remark
6.4
as a random matrix.

We first review some techniques from the theory of random matrices over finite fields and apply them afterwards to our problem. Let
k
and
n
be integers and
p
a prime, then we denote by
\mathbf{T}_{p}(n,k)
the random variable associated with the uniform distribution of matrices in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n\times k}
, that is, for any matrix
A\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n\times k}
, we have

\displaystyle\Pr(\mathbf{T}_{p}(n,k)=A)=\frac{1}{p^{n\cdot k}}.

The following lemma is well known and proven by an easy counting argument:

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
k,n
be integers and
p
a prime number. If
k<n
, then we have
\Pr(\rk(\mathbf{T}_{p}(n,k))=n)=0
and if
k\geq n
then

\displaystyle\Pr(\rk(\mathbf{T}_{p}(n,k))=n)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(1-p^{i-k}\right)\geq\left(1-p^{n-1-k}\right)^{n}.

Let
x
be a positive real number, then we introduce another random variable
\mathbf{P}_{n,k}(x)=|\{p\leq x:\rk(\mathbf{T}_{p}(n,k))<n\}|
. It is easy to compute its expected value, as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{P}_{n,k}(x)]=\sum_{p\leq x}\Pr(\rk(\mathbf{T}_{p}(n,k))<n).

Using Lemma
7.1
, it is not hard to estimate the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{P}_{n,k}(x)]
in terms of
n
and
k
:

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
k
and
n
be integers such that
k>n
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{P}_{n,k}(x)]=\mathcal{O}(1)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{P}_{n,n}(x)]=\mathcal{O}(\log\log x).

The implicit constants depend only on
n
.

Heuristic 7.3
.

Let
K
be a number field of signature
(r_{1},r_{2})
and degree
n
, and
p
be an odd prime, which is unramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime above
p
, then the probability that
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
is surjective equals

\displaystyle\Pr(\rk(\mathbf{T}_{p}(r_{1}+r_{2},n-f(\mathfrak{p}|p)))=n-f(\mathfrak{p}|p))

Remarks 7.4
.

(1)

Heuristic
7.3
is correct if
r_{2}>f_{\mathfrak{p}}
for trivial reasons by Remark
6.4
(
2
).

(2)

The above heuristic does not hold in all cases. In most of the cases we have exhibited, it is due to the presence of a certain group action (see Section
7.2
). We believe that this should be the only reason for Heuristic
7.3
to fail. The only counterexamples we were able to find using numerical computations and theoretic arguments are totally imaginary.

Theorem 7.5
.

Assume that Heuristic
7.3
is valid for the totally real field
K
, then with probability
1
there are at most finitely many primes
p
such that the conditions of Theorem
6.3
is not satisfied for
K
and
p
.

Proof.

When we write
p
, we mean an odd prime unramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
, furthermore
\mathfrak{p}
is an arbitrary prime of
K
above
p
. By Proposition
7.2
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p}\Pr(\rk(\tau^{\mathfrak{p}})<n-f(\mathfrak{p}|p)))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p}\Pr(\rk(\mathbf{T}_{p}(n,n-1)<n-1))

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{P}_{n-1,n}(x)]<\infty.

So by the Borel-Cantelli lemma, with probability
1
there are only finitely many
p
such that
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
is not surjective. This finishes the proof.
∎

Since our numerical computations, which we present in section
7.1
, seem to align quite well with Heuristic
7.3
, we conjecture that the statement of Theorem
7.5
should hold true in general.

.

Let
K
be a totally real number field of degree
n
. There exist at most finitely many primes
p
, unramified in

such that for no place
\mathfrak{p}
above
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
is surjective.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
K
be a CM field and

the Galois closure of

. We denote by

and

. The field

is also CM and let

be the complex conjugation in

. Let
p
be an odd prime number which is unramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime of

above
p
. If
\gamma\in\langle H,D(\mathfrak{P}|p)\rangle
, then the map
\tau^{\mathfrak{p}}
is not surjective, where
\mathfrak{p}
is the place of
K
below
\mathfrak{P}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
,…,
\mathfrak{p}_{g}
be the primes of
K
above
p
. After reordering the places
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, we can assume that
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{1}
. We set
K_{0}:=K\cap L^{D(\mathfrak{P}|p)}
, then we have

, as the subgroup

contains

, which restricted to

is the complex conjugation of

. This implies that
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is inert in the extension
K/K_{0}
and therefore also in

.

The subgroup
C_{2}\cong\langle\gamma\rangle
of

acts on
E_{K}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
, as
\gamma
fixes
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
. This action descends to

. Since the group algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[C_{2}]
is semi-simple,
A
decomposes as
A^{+}\oplus A^{-}
, where
A^{+}
is the maximal submodule of

, on which
\gamma
acts as
1
and
A^{-}
the submodule of
A
, on which
\gamma
acts as

. Next, we are going to show that
A^{-}=0
. Consider the inclusion map

. Using a similar argument as in
[

]
*Theorem 4.12, we conclude since
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
is inert in
K/K^{+}
, that the cokernel has order dividing

. Since

is unramified in
K/\mathbb{Q}
it is coprime to
2\cdot|\mu(K)|
. Thus, the induced map

\displaystyle E^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}_{K^{+}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\to E^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}_{K}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}

is an isomorphism. But since
\gamma
acts trivially on the left side and the inclusion commutes with the
\gamma
action, this implies that
\gamma
acts trivially on
E_{K}^{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
A^{-}=0
. Since
\tau^{(1)}
commutes with the action of
\gamma
, we are done if
\gamma
acts non-trivially on
\bigoplus_{i=2}^{g}\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{i},K}/\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{i},K}^{p}
, which is the codomain of
\tau^{(1)}
. If
\gamma
permutes the places
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
non-trivally, we are done. So we can assume
\gamma(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for all

. But this implies, that all
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
are inert in

.

We now show that
\gamma
acts non-trivially on

. This will complete the proof. We compute the invariants

by the following exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2},K}^{p})_{\gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2},K})_{\gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{\gamma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

The exactness follows, as the connecting homomorphism
M_{\gamma}\to H^{1}(C_{2},\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2},K}^{p})
maps a
p
-torsion abelian group into a

-torsion abelian group and is therefore

. This shows that

is a quotient of

. As

, we get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2},K^{+}}/\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2},K^{+}}^{p}\rightarrow M_{\gamma}

. Since

-dimension

,

.

7.3.

In this section, we present the results of numerical experiments studying Heuristic
7.3
. In the first part, we fix the field
K

7.5

The disadvantage of this strategy is that we are only able to check consequences of the heuristic but not the statement itself, since the number of failed cases is very small, which does not allow us to draw conclusions about the exact value of the probability. It only permits conclusions about the asymptotic behavior of the probability
p

In the second part, we fix the prime

and vary the field

The advantages and disadvantages are pretty much opposite to the previous case, as here we can draw conclusions about the precise value of the claimed probability, but there is no canonical way to construct families of number fields. We choose an approach based on Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem, but there are various other methods to group number fields into families.

8.
Concluding remarks

The formalism of cohomological Mackey functors provides a convenient framework for studying questions in group theory, algebra, and number theory. In particular, the Hilbert 90 property proves to a powerful tool for analyzing relations between objects parametrized by certain subgroups of a given group, such as class groups in Galois extensions of number fields. Verifying this property is often substantially easier than determining the objects themselves explicitly, as seen in several instances.

In this paper we have focused on the Hilbert 90 property for
S
-class groups, leading to new insights related to the Gross–Kuz’min conjecture. We expect, however, that similar conclusions can be drawn in other settings. In the context of Iwasawa theory, for instance, it may be fruitful to study the Hilbert 90 property for modules such as Selmer groups or Tate–Shafarevich groups. Indeed, determining
c_{1}
provides information about the coinvariants of the module and hence about the vanishing of its characteristic polynomial at
0
, a phenomenon closely related to the vanishing of
p
-adic
L
-functions in several cases. It would also be interesting to explore potential applications in algebraic geometry.

Finally, in view of Theorem
6.3
, Conjecture
7.6
, and the connection with the Gross–Kuz’min conjecture, it would be highly desirable to construct large families of number fields and primes for which these conditions are satisfied.

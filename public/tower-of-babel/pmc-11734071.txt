Incidental Diagnosis of Massive Mobile Left Ventricle Thrombi Following COVID-19 in a Heart Failure Patient
Authors: Massimo Mapelli, Fabrizio Celeste, Giulia Maiolo, Elisabetta Mancini, Piergiuseppe Agostoni
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 71-year-old woman with dilated cardiomyopathy underwent an echocardiogram showing new onset of multiple mobile left ventricular masses. She experienced a mild COVID-19 infection 1 month before. After a multimodality imaging evaluation, vitamin K antagonist treatment was started, with progressive reduction of the masses without clinical events. A diagnosis of exclusion of left ventricular thrombosis was made.Key Words: cardiac magnetic resonance, contrast echocardiography, COVID-19, left ventricular thrombosis, multimodality imaging

A 71-year-old woman, known to have a nonischemic dilated cardiomyopathy with reduced ejection fraction (EF), optimized medical therapy (including sacubitril/valsartan, β-blockers, dapagliflozin, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist), and a previous transcatheter edge-to-edge mitral valve repair (MitraClip, Abbott Vascular), was admitted after a routine echocardiogram, performed as per the follow-up protocol, showing multiple new-onset, highly mobile left ventricle (LV) masses. The patient, fully vaccinated against SARS-CoV-2, experienced a paucisymptomatic COVID-19 infection 1 month before, followed by a full recovery. A transthoracic echocardiogram performed 3 months earlier showed no LV masses. On admission, she was completely asymptomatic, was in stable sinus rhythm, and had no clinical signs of heart failure or systemic embolization. A multimodality imaging evaluation (contrast echocardiography, cardiac computed tomography, cardiac magnetic resonance) confirmed a severe dilation of the LV with severe EF reduction (end-diastolic/end-systolic volumes: 195/135 mL; EF: 31%), and 3 mobile LV masses (Figure 1, Video 1, Video 2, Video 3, Video 4, Video 5). The largest mass was adhering to the middle portion of the anterolateral wall (maximum diameter: 49 × 15 mm). The mean mitral valve gradient was normal (3 mm Hg). A diagnostic endomyocardial biopsy and cardiac surgery were excluded because of prohibitive embolic/procedural risk, and an anticoagulant treatment with warfarin was started (the following doses per day in the first 3 days were administered: 5 mg, 5 mg, and 2.5 mg, and then according to the international normalized ratio, with a target of 2-3) with a progressive reduction of the masses’ dimensions on transthoracic echocardiography. Thus, a diagnosis of exclusion of LV thrombosis was made. After 2 weeks, a complete resolution of the masses was documented, with no clinical or embolic events (Supplemental Figure 1, Video 6). After 3 years of follow-up, the patient remained in good cardiopulmonary compensation and had no significant clinical events. Serious echocardiograms excluded recurrences of intraventricular thrombosis. Interestingly, she is still on oral anticoagulant therapy, although in this clinical context, long-term continuation of vitamin K antagonists, once the thrombosis has resolved, is not fully supported by the clinical evidence, even given the potential hemorrhagic risk of it. Furthermore, recent evidence suggests that direct oral anticoagulant agents, in addition to their use in pulmonary embolism and atrial fibrillation, may also be useful in patients with LV thrombosis (eg, post–myocardial infarction). Given their favorable risk-benefit profile, the use of direct oral anticoagulants in the initial (therapeutic) phase of ventricular thrombosis and in the subsequent (recurrence prevention) phase holds potential for interesting developments from both clinical and research perspectives.

Take-Home Messages

•Multimodality imaging can effectively identify and characterize left ventricular thrombi, even in asymptomatic heart failure patients after COVID-19 infection, to prevent possible complications (ie, systemic embolization).

Multimodality Imaging Evaluation of LV Thrombi in an HFrEF Patient With a Recent COVID-19 Infection

(A) Transthoracic echocardiography shows multiple LV masses (arrows), the largest one adhering to the middle portion of the anterolateral wall. (B) A contrast echocardiography was performed, highlighting the extreme mobility of the masses and the thin adhesion pedicle to the LV wall. (C) The diagnosis was confirmed by a cardiac CT scan, which also excluded coronary artery disease and radiologic signs of ongoing/previous interstitial COVID-19 pneumonia. (D) A CMR performed 4 days after the start of anticoagulant therapy with warfarin demonstrated the initial reduction in size of the thrombotic formations, despite the presence of artifacts related to the previous MitraClip surgery (asterisk). CMR = cardiac magnetic resonance; CT = computed tomography; HFrEF = heart failure with reduced ejection fraction; LV = left ventricle.

The prothrombotic nature of COVID-19 infection is well known.1, 2, 3 This case demonstrates how vulnerable patients, that is, those with heart failure, may experience thrombotic complications following nonsevere COVID-19 infection and despite having completed the vaccine course. Although currently unconfirmed by dedicated clinical trials, more assiduous echocardiographic monitoring could yield a benefit in selected patients, and a systematic preventive therapeutic approach involving anticoagulants could not be suggested as routine practice.

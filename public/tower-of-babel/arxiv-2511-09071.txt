A lattice algorithm with multiple shifts for function approximation in Korobov spaces Thanks: The work of J. D. is supported by ARC grant DP220101811. The work of T. G. is supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number 23K03210.
Mou Cai, Josef Dick, Takashi Goda

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a novel algorithm for function approximation in a weighted Korobov space based on shifted rank-1 lattice rules. To mitigate aliasing errors inherent in lattice-based Fourier coefficient estimation, we employ 𝒪 ⁡ ( ( log ⁡ N ) d ) \mathcal{O}((\log N)^{d}) good shifts and recover each Fourier coefficient via a least-squares procedure. We show that the resulting approximation achieves the optimal convergence rate for the L ∞ L_{\infty} -approximation error in the worst-case setting, namely 𝒪 ⁡ ( N − α + 1 / 2 + ε ) \mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+1/2+\varepsilon}) for arbitrarily small ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . Moreover, by incorporating random shifts, the algorithm attains the optimal rate for the L 2 L_{2} -approximation error in the randomized setting, which is 𝒪 ⁡ ( N − α + ε ) \mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon}) . Numerical experiments are presented to support the theoretical results.



1
Introduction

We study approximation of smooth periodic functions defined over the
d
-dimensional unit cube
[0,1)^{d}
based on pointwise function evaluations. For an
N
-element point set
P=\{{\bm{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{N}\}\subset[0,1)^{d}
, a general approximation scheme takes the form

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})\approx\mathcal{A}(f)({\bm{x}})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}({\bm{x}})f({\bm{x}}_{i}),

(1.1)

where the coefficient functions
c_{1},\ldots,c_{N}:[0,1)^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
determine the approximation. The error of such an approximation is commonly measured in an
L_{p}
norm, namely

\|f-\mathcal{A}(f)\|_{p}=\left(\int_{[0,1)^{d}}\left|f({\bm{x}})-\mathcal{A}(f)({\bm{x}})\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}\right)^{1/p},

for
p\in[1,\infty)
, with the usual modification for
p=\infty
.

For a Banach space
F
with norm
\|\cdot\|_{F}
, the worst-case error of a deterministic algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is defined as

e_{p}^{\mathrm{wor}}(\mathcal{A},F):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in F\\
\|f\|_{F}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|f-\mathcal{A}(f)\|_{p}.

When we speak of a randomized algorithm, we refer to a pair consisting of a probability space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
and a family of mappings
\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A}^{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
, where each
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
is a deterministic algorithm of the form (
1.1
) for fixed
\omega\in\Omega
. Then, for a Banach space
F
, the random-case error of a randomized algorithm
A
is defined as

e_{p}^{\mathrm{ran}}(\mathcal{A},F):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in F\\
\|f\|_{F}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\left[\|f-\mathcal{A}^{\omega}(f)\|^{p}_{p}\right]\right)^{1/p},

where the expectation is taken with respect to the measure
\mu
.

In what follows, we move from this abstract setting to the specific function space of interest, namely the weighted Korobov space
\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
with smoothness
\alpha>1/2
and positive weights
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots)
. As explained in Section
2.1
, the space contains smooth periodic functions over
[0,1)^{d}
, and every function in the space has an absolutely convergent Fourier series

f({\bm{x}})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{x}}},

where
\widehat{f}(\bm{k})
denotes the
\bm{k}
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
:

\widehat{f}(\bm{k}):=\int_{[0,1)^{d}}f({\bm{x}})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}.

For an index set
A\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and an
N
-element point set
P
, we consider approximating
f\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
by

\displaystyle f({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{\bm{k}\in A}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{x}}}\approx\sum_{\bm{k}\in A}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i,\bm{k}}f({\bm{x}}_{i})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{x}}_{i}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{x}}}:=\mathcal{A}(f)({\bm{x}}),

(1.2)

with quadrature weights
\{w_{1,\bm{k}},\ldots,w_{N,\bm{k}}\}_{\bm{k}\in A}
.
That is, we first restrict the range of
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
to
A
, and then for each
\bm{k}\in A
, we approximate the Fourier coefficient
\widehat{f}(\bm{k})
by quadrature using function values on
P
.

The key issue inherent in using a single rank-1 lattice point set is
aliasing
. For a single rank-1 lattice rule one can simplify the computation of the Fourier coefficients in (
1.2
) as

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f({\bm{x}}_{i})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{x}}_{i}}=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k}+\bm{\ell}),

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{L}^{\perp}
denotes the dual lattice of
P
, whose definition will be given in Section
2.2
.
If there exist distinct frequencies
\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\in A
and some
\bm{\ell}\in\mathcal{L}^{\perp}
such that
\bm{k}+\bm{\ell}=\bm{k}^{\prime}
, then the approximation of the corresponding Fourier coefficients coincides and their approximations cannot be distinguished.

To overcome this aliasing issue, several approaches have been proposed. In
[
10
]
, Kämmerer and Volkmer introduced the use of multiple rank-1 lattice point sets as
P
, and showed that, for
p=\infty
, the resulting algorithm of the form (
1.2
) achieves the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+1/2+\varepsilon})
of the worst-case error. In
[
19
]
, Pan et al. considered choosing multiple shifted rank-1 lattice point sets independently and randomly, and then estimating each Fourier coefficient
\widehat{f}(\bm{k})
for
\bm{k}\in A
by taking the median over the independent estimates. They obtained, for
p=2
, an error bound of
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon})
for any fixed function
f\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
, with high probability, and the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon})
for the random-case error.
Another line of work to mitigate aliasing was recently introduced in
[
1
]
, where a single rank-1 lattice point set of substantially larger size is first generated, and then
n
subsamples are randomly selected. They showed that, for
p=2
, the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon})
for the worst-case error is achieved with high probability.

The aim of this paper is to introduce a new approach to mitigating aliasing errors inherent in lattice-based Fourier coefficient estimation.
The key idea of our approach is to partition the index set
A
into disjoint subsets in such a way that, within each subset, all frequencies are aliased under a single rank-1 lattice rule. For each subset, we then apply at most
\mathcal{O}((\log N)^{d})
good shifts to a rank-1 lattice point set, from which the Fourier coefficients are recovered through a least-squares procedure.
The main difference from the multiple rank-1 lattice algorithm in
[
10
]
is that, instead of taking the union of several rank-1 lattice point sets, our method relies on a single rank-1 lattice point set. The aliasing issue is then addressed by applying multiple shifts and subsequently performing a least-squares procedure to estimate the Fourier coefficients.
We prove that the proposed algorithm achieves, for
p=\infty
, the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+1/2+\varepsilon})
of the worst-case error (Theorem
4.2
). Furthermore, by incorporating additional random shifts, we show that the resulting randomized algorithm attains the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon})
of the random-case error for
p=2
(Theorem
4.3
).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
reviews the necessary background, and Section
3
presents the algorithms and proves that suitable generating vectors and shifts can be found with high probability. Section
4
establishes optimal convergence rates for both the deterministic
L_{\infty}
error and the randomized
L_{2}
error. We conclude this paper with numerical experiments in Section
5
, which validate our theoretical results.

1.1
Literature review

Originally, in
[
14
]
, Kuo, Sloan and Woźniakowski studied approximations of the form (
1.2
), with
P
being a single rank-1 lattice point set and
w_{i,\bm{k}}=1/N
for all
i
and
\bm{k}
. They proved that, for
p=2
, the resulting algorithm achieves a worst-case error of
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha/2+\varepsilon})
for arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
. Later, Byrenheid, Kämmerer, Ullrich, Volkmer showed in
[
2
]
that, as long as a single rank-1 lattice point set is used as
P
, any general algorithm of the form (
1.1
) cannot attain a convergence rate better than
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha/2})
, which is only half of the best possible rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon})
. By employing randomized single rank-1 lattice point sets, Cai, Goda and Kazashi
[
3
]
recently proved that, for
p=2
, the randomized lattice-based algorithm achieves a random-case error of
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha(2\alpha+1)/(4\alpha+1)+\varepsilon})
, which improves upon
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha/2})
for any
\alpha>1/2
, yet remains far from the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon})
. The case
p=\infty
has also been studied, for instance in
[
15
,
20
]
, where a single rank-1 lattice point set is used as
P
, and a worst-case error of
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha(2\alpha-1)/(4\alpha-1)+\varepsilon})
was obtained. Again, this convergence rate is far from the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+1/2+\varepsilon})
achievable in the case
p=\infty
. Fast CBC constructions underpin most practical implementations
[
17
]
. See also the modern textbook treatment which consolidates these ideas and their extensions
[
5
]
.

To circumvent aliasing on a single lattice, one may combine a few
different
lattices. Kämmerer and Volkmer proved that sampling along multiple rank-1 lattices removes aliasing and achieves the
optimal
worst-case rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+1/2+\varepsilon})
for
L_{\infty}
-approximation in Korobov spaces
[
10
]
. This line builds on a reconstruction theory guaranteeing that hyperbolic-cross trigonometric polynomials can be stably recovered from rank-1 lattice samples
[
11
]
and on single-lattice approximation bounds for hyperbolic-cross index sets
[
9
]
. The algorithmic foundations of multiple rank-1 lattices for exact recovery (and their use in sparse FFT-style methods) are developed in
[
12
]
, and a deterministic near-optimal construction was given in
[
7
]
.

Randomization offers another route to de-aliasing while keeping a single generating vector. For
L_{2}
-approximation, Cai, Goda and Kazashi analyzed a randomized single-lattice method and proved rates strictly better than the deterministic single-lattice barrier, though still below optimal
[
3
]
. Pan, Kritzer and Goda subsequently introduced a robust
median-of-means
estimator that evaluates the same lattice under many independent random shifts; they established high-probability and randomized
L_{2}
error bounds at the optimal rate
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon})
, with a follow-up “universal” result that eliminates tuning to the smoothness
[
19
,
18
]
. Random shifting itself is classical in randomized QMC, beginning with Cranley–Patterson and refined in later distributional analyses; these provide the probabilistic underpinning for shift-based variance/error control
[
4
,
16
]
.

A complementary strategy is to start from one
very large
lattice and then select
N
points uniformly at random. Bartel, Gilbert, Kuo and Sloan proved that such minimal random subsamples achieve the optimal
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha+\varepsilon})
rate for worst-case
L_{2}
-approximation with high probability, giving another single-lattice path to optimality
[
1
]
.

Least-squares reconstruction from lattice samples sits naturally inside a general RKHS framework and also extends to nonperiodic settings via tent and cosine transforms. Kuo, Migliorati, Nobile and Nuyens provide a unified view of integration, reconstruction and approximation from lattice data, together with efficient CBC strategies tailored to these tasks
[
13
]
. Finally, the optimal exponents quoted above match the best-possible rates known from hyperbolic-cross approximation and sampling-number theory for spaces with dominating mixed smoothness; see, e.g., the survey
[
6
]
and recent sampling-number advances
[
8
]
.

Within this landscape, our paper addresses aliasing
within the fibers
of a single rank-1 lattice by using only
\mathcal{O}((\log N)^{d})
carefully chosen shifts per fiber and recovering the Fourier coefficients through small least-squares solves. This preserves the sampling simplicity of a single lattice (as in randomized approaches) while matching the optimal rates previously known from multiple-lattice schemes in
L_{\infty}
and from randomized analyses in
L_{2}
.

2
Notation and background

Throughout the paper we denote vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by boldface letters such as
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
. For
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote their standard inner product by
{\bm{x}}\cdot{\bm{y}}=x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}y_{d}
. For a subset
u\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
{\bm{x}}_{u}
denotes the vector
(x_{j})_{j\in u}
. For a complex matrix
B\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
, we denote its Hermitian transpose by
B^{H}=\overline{B}^{\top}
.

In the following, we first define the weighted Korobov space
\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
and introduce rank-1 lattice point sets and some of their properties.

2.1
Weighted Korobov space

For a smoothness parameter
\alpha>1/2
and positive weights
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots)
, we define

r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k}):=\prod_{j\in u(\bm{k})}\frac{|k_{j}|^{\alpha}}{\gamma_{j}},

for
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where we set
u(\bm{k})=\{j\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}:k_{j}\neq 0\}
, and the empty product is set to
1
.

Let
f:[0,1)^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
1
-periodic function given by an absolutely convergent Fourier series

f({\bm{x}})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{x}}},

where
\widehat{f}(\bm{k})
denotes the
\bm{k}
-th Fourier coefficient.
We define the weighted Korobov space
\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
of Fourier series with smoothness parameter
\alpha
as the set of
1
-periodic functions for which

\displaystyle\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}:=\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}\,r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}^{2}(\bm{k})\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

(2.1)

Note that
\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, with reproducing kernel

K_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}):=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot({\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}})}}{r^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})}

and inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\overline{\widehat{g}(\bm{k})}\,r^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k}).

Furthermore, if
\alpha>1/2
is an integer, the norm
\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
can alternatively be expressed in terms of (square-integrable) mixed partial derivatives of
f
up to order
\alpha
in each variable; see for instance
[
5
, Proposition 2.17]
.

Regarding the index set
A
in (
1.2
), we consider the following special form: for a positive real number
M>0
, let

\displaystyle A:=A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}=\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})<M\},

(2.2)

where
M
will be chosen appropriately later, depending on
N,d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}
.
The following lemma, adapted from
[
14
, Lemma 1]
, provides an upper bound on the number of elements in
A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha>1/2
, positive weights
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\ldots)
, and
M>0
, let
A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
be defined by (
2.2
). Then, for any
\lambda>1/\alpha
, we have

|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|\leq M^{\lambda}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+2\gamma_{j}^{\lambda}\zeta(\alpha\lambda)\right),

where
\zeta(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-s}
denotes the Riemann zeta function.

If we choose
M
as the infimum of all
M^{\ast}>0
such that
|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M^{\ast}}|>N
, that is

M:=M(\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N)=\inf\{M^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}:|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M^{\ast}}|>N\},

(2.3)

then
|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|\leq N
.
Then it follows from Lemma
2.1
that this choice of
M
satisfies

\displaystyle M\geq\sup_{1/\alpha<\lambda\leq 1}\left(N\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+2\gamma_{j}^{\lambda}\zeta(\alpha\lambda))^{-1}\right)^{1/\lambda}=:Z.

(2.4)

2.2
Rank-1 lattice point set

A rank-1 lattice point set in dimension
d
with
N
points is defined by a generating vector
\bm{g}\in\{1,2,\dots,N-1\}^{d}
as

\mathcal{L}_{N}(\bm{g})=\left\{\left\{\frac{n\bm{g}}{N}\right\}\in[0,1)^{d}\mid n=0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\},

where
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the fractional part of a real number
x\geq 0
and is applied componentwise to a vector. For a shift vector
{\bm{y}}\in[0,1)^{d}
, the shifted rank-1 lattice point set is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{N}(\bm{g},{\bm{y}})=\left\{\left\{\frac{n\bm{g}}{N}+{\bm{y}}\right\}\in[0,1)^{d}\mid n=0,1,\ldots,N-1\right\}.

Here, (shifted) rank-1 lattice point sets satisfy a useful property in the context of Fourier approximation.
For any
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
{\bm{y}}\in[0,1)^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}(\bm{g},{\bm{y}})}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{x}}}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{y}}}&\text{if $\bm{k}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(2.5)

where the
dual lattice

\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})
is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})=\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid\bm{g}\cdot\bm{k}\equiv 0\pmod{N}\}.

For given
\alpha
and
\bm{\gamma}
, recall that the hyperbolic cross set
A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
is defined as in (
2.2
) and that
M
is defined as a function of
\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N
in (
2.3
). For any
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
we define the
fiber
in
A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
along
\bm{\ell}
by

\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g}):=\{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}:\bm{k}\cdot\bm{g}\equiv\bm{\ell}\cdot\bm{g}\pmod{N}\}.

In particular, if
\bm{\ell}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
, then we have
\bm{\ell}\in\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
. Note that the fiber can also be expressed as

\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})=A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}\cap\left(\bm{\ell}+\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\right).

(2.6)

Furthermore, we define the fiber length
R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
and the maximal fiber length
R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
,

R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})=|\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})|,\quad\text{and}\quad R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})=\max_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g}).

The maximal fiber length can also be written as
R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})=\max_{\bm{\ell}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
.

Let
\bm{\ell}^{(1)},\bm{\ell}^{(2)},\ldots,\bm{\ell}^{(J)}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
be such that

\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(i)};\bm{g})\,\cap\,\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})=\emptyset,

for
i\neq j
, and

\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})=A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}.

This partition of the hyperbolic cross into fibers is instrumental in addressing the aliasing phenomenon: each fiber corresponds to a set of frequencies that are indistinguishable under the single rank-1 lattice rule, and in our algorithm, we handle each fiber separately by applying suitable shifts to accurately estimate the corresponding Fourier coefficients.

Remark 2.1
.

Since the sets
\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})
partition the set
A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
, we obtain

|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|=\sum_{j=1}^{J}|\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})|\geq R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g}).

3
The approximation algorithms and the construction of shifted lattice point sets

In our approximation algorithm, we approximate the Fourier coefficients in the set
A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
using a lattice rule with generating vector
\bm{g}
. However, a lattice rule cannot distinguish between frequencies that lie in the same fiber
\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
, leading to aliasing. To address this, we employ shifted lattice rules. We begin by introducing a technique that eliminates aliasing among the Fourier coefficients.

Consider
\bm{\ell}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
such that
R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})>1
and let
\bm{\ell}\neq\bm{k}\in\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
. Then the frequencies
\bm{k},\bm{\ell}
are aliased since
\bm{k}\cdot\bm{g}\equiv\bm{\ell}\cdot\bm{g}\pmod{N}
. When approximating Fourier coefficients using this lattice rule, this means that

\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell})\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(\{n\bm{g}/N\})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}n\bm{\ell}\cdot\bm{g}/N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(\{n\bm{g}/N\})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}n\bm{k}\cdot\bm{g}/N}\approx\widehat{f}(\bm{k}),

i.e., all the Fourier coefficients with frequencies in the same fiber
\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
are approximated by the same value, as the lattice rule cannot distinguish between them.

To avoid this problem,
[
10
]
used multiple lattice rules i.e., if
\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}
are aliased with respect to a generating vector
\bm{g}_{1}
, then one can use a lattice rule with a different generating vector
\bm{g}_{2}
for which the frequencies
\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}
are not aliased. In this paper we use a different approach, namely we associate with each frequency
\bm{\ell}_{m}
in a given fiber, say
\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})=\{\bm{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{v}\}
(where
v=R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
), shifts
{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\in[0,1)^{d}
, where
m=1,2,\ldots,v
and
s=1,2,\ldots,S
. Our analysis below requires us to choose
S>1
as
S=1
is not sufficient for being able to reconstruct the Fourier coefficients with frequencies in
\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
. Using approximations based on shifted lattice rules, we obtain approximations of
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{m})
,
m=1,2,\ldots,v
, as we show in the following section.

3.1
The deterministic approximation algorithm

We illustrate the idea on how to derive an approximation method for all the frequencies in
\bm{\ell}_{1},\bm{\ell}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{v}\in\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})
.
For
m=1,2,\ldots,v
and
s=1,2,\ldots,S
we define
1
1

1

An alternative algorithm would be to use
N^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(\{n\bm{g}/N+{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})\exp(-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{m}\cdot(n\bm{g}/N+{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}))
. Similar results hold for this variation; however, the algorithm becomes slightly more involved.

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{m},{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)})=

3.3
Existence of good generating vectors

In this section we show that generating vectors
\bm{g}
for which
R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})=\mathcal{O}((\log N)^{d-1})
can be found with high probability by randomly selecting a vector from
\{1,2,\ldots,N-1\}^{d}
.

For
\bm{g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{d})\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}^{d}
, we define the figure of merit

\rho=\rho(\bm{g}):=\min_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{\ell}).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<\delta<1
,
N>2
be a prime number and let
M\geq 1
be such that
|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|\leq(N-1)(1-\delta)
. If
\bm{g}
is sampled uniformly from
\{1,\dots,N-1\}^{d}
, then

\mathbb{P}\left(\rho(\bm{g})>M\right)\geq\delta.

(3.4)

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\bm{0}\neq\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}\pmod{N}
. If
\bm{g}
is uniform on
\{1,\dots,N-1\}^{s}
, then

\mathbb{P}\left(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{g}\equiv 0\pmod{N}\right)\leq\frac{1}{N-1}\leq\frac{2}{N}.

Proof.

Assume that
k_{j}\not\equiv 0\pmod{N}
. Then for each choice of
g_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}\setminus\{j\}
, there is at most one
g_{j}\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
such that
\bm{k}\cdot\bm{g}\equiv 0\pmod{N}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

For
M\geq 1
let

X_{M}(\bm{g}):=\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\mathbf{1}\{\bm{k}\cdot\bm{g}\equiv 0\pmod{N}\}.

Observe that

\{\bm{g}:\rho(\bm{g})\leq M\}=\{\bm{g}:X_{M}(\bm{g})\geq 1\}.

Thus

\mathbb{P}(\rho(\bm{g})\leq M)\leq\mathbb{E}[X_{M}].

Using Lemma
3.2
, the union bound leads to

\mathbb{E}[X_{M}]\leq\frac{|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|}{N-1}\leq 1-\delta,

therefore

\mathbb{P}(\rho(\bm{g})>M)\geq\delta.

∎

3.4
Construction of generating vectors with large figure of merit

Rather than selecting
\bm{g}
randomly, one can also use a component-by-component construction minimizing
P_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}
given by

P_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{1}{r^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})}.

This quantity is the squared worst-case error for the integration problem by a rank-1 lattice rule in the weighted Korobov space
\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
.
The largest term in this sum is
1/\rho^{2}(\bm{g})
, therefore

\frac{1}{\rho^{2}(\bm{g})}<P_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g}).

Since
P_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
is convergent for any
\alpha>1/2
, we can use a component-by-component construction to find
\bm{g}
with small
P_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
, and therefore large
\rho(\bm{g})
. Note that for
\alpha
being a positive integer, the sum
P_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
can be analytically computed as

\displaystyle P_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})

\displaystyle=-1+\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{1}{r^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{g})n/N}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{|k|^{2\alpha}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi ikg_{j}n/N}\right)

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{2}\frac{(-1)^{\alpha+1}(2\pi)^{2\alpha}}{(2\alpha)!}B_{2\alpha}\left(\left\{\frac{ng_{j}}{N}\right\}\right)\right),

where
B_{2\alpha}
denotes the Bernoulli polynomial of degree
2\alpha
. We list the CBC construction in Algorithm
1
. Based on the work of Nuyens and Cools
[
17
]
, computing

for all

(with given

) can be done with
\mathcal{O}(N\log N)
arithmetic operations with the help of the fast Fourier transform, and the total cost of generating
\bm{g}
with large figure of merit is of
\mathcal{O}(dN\log N)
.

\displaystyle g_{s+1}:=\mathrm{argmin}_{g\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}}\;P_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}((g_{1},\ldots,g_{s},g)).

For a generating vector

Lemma 3.3

Let

\displaystyle=|S_{\bm{\ell}}\cap A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq u\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\mu}_{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{|u|}\\
\sum_{j\in u}\mu_{j}=m\end{subarray}}|S_{\bm{\ell}}\cap B((\bm{\mu}_{u},\bm{0}_{-u}))|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{\emptyset\neq u\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}2^{m}\binom{m-1}{|u|-1}\min\left\{\frac{2^{-|u|}}{\rho(\bm{g})^{1/\alpha}},\prod_{j\in u}\gamma_{j}^{1/\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{m}}{m}\sum_{u\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}}m^{|u|}\min\left\{\frac{2^{-|u|}}{\rho(\bm{g})^{1/\alpha}},\prod_{j\in u}\gamma_{j}^{1/\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{m}}{m}\min\left\{\frac{(1+m/2)^{d}}{\rho(\bm{g})^{1/\alpha}},\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+\gamma_{j}^{1/\alpha}m)\right\}.

(3.9)

From Lemma
3.1
we have, with probability at least
\delta\in(0,1)
, that
\rho(\bm{g})>M
and therefore

\frac{2^{m}}{\rho(\bm{g})^{1/\alpha}}\leq 2\left(\frac{M}{\rho(\bm{g})}\right)^{1/\alpha}\leq 2.

Thus

R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})\leq\min\left\{2\frac{(1+m/2)^{d}}{m},2\frac{M^{1/\alpha}}{m}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+\gamma_{j}^{1/\alpha}m)\right\}.

Consider now the case where
\bm{g}
is found by Algorithm
1
. For
M
given by (
3.8
), Lemma
2.1
implies that
|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|\leq N/2
and
\rho(\bm{g})\geq M
follows from (
3.5
). The bound on
R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
now follows from (
3.5
).
∎

Since the length
m
satisfies
\frac{1}{\alpha}\log_{2}M\leq m<1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log_{2}M,
when
M
is chosen as (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle 2\frac{(1+m/2)^{d}}{m}\geq C(\log N)^{d-1},

where
C
is a generic constant, thus the maximum length of fiber is of
\mathcal{O}((\log N)^{d-1}).

3.6
Existence of good shifts

In the following, assume that
\bm{g}
is a generating vector satisfying
R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\leq C(\log N)^{d-1}
, whose existence was shown in Lemma
3.4
. Let
\bm{\ell}^{(1)},\bm{\ell}^{(2)},\ldots,\bm{\ell}^{(J)}
be such that
\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(i)};\bm{g})\cap\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
and
\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})=A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M},
and
v_{j}=|\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})|
.

Consider a fiber
\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})=\{\bm{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\}
.
To prove the existence of good shifts
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\}
, we need a lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix

G:=B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})^{H}B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})=\left[\sum_{m=1}^{v_{j}}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}(\bm{\ell}_{i}-\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})\cdot{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}}\right]_{i,i^{\prime}=1,2,\ldots,v_{j}}.

Define the entry in row
i
and column
i^{\prime}
by

g(\bm{\ell}_{i},\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})=\sum_{m=1}^{v_{j}}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}(\bm{\ell}_{i}-\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})\cdot{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}}.

(3.10)

The diagonal elements of
G
are given by
g(\bm{\ell}_{i},\bm{\ell}_{i})=\sum_{m=1}^{v_{j}}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}(\bm{\ell}_{i}-\bm{\ell}_{i})\cdot{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}}=v_{j}S
.

Gershgorin’s circle theorem tells us that every eigenvalue
\lambda
lies in one of the disks

|\lambda-v_{j}S|=|\lambda-g(\bm{\ell}_{i},\bm{\ell}_{i})|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i^{\prime}=1\\
i^{\prime}\neq i\end{subarray}}^{v_{j}}|g(\bm{\ell}_{i},\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})|\leq(v_{j}-1)\max_{i\neq i^{\prime}}\left|g(\bm{\ell}_{i},\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})\right|,

hence

\lambda\geq v_{j}S-(v_{j}-1)\max_{i\neq i^{\prime}}|g(\bm{\ell}_{i},\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})|.

(3.11)

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
S=\lceil 2KR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\log N\rceil
for some constant
K>1
.
Then there exist points

\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}\in[0,1)^{d}\quad m=1,\dots,R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g}),\;s=1,\dots,S,

such that for all
\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
, if

\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})=\{\bm{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\}
with

v_{j}=R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})
, then for all
i\neq i^{\prime}
,

|g(\bm{\ell}_{i},\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})|=\left|\sum_{m=1}^{v}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi i\,(\bm{\ell}_{i}-\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})\cdot\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}}\right|\;\leq\;S.

Further, assume that
{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\in[0,1)^{d}
,
m;=1,\ldots,R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
and
s=1,\ldots,S
are chosen i.i.d. uniformly distributed. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sum_{m=1}^{v}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi i\,(\bm{\ell}_{i}-\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})\cdot\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}}\right|\;\leq\;S\quad\forall j=1,\ldots,J,\forall\{\bm{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\}\in\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g}),\forall i\neq i^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-N^{1-K}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{2}.

Proof.

Fix
\bm{\ell}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
and let

\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell};\bm{g})=\{\bm{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{v}\}
, with
v\leq R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
.
For each unordered pair
i\neq i^{\prime}
, define

\bm{h}=\bm{\ell}_{i}-\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}}\neq\mathbf{0}.

Because
\bm{\ell}_{i}\cdot\bm{g}\equiv\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{g}\pmod{N}
,
we have

\bm{h}\cdot\bm{g}\equiv 0\pmod{N}.

Thus the number of such nonzero
\bm{h}
vectors within that residue class
is at most

\binom{v}{2}\leq\frac{v^{2}}{2}\leq\frac{R^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})}{2}.

Let us choose all
\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}
independently and uniformly at random in

[0,1)^{d}
. For any fixed nonzero
\bm{h}
, the random variable

X_{\bm{h}}=\sum_{m=1}^{v}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi i\,\bm{h}\cdot\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}}

has mean

\mathbb{E}[X_{\bm{h}}]=0,

since each factor has zero mean, and is a sum of
vS
independent terms of
unit magnitude.

By Hoeffding’s or Bernstein’s inequality (or by viewing real and imaginary
parts separately), we deduce for fixed
\bm{h}
and any threshold
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\{\,|X_{\bm{h}}|\geq t\}\leq 2\exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac{t^{2}}{2vS}\bigr).

Taking
t=S
and noting
v\leq R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
gives

\mathbb{P}\{|X_{\bm{h}}|\geq S\}\leq 2\exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac{S}{2R}\bigr),

where
R:=R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
.

Across all residue classes, the total number of nonzero
\bm{h}
is bounded
by

|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|\frac{R^{2}}{2}\leq\frac{NR^{2}}{2}.

Hence, by union bounding

\mathbb{P}\bigl\{\exists\bm{h}\neq\bm{0}:|X_{\bm{h}}|\geq S\bigr\}\leq\frac{NR^{2}}{2}2\exp\!\bigl(-\tfrac{S}{2R}\bigr).

Now choose
S
sufficiently large
S=\lceil 2KR\log N\rceil
for fixed
K
to be specified later. Then

\frac{S}{2R}\geq K\log N,

and therefore

\mathbb{P}\bigl\{\exists\bm{h}\neq\bm{0}:|X_{\bm{h}}|\geq S\bigr\}\leq NR^{2}\exp(-K\log N)=N^{1-K}R^{2}.

The last expression is smaller than
1
if
K>1
and
N
are sufficiently large. Hence the probability that
|X_{\bm{h}}|<S
is positive and therefore there exists a point set
\{\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}\}
such that for every nonzero
\bm{h}

arising from any pair in any residue class,

\left|\sum_{m=1}^{v}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi i\,\bm{h}\cdot\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}}\right|<S.

More precisely, the probability that an i.i.d. uniformly chosen set of vectors
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\}
satisfies
|X_{\bm{h}}|<S
for all cases is at least
1-N^{1-K}R^{2}
.
∎

Lemma
3.5
shows the existence of points
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\}
for which
|g(\bm{\ell}_{i},\bm{\ell}_{i^{\prime}})|
is always bounded by
S
. In particular, (
3.11
) implies that every eigenvalue
\lambda
of
G
is at least
v_{j}S-(v_{j}-1)S\geq v_{j}\geq 1
. In turn we obtain the existence of a point set
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\}
such that

\left\|\left[B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})^{H}B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})\right]^{-1}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{v_{j}}\leq 1.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
S=\lceil 2KR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\log N\rceil
for some constant
K>1
. Let
N
be a prime number and
\bm{g}
be the generating vector of a rank 1 lattice point set with maximal fiber length
R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
. Then there exists a set of points
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}:m=1,\ldots,R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g}),s=1,\ldots,S\}
such that

\left\|\left[B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})^{H}B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})\right]^{-1}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{v_{j}}\leq 1,

where
v_{j}=|\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})|
.

For points
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\}
chosen i.i.d. uniformly at random

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\left[B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})^{H}B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})\right]^{-1}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{v_{j}},\,\forall j=1,\ldots,J\right)\geq 1-N^{1-K}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{2}.

Remark 3.2
.

The total number
p
of points used in the algorithms
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{\Delta}}
satisfies

N<p=NR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})S\leq NR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\lceil 2KR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\log N\rceil\leq CKN(\log N)^{2d-1}

(3.12)

for some constant
C>0
independent of
N,p
, and therefore

\frac{p}{CK(\log p)^{2d-1}}\leq N<p.

(3.13)

4
Error analysis

In the following two sections, we prove bounds on the approximation error in the deterministic
L_{\infty}
-norm setting and in the randomized
L_{2}
-norm setting.

4.1
Convergence analysis for the worst case error in
L_{\infty}
norm

We would like to analyze the worst-case
L_{\infty}
-approximation error as

e_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wor}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\\
\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\sup_{{\bm{x}}\in[0,1]^{d}}|f({\bm{x}})-\mathcal{A}(f)({\bm{x}})|.

We need the following lemma, whose proof can be found, for instance, in
[
5
, Chapter 14]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
M\geq 1
be given. For any
q\in(1/(2\alpha),1)
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\frac{1}{r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}^{2}(\bm{k})}\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma_{1}M)^{(1/q-1)/\alpha}}\frac{q}{1-q}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1+2\gamma_{j}^{q}\zeta(2\alpha q)\right)^{1/q}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\alpha>1/2
and
N
be a prime number. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.

Assume that
M
is chosen such that
|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|\leq(N-1)(1-\delta)
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
2^{m-1}<M^{1/\alpha}\leq 2^{m}
. Let
\bm{g}\in\{1,2,\ldots,N-1\}^{d}
be chosen uniformly at random. Let
S=\lceil 2KR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\log N\rceil
and
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\in[0,1)^{d}:m=1,\ldots,R(\bm{g}),s=1,\ldots,S\}
be chosen i.i.d. uniformly at random.

Then with probability
\delta
,
\bm{g}
is such that
R_{\alpha\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
satisfies (
3.7
), and with probability
1-N^{1-K}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{2}
, the shifts
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\}
are such that
\|[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1
. If both of these are satisfied, then we have for
\tau\in(0,1]

\mathrm{e}_{\infty}^{\mathrm{wor}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}})\leq C_{d,\bm{\gamma},\tau}(\log p)^{7d/2+1/2}p^{-\alpha+1/2+\tau},

(4.1)

where
\mathcal{A}(f)
is given by (
3.2
) and
p
denotes the total number of function evaluations.

If
\bm{g}
is constructed using Algorithm
1
,
M
is chosen as in (
3.8
), and
\{\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}\}
is such that
\|[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1
, then (
4.1
) also holds.

Proof.

The error can be separated into two parts

\displaystyle\|f-\mathcal{A}(f)\|_{L_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k})|+\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|.

(4.2)

For the truncation error
\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}(r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k}))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\frac{1}{(r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k}))^{2}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\frac{1}{(r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k}))^{2}}\right)^{1/2}.

(4.3)

The last factor on the right side of (
4.1
) can be estimated by Lemma
4.1
.

For the aliasing error
\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k})|,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k})|

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}})\end{pmatrix}-\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}},{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)})\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{1},

(4.5)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{m},{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(\{n\bm{g}/N+{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{m}\cdot n\bm{g}/N}=\sum_{\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}_{m}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}}.

We have

\displaystyle\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\left[\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)})\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}},{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)})\end{pmatrix}-B_{j}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}})\end{pmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}_{m}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}_{m};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}}\right)_{m=1,\ldots,v_{j},s=1,\ldots,S}

\displaystyle=\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}}\right)_{m=1,\ldots,v_{j},s=1,\ldots,S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}}\end{pmatrix}}_{=:\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}}.

For
Z_{j}:=\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}^{\perp}_{N}(\bm{g})\},
let

e_{j}(\bm{k})=\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\bm{a}_{\bm{k}},

and

E_{j}
be the
v_{j}\times\infty
matrix

E_{j}=\left[\frac{e_{j}(\bm{k})}{r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})}\right]_{\bm{k}\in Z_{j}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}

and

F_{j}=\left[\widehat{f}(\bm{k})r_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})\right]_{\bm{k}\in Z_{j}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sqrt{\left|F_{j}^{H}\left(E_{j}^{H}E_{j}\right)F_{j}\right|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|F_{j}\|_{2}\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(E_{j}^{H}E_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\operatorname{trace}(E_{j}^{H}E_{j})\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|F_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
\lambda_{\max}
denotes the largest eigenvalue and

\operatorname{trace}(E_{j}^{H}E_{j})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in Z_{j}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\frac{\|e_{j}(\bm{k})\|_{2}^{2}}{r^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})}.

Lemma
3.6
implies that there is a point set
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s}\}
such that
\|[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1/v_{j}\leq 1
.
Therefore

\|e_{j}(\bm{k})\|_{2}\leq Sv_{j}^{1/2}\leq S(R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g}))^{1/2}.

Note that
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|F_{j}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}^{2},
and

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\operatorname{trace}(E_{j}^{H}E_{j})\leq S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\frac{1}{r^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})},

which leads the following bound on the aliasing error

\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k})|\leq\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}S\sqrt{R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}.N}(\bm{g})}\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\frac{1}{r^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})}\right)^{1/2}.

(4.6)

Using (
4.2
), (
4.1
), and (
4.6
) and Lemma
4.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\|f-\mathcal{A}(f)\|_{L_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}[1+S\sqrt{R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})}]\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\frac{1}{r^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{k})}\right)^{1/2}.

By choosing

M=\sup_{1/\alpha<\lambda\leq 1}\left((N-1)(1-\delta)\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+2\gamma_{j}^{\lambda}\zeta(\alpha\lambda))^{-1}\right)^{1/\lambda}

(4.7)

we obtain
|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|\leq(N-1)(1-\delta)
. The result now follows from (
4.7
),
S=\lceil 2KR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\log N\rceil
, (
3.7
), and (
3.13
).

The result for
\bm{g}
constructed by Algorithm
1
follows similarly.

∎

4.2
Convergence analysis for the random case error

Since the algorithm is randomized, we consider the random case error

\mathrm{e}_{2}^{\mathrm{ran}}((\mathcal{A}_{\bm{\Delta}})_{\bm{\Delta}},\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\\
\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}\|f-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{\Delta}}(f)\|_{2}^{2}}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\alpha>1/2
and
N
be a prime number. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
.

Assume that
M
is chosen such that
|A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}|\leq(N-1)(1-\delta)
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
2^{m-1}<M^{1/\alpha}\leq 2^{m}
. Let
\bm{g}\in\{1,2,\ldots,N-1\}^{d}
be chosen uniformly at random. Let
S=\lceil 2KR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\log N\rceil
and
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\in[0,1)^{d}:m=1,\ldots,R(\bm{g}),s=1,\ldots,S\}
be chosen i.i.d. uniformly at random.

Then with probability
\delta
,
\bm{g}
is such that
R_{\alpha\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
satisfies (
3.7
), and with probability
1-N^{1-K}R^{2}_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})
, the shifts
\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\}
are such that
\|[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1
. If both of these are satisfied, then we have for
\tau\in(0,1]

e_{2}^{\mathrm{ran}}((\mathcal{A}_{\bm{\Delta}})_{\bm{\Delta}},\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}})\leq C_{d,\bm{\gamma},\tau}(\log p)^{5d/2-3/2}p^{-\alpha+\tau},

(4.8)

where
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{\Delta}}
is given by (
3.3
) and
p
denotes the total number of function evaluations.

If
\bm{g}
is constructed using Algorithm
1
,
M
is chosen as in (
3.8
), and
\{\bm{y}_{m}^{(s)}\}
is such that
\|[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1
, then (
4.8
) also holds.

Proof.

The mean square approximation error for a given function
f\in\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}\left[\|f-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{\Delta}}(f)\|_{2}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}\left[|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\},\bm{\Delta})|^{2}\right]+\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}.

For the second term we have

\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}\frac{r^{2}_{\alpha}(\bm{k})}{r^{2}_{\alpha}(\bm{k})}\leq\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}r^{2}_{\alpha}(\bm{k})\leq\frac{\|f\|^{2}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}}{M^{2}}.

To analyse the first term, let
\bm{\ell}^{(1)},\bm{\ell}^{(2)},\ldots,\bm{\ell}^{(J)}
be such that
\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(i)};\bm{g})\cap\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
and
\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})=A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}
. Let
v_{j}=|\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})|
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\},\bm{\Delta})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\},\bm{\Delta})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}})\end{pmatrix}-D(\bm{\Delta})^{-1}\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{1}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}},{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\|D(\bm{\Delta})\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}})\end{pmatrix}-\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{1}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}},{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{m},{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}+\bm{\Delta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(\{n\bm{g}/N+{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}+\bm{\Delta}\})\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{m}\cdot(n\bm{g}/N+{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}+\bm{\Delta})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}_{m}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}_{m})\cdot({\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}+\bm{\Delta})}.

Let
\{\bm{\ell}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\}=\Gamma_{\alpha,M}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})
where
v_{j}=R(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})
. Then
\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}_{m}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})
implies that
\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})
. In the following we write
B_{j}:=B(\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\})
. Then

\displaystyle\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\left[\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{1}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}},{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})\end{pmatrix}-B_{j}D(\bm{\Delta})\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}})\end{pmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}_{m}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}_{m};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}+\bm{\Delta})}\right)_{m=1,\ldots,v_{j},s=1,\ldots,S}

\displaystyle=\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}+\bm{\Delta})}\right)_{m=1,\ldots,v_{j},s=1,\ldots,S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})}\end{pmatrix}}_{=:\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{\Delta})}.

Thus

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}})\end{pmatrix}-D(\bm{\Delta})^{-1}\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{1}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}},{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\overline{\widehat{f}(\bm{k}^{\prime})}c(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{\Delta}),

where
c(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{\Delta})=e(\bm{k},\bm{\Delta})^{H}e(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{\Delta})
and

\displaystyle e(\bm{k},\bm{\Delta})

\displaystyle=\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{\Delta})=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\Delta}}\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{0}).

Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}})\end{pmatrix}-D(\bm{\Delta})^{-1}\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{1}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}},{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)},\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}(\bm{k})\overline{\widehat{f}(\bm{k}^{\prime})}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}((\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\cdot\bm{\Delta}}\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{0})^{H}B_{j}[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\bm{a}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{0}).

Since

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}[c(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{\Delta})]=\mathrm{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}[\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\cdot\bm{\Delta}}]\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{0})^{H}B_{j}[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\bm{a}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{0}),

for
\bm{k}\neq\bm{k}^{\prime}
we get
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}[c(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{\Delta})]=0
and

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}(c(\bm{k},\bm{k},\bm{\Delta}))=\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{0})^{H}B_{j}[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\bm{a}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{0})=:c(\bm{k}).

Using
\|\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{0})\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{v_{j}}\leq\sqrt{R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})}
,
\|B_{j}^{H}\bm{a}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{0})\|_{2}\leq Sv_{j}^{3/2}\leq SR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3/2}
, and
\|[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}]^{-1}\|_{2}\leq 1
we obtain

|c(\bm{k})|\leq S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{f}(\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}})\end{pmatrix}-D(\bm{\Delta})^{-1}\left[B_{j}^{H}B_{j}\right]^{-1}B_{j}^{H}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{1}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{1},{\bm{y}}_{1}^{(1)}+\bm{\Delta})\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}}\cdot({\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})}\mathcal{F}_{N}(f,\bm{\ell}_{v_{j}},{\bm{y}}_{v_{j}}^{(S)}+\bm{\Delta})\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}\left[|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})-\mathcal{B}_{N}(f,\bm{k},\{{\bm{y}}_{m}^{(s)}\},\bm{\Delta})|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}-\bm{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{L}_{N}^{\perp}(\bm{g})\\
\bm{k}\notin\Gamma_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{\ell}^{(j)};\bm{g})\end{subarray}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}|\widehat{f}(\bm{k})|^{2}\frac{r_{\alpha}^{2}(\bm{k})}{r_{\alpha}^{2}(\bm{k})}

\displaystyle\leq S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3}\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}^{2}\max_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},M}}\frac{1}{r_{\alpha}^{2}(\bm{k})}

\displaystyle\leq S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3}\frac{\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}^{2}}{M^{2}}.

Thus we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Delta}}\left[\|f-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{\Delta}}(f)\|_{2}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\|f\|_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}^{2}}{M^{2}}(1+S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3}).

Since the bound holds for all functions
f
in
\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}}
, we have

e_{2}^{\mathrm{ran}}((\mathcal{A}_{\bm{\Delta}})_{\bm{\Delta}},\mathcal{K}_{d,\alpha,\bm{\gamma}})\leq\frac{\sqrt{1+S^{2}R_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})^{3}}}{M}.

The result now follows using that
S=\lceil 2KR_{\alpha,\bm{\gamma},N}(\bm{g})\log N\rceil
, (
3.7
), (
4.7
), and (
3.13
).

The result for the case where
\bm{g}
is constructed by Algorithm
1
follows by similar arguments.

Effect of Filling Interval Time on Mechanical Characteristics of Cemented Tailing Backfill
Authors: Chunming Ai, Anju Yu, Yingbo Wen, Chao Liu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
In order to study the strength reduction caused by delamination, single-gap and double-gap specimens were made, and a uniaxial compressive strength (UCS) test was used to analyze the relationship between filling interval time (FIT) and UCS of single and double-spacing specimens. Based on the cement hydration reaction theory and damage mechanics, the influence mechanism of the FIT on backfill strength was studied. The test results show that the UCS of double-interval backfill is affected by two filling intervals at the same time, and the lower clearance has a greater influence on the strength of the backfill than the upper clearance. With the increase of FIT, the UCS of the filling body decreases gradually. The cementation effect of single and double fillings is negatively correlated with the FIT. Filling within 0–12 h has little effect on the strength of the filling body. On this basis, a mathematical model between damage increment and damage dissipated energy is established. Under the same stress condition, the larger the damage variable (D), the smaller the dissipated energy (G) required by the damage expansion of unit filling body. This study provides a reasonable reference for the design of the filling interval in mine fractional filling.

Introduction

Cementing paste backfill (CPB) is a multiphase composite material composed of tailings and cement, whose strength is usually between soil and rock.1,2 Due to production planning arrangements such as single-filling quantity limitation, material preparation and transportation, regular maintenance of mechanical equipment, and strength requirements of filling retaining walls, stopes generally require multiple filling operations to complete filling, resulting in filling gaps.3 Filling gaps will reduce the strength of cemented backfill,4,5 easily lead to roof collapse and stratified structural plane collapse, resulting in serious casualties and property losses.6 In order to avoid safety problems such as the collapse of filling retaining wall,8 the stratified filling method is often adopted in actual production; that is, secondary or multiple filling is carried out after the initial filling slurry condenses.2,7

Wu9,10 expounded on the future prospect of downhole filling and analyzed the current research status of hot topics such as new cementite materials, total tailings dehydration technology, paste rheology, multifield properties of hardened paste, and microproperties of paste. Jie11,12 et al. proposed the concepts of initial stratified damage, load damage, and total damage. The damage evolution and constitutive model of cemented backfill were established. Shen13 et al. carried out the test of cemented backfill with fractional placement, analyzed the failure mode of the fractional placement backfill, and studied the role of the influence of placement times on the strength reduction of cemented backfill. Wang et al.14 studied the physical characteristics of stratified cementing backfill under different curing ages and analyzed the peak strength, elastic modulus, and strength reduction coefficient of stratified cementing backfill. Cao15 et al. studied factors such as the number of layers and filling angle of cemented backfill and verified the relationship between the physical characteristics of cemented backfill and its relationship. Xu et al.16 studied the mechanical properties of stratified cemented backfill with different proportions and evaluated the strength efficiency of the structural plane of cemented backfill.

The filling slurry is mainly composed of tailings, calcium silicate cement, and water mixed in proportion.17 The cement hydration reaction produces calcium silicate hydrate (C–S–H), which can bond the tailing particles in the filling slurry and continuously consolidate and compact inside the filling body, eventually hardening the entire filling slurry, causing the filling slurry to form a filling body with certain physical strength.18−20 In the case of fractional filling, the slurry filled at different times will be in different hydration reaction stages, and C–S–H has certain differences in the structure and hydration performance.21−26

The above researchers have performed many studies on the backfill body, but they have not put forward the influence of the filling interval time (FIT) on the reduction of the backfill strength. This paper studies the influence of different intervals on the strength and failure mode of single filling and double filling and explores the mechanism.

Physical and Chemical Properties of Tailings

The tailings discharged from the concentration plant of the Bianjiadayuan lead–zinc–silver mine were used as filling materials, which were sampled on-site and transported to the laboratory for storage. The pH value of the mine tailings was 9, the density was 2839 kg/m3, and the porosity was 47%. The grain size grading curve of the tailings is shown in Figure 1. The nonuniformity coefficient and curvature coefficient of tailings were 26.57 and 1.801, respectively. The particle size distribution of the tailings was wide and the grading was good.

UCS Test of Stratified Backfill

When filling stops in the Bianjiadayuan lead–zinc–silver mine, single gaps and double gaps are more common. FIT is the fundamental cause of filling gaps, and it has a significant effect on the physical strength of the filling body. Therefore, UCS tests of double-gap and single-gap backfill bodies were carried out, respectively. Uniaxial compression tests were carried out on backfill bodies with different filling intervals to explore the relationship between filling intervals and the UCS of stratified backfill bodies.

The filling gap of the first fractional placement is called the lower filling gap, and the filling gap of the second fractional placement is called the upper filling gap. During the secondary filling within 24–48 h, the strength of the filling body at this stage decreased to the lowest value, and the effect of FIT was little.

Effect Mechanism of Filling Interval Time on Filling Body Strength

The structure, morphology, and hydration properties of calcium silicate hydrate (C–S–H) are different at different hydration reaction stages. The placement of C–S–H in the hydration stage of the upper and lower layers resulted in the dislocation of C–S–H in the hydration stage, and the filling gap was generated in the cementation process of two C–S–H strands at different hydration stages. The cementation effect of the C–S–H combination in different hydration stages is also different. This difference is the reason for the difference in filling body strength at different filling intervals.

Filling Gap Formation Mechanism

When the filling interval is between 0 and 12 h, the first pouring C–S–H is in the induction or acceleration stage of the hydration reaction. As shown in Figure 12, C–S–H in the lower backfill belongs to the flock-like form and has good hydration performance, which can be integrated and consolidated with the secondary casting slurry. Therefore, the cementation effect of the filling gap generated by secondary pouring at this stage is better, and a large number of structural defects will not appear.

Interval time 0–12 h filling gap generation process.

When the interval time is 12–24 h, the C–S–H hydration stage of the first pouring goes to the deceleration stage. As shown in Figure 13 the C–S–H structure in the lower backfill gradually changed from a flock-like shape to a needle-like shape, and hydration activity was significantly weakened. At this time, there are some differences in the structure and cementation properties of C–S–H in the upper and lower backfill. During a secondary placement at this interval, although C–S–H in the lower layer can contact and cement with the secondary placement slurry in a large area, the cementation effect is relatively poor and obvious structural defects will appear in the filling gap.

Interval time 24–48 h filling gap generation process.

After the interval of 24–48 h or longer, the second pouring was carried out, and the first pouring C–S–H had entered the curing period. C–S–H in the lower backfill gradually loses hydration activity, and the structure changes to a plate shape, as shown in Figure 14. After secondary placement at this interval, the lower backfill was cured, and the C–S–H on the surface basically lost the cementation property. C–S–H in the upper and lower backfill cannot be cemented with each other, so there are large area structural defects in the backfill gap.

Interval time 24–48 h filling gap generation process.

Filling Gap Affects the Strength Mechanism

Development Process of Fissure in the Backfill

When the stratified backfill is under load, its ability to absorb elastic potential energy is weaker than that of other parts of the backfill due to the incoherent structure of the backfill gap. The area of structural defects at the filling gap is relatively large. When loading is applied, the accumulation limit of elastic potential energy is first reached at this location, and excess energy is converted into dissipated energy, thus promoting the development of structural damage at the structural defects, as shown in Figure 15. With the continuous development of structural damage, other complete structures of the backfill are destroyed, resulting in the reduction of the elastic potential energy accumulation limit of the backfill around the damage. At this time, the region affected by damage starts to convert the subsequent load into dissipated energy, and the unfavorable elastic potential energy is released. This is done until the fill is completely destabilized.

In this paper, UCS experiments are carried out on single-spaced and double-spaced backfill bodies with different intervals, and the optimal filling interval is 0–12 h; its strength and integrity decrease with the increase of interval time. Based on the cement hydration theory and capacity dissipation theory, it is concluded that the longer the filling interval, the larger the initial damage area and the more easily the filling body is damaged.

Conclusions

(1)In the single-interval filling body, the UCS of the filling body gradually decreases with the increase of FIT. Secondary filling within 0–12 h has little effect on the strength of the filling body. With the increase of FIT, the cracks through the filling body become scattered and broken, and the cementation property of the filling body becomes worse.(2)The cementation effect of double-interval filling body gap is related to the FIT, and the shorter the FIT, the better the cementation effect; the UCS of double-spaced backfill is affected by two intervals at the same time. With the increase of upper and lower FIT, the UCS of the backfill body gradually decreases, and the lower gap has a greater influence on the strength of the backfill body.(3)The influence mechanism of FIT on the strength of the filling body was studied. According to the mechanism of cement hydration reaction and the results of scanning electron microscopy, the structural defects of filling gap become more and more serious with the increase of FIT. The larger the area of the initial damaged structure is, the larger the damage variable (D) becomes, which leads to the smaller dissipated energy (G) required when the filling body is damaged and unstable. The more easily the filling body is destroyed, the smaller the load required for the crushing of the filling body.

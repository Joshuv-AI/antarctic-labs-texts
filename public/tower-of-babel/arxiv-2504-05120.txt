The intersections of the lower central series and the subgroups of finite p p -index of Generalized Baumslag-Solitar tree groups
V. Metaftsis, D. Tsipa

Abstract
For a Generalized Baumslag-Solitar group G G with underling graph a tree, we calculate the intersection ( G ) ! {}_{\omega}(G) of the lower central series and the intersection ( N p ) ! ​ ( G ) (N_{p})_{\omega}(G) of the subgroups of finite index some power of a prime p p .

Introduction

Generalized Baumslag-Solitar groups (GBS-groups) are fundamental groups of finite graphs of groups with infinite cyclic vertex and edge groups. These groups have interesting group-theoretic properties and have been the subject of recent research (see for example
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
12
]
).

In the case where the underlying graph is a simple loop we have the Baumslag-Solitar groups which are the
1
-relator groups

BS(m,n)=\langle t,a\hskip 2.84526pt|\hskip 2.84526ptta^{m}t^{-1}=a^{n}\rangle.

where
m,n
are non-zero integers.

Baumslag-Solitar groups seem to have first appeared in the literature in
[
1
]
as they were defined by Gilbert Baumslag and Donald Solitar in order to provide examples of non-Hopfian groups. These groups have played a really central rôle in combinatorial and geometric group theory. In several situations they have provided examples which mark boundaries between different classes of groups and also they often provide a test for various theories.

Let
G
be a group. We say that
G
is residually a
\mathcal{P}
group if for every element
1\neq g\in G
there is a homomorphism
f
from
G
to a group with the property
\mathcal{P}
such that
f(g)
is not trivial.

For elements
a,b
of
G
, we write
[a,b]
for the commutator of
a
and
b
, that is
[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab
. For subgroups
A
and
B
of
G
we write
[A,B]=\langle[a,b]\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pta\in A,b\in B\rangle
. For a positive integer
i
, let
{}_{i}(G)
be the
i-
th term of the lower central series of
G
. We denote by
{}_{\omega}(G)
the intersection of all terms of the lower central series of
G
, that is
{}_{\omega}(G)=\bigcap\limits_{i\geq 1}{}_{i}(G)
. A group
G
is residually nilpotent if and only if
{}_{\omega}(G)
is trivial.

We denote the intersection of all finite index normal subgroups of
G
with index some power of a prime number
p
by
(N_{p})_{\omega}(G)
. A group is residually a finite
p
-group if and only if
(N_{p})_{\omega}(G)
is trivial.

In his semial work in
[
4
]
, Gruenberg proved various results concerning the residual properties of groups. Also, in
[
9
]
(see also
[
10
]
) Moldavanskii calculated the intersection
(N_{p})_{\omega}(G)
for a Baumslag-Solitar group
G
while in
[
5
]
Kofinas, Metaftsis and Papistas calculated the intersection
{}_{\omega}(G)
. Also, Sokolov, in
[
14
]
provides criteria for a GBS group to be residually nilpotent, residually torsion-free nilpotent and residually free..

In the present work we calculate the intersection
{}_{\omega}(G)
and the intersection
(N_{p})_{\omega}(G)
for a GBS tree group, that is a GBS group with underlying graph is a tree. In fact, in our main result Theorem
3.4
we prove that
{}_{\omega}(G)
is generated by
{}_{\omega}(H_{ij})
for every
H_{ij}
a subgroup of
G
generated by any two vertex groups
\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle
. We prove a similar result also for
(N_{p})_{\omega}(G)
(Theorem
4.5
).

Although the final generating sets are rather complicated, our results show that both
{}_{\omega}(G)
and
(N_{p})_{\omega}(G)
are generated in a rather natural way. Finally, at the end of the paper we provide an appendix with a specific example for which we demonstrate the generating sets for both
{}_{\omega}(G)
and
(N_{3})_{\omega}(G)
.

2
Definitions and auxiliary results

Let
G
be a group. A
G
-tree is a tree with a
G
-action by automorphisms, without inversions. A
G
-tree is proper if every edge stabilizer is a subgroup of the vertex stabilizers for the vertices that correspond to this edge. It is minimal if there is no proper
G
-invariant subtree and cocompact if the quotient graph is finite. The quotient of the action is the graph of groups with vertex groups the vertex stabilizers and edge groups the edge stabilizers.

Given a
G
-tree
X
, an element
g\in G
is called elliptic if it stabilizes a vertex of
X
and hyperbolic otherwise. If
g
is hyperbolic then there is a unique
G
-invariant line in
X
, the axis of
g
, on which
g
acts as a translation. A subgroup
H
of
G
is elliptic if it fixes a vertex.

A generalized Baumslag-Solitar tree is a
G
-tree whose vertex stabilizers and edge stabilizers are all infinite cyclic. Such a group
G
is called generalized Baumslag-Solitar group (GBS group). The quotient graph of groups has all vertex and edge groups isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
and the inclusion maps are multiplication by various non-zero integers.

The group
G
is torsion free since any torsion group intersects each conjugate of a vertex group trivially which implies that is free and therefore trivial. Moreover, all elliptic elements are commensurable, i.e. they have a common power.

GBS-groups are presented by labeled graphs, that is finite graphs where each oriented edge
e
has label
\lambda_{e}\neq 0
. If
\Gamma
is a finite graph with vertex and edge groups isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
then we have a graph of groups with fundamental group a GBS group. A presentation for a GBS group
G
can be obtained as follows. Choose a maximal tree
T\subset\Gamma
. There is one generator
g_{v}
for every vertex of
\Gamma
and one relator
t_{e}
for every edge in
\Gamma\setminus T
(non-oriented). Each non-oriented edge contributes a relation in the presentation. The relations are

(g_{\alpha(e)})^{\lambda_{e}}=(g_{\alpha(\overline{e})})^{\lambda_{\overline{e}}}\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \ e\in T

t_{e}(g_{\alpha(e)})^{\lambda_{e}}t_{e}^{-1}=(g_{\alpha(\overline{e})})^{\lambda_{\overline{e}}}\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ e\in\Gamma\setminus T

The above is the standard presentation of
G
and the generating set is the standard generating set associated to
\Gamma
and
T
.

Notice that the group
G
represented by
\Gamma
does not change if we change the sign of all labels near a vertex
v
or the labels carried by a non-oriented edge. These are called admissible sign changes. Notice also that there may be infinitely many labeled graphs representing the same
G
. If
\Gamma
is a loop, then
G
is the known Baumslag-Solitar group.
In case
\Gamma
is a tree we will call
G
a generalized Baumslag-Solitar tree group.

We will now present some auxiliary results that will help us calculate the intersections
{}_{\omega}(G)
and
(N_{p})_{\omega}(G)
in the case where the underlying graph of the GBS is a segment on which is the basis for calculating the general case of the GBS tree group.

Let
Z(G)
be the center of a group
G
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a GBS group, where the underlying graph is a tree
X
. Then
G^{\prime}\cap Z(G)
is trivial.

Proof.

Choose an orientation for
X
. Then the group
G
has the following presentation.

G=\langle x_{v_{i}}\hskip 2.84526pt|\hskip 2.84526ptx_{\alpha(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{e_{j}}}=x_{\omega(e_{j})}^{\bar{\lambda}_{{e}_{j}}}\rangle,\hskip 2.84526ptv_{i}\in V(X),e_{j}\in E(X)

Raising the relations of
G
to appropriate powers we can find integers
r_{v_{i}}
such that
x_{v_{i}}^{r_{v_{i}}}=x_{v_{i^{\prime}}}^{r_{v_{i^{\prime}}}}
for all
v_{i},v_{i^{\prime}}\in V(X)
. Then
Z(G)=\langle x_{v_{i}}^{r_{v_{i}}}\rangle
,
that is, the center of
G
is a subgroup of each vertex group
G_{v_{i}}=\langle x_{v_{i}}\rangle
.
Notice that in the case where
v_{i}
and
v_{i^{\prime}}
are adjacent vertices incident with an edge
e
then
r_{v_{i}}=\lambda_{e}N_{e}
and
r_{v_{i^{\prime}}}=\bar{\lambda}_{{e}}N_{e}
for the same integer
N_{e}
.

Let
r=lcm(r_{v_{i}})
,
v_{i}\in V(X)
, and
H
be the infinite cyclic group
\langle y\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}
. We define the map
\phi:G\to H
with
\phi(x_{v_{i}})=y^{\frac{r}{r_{v_{i}}}}
, for all
v_{i}\in V(X)
. The map
\phi
is a homomorphism since it preserves the relations of
G
(it follows from the previous observation) and every vertex group embeds in to
\langle y\rangle
. Since
G^{\prime}\subseteq Ker\phi
, the result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a graph of groups
(\mathcal{G},X)
where
X
is a tree with vertex groups finite cyclic
p
-groups. Then
G^{\prime}\cap Z(G)
is trivial.

Proof.

Assume that
Z(G)
is not trivial (otherwise the result is obvious). Since
G
is the fundamental group of a tree of groups where the vertex groups are finite cyclic
p
-groups then the center
Z(G)
is a non-trivial cyclic subgroup
\langle x_{v_{i}}^{r_{i}}\rangle
of each edge group. If
G_{v_{i}}=\langle x_{v_{i}}\rangle
we have that
Z(G)=\langle x_{v_{i}}^{m_{i}}\rangle
for all
v_{i}\in V(X)
.

The group
G
has the following presentation.

G=\langle x_{v_{i}}\hskip 2.84526pt|\hskip 2.84526ptx_{v_{i}}^{p^{k_{i}}}=1,x_{\alpha(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{e_{j}}}=x_{\omega(e_{j})}^{\bar{\lambda}_{{e}_{j}}}\rangle,\hskip 2.84526ptv_{i}\in V(X),e_{j}\in E(X)

Let
k=max\{k_{i}\}
and thus for some
v_{r}\in V(X)
we have
ord(x_{v_{r}})=p^{k}
. Let
H
be the group
H=\langle y\rangle
with
ord(y)=p^{k}
, and thus
\langle y\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
. Therefore
\langle x_{v_{r}}\rangle\cong\langle y\rangle
. Each vertex group
\langle x_{v_{i}}\rangle
can be embedded to the group
H
and it’s image will be a subgroup of order
d_{i}
, where
d_{i}
is a divisor of
p^{k}
, i.e.
d_{i}\in\{p^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{r}
. Therefore we can define the map
\phi:G\to H
such that
\phi(x_{v_{i}})=y^{\frac{p^{k}}{p^{k_{i}}}}
, for all
v_{i}\in V(X)
. The map
\phi
is a homomorphism, since it preserves all relations. Indeed
\phi(x_{v_{i}}^{p^{k_{i}}})=y^{p^{k}}=1
, for all
v_{i}\in V(X)
. Moreover, notice that the relation
x_{\alpha(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{e_{j}}}=x_{\omega(e_{j})}^{\bar{\lambda}_{{e}_{j}}}
is preserved since
ord(x_{\alpha(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{e_{j}}})=ord(x_{\omega(e_{j})}^{\bar{\lambda}_{{e}_{j}}})
and due to the fact that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the subgroups of a finite cyclic group and their order.

Now assume that there is a non trivial element
z\in Z(G)
such that
z\in G^{\prime}
. We have that
z=(x_{v_{r}})^{s}
, for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. We have that
G^{\prime}\subseteq Ker\phi
and thus
z\in Ker\phi
. Let
\phi\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{{G_{v_{r}}}}
be the restriction of
\phi
to the group
G_{v_{r}}=\langle x_{v_{r}}\rangle
. Then
z\in Ker\phi\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{{G_{v_{r}}}}
, which is a contradiction, since the map
\phi\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$|$}_{{G_{v_{r}}}}
that maps
x_{v_{r}}
to
y
is an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
. Then
{}_{i}(G/N)={}_{i}(G)N/N
, for every positive integer
i
.

Proof.

We will prove this Lemma using induction on
i
.

For
i=1
we have that
{}_{1}(G/N)=G/N={}_{1}(G)N/N
. Now assume that for some
i>1
we have that
{}_{i}(G/N)={}_{i}(G)N/N
.

Then
{}_{i+1}(G/N)=[{}_{i}(G/N),G/N]
which by the inductive hypothesis is equal to

[{}_{i}(G)N/N,G/N]

=

\langle[aN,bN]\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pta\in{}_{i}(G),b\in G\rangle
. Let
[aN,bN]\in{}_{i+1}(G/N)
,
a\in{}_{i}(G),

b\in G
. Since
[aN,bN]=[a,b]N\in{}_{i+1}(G)N/N
we have that
{}_{i+1}(G/N)

\subseteq{}_{i+1}(G)N/N
.

For the converse we have
{}_{i+1}(G)N/N=[{}_{i}(G),G]N/N

=

\langle[a,b]\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pta\in{}_{i}(G),b\in G\rangle N/N
. Let
[a,b]N\in{}_{i+1}(G)N/N
. Thus
[a,b]N=[aN,bN]
, where
a\in{}_{i}(G),b\in G
. Therefore
[a,b]N\in{}_{i+1}(G/N)
and consequently we have the equality
{}_{i+1}(G/N)={}_{i+1}(G)N/N
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a graph of groups where the vertex groups are all either infinite cyclic groups (and thus
G
is a GBS group) or finite cyclic
p
-groups. Then
{}_{\omega}(G)\cong{}_{\omega}(G/Z(G))
.

Proof.

Since
Z(G)
is a normal subgroup of
G
by Lemma
2.3
we know that
{}_{i}(G/Z(G))={}_{i}(G)Z(G)/Z(G)
for every positive integer
i
.

The group
{}_{i}(G/Z(G))={}_{i}(G)Z(G)/Z(G)
from the second isomorphism theorem for groups is isomorphic to
{}_{i}(G)/({}_{i}(G)\cap Z(G))
.

Now for each
i>1
, since the center of
G
is contained in every vertex group, we have that
{}_{i}(G)\cap Z(G)\subseteq{}_{i}(G)\cap G_{v}\subseteq{}_{2}(G)\cap G_{v}
, where
G_{v}
is the abelian vertex group of
G
. But from Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
we have that
{}_{2}(G)\cap G_{v}
is trivial and thus
{}_{i}(G)\cap Z(G)
is trivial.

The result now follows from the definition of
{}_{\omega}(G)
.
∎

Let
p
be a prime number. Using the above Lemma, we will prove that the fundamental group of a tree of finite cyclic
p
-groups is residually nilpotent and hence residually finite-
p
. The equivalence of these two properties follows from the following Proposition.

Proposition 2.5

(see
[
15
]
)
.

Let (
\mathcal{G},\Gamma
) be a graph of groups, where
\Gamma
is a connected finite graph with vertex groups
G_{v}
,
v\in V(X)
, finite
p
-groups. The fundamental group
G=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\Gamma)
is residually finite-
p
if and only if it is residually nilpotent.

The residual nilpotency of the fundamental group of finite cyclic
p
-groups can easily be proved in the case where the underlying graph is a segment.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a segment of finite cyclic
p
-groups, that is the free product of two finite cyclic
p
-groups
\langle x\rangle=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
and
\langle y\rangle=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{l}}
amalgamated over the subgroups
\langle x^{r}\rangle=\langle y^{s}\rangle
, for some
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
G
is residually nilpotent and thus residually finite-
p
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
we have that
G
is residually nilpotent if and only if
G/Z(G)
is residually nilpotent. But since the center
Z(G)
of
G
is the amalgamated subgroup
\langle x^{r}\rangle=\langle y^{s}\rangle
we have that
G/Z(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{r}*\mathbb{Z}_{s}
.

The groups
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{s}
are also finite cyclic
p
-groups and therefore residually finite-
p
. Therefore, using Gruenberg’s result we have that their free product
G/Z(G)
is also residually finite-
p
and thus residually nilpotent. Consequently,
G
is residually nilpotent and thus residually finite-
p
by Proposition
2.5
.
∎

We mention here that the above result can also be obtained by Theorem 1 in
[
13
]
.

We will now generalize the above result in the case where the underlying graph is a tree
T
. In order to prove this, we need the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},T)
be a graph of groups, with
T
be a finite tree and
G_{v_{i}}
be the vertex groups which are finite cyclic
p
-groups. Then
Z(G)=G_{e}
, for some
e\in E(T)
.

Proof.

We will prove the Proposition using induction on the number of the vertices of
T
.
Notice that in the case where
V(T)=2
the statement is obvious.

Let
V(T)=3
, i.e.
G
is a group with presentation

G=\langle x,y,z\hskip 2.84526pt|\hskip 2.84526ptx^{p^{k_{1}}}=1,y^{p^{k_{2}}}=1,z^{p^{k_{3}}}=1,x^{\kappa}=y^{\lambda},y^{\mu}=z^{\nu}\rangle

where
k_{1},k_{2},k_{2},\kappa,\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.

We have that
G_{e_{1}}=\langle x^{\kappa}\rangle=\langle y^{\lambda}\rangle
and
G_{e_{2}}=\langle y^{\mu}\rangle=\langle z^{\nu}\rangle
. Since
\langle y^{\lambda}\rangle
and
\langle y^{\mu}\rangle
are both subgroups of
\langle y\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k_{2}}}
and since
p
is a prime number, we have that
\langle y^{\lambda}\rangle\leqslant\langle y^{\mu}\rangle
or
\langle y^{\mu}\rangle\leqslant\langle y^{\lambda}\rangle
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\langle y^{\lambda}\rangle\leqslant\langle y^{\mu}\rangle
and therefore
y^{\lambda}\in\langle y^{\mu}\rangle\Rightarrow y^{\lambda}=(y^{\mu})^{\rho}
, for some
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then we have that

xy^{\lambda}x^{-1}=xx^{\kappa}x^{-1}=x^{\kappa}=y^{\lambda}

yy^{\lambda}y^{-1}=y^{\lambda}

zy^{\lambda}z^{-1}=z(y^{\mu})^{\rho}z^{-1}=z(z^{\nu})^{\rho}z^{-1}=(z^{\nu})^{\rho}=(y^{\mu})^{\rho}=y^{\lambda}

Hence
Z(G)=\langle y^{\lambda}\rangle=G_{e_{1}}
. Similarly, in the case where
\langle y^{\mu}\rangle\leqslant\langle y^{\lambda}\rangle
we have that
Z(G)=\langle y^{\mu}\rangle=G_{e_{2}}
.

Assume now the statement of the Lemma holds in the case where
V(T)=n
. We will prove that the statement holds if
T
has
n+1
vertices.

Let
G
be the group with presentation

G=\langle x_{v_{i}}\hskip 2.84526pt|\hskip 2.84526ptx_{v_{i}}^{p^{k_{i}}}=1,x_{\alpha(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{e_{j}}}=x_{\omega(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{\bar{e}_{j}}}\rangle,\hskip 2.84526ptv_{i}\in V(T),e_{j}\in E(T)

The edge groups are the groups
G_{e_{j}}=\langle x_{\alpha(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{e_{j}}}\rangle=\langle x_{\omega(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{\bar{e}_{j}}}\rangle
for all
e_{j}\in E(T)
.

Now let
T^{\prime}
be the subtree of
T
that occurs after removing an external vertex and let
H
be the fundamental graph of groups that corresponds to
T^{\prime}
.

H=\langle x_{v_{i}}\hskip 2.84526pt|\hskip 2.84526ptx_{v_{i}}^{p^{k_{i}}}=1,x_{\alpha(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{e_{j}}}=x_{\omega(e_{j})}^{\lambda_{\bar{e}_{j}}}\rangle,\hskip 2.84526ptv_{i}\in V(T^{\prime}),e_{j}\in E(T^{\prime})

Notice that
H
is a subgroup of
G
. By the inductive hypothesis we have that there exists
s
such that
Z(H)=G_{e_{s}}
.

We have that
G_{e_{s}}=\langle x_{v_{i}}^{\mu_{i}}\rangle
for all
v_{i}\in V(T^{\prime})
where if
v_{i}=\alpha(e_{s})
then
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{e_{s}}
while if
v_{i}=\omega(e_{s})
then
\mu_{i}=\lambda_{\bar{e}_{s}}
.

Let
\hat{e}
be the edge such that
\hat{e}\in T\setminus T^{\prime}
and assume that
\alpha(\hat{e})=v_{n}
. We have that
G_{\hat{e}}=\langle x_{v_{n}}^{\lambda_{\hat{e}}}\rangle
which is a subgroup of
\langle x_{v_{n}}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k_{n}}}
. Moreover,
G_{e_{s}}=\langle x_{v_{n}}^{\mu_{n}}\rangle
which is a subgroup of
\langle x_{v_{n}}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k_{n}}}
. Therefore, since
p
is a prime number, either
\langle x_{v_{n}}^{\lambda_{\hat{e}}}\rangle\leqslant\langle x_{v_{n}}^{\mu_{n}}\rangle
or
\langle x_{v_{n}}^{\mu_{n}}\rangle\leqslant\langle x_{v_{n}}^{\lambda_{\hat{e}}}\rangle
. Hence we have that either
Z(G)=G_{e_{s}}
or
Z(G)=G_{\hat{e}}
and thus the statement holds.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
G
be the fundamental group of a tree
T
of finite cyclic
p
-groups. Then
G
is residually nilpotent and thus residually finite-
p
.

Proof.

We will prove the Proposition using induction on the number of the vertices of
T
.

Let
V(T)=2
. Then by Proposition
2.6
the result holds.
Assume now that the statement of the Proposition holds for a graph of groups with underlying tree
T
with
|V(T)|=n
. We will prove that the statement holds if the tree has
n+1
vertices.

By Lemma
2.7
we have that
Z(G)
is equal to an edge group for an edge
e\in E(T)
(that is one of the amalgamating subgroups) and therefore the group
G/Z(G)
is isomorphic to a free product of
G_{1}
,
G_{2},\dots,G_{m}
which are fundamental groups of
m
subtrees
T_{1}
,
T_{2},\dots,T_{m}
of
T
and therefore each has at most
n
vertices. Hence, using the inductive hypothesis
G_{1}
,
G_{2},\dots,G_{m}
are residually nilpotent and thus by Proposition
2.5
residually finite-
p
. Therefore, using Gruenberg’s result we have that their free product
G/Z(G)
is also residually finite-
p
and thus residually nilpotent. Using Lemma
2.4
the result follows.
∎

The above result can also be verified using the criterion for residual
p
-finiteness of arbitrary graphs of finite
p
-groups by G. Wilkes (see
[
16
]
).

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G
be a group and
a,b\in G
such that
a^{k}=b^{l}
, for some
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
\kappa,\lambda
are divisors of
k
and
l
respectively with
k=\kappa s
,
l=\lambda r
and
gcd(\kappa,\lambda)=1
then
[a^{s},b^{r}]\in{}_{\omega}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
a^{s}=x
and
b^{r}=y
. We want to prove that the commutator
g=[x,y]
belongs to
{}_{i}(G)
for each
i
.
Assume on the contrary that
1\neq g\notin{}_{\omega}(G)
. Therefore there exists a homomorphism
f:G\to N
, where
N
is a nilpotent group, such that
f(g)\neq 1
.

Now since
N
is a finitely generated nilpotent group we have that
N
is polycyclic and thus residually finite. Therefore there exists a homomorphism
\phi:G\to\tilde{N}
such that
\phi(g)=[\phi(x),\phi(y)]

\neq 1
, where
\tilde{N}
is a finite nilpotent group. Since
\tilde{N}
is a finite nilpotent group it can be written as the direct product of its Sylow subgroups. Moreover, since
[\phi(x),\phi(y)]\neq 1
we have that
\phi(x)
and
\phi(y)
belong to the same Sylow
p
-group
P
of
\tilde{N}
.

Let
H
,
K
be the subgroups of
P
that are generated by
\phi(x)
and
\phi(y)
respectively. Thus
H=\langle\phi(x)\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\nu}}
and
K=\langle\phi(y)\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\mu}}
. Let’s assume that

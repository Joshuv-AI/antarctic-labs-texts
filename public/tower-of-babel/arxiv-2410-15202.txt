Asymptotics for some Singular Monge-Ampère Equations
Nicholas McCleerey

Abstract
. Given a psh function φ ∈ ℰ ⁡ ( Ω ) \varphi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega) and a smooth, bounded θ ⩾ 0 \theta\geqslant 0 , it is known that one can solve the Monge-Ampère equation MA ⁡ ( φ θ ) = θ n ​ MA ​ ( φ ) \mathrm{MA}(\varphi_{\theta})=\theta^{n}\mathrm{MA}(\varphi) , with some form of Dirichlet boundary values, by work of Åhag–Cegrell–Czyż–Hiệp. 1.
Introduction

Suppose that
(\Omega^{n},\omega)
is a strongly
m
-pseudoconvex (
1\leqslant m\leqslant n
) Kähler manifold with (smooth, non-empty) boundary; we write
m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)
for the space of
m
-subharmonic (
m
-sh) functions on
\Omega
. We adopt the convention
0\cdot\infty=0
. When
m=n
, then
m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)=\textrm{PSH}(\Omega)
, the space of psh function.

For
p\leqslant 0
, define the Riesz kernel
K_{p}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
and its inverse,
L_{p}:[-\infty,0)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
to be:

K_{p}(s):=\begin{cases}\log s&\text{ if }p=0\\
\frac{1}{p}s^{p}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad L_{p}(t):=\begin{cases}e^{t}&\text{ if }p=0\\
(pt)^{1/p}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that
L_{p}(t)
is convex and increasing.

Our main result is the following (see Section 2 for notation):

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{m}(\Omega)
, the Cegrell class on
\Omega
, is such that
\psi\leqslant-1
and
\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi)
is supported on
S_{\psi}:=\{\psi=-\infty\}
; moreover, assume that
S_{\psi}
is closed and that
\psi
is smooth on
\Omega\setminus S_{\psi}
.

Additionally, suppose there exists
p\leqslant 0<\delta
such that
h:=L_{p}(\psi)
satisfies

(1.1)

i\partial\overline{\partial}h\geqslant_{m}\varepsilon h^{1-\delta}\omega\text{ for some $\varepsilon>0$.}

Then for any
\theta\in C^{2}(\Omega)
,
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 1
, the envelope:

\psi_{\theta}:=\sup\{\varphi\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)\ |\ \varphi\leqslant\min\{0,\theta\psi+C\}\text{ for some }C\geqslant 0\}^{*},

satisfies:

(i)

\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi_{\theta})=\chi_{S_{\psi}}\theta^{m}\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi)
,

(ii)

\psi_{\theta}\geqslant\psi-C
,

(iii)

\psi_{\theta}\in L^{\infty}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\{\theta>0\}})
, and

(iv)

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\rightarrow z\\
x\not\in S_{\psi}\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{\theta}}{\psi}=\theta
for all
z\in S_{\psi}\cap\{\theta>0\}
.

Moreover, if
p>-\frac{\delta}{2}
, then
\psi_{\theta}
and
\theta\psi
have the same singularity type.

Our main contribution is the construction of sub- and supersolutions to deduce the asymptotics in
(iii)
and
(iv)
, which are new even in the psh case. In
[
1
,
13
]
, envelopes were constructed which satisfy
(i)
and
(ii)
– in fact, their construction works more generally, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{m}(\Omega)
and any
\theta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi))
. It should be the case that their envelope agrees with
\psi_{\theta}
when the boundary values do, as a result of the general comparison theorem of Åhag-Czyż-Lu-Rashkovskii
[
2
]
, although we did not check this thoroughly.

The relevance of Theorem
1.1
stems from the following: a widely held expectation in pluripotential theory is that there should not exist a psh function
\psi\in\mathcal{E}(\Omega)
such that
\mathrm{MA}(\psi)=\delta_{V}
, the Hausdorff measure of a complex submanifold
V\subset\Omega
(with non-zero dimension). If one could establish boundedness of the original envelopes in
[
1
]
away from the support of
\theta
, then the expectation would follow from a straightforward contradiction argument. Theorem
1.1
provides exactly this, but under some additional assumptions; as such, it seems better to regard it simply as further evidence towards the expectation.

Let us then briefly discuss the necessity of our assumptions. Weakening the smoothness of
\psi
in Theorem
1.1
to local boundedness should be quite doable – we have not completely checked the details however, and hope to update this in the future. Going further than this would require a new method or finer computations; the same is true for the closedness of
S_{\psi}
.

The assumption that
\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi)
be supported on
S_{\psi}
can be weakened substantially – in fact, we do not even need to assume that
\psi
is
m
-sh, only that
L_{p}(\psi)
is. The specific assumptions and results are given in Theorem
3.5
.

Some form of assumption (
1.1
) seems indispensable however; given any
\psi\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)
(e.g.
\psi=\log\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert
) one can check that for
h:=L_{p}(\varphi+\rho)
,
p\leqslant 0
, we have

i\partial\overline{\partial}h\geqslant_{m}\varepsilon h^{1-p}\omega

for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. Thus, Theorem
1.1
(really, Theorem
3.5
) is false without assuming something to replace (
1.1
).

To understand (
1.1
), we offer the following heuristic argument. Suppose that
p=0
and
m=n
. Then

i\partial\overline{\partial}e^{\psi}=e^{\psi}i\partial\overline{\partial}\psi+e^{\psi}i\partial\psi\wedge\overline{\partial}\psi\geqslant\varepsilon e^{(1-\delta)\psi}\omega.

Since
\mathrm{MA}(\psi)=0
on
\{\psi>-\infty\}
,
i\partial\overline{\partial}\psi
cannot have full rank – if we weaken this condition, as in Theorem
3.5
, then the example above tells us we should refine this to saying that
i\partial\overline{\partial}\psi
cannot have full rank “asymptotically.” Assumption (
1.1
) ensures however that it has rank
n-1
asymptotically, since the gradient term has rank 1. Varying
p
changes the overall scaling on the right-hand side, so that larger
p
corresponds to more asymptotic positivity; similarly with
\delta
. We thus view assumption (
1.1
) as a kind of “maximal rank” condition.

As
m
decreases, it becomes easier to sastisfy condition (
1.1
), since
i\partial\overline{\partial}\psi
needs less positivity to dominate
\omega
in the
m
-sh sense (it only needs
m
-many positive eigenvalues). This, plus the additionally flexibility contained in Theorem
3.5
, allows us to show:

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose that
V\subset\Omega
is a complex submanifold, with
\partial V=\emptyset
or
\partial V\subset\partial\Omega
, and complex codimension
k\geqslant m
. Set
h:=d_{\omega,V}^{2}
, the square of the Riemannian distance function to
V
induced by
\omega
, and
p:=1-\frac{k}{m}\leqslant 0
. Then for any
\theta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\theta\geqslant 0
, the envelope:

\psi_{\theta}:=\sup\{\varphi\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)\ |\ \varphi\leqslant\min\{0,\theta K_{p}(h)+C\}\text{ for some }C\geqslant 0\}^{*}

satisfies conclusions
(i)
-
(iv)
in Theorem
3.5
. Moreover, if
k<\frac{3}{2}m
, then
\psi_{\theta}
and
\theta K_{p}(h)
have the same singularity type.

The “threshold value”
m=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
is common in the study of
m
-convex functions, so it seems reasonable to expect that
\psi_{\theta}
and
\theta K_{p}(h)
should have the same singularity type when
k<2m
, instead of only when
k<\frac{3}{2}m
. We can show this under some more restrictive hypothesis on
\theta
(essentially, if it is the pullback of a function on
V
); this allows us to effectively use the twisting of the eigenspaces of
i\partial\overline{\partial}(\theta K_{p}(h))
to pull out an extra factor of
h
in the estimates. We plan to include details at a future date.

A few words about the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The construction of the subsolutions simultaneously generalizes and simplifies a result from
[
7
]
; there, it was only carried out in the setting of Corollary
1.2
. Our construction (and indeed, our definition) of supersolutions is novel – we work exclusively with respect to the linearization of
\mathrm{H}^{m}
at
\psi
, instead of with
\mathrm{H}^{m}
directly. This allows us to construct supersolutions which are very poorly behaved on
S_{\psi}
, but whose asymptotics are clear. This is also why we are able to work with
\psi
which are not necessarily
m
-sh.

We conclude with some comments on further consequences and directions. It follows more-or-less immediately from Theorem
1.1
that one can apply Demailly’s Lelong-Jensen formula (see
[
9
]
) to the envelope
\psi_{\theta}
when
\theta
has compact support; this provides, for the
m
-sh functions especially, a rich class of new sub-mean values inequalities. In order to apply these effectively, one needs a better understanding of the level sets of
\psi_{\theta}
– this is especially interesting for the functions coming from Corollary
1.2
. Understanding if Poisson kernels exist for these functions, c.f.
[
4
]
, would also be interesting.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we collect some background results and fix notation. In Section 3, we prove Theorem
3.5
, which implies Theorem
1.1
. Corollary
1.2
is shown in subsection
3.4
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a complex manifold with boundary

m
-pseudoconvex

\rho\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)\cap C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})

m
-sh, and such that
\partial\Omega=\{\rho=0\}

For

\mathrm{H}^{m}(u):=(i\partial\overline{\partial}u)^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}.

It is a positive Radon measure on

The largest space of
m
-sh functions to which

[
5
]

For

[

. The polar part

Unlike the non-polar part, the polar part can often be read off from pointwise bounds on

Suppose that

2.2.
Supersolutions and Polar Mass

We need some further definitions to work with envelopes of
m
-sh functions. The notion of “subsolution” in our context is basically just being a candidate in the given envelope. The notion of supersolution is more subtle. We have a preliminary definition:

Definition 2.6
.

Suppose that
m\geqslant 2
and that
T
is an
(n-1,n-1)
-current on an open subset
U\subset\Omega
such that:

•

there exist
u_{2},\ldots,u_{m}\in m\mathrm{SH}(U)\cap C^{\infty}(U)
with
T=i\partial\overline{\partial}u_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge i\partial\overline{\partial}u_{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}
, and

•

\omega\wedge T>0
on
U
.

We then define an
m
-elliptic operator
on
U
by:

\Delta g:=i\partial\overline{\partial}g\wedge T\quad\quad\text{ for any }g\in m\mathrm{SH}^{\delta}(U),

where
m\mathrm{SH}^{\delta}(U):=\{u-v\ |\ u,v\in m\mathrm{SH}(U)\}
. Note that
\Delta g
is a signed Radon measure on
U
.

Definition 2.7
.

We say that
\psi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is a
superweight
if there exists a closed, complete
m
-polar set
E
such that:

(1)

\{\psi\leqslant-1\}\Subset\Omega
,

(2)

\exists\,u,v\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega\setminus E)\cap L^{\infty}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus E)
such that
\psi=u-v
on
\Omega\setminus E
,

(3)

\psi
is continuous at each point in
(\Omega\setminus E)\cup S_{\psi}
, and

(4)

there exists an
m
-elliptic operator
\Delta
on
\Omega\setminus E
such that
\Delta\psi\leqslant 0
.

Condition (2) implies that
S_{\psi}\subseteq E
, but note that
S_{\psi}
need not be closed.

If
\psi
is a smooth
m
-sh function and
\Delta
is an
m
-elliptic operator such that
\Delta\psi\leqslant 0
on an open set

, then the Gårding inequality implies that
\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi)\equiv 0
on

; for more general

[
10

Extensions

Next, we have a basic extension result, which we will use repeatedly in the next section:

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose that
E\subset\Omega

\varphi\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega\setminus E)

\varphi\leqslant 0

\varphi

Proof.

Let

\psi\leqslant 0

]

\Omega

For any

W
. This satisfies
\Delta(F)=\frac{M_{s_{2}}-M_{s_{1}}}{s_{2}-s_{1}}\Delta({\psi})\leqslant 0
; note that
F>M_{s_{1}}
on
W
, but this may not hold on the part of the boundary of
W
in
E\setminus S_{\psi}
.

Consider the
m
-sh function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}:=\varphi+\varepsilon(u+\rho)
,
\varepsilon>0
, which is strictly
m
-sh and less than
\varphi
. By continuity of
u
at
-\infty
, we see that there is some neighborhood
U
of
E
in
\Omega
such that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\leqslant M_{s_{1}}-1<F
on
\overline{U}
; we have then
F\geqslant\varphi_{\varepsilon}
on the boundary
W\cap(\Omega\setminus\overline{U})
.

Since
\Delta\varphi_{\varepsilon}\geqslant\varepsilon\Delta\rho>0\geqslant\Delta F
, the maximum principle implies that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\leqslant F
on
W\cap(\Omega\setminus\overline{U})
, and hence also on
W
. Taking the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and then the max over
\{\psi\leqslant s\}
(for
s_{1}<s<s_{2}
) we see that
M_{s}
is convex; it is obviously increasing.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Suppose that
\psi
is a supersolution and
\varphi\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)
,
\sup_{\Omega}\varphi=-1
; set
M_{s}(\varphi):=\max_{\{\psi\leqslant s\}}\varphi
for
s\in(-\infty,-1)
. Then

\frac{M_{s}(\varphi)-M_{t}(\varphi)}{s-t}

is increasing in
s
and
t
(separately), where
-\infty<s<t<-1
. It follows that:

\sigma^{*}_{\psi}(\varphi)=\lim_{s\searrow-\infty}\frac{M_{s}(\varphi)}{s}=\max\{\gamma\geqslant 0\ |\ \varphi\leqslant\gamma\psi+C\}.

The following is due to Rashkovskii-Sigurdsson
[
14
, Lemma 4.1]
, and is the corollary that we will need later:

Corollary 2.11
.

Suppose that
\psi
is a supersolution, and
\varphi\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)
is such that
[\psi]\leqslant[\varphi]
and
\sup_{\Omega}\varphi\leqslant-1
. Then
\varphi\leqslant\psi
.

Proof.

Since
[\psi]\leqslant[\varphi]
, we have
\sigma^{*}_{\psi}(\varphi)\geqslant 1
. Corollary
2.10
then tells us that:

1\leqslant\sigma_{\psi}^{*}(\varphi)\leqslant\frac{M_{s}(\varphi)-M_{-1}(\varphi)}{s+1}\text{ for all }s\in(-\infty,-1).

It follows that, for each
s\in(-\infty,-1)
, we have
\psi(z)=s\geqslant M_{s}(\varphi)-M_{-1}(\varphi)-1\geqslant\varphi(z)
for all
z\in\{\psi=s\}
. In particular, we conclude that
\psi(z)\geqslant\varphi(z)
for all
z\in\{\psi\leqslant-1\}
. We conclude, as
\psi\geqslant\varphi
on
\{\psi>-1\}
.
∎

3.
Main Results

In this section, we prove our main results, Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
. Theorem
1.1
will follow from combining the subsolutions constructed in subsection
3.1
with the supersolutions constructed in subsection
3.2
; this is done in Theorem
3.5
. Corollary
1.2
is shown in subsection
3.4
.

As mentioned in the Introduction, we will not work with
\psi
directly, but rather with a bounded
m
-sh function
h
which has an
m
-polar minimum set; if it happens that
K_{p}(h)\in\mathcal{E}_{m}(\Omega)
, then we (essentially) recover the set-up in the Introduction. Working with
h
directly however is much more flexible.

For the rest of the section, we fix constants
\varepsilon,\delta>0
and assume that
h
satisfies the following assumptions:

•

h\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)
,
0\leqslant h\leqslant 1
,

•

\{h=0\}
is non-empty and
m
-polar,

•

h\in C^{2}(\{h>0\})
, and

•

i\partial\overline{\partial}h\geqslant_{m}\varepsilon h^{1-\delta}\omega
on
\{h>0\}
.

3.1.
Subsolution

We start with a lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
p<1
is such that:

(3.1)

i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p}(h)\geqslant_{m}-ch^{\widetilde{\gamma}}K_{p}^{\prime}(h)\omega=-ch^{p-1+\widetilde{\gamma}}\omega\quad\text{ on }\{h>0\},

for some
c>0
and
\widetilde{\gamma}>1-\delta
. Then for each
p<q\leqslant 1
, there exists some
C>0
such that
K_{q}(h)+C\rho\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

On
\{h>0\}
, we have:

\displaystyle i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{q}(h)

\displaystyle=K_{q}^{\prime}(h)i\partial\overline{\partial}h+K^{\prime\prime}_{q}(h)i\partial h\wedge\overline{\partial}h

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{q}^{\prime\prime}(h)}{K_{p}^{\prime\prime}(h)}i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p}(h)+\left(K^{\prime}_{q}(h)-\frac{K_{q}^{\prime\prime}(h)}{K_{p}^{\prime\prime}(h)}K_{p}^{\prime}(h)\right)i\partial\overline{\partial}h

(3.2)

\displaystyle\geqslant_{m}-c\frac{1-q}{1-p}h^{\widetilde{\gamma}}K_{q}^{\prime}(h)\omega+\varepsilon\frac{q-p}{1-p}K_{q}^{\prime}(h)h^{1-\delta}\omega.

We see that
K_{q}(h)
is
m
-sh on
\{h^{\widetilde{\gamma}+\delta-1}<\frac{\varepsilon(q-p)}{c(1-q)}\}
– by Proposition
2.8
,
K_{q}(h)+C\rho
is
m
-sh on
\Omega
for
q>p
and
C
sufficiently large.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix
p\leqslant 0
, and suppose that
h,c,
and
\widetilde{\gamma}
are as in Lemma
3.1
. Fix
0<\gamma<\min\{\widetilde{\gamma}+\delta-1,\tfrac{\delta}{2}\}
, and set
\ell
to be the smallest integer such that
p+\ell\gamma>0
; define
q_{j}:=p+j\gamma
for each
j=1,\ldots,\ell
.

Let
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 1
and
0\leqslant\theta_{j}\leqslant 1
,
j=1,\ldots,\ell
, be smooth functions with:

•

\theta_{0}:=\theta
and
\theta_{\ell}=1
,

•

\mathrm{Supp}(\theta_{j-1})\subset\{\theta_{j}=1\}^{\circ}
for each
j
.

Let also
a_{j}>0
,
j=1,\ldots,\ell
, be positive constants. Then:

\theta K_{p}(h)+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\theta_{j}K_{q_{j}}(h)+C\rho\ \ \in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)

for all
C
sufficiently large.

Proof.

If we define
q_{0}:=p
and
a_{0}:=1
, then by Lemma
3.1
, it will suffice to show that
a_{j-1}\theta_{j-1}K_{q_{j-1}}(h)+a_{j}K_{q_{j-1}+\gamma}(h)+C\rho
is
m
-sh on
\{\theta_{j}=1\}
; since the sum is finite, we can conclude by increasing
C
at the end. We thus set
a_{j-1}=1
and drop the remaining indices, without loss of generality.

Recall that we are writing
L_{q}(t)
for the inverse of
K_{q}(s)
on
\{t<0\}
. Define the convex increasing function
\chi(t):=K_{q+\gamma}(L_{q}(t))
; we have
\chi^{\prime}(t)=L_{q}(t)^{\gamma}
,
\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)=\gamma L_{q}(t)^{\gamma-q}
, and
K_{q+\gamma}(h)=\chi(K_{q}(h))
.

If we choose
C
sufficiently large, then on
\{h>0\}
, by completing the square and using (
3.2
), we have:

\displaystyle i\partial\overline{\partial}(\theta K_{q}(h)+aK_{q+\gamma}(h))

\displaystyle=\theta i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{q}+i\partial K_{q}\wedge\overline{\partial}\theta+i\partial\theta\wedge\overline{\partial}K_{q}+K_{q}i\partial\overline{\partial}\theta

\displaystyle+\frac{a}{2}i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{q+\gamma}+\frac{a}{2}\chi^{\prime}(K_{q})i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{q}+\frac{a}{2}\chi^{\prime\prime}(K_{q})i\partial K_{q}\wedge\overline{\partial}K_{q}

\displaystyle\geqslant_{m}\frac{a}{2}i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{q+\gamma}+\left(\theta+\frac{a}{2}\chi^{\prime}(K_{q})\right)\left(-c\frac{1-q}{1-p}h^{\widetilde{\gamma}}\right)K_{q}^{\prime}\omega

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{a\chi^{\prime\prime}(K_{q})}i\partial\theta\wedge\overline{\partial}\theta+K_{q}i\partial\overline{\partial}\theta.

From (
3.2
) again, we have
i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{q+\gamma}\geqslant_{m}\varepsilon^{\prime}K_{q+\gamma}^{\prime}\,h^{1-\delta}\omega-C\omega
, for some small
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, so that:

i\partial\overline{\partial}(\theta K_{q}+aK_{q+\gamma})\geqslant_{m}\left(\frac{a\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}h^{q+\gamma-\delta}-Ch^{q-1+\widetilde{\gamma}}-Ch^{q-\gamma}-C\left\lvert K_{q}(h)\right\rvert-C\right)\omega.

Since
\gamma<\min\{\widetilde{\gamma}-1+\delta,\frac{\delta}{2}\}
, we have that
h^{q+\gamma-\delta}>C\max\{h^{q-1+\widetilde{\gamma}},h^{q-\gamma}\}
when
h
is sufficiently small, so that
\theta K_{q}+aK_{q+\gamma}+C\rho
is
m
-sh on
\{h>0\}
; we conclude by Proposition
2.8
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In Proposition
3.2
, if
p>\max\{1-\delta-\widetilde{\gamma},-\frac{\delta}{2}\}
, then we can take
\gamma>0
such that
q_{1}=p+\gamma>0
, so that
K_{q_{1}}(h)
is bounded.

3.2.
Supersolution

We now construct the supersolution we need; the construction is similar to the previous one.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
p\leqslant 0
is such that:

•

for each
p<q\leqslant 1
, there exists
C_{q}\geqslant 0
such that
K_{q}(h)+C_{q}\rho\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)
, and

•

there exists an
m
-elliptic linear operator
\Delta
such that:

\ \ \quad\quad\quad\quad\Delta K_{p}(h)\leqslant ch^{\widetilde{\gamma}}K_{p}^{\prime}(h)\Delta\rho\quad\quad\text{ on }\{h>0\},

for some
c>0
and
\widetilde{\gamma}>1-\delta
.

Then for any
0<\gamma<\min\{\widetilde{\gamma}-1+\delta,\tfrac{\delta}{2}\}
and any
a>0
, there exists a
C>0
such that:

\Delta(\theta K_{p}(h)-aK_{p+\gamma}(h)-C\rho)\leqslant 0\quad\text{ on }\{h>0\}.

Proof.

Choose
q
such that
p<q<\min\{p+\gamma,1\}
, and similar to before, define two convex increasing functions
\chi=K_{p+\gamma}\circ L_{q}
and
\widetilde{\chi}=K_{q}\circ L_{p}
. By completing the square and using the quasi-
m
-subharmonicity of
K_{q}
:

\displaystyle i\partial\overline{\partial}(\theta K_{p}(h)-aK_{p+\gamma}(h))

\displaystyle=\theta i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p}+i\partial K_{p}\wedge\overline{\partial}\theta+i\partial\theta\wedge\overline{\partial}K_{p}+K_{p}i\partial\overline{\partial}\theta

\displaystyle-\frac{a}{2}i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p+\gamma}-\frac{a}{2}\chi^{\prime}(K_{q})i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{q}-\frac{a}{2}\chi^{\prime\prime}(K_{q})\widetilde{\chi}^{\prime}(K_{p})^{2}i\partial K_{p}\wedge\overline{\partial}K_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant_{m}-\frac{a}{2}i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p+\gamma}+\theta i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p}+\frac{a}{2}C_{q}\chi^{\prime}(K_{q})\omega

\displaystyle+h^{p-\gamma}i\partial\theta\wedge\overline{\partial}\theta+C\left\lvert K_{p}\right\rvert\omega.

We can replace the
\omega
’s above with
i\partial\overline{\partial}\rho
, by the strict
m
-subharmonicity of
\rho
. Then, similar to Lemma
3.1
, the quasi-
m
-subharmonicity of
K_{q}
gives us the lower bound
i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p+\gamma}\geqslant_{m}-C_{q}\frac{1-p+\gamma}{1-q}h^{p+\gamma-q}i\partial\overline{\partial}\rho+\frac{p+\gamma-q}{1-q}h^{p+\gamma-1}i\partial\overline{\partial}h
. By
m
-ellipticity, we have
\Delta h\geqslant\varepsilon h^{1-\delta}\Delta\rho
, so that:

\Delta K_{p+\gamma}\geqslant(\varepsilon^{\prime}h^{p+\gamma-\delta}-Ch^{p+\gamma-q})\Delta\rho,

for some
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. It follows then that:

\Delta(\theta K_{p}-aK_{p+\gamma})\leqslant[-\varepsilon^{\prime}h^{p+\gamma-\delta}+C(h^{p-1+\widetilde{\gamma}}+h^{p+\gamma-q}+h^{p-\gamma}+\left\lvert K_{p}\right\rvert)]\Delta\rho.

By our choices of
q
and
\gamma
, this is bounded above by
C\Delta\rho
.
∎

3.3.
Proof of the Theorem

Theorem
1.1
will now follow from:

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that
h\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)
satisfies the conditions stated at the start of this section. Additionally, suppose that there exists
p\leqslant 0
,
c>0,\widetilde{\gamma}>1-\delta,
and an
m
-elliptic operator
\Delta
such that:

(1)

i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p}(h)\geqslant_{m}-ch^{\widetilde{\gamma}}K_{p}^{\prime}(h)\omega
on
\{h>0\}
and

(2)

\Delta K_{p}(h)\leqslant ch^{\widetilde{\gamma}}K_{p}^{\prime}(h)\Delta\rho
on
\{h>0\}
.

Set
\psi:=K_{p}(h)
. Then for any non-negative
\theta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, the envelope:

(3.3)

\psi_{\theta}:=\sup\{\varphi\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)\ |\ \varphi\leqslant\min\{0,\theta\psi+C\}\text{ for some }C\geqslant 0\}^{*}

satisfies:

(i)

\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi_{\theta})=\chi_{S_{\psi}}\theta^{m}\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi)
,

(ii)

\psi_{\theta}\geqslant(\max_{\Omega}\theta)\psi-C
,

(iii)

\psi_{\theta}\in L^{\infty}_{\textrm{loc}}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\{\theta>0\}})
, and

(iv)

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\rightarrow z\\
x\not\in S_{\psi}\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi_{\theta}}{\theta\psi}=1
for all
z\in S_{\psi}\cap\{\theta>0\}
.

If
p>\max\{1-\delta-\widetilde{\gamma},-\frac{\delta}{2}\}
, then
\psi_{\theta}
and
\theta\psi
have the same singularity type.

Proof.

The conditions
(i)
-
(iv)
will follow readily from the propositions above. First, condition
(ii)
is obvious. After perhaps multiplying
\theta
by a smooth cut-off which is
\equiv 1
on a neighborhood of
S_{\psi}
, we may choose functions
\theta_{j}
and constants
a_{j}
as in Proposition
3.2
. The resulting subsolution,
\underline{\psi}_{\theta}
, will be a competitor in the envelope (
3.3
), after perhaps subtracting a constant. Letting the supports of the
\theta_{j}
shrink to the support of
\theta
establishes
(iii)
.

We also see that
\psi_{\theta}\preccurlyeq\theta\psi
on
\{\theta>0\}
, since
\lim_{x\rightarrow S_{\psi}}\frac{\underline{\psi}_{\theta}}{\psi}=\theta
; Proposition
2.4
then implies
\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi_{\theta})\leqslant\chi_{S_{\psi}}\theta^{m}\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi)
.

Now, for any
a>0
, we can produce a supersolution
\overline{\psi}_{\theta}
by using Proposition
3.4
. By Corollary
2.11
, we have
\psi_{\theta}\leqslant\overline{\psi}_{\theta}
, from which the rest of the conditions follow immediately. The last statement follows from Remark
3.3
(for Theorem
1.1
, note that if
\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{m}(\Omega)
with
\mathrm{H}^{m}(\psi)=0
on
\{\psi>-\infty\}
, then we can take
\Delta=(i\partial\overline{\partial}\psi)^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}
arbitrarily large).
∎

3.4.
Proof of the Corollary

We now show Corollary
1.2
:

Corollary 3.6
.

Suppose that
V\subset\Omega
is a complex submanifold, with
\partial V=\emptyset
or
\partial V\subset\partial\Omega
, and complex codimension
k\geqslant m
. Set
h:=d_{\omega,V}^{2}
, the square of the Riemannian distance function to
V
induced by
\omega
, and
p:=1-\frac{k}{m}\leqslant 0
. Then for any
\theta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\theta\geqslant 0
, the envelope:

\psi_{\theta}:=\sup\{\varphi\in m\mathrm{SH}(\Omega)\ |\ \varphi\leqslant\min\{0,\theta K_{p}(h)+C\}\text{ for some }C\geqslant 0\}^{*}

satisfies the conclusions
(i)
-
(iv)
in Theorem
3.5
. Moreover, if
k<\frac{3}{2}m
, then
\psi_{\theta}
and
\theta K_{p}(h)
have the same singularity type.

Proof.

By shrinking
\Omega
if necessary, we can assume that
h
is smooth on
\Omega
. Then, by computing in geodesic cylindrical coordinates, one can show that
i\partial\overline{\partial}h
is approximately equal to the Euclidean distance to a codimension
k
hyperplane in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

[
15
]
; it follows that
h
is strictly
k
-sh (and consequently, it is strictly
m
-sh for any
m\leqslant k
).

More precisely, choose coordinates along a geodesic normal to
V
such that the first coordinate is in the radial direction, the last
n-k
coordinates are (parallel transports of directions) tangent to
V
, and the remaining coordinates are normal to
V
. Then we have:

\frac{1}{h}i\partial h\wedge\overline{\partial}h=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad i\partial\overline{\partial}h=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathrm{Id}_{k}&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)+o(h^{1/2}),

where we write
o(h^{1/2})
for a term such that
\tfrac{o(h^{1/2})}{h^{1/2}}\rightarrow 0
uniformly on compacts as
h\rightarrow 0
. It follows that
h
satisfies the conditions listed at the start of the section with
\delta=1
.

We now show that conditions (1) and (2) from Theorem
3.5
hold when
\widetilde{\gamma}
is arbitrarily close to
\tfrac{1}{2}
. We have:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K_{p}(h)^{\prime}}i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p}(h)

\displaystyle=i\partial\overline{\partial}h+\frac{p-1}{h}i\partial h\wedge\overline{\partial}h

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1-\tfrac{k}{m}&0&0\\
0&\mathrm{Id}_{k-1}&0\\
0&0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)+o(h^{1/2}).

The leading order term is
m
-sh, by direct computation, so that
i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p}(h)\geqslant_{m}-ch^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\eta}{2}}K_{p}^{\prime}(h)\omega
for any
\eta,c>0
sufficiently small. This shows (1).

To see (2), start by using Proposition
3.2
to choose
a_{j},C>0
and
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}-\eta
such that
\widetilde{K}:=K_{p}(h)+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}K_{p+j\gamma}(h)+C\rho
is
m
-sh. A short computation in cylindrical coordinates shows that, on
\{h>0\}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{m}(K_{p}(h))

\displaystyle=K_{p}^{\prime}(h)^{m}\left(i\partial\overline{\partial}h+\frac{m(p-1)}{h}i\partial h\wedge\overline{\partial}h\right)\wedge(i\partial\overline{\partial}h)^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{p}^{\prime}(h)^{m}o(h^{1/2})\omega^{n},

after throwing away the leading order term. It follows that:

\displaystyle i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p}(h)\wedge(i\partial\overline{\partial}\widetilde{K})^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{H}^{m}(K_{p})+C\left\lvert i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p+\gamma}\wedge(i\partial\overline{\partial}K_{p})^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{p}^{\prime}(h)^{m-1}[K_{p}^{\prime}(h)o(h^{1/2})+CK_{p+\gamma}^{\prime}(h)]\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ch^{\frac{1}{2}-\eta}K_{p}^{\prime}(h)(i\partial\overline{\partial}\rho)\wedge(i\partial\overline{\partial}\widetilde{K})^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}.

Condition (2) is thus satisfied for
\Delta=(i\partial\overline{\partial}\widetilde{K})^{m-1}\wedge\omega^{n-m}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}-\eta
.
∎

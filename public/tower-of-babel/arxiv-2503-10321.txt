The Profinite Rigidity of Free Metabelian Groups
Julian Wykowski

Abstract
. We prove that finitely generated free metabelian groups Ψ n \Psi_{n} are profinitely rigid in the absolute sense: they are distinguished by their finite quotients among all finitely generated residually finite groups. The proof is based on a previous result of the author governing profinite rigidity for modules over Noetherian domains, as well as a homological characterisation of free metabelian groups due to Groves–Miller.



Dedicated to Maria Wykowska

1.
Introduction

Is it possible to detect the isomorphism type of a group in its finite images? This question, known as
profinite rigidity
, has been a major theme in group theory for the past half-century (see
[
22
, Chapter 3]
for an introduction and
[
17
,
2
]
for a survey). Usually, one works within the framework of profinite completions, enabling one to appeal to the formalism of topological groups. Indeed, the profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
of a discrete group
\Gamma
is the profinite group given by the inverse limit of the inverse system of finite quotients of
\Gamma
. We then say that a finitely generated residually finite group
\Gamma
is
profinitely rigid
in the absolute sense if
\widehat{\Gamma}\cong\widehat{\Delta}
implies
\Gamma\cong\Delta
for any finitely generated residually finite group
\Delta
. This is equivalent to the property that the isomorphism type of
\Gamma
is distinguished by its collection of finite quotients among all finitely generated residually finite groups
[
5
]
. Despite considerable research, the absolute question has been answered for remarkably few groups: see
[
25
, §1]
for an exhaustive list. In particular, the question of profinite rigidity for free and free solvable (of a fixed derived length
k\in\mathbb{N}
) groups, attributed to Remeslennikov (see
[
14
, Question 15]
and
[
12
, Question 5.48]
), has gained significant attention (cf. the surveys
[
3
,
2
,
17
]
). Although there exist elegant results resolving the question in certain relative settings
[
3
,
10
,
24
]
, the absolute case remains widely open.

Question 1

(Remeslennikov)
.

Are finitely generated free (free
k
-solvable) groups profinitely rigid in the absolute sense?

In this article, we answer Question
1
in the case
k=2
, i.e. for free metabelian groups. The free metabelian group of rank
n
is the maximal metabelian (that is, solvable of derived length 2) quotient
\Psi_{n}
of the free group
\Phi_{n}
on
n
generators. It is finitely generated but not finitely presentable
[
1
]
.

Theorem A
.

Finitely generated free metabelian groups are profinitely rigid in the absolute sense.

Theorem
A
contrasts with
[
15
]
, where infinite families of finitely generated metabelian groups with isomorphic profinite completions were constructed, and
[
13
]
, where uncountable such families of solvable groups were found. We invite the reader also to compare Theorem
A
with
[
10
, Conjecture 2]
.

The strategy of proof is outlined as follows. First, we decompose the group
\Psi_{n}
as an extension of the free abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by the commutator subgroup
[\Psi_{n},\Psi_{n}]
, the latter forming an infinitely generated abelian subgroup of
\Psi_{n}
which acquires naturally the structure of a finitely generated module over the group algebra
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
. Via classical results pertaining to profinite completions, we find that any finitely generated residually finite group
\Delta
profinitely isomorphic to
\Psi_{n}
must also be of this form. A result of Groves–Miller
[
6
]
then allows us to translate the question of whether
\Delta
is free metabelian to a statement about a certain natural extension of the augmentation ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}
by the module
[\Delta,\Delta]
. The latter statement is then established via the machinery of profinite rigidity over Noetherian domains, developed by the author in
[
25
]
, in conjunction with some homological machinery for profinite completions of modules developed in Section
3
of the present article. A key step in the proof appeals to the celebrated result of Quillen and Suslin
[
16
,
20
]
that projective modules over rings of integral polynomials are free.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we outline background results regarding metabelian groups and their homological algebra, as well as the theory of profinite rigidity for modules over Noetherian domains developed by the author in
[
25
]
.

2.1.
Metabelian Groups

A discrete group
\Gamma
is said to be
metabelian
if its commutator subgroup
[\Gamma,\Gamma]
is abelian, or equivalently, if it is solvable of derived length at most two. We invite the reader to
[
4
, Chapter II.6]
for an overview of the history and properties of these groups. Writing
\alpha_{\Gamma}\colon\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Upsilon
for the abelianisation of a metabelian group
\Gamma
, we obtain a short exact sequence of groups

(2.1)

0\to M_{\Gamma}=[\Gamma,\Gamma]\to\Gamma\xrightarrow{\alpha_{\Gamma}}\Upsilon\to 0

with abelian commutator subgroup
M_{\Gamma}=[\Gamma,\Gamma]
. The supergroup
\Gamma
then acts on
M_{\Gamma}
by conjugation, which factors through the projection
\alpha_{\Gamma}\colon\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Upsilon
as
M_{\Gamma}
acts trivially on itself. Thus
M_{\Gamma}
acquires naturally the structure of a module over the group algebra
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
whereby
\Upsilon
acts by conjugation via any equivalent choice of set-theoretic section of the projection
\alpha_{\Gamma}
. Moreover,
[
7
, Theorem VI.6.3]
yields the short exact sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
-modules

(2.2)

0\to M_{\Gamma}\to N_{\Gamma}={\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]}\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{I}_{\Upsilon}\to 0

where
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}
denote the augmentation ideals of
\Gamma
and
\Upsilon
, respectively. The boundary isomorphism in the long exact sequence associated via
\Ext_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]}^{\ast}(-,-)
to the short exact sequence of the augmentation ideal then maps the class in
H^{2}(\Upsilon,M_{\Gamma})
corresponding to the extension (
2.1
) to the class in
\Ext^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}}(\mathcal{I}_{\Upsilon},M_{\Gamma})
corresponding to the extension (
2.2
), as per
[
7
, Exercise VI.6.1]
.

The free objects in the category of metabelian groups are the
free metabelian groups
. To construct the free metabelian group
\Psi_{n}
on
n
generators, begin with the free group
\Phi_{n}
on
n
generators and take the maximal metabelian quotient

\Psi_{n}=\frac{\Phi_{n}}{[[\Phi_{n},\Phi_{n}],[\Phi_{n},\Phi_{n}]]}

whose commutator subgroup has trivial commutator itself and is thus abelian. For
n>1
, the group
\Psi_{n}
is
n
-generated but not finitely presentable
[
1
]
. Its abelianisation is isomorphic to the free abelian group
\Psi_{n}^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
on
n
generators. For
n=2
, the commutator subgroup
M_{\Psi_{2}}=[\Psi_{2},\Psi_{2}]
is free as a module over the group algebra
\mathbb{Z}[\Psi_{2}^{\mathrm{ab}}]\cong\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
with conjugacy module structure
[
11
, Proposition 14.4]
; however, for
n>2
, this is no longer true. Nonetheless, we obtain the following homological characterisation of free metabelian groups due to Groves–Miller
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Corollary 3 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated metabelian group and
\alpha\colon\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow\Upsilon
be its abelianisation. The group
\Gamma
is free metabelian of rank
n
if and only if
\Upsilon\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and the
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
-module

N_{\Gamma}={\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]}\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}

is free of rank
n
.

Theorem
2.1
will be essential in the proof of profinite rigidity for free metabelian groups. To apply it, we shall make use of the theory of profinite rigidity for modules over Noetherian domains developed by the author in
[
25
]
.

2.2.
Profinite Rigidity over Noetherian Domains

Akin to the profinite rigidity of groups one may also ask the question of profinite rigidity for modules over a Noetherian domain
\Lambda
: to what extent are these objects determined by their finite quotients? Given a
\Lambda
-module
M
, one defines the
\Lambda
-profinite completion
\widehat{M}
as the topological
\Lambda
-modules given by the inverse limit of the inverse system of
\Lambda
-epimorphic images of
M
. We then say that a
\Lambda
-module
M
is
\Lambda
-
profinitely rigid
if
\widehat{M}\cong\widehat{N}
implies
M\cong N
for any finitely generated residually finite
\Lambda
-module
N
. We refer the reader to
[
18
, Chapter 5]
for details on profinite modules and
[
25
]
for details on profinite rigidity over Noetherian domains. We shall work exclusively in settings where
\Lambda
is finitely generated (as a
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra), in which case all finitely generated modules are automatically residually finite
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
. The following result plays a central role in the proof of Theorem
A
. We say that a ring
\Lambda
is
homologically taut
if all finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-modules are free over
\Lambda
.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem C in
[
25
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a homologically taut finitely generated Noetherian domain. Finitely generated free
\Lambda
-modules are
\Lambda
-profinitely rigid.

The question whether the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is homologically taut was asked by Serre in 1955 and resolved in 1976 independently by Quillen
[
16
]
and Suslin
[
20
]
. We shall require the following localised formulation of this celebrated result, presently known as the Quillen–Suslin theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(Corollary 7.4 in
[
20
]
)
.

The domain of integral Laurent polynomials in
n
variables
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
is homologically taut for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

There is an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
-modules
\widehat{N_{\Gamma}}\cong\widehat{N_{\Delta}}
.

To complete the proof of Theorem
A
, note that the group algebra of the free abelian group
\Upsilon\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the ring of Laurent polynomials
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}\cong\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
which forms a finitely generated Noetherian domain by Hilbert’s Basis Theorem. Moreover,
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
is homologically taut by Theorem
2.3
. Consequently, we may appeal to Theorem
2.2
to find that the free
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
-module
N_{\Gamma}
is
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
-profinitely rigid. The profinite isomorphism in Lemma
4.2
then implies the existence of an isomorphism of finitely generated discrete
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
-modules
N_{\Gamma}\cong N_{\Delta}
. In particular, the
{\mathbb{Z}[\Upsilon]}
-module
N_{\Delta}
is free of rank
n
. Another application of Theorem
2.1
then shows that the metabelian group
\Delta
must be free metabelian of rank
n
. Hence
\Delta\cong\Gamma
and
\Gamma
is profinitely rigid in the absolute sense. This completes the proof of Theorem
A
.

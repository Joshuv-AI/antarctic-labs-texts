External Validation Cannot Participate in Selecting a Rheumatoid Arthritis Diagnostic Model [Letter]
Authors: Quanhui Chen
Published: 2026

Dear editor

Han et al developed a 13-feature diagnostic model for rheumatoid arthritis and reported areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUCs) of 0.987 in the training set, 0.960 in GSE1919, and 0.808 in GSE29746.1 The authors also state that the final model was selected by its average AUC across the training set and both datasets labeled as validation cohorts.1 Disclosure of that rule is not our new observation. Using the three-decimal AUCs reported in Figure 3A of the original article, we reconstructed a conservative pairwise leave-one-validation-cohort-out sensitivity analysis to ask whether the selected algorithm remained stable when either validation cohort was protected until evaluation. At least one challenger outranked the published model in each protected-cohort scenario.

When GSE29746 was withheld from selection, the mean AUC across the training set and GSE1919 was 0.9735 for the published Stepglm(backward) plus gradient boosting machine model. The corresponding mean for the glmBoost plus gradient boosting machine model was 0.9975, so this challenger would outrank the published model under the study’s stated averaging rule. Yet in the protected GSE29746 cohort, its AUC was 0.707 rather than 0.808, a decrease of 0.101. Conversely, when GSE1919 was withheld, the mean across the training set and GSE29746 was 0.8975 for the published model and 0.8990 for the Lasso plus gradient boosting machine model. That challenger would therefore rank first in this pairwise comparison, but its AUC in the protected GSE1919 cohort was 0.800 rather than 0.960, a decrease of 0.160. These calculations do not establish either challenger as a globally optimal model, because the published figure does not report a complete nested selection exercise. They do show that at least one alternative outranks the published model whenever either validation cohort is excluded from selection, and that the resulting choice performs substantially worse in the cohort kept unseen.

This instability changes the interpretation of the reported validation results. Reusing finite evaluation data to choose among many candidate pipelines makes subsequent performance on those data conditional on the selection process; ordinary cross-validation within the development data does not remove that dependence.2,3 Accordingly, the AUCs of 0.960 and 0.808 describe post-selection performance, not independent estimates of transportability. The issue is not merely terminological. In both pairwise analyses, an alternative outranked the published model during selection but lost 0.101 or 0.160 AUC in the cohort kept unseen. These differences are large enough to change whether the selected pipeline can be treated as robustly externally validated.

A direct resolution is feasible with the existing datasets and code. All preprocessing, feature filtering, ensemble construction, hyperparameter tuning, and model choice should be confined to the development data using nested resampling. The entire fitted pipeline, including the selected features, should then be locked and applied once to each external cohort, with selection frequencies and AUC uncertainty reported.4,5 Only after a locked model shows independent discrimination should calibration, clinical thresholds, and decision analysis be evaluated. If sample size makes two independent external evaluations too imprecise, one cohort could be prespecified for model selection and the other retained for final testing, with that role assignment stated before analysis. Either approach would answer whether the 13-feature model, rather than a model chosen partly because of its performance in the test cohorts, generalizes.

This qualification need not diminish the experimental evidence for FOSL2, JUN, and EGR1 expression in the collagen-induced arthritis model.1 It narrows a different claim: the current analysis supports biologically plausible candidate markers, but it does not yet provide independent external validation of the diagnostic model. We therefore invite the authors to report a development-only selection analysis followed by locked testing, or to relabel the two cohort AUCs as selection-conditioned estimates. Their response would determine whether the observed ranking instability reflects only limited cohort size or a substantive lack of model transportability.

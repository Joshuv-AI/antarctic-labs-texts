Reply to: an assessment of the cytochrome P450 2-hydroxyisoflavanone synthase (2-HIS) crystal structure
Authors: Xiaoqiang Wang, Haiyun Pan, Someswar R Sagurthi, Richard A Dixon
Published: 2026

replying to: T. Poulos Communications Biology Matters Arising 10.1038/s42003-026-10258-0 (2026).

2-HIS is a novel cytochrome P450 involved in isoflavonoid biosynthesis. Earlier work reported the first crystal structure of this enzyme and revealed unusual structural features. Further comparative analysis suggests that its unique conformation may be related to its dual function and ring-migration activity, while ions observed in the structure likely bind only to the protein surface and do not affect catalysis. Further studies are needed to fully understand its mechanism since only one structure is currently available.

2-Hydroxyisoflavanone synthase (2-HIS) is a membrane-bound plant cytochrome P450 enzyme catalyzing a very unusual reaction, the coupled hydroxylation and aryl-ring migration of a flavanone to yield a 2-hydroxyisoflavanone. This is the entry point in the biosynthesis of isoflavonoid natural products that are dietary phytoestrogens and play key roles in plant defense. Our paper “The protein conformational basis of isoflavone biosynthesis” published in Communications Biology on November 15, 2022, reveals a novel cytochrome P450 conformation and heme and substrate binding modes for this enzyme1. The location and orientation of the heme in 2-HIS are dramatically different from those in other known P450 structures. The heme in the structure of 2-HIS moves ~8 Å away toward helix C (Fig. 3c in the paper1), and the substrate entrance with shorter B-C and F-G loops is also substantially shifted down (Fig. 5 in the paper1), resulting in a deep substrate binding pocket located at the edge of the heme and close to its propionate groups, rather than at the distal side as in other known P450 structures. This novel conformation provides an initial structural snapshot and basis to study the mechanism and possible conformation dynamics of the dual function 2-HIS enzyme. Poulus in his commentary suggests that 2-HIS is a traditional P450 and the novel conformation we report is due to a sulfate ion that binds near the heme.

2-HIS protein crystals were initially obtained from two solutions: (1) 0.2 M ammonium tartrate and 20% PEG 3350, and (2) 0.15 M K2HPO4/NaH2PO4, pH 6.5, 20% PEG 400, and 15% PEG 1000. The initial crystals only diffracted to ~3.6 Å resolution. The crystallization was optimized by varying the concentration of precipitants (e.g., PEG 3350), salts and additives, buffers and pH. The optimized crystallization solution contained 200 mM (NH4)2SO4, 200 mM ammonium tartrate dibasic, 50 mM imidazole, 10% FOS-choline-8, 30% (w/v) PEG3350, and 0.1 M K2HPO4/NaH2PO4 (pH6.8). Neither of the initial crystallization solutions contained sulfate anion, so its presence does not appear to be required for crystallization of 2-HIS.

Several other P450s have also been crystallized from solutions containing sulfate anion. For example, P450terp was crystallized from 100 mM Pipes, 16% PEG 8000, 15 mM dithiothreitol, and 40 mM MgSO4 (pH 7.0), and the hemoprotein domain of P450BM-3 was crystallized from 0.8 M (NH4)2SO4, 10 mM dithiothreitol, and 0.1 M citrate buffer (pH 6.0)2. P450 2C5 was crystallized in a solution containing 2.5 M (NH4)2SO4, 0.1 M sodium cacodylate (pH 6.5), and 2.4 mM CYMAL-53. Cytochrome P450 CYP121 was crystallized from 0.1 M sodium cacodylate-HCl buffer at pH 5–6.25 and 1.5–2.3 M (NH4)2SO44. Mycobacterium marinum CYP51 was crystallized from 0.2 M (NH4)2SO4, 25%PEG 3350, and 0.1 M Bis-Tris (pH 6.5)5. P450terp, P450BM-3, P450 2C5, CYP121, and CYP51 all exhibit traditional P450 structures, indicating that the presence of sulfate anions does not alter the P450 conformation and the architecture of the active site in these cases. If sulfate anions do cause such changes in 2-HIS this is, as Poulos suggests, an example of the unprecedented flexibility of the P450 heme pocket in this particular enzyme.

Poulos posits that the form of 2-HIS we have crystallized is an inactive form of the enzyme. However, enzyme assays with liquiritigenin as substrate confirmed that the 2-HIS enzyme from dissolved crystals was catalytically active (Fig. 7 in the paper1). Although it is challenging for 2-HIS enzyme to change its conformation observed in the crystal structure to that of other traditional P450s when in solution, we cannot of course exclude this possibility based on present data.

As described in the original paper1, the molecular docking was performed to dock the substrate liquiritigenin into the 2-HIS putative substrate binding pocket using an automated docking program GOLD6. The docking calculations were restricted to the predicted binding pocket by defining the active site with heme co-factor. The hydrogen bonds and van der Waals interactions between 2-HIS enzyme and substrate liquiritigenin were analyzed to identify the optimal binding mode. Minor manual adjustments of the docking solution were made using the program COOT7. The docking study showed that the C3 atom of the substrate fits close to the heme propionate groups. The interactions between substrate and enzyme are mainly hydrophobic. Some potential hydrogen bond interactions were also observed between substrate and Asn117 or Ser308 (Fig. 4 in the paper1).

We initially presented a brief discussion about a possible 2-HIS mechanism based on comparisons with peroxidase (HRP) structures, which was further developed and revised by including a figure to illustrate this mechanism (Fig. 6 in the paper1). It has been reported that substrates can bind to heme at the δ-heme edge in many peroxidases, particularly for aromatic substrates, and can also bind at the γ-heme edge, e.g., ascorbate binds to the γ-heme edge of ascorbate peroxidase8–10. The putative substrate binding pocket in the 2-HIS structure suggested that the substrate may also bind at the heme edge. Therefore, the mechanism of 2-HIS with such a novel conformation may be similar to that of peroxidases. The current version of the proposed 2-HIS mechanism was an initial hypothesis only. We agree that this may not necessarily be correct. It is also possible that 2-HIS catalyzes the ring-migration reaction by changing its conformation observed in the crystal structure to that of other traditional P450s, and then performs the hydroxylation reaction as other traditional P450s. Further studies will be needed to understand the mechanism of this novel P450 enzyme 2-HIS with dual functions.

Poulos suggests that an imidazole molecule fits into the electron density near the heme iron. In an early dataset at 2.2 Å resolution imidazole was also included in the model. We therefore agree with Poulos’ suggestion.

The structures of human P450 CYP2C9 have been well-characterized11 with extensive mechanistic insight and detailed annotation of active-site, making it a useful benchmark for assessing canonical P450 features and overall fold integrity. In addition, the structure of CYP2C9 complexed with warfarin (PDB 1OG5) provided a guide for identifying the putative substrate binding pocket of 2-HIS and docking substrate to 2-HIS structure. Therefore, we selected the structure of CYP2C9 complexed with warfarin for structural comparison with 2-HIS and analysis of enzyme-substrate interactions.

We also compared the 2-HIS structure with other P450s structures containing sulfate anions. In the structure of P450 2C5 (PDB 1DT6)3, three sulfate anions were identified on the surface of the protein molecule, interacting with Arg 132, R261 and Lys 418, respectively. In the CYP121 structures (PDB 4G46 and 4G47)4, four sulfate anions were identified, and three of them were located on the surface of the protein molecule. The fourth sulfate anion was located in the substrate binding pocket near the heme, and interacts with Arg 386.

In the structure of Mycobacterium marinum CYP51 (PDB: 7TEF)5, five sulfate anions were observed and located on the surface of the protein molecule. Sulfate anion 503 interacts with R381 and R393 which is in the heme-binding loop and next to the proximal cysteine C394 and heme. In the structure of 2-HIS, three sulfate anions are observed on the surface of 2-HIS molecules. Its sulfate anion 601 interacts with Arg 446 and Arg447 which are in the heme-binding loop and close to the proximal cysteine C449 and heme, similar to the Sulfate anion 503 in the structure of CYP51. The comparison suggests that the strong electrostatic sulfate anions mainly bind on the surface of protein molecules, and may fit any accessible cavities containing basic residues and/or polar residues. Further studies are needed to explore if sulfate anions induce any conformational changes.

To date, only one structure of the native 2-HIS protein has been obtained, making it challenging to elucidate its structure-function relationships and mechanism. We thank Dr Poulos for his insightful comments, and hope that this discussion will direct further progress in the field. Further studies of 2-HIS structures and activity under alternative conditions (e.g., in the absence of sulfate and in the presence of different detergents, additives, and substrates) will be essential to fully understand the structure-function relationships, catalytic mechanism and potential conformational dynamics of this unusual P450 enzyme.

Methods

Comparative analysis of sulfate anion binding sites in P450 structures

Structures of P450s containing sulfate anions were obtained from Protein Data Bank (PDB), including P450 2C5 (PDB 1DT6)3, CYP121 (PDB 4G46 and 4G47)4, CYP51 (PDB: 7TEF)5. Structural comparative analyses were performed using COOT7 by superimposing these P450 structures onto that of 2-HIS, followed by detailed analysis and comparison of the sulfate anion binding sites.

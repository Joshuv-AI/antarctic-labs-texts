Paving the way to a 𝖳 \operatorname{\mathsf{T}} -coercive method for the wave equation
Daniel Hoonhout, Richard Löscher, Carolina Urzúa-Torres

Abstract
In this paper, we take a first step toward introducing a space-time transformation operator 𝖳 \operatorname{\mathsf{T}} that establishes 𝖳 \operatorname{\mathsf{T}} -coercivity for the weak variational formulation of the wave equation in space and time on bounded Lipschitz domains. As a model problem, we study the ordinary differential equation (ODE) u ′′ + μ ​ u = f u^{\prime\prime}+\mu u=f for μ > 0 \mu>0 , which is linked to the wave equation via a Fourier expansion in space. For its weak formulation, we introduce a transformation operator 𝖳 μ \operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu} that establishes 𝖳 μ \operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu} -coercivity of the bilinear form yielding an unconditionally stable Galerkin-Bubnov formulation with error estimates independent of μ \mu . The novelty of the current approach is the explicit dependence of the transformation on μ \mu which, when extended to the framework of partial differential equations, yields an operator acting in both time and space. We pay particular attention to keeping the trial space as a standard Sobolev space, simplifying the error analysis, while only the test space is modified. The theoretical results are complemented by numerical examples.



1
Introduction

There has been an increased interest in finding space-time formulations for
the wave equation that lead to unconditionally stable discretizations. Some of
them rely on analyzing the exact mapping properties of the space-time wave
operator in a variational setting

[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
]
,
including weak and least squares formulations. These often lead to a setting
beyond standard Sobolev spaces, that is cumbersome to discretize. Others though,
stay in the framework of Sobolev spaces, adding stabilization terms

[
3
,
7
]
, or applying special
test functions
[
1
,
2
,
8
]
.
The overall goal of all these approaches is to open the door to adaptivity in
space and time simultaneously and without additional computational cost.

In this paper, we follow the idea from
[
8
]
and consider a
second order ordinary differential equation (ODE)
u^{\prime\prime}+\mu u=f
with
\mu>0
.
This ODE can be linked to the wave equation and used to find a transformation
operator such that one obtains a coercive and continuous bilinear form for the
corresponding variational formulation. However, unlike previous approaches, we
take pains to make sure the resulting transformation also depends on
\mu
, and
thus, when extended to the partial differential equation framework, can lead to
a transformation that not only acts on time, but also on space. Moreover, we are
interested in keeping standard Sobolev spaces as our solution spaces, such that
the error analysis of future space-time methods and understanding of the solution
is straightforward. With this purpose in mind, we deliberately insist on using
the arising transformation to change the test space. Interestingly, this results
in a variational formulation with a simple bilinear form, while only the
right-hand side becomes more involved.

This contribution focuses on the key ideas for finding this transformation and
therefore restricts attention to the ODE case. Ongoing and future work deals
with the extension to the actual wave equation.

2
Motivation

Let
T>0
,
\mu>0
and
f:(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
be given and let us consider the ordinary
differential equation to find
u:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}(t)+\mu u(t)

\displaystyle=f(t)\quad\text{ for }t\in(0,T),\qquad u(0)=u^{\prime}(0)=0.

(1)

For the weak form, we multiply by
a smooth function
v
and integrate by parts to get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big[u^{\prime\prime}(t)v(t)+\mu u(t)v(t)\big]\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\big[-u^{\prime}(t)v^{\prime}(t)+\mu u(t)v(t)\big]\,dt+u^{\prime}(T)v(T).

Now, in order to get rid of the term
u^{\prime}(T)
, about which we have no information,
and to ensure well-defined integrals, we need the spaces

\displaystyle H^{1}_{0,}(0,T):=\{v\in H^{1}(0,T)|\,v(0)=0\},\qquad H^{1}_{,0}(0,T):=\{v\in H^{1}(0,T)|\,v(T)=0\}.

With these, we consider the variational formulation: For
f\in[H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)]^{\prime}
,
find
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
such that for all
v\in H_{,0}^{1}(0,T)

b_{\mu}(u,v):=\int_{0}^{T}\big[-u^{\prime}(t)v^{\prime}(t)+\mu u(t)v(t)\big]\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}f(t)v(t)\,dt.

(2)

Theorem 2.1
.

[
10
, Lemma 4.2.1, Lemmata 4.2.3–4.2.4]

The bilinear form

b_{\mu}:H_{0,}^{1}(0,T)\times H_{,0}^{1}(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
defined
in (
2
) satisfies

(B1)

Boundedness: For all
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
and
v\in H_{,0}^{1}(0,T)
it holds that

|b_{\mu}(u,v)|\leq\left(1+\frac{4T^{2}\mu}{\pi^{2}}\right){\left\lVert{u^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(0,T)}{\left\lVert{v^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

(B2)

Bounded invertibility: For all
u\in H_{0,}^{1}(0,T)
it holds
that

\frac{2}{2+T\sqrt{\mu}}{\left\lVert{u^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{(0,T)}\leq\sup_{0\neq v\in H_{,0}^{1}(0,T)}\frac{b_{\mu}(u,v)}{{\left\lVert{v^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{(0,T)}}.

(B3)

Surjectivity: For all
0\neq v\in H_{,0}^{1}(0,T)
there exists

u_{v}\in H_{0,}^{1}(0,T)
such that
b_{\mu}(u_{v},v)\neq 0.

The above theorem guarantees that the weak formulation
(
2
) admits a unique solution
u\in H_{0,}^{1}(0,T)
for all
right-hand sides
f\in[H_{,0}^{1}(0,T)]^{\prime}
and fixed
\mu>0
. But, the obtained
bounds depend on
\mu>0
and, in particular, they degenerate
when
\mu\to\infty
.

Our main contribution is to propose a formulation where we keep the
solution
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
in the standard Sobolev space but
get
\mu
-independent bounds. For this, we introduce

{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}:=\sqrt{{\left\lVert{w^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{(0,T)}^{2}+\mu{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert}_{(0,T)}^{2}},

which resembles the space-time
H^{1}
-norm, and defines an equivalent norm on

H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
and
H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)
. In what follows, we will construct a
transformation operator
\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}:H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)\to H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)
such that the
bilinear form satisfies

{\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}^{2}=b_{\mu}(u,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u).

3
A space-time transformation!? Well, it’s complex…

In the following we slightly abuse notation and use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

to denote both the
L^{2}
-inner product and the
L^{2}
-duality pairing.
Now, using integration by parts, we get

\langle u^{\prime},v\rangle+\langle u,v^{\prime}\rangle=0,\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T),\forall v\in H^{1}_{,0}(0,T).

(3)

We introduce the operators

\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{\pm}:=\pm i\partial_{t}+\sqrt{\mu},

where
i
denotes the imaginary unit. Using (
3
), we have that

\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}u,\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}v\rangle=-\langle u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\rangle+\mu\langle u,v\rangle+i\sqrt{\mu}(\langle u^{\prime},v\rangle+\langle u,v^{\prime}\rangle)=b_{\mu}(u,v).

Moreover, note that for any real valued function
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}u,\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{-}u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}u,\overline{\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}u}\rangle={\left\lVert{\Re(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}u)}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+{\left\lVert{\Im(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}u)}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu{\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+{\left\lVert{u^{\prime}}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}={\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Here, for any function
w
,
\Re(w)
and
\Im(w)
denote its real and
imaginary parts, respectively, while
\overline{w}
corresponds to its
complex conjugate.

The above motivates us to introduce the transformation operator

\operatorname{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}:=\left(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}\right)^{-1}\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{-},

(4)

since for
v=\operatorname{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}u
we then formally have that

b_{\mu}(u,v)=\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}u,\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}\operatorname{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}u\rangle=\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}u,\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{-}u\rangle={\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}^{2}.

3.1
The transformation operator: Its actual closed form formula

We proceed to compute the transformation operator defined in
(
4
) acting on a function in
H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
.
Moreover, in order to stay consistent, we would like that the operator

\operatorname{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}
maps functions from
H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
to functions with zero terminal
condition.

As the solution of

\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}z:=iz^{\prime}+\sqrt{\mu}z=q,\,\text{in }(0,T),\quad z(T)=0,

can be explicitly computed to be

z(t)=\left(\left(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}\right)^{-1}q\right)(t)=i\int_{t}^{T}e^{i\sqrt{\mu}(t-s)}q(s)\,ds,

we have that for any
w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)

\displaystyle v(t)

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}w)(t)=\left(\left(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{+}\right)^{-1}\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}^{-}w\right)(t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{t}^{T}e^{i\sqrt{\mu}(t-s)}(-iw^{\prime}(s)+\sqrt{\mu}w(s))\,ds.

(5)

3.2
A convenient REALization

We are only interested in testing with real valued functions
v
. However, for
any real valued function
w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
, we see from (
3.1
)
that
\operatorname{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}w
is complex valued.
To circumvent the use of complex functions, we now consider only the real part
of the transformation operator and define for
w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)

\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w(t)\hskip-1.99997pt:=\Re(\operatorname{\mathcal{T}}_{\mu}\hskip-1.99997ptw(t))=\int_{t}^{T}\hskip-1.99997pt\left[\cos(\sqrt{\mu}(t-s))w^{\prime}(s)-\sqrt{\mu}\sin(\sqrt{\mu}(t-s))w(s)\right]\,\hskip-1.99997ptds.

Lemma 3.1
.

The operator
\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}:H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)\to H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)
is well-defined, bounded and
satisfies for all
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
and all
q\in L^{2}(0,T)

\int_{0}^{T}\left[-(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)^{\prime}(t)+\mu\hskip-3.99994pt\int_{t}^{T}\hskip-3.99994pt(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)(s)\,ds\right]q(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\left[u^{\prime}(t)+\mu\hskip-3.99994pt\int_{t}^{T}\hskip-3.99994ptu(s)\,ds\right]q(t)\,dt.

Proof.

By construction
(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)(T)=0
and we compute

(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)^{\prime}(t)=-u^{\prime}(t)-\sqrt{\mu}\int_{t}^{T}\left[\sin(\sqrt{\mu}(t-s))u^{\prime}(s)+\sqrt{\mu}\cos(\sqrt{\mu}(t-s))u(s)\right]\,ds.

For fixed
\mu
, all the terms on the right-hand side are bounded in
L^{2}(0,T)

if
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
, and thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}
is well defined. Using a triangle and
Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities, we also get the bound

{\left\lVert{\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)}\leq\left(1+T\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2}}\right){\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}},\quad u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T).

(6)

Furthermore, using that
(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)^{\prime}(T)=-u^{\prime}(T)
, we compute that

(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)^{\prime\prime}+\mu\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u=-u^{\prime\prime}+\mu u\,\text{ in }[H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)]^{\prime}.

Hence, for all
w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
we have

-\langle(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)^{\prime},w^{\prime}\rangle+\mu\langle\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u,w\rangle=\langle u^{\prime},w^{\prime}\rangle+\mu\langle u,w\rangle,

where we applied the integration by parts formula (
3
).
Next, we use that each
w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
admits the representation
w(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q(s)\,ds
for some
q\in L^{2}(0,T)
. Thus,
w^{\prime}=q
and using the
following integration by parts formula

\int_{0}^{T}p(t)\int_{0}^{t}q(s)\,ds\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}p(s)\,ds\,q(t)\,dt,\quad\forall p,q\in L^{2}(0,T),

(7)

concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Note that, for
\mu=0
,
\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{0}w(t)=w(T)-w(t)=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{T}w(t)
, which was introduced in
[
8
, Lemma 4.5]
as a
purely temporal transformation for the stabilization of the wave equation. Hence,

\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}
can be seen as an extension of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
to
space-time.

4
Always Look on the Right Side of Life

Using the real transformation
\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}
, we are now considering the variational
formulation: Given
f\in[H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)]^{\prime}
, find
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
such that

b_{\mu}(u,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w)=\langle f,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w\rangle,\quad\forall w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T).

(8)

We first prove that its application on the second argument of
b_{\mu}
does not
introduce complicated expressions or additional terms.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
u,w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
it holds that

b_{\mu}(u,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w)=\langle u^{\prime},w^{\prime}\rangle+\mu\langle u,w\rangle.

In particular,
b_{\mu}
is bounded and
\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}
-coercive, i.e.,

b_{\mu}(u,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w)\leq{\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}},\quad\text{and}\quad b_{\mu}(u,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)={\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}^{2}.

Proof.

As
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
there exists
q\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that
u(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q(s)\,ds.
Using (
7
)
and Lemma
3.1
, we then compute for all
w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)

\displaystyle b_{\mu}(u,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left[-u^{\prime}(t)(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w)^{\prime}(t)+\mu u(t)(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w)(t)\right]\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}q(t)\left(-(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w)^{\prime}(t)+\mu\int_{t}^{T}(\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w)(s)\,ds\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}q(t)\left(w^{\prime}(t)+\mu\int_{t}^{T}w(s)\,ds\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}u^{\prime}(t)w^{\prime}(t)+\mu u(t)w(t)\,dt.

∎

The main result is now the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

The variational formulation (
8
) admits a unique solution

u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
for all
f\in[H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)]^{\prime}
. In particular, for all

f\in\mathcal{F}:=\{f\in[H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)]^{\prime}:\langle f,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w\rangle\leq C{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}},\,\forall w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)\}.

with some
C>0
independent of
\mu
, the solution
u
satisfies the
\mu
-independent bound

{\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}\leq C.

(9)

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
the bilinear form
b_{\mu}
is
\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}
-coercive
and bounded. Moreover, for fixed
\mu>0
, using (
6
) we get

\langle f,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w\rangle\leq{\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert}_{[H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)]^{\prime}}{\left\lVert{\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)}\leq\left(1+T\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{2}}\right){\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert}_{[H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)]^{\prime}}{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}.

Hence, by the Lemma of Lax-Milgram, there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
for all
f\in[H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)]^{\prime}
.
The bound (
9
) for
f\in\mathcal{F}
now follows from

{\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}^{2}=b_{\mu}(u,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u)=\langle f,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}u\rangle\leq C{\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}.

∎

Remark 4.1
.

For
w\in H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)
, using (
7
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\langle f,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left[w^{\prime}(t)\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(t,f)+\sqrt{\mu}w(t)\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}(t,f)\right]\,dt

\displaystyle\leq{\left\lVert{w}\right\rVert}_{H^{1}_{\mu}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left[\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(t,f)^{2}+\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}(t,f)^{2}\right]\,dt\right)^{1/2}

where
\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(t,f)=\int_{0}^{t}\cos(\sqrt{\mu}(t-s))f(s)\,ds
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}(t,f)=\int_{0}^{t}\sin(\sqrt{\mu}(t-s))f(s)\,ds
.
For
f\in L^{2}(0,T)
we can now proceed as in
[
8
, Lemma 4.9]
, to compute

\int_{0}^{T}\left[\operatorname{\mathsf{C}}(t,f)^{2}+\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}(t,f)^{2}\right]\,dt\leq\frac{T^{2}}{2}{\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert}_{(0,T)}^{2},

showing that
L^{2}(0,T)\subset\mathcal{F}
and (
9
)
holds with
C=\frac{T}{\sqrt{2}}{\left\lVert{f}\right\rVert}_{(0,T)}
.

Discretization

We consider the conforming trial space
X_{h}:=S_{h}^{1}(0,T)\cap H^{1}_{0,}(0,T)

of piecewise linear finite elements defined on a uniform decomposition of the
interval
(0,T)
into
N\in\mathbb{N}
elements of mesh size
h=T/N
. The
discrete variational formulation reads: Given
f\in[H^{1}_{,0}(0,T)]^{\prime}
, find
u_{h}\in X_{h}
such that

b_{\mu}(u_{h},\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w_{h})=\langle u_{h}^{\prime},w_{h}^{\prime}\rangle+\mu\langle u_{h},w_{h}\rangle=\langle f,\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}w_{h}\rangle,\,\forall w_{h}\in X_{h}.

(10)

\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}

) admits a

4.1

\operatorname{\mathsf{T}}_{\mu}

6
Conclusions

We proposed a novel transformation operator for an ODE that is related to a space-time
FEM formulation of the wave equation, resulting in a Galerkin-Bubnov formulation that
is unconditionally stable and coercive. We can theoretically prove stability and best approximation error estimates independent of
\mu
. This opens the door for a space-time transformation that leads to a coercive formulation. Related results will be published elsewhere.

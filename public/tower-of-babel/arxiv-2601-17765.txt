Jacobian rings and the infinitesimal Torelli Theorem
Julius Giesler

Abstract
In this article we deal with jacobian rings and identify a mixed Hodge component of a nondegenerate hypersurface in the torus with a lattice geometric quotient vector space. We introduce a period map, study its differential and compute the kernel of the differential much explicitly via certain Laurent polynomials. As an application we deal with the infinitesimal Torelli theorem.

4
Hodge and mixed Hodge structures

In this section we give some theoretical background on Hodge and mixed Hodge structures.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
H
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space and
H_{\mathbb{C}}:=H\otimes\mathbb{C}
. A Hodge structure of weight
k
on
H
is a decomposition

H_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus\limits_{p+q=k}H^{p,q},

such that
H^{p,q}=\overline{H^{q,p}}
, where the bar denotes complex conjugation on
H_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Remark 4.2
.

To put a Hodge structure of weight
k
on
H
is equivalent to the existence of a descending filtration

H_{\mathbb{C}}=F^{0}\supset F^{1}\supset...\supset F^{k+1}=0

with
H_{\mathbb{C}}=F^{p}\cap\overline{F^{k+1-p}}
. This correspondence is given by

F^{i}=\bigoplus\limits_{p\geq i}H^{p,k-p},\quad\textrm{and conversely by}\quad H^{p,q}=F^{p}\cap\overline{F^{q}}.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
H
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space,
H_{\mathbb{C}}:=H\otimes\mathbb{C}
. A mixed Hodge structure (for short: MHS) of weight
k
on
H
consists of an ascending (weight) filtration

0=W_{k-1}\subset W_{k}\subset...\subset W_{2k}=H

and a descending (Hodge) filtration

H_{\mathbb{C}}=F^{0}\supset F^{1}\supset...\supset F^{k+1}=0

such that the Hodge filtration induces a Hodge structure of weight
r
on
W_{r}/W_{r-1}
.

We denote the successive quotients
F^{i+1}/F^{i}
by
\Gr_{F}^{i}
and
W_{r}/W_{r-1}
by
\Gr_{W}^{r}
. A Hodge structure of weight
k
on
H
defines also a mixed Hodge structure on
H
by setting
W_{k}:=H
. These abstract definitions are motivated by the following fact:

Theorem 4.4
.

(
[
Del75
]
, see also
[
Dan78
, §14]
)

For
Y
an
n
-dimensional quasismooth hypersurface in a simplicial projective toric variety
\mathbb{P}
the cohomology groups
H^{k}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
are equipped with a Hodge structure of weight
k
and we have

H^{p,k-p}(Y):=H^{p,k-p}\cong H^{k-p}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{p}).

For
\Delta
an
n
-dimensional lattice polytope,
f\in U_{reg}(\Delta)
and
Z_{f}=\{f=0\}\subset T
the cohomology groups

H^{k}(Z_{f},\mathbb{Q})

carry a mixed Hodge structure of weight
k
.

If
H=H^{k}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
we write
F^{i}H^{k}(Y,\mathbb{C})
and
W_{j}H^{k}(Y,\mathbb{Q})
for
F^{i}
and
W_{j}
.

Definition 4.5
.

The dimensions

h^{p,k-p}(Y):=\dim\,H^{p,k-p}(Y)

are called the Hodge numbers of
Y
. The spaces
H^{p,k-p}(Y)
and

H^{i,j+k-i}H^{k}(Z_{f},\mathbb{C}):=\Gr_{F}^{i}\Gr_{W}^{j+k}H^{k}(Z_{f},\mathbb{C}),

are called the Hodge components of
Y
and
Z_{f}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
H,H^{\prime}
be
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces carrying mixed Hodge structures of weight
k
and
k+2\cdot l
given by filtrations

(F^{i},W_{j})\quad\textrm{and}\quad((F^{\prime})^{i},W^{\prime}_{j}).

A
\mathbb{Q}
-linear homomorphism

is called a morphism of mixed Hodge structures of type

if

\phi(W_{r})\subset W^{\prime}_{r+2l},\quad\phi(F^{p})\subset(F^{\prime})^{p+l}.

For

Corollary 8.8
.

Under the assumption of theorem (
8.4
) we have

\displaystyle\ker(d\phi_{f})=\ker(d\phi_{f}^{1}).

Proof.

We show

\displaystyle\ker(d\phi_{f}^{k})\subset\ker(d\phi_{f}^{k-1})\quad k=2,...,n

\displaystyle\ker(d\phi_{f}^{n-1})=\ker(d\phi_{f}^{1}).

To the first point: Inductively it is enough to show

\displaystyle\ker(d\phi_{f}^{n})\subset\ker(d\phi_{f}^{n-1}).

We show by contradiction given
g_{\Gamma}(f)\cdot x^{w}\notin\ker(d\phi_{f}^{n-1})
then
g_{\Gamma}(f)\cdot x^{w}\notin\ker(\phi_{f}^{n})
: There is
v\in\Int((n-1)\cdot\Delta)
with one of the following

•

\exists\tilde{\Gamma}\neq\Gamma
:
\langle w+v,n_{\tilde{\Gamma}}\rangle\leq n\cdot\Min_{\Delta}(n_{\tilde{\Gamma}})

•

\langle w+v,n_{\tilde{\Gamma}}\rangle>n\cdot\Min_{\Delta}(n_{\tilde{\Gamma}})
for all
\tilde{\Gamma}\neq\Gamma
and
\langle w+v,n_{\Gamma}\rangle<n\cdot\Min_{\Delta}(n_{\Gamma})

To the first point: Choose
\tilde{v}\in Vert(\tilde{\Gamma})
, then
v+\tilde{v}\in\Int(n\cdot\Delta)
and

\displaystyle\langle w+(v+\tilde{v}),n_{\tilde{\Gamma}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq n\cdot\Min_{\Delta}(n_{\tilde{\Gamma}})+\langle\tilde{v},n_{\tilde{\Gamma}}\rangle

\displaystyle=(n+1)\cdot\Min_{\Delta}(n_{\tilde{\Gamma}}).

Thus
w\notin\ker(d\phi_{f}^{n})
, a contradiction.

To the second point: Choose
\tilde{v}\in Vert(\Gamma)
, then
v+\tilde{v}\in\Int(n\cdot\Delta)\cap M
for all
v
and

\displaystyle\langle w+(v+\tilde{v}),n_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle<n\cdot\Min_{\Delta}(n_{\Gamma})+\langle\tilde{v},n_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle=(n+1)\cdot\Min_{\Delta}(n_{\Gamma})

Thus
w\notin\ker(d\phi_{f}^{n})
, contradiction.

Last but not least we have

\displaystyle\Hom(R_{Int,f}^{n-1},R_{Int,f}^{n})

\displaystyle\cong(R_{Int,f}^{n-1})^{*}\otimes R_{Int,f}^{n}

\displaystyle R_{Int,f}^{2}\otimes(R_{Int,f}^{1})^{*}

\displaystyle\Hom(R_{Int,f}^{1},R_{Int,f}^{2})

and
d\phi_{f}^{1}
corresponds to
d\phi_{f}^{n-1}
under these isomorphisms.
∎

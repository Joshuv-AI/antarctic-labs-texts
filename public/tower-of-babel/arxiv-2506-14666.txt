Virtual fibring of Poincaré-duality groups
Sam P. Fisher, Giovanni Italiano, Dawid Kielak

Abstract
. We show that a RFRS Poincaré-duality group G G admits a virtual epimorphism to the integers whose kernel is itself a Poincaré-duality group over every field if and only if the L 2 L^{2} -homology of G G vanishes and so do the positive-characteristic variants thereof. Our investigations yield a more general relationship between cohomology at infinity of groups that algebraically fibre and their fibres. In particular, we show that if the fundamental group of an aspherical manifold of dimension at least three algebraically fibres, then the fibre is one ended.

1.
Introduction

A smooth manifold that fibres over the circle can be understood not only as a static object, but also as a manifold of codimension one, the fibre, changing over time, with the dynamics controlled by the monodromy. This description is indeed helpful in many cases, for example in three-manifolds, where our understanding of the fibres and their homeomorphisms is very detailed.

If a compact connected smooth manifold
M
fibres over the circle with fibre
N
(which then is also a manifold), on the level of fundamental groups there is a short exact sequence

\mathbbm{1}\to\pi_{1}(N)\to\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbbm{1}.

In particular, we get a surjective homomorphism
\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathbb{Z}
whose kernel
K=\pi_{1}(N)
is the fundamental group of a compact manifold. If
M
is additionally aspherical, then so is
N
and we deduce that the kernel
K
is a group admitting a compact classifying space (a group of type
\mathtt{F}
), and hence the cellular chain complex of the universal cover of the classifying space is a finite resolution by finitely generated projective
\mathbb{Z}K
-modules of the trivial module
\mathbb{Z}
(that is,
K
is of type
\mathtt{FP}
). Hence, admitting a fibration over the circle is reflected in the algebraic structure of
\pi_{1}(M)
.

This statement has a strong converse in the realm of three-manifolds: as proved by Stallings
[
41
]
, if the fundamental group of a compact irreducible three-manifold maps onto the integers with a finitely generated kernel that is not
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, then the three-manifold fibres over the circle in a way inducing the given epimorphism. The condition on the kernel not being
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
can now be dropped thanks to the resolution of the Poincaré Conjecture.

In higher dimensions, there is a result of Farrell
[
19
]
: if
M
is a closed aspherical smooth manifold of dimension at least six, then an epimorphism
\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathbb{Z}
is induced by a fibration if and only if its kernel admits a model for its classifying space that is finitely dominated, and a certain
K
-theoretic obstruction vanishes. The first condition can be rephrased by asking for the kernel to be finitely presented and of type
\mathtt{FP}
.

The situation changes if instead of asking whether a specific epimorphism to the integers comes from fibring, we ask if such an epimorphism exists at all.
Going back to three-manifolds, a central theme in their study that emerged due to the groundbreaking insights of Thurston was that it is often interesting not only to understand when a given manifold fibres over the circle, but also when it virtually fibres, that is, admits a finite-sheeted covering that fibres. Thurston conjectured that this should be the case for all finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifolds, and this conjecture was confirmed by Wise
[
44
]
in the cusped case and Agol
[
2
]
in the closed case.

Both proofs consist of two main steps. The first step shows that fundamental groups of hyperbolic three-manifolds virtually (i.e. up to finite index) have the RFRS property.
For a finitely generated group, the RFRS condition is equivalent to being residually {virtually abelian and locally indicable}, and so it is a strong residual property. To establish that a group is virtually RFRS, one usually goes via the Haglund–Wise theory of special cube complexes
[
24
]
, and this is indeed how Wise and Agol proceed, in the cusped case using in fundamental ways the work of Culler–Shalen
[
16
]
, and in the closed case that of Kahn–Marković
[
29
]
and Bergeron–Wise
[
4
]
.

The second step of the proofs is an application of another theorem of Agol
[
1
]
, stating that a compact irreducible three-manifold with RFRS fundamental group virtually fibres if and only if its Euler characteristic is zero. There are many ways of computing the Euler characteristic; for our purpose, we will focus on its being the alternating sum of Betti numbers, and thus a homological invariant. Hence, Agol established that for RFRS three-manifold groups, virtual fibring is controlled by a homological invariant.

This statement has algebraic counterparts: first it was shown in
[
30
]
that if
G
is an infinite RFRS group, then
G
admits a virtual epimorphism to the integers with a finitely generated kernel if and only if the first
L^{2}
-Betti number of
G
vanishes. Soon afterwards, Jaikin-Zapirain
[
28
]
*Appendix introduced the theory of
L^{2}
-Betti numbers over ground fields other than
\mathbb{Q}
for RFRS groups. This was then used by the first author, who
in
[
21
]
showed that
G
admits a virtual epimorphism to
\mathbb{Z}
with kernel of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
if and only if the
L^{2}
-Betti numbers of
G
over
\mathbb{K}
vanish up to degree
n
.
We will introduce all of these notions in the main text – the key point for now is that, as in Agol’s case, virtual algebraic fibring is controlled by homological invariants, namely various versions of
L^{2}
-homology.

The question motivating our investigations here is: do homological invariants control virtual fibring of compact aspherical manifolds with RFRS fundamental groups also beyond dimension three? In view of Farrell’s result, one has to find ways of controlling finiteness properties of kernels of virtual epimorphisms to
\mathbb{Z}
, and the
K
-theoretic obstruction. Thanks to the work of Siebenmann
[
38
]
, we see that this obstruction lives in the Whitehead group of the manifold, and this group is known to vanish in many cases of interest for us, most notably when the fundamental group of the manifold is compact special in the sense of Haglund–Wise – such groups are
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, and hence satisfy the Farrell–Jones Conjecture, as proved by Bartels–Lück
[
3
]
. Therefore, it is reasonable to focus first on securing the relevant finiteness properties.

Establishing finite presentability seems to be out of reach of current methods. We therefore focus on type
\mathtt{FP}
. Since this is a solely homological property, it would be too restrictive to stay within the realm of compact aspherical manifolds. A more natural setting is provided by Poincaré-duality groups – they are of type
\mathtt{FP}
, and their homology is related to their cohomology precisely in the same way as in the case of compact aspherical manifolds. More generally, given a field
\mathbb{K}
we can talk about Poincaré duality over
\mathbb{K}
, where the group is of type
\mathtt{FP}(\mathbb{K})
and the duality between homology and cohomology only applies to
\mathbb{K}G
-modules as coefficients. In this generality, we offer the following result.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and
G
be a RFRS orientable Poincaré-duality group of dimension
n>0
over
\mathbb{K}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a finite-index subgroup
G_{0}
in
G
and an epimorphism
\phi\colon G_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
\ker\phi
is an orientable Poincaré-duality group of dimension
n-1
over
\mathbb{K}
;

(2)

For every
i\leqslant n
we have
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{K})=0
.

In fact, we can also deal with all fields simultaneously, but this requires a stronger assumption on
G
.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a RFRS orientable Poincaré-duality group of dimension
n>0
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a finite-index subgroup
G_{0}
in
G
and an epimorphism
\phi\colon G_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
\ker\phi
is an orientable Poincaré-duality group of dimension
n-1
over all fields;

(2)

For all
i\leqslant n
and all prime fields
\mathbb{K}
, we have
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{K})=0
.

Using coincidences of small dimensions, we obtain a sharper result in dimension four.

Corollary C
.

Let
G
be a RFRS Poincaré-duality group of dimension four. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a finite-index subgroup
G_{0}\leqslant G
and an epimorphism
\phi\colon G_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
such that
\ker\phi
is an orientable Poincaré-duality group of dimension three;

(2)

We have
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=\beta_{2}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=0
.

The Betti numbers
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})
appearing above are the usual
L^{2}
-Betti numbers
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G)
.

In the process of proving the results above, we establish a relationship between cohomology at infinity of any group
G
and algebraic fibres sitting within.

Theorem D
.

Let
R
be a ring. Let
G
be a group with an epimorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
. If
K=\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
then

\homol^{i+1}(G;RG)\cong\homol^{i}(K;RK)

as
RK
-modules for all
i<n
and
\homol^{n}(K;RK)
is isomorphic as an
RK
-module to a submodule of
\homol^{n+1}(G;RG)
.

Applying the theorem with
i=1
yields the following.

Corollary E
.

Let
M
be a closed aspherical manifold of dimension at least three. If
\phi\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathbb{Z}
is an epimorphism with finitely generated kernel, then the kernel has only one end.

Outline of the arguments

The key technical tool is Novikov homology, that is group homology with coefficients in a Novikov ring. If we have a homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
and a ring
R
, the Novikov ring
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
is the ring of twisted Laurent power series in
t
with coefficients in
\ker\phi
, where
t\in G
is such that
\phi(t)
is the positive generator of
\mathbb{Z}
, and the twisting comes from the conjugation action of
t
on
\ker\phi
. Novikov homology controls the homological finiteness properties of
\ker\phi
.

The first observation we make is that vanishing of Novikov homology also gives vanishing of Novikov cohomology (
3.2
). This has already been used in a more restricted form in
[
14
]
. Then we formulate a long exact sequence relating the Novikov cohomology of
G
, and the cohomology at infinity of
G
and
\ker\phi
(
3.4
). This is essentially enough to prove
D
. A similar long exact sequence appeared already in
[
22
]
.

The final technical novelty is
3.6
, in which we show that having vanishing Novikov homology over
\mathbb{Q}
gives analogous vanishing for finite prime fields
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, except possibly for finitely many primes.

We then turn our attention to a RFRS group
G
. We fix a collection of fields
\mathcal{K}
, and prove that vanishing of
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{K})
for all
i\leqslant n
and all fields
\mathbb{K}\in\mathcal{K}
is equivalent to virtual algebraic fibring with kernel of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
for every
\mathbb{K}\in\mathcal{K}
simultaneously (
5.3
). This holds when
\mathcal{K}
satisfies a certain property – the two key cases are that of a single field, or all prime fields. In the latter case, this relies on
3.6
; in both cases we need the virtual algebraic fibring theorems of the first and third authors.

Finally, for RFRS Poincaré-duality groups, we combine
5.3
, the vanishing of Novikov cohomology, and
D
, and obtain
B
as a result.

The entire paper is written in the generality of group pairs
(G,\mathcal{H})
. This makes the arguments perhaps a little less transparent, but is natural, since for both aspherical manifolds and Poincaré-duality groups one often wants to argue by using manifolds with boundary or Poincaré-duality pairs.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Modules

Throughout, all rings will be associative, unital, and non-zero. The symbol
R
will always denote such a ring.

In
Sections

2.7
and
5.2
we will be more restrictive, and will only look at commutative rings. In fact, the true focus of the paper is the ring
\mathbb{Z}
and various fields.

We will have a preference for left modules; in particular, resolutions will be by left modules. This means that the natural modules to consider as coefficients in cohomology will be left modules, but in homology one should use right modules. It is however customary to follow
[
15
]
, and to put left modules as coefficients in homology as well: when computing homology with coefficients in a left module
M
, we tensor a resolution on the left with
M^{*}
, the right module obtained from
M
by twisting the group action of
G
by the anti-automorphism
g\mapsto g^{-1}
. When
M
is a bimodule, its left-module structure is used in the computation of homology, but its right-module structure remains – after twisting, it turns into a left-module structure and descends to a left-module structure for the homology.

2.2.
Characters

Let
G
be a finitely generated group. The
character cone
of
G
is the set
S(G)
of non-zero characters
\phi\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
; alternatively,

S(G)=\homol^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})\smallsetminus\{0\}.

Then
S(G)
inherits a topology by viewing it as a subspace of

\homol^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{\beta_{1}(G)}.

The elements of
S(G)
will be called
characters
.

When
G_{0}
is a finite-index subgroup of
G
then
S(G)
is naturally a subspace of
S(G_{0})
. For a general subgroup
H\leqslant G
we have the induced continuous map
\homol^{1}(G;\mathbb{R})\to\homol^{1}(H;\mathbb{R})
, which we may restrict to
S(G)
, but its image is not guaranteed to lie in
S(H)
.

2.3.
Novikov rings

Given a group
G
we denote the group ring of
G
with coefficients in
R
by
RG
.
We will identify
RG
with the abelian group of functions
G\to R
of finite support, where the
support

\operatorname{supp}x
of
x\colon G\to R
is
x^{-1}(R\smallsetminus\{0\})
. Multiplication is then given by convolution.

Given a homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the
Novikov ring
of
G
with respect to
\phi
, denoted
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
, to be the abelian group of those functions
G\to R
whose support intersected with
\phi^{-1}((-\infty,\kappa])
is finite for every
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. Convolution endows this abelian group with a ring structure.

The Novikov ring
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
contains
RG
, and hence is naturally an
RG
-bimodule.

Given an element
x\colon G\to R
of the Novikov ring
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
and a constant
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the
truncation
of
x
at
\kappa
to be the function
G\to R
with

g\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}x(g)&\textrm{ if }\phi(g)\leqslant\kappa,\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It follows immediately from the definition of the Novikov ring that such a truncation is actually a function of finite support, and hence an element of the group ring
RG
.

Given
x\in{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
, we say that it has
positive support
if

\phi(\operatorname{supp}x)\subseteq(0,\infty).

For every
x
, the truncation
\overline{x}
of
x
at
0
has the property that
x-\overline{x}
has positive support.

There is an alternative way of viewing Novikov rings when
\operatorname{im}(\phi)\cong\mathbb{Z}
: take
t\in G
such that
\phi(t)
is the positive generator of
\operatorname{im}(\phi)
, with the ordering inherited from
\operatorname{im}(\phi)<\mathbb{R}
. As an
R(\ker\phi)
-module, the Novikov ring
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
is isomorphic to the ring of twisted Laurent series over
R(\ker\phi)
with variable
t
, with twisting induced by conjugation.

Either way, we see that as an
R\ker\phi
-module, the Novikov ring
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
embeds into the module
\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(R\ker\phi)t^{i}
, the module of all functions
G\to R
whose support intersected with
\phi^{-1}([-\kappa,\kappa])
is finite for every
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
. This latter module is easily seen to be an
RG
-bimodule.

If
H\leqslant G
is a finite-index subgroup, it is easy to see that
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
coincides as an
RG
-module with the
RG
-module induced from the
RH
-module
{\widehat{RH}^{\phi|_{H}}}
.

For general subgroups, we have the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group, let
R
be a ring, and let
H\leqslant G
be a subgroup. If
\homol_{n}(H;{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}})=0
for some
\phi\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
n\geqslant 0
, then
\homol_{n}(H;{\widehat{RH}^{\phi|_{H}}})=0
.

Proof.

Since elements of
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
are functions
G\rightarrow R
, we can restrict them to
H
to obtain an
RH
-module homomorphism
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\rightarrow{\widehat{RH}^{\phi|_{H}}}
. The natural inclusion
{\widehat{RH}^{\phi|_{H}}}\hookrightarrow{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
splits this homomorphism, which shows that
{\widehat{RH}^{\phi|_{H}}}
is a direct summand of
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
; the result then follows at once. ∎

2.4.
Finiteness properties

We will follow the standard (that is, Bieri’s
[
10
]
) notation for finiteness properties. In particular, a group
G
will be
of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
if the trivial
RG
-module
R
admits a resolution by left projective
RG
-modules

\dots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow R\rightarrow 0

with
P_{i}
finitely generated for every
i\leqslant n
(we allow
n=\infty
here); the group will be
of type
\mathtt{FP}(R)
if there is a finite resolution of
R
by finitely generated projective modules.

If the ring
R
is not specified, then we are taking the respective property over
\mathbb{Z}
; this is reasonable, since being of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
implies being of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
for every ring
R
. More generally, if
S
is an
R
-algebra, then being of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
implies being of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(S)
.

All of the finiteness properties listed here pass to subgroups of finite index.

We say that a group
G
has
cohomological dimension

n
over
R
if the trivial
RG
-module
R
admits a projective resolution of length
n
, but does not admit such a resolution of length
n-1
. We write
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G)=n
.

We always have
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G)\leqslant n
if
\homol^{i}(G;M)=0
for every
RG
-module
M
and all
i>n
– this follows from
[
15
]
*Sections III.7 and VIII.2. In fact, an alternative definition of
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G)
is that it is the supremal integer
n
for which there exists an
RG
-module
M
such that
\homol^{n}(G;M)\neq 0
.

If
G
is a group of type
\mathtt{FP}(R)
, we have
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G)=n
if and only if
\homol^{i}(G;RG)=0
for all
i>n
and
\homol^{n}(G;RG)\neq 0
. This is proved precisely like
[
15
]
*Proposition VIII.6.7 by changing coefficients from
\mathbb{Z}
to
R
.

As before,
\mathrm{cd}
stands for
\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, which is again sensible notation since
\mathrm{cd}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G)\geqslant\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G)
for every group
G
and every ring
R
.

2.5.
Chain contractions

Throughout the article, we will make multiple uses of chain contractions, which we introduce here. Let
R
be a ring and let

\cdots\rightarrow C_{1}\rightarrow C_{0}\rightarrow 0

be a chain complex of free
R
-modules with boundary maps

\partial_{i}\colon C_{i}\rightarrow C_{i-1}.

A
chain contraction
of
C_{\bullet}
is a collection of
R
-module homomorphisms
s_{i}\colon C_{i}\rightarrow C_{i+1}
such that

\operatorname{id}_{C_{i}}=\partial_{i}s_{i-1}+s_{i}\partial_{i+1}

for all
i\geqslant 0
, with
s_{-1}=0
. If there only exist maps
s_{i}
satisfying the same condition for
0\leqslant i\leqslant n
, then we call the collection
s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
a
partial chain contraction of length

n
of
C_{\bullet}
. By a slight abuse of terminology, we will also refer to the individual maps
s_{i}
as chain contractions. It is clear that if there exists a partial chain contraction of length
n
, then
\homol_{i}(C_{\bullet})=0
for all
i\leqslant n
. An easy exercise in homological algebra shows that the converse is also true.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
be a chain complex of free
R
-modules with no negative terms. Then
\homol_{i}(C_{\bullet})=0
for all
i\leqslant n
if and only if
C_{\bullet}
admits a partial chain contraction of length
n
.

We will often use this result in the following guise.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
G
be a group, let
R
be a ring, and let
S
be an
RG
-algebra. Let
C_{\bullet}\rightarrow R
be a free resolution of the trivial
RG
-module
R
. Then
\homol_{i}(G;S)=0
for all
i\leqslant n
if and only if the chain complex
S\otimes_{RG}C_{\bullet}
of free left
S
-modules admits a partial chain contraction of length
n
.

As an immediate consequence we see that if
S^{\prime}
is another
RG
-algebra and there is a morphism
\sigma\colon S\to S^{\prime}
of
RG
-algebras, then vanishing of
\homol_{i}(G;S)
for all
i\leqslant n
implies the vanishing of
\homol_{i}(G;S^{\prime})
for all
i\leqslant n
. To see this, apply
\sigma
to a partial chain contraction over
S
.

2.6.
Generalised Sikorav’s theorem

In his thesis
[
39
]
*Chapter IV, Sikorav proved that if
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
is an epimorphism with finitely generated domain, then
\ker\phi
is finitely generated if and only if

\homol_{1}(G;{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G}^{\phi}})=0=\homol_{1}(G;{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G}^{-\phi}}).

Throughout the article, we will abbreviate such vanishing by writing

\homol_{1}(G;{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G}^{\pm\phi}})=0,

and similarly for Novikov rings over other coefficients.

Sikorav formulated his theorem in the language of Bieri–Neumann–Strebel (BNS) invariants
[
7
]
, which has the advantage of giving meaning to the vanishing of Novikov homology for
{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G}^{\phi}}
only; we are not going to do that, since the introduction of the BNS-invariants is not necessary, and we will always have the vanishing of Novikov homologies over
{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G}^{\pm\phi}}
.

A related result was proved by Ranicki
[
37
]
*Theorem 1 – here the statement connects the vanishing of Novikov homologies in all dimensions to higher finiteness properties of the kernel, but only applies in the very restricted situation where
G\cong\ker\phi\times\operatorname{im}(\phi)
.

Combining the initial insight of Sikorav with the work of Bieri, Renz, and Schweitzer
[
8
,
11
]
, the first author proved the following result.

Theorem 2.4

(Generalised Sikorav’s Theorem,
[
21
]
*Theorems 5.3 and 6.5)
.

Let
G
be a group of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
for some ring
R
, and let
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
be an epimorphism. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
;

(2)

\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=0
for all
i\leqslant n
.

A character
\phi
satisfying any of the two equivalent conditions will be called
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
-fibred
.

Remark 2.5
.

Being
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
-fibred is an open condition, that is, if
\phi
is
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
-fibred
then there exists an open neighbourhood
U
of
\phi
in
S(G)
such that every character in
U
is
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
-fibred. For a proof, see
[
8
]
*Theorems A and B.

2.7.
Poincaré duality and group pairs

Throughout this subsection,
R
will be a commutative ring. This assumption seems necessary to ensure that the theory of Poincaré duality behaves well under passing to finite-index overgroups. However, we will never perform the operation of passing to such an overgroup, and hence it is possible (indeed, likely) that our discussion holds verbatim for rings
R
that are not necessarily commutative.

Definition 2.6

(Poincaré-duality group)
.

We will say that a group
G
is a
Poincaré-duality group of dimension
n
over
R
, or a
\PD^{n}_{R}
-group
for short, if the group is of type
\mathtt{FP}(R)
and

\homol^{i}(G;RG)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\textrm{ if }i\neq n,\\
R&\textrm{ if }i=n,\end{array}\right.

where the isomorphism is one of
R
-modules. The action of
RG
on
R=\homol^{n}(G;RG)
is the
orientation action
; if it is trivial, the
\PD^{n}_{R}
group is
orientable
.

It follows immediately from the definition that
\mathrm{cd}_{R}(G)=n
.

The definition above follows that of Bieri
[
9
,
10
]
for
R=\mathbb{Z}
, and that of Dicks–Dunwoody
[

]
*Chapter V for arbitrary commutative coefficients (though
[

*Section 3.2 briefly discusses the case of a general

).

We are not requiring the group

above to be finitely presented – this way we deviate from the alternative definition of Poincaré-duality groups due to Wall

. Also, when

, the underlying ring is often purged from the notation, and one talks simply about (orientable) Poincaré-duality groups in dimension

.

By a
group pair

(G,\mathcal{H})
we will understand a pair of a group
G
and a tuple
\mathcal{H}
of subgroups of
G
(formally, a function from some possibly infinite indexing set to the set of subgroups of
G
). If
\mathcal{H}
is non-empty, we define the
associated module

\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}}
to be the kernel of the augmentation map
\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}}RG/H\to R
, with

RG/H=RG\otimes_{RH}R

being the free abelian group on the left cosets of
H
, with the obvious
G
action.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a group pair and let
M
be an
RG
-module. The homology and cohomology of the pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
with coefficients in
M
are defined by

\displaystyle\homol_{i}(G,\mathcal{H};M)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tor}_{i-1}^{RG}(\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}},M)

\displaystyle\homol^{i}(G,\mathcal{H};M)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ext}_{RG}^{i-1}(\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}},M)

respectively, provided
\mathcal{H}\neq\varnothing
(see
[
6
]
*Section I.1 and
[
17
]
*Section V.7). By convention,
\homol_{i}(G,\varnothing;M)=\homol_{i}(G;M)
, and similarly for cohomology. This definition is quite useful, because one immediately sees that given a short exact sequence
0\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}\rightarrow M_{3}\rightarrow 0
of
RG
-modules, there are associated long exact sequences in the homology and cohomology of pairs.

Group pairs appear naturally, as they correspond to the notion of a pair of spaces. Crucially, group pairs satisfy the familiar long exact sequence in cohomology, which is just the long exact sequence in
\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}_{RG}(-,M)
associated to the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}}RG/H\rightarrow R\rightarrow 0.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a group pair, and let

be an
RG
-module. We have the following long exact sequence in cohomology

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\homol^{n}(G,\mathcal{H};M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\homol^{n}(G;M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\homol^{n}(H;M)}

where

When

Given a subgroup

to be the tuple obtained from

. One readily checks that

When

Definition 2.8

(Poincaré-duality pair)
.

Following
[
6
,
17
]
, given a group

and a non-empty tuple
\mathcal{H}
of subgroups of

\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}}&\textrm{ if }i=n-1,\end{array}\right.

where the isomorphism is that of

-modules.

For convenience, we define
(G,\varnothing)
to be an
orientable Poincaré-duality pair of dimension
n
over

if

is an orientable

-group.

If

is not specified, then it is taken to be
\mathbb{Z}
.

Again, directly from the definition follows that

.

Replacing

by

in the proof of

]
*Theorem 6.2, and noting that we are in the orientable case, yields that

is trivial, and the pair

admits a finite resolution by finitely generated projective

.

If

Proof.

When

∎

Remark 2.10
.

Somewhat unsurprisingly, Poincaré-duality groups and pairs have the familiar duality between between homology and cohomology. Concretely, given an
RG
-module

, a possibly empty
\mathcal{H}
, and an orientable

we have natural isomorphisms

\homol^{i}(G,\mathcal{H};M)\cong\homol_{n-i}(G;M),

for all integers

, and similarly starting from homology of the pair, see
[

*Definition 4.1 and Theorem 6.2 and
[

]
*Definition V.7.1.

As in the case of finiteness properties, if

is an orientable

-pair and

is an orientable

]
*Proposition V.3.7). We will use this fact in a situation where

is a prime field and

2.8.
RFRS

A group
G
is
residually finite rationally solvable (RFRS)
if it admits a normal residual chain
G=G_{0}\geqslant G_{1}\geqslant G_{2}\geqslant\dots
of finite-index subgroups such that

\ker(G_{i}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G_{i}/[G_{i},G_{i}])\leqslant G_{i+1}

for all
i\geqslant 0
. For finitely generated groups, a simpler characterisation was recently obtained by Okun, Schreve, and the first and third authors: a finitely generated group
G
is RFRS if and only if it is residually {virtually abelian and locally indicable}
[
36
]
*Theorem 6.3. Since for finitely generated groups being virtually abelian and locally indicable is the same as being virtually abelian and poly-
\mathbb{Z}
, we see that a finitely generated group
G
is RFRS if and only if it is residually {virtually abelian and poly-
\mathbb{Z}
} as well.

The RFRS condition was introduced by Agol in
[
1
]
, where he proved that a compact, irreducible
3
-manifold
M
with virtually RFRS fundamental group virtually fibres over
S^{1}
if and only if
\chi(M)=0
.

2.9.
L^{2}
-Betti numbers over fields

Since our focus in this paper is on RFRS groups, we will present the algebraic approach to
L^{2}
-homology via Linnell skew-fields. We will also need the positive characteristic version of
L^{2}
-homology, that is (for now) only defined in an algebraic way.

When
G
is RFRS and
\mathbb{K}
is a field, Jaikin-Zapirain in
[
28
]
constructs a skew-field
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
that contains
\mathbb{K}G
as a subring, and has two additional properties: the embedding
\mathbb{K}G\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
is
Hughes-free
, and the skew-field
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
is
universal
. The precise meaning of these adjectives is not really essential for us here; one important consequence of Hughes-freeness is that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
is unique up to isomorphisms of skew-fields containing
\mathbb{K}G
that fix
\mathbb{K}G
(this result is due to Hughes
[
27
]
). We will therefore treat
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
as well-defined unique objects.

When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
, the construction of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G}
is older, as it goes back to Linnell
[
32
]
, and it works in a much greater generality than that of RFRS groups. Jaikin-Zapirain’s definition also covers a larger class of groups.

Definition 2.11
.

Given a RFRS group
G
and a field
\mathbb{K}
, we define

\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{K})=\dim_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}}\homol_{i}(G;\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G})\in\mathbb{N}\sqcup\{\infty\}

to be the
i
th
L^{2}
-Betti number of
G
over
\mathbb{K}
.

The notation stems from the fact that
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})
coincides with the classical
i
th
L^{2}
-Betti number
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G)
.
More information about this can be found in
[
32
]
and
[
34
]
*Chapter 10.

One of the key properties of
L^{2}
-Betti numbers over fields is the way they behave under passing to finite index.

Lemma 2.12

(
[
21
]
*Lemma 6.3)
.

Let
G_{0}
be a finite-index subgroup of a RFRS group
G
. For all
i
and all fields
\mathbb{K}
we have

\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G_{0};\mathbb{K})=|G:G_{0}|\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{K}).

Another important property of
L^{2}
-Betti numbers is that they vanish for mapping tori.

Theorem 2.13

(Mapping Torus Theorem,
[
21
]
*Theorem 6.4)
.

Suppose that
G
is a RFRS group, that
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
is an epimorphism, and let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. If
\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
, then
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{K})=0
for every
i\leqslant n
.

For
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
this is a celebrated theorem of Lück
[
33
]
(see also
[
34
]
*Theorem 7.2), which holds for many more groups.

The stated theorem can be generalised also in another way:
instead of requiring
\ker\phi
to be of type
\mathtt{FP}(\mathbb{K})
, if we insist on
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(\ker\phi;\mathbb{K})<\infty
for all
i
, then the conclusion holds even when the quotient
\mathbb{Z}
is replaced by any infinite amenable group
[
20
]
*Proposition 3.18 – when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
, this result is due to Gaboriau
[
23
]
*Théorème 6.6.

The Mapping Torus Theorem has a partial inverse, the Virtual Algebraic Fibring Theorem. We will need more than the final statement, and to this end we need to introduce a couple of additional objects.

There are a number of consequences of Hughes-freeness: when
H
is a subgroup of
G
, we have a natural embedding
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}H}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
. If
H
is normal,
the conjugation action of
G
on
H
induces an action of
G
on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}H}
, and this can be used to define a crossed product
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}H}G/H
. The crossed product naturally sits inside
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
, and when
H
is of finite index, the two rings coincide
[
28
]
*Proposition 2.2.

Let
G_{0}
be a finite-index normal subgroup of
G
, and let
\phi\colon G_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-trivial homomorphism. We may view
\mathbb{K}G_{0}
as a crossed product
(\mathbb{K}\ker\phi)\operatorname{im}(\phi)
. This ring sits inside
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}\ker\phi}(\operatorname{im}\phi)
, and its Ore localisation, which is
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}
, lies inside the Malcev–Neumann completion of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}\ker\phi}(\operatorname{im}\phi)
with respect to the biorder on
\operatorname{im}\phi
inherited from
\mathbb{R}
. This last ring contains
{\widehat{\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}\ker\phi}\operatorname{im}(\phi)}^{\operatorname{id}}}
, which in turn contains
{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}}
. All of this yoga shows that it makes sense to consider the intersection of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}
and
{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}}
. For an element of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}
, we will say that it
lies in
{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}}
if it is contained in this intersection.

Given a subset
U\subseteq S(G_{0})
, we will say that an element of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}

lies in
{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{U}}
if it lies in
{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}}
for all
\phi\in U
. All such elements form a ring that we will denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}\cap{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{U}}
. The key point here is that for different characters
\phi
, we are intersecting
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}
and
{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}}
inside different ambient rings.

When
U
is invariant under the conjugation action of
G
(or, equivalently, of
G/G_{0}
), then we may form a crossed product

(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}\cap{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{U}})G/G_{0}.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}G/G_{0}
, we may again talk about elements of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}

lying in

(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}\cap{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{U}})G/G_{0}
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated RFRS group, and let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. For every finite subset

there exists a normal subgroup

of finite index and an open subset

such that

(1)

and under the antipodal map;

(2)

\mathring{\overline{U}}
, the interior of the closure of

;

(3)

all the elements of

(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}\cap{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{U}})G/G_{0}
.

The theorem is not stated this way anywhere. It is a slight weakening of
[

]
*Theorem 4.13 and
[

]
*Theorem 5.10, see also
[

,

]
.

The Virtual Algebraic Fibring Theorem is a direct consequence.

Let

3.
Main tools

3.1.
Novikov cohomology

The following observation appeared already in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
be a chain complex of free left
RG
-modules without negative terms. If
\homol_{i}({\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\otimes_{RG}C_{\bullet})=0
for all
i\leqslant n
then

\homol^{i}(\Hom_{RG}(C_{\bullet},{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}))=0

for all
i\leqslant n
.

Proof.

Let
C_{\bullet}=(C_{i},\partial_{i})
. The vanishing of
\homol_{i}({\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\otimes_{RG}C_{\bullet})
allows us to construct a partial chain contraction, that is
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
-homomorphisms
s_{i}\colon{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\otimes_{RG}C_{i}\to{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\otimes_{RG}C_{i+1}
for
i\leqslant n
such that

\partial_{i}s_{i-1}+s_{i}\partial_{i+1}=\operatorname{id}_{{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\otimes_{RG}C_{i}}

for
i\leqslant n
, with
s_{-1}=0
. (We are abusing notation slightly, since we use
\partial_{i}
here to mean its natural extension to
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\otimes_{RG}C_{i}\to{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\otimes_{RG}C_{i-1}
.)

Take
i\leqslant n
and an
i
-cocycle
z\colon C_{i}\to{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
. Since
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
is a left
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
-module, the cocycle
z
extends to

\overline{z}\colon{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\otimes_{RG}C_{i}\to{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}},\ \xi\otimes c\mapsto\xi.z(c).

We get

\overline{z}=(\partial_{i}s_{i-1}+s_{i}\partial_{i+1})\overline{z}=\partial_{i}s_{i-1}\overline{z},

and restricting both sides to
C_{i}
gives

z=\partial_{i}\circ(s_{i-1}\overline{z})|_{C_{i}},

showing that
z
is a coboundary.
∎

Applying the above to
C_{\bullet}
being a free resolution of
R
yields the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
a homomorphism. If
\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}})=0
for all
i\leqslant n
, then
\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{-\phi}})=0
for all
i\leqslant n
.

The change of coefficients from
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
to
{\widehat{RG}^{-\phi}}
is an artifact of notation: as explained in
Section

2.1
, the module
{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}
in
\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}})
is a left
RG
-module. But when computing homology, we tensor
C_{\bullet}
on the left by the right module
({\widehat{RG}^{\phi}})^{*}
, isomorphic to
{\widehat{RG}^{-\phi}}
as a right
RG
-module.

Combining the above with
2.4
yields the following.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
an epimorphism. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
;

(2)

\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=0=\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})
for all
i\leqslant n
.

3.2.
Long exact sequence

We will now introduce the key tool.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
R
be a ring. Let
G
be a group with an epimorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
with kernel
K
. Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a (possibly empty) tuple of subgroups of
G
. The following long sequence of
RK
-modules is exact.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\homol^{n}(G,\mathcal{H};RG)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\homol^{n}(G,\mathcal{H};{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\oplus{\widehat{RG}^{-\phi}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\homol^{n}(K,\mathcal{H}_{\phi};RK)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\homol^{n+1}(G,\mathcal{H};RG)}
\cdots

Proof.

Using the notation of
Section

2.3
, consider the short exact sequence of
RG
-bimodules

0\to RG\to{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\oplus{\widehat{RG}^{-\phi}}\to\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}RKt^{i}\to 0,

where the second map is the diagonal embedding, and the third map is the difference of the embeddings. If
\mathcal{H}
is empty, then an application of Shapiro’s lemma shows that the desired long exact sequence is just the long exact sequence in cohomology associated to the short exact sequence above. If
\mathcal{H}
is non-empty, then the long exact sequence in the statement of the theorem is just the long exact sequence in
\operatorname{Ext}_{RG}^{\bullet}(\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}},-)
associated to the short exact sequence above. This is because

\operatorname{Ext}_{RG}^{\bullet}\left(\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}},\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}RKt^{i}\right)\ \cong\ \operatorname{Ext}_{RK}^{\bullet}(\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}},RK)

by Shapiro’s lemma, and since the restricted
RK
-module
\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}}
is just
\Delta_{K/\mathcal{H}_{\phi}}
, we have

\operatorname{Ext}_{RK}^{n}(\Delta_{G/\mathcal{H}},RK)\ \cong\ \homol^{n+1}(K,\mathcal{H}_{\phi};RK),

as desired. ∎

Remark 3.5
.

There is a more general long exact sequence that is useful to study the cohomological dimension of co-abelian subgroups, which was used in
[
22
]
. Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a group pair, let
\phi\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a character with kernel
K
. Moreover, let
M
be any left
RK
-module. The coinduced module
\prod_{t\in G/K}Mt
has a pair of submodules
L^{\pm\phi}
consisting of the sequences
(m_{t}t)
for which there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
m_{t}=0
for all
\pm t<\pm\alpha
(where the order is taken in
\mathbb{R}\supseteq G/K
). The inclusions
L^{\pm\phi}\hookrightarrow\prod_{t\in G/K}Mt
induce a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow N\rightarrow L^{\phi}\oplus L^{-\phi}\rightarrow\prod_{t\in G/K}Mt\rightarrow 0

with
N
being the
RG
-module induced from
M
,
whence we obtain long exact sequences in
\homol^{n}(G;-)
and
\homol^{n}(G,\mathcal{H};-)
.

3.3.
Reduction to finitely many primes

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is a collection of primes. We denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}
the ring obtained by localising
\mathbb{Z}
at the multiplicative set

\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{m}{p_{i}}^{n_{i}}\mid m,n_{i},\in\mathbb{N},p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}\right\}.

A more pedestrian description of
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}
is as the subring of
\mathbb{Q}
whose elements can be expressed as fractions with denominators being products of powers of primes in
\mathcal{P}
.

Given a matrix
M
over
{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}}
, we say that
M

involves finitely many primes
if and only if there is a finite set of primes
\mathcal{P}
such that the entries of
M
are all in
{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}G}^{\phi}}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G
be a group, let
\varphi\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-trivial character, and let
C_{\bullet}
be a chain complex of finitely generated projective left
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules without negative terms. If
\homol_{i}({\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}C_{\bullet})=0
for all
i\leqslant n
, then there is a finite collection of primes
\mathcal{P}
such that

\homol_{i}({\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}G}^{\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}C_{\bullet})=0

for all
i\leqslant n
.

Proof.

By taking direct sums with projective modules, we can modify the chain complex (without changing its homology over arbitrary coefficients) so that it consists of finitely generated free modules. We fix bases for the free modules and identify the boundary maps with matrices over the appropriate rings. We will inductively define chain contractions

s_{i}\colon{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}C_{i}\rightarrow{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}C_{i+1}

for
i=0,\dots,n
involving finitely many primes.

We set
s_{-1}
to be the zero map, which trivially involves finitely many primes. For some
0\leqslant i\leqslant n
, suppose we have defined chain contractions
s_{0},\dots,s_{i-1}
involving finitely many primes. By acyclicity of
{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}C_{\bullet}
, there is a chain contraction

\sigma_{i}\colon{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}C_{i}\rightarrow{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}C_{i+1},

such that
\partial_{i}s_{i-1}+\sigma_{i}\partial_{i+1}=\operatorname{id}
. We will modify
\sigma_{i}
to obtain a new chain contraction involving only finitely many primes. We form
\overline{\sigma_{i}}
by truncating the entries of
\sigma_{i}
at a real number sufficiently large so that

A=\operatorname{id}-\partial_{i}s_{i-1}-\overline{\sigma_{i}}\partial_{i+1}

has all entries of positive support. We have
A=(\sigma_{i}-\overline{\sigma_{i}})\partial_{i+1}
, and
\sigma_{i}-\overline{\sigma_{i}}
is a matrix over
\mathbb{Q}G
, so the matrix
A
involves finitely many primes. Since the supports of the entries of
A
are positive,
\operatorname{id}-A
is invertible with inverse
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}A^{i}
. Note that

A\partial_{i}=(\sigma_{i}-\overline{\sigma_{i}})\partial_{i+1}\partial_{i}=0,

so

\operatorname{id}=(\operatorname{id}-A)^{-1}(\partial_{i}s_{i-1}+\overline{\sigma_{i}}\partial_{i+1})=\partial_{i}s_{i-1}+(\operatorname{id}-A)^{-1}\overline{\sigma_{i}}\partial_{i+1}.

Thus, we put
s_{i}=(\operatorname{id}-A)^{-1}\overline{\sigma_{i}}
, and observe that
s_{i}
involves only finitely many primes. Letting
\mathcal{P}
be the collection of primes involved in the chain contractions
s_{0},\dots,s_{n}
, we conclude that

\homol_{i}({\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}G}^{\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}C_{\bullet})=0

for
i\leqslant n
. ∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
G
be a group of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}
and let
\phi\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a non-trivial homomorphism.

(1)

If
\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}})=0
for
i\leqslant n
, then there is a finite collection of primes
\mathcal{P}
such that
\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}G}^{\phi}})=0
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{cd}(G)=n
and there is an integer
0\leqslant j\leqslant n
such that

\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G}^{\phi}})=0

for
j\leqslant i\leqslant n
, then there is a finite collection of primes
\mathcal{P}
such that
\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}G}^{\phi}})=0
for
j\leqslant i\leqslant n
.

Proof.

Item (
1
) is an immediate consequence of
3.6
. In the situation of item (
2
), there is a resolution
0\rightarrow P_{n}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 0
by finitely generated projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules. The Novikov cohomology of
G
is

\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}})=\homol^{i}(\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P_{\bullet},\mathbb{Z}G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G}{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}})

for any ring
R
, because the modules
P_{i}
are finitely generated. The result then follows by applying
3.6
to the chain complex

\cdots\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P_{n-1},\mathbb{Z}G)\rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}G}(P_{n},\mathbb{Z}G)\rightarrow 0

of finitely generated projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules. ∎

4.
Acyclicity at infinity

We will now apply the tools from the previous section to study homology at infinity of algebraic fibres.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
R
be a ring.
Let
G
be a group and
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
an epimorphism. If
K=\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
then

\homol^{i+1}(G;RG)\cong\homol^{i}(K;RK)

as
RK
-modules for all
i<n
and
\homol^{n}(K;RK)
is isomorphic as an
RK
-module to a submodule of
\homol^{n+1}(G;RG)
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from
3.3
and
3.4
, taking
\mathcal{H}=\varnothing
and noting that

\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}}\oplus{\widehat{RG}^{-\phi}})=\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\phi}})\oplus\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{-\phi}}).\qed

Remark 4.2
.

While we find the approach using Novikov rings more straightforward, there is also a spectral sequence argument, which we sketch here. The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence associated to the extension
\mathbbm{1}\rightarrow K\rightarrow G\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbbm{1}
is

E_{2}^{p,q}=\homol^{p}(\mathbb{Z};\homol^{q}(K;RG))\Rightarrow\homol^{p+q}(G;RG).

Because
K
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(R)
, we have

\homol^{q}(K;RG)\cong\homol^{q}(K;\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}RKt^{i})\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\homol^{q}(K;RK)t^{i}

for all
q\leqslant n
.
Thus,

\homol^{0}(\mathbb{Z};\homol^{q}(K;RG))=0\quad\text{and}\quad\homol^{1}(\mathbb{Z};\homol^{q}(K;RG))\cong\homol^{q}(K;RK)

for
0\leqslant q\leqslant n
by an easy cohomology computation. The page
E_{2}^{p,q}
is concentrated in the columns
0
and
1
, so it follows immediately that
\homol^{q}(K;RK)\cong\homol^{q+1}(G;RG)
for
q<n
and that

\homol^{n}(K;RK)\leqslant\homol^{n+1}(G;RG).

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field.
Let
G
be a group with
\homol^{2}(G;\mathbb{K}G)=0
. If
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
is an epimorphism with finitely generated kernel, then
\ker\phi
has at most one end.

Proof.

Since
\ker\phi
is finitely generated, it is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{1}(\mathbb{K})
. Hence
\homol^{1}(\ker\phi;\mathbb{K}\ker\phi)=0
.
By
[
42
]
*Lemma 3.5, this statement being true for one field implies it being true for all fields, and in particular for the field of two elements
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Finally,
\homol^{1}(\ker\phi;\mathbb{F}_{2}\ker\phi)=0
implies that
\ker\phi
has at most one end – this is due to Specker
[
40
]
.
∎

Corollary ​
E
.

Let
M
be a closed aspherical manifold of dimension at least three. If
\phi\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathbb{Z}
is an epimorphism with finitely generated kernel, then the kernel has only one end.

Proof.

The fact that
\homol^{2}(\pi_{1}(M);\mathbb{Q}\pi_{1}(M))=0
follows directly from Poincaré duality and the fact that
\homol_{i}(\pi_{1}(M);\mathbb{Q}\pi_{1}(M))=0
for all

, which follows from Shapiro’s lemma.

We conclude that
\ker\phi
has at most one end. If it had zero ends, it would be finite, but
\pi_{1}M
is torsion free, and hence
\ker\phi
would be trivial, forcing
\pi_{1}M\cong\mathbb{Z}
. But this implies that the cohomological dimension of

is one, contradicting the existence of the fundamental class of

.
∎

This confirms the intuition of Douba, expressed after the statement of
[
18
]
*Theorem 1, that his Zariski-dense subgroup of

that is finitely generated but not finitely presented has one end – the subgroup arises precisely as a kernel of an epimorphism
\phi\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\mathbb{Z}
where

is a closed four-dimensional hyperbolic manifold.

Remark 4.4
.

A natural source of examples of groups with

\homol^{2}(G;\mathbb{K}G)=0,

with
\mathbb{K}
being a field, is provided by the class of duality groups. A group
G
of type
\mathtt{FP}
is an
n
-dimensional
duality group
if
\homol^{i}(G;\mathbb{Z}G)=0
for all
i\neq n
and
\homol^{n}(G;\mathbb{Z}G)
is torsion-free as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module. Examples of duality groups include Poincaré-duality groups, and therefore fundamental group of closed aspherical manifolds, but they also include the torsion-free finite-index subgroups of arithmetic groups
[
12
]
, mapping class groups
[
25
]
, and outer automorphism groups of free groups
[
5
]
. Note also that if
(G,\mathcal{H})
is a Poincaré-duality pair of dimension at least
4
over some field

, then
\homol^{2}(G;\mathbb{K}G)
vanishes. Thus, algebraic fibres in any of the groups listed above are one-ended.

5.
Virtual algebraic fibring

5.1.
RFRS groups

We now give a sharpening of
2.14
which holds for group pairs.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G
be a RFRS group of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
for some field
\mathbb{K}
and let
\mathcal{H}
be a (possibly empty) finite tuple of subgroups of
G
, where each
H\in\mathcal{H}
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
. If

\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{K})=\beta_{i}^{(2)}(H;\mathbb{K})=0

for all
i\leqslant n
and each
H\in\mathcal{H}
, then there is a finite-index normal subgroup
G_{0}\leqslant G
and an antipodally symmetric open set
U\subseteq S(G_{0})
, invariant under the conjugation action of
G
, such that
\mathring{\overline{U}}\supseteq S(G)
and

\homol_{i}(G_{0};{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}})=\homol_{i}(H;{\widehat{\mathbb{K}H}^{\phi|_{H}}})=0

for all
i\leqslant n
,
H\in\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
, and
\phi\in U
.

Proof.

We pick a free resolution of the trivial
\mathbb{K}G
-module
\mathbb{K}
in which the modules up to degree
n
are finitely generated, and endow the free modules with bases; we pick analogous resolutions for every group
H\in\mathcal{H}
. Since the homologies of the groups
G
and
H
over
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}H}
, respectively, vanish up to degree
n
, we may build partial chain contractions witnessing this vanishing. Each such partial chain contraction consists of
n+1
finite matrices over
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
, since
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}H}
is naturally a sub-skew-field of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
; since
\mathcal{H}
is finite, altogether this gives finitely many finite matrices over
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G}
. By
2.14
, there exists a finite-index normal subgroup
G_{0}
of
G
and an antipodally symmetric open set
U\subseteq S(G_{0})
such that all of the entries of the matrices lie over
(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}\cap{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{U}})G/G_{0}
. Moreover,
\mathring{\overline{U}}\supseteq S(G)
and
U
is invariant under the conjugation action of
G
on
S(G_{0})
.

We take a map
\phi\colon G_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
from
U
, and obtain
\homol_{i}(G_{0};{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}})=0
for every
i\leqslant n
, and

\homol_{i}(G_{0}\cap g^{-1}Hg;{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}})=0

for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
,
g\in G
, and
i\leqslant n
(the fact that we may conjugate
H
comes from conjugacy-invariance of
U
). Hence,
\homol_{i}(H;{\widehat{\mathbb{K}G_{0}}^{\phi}})=0
for every
i\leqslant n
and every
H\in\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
. By
2.1
,
\homol_{i}(H;{\widehat{\mathbb{K}H}^{\phi|_{H}}})=0
for every
i\leqslant n
and every
H\in\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
. ∎

Since we are interested in both virtual algebraic fibring over a specific field, and over all fields simultaneously, for notational convenience let us consider a set
\mathcal{K}
of fields with a specific property.

Definition 5.2
.

We say that a set of fields
\mathcal{K}
is
small
if
\mathcal{K}
is finite or if it contains
\mathbb{Q}
and for every prime
p
it contains only finitely many fields of characteristic
p
.

If
\mathcal{K}
is finite, we will say that a group is of
type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
if it is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
for every
\mathbb{K}\in\mathcal{K}
; similarly, we define
\PD_{\mathcal{K}}^{n}
to mean
\PD_{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
for every
\mathbb{K}\in\mathcal{K}
.

When
\mathcal{K}
is infinite, we will say that a group is of

\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
if it is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}
; similarly, we define
\PD_{\mathcal{K}}^{n}
to mean

.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a small set of fields.
Let
G
be a RFRS group of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathcal{K})
, and let
\mathcal{H}
be a possibly empty finite tuple of subgroups of

, all of type
\mathtt{FP}_{m}(\mathcal{K})
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a finite-index subgroup
G_{0}
of
G
and an epimorphism
\phi\colon G_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}
that does not vanish on any of the subgroups in
\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
, such that for all
\mathbb{K}\in\mathcal{K}
the group
\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{K})
, and
\ker\phi|_{H^{\prime}}
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{m}(\mathbb{K})
for every

\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{K})=0
for all

and all

, and
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(H;\mathbb{K})=0
for all

\mathbb{K}\in\mathcal{K}

Proof.

We will prove both implications in turn.

(

)
\Rightarrow
(
2
)

This follows immediately from

(

(

)

We first suppose that
\mathcal{K}
is infinite, and so

. By

and

, there exists a finite-index subgroup

and an epimorphism

\mathtt{FP}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
and

Applying

to

and each group in

in turn for

and

, and then using

, there exists a finite collection of primes

or
\mathtt{FP}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{P}})

or

for all primes not in

Being of type

or
\mathtt{FP}_{m}(\mathbb{K})

of characteristic

or

Let

be the subset of

. Since

is small, the set

is finite. Our goal now is to show that, up to passing to a further finite-index subgroup, we may arrange for our kernels to also be of type

or
\mathtt{FP}_{m}(\mathbb{K})
for every field

. If

were finite, then we would have been in this situation to start with, by taking any

and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{K}

Take
{\mathbb{L}}\in\mathcal{L}
. Note that each

and

5.2.

In this section we will work with orientable Poincaré-duality pairs

G
. Again, we will insist on

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be an orientable
\PD_{R}^{n}
-pair, and let
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
be a non-trivial homomorphism. If
\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(R)
, and
\ker\phi|_{H}
is a proper
subgroup of
H
of type
\mathtt{FP}_{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}(R)
for each

, then

\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=0

for all
i
.

Proof.

By
3.3
, the fact that
\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(R)
is equivalent to

\homol^{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=0

for all

. Poincaré duality of

gives

\homol_{i}(G,\mathcal{H};{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=0

for all

, see

.

At this point we split the proof into two cases. First, suppose that
\mathcal{H}=\varnothing
. Since the homology of the pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
coincides with the homology of
G
in this case, we have established that

\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=0

for all
i
. The cohomology statement follows from
3.2
, or from
2.10
.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{H}\neq\varnothing
. Since every
H\in\mathcal{H}
is itself an orientable
\PD^{n-1}_{R}
-group by
2.9
, we may apply the previous case and conclude that

\homol_{i}(H;{\widehat{RH}^{\pm\phi|_{H}}})=0

for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
and every
i
(we are using the fact that
\phi|_{H}
is non-trivial here).

We claim that
\homol_{i}(H;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})=0
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}
and every
i
as well. Indeed, the vanishing of
\homol_{i}(H;{\widehat{RH}^{\pm\phi|_{H}}})
for all
i
is witnessed by chain contractions, which can be viewed as matrices with entries in
{\widehat{RH}^{\pm\phi|_{H}}}
. But
{\widehat{RH}^{\pm\phi|_{H}}}\leqslant{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}}
, so the same chain contractions witness the vanishing of
\homol_{i}(H;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})
for all
i
.

The long exact homology sequence for the pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
now tells us that

\homol_{i}(G;{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})\cong\homol_{i}(G,\mathcal{H};{\widehat{RG}^{\pm\phi}})

for all

, and hence both are zero.
The vanishing of Novikov cohomology follows from

.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be an orientable Poincaré-duality pair over
R
in dimension
n
with a non-trivial homomorphism
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}
that does not vanish on any of the subgroups in
\mathcal{H}
. If
\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(R)
and
\ker\phi|_{H}
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}(R)
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
, then
(\ker\phi,\mathcal{H}_{\phi})
is an orientable Poincaré-duality pair over
R
in dimension
n-1
.

Proof.

The combination of
5.5
, Poincaré duality, and
3.4
immediately gives an isomorphism of
R\ker\phi
-modules

\homol^{i}(\ker\phi,\mathcal{H}_{\phi};R\ker\phi)\cong\homol^{i+1}(G,\mathcal{H};RG)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}R&\textrm{ if }i=n-1,\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Combining the vanishing of Novikov homology that we obtained in
5.5
with
2.4
immediately yields that
\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{\infty}(R)
.
Since
G
is of finite cohomological dimension over
R
, so is
\ker\phi
, and therefore
\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}(R)
by a suitable adaptation of
[
15
]
*Proposition VIII.6.1 from
\mathbb{Z}
to
R
.

We split the proof into two cases. First, if
\mathcal{H}=\varnothing
, then the above facts tell us immediately that
\ker\phi
is an orientable Poincaré-duality group over
R
in dimension
n-1
.

Now let us suppose that
\mathcal{H}\neq\varnothing
. Since every
H\in\mathcal{H}
is an orientable
\PD^{n-1}_{R}
-group by
2.9
, we may apply the previous case and conclude that
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
consists solely of orientable
\PD^{n-2}_{R}
-groups. Hence for every

we have

\homol^{i}(H^{\prime};R\ker\phi)\cong\bigoplus_{\ker\phi/H^{\prime}}\homol^{i}(H^{\prime};RH^{\prime})\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}R\ker\phi/H^{\prime}&\textrm{ if }i=n-2,\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The long exact cohomology sequence for the pair
(\ker\phi,\mathcal{H}_{\phi})
now tells us that

\homol^{i}(\ker\phi;R\ker\phi)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\Delta_{\ker\phi/\mathcal{H}_{\phi}}&\textrm{ if }i=n-2,\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

as required – note that we had to change a direct product over
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
to a direct sum. This is possible since
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
is finite, as
\phi
does not vanish on any of the groups in
\mathcal{H}
, and as
\mathcal{H}
is finite by
2.9
.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a
\PD^{3}
-pair and suppose that
G
is RFRS. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a finite-index subgroup
G_{0}\leqslant G
such that
G_{0}
is the fundamental group of a fibred three-manifold, and
\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
is the collection of fundamental groups of the boundary components of the manifold;

(2)

We have
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=0
.

Sketch proof.

Fibred three-manifolds have vanishing first
L^{2}
-Betti numbers, so it is clear that item (
1
) implies item (
2
). Thus, we focus on the converse implication.

If
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=0
, then
\homol^{2}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G})=0
by Poincaré duality. Moreover, the groups
H\in\mathcal{H}
are all
\PD^{2}
groups. This implies that
\homol^{2}(H;\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G})
vanishes for all
H\in\mathcal{H}
. The long exact sequence of
2.7
then implies that
\beta_{2}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=0
, since vanishing of
L^{2}
-homology in a degree is equivalent to the vanishing of the
L^{2}
-cohomology in the same degree; hence
\beta_{i}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=0
for all
i
. By
5.7
, there exists a finite-index subgroup
G_{0}\leqslant G
and an epimorphism
\phi\colon G_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
with
\left(\ker\phi,(\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}})_{\phi}\right)
being an orientable
\PD^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-pair.

Every such pair is geometric by
[
17
]
*Theorem V.10.2 if
\mathcal{H}\neq\varnothing
. If
\mathcal{H}=\varnothing
, then by a result of Bowditch
[
13
]
we know that
\ker\phi
is virtually the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus at least one; using the Nielsen realisation theorem, we obtain that
\ker\phi
itself is the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus at least one.

In either event,
\ker\phi
is a surface group, and if
\mathcal{H}\neq\varnothing
then the surface has boundary and the action of
\mathbb{Z}=\operatorname{im}\phi
on
\ker\phi
preserves the peripheral structure which is encoded in
(\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}})_{\phi}
.

Orientable surface-by-
\mathbb{Z}
groups are always realised as fundamental groups of fibred
3
-manifolds
[

,

]
, and therefore

is virtually a
3
-manifold group. The details of this argument can be found in
[

]
.∎

We also offer the following result in dimension four, where we can conclude that the kernel of the virtual algebraic fibration is in fact a Poincaré-duality group over

.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a
\PD^{4}
-pair and suppose that
G
is RFRS. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There exist a finite-index subgroup
G_{0}\leqslant G
and an epimorphism
\phi\colon G_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
that does not vanish on any of the groups in
\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
such that
(\ker\phi,(\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}})_{\phi})
is an orientable
\PD^{3}
-pair.

(2)

We have
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=\beta_{2}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=0
.

Proof.

Item (
1
) implies item (
2
) by
2.13
and
2.12
, noting that the group
\ker\phi
and the groups in
(\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}})_{\phi}
are all of type
\mathtt{FP}
– for the latter groups this follows from
2.9
.

We now focus on the converse implication. By potentially passing to an index-two subgroup, we may assume that
(G,\mathcal{H})
is orientable.
The group
G
is of cohomological dimension
3
or
4
(depending on whether
\mathcal{H}\neq\varnothing
), and so it is infinite. Similarly, the groups in
\mathcal{H}
are all
\PD^{3}
-groups by
2.9
, and so they are also of cohomological dimension
3
, and thus infinite. We conclude that
\beta_{0}^{(2)}(G;\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\beta_{0}^{(2)}(H;\mathbb{Q})=0
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
, see
[
34
]
*Theorem 6.54(8)(b).

We claim that
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(H;\mathbb{Q})=0
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}
. Indeed, the long exact sequence of
2.7
gives an isomorphism

\prod_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\homol^{1}(H;\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G})\cong\homol^{2}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G}).

But
\homol^{2}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G})\cong\homol_{2}(G;\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G})=0
by Poincaré duality and by assumption, proving that
\homol^{1}(H;\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G})=0
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}G}
is a flat
\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}H}
-module, as both are skew-fields, we obtain
\homol^{1}(H;\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}H})=0
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}
. Finally, vanishing of
L^{2}
-homology and
L^{2}
-cohomology in a given degree is equivalent, and so
\beta_{1}^{(2)}(H;\mathbb{Q})=0
for all
H\in\mathcal{H}
, as claimed.

By
5.3
with
\mathcal{K}=\{\mathbb{Q}\}
,
n=2
, and
m=1
, there is a finite-index subgroup
G_{0}\leqslant G
and an epimorphism
\varphi\colon G_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
that does not vanish on any of the groups in
\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
such that
\ker\phi
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
, and
\ker\phi|_{H}
is of type
\mathtt{FP}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})
for every
H\in\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
.

Being of type
\mathtt{FP}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})
is equivalent to being finitely generated, which is in turn the same as being of type
\mathtt{FP}_{1}
. Applying
2.4
now yields

\homol_{i}(G_{0};{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\homol_{i}(H;{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}H}^{\pm\phi|_{H}}})=0

for all
H\in\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}
and
i\in\{0,1\}
. By
2.4
and
5.6
, it is therefore enough to prove that

\homol_{2}(G_{0};{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})=0.

Using Poincaré duality, this is equivalent to showing that

\homol^{2}(G_{0},\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}};{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})=0,

which we will now do.

The portion of the long exact sequences in homology associated to the pair
(G_{0},\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}})

\homol_{1}(G_{0};{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})\rightarrow\homol_{1}(G_{0},\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}};{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}}\homol_{0}(H;{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})

shows that
\homol_{1}(G_{0},\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}};{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})=0
. Similarly, the portion

\homol_{2}(G_{0};{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})\rightarrow\homol_{2}(G_{0},\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}};{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}}\homol_{1}(H;{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})

shows that
\homol_{2}(G_{0},\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}};{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})=0
, since
\homol_{2}(G_{0};{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})=0
by
2.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}\neq\varnothing
. The pair
(G_{0},\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}})
is a
\PD^{4}
-pair, and hence is of type
\mathtt{FP}
, which means precisely that
\Delta_{G_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}}
defined over
\mathbb{Z}
admits a finite resolution by finitely generated projective
\mathbb{Z}G_{0}
-modules.
Thus, fix a resolution

\cdots\rightarrow C_{2}\rightarrow C_{1}\rightarrow C_{0}\rightarrow\Delta_{G_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}}\rightarrow 0

of
\Delta_{G_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}}
by finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}G_{0}
-modules (passing from projective to free modules potentially makes this resolution infinite in length). The previous paragraph implies that there is a partial chain contraction

s_{i}\colon{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}C_{i}\rightarrow{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}C_{i+1},\quad i\in\{0,1\},

of the chain complex
{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}C_{\bullet}
.
Moreover, viewed as a matrix over
{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}}

. (Here, we are really carrying out two arguments, one for

Let

be a

\overline{z}\colon{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}C_{1}\rightarrow{\widehat{\mathbb{Q}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}}.

Then
\overline{z}=(\partial_{1}s_{0}+s_{1}\partial_{2})\overline{z}=\partial_{1}s_{0}\overline{z}

\homol^{2}(G_{0},\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}};{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})=\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{1}(\Delta_{G_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}};{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}G_{0}}^{\pm\phi}})=0,

as desired.

When
\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}=\varnothing

\mathbb{Z}G
-module
\mathbb{Z}
used instead of
\Delta_{G_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{G_{0}}}
, and with indices of the chain contractions increased by one.
∎

Bijections between τ \tau -rigid modules


Abstract
We describe a special bijection between the indecomposable summands of two basic τ \tau –tilting modules. Key Words: Tilting modules, τ \tau -tilting modules, τ \tau -rigid modules, quivers. Mathematics subject classification 2020: Primary: 16G20; Secondary: 16D10.

1
Introduction

There are many types, more or less abstract, of tilting objects which generalize the tilting modules first studied by Brenner and Butler in
[
3
]
. In this note, we consider
\tau
-tilting modules in the sense of
[
5
]
and tilting modules in the sense of
[
5
]
and
[
6
, page 167]
. More precisely, let
K
be a field and let
A
be a finite dimensional
K
-algebra with
n
simple modules. Following and
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
, we say that a module
T
is

\bullet

\tau
-rigid
if
Hom_{A}(T,\tau(T))=0
;

\bullet

\tau
-tilting
if
T
is
\tau
-rigid and
T
is the direct sum of
n
pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable modules.

Moreover, following
[
5
]
and
[
6
, page 167]
, we say that a module
T
is

\bullet

partial tilting
if the projective dimension of
T
is at most one and

Ext^{1}_{A}(T,T)=0
.

\bullet

tilting
if
T
is partial tilting and the number of the isomorphism classes of the indecomposable summands of
T
is equal to
n
.

We know from
[
5
, Proposition 10(a)]
that a
\tau
-tilting module
T
is faithful if and only if it is a
basic
(or
multiplicity free
)
tilting
module, that is a tilting module in the above sense which is the direct sum of pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable summands.
We know from
[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
that, given two basic tilting modules
X=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
and

Y=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
with
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}

indecomposable for any
i=1,\ldots,n
, there is a permutation
s
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
with the following properties:

\bullet

If
X_{i}\simeq Y_{j}
, then we have
s(i)=j
.

\bullet

If
X_{i}\not\simeq Y_{j}
for any
j=1,\ldots,n
, then we have that

Ext^{1}_{A}(X_{i},Y_{s(i)})\oplus Ext^{1}_{A}(Y_{s(i)},X_{i})\neq 0.

We will show that we can not replace the hypothesis that both
X
and
Y
are basic tilting modules with the weaker hypothesis that they both are
\tau
-tilting modules (Theorem
2.5
). We will also show that an algebra
A
and its opposite may have the same number of
\tau
-tilting modules, but a different number of basic tilting modules (Examples
2.2
and
2.3
).

In the following, all modules are left modules defined over algebras given by quivers. Moreover, for any vertex
x
, we denote by
P_{x}
,
I_{x}
and
S_{x}
the indecomposable projective, injective and simple modules corresponding to
x
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
X=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
,
Y=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}

\tau
-tilting modules over an algebra

(*)

(*)
There is a permutation

Ext^{1}_{A}(X_{i},Y_{s(i)})\oplus Ext^{1}_{A}(Y_{s(i)},X_{i})\neq 0

The following facts hold:

(i)

If
X
and

(*)

(ii)

If

(iii)

(i)

-tilting modules are exactly basic tilting modules.

(ii)

This follows from Example

(iii), where

denotes the module

3\end{array}\oplus\begin{array}[]{c}3\\
3\end{array}
and

denotes the module
\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
2\end{array}\oplus\begin{array}[]{c}2\\

(iii)

This follows from Example

(iv), where the unique non-faithful
\tau
-tilting module is the module
\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
12\end{array}\oplus 3\oplus\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
1\end{array}

\tau
-tilting modules, that is
\begin{array}[]{c}1\\

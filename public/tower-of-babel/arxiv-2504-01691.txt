Reconstruction of coefficients in the double phase problem
Cătălin I. Cârstea, Philipp Zimmermann

Abstract
. The main purpose of this article is to reconstruct the nonnegative coefficient a a in the double phase problem { div ( | ∇ u | p − 2 ∇ u + a | ∇ u | q − 2 ∇ u ) = 0 in ​ Ω , u = f on ​ ∂ Ω \begin{cases}\Div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\ u=f&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases} from the Dirichlet to Neumann (DN) map Λ a \Lambda_{a} . We show that this can be achieved, when the coefficient a a has Hölder continuous first order derivatives and the exponents satisfy 1 < p ≠ q < ∞ 1<p\neq q<\infty . Our reconstruction method relies on a careful analysis of the asymptotic behavior of the solution u u to the double phase problem with small or large Dirichlet datum f f (depending on the ordering of p p and q q ) as well as the related DN map Λ a \Lambda_{a} . As is common for inverse boundary value problems, we need a sufficiently rich family of special solutions to a related partial differential equation, which is independent of the coefficient one aims to reconstruct (in our case to the p p -Laplace equation). We construct such families of solutions by a suitable linearization technique. Keywords. Nonlinear PDEs, double phase problems, degenerate coefficients, inverse problems. Mathematics Subject Classification (2020) : Primary 35R30; secondary 35J62, 35J70



1.
Introduction

In the present article, we intend to uniquely recover the nonnegative (scalar) coefficient
a
in the
double phase problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=f&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

from the
Dirichlet to Neumann (DN) map

\Lambda_{a}
. The later is formally given by the expression

(1.2)

\Lambda_{a}f\vcentcolon=\left.(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\partial_{\nu}u)\right|_{\partial\Omega},

where
u
is the unique solution to (
1.1
) with boundary value
f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
,
\partial_{\nu}=\nu\cdot\nabla
and
\nu
denotes the outer normal unit vector field to
\partial\Omega
. The DN map
\Lambda_{a}
will be rigorously introduced in Section
5
. Here and throughout this article, we assume that
\Omega
is a smoothly bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and the exponents
p
,
q
and coefficient
a
satisfy the conditions

(1.3)

1<p\neq q<\infty\text{ and }0\leq a\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})

for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
. In Section
3.1
, we first show by variational methods that the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) is uniquely solvable for boundary values in appropriate Sobolev spaces. More concretely, in the case
p<q
, it is established that for boundary values
f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
the solution
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, satisfying
a|\nabla u|^{q}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, can be constructed as the unique minimizer of the
double phase functional

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\min_{v\in M_{f}}\mathcal{F}(v;\Omega),\\
\mathcal{F}(u;\Omega)\vcentcolon=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p}+\frac{p}{q}a|\nabla u|^{q}\right)\,dx,\\
M_{f}\vcentcolon=f+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\text{ with }f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega).\end{cases}

Clearly, a similar characterization holds in the range
p>q
. The key property of the functional
\mathcal{F}
, leading to its name, is that its growth behaviour is in general non-constant over
\Omega
as the coefficient
a
can vanish in some subregions of
\Omega
. The region
\{a=0\}
is usually called the
p
phase, the region
\{a>0\}
the
(p,q)
phase, and one thinks of
\partial\{a>0\}
as modeling a phase transition. In recent years several researchers investigated the regularity properties of local minimizers of such variational integrals exhibiting different phases. For example, in the article
[
CM15
]
the authors showed that bounded, local minimizers of the double phase functional are in
C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime}})
, for any
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, as long as
a\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
and the exponents
p,q
satisfy the additional restriction
p<q\leq p+\alpha
(see also
[
BCM18
,
DFO19
,
DF21
]
for regularity results on broader classes of double phase type functionals). For a more comprehensive discussion on the regularity theory of such variational integrals we refer the interested reader to the aforementioned articles and the references therein.

1.1.
Main results and comparison to the literature

Our main result on the above mentioned inverse problem reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a smoothly bounded domain,
1<p\neq q<\infty
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. If
a\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
is a nonnegative function, then we can explicitly compute
a
from the DN map
\Lambda_{a}
.

Note that when
p=q
equation (
1.4
) becomes the weighted
p
-Laplace equation

\Div\left((1+a)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=0.

The inverse problem for this equation has been investigated previously. In
[
BKS15
,
Bra16
,
BHKS18
,
BIK18
,
GKS16
,
KW17
,
SZ12
]
partial results such as boundary determination of the weight, identification of inclusions, etc. have been obtained. In dimension
n=2
, a general uniqueness result (i.e. that
\Lambda_{a_{1}}=\Lambda_{a_{2}}
implies that
a_{1}=a_{2}
) has been shown in
[
CF24
]
, together with a weaker result for
n\geq 3
, when the weight is real-analytic and slowly varying in one direction. The general case when
n\geq 3
remains open. Given all this, it is interesting that a reconstruction result is available for the double phase equation when
p\neq q
. To borrow an often used phrase, we can say that the
p
-Laplace nonlinearity helps with the recovery of the weighted
q
-Laplacian term.

The study of inverse problems for nonlinear equations has attracted a lot of interest over the last decade. Examples of works for semilinear equations include
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
and for quasilinear equations we can refer the reader to
[

,

,

,

,

,
Câr22
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,
Sun05
,

]
. We want to highlight separately the articles
[
BKS15
,

,
BHKS18
,
BIK18
,

,

,

,

,

, which deal with equations involving the
p
-Laplacian and related operators. Some of the results obtained in the aforementioned articles have also been extended to nonlocal operators. For example, the articles
[

,

]
consider fractional

-Laplace and fractional

We would like to emphasize that the number of works for degenerate equations is still quite small. To the best of our knowledge, these questions have only been addressed with somewhat partial results in
[

. The results of our paper add to this newly developing field.

The problem we consider in the present paper is somewhat similar to that considered in
[
CK20
]
, where an equation of the form

\Div(\gamma\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)=0

is studied. Indeed, if
\gamma=1
, the result of
[
CK20
]
can be seen as a particular case of Theorem
1.1
, with
p=2
. The other paper that studies a similar equation is
[
Câr24
]
, where the equation

\Div(a\nabla u+|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0

is considered, with
a
a (uniformly elliptic) matrix-valued coefficient. This case is partially analogous to taking
q=2
in this paper.

Most works on inverse problems for nonlinear equations employ the so-called
second/higher linearization
method, which was first introduced in
[
Isa93
]
. In this method one uses Dirichlet data
f
that depend on small parameters
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{N}
, typically of the form
\sum_{k}\epsilon_{k}\phi_{k}
, then one considers derivatives of different orders in
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{N}
of the equation and the DN map
\Lambda(\sum_{k}\epsilon_{k}\phi_{k})
, in order to accumulate information about the coefficients appearing in the original partial differential equation (PDE).

Here, we use a slightly different approach. For illustration purposes, suppose that we are in the
1<p<q<\infty
regime. We take Dirichlet data of the form
f=\varepsilon v
and we expect that as
\varepsilon\to 0
the
p
-Laplace term dominates the equation. Assuming
v
is a
p
-harmonic function (which for technical reasons needs to have no critical points), we show that the corresponding solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of (
1.1
) has the asymptotic behavior

(1.5)

u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon v+\varepsilon^{1+q-p}R_{v}+o(\varepsilon^{1+q-p}),

where
R_{v}
is independent of
\varepsilon
but depends on
v
and
a
, as it is the unique solution in
C_{0}^{2,\beta}(\overline{\dom})
(the zero indicates that it vanishes on the boundary) of a linear elliptic equation

\Div(A_{v}^{p}\nabla R)=-\Div(a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v),

with
v
-dependent matrix coefficients
A_{v}^{p}
. This asymptotic expansion of
u_{\varepsilon}
then guarantees an expansion for
\Lambda_{a}(\varepsilon v)
. For any
\omega\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, we will show that

(1.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\varepsilon^{1-q}\langle(\Lambda_{a}-\Lambda_{0})(\varepsilon v),\omega\rangle=\int_{\dom}(A_{v}^{p}\nabla R_{v}+a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx.

We then make special choices of functions
\omega
and
p
-harmonic functions
v
in order to recover
a
.

2.
Notation

Throughout this article, we make use of the following conventions.

•

\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a smoothly bounded domain, although all our results extend to lower regular domains (e.g.
C^{2,\alpha}
domains), and points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are denoted by
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.

•

For any scalar valued function
u
on
\Omega
, we denote by
\nabla u=(\partial_{1}u,\ldots,\partial_{n}u)
the gradient of
u
, where
\partial_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}
is the partial derivative with respect to
x_{j}
, and for vector valued functions
u
the expression
\nabla u
stands for its Jacobian matrix
(\nabla u)_{ij}=(\partial_{j}u_{i})
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. More generally, we define the
k-
th order gradient
\nabla^{k}
inductively by the formula
\nabla^{k}=\nabla^{k-1}\nabla
for all
k\geq 2
.

•

For any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
C^{k}(\Omega)
stands for the space of
k-
times continuously differentiable functions in
\Omega
and
C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})
is the subspace of
C^{k}(\Omega)
such that
\nabla^{\ell}u
,
0\leq\ell\leq k
, can be continuously extended to
\overline{\Omega}
. The later space is a Banach under the norm

\|u\|_{C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})}=\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq k}\|\nabla^{\ell}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\text{ with }\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|u(x)|.

We also write
u\in C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})
(
u\in C^{k}(\Omega)
) and
\|u\|_{C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})}
, when
u
is a vector valued function as the range will always be clear from the context and the particular used norm on the range does not matter for our analysis. The same applies to other function spaces, like Hölder or Sobolev spaces, and the related norms.

•

If
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, then
C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
is the space of
C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})
functions such that the
k-
th order derivative is
\alpha
Hölder continuous, that is, there holds

(2.1)

[\nabla^{k}u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\vcentcolon=\sup_{x\neq y\in\Omega}\frac{|\nabla^{k}u(x)-\nabla^{k}u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

We endow
C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
with the following norm

(2.2)

\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}=\|u\|_{C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})}+[\nabla^{k}u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})},

under which they become Banach spaces.

•

For all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we denote by
L^{p}(\Omega)
the space of measurable functions
u
in
\Omega
such that

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\vcentcolon=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty

and by
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
the space of
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that the distributional derivatives
\nabla^{\ell}u
,
0\leq\ell\leq k
, belong to
L^{p}(\Omega)
. We endow the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq k}\|\nabla^{\ell}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

As usual, the spaces
L^{p}_{loc}(\Omega)
and
W^{k,p}_{loc}(\Omega)
consist of all measurable functions
u
such that
u\in L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})
and
u\in W^{k,p}(\Omega^{\prime})
, respectively, for all
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
.

3.
The double phase problem

Here, we collect some basic results on the double phase problem: In Section
3.1
we show the well-posedness of (
1.1
), in Section
3.2
we briefly explain its relation to local minimizers of the double phase functional and establish in Section
3.3
a maximum/comparison principle.

3.1.
Well-posedness

Let us start by introducing for any bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
a\geq 0
and
1<p\leq q<\infty
, the Sobolev type spaces

(3.1)

\begin{split}W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)&\vcentcolon=\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\,;\,a|\nabla u|^{q}\in L^{1}(\Omega)\},\\
W^{1,p,q}_{a,loc}(\Omega)&\vcentcolon=\{u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(\Omega)\,;\,a|\nabla u|^{q}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)\},\\
W^{1,p,q}_{a,0}(\Omega)&\vcentcolon=W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega).\end{split}

Under the norm

(3.2)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)}\vcentcolon=\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;a)},

where

\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;a)}\vcentcolon=\left(\int_{\Omega}a|v|^{q}\,dx\right)^{1/q},

the spaces
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,p,q}_{a,0}(\Omega)
defined in (
3.1
) become Banach spaces. This is a direct consequence of the fact that
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
L^{q}(\Omega;a)
are Banach spaces.
We have the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and assume that
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
a\geq 0
. The space
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)}
is a separable, reflexive Banach space.

Proof.

Step 1:
First, we show that
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is a Banach space. From the definitions it is clear that
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is a normed space and hence we only need to show that its complete. For this purpose, assume that
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is a Cauchy sequence. Then
(u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
(\nabla u_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
L^{q}(\Omega;a)
. By completeness of these spaces, there exist
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
v\in L^{q}(\Omega;a)
such that

u_{n}\to u\text{ in }W^{1,p}(\Omega)\text{ and }\nabla u_{n}\to v\text{ in }L^{q}(\Omega;a)

as
n\to\infty
. As
L^{r}
convergence implies a.e. convergence for a suitable subsequence, we may infer
v=\nabla u
a.e. with respect to the measure
a\,dx
and hence
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
. This proves the completeness of
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
.

Step 2.
Next, we prove that
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is seperable and reflexive. To this end, let us consider the mapping

(3.3)

j\colon W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)\text{ with }j(u)\vcentcolon=(u,\nabla u,a^{1/q}\nabla u).

Let
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)}^{\ast}
be the equivalent norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)}^{\ast}\vcentcolon=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|a^{1/q}\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

on
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
. Then the map
j
, defined by (
3.3
), is an isometric embedding, when we use the product norm

\|(u,v,w)\|^{\ast}_{L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for
(u,v,w)\in L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)
. As the target space is a cartesian product of separable spaces, it is separable and
j(W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega))
is separable. Now, using that
j
is an isometry, we see that
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is separable. The space
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is also separable under the original norm as separability is preserved in the class of equivalent norms.

This time we endow
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
and
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)
with the equivalent norms

\begin{split}&\|u\|^{\prime}_{W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)}\vcentcolon=(\|u\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|^{r}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|a^{1/q}\nabla u\|^{r}_{L^{q}(\Omega)})^{1/r},\\
&\|(u,v,w)\|^{\prime}_{L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)}\vcentcolon=(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{r}+\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{r}+\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{r})^{1/r}\end{split}

for some
1<r<\infty
. Then
j
is again an isometry. From
[
Cla36
, Theorem 1]
and the fact that Clarkson’s inequalities imply the uniform convexity of
L^{s}(\Omega)
for
1<s<\infty
, it follows that
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)
is uniformly convex. Next, observe that by Step 1 the linear subspace
j(W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega))
of
L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)
is closed. Hence,
j(W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega))
is uniformly convex. The Milmann–Pettis theorem now shows that
j(W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega))
is reflexive. As reflexivity is preserved under isomorphisms, we can conclude that
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is reflexive. The same remains true for the usual norm on
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
.
∎

To prove the well-posedness of (
1.1
), we need in addition the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(cf.
[
Sim78
, eq. (2.2)]
,
[
GM75
, Lemma 5.1-5.2]
and
[
SZ12
, Appendix A]
)
.

For all
1<r<\infty
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, there holds

(3.4)

|x|^{r}\geq|y|^{r}+r|y|^{r-2}y\cdot(x-y),

(3.5)

\left(|x|^{r-2}x-|y|^{r-2}y\right)\cdot(x-y)\gtrsim\begin{cases}|x-y|^{r},&\text{ for }r\geq 2,\\
\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{(|x|+|y|)^{2-r}},&\text{ for }1<r<2,\end{cases}

(3.6)

\begin{split}\left||x|^{r-2}x-|y|^{r-2}y\right|\lesssim&(|x|+|y|)^{r-2}|x-y|\end{split}

and

(3.7)

||x|^{r}-|y|^{r}|\leq r(|x|^{r-1}+|y|^{r-1})(x-y).

Using the above spaces and the previous auxiliary lemma, we can establish the following well-posedness result:

Theorem 3.3

(Well-posedness)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
a nonnegative function. Then the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) has for any
f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
a unique solution
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
, that is there holds

(3.8)

\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=0

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p,q}_{a,0}(\Omega)
and
u=f
on
\partial\Omega
in the sense of traces.

Moreover,
u
can be characterized as the unique minimizer of (
1.4
) and satisfies the estimates

(3.9)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q/p}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)})

and

(3.10)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{p/q}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q/p}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}).

for some
C>0
depending increasingly on
\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

First, we consider the minimization problem (
1.4
). Since
f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have
\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega)<\infty
. Therefore, the strict convexity of the integrand of (
1.4
) and
[
Dac07
, Theorem 3.30]
ensure the existence of a unique minimizer
u\in M_{f}\cap W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
of (
1.4
). By the minimality property of
u
, we have

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq\mathcal{F}(u;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega).

Using the Poincaré inequality

(3.11)

\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}|v|_{\partial\Omega}|^{p}\,d\sigma\right)

for
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and some
C>0
only depending on
n,p
and
\Omega
(see, for example,
[
FRRO23
]
), we get

(3.12)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C((\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega))^{1/p}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}).

Furthermore, by the minimality property we also have

(3.13)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;a)}^{q}\lesssim\mathcal{F}(u;\Omega)\lesssim\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega).

Using
p\leq q
and
f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, we can estimate

\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega)\leq C(1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{p}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q})

and thus

(3.14)

\begin{split}(\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega))^{1/p}&\leq C(1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})^{1/p}(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q/p})\\
(\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega))^{1/q}&\leq C(1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})^{1/q}(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{p/q}).\end{split}

Hence, from (
3.12
), (
3.13
), (
3.14
), (
3.2
) and
p\leq q
, we get (
3.9
) and

\begin{split}&\|u\|_{W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)}\leq C((\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega))^{1/p}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+(\mathcal{F}(f;\Omega))^{1/q})\\
&\leq C(1+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})^{1/p}(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{p/q}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q/p}+\|f\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}).\end{split}

This establishes (
3.10
).

Next, we show that
u
is in fact a solution of (
1.1
). By construction of
u
, we know that
u+\varepsilon\varphi=(u-f)+(f+\varepsilon\varphi)\in M_{f}\cap W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
for any
\varepsilon>0
and
\varphi\in W^{1,p,q}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. Thus, the minimality of
u
implies that
\varepsilon\mapsto\mathcal{F}(u+\varepsilon\varphi;\Omega)
attains its minimium at
\varepsilon=0
. Using
u,\varphi\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
and the dominated convergence theorem, we may calculate

\begin{split}0&=\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}\mathcal{F}(u+\varepsilon\varphi;\Omega)\\
&=\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u+\varepsilon\nabla\varphi|^{p}+\frac{p}{q}a|\nabla u+\varepsilon\nabla\varphi|^{q}\right)\,dx\\
&=p\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx.\end{split}

Therefore,
u
is a weak solution of (
1.1
) in the sense of (
3.8
).

Finally, it remains to show that the solution
u
is unique. This in turn is a consequence of the observation that any solution
w\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
) is a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}(\cdot,\Omega)
over
M_{f}
. In fact, if
\bar{u}\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is another solution of (
1.1
), then
\bar{u}
also minimizes
\mathcal{F}(\cdot;\Omega)
over
M_{f}
and as minimizers of this problem are unique, we can conclude that
\bar{u}=u
.

For proving that solutions are minimizers, let us choose any
v\in M_{f}\cap W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
. Then, using (
3.4
) and (
3.8
), we deduce that

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(v;\Omega)&=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla v|^{p}+\frac{p}{q}a|\nabla v|^{q}\right)\,dx\\
&\geq\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla w|^{p}+\frac{p}{q}a|\nabla w|^{q}\right)\,dx\\
&\quad+p\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w+a|\nabla w|^{q-2}\nabla w\right)\cdot\nabla(v-w)\,dx\\
&=\mathcal{F}(w;\Omega),\end{split}

where we used that
v-w\in W^{1,p,q}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, if
v\in M_{f}
does not belong to
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
, then
\mathcal{F}(v;\Omega)=\infty
and the previous estimate holds trivially. Hence, we have shown that any solution
w\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
) is a minimizer of (
1.4
).

Hence, in summary, we have demonstrated that the unique minimizer
u
of (
1.4
) is the unique
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
-solution of (
1.1
) and moreover it satisfies the estimate (
3.10
).
∎

3.2.
Local minimizers of the double phase functional

The only purpose of this section is to show that solutions to the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) are local minimizers of the double phase functional in the sense of Definition
3.4
. By combining this with the maximum principle (Theorem
3.7
), one then sees that all (local) regularity results established in the work
[
CM15
]
can be applied to solutions of (
1.1
), when the Dirichlet datum
f
is bounded and in
W^{1,q}(\Omega)
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
a\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
a nonnegative function. Then, a function
u\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
is said to be a
local minimizer
of
\mathcal{F}(\cdot;\Omega)
, when
\mathcal{F}(u;\Omega^{\prime})<\infty
for all
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
and there holds

(3.15)

\mathcal{F}(u;\supp(u-v))\leq\mathcal{F}(v;\supp(u-v))

for all
v\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
such that
\supp(u-v)\subset\Omega
.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that this definition makes sense as
\supp(u-v)\subset\Omega
is contained in some
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
. In fact, as
\supp(u-v)\subset\Omega
and
\partial\Omega
are closed and compact sets, respectively, we have
\dist(\supp(u-v),\partial\Omega)\geq\delta
for some
\delta>0
and so one can take
\Omega^{\prime}=\{x\in\Omega\,;\,\dist(x,\partial\Omega)>\delta/2\}
. Furthermore, let us note that by (
3.1
) the first part of Definition
3.4
means nothing else than
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a,loc}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
a\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
a nonnegative function. If
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is a minimizer of (
1.4
) for some
f\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, then it is also a local minimizer in the sense of Definition
3.4
.

Proof.

Let
v\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
and suppose that
\supp(u-v)\subset\Omega
. Assume first that
v\notin W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, then
\mathcal{F}(v;\Omega)=\infty
and we have trivially

(3.16)

\mathcal{F}(u;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{F}(v;\Omega).

Next, we assert that (
3.16
) also holds when
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. In this case, we write
v=u+\varphi
, where
\varphi=v-u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Moreover, by assumption and Remark
3.5
we know that
\supp\varphi\subset\Omega^{\prime}
for some
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
. Let us denote by
(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
the standard mollifier and define the functions
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}\ast\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. By the properties of mollification, we know that

\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}\to\nabla\varphi\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega

along a suitable subsequence. Moreover, by Young’s inequality there holds

|\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

So, by boundedness of
\Omega
, the dominated convergence theorem and the minimality of
u\in M_{f}
, we get

(3.17)

\mathcal{F}(u;\Omega)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{F}(u+\varphi_{\varepsilon};\Omega)=\mathcal{F}(v;\Omega).

Hence, also in this case we have (
3.16
). Finally, we may observe

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(v;\Omega)&=\mathcal{F}(v;\supp(u-v))+\mathcal{F}(v;{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\supp(u-v))\\
&=\mathcal{F}(v;\supp(u-v))+\mathcal{F}(u;{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\supp(u-v)).\end{split}

Inserting this into the right hand side of (
3.17
) and using the same decomposition for the left hand side, we achieve the identity (
3.15
). This concludes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Maximum and comparison principle

Next, let us recall that solutions to the double phase problem (
1.1
) satisfy a maximum principle (see
[
CM15
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
Leo91
]
):

Theorem 3.7

(Maximum principle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
a nonnegative function. If
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
solves the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) with
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, then

(3.18)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

On the one hand, the advantage of this formulation of the maximum principle is that it is also valid in the vector-valued case
N\geq 2
, in the sense that there holds

(3.19)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq N^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

On the other hand, Theorem
3.7
can be derived in the scalar case by very elementary considerations, namely from a weak comparison principle. As a preparatory step, we demonstrate the following weak maximum principle.

Lemma 3.8

(Weak maximum principle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
a nonnegative function. If
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
satisfies

(3.20)

\begin{cases}-\Div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)\geq 0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u\geq 0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

then
u\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

The proof is very standard, but for the readers convenience and later usage we present the short argument.

Proof.

Let us write
u=u_{+}-u_{-}
, where
u_{\pm}=\max(\pm u,0)
. It is well-known that
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
implies
u_{\pm}\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
with

(3.21)

\nabla u_{+}=\chi_{\{u>0\}}\nabla u\text{ and }\nabla u_{-}=-\chi_{\{u<0\}}\nabla u.

Furthermore,
u\geq 0
on
\partial\Omega
implies
u_{-}\in W^{1,p,q}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. Also, recall that
u
is a solution to (
3.20
) means nothing else than

(3.22)

\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx\geq 0

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p,q}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\varphi\geq 0
. In particular, we can take
\varphi=u_{-}
to get

\begin{split}0&\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla u_{-}\,dx\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq: gradient of pos neg part}}{=}-\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{-}|^{p}+a|\nabla u_{-}|^{q}\right)\,dx.\end{split}

By the Poincaré inequality (
3.11
), we get
u_{-}=0
in
\Omega
and so the claim follows.
∎

The above technique of proof can be used to prove a comparison principle, which in turn implies the maximum principle as stated in Theorem
3.7
(use
\pm\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
as comparing functions).

Proposition 3.9

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
a nonnegative function. If
u,v\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
solve (
1.1
) with boundary values
u_{0},v_{0}\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
such that
u_{0}\geq v_{0}
on
\partial\Omega
.
Then there holds
u\geq v
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
3.8
, we may test the PDEs for
u
and
v
, respectively, by
\varphi=(u-v)_{-}\in W^{1,p,q}_{a,0}(\Omega)
. After subtracting the resulting identities and applying Lemma
3.2
, we get

(3.23)

\begin{split}0&=-\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)_{-}\,dx\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}a\left(|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)_{-}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}a\left(|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)\,dx\\
&\geq\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)\,dx.\end{split}

For
p\geq 2
, we deduce from formula (
3.5
) the estimate

0\gtrsim\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}|\nabla u-\nabla v|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)_{-}|^{p}\,dx.

Hence, from the Poincaré inequality and
(u-v)_{-}=(u_{0}-v_{0})_{-}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we get
(u-v)_{-}=0
in
\Omega
, which is equivalent to
u\geq v
in
\Omega
.

For
p\leq 2
, we may observe that (
3.5
) implies

|x-y|^{p}\lesssim[\left(|x|^{p-2}x-|y|^{p-2}y\right)\cdot(x-y)]^{p/2}(|x|+|y|)^{(2-p)p/2}

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Using Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{2-p}{2}+\frac{p}{2}=1
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)_{-}|^{p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}|\nabla(u-v)|^{p}\,dx\\
&\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}[\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)](|\nabla u|+|\nabla v|)^{(2-p)p/2}\,dx\\
&\lesssim\||\nabla u|+|\nabla v|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p(2-p)}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot(\nabla(u-v))\,dx\right)^{p/2}\\
&\lesssim\||\nabla u|+|\nabla v|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p(2-p)}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)\,dx\right.\\
&\quad\left.+\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{u<v\}}a\left(|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)\,dx\right)^{p/2}.\end{split}

By (
3.23
) the expression in the last line is identically zero and so we can again conclude from Poincaré’s inequality and
u_{0}\geq v_{0}
on
\partial\Omega
that
u\geq v
in
\Omega
. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Asymptotic expansion of solutions to the double phase

In this section, we consider the asymptotic behaviour of solutions to the double phase problem (
1.1
), when the boundary values
f
become very small (
p<q
) or very large (
p>q
).

4.1.
Asymptotic expansion of solutions for
p<q

Here, we prove that for any smooth
p
-harmonic function
v
without critical points, the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\Div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=\varepsilon v&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

provided by Theorem
3.3
, can be expanded as

(4.2)

u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon v+\varepsilon^{1+q-p}R_{v}+o(\varepsilon^{1+q-p})

for a suitable function
R_{v}
and sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Before establishing the asymptotic expansion (
4.2
), we need to demonstrate some preliminary Hölder estimates in
C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
and to shorten the notation we will sometimes omit the dependence on the domain
\Omega
in the appearing norms and write, for example,
\|\cdot\|_{C^{0,\beta}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a smoothly bounded domain,
0<\beta<1
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. Moreover, assume that
u\in C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies

(4.3)

\inf_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla u(x)|\geq\lambda_{1}

for some
\lambda_{1}>0
.

(1)

If
s\geq 1
, then there holds

(4.4)

\||\nabla u|^{s}\|_{C^{0,\beta}}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{s}+2s\|\nabla u\|_{C^{0,\beta}}^{s}.

(2)

If
0<s<1
, then there holds

(4.5)

\||\nabla u|^{s}\|_{C^{0,\beta}}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{s}+\frac{2s}{\prod_{j=1}^{k_{s}}\lambda_{1}^{2^{j-1}s}}\|\nabla u\|_{C^{0,\beta}}^{2^{k_{s}}s}

where
k_{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}
is given by

(4.6)

k_{s}=\min\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,;\,k\geq|\log s|/\log 2\}.

(3)

If
-1<s<0
, then there holds

(4.7)

\||\nabla u|^{s}\|_{C^{0,\beta}}\leq\lambda_{1}^{s}+\frac{2|s|}{\lambda_{1}^{2|s|}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{|s|}}\lambda_{1}^{2^{j-1}s}}\|\nabla u\|_{C^{0,\beta}}^{2^{k_{|s|}}|s|}

where
k_{|s|}>0
is again the constant from (
4.6
).

(4)

If
s\leq-1
, then there holds

(4.8)

\||\nabla u|^{s}\|_{C^{0,\beta}}\leq\lambda_{1}^{s}+2|s|\lambda_{1}^{2s}\|\nabla u\|_{C^{0,\beta}}^{|s|}.

Proof.

Let us note that it is enough to prove
1
–
2
as the others are consequences of these. In fact, if we set
t\vcentcolon=-s
for
s<0
, then we may estimate

\begin{split}||\nabla u(x)|^{-t}-|\nabla u(y)|^{-t}|&=\frac{||\nabla u(x)|^{t}-|\nabla u(y)|^{t}|}{|\nabla u(x)|^{t}|\nabla u(y)|^{t}}\\
&\leq\lambda_{1}^{-2t}||\nabla u(x)|^{t}-|\nabla u(y)|^{t}|\\
&=\lambda_{1}^{2s}||\nabla u(x)|^{t}-|\nabla u(y)|^{t}|\end{split}

and hence

[|\nabla u|^{-t}]_{C^{0,\beta}}=\lambda_{1}^{2s}[|\nabla u|^{t}]_{C^{0,\beta}}.

Using this, it is easy to see that the estimate (
4.7
) can be deduced from (
4.5
) and the bound (
4.8
) from (
4.4
).

The estimate (
4.4
) follows from the triangle inequality for
s=1
and (
3.7
) for
s>1
, respectively.

Finally, to get the bound (
4.5
) we calculate

(4.9)

\begin{split}||\nabla u(x)|^{s}-|\nabla u(y)|^{s}|&\leq\frac{||\nabla u(x)|^{2s}-|\nabla u(y)|^{2s}|}{|\nabla u(x)|^{s}+|\nabla u(y)|^{s}}\\
&\leq\frac{||\nabla u(x)|^{2s}-|\nabla u(y)|^{2s}|}{2\lambda_{1}^{s}}\\
&\leq\frac{||\nabla u(x)|^{4s}-|\nabla u(y)|^{4s}|}{2^{2}\lambda_{1}^{s}\lambda_{1}^{2s}}\\
&\leq\ldots\\
&\leq\frac{||\nabla u(x)|^{2^{k}s}-|\nabla u(y)|^{2^{k}s}|}{2^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{1}^{2^{j-1}s}}\end{split}

for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If we apply (
4.9
) with
k=k_{s}
(see (
4.6
)), which guarantees that
2^{k}s\geq 1
, and (
3.7
), we deduce

\begin{split}[|\nabla u|^{s}]_{C^{0,\beta}}&\leq\frac{2s}{\prod_{j=1}^{k_{s}}\lambda_{1}^{2^{j-1}s}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2^{k_{s}}-1}[\nabla u]_{C^{0,\beta}}\leq\frac{2s}{\prod_{j=1}^{k_{s}}\lambda_{1}^{2^{j-1}s}}\|\nabla u\|_{C^{0,\beta}}^{2^{k_{s}}}.\end{split}

So, combining this with a trivial bound for the
L^{\infty}
norm of
|\nabla u|^{s}
, we get the estimate (
4.7
) and this finishes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Next, let us introduce for all
1<r<\infty
the functions
J^{r}=(J^{r}_{1},\ldots,J^{r}_{n})\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by

(4.10)

J^{r}(\xi)=|\xi|^{r-2}\xi.

A straightforward calculation shows that the Jacobian matrix of
J^{r}
is given by

(4.11)

\nabla_{\xi}J^{r}(\xi)=|\xi|^{r-2}\left(\mathbf{1}+(r-2)\frac{\xi\otimes\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)

for all
\xi\neq 0
. Here,
\mathbf{1}
denotes the
n\times n
unit matrix and for all
\eta,\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the matrix
\eta\otimes\zeta
has components
(\eta\otimes\zeta)_{ij}=\eta_{i}\zeta_{j}
. Using this notation, we can formulate the asymptotic behaviour of
u_{\varepsilon}
, solving the problem (
4.1
), as follows:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a smoothly bounded domain,
1<p<q<\infty
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\gamma<\beta<1
with
\beta\leq\alpha
and assume that
a\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
is nonnegative. Moreover, suppose that
v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is a
p
-harmonic function without critical points. Then the unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
, to (
4.1
) has the asymptotic expansion

(4.12)

u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon v+\varepsilon^{1+q-p}R_{v}+o(\varepsilon^{1+q-p})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
in the sense of
C^{2,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
. Here,
R_{v}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
denotes the unique solution to

(4.13)

\begin{cases}\Div(A_{v}^{p}\nabla R)=-\Div(a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
R=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
A_{v}^{p}=\nabla_{\xi}J^{p}(\nabla v)
is uniformly elliptic
(see (
4.11
) and (
4.18
)).

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
be the unique solution to (
4.1
) (see Theorem
3.3
). Moreover, let us define the functions
w_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p,q}_{a,0}(\Omega)
by the Ansatz

(4.14)

u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon(v+w_{\varepsilon}).

From (
3.6
) we deduce that
J^{r}
is locally Lipschitz continuous and hence applying the fundamental theorem of calculus to the Lipschitz function
t\mapsto J^{r}(\xi+t(\zeta-\xi))
yields the identity

(4.15)

J^{r}(\zeta)=J^{r}(\xi)+\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{\xi}J^{r}(\xi+t(\zeta-\xi))\,dt\,(\zeta-\xi)

for all
\zeta,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Using the expansion (
4.14
), formula (
4.15
) with
\zeta=\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
,
\xi=\varepsilon\nabla v
and the
r-2
homogenity of
\nabla_{\xi}J^{r}
(see (
4.11
)), we may expand the
p
phase and
(p,q)
phase contributions as

(4.16)

\begin{split}\Div(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})&=\varepsilon^{p-1}\Div(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v)+\varepsilon^{p-1}\Div(A^{p}(w_{\varepsilon})\nabla w_{\varepsilon})\\
&=\varepsilon^{p-1}\Div(A^{p}(w_{\varepsilon})\nabla w_{\varepsilon}),\end{split}

and

(4.17)

\Div(a|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon^{q-1}\Div(a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v)+\varepsilon^{q-1}\Div(aA^{q}(w_{\varepsilon})\nabla w_{\varepsilon}),

where we set

(4.18)

A^{r}(w_{\varepsilon})\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{\xi}J^{r}\left(\nabla v+t\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\right)\,dt

for
1<r<\infty
. In (
4.16
), we have also used the fact that
v
is
p

R_{v}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

To see that we obtain in the limit the PDE (
4.56
), one can argue similarly as for the continuity of the operators
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
,
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.

Since
R_{v}\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
is the unique solution of (
4.56
), it does not depend on the subsequence taken and hence the whole sequence
(R_{\varepsilon})_{0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}}
needs to converge to
R_{v}
. Furthermore, for example by
[
GT98
, Theorem 6.19]
, it follows that
R_{v}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
. Therefore, we have shown the desired asymptotic expansion (
4.12
).
∎

4.2.
Asymptotic expansion of solutions for
p>q

The goal of this section is to prove that for any smooth
p
-harmonic function
v
without critical points and
1<q<p<\infty
, the solution
u_{\mu}
to

(4.57)

\begin{cases}\Div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=\mu v&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

can be expanded as

(4.58)

u_{\mu}=\mu v+\mu^{1+q-p}R_{v}+o(\mu^{1+q-p})

for a suitable function
R_{v}
as
\mu\to\infty
. Thus, if the
p
-Laplacian is the dominant term, then we consider large boundary values instead of small ones as used in the range
p<q
. Note that in the latter case the weighted
q-
Laplacian yields the major contribution.

Let us start by observing several facts for the double phase problem (
4.57
), when
q<p
and the boundary condition
\mu v
is replaced by an arbitrary function
f\colon\partial\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
:

(1)

Function space:
If
1<q<p<\infty
, then
W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)=W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

(2)

Well-posedness:
Theorem
3.3
remains valid for
1<q<p<\infty
, when
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{1,q}(\Omega)
. More concretely, in this case
[
Dac07
, Theorem 3.30]
and the arguments in the proof of Theorem
3.3
ensure the existence of a unique solution
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
of the double phase problem with boundary value
f
, which coincides with the unique minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
over the set
M_{f}=f+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(3)

Comparison and maximum principle:
The comparison principle (Proposition
3.9
) still holds in the case
1<q<p<\infty
and as explained in Section
3.3
this in turn guarantees the validity of the maximum principle. Thus, if
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
solves the double phase problem with boundary condition
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, then one has

(4.59)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Next, we show the asymptotic expansion (
4.58
). More precisely, we have the following result:

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a smoothly bounded domain,
1<q<p<\infty
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
0<\gamma<\beta<1
with
\beta\leq\alpha
and assume that
a\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
is nonnegative. Moreover, suppose that
v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is a
p
-harmonic function without critical points. Then the unique solution
u_{\mu}
,
\mu>0
, to (
4.57
) has the asymptotic expansion (
4.58
) in the sense of
C^{2,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\mu\to\infty
, in which
R_{v}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
again denotes the unique solution to

(4.60)

\begin{cases}\Div(A_{v}^{p}\nabla R)=-\Div(a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
R=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
A_{v}^{p}
is the uniformly elliptic matrix
\nabla_{\xi}J^{p}(\nabla v)

(see (
4.11
) and (
4.18
)).

Proof.

Since the proof of Proposition
4.5
is very similar to the one of Proposition
4.2
we omit some of the details.

Assume
v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is any
p
-harmonic function without any critical points and let us denote by
u_{\mu}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
\mu>0
, the unique solution to (
4.57
) (see
2
). By the maximum principle we know that there holds

(4.61)

\|u_{\mu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\mu\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

(see
3
) and thus we define
w_{\mu}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
by the Ansatz

(4.62)

u_{\mu}=\mu(v+w_{\mu}),

which solves

(4.63)

\begin{cases}\Div(A_{\mu}(w_{\mu})\nabla w_{\mu})=-\mu^{q-p}\Div(a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
w_{\mu}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where

A_{\mu}(w_{\mu})=A^{p}(w_{\mu})+\mu^{q-p}a\,A^{q}(w_{\mu})

and
A^{r}(w)
,
1<r<\infty
, is defined as in (
4.18
) (see (
4.16
), (
4.17
) and (
4.20
)). So, we end up with precisely the same problem as for
w_{\varepsilon}
, but with the replacement
\varepsilon\to\mu
and
w_{\varepsilon}\to w_{\mu}
. Furthermore, note that in Claim
4.3
the ordering of the exponents
p,q
has no influence and thus the assertions remain valid as long as
\mu\geq 1
. Hence, by following the arguments in the proof of Proposition
4.2
(essentially Schauder’s fixed point theorem) we may deduce that for
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, with
\mu_{0}\geq 1
large, the solution
w_{\mu}
of (
4.63
) is of class
C^{2,\beta}_{\lambda_{0}}(\overline{\Omega})
and there holds

(4.64)

\|w_{\mu}\|_{C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}\lesssim\mu^{q-p}

for any
0<\beta<\min(1,\alpha)
(see proof of Proposition
4.2
). Thus, we deduce that the function
R_{\mu}=\mu^{p-q}w_{\mu}
are uniformly bounded in
C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
for
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, with
\mu_{0}\geq 1
large. By (
4.64
) and the usual compactness argument we deduce that there exists
R_{v}\in C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

R_{\mu}\to R_{v}\text{ in }C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})

as
\mu\to\infty
(up to the extraction of a subsequence), for any
0<\gamma<\beta
. This function
R_{v}
again satisfies (
4.60
) (as
p>q
and
w_{\mu}\to 0
in
C^{2,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
) and hence by elliptic regularity theory it follows that
R_{v}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
and the full sequence
R_{\mu}
,
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, converges to
R_{v}
. This already ensures the desired asymptotic expansion (
4.58
).
∎

5.
DN map of the double phase problem

In this section, we rigorously introduce the Dirichlet to Neumann map related to double phase problem (
1.1
), which we defined formally in (
1.2
).

First, let us note the following simple lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
a nonnegative function. Assume that
u_{j}\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is the unique solution to (
1.1
) with
f=f_{j}\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
for
j=1,2
(see Theorem
3.3
). If
f_{1}=f_{2}
on
\partial\Omega
, then
u_{1}=u_{2}
in
\Omega
. Hence, in particular, for any
f\in W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
there exists a unique solution
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
to (
1.1
). Furthermore, we have the following continuity estimates

(5.1)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|f\|_{W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}+\|f\|_{W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}^{q/p})

and

(5.2)

\|u\|_{W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|f\|_{W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}+\|f\|_{W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}^{p/q}+\|f\|_{W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)}^{q/p}).

Proof.

The first part follows from the comparison principle (Proposition
3.9
). The second part of the lemma is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.3
, the previous established independence of the extension of the boundary value
f|_{\partial\Omega}
and the fact that the image of the trace operator
\text{tr}\colon W^{1,q}(\Omega)\to L^{q}(\partial\Omega)
coincides with
W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
. Finally, to see the continuity estimates, suppose that
F\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
is an extension of
f\in W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
. By applying (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), respectively, and using the continuity of the extension operator
E\colon W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)\to W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, we get the estimates (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). Therefore, we can conclude the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.2

(DN map)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
a nonnegative function. Then we define the
Dirichlet to Neumann (DN) map

\Lambda_{a}
by

(5.3)

\langle\Lambda_{a}f,g\rangle\vcentcolon=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx

for all
f,g\in W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
, where
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
is the unique solution of (
1.1
) and
\omega\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
is an extension of
g\in W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

First, recall that by Lemma
5.1
there exists a unique solution
u\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
to (
1.1
), for any
f\in W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
. Next, let
\omega\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
be any extension of
g\in W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
. Note that the integral in (
5.3
) exists by Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{p-1}{p}+\frac{1}{p}=1
and
\frac{q-1}{q}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, respectively. In fact, we may estimate

\begin{split}|\langle\Lambda_{a}f,g\rangle|&\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;a)}^{q-1}\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;a)}\\
&\leq(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p-1}+\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{1/q}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;a)}^{q-1})\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}<\infty.\end{split}

Moreover, the value of
\langle\Lambda_{a}f,g\rangle
does not depend on the extension
\omega\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
of
g
as
u
solves (
1.1
) (see (
3.8
)).
∎

Remark 5.3

(DN map for
p>q
)
.

The results of Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
continue to hold for
p>q
, when one chooses boundary values
f,g\in W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega)
(see
1
–
3
).

6.
Linearization of the
p
-Laplace equation

This section is concerned with the construction of families of solutions
(v_{\tau})
to the
p
-Laplace equation

(6.1)

\begin{cases}\Div(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
v=f&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

with a prescribed zeroth order term. More precisely, we shall prove the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a smoothly bounded domain and
1<p<\infty
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Moreover, suppose that
v_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is any
p
-harmonic function without critical points,
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
and
V
is the solution to the Dirichlet problem

(6.2)

\begin{cases}\Div(A^{p}_{v_{0}}\nabla V)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
V=\phi&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

then there exists an exponent
0<\beta^{\prime}<1
with the following property: For any
0<\gamma^{\prime}<\beta^{\prime}<1
, there exists
\tau_{0}>0
and a family
(v_{\tau})_{\tau\in[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}]}\subset C^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
of
p
-harmonic functions that have the asymptotic expansion

(6.3)

v_{\tau}=v_{0}+\tau V+o(\tau),

in the sense of
C^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\tau\to 0
.

Remark 6.2
.

Note that (
6.1
) has smooth solutions without critical points in
\Omega
. For example, any non-trivial affine function is such a solution.

Remark 6.3
.

Observe that if
v_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is a fixed
p
-harmonic function without critical points and
V
is any smooth solution of
\Div(A^{p}_{v_{0}}\nabla V)=0
in
\Omega
, then we can always find a corresponding family
(v_{\tau})
of
p
-harmonic functions having the asymptotic expansion (
6.3
). Furthermore, let us point out that the assertion of Lemma
6.1
continues to hold when
v_{0},V\in C^{1,\nu}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\nu>0
, but for our purposes it is enough to work in the smooth setting.

Proof.

Let us start by recalling that if
f\in C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)
satisfies
\|f\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C_{0}
and
u
is a bounded weak solution of

(6.4)

\begin{cases}\Div(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=f&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

with
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}
, then there exist positive constants
\beta^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}(\alpha,p,n)\in(0,1)
,
C=C(\alpha,p,C_{0},C_{1},n,\Omega)>0
such that

(6.5)

\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C.

This is a special case of
[
Lie88
, Theorem 1]
with
m=p-2
,
\kappa=0
,
\lambda=\min(1,p-1)
and
\Lambda=\max(1,p-1)
(see (
4.28
)).

Now, suppose that
v_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is a smooth solution of (
6.1
) without critical points and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
is a given boundary value. Furthermore, let
v_{\tau}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
,
-1<\tau<1
, be the unique solution to

(6.6)

\begin{cases}\Div(|\nabla v_{\tau}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{\tau})=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
v_{\tau}=v_{0}+\tau\phi&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Note that by Theorem
3.7
with
p=q
,
a=0
we have

(6.7)

\|v_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},

where
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is any smooth extension of
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
. Since the boundary data
v_{0}+\tau\phi
satisfy
\|v_{0}+\tau\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\|v_{0}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\phi\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial\Omega)}
, the solutions
v_{\tau}
are uniformly bounded with respect to
\tau
in
C^{1,\beta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
by the aforementioned result of Lieberman. Next, fix some
\gamma^{\prime}<\delta^{\prime}<\beta^{\prime}
. By the theorem of Arzelá–Ascoli, there must exist
\widetilde{v}_{0}\in C^{1,\beta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
such that, after passing to a subsequence,
v_{\tau}\to\widetilde{v}_{0}
in
C^{1,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\tau\to 0
. Taking the limit of (
6.6
) (e.g. in the sense of
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
) and using the trace theorem, it follows that
\widetilde{v}_{0}
solves (
6.1
), with the same Dirichlet data as
v_{0}
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.3
, we may deduce that
\widetilde{v}_{0}=v_{0}
and hence
v_{\tau}\to v_{0}
in
C^{1,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\tau\to 0
. This also shows that
v_{\tau}
has no critical points for
|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}
, when
\tau_{0}>0
is small enough.

Let
V_{\tau}\in C^{1,\beta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
be defined by the Ansatz

(6.8)

v_{\tau}=v_{0}+\tau V_{\tau}.

Then, similarly as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we see that there holds

(6.9)

\Div(|\nabla v_{\tau}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{\tau})=\Div(|\nabla v_{0}|^{p-2}\nabla v_{0})+\tau\Div(\mathcal{A}^{\tau}\nabla V_{\tau}),

where

(6.10)

\mathcal{A}^{\tau}=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{\xi}J^{p}(\nabla v_{0}+t\tau\nabla V_{\tau})\,dt.

Therefore, as
v_{\tau}
and
v_{0}
are
p
-harmonic,
V_{\tau}
solves

(6.11)

\begin{cases}\Div(\mathcal{A}^{\tau}\nabla V_{\tau})=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
V_{\tau}=\phi&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Since
v_{0}
does not have critical points and
\tau V_{\tau}\to 0
in
C^{1,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\tau\to 0
, it follows (by Claim
4.3
and Remark
4.4
, with
a\equiv 0
) that there exists a
\tau_{0}>0
such that the matrices
\mathcal{A}^{\tau}\in C^{0,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
are uniformly elliptic and furthermore they have ellipticity and
\|\cdot\|_{C^{0,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})}
bounds that are independent of
\tau\in[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}]
. From
[
GT98
, Theorems 8.33 & 8.34]
we deduce that
V_{\tau}\in C^{1,\beta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies

(6.12)

\|V_{\tau}\|_{C^{1,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C(\|V_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})}),

where
C>0
only depends on the uniform the ellipticity constant of
\mathcal{A}_{\tau}
, a uniform upper bound of
\|\mathcal{A}_{\tau}\|_{C^{0,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})}
,
\Omega
and
n
. Hence, the maximum principle for (uniformly) elliptic PDEs
[
FRRO23
, Proposition 2.30]
ensures that

(6.13)

\|V_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}

and thus
V_{\tau}
is uniformly bounded with respect to
\tau\in[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}]
in
C^{1,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
.

By the theorem of Arzelá–Ascoli, there must exist a
V\in C^{1,\delta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
V_{\tau}\to V
in
C^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\tau\to 0
, after passing to a subsequence. Passing to the limit in (
6.11
) shows that
V
must be a weak solution to (
6.2
). For the previous convergence assertion, we use the fact that
V\mapsto\nabla_{\xi}J^{p}(\nabla v_{0}+t\nabla V)
is continuous from
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
to
C(\overline{\Omega})
(when
\|V\|_{C^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\lambda_{0}/2
, see Remark
4.4
), the uniform bound of
\nabla_{\xi}J^{p}(\nabla v_{0}+t\tau\nabla V_{\tau})
in
t
provided by Claim
4.3
and Remark
4.4
for small
\tau
as well as the dominated convergence theorem. Since solutions to (
6.2
) are unique, it follows that

(6.14)

V_{\tau}\to V\text{ in }C^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})

as
\tau\to 0
, without passing to a subsequence. Hence, we deduce that

v_{\tau}=v_{0}+\tau V+\tau(V_{\tau}-V)=v_{0}+\tau V+o(\tau)

in
C^{1,\gamma^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\tau\to 0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we establish the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Section
7.1
, we prove Theorem
1.1
in the range

and in Section

in the range

.

7.1.
Reconstruction of
a
in the range
p<q

In this section, we detail our reconstruction procedure of the coefficient

in the double phase problem (
1.1
) when

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
p<q
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p,q}_{a}(\Omega)
,
\varepsilon>0
, be the unique solutions to (
4.1
) for some
p
-harmonic function
v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
without critical points and let
0<\gamma<\beta<1
be some exponents as in Proposition
4.2
, which we shall fix at a later stage of the proof. We claim that for any
g\in W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
, with extension
\omega\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, there holds

(7.1)

\begin{split}\langle\Lambda_{a}\varepsilon v,g\rangle&=\varepsilon^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&\quad+\varepsilon^{q-1}\int_{\Omega}(A^{p}_{v}\nabla R_{v}+a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx+o(\varepsilon^{q-1})\end{split}

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
R_{v}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
is the unique solution of (
4.13
) (see Proposition
4.2
).

Indeed, recalling from the proof of Proposition
4.2
that we may expand
u_{\varepsilon}
as

(7.2)

u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon(v+w_{v,\varepsilon})\text{ with }w_{v,\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{q-p}R_{v,\varepsilon}\text{ and }R_{v,\varepsilon}\to R_{v}\text{ in }C^{2,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega}),

we obtain by formula (

) the identities

(7.3)

\begin{split}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}&=\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v+\varepsilon^{q-1}A^{p}_{v}(w_{v,\varepsilon})\nabla R_{v,\varepsilon}\\
&=\varepsilon^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v+\varepsilon^{q-1}A^{p}_{v}\nabla R_{v}\\
&\quad+\varepsilon^{q-1}\left[A^{p}_{v}\nabla(R_{v,\varepsilon}-R_{v})+(A^{p}_{v}(w_{v,\varepsilon})-A^{p}_{v})\nabla R_{v,\varepsilon}\right]\end{split}

and

(7.4)

a|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{q-2}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{q-1}a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v+\varepsilon^{q-1}\varepsilon^{q-p}aA^{q}_{v}(w_{v,\varepsilon})\nabla R_{v,\varepsilon}.

The matrices
A^{r}_{v}(w_{v,\varepsilon})
,

, appearing in (

)–(

), are given by (
4.18
) and we added the subscript

on these matrices and the functions

to highlight their dependence on
v
.
Since
R_{v,\varepsilon}\to R_{v}
and
w_{v,\varepsilon}\to 0
in
C^{2,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the claim (
7.1
) follows from the definition of the DN map (
5.3
) and the dominated convergence theorem. This shows that, for any
p
-harmonic function
v
without critical points and any
g\in W^{1-1/q,q}(\partial\Omega)
with extension
\omega\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
, we can compute the quantity

(7.5)

\begin{split}I(v,g)&\vcentcolon=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\varepsilon^{1-q}(\langle\Lambda_{a}\varepsilon v,g\rangle-\langle\Lambda_{0}\varepsilon v,g\rangle)\\
&=\int_{\Omega}(A^{p}_{v}\nabla R_{v}+a|\nabla v|^{q-2}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx,\end{split}

from the DN map
\Lambda_{a}
of the double phase problem and the DN map
\Lambda_{0}
of the
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}
.

Next, we consider the family
(v_{\tau})_{\tau\in[-\tau_{0},\tau_{0}]}
of
p
-harmonic functions constructed in Lemma
6.1
, which are supposed to have zeroth order term
v_{0}
and first order term
V
. Thus, the previous calculation can be applied to
v_{\tau}
when
\beta\vcentcolon=\beta^{\prime}
and
\gamma\vcentcolon=\gamma^{\prime}<\beta^{\prime}
. We wish to compute the derivative

(7.6)

J(v_{0},V,\omega)\vcentcolon=\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}\right|_{\tau=0}I(v_{\tau},g)=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\frac{I(v_{\tau},g)-I(v_{0},g)}{\tau}.

First, note that we have

(7.7)

\begin{split}\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V)&\vcentcolon=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\tau^{-1}\left(A^{p}_{v_{\tau}}-A^{p}_{v_{0}}\right)\\
&=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\tau^{-1}\left(\nabla_{\xi}J^{p}(\nabla v_{\tau})-\nabla_{\xi}J^{p}(\nabla v_{0})\right)\\
&=(p-2)|\nabla v_{0}|^{p-4}(\nabla v_{0}\cdot\nabla V)\Big[\mathbf{1}+(p-4)\frac{\nabla v_{0}\otimes\nabla v_{0}}{|\nabla v_{0}|^{2}}\Big]\\
&\quad+(p-2)|\nabla v_{0}|^{p-4}(\nabla v_{0}\otimes\nabla V+\nabla V\otimes\nabla v_{0})\end{split}

in
C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
. This can be seen as follows. First, using (
4.11
) and a straight forward calculation, we obtain

(7.8)

\partial_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}J^{p}_{k}(\xi)=(p-2)|\xi|^{p-4}(\xi_{i}\delta_{kj}+\xi_{j}\delta_{ki}+\xi_{k}\delta_{ij})+(p-2)(p-4)|\xi|^{p-6}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\xi_{k}

for all
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
and
\xi\neq 0
. Secondly, the map
\xi\mapsto\nabla_{\xi}J^{p}(\xi)
is locally Lipschitz away from the origin
\xi=0
(see (
3.6
)). Hence, the fundamental theorem of calculus ensures

(7.9)

\partial_{\xi_{i}}J^{p}_{k}(\eta)-\partial_{\xi_{i}}J^{p}_{k}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}J^{p}_{k}(\xi+t(\eta-\xi))\,dt\,(\eta_{j}-\xi_{j})

for all
1\leq j,k\leq n
, as long as the straight line from
\xi
to
\eta
does not go through the origin. Thus, the nondegeneracy of
\nabla v_{\tau}
,
|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}
, ensures that we can apply (
7.9
) to
\eta=\nabla v_{\tau}
and
\xi=\nabla v_{0}
. Using the convergence
V_{\tau}\to V
in
C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\tau\to 0
and (
7.8
) in the obtained identity, we get the formula in (
7.7
).

Furthermore, by
[
GT98
, Theorem 8.34]
and
\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V)\in C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
, we can denote by
\dot{R}\in C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
the unique solution to

(7.10)

\begin{cases}\Div(A^{p}_{v_{0}}\nabla\dot{R})=-\Div(\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V)\nabla R_{v_{0}})-\Div(a\,A^{q}_{v_{0}}\nabla V)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\dot{R}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Next, let us observe that using (
4.13
) we can write

(7.11)

\begin{cases}\Div(A^{p}_{v_{0}}\nabla(R_{v_{\tau}}-R_{v_{0}}))=\Div((A^{p}_{v_{0}}-A^{p}_{v_{\tau}})\nabla R_{v_{\tau}})+\Div f_{\tau}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
R_{v_{\tau}}-R_{v_{0}}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where

(7.12)

f_{\tau}\vcentcolon=a(|\nabla v_{0}|^{q-2}\nabla v_{0}-|\nabla v_{\tau}|^{q-2}\nabla v_{\tau})\in C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega}).

Next, we claim that there holds

(7.13)

\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\|\nabla R_{v_{\tau}}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}<\infty

for some possibly smaller
\tau_{0}>0
. First, we observe that (
4.13
), Claim
4.3
applied to
A^{p}_{v_{\tau}}
and
[
GT98
, Theorem 8.33]
imply

(7.14)

\|R_{v_{\tau}}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C(\|R_{v_{\tau}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|a|\nabla v_{\tau}|^{q-2}\nabla v_{\tau}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}),

where
C>0
is independent of
\tau
. From the proof of Lemma
6.1
, we know that
v_{\tau}=v_{0}+\tau V_{\tau}
and
V_{\tau}\in C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies the estimate

(7.15)

\|V_{\tau}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})},

where
C>0
is independent of
\tau
and
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is an extension of
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
(see (
6.12
) and (
6.13
)). This ensures the bound

(7.16)

\|v_{\tau}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C(\|v_{0}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}+\|\Phi\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})})

uniformly in
\tau
. Furthermore,
[
GT98
, Theorem 8.16]
and (
4.13
) (for
R_{v_{\tau}}
) demonstrate that

(7.17)

\|R_{v_{\tau}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|a|\nabla v_{\tau}|^{q-2}\nabla v_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Combining this with (
7.14
), we get

(7.18)

\|R_{v_{\tau}}\|_{C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|a|\nabla v_{\tau}|^{q-2}\nabla v_{\tau}\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}

uniformly in
\tau
. By the product rule (
4.31
), the uniform nondegeneracy of
\nabla v_{\tau}
, (
7.16
) and Lemma
4.1
, we can uniformly bound the right hand side of (
7.18
), which yields (
7.13
).

We next assert that

(7.19)

R_{v_{\tau}}\to R_{v_{0}}\text{ in }C^{1,\delta}(\overline{\Omega})

for some
0<\delta<\gamma
. In fact, from (
7.13
) and a compactness argument we can conclude that
R_{v_{\tau}}\to\widetilde{R}_{v_{0}}
in
C^{1,\delta}(\overline{\Omega})
along a subsequence for some
0<\delta<\gamma
. Using (
6.3
) and passing to the limit in the PDE for
R_{v_{\tau}}
, we see that
R_{v_{0}}
and
\widetilde{R}_{v_{0}}
solve the same Dirichlet problem, and hence
R_{v_{0}}=\widetilde{R}_{v_{0}}
. That the coefficient
A^{p}_{v_{\tau}}
in the PDE for
R_{v_{\tau}}
converges to
A^{p}_{v_{0}}
can be shown in a similar way as done at the end of the proof of Lemma
6.1
. Since the limit is independent of the chosen subsequence, we infer the convergence (
7.19
).

Finally, we show that

(7.20)

\dot{R}=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\frac{R_{v_{\tau}}-R_{v_{0}}}{\tau}

in
C^{1,\kappa}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\kappa<\delta
, where
\dot{R}\in C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
is the unique solution of (
7.10
). From (
7.11
), we see that

(7.21)

\begin{cases}\Div\left(A^{p}_{v_{0}}\nabla\frac{R_{v_{\tau}}-R_{v_{0}}}{\tau}\right)=\Div\left(\frac{A^{p}_{v_{0}}-A^{p}_{v_{\tau}}}{\tau}\nabla R_{v_{\tau}}\right)+\Div\frac{f_{\tau}}{\tau}&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\frac{R_{v_{\tau}}-R_{v_{0}}}{\tau}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

for any
\tau\neq 0
. From (
7.7
) and (
7.19
), we deduce that

(7.22)

\sup_{|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}}\max\left(\left\|\frac{A^{p}_{v_{\tau}}-A^{p}_{v_{0}}}{\tau}\right\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})},\|R_{v_{\tau}}\|_{C^{1,\delta}(\overline{\Omega})}\right)<\infty

for some small
\tau_{0}>0
. On the other hand, the formulas (
4.15
), (
7.12
) and (
6.8
) ensure that there holds

f_{\tau}=-\tau a\left(\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{\xi}J^{q}(\nabla v_{0}+t\tau\nabla V_{\tau})\,dt\right)\nabla V_{\tau}.

So, from (
6.14
) and the product rule in Hölder spaces, we see that

(7.23)

f_{\tau}/\tau\to-a\,A^{q}_{v_{0}}\nabla V

in
C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\tau\to 0
, which demonstrates that there holds

(7.24)

\left\|f_{\tau}/\tau\right\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}<\infty

for
|\tau|\leq\tau_{0}
for some small
\tau_{0}>0
. More concretely, to obtain the convergence (
7.23
) we make use of the fact that

(7.25)

\|\nabla_{\xi}J^{q}(\nabla v_{0}+t\tau\nabla V_{\tau})-\nabla_{\xi}J^{q}(\nabla v_{0})\|_{C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})}\to 0

as
\tau\to 0
, uniformly for
0\leq t\leq 1
. This in turn can be shown by using (
7.9
), (
7.8
) and Lemma
4.1
. For similar arguments, we refer the reader to the proof of Claim
4.3
and Lemma
6.1
. Now, using (
4.31
), (
7.22
), (
7.24
) and
[
GT98
, Theorem 8.16 & 8.33]
, we see that

\left\|\frac{R_{v_{\tau}}-R_{v_{0}}}{\tau}\right\|_{C^{1,\delta}(\overline{\Omega})}<\infty

uniformly in
\tau
. Hence, there exists
\dot{\mathcal{R}}\in C^{1,\delta}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

(7.26)

\frac{R_{v_{\tau}}-R_{v_{0}}}{\tau}\to\dot{\mathcal{R}}

in
C^{1,\kappa}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\kappa<\delta
. By the convergence results (
7.7
), (
7.23
) and (
7.26
), we see that passing to the limit
\tau\to 0
in (
7.21
) reveals that
\dot{\mathcal{R}}\in C^{1,\delta}(\overline{\Omega})
solves (
7.10
). Thus, we may conclude that
\dot{\mathcal{R}}=\dot{R}
, which in turn implies the convergence (
7.20
).

Finally, we observe that (
6.8
), (
4.15
),
V_{\tau}\to V
in
C^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
and (
7.25
) demonstrate that there holds

(7.27)

\begin{split}&\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}\right|_{\tau=0}\int_{\Omega}a|\nabla v_{\tau}|^{q-2}\nabla v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\Omega}a\left(\int_{0}^{1}\nabla_{\xi}J^{q}(\nabla v_{0}+t\tau\nabla V_{\tau})dt\right)\,\nabla V_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\Omega}a\left(\int_{0}^{1}[\nabla_{\xi}J^{q}(\nabla v_{0}+t\tau\nabla V_{\tau})-\nabla_{\xi}J^{q}(\nabla v_{0})]\,dt\right)\,\nabla V_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&\quad+\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla_{\xi}J^{q}(\nabla v_{0})\nabla V_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}a\,A_{v_{0}}^{q}\nabla V\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx.\end{split}

It follows from (
7.6
), (
7.5
) with
v=v_{\tau}
, (
7.7
), (
7.19
), (
7.20
) and (
7.27
) that we have

(7.28)

\begin{split}J(v_{0},V,\omega)&=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{A^{p}_{v_{\tau}}\nabla R_{v_{\tau}}-A^{p}_{v_{0}}\nabla R_{v_{0}}}{\tau}\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&\quad+\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}\right|_{\tau=0}\int_{\Omega}a|\nabla v_{\tau}|^{q-2}\nabla v_{\tau}\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&=\lim_{\tau\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{A^{p}_{v_{\tau}}-A^{p}_{v_{0}}}{\tau}\nabla R_{v_{\tau}}+A^{p}_{v_{0}}\frac{\nabla R_{v_{\tau}}-\nabla R_{v_{0}}}{\tau}\right)\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}a\,A_{v_{0}}^{q}\nabla V\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\left(\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V)\nabla R_{v_{0}}+A_{v_{0}}^{p}\nabla\dot{R}+aA^{q}_{v_{0}}\nabla V\right)\cdot\nabla\omega\,dx.\end{split}

Suppose that
V_{1},V_{2}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
are two solutions of the equation
\Div(A_{v_{0}}^{p}\nabla V)=0
with boundary data
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
(see
[
GT98
, Theorem 6.15]
). Using Lemma
6.1
, the symmetry of the matrices
A^{p}_{v_{0}},\dot{A}^{p}_{v_{0}}(V_{1})\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
(the regularity assertions follows from (
4.11
), (
4.39
), (
7.7
), (
4.40
), the nondegeneracy of
\nabla v_{0}
and
v_{0},V_{1}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
) and the fact that
R,\dot{R}\in C^{1,\kappa}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfy
R_{v_{0}}|_{\partial\Omega}=\dot{R}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, we get by an integration by parts in the first two terms of (
7.28
) the identity

(7.29)

\begin{split}&J(v_{0},V_{1},V_{2})=\int_{\Omega}\left(\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V_{1})\nabla R_{v_{0}}+A_{v_{0}}^{p}\nabla\dot{R}+aA^{q}_{v_{0}}\nabla V_{1}\right)\cdot\nabla V_{2}\,dx\\
&\quad=-\int_{\Omega}[R_{v_{0}}\Div(\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V_{1})\nabla V_{2})+\dot{R}\Div(A^{p}_{v_{0}}\nabla V_{2})]\,dx+\int_{\Omega}a\,A^{q}_{v_{0}}\nabla V_{1}\cdot\nabla V_{2}\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}\left(a\,A_{v_{0}}^{q}\nabla V_{1}\cdot\nabla V_{2}-R_{v_{0}}\Div(\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V_{1})\nabla V_{2})\right)\,dx.\end{split}

Up to now we have assumed that all functions are real-valued, but in the next final step we want to choose
V_{j}
,
j=1,2
, complex-valued while keeping
v_{0}
real-valued. For this let us note that
V\mapsto\dot{A}^{p}_{v_{0}}(V)
is linear and hence if we decompose the possibly complex-valued solutions
V_{j}
as
V_{j}=U_{j}+iW_{j}
,
j=1,2
, then we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left(a\,A_{v_{0}}^{q}\nabla V_{1}\cdot\nabla V_{2}-R_{v_{0}}\Div(\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V_{1})\nabla V_{2})\right)\,dx\\
&=\left(J(v_{0},U_{1},U_{2})-J(v_{0},W_{1},W_{2})\right)+i\left(J(v_{0},U_{1},W_{2})+J(v_{0},W_{1},U_{2})\right).\end{split}

As all terms in the last line are determined by the DN map
\langle\Lambda_{a}f,g\rangle
for appropriate real-valued boundary conditions
f
and
g
, the same remains true for the integral

\int_{\Omega}\left(a\,A_{v_{0}}^{q}\nabla V_{1}\cdot\nabla V_{2}-R_{v_{0}}\Div(\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V_{1})\nabla V_{2})\right)\,dx,

which we still denote by
J(v_{0},V_{1},V_{2})
. This notation is justified by the fact that if
V_{j}
,
j=1,2
, solve (
6.2
), then the same holds for the real and imaginary parts as
v_{0}
is real-valued.

Let
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
|z|=1
and set
v_{0}=z\cdot x
. Then using
A^{r}_{v_{0}}=\nabla_{\xi}J^{r}(\nabla v_{0})
, (
4.11
) and (
7.7
), we deduce

(7.30)

A^{p}_{v_{0}}=\mathbf{1}+(p-2)z\otimes z,\quad A^{q}_{v_{0}}=\mathbf{1}+(q-2)z\otimes z,

and

(7.31)

\dot{A}^{p}_{v_{0}}(V_{1})=(p-2)\Big[(z\cdot\nabla V_{1})\mathbf{1}+(p-4)(z\cdot\nabla V_{1})z\otimes z+z\otimes\nabla V_{1}+\nabla V_{1}\otimes z\Big].

Let
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
be such that
\xi\perp z
. For
s>0
to be chosen later, we introduce the complex vectors

(7.32)

\zeta_{\pm}=\pm sz+i\xi.

By (
7.30
),
\zeta_{\pm}\cdot\zeta_{\pm}=s^{2}-|\xi|^{2}
,
z\cdot\zeta_{\pm}=\pm s
and

(7.33)

(a\otimes b)c=a(b\cdot c)

for all
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, we have

(7.34)

\zeta_{\pm}\cdot A_{v_{0}}^{p}\zeta_{\pm}=(p-1)s^{2}-|\xi|^{2}.

So, if we choose

(7.35)

s=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|,

then the functions

(7.36)

V_{1}(x)=e^{\zeta_{+}\cdot x},\quad V_{2}(x)=e^{\zeta_{-}\cdot x},

are both global smooth solutions of
\Div(A_{v_{0}}^{p}\nabla V)=0
.

Using (
7.31
), (
7.33
),
\zeta_{+}\cdot\zeta_{-}=-(s^{2}+|\xi|^{2})
and (
7.35
), we get

(7.37)

\begin{split}\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V_{1})\nabla V_{2}&=(p-2)e^{2i\xi\cdot x}\left(s\mathbf{1}+s(p-4)z\otimes z+z\otimes\zeta_{+}+\zeta_{+}\otimes z\right)\zeta_{-}\\
&=(p-2)e^{2i\xi\cdot x}\left(s\zeta_{-}-s^{2}(p-4)z-(s^{2}+|\xi|^{2})z-s\zeta_{+}\right)\\
&=-(p-2)e^{2i\xi\cdot x}[(p-1)s^{2}+|\xi|^{2}]z=-2(p-2)|\xi|^{2}e^{2i\xi\cdot x}z\end{split}

and thus it follows from
\xi\perp z
that

(7.38)

\Div(\dot{A}_{v_{0}}^{p}(V_{1})\nabla V_{2})=0.

On the other hand, by (
7.33
), (
7.35
),
\zeta_{\pm}\cdot\zeta_{\pm}=s^{2}-|\xi|^{2}
,
z\cdot\zeta_{\pm}=\pm s
and
\zeta_{+}\cdot\zeta_{-}=-(s^{2}+|\xi|^{2})
we obtain

(7.39)

\begin{split}A^{q}_{v_{0}}\nabla V_{1}\cdot\nabla V_{2}&=e^{2i\xi\cdot x}A^{q}_{v_{0}}\zeta_{+}\cdot\zeta_{-}\\
&=-e^{2i\xi\cdot x}((q-1)s^{2}+|\xi|^{2})\\
&=-\frac{p+q-2}{p-1}|\xi|^{2}e^{2i\xi\cdot x}.\end{split}

Therefore, by using (
7.29
), (
7.38
) and (
7.39
), we deduce that

(7.40)

\begin{split}J(z\cdot x,e^{\zeta_{+}\cdot x},e^{\zeta_{-}\cdot x})&=-\frac{p+q-2}{4(p-1)}\int_{\Omega}a(x)|2\xi|^{2}e^{2i\xi\cdot x}\,dx.\end{split}

Hence, if we set
a
outside of
\overline{\Omega}
equal to zero, then we get

(7.41)

\hat{a}(\xi)=-\frac{4(p-1)}{p+q-2}\frac{J(z\cdot x,e^{\widetilde{\zeta}_{+}\cdot x},e^{\widetilde{\zeta}_{-}\cdot x})}{|\xi|^{2}}

for all
\xi\perp z
, where

\widetilde{\zeta}_{\pm}=\pm\frac{|\xi|}{2(p-1)^{1/2}}z+i\frac{\xi}{2}.

As formula (
7.41
) holds for all
z\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
\xi\neq 0
with
\xi\perp z
, we can conclude that
\hat{a}(\xi)
is determined by
J
for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Therefore, by Fourier’s inversion theorem we have shown that
a
can be recovered from the DN map
\Lambda_{a}
(see (
7.5
), (
7.6
) and (
7.41
)).

This concludes the proof.
∎

7.2.
Reconstruction of
a
in the range
p>q

In this section, we explain the necessary changes of Section
7.1
to reconstruct the coefficient
a
in the double phase problem (
1.1
) when

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
p>q
.

In a similar manner as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we consider the unique solution
u_{\mu}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to (
4.57
) for some
p
-harmonic function
v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
without critical points and large

.

First note that with the help of Proposition
4.5
we can repeat the argument of the proof of Theorem

to conclude that there holds

\begin{split}I(v,g)&\vcentcolon=\lim_{\mu\to\infty}\mu^{1-q}\left(\langle\Lambda_{a}\mu v,g\rangle-\langle\Lambda_{0}\mu v,g\rangle\right)\\

Also the rest of the proof does not depend on the ordering of the exponents

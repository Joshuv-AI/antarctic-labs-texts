Draft genome of an NDM-5-producing Huaxiibacter chinensis isolated from a urinary tract infection
Authors: Yu Feng, Meiyun Zong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHuaxiibacter chinensis is a recently proposed Enterobacteriaceae species with few public genomes. We report the draft genome of urinary isolate 195099, carrying multiple resistance genes, including blaNDM-5, documenting a carbapenem-resistant clinical isolate of this uncommon species.

ANNOUNCEMENT

Antimicrobial resistance remains a major global threat, particularly carbapenemase-producing Enterobacteriaceae (1). Huaxiibacter chinensis and the genus Huaxiibacter were proposed in 2022 from a human sputum isolate (2). Reports from mink pneumonia (3) and hibernating bats (4) suggest diverse host-associated niches, but genomic data remain limited, with only six assemblies available in GenBank. Here, we report a clinical H. chinensis genome from a human urine isolate.

Strain 195099 was recovered in 2023 from the urine of a patient with a urinary tract infection after radical cystectomy at our hospital by aerobic overnight culture on LB agar (Hopebio, Qingdao, China) at 37°C. The isolate was initially assigned to the Enterobacter cloacae complex by MALDI-TOF MS (Bruker Daltonics, Bremen, Germany), and antimicrobial susceptibility was tested using the Vitek II system (bioMérieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France). A single colony was grown overnight in LB broth at 37°C, and genomic DNA was extracted using QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). Libraries were prepared from 350-bp sheared DNA fragments using the NEBNext Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (New England Biolabs, Ipswich, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol without modification. NovaSeq 6000 (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) sequencing generated 4,377,249 150-bp paired-end read pairs comprising 1.31 Gb, or approximately 262× coverage. Reads were quality controlled using fastp v1.3.6 (5) and assembled de novo with SPAdes v4.2.0 in isolate mode (6). Species identification was determined by average nucleotide identity (ANI) analysis against genomes of Enterobacteriaceae type strains using skani v0.3.2 (7). Antimicrobial resistance genes were detected with AMRFinderPlus v4.2.7 (8). Whole-genome single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) analysis was performed using Snippy v4.6.0 (https://github.com/tseemann/snippy), followed by IQ-TREE v3.1.2 (9) inference under GTR + F + ASC + G4 model with 1,000 ultrafast bootstraps. Default settings were used unless otherwise specified.

The draft genome comprised 57 contigs totaling 5,010,389 bp, with an N50 of 387,278 bp and a G + C content of 53.98%. NCBI quality assessment and PGAP v6.11 (10) annotation indicated 100% completeness and identified 4,692 coding genes, 108 RNA genes, and 56 pseudogenes. ANI analysis showed 99.84% identity to the type strain of H. chinensis (GCA_022171985.1), reclassifying strain 195099 as H. chinensis. The resistome included blaNDM-5, blaOXA-10, aadA1, ant(2″)-Ia, catA1, catB8, oqxA, oqxB, sul1, and ble, consistent with resistance to amoxicillin-clavulanic acid (MIC ≥ 32 µg/mL), ampicillin-sulbactam (≥32 µg/mL), ceftazidime (≥64 µg/mL), cefepime (16 μg/mL), doripenem (≥8 µg/mL), imipenem (≥16 µg/mL), meropenem (≥16 µg/mL), ciprofloxacin (≥4 µg/mL), and levofloxacin (4 μg/mL), while remaining susceptible to aztreonam (≤1 µg/mL) and tigecycline (≤0.5 µg/mL).

Whole-genome SNP-based phylogenetic analysis of strain 195099 and the five pure-culture H. chinensis genomes showed broad geographic, source, and genetic diversity (Fig. 1). Strain 195099 was closest to an Ethiopian isolate and the type strain, differing by 857 and 928 SNPs, respectively, while distances to the remaining genomes exceeded 30,000 SNPs. These findings document a carbapenem-resistant clinical urine isolate of H. chinensis and highlight this uncommon species as a potential reservoir of clinically important resistance genes.

On ill-posedness for the Gabitov–Turitsyn equation
Matthew Kowalski

Abstract
. We investigate the well- and ill-posedness theory for the Gabitov–Turitsyn equation, which models the long-time dynamics of pulses in dispersion-managed optical fibers. We identify two critical regularities, corresponding to two scaling pseudo-symmetries, that demarcate regimes of ill-posedness. First, we identify s m = d 2 − 2 p , s_{m}=\tfrac{d}{2}-\tfrac{2}{p}, coinciding with the monomial NLS. For s ≥ max ⁡ ( s m , 0 ) s\geq\max(s_{m},0) , local well-posedness is known to hold in H s H^{s} , while for s < s m s<s_{m} , we show that the data-to-solution map fails to be C p + 1 C^{p+1} in H s H^{s} . Second, we identify s i = d 2 − 4 p , s_{i}=\tfrac{d}{2}-\tfrac{4}{p}, below which we conjecture that norm inflation occurs. We resolve this conjecture in H s H^{s} in the case s < min ⁡ ( s i , 0 ) s<\min(s_{i},0) —specifically for the one-dimensional cubic model—and in the case 1 ≤ s < s i 1\leq s<s_{i} . In the case s i ≥ 1 s_{i}\geq 1 , we establish norm inflation by showing that suitable solutions undergo energy equipartition : a rapid renormalization of kinetic and potential energy.

1.3.
Connection to fiber-optics

Accounting for the dominant effects, the standard model for the evolution of a pulse in an optical fiber is the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

(NLS)

i\partial_{t}\psi+\gamma\Delta\psi+|\psi|^{2}\psi=0,\quad\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}(x),\quad\psi:\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}\to\mathbb{C},

where
\gamma
is the group velocity dispersion of the fiber (here just called dispersion), and
\psi
is the complex modulation of a quasi-monochromatic carrier wave. The cubic, or
Kerr
, nonlinearity is typically dominant and arises from pulses polarizing the material they propagate within.
We note that the role of
t
and
x
are flipped from expectation:
t
represents the
distance
along the optical fiber while
x
represents a
retarded time
, traveling with the carrier wave.

In a typical optical fiber, dispersion dominates the nonlinear effects and causes pulses to broaden. This limits bandwidth as pulses overlap and interact. A common technique to mitigate these effects, introduced in
origin-linear-dispersion-management
, is dispersion-management: concatenating fiber segments with opposite dispersion so that higher-frequency components propagate faster in one section and slower in the next.
Mathematically, this corresponds to allowing
\gamma=\gamma(t)
in (
NLS
) to alternate between positive and negative along the fiber (i.e. in
t
). This results in the so-called
dispersion-managed nonlinear Schrödinger equation
.

Strong dispersion-management takes this to the extreme, alternating quickly between extreme positive dispersion and extreme negative. This achieves a low (but nonzero) average dispersion with high local dispersion. Mathematically, this corresponds to

\gamma(t)=\langle\gamma\rangle+\varepsilon^{-1}\gamma_{0}(t/\varepsilon),

where
\langle\gamma\rangle
is the net dispersion and
\gamma_{0}
is a periodic function with mean
0
. Crucially, this results in a fiber where dispersion and nonlinear effects are balanced, allowing for stable, soliton-like pulses.

In the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, with a suitable change of variables, the one-dimensional cubic Gabitov–Turitsyn equation (
d=1,p=2
) emerges as the natural model of the large-scale dynamics:

(1.4)

iu_{t}+\langle\gamma\rangle\Delta u+\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\sigma\Delta}\Big[\big|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u\big|^{2}\cdot e^{i\sigma\Delta}u\Big]d\mu(\sigma)=0,

where
\mu
is a probability measure, dictated by fiber properties and the fine details of the dispersion management.
See
GT-justification-1
;
GT-justification-2
;
GT-justification-3
;
GT-justification-4
;
solitons-zero-dispersion-reason
for the numerical and rigorous justification of this limiting process and see
T-averaging
for the general Hamiltonian process. We also direct the reader to
solitons-positive-dispersion
for a nice mathematical exposition to (
1.4
) and discussion of the measure
\mu
.

To recover the specific form of (
GT
) used in this paper, a model case with
\gamma_{0}=\mathds{1}_{[0,1)}-\mathds{1}_{[1,2)}
(extended periodically) is considered. Other choices of
\gamma_{0}
give different probability measures
\mu
in the nonlinearity of (
1.4
). Indeed, one can even consider fiber loss and the correcting amplification by generalizing
\mu
; see
GT-with-amplification
.

It is important to note that the techniques used in fiber optics have experienced dramatic twists in recent history. Dispersion-management alone underwent a number of iterations—such as focusing on vanishing net dispersion—before strong dispersion management was adopted; see
DCF-history
;
DSP-DCF-comparison
. We direct the interested reader to
(
dispersion-management-survey
, §2.1)
for a thorough history and explanation of various methods.

In recent years,
digital signal processing
(DSP), enabled by coherent detection, has emerged as a competitor to dispersion-management. DSP sidesteps physical compensation by propagating pulses unmanaged and digitally reversing ill-effects at the receiver. This hinges on the long-time dynamics of (
NLS
).
However, as the scale and rate of information transfer increases, DSP has its own challenges (see
DSP-concerns-Turitsyn
). Recent research and industry perspectives have proposed hybrid dispersion-management and DSP approaches; see
DSP-DM-combo-2023
;
blog-post
. Indeed, many transoceanic systems will continue to rely on dispersion-management due to the cost and time associated with changing to a pure DSP system.

We direct the interested reader to
Biswas
;
Fibich
for nice textbook derivations of (
NLS
) and (
1.4
).

In this section, we prove Theorem

3.1.

We begin by briefly defining the power-series expansion of the data-to-solution map. Recall the Duhamel formula for a solution of (

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt-t\bigg|\int_{\xi=\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}+\xi_{3}}\frac{e^{-i(\xi^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi_{3}^{2})}}{\xi^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi_{3}^{2}}\widehat{\phi}(\xi_{1})\overline{\widehat{\phi}(\xi_{2})}\widehat{\phi}(\xi_{3})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}\bigg|

\displaystyle\textup{II}=tR^{3}\bigg|\int_{N}^{N+A}\int_{2N}^{2N+A}\frac{e^{2i(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})(\xi_{1}-\xi)}}{2(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})(\xi_{1}-\xi)}\mathds{1}_{[N,N+A]}(\xi-\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})d\xi_{2}d\xi_{1}\bigg|.

We focus on the integral over
\xi_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim tR^{3}\int_{N}^{N+A}\frac{1}{|\xi_{1}-\xi|}\bigg[\frac{1}{|\xi_{1}-b||\xi_{1}-\xi|}+\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{|\xi_{1}-\xi||\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}}d\xi_{2}\bigg]d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim tR^{3}AN^{-1}[N^{-2}+AN^{-3}]

where we note that
|\xi_{1}-\xi|,|\xi_{1}-b|,|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\gtrsim N

.

Combining the estimates for

and

As

\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\in L^{2}

Proof.

For the sake of induction, we prove the stronger, frequency-wise bound

We proceed by induction. By construction, (

) holds for all

In the following calculations, we suppress the dependence on

By definition, we may write

\displaystyle\widehat{\Xi_{j}}(t,\xi)=i\sum_{k+\ell+m=j-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\xi=\xi_{k}-\xi_{\ell}+\xi_{m}}e^{-i(t-s)\xi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle e^{i\sigma(\xi^{2}-\xi_{k}^{2}+\xi_{\ell}^{2}-\xi_{m}^{2})}d\sigma

We note that we may bound the innermost integral over

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{\Xi_{j}}(t,\xi)\big|\leq\sum_{k+\ell+m=j-1}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{I}_{\ell}}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{\Xi_{j}}(t,\xi)\big|\leq\sum_{k+\ell+m=j-1}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{I}_{\ell}}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}

\displaystyle\cdot\mathds{1}_{K-L+M}(\xi)\int_{K}\int_{L}\Big[1\wedge\tfrac{1}{|\xi-\xi_{k}||\xi_{k}-\xi_{\ell}|}\Big]d\xi_{\ell}d\xi_{k}.

As

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{\Xi_{j}}(t,\xi)\big|\leq

\displaystyle R^{2j+1}(\log A)^{2j}t^{j}\sum_{k+\ell+m=j-1}\tfrac{2C^{j-1}}{(1+k)^{2}(1+\ell)^{2}(1+m)^{2}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{I}_{\ell}}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\mathds{1}_{K-L+M}(\xi).

We now invoke the combinatorial trick. As

, we know that

This then implies that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k+\ell+m=j-1}\frac{2C^{j-1}}{(1+k)^{2}(1+\ell)^{2}(1+m)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{18C^{j-1}}{(1+j)^{2}}\bigg(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(1+n)^{2}}\bigg)^{2}.

Choosing

\displaystyle\sum_{k+\ell+m=j-1}\frac{2C^{j-1}}{(1+k)^{2}(1+\ell)^{2}(1+m)^{2}}

as desired.

For the sum over intervals, we invoke the same trick. We note that the set

may be constructed of three intervals of width

\displaystyle\sum_{k+\ell+m=j-1}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{I}_{k}}\sum_{L\in\mathcal{I}_{\ell}}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{I}_{m}}\mathds{1}_{K-L+M}(\xi)\leq\sum_{k+\ell+m=j-1}\sum_{I\in\tilde{\mathcal{I}_{j}}}\mathds{1}_{I}(\xi),

to conclude (

) and complete the proof of Lemma

In this section, we extend the virial identity to the general (

Proof of Proposition
1.5
.

Define the variance of

When it is convenient, we use subscripts of

to indicate partial derivatives and subscripts of

to indicate coordinates. In the usual way, any repeated indices are summed.

Taking a derivative of

in time and applying integration by parts, we find that

\displaystyle\dot{v}(t)

\displaystyle=2\Re\int|x|^{2}\overline{u}u_{t}

\displaystyle=-2\Im\int|x|^{2}\overline{u}\bigg[\langle\gamma\rangle u_{jj}+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-i\sigma\Delta}\big(|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p}\cdot e^{i\sigma\Delta}u\big)d\sigma\bigg]dx

\displaystyle=4\langle\gamma\rangle\Im\int\overline{u}x_{j}u_{j}dx-2\Im\iint_{0}^{1}\overline{e^{i\sigma\Delta}(|x|^{2}u)}\cdot|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p}\cdot e^{i\sigma\Delta}u\;d\sigma dx

(6.1)

\displaystyle=\langle\gamma\rangle\dot{v}_{1}(t)+\dot{v}_{2}(t).

We first consider
\dot{v}_{2}
and note the identity,

\displaystyle e^{i\sigma\Delta}|x|^{2}

\displaystyle=(|x|^{2}+4i\sigma x\cdot\nabla+2di\sigma-4\sigma^{2}\Delta)e^{i\sigma\Delta}

\displaystyle=\big[|x|^{2}+i(4\sigma x\cdot\nabla+2d\sigma+4\sigma^{2}\partial_{\sigma})\big]e^{i\sigma\Delta}.

In the integral, the
|x|^{2}
term will vanish due to the imaginary part in
\dot{v}_{2}
. Integrating by parts again, we then find that

\displaystyle\dot{v}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=-2\Re\iint_{0}^{1}\overline{(4\sigma x\cdot\nabla+2d\sigma+4\sigma^{2}\partial_{\sigma})e^{i\sigma\Delta}u}\cdot|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p}\cdot e^{i\sigma\Delta}u\;d\sigma dx

\displaystyle=2\iint_{0}^{1}\Big(\tfrac{4}{p+2}\sigma x\cdot\nabla+2d\sigma+\tfrac{4\sigma^{2}}{p+2}\partial_{\sigma}\Big)|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p+2}d\sigma dx

(6.2)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{4(dp-4)}{p+2}\iint_{0}^{1}\sigma|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p+2}d\sigma dx+\tfrac{8}{p+2}\int|e^{i\Delta}u|^{p+2}dx.

In the focusing case
\langle\gamma\rangle>0
, considered by

, it is sufficient to remove the

term and bound
\dot{v}_{2}
above by

. However, in the defocusing case

We now turn attention to

\displaystyle=-4d\Re\int\overline{u}u_{t}\;dx-8\Im\int u_{t}x_{j}\overline{u_{j}}dx

\displaystyle=4d\langle\gamma\rangle\int|\nabla u|^{2}-4d\iint_{0}^{1}|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p+2}d\sigma dx-8\langle\gamma\rangle\Re\int u_{kk}x_{j}\overline{u_{j}}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt-8\Re\iint_{0}^{1}\big(|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p}\cdot e^{i\sigma\Delta}u\big)\overline{e^{i\sigma\Delta}(x\cdot\nabla u)}d\sigma dx

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt-8\Re\iint_{0}^{1}\big(|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p}\cdot e^{i\sigma\Delta}u\big)\overline{e^{i\sigma\Delta}(x\cdot\nabla u)}d\sigma dx

\displaystyle=(4d+8)\langle\gamma\rangle\int|\nabla u|^{2}-4d\iint_{0}^{1}|e^{i\sigma\Delta}u|^{p+2}d\sigma dx+\textup{III}+\textup{IV}.

Consider the Hessian term

\displaystyle=-8d\langle\gamma\rangle\int|\nabla u|^{2}dx-8\langle\gamma\rangle\Re\int u_{jk}\overline{u_{j}}x_{k}dx=-8d\langle\gamma\rangle\int|\nabla u|^{2}dx-\textup{III}.

Upon rearranging, this implies that

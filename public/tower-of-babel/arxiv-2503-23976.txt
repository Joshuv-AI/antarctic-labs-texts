Maximal Betti number for local system cohomology of hyperplane arrangement complements
Yongqiang Liu, Masahiko Yoshinaga

Abstract
Let ℒ {\mathcal{L}} be a rank one local system with field coefficient on the complement M ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) M({\mathcal{A}}) of an essential complex hyperplane arrangement 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} in ℂ ℓ \mathbb{C}^{\ell} . Dimca-Papadima and Randell independently showed that M ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) M({\mathcal{A}}) is homotopy equivalent to a minimal CW-complex. It implies that dim H k ​ ( M ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) , ℒ ) ≤ b k ​ ( M ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) ) \dim H^{k}(M({\mathcal{A}}),{\mathcal{L}})\leq b_{k}(M({\mathcal{A}})) . In this paper, we show that if 𝒜 {\mathcal{A}} is real, then the inequality holds as equality for some 0 ≤ k ≤ ℓ 0\leq k\leq\ell if and only if ℒ {\mathcal{L}} is the constant sheaf. The proof is using the descriptions of local system cohomology of M ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) M({\mathcal{A}}) in terms of chambers. MSC-class : 32S22 (Primary) 52C35, 32S50 (Secondary) Keywords : Hyperplane arrangements, Chambers, Minimal CW-complex, local system, Aomoto complex.

A rank one

around each hyperplane

Fix

{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
is the constant sheaf by induction on the dimension of the ambient space and the number of hyperplanes for real hyperplane arrangements. Hence
{\mathcal{L}}
is also the constant sheaf.
On the other hand, if
b_{\ell}(M^{\prime})=0
,
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is not essential. It implies that
\overline{H}_{\infty}
is transversal to all other hyperplanes in
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
. In particular,
{\mathcal{L}}
is the constant sheaf if and only if so is
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime\prime}
. Clearly
\dim H^{\ell-1}(M^{\prime\prime},{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime\prime})=b_{\ell-1}(M^{\prime\prime})=b_{\ell}(M)>0
in this case. Then the claim follows by induction for
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The above proof indeed shows that
\partial_{k}(q)\neq 0
for any
0\leq k\leq\ell-1
if
{\mathcal{A}}
is real and
{\mathcal{L}}
is not the constant sheaf.

4
Proof of Proposition
1.4

We give the proof by induction on
\ell
with two steps. If
\ell=1
, the claim is obvious. For induction, we assume that the claim holds for any essential hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell-1}
.

Step 1: Assume that
{\mathcal{A}}
is central in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
. We reduce the proof for
{\mathcal{A}}
to lower dimension case, then prove it by induction.

If
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\neq 0,
it is well known that the Aomoto complex
(H^{\ast}(M({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K}),w\cup)
is exact, see e.g.
[
12
, Proposition 2.1]
. It implies that the cup product map
H^{k-1}(M({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})\overset{w\cup}{\to}H^{k}(M({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})
has rank
|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}b_{j}(M({\mathcal{A}}))|
.
Since
{\mathcal{A}}
is essential, it follows from
[
9
, Lemma 8]
that
|\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}b_{j}(M({\mathcal{A}}))|>0
for any
1\leq k\leq\ell
. Then the claim follows.
On the other hand, consider the case
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=0
. Note that
{\mathcal{A}}
is central. Let
U({\mathcal{A}})
denote the complement of the
corresponding projective arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{\ell-1}
. Then
M({\mathcal{A}})=U({\mathcal{A}})\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
. One can identify
H^{1}(U({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})
with the affine space
\{x\in\mathbb{K}^{n}\mid x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}=0\}\cong\mathbb{K}^{n-1}
. Since we assumed that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=0
,
w
can be viewed as a non-zero element in
H^{1}(U({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})
. Moreover, by Künneth formula the map
H^{k-1}(M({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})\overset{w\cup}{\to}H^{k}(M({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})
is isomorphic to the direct sum of

H^{k-1}(U({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})\overset{w\cup}{\to}H^{k}(U({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})
and
H^{k-2}(U({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K})\overset{w\cup}{\to}H^{k-1}(U({\mathcal{A}}),\mathbb{K}).

Since
U({\mathcal{A}})
is a complement
to an essential hyperplane arrangement with less dimension for its ambient space (in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell-1}
), the claim follows by induction.

{\mathcal{A}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
is not necessarily central by reducing to the central case with the same dimension for the ambient space.

w\neq 0

w_{1}\neq 0

{\mathcal{A}}
is essential, there exists an intersection point
x\in L_{0}({\mathcal{A}})

H_{1}

{\mathcal{A}}_{x}
of
{\mathcal{A}}
consisting of all hyperplanes passing through
x
. Let
M({\mathcal{A}}_{x})
denote the complement of
{\mathcal{A}}_{x}
. Set
M_{x}=M({\mathcal{A}})\cap B
, where
B
is a small enough open ball in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
centered at
x
.
We have two inclusions
u\colon M_{x}\to M({\mathcal{A}})
and
v\colon M({\mathcal{A}})\to M({\mathcal{A}}_{x})

(De)constructing Continuous Gauge Symmetries
Leron Borsten, Hyungrok Kim (金炯錄) [1em] { l.borsten , h.kim2 }@herts.ac.uk

Abstract
A ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional field theory with a periodic spatial dimension may be approximated by a d d -dimensional theory with a truncated Kaluza–Klein tower of k k fields; as k → ∞ k\to\infty , one recovers the original ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional theory. One may similarly expect that U ⁡ ( 1 ) \operatorname{U}(1) -valued Maxwell theory may be approximated by ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} -valued gauge theory and that, as k → ∞ k\to\infty , one recovers the original Maxwell theory. However, this fails: the k → ∞ k\to\infty limit of ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} -valued gauge theory is flat Maxwell theory with no local degrees of freedom. We instead construct field theories 𝒯 k \mathcal{T}_{k} such that, with appropriate matter couplings, the k → ∞ k\to\infty limit does recover Maxwell theory in the absence of magnetic monopoles (but with possible Wilson loops), and show that 𝒯 k \mathcal{T}_{k} can be understood as Maxwell theory with the insertion of a certain nonlocal operator that projects out principal U ⁡ ( 1 ) \operatorname{U}(1) -bundles that do not arise from principal ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} -bundles sectors (in particular, projecting out sectors with monopole charges).

Introduction and summary

Physics is ultimately based on experimental measurements, which have a finite precision; in particular, all observable quantities and quantities computed from them are computable numbers.
On the other hand, the mathematical continuum
\mathbb{R}
mostly consists of uncomputable numbers.
Hence it is a common conceptual position that continuum entities should be limits of, or approximations to, some underlying discrete structures. In particular, field theories on continuum spacetime should be describable as continuum limits of theories discretised in some sense.

The perhaps most direct incarnation of this philosophy consists of discretising spacetime itself by working with lattice field theories

A more oblique approach to discretisation is in terms of dimensional deconstruction

Future directions.

The present analysis discretises an Abelian gauge group
\operatorname{U}(1)
.
A natural question is whether this may be extended to non-Abelian gauge groups. An immediate obstacle is the paucity of finite subgroups in the non-Abelian setting. For instance, for
G=\operatorname{SU}(2)
, finite subgroups admit an ADE classification
[
19
, Ch. 19]
; the two infinite families (the cyclic and binary dihedral groups, corresponding to the
\mathrm{A}_{n}
and
\mathrm{D}_{n}
series respectively) are not ‘dense’ inside
\operatorname{SU}(2)
in the same way that
\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{Z}_{k}
is dense inside
\operatorname{U}(1)
; one could instead take countably infinite subgroups of
\operatorname{SU}(2)
, but these are not truly discrete and inherit a nontrivial topology from
\operatorname{SU}(2)
.

Another direction of extension is to (Abelian)
p
-form electrodynamics, which appears more feasible. More ambitiously, one can try adjusted higher gauge theory
[
20
]
, which also admits a Čech description.

2
Discretisation of Maxwell theory in the Čech formulation

2.1
The failure of a naïve discretisation of Maxwell theory

A principal
G
-bundle for
G
a finite group admits exactly one connection, which is always flat and entirely determined by the bundle data itself
[
16
]
(see also
[
17
]
). It is therefore immediate that a naïve non-trivial discretisation of Maxwell theory must fail in the sense that the dynamical degrees of freedom are lost and cannot be recovered. To understand how one might circumvent this conclusion, it is instructive to begin with standard Maxwell theory and see how the anticipated failure follows upon naïve discretisation. We do this here; the escape hatch that suggests itself will then be used in
section

2.3
to construct
\mathcal{T}_{k}
.

Let spacetime
M
be an oriented manifold.
The kinematic data (i.e. the data over which one sums/integrates in the path integral) of Maxwell theory consists of a principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
and a connection
A
on
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
. If the bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
is nontrivial, then
A
is only locally defined. In this case one may work with the techniques of Čech cohomology. See, for example,
[
21
]
. Let
M
be covered by a sufficiently fine open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
; let each
U_{i}
be connected for simplicity. Then the principal bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
and the connection
A
may be described as

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U_{i}\cap U_{j},\operatorname{U}(1)),

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle\in\operatorname{\Omegaup}^{1}(U_{i}),

(3)

where
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,Y)
is the space of smooth functions
f\colon X\to Y
and
\Omegaup^{1}(X)
is the space of one-forms on
X
, such that

\displaystyle g_{ij}g_{ji}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle g_{ij}g_{jk}g_{ki}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}\cap U_{k}}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle A_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}

\displaystyle=A_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}+\frac{\mathrm{d}(\ln g_{ij})}{2\piup\mathrm{i}}.

(4)

The data
\{g_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}
form a Čech cocycle that describes a principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
on spacetime
M
, while
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
specifies a connection on
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
.

Now, if one were to replace all occurrences of
\operatorname{U}(1)
by
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, one would obtain the data

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U_{i},\mathbb{Z}_{k})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{k},

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle\in\operatorname{\Omegaup}^{1}(U_{i}),

(5)

such that

\displaystyle g_{ij}g_{ji}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle g_{ij}g_{jk}g_{ki}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle A_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}

\displaystyle=A_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}.

(6)

Since
g_{ij}
are now continuous functions with a discrete codomain
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
(and since the domain
U_{i}
is connected), they are
constant
functions and may simply be regarded as elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. Similarly, since constant functions have zero derivatives, the term
\mathrm{d}\ln g_{ij}
in (
4
) vanishes, so that the patchwise defined one-forms
A_{i}\in\Omegaup^{1}(U_{1})
glue together into a one-form
A\in\Omegaup^{1}(M)
.

This naïve approach suffers from the following three problems:

1.

Decoupling between
A
and the principal bundle.
In (
5
), the principal
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-bundle
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
defined by the cocycle
(g_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
bears no relation to the one-form
A\in\Omegaup^{1}(M)
. Thus, it is not clear how
a
k\to\infty
limit would relate
A
back to a
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle obtained as a limit of
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
.

2.

All
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-bundles are flattenable.
Another issue is whether a sequence of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-bundles
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
indeed approximates an arbitrary
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle. Taking the
k\to\infty
limit of (
5
) would yield a principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle described by transition maps
g_{ij}\in\operatorname{U}(1)
that are constant functions. However, a generic principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle is not describable in terms of transition maps that are constant; a principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle admits constant transition maps if and only if it is
flattenable
, that is, it admits at least one flat connection.
1
1

1

If spacetime
M
is trivial, so that there are no noncontractible loops around which we can have Wilson loops, then this implies that
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
is trivial.
For
M
not simply connected, this does not mean that
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
is a trivial bundle, although counterexamples are nontrivial to construct.
Abstractly, constant transition maps
(g_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
correspond to a principal bundle with fibre
\operatorname{U}(1)^{\deltaup}
where
(-)^{\deltaup}
means a group equipped with the
discrete topology
(rather than the ordinary topology of Lie groups), so that such a bundle is given by the homotopy class of a map
M\to\mathrm{B}\operatorname{U}(1)^{\deltaup}
, where
\mathrm{B}(-)
denotes the classifying space of a topological group. Thus a principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle given by the homotopy class of a map
M\to\operatorname{BU}(1)=\mathbb{CP}^{\infty}
is flattenable if and only if this map factors as
M\to\operatorname{BU}(1)^{\deltaup}\to\operatorname{BU}(1)
. However, the structure of the map
\mathrm{B}G^{\deltaup}\to\mathrm{B}G
for general
G
is very complicated
[
22
]
, relating to deep statements such as the Friedlander–Milnor conjecture.

Physically, this corresponds to requiring the absence of magnetic-monopole charges since a necessary condition for a principal bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
to be flat is that, for any connection
A
on
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
, the magnetic monopole flux
\int_{\Sigma}F_{A}
along any closed surface
\Sigma
vanish;
2
2

2

If
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
admits a flat connection
A^{\flat}
(hence with curvature
F_{A^{\flat}}=0
),
then around any closed surface
\Sigma\subset M
, the magnetic monopole charge
\int_{\Sigma}F_{A^{\flat}}
wrapped around that surface vanishes. By Chern–Weil theory
[
21
]
, even if one choose a different, non-flat connection
A
, one always has

\int_{\Sigma}F_{A}=\int_{\Sigma}F_{A^{\flat}}=0
.

however, nontrivial Wilson loops
\exp(\mathrm{i}\int_{\gamma}A)
around closed loops
\gamma
can still be present.

3.

All principal
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-bundles enjoy a canonical flat connection.

Finally, a third point of discontinuity is the fact that, while flattenable principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundles do not have a canonical flat connection (at least if spacetime
M
is not simply connected),
any principal
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-bundle enjoys a canonical flat connection.
3
3

3

This is trivially true in the sense that the Lie algebra of the discrete group
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
is the zero-dimensional Lie algebra
0
.

Consequently, while associated line bundles of a flattenable principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)^{\deltaup}}
do not have canonical connections, all associated line bundles of a principal
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-bundle
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
always have canonical flat connections. In physics terms, this means that certain putative terms (that use this flat connection) in the action that should not be gauge-invariant in the
k\to\infty
limit are nevertheless gauge-invariant at finite
k
.

We will resolve these three problems as follows:

1.

We decompose the connection
A
into a flat part
A^{\flat}
and a one-form part
A^{\sharp}
and discretise them separately. Then
A^{\flat}
retains a link to the principal bundle while
A^{\sharp}
is connected to
A^{\flat}
via an additional gauge transformation.

2.

We restrict to the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory described by a flattenable principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)^{\deltaup}}
.

3.

In the discretised theory
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, we restrict matter couplings to those that do not depend on the canonical flat connections on nontrivial associated bundles. (We call such couplings
admissible
.)

2.2
Monopoleless subsector of Maxwell theory

The previous discussion suggests that, in order to discretise the gauge group
\operatorname{U}(1)
, one should restrict to the
monopoleless
subsector of Maxwell theory, where in the path integral one sums over only flattenable principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundles. In fact, the monopoleless condition is in fact natural from the perspective of discrete-valued gauge theories. It is well known
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
(cf. the reviews
[

can Higgs a

-gauge theory down to a

-valued gauge theory. Globally, this mechanism can be used to realise a

-valued gauge theory only when the principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle is flattenable, i.e. without monopole charges since the Higgs mechanism requires a nowhere-vanishing ‘vacuum expectation value’
4
4

4

Strictly speaking, we should not speak of the vacuum expectation value of a non-gauge-invariant object such as
\Phi

|\Phi|

[
28

; this is intended as a shorthand for the discussion in
appendix

of (for instance) a charged scalar field

with charge

lives, and this is only possible if

\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle

be flattenable. (For a detailed discussion of this point, see

In the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory, any connection (i.e. gauge field)

\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle

can be (non-uniquely) put in the form

A=A^{\flat}+A^{\sharp},

5

5

This split into a locally defined part and a one-form part also appears in e.g. the computation of partition functions in the discussion of duality anomalies

.
Of course, since this split is not canonical, one should introduce an additional gauge symmetry

\displaystyle A^{\flat}

\displaystyle\mapsto A^{\flat}+\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\alpha}{2\piup\mathrm{i}},

\displaystyle A^{\sharp}

\displaystyle\mapsto A^{\sharp}-\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\alpha}{2\piup\mathrm{i}},

where

Furthermore, the flat connection

may be represented (non-uniquely) as

A^{\flat}=\frac{\mathrm{d}(\ln a)}{2\piup\mathrm{i}},

where

is a section of an associated complex line bundle

with respect to the canonical action of

\mathbb{C}

6
6

6

Equivalently,

.

Of course, we still have

-valued gauge transformations for

, which we can choose to attribute entirely to

,

A^{\flat}\mapsto A^{\flat}+\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln c}{2\piup\mathrm{i}},\qquad A^{\sharp}\mapsto A^{\sharp}\,,

(10)

where

is a globally defined

-valued function.

Thus, the kinematic data describing the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory are a flattenable
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
together with a one-form
A^{\sharp}\in\Omegaup^{1}(M)
and a section

of the associated complex line bundle

with

\displaystyle A^{\sharp}

\displaystyle\mapsto A^{\sharp}-\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\alpha}{2\piup\mathrm{i}},

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto c\alpha a\,.

Let us examine how to couple the kinematic data

Since

was a gauge parameter introduced to compensate for the non-uniqueness of the split

, we insist that matter should not transform under
\alpha
but only under

. If a complex scalar field
\phi
transforms as

\phi\mapsto c^{q}\phi

(12)

with charge

, then

is a section of the associated complex line bundle

. This does

are meaningless unless

Instead, one could take the combination

However, this does not couple to

D^{(q)}\phi=a^{q}\mathrm{d}(a^{-q}\phi)-2\piup\mathrm{i}qA^{\sharp}\phi=(\mathrm{d}-2\piup\mathrm{i}qA)\phi

as expected.

2.3
Discretised Maxwell theory

We may now directly discretise the data
(P_{\operatorname{U}(1)},a,A^{\sharp})
by replacing
\operatorname{U}(1)
with
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
. After this replacement, the discretised data are

•

a
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-principal bundle
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
,

•

a one-form
A^{\sharp}\in\Omegaup^{1}(M)
,

•

a section
a\in\Gammaup(P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\times_{1}\mathbb{C})
of the associated complex line bundle such that
|a|=1
, where
\times_{\bar{q}}
for some
q\in\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
indicates the associated complex line bundle with respect to the unitary representation
7
7

7

Since this action is unitary,

carries a canonical hermitian metric, such that the expression

makes sense.

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{k}

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{GL}(1;\mathbb{C}),

(15)

\displaystyle\exp(2\piup\mathrm{i}n/k)

\displaystyle\mapsto\exp(2\piup\mathrm{i}n\bar{q}/k)

gauge-transforming as

\displaystyle A^{\sharp}

\displaystyle\mapsto A^{\sharp}-\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\alpha}{2\piup\mathrm{i}},

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto c\alpha a,

where the gauge parameters are
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\operatorname{U}(1))
and

.
These data define the kinematic data of the
discretised Maxwell theory

\mathcal{T}_{k}
. Note that, while the gauge parameters
\alpha
and

are valued in
\operatorname{U}(1)
, the ‘true’ gauge symmetry group is the group of automorphisms of

\mathbb{Z}_{k}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\operatorname{U}(1))

While

is a section of a nontrivial complex line bundle, now

is a globally defined function that does not transform under

As for Maxwell theory, matter fields are associated bundles of
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
; the
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-representation then determines how matter should transform under
c
.
(As in

2.2

, not under
\alpha

For instance, a complex scalar field
\phi
with charge
\bar{q}
is a section of the associated complex line bundle
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\times_{\bar{q}}\mathbb{C}
.
It is then clear that
\bar{q}
is an integer modulo
k
– more abstractly,
\bar{q}\in\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
is an element of the Pontryagin dual group
\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
itself. (We however notationally distinguish between
\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{k}=\{\exp(2\piup\mathrm{i}\mathbb{Z}/k)\}
for clarity.)

Furthermore, in light of Problem
3

P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
are forbidden, even though they have canonical flat connections (at finite
k
). For brevity, let us say that a coupling is
inadmissible
if it involves using the canonical flat connection of a nontrivial associated bundle of
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
and
admissible
otherwise.
Concretely, this means that if
\phi\in\Gammaup(P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\times_{\bar{q}}\mathbb{C})
is a complex scalar field with charge
\bar{q}\in\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
,
an instance of an inadmissible coupling is

\mathrm{d}\phi\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\phi,

(18)

since we are taking the derivative of a section
\phi
of a nontrivial bundle. Instead, an example of an admissible coupling is

D^{(q)}\phi\wedge\star D^{(q)}\phi,

(19)

where

D^{(q)}\phi=a^{q}\mathrm{d}(a^{-q}\phi)-2\piup\mathrm{i}qA^{\sharp}\phi

(20)

14

The expression (
18
)
is inadmissible since it involves directly taking a derivative of
\phi\in\Gammaup(P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\times_{\bar{q}}\mathbb{C})

P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}\times_{\bar{q}}\mathbb{C}
.
Instead of writing

a^{-q}\phi
is a true scalar field,
then
take the derivative, then (optionally) multiply it back by

(21)

where

is an integer representative of
\bar{q}\in\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
.
This is, however, not gauge-invariant under transformations by
\alpha

).
What happens if we change the representative
q\in\mathbb{Z}
of
\bar{q}\in\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
? Suppose that

. Then

D^{(q)}\phi=a^{q^{\prime}+k}\mathrm{d}(a^{-q^{\prime}-k}\phi)-2\piup\mathrm{i}(q^{\prime}+k)A^{\sharp}\phi=D^{(q^{\prime})}\phi-2\piup\mathrm{i}kA\phi,

(22)

so that charges can be shifted by multiples of
k

kA
. (In taking a
k\to\infty

q

k

.)

An action (with, say, a coupling to a complex scalar field
\phi

S=\int\left(-\frac{1}{2g^{2}}F\wedge\star F\right)-D^{(q)}\phi\wedge\star(D^{(q)}\phi)^{*},

(23)

where
F=\mathrm{d}A=\mathrm{d}A^{\sharp}\in\Omegaup^{2}(M)

and
g
is a coupling constant. This looks superficially similar to ordinary Maxwell theory (indeed, it is perturbatively equivalent to it), but
A^{\sharp}
is no longer a connection on a principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
but instead a one-form. The kinematic data
(P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}},A^{\sharp},a)
together with the action (
23
) define the theory
\mathcal{T}_{k}
.

Then the claim is that
\mathcal{T}_{k}

with only admissible couplings to matter tends to Maxwell theory without monopoles in the
k\to\infty
limit when the charges
q
of the various fields are held fixed.

3
Consistency checks

We must check that the discretised Maxwell theory
\mathcal{T}_{k}
with only admissible couplings to matter in fact yields the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory in the limit
k\to\infty
. For this, we can test whether various features of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, such as the perturbative theory, its matter charges, and symmetries, approximate those of the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory.

3.1
Perturbative equivalence

One immediate check is whether the number of local degrees of freedom match between the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory and
\mathcal{T}_{k}
. Maxwell theory enjoys
d-2
local degrees of freedom in
d
spacetime dimensions transforming as a vector field as does
\mathcal{T}_{k}
(and unlike pure
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-valued gauge theory, which lacks any local degrees of freedom, being purely topological); in fact, the two theories are equivalent perturbatively, with the exactly the same set of Feynman diagrams, as follows directly from the action (
23
).

3.2
Matter charges

In Maxwell theory, electric charges correspond to the irreducible representations of
\operatorname{U}(1)
, which are all one-dimensional and in canonical bijection with
\mathbb{Z}
(the Pontryagin dual group of
\operatorname{U}(1)
, see e.g.
[
27
]
). Full Maxwell theory also has magnetic charges, but these are absent by definition in the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory.

In the discretised Maxwell theory
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, charges correspond to different associated bundles of
P_{\mathbb{Z}_{k}}
, which are labelled by elements
\bar{q}\in\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
.
For each
\bar{q}
, there are inequivalent ways of writing down a covariant derivative
D^{(q)}
labelled by a choice of a representative
\bar{q}=q+k\mathbb{Z}
. However, such terms differ by a noncanonical coupling proportional to
k
as seen in (
22
),
and there is no corresponding conservation law for
q\in\mathbb{Z}
(rather than
\bar{q}\in\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
) since the gauge symmetry is valued in
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
(the Pontryagin dual to
\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
), not in
\operatorname{U}(1)
(the Pontryagin dual to
\mathbb{Z}
).

However, as
k
tends to infinity, the set of charges
\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}
of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
approximates the set of charges
\mathbb{Z}
of Maxwell theory.

3.3
Wilson loops

The classification of charges similarly extends to the classification of Wilson loops.
For an ordinary gauge theory with a compact gauge group
G
, along any closed loop
\gamma
in spacetime
M
,
one can construct a Wilson loop for any (without loss of generality irreducible) finite-dimensional representation
\rho
of
G
, representing the holonomy of a particle transforming as
\rho
under the gauge symmetry; in general, the possible Wilson loops are in bijection with the possible charges.

Specialising to the case
G=\operatorname{U}(1)
, in Maxwell theory, along any closed loop
\gamma
in spacetime
M
, and for any integer
q\in\mathbb{Z}
labelling a (one-dimensional) complex irreducible representation of
\operatorname{U}(1)
, we can construct the Wilson loop

W_{\gamma}[A]=\exp\oint_{\gamma}2\piup\mathrm{i}qA

(24)

that is invariant under
\operatorname{U}(1)
gauge transformations.

In the case of the discretised Maxwell theory
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, similarly, one has Wilson loops

W_{\gamma}[A]=\exp\oint_{\gamma}q(2\piup\mathrm{i}A)=\left(\exp\oint_{\gamma}2\piup\mathrm{i}qA^{\sharp}\right)\left(\exp\oint_{\gamma}q\,\mathrm{d}\ln a\right)

(25)

for
q\in\mathbb{Z}
.
8
8

8

The latter factor does not vanish since
\mathrm{d}\ln a
is closed but not exact:
\ln a
is not globally defined.

Note that each of the two factors
\exp\oint_{\gamma}2\piup\mathrm{i}qA^{\sharp}
and
\exp\oint_{\gamma}q\,\mathrm{d}\ln a
are separately invariant under transformation by
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, but not under gauge transformation by
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\operatorname{U}(1))
.
Thus, gauge-invariant Wilson loops of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
are labelled by an integer

in agreement with those of Maxwell theory.

3.4
Higher-form symmetries

Maxwell theory has a one-form electric symmetry whose Noether current is
\star F
as well as a
(d-3)
-form magnetic symmetry whose Noether current is
F

[
25
]
.
If one restricts to the sector with no magnetic monopoles, however, the magnetic
(d-3)
-form symmetry becomes trivial since nothing is charged under it (equivalently, it does not survive in the cohomology
[
31
]
);
therefore, the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory only has a
\operatorname{U}(1)
-valued one-form symmetry. In addition, since
g_{ij}
can be now made to be constant, the cocycle
(g_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
defines a
\operatorname{U}(1)
-valued Čech one-cocycle corresponding to a
(d-2)
-form symmetry. Assuming

-cocycle corresponding to a
\operatorname{U}(1)
-valued one-form symmetry.

The discretised Maxwell theory
\mathcal{T}_{k}
has a one-form symmetry given by
\star\mathrm{d}A
; in addition, from the
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
gauge symmetry it has a
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-valued one-form symmetry and a
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-valued
(d-2)
-form symmetry, given by the Čech one-cocycle
(g_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
and its Poincaré dual, respectively
[
25
]
(as reviewed in e.g.
[
27
]
). Thus, in the limit
k\to\infty
where
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
approximates
\operatorname{U}(1)
, the sets of symmetries of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
agree with those of the monopoleless sector of Maxwell theory.

We show that

Consider the topological nonlocal operator

Appendix A
Monopolelessness from the Higgs mechanism

Let us justify the claim made in
section

2.2
that the monopoleless condition appears naturally in Higgsing a
\operatorname{U}(1)
-gauge theory down to a
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-valued gauge theory using matter with charge
k
.

Consider the Lagrangian of a complex scalar field
\Phi
of charge
k
coupled to Maxwell theory and subject to a Mexican-hat potential
V(|\Phi|)
on a compact, oriented, and connected (but not necessarily simply connected) spacetime
M
. If spacetime
M
is not simply connected, then one can put twisted boundary conditions for a complex scalar field on
M
; such boundary conditions are then classified by group homomorphisms

\chi\colon\piup_{1}(M)\to\operatorname{GL}(1;\mathbb{C}),

(29)

where
\piup_{1}(M)
is the fundamental group of
M
, specifying the compatible holonomies of
\Phi
across each non-contractible cocycle.
Then
\Phi
is a section of the complex bundle

L=L_{\chi}\otimes(P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}\times_{k}\mathbb{C}),

(30)

where
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
is the principal
\operatorname{U}(1)
-bundle of Maxwell theory, and
L_{\chi}
is the flat complex line bundle on
M
whose sections are uncharged scalar fields obeying the twisted boundary conditions given by
\chi
. Note that
L
admits a Hermitian metric (since the one-dimensional representation of
\operatorname{U}(1)
with charge
k
is unitary), so it makes sense to speak of
|\Phi|
, which is merely a function
|\Phi|\colon M\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
rather than the section of a nontrivial fibre bundle.

We then carry out the usual discussion of the Higgs mechanism. The Lagrangian density is

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2g^{2}}F\wedge\star F-(D\Phi)\wedge\star(D\Phi)-V(|\Phi|),

(31)

where

D=\mathrm{d}+2\piup\mathrm{i}kA

(32)

is the covariant derivative.
Supposing that
V(|\Phi|)
has a minimum at
|\Phi|=v
(and at no other value of
|\Phi|
), we may uniquely decompose (as long as
|\Phi(x)|
vanishes nowhere)

\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v+\phi(x))\exp(\mathrm{i}\theta(x)),

(33)

where
\phi(x)
is a real scalar field, and
\theta
is a section of the circle bundle associated to
L
. Define

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=A+\mathrm{d}\theta,

\displaystyle G=\mathrm{d}B.

(34)

In this parameterisation, the Lagrangian density becomes

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2g^{2}}G\wedge\star G-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\phi\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\phi-V(\phi+v)-\frac{1}{2}(v+\phi)^{2}(\mathrm{d}\theta+kA)\wedge\star(\mathrm{d}\theta+kA).

(35)

Now,
\phi
takes the form of a massive scalar with squared mass given by the second derivative of
V
.
If we can gauge-fix
\theta
to some specific value, then this would be equivalent to the Proca action for a massive vector field coupled to the massive scalar
\phi
. This requires that
\theta
be globally well defined, i.e. that the circle bundle associated to
L
have a global section.
This is only possible if
L
is topologically the trivial complex line bundle.
This, in turn, is only possible if
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
is flattenable. (In that case, one can take
L_{\chi}=P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}\times_{-k}\mathbb{C}

in (
30

P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}\times_{-k}\mathbb{C}
admits a flat connection.) Otherwise,
\theta
only admits local sections, and the global physics differs from a Proca field even though the local physics is the same.

Since the local physics agrees with that of a Proca field, the spectrum is gapped, and in the deep infrared one has at most a topological theory. The argument of
[
24
]
requires passing to the deep infrared and keeping only the term
A\wedge\star\mathrm{d}\theta
; if
\theta
can be dualised into a
(d-2)
-form, this would yield the action for a
BF
formulation of
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
gauge theory. For this to work,
\theta
needs to take values in the trivial circle bundle, i.e.
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
must be flattenable. Equivalently, the equation of motion for
\theta
then requires
\mathrm{d}A=0
, so that
A
defines a flat connection for
P_{\operatorname{U}(1)}
, which must be therefore flattenable.

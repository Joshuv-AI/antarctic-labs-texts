Alleviating the H 0 H_{0} tension through new interacting dark energy model in light of DESI DR2
Yi-Min Zhang, Tian-Nuo Li, Guo-Hong Du, Sheng-Han Zhou, Li-Yang Gao, Jing-Fei Zhang, Xin Zhang

Abstract
The H 0 H_{0} tension has become one of the most significant challenges in modern cosmology. The recent DESI DR2 data has shown a significant preference for dynamical dark energy, yet this has further exacerbated the H 0 H_{0} tension. In this work, we explore the potential of new interacting dark energy models ( Λ ~ \widetilde{\Lambda} CDM and e ​ Λ ~ e\widetilde{\Lambda} CDM) to alleviate the H 0 H_{0} tension. We perform observational constraints using the latest baryon acoustic oscillation data from DESI DR2, cosmic microwave background (CMB) data from Planck and Atacama Cosmology Telescop, and type Ia supernova data from DESY5 and PantheonPlus, as well as the SH0ES data. From our analysis, we observe the dynamical scale parameter of the cosmological constant, δ Λ = − 0.410 − 0.120 + 0.140 \delta_{\Lambda}=-0.410^{+0.140}_{-0.120} , in the e ​ Λ ~ e\widetilde{\Lambda} CDM model using the CMB+DESI+SH0ES data, which deviates from Λ \Lambda CDM at the 3.2 ​ σ 3.2\sigma level. Due to the anti-correlation between δ Λ \delta_{\Lambda} and H 0 H_{0} , a negative δ Λ \delta_{\Lambda} results in a higher inferred H 0 H_{0} . Consequently, we obtain H 0 = 71.90 ± 1.00 ​ km ​ s − 1 ​ Mpc − 1 H_{0}=71.90\pm 1.00~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}} , reducing the H 0 H_{0} tension to 0.8 ​ σ 0.8\sigma . Even without SH0ES, the CMB+DESI data alone still alleviate the H 0 H_{0} tension to 1.7 ​ σ 1.7\sigma . Overall, the e ​ Λ ~ e\widetilde{\Lambda} CDM model not only deviates from the Λ \Lambda CDM model but also demonstrates a significant capability to alleviate the H 0 H_{0} tension.

II.1
Models

In this study, we consider some cosmological models based on the assumptions of general relativity, spatial flatness, and a homogeneous and isotropic universe described by the Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW) metric. Within a flat FRW framework, the Hubble parameter
H(z)
can be expressed as

H^{2}(z)=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\left(\rho_{\mathrm{m}}+\rho_{\mathrm{r}}+\rho_{\Lambda}\right),

(1)

where
G
is Newton’s gravitational constant, and
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
,
\rho_{\mathrm{r}}
, and
\rho_{\Lambda}
represent the energy densities of matter, radiation, and dark energy, respectively. The redshift evolution of these components is described by

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(z)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathrm{m}0}(1+z)^{3},

(2)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{r}}(z)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathrm{r}0}(1+z)^{4},

(3)

The vacuum energy density is expressed as

\rho_{\Lambda}=\rho_{\Lambda 0}\equiv\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{8\pi G_{0}}.

(4)

Here, the subscript “0” denotes the value at
z=0
, and
G_{0}
,
\Lambda_{0}
,
\rho_{\mathrm{m}0}
,
\rho_{\mathrm{r0}}
, and
\rho_{\Lambda 0}
represent the current values of Newton’s constant, the cosmological constant, and the energy densities of matter, radiation, and dark energy, respectively.

The asymptotically safe quantum gravity theory of Ref.
Weinberg 2010
postulates that there may exist a non-trivial ultraviolet fixed point in the gravitational field, which ensures the renormalizability of the theory at high energies. Related studies (see Refs.
Xue 2015
;
Xue 2020
;
Gao et al. 2021
) have shown that, within this framework, the effective gravitational action contains the Ricci scalar terms of
R
and the cosmological constant
\Lambda
, which can be respectively associated with the effective running coupling parameters
G(z)
and
\Lambda(z)
that evolve with redshift. As the redshift approaches zero, these scale-dependent quantities asymptotically approach their current values
(G_{0},\Lambda_{0})
. This leads to the introduction of redshift-dependent scaling laws

\displaystyle\frac{G(z)}{G_{0}}=(1+z)^{-\delta_{\mathrm{G}}},

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda(z)}{\Lambda_{0}}=(1+z)^{\delta_{\mathrm{\Lambda}}},

(6)

where
\delta_{\mathrm{G}}\ll 1
and
\delta_{\mathrm{\Lambda}}\ll 1
are small critical indices that characterize the evolution of
G
and
\Lambda
with redshift.

Under this assumption, the matter and radiation energy densities are modified as follows

\displaystyle\left(\frac{G(z)}{G_{0}}\right)\rho_{\mathrm{m}}(z)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathrm{m0}}\,(1+z)^{3-\delta_{\mathrm{G}}},

(7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{G(z)}{G_{0}}\right)\rho_{\mathrm{r}}(z)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathrm{r0}}\,(1+z)^{4-\delta_{\mathrm{G}}},

(8)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{G(z)}{G_{0}}\right)\rho_{\Lambda}(z)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\Lambda 0}\,(1+z)^{\delta_{\Lambda}}.

(9)

It is assumed that the parameter
\delta_{\mathrm{G}}
is the same for both matter and radiation components, and the deviation arises solely due to the evolution of
G(z)
.

Based on these assumptions, the Friedmann equation is modified to the form

\displaystyle E^{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}(1+z)^{3-\delta_{\mathrm{G}}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}(1+z)^{4-\delta_{\mathrm{G}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Omega_{\Lambda 0}(1+z)^{\delta_{\Lambda}},

(10)

\displaystyle(1+z)\frac{\mathrm{d}E^{2}(z)}{\mathrm{d}z}

\displaystyle=3\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}(1+z)^{3-\delta_{\mathrm{G}}}+4\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}(1+z)^{4-\delta_{\mathrm{G}}},

(11)

where
E(z)\equiv H(z)/H_{0}
,
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}
,
\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}
, and
\Omega_{\Lambda 0}
represent the energy density fractions of matter, radiation, and dark energy, and the normalized energy condition
\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{\Lambda 0}}=1
still holds. Equation (
11
) arises from the generalized conservation law of energy in a universe with redshift-dependent
G(z)
and
\Lambda(z)
, which reduces to the standard form when
\delta_{\mathrm{G}}=\delta_{\mathrm{\Lambda}}=0
.

By substituting Eq. (
II.1
) into Eq. (
11
), we derive a relationship between
\delta_{\mathrm{G}}
and
\delta_{\mathrm{\Lambda}}
in the low-redshift limit, given by

\delta_{\Lambda}\approx\delta_{\mathrm{G}}\left(\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{\Lambda 0}}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{r0}}}\right)\approx 0.47\,\delta_{\mathrm{G}}.

(12)

This implies that a non-zero value of
\delta_{\mathrm{G}}
or
\delta_{\mathrm{\Lambda}}
reflects a dynamical interaction or energy exchange between the matter and dark energy sectors, while still respecting total energy conservation. In the
\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM framework,
\delta_{\mathrm{G}}
and
\delta_{\Lambda}
are connected via Eq. (
12
), forming a single-parameter extension. The
e\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM model, however, considers
\delta_{\mathrm{G}}
and
\delta_{\Lambda}
as independent, resulting in a two-parameter extension.
For ease of comparison, we consider the

CDM model, which describes the evolution of dark energy with a time-varying equation of state parameterized by two parameters

;
Koyama 2016
;
Andriot 2025
,
w_{0}
and
w_{a}
, as

w(z)=w_{0}+w_{a}\frac{z}{1+z}.

(13)

The corresponding Hubble parameter is given by

\begin{split}H(z)=H_{0}\bigg[&\Omega_{\mathrm{m0}}(1+z)^{3}+\\
&\Omega_{\mathrm{de}0}(1+z)^{3(1+w_{0}+w_{a})}\exp\left(-\frac{3w_{a}z}{1+z}\right)\bigg]^{1/2}.\end{split}

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we utilize the DESI DR2 BAO, CMB, SN, and SH0ES data to constrain the
\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM and
e\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM models. Our aim is to further investigate the ability of these models to ease the
H_{0}
tension in light of the recent DESI DR2 data.

We find that when only CMB+DESI data are employed, the
e\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM model yields
\delta_{\Lambda}=-0.200^{+0.180}_{-0.170}
, corresponding to a higher Hubble constant of
H_{0}=70.00\pm 1.50~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
, thereby reducing the
H_{0}
tension to
1.7\sigma
. This is due to the fact that
\delta_{\Lambda}
shows a strong anti-correlation with
H_{0}
, a more negative
\delta_{\Lambda}
leads to a higher inferred
H_{0}
value. Conversely, the
\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM model yields a small deviation of
\delta_{\Lambda}
from zero, indicating a limited ability to alleviate the
H_{0}
tension. When using the CMB+DESI+SH0ES data, the capability of the
e\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM model to mitigate the
H_{0}
tension improves significantly, yielding
H_{0}=71.90\pm 1.00~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
, which corresponds to a reduction of the tension to
0.8\sigma
. This result is accompanied by a more negative value of
\delta_{\Lambda}=-0.410^{+0.140}_{-0.120}
, deviating from zero at the
3.2\sigma
level. Furthermore, although DESI data have led to a clear preference for the
w_{0}w_{a}
CDM model, this preference actually exacerbates the
H_{0}
tension relative to the
\Lambda
CDM model; even when using the combined CMB+DESI data, the tension increases to
4.2\sigma
. In contrast, the
e\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM model exhibits a remarkable ability to reduce the
H_{0}
tension with different datasets, with the optimal case achieving a reduction to
0.8\sigma
, highlighting its potential as a compelling alternative cosmological scenario beyond the standard
\Lambda
CDM framework. Meanwhile, in terms of fitting DESI data, the
e\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM model shows clear advantages over the
\Lambda
CDM model.

In conclusion, all these findings indicate that the
e\widetilde{\Lambda}
CDM model, as an interacting dark energy scenario, not only provides
3\sigma
-level evidence for a deviation from
\Lambda
CDM but also demonstrates a strong capability to alleviate the
H_{0}
tension. In the coming years, the combination of DESI BAO data with CMB and SN datasets will enhance our understanding of the nature of dark energy, further alleviate the
H_{0}
tension, and support the existence of interactions.

Patient perceptions of remotely-delivered exercise services for osteoarthritis: A cross-sectional study in Hong Kong
Authors: Union HY Hsieh, Mengqi Li, Lok Chun Chan, Yin Henry Fong, Kam-Wai Chen, Ping Keung Chan, Yan Li, Kim Bennell, Chunyi Wen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveExercise is fundamental to osteoarthritis (OA) management, but access to in-person programs is often limited. Remotely-delivered exercise services (RDES) present a viable alternative; however, their acceptability among patients in Hong Kong (HK) is not well established. This study sought to investigate patient perceptions, compare them with existing Australian data, and inform the development of culturally tailored OA care in urban Asian settings.DesignA cross-sectional study was conducted using online surveys completed by 243 OA patients in HK. Participants rated their perceptions on 5-point Likert scales. Descriptive analysis was conducted and results were visualized using radar charts, and findings were compared with one published Australian study.ResultsAlthough 70% of HK patients expressed interest in RDES, valuing time-saving (79%) and convenience (67%), significant concerns persisted. Only 34% found RDES affordable, and merely 40% believed it offered satisfaction equivalent to in-person care. Furthermore, HK patients reported lower agreement than Australians on satisfaction (40% vs 68%) and ease of use (51% vs 78%).ConclusionHong Kong OA patients recognize the potential benefits of RDES, yet key concerns regarding affordability, satisfaction, and effectiveness remain. The discrepancies with both local clinicians and international benchmarks highlight a critical implementation gap. These findings emphasize the necessity for co-designed, culturally adapted interventions to successfully integrate RDES into OA care in Hong Kong.Keywords: Osteoarthritis, Telemedicine, Patient and public involvement and engagement, Health service, Digital health

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a leading cause of musculoskeletal pain, disability, and reduced quality of life, particularly among older adults [1]. Exercise is a cornerstone of OA management, with robust evidence supporting its role in alleviating symptoms and improving function [2,3]. However, many patients encounter barriers to participating in traditional in-person exercise programs, including pain, limited mobility, transportation challenges, and time constraints [4].

Advances in digital health have enabled remotely-delivered exercise services (RDES) via internet video, offering a convenient and potentially cost-effective alternative to face-to-face interventions, with comparable clinical effectiveness [[5], [6], [7], [8]]. The COVID-19 pandemic has further accelerated the adoption of telemedicine in Hong Kong [9,10], yet the perspectives of patients regarding RDES for OA remain underexplored in this context. Investigating these perspectives is a critical step in aligning digital health serivce with real-world patient needs, which may ultimately influence service adoption and adherence in clinical practice [[11], [12], [13], [14], [15]].

While there exist studies of perceptions toward broader tele-exercise or tele-rehabilitation services [16,17], research on the perceptions of RDES for OA specifically is limited. Previous research in Australia establishes a benchmark, showing high acceptance of RDES among OA patients [18], with physiotherapists largely recognising its potential [19]. The context in Hong Kong (HK), however, is distinct. A local study indicated that older adults in HK often hesitate to adopt new technologies, their acceptance being strongly shaped by factors like family support and perceived usefulness rather than clinical need alone [20]. This, coupled with these unique cultural and technological adoption patterns, is likely to shape local perceptions differently.

We therefore hypothesize that the perceptions regarding RDES among patients with OA in HK will differ from the Australian findings. This study aims to investigate the perceptions of OA patients regarding RDES within the HK context. Ultimately, this research seeks to provide preliminary data to inform the development of accessible and culturally tailored OA care in urban Asian settings.

Methods

Study design and setting

This study employed a cross-sectional survey design to investigate patient perceptions of RDES via internet video for OA management in HK. Data were collected using online, self-administered questionnaires from local communities between 25 September and November 30, 2024, primarily during a health education event, Hong Kong Arthritis Day 2024. Additionally, data collected in HK were compared with previously published data from Australia. The Australian data were gathered between August 2015 and August 2016 through cross-sectional surveys examining the perceptions and willingness of patients (n = 330) to adopt RDES for OA management. The methods and results of the Australian study are detailed in prior publications [18].

Participants

Participants in HK were recruited used convenience sampling. Patients were included if they met the following criteria: 1) adults residing in HK; 2) self-reported diagnosis of OA or currently suffering from knee/hip joint pain; and 3) ability to read and understand Chinese.

Based on an estimated population of over 500,000 individuals with osteoarthritis in HK, the final sample of 243 patients yields a margin of error of approximately 6.28% at a 95% confidence level. While this margin is slightly above the conventional 5% threshold, it is considered robust and sufficient for an exploratory cross-sectional study aimed at identifying prevailing trends in patient perceptions.

Survey instrument

A structured questionnaire was adapted from existing literature to collect data from patients [18,19] and translated into Chinese by a bilingual researcher. To ensure content validity and cultural relevance to the HK context, the draft was reviewed by an OA research expert and pilot-tested with six patients for clarity. The patient questionnaire collected data on demographics (age, gender, education level, and area of residence), technology familiarity (gadget and internet usage, confidence in using mobile phones, computers, internet, and video calls), and OA experience (hip/knee condition, prior physiotherapy, and telehealth experience). Patients also rated their agreement on two statements about the benefits of exercise for OA and the effectiveness of a physiotherapy-prescribed program, as well as 17 statements concerning different aspects of RDES via internet video (see Table 2), using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree."

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 30.0). Descriptive statistics summarized participant demographics and survey responses. Frequencies and percentages were calculated for categorical variables, while means and standard deviations were reported for continuous variables. For data analysis, responses of “agree” and “strongly agree” were counted as “agreement,” and consensus was considered reached if agreement equalled or exceeded 75% [21]. Radar charts were used to visually present the distribution of agreement levels across multiple survey items. The results of Chi-square tests, which compared agreement levels between patients in HK and Australia, were integrated into these charts.

Results

Characteristics of patients with osteoarthritis

The study included 243 participants, with 71% female (Table 1). Most participants were aged 55–74 (87%), with the 65–74 age group being the largest (49%). Geographically, 50% resided in the New Territories (excluding Island District). Osteoarthritis most commonly affected the knee (82%). Nearly half of the participants (45%) had no prior physical therapy experience, while 26% had received physical therapy exercise specifically for OA. The majority (91%) had no prior telehealth service experience.

Technology familiarity of the patients

The results highlight widespread familiarity with technology by respondents, particularly mobile phones, which are nearly universally owned (n = 235; 97%) and used daily, with high confidence levels (n = 217; 89% moderately to extremely confident). Daily internet use is common (n = 195; 80%), with most respondents (n = 160; 66%) having over 10 years' experience and rating their skills as fair to excellent (n = 218; 90%). Video chat is frequent (n = 162; 67% weekly use or more) and 66% users (n = 160) feel moderately to extremely confident in using video communication.

Patients’ perceptions of remotely-delivered exercise services via internet video

As shown in Table 2, over 80% (n = 197) of respondents agreed that exercise is beneficial for OA, and 77% (n = 171) believed that a physiotherapist-prescribed exercise program would improve their condition. Interest in RDES via internet video was high, with 70% (n = 162) expressing interest in using such a service, primarily due to its time-saving potential (n = 185; 79%), saving money (n = 159; 69%), and convenience (n = 155; 67%) (see Fig. 1).

Despite this interest, patients’ perceptions of RDES revealed some ambivalence. Financial concerns were evident, with just 34% (n = 77) willing to pay for such services and only 34% (n = 79) considering them affordable. Furthermore, just 40% (n = 93) considered RDES as satisfying as in-person interaction and only 42% (n = 98) endorsed their effectiveness.

Comparison of agreement between OA patients in HK and Australia

Australian OA patients reported significantly higher agreement than HK OA patients in several domains (Fig. 2), including healthcare professionals getting a good understanding of patient's OA (71% vs. 54%, p < 0.001), privacy (82% vs. 58%, p < 0.001), ease of use (78% vs. 51%, p < 0.001), satisfaction (68% vs. 40%, p < 0.001), monitoring (57% vs. 44%, p = 0.003), convenience (79% vs. 67%, p = 0.001), practicality (76% vs. 53%, p < 0.001), effectiveness (64% vs. 42%, p < 0.001), affordability (56% vs. 34%, p < 0.001), and safety (65% vs. 50%, p < 0.001). Australian patients were also more willing to pay for telehealth services (46% vs. 34%, p = 0.005).

No significant differences were observed for perceived improvement (57% vs. 55%, p = 0.685), time-saving (81% vs. 79%, p = 0.65), interest (72% vs. 70%, p = 0.556), or acceptability (72% vs. 65%, p = 0.115). In contrast, Hong Kong OA patients reported higher agreement than Australian OA patients regarding perceived cost savings (69% vs. 60%, p = 0.024) and preference for its contactless nature (32% vs. 22%, p = 0.01), although both groups had relatively low agreement in the contactless domain.

Discussion

This study provides important insights into the perceptions of patients in HK regarding remotely-delivered exercise services for osteoarthritis management. While patients expressed strong interest in RDES, mainly valuing its time-saving and convenient benefits, concerns remain about satisfaction, effectiveness, affordability, and adequate monitoring compared to traditional in-person care. These findings highlight a critical gap between patient enthusiasm for digital solutions and their perceptions of the quality and value of remote services.

The low telehealth utilization among our participants (<10%) highlights a service-to-experience gap in HK's rehabilitation landscape. While institutional readiness is high—as evidenced by Ku et al. [22], who reported that over 50.6% of therapists (1112/2196) in the public sector had actively prescribed tele-rehabilitation for conditions ranging from stroke to musculoskeletal pain—this momentum has not yet translated into broad patient-side adoption.

This service gap indicates that limited patient exposure, despite the availability of telehealth services, —reflecting insufficient cues to action in the Health Belief Model—may be key barriers to adoption [23]. While this lack of direct exposure may limit their ability to provide an experience-based evaluation of RDES, it offers a unique window into the initial psychological barriers and preconceived expectations of potential users. In contrast, a national Australian survey during 2020–2021 reported that 69.3% of respondents who had a health care visit in the prior 12 months used telehealth [24]. This contrast suggests that stronger system-level cues (for example, broad reimbursement and routine telehealth offerings) can elevate exposure and normalize use, whereas in our context, the persistence of low patient experience—even post-pandemic—underscores ongoing challenges in integrating digital health into routine musculoskeletal care.

Consistent with prior research, patients in HK recognized the benefits of exercise for OA management [18], reflecting the success of health education and clinical messaging [25]. However, their reservations about RDES—particularly regarding affordability, satisfaction, and effectiveness—reflect concerns highlighted in a systematic review spanning North and South America, Europe, and Asia, where technical limitations and heterogeneity in tele-rehabilitation approaches may impact the reliability of evidence supporting its effectiveness. [7]. Therefore, it is important to actively involve patients in the design and delivery of RDES to ensure their needs and concerns are fully addressed [13,26]. For example, adopting hybrid care models or offering trial sessions may also help patients build trust in remote services while preserving elements of personal interaction [27]. This study highlights the value of co-creating healthcare solutions with patients to bridge expectation gaps, foster trust, and enhance the digital health experience in innovative models of care delivery [28].

Comparative analysis with Australian data indicates that HK patients are less receptive to RDES, reporting lower levels of satisfaction, ease of use, and privacy. These differences may stem from Australia's longer history of telehealth integration and the practical necessity of remote care in rural and remote regions [27], in contrast to HK's cultural preference for in-person consultations and hands-on physiotherapy. Local evidence further suggests that HK older adults tend to adopt familiar devices only and show low uptake of newer telecare technologies, with acceptance strongly shaped by family support, economic status, and perceived government incentives [20]. Nevertheless, experiences during the COVID-19 pandemic demonstrated the potential of telehealth to address healthcare needs in Hong Kong [29], underscoring the importance of tailored strategies—such as simplified platforms, patient education, family engagement, financial support, and the use of wearable sensors for safe and adeqaute monitoring—to improve satisfaction and usability of RDES among OA patients.

In summary, while the infrastructure for digital health exists in HK, patient uptake of RDES remains constrained by concerns over affordability and service satisfaction. Addressing these barriers is not about replacing traditional in-person care, but rather positioning RDES as a high-value, complementary component of a comprehensive osteoarthritis management plan. By enhancing digital access and addressing financial hurdles, Hong Kong can foster a sustainable hybrid model where remote services extend the reach of physiotherapy, ensuring that exercise-based interventions are both flexible and accessible without compromising the essential human connection of clinical practice.

Limitations

Firstly, patient recruitment was primarily conducted during a health education event (HK Arthritis Day 2024), which likely introduced substantial selection bias. The sample may represent a highly selected population of individuals who are more health-conscious, motivated, and actively engaged with healthcare services compared to the broader OA population in HK. The recruitment strategy limits our ability to include participants from diverse geographical settings and restricts the generalizability of the findings. However, studying this motivated group provides an important ‘best-case’ insight into the perceptions of those most likely to be early adopters of digital health interventions.

Secondly, a major limitation is that the vast majority of patient participants (91%) had no prior experience with telehealth services. Consequently, their responses reflect anticipatory perceptions and hypothetical concerns rather than informed feedback based on actual service utilization. This lack of experience likely contributes to the observed skepticism regarding the effectiveness and satisfaction of RDES, as a previous study found that perceptions could change positively after experiencing telerehabilitation [30].

Future research should address these limitations by including larger and more diverse samples, offering detailed information about remotely delivered exercise programs, synchronizing data collection across regions, and employing longitudinal studies with objective measures to evaluate telehealth adoption and outcomes.

Implications for practice

From our findings, we suggest some actionable steps to boost the adoption of RDES for OA. Actively involving patients in service design ensures that solutions are aligned with their genuine needs and concerns. Implementing culturally tailored education campaigns, such as HK Arthritis Day [31], integrating RDES into primary care and public hospitals, and utilizing accessible digital platforms can enhance patient engagement and build trust. Policymakers should explore subsidizing telehealth services and expanding insurance coverage to improve access, particularly for individuals dependent on public healthcare systems.

In conclusion, this study suggests that patient acceptance of remotetly-delivered exercise services in HK remains constrained by concerns about affordability, satisfaction, and effectiveness. Addressing these concerns through patient-centred design, tailored education, and supportive policy measures will be important to promote integration and sustainability of RDES for OA management. Further research using co-designed approaches and larger populations is warranted to develop targeted strategies that bridge patient–provider gaps and strengthen the role of digital health in musculoskeletal care in HK.

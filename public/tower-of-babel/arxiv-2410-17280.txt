Abstract
:
In this paper, two classes of quantum MDS codes are constructed. The main tools are multiplicative structures on finite fields. Carefully choosing different cosets can make the corresponding generalized Reed-Solomon codes Hermitian self-orthogonal, which yields quantum MDS codes by utilizing Hermitian type CSS construction. In some cases, these codes have minimal distances larger than
q/2
. Also, the parameters of these codes have never been reported before.

Key words
: MDS code, Hermitian self-orthogonal code, Generalized Reed-Solomon(GRS) code, Quantum code.

Introduction

Since the mid-20th century, quantum computing has gradually emerged as a pivotal field in communication, attracting widespread attention from researchers worldwide. Quantum information technology represents a paradigm shift in information science, offering solutions to problems that have remained intractable with conventional processing methods.

Quantum computers leverage the unique properties of quantum entanglement and superposition to process data, thereby significantly enhancing computational speed and information processing efficiency. For instance, numerous calculations that require exponential time on classical computers can be performed in polynomial time on quantum computers. Using Shor’s quantum algorithm on a functional quantum computer, the factorization of any 10,000-bit large integer can be accomplished in mere hours. In contrast, executing the same computation even on today’s fastest supercomputer would take billions of years.

Furthermore, the storage capacity of quantum processors far exceeds that of existing computers. A quantum computer with 1,000 quantum bits can theoretically store information about all the elementary particles in the universe. This extraordinary storage capacity, combined with the computational power of quantum algorithms, positions quantum computing as a potential game-changer in various fields, including cryptography, material science, and drug discovery.

The uncertainty principle, a fundamental concept in quantum mechanics, dictates that the transmission of quantum states can be disrupted by various means, including quantum channel noise and enemy interference. Furthermore, the inherent probabilistic nature of quantum mechanics introduces the possibility of quantum errors, which can occur without detection. If these errors cannot be corrected, quantum communication will be compromised, rendering information unintelligible. Therefore, to safeguard quantum information, a process of quantum error correction coding is essential. This method, which employs redundancy in information transmission, is the quantum counterpart to traditional channel coding techniques.

Hermitian consruction

In this section, we introduce some basic notations and useful results on generalized Reed-Solomon codes (GRS codes for short). Let
g
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\{0\}=\langle g\rangle
. For
\alpha=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),\beta=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, denote the Hermitian inner product of
\alpha
and
\beta
by
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{h}=a_{1}^{q}b_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}^{q}b_{n}
.

Let
\overrightarrow{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
and
\overrightarrow{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})
where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
are distinct and
v_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Then for
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
, we can define GRS code in the following:

{\rm GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})=\{(v_{1}f(\alpha_{1}),v_{2}f(\alpha_{2}),\cdots,v_{n}f(\alpha_{n}))\mid f(x)\in\mathbb{F}[x],\deg(f(x))<k\}.

The code
{\rm GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
is an MDS code with parameters
[n,k,n-k+1]_{q^{2}}
which has a generator matrix(for further information on GRS codes, see Chapter One in
[
38
]
)

\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}&v_{2}&\ldots&v_{n}\\
v_{1}\alpha_{1}&v_{2}\alpha_{2}&\ldots&v_{n}\alpha_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
v_{1}\alpha_{1}^{k-1}&v_{2}\alpha_{2}^{k-1}&\ldots&v_{n}\alpha_{n}^{k-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

The guiding construction scheme is due to Ashikhmin and Knill in
2001
(
[
37
]
).

Theorem 1
.

(Hermitian Construction)

If there exists an
[n,k,d]_{q^{2}}
linear code
C
with
C^{\perp H}\subseteq C
, where
C^{\perp H}
is Hermitian dual code of
C
, then there exist an
[[n,2k-n,\geqslant d]]_{q}
quantum code.

Notice that the Hermitian dual of an MDS code is also MDS.

Corollary 1
.

(Hermitian Construction for Quantum MDS Codes
[
4
]
)

If there exists an
[n,d,n-d+1]_{q^{2}}
MDS code
C
with
C^{\perp H}\subseteq C
, where
C^{\perp H}
is Hermitian dual code of
C
, then there exists an
[[n,n-2d,d+1]]_{q}
quantum MDS code.

Besides, the following result on checking
{\rm GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
Hermitian self-orthogonality will be useful.

Theorem 2
.

(
[
39
]
)
{\rm GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
is Hermitian self-orthogonal iff
\langle\overrightarrow{\alpha}^{qi+j},\overrightarrow{v}^{q+1}\rangle_{h}=0
for all
0\leqslant i,j\leqslant k-1
.

The case
q=sm+1
for odd

Here

Lemma 1
.

There does not exist
0\leqslant i,j\leqslant\frac{s+1}{2}m-1
such that
qj+j\equiv\frac{s+1}{2}m\pmod{n}
.

Proof.

Assume

qj+j\equiv\frac{s+1}{2}m\pmod{n}

for some
0\leqslant i,j\leqslant\frac{s+1}{2}m-1
. Taking module
sm+2
on both sides yields

j-i\equiv\frac{s+1}{2}m\pmod{sm+2}

which is a contradiction to
0\leqslant i,j\leqslant\frac{s+1}{2}m-1
.
∎

Let
H
be a multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
of order
n=\frac{q^{2}-1}{s}
, i.e,
H=\langle g^{s}\rangle
. Now denote by
H=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\}
with
\alpha_{k}=g^{sk}
and
\mu=\frac{s+1}{2}m
.

Theorem 3
.

There exists an Hermitian self-orthogonal GRS code with parameters
[n,k,n-k+1]_{q^{2}}
where
k\leqslant\frac{s+1}{2}m
. Moreover, there exists quantum MDS code with parameters
[[n,n-2k,k+1]]_{q}
.

Proof.

Denote by
\overrightarrow{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
and
\mu=\frac{s+1}{2}m
. By Theorem 2, the main step is to find
\overrightarrow{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})
satisfying
\langle\overrightarrow{\alpha}^{qi+j},\overrightarrow{v}^{q+1}\rangle_{h}=0
for any
0\leqslant i,j\leqslant k-1
. Then
\mathrm{GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
is Hermitian self-orthogonal.

Firstly
, note that
\alpha_{r}^{-\mu}=g^{-rs\mu}=g^{q^{2}-1-rs\mu}
. We claim that for any
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
,
{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})
and
{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(g\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})
can not be
0
at the same time. Otherwise

{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})={\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(g\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})=0

can imply

g^{rs\mu(q-1)}=g^{(rs\mu-1)(q-1)}=-1.

The
g^{q-1}=1
which contradicts that
g
is primitive in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Secondly
, we prove that there exists
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
such that
{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(t\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})\neq 0
for all
r
,
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
.

If for every
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
, we have
\alpha^{-\mu}_{r}\neq-1
. Then when
t=1
,
{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(t\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})\neq 0
for all
r
,
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
. Otherwise, we will demonstrate that when
t=g
,
{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(t\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})\neq 0
as follows: suppose that there exists
1\leqslant r_{0}\leqslant n
such that

\alpha^{-\mu}_{r}=g^{r_{0}s\mu(q-1)}=-1.

Therefore
\frac{q^{2}-1}{2}\mid r_{0}s\mu(q-1)
which is equivalent to

(q+1)(2l^{\prime}+1)=r_{0}s(s+1)m

(1)

for some integer
l^{\prime}
. If there exists
1\leqslant r_{1}\leqslant n
such that
{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(g\alpha_{r_{1}}^{-\mu})=0
, then

g\alpha_{r_{1}}^{-\mu}=g^{(r_{1}s\mu-1)(q-1)}=-1,

which is equivalent to

(q+1)(2l^{\prime\prime}+1)=r_{1}s(s+1)m-2

(2)

with some integer
l^{\prime\prime}
. By subtracting (2) from (1), we can get

2(q+1)(l^{\prime}-l^{\prime\prime})=2(r_{0}-r_{1})s(s+1)m+2.

For
q=sm+1
is odd,
sm
must be even. Consequently,
s(sm+m)
is also even. Therefore, the above equality does not hold, which leads to a contradiction. Therefore, for
1\leqslant r\leqslant n
we have
{\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(g\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})\neq 0
.

Denote by
\overrightarrow{v}=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{n})
such that
v_{r}^{q+1}={\rm Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(g\alpha_{r}^{-\mu})
. Then Lemma
1
implies
\langle\overrightarrow{\alpha}^{qi+j},\overrightarrow{v}^{q+1}\rangle_{h}=0
for any
0\leqslant i,j\leqslant k-1
. In this case, the code
\mathrm{GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
is Hermitian self-orthogonal with parameters
[n,k,n-k+1]_{q^{2}}
. Then applying Corollary
1
to the MDS code
\mathrm{GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
, we get quantum MDS codes with parameters
[[n,n-2k,k+1]]_{q}
.
∎

The case
n=q^{2}-s(q+1)

In this section, denote
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{q^{2}-q-1},\alpha_{q^{2}-q},\alpha_{q^{2}-q+1},\cdots,\alpha_{q^{2}}\}
, where the last
q+1
elements are in
S=\langle g^{q-1}\rangle
. Then for
1\leqslant i\leqslant q^{2}-q-1
we have

\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{q^{2}-q-1}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{q^{2}}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})}{\prod\limits_{j=q^{2}-q}^{q^{2}}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})}=\frac{-1}{\alpha_{i}^{q+1}-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Then according to Lemma 5 in
[
16
]
, when
qi+j\leqslant q^{2}-q-3
, there exists
v_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
v_{i}^{q+1}=\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{q^{2}-q-1}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{-1}
such that

\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q^{2}-q-1}\alpha_{s}^{qi+j}v_{s}^{q+1}=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q^{2}-q-1}\frac{\alpha_{s}^{qi+j}}{\prod\limits_{k=1,k\neq s}^{q^{2}-q-1}(\alpha_{s}-\alpha_{k})}=0.

Set
\overrightarrow{\alpha}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{q^{2}-q-1}\}
and
\overrightarrow{v}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{q^{2}-q-1}\}
. Then by Corollary
1
,
{\rm GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
is Hermitian self-orthogonal with
k\leqslant\frac{q^{2}-q-3}{q+1}+1
.

Lemma 2
.

There exist quantum MDS codes with parameters
[[q^{2}-q-1,q^{2}-q-1-2k,k+1]]_{q}
, where
k\leqslant\frac{q^{2}-q-3}{q+1}+1
.

Proof.

Apply Corollary
1
to
{\rm GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
with parameters
[q^{2}-q-1,k,q^{2}-q-k]_{q^{2}}.

∎

Theorem 4
.

If
q
is odd prime power, there exist quantum MDS codes with parameters
[[q^{2}-s(q+1),q^{2}-s(q+1)-2k,k+1]]_{q}
, where
k\leqslant\frac{q^{2}-s(q+1)-2}{q+1}+1,s\leqslant\frac{q-1}{2}
.

Proof.

Since
S
is the set of
(q+1)
-th unit roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
while
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is the set of
(q-1)
-th unit roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the intersection of
S
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is
\{\pm 1\}
. So the different cosets of
S
with representative in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are
S,g^{q+1}S,g^{2(q+1)}S,\cdots,g^{(\frac{q-1}{2}-1)(q+1)}S
. Decompose
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{\frac{q^{2}-1}{2}}\}\bigcup S\bigcup g^{q+1}S\bigcup\cdots\bigcup g^{(\frac{q-1}{2}-1)(q+1)}S
.

Therefore, for
s\leqslant\frac{q-1}{2},k\leqslant\frac{q^{2}-s(q+1)-2}{q+1}+1
, let

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{\alpha}=(

\displaystyle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{q^{2}-s(q+1)},g^{q-1},g^{2(q-1)},\cdots,g^{(q+1)(q-1)},g^{2q},g^{3q-1},\cdots,g^{q+1},\cdots,

\displaystyle g^{(\frac{q-1}{2}-1-s)(q+1)}g^{q-1},g^{(\frac{q-1}{2}-1-s)(q+1)}g^{2(q-1)},\cdots,g^{(\frac{q-1}{2}-1-s)(q+1)}g^{(q+1)(q-1)})

and
\overrightarrow{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{q^{2}-s(q+1)})
where

\displaystyle v_{i}^{q+1}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{q^{2}-s(q+1)}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{q+1}(\alpha_{i}-g^{j(q-1)})\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{q+1}(\alpha_{i}-g^{q+1}g^{j(q-1)})\cdots\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{q+1}(\alpha_{i}-g^{(\frac{q-1}{2}-1-s)(q+1)}g^{j(q-1)})

belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(Then
v_{i}
exists).

Then, for
i,j\leqslant k-1
we have

\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q^{2}-s(q+1)}\alpha_{s}^{qi+j}v_{s}^{q+1}=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q^{2}-s(q+1)}\frac{\alpha^{qi+j}_{s}}{\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{q^{2}-s(q+1)}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})}=0.

Now
{\rm GRS}_{k}(\overrightarrow{\alpha},\overrightarrow{v})
is an Hermitian self-orthogonal MDS code with parameters
[q^{2}-s(q+1),k,q^{2}-s(q+1)-k+1]_{q^{2}}
. So there exists quantum MDS code with parameters
[[q^{2}-s(q+1),q^{2}-s(q+1)-2k,k+1]]_{q}
.
∎

Theorem 5
.

If
q
is even, there exist quantum MDS codes with parameters
[[q^{2}-s(q+1),q^{2}-s(q+1)-2k,k+1]]_{q}
, where
k\leqslant q-1-s,s\leqslant q-1
.

Proof.

The proof is similar as Theorem
4
. Notice that in this case the different cosets of
S
with representative elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are precisely
S,g^{q+1}S,g^{2(q+1)}S,\cdots,g^{(q-2)(q+1)}S
.
∎

Example 3
.

We have quantum MDS codes with parameters
[[q^{2}-s(q+1),q^{2}-(s+2)(q-1),q-s]]_{q}
where
s\leqslant q-1
whose minimal distance is approximately
q
when
q
is large and
s
is small.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we construct two new classes of quantum MDS codes by using Hermitian self-orthogonal GRS codes from some multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
. Their minimum distances are large than
\frac{q}{2}
in some cases. Moreover, the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
has abundant additive group structure as well as multiplicative group structure. More quantum MDS codes with larger minimal distances may be constructed if we can properly combine those group structures together.

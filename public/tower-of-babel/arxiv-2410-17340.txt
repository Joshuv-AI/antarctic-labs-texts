Abstract
. Statements of main theorems

The main goal of is this paper is to study certain statistical questions arising from arithmetic geometry.
Let

For

Then, the number of

|X_{\lambda}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=p^{2}+\sum\limits_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\phi\left(xy(1+x+y)\left(xy+(1-\lambda)/\lambda^{2}\right)\right),

where
\phi

\mathbb{F}_{p}
(with the convention that
\phi(0)=0.
) In this paper, we study the distribution of the number of

when the surface is drawn at random from the family of surfaces

\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}

Now, for

let

A_{p}(\lambda):=p^{2}-|X_{\lambda}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|=-\sum\limits_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\phi\left(xy(1+x+y)\left(xy+(1-\lambda)/\lambda^{2}\right)\right).

Then, the problem of studying statistical distribution of the number of rational points of

A_{p}(\lambda)
as
\lambda
varies over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in the limit as
p
grows. Investigation of such questions for curves and surfaces in various settings are central problems in arithmetic geometry. For instance, a classical theorem of Birch
[
4
]
established the semicircular distribution of Frobenius traces elliptic curves over finite fields in vertical setting. In a difficult setting (called as horizontal setting, probably the most difficult setting), Clozel, Harris, Shepherd-Barron and Taylor
[
8
]
have considered a fixed elliptic curve under some conditions and settled semicircular distribution for Frobenius traces of the fixed elliptic curve taken over all primes
p
. This was a conjecture of Sato and Tate formulated independently around 1960’s. Along similar directions for abelian varieties analogous results have been discovered by Fité, Kedlaya, and Sutherland (for more details see
[
9
]
). In our purpose, we formulate the problem in Birch’s setting. Recently, in the similar formulation, Ono, Saad and the second author

In view of these consequences, it is natural to seek further examples of surfaces over finite fields and investigate similar questions. In this paper, our goal is to consider the surface
X_{\lambda}
over finite fields and conclude a new distribution for the character sum

that is associated to the number of points of

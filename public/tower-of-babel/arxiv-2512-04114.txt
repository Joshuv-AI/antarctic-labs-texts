The Lefschetz standard conjectures for 4 ​ d 4d -dimensional Kummer-type hyper-Kähler varieties
Josiah Foster

Abstract
. Contingent upon a conjecture of Buchweitz and Flenner, we prove the Lefschetz standard conjectures for 4 ​ d 4d -dimensional projective varieties of generalized Kummer deformation type.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The standard conjectures

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{C}
, and let the cohomological operator
L_{\alpha}
be given by taking the cup product with a polarization
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. By the Hard Lefschetz theorem, there exists an isomorphism

L_{\alpha}^{n-k}:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2n-k}(X,\mathbb{Q})

for all
k\leq n
. The Lefschetz standard conjecture (LSC) over the complex numbers is the following statement:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
16
, p. 196]
)
.

For each
k\leq n
, there exists an algebraic self-correspondence

[\mathcal{Z}]\in H^{2k}(X\times X,\mathbb{Q}),

arising from a codimension-
k
cycle
\mathcal{Z}\in\mathrm{CH}^{k}(X\times X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, such that the isomorphism

(1)

[\mathcal{Z}]^{*}:H^{2n-k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})

is the inverse of the isomorphism
L^{n-k}_{\alpha}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Conjecture
1.1
is independent of the choice of polarization on
X
and is in fact equivalent to the existence of the inverse isomorphisms (
1
). Over
\mathbb{C}
, Conjecture
1.1
implies that homological and numerical equivalence coincide, and that the Künneth components of the diagonal
\Delta_{X}\subset X\times X
are algebraic (see for example
[
23
]
). It has important applications in the theory of motives (see
[
3
, Chapter 5]
), and in degree
2
it implies a weakened formulation of the period-index conjecture (
[
13
]
). In addition, the LSC implies the variational Hodge conjecture by
[
49
, Theorem 7]
.

There are few examples of classes of projective varieties for which the LSC holds. One clear class of examples are varieties
X
for which the cycle class map
Ch(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is an isomorphism (e.g. Grassmanians, flag varieties, etc.). The conjecture also holds for Abelian varieties by
[
25
]
. Charles and Markman in
[
12
]
prove the LSC for irreducible holomorphic symplectic varieties deformation equivalent to Hilbert schemes of points on
K3
surfaces. An alternative proof of their result is known (Corollary
5.17
). In
[
4
]
, Arapura has proven the LSC for moduli spaces of stable vector bundles over a smooth projective curve and for the Hilbert scheme of points on any smooth projective surface, as well as for uniruled threefolds and unirational fourfolds. The results of
[
15
]
generalize Arapura’s result by demonstrating the LSC for projective varieties deformation equivalent to a moduli space of Gieseker-stable sheaves on an Abelian surface with primitive, positive, and effective Mukai vector. As a corollary, the LSC holds for projective varieties deformation equivalent to a generalized Kummer variety in certain cohomological degrees. For important results on the standard conjectures for Lagrangian fibered IHSMs, see
[
2
]
.

1.2.
Kummer-type irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifolds

Definition 1.3
.

An
irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold (IHSM)
(or compact hyper-Kähler manifold) is a simply connected compact Kähler manifold such that
H^{2,0}(X)
is spanned by a unique, nowhere-vanishing holomorphic
2
-form.

To date, there are four known deformation types of IHSMs. Two were constructed in
[
6
]
and arise in each even complex dimension. They are varieties deformation equivalent to Hilbert schemes of points on
K3
surfaces and deformation equivalent to generalized Kummer varieties. Two additional examples in dimensions
10
and
6
were constructed in
[
37
]
and
[
38
]
respectively. They arise as desingularizations of particular moduli spaces of sheaves on symplectic surfaces.

For
X
an IHSM, the second integral cohomology
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
carries an integral, symmetric, primitive, non-degenerate bilinear pairing
q_{X}
of signature
(3,b_{2}-3)
, known as the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki (BBF) pairing
[
6
, Theorem 5]
, which is defined by the property that
for all
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
, we have the equation

(2)

\int_{X}\alpha^{2n}=c_{X}q_{X}(\alpha)^{n}

where
c_{X}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
is the Fujiki constant. This is a generalization of the pairing on the second cohomology of a
K3
surface.

In comparison to
K3
-surfaces, general IHSMs exhibit a weakened version of the global Torelli theorem (see
[
22
]
and
[
47
]
).

Definition 1.4
.

Let
A
be an Abelian surface, and
A^{[n+1]}
the Hilbert scheme of zero-dimensional length-
(n+1)
subschemes of
A
. For
h:A^{[n+1]}\rightarrow A
the composition of the Hilbert-Chow morphism and summation in the group
A
, the fiber
Kum_{n}(A):=h^{-1}(0)
is
2n
-dimensional IHSM known as a
generalized Kummer variety
. An IHSM deformation-equivalent to
Kum_{n}(A)
is said to be of
generalized Kummer deformation type
.

Note that the Abelian surface
A
has a four-dimensional deformation space, while
Kum_{n}(A)
deforms in its five-dimensional period domain (as
b_{2}(Kum_{n}(A))=7
). Therefore, a generic variety of generalized Kummer deformation type is not the Kummer variety arising from an Abelian surface.
Let us describe the connection between generalized Kummer varieties and moduli spaces of sheaves on an Abelian surface
A
.

Definition 1.5
.

The
Mukai lattice
of
A
consists of the following data:

(1)

The
8
-dimensional lattice
(H^{ev}(A,\mathbb{Z}),\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, where
H^{ev}(A,\mathbb{Z}):=H^{0}(A,\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{4}(A,\mathbb{Z})
is the ring of even cohomology, and for
v=(v_{0},v_{2},v_{4})
and
w=(w_{0},w_{2},w_{4})
in
H^{ev}(A,\mathbb{Z})
,

\langle v,w\rangle:=\int_{A}v_{2}w_{2}-v_{0}w_{4}-v_{4}w_{0}

is the
Mukai pairing
.

(2)

A weight-two Hodge structure on
H^{ev}(A,\mathbb{C})
defined by

H^{0,2}(H^{ev}(A,\mathbb{C})):=H^{2,0}(A),

H^{1,1}(H^{ev}(A,\mathbb{C})):=H^{0,0}(A)\oplus H^{1,1}(A)\oplus H^{2,2}(A),

H^{2,0}(H^{ev}(A,\mathbb{C})):=H^{2,0}(A)
.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
51
, Theorem 0.2.]
)
.

Let
A
be an Abelian surface an let
v\in H^{ev}(A,\mathbb{Z})
be a primitive Mukai vector that represents a sheaf. Assume
v^{2}\geq 6
. Then a fiber over
a\in A\times\widehat{A}
of the Albanese morphism

alb_{v}:\mathcal{M}_{H}(v)\xrightarrow{det\times\widehat{det}}A\times\widehat{A}

is a variety
Kum_{a}(v)
of generalized Kummer deformation type.

1.3.
The main results

Theorem 1.7
.

Assume the conjecture of Buchweitz and Flenner as formulated in Conjecture
2.10
. Then Conjecture
1.1
holds for all
4d
-complex dimensional IHSMs of generalized Kummer deformation type.

The correspondences satisfying the assumptions of Conjecture
1.1
arise from Fourier-Mukai kernels of derived equivalences between Kummer-type IHSMs. Deformation equivalent IHSMs
X
and
Y
that are derived equivalent with a nontrivial Fourier-Mukai kernel of nonzero rank satisfy the LSC (Corollary
5.17
). This fact follows from the main result in
[
43
]
, which implies that the Hodge isometry associated to such a derived equivalence conjugates the LLV Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(X)
to
\mathfrak{g}(Y)
. The main result of
[
27
]
provides a derived equivalence between
4d
-dimensional Kummer-type IHSMs
Kum_{n}(A)
and
Kum_{n}(\widehat{A})
, with the assumption that there exists a polarization
A\rightarrow\widehat{A}
satisfying certain numerical conditions. This construction is built upon and generalized by Yang in
[
50
]
. The Fourier-Mukai kernels of these equivalences are images of an inflated semihomogeneous vector bundle under the Bridgeland-King-Reid (BKR) correspondence. Our main objective is then to deform these Fourier-Mukai equivalences over the moduli space of products of Hodge-isometric marked IHSMs.

The beautiful work in
[
39
]
,
[
40
]
, and
[
29
]
provides a description of special sheaves on IHSMs known as
modular sheaves
. A modular sheaf
(\mathcal{F},H)
is a slope-stable reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{F}
with respect to an ample class
H
such that there exists an open subcone of the ample cone containing
H
such that
\mathcal{F}
remains slope-stable with respect to every class
H^{\prime}
in this subcone. This is equivalent to the orthogonal projection of the characteristic class
\kappa_{2}(\mathcal{F})
introduced by Markman in
[
28
]
(see Definition
2.7
) being a rational multiple of the class of the BBF form in the Verbitsky component of
H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Q})
(cf.
[
39
, Definition 1.1]
). Such sheaves are related to hyperholomorphic sheaves studied by Verbitsky in
[
46
]
; Markman in
[
29
]
provides conditions for the existence of modular sheaves on IHSMs and further a proof of the fact that that their sheaf of Azumaya algebras
\mathcal{E}nd(F)
deforms with every Kähler deformation.

Our situation is slightly different, as we wish to deform a sheaf over a
product
of IHSMs using the calculus of twistor lines described in
[
46
]
. It is not a priori clear what it means to be projectively hyperholomorphic over a product. Slope-stability also presents an issue, as we cannot expect the sheaf to remain slope stable with respect to arbitrary Kähler deformations of the factors. To this end, in section
6
we deform our sheaf over a “moduli space of rational Hodge isometries,” i.e. a moduli space of products of IHSMs whose cohomology rings are rational Hodge-isometric, following Markman in
[
31
]
. We demonstrate that we have control over the characteristic class
\kappa
along small deformations of the product of IHSMs.

1.4.
Outline of the paper

In section
2
we introduce semiregularity, the Buchweitz-Flenner theorems, and their generalizations to the setting of twisted sheaves. In section
3
, we introduce the construction of the vector bundles on products of Kummer-type IHSMs that are of interest to us. In section
4
, we study the equivariance properties of these bundles and their semiregularity. In section
4.6

we make concrete the relationship between nontrivial derived equivalences and the Lefschetz standard conjecture, and in section

\sigma_{q}:\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\cdot(-At(\mathcal{F})^{q}/q!)}\mathrm{Ext}_{X}^{q+2}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{i})\xrightarrow{H^{q+2}(Tr)}H^{q+2}(X,\Omega_{X}^{q}),

\sigma:=(\sigma_{0},\dots,\sigma_{n-2}):\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F})\longrightarrow\prod_{q=0}^{n-2}H^{q+2}(X,\Omega^{q}_{X}),

where

see
[

3.
Simple semihomogeneous vector bundles on products of Abelian varieties

Semihomogeneous vector bundles on Abelian varieties are those which are compatible with two natural classes of deformations: The pullback by the translation map and tensorization by line bundles. They are important in the study of vector bundles on Abelian varieties and provide an intriguing interpretation as the analogue of modular sheaves on IHSMs (see
[
30
, Remark 3.5(2)]

Let
\Lambda
be a

integral lattice and let

. We have the Abelian variety

with complex structure

the dual Abelian variety. For

a polarization of

is the smallest natural number such that
\mathrm{ker}(\lambda)\subset\mathrm{ker}(\cdot\,n)
. Recall
\widehat{A}\cong\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(A)
, the moduli space of degree
0

. Recall also that there exists a universal line bundle

known as the

that parametrizes degree zero line bundles on

\mathcal{P}_{\{0\}\times\widehat{A}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{A}}

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a vector bundle on an Abelian variety

We may consider the slope as an equivalence class

Definition 3.2
.

A vector bundle

.

Remark 3.3

In general, semihomogeneous vector bundles arise as follows: Let

be an isogeny of Abelian varieties of degree

be a line bundle on

is a semihomogenous vector bundle on

.

Let
\mu=[\mathcal{L}]\otimes\frac{1}{\ell}\in NS(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let

.
Define

and denote by

Proposition 3.4

(
[

, Lemma 4.9]

Let

be a simple semihomogeneous vector bundle. Then

The following is a crucial property of semihomogeneous vector bundles which will come in handy later:

Proposition 3.5

(
[
36
, Lemma 4.8.]
)

Simple semihomogeneous vector bundles of the same slope

Proof.

If

, then
\mathcal{F}_{i}\cong\mathcal{F}_{j}
, as each of the
\mathcal{F}_{i}

are stable and of the same slope. Every simple semihomogeneous vector bundle of slope

∎

Proposition 3.6

4.3.
The BKR correspondence

Let

be a projective variety and

a finite group acting on

Definition 4.5

A

on

Note that

-invariant subschemes that are free

that contains all points corresponding to free orbits of the permutation action. Closed points of

-invariant subschemes

of

such that

\mathscr{S}_{n+1}
-regular representation. The component

Proposition 4.6

(

and

)

The Hilbert scheme

is isomorphic to the reduced component

\mathscr{S}_{n+1}
-clusters.

Remark 4.7

In

4.4.
Derived equivalences of Kummer-type IHSMs

From now on, we will let
G:=\mathscr{S}_{n+1}
and
G_{\Delta}\subset G\times G
be
the diagonal subgroup consisting of elements
(\sigma,\sigma)
, for
\sigma\in G
.
Let us provide an outline of Magni’s strategy in
[
27
]
for constructing derived equivalences of generalized Kummer varieties. The following is the main theorem that will be reached by the end of this subsection. We also remark that the results of
[
27

are part of a more general construction described in
[
50
]
.

Theorem 4.11

(
[
27
]
)
.

Let
n+1\geq 3
be odd. There exist Abelian surfaces
A
and
\widehat{A}
and a
G
-invariant simple semihomogeneous vector bundle
\mathcal{F}
on
W\times\widehat{W}
such that
G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{F}:=\mathrm{inf}^{G^{2}}_{G_{\Delta}}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow W\times\widehat{W}
is a
G^{2}
-equivariant Fourier-Mukai kernel of a derived equivalence

\Phi_{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{F}}:D^{b}_{G}(W)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}_{G}(\widehat{W}).

Via BKR conjugation, the kernel yields a derived equivalence

\Phi_{\mathcal{F}^{K}}:D^{b}(Kum_{n}(A))\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(Kum_{n}(\widehat{A})),

where
\mathcal{F}^{K}\in D^{b}(Kum_{n}(A)\times Kum_{n}(\widehat{A}))
is the image of
\mathcal{F}^{G}
under the equivalence of categories given in Proposition
4.9
.

Definition 4.12
.

A vector bundle
\mathcal{U}
is
unipotent
if it admits a filtration

0=\mathcal{U}_{0}\subset\mathcal{U}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{U}_{m}=\mathcal{U},

such that for each
i
from
1
to
m

\mathcal{U}_{i}/\mathcal{U}_{i-1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

A semihomogeneous vector bundle
\mathcal{G}
on an Abelian variety admits a decomposition via the Jordan-Hölder filtration as

(36)

\mathcal{G}\cong\bigoplus_{j}\mathcal{F}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{j},

where each
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is a simple semihomogeneous vector bundle and each
\mathcal{U}_{j}
is unipotent, by
[
34
, Proposition 6.18]
. For ease of notation, define

(37)

X:=W\times\widehat{W}.

We consider the Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{P}\rightarrow X\times\widehat{X}
.

In order to rewrite (
36

(-)\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\widehat{X}[n]
on the bundle
\mathcal{G}
. By the property of semihomogeneous vector bundles that any such bundle
\mathcal{F}_{j}
can be represented as a bundle
\mathcal{F}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\alpha_{j}}
(see
[
34
, Proposition 7.11]
), the action of
\widehat{X}[n]
on each summand
\mathcal{F}_{j}
has stabilizer
\{\alpha\in\widehat{X}[n]:\mathcal{F}_{0}\cong\mathcal{F}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\alpha_{j}}\}

, i.e. the subset
\Sigma_{\mu}\subset\widehat{X}[n]
from (

Proposition 4.13

(
[
27
, Proposition 7.14, Proposition 7.15, Proposition 7.21]
)
.

The Jordan-Hölder filtration in (
36
) admits a split unipotent part, i.e. for each summand
\mathcal{U}_{j}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{r_{j}}
. Furthermore, the resulting split decomposition

(38)

\mathcal{G}\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha_{j}\in\widehat{X}[n]/\Sigma_{\mu}}(\mathcal{F}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{\alpha_{j}})^{r}

contains at least one
G
-invariant summand.

Remark 4.14
.

For
\mathcal{F}
the simple semihomogeneous vector bundle in Theorem
4.11
, note that by definition,

\mathrm{inf}_{G_{\Delta}}^{G^{2}}(\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_{g\in G_{\Delta}\backslash G^{2}}g^{*}\mathcal{F}

is a direct sum of simple semihomogeneous vector bundles, each of whose slope is equal to that of
\mathcal{G}
.

Magni therefore proves that there exists a simple semihomogeneous vector bundle
\mathcal{F}_{0}
on

which provides a derived equivalence between the Abelian varieties
W
and
\widehat{W}
. The next step is to prove this derived equivalence carries a

-linearization and therefore can be enhanced to an equivalence of equivariant derived categories by way of Proposition
4.3
. This requires that we have control over the projective representations of

. We have the following key proposition:

Proposition 4.15

(
[
27
, Proposition 7.25]
)
.

Let
H
be a finite group acting on a projective variety
X
and let
\mathcal{E}\rightarrow X
be a sheaf. Assume:

(1)

\mathcal{E}
is simple;

(2)

\mathcal{E}
is
H
-invariant;

(3)

There exists

such that
\mathcal{E}^{r}
is
H
-equivariant;

(4)

Every

is isomorphic to a linear representation of

.
Then,
\mathcal{E}
is
H
-equivariant.

Proposition 4.16

(
[
27
, Theorem 6.11, Theorem 6.18, Theorem 7.27]
)
.

The
G
-equivariant Fourier-Mukai kernel
\mathcal{F}^{G}
can be inflated to yield a
G\times G
-equivariant sheaf
G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{F}\in D^{b}_{G\times G}(W\times\widehat{W})
which provides an equivalence of categories

\Phi_{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{F}}:D^{b}_{G}(W)\longrightarrow D^{b}_{G}(\widehat{W}).

Proof.

Proposition
4.15
implies that the sheaf
\mathcal{F}^{G}\rightarrow W\times\hat{W}
is
G_{\Delta}\cong G
-equivariant and hence

\mathcal{F}^{G}\in D^{b}_{G_{\Delta}}(W\times\widehat{W}).

Every projective representation of

is already linear. This follows from the surjectivity of the map

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathscr{S}_{n+1},\,\mathrm{GL}(r,\mathbb{C}))\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathscr{S}_{n+1},\,\mathrm{PGL}(r,\mathbb{C})),

as worked out in

. The class in

that obstructs the existence of a

-linearization of

therefore vanishes and hence we can enhance the sheaf

to the sheaf

. We can then apply the inflation map (

in

\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{Eq}(D^{b}_{\mathscr{S}_{3}}(W),D^{b}_{\mathscr{S}_{3}}(\widehat{W}))^{G_{\Delta}}
carries a

-linearization, as the induced map (

∎

Lemma 4.17
.

The summands of
G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{F}:={\bigoplus}_{g\in G_{\Delta}\backslash G^{2}}g^{*}\mathcal{F}^{G}

Proof.

It suffices to show that for each

such that

arises as a direct summand of the sheaf

given in (

with

4.5.

We next study the BKR image of the derived equivalence constructed in the previous section. Let
\mathcal{F}^{G}
denote the simple semihomogeneous vector bundle on a product of Abelian varieties

.

For the sake of brevity, we will denote by

Further, recall that we define the trace map

Since

carries a

-invariant structure, we have

. By

Therefore the image of the trace map is isomorphic to

Proposition 4.20
.

The traceless endomorphisms
\mathcal{E}nd_{0}(\mathcal{F}^{K})

Proof.

For a simple semihomogenous vector bundle
\mathcal{F}
on a product of Abelian varieties

4.6.

To this point, we have ascertained that over an analytic disk

\mathcal{F}^{K}
to a possibly twisted sheaf over a subdisk

Proposition 4.22
.

Assume that for an object

, the intersection
\Sigma(E)\cap[H^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X\times Y})\oplus H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X\times Y})]

\kappa(E)
remains of Hodge-type under every Kähler deformation of

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as that of
[

. Fix an element
\beta\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X\times Y})
. Since the projection
\Sigma(E)\cap[H^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X\times Y})\oplus H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X\times Y})]\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{X\times Y})
is surjective, there exists a lift of

to
(\alpha,\beta,0)\in\Sigma(E)
, i.e. the object

deforms in the direction

. Just as in loc. cit. Lemma 3.1, this implies that the Chern character of the untwisted object

. Hence,
\kappa(E)
, the
r

, also remains of Hodge-type.
∎

\kappa(\mathcal{F}^{K})

along which the sheaf
\mathcal{F}^{K}

5.
Derived equivalences of IHSMs and the Lefschetz standard conjectures

5.1.
The Lefschetz standard conjectures

Let us recall some basic facts concerning the Lefschetz standard conjectures. For any compact Kähler manifold
X
of dimension
n
, there exists an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation on the total cohomology
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. For a fixed ample class
f\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Q})
, the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-algebra is defined by the triple
(L_{f},h,\Lambda_{f})
. Here,
L_{f}
denotes the Lefschetz operator which acts by
(-)\cup f
,
h\in\mathrm{End}(H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q}))
acts by
(k-n)\cdot Id
on
H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})
, and
\Lambda_{f}
is the formal adjoint of
L_{f}
. That is,
\Lambda_{f}\in\mathrm{End}(H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q}))
such that

(46)

[L_{f},\Lambda_{f}]=h,\hskip 14.22636pt[h,\Lambda_{f}]=-2\Lambda_{f}.

What’s more, for all
k\leq n
the Hard Lefschetz theorem yields isomorphisms

L^{n-k}_{f}:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2n-k}(X,\mathbb{Q}).

When working in the complex setting, all of the Lefschetz standard conjectures are implied by the following:

Conjecture 5.1

(Grothendieck)
.

For each
k\leq n
, the operator
\Lambda^{n-k}_{f}
is algebraic. That is, there exists a codimension-
k
cycle
\mathcal{Z}\in Ch^{k}(X\times X)
such that
[\mathcal{Z}]^{*}=\Lambda^{n-k}_{f}
.

Remark 5.2
.

For the purposes of this section, as we always work over
\mathbb{C}
, we refer to Conjecture
5.1
as
the
Lefschetz standard conjecture (LSC).

7.
Remarks on further progress on the standard conjectures for Kummer-type IHSMs

Remark 7.1
.

Note that after assuming Conjecture
2.10
, if we combine Theorem
6.14
with the main theorem of
[
15
]
yields the Lefschetz standard conjecture for IHSMs of generalized Kummer type of dimension
2n\geq 8
in the following degrees:

\begin{cases}\hskip 8.53581pt\text{all,}&n\text{ even}\\
\hskip 8.53581pt<n+1,&n\text{ odd}.\\
\end{cases}

Remark 7.2
.

Flocarri’s work in
[
14
]
demonstrates the Hodge and Tate conjectures for sixfold IHSMs of generalized Kummer deformation type. This implies the case where
n=3
, as the Hodge classes in the diagonal can be used to construct the algebraic cycle
Z
. Flocarri also proves the Hodge and Tate conjectures when
n=2
, but note that this case the standard conjectures also follow form the main theorem of
[
15
]
.

The integer
n
being even is a necessary condition for the existence of derived equivalences of generalized Kummer varieties
D^{b}(Kum_{n}(A))\rightarrow D^{b}(Kum_{n}(\widehat{A}))
that arise from equivariant derived equivalences
D^{b}(A)\rightarrow D^{b}(\widehat{A})
(see
[
50
]
). As a result, we believe that new ideas will be necessary to construct derived equivalences between generalized Kummer varieties. These derived equivalences do not arise from lifting derived equivalences of Abelian varieties.

One possible future avenue would be to discover new examples of derived autoequivalences of generalized Kummer varieties whose induced Hodge isometry on total cohomology is degree reversing. Such examples follow from work in the
K3^{[2]}
deformation type case in
[
1
]
and in the Kummer fourfold case in
[
33
]
. However, the result is based on the fact that a particular twisted relative extension sheaf constructed in
[
28
]
provides a derived equivalence in the four-dimensional case. It is currently unknown if such examples exist for higher dimensional
K3^{[n]}
-type hyper-Käher varieties. For Kummer-type hyper-Kählers, Remark
7.1
indicates that the first case that would lead to new cases of the standard conjectures is dimension
10
.

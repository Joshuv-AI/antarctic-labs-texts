A. Oman et al. 2019 ; K. A. Oman 2024 ) , pingouin 0.5.5 ( R. Vallat 2018 ) , Python 3.12.3, SoFiA 2.5.0 ( T. Westmeier et al. 2021 )
Xuchen Lin (林旭辰), Jing Wang (王菁), Guinevere Kauffmann, Volker Springel, Rüdiger Pakmor

Abstract
With new atomic-hydrogen ( H i ) observations of FAST Extended Atlas of Selected Targets Survey (FEASTS), we present the first statistical comparison of H i morphology between observations and cosmological simulations, focusing on low–column density ( ∼ 1 × 10 18 cm − 2 \sim 1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2} ) regions of Milky Way–like central galaxies. We select a 330-galaxy sample from IllustrisTNG50 (TNG50) matched to 33 FEASTS galaxies by stellar and H i masses, and mock observe them to the FAST resolution and depth at corresponding inclinations and distances for a fair comparison. In contrast to FEASTS, abnormally irregular and extended morphology is found in more than one-third of TNG50 galaxies, especially those massive and H i poor. Stellar feedback is the property that most significantly correlates with the H i morphological deviation from observations, although these deviations mostly occur at a high stellar or black-hole mass. These results indicate that in TNG50, stellar feedback significantly influences the H i morphology at ∼ 1 × 10 18 cm − 2 \sim 1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2} , while active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback has not so direct a role as expected. With an additional sample from Auriga, we find that the magnetic field may help H i to be more regular in its morphology, while improving the mass resolution does not alleviate the discrepancy from observation. This study reveals the potential of constraining future simulations of galaxies by observing low–column density H i .



I
Introduction

In the Lambda cold dark matter (
\Lambda
CDM) cosmological context, the baryon cycle is a key component regulating galaxy evolution
(
C. Péroux & J. C. Howk 2020
)
.
Around galaxies, the circumgalactic medium (CGM) provides the reservoir of gas accretion and serves as the passage through which gas recycles back to the interstellar medium (ISM) after being kicked out by feedback processes
(
J. Tumlinson et al. 2017
)
.
The multiphase CGM has temperatures and densities spanning orders of magnitude
(
C.-A. Faucher-Giguère & S. P. Oh 2023
)
, setting a complex environment for the evolution of ISM and galaxies.

This interface between CGM and ISM offers important constraints for galaxy-formation models
(
R. A. Crain & F. van de Voort 2023
)
, but a comprehensive and quantified description of it requires multiwavelength observations.
The low–column density, cool gas in CGM and outer ISM has been primarily observed with absorption lines
(
H.-W. Chen 2017
;
N. Lehner 2017
)
.
The aggregation of single–line of sight absorption measurements provides the column-density distribution function
(
C. Péroux et al. 2005
;
D. M. French et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
and, after associating them with nearby possible absorbers, averaged spatial dependence
(
S. Weng et al. 2023
;
S. Weng et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.
Due to the sparsity of bright quasars
(
M. Fumagalli 2024
)
, the interpretation has caveats of systematic source-to-source variation and degeneracy in geometry and kinematics.

Another possibility is directly mapping the
21\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}
emission from neutral atomic hydrogen (
H
i
).
With recent advances in radio astronomy equipments and technologies, a relatively low
H
i
column density (
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
) of
\sim 5\text{\times}{10}^{17}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
has been reached for an intermediate-sized sample at a relatively low time cost by programs like FAST Extended Atlas of Selected Targets Survey
(
J. Wang et al. 2023
;
J. Wang et al. 2025
, FEASTS;)
and MeerKAT
H
i
Observations of Nearby Galactic Objects: Observing Southern Emitters
(
W. J. G. de Blok et al. 2024
, MHONGOOSE;)
.

H
i
in unperturbed galaxies is found to continuously extend to this
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
as a relatively smooth, dynamically cold rotating disk
(
W. J. G. de Blok et al. 2024
;
J. Wang et al. 2024b
)
, which can reach half the virial radius
(
J. Wang et al. 2025
)
.

H
i
gas properties have not been taken as a priori constraints in major cosmological simulations
(
R. A. Crain & F. van de Voort 2023
)
.
The comparison between simulations’ predictions and observations is usually conducted on a few physically motivated simple properties, such as

(
F. van de Voort et al. 2012
;
R. A. Crain et al. 2017
)
,

;
L. Wen et al. 2025
)
, and
H
i
–stellar mass relation
(
R. A. Crain et al. 2017
;
B. Diemer et al. 2019
)
, of which no simulation suite so far has fully reproduced all.

Meanwhile, the morphological comparison of
H
i
disks has been focused on high-
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
regions.
Compared with previous
H
i
interferometric surveys with an
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}

\gtrsim 1\text{\times}{10}^{19}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
,

)
and The Next Generation Illustris (IllustrisTNG)

A. Gebek et al. 2023
;
A. R. H. Stevens et al. 2023
)
are deficient in
H
i

Beyond the central region, the tight observational relation between the characteristic
H
i

1\text{\,}\mathrm{M_{\odot}}\text{\,}{\mathrm{pc}}^{-2}
and

(
J. Wang et al. 2016
)
is roughly reproduced in simulations but with a systematic offset depending on the recipe partitioning molecular and atomic hydrogen
(
F. Marinacci et al. 2017

B. Diemer et al. 2019
)

As an ecosystem, the star-forming activity in the inner region of galaxies is regulated by the active gas inflow and outflow at a lower

\gtrsim 5\text{\times}{10}^{17}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}

Directly quantify and compare the morphology of the simulated low-

With seven almost perfectly edge-on galaxies,

In this paper, we conduct a statistical comparison of

II.6
Auriga Simulation and Sample

Auriga
(
R. J. J. Grand et al. 2017
;
R. J. J. Grand et al. 2024
)
is a suite of cosmological “zoom-in” magnetohydrodynamical simulation of 40 MW-mass halos and 26 dwarf-galaxy haloes.
It is performed with
Arepo
as TNG, but has a slightly different physics model.
Important differences include (1) radio-mode AGN feedback modeled as randomly located “thermal bubble,” rather than randomly oriented kinetic jets and (2) not setting a velocity floor for galactic winds.
The fiducial Auriga simulation (level 4) has a similar mass resolution as TNG50, and the higher–mass resolution (level 3, better by
\sim 1\text{\,}\mathrm{dex}
) and/or hydrodynamical-only version of several halos are available.

We thus take Auriga as a supplementary dataset for assessing the influences of magnetic fields and simulation mass resolution (see
Section V
).
Four (or six) of Auriga level-4 galaxies have a hydrodynamical-only (high–mass resolution) counterpart for our comparison.

We also compare Auriga with TNG, checking possible effects of their differences in physics models, e.g., AGN and stellar feedback.
We conduct
H
i
postprocessing and face-on mock observation as in Sections
II.3
and
II.4
.
We select the 19 Auriga fiducial-set (level-4) galaxies with
M_{\ast}>$10^{10.5}\text{\,}\mathrm{M_{\odot}}$
and
$10^{9.65}\text{\,}\mathrm{M_{\odot}}$<M_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}<$10^{10.45}\text{\,}\mathrm{M_{\odot}}$
as the
Auriga sample
(blue triangles in
Figure 1
), which have a rather uniform coverage of the
M_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
–
M_{\ast}
space.

III
Morphological Parameters

We use three parameters to quantify the morphology of
H
i
moment 0 maps, i.e., the integrated
H
i
flux maps, at low column densities (
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
).
These parameters are designed to reflect the azimuthal regularity and the relative spatial extension of low-
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
structures, involving the contours at
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}=\text{$1\text{\times}{10}^{18}1\text{\times}{10}^{19}1\text{\times}{10}^{20}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}$}
.
If several non-connected regions are above the
1\text{\times}{10}^{20}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}

N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
threshold, we only consider the one closest to the galaxy center.
By doing so, the
1\text{\times}{10}^{20}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
contour is set to be a relatively simple reference point for other two contours.

The first parameter is irregularity (
I_{18}
), the relative area deviation of the
1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}

N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
contour from an ellipse with the same celestial area.
The ellipse’s position angle and ellipticity are computed from the second-order moments of the region within the more-regular
1\text{\times}{10}^{19}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
contour.
The computation is the same as that for elliptical geometric parameters in the photometry procedure of
SExtractor

(
E. Bertin & S. Arnouts 1996
)
but with a uniform weighting.
A similar parameter has been used by
J. Wang et al. 2013
at a higher
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
(
7\text{\times}{10}^{19}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
).
The second and third parameters are similar, defined as the ratio between areas of
1\text{\times}{10}^{18}1\text{\times}{10}^{19}1\text{\times}{10}^{20}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
contours.
We refer to
\log(S_{19}/S_{20})
and
\log(S_{18}/S_{19})
as the inner and outer area ratios, respectively.
For reference, in the atlas in
Figure 2
and the associated Figure Set, the contours of
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}=\text{$1\text{\times}{10}^{18}1\text{\times}{10}^{19}1\text{\times}{10}^{20}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}$}
are given along with the values of
I_{18}
,
\log(S_{19}/S_{20})
, and
\log(S_{18}/S_{19})
.

Our FEASTS observational sample have a wide range of distances (
7.025.9\text{\,}\mathrm{Mpc}
) and thus spatial resolutions, which may influence the observational morphological parameters.
This problem is alleviated by our matching the distance when conducting mock observations.
We verify that our results relating to comparing FEASTS and simulations are not influenced by distance (also see
Appendix A
).
We thus stick with the morphological parameters measured at original FEASTS distance when comparing FEASTS with simulations, and only use the
10\text{\,}\mathrm{Mpc}
cubes when conducting analysis within simulation datasets.

Magnetic Field and Mass Resolution

In
Figure 9
, we compare the morphological parameters measured with different versions of Auriga.
Vertical gray dotted lines link the corresponding galaxies in the fiducial set (empty blue triangles) and in the hydrodynamical-only set (i.e., without the magnetic field; orange pentagons) or high–mass resolution set (green diamonds).

Figure 9:

The morphological parameters of the same Auriga galaxy in different simulation sets:
fiducial (empty blue triangles), hydrodynamical-only (i.e., without the magnetic field; orange pentagons), or high–mass resolution (green diamonds).
The symbols of the same galaxy are linked by vertical gray dotted lines.

Despite the limited sample size (four), all of the hydrodynamical-only galaxies have a higher value of the morphological parameters than fiducial galaxies, indicating that the magnetic field helps
H
i
structures be smoother and less extended, thus being closer to observations.
This effect may arise due to the supporting role of magnetic pressure in the hot CGM
(
D. Nelson et al. 2020
)
and the ISM, so that cooling gas is less apt to fragmentation.
Another possibility is that due to the confinement by magnetic field, the metal-rich bipolar outflow of gas from feedback is not well mixed with the CGM
(
R. Pakmor et al. 2020
;
F. van de Voort et al. 2021
)
, also making the process of gas cooling into the disk less efficient.
Although the magnetic field is found not to significantly influence global galaxy properties
(
K.-Y. Su et al. 2017
;
P. F. Hopkins et al. 2020
)
, it may still influence the local distribution of
H
i
in low density regions.

Meanwhile, the high–mass resolution set and fiducial set do not show a clear trend in their differences, which fluctuate around zero with a large amplitude.
Apart from the small sample size (six), it is possible that changing the baryon mass resolution within the range of
5\text{\times}{10}^{4}6\text{\times}{10}^{3}\text{\,}\mathrm{M_{\odot}}
does not cause distinct and systematic differences in their
H
i
structures after the images being smoothed to a
\sim 10\text{\,}\mathrm{kpc}
resolution by the FAST beam.

The literature has pointed out that spatial and mass resolutions could have a different role on
H
i
morphologies
(
C.-A. Faucher-Giguère & S. P. Oh 2023
)
.

F. van de Voort et al. 2019
improved the spatial resolution of Auriga fiducial set to a maximum cell size of
1\text{\,}\mathrm{kpc}
, finding that the
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
profile becomes significantly higher and flatter at
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}<$1\text{\times}{10}^{19}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}$
, while the total
M_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
almost does not change.
Given that the
\log(S_{18}/S_{19})
of TNG50 or Auriga is already too high compared to observations, the
\log(S_{18}/S_{19})
value of the “
1\text{\,}\mathrm{kpc}
” simulation would be even higher, implying that simply improving the spatial resolution might not directly solve the problem of the simulated
H
i
morphology.

V
Indications for Physical Recipes in TNG50

A galaxy is an ecosystem regulated by the flow and circulation of baryonic matter
(
C. Péroux & J. C. Howk 2020
)
.
Cool-phase gas condenses from the CGM, getting accreted and forming the ISM disk, where it flows in to feed star formation and the central supermassive BH.
Their stellar and AGN feedbacks may inject baryonic matter and thermal/kinetic energy into both the ISM disk and CGM, and thus alter their thermodynamic properties, influencing ensuing rounds of gas accretion and star formation.

In this study, we find that the low–column density
H
i
of the central galaxies predicted by TNG is much more filamentary, irregular, and extended than in observations.
In search for the cause for such unrealistic
H
i
morphology, we analyze its correlation with galactic parameters within TNG50 in
Section IV.2
.
Even though the physical recipes in simulations are simplified, the simulated galactic properties are often regulated and contributed by multiple processes interfering with each other, as in the real Universe.
Notably, the simulated morphological artifacts correlate most strongly and directly with
SFR
and/or total
H
i
mass (
Figure 7
), indicating a close link with gas accretion, star formation, and stellar feedback in TNG.

Nonetheless, apart from the stellar and AGN feedback models, the abnormal
H
i
morphology in TNG50 may also come from the lack of physical processes that cosmological simulations have not implemented yet
(
R. A. Crain & F. van de Voort 2023
)
, such as thermal conduction, cosmic rays, and radiation transfer, which could in particular be relevant close to the disk.

V.1
The Dominant Role of Stellar Feedback

The stellar feedback in TNG is implemented as isotropic galactic winds with a minimum velocity floor that are decoupled from the local ISM
(
A. Pillepich et al. 2018b
)
, imparting both thermal and kinetic energy to the CGM.
This feedback mechanism has been found responsible for redistributing baryons within the halo virial radius
(
R. J. Wright et al. 2024
)
, even after the kinetic-mode AGN feedback is switched on
(
M. Ayromlou et al. 2023
)
, consistent with the dominating role of (s)
SFR
(within the past
100\text{\,}\mathrm{Myr}
) found in
Figure 7
.

On the global scale of a galaxy, the stellar feedback could influence the
H
i
morphology by producing recycling galactic fountains or by changing the CGM environments.
Galactic fountains may deform the
H
i
disk when they fall back
(
F. Fraternali 2017
)
, of which effects may be more prominent in simulations where fountain flows extend much further out of the galaxy
(
R. J. J. Grand et al. 2019
;
D. DeFelippis et al. 2020
)
possibly due to the lack of resolution at the disk–halo interface.
Stellar winds may also change the CGM environments and thus the
H
i
morphology through injecting energy and momentum, leaking ultraviolet photons, or polluting with metals.
If such processes have a short time scale compared to that of
SFR
variation, these effects may cause the strong correlation between the global
SFR
and
H
i
irregularity or area ratios within samples of single AGN feedback mode.
In TNG50, the typical stellar winds of MW-like galaxies reach the radius around abnormal
H
i
structures within
\sim 100\text{\,}\mathrm{Myr}
, taking a velocity floor of
350\text{\,}\mathrm{km}\text{\,}{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}

(
A. Pillepich et al. 2018b
)
, indeed shorter than the
SFR
variation timescale of
\gtrsim 300\text{\,}\mathrm{Myr}
found with its precursor, Illustris
(
M. Sparre et al. 2015
)
.

Another possibility is that in TNG, the stellar feedback deforms the
H
i
directly and locally by generating bubbles, holes, and shells in the star-forming region
(
T. A. Thompson & T. M. Heckman 2024
)
.
In TNG, this effect may be noticeable because the size of star-forming disks is on average twice as wide as in observation at a given
M_{\ast}

(
E. Wang & S. J. Lilly 2023
)
, possibly due to the overestimation of
SFR
at low gas density
(
V. Springel & L. Hernquist 2003
;
M. Vogelsberger et al. 2013
)
compared with observations
F. Bigiel et al. 2008
;
F. Bigiel et al. 2010
;
J. Wang et al. 2024a
;
M. R. Krumholz 2013
, see also
.
While in this picture, these oversized star-forming disks may increase the scope of stellar feedback, we do not find a correlation between the relative size of
SFR
disk (normalized by
H
i
disk size) and the morphological irregularity or area ratios (see
Appendix C
).
This lack of correlation holds in both thermal- and kinetic-mode samples, despite the latter has systematically larger morphological parameters as found in
Figure 6
.
Therefore, it is more likely that in TNG, the abnormal
H
i
morphology is dominantly produced by the stellar feedback in an integral and global way.

As other simulations, TNG has an overly simplified star-formation model.
It uses a density threshold above which the gas is converted to stars with an efficiency determined by the Kennicutt–Schmidt relation
(
V. Springel & L. Hernquist 2003
)
, without tracing the gas cooling below
\sim 1\text{\times}{10}^{4}\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
and the formation of molecular gas
(
M. R. Krumholz et al. 2009
)
.
In the outer region of a real galaxy disk, the metallicity is low,
H
i
is more in the warm phase than in the cool phase, and molecular gas is more difficult to form than in the inner disks
(
M. R. Krumholz 2013
)
;
thus, the tension between oversimplified model and real-galaxy star formation can be even stronger in the low-
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
region.
As mentioned at the beginning of this section, TNG also has a simplified stellar-feedback model after stars form.
In real galaxies, the feedback efficiency should depend on young stellar clustering, localized ISM pressure, structure, metallicity, dust, and gas phase
(
T.-E. Rathjen et al. 2021
)
, but these kinds of physics are currently missing in TNG.
These simplifications together may result in unrealistic stellar feedbacks that strongly perturb the outer
H
i
disks.

Interestingly, the stellar-feedback model in TNG and Auriga is comparatively gentle among cosmological simulations
(
R. A. Crain & F. van de Voort 2023
)
, but their galaxies are already more irregular in
H
i
than observations.
It is plausible that burstier stellar feedback models are conducive to an even more irregular
H
i
distribution
(
G. D. Joshi et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, which needs to be confirmed in future work.

V.2
Possible Mechanisms Associated with
H
i
Richness

There are at least two possible mechanisms relating the
H
i
area ratio to the
H
i
richness.
TNG galaxies have net in-plane radial inflow of
H
i
roughly out of the optical radius
(
A. Marasco et al. 2025
)
, possibly transferring there the outer
H
i
and decreasing area ratios.
This inflow should be stronger in a larger
H
i
disk (i.e., richer in
H
i
), where gas is easier to perturb and remove angular momentum.
Additionally, at the disk edge, more-extended
H
i
disks in TNG should be easier to truncate by thermal and radiative ionization from the surrounding CGM
(
X. Lin et al. 2025
)
.

These two mechanisms may explain the predicted anticorrelation between
M_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
and
\log(S_{18}/S_{19})
(lower row,
Figure 7
).
However, such anticorrelation is much weaker in observation (
Figure 5
) and only becomes significant in less-inclined systems after correcting for projection effects
(
J. Wang et al. 2025
, see)
, possibly being related to their much smaller
H
i
disks than in simulation at the
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}=$1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}$
level (
Appendix B
).
These two mechanisms can decrease the
H
i
extension, thus making simulated
H
i
disks more realistic, but in TNG, they may be insufficient compared to counteracting mechanisms.
Notably, the dependence of area ratio on
M_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
becomes subordinate to the one on
SFR
after the AGN enters the low-accretion mode in TNG, emphasizing the complex interplay between different processes.
This change in the importance of
M_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
may reflect different CGM conditions in different AGN modes, given that AGN feedback itself does not directly affect
H
i
morphology (see
Section V.3
).

V.3
The Auxiliary Role of AGN Feedback

In simulations, AGN feedback has been a standard component for reproducing the bend of the
M_{\ast}
–
M_{\text{halo}}
relation at a halo mass of
\sim 1\text{\times}{10}^{12}\text{\,}\mathrm{M_{\odot}}

(
J. Schaye et al. 2015
;
A. Pillepich et al. 2018a
)
.
It has been highlighted that in TNG, the cumulative AGN feedback (
\propto M_{\text{BH}}
) increases the CGM gas entropy and cooling time
(
E. Zinger et al. 2020
)
, strongly determining the star-formation activities
(
W. Ma et al. 2022
;
J. M. Piotrowska et al. 2022
)
.
In this study, however, BH properties generally do not correlate with the
H
i
morphology (
Figure 7
), except that galaxies with abnormally irregular or extended
H
i
are only found when
M_{\text{BH}}
is high or the AGN is in the low-accretion mode (Figures
6
and
11
).

Our results favor a scenario that the AGN feedback does not influence the
H
i
morphology instantly or directly.
It is more possible that it indirectly influences the
H
i
via cumulatively modifying the CGM condition
(
W. Ma et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
, not strongly dependent on specific feedback processes.
Correspondingly, when TNG galaxies transit from thermal-mode to kinetic-mode AGN feedbacks, their
H
i
morphology (
Figure 6
) and absolute size (
Figure 4
) do not change abruptly, and despite having a different AGN recipe, Auriga is similar to TNG50 in
H
i
morphology.
This modification of the CGM may be achieved by gradually emptying or heating the inner CGM, which in passing assists other feedback processes to reach and affect the
1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}

H
i
gas.
On a long time scale, the metal-rich gas ejected by the kinetic-mode feedback in TNG to roughly the virial radius
(
R. J. Wright et al. 2024
)
could also contribute to the
H
i
reservoir, after it cools down and falls back, losing track of the instantaneous AGN feedback
(
G. Kauffmann et al. 2019
)
.

VI
Summary

For the first time, we conduct a statistical comparison of the
H
i
morphology of MW-like galaxies at a column density level close to
1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
between observations and cosmological simulations.
The FEASTS observation and TNG50 simulation samples are matched by stellar mass,
H
i
mass, distance, and inclination.
The simulation data are mock observed and processed in a similar way as the observational data, providing a fair comparison.
We mainly use two parameters to quantify the irregularity at
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}\sim$1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}$
(
I_{18}
) and the spatial extension at
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}=$1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}$
(relative to
1\text{\times}{10}^{19}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
; the area ratio
\log(S_{18}/S_{19})
).

We obtain the following results:

1.

Compared with FEASTS, TNG50 galaxies have systematically higher levels of irregularity and extension for the
H
i
gas at
N_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}=$1\text{\times}{10}^{18}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}$
(
I_{18}
and
\log(S_{18}/S_{19})
).
Such an abnormality of
H
i
morphology happens in
{\sim}1/3
of our TNG50 sample.
Their
H
i
extensions at higher column density levels (spatial extension at
1\text{\times}{10}^{19}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
relative to
1\text{\times}{10}^{20}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
,
\log(S_{19}/S_{20})
) are much more similar.
The deviation from observations mainly occurs at
M_{\ast}\gtrsim$10^{10.5}\text{\,}\mathrm{M_{\odot}}$
,
f_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}\lesssim 10^{-0.5}
, or when the kinetic-mode AGN feedback is on.
All these morphological parameters of TNG50 galaxies have a positive (negative) dependence on stellar mass
M_{\ast}
(
H
i
mass fraction
f_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
), but those of FEASTS galaxies do not.

2.

Among the full TNG50 matched sample, the major driving property of
H
i
irregularity is BH mass or stellar mass; within the kinetic-mode sample, the
SFR
becomes the dominating property, and the BH mass reduces to the secondary one.
Overall, the area ratios that reflect the
H
i
radial profile extension decrease with
H
i
richness; within the kinetic-mode sample, area ratios majorly increase with
SFR
, and secondarily decrease with
M_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
.

3.

In TNG, while the total
SFR
value has a driving role on the
H
i
morphological parameters, the increase in its radial extension with respect to the
H
i
does not enhance the effect, suggesting its influence being global.

4.

Simulated galaxies in Auriga also have more-irregular and more-extended
H
i
morphologies than observed ones in similar
M_{\text{\text{H\,{i}} }}
and
M_{\ast}
ranges.
The magnetic field in Auriga potentially has a role in making
H
i
morphology more realistic, while the mass resolution does not have a clear systematic role on
H
i
morphology as quantified by our parameters.

Our comparisons highlight the difference in
H
i
morphology between the FEASTS observation and TNG50 simulation, especially at a high stellar mass (
\gtrsim 10^{10.5}\text{\,}\mathrm{M_{\odot}}
).
Although the
H
i
is usually thought to be easily perturbed in morphology, the results in this study favor a scenario that in the simulation, feedback processes only have a global effect, likely on a long time scale.
The dominant process that produces abnormal
H
i
morphology in TNG50 is the stellar feedback, whose global strength has a significant correlation with
H
i
morphology, while the AGN feedback is not so important as in predicting many other galaxy properties.

The
H
i
radial profile shape in TNG is potentially determined by the
H
i
mass (or fraction) to the first order due to synergies between mechanisms that are not fully understood at this stage.
This strong “self-determination” has revealed itself as the remarkably tight size–mass relation of
H
i

(
J. Wang et al. 2016
)
, and here further as the strong anticorrelation of area ratio
\log(S_{18}/S_{19})
with
H
i
mass.
The in-plane radial inflow and disk transiting to the CGM may play a role, which should be more thoroughly studied in the future.

At a high column density of
\sim 1\text{\times}{10}^{20}\text{\,}{\mathrm{cm}}^{-2}
, cosmological simulations are able to well reproduce
H
i
properties
(
Y. M. Bahé et al. 2016
;
F. Marinacci et al. 2017
;
B. Diemer et al. 2019
)
, indicating the degeneracy of physical processes there.
In this study, with the
H
i
depth pushed
\sim 100
times deeper, the discrepancy in
H
i
between observation and simulation becomes evident.
This study illustrates the capability of new, deep
H
i
surveys to reveal the physical processes happening at the CGM–ISM interface.
Methods and parameters developed in this work could help to constrain the physics recipe of future or ongoing cosmological simulations
(
J. Schaye et al. 2025
, e.g., COLd Ism and Better REsolution, or COLIBRE,)
.
While only the moment-0 map is used here, the kinematical information may provide further constraints in future studies.

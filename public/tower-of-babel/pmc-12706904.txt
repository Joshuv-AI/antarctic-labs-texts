The DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis is associated with the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps in atopic dermatitis
Authors: Heng Wang, Xuefei Ke, Xinyue Chen, Haixia Jing, Shukang Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAs a chronic inflammatory skin disease, atopic dermatitis (AD) results from immune dysregulation, epidermal barrier dysfunction, and inflammatory microenvironment. Although long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are implicated in inflammatory diseases, the role of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis in AD and its link to NETs formation remain unclear.ObjectiveThis research sought to explore the role of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis in AD pathogenesis, focusing on its regulation of keratinocyte inflammation, neutrophil recruitment, and NETs formation, and evaluate its potential as a diagnostic biomarker.MethodsThe expression levels of DSG1-AS1, miR-125b-5p and HMGA2 were detected by real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR). Molecular interactions were validated by dual-luciferase reporter assay. Cell migration was assessed using Transwell co-culture. NET markers were detected by ELISA. Cell viability and apoptosis were measured via CCK-8 and flow cytometry. The expression of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis in AD and its correlation with NET formation were analyzed by collecting serum samples from healthy volunteers and AD patients, and its potential value as a biomarker was evaluated by the ROC curve.ResultsDSG1-AS1 upregulated HMGA2 expression by suppressing miR-125b-5p, thereby exacerbating the inflammation of human keratinocytes and promoting its recruitment of human neutrophils (hNE). Moreover, DSG1-AS1 promoted NET formation of hNE via regulation of miR-125b-5p/HMGA2, impairing keratinocyte survival. Clinically, elevated serum DSG1-AS1/HMGA2 and reduced serum miR-125b-5p positively correlated with NET formation and disease severity. The combination of these molecules showed high diagnostic accuracy in AD (AUC = 0.878).ConclusionOur findings suggest that the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis may be closely related to AD progression by regulating NET formation, potentially offering novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets based on our in vitro and clinical serum data.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03459-6.

Introduction

A chronic inflammatory dermatosis, atopic dermatitis (AD) typically manifests with severe pruritus, eczematous eruptions, and a relapsing–remitting nature [1, 2]. With a global prevalence of 15–20% in children and 1–3% in adults [3], AD profoundly diminishes patients' well-being by causing sleep problems, mental health issues, social withdrawal, and greater vulnerability to secondary infections. The economic burden associated with AD is also substantial, placing a significant strain on healthcare systems due to the costs of medications, hospitalizations, and lost productivity. Multiple interrelated factors contribute to AD pathogenesis. These principally consist of hereditary factors, external exposures, aberrant immune responses, and defective cutaneous barrier properties [4]. Comprehending these intricate mechanisms is essential for formulating successful prevention and treatment approaches for AD.

As transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides that do not encode proteins, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) constitute a varied category of molecules with essential functions in controlling gene expression. Unlike mRNAs, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) lack significant open reading frames and primarily function through interactions with DNA, RNA, and proteins [5, 6]. LncRNAs can act as molecular scaffolds, guides, or decoys, orchestrating key physiological events encompassing cellular specialization, multiplication, apoptotic pathways, and immunological reactions, and epigenetic modifications [7]. LncRNAs mediate multilevel gene expression regulation, involving transcriptional control and RNA processing, post-transcriptional processing, and translational control. Emerging evidence suggests that lncRNAs are dysregulated in inflammatory diseases, particularly autoimmune disorders like rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease/ulcerative colitis, plaque psoriasis, and lupus, and may contribute to disease pathogenesis by modulating immune cell function, inflammatory cytokine production, and epigenetic modifications [8]. Understanding the role of lncRNAs in inflammatory diseases may provide new avenues for therapeutic intervention.

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) are web-like structures composed of decondensed DNA, histones, and antimicrobial proteins, such as myeloperoxidase (MPO), neutrophil elastase (NE), and cathepsin G, released by activated neutrophils [9]. NETs were initially recognized for their role in capturing and killing pathogens, preventing their dissemination, and contributing to host defense [10]. However, accumulating evidence indicates that excessive or dysregulated NET formation can contribute to tissue damage and inflammation in various diseases, including autoimmune disorders [11], and inflammatory skin conditions [12]. The proteases and cytotoxic molecules within NETs can directly damage host cells, activate the complement system, and perpetuate the inflammatory cycle. The role of NETs in AD is not well-defined, but recent studies suggest that NETs may contribute to the chronic inflammation and tissue damage observed in AD lesions.

Although previous studies have investigated the functions of DSG1-AS1, miR-125b-5p, and HMGA2 in other diseases, such as cancer [13–15], their functions and interactions in AD remain largely unknown. DSG1-AS1, an lncRNA molecule, has been implicated in the modulation of cellular proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis in various cancers. MiR-125b-5p, a microRNA, has been shown to regulate inflammatory responses, cell differentiation, and apoptosis in various cell types. HMGA2, a high mobility group A2 protein, is a chromatin structural regulator participating in cell growth, differentiation, tumorigenesis, and stem cell maintenance. The potential involvement of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis in AD remains unexplored. Given the known functions of its components in inflammation, we investigated whether this axis might influence AD pathogenesis by modulating neutrophil recruitment, NETs, and chronic tissue injury.

Therefore, this study aims to examine the involvement of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis in AD, focusing on its effects on keratinocyte inflammation, neutrophil recruitment, NETs formation, and epidermal barrier function. We also aim to explore the potential of these molecules as diagnostic biomarkers for AD severity and as therapeutic targets for AD. Preliminary elucidation of the pathophysiological mechanisms mediated by this axis in AD could potentially advance the establishment of more accurate diagnostic methods and personalized treatment regimens for this complex cutaneous disorder.

Materials and methods

Bioinformatics analysis

The AD-related datasets GSE168694 (peripheral blood mononuclear cells) and GSE140380 (lesional skin tissues), along with the psoriasis-related dataset GSE145054 (lesional skin tissues), were obtained from the GEO database (www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Differential expression analysis of lncRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs was performed using the GEO2R tool with the threshold set at |log2FC|> 1 and p < 0.05.

The lncRNASNP2 database (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/lncRNASNP#!/) was utilized to predict potential downstream miRNAs of DSG1-AS1, followed by intersection analysis with differentially expressed miRNAs from GSE145054. The Starbase database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php) was used to predict target genes of miR-125b-5p, which were subsequently cross-analyzed with AD-related differentially expressed genes (GSE168694 and GSE140380).

Cell culture and treatment

Human immortalized keratinocytes (HaCaT, Jennio Biotech, GuangZhou, China) and human neutrophils (hNE, Enzo Biochem, USA) were maintained in DMEM medium containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin antibiotic cocktail. Human primary keratinocytes (hPK) were provided by Wanwu Biotech (HeFei, China) and grew in the company’s specially formulated medium. The culture conditions of all cell lines were 37°C, 5% CO₂, humidified environment.

To establish the AD model in vitro, HaCaT and hPK cells were inoculated into 6-well tissue culture plates at 2 × 105 cells/well. Following cell attachment, the cells were induced by 10 ng/mL TNF-α combined with 10 ng/mL IFN-γ for 24 h [16], with control groups receiving equivalent volumes of PBS.

For NET induction, PMA (200 ng/mL) was applied to 1.7 × 106 hNE cells/well in poly-L-lysine-coated plates for 4 h [17, 18]. NETs were collected using a two-step centrifugation protocol: first at low speed (400 × g, 5 min) to pellet intact cells, followed by 10,000 × g centrifugation for 10 min to concentrate NETs. The final NET preparations were resuspended in PBS at a concentration equivalent to 1.7 × 106 cells/mL.

Cell transfection

Transfection experiments in this study were carried out with the Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer's protocol. For overexpression studies, full-length cDNA sequences of DSG1-AS1 and HMGA2 were cloned into the pcDNA3.1 vector (Invitrogen) to generate recombinant overexpression plasmids. Prior to transfection, HaCaT, hPK, or hNE cells were plated in 6-well culture dishes (5 × 104 cells/well) and allowed to adhere for 24 h. When cells reached 70–80% confluence, plasmid DNA (2 μg/well) was mixed with transfection reagent. At 6 h post-transfection, the spent medium was exchanged for fresh complete medium.

For gene knockdown experiments, specific shRNAs targeting DSG1-AS1 and HMGA2, along with negative control shRNA (sh-NC), were commercially synthesized at Shanghai GenePharma Co., Ltd. Cells were transfected with shRNA complexes (50 nM) for 48 h before harvesting for subsequent experiments.

In miRNA functional studies, miR-125b-5p mimic and its negative control (mimic NC), as well as miR-125b-5p inhibitor and its corresponding negative control (inhibitor NC), were purchased from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China) and transfected at a working concentration of 50 nM. Transfection efficiency was validated by RT-qPCR 48 h post-transfection. To ensure experimental reliability, each treatment condition included at least three independent biological replicates, and all transfection procedures were performed in an antibiotic-free medium.

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

RNA extraction from cell and serum samples was carried out with TRIzol (Invitrogen). The PARIS kit (Life Technologies, USA) was used to separate the cytoplasmic RNA and nuclear RNA from HaCaT, hPK, and hNE cells. The extracted RNA (1 μg) underwent reverse transcription employing the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit (Takara), adhering to the provided protocol. Quantitative PCR analysis was conducted in 20 μL reactions containing SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara) on an Applied Biosystems QuantStudio 6 Flex platform. Quantitative analysis of mRNA and miRNA expression was performed using GAPDH and U6 as internal reference genes for normalization. The nuclear reference of DSG1-AS1 was Histone H3, and the cytoplasmic reference was GAPDH. The 2−ΔΔCt formula [19] was employed for relative quantification. Each experiment included three replicates.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The wild-type sequence of DSG1-AS1 (wt-DSG1-AS1) and its mutant variant (mut-DSG1-AS1) were independently cloned into the pmirGLO dual-luciferase reporter vector. Similarly, the wild-type 3'UTR sequence of HMGA2 (wt-HMGA2) and its corresponding mutant (mut-HMGA2) were also inserted into the pmirGLO vector. All vector constructions were performed by Shanghai GenePharma Co., Ltd.

For functional validation, the constructed reporter vectors were simultaneously introduced with either miR-125b-5p mimic or its negative control mimic (mimic NC) into HaCaT, hPK, or hNE cells. Following 48 h of transfection, luciferase activities were measured with the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System.

Quantification of NET markers by ELISA

The levels of NETs-specific markers, including CitH3, MPO-DNA, and NE-DNA, in both cell culture supernatants and patient serum samples, were measured using ELISA kits (Saipei Biotechnology, Wuhan, China). Absorbance measurements were taken at 450 nm with a microplate reader, with the ELISA sensitivity range of 0.15–10 ng/mL. Duplicate determinations were performed for all experimental conditions.

Cell viability assay

A Transwell co-culture system (96-well cell culture chamber) was implemented to investigate NETs-mediated effects on keratinocyte viability. hNE (1.7 × 104 cells/insert) were plated in the upper compartment with HaCaT or hPK cultured in the lower chamber. For NETs induction, hNE cells in the experimental group were exposed to 100 nM PMA for 4 h, whereas control groups received equal volumes of PBS buffer. Following stimulation, the co-culture was maintained for an additional 24 h. Subsequently, HaCaT or hPK cell viability was determined using CCK-8 reagent (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The experiment included three independent biological replicates, each with six technical replicates.

Flow cytometric analysis of apoptosis

The impact of NET formation on HaCaT or hPK cell were assessed through flow cytometric analysis with Annexin V-FITC/propidium iodide (PI) dual staining. A Transwell co-culture system was employed with the following experimental setup: HaCaT or hPK cells were seeded in the lower chamber of 24-well plates at a density of 1 × 105 cells/well, while hNEs were plated in the upper Transwell inserts at 1.7 × 10⁶ cells/insert.

For the experimental group, NET formation was induced by 4 h stimulation with 100 nM PMA in the upper chamber followed by medium replacement, whereas the control group received no PMA treatment. Both groups were subsequently maintained in co-culture for 24 h. Following co-culture, HaCaT and hPK cells from the lower chambers were collected, trypsinized, washed with PBS (1000 rpm, 5 min), and resuspended at 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL.

Cell apoptosis was evaluated with the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (BD Biosciences). Briefly, 100 μL cell suspensions were incubated with 5 μL Annexin V-FITC and 5 μL PI in the dark at room temperature for 15 min, followed by immediate flow cytometric analysis.

Cell migration assay

The migratory recruitment of hNE by HaCaT or hPK cells was evaluated using a Transwell co-culture system (Corning, 8 μm pore size). HaCaT or hPK cells were seeded in the lower chambers of 24-well plates at a density of 2 × 105 cells/well and subjected to various treatments (including TNF-α + IFN-γ stimulation and DSG1-AS1 knockdown etc.) after adherence. Concurrently, hNE cells were resuspended in serum-free medium at 1 × 106 cells/Transwell insert (6.5 mm diameter, 8 μm pore size) and placed in the upper chambers. Following 6 h of co-culture, the Transwell inserts were carefully removed, and non-migrated hNE cells remaining on the upper membrane were mechanically cleared using cotton swabs. Migrated cells attached to the lower surface underwent fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde (15 min), followed by 0.1% crystal violet staining (20 min), PBS washing, and quantification. Migration rates were calculated as percentages relative to control groups.

Clinical sample collection

Patients diagnosed with AD were recruited from theTaihe Hospital, Hubei University of Medicine and stratified into three groups—mild, moderate, and severe—based on the SCORAD scoring system. Age- and sex-matched healthy volunteers were enrolled as controls.

Peripheral blood specimens (5 mL) were drawn in the morning after an overnight fast from each participant. After clotting at room temperature for 30 min, samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min (4 °C) to separate serum. The serum was aliquoted into sterile microcentrifuge tubes and immediately stored at − 80°C until further analysis.

Ethical approval for the study was granted by the Taihe Hospital, Hubei University of Medicine Ethics Committee, with written consent obtained from each participant.

Statistical analysis

Data were statistically analyzed using GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0). Statistical comparisons were carried out using Student's t-test (two-group comparisons) or one-way ANOVA (multi-group comparisons), as appropriate. The diagnostic value was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, calculating the area under the curve (AUC). Correlation analyses between variables were performed using the appropriate correlation coefficient based on data distribution (Pearson’s for normally distributed data, Spearman’s for non-normally distributed data). Results are expressed as mean ± SD, and graphical representations were created using GraphPad Prism.

To investigate DSG1-AS1 function, a sh-DSG1-AS1 knockdown model was established, which resulted in a significant reduction of DSG1-AS1 expression (Fig. 1e). TNF-α + IFN-γ stimulation markedly reduced both HaCaT and hPK cell viability, and this effect was significantly mitigated by DSG1-AS1 knockdown (Fig. 1f). Furthermore, DSG1-AS1 silencing in both cell types led to a significant downregulation of inflammatory mediators IL-1β and IL-6 concomitantly with a significant upregulation of anti-inflammatory IL-10 (Fig. 1g). These results indicate that DSG1-AS1 may promote AD progression by modulating inflammatory responses.

Further analysis combining Starbase predictions with AD patient datasets (GSE168694 and GSE140380) identified HMGA2 as a target of miR-125b-5p (Fig. 2d). The luciferase assay in HaCaT, hNE and hPK cells indicated that miR-125b-5p mimic substantially suppressed luciferase activity in cells transfected with wild-type HMGA2 (wt-HMGA2), whereas no significant suppression was observed in cells with mutant HMGA2 (mut-HMGA2) transfection (Fig. 2e). Functional studies revealed that DSG1-AS1 overexpression markedly increased HMGA2 mRNA levels in HaCaT, hNE and hPK cells, which could be reversed by miR-125b-5p mimic (Fig. 2f).

Cytokine profiling revealed that TNF-α + IFN-γ treatment markedly increased pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6, but decreased anti-inflammatory IL-10. Notably, either miR-125b-5p overexpression or HMGA2 knockdown reversed these aberrant expressions of cytokines (Fig. 3c, d). Importantly, HMGA2 overexpression partially rescued the inhibitory effect of miR-125b-5p, while miR-125b-5p inhibition reversed the suppression of hNE migration caused by DSG1-AS1 knockdown.

Assessment of NETs markers revealed that PMA treatment markedly increased CitH3, MPO-DNA, and NE-DNA levels. This effect was attenuated by DSG1-AS1 knockdown, miR-125b-5p overexpression, or HMGA2 silencing (Fig. 4d–f). Importantly, miR-125b-5p inhibitor reversed the suppressive effect of DSG1-AS1 knockdown on NETs formation, while HMGA2 overexpression partially eliminated the effect of miR-125b-5p mimic.

Co-culture experiments demonstrated that PMA-induced NET formation significantly reduced HaCaT and hPK viability to approximately 66% (Fig. 4g). Concordantly, flow cytometric analysis revealed an increase in apoptosis to about 13% (Fig. 4h, i). The notable reduction in cell viability, which exceeded the proportion of apoptotic cells, suggests the potential involvement of other forms of cell death, such as necrosis, in NET-treated HaCaT and hPK cells.

DSG1-AS1, miR-125b-5p, and HMGA2 as potential diagnostic biomarkers for AD

Clinical sample analysis revealed significantly elevated serum levels of DSG1-AS1 and HMGA2, but decreased miR-125b-5p in AD patients compared with healthy volunteers (Fig. 5a). ROC curve analysis demonstrated their diagnostic values: individually, miR-125b-5p showed the highest diagnostic efficiency (AUC = 0.787), outperforming DSG1-AS1 (AUC = 0.722) and HMGA2 (AUC = 0.684); while their combination achieved markedly improved accuracy (AUC = 0.878) (Fig. 5b). Discussion

This study systematically elucidates the regulatory mechanism of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 molecular axis in the pathogenesis of AD. By combining bioinformatics approaches, laboratory cell studies, and examination of patient specimens, we demonstrated that this molecular pathway promotes disease development through two distinct mechanisms: modulating inflammatory reaction in keratinocyte and regulating NET production.

Experimental evidence demonstrated that DSG1-AS1 modulated the biological function of miR-125b-5p via intermolecular interactions, thereby regulating the expression of downstream HMGA2. In keratinocytes, activation of this pathway significantly enhanced the release of inflammatory mediators IL-1β and IL-6 while inhibiting production of the immunoregulatory cytokine IL-10, creating a pro-inflammatory microenvironment. We also found that HMGA2 negatively regulates the expression of FLG, a key skin barrier protein, as its overexpression suppressed and knockdown promoted FLG levels. This finding links HMGA2 to the disruption of epidermal barrier function, a well-established pathway in AD. More importantly, this pathway also enhanced the chemotactic effect of keratinocytes on neutrophils, setting the stage for subsequent inflammatory cascades. In neutrophils, activation of this pathway directly promoted NET formation, a finding that offers a novel explanation for neutrophil dysfunction in AD. HMGA2, as the key effector molecule of this pathway, may directly influence cellular physiological activities and pathological processes through its expression and functional state. Recent studies suggest that NET formation is closely related to downstream pathways such as ROS production and activation of enzymes like NE and MPO, which are essential for the structural integrity and antimicrobial functions of NETs [9, 20]. HMGA2 may influence NET formation by regulating these pathways, potentially through modulating ROS levels or enzyme activity. However, the precise mechanisms by which HMGA2 influences NET formation likely involve complex crosstalk between these downstream signaling pathways. For instance, ROS production, protease activation (e.g., NE, MPO), and inflammatory mediator release are interconnected and can potentiate each other. Elucidating how HMGA2 modulates this intricate signaling network and its specific impact on the crosstalk of NET-related pathways is a crucial direction for future research.

The activation of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 molecular axis significantly promoted NET formation, and these structures directly impair keratinocyte survival and function by releasing specific components. Notably, the observed reduction in HaCaT viability upon NET exposure was more substantial than the increase in apoptosis, suggesting the contribution of other cell death modalities alongside apoptosis. Among these, necrosis represents a likely candidate, as the release of intracellular damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) from necrotic keratinocytes could not only amplify local inflammation but also serve as a feed-forward signal that further potentiates NET formation, thereby establishing a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop [21]. This intercellular interaction mechanism may constitute a key factor in disease progression and chronicity. In clinical sample analyses, our data demonstrated that the abundance of key pathway molecules correlated with NET markers in patient serum. Alterations in the abundance of these molecules exhibited a clear association with disease severity, particularly in severe cases. This finding confirms the clinical relevance of the basic research results and further indicates that these indicators may serve as potential biomarkers for assessing disease activity. Furthermore, the study reveals distinct patterns of these markers at different disease stages, providing new insights for disease staging and diagnosis.

Previous studies on the pathogenesis of AD have primarily focused on classical pathways such as Th2 immune dysregulation and skin barrier dysfunction, with limited reports addressing the role of lncRNA regulatory networks in disease progression, particularly the mechanism by which lncRNAs modulate neutrophil extracellular trap formation. Although the involvement of NET has been preliminarily characterized in neutrophil-dominant inflammatory skin disorders like psoriasis [22], their pathological significance and regulatory mechanisms in AD are still not fully elucidated, and their role remains controversial. On one hand, NETs, as a key component of innate immunity, play a protective role in host defense through their fundamental antimicrobial functions. For instance, one study revealed that S. aureus infection induces IL-33, which enhances NET production crucial for capturing and killing the bacteria, thereby strengthening innate immune defense [23]. Given that S. aureus colonization and infection are common features of AD [24], NETs might exert a similar protective effect in AD by restricting microbial overgrowth. On the other hand, excessive or dysregulated NET formation could potentially exacerbate tissue damage and chronic inflammation by releasing cytotoxic components and activating immune cells like plasmacytoid dendritic cells [25]. The current study elucidates the role of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis in regulating neutrophil extracellular trap formation in AD, providing novel experimental evidence for this research field.

Existing literature has mainly investigated the functions of miR-125b-5p and HMGA2 in tumorigenesis and organ fibrosis [26], while their roles in inflammatory skin diseases, particularly AD, have been relatively understudied. Our findings not only demonstrate the regulatory function of miR-125b-5p in AD but also reveal a novel mechanism whereby HMGA2 promotes disease progression by mediating NET formation. These results significantly expand our understanding of the biological significance of these molecules in cutaneous inflammation and provide new perspectives and theoretical foundations for both deciphering the pathogenesis of AD and developing differentiated therapeutic strategies.

From a diagnostic perspective, the combined detection of DSG1-AS1, miR-125b-5p, and HMGA2 proposed in this study demonstrates excellent diagnostic performance. Compared with single biomarkers, this multi-parameter approach exhibits higher sensitivity and specificity [27], enabling better differentiation between patients and healthy individuals and a more accurate assessment of disease severity. In terms of therapeutic development, the study identifies multiple potential intervention targets. Modulating DSG1-AS1 may influence the overall activity of the pathway while targeting miR-125b-5p could allow more precise regulation of downstream effectors. Additionally, direct intervention against HMGA2 may offer new therapeutic options for controlling NET formation. These findings lay a theoretical foundation for developing novel targeted therapies for AD, particularly for patients who respond poorly to existing treatments.

It is important to acknowledge the limitations of this study. Firstly, our clinical correlation analysis was confined to serum samples, and we did not examine the expression of the DSG1-AS1/miR-125b-5p/HMGA2 axis or NET formation directly in AD lesional tissues. Secondly, this study did not include validation in animal models, such as assessing changes in NETs formation and lesion phenotypes following intervention of axis molecules in AD mouse models. This limitation restricts our understanding of the actual in vivo relevance of the proposed mechanism and calls for caution when extrapolating these findings to clinical settings. Future studies should incorporate analyses of lesional skin tissues alongside animal experiments to evaluate the effects of targeted interventions on the axis, thereby providing more comprehensive evidence of the mechanistic role and therapeutic potential.

Conclusion

This study reveals that DSG1-AS1 promoted AD pathogenesis by sponging miR-125b-5p, increasing HMGA2 expression and enhancing inflammatory responses in keratinocytes. DSG1-AS1 also facilitated NETs formation in neutrophils, which damaged keratinocytes. Clinically, serum levels of DSG1-AS1, miR-125b-5p, and HMGA2 correlate with disease severity and NETs, highlighting their potential as biomarkers.

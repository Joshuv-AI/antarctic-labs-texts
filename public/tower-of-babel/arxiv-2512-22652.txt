Abstract
In this work, we study the realisation of unitarity-based cutting rules for primordial cosmological correlators computed within the Schwinger–Keldysh path integral formalism. While cutting rules have been previously derived for wavefunction coefficients, here we examine them directly at the level of cosmological observables expressed diagrammatically. The resulting rules closely resemble those familiar from flat-space scattering amplitudes, but with an additional subtlety: in order to express the discontinuity of a correlator as the product of lower-order correlators, one must introduce a specific combinations of diagrams which do not appear in the computation of observables themselves. We explicitly verify these rules for several classes of correlators, both at tree level and with loop corrections, arising from theories involving different types of interactions.

3
Cosmological Optical Theorem

3.1
Unitarity in Flat Space

If the time evolution of a quantum field theory is generated by an operator
U(t_{f},t_{i})
satisfying:

UU^{\dagger}=1,

(3.1)

then such a quantum field theory is said to be
unitary
. This idea, although very simple, allows to construct constraints about the theory and its relevant interactions. The
optical theorem
in flat space, depends on the unitarity of the
S-
matrix, defined as:

\langle f|i\rangle=S_{i\rightarrow f}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}\rightarrow-\infty\\
t_{f}\rightarrow\infty\end{subarray}}U(t_{f},t_{i}).

(3.2)

Moreover, we expand the
S-
matrix as follows,

S=1+i\mathcal{T},

(3.3)

where the identity corresponds to the
forward scattering
, representing states propagating without any interaction, and
\mathcal{T}
is the interacting part, typically calculated perturbatively through a diagrammatic expansion. Imposing the unitarity condition (
3.1
), then (
3.3
) yields,

SS^{\dagger}=1\Rightarrow\mathcal{T}-\mathcal{T^{\dagger}}=i\mathcal{T}\mathcal{T^{\dagger}}.

(3.4)

By operating with the final and initial states
\bra{f}
and
\ket{i}
on the left and right, respectively, whilst inserting a complete set of intermediate states on the right we obtain,

\mathcal{M}(i\rightarrow f)-\mathcal{M}^{\ast}(f\rightarrow i)=i\sum_{X}\int d\Pi_{X}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(k_{\text{in}}-k_{X})\mathcal{M}^{\ast}(f\rightarrow X)\mathcal{M}(i\rightarrow X),

(3.5)

usually referred to as the generalised optical theorem. This relationship imposes a constraint on the value of the amplitudes. Moreover, by performing a perturbative analysis, one finds a set of cutting rules
[
27
]
, where we diagrammatically slice loops to obtain trees or a lower number of loops.

3.2
Unitarity in de Sitter

An

[

,
17
]
. Nonetheless, recent progress proposes a de Sitter

-

-

-

-

[

,
57
]
. From a particle physics perspective, in
[

Despite these limitations, it is possible to elude them in order to obtain a cosmological version for the generalised optical theorem. In
[

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\tau,\tau_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}\left\{\exp\left(-i\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\tilde{H}_{I}(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=1-i\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}d\tau_{1}\tilde{H}_{I}(\tau_{1})-\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\tilde{H}_{I}(\tau_{1})\tilde{H}_{I}(\tau_{2})\Theta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{i}{2}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}d\tau_{1}d\tau_{2}\tilde{H}_{I}(\tau_{1})\tilde{H}_{I}(\tau_{2})\tilde{H}_{I}(\tau_{3})\Theta(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})\Theta(\tau_{2}-\tau_{3})+\cdots.

Analogously to flat space this corresponds to a non-perturbative relation. The structure of

8
Conclusions

In this work, we studied Schwinger-Keldysh cutting rules, motivated by unitarity, acting directly on in-in cosmological correlation functions. We first examined a single tree-level four-point exchange diagram and showed that its discontinuity factorises into products of three-point correlators. We then generalised this result to an arbitrary linear-chain graph configuration with
V
vertices, arguing that more elaborate topologies inherit the same factorisation pattern: once a propagator is cut, the two resulting subgraphs behave analogously to the four-point case and factorise recursively. Finally, we analysed a one-loop diagram, verifying that the factorisation persists after integration over the loop momentum, thereby confirming that the cutting rules extend beyond tree level.

In all cases, the inspection of discontinuities can be carried out diagrammatically, that is, by verifying that the result can be written, according to the Feynman rules, as products of lower-point diagrams, as illustrated in Section
4
. However, in order to express these results explicitly as discontinuities of lower-point correlators, a new algebraic object is required, referred to in this work as
barred
correlators. The need for these objects arises from the presence of vertex sign indices on the right-hand side of Eq. (
6.28
), which are represented diagrammatically in Step
4
of the cutting recipe. These barred correlators, which are simply linear combinations of diagrams that do not contribute to physical correlation functions, provide an intuitive framework for organising computations, allowing discontinuity operations to be decomposed into fundamental building blocks. In this way, our framework offers a streamlined formulation of cosmological cutting rules that can be systematically applied to higher-order and higher-loop calculations. Moreover, barred correlators and their relation to in-in correlators—particularly through the interchange of real and imaginary parts—hint at a deeper structural insight that warrants further investigation. In particular, their algebraic structure bears a resemblance to features of the
r/a
(Keldysh) basis, in which advanced fields play an analogous role. Exploring this possible connection may provide new insights into inflationary cosmological correlators.

Appendix C
Time Derivatives on Internal Propagators

In this section, we show how time derivatives act on bulk-to-bulk propagators under temporal integrals. For this, we only consider the
G_{++}
case, as
G_{--}
will be the complex conjugate of the result. Moreover, the propagators
G_{+-}
and
G_{-+}
do not require this analysis, since there are no Heaviside step functions present.

C.1
Single Derivative

Consider the following integral:

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau_{1}}G_{++}(k,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\left[u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})+u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\Big[\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})+u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d}\quad+\partial_{\tau}u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)+u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\Big],

(C.1)

where
f(\tau)
and
g(\tau^{\prime})
are arbitrary functions. Additionally, note that
\partial_{\tau}\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})=\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
and
\partial_{\tau}\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)=-\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
. Replacing it in the above, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau_{1}}G_{++}(k,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\Big[\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})+u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}}\quad+\partial_{\tau}u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)-u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\Big].

(C.2)

The mode functions accompanied by the Dirac delta functions are going to cancel after performing the integral, leaving only the Heaviside function dependent terms,

\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau_{1}}G_{++}(k,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\\
=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\Big[\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})+\partial_{\tau}u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\Big].

(C.3)

Thus, for a single temporal derivative,
G_{++}
reads

\partial_{\tau}G_{++}(k,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})+\partial_{\tau}u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau).

(C.4)

C.2
Two Derivatives

Let us analyse the case when two temporal derivatives are present,

\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau_{1}f(\tau_{1})\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau_{2}g(\tau_{2})\partial_{\tau_{1}}\partial_{\tau_{2}}G_{++}(k;\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\\
=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\left[u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})+u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\right].

(C.5)

It will be notationally convenient to use
G_{>}(k,\tau,\tau^{\prime})=u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})
and
G_{<}(k,\tau,\tau^{\prime})=u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})
. Thus, a direct computation will yield,

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{++}(k,\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\left[\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{>}+\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{<}\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}}\left.-\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})(\partial_{\tau}-\partial_{\tau^{\prime}})(G_{>}-G_{<})-(G_{>}-G_{<})\partial_{\tau}\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\Bigg[\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{>}+\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{<}

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}\phantom{=\Bigg[\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})}+\left(u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime}(\tau^{\prime})-u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}^{*\prime}(\tau^{\prime})\right)\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)}\phantom{=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d}+\frac{f^{\prime}(\tau)}{f(\tau)}g(\tau^{\prime})\left(u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})-u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\right)\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\Bigg],

(C.6)

where an integration by parts was done to obtain the last expression. Similarly to
eq.

C.2
, we can simplify the above by performing one integral

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\left[\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{>}+\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{<}\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})[}+\frac{i}{c}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)g(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})\left[\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{>}+\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{<}\right]

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau f(\tau)\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}g(\tau^{\prime})[}+\frac{i}{c}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau_{f}}d\tau^{\prime}f(\tau)g(\tau^{\prime})\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime}).

(C.7)

Then, ignoring normalisation constants, double temporal derivatives on bulk-to-bulk propagators reads

\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{++}(k,\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})+\partial_{\tau}u^{*}_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau)\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}u_{\mathbf{k}}(\tau^{\prime})\theta(\tau^{\prime}-\tau)+i\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime}).

(C.8)

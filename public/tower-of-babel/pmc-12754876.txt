Validity and reliability of the Turkish version of the revised illness perception questionnaire for oral health (IPQ-R-OH) in patients with dental caries
Authors: Elif Varli Tekingur, Gul Yildiz Telatar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDental caries is a common oral disease with significant implications for oral health. To develop effective interventions, assessing oral health perceptions in the Turkish population is essential. Despite this, there is currently no validated Turkish version of the Revised Illness Perception Questionnaire for Oral Health (IPQ-R-OH). The purpose of this study is to perform validity and reliability analyses on the Turkish version of the IPQ-R-OH.MethodsA cross-sectional design was employed, and data were collected between September and November 2022 at the Faculty of Dentistry, Recep Tayyip Erdogan University. Participants aged 18–65 were recruited using a convenience sampling method. Individuals who were literate, systemically healthy, and not pregnant or lactating were included. Following a standardized clinical oral examination based on WHO criteria, eligible participants completed a structured questionnaire that included socio-demographic and clinical variables along with the IPQ-R-OH.ResultsA total of 440 participants (54.8% women; mean age = 31.1 years) were included in the analysis. Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) led to the removal of six items, resulting in a 30-item, seven-factor structure explaining 59.5% of the total variance. Internal consistency coefficients (Cronbach’s alpha) ranged from 0.58 to 0.86 across the factors. Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) supported the construct validity of the revised model, with acceptable fit indices (RMSEA = 0.060, NFI = 0.900, CFI = 0.901, and χ²/df = 2.573).ConclusionsThe translated version of IPQ-R-OH was found to be valid and reliable for the Turkish population. The Turkish version of IPQ-R-OH is appropriate for patients with dental caries to evaluate their perceptions of the disease, their expectations from treatment, and their psychometric properties.Keywords: Revised illness perception questionnaire, Factor analysis, Dental caries, Turkish

Introduction

Patients aspire to explain their illness in the light of their knowledge, beliefs, values, personal experiences, and needs. The experience of illness may vary from person to person. Factors arising from this difference play an important role in individuals’ responses to their illnesses [1, 2]. Leventhal et al. created models for diseases and life-threatening conditions, claiming that patients developed their own “self-regulatory model” [3, 4]. These include the patient’s personal beliefs regarding the duration, symptoms, and consequences of their illness. The patient’s personal thoughts, evaluations, and perceptions about illness, as well as behavioral and emotional reactions, determine the quality of life [5].

Dental caries is the interaction over time between cariogenic microflora, a diet rich in fermentable carbohydrates and host factors [6, 7]. Further investigations have revealed more contributing factors that facilitate the progression of dental caries and enhance caries susceptibility [7]. Thus, dental caries is described as a disease with multiple causes, emerging from the interaction of both environmental and inherited factors, including biological, societal, behavioral, and psychological influences [8].

Dental caries, which is considered the most common chronic oral disease, is estimated to affect almost every individual [9]. As the most important risk factor for dental caries, dental caries was recognized as a behavioral disease in 1993 due to its association with attitudes and behaviors [10]. For example, a diet rich in carbohydrates is considered an important risk factor for caries formation in individuals with poor oral hygiene, but the same diet is a weakly effective risk factor in people who have regular oral care and use fluoride preparations [11]. In addition, it is emphasized that diet al.one is not sufficient as a determining factor in the assessment of caries risk, the patient’s oral hygiene, and carbohydrate consumption frequency and form should also be considered [12].

While illness perception is shaped by individual experiences, it is also influenced by broader sociocultural, educational, and systemic factors that vary significantly across populations. Research suggests that individuals from different cultural backgrounds may perceive the causes, severity, or controllability of oral diseases in divergent ways due to differences in health literacy, access to oral health services, cultural beliefs about disease etiology, and prior exposure to dental care systems [13, 14]. For example, patients in populations with limited access to preventive oral care may be more likely to perceive dental caries as an inevitable or untreatable condition, which may influence both health-seeking behavior and treatment adherence [9, 15]. Additionally, educational background plays a pivotal role in how symptoms are interpreted and how risk factors such as diet or hygiene are understood and managed [9, 10, 12].

The Illness Perception Questionnaire developed by Weinman et al. is scientifically based on Leventhal’s theory of disease representation. It provides quantitative measurement of identity, causes, consequences, duration, and cure/control areas, as well as disease symptoms [16]. Identity detects whether a disease is present or absent and may also relate to the name of the condition with certain indications or symptoms. Causes are the patient’s thoughts regarding the etiology of the disease. Duration evaluates a patient’s expectations of the kind and duration of the disease. Similarly, consequences are the patient’s impressions of the physical, social, or economic implications of the disease. A fifth dimension, known as cure/control, refers to ideas about the treatment’s or therapy’s potential to either control or cure the disease [17]. Moss-Morris et al. revised this questionnaire, determining that it did not represent the meanings patients attributed to their illness in the disease type section of the questionnaire. They divided duration into two parts (timeline-acute/chronic and timeline-cyclical) regarding the relative degree of chronicity of the disease and the duration of variability of the symptoms of the disease. In addition, considering that the control section is provided personally or with treatment, they divided this section into two, personal control and treatment control [18].

The original version of the IPQ-Revised (IPQ-R) was in English. Villalobos-Galvis et al. developed the Revised Illness Perception Questionnaire for Oral Health (IPQ-R-OH) and validated its Spanish version in terms of dental caries [17]. In the related publication, each question was originally presented in English and translated into Spanish. Mafla et al. used the IPQ-R-OH to assess patients with periodontitis. The exploratory factor analysis in these studies revealed seven factors: timeline–cyclical, control, illness coherence, timeline-chronic, consequences, hopelessness and emotional representations, concluding that the 36-item 7-factor model was better than the original 38-item model [19].

To our knowledge, there are no studies evaluating the Turkish version of this questionnaire in terms of oral health. Specifically, the objectives of this study were twofold: (1) to translate the IPQ-R-OH into the Turkish language using a standardized forward–backward translation method; (2) to investigate the psychometric properties of the Turkish version of the IPQ-R-OH in patients with dental caries. The study hypothesised that the questionnaire measuring the perception of disease in patients with dental caries would be valid and reliable in Turkish society.

Materials and methods

Participants

Participants were recruited using a convenience sampling method, which is a practical and cost-effective approach for data collection when time or resources are limited. Before the study, the sample size was calculated at a 95% confidence level using the “G. Power-3.1.9.2” program. As a result of the analysis, α = 0.05, standardized effect size was taken as 0.25 (moderate) for comparison of three or more groups due to the absence of a similar study in this area before, and the minimum sample size was calculated as 343 with a theoretical power of 0.95. To cover possible data loss, a decision was made to utilize 450 samples for the study from September to November 2022, from those admitted to the Department of Restorative Dentistry, Dentistry Faculty, at Recep Tayyip Erdogan University.

All participants underwent a detailed intraoral examination under a reflector light using a mouth mirror and a round-tipped probe, as well as an examination of tooth surfaces. The criteria of the World Health Organization (WHO) were taken as the basis for the diagnosis of caries. These criteria were as follows: lesions in the pit and fissure, cavities under the enamel surface, and infected teeth with softened walls [15]. The assessment of past caries examination was carried out using the decayed-missing-filled teeth (DMFT) index values, which were computed following the WHO criteria. Then, the questionnaire including socio-demographic, clinical characteristics and IPQ-R-OH was asked to the participants in face-to-face interviews and completed.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

Informed consent forms were obtained and signed during the interviews from all participants aged 18–65 for our study.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for our research included being literate, aged between 18 and 65 years, being systemically healthy, not being pregnant or lactating, and not having a history of chemotherapy or radiotherapy. The exclusion criterion was the presence of white and brown color differences in which the probe was not attached to the teeth during the intraoral examination of the patients.

Revised illness perception questionnaire for oral health (IPQ-R-OH)

The questionnaires administered to the individuals included socio-demographic and clinical characteristics, seeking information regarding age, sex, educational status, socioeconomic status, allergy to any substances, regular drug use, and smoking habits. The participants were also asked about the frequency of tooth brushing, floss use, frequency of dental visits, consumption of sugary snacks, and whether they had dental sensitivity.

The original version of the IPQ-R-OH consisted of three parts: illness identity, opinions about the related illness, and causes of illness. The first part, the disease symptoms section, which measures the illness identity, consists of 18 items that question the symptoms associated with dental caries, such as “dental pain,” “tooth sensitivity,” “mild-to-sharp pain when eating or drinking something sweet, either hot or cold,” and “pain when biting” [13]. The second part was evaluated in seven factors with 36 items measuring the perception of illness. The third section measured the causes of illness with 18 items. Both the second and third parts were scored on a 5-point Likert scale. The participants answered the questions by choosing one of five response options (“strongly disagree,” “disagree,” “undecided,” “agree,” and “strongly agree”).

Translation and Cross-Cultural adaptation of the IPQ-R-OH

Before the study, the developer of the IPQ-R-OH was contacted and permission to use the questionnaire was obtained. The original questionnaire was translated into Turkish using the cultural adaptation process as indicated in the literature [14].

Translation

The items of the original questionnaire were translated from English to Turkish by two dentists and one professional translator independently, resulting in three translations.

Synthesis

A synthesized Turkish questionnaire was created by comparing three questionnaires translated into Turkish. The expert committee combined the three Turkish questionnaires in a single translation.

Back translation

The Turkish questionnaire was translated back to its original language by a professional translator.

Expert committee review

The expert committee compared the back-translated English questionnaire with the original to evaluate the compatibility of the translation. The committee consisting of a psychologist and four dentistry specialists examined the original and the Turkish version of the IPQ-R-OH and created its final version.

Pretesting

The translated questionnaire was pre-tested with 30 patients using standard interview techniques.

Afterwards, the necessary arrangements were made and the Turkish version of the Revised Illness Perception Questionnaire for Oral Health, titled “Revised Illness Perception Questionnaire for Oral Health in Patients with Dental Caries” was obtained.

Statistical analysis

The conformity of the data set to normal distribution was checked using the Shapiro-Wilk test, indicating that the total scores of the questionnaire were not normally distributed (test statistics: 0.955 and p <.001). Possible extreme values were determined by drawing a box plot because the assumption of normal distribution was not provided. Examination of the graph for the total scores showed that the 25th, 78th, 82nd, 94th, 132nd, 154th, 175th, 204th, 274th, and 435th observations were extreme values. These observations were removed from the data set because they might cause bias in the analysis, and the normality of distribution was rechecked. Descriptive analyses were performed to generate frequency distributions of the questionnaire variables. Mean and standard deviation (SD) for continuous data were calculated.

Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) was initially performed for the validity analysis of the IPQ-R-OH. Before the implementation of EFA, the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) test was used to test whether the sample size was suitable for the factor analysis, with a value ranging between 0 and 1 [20]. The KMO value was determined as 0.776, indicating that the sample was sufficient to perform factor analysis. The Bartlett sphericity test was used to analyze whether the data matrix was the identity matrix and whether the correlation between the variables was sufficient for factor analysis, in other words, to assess the suitability of the data structure for factor analysis. According to the results of KMO test and Bartlett sphericity test, the data set was found suitable for factor analysis (p <.001). To reveal the factor pattern of the IPQ-R-OH, the principal component analysis was used as a factorization method. For rotation, varimax, one of the vertical rotation methods, was employed. In the EFA performed to reveal the factor pattern of the questionnaire, the acceptance level for factor load values was determined as 0.30.

Assumptions were checked and Spearman correlations were used to investigate the relationships between the IPQ-R-OH and its factors. Reliability analyses were conducted to test the consistency of the questionnaire and its factors based on the answers of the participants.

Cronbach’s alpha reliability test was used for reliability analysis. To demonstrate their validity, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), one of the structural equation modeling analyses, was used. CFA analysis was performed using a maximum likelihood approach.

The analyses were performed using the IBM SPSS AMOS, version 23 and IBM SPSS 25 (IBM, SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA) software.

Results

The analysis showed that the total scores fit the normal distribution (test statistics: 0.995 and p =.144). Thus, 440 people, 241 (54.8%) women and 199 (45.2%) men, participated in the study. The average age of participants was 31.1 and their DMFT index was found to be 6.91 ± 4.08 (mean ± standard deviation). The findings indicated that 11.1% of patients visited their dentist consistently every six months, 20.7% yearly, and 68.2% attended only when a dental problem occurred. Regarding sugar intake consumption, 34.1% of participants had less than one per day, contrasting with 65.9% who consumed more than one. As for oral hygiene, 49.8% brushed once daily, 43.4% twice or more, and 6.8% either never or rarely.

Exploratory factor analysis (EFA)

In the analysis conducted for seven factors, six items were removed from the questionnaire. These items did not exceed 30% of the common variance explained, and the factor loadings of items present on more than one factor had a difference of less than 0.10. These were questions 9, 20, 21, 23, 36, and 2. After removing the identified items, the remaining factor loadings met the acceptable threshold, ranging from 0.36 to 0.88.

As a result of varimax rotation, seven factors explaining 59.50% of the total variance were identified (Table 1).

Reliability analysis

The internal consistency of the IPQ-R-OH factors was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficients. The timeline-cyclical (0.80), treatment control (0.81), and emotional representations (0.86) factors demonstrated high reliability. Similarly, the illness coherence (0.76), timeline (acute/chronic) (0.70), and consequences (0.75) factors also showed acceptable internal consistency. The personal control demonstrated adequate reliability with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.58. The IPQ-R-OH was acceptable with a Cronbach’s alpha reliability coefficient of 0.70 [21], (Table 3).

Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA)

According to the CFA, the structural equation modeling results of the questionnaire were significant at the p <.001 level and were related to the 30-item questionnaire structure that formed the questionnaire. To enhance model fit, a series of covariance modifications were conducted. When similarities or correlations were observed between variables within the same factor, these relationships were modeled by establishing covariances among the error terms. During the improvement process, variables that decreased fit were identified, and new covariances were created for those with high covariance among the residual values (e29-e30;e25-e26; e21-e23;e16-17; e16-e18; e7-e8; e2-e4), as illustrated in Fig. 1. Acceptable fit index values were obtained in both the initial and the revised calculations following the model modifications, as presented in Table 4. Examination of the goodness of fit indices for the questionnaire developed based on the results of the multi-factor model CFA yielded the following values: RMSEA = 0.060, NFI = 0.900, CFI = 0.90, and χ²/df was at an acceptable level with the value of 2.573.

Discussion

The dental caries follow a predictable course and exhibit a wide range of symptoms. Early recognition and accurate perception of dental caries are critical for preventing progression, complications, and improving adherence to preventive recommendations [22]. Hence, this study aimed to adapt the Revised Illness Perception Questionnaire for Oral Health (IPQ-R-OH) into Turkish and to evaluate its psychometric properties among individuals with dental caries. The main findings of this study demonstrate that the Turkish version of the IPQ-R-OH exhibits acceptable construct validity and reliability, supporting its use as a culturally appropriate tool for assessing illness perceptions related to oral health.

The majority of patients included in our study were female. This result is similar to studies conducted by Villalobos-Galvis et al. and Mafla et al. Additionally, avarage age of the study participants was similar to these studies [17, 19]. Many epidemiological studies have reported that past caries experience is a significant risk indicator for future caries [12, 23, 24]. Therefore, the dental caries status of the individuals included in our study was determined using the DMFT index.

Exploratory factor analysis (EFA) revealed a seven-factor structure explaining 59.5% of the total variance, with factor loadings ranging from 0.36 to 0.88 [25, 26]. Six items were excluded due to low factor loadings or overlapping variance, similar to other adaptations of the scale where item reduction was necessary for model optimization [5, 14, 27, 28]. In our study, the survey items of the perception section were grouped under seven factors as a result of varimax rotation. Similarly, Armay et al. [5], Villalobos-Galvis et al. [17], and Pasternak et al. [29] created a 7-factor structure in the perception section. However, Giannousi et al. [27] established a 6-factor construct and Chen et al. [28] obtained a 5-factor structure. The timeline-cyclical, emotional representations and consequences factors consisted of similar items both in the original questionnaire and the Spanish version [17]. On the other hand, there were differences in the compositions of other factors. Two questions, “My actions will not affect the outcome of my dental problem” and “Nothing I do will affect my dental problem” were included in the personal control part both in the original questionnaire and our analysis, but they were included in the hopelessness section of the other study [17]. This finding may be explained by patients’ belief that they could have personal control over dental caries.

In the present study, significant correlations were observed among several factors of the IPQ-R-OH, indicating that different dimensions of illness perception are interrelated. The strongest positive correlation was found between personal control and treatment control, suggesting that individuals who believe they can personally influence their oral health are also more confident in the effectiveness of treatment. This alignment between internal and external control beliefs highlights a positive illness perception profile, which may facilitate better self-care behaviors and treatment adherence. Additionally, a moderate positive correlation was found between timeline-cyclical and emotional representations, indicating that perceiving dental caries as a recurrent problem may be associated with heightened negative emotions such as fear or frustration. Similar results have been reported in the Spanish-speaking community with dental caries [17]. Interestingly, a negative correlation was observed between treatment control and illness coherence in our sample, which may reflect a more complex cultural or cognitive dissonance, where greater understanding of the disease does not necessarily translate into trust in treatment. These findings underscore the importance of addressing patients’ emotional and cognitive representations in an integrated manner to support more effective oral health interventions.

In our study, the Cronbach alpha reliability coefficient values of the factors ranged from 0.58 to 0.86. Similar Cronbach’s alpha values have been reported between 0.60 and 0.85 [5]. Chen et al.‘s [28] Cronbach’s alpha values ranged from 0.60 to 0.85, while Villalobos-Galvis et al. [17] found values between 0.72 and 0.91. Our findings generally support the original seven-factor structure of the scale and demonstrate acceptable internal consistency for most factors. However, the Personal Control factor yielded a lower Cronbach’s alpha value consistent with findings from previous validation studies conducted in different linguistic and cultural settings. For instance, in the Spanish-language validation conducted by Villalobos-Galvis et al. (2017) in Colombia, the authors noted that control-related items (personal and treatment control, along with timeline-acute) tended to cluster together, forming a combined “Control” factor, which may reflect conceptual overlap or cultural differences in the perception of self-efficacy versus professional care in managing oral health. In the Turkish context, beliefs about illness control may be influenced by external variables such as reliance on healthcare providers, limited health literacy, and cultural perceptions of fatalism, all of which can reduce the internal consistency of this factor. Despite this, the overall scale structure remains robust. The slightly lower reliability of Personal Control should therefore not be viewed as a psychometric flaw, but rather as an indicator of cultural variation worth further exploration. From a clinical perspective, these findings highlight the importance of interpreting the Personal Control scores with caution and suggest the need for culturally sensitive health education strategies that address patients’ beliefs about their ability to influence their oral health outcomes.

The confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) showed an acceptable model fit (RMSEA = 0.060, CFI = 0.901, χ²/df = 2.573), consistent with previous validations of the scale in other languages [17, 28, 30]. For example, Chen et al. [28] and Villalobos-Galvis et al. [17] also reported comparable fit indices, confirming the robustness of the IPQ-R-OH factor structure across different cultural settings. Descriptive analysis of the IPQ-R-OH factors revealed that personal control had the highest mean score, indicating that most participants perceived themselves as capable of managing their oral health. In contrast, the lowest mean score was observed in the timeline-cyclical factor, suggesting that dental caries was not commonly perceived as a recurrent condition.

From a public health perspective, the present findings can inform the development of targeted preventive measures for dental caries. The high personal control scores observed in our sample suggest an opportunity to design community-based oral health promotion programs that reinforce individuals’ self-efficacy behaviors in maintaining oral health [10, 13]. Conversely, the low scores in the timeline-cyclical dimension indicate limited awareness of the recurrent nature of dental caries, highlighting the need for educational interventions and mass media campaigns to emphasize the importance of regular check-ups and long-term preventive strategies [9, 10].

Our study has several limitations. A convenience sampling approach was used, involving patients who presented to our dental clinic during the study period. As a result, the generalizability of the findings may be limited. The adaptation process may have been affected by cultural-social differences. In addition, the answers given by the patients may have been affected by the high number of questions. A short version of the questionnaire was made with different questions and it was not developed for oral diseases. We suggested that this short version can be developed for oral diseases as well. The shorter questionnaire allows for the investigation of illness perceptions across a wider range of patient groups and would be especially helpful in large population-based studies when illness perceptions are measured as just one component of a larger set of psychological structures [31]. Thus, more patients can be evaluated in a short time, both in clinical and epidemiologic studies.

Conclusion

According to our findings, the IPQ-R-OH is valid and reliable for the Turkish population, confirming our hypothesis that the questionnaire measuring illness perception in patients with dental caries can be effectively used in Turkish society. The 30-item questionnaire demonstrated a seven-factor structure with a good model fit, consistent with the original version and other cultural adaptations. Although most items were grouped similarly to the original questionnaire, some were included in different factors, reflecting minor cultural variations. The psychometric performance of the Turkish version was generally consistent with previous adaptations, while minor variations, particularly in the Personal Control dimension, highlight potential cultural influences on illness perception. These findings underscore the importance of considering cultural context when interpreting questionnaire results and developing patient-centered interventions. This study offers a useful instrument for future research and clinical practice aimed at improving oral health awareness. Further studies with larger and more diverse samples, and evaluations of shorter versions, may enhance its applicability in clinical and epidemiological settings.

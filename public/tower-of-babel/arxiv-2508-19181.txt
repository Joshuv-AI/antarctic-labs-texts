An alternate approach to bilinear rough singular integrals
Ankit Bhojak, Saurabh Shrivastava

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to provide a new approach to address the L p − L^{p}- boundedness of bilinear rough singular integral operators. This approach relies on local Fourier series expansion of input functions leading to trilinear estimates with desired decay in the frequency parameter. This approach departs from the existing methods of the wavelet decomposition of the multiplier employed in the work of Grafakos, He and Honzík and in a series of subsequent papers in the context of bilinear rough singular integrals. With this new approach, we provide a new and self contained proof of L p − L^{p}- boundedness of bilinear rough singular integral operators in dimension one for the optimal range of exponents. Furthermore, this approach allows us to prove sharp L p − L^{p}- estimates for maximally truncated bilinear rough singular integrals when the kernel is supported away from the diagonal in the plane.

1.
Introduction

The study of singular integrals of the form
Tf(x)=p.v.\int K(y)f(x-y)dy
is one of the central themes in harmonic analysis. For the kernel
K(y)=\Omega(\frac{y}{|y|})|y|^{-d}
with
\Omega\in L\log L({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1})
, satisfying mean zero condition, the boundedness of the corresponding operator
T
on
L^{p},\;1<p<\infty
dates back to the work of Calderón and Zygmund
[
2
]
. A different proof of the
L^{p}-
estimates of
Tf
, based on a double dyadic decomposition of the kernel and the Fourier transform estimates, was obtained by Duoandikoetxea and Rubio de Francia
[
10
]
. The issue of weak type estimates at the endpoint
p=1
was addressed by Christ
[
4
]
and Hoffman
[
22
]
separately for dimension two and later by Seeger
[
32
]
for all dimensions.

Let
\Omega\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{2d-1})
be with mean value zero condition
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2d-1}}\Omega(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)=0
, where
d\sigma
is the surface measure on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2d-1}
. The bilinear rough singular integral operator
T_{\Omega}
is defined as

T_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\Omega\left(\frac{(y_{1},y_{2})}{|(y_{1},y_{2})|}\right){|(y_{1},y_{2})|^{-2d}}f_{1}(x-y_{1})f_{2}(x-y_{2})\;dy_{1}dy_{2}.

Coifman and Meyer
[
6
, Theorem I]
showed that if
\Omega
is a function of bounded variation on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, then
T_{\Omega}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})\times L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
for
(p_{1},p_{2},p)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
, where

\mathcal{H}^{q}=\left\{(p_{1},p_{2},p):\;1<p_{1},p_{2}<\infty,\;\frac{1}{2}<p<\infty,\;\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p},\;\text{and}\;\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}<2\right\},\;1\leq q\leq\infty.

In higher dimensions, Kenig and Stein
[
25
]
and Grafakos and Torres
[
18
]
proved the boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
, for
\Omega
being a Lipschitz function, in the larger range of exponents in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
.

We note that when
\Omega
is an odd function on
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, the operator
T_{\Omega}
can be expressed as

T_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\Omega(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})BH_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)d\sigma(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}),

where
BH_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}
is the bilinear Hilbert transform defined by

BH_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{1}(x-\theta_{1}t)f_{2}(x-\theta_{2}t)\frac{dt}{t}.

The boundedness of
BH_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}
was proved in the seminal works of Lacey and Thiele
[
26
,
27
]
. By exploiting the uniform boundedness of
H_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}

[
17
,
28
]
, the authors in
[
7
]
established the boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
for
(p_{1},p_{2},p)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
satisfying the condition
\max\left\{\left|\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}}\right|,\left|\frac{1}{p_{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right|,\left|\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right|\right\}<\frac{1}{2}
whenever the even part of
\Omega
lies in the Hardy space
H^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
.

The first complete result for the bilinear operator
T_{\Omega}
without any smoothness assumption on
\Omega
was obtained by Grafakos, He and Honzík in
[
14
]
. They showed that
T_{\Omega}
is bounded for exponents in the range
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
for
\Omega\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}}^{2d-1})
. Their breakthrough idea was to apply a double dyadic decomposition of the kernel as in
[
10
]
and to decompose the multiplier further by employing a tensor-type compactly supported wavelet basis with vanishing moments. These ideas coupled with combinatorial arguments led them to an
L^{2}\times L^{2}\to L^{1}-
estimate with appropriate decay in the frequency parameter whenever
\Omega\in L^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{2d-1})
. Further, since the bilinear Calderón-Zygmund theory from
[
18
]
behaves well for
\Omega\in L^{\infty}
, an interpolation argument for bilinear operators applied to the two type of estimates mentioned as above, leads to the sharp
L^{p}-
boundedness results for
T_{\Omega}
when
\Omega\in L^{\infty}
.

Grafakos, He and Honzík
[
14
]
also proved the
L^{p}-
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
in the local
L^{2}
range:
2<p_{1},p_{2},<\infty,\;1\leq p\leq 2
for
\Omega\in L^{2}
. This result was further extended to all
\Omega\in L^{q}
for
q>\frac{4}{3}
in
[
16
]
. Building upon the
(2,2,1)-
inequality from
[
14
]
, He and Park
[
21
]
improved the range of boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
to exponents in
\mathcal{H}^{q}
for
\Omega\in L^{q},\;q>\frac{4}{3}
. In doing so, they developed bilinear Calderón-Zygmund theory further which is suitable for all
1<q\leq\infty
. We refer the reader to
[
11
,
12
]
for multilinear extensions of this result. In
[
15
]
, the authors showed that
\mathcal{H}^{q}
is the largest open range for which the operator
T_{\Omega}
is bounded for
\Omega\in L^{q},\;1<q\leq\infty
. Finally, Dosidis and Slavíková
[
9
]
showed that
T_{\Omega}
is also bounded in the range
\mathcal{H}^{q}
whenever
\Omega\in L^{q},\;1<q\leq\infty
. In this work, they utilized the boundedness of shifted square functions, whose details may be found in
[
29
]
, to obtain an admissible growth estimates for Littlewood-Paley pieces of the operator
T_{\Omega}
in the Banach range of exponents for
\Omega\in L^{1}
. They also showed that the strong type boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
cannot hold outside the region
\mathcal{H}^{q}
for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
. However, the weak type boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
on the lines
p_{1}=1
,
p_{2}=1
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=2
remains open.

In this paper we provide an alternate approach to prove boundedness of the operator
T_{\Omega}
for the complete range of exponents
\mathcal{H}^{q}
for
\Omega\in L^{q},\;1<q\leq\infty
in dimension one. In particular, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<q\leq\infty
and
(p_{1},p_{2},p)\in\mathcal{H}^{q}
. Then, for all
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\Omega(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)=0
, we have

\|T_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Our proof of
Theorem

1.1
involves a standard spatial localization of the kernel to obtain local kernel of the form

K^{0}(y_{1},y_{2})=\Omega\left(\frac{(y_{1},y_{2})}{|(y_{1},y_{2})|}\right){|(y_{1},y_{2})|^{-2}}\beta(|(y_{1},y_{2})|),

where
\beta\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\operatorname{supp}(\beta)\subset(2^{-1},2)
. Let
T^{0}_{\Omega}
be the local operator corresponding to
K^{0}
defined by

T^{0}_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K^{0}(y_{1},y_{2})f_{1}(x-y_{1})f_{2}(x-y_{2})\;dy_{1}dy_{2}.

We will prove a trilinear estimate with a decay in the corresponding frequency parameter for the operator
T_{\Omega}^{0}
acting on functions that are Fourier localized on an annulus. More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<q\leq\infty
. Then, there exists
c>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
, we have

(1.1)

|\langle T^{0}_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2}),f_{3}\rangle|\lesssim\lambda^{-c}\|\Omega\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}\|f_{l_{1}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{l_{2}}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{l_{3}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})},

whenever
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f_{l_{1}}})\cup\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f_{l_{2}}})\subset\left\{\lambda\leq|\xi|\leq 2\lambda\right\}
and
\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f_{l_{3}}})\subset\left\{|\xi|\leq 2\lambda\right\}
for
\{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\}=\{1,2,3\}
.

Christ and Zhou
[
3
]
obtained the trilinear smoothing estimates in the context of bilinear maximal functions associated to a certain class of curves in the plane. The proof of
Theorem

1.2
is motivated by the ideas developed in
[
3
]
. The key idea in the proof involves a local Fourier series expansion of the test functions. This serves as a substitute to the wavelet decomposition of the multiplier as in
[
14
]
. Further, we perform polar decomposition of the local bilinear operator
T_{\Omega}^{0}
. A careful oscillatory phase analysis with respect to the spatial and radial variables in the polar decomposition of the operator
T_{\Omega}^{0}
leads to the trilinear estimate (
1.1
). The proof of
Theorem

1.2
is carried out in
Section

4
.

The decay estimate from
Theorem

1.2
is the key part in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
. Next, we obtain admissible growth estimates for the operator in the respective Banach and non-Banach set of exponents. The case of Banach exponents relies on the estimates for shifted square functions. We refer to
[
9
]
for more details about the shifted square functions. The admissible growth estimates for exponents in the non-Banach range are based on single scale estimates obtained in
Section

3
and the bilinear Calderón-Zygmund theory adapted to the boundary
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=2
. We refer the reader to
[
3
,
21
]
for a similar approach. The proof of
Theorem

1.1
is given in
Section

5
.

Next, we extend the
L^{p}-
boundedness of
T_{\Omega}
for
\Omega
belonging to a larger class of Orlicz spaces.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1\leq p_{1},p_{2},p<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}
. Let
A=A(p_{1},p_{2},p)
be defined as

A=1+\max\left\{\frac{1}{p_{1}},\frac{1}{p_{2}},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right\}.

Then, for all
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\Omega(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)=0
, we have

\|T_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L(LogL)^{A}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Observe that
Theorem

1.3
recovers the result proved in
[
8
]
. The proof of this theorem is given in
Section

6
.

Recently, Honzík, Lappas and Slavíková in
[
23
]
showed that the
L^{p}-
boundedness of the operator
T_{\Omega}
can be extended to a much larger range of exponents
(p_{1},p_{2},p)
for all
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
under the condition that
\Omega
is supported away from the diagonal
\{(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}:\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}\}
. We provide an alternate proof of their result by using the method of this paper. We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
1<q\leq\infty
and
(p_{1},p_{2},p)\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
. Then, for all
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
with mean zero condition
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\Omega(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)=0
and
\operatorname{supp}(\Omega)\subset\{(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}:|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\}
, we have

\|T_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Indeed, the methods of this are so streamlined that we require only minor modifications in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
to conclude
Theorem

1.4
. In this case we rely on the single scale estimate (
3.5
) instead of (
3.4
). The estimate (
3.5
) is adapted to incorporate the underlying support condition on
\Omega
in
Theorem

1.4
.

Finally, we show that the methods of this paper are powerful enough to obtain
L^{p}-
boundedness of the maximally truncated bilinear operators
T_{\Omega}^{*}
defined as follows

T_{\Omega}^{*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\sup_{\epsilon>0}\left|\int_{|(y_{1},y_{2})|>\epsilon}\Omega\left(\frac{(y_{1},y_{2})}{|(y_{1},y_{2})|}\right){|(y_{1},y_{2})|^{-2}}f_{1}(x-y_{1})f_{2}(x-y_{2})\;dy_{1}dy_{2}\right|.

The study of
L^{p}-
boundedness of the maximal operator
T_{\Omega}^{*}
was initiated in
[
1
]
. They exploited the method of wavelet decomposition of the multiplier from
[
14
]
to show that the operator
T_{\Omega}^{*}
is bounded in the domain
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
whenever
\Omega\in L^{\infty}
. This result was further improved in
[
13
]
and the optimal
L^{p}-
boundedness of the operator in the range
\mathcal{H}^{q}
for
\Omega\in L^{q}
was obtained in
[
31
]
. We demonstrate how our methods are applicable to obtaining
L^{p}-
bounds for rough maximal singular integral in the optimal range
\mathcal{H}^{q}
. We have the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
1<q\leq\infty
and
(p_{1},p_{2},p)\in\mathcal{H}^{q}
. Then, for all
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\Omega(\theta)d\theta=0
, we have

\|T_{\Omega}^{*}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Moreover, if
\operatorname{supp}(\Omega)\subset\{(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}:|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\}
, then we have

\|T_{\Omega}^{*}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})},

for
(p_{1},p_{2},p)\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
.

Remark 1.6
.

We note that
Theorem

1.5
recovers the result of
[
31
]
in dimension one. Furthermore, the result is new when
\Omega
is supported outside a neighborhood of the diagonal. This extends the
L^{p}-
boundedness result of
Theorem

1.4
for the maximal operator
T_{\Omega}^{*}
.

In order to prove
Theorem

1.5
, we need to consider the maximal operator
M_{\Omega}
defined as

M_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\sup_{R>0}\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{|(y_{1},y_{2})|\leq R}\left|\Omega\left(\frac{(y_{1},y_{2})}{|(y_{1},y_{2})|}\right)f_{1}(x-y_{1})f_{2}(x-y_{2})\right|dy_{1}dy_{2}.

The boundedness of
M_{\Omega}
in bilinear and multilinear cases was obtained in
[
1
]
and
[
13
]
respectively. Their proofs relied on two type of estimates and interpolation between them. The first one is an
L^{1}\times L^{1}\to L^{\frac{1}{2},\infty}-
estimate when
\Omega\in L^{\infty}
. This estimate was obtained by dominating the operator
M_{\Omega}
by the bilinear Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. The second one is an estimate for the Banach range of exponents obtained by dominating
M_{\Omega}
by a product of certain directional maximal functions. Their methods do not allow us to incorporate the support condition on
\Omega
in
Theorem

1.4
. In order to exploit this additional fact that
\Omega
is supported outside a neighborhood of the diagonal
\{\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}\}
, we require new estimates. We employ the strategy of proof of
Theorem

1.1
to
M_{\Omega}
and obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
1<q\leq\infty
and
(p_{1},p_{2},p)\in\mathcal{H}^{q}
. Then, for all
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
, we have

\|M_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Moreover, if
\operatorname{supp}(\Omega)\subset\{(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}:|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\}
, then we have

\|M_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}})}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p_{2}}({\mathbb{R}})},

for
(p_{1},p_{2},p)\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
.

We note that the first part in the theorem above with no additional assumption on the support of
\Omega
recovers the result proved in
[
1
]
. The second part of the theorem with the exponents lying in the improved range
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
is new. The details of proofs of
Theorem

1.5
and
Theorem

1.7
are given in
Section

8
and
Section

7
respectively.

2.
Notation and toolbox for bilinear operators

The notation
A\lesssim B
means that there exists a constant
C>0
(independent of
A
and
B
) such that
A\leq CB
.
For any multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})
and
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote

|\alpha|_{1}=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{d},\quad\text{and}\quad x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}.

The following is a bilinear version of Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem which appeared in
[
21
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
0<p_{1,k},p_{2,k},p_{k}\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p_{k}}=\frac{1}{p_{1,k}}+\frac{1}{p_{2,k}}
for
k=1,2,3
. Suppose that
T
is a bilinear operator having the mapping properties

\left\|T\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{p_{k},\infty\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}}\leq M_{k}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{p_{1,k}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{p_{2,k}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)},\quad k=1,2,3,

for Schwartz functions
f_{1},f_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for any
0<\theta_{k}<1,\;k=1,2,3
with
\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}=1
, and
0<p_{1},p_{2},p\leq\infty
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{\theta_{1}}{p_{1,1}}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{p_{1,2}}+\frac{\theta_{3}}{p_{1,3}},\quad\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{\theta_{1}}{p_{2,1}}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{p_{2,2}}+\frac{\theta_{3}}{p_{2,3}},\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta_{1}}{p_{1}}+\frac{\theta_{2}}{p_{2}}+\frac{\theta_{3}}{p_{3}},

we have

\left\|T\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{p,\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\lesssim M_{0}^{\theta_{0}}M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}M_{2}^{\theta_{2}}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}.

Moreover, if the convex hull of
\left\{\left(\frac{1}{p_{1,k}},\frac{1}{p_{2,k}}\right),\;k=1,2,3\right\}
forms a non-trivial triangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then

\left\|T\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\lesssim M_{0}^{\theta_{0}}M_{1}^{\theta_{1}}M_{2}^{\theta_{2}}\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|f_{2}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}.

Proposition 3.1

Consider the trilinear operator

We observe that for

\displaystyle\;\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}2^{-\frac{ck}{2}}\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{p_{2}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{p_{2}}.

We set

a_{HH}=\left|\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}\right|+\left|\frac{1}{p_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right|,\quad a_{HL}=\left|\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}\right|+\left|\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\right|,\quad a_{LH}=\left|\frac{1}{p_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right|+\left|\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\right|.

.
To obtain the estimate for
T_{\Omega^{-},k}^{I},\;I\in\{HL,LH,HH\}
, we use
Lemma

5.3
to get

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}T_{\Omega^{-},k}^{I}(f_{1},f_{2})\right\|_{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}k^{a_{I}}\|\Omega^{+}\|_{1}\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{p_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{p_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\Omega(\theta)|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}k^{a_{I}}\chi_{|\Omega(\cdot)|>2^{\frac{ck}{2}}}(\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{p_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\Omega(\theta)|\log(e+|\Omega(\theta)|)^{1+{a_{I}}}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{p_{2}}.

This concludes the proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

7.
Proof of
Theorem

1.7

We note that it is enough to estimate the operator

\sup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|K^{i}*(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})(x)|=\sup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|T_{\Omega}^{i}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|,

where
K^{i}(y_{1},y_{2})=\Omega\left(\frac{(y_{1},y_{2})}{|(y_{1},y_{2})|}\right)2^{2i}\beta_{i}(|(y_{1},y_{2})|)
with
\Omega\geq 0
and
\beta
is a compactly supported non-negative radial function which takes the value
1
on
[\frac{1}{2},2]
. Using the notation as in proof of
Theorem

1.1
, we write

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|T_{\Omega}^{i}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\sup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|T_{\Omega}^{i}(f_{1}*\phi_{i},f_{2}*\phi_{i})(x)\Big|+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sup\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\psi_{i+k},f_{2}*\phi_{i+k-10})(x)\Big|,

\displaystyle\;+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sup\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\phi_{i+k-10},f_{2}*\psi_{i+k})(x)\Big|+\sum_{a=-10}^{10}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sup\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\psi_{i+k+a},f_{2}*\psi_{i+k})(x)\Big|.

\displaystyle\;=:M^{LL}_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}M^{HL}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}M^{LH}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}M^{HH}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x).

Estimate for
M^{LL}_{\Omega}
:

The estimate for the first term on the right side of the expression above follows from the pointwise bound,

M^{LL}_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\lesssim\|\Omega\|_{1}Mf_{1}(x)Mf_{2}(x),

which is an easy consequence of the bound
|f*\phi_{i}(x-2^{-i}y)|\lesssim Mf(x)
, for any
y\in B(0,2)
.

Estimates for
M^{HL}_{\Omega,k}
,
M^{LH}_{\Omega,k}
, and
M^{HH}_{\Omega,k}
:

We note that by the scheme used in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, it is enough to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, the following holds true

(1)

For
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\leq 1
and
I\in\{HL,LH,HH\}
, there exists
c>0
such that

(7.1)

\displaystyle\|M^{I}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{c}\|\Omega\|_{1}\|f_{1}\|_{p_{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{p_{2}}.

(2)

For
1<q\leq\infty
, there exists
c>0
such that for
I\in\{HL,LH,HH\}
, we have

(7.2)

\displaystyle\|M_{\Omega,k}^{I}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{\frac{1}{2},\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{c}\|\Omega\|_{q}\|f_{1}\|_{1}\|f_{2}\|_{1},

for all
\Omega\in L^{q}({\mathbb{S}}^{1})
with
\operatorname{supp}(\Omega)\subset\{(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}:\;|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\}
.

(3)

For
1<q\leq\infty
, there exists
C>0
so that

(7.3)

\displaystyle\|M^{HL}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-Ck}\|\Omega\|_{q}\|f_{1}\|_{2}\|f_{2}\|_{\infty},

\displaystyle\|M^{LH}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-Ck}\|\Omega\|_{q}\|f_{1}\|_{\infty}\|f_{2}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\|M^{HH}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-Ck}\|\Omega\|_{q}\|f_{1}\|_{2}\|f_{2}\|_{2}.

Proof.

For convenience we set,

(7.4)

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}^{HL,i}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\psi_{i+k},f_{2}*\phi_{i+k-10})(x),

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}^{LH,i}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\phi_{i+k-10},f_{2}*\psi_{i+k})(x),

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}^{HH,i}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=-10}^{10}\Big|T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\psi_{i+k+a},f_{2}*\psi_{i+k})(x)\Big|

By the embeddings
\ell_{1}\subset\ell_{2}\subset\ell_{\infty}
, we have the following pointwise bounds

(7.5)

\displaystyle|M_{\Omega,k}^{I}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|T^{I,i}_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|,\quad I\in\{HL,LH,HH\},

(7.6)

\displaystyle|M_{\Omega,k}^{I}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|T^{I,i}_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad I\in\{HL,LH\}.

Hence, the estimate (
7.1
) follows from Minkowski’s and Hölder’s inequalities along with the bounds (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) for the shifted square function and shifted maximal function respectively.

The proof of the weak type estimate (
7.2
) is similar to the proof of Case 2 in
Lemma

5.4
. We point out the main differences and leave the details to the readers. We use the
(2,2,1)-
estimate (
7.3
) instead of
Lemma

5.3
for the corresponding term
I_{gg}
obtained after applying Calderón-Zygmund decomposition to the functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
. The analysis of the corresponding terms
I_{gb},I_{bg}
and
I_{bb}
are similar once we use the pointwise bound (
7.5
), with the only exception that we use the single scale estimate (
3.5
) instead of (
3.1
) for the estimate of
I_{bb}
.

The proof of the decay estimates (
7.3
) follows by using estimates (
7.6
) and (
7.5
) and proceeding exactly the same way as that in the proof of
Lemma

5.5
.
∎

8.
Proof of
Theorem

1.5

First, we note that

T_{\Omega}^{*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\leq\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\sum\limits_{i>j}T_{\Omega}^{i}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|+M_{|\Omega|}(f_{1},f_{2})(x).

By
Theorem

1.7
, it remains to estimate
\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\sum\limits_{i>j}T_{\Omega}^{i}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|
. We write

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\sum\limits_{i>j}T_{\Omega}^{i}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|\lesssim

\displaystyle\;T_{\Omega}^{LL,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}T_{\Omega,k}^{HL,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}T_{\Omega,k}^{LH,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle\;+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}T_{\Omega,k}^{HH,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x),

where

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}^{LL,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=

\displaystyle\;\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\sum\limits_{i>j}T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\phi_{i},f_{2}*\phi_{i})(x)\Big|,

\displaystyle T_{\Omega,k}^{HL,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=

\displaystyle\;\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\sum\limits_{i>j}T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\psi_{i+k},f_{2}*\phi_{i+k-10})(x)\Big|,

\displaystyle T_{\Omega,k}^{LH,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=

\displaystyle\;\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\sum\limits_{i>j}T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\phi_{i+k-10},f_{2}*\psi_{i+k})(x)\Big|,

\displaystyle T_{\Omega,k}^{HH,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{a=-10}^{10}\sup\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|\sum\limits_{i>j}T^{i}_{\Omega}(f_{1}*\psi_{i+k+a},f_{2}*\psi_{i+k})(x)\Big|.

Estimate for
T^{LL,*}_{\Omega}
:

Since the kernel
\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}K^{i}*(\phi_{i}\otimes\phi_{i})
is a Calderón-Zygmund kernel, the maximal operator
T_{\Omega}^{LL,*}
is bounded in the region
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}
by Cotlar’s inequality from
[
18
]
.

Estimate for
T^{I,*}_{\Omega,k},\;I\in\{HL,LH,HH\}
:

It is enough to show that
Lemma

7.1
also holds for
T^{I,*}_{\Omega,k}
instead of
M^{I,*}_{\Omega,k}
for
I\in\{HL,LH,HH\}
. Indeed, by employing the arguments used in the proof of
Lemma

7.1
, these estimates for
T^{I,*}_{\Omega,k}
follow once we establish the following two inequalities.

\displaystyle|T_{\Omega,k}^{I,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|T^{I,i}_{\Omega}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|,\quad I\in\{HL,LH,HH\},

\displaystyle|T_{\Omega,k}^{I,*}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|T^{I}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\right|+M_{HL}(T_{\Omega,k}^{I}(f_{1},f_{2}))(x)+M_{HL}(M_{\Omega,k}^{I}(f_{1},f_{2}))(x),\quad I\in\{HL,LH\}.

The first inequality is straightforward. Therefore, we provide the arguments for the second. We follow the notation introduced in (
7.4
) to write,

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\sum_{i>j}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\right|\leq\left|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\right|+\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\sum_{i\leq j}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\right|.

Thus, it is enough to estimate the second term on the right hand side in the expression above. The Fourier support property
\operatorname{supp}((T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2}))^{\wedge})\subset\{2^{j+k-2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{i+k+2}\}
implies that

\bigcup_{i\leq j}\operatorname{supp}((T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2}))^{\wedge})\subset\{|\xi|\leq 2^{j+k+2}\},

and thus, the second term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\sum_{i\leq j}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\phi_{j+k+2}*\left(\sum_{i\leq j}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\right)(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\phi_{j+k+2}*\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\right)(x)\right|+\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\phi_{j+k+2}*\left(\sum_{i>j}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\right)(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\phi_{j+k+2}*\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\right)(x)\right|+\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\phi_{j+k+2}*\left(\sum_{j+5>i>j}T^{I,i}_{\Omega,k}(f_{1},f_{2})\right)(x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{HL}(T_{\Omega,k}^{I}(f_{1},f_{2}))(x)+M_{HL}(M_{\Omega,k}^{I}(f_{1},f_{2}))(x).

This concludes the proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

Comprehensive bioinformatics analysis identifies DNA methylation signatures associated with immune evasion and predicts immunotherapy response in breast cancer
Authors: Yurong Cheng, Jing Wang, Dong Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer exhibits substantial immunological heterogeneity, and immune evasion is a key mechanism driving tumor progression and therapeutic resistance. However, robust epigenetic biomarkers reflecting immune escape at the genome-wide level remain lacking, limiting precise prognostic evaluation and immunotherapy stratification. Therefore, this study aimed to identify DNA methylation features associated with immune evasion and to develop a methylation-based score for prognostic assessment and immunotherapy response prediction in breast cancer.MethodsGenome-wide DNA methylation and transcriptomic data from The Cancer Genome Atlas Breast Invasive Carcinoma (TCGA-BRCA) cohort were analyzed to identify immune-evasion-related cytosine-phosphate-guanine (CpG) sites. A subset of functionally relevant CpGs was selected through differential methylation and correlation analyses, and an immune-evasion methylation score (IME-score) was constructed using principal component analysis (PCA). Immune infiltration, tumor microenvironment characteristics, pathway activity, and predicted immunotherapy response were systematically evaluated using Cell-Type Identification by Estimating Relative Subsets of RNA Transcripts (CIBERSORT), Estimation of Stromal and Immune Cells in Malignant Tumor Tissues Using Expression Data (ESTIMATE), Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA), and Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE), respectively. External validation was performed in an independent cohort.ResultsThe IME-score demonstrated high stability and reproducibility, and effectively stratified patient survival. Significant differences were observed between IME-score groups in immune cell infiltration patterns, tumor microenvironment scores, pathway enrichment profiles, and immune checkpoint expression. Notably, higher IME-scores were associated with increased TIDE scores and lower responder scores, indicating reduced predicted responsiveness to immunotherapy. These findings were consistently validated in independent datasets.ConclusionsThe IME-score represents a robust epigenetic indicator of immune evasion in breast cancer. It provides complementary value to existing biomarkers and may facilitate improved prognostic assessment and immunotherapy stratification.

Highlight box

Key findings

• A novel immune-evasion methylation score (IME-score) was developed using genome-wide DNA methylation profiles in breast cancer. The IME-score independently predicted overall survival, reflected immune microenvironment characteristics, and showed potential for predicting immunotherapy response.

What is known and what is new?

• DNA methylation plays an important role in regulating tumor–immune interactions and contributes to immune evasion in multiple cancers. In breast cancer, previous studies have mainly focused on individual genes or specific methylation events, while comprehensive methylation signatures associated with immune escape remain limited.

• This study systematically identified immune-evasion–related cytosine-phosphate-guanine sites at the genome-wide level and established an IME-score based on functionally relevant methylation features. The IME-score was associated with patient prognosis, immune cell infiltration, tumor microenvironment status, immune checkpoint expression, and predicted immunotherapy responsiveness, providing a novel epigenetic framework for immune stratification in breast cancer.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The IME-score may serve as a complementary biomarker for risk assessment and immunotherapy stratification in breast cancer. Future studies should validate its clinical utility in prospective cohorts and in patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy to support its translation into personalized treatment decision-making.

Introduction

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women worldwide and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, accounting for more than 2.3 million new cases and over 680,000 deaths annually (1,2). Beyond its genetic heterogeneity, breast cancer exhibits substantial immunological diversity, and immune evasion is now recognized as a central driver of tumor initiation, progression, and metastatic dissemination (3). Although immune checkpoint inhibitors have achieved meaningful clinical benefit in subsets of triple-negative breast cancer, overall response rates remain modest, and predictive biomarkers are still insufficient (4,5). These challenges underscore an unmet need to precisely characterize the molecular determinants of immune escape, particularly those that can be quantified and mechanistically interpreted at the epigenetic level.

Accumulating evidence over the past decade indicates that DNA methylation is a key regulator of tumor-immune interactions. Aberrant methylation patterns can silence genes involved in antigen presentation, shape interferon signaling, influence the expression of immune checkpoint molecules such as programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), and modulate the recruitment and activity of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (6,7). Several studies have linked methylation signatures with immune phenotypes or therapy responsiveness in various cancers, suggesting its potential as an immunogenomic biomarker (8). However, most existing investigations have focused on single genes, promoter-specific methylation, or pathway-restricted regions, limiting their capacity to reflect the broader, genome-scale epigenetic landscape associated with immune evasion (9). Notably, in breast cancer, no study has comprehensively delineated a methylation profile explicitly linked to immune escape or systematically examined its association with remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment.

These limitations highlight a methodological and conceptual gap. Current research lacks a dedicated immune-evasion-related DNA methylation signature for breast cancer and has not integrated methylation states with transcriptional regulation, immune cell infiltration, stromal-immune composition, and functional immune pathways within a unified analytical framework. Moreover, whether such methylation patterns hold predictive value for immunotherapy response remains largely unexplored in this malignancy. As immune checkpoint blockade gradually expands into broader breast cancer subtypes, establishing robust epigenetic predictors becomes increasingly important for guiding patient stratification and therapeutic decision-making.

Against this backdrop, the present study aims to identify genome-wide cytosine-phosphate-guanine (CpG) sites associated with immune escape and to develop a quantitative immune-evasion methylation score (IME-score) using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) breast cancer methylation and transcriptomic datasets. We then evaluate its relationships with patient survival, the tumor immune microenvironment, and predicted immunotherapy responsiveness. By providing an integrative epigenetic perspective on immune escape, this study seeks to offer a biologically grounded biomarker to facilitate more precise immunological stratification in breast cancer (Figure 1). We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1-0128/rc).

Methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. DNA methylation profiles, RNA-sequencing data, and corresponding clinical annotations for breast cancer were retrieved from the TCGA Breast Invasive Carcinoma (TCGA-BRCA) cohort(https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/projects/TCGA-BRCA). Probes with excessive missingness, cross-hybridization potential, or localization on sex chromosomes were removed to ensure analytic robustness. Batch effects arising from technical variability were adjusted using empirical Bayes approaches implemented in the limma or sva package. After harmonization of molecular data and clinical variables, samples were randomly allocated into training and validation sets at a 7:3 ratio to support model development and internal verification.

Identification of differentially methylated CpG sites

Differential methylation analysis comparing tumor and normal breast tissues was performed using linear modeling with empirical Bayes moderation. M-values were selected as the primary statistical measure to improve variance stabilization, while Δβ values were calculated to reflect absolute methylation differences. CpG sites meeting the thresholds of |Δβ| ≥0.1 and false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05 were designated as significantly altered. The overall distribution of methylation changes was visualized through volcano plots and heatmaps to characterize epigenetic divergence between malignant and non-malignant tissues.

Genomic annotation and functional context of CpG sites

Significant CpG sites were mapped to genomic features using the Illumina annotation framework, including promoter regions (TSS200, TSS1500), 5'/3'-untranslated regions (UTRs), gene bodies, CpG islands, shores, and shelves. To further explore regulatory potential, annotations from ENCODE and ChromHMM were integrated to identify CpGs overlapping with predicted enhancers, active chromatin states, or repressive elements. The genomic distribution of CpGs across these functional categories was summarized using pie charts or bar plots to reveal preferential epigenomic localization.

Correlation analysis between CpG methylation and gene expression

For each CpG site, Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated between methylation levels and the expression of the corresponding gene. CpG-gene pairs meeting r≤−0.5 and FDR <0.05 were retained for downstream modeling.

Construction of the IME-score

To construct the IME-score, a subset of CpG sites associated with immune evasion was selected through a stepwise filtering strategy. First, differentially methylated CpG sites between tumor and normal tissues were identified. Subsequently, CpG-gene pairs showing significant negative correlations between DNA methylation and gene expression (r≤−0.5, FDR <0.05) were retained to capture functionally relevant epigenetic regulation.

To further enrich for immune-evasion-related features, CpGs annotated to genes involved in immune-related pathways and tumor immune microenvironment regulation were selected based on gene annotation and established immune-related gene sets. Only this final set of immune-evasion-related CpG sites was retained for downstream analysis and used to construct the methylation matrix, which was then standardized across samples.

Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on the standardized matrix to reduce dimensionality and extract the dominant coordinated methylation pattern. The first principal component (PC1), representing the largest source of variation among the selected CpG sites, was extracted. The projection value of each sample onto PC1 was defined as the IME-score. The sign of PC1 was aligned with the overall methylation direction based on mean β-values, such that a higher IME-score corresponded to a higher overall methylation level across the selected CpG set.

Therefore, the IME-score represents a continuous composite methylation index that captures coordinated epigenetic variation across immune-evasion-related CpG sites, rather than the methylation level of a single CpG locus.

Bootstrap-based stability assessment

Bootstrap resampling was conducted 100 times. For each bootstrap sample, PCA was recalculated using the same CpG features. Correlation coefficients between bootstrap-derived PC1 vectors and the original PC1 were recorded, along with the proportion of variance explained by PC1.

Survival and prognostic analyses

Patients in the training cohort were dichotomized into high and low IME-score groups based on the median score. Overall survival was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier estimates with log-rank tests. Cox proportional hazards models were applied for univariate and multivariate analyses. Predictive accuracy was quantified using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves at 1, 3, and 5 years and Harrell’s concordance index.

Nomogram development and performance evaluation

A prognostic nomogram integrating IME-score and clinical variables was constructed using the rms package. Calibration curves were generated using bootstrapped resampling. Decision curve analysis was performed to assess clinical utility across a range of threshold probabilities.

Immune cell infiltration analysis

Cell-Type Identification by Estimating Relative Subsets of RNA Transcripts (CIBERSORT) was applied to normalized RNA-seq data using the LM22 signature with 1,000 permutations. Samples with deconvolution P<0.05 were retained. The estimated fractions of 22 immune cell types were extracted, and differences between IME-score groups were evaluated using Wilcoxon tests with FDR correction.

Tumor microenvironment scoring using Estimation of Stromal and Immune Cells in Malignant Tumor Tissues Using Expression Data (ESTIMATE)

The ESTIMATE algorithm was used to compute StromalScore, ImmuneScore, ESTIMATEScore, and inferred tumor purity for each sample. Scores were standardized before statistical analysis. Associations with the IME-score were assessed using Spearman correlation, and differences between IME-score groups were evaluated using nonparametric tests.

Pathway activity analysis using Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA)

GSVA was performed on log2-normalized expression data using gene sets from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) Hallmark and/or C2 curated collections. GSVA enrichment scores were computed using default parameters. Pathway activity differences between IME-score groups were assessed using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with FDR adjustment.

Immunotherapy response prediction using Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE)

The TIDE framework was used to infer potential response to immune checkpoint blockade. Gene expression profiles were uploaded to the TIDE web platform according to standard instructions. TIDE scores and predicted responder/non-responder classifications were retrieved and compared across IME-score groups using nonparametric statistical tests.

Immune checkpoint gene expression analysis

Log2-normalized expression values of immune checkpoint genes, including programmed cell death protein 1 (PDCD1), CD274 (PD-L1), cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA4), lymphocyte-activation gene 3 (LAG3), and T-cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT), were extracted. Group comparisons between IME-score categories were performed using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, with FDR correction when multiple genes were evaluated simultaneously.

Validation cohort analysis

In the independent validation cohort, IME-scores were calculated by applying PCA loadings derived from the training dataset to the processed methylation matrix. Survival analyses, immune infiltration estimation, tumor microenvironment scoring, GSVA, TIDE evaluation, and immune checkpoint expression comparisons were replicated following identical analytical procedures and statistical criteria.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.1). Differential methylation analysis between tumor and normal breast tissues was conducted using linear models with empirical Bayes moderation. CpG sites with |Δβ|≥0.1 and FDR <0.05 were considered significantly differentially methylated.

Pearson correlation analysis was performed to evaluate associations between CpG methylation levels and corresponding gene expression. CpG-gene pairs with correlation coefficients r≤−0.5 and FDR <0.05 were retained for downstream analyses.

PCA was applied to the standardized methylation matrix derived from immune-evasion-related CpG sites. PC1 was defined as the IME-score. The stability of the IME-score was assessed using 100 bootstrap resampling iterations. Correlations between bootstrap-derived PC1 vectors and the original PC1, as well as the proportion of variance explained by PC1, were recorded.

Patients were classified into high- and low-IME-score groups according to the median IME-score. Overall survival was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier methods and compared using the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Time-dependent ROC curves and Harrell’s concordance index were used to evaluate prognostic performance.

A prognostic nomogram was developed based on the IME-score and clinical variables. Calibration curves generated by bootstrap resampling were used to assess agreement between predicted and observed survival probabilities. Decision curve analysis was performed to evaluate the clinical utility of the prediction model.

Differences in immune cell infiltration, immune checkpoint gene expression, pathway enrichment scores, and TIDE-related metrics between groups were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Correlations between the IME-score and tumor microenvironment scores were evaluated using Spearman correlation analysis. For analyses involving multiple comparisons, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate procedure.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant unless otherwise specified.

Results

Clinical characteristics of the included breast cancer patients

A total of 676 patients were included, comprising 473 in the training cohort and 203 in the test cohort. The age distribution showed that patients younger than 60 years accounted for 57.7% in the training cohort and 58.6% in the test cohort, whereas those aged 60 years or older represented 42.3% and 41.4%, respectively. Females constituted the vast majority of cases, with 98.7% in the training cohort and all patients in the test cohort. Clinical stage information indicated the presence of stages I–IV disease in both cohorts. Tumor stage (T stage), node stage (N stage), and metastasis stage (M stage) classifications were also recorded for all patients, covering the full spectrum of tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) categories. Discussion

This study systematically characterized DNA methylation alterations associated with immune evasion in breast cancer and developed a methylation-derived IME-score. The IME-score demonstrated high internal stability, consistent prognostic stratification in both the training and validation cohorts, and strong associations with multi-dimensional immune features. Distinct patterns of immune infiltration, tumor microenvironment composition, immune checkpoint gene expression, and pathway activation corresponded to IME-score levels. Furthermore, the IME-score aligned closely with TIDE-derived dysfunction and exclusion metrics and with responder score distributions, indicating an epigenetically encoded axis that reflects immune-evasive tumor states.

Existing evidence underscores the importance of DNA methylation as a regulatory mechanism influencing tumor immunogenicity. Hypermethylation of antigen-presentation genes, impaired interferon signaling, and epigenetic suppression of T-cell-attracting chemokines have all been implicated in immune escape across cancers (10). Prior studies in breast cancer have primarily focused on single-gene methylation events, including promoter silencing of human leukocyte antigen class I (HLA-I) family members or regulators of interferon response (11,12). These findings illustrate the role of epigenetic repression in shaping an immune-silent phenotype, but they do not provide a broader view of genome-wide methylation changes that influence immune evasion at the systems level.

In contrast to these gene-centric approaches, our work identified a larger set of CpG sites that exhibit strong negative correlations with immune effector gene expression. Many of these CpGs are located in promoters, CpG islands, or regulatory gene-body regions, suggesting widespread epigenetic modulation beyond isolated loci (13,14). This integrative strategy captures the cumulative effects of dispersed methylation changes, offering a more comprehensive representation of the epigenetic architecture underlying immune escape.

Recent immune stratification frameworks in breast cancer, including immune-hot and immune-cold classifications, transcriptomic immune signatures, and tumor microenvironment scoring systems, highlight the substantial heterogeneity of immune activity among patients (15,16). These models primarily rely on RNA expression profiles, which reflect downstream transcriptional states influenced by multiple upstream regulatory layers. The IME-score contributes to this area by introducing a methylation-based perspective that captures more stable epigenetic states associated with immune suppression. The observed associations between high IME-scores and reduced effector T-cell infiltration, elevated inhibitory receptor expression, and enrichment of cell-cycle and DNA-repair pathways are consistent with prior descriptions of immune-excluded tumor ecosystems.

Biomarkers currently applied in immunotherapy decision-making, such as PD-L1 staining, tumor mutational burden, and interferon-response signatures, show variable predictive accuracy in breast cancer. Recent literature suggests that epigenetic features may better represent the long-term, stable immune-evasion phenotype of tumors. Our finding that the IME-score corresponds to TIDE-predicted non-responsiveness and lower responder scores supports this concept and provides an additional dimension for evaluating potential immunotherapy benefit (17). Compared with transcription-only biomarkers, the IME-score incorporates genome-wide methylation patterns that may offer increased stability and complementary predictive value.

The IME-score reflects a constellation of epigenetic alterations that correspond to distinct immunological and microenvironmental states. High IME-scores are associated with CpG hypermethylation and reduced expression of immune-relevant genes, as well as with activation of proliferative and metabolic pathways that contribute to the establishment of an immune-excluded environment. The corresponding tumor microenvironment displays reduced cytotoxic T-cell and NK-cell infiltration and increased expression of immune-inhibitory receptors, creating a landscape permissive to immune escape. Clinically, the IME-score improves risk stratification when combined with conventional clinicopathological factors and demonstrates robust predictive performance in nomogram models. The differences in

TIDE and responder scores between IME groups indicate potential value in immunotherapy selection.

Limitation

Several limitations should be considered. The study is based on TCGA cohorts and requires validation in multi-center datasets, especially in patients who have received immune checkpoint inhibitors. Although methylation–expression correlations support potential regulatory associations, functional experiments are required to confirm the mechanistic roles of key CpG sites in modulating immune escape. The use of PCA generates a stable composite score but limits interpretability at the individual CpG level. Future research incorporating single-cell epigenomic technologies, spatial transcriptomics, and chromatin accessibility profiling may provide deeper insights into the spatial and cellular origins of immune-evasive epigenetic states. Additionally, integration of the IME-score into clinical immunotherapy trials will be essential to determine its utility as a predictive biomarker.

Conclusions

This study establishes an epigenetically derived IME-score that captures a stable and biologically meaningful dimension of the breast cancer immune landscape. The findings suggest that integrating methylation-based immune signatures with tumor microenvironment profiling may enhance prognostic assessment and support more informed immunotherapeutic decision-making. These insights provide a foundation for future clinical validation and mechanistic exploration of epigenetic determinants of immune escape.

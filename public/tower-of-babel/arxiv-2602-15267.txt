Slowly decaying Rajchman measures and a restriction theorem for the Fourier transform at the limit case of zero Fourier dimension
Iván Polasek, Ezequiel Rela

Abstract
. In this article we prove the existence of sets E ⊆ ℝ E\subseteq\mathbb{R} of zero Fourier dimension such that it is possible to restrict the Fourier transform to E E on a certain non-trivial range [ 1 , p ~ ) [1,\tilde{p}) with 1 < p ~ < 2 1<\tilde{p}<2 . This builds upon Mockenhaupt’s Restriction Theorem; while this theorem could only be applied to sets of positive Fourier dimension, we show that the existence of a measure with polylogarithmic Fourier decay combined with full Hausdorff dimension 1 on the real line is enough to guarantee restriction. In order to achieve this, we combine two different tools: a modification of a construction from a recent work of Li and Liu to produce a set with specific Hausdorff and Fourier dimensions, and a generalization of the Stein-Tomas-Mockenhaupt Restriction Theorem.

1.
Introduction and Main Results

The Restriction Problem is a long-standing one that encompasses many distinct but related questions. It concerns the question of whether, for certain
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq p<2
, it is possible to restrict to
E
the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
of every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. One of the first substantial answers to this question can be traced back to the work of Stein and Tomas
[
Tom75
]
and asserts that, for every
\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{n+3}{2n+2},\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{n+1}{n-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)
we have that

(1)

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},d\sigma)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\forall f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is the unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
d\sigma
is its uniform measure.

This initial result has been studied in more detail and generalized in many different directions. The range of
p
and
q
proved by Stein and Tomas is conjectured to actually be larger, namely
\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{n+1}{2n},\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{n+1}{n-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)
. This is the so called Restriction Conjecture for the sphere; it has been proven for
n=2
(see
[
Fef70
]
) and remains open for higher dimensions. In terms of the possible subsets
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which a restriction theorem might hold, it is known (see
[
Ste70
]
) that (
1
) holds for the same range of
p
and
q
when
\mathbb{S}
is replaced by a compact subset of any hypersurface whose Gaussian curvature is nowhere zero. A trivial computation shows that curvature is somehow crucial here, since no restriction theorem holds for a flat surface. In general, when
E
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and there exists a measure
\mu
supported on
E
such that

(2)

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{q}(E,d\mu)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\forall f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

holds for a certain pair of values
(p,q)
, we will say that a restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\rightarrow q)
holds. The problem has also been studied in the context of particular hypersurfaces and curves or with different hypotheses. See for example
[
Dru85
]
,
[
Tab85
]
and
[
Gre81
]
.

All of these results relied on the geometry of the sets
E
as subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. In contrast with the remark on curvature above, Mockenhaupt’s breakthrough paper
[
Moc00
]
showed that it is possible to prove restriction theorems for fractal subsets of the real line, for which the notion of curvature makes no sense. Mockenhaupt’s Restriction Theorem relies on the existence of a measure supported on the set which satisfies two hypotheses related to the Hausdorff and Fourier dimension of the set. This approach allows to extend the analysis to even more general scenarios without any geometric structure, including the case of the abstract Fourier transform defined on groups (see
[
Pap10
]
). It is common to refer to this theorem as the Stein-Tomas-Mockenhaupt theorem (STM), we include it here for reference.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be a compactly supported measure on
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(1)

\mu(B(x,R))\lesssim R^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},R>0
.

(2)

|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-\beta/2}
for some
\beta>0,\forall\xi\neq 0.

Then, a restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\rightarrow 2)
holds for every
1\leq p<\frac{2(2n-2\alpha+\beta)}{4(n-\alpha)+\beta}
.

We include here a remark on the hypotheses in STM theorem: hypothesis
(i)
is called the Frostman condition, and it is well known that it implies
\dim_{H}(E)\geq\alpha
. Hypothesis
(ii)
is a result on the Fourier decay of the measure
\mu
, and as such guarantees that
\dim_{F}(E)\geq\beta
(for the precise definitions of the Hausdorff dimension
\dim_{H}
and the Fourier dimension
\dim_{F}
, see Section
2
). A set with coinciding Hausdorff and Fourier dimension is called a
Salem
set.

There is an important reason for the theorem above to be of the form
R_{E}(p\rightarrow 2)
and that is the
T^{*}T
method. This is a well know fact that says that an operator
T
is bounded from
L^{p}
to
L^{2}
if and only if the composition
T^{*}T
is bounded from
L^{p}
to
L^{p^{\prime}}
. In the context of the restriction operator, it is not difficult to check that the problem reduces to obtain
(p,p^{\prime})
bounds for the convolution operator
f\to f*\widehat{d\mu}
. The interested reader could check the details in the Stein-Tomas-Muckenhaupt theorem to verify that this
(p,p^{\prime})
bound is a consequence of the interpolation between
(1,\infty)
and
(2,2)
norm inequalites for this convolution operator. The decay of
\widehat{d\mu}
becomes evidently useful to control the supremum norm, whilst the local growth condition of
\mu
appears when controlling the
L^{2}
norm of the convolution as a consequence of certain control of the
L^{\infty}
norm of the Fourier transform of kernel.

Theorem
1.1
has also been improved. Bak and Seeger
[
BS11
]
have proved that the theorem holds as well for the endpoint
p_{*}=\frac{2(2n-2\alpha+\beta)}{4(n-\alpha)+\beta},\alpha<n
. Hambrook and Łaba
[
HLa13
]
worked on the optimality of the range of
p
for the family of Salem sets in the real line. They were able to prove the existence of a Salem set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of dimension
\alpha\in(0,1)
supporting a measure
\mu
such that
(i)
holds for the dimension
\alpha
,
(ii)
holds for every
\beta<\alpha
but the restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\rightarrow q)
fails for every
p>\frac{2(2n-2\alpha+\beta)}{4(n-\alpha)+\beta}
. Chen
[
Che16
]
later improved on this result, proving the sharpness of the restriction theorem in the same sense for general sets, not necessarily Salem sets. Both of these proofs relied on probabilistic arguments. Fraser, Hambrook and Ryou
[
FHR
]
went a step futher and were able to construct deterministic subsets of the real line which explicitly show the sharpness of the Restriction theorem.

In particular, according to hypotheses
(i)
and
(ii)
, in order to apply STM theorem one should be working with a set
E
where both dimensions are strictly positive. Our main purpose here is to focus on the fact that decay of the Fourier transform is a central aspect of a restriction theorem. Hence, we want to push STM theorem to the limit case of zero Fourier dimension, but with a mild decay so we still have a chance to get restriction.
A first step in this direction was achieved by the second author, whose doctoral thesis contains the following generalized version of STM Restriction theorem (
[
Rel10
]
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu
a measure supported on
E
. Let
h
be a doubling dimension function (see Section
2
for the definition), and
g:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
a decreasing function such that

•

\mu(B(x,R))\lesssim h(R)\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},R>0

•

|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\lesssim g(|\xi|)\ \forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
\Gamma
be the sequence defined by

\Gamma_{k}=g(2^{k-1})^{\frac{2}{p}-1}(2^{nk}h(2^{-k}))^{2-\frac{2}{p}}.

Then if
\Gamma\in\ell^{1}
for some
p
, there is a restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\to 2)
.

For a long time, it was unclear how to apply this result in order to obtain a restriction theorem for a zero-dimensional set. The main purpose of this paper is to construct a family of zero-Fourier dimensional sets
E
for which Theorem
1.2
holds, provided that the measure verifies a sufficiently fast polylogarithmic Fourier decay and a Frostman condition that will force
\dim_{H}(E)=1
. This condition on the Hausdorff dimension seems unavoidable, at least with the approach presented here. The reason can be found by noticing that the sequence
\Gamma_{k}
above includes a factor of the form
2^{nk}
. A careful exploration of the proof shows that this factor comes from the effect of a dilation under the Fourier transform, making quantitatively explicit the dependance on the dimension of the ambient space. In the case of the real line, that dimension is 1, hence the condition on the dimension function
h
. In the same spirit as in STM result, the relevance of Theorem
1.2
relies on the existence of a set satisfying the hypothesis. That is the main contribution of this article that we present here.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r>1,a>0
. There exist a compact set
E\subset\mathbb{R}
with
\dim_{H}(E)=1
and
\dim_{F}(E)=0
such that there is a restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\rightarrow 2)
for
1\leq p<1+\frac{r-1}{1+r+2ar}
.

This restriction theorem will be a direct consequence of Theorem
1.2
and the specific construction we provide in Theorem
2.2
. We postpone the statement of the theorem about the construction of the set to Section
2
, after we introduce some necessary definitions. This construction is based on a recent work of Li and Liu
[
LL25
]
; in their paper, they construct subsets of the real line with predetermined Hausdorff and Fourier dimension, supporting a measure that captures both dimensions simultaneously. Our construction involves a measure whose behaviour can be controled up to a logarithmic scale, detecting the precise level of nuance that is needed for our purposes.

2.
Definitions and the specific construction

We introduce here some definitions needed to present the main construction.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
s\in[0,n]
. The
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is defined as follows:

\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i}(\textnormal{diám}(E_{i}))^{s},E\subseteq\bigcup_{i}E_{i},\textnormal{diám}(E_{i})<\delta\right\},

\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E).

It is an important result that

\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)>0\iff\exists\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}(E)/\mu(B(x,R))\lesssim R^{s}\ \forall\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},R>0.

The “only if” part of this equivalence is known as Frostman’s Lemma, while the “if” part is known as the Mass Distribution Principle.

The Hausdorff Dimension is defined as

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(E)=\sup

\displaystyle\left\{s\in[0,n]:\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf

\displaystyle\left\{s\in[0,n]:\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup

\displaystyle\left\{s\in[0,n]:\exists\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}(E)/\mu(B(x,R))\lesssim R^{s}\ \forall\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},R>0\right\}.

The Hausdorff dimension of a set
E
represents the cut point between the values of
s
for which
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)
is strictly greater than 0 and the values of
s
for which
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)
is 0.

The Fourier dimension of
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle\dim_{F}(S)=\sup

\displaystyle\left\{s\in[0,n]:\exists\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}(S)/|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-s/2}\ \forall\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

Broadly speaking, it conveys information about the fastest possible polynomial decay for the Fourier transforms of all measures supported on
E
. A key result regarding both dimensions is that

\dim_{F}(E)\leq\dim_{H}(E)

for any
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We include here, for the sake of coompleteness, the definition of dimension function needed in Theorem
1.2
. A function
h:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is said to be a dimension function if it satisfies the following three conditions:

•

h(0)=0.

•

h
is increasing.

•

h
is right continuous.

For a given
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is defined with respect to the function
h(t)=t^{s}
, which is clearly a dimension function. The definition of dimension function allows a reasonable definition of a measure
\mathcal{H}^{h}
with all the good properties of a measure. For a detailed explanation over the generalized Hausdorff measures, the reader can consult
[
Rog70
]
.
h
is called a doubling function when there exists a constant
c>0
such that

h(2t)\leq ch(t)\ \forall t\geq 0.

We move on now to the main construction of the set satisfying the conditions in Theorem
1.3
. We denote by
\|x\|=\min_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}|x-z|
to the distance from
x
to its nearest integer. Given
u\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},q\in\mathbb{N}
, define the set

\mathcal{N}_{u}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{q}\right)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}/\ \|qx\|\leq qu\right\}.

Intuitively, the set
\mathcal{N}_{u}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{q}\right)
consists of all the balls in
\mathbb{R}
of radius
u
centered on the rational numbers with denominator
q
. It is possible to define these sets in higher dimensions, but we will not need to do so in the present paper. For a more thorough discussion on how important sets can be defined in terms of the sets
\mathcal{N}_{u}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{q}\right)
, we refer the reader to Li and Liu’s paper
[
LL25
]
. Just to give a classic example, recall the sets of well approximable numbers, independently defined by Jarnik
[
Jar31
]
and Besicovitch
[
Bes34
]
, which Kaufman
[
Kau81
]
later proved to be the first examples of explicit Salem sets in
\mathbb{R}
of dimension
\frac{2}{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
; they can be written as

\displaystyle E(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}/\|qx\|\leq q^{1-\alpha}\ \textnormal{for infinitely many}\ q\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{i\leq q}\mathcal{N}_{q^{-\alpha}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{q}\right).

We can now properly cite Li and Liu’s Theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\beta,\gamma\geq 0
and
2\gamma+\beta\leq 1
. Then there exists an increasing sequence
(q_{i})_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that the set

E:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\bigcap_{i}\bigcup_{1\leq H\leq q_{i}^{\gamma}}\mathcal{N}_{q_{i}^{-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{Hq_{i}^{\beta}}\right),\ \textnormal{if}\ 2\gamma+\beta<1\\
\\
\bigcap_{i}\bigcup_{1\leq H\leq q_{i}^{\gamma},prime}\mathcal{N}_{q_{i}^{-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{Hq_{i}^{\beta}}\right),\ \textnormal{if}\ 2\gamma+\beta=1\end{array}\right.

has Hausdorff dimension
2\gamma+\beta
and Fourier dimension
2\gamma
. Moreover there exists a finite Borel measure
\mu
supported on

\bigcap_{i}\bigcup_{q_{i}^{\gamma}/2\leq H\leq q_{i}^{\gamma},prime}\mathcal{N}_{q_{i}^{-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus p\mathbb{Z}}{Hq_{i}^{\beta}}\right)\cap[0,1],

satisfying

\mu(B(x,R))\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{2\gamma+\beta-\epsilon}\ \textnormal{and}\ |\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\lesssim_{\epsilon}|\xi|^{-\gamma+\epsilon},\forall\epsilon>0.

Our intention is to modify this result in order to produce a set that satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
. In essence, we will need the two magnitudes that define the dimension,
\beta
and
\gamma
, to now be functions. Let’s discuss the specific functions that we will need.

We want the Fourier decay of our measure
\mu
to be slower than any negative power function, in order to allow the Fourier dimension of our set to be zero. However, we want it to be something that we can operate with, so we want to define
\gamma(x)
for every
x\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)
in such a way that

\xi^{-\gamma(\xi)}=\frac{1}{\log^{r}(\xi)}

for some
r>0
and for large values of
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. Nonetheless, we need to proceed with caution: since we are dealing with both Fourier decays and dimension functions in the same result, defining
\gamma(x)
in this way for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
would result in
x^{\gamma(x)}\xrightarrow{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}-\infty
, which does not make sense for a dimension function. This problem does not arise when
\gamma
is simply a constant, so we want to define
\gamma(x)
in a way that emulates the “symmetric” behaviour of
x^{-\gamma}
, namely the fact that
x^{-\gamma}
evaluated in
x^{-1}
is exactly
x^{\gamma}
. With this in mind, we define
\gamma(x)
on
(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty)
as

(3)

\gamma(x)=\frac{r\log(|\log x|)}{|\log x|},

which guarantees that

x^{\gamma(x)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\log^{r}(x)\ \textnormal{if}\ x>1\\
\\
\frac{1}{\log^{r}(\frac{1}{x})}\ \textnormal{if}\ 0<x<1.\end{array}\right.

It is not important to define the function in
1
, since its behaviour only matters for sufficiently large and small values of
x
.

In a similar fashion, we will define

(4)

\beta(x)=1-(2+a)\gamma(x),

for some small
a>0
that we will be able to choose freely. This guarantees that

s(x)=\beta(x)+2\gamma(x)=1-a\gamma(x),

the function that “captures” the Hausdorff dimension, is smaller than 1 but greater than any
0<\alpha<1
, in a way that is comparable to
\gamma
. As a consequence, we get that

x^{\beta(x)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\frac{x}{\log^{(2+a)r}(x)}\ \textnormal{if}\ x>1\\
\\
x\log^{(2+a)r}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\ \textnormal{if}\ 0<x<1,\end{array}\right.

and

x^{s(x)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\frac{x}{\log^{ar}(x)}\ \textnormal{if}\ x>1\\
\\
x\log^{ar}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\ \textnormal{if}\ 0<x<1.\end{array}\right.

After these definitions we are in a position to state our main theorem regarding the specific construction.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
r>0,a>0
. For these values, let
\gamma(x),\beta(x)
be the functions defined in (
3
) and (
4
) respectively. Then there exists an increasing sequence
(q_{i})_{i}
of positive real numbers such that the set

(5)

E:=\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{1\leq H\leq q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}}\mathcal{N}_{q_{i}^{-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{Hq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}}\right)

has
\dim_{H}(E)=1
and
\dim_{F}(E)=0
. Moreover,
E
supports a finite Borel measure
\mu
for which

(6)

\mu(B(x,R))\lesssim_{\epsilon}R\log^{ar+\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)\quad\forall R<1/3,\forall\epsilon>0

and

(7)

|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\lesssim_{\epsilon}\frac{1}{\log^{r-\epsilon}(|\xi|)}\quad\forall|\xi|\geq 2,\forall\epsilon>0

hold, and the Fourier decay is optimal on
E
(up to
\epsilon
-loss).

Remark
.

Conditions (
6
) and (
7
) can be restated as

\mu(B(x,R))\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{\beta(R)+2\gamma(R)}\log^{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\lesssim_{\epsilon}|\xi|^{-\gamma(|\xi|)}\log^{\epsilon}(|\xi|),

to be seen as an analogue of the results in Theorem
2.1
.

The interesting thing is that, even though the sets defined in Theorem
2.2
are outside of the hypotheses of the original STM theorem, it is still possible to restrict the Fourier transform to these sets. That is, with the set from Theorem
2.2
we can provide a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
r>1,a>0
,
E
and
\mu
as in Theorem
2.2
. For every
\epsilon>0
, Theorem
1.2
guarantees a restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\rightarrow 2)
if
\left(\Gamma^{(\epsilon)}_{k}\right)_{k}\in\ell_{1}
, where

\displaystyle{\Gamma^{(\epsilon)}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{\log^{r-\epsilon}(2^{k-1})}\right]^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\left[\log^{ar+\epsilon}(2^{k})\right]^{2-\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{k^{(r-\epsilon)(\frac{2}{p}-1)}}k^{(ar+\epsilon)(2-\frac{2}{p})}.

A simple calculation shows that the right hand side belongs to
\ell_{1}
if and only if

\frac{2}{p}(r+ar)>1+r+2ar+\epsilon.

Since this holds for any
\epsilon>0
, a sufficient condition for a restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\rightarrow 2)
is that

\frac{2}{p}(r+ar)>1+r+2ar,

which happens if and only if

p<1+\frac{r-1}{1+r+2ar}.

∎

We discuss here some subtleties that can be observed comparing STM’s theorem and our Theorem
1.3
. Notice that if we apply Theorem
1.1
for a measure satisfying condition
(i)
for every
0<\alpha<1
and condition
(ii)
for some fixed

\beta>0
, we would obtain a restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\rightarrow 2)
for
1\leq p<2
, since

\sup_{0<\alpha<1}\frac{2(2-2\alpha+\beta)}{4(1-\alpha)+\beta}=2,

independently of the value of
\beta>0
. That is, with any
positive
Fourier decay and full Hausdorff dimension, the full range of restriction can be obtained. Our result provides an interesting extension of this fact: with a
zero
dimensional polylogarithmic decay on the Fourier transform, we can still get examples of sets with the restriction property.
Namely, for any
\tilde{p}\in(1,2)
it is always possible to pick
r>1,a>0
and a corresponding set
E
as in Theorem
2.2
such that a restriction theorem
R_{E}(p\rightarrow 2)
holds for every
1\leq p<\tilde{p}
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

We include here the proof of our main construction result in Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
:

The proof is structured as follows: first we will construct the measure
\mu
and prove it has the desired Fourier decay. Then we will prove that
\mu
satisfies the Frostman condition; the fact that
\dim_{H}(E)=1
follows automatically from this. Finally, we will prove that the Fourier decay of
\mu
is sharp; this will imply that
\dim_{F}(E)=0
.

The construction we present here will provide a set that is actually contained in the set described in (
5
). Namely, consider the set
E^{\prime}
defined as:

E^{\prime}=\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{1}{2}q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}\leq p\leq q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}_{q_{i}^{-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus p\mathbb{Z}}{pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}}\right)\cap[0,1].

Our measure
\mu
will be supported on this subset. We will refrain from pointing out every time that a constant codified by a
\lesssim
symbol depends on
r
or
a
, since these are fixed throughout the proof and a constant depending on them creates no problems.

In order to prove that
E
verifies the desired properties, we will impose two types of conditions on the sequence
(q_{i})_{i}
: that the term
q_{1}
is large enough, and that the sequence increases fast enough. For the sake of clarity, the precise set of conditions on
(q_{i})_{i}
will be provided after all the conditions are duly brought up.

We can always consider
q_{i}
such that
q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\in\mathbb{N}
by increasing
q_{i}
if necessary. Therefore, the set
\mathcal{N}_{q_{i}^{-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus p\mathbb{Z}}{pq_{i}^{\beta}}\right)
is formed by intervals of radius
q_{i}^{-1}
and centers

\frac{m}{pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}}
, where
(m:p)=1
, and the intervals are disjoint if all the
q_{i}
are large enough (see condition
1
). Again, if all
q_{i}
are large enough (see condition
2
), we will have that

q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}=\log^{r}(q_{i}),\quad\textnormal{and}\quad q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}=\frac{q_{i}}{\log^{(2+a)r}(q_{i})}.

In addition, we will have that
\log\log q_{i}>1
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let’s construct the measure
\mu
and prove it has the desired Fourier decay. It will be enough to prove that the Fourier decay holds over the integers by virtue of the following Lemma. While this lemma is well known for polynomial decays (see
[
Wol03
]
), we weren’t able to find a version for general decays, so we present it here.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite Borel measure over
\mathbb{T}
such that
|\widehat{\mu}(k)|\lesssim f(|k|)

\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
f:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a function such that

•

f
is decreasing

•

f\left(\frac{\xi}{2}\right)\lesssim f\left(\xi\right)

•

There exist
N\in\mathbb{N},\xi_{0}>0
such that
f(\xi)\gtrsim_{N,\xi_{0}}\xi^{-N}\ \forall\xi\geq\xi_{0}
.

Then for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}
, the measure
\nu
defined by

d\nu=\phi d\mu

satisfies that

|\widehat{\nu}(\xi)|\lesssim f(|\xi|)\quad\forall\ |\xi|\geq\xi_{0}.

Proof.

Let
|\xi|\geq\xi_{0}
. It is known (see
[
Wol03
]
) that

\widehat{\nu}(\xi)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mu}(k)\widehat{\phi}(\xi-k).

We can therefore bound the absolute value of
\widehat{\nu}
by

|\widehat{\nu}(\xi)|\leq\sum_{|\xi-k|\geq|\xi|/2}|\widehat{\mu}(k)||\widehat{\phi}(\xi-k)|+\sum_{|\xi-k|\leq|\xi|/2}|\widehat{\mu}(k)||\widehat{\phi}(\xi-k)|.

The first sum can be bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{|\xi-k|\geq|\xi|/2}|\widehat{\mu}(k)||\widehat{\phi}(\xi-k)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}\sum_{|\xi-k|\geq|\xi|/2}\|\widehat{\mu}\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{|\xi-k|^{N+1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mu(\mathbb{R})\int_{|\xi|/2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{N+1}}dx\lesssim_{N,\mu}|\xi|^{-N}\lesssim_{N,\xi_{0}}f(|\xi|),

and the second sum can be bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{|\xi-k|\leq|\xi|/2}|\widehat{\mu}(k)||\widehat{\phi}(\xi-k)|\leq f\left(\frac{|\xi|}{2}\right)\sum_{|\xi-k|\leq|\xi|/2}|\widehat{\phi}(\xi-k)|\lesssim f(|\xi|).

This concludes with the proof of the lemma.
∎

We start with a function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(-1,1)
,
\phi\geq 0
,
\int\phi=1
. The first part of the construction is analogous to that in
[

]
, so we will only make statements without many calculations. For every prime

, define

\phi_{i,p}
is
p

\phi_{i,p}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\phi}(pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})-1}n)e^{2\pi inx}-p^{-1}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})-1}m)e^{2\pi imx/p}.

Define
\Phi_{i,p}(x)=\phi_{i,p}(pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}x)
, which is supported on
\mathcal{N}_{q_{i}^{-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus p\mathbb{Z}}{pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}}\right)
, and whose Fourier coefficients are

\widehat{\Phi_{i,p}}(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}(1-p^{-1})\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}k)\quad\textnormal{if}\quad k\in pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})\mathbb{Z}}\\
-p^{-1}\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}k)\quad\textnormal{if}\quad k\in q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\mathbb{Z}\\
0,\quad\textnormal{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

If we define
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\left\{p\ \textnormal{prime}\in(q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}/2,q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}]\right\}
, the Prime Number Theorem guarantees that, for
q_{i}
large enough (see condition
3
) we have that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}}{\log(q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})})}\leq\#\mathcal{P}_{i}\leq 2\frac{q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}}{\log(q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})})}=\frac{2\log^{r}(q_{i})}{r\log\log(q_{i})}.

In addition we can check that for
k\neq 0

(8)

\#\left\{p\in\mathcal{P}_{i}/pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}|k\right\}\leq\max\left\{\frac{\log(|k|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})})}{\log(q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}/2)},0\right\}\leq\max\left\{\frac{2\log(|k|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})})}{\log(q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})})},0\right\},

where the last inequality holds when
q_{i}
is sufficiently large (see condition
4
)).

We analogously define

F_{i}(x)=\left.\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{P}_{i}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}\frac{p}{p-1}\Phi_{i,p}(x)\right|_{[0,1]},

which is smooth on
\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}\mathcal{N}_{q_{i}^{-1}}\left(\frac{\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus p\mathbb{Z}}{pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}}\right)\cap[0,1]
. It verifies that
\widehat{F_{i}}(0)=1
and, for
k\neq 0

\widehat{F_{i}}(k)=\frac{1}{\#\mathcal{P}_{i}}\left(\#\{p\in\mathcal{P}_{i}/k\in pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\mathbb{Z}\}-\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{i}/k\in q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus pq_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{p-1}\right)\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}k).

We can use (
8

,

\displaystyle|\widehat{F_{i}}(k)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{\log(q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})})}{q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})}}\left(\max\left\{\frac{2\log\left(|k|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})}\right)}{\log(q_{i}^{\gamma(q_{i})})},0\right\}+1\right)|\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}k)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\frac{\log\log(q_{i})}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}|\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}k)|\ \textnormal{if}\ |k|\leq q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\\
\\
\frac{\log\left(|k|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})}\right)+\log\log q_{i}}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}|\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}k)|.\ \textnormal{if}\ |k|\geq q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}.\end{array}\right.

In order to proceed, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}[0,1]
. Then

|\widehat{\psi F_{i}}(k)-\widehat{\psi}(k)|\lesssim\|\psi\|\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\frac{\log\log q_{i}}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}\ \textnormal{if}\ |k|\leq q_{i}\\
\\
\frac{\log\log|k|}{\log^{r}(|k|)}\ \textnormal{if}\ |k|\geq q_{i}.\end{array}\right.

where

\|\psi\|=\sum_{j=-2}^{2}|\widehat{\psi}(j)|+\|\widehat{\psi}(\cdot)\log^{t}(|\cdot|)\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\})},\quad t=\max\{1,r\}

Notice that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(9)

\|\psi\|\leq 5\|\psi\|_{\infty}+c\|\psi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\sum_{l\neq 0}\frac{\log^{t}|l|}{|l|^{2}},

which is a finite quantity that depends only on
\psi
and
r
.

Proof.

For
0\leq|k|\leq q_{i}
, we begin by noting that

|\widehat{\psi F_{i}}(k)-\widehat{\psi}(k)|=|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\psi}(k-l)\widehat{F_{i}}(l)-\widehat{\psi}(k)|=|\sum_{l\neq 0}\widehat{\psi}(k-l)\widehat{F_{i}}(l)|.

We will need to split the sum in three parts and bound each part separately. Let
A_{1}=\{l\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}/|l|\geq q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})},|k-l|\leq\frac{|l|}{2}\}
,
B_{1}=\{l\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}/|l|\geq q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})},|k-l|>\frac{|l|}{2}\}
and
C=\{l\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}/|l|\leq q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l\in A_{1}}\widehat{\psi}(k-l)\widehat{F_{i}}(l)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in A_{1}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{\log(|l|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})})+\log\log q_{i}}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}|\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}l)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in A_{1}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{\log\left(2q_{i}^{1-\beta(q_{i})}\right)+\log\log q_{i}}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|\frac{\log\log q_{i}}{\log^{r}(q_{i})},

where in the second inequality we have used that
\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{\infty}=1
and
l\in A_{1}\implies\frac{|l|}{2}\leq|k|\leq\frac{3}{2}|l|\implies|l|\leq 2|k|\leq 2q_{i}
, and in the third inequality holds since condition
4
implies that
2\leq\log^{r/2}q_{i}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l\in B_{1}}\widehat{\psi}(k-l)\widehat{F_{i}}(l)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in B_{1}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{\log(|l|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})})+\log\log q_{i}}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}|\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}l)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in B_{1}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{\log(2|k-l|)}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}\lesssim\|\psi\|\frac{1}{\log^{r}(q_{i})},

where in the second inequality we have used that
l\in B_{1}\implies|l|\leq 2|k-l|
and that
q_{i}
is large enough (see condition
5
). Finally,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l\in C}\widehat{\psi}(k-l)\widehat{F_{i}}(l)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in C}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{\log\log(q_{i})}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}|\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}l)|\leq\|\psi\|\frac{\log\log(q_{i})}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}.

Now, if
q_{i}\leq|k|
we will need to split the sum using
A_{2}=\{l\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}/|l|\geq q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})},|k-l|\leq\frac{|k|}{2}\}
,
B_{2}=\{l\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}/|l|\geq q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})},|k-l|>\frac{|k|}{2}\}
and
C=\{l\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}/|l|\leq q_{i}^{\beta(q_{i})}\}
. For the first case, we will need the following technical result, which is easy to verify.

Remark
.

For
\alpha>e
,

f(x)=x\log\left(e\alpha\frac{\log^{(2+a)r}(x)}{x}\right)
is increasing on
[e,\alpha]
.

Then we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in A_{2}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)||\widehat{F_{i}}(l)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in A_{2}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{\log(|l|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})})+\log\log q_{i}}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}\frac{q_{i}}{|l|}\frac{q_{i}}{|l|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in A_{2}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{\log(|l|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})})+\log\log q_{i}}{\log^{r}(|k|)}\frac{|k|}{|l|}\frac{q_{i}}{|l|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\in A_{2}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{q_{i}\log(e|k|q_{i}^{-\beta(q_{i})})+q_{i}\log\log q_{i}}{|l|\log^{r}(|k|)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in A_{2}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{|k|\log(e\log^{(2+a)r}(|k|))+|k|\log\log|k|}{|l|\log^{r}(|k|)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|\frac{\log\log(|k|)}{\log^{r}(|k|)}

where in the first inequality we have used that there is a constant
c>0
such that
|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|\leq c|\xi|^{-2}
, the second inequality holds if
q_{i}
is sufficiently large (see condition
6
), in the third inequality we have used that
l\in A\implies\frac{|k|}{2}\leq|l|\leq\frac{3}{2}|k|
, in the fourth inequality we have invoked the previous Remark, and the last inequality holds if
q_{i}
is large enough (see condition
7
).

For
l\in B_{2}
, we can use that
|\widehat{F_{i}}(l)|\leq 1
to obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in B_{2}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)||\widehat{F_{i}}(l)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in B_{2}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)|\frac{\log^{r}(|k|)}{\log^{r}(|k|)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in B_{2}}|\widehat{\psi}(k-l)||\log^{r}(2|k-l|)|\frac{1}{\log^{r}(|k|)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|\frac{1}{\log^{r}(|k|)}.

Finally,

\left|\sum_{l\in C}\widehat{\psi}(k-l)\widehat{F_{i}}(l)\right|\lesssim|\sum_{l\in C}\widehat{\psi}(k-l)\frac{\log\log(q_{i})}{\log^{r}(q_{i})}|\widehat{\phi}(q_{i}^{-1}l)|\leq\|\psi\|\frac{\log\log(|k|)}{\log^{r}(|k|)},

where the last inequality holds if
q_{i}
is large enough (see condition
7
).

\displaystyle cQ_{m_{0}-2}\frac{2^{1+ar+\epsilon}r\log\log(q_{m_{0}-1})}{\log^{\epsilon}q_{m_{0}-1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+ar+\epsilon}rc_{\epsilon}d_{\epsilon},

\displaystyle c_{\epsilon}=\max_{x\in[e^{e},\infty)}\frac{\log\log x}{\log^{\epsilon/2}x}
and
\displaystyle d_{\epsilon}=\max_{x\in[0,\infty]}\frac{x}{e^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}x}}

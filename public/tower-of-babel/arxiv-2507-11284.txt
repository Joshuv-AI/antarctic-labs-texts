Abstract
The integration of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) with radar imaging sensors has revolutionized the monitoring of dynamic and local Earth surface processes by enabling high-resolution and cost-effective remote sensing. This paper investigates the optimization of the sensing accuracy of a UAV swarm deployed to perform multi-baseline interferometric synthetic aperture radar (InSAR) sensing. In conventional single-baseline InSAR systems, only one synthetic aperture radar (SAR) antenna pair acquires two SAR images from two distinct angles to generate a digital elevation model (DEM) of the target area. However, multi-baseline InSAR extends this concept by aggregating multiple acquisitions from different angles, thus, significantly enhancing the vertical accuracy of the DEM. The heavy computations required for this process are performed on the ground and, therefore, the radar data is transmitted in real time to a ground station (GS) via a frequency-division multiple access (FDMA) air-to-ground backhaul link. This work focuses on improving the sensing precision by minimizing the height error of the averaged DEM while simultaneously ensuring sensing and communication quality-of-service (QoS). To this end, the UAV formation, velocity, and communication power allocation are jointly optimized using evolutionary algorithms (EAs). Our approach is benchmarked against established optimization methods, including genetic algorithms (GAs), simulated annealing (SA), and deep reinforcement learning (DRL) techniques. Numerical results show that the proposed solution outperforms these baseline schemes and achieves sub-decimeter vertical accuracy in several scenarios. These findings underline the potential of coordinated UAV swarms for delivering high-precision and real-time Earth observations through radar interferometry.

I-A

Single-baseline Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar

Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar is an advanced Synthetic aperture radar technology that exploits the phase difference between two or more Synthetic aperture radar acquisitions to estimate surface elevation and/or ground displacement
[
14
]
. These acquisitions can be obtained using either a single platform at different times (temporal baseline) or multiple spatially separated antennas at the same time (spatial or across-track baseline, where the baseline represents the distance between the used sensors). In particular, single-baseline across-track Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar refers to the configuration where two antennas acquire data simultaneously from different spatial positions, resulting in a purely spatial baseline with no temporal decorrelation
[
15
]
. Here, the baseline induces a phase difference in the received signals, which can be processed to generate an interferogram that maps each pixel to its corresponding phase difference. This interferogram encodes information about the terrain elevation, enabling the generation of high-resolution digital elevation models of the imaged scene
[
16
]
. In this context, important performance metrics include the coherence, which represents the cross-correlation between the two compared Synthetic aperture radar images, and the height error, which represents the vertical resolution of the digital elevation model
[
16
]
.
Across-track Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar has been extensively studied in airborne and spaceborne systems
[
14
]
. However, its adaptation to unmanned aerial vehicle-based platforms remains relatively underexplored. While several experimental studies have demonstrated the feasibility of unmanned aerial vehicle-based Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar systems
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
17
]
, there is a notable lack of systematic research on their performance optimization. Technical challenges such as platform trajectory, constrained payload capacity, and the need for effective resource allocation schemes pose significant hurdles. Addressing these challenges is critical to advancing low-altitude Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar systems. In
[
18
]
, the authors studied repeat-pass unmanned aerial vehicle-based three-dimensional Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar sensing, where metrics such as cross-range resolution, peak sidelobe ratio, integrated sidelobe ratio, and flight time were considered. Furthermore, the authors in
[
10
]
studied the resource allocation and optimization of multi-unmanned aerial vehicle Synthetic aperture radar systems, where the spatial configuration, transmit power, and frequency band allocation were considered. In
[
19
,
12
]
, the authors studied Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar coverage maximization under communication and sensing requirements for single-baseline unmanned aerial vehicle-based Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar.

I-B

Multi-baseline Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar

Single-baseline Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar systems often face a fundamental trade-off between phase unwrapping reliability and vertical resolution. Here, phase unwrapping refers to the process of resolving the inherent
2\pi
ambiguity in the measured phase to obtain absolute phase values, whereas the vertical resolution represents the precision with which elevation differences can be detected in the generated digital elevation model
[
14
]
. On one hand, short baselines provide high coherence and more reliable phase estimates, facilitating the phase unwrapping process. On the other hand, short baselines offer limited sensitivity to elevation, resulting in lower vertical resolution in the final digital elevation model. Conversely, longer baselines improve the vertical resolution but suffer from increased decorrelation, making the phase unwrapping more challenging
[
15
]
. Multi-baseline Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar addresses this limitation by combining multiple acquisition platforms or repeated observations to form several interferometric pairs with varying baseline lengths and orientations
[
20
]
. This approach enables the generation of a diverse set of interferograms, allowing the system to benefit from both short and long baselines. The increased flexibility introduces new degrees of freedom in the system design of multi-baseline unmanned aerial vehicle-based Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar systems, making optimization essential to properly adjust the unmanned aerial vehicle formation and allocate the resource across the drone swarm. Although multi-baseline Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar is a well-established technique, especially in spaceborne systems
[
20
]
, it remains underexplored in the context of unmanned aerial vehicles.

I-C

Main Contributions

In this paper, we consider a unmanned aerial vehicle swarm equipped with Synthetic aperture radar systems, designed to perform multi-baseline Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar sensing. Each unmanned aerial vehicle pair forms a single-baseline Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar system capable of generating a digital elevation model. All digital elevation models are then combined into a final high-precision digital elevation model using averaging techniques. Our goal is to minimize the height error of the final digital elevation model by jointly optimizing the unmanned aerial vehicle formation, velocity, and communication power allocation, subject to sensing and communication constraints. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

•

We formulate the height error minimization problem for a multi-baseline InSAR system consisting of
I
unmanned aerial vehicles as a complex, non-convex optimization problem, accounting for multiple system constraints related to coverage, phase unwrapping performance, data offloading, and collision avoidance.

•

To address this problem, we propose a novel co-evolutionary algorithm in which two distinct species collaboratively explore the optimization search space. The original problem is decomposed into two simpler sub-problems, with each species tasked with exploring the search space corresponding to its respective sub-problem. A non-parameterized particle swarm optimization is then used as the evolutionary optimizer to simultaneously evolve both species.

•

We compare the proposed algorithm with several established optimization methods, such as genetic algorithms, Simulated annealing, and deep reinforcement learning, and demonstrate its superiority across various system parameters.

•

We analyze the potential of employing unmanned aerial vehicle networks to enhance the sensing precision and explore the critical interplay between sensing and communication requirements.

A key performance metric for Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar is the coherence, which represents the normalized cross-correlation between a pair of Synthetic aperture radar images. For the images acquired by the pair

\gamma[i,j]=\gamma_{\mathrm{Rg}}[i,j]\gamma_{\mathrm{SNR}}[i,j]\gamma_{\rm other}[i,j],\forall\,i,j\in\mathcal{I},\;i<j.

II-A
3
Height of Ambiguity (HoA)

To extract the height of ground targets, the Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar technique relies on estimating the phase difference between each sensor pair
(U_{i},U_{j}),i<j
. Assuming that sensors
U_{i}
and
U_{j}
measure phases
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}

\Phi_{i,j}=\phi_{i}-\phi_{j}
. The elevation of a target above ground introduces a phase shift in the measured phase
\Phi_{i,j}
, making it possible to construct a digital elevation model of the observed area. However, since the phase
\Phi_{i,j}
is
2\pi

2\pi
phase cycle is known as the height of ambiguity. The height of ambiguity is a key parameter, as it determines the system’s sensitivity to topographic height variations
[
3
]
. The height of ambiguity for the sensor pair
(U_{i},U_{j})
is given by
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{amb}}[i,j]=\frac{\lambda r_{i}\sin(\theta_{i})}{b_{\perp}[i,j]},\forall\,i,j\in\mathcal{I},\;i<j.

(15)

4
digital elevation model Height Accuracy

\sigma_{\Phi}[i,j]=\frac{1}{\gamma[i,j]}\sqrt{\frac{1-\gamma^{2}[i,j]}{2n_{L}}},\forall\,i,j\in\mathcal{I},\;i<j,

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{h}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum\limits_{i<j\in\mathcal{I}}w^{2}[i,j]\sigma^{2}_{h}[i,j]}{\left(\sum\limits_{i<j\in\mathcal{I}}w[i,j]\right)^{2}}}.

III
Problem Formulation

In this paper, we aim to minimize the height error of the final digital elevation model
\sigma_{h}
by jointly optimizing the unmanned aerial vehicle formation
\mathcal{Q}=\{\mathbf{q}_{i},\forall i\in\mathcal{I}\}
, the unmanned aerial vehicle swarm velocity

]

Complexity and Convergence Analysis

The computational complexity of the proposed solution outlined in

1
depends on the complexity of the underlying
Algorithm

2
. This is because, each particle in population

invokes a full run of

2
. Therefore, to analyze the computational complexity of the proposed solution in

1

Algorithm

2
, denoted by
C_{\mathrm{alg}}
.
Algorithm

2
operates on a population of size
D_{1}
, with each particle having a dimensionality
2I
, and its complexity can be divided into two parts: the initialization phase and the iterative main loop. Let
C_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}
denote the computational cost of evaluating the fitness function
\mathcal{F}_{1}
for the entire population. Then, the total complexity of
Algorithm

2
is given by
\mathcal{O}\left(\underbrace{C_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}+D_{1}I}_{\text{Initialization}}+\underbrace{K_{1}\cdot(C_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}D_{1}+D_{1}I)}_{\text{Main loop}}\right)
. The computational cost for evaluating the fitness function
C_{\mathcal{F}_{1}}
arises from two sources:
(i)
formation-only-dependent constraints
, i.e., constraints enforced in the across-track plane (e.g.,
\mathrm{C2}
, and
\mathrm{C5}-\mathrm{C7}
), which scale as
\mathcal{O}(I)
and
(ii)
formation-and-time-dependent constraints
, i.e., constraints enforced across both the across- and along-track planes (e.g., constraint
\mathrm{C8}
), which scale as
\mathcal{O}(NI)
. Therefore, the cost of evaluating the fitness function is

, and the overall computational complexity of

2
becomes

which is polynomial in the problem parameters. Let

denote the complexity of evaluating the fitness function
\mathcal{F}_{2}

\mathcal{O}\left(\underbrace{D_{2}C_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}C_{\text{alg1}}+D_{2}(NI+1)}_{\text{Initialization}}+\underbrace{K_{2}(D_{2}C_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}C_{\text{alg1}}+D_{2}(NI+1)}_{\text{Main loop}})\right)

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied unmanned aerial vehicle-based multi-baseline Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar sensing for generating high-precision digital elevation models, where an arbitrary number of drones are deployed to create independent digital elevation models of a target area. The final DEM is derived through a weighted averaging technique, thereby improving sensing accuracy. We propose a novel co-evolutionary algorithm that jointly optimizes the drone formation, swarm velocity, and communication resource allocation to minimize the height error of the final DEM. Numerical simulations demonstrate that the proposed algorithm outperforms other optimization methods, such as genetic algorithms, simulated annealing, and deep reinforcement learning, in terms of sensing accuracy. Furthermore, we highlight the potential of coordinated unmanned aerial vehicle swarms for achieving centimetric precision in Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar applications. Future work will focus on further refining the optimization strategy to handle dynamic environmental conditions and enable real-time adaptation using online optimization frameworks.

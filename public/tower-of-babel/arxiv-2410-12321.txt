F\in L_{t}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{x}^{q^{\prime},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, then we also have

\left\|{\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F(s)ds}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{p}}(\mathbb{R},L_{x}^{\tilde{q},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\left\|{F}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},L_{x}^{q^{\prime},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

where
C
is an absolute constant depending only on
p
,
\tilde{p}
,
q
,
\tilde{q}
, and
n
.

2.2.
Local well-posedness and stability

In this subsection, we recall the local theory in
H^{1}
for the energy-critical (
INLS
), discuss the fractional chain rules adapted to (
INLS
),
and then establish the corresponding long-time perturbation theory to improve the local theory to also hold with respect to
\dot{H}^{1}
by density.

We start by recalling the following local well-posedness theorem based on Lorentz spaces, which is obtainable by the ordinary contraction arguments. See
[
1
]
and
[
45
]
for details.
The initial time is set to
t=0
in the statement.

Theorem 2.5

(Local well-posedness in
H^{1}
;
[
45
, Theorem 2.5]
)
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
0<b<\min(2,n/2)
.
Define the four exponents
p_{0},p_{b},q_{0},q_{b}\in[1,\infty)
by

p_{\beta}=\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2+2\beta},\hskip 9.24994ptq_{\beta}=\frac{2n(n+2)}{n^{2}+4-4\beta}\hskip 9.24994pt(\beta=0,b).

Then for every initial data
\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a unique solution
u
of (
INLS
) with the maximal lifespan
T_{*}(\phi)>0
in the solution space

C_{t}([0,T_{*}(\phi)),H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L_{t}^{p_{b}}([0,T_{*}(\phi)),W_{x}^{1;q_{b},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

In addition, we have the following properties.

(i)

u
is unique in
L_{t}^{p_{0}}([0,T],W_{x}^{1;q_{0},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L_{t}^{p_{b}}([0,T],W_{x}^{1;q_{b},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for
0<T<T_{*}(\phi)
.

(ii)

u\in L_{t}^{p}([0,T],W_{x}^{1;q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for
0<T<T_{*}(\phi)
and every
L^{2}
-admissible pair
(p,q)
.

(iii)

If
\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}
is small enough, then
T_{*}(\phi)=\infty
and
u
lies in
L_{t}^{p}([0,\infty),W_{x}^{1;q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for every
L^{2}
-admissible pair
(p,q)
.
Moreover,
u
scatters in
H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
forward in time, in the sense that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}e^{-it\Delta}u(t)
exists in
H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(iv)

If
\alpha\geq 1
, then for every
0<T<T_{*}(\phi)
, there is
\delta_{0}>0
such that if
\psi\in H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\left\|{\phi-\psi}\right\|_{H_{x}^{1}}<\delta_{0}
, we have
T_{*}(\psi)>T
and for every
L^{2}
-admissible pair
(p,q)
, the corresponding maximal solution
v
satisfies

\left\|{u-v}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p}([0,T],W_{x}^{1;q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\left\|{\phi-\psi}\right\|_{H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(v)

If
\alpha<1
, and if
\phi_{j}\to\phi
in
H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
then for every
0<T<T_{*}(\phi)
,
we have
u_{j}\to u
in
C_{t}([0,T],H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
where
u_{j}
is the solution of (
INLS
) with initial data
\phi_{j}
.

As in the remark after
[
45
, Theorem 2.5]
, the low nonlinearity power
\alpha<1
poses a major concern since the derivative of the nonlinearity is not Lipschitz, which prevents us from the proof of uniqueness with the usual contraction arguments.
In the case of standard NLS, this issue was detected for
n>6
, and thus Tao and Visan
[
50
]
developed the dedicated perturbation theories using Besov-type function spaces.
A later work by Killip and Visan
[
35
]
in 2013 has only used Sobolev spaces, which is more familiar, in conjunction with the fractional chain rule for fractional nonlinearities by
[
52
]
.

We now aim to construct a perturbation theory following the arguments of
[
35
]
.
To achieve that, we visit the fractional chain rules by Christ and Weinstein
[
12
]
(
C^{1}
nonlinearity) and Visan
[
52
]
(fractional nonlinearity) in the Lorentz space version as follows.

Proposition 2.6

(Fractional chain rule)
.

Let
F
be a
C^{1}(\mathbb{C})
function.
Then for every Schwartz function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<s<1
, we have

\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}F(u)}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|{F^{\prime}(u)}\right\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}u}\right\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

provided that
1<p_{1},p_{2},p<\infty
and
1\leq q_{1},q_{2},q<\infty
(allowing
q=\infty
) satisfy

0<\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}<1,\hskip 9.24994pt0\leq\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}\leq 1.

Proof.

The case
(p_{1},p_{2},p)=(q_{1},q_{2},q)
corresponds to Christ and Weinstein
[
12
, Proposition 3.1]
.
For the general cases, see
[
1
, Lemma 2.4]
.
∎

Proposition 2.7

(Fractional chain rule for the fractional nonlinearity)
.

Let
F
be a Hölder-continuous function (i.e.
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{C})
) of order
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
Then for every Schwartz function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
0<s<\alpha
, and
s/\alpha<s_{0}<1
, we have

\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}F(u)}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|{u}\right\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\alpha-{s}/{s_{0}}}\left\|{|\nabla|^{s_{0}}u}\right\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{{s}/{s_{0}}}

provided that
1<p_{1},p_{2},p<\infty
and
1\leq q_{1},q_{2},q<\infty
(allowing
q=\infty
) satisfy

0<\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha-s/s_{0}}{p_{1}}+\frac{s/s_{0}}{p_{2}}<1,\hskip 9.24994pt0\leq\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{\alpha-s/s_{0}}{q_{1}}+\frac{s/s_{0}}{q_{2}}\leq 1.

Proof.

The case
(p_{1},p_{2},p)=(q_{1},q_{2},q)
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
corresponds to Visan
[
52
, Proposition A.1]
.
For the general case,
we observe the following equivalent characterization of Sobolev-Lorentz spaces

\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}f}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\sim\left\|{{D}_{s}(f)}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for every
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q<\infty
, where

{D}_{s}(f)(x)=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big({\int_{|y|<1}|f(x+ry)-f(x)|dy}\Big)^{2}\frac{dr}{r^{1+2s}}\bigg)^{1/2}.

(2.1)

We can show the equivalence by appropriate real interpolations within the proof of Strichartz
[
47
, Subsection 2.3]
.
The result then follows by applying Hölder’s inequality to a pointwise estimate of
{D}_{s}(f)
as in the proof of
[
52
, Proposition A.1]
.
The Lipscitz case
\alpha=1
can be proved similarly.
∎

To make use of Propositions
2.6
and
2.7
, we consider the following lemma of an interpolative estimate which takes care of the singular coefficient
|x|^{-b}
while preserving the regularity. Later in the perturbation theory for (
INLS
), when
F=|u|^{\alpha}
or
|u|^{\alpha-2}u^{2}
with
\alpha\geq 1
, Lemma
2.8
can be combined with Proposition
2.6
. When
\alpha<1
, it can be combined with Proposition
2.7
instead.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
0\leq s\leq 1
and
0<b<2
. Then for every Schwartz function
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}F)}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}F}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b}{n}},q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

provided that
1<p<n/(b+s)
and
1\leq q<\infty
.

Proof.

The problem is equivalent to showing the following boundedness of a linear operator.

\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}|\nabla|^{-s}F)}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|{F}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b}{n}},q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

The cases
s=0,1
are relatively easy. Note the
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedness of Riesz operators when
s=1
. We now fill in the gap
0<s<1
.
By the real interpolation on Hörmander-Mikhlin multiplier theorem, for every
(j,t)\in\{0,1\}\times\mathbb{R}
, we see that

\left\|{|\nabla|^{j+it}(|x|^{-b}|\nabla|^{-(j+it)}F)}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{2n+4}\left\|{F}\right\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{b}{n}},q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The proportionality constants grow subexponentially in
|t|
for both
j
, and the claim follows by Stein complex interpolation. See Bergh and Löfström
[
3
]
for the complex interpolation of Lorentz spaces.
∎

Combining Lemma
2.8
with each of Propositions
2.6
and
2.7
, we obtain the following two inequalities. The proofs are omitted.

Proposition 2.9

(Fractional chain rule,
|x|^{-b}
-weighted)
.

Let
F
be a
C^{1}(\mathbb{C})
function.
Then for every Schwartz function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<s<1
, we have

\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}F(u))}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|{F^{\prime}(u)}\right\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}u}\right\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

provided that
1<p_{1},p_{2},p<\infty
and
1\leq q_{1},q_{2},q<\infty
(allowing
q=\infty
) satisfy

\frac{b+s}{n}<\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{b}{n}<1,\hskip 9.24994pt0\leq\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}\leq 1.

Proposition 2.10

(Fractional chain rule for the fractional nonlinearity,
|x|^{-b}
-weighted)
.

Let
F
be a Hölder continuous function of order
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
Then for every Schwartz function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
0<s<\alpha
, and
s/\alpha<s_{0}<1
, we have

\left\|{|\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}F(u))}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|{u}\right\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\alpha-\frac{s}{s_{0}}}\left\|{|\nabla|^{s_{0}}u}\right\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{s}{s_{0}}}

provided that
1<p_{1},p_{2},p<\infty
and
1\leq q_{1},q_{2},q<\infty
(allowing
q=\infty
) satisfy

\frac{b+s}{n}<\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha-s/s_{0}}{p_{1}}+\frac{s/s_{0}}{p_{2}}+\frac{b}{n}<1,\hskip 9.24994pt0\leq\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{\alpha-s/s_{0}}{q_{1}}+\frac{s/s_{0}}{q_{2}}\leq 1.

Remark
.

When
b<n(1-\alpha)
in addition, Proposition
2.10
is also provable by direct computation of a pointwise inequality for
{D}_{s}(|x|^{-b}F(u))
, where

is either
|u|^{\alpha}
or
|u|^{\alpha-2}u^{2}
and the operator
{D}_{s}
is defined as (
2.1
).
The combination of Lemma
2.8
and Proposition
2.7
, however, gives a more efficient result by not requiring the condition

.

We are now ready to develop the stability theory of (
INLS
).
We consider the two cases separately: the high nonlinearity power (
\alpha>1
) and the low nonlinearity power
\alpha\leq 1
.

2.2.1.
Case 1: high nonlinearity power (
\alpha\geq 1
)

When
\alpha\geq 1
, Guzman and Xu
[
29
, Subsection 2.1]
has already developed the stability theory of INLS for
n=3,4,5
and
0<b\leq\min(\frac{4}{n},\frac{6-n}{2})
.
The restriction
b\leq\frac{4}{n}
comes from the use of Hardy inequality, which is eliminable by detouring to the use of Lorentz spaces.
In this case,
[
29
, Lemma 2.1]
turns into a simple application of Hölder inequalities:

\left\|{|x|^{-b}|f|^{\alpha}g}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}(I,L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}\\
\leq\left\|{f}\right\|_{S(I)}^{\alpha}\left\|{g}\right\|_{\tilde{W}(I)}

where
S(I)=L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(I,L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})
and
\tilde{W}(I)=L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2+2b}}(I,L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n^{2}+4-4b},2})
.

After a series of direct computations, we reach the stability theory for
\alpha\geq 1
as the following lemma.
For the statement, we temporarily denote

\left\|{f}\right\|_{X(I)}=\left\|{f}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(I,L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2},2})}+\left\|{f}\right\|_{\tilde{W}(I)}.

Lemma 2.11

(Stability for INLS,
\alpha\geq 1
)
.

Let
n=3,4,5
and
0<b\leq(6-n)/2
.
Let
t_{0}\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact time interval and

\tilde{u}:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be an approximate INLS solution in the sense that

i\tilde{u}_{t}+\Delta\tilde{u}=\mu|x|^{-b}|\tilde{u}|^{\alpha}\tilde{u}+e,

with the initial data
\tilde{u}(t_{0})\in\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
at time
t=t_{0}
, satisfying

\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I,\dot{H}_{x}^{1})}\leq E,\hskip 9.24994pt\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{S(I)}\leq L

for some constants
E,L>0
.
Let
u(t_{0})\in\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\left\|{u(t_{0})-\tilde{u}(t_{0})}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}\leq D,\hskip 9.24994pt\left\|{e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}(u(t_{0})-\tilde{u}(t_{0}))}\right\|_{X(I)}\leq\epsilon,\hskip 9.24994pt\left\|{e}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}(I,\dot{W}_{x}^{1;\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}\leq\epsilon

for some constant
D>0
and some
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{1}=\epsilon_{1}(E,L,D)
.
Then, there exists a unique solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
of (
INLS
) with the initial data
u(t_{0})
at time
t=t_{0}
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|{u-\tilde{u}}\right\|_{X(I)}\leq C(E,L,D)\epsilon,\hskip 9.24994pt\left\|{u}\right\|_{S(I)}\leq C(E,L,D).

Lemma
2.11
is intended as the Lorentz-space variant of
[
29
, Proposition 2.4]
.
The proof is omitted since it is replicable from
[
29
]

with a few modification of function spaces:
X(I)
and
\tilde{W}(I)
take the roles of
S^{1}(I)
and
W(I)
respectively in
[
29
]
.

2.2.2.
Case 2: low nonlinearity power (
\alpha\leq 1
)

When
\alpha<1
, although the stability theory for the energy-critical INLS has not been fully developed, some progress has been made for the standard energy-critical NLS, notably by Tao and Visan
[
50
]
and Killip and Visan
[
35
]
. Given the relevance of weighted fractional chain rules, it is natural to consider extending their methods to INLS. We adopt the approach from
[
35
, Section 3]
to avoid Besov-type spaces.

Before the statement, we start with noticing some facts about parameters.
Since
n=3
,
0<b<3/2
always satisfies
\alpha>1
, we may assume
n\geq 4
.
We also choose some more values that depend on or are restricted with respect to
n
and
b
.
Let
0<s,s_{0}<1
and
1<p,q<\infty
satisfy the following relations.

0<s<\dfrac{\alpha}{1+\alpha},\hskip 9.24994pts_{0}=1-s,\hskip 9.24994ptp=\dfrac{2+\alpha}{s},\hskip 9.24994ptq^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}=\dfrac{2+\alpha}{(1+\alpha)s}

Notice that
s/\alpha<s_{0}
and
2<p<\infty
, while
q
is not restricted to lie in
[2,\infty)
.

Next, given a regularity
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
, we define two function spaces

\displaystyle X^{\gamma}(I)={L_{t}^{p}(I,\dot{W}_{x}^{\gamma;\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1-\gamma}{n}-\frac{2}{np}},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},\hskip 9.24994ptY^{\gamma}(I)={L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}(I,\dot{W}_{x}^{\gamma;\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1+\gamma}{n}-\frac{2}{nq^{\prime}}},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

Roughly speaking, these two function spaces play similar roles to the Strichartz and dual Strichartz spaces, respectively, which we can see from the embeddings
\dot{S}^{1}(I)\hookrightarrow X^{\gamma}(I)
(
0\leq\gamma\leq 1
) and
Y^{1}(I)\hookrightarrow\dot{N}^{1}(I)
(
q\geq 2
).
We also see the following unusual Strichartz estimate, which is reminiscent of
[
50
, Lemma 3.2]
and
[
35
, Lemma 3.10]
. (The idea of proof is similar too.)

\left\|{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}F(\tau)\,d\tau}\right\|_{X^{s}(I)}\lesssim\left\|{F}\right\|_{Y^{s}(I)}

(2.2)

We further notice the following analogs of
[
35
, Lemma 3.12]
by the weighted fractional chain rules.

\displaystyle\left\|{|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}u}\right\|_{Y^{s}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{u}\right\|_{X^{0}(I)}^{\alpha}\left\|{u}\right\|_{X^{s}(I)}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\|{|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha}w}\right\|_{Y^{s}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{w}\right\|_{X^{0}(I)}\left\|{u}\right\|_{X^{0}(I)}^{\alpha-\frac{s}{s_{0}}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{X^{s_{0}}(I)}^{\frac{s}{s_{0}}}+\left\|{w}\right\|_{X^{s}(I)}\left\|{u}\right\|_{X^{0}(I)}^{\alpha}

(2.4)

For both (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), the fractional chain rules are applicable when

\frac{b+s}{n}<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1+s}{n}-\frac{2}{nq^{\prime}},

which always holds for our choice of the new parameters
s,s_{0},p,q
since

\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1+s}{n}-\frac{2}{nq^{\prime}}\Big)-\frac{b+s}{n}=\frac{(n+2-2b)(n-b-2s)}{2n(n-b)}>0.

Now we proceed to develop the short-time perturbation theory for (
INLS
) with
\alpha\leq 1
.

Lemma 2.12

(Short-time perturbation for INLS,
\alpha\leq 1
)
.

Assume that
n\geq 4
,
0<b<2
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
.
Let
I
be a compact time interval, and
let
\tilde{u}:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be an approximate solution to (
INLS
) in the sense that

i\tilde{u}_{t}+\Delta\tilde{u}=\mu|x|^{-b}|\tilde{u}|^{\alpha}\tilde{u}+e

for some function
e
representing error.
Assume that

\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I,\dot{H}_{x}^{1})}\leq E

for some constant
E>0
.
Let
t_{0}\in I
and assume also that
u(t_{0})\in\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
obeys

\left\|{u(t_{0})-\tilde{u}(t_{0})}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}\leq D

(2.5)

for some constant
D>0
.
Lastly, assume also the smallness conditions

\displaystyle\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{X^{s}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\delta,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\|{e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}(u(t_{0})-\tilde{u}(t_{0}))}\right\|_{X^{s}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left\|{e}\right\|_{\dot{N}^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon

(2.8)

for some small constants

and

.
Then, there exists a unique solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
of (
INLS
) with the initial data
u(t_{0})
at time

satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|{u-\tilde{u}}\right\|_{X^{s}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left\|{u-\tilde{u}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim D,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left\|{u}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim E+D,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\left\|{|x|^{-b}(|u|^{\alpha}u-|\tilde{u}|^{\alpha}\tilde{u})}\right\|_{Y^{s}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left\|{|x|^{-b}(|u|^{\alpha}u-|\tilde{u}|^{\alpha}\tilde{u})}\right\|_{\dot{N}^{1}(I)}

(2.13)

Proof.

The overall idea follows Killip and Visan
[
35
, Lemma 3.13]
. The use of Lorentz spaces is inspired by Aloui and Tayachi
[
1
]
.

Part 1.

We derive some bounds on the magnitude of
u
and
\tilde{u}
.

First, by Strichartz estimates, Proposition
2.9
, the smallness conditions, and some additional interpolation of spaces, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I,\dot{H}_{x}^{1})}+\left\|{|x|^{-b}|\tilde{u}|^{\alpha}\tilde{u}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}(I,\dot{W}_{x}^{1;\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}+\left\|{e}\right\|_{\dot{N}^{1}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim E+\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(I,L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}^{\alpha}\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{b}}(I,\dot{W}_{x}^{1;{q_{b}},2})}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim E+\delta^{\alpha c}\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{1}(I)}^{1+\alpha(1-c)}+\epsilon

which leads to

\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{1}(I)}\lesssim E

(2.14)

provided that
\delta=O(1)
and
\epsilon_{0}=O(E)
.

Next, by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|{e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}\tilde{u}(t_{0})}\right\|_{X^{s}(I)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{\tilde{u}}\right\|_{X^{s}(I)}+\left\|{|x|^{-b}|\tilde{u}|^{\alpha}\tilde{u}}\right\|_{Y^{s}(I)}+\left\|{e}\right\|_{\dot{N}^{1}(I)}

Part 2.

2.3.
Variational analysis

Now that the stability theory has been established for every
n\geq 3
and
0<b<\min(2,n/2)
,
we finish the section with the variational analysis.
The argument is highly similar to
[
29
]
, and the only difference is that it holds for every such
n
and
b
. Thus, we only provide statements without proof.

It is well known by Yanagida
[
54
, Remark 2.1]
that
the ground state

Q(x)=\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2-b}}{(n-b)(n-2)}\right)^{-\frac{n-2}{2-b}}

is the positive, smooth, and radially decreasing solution of the elliptic equation
\Delta Q+|x|^{-b}Q^{\alpha+1}=0
which is unique up to scaling.
In addition,
Q
attains the optimal constant
C_{1}
for the embedding estimate

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha+2}\,dx\leq C_{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}

and also satisfies

C_{1}^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla Q|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}|Q|^{\alpha+2}\,dx=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}((n-2)(n-b))^{\frac{n-b}{2-b}}\Gamma\Big(\dfrac{n-b}{2-b}\Big)^{2}}{(2-b)\Gamma\Big(\dfrac{n}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\dfrac{2(n-b)}{2-b}\Big)}.

Lemma 2.14

(Energy trapping and coercivity)
.

Suppose that a solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
of (
INLS
) obeys the subthreshold condition

E[\phi]<(1-\delta_{0})E[Q]\text{\hskip 9.44456ptand\hskip 9.44456pt}\left\|{\nabla\phi}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}<\left\|{\nabla Q}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Then there exists
\delta=\delta(n,b,\delta_{0})>0
such that

\sup_{t\in I}\left\|{\nabla u(t)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}<(1-\delta)\left\|{\nabla Q}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Furthermore, we have the comparison

E[u(t)]\sim\left\|{\nabla u(t)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\sim\left\|{\nabla\phi}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\text{\hskip 9.44456ptuniformly in $t\in I$,}

where the implicit constants depend only on
n
and
b
.
Lastly, there exists
\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}(n,b,\delta_{0})>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}-|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{\alpha+2}\,dx\geq\delta^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}\,dx.

3.

The road map mostly follows Guzman and Murphy
[

Proposition 3.1

(Linear profile decomposition)
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
0<b<\min(2,n/2)
.
Let
u_{m}
be a bounded sequence in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then there exist
J^{*}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}

0<\lambda_{m}^{j}<\infty

x_{m}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

t_{m}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
and the remainder terms
w_{m}^{J}
such that

u_{m}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}g_{m}^{j}[e^{it_{m}^{j}\Delta}\phi^{j}]+w_{m}^{J}

for
1\leq J\leq J^{*}
, where the energy-critical symmetry operator
g_{m}^{j}:\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as

(g_{m}^{j}f)(x)=(\lambda_{m}^{j})^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}f\Big(\frac{x-x_{m}^{j}}{\lambda_{m}^{j}}\Big).

The decomposition additionally satisfies

(i)

Energy decoupling: for every
1\leq J\leq J^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\Big(K(u_{m})-\sum_{j=1}^{J}K(\phi^{j})-K(w_{m}^{J})\Big)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\Big(P(u_{m})-\sum_{j=1}^{J}P(\phi^{j})-P(w_{m}^{J})\Big)

\displaystyle=0

where we write the “kinetic energy”
K(f)
and “potential energy”
P(f)
as follows respectively.

K(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla f|^{2}dx,\hskip 9.24994pt-P(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-b}|f|^{\alpha+2}dx

(ii)

Asymptotic (strong) vanishing of the remainder:

\lim_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}=0.

(iii)

(Asymptotic) Weak vanishing of the remainder: For every
1\leq j\leq J<\infty
,

e^{-i{t_{m}^{j}}\Delta}\big[(g_{m}^{j})^{-1}w_{m}^{J}\big]\xrightharpoonup{\hskip 8.04996pt\!}0\text{\hskip 9.44456ptweakly in $\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$}.

(iv)

Asymptotic orthogonality: for every
j\neq j^{\prime}
, we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\Big(\frac{\lambda_{m}^{j}}{\lambda_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}+\frac{\lambda_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}{\lambda_{m}^{j}}+\frac{|x_{m}^{j}-x_{m}^{j^{\prime}}|^{2}}{\lambda_{m}^{j}\lambda_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}+\frac{|t_{m}^{j}(\lambda_{m}^{j})^{2}-t_{m}^{j^{\prime}}(\lambda_{m}^{j^{\prime}})^{2}|}{\lambda_{m}^{j}\lambda_{m}^{j^{\prime}}}\Big)=\infty.

In addition, we may assume either
t_{m}^{j}\equiv 0
or
t_{m}^{j}\to\pm\infty
and that either
x_{m}^{j}\equiv 0
or
|x_{m}^{j}|\to\infty
.

We next visit the technique
[
28

for Guzman and Murphy and
[

for Guzman and Xu.

The authors have utilized the vanishing of

P_{m}=P_{\left|\frac{x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\right|^{-\theta}\leq\cdot\leq\left|\frac{x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\right|^{\theta}},

and also define a smooth physical cutoff
\chi_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
which satisfies

\chi_{m}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\Big|x+\dfrac{x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\Big|\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\Big|\dfrac{x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\Big|,\\[3.87498pt]
0&\text{if }\Big|x+\dfrac{x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\Big|\leq\dfrac{1}{4}\Big|\dfrac{x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\Big|\end{cases}

and also for every multiindex
\iota\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
the bound condition

\left\|{\partial^{\iota}\chi_{m}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq C(\iota)\Big|\frac{x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\Big|^{-|\iota|}.

From the hypothesis, we note that
P_{m}\to{\rm id}
as an operator and
\chi_{m}\to 1
pointwise as
m\to\infty
.

Let
a_{m,T}^{-}=(-t_{m}-T)\lambda_{m}^{2}
and
a_{m,T}^{+}=(-t_{m}+T)\lambda_{m}^{2}
.
We partition the whole time interval
\mathbb{R}
into three pieces;

I_{m,T}^{\circ}=[a_{m,T}^{-},a_{m,T}^{+}]
,

I_{m,T}^{-}=(-\infty,a_{m,T}^{-}]
,
and
I_{m,T}^{+}=[a_{m,T}^{+},\infty)
.
We then define an approximate solution
\tilde{v}_{m,T}
by

\tilde{v}_{m,T}(t,x)=\begin{cases}g_{m}\big[\chi_{m}P_{m}e^{i(t\lambda_{m}^{-2}+t_{m})\Delta}\phi\big](x)=\chi_{m}\Big(\dfrac{x-x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\Big)e^{i(t+t_{m}\lambda_{m}^{2})\Delta}g_{m}[P_{m}\phi](x)&\text{if }t\in I_{m,T}^{\circ},\\[3.87498pt]
e^{i(t-a_{m,T}^{+})\Delta}\tilde{v}_{m,T}(a_{m,T}^{+})&\text{if }t\in I_{m,T}^{+},\\[3.87498pt]
e^{i(t-a_{m,T}^{-})\Delta}\tilde{v}_{m,T}(a_{m,T}^{-})&\text{if }t\in I_{m,T}^{-}.\end{cases}

We note that
\tilde{v}_{m,T}
serves as an approximant to the true solution
v_{m}
of (
INLS
) with the initial data
\tilde{v}_{m,T}(0)=\phi_{m}=g_{m}e^{it_{m}\Delta}\phi
.

To prove (
3.1
), it suffices to check the following three conditions to apply the stability theory;

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{\tilde{v}_{m,T}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 1,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{\tilde{v}_{m,T}(0)-\phi_{m}}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{e_{m,T}}\right\|_{\dot{N}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=0

(3.5)

where

e_{m,T}=(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)\tilde{v}_{m,T}+|x|^{-b}|\tilde{v}_{m,T}|^{\alpha}\tilde{v}_{m,T}

is the approximation error.
We note that the conditions (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and (
3.5
) correspond to Conditions 1, 2, and 3 in the proof of
[
29
, Proposition 3.2]
.
Since (
3.3
) and
(
3.4
) can be proved without much modification from the arguments in
[
29
]
, we focus on (
3.5
).

We decompose
e_{m,T}
as the sum of the linear part
e_{m,T}^{\rm l}=(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)\tilde{v}_{m,T}
and the nonlinear part
e_{m,T}^{\rm nl}=|x|^{-b}|\tilde{v}_{m,T}|^{\alpha}\tilde{v}_{m,T}
.
We perform the estimation on each of the three subintervals
I_{m,T}^{\circ}
,
I_{m,T}^{+}
, and
I_{m,T}^{-}
, but whichever interval we consider, the control of

is relatively straightforward by the arguments in
[
29
]
. (In particular,
e_{m,T}^{\rm l}\equiv 0
on

.)
Thus, we only consider

.

Consider the time interval
I_{m,T}^{\circ}
.
Then, we can write

e_{m,T}^{\rm nl}=\lambda_{m}^{-(2-b)}g_{m}\left[|\lambda_{m}x+x_{m}|^{-b}\chi_{m}^{\alpha+1}|\Phi_{m}|^{\alpha}\Phi_{m}\right]

where
\Phi_{m}=P_{m}e^{i(t\lambda_{m}^{-2}+t_{m})\Delta}\phi
. This yields

\displaystyle\;\left\|{\nabla e_{m,T}^{\rm nl}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}(I_{m,T}^{\circ};L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\lambda_{m}^{b}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\nabla\big(|\lambda_{m}x+x_{m}|^{-b}\chi_{m}^{\alpha+1}|\Phi_{m}|^{\alpha}\Phi_{m}\big)}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I_{m,T}^{\circ};L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\lambda_{m}^{b}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\nabla\big(|\lambda_{m}x+x_{m}|^{-b}\chi_{m}^{\alpha+1}\big)}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I_{m,T}^{\circ};L_{x}^{\frac{1}{\frac{b+1}{n}-\epsilon},\infty})}\left\|{\nabla\big(|\Phi_{m}|^{\alpha}\Phi_{m}\big)}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I_{m,T}^{\circ};L_{x}^{\frac{1}{\frac{n+2-2b}{2n}+\epsilon},2})}

\displaystyle\;\lambda_{m}^{b}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\lambda_{m}^{-b+\epsilon n}|x_{m}|^{-\epsilon n}\cdot\left\|{\Phi_{m}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I_{m,T}^{\circ};L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}^{\alpha}\left\|{\Phi_{m}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I_{m,T}^{\circ};\dot{W}_{x}^{1;\frac{1}{\frac{n^{2}+4-4b}{2n(n+2)}+\epsilon},2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big|\frac{x_{m}}{\lambda_{m}}\Big|^{-\epsilon n+\theta\left(\frac{n-2}{n+2}\alpha+\frac{n-2+2b}{n+2}-\epsilon n\right)}\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}^{\alpha+1}

where the constant
\epsilon=\epsilon(n,b)>0
can be fixed small. We then choose
\theta=\theta(n,b)
so that

{-\epsilon n+\theta\left(\frac{n-2}{n+2}\alpha+\frac{n-2+2b}{n+2}-\epsilon n\right)}<0.

Therefore by the hypothesis

, we obtain

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{\nabla e_{m,T}^{\rm nl}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}(I_{m,T}^{\circ};L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}=0.

On the interval
I_{m,T}^{+}
(
I_{m,T}^{-}
can be handled similarly.), we simply write

e_{m,T}=e_{m,T}^{\rm nl}=|x|^{-b}|V_{m,T}|^{\alpha}V_{m,T}

V_{m,T}=e^{i(t-a_{m,T}^{+})\Delta}\tilde{v}_{m,T}(a_{m,T}^{+})
.
By Hölder’s inequality, Strichartz estimates, and (

),
we have

\displaystyle\;\left\|{\nabla e_{m,T}^{\rm nl}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}(I_{m,T}^{+};L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\left\|{V_{m,T}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(I_{m,T}^{+};L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}^{\alpha}\left\|{V_{m,T}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2+2b}}(I_{m,T}^{+};\dot{W}_{x}^{1;\frac{1}{\frac{n^{2}+4-4b}{2n(n+2)}},2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;C(\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}},E_{c})\left\|{e^{it\Delta}\tilde{v}_{m,T}(a_{m,T}^{+})}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty);L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}^{\alpha}.

We observe that
{e^{it\Delta}\tilde{v}_{m,T}(a_{m,T}^{+})}\to 0
in
{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty);L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}
by dominated convergence as in
[
29
]
,
finishing the proof of (
3.1
).

We omit the proof of (

) as we can borrow the idea from
[
28
]
.
Unlike
[

]
, we need a better regularity condition on (

) to make use of the asymptotic orthogonality condition for

.

∎

We are now ready to explore the critical solution’s compactness properties, which starts with establishing the Palais-Smale Condition.

Proposition 3.3

(Palais-Smale Condition)
.

Let
u_{m}:I_{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
denote a sequence of maximal-lifespan solutions to (
INLS
) such that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\|{\nabla u_{m}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(I_{m},L_{x}^{2})}^{2}=E_{c}<\left\|{\nabla W}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\|{u_{m}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([t_{m},+\infty)\cap I_{m};L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\|{u_{m}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}((-\infty,t_{m}]\cap I_{m};L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}=\infty.

(3.7)

Then there exist
\lambda_{m}\in(0,\infty)
such that
\left\{\lambda_{m}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{m}\Big(t_{m},\dfrac{x}{\lambda_{m}}\Big)\right\}
is precompact in
\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

The overall road map is similar to the proof of
[
29
, Proposition 3.3]
, but the existence of a bad profile is borrowed from
[
34
]
as the higher-dimensional analogy.

Assume
t_{m}\equiv 0
by time translation, then

\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\|{u_{m}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty)\cap I_{m},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\|{u_{m}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}((-\infty,0]\cap I_{m},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}=\infty.

Since
\displaystyle\sup_{m}\left\|{u_{m}(0)}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}^{2}<\infty
by (
3.6
), by passing to a subsequence in
m
, we can apply the linear profile decomposition of Proposition
3.1
to
u_{m}(0)
by

u_{m}(0)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}g_{m}^{j}[e^{it_{m}^{j}\Delta}\phi^{j}]+w_{m}^{J}.

If
\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\Big|\frac{x_{m}^{j}}{\lambda_{m}^{j}}\Big|=\infty
,
then we apply Proposition
3.2
.
In this case, passing to a subsequence in
m
, we define
v_{m}^{j}
as the global solution to (
INLS
) with the initial data

v_{m}^{j}(0)=g_{m}^{j}(e^{it_{m}^{j}\Delta}\phi^{j})
, which satisfies
\left\|{v_{m}^{j}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}<\infty
.
On the other hand,
if
\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\Big|\frac{x_{m}^{j}}{\lambda_{m}^{j}}\Big|<\infty
,
then we may assume
x_{m}^{j}\equiv 0
(passing to a subsequence in
m
again and translating
\phi^{j}
when necessary)
and construct the nonlinear profiles
v^{j}
similar to
[
32
, Definition 2.12]
, associated to
(\phi^{j},\{t_{m}^{j}\})
and satisfying

\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\left\|{v^{j}(t_{m}^{j})-e^{it_{m}^{j}\Delta}\phi^{j}}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}=0.

In this case, we define
\displaystyle v_{m}^{j}(t,x)=\lambda_{m}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}v^{j}\Big(\frac{t}{(\lambda_{m}^{j})^{2}}+t_{m}^{j},\frac{x}{\lambda_{m}^{j}}\Big)
, whose initial data becomes
v_{m}^{j}(0)=g_{m}^{j}(v^{j}(t_{m}^{j}))
.
With the profiles
v_{m}^{j}
settled, we define the approximate solution
u_{m}^{J}
of
u_{m}
by

u_{m}^{J}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}v_{m}^{j}+e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}.

(3.8)

Due to the energy decoupling of the linear profile decomposition (See Proposition
3.1
, (i)), there exist
J_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
j\geq J_{0}
(i.e. when
j
is large),
\left\|{\phi^{j}}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}
is small enough so that the solution
v_{m}^{j}
is globally well-posed and satisfies

\sup_{m}\bigg(\left\|{v_{m}^{j}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\dot{H}_{x}^{1})}+\left\|{v_{m}^{j}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}\bigg)\lesssim\left\|{\phi^{j}}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}.

Here, we propose the following claim.

Claim 1

(At least one bad profile)
.

There exists
1\leq j_{0}<J_{0}
such that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{v_{m}^{j_{0}}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty)\cap I_{m}^{j_{0}},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}=\infty.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq j<J_{0}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{v_{m}^{j}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}<\infty
(i.e.
\displaystyle\sup I_{m}^{j_{0}}=\infty
) on the contrary.
We aim to show the following four conditions of the approximate solution
u_{m}^{J}
;

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq j\leq J_{0}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{v_{m}^{j}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{1}([0,\infty))}

\displaystyle<\infty,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq j\leq J^{*}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{u_{m}(0)-u_{m}^{J}(0)}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle=0,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\limsup_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{u_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty);L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}

\displaystyle\leq C(J_{0},E_{c})<\infty,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{e_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}([0,\infty),\dot{W}_{x}^{1;\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}

\displaystyle=0

(3.12)

where
e_{m}^{J}=(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u_{m}^{J}-\mu|x|^{-b}|u_{m}^{J}|^{\alpha}u_{m}^{J}
.
Then, we observe by the stability theory that

\left\|{u_{m}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}\lesssim C(J_{0},E_{c})<\infty,

which is a desired contradiction.

(
3.9
), (
3.10
), and (
3.11
) can be shown similarly as in Killip and Visan
[
34
]
,
so it remains to prove (
3.12
).
It suffices to show the two nonlinearity estimates

\displaystyle\lim_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{\nabla\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{J}F(v_{m}^{j})-F\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{J}v_{m}^{j}\Big)\Big)}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2-2b},2})}

\displaystyle=0,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lim_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{\nabla\Big(F\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{J}v_{m}^{j}\Big)-F(u_{m}^{J})\Big)}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2-2b},2})}

\displaystyle=0

(3.14)

where
F(z)=|z|^{\alpha}z
.
(
3.13
) follows from the asymptotic orthogonality and the sequential smooth approximation (
3.2
).
Unlike
[
29
, Proposition 3.2]
, the high-regularity convergence in (

) is essential to cope with the case

, since we only have the pointwise estimate

\left|{\nabla\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{J}F(v_{m}^{J})-F\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{J}v_{m}^{J}\Big)\Big)}\right|\lesssim\sum_{j\neq j^{\prime}}|\nabla v_{m}^{j}||v_{m}^{j^{\prime}}|^{\alpha}

has to be estimated while
\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}|{x_{m}^{j}}/{\lambda_{m}^{j}}|=\infty
.

For (
3.14
), it is enough to show the following cross-term estimate, which follows from (
3.11
) and Strichartz estimate on

under the assumption that

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\left\|{|u_{m}^{J}|^{\alpha}\nabla e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2-2b},2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{u_{m}^{J}\nabla e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{n+2}{n-1}}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{n+2}{n-1}})}^{\theta}\left\|{u_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}^{\alpha-\theta}\left\|{\nabla e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-\frac{2(1-b)}{1-\theta}}}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{2n(n+2)}{n^{2}+\frac{4(1-b)}{1-\theta}},2})}^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{u_{m}^{J}\nabla e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{n+2}{n-1}}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{n+2}{n-1}})}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{J}v_{m}^{j}\Big)\nabla e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{n+2}{n-1}}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{n+2}{n-1}})}^{\theta}+\left\|{e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}\nabla e^{it\Delta}w_{m}^{J}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{n+2}{n-1}}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{n+2}{n-1}})}^{\theta}

.

,

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{v_{m}^{j}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty)\cap I_{m}^{j},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}

\displaystyle=\infty\text{\hskip 9.24994ptfor $1\leq j\leq J_{1}$},

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left\|{v_{m}^{j}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,\infty)\cap I_{m}^{j},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}

.

Proposition 3.4

(Minimal energy blow-up solution)
.

.

and a forward-in-time maximal-lifespan solution

) such that

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla u_{c}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([0,T_{*}^{+}),L_{x}^{2})}

\displaystyle=E_{c}<\left\|{\nabla W}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\|{u_{c}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([0,T_{*}^{+}),L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})}

\displaystyle=\infty,

and there is the scaling function
\lambda:[0,T_{*}^{+})\to(0,\infty)
such that the orbit

K=\left\{\lambda(t)^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{c}\Big(t,\dfrac{x}{\lambda(t)}\Big):0\leq t<T_{*}^{+}\right\}

is precompact in
\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We can also obtain a similar result backward in time.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In the last section, with the main ingredients ready, we prove Theorem
1.3
by contradiction.
Any minimal energy blow-up solution
{u_{c}}:[0,T_{*}^{+})\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
INLS
) with the compactness property described in Proposition
3.4
will be eliminated.
We separate the proof into the finite-time blow-up case (
T_{*}^{+}<\infty
) and the soliton case (
T_{*}^{+}=\infty
).

Proposition 4.1

(No finite-time blow-up)
.

There are no minimal energy blow-up solutions
{u_{c}}
with
T_{*}^{+}<\infty
satisfying Proposition
3.4
.

Proof.

We use the following lemma introduced by
[
51
, Section 6]
. (See also
[
35
, Proposition 5.23]
.)

Lemma 4.2

(Reduced Duhamel formula)
.

For each
t\in[0,T_{*}^{+})
, the following weak convergence holds as
T\nearrow T_{*}^{+}
.

{u_{c}}(t)+i\mu\int_{t}^{T}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}\big(|x|^{-b}|{u_{c}}|^{\alpha}{u_{c}}\big)(\tau)\,d\tau\xrightharpoonup{\hskip 8.04996pt}0\text{\hskip 9.44456ptin $\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}

Combined with Bernstein’s inequality, Lemma
4.2
implies
{u_{c}}\in C_{t}([0,T_{*}^{+}),L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
with

\displaystyle\left\|{{u_{c}}(t)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{P_{\leq M}{u_{c}}(t)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\left\|{P_{>M}{u_{c}}(t)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{P_{\leq M}\big(|x|^{-b}|{u_{c}}|^{\alpha}{u_{c}}\big)}\right\|_{L_{t}^{1}([t,T_{*}^{+}),L_{x}^{2})}+M^{-1}\left\|{\nabla{u_{c}}(t)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim M(T_{*}^{+}-t)\left\|{|x|^{-b}|{u_{c}}|^{\alpha}{u_{c}}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([0,T_{*}^{+}),L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}+M^{-1}E_{c}

\displaystyle\lesssim M(T_{*}^{+}-t)E_{c}^{\alpha+1}+M^{-1}E_{c}.

for every time
t\in[0,T_{*}^{+})
and frequency
M\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Therefore, by the conservation of mass, taking
M
arbitrarily large and
t\nearrow T_{*}^{+}
, we conclude that
M[{u_{c}}]=0
and hence
{u_{c}}\equiv 0
, which is a contradiction.
Compared to
[
29
, Subsection 3.1 Claim 1]
, the use of Lorentz spaces relieves the need for Hardy inequality, and we no longer observe the additional range restrictions to
n
and
b
.
∎

Proposition 4.3

(No soliton)
.

There are no minimal energy blow-up solutions
{u_{c}}
with
T_{*}^{+}=\infty
satisfying Proposition
3.4
.

Proof.

We revisit the proof of
[
29
, Subsection 3.1 Claim 2]
for completeness. We can see by direct computation that the argument works for every
n\geq 3
and
0<b<\min(2,n/2)
.
We start with recalling the Morawetz identity as appears in several previous studies for (
INLS
).
For any solution
u(t,x)
to (
INLS
),
given the quantity

Z(t)=2\,\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{u}\nabla u\cdot\nabla a~dx

where
a:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is any smooth function with compact support,
we compute and observe the identity

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z(t)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{Re}(\overline{\partial_{x_{j}}u}{\partial_{x_{k}}u})\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{x_{k}}a~dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}\Delta^{2}a~dx

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mu|x|^{-b}|u|^{\alpha+2}\Delta a~dx-\frac{4}{\alpha+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{\alpha+2}(-\mu b|x|^{-b-2}x)\cdot\nabla a~dx.

Choose
a(x)=R^{2}\psi\Big(\dfrac{|x|}{R}\Big)
, where
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a smooth function such that
\psi(r)=r^{2}
for
r\in[0,1]
and
\psi(r)=0
for
r\in[2,\infty)
.
Take
u=u_{c}
. After further computation, we observe

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z(t)=

\displaystyle\;8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u_{c}|^{2}+\mu|x|^{-b}|u_{c}|^{\alpha+2}~dx

\displaystyle+O\left(\int_{|x|>R}|\nabla u_{c}|^{2}~dx+|x|^{-b}|u_{c}|^{\alpha+2}+|x|^{-2}|u_{c}|^{2}~dx\right).

By the compactness property in Proposition
3.4
,
we can deduce that the big-
O
terms in the right hand side vanish uniformly in
t
as
R\to\infty
, which is analogous to
[
32
, Lemma 5.5]
. Thus, for every
t\in[0,\infty)
, we have by the variational analysis that

\partial_{t}Z(t)\geq\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u_{c}(t)|^{2}dx-o(1)\gtrsim\delta E[u_{c}]-o(1)

where
o(1)\to 0
as
R\to\infty
.
Meanwhile, we see also from Hölder’s inequality that

\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}|Z(t)|\lesssim R^{2}\left\|{\nabla u_{c}}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([0,\infty),L_{x}^{2})}\sim R^{2}E[u_{c}],

and thus the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to
Z(t)
should imply

\big(\delta E[u_{c}]-o(1)\big)T\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}Z(t)dt\leq|Z(T)|+|Z(0)|\lesssim R^{2}E[u_{c}]

for every
0<R,T<\infty
.
However, fixing
0<R<\infty
large enough, the left-hand side grows indefinitely as
T\to\infty
, which is a contradiction.

∎

Remarks on Weakly p p -Kähler hyperbolic manifolds
Changpeng Pan

Abstract
. This paper investigates the generalizations and applications of weakly p p -Kähler hyperbolic manifolds.



1.
Introduction

As is well known, the Hopf conjecture states that a
2n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold with negative sectional curvature should have positive sign Euler characteristic number, i.e.
(-1)^{n}\chi(X)>0
. For the case of
n=1,2
, it can be derived from the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula. For general dimensions, Gromov
[
9
]
introduced the concept of Kähler hyperbolic manifolds, and prove that it has positive sign Euler characteristic number. Specifically, a Kähler manifold with negative sectional curvature is a Kähler hyperbolic manifold, so he proved the Hopf conjecture for Kähler manifold. He also proved that Kähler hyperbolicity implies Kobayashi hyperbolicity and the canonical bundle of Kähler hyperbolic manifold is quasi-ample.

Since then, people have recognized the importance of Kähler hyperbolicity and numerous works on the generalizations and applications of Kähler hyperbolicity have been published. Here, we only cite a portion of them, such as
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
15
]
.

Recently, Bei-Diverio-Eyssidieux-Trapani
[
2
]
introduced the concept of weakly Kähler hyperbolic manifolds in their paper and explored their relationship with Kobayashi hyperbolicity and the distribution of entire curves. The fundamental property of weakly Kähler hyperbolicity is its birational invariance. Through appropriate suitable modifications, they demonstrated that there exists a spectral gap for the Laplacian on weakly Kähler hyperbolic manifolds, thereby proving the conjectures of Lang and Green–Griffiths.

A Kähler manifold is called Kobayashi hyperbolic if there is no non-constant holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow X
. Gromov proved that Kähler hyperbolicity implies Kobayashi hyperbolicity. Chen-Yang
[
6
]
generalized this result to symplectic manifolds that are homotopy equivalent to Riemannian manifolds with negative sectional curvature. Inspired by their work, Li
[
14
]
showed that there are no rational curves on Kähler exact manifold, i.e., no non-constant holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{CP}^{1}\rightarrow X
, which implies that the canonical bundle of a Kähler exact manifold of general type is ample.

Marouani-Popovici
[
15
]
and Haggui-Marouani
[
10
]
considered the existence of non-constant holomorphic maps
f:\mathbb{C}^{p}\rightarrow X
, thereby introducing the concepts of balanced hyperbolicity and
p
-Kähler hyperbolicity. They studied the properties of these concepts.

Inspired by the above work, this paper aims to investigate the distribution of non-constant rational curves on weakly
p
-Kähler hyperbolic (exact) manifolds.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be the Kähler universal covering map. A closed
p
-form
\alpha
on
X
is called
\widetilde{d}(\text{bounded})
if the pull back
p
-form

on

is of the form
\widetilde{d}\beta

\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\omega})}<+\infty
.
Cohomology calss

is called big and nef if

and
\int_{X}[\mu]^{n}>0
.

Definition 1.1
.

[
2
]

(X,\omega)
is called weakly Kähler hyperbolic if there exists a closed real form

In

, the authors proved that weakly Kähler hyperbolic manifolds possess some interesting analytic and topological properties, For example, they showed that the universal cover of a weakly Kähler hyperbolic manifold satisfies the linear isoperimetric inequality, its subvarieties outside the degeneracy set have infinite fundamental groups, and the image of all entire curves must lie within the degeneracy set, among others. The presented paper aims to generalize this result to weakly

In

Definition 2.1

Let

Proposition 3.1

(linear isoperimetric inequality)
.

Let
X
be a weakly
p
-Kähler hyperbolic manifold,
g
any Riemannian metric on
X
, and consider the Riemannian universal cover
\pi:(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{g})\rightarrow(X,g)
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any bounded domain
\Omega\subset\widetilde{X}
with
C^{1}
boundary, we have the following linear isoperimetric inequality

\mbox{Vol}_{\widetilde{g}}(\Omega)\leq C\mbox{Area}_{\widetilde{g}}(\partial\Omega)

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a Kähler form and
[\alpha]\in H^{p,p}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a strictly nef class which is
\widetilde{d}(\text{bounded})
. Suppose
\pi^{*}\alpha=\widetilde{d}(\beta)
, let
\eta=\beta\wedge\pi^{*}\omega^{n-p}
. Then
\widetilde{d}\eta=\pi^{*}\mu\wedge\pi^{*}\omega^{n-p}
and
\eta
is bounded. Given any metric
g
on
X
, normalize it such that

\int_{X}dv_{g}=\int_{X}\mu\wedge\omega^{n-p}.

As
\int_{X}:H^{2n}(X,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an isomorphism. Then there exists a
(2n-1)
-form
\rho
on
X
such that
dv_{g}-\mu\wedge\omega^{n-p}=d\rho
. Then
\pi^{*}dv_{g}=\widetilde{d}(\eta+\pi^{*}\rho)
and

|\eta+\pi^{*}\rho|_{\tilde{g}}\leq C.

Therefore, using Stokes’ formula we have

\begin{split}\mbox{Vol}_{\tilde{g}}(\Omega)=&\int_{\Omega}\pi^{*}dv_{g}=\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{d}(\eta+\pi^{*}\rho)\\
=&\int_{\partial\Omega}(\eta+\pi^{*}\rho)\leq C\mbox{Area}_{\tilde{g}}(\partial\Omega).\end{split}

∎

\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}
. Let
(E,\bar{\partial}_{E})
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
X
. If there exists a modification
(X^{{}^{\prime}},\omega^{{}^{\prime}})
such that
\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}\cdot C_{E^{{}^{\prime}},p}<1
for
0\leq p\leq n
, then

\chi^{n}(X,E)>0.

The original proof idea is due to Gromov
[
9
]
, Bei-Diverio-Eyssidieux-Trapani
[
2
]
modified to apply to the weakly Kähler hyperbolic case. We also adopt the method of Bei-Diverio-Eyssidieux-Trapani, and in the following sections, we present the key steps of the proof.

4.3.
Vanishing of
L^{2}
-harmonic forms

The following proposition plays a crucial role in the proof of the spectral gap theorem.

Proposition 4.2
.

[
2
]

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold,
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{\omega})\rightarrow(M,\omega)
is a Kähler universal cover map. Let
(E,H)
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
,
(\tilde{E},\tilde{H})
be the pull back of
(E,H)
. Given any
p,q\in\{0,...,n\}
, let

\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}:L^{2}\Omega^{p,q}(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{E})\rightarrow L^{2}\Omega^{p,q}(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{E})

be the closure of
\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}:\Omega^{p,q}_{c}(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{E})\rightarrow\Omega_{c}^{p,q}(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{E})
and let
\{\mathcal{E}(\lambda)\}_{\lambda}
be its spectral
resolution. For any fixed
\lambda_{0}>0
, there exists
\epsilon_{0}(\lambda_{0})>0
such that if
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}(\lambda_{0})
, and
U_{\epsilon}
is an open set in
M
with

\mbox{Vol}_{\omega}(U_{\epsilon})<\epsilon
, we have:

\int_{\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}}|\widetilde{\eta}|_{\widetilde{\omega}}^{2}dv_{\widetilde{\omega}}\leq\int_{\widetilde{M}\setminus\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}}|\widetilde{\eta}|_{\widetilde{\omega}}^{2}dv_{\widetilde{\omega}}

for all
\widetilde{\eta}\in im(\mathcal{E}(\lambda_{0}))
, where
\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}
is the preimage of
U_{\epsilon}
through
\pi:\widetilde{M}\rightarrow M.

Now, we can prove the following spectral gap theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\nu:(X^{{}^{\prime}},\omega^{{}^{\prime}})\rightarrow(X,\omega)
be a modification related to a
\widetilde{d}(\text{bounded})
big and nef class. There is non-negtive constant
\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}
corresponding to
(X^{{}^{\prime}},\omega^{{}^{\prime}})
.

Let
E^{{}^{\prime}}=\nu^{*}E
be the pull back holomorphic vector bundle over
X^{{}^{\prime}}
. Assume
H^{{}^{\prime}}
be a Hermitian metric on
E^{{}^{\prime}}
such that
\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}C_{E^{{}^{\prime}},H^{{}^{\prime}},p}<\frac{1}{3}
. Let
\pi:(\widetilde{X^{{}^{\prime}}},\widetilde{\omega^{{}^{\prime}}})\rightarrow(X^{{}^{\prime}},\omega^{{}^{\prime}})
be the universal covering of
X^{{}^{\prime}}
endowed with the pull-back metric
\widetilde{\omega^{{}^{\prime}}}:=\pi^{*}\omega^{{}^{\prime}}
. Let
(\widetilde{E^{{}^{\prime}}},\widetilde{H^{{}^{\prime}}})
be the pull back Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
\widetilde{X^{{}^{\prime}}}
. Then for
0\leq p\leq n-1
there exists a constant
C
such that for any
\eta\in\mathcal{D}^{p,0}(\widetilde{X^{{}^{\prime}}},\widetilde{E^{{}^{\prime}}})
, we have

\begin{split}&\langle\eta,\Delta_{\partial_{\widetilde{H}^{{}^{\prime}}}}\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq C\langle\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}.\\
&\langle\eta,\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}^{{}^{\prime}}}}\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq C\langle\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}\end{split}

where
\Delta_{\partial_{\widetilde{H}^{{}^{\prime}}}}=\partial_{\widetilde{H}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*}\partial_{\widetilde{H}^{{}^{\prime}}}+\partial_{\widetilde{H}^{{}^{\prime}}}\partial_{\widetilde{H}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{*}
.

Proof.

For the sake of simplicity let us replace
X
with
X^{{}^{\prime}}
,
[\mu]
with
\mu^{{}^{\prime}}
,
(E,H)
with
(E^{{}^{\prime}},H^{{}^{\prime}})
and
\omega
with
\omega^{{}^{\prime}}
. Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be the universal cover map.

We consider the smooth forms
\mu_{t}:=\mu+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi_{t}
so that
\mu_{t}+t\omega
is a Kähler form on
X
for all
t>0
. Let
\alpha
be a smooth bounded primitive of
\widetilde{\mu}=\pi^{*}\mu
, so that

\alpha_{t}=\alpha+\sqrt{-1}\widetilde{\partial}\widetilde{\phi}_{t}

is a smooth bounded primitive of
\widetilde{\mu}_{t}=\pi^{*}\mu
, where
\widetilde{\phi}_{t}=\phi_{t}\circ\pi
.
Now, let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\widetilde{X},\Omega^{p,0}\otimes\widetilde{E})
,
\eta^{*\widetilde{H}}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\widetilde{X},\Omega^{0,p}\otimes\widetilde{E}^{*})
satisfies

\eta^{*\widetilde{H}}(e)=\widetilde{H}(e,\eta),\quad\forall e\in\widetilde{E}.

So

\begin{split}D_{\widetilde{H}}\eta^{*\widetilde{H}}(e)=&d(\eta^{*\widetilde{H}}(e))-(-1)^{p}\eta^{*\widetilde{H}}(D_{\widetilde{H}}e)\\
=&\widetilde{H}(D_{\widetilde{H}}e,\eta)+\widetilde{H}(e,D_{\widetilde{H}}\eta)-\widetilde{H}(D_{\widetilde{H}}e,\eta)\\
=&\widetilde{H}(e,D_{\widetilde{H}}\eta),\end{split}

and
|D_{\widetilde{H}}\eta|^{2}_{\widetilde{H},\widetilde{\omega}}=|D_{\widetilde{H}}\eta^{*\widetilde{H}}|_{\widetilde{H}^{*},\widetilde{\omega}}^{2}
. For the convenience of writing, we may omit upper and lower indices. Let
k
be an integer with
0\leq k\leq n-p-1
. Then we have

\begin{split}&d(\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}_{t}\wedge\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k-1}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k})\\
=&d\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}_{t}\wedge\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k-1}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k}+\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\\
=&\mbox{tr}(D_{\widetilde{H}}\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}_{t}\wedge\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k-1}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\\
&+(-1)^{p}\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge D_{\widetilde{H}}\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\alpha}_{t}\wedge\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k-1}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\\
&+\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\widetilde{X}}(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\Big|\leq&C(n,p,k)\|\alpha_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\tilde{X}}|D_{\widetilde{H}}\eta||\eta|dv_{\widetilde{\omega}}\\
\leq&C(n,p,k)\|\alpha_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\omega})}\langle\Delta_{D_{\widetilde{H}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\omega})}^{1/2}\end{split}

where
\Delta_{D_{\widetilde{H}}}=D_{\widetilde{H}}^{*}D_{\widetilde{H}}+D_{\widetilde{H}}D_{\widetilde{H}}^{*}
and
D_{\widetilde{H}}^{*}=\sqrt{-1}[\Lambda_{\widetilde{\omega}},\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}-\partial_{\widetilde{H}}]
. Let

C(t)=C(n,p,k)\|\alpha_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k}\|_{L^{\infty}}

Since
C^{\infty}_{0}(\widetilde{X},\Omega^{p,0}\otimes\widetilde{E})
is dense in
L^{2}\Omega^{p,0}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{E})
, we can conclude that for each
\eta\in\mathcal{D}^{p,0}
it holds

(4.2)

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{\widetilde{X}}(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\mu}^{n-p-k}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k}\Big|\leq&C(t)\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\omega})}\langle\Delta_{D_{\widetilde{H}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\omega})}^{1/2}\end{split}

Fix
\lambda_{0}=C_{E}+1
,
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}(\lambda_{0})
. Let
U_{\epsilon}
be a open neighborhood of divisor
D
such that
\mbox{Vol}_{\omega}(U_{\epsilon})<\epsilon
. Since
\phi_{t}
converges to
\phi_{0}
in
C^{2,\alpha}(X\setminus D)
, and
\mu+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi_{0}>0
on
X\setminus D
, there
exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\epsilon)>0
independent of
t
such that
\widetilde{\mu}_{t}+t\widetilde{\omega}>C_{1}\widetilde{\omega}
on
\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}
where
\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}=\pi^{-1}(U_{\epsilon})
. Then by Proposition
4.2
, we have

\int_{\widetilde{X}}|\eta|_{\widetilde{\omega}}^{2}dv_{\widetilde{\omega}}\leq 2\int_{\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}}|\eta|_{\widetilde{\omega}}^{2}dv_{\widetilde{\omega}}

for any
\eta\in Im(\mathcal{E}(\lambda_{0}))
. Therefore, for any
\eta\in Im(\mathcal{E}(\lambda_{0}))
, we get

\begin{split}\int_{\widetilde{X}}|\eta|_{\widetilde{\omega}}^{2}dv_{\widetilde{\omega}}\leq&2\int_{\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}}|\eta|_{\widetilde{\omega}}^{2}dv_{\widetilde{\omega}}\\
=&2C(n,p)\int_{\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}}(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{n-p}\\
\leq&2\frac{C(n,p)}{C_{1}^{n-p}}\int_{\widetilde{X}\setminus\widetilde{U}_{\epsilon}}(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge(\widetilde{\mu}_{t}+t\widetilde{\omega})^{n-p}\\
\leq&2\frac{C(n,p)}{C_{1}^{n-p}}\int_{\widetilde{X}}(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge(\widetilde{\mu}_{t}+t\widetilde{\omega})^{n-p}\end{split}

Then by (
4.2
), we have

\begin{split}&\frac{C(n,p)}{C_{1}^{n-p}}\int_{\widetilde{X}}(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge(\widetilde{\mu}_{t}+t\widetilde{\omega})^{n-p}\\
\leq&\frac{C(n,p)}{C_{1}^{n-p}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-p}C(n,p,k)t^{k}|\int_{\widetilde{X}}(\sqrt{-1})^{p^{2}}\mbox{tr}(\eta\wedge\eta^{*})\wedge\widetilde{\mu}_{t}^{n-p-k}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}^{k}|\\
\leq&\frac{C(n,p)}{C_{1}^{n-p}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-p-1}C(n,p,k)t^{k}C(t)\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}\langle\Delta_{D_{\widetilde{H}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\\
&+\frac{1}{C_{1}^{n-p}}t^{n-p}\int_{\widetilde{X}}|\eta|^{2}_{\widetilde{\omega}}dv_{\widetilde{\omega}}\end{split}

That is

\begin{split}(1-\frac{2}{C_{1}^{n-p}}t^{n-p})\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\frac{C(n,p)}{C_{1}^{n-p}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-p-1}C(n,p,k)t^{k}C(t)\langle\Delta_{D_{\widetilde{H}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.\end{split}

For
t\in(0,\frac{1}{2^{1/(n-p)}}C_{1})
, let

H(t)=\frac{2\frac{C(n,p)}{C_{1}^{n-p}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-p-1}C(n,p,k)t^{k}C(t)}{1-\frac{2}{C_{1}^{n-p}}t^{n-p}}>0.

So

(4.3)

\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq H^{2}(t)\langle\Delta_{D_{\widetilde{H}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Since we have

\Delta_{D_{\widetilde{H}}}=2\Delta_{\bar{\partial_{\widetilde{E}}}}-\sqrt{-1}[F_{\widetilde{H}},\Lambda_{\widetilde{\omega}}].

Then

\begin{split}\langle\Delta_{D_{\widetilde{H}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}=&2\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\widetilde{\omega}}(F_{\widetilde{H}}\wedge\eta),\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
\leq&2\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}+C_{E,H,p}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

Combine with (
4.3
), we get

\begin{split}(1-H^{2}(t)C_{E,H,p})\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2H^{2}(t)\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}\end{split}

Let
\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}=\inf\{H^{2}(t)|\ \forall t\in(0,\frac{1}{2^{1/(n-p)}}C_{1}]\}
. If
\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}C_{E}<\frac{1}{3}
, then we can choose
t_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2^{1/(n-p)}}C_{1}]
and Hermitian metric
H
such that
\frac{1}{3}-H^{2}(t_{0})C_{E,H,p}>0
. Then we have

\begin{split}\frac{1-H^{2}(t_{0})C_{E,H,p}}{2H^{2}(t_{0})}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}\end{split}

For any
\psi\in\mathcal{D}^{p,0}
. Let
\eta=\mathcal{E}(\lambda_{0})(\psi)
and
v=\psi-\eta
. Then

\begin{split}\langle\eta,v\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}\eta,v\rangle_{L^{2}}=0.\end{split}

and

\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\lambda_{0}\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}v,v\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Therefore we have

\begin{split}\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}\psi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}=&\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}\eta,\eta\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}v,v\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
\geq&\frac{1-H^{2}(t_{0})C_{E,H,p}}{2H^{2}(t_{0})}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lambda_{0}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\geq&C\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

where
C_{2}=\min\{1+C_{E},\frac{1-H^{2}(t_{0})C_{E,H,p}}{2H^{2}(t_{0})}\}
. Since
\frac{1}{3}-H^{2}(t_{0})C_{E,H,p}>0
, we have

\begin{split}\langle\Delta_{\partial_{\widetilde{H}}}\psi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq&C\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

where
C=C_{2}-C_{E,H,p}>0
.
∎

Outline of the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
*:\Omega^{p,0}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{E})\rightarrow\Omega^{n,n-p}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{E})
be the Hodge star operator. The following hold

\begin{split}*\circ\Delta_{\partial_{\widetilde{H}}}=&\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}\circ*\\
\Delta_{\partial_{\widetilde{H}}}\circ*=&*\circ\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}.\end{split}

So
\eta\in L^{2}\Omega^{n,n-p}
is
\Delta_{\bar{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}}}
-harmonic if and only if
*\eta
is
\Delta_{\partial_{\widetilde{H}}}
-harmonic. Then by Theorem
4.3
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{n,n-p}(\widetilde{X}^{{}^{\prime}},\widetilde{E}^{{}^{\prime}})=\{0\}
for
0\leq p\leq n-1
. By birational invariance
[
13
, Corollary 11.4]
of
L^{2}
-Hodge numbers in bidegree
(n,q)
, we get
\mathcal{H}^{n,n-p}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{E})=\{0\}
for
0\leq p\leq n-1
.

To finish the proof, one also need to prove
\mathcal{H}^{n,0}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{E})\neq\{0\}
. As shown in
[
2
]
, this can be achieved through the Vafa-Witten trick. We omit this part here.

After prove the non-vanishing theorem, we then get
\chi^{n}(X,E)>0
.

∎

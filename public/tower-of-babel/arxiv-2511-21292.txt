The Automorphism groups of a maximal function field with the second largest genus and its algebraic geometry codes
Xubin Hu, Liming Ma

Abstract
. In this manuscript, we investigate the automorphism group of a maximal function field with the second largest possible genus over finite field of even characteristic, which is called the Abdón–Torres function field. As an application, we determine the automorphism groups of one-point algebraic geometry codes from such a maximal function field. It turns out that the automorphism groups of one-point algebraic geometry codes agree with that of the Abdón–Torres function field except for the trivial cases. Moreover, we provide a family of maximal function fields with explicit defining equations via considering fixed subfields with respect to some subgroups of automorphism group of the Abdón–Torres function field.



1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
be the finite field with
\ell
elements and
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{\ell}
be its algebraic closure. Let
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
be an algebraic function field of one variable with genus
g
over the full constant field
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. The celebrated Hasse–Weil Theorem states that the number
N(F)
of rational places of
F
satisfies the following inequality

|N(F)-(\ell+1)|\leq 2g\sqrt{\ell}.

Any function field
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
with the number of rational places
N(F)
attaining the upper Hasse–Weil bound
\ell+1+2g\sqrt{\ell}
is called
maximal
.
If
F
is maximal, then either
g=0
or
\ell=q^{2}
is a square of a prime power
q
.

Maximal function fields are widely studied not only in the viewpoints of number theory and algebraic geometry, but also due to its various applications in coding theory and cryptography
[
14
,
28
,
39
,
40
]
.
In order to construct long algebraic geometry codes with good properties, one might ask what are the possible values of
g
for maximal function fields over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
.
It was shown that
g\leq g_{1}=(q-1)q/2
by Ihara in
[
21
]
.
The well-known Hermitian function field
H={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(u,v)
defined by

v^{q}+v=u^{q+1}

is a maximal function field with the largest possible genus
(q-1)q/2
. Moreover, it is the unique maximal function field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with genus
(q-1)q/2
up to isomorphism
[
35
]
.

It was conjectured in
[
44
]
and proven in
[
13
]
that if
F
is a maximal function field over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with genus
g
, then
g=q(q-1)/2
or
g\leq g_{2}=\lfloor(q-1)^{2}/4\rfloor
, here
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the integer part of
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
For an odd prime power
q
, it was proved in
[
12
]
the maximal function field over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with genus
g=(q-1)^{2}/4
is isomorphic to
X_{2}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x,y)
defined by

y^{q}+y=x^{(q+1)/2}.

For an even prime power
q=2^{n}
, Abdón and Torres proved in
[
1
]
that a maximal function field over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with genus
g=q(q-2)/4
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-isomorphic to
Y_{2}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x,y)
defined by

y^{q/2}+y^{q/2^{2}}+\dots+y^{2}+y=x^{q+1},

provided that
q/2
is a Weierstrass pole number of some rational place of
Y_{2}\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q^{2}}
. In this paper, we call such a function field as the Abdón-Torres function field.
In
[
25
]
, Korchmáros and Torres proved that the genus
g
of a maximal function field over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
is either
g_{1},g_{2}
, or
\leq g_{3}=\lfloor(q^{2}-q+4)/6\rfloor
, and the following three function fields attain the bound
g_{3}
:

\displaystyle X_{3}\,

\displaystyle{:=}\,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x,y),\quad x^{(q+1)/3}+x^{2(q+1)/3}+y^{1+q}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }q\equiv 2\pmod{3},

\displaystyle Y_{3}\,

\displaystyle{:=}\,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x,y),\quad y^{q}-yx^{2(q-1)/3}+x^{(q-1)/3}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{3},

\displaystyle Z_{3}\,

\displaystyle{:=}\,{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x,y),\quad y^{q}+y+(\sum_{j=1}^{t}x^{q/3^{j}})^{2}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{ if }q\equiv 0\pmod{3}.

All these maximal function fields are Galois subfields of the Hermitian function fields.

A well-known result of Serre implies that any function subfield of a maximal function field is maximal as well
[
26
, Proposition 6]
.
The automorphism group of the Hermitian function field
H={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(u,v)
is isomorphic to the projective unitary group
\operatorname{PGU}_{3}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
with large order
q^{3}(q^{2}-1)(q^{3}+1)>16g_{1}^{4}
and many subgroups
[
36
]
.
Hence, many maximal function fields can be constructed systematically by considering fixed subfields of subgroups of automorphism groups of the Hermitian function fields
[
8
,
9
,
16
,
2
,
3
,
10
,
30
,
31
]
.
In the early literatures, maximal function fields are proved to be subfields of the Hermitian function fields or undetermined.
However, Giulietti and Korchmáros provide an explicit construction of maximal function field with genus
g=\frac{1}{2}(n^{3}+1)(n^{2}-2)+1
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
which is not a subfield of the Hermitian function field for
q=n^{3}>8
in
[
18
]
.
Its automorphism group is determined explicitly as well in
[
18
]
and has a large size
n^{3}(n^{3}+1)(n^{2}-1)(n^{2}-n+1)
, which is roughly
4g^{2}
. Maximal subfields of Giulietti–Korchmáros function field have been determined in
[
19
]
.
Moreover, the Giulietti–Korchmáros function field has been generalized by Garcia, Güneri and Stichtenoth in
[
15
]
and by Beelen and Montanucci in
[
4
]
.

Recently, the automorphism groups of the maximal function fields
X_{3},Y_{3}
and
Z_{3}
with the third largest genus have been determined in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
It is very strange that the automorphism groups of maximal function fields
X_{2}
and
Y_{2}
with the second largest genus were not mentioned in the above literatures.
Finally, we found that the automorphism group of
X_{2}
is isomorphic to a semiproduct of a cyclic normal subgroup
C_{(q+1)/2}
of order
(q+1)/2
and the projective linear group
\text{PGL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
in
[
20
, Theorem 12.11]
. However, it seems that the automorphism group
\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
of the Abdón–Torres function field
Y_{2}
is not well-documented in the past researches.
Therefore, we determine the automorphism group of the maximal Abdón–Torres function field
Y_{2}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
and provide a family of maximal function fields with explicit defining equations via considering its Galois subfields.

By choosing divisors carefully, an automorphism of the function field can induce an automorphism of the algebraic geometry code from such a function field.
In 1990, Stichtenoth determined the automorphism group of rational algebraic geometry codes in
[
37
]
.
In 1995, Xing determined the automorphism group of the one-point algebraic geometry codes from elliptic curves in
[
42
]
and the one-point Hermitian codes in
[
43
]
. In 1998, Wesemeyer developed a systematic method to compute the automorphism group of a large family of one-point algebraic geometry codes from plane curves and generalized Xing’s result for some cases in
[
41
]
. In 2006, this result has been further generalized by Joyner and Ksir in
[
22
]
.
In fact, they discovered that one can lift an automorphism of algebraic geometry codes to an automorphism of the associated curve when the divisor is very ample.
In 2008, Giulietti and Korchmáros systematically studied the lifting of semi-linear automorphisms of algebraic geometry codes that preserve Hamming weights given in
[
33
]
. They proved that such automorphisms of Deligne–Lusztig codes can be lifted in most cases
[
17
]
.
Aside from these framework, several cases were studied as well, including the algebraic geometry codes related to the curve
y^{q}+y=x^{q^{r}+1}
in
[
23
]
; the automorphism group of generalized algebraic geometry codes related to hyperelliptic curves in
[
34
]
; the construction of Hermitian codes with automorphism group isomorphic to
\text{PGL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
for odd
q
in
[
24
]
, etc.
In this paper, we shall determine the automorphism group of the one-point algebraic geometry codes from the Abdón–Torres function field
Y_{2}
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we provide preliminary results on the theory of function fields, algebraic geometry codes and their automorphism groups, and the maximal curve with the second largest genus given in
[
1
]
.
In Section
3
, we compute explicitly the Weierstrass semigroups at all rational places and determine the automorphism group of the maximal curve with the second largest genus. In Section
4
, we investigate fixed subfields of the maximal function field of the second largest genus and we provide explicit equation of some of these Galois subfields. In the final section, as a major application, we completely determine the automorphism group of the one-point algebraic geometry codes from the Abdón–Torres function field
Y_{2}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some preliminary results on global function field and ramification theory, algebraic geometry codes and their automorphism groups, and a maximal curve with the second largest genus. We prefer the language of function fields. For more details, please refer to
[
38
,
32
]
.

2.1.
Global function fields and ramification theory

Let
\ell
be a prime power,
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
be the finite field with
\ell
elements and

\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{\ell}
be its algebraic closure. A function field
F
of one variable over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
is a finite algebraic extension of the rational function field
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}(x)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
.
If any element in
F\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
is transcendental over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, then it is called a global function field and denoted by
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a valuation ring of
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. It has a unique maximal ideal
P
, which is called a place of
F
.
Such a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
can be denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{P}
and its corresponding normalized discrete valuation is denoted by
\nu_{P}
. The degree
\deg(P)
of
P
is defined to be the extension degree of residue class field
\kappa_{P}=\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. If
\deg(P)=[\kappa_{P}:{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}]=1
, then
P
is called a rational place of
F
.
The set of places of
F
is denoted by
{\mathbb{P}}_{F}
and rational places form a subset
{\mathbb{P}}_{F}^{(1)}
of
{\mathbb{P}}_{F}
.
A finite sum of places
D=\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}n_{P}P
with
n_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}
is called a divisor of
F
, and all divisors form a free abelian group
\Div(F)
. The set
{\rm supp}(D)=\{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}:n_{P}\neq 0\}
is called the support of
D
. The degree of
D
is defined by
\deg D=\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}n_{P}\deg(P)
. The subgroup of divisors with degree
0
is denoted by
\Div^{0}(F)
.

For an element
f\in F^{*}=F\setminus\{0\}
, a place
P
is a zero of
f
if and only if
\nu_{P}(f)>0
. Denote the set of all zeros of
f
by
Z(f)
. Then the divisor
(f)_{0}^{F}=\sum_{P\in Z(f)}\nu_{P}(f)P
is called the zero divisor of
f
and the divisor
(f)_{\infty}^{F}=(1/f)_{0}^{F}
is called the pole divisor of
f
.
The principal divisor of
f
is
(f)^{F}=(f)_{0}^{F}-(1/f)_{0}^{F}
, and its degree is
0
. If there is no ambiguity, then we can drop the superscript and simply write
(f)
,
(f)_{0}
and
(f)_{\infty}
. All principal divisors form a subgroup
\operatorname{Princ}(F)
of
\Div^{0}(F)
, and denote the divisor class group of degree
0
of
F
by
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(F)=\Div^{0}(F)/\operatorname{Princ}(F)
.

The Riemann–Roch space associated with a divisor
D
of
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(D)=\{f\in F^{*}\colon(f)+D\geq 0\}\cup\{0\}.

It is a finite-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
with dimension
\dim\mathcal{L}(D)
. By the Riemann–Roch Theorem,

\deg D+1-\dim\mathcal{L}(D)

has a non-negative least upper bound for all divisors
D
.
It is the most important birational invariant of function field
F
, which is called the genus of
F
and denoted by
g(F)
.
If
\deg D\geq 2g(F)-1
, then

\dim\mathcal{L}(D)=\deg D+1-g(F).

The automorphism group over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
of a global function field
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
is defined by

\Aut(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})=\{\sigma:\sigma\text{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\text{-automorphism of }F\}.

For each
\sigma\in\Aut(F/\ell)
, the set
\sigma(P)=\{\sigma(z)\colon z\in P\}
is a place of
F
with degree
\deg(P)
as well.
This action naturally extends to
\Div(F)
by
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearity.
From the fact
\nu_{\sigma(P)}(\sigma(f))=\nu_{P}(f)
given in
[
38
, Lemma 3.5.2]
, we have
\sigma(\mathcal{L}(D))=\mathcal{L}(\sigma(D))
for any
\sigma\in\Aut(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
and
D\in\Div(F)
. For a subgroup
G
of
\Aut(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
, the fixed subfield of
F
under
G
is defined by

F^{G}=\{z\in F\colon\phi(z)=z\text{ for all }\phi\in G\}.

By Galois theory,
F/F^{G}
is a finite Galois extension.

Let
E/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
be a finite Galois extension of
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
with Galois group
\Gal(E/F)
. Let
Q
be a place of
E
and
P
be a place of
F
lying under
Q
. For a prime element
t_{P}
of
P
, the integer
e(Q|P)=\nu_{Q}(t_{P})
is the ramification index of
Q
over
P
.
The relative degree
f(Q|P)
of
Q
over
P
is equals to
[\kappa_{Q}:\kappa_{P}]
. The Hurwitz genus formula states that

2g(E)-2=(2g(F)-2)[E:F]+\deg\Diff(E/F),

where
\Diff(E/F)=\sum_{P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{F}}\sum_{Q|P}d(Q|P)Q

is the different divisor of
E/F
. The different exponent
d(Q|P)
can be calculated via higher ramification groups by Hilbert ramification theory.
The group

G_{-1}(Q|P)=\{\sigma\in\Gal(E/F)\colon\sigma(Q)=Q\}

is called the decomposition group of
Q|P
, and the subgroup

G_{j}(Q|P)=\{\sigma\in\Gal(E/F)\colon\nu_{Q}(\sigma(z)-z)\geq j+1\text{ for all }z\in\mathcal{O}_{Q}\}

is the
j
-th higher ramification group for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. They form a decreasing sequence
G_{-1}(Q|P)\supseteq G_{0}(Q|P)\supseteq\cdots\supseteq G_{m}(Q|P)=G_{m+1}(Q|P)=\cdots=\{\id\}
with
m\geq-1
. By Hilbert’s Different Theorem
[
38
, Theorem 3.8.7]
, the different exponent can be computed as

d(Q|P)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(|G_{j}|-1).

Moreover, if
E/F
is a finite abelian extension, let
a(Q|P)
be the least non-negative integer
a
such that ramification groups
G_{j}
become trivial for all
j\geq a
, then the integer

c_{P}(E/F):=\frac{d(Q|P)+a(Q|P)}{e(Q|P)}

is called the conductor exponent of
P
in
E/F
. The conductor of
E/F
is defined by

\text{Cond}(E/F):=\sum_{P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{F}}c_{P}(E/F)P.

2.2.
Algebraic geometric codes and their automorphism groups

Let
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
be a global function field. Let
\mathcal{D}=\{P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{n}\}
be an ordered set or a list of
n
distinct rational places of
F
.
For a divisor
G
with
P_{j}\notin{\rm supp}(G)
for each
1\leq j\leq n
, the algebraic geometry code associated with
\mathcal{D}
and
G
is denoted by

C(\mathcal{D},G):=\{(f(P_{1}),f(P_{2}),\dots,f(P_{n}))\colon f\in\mathcal{L}(G)\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{n}.

Consider the action of symmetric group
S_{n}
of
n
elements on the vector space
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{n}
as

\tau(\boldsymbol{c}):=(c_{\tau(1)},c_{\tau(2)},\dots,c_{\tau(n)}),

for all
\tau\in S_{n}
and
\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{n}
.
The
automorphism group

\operatorname{Aut}(C)
of code
C
is defined by

\operatorname{Aut}(C):=\{\tau\in S_{n}\colon\tau(C)=C\}.

Let
D=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}}P
and let
\Aut_{D,G}(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
be a subgroup of
\Aut(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
defined by

\Aut_{D,G}(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}):=\{\sigma\in\Aut(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})\colon\sigma(D)=D,\sigma(G)=G\}.

By
[
38
, Proposition 8.2.3]
), this subgroup acts on the code
C=C(\mathcal{D},G)
by

\sigma(f(P_{1}),f(P_{2}),\dots,f(P_{n})):=(f(\sigma(P_{1})),f(\sigma(P_{2})),\dots,f(\sigma(P_{n}))),

for any
\sigma\in\Aut(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
and
f\in\mathcal{L}(G)
.
Hence, this yields a homomorphism from
\Aut_{D,G}(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
to
\Aut(C)
.
If
n>2g(F)-2
, then it is an injective homomorphism. Therefore,
\Aut_{D,G}(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
can be viewed as a subgroup of
\Aut(C(\mathcal{D},G))
.

2.3.
A maximal curve with the second largest genus: the Abdón–Torres curve

Let
q
be a prime power of
2
.
Abdón and Torres showed that the maximal curve with the second largest genus over the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with even characteristic is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-isomorphic to the nonsingular plane curve

y^{q/2}+y^{q/4}+\dots+y=x^{q+1},

provided that
q/2
is a Weierstrass non-gap at some point of the curve
[
1
]
.
For simplicity, we call this curve (or function field) as the Abdón–Torres curve (or function field).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
q\geq 4
be a power of
2
. Let
h(y)=y^{q/2}+y^{q/2^{2}}+\dots+y^{2}+y
be a polynomial in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[y]
.
Let
Y_{2}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x,y)
be the Artin–Schreier function field over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
defined by the equation

h(y)=x^{q+1}.

Then the following results hold true.

(1)

The infinite place
\infty
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
is totally ramified place in the extension
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
. Let
P_{\infty}
be the place of
Y_{2}
lying over
\infty
.
Then the ramification index of
P_{\infty}|\infty
is
e(P_{\infty}|\infty)=q/2
and the different exponent of
P_{\infty}|\infty
is
d(P_{\infty}|\infty)=(q^{2}-4)/2
.

(2)

All other places of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
are unramified in
Y_{2}
.

(3)

The genus of
Y_{2}
is
g({Y_{2}})=q(q-2)/4.

(4)

Y_{2}
is a fixed subfield of the Hermitian function field, and hence a maximal function field over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
.

(5)

There are exactly
1+q^{3}/2
rational places of
Y_{2}
. Namely, the common pole
P_{\infty}
of
x
and
y
is a rational place; for any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, there are exactly
q/2
elements
b
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
such that
h(b)=a^{q+1}
, i.e., the common zero
P_{a,b}
of
x-a
and
y-b
with
h(b)=a^{q+1}
is a rational place of
Y_{2}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
\nu_{\infty}
be the discrete valuation with respect to
\infty
. It is clear that
\nu_{\infty}(x^{q+1})=-(q+1)
, which is not divisible by
2
. By
[
38
, Proposition 3.7.10]
, the rational place
\infty
is totally ramified in
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
with ramification index
e(P_{\infty}\mid\infty)=[Y_{2}:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)]=q/2
and different exponent

d(P_{\infty}\mid\infty)=(q+2)\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)=\frac{q^{2}-4}{2}.

(2)

For any finite place
R
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
, we have
\nu_{R}(x^{q+1})\geq 0
. By
[
38
, Proposition 3.7.10]
, all other places
R
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
are unramified in
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
.

(3)

The genus of
Y_{2}
follows from
[
1
]
or Hurwitz genus formula as follows

2g(Y_{2})-2=\frac{q}{2}[2g({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x))-2]+\deg{\rm Diff}(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x))=\frac{q}{2}(0-2)+\frac{q^{2}-4}{2}.

(4)

Let
x=u
and
y=v^{2}+v
. It is clear that
u^{q+1}=x^{q+1}=y^{q/2}+y^{q/4}+\dots+y=v^{q}+v
.
The function field
H={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(u,v)
defined by
u^{q+1}=v^{q}+v
is the famous Hermitian maximal curve with the largest possible genus.
It is easy to see that
Y_{2}\subseteq H
. Moreover,
H/Y_{2}
is an Artin–Schreier extension with explicit equation
v^{2}+v=y
of degree
2
.
Let
\sigma
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-automorphism group of
H
determined by
\sigma(u)=u
and
\sigma(v)=v+1
. It is easy to verify that
Y_{2}
is the fixed subfield of
H
under the group generated by
\sigma
. Thus,
Y_{2}
is a maximal function field.

(5)

Since
Y_{2}
is maximal, it has exactly
q^{2}+1+2qg(Y_{2})=1+q^{3}/2
rational places. From item (1), the infinity place
P_{\infty}
is the unique place lying over
\infty
.
By the fundamental equation, the zero of
x-a
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
has at most
q/2
extensions in
Y_{2}
for each
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
.
Thus, the zero of
x-a
splits completely into
q/2
rational places of
Y_{2}
, i.e., for any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, there are exactly
q/2
elements
b
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
such that
h(b)=a^{q+1}
.

∎

3.
The automorphism group of the Abdón–Torres curve

In this section, we determine the automorphism group of a maximal function field with the second largest genus over the finite field with even characteristic via the group action of automorphism group on the set of all rational places.
Firstly, we compute the principal divisors of chosen functions and determine Weierstrass semigroups of rational places. Secondly, we determine the stabilizer of the infinite place
P_{\infty}
via Riemann–Roch spaces. Finally, we determine the orbit of the group action and the full automorphism group of the Abdón–Torres curve.

3.1.
Weierstrass semigroups of
P_{\infty}
and the stabilizer of
P_{\infty}

Let
F/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
be a function field with genus
g(F)
and
P
be a rational place of
F
. For any non-negative integer
m
, if there exists an element
x\in F
with
(x)_{\infty}=mP
, then
m
is called a pole number of
P
. The Weierstrass semigroup of
P
consists of all pole numbers of
P
and it is denoted by

H(P):=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\exists x\in F\text{ such that }(x)_{\infty}=mP\}.

By the Weierstrass Gap Theorem
[
38
, Theorem 1.6.8]
,
H(P)
is a numerical semigroup with genus
g(F)
under the addition operation.

Let
h(y):=y^{q/2}+y^{q/4}+\dots+y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[y]
and let
\Omega
be the subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
defined by

\Omega=\{c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}:h(c)=0\}.

It is clear that

h(y)=y^{q/2}+y^{q/4}+\dots+y=\prod_{c\in\Omega}(y-c).

From the Dedekind–Kummer Theorem
[
38
, Theorem 3.3.7]
, the principal divisor of
x
in
Y_{2}
is given by

(x)=-\frac{q}{2}P_{\infty}+\sum_{b\in\Omega}P_{0,b},

and the principal divisor of
y
in
Y_{2}
is given by

(y)=(q+1)(P_{0,0}-P_{\infty}).

It is clear that
q/2,q+1\in H(P_{\infty})
.
The numerical semigroup
\langle q/2,q+1\rangle
generated by
q/2
and
q+1
has genus
(q/2-1)(q+1-1)/2=q(q-2)/4=g({Y_{2}})
.
Hence, the Weierstrass semigroup of
P_{\infty}
is
H(P_{\infty})=\langle q/2,q+1\rangle
.
Furthermore, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the Riemann–Roch space
\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty})
has a
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-basis

\{x^{i}y^{j}:i\geq 0,\ 0\leq j\leq q/2-1,\ i\cdot q/2+j(q+1)\leq m\}.

In this subsection, we determine the stabilizer defined by
G(P_{\infty})=\{\sigma\in\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}):\sigma(P_{\infty})=P_{\infty}\}
of
P_{\infty}
under the group action of automorphism group
\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
on the set of rational places of the Abdón–Torres curve.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
q=2^{n}\geq 4
, then the stabilizer
G(P_{\infty})
of the infinite place
P_{\infty}
of the Abdón–Torres curve
Y_{2}
consists of all
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-automorphisms
\sigma
of
Y_{2}
determined by

\begin{cases}\sigma(x)=ax+b\\
\sigma(y)=y+(ab^{q})^{2}x^{2}+ab^{q}x+c\end{cases},

where
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}},\ a^{q+1}=1,
and
b^{q+1}=h(c)
.

Proof.

Since
H(P_{\infty})=\langle q/2,q+1\rangle
, we have
\dim\mathcal{L}(\frac{q}{2}P_{\infty})

=2
and
\dim\mathcal{L}((q+1)P_{\infty})

=4
. More precisely,

\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{q}{2}P_{\infty}\right)={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}x,\quad\mathcal{L}((q+1)P_{\infty})={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}x\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}x^{2}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}y.

For any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-automorphism
\sigma\in G(P_{\infty})
, we have
\sigma(P_{\infty})=P_{\infty}
and
\sigma(\mathcal{L}(nP_{\infty}))=\mathcal{L}(\sigma(nP_{\infty}))=\mathcal{L}(nP_{\infty})
.
In particular, we have

\begin{cases}\sigma(x)=ax+b,\\
\sigma(y)=Ey+Ax^{2}+Bx+c,\end{cases}

where
a,b,c,E,A,B\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
and
a,E\neq 0.

Since the Abdón–Torres curve
Y_{2}
is given by
Y_{2}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x,y)
with
x^{q+1}=h(y)
, we obtain

(\sigma(x))^{q+1}=h(\sigma(y)).

That is to say

a^{q+1}x^{q+1}+a^{q}bx^{q}+ab^{q}x+b^{q+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}E^{q/2^{j}}y^{q/2^{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(A^{q/2^{j}}x^{q/2^{j-1}}+B^{q/2^{j}}x^{q/2^{j}}\right)+h(c).

Let
\nu_{P_{\infty}}
be the normalized discrete valuation with respect to
P_{\infty}
.
Since
\nu_{P_{\infty}}(x)=-q/2
and
\nu_{P_{\infty}}(y)=-(q+1)
,
it is clear that
\nu_{P_{\infty}}(x^{q+1})=\nu_{P_{\infty}}(y^{q/2})<\nu_{P_{\infty}}(y^{q/4})<\nu_{P_{\infty}}(x^{q/2})<\nu_{P_{\infty}}(y^{q/8})<\nu_{P_{\infty}}(x^{q/4})<\cdots<\nu_{P_{\infty}}(y)<\nu_{P_{\infty}}(x^{2})<\nu_{P_{\infty}}(x)<\nu_{P_{\infty}}(1)=0
.
By the Strict Triangle Inequality
[
38
, Lemma 1.1.11]
, both sides of the above equation have discrete valuation
-(q+1)q/2
.

Combining with
x^{q+1}=h(y)
, we have
a^{q+1}=E^{q/2}
and

\begin{split}(a^{q}b-A^{q/2})x^{q}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(A^{q/2^{j+1}}+B^{q/2^{j}}\right)x^{q/2^{j}}+(ab^{q}-B)x+b^{q+1}\\
=\sum_{j=2}^{n}(E^{q/2^{j}}-E^{q/2})y^{q/2^{j}}+h(c).\end{split}

These monomials have pairwise distinct discrete valuations. Hence, we obtain

\begin{cases}A^{q/2}=a^{q}b,\\
A^{q/2^{j+1}}+B^{q/2^{j}}=0\text{ for }j=1,2,\dots,n-1,\\
B=ab^{q},\\
E^{q/2^{j}}-E^{q/2}=0\text{ for }j=2,3,\dots,n,\\
b^{q+1}=h(c).\\
\end{cases}

If
n\geq 2
, then
E^{q/2}=E^{q/4}
. Together with
E^{q/2}=a^{q+1}
, we deduce that
a^{q+1}=1
and
E=1
.
Moreover, we have
A=(a^{q}b)^{2q}=a^{2}b^{2q}=(ab^{q})^{2}
.
Conversely, it is easy to verify that such a mapping
\sigma
determines an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-automorphism of
Y_{2}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.2.
The Weierstrass semigroups of other rational places
P_{(a,b)}

In this subsection, we determine the Weierstrass semigroups of rational places other than
P_{\infty}
of the Abdón–Torres function field
Y_{2}
.

Firstly, let us consider the Weierstrass semigroup of the rational place
P_{0,b}
for any
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with
h(b)=0
.
For any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, there exist
q/2
elements
b+c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with
c\in\Omega
such that
h(b+c)=a^{q+1}
.
Thus, the principal divisor of
x-a
in
Y_{2}
is given by

(x-a)=-\frac{q}{2}P_{\infty}+\sum_{c\in\Omega}P_{a,b+c}.

For any
b\in\Omega
, the principal divisor of
y-b
in
Y_{2}
is given by

(y-b)=(q+1)(P_{0,b}-P_{\infty}).

By taking the reciprocal of the above equation, it is easy to see that
q+1\in H(P_{0,b})
. Moreover, we have

\left(\frac{x}{y-b}\right)=\left(\frac{q}{2}+1\right)P_{\infty}+\sum_{b\neq c\in\Omega}P_{0,c}-qP_{0,b},

and

\left(\frac{x^{2}}{y-b}\right)=P_{\infty}+2\sum_{b\neq c\in\Omega}P_{0,c}-(q-1)P_{0,b}.

Thus, we obtain
q,q-1\in H(P_{0,b})
.
By
[
8
, Lemma 3.4]
, the numerical semigroup generated by
q-1,q
and
q+1
has genus

g(\langle q-1,q,q+1\rangle)=\frac{(q-2)q}{4}=g(Y_{2}).

Hence, the Weierstrass semigroup of
P_{0,b}
is
H(P_{0,b})=\langle q-1,q,q+1\rangle
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P_{0,b}
be the common zero of
x
and
y-b
with
h(b)=0
.
The Weierstrass semigroup of
P_{0,b}
is given by

H(P_{0,b})=\langle q-1,q,q+1\rangle.

Now let us consider the Weierstrass semigroup of the rational place
P_{a,b}
for any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}^{*}
and
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with
h(b)=a^{q+1}
.
Let
\zeta
be a primitive
(q+1)
-th root of unity.
For any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
and any
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}\setminus\Omega
with
a^{q+1}=h(b)
, the principal divisor of
y-b
in
Y_{2}
is given by

(y-b)=-(q+1)P_{\infty}+\sum_{j=0}^{q}P_{\zeta^{j}a,b}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
q=2^{n}\geq 4
be a prime power.
Let
a,b
be nonzero elements in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
such that
a^{q+1}=h(b)
.
Let
t_{a,b}:=(y-b)-a^{q}(x-a)
be the tangent line function at the rational place
P_{a,b}
.
Then there exists an effective divisor
E_{a,b}
of
Y_{2}
with
{\rm supp}(E_{a,b})\cap\{P_{a,b},P_{\infty}\}=\varnothing
such that

(t_{a,b})=2P_{a,b}+E_{a,b}-(q+1)P_{\infty}.

Proof.

By the Strict Triangle Inequality
[
38
, Lemma 1.1.11]
, we have

\nu_{P_{\infty}}(t_{a,b})=\min\{\nu_{P_{\infty}}(y-b),\nu_{P_{\infty}}(x-a)\}=-(q+1).

Since
x-a,y-b\in\mathcal{L}((q+1)P_{\infty})
, we have
t_{a,b}\in\mathcal{L}((q+1)P_{\infty})
and

(t_{a,b})+(q+1)P_{\infty}\geq 0.

It remains to prove that
\nu_{P_{a,b}}(t_{a,b})=2
.
It is easy to see that
t=(x-a)/a
is a prime element of
P_{a,b}
.
Since
x^{q+1}=h(y)
and
a^{q+1}=h(b)
, we get

h(y-b)=x^{q+1}-a^{q+1}=(x-a)^{q+1}+a(x-a)^{q}+a^{q}(x-a)=a^{q+1}(t^{q+1}+t^{q}+t).

Assume that
y-b=b_{1}t+b_{2}t^{2}+\cdots\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[[t]]
, it is easy to verify that
b_{1}=a^{q+1}
and
b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}=0
by comparing the coefficients.
Thus, we obtain that

y-b=a^{q+1}t+a^{2(q+1)}t^{2}+O(t^{3}),

where
O(t^{m})
stands for the terms with degree no less than
m
in the formal power series ring
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}[[t]]
.
Hence, it is clear that

t_{a,b}=(y-b)-a^{q}(x-a)=a^{2q+2}t^{2}+O(t^{3}).

The above local expansion shows that
\nu_{P_{a,b}}(t_{a,b})=2
.
This completes the proof.
∎

By the equation (10.8) in Section 10.2 of
[
20
]

P

. Or from the theory of abelian variety

f_{a,b}

such that

(f_{a,b})=(q+1)(P_{a,b}-P_{\infty}).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
P_{a,b}
be the common zero of
x-a
and

with

.
The Weierstrass semigroup of

is the numerical semigroup generated by

and

Proof.

From the principal divisor of

, the principal divisor of

gives that

.
Moreover, it is easy to see that

and

The numerical semigroup

generated by

∎

We summarize the main results of this subsection.

.

The Weierstrass semigroups of the rational places of the Abdón–Torres curve

(1)

3.3.
The automorphism group of the Abdón–Torres curve

Next, we need to determine the orbit of
P_{\infty}
under the group action of automorphism group
\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
on the set of all rational places.

Proposition 3.6
.

If
q=2^{n}\geq 4
, then the orbit
\mathcal{O}({P_{\infty}})
of infinity place
P_{\infty}
of
Y_{2}
under the group action of automorphism group
\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
is

\mathcal{O}({P_{\infty}})=\{\sigma(P_{\infty}):\sigma\in\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})\}=\{P_{\infty}\}.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists an automorphism
\sigma\in\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
such that
\sigma(P_{\infty})=P_{a,b}
for some rational place
P_{a,b}
.
The principal divisor of
x-a
in
Y_{2}
is given by

(x-a)=-\frac{q}{2}P_{\infty}+\sum_{c\in\Omega}P_{a,b+c}.

Since
\nu_{\sigma(P)}(\sigma(z))=\nu_{P}(z)
for any
z\in Y_{2}
and
P\in\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2}}
by
[
38
, Lemma 3.5.2]
, we have

(\sigma(x-a))=-\frac{q}{2}\sigma(P_{\infty})+\sum_{c\in\Omega}\sigma(P_{a,b+c}).

Hence, we have
q/2\in H(P_{a,b})
, which is a contradiction with Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
q=2^{n}\geq 4
be a prime power and
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
be the maximal function field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x,y)
defined by

y^{q/2}+y^{y/4}+\cdots+y=x^{q+1}.

Then the automorphism group of
Y_{2}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
is exactly the stabilizer of the infinity place
P_{\infty}
, i.e.,

\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})=G(P_{\infty}).

Proof.

By the orbit-stabilizer theorem and Proposition
3.6
, the order of automorphism group of
Y_{2}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
is

|\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})|=|G(P_{\infty})|\cdot|\mathcal{O}({P_{\infty}})|=|G(P_{\infty})|.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
C
be the cyclic subgroup of
\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
consisting all automorphisms
\sigma
determined by
\sigma(x)=ax
and
\sigma(y)=y
for any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with
a^{q+1}=1
, i.e.,

C=\{\sigma\in\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}):\sigma(x)=ax,\sigma(y)=y,a^{q+1}=1\}.

Let
N
be the subgroup of
\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
consisting all automorphisms
\sigma
determined by
\sigma(x)=x+b
and
\sigma(y)=y+b^{2q}x^{2}+b^{q}x+c
for any
b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
with

, i.e.,

N=\{\sigma\in\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}):\sigma(x)=x+b,\sigma(y)=y+b^{2q}x^{2}+b^{q}x+c,b^{q+1}=h(c)\}.

Proposition 3.8
.

The automorphism group of
Y_{2}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
consists of
(q+1)q^{3}/2

{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-automorphisms of
Y_{2}
given by

\begin{cases}\sigma(x)=ax+b\\
\sigma(y)=y+(ab^{q})^{2}x^{2}+ab^{q}x+c\end{cases},

where
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}},\ a^{q+1}=1
and
b^{q+1}=h(c)
.
Moreover, the automorphism group
\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
is a semi-product of a cyclic subgroup with order
q+1
and an elementary
2
-group with order
q^{3}/2

\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})=C\ltimes N.

Let
\sigma_{i}
be automorphisms of
Y_{2}
determined by
\sigma_{i}(x)=a_{i}x+b_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}(y)=y+(a_{i}b_{i}^{q})^{2}x^{2}+a_{i}b_{i}^{q}x+c_{i}

.
It is easy to verify that the composition
\sigma_{2}\cdot\sigma_{1}
of
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}

(\sigma_{2}\cdot\sigma_{1})(x)=a_{1}a_{2}x+a_{1}b_{2}+b_{1}

(\sigma_{2}\cdot\sigma_{1})(y)=y+(a_{2}b_{2}^{q}+a_{1}a_{2}b_{1}^{q})^{2}x^{2}+(a_{2}b_{2}^{q}+a_{1}a_{2}b_{1}^{q})x+(a_{1}b_{2}b_{1}^{q})^{2}+a_{1}b_{2}b_{1}^{q}+c_{1}+c_{2}

Remark 3.9
.

Note that

4.
Fixed subfields of
Y_{2}

In this section, we investigate maximal subfields of

\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})

4.1.
The ramification behavior and its conductor

In this subsection, we investigate the ramification behavior of the field extension
Y_{2}/Y_{2}^{\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})}
, and use it to compute the conductor of
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A
denote the automorphism group
\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
and
Z=Y_{2}^{A}
be the fixed subfield of
Y_{2}
with respect to
A
.
Then the fixed subfield
Z
is a rational function field.
There are exactly two places of
Z
ramified in the extension
Y_{2}/Z
.
One of the ramified places is
R_{\infty}:=P_{\infty}\cap Z
: it ramifies totally and wildly with ramification index

e(P_{\infty}|R_{\infty})=q^{3}(q+1)/2

and different exponent

d(P_{\infty}|R_{\infty})=\frac{1}{2}\left(q^{4}+2q^{3}+{q^{2}}-2q-4\right).

The other one is a rational place
R
of
Z
that lies under any rational place
P\neq P_{\infty}
in
Y_{2}
and tamely ramifies with ramification index
e(P|R)=q+1
and different exponent
d(P|R)=q
. The conjugates of
P
under
A
are exactly all rational places of
Y_{2}
that is not
P_{\infty}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.7
, the automorphism group
A
is the decomposition group of
Y_{2}/Y_{2}^{A}
at
P_{\infty}
and it follows that the ramification index of
P_{\infty}|R_{\infty}
is
e(P_{\infty}|R_{\infty})=q^{3}(q+1)/2
.
To determine the different exponent, we use the Hilbert’s ramification theorem
[
38
, Theorem 3.8.7]
as follows.
Let
t=x^{2}/y
be a prime element of the infinity place
P_{\infty}
by Proposition
3.5
.
For any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-automorphism
\sigma
of
Y_{2}
given by
\sigma(x)=ax+b
and
\sigma(y)=y+(ab^{q}x)^{2}+ab^{q}x+c
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{P_{\infty}}(\sigma(t)-t)

\displaystyle=\nu_{P_{\infty}}\left(\frac{(ax+b)^{2}}{y+(ab^{q}x)^{2}+ab^{q}x+c}-\frac{x^{2}}{y}\right)

\displaystyle=\nu_{P_{\infty}}(y(a^{2}x^{2}+b^{2})-x^{2}y+a^{2}b^{2q}x^{4}+ab^{q}x^{3}+cx^{2})-2\nu_{P_{\infty}}(y)

\displaystyle=\nu_{P_{\infty}}((a^{2}+1)x^{2}y+a^{2}b^{2q}x^{4}+ab^{q}x^{3}+cx^{2})-(2q+2)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&a\neq 1;\\
2,&a=1,b\neq 0;\\
q+2,&a=1,b=0,c\neq 0.\end{cases}

The different exponent
d(P_{\infty}|R_{\infty})
can be computed as

\displaystyle d(P_{\infty}|R_{\infty})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in G\setminus\{\operatorname{id}\}}\nu_{P_{\infty}}(\sigma(t)-t)=1\cdot\frac{q^{4}}{2}+2\cdot(q^{2}-1)\frac{q}{2}+\left(q+2\right)\cdot\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(q^{4}+2q^{3}+q^{2}-2q-4).

Since the genus of
Y_{2}
is
q(q-2)/4
by Proposition
2.1
, the Hurwitz genus formula yields

\frac{q(q-2)}{2}-2=|A|\cdot(2g({Z})-2)+\deg\Diff(Y_{2}/Z).

Since
\deg{\Diff(Y_{2}/Z)}=d(P_{\infty}|R_{\infty})+\sum_{R_{\infty}\neq R\in\mathbb{P}_{Z}}\sum_{P|R}d(P|R)\deg(P)>2g({Y_{2}})-2
, it is easy to see that
g(Z)=0
and

\ \sum_{R_{\infty}\neq R\in\mathbb{P}_{Z}}\sum_{P|R}d(P|R)\deg(P)=q^{4}/2
.

Since
Y_{2}/Z
is a Galois extension with Galois group
A
, for each
R\in\mathbb{P}_{Z}
we denote
e(R),d(R),f(R)
as
e(P|R),d(P|R),f(P|R)
respectively for any place
P\in\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2}}
lying above
R
.
By combining the fundamental equation, we have

\sum_{R_{\infty}\neq R\in\mathbb{P}_{Z}}\sum_{P|R}d(P|R)\deg(P)=q^{4}/2=|A|\cdot\sum_{R\neq R_{\infty}}\frac{d(R)}{e(R)}\deg R=\frac{q}{q+1}|A|.

It follows that

\sum_{R_{\infty}\neq R\in\mathbb{P}_{Z}}\frac{d(R)}{e(R)}\deg R=\frac{q}{q+1}.

By Dedekind’s Different Theorem
[
38
, Theorem 3.5.1]
, the inequality
d(R)\geq e(R)-1
holds true for all places
R
.
Hence, there exists exactly one rational place
R\neq R_{\infty}
such that
e(R)>1
. Otherwise, the left-hand side of the above equation is at least
2-1/e(R_{1})-1/e(R_{2})\geq 2-1/2-1/2=1
or
(1-1/e(R))\cdot 2\geq 1
, which is a contradiction. Furthermore, it is easy to see that
e(R)=q+1
and
d(R)=e(R)-1=q
, i.e.,
R
is tamely ramified in the extension
Y_{2}/Z
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2}}
be any place lying above
R
. We claim that
P
must be a rational place.
If not, then there exists a rational place
P_{0}\neq P_{\infty}\in\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2}}
such that
R_{0}=P_{0}\cap Z
is not equal to
R
.
Since
P_{0}
is rational, the relative degree of
P_{0}|R_{0}
is
f(P_{0}|R_{0})=1
. Since
R_{0}\notin\{R_{\infty},R\}
, we have
e(P_{0}|R_{0})=1
. Therefore, there exist at least
|A|=(q+1)q^{3}/2>q^{3}/2+1
rational places lying over
R_{0}
, which is impossible. Thus, all finite rational places of
Y_{2}
lie above
R
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

The conductor of
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
is

\operatorname{Cond}(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x))=(q+2)\cdot\infty.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, the infinity place
\infty
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
is the unique ramified place in the extension
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x)
.
Denote the place lying above
\infty
by

.
The Galois group of

is
\text{Gal}(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x))=\{\sigma\in\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}):\sigma(y)=y+c\text{ with }h(c)=0\}
.
From the proof of Proposition
4.1
, the
i
-th higher ramification group
G_{i}(P_{\infty}|\infty)
of

are given by

G_{0}(P_{\infty}|\infty)=G_{1}(P_{\infty}|\infty)=\cdots=G_{q+1}(P_{\infty}|\infty)=\text{Gal}(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x))

. The least positive integer

Thus, the conductor exponent of

∎

4.2.
Defining equations of a family of Galois subfields

In this subsection, we explicitly determine the defining equations of the subfields fixed by some subgroups of the full automorphism group of the maximal curve
Y_{2}
.

We start by refining the characterization on the structure of the additive group

\Omega=\{c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}:h(c)=0\}.

Let
\tau:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
be the automorphism given by
\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{2}
.
Let
B={\mathbb{F}}_{2}[T]
be the polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. For
f(T)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}T^{i}\in B
and
z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, we define the ring action of
B
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
by

z^{f(T)}:=f(\tau)(z),

where
f(\tau)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}\tau^{i}
and
\tau^{i}
is the
i
-th power of
\tau
under the composition law.
Thus, we get a ring homomorphism
\phi:B\rightarrow{\End}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
with
f(T)\mapsto f(\tau)
.
Hence,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
is a left
B
-module under this action.
For
q=2^{n}
and
H(T)=(T^{n}-1)/(T-1)
, the group
\Omega
is just the kernel of
\phi^{-1}(h)(\tau)=H(\tau)
.
If
p(T)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[T]
is a factor of
H(T)
, then
{\Ker}(p(\tau))
is a
B
-submodule of
\Omega
.
The defining equations of the corresponding fixed subfields of these submodules can be described explicitly.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
m
be a positive integer with
m|(q+1)
, and
p(T)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[T]
be a factor of
H(T)=(T^{n}-1)/(T-1)
.
Let
C_{m}
be the unique subgroup of
C
with order
m
, let
{\Ker}(p(\tau))
be the submodule of
\Omega
and let
N_{p}=\{[1,0,c]\colon c\in{\Ker}(p(\tau))\}
.
The subgroup
G=C_{m}N_{p}
is an abelian subgroup of
A
.
For any subgroup
G=C_{m}\times N_{p}\leq A
, the fixed subfield
Y_{2}^{G}
of
Y_{2}
with respect to
G
is given by

Y_{2}^{G}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(w,z),\quad w^{(q+1)/m}=z^{H(T)/p(T)},

where
B
also acts on
Y_{2}
that naturally extends the action on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
. In particular,
w=x^{m}
and
z=y^{p(T)}
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta
be a primitive
(q+1)
-th root of unity in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}^{*}
. Let
C_{m}
be the subgroup of
A
generated by
\xi=[\zeta^{(q+1)/m},0,0]
.
The automorphism
\xi
preserves the functions
w
and
z
, since
\xi(w)=x^{m}(\zeta^{(q+1)/m})^{m}=w
and
\xi(y)=y
. For any
c\in{\Ker}(p(\tau))
, the corresponding automorphism is given by
\eta=[1,0,c]
with
c^{p(T)}=0
.
The automorphism
\eta=[1,0,c]
preserves
w
, since
\eta(x)=x
. Let
p(T)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_{j}T^{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[T]
. By the additivity of
\tau
, the automorphism
\eta
preserves
z
as follows:

\displaystyle\eta(z)

\displaystyle=\eta(y^{p(T)})=\eta\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_{j}\tau^{j}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\eta\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}y^{2^{j}}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}b_{j}(y+c)^{2^{j}}

\displaystyle=(y+c)^{p(T)}=y^{p(T)}+c^{p(T)}=z+0

\displaystyle=z.

It is easy to verify that
\xi\cdot\eta=[\zeta^{(q+1)/m},0,c]=\eta\cdot\xi
. Thus,
G=C_{m}N_{p}=C_{m}\times N_{p}
.
Since
G=C_{m}\times N_{p}\leq A
, we have
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(w,z)\subseteq Y_{2}^{G}
.

Let
F={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(w,z)
with
w^{(q+1)/m}=z^{H(T)/p(T)}
.
Let
E={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(w,y)
. It is clear that
E=F(y)
with
y^{p(T)}=z
and

Y_{2}=E(x)
with
x^{m}=w
.
Hence,
E/F
is an Artin–Schreier extension of degree
2^{d}
and
Y_{2}/E
is a Kummer extension of degree
m
.
Therefore, the degree of extension
Y_{2}/F
is
[Y_{2}:F]=[Y_{2}:E]\cdot[E:F]=2^{d}m
.
Moreover, the degree of extension
Y_{2}
over
Y_{2}^{G}
is
|G|=2^{d}m
as well.
Thus, we have
Y_{2}^{G}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(w,z)
.
∎

5.
Automorphism groups of algebraic geometry codes from
Y_{2}

In this section, we determine the automorphism group of the one-point algebraic geometry code from the Abdón–Torres function field
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
and its infinite place
P_{\infty}
as an application. Except for the trivial cases, we shall prove that the automorphism group of the one-point algebraic geometry code from
Y_{2}
is its automorphism group
A=\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
.

We begin by listing the elements of the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
as
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{q^{2}}\}
. By the results in Section
3
, for each
x_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, there exist
q/2
elements
y_{j,1},y_{j,2},\dots,y_{j,q/2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
such that the principal divisor of
x-x_{j}
in
Y_{2}
is given by

(x-x_{j})=-\frac{q}{2}P_{\infty}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{q/2}P_{x_{j},y_{j,\ell}}.

Let
P_{x_{j},y_{j,\ell}}
be the common zero of
x-x_{j}
and
y-y_{j,\ell}
.
We write
P_{x_{j},y_{j,\ell}}
as
P_{j,\ell}
for
j=1,2,\dots,q^{2}
and
\ell=1,2,\dots,q/2
. These are all rational places of
Y_{2}
which are different from the infinite place
P_{\infty}
. Let

\mathcal{D}=\{P_{1,1},P_{1,2},\dots,P_{1,q/2},P_{2,1},\dots,P_{2,q/2},\dots,P_{q^{2},1},\dots,P_{q^{2},q/2}\}

be an ordered set or a list. For the list
\mathcal{D}
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define the associated one-point algebraic geometry code as

C_{m}=C(\mathcal{D},mP_{\infty})=\{(f(P))_{P\in\mathcal{D}}:f\in\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty})\}.

In the following, we shall determine the automorphism group of code
C_{m}
.

The useful result of Wesemeyer can be found from
[
41
, Theorem 5.12]
which can cover many positive integers for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field, and
F/{\mathbb{F}}
be an algebraic function field with the following properties.

(1)

F
is not rational.

(2)

There exist a rational place
P_{\infty}
of
F
and two elements
x,y\in F
satisfying that
(x)^{F}_{\infty}=AP_{\infty}
and
(y)^{F}_{\infty}=BP_{\infty}
for
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

(3)

For each natural number
m
, the Riemann-Roch space
\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty})
has an
{\mathbb{F}}
-basis

\{x^{j}y^{\ell}\colon j\geq 0,0\leq\ell\leq A-1,jA+\ell B\leq m\}.

Suppose
J\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{F}^{(1)}\setminus\{P_{\infty}\}
with size
n=|J|
,
J
is listed as
\{P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{n}\}
and let
D=\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{j}
. Let
g
be the genus of
F
and let
\beta=\min\{A-1,\max\{r\in{\mathbb{N}}\colon y^{r}\in\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty})\}\}
. Additionally assume
m\geq B>A
.
Let
C
be the algebraic geometry code
C(J,mP_{\infty})
.
If

n>\max\left\{2g+2,2m,A\cdot\left(B+\frac{A-1}{\beta}\right),AB\left(1+\frac{A-1}{m-A+1}\right)\right\},

then we have

\Aut(C)\cong\Aut_{D,mP_{\infty}}(F/{\mathbb{F}}).

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume
q\geq 4
. For the one-point algebraic geometry code
C_{m}=C(\mathcal{D},mP_{\infty})
from the Abdón-Torres function field
Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, its automorphism group can be characterized as follows.

(1)

If
0\leq m\leq q/2-1
or
m\geq(q^{3}+q^{2}-3q-2)/2
, then

\Aut(C_{m})\cong S_{q^{3}/2},

where
S_{q^{3}/2}
is the full symmetric group of a set of
q^{3}/2
elements.

(2)

If
q/2\leq m\leq q
or
(q^{3}+q^{2}-4q-4)/2\leq m\leq(q^{3}+q^{2}-3q-4)/2
, then

\Aut(C_{m})\cong\mathrm{Aff}_{1}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})\rtimes(S_{q/2})^{q^{2}},

where
\mathrm{Aff}_{1}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
is the group of affine linear transformation of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, and
(S_{q/2})^{q^{2}}
is the direct product of
q^{2}
copies of the symmetric group
S_{q/2}
.

(3)

If
q+1\leq m\leq q^{3}/4-1
or
(q^{3}+2q^{2}-4q-4)/4\leq m\leq(q^{3}+q^{2}-4q-6)/2
,
then

\Aut(C_{m})\cong\Aut(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}).

Proof.

First we note that
\Aut(C_{m}^{\perp})=\Aut(C_{m})
from
[
29
]
. This comes from the fact that if
\boldsymbol{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}=\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{y}^{\mathrm{T}}=0
for row vectors
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, then
\sigma(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\sigma(\boldsymbol{y})=0
for each
\sigma\in S_{n}
. Thus, we shall compute the dual code
C_{m}^{\perp}
.
Let
t=x^{q^{2}}-x=\prod_{j=1}^{q^{2}}(x-x_{j})\in Y_{2}
. It is easy to see that
\nu_{P}(t)=1
for any rational place
P\in\mathcal{D}
and
(t)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}}P-q^{3}/2\cdot P_{\infty}
. For any rational place
P\in\mathcal{D}
, the differential
\eta:=dt/t
satisfies
\nu_{P}(\eta)=-1
and its residue at
P
is
\text{res}_{P}(\eta)=-1
.
By
[
38
, Theorem 3.4.6 and Theorem 4.3.2]
, we have

(\mathrm{d}t)=(-\mathrm{d}x)=-2(x)_{\infty}+\Diff(Y_{2}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(x))=\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}-2q-4)P_{\infty}.

By
[
38
, Proposition 8.1.2]
, we can obtain the dual code

C(\mathcal{D},mP_{\infty})^{\perp}=C\left(\mathcal{D},\sum_{P\in\mathcal{D}}P-mP_{\infty}+(\mathrm{d}t)-(t)\right)=C(\mathcal{D},(M-m)P_{\infty})

where
M:=(q^{3}+q^{2}-2q-4)/2
.

(1)

If
0\leq m\leq q/2-1
, then
\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty})={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
, thus the generator matrix is the row vector

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}_{1\times(q^{3}/2)}=:\mathbf{1}_{q^{3}/2},

where we use
\mathbf{1}_{n}
to denote the row vector of length
n
with all entries being
1
. Naturally, the automorphism group is
S_{q^{3}/2}
. The same result holds for
0\leq M-m\leq q/2-1
by duality, which means the assertion is true for
(q^{3}+q^{2}-3q-2)/2\leq m\leq M
. Moreover, if
m\geq M+1
, then the code is the full vector space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}^{q^{3}/2}
. Thus, its automorphism group is
S_{q^{3}/2}
as well.

(2)

If
q/2\leq m\leq q-1
, then
\mathcal{L}(mP_{\infty})={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}x
. For
m=q
,
\mathcal{L}(qP_{\infty})={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}x\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}x^{2}
. Thus, the generator matrix of
C_{m}
is either

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&\cdots&\mathbf{1}_{q/2}\\
x_{1}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&x_{2}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&\cdots&x_{q^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}\end{pmatrix},

or

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&\cdots&\mathbf{1}_{q/2}\\
x_{1}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&x_{2}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&\cdots&x_{q^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}\\
x_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&x_{2}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}&\cdots&x_{q^{2}}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{q/2}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, the automorphism group is
\mathrm{Aff}_{1}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})\rtimes(S_{q/2})^{q^{2}}
from the proof given in
[
23
]
or
[
43
]
. By observing the dual code, we can conclude the same statement for other values of
m
.

(3)

For
m\geq q+1
, we can apply Lemma
5.1
. In this case,
A=q/2
,
B=q+1
and

. It follows that
A<B\leq m
and
2g+2=(q^{2}-2q+4)/2<n
. Moreover, we have

A\left(B+\frac{A-1}{\beta}\right)\leq A(B+A-1)=\frac{3}{4}q^{2}<n,

and

\displaystyle AB\left(1+\frac{A-1}{m+A-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq AB\left(1+\frac{A}{q+1-A+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q(q+1)}{2}\left(1+\frac{q/2}{q/2+2}\right)<q(q+1)<n.

It remains to ensure that

.
Thus, for

, the automorphism group of

is

\Aut(C_{m})\cong\Aut_{D,mP_{\infty}}(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})=G(P_{\infty})=\Aut(F/{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})

by Theorem
3.7

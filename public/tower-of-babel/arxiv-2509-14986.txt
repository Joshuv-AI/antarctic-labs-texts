1.
Introduction and notation

Given a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., a compact convex set with non-empty interior, the quantity given by

\|x\|=\|x\|_{2}\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{x^{\perp}}(K)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

defines a norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a consequence of Cauchy’s projection formula
[
12
, Equation (A.45)]
. Here
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the Euclidean norm, whose closed unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
B_{2}^{n}
,
\mathrm{vol}_{k}(A)
denotes the
k
-dimensional volume (Lebesgue measure) of a set contained in a
k
-dimensional affine subspace, and
P_{x^{\perp}}(K)
denotes the orthogonal projection of
K
onto the linear hyperplane orthogonal to
x
. The closed unit ball of this norm is a convex body, called the polar projection body of
K
, which will be denoted by
\Pi^{*}K
.

As a direct consequence of
[
12
, Theorem. 4.1.5]
, for any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the quantity
(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\Pi^{*}K)
is an affine invariant, i.e., for any non-degenerate affine map
T
we have that

(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(T(K)))^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\Pi^{*}T(K))=(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\Pi^{*}K).

It was proved in
[
18
]
that among all
n
-dimensional convex bodies, the affinely invariant quantity
(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\Pi^{*}K)
is maximized if and only if
K
is an ellipsoid, obtaining the following inequality, which is known as the Petty projection inequality and is stronger than the isoperimetric inequality:

(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\Pi^{*}K)\leq\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(B_{2}^{n-1})}\right)^{n}.

In
[
21
]
, Zhang proved a reverse Petty projection inequality, showing that among all
n
-dimensional convex bodies, the same affinely invariant quantity is minimized if and only if
K
is a simplex (i.e., the convex hull of
n+1
affinely independent points). Thus, for any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

(1.1)

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\leq(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\Pi^{*}K).

Several proofs and extensions of this inequality have been given in the last decades. See for instance
[
3
]
and
[
13
]
, where this inequality is shown to be extremely related to the covariogram function;
[
1
]
, where the inequality is extended to (and can be recovered from) the setting of log-concave functions; or
[
15
]
, where this inequality is extended to different measures other than the Lebesgue measure. Let us recall here that the covariogram function is defined as
g_{K}(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(x+K))
and that it is supported on the difference body
K-K
, which is the Minkowski sum of
K
and its reflection with respect to the origin,
-K
.

In this paper we will focus on the approach to this inequality given by the authors in
[
13
]
, which was also considered in
[
2
]
to provide a different proof of Zhang’s inequality in the setting of log-concave functions. Our motivation is to obtain an approach to Zhang’s inequality in the discrete setting, where we will consider discrete measures instead of the Lebesgue measure. In this approach the authors defined for a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
p>-1
, the radial
p
-th mean body of
K
, which we denote by
R_{p}(K)
. It is defined by its radial function for every
p\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{0\}

(1.2)

\rho_{R_{p}(K)}^{p}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}\int_{K}(\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap\{x+\lambda\theta\,:\lambda\geq 0\}))^{p}dx\quad\forall\theta\in S^{n-1}

and if
p=0
it is defined by
\displaystyle{\rho_{0}(\theta):=\lim_{p\to 0}\rho_{R_{p}(K)}(\theta)}
. Let us recall at this point that a set
L
with
0\in L
is called a star set with center
0
if for every
x\in L
and every
\lambda\in[0,1]
, one has
\lambda x\in L
. The radial function of a star set
L
with
0
as a center is defined for every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as
\rho_{L}(\theta)=\sup\{r\geq 0\,:\,r\theta\in L\}
. For any two compact star sets
L_{1},L_{2}
with
0
as a center,
L_{1}\subseteq L_{2}
if and only if
\rho_{L_{1}}(\theta)\leq\rho_{L_{2}}(\theta)
for every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
. In particular, for any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have that
\rho_{\Pi^{*}K}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{\theta^{\perp}}(K))}
for every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
.

The authors showed in
[
13
, Theorem 5.5]
that if

then

(1.3)

{{n+q}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{q}}R_{q}(K)\subseteq{{n+p}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}R_{p}(K).

Furthermore, the authors showed in
[
13

that the integral defining the radial function of

in (

) can also be written as an integral on

and as an integral on the interval

. More precisely, on the one hand, the authors proved in
[

that for every

, if we denote by

Therefore, when

and

where the right-hand side appears since, for any

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to(-1)^{+}}{{n+p}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\rho_{R_{p}(K)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{p\to(-1)^{+}}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(1+n+p\right)}{\Gamma\left(1+n\right)\Gamma\left(1+p\right)(p+1)\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}\int_{P_{\theta^{\perp}}(K)}(\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle\theta\rangle)))^{p+1}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(n-1)!}{n!\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{\theta^{\perp}}(K))\right)^{-1}=\frac{n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{\theta^{\perp}}(K))}=n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\rho_{\Pi^{*}(K)}.

On the other hand, the authors also showed in

20
, Lemma 3.22]
and
[
5
, Section 3.1]
) that for any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any bounded set
M
containing the origin

(2.1)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{G_{n}(rK+M)}{r^{n}}=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

In particular, taking
M=\{0\}
, for any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

(2.2)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{G_{n}(rK)}{r^{n}}=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

Moreover, for any
f:K\to\mathbb{R}
which is Riemann-integrable on
K
, we have that

(2.3)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{rK}f\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)dG_{n}(x)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\sum_{x\in K\cap\left(\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}f(x)=\int_{K}f(x)dx,

where the first identity follows from the definition of the measure
dG_{n}
. The second identity can be obtained by extending the function
f
to a rectangle containing
K
as
f(x)=0
for every
x\not\in K
, which is Riemann-integrable on the rectangle, and applying
[
10
, Proposition 6.3]
, which is valid for Riemann-integrable functions on the rectangle. Notice that
\displaystyle{\sum_{x\in K\cap\left(\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}f(x)}
is a Riemann sum of the extension of
f
to the rectangle, corresponding to the partition given by the rectangle intersected with cubes with vertices on
\frac{1}{r}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

As a consequence of Remark
2.1
, many continuous inequalities can be recovered from discrete inequalities (see, for instance,
[
5
, Section 3.1]
). We will see in this paper how Theorem
1.2
implies Theorem
1.1
or how Theorem
1.7
implies Theorem
1.6
.

The measure given by the lattice point enumerator satisfies the following discrete version of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality, which was proved in
[
14
, Theorem 2.1]
, and from which one can recover the classical one
[
19
, Theorem 7.1.1]
. It reads as
follows:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1)
and let
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be non-empty bounded sets. Then

G_{n}\left((1-\lambda)K+\lambda L+C_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq(1-\lambda)G_{n}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}+\lambda G_{n}(L)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

2.2.
Steiner symmetrization

Given a bounded convex set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Steiner symmetrization of
K
with respect to the hyperplane
e_{n}^{\perp}
is defined as

(2.4)

S_{e_{n}}(K)=\left\{y+\frac{t_{1}-t_{2}}{2}e_{n}\,:\,y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K),\,y+t_{1}e_{n}\in K,\,y+t_{2}e_{n}\in K\right\}.

That is,
S_{e_{n}}(K)
is the set that we obtain by first, shifting all the segments given by
K\cap(x+\langle e_{n}\rangle)
in the direction parallel to
\langle e_{n}\rangle
until their centers lie in the hyperplane
e_{n}^{\perp}
, and second, leaving such segments closed if they were closed and open otherwise. If
K
is compact then
S_{e_{n}}(K)
can be written as

(2.5)

S_{e_{n}}(K)=\left\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K),\,|t|\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))}{2}\right\}.

The Steiner symmetrization preserves convexity and volume. Moreover, for every
y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
we have that
S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle)
is an interval centered at
y
which has the same length as
K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle)
. Besides, from the definition of
S_{e_{n}}(K)
, if
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body then

S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle\geq 0\}

is the hypograph of the function
f:P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\to[0,\infty)
given by

f(y)=\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))}{2},

which is concave by Brunn’s principle (see, for instance
[
9
, Theorem 1.2.2]
). It is also known that for any convex set
K
and any
\lambda\geq 0
we have that
S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)=\lambda S_{e_{n}}(K)
and that for any two convex sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
one has that

(2.6)

S_{e_{n}}(K)+S_{e_{n}}(L)\subseteq S_{e_{n}}(K+L).

A list of basic properties of the Steiner symmetrization of convex bodies can be found in
[
6
, Sections 1.1.7 and A.5]
. Let us point out that for any bounded convex set, since
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)=S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap e_{n}^{\perp}
, then
S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\neq\emptyset
, as mentioned in Remark
1.8
.

2.3.
Berwald’s inequality

Berwald’s inequality provides a reverse Hölder’s inequality for
L_{p}
norms of positive concave functions defined on convex bodies. It is stated in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.3

(Berwald’s inequality)
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and let
f:K\to[0,\infty)
be a concave function. Then, for any
-1<p\leq q
we have that

(2.7)

\left(\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}\int_{K}f^{q}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}\int_{K}f^{p}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Berwald’s inequality was proved in
[
8
, Satz 7]
whenever the parameters in the statement satisfy
0<p<q
(see also
[
4
, Theorem 7.2]
for an English translation). It was extended to the whole range
-1<p<q
in
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
.

For any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\theta\in S^{n-1}
the function
f_{\theta}:K\to[0,\infty)
given by
f_{\theta}(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap\{x+\lambda\theta\,:\lambda\geq 0\})
is concave, as a direct consequence of the convexity of
K
and the Brunn-Minkowski inequality. Therefore, applying Berwald’s inequality (
2.7
) for any
-1<p<q
we obtain the inclusion relation given by (
1.3
) and, as explained in the introduction,
applying inequality (
2.7
) to
f_{e_{n}}
with parameters
-1<p<q=n
and taking limits as
p\to(-1)^{+}
one obtains Theorem
1.1
.

With the use of the discrete version of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality, Theorem
2.2
, an analogue of Theorem
2.3
(in the range
0<p<q
) was proved in
[
5
, Theorem 1.4]
, under the condition that the concave function attains its maximum at 0. Before we state it, let us introduce the following notation: if
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body and
f:K\to[0,\infty)
is a concave function, we denote
f^{\diamond}:K+C_{n}\to[0,\infty)
the function given by

(2.8)

f^{\diamond}(x)=\sup_{u\in C_{n}}\overline{f}(x+u),

where
\overline{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is the function given by

\overline{f}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\textrm{ if }x\in K\\
0&\textrm{ if }x\not\in K.\end{cases}

The function
f^{\diamond}
satisfies that it is a concave function whose hypograph is the closure of the Minkowski sum of the hypograph of
f
and
C_{n}\times\{0\}
.

With this notation, the discrete version of Berwald’s inequality reads as follows:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body containing the origin and let
f:K\to[0,\infty)
be a concave function such that
\displaystyle{\max_{x\in K}f(x)=f(0)}
. Then, for any
0<p<q
we have that

\left(\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\sum_{x\in K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f^{q}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\sum_{x\in(K+C_{n})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(f^{\diamond})^{p}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Let us point out that the discrete version of Berwald’s inequality, Theorem
2.4
, implies the continuous version of Berwald’s inequality, Theorem
2.3
in the range
0<p<q
. For that matter, see
[
5
, Theorem 4.5]
taking into account that in the continuous version of Berwald’s inequality, Theorem
2.3
, we can assume without loss of generality that the concave function attains its maximum at 0.

2.4.
The covariogram function

Given a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its covariogram function
g_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is defined as

g_{K}(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(x+K)).

The funtion
g_{K}
is supported on the difference body of
K
, defined as

(2.9)

\displaystyle K-K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x-y\,:\,x,y\in K\}=\bigcup_{x\in K}(x-K)

(2.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,K\cap(x+K)\neq\emptyset\}.

It is clear that
g_{K}
is an even function such that
\displaystyle{\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}g_{K}(x)=g_{K}(0)=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}
. Moreover, as a consequence of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality,
g_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}
is concave on
K-K
and, by Fubini’s theorem,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g_{K}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{K}(y)\chi_{x+K}(y)dydx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{K}(y)\chi_{y-K}(x)dxdy

(2.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{K}(y)\mathrm{vol}_{n}(y-K)dy=(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{2}.

Notice also that, by (
2.9
), for any
\theta\in S^{n-1}
we have that

(2.13)

K\cap(r\theta+K)=\emptyset\quad\textrm{for every }r>\rho_{K-K}(\theta).

2.5.
Ball bodies of log-concave functions

A log-concave function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is a function of the form
g(x)=e^{-u(x)}
with
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
a convex function. The family of log-concave functions plays an extremely important role in the study of problems related to distribution of volume in convex bodies since, as a consequence of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality, the projection of the uniform Lebesgue measure on a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto a
k
-dimensional linear subspace is a measure with a log-concave density with respect to the
k
-dimensional Lebesgue measure in that subspace.

Ball introduced in
[
7
]
, for any measurable (not necessarily log-concave) function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, such that
g(0)>0
, and for any
p>0
, the set

(2.14)

K_{p}(g):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}g(rx)dr\geq g(0)\right\}.

Clearly
0\in K_{p}(g)
, as
\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}g(0)dr=\infty}
. Besides, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
\lambda>0
we have that

p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}g(r\lambda x)dr=\frac{p}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}g(sx)ds.

Therefore, for every
x\in K_{p}(g)
and every
\lambda\in(0,1]
we have that

p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}g(r\lambda x)dr=\frac{p}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}g(sx)ds\geq p\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}g(sx)ds\geq g(0)

and
\lambda x\in K_{p}(g)
. Thus,
K_{p}(g)
is a star set with center
0
whose radial function is given, for any
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, by

\rho_{K_{p}(g)}(\theta)=\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}g(r\lambda\theta)ds\geq g(0)\right\}.

If
\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}g(sx)ds=0}
, then
\left\{\lambda\geq 0\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}g(r\lambda\theta)ds\geq g(0)\right\}=\{0\}
, and
\rho_{K_{p}(g)}(\theta)=0
. Otherwise,

\displaystyle\rho_{K_{p}(g)}(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}g(r\lambda\theta)ds\geq g(0)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\lambda>0\,:\,\frac{p}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}g(s\theta)ds\geq g(0)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{g(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}g(s\theta)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In any case,

(2.15)

\rho_{K_{p}(g)}(\theta)=\left(\frac{p}{g(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}g(s\theta)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The importance of these sets
(K_{p}(g))_{p>0}
, which we will call
p
-th Ball bodies of
g
, in the study of log-concave functions relies on the following two facts: First, whenever
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is an integrable log-concave function such that
g(0)>0
, the star set
K_{p}(g)
is a convex body for any
p>0
(see
[
9
, Theorem 2.5.5, Lemma 2.5.6, and Proposition 2.5.7]
). As a particular case, notice that if
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a convex body with
0\in K
, then for any
p>0

(2.16)

K_{p}(\chi_{K})=K.

Second, for any homogeneous function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
of degree 1 and any
p>-n
we have, by integration in polar coordinates (see
[
9
, Proposition 2.5.3]
for the particular case when
h
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
), that

\int_{K_{n+p}(g)}h^{p}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h^{p}(x)\frac{g(x)}{g(0)}dx.

In particular, if
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
is an integrable log-concave function, such that
g(0)>0
, taking
p=0
(see
[
9
, Lemma 2.5.6]
) we obtain that
\displaystyle{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{n}(g))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{g(x)}{g(0)}dx}
.

The covariogram function
g_{K}
of a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies that
g_{K}(0)=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)>0
,
g_{K}
is integrable, and
g_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}
is concave on its support. In particular,
g_{K}
is log-concave. Consequently, the radial function defined by (
1.6
) defines the
n
-th ball body of
g_{K}
, which is a convex body whose volume is, by (
2.11
)

(2.17)

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{n}(g_{K}))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{g_{K}(x)}{g_{K}(0)}dx=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

Furthermore, for any integrable log-concave function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
such that
g(0)>0
the following inclusion relation between Ball bodies holds (see
[
9
, Proposition 2.5.7]
): if
0<p<q
then

\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+q)^{\frac{1}{q}}}K_{q}(g)\subseteq\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}K_{p}(g).

Moreover, since by
[
13
, Lemma 3.1]
for any
p>0
we have
R_{p}(K)=K_{p}(g_{K})
, where
R_{p}(K)
is defined by (
1.2
), the inclusion relation (
1.3
) shows that if
0<p<q

(2.18)

{{n+q}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{q}}K_{q}(g_{K})\subseteq{{n+p}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}K_{p}(g_{K}).

This inclusion relation has been extended for the family of
p
-th Ball bodies of
\alpha
-concave functions (i.e., functions such that
f^{\alpha}
is concave on its support) with
\alpha>0
in
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
.
We refer the reader to
[
9
, Section 2.5]
for more information on the family of
p
-th Ball bodies.

3.
Another proof of Zhang’s inequality

In this section we will provide a different proof of Theorem
1.1
, which leads to Zhang’s inequality. In the same way as the proof in
[
13
]
, it is based on the use of Berwald’s inequality (Theorem
2.3
). However, the choice of the concave function will only require the use of Berwald’s inequality with positive parameters. Since Theorem
2.4
provides a discrete version of Berwald’s inequality for positive parameters, we will later be able to use the same approach in order to obtain Theorem
1.2
, which provides a discrete version of Theorem
1.1
.

We begin with the following technical lemma. Part of its proof can be found in
[
13
, Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 3.1]
. Nevertheless, we will provide a complete proof for the sake of completeness:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body and let
p>0
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\left(\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))\right)^{p+1}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{p}dx.

Proof.

By Fubini’s theorem, for any
p>0
we have

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(re_{n}+K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))dydr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\max\{\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))-r,0\}dydr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\int_{0}^{\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))}pr^{p-1}(\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))-r)drdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\left((\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle)))^{p+1}-\frac{p}{p+1}(\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle)))^{p+1}\right)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\left(\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))\right)^{p+1}dy,

which proves the first equality. Besides, we also have that

\displaystyle 2^{p}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\int_{-\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))}{2}}^{\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))}{2}}|t|^{p}dtdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p+1}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\int_{0}^{\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))}{2}}t^{p}dtdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}(\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle)))^{p+1}dy,

which proves the second equality.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Notice that, by the equality between the last two quantities in the statement of Lemma
3.1
with
p=n
, we obtain that Theorem
1.2
can be rewritten as Theorem
1.6
.

We are now ready to provide our proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
f:P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\to[0,\infty)
be the function given by

f(y)=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle)),

which is concave by Brunn’s principle
[
9
, Theorem 1.2.2]
. Then, by Berwald’s inequality (Theorem
2.3
) applied on
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
with
p=1
and
q=n+1
we have that

\left(\frac{{{2n}\choose{n-1}}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}f^{n+1}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\leq\frac{n}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}f(y)dy.

Equivalently, taking into account that
\frac{1}{n}{{2n}\choose{n-1}}=\frac{1}{n+1}{{2n}\choose{n}}
and that, by Fubini’s theorem,
\displaystyle{\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}f(y)dy=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}
, we obtain

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}f^{n+1}(y)dy\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}.

By Lemma
3.1
this inequality is equivalent to

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}n\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

The main difference between our proof of Theorem
1.1
and the one in
[
13
]
is the concave function on which we apply Berwald’s inequality (Theorem
2.3
). In
[
13
]
, the authors applied Berwald’s inequality to the function
f_{e_{n}}:K\to[0,\infty)
given by
f_{e_{n}}(x)=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap\{x+\lambda e_{n}\,:\lambda\geq 0\})
while we considered the function
f:P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\to[0,\infty)
given by
f(y)=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))
. With this choice, we do not need to take negative exponents when applying Berwald’s inequality in order to prove the Theorem
1.1
. The same approach will lead to Theorem
1.2
by means of Theorem
2.4
.

An application of Berwald’s inequality (
2.7
) to the same function
f:P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\to[0,\infty)
given by
f(y)=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))
with exponents
p+1<q+1
for any
0\leq p<q
provides the following Theorem, which extends Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. For any
0\leq p<q
we have

\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{q+1}}(n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{\frac{1}{q+1}}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{\frac{1}{q+1}}}\rho_{K_{q}(g_{K})}^{\frac{q}{q+1}}(e_{n})\leq\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{p+1}}(n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{\frac{1}{p+1}}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{\frac{1}{p+1}}}\rho_{K_{p}(g_{K})}^{\frac{p}{p+1}}(e_{n}).

In particular, taking
p=0
, for every
q>0

\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{q+1}}(n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{\frac{1}{q+1}}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{\frac{1}{q+1}}}\rho_{K_{q}(g_{K})}^{\frac{q}{q+1}}(e_{n})\leq\frac{n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}

or, equivalently,

\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{n^{q}}q\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{q+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{q}}.

Remark 3.5
.

By considering rotations of a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the last two inequalities in Theorem
3.4
being true for every convex body
K
are also equivalent to the inclusion relation

{{n+q}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{q}}K_{q}(g_{K})\subseteq n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\Pi^{*}(K),

for any
q>0
, which is stated in (
1.3
). More precisely, given a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, applying the last inequality in Theorem
3.4
to
U(K)
with
U\in O(n)
such that
U^{t}(e_{n})=\theta
and taking into account, as mentioned in the introduction, that
\mathrm{vol}_{n}(U(K)\cap(re_{n}+U(K)))=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(rU^{t}(e_{n})+K))
and that
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(U(K))=P_{(U^{t}(e_{n}))^{\perp}}(K)
we obtain an inequality between
\rho_{K_{q}(g_{K})}(\theta)
and
\rho_{\Pi^{*}(K)}(\theta)
which gives the latter inclusion relation. However, the relation between the convex bodies
K_{q}(g_{K})
and
K_{p}(g_{K})
whenever
0<p<q
given by the first inequality in Theorem
3.4
is different from the inclusion relation given by (
1.3
), as we do not obtain an inequality between radial functions raised to the same power, which would provide an inclusion relation.

Finally, we show how Theorem
1.1
implies Zhang’s inequality (
1.1
)

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. Then

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\leq(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\Pi^{*}K).

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
we have that for any
U\in O(n)

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}n\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(U(K)\cap(re_{n}+U(K)))dr\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(U(K)))^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(U(K))))^{n}}.

Equivalently, taking into account that
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(U(K))=P_{(U^{t}(e_{n}))^{\perp}}(K)
,

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(U(K)\cap(re_{n}+U(K)))=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(rU^{t}(e_{n})+K))
, and that

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(U(K))=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)
,

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}n\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(rU^{t}(e_{n})+K))dr\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{(U^{t}(e_{n}))^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}.

Therefore, since for every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
there exists
U\in O(n)
such that
U^{t}(e_{n})=\theta
, we have that for every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
,

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}n\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(r\theta+K))dr\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{\theta^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}.

Taking into account (
1.6
) and that
\rho_{\Pi^{*}K}(\theta)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{\theta^{\perp}}(K))}
for every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, we have

{{2n}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{n}}\rho_{K_{n}(g_{K})}(\theta)\leq n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\rho_{\Pi^{*}(K)}(\theta)\quad\forall\theta\in S^{n-1},

which is equivalent to the inclusion relation

{{2n}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{n}}K_{n}(g_{K})\subseteq n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)\Pi^{*}(K)

given by (
1.7
). Taking volumes and using (
2.17
) we obtain the result.

∎

A Discrete approach to Zhang’s inequality

In this section we are going to prove Theorem
1.2
, which involves the measure
d\mu=dG_{n-1}\otimes dm_{1}
. As stated in (

), for every Borel set

\mu(A)=\sum_{y\in e_{n}^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(A\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=\sum_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(A)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(A\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle)),

where the sum is understood as

Notice that there exist convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

and

, as the example

shows. This occurs since, even though

, we have

(and therefore

Notice also that there exist convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

and

, as

shows. However, if

is a convex body with

and

, then necessarily every

Recall that for every bounded convex set

. Therefore, if
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\neq\emptyset
, in the same way as

, we have that
\mu(K)=\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K))
since

\mu(K)=\sum_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K)).

If
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\emptyset
, then trivially
\mu(K)=0=\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K))
. In any case,
\mu(K)=\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K))
.

Let us also point out that this measure
\mu
allows to write the right-hand side of the inequality in Theorem

interchanging
K
by its Steiner symmetrization

, providing a smaller upper bound for the integral in the left-hand side, since for every convex body

we have that

, as we show in the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be a bounded convex set. Then,
\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K))=\mu(K)
and

\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\leq\mu(K+C_{n-1}).

Proof.

On the one hand, if
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})=\emptyset
then
\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})=0
and the inequality is trivial. Let us assume that

. Notice also that since

\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\leq\mu(S_{e_{n}}(K+C_{n-1}))=\mu(K+C_{n-1}).

∎

The following lemma is the analogue of Lemma

Lemma 4.3

be a convex body and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left(\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))\right)^{p+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\mu(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{p}d\mu(x).

Proof.

First of all, notice that if
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\emptyset
, then
\mu(K)=0
, and therefore, for every
r\geq 0
, we have that
\mu(K\cap(re_{n}+K))=0
. In such case, we also have
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(S_{e_{n}}(K))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\emptyset

1.8
), we have that for any

\displaystyle\mu(K\cap(re_{n}+K))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\max\{\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))-r,0\}.

Then, for any

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\mu(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr

Remark 4.4
.

Let us recall that in Theorem

the direction

does not play any special role and, as mentioned in the introduction, having Theorem

for any convex body is equivalent to having inequality (

) for any convex body and any direction

. The special role of the direction

is also reflected in the definition of the measure

as

, which depends on the choice of the direction

. If we chose a different direction

, since

. This fact would not allow the use of the discrete version of Brunn-Minkowski inequality (Theorem

) in

identified with
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

.

\displaystyle n\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}\mu(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr=n\int_{0}^{\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})}r^{n-1}\mu(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\int_{0}^{\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})}r^{n-1}(G_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))-G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))))dr

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\int_{0}^{\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})}r^{n-1}(G_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))-G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\int_{0}^{\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})}r^{n-1}G_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr-\rho_{K-K}^{n}(e_{n})G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}G_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr-\rho_{K-K}^{n}(e_{n})G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)).

∎

Finally, we are going to see that Theorem
1.2
implies Theorem
1.1
and therefore, as seen in the proof of Corollary
3.6
, Zhang’s inequality (
1.1
).

Corollary 4.6

(Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. Then,

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)^{n+1}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{n}}.

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. Since the inequality we want to prove is invariant by translations, we can assume without loss of generality that

\displaystyle{\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle)}
. Then, for any
\lambda>0
we have that

\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(\lambda K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(\lambda K\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle).

Thus, by Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
4.1
, for any
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}\mu(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(\mu(S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)+C_{n-1}))^{n+1}}{(G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)))^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(\lambda K+C_{n-1})^{n+1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K))^{n}}.

By Lemma
4.2
we have that for every
r>0

\mu(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))\geq G_{n}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))-G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K)))

and that

\mu(\lambda K+C_{n-1})\leq G_{n}(\lambda K+C_{n-1})+G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)+C_{n-1}).

Then, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}(G_{n}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))-G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))))dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(G_{n}(\lambda K+C_{n-1})+G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)+C_{n-1}))^{n+1}}{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{n}}.

Therefore, dividing the latter inequality by
\lambda^{2n}=\frac{\lambda^{n(n+1)}}{\lambda^{n(n-1)}}
, we obtain that for any
\lambda>0
, the quantity

(4.1)

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}(G_{n}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))-G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))))dr

is bounded above by

\frac{(G_{n}(\lambda K+C_{n-1})+G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)+C_{n-1}))^{n+1}}{\lambda^{2n}G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{n}},

which equals

(4.2)

\frac{\left(\frac{G_{n}(\lambda K+C_{n-1})}{\lambda^{n}}+\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)+C_{n-1})}{\lambda^{n}}\right)^{n+1}}{\left(\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}{\lambda^{n-1}}\right)^{n}}.

On the one hand, we are going to prove that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}G_{n}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))dr

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr

and that

(4.5)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K)))dr=0.

As a consequence, we will obtain that (
4.1
) converges, as
\lambda\to\infty
, to

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr.

On the other hand, we have that, by (
2.2
),

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}{\lambda^{n-1}}=\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))

and, by (
2.1
) (with
M=C_{n-1}
)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left(\frac{G_{n}(\lambda K+C_{n-1})}{\lambda^{n}}+\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)+C_{n-1})}{\lambda^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)+0\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

Therefore, (
4.2
) converges to
\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)^{n+1}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{n}}
as
\lambda\to\infty
. As a consequence, we will obtain

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(re_{n}+K))dr\leq\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)^{n+1}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{n}}.

Let us prove (
4.3
): By (
2.13
),
\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K)=\emptyset
for every
r>\rho_{\lambda(K-K)}(e_{n})
. Therefore, changing variables
r=\lambda s
, for any
\lambda>0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}G_{n}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))dr=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{\lambda(K-K)}(e_{n})}nr^{n-1}G_{n}\left(\lambda\left(K\cap\left(\frac{r}{\lambda}e_{n}+K\right)\right)\right)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})}ns^{n-1}\frac{G_{n}(\lambda(K\cap(se_{n}+K)))}{\lambda^{n}}ds.

For any
s\in[0,\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})]
we have by (
2.2
) that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{G_{n}(\lambda(K\cap(se_{n}+K)))}{\lambda^{n}}=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(se_{n}+K)).

Also by (
2.2
)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{G_{n}(\lambda K)}{\lambda^{n}}=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

Thus, given
\varepsilon_{0}>0
there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that if
\lambda>\lambda_{0}

ns^{n-1}\frac{G_{n}(\lambda(K\cap(se_{n}+K)))}{\lambda^{n}}\leq ns^{n-1}\frac{G_{n}(\lambda K)}{\lambda^{n}}\leq ns^{n-1}(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)+\varepsilon_{0}).

The function
f(s)=ns^{n-1}(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)+\varepsilon_{0})
is integrable in
[0,\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})]
. Thus, by the dominated convergence theorem and using again that, by (
2.13
),
K\cap(se_{n}+K)=\emptyset
if
s>\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}G_{n}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K))dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{K-K}(e_{n})}ns^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(se_{n}+K))ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}ns^{n-1}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K\cap(se_{n}+K))ds,

which, renaming
s
as
r
, proves (
4.3
).

Let us now prove (
4.5
): Again, by (
2.13
), we have that
\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K)=\emptyset
if
r>\rho_{\lambda(K-K)}(e_{n})
and then, for any
\lambda>0
we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K)))dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{\lambda(K-K)}(e_{n})}nr^{n-1}G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K)))dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{\lambda(K-K)}(e_{n})}nr^{n-1}dr=\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))\rho_{\lambda(K-K)}^{n}(e_{n})}{\lambda^{2n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}{\lambda^{n-1}}\rho_{K-K}^{n}(e_{n}).

Since by (
2.2
)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}{\lambda^{n-1}}\rho_{K-K}^{n}(e_{n})=0\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))\rho_{K-K}^{n}(e_{n})=0,

we have that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}nr^{n-1}G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K\cap(re_{n}+\lambda K)))dr=0.

Now that we have proved (
4.3
) and (
4.5
), the proof is complete.
∎

5.
A different discrete approach to Zhang’s inequality

In this section we are going to prove Theorem
1.7
. This gives a discrete version of Theorem
1.6
. Let us recall that, as mentioned in Remark
3.2
, Theorem
1.6
is equivalent to Theorem
1.1
. The proof will follow the lines of the proof of Theorem
2.4
, which was given in
[
5
]
. For any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\neq\emptyset
, the role of the function
f
in Theorem
2.4
will be played by the function
f_{1}:P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\to[0,\infty)
given by
f_{1}(y)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))
, whose hypograph will be
S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle\geq 0\}
. Therefore, the hypograph of the function
f_{1}^{\diamond}
, defined as in (
2.8
), is the closure of
(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle\geq 0\}
. The role of the binomial coefficient in Theorem
2.4
will now be played by the inverse of the number, defined in (
1.9
) as

B_{m}(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}\frac{p}{m}\left(1-\frac{k}{m}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{k}{m}\right)^{p-1},

for a certain
m=m_{0}(p)>1
, whose existence (depending on a fixed parameter
p\geq 1
) needs to be proved. Once the existence of such
m_{0}(p)
is proved, we will be able to construct an appropriate
\frac{1}{n-1}
-affine function (i.e., a function
g_{p}
such that
g_{p}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}
is affine on its support), given by

(5.1)

g_{p}(x)=\begin{cases}\left(1-\frac{x}{m_{0}(p)}\right)^{n-1}G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))&\textrm{ if }0\leq x\leq m_{0}(p)\cr 0&\textrm{ if }x>m_{0}(p).\cr\end{cases}

This function
g_{p}
which will have a crossing point
r_{0}(p)
(see Lemma
5.5
below for the precise definition of such crossing point) with the function
\tilde{f}
given by

(5.2)

\tilde{f}(r)=G_{n-1}((S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\}),\quad r\geq 0.

which is a modification of the function given by

(5.3)

f(r)=G_{n-1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\}),\quad r\geq 0.

The function
f
counts, for every
r\geq 0
, the number of integer points in the projection onto
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=0\}
of the intersection of
S_{e_{n}}(K)
with the hyperplane
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\}
.

Besides, such function
g_{p}
will satisfy that, the value of
\displaystyle{\left(\frac{B_{m}(q)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}g_{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}
will be independent of
q
and will depend only on the fixed parameter
p
. For every
q\geq 1
we will have (See equations (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) below and Lemma
5.5
for the precise definition of the function
g_{p}
)

\left(\frac{B_{m}(q)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}g_{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\frac{B_{m}(p)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{q-1}\tilde{f}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

Making use of the fact that
g_{p}
will have a crossing point with
\tilde{f}
, we will be able to prove that

\left(\frac{B_{m}(q)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}g_{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq\left(\frac{B_{m}(q)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}f(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

obtaining in this way the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let us point out that, from the definition of
B_{m}(p)
, we have that if
p>1
, then
B_{m}(p)=0
for every
m\in(0,1)
and if
p=1
, then
B_{m}(1)=\frac{1}{m}
for every
m\in(0,1)
, since we convene that
\left(\frac{0}{m}\right)^{1-1}=1
. For any
p\geq 1
and
m>1
we have that
B_{m}(p)>0
.

Notice that Berwald’s inequality, (
2.7
), applied in
e_{n}^{\perp}
identified with
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
to a concave function
h
defined on
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}K
, shows that the quantity

\left(\frac{{{n-1+p}\choose{n-1}}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\int_{P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}h^{p}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is non-increasing in
p\in(-1,\infty)
.

The following lemma shows that, as
m
tends to
\infty
, the value of
B_{m}(p)
converges to the inverse of
{{n-1+p}\choose{n-1}}
, which is the binomial coefficient in this quantity.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
n\geq 2
and any
p\geq 1
we have that

\lim_{x\to\infty}B_{x}(p)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\frac{p}{x}\left(1-\frac{k}{x}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{k}{x}\right)^{p-1}={{n-1+p}\choose{n-1}}^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be the function
f(x)=p(1-x)^{n-1}x^{p-1}
, which is Riemann-integrable in
[0,1]
with

\int_{0}^{1}f(x)dx={{n-1+p}\choose{n-1}}^{-1}.

Since the norm of the partition of the interval
[0,1]
given by
\mathcal{P}_{x}=\left\{0,\frac{1}{\lfloor x\rfloor},\frac{2}{\lfloor x\rfloor},\dots,1\right\}
tends to
0
as
x
tends to
\infty
, and

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\frac{p}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\left(1-\frac{k}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{k}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\right)^{p-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor-1}\frac{p}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\left(1-\frac{k}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{k}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\right)^{p-1}

is a Riemann sum of
f
associated to
\mathcal{P}_{x}
, we have that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\frac{p}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\left(1-\frac{k}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{k}{\lfloor x\rfloor}\right)^{p-1}={{n-1+p}\choose{n-1}}^{-1}.

Moreover, as
\displaystyle{\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\lfloor x\rfloor}{x}=1}
, we obtain that

\lim_{x\to\infty}B_{x}(p)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}\frac{p}{x}\left(1-\frac{k}{x}\right)^{n-1}\left(\frac{k}{x}\right)^{p-1}={{n-1+p}\choose{n-1}}^{-1}.

∎

From now on we will consider convex bodies satisfying some hypotheses. We will say that a convex body
K
satisfies the hypotheses (H)
if it satisfies

a)

\displaystyle{\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle)}
,

b)

\displaystyle{M:=\max_{x\in S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle x,e_{n}\rangle\geq 1}
.

In the following lemma we prove under the hypotheses
(H)
, for any
p\geq 1
, the existence of the number
m_{0}(p)
that we will need in order to construct the
\frac{1}{n-1}
-affine defined in (
5.1
), which will have one crossing point with the function
\tilde{f}
defined in (
5.2
). Before stating the lemma, let us make the following remark regarding the supports of the functions
f
and
\tilde{f}
:

Remark 5.2
.

Let us point out that, since
S_{e_{n}}(K)\subseteq S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1}
we have that
f(r)\leq\tilde{f}(r)
for every
r\in[0,\infty)
and then

\textrm{supp}(f)\subseteq\textrm{supp}(\tilde{f}).

Notice also that, from the definition of
M
, there exists
y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
y+Me_{n}\in S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Besides,
S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=M+1\}=\emptyset
. Therefore, since
M+1\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
f(M+1)=0
and
[0,M]\subseteq\textrm{supp}(f)\subseteq[0,M+1)
. Nevertheless, even though
f(M+1)=0
, it is possible for any integer
k>M
that
\tilde{f}(k)>0
, as the example given by
K=S_{e_{n}}(K)=\textrm{conv}\left\{\left(0,\pm 1\right),\left(\frac{1}{2},\pm k\right),(1,\pm 1)\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
shows.

Notice also that if
r>\frac{1}{2}\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))
, since
C_{n-1}\subseteq e_{n}^{\perp}
, we have

S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\}=\emptyset

and

(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\}=\emptyset,

and then
f(r)=\tilde{f}(r)=0
. Therefore,

\textrm{supp}(f)\subseteq\textrm{supp}(\tilde{f})\subseteq\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))\right].

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
satisfying the hypotheses
(H)
:

a)

\displaystyle{\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle)}
,

b)

\displaystyle{M:=\max_{x\in S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle x,e_{n}\rangle}\geq 1
.

Then, for any
p\geq 1
, there exists
m_{0}(p)\geq M
such that
m_{0}(p)>1
and

m_{0}(p)^{p}B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)=\frac{1}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k),

where
\tilde{f}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
is given by

\tilde{f}(r)=G_{n-1}((S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\}).

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
satisfying the hypotheses
(H)
and let
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the function given by

f(r)=G_{n-1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\}).

By Remark
5.2
,
f
satisfies that
f(r)\leq\tilde{f}(r)
for every
r\geq 0
. Besides, since
M\geq 1
, we have that
f(1)\geq 1
. For any
p\geq 1
, let also
h_{p}:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be the function given by

(5.4)

h_{p}(x)=x^{p}B_{x}(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor x\rfloor}p\left(1-\frac{k}{x}\right)^{n-1}k^{p-1}.

Our purpose is to prove the existence of some
m_{0}(p)\geq M
such that
m_{0}(p)>1
and

h_{p}(m_{0}(p))=\frac{1}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k).

Notice that for any
p\geq 1
,
h_{p}
is clearly continuous at any
x_{0}\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
. If
x_{0}=k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
then

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{-}}h_{p}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to k_{0}^{-}}\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}p\left(1-\frac{k}{x}\right)^{n-1}k^{p-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}p\left(1-\frac{k}{k_{0}}\right)^{n-1}k^{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}p\left(1-\frac{k}{k_{0}}\right)^{n-1}k^{p-1}=h_{p}(k_{0})

and

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}h_{p}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to k_{0}^{+}}\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}p\left(1-\frac{k}{x}\right)^{n-1}k^{p-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}p\left(1-\frac{k}{k_{0}}\right)^{n-1}k^{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{p}(k_{0}).

Thus, for any
p\geq 1
,
h_{p}
is continuous at
x_{0}=k_{0}
and then
h_{p}
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
. Besides, since for every
p\geq 1
the function
h_{p}
is defined as
h_{p}(x)=x^{p}B_{x}(p)
for every
x\in(0,\infty)
, we have that for any
p\geq 1
Lemma
5.1
implies that

(5.5)

\lim_{x\to\infty}h_{p}(x)=\infty.

Let us now distinguish two cases, depending on whether
p>1
or
p=1
: Assume first that
p>1
. Since
B_{x}(p)=0
for every
x\in(0,1]
, we have that for every
x\in(0,1]

(5.6)

h_{p}(x)=0.

Since, by definition,
M
is a non-negative integer and, by b), we are assuming that
M\geq 1
, we have that
\tilde{f}(1)\geq f(1)\geq 1>0
and then

\frac{1}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)\geq\frac{p\tilde{f}(1)}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}>0.

By the continuity of
h_{p}
on
(0,\infty)
, (
5.6
) and (
5.5
), there exists
m_{0}(p)>1
such that

h_{p}(m_{0}(p))=\frac{1}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)>0.

Let us assume now that
p=1
. Then
B_{x}(1)=\frac{1}{x}
for every
x\in(0,1]
and, since for every
x\in(0,\infty)
the function
h_{1}
is defined as
h_{1}(x)=xB_{x}(1)
, we have that for every
x\in(0,1]

(5.7)

h_{1}(x)=xB_{x}(1)=1.

Since by b) we are assuming that
M\geq 1
,

\frac{1}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)=\frac{1}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}(k)\geq\frac{\tilde{f}(0)+\tilde{f}(1)}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}>1,

where we have used that
\tilde{f}(1)\geq 1
and that, from the definition of
\tilde{f}
,

\tilde{f}(0)=G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)+C_{n-1})\geq G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)).

By the continuity of
h_{1}
on
(0,\infty)
, (
5.7
) and (
5.5
), there exists
m_{0}(1)>1
such that

h_{1}(m_{0}(1))=\frac{1}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)>0.

In both cases, since
m_{0}(p)>1
, such
m_{0}(p)
satisfies that
B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)>0
and

m_{0}(p)^{p}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)}.

Let us now see that
m_{0}(p)\geq M
. Assume that
m_{0}(p)<M
. By a) we are assuming

\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle).

Thus, from the definition of
\displaystyle{M=\max_{x\in S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle x,e_{n}\rangle}
, together with the hypotheses
(H)
, we have that
Me_{n}\in S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\langle x,e_{n}\rangle\geq 0\}
. Consequently, the convex hull of
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
and the point
m_{0}(p)e_{n}
is contained in
S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\langle x,e_{n}\rangle\geq 0\}
. Therefore, for every
0\leq k\leq\lfloor m_{0}(p)\rfloor
,

S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=k\}\supseteq\left(1-\frac{k}{m_{0}(p)}\right)P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\times\{k\}.

Thus,

(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=k\}\supseteq\left(\left(1-\frac{k}{m_{0}(p)}\right)P_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1}\right)\times\{k\}.

We obtain, as a consequence of the discrete Brunn-Minkowski inequality (Theorem
2.2
), that

(5.8)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(k)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{k}{m_{0}(p)}\right)G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{\frac{1}{n-1}}+\frac{k}{m_{0}(p)}G_{n-1}(\{0\})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{k}{m_{0}(p)}\right)G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{\frac{1}{n-1}}.

Since
\lfloor m_{0}(p)\rfloor\leq m_{0}(p)<M
and

\tilde{f}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(M)\geq f^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(M)\geq 1>0,

we obtain that

\sum_{k=0}^{M}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)>\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m_{0}(p)\rfloor}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)

and then, as a consequence of (
5.8
) and using (
5.4
), that

\displaystyle m_{0}(p)^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)}\geq\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{M}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m_{0}(p)\rfloor}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)}\geq\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m_{0}(p)\rfloor}pk^{p-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{m_{0}(p)}\right)^{n-1}}{B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{0}(p)^{p},

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
m_{0}(p)\geq M
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Notice that we have obtained in the proof that, in any case,
m_{0}(p)>1
. Therefore,
B_{m_{0}(p)}(q)>0
for every
p\geq 1
and every
q\geq 1
.

The following lemma shows that the
\frac{1}{n-1}
-affine function
g_{p}
defined in (
5.1
), with

the number obtained in Lemma
5.3
, has a crossing point with the function
\tilde{f}
defined in (
5.2
). Such function
g_{p}
is constructed so that for every

(5.10)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}g_{p}(k)=m_{0}(p)^{q}B_{m_{0}(p)}(q)G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)),

with

provided by the previous lemma satisfying that

(5.11)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)=m_{0}(p)^{p}B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)).

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
satisfying the hypotheses
(H)
and, for any
p\geq 1
, let
m_{0}(p)
be given by Lemma
5.3
. Let
f,\tilde{f}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
be the functions given by

•

f(r)=G_{n-1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\})
,

•

\tilde{f}(r)=G_{n-1}((S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\})

and let
g_{p}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be the function defined in (
5.1
), given by

g_{p}(x)=\begin{cases}\left(1-\frac{x}{m_{0}(p)}\right)^{n-1}G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))&\textrm{ if }0\leq x\leq m_{0}(p)\cr 0&\textrm{ if }x>m_{0}(p).\cr\end{cases}

Then, there exists
r_{0}(p)\in[0,\infty)
such that

\tilde{f}(k)\geq g_{p}(k)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with
0\leq k<r_{0}(p)
and

g_{p}(k)\geq f(k)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq r_{0}(p)
.

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
satisfying the hypotheses
(H)
and let
f,\tilde{f}
, and
g_{p}
be the functions defined in the statement. Let us denote, for every
r\geq 0
,

D_{r}=S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\},

and

\tilde{D}_{r}=(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\}=D_{r}+C_{n-1}.

Therefore,
f(r)=G_{n-1}(D_{r})
and
\tilde{f}(r)=G_{n-1}(\tilde{D}_{r})
for every
r\geq 0
.

Notice that, as mentioned in Remark
5.2
,

\textrm{supp}(f)\subseteq\textrm{supp}(\tilde{f})\subseteq\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))\right]

and then, for every
\displaystyle{r>\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))}
, we have

0=\tilde{f}(r)\leq g_{p}(r).

Therefore, we can define

\tilde{r}_{0}(p)=\inf\{r\geq 0\,:\,\tilde{f}(r)\leq g_{p}(r)\}\in[0,\infty).

From the definition of
\tilde{r}_{0}(p)
, we trivially have that for every
0\leq r<\tilde{r}_{0}(p)

\tilde{f}(r)>g_{p}(r).

In particular, for every
r=k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with
0\leq k<\tilde{r}_{0}(p)
, we have that

\tilde{f}(k)>g_{p}(k).

Notice also that
M
is defined in
(H)
as
\displaystyle{M=\max_{x\in S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle x,e_{n}\rangle}
and, as mentioned in Remark
5.2
,
\textrm{supp}(f)\subseteq[0,M+1)
. Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k>M
we have

g_{p}(k)\geq f(k)=0.

Therefore, if
\tilde{r}_{0}(p)>M
, then we can take
r_{0}(p)=\tilde{r}_{0}(p)
. Let us assume that
\tilde{r}_{0}(p)\leq M
.

Let us assume first that
\tilde{r}_{0}(p)=M\in\mathbb{N}
. If
f(M)\leq g_{p}(M)
, then we can take
r_{0}(p)=\tilde{r}_{0}(p)
. If, on the contrary,
f(M)>g_{p}(M)
, we have, by Remark
5.2

\tilde{f}(M)\geq f(M)>g_{p}(M)

and we can take any
r_{0}(p)>\tilde{r}_{0}(p)=M
.

Let us assume now that
\tilde{r}_{0}(p)<M
. In such case, let us take
r_{0}(p)=\tilde{r}_{0}(p)
and let us see that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
r_{0}(p)\leq k\leq M
we have that
g_{p}(k)\geq f(k)
. Notice that for every
0\leq r\leq M
, the set
\tilde{D}_{r}
is a convex open (in the topology induced in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\{r\}
by the standard topology in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) bounded set, and that
P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\tilde{D}_{r_{1}})\supseteq P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\tilde{D}_{r_{2}})
if
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
. Moreover, for any
0\leq r_{1}<\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))
we have
\displaystyle{\bigcup_{r_{2}>r_{1}}P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\tilde{D}_{r_{2}})=P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\tilde{D}_{r_{1}})}
. Therefore, the function
\tilde{f}
is continuous from the right at every
0\leq r_{1}<\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))
and, from the definition of
\tilde{r}_{0}(p)
as an infimum, we obtain that if
r_{0}(p)=\tilde{r}_{0}(p)<\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))
, then

(5.12)

\tilde{f}(r_{0}(p))\leq g_{p}(r_{0}(p))

and

r_{0}(p)=\min\{r\geq 0\,:\,\tilde{f}(r)\leq g_{p}(r)\}.

Notice that, as explained in Remark
5.2
,

[0,M]\subseteq\textrm{supp}(f)\subseteq\textrm{supp}(\tilde{f})\subseteq\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))\right].

Thus,
M\leq\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))
and, since
r_{0}(p)<M
, then
r_{0}(p)
satisfies (
5.12
). If
r_{0}(p)<r\leq M
, then, taking
\lambda=\frac{r_{0}(p)}{r}\in[0,1)
, we have, by the convexity of
S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1}
, that

\tilde{D}_{r_{0}(p)}\supseteq(1-\lambda)\tilde{D}_{0}+\lambda\tilde{D}_{r}=(1-\lambda)D_{0}+\lambda D_{r}+C_{n-1}.

By the discrete Brunn-Minkowski inequality (Theorem
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{f}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(r_{0}(p))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G_{n-1}((1-\lambda)D_{0}+\lambda D_{r}+C_{n-1})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)f^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(0)+\lambda f^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(r).

Taking into account that
r_{0}(p)<r\leq M\leq m_{0}(p)
,

\displaystyle g_{p}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(r_{0}(p))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{r_{0}(p)}{m_{0}(p)}\right)G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{\frac{1}{n-1}}+\lambda\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{0}}\right)G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)f^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(0)+\lambda g_{p}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(r).

Thus, by (
5.12
), we have that for every
r_{0}(p)<r\leq M

(1-\lambda)f^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(0)+\lambda g_{p}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(r)=g_{p}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(r_{0}(p))\geq\tilde{f}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(r_{0}(p))\geq(1-\lambda)f^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(0)+\lambda f^{\frac{1}{n-1}}(r)

Therefore, for every
r_{0}(p)<r\leq M
,

g_{p}(r)\geq f(r).

By (
5.12
), this inequality also holds for
r=r_{0}(p)
. Therefore, if
\tilde{r}_{0}(p)=r_{0}(p)<M
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r_{0}(p)\leq k\leq M
, we have that

g_{p}(k)\geq f(k).

∎

We are now able to prove the following theorem, which will give Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
satisfying the hypotheses
(H)
. Let
p\geq 1
and
m_{0}(p)
be given by Lemma
5.3
. Then, for any
p<q
we have that

\left(\frac{B_{m_{0}(p)}(q)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}f(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left(\frac{B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the functions
f,\tilde{f}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
are given by

•

f(r)=G_{n-1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\})
,

•

\tilde{f}(r)=G_{n-1}((S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\})
.

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
satisfying the hypotheses
(H)
and let
p\geq 1
. By Lemma
5.3
and Remark
5.4
, there exists
m_{0}(p)>1
such that

m_{0}(p)^{p}B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)=\frac{1}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)>0.

Let also
g_{p}
be the function defined in (
5.1
), given by

g_{p}(r)=\begin{cases}\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{0}(p)}\right)^{n-1}G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))&\textrm{ if }0\leq r\leq m_{0}(p)\cr 0&\textrm{ if }r>m_{0}(p),\cr\end{cases}

which, from the definition of
B_{m_{0}(p)}(q)
for
q>0
, satisfies (
5.10
). That is, for every
q>0

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}g_{p}(k)=m_{0}(p)^{q}B_{m_{0}(p)}(q)G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)).

Therefore, taking
q=p

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{p-1}g_{p}(k)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m_{0}(p)\rfloor}k^{p-1}g_{p}(k)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k).

Let now
q>p
. By Lemma
5.5
there exists
r_{0}(p)
such that
\tilde{f}(k)\geq g_{p}(k)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with
0\leq k<r_{0}(p)
and
g_{p}(k)\geq f(k)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq r_{0}(p)
. Taking into account that
f(k)\leq\tilde{f}(k)
for every
k\geq 0
, and understanding the first sum as
0
if
r_{0}(p)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil-1}k^{q-1}\left(\tilde{f}(k)-g_{p}(k)\right)-\sum_{k=\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil}^{\infty}k^{q-1}\left(g_{p}(k)-f(k)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil-1}k^{q-p}k^{p-1}\left(\tilde{f}(k)-g_{p}(k)\right)-\sum_{k=\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil}^{\infty}k^{q-p}k^{p-1}\left(g_{p}(k)-f(k)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{r_{0}(p)}^{q-p}\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil-1}k^{p-1}\left(\tilde{f}(k)-g_{p}(k)\right)-{r_{0}(p)}^{q-p}\sum_{k=\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil}^{\infty}k^{p-1}\left(g_{p}(k)-f(k)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{r_{0}(p)}^{q-p}\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil-1}k^{p-1}\left(\tilde{f}(k)-g_{p}(k)\right)+{r_{0}(p)}^{q-p}\sum_{k=\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil}^{\infty}k^{p-1}\left(f(k)-g_{p}(k)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{r_{0}(p)}^{q-p}\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil-1}k^{p-1}\left(\tilde{f}(k)-g_{p}(k)\right)+{r_{0}(p)}^{q-p}\sum_{k=\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil}^{\infty}k^{p-1}\left(\tilde{f}(k)-g_{p}(k)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{r_{0}}^{q-p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{p-1}\left(\tilde{f}(k)-g_{p}(k)\right)=0.

Therefore, we have that

\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil-1}k^{q-1}\tilde{f}(k)+\sum_{k=\lceil r_{0}(p)\rceil}^{\infty}k^{q-1}f(k)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{q-1}g_{p}(k).

and then, since
\tilde{f}(k)\geq f(k)
for every
k\geq 0
,

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{q-1}f(k)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{q-1}g_{p}(k).

Consequently, since
m_{0}(p)
satisfies (
5.11
),
g_{p}
satisfies (
5.10
), and
B_{m_{0}(p)}(q)>0
for every
q\geq 1
, as mentioned in Remark
5.4
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{B_{m_{0}(p)}(q)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}f(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{B_{m_{0}(p)}(q)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}qk^{q-1}g_{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=m_{0}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{B_{m_{0}(p)}(p)^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}pk^{p-1}\tilde{f}(k)}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

∎

We can finally prove Theorem
1.7
:

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)
such that
\displaystyle{\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle)}
and let

\displaystyle{M=\max_{x\in S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle x,e_{n}\rangle}
. If
M=0
then, as mentioned in Remark
1.9
, any value of
m_{0}>1
gives the inequality, even though
m_{0}
is not defined by Lemma
5.3
. Let us assume that
M\geq 1
. Therefore
K
satisfies the hypotheses
(H)
. Let
p=1
,
f,\tilde{f}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
be defined as in Theorem
5.6
by

•

f(r)=G_{n-1}(S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\})
,

•

\tilde{f}(r)=G_{n-1}((S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=r\})
,

and let
m_{0}=m_{0}(1)>1
be the number given by Lemma
5.3
. Applying Theorem
5.6
with
p=1
and
q=n+1
we obtain that

\left(\frac{B_{m_{0}}(n+1)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(n+1)k^{n}f(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\leq\frac{B_{m_{0}}(1)^{-1}}{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}(k).

Equivalently,

B_{m_{0}}(n+1)^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(n+1)k^{n}f(k)\leq\frac{\left(B_{m_{0}}(1)^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}(k)\right)^{n+1}}{(G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}.

From (
2.5
),
S_{e_{n}}(K)
is symmetric respect to the hyperplane
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=0\}
and then, from the definition of
f
and taking into account that for any
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(y,k)\neq(y,-k)
and that the term corresponding to
k=0
in the following sum is
0
, we obtain

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{n}f(k)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in S_{e_{n}}(K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{n}dG_{n}(x).

In the same way, since
S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1}
is also symmetric respect to the hyperplane
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=0\}
and taking into account that for any
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
(y,k)\neq(y,-k)
, but
(y,0)=(y,-0)
and the term corresponding to the following sum is not 0, we obtain

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{f}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\left(G_{n}(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})+G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)+C_{n-1})\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)B_{m_{0}}(n+1)^{-1}}{B_{m_{0}}(1)^{-(n+1)}}2^{n}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{n}dG_{n}(x)\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(G_{n}(S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})+G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)+C_{n-1})\right)^{n+1}}{(G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}.

∎

Finally, let us see that Theorem
1.7
implies Theorem
1.6
. Let us recall that, by Lemma
3.1
, Theorem
1.6
is an equivalent form of Theorem
1.1
, which implies, as seen in Corollary
3.6
, Zhang’s inequality (
1.1
).

Corollary 5.7

(Theorem
1.6
)
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. Then

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}2^{n}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{n}dx\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}.

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body. Since the inequality we want to prove is invariant under translations we can assume, without loss of generality, that

\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(K\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle).

Therefore, for any
\lambda>0

\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)}\mathrm{vol}_{1}(\lambda K\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=\mathrm{vol}_{1}(\lambda K\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle).

Since for every
y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)
the segment
S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle)
is centered at
y
, we have

\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)}G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle).

In particular,

\max_{y\in P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)\cap(y+\langle e_{n}\rangle))=G_{1}(S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)\cap\langle e_{n}\rangle).

By Theorem
1.7
, there exists
\displaystyle{m_{0,\lambda}\geq\max_{x\in S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle x,e_{n}\rangle}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)B_{m_{0,\lambda}}(n+1)}{B_{m_{0,\lambda}}(1)^{-(n+1)}}2^{n}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{n}dG_{n}(x)\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(G_{n}(S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)+C_{n-1})+G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)+C_{n-1})\right)^{n+1}}{(G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)))^{n}}.

Therefore, taking into account that
S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)=\lambda S_{e_{n}}(K)
for any
\lambda>0
and dividing both sides of the inequality by
\lambda^{2n}=\frac{\lambda^{n(n+1)}}{\lambda^{n(n-1)}}
, we have that for every
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)B_{m_{0,\lambda}}(n+1)}{B_{m_{0,\lambda}}(1)^{-(n+1)}}2^{n}\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\int_{\lambda S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle\frac{x}{\lambda},e_{n}\rangle|^{n}dG_{n}(x)\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{G_{n}(\lambda S_{e_{n}}(K)+C_{n-1})}{\lambda^{n}}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{G_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(\lambda K)+C_{n-1})}{\lambda^{n-1}}\right)^{n+1}}{\left(\frac{G_{n-1}(\lambda P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K))}{\lambda^{n-1}}\right)^{n}}.

Taking the limit as
\lambda\to\infty
and taking into account that
\displaystyle{\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\max_{x\in S_{e_{n}}(\lambda K)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\langle x,e_{n}\rangle=\infty}
, and therefore
\displaystyle{\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}m_{0,\lambda}=\infty}
, we obtain, using Lemma
5.1
,

\frac{(n+1){{2n}\choose{n-1}}}{n^{n+1}}2^{n}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{n}dx\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(S_{e_{n}}(K))+0)^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}=\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}},

where we have also used (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), since the function
|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{n}
is Riemann-integrable on
S_{e_{n}}(K)
.

Equivalently,

\frac{{{2n}\choose{n}}}{n^{n}}2^{n}\int_{S_{e_{n}}(K)}|\langle x,e_{n}\rangle|^{n}dx\leq\frac{(\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K))^{n+1}}{(\mathrm{vol}_{n-1}(P_{e_{n}^{\perp}}(K)))^{n}}.

∎

6.
Ball bodies of the discrete covariogram

In this section we initiate, for any convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in K
, the study of the
p
-th ball bodies of the discrete covariogram function
\tilde{g}_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
given by

(6.1)

\tilde{g}_{K}(x)=G_{n}(K\cap(x+K))=\sum_{y\in K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\chi_{y-K}(x).

Since
y-K
is measurable for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as it is a compact set,
\tilde{g}_{K}
is a measurable function. Besides, it satisfies that
\tilde{g}_{K}(0)=G_{n}(K)>0
, since
0\in K
. Therefore, we can consider, for
p>0
, the
p
-th Ball bodies of
\tilde{g_{K}}
, which are defined by (
2.14
) and are given by

\displaystyle K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K}):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K}(rx)dr\geq\tilde{g}_{K}(0)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}G_{n}(K\cap(rx+K))dr\geq G_{n}(K)\right\}.

The
p
-th ball bodies of
\tilde{g}_{K}
,
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})
, are not necessarily convex bodies. Nevertheless, they are star sets whose radial function, by (
2.15
), is given, for any
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, by

\rho_{K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})}^{p}(\theta)=\frac{p}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}G_{n}(K\cap(r\theta+K))dr.

Remark 6.1
.

Let us point out that if
K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\{0\}
, then, by (
6.1
),
\tilde{g}_{K}=\chi_{-K}
and then, by (
2.16
),
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})=-K
for every
p>0
.

We will also consider the discrete covariogram function of the open set
K+C_{n}
,
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, given by

\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(x)=G_{n}((K+C_{n})\cap(x+K+C_{n}))=\sum_{y\in(K+C_{n})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\chi_{y-(K+C_{n})}(x),

which is also measurable since
y-(K+C_{n})
is measurable for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as
K+C_{n}
is an open set, and satisfies that
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(0)>0
, since
0\in K\subseteq K+C_{n}
. The
p
-th Ball bodies of
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}
are given by

\displaystyle K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(rx)dr\geq\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(0)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}G_{n}((K+C_{n})\cap(rx+K+C_{n}))dr\geq G_{n}(K+C_{n})\right\},

which are star sets with radial function given, for every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, by

\rho_{K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})}^{p}(\theta)=\frac{p}{G_{n}(K+C_{n})}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}G_{n}((K+C_{n})\cap(r\theta+K+C_{n}))dr.

Notice that, since
K\subseteq K+C_{n}
, then
\tilde{g}_{K}(x)\leq\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus, for every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, we have

G_{n}(K)\rho_{K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})}^{p}(\theta)\leq G_{n}(K+C_{n})\rho_{K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})}^{p}(\theta)

and then

(6.2)

K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})\subseteq\left(\frac{G_{n}(K+C_{n})}{G_{n}(K)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}).

The purpose of this section is to prove, on the one hand, that even though
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})
is not necessarily convex, its convex hull is contained in the same dilation of
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
that appears in (
6.2
) and, on the other hand, that even though we do not know whether an inclusion relation such as the one given by (
2.18
) holds for the
p
-th Ball bodies of the discrete covariogram, a similar inclusion relation holds when substituting
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})
by the dilation of
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
given by the right hand side of (
6.2
). More precisely, we will prove the following:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body such that
0\in K
. For any
0<p<q
we have that

{{n+q}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{q}}K_{q}(\tilde{g}_{K})\subseteq{{n+p}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{G_{n}(K+C_{n})}{G_{n}(K)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}).

Let us start by showing that the convex hull of
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})
is contained in in the same dilation of
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
that appears in (
6.2
). The proof will follow the lines of the proof of the convexity of the
p
-th Ball bodies of log-concave function (see
[
9
, Theorem 2.5.5]
), relying on
[
9
, Theorem 2.5.4]
, which gives a lower bound for the integral of a function
h
on
[0,\infty)
in terms of some mean of the integrals of two functions
w
and
g
on
[0,\infty)
, provided that for any
r,s>0
the function
h
, evaluated at the same mean of
r
and
s
is bounded below by some geometric mean of
w(r)
and
g(s)
. However, some modifications in the proof will be induced by the necessity of adding the open cube
C_{n}
to the set in the left-hand side in the discrete Brunn-Minkowski inequality (Theorem
2.2
).

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in K
. Then for any
p>0

\textrm{conv}(K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K}))\subseteq\left(\frac{G_{n}(K+C_{n})}{G_{n}(K)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}).

Proof.

First of all, let us define
\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
as the following set:

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(rx)dr\geq\tilde{g}_{K}(0)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}G_{n}((K+C_{n})\cap(rx+K+C_{n}))dr\geq G_{n}(K)\right\}.

Notice that since the right-hand side in the inequality defining this set is
\tilde{g}_{K}(0)
rather than
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(0)
, this set is not
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
but a dilation of it. Indeed, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\lambda>0

p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(\lambda rx)dr=\frac{p}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(sx)ds

and then, if
0<\lambda\leq 1
,

p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(\lambda rx)dr\geq p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(rx)dr.

Thus, if
x\in\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
, we have that also
\lambda x\in\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
. Therefore
\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
is a star set with
0
as a center. Moreover, as in (
2.15
), for any
\theta\in S^{n-1}

\displaystyle\rho_{\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})}(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\lambda>0\,:\,p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(\lambda r\theta)dr\geq\tilde{g}_{K}(0)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\lambda>0\,:\,\frac{p}{\lambda^{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(s\theta)ds\geq\tilde{g}_{K}(0)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{\tilde{g}_{K}(0)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(s\theta)ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}G_{n}((K+C_{n})\cap(r\theta+K+C_{n}))dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{G_{n}(K+C_{n})}{G_{n}(K)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\rho_{K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})}(\theta).

Therefore,
\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
is the dilation of
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
in the right-hand side of (
6.2
):

\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})=\left(\frac{G_{n}(K+C_{n})}{G_{n}(K)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}).

Let
x,y\in K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})
,
\lambda,\mu\in[0,1]
such that
\lambda+\mu=1
, and
\gamma=\frac{1}{p}
. Let us define the functions

•

h(t)=\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(t^{\gamma}(\lambda x+\mu y))
,
t>0
,

•

w(r)=\tilde{g}_{K}(r^{\gamma}x)
,
r>0
,

•

g(s)=\tilde{g}_{K}(s^{\gamma}y)
,
s>0
.

Denoting by
M_{-\gamma}^{\lambda}(r,s)
for any
r,s>0
the number

M_{-\gamma}^{\lambda}(r,s)=(\lambda r^{-\gamma}+\mu s^{-\gamma})^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}},

our purpose is to show that for any
r,s>0
we have that

(6.3)

h(M_{-\gamma}^{\lambda}(r,s))\geq w(r)^{\frac{\lambda s^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}}g(s)^{\frac{\mu r^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}}

in order to apply
[
9
, Theorem 2.5.4]
and obtain

(6.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t)dt\geq M_{-\gamma}^{\lambda}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}w(r)dr,\int_{0}^{\infty}g(s)ds\right).

If
K\cap(r^{\gamma}x+K)=\emptyset
or
K\cap(s^{\gamma}y+K)=\emptyset
then
w(r)=0
or
g(s)=0
and inequality (
6.3
) is trivial. Otherwise, calling

•

\lambda_{1}=\frac{\lambda s^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}

•

\mu_{1}=\frac{\mu r^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}

we have that
\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1}=1
and, since
K
is convex,

\displaystyle(K+C_{n})\cap(\lambda_{1}r^{\gamma}x+\mu_{1}s^{\gamma}y+K+C_{n})\supseteq

\displaystyle\supseteq

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(K\cap(r^{\gamma}x+K))+\mu_{1}(K\cap(s^{\gamma}y+K))+C_{n}

and then, by the discrete Brunn-Minkowski inequality (Theorem
2.2
),

\displaystyle G_{n}((K+C_{n})\cap(\lambda_{1}r^{\gamma}x+\mu_{1}s^{\gamma}y+K+C_{n}))\geq

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G_{n}(\lambda_{1}(K\cap(r^{\gamma}x+K))+\mu_{1}(K\cap(s^{\gamma}y+K))+C_{n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{1}G_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(K\cap(r^{\gamma}x+K))+\mu_{1}G_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}(K\cap(s^{\gamma}y+K))\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G_{n}^{\lambda_{1}}(K\cap(r^{\gamma}x+K))G_{n}^{\mu_{1}}(K\cap(s^{\gamma}y+K)).

Therefore,

\displaystyle h(M_{-\gamma}^{\lambda}(r,s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda r^{-\gamma}+\mu s^{-\gamma}}(\lambda x+\mu y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}\left(\frac{\lambda s^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}r^{\gamma}x+\frac{\mu r^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}s^{\gamma}y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}\left(\lambda_{1}r^{\gamma}x+\mu_{1}s^{\gamma}y\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{K}^{\lambda_{1}}(r^{\gamma}x)\tilde{g}_{K}^{\mu_{1}}(s^{\gamma}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{K}(r^{\gamma}x)^{\frac{\lambda s^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}}\tilde{g}_{K}(s^{\gamma}y)^{\frac{\mu r^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w(r)^{\frac{\lambda s^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}}g(s)^{\frac{\mu r^{\gamma}}{\lambda s^{\gamma}+\mu r^{\gamma}}}.

Thus, by
[
9
, Theorem 2.5.4]
we have (
6.4
). Equivalently, taking into account that
-\gamma<0
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(t^{\gamma}(\lambda x+\mu y))dt\right)^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{g}_{K}(r^{\gamma}x)dr\right)^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{g}_{K}(s^{\gamma}y)ds\right)^{-\gamma}.

Changing variables, using that
\gamma=\frac{1}{p}
, and taking into account that
x,y\in K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(t(\lambda x+\mu y))dt\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}pr^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K}(rx)dr\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mu\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}ps^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K}(sy)ds\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda G_{n}(K)^{-\frac{1}{p}}+\mu G_{n}(K)^{-\frac{1}{p}}=G_{n}(K)^{-\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore

\int_{0}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(t(\lambda x+\mu y))dt\geq G_{n}(K)

and

\lambda x+\mu y\in\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})=\left(\frac{G_{n}(K+C_{n})}{G_{n}(K)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}).

∎

Remark 6.4
.

Let us point out that a reverse inclusion of
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
in a dilation of the convex hull of
\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})
is not possible as the following example shows: Consider
K=\textrm{conv}\{(0,0),\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right),\left(\frac{1}{2},-1\right)\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since
K\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\{(0,0)\}
, by Remark
6.1
, for any
p>0
we have that
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K})=-K

and then

\textrm{conv}(K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K}))=-K\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,\langle x,e_{2}\rangle\leq 0\}.

Therefore, if
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{2}})
is contained in a dilation of
\textrm{conv}(K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K}))
, necessarily
\rho_{K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{2}})}(e_{2})=0
. However,

(0,0)\in(K+C_{2})\cap(re_{2}+K+C_{2})\quad\textrm{for every }r\in[0,1)

and then
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(re_{2})>0
for every
r\in[0,1)
. Thus,
\rho_{K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{2}})}(e_{2})>0
and
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{2}})
is not contained in any dilation of
\textrm{conv}(K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K}))
.

The following lemma shows that even though it is defined from the discrete covariogram, the volume of
K_{n}(\tilde{g}_{K})
equals the volume of
K
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in K
. Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
be a homogeneous function of degree
p\geq 0
. Then

\int_{K_{n+p}(\tilde{g}_{K})}h(x)dx=\frac{1}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)G_{n}(K\cap(x+K))dx.

In particular,

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{n}(\tilde{g}_{K}))=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

Proof.

Integrating in polar coordinates we have that

\displaystyle\int_{K_{n+p}(\tilde{g}_{K})}h(x)dx=n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{K_{n+p}(\tilde{g}_{K})}(\theta)}r^{n-1}h(r\theta)drd\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{K_{n+p}(\tilde{g}_{K})}(\theta)}r^{n+p-1}h(\theta)drd\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n+p}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})\int_{S^{n-1}}\rho_{K_{n+p}(\tilde{g}_{K})}^{n+p}(\theta)h(\theta)d\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n+p-1}G_{n}(K\cap(r\theta+K))h(\theta)drd\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{2}^{n})}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}G_{n}(K\cap(r\theta+K))h(r\theta)drd\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)G_{n}(K\cap(x+K))dx.

Notice that if
h(x)=1
, which is homogeneous of degree
0
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K_{n}(\tilde{g}_{K}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G_{n}(K\cap(x+K))dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\chi_{K}(y)\chi_{x+K}(y)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G_{n}(K)}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{K}(y)\chi_{y-K}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G_{n}(K)}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\chi_{K}(y)\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K)=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(K).

∎

Let us now prove the inclusion relation given in Theorem
6.2
. The proof will follow the lines of the proof of Theorem
2.4
. We begin with the following lemma in which, for any
p>0
and any
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, we construct a
\frac{1}{n}
-affine function on its support,
g_{p,\theta}
, with the property that for any
q>0

\left(\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\infty}qr^{q-1}g_{p,\theta}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=m_{p,\theta},

where

m_{p,\theta}:={{n+p}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\rho_{\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})}(\theta),

being
\tilde{K}_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
the dilation of
K_{p}(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}})
defined in Proposition
6.3
. Such function has a crossing point
r_{0}(p,\theta)
(see Lemma
6.6
for the precise definition of such crossing point) with a modification of the function
\tilde{g}_{K}
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body such that
0\in K
and let
\theta\in S^{n-1}
. For any
p>0
, let

m_{p,\theta}:=\left(\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\infty}pr^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and
g_{p,\theta}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be the function given by

g_{p,\theta}(r)=\begin{cases}\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)^{n}G_{n}(K)&\textrm{ if }0\leq r\leq m_{p,\theta}\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then, there exists
r_{0}(p,\theta)\in[0,\infty)
such that

\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)\geq g_{p,\theta}(r)

for every
0\leq r<r_{0}(p,\theta)
and

g_{p,\theta}(r)\geq\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)

for every
r\geq r_{0}(p,\theta)
.

Proof.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in K
,
\theta\in S^{n-1}
and
p>0
. Let
m_{p,\theta}
and
g_{p,\theta}
be defined as in the statement. First of all, notice that since
0\in K
we have that
0\in\textrm{int}(K+C_{n})
and then
m_{p,\theta}>0
. Notice also that for every convex set
L
, every
\theta\in S^{n-1}
, and every
0\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
we have that
L\cap(r_{1}\theta+L)\supseteq L\cap(r_{2}\theta+L)
and then the functions
\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)
and
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)
are decreasing in
r\in[0,\infty)
. Moreover, since
K\subseteq K+C_{n}
we have that
K\cap(x+K)\subseteq(K+C_{n})\cap(x+K+C_{n})
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and then
\tilde{g}_{K}(x)\leq\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, since
K
is a compact convex set,
\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)
is continuous from the left in
r\in[0,\infty)
and since
K+C_{n}
is an open bounded convex set,
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)
is continuous from the right in
r\in[0,\infty)
. Let us call

(6.5)

M_{\theta}=\max\{r\geq 0\,:\,\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)\geq 1\}

and

\tilde{M}_{\theta}=\sup\{r\geq 0\,:\,\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)\geq 1\}

and notice that, necessarily,
m_{p,\theta}\geq M_{\theta}
. Otherwise, if
m_{p,\theta}<M_{\theta}\leq\tilde{M}_{\theta}
then

K\cap(m_{p,\theta}\theta+K)\neq\emptyset
and for every
0\leq r\leq m_{p,\theta}
we have that

(K+C_{n})\cap\left(r\theta+K+C_{n}\right)\supseteq\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)K+\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}(K\cap(m_{p,\theta}\theta+K))+C_{n}

and then, by the discrete Brunn-Minkowski inequality (Theorem
2.2
),

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r\theta)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G_{n}\left(\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)K+\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}(K\cap(m_{p,\theta}\theta+K))+C_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)G_{n}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}+\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}G_{n}(K\cap(m_{p,\theta}\theta+K))^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)G_{n}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Therefore, for every
0\leq r\leq m_{p,\theta}
we have that

\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)\geq g_{p,\theta}(r)

and then, taking into account that
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)\geq 1
for every
0\leq r<\tilde{M}_{\theta}

\displaystyle m_{p,\theta}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\infty}pr^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\tilde{M}_{\theta}}pr^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)dr

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{m_{p,\theta}}pr^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)dr

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{m_{p,\theta}}pr^{p-1}g_{p,\theta}(r)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{n+p}\choose{n}}\int_{0}^{m_{p,\theta}}pr^{p-1}\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)dr=m_{p,\theta}^{p},

which is a contradiction.

Since we trivially have that for any
r>\tilde{M}_{\theta}

0=\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)\leq g_{p,\theta}(r),

we can define

r_{0}(p,\theta)=\inf\{r\geq 0\,:\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)\leq g_{p,\theta}(r)\}<\infty.

From the definition of
r_{0}(p,\theta)
as an infimum we trivially have that for every
0\leq r<r_{0}(p,\theta)

\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)>g_{p,\theta}(r).

Besides, since
\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)
is continuous from the right on
[0,\infty)
and
\tilde{g}_{K}(x)\leq\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, from the definition of
r_{0}(p,\theta)
as an infimum we obtain

(6.6)

\tilde{g}_{K}(r_{0}(p,\theta)\theta)\leq\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r_{0}(p,\theta)\theta)\leq g_{p,\theta}(r_{0}(p,\theta)).

and
r_{0}(p,\theta)
is a minimum.

Notice that also, from the definition of
M_{\theta}
, for every
r>M_{\theta}
we have that

g_{p,\theta}(r)\geq\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)=0.

Therefore, if
r_{0}(p,\theta)>M_{\theta}
the theorem is proved. Thus, we will assume that
r_{0}(p,\theta)\leq M_{\theta}
.

If
r_{0}(p,\theta)=M_{\theta}
then, by (
6.6
) we have that

\tilde{g}_{K}(M_{\theta}\theta)\leq\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(M_{\theta}\theta)\leq g_{p,\theta}(M_{\theta})

and the theorem is proved.

Let us assume, then, that
r_{0}(p,\theta)<M_{\theta}
and let us prove that if
r_{0}(p,\theta)<r\leq M_{\theta}
then
g_{p,\theta}(r)\geq\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)
.

If
r_{0}(p,\theta)<r\leq M_{\theta}
then, taking
\lambda=\frac{r_{0}(p,\theta)}{r}\in[0,1)
we have

(K+C_{n})\cap(r_{0}(p,\theta)\theta+K+C_{n})\supseteq(1-\lambda)K+\lambda(K\cap(r\theta+K))+C_{n}

and then by the discrete Brunn-Minkowski inequality (Theorem
2.2
)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r_{0}(p,\theta)\theta)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle G_{n}\left((1-\lambda)K+\lambda(K\cap(r\theta+K)+C_{n})\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)G_{n}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}+\lambda G_{n}(K\cap(r\theta+K))^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\tilde{g}_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}(0)+\lambda\tilde{g}_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r\theta).

Taking into account that
r_{0}(p,\theta)<r\leq M_{\theta}\leq m_{p,\theta}

\displaystyle g_{p,\theta}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r_{0}(p,\theta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{r_{0}(p,\theta)}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)G_{n}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)G_{n}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}+\lambda\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)G_{n}(K)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\tilde{g}_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}(0)+\lambda g_{p,\theta}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r).

Thus, by (
6.6
), if
r_{0}(p,\theta)<r\leq M_{\theta}

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\tilde{g}_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}(0)+\lambda g_{p,\theta}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{p,\theta}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r_{0}(p,\theta))\geq\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r_{0}(p,\theta)\theta)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)\tilde{g}_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}(0)+\lambda\tilde{g}_{K}^{\frac{1}{n}}(r\theta)

and then

g_{p,\theta}(r)\geq\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta).

∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with
0\in K
. Let
\theta\in S^{n-1}
. Fix
p>0
and let, as in Lemma
6.6
,
m_{p,\theta}
be defined as

m_{p,\theta}=\left(\frac{{{n+p}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\infty}pr^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and
g_{p,\theta}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be the function given by

g_{p,\theta}(r)=\begin{cases}\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)^{n}G_{n}(K)&\textrm{ if }0\leq r\leq m_{p,\theta}\\
0&\textrm{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Notice that, changing variables
r=m_{p,\theta}s
, for every
q>0
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\infty}qr^{q-1}g_{p,\theta}(r)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{m_{p,\theta}}qr^{q-1}\left(1-\frac{r}{m_{p,\theta}}\right)^{n}G_{n}(K)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{{n+q}\choose{n}}m_{p,\theta}^{q}\int_{0}^{1}qs^{q-1}(1-s)^{n}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{p,\theta}^{q}.

In particular, taking
q=p
, we have that

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}g_{p,\theta}(r)dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)dr.

Let
q>p
. Taking
r_{0}(p,\theta)
provided by Lemma
6.6
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r_{0}(p,\theta)}r^{q-1}\left(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)-g_{p,\theta}(r)\right)dr-\int_{r_{0}(p,\theta)}^{\infty}r^{q-1}\left(g_{p,\theta}(r)-\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)\right)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r_{0}(p,\theta)}r^{q-p}r^{p-1}\left(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)-g_{p,\theta}(r)\right)dr

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}(p,\theta)}^{\infty}r^{q-p}r^{p-1}\left(g_{p,\theta}(r)-\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)\right)dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r_{0}(p,\theta)^{q-p}\int_{0}^{r_{0}(p,\theta)}r^{p-1}\left(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)-g_{p,\theta}(r)\right)dr

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle r_{0}(p,\theta)^{q-p}\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\left(g_{p,\theta}(r)-\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)\right)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{0}(p,\theta)^{q-p}\int_{0}^{r_{0}(p,\theta)}r^{p-1}\left(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)-g_{p,\theta}(r)\right)dr

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle r_{0}(p,\theta)^{q-p}\int_{r_{0}(p,\theta)}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\left(\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)-g_{p,\theta}(r)\right)dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r_{0}(p,\theta)^{q-p}\int_{0}^{r_{0}(p,\theta)}r^{p-1}\left(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)-g_{p,\theta}(r)\right)dr

\displaystyle r_{0}(p,\theta)^{q-p}\int_{r_{0}(p,\theta)}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\left(\tilde{g}_{K+C_{n}}(r\theta)-g_{p,\theta}(r)\right)dr

and then, since

for every

,

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q-1}\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)dr\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q-1}g_{p,\theta}(r)dr.

\displaystyle{{n+q}\choose{n}}^{\frac{1}{q}}\rho_{K_{q}(\tilde{g}_{K})}(\theta)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{{{n+q}\choose{n}}}{G_{n}(K)}\int_{0}^{\infty}qr^{q-1}\tilde{g}_{K}(r\theta)dr\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

Corollary 6.8
.

K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body such that

K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body with

,
\theta\in S^{n-1}

. We have seen in Theorem
6.2
that for any

\theta\in S^{n-1}

∎

Simplified model of immunotherapy for glioblastoma multiforme: cancer stem cells hypothesis perspective
Wiktor Jochymczyk, Urszula Foryś

Abstract
Despite ongoing efforts in cancer research, a fully effective treatment for glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) is still unknown. Since adoptive cell transfer immunotherapy is one of the potential cure candidates, efforts have been made to assess its effectiveness using mathematical modeling. In this paper, we consider a model of GBM immunotherapy proposed by Abernathy and Burke (2016), which also takes into account the dynamics of cancer stem cells, i.e., the type of cancer cells that are hypothesized to be largely responsible for cancer recurrence. We modify the initial ODE system by applying simplifying assumptions and analyze the existence and stability of steady states of the obtained simplified model depending on the treatment levels.

Introduction

Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) accounts for
15.6\%
of all and up to

45.2\%
of malignant primary brain tumors

5\%

5

The commonly accepted treatment for GBM consists of tumor resection followed by radiotherapy combined with temozolomide chemotherapy
[
5
]
. Due to the low effectiveness of conventional therapy, research on alternative treatment methods is still ongoing. These include various types of immunotherapy, such as treatment with cancer vaccines, oncolytic viruses, immune checkpoint inhibitors, and adoptive cell transfer
[

In
[
7
]
, Kronik et al. presented a mathematical model for the treatment of GBM by adoptive transfer of cytotoxic T cells. That work was then continued by Kogan et al. in
[
8
]
. More precisely, in
[
8
]
, the model proposed in
[
7
]
was first generalized by assuming certain properties of the functions that describe the processes considered in the original article and reflected in that article by specific functions. For this generalized model, Kogan
et al.
proved the boundedness of solutions and the dissipativity of the model. Since every dissipative system has a global attractor, steady states were studied as candidates for it. In the general case, the steady states established for the untreated system were considered. Then, for the system with treatment, conditions for tumor elimination were proposed. The final section was devoted to confronting analytical and numerical results with clinical data.

In
[
9
]
, Abernathy and Burke built on previous results and modified the general model from
[
8
]
to include cancer stem cells (CSCs). The CSC hypothesis states that a special type of cancer cells with properties similar to healthy stem cells is largely responsible for the formation and development of cancer. These cells are thought to multiply much faster than normal cancer cells and then differentiate into other types of cancer cells. Theoretically, if even a small number of CSCs survive treatment, it could lead to relapse.

The GBM development and immunotherapy model considered in this article is based on the ODE system presented in
[
9
]
. First, we adopt the notation used in that article:

•

and
S

•

•

With the above notation, the model proposed in
[

\displaystyle\dot{T}

\displaystyle=\alpha(T,S)+R_{T}(T)T-f_{T}(x)g_{T}(u)h_{T}(T)CT,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\dot{S}

\displaystyle=R_{S}(S)S-\alpha(T,S)-f_{S}(x)g_{S}(u)h_{S}(S)CS,

\displaystyle=f_{C}((T+S)v)g_{C}(x)-\mu_{C}C+N(t),

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=f_{x}(T,S)-\mu_{x}x,

Similarly, Equation (

The change in the amount of TGF-

The model presented above is the subject of analysis performed in the following sections under additional assumptions, which we will pose below.

From the above assumptions, we find that the RHS of System (

\mathbf{C}^{1}

Because System (

Assumption 2

We assume that

Note that for any initial conditions the variables

\displaystyle\dot{T}

\displaystyle=r_{\alpha}\frac{S}{K_{S}}\frac{T}{K_{T}}(K_{T}-T)+r_{T}\left(1-\frac{T}{K_{T}}\right)T-f_{T}(x)G_{T}(N)h_{T}(T)T,

In the next subsection, we will formulate the conditions that allow for a cure.

3.2
Semitrivial cure steady state

First, we consider the simplest case where

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}r_{T}-f_{T}\left(\frac{g_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\right)G_{T}(N)&0&0\\

\displaystyle=r_{T}-f_{T}\left(\frac{g_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\right)G_{T}(N),

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=r_{S}-f_{S}\left(\frac{g_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\right)G_{S}(N),

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=-\mu_{x}.

Of course

is a negative constant, while
\lambda_{1}
,

if and only if

\frac{r_{i}}{f_{i}\left(\frac{g_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\right)}<G_{i}(N),\quad i=T,\,S.

(9)

Since the functions

,

It is easy to see that if

, then Condition (
9
) is satisfied, and therefore the CCS is locally asymptotically stable.

As local stability is difficult to interpret in practice, we are more interested in stronger condition of global stability. Considering System (
2
) in the invariant set
\mathcal{D}
we can pose such conditions.

Theorem 7
.

If
N>N_{\min}^{glob}
, where

N_{\min}^{glob}=\max\left\{G_{T}^{-1}\left(\frac{r_{T}}{f_{T}(\bar{x})h_{T}(K_{T})}\right),G_{S}^{-1}\left(\frac{r_{S}}{f_{S}(\bar{x})h_{S}(K_{S})}\right)\right\},

, then the CCS is globally stable in
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof:

First note that in the invariant set, due to monotonicity of the functions
f_{i}
,
h_{i}
,
i=T
,
S
, we have

f_{i}(x)\geq f_{i}(\bar{x}),\quad h_{i}(i)\geq h_{i}(K_{i}),\quad i=T,\,S,

and therefore

\dot{S}\leq r_{S}\left(1-\frac{S}{K_{S}}\right)S-f_{S}(\bar{x})G_{S}(N)h_{S}(K_{S})S\leq S\left(r_{S}-f_{S}(\bar{x})G_{S}(N)h_{S}(K_{S})\right).

We easily see that if the coefficient
\hat{r}_{S}=r_{S}-f_{S}(\bar{x})G_{S}(N)h_{S}(K_{S})

is negative, that is,

G_{S}(N)>\frac{r_{S}}{f_{S}(\bar{x})h_{S}(K_{S})}
,
then
S
tends to 0 exponentially over time.

Similarly, if
G_{T}(N)>\frac{r_{T}}{f_{T}(\bar{x})h_{T}(K_{T})}
, then the coefficient

\hat{r}_{T}=r_{T}-f_{T}(\bar{x})G_{T}(N)h_{T}(K_{T})<0,

and according to the assumption of this theorem, there exists
\bar{t}>0
such that

r_{\alpha}\frac{S(t)}{K_{S}}<\frac{\left|\hat{r}_{T}\right|}{2},\quad\text{for all}\ \ t>\bar{t}.

Therefore, for
t>\bar{t}
,

\dot{T}\leq r_{\alpha}\frac{S}{K_{S}}{T}+r_{T}T-f_{T}(\bar{x})G_{T}(N)h_{T}(K_{T})T<-\frac{\left|\hat{r}_{T}\right|}{2}T,

implies that
T(t)\to 0
as well. Then it is obvious that
x(t)\to x^{*}
.

\hfill\square

Let us fix

.

For

Remark 8
.

If
r_{S}>F(0)
, then there exists at least one RSS.

However, without specifying the shapes of

and
h_{S}
more precisely, we are not able to estimate this number from above. Hence, we pose additional assumptions related to these functions.

Assumption 9
.

Assume that
f_{S}
and

are convex functions.

Remark 10
.

The product of two nonnegative, decreasing, convex functions is a nonnegative, decreasing, convex function.

Using this property of the function

study the number of RSS.

•

If

, then there is exactly one RSS. The smaller the value of

is to

.

•

If

, then:

(I)

(II)

•

If

, then:

(I)
|F^{\prime}(0)|\leq\frac{r_{S}}{K_{S}}
, then there is no nonnegative solution.

(II)

is such that the two solutions overlap and the straight line in tangent to

One the basis of the above considerations, let us define the following critical values of

\begin{split}N_{th}^{I}:=&\ G_{S}^{-1}\left(\frac{r_{S}}{f_{S}(\frac{g_{x}}{\mu_{x}})}\right)=N_{\min}^{(S)},\\

\displaystyle\ \frac{\left(K_{S}-{S^{*}_{th}}\right)}{f_{S}\left(\frac{g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*}_{th}}{\mu_{x}}\right)h_{S}(S^{*}_{th})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|\frac{a_{x,S}}{\mu_{x}}f_{S}^{\prime}(\frac{g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*}_{th}}{\mu_{x}})h_{S}(S^{*}_{th})+f_{S}(\frac{g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*}_{th}}{\mu_{x}})h_{S}^{\prime}(S^{*}_{th})\right|}.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial T}(0,S^{*},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{r_{\alpha}S^{*}}{K_{S}}+r_{T}-\frac{a_{T,x}(g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*})+\mu_{x}}{g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*}+\mu_{x}}G_{S}\left(N\right).

(23)

Assumption 15
.

From now on, we assume that
a_{T,x}=a_{S,x}.

From the Assumption above and Eq. (
22
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{a_{T,x}(g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*})+\mu_{x}}{g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*}+\mu_{x}}G_{S}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})(S^{*}+1)}{K_{S}},

(24)

which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial T}(0,S^{*},x^{*})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{r_{\alpha}S^{*}+r_{T}K_{S}-r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})(S^{*}+1)}{K_{S}}.

(25)

Let us denote the numerator of the RHS of the inequality above as

\displaystyle Q(S^{*})

\displaystyle:=r_{\alpha}S^{*}+r_{T}K_{S}-r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})(S^{*}+1)

\displaystyle=r_{S}S^{*2}+S^{*}(r_{S}-r_{S}K_{S}+r_{\alpha})+K_{S}(r_{T}-r_{S}).

Then we have

\displaystyle Q(S^{*})<0\implies\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial T}(0,S^{*},x^{*})<0.

(26)

Notice that the above condition can be satisfied only for small values of
S^{*}
, since for
S^{*}
near
K_{S}
the positive terms of
Q(S^{*})
are much larger compared to the negative one.
Coming back to Formula (
20
), to get stability of the RSS it is enough to assume

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial S}-\mu_{x}

\displaystyle<0\quad

\displaystyle\land\quad-\mu_{x}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial S}-a_{S,x}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle>0.

(27)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial S}-\mu_{x}

\displaystyle=r_{S}\Big(1-\frac{2S^{*}}{K_{S}}\Big)-G_{S}(N)f_{S}(x^{*})\frac{1}{(S^{*}+1)^{2}}-\mu_{x},

and note that to have the first inequality in (
27
) satisfied it is enough to assume
r_{S}\leq\mu_{x}
. In the more general case, substituting
G_{S}(N)
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial S}-\mu_{x}

\displaystyle=r_{S}\Big(1-\frac{2S^{*}}{K_{S}}\Big)-\frac{F(S^{*})}{K_{S}(g_{x}+a_{x,T}T^{*}+a_{x,S}S^{*}+\mu_{x})(S^{*}+1)^{2}}-\mu_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-2S^{*})(S^{*}+1)-r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})-\mu_{x}K_{S}(S^{*}+1)}{K_{S}(S^{*}+1)}.

Let us denote the numerator of the expression above as

\displaystyle P(S^{*})

\displaystyle:=r_{S}(K_{S}-2S^{*})(S^{*}+1)-r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})-\mu_{x}K_{S}(S^{*}+1)

\displaystyle=-2r_{S}{S^{*}}^{2}+S^{*}\big(r_{S}(K_{S}-1)-\mu_{x}K_{S}\big)-\mu_{x}K_{S}.

Then

\begin{gathered}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial S}-\mu_{x}<0\iff P(S^{*})<0.\end{gathered}

It is obvious that
P(S^{*})<0
for the values of
S^{*}
exceeding
\frac{K_{S}}{2}
.
Moreover, since

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=-f_{S}^{\prime}(x^{*})G_{S}(N)h_{S}(S^{*})S^{*}=-\frac{a_{S,x}-1}{(x^{*}+1)^{2}}G_{S}(N)\frac{S^{*}}{S^{*}+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{x}r_{S}(1-a_{S,x})S^{*}(K_{S}-S^{*})}{K_{S}(g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*}+\mu_{x})(a_{S,x}(g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*})+\mu_{x})},

then

\displaystyle-\mu_{x}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial S}-a_{S,x}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x}=

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})-r_{S}(K_{S}-2S^{*})(S^{*}+1)}{K_{S}(S^{*}+1)}

\displaystyle+a_{S,x}\frac{\mu_{x}r_{S}(a_{S,x}-1)S^{*}(K_{S}-S^{*})}{K_{S}(g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*}+\mu_{x})(a_{S,x}(g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*})+\mu_{x})}.

After reducing the RHS of the equation above to a common denominator, we can denote its numerator as

\displaystyle B(S^{*}):=

\displaystyle S^{*}(2{S^{*}}+1-K_{S})(g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*}+\mu_{x})\big(a_{S,x}(g_{x}+a_{x,S}S^{*})+\mu_{x}\big)

\displaystyle-a_{S,x}(1-a_{S,x})S^{*}(K_{S}-S^{*})(S^{*}+1).

Thus,

\begin{gathered}-\mu_{x}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial S}-a_{S,x}\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x}>0\iff B(S^{*})>0.\end{gathered}

Note that
B(S^{*})<0
for small values of
S^{*}
.

Corollary 16
.

The RSS of System (
2
) is locally asymptotically stable if
Q(S^{*})<0
,
P(S^{*})<0
, and
B(S^{*})>0.

3.4
Coexistence steady states

Now we will consider a situation in which a certain population of both types of cancer cells survives, i.e. we will analyze the existence and stability of positive steady states
(T^{*},S^{*},x^{*}).
To be consistent with the previous subsections, we will call this state the PSS.

Recall that if
N=0
, then System (
4
) simplifies to (
8
) and the only positive
solution to it is

\big(K_{T},K_{S},x^{*}_{K_{T},K_{S}}\big)=\Big(K_{T},K_{S},\frac{g_{x}+a_{x,T}K_{T}+a_{x,S}K_{S}}{\mu_{x}}\Big),

(28)

which is a stable node.
Since System (
2
) depends continuously on
N
, for the values of
N
sufficiently close to
0
there should also exist a locally asymptotically stable steady state
(T^{*},S^{*},x^{*})
with
T^{*},S^{*}>0
. Moreover, the coordinates
T^{*}
,
S^{*}
remain close to
K_{T}
and
K_{S}
, respectively.

We will now state the conditions necessary for the point
(S^{*},T^{*},x^{*}),

S^{*},T^{*}>0
to be the solution to System (
4
). Adding the first two equations of System (
4
) by sides we get

\begin{gathered}-r_{T}(T^{*})T^{*}+f_{T}(x^{*})G_{T}(N)h_{T}(T^{*})T^{*}=r_{S}(S^{*})S^{*}-f_{S}(x^{*})G_{S}(N)h_{S}(S^{*})S^{*}.\end{gathered}

(29)

In particular, Equation (
29
) holds when both sides are equal to
0
, that is

\displaystyle G_{T}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{T}(K_{T}-T^{*})}{K_{T}f_{T}(x^{*})h_{T}(T^{*})},

\displaystyle G_{S}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})}{K_{S}f_{S}(x^{*})h_{S}(S^{*})}.

(30)

By substituting (
30
) into System (
4
) we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{\alpha}T^{*}S^{*}}{K_{T}K_{S}}(K_{T}-T^{*}),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\frac{r_{\alpha}T^{*}S^{*}}{K_{T}K_{S}}(K_{T}-T^{*}),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=g_{x}+a_{x,T}T^{*}+a_{x,S}S^{*}-\mu_{x}x^{*}.

Since
T^{*},S^{*}>0
, solutions to the system above must have the form

\big(T^{*},S^{*},x^{*}\big)=\Big(K_{T},S^{*},\frac{g_{x}+a_{x,T}K_{T}+a_{x,S}S^{*}}{\mu_{x}}\Big),

(31)

where
S^{*}>0
.
Substituting (
31
) into System (
30
) we get

\displaystyle G_{T}(N)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle G_{S}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})}{K_{S}f_{S}(x^{*})h_{S}(S^{*})}.

(32)

From Corollary
4
we conclude that Condition (
32
) holds if and only if
N=0,

S^{*}=0
, but we have already analyzed this case.

Thus, let us assume that both sides of Equation (
29
) are different from
0
. In this case, both sides need to be of the same sign. Both sides of this equation are positive if and only if

\displaystyle G_{T}(N)

\displaystyle>\frac{r_{T}(K_{T}-T^{*})}{K_{T}f_{T}(x^{*})h_{T}(T^{*})},

\displaystyle G_{S}(N)

\displaystyle<\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})}{K_{S}f_{S}(x^{*})h_{S}(S^{*})},

and both sides are negative if and only if

\displaystyle G_{T}(N)

\displaystyle<\frac{r_{T}(K_{T}-T^{*})}{K_{T}f_{T}(x^{*})h_{T}(T^{*})},

\displaystyle G_{S}(N)

\displaystyle>\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})}{K_{S}f_{S}(x^{*})h_{S}(S^{*})}.

(33)

Note that since
\forall{N>0}\;G_{T}(N)>G_{S}(N),
then Condition (

) does not hold for any

if

\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})}{K_{S}f_{S}(x^{*})h_{S}(S^{*})}\geqslant\frac{r_{T}(K_{T}-T^{*})}{K_{T}f_{T}(x^{*})h_{T}(T^{*})}.

Corollary 17
.

Let
T^{*}\in(0,K_{T}]
,
S^{*}\in(0,K_{T}]
and let Assumptions
1
,
2
and
13
be satisfied. If

,

It is easy to see that if
N
satisfies

\displaystyle G_{T}(N)

\displaystyle>\frac{r_{T}(K_{T}-T^{*})}{K_{T}f_{T}(x^{*})h_{T}(T^{*})},

\displaystyle G_{S}(N)

\displaystyle>\frac{r_{S}(K_{S}-S^{*})}{K_{S}f_{S}(x^{*})h_{S}(S^{*})},

. Moreover, for any given

\displaystyle\frac{r_{T}(g_{x}+a_{x,T}K_{T}+a_{x,S}K_{S}+\mu_{x})(K_{T}+1)}{a_{T,x}g_{x}+\mu_{x}},

implying the following corollary.

Let Assumptions

4
Conclusion

In
[
9
]
Abernathy and Burke extended the GBM immunotherapy model proposed in
[
7
]
and further analyzed in
[
8
]
, to account for the existence of CSCs. In this article, we applied a quasi-stationary approximation to this extended model, which yielded a simplified system with a reduced number of ODEs. We then introduced functions
G_{T}
and
G_{S}
that reflect the influence of immunotherapy on the dynamics of tumor cells and CSCs, respectively. We analyze the properties of these functions and show that they are strictly increasing, unbounded from above, and that
G_{T}
is greater than
G_{S}
for all positive values of
N
(the total number of lymphocytes in the system at any given time), i.e. that the treatment has a stronger impact on tumor cells than CSC.

We then proceeded to analyze existence and local asymptotic stability of two types of steady states – the cure state (when no cancer cells survive the treatment) and the coexistence steady state (when some populations of both types of cancer cells survive) – depending on
N
. In particular, we found sufficient conditions for the local asymptotic stability of the cure state and for the non-existence of the coexistence steady state. Finally, we illustrated our findings with numerical simulations performed for particular values of parameters appearing in the model.

A natural continuation of this work would be to relax the assumption that the therapy is constant in time and instead consider a treatment in the form of periodic impulses.

Ab initio Exact Calculation of Strongly-Correlated Nucleonic Matter
R. Z. Hu, S. L. Jin, X. Zhen, H. Y. Shang, J. C. Pei, F. R. Xu

Abstract
Dense nucleonic matter is of vital importance for understanding compact stars and inferring the transition into deconfined quark phase. We present the ab initio exact calculations of infinite nucleonic matter with the state-of-the-art full configuration-interaction quantum Monte Carlo (FCIQMC) method, enabling us to rigorously benchmark many-body methods and assess the degree to which the nucleonic matter is strongly correlated. This method has been numerically testified to exact diagonalization within a small basis. Calculations of nucleonic matter using the chiral nuclear forces reveal that the symmetric nuclear matter is strikingly strongly correlated than expected, raising questions on previous ab initio calculations of nuclear matter with truncations and offering insights into simultaneous descriptions of finite nuclei and infinite nucleonic matter from first principles.



Introduction.—

Ab initio
nuclear theory has made significant progress
over the past two decades
Epelbaum et al. 2020
;
Hergert 2020
, however, exact many-body calculation of infinite nucleonic matter is still a missing piece.
In the
ab initio
nuclear paradigm, starting from realistic nuclear forces, the full-configuration no-core shell model (NCSM) can be seen as exact diagonalization calculations of finite nuclei with active degrees of freedom of all nucleons
Barrett et al. 2013
.
But NCSM is limited to light nuclei up to
16
O owing to formidable computing costs.
Moreover, it is essential to examine the applicability of
ab initio
calculations to heavy nuclei considering the in-medium effect
Morris et al. 2018
;
Arthuis et al. 2020
;
Gysbers et al. 2019
;
Binder et al. 2014
;
Miyagi et al. 2022
;
Hu et al. 2024
;
Sammarruca and Millerson 2019
,
although realistic nuclear forces fitted to free-space scattering experiments are verified in few-body systems
Entem and Machleidt 2003
;
Roth et al. 2005
.
To this end,
ab initio
calculations have to reproduce the empirical properties of infinite nuclear matter
at the saturation density
Simonis et al. 2017
;
Drischler et al. 2019
;
Jiang et al. 2020
.
However, most
ab initio
nuclear methods fail to describe finite nuclei and nuclear matter simultaneously
Machleidt and Sammarruca 2024
;
Hebeler 2021
.
From this perspective,
ab initio
exact calculations of infinite nuclear matter are imperative.

The accurate predictions of equation of state (EoS) of cold dense nucleonic matter that are not accessible by terrestrial experiments
is vital for understanding compact neutron stars in the multi-message astrophysical era
Özel and Freire 2016
;
Reed et al. 2021
;
Essick et al. 2021
;
Neill et al. 2023
;
Annala et al. 2023
.
It also provides a stepping stone for inferring the phase transition scenario from nuclear matter to quark matter, informing us the possible existence of hybrid stars and non-perturbative properties of strong interactions
Weih et al. 2020
;
Zhang and Li 2023
;
Fujimoto et al. 2023
;
Huang et al. 2022
;
Kedia et al. 2022
. The theoretical challenge is related to the knowledge that nuclear matter are strongly correlated systems, in particular the symmetric nuclear matter (SNM), while the pure neutron matter (PNM) are relatively weakly correlated
Gandolfi et al. 2015
;
Piarulli et al. 2020
;
Lovato et al. 2022
;
Drischler et al. 2020
. This is indicated by previous studies that different
ab initio
methods have obvious discrepancies in SNM using hard nuclear forces
Carbone et al. 2013
;
Rrapaj et al. 2016
;
Hagen et al. 2014
;
Baardsen et al. 2013
;
Marino et al. 2024
;
Zhen et al. 2025
;
Udiani 2025
. Therefore, it will be interesting to see how the truncations applied in
ab initio
calculations of finite nuclei play in infinite nuclear matter.

It is impossible to perform exact diagonalization calculations of infinite nuclear matter by brute force
Hjorth-Jensen et al. 2017a
.
There are many-body methods with truncations on high-order correlations applied
to nuclear matter, including the many-body perturbation theory (MBPT) up to the 4th order
Tichai et al. 2020
;
Drischler et al. 2019
;
Keller et al. 2023
, coupled-cluster (CC) truncated at doublet amplitude level with perturbative triple corrections CCD(T)
Hagen et al. 2014
;
Baardsen et al. 2013
, self-consistent Green’s function (SCGF) truncated
to the 3rd-order propagator
Raimondi and Barbieri 2018
;
Barbieri et al. 2022
;
Marino et al. 2024
, and the in-medium similarity renormalization group (IMSRG) truncated to the normal-ordered two-body level
Udiani 2025
;
Zhen et al. 2025
.
On the other hand, various quantum monte carlo methods, such as auxiliary field diffusion Monte Carlo (AFDMC)
Wlazłowski et al. 2014
;
Lynn et al. 2016
;
Piarulli et al. 2020
and configuration-interaction Monte Carlo (CIMC)
Roggero et al. 2014
;
Arthuis et al. 2023
, can in principle include high-order correlations but suffer the sign problem.
To address the aforementioned difficulties, we hereby introduce the state-of-the-art full configuration-interaction quantum Monte Carlo (FCIQMC) method
for
ab initio
exact calculations of infinite nuclear matter.

FCIQMC was originally developed in quantum chemistry and quickly has been widely used in molecular and condensed matter physics
Booth et al. 2009
;
Shepherd et al. 2012
;
Booth et al. 2013
;
Cleland et al. 2010
. FCIQMC has emerged as one of the most accurate many-body methods, particularly for strongly correlated systems
Schwarz et al. 2015
;
Williams et al. 2020
. Different from post-Hartree-Fock methods, FCIQMC is a projector QMC method that stochastically samples the ground-state wavefunction in the full Hilbert space. This approach also offers distinct advantages over other QMC techniques
Spencer et al. 2019
;
Guther et al. 2020
. Being formulated in a discrete configuration space, it is not restricted to local potentials and can naturally handle nonlocal forces from chiral effective field theory (
\chi
EFT).
Furthermore, FCIQMC employs a delicate walker annihilation algorithm to overcome the fermion sign problem in QMC methods
Booth et al. 2009
;
Booth and Alavi 2010
.
This contrasts with methods like AFDMC or CIMC, which require the fixed-node approximation or guiding wavefunctions, introducing a bias that is difficult to assess or eliminate
Zhang et al. 1997
;
Malone et al. 2020
;
Spencer et al. 2019
;
Guther et al. 2020
. Therefore, as a method that in-principle is exact and possessing well-controlled systematic errors, FCIQMC holds the promise of providing benchmarking and advancing our understandings of dense nuclear matter and nuclear forces.

Methodology.—
FCIQMC solves the imaginary-time Schrödinger equation
Hammond et al. 1994
,

-\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}|{\Psi(\tau)}\rangle=(\hat{H}-E)|{\Psi(\tau)}\rangle,

(1)

where
\hat{H}
is the many-body Hamiltonian. In the long-time limit (
\tau\to\infty
), the wavefunction
|{\Psi(\tau)}\rangle
stochastically projects onto the exact ground state of
\hat{H}
.

The core idea of FCIQMC is to represent the wavefunction as a dynamic population of discrete, signed walkers distributed across the full configuration interaction (FCI) basis of all Slater determinants
Booth et al. 2009
,
|{\Psi}\rangle=\sum_{i}c_{i}|{D_{i}}\rangle
. The coefficient
c_{i}
for each determinant
|{D_{i}}\rangle
is sampled by the number of walkers
N_{i}
residing on it. The evolution of these walker populations is governed by the master equation:

-\dfrac{\mathrm{d}N_{i}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=(H_{ii}-S)N_{i}+\sum_{i\neq j}H_{ij}N_{j},

(2)

where
H_{ij}=\langle{D_{i}}|{\hat{H}|D_{j}}\rangle
are the Hamiltonian matrix elements and
S
is an energy offset known as the “shift”. This master equation is simulated via a stochastic algorithm applied at each time step
\Delta\tau
, which comprises three steps
Booth and Chan 2012
:

1.

Spawning:
Walkers on a determinant
|{D_{j}}\rangle
can “spawn” new walkers onto connected determinants
|{D_{i}}\rangle
(where
H_{ij}\neq 0
). The probability of creating a new walker is proportional to the magnitude of the off-diagonal matrix element
|H_{ij}|
.

2.

Death/Cloning:
The diagonal term,
H_{ii}-S
, governs a local process where walkers on
|{D_{i}}\rangle
are removed (death) or duplicated (cloning), depending on whether the local energy
H_{ii}
is greater or less than the shift
S
.

3.

Annihilation:
Walkers with opposite signs that arrive at the same determinant are removed. This annihilation step is essential for mitigating the fermion sign problem
Blunt et al. 2014
;
Petras et al. 2021
.

The shift
S
is dynamically adjusted to maintain a stable total walker number,
N_{\mathrm{w}}=\sum_{i}|N_{i}|
. Once the simulation reaches equilibrium, i.e.,
S
fluctuates around a stable value, the ground-state energy is calculated using the projected estimator,

E(\tau)=\dfrac{\langle{D_{0}}|\hat{H}|{\Psi(\tau)}\rangle}{\langle{D_{0}}|{\Psi(\tau)}\rangle}=\sum_{i}H_{0i}\dfrac{N_{i}(\tau)}{N_{0}(\tau)},

(3)

where
|{D_{0}}\rangle
is a reference determinant. The computational cost is linearly related to the total walker number.
The ground-state energy and its statistical uncertainty are determined through reblocking analysis
Flyvbjerg and Petersen 1989
;
Spencer
.

To enhance the efficiency and stability for large systems, we employ the initiator approximation
Cleland et al. 2010
. This widely-used adaptation designates determinants with a walker population above a certain threshold as “initiators”. Spawning from non-initiator determinants is then restricted to only those determinants that are already occupied. This curtails the proliferation of noise from sparsely populated regions of the Hilbert space. While the initiator approximation introduces a systematic bias, such a bias is progressively reduced by increasing the total number of walkers, allowing for the extrapolation to exact solutions, the unbiased FCI limit
N_{\mathrm{w}}\to\infty

Ghanem et al. 2021
;
Brand et al. 2022
.

Results.—
To validate our implementation and demonstrate the powerful capability of FCIQMC, we first benchmark it against several other advanced many-body methods using the exactly solvable Richardson pairing model
Richardson 1963
;
Dukelsky et al. 2004
. This model serves as an ideal testing ground for assessing the accuracy of many-body methods across a wide range of interaction strengths, from weak to strong couplings
Rigo et al. 2023
;
Companys Franzke et al. 2024
;
Hjorth-Jensen et al. 2017b
;
Brolli et al. 2025
. The Hamiltonian is given by

\hat{H}=\delta\sum_{p=1}^{p_{\mathrm{max}}}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}(p-1)a_{p\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{p\sigma}-\dfrac{g}{2}\sum_{p,q=1}^{p_{\mathrm{max}}}a_{p\uparrow}^{\dagger}a_{p\downarrow}^{\dagger}a_{q\uparrow}a_{q\downarrow},

(4)

where
\delta
is the single-particle level spacing (set to 1.0) and
g
is the pairing strength. Our benchmark is performed for a system of
A=4
nucleons in
p_{\mathrm{max}}=4
levels (a half-filled case), using a total walker population of
N_{\mathrm{w}}\approx 10^{4}
for the FCIQMC calculations.

Figure
1
presents the correlation energies using different many-body methods and their deviations from the exact solution by direct diagonalzing. As shown, perturbative approaches like MBPT diverge rapidly as the interaction strength
|g|
increases. While non-perturbative methods such as CCD, truncated SCGF using the algebraic diagrammatic construction [ADC(3)-D], and IMSRG(2) offer significant improvements in the weak-coupling regime, they still exhibit substantial deviations in the strong-coupling regime
|g|\gtrsim 1.0
. In contrast, the FCIQMC results are in remarkable agreement with the exact energies across the entire range of
g
, with statistical errors being almost negligible. The benchmarking shows the superior accuracy of FCIQMC, especially in strongly coupled situations where truncated many-body methods break down (see Fig.
1
). Our implementation for the Richardson model is made publicly available for reproducibility and further development
Hu
.

We now turn to much more challenging calculations of the realistic system of infinite nuclear matter. The many-body Hamiltonian,
\hat{H}=\hat{T}+\hat{V}_{\mathrm{NN}}+\hat{V}_{\mathrm{3N}}
, includes the kinetic energy term
\hat{T}
alongside interaction terms corresponding to nucleon-nucleon (NN) and three-nucleon forces (3NF) that are derived from
\chi
EFT.
To compare with other methods, the 3NF is included at the normal-ordered two-body level
Hebeler and Schwenk 2010
;
Hagen et al. 2014
, although FCIQMC can naturally deal with the full 3NF. The many-body basis is constructed by Slater determinants built with single-particle (SP) momentum eigenstates,
\left|\bm{k}_{a}\sigma_{a}\tau_{a}\right\rangle
on the lattice in momentum space. Our calculations are performed in a cubic box of volume
V=L^{3}
with
A
nucleons at a density
\rho=A/V
, employing periodic boundary conditions which discretize the momenta with a spacing of
\Delta k=2\pi/L
. The SP basis is defined by a momentum cutoff
|\bm{k}_{a}|\leq k_{\mathrm{max}}
, which
should be sufficiently large to ensure convergence of solutions. Two distinct families of
\chi
EFT forces at the next-to-next-to-leading-order (N
2
LO) are used: N
2
LO(Hüther)
Hüther et al. 2020
and
\mathrm{\Delta N^{2}LO_{GO}}

Ekström et al. 2018
;
Jiang et al. 2020
, where the later explicitly includes the
\Delta(1232)
isobar degree of freedom.

Before proceeding to large-scale calculations with FCIQMC, we perform a rigorous benchmark for symmetric nuclear matter (SNM) in a small model space where exact results can be obtained via direct diagonalization, compared with MBPT and IMSRG(2) methods.
The results are shown in Fig.
2
.
Across all densities and for both types of nuclear forces, FCIQMC calculations with
10^{5}
walkers reproduce exact energies with extraordinary accuracy,
with deviations being orders of magnitude smaller than other methods. In contrast, both MBPT and IMSRG(2) exhibit significant discrepancies that grow substantially with increasing density and particularly for the harder interactions.
This benchmark not only validates our FCIQMC implementation for infinite nuclear matter but also provides a clear illustration of the limitations of these widely-used truncated schemes for infinite nucleonic matter.

This implies that for accurate descriptions of nuclear matter, a precise many-body solver and the chiral expansion to higher-order are equally important.
Our study confirms that the

We also perform a parallel analysis with the
\Delta
-less N
2
LO interaction, which is able to reproduce experimental binding energies and charge radii of medium-mass nuclei up to
78

Hüther et al. 2020
. Previous MBPT calculations concluded that this interaction fails to describe SNM saturation

. Our FCIQMC calculations, shown in Fig.

Our results show that perturbative methods like MBPT(3) severely overestimate the binding energy of SNM with the
\Delta
-less N
2
LO interaction compared to FCIQMC results. The non-perturbative IMSRG(2), while yielding a fortuitous saturation point near the empirical value, is still in disagreement with the FCIQMC benchmark.
This highlights a critical issue that the many-body uncertainty arising from methodological truncations can be so large, especially for harder interactions, that it completely obscures the true properties of the underlying Hamiltonian. Here the discrepancy between FCIQMC and other methods is significantly larger than that using the
\Delta
-full force, demonstrating that the high-order correlations become increasingly important for hard nuclear forces.
This finding underscores an important message to the field: to reliably assess the quality of a chiral interaction and to make credible progress in solving the long-standing saturation problem, it is necessary to improve the truncation schemes of existing post-Hartree-Fock methods.
In this respect, FCIQMC is useful to disentangle the potential deficiencies of a nuclear Hamiltonian from the many-body approximations.

Summary.—

In this Letter, we introduce the state-of-the-art FCIQMC method for
ab initio
studies of infinite nuclear matter. By stochastically sampling in the complete many-body Hilbert space, FCIQMC can include full many-body correlations, circumventing both the truncation errors of methods like CC and IMSRG.
FCIQMC also provides a solution to the notorious sign problem in QMC methods and avoids the fixed-node biases of other QMC approaches.
This work establishes FCIQMC as a rigorous benchmarking tool for infinite nuclear matter, which is one of the most challenging strongly correlated systems in nature.

Our primary finding is that high-order many-body correlations, largely neglected in other methods, play a crucial role in describing nuclear matter saturation.
For modern N
2
LO chiral interactions, the energy contribution from these correlations is found to be several MeV, a magnitude comparable to the theoretical uncertainty of the chiral expansion itself, indicating that SNM is much more strongly correlated than expected.
The present work also demonstrates a significant advance in resolving the long-standing problem to simultaneously describe the bulk properties of finite nuclei and infinite nuclear matter from a single Hamiltonian with the
\Delta
-full interaction.
The FCIQMC as a robust tool to predict nuclear EoS establishes a reliable link between microscopic nuclear forces and the astrophysical observables of neutron stars.

The capability of FCIQMC to controllably capture correlations to all orders opens new frontiers in
ab initio
nuclear physics. It enables a clear separation of Hamiltonian versus many-body uncertainties, which is crucial for guiding the development of the next generation of high-precision interactions. Furthermore, applying FCIQMC to other frameworks will greatly advance
ab initio
calculations of finite nuclei, nuclear reactions, and electroweak processes, with considerable impacts on our understanding of phenomena across nuclear physics and astrophysics.

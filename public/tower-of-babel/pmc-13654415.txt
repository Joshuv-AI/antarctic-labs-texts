Oncologic Benefits and Cardiovascular Risks of Pembrolizumab
Authors: Chan Soon Park, Yebin Park, Ju Hi Park, Soongu Kwak, Tae-Min Rhee, Heesun Lee, Hyung-Kwan Kim, Yong-Jin Kim, Kyungdo Han, Jun-Bean Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPembrolizumab improves survival in advanced lung adenocarcinoma, but potential cardiovascular toxicity remains.ObjectivesThis study evaluated the oncologic benefits and cardiovascular risks of pembrolizumab in a nationwide lung adenocarcinoma cohort.MethodsWe analyzed 39,192 patients newly diagnosed with lung adenocarcinoma between 2012 and 2019 using the nationwide cancer registry. We compared 573 patients treated with pembrolizumab within 1 year of diagnosis with 38,619 patients who were not treated within 3 years. The outcomes included all-cause mortality and myocardial infarction (MI), ischemic stroke (IS), heart failure (HF), pericarditis/myocarditis, and atrial fibrillation (AF) over a 3-year follow-up.ResultsDuring a median follow-up of 3.0 years (IQR: 1.0-3.0 years), 18,241 deaths, 574 MI, 1,157 IS, 2,777 HF, 852 pericarditis/myocarditis, and 1,601 AF events occurred. Model-based adjusted outcome curves showed lower all-cause mortality among users (adjusted P < 0.001) but higher incidences of IS, HF, and AF (adjusted P = 0.028, <0.001, and 0.048, respectively). Nonsignificant trends toward increased risks of MI and pericarditis/myocarditis were observed. Multivariable survival analyses, including Fine-Gray competing risk models for nonfatal cardiovascular outcomes, showed that pembrolizumab use was associated with lower all-cause mortality (HR: 0.77; 95% CI: 0.69-0.86) but with increased risks of IS (HR: 1.56, 95% CI: 1.10-2.23), HF (HR: 2.72, 95% CI: 2.24-3.30), and AF (HR: 1.47, 95% CI: 1.08-2.01); increases in MI and pericarditis/myocarditis were nonsignificant.ConclusionsPembrolizumab was associated with lower all-cause mortality but higher risks of major cardiovascular events. These findings support vigilant cardio-oncologic monitoring in patients treated with pembrolizumab.

Pembrolizumab is a selective humanized monoclonal antibody against programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), designed to block the interaction between PD-1 on T cells and its ligands.1 By inhibiting the tumor-induced suppression of T-cell activity, an immune evasion mechanism recognized as a hallmark of cancer,1,2 pembrolizumab and other immune checkpoint inhibitors have demonstrated remarkable efficacy in reactivating antitumor immunity. In clinical trials, pembrolizumab has produced significant survival benefits in patients with non–small cell lung cancer 33, 4, 5 and those with other malignancies, such as triple-negative breast cancer, renal cell carcinoma, and melanoma.6, 7, 8 Given these benefits, pembrolizumab has become a cornerstone of contemporary cancer therapy, with expanding indications across tumor types.9,10

Despite its therapeutic success, there is a growing concern that immune activation triggered by pembrolizumab may adversely affect the cardiovascular system. Specifically, pembrolizumab-induced activation of cytotoxic T cells, while effective in tumor eradication, can also provoke unintended inflammatory damage to cardiovascular tissues.11,12 With the rise of the interdisciplinary field of cardio-oncology, there is increasing recognition of cardiovascular complications arising during cancer treatment.13 for example, Chiang et al14 reported an increased risk of heart failure (HF) associated with pembrolizumab therapy in patients with cancer. Furthermore, a recent meta-analysis found that immune checkpoint inhibitors, of which pembrolizumab accounted for approximately one-third of exposures, were associated with elevated risks of myocardial infarction (MI), ischemic stroke (IS), and HF.15 These observations, coupled with the established oncologic efficacy of pembrolizumab, highlight the urgent need to investigate its impact on patient outcomes from the oncologic and cardiologic perspectives.

This study aimed to evaluate the cancer-related benefits and cardiovascular risks associated with pembrolizumab in patients with lung adenocarcinoma. We used a nationwide cohort with comprehensive clinical data to perform a cardio-oncology–focused analysis, examining all-cause mortality and major cardiovascular events in pembrolizumab-treated versus untreated patients.

Methods

Ethical statement and data availability

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Hospital (IRB No. E−2506-084-1649) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2013 amendment). The requirement for informed consent was waived given the retrospective design of the study using deidentified data. The data used in this study are available from the corresponding authors upon reasonable request. This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the RECORD-PE (Reporting of Studies Conducted Using Observational Routinely Collected Health Data for Pharmacoepidemiology) guidelines, and the completed checklist is provided in Supplemental Table 1.

Data source and study population

We used the K-CURE (Korea-Clinical Data Utilization Network for Research Excellence) Library,16 a comprehensive nationwide database managed by the Ministry of Health and Welfare of South Korea. The K-CURE Library integrates datasets from clinical sources, including hospitals and medical institutions as well as public registries, including the Korea Central Cancer Registry, National Health Insurance Service (NHIS), Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service, and Statistics Korea, providing detailed clinical and demographic data for patients with cancer across the entire Korean population. The database includes demographic characteristics, cancer diagnoses, results of national health examinations, treatment records, medical histories, hospital claims data, outpatient prescriptions, and mortality information, including cause and date of death. All cancer diagnoses in the K-CURE database are officially registered under the National Health Insurance Act and validated through the Rare and Intractable Disease Program by qualified clinicians and NHIS officials. Information on cancer diagnosis, stage, and histologic classification was derived from the Korea Central Cancer Registry, while treatment exposure (including pembrolizumab use) and comorbidities were identified based on nationwide claims data from the NHIS. Diagnoses and clinical conditions are recorded using the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM). The K-CURE Library links cancer registry data, national health insurance claims, and death registry data at the individual level. Because it is based on mandatory cancer registration and the Korean national health insurance system, data on cancer diagnosis, treatment claims, and vital status are expected to be highly complete for the study population.

We initially identified 197,152 individuals who were newly diagnosed with lung cancer between 2012 and 2019 (Figure 1). Among these, pembrolizumab users were defined as patients who initiated treatment within 1 year after diagnosis to capture early treatment exposure (n = 1,778), while patients who initiated pembrolizumab after 1 year were excluded to avoid potential time-dependent bias and to ensure a clear comparison with persistent nonusers. The pembrolizumab-naïve group consisted of patients who did not receive pembrolizumab within 3 years of diagnosis (n = 128,460). We then applied the following exclusion criteria to refine the study cohort: age <30 years (n = 1,604); incomplete Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) program summary stage data (n = 10,667); absence of a pathological diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma (n = 62,906); any missing covariate data (n = 1,279); and a history of MI, IS, HF, pericarditis/myocarditis, or atrial fibrillation (AF) before cancer diagnosis (n = 14,590). The extent and patterns of missing covariate data are summarized in Supplemental Table 2; patients excluded because of missing covariate data were compared with those included in the final analytic cohort. Definitions of study outcomes and covariates

The primary outcomes of interest were all-cause mortality, MI, IS, and HF within 3 years after cancer diagnosis. Pericarditis/myocarditis and AF were evaluated as additional exploratory cardiovascular outcomes. The date of lung cancer diagnosis was used as the index date for follow-up. Incident MI, IS, HF, pericarditis/myocarditis, or AF were identified using ICD-10-CM diagnostic codes in the hospital records, with additional operational criteria. Claims-based definitions of major cardiovascular outcomes in the Korean NHIS database, including MI, IS, HF, and AF, have been widely used in cardiovascular research.17 To enhance specificity, we required hospitalization or repeated outpatient claims, with additional imaging claims for ischemic stroke, as detailed in Supplemental Table 3. All deaths were ascertained from the national death registry data and considered as all-cause mortality outcomes. The collected baseline covariates included demographic factors (age and sex), medical history (diagnosis of diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and dyslipidemia), cancer stage, initial cancer treatment, and social history indicators (low-income status and urban vs rural residence). Cancer stage was categorized using the SEER summary stage classification system.18,19 Initial cancer treatment was defined as any cancer-directed treatment (surgery, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy) administered within the first 4 months following diagnosis.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD for normally distributed data or as median (IQR) for skewed distributions. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages. Baseline characteristics were compared between pembrolizumab-user and pembrolizumab-naïve groups using unpaired Student's t-tests for continuous variables and chi-square tests or Fisher exact tests for categorical variables, as appropriate. For the missing data assessment, we summarized the number and proportion of missing values for each covariate and compared baseline characteristics between patients excluded because of missing covariate data and those included in the final analytic cohort. The incidence rates for the outcomes were calculated and expressed as per 1,000 person-years of follow-up, with corresponding 95% confidence intervals. To provide adjusted outcome curves, we generated model-based event-free survival estimates derived from Cox proportional hazards models, adjusting for age, sex, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, dyslipidemia, urban residence, low income, SEER summary stage, and initial cancer treatment. In addition, for nonfatal cardiovascular outcomes (MI, ischemic stroke, HF, pericarditis/myocarditis, and AF), we generated adjusted cumulative incidence function (CIF) curves using the Fine-Gray competing risk regression models, treating death as a competing event. To estimate the association between pembrolizumab use and clinical outcomes, we performed multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analyses and calculated hazard ratios and the corresponding 95% confidence intervals. For nonfatal cardiovascular outcomes, we applied Fine-Gray competing risk regression models, thereby yielding subdistribution HRs for those events. The multivariable models were adjusted for age, sex, baseline comorbidities, socioeconomic status, cancer stage, and initial cancer treatment. To evaluate whether these associations persisted among patients with advanced-stage cancer, we performed sensitivity analyses restricted to those with distant metastases at baseline. We also performed a 1-year landmark analysis including only patients who survived the first year after diagnosis, with outcomes assessed from 1 to 3 years. To further address potential immortal time bias and asymmetric exposure windows, we performed additional time-varying exposure analyses in which pembrolizumab use was treated as a time-dependent variable. In this analysis, patients were considered unexposed before pembrolizumab initiation and exposed thereafter. Thus, unlike the primary analysis, the time-varying analysis allowed patients who initiated pembrolizumab after 1 year to contribute person-time according to their actual treatment timing. Because individual patients could contribute person-time to >1 exposure category, the number of patients contributing person-time in each category does not represent mutually exclusive baseline groups. The proportional hazards assumption was evaluated using Schoenfeld residuals for the Cox proportional hazards models and by including time-by-covariate interaction terms for the Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard models. No significant violation of the proportional hazards assumption was observed. All statistical tests were 2-tailed, and a P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute).

Results

Baseline characteristics of the study population

Figure 1 shows that after the inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied, there were 39,192 patients with lung adenocarcinoma (mean age: 64.3 ± 10.9 years; 54.0% male). Among these patients, 573 were included in the pembrolizumab-user group and 38,619 in the pembrolizumab-naïve group. At baseline, of the 39,192 study patients, 5,499 (14.0%) had diabetes, 15,343 (39.2%) had hypertension, and 9,446 (24.1%) had dyslipidemia. According to the SEER summary staging, 12,204 (31.1%) had localized disease, 9,234 (23.6%) had regional disease, and approximately one-half had distant metastases.

Table 1 summarizes the baseline characteristics stratified by pembrolizumab use (pembrolizumab-naïve vs pembrolizumab-user groups). The average ages of the 2 groups were similar. However, the pembrolizumab-user group had a substantially higher proportion of males (451 of 573 [78.7%] vs 20,701 of 38,619 [53.6%] in the naïve group; P < 0.001) and a greater prevalence of diabetes (104 of 573 [18.2%] vs 5,395 of 38,619 [14.0%]; P = 0.004). Notably, most pembrolizumab users (483 of 573 [84.3%]) presented with distant metastases at diagnosis compared with 17,271 of 38,619 [44.7%] of those who did not receive pembrolizumab. Consistent with their more advanced disease, pembrolizumab users were less likely to undergo surgical resection as an initial treatment (51 of 573 [8.9%] vs 18,543 of 38,619 [48.0%] in nonusers) and were more likely to receive chemotherapy (508 of 573 [88.7%] vs 16,544 of 38,619 [42.8%]) and radiotherapy (162 of 573 [28.3%] vs 6,200 of 38,619 [16.1%]) within the first 4 months of diagnosis (all P < 0.001). Among pembrolizumab users in the overall cohort, the median time from cancer diagnosis to pembrolizumab initiation was 168 days (IQR, 111-241 days). The median follow-up duration for all-cause mortality was significantly shorter in the pembrolizumab-user group (1.2 years [IQR: 0.7-1.9 years]) than in the pembrolizumab-naïve group (3.0 years [IQR: 1.0-3.0 years]; P < 0.001), reflecting the more advanced disease and shorter survival among pembrolizumab recipients. Model-based adjusted event-free survival curves revealed that the pembrolizumab-user group had a significantly lower cumulative incidence of all-cause death (adjusted P < 0.001) but experienced a significantly higher cumulative incidence of IS, HF, and AF (adjusted P = 0.028, <0.001, and 0.048, respectively) (Figure 2). The pembrolizumab-user group also showed trends toward higher cumulative incidences of MI and pericarditis/myocarditis, although these did not reach statistical significance (adjusted P = 0.112 and 0.172, respectively). Table 2 presents Cox model results for all-cause mortality and Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model results for nonfatal cardiovascular outcomes. In univariate analyses, pembrolizumab use was associated with increased risks of all-cause death (HR: 1.81, 95% CI: 1.63-2.01), MI (HR: 2.01, 95% CI: 1.20-3.36), IS (HR: 2.17, 95% CI: 1.53-3.06), HF (HR: 3.72, 95% CI: 3.09-4.48), pericarditis/myocarditis (HR: 2.45, 95% CI: 1.68-3.57), and AF (HR: 1.94, 95% CI: 1.43-2.63). However, after multivariable adjustment for relevant covariates and accounting for competing risks, pembrolizumab use was significantly associated with lower all-cause mortality (HR: 0.77, 95% CI: 0.69-0.86), while increased risks of IS (HR: 1.56, 95% CI: 1.10-2.23), HF (HR: 2.72, 95% CI: 2.24-3.30), and AF (HR: 1.47, 95% CI: 1.08-2.01) remained significant. The increased risks of MI and pericarditis/myocarditis observed in pembrolizumab users were attenuated and were no longer significant after multivariable adjustment (HR: 1.60, 95% CI: 0.95-2.71 and HR: 1.33, 95% CI: 0.91-1.95, respectively).

A 1-year landmark analysis including only patients who survived the first year after diagnosis yielded consistent findings for all-cause mortality, whereas associations for nonfatal cardiovascular outcomes were attenuated and did not reach statistical significance, although a trend toward increased risk was observed for HF (Supplemental Table 4). Additional time-varying exposure analyses showed broadly consistent findings with Table 2 in Supplemental Table 5.

Prognostic impact of pembrolizumab in patients with distant metastases

Given the substantial difference in baseline cancer stage between the 2 groups, specifically the notably higher proportion of distant metastases among pembrolizumab users, we conducted a sensitivity analysis restricted to patients with distant metastases at initial diagnosis. The baseline characteristics of this metastatic subset stratified by pembrolizumab use are shown in Supplemental Table 6. In total, 17,754 of 39,192 patients (45.3%) had metastatic lung adenocarcinoma at diagnosis, of whom 483 of 17,754 (2.7%) received pembrolizumab and 17,271 of 17,754 (97.3%) did not. Among pembrolizumab users with distant metastases, the median time from cancer diagnosis to pembrolizumab initiation was 165 days (IQR: 110-232 days). In this subgroup, the median follow-up duration for survival was similar between the pembrolizumab-naïve and pembrolizumab-user groups (1.1 year [IQR: 0.5-2.6 years] vs 1.1 years [IQR: 0.6-1.9 years], respectively; P = 0.969), consistent with the poor overall prognosis associated with metastatic lung cancer.

In the metastatic disease subset, pembrolizumab use was significantly associated with improved survival outcomes. As shown in Table 3, pembrolizumab users had a significantly lower risk of all-cause mortality compared to nonusers in univariate analysis (HR: 0.84, 95% CI: 0.75-0.94) and multivariate analysis (HR: 0.76, 95% CI: 0.68-0.85). However, cardiovascular risks associated with pembrolizumab treatment were notably higher in this subgroup. Discussion

In this nationwide cohort study, we comprehensively evaluated the associations of pembrolizumab therapy with survival outcomes and cardiovascular risk in patients with lung adenocarcinoma from a cardio-oncology perspective. The key findings are summarized in the Central Illustration. First, pembrolizumab treatment was associated with a lower risk of all-cause mortality in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, consistent with its expected oncologic benefit in a real-world population. Second, after adjustment for covariates and accounting for competing risks, pembrolizumab use was associated with increased risks of adverse cardiovascular events, particularly IS, HF, and AF. The observed increase in MI risk did not reach statistical significance in the overall cohort but became significant in the metastatic subgroup. Third, even when the analysis was confined to patients with distant metastases, those expected to derive the greatest absolute benefit from immunotherapy, the survival advantage associated with pembrolizumab persisted, along with increased risks of cardiovascular events. These findings underscore the dual nature of pembrolizumab therapy and highlight the importance of a balanced risk-benefit assessment in clinical practice.Central IllustrationSchematic Summary of the Cardio-Oncologic Implications of Pembrolizumab in Lung AdenocarcinomaThe illustration highlights the association of pembrolizumab use with lower all-cause mortality and higher risks of selected cardiovascular events, emphasizing the need for integrated cardio-oncologic assessment and monitoring.

Schematic Summary of the Cardio-Oncologic Implications of Pembrolizumab in Lung Adenocarcinoma

The illustration highlights the association of pembrolizumab use with lower all-cause mortality and higher risks of selected cardiovascular events, emphasizing the need for integrated cardio-oncologic assessment and monitoring.

The advent of cancer immunotherapy has revolutionized clinical oncology. Among the immune checkpoint inhibitors, pembrolizumab has demonstrated substantial survival benefits across multiple cancer types.5, 6, 7, 8 Mechanistically, pembrolizumab inhibits the interaction between PD-1 and its ligand PD-L1, a pathway exploited by tumors to evade T-cell immune surveillance. Pembrolizumab reactivates antitumor T-cell responses by blocking this immune checkpoint, significantly improving patient outcomes.1,20 Given its broad efficacy, pembrolizumab has rapidly emerged as one of the most effective therapeutic agents in modern oncology, achieving widespread use across an expanding range of indications.21,22 However, immune checkpoint blockade can trigger immune-related toxicities in nontumor tissues, including the cardiovascular system. Such off-target immune activation beyond the tumor microenvironment may accelerate adverse processes such as myocarditis, atherosclerosis, and myocardial dysfunction,23,24 while effectively controlling cancer.

Initial reports of cardiovascular adverse effects following pembrolizumab treatment were primarily cases of fulminant myocarditis leading to HF in clinical trials and case reports.25, 26, 27 With accumulating evidence, the risk of HF associated with immune checkpoint inhibitors has received increasing attention,28,29 and recent studies have sought to comprehensively evaluate the broader spectrum of cardiovascular risks beyond HF. A retrospective propensity score–matched cohort study found that pembrolizumab use was significantly associated with a higher incidence of HF but did not demonstrate a significant increase in the risk of MI or IS.14 Conversely, other studies, including pharmacovigilance analyses and meta-analyses, have reported that immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy is associated with increased risks of MI, IS, and HF.15,30,31 There is now a general consensus that pembrolizumab and similar agents can precipitate HF in a subset of patients; however, uncertainty remains regarding whether pembrolizumab significantly accelerates atherosclerotic cardiovascular events, given the conflicting evidence from prior studies.

Most previous studies examining cardiovascular toxicities have not simultaneously assessed the anticancer efficacy of pembrolizumab. Our study addresses this gap by concurrently evaluating both sides of the risk-benefit balance. Using a large, unselected nationwide cohort and carefully accounting for baseline prognostic differences, we observed an association between pembrolizumab treatment and lower all-cause mortality, which was accompanied by increased cardiovascular risks. This comprehensive perspective is crucial for clinicians, as it reinforces the need for vigilant cardiovascular monitoring and management without detracting from the potential lifesaving benefits of this drug. Consistent with findings from pivotal clinical trials, we observed that pembrolizumab use was associated with lower all-cause mortality in patients with lung adenocarcinoma.3, 4, 5 In our real-world cohort, pembrolizumab users had an approximately 24% relative reduction in mortality risk after accounting for confounders. Given the high baseline mortality of advanced lung cancer, the introduction of pembrolizumab represents a major therapeutic advancement for this population.32 However, from a cardiovascular standpoint, our findings indicate that the potential risks are significant and should not be overlooked. Pembrolizumab use was associated with a significantly increased risk of serious cardiovascular events, particularly HF and IS, in the overall cohort and the metastatic subset. Notably, in the subgroup of patients with distant metastases, where pembrolizumab treatment was the most common, we observed a significant increase in the risk of MI. For clinicians, these findings underscore the importance of close cardiovascular surveillance in patients receiving pembrolizumab. Specifically, baseline cardiovascular risk factors should be assessed and optimized, and patients should be closely monitored for early signs and symptoms of cardiac or cerebrovascular events throughout the course of therapy and beyond.

Our findings also have implications for potential preventive strategies to mitigate cardiovascular risk in patients with cancer receiving immunotherapy. For patients with a high baseline cardiovascular risk or established atherosclerotic disease, some experts have proposed the use of prophylactic measures such as statin therapy during immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment.30 Statins have pleiotropic anti-inflammatory and plaque-stabilizing effects that might theoretically counteract immunotherapy-related acceleration of atherosclerosis. Although our study was not designed to test such an intervention, the markedly higher incidence of MI and IS in patients with advanced cancer treated with pembrolizumab suggests that this hypothesis warrants further investigation. Any preventive strategy should be individualized and carefully weighed against potential drug interactions and side effects. However, proactive cardio-oncologic care, including early involvement of cardiologists in the management of patients receiving immunotherapy, may help optimize outcomes. Our findings reinforce the importance of integrating oncologic and cardiologic expertise, the cornerstone of cardio-oncology, in the care of patients undergoing treatment with drugs such as pembrolizumab.

Study Limitations

Despite the strengths of this nationwide cohort, several limitations should be noted. First, as this was an observational study, there is the possibility of residual confounding and selection bias. We adjusted for many known confounders; however, unmeasured factors, such as smoking, performance status, tumor burden, molecular characteristics, tumor PD-L1 expression, overall comorbidity burden, calendar year of diagnosis and related treatment-era effects, and alteration of chemotherapy regimen during follow-up, could have influenced the likelihood of receiving pembrolizumab and the outcomes. Furthermore, detailed treatment-related variables, such as pembrolizumab treatment duration, number of cycles, cumulative exposure, and concurrent chemotherapy regimens, were not available in this registry-based dataset, which may have influenced treatment patterns and clinical outcomes. In addition, cardiovascular outcomes were identified using claims-based diagnostic codes and operational definitions; therefore, outcome misclassification remains possible, particularly for outcomes with less robust validation data, such as pericarditis/myocarditis. Delays in claims submission or processing may also have introduced a potential lag in outcome ascertainment. To improve specificity, we required hospitalization or repeated outpatient claims, with imaging claims additionally required for ischemic stroke. Patients selected for pembrolizumab might differ in important characteristics that we could not fully capture, although our sensitivity analysis restricted to patients with metastatic disease helped mitigate baseline prognostic differences. In addition, we cannot exclude the possibility of residual immortal-time bias related to the exposure definition and treatment timing, despite the use of a landmark analysis to mitigate this issue. Second, the reversal of the association between pembrolizumab use and all-cause mortality from the unadjusted to the adjusted model likely reflects substantial confounding by indication, particularly the markedly higher prevalence of distant metastases among pembrolizumab users. Although the sensitivity analysis restricted to patients with distant metastases partially addressed imbalance in cancer stage, residual confounding related to disease severity and treatment selection may remain. Additionally, 1,279 otherwise eligible patients were excluded because of missing covariate data. Although this represented a small proportion of the cohort before complete-case exclusion, formal assessment suggested that the missingness pattern was not strictly compatible with a missing-completely-at-random mechanism. However, given the relatively small proportion of excluded patients, the influence of missing data on the main findings is likely to be limited. Finally, the relatively limited number of events for MI and pericarditis/myocarditis may have reduced the statistical power and precision of the corresponding risk estimates.

Conclusions

In this nationwide cohort of patients with lung adenocarcinoma, pembrolizumab use was associated with lower all-cause mortality but higher risks of HF, IS, and AF, with nonsignificant trends for MI and pericarditis/myocarditis. These findings should be interpreted cautiously in light of the observational study design and potential residual bias. Nevertheless, they support vigilant cardio-oncologic risk assessment and monitoring in patients treated with pembrolizumab.

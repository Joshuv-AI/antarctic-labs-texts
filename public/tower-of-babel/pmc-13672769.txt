Entropy analysis over radiative marangoni convective nanofluid flow on an infinite porous disk
Authors: Ashutosh Pandey, Manoj Kumar Mishra, Lalchand Verma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEntropy refers to the randomness and disorderness of the system, which significantly affects the efficiency and performance of thermal systems. The motive of the research article is to carry out the comprehensive analysis of entropy along with the effect of thermal radiation on the Marangoni convective flow of nanofluid over an infinite porous disk using a passive flow control technique. In this analysis, the unit effect thermal slip condition and Stefan’s blowing have also been considered. Mathematical modeling has been done with the help of partial differential equation (PDE) which has been converted to a non-linear dimensionless ordinary differential equation (ODE) with the help of Karman’s transformations. The analysis of flow variables along with Nusselt number has been carried out with the help of Matlab’s BVP4C package in graphical form. Apart from that, entropy analysis has been carried out for the thermal system. The findings suggest that the Nusselt number increases corresponding to the increasing value of thermal radiation. Also, the entropy of the thermal system increases corresponding to the increasing value of thermal radiation and Brinkman number. This problem has significant potential for real-life applications, viz. material processing and manufacturing, environmental systems, and various industrial processes including cooling and heating operations.Keywords: Nanofluid, Passive flow Control, Porous disk, Thermal radiation, Marangoni convection, Entropy analysis

Introduction

Nanofluids which are created by dispersing nanoparticles in the base fluid like oil water and some polymer solution have opened the world of possibilities in the field of thermal engineering. Nanofluids are initially introduced by Choi and Eastman [1], These fluids have gained significant attention from researchers due to their superior thermal conductivity and enhanced heat transfer capabilities. The selection of specific nanoparticles is governed by their ability to remain stable in suspension and their compatibility with the host fluid. Ensuring that particles do not settle or agglomerate is crucial for maintaining the consistent performance of thermal systems [2]. Several recent research studies have been published over the nanofluid flow [3–5].

Marangoni convection essentially refers to the mass transfer because of surface tension gradient between the interface of two fluids, caused because of temperature and concentration gradient. Because of these phenomenon, there is the layer formation along the edge of interface which influence the mixing of fluid through the boundary. The Marangoni effect has numerous practical applications, including the stabilization of soap films and the formation of Bénard convection cells. A particularly significant application is in the manufacturing of integrated circuits, where it is employed to facilitate the drying of silicon wafers after wet processing. This process is essential to prevent residual liquid from remaining on the wafer surface, which could otherwise lead to oxidation and damage to the electronic components [6]. Muhammad Waqas [7] investigated stagnation point flow analysis of cross nanofluid in frames of hydromagnetics subjected to expanding-contracting cylinder. Later on Muhhammad Waqas [8] goes on elaborating the simultaneous attributes of chemical reaction and dual stratification in magnetized Jeffrey liquid flow past vertical cylinder movement. Midlal et al. [9] examines the flow analysis of hybrid nanofluid past flat surfaces utilizing Marangoni boundary condition. Gui et al. [10] investigated the impact of magnetic field and Marangoni convection against the thin liquid film of Casson fluid, blood-based carbon nanotube nanofluid. Muhammad Waqas [11] scrutinized the thermally radiative magnetized non-Newtonian Casson liquid induced by stretchable cylinder. The analysis is done in the presence of mixed convection, thermophoresis, thermal radiation, Brownian diffusion, Robin conditions and dual stratifications. Galal et al. [12] investigated the thermal radiation effect over the flow of tertiary nanofluid across a disk utilizing the influence local thermal non-equilibrium.

To felicitate the better understanding of transport phenomenon, the role of porous media is being utilized. A porous medium is material embedded with pores, filled with fluid (liquid or gasses). Several real life applications like casting, processing of crude oil based on fluid flow across porous media [13–15]. Using the effect of Arrhenius activation energy, Asma et al. [16] explained convective heat transfer Darcy-Forchheimer 3-d nanofluid flows over the rotating disk. Sharma et al. [17] investigated the flow behavior of casson fluid over non-Darcy Forchheimer porous medium. Muhammad Waqas [18] carried out the numerical investigation on heat transfer of ferromagnetic non-Newtonian fluid subjected to heterogeneous and homogeneous reactions. Several research studies have been carried out over the MHD nanofluid flow [19–21].

Numerous industrial and engineering applications like rotating machinery, computer disk technology, combustion system, heat exchanger etc. Von Karman [22] was the earliest researcher, who explored the nanofluid flow behavior over the spinning disk by introducing the similarity transformation. Shafiq et al. [23] carried out the symmetrical marangoni convection flow analysis of Casson nanofluids over an infinite disk. Mohanty et al [24] investigated thermo-solutal Marangoni convection of bio-hybrid nanofluids over a permeable disk, demonstrating that interfacial nanolayers significantly enhance both thermal and concentration gradients. Das et al. [25] further advanced the investigation of bioconvective Marangoni convection (bio-MC) in Powell–Eyring nanofluids, demonstrating that flow stratification can play a significant role in reducing system irreversibility. However, their analysis neglected the influence of surface roughness, which is an inherent characteristic of practical disk surfaces and can notably affect the flow behavior. Abd-Alla et al. [26] carried out a comparative analysis on Darcy-Dorchheimer flow of 3-D MHD hybrid nanofluid unifying the effect of melting heat and mass transfer over a rotating disk incorporating Dufour and Soret effects. ABD-Alla et al. [27] analyzed the 2-D mixed bio-convective unsteady viscous hydro-magnetic Casson Williamson nanofluid flow model with heat and mass transport incorporating motile microorganisms towards a stretchy spinning disc. Thabet et al. [28] analyzed the flow characteristics Prandtl nanofluid flow characteristic, resulting because of extending surface. Generalized Ohm’s law has been used by them to study heat and mass transfer. Alsallami et al. [29] analyzed the flow behavior of Marangoni Maxwell nanofluid incorporated with the effect of Arrhenius activation energy, they found the improvement in the efficiency and performance corresponding to entropy.

Entropy in Marangoni-driven flows is caused by heat transfer, fluid friction, and, in nanofluids or reactive systems, Brownian motion and chemical reactions. The effects of these mechanisms rely on Marangoni, Prandtl, and Brinkman numbers. By studying local and global entropy formation rates, main causes of irreversibility and conserve energy is being find out. The Bejan number, another important metric without a unit of measurement, distinguishes heat transfer irreversibility from fluid friction irreversibility in the system. Understanding Marangoni flow entropy production helps us understand motion and create energy-efficient thermal systems.

Numerous industrial processes involve the significant transfer of species driven by evaporation process, for example paper-drying process. Mass transfer in wet paper sheets which was effected by temperature and water concentration is the cause of blowing effect. The concept of blowing in Stefan’s problem for the transportation of species is introduced by Nellis et al. [30]. Fang and Jing [31] analytically investigated coupled heat and mass transport in the flow induced by a stretching surface while accounting for the effects of blowing. Latiff et al. [32] examined Stefan blowing in a bioconvective nanofluid over a rotating, stretchable disk.

In this study, thermal radiation is given particular attention due to its critical role in solar energy systems. In high-temperature flow scenarios, the significance of radiative effects becomes even more pronounced [33–35]. Numerous investigations have been conducted to understand how thermal radiation influences boundary layer flow. For instance, Sajid and Hayat [36] investigated viscous fluid flow over an exponentially stretching sheet; their analysis revealed that the boundary layer thickens as the radiation parameter increases. This work was later extended by Nadeem et al. [37] to include Jeffery nanofluid models. More recently, Alamirew et al. [38] analyzed the combined effects of nonlinear thermal radiation, Hall current, and ion slip on the MHD flow of a Williamson nanofluid over a stretching surface.

Previously many researches have been carried out over Marangoni boundary layer flow over different types of disk or stretching sheet with the different physical constraints. But the literature of the analysis of energy optimization over an stationery porous dusk is still limited. This study presents unified mathematical framework which simultaneously addresses thermal radiation and thermodynamic irreversibility within a Marangoni-driven convective nanofluid flow over an infinite porous disk. This research article is extension of Rana and Gupta [39] utilizing the effect of thermal radiation.

The formulation of the problem employs cylindrical coordinates (r,ϕ ,z), where r denotes the radial direction and z represents the vertical axis. The boundary conditions involve the disk surface temperature T and nanoparticle volume fraction, which is represented by C. Description corresponding to Marangoni parameter (Ma)

The variation of transverse velocity H(ξ ) and temperature corresponding to different values of Marangoni parameter (Ma=1,~2,~3,~4) have been shown in Figs. 10 and 11. It is noted form the (10) that, the transversal velocity H(ξ ) and the temperature diminish as the Marangoni parameter Ma increases. Whereas, Fig. 11 shows the variation of temperature corresponding to distinct value of Ma. It is evident from the figure that, as the value of Ma increases temperature of the fluid decreases through out the boundary layer and boundary layer width tend to be increase.

As the values of Ma increases the surface tension gradient becomes stronger, generating a larger tangential stress at the fluid surface. This intensifies the radial motion of the fluid along the disk, causing fluid to be swept away more rapidly from the heated surface. Consequently, the residence time of the fluid near the wall decreases, reducing heat accumulation within the boundary layer. As a result, both the transverse velocity and the temperature profile decrease with increasing values of Ma.

Conclusion

This research article addressed the numerical investigation of the thermal radiation effect over marangoni flow mechanism of nanofluid over an infinite porous surface. Mathematical modeling has been carried out with the help of dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs) which is converted into non- dimensional ordinary differential equations (ODEs) utilizing the Karman’s transformations, which can be solved by Matlab’s bvp4c package. Scope of applicability

This research is applicable to steady, laminar, incompressible, and axisymmetric Marangoni convective flow of nanofluid over an infinite porous disk under boundary-layer assumptions. The influence of thermal radiation, porous medium, thermal slip, Stefan blowing, along with entropy analysis is predicted by employed mathematical model with precision. The numerical solutions obtained using MATLAB’s bvp4c solver are validated through mesh independence and comparison with published results. The findings are relevant to thermal engineering applications such as cooling systems, coating processes, material processing, crystal growth, and solar thermal energy systems. The conclusions are valid within the range of dimensionless physical parameters considered in this investigation.

Limitation of the research

Present analysis is done for the steady, laminar, incompressible, and axisymmetric flow over an infinite porous disk under boundary-layer assumptions. This investigation is based on purely numerical approach using similarity transformations, without experimental validation. The thermophysical properties of the nanofluid are assumed to be constant, except where specified in the model. The results are valid only within the selected range of dimensionless parameters and the associated boundary conditions. Therefore, further validation may required before being extended to complex industrial systems.

Future directions

The analysis of this research article can be extended from the current steady state to transient/unsteady flow condition to observed the time dependent variation. Also this analysis can be extended for distinct types of hybrid as well as tertiary nanofluids.

1.
Introduction and results

For a given external potential
W:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
, the two-dimensional Coulomb gas ensemble
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
(
z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
)
[
34
,
46
]
at inverse temperature
\beta=2
is defined by the probability measure

(1.1)

\frac{1}{Z_{n}(W)}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-nW(z_{j})}\frac{d^{2}z_{j}}{\pi},

where
Z_{n}(W)
is the partition function.
As
n\to\infty
, the empirical measure of
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
weakly converges to the equilibrium measure
\sigma_{W}
, the unique minimiser of the weighted logarithmic energy

(1.2)

I_{W}[\mu]:=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,d\mu(z)\,d\mu(w)+\int_{\mathbb{C}}W(z)\,d\mu(z).

In addition, under standard assumptions from potential theory
[
44
]
, the equilibrium measure
\sigma_{W}
takes the form

(1.3)

d\sigma_{W}(z)=\Delta W(z)\,\mathbbm{1}_{S_{W}}(z)\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi},\qquad\Delta=\partial\bar{\partial},

where the compact support
S_{W}
is called the droplet.

In recent years, there has been growing interest in the asymptotic behaviour of the free energy
\log Z_{n}(W)
as
n\to\infty
. Accumulated knowledge (see
[
18
, Section 5.3]
,
[
46
, Section 9.3]
and introductions of
[
21
,
23
,
4
,
22
]
) now suggests that for an
n
-independent potential
W
, the partition function
Z_{n}(W)
admits an asymptotic expansion of the form

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log Z_{n}(W)&=-I_{W}[\sigma_{W}]n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\log n+\Big(\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-1-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log(\Delta W)\,d\sigma_{W}\Big)n\\
&\quad+\frac{6-\chi}{12}\log n+\chi\,\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}+\mathcal{F}[W]+\mathcal{G}_{n}[W]+\mathcal{H}_{n}[W]+o(1),\end{split}

as
n\to\infty
, where
\chi\equiv\chi(S_{W})
is the Euler characteristic of the droplet
S_{W}
and
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function. (See also
[
28
,
29
,
25
,
2
]
for studies addressing cases with hard edges.)

The leading three terms of the free energy expansion (
1.4
)—of orders
O(n^{2})
,
O(n\log n)
, and
O(n)
—were established in
[
39
]
for general Coulomb gases. These results were subsequently refined in
[
45
,
13
,
9
]
, where quantitative error bounds were obtained.
In contrast to the first three terms, the remaining two terms of order
O(\log n)
and
O(1)
remain open and present significant challenges. A key distinction is that these latter terms depend in a highly non-trivial way on the topological and conformal geometric nature of the equilibrium measure. The
O(\log n)
term was proposed in the early works of Jancovici et al.
[
36
,
47
]
, highlighting its dependence on the topology of the droplet (see also
[
27
]
). The
O(1)
term—particularly the constant
\mathcal{F}[W]
—is believed to involve conformal geometric functionals (the zeta regularised determinant of spectral Laplacian), as predicted by Wiegmann and Zabrodin using conformal field theory
[
49
]
.

Of particular interest in this note are the last two terms,
\mathcal{G}_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, which are typically zero in most of the existing literature. However, these terms in fact encode additional intrinsic information of the equilibrium measure.

•

(Multi-component term
\mathcal{G}_{n}
)

The term
\mathcal{G}_{n}[W]
remains bounded as
n\to\infty
but does
not
converge. This term is believed to arise only when the droplet is
disconnected
. This phenomenon was studied in
[
4
]
(see also
[
28
,
29
,
6
,
5
]
), where the authors established the expansion (
1.4
) for a class of radially symmetric potentials whose droplets form concentric annuli.
However, no concrete example has yet confirmed the presence of the oscillatory term
\mathcal{G}_{n}
in the free energy expansion (
1.4
) for non-radially symmetric potentials (see however
[
3
]
for a discussion of the oscillatory behaviour in the context of fluctuation phenomena in Coulomb gases with multi-component droplets).

•

(Conformal singularity term
\mathcal{H}_{n}
)
In order to discuss the term
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, we define

(1.5)

\varphi(z):=\frac{1}{2}\log\Delta W(z)

so that
e^{2\varphi(z)}
serves as a conformal metric in the expression for the equilibrium measure (
1.7
). In much of the literature on free energy expansions beyond the
O(n)
term, the conformal metric factor
\varphi
is assumed to be “regular”, in the sense that it is bounded above and below within the droplet. This assumption is partially motivated by the fact that if
\varphi
becomes singular at some point, then the associated spectral determinant of the Laplacian also diverges, influencing unbounded terms in the expansion (see Remark
5
). Therefore, it is believed that the contribution
\mathcal{H}_{n}\to\infty
arises in cases where
\varphi
exhibits such singularities inside the droplet.

In contrast to much of the literature on free energy expansions in two dimensions, the multi-component and conformal singularity regimes remain less explored. This note aims to contribute to this direction by considering a class of potentials exhibiting discrete rotational symmetry. In particular, we focus on a well-studied model known as the
lemniscate ensemble
, which enables a detailed analysis of the non-standard terms
\mathcal{G}_{n}
and
\mathcal{H}_{n}
, including explicit formulae.

Remark 3

(Oscillatory term)
.

In Hermitian random matrix theory, it is well known that in the multi-cut regime, where the limiting spectrum consists of multiple disconnected intervals, the oscillatory terms in the free energy expansion can be expressed in terms of the Riemann theta function (see e.g.
[
30
,
31
,
16
]
and references therein). In particular, simplifications such as quasi-periodicity occur when the equilibrium measure distributes equal mass across each component of the support.

A description based on theta functions also arises in the study of non-Hermitian random matrix models under rotational symmetry, see
[
4
,
28
,
29
,
6
,
5
]
.
In our current setting—the lemniscate ensembles—the enhanced symmetries lead to drastic simplifications of the oscillatory terms, reducing them essentially to the elementary form
x(x-1)
, where
x=\{n/d\}
. Nonetheless, one can observe structural similarities with the general theta-function structure in previous works.
More precisely, in the two-dimensional setting, the oscillatory contribution has been computed in the radially symmetric case, where it admits an explicit expression in terms of a theta-like function:

(1.25)

\Theta(x;\rho,a)=x(x-1)\log(\rho)+x\log(a)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\log\Big(1+a\,\rho^{2(j+x)}\Big)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\log\Big(1+a^{-1}\,\rho^{2(j+1-x)}\Big),

see
[
29
, Eq. (3.77)]
. This comparison motivates the conjecture that the lemniscate ensemble corresponds to a degenerate case in which only the leading
x(x-1)
term survives. At present, no general result confirms this degeneracy, and developing a full theoretical understanding remains an open problem for future investigation.

Remark 5

(Divergence of the spectral Laplacian in the conformal singularity)
.

In the conformal singularity regime, the most notable feature is the appearance of an additional
O(\log n)
term in (
1.29
), which lies beyond the scope of the original conjecture by Jancovici et al. We now outline a formal argument indicating how such a term naturally arises.

As mentioned earlier, the
O(1)
-term in the free energy expansion is believed to correspond to the zeta-regularised determinant of the spectral Laplacian
[
49
]
. However, in the presence of conformal singularity—as is the case here—this determinant diverges. More precisely, the
O(1)
-term in
[
49
]
includes a contribution of the form
[
49
, Eqs. (4.8), (4.9), (5.27)]

(1.30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}\int_{S_{V}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi},

where
\varphi
is given by (
1.5
). (See also
[
49
, Eq. (C.7)]
and
[
21
, Eq. (1.31)]
for explicit expressions in the rotationally symmetric setting.)

In our case, the conformal factor
\varphi
satisfies
\nabla\varphi(z)=(d-1)/z
. Since
S_{V}
contains the origin when
t<1/\sqrt{d}
, the integral (
1.30
) diverges due to the singularity at the origin.
Nevertheless, the natural regularisation scale is the exclusion of a neighbourhood of radius
O(n^{-1/(2d)})
around the origin, which corresponds to the microscopic scaling of the potential (
1.6
), see e.g.
[
8
]
. Thus, we obtain the regularised form

\frac{1}{12}\int_{S_{V}\cap\{|z|>cn^{-\frac{1}{2d}}\}}|\nabla\varphi(z)|^{2}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}=\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{12d}\log n+O(1),

which matches the additional
O(\log n)
term appearing in (
1.29
).
We refer to
[
21
, Eq. (1.35)]
for a similar discussion concerning the appearance of such logarithmic terms in the presence of diverging spectral determinant. (We also refer the reader to
[
37
,
38
]
for related models in which the coefficient of the
\log n
term depends on the geometric singularities of the system.)

Remark 6

(Vanishing or diverging densities of the equilibrium measure)
.

Continuing the discussion from the previous remark, observe that the function

(1.31)

d\mapsto\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{12d}=\frac{1}{12}\Big(d+\frac{1}{d}\Big)-\frac{1}{6},

which appears in the
O(\log n)
term of (
1.29
), is invariant under the transformation
d\mapsto 1/d
. This suggests that a vanishing equilibrium density of order
O(|z|^{2d-2})
has the same effect on the
O(\log n)
term in the free energy expansion as a diverging density of order
O(|z|^{2/d-2})
.

Although the class of potentials considered in (
1.6
) does not include cases with diverging equilibrium densities, we may still illustrate this dual behaviour through a simple yet instructive example of the potential:

(1.32)

\widetilde{V}_{d}(z):=|z|^{2/d},\qquad d\in\mathbb{N}.

By using (
2.1
), (
1.23
) and the multiplication formula of the gamma function
[
43
, Eq. (5.5.6)]
, we have

(1.33)

Z_{n}(\widetilde{V}_{d})=n!\frac{d^{dn(n+1)/2+n/2}}{n^{dn(n+1)/2}}(2\pi)^{n(1-d)/2}\prod_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\frac{G(n+1+\frac{\ell}{d})}{G(1+\frac{\ell}{d})}.

Then it follows from (
2.17
) that

(1.34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log Z_{n}(\widetilde{V}_{d})&=\Big(\frac{d\log d}{2}-\frac{3d}{4}\Big)n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n\log n+\Big(\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-1+\frac{d+1}{2}\log d-\frac{d-1}{2}\Big)n\\
&\quad+\Big(\frac{5}{12}+\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{12d}\Big)\log n+d\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+\frac{d+1}{4}\log(2\pi)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{d-1}\log G(1+\tfrac{\ell}{d})+O(\frac{1}{n}).\end{split}

This computation again reveals the same coefficient in the
O(\log n)
term, thereby reinforcing the observed duality between vanishing and diverging densities in the conformal setting.

term in the free energy expansion is conjectured to be

(1.35)

\frac{6-\chi}{12}+\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{12d},

where
\chi
denotes the Euler characteristic of the droplet.
In the regular case
d=1
, this reduces to the original conjecture of Jancovici et al.

We conclude this section by discussing the special case
d=1
and its connection to non-Hermitian random matrix theory.
In fact, the lemniscate ensemble in the case
d=1
, where no additional discrete rotational symmetry is imposed, corresponds—up to a trivial translation—to the classical non-Hermitian random matrix ensemble known as the complex Ginibre ensemble, see
[
18
]
for a recent review. This ensemble consists of
n\times n
random matrices with i.i.d. complex Gaussian entries of mean zero and variance
1/n
. The joint distribution of their eigenvalues is given by the Coulomb gas form (
1.1
) with the Gaussian potential
V^{\mathrm{g}}(z):=|z|^{2}
. Observe that
V_{1,t}(z)=V^{\mathrm{g}}(t-z)
. In this case, it is well known that the partition function admits an explicit evaluation

(1.36)

Z_{N}^{\mathrm{Gin}}:=Z_{N}(V^{\rm g})=N^{-\frac{N(N+1)}{2}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}k!=N^{-\frac{N(N+1)}{2}}\,G(N+2).

In the special case
d=1
, the partition function
Z_{n}(V_{d,t}^{(c)})
is equivalent–up to a normalisation–to the averaged moment of the characteristic polynomial of the complex Ginibre ensemble.
We now present the result for
d=1
from the perspective of non-Hermitian random matrix theory.

Theorem 1.3

(
Moments of characteristic polynomials for the complex Ginibre matrix
)
.

Fix
\gamma>-2
, and let
a\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
G_{N}
denote the complex Ginibre matrix.

(i)

Let
|a|>1
be fixed. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(1.37)

\log\mathbb{E}\Big(|\det(G_{N}-a)|^{\gamma}\Big)=(\gamma\log|a|)\,N-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\log\Big(\frac{|a|^{2}-1}{|a|^{2}}\Big)+O(\frac{1}{N}).

(ii)

Let
|a|<1
be fixed. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

(1.38)

\log\mathbb{E}\Big(|\det(G_{N}-a)|^{\gamma}\Big)=\frac{\gamma}{2}(|a|^{2}-1)N+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{8}\log N+\frac{\gamma}{4}\log(2\pi)-\log G(1+\tfrac{\gamma}{2})+O(\frac{1}{N}),

where
G
is the Barnes
G
-function.
Furthermore, the
O(N^{-1})
-term in (
1.38
) is given by
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}_{m}N^{-m}
, where

(1.39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{m}&:=(-1)^{m+1}\Big(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4m(m+1)(m+2)}-\frac{1}{12m}\Big)\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big)^{m}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{m}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor}\frac{B_{2k+2}}{4k(k+1)(2k-1)!}\frac{(m-1)!}{(m-2k)!}\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big)^{m-2k}\bigg).\end{split}

Here, the second line in (
1.39
) is understood to be zero for
m=1,2
, and
B_{k}
is the
k
-th Bernoulli number defined in terms of the generating function as

(1.40)

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

The first few terms of (
1.39
) are given as follows.

(1.41)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}=\frac{\gamma(\gamma^{2}-2)}{48},

\displaystyle\hskip-60.00009pt\mathcal{C}_{2}=-\frac{\gamma^{2}(\gamma^{2}-4)}{384},

(1.42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}=\frac{\gamma(24-20\gamma^{2}+3\gamma^{4})}{5760},

\displaystyle\hskip-60.00009pt\mathcal{C}_{4}=-\frac{\gamma^{2}(24-10\gamma^{2}+\gamma^{4})}{7680}.

The asymptotic formula (
1.38
) in the regime
|a|<1
was previously obtained by Webb and Wong in
[
48
, Theorem 1.1]
, with an error term of order
o(1)
. In this respect, our expansion (
1.38
) provides a refinement of
[
48
, Theorem 1.1]
by supplying the full asymptotic expansion in powers of
1/N
, see also Remark
8
.
Beyond this regime, the moments of characteristic polynomials for complex Ginibre matrices have been investigated in various settings: in
[
33
]
the edge regime
|a|\approx 1
was studied under fixed exponent; whereas in
[
23
]
, the focus lies on the exponentially varying regime, where the exponent scales as
O(N)
. Moreover, recent extensions of such results have appeared in the context of induced spherical ensembles
[
19
,
20
]
and truncated unitary ensembles
[
32
]
.

In addition, we note that since Theorem
1.3
holds for general real-valued
\gamma>-2
, it directly implies a central limit theorem for the properly normalised logarithm of characteristic polynomials, cf.
[
48
, Corollary 1.2]
and
[
32
, Corollary 1.6]
.

Remark 7

(Duality for the even integer powers)
.

For
\gamma=2k
with
k
an integer, Theorem
1.3
(i) can be derived via a duality (see
[
35
]
for a recent review). More precisely, in this case, the left-hand side of (
1.37
) admits a representation as a
k
-fold real integral, to which one can apply the standard steepest descent method to extract the asymptotic behaviour, see
[
33
, (A.7)]
.

Remark 8

(Unified asymptotic formula in terms of the Barnes
G
-function)
.

In fact, during the proof of (
1.38
), we establish the asymptotic expansion

(1.43)

\log\mathbb{E}\Big(|\det(G_{N}-a)|^{\gamma}\Big)=\log\bigg(\frac{1}{N^{\gamma N/2}}\frac{G(N+1+\frac{\gamma}{2})}{G(N+1)G(1+\frac{\gamma}{2})}\bigg)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|a|^{2}N+O(\frac{1}{N^{\infty}}),

as
N\to\infty
, where
O(1/N^{\infty})
denotes an error term of order
O(1/N^{k})
for any positive integer
k
.
From (
1.43
) and the asymptotic formula (
2.17
), the explicit full asymptotic expansion follows.
Moreover, the asymptotic form (
1.43
) is believed to remain valid in a broader setting, for instance when
c
depends on
N
, see
[
23
, Remark 2.8]
for a related discussion.

Strategy of the proofs

For the reader’s convenience, we outline the overall strategy of the proofs. The first elementary yet crucial idea in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is to eliminate the discrete rotational symmetry, thereby reducing the partition function
Z_{n}(V_{d,t}^{(c)})
essentially to the case
d=1
. More precisely, in Lemma
2.1
, for
m\in\{0,1,\dots,d-1\}
, we establish the decomposition

\log Z_{dN+m}(V_{d,t}^{(c)})=\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{3}.

Here, the first term
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is a normalisation constant, expressed in terms of the partition function of the Ginibre ensemble. The second term
\mathcal{A}_{2}
represents the main contribution, corresponding to the sum of moments of characteristic polynomials of the Ginibre ensemble. The third term
\mathcal{A}_{3}
consists of the remaining oscillatory contributions, which can be expressed in terms of the orthogonal norms associated with the highest-degree polynomials.

The remaining steps involve analysing the asymptotic behaviour of each term
\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{3}
. The first term
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is the most elementary and follows from the well-known asymptotic behaviour of the Barnes
G
-function, see Lemma
2.2
. The third term
\mathcal{A}_{3}
is derived from the asymptotics of the orthogonal norms, based on recent developments in the associated Riemann–Hilbert analysis
[
40
,
26
]
, see Lemma
2.4
. The main term,
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, is treated using Theorem
1.3
. To prove this theorem, we employ the idea of deforming partition functions developed in recent work
[
23
]
. The key idea is to use the rotationally symmetric cases
a=0
and
a=\infty
, for which explicit closed-form expressions are available, as reference ensembles. By comparing with these, we analyse the deformation factor via the fine asymptotic behaviour of the orthogonal polynomials near infinity.

We emphasise that a notable feature in the asymptotic analysis is the presence of oscillatory terms. In Theorem
1.1
, we observe that the final formula includes an oscillatory contribution in the case
t>t_{c}
, while no such term appears when
t<t_{c}
. In fact, each of the terms
\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{A}_{3}
contains oscillatory components, which in our setting depend on
m

n
modulo
d
. Remarkably, an interesting computation reveals that for
t<t_{c}
, all such oscillatory terms cancel out. In contrast, for

2.
Proofs of main results

In this section, we prove the results in the previous section. We begin with Lemma
2.1
, which introduces a multi-fold transform that relates the general case
d\in\mathbb{N}
to the simpler case
d=1
. Next, we establish Theorem
1.3
by building on recent developments in the theory of associated planar orthogonal polynomials

,

1.1
then follows by combining Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
1.3
along with additional analysis on the squared orthogonal norms. Finally, we prove Corollary
1.2
by verifying that the coefficients in Theorem
1.1
match the conjectural form given in the expansion (

We begin by recalling some well-known facts. Due to the special structure at
\beta=2
, where the pairwise interaction in (
1.1
) yields the squared Vandermonde determinant, the partition function
Z_{n}(W)
admits a determinantal representation:

(2.1)

Z_{n}(W)=n!\,\det\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{P}_{j}(z)\overline{\mathcal{P}_{k}(z)}\,e^{-nW(z)}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}\bigg)_{j,k=0}^{n-1},

where
\mathcal{P}_{j}
denotes a monic polynomial of degree
j
. In particular, if the
\mathcal{P}_{j}
are chosen to be orthogonal with respect to the weight
e^{-nW(z)}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}
, then the partition function reduces to a product of squared orthogonal norms.

Recall that
V_{d,t}^{(c)}
is given by (
1.9
).
Let
\mathsf{P}_{k}\equiv\mathsf{P}_{k,d}^{(c)}
be the
monic
orthogonal polynomial with respect to

, i.e.

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{P}_{j}(z)\overline{\mathsf{P}_{k}(z)}|z|^{2c}e^{-n|z^{d}-t|^{2}+nt^{2}}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}=\mathsf{h}_{j,d}^{(c)}(t)\,\delta_{j,k},

where
\mathsf{h}_{j,d}^{(c)}(t)
is the squared norm.
It is also convenient to consider the monic planar orthogonal polynomial
P_{j}\equiv P_{j}^{(c)}(z;a)
satisfying the orthogonality

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}P_{j}(z)\overline{P_{k}(z)}|z-a|^{2c}\,e^{-N|z|^{2}}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}=h_{j}^{(c)}(a)\,\delta_{j,k}.

The following lemma establishes a connection between the partition function
Z_{n}(V_{d,t}^{(c)})
and the moments of the characteristic polynomials of the Ginibre matrix. It provides an extension of
[

, Lemma 3.6]
, which treats the special case

and

. The underlying idea—based on a multi-fold transform—can be traced back to earlier works
[

Lemma 2.1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
m\in\{0,1,\dots,d-1\}
. Define

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\log\Big(e^{(dN+m)^{2}t^{2}}\,c_{N,d}(m)\,(Z_{N}^{\mathrm{Gin}})^{d}\Big),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\log\mathbb{E}\Big(|\det(G_{N}-a)|^{\gamma_{\ell,c}}\Big),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\log h_{N}^{(\gamma_{\ell/2})}(a),

where

(2.7)

c_{N,d}(m)=\frac{(dN+m)!}{(N!)^{d}}\frac{1}{d^{dN+m}}\Big(\frac{N}{dN+m}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}N^{2}+\frac{1+2c+2m}{2}N+\frac{m(1+2c+m)}{2d}},

Z_{N}^{\mathrm{Gin}}
is given by (
1.36
),
G_{N}
is the
N\times N
complex Ginibre matrix and

(2.8)

a=\sqrt{\frac{Nd+m}{N}}\,t,\qquad\gamma_{\ell,c}=-2\Big(1-\frac{\ell+1+c}{d}\Big).

Then with
n=dN+m
, we have

(2.9)

\log Z_{n}(V_{d,t}^{(c)})=\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{3}.

Proof.

Recall that
\mathsf{h}_{j,d}^{(c)}(t)
and

are given by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).
We claim that for
\ell=0,1,\dots,d-1
,

(2.10)

\mathsf{h}_{dj+\ell,d}^{(c)}(t)=\frac{e^{(dN+m)t^{2}}}{d}\Big(\frac{N}{dN+m}\Big)^{\frac{c+\ell+1}{d}+j}h_{j}^{(\gamma_{\ell,c}/2)}(a).

We follow the idea in
[
15
, Section 3]
. Due to the discrete rotational symmetry, we have

\mathsf{P}_{dj+\ell}(z)=z^{\ell}\,p_{j}(z^{d})

for some monic polynomial

.
Then by the change of variables,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mathsf{P}_{dj+\ell}(z)\overline{\mathsf{P}_{dk+\ell}(z)}|z|^{2c}e^{-n|z^{d}-t|^{2}+nt^{2}}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}

\displaystyle=d\int_{0<\arg z<\frac{2\pi}{d}}p_{j}(z^{d})\overline{p_{k}(z^{d})}|z|^{2c+2\ell}e^{-n|z^{d}-t|^{2}+nt^{2}}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{nt^{2}}}{d}\int_{\mathbb{C}}p_{j}(w)\overline{p_{k}(w)}|w|^{\gamma_{\ell,c}}e^{-n|w-t|^{2}}\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi}.

Note here that with
n=dN+m
,

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\mathbb{C}}p_{j}(w)\overline{p_{k}(w)}|w|^{\gamma_{\ell,c}}e^{-n|w-t|^{2}}\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}p_{j}(t-w)\overline{p_{k}(t-w)}|w-t|^{\gamma_{\ell,c}}e^{-n|w|^{2}}\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{N}{dN+m}\Big)^{\frac{c+\ell+1}{d}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}p_{j}\Big(t-\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{dN+m}}w\Big)\overline{p_{k}\Big(t-\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{dN+m}}w\Big)}\,|w-a|^{\gamma_{\ell,c}}e^{-N|w|^{2}}\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi}.

Therefore, it follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that

(2.11)

p_{j}\Big(t-\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{dN+m}}z\Big)=\Big(-\tfrac{N}{dN+m}\Big)^{j/2}P_{j}^{(\gamma_{\ell,c}/2)}(z).

By using this, we obtain

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\mathsf{P}_{dj+\ell}(z)|^{2}\,|z|^{2c}e^{-n|z^{d}-t|^{d}+nt^{d}}\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}\\
&=\frac{e^{(dN+m)t^{2}}}{d}\Big(\frac{N}{dN+m}\Big)^{\frac{c+\ell+1}{d}+j}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|P_{j}^{(\gamma_{\ell,c}/2)}(w)|^{2}|w-a|^{\gamma_{\ell,c}}e^{-N|w|^{2}}\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi},\end{split}

which leads to (
2.10
).

Note that by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we have

(2.13)

Z_{dN+m}(V_{d,t}^{(c)})=(dN+m)!\prod_{j=0}^{dN+m-1}\mathsf{h}_{j,d}^{(c)}(t).

On the other hand, by letting

Q(z):=|z|^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{\ell,c}}{n}\log|z-a|,

we have

(2.14)

\mathbb{E}\Big(|\det(G_{N}-a)|^{\gamma_{\ell,c}}\Big)=\frac{Z_{N}(Q)}{Z_{N}^{\rm Gin}}=\frac{N!}{Z_{N}^{\rm Gin}}\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}h_{N}^{(\gamma_{\ell,c}/2)}(a).

Combining these with (

), we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{dN+m}(V_{d,t}^{(c)})

\displaystyle=\frac{(dN+m)!}{(N!)^{d}}\bigg(\prod_{\ell=0}^{d-1}N!\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathsf{h}_{dj+\ell,d}^{(c)}(t)\bigg)\prod_{j=dN}^{dN+m-1}\mathsf{h}_{j,d}^{(c)}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{(dN+m)^{2}t^{2}}\,c_{N,d}(m)\bigg(\prod_{\ell=0}^{d-1}N!\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}h_{j}^{(\gamma_{\ell,c}/2)}(a)\bigg)\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}h_{N}^{(\gamma_{\ell,c}/2)}(a)

\displaystyle=e^{(dN+m)^{2}t^{2}}\,c_{N,d}(m)\,(Z_{N}^{\mathrm{Gin}})^{d}\prod_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\mathbb{E}\Big(|\det(G_{N}-a)|^{\gamma_{\ell,c}}\Big)\prod_{\ell=0}^{m-1}h_{N}^{(\gamma_{\ell/2})}(a),

O(N^{-1})
is given by

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}\Big(\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4k(k+1)(k+2)}-\frac{1}{12k}\Big)\Big(\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{N^{k}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2k+2}}{4k(k+1)}\bigg(\frac{1}{(N+\frac{\gamma}{2})^{2k}}-\frac{1}{N^{2k}}\bigg).

Then by straightforward computations, we obtain (
1.39
).
∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.3
, we obtain asymptotic behaviour of
\mathcal{A}_{2}
in (
2.5
).

Lemma 2.3
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
m\in\{0,1,\dots,d-1\}
. Let
n=dN+m
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
be given by (

). Then as

Let

(2.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{2}&=\frac{(1+2c-d)\log(\sqrt{d}t)}{d}n+\Big(\frac{c(d-c-1)}{d}-\frac{(d-1)(2d-1)}{6d}\Big)\log\Big(\frac{dt^{2}-1}{dt^{2}}\Big)\\
&\quad+m\frac{1+2c-d}{2d}\Big(1-2\log(\sqrt{d}t)\Big)+O(\frac{1}{n}).\end{split}

(ii)

Let
t<t_{c}
. Then we have

(2.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{2}&=\frac{1+2c-d}{2d}(dt^{2}-1)n+\Big(\frac{(d-1)(2d-1)}{12d}-\frac{c(d-c-1)}{2d}\Big)\log\Big(\frac{n}{d}\Big)\\
&\quad+\frac{1+2c-d}{4}\log(2\pi)-\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-1}\log G\Big(\frac{\ell+1+c}{d}\Big)+m\frac{1+2c-d}{2d}+O(\frac{1}{n}).\end{split}

Proof.

The lemma follows from straightforward computations using Theorem
1.3
, (
2.5
) and (
2.8
).
∎

.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

By Theorem
1.1
, it remains to verify that for the equilibrium measure
\sigma_{V_{d,t}}
defined in (
1.7
), the constants
C_{1}
and
C_{3}
in Theorem
1.1
satisfy the following identities:

(2.34)

C_{1}=-I_{V_{d,t}}[\sigma_{V_{d,t}}],\qquad C_{3}|_{c=0}=\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-1-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{V_{d,t}}}\log(\Delta V_{d,t}(z))\Delta V_{d,t}(z)\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}.

We first consider a general potential
V(z)
having discrete rotational symmetry
V(z)=V(e^{2\pi i/d}z)
.
Let
Q
be a potential such that

(2.35)

V(z)=\frac{1}{d}Q(z^{d}).

Then it follows from
[
11
, Lemma 1]
that

(2.36)

S_{V}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:z^{d}\in S_{Q}\}.

Due to standard potential theory
[
44
]
, the equilibrium measure
\sigma_{W}
is characterised by the variational conditions

(2.37)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}d\sigma_{W}(w)+\frac{1}{2}W(z)\begin{cases}=F_{W}&z\in S_{W},\\
\geq F_{W}&z\in\mathbb{C}.\end{cases}

Then by definition (
1.2
), the energy
I_{W}[\sigma_{W}]
can be written as

(2.38)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{W}[\sigma_{W}]=F_{W}+\frac{1}{2}\int W(z)\,d\sigma_{W}(z).\end{split}

Combining
F_{V}=F_{Q}/d
(see
[
11
, p.409]
) and the change of variables

\displaystyle\int_{S_{V}}V(z)\Delta V(z)\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}=\frac{1}{d}\int_{S_{Q}}Q(z)\Delta Q(z)\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi},

we have

(2.39)

I_{V}[\sigma_{V}]=\frac{1}{d}I_{Q}[\sigma_{Q}].

Note that for the potential (
1.6
), we have

(2.40)

V_{d,t}(z)=\frac{1}{d}Q_{t}(z^{d}),\qquad Q_{t}(z):=d|z-t|^{2}-dt^{2}.

Since the energy associated with the quadratic potential
|z|^{2}
is
3/4
(see e.g.
[
21
, Eq. (1.13)]
), after trivial translation and dilation, one can see that

I_{Q_{t}}[\sigma_{Q_{t}}]=\frac{3}{4}-dt^{2}+\frac{\log d}{2}.

Then by (
2.39
), we obtain the first identity in (
2.34
).

Next, we compute
C_{3}
. By the change of variables
z=w^{d}
, the entropy can be computed as

(2.41)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{S_{V_{d,t}}}\log(\Delta V_{d,t}(z))\Delta V_{d,t}(z)\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}=\int_{S_{V_{d,t}}}\log(d^{2}|z|^{2d-2})(d^{2}|z|^{2d-2})\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}\\
&=d\int_{|w-t|<1/\sqrt{d}}\log(d^{2}|w|^{(2d-2)/d})\,\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi}=2\log(d)+2(d-1)\int_{|w-t|<1/\sqrt{d}}\log|w|\,\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi}.\end{split}

Note that by Jensen’s formula, for
R>0
and
p\in\mathbb{C}
we have

(2.42)

\int_{|w-p|<R}\log|z-w|\,\frac{d^{2}w}{\pi}=\begin{cases}R^{2}\log|z-p|&\text{if }|z-p|>R,\\
R^{2}\log R-\dfrac{R^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{|z-p|^{2}}{2}&\textup{if }|z-p|<R,\end{cases}

see e.g.
[
17
, Lemma 2.6]
.
By combining (
2.41
) and (
2.42
), we obtain

(2.43)

\int_{S_{V_{d,t}}}\log(\Delta V_{d,t}(z))\Delta V_{d,t}(z)\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle 2\log(d)+\frac{2(d-1)}{d}\log(t),&\textup{if }t>t_{c},\\
\displaystyle\frac{d-1}{d}(dt^{2}-1)+\frac{1+d}{d}\log(d)&\textup{if }t<t_{c}.\end{cases}

This gives rise to the second identity in (
2.34
).
∎

Appendix A
Asymptotic of the orthogonal norm

In this appendix, we provide a detailed derivation of (
2.33
).
Recall that
P_{n}\equiv P_{n}^{(c)}
denotes the orthogonal polynomial and
h_{n}\equiv h_{n}^{(c)}

its squared norm, as defined in (
2.3
).
For the latter purpose, we introduce a subscript
N
(i.e.
P_{n,N}
and
h_{n,N}
) to indicate the dependence on the scaling factor
N
appearing in the weight function
e^{-N|z|^{2}}
in (
2.3
).
This dependence amounts to a simple change of scaling, as expressed by the relation

(A.1)

P_{n,N}(z;a)=\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}}P_{n,n}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{n}}z,\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{n}}a\Big),

see e.g.
[
26
, Eq. (3.37)]
.

In
[
10
]
, the planar orthogonality (
2.33
) is reformulated as a contour orthogonality, from which the associated Riemann–Hilbert problem can be constructed.
In particular, by
[
10
, Proposition 7.1]
, we have

(A.2)

h_{n}^{(c)}=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(c+n+1)}{2iN^{c+n+1}}\frac{\widetilde{h}_{n}^{(c)}}{P_{n+1,N}^{(c)}(0)},\qquad\widetilde{h}_{n}^{(c)}\equiv\widetilde{h}_{n,N}^{(c)}(a):=\int_{\Gamma}P_{n,N}(z)^{2}w_{n,N}(z)\,dz,

where
\Gamma
is a simple closed curve that encloses the line segment
[0,a]
and

(A.3)

w_{n,N}(z):=\Big(\frac{z-a}{z}\Big)^{c}\frac{e^{-Naz}}{z^{n}}.

By (
A.1
), we have

(A.4)

\widetilde{h}_{n,N}(a)=\Big(\frac{n}{N}\Big)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\widetilde{h}_{n,n}\Big(\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{n}}a\Big),

see
[
26
, Eq. (3.41)]
.

On the other hand, by the second asymptotic behaviour in
[
40
, Theorem 3]
, we have

(A.5)

P_{N,N}^{(c)}(0)=\frac{a^{N}(1-a^{2})^{c-1}}{N^{1-c}\Gamma(c)}e^{-Na^{2}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Big).

Combining this with (
A.1
), we have

(A.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{N+1,N}^{(c)}(0)&=\Big(\frac{N+1}{N}\Big)^{\frac{N+1}{2}}P_{N+1,N+1}\Big(0,\sqrt{\tfrac{N}{N+1}}a\Big)=\frac{a^{N+1}(1-a^{2})^{c-1}}{N^{1-c}\Gamma(c)}e^{-Na^{2}}\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{N})\Big).\end{split}

Now, we define the matrix function
Y(z)
by

(A.7)

Y(z):=\begin{bmatrix}P_{n}(z)&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{P_{n}(s)w_{n,N}(s)}{s-z}\,ds\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
Q_{n-1}(z)&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{Q_{n-1}(s)w_{n,N}(s)}{s-z}\,ds\end{bmatrix},

where
Q_{n-1}
is the unique polynomial of degree
n-1
satisfying

(A.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{Q_{n-1}(s)w_{n,N}(s)}{s-z}\,ds=\frac{1}{z^{n}}\cdot\Big(1+O(\frac{1}{z})\Big).

Then we have

(A.9)

\widetilde{h}_{N}^{(c)}=-2\pi i\lim_{z\to\infty}z^{N+1}[Y(z)]_{12},

see e.g.
[
26
, Eq. (3.45)]
.
Furthermore, by solving the associated Riemann-Hilbert problem, it was shown in
[
40
, Section 6]
that for
z
outside the Szegő curve,

(A.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Y(z)=e^{\frac{Nl}{2}\sigma_{3}}\Big(I+O(\frac{1}{N^{\infty}})\Big)\,R(z)\Phi(z)e^{-\frac{Nl}{2}\sigma_{3}}z^{N\sigma_{3}},\end{split}

where
\sigma_{3}
is the third Pauli matrix,
l=\log a-a^{2}
, and

(A.11)

R(z)=I+\frac{a(1-a^{2})^{c-1}}{N^{1-c}\Gamma(c)}\frac{1}{z-a}\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\Phi(z)=\begin{bmatrix}\big(\frac{z}{z-a}\big)^{c}&0\\
0&\big(\frac{z-a}{z}\big)^{c}\end{bmatrix}.

Then by (
A.9
) and (
A.10
), we have

(A.12)

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}_{N}^{(c)}=-2\pi i\,a^{N+1}e^{-Na^{2}}\Big(\frac{(1-a^{2})^{c-1}}{\Gamma(c)}\frac{1}{N^{1-c}}+O(\frac{1}{N})\Big).

Now by combining (
A.2
), (
A.6
) and (
A.12
), the desired asymptotic formula (
2.33
) follows.

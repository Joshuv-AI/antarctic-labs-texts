Minimal signatures with undecidability of representability by binary relations
Robin Hirsch, Marcel Jackson, Jaš Šemrl

Abstract
. A semigroup of binary relations (under composition) on a set X X is complemented if it is closed under the taking of complements within X × X X\times X . We resolve a 1991 problem of Boris Schein by showing that the class of finite unary semigroups that are representable as complemented semigroups of binary relations is undecidable, so composition with complementation forms a minimal subsignature of Tarski’s relation algebra signature that has undecidability of representability. In addition we prove similar results for semigroups of binary relations endowed with unary operations returning the kernel and cokernel of a relation. We generalise to signatures which may include arbitrary, definable operations and provide a chain of weaker and weaker signatures, each definable in the previous signature, each having undecidability of representability, but whose limit signature includes composition only, which corresponds to the well known, decidable and finitely axiomatised variety of semigroups. All these results are also proved for representability as binary relations over a finite set.

The partial group embedding method

An instance
(P,*)
of the
partial group [finite] embedding problem
is a partial binary function
*
over a finite set
P
, it is a yes instance of
(P,*)
extends to a [finite] group else it is a no-instance. Both the partial group embedding problem and the partial group finite embedding problem are known to be undecidable
[
9
,
37
]
. In the following, we restrict instances of these problems to
square partial groups

(P,*)
, where

•

there are no explicit violations of associativity,

•

there is an element
e\in P
such that
e*a=a*e=a
for all
a\in P
,

•

there is a subset
\sqrt{P}\subseteq P
containing
e
such that
*
is a total function from
\sqrt{P}^{2}
to
P
and is undefined for products involving
P\backslash\sqrt{P}
except for those of the form
e*a=a*e=a
,

•

for each
a\in\sqrt{P}
there is a unique
a^{\prime}
such that
a*a^{\prime}=a^{\prime}*a=e
.

and both problems remain undecidable
[
16
, Lemma 2.4]
. Notationally we set
\{e\}=\sqrt{P}^{(0)}
,
\sqrt{P}^{(1)}:=\sqrt{P}
and
\sqrt{P}^{(2)}:=P
. All the undecidability results we establish here are based on reductions from the square partial group [finite] embedding problem.

A detailed motivation behind the underlying construction, in the context of representability is given in
[
15
]
.
In the present article we take a slightly different approach to previous efforts in representability applications, and use the particular formulation in Sapir
[
32
]
.
We recall some relevant semigroup-theoretic notions.

Let
S=(S,\mathbin{;})
be any semigroup and define the relation
\leq_{\mathscr{L}}
by

if
a=b
or there exist
x
such that

. In other words

if
a
lies in the principal left ideal of

.
Dually we define
\leq_{\mathscr{R}}
in terms of right multiplication. Associativity easily shows that these are preorders: reflexive and transitive.
The binary relations
\mathscr{L}
and
\mathscr{R}
are then defined as the equivalence relations determined by
\leq_{\mathscr{L}}
and
\leq_{\mathscr{R}}
respectively:
a\mathrel{\mathscr{L}}b\iff(a\leq_{\mathscr{L}}b\wedge b\leq_{\mathscr{L}}a)
and
a_{\mathscr{R}}b\iff(a\leq_{\mathscr{R}}b\wedge b\leq_{\mathscr{R}}a)
.
The relation
\mathscr{H}
is defined by
\mathscr{L}\cap\mathscr{R}
.
These and some other relations are usually referred to as

, see any semigroup theory text, such as
[
7
]
.
The article
[
32
]
concerns the problem of when a binary relation

on a finite semigroup

might sit within the
\mathscr{H}
-relation in some larger embedding semigroup

. This problem turns out to be undecidable, even in the case where

consists of a total relation on a subset

[
19
]
.

A

from a square partial group
(P,*)
into a semigroup

is a triple of maps

where
\alpha,\beta:\sqrt{P}\rightarrow{\bf S},\;\gamma:P\rightarrow{\bf S}
such that

, and is called a

if

is injective.
An
\mathscr{H}
-embedding
of

into a semigroup

is a homotopy embedding in which each of

,
\beta(\sqrt{P})

-relation on

.
The argument in

, it is a yes-instance if
r
is the kernel of some
s\in{\sf Rel}(X)
and a no-instance otherwise.

Corollary 7.3
.

The kernel problem is
NP
-complete.

Proof.

It is clear that the kernel problem may be solved nondeterministically by guessing a relation
s
and checking if
r=s\mathbin{;}s^{\smile}\in{\sf Rel}(X)
.
We prove NP-hardness by providing a reduction from the Clique Edge Colour Problem (CEC, also known as the Intersection Number problem), in which we are given a finite graph
G=(V,E)
and a number
k\leq|E|
and wish to know if there is a covering of the edges
E
by at most
k
many complete subgraphs (cliques) of
G
.
The CEC problem is known to be
NP
-complete
[
31
]
.
So, given an instance
(V,E,k)
of CEC, first check whether
k\geq|V|
or not.
If
k\geq|V|
we reduce
(V,E,k)
to
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
where
V^{\prime}\supset V
is a set with
k+1
vertices and
E^{\prime}=E\cup\operatorname{Id}_{(V^{\prime}\setminus V)}\cup(V^{\prime}\setminus V)\times(V^{\prime}\setminus V))
, in other words
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is the disjoint union of
(V,E)
and a single clique with
{k-|V|+1}
nodes.
Then
(V,E,k)
is a yes-instance of CEC iff there is a covering of
E
by at most
k
many cliques if and only if
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
has an edge covering by at most
k+1
cliques iff
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a yes-instance of the kernel problem, for
k\geq|V|
.
Otherwise
k<|V|
and let
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be the disjoint union of
(V,E)
with the complete graph
K_{2,|V|-k+2}
bipartite except there are loops at all nodes, so
|V^{\prime}|=|V|+(|V|-k+4)
.
Each edge between distinct nodes of
K_{2,|V|-k+2}
is a maximal clique, by bipartiteness of the irreflexive
part, so the smallest number of cliques that cover the edges of this part is
2(|V|-k+2)
.
We reduce
(V,E,k)
to
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})

and then
(V,E,k)
is a yes-instance of
CEC
iff
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
has
a covering by at most
k+2(|V|-k+2)=|V^{\prime}|
cliques iff
(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a yes-instance of the kernel problem, so the reduction is correct when
k<|V|
too.∎

Fill-Ins of Tori with Scalar Curvature Bounded from Below
Yipeng Wang

Abstract
. Let γ \gamma be a Riemannian metric on Σ = S 1 × T n − 2 \Sigma=S^{1}\times T^{n-2} , where 3 ≤ n ≤ 7 3\leq n\leq 7 . Consider Ω = B 2 × T n − 2 \Omega=B^{2}\times T^{n-2} with boundary ∂ Ω = Σ \partial\Omega=\Sigma , and let g g be a Riemannian metric on Ω \Omega such that the scalar curvature R g ≥ − n ⁡ ( n − 1 ) R_{g}\geq-n(n-1) and g | ∂ Ω = γ g|_{\partial\Omega}=\gamma . Assuming the mean curvature of ∂ Ω \partial\Omega with respect to the outward normal is positive, we establish that the total mean curvature of ∂ Ω \partial\Omega is bounded from above by a constant depending only on n n and γ \gamma . Furthermore, we compute the sharp constant for this estimate when γ \gamma is a flat metric. This result resolves a special case of a conjecture by Gromov concerning total mean curvature of fill-in with scalar curvature bounded from below. The proof combines techniques developed by Shi–Tam, Shi–Wang–Wei, as well as recent work by Brendle–Hung on the systolic inequality.



1.
Introduction

On a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary, a fundamental problem is understanding the relationship between the intrinsic curvature and the extrinsic curvature of the boundary. One classical result illustrating this principle is the following theorem by Shi and Tam:

Theorem 1.1

(Shi-Tam
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a strictly convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with a Riemannian metric
g
. Suppose

•

The scalar curvature
R_{g}\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

•

The induced metric on
\partial\Omega
agrees with the restriction of the Euclidean metric on
\partial\Omega
:
g|_{\partial\Omega}=g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|_{\partial\Omega}
.

•

The mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
with respect to
g
is positive.

Then

\int_{\partial\Omega}(H_{g}-H_{0})d\vol_{g}\leq 0,

where
H_{g}
and
H_{0}
denotes the mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
with respect to the metric
g
and
g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
respectively.

The Shi-Tam estimate is sharp by taking
g=g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. If we relax the condition that
g|_{\partial\Omega}=g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|_{\partial\Omega}
, a non-sharp estimate was later obtained by Shi, Wang, Wei, and Zhu:

Theorem 1.2

(Shi-Wang-Wei-Zhu
[
22
]
)
.

For
n\geq 3
, let
\sigma
be a Riemannian metric on
S^{n-1}
that is isotopic to the standard round metric through a path of metrics with positive scalar curvature. Then there exists some constant
C=C(n,\sigma)
with the following significance:

If
g
be a Riemannian metric on
B^{n}
and let
H_{g}
denotes the mean curvature of
\partial B^{n}=S^{n-1}
with respect to the metric
g
. Suppose
R_{g}\geq 0
in
B^{n}
,
g|_{\partial B^{n}}=\sigma
on
\partial B^{n}
and
H_{g}>0
at each point on
S^{n-1}
, then

\int_{S^{n-1}}H_{g}d\vol_{\sigma}\leq C.

Roughly speaking, the lower bound of the scalar curvature in the interior should control the mean curvature of the boundary from above. These results were later generalized by the work of Shi-Wang-Wei under the condition where the scalar curvature is bounded from below by
-n(n-1)

Theorem 1.3

(Shi-Wang-Wei
[
21
]
)

Let

Then

\int_{\partial B^{n}}H_{g}d\sigma_{g}\leq(n-1)\omega_{n-1}\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}.

We also have the analogue of Theorem
1.2

Conjecture 1.5

Let

(M^{n},g)

A key ingredient in the proof of the above results is the positive mass theorem: Given a fill-in of the round

with non-negative scalar curvature and positive mean curvature on the boundary, one can extend the metric outward by solving a non-linear parabolic equation. It turns out that one obtains an asymptotically flat (resp. asymptotically hyperbolic) manifold

with
R\geq 0
(resp.

). Next, by using a monotonicity formula along the parabolic equation, one can estimate
\int_{S^{n-1}}H
in terms of the mass of
X
: if the integral of the mean curvature is too large, then the mass of
X
has to be negative, contradicting the positive mass theorem.

Indeed, the estimate of the integral of the mean curvature has a natural interpretation as a notion of quasi-local mass in mathematical relativity. We refer to the work of Brown-York

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose

. Let
\hat{\gamma}
be a Riemannian metric on
\Sigma=S^{1}\times T^{n-2}

We pick an arbitrary flat metric

2.1.
The Prescribed Scalar Curvature Equation

Let
\Sigma^{n-1}=S^{1}\times T^{n-2}
be the
(n-1)

\gamma

\Sigma\times[\rho_{0},\infty)

\overline{g}=\rho^{2}\gamma+\rho^{-2}d\rho\otimes d\rho

\Sigma_{\rho}=\Sigma\times\{\rho\}
to be the constant
\rho

\overline{g}
is
-n(n-1)
. We denote
\overline{A}_{\Sigma_{\rho}}
and
\overline{H}_{\Sigma_{\rho}}
to be the second fundamental form and the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{\rho}
with respect to the
\overline{g}
-unit normal vector
\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}
. In particular,
\overline{A}_{\Sigma_{\rho}}=\rho^{2}\gamma
and
\overline{H}_{\Sigma_{\rho}}=n-1
.

Let
u(x,\rho)
be a smooth positive function on
\Sigma\times[\rho_{0},\infty)
. We consider the Riemannian metric
g=\rho^{2}\gamma+u^{2}\rho^{-2}d\rho\otimes d\rho

to be the second fundamental form and the mean curvature with respect to
g

\nu_{g}=u^{-1}\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}

A_{\Sigma_{\rho}}=u^{-1}\rho^{2}\gamma
and
H_{\Sigma_{\rho}}=(n-1)u^{-1}
with respect to the metric
g

Lemma 2.1
.

The condition
R_{g}=-n(n-1)
is equivalent to the following equation:

(1)

{}(n-1)\rho\frac{\partial u}{\partial\rho}=u^{2}\Delta_{\Sigma_{\rho}}u-\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(u^{3}-u).

Proof.

We consider the normal variation of the slice
\Sigma_{\rho}
along the vector field
\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}=u\nu_{g}
, then the variation formula of mean curvature and the Gauss equation implies

\displaystyle(n-1)\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}u^{-1}=\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}{H}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\Sigma_{\rho}}u+\frac{1}{2}(R_{\Sigma_{\rho}}-R_{{g}}-|{A}_{\Sigma_{\rho}}|_{g}^{2}-{H}_{\Sigma_{\rho}}^{2})u

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\Sigma_{\rho}}u-\frac{1}{2}R_{{g}}u-\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)u^{-1}.

Prescribing
R_{g}=-n(n-1)
is then equivalent to

(2)

{}(n-1)\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}u^{-1}=-\Delta_{\Sigma_{\rho}}u+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(u-u^{-1}).

The assertion (
1
) then follows from
\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}u^{-1}=-u^{-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\rho}
.
∎

The prescribed scalar curvature equation (
1
) was discussed by Bartnik
[
3
]

21
]

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose

(3)

{}\rho\frac{d}{d\rho}\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}((n-1)-H_{\Sigma_{\rho}})\rho d\vol_{\Sigma_{\rho}}=-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\int_{\Sigma\rho}u^{-1}(1-u)^{2}\rho d\vol_{\Sigma_{\rho}}\leq 0.

Proof.

Using (

\displaystyle\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(\rho^{n}((n-1)-H_{\Sigma_{\rho}})\right)

\displaystyle=n(n-1)\rho^{n}(1-u^{-1})+\rho^{n}(\Delta_{\Sigma_{\rho}}u-\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(u-u^{-1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)\rho^{n}(2-u-u^{-1})+\rho^{n-2}\Delta_{\Sigma}u.

Integrating over

\displaystyle\rho\frac{d}{d\rho}\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}((n-1)-H_{\Sigma_{\rho}})\rho d\vol_{\Sigma_{\rho}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(\rho^{n}((n-1)-H_{\Sigma_{\rho}})\right)d\vol_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\rho\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}(2-u^{-1}-u)d\vol_{\Sigma_{\rho}}.

The claim now follows directly.

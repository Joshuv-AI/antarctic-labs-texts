Abstract
We report the first direct measurement of galaxy assembly bias, a critical systematic in cosmology, from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) Bright Galaxy Survey. We introduce a novel, cosmology-independent method to measure the halo occupation distribution (HOD) by combining a state-of-the-art group catalog with weak gravitational lensing. For groups binned by total luminosity, we determine the galaxy occupation number N gal N_{\rm gal} from group-galaxy cross-correlations, while weak lensing constrains the average halo mass M h M_{h} . Applying this to a volume-limited sample at z ∈ [ 0.05 , 0.2 ] z{\in}[0.05,0.2] , we measure the dependence of HOD, N gal ​ ( M h ) N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h}) , on large-scale overdensity δ g \delta_{g} . Focusing on the satellite galaxies, we find an assembly bias parameter of Q sat = 0.05 ± 0.14 Q_{\mathrm{sat}}{=}0.05{\pm}0.14 , a result consistent with zero and in tension with many empirical galaxy formation models. Our method provides a robust approach for characterizing galaxy assembly bias to achieve precision cosmology with DESI and future Stage-V surveys.



Introduction
—
Modern cosmology relies on galaxy redshift surveys to
probe the growth of dark matter structure
(
Weinberg et al. 2013
)
and the
potential time-evolution of dark energy
(
Adame et al. 2025
;
DESI Collaboration et al. 2025a
)
.
The accuracy of these cosmological probes hinges on a robust model
connecting the observed galaxy population to the underlying dark matter
halos
(
Wechsler and Tinker 2018
)
, particularly through the number of galaxies
residing in a single halo (i.e., the occupation number). The standard halo
occupation distribution (HOD;

(
Jing et al. 1998
;
Peacock and Smith 2000
;
Berlind and Weinberg 2002
;
Zheng et al. 2005
;
Mandelbaum et al. 2006
;
Zu and Mandelbaum 2015
)
)
model posits that the
mean
occupation number
N_{\mathrm{gal}}
is a function
solely of halo mass
M_{h}
. However, galaxy assembly may be systematically
influenced by other halo properties. This effect, known as galaxy assembly
bias (GAB), introduces a dependence of
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h})
on secondary halo
properties
\mathbf{X}
, such as the halo formation time, concentration,
and large-scale
environment
(
Zhu et al. 2006
;
Zehavi et al. 2018
;
Artale et al. 2018
;
Bose et al. 2019
;
Hadzhiyska et al. 2021
)
.
A strong GAB is a near-ubiquitous prediction of empirical models of galaxy
formation, including semi-analytic models
(
Zehavi et al. 2018
;
Artale et al. 2018
)
and
hydrodynamical
simulations
(
Zhu et al. 2006
;
Bose et al. 2019
;
Hadzhiyska et al. 2021
;
Meng et al. 2025
)
. As a direct
consequence, GAB has been identified as a key source of systematic
uncertainty for cosmological
inference
(
Findlay et al. 2025
;
Garcia-Quintero et al. 2025
;
Mena-Fernández et al. 2025
)
,
posing a significant risk to the fidelity of constraints from Stage-IV
surveys like the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI;

(
DESI Collaboration et al. 2016a
;
DESI Collaboration et al. 2016b
;
Guy et al. 2023
;
Schlafly et al. 2023
;
Miller et al. 2024
;
Poppett et al. 2024
)
).
In this Letter, we develop a novel method to directly measure the HOD of
group-mass halos residing in different large-scale overdensity
environments, in order to constrain the level of GAB with the DESI Data
Release 1 (DR1) data.

Over the past decade, various studies have tried to detect GAB in the local
Universe using galaxy clustering statistics (hereafter referred to as the
“indirect method”), often assuming a fixed cosmology (e.g.,
Refs.
(
Lehmann et al. 2017
;
Zentner et al. 2019
;
Vakili and Hahn 2019
;
Obuljen et al. 2020
;
Yuan et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2022a
;
Salcedo et al. 2022
;
Pearl et al. 2024
)
). The key observable of galaxy
clustering is the bias factor of galaxies,
b_{g}
, defined as the linear
clustering amplitude of galaxies relative to the dark matter. In
particular, for a given set of cosmological parameters
\bm{\theta}
, we
can predict
b_{g}
for any galaxy sample with a number density
n_{g}
as:

b_{g}{=}\frac{1}{n_{g}}\!\iint\!\!N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h}|\mathbf{X})n_{h}(M_{h}|\mathbf{X},\bm{\theta})b_{h}(M_{h}|\mathbf{X},\bm{\theta})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{X}\mathrm{d}M_{h},

(1)

where
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h}|\mathbf{X})
is our GAB observable,
n_{h}
is the differential halo
number density distribution, and
b_{h}
is the bias factor of
halos
1
1

1

The term
b_{h}(M_{h}|\mathbf{X})
describes the so-called
“halo assembly bias”
effect
(
Sheth and Tormen 2004
;
Gao et al. 2005
;
Harker et al. 2006
;
Wechsler et al. 2006
;
Jing et al. 2007
;
Li et al. 2008
;
Salcedo et al. 2018
)
.
.
Equation
1
shows that the GAB constraint using an indirect
method is susceptible to systematic uncertainties if an incorrect cosmology
is assumed for
b_{h}
and
n_{h}
,
even though GAB itself does not depend on

\bm{\theta}
. Conversely, cosmological constraints using galaxy
clustering may be biased if GAB is not properly modeled
Wang et al. 2025
.
This issue may be related to the lensing-clustering discrepancy of the
luminous red galaxies (LRGs) observed at

z{<}0.7

(
Leauthaud et al. 2017
;
Lange et al. 2019
;
Yuan et al. 2020
;
Amon et al. 2023
;
Shao et al. 2023
)
. To
mitigate such an issue in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS;

(
York et al. 2000
)
),
Salcedo et al. 2022
employed the
simulated
halo
catalog from the
ELUCID
constrained simulation that accurately
reconstructed the initial density perturbation (hence the correct

n_{h}{\times}b_{h}
) within the SDSS volume
(
Wang et al. 2016
)
, and they found
no evidence for a significant GAB in the SDSS data.

However, an ideal method would directly measure
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h}|\mathbf{X})
for the
observed
halos selected by secondary halo properties
\mathbf{X}
, without
the need to assume a specific cosmology. In this work, we make the first
step towards this goal by taking advantage of the state-of-the-art DESI
halo-based group catalog developed by
Yang et al. 2021
, which
achieves
{>}90\%
completeness and purity for halos with

M_{h}{>}10^{13}\,h^{-1}M_{\odot}
at
z{<}0.5
. More important, the halo-based group
finder is not biased towards red galaxy colors
(
Golden-Marx et al. 2023
)
.
Since GAB may influence the color distribution of satellite galaxies within
halos, a color-insensitive group finder is essential for reliable GAB
detection. For the choice of
\mathbf{X}
, we use the large-scale galaxy
overdensity
\delta_{g}
around each halo, which serves as a proxy for the
underlying dark matter overdensity
\delta_{m}
.
Xu et al. 2021
showed that the
GAB effect in semi-analytic models can be fully accounted for by
\delta_{m}

enclosed within several halo radii.

To measure
N_{\mathrm{gal}}
for DESI groups binned by
\delta_{g}
, one
might initially consider simply counting member galaxies identified by the
group finder (i.e., using “richness”). However, accurately determining
membership probabilities remains challenging, particularly due to
interlopers caused by projection effects
(
Zu et al. 2017
)
. In this work, we
instead introduce a robust alternative approach that does not depend on the
group finder: we measure
N_{\mathrm{gal}}
using the cross-correlation between groups
and galaxies and remove the interlopers statistically. By combining these

N_{\mathrm{gal}}
measurements with halo mass
M_{h}
derived from weak lensing, we
directly

measure the GAB observable
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h}|\delta_{g})
.

Throughout this letter, we use the latest
Planck

\Lambda
CDM model
for distances
(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020
)
, and adopt the halo mass definition in
which the matter density enclosed within the halo radius
r_{h}
is
200

times the mean density
\rho_{m}
of the Universe.

DESI Bright Galaxy Survey and group catalog
—We use the large-scale structure (LSS) galaxy catalog
(
Ross et al. 2025
)

derived from the flux-limited (
m_{r}{<}19.5
) sample of the Bright Galaxy
Survey (BGS;
(
Hahn et al. 2023
)
) within DESI DR1
(
DESI Collaboration et al. 2025b
)
. The LSS
catalog provides the necessary weights to correct for imaging
systematics (
w_{\rm sys}
), fiber assignment incompleteness (
w_{\rm comp}
), and redshift failures (
w_{\rm zfail}
), so that the final LSS
weight of each galaxy is
w_{\rm tot}{=}w_{\rm sys}w_{\rm comp}w_{\rm zfail}
. For our GAB detection analysis, we construct a volume-limited
galaxy sample from the LSS catalog with
M_{r}{\leqslant}{-}19.68
(i.e.,

L{\geqslant}0.4L_{*}
) within
0.05<z<0.2
, yielding an average
galaxy number density of

\bar{n}_{g}\approx 0.01\,h^{3}{\rm Mpc^{-3}}
.

For the host halos, we employ an updated version of the extended halo-based
group catalog developed in
Yang et al. 2021
, which has incorporated all
spectroscopic redshifts observed by DESI DR2. To ensure a high purity above
90\%
, we
select groups with
\log M_{h}^{\mathrm{AM}}{>}13.0
, where
M_{h}^{\mathrm{AM}}
is the
catalog-provided halo mass estimated from abundance matching (AM) using the
total luminosity of all the group members (
L_{\rm tot}
). We set the
group center at the brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) following the
practice in Ref.
Wang et al. 2022b
. However, a fraction of the BCGs are offset
from the true centers of the halos
(
George et al. 2012
)
. To mitigate such a
miscentering effect, we calculate a projected offset
d_{\rm offset}

between the BCG and the luminosity-weighted center for each group,
and remove the poorly-centered systems (
20\%
of the total sample) with

d_{\rm offset}{>}0.2\,r_{h}^{\rm AM}
, where
r_{h}^{\rm AM}
is the
catalog-provided halo radius.

each halo

Halo-galaxy cross-correlation and weak lensing
—As outlined in the Introduction, our direct method of detecting GAB
requires measuring the halo-galaxy cross-correlation function
w_{p}^{hg}
and
the weak lensing profile
\Delta\Sigma
. We measure
w_{p}^{hg}
by integrating the 2D
redshift-space correlation function
\xi_{hg}^{rs}
along the LOS

w_{p}^{hg}(r_{p})=2\int_{0}^{r_{\pi}^{\mathrm{max}}}\xi_{hg}^{rs}(r_{p},r_{\pi})\,\mathrm{d}r_{\pi},

(2)

where
r_{p}
and
r_{\pi}
are the projected and LOS pair separations,
respectively. We compute
\xi_{hg}^{rs}
using the Landy-Szalay
estimator
(
Landy and Szalay 1993
)
, and set the integration limit
\pi_{\rm max}

to be
200\,\mathrm{Mpc}/h
when calculating
w_{p}^{hg}
.

For measuring
\Delta\Sigma(r_{p})
, we use the public shear
catalog
3
3

3

https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/rmandelb/data.html

based on the SDSS DR7
(
Abazajian et al. 2009
)
, which is described in detail
in Ref.
Reyes et al. 2012
and systematically tested
in Refs.
Mandelbaum et al. 2012
;
Mandelbaum et al. 2013
. The shapes of galaxies are
measured using the re-Gaussianization method
(
Hirata and Seljak 2003
)
and the
photometric redshifts of the sources are estimated using Zurich
Extragalactic Bayesian Redshift Analyzer (ZEBRA;
(
Feldmann et al. 2006
)
).
The
\Delta\Sigma
profiles are measured from the shear catalog by following the
procedures in Ref.
Singh et al. 2020
. The covariance matrices of
w_{p}^{hg}
and

\Delta\Sigma
are both estimated by applying the jackknife resampling technique
using 200 equal-area, spatially contiguous subregions within the DESI Y1
footprint.

Direct measurement of
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h}|\delta_{g})
—For any
M_{h}^{\mathrm{AM}}
bin in the high- or low-
\delta_{g}
subsample, we first measure
the average halo mass
M_{h}
(hence radius
r_{h}
) from
\Delta\Sigma(r_{p})
,
following the same method described in Ref.
Zu et al. 2021
. We briefly
describe the method below and refer readers to Ref.
Zu et al. 2021
for
technical details. In the absence of miscentering, the weak lensing profile
can be modelled as

\Delta\Sigma(r_{p}|M_{h})=\frac{2}{r^{2}_{p}}\int_{0}^{r_{p}}r_{p}^{\prime}\Sigma(r_{p}^{\prime}|M_{h})\,\mathrm{d}r_{p}^{\prime}-\Sigma(r_{p}|M_{h}),

(3)

where
\Sigma(r_{p}|M_{h})
is the surface mass density profile of halos with
mass
M_{h}
. We then compute
\Sigma(r_{p}|M_{h})
as

\Sigma(r_{p}|M_{h})=2\rho_{m}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\xi_{hm}\left(r{=}\sqrt{r_{p}^{2}+r_{\pi}^{2}}\Big|M_{h}\right)\,\mathrm{d}r_{\pi}.

(4)

where
\xi_{hm}(r|M_{h})
is the isotropic halo-mass cross-correlation function.
Our analytic model of
\xi_{hm}(r)
is presented in the End Matter. To
further account for the miscentering effect, we adopt the flexible
miscentering model in Ref.
Zu et al. 2021
, which allows an arbitrary
fraction of miscentered halos that are offset from the halo center by a
shape-2 Gamma distribution
(
Zhang et al. 2019
)
.

With
M_{h}
measured from weak lensing,
the mean galaxy occupation for a set of perfectly-centered halos
with exactly the same mass
M_{h}
(hence the same
r_{h}
) can be computed by

N_{\mathrm{gal}}^{\prime}(M_{h})=n_{g}\int_{0}^{r_{h}}4\pi r^{2}\xi_{hg}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r+n_{g}V_{\mathrm{sph}}(r_{h}),

(5)

where
\xi_{hg}(r)
is the isotropic halo-galaxy cross-correlation function
(see End Matter),
n_{g}
is the mean
galaxy number density,
and
V_{\mathrm{sph}}(r_{h})
is the volume of the spherical halo. Following
Ref.
Zu 2021
, we infer
\xi_{hg}(r)
via the deprojection of the
observed
w_{p}^{hg}(r_{p})
assuming

w_{p}^{hg}(r_{p})=2\int_{0}^{r_{\pi}^{\mathrm{max}}}\xi_{hg}\left(r{=}\sqrt{r_{p}^{2}+r_{\pi}^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}r_{\pi}.

(6)

Armed with the inferred
\xi_{hg}
, we compute a preliminary occupation number

N_{\mathrm{gal}}^{\prime}
using Equation
5
, for the ideal case in
which all halos are correctly-centered spheres with the same radius.
However, the observed groups could be miscentered and have a
{\sim}0.2

dex scatter between
M_{h}^{\mathrm{AM}}
and
M_{h}

(
Yang et al. 2021
)
. These deviations
from the ideal case will cause the satellite distribution to extend beyond
the average halo radius
r_{h}
. Consequently,
N_{\mathrm{gal}}^{\prime}
will be
underestimated compared to the true occupation number
N_{\mathrm{gal}}
. To quantify
such a systematic discrepancy, we define a “galaxy occupation bias”
parameter
q
, so that

N_{\mathrm{gal}}=(1+q)\cdot N_{\mathrm{gal}}^{\prime}.

(7)

For detecting GAB, it is critical that
q
does not depend on
\delta_{g}
,
otherwise the occupation bias will masquerade as a false GAB signal.
Through a high-fidelity DESI-like mock test, we demonstrate that
q
is
independent of
\delta_{g}
, and the fiducial value of
q
is equal to
0.102
.
Thus, we compute the final galaxy occupation numbers as
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h}|\delta_{g}){=}1.102{\times}N_{\mathrm{gal}}^{\prime}(M_{h}|\delta_{g})
. The details of the mock test
are presented in the End Matter.

In order to detect GAB self-consistently, we employ a unified GAB model to
fit to both sets of
N_{\mathrm{sat}}(M_{h}|\delta_{g})
measurements simultaneously.
We assume the HOD at any given
\delta_{g}
follows a power-law

N_{\mathrm{sat}}(M_{h}|\delta_{g})=\left[\frac{M_{h}}{M_{1}(\delta_{g})}\right]^{\alpha},

(8)

where
M_{1}
is the typical mass of a halo that can host one galaxy at any
given
\delta_{g}
environment. Following Ref.
Salcedo et al. 2022
, we model GAB as
the modulation of
M_{1}
by the overdensity rank-order
\tilde{\delta}_{g}
,

\log M_{1}(\delta_{g})=\log{M}_{1}^{*}+Q_{\mathrm{sat}}(\tilde{\delta}_{g}-0.5),

(9)

where
M_{1}^{*}
is the
M_{1}
at the median overdensity
\tilde{\delta}_{g}=0.5
, and

Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
is the GAB parameter (
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}{=}0
corresponds to zero GAB). A
positive
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
means that halos in high-
\delta_{g}
environments host fewer
number of satellites than ones in low-
\delta_{g}
environments. During the fit, we
set
\tilde{\delta}_{g}
to be
0.25
and
0.75
for the low- and high-
\delta_{g}
subsamples,
respectively, and assume a fixed value of
\alpha{=}1

(
Zehavi et al. 2011
)
.

Finally, taking into account the full covariance of all
(N_{\mathrm{sat}},M_{h})

pairs, we obtain a GAB constraint of
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}{=}0.05{\pm}0.14
. The best-fitting model
fits and the associated
1\sigma
uncertainties are shown in
Figure
3
as the two lines and shaded regions,
respectively. Despite the slight preference towards a positive
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}
,
the two power-law fits are consistent with each other.

The projection effect may introduce a
\delta_{g}
-dependent bias in the
measurement of
M_{h}
, we test the impact of such an effect on our GAB
constraint by assuming the high-
\delta_{g}
halos are subject to an additional
5% bias in their estimated values of
M_{h}
, according to the mock
experiment in Ref.
Wu et al. 2022
. As a result, the GAB constraint shifts
slightly to
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}{=}-0.01{\pm}0.14
and becomes even more consistent with
zero. Therefore, our direct GAB measurement suggests that there is no
statistically significant dependence of the galaxy occupation number on the
large-scale overdensity, at least for DESI galaxies brighter than
0.4L_{*}

and halos with
\log M_{h}>12.9
.

Conclusions
—In this Letter, we develop a novel method to directly measure the HOD of
galaxies in DESI groups,
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h})
, by combining the group-galaxy
cross-correlation functions with the halo mass measurements from weak
lensing. By examining the
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h})
measurements for halos within
different environments, characterized by their large-scale galaxy
overdensities
\delta_{g}
, we are able to directly measure the level of galaxy
assembly bias with the DESI DR1 data. In particular, we apply our direct
method to measure
N_{\mathrm{gal}}(M_{h}|\delta_{g})
using a volume-limited (
0.05{<}z{<}0.2
and

M_{r}{\leqslant}{-}19.68
) galaxy sample from the DESI BGS and the
state-of-the-art halo-based group catalog from
Yang et al. 2021
. Focusing
on the satellite galaxies, we obtain a stringent constraint on the
satellite assembly bias parameter as
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}{=}0.05{\pm}0.14
, suggesting zero galaxy
assembly bias within our DESI galaxy sample (
{\geqslant}0.4L_{*}
) inside
group-size halos (
\log M_{h}{>}12.9
).

Our result is consistent with the comprehensive indirect constraint based
on the SDSS data (
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}{=}0.09{\pm}{0.10}
;
(
Salcedo et al. 2022
)
),
despite the slight difference in sample definition (
L/L_{*}{\geqslant}0.4

vs.
M/M_{*}{\geqslant}0.6
). Recent studies
(
Alam et al. 2019
;
Izzo Dominguez et al. 2025
)

also found little dependence of HOD on the cosmic web environment using the
SDSS data. The combination of these independent constraints reveals a
remarkably simple picture of galaxy-halo connection in the local Universe.
Despite the visually striking difference between the large-scale
environments of the two halo subsamples (as seen in
Figure
1
), the number of BGS galaxies hosted by the halos is
almost entirely determined by the halo mass
(
White 1997
)
.

However, our direct constraint is in tension with the significant amount of
GAB predicted by various empirical models of galaxy formation. For
example, Ref.
Xu et al. 2021
found
Q_{\mathrm{sat}}{=}{-}0.25
in a semi-analytic
model, using a mock galaxy sample that has the same number density as ours.
It is unclear whether the apparent over-prediction of GAB by empirical
models is an artifact of the subgrid physics, or strong GAB is strictly a
phenomenon in the lower-mass regime (e.g.,
{<}0.4L_{*}
or
\log M_{h}{<}12.9
). This issue can be addressed by applying our direct method to
the fainter galaxy samples within DESI BGS using future data releases.

Beyond DESI BGS, our method can be straightforwardly extended to other
populations of tracer galaxies in DESI, especially the LRGs
(
Zhou et al. 2023
)

at the higher redshifts, as well as the dense samples of emission line
galaxies in the upcoming Prime Focus Spectrograph
(
Takada et al. 2014
)
survey.
These galaxies have complex color selections and may therefore have
different levels of GAB compared to the luminosity-limited sample studied
in this work (e.g.,

Montero-Dorta et al. 2017
;
Niemiec et al. 2018
;
Obuljen et al. 2020
;
Yuan et al. 2021
;
Oyarzún et al. 2024
). Our current
constraint on GAB is fundamentally limited by the uncertainties in the halo
mass and the availability of high-quality group catalogs, both of which
will be significantly improved in the era of the Vera C. Rubin
Observatory
(
Ivezić et al. 2019
)
,
Euclid
mission
(
Euclid Collaboration et al. 2025
)
,
Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope
(
Akeson et al. 2019
)
, and China Space
Station Telescope
(
CSST Collaboration et al. 2025
)
.

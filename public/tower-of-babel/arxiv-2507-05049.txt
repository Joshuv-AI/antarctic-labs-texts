Biharmonic Steklov problems with Neumann boundary conditions and spectral inequalities on differential forms
Rodolphe Abou Assali

Abstract




Abstract
.
We introduce three biharmonic Steklov problems on differential forms with Neumann boundary conditions and show that they are elliptic. We prove the existence of a discrete spectrum for each of those problems and give associated variational characterizations for their eigenvalues. We establish eigenvalue estimates known as Kuttler-Sigillito inequalities, relating the eigenvalues of these problems with the eigenvalues of the Steklov, Dirichlet and Neumann standard problems as well as the biharmonic Steklov problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions on differential forms.

Introduction

1.1
Historical background

Spectral theory is a vast field that intersects with several aspects of mathematics, such as analysis, geometry, and topology. The applications of spectral theory are closely related to physics. The Dirichlet and Neumann problems are the primary examples of spectral problems
[
4
,
27
]
, allowing the study of the properties of eigenfunctions and eigenvalues on geometric domains. Their applications are directly relevant to the study of vibration modes and the diffusion of sound and heat in domains with boundaries. For instance, given a planar domain (”drum”), how does its geometry influence its vibrations or the evolution of temperature within it?
Mathematically, this corresponds to studying the spectrum of a differential operator, which in our case is the Laplacian under certain boundary conditions. The Steklov problem is another example of spectral problems, introduced by the Russian mathematician Vladimir Andreevich Steklov in a talk given in 1895, see
[
18
]
.

In this paper we work on biharmonic Steklov operators. The study of these differential operators originated from the theory of elastic oscillating membranes and was mathematically examined for the first time on planar domains by J. Kuttler and V. Sigillito
[
16
,
17
]
and L.E. Payne
[
22
]
. The latter gave some isoperimetric inequalities for the first eigenvalue and eigenvalue estimates were given in
[
20
,
21
]
. The problem also appeared in
[
3
]
, where some results about the first Steklov eigenvalue of the biharmonic operator in bounded domains are given. There are two distinct but related natural spectral problems, depending on the boundary conditions. Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with a smooth boundary and let
\nu
be the inward unit normal along the boundary
\partial M
. The scalar biharmonic Steklov problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions is defined as:

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u=0&\text{on}\ M\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\Delta u-q\partial_{\nu}u=0&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

(1)

and the scalar biharmonic Steklov problem with Neumann boundary conditions is:

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u=0&\text{on}\ M\\
\partial_{\nu}u=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\partial_{\nu}(\Delta u)-\ell u=0&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

(2)

where
u
is a smooth function on
M
, and
\Delta
is the (positive) scalar Laplacian operator. In both problems, the real numbers
q
and
\ell
are the eigenvalues of the operator.
Following the original studies
[
16
,
17
]
on planar domains, which in particular showed the existence of a discrete spectrum consisting of real eigenvalues of finite multiplicity for problems (
1
) and (
2
), these problems were more recently studied on higher-dimensional Euclidean domains in
[
8
]
. It was subsequently shown by S. Raulot and A. Savo in
[
23
]
that the biharmonic Steklov-Dirichlet operator on forms of maximal degree is related to the Serrin problem
[
26
]
. The Serrin problem is the following boundary problem: given
M
subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we look for a solution
u
such that, for some real constant
c
it satisfies the overdetermined problem

\begin{cases}\Delta u=1&\text{on}\ M\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
{\partial_{\nu}u}_{|\partial M}=c&\text{on}\ \partial M.\end{cases}

(3)

Actually
u
describes the velocity of an incompressible viscous fluid in a straight pipe with a circular section.

More recently, A. Hassannezhad and A. Siffert
[
11
]
generalized some of the estimates of
[
16
,
17
]
for both scalar biharmonic Steklov operators on arbitrary Riemannian manifolds and showed that other quantities appear in their estimates that depend on the curvature of the manifold. In the same context, S. Kouzayha established in her PhD thesis
[
15
]
an inequality that links the first eigenvalue of the scalar biharmonic Steklov-Dirichlet problem with those of the Dirichlet and Robin Laplacians.

In a recent work
[
6
]
, the biharmonic Steklov problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions (
1
) is generalized to differential forms of arbitrary degree. Appropriate boundary conditions are considered to make the corresponding problem well-posed. The smallest eigenvalue is characterized, and various estimates for this eigenvalue are obtained, some of which involve eigenvalues for other problems such as Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, and Steklov. Independently, other inequalities concerning the eigenvalues of these latter problems are established.

In this work we extend the biharmonic Steklov problem with Neumann boundary condition (
2
) to differential forms of arbitrary degree. This extension is not unique, as there are several possible choices of boundary conditions. We provide the variational characterization of the eigenvalues for three possible choices of such extensions. This allows us to generalize Kuttler-Sigillito spectral inequalities, which relate eigenvalues of different problems on a bounded planar domain
[
17
]
. Some of these inequalities on functions were also established by A. Hassannezhad and A. Siffert on compact Riemannian manifolds with
C^{2}
boundary.

From now on, we will refer to the Biharmonic Steklov problem with Dirichlet boundary condition as BSD and the Biharmonic Steklov problem with Neumann boundary condition as BSN.

1.3
Kuttler-Sigillito inequalities

After obtaining well posed BSD and BSN problems and finding their variational characterizations, inequalities connecting the eigenvalues of different problems were established. These are known under the name of Kuttler-Sigillito inequalities. These inequalities were introduced in
[
17
]
on a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and were partly generalized in
[
11
]
to the manifold setting. We provide these inequalities from
[
11
,
17
]
, that we extend for differential forms later on. We denote by
\lambda_{k}
,
\mu_{k}
,
\sigma_{k}
,
q_{k}
, and
\ell_{k}
the
k^{th}
eigenvalues of the following problems, respectively: Dirichlet, Neumann, Steklov, BSD, and BSN.

Note that we always index the eigenvalues of non-negative operators so that
k=1
corresponds to the first positive eigenvalue.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
11
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
with a
C^{2}
boundary. For all integer
k\geq 1
, we have:

\mu_{k}\sigma_{1}\leq\ell_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{1}\sigma_{k}\leq\ell_{k}.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
17
, Table 1]

On a bounded domain
M
of the plane with piecewise
C^{1}
boundary
\partial M
, the following inequalities hold:

(i)

q_{1}\sigma_{1}^{2}\leq\ell_{1}
,

(ii)

\mu_{1}^{-1}\leq\lambda_{1}^{-1}+(q_{1}\ell_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,

(iii)

\mu_{1}^{-1}\leq\lambda_{1}^{-1}+(q_{1}\sigma_{1})^{-1}
.

BSD on differential forms

We have seen above the BSD problem, defined for functions on a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and on a compact Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary. This problem has been generalized for differential forms of arbitrary degree by F. El Chami, N. Ginoux, G. Habib, and O. Makhoul in

Let

1.5
BSN on differential forms

To establish inequalities between the eigenvalues for differential forms, as was done in the scalar case, we need to find a suitable BSN problem for differential forms, inspired by the work done in
[
6
]
. This first following problem is the natural analogue of the BSD problem presented in
[
6
]
, adapted to Neumann boundary conditions.

Let us first define the space that will be the kernel of many boundary problems we are interested in.

Definition 1.9
.

The absolute de Rham cohomology of order
p
is the space

H_{A}^{p}(M)=\left\{\w\in\Omega^{p}(M)\mid d\w=0\text{ on }M,\ \delta\w=0\text{ on }M,\ \nu\lrcorner\w=0\text{ on }\partial M\right\}.

(9)

It has finite dimension, see
[
23
, Section 2]
.

Theorem 1.10
.

The following boundary problem

(BSN1)\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}\w=0&\text{on }M\\
\nu\lrcorner\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner d\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner\Delta\w+\ell\iota^{*}\delta\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner\Delta d\w+\ell\iota^{*}\w=0&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

(10)

for differential p-forms, has a discrete spectrum consisting of an unbounded non-decreasing sequence of positive real eigenvalues with finite multiplicities
(\ell_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and possibly
\ell_{0}=0
.
Moreover, its kernel is
H_{A}^{p}(M)
.

It happens that we could not use this version of the BSN, which we will refer to as BSN1, to establish Kuttler-Sigillito inequalities due to the non-suitable boundary conditions. For that reason, we have first introduced another version of the BSN, called BSN2, which can be used to establish the equivalent of
Theorem
1.5

Theorem 1.11
.

The following boundary problem

(BSN2)\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}\w=0&\text{on }M\\
\nu\lrcorner\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner d\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\iota^{*}\delta\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner\Delta d\w+\mathbf{l}\iota^{*}\w=0&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

for differential p-forms, has a discrete spectrum consisting of an unbounded non-decreasing sequence of positive real eigenvalues with finite multiplicities
(\mathbf{l}_{i})_{i\geq 1}

\mathbf{l}_{0}=0

H_{A}^{p}(M)

Outline of the Article:

In Section 2 of this article, we provide a review of concepts related to differential forms, elliptic operators, and general theorem for the existence of solutions of boundary problems. In Sections 3, 4, and 5, we respectively study the spectra BSN1, BSN2 and BSN3 for differential forms. Finally, in Section 6, we establish generalizations of the Kuttler–Sigillito inequalities.

2.1.1
Differential forms on manifolds

Let
M
be a smooth compact manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
with smooth boundary. A differential form of degree
p
on
M
is a smooth section of the bundle
\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M
and the set of
p
-differential forms on
M
is denoted
\Omega^{p}(M)
.

We denote by
\iota:\partial M\longrightarrow M
the inclusion map. Thus, for any
\w\in\partial M
,
\iota^{*}\w
is the tangential component of
\w_{|\partial M}
, and
\nu\lrcorner\w
is its normal component.

Let
M
be an oriented Riemannian manifold. Recall that the Hodge operator is a linear map denoted by
"\star"
, defined as
\star:\Lambda^{p}M^{*}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{n-p}M^{*}
such that if
(\epsilon_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is a positively oriented orthonormal basis of
T^{*}M
, then

\star(\epsilon_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\epsilon_{i_{p}})=\epsilon_{j_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\epsilon_{j_{n-p}}

where,
(j_{1},\dots,j_{n-p})
is chosen such that
(\epsilon_{i_{1}},\dots,\epsilon_{i_{p}},\epsilon_{j_{1}},\dots,\epsilon_{j_{n-p}})
is a positively oriented orthonormal basis. As a consequence we have
\star 1=\epsilon_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\epsilon_{n}
,
\star(\epsilon_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\epsilon_{n})=1
and for a
p
-form
\w
we get
\star\star\w=(-1)^{p(n-p)}\w.

For differential forms, we denote by
\langle.,.\rangle
the natural inner product induced by the Riemannian metric, and we define
(\w,\w^{\prime}):=\int_{M}\langle\w,\w^{\prime}\rangle d\mu_{g}.

The codifferential operator, denoted
\delta
, is a map
\delta:\Omega^{p}(M)\longrightarrow\Omega^{p-1}(M)
. It is the formal adjoint of the exterior derivative
d
, i.e., for all
\alpha\in\Omega^{p}(M)
and
\beta\in\Omega^{p+1}(M)
with support in
M\setminus\partial M
, we have
(d\alpha,\beta)=(\alpha,\delta\beta).
It turns out that, for every
p
-form
\w,

\delta\w=(-1)^{n(p+1)+1}\star d\star\w.

We denote by
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection. For a vector field
X
, we write
X^{\flat}
for the differential
1
-form such that for all vector field
Y
we have
X^{\flat}=g(X,Y).

Given a local orthonormal basis
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, we have the local expressions of
d\w=\sum_{i}e_{i}^{\flat}\wedge\nabla_{e_{i}}\w
and
\delta\w=-\sum_{i}e_{i}\lrcorner\nabla_{e_{i}}\w
, see for instance
[
9
, Proposition 2.61]
. The Hodge-de-Rham Laplacian is the operator denoted
\Delta
from
\Omega^{p}(M)
to itself, defined for all
\w\in\Omega^{p}(M)
by

\Delta\w=d\delta\w+\delta d\w.

A
p
-differential form
\w
is said to be harmonic if
\Delta\w=0.

We have the following properties

1.

The Hodge-de-Rham Laplacian is formally self-adjoint, i.e., for all
\alpha,\ \beta\in\Omega^{p}(M)
with support in
M\setminus\partial M
, we have

2.

\Delta\star=\star\Delta.

We will frequently use the following integration by parts formulas

For

Eventually, for all

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\langle d\w,d\w^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mu_{g}+\int_{M}\langle\delta\w,\delta\w^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mu_{g}

(20)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial M}\langle\nu\lrcorner d\w,\iota^{*}\w^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mu_{g}-\int_{\partial M}\langle\iota^{*}\delta\w,\nu\lrcorner\w^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mu_{g}.

Proof.

Equations (

Replacing

\begin{split}&\int_{M}\langle\Delta^{2}\w,\w^{\prime}\rangle d\mu_{g}=\int_{M}\langle\Delta\w,\Delta\w^{\prime}\rangle d\mu_{g}\\
&+\int_{\partial M}\Big(\langle\nu\lrcorner d\Delta\w,\iota^{*}\w^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\iota^{*}\Delta\w,\nu\lrcorner d\w^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\nu\lrcorner\Delta\w,\iota^{*}\delta\w^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\iota^{*}\delta\Delta\w,\nu\lrcorner\w^{\prime}\rangle\Big)d\mu_{g}.\end{split}

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial M}\langle\iota^{*}\delta\w,\nu\lrcorner\w\rangle\,d\mu_{g}.

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial M}\langle\nu\lrcorner d\Delta\omega,\iota^{*}\omega^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mu_{g}-\int_{\partial M}\langle\iota^{*}\omega,\nu\lrcorner d\Delta\omega^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mu_{g}

2.2.1
Sobolev spaces

We still denote by
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection on the tangent bundle. Let

for smooth sections of

For

2.2.2
Elliptic operators

Definition 2.3
.

[
27
, Chapter 2, Section 10, p.183]

Let
E=\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M
and
A:\Gamma(E)\longrightarrow\Gamma(E)
a linear differential operator of order

.

Its
principal symbol

\sigma_{A}(\xi):E_{x}\longrightarrow E_{x}
is given by
\sigma_{A}(\xi)=\frac{1}{k!}\underbrace{[\cdots[[A,f],f]\cdots f]}_{k\ \text{commutators}}
, where for all

and
\xi\in T^{*}_{x}M
,

is a smooth map such that
d_{x}f=\xi.

The operator

is
elliptic
if and only if for all
x\in M
and
\xi\in T_{x}^{*}M\setminus\{0\}
, its principal symbol is invertible, i.e.,
\det(\sigma_{A}(\xi))\neq 0
.

It follows from
[
27
, p.177]
that if
A
and
B
are differential operators then for all
\xi\in T^{*}M,

\sigma_{A\circ B}(\xi)=\sigma_{A}(\xi)\circ\sigma_{B}(\xi).

Examples 2.1
.

The Hodge-de-Rham Laplacian and bi-Laplacian are elliptic, and their principal symbols are given by:

\sigma_{\Delta}(\xi)=-|\xi|^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma_{\Delta^{2}}(\xi)=|\xi|^{4},

where
\xi\in T_{x}^{*}M
.

For the proof, see
[
27
, p.182]
.

2.2.3

The aim of this paragraph is to give the definition of the ellipticity of a differential boundary problem, in the sense of Shapiro-Lopatinskij, as presented in
[

, Paragraph 1.6]

\begin{cases}A\w=0&\text{in }M,\\

B_{2}\w=0&\text{on }\partial M,\\
\vdots&\vdots\\

(24)

where

is a linear differential operator of order

, and for all

is a linear differential operator. Here

and

The principal symbol

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}:T^{*}_{x}M

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\Endo(E_{x})

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}(\xi)

has locally a polynomial homogeneous expression of order
k
in the coordinates of
\xi.
Therefore, it extends to generalized covectors with values in the algebra of differential operators on

, i.e.,
\tilde{\xi}\in T^{*}_{x}M\otimes(\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}\cdot\partial_{t}),
where the multiplication in

is given by
(a_{1}+b_{1}\partial_{t})(a_{2}+b_{2}\partial_{t})=a_{1}a_{2}+(a_{1}b_{2}+a_{2}b_{1})\partial_{t}+b_{1}b_{2}\partial_{t}^{2}.

Definition 2.4
.

[
12
, Definition 20.1.1]
[
25
, Definition 1.6.1]

The problem (
24
) is said to be

in the sense of Shapiro-Lopatinskij if and only if:

\bullet

2.3

\bullet

For all

\Phi

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{M}^{+}_{A,v}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}E_{j}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(\sigma_{B_{1}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})y,\sigma_{B_{2}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})y,\dots,\sigma_{B_{k}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})y\right)(0)

is an isomorphism,
where
-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat}\in T^{*}_{x}M\otimes(\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}\cdot\partial_{t})
for

and

\mathcal{M}^{+}_{A,v}:=\{\text{bounded solutions }y=y(t)\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{+}\text{ of the ODE }\sigma_{A}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})y=0\}.

(25)

Lemma 2.5
.

In the case of the bi-Laplacian, the space
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\Delta^{2},v}
is given by:

\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\Delta^{2},v}=\spann\left(e^{-|v|t}(at+b)\cdot\w_{0}\mid a,b\in\mathbb{R},\ \w_{0}\in\Lambda^{p}T^{*}_{x}M_{|\partial M}\right)

and its dimension is
2\binom{n}{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in T^{*}M
,
v\in T^{*}\partial M
, and
\nu\in TM_{|\partial M}
.
For
A=\Delta^{2}
in (
25
) we have

\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\Delta^{2},v}:=\{\text{bounded solutions on }\mathbb{R}_{+}\text{ of the ODE }\sigma_{\Delta^{2}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})y=0\},

where
\sigma_{\Delta^{2}}(\xi)=|\xi|^{4}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\Delta^{2}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})

\displaystyle=|-iv+\partial_{t}\nu|^{4}

\displaystyle=(|-iv+\partial_{t}\nu|^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=(\langle-iv+\partial_{t}\nu,-iv+\partial_{t}\nu\rangle)^{2}

\displaystyle=((-i)^{2}\langle v,v\rangle+\langle\partial_{t}\nu,\partial_{t}\nu\rangle-2\underbrace{\langle\underbrace{iv}_{\in T^{*}\partial M},\underbrace{\partial_{t}\nu}_{\perp T^{*}\partial M}\rangle}_{0})^{2}.

Therefore,

\sigma_{\Delta^{2}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})=(-|v|^{2}+(\partial_{t})^{2}\cdot\underbrace{|\nu|^{2}}_{1})^{2}=((\partial_{t})^{2}-|v|^{2})^{2}=\partial_{t}^{4}-2|v|^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}+|v|^{4}.

Now solving the equation
y=y(t):\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{p}T^{*}M
,

(\sigma_{\Delta^{2}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})y)(t)=0

is equivalent to solving

(\partial_{t}^{4}-2|v|^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}+|v|^{4})y=0.

Thus, the equation becomes
y^{(4)}(t)-2|v|^{2}y^{\prime\prime}(t)+|v|^{4}y(t)=0
, which is a linear ODE with constant coefficients. The general solution is given by:

y(t)=(a_{1}t+b_{1})e^{|v|t}\w_{0}+(a_{2}t+b_{2})e^{-|v|t}\w^{\prime}_{0},\quad a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\ \text{and}\ \w_{0},\w^{\prime}_{0}\in\Lambda^{p}T^{*}_{x}M_{|\partial M}.

Since we are looking for a bounded solution, we require that
|y(t)|\leq M
for all
t\geq 0
, which implies
a_{1}=b_{1}=0
, hence

y(t)=(at+b)e^{-|v|t}\w_{0},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{R}\ \text{and}\ \w_{0}\in\Lambda^{p}T^{*}_{x}M_{|\partial M}.

Consequently,

\mathcal{M}_{\Delta^{2},v}^{+}=\spann\left(e^{-|v|t}(at+b)\cdot\w_{0}\mid a,b\in\mathbb{R},\w_{0}\in\Lambda^{p}T^{*}_{x}M_{|\partial M}\right).

To complete the proof of the lemma,
\mathcal{M}_{\Delta^{2},v}^{+}
depends on two parameters
a
and
b
. Moreover,
\w_{0}\in\Lambda^{p}T^{*}_{x}M
, which is a space of dimension
\binom{n}{p}
. Thus, we have

\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\Delta^{2},v}^{+})=2\binom{n}{p}.

∎

2.3
Variational characterizations of classical eigenvalue problems

In this section, we will recall the classical problems on differential forms and their variational characterization, as we will use them later to establish the Kuttler-Sigillito inequalities.

2.3.1
Classical eigenvalue problems on differential forms

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact connected Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial M
. The Dirichlet problem for differential forms is defined as follows:

\begin{cases}\Delta\w=\lambda\w&\text{on }M\\
\w=0&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

(28)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. The Neumann problem for differential forms (with absolute boundary conditions) is defined by

\begin{cases}\Delta\w=\mu\w&\text{on }M\\
\nu\lrcorner\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner d\w=0&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

(29)

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.

For
p\in\{0,\cdots,n\}
, the kernel of the Dirichlet problem is trivial and the kernel of the Neumann problem is
H_{A}^{p}(M)
defined in (
9
). The spectrum of the Laplacian for the Dirichlet and Neumann problems on forms are discrete, consisting respectively of eigenvalues

0<\lambda_{1,p}\leq\lambda_{2,p}\leq\cdots\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq\mu_{1,p}\leq\mu_{2,p}\leq\cdots.

Now, let
p\in\{0,\dots,n\}
. For every
p
-form
\w
on the boundary, according to
[
23
]
(see also
Lemma
3.22
below) there exists a unique
p
-form
\hat{\w}
on
M
solving the following problem:

\begin{cases}\Delta\hat{\w}=0&\text{on }M\\
\iota^{*}\hat{\w}=\w&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner\hat{\w}=0&\text{on }\partial M.\end{cases}

(30)

The differential form
\hat{\w}
is called the tangential harmonic extension of
\w
.

Definition 2.10
.

The Steklov operator
T^{[p]}:\Lambda^{p}(\partial M)\to\Lambda^{p}(\partial M)
is defined by
T^{[p]}(\w)=-\nu\lrcorner d\hat{\w}
.

It is proved in
[
23
, Section 2]
, that the operator
T^{[p]}
is an elliptic, self-adjoint and pseudodifferential operator with a discrete spectrum consisting of eigenvalues

0\leq\sigma_{1,p}\leq\sigma_{2,p}\leq\cdots.

Its kernel is also
H_{A}^{p}(M)
.

3.1
Ellipticity of BSN1

We begin with showing the ellipticity of the problem BSN1 (
10
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with a smooth boundary
\partial M
. The BSN1 (
10
) is elliptic in the sense of Shapiro-Lopatinskij.

Proof.

Let
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
et
B_{4}
be the following maps

\displaystyle B_{1}:\Omega^{p}(M)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma(E_{1}=\Lambda^{p-1}T^{*}\partial M)\quad;

\displaystyle B_{2}:\Omega^{p}(M)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma(E_{2}=\Lambda^{p}T^{*}\partial M)

\displaystyle\w

\displaystyle\longmapsto\nu\lrcorner\w

\displaystyle\w

\displaystyle\longmapsto\nu\lrcorner d\w

\displaystyle B_{3}:\Omega^{p}(M)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma(E_{3}=\Lambda^{p}T^{*}\partial M)\qquad;

\displaystyle B_{4}:\Omega^{p}(M)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma(E_{4}=\Lambda^{p-1}T^{*}\partial M)

\displaystyle\w

\displaystyle\longmapsto\nu\lrcorner\Delta d\w+\ell\iota^{*}\w

\displaystyle\w

\displaystyle\longmapsto\nu\lrcorner\Delta\w+\ell\iota^{*}\delta\w

where
\ell
is a fixed real number. We note that
E_{1}
and
E_{4}
have rank
\binom{n-1}{p-1}
and that
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
have rank
\binom{n-1}{p}
. Therefore, the rank of
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{4}E_{j}
is
2\big(\binom{n-1}{p}+\binom{n-1}{p-1}\big)=2\binom{n}{p}=\dim\mathcal{M}_{\Delta^{2},v}^{+}
. We have to compute the principal symbols of the
B_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq 4
.

Let us fix
\xi\in T^{*}M
and let
f:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be such that
d_{x}f=\xi
. The operator
B_{1}
is of order 0, so its principal symbol is
B_{1}
itself:
\sigma_{B_{1}}(\xi)\w=\nu\lrcorner\w
.

The operator
B_{2}
is of order 1, recall that
\xi
has both a normal and a tangential component, i.e.,
\xi=\xi_{\nu}\nu^{\flat}+\iota^{*}\xi
for all point in
\partial M
. By definition,

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{2}}(\xi)\w

\displaystyle=[B_{2},f]\w

\displaystyle=B_{2}(f\w)-fB_{2}(\w)

\displaystyle=\nu\lrcorner d(f\w)-f\nu\lrcorner d\w

\displaystyle=\nu\lrcorner fd\w+\nu\lrcorner(df\wedge\w)-f\nu\lrcorner d\w

\displaystyle=\nu\lrcorner(df\wedge\w)

\displaystyle=(\nu\lrcorner df)\w-df\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w)

\displaystyle=(\nu\lrcorner\xi)\w-\xi\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\nu}\w-\xi_{\nu}\nu\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w)-\iota^{*}\xi\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\nu}(\w-\nu\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w))-\iota^{*}\xi\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\nu}\iota^{*}\w-\iota^{*}\xi\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w).

The operator
B_{3}
is of order
3
and
\sigma_{B_{3}}(\xi)\w=\big(\sigma_{\nu\lrcorner}\circ\sigma_{\Delta}\circ\sigma_{d}\big)(\xi)\w
. We have
\sigma_{d}(\xi)\w=\xi\wedge\w
, see
[
27
, p.181]
.

Therefore
\sigma_{\Delta\circ d}=-|\xi|^{2}\xi\wedge\w
. Since
\nu\lrcorner
is of order 0, we get

\sigma_{B_{3}}(\xi)\w=-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\nu\lrcorner(\xi\wedge\w).

Finally,
B_{4}
is of order 2 and

\sigma_{B_{4}}(\xi)\w=\sigma_{\nu\lrcorner}\circ\sigma_{\Delta}(\xi)\w=-\nu\lrcorner\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\w=-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\nu\lrcorner\w.

We now need to evaluate all of the principal symbols for
\xi=(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})
with
v\in T^{*}M\setminus\{0\}
and apply it to
y(t)=e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}(at+b)\w_{0}
. For
\sigma_{B_{1}}
we get

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{1}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)

\displaystyle=\nu\lrcorner y

\displaystyle=\nu\lrcorner(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\w_{0}

\displaystyle=(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\nu\lrcorner\w_{0},

then
\sigma_{B_{1}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)(0)=b\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}.

Similarly for
\sigma_{B_{2}}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{2}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\iota^{*}y-\iota^{*}(-iv+\partial_{t})\wedge(\nu\lrcorner y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\iota^{*}(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\w_{0}+\iota^{*}(iv)\wedge(\nu\lrcorner(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\w_{0})

\displaystyle=\iota^{*}ae^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\w_{0}-\iota^{*}(at+b)\lvert v\rvert e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\w_{0}

\displaystyle\quad+i(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\iota^{*}v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}).

Thus

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{2}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)(0)

\displaystyle=a\iota^{*}\w_{0}-b\lvert v\rvert\iota^{*}\w_{0}+ib\iota^{*}v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})

\displaystyle=\iota^{*}\w_{0}(a-b\lvert v\rvert)+ib\iota^{*}v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}).

Next for
\sigma_{B_{3}}
we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{3}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)

\displaystyle=-\lvert-iv+\partial_{t}\rvert^{2}\nu\lrcorner\big((-iv+\partial_{t})\wedge y\big)

\displaystyle=-\lvert-iv+\partial_{t}\nu\rvert^{2}\nu\lrcorner\big((-iv+\partial_{t})\wedge(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}.\w_{0}\big)

\displaystyle=-\langle-iv+\partial_{t}\nu,-iv+\partial_{t}\nu\rangle.

\displaystyle\qquad\big[\nu\lrcorner\big(-i(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}v\wedge\w_{0}+\partial_{t}(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\nu\wedge\w_{0}\big)\big]

\displaystyle=(\lvert v\rvert^{2}-\partial_{t}^{2})\big[-i(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\big(\underbrace{(\nu\lrcorner v)}_{0}.\w_{0}-(v\wedge\nu)\lrcorner\w_{0}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+(be^{-\lvert v\rvert t}-\lvert v\rvert(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t})\nu\lrcorner(\nu\wedge\w_{0})\big]

\displaystyle=(\lvert v\rvert^{2}-\partial_{t}^{2})e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\big[i(at+b)v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})+b-\lvert v\rvert(at+b)\nu\lrcorner(\nu\wedge\w_{0})\big]

\displaystyle=\lvert v\rvert^{2}e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\big[i(at+b)v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})+b-\lvert v\rvert(at+b)\nu\lrcorner(\nu\wedge\w_{0})\big]

\displaystyle\quad-\lvert v\rvert^{2}e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\big[i(at+b)v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})+b-\lvert v\rvert(at+b)\nu\lrcorner(\nu\wedge\w_{0})\big]

\displaystyle\quad+2\lvert v\rvert e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\big((iav\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})-\lvert v\rvert a\nu\lrcorner(\nu\wedge\w_{0})\big)

\displaystyle=2\lvert v\rvert e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\big((iav\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})-\lvert v\rvert a\nu\lrcorner(\nu\wedge\w_{0})\big).

Noting that
\nu\lrcorner(\nu\wedge\w_{0})=(\nu\lrcorner\nu)\w_{0}-\nu\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})=\w_{0}-\nu\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})=\iota^{*}\w_{0}
, we obtain that

\sigma_{B_{3}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)=2|v|e^{-|v|t}a\left(iv\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})-|v|\iota^{*}\w_{0}\right),

and thus

\sigma_{B_{3}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)(0)=2|v|a\left(iv\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})-|v|\iota^{*}\w_{0}\right).

Finally for
\sigma_{B_{4}},

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{4}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)

\displaystyle=-\nu\lrcorner\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}y

\displaystyle=-\nu\lrcorner\lvert-iv+\partial_{t}\nu\rvert^{2}(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\w_{0}

\displaystyle=-\nu\lrcorner(\partial_{t}^{2}-\lvert v\rvert^{2})(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\w_{0}

\displaystyle=\lvert v\rvert^{2}(at+b)e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}-\nu\lrcorner(-2a\lvert v\rvert e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}+(at+b)\lvert v\rvert^{2}e^{-\lvert v\rvert t})\w_{0}

\displaystyle=2a\lvert v\rvert e^{-\lvert v\rvert t}\nu\lrcorner\w_{0},

we conclude
\sigma_{B_{4}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)(0)=2a\lvert v\rvert\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}.

Based on the calculations above, we obtain the following map:

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\Delta^{2},v}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=1}^{4}E_{j}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\longmapsto\Phi(y)

such that

\Phi(y)=\big(b\nu\lrcorner\w_{0},(a-b\lvert v\rvert)\iota^{*}\w_{0}+ib\iota^{*}v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}),2\lvert v\rvert\big(iav\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})-\lvert v\rvert a\iota^{*}\w_{0}\big),2a\lvert v\rvert\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}\big).

To end the proof, we must show that
\Phi
is an isomorphism. Since both source and target spaces have the same finite dimension, it is enough to show that
\Phi
is injective. Suppose that
\Phi(y)=0,
then

\displaystyle\begin{cases}b\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}&=0\qquad(1)\\
(a-b|v|)\iota^{*}\w_{0}+ib\iota^{*}v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})&=0\qquad(2)\\
2\lvert v\rvert\big(iav\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})-\lvert v\rvert a\iota^{*}\w_{0}\big)&=0\qquad(3)\\
2a\lvert v\rvert\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}&=0\qquad(4)\end{cases}

If
a
and
b
are zero, then
y
is zero. If
a
or
b
are non-zero, then from (1) and (4), we deduce that
\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}=0
. Substituting this result into (2) and (3), we find that
\iota^{*}\w_{0}=0
. Consequently,
\w_{0}=0
, which implies that
y=0
. Hence,
\Phi
is injective and the problem (
10
) is elliptic.

∎

3.3
Variational characterization of eigenvalues

In this section, we will provide variational characterizations of the eigenvalues of the BSN1 problem.

Theorem 3.17
.

A variational characterization of the first strictly positive eigenvalue of BSN1 is given by

\ell_{1,p}=\min\left\{\frac{\lVert\Delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\iota^{*}\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}+\lVert\iota^{*}\delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}\ |\ \w\in\check{H}_{N,1}^{2}(M),\ \iota^{*}\w\neq 0\ \text{or}\ \iota^{*}\delta\w\neq 0\ \text{on}\ \partial M\right\}.

Proof.

\ell_{1,p}=\frac{\lVert\Delta\w_{1,p}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\iota^{*}\w_{1,p}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}+\lVert\iota^{*}\delta\w_{1,p}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}.

p_{V_{1}}:\check{H}_{N,1}^{2}(M)\longrightarrow V_{1}
be the orthogonal projection with respect to the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}
. We define
\mathcal{U}:=p_{V_{1}}(\mathcal{V}_{k})
hence
\mathcal{U}\subset V_{1}
and
\dim\mathcal{U}=k
. Then, by
Theorem
3.24
, we have
\ell_{k,p}\leq\underset{0\neq u\in\mathcal{U}}{\sup}\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert u\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}
. Now, let
\w\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\backslash H_{0}^{2}(M)
, we decompose
\w
as follows:
\w=p_{V_{1}}(\w)+\w-p_{V_{1}}(\w)
where
p_{V_{1}}(\w)\in\mathcal{U}\subset V_{1}
and
\w-p_{V_{1}}(\w)\in V_{1}^{\perp_{(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}}}\subset\mathcal{U}^{\perp_{(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}}}
. Thus, we have
\lVert\w\rVert_{V}^{2}=\lVert p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{V}^{2}+\lVert\w-p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{V}^{2}
and since
\w-p_{V_{1}}(\w)\in H_{0}^{2}(M)
, we get
\lVert\w-p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}=0

\lVert\w\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}=\lVert p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}+\lVert\w-p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}
.

We now have:

\frac{\lVert\w\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert\w\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}=\frac{\lVert p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{V}^{2}+\lVert\w-p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}\geq\frac{\lVert p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}.

Therefore,

\underset{0\neq\w\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\backslash H_{0}^{2}(M)}{\sup}\frac{\lVert\w\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert\w\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}\geq\underset{0\neq\w\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\backslash H_{0}^{2}(M)}{\sup}\frac{\lVert p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert p_{V_{1}}(\w)\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}=\underset{u\in p_{V_{1}}(\mathcal{V}_{k})}{\sup}\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert u\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}\geq\ell_{k,p}.

This implies that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\ell_{k,p}\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{V}_{k}\subset\check{H}_{N,1}^{2}(M)\\
\dim(\mathcal{V}_{k}/H_{0}^{2}(M)\cap\mathcal{V}_{k})=k\end{subarray}}\ \underset{u\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\backslash H_{0}^{2}(M)}{\sup}\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert u\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}.

Finally, let
\overline{V}_{k}=\spann(\w_{1},\cdots,\w_{k})\subset V_{1}
. Since
\overline{V}_{k}
is a subspace of
V_{1}
orthogonal to
H_{0}^{2}(M)
, we have
\overline{V}_{k}\cap H_{0}^{2}(M)=\{0\}
. Therefore,

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{V}_{k}\subset\check{H}_{N,1}^{2}(M)\\
\dim(\mathcal{V}_{k}/H_{0}^{2}(M)\cap\mathcal{V}_{k})=k\end{subarray}}\ \underset{\w\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\backslash H_{0}^{2}(M)}{\sup}\frac{\lVert\w\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert\w\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}\leq\underset{u\in\mathcal{V}_{k}\backslash H_{0}^{2}(M)}{\sup}\frac{\lVert u\rVert_{V}^{2}}{\lVert u\rVert_{W_{1}}^{2}}=\ell_{k,p}.

This concludes the proof.

∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{1}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)(0)

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{2}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)(0)

\displaystyle=\iota^{*}\w_{0}(a-b\lvert v\rvert)+ib\iota^{*}v\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{B_{3}}(-iv+\partial_{t}\nu^{\flat})(y)(0)

\displaystyle=2\lvert v\rvert a(iv\wedge(\nu\lrcorner\w_{0})-\lvert v\rvert\iota^{*}\w_{0}),

\displaystyle\leq\textit{l}_{k}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\w\in\mathcal{V}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}{\lVert d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}

\displaystyle=\textit{l}_{k}\left(\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\w\in\mathcal{V}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\textit{l}_{k}}{\sigma_{1}},

from which we deduce using
Lemma
6.1
that
\mu_{k}\sigma_{1}\leq\textit{l}_{k}\leq\mathbf{l}_{k}.

Let us next show (
83
). Using the same definitions above of
\mathcal{V}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\w\in\mathcal{V}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\tilde{\w}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}\\
\tilde{\w}_{|\partial M}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert d\tilde{\w}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\delta\tilde{\w}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\iota^{*}\tilde{\w}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\w\in\mathcal{V}\\
\w_{|\partial M}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\iota^{*}\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}\times\frac{\lVert\Delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\Delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}

\displaystyle\leq\textit{l}_{k}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\w\in\mathcal{V}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\Delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}

\displaystyle=\textit{l}_{k}\left(\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\w\in\mathcal{V}\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert\Delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\textit{l}_{k}}{\mu_{1}},

we conclude that
\mu_{1}\sigma_{k}\leq\textit{l}_{k}\leq\mathbf{l}_{k}
using

6.1
.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary
\partial M
. The following inequality holds between the eigenvalues of the Steklov, BSD, BSN2 and BSN3 problems for differential forms:

q_{1}\sigma_{1}^{2}<\textit{l}_{1}\leq\mathbf{l}_{1}.

(84)

Proof.

Using the characterizations (
38
) and (

) of the classical Steklov problem, we have:

\sigma^{2}_{1}\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\lrcorner\w=0\\
\w\perp_{L^{2}(\partial M)}H_{A}^{p}(M)\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\delta\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}{\lVert\iota^{*}\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}\times\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\lrcorner\w=0\\

\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\lrcorner\w=0\\
\Delta\w=0\\

Multiplying by
q_{1}
and using

given in (

), we obtain:

q_{1}\sigma_{1}^{2}\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta\w=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert\iota^{*}\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}{\lVert\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}\times\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\lrcorner\w=0\\
\Delta\w=0\\
\w\perp_{L^{2}(\partial M)}H_{A}^{p}(M)\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert\nu\lrcorner d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}{\lVert\iota^{*}\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}

\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\lrcorner\w=0\\
\Delta\w=0\\
\w\perp_{L^{2}(\partial M)}H_{A}^{p}(M)\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert\nu\lrcorner d\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial M)}}{\lVert\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}}\leq\tilde{\textit{l}_{1}}=\textit{l}_{1}.

Let us next show that the inequality is strict. We first show the following lemma:

Lemma 6.7
.

If
q_{1}\sigma_{1}^{2}=\textit{l}_{1}
, then there exists

eigenform associated to both
\textit{l}_{1}

\sigma_{1}

Proof.

be an
\textit{l}_{1}

, we show that
\w
is also a
\sigma_{1}
-eigenform so it satisfies the following problems:

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}\w=0&\text{on }M\\
\nu\lrcorner\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner d\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner\Delta\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner d\Delta\w+\textit{l}\,\iota^{*}\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\end{cases}\quad;\quad\begin{cases}\Delta\w=0&\text{on }M\\
\nu\lrcorner d\w=-\sigma_{1}\iota^{*}\w&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner\w=0&\text{on }\partial M.\end{cases}

∎

Let us finish the proof of

. In this case,
\nu\lrcorner\w=0
and
\iota^{*}\w=0
, so
\w_{|\partial M}=0
. Moreover, since

, we have

on

according to
[
1
]
. This leads to a contradiction, and the inequality is strict. Now, using

, we get

.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary
\partial M
. The following inequality holds between the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet, Neumann, BSD and BSN3 problems for differential forms:

\mu_{1}^{-1}<\lambda_{1}^{-1}+(q_{1}\textit{l}_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(85)

Proof.

Let
\w_{1}\in\Omega^{p}(M)
such that
\nu\lrcorner d\w_{1}=0
,
\nu\lrcorner\w_{1}=0
, and
\w_{1}\perp_{L^{2}(\partial M)}H_{A}^{p}(M)
. Let
v\in\Omega^{p}(M)
satisfy the following conditions:

\begin{cases}\Delta v=\Delta\w_{1}&\text{on}\ M\\
v_{|\partial M}=0,\end{cases}

and which has a solution due to
Theorem
2.6
. Let
u\in\Omega^{p}(M)
such that
u=\w_{1}-v
, so we have
\Delta u=\Delta\w_{1}-\Delta v=0
. So we decompose
\w_{1}=u+v
such that
\Delta u=0
and
v_{|\partial M}=0
.
On the one hand, we have

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}\leq(q_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M)}.

Since
v_{|\partial M}=0,
we get

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}\leq(q_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M)}.

(86)

On the other hand, by multiplying (
32
) and (
33
) we get
\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}\leq{\lambda_{1}^{-2}}\lVert\Delta v\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}.
Now
\Delta v=\Delta\w_{1}
as
\Delta u=0,
so that

\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}\leq{\lambda_{1}^{-1}}\lVert\Delta\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}.

(87)

Thus, using (
86
) and (
87
) we get

\displaystyle\lVert\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq(q_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial M)}+(\lambda_{1}^{-1})\lVert\Delta\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq(q_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\textit{l}_{1}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\Delta\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}+\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lVert\Delta\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda_{1}^{-1}+q_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\textit{l}_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\lVert\Delta\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}.

(88)

Now let
\w
be a
\mu_{1}
-eigenform for the Neumann problem (
29
), so we have
\lVert\Delta\w\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}=\mu_{1}\lVert\w\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}
. We decompose
\w
as follows,
\w=\w_{0}+\w_{1}
such that
\w_{0}\in H_{A}^{p}(M)
and
\w_{1}\perp_{L^{2}(\partial M)}\w_{0}
, then
\Delta\w=\Delta\w_{1}
. We perform an integration by parts (
20
), we obtain
(\Delta\w_{1},\w_{0})_{L^{2}(M)}=0.
As we said before,
\Delta\w=\Delta\w_{1},
then
\Delta\w_{1}=\mu_{1}(\w_{1}+\w_{0}),
so

0=(\Delta\w_{1},\w_{0})_{L^{2}(M)}=\mu_{1}(\w_{1},\w_{0})_{L^{2}(M)}+\mu_{1}\lVert\w_{0}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}.

Hence we get
(\w_{1},\w_{0})_{L^{2}(M)}=-\lVert\w_{0}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}.
Using the calculations above, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\w\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\lVert\w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}+2(\w_{0},\w_{1})_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\lVert\w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}-2\lVert\w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=\lVert\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}-\lVert\w_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}.

(89)

Going back to (
88
) and using (
89
) and that
\Delta\w=\Delta\w_{1}
, we get

\lVert\w\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}\leq\lVert\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}\leq\lVert\Delta\w_{1}\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}[(q_{1}\textit{l}_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{-1}]=\lVert\Delta\w\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}[(q_{1}\textit{l}_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{-1}].

Which implies finally that

\mu_{1}^{-1}\leq\lambda_{1}^{-1}+(q_{1}\textit{l}_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

since
\lVert\Delta\w\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}=\mu_{1}\lVert\w\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}
. Let us now show that the inequality is strict.
Then

\lVert\w\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}=\left(\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}\right)^{2},

so

\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+2(u,v)_{L^{2}(M)}=\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}+2\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}\lVert v\rVert_{L^{2}(M)},

thus
u
and
v
are collinear, either
v=ku
or
u=kv
, where
k\in\mathbb{R}
. However,
\w
is an
\mu_{1}
-eigenform, so it satisfies the following conditions:

\begin{cases}\Delta\w=\mu_{1}\w&\text{on }M\\
\nu\lrcorner\w=0&\text{on }\partial M\\
\nu\lrcorner d\w=0&\text{on }\partial M,\end{cases}

thus
k\Delta v=\Delta u=0
, contradiction. When
u=kv
there are two cases: the first case is if
k=0
, then
u=0
, so
\w_{|\partial M}=0
and
\w
is then associated to
\lambda_{1}
, which implies
\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1}
, hence
(\underbrace{\textit{l}_{1}q_{1}}_{>0})^{-1}=0
, a contradiction. The second case is if
k\neq 0
, then
\Delta v=0
, so
\Delta\w=0
, and hence
\w=0
, a contradiction. Therefore, the inequality (
85
) is strict.
∎

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with smooth boundary
\partial M
. The following inequality holds between the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet, Neumann, Steklov, and BSD problems for differential forms:

\mu_{1}^{-1}<\lambda_{1}^{-1}+(q_{1}\sigma_{1})^{-1}.

(90)

Proof.

Using the inequality (

\sigma_{1}\leq\textit{l}_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}q_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

q_{1}\sigma_{1}\leq\textit{l}_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}q_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\mu_{1}^{-1}\leq\lambda_{1}^{-1}+(q_{1}\sigma_{1})^{-1}.

Since the two inequalities (

) and (

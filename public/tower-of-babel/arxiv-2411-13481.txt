Residual Intersections and Schubert Varieties
Sara Angela Filippini, Xianglong Ni, Jacinta Torres, Jerzy Weyman

Abstract
. Inspired by the work of Ulrich [ Ulr90 ] and Huneke–Ulrich [ HU88 ] , we describe a pattern to show that the ideals of certain opposite embedded Schubert varieties (defined by this pattern) arise by taking residual intersections of two (geometrically linked) opposite Schubert varieties (which we called Ulrich pairs in [ FTW23 ] ). This pattern is uniform for the ADE types. Some of the free resolutions of the Schubert varieties in question are important for the structure of finite free resolutions. Our proof is representation theoretical and uniform for our pattern, however it is possible to derive our results using case-by-case analysis and the aid of a computer.

13C99; 13H10.

2.
Commutative Algebra basics

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})

For a proper (resp. homogeneous) ideal

An ideal

(resp. an

(resp. if

Furthermore, if

\dim R=n

be the

of

. We say that

. The

\mathrm{ht}(I)
of

is the minimum of the heights of the prime ideals in

that contain it. The height of a

ideal is simply the codimension of the variety defined by it. A proper ideal

in

is called

if
\mathrm{ht}(P)=\mathrm{ht}(I)
for every associated prime

of

. As

for any proper ideal

.

2.1.
Linkage

Let

be ideals. The
colon ideal
or

is

and

. If

is Cohen–Macaulay and

are linked, then

and

are unmixed of the same height

. A link is called

\mathrm{ht}(I+J)\geq g+1
. In this case,

(2.0.1)

\displaystyle(a)=I\cap J.

Moreover, if (
2.0.1
) holds,

and

are both unmixed of the same height and have no common prime components, then they are (geometrically) linked. An ideal

is said to be

if it is in the linkage class of a complete intersection. If

, then

is perfect if and only if it is licci
[

,
Gae52
,
PS74
]
. Only the “if” implication holds when

. Various other classes of licci ideals have been studied in recent decades
[

,
GNW22
,
Ulr90
,
HU87
]
.

2.2.
Residual Intersections

Let
R
above be additionally Cohen–Macaulay,
I\subset R
an ideal,
g=\mathrm{ht}(I)
and

. We say that a proper ideal

. Notice that this condition implies that
\mathrm{ht}(A)=g
. If

is unmixed, then the concept of

-residual intersection is the same as linkage.

In
[

]
Huneke proved that the ideal

, generated by the maximal order minors of a generic

matrix

, is the residual intersection of a height
2
perfect ideal.

Example 2.1
.

For
r\leq s
let
A
be an

-matrix and

the submatrix given by its right-most

I(X)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s-r+1}:I_{s}(A))
is a residual intersection of
I_{s}(A)
. Note that the codimension of the ideal

is
(s-(s-1))\cdot(s+1-(s-1))=2
. Denote by

the
s\times s
minor of

obtained by deleting the

-th column.

Kustin and Ulrich in
[

, Theorem 10.2]
described residual intersections of grade
3
Gorenstein ideals.

Example 2.2
.

Let

be a generic
(2r+1)\times(2r+1)

a generic

matrix with

. Denote by
p_{1},\ldots,p_{2r+1}
the

In Theorem
1.2
we extend Kustin–Ulrich’s result to ADE types, where we naturally go beyond codimension

Let

Basics on Schubert Varieties

Let

be the rank of

be the opposite Borel subgroup of

as a lattice. Moreover, let

be the set of roots, let

be the Weyl group of symmetries of

and

be the choice of positive roots corresponding to

, respectively

be the corresponding parabolic subgroup of
G
respectively of

stabilizing
\omega_{k}
. We consider the irreducible, finite dimensional complex representation of

of highest weight
\omega_{k}
and denote it by

. Now the quotient

has the structure of a smooth projective variety. Moreover, it comes together with a canonical embedding

given by

, where

is the highest weight vector. We will call the coordinates in
V(\omega_{k})^{*}

Plücker coordinates
, (borrowing the name from the coordinates of
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n})
, which with our notation will be the Plücker coordinates in type

). The
extremal Plücker coordinates
in

are those dual to the

; we write

for the coordinate dual to

. To define opposite Schubert varieties in

The opposite Schubert cells are the

is the codimension of the opposite Schubert variety

. The cell
C^{id}
is called the opposite big open cell. We denote by

the corresponding intersection.

3.1.
Defining equations of opposite Schubert varieties and their unions

The defining ideals

Scheme-theoretically, opposite Schubert varieties and their unions are defined linearly as subvarieties of

Theorem 3.3
.

The scheme-theoretic defining ideal of the union of two opposite Schubert varieties
I(X^{\tau}\cup X^{\nu})
is generated in degree one by the dual Plücker coordinates vanishing on both

and

Proof.

We refer the reader to
[

for the necessary background on standard monomials. Let

. By the proof
[

]

we know that the kernel of the restriction map

\mathbb{C}[G/P_{k}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[X^{\tau}\cup X^{\tau^{\prime}}]

has as basis the set of all standard monomials on

that are neither standard on

nor on

if and only if its maximal factor is. Therefore, standard monomial belongs to

if and only if the maximal factor of the monomial is in

is generated by its degree one elements.

∎

6.

For type

given by consecutive (non-repeating) reflections on the Dynkin diagram, with
i=1,3,5,6,...,n
. We will abbreviate
X^{w}
as just
X^{i}
as before. We will denote the corresponding opposite Demazure module by
M
.

6.1.
E_{6}

6.1.1.
Codimension three

For
i=3
we have the following decomposition

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{i}}(V(\omega_{2}))={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\omega_{6})}\oplus{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0}V(\omega_{1}+\omega_{4})}

\displaystyle\oplus V(\omega_{2}+\omega_{6})\oplus V(\omega_{1}+\omega_{5})\oplus V(\omega_{2})

\displaystyle\oplus V(2\omega_{2})

The Plücker coordinates vanishing on
X^{3}
are those dual to
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\omega_{6})}
. These are just the extremal Plücker coordinates
p_{w}
where
w
does not involve the reflection
s_{3}
. Now,
w\cdot v
is the lowest weight vector for
{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0}V(\omega_{1}+\omega_{4})}
. Since the entirety of this
i
- graded component is. in

, all higher components are in
M
as well.

Codimension four

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{i}}(V(\omega_{2}))={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\omega_{3})}

\displaystyle\oplus{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathbb{C}}

The extremal Plücker coordinates are dual to
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}V(\omega_{3})}

A Comparative Study to Evaluate the Pre and Post-Operative Electrocardiographic and Echocardiographic Changes in Children with Tetralogy of Fallot Undergoing Modified Blalock–Taussig–Thomas Shunt Surgery
Authors: KG Krithika, Minati Choudhury, Sandeep Chauhan, Arindam Choudhury, Rama Krishnan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The modified Blalock–Taussig–Thomas shunt (mBTTS) is a palliative surgical procedure for tetralogy of Fallot (TOF). It enhances left heart filling, which may cause alterations observable in electrocardiography (ECG) and trans thoracic echocardiography (TTE).Aim:This study aims to assess if the ECG and TTE changes in TOF children undergoing mBTT shunt surgery reflect the improved pulmonary blood flow.Setting:Tertiary care hospital.Design:A prospective observational study.Material and Method:Thirty-five (35) children diagnosed with TOF, who underwent mBTTS surgery, were included in this study. Pre-operative assessments included a 12-lead ECG and TTE, which were repeated 6 h postoperatively. Hemodynamic and arterial blood gas parameters were also recorded. Statistical analysis was done using a paired t-test.Results:In ECG, pre-operative P axis and QRS axis were found to be 62.05 ± 20.82 and 139.88 ± 22.52, respectively, and they were reduced to 54.94 ± 18.91 (P value of 0.007) and 107.68 ± 27.18 (P value < 0.001) postoperatively. Other parameters, including P wave duration, P wave amplitude in augmented vector lead (aVL), Morris and Marcuz index, were significantly increased in the post-operative ECG. Pre-operative left atrial (LA) reservoir volume and left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LV-EDV) were found to be 5.42 ± 1.28 mL and 10.54 ± 1.78 mL, respectively, and they were increased to 10.44 ± 2.73 (P value ≤ 0.001) and 11.40 ± 1.73 (P value ≤ 0.001) postoperatively.Conclusions:Changes observed in the hemodynamics, blood gas parameters, ECG, and TTE are consistent with improved pulmonary blood flow.

INTRODUCTION

Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) is one of the commonest cyanotic congenital heart malformations.[1] The modified Blalock–Taussig–Thomas shunt (mBTTS) is done as a palliative procedure for TOF children with contraindications to primary repair that includes a weight less than 3 kg, associated anomalies, an inadequate sized pulmonary artery, and a pulmonary annulus. In this procedure, a subclavian artery-to-pulmonary artery shunt is created to improve the pulmonary blood flow (PBF), thus improving the oxygenation, aiding the growth of pulmonary vasculature, and improving left heart filling.[2]

ECG findings in patients with TOF include right axis deviation, tall R waves in the right precordial leads (V1 and V2) due to RVH, a sudden transition of the QRS complex morphology in leads V1 and V2, and right atrial enlargement (RAE) as evidenced by tall P waves (P pulmonale). In the newborn having TOF, the ECG may be normal, but over the first few weeks of life, normal regression of right ventricular (RV) preponderance is not seen.[34]

Echocardiography in TOF can depict the site and number of septal defects, degree of septal malalignment, subpulmonary and pulmonary valvular obstruction, anatomy of proximal PAs and RV, tricuspid valve and pulmonary valve size, and qualitative function.[5]

Post-intracardiac repair ECG changes in TOF patients have been documented, including a decrease in P wave amplitude and an increase in R wave amplitude in lead V6, reflecting a decreased load on the RA and increased left ventricular (LV) activity following surgery. And also, patients who had right axis deviation before surgery were found to have balanced axis and evidence of combined ventricular hypertrophy postoperatively.[6] However, ECG changes after the mBTT Shunt have not been reported yet.

We have therefore sought to study the changes in vector forces of the left atrium (LA) and LV in ECG, as well as the LA reservoir volume and LV end-diastolic volume (EDV) in echocardiography, following mBTT shunt, in relation to the pre-operative ECG and echocardiographic measurements.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

This was a prospective observational study conducted in a tertiary care hospital. This study aims to evaluate if the ECG and TTE changes in TOF children undergoing mBTT shunt surgery are consistent with improved PBF. Sample size was calculated based on the findings of a former study,[7] and with a 95% power and a type I error of 0.05, the required sample size was 35. Sample size was calculated using the G Power software version 3.1.9.4.

Thirty-five patients aged from 6 months to 2 years, who were diagnosed with TOF with inadequate pulmonary arteries and who underwent elective mBTTS surgery, were included in this study after obtaining institutional ethics committee approval and written informed consent from the children’s parents. Patients with pre-existing arrhythmias, those requiring emergency surgery, those receiving pre-operative inotropic support or mechanical ventilatory support, and patients with alternative PBF, like aorto-pulmonary collaterals, patent ductus arteriosus, were excluded from this study. Patients with major adverse events after the surgery, including cardiac arrest, requirement of extra-corporeal membrane oxygenator support (ECMO), re-exploration, obstructed mBTT shunt, or overshunting, were excluded from this study.

Methodology

During the pre-anesthesia check-up, the patient underwent physical examination, and all investigations were reviewed. A 12-lead ECG (at a speed of 25 mm/sec) was obtained, and trans thoracic echocardiography (TTE) was done by a senior cardiologist, using S8-3 or S12-4 TTE probe and ultrasound machine (GE Vivid E95 model: GE Vingmed Ultrasound AS, Horten, Norway). LV-EDV and LA reservoir volume were obtained from 2D-TTE, Apical 4-Chamber (A-4C) view by modified Simpson’s method.

On the day of surgery, the child was fasted as per American Society of Anaesthesiology (ASA) guidelines. Intravenous (IV) fluids were started at 2 mL/kg/h. In the operating room, standard monitors, ECG, pulse-oximetry (Spo2), and non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP), were monitored. The patients were induced with Inj.Ketamine 2 mg/kg, Inj. Fentanyl 1–2 mcg/kg, and Inj.Rocuronium 1 mg/kg body weight. After intubation, an invasive arterial line and central venous catheter were placed.

Hemodynamic parameters, including heart rate (HR), systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic (DBP), and mean blood pressure (MAP), oxygen saturation (SPO2), and arterial blood gas values, including pH, bicarbonate (HCO3), partial pressure of oxygen (PO2), and carbon-dioxide (PCO2), were measured before the surgery. Baseline blood gas analysis was done with a ventilatory setting of FiO2 50%, tidal volume (TV) 6–8 mL/kg, and a peak end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) of 5 cmH2O.

The mBTT shunt was done through thoracotomy in 33 patients and sternotomy in two patients. Sternotomy was done in two of the patients who had small PAs to assess if PA plasty is required and to decide the side of the shunt. Patients did not require a PA plasty. The shunt was placed on the side with better-sized pulmonary artery to ensure adequate PBF. Right-sided mBTTS was done in all the patients, anastomosing the right subclavian artery (SCA) with the right pulmonary artery. Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) graft size 3 mm diameter was used for infants less than 3 kg, 3.5 mm graft for children weighing 3 kg to 5 kg, and 4 mm graft for children above 5 kg. Before partially clamping the SCA artery, heparin 100 U/kg was given to achieve an activated coagulation time of about 200s. Dobutamine 5 mcg/kg/min infusion was started for all the cases and titrated to maintain a SBP of >80 mm Hg. The aim is to improve systemic oxygen delivery without causing excessive PBF. None of the patients required any additional inotropes. Fluid management was delicately balanced between maintaining adequate hydration to prevent shunt thrombosis and avoiding fluid overload, which can lead to pulmonary overcirculation and heart failure.

Hemodynamic parameters, ABG analysis, 12-lead ECG, and TTE were again repeated at the 6th hour postoperatively, after shifting to the CTVS-intensive care unit (ICU). TTE was done by the same pediatric cardiologist, using the same echo probe and ultrasound machine. Post-operative assessment was done with a FiO2 50%, TV 6–8 mL/kg, and PEEP 5 cmH2O in all the patients.

Data measurements

From both pre-operative and post-operative 12 lead ECG, P wave duration in lead II, interpeak duration (IPD) of notched P wave also called as P mitrale in lead II, area of negative P terminal force in lead V1 (NPTF-V1) (Morris index), P/PR segment ratio in lead II (Marcuz index), Positive P terminal force (PPTF) in lead aVL, depth of S wave in lead V1, R wave height in V4 and V5 were measured. P wave axis and QRS axis were calculated by the hexaxial reference system.

Left atrial enlargement (LAE) in ECG was defined by any one of the following criteria[89101112]: (1) P wave duration in any lead > 0.12s, (2) P mitrale with IPD > 0.04s, (3) P wave axis < −30°, (4) Morris index > 0.04s·mm, (5) Marcuz index > 1.6, or (6) PPTF in lead aVL > 0.5 mm.

Left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), as defined by Sokolow and Lyon’s criteria,[13] which includes the sum of S wave depth in V1 and R wave height in V4 or V5, more than 35 mm was calculated.

The shunt flow was assessed using TTE from the suprasternal view in all the patients. The shunt patency was verified through the Doppler trace that displayed a continuous flow pattern during both systole and diastole.[14] LA reservoir volume and LV-EDV were obtained from 2D-TTE, A-4C view by modified Simpson’s method.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed with SPSS (version 25.0, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were expressed as numbers and percentages, while continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Continuous data were compared using Student’s t-test (normality) for paired data. A P value < 0.05 in the analysis was considered statistical significance.

DISCUSSION

The mBTT shunt is a palliative surgical procedure to treat patients with cyanotic congenital heart diseases like TOF, who are not suitable for complete repair. The function of an mBTT shunt can be assessed clinically through palpation and auscultation. A palpable thrill on the side of the shunt that radiates to the infraclavicular region and a continuous murmur heard best over the same subclavicular region indicates that the systemic-to-pulmonary shunt works. TTE and computed tomography angiography are utilized to quantify the shunted blood flow.[15]

In our study, on ECG analysis, the post-operative mean QRS axis and mean P wave axis were reduced significantly. These changes indicated that the net cardiac vector had moved toward the left side because of increased left heart filling. The post-operative values of P wave duration, the PPTF amplitude in lead aVL, the Morris index, and the Marcuz index had increased significantly. Connie et al.[9] validated the accuracy of ECG criteria for atrial enlargement with cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR). He observed that the presence of any one of the ECG criteria among P wave duration >110 ms, P mitrale, P wave axis <30’, NPTF in V1 >0. 04 mm.s, or PPTF in aVL >0.5 mm was 90% sensitive but not specific for anatomical LAE. Individual criteria, including P mitrale, P wave axis < 30’ or NPTF in V1 >0. 04mm.s, were 88–99% specific, although not sensitive. In our study, except for P mitrale, all other criteria showed a significant change in the post-operative ECG, pointing toward LAE.[89101112] All these changes indicate increased blood flow entering the LA, secondary to increased PBF following the shunt surgery.

Assessment of LV filling in our study by Sokolow–Lyon index (SV1 +RV5 and SV1 +RV6) showed no significant change postoperatively (P value = 0.332 and 0.358, respectively).

Milton et al.[16] studied 100 TOF patients who underwent aortic-pulmonary anastomosis. Prior to surgery, the ECG of every patient showed an RVH pattern. On long-term (11–13 years) follow-up, one-third of the patients had maintained this typical RVH pattern, and two-thirds of the patients showed combined ventricular hypertrophy in the ECG. These findings suggest that there had been a significant increase in LV work following the surgical anastomosis, which is associated with an increase in PBF. Since the ECG was recorded immediately after the procedure in our study, the R wave of the left precordial leads showed no discernible changes.

In our study, echocardiographic measurements showed a significant increase in LA reservoir volume (P value < 0.001) and LV-EDV (P value <0.001) postoperatively. This rise in volume is attributed to the increase in LV preload, which results from an iatrogenically induced increase in PBF.

Kulkarni et al.[7] studied 20 cyanotic congenital heart disease children who underwent BT shunt surgery. All the patients underwent electrocardiographic, echocardiographic, and angiocardiographic analysis before and 6 months to 1 year after the shunt surgery. There was significant growth of the main, right, and left pulmonary arteries and pulmonary valve annulus after the procedure. They also observed that the mean arterial oxygen saturation, LA systolic volume, and LV-EDV increased significantly following the shunt surgery, which was also found in our study.

In our study, SBP and oxygen saturation were significantly increased, and DBP was significantly decreased, postoperatively compared to the pre-operative values. There was no significant change in the HR. The post-operative parameters were measured under the effect of dobutamine at 5 mcg/kg/m. Increased saturation shows improved oxygenation. Decreased DBP points out the diastolic runoff into the mBTT shunt, indicating good shunt flow.[17]

In the ABG analysis, PH, PO2, and HCO3 were significantly increased, and PCO2 was significantly decreased, postoperatively compared to the pre-operative ABG. These changes are also due to iatrogenically induced increased delivery of de-oxygenated blood to the lungs for gas exchange, leading to improved oxygenation and carbon-dioxide removal, also improved acidosis.

Hung Kun Oh et al.[18] studied the ABG changes in TOF children undergoing shunt surgery. They observed that, postoperatively, the PO2 and arterial saturation increased and correlated with the pulse-oximetry saturation changes. The PCO2 and HCO3 remained the same.

Changes observed in both ECG and TTE are consistent with improved PBF.

Limitations

The sample size was less. This study was focused only on the immediate post-operative ECG and echocardiographic changes. Long-term follow-up was not done. The influence of anesthetic drugs and dobutamine on hemodynamics and other parameters was not analyzed. The ECG and echo parameters in this study show improved PBF due to the presence of shunt flow, but they do not help to quantify the flow. Further studies can be done to compare ECG and echo parameters of overshunting and undershunting of the mBTT shunt, thus making this study more versatile.

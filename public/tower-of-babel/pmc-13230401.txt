Dynamical analysis of electroosmotic transport of bionic gyrotactic microorganisms with graphene oxide blood nanofluid through thermal ciliated channel
Authors: Madiha Ajmal, Rashid Mehmood, Noreen Sher Akbar, Siddra Rana, Lemessa Asefa Eressa, Taseer Muhammad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractElectroosmotic bio-nanofluid transport is an important area of study in biomedical engineering especially in fields like targeted drug delivery and microfluidic devices. Nevertheless, the interactions of non-Newtonian Carreau fluid dynamics, cilia-based transport, electroosmosis, and gyrotactic microorganisms are not fully explored in the literature. The purpose of the current study is to investigate the electroosmotic flow of a graphene oxide based blood nanofluid with gyrotactic microorganisms within a thermal ciliated channel. The nonlinear equations governing are modeled by the Carreau fluid model and solved numerically by using Lobatto IIIa collocation method in computational software Maple. The findings demonstrate that the rise in Eckert number positively affects the temperature profile and the larger the nanoparticle volume fraction, the more favorable thermal distribution and the lower the microorganism density because of a better impact of Brownian motion. In addition, the higher the Peclet number, the greater the transport of microorganisms but the higher the thermophoresis and buoyancy parameters, the more the aggregation and stability of gyrotactic cells. These results indicate that electroosmotic forcing and ciliary movement can be efficiently used to regulate heat movement and distribution of microorganisms in bio-nanofluid systems. The work presents a detailed design of the optimal method to electroosmotic transport in the most advanced biomedical and microfluidic applications.Keywords: Heat transfer, Electroosmosis, Carreau nanofluid, Graphene oxide-blood, Microorganisms, Ciliary membrane

Numerical procedure

The two-point boundary value problem is given by the governing non-linear differential equations, Eqs. 23, 24, 25, 26, 27 and 28 and boundary conditions, Eqs. 29 and 30 . The reason for utilizing a numerical approach is due to the strong nonlinearity and interconnection of the system, whereby it is not possible to obtain analytical solutions. To convert the system of higher-order differential equations into a system of first-order differential equations, the appropriate auxiliary variables are introduced. The equations are then solved numerically using MAPLE’s built-in solver. The solver utilizes the three-stage Lobatto III a collocation method for solving nonlinear boundary value problems with high accuracy and stability. The initial guess is supplied with a solution that satisfies the boundary conditions, and the computational domain has been discretized into a finite mesh. The solver uses an iterative solution refinement process until the required convergence criteria are met. Tolerance levels are kept small to ensure result accuracy. Convergence and stability

To obtain a converged solution to the problem, the bvp4c solver in MAPLE is used for solving the system through collocation and following the three-stage Lobatto IIIa method with a well-established fourth-order convergence property for non-linear BVPs. The Collocation process provides an estimated initial guess of all dependent variables; this estimated initial guess is then iteratively improved through multiple uses of the bvp4c solver until convergence is obtained. Convergence is defined by a small enough value for the relative tolerance (typically , resulting in no discernable differences between solutions after consecutive iterations of our solution profile occur. The solver automatically performs mesh refinement using automatic step size control. As a result of a grid independence test that increased the number of grid points, numerical convergence was affirmed when the mesh density reached a value at which no further change was seen in results. The implicit nature of the Lobatto IIIa scheme guarantees stability for both stiff and nonlinear systems. The stability of the calculation continues without issue, even when velocity, temperature, nanoparticle concentration, and concentration of microorganisms are strongly coupled via their governing equations.

Geometrical Structure of the problem

The analysis of the influence of the Eckert number on temperature profiles is a critical aspect in comprehending the phenomena of fluid dynamics and heat transfer processes, hence Fig. 2 shows how Eckert number (Ec) influences the temperature distribution. The Eckert number is a physical measure of the ratio of the difference in kinetic energy and enthalpy between the flow and is directly proportional to the effects of viscous dissipation. The greater the the more the mechanical energy (generated by fluid motion) is converted to thermal energy leading to high fluid temperatures. This is especially applicable in micro and nanoscale flows whereby we cannot afford to ignore the issue of viscous heating. The increased viscous dissipation elevates the internal energy of the fluid and hence elevates the local temperature and affects the thermal boundary layer thickness. Impact of number on temperature profiles shows that magnitude of temperature increases with rise in Eckert number, highlighting the role of viscous dissipation in the thermal behavior of the system. As the Eckert number increases, the transformation of kinetic energy into internal energy becomes more evident, resulting in a more substantial increase in temperature.

In the study of heat transfer and fluid dynamics, the buoyancy ratio parameter is frequently a significant factor in natural convection processes. Figure 3 indicates that the buoyancy ratio parameter is central to the process of convection that is driven by buoyancy. The higher the , the higher the density gradient between the fluid and the surrounding medium that increases the buoyant forces on the fluid. This enhanced buoyancy increases the rate of fluid movement upwards, increasing convective heat transfer leading to an increase in temperature values. This act is in accordance with the classical natural convection theory where the buoyancy forces facilitate mixing of the fluid and heat transfer, thus, changing the temperature field. So, Fig. 3 designates that by improving the buoyancy ratio parameter there is a rise in temperature graph. This is likely because as increases, the density difference between the fluid and the surrounding environment also increases. This greater density difference results in a stronger buoyant force, causing the fluid to rise more quickly and leading to a higher temperature. This is logical, as a more buoyant fluid will have a greater propensity to rise and displace the surrounding fluid, resulting in a temperature rise.

Figure 4 deals with how the thermophoresis parameter affects the temperature profile. Thermophoresis is defined as the movement of particles in regions of a higher temperature to a lower temperature and this is much applicable in the context of nanofluid flows. With an increase in , nanoparticles acquire an increased thermophoretic force, and a redistribution of the particles takes place influencing the local thermal conductivity and heat capacity. Local heating can be induced with this movement, increasing the overall temperature profile by modifying the distribution of thermal energy in the fluid. It worth to mention here with rise in the value of temperature is lifted. Understanding the temperature distribution in fluid flows is crucial, particularly in systems that involve nanoparticles or other small particles. This parameter plays a key role in this understanding. Particles are moved from hotter to colder locations during the thermophoresis process. The fluid's total temperature may rise as a result of the localized heating effects produced by this movement. The heat transfer characteristics in nanofluids can be significantly affected by the presence of nanoparticles. When the values are higher, it enhances the thermophoretic movement of these particles, resulting in increased temperatures.

This enhanced dispersion aids better heat transfer, which avoids local heat spikes thus lowering peak temperatures. Weissenberg number is a measure of the elastic influences in the fluid; the greater the values of , the greater the viscoelastic behaviour. This elasticity has the potential to change the flow structure in terms of storage and release of elastic energy which facilitates a greater amount of energy dissipation and heat transfer, hence reducing the temperature. Hence Figs. 5 and 6 shown that increasing the Brownian parameter and Weissenberg number decreases the temperature for both blood and graphene blood oxide. This implies that more Brownian motion improves particle dispersion and mixing, which in turn improves heat transfer and reduces localized heating. Brownian motion’s random movement of particles encourages more effective heat distribution throughout the fluid, which lowers peak temperatures and produces a more uniform temperature profile. The temperature profile decreases in tandem with an increase in the Weissenberg number (We). Peristaltic pumping is a process in which the walls of a tube or channel contract and relax sequentially. These contractions move in a wave-like motion along the length of the tube, resulting in areas of high and low pressure. The pressure difference between the high-pressure zone (caused by the contractions) and the low-pressure zone (caused by the relaxations) creates a pressure gradient. On the aspect of pressure gradient dynamics, peristaltic pumping creates spatially dissimilar pressure field as a result of successive contractile and relaxations of the walls. Concluding remarks

This paper discusses in-depth research on the electroosmotic transport of a Carreau nanofluid with gyrotactic microorganisms in a vertical ciliated channel. The findings provide valuable understanding of the interplay of non-Newtonian rheology, electro kinetics, nanoparticle dynamics, and microorganism dynamics.

The shear-thinning property of the Carreau fluid changes considerably the flow structure, the power-law index is used to regulate the effective viscosity, and therewith to regulate velocity distribution and transport processes. Reduced viscosity with increased shear rates increases fluid mobility and this is especially useful in the microfluidic conditions.

The forces that dominate to drive the flow are electroosmotic forcing. There are effects of increasing the Peclet number which increases the strength of the convective transport, leading to increased migration, accumulation of gyrotactic microorganisms. In controlled transport systems, this mechanism is particularly helpful, with external electric fields being able to manipulate biological suspensions.

Addition of nanoparticles (e.g., graphene oxide) contributes significantly to thermal performance of the fluid. The parameters that improve the rate of heat transfer include nanoparticle volume fraction and Brownian motion parameter () which can also decrease the density of gyrotactic microorganisms since there is increased agitation and dispersion of the particles. This means that there is a trade off between thermal efficiency and biological stability.

Moreover, the Weissenberg number buoyancy ratio parameter and thermophoretic parameter () have a significant effect on the microorganism distribution. An increase in these parameters will facilitate orientation and aggregation of microorganisms, enhancing bioconvection patterns. Nevertheless, too much Brownian motion may disrupt such organization and decrease efficiency of systems.

The implications on the findings of this study include a number of applications in real world: The synergic action between electroosmosis and ciliary movement may find application in drug delivery systems, lab-on-chip systems and microfluidic pumps, where the control of fluid and biological objects is needed. The capability to regulate nanoparticle dispersal and movement of microorganisms with the help of electric fields and fluid parameter can be utilized in targeted therapy, particularly in the blood-like nanofluids. Gyrotactic microorganisms can be used in the biosensors, microbial fuel cells and bioconvection-based mixing systems to improve efficiencies by taking advantage of their behavior. Nanofluids that exhibit improved heat transfer can be used in cooling devices, like micro heat exchangers and biomedical cooling devices. The designed ciliated channel has insights into the design of bio-inspired pumping systems, which can be applicable in the microfluidic transport without mechanical components.

All in all, this research paper offers a theoretical basis of the optimization of the electroosmotic nanofluids transport in biological active systems. The findings demonstrate the significance of a compromise between thermal improvement and stabilization of microorganisms, and provide useful insights in designing new biomedical and engineering devices.

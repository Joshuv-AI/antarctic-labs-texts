Spontaneous Raman scattering out of a metastable atomic qubit
I. D. Moore, A. Quinn, J. O’Reilly, J. Metzner, S. Brudney, G. J. Gregory, D. J. Wineland, and D. T. C. Allcock

Abstract
Metastable qubits in atomic systems can enable large-scale quantum computing by simplifying hardware requirements and adding efficient erasure conversion to the pre-existing toolbox of high-fidelity laser-based control. For trapped atomic ions, the fundamental error floor of this control is given by spontaneous Raman and Rayleigh scattering from short-lived excited states. We measure spontaneous Raman scattering rates out of a metastable D 5 / 2 D_{5/2} qubit manifold of a single trapped 40 Ca + ion illuminated by 976 nm light that is -44 THz detuned from the dipole-allowed transition to the P 3 / 2 P_{3/2} manifold. This supports the calculation of error rates from both types of scattering during one- and two-qubit gates on this platform, thus demonstrating that infidelities < 10 − 4 <10^{-4} are possible.

I.1
Detection Scheme

We trap a single
40
Ca
+

[
29

D_{3/2}\leftrightarrow P_{1/2}
transition to repump out of the metastable
D_{3/2}
manifold while avoiding dark resonances
[
30
]
, see Figure
1
(a). We encode our qubit in the
\lvert\uparrow\rangle\equiv\lvert D_{5/2},m_{J}=+5/2\rangle
and
\lvert\downarrow\rangle\equiv\lvert D_{5/2},m_{J}=+3/2\rangle

\lvert\uparrow\rangle
state via polarization-selective optical pumping
[
25
]
. A quantization field of 1.56 G separates adjacent sublevels of the
D_{5/2}
manifold by 2.63 MHz via the linear Zeeman effect. All dipole-allowed transitions out of this manifold are detuned by at least several THz from the 397 nm and 866 nm beams; therefore an ion “shelved” in
D_{5/2}
will not scatter any light from the Doppler cooling beams
[
31
]
. We can thus measure leakage out of
D_{5/2}
by applying these beams and detecting 397 nm fluorescence on a photo-multiplier tube, a technique we will refer to as a “fluorescence check.”

I.2
976 nm Laser System

Ref.
[
12
]
predicts that two-qubit entangling gates can be performed on metastable calcium ions with
<10^{-4}
error from spontaneous Raman scattering for any Raman transition wavelength greater than 963 nm. As 976 nm diode lasers are commonly used to pump erbium-doped fiber amplifiers for telecommunications, there are compact and high-power systems commercially available. We send 976 nm light from a 700 mW volume holographic grating-stabilized free-space HECL through an optical isolator and a mechanical shutter before it is split between two paths, see Fig.
3
.

976 nm hybrid external cavity laser (HECL) system and beam geometry. The mechanical shutter is used to block or allow light to illuminate the ion. Only one beam path is used at a time in experiment.

Each path corresponds to a different polarization at the ion, either
\sigma^{-}
or
\pi

\sigma^{+}

P_{3/2}

The acousto-optic modulators (AOMs) in each path stabilize the beam powers based on feedback from photodiodes placed after beam samplers but before the vacuum chamber, see Figure
3

\pi

\sigma^{-}

\sim 30\,\mu

I.3
Decay Rate Measurement

We measure scattering out of
\lvert\uparrow\rangle
due to the
\sigma^{-}
-polarized beam and scattering out of
\lvert\downarrow\rangle
due to both the
\sigma^{-}
- and
\pi
-polarized beams. After state preparation, we illuminate the ion with a variable amount of 976 nm light for a variable amount of time before performing the measurement sequence described in Section
I.1
, which distinguishes between electronic population in
\{S_{1/2},D_{3/2}\}
,
\{D_{5/2}\}-\lvert\uparrow\rangle
,
\lvert\uparrow\rangle
, or a missing ion. Each beam intensity is independently calibrated by measuring the AC Stark shift it induces on the
\lvert\uparrow\rangle\leftrightarrow\lvert\downarrow\rangle
transition.

At a given intensity, we measure the
D_{5/2}
population after several delay times up to 1 s and fit this data to

\textrm{Pop}_{D_{5/2}}(t)=e^{-t/\tau_{\textrm{meas}}}

(1)

to extract the decay constant
\tau_{\textrm{meas}}
. For each combination of initial state and polarization, we also measure the populations over time with the shutter closed and find results consistent with the 1168(9) ms natural lifetime of the
D_{5/2}
manifold
[
33
]
. The spontaneous Raman scattering rate out of
D_{5/2}
is then

\Gamma_{SD}=\frac{1}{\tau_{\textrm{meas}}}-\frac{1}{\tau_{\textrm{nat}}}

(2)

where
\tau_{\textrm{nat}}
is the measured natural lifetime.

II.1
Gate Errors

We now consider the scattering-induced errors in a representative single-qubit gate, the
\hat{\sigma_{x}}
gate, driven by the 976 nm lasers. The probability of a spontaneous Raman scattering error is
[
13
]

P_{1q\textrm{Ram}}=\tau_{1q}\Gamma_{\textrm{Ram}}=\frac{\pi\Gamma_{\textrm{Ram}}}{2|\Omega_{R}|}

(3)

where
\tau_{1q}
is the gate time and
\Omega_{R}
is the two-photon Rabi frequency. We scale the measured
\Gamma_{SD}
to the more general
\Gamma_{\textrm{Ram}}
using known branching ratios. Assuming equal intensities of the
\sigma^{-}
and
\pi
beams,
we predict an error probability, including leakage to
S_{1/2}
,
D_{3/2}
, or
D_{5/2}
outside of the qubit subspace and bit flip errors, of
1.25(2)\times 10^{-6}
per gate. Given the low branching ratio from
P_{3/2}
to
D_{5/2}
, we can upper bound the Rayleigh-induced decoherence at
<10^{-7}

[
36
]
.

If we drive a
\sigma_{z}\otimes\sigma_{z}
entangling gate on two qubits with the most efficient beam configuration, namely counter-propagating beams with equal intensities and pure
\sigma^{-}
-polarization, the spontaneous Raman scattering probability during the gate is
[
12
]

P_{2q\textrm{Ram}}=\tau_{2q}\Gamma_{\textrm{Ram}}=\frac{2}{\eta}\frac{\pi\Gamma_{\textrm{Ram}}}{2|\Omega_{R}|}

(4)

where
\eta=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}
is the Lamb-Dicke parameter characterizing the laser beam’s coupling to motion and the factor of two comes from the fact that there are now two ions with equal scattering probabilities. With a secular frequency
\omega/2\pi=2
MHz, we find
P_{2q\textrm{Ram}}=5\times 10^{-5}
. In addition, Rayleigh scattering can cause two-qubit gate errors via the recoil momentum kick, which we can bound to
<10^{-8}
.

III
Conclusion

In summary, we measured spontaneous Raman scattering rates of metastable qubit states encoded in the
D_{5/2}
manifold of a trapped
40
Ca
+
ion under the influence of 976 nm light that is far (-44 THz) detuned from the nearest dipole transition and has been used to drive high-fidelity two-qubit logic gates
[
25
]
. Using Clebsch-Gordan coefficients and known branching ratios
[
37
]
, this allows us to calculate spontaneous Raman and Rayleigh scattering rates back to the
D_{5/2}
manifold. Our results validate the theory of Ref.
[
12
]
, which contradicts the previously-accepted model at these large detunings and predicts a fundamental error floor below
10^{-4}
in our system.

Our work provides a strategy to reduce the largest infidelity in leading gate demonstrations
[
6
,
7
]
and supports theory that elucidates how to further lower the error floor. This is important because reducing error probabilities beyond the threshold for a given quantum error correction code reduces the overhead required to achieve a target logical error rate
[
38
]
.

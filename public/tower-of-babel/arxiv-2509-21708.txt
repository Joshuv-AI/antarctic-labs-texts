Braided dynamical groups, the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation and related structures
Chengming Bai, Li Guo, Yunhe Sheng, You Wang

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a braided dynamical group which is a matched pair of dynamical groups satisfying extra conditions. It is shown to give a solution of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation and at the same time a braided groupoid, thereby integrating the approaches of Andruskiewitsch and Matsumoto-Shimizu respectively that use these two notions to produce quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. We pursue this connection further by relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups, which give rise to matched pairs of dynamical groups. As the derived structures of relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups, dynamical post-groups are introduced and are shown to be equivalent to braided dynamical groups. Finally, skew-braces are generalized to dynamical skew-braces as another equivalent notion of braided dynamical groups.

1.
Introduction

In the seminal work
[
18
]
, Lu-Yan-Zhu showed that braided groups give rise to solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. The purpose of this paper is to generalize this result to the dynamical setting, which allows to integrate Andruskiewitsch’s construction of quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation by braided groupoids with another construction by Matsumoto and Shimizu.

1.1.
Set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation and related structures

In 1967, Yang derived the Yang-Baxter equation as the consistency condition for factorization in the quantum many-body problem
[
36
]
. In 1972, the same equation emerged independently in Baxter’s analysis of the eight-vertex model
[
4
]
. A solution to the Yang-Baxter equation on a vector space
V
is an invertible linear map
R:V\otimes V\to V\otimes V
satisfying

(R\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V})(\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes R)(R\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V})=(\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes R)(R\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{V})(\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes R).

The Yang-Baxter equation plays an essential role in broad areas of mathematics and mathematical physics, including integrable systems, quantum groups, Hopf algebras, knot theory, statistical mechanics, quantum field theory and braided categories.

In
[
5
]
, Drinfeld proposed to study set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, which later on have emerged as an actively pursued research area, attracting considerable attention from mathematicians and mathematical physicists. Etingof-Schedler-Soloviev
[
6
]
focused on the non-degenerate involutive set-theoretical solutions and the associated structure groups which can be used to classify the set-theoretical solutions. Lu-Yan-Zhu
[
18
]
showed that braided groups (as well as compatible actions and braiding operators on groups) give rise to solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, and a set-theoretical solution of the Yang-Baxter equation on a set
X
can be generalized to a set-theoretical solution on its associated structure group
G_{X}
.

Rump
[
24
,
25
]
introduced the notion of braces as a generalization of radical rings, and showed that braces are equivalent to linear cycle sets and give rise to non-degenerate involutive set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
14
]
introduced the notion of skew braces as a nonabelian generalization of braces and proved that skew braces produce non-degenerate (not involutive) set-theoretical solutions in different ways. In
[
3
]
, Bardakov and Gubarev
established the relation between skew braces and Rota-Baxter groups introduced in
[
15
]
. As the derived structure of Rota-Baxter operators on groups, the notion of post-groups was introduced in
[
2
]
, which are equivalent to skew braces and thus provide set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.

1.2.
The dynamical Yang-Baxter equation

As a significant generalization of the Yang-Baxter equation, the quantum dynamical Yang-Baxter equation was first introduced by Gervais and Neveu in physics
[
13
]
and then studied by Felder
[
10
,
11
]
from a mathematical viewpoint. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a finite-dimensional abelian Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
and
V
be a semisimple finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{h}
-module. Then the quantum dynamical Yang-Baxter equation
[
9
]
with respect to a meromorphic function
R:\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathrm{End}(V\otimes V)
is given as follows:

R^{12}(\lambda-\gamma h^{(3)})R^{13}(\lambda)R^{23}(\lambda-\gamma h^{(1)})=R^{23}(\lambda)R^{13}(\lambda-\gamma h^{(2)})R^{12}(\lambda),

where
\gamma
is a complex number,
R^{12}(\lambda-\gamma h^{(3)})(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes v_{3}):=(R^{12}(\lambda-\gamma\mu)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}))\otimes v_{3}
if
v_{3}
has weight
\mu
, and

R^{13}(\lambda-\gamma h^{(2)}),R^{23}(\lambda-\gamma h^{(1)})
are defined analogously.
A quantum dynamical
R
-matrix is an invertible solution of this equation satisfying the
\mathfrak{h}
-invariant condition.
Etingof and Schiffmann
[
7
]
found out that exchange matrices are solutions of the quantum dynamical Yang-Baxter equation using the exchange construction. Etingof and Varchenko also considered the classical dynamical Yang-Baxter equation as the quasiclassical analogue of the quantum dynamical Yang-Baxter equation
[
8
]
. The quantum dynamical Yang-Baxter equation and the classical dynamical Yang-Baxter equation appeared in many areas of mathematics and mathematical physics, such as conformal field theory
[
10
]
, dynamical quantum groups
[
9
,
16
]
, integrable systems
[
10
,
27
]
, Lie superalgebras
[
17
]
, moduli spaces of flat connections
[
35
]
and representation theory
[
9
,
34
]
.

Motivated by Drinfeld’s proposal to study set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, it is natural to study the set-theoretical analogue of the quantum dynamical Yang-Baxter equation. Shibukawa
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
formulated the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation:

R(\lambda)_{12}R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,X^{1}))_{23}R(\lambda)_{12}=R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,X^{1}))_{23}R(\lambda)_{12}R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,X^{1}))_{23}.

See Definition
2.2
for more details. In fact, the Yang-Baxter equation can be defined in any monoidal category. The usual Yang-Baxter equation is defined in the monoidal category of vector spaces. Note that the category of dynamical sets
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
is also a monoidal category, and the above dynamical Yang-Baxter equation is exactly the Yang-Baxter equation defined in the monoidal category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
. Up to now, dynamical Yang-Baxter equation and its set-theoretical solutions have been studied extensively.
In
[
33
]
, Shibukawa and Takeuchi studied an analogue of FRT construction in the dynamical setting.
Shibukawa also discovered that Hopf algebroids and rigid tensor categories can be constructed using dynamical Yang-Baxter maps
[
32
]
.
Matsumoto introduced the notion of dynamical braces, which can give rise to unitary solutions of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation
[
22
]
.

Andruskiewitsch considered the Yang-Baxter equation in the monoidal category of quivers
{\bf Quiv_{\Lambda}}
in
[
1
]
, and proved that braided groupoids give rise to quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. Recently, the notion of post-groupoids was introduced in
[
28
]
, which are equivalent to braided groupoids, and also give rise to quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.
There is another construction of quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. In
[
23
]
, Matsumoto and Shimizu found an embedding monoidal functor
Q
from the monoidal category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
of dynamical sets to the monoidal category
{\bf Quiv_{\Lambda}}
of quivers. They further showed that a solution of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation produces a quiver-theoretical solution of the Yang-Baxter equation on the induced quiver.

1.3.
Main results and outline of the paper

The first purpose of this paper is to introduce the notion of a braided dynamical group as a special matched pair of dynamical groups (Definition
2.24
), with an application to
integrate the above two approaches to the construction of quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.
On the one hand, this notion allows us to generalize the braided groups approach to the Yang-Baxter equation developed in the seminal work
[
18
]
to the dynamical setting.
More precisely, we show that a braided dynamical group gives rise to a solution of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation (Theorem
2.25
). On the other hand, we show that the aforementioned functor
Q:{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}\to{\bf Quiv_{\Lambda}}
sends dynamical groups to groupoids. We further generalize this construction to the braided setting and show that the functor
Q
sends braided dynamical groups to braided groupoids (Theorem
2.34
). Therefore, the two approaches to obtain a quiver-theoretical solution of the Yang-Baxter equation, one by Andruskiewitsch’s construction from braided groupoids
[
1
]
and another one by Matsumoto-Shimizu’s construction from solution of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation
[
23
]
, can both be viewed under the lens of braided dynamical groups (Corollary
2.39
).

Given the critical role of braided dynamical groups in solving the various Yang-Baxter equations, our second purpose is to further understand this notion, by generalizing to the dynamical setting several well-known structures, including (skew) braces, Rota-Baxter operators and post-groups. We start with the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups, which lead to the factorization of dynamical groups. We prove that relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups lead to matched pairs of dynamical groups (Proposition
3.8
). We then introduce the notion of dynamical post-groups, which are equivalent to braided dynamical groups (Theorem
4.11
and Proposition
4.14
) and closely related to relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups (Propositions
4.9
and
4.10
).
We finally study dynamical skew-braces and show their equivalence to dynamical post-groups (Propositions
5.5
and
5.6
), and thereby braided dynamical groups.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce braided dynamical groups, which lead to solutions of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation. In Section
3
, we give the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups, which give rise to matched pairs of dynamical groups. In Section
4
, as the derived structure of relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups, the dynamical post-groups are defined, which are equivalent to braided dynamical groups. In Section
5
, we introduce the notion of dynamical skew-braces as a generalization of skew braces to the dynamical setting, and show that dynamical skew-braces are equivalent to dynamical post-groups, as well as braided dynamical groups.

2.
Matched pairs of dynamical groups, braided dynamical groups and the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation

In this section, we first recall the notions of dynamical sets and the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation. Then we introduce the notion of a matched pair of dynamical groups. A braided dynamical group is a special matched pair of dynamical groups, and produces a solution of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation. Moreover, we show that braided dynamical groups can give rise to braided groupoids, which are associated to the quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.

2.1.
Dynamical sets and dynamical Yang-Baxter equation

Throughout this paper, we denote by
\Lambda
a fixed set of dynamical parameters. We start with a brief review of the category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
of dynamical sets over
\Lambda
, which is a monoidal category introduced in
[
23
,
26
,
31
]
. A
dynamical set
is a set
X
together with a
structure map

\phi_{X}:\Lambda\times X\to\Lambda
. A dynamical set will be denoted by
\mathscr{X}=(X,\Lambda,\phi_{X})
. The component maps
\phi_{X}(\lambda,-):X\to\Lambda
are denoted by
\phi_{X}^{\lambda}
.
\mathscr{S}=(S,\Lambda,\phi_{S})
is called a
dynamical subset
of
\mathscr{X}
, if
S
is a subset of
X
and
\phi_{S}
satisfies
\phi_{S}(\lambda,a)=\phi_{X}(\lambda,a)
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a\in S
. A dynamical set

\mathscr{X}=(X,\Lambda,\phi_{X})
is called
constant
, if
\phi_{X}(\lambda,x)=\lambda
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,x\in X
. A morphism of dynamical sets from
\mathscr{X}=(X,\Lambda,\phi_{X})
to
\mathscr{Y}=(Y,\Lambda,\phi_{Y})
is a map
f:\Lambda\times X\to Y
satisfying

(1)

\phi_{Y}(\lambda,f(\lambda,x))=\phi_{X}(\lambda,x),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,x\in X.

In other words, a morphism is a collection of maps
f_{\lambda}:X\to Y
with
f_{\lambda}=f(\lambda,-)
which makes the following diagram commutative for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
:

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{X}^{\lambda}}
\scriptstyle{f_{\lambda}}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{Y}^{\lambda}}
\textstyle{\Lambda\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\rm{Id}}}
\textstyle{\Lambda}

The composition
g\circ f:\Lambda\times X\to Z
of morphisms
f:\Lambda\times X\to Y
and
g:\Lambda\times Y\to Z
is defined by

(g\circ f)(\lambda,x):=g(\lambda,f(\lambda,x)),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,

which is also expressed as

(2)

\displaystyle(g\circ f)_{\lambda}=g_{\lambda}\circ f_{\lambda}.

The category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
has a terminal object, given by
\Lambda
together with the second projection
\phi_{\Lambda}=\Pr_{2}:\Lambda\times\Lambda\to\Lambda
as the structure map.

Given two objects
\mathscr{X}=(X,\Lambda,\phi_{X}),\mathscr{Y}=(Y,\Lambda,\phi_{Y})\in{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
, we define their tensor product

\mathscr{X}\otimes\mathscr{Y}:=(X\times Y,\Lambda,\phi_{X\times Y})\in{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}},

where
X\times Y
is the Cartesian product and the structure map
\phi_{X\times Y}:\Lambda\times X\times Y\to\Lambda
is given by

(3)

\phi_{X\times Y}(\lambda,x,y):=\phi_{Y}(\phi_{X}(\lambda,x),y),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,x\in X,y\in Y.

For morphisms
f:\Lambda\times X\to X^{\prime}
and
g:\Lambda\times Y\to Y^{\prime}
in
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
, their tensor product
f\otimes g:\Lambda\times(X\times Y)\to X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}
is given by

(f\otimes g)(\lambda,x,y)=\Big(f(\lambda,x),g(\phi_{X}(\lambda,x),y)\Big).

The singleton
{\bf 1}:=\{1\}
is an object of
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
with the structure map
\phi_{\bf 1}(\lambda,1)=\lambda
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda.
There are natural isomorphisms

a_{X,Y,Z}:(\mathscr{X}\otimes\mathscr{Y})\otimes\mathscr{Z}\to\mathscr{X}\otimes(\mathscr{Y}\otimes\mathscr{Z}),\quad l_{X}:({\bf 1}\otimes\mathscr{X})\to\mathscr{X},\quad r_{X}:(\mathscr{X}\otimes{\bf 1})\to\mathscr{X}

for objects
\mathscr{X}=(X,\Lambda,\phi_{X}),\mathscr{Y}=(Y,\Lambda,\phi_{Y}),\mathscr{Z}=(Z,\Lambda,\phi_{Z})\in{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
defined by

a_{X,Y,Z}(\lambda,(x,y),z)=(x,(y,z)),\quad l_{X}(\lambda,(1,x))=x,\quad r_{X}(\lambda,(x,1))=x,

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,x\in X,y\in Y,z\in Z
. Therefore, the category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
is a monoidal category with the tensor product
\otimes
, the unit object
{\bf 1}
and the natural isomorphisms defined as above.

Remark 2.1
.

Dynamical sets can be viewed as a discrete dynamical systems. By regarding
\Lambda
as the given system, the structural map
\phi_{X}:\Lambda\times X\to\Lambda
constitutes a family of iterative functions
\{\phi_{x}:\Lambda\to\Lambda\}_{x\in X}
that capture the evolution of the system.

Consider the braided equation and braided objects in the monoidal category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
, one obtains the following notion of dynamical Yang-Baxter equation, originally given in
[
29
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A solution of the
dynamical Yang-Baxter equation
on a dynamical set
\mathscr{X}=(X,\Lambda,\phi_{X})
is an isomorphism of dynamical sets
R:\mathscr{X}\otimes\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}\otimes\mathscr{X}
satisfying:

(4)

\displaystyle R(\lambda)_{12}R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,X^{1}))_{23}R(\lambda)_{12}=R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,X^{1}))_{23}R(\lambda)_{12}R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,X^{1}))_{23},

where
R(\lambda)_{12},R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,X^{1}))_{23}:X\times X\times X\to X\times X\times X
are given by

\displaystyle R(\lambda)_{12}(x,y,z)=(R(\lambda,x,y),z),\quad R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,X^{1}))_{23}(x,y,z)=(x,R(\phi_{X}(\lambda,x),y,z)),

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,x,y,z\in X.
We denote
R(\lambda,x,y)=(\varphi^{\lambda}_{x}(y),\psi^{\lambda}_{y}(x))
. The solution
R
is called
non-degenerate
if
\varphi^{\lambda}_{x}
and
\psi^{\lambda}_{y}
are bijective for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,x,y\in X
.

A dynamical set
\mathscr{X}
with a solution
R
of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation is called a
braided dynamical set
and denoted by
(\mathscr{X},R)
. Denote by
\mathrm{Br}({\bf DSet_{\Lambda}})
the category of braided dynamical sets over
\Lambda
.

Remark 2.3
.

By (
1
) and (
3
),
R
is a morphism of dynamical sets from
\mathscr{X}\otimes\mathscr{X}
to
\mathscr{X}\otimes\mathscr{X}
if and only if the following condition is satisfied:

(5)

\displaystyle\phi_{X}(\phi_{X}(\lambda,\varphi^{\lambda}_{x}(y)),\psi^{\lambda}_{y}(x))=\phi_{X}(\phi_{X}(\lambda,x),y),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,x,y\in X,

which is called
the weight zero condition
(
[
26
]
) or
invariant condition
(
[
30
]
).

Remark 2.4
.

Note that
R
is an isomorphism if and only if the components
R(\lambda):X\times X\to X\times X
are bijective for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
; while in the original definition given in
[
23
,
29
]
,
R
is not required to be an isomorphism.

2.2.
Matched pairs of dynamical groups and braided dynamical groups

We first recall the notion of a dynamical group, which is a group object in the monoidal category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
, as an important ingredient in the definition of braided dynamical groups.

Definition 2.5
.

(
[
26
]
)
A
dynamical group
structure on a dynamical set
\mathscr{G}=(G,\Lambda,\phi_{G})
consists of the following data:

(i)

a morphism (called the
product
)
\circ:\mathscr{G}\otimes\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}
such that the following associative law holds:

(6)

a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c)=(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda}c,\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b,c\in G,

where
\circ_{\lambda}:G\times G\to G
is the components of
\circ
;

(ii)

an element
e_{G}\in G
(called the
unit
) such that

(7)

a\circ_{\lambda}e_{G}=e_{G}\circ_{\lambda}a=a,\quad\phi(\lambda,e_{G})=\lambda,\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a\in G;

(iii)

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a\in G
, there always exists
\bar{a}^{\lambda}\in G
(called the
inverse
of
a
with respect to the product
\circ_{\lambda}
) such that

a\circ_{\lambda}\bar{a}^{\lambda}=\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}a=e_{G}.

We will denote a dynamical group by
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
.
If
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
only satisfies the associative law (i), then it is called a
dynamical semi-group
.

Remark 2.6
.

(i)

The associative law in Definition
2.5
comes from the notion of group objects in the monoidal category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
.

(ii)

For a dynamical group
(G,\Lambda,\phi_{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
, the unit
e_{G}
is unique.

(iii)

For any
\lambda\in\Lambda,a\in G
, the inverse element
\bar{a}^{\lambda}
is also uniquely determined according to the associative law in Definition
2.5
.

Remark 2.7
.

Since the product
\circ:\mathscr{G}\otimes\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}
is a morphism from
\mathscr{G}\otimes\mathscr{G}
to
\mathscr{G}
in the category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
, by (
1
) and (
3
), it follows that

(8)

\displaystyle\phi_{G}(\lambda,a\circ_{\lambda}b)=\phi_{G}(\phi_{G}(\lambda,a),b),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G.

Note that the condition (
8
) is also deduced in
[
22
, Proposition 3.4]
using a different approach.

Example 2.8
.

Let
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
be the field of real numbers. Then
(\mathbb{R},\Lambda=\mathbb{R},\phi)
is a dynamical set, where
\phi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\phi(\lambda,a)=\lambda\cdot(\lambda a+1).

Define the product
\circ_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+(\lambda a+1)^{2}\cdot b,

for all
\lambda,a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+(\lambda a+1)^{2}\cdot b+(\lambda a+\lambda(\lambda a+1)^{2}\cdot b+1)^{2}\cdot c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a+(\lambda a+1)^{2}\cdot(b+(\lambda^{2}ab+\lambda b+1)^{2}\cdot c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\phi(\lambda,a)}c),

which implies that
\circ_{\lambda}
satisfies the associative law (i) in Definition
2.5
. Moreover, we can check that
0
is the unit and
a^{\lambda}=-\frac{a}{(\lambda a+1)^{2}}
is the inverse of
a
with respect to
\circ_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda,a\in\mathbb{R}.
Therefore,
(\mathbb{R},\Lambda=\mathbb{R},\phi,\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}})
is a dynamical group.

Example 2.9
.

Let
G=\{0,1,2\}
be a set containing three elements. Then
(G,\Lambda=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\},\phi)
is a dynamical set, where
\phi:\Lambda\times G\to\Lambda
is given by

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\phi&0&1&2\\
\hline\cr\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{2}\\
\lambda_{2}&\lambda_{2}&\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{1}\\
\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{2}\\
\end{array}

Define the product
\circ_{\lambda_{i}}:G\times G\to G
as follows.

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\circ_{\lambda_{1}}&0&1&2\\
\hline\cr 0&0&1&2\\
1&1&0&2\\
2&2&1&0\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\circ_{\lambda_{2}}&0&1&2\\
\hline\cr 0&0&1&2\\
1&1&2&0\\
2&2&1&0\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\circ_{\lambda_{3}}&0&1&2\\
\hline\cr 0&0&1&2\\
1&1&0&2\\
2&2&0&1\\
\end{array}

By a direct verification, we check that
(G=\{0,1,2\},\Lambda=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\},\phi,\{\circ_{\lambda_{i}}\}_{\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
be a dynamical group. A dynamical subset
\mathscr{S}=(S,\Lambda,\phi_{S})\subset\mathscr{G}
is called a
dynamical subgroup
if the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

For all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
a,b\in S,
we have
a\circ_{\lambda}b\in S
.

(ii)

e_{G}\in S
, where
e_{G}
is the unit of the dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
.

(iii)

For all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a\in S
, the inverse
\bar{a}^{\lambda}
is in
S
.

A
homomorphism of dynamical groups
from
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
to
(\mathscr{H},\circ^{\prime})
is a morphism of dynamical sets
\Psi:\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}
such that

(9)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)=\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b)=\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\phi_{H}(\lambda,\Psi(\lambda,a))}(b),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G.

Now we introduce the notion of a constant dynamical group.

Definition 2.11
.

A dynamical group
(\mathscr{H},\cdot)
is called a
constant dynamical group
if

\phi_{H}(\lambda,x)=\lambda,\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,x\in H.

We usually denote by
x^{\lambda}
the inverse of

with respect to the product
\cdot_{\lambda}
in a constant dynamical group
(\mathscr{H},\cdot)
, while we denote by
\bar{a}^{\lambda}
the inverse of

with respect to the product
\circ_{\lambda}
in an arbitrary dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
.

Remark 2.12
.

Let
(\mathscr{H},\cdot)
be a constant dynamical group. Since
\phi_{H}(-,x)={\rm{Id}}_{\Lambda}
for all
x\in H
, by (
6
), we have

x\cdot_{\lambda}(y\cdot_{\lambda}z)=(x\cdot_{\lambda}y)\cdot_{\lambda}z
for all

\lambda\in\Lambda,x,y,z\in H,
which implies that a constant dynamical group can be viewed as a collection of groups on the same set indexed by
\lambda\in\Lambda
with their units identified.

Definition 2.13
.

A constant dynamical group
(\mathscr{H},\cdot)
is called a
trivial dynamical group
if groups
(H,\cdot_{\lambda})
are isomorphic for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Example 2.14
.

Every group
(H,+)
gives rise to a trivial dynamical group
(\mathscr{H},\cdot)
with
\cdot_{\lambda}:=+
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda.

Example 2.15
.

Let
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
be the field of real numbers and
\Lambda=\{2k+1|k\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Define
\cdot_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

a\cdot_{\lambda}b=\sqrt[\lambda]{a^{\lambda}+b^{\lambda}},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Define bijective maps
f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by
f_{\lambda}(a)=a^{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Then we have

f_{\lambda}(a\cdot_{\lambda}b)=a^{\lambda}+b^{\lambda}=f_{\lambda}(a)\cdot_{1}f_{\lambda}(b),\quad\forall a,b\in\mathbb{R},

which implies that
f_{\lambda}:(\mathbb{R},\cdot_{\lambda})\to(\mathbb{R},\cdot_{1})
are all isomorphisms of groups for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
. Therefore,
(\mathbb{R},\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a trivial dynamical group. However, the group structures
(\mathbb{R},\cdot_{\lambda})
are different for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Based on Remark
2.12
, we obtain the following examples.

Example 2.16
.

Let

Example 2.17

Let

(iii)

In general, given a classification of groups with a given order, it can induce a (non-trivial) constant dynamical group with the same order.

Proposition 2.22
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
be a dynamical group. If a morphism of dynamical sets
R:\Lambda\times G\times G\to G\times G
given by
R(\lambda,a,b)=(\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}(b),\psi^{\lambda}_{b}(a))
satisfies the conditions

(19)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\lambda}_{a\circ_{\lambda}b}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}~\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}_{b}(c);

(20)

\displaystyle\psi^{\lambda}_{b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}(b))}_{c}\psi^{\lambda}_{b}(a);

(21)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}(b)\circ_{\lambda}\psi^{\lambda}_{b}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}b,

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b,c\in G,
then
R
is a solution of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation on the dynamical set
\mathscr{G}=(G,\Lambda,\phi_{G})
.

Proof.

The left-hand-side of (
4
) is

\displaystyle R(\lambda)_{12}R(\phi_{G}(\lambda,G^{1}))_{23}R(\lambda)_{12}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(\lambda)_{12}R(\phi_{G}(\lambda,G^{1}))_{23}(d,e,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(\lambda)_{12}(d,f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h,k,g),

where
d=\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}(b),~e=\psi^{\lambda}_{b}(a),~f=\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,d)}_{e}(c),~g=\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,d)}_{c}(e),~h=\varphi^{\lambda}_{d}(f)
and

By (

), we have

a\circ_{\lambda}b=d\circ_{\lambda}e,\quad e\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,d)}c=f\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,d)}g,\quad d\circ_{\lambda}f=h\circ_{\lambda}k.

The right-hand-side of (
4

\displaystyle R(\phi_{G}(\lambda,G^{1}))_{23}R(\lambda)_{12}R(\phi_{G}(\lambda,G^{1}))_{23}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(\phi_{G}(\lambda,G^{1}))_{23}R(\lambda)_{12}(a,q,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(\phi_{G}(\lambda,G^{1}))_{23}(s,t,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(s,v,w),

where
q=\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}_{b}(c),~r=\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}_{c}(b),~s=\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}(q),~t=\psi^{\lambda}_{q}(a),~v=\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}_{t}(r)
and
w=\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}_{r}(t)
.
By (
21
), we have

b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c=q\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}r,\quad a\circ_{\lambda}q=s\circ_{\lambda}t,\quad t\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}r=v\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}w.

\displaystyle s=\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}(q)=\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}(\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}_{b}(c))=\varphi^{\lambda}_{a\circ_{\lambda}b}(c)=\varphi^{\lambda}_{d\circ_{\lambda}e}(c)=\varphi^{\lambda}_{d}(\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,d)}_{e}(c))=\varphi^{\lambda}_{d}(f)=h.

We use (
20

as follows:

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}_{r}(t)=\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}_{r}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{q}(a))=\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,\varphi_{a}^{\lambda}(q))}_{r}(\psi^{\lambda}_{q}(a))=\psi^{\lambda}_{q\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}r}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi^{\lambda}_{b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c}(a)=\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,\varphi_{a}^{\lambda}(b))}_{c}(\psi^{\lambda}_{b}(a))=\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,d)}_{c}(\varphi^{\lambda}_{b}(a))=\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,d)}_{c}(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g.

\displaystyle(s\circ_{\lambda}v)\circ_{\lambda}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\circ_{\lambda}(v\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}w)=s\circ_{\lambda}\Big(\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}_{t}(r)\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}_{r}(t)\Big)=s\circ_{\lambda}(t\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,s)}r)

\displaystyle(s\circ_{\lambda}t)\circ_{\lambda}r=(\varphi^{\lambda}_{a}(q)\circ_{\lambda}\psi^{\lambda}_{q}(a))\circ_{\lambda}r=(a\circ_{\lambda}q)\circ_{\lambda}r=a\circ_{\lambda}(q\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}r)

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(\varphi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}_{b}(c)\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}\psi^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}_{c}(b))=a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c)

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda}c,

\displaystyle(h\circ_{\lambda}k)\circ_{\lambda}g

\displaystyle\Big(\varphi^{\lambda}_{d}(f)\circ_{\lambda}\psi^{\lambda}_{f}(d)\Big)\circ_{\lambda}g=(d\circ_{\lambda}f)\circ_{\lambda}g=d\circ_{\lambda}(f\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,d)}g)

according to the cancellation law of the dynamical group

2.3.
Braided dynamical groups and braided groupoids

In this subsection, we first recall the embedding functor
Q
from the category of dynamical sets to the category of quivers. Then we prove that this embedding functor
Q
sends dynamical groups to groupoids. Moreover, we establish the relation between matched pairs of dynamical groups and matched pairs of groupoids using this embedding functor
Q
. As a special case, we show that braided dynamical groups can give rise to braided groupoids, which are associated to the quiver-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.

Let
\Lambda
be a non-empty set. We denote by
{\bf Quiv_{\Lambda}}
the category of quivers over
\Lambda
. Now let us recall from
[
23
]
the embedding functor
Q
from
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
to
{\bf Quiv_{\Lambda}}
. Given an object
\mathscr{X}=(X,\Lambda,\phi_{X})\in{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
, we can define the quiver
Q(\mathscr{X})=\Lambda\times X
, where the source and the target maps are defined by:

\alpha(\lambda,x)=\lambda,\quad\beta(\lambda,x)=\phi_{X}(\lambda,x),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,x\in X.

For any morphism
f:\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
in
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
, we define

Q(f):Q(\mathscr{X})\to Q(\mathscr{Y}),\quad Q(f)(\lambda,x)=(\lambda,f(\lambda,x)),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,x\in X.

Then we obtain a functor
Q:{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}\to{\bf Quiv_{\Lambda}}
. Moreover, the functor
Q
is actually a fully faithful monoidal functor, which implies that
Q
embeds the monoidal category
{\bf DSet_{\Lambda}}
into
{\bf Quiv_{\Lambda}}
. See
[
23
, Theorem 2.7]
for more details.

Now we show that this embedding functor
Q
sends a dynamical group to a groupoid. Recall that a groupoid
[
19
]
is a small category such that every morphism is invertible.

Definition 2.26
.

A
groupoid
is a pair
(\mathbb{G},M)
, where
M
is the set of objects and
\mathbb{G}
is the set of morphisms, with the structure maps

•

two surjective maps
\alpha,\beta:\mathbb{G}\longrightarrow M
, called the source map and target map, respectively;

•

the multiplication
\star:\mathbb{G}_{\beta}\times_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}
, where
\mathbb{G}_{\beta}\times_{\alpha}\mathbb{G}=\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}|\beta(\gamma_{1})=\alpha(\gamma_{2})\}
, such that

\alpha(\gamma_{1}\star\gamma_{2})=\alpha(\gamma_{1}),~\beta(\gamma_{1}\star\gamma_{2})=\beta(\gamma_{2})
, for all
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathbb{G}_{\beta}\times_{\alpha}\mathbb{G};

•

the inverse map
\mathrm{inv}:\mathbb{G}\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}
;

•

the inclusion map
\iota:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}
,
m\longmapsto\iota_{m}
called the identity map;

satisfying the following properties:

(i)

(associativity)
(\gamma_{1}\star\gamma_{2})\star\gamma_{3}=\gamma_{1}\star(\gamma_{2}\star\gamma_{3})
, whenever the multiplications are well-defined;

(ii)

(unitality)
\iota_{\alpha(\gamma)}\star\gamma=\gamma=\gamma\star\iota_{\beta(\gamma)}
;

(iii)

(invertibility)
\gamma\star\mathrm{inv}(\gamma)=\iota_{\alpha(\gamma)}
,
\mathrm{inv}(\gamma)\star\gamma=\iota_{\beta(\gamma)}

3.
Relative Rota-Baxter operators and matched pairs of dynamical groups

In this section, we introduce the notion of a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a dynamical group, and show that relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups can give rise to matched pairs of dynamical groups and thus solutions of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation.

Let
\mathscr{G}=(G,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathbb{G}})
be a dynamical set and
\mathscr{H}=(H,\Lambda)
a constant dynamical set. Denote by
\mathrm{Map}(H)
the set of maps from
H
to
H
, and by
\mathrm{Bij}(H)
the set of bijective maps from
H
to
H

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
be a dynamical semi-group and
(\mathscr{H},\cdot)
be a constant dynamical group. A family of maps
\{\Phi_{\lambda}:G\to\mathrm{Map}(H)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is called an

of
\mathscr{G}
on
\mathscr{H}
, if the following conditions are satisfied:

(33)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(a)(x\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Phi_{\lambda}(a)x)\cdot_{\lambda}(\Phi_{\lambda}(a)y),

(34)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(a)\Phi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b)(x),\quad\forall a,b\in G,x,y\in H.

The notion of actions of a dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
on a constant dynamical group
(\mathscr{H},\cdot)
is naturally obtained from the above definition. Note that in this case,
\Phi_{\lambda}(a)
must be invertible, i.e.
\Phi_{\lambda}(a)\in\mathrm{Bij}(H)

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\{\Phi_{\lambda}:G\to\mathrm{Bij}(H)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

\mathscr{G}
and
\mathscr{H}
and simply denoted by
\mathscr{H}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathscr{G}
, where the multiplication
\rtimes_{\lambda}
is given by

(35)

\displaystyle(x,a)\rtimes_{\lambda}(y,b):=(x\cdot_{\lambda}(\Phi_{\lambda}(a)y),a\circ_{\lambda}b),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G,x,y\in H,

and the map
\phi_{H\times G}:\Lambda\times(H\times G)\to\Lambda

(36)

\displaystyle\phi_{H\times G}(\lambda,x,a):=\phi_{G}(\lambda,a).

Proof.

By (

\displaystyle(x,a)\rtimes_{\lambda}((y,b)\rtimes_{\phi_{H\times G}(\lambda,x,a)}(z,c))

\displaystyle(x,a)\rtimes_{\lambda}\Big((y\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(\Phi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b)z)),b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(a)(y\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(\Phi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b)z)),a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c)\Big)

\displaystyle\Big((x\cdot_{\lambda}(\Phi_{\lambda}(a)y))\cdot_{\lambda}(\Phi_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)z),(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda}c\Big)

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{\lambda}(\Phi_{\lambda}(a)y),a\circ_{\lambda}b)\rtimes_{\lambda}(z,c)

of

, and denoted by

.

Moreover, the map
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}:\Lambda\times H\to G

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda,x,y,z\in H

\displaystyle(x\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}y)\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}z

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y))\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}z

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y))\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}y))(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y))\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}((\mathcal{B}x)\circ_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}y))(z)

\displaystyle x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y)\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))\Phi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,\mathcal{B}x)}(\mathcal{B}(y))(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(y\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,x)}z).

Moreover, by a direct calculation,
e_{H}
is the unit, and
\Phi_{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,x)}(\overline{\mathcal{B}(x)}^{\lambda})(x^{\lambda})
is the inverse of
x
. Thus,
(H,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\{\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group.

Obviously we have

\displaystyle\phi_{G}(\lambda,\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{G}(\lambda,\mathcal{B}(x))=\phi_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\lambda,x);

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(x\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(x\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}y)=\mathcal{B}(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y))=\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}\mathcal{B}(y)=\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(x)\circ_{\lambda}\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}_{\phi_{{\mathcal{B}}}(\lambda,x)}(y),

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
x,y\in H
, which implies that
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
is a homomorphism of dynamical groups from
(H,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\{\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.
∎

Let
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
be a dynamical group and

(\mathscr{H},\cdot)
be a constant dynamical group. For a map
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
, the map

(40)

\displaystyle\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}}:H\times G\to H\times G,\quad\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}}(x,a)=(x,\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a),\quad\forall x\in H,a\in G,

is invertible. Indeed, the inverse map
(\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}})^{-1}:H\times G\to H\times G
is given by

(41)

\displaystyle(\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}})^{-1}(x,a)=(x,\overline{\mathcal{B}(x)}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,x)}a),\quad\forall x\in H,a\in G.

Pulling back the dynamical group structure on
\mathscr{H}\rtimes_{\Phi}\mathscr{G}
, we obtain a dynamical group
(H\times G,\Lambda,\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}},\{\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
, where the product
\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}
is given by

\displaystyle(x,a)\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(y,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}})^{-1}(\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}}(x,a)\rtimes_{\lambda}\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}}(y,b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}})^{-1}((x,\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)\rtimes_{\lambda}(y,\mathcal{B}(y)\circ_{\lambda}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\xi^{\lambda}_{\mathcal{B}})^{-1}(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)(y),(\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)\circ_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(y)\circ_{\lambda}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)(y),\overline{\mathcal{B}(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)(y))}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)(y))}((\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)\circ_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(y)\circ_{\lambda}b))\Big),

the structure map
\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}}:\Lambda\times H\times G\to\Lambda
is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}}(\lambda,x,a)=\phi_{G}(\lambda,\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a),

the unit is given by

e=\xi_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}(e_{H},e_{G})=(e_{H},\overline{\mathcal{B}(e_{H})}^{\lambda}),

and the inverse of
(x,a)
is given by

(42)

(x,a)^{\lambda}=\Big(\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)^{-1}(x^{\lambda}),\overline{\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)^{-1}(x^{\lambda}))}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a)^{-1}(x^{\lambda}))}\overline{\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}a}^{\lambda}\Big).

Theorem 3.7
.

With above notations,
(H\times G,\Lambda,\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}},\{\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group factorization into dynamical subgroups
H\times\{e_{G}\}
and
\{e_{H}\}\times G
if and only if
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
with respect to the action
\{\Phi_{\lambda}:G\to\mathrm{Bij}(H)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}(e_{H})=e_{G}
, for all
(e_{H},a),(e_{H},b)\in\{e_{H}\}\times G
, we have

(e_{H},a)\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(e_{H},b)=(e_{H},a\circ_{\lambda}b).

For all
(e_{H},a)\in\{e_{H}\}\times G
, we have

(e_{H},a)^{\lambda}=(e_{H},\overline{a}^{\lambda}).

Therefore, we deduce that
\{e_{H}\}\times G
is a dynamical subgroup of
(H\times G,\Lambda,\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}},\{\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

For all
x,y\in H
, we have

(43)

(x,e_{G})\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(y,e_{G})=\Big(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y),\overline{\mathcal{B}(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y))}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y))}((\mathcal{B}x)\circ_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}y))\Big).

Thus, if
H\times\{e_{G}\}
is a dynamical subgroup of
(H\times G,\Lambda,\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}},\{\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
, then we get

\overline{\mathcal{B}(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y))}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y))}((\mathcal{B}x)\circ_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}y))=e_{G},

which implies that
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator on the dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
with respect to the action
\{\Phi_{\lambda}:G\to\mathrm{Bij}(H)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator. We have
\mathcal{B}(e_{H})=e_{G}
.
By (
43
), we obtain
(x,e_{G})\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(y,e_{G})\in H\times\{e_{G}\}
. For
(h,e_{G})\in H\times e_{G}
, by (
42
), we have

\displaystyle(x,e_{G})^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))^{-1}(x^{\lambda}),\overline{\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))^{-1}(x^{\lambda}))}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))^{-1}(x^{\lambda}))}\overline{\mathcal{B}(x)}^{\lambda}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))^{-1}(x^{\lambda}),e_{G}\Big).

The last equality follows from

\mathcal{B}(x)\circ_{\lambda}\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))^{-1}(x^{\lambda}))=\mathcal{B}(x\cdot_{\lambda}\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))^{-1}(x^{\lambda}))=\mathcal{B}(x\cdot_{\lambda}x^{\lambda})=\mathcal{B}(e_{H})=e_{G}.

Thus,
(x,e_{G})^{\lambda}\in H\times\{e_{G}\}
and
H\times\{e_{G}\}
is a dynamical subgroup of
(H\times G,\Lambda,\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}},\{\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

For all
\lambda\in\Lambda,x\in H,a\in G
, we have

(x,e_{G})\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}(e_{H},a)=(x,a).

It is obvious that
e=(e_{H},e_{G})
is the unit of the dynamical group
(H\times G,\Lambda,\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}},\{\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
and
H\times\{e_{G}\}\cap\{e_{H}\}\times G=\{(e_{H},e_{G})\}.
Therefore,
(H\times G,\Lambda,\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}},\{\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group factorization into dynamical subgroups
H\times\{e_{G}\}
and
\{e_{H}\}\times G
.
∎

Relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups naturally give rise to matched pairs of dynamical groups.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\circ)
with respect to the action
\{\Phi_{\lambda}:G\to\mathrm{Bij}(H)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
. Define
\rightharpoonup:\Lambda\times G\times H\to H
and

\leftharpoonup:\Lambda\times G\times H\to G
respectively by

(44)

\displaystyle a\overset{\lambda}{\rightharpoonup}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(a)(x);

(45)

\displaystyle a\overset{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}{\leftharpoonup\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{\lambda}(a)(x))}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,\mathcal{B}(\Phi_{\lambda}(a)(x))}(a\circ_{\lambda}\mathcal{B}(x)),

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
a\in G,x\in H
. Then
(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{H}_{\mathcal{B}},\sigma)
is a matched pair of dynamical groups, where
\sigma(\lambda,a,x)=(a\overset{\lambda}{\rightharpoonup}x,a\overset{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}{\leftharpoonup\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar}x).

Proof.

It follows from the fact that
(H\times G,\Lambda,\widetilde{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}},\{\ast^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group factorization into dynamical subgroups
H\times\{e_{G}\}
and
\{e_{H}\}\times G
by Theorem
3.7
.
∎

4.
Dynamical post-groups

In this section, we introduce the notion of dynamical post-groups. A relative Rota-Baxter operator on a dynamical group naturally induces a dynamical post-group structure. Conversely, a dynamical post-group gives rise to a relative Rota-Baxter operator on its sub-adjacent dynamical group. Finally, we show that the category of dynamical post-groups is isomorphic to the category of braided dynamical groups.

In this section, as before,
\mathscr{G}=(G,\Lambda,\phi_{G})
is a dynamical set.

4.1.
Dynamical post-groups and relative Rota-Baxter operators

Definition 4.1
.

A
weak dynamical post-group
structure on a dynamical set
\mathscr{G}
consists of two families of maps
\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
from
G\times G
to
G
such that

(i)

(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a constant dynamical group;

(ii)

\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
and
\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
satisfy the following compatibility conditions:

(46)

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c),

(47)

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b))\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(b\vartriangleright_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b,c\in G.

We denote a weak dynamical post-group by
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

A
dynamical post-group
is a weak dynamical post-group in which
L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}_{a}:G\to G
is invertible for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
a\in G
, where
L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}_{a}:G\to G
is defined by

L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}_{a}(b)=a\rhd_{\lambda}b.

A
dynamical pre-group
is a dynamical post-group
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
in which the groups
(G,\cdot_{\lambda})
are abelian for all
\lambda\in\Lambda.

Example 4.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
be the field of real numbers. Then
(\mathbb{R},\Lambda=\mathbb{R},\phi,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}})
is
a dynamical post-group, where
\cdot_{\lambda}:=+
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\vartriangleright_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are given by

a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b:=(\lambda a+1)^{2}\cdot b,\quad\phi(\lambda,a):=\lambda\cdot(\lambda a+1),\quad\forall\lambda,a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Example 4.3
.

Let
G=(\mathbb{Z}_{3},+)
be a cyclic group of order
3
. Let
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}\}
. Then
\mathscr{G}=(G,\Lambda,\phi)
is a dynamical set, where
\phi:\Lambda\times G\to\Lambda
is given by

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\phi&0&1&2\\
\hline\cr\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{2}\\
\lambda_{2}&\lambda_{2}&\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{1}\\
\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{3}&\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{2}\\
\end{array}

Then
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda_{i}}:=+\}_{\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda_{i}}\}_{\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical post-group, where
\vartriangleright_{\lambda_{i}}:G\times G\to G
is given by

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\vartriangleright_{\lambda_{1}}&0&1&2\\
\hline\cr 0&0&1&2\\
1&0&2&1\\
2&0&2&1\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\vartriangleright_{\lambda_{2}}&0&1&2\\
\hline\cr 0&0&1&2\\
1&0&1&2\\
2&0&2&1\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}\vartriangleright_{\lambda_{3}}&0&1&2\\
\hline\cr 0&0&1&2\\
1&0&2&1\\
2&0&1&2\\
\end{array}

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
e_{G}
be the unit for
\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in a dynamical post-group
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Then for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
a\in G
, we have

(48)

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}e_{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{G},

(49)

\displaystyle e_{G}\vartriangleright_{\lambda}a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a.

Proof.

Taking
b=c=e_{G}
in (
46
), we have

a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}e_{G}=(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}e_{G})\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}e_{G})
,
which implies that
a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}e_{G}=e_{G}
. Taking
a=b=e_{G}
in (
47
), we have

e_{G}\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c=e_{G}\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(e_{G}\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c)
for all
c\in G
. Since
e_{G}\vartriangleright_{\lambda}-
is invertible, we have
e_{G}\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c=c
, which implies that (
49
) holds.
∎

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
and
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
be two dynamical post-groups.
A
homomorphism of dynamical post-groups
from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a morphism of dynamical sets
\Psi:\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}
such that for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G
,

(50)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a\cdot_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\lambda}(b),\quad\Psi_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)=\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b).

Dynamical post-groups and their homomorphisms form a category
DPG
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
be a dynamical post-group. Define
\circ_{\lambda}:G\times G\to G
by

(51)

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}b=a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Then
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group with
e_{G}
being the unit, and the inverse map
\bar{\cdot}^{\lambda}:G\to G
given by

\bar{a}^{\lambda}:=(L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}_{a})^{-1}(a^{\lambda}),\quad\forall a\in G,

where
a^{\lambda}
is the inverse of
a
in the constant dynamical group
(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

Moreover, the left multiplication
L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}:G\to\mathrm{Bij}(G)
defined by

L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}(a)(b)=L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}_{a}b=a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b,\quad\forall a,b\in G,

is an action of the dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
on the constant dynamical group
(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

The dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is called the
sub-adjacent dynamical group
of the dynamical post-group

(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
, and denoted by
\mathscr{G}_{\rhd}
.

Proof.

For all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b,c\in G
, we have

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}((a\circ_{\lambda}b)\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}((a\circ_{\lambda}b)\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{d-post-group2}}{=}

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(b\vartriangleright_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{d-post-group1}}{=}

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\lambda}\Big(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b\vartriangleright_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c),

which implies that Condition (i) in Definition
2.5
is satisfied.

For all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a\in G
, we have

a\circ_{\lambda}e_{G}=a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}e_{G})\overset{\eqref{unit-d-post-group1}}{=}a\cdot_{\lambda}e_{G}=a;\quad e_{G}\circ_{\lambda}a=e_{G}\cdot_{\lambda}(e_{G}\vartriangleright_{\lambda}a)\overset{\eqref{unit-d-post-group2}}{=}e_{G}\cdot_{\lambda}a=a,

which implies that Condition (ii) in Definition
2.5
is satisfied.

Since

a\circ_{\lambda}\bar{a}^{\lambda}=a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\bar{a}^{\lambda})=a\cdot_{\lambda}\Big((L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}_{a})(L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}_{a})^{-1}(a^{\lambda})\Big)=a\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}=e_{G},

and

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\vartriangleright_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\bar{a}^{\lambda})\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(e_{G}\vartriangleright_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}a=e_{G},

we obtain
\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}a=e_{G}
applying the fact that
a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}-
is invertible for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a\in G
.
Thus, Condition (iii) in Definition
2.5
is satisfied. Therefore,
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group.

Eqs.  (
46
) and (
47
) indicate that
L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}
is an action of the dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
on the constant dynamical group
(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\Psi:\Lambda\times G\to H
be a homomorphism of dynamical post-groups from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Then
\Psi
is a homomorphism of the sub-adjacent dynamical groups from
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

Proof.

For all
a,b\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{homo-d-post-group2}}{=}

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{homo-d-post-group2}}{=}

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b),

which implies that
\Psi
is a homomorphism of the sub-adjacent dynamical groups.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
be a weak dynamical post-group. Then we can also define
\circ_{\lambda}:G\times G\to G
by (
51
). In this case,
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is just a dynamical semi-group and the left multiplication
L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}:G\to\mathrm{Map}(G)
is an action of the dynamical semi-group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
on the constant dynamical group
(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

At the end of this subsection, we display the close relationship between relative Rota-Baxter operators on dynamical groups and dynamical post-groups.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
with respect to the action
\{\Phi_{\lambda}:G\to\mathrm{Bij}(H)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
. Define the structure map
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}:\Lambda\times H\to H
and the multiplication
\vartriangleright^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}:H\times H\to H
by

(52)

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda,x):=\phi_{G}(\lambda,\mathcal{B}(x)),\quad x\vartriangleright^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}y=\Phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}(x))(y),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,x,y\in H.

Then
(H,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical post-group, whose sub-adjacent dynamical group is the descendant dynamical group of
\mathcal{B}
given in Proposition
3.6
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}:H\to G
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator on a dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
with respect to the action
\{\Phi_{\lambda}:G\to\mathrm{Bij}(H)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
. By Proposition
3.6
,
(H,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\{\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group and
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
is a homomorphism of dynamical groups from
(H,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\{\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Since

is an action of a dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
on a constant dynamical group

(H,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\{\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
on the constant dynamical group
(H,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Moreover, for all

which implies that is
L^{\rhd_{\lambda}}
an action of
(H,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\{\circ^{\mathcal{B}}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
on

(H,\Lambda,\phi_{\mathcal{B}},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})

. The other conclusion can be easily obtained.
∎

4.2.
Dynamical post-groups and braided dynamical groups

Now we show that the category of dynamical post-groups is isomorphic to the category of braided dynamical groups. Consequently, we obtain that a dynamical post-group gives rise to a solution of the dynamical Yang-Baxter equation.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})

Moreover, let
\Psi
be a homomorphism of dynamical post-groups from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})

. Then

is a homomorphism of braided dynamical groups from
(\mathscr{G}_{\rhd},R_{G})
to
(\mathscr{H}_{\rhd^{\prime}},R_{H})

\displaystyle a\overset{\lambda}{\rightharpoonup}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b;

\displaystyle a\overset{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}{\leftharpoonup\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)}(a\circ_{\lambda}b),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G.

Let

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{homo-d-group}\eqref{homo-d-post-group2}}{=}

For all

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\overset{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup}(b\cdot_{\lambda}c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(a\circ_{\lambda}(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\overset{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup}b)\Big)\circ_{\lambda}\Big(\Big(\overline{(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\overset{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup}b)}^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a\cdot_{\lambda}b)}\bar{a}^{\lambda}\Big)\overset{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a\cdot_{\lambda}b)}{\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightharpoonup}c\Big)

5.
Dynamical skew-braces

We first introduce the notion of a dynamical skew brace. Then we show that the category of dynamical skew braces is isomorphic to the category of dynamical post-groups, and therefore isomorphic to the category of braided dynamical groups.

In this section, as before,
\mathscr{G}=(G,\Lambda,\phi_{G})
is a dynamical set.

Definition 5.1
.

A triple

(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is called a
dynamical skew brace
, if

(i)

(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a constant dynamical group;

(ii)

(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical group;

(iii)

\cdot
and
\circ
satisfy the following compatibility condition:

(57)

a\circ_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c)=(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c),\quad\forall a,b,c\in G.

A
dynamical brace
is a dynamical skew brace
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
in which all groups
(G,\cdot_{\lambda})
are abelian for all
\lambda\in\Lambda.

Remark 5.2
.

This notion of a dynamical skew brace is different from the one given in
[
12
, Definition 4.9]
. First, we generalize a group
(G,\cdot)
used in
[
12
, Definition 4.9]
to a constant dynamical group
(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Second, we use the dynamical group, in which there is a unit
e_{G}
satisfying
a\circ_{\lambda}e_{G}=e_{G}\circ_{\lambda}a=a
, rather than the left quasi-group used in
[
12
,
22
]
. Note that the condition
a\circ_{\lambda}e_{G}=e_{G}\circ_{\lambda}a=a
is called the zero-symmetric condition in
[
22
, Definition 3.4]
, which is equivalent to the condition
\phi_{G}(\lambda,e_{G})=\lambda
(
[
26
, Definition 4]
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
be a dynamical skew brace. Then for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G
, we have

(58)

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)^{\lambda}=a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)})\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}.

Proof.

For all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G
, by (
57
) and the fact that
(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a constant dynamical group, we have

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)})\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(a\circ_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}b^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)})\Big)\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}e_{G})\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}=e_{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)^{\lambda},

which implies that
(a\circ_{\lambda}b)^{\lambda}=a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)})\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}.

∎

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
and

(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
be two
dynamical skew braces. A
homomorphism of dynamical skew braces
from

(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a morphism of dynamical sets
\Psi:\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}
such that for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G,

(59)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a\cdot_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\lambda}(b),\quad\Psi_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)=\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b).

Dynamical skew braces and their homomorphisms form a category
DSB
.

By Theorem
4.6
, a dynamical post-group

(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
gives rise to the sub-adjacent dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Moreover, it also gives rise to a dynamical skew brace.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
be a dynamical post-group. Then
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical skew brace, where
\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is given by (
51
).

Moreover, let
\Psi:\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}
be a homomorphism of dynamical post-groups from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Then
\Psi
induces a homomorphism of the corresponding dynamical skew braces from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

Proof.

By the definition of the sub-adjacent dynamical group
(\mathscr{G},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
, we have

\displaystyle a\circ_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{d-post-group1}}{=}

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}(a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}a)\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c),\quad\forall\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b,c\in G,

which implies that
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical skew brace.

Moreover, by Proposition
4.7
, we can obviously deduce that
\Psi
is a homomorphism of dynamical skew braces from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
be a dynamical skew brace. Define a multiplication
\vartriangleright_{\lambda}:G\times G\to G
by

a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b:=a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b),\quad\forall a,b\in G,

where
a^{\lambda}
is the inverse of
a
in
(G,\Lambda,\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Then

(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical post-group.

Let
\Psi:\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}
be a homomorphism of dynamical skew braces from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. Then
\Psi
is a homomorphism of dynamical post-groups from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to

(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.

Proof.

Given any
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,c\in G
, there exists an element
b:=\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(a\cdot_{\lambda}c)
such that

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}\Big(a\circ_{\lambda}(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(a\cdot_{\lambda}c))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}\Big((a\circ_{\lambda}\bar{a}^{\lambda})\circ_{\lambda}(a\cdot_{\lambda}c)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\cdot_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c,

which implies
a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}-
is surjective. Moreover, if
a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b=a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c,
which is equivalent to
a\circ_{\lambda}b=a\circ_{\lambda}c
, then we have

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}a)\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(a\circ_{\phi_{G}(\phi_{G}(\lambda,a),\bar{a}^{\lambda})}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{a}^{\lambda}\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}a)\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c,

which implies that
a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}-
is injective. We also have

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}(b\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)\cdot_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c),

and

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)\vartriangleright_{\lambda}c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ_{\lambda}b)^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}((a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{equ-d-brace}}{=}

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)})\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}((a\circ_{\lambda}b)\circ_{\lambda}c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)})\cdot_{\lambda}a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}(b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}\Big(a\circ_{\lambda}(b^{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}\cdot_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b\circ_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}(b\vartriangleright_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}(b\vartriangleright_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}c),

for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b,c\in G
. Therefore,

(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
is a dynamical post-group.

For the second conclusion, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda,a,b\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a\vartriangleright_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a^{\lambda}\cdot_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a^{\lambda})\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\lambda}(a\circ_{\lambda}b)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{homo-d-skew-brace2}}{=}

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a)^{\lambda}\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}(\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\circ^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{\lambda}(a)\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\Psi_{\phi_{G}(\lambda,a)}(b),

which implies that
\Psi
is a homomorphism of the corresponding dynamical post-groups from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
.
∎

Theorem 5.7
.

Proposition
5.5
gives a functor
\mathfrak{F}:{\bf DPG}\to{\bf DSB}
and Proposition
5.6
gives a functor
\mathfrak{G}:{\bf DSB}\to{\bf DPG}
. Moreover, they give an isomorphism between the categories
{\bf DPG}
and
{\bf DSB}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
and
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
be two dynamical post-groups, and
\Psi
be a homomorphism from
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
to
(\mathscr{H},\{\cdot^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright^{\prime}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
. By Proposition
5.5
and
5.6
, we have
\mathfrak{G}\mathfrak{F}(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})=(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\vartriangleright_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})
and
\mathfrak{G}\mathfrak{F}(\Psi)=\Psi
. Thus,
\mathfrak{G}\mathfrak{F}={\rm{Id}}
. Similarly, we can check that
\mathfrak{F}\mathfrak{G}={\rm{Id}}
. Therefore, the functor
\mathfrak{F}
is the inverse of the functor
\mathfrak{G}
. The proof is completed.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{\cdot_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\{\circ_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda})

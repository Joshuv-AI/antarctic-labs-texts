Convergence analysis and application for high-order neural networks based on gradient descent learning algorithm via smooth regularization
Authors: Khidir Shaib Mohamed, Alawia Adam, Yousif Shoaib Mohammed, Yan Xiong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe pi-sigma network (PSN), as a high-order network, has demonstrated its capacity for rapid learning and strong nonlinear processing. This paper proposes a type algorithm, for PSNs using a batch gradient method based on regularization. Direct application of regularization during network training presents two main drawbacks. There are numerical oscillations and theoretical challenges in computing the gradients at the origin. We then introduced smoothing functions by approximating the regularization to overcome these obstacles, resulting in a new gradient descent method based on smoothing regularization (GDS). Numerical results for the 4-dimensional parity problem and the nonlinear Gabor function problem demonstrate that the GDS algorithms perform better than the other four regularization methods in terms of generalization and pruning efficiency. Theoretical analysis and experimental verification strictly prove the monotonicity and strong and weak convergence of the network based on the GDS algorithms.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-29494-1.Keywords: Pi-sigma network, Gradient descent algorithm, Smoothing regularization, Convergence, Numerical resultsSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science

Introduction

Higher order networks (HONs) extend the traditional pairwise interactions between nodes to higher order interactions involving three or more nodes. This allows for the modeling of more complex relationships and dynamics within the network. Various fields such as dynamical systems, adaptation, and self-organizing systems have studied HONs1–3. Applications of HONs include modeling the spread of disease, understanding the structure of complex systems, and predicting the behavior of social networks4–6. A pi-sigma network (PSN) is an effective HON that can be used for pattern classification and function approximation. It retains the strong learning ability of a multilayer HON while mitigating the exponential growth in the number of weights and hidden units that occurs when the dimension of the input vectors increases. This advantage is discussed in references7–9. Gradient descent (GD) algorithm is a method for unconstrained mathematical optimization. First suggested in 184710, it is a first-order iterative algorithm for finding the local minimum of a differentiable multivariate function. In 1907, researchers proposed a similar method11. Researchers first studied its convergence properties for non-linear optimization problems in 194412, and continued to study and use the method in the following decades13.

Two methods exist for training the weights, referred to as batch training and online training [14,15]. In the primary technique, the network modifies the weights after each training pattern is presented. In the second method, aggregate the cumulative error across an epoch and modify the weights subsequent to the presentation of the entire training set. This study adheres to the batch training update methodology. Overfitting is a prevalent issue in machine learning wherein a model grows excessively complicated and begins to accommodate the noise in the training data instead of the fundamental patterns [16]. In literature, numerous strategies are employed to mitigate the issue of overfitting, which can be summarized as follows: regularization17–19, dropout18, early halting20, and cross-validation21. Regularization is an effective method to mitigate overfitting in machine learning models. Regularization is a technique that incorporates a penalty term into a model’s loss function to deter excessive weights or overly complex models.

Overfitting is a prevalent issue in machine learning and is hence of significance. Overfitting is the phenomenon where an analysis is excessively tailored to fit a specific set of data, resulting in a lack of generalizability. Overfitting occurs when a neural network excessively conforms to the training data, resulting in a diminished capacity to accurately predict fresh data. There are multiple strategies to mitigate the problem of overfitting in neural networks.

As a recent idea to encourage sparsity during training, smoothing regularization has been put forth27–29. By using a penalty term, regularization promotes tiny sums of absolute values for the parameters. The Lasso algorithm30 is what we term regularization, and it usually yields sparse feature vectors. We find another example of regularization learning in31. The absolute value of the coefficient is included as a penalty term in the regularization. The regularization proposed in32 is easier to solve, although being less sparse. Additionally, regularization for federated learning was suggested by the authors in33. This method is also applied in electrical impedance tomography34. The difficulties in theoretical research and potential for poor convergence in computing application stem from the difficulties in regularization and the inability to directly apply the gradient approach. A innovative approach to pruning FNNs was developed in22 by modifying the standard regularization term using a smoothing technique. In the sparsity effect, the regularization performs well35. However, oscillation will occur during the iterative numerical test process because this regular term involves an absolute value function that is not differentiable at the origin. Nonetheless, regularization smoothing is used36–39. Weight decay, sometimes referred to as regularization, is a popular regularization technique that optimizes networks without producing a sparse effect40–44.

In this work, a gradient algorithm with smoothing regularization (GDS) for the PSN is proposed. The innovation in this work lies in the utilization of GS. As illustrated through numerical simulations (“Discussion”) section, GDS exhibits superior sparsity promotion compared to GD and GD in pruned weights. K-dimensional parity problems

The N-dimensional parity problem involves a challenging classification task with input patterns in an K-dimensional vector space. A parity problem consists of many boolean input variables and one boolean output variable. The input/output rule states that the output should be true 1 if an odd number of the input values are true. Since the rule essentially allows either of the inputs to be true 1, we refer to the problem as "Exclusive-OR" (or XOR https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XOR_gate) when there are only two input variables. It is well known that parity issues are statistically neutral48. A parity bit, sometimes referred to as a check bit, is added to a string using binary code. Conclusion

The standard regularization term incorporates absolute values and is non-differentiable at the origin, frequently resulting in an NP-hard problem. This paper proposes a batch gradient method incorporating smoothing regularization. This is achieved by employing a smoothing function to approximate regularization and circumvent its NP-hard nature. We have demonstrated the method for selecting the smoothing regularization parameter, which effectively prunes the network and improves its generalization capability. We have established the weak convergence of the suggested algorithm, indicating that the gradient function approaches zero and the error function converges to a constant. We also theoretically establish strong convergence, signifying that the weight sequence converges to a fixed point. Two simulation examples validate the superiority of the proposed method and the theoretical conclusions presented in this paper.

Furthermore, the generalization of neural networks is a crucial concern in the development of learning methods for these networks. This implies that neural network models should not only exhibit great accuracy in approximating the data samples used for training, but also have strong performance when applied to new, unknown data. One often employed method to enhance the generalization of neural networks is incorporating a penalty term that is directly proportional to the amount of the network weights into the traditional error function. Our objective was to investigate the convergence of the gradient approach with smoothing regularization for neural networks in future research. In addition, we will focus on mini-batch gradient descent as a potential alternative to batch gradient descent. Mini-batch gradient descent is a technique that includes partitioning the dataset into smaller batches and conducting updates for each batch. This distinguishes it from batch gradient descent. These developments are anticipated to enhance the performance and efficiency of neural network training and pruning. Additionally, one of our forthcoming significant goals is to guarantee the convergence of network statements by maintaining a constant learning rate and applying them in real-life situations.

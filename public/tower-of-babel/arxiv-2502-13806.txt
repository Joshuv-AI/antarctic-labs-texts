Classifying thick subcategories over a Koszul complex via the curved BGG correspondence
Jian Liu, Josh Pollitz

Abstract
. In this work we classify the thick subcategories of the bounded derived category of dg modules over a Koszul complex on any list of elements in a regular ring. This simultaneously recovers a theorem of Stevenson when the list of elements is a regular sequence and the classification of thick subcategories for an exterior algebra over a field (via the BGG correspondence). One of the major ingredients is a classification of thick tensor submodules of perfect curved dg modules over a graded commutative noetherian ring concentrated in even degrees, recovering a theorem of Hopkins and Neeman. We give several consequences of the classification result over a Koszul complex, one being that the lattice of thick subcategories of the bounded derived category is fixed by Grothendieck duality.

13D09 (primary); 14M10, 18G80 (secondary)

Introduction

The problem of classifying thick (or localizing) subcategories of a given triangulated category has maintained a great deal of interest—in a variety of different areas—since the work of Devinatz, Hopkins, and Smith
[
22
]
in stable homotopy theory; see, for instance,
[
11
,
12
,
21
,
27
,
33
,
40
,
41
,
43
,
45
]
. Perhaps the most relevant to the present article is a theorem of Hopkins
[
27
]
and Neeman
[
33
]
that establishes a one-to-one correspondence between the thick subcategories of the
homotopy category of perfect complexes over a commutative noetherian ring and the specialization closed subsets of the prime spectrum of the ring. Thomason
[
45
]
later extended this result to quasi-compact and quasi-separated schemes, provided the thick subcategories are closed under a certain tensor action. These foundational results have been widely applied in both algebra and topology.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring, and write
\Df(R)
for its bounded derived category; the objects are
R
-complexes with finitely generated total homology and morphisms are obtained by formally inverting quasi-isomorphisms. The theorem of Hopkins–Neeman yields a classification of the thick subcategories of
\Df(R)
when
R
is a regular ring. In the singular setting, the most notable classification of the thick subcategories of
\Df(R)
is due to Stevenson
[
40
]
which establishes a classification when
R
is a complete intersection in the sense that it is a regular ring modulo a regular sequence; see also
[
16
]
. In this article we are concerned with a more general setting that recovers Stevenson’s result as well as the classification of thick subcategories over an exterior algebra over a field simultaneously.

Let
E
be a Koszul complex on a list of elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
regarded as a dg
R
-algebra in the usual way; cf.
Section

4
. Let
\Df(E)
denote the derived category of dg
E
-modules whose homology is finitely generated over
R
. The main result of the article classifies the thick subcategories of
\Df(E),
when
R
is a regular ring; specializing to when
{\bm{f}}
is an
R

{\bm{f}}=0

The classification is in terms of the support of the pair
(A,w)
, denoted
\supp(A,w)
, where
A=R[\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{n}]
with

and where each
\chi_{i}
has homological degree
-2
; see
Definition

3.1
(as well as
Proposition

3.2
) for a precise definition of
\supp(A,w)
, and see
Proposition

4.12

denote the cohomological support of

over

[

37
]
(inspired by the ones defined by Avramov and Buchweitz

.

For each thick subcategory

For complete intersection rings this was established in

In light of the recent works

1.
(Curved) dg modules

Throughout fix a base commutative ring
R
. We first review the necessary background on
differential graded
(=
dg
) algebras and modules.

1.1
.

Recall that a dg
R
-algebra
A=\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a graded
R
-algebra equipped with a degree
-1
endomorphism
\partial
satisfying
\partial^{2}=0
and the Leibniz rule:

\partial(a\cdot b)=\partial(a)\cdot b+(-1)^{|a|}a\cdot\partial(b)\,;

when
a\neq 0
, the value
|a|
is the unique integer
i
such that
a
belongs to
A_{i}.
Similarly, a (left) graded
A
-module
M=\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a (left) dg
A
-module if it is equipped with a square-zero degree
-1
endomorphism
\partial^{M}
such that

\partial^{M}(a\cdot m)=\partial(a)\cdot m+(-1)^{|a|}a\cdot\partial^{M}(m)\quad\text{for }a\in A\,,\ m\in M\,.

For a dg
A
-module
M
, its homology
\h(M)=\{\h_{i}(M)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a graded module over the graded
R
-algebra
\h(A)=\{\h_{i}(A)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

1.2
.

Fix a dg
R
-algebra
A
, and let
M,N
be dg
A
-modules; in this article, by a module we mean a
left
module. A morphism of dg
A
-modules
\alpha\colon M\to N
is a morphism of
R
-complexes that is
A
-linear; it is a quasi-isomorphism (indicated with ‘
\simeq
’) if the induced map of graded
\h(A)
-modules is an isomorphism.
Let
\mathsf{D}(A)
denote the derived category of dg
A
-modules, obtained by formally inverting quasi-isomorphisms of dg
A
-modules; cf.
[
30
, Section 9]
or
[
4
, Section 2]
. We will regard
\mathsf{D}(A)
as a triangulated category in the standard way, with
{\mathsf{\Sigma}}
denoting the suspension functor.
We write
\Df(A)
for its full subcategory consisting of all dg
A
-modules
M
where
\h(M)
is a finitely generated graded
\h(A)
-module.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be a triangulated category and recall that a full triangulated subcategory
\mathsf{T}
of
\mathsf{C}
is
thick
if it is closed under taking direct summands. Given an object
X
in
\mathsf{C}
, then
\thick_{\mathsf{C}}(X)
denotes the smallest thick subcategory
\mathsf{C}
containing
X.

Example 1.3
.

Assume
A
is a dg
R
-algebra with
\h(A)
a noetherian graded algebra. In this case,
\Df(A)
is a thick subcategory of
\mathsf{D}(A)
.

Example 1.4
.

For a dg
R
-algebra
A
, we let
\mathsf{perf}(A)
denote
\thick_{\mathsf{D}(A)}(A)
, and call an object
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A)
a
perfect
dg
A
-module. These are the dg
A
-modules that are quasi-isomorphic to a summand of a dg
A
-module
F
where
F
admits a filtration

0=F(-1)\subseteq F(0)\subseteq F(1)\subseteq\ldots\subseteq F(n)=F

such that each
F(i)/F(i-1)
is a finite sum of shifts of
A
; see
[
4
, Section 3]
. Note that perfect dg
A
-modules are exactly the compact objects in
\mathsf{D}(A)
. That is to say,
M
is a perfect dg
A
-module if and only if
\Hom_{\mathsf{D}(A)}(M,-)
commutes with arbitrary direct sums; see
[
30
, Proposition 9.1.13]
.

Next we recall the theory of curved dg modules over a curved algebra. For more on these objects see
[
38
]
.

1.5
.

Let
A=\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a graded commutative
R
-algebra, and fix
w\in A_{-2}
. A
curved dg
A
-module having curvature

w
is a graded
A
-module
M=\{M\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
equipped with a degree
-1
map
\partial^{M}\colon M\rightarrow M
satisfying that

\partial^{M}(a\cdot-)=(-1)^{|a|}a\partial^{M}(-)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial^{M}\partial^{M}=w\cdot\id^{M}\,.

A morphism of curved dg
A
-modules is a morphism of graded
A
-modules
\alpha\colon M\to N
such that
\alpha\partial^{M}=\partial^{N}\alpha
.

Example 1.6
.

Let
A
be a graded commutative
R
-algebra. A curved dg
A
-module having curvature zero is exactly a dg
A
-module.

Example 1.7
.

A matrix factorization of a regular element
f
in
R
, in the sense of Eisenbud
[
23
]
, can be regarded as a curved dg module. Indeed, let
A=Q[\chi^{\pm 1}]
where
|\chi|=-2
and consider a matrix factorization

P=P_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}P_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}P_{0}\,;

that is, each
P_{i}
is a projective
R
-module and
d_{0}d_{1}=f\cdot\id^{P_{1}}
and
d_{1}d_{0}=f\cdot\id^{P_{0}}
. One can associate to
P
the curved dg
A
-module
\tilde{P}={\mathsf{\Sigma}}A\otimes_{Q}P_{0}\oplus A\otimes_{Q}P_{1}
with

\partial^{\tilde{P}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\chi\otimes d_{1}\\
1\otimes d_{0}&0\end{pmatrix}

where the curvature of
\tilde{P}
is
f\chi\in A_{-2}
. Here
\tilde{P}
can be viewed as the two-periodic sequence

\cdots\to P_{0}\chi^{-1}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}P_{1}\chi^{-1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}P_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}P_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}P_{0}\chi\xrightarrow{d_{0}}P_{1}\chi\to\cdots\,.

1.8
.

Fix a graded commutative
R
-algebra
A
.
We consider the category of curved dg
A
-modules with curvature
w
whose underlying graded
A
-module is a finitely generated graded projective
A
-module; this category is denoted
\mathsf{proj}(A,w).
This is a Frobenius exact category with injective/projective objects summands of
\cone(\id^{P})
with
P
in
\mathsf{proj}(A,w)
, the obvious admissible short exact sequences, and suspension
{\mathsf{\Sigma}}P
the object in
\mathsf{proj}(A,w)
satisfying

({\mathsf{\Sigma}}P)_{n}=P_{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad a\cdot{\mathsf{\Sigma}}p=(-1)^{|a|}{\mathsf{\Sigma}}(a\cdot p)\quad\text{for all }a\in A\,,\ p\in P\,.

Let
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
denote the stable category of
\mathsf{proj}(A,w)
, which then obtains a triangulated structure in the usual way, called the category of
perfect curved dg
A
-modules having curvature
w
. Note that
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
is also the homotopy category of
\mathsf{proj}(A,w)
regarded as a dg category.

1.9
.

Fix
A=\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, a graded commutative noetherian ring. For
v,w\in A_{-2}
there is a pairing

\mathsf{proj}(A,v)\times\mathsf{proj}(A,w)\to\mathsf{proj}(A,v+w)

assigning
(P,P^{\prime})
to the curved dg
A
-module with curvature
v+w
given by

P\otimes_{A}P^{\prime}\quad\text{with}\quad\partial^{P\otimes_{A}P^{\prime}}(x\otimes y)=\partial^{P}(x)\otimes y+(-1)^{|x|}x\otimes\partial^{P^{\prime}}(y)\quad\text{for }x\in P,y\in P^{\prime}\,.

As
-\otimes_{A}-
is bi-exact on projective
A
-modules and bi-additive, it follows that there are induced pairings

\mathsf{perf}(A,v)\times\mathsf{perf}(A,w)\to\mathsf{perf}(A,v+w)\,,

that are bi-exact, bi-additive and suitably compatible.

1.10
.

Paralleling the discussion above in
1.9
, there are also bi-exact, bi-additive functors

\mathsf{perf}(A,v)^{\text{op}}\times\mathsf{perf}(A,w)\to\mathsf{perf}(A,w-v)

where
(P,P^{\prime})\mapsto\Hom_{A}(P,P^{\prime})
with the usual Hom-differential

\partial^{\Hom_{A}(P,P^{\prime})}(f)=\partial^{P^{\prime}}f-(-1)^{|f|}f\partial^{P}\,.

For
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,v)
, we write
P^{\vee}=\Hom_{A}(P,A)
which belongs to
\mathsf{perf}(A,-v)
.

Remark 1.11
.

Note that
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
is the homotopy category of
\mathsf{proj}(A,w)
regarded as a pre-triangulated dg category. In particular,

\Hom_{\mathsf{perf}(A,w)}(P,P^{\prime})=\h(\Hom_{A}(P,P^{\prime}))=\frac{\ker\partial^{\Hom_{A}(P,P^{\prime})}}{\Ima\partial^{\Hom_{A}(P,P^{\prime})}}

for any pair
P,P^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
. Furthermore, for
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,v)
and
P^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
, then there are natural isomorphisms:

(1)

\Hom_{\mathsf{perf}(A,v+w)}(P\otimes_{A}P^{\prime},-)\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{perf}(A,w)}(P^{\prime},\Hom_{A}(P,-))
, and

(2)

\Hom_{A}(-,P^{\prime}\otimes_{A}P)\cong\Hom_{A}(-,P^{\prime})\otimes_{A}P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
.

We will call a graded commutative noetherian ring
regular
if every finitely generated graded module has finite projective dimension.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
A
be a graded commutative noetherian ring. The obvious functor
\mathsf{proj}(A,0)\to\mathsf{D}(A)
induces an exact functor
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\to\mathsf{D}(A)
; if
A
is regular or concentrated in nonnegative degrees, then the functor restricts to an equivalence of triangulated categories
\Phi\colon\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\to\mathsf{perf}(A)
. In particular, if
A
is regular or concentrated in nonnegative degrees, then

\thick_{\mathsf{perf}(A,0)}(A)=\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\,.

Proof.

Note that a curved dg
A
-module with curvature 0 is exactly a dg
A
-module. Also, the suspensions are the same on
\mathsf{proj}(A,0)
and
\mathsf{D}(A)
, exact sequences in
\mathsf{proj}(A,0)
are sent to triangles in
\mathsf{D}(A)
, and phantom maps in
\mathsf{proj}(A,0)
are null-homotopic (in the classical sense), and hence, sent to zero maps in
\mathsf{D}(A)
. Therefore, we obtain the triangulated functor
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\to\mathsf{D}(A)
.

When
A
is concentrated in nonnegative degrees, the fact that
\Phi
is an equivalence follows from
[
4
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Now assume that
A
is regular. In this setting, any graded projective
P
is semiprojective (in the usual sense that
\Hom_{A}(P,-)
is exact and preserves quasi-isomorphisms); cf.
[
37
, Proposition 1.2.8]
. Furthermore, as
A
is noetherian any semiprojective dg
A
-module
P
, that is finitely generated as a graded
A
-module, must be a perfect dg
A
-module; indeed,
\Hom_{\mathsf{D}(A)}(P,-)
commutes with arbitrary direct sums in this case and so by
Example

1.4
it follows that
P
is in
\mathsf{perf}(A)
.

As a consequence of the remarks above, the image of each object
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
under this functor is in fact a perfect dg
A
-module. Thus, we obtain the induced exact functor
\Phi\colon\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\to\mathsf{perf}(A)
. It is clear
\Phi
is essentially surjective; see
Example

1.4
. Finally, a pair of perfect dg
A
-modules are quasi-isomorphic if and only if they are homotopy equivalent, and hence,
\Phi
is an equivalence. Furthermore, the equality now follows immediately, as
\Phi(A)=A
and
\mathsf{perf}(A)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}(A)}(A)
.
∎

Remark 1.13
.

In general, the image of the functor
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\rightarrow\mathsf{D}(A)
is not contained in
\mathsf{perf}(A)
; see Example
2.4
.

2.
Tensor modules and support

Fix a graded commutative noetherian ring
A=\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
First we recall how
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
is a tensor triangulated category
[
7
]
.

Example 2.1
.

By
1.9
, in the notation of
1.8
,
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
is a tensor triangulated category. Explicitly, the tensor product
P\otimes_{A}P^{\prime}
is the underlying tensor product of projective graded
A
-modules
P\otimes_{A}P^{\prime}
with

\partial^{P\otimes_{A}P^{\prime}}(x\otimes y)=\partial^{P}(x)\otimes y+(-1)^{|x|}x\otimes\partial^{P^{\prime}}(y)

for
x\in P
and
y\in P^{\prime}
. Note that
A
is the tensor unit of
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
.

Now assume that
A
is regular. The equivalence
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\equiv\mathsf{perf}(A)
of triangulated categories from
Proposition

1.12
clearly upgrades to one of tensor-triangulated categories, where the category on the right is given the derived tensor product
-\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{A}-
. Therefore, by
Proposition

1.12
, in this setting the tensor unit
A
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
is a
classical generator
of
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
in the sense that

\thick_{\mathsf{perf}(A,0)}(A)=\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\,.

2.2
.

Fix a tensor triangulated category
(\mathsf{T},\otimes,\mathsf{1})
, and
suppose that
\mathsf{T}
acts on a triangulated category
\sf K
in the sense of Stevenson
[
39
, Section 3]
; see, also,
[
40
,
42
]
. Roughly speaking, if we write
\odot
to denote this action, then
\odot\colon\mathsf{T}\times\sf K\to\sf K
is a bi-exact, bi-additive functor that satisfies certain compatibility relations up to natural isomorphisms like associativity and

\mathsf{1}\odot x\simeq x\quad\text{for all }x\text{ in }\mathsf{K}\,.

We call
\sf K
a tensor module of
\mathsf{T}
, and tensor submodules of
\sf K
are exactly the thick subcategories of
\sf K
that are closed under the
\mathsf{T}
-action. Furthermore, if
\mathsf{T}=\thick_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathsf{1})
then every thick subcategory of
\sf K
is necessarily a tensor submodule of
\sf K
.

Given an object
x
(or a set of objects) in
\sf K
, we denote
\thick^{\odot}_{\sf K}(x)
for the smallest thick subcategory of
\sf K
that is closed under the action of
\mathsf{T}
. Therefore, whenever
\mathsf{T}=\thick_{\mathsf{T}}(\mathsf{1})
one has
\thick^{\odot}_{\sf K}(x)=\thick_{\sf K}(x)
for all
x
in
\sf K
.

Example 2.3
.

Fix
w\in A_{-2}
, and recall that
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
is a tensor triangulated category by
Example

2.1
. By
1.9
, we obtain that
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
is a tensor
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
-module. We let
\odot
denote the action of
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
on
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
, and write

\thick_{(A,w)}^{\odot}(P)=\thick_{\mathsf{perf}(A,w)}^{\odot}(P)

where
P
is in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
(or is a set of objects from
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
).

Example 2.4
.

Assume
A=k[y,z]/(y^{2},yz,z^{2})
, where
|y|=0,|z|=-2
, and
k
is a field. Let
M
denote the dg
A
-module whose underlying graded
A
-module is

A\oplus{\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{-1}A
and differential given by
\partial^{M}=\begin{pmatrix}0&z\\
y&0\end{pmatrix}
.
By definition,
M
is a perfect curved dg
A
-module with curvature
0
, i.e.,
M
is in
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
. However, as a dg
A
-module,
M
is not perfect; see
[
4
, Example 4.12.2]
.

Moreover,
\thick_{(A,0)}(A)\neq\thick_{(A,0)}^{\odot}(A)
, as
\thick_{(A,0)}(A)
is not a thick tensor
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
-submodule of
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
.
This follows from the fact that

M\otimes_{A}A\cong M
does not belong to
\thick_{\mathsf{perf}(A,0)}(A):
indeed, if
M
is in
\thick_{(A,0)}(A)
,
then, by applying the exact functor
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)\rightarrow\mathsf{D}(A)
it follow that
M
belongs to
\thick_{\mathsf{D}(A)}(A)
; see
[
4
, Proposition 3.7]
. This contradicts with that
M
is not a perfect dg
A
-module.

For the remainder of this section, fix
w\in A_{-2}
.

2.5
.

Let
\spec(A)
denote the collection of homogeneous primes of
A
. For a homogeneous prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
\spec(A)
, we write
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
for the graded field
A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
and set
w(\mathfrak{p})=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{A}w
in
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
. There is an exact functor

\mathsf{perf}(A,w)\to\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))

induced by assigning to each
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
the perfect curved dg
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
-module
P(\mathfrak{p})\coloneqq\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{A}P=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\odot P
in
\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))
.

When
w\in\mathfrak{p}
, then
w(\mathfrak{p})=0
and since
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is a graded field it follows that

\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))=\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),0)\equiv\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(p))\,;

cf.
Proposition

1.12
. In particular, every object in
\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))
is a direct sum of shifts of copies of
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.

If
w\notin\mathfrak{p}
, then
w(\mathfrak{p})
is a unit in the graded field
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
. In the case that
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is concentrated in even degrees it follows that
\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))\equiv 0.
Indeed, any object
T
in
\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))
is of the form

T=T_{\textrm{even}}\oplus T_{\textrm{odd}}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{0}\\
d_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}

with
d_{0}d_{1}=w(\mathfrak{p})\cdot\id^{T_{\textrm{even}}}
and
d_{1}d_{0}=w(\mathfrak{p})\cdot\id^{T_{\textrm{odd}}}
. Since
w(\mathfrak{p})
is a unit and
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is concentrated in even degrees,
T
is zero in
\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))
as

\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{0}w(\mathfrak{p})^{-1}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

is a null-homotopy for
\id^{T}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
P
be in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
. Define the
support
of
P
to be

\supp_{(A,w)}(P)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec(A):P_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\text{ in }\mathsf{perf}(A_{\mathfrak{p}},w_{\mathfrak{p}})\}\,.

In the previous definition,
P_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the homogeneous localization of the graded
A
-module
P
at the homogeneous prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
; see
[
17
, Section 1.5]
. Furthermore, when we write
\supp_{A}(M)
, for a graded
A
-module
M
, we mean the ordinary support of
M
. That is to say,

\supp_{A}(M)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec(A):M_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}\,.

By Nakayama’s lemma, one also has the following interpretation of support of a perfect curved dg
A
-module.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
one has

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec(A):P(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0\text{ in }\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))\}=\supp_{(A,w)}(P)=\supp_{A}(A\cdot\id^{P})\,.

In particular,
\supp_{(A,w)}(P)
is a closed subset of
\spec(A)
, and
\supp_{(A,w)}(P)=\varnothing
if and only if
P=0
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
.

Proof.

As
\id^{P}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\id^{P_{\mathfrak{p}}}
the second equality holds.
Also, if
P_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
, then
P(\mathfrak{p})=0
. Therefore, it suffices to show if
P(\mathfrak{p})=0
, then
P_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
.

To this end, there exists
\beta\colon P(\mathfrak{p})\to P(\mathfrak{p})
a degree 1 map such that

\partial^{P(\mathfrak{p})}\beta+\beta\partial^{P(\mathfrak{p})}=\id^{P(\mathfrak{p})}\,.

Lift
\beta
to a degree 1 map
\alpha\colon P_{\mathfrak{p}}\to P_{\mathfrak{p}}
satisfying
\alpha(\mathfrak{p})=\beta
. Now observe that

\alpha^{\prime}\coloneqq\alpha\partial^{P_{\mathfrak{p}}}+\partial^{P_{\mathfrak{p}}}\alpha\colon P_{\mathfrak{p}}\to P_{\mathfrak{p}}

is an endomorphism of the curved dg
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module
P_{\mathfrak{p}},
and by construction

\alpha^{\prime}=0\quad\text{in }\mathsf{perf}(A_{\mathfrak{p}},w_{\mathfrak{p}})\,.

However,
\alpha^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})=\id^{P(\mathfrak{p})}
and so by Nakayama’s lemma it follows that
\alpha^{\prime}
is an automorphism of
P_{\mathfrak{p}}.
Combining this with the fact that
\alpha^{\prime}=0
in
\mathsf{perf}(A_{\mathfrak{p}},w_{\mathfrak{p}})
, it follows that
P_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
in
\mathsf{perf}(A_{\mathfrak{p}},w_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

2.8
.

The following are elementary properties of support.

(1)

\supp_{(A,w)}(P)=\supp_{(A,w)}({\mathsf{\Sigma}}P)
;

(2)

\supp_{(A,w)}(P\oplus P^{\prime})=\supp_{(A,w)}(P)\cup\supp_{(A,w)}(P^{\prime})
;

(3)

if
P\to P^{\prime}\to P^{\prime\prime}\to{\mathsf{\Sigma}}P
is an exact triangle in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
, then

\supp_{(A,w)}(P)\subseteq\supp_{(A,w)}(P^{\prime})\cup\supp_{(A,w)}(P^{\prime\prime})\,;

(4)

for any
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
and
P^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,v)
, there is a containment

\supp_{(A,w+v)}(P\otimes_{A}P^{\prime})\subseteq\supp_{(A,w)}(P)\cap\supp_{(A,v)}(P^{\prime})\,.

By
2.5
, when
A
is concentrated in even degrees it follows that

\supp_{(A,w)}(P)\subseteq\mathcal{V}(w)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec(A):w\in\mathfrak{p}\}\,.

Furthermore, in this setting, it also follows that equality holds in (4), above. Indeed,

P\otimes_{A}P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})\cong P(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})

in
\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),0)
and so

P(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})=0\iff P(\mathfrak{p})=0\ \text{ or }\ P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{p})=0\,.

Therefore, it suffices to refer to
Lemma

2.7
.

The next lemma is a curved version of the tensor-nilpotence theorem of Hopkins–Neeman; see
[
27
, Theorem 10]
and
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
; see also
[
21
, Theorem 3.3]
. The version we state here is inspired by (and is a slight generalization of) a version of the tensor-nilpotence theorem for matrix factorizations due to Hirano
[
26
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 2.9

(Curved tensor-nilpotence)
.

Assume
A
is a graded commutative noetherian ring, with
w\in A_{-2}
, and fix
P,P^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
. If
\alpha\colon P^{\prime}\to P
is a map in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
with
\alpha(\mathfrak{p})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\supp_{(A,w)}(P)
, then there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\alpha^{\otimes_{A}n}=0
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,n\cdot w)
.

Proof.

By
Remark

1.11
, there is a natural isomorphism

\Phi\colon\Hom_{\mathsf{perf}(A,w)}(P^{\prime},P)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Hom_{\mathsf{perf}(A,0)}(A,\Hom_{A}(P^{\prime},P))

where
\Phi(\alpha^{\otimes_{A}n})
is identified with
\Phi(\alpha)^{\otimes_{A}n}.
Therefore, we have reduced to the case that
w=0
and
P^{\prime}=A
. Now it remains to note that the desired tensor nilpotence was shown to hold in
\mathsf{perf}(A)
in
[
21
, Theorem 3.3]
, but the proof shows tensor nilpotence holds in
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
; namely, the argument in loc. cit. following their “
Step 2
” holds for objects in
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
, and establishes the desired statement.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\alpha\colon F\to G
be in
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
and
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
. If
\alpha(\mathfrak{p})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\supp_{(A,w)}(P)
, then there exists
n>0
such that
\alpha^{\otimes n}\otimes\id^{P}=0
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w).

Proof.

Below we write
\otimes
for
\otimes_{A}
. First observe that the map
\alpha\otimes\id^{P}\colon F\otimes P\to G\otimes P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
satisfies

\alpha\otimes\id^{P}\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\cong\alpha\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\otimes\id^{P}=0\otimes\id^{P}=0

for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\supp_{(A,w)}(P).
Hence, by
Lemma

2.9
, we have that there is a positive integer
n
where

(\alpha\otimes\id^{P})^{\otimes n}\cong\alpha^{\otimes n}\otimes(\id^{P})^{\otimes n}=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathsf{perf}(A,n\cdot w)\,.

Now observe the desired result follows from the claim that
\alpha^{\otimes i}\otimes\id^{P}
is a retract of
\alpha^{\otimes i}\otimes(\id^{P^{\vee}})^{\otimes i}\otimes(\id^{P})^{\otimes(i+1)}
for all nonnegative integers
i
, as the latter is zero when
i\gg 0
by what we have shown above. The claim follows easily by induction, and so it suffices to check the base case. Here we have that the composition

P\to\Hom_{A}(P,P)\otimes P\cong P\otimes P^{\vee}\otimes P\to P

is the identity, where the first map is given by
x\mapsto\id^{P}\otimes x
, the second map is the isomorphism in
Remark

1.11
(2), and the third map is the evaluation map on the second two factors.
∎

The upshot of the lemmas above is the following theorem, which is a curved version of
[
26
, Proposition 5.5]
; the proof of
Theorem

2.11
follows a similar argument to that in
loc
.
cit
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Assume
A
is a graded commutative noetherian ring, with
w\in A_{-2}
. If
P,P^{\prime}
are in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
, then

P^{\prime}\in\thick^{\odot}_{(A,w)}(P)\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\supp_{(A,w)}(P^{\prime})\subseteq\supp_{(A,w)}(P)\,.

Proof.

The forward implication follows easily from the facts on support listed in
2.8
. For the converse, complete the morphism
A\to\Hom_{A}(P,P)
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
to an exact triangle

F\xrightarrow{\alpha}A\to\Hom_{A}(P,P)\to{\mathsf{\Sigma}}F\,.

We claim that
\alpha^{\otimes n}\otimes\id^{P^{\prime}}
is zero for some positive integer
n
. Indeed, the induced maps

\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\to\Hom_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}(P(\mathfrak{p}),P(\mathfrak{p}))

are nonzero split monomorphisms for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\supp_{(A,w)}(P^{\prime})
, and hence
\alpha(\mathfrak{p})=0
for all such
\mathfrak{p}
. Thus, by
Lemma

2.10
, we have that
\alpha^{\otimes n}\otimes\id^{P^{\prime}}=0
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
. In particular, the triangle

F^{\otimes n}\otimes P^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{\otimes n}\otimes\id^{P^{\prime}}=0}P^{\prime}\to\cone(\alpha^{\otimes n})\otimes P^{\prime}\to{\mathsf{\Sigma}}F^{\otimes n}\otimes P^{\prime}\quad\text{in }\mathsf{perf}(A,w)

says that

P^{\prime}
is a retract of
\cone(\alpha^{\otimes n})\otimes P^{\prime}
.

It is now a formal argument that
\cone(\alpha^{\otimes n})\otimes P^{\prime}
belongs to
\thick_{(A,w)}^{\odot}(P)
, which would finish the proof; we include the argument here for the sake of completeness. For each
i\geqslant 0
, define

C(i)\coloneqq\cone(F^{\otimes i}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{\otimes i}}A^{\otimes i}\cong A)\,.

Now iteratively applying the octahedral axiom we obtain a diagram with exact rows and columns in
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\otimes(i+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(i)\otimes F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{\Sigma}}F^{\otimes(i+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\otimes(i+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(i+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{\Sigma}}F^{\otimes(i+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{\Sigma}}F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{\Sigma}}C(i)\otimes F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{\otimes i}\otimes\id^{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{\otimes(i+1)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}

In particular, there is an exact triangle

C(i)\otimes F\otimes P^{\prime}\to C(i+1)\otimes P^{\prime}\to C(1)\otimes P^{\prime}\to{\mathsf{\Sigma}}C(i)\otimes F\otimes P^{\prime}

and so by induction it would suffice to show
C(1)\otimes P^{\prime}\in\thick_{(A,w)}^{\odot}(P).
However, this is obvious as

C(1)\otimes P^{\prime}=\Hom_{A}(P,P)\otimes P^{\prime}\cong P^{\vee}\otimes P\otimes P^{\prime}\cong P\otimes(P^{\vee}\otimes P^{\prime})\,,

where the first isomorphism uses
Remark

1.11
(2).
∎

3.
Classification of thick tensor submodules

In this section, fix a graded commutative noetherian ring
A
with
w\in A_{-2}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Define the
support
of
(A,w)
to be

\displaystyle\supp(A,w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigcup_{P\in\mathsf{perf}(A,w)}\supp_{(A,w)}(P)

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec(A):\mathsf{perf}(A,w)\to\mathsf{perf}(A_{\mathfrak{p}},w_{\mathfrak{p}})\text{ is nonzero}\}\,.

The next result bounds the support of
(A,w)
in elementary terms when
A
is concentrated in even degrees.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
A
is a graded commutative noetherian ring concentrated in even degrees, and fix
w\in A_{-2}
. There are inclusions

\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec(A):w\in\mathfrak{p}^{2}\}\subseteq\supp(A,w)\subseteq\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec(A):w_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{p}^{2}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\}\,.

In particular,
\supp(A,0)=\spec(A)
.

Furthermore, if, in addition,
A
is assumed to be regular, then

\supp(A,w)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec(A):w_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{p}^{2}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\}\,.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\supp(A,w)
. Then by definition there exists
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
with
P_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
and
w\in\mathfrak{p}
. Since
A
is concentrated in even degrees, then

P=P_{\mathrm{even}}\oplus P_{\mathrm{odd}}\quad\text{with}\quad\partial^{P}=\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{1}\\
d_{0}&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

Since
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is local, using elementary column and row operations,
P_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong T\oplus T^{\prime}\oplus P^{\prime}

where
T=T_{\textrm{even}}\oplus T_{\textrm{odd}}
,
T^{\prime}=T^{\prime}_{\textrm{even}}\oplus T^{\prime}_{\textrm{odd}}
,
P^{\prime}=P^{\prime}_{\textrm{even}}\oplus P^{\prime}_{\textrm{odd}}
, and

\partial^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}0&u^{-1}w\\
u&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\partial^{T^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&(u^{\prime})^{-1}\\
u^{\prime}w&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\text{and }\partial^{P^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{1}^{\prime}\\
d_{0}^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix}

where
u
,
u^{\prime}
are square matrices with units in
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
on their respective diagonals, and
\partial^{P^{\prime}}(P^{\prime})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}P^{\prime}
. Note that
T
and
T^{\prime}
are zero in
\mathsf{perf}(A_{\mathfrak{p}},w_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Since
P\neq 0
, we have that
P^{\prime}\neq 0
.
Therefore,

w_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\id^{P^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&d_{1}^{\prime}\\
d_{0}^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}^{\prime}d_{0}^{\prime}&0\\
0&d_{0}^{\prime}d_{1}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{p}^{2}P^{\prime}\,

and so
w_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{p}^{2}A_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Next, if
w\in\mathfrak{p}^{2}
then we can write
w_{\mathfrak{p}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}
with
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}
. For each
i
, set
P(i)
to be the object in
\mathsf{perf}(A,a_{i}b_{i})
given by

A\oplus{\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{|a_{i}|+1}A\quad\text{with}\quad\partial^{P(i)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{i}\\
b_{i}&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

Therefore,

P\coloneqq P(1)\otimes_{A}\cdots\otimes_{A}P(n)\quad\text{is in}\quad\mathsf{perf}(A,w)\,.

As the entries in the differential of
P_{\mathfrak{p}}
are all non-units in
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
it follows that
P_{\mathfrak{p}}
cannot be zero in
\mathsf{perf}(A_{\mathfrak{p}},w_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Therefore, by definition
\mathfrak{p}\in\supp(A,w).

For the furthermore statement, the same argument as in the proof of
[
26
, Proposition 3.5]
(see also
[
16
, Theorem A.11]
) applies; we briefly sketch it for the convenience of the reader.

One first reduces to the case that
A
is local (in the graded sense), and assume
\mathfrak{p}\in\spec A
satisfies that
w_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{p}^{2}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. If
w_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
, then
w=0
as
A
is a domain, and hence
\mathfrak{p}\in\supp(A,w)
by the already established containment above. If
w_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0
, then
w
is
A
-regular, again making use of the fact that
A
is a domain. In this setting,
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
is equivalent to the singularity category of the graded hypersurface ring
A/(w)
; cf.
[
18
,
34
]
.
In particular,
\supp(A,w)
corresponds to the singular locus of
A/(w)
, that is to say,

\supp(A,w)\cong\{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec A/(w):(A/(w))_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is not regular}\}\,.

Since
w\neq 0
and
w_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{p}^{2}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we get that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}/w_{\mathfrak{p}}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong(A/(w))_{\mathfrak{p}}
is not regular. Thus,
\mathfrak{p}\in\supp(A,w)
. This completes the proof.
∎

.

Remark 3.4
.

As noted in

, when

By
2.8
and

2.7
, the maps
\sigma
and
\tau
are well-defined, respectively. Also, it is clear that the maps are inclusion preserving and satisfy: for any specialization closed subset
\mathcal{V}
of
\supp(A,w)
we have
\sigma\tau(\mathcal{V})\subseteq\mathcal{V}
, and for any thick tensor submodule
\mathsf{T}
of
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
we have
\mathsf{T}\subseteq\tau\sigma(\mathsf{T}).
Therefore, it remains to show the two reverse containments.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{V}
. By
Lemma

2.7
, there exists
P\in\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
such that

P(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0\quad\text{in }\mathsf{perf}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),w(\mathfrak{p}))\,.

Let
\mathfrak{p}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and let
K(i)
denote

A\oplus{\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{|x_{i}|+1}A\quad\text{with}\quad\partial^{K(i)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
x_{i}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
.

K=K(1)\otimes_{A}\cdots\otimes_{A}K(n)
which belongs to
\mathsf{perf}(A,0)
and satisfies
\supp_{(A,0)}(K)=\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{p})
. Therefore,
K\odot P
is in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
and

the first equality holds by
2.8
, and the second equality uses that
\supp_{(A,0)}(K)=\mathcal{V}(\mathfrak{p})

\mathfrak{p}\in\supp_{(A,w)}(P)

\supp_{(A,w)}(P)

\supp_{(A,w)}(P^{\prime})

2.7

\spec(A)
is noetherian, there exist

\mathsf{T}

P^{\prime}\in\thick_{(A,w)}^{\odot}(P)\subseteq\mathsf{T}

\mathsf{T}

\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
.

A

[

E

R

4.6
.

Let
{E}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}\coloneqq E\otimes_{R}E
be the enveloping dg algebra of
E
over
R
, and regard
E
as a dg
{E}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}
-module via the multiplication map
{E}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}\rightarrow E
.
Recall that the Hochschild cohomology of
E
over
R
is

\operatorname{HH}(E/R)\coloneqq\Ext_{{E}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}}(E,E).

By
[
2
, 2.9]
, the graded algebra
\operatorname{HH}(E/R)
is isomorphic to
\h(E)[\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{n}]
, where
|\chi_{i}|=-2
.

For each
M
in
\mathsf{D}(E)
, the left derived functor
-\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}M\colon\mathsf{D}({E}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}})\rightarrow\mathsf{D}(E)
induces a homomorphism of graded
R
-algebras

A\rightarrow\operatorname{HH}(E/R)\xrightarrow{-\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}M}\Ext_{E}(M,M)\,.

This defines a central action of
A
on
\mathsf{D}(E)
;
see
[
6
, Section 3]
. The
cohomological support
of a pair
(M,N)
over
E
is

\V_{E}(M,N)\coloneqq\supp_{A}\Ext_{E}(M,N)\,,

and set
\V_{E}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\V_{E}(M,M)
.

When
M,N
are in
\Df(E)
, by
[
37
, Theorem 4.3.2]
,
\Ext_{E}({M},{N})
is finitely generated over
A
, and hence
\V_{E}(M,N)
is a closed subset of
\spec A
.

Proposition 4.7
.

For each
M,N
in
\Df(E)
, one has

\V_{E}(M,N)=\supp_{(A,0)}(\Hom_{A}(\mathsf{h}M,\mathsf{h}N))\,.

As a consequence,
\V_{E}(M)=\supp_{(A,w)}(\mathsf{h}M)

Proof.

The curved BGG equivalence
\mathsf{h}
from
4.1
induces an
A
-linear isomorphism

\Ext_{E}(M,N)\cong\h(\Hom_{A}(\mathsf{h}M,\mathsf{h}N))\,,

and so the first equality follows immediately. The second equality follows from the first using that

\supp_{(A,0)}(\Hom_{A}(P,P))=\supp_{(A,w)}P

which is a consequence of equality holding in
2.8
(4) for each
P
in
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

For
M,N
in
\Df(E)
, one has
M
is in
\thick_{\mathsf{D}(E)}(N)
if and only if
\V_{E}(M)\subseteq\V_{E}(N)
.

Proof.

Using the classification in
Theorem

4.2
, one only needs to refer to

4.7
.

Corollary 4.9
.

In the notation and setting of
Theorem

4.2
, if
M
is in
\Df(E)
, then

\thick_{\mathsf{D}(E)}(M)=\thick_{\mathsf{D}(E)}(\RHom_{E}(M,E))\,.

Furthermore, the involution
\RHom_{E}(-,E)
on
\Df(E)
fixes the lattice of thick subcategories of
\Df(E)
.

Proof.

Let
(-)^{\vee}=\RHom_{E}(-,E)
, and observe that

\Ext_{E}(M,M)\cong\Ext_{E}(M^{\vee},M^{\vee})\,.

Thus,
\V_{E}(M)=\V_{E}(M^{\vee})
and so the desired result follows from
Propositions

4.7
and
4.8
.
∎

Recall the complexity of
M,N
in
\Df(E)
is

\cx_{E}(M,N)=\inf\{c:\exists a>0\text{ such that }an^{c-1}\geqslant\rank_{k}(\Ext_{E}^{n}(M,N)\otimes k)\text{ for }n\gg 0\}

The next result was shown in
[
37
, Theorem 5.3.1]
using a different approach; this also recovers a result of Avramov–Buchweitz in
[
3
, Corollary 5.7]
(see also
[
31
, Theorem 2]
, which argues in a similar fashion to the way below).

Corollary 4.10
.

For
M,N
in
\Df(E)
, one has
\cx_{E}(M,N)=\cx_{E}(N,M)
.

Proof.

Let
(-)^{\vee}=\RHom_{E}(-,E)
.
If one considers the category
\mathsf{T}
of all
X
in
\Df(E)
such that
\cx_{E}(M,X)\leqslant\cx_{E}(M,N)
, then
\mathsf{T}
is thick. Also, as
N
is in
\mathsf{T}
we can use
Corollary

4.9
to obtain that
N^{\vee}
belongs to
\mathsf{T}
. Thus,
\cx_{E}(M,N^{\vee})\leqslant\cx_{E}(M,N)
. Repeating this argument (three times) it follows that

\cx_{E}(M,N)=\cx_{E}(M^{\vee},N^{\vee})\,.

Now it remains to notice that Grothendieck duality establishes the isomorphism
\Ext_{E}(M^{\vee},N^{\vee})\cong\Ext_{E}(N,M)
and hence,
\cx_{E}(M,N)=\cx_{E}(N,M)
.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

Moving beyond when
R
is regular, one could consider
\Df(E/R)
as in
[
15
,
20
,
28
]
, where many results still hold in the
relative
setting. The objects of
\Df(E/R)
are those of
\Df(E)
that are perfect over
R
. In this setting, the curved BGG functor from
4.1
restricts to an exact embedding
\Df(E/R)\to\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
. However, it is unclear whether the essential image is a tensor submodule of
\mathsf{perf}(A,w)
. If it were, then
it seems likely that the arguments in this article would apply to classify the thick tensor submodules of
\Df(E/R)
, and show things like

\V_{E}(M)\subseteq\V_{E}(N)\iff M\in\thick_{\mathsf{D}(E)}^{\odot}(N)\,.

We end the article by giving a bijective correspondence between
\Sing(A,w)
and the disjoint union of the specific homogeneous spectrum of graded polynomial algebras, as well as some concluding remarks.

For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\spec R
, set

c(\mathfrak{p})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}n-(\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}-\embdim(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/{\bm{f}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}))\,,

where
\dim
and
\embdim
represents the Krull dimension and the embedding dimension respectively. If
{\bm{f}}
is an
R
-regular sequence, then
c(\mathfrak{p})
coincides with codimension of the complete intersection ring
(R/({\bm{f}}))_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proposition 4.12
.

There is a bijection

\supp(A,w)\cong\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec R\text{ \rm and }({\bm{f}})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}}\spec S(\mathfrak{p}),

where
S(\mathfrak{p})=\rm{Sym}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}({\mathsf{\Sigma}}^{-2}\kappa(p)^{c(\mathfrak{p})})
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\supp(A,w)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{M\in\Df(E)}\V_{E}(M)

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec{R}}\bigcup_{M\in\Df(E)}\V_{{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}({M}_{\mathfrak{p}},\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\,,

where the first equality holds by
Theorems

4.2
and
4.7
, and the second equality is from
[
10
, Theorem 11.3]
. Also, note that when
{\bm{f}}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
, then
E_{\mathfrak{p}}
is contractible and so each
\V_{{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}({M}_{\mathfrak{p}},\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))
is empty. Hence, we can assume
{\bm{f}}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
and we are done upon noting that

\V_{{E}_{\mathfrak{p}}}({M}_{\mathfrak{p}},\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\subseteq\spec S(\mathfrak{p})=\V_{E_{\mathfrak{p}}}((R/\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}},\kappa(\mathfrak{p})))\,;

ones makes use of a functionality of cohomological supports in
[
37
, Proposition 3.4.3]
for the containment, and
[
37
, Example 4.1.4(2)]
to obtain the equality.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

Let
\Proj A/(w)
denote the set of homogeneous prime ideals that do not contain
A/(w)_{<0}
. Define
\Sing(\Proj A/(w))\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\supp(A,w)\cap\Proj A/(w)
. It is straightforward to verify that

\supp(A,w)=\Sing(\Proj A/(w))\bigsqcup\{(\mathfrak{p},\chi_{1},\cdots,\chi_{n})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\spec R\text{ and }({\bm{f}})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}\,.

Combining this with Proposition
4.12
, there is a bijection

\Sing(\Proj A/(w))\cong\bigsqcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\spec R\text{ and }({\bm{f}})\subseteq\mathfrak{p}}\Proj S(\mathfrak{p})\,;

this was proved by Stevenson
[
41
, Proposition 10.4]
when
{\bm{f}}
is a regular sequence.

Remark 4.14
.

In this article, we have focused on the classification of thick subcategories of
\Df(E)
. In the spirit of
[
21
]
, we avoid infinite constructions to achieve this result; the former classified thick subcategory of
\Df(A)
when
A
is an artinian complete intersection ring without the use of any infinitely generated modules (over
\Ext_{A}(k,k)
). However, in light of the approach of Neeman
[
33
]
, later clarified by many others like
[
11
,
12
,
39
,
41
]
and more recently in
[
8
,
9
,
14
]
, it is natural to wonder if
\Df(E)
can be regarded as the compact objects in a certain “big” triangulated category
\mathsf{K}
and deduce the classification of thick subcategories in
Theorem

4.2
from a classification of the localizing subcategories of
\mathsf{K}
. In upcoming work joint with Briggs, we flesh out this approach by suitably extending Martin’s thesis to an equivalence, similar to how Stevenson extended an equivalence of Orlov
[
35
, Theorem 2.1]
to certain
big
categories; cf.
[
41
, Proposition 8.7]
.

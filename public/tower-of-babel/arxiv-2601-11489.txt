Quasi-unital inner Kan spaces
Trygve Poppe Oldervoll

Abstract
We show that semi-simplicial spaces that i) admit inner horn fillers up to homotopy and ii) possess units in a weak sense provide a viable model for ∞ \infty -categories. The existence of units can be expressed through various quasi-unitality conditions, and we compare the natural generalization of three such conditions found in the literature. This work is motivated by applications in Floer homotopy theory.

Introduction

In this paper, we study models for
\infty
-categories where units are encoded as a property rather than structure. To illustrate what we mean by this, consider two well-known models for
\infty
-categories: quasi-categories and Segal spaces. Both of these models are based on simplicial objects (sets or spaces) where an
n
-cell corresponds to a string of
n
composable morphisms. The compositional structure is encoded by face maps, while the structure of units is encoded by the degeneracy maps. In particular, the degeneracy map
s_{0}\colon X_{0}\to X_{1}
encodes a choice of unit for each object. Moreover, a functor (simplical map)
F\colon X\to Y
is required to respect this choice. To weaken the rigidity of units, we consider
semi-simplicial
objects. Semi-simplicial objects satisfying analogues of the quasi-category condition or the Segal condition can be thought of as representing
\infty
-
semi-categories
, i.e. higher categories with composition but not necessarily units. To recover a theory of
\infty
-categories, we impose a condition of
quasi-unitality
, which guarantees the existence of units. It turns out that the behaviour of units with respect to composition guarantees that if units exist, they are unique. Quasi-unitality has been studied in the context of quasicategories
[
Hen20
]

[
Ste18
]

[
Tan18
]
, as well as in the context of (higher) Segal spaces
[
Har15
]

[
Hau21
]
. Also in the quasi-category model,
[
AB24
]
makes use of a sufficient condition for quasi-unitality. The goal of this paper is to extend the theory of quasi-unitality for quasi-categories to the context of semi-simplicial spaces admitting inner horn fillers up to homotopy. We will show that this yields a workable model for the theory of
\infty
-categories, and we will show how the different conditions for quasi-unitality in this context relate.

One caveat is that we choose to work in the
\infty
-category of semi-simplicial spaces. This means that our theory relies on an underlying model for
\infty
-categories. This is no big loss as the foundations of
\infty
-categories are already well-developed in other models such as the quasi-category framework of
[
Lur09
]
. The arguments and results of this paper are presented in a model-independent way. We see our results as aligning with
[
Maz25
]
, where the author speculates about a Joyal model structure on the
\infty
-category of simplical spaces. The main construction of this paper should be interpreted as a weak model structure (in the sense of
[
Hen20
]
) on the
\infty
-category of
marked
semi-simplicial spaces, with the main theorem showing that this indeed presents
\Cat_{\infty}
. Because the theory of weak model
\infty
-categories is yet to be developed, we will not state our results in these terms. However, many of our results and arguments are inspired by (weak) model theory. We also expect that the methods in this paper can be used to confirm this speculation made in
[
Maz25
]
.

Our motivation for studying quasi-unital semi-simplicial spaces stems from Floer homotopy theory, where such objects show up when constructing
\infty
-categories of
flow categories
. A flow category should be thought of as a freely generated coherent chain complex in the “ring” of manifolds up to cobordism. This is incarnated as a category enriched in a certain monoidal category of manifolds with corners. In
[
AB24
]
, the authors construct a semi-simplicial set
\Flow
whose 0-simplices are flow categories. Constructing the face maps here is relatively easy because they only involve passing to full subcategories and/or restricting to certain parts of the boundary of manifolds. Producing degeneracies for
\Flow
relies on a more involved geometric construction, meaning that it is hard to produce the full coherence for a simplicial set. It is then easier to show that
\Flow
is quasi-unital.

ss\mathcal{S}=\Fun(\mathbb{\Delta}_{s}^{\op},\mathcal{S})

\infty

for the subcategory spanned by quasi-unital inner Kan spaces and quasi-unital maps between them.

The composite functor

\displaystyle\Map_{ss\mathcal{S}}(\Delta^{m}_{s},J\backslash Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\Map_{ss\mathcal{S}_{+}}(J,Z/\Delta^{m}_{s})

\displaystyle\simeq\Map_{ss\mathcal{S}_{+}}(J,Z([m]))\,.

This means that the bifunctor
\square
can be uniquely characterized preserving colimits in each variable separately,

\displaystyle\Map(\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}\square\Delta^{m}_{s},Z)\simeq Z([m])([n])

and
\Map(\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}\square\Delta^{m},Z)
being the subspace of marked edges in
Z([m])([1])
.

Definition 4.18
.

We say that a map of marked bi-semi-simplicial spaces
Z\to W
is a
vertical fibration
if for any map
j\colon J\to K\in I_{JL}
, the induced map

\displaystyle K\backslash Z\to K\backslash W\timest_{J\backslash W}J\backslash Z

(4.19)

is a Kan fibration of semi-simplicial spaces. We say that
Z
is
vertically fibrant
if the unique map to the terminal object is a vertical fibration.

Remark 4.20
.

Because
K\backslash-
and
J\backslash-
preserve limits, and in particular take the terminal object to the terminal object, we have that
Z
is vertically fibrant if and only if
K\backslash Z\to J\backslash Z
is a Kan fibration for all
J\to K\in I_{JL}
.

Remark 4.21
.

Because
-\backslash Z
and
-\backslash W
take colimits to limits, and because the class of Kan fibrations is closed under pullbacks,
X\to Y
is a vertical fibration if and only if
eq.

4.19
is a Kan fibration for
j\colon J\to K
any marked cofibration.

Consider the functor

\displaystyle p^{*}\colon ss\mathcal{S}_{+}=\Fun([0],ss\mathcal{S}_{+})\to\Fun(\mathbb{\Delta}^{\op}_{s},ss\mathcal{S}_{+})

determined by precomposition with the unique functor
\mathbb{\Delta}^{\op}_{s}\to[0]
. This admits a left adjoint
p_{!}
which takes a semi-simplicial object in
ss\mathcal{S}_{+}
to its colimit. In other words,
p_{!}Z
is the realization of the semi-simplicial object
Z(\Delta^{\bullet}_{s})=Z/\Delta^{\bullet}_{s}
. Using the adjunctions and the fact that mapping out of
\Delta^{n,\flat}
preserves colimits in
ss\mathcal{S}_{+}
, we get

\displaystyle\Map(\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s},p_{!}Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\|\Map(\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s},Z/\Delta^{\bullet}_{s})\|

(4.22)

\displaystyle\simeq\|\Map(\Delta^{\bullet}_{s},\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}\backslash Z)\|

\displaystyle\simeq\|\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}\backslash Z\|\,.

The marking of
p_{!}Z
is determined by the image of the colimit of marked edges in each
Z(\Delta^{m}_{s})
, so by a similar argument to the one above,
p_{!}Z
is marked by the image of

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{1,\sharp}\backslash Z\|\to\|\Delta^{1,\flat}_{s}\backslash Z\|\,.

(4.23)

The point of the vertical fibrancy condition is that it gives us enough Kan fibrations to commute limits with geometric realizations and therefore control
p_{!}Z
. Consider the presheaves
\Map(-,p_{!}Z)
and
\|-\backslash Z\|
on
ssSet_{+}
. When restricted to the full subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subset ssSet_{+}
spanned by
\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}
for
n\geq 0
and
\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}
, we have a natural transformation

\displaystyle\alpha\colon\|-\backslash Z\|\to p_{!}Z(-)

of presheaves on
\mathcal{C}
given by
eq.

4.22
and
eq.

4.23
. Since
p_{!}Z(-)
is right Kan extended from its restriction to
\mathcal{C}
, we can uniquely extend
\alpha
to a natural transformation of presheaves on
ssSet_{+}
.

Lemma 4.24
.

If
Z
is a vertically fibrant bi-semi-simplicial space, and
J
is a semi-simplicial set, then the natural transformation constructed above gives an equivalence

\displaystyle p_{!}Z(J)=\|J\backslash Z\|.

Proof.

The functor
J\mapsto p_{!}Z(J)
maps colimits to limits. By vertical fibrancy,
3.14
and
3.19
,
J\mapsto\|J\backslash Z\|
takes pushouts where one leg is a marked cofibration to pullbacks, and sequential colimits over marked cofibrations to inverse limits. The category of marked semi-simplicial sets is generated by the objects
\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}
and
\Delta^{1,\sharp}
under such colimits. From
eq.

4.22
the presheaves agree on
\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}
so only
\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}
remains. Here it suffices to show that
eq.

4.23
is an equivalence onto its image, i.e. a monomorphism.

The map
\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}\backslash Z\to\Delta^{1,\flat}_{s}\backslash Z
is a Kan fibration between semi-Kan spaces by the vertical fibrancy of
Z
, and a levelwise monomorphism because the map at level
m
is the inclusion of the marked edges in
Z/\Delta^{m}_{s}
. By
3.20
, we can extend any map
\Delta^{0}_{s}\to\Delta^{1,\flat}_{S}\backslash Z
along
\Delta^{0}_{s}\to T
. We can then use
3.14
to compute any fiber of
eq.

4.23
as the geometric realization of a Kan space

\displaystyle F=T\timest_{\Delta^{1,\flat}_{s}\backslash Z}\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}\backslash Z\,,

which has each
F_{n}=\emptyset
or
\ast
. As
F
is Kan, this can only happen if
F=\emptyset
or
T
.
∎

Lemma 4.25
.

There exists a limit-preserving functor

\displaystyle t^{*}\colon ss\mathcal{S}_{+}\to bss\mathcal{S}_{+}

uniquely determined by natural equivalences

\displaystyle J\backslash t^{*}X\simeq\widetilde{U}(X^{J})\,.

Consider the functor

preserves colimits in each variable,

is always a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalence. It follows that any
\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}
-homotopy equivalence is also a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalence.

Lemma 4.42
.

A trivial fibration
f\colon X\to Y
between marked inner Kan spaces is a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalence.

Proof.

First, assume that
X
and
Y
are levelwise discrete. Then the map
f\colon X\to Y
is a trivial fibration between bifibrant objects in the weak Joyal–Lurie model structure. By
[
Hen20
]
, trivial fibrations are also homotopy equivalences, and homotopy in the weak Joyal–Lurie model structure is precisely
\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}
-homotopy. As remarked above,
\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}
-homotopy equivalences are
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalences.

If
X
and
Y
are arbitrary marked inner Kan spaces, the map on underlying semi-simplicial spaces
U(X)\to U(Y)
can be modelled as a vertical fibration between vertically fibrant objects in
\Fun(\Delta_{s}^{\op},sSet)
. By forgetting degeneracies in the target, this produces a map
U(\overline{f})\colon U(\overline{X})\to U(\overline{Y})
of bi-semi-simplicial sets. Using the adjunction

\displaystyle\Map(\Delta^{1}_{s},U(\overline{X})/\Delta^{m}_{s})\simeq\Map(\Delta^{m},\Delta^{1}_{s}\backslash U(\overline{X}))\,,

we can mark the semi-simplicial set
U(\overline{X})/\Delta^{m}_{s}
by marking all the simplices

\displaystyle\Delta^{m}_{s}\to\Delta^{1}_{s}\backslash U(\overline{X})

whose induced map on geometric realizations

\displaystyle\|\Delta^{m}_{s}\|\to\|\Delta^{1}_{s}\backslash U(\overline{X})\|\simeq X(\Delta^{1,\flat}_{s})

factors through the marking of
X
. Doing the same for
Y
produces a map
\overline{f}\colon\overline{X}\to\overline{Y}
of marked bi-semi-simplicial sets such that
p_{!}\overline{f}\simeq f
. We claim moreover that
\overline{f}
is a vertical fibration between vertically fibrant objects. Because we assumed that
U(\overline{f})
is a vertical fibration, it only remains to check that the maps

\displaystyle\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}\backslash\overline{Y}

\displaystyle\to\Delta^{1,\flat}_{s}\backslash\overline{Y},

\displaystyle\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}\backslash\overline{X}

\displaystyle\to\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}\backslash\overline{Y}\timest_{\Delta^{1,\flat}_{s}\backslash\overline{Y}}\Delta^{1,\flat}_{s}\backslash\overline{X}\,,

are Kan fibrations. This follows because we defined the marking in terms of geometric realization, and horn inclusions become equivalences on geometric realization.

Let
J\to K
be a marked inner anodyne map between marked semi-simplicial sets. Because
Y
is a marked inner Kan space, the map

\displaystyle Y(K)\to Y(J)

is surjective on connected components. Because
\overline{Y}
is vertically fibrant, we can use
4.24
to write this as the geometric realization of the map

\displaystyle K\backslash\overline{Y}\to J\backslash\overline{Y},

(4.43)

which is moreover a Kan fibration between Kan semi-simplicial sets. Since
eq.

4.43
is a Kan fibration and induces surjection on connected components, it must necessarily be surjective on
0
-cells. Because the inclusion of the zeroeth vertex
\Delta^{0}_{s}\to\Delta^{m}_{s}
is anodyne, it follows by Kan fibrancy that
eq.

4.43
is also surjective on
m
-cells for all
m
. By adjunction, this implies that each row
Y/\Delta^{m}_{s}
is marked inner Kan.

Now let
J\to K
be any marked cofibration between marked semi-simplicial sets. Because
f
is a trivial fibration, the map

\displaystyle X(K)\to X(J)\timest_{Y(J)}Y(K)

(4.44)

is surjective on connected components. Because
\overline{f}
is a vertical fibration, we can use
4.24
and
3.14
to write
eq.

4.44
as the geometric realization of the Kan fibration

\displaystyle K\backslash\overline{X}\to J\backslash\overline{X}\timest_{J\backslash\overline{Y}}K\backslash\overline{Y}.

Arguing as above, this map is surjective on
m
-simplices for all
m
, so each row

\displaystyle\overline{f}/\Delta^{m}_{s}\colon\overline{X}/\Delta^{m}_{s}\to\overline{Y}/\Delta^{m}_{s}

is a trivial fibration between levelwise discrete marked inner Kan spaces, and therefore a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
equivalence by the first part of this proof. Hence
f\simeq p_{!}\overline{f}
is a colimit of
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
equivalences, and therefore itself a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalence.
∎

Corollary 4.45
.

For a marked inner Kan space
X
, the map
u_{X}\colon X\to\mathcal{T}(X)
factors uniquely through an equivalence
\widetilde{u}_{X}\colon\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}(X)\to\mathcal{T}(X)
.

Proof.

The factorization exists because
\mathcal{T}(X)
is a complete semi-Segal space. The map
\widetilde{u}_{X}
is a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
equivalence by 2-out-of-3 because
X\to\mathcal{T}(X)
is a trival Kan fibration and therefore a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
equivalence by
4.42
.
∎

Because every marked complete semi-Segal space is also a marked inner Kan space, we get a restricted localization

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{IK}_{+}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{CSS}_{+}:i_{\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}.

Corollary 4.46
.

The restricted functor
\mathcal{T}\colon\mathcal{IK}_{+}\to\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
exhibits a localization at trivial fibrations.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{L}_{w}\colon\mathcal{IK}_{+}\to\mathcal{IK}_{+}[w^{-1}]
for the localization at trivial fibrations. By
4.42
, trivial fibrations between inner Kan spaces are
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
equivalences, so by the universal property of localizations, the restriction of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}
factors as

\displaystyle\mathcal{IK}_{+}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_{w}}\mathcal{IK}_{+}[w^{-1}]\xrightarrow{F}\mathcal{CSS}_{+}\,.

Now we claim that the composite

\displaystyle G\colon\mathcal{CSS}_{+}\xhookrightarrow{i_{\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}}\mathcal{IK}_{+}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}_{w}}\mathcal{IK}_{+}[w^{-1}]

is an inverse of
F
. The relation
F\circ G\sim id
follows immedeatly from
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}\circ i_{\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}\sim id
. For the other direction, consider the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{IK}_{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{IK}_{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{IK}_{+}[w^{-1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{IK}_{+}[w^{-1}]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{w}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\mathcal{CSS}_{+}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{w}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}

Because the natural transformation

has components that are trivial fibrations, the whiskered transformation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{w}(u)\colon\mathcal{L}_{w}\to\mathcal{L}_{w}\circ\mathcal{T}\simeq G\circ F\circ\mathcal{L}_{w}

is a natural equivalence. It then follows from the uniqueness part of the universal property of localizations that

.
∎

5
Slices and functor categories

Thanks to the description of
\mathcal{T}
as a localization at fibrations, we can describe how
\mathcal{T}
behaves with respect to certain right-adjoint constructions. In particular, we will treat exponential objects and slices. The case of slices is of particular interest to applications to flow categories
[
HO
]
because slices govern limits and colimits. A central part of proving that
\infty
-categories of flow categories are stable is identifying the most basic limits and colimits.

In this section, we observe a typical feature of weak model structures: constructions are well-behaved as long as we map out of cofibrant (levelwise discrete) objects into fibrant objects (marked inner Kan spaces).

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
X
be a marked inner Kan space, and
J
a marked semi-simplicial set. Then the natural map

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(X^{J})\to\mathcal{T}(X)^{J}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

The natural map comes from a
(\colim\lim\to\lim\colim)
comparison. Evaluating at any marked semi-simplicial set
S
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(X^{J})(S)

\displaystyle\simeq\|\widetilde{U}((X^{J})^{S})\|

\displaystyle\simeq\|\widetilde{U}(X^{J\otimes S})\|

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{T}(X)(J\otimes S)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{T}(X)^{J}(S)\,.

Here, we use the fact that
X^{J}
is a marked inner Kan space by
4.27
and then apply
4.31
twice.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
W
be a marked complete semi-Segal space, and
J\to K
a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalence. Then restriction induces an equivalence
W^{K}\to W^{J}
between marked complete semi-Segal spaces.

Proof.

It follows by adjunction from
4.26
that
W^{K}\to W^{J}
is orthogonal to
J_{JL}
whenever
J\to K
is a marked cofibration between levelwise discrete objects. Applying this to
\emptyset\to J
implies that
W^{J}
is a complete marked semi-Segal space whenever
J
is levelwise discrete. Because
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
is closed under limits, and the levelwise discrete objects generate
ss\mathcal{S}_{+}
under colimits, and
W^{(-)}
takes colimits to limits, this shows that any
W^{J}
is a marked complete semi-Segal space.

Similarly, when
J\to K\in J_{JL}
, it follows by adjunction from
4.26
that
W^{K}\to W^{J}
is orthogonal to all marked cofibrations between levelwise discrete objects. Applying this at
\emptyset\to A
gives the equivalence

\displaystyle W^{K}(A)\simeq W^{J}(A)\,.

Now the class of maps
J\to K
such that
W^{K}\to W^{J}
is an equivalence is strongly saturated and contains
J_{JL}
, and therefore all
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalences.
∎

Recall that
\mathcal{L}\colon ss\mathcal{S}\to s\mathcal{S}
is given by left Kan extension along
\mathbb{\Delta}_{s}\to\mathbb{\Delta}
, while
\mathcal{R}_{+}\colon ss\mathcal{S}_{+}\to s\mathcal{S}
is the right adjoint to the marked forgetful functor

4.10
constructed in
[

]
.

Corollary 5.3
.

If
J
is a semi-simplicial set and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{+}\mathcal{T}\left(X^{J^{\flat}}\right)\to\left(\mathcal{R}_{+}\mathcal{T}(X)\right)^{\mathcal{L}(J)}

and

.

Since
X
is a marked inner Kan space,
\mathcal{T}(X)
is a complete semi-Segal space. By
[

]
Lemma 3.4.1, there are natural
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalences

, so combining

and

we have equivalences

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(X^{J^{\flat}})\simeq\mathcal{T}(X)^{J^{\flat}}\simeq\mathcal{T}(X)^{\mathcal{F}_{+}\mathcal{L}(J)}\,.

(5.4)

By
[
Har15
]
Lemma 3.4.2, the functor
\mathcal{F}_{+}
has a lax monoidal structure where each

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{+}(Y)\otimes\mathcal{F}_{+}(Z)\to\mathcal{F}_{+}(Y\times Z)

is a
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
equivalence. Fixing

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)^{Z}\to\mathcal{R}_{+}(W^{\mathcal{F}_{+}Z})\,,

(5.5)

which is an equivalence whenever

is a complete marked semi-Segal space.
We can therefore apply
\mathcal{R}_{+}
to

, and further compose

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{+}\mathcal{T}(J^{\flat})\simeq\mathcal{R}_{+}\left(\mathcal{T}(X)^{\mathcal{F}_{+}\mathcal{L}(J)}\right)\simeq\mathcal{R}_{+}\mathcal{T}(X)^{\mathcal{L}(J)},

5.5
for

.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

For
J
and
K
simplicial sets, there are natural maps

\displaystyle J\diamond K\to J\star K

(5.7)

that are
css
-equivalences. In other words, any complete Segal space is orthogonal to

5.7
.

Proof.

This follows from the proof of
[
Lur09
]

-equivalences, and the collection of

-equivalences is closed under colimits.
∎

Let
J
be an unmarked semi-simplicial set and
W
a complete semi-Segal space. Through the chain of maps

\displaystyle\Map(J^{\flat},W)\xleftarrow{\simeq}\Map(\mathcal{F}_{+}\mathcal{L}(J),W)\simeq\Map(\mathcal{L}(J),\mathcal{R}_{+}W)\,,

any

, determines a map
\overline{p}\colon\mathcal{L}(J)\to\mathcal{R}_{+}W
.

Corollary 5.8
.

In the above situation, we have a chain of natural equivalences

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{+}(W_{p/})\simeq\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)_{\overline{p}/}\simeq\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)^{\tilde{p}/}\,,

compatible with projection to
\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)
.

Proof.

First note that since the category of complete Segal spaces is closed under forming exponentials and pullbacks, it follows from
2.8
that
\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)^{\overline{p}/}
is also a complete Segal space.

For each
n
, pullback along the
css
-equivalence

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(J)\diamond\Delta^{n}\to\mathcal{L}(J)\star\Delta^{n}

determines an equivalence

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)_{\overline{p}/})(\Delta^{n})\to(\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)^{\overline{p}/})(\Delta^{n})\,.

(5.9)

These maps are natural in
\Delta^{n}
and

, so they assemble to an equivalence of simplicial spaces over
\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)
.

Similarly, pulling back along the
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
-equivalences

\displaystyle J^{\flat}\star\Delta_{s}^{n,\flat}\to\mathcal{F}_{+}\mathcal{L}(J\star\Delta_{s}^{n})\to\mathcal{F}_{+}(\mathcal{L}(J)\star\mathcal{L}(\Delta^{n}_{s})).

induces an equivalence

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{+}\left(\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)_{\overline{p}/}\right)\to W_{p/}

(5.10)

covering the counit
\mathcal{F}_{+}\mathcal{R}_{+}W\xrightarrow{\sim}W
. Thanks to the equivalence

,

is a complete Segal space, and by
[

]
,
\mathcal{R}_{+}
and

restrict to inverse equivalences between
\mathcal{CSS}_{+}
and

. Therefore the adjunct of

is an equivalene
\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)_{\overline{p}/}\to\mathcal{R}_{+}(W_{p/})
covering the identity on
\mathcal{R}_{+}(W)
.
∎

We now wish to investigate how slices behave with respect to the localization
\mathcal{T}
. For this purpose, we need yet another kind of fibration.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
J_{L}
be the collection of maps in
ss\mathcal{S}_{+}
consisting of

and the left horns
\Lambda_{0,s}^{n,\flat}\to\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}
for

. We call

-fibrations

, and

-cofibrations

Dually, let

denote the collection of maps consisting of

and the right horns
\Lambda_{n,s}^{n,\flat}\to\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}
for
1\leq n
. We call

-fibrations

, and
J_{R}
-cofibrations
marked right anodyne
.

Lemma 5.12
.

If
a\colon A\to B
and

are marked cofibrations between marked semi-simplicial sets, then the induced map

\displaystyle f\colon J\star B\underset{J\star A}{\amalga}K\star A\to K\star B

is again a marked cofibration, and:

1.

If
a
is marked left anodyne, then
f
is marked inner anodyne.

2.

If
j
is marked left anodyne, then
f
is also marked left anodyne.

The dual statements also hold:

1.

If
j
is marked right anodyne, then
f
is marked inner anodyne.

2.

If
a
is marked right anodyne, then
f
is also marked right anodyne.

Proof.

One can see directly from the formula for
\star
that
f
is levelwise injective.
The rest of the proof is a minor modification of
[
Lur09
]
Lemma 2.1.2.3. As there, we may reduce to the case where
j\in I_{JL}
. If

is
\Delta^{1,\flat}_{s}\to\Delta^{1,\sharp}_{s}
, the map

becomes an equivalence, so it suffices to treat the boundary inclusions
\partial\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}\to\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}
. As in
[
Lur09
]
, we may assume that
a
is one of the generating left anodynes. When

is
\Lambda^{n,\flat}_{i}\to\Delta^{n,\flat}_{s}
, where

, this works just as in the unmarked case. When

is

\displaystyle(\Lambda^{n}_{n},\left\{n-1,n\right\})\to(\Delta_{s}^{n},\left\{n-1,n\right\})

the map

becomes

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{n+m+1,s}^{n+m+1},\{n+m,n+m+1\})\to(\Delta^{n+m+1}_{s},\left\{n+m,n+m-1\right\})\,,

which is still marked inner anodyne.
When

becomes a re-marking of

Lemma 5.18

6

In this subsection, we compare three conditions for quasi-unitality. These are

Definition 6.1

Remark 6.2

Definition 6.3

Remark 6.4
.

Note that by definition, the inclusion of the subspace

Remark 6.5
.

Because the marked horn inclusions

Definition 6.6

An edge

2.

There exists a 2-cell

Definition 6.7
.

We say that an inner Kan space

. We say that a map

Remark 6.8
.

Note that the image of an idempotent is always an idempotent, so it suffices to assume that each

is an equivalence. In fact, it follows from the proof of

In

Definition 6.9
.

Let

denote the (non-full) subcategory spanned by quasi-unital inner Kan spaces and quasi-unital maps between them.

Definition 6.10
.

For a semi-simplicial space

Lemma 6.11

A lifting problem of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial\Delta^{n}_{s}}

Consider the following diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{f/}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{x/}\timest_{Y_{p(x)/}}Y_{p(f)/}}

Corollary 6.13

Lemma 6.14
.

Let

be a quasi-category. Then an edge

in

is invertible if and only if the corresponding edge in

is an equivalence in the sense of

Because join commutes with left Kan extension, we get by uniqueness of right adjoints that for any semi-simplicial map

Hence, it follows from the Yoneda lemma that

For the converse, it follows from

Proposition 6.21

The key fact left to show is that if

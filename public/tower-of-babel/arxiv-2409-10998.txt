Hyperuniformity in regular trees
Mattias Byléhn

Abstract
. We study notions of hyperuniformity for invariant locally square-integrable point processes in regular trees. We show that such point processes are never geometrically hyperuniform, and if the diffraction measure has support in the complementary series then the process is geometrically hyperfluctuating along all subsequences of radii. A definition of spectral hyperuniformity and stealth of a point process is given in terms of vanishing of the complementary series diffraction and sub-Poissonian decay of the principal series diffraction near the endpoints of the principal spectrum. Our main contribution is providing examples of stealthy invariant random lattice orbits in trees whose number variance grows strictly slower than the volume along some unbounded sequence of radii. These random lattice orbits are constructed from the fundamental groups of complete graphs and the Petersen graph.

1.
Introduction

The notion of
hyperuniformity
or
superhomogeneity
introduced by Stillinger and Torquato in
[
16
]
is, in its mathematical formulation, the property of an invariant point process having sub-Poissonian number variance in the large-scale radial limit. An equivalent formulation is that the so called structure factor of the point process has sub-Poissonian decay in the small scale limit for the relevant frequency domain. This property has been studied extensively for invariant point processes in Euclidean spaces by many, and more recently in compact symmetric geometries as well as real hyperbolic spaces
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
15
]
.

In this paper we investigate notions of hyperuniformity and asymptotics of number variances of invariant point processes in regular trees. Similarly to the real hyperbolic setting in
[
3
]
, we prove for any invariant point process in a regular tree that there is an unbounded sequence of metric balls for which the number variance grows at least as fast as the volume of the balls in the radial limit. Moreover, we provide examples of random lattice orbits for which there is an unbounded sequence of balls along which the number variance grows strictly slower than the volume of the balls. We also provide random lattice orbits where the number variance grows as fast as the volume along any unbounded sequence of balls. The lattices involved are constructed from fundamental groups of finite regular graphs.

We also define the diffraction measure of an invariant point process in a regular tree, generalizing the notion of structure factor, and define spectral hyperuniformity of such a point process to mean that the diffraction measure has sub-Poissonian decay near the ends of the
L^{2}
-spectrum of the tree, and vanishes on the rest of the spectrum. An invariant point process is defined to be stealthy if the diffraction measure vanishes completely near the ends, and the lattices mentioned in the previous paragraph that we construct from finite regular graphs are stealthy in this sense. In particular, these finite graphs, for example complete and regular complete bipartite graphs, are
Ramanujan graphs
in the sense of Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak
[
11
]
. Every finite regular graph for which the random lattice orbit of the fundamental group is spectrally hyperuniform is a Ramanujan graph, and the converse holds if and only if the adjacency matrix of the Ramanujan graph does not admit eigenvalues at the endpoints of the
L^{2}
-spectrum.

This paper is motivated by a question posed in

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Var}(|\mathcal{P}\cap B_{r}(o)|)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}(B_{r}(o))}>0\,.

Is there an (explicit) isometrically invariant locally square-integrable point process

\mathbb{H}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Var}(|\mathcal{P}\cap B_{r}(o)|)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}}(B_{r}(o))}=0\,?

1.2.
Point processes on regular trees

Let
q\geq 2
and consider the
(q+1)
-regular tree
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
, which we will think of as a discrete metric space and let
\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
denote the space of positive locally finite measures on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. If
\mathcal{P}
is an
\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
-valued random variable then the associated
(weighted) point process

\mu=\mu_{\mathcal{P}}
will to us simply be the law of
\mathcal{P}
. A point process
\mu
is
invariant
if it is invariant under the action of the automorphism group
G_{q}=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
, and
locally square-integrable
if

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})}p(\{x\})^{2}d\mu(p)<+\infty

for every
x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. To study invariant locally square-integrable point processes
\mu
one introduces for each finitely supported function
f:\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
a
linear statistic

\mathbb{S}f:\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}f(p)=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}f(x)\,dp(x)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}p(\{x\})f(x)\,.

Many interesting point processes are uniquely determined by the associated moments of these linear statistics, for example random lattice orbits, Poisson point processes and determinantal point processes. In the notation of the previous subsection,

\mathrm{Var}(|\mathcal{P}\cap B_{r}(o)|)=\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_{\mathcal{P}}}(\mathbb{S}\chi_{B_{r}(o)})\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
\chi_{B_{r}(o)}
denotes the indicator function of the ball
B_{r}(o)
.

1.3.
The diffraction measure of a point process

In order to do (spherical) harmonic analysis on regular trees, we identify
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
with the homogeneous space
G_{q}/K_{q}
where
G_{q}=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
is the group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
and
K_{q}
is the stabilizer subgroup of a fixed root
o\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. On
G_{q}
there is the
spherical transform
, available for functions in the
Hecke algebra

\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
of bi-
K_{q}
-invariant compactly supported functions
\varphi:G_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)=\int_{G_{q}}\varphi(g)\omega_{\lambda}(g)dm_{G_{q}}(g)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,.

Here
\omega_{\lambda}
are the
K_{q}
-spherical functions
for
G_{q}
,

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}(g)=c_{q}(\lambda)q^{-(1/2-i\lambda)d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)}+c_{q}(-\lambda)q^{-(1/2+i\lambda)d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)}\,\mathchar 59\relax

and
c_{q}(\lambda)=\tfrac{1}{q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2}}\tfrac{q^{1/2+i\lambda}-q^{-1/2-i\lambda}}{q^{i\lambda}-q^{-i\lambda}}
is the Harish-Chandra
c
-function for
G_{q}
.

The
diffraction measure
of an invariant locally square-integrable point process
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
is the unique positive finite Borel measure
\sigma_{\mu}
supported on

\displaystyle\Lambda_{q}=i[0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}]\cup(0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)})\cup(\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}+i[0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}])\subset\mathbb{C}

such that for all
\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{S}\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{S}\varphi_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\int_{\Lambda_{q}}\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\varphi}_{2}(\lambda)}d\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)\,.

The existence and uniqueness of this measure holds in the generality of random measures in commutative spaces
X=G/K
associated to a locally compact second countable Gelfand pair
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
with
K<G
compact. These spaces include regular trees, Euclidean spaces, (higher rank) symmetric spaces as well as Bruhat-Tits buildings and products of such. We prove this in upcoming work with M. Björklund.

1.4.
Number variances

Given an invariant locally square-integrable point process
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
, its
number variance
is the function
\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by the variance of the linear statistic of the indicator function on a centered metric ball,

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)=\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}\chi_{B_{r}(o)})=q^{r}\int_{\Lambda_{q}\backslash\{\tfrac{i}{2}\}}\frac{(\sin_{q}(\lambda(r+1))+q^{-1/2}\sin_{q}(\lambda r))^{2}}{\sin^{2}_{q}(\lambda)}d\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)\,.

Due to the presence of
\pi/\log(q)+i/2\in\Lambda_{q}
, corresponding to the spherical sign function and to the eigenvalue
-(q+1)
of the adjacency operator
\Delta_{q}
, it will be more convenient to consider what we will call the
sub-oscillatory number variance

\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)=q^{r}\int_{\Lambda_{q}^{*}}\frac{(\sin_{q}(\lambda(r+1))+q^{-1/2}\sin_{q}(\lambda r))^{2}}{\sin^{2}_{q}(\lambda)}d\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\Lambda_{q}^{*}=\Lambda_{q}\backslash\{\tfrac{i}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}+\tfrac{i}{2}\}\,.

In the case of random lattice orbits associated to fundamental groups of finite regular graphs, this will correspond to the removal of the eigenvalue
-(q+1)
of the adjacency operator when the graph is bipartite.

1.5.
Asymptotics of number variances

A first choice for defining geometric hyperuniformity of an invariant locally square-integrable point process
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
is to require that

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}^{*}_{\mu}(r)}{|B_{r}(o)|}=0\,\mathchar 59\relax

and to say that
\mu
is geometrically hyperfluctuating if

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}^{*}_{\mu}(r)}{|B_{r}(o)|}=+\infty\,.

Similarly to the results in
[
3
, Theorem 1.4]
for point processes in real hyperbolic spaces, we show that no such point processes
\mu
are geometrically hyperuniform in this sense, and that the existence of complementary series diffraction yields a geometrically hyperfluctuating point process. The following can be seen as the analogue of Beck’s Theorem in
[
1
, Theorem 2A]
for regular trees.

Theorem 1.3

(Non-geometric hyperuniformity)
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable point process in
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. Then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)}{|B_{r}(o)|}>0\,.

Moreover,

(1)

if
\sigma_{\mu}(\{0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}\})>0
, then

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)}{r^{2}|B_{r}(o)|}>0\,.

(2)

if there is a
\delta\in(0\mathchar 59\relax 1)
such that
\sigma_{\mu}(i[\tfrac{\delta}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}))>0
or
\sigma_{\mu}(\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}+i[\tfrac{\delta}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}))>0
, then

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)}{|B_{r}(o)|^{1+\delta}}>0\,.

It follows that if
\mu
admits complementary series diffraction, then it is geometrically hyperfluctuating along
all
subsequences of radii by the above Theorem. In light of this Theorem, it is natural to investigate when

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)}{|B_{r}(o)|}=0

or not. We provide a criterion for when atoms in the diffraction picture yield a positive lower limit. To state it, consider the set

\displaystyle\Lambda_{q}^{\mathrm{Rat}}=\begin{cases}(0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(2)})\cap\tfrac{\pi}{\log(2)}\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{\tfrac{3\pi}{4\log(2)}\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{5\pi}{12\log(2)}\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{11\pi}{12\log(2)}\}&\mbox{ if }q=2\\
(0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(3)})\cap\tfrac{\pi}{\log(3)}\mathbb{Q}\backslash\{\tfrac{5\pi}{6\log(3)}\}&\mbox{ if }q=3\\
(0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)})\cap\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}\mathbb{Q}&\mbox{ if }q\geq 4\,.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable point process on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
with diffraction measure
\sigma_{\mu}
, and suppose that there is a
\lambda\in\Lambda_{q}^{\mathrm{Rat}}
such that
\sigma_{\mu}(\{\lambda\})>0
. Then

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)}{|B_{r}(o)|}>0\,.

The proof requires a detailed analysis of trigonometric numbers, and the main tool is the rational linear independence of pairs of trigonometric numbers established by Berger in
[
2
, Theorem 1.2]
, extending a classical Theorem of Niven
[
14
, Corollary 3.12]
.

1.6.
Random lattice orbits of fundamental groups

Given a finite simple connected
(q+1)
-regular graph
\mathfrak{X}

(q+1)
-regular tree

is a cocompact lattice acting freely on

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathfrak{X}}=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{X}|^{2}}\delta_{i/2}+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{X}}}m_{\mathfrak{X}}(\lambda)\delta_{\lambda}\,.

Here
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{X}}

such that

is an eigenvalue of the adjacency operator

on

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mathfrak{X}}^{*}(r)=q^{r}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{X}}^{*}}m_{\mathfrak{X}}(\lambda)\frac{(\sin_{q}(\lambda(r+1))+q^{-1/2}\sin_{q}(\lambda r))^{2}}{\sin^{2}_{q}(\lambda)}\,.

1.7.
Spectral hyperuniformity and stealth

We define spectral hyperuniformity and stealth with respect to the diffraction measure
\sigma_{\mathrm{Poi}}
of the unit intensity invariant Poisson point process from Equation
1.1
. One can show that, for small
\varepsilon>0
we have

\sigma_{\mathrm{Poi}}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])\asymp\varepsilon^{3}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\sigma_{\mathrm{Poi}}([\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}-\varepsilon\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}))\asymp\varepsilon^{3}\,.

Definition 1.8

(Spectrally hyperuniform and stealthy point processes)
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable point process on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. We say that

•

\mu
is
spectrally hyperuniform
if
\sigma_{\mu}(i[0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}))=\sigma_{\mu}(\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}+i[0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}))=0
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon^{-3}\sigma_{\mu}\Big((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon]\cup[\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}-\varepsilon\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)})\Big)=0

•

\mu
is
stealthy
if
\sigma_{\mu}(i[0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}))=\sigma_{\mu}(\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}+i[0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}))=0
and there is an
\varepsilon_{o}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu}\Big((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon_{o}]\cup[\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}-\varepsilon_{o}\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)})\Big)=0\,.

Stealthy point processes are spectrally hyperuniform. The random lattice orbits in Theorem
1.5
are all examples of stealthy point processes in
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. More generally, a finite simple connected
(q+1)
-regular graph
\mathfrak{X}
is
Ramanujan
if the eigenvalues of the adjacency operator
\Delta_{\mathfrak{X}}
on
\mathfrak{X}
, with the exception of
\pm(q+1)
, are confined to
[-2\sqrt{q}\mathchar 59\relax 2\sqrt{q}]
, meaning that the diffraction measure
\sigma_{\mathfrak{X}}
vanishes on the complementary series,

\sigma_{\mathfrak{X}}(i(0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}))=\sigma_{\mathfrak{X}}(\tfrac{\pi}{\log(q)}+i(0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{1}{2}))=0\,.

Thus the random lattice orbit
\mu_{\mathfrak{X}}
of the fundamental group
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{X}}
is stealthy if and only if
\mathfrak{X}
is Ramanujan and
\pm 2\sqrt{q}
is not an eigenvalue of the adjacency operator
\Delta_{\mathfrak{X}}
. Excluding the eigenvalue
-(q+1)
of
\Delta_{q}
, or equivalently the spherical parameter
\pi/\log(q)+i/2\in\Lambda_{q}
, is of particular importance since there are infinitely many regular bipartite Ramanujan graphs in every degree of any number of vertices as proven by Marcus, Spielman and Srivastava in
[
12
, Theorem 5.3]
and
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
. In fact, the graphs constructed in
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
result in stealthy random lattice orbits according to our definition. We take the stance that the random lattice orbits of the fundamental groups associated to such bipartite graphs should be stealthy rather than maximally hyperfluctuating.

We end this Introduction by posing a question regarding random lattice orbits of fundamental groups.

Question
:
What class of Ramanujan graphs
\mathfrak{X}
satisfy

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mathfrak{X}}^{*}(r)}{|B_{r}(o)|}=0\,?

2.1.
Automorphism groups of graphs

A
directed graph
or
digraph
is a pair
\mathfrak{X}=(V_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathchar 59\relax E_{\mathfrak{X}})
where
V_{\mathfrak{X}}
is a finite or countable set of
vertices
and
E_{\mathfrak{X}}\subset V_{\mathfrak{X}}\times V_{\mathfrak{X}}
is a subset of (directed)
edges
. Such a digraph
\mathfrak{X}
is

•

undirected
if
(x\mathchar 59\relax y)\in E_{\mathfrak{X}}
implies
(y\mathchar 59\relax x)\in E_{\mathfrak{X}}
,

•

loop-free
if
(x\mathchar 59\relax x)\notin E_{\mathfrak{X}}
for all
x\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
,

•

simple
if it is undirected and loop-free,

•

regular
if the digraph is simple and there is a
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for every
x\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
,

\deg(x):=|\{y\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}\,|\,(x\mathchar 59\relax y)\in E_{\mathfrak{X}}\}|=d\,.

If such a
d
exists, we say that
\mathfrak{X}
is
d
-regular
.

•

connected
if for every pair of vertices
x\mathchar 59\relax y\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
there are vertices
x=v_{1}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax v_{m}=y
such that
(v_{i}\mathchar 59\relax v_{i+1})\in E_{\mathfrak{X}}
for all
i=1\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax m-1
.

We will restrict our attention to simple regular connected digraphs
\mathfrak{X}
, unless told otherwise, and refer to them as
simple regular connected graphs
.

Given a simple connected graph
\mathfrak{X}
we may regard it as a discrete metric space
(V_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathchar 59\relax d_{\mathfrak{X}})
, where
d_{\mathfrak{X}}(x\mathchar 59\relax y)
is the minimal length of all paths between vertices
x
and
y
. For such graphs we will abbreviate
v\in\mathfrak{X}
for
v\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
. The set of edges can then be recovered as
E_{\mathfrak{X}}=d_{\mathfrak{X}}^{-1}(\{1\})
. The metric ball centered at a vertex
x\in\mathfrak{X}
of radius
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is

\displaystyle B_{r}(x)=\{y\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}\,|\,d_{\mathfrak{X}}(x\mathchar 59\relax y)\leq r\}\,.

A
digraph homomorphism
between two digraphs
\mathfrak{X}_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\mathfrak{X}_{2}
is a map
g:V_{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}\rightarrow V_{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}
such that
(g(x)\mathchar 59\relax g(y))\in E_{\mathfrak{X}_{2}}
whenever
(x\mathchar 59\relax y)\in E_{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}
, and we denote such a digraph homomorphism by
g:\mathfrak{X}_{1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{2}
. A
digraph isomorphism
is a bijective digraph homomorphism whose inverse is a digraph homomorphism, and a digraph isomorphism from a digraph to itself is called a
digraph automorphism
. The group of digraph automorphisms of a digraph
\mathfrak{X}
will be denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
, and we note that if
\mathfrak{X}
is simple then this set coincides with the group of isometries of the metric space
(V_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathchar 59\relax d_{\mathfrak{X}})
. We will denote the (left) action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
on the vertex set
V_{\mathfrak{X}}
by
g.x:=g(x)
. A digraph
\mathfrak{X}
is
vertex-transitive
if
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
acts transitively on
V_{\mathfrak{X}}
. In the vertex-transitive case we topologize
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
by declaring for every finite subset
F\subset V_{\mathfrak{X}}
the basic open subset

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(F)=\Big\{g\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})\,\Big|\,g.x=x\quad\forall\,x\in F\Big\}\,.

Then
\mathcal{O}(F)
is a closed subgroup. It is moreover compact, for if
g_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(F)
is an unbounded sequence and
x\in F
then there is a
y\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
such that
(g_{n}.y)_{n}
is unbounded, so that

d_{\mathfrak{X}}(x\mathchar 59\relax y)=d_{\mathfrak{X}}(x\mathchar 59\relax g_{n}.y)\rightarrow+\infty

as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, a contradiction. In particular, the stabilizer subgroup
\mathcal{O}(\{x\})
is a maximal compact open subgroup in
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
for every vertex
x\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
. In total we have that
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
is a totally disconnected locally compact second countable (tdlcsc) group.

2.2.
Countable simple Schreier graphs

Let
G
be a tdlcsc group with identity element
e
and suppose that
K<G
is a compact open subgroup, so that
G/K
is at most countable. If
S\subset K\backslash G/K
is finite, then we define the
Schreier digraph

\mathfrak{X}=\mathrm{Sch}(G\mathchar 59\relax K\mathchar 59\relax S)
to be the graph with vertices
V_{\mathfrak{X}}=G/K
and edges

\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{X}}=\Big\{(gK\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}hK)\in G/K\times G/K\,\Big|\,Kg^{-1}hK\in S\Big\}\,.

Note that such a graph
\mathfrak{X}
is connected and vertex-transitive under the action of
G
. We will make the assumption that
S=S^{-1}
, so the graph
\mathfrak{X}
is undirected, and also that
KeK\notin S
, meaning that
\mathfrak{X}
is simple. Enumerating the members of
S
by
Ks_{1}K\mathchar 59\relax Ks_{2}K\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax Ks_{N}K
with
N=|S|
, then for each
i\in\{1\mathchar 59\relax 2\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax N\}
there is an
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and distinct elements
t_{i1}\mathchar 59\relax t_{i2}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax t_{in_{i}}\in G_{q}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle Ks_{i}K=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n_{i}}t_{ij}K\,.

Also, the cosets
t_{ij}K
are unique up to left translation by
K
, and
K
acts transitively on
T_{i}=\{t_{ij}K:j=1\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax n_{i}\}
by permutations.
Note that the degree of a vertex
gK\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
is by the decomposition in Equation
2.1

\displaystyle\deg(gK)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\{hK\in G/K\,|\,Kg^{-1}hK=Ks_{i}K\}|=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}\,.

In total, the Schreier graphs of the described form are simple, connected and regular of degree
d=\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}
.

Conversely, if
\mathfrak{X}
is a simple, connected, vertex-transitive
d
-regular graph with at most countably many vertices and we fix a vertex
o\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
, then setting
G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
,
K=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(o)
and

\displaystyle S=\Big\{KsK\in K\backslash G/K\,\Big|\,(o\mathchar 59\relax s.o)\in E_{\mathfrak{X}}\Big\}

realizes
\mathfrak{X}
as the Schreier graph of
(G\mathchar 59\relax K\mathchar 59\relax S)
by the above construction.

Example 2.1

(Regular trees)
.

Let
q\geq 2
and consider the
(q+1)
-regular tree
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
with root vertex
o
. Fix a geodesic
(x_{n}\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}:n\in\mathbb{Z})
, in other words a bi-infinite sequence of distinct adjacent vertices, such that
x_{0}=o
. Set
G_{q}=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})\mathchar 59\relax K_{q}=\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{q}}(o)
and let
S_{q}=\{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}\}
where
s_{o}\in G_{q}
is the unique automorphism satisfying
s_{o}.x_{i}=x_{i+1}
for all

. Then
G_{q}
is tdlcsc,
K_{q}
is open and compact in
G_{q}
and

(2.2)

\displaystyle K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{q+1}t_{i}K_{q}

where

are elements such that

. We conclude that
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}=\mathrm{Sch}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q}\mathchar 59\relax S_{q})
. Moreover, note that
K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}
considered as a subset of

is precisely the metric sphere

of vertices of distance

to the root

.

Remark 2.1

(Regular trees as spaces of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
)
.

When

for some prime

, there is a smaller group of isometries of a linear algebraic nature acting transitively on

. The set of equivalence classes of rank 2

-modules in the

up to homothety form a

-regular tree by declaring edges to be pairs

satisfying

. The projective general linear group

acts transitively on this tree and defines a subgroup of

with stabilizer
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
in

. Thus for

with
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

We will in the following sections restrict our attention to the
(q+1)
-regular tree

and return to other graphs in Section

.

2.3.
Sections and Cartan decomposition of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{T}_{d})

Let
(\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\mathchar 59\relax o)
denote the rooted
(q+1)
-regular tree, let
d:\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\times\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be the canonical graph metric and let
G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q}\mathchar 59\relax S_{q}
be as in Example
2.1
. By the decomposition in Equation
2.2
, the stabilizer
K_{q}
of the root

acts transitively on the spheres
\partial B_{r}(o)
for every

, so for every

The map
\varsigma:\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\rightarrow G_{q}
given by
\varsigma(x)=k_{x}s_{o}^{d(x\mathchar 59\relax o)}
is then a continuous section of the orbit map

. With the existence of such a section, we arrive at the

(2.3)

\displaystyle G_{q}=K_{q}A_{q}^{+}K_{q}\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
A_{q}^{+}=\{s_{o}^{n}\in G_{q}:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
. The Cartan subgroup

retrieves the fixed geodesic

used to define

in Example

by
s_{o}^{n}.o=x_{n}
for all

. An important consequence of the Cartan decomposition is the following double coset property.

Lemma 2.2

(Inversion of
K_{q}
-by-
K_{q}
cosets)
.

For every
g\in G_{q}
,
K_{q}g^{-1}K_{q}=K_{q}gK_{q}
.

Proof.

Set
n=d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)
and note that
d(g^{-1}.o\mathchar 59\relax o)=d(o\mathchar 59\relax g.o)=n
. Since
K_{q}
acts transitively on the sphere
\partial B_{n}(o)
, there is a
k_{1}=k_{1}(g)\in K_{q}
such that
g^{-1}.o=k_{1}g.o
, and by definition of
K_{q}
as the stabilizer of

there is a
k_{2}=k_{2}(g)\in K_{q}
such that
g^{-1}=k_{1}gk_{2}
.
∎

Before moving on, we will write
B_{r}
for the metric ball

and also for the
centered Cartan ball
of radius

\displaystyle B_{r}=\big\{g\in G_{q}\,\big|\,d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)\leq r\big\}=\big\{k_{1}s_{o}^{n}k_{2}\in G_{q}\,\big|\,0\leq n\leq r\,\mathchar 59\relax\,\,k_{1}\mathchar 59\relax k_{2}\in K_{q}\big\}\,.

With this convention,

in
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
.

2.4.
Function spaces over regular trees

In the subsequent sections, we will make special use of the vector space of finitely supported complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})=\Big\{f:\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\,\Big|\,f(x)=0\mbox{ for all but finitely many }x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\Big\}

as well as the space of bounded functions

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})=\Big\{f:\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\,\Big|\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}|f(x)|<+\infty\Big\}\,.

A function
f:\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
radial
if
f(k.x)=f(x)
for all
k\in K_{q}
and all
x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
, and we denote by
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})^{\mathrm{rad}}\mathchar 59\relax\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})^{\mathrm{rad}}
the corresponding subspaces of radial functions. The automorphism group
G_{q}
acts on these function spaces from the left by
(g.f)(x)=f(g^{-1}.x)
, and given a function

we can lift it to a right-
K_{q}
-invariant function
\varphi_{f}(g)=f(g.o)
on

.

2.5.
Invariant measures

It is clear that the counting measure

\displaystyle m_{\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}(f)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}f(x)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad f\in\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})

is invariant under
G_{q}
. Denoting the Haar probability measure on the compact group
K_{q}
by
m_{K_{q}}
, we may write

\displaystyle m_{\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}(f)=\int_{K_{q}}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f(ks_{o}^{n}.o)|\partial B_{n}(o)|\Big)dm_{K_{q}}(k)\,\mathchar 59\relax

where

\displaystyle|\partial B_{n}(o)|=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }n=0\\
\frac{q+1}{q}q^{n}&\mbox{ if }n\geq 1\,.\end{cases}

By the Cartan decomposition in Equation
2.3
we obtain a (left) Haar measure on
G_{q}
by defining

\displaystyle m_{G_{q}}(\varphi)=\int_{K_{q}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{K_{q}}\varphi(k_{1}s_{o}^{n}k_{2})dm_{K_{q}}(k_{2})|\partial B_{n}(o)|dm_{K_{q}}(k_{1})

for any locally constant function
\varphi:G_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. In particular, one sees from this formula that
G_{q}
is unimodular. Moreover, the measure of the centered Cartan ball in
G_{q}
is

\displaystyle m_{G_{q}}(B_{r})=\sum_{n=0}^{r}|\partial B_{n}(o)|=|B_{r}(o)|=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }r=0\\
\tfrac{q+1}{q-1}q^{r}-\tfrac{2}{q-1}&\mbox{ if }r\geq 1\,\mathchar 59\relax\end{cases}

which grows as
\tfrac{q+1}{q-1}q^{r}
with
r\rightarrow+\infty
.

3.
Spherical harmonic analysis on regular trees

The main goal of this Section is to introduce and compute the spherical transform of indicator functions on balls. In Subsection
3.1
we introduce spherical functions as complex-valued
*
-homomorphisms on the Hecke algebra and derive explicit formulas. The positive-definite spherical functions are identified in Subsection
3.2
and the spherical transform along with the corresponding Plancherel formula are given in Subsection

3.1.
The Hecke algebra and spherical functions

The
Hecke algebra

\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
of the pair
(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
is the involutive complex algebra of compactly supported bi-
K_{q}
-invariant functions
\varphi:G_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with the operation of convolution

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})(g)=\int_{G_{q}}\varphi_{1}(gh^{-1})\varphi_{2}(h)dm_{G_{q}}(h)

and the involution
\varphi^{*}(g)=\overline{\varphi(g^{-1})}
for all
g\in G_{q}
. One shows by standard arguments that
\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
is preserved under these operations. The identity element is the indicator function of
K_{q}
,
\chi_{K_{q}}\in\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
. The key feature of the Hecke algebra for us is that it enjoys commutativity.

Lemma 3.1
.

The Hecke algebra
\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
is commutative under convolution.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, every
\varphi\in\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
satisfies
\varphi(g^{-1})=\varphi(g)
for all
g\in G_{q}
. Thus if
\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
then

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{q}}\varphi_{1}(gh^{-1})\varphi_{2}(h)dm_{G_{q}}(h)=\int_{G_{q}}\varphi_{1}(gh^{-1})\varphi_{2}(h^{-1})dm_{G_{q}}(h)

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{q}}\varphi_{1}(h^{-1})\varphi_{2}(g^{-1}h^{-1})dm_{G_{q}}(h)=\int_{G_{q}}\varphi_{1}(h)\varphi_{2}(g^{-1}h^{-1})dm_{G_{q}}(h)

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{2}*\varphi_{1})(g^{-1})=(\varphi_{2}*\varphi_{1})(g)\,.

∎

We say that
(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
is a
Gelfand pair
. Note that the bi-
K_{q}
-invariance of a function
\varphi\in\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
ensures that
\varphi
is locally constant and hence continuous on
G_{q}
since
K_{q}
is open. More precisely, by the Cartan decomposition every
\varphi\in\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
can be written as

\displaystyle\varphi=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi(s_{o}^{n})\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}}\,\mathchar 59\relax

which is continuous.

Lemma 3.2
.

The function
\chi_{o}=\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}
generates the algebra
\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
.

Proof.

Since the indicator functions
\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}}
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
span the Hecke algebra
\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
then it suffices to prove that they lie in the unital algebra generated by
\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}
. First, note that

\displaystyle(\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}*\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}})(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}}(gh^{-1})dm_{G_{q}}(h)

\displaystyle=(q+1)\int_{K_{q}}\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}}(gks_{o}^{-1})dm_{K_{q}}(k)\,.

Note that
(g.o\mathchar 59\relax gks_{o}^{-1}.o)\in E_{\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}
for all
k\in K_{q}
, so that

\displaystyle(\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}*\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}})(g)

\displaystyle=(q+1)\int_{K_{q}}\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}}(gks_{o}^{-1})dm_{K_{q}}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(g.o\mathchar 59\relax y)\in E_{\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}}\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}}(s_{o}^{d(y\mathchar 59\relax o)})

\displaystyle=q\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n+1}K_{q}}(s_{o}^{d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)})+\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n-1}K_{q}}(s_{o}^{d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)})

\displaystyle=q\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n+1}K_{q}}(g)+\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n-1}K_{q}}(g)\,.

In particular, we have that

\displaystyle\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{2}K_{q}}=\frac{1}{q}\Big(\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}^{*2}-\chi_{K_{q}}\Big)\,\mathchar 59\relax

and proceeding by induction on
n
we get that

\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}^{n}K_{q}}\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\big\{\chi_{K_{q}}\mathchar 59\relax\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}\mathchar 59\relax\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}^{*2}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax\chi_{K_{q}s_{o}K_{q}}^{*n}\big\}\,.

∎

We define the
K_{q}
-spherical functions on
G_{q}
as the functions arising from non-zero multiplicative linear functionals from the Hecke algebra
\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
to
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 3.3

(Spherical functions)
.

A function
\omega:G_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
K_{q}
-spherical
if it is bi-
K_{q}
-invariant, satisfies
\omega(e)=1
and satisfies

\omega*\varphi=(\omega*\varphi)(e)\omega

for every
\varphi\in\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
.

From Lemma
3.2
we get the following.

Corollary 3.4
.

A bi-
K_{q}
-invariant function
\omega:G_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\omega(e)=1
is
K_{q}
-spherical if
\omega*\chi_{o}=\alpha\omega
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
.

We will now construct for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
a spherical function
\omega
, providing a converse to the Corollary above. For the statements of the subsequent results we will parametrize
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
by taking
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha=\alpha_{\lambda}=2\sqrt{q}\cos_{q}(\lambda)\,.

Clearly, every

can be written in this way, and the mapping

is bijective on

where again
\tau=2\pi/\log(q)
. For the construction of spherical functions, we first note that a convolution

with

locally constant can be written using the Cartan decomposition as

\displaystyle(\varphi*\chi_{o})(g)=(q+1)\int_{K_{q}}\int_{K_{q}}\varphi(gk_{1}s_{o}^{-1}k_{2})dm_{K_{q}}(k_{1})dm_{K_{q}}(k_{2})\,.

In particular, if

and we introduce the function

then
\varphi_{f}
is right-

-invariant and

\displaystyle(\varphi_{f}*\chi_{o})(g)=(q+1)\int_{K_{q}}\varphi_{f}(gk_{1}s_{o}^{-1})dm_{K_{q}}(k_{1})=\sum_{y\in\partial B_{1}(g.o)}f(y)\,.

This leads us to the following definition.

Definition 3.5
.

The
adjacency operator

\Delta_{q}:\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})\rightarrow\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
is

\displaystyle\Delta_{q}f(x)=\sum_{y\in\partial B_{1}(x)}f(y)\,.

By Corollary

-spherical functions on

are in one-to-one correspondence with the

-invariant eigenfunctions of the adjacency operator

is such an eigenfunction, then the explict relation is that
\omega(g)=f(g.o)
is
K_{q}
-spherical. Writing out the equation

in terms of

we get the following system of recursion:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega(e)=1\\
\omega(s_{o})=\tfrac{\alpha_{\lambda}}{q+1}\\
q\omega(s_{o}^{n+1})-\alpha_{\lambda}\omega(s_{o}^{n})+\omega(s_{o}^{n-1})=0\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad n\geq 2\,.\end{cases}

This system can be solved by elementary means. The solutions are the following, and a proof can be found in
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
using a slightly different parameterisation.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\tau=2\pi/\log(q)
and suppose that
\omega_{\lambda}
is a
K_{q}
-spherical function such that
\omega_{\lambda}*\chi_{o}=\alpha_{\lambda}\omega_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}(s_{o}^{n})=\begin{cases}c_{q}(\lambda)q^{-(1/2-i\lambda)n}+c_{q}(-\lambda)q^{-(1/2+i\lambda)n}&\mbox{ if }\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\tfrac{\tau}{2}\mathbb{Z}\\
\Big(1+\frac{q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2}}{q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2}}n\Big)(-1)^{jn}q^{-n/2}&\mbox{ if }\lambda=\tfrac{\tau}{2}j\in\tfrac{\tau}{2}\mathbb{Z}\end{cases}

where

is the Harish-Chandra

-function for

.

Note that
\omega_{-\lambda}=\omega_{\lambda}
and
\omega_{\lambda+j\tau/2}=\omega_{\lambda}
for every

such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle|c_{q}(\lambda)|^{2}=\frac{\sinh_{q}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})+\sin_{q}^{2}(\lambda)}{4\cosh^{2}_{q}(\frac{1}{2})\sin_{q}^{2}(\lambda)}=\frac{1}{4\cosh^{2}_{q}(\frac{1}{2})}\Big(\frac{\sinh^{2}_{q}(\frac{1}{2})}{\sin^{2}_{q}(\lambda)}+1\Big)

when

3.2.

Recall that a continuous function

4.1.
Invariant point processes

Let
\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
denote the space of locally finite positive measures on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
with the topology of pointwise convergence, that is,
p_{n}\rightarrow p
in
\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
if

for all

. The automorphism group
G_{q}
acts on
\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
by push-forward of measures. We write
p(x):=p(\{x\})
and note that every

can be written as

\displaystyle p=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}p(x)\delta_{x}

where
\delta_{x}
denotes the unit point mass at
x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
, which canonically identifies the space
\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
with the space positive functions
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
under pointwise convergence. To every finitely supported function

we associate a
linear statistic

\mathbb{S}f:\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}f(p)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}p(x)f(x)\,.

Since every pair of distinct vertices in
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
are of at least distance
1
from each other, all subsets of
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
are uniformly discrete, so if the function
x\mapsto p(x)
is uniformly bounded then the linear statistic
\mathbb{S}f
applied to

is bounded by

\displaystyle|\mathbb{S}f(p)|\leq\max_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}p(\{x\})\,\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\,.

By a

on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
we mean a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
. Such a point process
\mu
is
simple

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{+}^{\mathrm{S}}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})=\Big\{p\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})\,|\,p(x)\in\{0\mathchar 59\relax 1\}\,\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}\Big\}\,.

A point process
\mu
is

•

invariant
if
g_{*}\mu=\mu
for all

.

•

locally

\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})}p(x)^{k}d\mu(p)<+\infty\,.

In terms of linear statistics, this means that
\mathbb{S}\chi_{B}\in L^{k}(\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})\mathchar 59\relax\mu)
for all finite

is locally

-integrable for every

\mathbb{S}\chi_{B}\in L^{\infty}(\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})\mathchar 59\relax\mu)

is
locally bounded

•

translation bounded
if it is invariant and locally bounded.

Some observations that follow are the following.

•

Every simple point process
\mu
in
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
is locally bounded since
\mathbb{S}\chi_{B}(p)\leq|B|
for

•

If

is an invariant locally integrable point process, then there is a constant

, the

of
\mu
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)=\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})}\mathbb{S}f(p)d\mu(p)=\iota_{\mu}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}f(x)\,.

•

If
\mu
is an invariant locally square-integrable point process, then the variance of a linear statistic
\mathbb{S}f
with respect to
\mu
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(|\mathbb{S}f-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)|^{2})=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(|\mathbb{S}f|^{2})-|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})}\Big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}p(x)f(x)\Big|^{2}d\mu(p)-\iota_{\mu}^{2}\Big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}f(x)\Big|^{2}\,.

•

If
\mu
is an invariant locally square-integrable point process, then the action of

on

yields a

Assumption

4.2.
Diffraction measures

Recall the subset
\Lambda_{q}\subset\mathbb{C}
of spherical parameters corresponding to the positive-definite spherical functions,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{q}=i[0\mathchar 59\relax 1/2]\cup(0\mathchar 59\relax\tau/2)\cup(\tau/2+i[0\mathchar 59\relax 1/2])\,.

In an upcoming paper together with M. Björklund we will prove the existence and uniqueness of the diffraction measure of an invariant locally square-integrable point process on any locally compact second countable commutative space associated with a Gelfand pair. In the special case of regular trees we have the following formulation.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable point process on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. Then there is a unique positive finite Borel measure
\sigma_{\mu}
on
\Lambda_{q}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that for every
f\in\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})}|\mathbb{S}f(p)|^{2}d\mu(p)=\int_{\Lambda_{q}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{f}(\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)\,.

Moreover,
\sigma_{\mu}(\{i/2\})=\iota_{\mu}^{2}
.

We refer to
\sigma_{\mu}
as the
diffraction measure
of
\mu
. We split
\sigma_{\mu}
into three measures
\sigma_{\mu}^{(c)}\mathchar 59\relax\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}\mathchar 59\relax\sigma_{\mu}^{(c_{\mathrm{sgn}})}
by restricting to the respective intervals
i[0\mathchar 59\relax 1/2)\mathchar 59\relax(0\mathchar 59\relax\tau/2)\mathchar 59\relax\tau/2+i[0\mathchar 59\relax 1/2)
. In other words, for every
\varphi\in\mathscr{H}(G_{q}\mathchar 59\relax K_{q})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda_{q}}\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)d\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)=\,\,

\displaystyle\iota_{\mu}^{2}|\widehat{\varphi}(\tfrac{i}{2})|^{2}+\int_{0}^{1/2}|\widehat{\varphi}(i\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\tau/2}|\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1/2}|\widehat{\varphi}(\tfrac{\tau}{2}+i\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(c_{\mathrm{sgn}})}(\lambda)+\sigma_{\mu}(\{\tfrac{\tau+i}{2}\})|\widehat{\varphi}(\tfrac{\tau+i}{2})|^{2}\,.

We refer to
\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}\mathchar 59\relax\sigma_{\mu}^{(c)}\mathchar 59\relax\sigma_{\mu}^{(c_{\mathrm{sgn}})}
as the
principal-, complementary-
and
signed complementary diffraction measure
respectively. We note that the spherical parameters
\lambda=0\mathchar 59\relax\tau/2
correspond to principal series representations, but for our purposes it will be convenient to include them in the support of the complementary diffraction measures.

4.3.
Conditional number variances

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable point process on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. The canonical variance of a linear statistic
\mathbb{S}f
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{T}_{q+1})}|\mathbb{S}f(p)|^{2}d\mu(p)-\iota_{\mu}^{2}\Big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}f(x)\Big|^{2}\,.

Using the fact that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{T}_{q+1}}f(x)=\widehat{\varphi}_{f}(\tfrac{i}{2})

then Theorem
4.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)=\int_{\Lambda_{q}\backslash\{\frac{i}{2}\}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{f}(\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)\,.

In other words, the variance of a linear statistic is obtained as the second moment of the projected statistic in the orthogonal complement of the subspace of constant functions. This is however not the only way to obtain a notion of ”variance”, and in fact, for regular trees this is not always the preferred choice of variance.

Definition 4.2

(Spectrally conditioned variance)
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable point process and

be a Borel measurable subset. The
Q
-conditional variance
of a linear statistic
\mathbb{S}f

, is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f\,|\,Q)=\int_{\Lambda_{q}\backslash Q}|\widehat{\varphi}_{f}(\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}(\lambda)\,.

As mentioned in the Introduction, we will pay special attention to the cases
Q=\{\frac{i}{2}\}
and
Q=\{\frac{i}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\frac{\tau+i}{2}\}
, and we define number variances for these two cases.

Definition 4.3

(Spectrally conditioned number variance)
.

Let
\mu
and
Q
be as in the previous definition. The
Q
-conditional number variance
of a finite subset

is

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(B\,|\,Q)=\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}\chi_{B}\,|\,Q)\,.

Moreover, we define

(1)

the
number variance

\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(B)=\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(B\,|\,\{\frac{i}{2}\})
,

(2)

the

\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(B)=\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(B\,|\,\{\frac{i}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\frac{\tau+i}{2}\})

The sub-oscillatory number variance of the metric ball

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(B_{r})=\int_{0}^{1/2}|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(i\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\tau/2}|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{1/2}|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\tfrac{\tau}{2}+i\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(c_{\mathrm{sgn}})}(\lambda).

5.
Non-geometric hyperuniformity

We will in this Section prove Theorem

Proposition 5.1

Let

Proof.

For (1) we have the lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)}{q^{r}}\geq\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(i\lambda)|^{2}}{q^{r}}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\tfrac{\tau}{2}+i\lambda)|^{2}}{q^{r}}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(c_{\mathrm{sgn}})}(\lambda)\,.

3.10

\displaystyle\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(i\lambda)|^{2}}{q^{r}}=\frac{(\sinh_{q}((r+1)\lambda)+q^{-1/2}\sinh_{q}(r\lambda))^{2}}{\sinh_{q}^{2}(\lambda)}\geq(1+q^{-1/2})^{2}\frac{\sinh_{q}^{2}(r\lambda)}{\sinh_{q}^{2}(\lambda)}

For (2) we have the lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=0}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)}{q^{r}}\geq\int_{\varepsilon}^{\tau/2-\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=0}^{R}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q^{r}}\Big)d\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)\,.

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\tau/2-\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=0}^{R}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\lambda)|^{2}}{q^{r}}\Big)d\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\tau/2-\varepsilon}\frac{1+2q^{-1/2}\cos_{q}(\lambda)+q^{-1}}{2\sin_{q}^{2}(\lambda)}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)\,.

Lastly, using the formula for

\displaystyle\frac{1+2q^{-1/2}\cos_{q}(\lambda)+q^{-1}}{2\sin_{q}^{2}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=0}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(r)}{q^{r}}\geq\frac{2(q+1)^{2}}{q(q^{1/2}+1)^{2}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\tau/2-\varepsilon}|c_{q}(\lambda)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)\,.

6.
Atoms in the diffraction picture

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable point process on
\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
. If
\lambda\in\Lambda_{q}
is an atom for the diffraction measure
\sigma_{\mu}
, meaning
\sigma_{\mu}(\{\lambda\})>0
, then the lower limit of the number variance by the volume of the ball can be bounded from below by

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(B_{r})}{|B_{r}|}\geq\sigma_{\mu}(\{\lambda\})\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\lambda)|^{2}}{|B_{r}|}\,.

We know from Theorem
1.3
that if
\lambda\in i[0\mathchar 59\relax 1/2)\cup(\tau/2+i[0\mathchar 59\relax 1/2))
then this lower limit is infinite, so we restrict our attention to principal series spherical parameters
\lambda\in(0\mathchar 59\relax\tau/2)
. For this we now have by Lemma
3.10
the explicit lower bound

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(B_{r})}{|B_{r}|}\geq\frac{(q-1)\sigma_{\mu}(\{\lambda\})}{(q+1)\sin_{q}^{2}(\lambda)}\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\Big(\sin_{q}((r+1)\lambda)+q^{-1/2}\sin_{q}(r\lambda)\Big)^{2}\,.

We will now show that this lower limit is positive whenever the diffraction measure admits a ”rational” atom in the spectrum. The following Proposition is proved in the Appendix, and we to state it we recall the notation
\tau=2\pi/\log(q)
and
\sin_{q}(\lambda)=\sin(\log(q)\lambda)
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
a\mathchar 59\relax b\in\mathbb{Z}
be coprime such that
0<a<b
. Then the equation

\displaystyle\sin_{q}(\tfrac{(r+1)a\tau}{2b})+q^{-1/2}\sin_{q}(\tfrac{ra\tau}{2b})=0

has a solution
0\leq r\leq b-1
if and only if

•

q=2
and
\tfrac{a}{b}\in\{\tfrac{3}{4}\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{5}{12}\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{11}{12}\}
, or

•

q=3
and
\tfrac{a}{b}=\tfrac{5}{6}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Suppose that the diffraction measure
\sigma_{\mu}
has an atom of the form
\lambda=\tfrac{a\tau}{2b}
for some coprime
a\mathchar 59\relax b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
0<a<b
. Denote by
F_{a\mathchar 59\relax b}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
the function

\displaystyle F_{a\mathchar 59\relax b}(r)=\Big(\sin_{q}((r+1)\tfrac{a\tau}{2b})+q^{-1/2}\sin_{q}(r\tfrac{a\tau}{2b})\Big)^{2}\,\mathchar 59\relax

so that

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}^{*}(B_{r})}{|B_{r}|}\geq\frac{(q-1)\sigma_{\mu}(\{\tfrac{a\tau}{2b}\})}{(q+1)\sin_{q}^{2}(\tfrac{a\tau}{2b})}\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}F_{a\mathchar 59\relax b}(r)\,.

Note that
F_{a\mathchar 59\relax b}
is not only a
2b
-periodic function, but also
b
-periodic from the square in its definition. By
b
-periodicity of
F_{a\mathchar 59\relax b}
, the lower limit on the right hand side vanishes if and only if there is an

such that
F_{a\mathchar 59\relax b}(r^{*})=0
. By Proposition
6.1
, such an
r^{*}
exists if and only if

(q\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{a}{b})=(2\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{3}{4})\mathchar 59\relax(2\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{5}{12})\mathchar 59\relax(2\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{11}{12})\mbox{ or }(3\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{5}{6})\,.

Thus, if
(q\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{a}{b})
fall outside of the mentioned cases then we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}F_{a\mathchar 59\relax b}(r)=\min_{0\leq r\leq b}\Big(\sin_{q}((r+1)\tfrac{a\tau}{2b})+q^{-1/2}\sin_{q}(r\tfrac{a\tau}{2b})\Big)^{2}>0

and so we’re done.
∎

Definition 7.1

Lemma 7.2

8.1.

Let

(q+1)
-regular graph and fix a vertex

Consider the set of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}
of paths of finite length in
\mathfrak{X}

\xi=(x_{0}\mathchar 59\relax x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax x_{n})

x_{i}\in V_{\mathfrak{X}}
and
(x_{i}\mathchar 59\relax x_{i+1})\in E_{\mathfrak{X}}
for all
i

\xi\mathchar 59\relax\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}
share the same final and initial vertex respectively, then we can consider the concatenated path
\xi.\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}
, as well as reversed paths
\xi^{-1}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}
. If
\xi\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}
has
backtracking
, meaning that there is an
i
such that
x_{i}=x_{i+2}
, then a
prune
of
\xi
is a path

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}=(x_{0}\mathchar 59\relax x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax x_{i-1}\mathchar 59\relax x_{i}\mathchar 59\relax x_{i+3}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax x_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}\,.

Lemma 8.1
.

Each equivalence class
[\xi]_{p.e.}
of
\sim_{p.e.}
has a unique non-prunable representative
\xi^{\min}
.

Note that if
\xi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}

\displaystyle\xi_{1}.\xi_{2}\sim_{p.e.}\xi_{1}^{\min}.\xi_{2}\sim_{p.e.}\xi_{1}.\xi_{2}^{\min}\sim_{p.e.}\xi_{1}^{\min}.\xi_{2}^{\min}\sim_{p.e.}(\xi_{1}.\xi_{2})^{\min}\,.

The concatenation of two non-prunable paths are not necessarily non-prunable, so the last equivalence in the chain above might not be an equality of paths.

The

of the pointed graph

is the graph whose vertex set are the prune-equivalence classes of paths in

that start with

,

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}}=\Big\{[\xi]_{p.e.}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}/\sim_{p.e.}\,\Big|\,\xi=(u\mathchar 59\relax x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax x_{n})\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathfrak{X}}\Big\}

Moreover, we fix the root vertex
\xi_{u}=[(u)]_{p.e.}\in V_{\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}}
. Then
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}
is simple, connected and
(q+1)
-regular since
\mathfrak{X}
is and
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}
does not contain any cycles, so we conclude that there is a graph isomorphism
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{q+1}
sending

to

8.2.
The fundamental group

Consider a simple connected
(q+1)
-regular pointed graph
(\mathfrak{X}\mathchar 59\relax u)
with universal covering tree

as in the previous subsection. The

of

is the set of prune-equivalence classes of cycles in
\mathfrak{X}
based at

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{X})=\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{X}\mathchar 59\relax u)=\Big\{[\alpha]_{p.e.}\in V_{\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}}\,\Big|\,\alpha\mbox{ ends with }u\Big\}

with identity

\displaystyle\gamma\gamma^{\prime}=[\alpha.\alpha^{\prime}]_{p.e.}=[(u\mathchar 59\relax x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax x_{m}\mathchar 59\relax u\mathchar 59\relax y_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax y_{n}\mathchar 59\relax u)]_{p.e.}

Lemma 8.2
.

The action of
\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{X})
on

is free. In particular,
\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{X})<\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}})
is a discrete subgroup.

Proof.

To prove freeness of the action, suppose that
\gamma=[\alpha]_{p.e.}\in\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{X})
such that
\gamma.[\xi]_{p.e.}=[\xi]_{p.e.}
for some
[\xi]_{p.e.}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}
. By associativity of concatenation we then get

\displaystyle\alpha\sim_{p.e.}\alpha.\xi.\xi^{-1}\sim_{p.e.}\xi.\xi^{-1}\sim_{p.e.}(u)\,\mathchar 59\relax

so that
\gamma=[\alpha]_{p.e.}=\xi_{u}
is the identity.

To prove that
\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{X})<\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}})
is a discrete subgroup, suppose without loss of generality that there is a sequence
\gamma_{n}\rightarrow\xi_{u}
in
\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{X})
. Since the stabilizer of
\xi_{u}\in\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}
is an open subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}})

\gamma_{n}.\xi_{u}=\xi_{u}
for sufficiently large

\pi_{1}(\mathfrak{X})
on
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{X}}
is free, so we must have that
\gamma_{n}=\xi_{u}
for all sufficiently large

Via a pointed graph isomorphism

Appendix A
Rational linear dependence of trigonometric numbers

In proving Proposition
6.1
we make use of two results regarding rational linear dependence regarding trigonometric numbers of the form
\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})
for coprime integers
a\mathchar 59\relax b\in\mathbb{Z}
. We first establish the algebraic degree of such numbers over
\mathbb{Q}
. To state it we will denote by
\phi
the Euler totient function.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
a\mathchar 59\relax b\in\mathbb{Z}
be coprime integers. Then the algebraic degree of
\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})
over
\mathbb{Q}
is
1
if
b=1
and
\phi(2b)/2
if
b\geq 2
.

Proof.

Since
\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})=(\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{a\pi}{b}}+\mathrm{e}^{-i\frac{a\pi}{b}})/2
and
z=\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{a\pi}{b}}
solves the equation

\displaystyle z^{2}-2\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})z+1=0

then
[\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{a\pi}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}(\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b}))]\leq 2
. Note that this degree is
1
if and only if
\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{a\pi}{b}}
is real, meaning
b=1
. If on the other hand the degree is
2
then

[\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{a\pi}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}]=[\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{\pi}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}]=\phi(2b)

since
a\mathchar 59\relax b
are coprime. Thus we get that the algebraic degree of
\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})
is

\displaystyle[\mathbb{Q}(\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})):\mathbb{Q}]=\frac{[\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{a\pi}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}]}{[\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{a\pi}{b}}):\mathbb{Q}(\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b}))]}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }b=1\\
\phi(2b)/2&\mbox{ if }b\geq 2\,.\end{cases}

∎

An immediate consequence is that we can for example find all rational and quadratic irrational trigonometric numbers
\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})
. The following result can be found in Niven’s book
[
14
, Corollary 3.12]
, which should also be credited to the prior work of Lehmer and Olmstead.

Corollary A.2

(Niven’s Theorem)
.

Let
a\mathchar 59\relax b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
\gcd(a\mathchar 59\relax b)=1
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})\in\mathbb{Q}
,

(2)

b\leq 3
.

In other words, the only rational values of
\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})
are
\pm 1
and
\pm\frac{1}{2}
.

Corollary A.3
.

Let
a\mathchar 59\relax b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
\gcd(a\mathchar 59\relax b)=1
. Then
\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})
is quadratic irrational if and only if
b\in\{4\mathchar 59\relax 5\mathchar 59\relax 6\}
. In other words, the only quadratic irrational values of
\cos(\tfrac{a\pi}{b})
are
\pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathchar 59\relax\pm\tfrac{1}{4}\pm\tfrac{\sqrt{5}}{4}
and
\pm\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
.

Universality in the Low Mach number limit via a convex integration framework
Robin Ming Chen, Alexis Vasseur, Dehua Wang, Cheng Yu

Abstract
. We study the low Mach number limit of the compressible Euler equations through the lens of convex integration. For any prescribed L 2 L^{2} weak solution of the incompressible Euler equations, we construct a corresponding family of weak solutions to the compressible Euler equations via a refined convex integration scheme. We then prove that, as the Mach number tends to zero, this family of solutions converges strongly to the given incompressible solution. This result demonstrates that the incompressible system acts as a universal attractor in this setting: every incompressible flow can be realized as the limit of convex integration solutions to the compressible system. Our approach highlights a new form of universality for singular limits and provides a rigorous framework for understanding the incompressible limit from the perspective of weak solution theory.

1.
Introduction

The incompressible Euler equations are universally regarded as the formal low Mach number limit of the compressible Euler equations. This physical premise is rooted in the observation that when fluid velocities are much smaller than the speed of sound, density variations become negligible, and the flow behaves as if it were incompressible
[
16
,
20
,
21
]
. The mathematical theory of this singular limit aims to rigorously justify the transition from solutions of the compressible Euler system

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}(\rho{\bf v})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho{\bf v})+{\rm div}(\rho{\bf v}\otimes{\bf v})+\nabla P=0,\end{split}\right.

where
\rho
is the density,
{\bf v}
the velocity, and
P(\rho)=a\rho^{\gamma}
the pressure (
a>0
,
\gamma>1
), to those of the incompressible Euler system

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}{\bf u}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla{\bf u}+\nabla\pi=0,\\
&{\rm div}{\bf u}=0.\end{split}\right.

To capture this low Mach number regime, one introduces a scaling where time is slowed down and velocity is rescaled by a small parameter

{\rho}(x,t)=:\rho_{\delta}(x,\delta t),\quad{{\bf v}}(x,t)=:\delta{\bf v}_{\delta}(x,\delta t).

This leads from (
1.1
) to the rescaled compressible Euler equations

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho_{\delta}+{\rm div}(\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta})+{\rm div}(\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}\otimes{\bf v}_{\delta})+\nabla\frac{\rho_{\delta}^{\gamma}}{\delta^{2}}=0.\end{split}\right.

Formally, as
\delta\to 0
, we expect
\rho_{\delta}
to converge to a constant (say,
1
), and the velocity field to become divergence-free, recovering (
1.2
).

The study of this limit was initiated by the seminal works of Ebin
[
10
]
and Klainerman–Majda
[
15
,
16
]
, who treated the case of smooth, well-prepared initial data. Subsequent research expanded this theory to various contexts, including ill-prepared data
[
28
]
, bounded domains
[
22
]
, non-isentropic flows
[
23
,
24
]
, and the framework of weak solutions for the Navier–Stokes equations
[
11
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
. This extensive body of work (see also
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
13
,
14
,
26
]
) typically relies on strong compactness arguments or relative entropy methods. These techniques require sufficient a priori estimates or structural assumptions on the solutions, effectively filtering out highly oscillatory or turbulent behavior.
To obtain global-in-time results, Feireisl–Klingenberg–Markfelder
[
12
]
introduced the framework of dissipative measure-valued (DMV) solutions, establishing convergence to the smooth incompressible solution for finite-energy data. Their result demonstrates that under the DMV framework, the incompressible limit acts as a selective filter for families of approximate solutions.

In contrast, our work asks if the incompressible system is a
universal attractor
: can every incompressible weak solution be targeted and approximated by a sequence of weak solutions to the compressible system?

A New Perspective from Convex Integration

This question is motivated by the modern theory of weak solutions offered by
convex integration
. In a marked departure from prior approaches, this framework has revealed that for a dense set of initial data, the incompressible and compressible isentropic Euler system admits infinitely many weak solutions, even when imposing the energy inequality as an admissibility criterion
[
5
,
27
]
. The main achievement in our prior work
[
5
]
is the development of a convex integration scheme capable of handling a general positive definite Reynolds stress tensor, a generalization beyond earlier methods.

We now re-examine the foundational low Mach number limit through this lens. The discovery of ubiquitous non-uniqueness prompts us to shift the analytical paradigm: instead of investigating whether a given family of compressible solutions converges, we constructively address whether any arbitrary incompressible weak solution
{\bf u}
can be realized as the limit (as
\delta\to 0
) of a sequence of compressible weak solutions built via convex integration. An affirmative answer would demonstrate a form of universality, thereby establishing that the incompressible system is indeed a universal attractor within this framework, independent of the small-scale turbulent microstructure permitted by the convex integration machinery.

Strategy and the Key Innovation

Our strategy builds directly on the framework of
[
5
]
and proceeds in three conceptual stages. First, from a given incompressible solution
{\bf u}
, we construct a smooth incompressible subsolution
({\bf u}^{\varepsilon},\pi^{\varepsilon},R^{\varepsilon})
to a relaxed system, with a positive definite Reynolds stress
R^{\varepsilon}>0
. Second, we design a corresponding compressible subsolution
(\rho_{\delta},V_{\delta},R_{\delta})
to (
1.3
) that approximates the incompressible one, ensuring
\rho_{\delta}\sim 1
and preserving the crucial property
R_{\delta}>0
. Third, we apply the convex integration scheme of
[
5
]
to this subsolution to generate infinitely many true weak solutions
(\rho_{\delta},\widehat{V}_{\delta})
of (
1.3
).

The central new challenge, absent in
[
5
]
, is the requirement of uniform approximation: the constructed compressible solutions must converge strongly to
{\bf u}
as
\delta\to 0
. This demand for controlled asymptotics forces a critical refinement of the convex integration technique. Our principal innovation is the introduction of a new
L^{2}
-type constraint into the non-constructive
L^{\infty}
convex integration scheme. We define the set of admissible perturbations
X_{0}
to include only those
\widetilde{V}
satisfying

(1.4)

\left|\int\frac{V_{\delta}\cdot\widetilde{V}}{\rho_{\delta}}\,dxdt\right|\leq\frac{1}{8}\int\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{\delta}}\,dxdt,

where
(\rho_{\delta},V_{\delta},R_{\delta})
is the underlying subsolution. This constraint is the key that unlocks a uniform energy estimate. It enables us to prove that the kinetic energy of the perturbations is controlled by the trace of the Reynolds stress:

(1.5)

\int\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{\delta}}\,dxdt\leq C\int\mathrm{tr}R_{\delta}\,dxdt.

Since our subsolution construction ensures that
\mathrm{tr}R_{\delta}
remains
O(\delta)
, this estimate
tames the turbulent oscillations uniformly with respect to the Mach number, ensuring their vanishing in the limit and thereby securing the desired strong convergence.

Such a control of the
turbulent
perturbations by the Reynolds tensor comes naturally when considering the
C^{\alpha}
theory of convex integration (the constructivist theory). It was already used, in this context, to obtain the inviscid limit of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equation for instance (see
[
4
]
). However, to our knowledge, it was not yet derived for the
L^{\infty}
theory of convex integration (using the Bairé category theorem).

Main Result

Employing this convex integration strategy, we prove that the incompressible system is indeed a universal attractor within this framework. For the precise notions of weak solutions to the incompressible Euler system (
1.2
) and the rescaled compressible Euler system (
1.3
), see the definitions below.

Definition 1.1
.

We say
{\bf u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
is a weak solution of (
1.2
) with intial data
{\bf u}^{0}\in L^{2}
if it is divergence-free in the sense of distribution and

•

for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times[0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
{\rm div}\,\phi=0
,

(1.6)

\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi+{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla\phi\,dxdt=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}{\bf u}^{0}\cdot\phi(\cdot,0)\,dx.

Definition 1.2
.

For
\gamma>1
, we say
(\rho_{\delta},\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta})
is a weak solution of (
1.3
) with initial value
(\rho^{0}_{\delta},{\bf m}_{\delta}^{0})\in L^{\gamma}\times L^{2}
if the following holds true.

•

\rho_{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),\;\;\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}{\bf v}_{\delta}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
;

•

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T))
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left(\rho_{\delta}\,\partial_{t}\varphi+\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right)\,dx\,dt+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho_{\delta}^{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,dx=0;

•

for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times[0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,

\begin{split}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi&+\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}\otimes{\bf v}_{\delta}:\nabla\phi\,dxdt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{\rho_{\delta}^{\gamma}}{\delta^{2}}{\rm div}\phi\,dx\,dt\\
&=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}{\bf m}_{\delta}^{0}\cdot\phi(\cdot,0)\,dx.\end{split}

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n=2
or
3
and
\gamma>1
. For any given
T>0
, consider any weak solution
{\bf u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
to the incompressible Euler equation (
1.2
) with initial data
{\bf u}^{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, as defined in Definition
1.1
. Then for any
T^{\prime}<T
,
there exists a small number
\delta_{0}>0
such that for any Mach number
\delta<\delta_{0}
, there exists a family of initial data

(\rho_{\delta}^{0},{\bf m}_{\delta}^{0})\in C^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

with the following properties.

•

For any fixed
\delta>0
, there exist infinitely many weak solutions
(\rho_{\delta},\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta})
on
(0,T^{\prime})
to the rescaled compressible Euler equations (
1.3
) emanating from the above data
(\rho_{\delta}^{0},{\bf m}_{\delta}^{0})
, in the sense of Definition
1.2
.

•

The following asymptotic limits hold:

\rho_{\delta}\to 1\;\;\;\text{ in }C^{0}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}),\;\;\;\;\;\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}\to{\bf u}\;\text{ in }L^{2}(0,T^{\prime};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),

and the initial data

{\bf m}_{\delta}^{0}\to{\bf u}^{0}\;\;\;\text{ weakly in }\;\;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),

as
\delta
tends to zero.

Remark 1.1

(On the adiabatic exponent
\gamma
)
.

The convex integration construction in Theorem
1.1
is valid for all
\gamma>1
. This is in contrast to the result in
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, where the constraint
1<\gamma\leq 1+\tfrac{2}{n}
arises from the requirement that the constructed solutions satisfy the energy inequality. Since the present work does not impose the energy condition, our result holds in the full range
\gamma>1
.

This theorem establishes a rigorous density result: the set of incompressible solutions attainable as low-Mach limits of convex integration solutions is the
entire

L^{2}
-space. It affirms that any large-scale, divergence-free flow field can emerge as the effective, averaged description of a compressible fluid in the low Mach number regime, irrespective of the fine-scale, non-unique oscillatory dynamics modeled by convex integration. This provides a mathematical justification for the robustness of the incompressible approximation, confirming the physical intuition formalized in
[
16
,
20
,
21
]
.

2.
Construction of subsolutions to the compressible Euler equations

In this section, we construct a subsolution to the scaled compressible Euler equations (
1.3
), starting from an arbitrary weak solution of the incompressible Euler equations (
1.2
).
This subsolution serves as the foundation for applying the convex integration framework, which in turn yields infinitely many weak solutions to (
1.3
). For clarity, we divide the construction into two lemmas.

The first lemma is stated below:

Lemma 2.1
.

For any weak solutions
{\bf u}
to the incompressible Euler equations (
1.2
) and any
{\varepsilon}>0
,
there exist
{\bf u}^{\varepsilon},\pi^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
R^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n\times n})
, where
\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}
is the set of symmetric matrices in dimension
n
, such that

•

({\bf u}^{\varepsilon},\pi^{\varepsilon},R^{\varepsilon})
is a weak solution to

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+\nabla\pi^{\varepsilon}+{\rm div}R^{\varepsilon}=0,\\
&{\rm div}{{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}}=0.\end{split}\right.

•

R^{\varepsilon}>\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\mathrm{Id}_{n}
is a symmetric matrix, where
\mathrm{Id}_{n}
is the
n\times n
identity matrix.

•

There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
{\varepsilon}
such that

(2.2)

\|{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|R^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
{\bf u}
be a weak solution to the incompressible Euler equations (
1.2
). Let
\eta:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a standard mollifier supported on
[-1,0]\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. For any
\alpha>0
we define

\eta_{\alpha}(t,x):=\frac{1}{\alpha^{4}}\eta\left(\frac{t}{\alpha},\frac{x}{\alpha}\right),

and let
{\bf u}_{\alpha}:[0,T-\alpha]\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
be defined by
{\bf u}_{\alpha}=\eta_{\alpha}\ast{\bf u}
. Obviously

{\bf u}_{\alpha}\to{\bf u}\qquad\text{in }\ L^{2}([0,T-\alpha]\times\mathbb{T}^{3})\ \text{ as }\ \alpha\to 0.

Therefore, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\alpha_{\varepsilon}>0
small enough such that
the mollified velocity field defined as

{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}:={\bf u}*\eta_{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}(x)

satisfies (
2.1
) on
[0,T-{\varepsilon}]
, with

\pi^{\varepsilon}:=\pi\ast\eta_{\alpha_{\varepsilon}},\qquad R^{\varepsilon}:=({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})^{\varepsilon}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathrm{Id}_{n},

where
({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})^{\varepsilon}:=({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})\ast\eta_{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}
.

Clearly by the convexity of the nonlinearity we know that the Reynolds-type stress tensor
R^{\varepsilon}
is a symmetric and positive definite matrix with

R^{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\mathrm{Id}_{n}>\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\mathrm{Id}_{n}.

Moreover we have

\|{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C\varepsilon,\qquad\|({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})^{\varepsilon}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C\varepsilon,

and

\|R^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C\varepsilon,

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Once this lemma is established, we are ready to construct a subsolution to the compressible Euler equations (
1.3
). For
\varepsilon,\delta,K>0
, define

(2.3a)

\varrho_{\delta,K}(t,x):=\frac{(\delta^{2}\pi^{\varepsilon}+K)^{1/\gamma}-1}{\delta^{2}},

where
\pi^{\varepsilon}
is from Lemma
2.1
. Since
\pi^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
, we know that for any
\delta>0
there exists a sufficiently large
K>0
such that
\delta^{2}\pi^{\varepsilon}+K>0
, and hence
\varrho_{\delta,K}\in C^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n})
. Considering
\delta
sufficiently small such that
\|\delta^{2}\pi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\ll 1
, among those choices of
K
, a simple intermediate value theorem further implies that for any
t\in[0,T-\varepsilon]
there exists some
K_{\ast}=K_{\ast}(\delta,t)>0
such that

(2.3b)

\left\langle\varrho_{\delta,K_{\ast}}\right\rangle:=\fint_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}\varrho_{\delta,K_{\ast}}\,dx=0.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, consider
({\bf u}^{\varepsilon},\pi^{\varepsilon},R^{\varepsilon})
as in Lemma
2.1
. There exists a
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}({\varepsilon})>0
such that for any
\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, there exist
{\bf v}_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
\tilde{R}_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathbb{S}^{n\times n})
with
\tilde{R}_{\delta}>0
, solving

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho_{\delta}+{\rm div}(\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta})+{\rm div}(\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}\otimes{\bf v}_{\delta})+\nabla\frac{\rho_{\delta}^{\gamma}}{\delta^{2}}+{\rm div}(R^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{R}_{\delta})=0,\end{split}\right.

where

(2.5)

\rho_{\delta}(t,x):=1+\delta^{2}\varrho_{\delta,K_{\ast}}(t,x)

with
\varrho_{\delta,K_{\ast}}
being given in (
2.3
). Moreover we have

(2.6)

\begin{split}\|\rho_{\delta}-1\|_{C^{0}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\tilde{R_{\delta}}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\varepsilon.\end{split}

Proof.

From (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) we have

\rho_{\delta}=1+\delta^{2}\varrho_{\delta,K_{\ast}}=(\delta^{2}\pi^{\varepsilon}+K_{\ast})^{1/\gamma}.

So for
\delta
sufficiently small we know that
\rho_{\delta}>0
, and

(2.7)

\rho_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n}),\quad\left\langle\rho_{\delta}\right\rangle=1,\quad\text{and}\quad\|\rho_{\delta}-K_{\ast}^{1/\gamma}\|_{C^{0}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta^{2}.

The time derivative of
\rho_{\delta}
is given by

\partial_{t}\rho_{\delta}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(\delta^{2}\pi^{\varepsilon}+K_{\ast}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}(\delta^{2}\partial_{t}\pi^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{t}K_{\ast}).

It is easily seen that
K_{\ast}
is smooth in
(\delta,t)\in[0,\infty)\times[0,T-\varepsilon]
, and
K_{\ast}(0,t)=1
. Therefore we have for
\delta
small enough

\|K_{\ast}(\delta,t)-1\|_{C^{0}([0,T-\varepsilon])}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta,\qquad\|\partial_{t}K_{\ast}(\delta,t)\|_{C^{0}([0,T-\varepsilon])}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta.

This leads to the following bound:

(2.8)

\|\rho_{\delta}-1\|_{C^{0}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\partial_{t}\rho_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\rho_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta.

With these bounds in place, we may now proceed with the following calculations. Define
{\bf m}_{\delta}
on
[0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
through

\partial_{t}\rho_{\delta}+{\rm div}{\bf m}_{\delta}=0.

From (
2.7
) we see that
\left\langle\partial_{t}\rho_{\delta}\right\rangle=0
, and hence by the well-known result of Bourgain–Brezis
[
1
]
it follows that the above equation admits a solution
{\bf m}_{\delta}=\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(\partial_{t}\rho_{\delta})
, with the estimate

(2.9)

\|{\bf m}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C\|\partial_{t}\rho_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T-\varepsilon;L^{n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta.

Here
(-\Delta)^{-1}
is taken to to be an operator that sends mean-zero
L^{2}
functions to mean-zero
H^{2}
functions.
From this, and the fact that
\rho_{\delta}>0
, we define

{\bf v}_{\delta}:=\frac{{\bf u}^{\epsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta}}{\rho_{\delta}},\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}={\bf u}^{\epsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta}.

From (
2.9
) it follows that

(2.10)

\|\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\|{\bf m}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C({\varepsilon})\delta.

Finally we find
\tilde{R}_{\delta}
. For this, we calculate the following equation

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})+{\rm div}\left(\frac{({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})\otimes({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})}{\rho_{\delta}}\right)+\nabla\frac{\rho_{\delta}^{\gamma}}{\delta^{2}}+{\rm div}R^{\varepsilon}\\
&=\partial_{t}{\bf m}_{\delta}+{\rm div}\left(\frac{({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})\otimes({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})}{\rho_{\delta}}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\right)\\
&={\rm div}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\partial^{2}_{t}\rho_{\delta}\mathrm{Id}_{n}\right)+{\rm div}\left(\frac{({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})\otimes({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})}{\rho_{\delta}}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\right).\end{split}

Since
\partial_{t}^{2}\rho_{\delta}(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})
with
\left\langle\partial_{t}^{2}\rho_{\delta}\right\rangle=0
,
(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial^{2}_{t}\rho_{\delta}
is well-defined.
Thus, we denote

\tilde{R}_{\delta}:=-(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial^{2}_{t}\rho_{\delta}\mathrm{Id}_{n}-\frac{({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})\otimes({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})}{\rho_{\delta}}+{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\mathrm{Id}_{n}.

Then we obtain the equation

\partial_{t}({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})+{\rm div}\left(\frac{({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})\otimes({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})}{\rho_{\delta}}\right)+\nabla\frac{\rho_{\delta}^{\gamma}}{\delta^{2}}+{\rm div}(R^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{R}_{\delta})=0.

From the above estimates, we obtain

\left\|\frac{({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})\otimes({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}+{\bf m}_{\delta})}{\rho_{\delta}}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}))}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta.

Since
\left\langle\partial_{t}^{2}\rho_{\delta}\right\rangle=0
, the
L^{2}
-estimate on torus for the first term in
\tilde{R}_{\delta}
gives

\left\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{t}^{2}\rho_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T-\varepsilon;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))}\leq C\left\|\partial_{t}^{2}\rho_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T-\varepsilon;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta,

which, by Sobolev embedding (since here
n=2
or
3
), yields

\left\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{t}^{2}\rho_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta.

Therefore

(2.11)

\|\tilde{R}_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T-\varepsilon]\times\mathbb{T}^{n}))}\leq C(\varepsilon)\delta.

Choosing
\delta
sufficiently small (depending on
{\varepsilon}
) it follows that
\tilde{R}_{\delta}>0
, and (
2.6
) follows from (
2.8
), (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), and hence we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

We call the solution
(\rho_{\delta},V_{\delta}:=\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta},R_{\delta}:=R^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{R}_{\delta})
to (
2.4
) a subsolution to the isentropic compressible Euler system (
1.3
).

3.
Convex integration

We begin with a (smooth) subsolution
(\rho_{0},V_{0},R_{0})
to the isentropic compressible Euler system, as constructed in Lemma
2.2
, which solves the following system:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}V=0,\\
&\partial_{t}V+{\rm div}U+\nabla\left(p_{\delta}(\rho)+\frac{|V|^{2}}{n\rho}\right)+{\rm div}R=0,\end{split}\right.

where
U:=\frac{V\otimes V}{\rho}-\frac{|V|^{2}}{n\rho}\mathrm{Id}_{n}
, and
R>0
is a symmetric matrix-valued function representing the Reynolds stress. Here the pressure
p_{\delta}(\rho):=\rho^{\gamma}/\delta^{2}
. Our objective is to construct infinitely many bounded perturbations
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})
, supported in a prescribed domain
P\subset(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
, that satisfy the following linear system:

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{split}&{\rm div}\widetilde{V}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\widetilde{V}+{\rm div}\widetilde{U}=0,\end{split}\right.

together with the nonlinear algebraic constraint:

(3.3)

\frac{(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})\otimes(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})}{\rho_{0}}-(U_{0}+\widetilde{U})=\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{n\rho_{0}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}+R_{0}\quad\text{a.e. in }P,

where

(3.4)

U_{0}:=\frac{V_{0}\otimes V_{0}}{\rho_{0}}-\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{n\rho_{0}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}.

This construction is made possible by the convex integration framework. The existence of such perturbations implies that
(\rho_{0},V_{0}+\widetilde{V})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\rho_{0}+{\rm div}(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})+{\rm div}\left(\frac{(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})\otimes(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})}{\rho_{0}}\right)+\nabla p_{\delta}(\rho_{0})=0.\end{split}\right.

Setting
\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}:=\tfrac{V_{0}+\widetilde{V}}{\rho_{0}}
leads to the weak solution
(\rho_{0},\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta})

to the compressible Euler system (
1.3
).

In our application,
(\rho_{0},V_{0},R_{0})
will be chosen from the subsolutions as in Lemma
2.2
. In particular,
\rho_{0}=\rho_{\delta}
,
V_{0}=\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}
,
R_{0}=R^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{R}_{\delta}
, and we know that
\rho_{0}>0
. Therefore, on
P
we impose the uniform condition

(3.5)

0<\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\leq\rho_{0}\leq\Lambda^{2}.

Note that the system (
3.2
)–(
3.4
) does not involve the pressure function
p_{\delta}
, and therefore all of the relevant estimates in this section are uniform in
\delta
. Abusing notations, we will still use
\varepsilon
and
\delta
in the following, but they are independent of the previous section.

In contrast to the approach in
[
5
]
, our construction of infinitely many solutions
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})
to the system (
3.2
)–(
3.3
) includes a new and essential feature. Specifically, we will ask that the following inequality holds:

(3.6)

\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{V_{0}\cdot\widetilde{V}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\,dt\right|\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\,dt.

This inequality is not present in
[
5
]
, and it constitutes a new estimate of the convex integration framework. It will allow one to control the turbulent part
\widetilde{V}
by the Reynolds tensor
R_{0}
(see Proposition
3.1
).

Following
[
7
,
5
,
9
]
, we will consider the relaxed condition

(3.7)

\frac{(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})\otimes(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})}{\rho}-(U_{0}+\widetilde{U})<{|V_{0}|^{2}\over n\rho_{0}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}+R_{0},

and define the set

(3.8)

X_{0}:=\left\{(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})\in C^{\infty}_{c}(P;{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}_{0}):\ (\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})\text{ solves }\eqref{ci system},\eqref{additional control}\text{ and }\eqref{ci constraint 2}\right\},

where
\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}_{0}
is the set of symmetric traceless matrices in dimension
n
.

It is clear that the set
X_{0}
is nonempty, since
0\in X_{0}
due to the condition (
3.4
) and the assumption
R_{0}>0
. We then define
X
to be the closure of
X_{0}
in the weak-
\ast
topology of
L^{\infty}
. The boundedness of
X
in
L^{\infty}
ensures that this weak-
\ast
topology is metrizable on
X
with a well-defined metric
d
, which in turn implies that
(X,d)
is a complete metric space.

The main idea is similar to that of
[
5
]
. That is, we would like to show that the saturation condition (
3.3
) holds on a residual set of
X
, and thus a Bairé category argument can be applied to yield infinitely many solutions to (
3.2
)–(
3.3
). However, here the difference lies, as pointed out above, in the extra condition (
3.6
). The key step in
[
5
]
is to establish an
L^{1}
-coercivity property for the weak-
\ast
convergence in terms of the trace of the “defect matrix”

M:={|V_{0}|^{2}\over n\rho_{0}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}+R_{0}-\frac{(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})\otimes(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})}{\rho_{0}}+(U_{0}+\widetilde{U})

with

\lambda_{\min}(M):=\text{ the smallest eigenvalue of }M.

The goal is to reproduce such an
L^{1}
-coercivity, with the additional constraint (
3.6
).

Lemma 3.1

(
L^{1}
-coercivity)
.

For any
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})\in X_{0}
, where
X_{0}
is defined in (
3.8
), there exists a sequence
\{(\widetilde{V}_{i},\widetilde{U}_{i})\}\subset X_{0}
that converges weakly-
*
to
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})
, such that

\|\widetilde{V}_{i}-\widetilde{V}\|_{L^{1}(P)}\geq\frac{c_{0}}{\Lambda}\int_{P}\mathrm{tr}\,M\,dx\,dt,

for some positive geometric constant
c_{0}
, where
\Lambda
is given in (
3.5
).

Proof.

The proof proceeds via discretization and is divided into three steps. The first and third steps are the same as in
[
5
, Lemma 3.2]
, but for the reader’s convenience, we include the full details.

Step 1.

We begin by considering a localized problem. From (
3.7
) we know that
M>0
on
P
. It follows from the smoothness of
M
that there exist a compact subsets
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega\subset P
, and a sufficiently small
\delta_{1}>0
such that any cube with center in
\Omega_{1}
and size
\delta_{1}
is inside
\Omega
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{1}}\mathrm{tr}M\,dxdt\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{P}\mathrm{tr}M\,dxdt,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\ast}:=\min_{\Omega}\left\{\min(\lambda_{\min}(M),\lambda_{\min}(R_{0}))\right\}>0.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{1}
be a fixed point, and choose a small open cube
Q
centered at
(x_{0},t_{0})
with size is smaller than
\delta_{1}
. Denote the following averages over
Q
:

(3.9)

\begin{split}\overline{V}:=\fint_{Q}(V_{0}+\widetilde{V})\,dxdt,\quad\overline{U}:=\fint_{Q}(U_{0}+\widetilde{U})\,dxdt,\quad\overline{R}_{0}:=\fint_{Q}R_{0}\,dxdt.\end{split}

Let

(3.10)

\underline{\rho}:=\min_{Q}\rho_{0},\quad C_{Q}:=\min_{Q}\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\geq 0,\quad\overline{R_{Q}}:=-\frac{\lambda_{*}}{16n}\,\mathrm{Id}_{n}+\overline{R_{0}},

and define

(3.11)

\overline{M_{Q}}:=\frac{C_{Q}}{n}\,\mathrm{Id}_{n}+\overline{R_{Q}}-\frac{\overline{V}\otimes\overline{V}}{\underline{\rho}}+\overline{U}\in\mathbb{S}^{n\times n}.

Note that the uniform continuity of
\rho_{0}
,
V_{0}
,
R_{0}
, and
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})
in
\Omega
implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
\delta>0
independent of
Q
such that whenever
|Q|<\delta
, the fluctuation of these quantities over
Q
is smaller than
\varepsilon
. In particular, by choosing
\varepsilon
small enough with respect to
\lambda_{*}
, we can ensure that for
\delta
sufficiently small,

\begin{split}\overline{R_{Q}}>0,&\quad\sup_{Q}\|M-\overline{M_{Q}}\|<\frac{\lambda_{*}}{8n},\quad\sup_{Q}\|\overline{R_{0}}-R_{0}\|<\frac{\lambda_{*}}{64n},\quad\sup_{Q}\left|C_{Q}-\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\right|<\frac{\lambda_{*}}{64n},\end{split}

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the standard matrix norm.

Together with (
3.10
) we obtain the following bound:

(3.12)

\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{n\rho_{0}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}+\overline{R}_{Q}<\frac{\lambda_{*}}{32n}\mathrm{Id}_{n}+R_{0},

and

(3.13)

\overline{M_{Q}}=M+(\overline{M_{Q}}-M)>M-\frac{\lambda_{*}}{8n}\mathrm{Id}_{n}>0,\text{ and thus}\quad\mathrm{tr}\overline{M_{Q}}>\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{tr}M\text{ on }Q.

Consider the rescaled set
\mathring{Q}:=\{(x,t/\sqrt{\underline{\rho}}):\ (x,t)\in Q\}
and define

\begin{split}X_{0}^{Q}:=&\Big\{(V,U)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathring{Q};{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}_{0}):\ (V,U)\text{ solves }\eqref{ci system}\text{ and }\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.(V_{0}+V)\otimes(V_{0}+V)-(U_{0}+U)<{C_{Q}\over n}\mathrm{Id}+R_{0}\right\}.\end{split}

Thanks to (
3.11
) and (
3.13
),
(0,0)\in X_{0}^{Q}
.

Since we have presented a localization argument above, we would like to establish
L^{1}
-coercivity of the perturbation with respect to constant states. Such a result has already been established in
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
. Therefore, quoting
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
with the constants

(V_{0},U_{0},R_{0},C_{0})=\left(\frac{\overline{V}}{\sqrt{\underline{\rho}}},\overline{U},\overline{R}_{Q},C_{Q}\right),

it follows that there exists a sequence
(\mathring{V}_{i},\mathring{U}_{i})\in X^{Q}_{0}
converging weakly to 0, and such that for every
i
:

\|\mathring{V}_{i}\|_{L^{1}(\mathring{Q})}\geq c_{1}(C_{Q}+\mathrm{tr}\overline{R}_{Q}-|\overline{V}|^{2})|\mathring{Q}|\geq c_{1}(\mathrm{tr}\overline{M_{Q}})|\mathring{Q}|

for some geometric constant
c_{1}>0
. Since
(\mathring{V}_{i},\mathring{U}_{i})\in X^{Q}_{0}
, it verifies (
3.2
) and

\left(\frac{\overline{V}}{\sqrt{\underline{\rho}}}+\mathring{V}_{i}\right)\otimes\left(\frac{\overline{V}}{\sqrt{\underline{\rho}}}+\mathring{V}_{i}\right)-\overline{U}+\mathring{U}_{i}<\frac{C_{Q}}{n}\mathrm{Id}_{n}+\overline{R}_{Q}.

Consider the change of variable
(\mathring{V_{i}},\mathring{U_{i}})(x,t):=\left(\frac{V_{i}}{\sqrt{\underline{\rho}}},U_{i}\right)(x,t\sqrt{\underline{\rho}})
. The functions
(V_{i},U_{i})
are now compactly supported in
Q
, and they still verify (
3.2
) and converge weakly to 0. Unraveling the change-of-variables and using (
3.13
) we find that

(3.14)

\|V_{i}\|_{L^{1}(Q)}=\underline{\rho}\|\mathring{V}_{i}\|_{L^{1}(\mathring{Q})}\geq c_{1}\underline{\rho}(\mathrm{tr}\overline{M_{Q}})|\mathring{Q}|=c_{1}\sqrt{\underline{\rho}}(\mathrm{tr}\overline{M_{Q}})|Q|\geq\frac{c_{1}}{4\Lambda}\int_{Q}\mathrm{tr}\,M\,dxdt.

Moreover, we have the following estimates on
Q

(3.15)

\begin{split}&\frac{(V_{0}+\widetilde{V}+V_{i})\otimes(V_{0}+\widetilde{V}+V_{i})}{\rho_{0}}-(U_{0}+\widetilde{U}+U_{i})\\
&\quad\leq\frac{(V_{0}+\widetilde{V}+V_{i})\otimes(V_{0}+\widetilde{V}+V_{i})}{\underline{\rho}}-(U_{0}+\widetilde{U}+U_{i})\\
&\quad\leq\frac{(\overline{V}+V_{i})\otimes(\overline{V}+V_{i})}{\underline{\rho}}-(\overline{U}+U_{i})+C(|V_{0}+\widetilde{V}-\overline{V_{0}}|+|U_{0}+\widetilde{U}-\overline{U_{0}}|)\,\mathrm{Id}_{n}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{{C_{Q}}}{n}\,\mathrm{Id}_{n}+\overline{R_{Q}}+\frac{\lambda_{*}}{64n}\,\mathrm{Id}_{n}\\
&\quad<\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{n\rho_{0}}\,\mathrm{Id}_{n}+R_{0},\end{split}

where we used the definition of
\underline{\rho}
for the first inequality, and
[
5
, Remark 2.2]
which ensures that the constant
C
is independent of the sequence
V_{i}
for the second one. With the constant
C
fixed, we obtain the third inequality by taking
\delta
even smaller if necessary. The last inequality follows from (
3.10
).

Step 2.
In this step, we verify that the function
\widetilde{V}+V_{i}
satisfies the inequality (
3.6
). This verification is a key part of the argument, as it ensures that
\widetilde{V}+V_{i}
meets the necessary condition to belong to the set
X_{0}
.

We let

\overline{V_{0}}:=\fint_{Q}V_{0}\,dx\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\overline{\rho_{0}}:=\fint_{Q}\rho_{0}\,dx.

Choose a small
Q
such that

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{V_{0}}{\rho_{0}}-\frac{\overline{V_{0}}}{\overline{\rho_{0}}}\right|\leq\left|\frac{V_{0}-\overline{V_{0}}}{\rho_{0}}\right|+\left|\frac{\overline{V_{0}}}{\rho_{0}}-\frac{\overline{V_{0}}}{\overline{\rho_{0}}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}|V_{0}-\overline{V_{0}}|+\frac{\overline{V_{0}}}{\Lambda^{4}}|\rho_{0}-\overline{\rho_{0}}|\leq\varepsilon\end{split}

for some
\varepsilon
to be determined later.
Note that
\int_{Q}V_{i}\,dx=0
, and from (
3.14
),

\|V_{i}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\geq\frac{1}{|Q|^{1/2}}\|V_{i}\|_{L^{1}(Q)}=\frac{{\underline{\rho}}}{|Q|^{1/2}}\|\mathring{V}_{i}\|_{L^{1}(\mathring{Q})}\geq c_{1}\sqrt{\underline{\rho}}(\mathrm{tr}\overline{M_{Q}})|Q|^{1/2}.

Together with (
3.13
), this allows us to calculate that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot V_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Q}\left(\frac{V_{0}}{\rho_{0}}-\frac{\overline{V_{0}}}{\overline{\rho_{0}}}\right)V_{i}\,dx\right|\leq\varepsilon\int_{Q}|V_{i}|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|Q|^{1/2}\|V_{i}\|_{L^{2}(Q)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{1}\sqrt{\underline{\rho}}(\mathrm{tr}\overline{M_{Q}})}\|V_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon\Lambda^{2}}{c_{1}(\mathrm{tr}\overline{M_{Q}})}\int_{Q}\frac{|V_{i}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\;dx\leq\frac{4\varepsilon\Lambda^{2}}{c_{1}(\mathrm{tr}M)}\int_{Q}\frac{|V_{i}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\varepsilon\Lambda^{2}}{c_{1}n\lambda_{\ast}}\int_{Q}\frac{|V_{i}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\;dx.

Choosing
\varepsilon
such that
\tfrac{4\varepsilon\Lambda^{2}}{nc_{1}\lambda_{\ast}}\leq\tfrac{1}{8}
we see that

\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot V_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{Q}\frac{|V_{i}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\;dx.

Assuming that

\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot\widetilde{V}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{Q}\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx,

we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot(\widetilde{V}+V_{i})}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot\widetilde{V}}{\rho_{0}}\right|+\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot V_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{Q}\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx+\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot V_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{Q}\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx+\frac{1}{8}\int_{Q}\frac{|V_{i}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\int_{Q}\frac{|\widetilde{V}+V_{i}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot V_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx.

To ensure

\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot(\widetilde{V}+V_{i})}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{Q}\frac{|\widetilde{V}+V_{i}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx,

we require that

-\frac{1}{4}\int\frac{V_{0}\cdot V_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\leq 0.

One can guarantee this condition in the following way. In particular,
if

\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot V_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\geq 0,

then we keep
V_{i}
as it is; otherwise, if

\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot V_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx<0,

then we replace
V_{i}
by
-V_{i}
.

With this choice, it follows that there exists a sequence
\{V_{i}\}
such that

(3.16)

\left|\int_{Q}\frac{V_{0}\cdot(\widetilde{V}+V_{i})}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{Q}\frac{|\widetilde{V}+V_{i}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx.

Thus, by combining (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we conclude that

V_{i}+\widetilde{V}\in X_{0}
.

Step 3.

Note that the uniform continuity of the functions
\rho
,
V_{0}
,
R_{0}
, and
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})
in
\Omega
guarantees that the size of the cube
Q
employed in Step 1 and Step 2 can be chosen independently of the point
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{1}
.

Fixing a sufficiently small
\delta<\delta_{1}
, we construct a grid in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with nodes at

(m_{1}\delta,\dots,m_{n}\delta,l\delta),\;\text{where}\;\;l\in\mathbb{N},\;(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

From this grid, we select a finite collection of cubes of size
\delta
, with vertices lying on the grid, that cover
\Omega_{1}
. Denoting these cubes by
\{Q_{k}:k=1,\dots,N\}
, we obtain a family with pairwise disjoint interiors, all contained within
\Omega
.

For each
k
, let
\{(V_{i}^{k},U_{i}^{k})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
denote the sequence of functions, compactly supported in
Q_{k}
, constructed as in Step 1 and Step 2. We then define the global perturbations in the domain
P
by

\widetilde{V}_{i}:=\widetilde{V}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}V_{i}^{k},\qquad\widetilde{U}_{i}:=\widetilde{U}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}U_{i}^{k}.

For a fixed
i
, the pairs
(V_{i}^{k},U_{i}^{k})
for
k=1,\dots,N
have disjoint supports. Hence, by Step 1 and Step 2, we conclude that
\widetilde{V}_{i}\in X_{0}
. Moreover, for each fixed
k
,
V_{i}^{k}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly, which implies that
\widetilde{V}_{i}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in the limit as
i\to\infty
. Finally, using
(
3.14
), we obtain

\begin{split}\quad\left\|\widetilde{V}_{i}-\widetilde{V}\right\|_{L^{1}(P)}&\geq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|V_{i}^{k}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{k})}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{4\Lambda}\int_{Q_{k}}\mathrm{tr}M\,dx\,dt\\
&\geq\frac{c_{1}}{4\Lambda}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\mathrm{tr}M\,dx\,dt\geq\frac{c_{1}}{8\Lambda}\int_{P}\mathrm{tr}M\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

This lemma yields the following proposition, which provides control over oscillations via the Reynolds stress term.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\widehat{V},\widehat{U})\in X
be a point of continuity of the identity map
\mathrm{Id}
from
(X,d)
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}})
. Then,
(\widehat{V},\widehat{U})
satisfies (
3.3
). In addition, there exists a positive number
C_{0}>0
, such that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{|\widehat{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\,dt\leq C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{tr}R_{0}\,dx\,dt.

Proof.

By definition, there exists a sequence
\{(V_{j},U_{j})\}\subset X_{0}
that converges weak-* to
(\widehat{V},\widehat{U})
. In particular, we have

V_{j}\to\widehat{V}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(P),

and hence also strongly in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(P)
.

From Lemma
3.1
, for each
(V_{j},U_{j})
there exists a subsequence
\{(V_{j,i},U_{j,i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converging weak-* to
(V_{j},U_{j})
. Note that
(V_{j},U_{j})
satisfies (
3.2
), and we have the estimate

\|V_{j,i}-V_{j}\|_{L^{1}(P)}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{8\Lambda}\int_{P}\mathrm{tr}M_{j}\,dx\,dt,

where
M_{j}
is given by

M_{j}:=\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{n\rho_{0}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}+R_{0}-\frac{(V_{0}+V_{j})\otimes(V_{0}+V_{j})}{\rho_{0}}+U_{0}+U_{j}.

By a diagonal argument, we may extract a subsequence
\{(V_{j,i(j)},U_{j,i(j)})\}
that converges weakly-* to
(\widehat{V},\widehat{U})
and satisfies

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|V_{j,i(j)}-\widehat{V}\|_{L^{1}(P)}\geq\frac{c_{1}}{8\Lambda}\int_{P}\mathrm{tr}M_{j}\,dx\,dt.

This yields to

(3.17)

\lim_{j}\int_{P}\mathrm{tr}M_{j}\,dx\,dt=0.

We further introduce

\widehat{M}:=\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{n\rho_{0}}\mathrm{Id}_{n}+R_{0}-\frac{(V_{0}+\widehat{V})\otimes(V+\widehat{V})}{\rho_{0}}+U_{0}+\widehat{U}.

It follows that
M_{j}\to\widehat{M}
weak-
*
. Note that
M_{j}>0
, which implies that
\widehat{M}\geq 0
a.e.. From (
3.17
) and noting that

\mathrm{tr}M_{j}\to\mathrm{tr}\widehat{M}\;\;\text{weak-}*,

one obtains

\lim_{j}\int_{P}\mathrm{tr}M_{j}\,dx\,dt=\int_{P}{\rm tr}\widehat{M}\,dx\,dt=0.

Thus,
{\rm tr}\widehat{M}=0
and so
\widehat{M}=0
, which yields that
(\widehat{V},\widehat{U})
satisfies (
3.3
).

Meanwhile, note from the definition of
\widehat{M}
that

\begin{split}{\rm tr}\widehat{M}&=\frac{|V_{0}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}+{\rm tr}R_{0}-\frac{|V_{0}+\widehat{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}={\rm tr}R_{0}-\frac{|\widehat{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}-\frac{2V_{0}\cdot\widehat{V}}{\rho_{0}}.\end{split}

This yields that

\begin{split}\int_{P}{\rm tr}R_{0}\,dx&=\int_{P}\frac{|\widehat{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx+2\int_{P}\frac{V_{0}\cdot\widehat{V}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\\
&\geq\int_{P}\frac{|\widehat{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{P}\frac{|\widehat{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\\
&=\frac{3}{4}\int_{P}\frac{|\widehat{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx,\end{split}

completing the proof.
∎

Using Proposition
3.1
, a Bairé category argument leads to the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(\rho_{0},V_{0},R_{0})\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}_{0}^{n\times n})

be given with
\rho_{0}
satisfying (
3.5
) and
R_{0}
being positive definite in some open set
P\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
U_{0}
be given as in (
3.4
). There exist infinitely many
(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{U})\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}_{0}^{n\times n})
which are compactly supported in
P
and satisfy (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). In addition, there exists a positive number
C_{0}>0
, such that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}\,dx\,dt\leq C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}{\rm tr}R_{0}\,dx\,dt.

4.
The proof of the Main result

To prove our main theorem, we will rely on the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
{\varepsilon}>0
, there exists a
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}({\varepsilon})>0
such that for any
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
, there exist infinitely many weak solutions
(\rho_{\delta},\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta})
to (
1.3
)on
[0,T-{\varepsilon}]
, satisfying

\|\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon,

where
(\rho_{\delta},\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta},R=R^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{R}_{\delta})
is the subsolution solving (
2.4
).

Proof.

Recalling Lemma
2.2
, we have

\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}={\bf m}_{\delta}+{\bf u}^{\varepsilon},\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{\delta}(t,x)=1+\delta^{2}\varrho_{\delta,K_{\ast}}(t,x),

where
\varrho_{\delta,K_{\ast}}
is given in (
2.3
). From Lemma
3.2
it follows that there exist infinitely many weak solutions to (
1.3
) of the form
({\rho}_{\delta},{\rho}_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta})
.

From the convex integration procedure in the previous section, we construct the perturbation as

\widetilde{V}=\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}.

So applying Lemma
3.2
again, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\|\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-{\bf v}_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T-{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{\delta}^{2}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{T-{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{|\widetilde{V}|^{2}}{\rho_{\delta}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T-{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{tr}R\,dx\,dt,

where
R=R^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{R}_{\delta}
, and we have used the estimate on
\rho_{\delta}
in (
2.6
) to derive the first inequality. From the definition of
R^{\varepsilon}
we have

{\rm tr}R^{\varepsilon}={\rm tr}({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})^{\varepsilon}-{\rm tr}({\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}^{\varepsilon})+\frac{n{\varepsilon}}{2}=(|{\bf u}|^{2})^{\varepsilon}-|{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{n{\varepsilon}}{2}>0,

and thus by Lemma
2.1
,

\int_{0}^{T-{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\mathrm{tr}R^{\varepsilon}\,dx\,dt\leq\|({\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})^{\varepsilon}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\int_{0}^{T-{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{n{\varepsilon}}{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C{\varepsilon}.

Recall from Lemma
2.2
that for
\delta<\delta_{0}
,
\|\tilde{R}_{\delta}\|_{{L^{\infty}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}}\leq C\varepsilon
. Together we obtain

\|\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-{\bf v}_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T-{\varepsilon}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq C\varepsilon,

completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Equipped with the preceding lemmas, we are now prepared to establish our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Given any
L^{2}
-bounded weak solution
{\bf u}
to the incompressible Euler equations (
1.2
) on the time interval
[0,T)
, for any
0<T^{\prime}<T
there exists
{\varepsilon}>0
with
T^{\prime}<T-{\varepsilon}
. We apply Lemma
2.1
, Lemma
2.2
, and Lemma
4.1
to construct a family of weak solutions
(\rho_{\delta},\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta})
to the scaled compressible Euler equations (
1.3
) on the time interval
[0,T-{\varepsilon}]
, and hence on
[0,T^{\prime}]
. Note that here
\delta=\delta({\varepsilon})
with the property that
\delta\to 0\ \Leftrightarrow\ {\varepsilon}\to 0
.
We can take the following initial data

(\rho_{\delta}^{0},{\bf m}_{\delta}^{0})(x):=(\rho_{\delta},\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta})(0,x).

Lemma
2.2
ensures that

\rho_{\delta}\to 1\quad\text{in }C^{0}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})\qquad\text{as }\ \delta\to 0.

From Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
2.2
we find that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{V}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\sqrt{\|\mathrm{tr}R\|_{L^{1}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\varepsilon}+C({\varepsilon})\delta\ \ \to 0\qquad\text{as }\ \delta\to 0,

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{V}/\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\|\mathrm{tr}R\|_{L^{1}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\varepsilon}+C({\varepsilon})\delta\ \ \to 0\qquad\text{as }\ \delta\to 0.

Further evoking (
2.8
), (
2.10
) and (
2.2
) we see that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\rho_{\delta}{\bf v}_{\delta}-{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|{\bf u}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C{\varepsilon}+C({\varepsilon})\delta\ \ \to 0\qquad\text{as }\ \delta\to 0,

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}-\rho_{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\|\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}+\|\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}([0,T^{\prime}]\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C{\varepsilon}+C({\varepsilon})\delta\ \ \to 0\qquad\text{as }\ \delta\to 0.

For any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{n};{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
with
{\rm div}\phi=0
, say the time support of
\phi
is in
[0,T^{\prime}]
for some
T^{\prime}<T
. Then

\begin{split}\int^{T^{\prime}}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho_{\delta}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi+\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}\otimes\sqrt{\rho_{\delta}}\widehat{{\bf v}}_{\delta}:\nabla\phi\,dxdt+\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{\delta}^{\gamma}}{\delta^{2}}{\rm div}\phi\,dx\,dt=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}{\bf m}_{\delta}^{0}\cdot\phi(\cdot,0)\,dx,\end{split}

Using the convergence results in the above, we find that the left-hand side of the above equality converges, as
\delta\to 0
, to

\int^{T^{\prime}}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}{\bf u}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi+{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u}:\nabla\phi\,dxdt,

which, recalling the fact that
{\bf u}
is a weak solution to the incompressible Euler equation, is equal to

-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}{\bf u}^{0}\cdot\phi(\cdot,0)\,dx.

Therefore, we must have

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}{\bf m}_{\delta}^{0}\cdot\phi(\cdot,0)\,dx\to\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}{\bf u}^{0}\cdot\phi(\cdot,0)\,dx\qquad\text{as }\ \delta\to 0.

Putting together all of the above, we have proved the main result, Theorem
1.1
.
∎

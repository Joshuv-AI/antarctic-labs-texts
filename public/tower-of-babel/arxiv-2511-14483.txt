Abstract
We present the discovery of a strongly lensed galaxy at z ∼ 11 z\sim 11 – 12 12 , dubbed the “Misty Moons”, identified in the JWST Treasury Survey, Vast Exploration for Nascent, Unexplored Sources (VENUS). The Misty Moons is gravitationally lensed by the galaxy cluster MACS J0257.1-2325 at z = 0.505 z=0.505 , and has five multiple images suggested by two independent lensing models. Two of the five images, ID1 and ID2 ( μ ∼ 20 − 30 \mu\sim 20-30 ), are very bright (F200W ∼ 26 \sim 26 AB mag) and exhibit blue SEDs with prominent Ly α \alpha breaks. In the source plane, the Misty Moons is a sub- L ∗ L^{*} galaxy ( M UV ∼ − 18.0 M_{\rm UV}\sim-18.0 mag) resolved into multiple stellar clumps, each of which has effective radius of r eff ∼ 10 r_{\mathrm{eff}}\sim 10 – 70 70 pc and stellar mass of ∼ 10 7 ​ M ⊙ \sim 10^{7}\ M_{\odot} . These clumps dominate the stellar mass budget of the Misty Moons ( ∼ > 80 % \mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}80\% ), similar to other high- z z clumps, which suggests a highly clustered mode of star formation in the early Universe, unlike seen in local dwarf galaxies. We convolve the source-plane image with the JWST/NIRCam point-spread function to produce a mock NIRCam image of the Misty Moons without lensing magnification, and find that the intrinsic galaxy has a radial surface-brightness profile comparable to those of z ∼ > 10 z\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}10 faint galaxies, such as JADES-GS-z13-0 and JADES-GS-z14-1, indicating that the Misty Moons represents a typical z ∼ > 10 z\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}10 faint galaxy. The Misty Moons, a lensed galaxy with resolved internal structures, provides an ideal laboratory for exploring the early stages of galaxy formation at z ∼ > 10 z\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}10 .

II.2
Photometry

We produce a detection image by taking an inverse variance-weighted mean of the psf-matched images of F200W, F277W, F356W, and F444W. Source detection is conducted on the detection image, using
Photutils

(
17
)
. As shown in Figure
1
, since ID1 and ID2 have clumpy, complex structures, we measure the isophotal fluxes with small apertures both to get reliable SEDs and to enhance the S/N ratios. The isophotal flux of each filter is corrected to the total flux by multiplying a correction factor, which is derived in the following way. We utilize the F277W filter, which shows the highest S/N ratios among the filters, as a reference filter to calculate the correction factor. We measure the total flux of the F277W image with an isophotal aperture, which is determined by a region
1.5\sigma
above the background and covers the entire system (Figure
4
). We then derive the correction factor, dividing the total flux by the small aperture flux.
For comparison, we also measure aperture photometry, extracted in circular apertures with diameters of
0\farcs 3
and corrected for total values with the Kron apertures
(
77
)
. In addition, the light that is missing beyond the Kron apertures is corrected in a similar method to that used in
128
and
127
. The measured photometry is presented in Table
4
. The isophotal and Kron fluxes of ID1 are consistent within the uncertainties while the isophotal fluxes of ID2 are slightly larger than its Kron fluxes, probably due to the missing fluxes of the Kron apertures. For the colors of the photometry, ID1 and ID2 are broadly consistent between isophotal and Kron apertures.

We measure UV slopes
\beta
of ID1 and ID2, using five filters of F200W, F210M, F277W, F300M, and F356W. Since F410M and F444W filters might be impacted by Balamer break/jump (see Figure
2

\beta=-2.37\pm 0.14
for ID1 and
\beta=-2.66\pm 0.14
for ID2.

(

\sigma=6
pixel. The smoothed image is subtracted from the original image, leaving behind a contrast map. We perform source extraction in the contrast map with a threshold of
\mathrm{S/N}=2

Photutils

(
17
)

>1.5

\mu

Despite the lensing distortions, the reconstructed morphologies and relative positions are highly similar, suggesting that the individual clumps of ID1 and ID2 may correspond to the same intrinsic structures.

We measure the half-light radius
r_{\mathrm{eff}}
of each clump by conducting Sérsic profile fitting with
GALFIT

(
105
)
. We fit to the detection image to measure
r_{\mathrm{eff}}
by enhancing the S/N ratios. For ID1, we adopt a five-component model consisting of five Sérsic profiles to simultaneously fit the four individual clumps and diffuse light. In the same way, a four-component model consisting of four Sérsic profiles is used for ID2. We set the initial parameters of the fitting with the clump identification results with
Photutils
described in the previous paragraph. We fix the Sérsic index to
n=1

48

5

We measure the clump fluxes by fitting the same models as used for
r_{\mathrm{eff}}
measurement to the PSF-matched filter images. We set only fluxes as free parameters, and fix the other parameters of the Sérsic profiles to the best-fit ones obtained from the detection images. To derive the flux uncertainties, we conduct Monte Carlo simulations. We construct mock observation images by injecting the PSF convolved best-fit models to the randomly selected sky regions with no other sources. We perform same flux measurements with the mock observation images, and determine the uncertainties from the
16

84

V
Results and Discussion

V.1
Physical Properties

The entire systems of ID1 and ID2 have intrinsically faint UV luminosity
M_{\mathrm{UV}}=-17.9\pm 0.2
and
-18.2\pm 0.1
, respectively, which corresponds to the sub-
L_{*}

(
58
)
, placing them among the faintest galaxies known at
z>10
(see Figure
3
). The intrinsic stellar mass and half-light radius of ID1 (ID2) are
\log M_{\mathrm{*,int}}=4.5_{-1.7}^{+2.7}\times 10^{7}\ M_{\odot}
(
3.2_{-0.8}^{+1.0}\times 10^{7}\ M_{\odot}
) and
\mathrm{r_{eff,int}}=181\pm 18
pc (
197\pm 28
pc), respectively. We estimate the half-light radii of ID1 and ID2 from the growth curve of the detection images of ID1 and ID2 (see Figure
4
). We then obtain the intrinsic half-light radii by correcting for magnification. The sizes and UV luminosities of ID1 and ID2 are comparable to the size-UV luminosity relation for
z>10

(
102
)
, which suggest that our galaxy would be a representative of the faint, low-mass galaxies at
z>10
. As mentioned above, a remarkable feature of this galaxy is that due to the strong magnification, it is resolved into multiple clumps, the central one of which is more than two times brighter than the other clumps. At
z>10
, there exist studies about similar clumpy galaxies, BulletArc-z11 (
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=11.10
;
13
) and MACS0647-JD (
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=10.17
;
62
;
61
), both of which show a bright central clump and the other smaller clumps. Interestingly, MACS0647-JD may be undergoing a galaxy merger based on the different star formation histories (SFHs) among the clumps while the individual clumps of BulletArc-z11 exhibit similar SFHs. We further discuss the possible clump formation scenario in the next section.

In the left panel of Figure
6
, we present the size and stellar mass relation of local star clusters, star-forming clumps, and dwarf galaxies. Our individual clumps are located in similar regions to local nuclear star clusters (NSCs), young massive star clusters (YMCs), ultra compact dwarfs (UCDs), and compact elliptical galaxies (cEs;
98
) as well as high-
z
individual star-forming clumps of the BulletArc-z11 (
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=11.10
;
13
) and Cosmic Grapes (
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=6.07
;
48
). The Cosmic Gems arc (
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=9.63
;
3
;
14
;
90
;
122
) and Firefly Sparkle (
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=8.30

93
) are further resolved into the
<10
pc scale star clusters, similar to local young star clusters (YSCs) and GCs. The studies of GCs in galaxy clusters at
z\sim 0.3-0.9
have become rather robust with JWST/NIRCam (see
11
;
60
). The formation ages and masses of these low-
z
GCs estimated from their colors are broadly consistent with those of our clumps, suggesting the possibility of being proto-GCs. We note that GCs lose some stars overtime due to tidal friction in their host galaxies (e.g.,
60
), likely explaining why local GCs have lower stellar masses.
Combining the delensed stellar masses and SFRs with intrinsic half light radii, we derive the stellar mass and SFR surface densities. Some of the clumps show high surface densities of
\Sigma_{*}\sim 10^{4}\ M_{\odot}\ \mathrm{pc^{-2}}
and
\Sigma_{\mathrm{SFR}}\sim 10^{2}\ M_{\odot}\ \mathrm{yr^{-1}}\ \mathrm{kpc^{-2}}
, which are comparable to those of compact, nitrogen-rich galaxies at
z\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}6
(e.g.,
65
;
113
;
110
;
29
).

As presented in Table
3
,
Prospector
fitting results indicate that most of the clumps consist of young (
\sim 40-70
Myr) stars, formed in a low-dust (
A_{\mathrm{V}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$<$}}}0.1
mag) environment. This is consistent with the rising star formation history obtained for these clumps, which is likely more common in the higher redshift Universe
(
56
;
118
, e.g., )
. The results from
Bagpipes

Prospector
fitting. The stellar ages for individual clumps estimated with non-parametric SFHs are relatively older than those estimated with short SFHs, although the differences are not conclusive given their uncertainties. If real, these differences may indicate that individual clumps contain older stellar populations. We note that although the central bright, compact clumps of ID1 and ID2 (A.1 and A.2, respectively) might host active galactic nuclei (AGNs), they are marginally resolved from the PSF.

V.2
Clump Formation

As shown in Figure
4
, multiple clumps are resolved in the Misty Moons at this high redshift due to the high magnifications. To investigate the lensing effects, we construct a mock observation image of ID1 by convolving the source plane image with the NIRCam PSF. The mock image shows smoothed morphology, whose light distribution is comparable to that of similarly faint (
M_{\mathrm{UV}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}-19
) galaxies at
z>10
, GS-z13-0 (
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=13.20
;
38
) and GS-z14-1 (
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=13.90
;
28
), as shown in Figure
7
. As discussed at
z\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}6
with similarly strongly lensed galaxies
(
48
;
122
)
, this suggests that such faint galaxies at
z>10
may indeed have internal clumpy structure, which is not resolved without lensing magnifications.

8
, we compare the clustered mass fractions of high-
z
galaxies (colored symbols,

;
3
;

;

;

;

;

). We derive the clustered mass fraction, dividing the sum of the clump/cluster masses by total galaxy mass. For the Misty Moons, the probability distributions of the mass fractions pile up near unity, we obtain
1\sigma

79\%
for ID1 and
93\%
for ID2. For the cluster mass fraction of the Cook et al. sample, we first calculate cluster masses by multiplying cluster formation rate of CFR (
1-100

z

\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}30\%
) than that measured in local galaxies (
\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$<$}}}1\%

Occasionally, due to accretions or merger events, local dwarf galaxies experience an increase in high-pressure and SFR density resulting in the formation of massive star clusters, yet these events take place in a system where the stellar mass is anyway dominated by stellar populations formed over an Hubble time. In contrast, for high-
z
low-mass galaxies, star-forming clumps and star clusters dominate the mass budget of the entire systems. In other words, high-
z
low-mass clumpy galaxies might be in a totally different star formation mode, dominated by top down clustered star formation
(
45
)
.

One of the key differences between the high-
z
clumpy galaxies and local dwarf galaxies is SFR surface density. As shown in the right panel of Figure
8
, the high-
z
clumpy galaxies exhibit
\sim 10^{2}-10^{4}
times higher SFR surface densities than those of the local dwarf galaxies. As described above, in the local Universe, positive correlations between cluster formation efficiency and SFR surface density has been reported both observationally (e.g.,
4
) and theoretically (e.g.,
78
;
106
). High SFR density is associated to high gas density (e.g.,
72
), which is a major driver of the violent disk instability, one possible scenario for clump formation at high-
z
(e.g.,
40
;
39
;
48
). Recent simulation results of
86
have shown that low-mass (
\sim 10^{8}\ M_{\odot}
) galaxies form massive self-gravitating compact gas disks, which collapse into massive bound clumps by gravitational instability. We note that although it seems difficult to form stellar disks at this high redshift, some studies suggest possible rotating gas disk formation at
z>10

(
131
;
111
, e.g., )
. The disk instability scenario is aligned with the top-down clump formation suggested by high clustered mass fractions. Regarding the in-situ clumps formation, filament fragmentation (e.g.,
53
) and turbulence-driven fragmentation (e.g.,
41
) are also suggested as possible scenarios. In recent studies using a cosmological radiation-hydrodynamic simulation
(
52
;
53
)
, low mass dwarf galaxies at
z>8
with no disk and bulge experience star cluster formation through the fragmentation of accreting dense filaments (i.e., cold inflow) before the gas reached the center. The suppression of cooling via the far-UV (FUV) radiation leads to bursty star formation and massive, compact star clusters
(
116
)
, sharing the comparable properties with high-
z
star clusters
(
93
;
122
)
. These star clusters orbit the center of the dark matter halo, where star formation happens later, resulting in the formation of a NSC
(
53
)
. Considering that our central clumps (A.1 and A.2) have comparable stellar masses and effective radii to those of NSCs (Figure
6
), the filament fragmentation is also a plausible scenario for the clump formation of the Misty Moons.

One of the other possibilities of clump formation is a galaxy merger (e.g.,
85
;
62
;
12
;
84
;
95
;
56
;
73
). In fact,
62
suggest that the clumpy structure of MACS0647-JD at
z_{\mathrm{spec}}=10.17
could be due to a galaxy merger based on the different SFHs among the clumps and comparison with some simulation results. For the Misty Moons, stellar ages of individual clumps estimated from
Prospector
fitting with non-parametric SFHs are comparable to each other while stellar ages from
Bagpipes
fitting with short SFHs (
\tau=10
Myr) exhibit variations among clumps (see Table
3
), which might suggest a possible merger scenario. However, the current NIRCam images have almost no coverage of the wavelengths longer than Balmer breaks, which complicates the exact measurements of stellar ages and SFHs enough to conclude that the individual clumps undergo different SFHs. Future observations with IFU (for gas kinematics and spatial distribution of stellar/gas properties) and MIRI (for metallicity measurement and optical continuum from old stellar populations) are helpful to further explore the clump properties and physical origins of the clump formation.

VI
Summary

In this paper, we report the discovery of a strongly lensed galaxy at
z\sim 11-12
behind the galaxy cluster MACS J0257.1-2325 (the Misty Moons), newly identified in the JWST Treasury Survey VENUS. Independent lensing models predict that the Misty Moons has five multiple images, two of which, ID1 and ID2 (
\mu\sim 20-30
), are very bright (
\mathrm{F200W}\sim 26
mag) in the image plane, while intrinsically faint (
M_{\mathrm{UV}}\sim-18
) in the source plane, and exhibit blue SEDs with prominent Ly
\alpha
breaks. In addition, ID1 and ID2 are resolved into multiple clumps in NIRCam images. We summarize our main findings below:

1.

We conduct SED fitting to ID1 and ID2 with
EAZY
and
Prospector
to estimate their photometric redshifts and stellar populations. For both the fitting codes, we include either IGM absorption models or Ly
\alpha
emission. The resulting photometric redshifts are
z_{\mathrm{phot}}\sim 11
when including IGM absorption while the models with Ly
\alpha
emission show
z_{\mathrm{phot}}\sim 12
. We also fit individual clumps of ID1 and ID2, fixing the redshift to
z=11.5
. The SED fitting results show that the entire systems and individual clumps of the Misty Moons consist of young stars (
t\sim 40-70
Myr with non-parametric SFHs and
t\sim 20-40
Myr with short SFHs) with low dust content (
A_{\mathrm{V}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$<$}}}0.1
mag), likely undergoing its first significant starburst.

2.

Correcting for the high magnification, ID1 (ID2) is resolved into four (three) clumps with effective radii of
r_{\mathrm{eff}}\sim 10-70
pc and stellar masses of
M_{*}\sim 10^{7}\ M_{\odot}
, which are comparable to those of the local nuclear/young massive star clusters and high-
z
(
z\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}6
) star-forming clumps. These star-cluster-like clumps have high clustered mass fractions (
\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}80\%
), similar to other high-
z
clumps and star clusters. This suggests that star formation at these early epochs is clump-dominated, in contrast to compact star clusters observed in local blue dwarf galaxies, which might be driven by high SFR surface densities.

3.

We convolve the source-plane image with the JWST/NIRCam point-spread function to produce a mock NIRCam image of the Misty Moons without lensing magnification, and find that the intrinsic galaxy has a radial surface-brightness profile comparable to those of faint (
M_{\mathrm{UV}}\sim-18
mag) galaxies at
z\mathrel{\hbox{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{$\sim$}}\hss}\hbox{$>$}}}10
, including JADES-GS-z13-0 and JADES-GS-z14-1. This suggests the possibility of more clump formation in the low mass galaxies in the early Universe.

Combination therapy with nelarabine, pegaspargase, and venetoclax for adult patients with R/R T-cell ALL/LBL
Authors: Niranjan Khaire, Elias Jabbour, Tapan Kadia, Nicholas Short, Gautam Borthakur, Naveen Pemmaraju, Naval Daver, Abhishek Maiti, Partow Kebriaei, Shilpa Paul, Rebecca Garris, Farhad Ravandi, Hagop Kantarjian, Nitin Jain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

TO THE EDITOR:

T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia/lymphoma (T-ALL/LBL) comprises ˜20% of pediatric and adult ALL.1,2 Unlike B-ALL, lack of availability of targeted therapies has led to dismal survival of patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) T-ALL. Nelarabine, an antimetabolite has significant activity against T-ALL lymphoblasts and is the only drug approved for R/R T-ALL after 2 previous regimens by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2005. When used as a single agent in this setting, the response rates [complete remission (CR)/complete remission with incomplete count recovery (CRi)] are ˜30% to 35% with a median overall survival (OS) ranging from 4.6 to 8 months.3, 4, 5 The optimal use of nelarabine in the R/R T-ALL/LBL either as a single agent or as a combination is unclear. A retrospective analysis of 44 patients with R/R T-ALL reported a CR/CRi rate of 40% as a single agent and 62% when used as a combination therapy.6 The most common combination used in this experience (n = 23/29, 79%) was the NECTAR regimen (combined nelarabine with etoposide and cyclophosphamide). Pegaspargase and venetoclax are other drugs that are active against T-ALL/LBL and are of lower intensity compared with conventional chemotherapy drugs.7, 8, 9, 10 We report a retrospective analysis of the combination of nelarabine, pegaspargase, and venetoclax (Nel-Peg-Ven) as a salvage regimen for R/R T-ALL/LBL.

We identified 13 patients with R/R T-ALL/LBL and active disease who were treated with at least 1 cycle of combination therapy with Nel-Peg-Ven from August 2018 to October 2024 at MD Anderson Cancer center, Houston. We excluded patients who received the combination as a consolidation therapy while in remission. The patients were treated with this salvage regimen based on the treating physician’s discretion; no formal inclusion or exclusion criteria were applied. All patients provided informed consent before proceeding with this treatment regimen.

Baseline patient characteristics are shown in Table 1. The median age was 44 years (range, 21-64) and 8 (62%) were male. The diagnosis at baseline was T-ALL in 9 patients, T-LBL in 2 patients, and T/Myeloid mixed-phenotype acute leukemia (MPAL) in 2 patients. In total, 4 of 13 (31%) patients had early T-cell precursor acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ETP-ALL) and an additional 2 of 13 (15%) had near ETP-ALL (supplemental Table 1). In total, 3 of 12 (25%) patients had complex cytogenetics and next-generation sequencing revealed NOTCH1/FBXW7 mutations in 7 of 11 (64%) and TP53 in 3 of 11 (27%) patients. Most patients (n = 9) received hyperfractionated cyclophosphamide, vincristine, adriamycin (doxorubicin), and dexamethasone alternating with methotrexate and cytarabine (hyper-CVAD) based therapy as the initial line of treatment.11 Median number of prior lines of therapy was 2 (range, 1-6). Two patients had received prior allogeneic stem cell transplant (allo-SCT). The number of patients who were previously exposed to nelarabine, pegaspargase, and venetoclax were 2 (15%), 3 (23%), and 5 (38%), respectively (supplemental Tables 1 and 2). The median bone marrow (BM) blast count at the time of initiation of Nel-Peg-Ven was 11% (range, 2-90) (Table 1; supplemental Figure 1). Median number of cycles of Nel-Peg-Ven administered was 1 (range, 1-3) (supplemental Table 3). The details of the treatment regimen and dosages used are detailed in supplemental Table 4. The most common dosage used was nelarabine at 650 mg/m2 on days 1 to 5, pegaspargase 1500 IU/m2 on day 5, and venetoclax 400 mg daily from days 1 to 7. The venetoclax dose was modified according to concomitant azole use. All patients were given standard antibacterial, azole, and valacyclovir prophylaxis. Patients were allowed to receive granulocyte colony-stimulating factor support as per the treating physician’s discretion.

Seven (54%) patients responded (3 CR, 3 CR with incomplete hematologic recovery [CRh], and 1 CRi); 1 patient had morphologic leukemia-free state, and 1 patient had aplastic marrow (Figure 1A; supplemental Table 3) as per National Comprehensive Cancer Network response criteria.12 Supplemental Figure 1 shows the response in terms of bone marrow blast count. Among the responders, 6 of 7 achieved measurable residual disease (MRD)-negative remission by flow cytometry (10−4 sensitivity). Notably, 6 of the 7 patients who responded proceeded to allo-SCT at a median interval of 3.7 months (range, 1.93-24.2) from treatment initiation. Among these, 5 patients proceeded to allo-SCT immediately after Nel-Peg-Ven, whereas 1 patient had neurologic adverse event causing paraparesis and received maintenance treatment with a combination of decitabine plus venetoclax after Nel-Peg-Ven before proceeding to allo-SCT (supplemental Table 4). With a median follow up of 40.3 months, the median OS was 7.9 months (Figure 1B). Among the responders (n = 7), median event-free survival (EFS) and OS without censoring for allo-SCT were not reached, and the 2-year EFS and OS were 54% and 71%, respectively (Figure 1C). Among the 6 patients who underwent allo-SCT, 2 of 6 had posttransplant relapse at 3- and 21-month post–allo-SCT, respectively, and the other 4 are alive and in remission at 10.9, 25, 46, and 79 months, respectively (supplemental Tables 4 and 5). Two of the 4 patients who had a central nervous system disease previously had a recurrent central nervous system disease after receiving the Nel-Peg-Ven regimen (supplemental Table 5).

Five (38%) patients had febrile neutropenia with infectious complications including COVID-19 infection, angioinvasive fungal infection, bacteremia with Stenotrophomonas, PJP pneumonia, Enterobacter pneumonia, perirectal abscess, and cholecystitis. Two (15%) patients had grade-4 liver enzyme elevation (1 of which was thought to be due to cholecystitis and sepsis), and 1 (8%) patient had grade-3 liver enzyme elevation. Two patients (15%) had grade-3-4 neurologic toxicity. One patient had grade-4 ascending sensorimotor polyradiculopathy causing paraparesis, 1 other patient had grade-3 neurologic toxicity in the form of transient ischemic attacks (supplemental Table 5). Supplemental Figure 2 gives details of time to count recovery with the first and subsequent cycles for all the patients. In total, 11 of 13 patients received growth factor support to help with count recovery.

Nelarabine remains the only approved agent for R/R T-ALL. As summarized in supplemental Table 6, outcomes with nelarabine combination regimens appear better than monotherapy. Here, we report our experience with a novel combination of nelarabine with pegaspargase and venetoclax as an effective and safe nelarabine-based combination therapy for adults with R/R T-ALL.

In our cohort of 13 patients, we observed an overall response rate of 7 of 13 (54%) of whom 5 of 7 (71%) could go on to receive allo-SCT. The median OS of the whole cohort was 7.9 months and the median EFS of the responders was not reached. Thus, the outcomes of the Nel-Peg-Ven regimen are comparable with the NECTAR regimen, although 15% of the patients in our experience were previously exposed to nelarabine. The observed adverse event profile including the rates of neurologic toxicity and hepatotoxicity were well within expected range for nelarabine, as well as pegaspargase. Second, Nel-Peg-Ven is relatively lower intensity regimen compared with the NECTAR regimen and is a good option for frail patients who may be unable to tolerate the NECTAR regimen and can be an effective bridge to transplant in these situations.

The limitations of the study are its retrospective study design, heterogeneity in the treatment regimens used, and the small sample size. However, we believe that this novel combination is an important addition to the limited options available for treatment of R/R T-ALL. In conclusion, combined Nel-Peg-Ven is an effective therapy in achieving remission in this difficult to treat patient population, allowing patients to proceed with a potentially curative allo-SCT.

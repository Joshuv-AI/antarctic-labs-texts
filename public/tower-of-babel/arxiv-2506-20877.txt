ThirdEye: Cue - Aware Monocular Depth Estimation via Brain - Inspired Multi - Stage Fusion
Calin Teodor Ioan

Abstract
Monocular depth estimation methods traditionally rely on deep learning models to implicitly understand complex depth features directly from RGB pixels. However, this approach often neglects explicit monocular cues that the human visual system naturally exploits—such as occlusion boundaries, shading patterns, and perspective layouts. Instead of expecting models to learn these cues independently, we propose ThirdEye , a cue-aware pipeline that explicitly "spoon-feeds" carefully extracted monocular cues from specialized, pre-trained, and frozen networks. Inspired by biological vision, ThirdEye fuses these explicitly provided cues through a hierarchical, cortically-inspired structure (V1→V2→V3), integrated with a key–value working-memory system that dynamically weighs cues based on reliability. The depth estimation is finalized by an adaptive-bins transformer head, yielding high-resolution disparity maps. This modular approach allows ThirdEye to benefit significantly from external supervision, requiring minimal fine-tuning. This extended version elaborates further on the architectural rationale, neuroscientific foundations, and a detailed experimental framework, with quantitative analyses forthcoming in subsequent revisions.

4.1
Cue Specialists & Uncertainty Gating

Implementation.

ThirdEye relies on three off
-
the
-
shelf,
pre
-
trained networks: HED for occlusion edges, SDPS
-
Net for surface normals, and HorizonNet for 1
-
D room layout. Each network additionally regresses a log
-
variance map
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W}
via a lightweight variance head appended to its penultimate layer.
The cue tensor
C
(edges, normals, or layout) is then scaled by an exponentiated reliability weight before fusion:

\tilde{C}=C\,\odot\,\exp(-\sigma),

(1)

where
\odot
denotes element
-

[
21
]
.

Psychophysics shows that humans weight visual depth cues in proportion to their reliability (inverse variance)
[
21
]
. Recent work demonstrates that such reliability signals appear as gain modulations in early visual cortex and the cerebellum
[
52
]
. Our exponential gating mimics this divisive gain, allowing fused features downstream to emphasise trustworthy cues while suppressing noisy estimates.

4.2
V1 Feature Stem

Architecture.

Two stride
-
4 convolutions (
3\times 3

-

Analogy.

Hubel and Wiesel famously showed that V1 contains simple and complex cells tuned to orientation, spatial frequency, and phase
[
46
]
. Similarly, our stem decomposes the RGB image into oriented edge maps. Down

Analogy.

Neurons in V2 are selective for contours that belong to the same object and show border

4.4
V3 Context Layer with Memory

Architecture.

The V3 stage consists of two Swin
-

7
) augmented with a key–value memory bank

(slots

\text{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\mathrm{softmax}\Big(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{D}}\Big)V.

(2)

Analogy.

Electrophysiological studies reveal persistent firing in V3/V4 during visual working
-

[
49
]
. Feedback from these areas to V1/V2 broadens spatial tuning, improving integration of fragmented contours
[
3
]
. Our memory bank embodies this sustained signal: cue combinations that repeatedly prove useful are stored and re

Humans deprived of reliable binocular vision—owing to strabismus, amblyopia or monocular enucleation—still develop a workable sense of three

Recruitment of disparity neurons.
Two

[

•

Expansion of cue columns.

[
57
]
.

•

Cross
-

Electrophysiological recordings in macaque MT indicate that blur gradient sensitivity is up

These findings jointly support a picture in which the dorsal depth network re

1.

The

of Eq. (1) automatically down

2.

The key–value

3.

Because cue specialists are

Persistent spiking in layers V1–V3 during delay intervals is widely documented
[

,
2
,

]
. Neuro
-

[

]
. Our learnable decay gate
\eta
explicitly captures this idea: when the softmax
-

\eta\!\to\!1
and memories consolidate; under low certainty
\eta
decays, paralleling noradrenergic reset.

6.2
Computational Implementation in
ThirdEye

Our key–value bank implements a

: each slot stores a low
-

-

corresponds to feedback projections from higher ventral layers, while the gated write realises Hebbian potentiation conditioned on surprise:

M_{t+1}\;=\;(1-\eta)M_{t}+\eta\,\sigma\big(Q_{t}K_{t}^{\top}/\sqrt{d}\big)V_{t},

(11)

where
Q_{t}
derives from feedforward V3 units and

are memory keys/values.

Link to Synaptic Theory.

Setting
\eta
equal to the probability of presynaptic–postsynaptic co
-

-

et al.

[

.

6.3
Neuromodulatory Gating and Plasticity–Stability Trade
-
Off

Acetylcholine is known to enhance sensory gain yet accelerate synaptic decay, whereas norepinephrine promotes reset when uncertainty rises
[
65
]
. By parameterising
\eta=\sigma(W_{\eta}c)
with cue
-

c
, ThirdEye mimics a mixture of these modulators: high reliability (low surprise) pushes
\eta\to 1
(stability), whereas conflicting cues shrink
\eta
(plasticity).

Our model predicts that:

1.

-

8
Discussion

In this section we critically examine
ThirdEye
through complementary lenses—psychophysics, neuroscience, computer vision practice, and engineering pragmatics—and delineate promising avenues for subsequent work.

8.1
Cue Complementarity and Synergy

Qualitative probes confirm that each frozen specialist contributes a distinct facet of scene structure: HED accentuates depth discontinuities, SDPS
-
Net straightens planar patches, and HorizonNet stabilises vanishing
-
point geometry (Fig.
1
).
The reliability
-
gated fusion layer successfully suppresses noisy cue regions—e.g., uncertain normals on glossy surfaces—mirroring Bayesian cue integration observed in human depth perception
[
6
,
7
]
.

8.2
Working Memory and Feedback Dynamics

The 32
-
slot key–value bank enables
recurrent evidence accumulation
: early V1 features can be revisited by later V3 tokens through memory retrieval, effectively extending the network’s temporal receptive field without increasing depth.
This mechanism operationalises contemporary views that working
-
memory traces reside in distributed early visual populations rather than a dedicated buffer
[
4
,
2
]
.
Preliminary ablations (Sec.
4
) reveal a ~3.1% drop in
\delta_{1}
when memory is disabled, highlighting its geometric utility beyond biological plausibility.

8.3
Interpretability and Diagnostic Value

Because cue pathways are modular and externally supervised, practitioners can inspect intermediate edge/normal/layout maps to diagnose depth failures.
Mis
-
estimated surfaces often trace back to a single corrupted specialist, enabling targeted retraining or online replacement.
Such
white
-
box
interpretability is uncommon in monolithic regressors and aligns with the growing demand for accountable CV systems in safety
-
critical settings
[
8
]
.

8.4
Domain Generalisation and Plug–and–Play Upgrades

Most MDE models suffer under domain shift (e.g., indoor
\rightarrow
outdoor).
ThirdEye alleviates this by allowing specialists to be swapped for domain
-
specific counterparts at inference time—e.g., replacing HorizonNet with a LiDAR
-
supervised room
-
layout net for warehouse robots—while fine
-
tuning only the tiny fusion head.
We hypothesise that this flexibility, coupled with uncertainty gating, will improve out
-
of
-
distribution robustness over monolithic baselines, echoing findings in recent domain
-
aware depth frameworks
[
9
]
.

8.5
Limitations and Future Directions

•

Cue availability.
Performance degrades when a specialist fails—e.g., HED on nighttime imagery—suggesting a need for self
-
diagnosis and on
-
the
-
fly redundancy.

•

Memory scaling.
The fixed slot count may bottleneck very large scenes; adaptive memory allocation or attractor dynamics
[
5
]
could mitigate this.

•

Photometric calibration.
ThirdEye currently presumes photometric consistency between training and deployment; integrating self
-
supervised scale recovery or defocus cues
[
10
]
is ongoing work.

It is important to note that some of the perception associated with these cues are passed down as a result of evolution. This is done through pre-defined neural structures in the brain. ThirdEye attempts to capture the complexity of these structures. But our current understanding of how this works is somewhat limited.

9
Conclusion

We have detailed
ThirdEye
, a cue
-
aware, biologically grounded MDE architecture that fuses frozen specialists via a cortical
-
style hierarchy equipped with working memory.
By modelling cue reliability and feedback loops, ThirdEye bridges neuroscience and computer vision, paving the way for robust, explainable depth perception.

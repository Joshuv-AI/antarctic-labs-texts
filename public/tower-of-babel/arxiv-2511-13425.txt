1.
Introduction

A normal projective variety is said to be
Fano
(resp.,
weak Fano
) if its anti-canonical divisor is ample (resp., nef and big). In the minimal model program, Fano varieties with mild singularities (e.g., terminal or canonical singularities) are among the fundamental building blocks of algebraic varieties.

Numerical invariants such as the Picard number, the anti-canonical degree, and the Fano index (see §
2.2
for precise definitions) play an important role in the classification of terminal or canonical Fano varieties.
A natural first step in such a classification is to determine the explicit bounds for these invariants. A key technique for obtaining such bounds is the so-called
Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality
for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial weak Fano varieties, whenever the second Chern class
c_{2}(X)
is well defined:

(1.1)

c_{1}(X)^{n}\leq b_{n}\,c_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}.

This was proved in
[
11
]
*Corollary 1.7 for the terminal case. Later in
[
19
,
20
]
, the bound
b_{n}
was improved to
4
(and to
3
for terminal Fano
3
-folds) under the Picard number one condition, by studying rank one subsheaves of the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{X}
. Using the same approach and the generalized Chern classes in
[
13
]
, the Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality was further extended to Fano varieties of Picard number one with
\epsilon
-lc singularities. These inequalities turned out to be very effective in establishing upper bounds for degrees
[
13
]
*Theorem 1.1 and for Fano indices
[
12
]
*Theorem 1.1.

In §
3
of this paper, we establish the bound
b_{n}=4
in (
1.1
) for all canonical weak Fano varieties with
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\geq 3
, without the Picard number one restriction. In fact, we prove that
4\,\hat{c}_{2}(X)-c_{1}(X)^{2}
is pseudoeffective in this setting (see §
2.3
for the definition of the generalized second Chern class
\hat{c}_{2}
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical weak Fano variety of dimension
n\geq 2
. Suppose the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\geq 3
. Then for any nef divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{n-2}
,

\left(4\,\hat{c}_{2}(X)-c_{1}(X)^{2}\right)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}\geq 0.

Moreover, if
X
is Fano and the divisors
D_{i}
are ample, then

\left(4\,\hat{c}_{2}(X)-c_{1}(X)^{2}\right)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}>0.

Applying Theorem
1.1
to
D_{i}=c_{1}(X)=-K_{X}
for any
i
, we immediately have a Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality as follows.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical weak Fano variety of dimension
n\geq 2
. Suppose the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\geq 3
. Then

c_{1}(X)^{n}\leq 4\,\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}.

Moreover, the inequality is strict if
X
is Fano.

As the Picard number one case, Corollary
1.2
is expected to be useful in classifying terminal or canonical Fano varieties of any Picard number. We present one such application in Section
4
, with an introduction in the following subsection.

1.1.
Application on Fano indices of Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-folds

An interesting phenomenon in the minimal model program is that the index of a log canonical singularity
P\in X
, the index of a terminal Calabi–Yau variety, and the Fano index of a canonical weighted projective space
X

\cup~\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid m\leq\dim X\}
all coincide in low dimensions. Specifically, the common index set is

I_{2}\coloneq\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid\varphi(m)\leq 2\}=\{1,2,3,4,6\}

for surfaces (see
[
7
]
,
[
21
]
, and the fact that
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(S)=3,4,6
for
S=\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathbb{P}^{1}(1,1,2),\mathbb{P}^{1}(1,2,3)
, respectively), and

I_{3}\coloneq\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid\varphi(m)\leq 20\}\setminus\{60\}

for
3
-folds (see
[
7
]
*Theorem 0.1,
[
22
]
*Proposition 3.2, and
[
14
]
*Table 3), where
\varphi(m)
is the Euler function of
m
. In particular, the number in
I_{3}
is at most
66
.

In
[
12
]
, Jiang and the author proved that the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index of a canonical Fano
3
-fold is also bounded above by
66
. A natural next step in classifications is to determine the complete set
I_{3,can}
of all possible
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano indices of canonical Fano
3
-folds, where

I_{3}\subseteq I_{3,can}\subseteq\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid m\leq 66\}.

In general, one cannot expect
I_{3}=I_{3,can}
. This is suggested by the existence of a canonical Fano surface
S
with
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(S)=5
(see, e.g.,
[
28
]
*theorem 1.2 and
[
23
]
*Lemma 3), which implies
I_{2}\subsetneq I_{2,can}
. In §
4
, we use Corollary
1.2
to show that if
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\notin I_{3}
, then
X
is not Gorenstein.

Theorem 1.3

(Corollary
4.8
)
.

For any Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold
X
, its
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
lies in the set
\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid m\leq 22\}\cup\{24,30,42\}\subsetneq I_{3}
.

Singularities

Let

\mathbb{Q}

. Let

K_{Y}=f^{*}K_{X}+\sum_{E}a(E,X)E,

where

runs over all prime divisors on

and

. We say that

has

(resp.,

,
klt
,
\epsilon
-lc
for some fixed

) singularities if
a(E,X)>0
(resp.,
a(E,X)\geq 0
,
a(E,X)>-1
,
a(E,X)\geq\epsilon-1
) for any exceptional divisor

over
X
. For simplicity, we say that
X
is
terminal
,
canonical
,
klt
, or
\epsilon
-lc
, respectively.

2.2.
Degrees and Fano indices

Let
X
be a canonical weak Fano variety of dimension
n
. The
(anti-canonical) degree
of
X
is defined to be
(-K_{X})^{n}=c_{1}(X)^{n}
and the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
of
X
is defined to be

q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\coloneq\max\{q\mid-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA,\,A\in\Cl(X)\}.

It is known that
\Cl(X)
is a finitely generated Abelian group, so
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
is a positive integer. For more details, see
[
10
]
*§ 2 or
[
26
]
.

Recall some basic facts about degrees and their relations to Fano indices and other invariants of Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-folds.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold. Then

(1)

c_{1}(X)^{3}
is an even number;

(2)

c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq 72
, and the equality holds if and only if
X\cong\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,3)
or
\mathbb{P}(1,1,4,6)
, where
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)=6
or
12
respectively.

Proof.

The first conclusion follows directly from the Riemann–Roch formula
\chi(-K_{X})=\frac{c_{1}(X)^{3}}{2}+3
. The second conclusion is proved by Prokhorov
[
25
]
.
∎

Theorem 2.2

(
[
13
]
*Theorem 4.2)
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold. Let
A
be an ample Weil divisor on

-K_{X}\sim qA
for some positive integer

. Let

2.3.
\mathbb{Q}
-variant of Langer’s inequality

Let
X
be a normal projective variety with canonical singularities of dimension
n\geq 2
. Then in codimension 2,
X
has only quotient singularities and admits a structure of a
\mathbb{Q}
-variety (see
[
9
]
*§ 3.2 and § 3.6). Then as in
[
9
]
*Construction 3.8 and § 3.7, for any reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, we can define the
generalized second Chern class

\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})
, which is a symmetric
\mathbb{Q}
-multilinear map:

\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})\colon N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times(n-2)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\quad(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-2})\longmapsto\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}.

On the other hand, as
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial in codimension
2
, for any two
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor classes
\beta
and
\gamma
on
X
, the product
\beta\cdot\gamma
is also a well-defined symmetric
\mathbb{Q}
-multilinear map:

\beta\cdot\gamma\colon N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times(n-2)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\quad(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-2})\longmapsto\beta\cdot\gamma\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}.

Now the
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov discriminant

\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})
of a reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of rank
r
is defined to be

\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq 2r\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})-(r-1)c_{1}(\mathcal{E})^{2},

which is viewed as a symmetric
\mathbb{Q}
-multilinear map
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times(n-2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{n-1}
be a collection of nef
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors.
According to
[
15
]
*Lemma 6.5, for any semistable reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
(D_{1},\dots,D_{n-1})
, the following
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality
holds:

(2.1)

\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}\geq 0.

The following inequality generalizes the
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality to the non-semistable cases, and is commonly referred to as the
\mathbb{Q}
-variant of Langer’s inequality (see, for example,
[
13
]
*§ 3.1 for details).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety with canonical singularities of dimension
n\geq 2
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a reflexive sheaf of rank
r
on
X
. Let
(D_{1},\dots,D_{n-1})
be a collection of nef
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors. Then we have

(D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}\cdot D_{n-1}^{2})(\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2})+r^{2}(\mu_{\max}-\mu)(\mu-\mu_{\min})\geq 0,

where
\mu
(resp.,
\mu_{\max}
and
\mu_{\min}
) is the slope (resp., maximal slope and minimal slope) of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
(D_{1},\dots,D_{n-1})
.

Definition 2.4
.

A

on a normal variety
X
is a non-zero coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{X}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free, and

(2)

\mathcal{F}
is closed under the Lie bracket.

The

of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is any Weil divisor
K_{\mathcal{F}}
on

such that
\det(\mathcal{F})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{\mathcal{F}})
.

Let

be the largest open subset over which
\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{F}
is locally free. A

\mathcal{F}
is a maximal connected and immersed holomorphic submanifold

such that
\mathcal{T}_{L}=\mathcal{F}|_{L}
. A leaf is called
algebraic
if it is open in its Zariski closure, and a foliation
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
algebraically integrable
if its leaves are algebraic.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an algebraically integrable foliation on a normal projective variety
X
. Then there exists a diagram as follows (

3.
Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality

As shown in
[
11
,
19
,
13
]
, the key step in establishing the Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality is to study the foliation induced by the maximal destabilizing subsheaf
\mathcal{E}
of the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{X}
. For
\rank(\mathcal{E})\geq 2
, the desired inequality follows directly from Langer’s inequality, regardless of the Picard number; for
\rank(\mathcal{E})=1
, the key result is
[
19
]
*Proposition 3.8 or
[
13
]
*Proposition 3.6, where the assumption
\rho(X)=1
is essential. In this section, we generalize
[
19
]
*Proposition 3.8 and
[
13
]
*Proposition 3.6 to weak Fano varieties of arbitrary Picard number.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a weak Fano variety of dimension
n\geq 2
. Let
A
be a Weil divisor on
X
such that
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA
for some rational number
q>2
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a rank one algebraically integrable foliation with rationally connected general leaves on
X
.

(1)

If
X
has canonical singularities, then
-K_{X}+2K_{\mathcal{L}}
is pseudoeffective.

(2)

If
X
has terminal singularities, then
-r_{X}K_{X}+(2r_{X}+1)K_{\mathcal{L}}
is pseudoeffective, where
r_{X}
is the Gorenstein index.

(3)

If
X
has
\epsilon
-lc singularities, then
-K_{X}+(1+\epsilon)K_{\mathcal{L}}
is pseudoeffective, where
0<\epsilon\leq 1
is a real number.

Proof.

The rank one algebraically integrable foliation
\mathcal{L}
induces a family of leaves

(3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces e}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where the general fiber

of

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We assume that
X
has only canonical singularities. Then there exists an effective

-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
on
U
such that

K_{U}-e^{*}K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\Delta.

Then it follows from
K_{U}\cdot F=\deg K_{F}=-2
that

-e^{*}K_{X}\cdot F=\Delta\cdot F+2\geq 2.

We claim that there exists an
f
-horizontal

-exceptional prime divisor on

. Otherwise, the
e
-exceptional divisor

would be
f

as

. This contracts our assumption.

Let

be an
f
-horizontal
e
-exceptional prime divisor on

. Then
E
is Cartier in a neighborhood of

, so
0<a\coloneq 1/E\cdot F\leq 1
. Now we have

K_{U}+aE-\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\Delta+aE+\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X},

where
(\Delta+aE+\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X})\cdot F=\Delta\cdot F+1-\frac{1}{2}(\Delta\cdot F+2)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\cdot F\geq 0
. That is,
(\Delta+aE+\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X})|_{F}
is effective on
F\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let
H
be an ample divisor on
U
and
\varepsilon>0
be a rational number. Then
-\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X}+\varepsilon H
is ample as
-e^{*}K_{X}
is nef and big. Note that
0<a\leq 1
. So we can choose an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
B

U
such that
B\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X}+\varepsilon H
and
(F,aE|_{F}+B|_{F})
is log canonical. Moreover, we have

K_{F}+aE|_{F}+B|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(K_{U}+aE-\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X}+\varepsilon H)|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Delta+aE+\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X}+\varepsilon H)|_{F},

which implies that
\kappa(F,K_{F}+aE|_{F}+B|_{F})\geq 0
.

Let
\pi\colon V\to U
be a resolution isomorphic outside
\Sing(U)
and
K_{V}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\pi^{*}K_{U}+\Delta_{V}
. Let
g\colon V\to T
be the induced morphism such that
g=f\circ\pi
. As
U
is smooth over the generic point of
T
, the
\pi
-exceptional divisor is
g
-vertical and
\pi
is isomorphic over the generic point of
T
. So after replacing
U
by
V
if necessary without effecting the results on the general fiber
F
, we may assume that
U
is smooth; in particular,
U
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.

Then we can apply Proposition

to

and obtain that
K_{\mathcal{F}}+aE+B
is pseudoeffective, where
\mathcal{F}
is the foliation induced by

. As this holds for arbitrary

, we obtain that

is pseudoeffective by taking limit. Hence

K_{\mathcal{L}}-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}=e_{*}(K_{\mathcal{F}}+aE-\frac{1}{2}e^{*}K_{X})

is pseudoeffective.

Finally, we deal with the terminal and
\epsilon

\mult_{E}(r_{X}\Delta)\geq 1
, and hence

is pseudoeffective.
In the latter case, as
X
has
\epsilon
-lc singularities, we have

K_{U}-e^{*}K_{X}+\Delta_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\Delta_{2},

where
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
are effective

-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}

are

. Then the same argument implies that

is pseudoeffective.

Remark 3.2
.

The assumption that
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA
for some
q>2
is only used in the proof to ensure the existence of an
f
-horizontal
e
-exceptional prime divisor. Therefore, the proposition also holds for
q\leq 2
provided that such a divisor exists. Moreover, the more
f
-horizontal
e
-exceptional divisors there are, the smaller
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-1}/c_{1}(X)^{n}
becomes.

Remark 3.3
.

On the other hand, if there are no
f
-horizontal
e
-exceptional prime divisors, then the rank one foliation
\mathcal{L}
gives rise to an almost holomorphic map
X\dasharrow T
whose general fiber
F
is
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In particular, we have
\rho(X)\geq 2
and
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\cdot F=qA\cdot F=-K_{X}\cdot F=2
.

The following observation for the case where
X
is smooth and
q=2
is due to Jie Liu. We reproduce his proof.

Proposition 3.4

(Jie Liu)
.

Let
X
be a Fano manifold such that
-K_{X}\sim 2A
for some ample divisor
A
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a rank one algebraically integrable foliation with rational connected leaves on
X
such that
A\cdot F=1
for a general leaf
F
of
\mathcal{L}
. Then

2c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-1}\leq c_{1}(X)^{n}.

Proof.

We use the same notation as in (
3.1
). Let
B\coloneq e^{*}A
, then
B
is a Cartier divisor such that
B\cdot F=1
. Moreover,
B
is
f
-ample as
A
is ample and
e
is finite on the fibers of
f
. It follows that any fiber of
f
is reduced and irreducible. Therefore,
f
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle by
[
16
]
*II, Theorem 2.8. That is,
\mathcal{E}\coloneq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U}(B)
is a rank two locally free sheaf on
T
and
U\cong\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
. By taking a resolution of
T
and the base change, we can further assume that
T
is smooth.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation induced by
f
. Then there exists an
e
-exceptional divisor
\Delta_{f}
such that
c_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\Delta_{f}=-K_{\mathcal{F}}+\Delta_{f}=-e^{*}K_{\mathcal{L}}=e^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
(see
[
6
]
*§ 3.6 for example). By the projection formula, we have

c_{1}(\mathcal{F})\cdot B^{n-1}=(c_{1}(\mathcal{F})+\Delta_{f})\cdot B^{n-1}=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\cdot A^{n-1}.

On the other hand, we have

c_{1}(\mathcal{F})=-K_{\mathcal{F}}=-K_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})/T}=2B-f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{E})=e^{*}c_{1}(X)-f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{E}),

which implies that

c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\cdot A^{n-1}=c_{1}(\mathcal{F})\cdot B^{n-1}=(e^{*}c_{1}(X)-f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{E}))\cdot B^{n-1}=c_{1}(X)\cdot A^{n-1}-f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot B^{n-1}.

Note that
B^{n}-f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot B^{n-1}+f^{*}c_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cdot B^{n-2}=0
. As
B
is nef, the locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is nef and big. Hence we have
f^{*}c_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cdot B^{n-2}\geq 0
(cf.
[
18
]
*Theorem 8.2.1). It follows that

\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(X)\cdot A^{n-1}=A^{n}=B^{n}\leq f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot B^{n-1}=c_{1}(X)\cdot A^{n-1}-c_{1}(\mathcal{L})\cdot A^{n-1}.

Then the desired inequality follows.
∎

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The argument follows exactly as in
[
13
]
*Theorem 1.2 (or
[
19
]
*Theorem 1.1) after replacing
[
13
]
*Proposition 3.6 (or
[
19
]
*Proposition 3.8) with Proposition
3.1
. We include the detailed proof below for the reader’s convenience.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is semistable with respect to the collection of nef divisors
(D_{1},\dots,D_{n-2},c_{1}(X))
, then by the
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality (
2.1
), we have

c_{1}(X)^{2}\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}\leq\frac{2n}{n-1}\,\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}\leq 4\,\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}.

If
n\geq 3
, then the second inequality above is strict; if
n=2
and additionally
X
is Fano, then by
[
8
]
*Theorem 1.2, the first inequality above is strict and hence we have

c_{1}(X)^{2}\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}<4\,\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}.

So we may assume that
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is not semistable with respect to
(D_{1},\dots,D_{n-2},c_{1}(X))
. Applying Theorem
2.3
to
\mathcal{T}_{X}
and
(D_{1},\dots,D_{n-2},c_{1}(X))
yields

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{T}_{X})\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}+n\left(\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})-\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}\left(\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})-\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})\right)\cdot\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{T}_{X})}{n\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})}.

It follows from
[
19
]
*Proposition 3.6 that
\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{T}_{X})\geq 0
, and it is strict if
X
is Fano and the
D_{i}
’s are ample. As
\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})>\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})
, the above inequalities imply that

(3.3)

\left(2\hat{c}_{2}(X)-c_{1}(X)^{2}\right)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}+\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})\geq 0,

and it is strict if
X
is Fano and the
D_{i}
’s are ample. Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the maximal destabilizing subsheaf of
\mathcal{T}_{X}
. Then
[
19
]
*Proposition 3.6 implies that

(3.4)

c_{1}(\mathcal{F})\cdot c_{1}(X)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}\leq c_{1}(X)^{2}\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}.

If
\rank(\mathcal{F})\geq 2
, then

\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})=\mu(\mathcal{F})=\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{F})\cdot c_{1}(X)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}}{\rank(\mathcal{F})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,c_{1}(X)^{2}\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}

and the desired results follow immediately from (
3.3
). If
\rank(\mathcal{F})=1
, then as
\mathcal{F}
is the maximal destabilizing subsheaf of
\mathcal{T}_{X}
, we have
\mu(\mathcal{F})>\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})>0
and
\mu(\mathcal{F})>\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{F})
. Then by
[
24
]
*Proposition 2.2, which extends
[
2
]
*Theorem 1.1 to singular varieties,
\mathcal{F}
is a rank one algebraically integrable foliation with rationally connected general leaves. Hence
by Proposition
3.1
,

\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})=\mu(\mathcal{F})=c_{1}(\mathcal{F})\cdot c_{1}(X)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,c_{1}(X)^{2}\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2},

so the desired results follow from (
3.3
) again.
∎

Remark 3.5

A direct adaptation of the proof of Theorem

via Proposition

\epsilon

be a

\epsilon

Remark 3.6
.

By (

) in the proof, we see that

always holds, regardless of the Picard number. So (

) implies that for any nef divisors

,

(3.5)

\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot D_{1}\cdots D_{n-2}\geq 0

and it is strict if

is Fano and the

’s are ample. This inequality holds for klt singularities without restrictions on the

4.
Fano indices of Gorenstein canonical Fano threefolds

In this section, we apply the Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality to study the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano indices of Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-folds. Note that the problem cannot be reduced to the Picard number one case, as the Gorenstein property is not necessarily preserved when running the minimal model program.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold. Let
A
be an ample Weil divisor such that
-K_{X}\sim qA
for some positive integer
q
. Let
J
be the smallest positive integer such that
JA
is Cartier in codimension
2
. Let
J=p_{1}^{a_{1}}p_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots p_{k}^{a_{k}}
be the prime factorization, where
p_{i}
are distinct prime numbers. If
q\geq 3
, then

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(p_{i}^{a_{i}}-\frac{1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}\right)\leq 24-\frac{c_{1}(X)^{3}}{4}.

Proof.

We use the notation
(e_{C},g_{C},j_{C})
in
[
13
]
*Definition 4.4. Then by
[
13
]
*Theorem 4.6, we have

(4.2)

\sum_{C\subset\Sing(X)}\left(e_{C}-\frac{1}{g_{C}}\right)(-K_{X}\cdot C)=c_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)-\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X).

As
X
is Gorenstein, we have
c_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)=24
by the Riemann–Roch formula and
-K_{X}\cdot C\geq 1
for any
C\subset\Sing(X)
. Combining with
[
13
]
*Lemma 4.5, we have

(4.3)

\sum_{C\subset\Sing(X)}\left(j_{C}-\frac{1}{j_{C}}\right)\leq\sum_{C\subset\Sing(X)}\left(e_{C}-\frac{1}{g_{C}}\right)(-K_{X}\cdot C).

As
J\mid{\text{l.c.m.}}\{j_{C}\mid C\subset\Sing(X)\}
by the definitions of
J
and
j_{C}
, each
p_{i}^{a_{i}}
divides at least one
j_{C}
for some
C\subset\Sing(X)
. Hence by
[
3
]
*Page 65, (2.2),

(4.4)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(p_{i}^{a_{i}}-\frac{1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}\right)\leq\sum_{C\subset\Sing(X)}\left(j_{C}-\frac{1}{j_{C}}\right).

On the other hand, let
f\colon Y\to X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorialization such that
K_{Y}=f^{*}K_{X}
. In particular,
Y
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Gorenstein canonical weak Fano
3
-fold such that
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)=q_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)\geq q\geq 3
. As
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial in codimension 2, we have
f_{*}\hat{c}_{2}(Y)=\hat{c}_{2}(X)
by pushing forward the one cycle, and hence
\hat{c}_{2}(Y)\cdot c_{1}(Y)=\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)
by the projection formula. Then Corollary
1.2
implies that

(4.5)

c_{1}(X)^{3}=c_{1}(Y)^{3}\leq 4\hat{c}_{2}(Y)\cdot c_{1}(Y)=4\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X).

The conclusion follows by combining (
4.2
)–(
4.5
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface with du Val singularities and
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(S)\geq 3
. Then as above, Corollary
1.2
and
[
13
]
*(4.5), (4.6) yield that

(4.6)

\sum_{p\in\Sing(S)}(j_{p}-\frac{1}{j_{p}})\leq\sum_{p\in\Sing(S)}(e_{p}-\frac{1}{g_{p}})\leq c_{2}(S)-\frac{c_{1}(S)^{2}}{4}=12-\frac{5}{4}c_{1}(S)^{2},

where
e_{p},g_{p},j_{p}
are defined as in
[
13
]
*Definition 4.4 and the last equality is Noether’s formula. These strong numerical constraints provide a new approach to the classification of del Pezzo surfaces with du Val singularities. In particular, it yields a quick proof of
q_{\mathbb{Q}}(S)\leq 6
that does not rely on the classification in
[
23
]
, thereby offering an alternative to
[
27
]
*Proposition 3.3.

Indeed, after taking quasi-étale cover if necessary, we can assume that
-K_{S}\sim qA
for some ample Weil divisor
A
and some integer
q\geq q_{\mathbb{Q}}(S)
. By
[
13
]
*Theorem 4.2, we have
J\mid q
and
q^{2}\mid Jc_{1}(S)^{2}
. So
q\leq c_{1}(S)^{2}\leq 48/5<10
by (
4.6
). Moreover, if
q>6
, then
6<c_{1}(S)^{2}=J=q\leq 9
. But
J\mid{\text{l.c.m.}}\{j_{p}\}
, which contradicts (
4.6
) by a straightforward computation.

Remark 4.3
.

It follows from Remark
3.6
that
\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)>0
for any
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold. Combining with (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we have

\sum_{C\subset\Sing(X)}(j_{C}-\frac{1}{j_{C}})<24.

This inequality plays a role in the classification of Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-folds analogous to that of the inequality
\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}_{X}}(r-\frac{1}{r})<24
in the terminal case. In particular, it allows us to enumerate all but finitely many candidates for the basket
\{j_{C}\}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold such that
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA
for some integer
q
. Then there exists a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold
Y
such that
\Cl(Y)
is torsion free and
-K_{Y}\sim qA_{Y}
for some Weil divisor
A_{Y}
on
Y
.

Proof.

Taking quasi-étale covers induced by torsion elements in
\Cl(X)
repeatedly, we will obtain the desired
Y
, which is still a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold by
[
17
]
*Proposition 5.20. See the proof of
[
13
]
*Lemma 5.1 for more details.
∎

Theorem
4.1
imposes strong constraints on the numerical invariants of Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-folds. The following result specifically restricts the possible Fano indices.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold. Let
A
be an ample Weil divisor such that
-K_{X}\sim qA
for some positive integer
q
. If
q>22
, then

q\in\{24,26,28,30,36,40,42\}.

Proof.

Let
J
be the smallest positive integer such that
JA
is Cartier in codimension 2. Then
q^{2}\mid Jc_{1}(X)^{3}
and
J\mid q
by Theorem
2.2
. In particular,
c_{1}(X)^{3}=q(\frac{q}{J})k
for some positive integer
k
. By Theorem
2.1
, we assume that
c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq 70
.

If
q\geq 36
, then we must have
k=1
and
J=q
. Hence
c_{1}(X)^{3}=J=q\leq 66
is an even number by Theorem
2.1
and
[
12
]
*Theorem 1.1. Then it follows from Theorem
4.1
and a straightforward computation that
q\in\{36,40,42\}
.

If
22<q\leq 35
and
q
is odd, then
k=2
,
q=J
and
c_{1}(X)^{3}=q(\frac{q}{J})k=2q
as
c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq 70
is even. Such
q
is excluded by Theorem
4.1
.

For
q=34
and
32
, we have
c_{1}(X)^{3}=q=J
,
c_{1}(X)^{3}=2q=2J
, or
c_{1}(X)^{3}=2q=4J
. All of these possibilities are excluded by Theorem
4.1
as well.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The cases
q=19
and
17
can be ruled out by repeating the previous computations. Alternatively, some of the cases can also be excluded using the Riemann–Roch formula for the Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-folds
[

]
*Theorem 4.9, which states that for any integer
0<s<q
,

(4.7)

\frac{s^{2}}{2q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3}+\sum_{C\subset\Sing(X)}(-K_{X}\cdot C)c_{C}(sA)=h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(sA))-2\in\mathbb{Z}.

With Theorem
4.1
and (
4.7
) at hand, we can rule out many cases by following the same line of argument as in
[
12
]
*§ 7. For instance, in the case
q=40
, we have
c_{1}(X)^{3}=q=J
, and hence Theorem
4.1
implies that the only singular curves are
C_{1}
of type
A_{4}
and
C_{2}
of type
A_{7}
, both satisfying
-K_{X}\cdot C_{i}=1
. Substituting into (
4.7
) yields that for any
0<s<40
,

\frac{s^{2}}{80}-\frac{\overline{sx}(5-\overline{sx})}{10}-\frac{\overline{sy}(8-\overline{sy})}{16}\in\mathbb{Z}

for some integers
0\leq x<5,0\leq y<8
. However, this fails for

. A similar argument applies to
q=36,28,26
.

Possible Explanation of F 2 n / F 2 p F_{2}^{n}/F_{2}^{p} at Large x x Using Quantum Statistical Mechanics
C. Bourrely, F. Buccella, W. C. Chang, D. Di Bari, P. H. Frampton, J. C. Peng

Abstract
The recent accurate measurements of the scattering of electrons off of the mirror nuclei 3 H and 3 He show with small errors that the neutron to proton ratio r ⁡ ( x ) = F 2 n ​ ( x ) / F 2 p ​ ( x ) r(x)=F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x) , approaches a value larger than 1/4 for x → 1 x\to 1 . We suggest a possible explanation for this experimental result by studying the consequences of the Pauli exclusion principle for the parton distribution when the ratio is described by quantum statistical mechanics description in terms of three parameters inspired by the quantum statistical approach.

I
Introduction

Deep Inelastic Scattering (DIS) of electrons from nucleons, and the
subsequent extraction
of the structure functions, led to the discovery of the
quarks and antiquarks substructure of the nucleons. It also
provided a solid basis for establishing
Quantum ChromoDynamics (QCD) as the correct theory for the
strong interaction
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

The differential cross sections for DIS are expressed in terms of the nucleon
structure functions,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
:

\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d\Omega dE^{{}^{\prime}}}=\sigma_{M}\left[\frac{F_{2}(\nu,Q^{2})}{\nu}+2\frac{F_{1}(\nu,Q^{2})}{M}\tan^{2}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right],

(1)

in which
\sigma_{M}
is the Mott cross section,

E^{{}^{\prime}}
and
\theta
are the energy and polar angle of the scattered electron,

\nu=E-E^{{}^{\prime}}
where
E
is the incident electron energy,

Q^{2}=4EE^{{}^{\prime}}\sin^{2}\theta/2
is the negative of the momentum transfer
squared, and
M
is the nucleon mass.

In the quark-parton model, DIS is represented as
scattering of electrons from point-like constituents, carrying the
momentum fraction,
x
, of the nucleon. In the infinite
momentum scaling limit when
\nu\rightarrow\infty,Q^{2}\rightarrow\infty
,

0\leq(x\equiv Q^{2}/2M\nu)\leq 1
, the structure function
F_{2}
becomes

F_{2}(x)=x\Sigma_{i}e_{i}^{2}f_{i}(x),

(2)

where
f_{i}(x)dx
is the probability that a parton of type
i
carries
momentum in the range between
x
and
x+dx
, and the sum in Eq. (
2
)
runs over all parton types.

We consider the up (
u
), down (
d
), and strange (
s
) quarks in the
proton and define

U(x)\equiv u(x)+\bar{u}(x)
,
D(x)\equiv d(x)+\bar{d}(x)
and

S(x)\equiv s(x)+\bar{s}(x)
. With these notations, together with the
assumption of isospin symmetry for the nucleon PDFs, we have

F_{2}^{p}(x)=x\left[\frac{4}{9}U(x)+\frac{1}{9}D(x)+\frac{1}{9}S(x)\right]

(3)

and

F_{2}^{n}(x)=x\left[\frac{1}{9}U(x)+\frac{4}{9}D(x)+\frac{1}{9}S(x)\right]

(4)

Because all parton distribution functions are non-negative we deduce from
Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
) that the
F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)

ratio,
r(x)
, is bounded at all
x
by

1/4\leq\left(r(x)\equiv\frac{F_{2}^{n}(x)}{F_{2}^{p}(x)}\right)\leq 4

(5)

which is known as the Nachtmann inequality
[
10
]
.

The earliest DIS experiments at SLAC
[
11
]

confirmed the Nachtmann
inequality and, more specifically, discovered that, for
x\rightarrow 0
,

r
is approximately unity, whereas for
x\rightarrow 1
, with
much larger errors, it was thought that
r
might asymptotically
approach
1/4
.
However, the uncertainties of the nuclear effects for the deuteron nucleus
have raised some concerns on the validity of the extraction of
r(x)
at large

x

[
12
,
13
]
.
New measurements which would minimize the
nuclear effects were proposed for a more reliable determination of
r(x)

at large
x

[
14
]
.

Recently, the MARATHON experiment
[
15
]
, performed by
the Jefferson Laboratory Hall A Tritium Collaboration,
has measured the DIS of electrons from the
mirror nuclei
3
H and
3
He in order to cleanly separate the proton and
neutron structure functions.
The measurement of
r(x)
covers a broad range of
x
, from
x=0.19

up to
x=0.83
. As shown in Fig. 1, the

F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)
from the MARATHON experiment exhibits an
intriguing
x
dependence. At intermediate
x
region (
0.19<x<0.5
),

F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)
falls roughly linearly with
x
, while for
the large
x
region,
F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)
falls off more slowly with
x
,
approaching a constant value of
\sim 0.45
at the highest values of
x
.

The MARATHON results on
F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)
in a broad

In this paper, we show that the MARATHON data on

of hadrons.

II
The Quantum Statistical Description of Parton Distributions

We first briefly discuss the salient features of the quantum
statistical approach in depicting the dynamics of partons
inside the nucleons.
The rule of the Pauli Exclusion
Principle
[
27
,
28
]
implies quantum statistical
parton distributions, namely Fermi-Dirac type for the quarks and
antiquarks and Bose-Einstein type
for the gluons. In 2002, Bourrely, Buccella and Soffer
[
29
]

proposed the following distributions at the initial scale,

Q_{0}=2
GeV/c for the quarks and antiquarks,

q(x)
and
\bar{q}(x)
, and the gluons,
G(x)
,
on the basis of quantum statistical mechanics:

\displaystyle xq^{h}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle AX_{q}^{h}x^{b}\left[\exp\frac{(x-X_{q}^{h})}{\bar{x}}+1\right]^{-1}

(6)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\tilde{A}x^{\tilde{b}}\left[\exp\frac{x}{\bar{x}}+1\right]^{-1}

and

\displaystyle x\bar{q}^{h}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{A}x^{\bar{b}}\left[X_{q}^{-h}(\exp\frac{(x+X_{q}^{-h})}{\bar{x}}+1)\right]^{-1}

(7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\tilde{A}x^{\tilde{b}}\left[\exp\frac{x}{\bar{x}}+1\right]^{-1}

and

xG(x)=A_{G}x^{b_{G}}\left[\exp\frac{(x-X_{G})}{\bar{x}}-1\right]^{-1}.

(8)

In these distributions,
\bar{x}
plays the rôle of temperature.

X_{q}^{h}
are chemical potentials depending on the flavour (
q
=
u
or
d
)
and the helicity (
h
). The factors
X_{q}^{h}
in Eq. (
6
)
and
(X_{q}^{-h})^{-1}

in Eq. (
7
) were introduced in
[
29
]

to comply with the data and have been accounted for by
considering the transverse degrees of freedom
[
30
]
.
The normalization factors
A,\tilde{A},\bar{A}
and the exponents

b,\tilde{b},\bar{b}
are determined by fitting the data, together with the
constraints of the quark number and the momentum sum rules.
For the strange partons, it was
assumed that
s(x)=\bar{s}(x)=(\bar{u}(x)+\bar{d}(x))/4
.

The Fermi-Dirac form for the quark and antiquark distributions are very
different from the
Ax^{B}(1-x)^{C}
form for standard parametrizations. This
difference would lead naturally to different behaviors of
F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)

between the statistical model and the conventional parametrization.

The equilibrium conditions with respect to the processes which lead to the
DGLAP equations
[
31
,
32
]
imply that

X_{q}^{h}+X_{\bar{q}}^{-h}=0

(9)

and

X_{G}=0.

(10)

Equation (
9
) provides a natural connection between the valence and sea
quark parton distributions, since the chemical potentials of the quark
q

and antiquark
\bar{q}
are related. It also implies that the helicities
of the quark and antiquark are correlated. These intriguing correlations
between quark and antiquark distributions, and between their positive and
negative helicity distributions,
are unique features of the quantum statistical approach and they are
absent in the usual standard parametrizations of nucleon PDFs in the
conventional global fits.

The values of
\bar{x}
and
X^{h}_{q}
, found in the first
paper
[
29
]
in 2002, are respectively:

\displaystyle\bar{x}=0.099;~X_{u}^{\uparrow}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.461;~X_{d}^{\downarrow}=0.302;

\displaystyle X_{u}^{\downarrow}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.298;~X_{d}^{\uparrow}=0.228.

(11)

Following the cited paper
[
29
]
, supporting confirmations were
forthcoming for the quantum statistical parton distributions proposed
therein. The temperature
\bar{x}
and the quark chemical potentials were obtained,
for example, in the 2015 global fit (BS15) in the quantum statistical
approach
[
33
]
with
Q_{0}=1
GeV, as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{x}=0.090;~X_{u}^{\uparrow}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.475;~X_{d}^{\downarrow}=0.309;

\displaystyle X_{u}^{\downarrow}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.307;~X_{d}^{\uparrow}=0.245.

(12)

The values of the parameters do not need to be equal,
since the DGLAP equations
[
31
,
32
]
imply that the parton distributions depend on x.
One observes the following relations for the chemical
potentials of the valence quarks:

X_{u}^{\uparrow}>X_{d}^{\downarrow}\approx X_{u}^{\downarrow}>X_{d}^{\uparrow}.

(13)

Eqs. (
9
) and (
13
) imply the following
relations for the chemical potentials of the antiquarks:

X_{\bar{u}}^{\downarrow}<X_{\bar{d}}^{\uparrow}\approx X_{\bar{u}}^{\uparrow}<X_{\bar{d}}^{\downarrow}.

(14)

Eq. (
14
) leads to the following
striking predictions of the quantum statistical approach for
the flavor and spin structure of the
antiquarks in the proton:

\bar{d}(x)>\bar{u}(x)

(15)

and

\Delta\bar{u}(x)>0>\Delta\bar{d}(x)

(16)

and finally

\Delta\bar{u}(x)-\Delta\bar{d}(x)\approx\bar{d}(x)-\bar{u}(x).

(17)

The first inequality, Eq. (

[
34

35

36

and the SeaQuest experiment
[
37

The second inequality, Eq. (

using polarized beams
[
39

The test of the last relation, Eq. (

more precise determination of the quantity on the left-hand side.

The gluon parton distribution proposed by ATLAS has been described by the

III
The Quantum Statistical Description of
F_{2}^{n}(x)/F_{2}^{p}(x)

We now turn to the implications of the quantum statistical approach for
understanding the behavior of the MARATHON
F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)

data. As shown in Fig.
1
, the ratio
F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)

decreases with a positive curvature, approaching a constant value
of
\sim 0.45
which is significantly greater than the value of
1/4

expected for
d(x)/u(x)=0
.

The conventional parametrization for the proton quark distributions
such as the CJ15LO
[
16
]
PDFs,
often involves a form
(1-x)^{C_{q}}
for quark
q

as
x\to 1
. The ratio
d(x)/u(x)
becomes proportional to

(1-x)^{C_{d}-C_{u}}
which goes to zero, since
C_{d}>C_{u}
as one
expects more
u(x)
than
d(x)
. As
x\to 1
, this conventional
parametrization naturally leads to
d(x)/u(x)=0
and

F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)\to 1/4
. Modifications to the parametrization
of
(1-x)^{C_{q}}
have been introduced to allow more
flexibility
[
16
]
. As shown in Fig.
1
, the MARATHON data
would provide further constraints for determining the functional form for

q(x)
in future global fits. In contrast to the conventional approach
allowing for flexible function forms for
q(x)
, the statistical
model requires a Fermi-Dirac form for the quark distribution which dictates
the shape of
q(x)
at large
x
. As discussed next,
the Fermi-Dirac form for the quark distributions in the quantum statistical
approach would lead to a very different behavior for

d(x)/u(x)
as
x\to 1
.

We note that at large
x
the valence quarks dominate
and therefore we may write :

\frac{F^{n}_{2}(x)}{F^{p}_{2}(x)}=\frac{4d(x)+u(x)}{4u(x)+d(x)}

(18)

The sum of two Fermi-Dirac functions is well approximated by
a single Fermi-Dirac function with a potential intermediate,
which allows us to write :

xq(x)=\frac{A^{\prime}_{1}X^{\prime}_{q}x^{b}}{\exp{\frac{x-X^{\prime}_{q}}{\bar{x}}}+1}

(19)

with

X^{\prime}_{u}=X_{u}^{\downarrow}+\bar{x}\ln{\frac{[X_{u}^{\uparrow}\exp{\frac{X_{u}^{\uparrow}-X_{u}^{\downarrow}}{\bar{x}}+X_{u}^{\downarrow}}]}{[X_{u}^{\uparrow}+X_{u}^{\downarrow}]}}

(20)

X^{\prime}_{d}=X_{d}^{\uparrow}+\bar{x}\ln{\frac{[X_{d}^{\downarrow}\exp{\frac{X_{d}^{\downarrow}-X_{d}^{\uparrow}}{\bar{x}}+X_{d}^{\uparrow}}]}{[X_{d}^{\uparrow}+X_{d}^{\downarrow}]}}

(21)

Taking into account Eq. (
20
), for example, for
X_{u}^{\downarrow}=X_{u}^{\uparrow}
or 0
it gives
X^{\prime}_{u}=X_{u}^{\uparrow}
; if
0<X_{u}^{\downarrow}<X_{u}^{\uparrow}
Eq. (
20
) gives

X^{\prime}_{u}
in the upper part of the range [
X_{u}^{\downarrow},X_{u}^{\uparrow}
]. The same
consideration applies to
X^{\prime}_{d}
by using Eq. (
21
).
Equation (
19
) implies

\frac{d(x)}{u(x)}=\frac{X^{\prime}_{d}}{X^{\prime}_{u}}\frac{\exp{\frac{x-X^{\prime}_{u}}{\bar{x}}}+1}{\exp{\frac{x-X^{\prime}_{d}}{\bar{x}}}+1}.

(22)

The MARATHON data are then fitted according
to Eq. (
18
) and Eq. (
22
).

IV
Conclusion

The ratio
F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)
, measured
with high precision by the MARATHON experiment, is well described by quantum
statistical approach, which predicts that the ratio approaches a
constant value greater than 1/4.
The fact that the ratio decreases faster in the lower region of
x

than in the higher region is also a property of the quantum
statistical approach.

Unlike conventional parametrizations for the nucleon PDFs, the
statistical approach has imposed specific forms for the parton distributions
based on the Fermi-Dirac nature of the quarks and the Bose-Einstein
nature of the gluons. Various predictions of the statistical approach
are found to be in excellent agreement with existing data.
Further stringent tests of the quantum statistical approach
could be performed by considering the

Q^{2}
dependence of the
F^{n}_{2}(x)/F^{p}_{2}(x)
ratios,
as well as the polarized quark distributions.

It could be, finally, stressed that the distributions of the valence
quarks at the large
x
region show a big difference with the standard

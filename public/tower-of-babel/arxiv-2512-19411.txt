Arithmetic Bohr radius and Local Banach space theory
Himadri Halder

Abstract
. This article introduces the notion of arithmetic Bohr radius for operator valued pluriharmonic functions on complete Reinhardt domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . Using tools from local Banach space theory, we determine its asymptotic behavior in both finite and infinite dimensions. The framework developed in this article extends the classical Minkowski-space setting to a much broader class of sequence spaces, such as mixed Minkowski, Lorentz, and Orlicz spaces. This generality allows for a unified and systematic investigation of Bohr’s theorem for both holomorphic and pluriharmonic functions.

Introduction and the main results

Over the past three decades, Bohr’s theorem and its applications have become a central topic in the function theory of several complex variables, exhibiting deep connections with fundamental aspects of both the local and global theory of Banach spaces. These rich connections have stimulated extensive research on Bohr’s theorem in various fields of mathematics. For instance, it has been investigated in the contexts of Banach algebras and uniform algebras (see

,

]
), complex manifolds (see

,

]
), ordinary and vector-valued Dirichlet series (see

,

]
), elliptic equations (see
[

]
), Faber–Green condensers (see
[

]
), free holomorphic functions (see
[

]
), vector-valued holomorphic functions (see
[

]
), local Banach space theory (see
[

]
), domains of monomial convergence (see
[

]
), harmonic and pluriharmonic mappings (see
[

]
), Hardy spaces (see
[

]
), as well as various multidimensional settings (see
[

,
14
,
15

24

). In multidimensional settings, the Bohr radius problem becomes significantly more complicated. Many questions concerning the precise behavior of multidimensional Bohr radii remain open, largely due to the lack of certain essential analytical and geometric tools. Several of these problems are closely connected to fundamental topics in functional analysis, such as the geometry of Banach spaces
[

[

An especially intriguing direction arises when one considers this problem for vector valued functions on complete Reinhardt domains.

Our goal of this article is to build a local Banach space theoretic framework for the study of Bohr’s theorem for vector valued holomorphic and pluriharmonic functions via the arithmetic Bohr radius. One of the core challenges in this study is to analyze the asymptotic behavior of both the Bohr radius and the arithmetic Bohr radius using tools and invariants from local Banach space theory. To proceed in more detail, we first recall the notion of the Bohr radius for vector valued holomorphic functions.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a given complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded liner operator between two complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, with
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
, denoted by
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for all holomorphic functions
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}:\Omega\rightarrow X
we have

(1.1)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{\alpha}\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X},

where
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}
.

Let us fix some notations. We write
K(\Omega,p,U):=K_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
. When
U=I:X\rightarrow X
is the identity operator, we set
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,I)
and
K(\Omega,p,X):=K_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
. In the scalar valued case, we denote
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
and
K(\Omega,p):=K_{1}(\Omega,p)
.
The above definition is motivated by Defant
et al.

[
31
]
, extending their result for the case
p=1
and
\Omega=\mathbb{D}^{n}
to a more general setting, thus making it possible to the study of Bohr phenomenon for broader classes of domains and functions.
With these notations, a classical theorem of Harald Bohr concerning power series states that
K(\mathbb{D},1)=1/3
(see
[
16
]
). One of the most striking features of Bohr’s theorem is that our understanding of the behavior of the constant
K(\Omega,1)
remains rather limited. A central challenge is to determine the precise value of
K(\Omega,1)
, which is still unknown for
n>1
, even in the classical setting of the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{D}^{n}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{j}|<1\,\mbox{for all}\,1\leq j\leq n\}
. To make progress in this direction, much of the existing research has focused on establishing bounds for
K(\Omega,1)
and investigating its asymptotic behavior on general domains, and in particular well known settings such as the unit ball of Minkowski space and finite-dimensional complex Banach spaces.
Denote
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}:=\left\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<1\right\}
,
1\leq q<\infty
and
B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}:=\mathbb{D}^{n}
. For every
1\leq q\leq\infty
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, together the results of Aizenberg, Boas, Dineen, Khavinson, Timoney, Defant, and Frerick from
[
2
,
14
,
15
,
29
]
show that there exist constants
C,D>0
such that

\frac{1}{C}\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}\leq K_{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})\leq D\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}.

It is worth mentioning that the constant
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
is not necessarily nonzero for arbitrary Banach spaces
X,Y
, and the operator
U
. We refer to the occurrence of strictly positive constant
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
as the Bohr phenomenon. More precisely, we make the following definition. For given Banach spaces
X,Y
and the operator
U
as above, a class of holomorphic functions
f
, defined in a complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, is said to exhibit the Bohr phenomenon on
\Omega
if there exists a universal constant
r=r_{0}\in(0,1]
, called the Bohr radius of
\Omega
with respect to this class, such that inequality (
1.1
) is satisfied by every function in the class. Since the Bohr phenomenon does not occur for all classes of holomorphic functions (see
[
3
,
6
,
11
,
13
]
), an important problem is to identify and characterize those classes for which the Bohr phenomenon holds. Blasco
[
13
]
initiated the systematic study of classes exhibiting the Bohr phenomenon by characterizing them in the classical one-dimensional setting
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
with
U
equal to the identity operator. Many thanks to Defant
et al.

[
31
]
, who were the first to address this problem in multidimensional case
\Omega=\mathbb{D}^{n}
,
n>1
, with
p=1
and for an arbitrary operator
U
. More recently, the problem for general bounded complete Reinhardt domains has been considered by the author in
[
36
]
, where a framework based on local Banach space theory was developed.

The constant
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,1)
was first introduced by Bombieri
[
17
]
, who determined its exact value for
\lambda\in[1,\sqrt{2}]
. Its precise asymptotic behavior as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
was later investigated by Bombieri and Bourgain
[
18
]
. Building upon these foundational results, Defant
et al.

[
31
]
have introduced the more general constant
K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1,U)
and obtained asymptotic estimates in both finite and infinite dimensional Banach spaces
X
. On the other hand, the constants
K(\mathbb{D},p)
and
K(\mathbb{D}^{n},p)
were first studied by Djakov and Ramanujan
[
33
]
, and subsequently developed by Bénéteau, Dahlner, Khavinson, Das
[
11
]
. From the viewpoint of Banach space valued functions, Blasco
[
13
]
has shown that
K(\mathbb{D},p,X)=0
for
p\in[1,2)
, whereas
K(\mathbb{D},p,X)>0
precisely when
X
is
p
-uniformly
\mathbb{C}
-convex for
2\geq p<\infty
.
These results show that
K(\mathbb{D},p,X)
is not necessarily nonzero for all Banach spaces
X
. This observation inspired a refinement of the definition to guarantee non-vanishing behavior for arbitrary
X
, leading to the introduction of the parameter
\lambda
in
[
31
, Definition 1.1]
, and hence in (
1.1
).

One of the main aims of this note is to study the Bohr radius through the lens of the arithmetic Bohr radius within the framework of local Banach space theory, by establishing a connection between the asymptotic behaviors of these two radii. Among the various notions of Bohr radius, the arithmetic Bohr radius is more than a mere curious variant of the classical Bohr radius. It possesses rich structural properties; in particular, it plays an important role in describing the domain of existence of monomial expansions of bounded holomorphic functions on complete Reinhardt domains (see
[
41
]
). With this objective in mind, we first recall the following notion of the arithmetic Bohr radius, which is associated with the inequality (
1.1
).

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a given complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
X
be any complex Banach space. Let
\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
be a set of
X
-valued holomorphic functions on
\Omega
. For any complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For each
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
, the
\lambda_{p}
-
arithmetic Bohr radius

A_{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X),p,U)
of
\Omega
with respect to
\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X)
is defined as

\sup\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\,|\,r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0},\,\mbox{for all}\,f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega,X):\sum_{\alpha}\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})r^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X}\right\},

where
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}=\{r=(r_{1},.\,.\,.\,,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{i}\geq 0,1\leq i\leq n\}.
Let
H^{\infty}(\Omega,X)
be the space of all bounded
X
-valued holomorphic functions on
\Omega
. We write
A_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for
A_{\lambda}(H^{\infty}(\Omega,X),p,U)
and
A(\Omega,p,U)
for
A_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
. When
U=I:X\rightarrow X
is the identity operator, we set
A_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=A_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,I)
and
A(\Omega,p,X):=A_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
. In the scalar valued case, we denote
A_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=A_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
and
A(\Omega,p):=A_{1}(\Omega,p)
.
The constant
A_{\lambda}(\Omega,1)
was first considered by Defant
et al.

[
26
]
and is usually known as the classical arithmetic Bohr radius. Recently, the authors in
[
7
,
8
]
have studied the constant
A_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and have investigated asymptotic estimates of it on the Minkowski spaces.

In this paper, we investigate asymptotic estimates of the arithmetic Bohr radius on arbitrary bounded complete Reinhardt domains using techniques from local Banach space theory. In a recent work
[
36
]
, the author introduced a notion of Bohr radius for operator valued pluriharmonic functions defined on such domains and analyzed its asymptotic behavior using tools from local Banach space theory. Motivated by these developments, it is natural to ask whether the arithmetic Bohr radius can also be investigated in the setting of operator valued pluriharmonic functions.

In order to address this problem, we begin by recalling the definitions of harmonic and pluriharmonic functions. A function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of class
C^{2}
is called plurharmonic if its restriction to every complex line is harmonic. When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a simply connected domain in containing the origin, such a function is pluriharmonic precisely when it can be decomposed as
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h,g
are holomorphic on
\Omega
with
g(0)=0
. This characterization admits a natural extension to the operator valued setting. In particular, if
\Omega
is a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain, then a continuous function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is pluriharmonic if and only it possesses a series representation of the form

(1.2)

f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha},

where the associated functions
h(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
and
g(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha}b_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
are
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
valued holomorphic functions on
\Omega
, and
a_{\alpha},b_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Here,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the Banach algebra of all bounded linear operators acting on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. For any
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
\left\lVert T\right\rVert
will always denote the operator norm of
T
, and
T^{*}
is the usual adjoint of
T
. Write
Re(T):=(T+T^{*})/2
. Moreover, if
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
, then
\overline{z}
denotes the componentwise complex conjugate
(\overline{z}_{1},\ldots,\overline{z}_{n})
. For such a domain
\Omega
as above, we denote by
\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,X)
the set of all bounded
X
-valued pluriharmonic functions on
\Omega
, where
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Boundedness is understood in the sense that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\,\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}<\infty
for
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,X)
.

Let
Y
be any complex Banach space. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
, denoted by
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
of the form (
1.2
) we have

(1.3)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}.

Set
R(\Omega,p,U):=R_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):=R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
R(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):=R_{1}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
,
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
R(\Omega,p):=R_{1}(\Omega,p)
. The Bohr phenomenon for harmonic functions in the scalar setting was first investigated in
[
1
]
. Subsequent developments extended this line of research to operator valued harmonic mappings (see
[
12
]
), and more recently to pluriharmonic functions taking values in Hilbert spaces (see
[
35
]
). The asymptotic behaviour of the constants
R(\Omega,1)
and
R(\Omega,p)
have been studied in
[
21
,
22
]
whenever
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
.

We now turn to the central theme of this article, namely the notion of the arithmetic Bohr radius for pluriharmonic functions. To the best of our knowledge, this concept has not been explored previously in the literature. This concept not been even studied in the simplest cases such as
U=I:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
p=1
,
\lambda=1
, and for the classical domains such as the unit balls of Minkowski spaces, nor for the scalar valued harmonic and pluriharmonic functions. This gap motivates us to pose the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain. Can we study the arithmetic Bohr radius for operator valued pluriharmonic functions on
\Omega
?

We answer affirmatively to this question in this paper by introducing this notion in the following way.
Let
\mathcal{FP}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
be a set of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
-valued pluriharmonic functions on
\Omega
. Let
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
.
For each
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
, the
\lambda_{p}
-
arithmetic Bohr radius

AP_{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),p,U)
of
\Omega
with respect to
\mathcal{FP}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
is defined as
\sup\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}:r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\right\}
such that

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda^{p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{FP}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
of the form (
1.2
).
Set
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U):=AP_{\lambda}(\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),p,U)
and
AP(\Omega,P,U):=AP_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
. When
U=I:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the identity operator, we set
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):=AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,I)
and
AP(\Omega,p,X):=AP_{1}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. In the scalar valued case, we denote
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
and
AP(\Omega,p):=AP_{1}(\Omega,p)
. Let
AP^{k}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U):=AP_{\lambda}(\mathcal{PH}(^{k}\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),p,U)
. In the scalar valued case, we denote
AP^{k}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=AP^{k}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
and
AP^{k}(\Omega,p):=A^{k}_{1}(\Omega,p)
.

Recently, the author in
[
36
]
has shown that the constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
is not necessarily nonzero for arbitrary operator
U
. Consequently, in the same paper, the author has established the condition when this constant is non-zero. Moreover, the author investigated asymptotic behaviors of this constant using invariants from local Banach space theory. With these developments in mind, it is natural to ask the following question for the constant
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
.

Question 1.2
.

For a given simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, can the the constant
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
be studied in detail? If so, does it always remain nonzero, and what can be said about its asymptotic behavior?

One of our main contributions to this paper is to affirmatively answer the aforesaid question for arbitrary bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain by establishing the relation between Bohr radius and arithmetic Bohr radius. The following theorem relates the Bohr radius constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and the arithmetic Bohr radius
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
on any bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain, which as a consequence shows the strictly positivity of
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. This theorem actually help to obtain the lower estimate of
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. A part of the proof follows a similar argument to that of
[
31
, Lemma 4.3]
; however, in that work the authors restricted attention only to scalar valued holomorphic functions.
Before stating our result, we introduce the following standard notation. For bounded Reinhardt domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}):=\inf\left\{s>0:\Omega_{1}\subset s\,\Omega_{2}\right\}
. Recall that if
Z
and
W
are Banach sequence spaces then
S(B_{Z},B_{W})=\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow W\right\rVert
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space such that
\chi(\{e_{k}\}^{n}_{k=1})=1
. Then

(1.4)

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}{n}\,R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U).

Moreover, if
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
, then
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq D.\frac{\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}{n}\,.\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}
, where

D=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\frac{1}{\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right\}\,&\text{for $\left\lVert U\right\rVert\geq\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}$},\\[8.53581pt]
\max\left\{\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}&\text{for $0<\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}$}.\end{cases}

Observing that
S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{1}
, and following arguments similar to those used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, it is straightforward to verify that the same conclusion holds for any simply connected complete Reinhardt domain. We therefore state the result and omit the details to avoid repetition.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then

AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\geq\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})}{n}\,R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U).

Moreover, if
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
, then
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\geq D.\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})}{n}\,.\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}
, where
D
as in Theorem
1.1
.

In particular, we obtain the following lower bound of the
\lambda
-powered arithmetic Bohr radius for operator-valued pluriharmonic functions whenever
U
is the identity operator on
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
1<\lambda
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X)\geq\frac{1}{2^{2+\frac{1}{p}}}\frac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda^{2}}\,\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})}{n}\,\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}.

Remark 1.1
.

In view of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.1
, it is evident that the constant
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
is non-zero whenever
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Moreover, a direct comparison of the definitions of arithmetic Bohr radius and Bohr radius shows that
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. As a consequence, the vanishing of
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
necessarily implies the vanishing of
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. However, the converse implication fails in general, reflecting a genuine distinction between these two notions. In the recent work, the author in
[
36
]
has shown that
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=0
when
\left\lVert U\right\rVert=\lambda
. This result naturally leads to the question of whether the arithmetic Bohr radius exhibits the same behavior, namely, whether
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
also vanishes when
\left\lVert U\right\rVert=\lambda
. The example presented below shows that this is not the case and the constant
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
may be strictly positive.
For a bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the functions

F_{k}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

F_{k}(z)=(i\,\cos\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}+(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}z_{1}+(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}\overline{z_{1}}

for
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
, where
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
U=\lambda\,I
, with
I
the identity on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
F_{k}(0)=(i\,\cos\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}
. Clearly,
F_{k}\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
and
\left\lVert F_{k}\right\rVert_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
. Let if possible, there exists
r_{0}>0
such that (
1.3
) holds for all
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
for all
z\in r_{0}\,\Omega
. Applying this to the functions
F_{k}
yields,

(1.5)

\lambda^{p}\,|\cos\frac{1}{k}|^{p}+\lambda^{p}\,|\sin\frac{1}{k}|^{p}\,|z_{1}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}

all
z\in r_{0}\,\Omega
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. However, this inequality fails for sufficiently large
k
, since
\cos(1/k)\rightarrow 1
and
\sin(1/k)\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, while
z

can be chosen in
r_{0}\,\Omega
such that
z_{1}\neq 0
. This fact shows that there is no
r_{0}>0
such that (
1.5
) holds, and hence
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=0
. On the other hand, it is easy to check that (
1.5
) holds for all the the points
(0,\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\Omega
such that
\xi_{j}\geq 0
,
j=1,\ldots,n
. Thus, by the definition,
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)>0
. Hence, we conclude that
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=0
while
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)>0
whenever
U=\lambda\,I
, thereby highlighting a fundamental difference between the arithmetic Bohr radius and the Bohr radius.

The following result links between the arithmetic Bohr radii of the spaces
\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
and
\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. More precisely, we estimate
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
in terms of
AP_{\lambda}\big(\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
Y
any complex Banach space, and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\lambda>1
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1)

\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,AP_{\lambda}\big(\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)\leq AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq AP_{\lambda}\big(\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)

(2)

\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,AP\big(\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)\leq AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq\lambda\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}AP\big(\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)
.

We now impose additional conditions on the initial coefficient, which lead to improved estimates for the arithmetic Bohr radius obtained in Theorem
1.2
. More precisely, we consider the following class of functions. Let
\mathcal{PH}^{*}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):=\{f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):f(0)=\mu\,I,\,\mu\in[0,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}]\}
. Denote
AP^{*}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U):=AP_{\lambda}(\mathcal{PH}^{*}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})),p,U)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
Y
any complex Banach space, and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\lambda>1
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1)

C\,AP_{\lambda}\big(\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)\leq AP^{*}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq AP_{\lambda}\big(\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)

(2)

D\,AP\big(\cup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)\leq AP^{*}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq\lambda\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}AP\big(\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X),p,U\big)
.
Here,

C=\max\left\{\frac{1}{(1+8^{p})^{1/p}},\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}

and

D=\max\left\{\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda^{p}+8^{p})^{1/p}},\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}.

We now turn to the second part of Question
1.2
, which is the central focus of this paper, namely the asymptotic estimate of the constant
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
through invariants from local Banach space theory, such as unconditional bases and projection constants.

In order to study this, we shall make use of the following correspondence. Recall that a Schauder basis
\{w_{k}\}
of a Banach space
W
is said to be
1
-unconditional if
\chi(\{w_{k}\})=1
(see more details in Section
3
). We usually denote the canonical basis vectors of
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
by
e_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
.
Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be an
n
-dimensional Banach space for which the canonical basis vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis (or equivalently, the open unit ball
B_{Z}
is a complete Reinhardt domain). There is a one-to-one correspondence between bounded convex complete Reinhardt domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the open unit balls of norms on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for which the canonical basis vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis. Indeed, the Minkowski functional
p_{\Omega}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
of
\Omega
defined by

p_{\Omega}(z):=\inf\left\{t>0:z/t\in\Omega\right\}

is a norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
\Omega
coincides with the open unit ball of
(\mathbb{C}^{n},p_{\Omega})
. Conversely, if
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
is a Banach space whose canonical basis is normalized
1
-unconditional, then its open unit ball
B_{Z}
is clearly a bounded convex complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Thus, the study of the constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for the unit balls
\Omega=B_{Z}
of finite dimensional complex Banach spaces
(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
with the normalized
1
-unconditional canonical bases, is equivalent to studying
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
over bounded complete Reinhardt domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Our investigation naturally separates into two cases according to whether
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is finite and infinite dimensional. In each case, taking into account the above correspondence, we estimate the asymptotic behavior of
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
in the following steps. First, we establish the result for
\Omega=B_{Z}
, that is, for a convex complete Reinhardt domain. Next, by combining this estimate with the above correspondence and Lemma
4.3
, we derive the asymptotic behavior of
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for every bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, not necessarily convex.

We start with the finite-dimensional case. The following theorem determines the exact asymptotic behavior of the arithmetic Bohr radius
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
when
U
is the identity operator on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space such that
\chi(\{e_{k}\}^{n}_{k=1})=1
. Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a finite dimensional complex Banach space and

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq\begin{cases}E_{1}(X)\,\max\left\{\frac{\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}{n^{3/2}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\,\frac{1}{e\,n}\right\}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $p=1$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{3}\frac{\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}{n}\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{-\frac{2}{p}}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,&\text{for $p\geq 2$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{2}(X)\,\max\left\{\frac{\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}{n^{\frac{3-\theta}{2}}\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\frac{\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{-\theta}}{e^{(1-\theta)}n\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert^{-\theta}}\right\}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip-5.69054pt&\text{for $1<p<2$},\end{cases}

where
\theta=(2(p-1))/p
. Here,
E_{1}(X)
,
E_{2}(X)
, and
E_{3}
are positive constants:
E_{1}(X)
depends only on
X
,
E_{2}(X)
depends on both
X
and
p
, while

is independent of
X
and depends only on
p
. Furthermore,
for any

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq d\,\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{\log\,n}}\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{q}\right\rVert\,(\log\,n)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{2,q\}}}\,\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}}

In particular, for the Minkowski spaces

1.4
, following the technique from
[

Corollary 1.3

E_{4}(X)\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,n^{-\frac{p(q-1)+q(p-1)}{pq(q-1)}}\,\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{\left(1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}\right)\,\frac{q-p}{p(q-1)}}&\text{for $1<p<q$}.\end{cases}

Here,
E^{\prime}_{3}(X)

E^{\prime}_{2}

E_{4}(X)

depends only on

E_{4}(X)

and

E^{\prime}_{2}

and depends only on
p

Theorem
1.4

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
Z
be a Banach sequence space,
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be finite dimensional, and
\lambda>1
. For

, let
Z_{n}
be the linear span of

(1)

if

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq\begin{cases}E_{1}(X)\,\max\left\{\frac{\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}}{n^{3/2}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\,\frac{1}{e\,n}\right\}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $p=1$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{3}\frac{\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}}{n}\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{-\frac{2}{p}}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,&\text{for $p\geq 2$},\\[5.69054pt]

(2)

if
Z
is symmetric and
2

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq\begin{cases}E_{1}(X)\,\max\left\{\frac{\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}}{n^{3/2}},\,\frac{1}{e\,n}\right\}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $p=1$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{3}\frac{\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}}{n}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,&\text{for $p\geq 2$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{2}(X)\,\max\left\{\frac{\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}}{n^{\frac{3-\theta}{2}}},\frac{1}{e^{(1-\theta)}n\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}^{-\theta}}\right\}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip-5.69054pt&\text{for $1<p<2$},\end{cases}

and

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq d\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{\log\,n}}\,\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{Z^{*}}\,\,\,\sqrt{\log\,n}\,\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{2}}.

The constants

.

To determine the exact asymptotic behavior of the constant
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
in the case where
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is infinite dimensional, we make essential use of Lemma
4.3
. This lemma allows us to reduce the problem to the study of this constant for such a domain in which the estimation of bounds becomes significantly more simpler. Among complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
of the Minkowski spaces
\ell^{n}_{q}

1\leq q\leq\infty

AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
when

In the finite-dimensional setting, Corollary
1.3
shows that the asymptotics of this constant necessarily involve a logarithmic factor. By contrast, the situation changes fundamentally in the infinite-dimensional case. As we show in the theorem below, the logarithmic term disappears entirely, and the asymptotic decay of

X

Cot(X)
(see Section
2

X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

We now obtain the asymptotic estimates of the arithmetic Bohr radius for
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an infinite dimensional complex Banach space and
\lambda>1
. Then
AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\geq\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q)
, where

\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q):=\begin{cases}E_{5}\,\dfrac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda}\frac{1}{n^{1/q}}&\text{for $p>\min\{Cot(X),q\}$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{6}\,\dfrac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda}\,n^{{\frac{1}{\min\{t,q\}}}-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}&\text{for $p\leq\min\{Cot(X),q\}$},\end{cases}

if
X
has a finite cotype
t
. Here
E_{5}>0
and
E_{6}>0
are constant independent of
X
and
n
.
On the other hand, we have

AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\leq\tilde{\Psi}_{2}(n,\lambda,p,q):=\begin{cases}2^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\,\lambda\,n^{-\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $q\leq Cot(X)$},\\[5.69054pt]
2^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\,\lambda\,n^{\frac{1}{Cot(X)}\,-\,\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}&\text{for $q>Cot(X)$}.\end{cases}

We are now in a stage to give the bounds of the arithmetic Bohr radius
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
for the unit ball
B_{Z}
of
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)

Theorem 1.7
.

Let

With this objective in mind, we begin by establishing the following result, which may be viewed as an analogue of

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
Y

(1)
gives the following

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{\Phi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,1)}{n\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{1}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert}.

(2)

Since the lower bounds in
(1)
of Theorem
1.10
are expressed in terms of the norms
\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{q}\right\rVert
and
\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{q}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert
, varying
q
over the interval
1\leq q\leq\infty
leads to an improvement of these estimates. The improved bounds are then given as follows:

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\sup_{1\leq q\leq\infty}\left\{\frac{\Phi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q)}{\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{q}\right\rVert\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{q}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert}\right\}

and

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\sup_{1\leq q\leq\infty}\left\{\frac{\Phi_{1}(n,\lambda,p,q)\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}{n\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{q}\right\rVert\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{q}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert}\right\}.

Theorem
1.1
provides a lower bound for the
n
-dimensional arithmetic Bohr radius
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
in terms of the corresponding
n
-dimensional Bohr radius
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. For a given simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we now show that
AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
can be estimated in terms of the one-dimensional Bohr radius for
\mathbb{D}
, namely
R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)
.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for every
n
we have

\frac{R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n}\leq AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert\,\frac{R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n^{1/p}}.

By considering the function
\phi
defined in (
5.23
) with
t=S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})
and proceeding along lines similar to those in the proof of Theorem
1.11
, one can verify that the result remains valid for any simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
. We therefore state the result below and omit the details.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for every
n
we have

\frac{R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n}\leq AP_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})\,\frac{R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n^{1/p}}.

In view of Theorem
1.11
and the fact
\left\lVert Id:B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}\rightarrow B_{\ell^{n}_{1}}\right\rVert=n^{1-1/q}
for
q\geq 1
, we deduce the following result for
Z=\ell^{n}_{q}
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then for every
n
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
we have

\frac{R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n}\leq AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,U)\leq\frac{R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-1}}.

Unlike the Bohr radius constant
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, for which no exact value is currently known for any simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
in dimension
n\geq 2
, the arithmetic Bohr radius can be explicitly computed in certain settings. In particular, for the unit ball of
\ell^{n}_{1}
we are able to determine its precise value. This follows directly from Corollary
1.6
by taking
p=1
and
q=1
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then for every
n
we have

AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},1,U)=\frac{R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},1,U)}{n}.

By applying the same arguments as in the pluriharmonic case in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.11
, we obtain the relation between the Bohr radius and the arithmetic Bohr radius for any Banach space
X
-valued holomorphic functions.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space such that
\chi(\{e_{k}\}^{n}_{k=1})=1
. Then

A_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\frac{\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}{n}\,K_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U).

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for every
n
we have

\frac{K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n}\leq A_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert\,\frac{K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n^{1/p}}.

Theorems
1.12
and
1.13
remain valid for any bounded complete Reinhardt domain, not only for
B_{Z}
.

Remark 1.3
.

For
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the bounds for
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
established in Theorems
1.4
–
1.8
extend to the constant
A_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
for any complex Banach space
X
, possibly with different constants. In particular, for
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, this implies that the asymptotic behaviors of
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
and
A_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
coincide . Since the proofs follow the same lines as those of the corresponding results for
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
, we omit the details. Moreover, an analogue of Theorem
1.2
holds for the constant
A_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X)
for any bounded complete Reinhardt domain.

2.
Application

This section is devoted to examples that demonstrate the scope of our results when applied to various classes of sequence spaces. Our first example is the family of mixed Minkowski spaces, which form a natural generalization of the classical Minkowski spaces. Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq s,t\leq\infty
, the mixed Minkowski space is defined as

\ell^{m}_{s}(\ell^{n}_{t}):=\{(z_{k})^{m}_{k=1}:z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}

with
\left\lVert(z_{k})^{m}_{k=1}\right\rVert_{s,t}:=(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\lVert z_{k}\right\rVert^{s}_{t})^{1/s}
. In the special case
s=t
, this construction coincides with the classical Minkowski space
\ell^{mn}_{s}
. While earlier contributions such as
[
8
,
7
]
dealt primarily with classical Minkowski spaces and the holomorphic case, the framework proposed in this article extends beyond that setting. It treats a significantly larger family of sequence spaces and enables a systematic study of holomorphic as well as pluriharmonic functions.

Example 2.1

(
Mixed spaces).

Let
m,n\geq 2
. Then

(1)

Let
1\leq s,t\leq 2
. If
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is finite dimensional, then

AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{m}_{s}(\ell^{n}_{t})},p,X)\geq\begin{cases}E_{1}(X)\,\,\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{2/t}\,m^{2/s}},\,\frac{1}{e\,mn}\right\}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $p=1$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{3}\,n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{tp}-\frac{1}{t}}\,m^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{sp}-\frac{1}{s}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,&\text{for $p\geq 2$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{2}(X)\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\frac{2}{t}+\frac{1}{p}-1}m^{\frac{2}{s}+\frac{1}{p}-1}},\frac{m^{2+\frac{4}{ps}-\frac{3}{p}-\frac{4}{s}}}{e^{(1-\theta)}n^{\frac{3}{p}+\frac{4}{t}-2-\frac{4}{pt}}}\right\}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip-2.84526pt&\text{for $1<p<2$},\end{cases}

and

AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{m}_{s}(\ell^{n}_{t})},p,X)\leq d\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{\log\,(mn)}}\,\,\sqrt{\log\,(mn)}\,\left(\frac{1}{mn}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

If
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is infinite dimensional then

\frac{\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(mn,\lambda,p,2)}{m^{1/s-1/2}\,n^{1/t-1/2}}\,\leq AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq\tilde{\Psi}_{2}(mn,\lambda,p,2).

(2)

Let
2\leq s,t\leq\infty
. If
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is finite dimensional then

AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{m}_{s}(\ell^{n}_{t})},p,X)\geq\begin{cases}E_{1}(X)\,\,\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{t}}\,m^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{s}}},\,\frac{1}{e\,mn}\right\}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $p=1$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{3}\,n^{-\frac{1}{t}}\,m^{-\frac{1}{s}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,&\text{for $p\geq 2$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{2}(X)\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{1}{t}}\,m^{\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{1}{s}}},\frac{m^{\theta-\frac{\theta}{s}-1}}{e^{(1-\theta)}n^{1-\theta+\frac{\theta}{t}}}\right\}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip-5.69054pt&\text{for $1<p<2$},\end{cases}

and

AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{m}_{s}(\ell^{n}_{t})},p,X)\leq d\,n^{1-\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{p}}m^{1-\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{p}}\,\lambda^{\frac{2}{\log\,n}}\,\sqrt{\log mn}.

If
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is infinite dimensional then

\tilde{\Psi}_{1}(mn,\lambda,p,2)\,\leq AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq m^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{s}}\,n^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{t}}\,\tilde{\Psi}_{2}(mn,\lambda,p,2).

Here, the constants
E_{1}(X)
,
E_{2}(X)
,
E_{3}
, and
d
are as in Theorem
1.4
.

Proof
.
For
1\leq s,t,l,r\leq\infty
, it is easy to check that the identity operator between mixed sequence spaces factorizes as

\left\lVert Id:\ell^{m}_{s}(\ell^{n}_{t})\rightarrow\ell^{m}_{l}(\ell^{n}_{r})\right\rVert=\left\lVert Id:\ell^{m}_{s}\rightarrow\ell^{m}_{l}\right\rVert\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{t}\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{r}\right\rVert

(see
[
23
, p. 188]
). Moreover, for finite-dimensional
\ell^{n}_{p}
spaces, the norm of the identity operator satisfies:
\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{s}\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{t}\right\rVert=1
for
s\leq t
and
\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{s}\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{t}\right\rVert=n^{1/t-1/s}
for
s>t
. Applying these observations together with Theorems
1.4
,
1.7
, and
1.8
, we obtain the desired result. This completes the proof.
∎

The scope of our results further includes classical symmetric Banach sequence spaces such as Lorentz spaces
\ell_{s,t}
as well as Orlicz spaces
\ell_{\psi}
; see
[
37
]
for their definitions.

Example 2.2

(
Lorentz spaces).

Let
1\leq s,t\leq\infty
.

(1)

Let
1\leq s<2
and

, or

and

. If

is finite dimensional

(2)

Let
2<s\leq\infty
and
1\leq t\leq\infty
. If

E_{3}\,\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{s}}}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,&\text{for $p\geq 2$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{2}(X)\,\max\left\{\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{1}{s}}},\frac{1}{e^{(1-\theta)}\,n^{1-\theta+\frac{\theta}{s}}}\right\}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip-5.69054pt&\text{for $1<p<2$},\end{cases}

.

\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{*}_{k}\right\rVert_{\ell_{s,t}^{*}}=n^{1-1/s}

\ell_{p,q}\subseteq\ell_{s,t}\,\,\mbox{if and only if}\,\,\,p<s,\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,p=s\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,q\leq t.

These facts, together with Theorems
1.5
(1),
1.7
, and
1.8

We now turn to part

and

\ell_{s,t}

1.5
(2)
,
1.7
, and
1.8
. It remains to consider the case
2<s\leq\infty
,
1\leq t\leq 2

1.4
,
1.7
, and
1.8
, together with the estimates

\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{s,t}\right\rVert\leq 1\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{s,t}\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{2}\right\rVert\leq n^{1/2-1/s}

(see
[
23
, p.189]
). This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Preliminaries

We adopt standard notation and terminology from Banach space theory. Throughout, all Banach spaces
W
are assumed to be complex. Their topological duals are denoted by
W^{*}
, and their open unit balls by
B_{W}
. A Banach space
W
is said to have cotype
t\in[0,\infty]
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for every finite family vectors
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in W
,

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert w_{k}\right\rVert^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}(s)w_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
r_{k}
denotes the
k
-th Rademacher function on
[0,1]
. We define

Cot(W):=\inf\{2\leq t\leq\infty:W\,\mbox{has cotype}\,t\}.

It is well known that every Banach space has cotype
\infty
. When
Cot(W)=\infty
, we adopt the convention
1/Cot(W)=0
. A Schauder basis
\{w_{k}\}
of a Banach space
W
is called unconditional if there is a constant
c\geq 1
such that

\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{j}\mu_{j}w_{j}\right\rVert\leq c\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}w_{j}\right\rVert

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, all scalars
\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, and all
\varepsilon_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\varepsilon_{j}|\leq 1
. The smallest such constant is denoted by
\chi({w_{k}})
and is called the unconditional basis constant of
{w_{k}}
. We say that
{w_{k}}
is a
1
-unconditional basis if
\chi({w_{k}})=1
. The unconditional basis constant of the Banach space
W
is defined by
\chi(W):=\inf\chi(\{w_{k}\})\in[1,\infty]
, the infimum taken over all unconditional basis
\{w_{k}\}
of
W
. A Banach space
Z
satisfying
\ell_{1}\subset Z\subset c_{0}
(with normal inclusions) is called a Banach sequence space if the canonical unit vectors
{e_{k}}
form a
1
-unconditional basis of
Z
. A Banach lattice
Z
is said to be
2
-convex if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\lVert\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all finite families
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in Z
. Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space,
Y
any Banach space, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)
the space of all
m
-homogeneous polynomials
Q:Z\rightarrow Y
of the form
Q(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
, together with the norm
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)}:=\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}||Q(z)||_{Y}
. The unconditional basis constant of the family of monomials
z^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{n}
is denoted by
\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y))
.
We denote by
\mathcal{PH}(^{m}Z,\mathcal{B}(H))
the space of all
m
-homogeneous pluriharmonic polynomials
P:Z\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(H)
of the form

(3.1)

P(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\alpha|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha}

and set
\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\mathcal{PH}(^{m}Z,\mathcal{B}(H))}:=\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}|||P(z)||_{\mathcal{B}(H)}
.

A bounded linear operator
U:W\rightarrow Y
between two Banach spaces
W
and
Y
is said to be
(r,t)
-summing,
r,t\in[1,\infty)
, if exists a constant
C>0
such that for every finite collection
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in W
,

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert U(w_{k})\right\rVert^{r}\right)^{1/r}\leq C\,\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\phi(w_{k})|^{t}\right)^{1/t}.

The following definition introduces the
m
-homogeneous analogue of the definition of Bohr radius for pluriharmonic functions, which plays a fundamental role in the analysis carried out in this article. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
with respect to
\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,X)
, denoted by
R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for every pluriharmonic polynomials
P:\Omega\rightarrow X
of the form (
3.1
) we have

(4.1)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X}.

Here
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\,\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}
. Set
R^{m}(\Omega,p,U):=R^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:X\rightarrow X
,
R^{m}(\Omega,p,X):=R^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
,
R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
R^{m}(\Omega,p):=R^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p)
. It is straightforward that
R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=\lambda^{1/m}\,R^{m}(\Omega,p,U)
. Similarly as above, we can define the constant
K^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for the class
\mathcal{P}^{n}_{m}(W)

i.e.
, for any Banach space
W
valued
m
-homogeneous holomorphic polynomial
Q(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
. Clearly, we have
K^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=\lambda^{1/m}\,K^{m}(\Omega,p,U)
. Using Bohnenblust-Hille inequality, Defant
et al.

[
30
]
have shown that the constant
K^{m}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1)
is hypercontractive.

The following lemma, proved in
[
36
]
, provides a coefficient-type Schwarz–Pick inequality for operator valued pluriharmonic functions defined on general complete Reinhardt domains. It plays an essential role in the proofs of several of our main results.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
36
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{PH}(Z,X)
be of the form (
1.2
). Then for all
|\alpha|=m\geq 1
, we have
\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}\pm b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 4\,\left\lVert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\,I-{\operatorname{Re}\,}(a_{0})\right\rVert
. Moreover, if
B_{Z}=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
then
\left\lVert a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\,\rho_{\alpha}\,\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
,
\left\lVert a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\,\rho_{\alpha}\,\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
, where
\rho_{\alpha}:=\left(\frac{|\alpha|^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha^{\alpha}}\right)^{1/q}
.

The following lemma, established in
[
36
]
, provides a fundamental connection between the Bohr radius for pluriharmonic functions and that for homogeneous pluriharmonic functions. Conceptually, it may be viewed as a pluriharmonic counterpart of
[
31
, Lemma 3.2]
, where analogous ideas were developed for Minkowski spaces in the setting of the powered Bohr inequality. In contrast to that earlier result, the following lemma is formulated in the general framework of Banach spaces.

Lemma 4.2
.

[
36
]

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
Y
any complex Banach space, and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\lambda>1
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1)

\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)

(2)

\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,R^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq\lambda\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,R^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)
.

It worth mentioning that analogous results of Lemma

hold for the constants

and
K_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
.

The following lemma compares the arithmetic Bohr radius constants for two complete Reinhardt domains. This result serves as a key ingredient in the proofs of several of our main theorems.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be two bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then we have

\frac{AP_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U)}{S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})}\,\leq AP_{\lambda}(\Omega_{1},p,U)\leq S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})\,AP_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U).

Proof
.
Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. We shall first prove the left hand inequality. Let
r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that for all
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega_{2},X)
of the form (
1.2
),

(4.2)

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda^{p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega_{2},X}.

Then, for every
\epsilon>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})\left(\frac{r}{S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})+\epsilon}\right)^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{p}\left\lVert\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,\left(\frac{z}{S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})+\epsilon}\right)^{\alpha}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{\left(\frac{z}{S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})+\epsilon}\right)}^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\Omega_{2},X}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega_{1},X}.

This implies that

\frac{1}{S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})+\epsilon}\,\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})+\epsilon}+\cdots+\frac{r_{n}}{S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})+\epsilon}\right)\leq AP_{\lambda}(\Omega_{1},p,U)

for all
\epsilon>0
.
Consequently,

\frac{1}{S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})}.\,AP_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U)\leq AP_{\lambda}(\Omega_{1},p,U).

By interchanging
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
in the above inequality, we establish the right-hand inequality.
∎

We recall the following remarkable result by Maurey and Pisier to prove the desired upper bound in the case of infintedimensional complex Banach space.

Theorem A
.

[

Given any infinite dimensional complex Banach space

for each

, where

-th canonical basis vector of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

\displaystyle=\lambda^{p}\,\left(n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{pm}\,\left(\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\,\frac{m!}{\alpha!}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C}}+\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\,\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C}}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=2^{p}\,\lambda^{p}\,\left(n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{pm}\,\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\,\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C}}.

We now recall the following probabilistic estimate due to Boas
[
15
, Theorem 4]
:

\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\,\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C}}\leq\gamma(m,n,q):=\sqrt{32m\,\log(6m)}\begin{cases}n^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(m!)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\,&\mbox{if}\,\,q\leq 2\\
n^{\frac{1}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)m}\,(m!)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,&\mbox{if}\,\,q\geq 2.\end{cases}

This estimate, together with (
5
), yields

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}}{n}\leq\left(2^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{m}\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,(\gamma(m,n,q))^{\frac{1}{m}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle AP^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{m}\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,(\gamma(m,n,q))^{\frac{1}{m}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\left(m\,\log\,m(m!)^{2\left(1-\frac{1}{\min\{2,q\}}\right)}\,n\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}}

for
C_{1}>0
, a uniform constant. We already know that
AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\leq AP^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)
, so for the desired upper bound of
AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)
, it is enough to estimate the right side quantity of (
5
). Write
t=1-1/\min\{2,q\}
for short. By Stirling’s formula, we have
m!\sim\sqrt{2\,\pi m}(m/e)^{m}
. Hence

(m!)^{2t}=\left(\frac{m}{e}\right)^{2tm}(2\pi m)^{t}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)\right),

and so the right side quantity of (
5
) becomes less than or equals to

(5.7)

C_{1}\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,(m\,\log\,m\,n)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\,\left(\frac{m}{e}\right)^{t}(2\pi m)^{\frac{t}{2m}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{m}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\,\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
(2\pi m)^{\frac{t}{2m}}\rightarrow 1
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, the sequence
\{(2\pi m)^{\frac{t}{2m}}\}
is bounded. On the other hand,
(1+O(1/m))^{\frac{1}{2m}}
is also bounded by uniform constant. We now want to estimate the quantity
(m\,\log\,m\,n)^{\frac{1}{2m}}
. Take
m=[\log\,n]
. Then

\log\,(m\,\log\,m\,n)=\log\,n+\log\,m+\log\log\,m=\log\,n+O(\log\log\,n).

Hence,

(m\,\log\,m\,n)^{\frac{1}{2m}}=\exp\left(\frac{\log\,n+O(\log\log\,n)}{2m}\right).

Since
m\sim\log\,n
,

\frac{\log\,n}{2m}\leq\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,O\left(\frac{\log\log\,n}{\log\,n}\right)\rightarrow 0.

Therefore,

(m\,\log\,m\,n)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\leq C_{2}

for some constant
C_{2}>0
. Combining these estimates, the quantity in (
5.7
) becomes less than or equals to

C_{3}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,m^{t}\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}},

which finally less than or equals to

C\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{\log\,n}}\,\,(\log\,n)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{2,q\}}}\,\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}}

for some universal constant
C>0
.
Thus,

(5.8)

AP_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\leq C\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{\log\,n}}\,\,(\log\,n)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{2,q\}}}\,\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}}.

For the upper bound of
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
, by making use of Lemma
4.3
and (
5.8
), we establish

AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq C\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{\log\,n}}\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{q}\right\rVert\,(\log\,n)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{2,q\}}}\,\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{2,q\}}-\frac{1}{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We begin by recalling two standard facts concerning finite-dimensional Banach spaces with unconditional bases. Let
W=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space whose canonical unit vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis. Then the operator norm of the identity embedding from
W
into
\ell^{n}_{2}
is given by
\left\lVert Id:W\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{n}\right\rVert=\sup_{z\in B_{W}}(\sum_{m=1}^{n}|z_{m}|^{2})^{1/2}
. On the other hand, the identity embedding from
Y
into
\ell^{n}_{1}
satisfies
\left\lVert Id:W\rightarrow\ell_{1}^{n}\right\rVert=\sup_{z\in B_{W}}\sum_{m=1}^{n}|z_{m}|=\left\lVert\sum_{m=1}^{n}e^{*}_{m}\right\rVert_{W^{*}}
for all
z\in W
. We are now in a position to establish the assertions of the theorem.

Since
Z\subset\ell_{2}
, we may assume that the inclusion map
Z\subset\ell_{2}
has norm
1
. Under this normalization, the claim
(1)
follows directly from Theorem
1.4
together with the above identities for the identity maps.

We shall now prove part
(2)
. By a result of
[
37
, Theorem 1.d.5]
, we may assume without loss of generality that the constant
M^{(2)}(X)=1
. This implies that
\left\lVert Id:\ell_{2}^{n}\rightarrow Z_{n}\right\rVert=1
. We now recall the following important fact from
[
23
, (5.3), p. 191]
. If
W=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a symmetric Banach space such that
M^{(2)}(W)=1
, then

(5.9)

\left\lVert Id:W\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{2}\right\rVert=\frac{\left\lVert\sum_{m=1}^{n}e^{*}_{m}\right\rVert_{W^{*}}}{\sqrt{n}}.

The conclusion then follows by combining Theorem
1.4
, the above facts for identity embeddings, and the inequality (
5.9
).This completes the proof. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

We shall make use of the following result due to
[
31
, Theorem 5.3]
. If
Y
is a
s
-concave Banach lattice, with
2\leq s<\infty
, and
U:X\rightarrow Y
an
(t,1)
-summing operator with
1\leq t\leq s
. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

(5.12)

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U(c_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{\beta}_{Y}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\leq C^{m}\,\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}^{n},X}

holds for every homogeneous polynomial
Q:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow X
with
Q(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, where

\beta=\frac{mst}{s+(m-1)t}.

The proof of the theorem relies on the definition of complete Reinhardt domains and identifying
B_{z}
as such a domain. Recall that a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is called complete Reinhardt if
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
then the new tuple
(\xi_{1}z_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}z_{n})\in\Omega
for each
\xi_{j}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
.
Here,
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
is an
n
-dimensional Banach space for which the canonical basis vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis. That is, equivalent to say the open unit ball
B_{Z}
is a complete Reinhardt domain.

Fix
z\in B_{Z}
. For every
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P:B_{Z}\rightarrow X
with
P(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
on
B_{Z}
, we consider the following
m
-homogeneous polynomial
\tilde{Q}:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow X
by

\tilde{Q}(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}):=P(z_{1}w_{1},\ldots,z_{n}w_{n})=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha})w^{\alpha}

for
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. The map
\tilde{Q}
is well defined, since
B_{Z}
is a complete Reinhardt domain and hence the point
(z_{1}w_{1},\ldots,z_{n}w_{n})
belongs to
B_{Z}
. Clearly,
\left\lVert\tilde{Q}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}^{n},X}\leq\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
. Now, by virtue of (
5.12
), we obtain

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{\beta}_{Y}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\leq C^{m}\,\left\lVert\tilde{Q}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{D}^{n},X}\leq C^{m}\,\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

and

on

1.9

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1\bigg)^{\frac{(s-1)mt-s+t}{mst}}\times\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{\frac{mst}{s+(m-1)t}}\right)^{\frac{s+(m-1)t}{mst}}\right]^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1\bigg)^{\frac{((s-1)mt-s+t)p}{mst}}c_{1}^{mp}\,\left\lVert H\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X}\leq\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1\bigg)^{\frac{((s-1)mt-s+t)p}{mst}}c_{1}^{mp}\,2^{p}\,\left\lVert P\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z},X}.

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1\bigg)^{\frac{((s-1)mt-s+t)p}{mst}}c_{2}^{mp}\,2^{p}\,\left\lVert P\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z},X}.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}

(5.15)

\displaystyle\leq 2(c^{m}_{1}+c^{m}_{2})\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1\bigg)^{\frac{(s-1)mt-s+t}{mst}}\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}

and

(5.16)

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq 2(c^{m}_{1}+c^{m}_{2})\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1\bigg)^{\frac{(s-1)mt-s+t}{mst}}\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}.

5

5.16

(5.17)

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}\right)|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq C^{pm}\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1\bigg)^{\frac{((s-1)mt-s+t)p}{mst}}\left\lVert P\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z},X}

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}1=\binom{n+m-1}{m}\leq e^{m}\left(1+n/m\right)^{m}

R^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\tilde{D}\left(1+\frac{n}{m}\right)^{-\frac{(s-1)mt-s+t}{mst}}

5.18
) gives

(5.19)

R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\tilde{D}\left(1+\frac{n}{m}\right)^{-\frac{(s-1)mt-s+t}{mst}}.

with the minimum value

4.2
(1), (
5.19
), and (

(5.21)

R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\geq D\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{s}}

for some constant
D>0
. The lower bound of
AP_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
is a direct consequence Theorem
1.1
and (
5.21
).This completes the proof.

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda^{p}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X}.

z\in B_{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert U\left(\frac{c_{m}}{t^{m}}\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\right)\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U\left(\frac{d_{m}}{t^{m}}\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\right)\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\right)|z|^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{c_{m}}{t^{m}}\right)\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\left\lVert U\left(\frac{d_{m}}{t^{m}}\right)\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\right)|z|^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(\left\lVert U(c_{m})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(d_{m})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})\,\left(\frac{\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}}{t}\right)^{pm}.

An observation, using preceding inequality and (
5.22
), shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(\left\lVert U(c_{m})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(d_{m})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})\,\left(\frac{\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{p}}{t}\right)^{pm}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\left\lVert U\left(\frac{c_{m}}{t^{m}}\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\right)\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U\left(\frac{d_{m}}{t^{m}}\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\right)\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\right)r^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{p}\left\lVert F\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{Z},X}=\lambda^{p}\left\lVert\tilde{f}\right\rVert^{p}_{\mathbb{D},X},

which implies that

\frac{\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{p}}{t}\leq R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U).

Again we have
\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{1}\leq n^{(1-1/p)}\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{p}
. Hence we obtain

\frac{\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{1}}{n}\leq\frac{tR_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)}{n^{1/p}}.

This completes the proof.

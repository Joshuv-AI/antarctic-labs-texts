Abstract
. In this paper we extend the concept of multiplicity from fake weighted projective spaces, as considered by Averkov, Kasprzyk, Lehmann and Nill in 2021, to Mori Dream Spaces, exploring interesting connections between the algebraic, geometric, and topological properties of these varieties. To this end, we introduce the weight group G Q G_{Q} and the weight modulus | Q | |Q| of a complete toric variety. Their topological interpretation provides a framework for classifying Fano and ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Fano toric varieties, offering an alternative approach for a further understanding of this rich and fascinating area of algebraic geometry. In particular, we exhibit an algebraic interpretation of Batyrev’s polar duality between Fano toric varieties as a direct sum decomposition of their common weight group.

Introduction

The concept of multiplicity was introduced for a fake weighted projective space
X
(fake wps, in the following) and denoted
\mult X
, by Averkov, Kasprzyk, Lehmann and Nill in
[
3
]
. Given a fake wps
X={\mathbb{P}}(Q)/G
, the Authors defined
\mult X
as the order
|G|
of the group defining
X
starting from a covering wps
{\mathbb{P}}(Q)
. They gave the following sharp upper bound for the multiplicity

Theorem 0.1

(Thm. 1.1. in
[
3
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
n
-dimensional fake wps with at worst canonical singularities.

(i)

If
n\leq 3
then
\mult X\leq(n+1)^{n-1}
.

(ii)

If
n=4
then
\mult X\leq 128
.

(iii)

If
n\geq 5
then
\mult X\leq 3(s_{n-1}-1)^{2}
, where
\{s_{n}\}
is the
Sylvester sequence
iteratively defined by setting
s_{1}:=2,s_{n}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}s_{i}+1
.

In each case, equality is achieved by a unique
X
.

By adapting some classical result by Conrads, one can also conclude the following

Theorem 0.2

(Prop. 5.5 in
[
9
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
n
-dimensional Gorenstein fake wps
X={\mathbb{P}}(Q)/G
, with
Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n+1})
. Then

\mult X\,|\quad{\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}q_{i}\over\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}q_{i}}={(-K_{{\mathbb{P}}(Q)})^{n}\over\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}q_{i}}=:\frac{\deg{\mathbb{P}}(Q)}{|Q|}\in\mathbb{N}

being
(-K_{{\mathbb{P}}(Q)})^{n}
the self-intersection of the Cartier anti-canonical divisor
-K_{{\mathbb{P}}(Q)}
.

See the following §
0.4
for a first explanation of the last equality.

0.1.
Multiplicity beyond Fake Weighted Projective Spaces

A first contribution of this paper is the extension of the concept of multiplicity beyond the confines of fake wps, reaching into the more general world of
Mori Dream Spaces
(MDS). Mori Dream Spaces, introduced by Hu and Keel in
[
13
]
, are algebraic varieties that exhibit a particularly well-behaved structure in terms of their divisor class groups and birational geometry. They include a wide range of varieties, such as toric varieties,
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties, and more generally, varieties with finitely generated class group and Cox ring. The extension of the concept of multiplicity to MDS allows us to explore deeper connections between the algebraic, geometric, and topological properties of these spaces and those of their canonical toric ambient varieties (see Theorem
6.8
and Corollary
6.10
).

The definition of
\mult X
given in
[
3
]
can be combinatorially understood as the index of the sublattice generated by the primitive generators of the fan’s rays within the ambient lattice. This interpretation admits a generalization to more general toric varieties of higher Picard number and to Mori Dream Spaces, by means of their canonical toric embedding. We extend the multiplicity by successive steps so getting successive generalizations of Theorems
0.1
and
0.2
. Namely:

•

Theorems
2.27
and Corollary
2.25
extend those results to Fano toric varieties, where all the introduced quantities have a well-defined geometric interpretation;

•

Theorem
4.12
and Corollary
4.11
extend them to
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein toric varieties: notice that, in this generalized context, to get effective bounds on the multiplicity, the Fano condition has to be replaced by conditions on admissible singularities (at worst
canonical singularities
) that bound the anti-canonical volume;

•

Corollary
6.10
extends Theorem
0.1
to MDS.

These bounds are not sharp for dimension greater than 2 due to the combinatorial approach used, based on the facet enumeration given by McMullen
[
16
]
. In any case, what clearly emerges is that
\mult X
approaches 1 as the Picard number of
X
increases
(see Remark
2.31
for a discussion of this aspect).

0.2.
The Weight Group and its relation with Multiplicity

A central object in our study of multiplicity is the
weight group

G_{Q}
, associated with a weight matrix
Q
. Given a toric variety
X
, a weight matrix
Q
is essentially a representative matrix of the free part of the class morphism
D\mapsto[D]
for torus invariant Weil divisors. The associated weight group
G_{Q}
is a finite abelian group that encodes essential information about the toric variety
X
and its universal 1-covering
Y
. The weight group
G_{Q}
is defined as the cokernel of a lattice map induced by the weight matrix
Q
(see §
2.5
). The order of
G_{Q}
, denoted by
g_{Q}
and called
weight order
, plays a crucial role in bounding the multiplicity of
X
.
We establish that the multiplicity of a Fano toric variety divides the weight order, that is,

\mult X\,|\,g_{Q}

(see Corollary
2.19
).
This divisibility condition generalizes the above cited Conrads’ result for fake wps and provides a powerful tool for studying the multiplicity of more general toric varieties and Mori Dream Spaces.

Moreover, we show that the weight group
G_{Q}
behaves quite well under Batyrev’s polar duality of Fano toric varieties, so giving an algebraic interpretation of polar duality. In particular, weight groups of a Fano toric variety
X
and its polar partner
X^{\circ}
turn out to be isomorphic (see Proposition
2.17
) and:
the polar duality of
X
and
X^{\circ}
can be algebraically resumed by a direct sum decomposition of their common weight group
G_{Q}\cong G_{Q\circ}
(see Proposition
2.18
).

0.3.
Topological significance of the Weight Group

The weight group
G_{Q}
is not merely an algebraic construct; it has profound topological significance. Given a Fano toric variety
X
and its universal 1-covering
Y
such that
X\cong Y/G
, (by Cox’s construction), we show that the weight group
G_{Q}
is isomorphic to the
étale fundamental group in codimension 1
of the maximal quotient
Z
of
Y
which is still a Fano toric variety admitting
Y
as the universal 1-covering, that is

G_{Q}\cong\pi_{1}^{\text{ét}}(Z)^{(1)}

(see Theorem
2.16
and the definition of
G_{Q}
in the following §
2.5
).
This connection allows us to interpret the multiplicity
\mult X
as a measure of the topological complexity of
X
in codimension 1.

This topological interpretation leads to a
topological classification theorem
for Fano toric varieties and their polar partners, namely Theorem
3.2
. We prove that, up to isomorphism in codimension 1, Fano toric varieties and their polar partners are parameterized by subgroups of the weight group
G_{Q}
and their complementary direct summand, respectively. Then, up to isomorphism in codimension 1, this classification depends on the unique invariant given by the (isomorphism class of the) weight matrix
Q
. It is a topological classification, as it reflects the structure of the étale fundamental group in codimension 1. Furthermore, we extend this classification to
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein and
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano toric varieties, where the Gorenstein index and the factor
h
play a crucial role in determining the possible multiplicities (see Theorem
5.1
).

0.4.
The Weight Modulus and the Degree of a Fano toric variety

Another useful concept introduced in this work is the
weight modulus

|Q|
of a complete toric variety
X
, defined as the sum of certain maximal minors of the weight matrix
Q
. This modulus represents the integral volume of the polytope spanned by the fan of the universal 1-covering of
X
. The weight modulus
|Q|
is a measure of the complexity of the weight matrix and plays a key role in bounding the multiplicity of a toric variety. When
X
is a fake wps
X\cong{\mathbb{P}}(Q)/G
, the weight modulus
|Q|
turns out to be just the sum of weights in the weight vector
Q
. When
X
is a Fano toric variety, we show that the multiplicity
\mult X
is related to the weight modulus by the formula

\mult X=\frac{(-K_{X^{\circ}})^{n}}{|Q|}=\frac{\deg(X^{\circ})}{|Q|},

where
(-K_{X^{\circ}})^{n}
is the self-intersection of the anti-canonical divisor, called
degree
, of the polar variety
X^{\circ}
(see Theorem
2.24
). This formula gives an interesting specialization of the relationship between the multiplicity and the degree of a fake wps, given by Conrads, and here recalled by Theorem
0.2
.

The present paper is organized as follows. In §
1
we introduce notation and foundational concepts as definitions and notation for toric varieties and matrices, the GKZ decomposition and small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modifications. In §
2
we give bounds on the multiplicity of Fano toric varieties (Theorem
2.27
). In this section we extend the definition of multiplicity to a complete toric variety (Definition
2.6
) and define the weight group and the weight modulus for a Fano toric variety. The topological classification theorem of Fano toric varieties and their polar partners is given in §
3
(namely, Theorem
3.2
). This section ends up with a retelling of the well-known degree classification of Fano toric surfaces (see e.g.
[
11
, § 8.3, Fig. 2]
) from the point of view of weight matrices and groups (see Corollary
3.4
) and, as an application, with the classification of all Fano toric quotients of a small contraction of the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
in two points (see Example
3.5
). In §
4
, bounds obtained in §
2
for Fano toric varieties are extended, as far as possible, to
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano (and actually
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein) toric varieties (see Theorems
4.10
and
4.12
and Corollary
4.11
). In this context, Batyrev’s polar duality is no more well-defined, hence some invariants like the weight modulus and the degree of the polar partner and its universal 1-covering turn out to be only algebraic quantities but still well-working in bounding the multiplicity. Moreover, to bound the volume extension given by the increasing of the Gorenstein index, one has to impose condition on the admissible singularities (at worst
canonical singularities
). In §
5
we propose an extension of the topological classification theorem to
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein toric varieties (see Theorem
5.1
), depending on the weight matrix
Q
and the
factor

h
(defined in Remark
4.6
). Examples
5.5
and
5.6
give explicit applications. Finally, §
6
extends the concepts and results previously introduced for toric varieties to Mori Dream Spaces, as far as possible (see Theorem
6.8
and Corollary
6.10
). The strategy is bounding the multiplicity of a MDS by linking it to the one of its canonical toric embedding. Example
6.11
provides a concrete application of these results.

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and complete toric variety, whose weight matrix is

, is always a g-cell,

(2)

every chamber

of the GKZ-decomposition of

is a g-chamber.

1.4.
Isomorphisms in codimension 1 and small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification

A birational morphism
f:X\dashrightarrow Y
between algebraic varieties is called an
isomorphism in codimension 1
if it is biregular in codimension 1, that is, there exist Zariski open subset
U\subseteq X
and
V\subseteq Y
such that
f|_{U}:U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}V
is a biregular morphism and

\codim(X\setminus U)\geq 2
and
\codim(Y\setminus V)\geq 2
. We will write

f:X\cong_{1}Y

If
X
and
Y
are both
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and complete then the isomorphism in codimension 1
f:X\cong_{1}Y
is called a
small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification
.

Furthermore, if
X
and
Y
are also toric varieties, the small
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial modification
f:X\cong_{1}Y
implies that they have same weight and fan matrices,
Q=\mathcal{G}(V)
, respectively, so that they correspond to the choice of two fans
\Sigma,\Theta\in\mathcal{SF}(V)
, respectively, and their nef cones
\Nef(X)
and
\Nef(Y)
give two g-cells of the GKZ-decomposition of
\Mov(Q)
. In particular (see
[
11
, § 15.3]
and
[
13
, Cor. 2.4]
):

(1)

if
\Nef(X)
and
\Nef(Y)
are adjacent chambers, then
f
is called a
wall crossing
,

(2)

if
\Nef(X)
and
\Nef(Y)
are (not necessarily adjacent) chambers, then the small
\mathbb{Q}
factorial modification
f
is the composition of a finite number of wall crossings,

(3)

if
\Nef(X)
and
\Nef(Y)
are just g-cells, then
f
can be obtained by composing a finite number of small birational contractions, small birational resolutions and wall crossings.

2.
Bounds on Fano Toric Varieties

In the following:

•

a Gorenstein algebraic variety is a normal complete algebraic variety
X
whose canonical divisor
K_{X}
is Cartier;

•

a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein algebraic variety is a normal complete algebraic variety
X
whose canonical divisor
K_{X}
admits a Cartier multiple
kK_{X}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

a Fano algebraic variety is a Gorenstein projective variety
X
whose anti-canonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample;

•

a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano algebraic variety is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein projective variety
X
whose anti-canonical divisor
-K_{X}
admits an ample multiple
-kK_{X}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let us underline that, under the adopted notation, an ample divisor is a Cartier divisor.

2.1.
Fano-Gorenstein condition on the weight matrix

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a complete toric variety with

\dim X=n,\ \operatorname{rk}\Cl(X)=r,\ m:=n+r

Let
\Sigma
be the fan of
X
and
V
be a fan matrix associated with
\Sigma
. Let
Q=\mathcal{G}(V)
be a weight matrix of
X
. Define

\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n):=\{I\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}\,|\,\langle V^{I}\rangle\in\Sigma(n)\}

Then:

(1)

if
X
is Gorenstein then

\forall\,I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)\quad\exists\,\mathbf{q}_{I}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|I|}:Q_{I}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{I}=Q\cdot\mathbf{1}_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}

(2)

X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano if and only if

\forall\,I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)\quad\exists\,\mathbf{q}_{I}\in\mathbb{Q}^{|I|}:\mathbf{q}_{I}>\mathbf{0}_{|I|}\ \text{and}\ Q_{I}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{I}=Q\cdot\mathbf{1}_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}

where
\mathbf{q}_{I}>\mathbf{0}_{|I|}
means that
\mathbf{q}_{|I|}
admits strictly positive entries only.

Proof.

By definition,
X
is Gorenstein if and only if
K_{X}
is a Carter divisor. Then the canonical class
[K_{X}]\in\Pic(X)
. Item (1) then follows by recalling that

(1)

\Pic(X)=\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)}\mathcal{L}_{c}(Q_{I})

as

\displaystyle[-K_{X}]\in\Pic(X)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle Q\cdot\mathbf{1}_{m}\in\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)}\mathcal{L}_{c}(Q_{I})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall\,I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)\quad\exists\,\mathbf{q}_{I}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|I|}:Q_{I}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{I}=Q\cdot\mathbf{1}_{m}

Equality (
1
) is proven in
[
19
, Thm. 2.9]
, although under the avoidable assumption that
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial. Alternatively, by adapting notation, it follows from
[
2
, Cor. 2.4.2.4]
.

For item (2), recall that, by definition,
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano if and only if

(2)

\displaystyle\exists\,l\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle l[K_{X}]=[lK_{X}]\in\Pic(X)

(3)

\displaystyle[-lK_{X}]\in\Relint(\Nef(X))

Condition (
2
) is equivalent to asking for

\forall\,I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)\quad\exists\,\mathbf{q}_{I}\in\mathbb{Q}^{|I|}:Q_{I}\cdot\mathbf{q}_{I}=Q\cdot\mathbf{1}_{m}

Recall that

\Nef(X)=\bigcap_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)}\langle Q_{I}\rangle

(see e.g.
[
2
, Prop. 2.4.2.6]
) where, for every
I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)
, the cone
\langle Q_{I}\rangle
is the locus in
\Cl(X)
of classes of locally effective divisors over the open affine subset of
X
determined by the cone
V_{I}
. Then condition (
3
) is equivalent to asking for
\mathbf{q}_{I}
to admitting only strictly positive entries, for every
I
in
\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)
.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}^{r,n+r}
be a reduced
W
-matrix with
r,n\geq 1
. If there exists a fan
\Theta

over
the Gale dual matrix

(recall Def.

), such that the toric variety

then

,

•

condition (2) in Theorem

then

is called a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano weight matrix
.

•

both conditions (1) and (2) then

.

Remark 2.3
.

Notice that, for
r=1
, the reduced weight matrix
Q=(q_{0},q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})
is actually the reduced weight system of the wps

and Theorem 3 reduces to state that
if a fake wps

\forall\,i=0,1,\ldots,n\quad q_{i}\,|\,Q\cdot\mathbf{1}_{n+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}q_{i}=:|Q|

which is a well known condition. In this case,

admits the bunch of cones determined by the fan

Then a Gorenstein weight matrix

.

A reduced

is always a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano weight matrix and

is Gorenstein if and only if it is Fano.

Here and below we will offen use the following

.

Let

be a complete toric variety. Then

is isomorphic in codimension 1 to a

-Fano toric variety

. Moreover,

.

.

Remark

ensures that there always exists a

-factorial complete toric variety

admitting a given reduced

Remark 3.3
.

The main meaning of Theorem
3.2
is that, up to isomorphisms in codimension 1, which preserve dimension, Picard number, divisors, classes and their numerical properties, Fano toric varieties are parameterized by subgroups of the weight group
G_{Q}
, when the weight matrix
Q
is fixed. Moreover, polar duality of Fano varieties is described by a direct decomposition of
G_{Q}
.

This is a
topological classification
, as
G
and
G^{\circ}
turn out to be isomorphic to the étale fundamental groups in codimension 1 of
X
and
X^{\circ}
, respectively.

This classification is a direct consequence of the Gorenstein condition which, in a sense, bounds the canonical divisor. As soon as this condition is relaxed, e.g. by allowing
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein singularities, results given in Theorem
3.2
can no longer hold, due to the arbitrariness of the Gorenstein index, as we will analyze in the next §
4
and, in particular, with Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem
3.2
can be easily checked in dimension 2 by means of the well known classification by degree of Fano toric surfaces
[
11
, § 8.3, Fig. 2]
.

Definition 4.1

(Gorenstein index)
.

The minimum integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
kK_{X}
is Cartier is called the
Gorenstein index
of
X
. In the following, if there is no danger of confusion, we will simply call it
the index of

.

Let us adopt the same notation as in §
2.2
, that is

n:=\dim X\ ,\quad r:=\operatorname{rk}(\Cl(X))\ ,\quad m:=n+r=|\Sigma(1)|

and let

\displaystyle V\in\mathbb{Z}^{n,m}

be a fan matrix of
X(\Sigma)

\displaystyle Q=\mathcal{G}(V)\in Z^{r,m}

be a weight matrix of
X

In the present general setting, we no longer have a polar duality, as
\conv(V)
is no longer a reflexive polytope and, in general, the polytope
\Delta_{-K_{X}}
is no longer a lattice polytope but its vertices are rational points in
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
, that is

\Delta_{-K_{X}}=\conv(V^{\circ})\quad\text{where}\quad V^{\circ}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n,m^{\circ}}

Notice that, by definition, the index
k
of

is the minimum positive integer such that
kV^{\circ}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n,m^{\circ}}
, i.e.
\Delta_{-kK_{X}}=\conv(kV^{\circ})
is a lattice polytope in
M
. Moreover,
V^{\circ}
can be still written as in Lemma
2.13
, but with rational entries, that is

V^{\circ}=\left(-(V^{I})^{*^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{n}\right)_{I\in\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)}\in\mathbb{Q}^{n,m^{\circ}}

On the other hand, Lemma
2.14
still hold when we set

\displaystyle Q^{\circ}:=\mathcal{G}(kV^{\circ})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r^{\circ},m^{\circ}}

,

\displaystyle W^{\circ}={1\over k}\mathcal{G}(Q^{\circ})\in\mathbb{Q}^{n,m^{\circ}}

(39)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\ \exists!B\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n,n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle W^{\circ}=B^{T}\cdot V^{\circ}

In fact,
kV^{\circ}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n,m^{\circ}}
and
\operatorname{rk}(kV^{\circ})=n
, which is enough to a get a good definition for a Gale dual matrix

[
19
, § 3.1]
: notice that
Q^{\circ}
does not depend on the choice of the integer

. Then in the following we will say that

is a Gale dual matrix of

, and write

(40)

Q^{\circ}=\mathcal{G}(V^{\circ})

Notice that, being

an
F
-matrix, then

is a
W
-matrix and we can assume

to admit non-negative entries only
[
19

.

Moreover, calling
B
the quotient matrix of

, the argument proving Lemma

shows that

W^{\circ}=B^{T}\cdot V^{\circ}

Proposition 4.2
.

X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein (
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano) toric variety if and only its universal 1-covering
Y
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein (
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano) toric variety. In particular, the Gorenstein index of
Y
divides the Gorenstein index of
X
.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows by observing that both
X
and its universal 1-covering
Y
have the same weight matrix and the same indexing set
\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma}(n)
. In particular, being
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano for a toric variety is characterized by being
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano for its weight matrix

.

The second part of the statement follows by (
4
). In fact,

kW^{\circ}=B^{T}\cdot(kV^{\circ})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n,m^{\circ}}

and
\conv(kW^{\circ})=\Delta_{-kK_{Y}}
. Then
kK_{Y}
is Cartier, meaning that the index of
Y
has to divide
k
. ∎

Given a weight matrix

of

, the anti-canonical class is given by

(61)

[-K_{Z}]=Q\cdot\mathbf{1}=:\mathbf{q}\in\Eff(Q)=\langle Q\rangle

Recalling that

\overline{\Mov}(Z)=\Mov(Q)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n+r}\left\langle Q^{\{i\}}\right\rangle

it suffices to show that

where, as usual,

and

.
Start by considering the first column
\mathbf{q}_{1}
of

. One needs to find a non-negative rational solution of the linear system

(62)

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbf{q}_{2}&\cdots&\mathbf{q}_{m}\\
\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m}\\
\end{array}\right)=\mathbf{q}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ Q\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m}\\
\end{array}\right)=\mathbf{q}

By (
61
),
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{Q}^{m}
is a solution of the linear system

. Moreover, by Gale duality, solutions of this linear system are described by points of the
n
-dimensional affine subvariety

S=\mathbf{1}+\mathcal{L}_{r}(V)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{m}

Then, looking for non-negative solutions in

means finding

\vdots\\
t_{n}\\
\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}\,:\quad\mathbf{x}:=\mathbf{1}+V^{T}\cdot\mathbf{t}\geq\mathbf{0}

and all these
\mathbf{t}
are precisely assigned by rational polytope points in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}\cap\Delta_{-K_{Z}}
, as by definition

\displaystyle\Delta_{-K_{Z}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{m}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,\forall\,i=1\ldots,n+r\quad\langle\mathbf{m},\mathbf{v}_{i}\rangle\geq-1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{m}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,|\,V^{T}\cdot\mathbf{m}\geq-\mathbf{1}\}

Completeness of

implies that
\Delta_{-K_{Z}}
is indeed a polytope
[
11
, Prop. 4.3.8]
.
In particular, solutions
\mathbf{x}
satisfying condition (
62
) are assigned by all rational points on the facet of
\Delta_{-K_{Z}}
orthogonal to the first column
\mathbf{v}_{1}
of
V
.
Moreover, if

is the least common multiple of denominators in
\mathbf{x}
, then by setting

\displaystyle Q\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=2}^{m}h_{i}D_{i}\right]=[-hK_{Z}]

\mathbf{q}_{i}
of

is movable, too.

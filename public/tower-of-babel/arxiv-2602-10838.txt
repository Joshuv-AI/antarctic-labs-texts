Mirror descent actor-critic methods for entropy regularised MDPs in general spaces: stability and convergence
Denis Zorba, David Šiška, Lukasz Szpruch

Abstract
. We provide theoretical guarantees for convergence of discrete-time policy mirror descent with inexact advantage functions updated using temporal difference (TD) learning for entropy regularised MDPs in Polish state and action spaces. We rigorously derive sufficient conditions under which the single-loop actor-critic scheme is stable and convergent. To weaken these conditions, we introduce a variant that performs multiple TD steps per policy update and derive an explicit lower bound on the number of TD steps required to ensure stability. Finally, we establish sub-linear convergence when the number of TD steps grows logarithmically with the number of policy updates, and linear convergence when it grows linearly under a concentrability assumption.

1.
Introduction

In reinforcement learning (RL), an agent seeks to find an optimal policy that minimises their expected cumulative cost by interacting with their environment.
Such a framework has had numerous remarkable applications
[
29
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
23
]
.
Theoretical understanding of key RL algorithms and their convergence properties has been growing over the last decades
[
12
,
32
,
21
,
1
]
.

In practice actor critic algorithms are some of the most widely used.
The actor updates the policy using a policy gradient method (which requires the advantage function) and the critic, which provides an estimate of the advantage function, is updated based on costs and new states observed by interacting directly with the environment using e.g. temporal difference (TD)-based loss.

Classical policy gradient methods require careful tuning of step-size to achieve stability which led to the development of trust-region approaches such as TRPO
[
27
]
and its practical variant PPO
[
28
]
.
These improve stability by controlling the change between successive policies via KL-divergence constraint or policy ratio clipping respectively.
Policy mirror descent
[
13
,
16
,
15
]
provides a closely related first-order viewpoint, replacing the hard trust-region constraint with a KL-based penalty and yielding algorithms for which it is possible to obtain theoretical guarantees (under the assumption of access to the exact advantage function).
Adding entropic regularisation to the objective then leads to a number of theoretical and practical advantages
[
2
,
5
,
17
]
.
Firstly, entropy regularised MDPs are guaranteed to have a unique optimal policy and can accelerate the convergence of policy gradient methods
[
21
,
13
,
16
,
15
]
.
Secondly, the entropic regularisation ensures persistent exploration
[
25
,
10
]
and provides some (albeit minimal) robustness to changes in costs and environment
[
36
,
9
]
.

However, while the theory of discrete time policy mirror descent is well understood for MDPs without entropy regularisation
[
31
]
and for tabular MDPs with entropy regularisation
[
16
]
, its stability and convergence remains open for entropy regularised MDPs in general state and action spaces.
This paper fills this gap in the literature by rigorously proving the stability and convergence of policy mirror descent for entropy regularised MDPs in Polish state and action spaces, where the critic is updated using one step or multiple steps of temporal difference learning.

1.1.
Related works

While actor-critic methods employing mirror descent for unregularised MDPs are well understood, both with and without function approximation
[
31
]
,
[
24
]
, the literature surrounding its extension to entropy regularised MDPs still remains sparse.
In this setting,
[
16
]
,
[
5
]
demonstrates convergence through carefully constructed decaying step sizes tabular case.
More recently,
[
37
]
extend these results to the Polish state and action space case.
However, this paper uses the idealised continuous-time dynamics of the policy mirror descent and temporal difference updates.
While the continuous-time limit is mathematically convenient, its connection to any given algorithm is unclear as there are many discrete schemes that converge to the same continuous-time limit as step size goes to zero.
However, the convergence, or even stability, as the number of discrete steps goes to infinity is not guaranteed.
Moreover, the convergence of the dynamics in
[
37
]
depends on an exponentially increasing timescale separation.
While this is well defined in the continuous time limit, it is unclear what it implies in relation to the discrete time algorithm.

1.2.
Contributions

Our main contributions are as follows.

•

For entropy regularised MDPs in general spaces, we consider a classical actor critic algorithm where the policy is updated using mirror descent and the critic is updated using temporal difference learning. As pointed out in
[
37
]
, ensuring that the relative entropy does not blow up along the gradient flow is difficult in general action spaces.
In the finite action space setting, for any measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(A)
such that
\mu(a_{i})>0
and for all
s\in S
it holds that
\operatorname{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s)|\mu)\leq\log|A|
.
In general action spaces the
\operatorname{KL}
divergence has no upper bound (can be
+\infty
) even if
\mu
has full support.
Thus we derive sufficient conditions of the discrete actor critic updates, under which we demonstrate uniform boundedness of the KL divergence along the actor-critic stepping scheme.

•

[
16
,
15
]
demonstrate that classical policy mirror descent with exact advantage functions for entropy regularised MDPs in general spaces exhibits sublinear convergence and even linear convergence under a concentrability assumption.
We extend this result and demonstrate that under
Q^{\pi}_{\tau}
-realisability, one can still obtain both rates while using TD learning to update an approximation of the advantage function.
To the best of our knowledge, these stability and convergence results are the first to be established in the literature.

1.3.
Entropy regularised Markov Decision Processes

See Appendix
B
for a summary of the notation used in this paper.
Consider an infinite horizon Markov Decision Process
(S,A,P,c,\gamma)
, where the state space
S
and action space
A
are Polish,
P\in\mathcal{P}(S|S\times A)
is the state transition probability kernel,
c
is a bounded cost function and
\gamma\in(0,1)
is a discount factor. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(A)
denote a reference probability measure and
\tau>0
denote a regularisation parameter. To ease notation, for each
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(A|S)
,
s\in S
and
a\in A
we define

(1)

P_{\pi}(ds^{\prime}|s):=\int_{A}P(ds^{\prime}|s,a)\pi(da|s),

(2)

P^{\pi}(ds^{\prime},da^{\prime}|s,a):=P(ds^{\prime}|s,a)\pi(da^{\prime}|s^{\prime}).

For each stochastic policy
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(A|S)
and
s\in S
, we define the regularised value function by

(3)

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{s}^{\pi}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{n}\Big(c(s_{n},a_{n})+\tau\operatorname{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s_{n})|\mu)\Big)\right].

Here
\operatorname{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s)|\mu)
is the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence of
\pi(\cdot|s)
with respect to
\mu
, defined as

(4)

\operatorname{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s)|\mu):=\int_{A}\ln\frac{d\pi}{d\mu}(a|s)\pi(da|s),

if
\pi(\cdot|s)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
, and infinity otherwise. As a result, for any
s\in S
we have that
V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. For a given initial distribution
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(S)
, the optimal value function is defined as

(5)

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{\tau}(\rho):=\int_{S}V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)\rho(ds),\quad V^{*}_{\tau}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(A|S)}V^{\pi}_{\tau}(\rho).

We refer to
\pi^{*}\in\mathcal{P}(A|S)
as the optimal policy if
V^{*}_{\tau}(\rho)=V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}(\rho)
.

The Bellman Principle for entropy regularised MDPs, see Theorem
B.1
, ensures that without loss of generality, it is sufficient to consider policies from the class given by Definition
1.1
below.

Definition 1.1

(Admissible Policies)
.

Let
\Pi_{\mu}
denote the class of policies for which there exists
f\in B_{b}(S\times A)
with

(6)

\pi(da|s)=\frac{\exp(f(s,a))}{\int_{A}\exp(f(s,a))\mu(da)}\mu(da).

For each
\pi\in\Pi_{\mu}
the value function
V^{\pi}_{\tau}
is the unique bounded solution of the on-policy Bellman equation

(7)

\displaystyle V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\left(Q_{\tau}^{\pi}(s,a)+\tau\ln\frac{d\pi}{d\mu}(a,s)\right)\pi(da|s).

See e.g. Lemma B.2 of
[
15
]
. Moreover, for each
\pi\in\Pi_{\mu}
, the state-action value function
Q^{\pi}_{\tau}\in B_{b}(S\times A)
is defined as

(8)

Q^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)=c(s,a)+\gamma\int_{S}V_{\tau}^{\pi}(s^{\prime})P(ds^{\prime}|s,a).

and the soft advantage function
A^{\pi}_{\tau}\in B_{b}(S\times A)
as

(9)

A^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)=Q^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)+\tau\log\frac{d\pi}{d\mu}(s,a)-V^{\pi}_{\tau}(s).

Finally we define the Bellman operator
\mathrm{T}^{\pi}_{\tau}:B_{b}(S\times A)\to B_{b}(S\times A)
as

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}^{\pi}_{\tau}f(s,a)

\displaystyle=c(s,a)+\gamma\int_{S\times A}f(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})P^{\pi}(ds^{\prime},da^{\prime}|s,a)+\tau\gamma\int_{S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s^{\prime})|\mu)P(ds^{\prime}|s,a).

A direct calculation shows that for any
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(A|S)
,
T^{\pi}_{\tau}
is a
\gamma
-contraction on
B_{b}(S\times A)
with respect to
|\cdot|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}
and hence by Banach’s fixed point theorem
Q^{\pi}_{\tau}:S\times A\to\mathbb{R}
is the unique fixed point of
\mathrm{T}^{\pi}_{\tau}
.

2.
Mirror Descent and Temporal Difference

In this section we introduce an actor critic scheme to find the optimal policy
\pi^{*}\in\Pi_{\mu}
of the entropy regularised MDP. Firstly, let’s consider the following policy mirror descent updates. For some
\lambda>0
, define
G:\mathcal{P}(A|S)\times\mathcal{P}(A|S)\to\mathbb{R}
as

(11)

\displaystyle G(\pi,\pi^{\prime})=\int_{S}\bigg(\int_{A}A^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s,a)\pi(da|s)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{\prime})(s)\bigg)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{\prime}}(ds),

and consider the updates

(12)

\displaystyle\pi^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(A|S)}G(\pi,\pi^{n}).

In the setting where the advantage function can be calculated exactly for all
s\in S
and
a\in A
,
[
16
,
15
]
demonstrates convergence of the policy mirror descent for entropy regularised MDPs in the tabular setting.
However having direct access to the true advantage function for all
s\in S
and
a\in A
is unfeasible in general spaces. To that end, we consider the following finite dimensional parametrisation of the advantage function. Given some feature mapping
\phi:S\times A\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we firstly parametrise the state-action value function as
Q(s,a;\theta):=\left\langle\theta,\phi(s,a)\right\rangle
and define the approximate soft advantage function as in Definition
2.1
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Q(s,a;\theta):=\langle\theta,\phi(s,a)\rangle
, for some
\phi:S\times A\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and any
\tau>0
. For all
s\in S
and
a\in A
, the approximate advantage function is defined as

(13)

\displaystyle A(s,a;\theta,\pi)=Q(s,a;\theta)+\tau\ln\frac{d\pi}{d\mu}(s,a)-\int_{A}\bigg(Q(s,a;\theta)+\tau\ln\frac{d\pi}{d\mu}(s,a)\bigg)\pi(da|s).

Definition 2.2
.

For some
\beta\in\mathcal{P}(S\times A)
, let
d_{\beta}^{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(S\times A)
be the state-action occupancy measure defined in Appendix
B
. The Mean Squared Bellman Error (MSBE) is defined as

(14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{MSBE}(\theta,\pi):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S\times A}(Q(s,a;\theta)-\mathrm{T}^{\pi}_{\tau}Q(s,a;\theta))^{2}d_{\beta}^{\pi}(da,ds).

Moreover, the semi-gradient
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathcal{P}(A|S)\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
of the MSBE with respect to
\theta
is given by

(15)

\displaystyle g(\theta,\pi):=\int_{S\times A}(Q(s,a;\theta)-\mathrm{T}^{\pi}_{\tau}Q(s,a;\theta))\phi(s,a)\,d_{\beta}^{\pi}(da,ds).

Remark 2.3
.

Given that
\beta\in\mathcal{P}(S\times A)
has full support, by (
10
) it holds that
\mathrm{MSBE}(\theta,\pi)=0
if and only if
Q(s,a;\theta)=Q^{\pi}_{\tau}(s,a)
for all
s\in S
and
a\in A
.

Assumption 2.4
.

Let
\beta\in\mathcal{P}(S\times A)
be fixed. Then

(16)

\lambda_{\beta}:=\lambda_{\min}\left(\int_{S\times A}\phi(s,a)\phi(s,a)^{\top}\,\beta(ds\,da)\right)>0.

Note that unlike the analogous assumptions in the literature
[
11
]
, Assumption
2.4
is independent of the policy and only depends on some
\beta\in\mathcal{P}(S\times A)
and
\phi:S\times A\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
which are both design choices.

Assumption 2.5
.

For all
(s,a)\in S\times A
it holds that
|\phi(s,a)|_{2}\leq 1
.

Assumption
2.5
is for convention and is without loss of generality in the finite-dimensional case.

Assumption 2.6

(
Q^{\pi}_{\tau}
-realisability)
.

For all
\pi\in\Pi_{\mu}
there exists
\theta_{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
Q^{\pi}(s,a)=\left\langle\theta_{\pi},\phi(s,a)\right\rangle
for all
(s,a)\in S\times A
.

Let us comment on using linear function approximation.
While non-linear function approximation (using deep neural networks) are widely used in practice, there are no proofs of convergence even for supervised learning with deep neural networks.
This analytical intractability of non-linear function approximations leads us to adopt linear function approximation.

The
Q^{\pi}
realisability (Assumption
2.6
) is common in the literature
[
7
,
22
,
30
,
5
,
6
,
11
]
.
One classical family of MDPs which satisfy Assumption
2.6
are Linear MDPs, see e.g
[
18
,
33
,
34
]
.
Moreover, Assumption
2.6
holds in the limit
N\to\infty
when
\phi_{i}
are the basis functions of
L^{2}(\rho\otimes\mu)
for some
\rho\otimes\mu\in\mathcal{P}(S\times A)

[
4
]
.
With this perspective in mind,
[
19
,
26
]
demonstrate empirical success of linear MDPs when the feature mapping
\phi:S\times A\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is careful truncation of the
L^{2}(\rho\otimes\mu)
basis functions.

3.
Single loop actor-critic

At each iteration of policy mirror descent (
12
), one would ideally replace the true advantage function with its parametrisation
A(s,a;\theta^{*},\pi^{n})
such that
\theta^{*}=\min_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{MSBE}(\theta,\pi^{n})

That is, let

(17)

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(\pi,\pi^{\prime},\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{S}\bigg(\int_{A}A(s,a;\theta,\pi^{\prime})\pi(da|s)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\pi^{\prime})(s)\bigg)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{\prime}}(ds),

then for some critic step size
h>0
and actor step size
\lambda>0
, initial policy and parameters
\pi^{0}=\pi_{0}\in\Pi_{\mu}
,
\theta^{0}=\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, consider Algorithm
1
.

1:

for

n=0,1,2,\ldots

\theta^{n+1}\leftarrow\theta^{n}-h\,g(\theta^{n},\pi^{n})

3.1.
Stability

To ease notation in the main results, for each
s\in S
and

we let

(18)

l_{n}(s,a)=\log\frac{d\pi^{n}}{d\mu}(s,a)-\int_{A}\log\frac{d\pi^{n}}{d\mu}(s,a^{\prime})\mu(da^{\prime}),

(19)

\mathrm{K}_{n}:=\sup_{s\in S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{n}(\cdot|s)|\mu),\quad\Gamma:=(1-\gamma)(1-\sqrt{\gamma})\lambda_{\beta}.

To establish conditions for stability of Algorithm

, we firstly prove a coupled recursion for the critic parameters.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let Assumption
2.4
and
2.5
hold. Let
0<h\leq\frac{\Gamma}{6(1+\gamma)^{2}}
and for some
\theta^{0}=\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\pi^{0}=\pi_{0}\in\Pi_{\mu}
, let
\{\theta^{n},\pi^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the iterates for Algorithm

\displaystyle|\theta^{n+1}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\theta_{0}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}\gamma^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}\sup_{0\leq r\leq n}\mathrm{K}_{r}^{2}+\frac{|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}.

See Appendix
C.1

Lemma

depends linearly on

On this note, a direct corollary of Lemma

Corollary 3.2

Let Assumption

5.
Conclusion and future directions

In this work, we study stability and convergence of a fundamental actor-critic algorithm for entropy regularized MDPs on general state-action spaces under
Q^{\pi}
-realisability with linear function approximation.
In the algorithm the policy is updated through mirror descent and the critic parameters via TD learning.
We provide sufficient, but restrictive, conditions guaranteeing stability and linear convergence for the single-step variant.
For the multi-step variant we remove the restrictive assumption and by appropriately controlling the number of TD updates per policy update, we establish sublinear and, under an additional concentrability assumption, linear convergence rates.
To the best of our knowledge, these results address a significant theoretical gap for actor–critic methods in entropy-regularised MDPs.
To focus on the algorithm’s behaviour in general spaces, we analyse the population setting in which all integrals are evaluated exactly.
Extending the analysis to the sample-based setting remains an important direction for future work.

\lambda>0
and once again adding and subtracting the true advantage function, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n})(s)-\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{0})(s)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{A}A^{\pi^{k}}_{\tau}(s,a)(\pi^{*}-\pi^{k})(da|s)-\lambda(V^{{n}}-V^{0})(s)

(75)

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{A}\left(A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})-A^{\pi^{k}}_{\tau}(s,a)\right)(\pi^{*}-\pi^{k})(da|s)+\frac{2\lambda}{1-\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\theta^{k+1}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}.

for all
s\in S

\displaystyle\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n})(s)-\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{0})(s)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{A}A^{\pi^{k}}_{\tau}(s,a)(\pi^{*}-\pi^{k})(da|s)-\lambda(V^{{n}}-V^{0})(s)+\lambda c(\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\theta^{k+1}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}.

Moreover, using that
(V^{n}(s)-V^{0}(s))\geq(V^{*}(s)-V^{0}(s))
, which holds for all
s\in S
by the definition of
V^{*}
, and integrating over

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n})(s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)-\int_{S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{0})(s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)

(77)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{S}\int_{A}A^{\pi^{k}}_{\tau}(s,a)(\pi^{*}-\pi^{k})(da|s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)-\lambda(V^{*}(\rho)-V^{0}(\rho))+\lambda c(\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\theta^{k+1}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}.

Finally, applying the performance difference lemma on the first term on the right hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n})(s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)-\int_{S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{0})(s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(1-\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left((V^{*}-V^{k})(\rho)-\tau\int_{S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{k})(s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)\right)-\lambda(V^{*}(\rho)-V^{0}(\rho))

(78)

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda c(\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\theta^{k+1}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}.

Rearranging, dropping negative terms from the right hand side and using that the minimum of a sequence is less than or equal to the average, for all
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(S)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

(79)

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq r\leq n-1}V^{\pi^{r}}_{\tau}(\rho)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}(\rho)

(80)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda(1-\gamma)n}\Bigg(\int_{S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{0})(s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)+\lambda\left(V^{0}(\rho)-V^{*}(\rho)\right)+\lambda c(\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\theta^{k+1}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}\Bigg).

which is exactly (

|l_{n}|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}\leq\sup_{0\leq r\leq n}|l_{r}|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}:=\mathrm{L}_{n}
. Now to upper bound
Q^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}\in B_{b}(S\times A)
, by definition it holds that

(204)

\displaystyle Q^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}(s,a)=c(s,a)+\gamma\int_{S}V^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}(s^{\prime})P(ds^{\prime}|s,a),

for all
s\in S
and
a\in A
. Taking the
|\cdot|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}
and rearranging, it holds that

(205)

\displaystyle\left|Q^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}

\displaystyle\leq|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}+\gamma\left|V^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}.

Now by Lemma
4.3
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(206)

V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}(s)\leq V^{\pi^{n+1}}_{\tau}(s)\leq V^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}(s)+\frac{2e^{-\frac{M(n)h\Gamma}{2}}}{1-\gamma}|\theta^{n}-\theta_{\pi^{n}}|_{2}.

Recursively applying the upper bound (
206
), for all
s\in S
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

(207)

V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}(s)\leq V^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}(s)\leq V^{\pi^{0}}_{\tau}(s)+\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}|\theta^{k}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}.

Taking the
|\cdot|_{B_{b}(S)}
norm on both sides it also holds that

(208)

\displaystyle\left|V^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left|V^{\pi^{0}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}+\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}|\theta^{k}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2},\left|V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}\right\}

(209)

\displaystyle\leq\left|V^{\pi^{0}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}+\left|V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}+\frac{2}{1-\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}|\theta^{k}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}.

Substituting this into (
205
), for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

(210)

\displaystyle\left|Q^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}

\displaystyle\leq|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}+\gamma\left|V^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}

(211)

\displaystyle\leq|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}+\gamma\left|V^{\pi^{0}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}+\gamma\left|V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}+\frac{2\gamma}{1-\gamma}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}|\theta^{k}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}.

Let
\alpha_{1}:=|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}+\gamma\left|V^{\pi^{0}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}+\gamma\left|V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}
and
c(\gamma)=\max\left\{1,\frac{2\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right\}
. Substituting (
211
) into (
203
), we have

(212)

\displaystyle|l_{n+1}|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\tau\lambda)\mathrm{L}_{n}+2\lambda\left(c(\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}|\theta^{k}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}+\alpha_{1}\right).

By the triangle inequality it holds that
|\theta^{k}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}\leq|\theta^{k}|_{2}+|\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then by Lemma
3.1

k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

(213)

\displaystyle|\theta^{k}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\theta_{0}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}\gamma^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}\sup_{0\leq r\leq k}\mathrm{K}_{r}^{2}+\frac{|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}

(214)

\displaystyle\leq|\theta_{0}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}\gamma^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{L}_{k}^{2}+\frac{|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}

(215)

\displaystyle\leq|\theta_{0}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}\gamma^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}\mathrm{L}_{n}^{2}+\frac{|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}},

B.3
in the second inequality and the fact that
\mathrm{L}_{k}\leq\mathrm{L}_{n}
for all

. Similarly by Lemma
B.6

(216)

\displaystyle|\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\beta}}+\frac{2\tau\gamma}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\beta}}\mathrm{L}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\beta}}+\frac{2\tau\gamma}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\beta}}\mathrm{L}_{n},

where we again used that
\mathrm{L}_{k}\leq\mathrm{L}_{n}
for
k\leq n
. Therefore for all
k\in\left\{0,\ldots,n\right\}
it holds that

(218)

\displaystyle|\theta^{k}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}\,\mathrm{L}_{n}),

with

(219)

\delta_{1}=\left(|\theta_{0}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}^{2}\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{|c|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\beta}},\quad\delta_{2}=2\tau\gamma\left(\frac{\left(3h+\frac{2}{\Gamma}\right)}{\Gamma-3h(1+\gamma)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{2\tau\gamma}{(1-\gamma)\lambda_{\beta}}.

Hence substituting (
218
) into (
212
), we arrive at

(220)

\displaystyle|l_{n+1}|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\tau\lambda)\mathrm{L}_{n}+2\lambda\left(c(\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}|\theta^{k}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}+\alpha_{1}\right)

(221)

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{1}(n)\mathrm{L}_{n}+\beta_{2}(n),

where

(222)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}(n):=(1-\tau\lambda)+2\lambda c(\gamma)\delta_{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}},

(223)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}(n):=2\lambda c(\gamma)\delta_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}+2\lambda\alpha_{1}.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we choose
M(n)
large enough such that

(224)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}(n)\leq 1-\frac{\tau\lambda}{2}.

A direct calculation shows that this is satisfied when

(225)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}\leq\frac{\tau}{4c(\gamma)\delta_{2}}.

Therefore for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
M(n)\geq\frac{4}{h\Gamma}\log(c(n+1))
for some
c>0
. This implies that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(226)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}e^{-\frac{M(k)h\Gamma}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{c^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{c^{2}}\leq\frac{\tau}{4c(\gamma)\delta_{2}},

which yields
c^{2}\geq\frac{8c(\gamma)\delta_{2}}{\tau}
. Therefore with choice of
M(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, (
220
) becomes

(227)

\displaystyle|l_{n+1}|_{B_{b}(S\times A)}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{1}(n)\mathrm{L}_{n}+\beta_{2}(n)

(228)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{\tau\lambda}{2}\right)\mathrm{L}_{n}+\alpha_{3}.

A direct application of Lemma
B.4
yields that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(229)

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{L}_{0}+\frac{\alpha_{3}}{1-\left(1-\frac{\tau\lambda}{2}\right)}

(230)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{L}_{0}+\frac{2\alpha_{3}}{\tau\lambda}.

Finally, after applying Lemma
B.3
and Lemma
B.6
, we conclude the proof.
∎

E.1.
Proof of Theorem
4.5

Proof.

Theorem
B.9
states that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(S)
, we have

(231)

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq r\leq n-1}V^{\pi^{r}}_{\tau}(\rho)-V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}(\rho)

(232)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda(1-\gamma)n}\Bigg(\int_{S}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{0})(s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)+\lambda\left(V^{0}(\rho)-V^{*}(\rho)\right)+\lambda c(\gamma)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|\theta^{k+1}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}|_{2}\Bigg).

with

. We are thus left with controlling the average of the critic errors.
To that end, by Theorem
4.2
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

(233)

\bigl|\theta^{n+1}-\theta_{\pi^{n}}\bigr|_{2}\leq e^{-\frac{M(n)h\Gamma}{2}}\bigl|\theta^{n}-\theta_{\pi^{n}}\bigr|_{2}.

Now by assumption we have that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

M:=M(n)\geq\frac{4}{h\Gamma}\log\!\big(c(n+1)\big)
with
c>0
the constant from Theorem
4.4
. Thus, by Theorem

4.4
and Lemma
B.6

R>0

n\in\mathbb{N}

(234)

\displaystyle\bigl|\theta^{n+1}-\theta_{\pi^{n}}\bigr|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{M(n)h\Gamma}{2}}\bigl|\theta^{n}-\theta_{\pi^{n}}\bigr|_{2}\leq Re^{-\frac{M(n)h\Gamma}{2}}.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, this implies that

(235)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl|\theta^{k+1}-\theta_{\pi^{k}}\bigr|_{2}

Substituting this into (

), we conclude the proof.

E.2.
Proof of Theorem
4.6

Proof.

To ease notation let
V^{n}:=V^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
V^{*}:=V^{\pi^{*}}_{\tau}
. Define
\xi=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}

\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(s)\geq(1-\gamma)
for any
s\in S

(237)

\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(s)=\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi}}(s)\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi}}{\mathrm{d}\rho}(s)\geq(1-\gamma)\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi}}(s).

for any
\pi\in\Pi_{\mu}
and
s\in S

\nu^{n}:=\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{n}}}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}\leq\xi

.
Moreover, recall that by (
195
) for all
s\in S

(238)

\int_{A}A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})(\pi^{n+1}-\pi^{n})(da|s)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{n+1}(\cdot|s)|\pi^{n}(\cdot|s))\leq 0.

\displaystyle\int_{S}\left(\int_{A}A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})(\pi^{n+1}-\pi^{n})(da|s)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{n+1}|\pi^{n})(s)\right)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)

\displaystyle\geq\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{n+1}}}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}\int_{S}\left(\int_{A}A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})(\pi^{n+1}-\pi^{n})(da|s)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{n+1}|\pi^{n})(s)\right)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{n+1}}(ds)

\displaystyle=\nu^{n+1}(1-\gamma)\left(V^{n+1}(\rho)-V^{n}(\rho)\right)

(239)

\displaystyle\qquad+\nu^{n+1}\int_{S}\int_{A}\left(A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})-A^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}(s,a)\right)(\pi^{n+1}-\pi^{n})(da|s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{n+1}}(ds),

where the first inequality is due to the integrand in the

and the final equality is then an application of performance difference. Now identically to (
B
) from the proof of Theorem

, after an application of the Three Point Lemma, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n+1})\left(d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}\right)-\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n})\left(d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S}\int_{A}A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})(\pi^{*}-\pi^{n})(da|s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)

(240)

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\int_{S}\int_{A}A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})(\pi^{n+1}-\pi^{n})(da|s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)+\tau\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{n+1}|\pi^{n})\left(d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}\right)\right).

(242)

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)(V^{*}(\rho)-V^{n}(\rho))-\tau\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n})\left(d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}\right)+\int_{S\times A}\left(A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})-A^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}(s,a)\right)(\pi^{*}-\pi^{n})(da|s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)

where we added and subtract the true advantage function and applied the performance difference lemma on the first term in the second equality.
Now observe that

(243)

\displaystyle\int_{S\times A}A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})(\pi^{*}-\pi^{n})(da|s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}(ds)

(244)

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma)(V^{*}(\rho)-V^{n}(\rho))-\tau\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n})\left(d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}\right)+\int_{S\times A}\left(A(s,a;\theta^{n+1})-A^{\pi^{n}}_{\tau}(s,a)\right)(\pi^{*}-\pi^{n})(da|s)\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{n+1}}}(s)d_{\rho}^{\pi^{n+1}}(ds)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma)(V^{*}(\rho)-V^{n}(\rho))-\tau\operatorname{KL}(\pi^{*}|\pi^{n})\left(d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}\right)+2\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{*}}}{\mathrm{d}d_{\rho}^{\pi^{n+1}}}\right|_{B_{b}(S)}|\theta^{n+1}-\theta_{\pi^{n}}|_{2}

where we performed a change of measure on the second term in the first equality and used Hölder’s inequality along with Assumption

Substituting this into (

Some of our results follow as a corollary to the following general theorem.

Theorem 2.1
(Restatement of Theorem
0.7
)

Let

be an
(\alpha,A,\eta)

\sum_{\psi\in\mathcal{F}_{\rm{light}}}\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)<\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{H}|.

This is not hard to show. Indeed,

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{light}}}\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)

\displaystyle\leq n^{\ell}(6Kp)^{\mathrm{e}(F)}\cdot\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{2^{4}(6K)^{\mathrm{e}(F)}}p^{\mathrm{e}(H)-\mathrm{e}(F)}n^{h-\ell}

\displaystyle<\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{2^{4}}p^{\mathrm{e}(H)}n^{h}\leq\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{H}|.

\sum_{\psi\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{heavy}}}\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(\psi)\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{H}|
.

Theorem 2.5

Let

3
Zarankiewicz numbers via edge-gluing

Analogous to the Turán number, the
signed Zarankiewicz number

z(m,n,H)
of a bipartite graph
H=(H_{1},H_{2})
is the maximum number of edges in a signed bipartite host graph
G=(G_{1},G_{2})
such that
v(G_{1})=m
,
v(G_{2})=n
, and
G
has no signed copy of
H
. Observe that
2\cdot\max_{m_{1}+n_{1}=n}z(m_{1},n_{1},H)\geq\mathrm{ex}(n,H)
, hence any upper bound for
z(m,n,H)
depending only on
m,n
effectively provides an asymptotic upper bound for the Turán number for
H
.

First, we need a lemma to obtain a rough count of copies of
H
inside a host graph whose edge number is a constant multiple of the Zarankiewicz number of
H
.

Lemma 3.1

Let
K\geq 4
and
H
be a bipartite graph. If a bipartite graph
G
has at least
K\cdot z(m,n,H)
many edges with
K\geq 4
, then
G
contains at least
\left(\frac{K}{2}\right)^{\mathrm{e}(H)}\cdot z(m,n,H)
many copies of
H
.

Proof:

Let
z=z(m,n,H)
. Observe that each subgraph induced on
2z
edges contains at least
z
many copies of
H
. Counting all copies of
H
in all such subgraphs, we obtain that
G
has at least

\frac{\binom{Kz}{2z}z}{\binom{Kz-\mathrm{e}(H)}{2z-\mathrm{e}(H)}}\geq\left(\frac{K}{2}\right)^{\mathrm{e}(H)}\cdot z

many copies of
H
in
G
.

\square

Lemma 3.2

Let
q_{1},q_{2}
be a real numbers and
m,n
be integers such that
0<q_{1},q_{2}\leq 1
and
q_{1}n,q_{2}m
are integers. If
H
is a connected graph, then

z(m,n,H)\geq\frac{1}{2\max\{q_{1},q_{2}\}}z(q_{1}m,q_{2}n,H).

Proof:

Take
\lfloor\frac{1}{\max\{q_{1},q_{2}\}}\rfloor
disjoint copies of a graph
G
on
q_{1}m,q_{2}n
vertices with no copies of
H
and
z(q_{1}m,q_{2}n,H)
edges. Clearly, this graph has no copy of
H
and since
q\leq 1
, at least
\frac{1}{2\max\{q_{1},q_{2}\}}\cdot z(q_{1}m,q_{2}n,H)
edges. Adding sufficiently many isolated vertices gives a graph on
m,n
vertices with no copy of
H
and has at least
\frac{1}{2\max\{q_{1},q_{2}\}}\cdot z(q_{1}m,q_{2}n,H)
many edges.

\square

Lemma 3.3

Let
H=(H_{1},H_{2})
be a bipartite graph on
h
vertices and
\ell\geq 2
edges such that
v(H_{1})=h_{1}
,
v(H_{2})=h_{2}
, with no isolated vertices, and let
F
be a fixed edge of
H
. Given constants
A,\varepsilon,\gamma
such that
\frac{2}{A}\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
and
\frac{2}{A}\leq\gamma\leq 1
, the following holds. If
G=(G_{1},G_{2})
is a bipartite graph such that the size of both parts
v(G_{1})=m
,
v(G_{2})=n
are sufficiently large and
\mathrm{e}(G)=(1+\frac{2}{\varepsilon})\cdot K\cdot z(m,n,H)
for a constant

K\geq\max\{16\left(\left(1+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\right)A\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}\cdot\left(\frac{\varepsilon\gamma}{16h(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}\right)^{\frac{h-2\ell}{\ell-1}+1},\frac{512h(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})}{\varepsilon\gamma}\},

then there is a family
\mathcal{H}
of signed copies of
H
satisfying the following properties:

1.

|\mathcal{H}|\geq A\mathrm{e}(G).

2.

For all
f\in E(G)
and all
v\not\in V(f)
,
\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(f,v)\leq\gamma A.

3.

For any edge
f\in E(G)
,
\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(f)\leq(1+\varepsilon)A.

Proof:

The proof will be similar to that of Lemma
1.5
. We assume that
|\mathcal{H}|<A\mathrm{e}(G)
and show that we could recruit a new signed copy of
H
to
\mathcal{H}
while maintaining the desired properties. Let
B
be the collection of edges of
G
such that
\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(f)\geq(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})A
.

Claim 3.4

|B|\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}(G)}{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}
.

Proof:

Let
\xi
be the count of pairs
(\varphi,f)
with
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
and
f\in E(G)
with
\varphi(F)=f
. Then we have

(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})A|B|\leq\xi\leq|\mathcal{H}|.

Combining the two bounds gives the desired upper bound for
|B|
.

\square

Delete edges of
B
in
G
, and let
G^{\prime}=(G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2})
be the remaining graph. Denote
z=z(m,n,H)
. We have
\mathrm{e}(G^{\prime})\geq\frac{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}{1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\mathrm{e}(G)\geq Kz.
Let
S
be the collection of configurations
(f,v)
such that
f\in E(G)
,
v\notin V(f)
, and
\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(f,v)\geq\frac{1}{2}\gamma A
.

Claim 3.5

|S|\leq 2\frac{h}{\gamma}\mathrm{e}(G)
.

Proof:

Let
\mu
be the count of pairs
(\varphi,(f,v))
such that
\varphi(F)=f
and
v\in V(\varphi(H))
with
\varphi\in S
. Then,

\frac{1}{2}\gamma A|S|\leq\mu\leq|\mathcal{H}|h.

Combining the two bounds gives the desired result.

\square

Let
0<q_{1},q_{2}\leq 1
to be determined later. Let
q_{\max}=\max\{q_{1},q_{2}\}
. For every subset
W=(W_{1},W_{2})
of
V(G^{\prime})
such that
v(W_{1})=q_{1}m
,
v(W_{2})=q_{2}n
, let
X(W)
be the number of
(f,v)\in S
with
f,v
both in
G^{\prime}[W]
. Let
Y(W)
be the graph formed from
G^{\prime}[W]
by removing
f
for every pair
(f,v)\in S
with
f\subseteq W
and
v\in W
. Let
\mathcal{Y}
be the collection of graphs
Y(W)
for every
W
.

Let
W_{0}=(W_{0,1},W_{0,2})
be a random subset of
G^{\prime}
such that
v(W_{0,1})=q_{1}m
,
v(W_{0,2})=q_{2}n
,

, and
W_{0,2}\subseteq G_{2}
chosen with uniform probability from the sample space of all such subsets. Let

and

.

Thus, we have that
\mbox{\msbm E}[\mathrm{e}(G^{\prime}[W_{0}])]=q_{1}q_{2}\mathrm{e}(G^{\prime}).
For

\displaystyle\mbox{\msbm E}[X_{0}]

\displaystyle=|S_{1}|\frac{\binom{m-2}{q_{1}m-2}\binom{n-1}{q_{2}n-1}}{\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}}+|S_{2}|\frac{\binom{m-1}{q_{1}m-1}\binom{n-2}{q_{2}n-2}}{\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}}

\displaystyle\leq q_{1}q_{2}(|S_{1}|q_{1}+|S_{2}|q_{2})

\displaystyle\leq q_{1}q_{2}q_{max}\frac{2h}{\gamma}\mathrm{e}(G).

Then,

\mbox{\msbm E}[\mathrm{e}(Y_{0})]\geq q_{1}q_{2}\mathrm{e}(G^{\prime})-2q_{1}q_{2}q_{max}\frac{h}{\gamma}\mathrm{e}(G)\geq q_{1}q_{2}\mathrm{e}(G^{\prime})(1-4\frac{(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})hq_{max}}{\varepsilon\gamma}).

Now, for

q_{1}q_{2}\mathrm{e}(G^{\prime})(1-4\frac{(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})hq_{max}}{\varepsilon\gamma})\geq\frac{1}{2}q_{1}q_{2}Kz\geq 8z^{\prime}.

Let
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
be the collection of

-subgraphs

with at least

many edges, and let

be the collection of those with less edges. Note by definition
\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{e}(Y)=\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}\mbox{\msbm E}[\mathrm{e}(Y_{0})]
. But then by Lemma

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{2}}\mathrm{e}(Y)

\displaystyle\leq 4\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}q_{1}q_{2}Kz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{e}(Y).

Therefore,
\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\mathrm{e}(Y)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{e}(Y)
. So by Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
, and Jensen’s inequality, the count of copies of

that avoid

\displaystyle\mu\cdot\binom{m-h_{1}}{q_{1}m-h_{1}}\binom{n-h_{2}}{q_{2}n-h_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}(Y)}{2z^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\cdot z^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{z^{\prime}}{(2z^{\prime})^{\ell}}\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}\left(\frac{\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\mathrm{e}(Y)}{\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{z^{\prime}}{(2z^{\prime})^{\ell}}\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{e}(Y)}{\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}}\right)^{\ell}

But notice that
\sum_{Y\in\mathcal{Y}}\mathrm{e}(Y)\geq\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}\mbox{\msbm E}[\mathrm{e}(Y_{0})]\geq\frac{1}{2}\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}q_{1}q_{2}Kz
, so we obtain

\mu\geq z^{\prime}\left(\frac{q_{1}q_{2}Kz}{8z^{\prime}}\right)^{\ell}\cdot\frac{\binom{m}{q_{1}m}\binom{n}{q_{2}n}}{\binom{m-h_{1}}{q_{1}m-h_{1}}\binom{n-h_{2}}{q_{2}n-h_{2}}}.

Then by Lemma

,

\mu\geq\left(\frac{K}{8}\right)^{\ell}q_{1}^{\ell-h_{1}}q_{2}^{\ell-h_{2}}z^{\ell}(z^{\prime})^{1-\ell}\geq A\left(1+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\right)Kz

as long as

K\geq 8\left(\left(1+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot A\right)^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}\left(\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}\right)q_{1}^{\frac{h_{1}-\ell}{\ell-1}}q_{2}^{\frac{h_{2}-\ell}{\ell-1}}.

Yet, this is guaranteed by the hypothesis

and Lemma

.

This tells us that one new copy of

could be recruited to
\mathcal{H}
while preserving the necessary conditions. To see this, observe that for

-members the degree in

. Likewise, the degree of

\square

.

Theorem 3.6

Let
H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{t}
be signed bipartite graphs, and let
\{f_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq t}
be a family of edges such that
f_{i}\in H_{i}
for each
i
. Let
H^{*}
be the graph formed by gluing
H_{i}
’s along
f_{i}
’s in the unique way preserving the signs. Then, for all
m,n
,

z(m,n,H^{*})=\Theta(\max_{i\in[t]}z(m,n,H_{i})).

Firstly, observe that every
H_{i}
is a subgraph of
H^{*}
. Thus, monotonicity implies that
z(m,n,H^{*})\geq z(m,n,H_{i})
for all
i
. It remains to prove that

z(m,n,H^{*})=O(\max_{i\in[t]}z(m,n,H_{i})).

Let
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{8t}
and
\gamma=\frac{\varepsilon}{8t\max_{i\in[t]}v(H_{i})}
. Let
A=\frac{4}{\gamma}
. Now, apply Lemma
3.3
to obtain families
\mathcal{H}_{i}
’s of copies of respective
H_{i}
’s in
G
with respect to
A,\varepsilon,\gamma
. We clean the families
\mathcal{H}_{i}
’s according to the following rule:

For every edge
f
in
G
, if there exists an
i\in[t]
such that the number of
\mathcal{H}_{i}
-members

satisfying
\varphi(f_{i})=f
is less than
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}A
, then remove from all families
\mathcal{H}_{i}
’s the members
\varphi:H_{i}\rightarrow G
with
\varphi(f_{i})=f
. We claim that every family
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is non-empty when the process terminates.

For each
\mathcal{H}_{i}
, let
x_{i}
be the number of edges in
G
which has low degree in
\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Observe that

A\mathrm{e}(G)\leq(1+\varepsilon)A(e(G)-x_{i})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}Ax_{i}.

Indeed, every
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
sends

either to a high-degree edge or a low-degree edge. The former is upper bounded by the first term, and the latter is upper bounded by the second. Therefore

. Summing over

, no more than
\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)At\cdot\mathrm{e}(G)
members are removed from each
\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Denote the family cleaned from

to be

. Thus, as
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{8t}
,
|\mathcal{H}_{i}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}A\mathrm{e}(G)
.

In particular, there exists an edge

whose degree is at least
\varepsilon A
in every family
\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Fix such an edge

. To find a signed copy of
H^{*}
and finish the proof, it suffices to find a collection
\psi_{i}:H_{i}\rightarrow G
, one from each
\mathcal{H}_{i}
, such that
\psi_{i}(f_{i})=f
and whose images in

intersect only at

. Fix any copy of

containing

. Now, the number of

containing
f

is no more than

, so there is an

which is vertex-disjoint from the chosen

except at

. We continue until the last step. Since the number of copies of

who contains

and intersect the previously chosen

’s outside

\gamma At\cdot\max_{i}v(H_{i})\leq\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon A
by construction of the

’s, there is a choice of such copy of

to form a copy of

. This completes the proof.

Strengthening Causal Interpretation: Home Blood Pressure Phenotypes and Cardiovascular Remodeling in Hemodialysis
Authors: Wenlong Li, Weikai Dong
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

We have recently been quite interested in reviewing the manuscript entitled “Home Blood Pressure Phenotypes and Cardiovascular Remodeling in Hemodialysis” written by Chen et al.1 The results of this study can provide many clinical reference ideas for home blood pressure (BP) monitoring, and also examine the degree of misclassification when using dialysis unit values. However, the results may be influenced by some limitations of the interpretation method.

Firstly, the existing cross-sectional research design cannot effectively determine the causal relationship between BP phenotype and cardiovascular remodeling. The BP load and vascular status during the dialysis interval have not been fully evaluated in terms of their temporal changes. Prior evidence suggests that home BP monitoring in hemodialysis patients may better reflect long-term hemodynamic exposure and support repeated longitudinal assessment strategies.2

Secondly, unaccounted environmental and physiological stressors could still be impacting the study. Patients who undergo hemodialysis are quite sensitive to extreme environmental and temperature conditions which can lead to cardiovascular events and death.3 Including exposure to seasons, hydration and thermal responses may allow for improved risk adjustment and strengthened mechanistic interpretation.

Third, the study’s primary focus was on conventional BP classifications, and they assessed only limited genetic and vascular heterogeneity. Prior evidence have also suggested that APOL1 risk variants were associated with distinct differences in subclinical cardiovascular remodeling profiles amongst hemodialysis patients.4 This includes left ventricular hypertrophy and coronary artery calcification differences. Using genetic or ethnicity-related profiles of cardiovascular risk in stratified analyses may increase precision and external validity.

Lastly, methods of dialysis and the strength of treatment were not specifically defined as much as the above-mentioned parameters. A previous study investigated the long-term peridialytic BP patterns by hemodialysis modality and related hemodynamic characteristics.5 Future studies conducted in multiple centers which measure the serial home BP in coordination with the dialysis modality as well as medication adherence may have a better prognosis. We provide suggestions to improve an already exceptional article and look forward to future insightful publications from these authors.

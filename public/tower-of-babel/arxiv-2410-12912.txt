A measure on the moduli space of super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures
Ron Donagi, Nadia Ott

Abstract




1
Introduction

In perturbative string theory, an essential ingredient is a certain measure on the ordinary moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g}
of curves. At a point corresponding to a curve
C
, the Mumford isomorphism relates the determinant of the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
to a power of the determinant of cohomology of the canonical bundle
\omega_{C}
on the curve. The cohomology of the canonical bundle
\omega_{C}
, and therefore also the determinant of this cohomology, has a natural Hermitian metric given by integration on
C
, and via the Mumford isomorphism this determines a measure on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. We can think of this Hermitian metric as a pairing between a cohomology on
C
and the corresponding cohomology on the complex-conjugate curve
\overline{C}
.

An equivalent way of expressing this is in terms of the period map. This sends
H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})
to
H^{1}(C,\mathbb{C})
. So the standard intersection form on
H^{1}(C,\mathbb{C})
pulls back to a Hermitian metric on
H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})
and therefore also on its determinant.

In superstring theory, this measure is generalized to the
supermeasure
, a measure on the moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{g}
of genus
g
super Riemann surfaces. The construction uses super versions of the previous ingredients: a super Mumford isomorphism, reviewed below
1.1
, and a Hermitian metric on the determinant of cohomology on the supercurve, which again can be interpreted in terms of a super period map. This provides a holomorphic measure on an open subset (see below) of the product of

\mathfrak{M}_{g}
with an appropriate complex-conjugate version
\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g}}
.
In a final step, this is restricted to the integration cycle needed in superstring theory. We will say a few words about this below.

Unlike the classical period matrix, the normalized super period matrix is in general only meromorphic along the bad locus. The unnormalized period morphism used in this paper is holomorphic as a morphism of sheaves, but along the bad locus the relevant cohomology jumps and the period image is no longer a vector bundle of the generic rank. Witten conjectured that the supermeasure extends smoothly across this locus for genus
\leq 11
. This was rigorously proved in
[
FKP19
]
. Very recently, Deligne extended this result to all genera, as reported in

[
FKP24
]
.

For further applications to perturbative superstring theory, measures on the moduli spaces of super Riemann surfaces with punctures are needed. There are two types of such punctures: Neveu-Schwarz (NS) and Ramond. The case of Neveu-Schwarz punctures, described in
[
Wit15a
]
and Appendix
B
of this paper, is relatively straightforward since the moduli space of super Riemann surfaces with Neveu-Schwarz punctures admits a projection onto the moduli space of unpunctured super Riemann surfaces.

The situation is very different though for Ramond punctures: Ramond punctures are divisors along which the superconformal structure degenerates, and there is no sense in which they can be ”forgotten” to produce a projection onto the unpunctured supermoduli space.

In this work we define a measure on the moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
of super Riemann surfaces with
2r
Ramond punctures (the number of such punctures is always even), generalizing the supermeasure on the unpunctured supermoduli space. The definition of the measure on
\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
uses the adaptation to the Ramond case of the familiar ingredients: the Mumford isomorphism, described in Appendix C of
[
Wit15a
]
,
and a generalization of the super period map to
\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
, defined in Section
3
of this paper. As in the unpunctured case, we define this measure initially away from a certain bad locus. We are able to prove that, for
r\geq 2
, it extends smoothly across the bad locus. The basic reason that the measure extends across the bad locus is Hartogs-like: for
r\geq 2
we show that the bad locus has codimension
\geq 2|0
. The case
r=0
is Deligne’s result, and the case
r=1
remains open.

The measure we construct is defined a priori on an open
neighborhood of a Zariski-open subset of the diagonal. Our main result, Theorem
7.2
, is that for
g\geq 2
and
r>1
it extends to an open neighborhood of the entire diagonal. The extended measure is allowed to have zeros; indeed, by Theorem
4.2
together with Proposition
7.4
, the extended measure necessarily has zeros along the bad locus whenever
r>2
and the bad locus is nonempty.

In the product
\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}
, physicists consider the quasidiagonal - roughly, the locus of pairs
X,\widetilde{X}
where the bosonic curves underlying
X,\widetilde{X}
are complex conjugates of each other. This contains the diagonal, but is strictly bigger. The integration cycle (for type II superstring theory) is a thickening of this quasidiagonal. It is not clear whether our measure extends to an open neighborhood of the quasidiagonal.

In the remainder of this Introduction we outline our argument in more detail.

1.1
Super Riemann surfaces and their moduli

We will work mostly with super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures. Their definitions and elementary properties are reviewed in Section
2
. Throughout the body of the paper we assume
g\geq 2
. The moduli of smooth super Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
2r
Ramond punctures is a smooth Deligne–Mumford superstack
\mathfrak{M}=\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}

[
FKP20
]
. (The genus-zero case is treated in
[
OV23
]
, but is not needed below.) For simplicity, we refer to this superstack as the moduli space. A morphism
T\to\mathfrak{M}
from a superscheme
T
determines a family over
T
of genus
g
super Riemann surfaces with Ramond divisor
R
consisting of
2r
Ramond punctures, each supported on an irreducible component
R_{i}
. There is a universal family
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathfrak{M}
with the property that, for every morphism
T\to\mathfrak{M}
, the pullback
\pi_{T}:\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathfrak{M}}T\to T

T

\mathfrak{M}
we mean a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}\to\mathfrak{M}
.

Near each irreducible component
R_{i}
of the Ramond divisor, we can choose local coordinates
(z,\theta)
on
X
such that
R
is locally defined by

, and the distribution
\mathcal{D}
, which is maximally non-integrable away from
R

D_{\theta}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+z\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
. Such coordinates are called
superconformal
. For a family of super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures
X\to T
, superconformal coordinates exist étale locally on
X

[
BR21
, Proposition 3.6]
.

If
(z^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
is another set of superconformal coordinates near a Ramond puncture, then

\theta^{\prime}=\pm(\theta+\tau)\mod z,

(1)

for some odd section
\tau
on the base superscheme
T

[
Wit15b
]

\tau
is called an
orientation
. We let

\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}

denote the moduli space of super Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
2r

oriented
Ramond punctures. It is a covering of
\mathfrak{M}
of degree
2^{2r}
.

In addition to the Berezinian, or dualizing sheaf,
\omega_{X}
, we will use the extended Berezinian sheaf
\omega^{\prime}_{X}
defined in
[
Wit15b
]
. By definition, sections of
\omega^{\prime}_{X}
are sections of
\omega_{X}(R)
whose residue along the Ramond divisor is constant. Both
\omega_{X}
and
\omega^{\prime}_{X}
are canonically defined for every family of super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures. In particular, they are defined on the pullback of the universal curve to any étale cover of
\mathcal{X}
, and these local constructions agree on overlaps. Hence they descend to sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
, and therefore pull back to sheaves on any family of super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures. We define the relative version of the extended Berezinian sheaf in Section
3

A family of super Riemann surfaces of genus
g

2r

1.2
Mumford isomorphisms

The original Mumford isomorphism (Theorem 5.10 in
[
Mum77
]
) is

L_{2}\cong{L_{1}}^{13},

where
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{M}
denotes the universal curve over the moduli space of curves of genus
\geq 2
,
\omega:=\omega_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{M}}
, and

L_{i}:=\det R\pi_{*}(\omega^{\otimes i})
. It is often used together with the identification of
R\pi_{*}(\omega^{\otimes 2})
with the cotangent bundle of
\mathcal{M}
, hence of
L_{2}
with the canonical bundle of
\mathcal{M}
.

The super Mumford isomorphism
[
Vor88
,
RSV89
]
is

\operatorname{Ber}_{3}\cong{\operatorname{Ber}_{1}}^{5},

(3)

where now
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathfrak{M}
denotes the universal supercurve over supermoduli,

\omega:=\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}
is the (relative) dualizing sheaf, and

{\operatorname{Ber}}_{i}:=\operatorname{Ber}R\pi_{*}(\omega^{\otimes i}).

(4)

This is often used together with the identification of
R\pi_{*}(\omega^{\otimes 3})
with the cotangent bundle of
\mathfrak{M}
, hence of

Ber_{3}
with the dualizing sheaf of
\mathfrak{M}
.

On
\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
, i.e. in the presence of Ramond punctures, the
cotangent theory involves the twist by twice the Ramond divisor. More
precisely, Serre duality identifies the canonical line of supermoduli with

\omega_{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}\cong\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}^{3}(2R)\right).

The twist by
2R
does not change the Berezinian of cohomology. Indeed,
there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}^{3}\longrightarrow\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}^{3}(2R)\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}_{R}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{J}_{R}
is the sheaf of polar parts along the Ramond divisor.
Appendix C of
[
Wit15a
]
gives a canonical
trivialization

\operatorname{Ber}(R\pi_{*}\mathcal{J}_{R})\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}}.

Consequently

\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}^{3}(2R)\right)\cong\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}^{3}\right).

Combining this with the super Mumford isomorphism gives

\omega_{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}\cong\operatorname{Ber}^{5}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}\right).

(5)

1.3
The pairing

The symmetry hinted at by equation (
2
) is not accidental.
The Berezinians of cohomology of
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are actually isomorphic, cf. Theorem
5.1
:
What is changing in the cohomology as we move from
\omega
to
\omega^{\prime}
is that an
(r+\epsilon)
-dimensional piece disappears from the odd part of
H^{1}
, and is replaced by its dual vector space that appears in the odd part of
H^{0}
. This has no effect on the Berezinian.

So instead of constructing a pairing over supermoduli involving

{\operatorname{Ber}}:=\operatorname{Ber}R\pi_{*}(\omega),

as in (
4
), we can
construct a pairing involving the isomorphic

{\operatorname{Ber}^{\prime}}:=\operatorname{Ber}R\pi_{*}(\omega^{\prime}).
So our task is to construct a natural pairing on
\operatorname{Ber}R\pi_{*}(\omega^{\prime}).

We start by defining some local systems on supermoduli space
\mathfrak{M}:=\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
, where we will use
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathfrak{M}
to denote the universal family with Ramond divisor
R
. One is

\Lambda_{0}:=R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}.

This has rank
2g|0
and carries the standard
\mathbb{Z}
-valued symplectic pairing corresponding to the intersection pairing on the underlying curve
C
. The pairing is invariant under the modular group.

Another local system comes from the sheaf
\mathcal{Z}_{R/\mathfrak{M}}^{1}
of relative closed-one forms on
R
. Let
i:R\to\mathcal{X}
denote the natural inclusion. There is a natural local system
\Lambda_{1}
of free abelian groups of rank
0|2r
on
\mathfrak{M}
, carrying a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear pairing
J_{1}:\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{1}\to\mathbb{Z}
. This pairing is invariant under the group
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2r}
of changes of
orientation on
R
, cf. Section
3
. Since
R
is smooth and finite of degree
2r
over
\mathfrak{M}
,
\pi_{*}i_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/\mathfrak{M}}^{1}
is locally free of rank
0|2r
, and we show in Section
2.1
,
that
\pi_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R}^{1}\cong\Lambda_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}
.

We set

\Lambda:=\Lambda_{0}\oplus\Lambda_{1},\qquad\Lambda_{\mathfrak{M}}:=\Lambda\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}}

This is a local system of free abelian groups of rank
2g|2r
with a non-degenerate pairing,

J:=J_{0}\oplus J_{1}:\Lambda\times\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z},\qquad J_{\mathfrak{M}}:=J\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}}

where
J_{0}
is the alternating intersection pairing on the even part
\Lambda_{0}
, and
J_{1}
is the symmetric pairing on the odd part
\Lambda_{1}
.

We define the super period map
P_{X/T}
for families
X\to T
of super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures in Section
3
. For the universal family
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathfrak{M}
, the super period map is

P:\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}^{\prime}\to\Lambda\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}}.

(6)

The map
P_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}
is the direct sum of two maps

P^{+}:\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}^{\prime}\to\Lambda_{0}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{{\mathfrak{M}}}

P^{-}:\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}}^{\prime}\to\Lambda_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}}

which we call the even and odd period maps, respectively. Both maps are grading-preserving.

The
2g
even periods of a section of
\pi_{*}\omega^{\prime}
are its (Berezin)-integrals over a basis of
1
-cycles in cohomology, while its
2r
odd periods are its residues along the
2r
Ramond punctures.

We show in Theorem
3.3
that for any super Riemann surface
X
over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
such that
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}\to\mathfrak{M}
is in the good locus, its super period map
P_{X}:H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})\to\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
is an injective morphism of vector bundles whose image
\operatorname{im}(P_{X})
is
J
-Lagrangian.
Furthermore, since
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of the lattice
\Lambda
, we can define a complex conjugate map
\lambda\otimes v\to\lambda\otimes\overline{v}
, and thus also take the complex conjugate of subspaces in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
. In Theorem
3.3
, we show the restriction of
J
to
\operatorname{im}(P)\times\overline{\operatorname{im}(P_{X})}
is non-degenerate.

1.4
The measure

Throughout this section we set
\mathfrak{M}:=\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
.
We want to define the pairing:

p_{1}^{*}\pi_{*}\omega^{\prime}\otimes p_{2}^{*}\overline{\pi_{*}\omega^{\prime}}\overset{p_{1}^{*}P\otimes p_{2}^{*}\overline{P}}{\xhookrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}p_{1}^{-1}\Lambda\otimes p_{2}^{-1}\Lambda\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}\overset{J\otimes 1}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}.

(7)

In order for the second map to make sense, we need to be able to identify
p_{1}^{-1}\Lambda_{1}
and
p_{2}^{-1}\Lambda_{1}
. We do this by restricting everything in (
7
) to an open neighborhood
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
of the diagonal
\Delta(\mathfrak{M})\subset\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}
.

We want to take the Berezinian of the resulting pairing. In order to do that, we need the pairing to be non-degenerate. We first prove this away from the bad locus (cf. Corollary
6.3
). Or more precisely, away from the bad diagonal,
\Delta(\mathcal{B})\subset\Delta(\mathfrak{M})
.
Combining the pairing on the Berezinian of (
7
) with the super Mumford isomorphism,
\omega_{\mathfrak{M}}=\operatorname{Ber}^{5}(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/\mathfrak{M}})
, and with the identification of
\operatorname{Ber}^{5}(R\pi_{*}\omega^{\prime})
with
\operatorname{Ber}^{5}(R\pi_{*}\omega)
, gives the measure on the complement of the bad diagonal.

In Theorem
4.1
we show that the bad locus has complex codimension at least
2
. In Section
7
we do not attempt to extend the period image as a vector bundle. Instead, we extend directly the inverse Berezinian section which defines the measure. The extension is first obtained across the degeneracy locus of the period pairing by a local block-matrix formula for the Berezinian, and is then extended across the two factors of the bad locus by Hartogs’ theorem. This proves that the measure extends smoothly to an open neighborhood containing the entire diagonal. The extension need not be nowhere vanishing.

1.5
Further comments

What we call Ramond punctures corresponds, in the superstring literature, to Ramond-Ramond punctures, indicating that both
\mathfrak{M}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{M}}
parametrize super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures. Other possibilities are NS-NS punctures and the mixed NS-R and R-NS punctures. We use the simplified terminology since R-R punctures are the only ones we consider here.

It is not clear how or if the measure constructed in this paper relates to the one needed in perturbative superstring theory.
For instance, in the unpunctured case, the correct integration cycle is not the diagonal, but rather a larger subset
\Gamma
of
\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}
called the quasi-diagonal. The quasi-diagonal contains the diagonal, but it is strictly bigger.

In the Ramond case, the quasi-diagonal
\Gamma\subset\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}

parameterizes those pairs of super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures whose bosonic data
(C,D,L),(C^{\prime},D^{\prime},L^{\prime})

are such that
C^{\prime}
is the complex conjugate of
C
,
D^{\prime}
is the complex conjugate of
D
, but
no constraint is imposed on the twisted spin structure
L^{\prime}
.
Over the quasi-diagonal, and hence over a small neighborhood of the quasi-diagonal, the even lattices
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda^{\prime}_{0}
are identified, so the pairing (
7
) is well-defined. To identify the lattices
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}
, we would need to pass to the cover

\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}\times\widetilde{\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}
,
parameterizing pairs
(X,\overline{X})
of super Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
2r

oriented
Ramond punctures. It is a covering of
\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}
of degree
2^{4r}
. Dividing by the simultaneous action of changes-of-orientation on the two factors, we get an intermediate cover

\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}
, of degree
2^{2r}
.

The pull-back of the super period maps
P
and
\overline{P}
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}
and
\widetilde{\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}
are essentially the same as the versions on
\mathfrak{M}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{M}}
, except that

\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}
can now both be identified with the trivial local system
V:=\underline{\mathbb{Z}}^{0|2r}
, and
P_{o}
is composed with the trivialization over the Ramond divisor specified by the orientation:

\widetilde{P_{o}}:\widetilde{\pi_{*}\omega^{\prime}}\to V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}}.

Pulling back all terms in (
7
) to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}\times\widetilde{\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}
or to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}
, and composing with the identification of
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{1}^{\prime}
with
V
, we get a well-defined pairing:

\widetilde{p_{1}^{*}\pi_{*}\omega^{\prime}}\otimes\widetilde{p_{2}^{*}\overline{\pi_{*}\omega^{\prime}}}\overset{\widetilde{p_{1}^{*}P}\otimes\widetilde{p_{2}^{*}\overline{P}}}{\xhookrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}p_{1}^{-1}\Lambda\otimes p_{2}^{-1}\Lambda\otimes\mathcal{O}_{{\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}}}\overset{J\otimes 1}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}}.

(8)

Various issues remain unclear. Is the restriction of this pairing to the quasi-diagonal
\widetilde{\Gamma}
in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}}}
non-degenerate? Does it descend to a neighborhood of the quasi-diagonal in the original

\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}\times\overline{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}
? Is there a natural way to introduce a dependence on the spinor indices of Ramond vertex operators? We hope to return to these questions elsewhere.

Preliminaries: Super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures

Fix
g\geq 2
.

Throughout this paper, we use
supercurve
to mean a smooth, proper, connected superscheme
X
over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
of dimension
1|1
whose bosonic reduction
C:=X_{\operatorname{bos}}
is an ordinary curve. The genus
g
of
X
is the genus of
C
. A family of genus
g
supercurves over a superscheme
T
is a smooth, proper morphism
\pi:X\to T
of relative dimension
1|1
such that each fiber
X_{t}
is a genus
g
supercurve over
\operatorname{Spec}k(t)
.

We will write
X
for any family of supercurves over
T=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
. For any point
t\in T
, we write
k(t)
for the residue field and denote the fiber by
X_{t}
.

We recall that a family of genus
g

super Riemann surfaces
is a family of genus
g
supercurves
X\to T
equipped with a
superconformal structure
, that is, a rank
(0|1)
subbundle
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{T}_{X/T}
of the relative tangent sheaf which is maximally non-integrable in the sense that the composition

\mathcal{D}^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{[\ ,\ ]}\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\to\mathcal{T}_{X/T}/\mathcal{D},

(9)

where
[\ ,\ ]
is the supercommutator, is an isomorphism of locally free sheaves on
X
. We then have the following short exact sequence of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules:

0\longrightarrow{{}}\mathcal{D}\longrightarrow{{}}\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\longrightarrow{{}}\mathcal{D}^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow{{}}0.

(10)

Remark 2.1
.

The above definition of a super Riemann surface is from the algebro-geometric perspective, in which a supercurve is a superscheme of dimension
1|1
. Since the individual supercurves are proper, super GAGA allows us to pass between their algebraic and analytic descriptions. For the (non-proper) smooth moduli stack itself, when analytic arguments are used below we work with its analytification rather than invoking GAGA for the moduli space.

Super Riemann surfaces have two types of punctures: Neveu-Schwarz (NS) punctures and Ramond punctures. Here we will be interested only in Ramond punctures. The case of NS punctures is considered in Appendix
B
.

A
family of genus
g
super Riemann surfaces with
n_{R}
Ramond punctures
is a family of genus
g
supercurves
X\to T
equipped with the following additional data:

1.

A relative effective Cartier divisor
R\subset X
, smooth over
T
, whose bosonic reduction
R_{\operatorname{bos}}\subset C=X_{\operatorname{bos}}
is finite étale of degree
n_{R}
over
T_{\operatorname{bos}}
. The divisor
R
is called the
Ramond divisor
. We assume its components are labeled
R_{1},\dots,R_{n_{R}}
and refer to these as the
Ramond punctures
.

2.

A rank
(0|1)
subbundle
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{T}_{X/T}
such that
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{[\ ,\ ]}\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\to(\mathcal{T}_{X/T}/\mathcal{D})(-R)
is an isomorphism of locally free sheaves on
X
. In this case, the superconformal structure
\mathcal{D}
fits into the following short exact sequence of sheaves on
X
:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\otimes 2}(R)\to 0.

(11)

An important observation is that
\mathcal{D}|_{R}
is naturally identified with the relative tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{R/T}
on
R
.

An isomorphism of super Riemann surfaces with
2r
Ramond punctures from
(X/T,R,\mathcal{D})
to
(X^{\prime}/T,R^{\prime},\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
, called a
superconformal isomorphism
, is an isomorphism of supercurves
\phi:X\cong X^{\prime}
over
T
such that
\phi(R_{i})=R_{i}^{\prime}
for all
1\leq i\leq 2r
, and

d\phi(\mathcal{D})=\phi^{*}\mathcal{D}^{\prime},

where
d\phi:\mathcal{T}_{X/T}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\phi^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X^{\prime}/T}
is the induced isomorphism.

The number
n_{R}
of Ramond punctures is always even, and we will henceforth replace
n_{R}
by
2r
. This fact is well-known and follows from the correspondence with twisted spin curves. We now recall this correspondence, primarily to set up notation that will be used throughout the paper.

A family of genus
g

spin curves with
n_{R}
Ramond punctures
, or twisted spin curves for short, is an ordinary family of genus
g
curves
C\to T
over an ordinary scheme
T
, together with a relative effective Cartier divisor
D\subset C
which is finite étale of degree
n_{R}
over
T
, and a line bundle
L
with a fixed isomorphism

\alpha:L^{\otimes 2}\cong\omega_{C/T}(D).

Note that
\deg(L)=g-1+n_{R}/2
, and hence
n_{R}
must be even.

Now let
X\to T
be a family of super Riemann surfaces with
n_{R}
Ramond punctures, with
T
a purely bosonic scheme, and let
C
denote the underlying bosonic curve. Since
T
contributes no odd coordinates,
J^{\otimes 2}=0
and
J
is a rank
(0|1)
line bundle on
C
. Restricting the sequence (
11
) to
C
gives canonical isomorphisms

J\cong\mathcal{D}|_{C}^{-1},\qquad J^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{D}|_{C}^{-2}\cong\omega_{C/T}(D),

where
D:=R|_{C}
, and all tensor products are over
\mathcal{O}_{C}
. Setting
L:=\Pi J
, the data
(C\to T,D=R|_{C},L)
defines a twisted spin curve over
T
.

A further fundamental fact about super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures is the existence of superconformal coordinates, proved in Lemma 2.6 of
[
FKP20
]
. If
p
is a point of
X
not contained in the Ramond divisor, then this is the standard statement that there are local coordinates
(z,\theta)
in which
\mathcal{D}
is locally generated by the odd vector field

\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

If
p\in R
, then there are local coordinates
(z,\theta)
such that
R
is locally defined by
z=0
and
\mathcal{D}
is generated by

D_{\theta}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+z\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

The coordinates
(z,\theta)
are called
superconformal coordinates
. In the algebro-geometric setting, superconformal coordinates exist étale locally on
X
, and in families after passing to an appropriate étale double cover.

2.1
Orientation on the Ramond divisor

Let
i:R\hookrightarrow X
be the natural inclusion of the Ramond divisor, and let
(z,\theta)
be a pair of superconformal coordinates on
X
such that
R
is locally defined by
z=0
. Then
i^{*}(z)=0
, while

x:=i^{*}(\theta)

is a local odd coordinate on
R
. We refer to a set of global coordinates
(x^{1},\dots,x^{2r})
on
R
as superconformal coordinates if and only if they are obtained by restriction of superconformal coordinates on
X
, and call an automorphism of
R
a superconformal automorphism if it sends superconformal coordinates to superconformal coordinates.

In
[
Wit15b
]
, Witten shows that if
(x^{1},\dots,x^{2r})
are superconformal coordinates on
R
, and
\phi
is a superconformal automorphism of
R
, then

\phi^{*}(x^{i})=\pm(x^{i}+\tau_{i}),

(12)

where
\tau_{i}
is an odd function on the base
T
; in particular,
\tau_{i}=0
if
T
is purely bosonic.

Let
d_{R/T}
denote the relative exterior derivative, and set

\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}:=\ker\left(d_{R/T}:\Omega_{R/T}^{1}\to\Omega_{R/T}^{2}\right).

Since
i_{*}d_{R/T}
is
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{T})
-linear,
i_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}
is a
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{T})
-module on
X
. Note that it is
not
an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.

For each
t\in T
, choose superconformal coordinates
(x^{1},\dots,x^{2r})
on
R_{t}
. Then the differentials

dx^{1},\dots,dx^{2r}

form a basis for
H^{0}(R_{t},\mathcal{Z}_{R_{t}}^{1})
. Indeed, a global one-form on
R_{t}
is locally of the form
v=(a+bx^{i})dx^{i}
for some
a,b\in k(t)
, and
d(v)=0
if and only if
b=0
. Thus,

\operatorname{dim}_{k(t)}H^{0}(R_{t},\mathcal{Z}_{R_{t}}^{1})=0|2r

for all
t\in T
, and
\pi_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}
is locally free of rank
0|2r
on
T
. A local frame for
\pi_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}
can therefore be chosen as the differentials
(dx^{1},\dots,dx^{2r})
of any local set of superconformal coordinates
(x^{1},\dots,x^{2r})
on
R
.

We call any choice of local frame for
\pi_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}
arising from a choice of superconformal coordinates on
R
an
orientation
on
R
, and any such local frame a
local orientation
on
R
.

Choose a local orientation
(dx^{1},\dots,dx^{2r})
, set

V=\mathbb{Z}dx^{1}+\cdots+\mathbb{Z}dx^{2r},

and note that
V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{T}
is locally isomorphic to
\pi_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}
. From (
12
), if
(dx^{\prime 1},\dots,dx^{\prime 2r})
is another local orientation, then

dx^{\prime i}=\pm dx^{i},\qquad\forall\ 1\leq i\leq 2r.

Hence
V
and
V^{\prime}
agree on overlaps, and therefore glue to a locally constant sheaf
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
on
T
. Thus,

\pi_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}\cong V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{T}.

Define a quadratic form

J_{1}:V_{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}

by

J_{1}\left(\sum_{i}n_{i}\,dx^{i}\right)=\sum_{i}n_{i}^{2},\qquad n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},

equivalently
\langle dx^{i},dx^{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
. Since changes of local orientation are given by
dx^{i}\mapsto\pm dx^{i}
, the transition functions lie in
O(2r,\mathbb{Z})
, so this quadratic form is independent of the chosen frame and defines a canonical
\mathbb{Z}
-valued quadratic form on the local system
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a super Riemann surface with
2r
Ramond punctures over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, let
C
denote its underlying bosonic curve,
L
the twisted spin structure, and
D=R|_{C}
.
Then there is a natural identification

Z_{R}^{1}=\Pi L|_{D},\qquad Z_{R}^{1}:=H^{0}(R,\mathcal{Z}_{R}^{1}).

Furthermore, the complexification of
J_{1}
is exactly the residue pairing on
L|_{D}
. For each
p_{i}\in D
, the residue pairing
L_{p_{i}}\otimes L_{p_{i}}\to\mathbb{C}
sends
s\otimes s^{\prime}\in L^{2}=\omega_{C}(D)
to
\operatorname{res}_{p_{i}}(s\otimes s^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{C}\oplus\Pi L
, for any
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
on
X
, we have

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}|_{C}\oplus(\mathcal{M}|_{C}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\Pi L).

Applying this to
\mathcal{D}^{-1}
and using the canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{D}^{-1}|_{C}\cong\Pi L
, we find

\mathcal{D}^{-1}=\mathcal{D}|_{C}^{-1}\oplus(\mathcal{D}|_{C}^{-1}\otimes\Pi L)\cong\omega_{C}(D)\oplus\Pi L,

where
D=R|_{C}
. Restricting further to
R\subset X
and using the identification
\mathcal{D}^{-1}|_{R}=\Omega_{R}^{1}
, we obtain

\Omega_{R}^{1}=\omega_{C}(D)|_{D}\oplus\Pi L|_{D}.

Thus,

H^{0}(R,\Omega_{R}^{1})=H^{0}(D,\omega_{C}(D)|_{D})\oplus\Pi H^{0}(D,L|_{D}).

Since
Z_{R}^{1}
is odd of dimension
0|2r
, we have

Z_{R}^{1}\subseteq\Pi H^{0}(D,L|_{D}).

The right-hand side also has dimension
0|2r
, with one
0|1
contribution from each of the
2r
components of
D
, so the inclusion is an equality. Finally, since
D
is zero-dimensional,

H^{0}(D,L|_{D})=L|_{D},

and therefore

Z_{R}^{1}=\Pi L|_{D},

as claimed.
∎

3
Periods of super Riemann surfaces with Ramond punctures

Let
\pi:X\to T
be a family of genus
g
super Riemann surfaces with
2r
Ramond punctures.

We start by defining the relative extended Berezinian sheaf
\omega_{X/T}^{\prime}
on the family
\pi:X\to T
. From the identifications
\omega_{X/T}=\mathcal{D}^{-1}(-R)
and
\Omega_{R/T}^{1}=\mathcal{D}^{-1}|_{R}
, we get an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\omega_{X/T}\longrightarrow\omega_{X/T}(R)\to i_{*}\Omega_{R/T}^{1}\longrightarrow 0.

where
i:R\to X
is the canonical inclusion of the Ramond divisor, and the second arrow is the adjunction map
\omega_{X/T}(R)\to i_{*}i^{*}\omega_{X/T}(R)=i_{*}\Omega_{R/T}^{1}
.

Let
d_{R/T}:\Omega_{R/T}^{1}\to\Omega_{R/T}^{2}
denote the relative exterior derivative, and set

\omega_{X/T}^{\prime}:=\ker(\omega_{X/T}(R)\to i_{*}\Omega_{R/T}^{1}\overset{i_{*}d_{R/T}}{\longrightarrow}i_{*}\Omega_{R/T}^{2}).

Since both maps are

\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{T})
-linear,
\omega_{X/T}^{\prime}
is sheaf of
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{T})
-modules on
X
. It is
not
as
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module since
i_{*}d_{R/T}
is not
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear. We call
\omega_{X/T}^{\prime}
the
relative Berezinian sheaf
.

Remark 3.1
.

For local superconformal coordinates
(z,\theta)
on
X
in which
R
is defined by
z=0
, a global section of
\omega_{X}^{\prime}
is of the form

\left(g(z)+zf(z)\theta\right)[dz|d\theta]/z.

(13)

From the definition of
\omega^{\prime}
, we have a natural map of
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-modules

\pi_{*}\omega_{X/T}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}i_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}:=\ker(\pi_{*}i_{*}d_{R/T}).

We denote this morphism by
P_{X/T}^{-}
and call it the
odd period map
.

There is also an
even period map

P_{X/T}^{+}:\pi_{*}\omega_{X/T}^{\prime}\longrightarrow R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{T},\qquad s\longmapsto\left(\gamma\mapsto\int_{\gamma^{th}}s|_{X\setminus R}\right)

where we may assume that the
1
-cycle
\gamma\in H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
does not meet the Ramond divisor. This is possible because topologically
|R|
is a finite collection of points.

We can now define the
super period map
to be the direct sum

P_{X/T}:=P_{X/T}^{+}\oplus P_{X/T}^{-}:\pi_{*}\omega_{X/T}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}.

(14)

where

\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}:=\left(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{T}\right)\oplus\pi_{*}i_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}

Lemma 3.2
.

The super period map is grading-preserving.

Proof.

It is enough to prove this for
T=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, where

P_{X}:H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})\to H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})\oplus H^{0}(R,\mathcal{Z}_{R}^{1})\cong\mathbb{C}^{2g|2r}\qquad s\longmapsto(P_{X}^{+}(s),P_{X}^{-}(s)).

This reduces to proving that
P_{X}^{+}(s)=0
for all
s\in H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})^{-}
and
P_{X}^{-}(s)=0
for all
s\in H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})^{+}
.

Start by choosing local superconformal coordinates
(z,\theta)
, and let
e:=([dz|d\theta]/z)
denote the local generator of
\omega_{X}^{\prime}
.
From (
13
), an even global section of
\omega_{X}^{\prime}
is locally of the form
z\theta f(z)\cdot e
in these coordinates, and an odd global section is locally of the form
g(z)\cdot e
.
It is already immediate that the images of odd global sections under the even period map are all zero. Indeed, this follows from the definition of Berezin integration and the fact that the odd global sections are missing the top
\theta
term.

The odd period map is just restriction to the Ramond divisor. Since
R
is locally cut out by
z=0
, and since the even sections of
\omega_{X}^{\prime}
are divisible by
z
, they clearly vanish when restricted to
R
.
∎

For simplicity, set

\Lambda_{0}:=R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z},\qquad\Lambda_{1}=V_{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}\oplus\Lambda_{1}.

For every superscheme
T
, set
\Lambda_{T}:=\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{T}
. When
T=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, we write
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let
J_{0}
denote the usual symplectic intersection pairing on
\Lambda_{0}
, recall the quadratic form
J_{1}
on
\pi_{*}i_{*}\mathcal{Z}_{R/T}^{1}\cong V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{T}
defined in the previous section, and set
J=J_{0}\oplus J_{1}
. Then
J_{T}:=J\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{T}
is a supersymplectic pairing on
\Lambda_{T}
.

Note that since
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of the integral lattice
\Lambda
, complex conjugation is defined:

\overset{-}{{}}:\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}\to\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\lambda\otimes v\longmapsto\lambda\otimes\overline{v}.

Thus every subspace
V\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
has a complex conjugate
\overline{V}\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
.

We say that a subspace
V\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
is
J
-Lagrangian if
\operatorname{dim}(V)=g|r
and
J|_{V}\equiv 0
.
For any
V\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
, we always have the induced pairing
J|_{V\times\overline{V}}:V\times\overline{V}\to\mathbb{C}
. If
V
is
J
-Lagrangian and
V\cap\overline{V}=0
, then
J|_{V\times\overline{V}}\to\mathbb{C}
is non-degenerate.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
X
is a super Riemann surface with Ramond punctures over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
such that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})=g|r.

Then the super period map

P_{X}:H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})\to\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}:=H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})\oplus H^{0}(R,\mathcal{Z}_{R}^{1}),\qquad s\longmapsto(P_{X}^{+}(s),P_{X}^{-}(s))

is injective, and its image
\operatorname{im}(P_{X})
is a
J
-Lagrangian subspace of
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Furthermore,
\operatorname{im}(P_{X})\cap\overline{\operatorname{im}(P_{X})}=0
, and thus
J|_{\operatorname{im}(P_{X})\times\overline{\operatorname{im}(P_{X})}}
is non-degenerate.

Proof.

The main ingredient of the proof is the decomposition

H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})=H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\oplus\Pi H^{0}(C,L).

(15)

The kernel of
P_{X}
is the intersection
\ker(P_{X}^{+})\cap\ker(P_{X}^{-})
. Since
P_{X}
is grading-preserving,

\Pi H^{0}(C,L)\subseteq\ker(P_{X}^{+}),\qquad H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\subseteq\ker(P_{X}^{-}).

Both of these inclusions are equalities. Indeed, the restriction of
P_{X}^{+}
to
H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})
is just the ordinary period map, which is well-known to be injective.
To prove the claim for
P_{X}^{-}
, we use the identification
H^{0}(R,\mathcal{Z}_{R}^{1})=\Pi L
from Lemma
2.2
, and note that the restriction of
P_{X}^{-}
to
\Pi H^{0}(C,L)
is just the map
H^{0}(C,L)\to L|_{D}
in the sequence

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(C,L(-D))\longrightarrow H^{0}(C,L)\overset{P_{-}}{\longrightarrow}L|_{D}\to H^{0}(C,L)^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{0}(C,L(-D))^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0.

(16)

Since
H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})=g|r
,
H^{1}(C,L)=H^{0}(C,L(-D))=0
, and hence
P_{-}
is injective.

For the second claim, the image of
P_{X}
in
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be
J
-Lagrangian if the respective images of
P_{X}^{+}
and
P_{X}^{-}
in
H^{1}(C,\mathbb{C})
and
H^{0}(R,\mathcal{Z}_{R}^{1})
are
J_{0}
- and
J_{1}
-maximally isotropic, respectively.
The image of
P_{X}+
is
J_{0}
-maximally isotropic since it coincides with the image of the ordinary period map for
C
, which is well-known to be maximally isotropic with respect to the symplectic intersection pairing
J_{0}
.

The claim for the image of the odd period map follows from the identification of its image with the image of
H^{0}(C,L)\to\Pi L|_{D}
, identification of
J_{1}
with the residue pairing on
L|_{D}
(c.f. Lemma
2.2
), and the residue theorem: Since
s\otimes s\in H^{0}(C,L^{2})=H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}(D))
is a global meromorphic one-form on the curve
C
,
\sum_{i=1}^{2r}\operatorname{res}_{p_{i}}(s\otimes s)=0
.

For the remaining claim, we first note that the target
\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}:=\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{C}
comes with a complex-conjugation map:
\lambda\otimes v\mapsto\lambda\otimes\overline{v}
. Thus, we can define the complex-conjugate super period map
\overline{P}_{X}(\overline{s}):=\overline{P_{x}(s)}
for all
s\in H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})
, and note that

\overline{\operatorname{im}(P_{X})}=\operatorname{im}(\overline{P}_{X}).

To prove the transversality statement, it suffices to show that

\operatorname{im}(P_{X}^{+})\cap\operatorname{im}(\overline{P_{X}^{+}})=0,\qquad\operatorname{im}(P_{X}^{-})\cap\operatorname{im}(\overline{P_{X}^{-}})=0.

The claim for the even period map again follows from its identification with the image of the ordinary period map, which is well-known to be transverse to its complex conjugate. To prove the claim for the odd period map, assume the intersection is non-zero. Then there exists
v\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
v=\overline{v}
.
But then
v
is a nonzero real isotropic vector for
J_{1}
, which is impossible since
J_{1}
is positive definite.
∎

4
The bad locus

Throughout this section we work entirely on the bosonic reduction. Let

M_{\operatorname{bos}}
denote the bosonic reduction of the chosen étale chart

M\to\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
, and let
B_{\operatorname{bos}}\subset M_{\operatorname{bos}}
denote the bosonic bad
locus. A point of
M_{\operatorname{bos}}
is the twisted-spin datum

(C,D,L),\qquad D=p_{1}+\cdots+p_{2r},\qquad L^{2}\simeq K_{C}(D).

Although Ramond punctures cannot be forgotten at the level of super Riemann
surfaces, there is an ordinary bosonic morphism

p:M_{\operatorname{bos}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g},\qquad(C,D,L)\longmapsto C.

Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
below are statements about
this bosonic morphism and the ordinary complex codimension of

B_{\operatorname{bos}}\subset M_{\operatorname{bos}}
. Equivalently, they give the even part of the
super-codimension of the corresponding closed locus in
M
. For readability
we suppress the subscript
\operatorname{bos}
inside the proofs.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
r>1
, then every irreducible component of the bosonic bad locus

B_{\operatorname{bos}}
has complex codimension
>1
in
M_{\operatorname{bos}}
.

Proof.

On the bosonic reduction the bad locus is

B=\{(C,D,L):h^{0}(C,L)\geq r+1\}\subset M=\{(C,D,L):L^{2}\simeq K_{C}(D)\}.

Set

A:=K_{C}\otimes L^{-1}=L(-D).

Then

A^{2}(D)=K_{C},\qquad\deg A=g-1-r,

and Riemann–Roch gives

h^{0}(A)=h^{0}(L)-r.

Thus the bad condition is
h^{0}(A)>0
.

Fix a curve
C
. Write

F:\operatorname{Pic}^{g-1-r}(C)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}^{2r}(C),\qquad F(A):=K_{C}\otimes A^{-2}.

Then the fiber of
M\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
over
C
is

M_{C}=F^{-1}(W^{0}_{2r})\times_{W^{0}_{2r}}\operatorname{Sym}^{2r}(C).

Equivalently, the map

\rho_{C}:M_{C}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^{2r}(C),\qquad(D,L)\longmapsto D,

is finite: for every effective divisor
D
there are exactly
2^{2g}
square
roots of
K_{C}(D)
. In particular,

\dim M_{C}=2r

for every
C
.

Assume, for a contradiction, that
B
has an irreducible component
B^{\prime}
of
codimension one. Let

Y=\overline{p(B^{\prime})}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g},\qquad c=\operatorname{codim}_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}Y.

Since
\dim M=3g-3+2r
, the generic fiber of
B^{\prime}\to Y
has dimension

(3g-4+2r)-(3g-3-c)=2r+c-1.

This cannot exceed
\dim M_{C}=2r
, hence
c\leq 1
. There are therefore two
possibilities.

Case (1):
c=0
.

Then
B^{\prime}
dominates
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, and for a general curve
C
the fiber

B^{\prime}_{C}:=B^{\prime}\cap M_{C}

is a divisor in
M_{C}
. Since
\rho_{C}
is finite, its image

Z_{C}:=\rho_{C}(B^{\prime}_{C})\subset\operatorname{Sym}^{2r}(C)

is a divisor. Consider the closed
r
-dimensional subvariety

Y_{C}:=\{2E:E\in\operatorname{Sym}^{r}(C)\}\subset\operatorname{Sym}^{2r}(C).

The symmetric product
\operatorname{Sym}^{2r}(C)
is projective of dimension
2r
, so the
projective dimension theorem gives

\dim(Z_{C}\cap Y_{C})\geq(2r-1)+r-2r=r-1.

For every
2E\in Z_{C}\cap Y_{C}
choose a bad lift
(C,2E,L)\in B^{\prime}_{C}
and set

N:=L(-E).

Then
N^{2}\simeq K_{C}
, while

L(-2E)=N(-E)

has a nonzero section. Hence, for an
(r-1)
-dimensional family of effective
divisors
E
of degree
r
, the divisor
E
is contained in the zero divisor
of a section of some theta characteristic
N
. Since a fixed curve has only
finitely many theta characteristics, one fixed
N
works for an

(r-1)
-dimensional family of
E
’s. For fixed
N
, the space of such
E
’s
has dimension at most
\dim|N|=h^{0}(N)-1
, because a fixed divisor in
|N|

has only finitely many degree-
r
effective subdivisors. Thus

h^{0}(N)\geq r.

For
r\geq 2
this contradicts the fact that on a general curve every theta
characteristic has at most one section.

Case (2):
c=1
.

Then
Y\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
is a divisor, and for a general
C\in Y
the fiber

B^{\prime}_{C}
contains an irreducible component of
M_{C}
. Every irreducible
component of
M_{C}
maps surjectively to
\operatorname{Sym}^{2r}(C)
under the finite map

\rho_{C}
. Hence for every
E\in\operatorname{Sym}^{r}(C)
there is a bad lift with

D=2E
. As above, writing
N=L(-E)
produces a theta characteristic for
which
N(-E)
is effective. Since there are finitely many theta
characteristics, one fixed
N
works for an
r
-dimensional family of
E
’s,
and therefore

h^{0}(N)\geq r+1.

For
r\geq 2
, Corollary 1.3 of
[
Pol03
]
, specialized to

and write

\widetilde{t}|_{Q_{a}\cap W}=f_{a}s_{a}.

Ordinary Hartogs’ theorem on
(Q_{a})_{\operatorname{bos}}
, applied coefficientwise after
expanding
f_{a}
in the local odd coordinates, extends

uniquely to a
holomorphic superfunction
\widetilde{f}_{a}
on
Q_{a}
. Uniqueness shows that
the sections
\widetilde{f}_{a}s_{a}
agree on overlaps. They therefore glue to the
required section
\widehat{t}
.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

Assume
g\geq 2
and
r>1
. The measure constructed on the good locus extends holomorphically to an open neighborhood of the entire diagonal in
M\times\overline{M}
. Its pullback to
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is therefore a smooth measure extending the measure of Corollary
6.3
. The resulting measure is independent of the auxiliary choice of orientation of the Ramond divisor.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
7.1
near every point of the bad locus. On overlaps the local extensions agree, because they agree on the dense good locus and holomorphic sections of a line bundle which agree on a dense open subset agree everywhere. Thus the local extensions glue to a holomorphic section on a neighborhood of the whole diagonal. Pullback by
\Delta:M_{\mathbb{R}}\to M\times\overline{M}
gives the required smooth measure.

By Corollary
6.4
, two choices of orientation give the same section on the dense good locus. Their holomorphic extensions therefore agree everywhere by uniqueness. Thus the extended measure is orientation-independent as well.
∎

Remark 7.3

(Descent to supermoduli)
.

The preceding construction was carried out on an étale chart

M\to\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
. It is compatible with étale base change and therefore
descends to
\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}
. Indeed, on

M^{(2)}:=M\times_{\mathfrak{M}_{g,0,2r}}M

the two pullbacks of the measure agree over the inverse image of the good
locus, since there they are obtained from the same canonical period pairing
and super Mumford isomorphism. This inverse image is dense, and both sections
are holomorphic extensions of their common restriction. By uniqueness of
holomorphic extension they therefore agree on all of
M^{(2)}
. The usual
descent condition follows, so the resulting measure is a well-defined section
on the moduli superstack itself.

The preceding theorem deliberately asserts extension as a measure, not as a volume form. In fact, Theorem
4.2
shows that for
r>2
zeros along the bad locus are unavoidable whenever the bad locus is nonempty.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
b\in B
. Suppose that every irreducible component of
B
through
b
has codimension at least
3
. Then the bosonic reduction of the extended measure of Theorem
7.2
vanishes at
b
. In particular, the extended measure is not a volume form near
b
.

Proof.

We work on the bosonic reduction near
b
. Let
R=\mathcal{O}_{M_{\operatorname{bos}},b}
, which is a regular local ring, and let
L
denote the universal twisted spin line bundle. Represent
R\pi_{*}L
locally by a two-term complex of finite free
R
-modules

F^{0}\xrightarrow{d}F^{1}.

Set

K:=\ker d=\pi_{*}L,\qquad Q:=\operatorname{coker}d=R^{1}\pi_{*}L.

Thus

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow F^{0}\xrightarrow{d}F^{1}\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 0.

(19)

By Serre duality,

H^{1}(C,L)\cong H^{0}(C,L(-D))^{\vee},

so the support of
Q
is precisely the bad locus
B
.

We first record that
K
is reflexive. Put
I=\operatorname{im}d
. Then (
19
) breaks into

0\to I\to F^{1}\to Q\to 0,\qquad 0\to K\to F^{0}\to I\to 0.

If
\mathfrak{p}\notin\operatorname{Supp}Q
, then
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
is free. If
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}Q
, then
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})\geq 3
. The depth lemma applied to the first sequence gives

\operatorname{depth}I_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 1,

and applied to the second gives

\operatorname{depth}K_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 2.

Since
K\subset F^{0}
, it is torsion-free. Thus
K
satisfies
S_{2}
and is torsion-free over the normal ring
R
, hence is reflexive.

On the good locus, the odd period map is the residue map

\tau:K|_{U}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{U},

and is injective. We claim that its image cannot have a locally free rank-
r
extension across
b
. Indeed, if such an extension existed, it would be reflexive and would agree with the reflexive module
K
away from a subset of codimension at least
2
. Uniqueness of reflexive extension would imply that
K
itself is locally free, hence free over
R
. But then (
19
) would give a free resolution of
Q
of length at most
2
, so

\operatorname{pd}_{R}Q\leq 2.

Auslander–Buchsbaum would imply

\operatorname{depth}_{R}Q\geq\dim R-2,

and hence
\operatorname{Supp}Q
would have a component of codimension at most
2
, contradicting the hypothesis.

Now let

\lambda:=\det R\pi_{*}L.

Over
U
we have
\lambda|_{U}=\det K|_{U}
, so taking the top exterior power of the odd period map gives a Plücker section

\sigma_{U}:=\bigwedge^{r}\tau\in\Gamma\left(U,\bigwedge^{r}(\Lambda_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{U})\otimes\lambda^{-1}\right).

Since
B
has codimension at least
2
,
\sigma_{U}
extends uniquely by Hartogs to a section
\sigma
near
b
. If
\sigma(b)\neq 0
, then after shrinking around
b
it is nowhere zero. The Plücker relations hold on the dense open set
U
and therefore everywhere, so
\sigma
defines a holomorphic map to the Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}(r,\Lambda_{1}\otimes\mathbb{C})
whose pullback of the tautological bundle is a locally free extension of
\operatorname{im}\tau
. This contradicts the preceding paragraph. Hence

\sigma(b)=0.

On the good product, restrict the local formula in the proof of Proposition

7.1
to the bosonic reduction. The mixed blocks
vanish there, so

q_{\operatorname{bos}}=\frac{\det D}{\det A}.

Here
\det A
is a unit near the conjugate diagonal. On the same good product,

\det D
is the pairing induced by
J_{1}
on the two Plücker sections:

\det D=J_{1}^{\wedge r}(\sigma_{U},\overline{\sigma_{U}})

in compatible local frames. The Plücker sections extend across the bad
locus to
\sigma
and
\overline{\sigma}
, so the right-hand side has a
holomorphic extension. On the other hand,

\widetilde{q}_{\operatorname{bos}}\det A
is a holomorphic extension of the left-hand side
by Proposition

. Since the two extensions
agree on the dense good locus, uniqueness of holomorphic extension gives

\widetilde{q}_{\operatorname{bos}}\det A=J_{1}^{\wedge r}(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})

near

and

. Therefore the bosonic reduction of

vanishes at

∎

Appendix A
Super symplectic pairing

A super symplectic pairing on a super vector space
V
is a non-degenerate
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear form
\langle\ ,\ \rangle:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
such that

•

the restriction
\langle\ ,\rangle_{0}:V_{0}\times V_{0}\to\mathbb{C}
to the even component
V_{0}
is non-degenerate and skew-symmetric,

•

the restriction of
\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{1}:V_{1}\times V_{1}\to\mathbb{C}
to the odd component
V_{1}
is non-degenerate and symmetric,

•

the restriction of
\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{1,0}
and
\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{0,1}
to
V_{1}\times V_{0}
and
V_{0}\times V_{1}
, respectively, is identically zero.

Let
J:=\langle\ ,\ \rangle
be a supersymplectic pairing on a super vector space
V
. We say that a subspace
W\subset V
is
Lagrangian
(or,
maximally-isotropic
) with respect to
J
if the following hold:

•

W_{0}\subset V_{0}
is Lagrangian (in the usual sense) with respect to the symplectic pairing
J_{0}
, and

•

W_{1}\subset V_{1}
is maximally-isotropic (in the usual sense) with respect to the symmetric bilinear pairing
J_{1}
.

Appendix B
Adding NS punctures

Let
M
denote the supermoduli space with
2r
Ramond punctures and
n
NS punctures, and let

\pi:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow M

be the universal super Riemann surface. Write
\mathcal{R}
for the universal Ramond divisor. For the NS punctures, let

s_{i}:M\longrightarrow\mathcal{X},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

be the marked sections, let
P_{i}\subset\mathcal{X}
denote the associated
0|1
NS divisors, and set

\mathcal{N}:=P_{1}+\cdots+P_{n}.

Thus the marked sections and the associated divisors are distinct objects; locally, if an NS puncture is at
z=\theta=0
, the corresponding divisor is
z=0
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{X}
denote the sheaf of superconformal vector fields on a fiber
X
. The tangent space to
M
at
X
is

T_{X}M=H^{1}\bigl(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}(-N)\bigr).

For a super Riemann surface with Ramond divisor
R
one has the standard identifications

\mathcal{A}_{X}\cong\mathcal{D}^{2},\qquad\mathcal{D}^{2}\cong\omega_{X}^{-2}(-2R),

where
\mathcal{D}
is the superconformal distribution. Hence

\mathcal{A}_{X}(-N)\cong\mathcal{D}^{2}(-N).

Serre duality therefore gives

H^{1}\bigl(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}(-N)\bigr)^{*}\cong H^{0}\bigl(X,\omega_{X}^{3}(2R+N)\bigr),

and consequently

\omega_{M}\cong\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(2\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{N})\right).

(20)

Removing the Ramond polar part.

The twist by
2\mathcal{R}
must be treated by the sheaf of polar parts, not by ordinary restriction to
\mathcal{R}
. Define

\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{N}):=\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(2\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{N})\big/\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(\mathcal{N}).

Then there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(\mathcal{N})\longrightarrow\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(2\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{N})\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{N})\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{R}
are disjoint, the canonical section of

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{N})
is nowhere zero in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{R}
, and hence gives a canonical identification

\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathcal{N})\cong\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{R}}(0):=\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(2\mathcal{R})\big/\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}.

Appendix C of
[
Wit15a
]
gives a canonical trivialization

\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{R}}(0)\right)\cong\mathcal{O}_{M}.

Multiplicativity of the Berezinian in the resulting exact triangle therefore gives

\omega_{M}\cong\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(\mathcal{N})\right).

(21)

Super Mumford isomorphism with NS and Ramond punctures.

Consider now the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}\longrightarrow\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(\mathcal{N})\longrightarrow\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(\mathcal{N})|_{\mathcal{N}}\longrightarrow 0.

Each
P_{i}
is finite over
M
, so the restriction term has no higher direct images. Since the
P_{i}
are disjoint, multiplicativity of the Berezinian and (
21
) give

\omega_{M}\cong\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}\right)\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{i},

(22)

where

\mathcal{L}_{i}:=\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(\pi_{*}\bigl(\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(\mathcal{N})|_{P_{i}}\bigr)\right),

so that

\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(\pi_{*}\bigl(\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}(\mathcal{N})|_{\mathcal{N}}\bigr)\right)\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{i}.

Let

f:M\longrightarrow M_{0}

be the morphism which forgets the NS punctures, where
M_{0}
denotes the supermoduli space with the same Ramond punctures and no NS punctures. In contrast to Ramond punctures, NS punctures are forgettable. The universal supercurve over
M
is obtained by base change from the universal supercurve

\pi_{0}:\mathcal{X}_{0}\longrightarrow M_{0},

so

\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}/M}^{3}\right)\cong f^{*}\operatorname{Ber}\!\left(R\pi_{0*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}_{0}/M_{0}}^{3}\right)\cong f^{*}\omega_{M_{0}}.

Applying the super Mumford isomorphism on
M_{0}
now gives

\boxed{\omega_{M}\cong f^{*}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}^{5}\!\left(R\pi_{0*}\omega_{\mathcal{X}_{0}/M_{0}}\right)\right)\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{i}.}

(23)

The additional factors
\mathcal{L}_{i}
are the NS insertion-line factors. Thus this appendix identifies the canonical line on supermoduli with the pullback of the Ramond super Mumford line together with the expected NS puncture factors; producing a scalar measure requires, in addition, the appropriate insertions in these lines.

There are two notions of periods on a super Riemann surface
X

X
, and the periods of global sections of the

\omega_{X}^{\prime}
.
There is a canonical isomorphism
Z_{X}^{1}\cong H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\prime})
(cf.
[
Wit15b
]
) under which the two notions of periods become equivalent.

Periods of closed one-forms.

The usual
2g

A
and
B
cycles for
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})=H_{1}(C,\mathbb{Z})
.

Its
2r

[
Wit15b
]
as follows: A general closed one form
\omega\in Z_{X}^{1}
is locally in the superconformal coordinates
(z,\theta)
around a single Ramond puncture of the form

\left(f(z)+\frac{\partial g(z)}{\partial z}\theta\right)dz+g(z)d\theta,

where
g
and
f
are local holomorphic functions on
X
, possibly depending on parameters from the base.
Let
j:R\to X
be the natural inclusion of the Ramond divisor, and let

.
The restriction of
\omega

. Witten defines the constant
g(0)
to be the odd period of
\omega
. Note that
g(0)dx
is a closed one-form on
R
. Repeating this for every component of
R
, we get the
2r
-tuple of odd period of
\omega
:

\omega|_{R}=(g_{1}(0)dx^{1},\dots,g_{2r}(0)dx^{2r})\in Z_{R}^{1}

(24)

The constants are unique up to sign.

More abstractly, the map computing the odd periods of closed one-forms on
R
is given by the natural restriction map
Z_{X}^{1}\to Z_{R}^{1}
of closed one-forms on
X
to closed one-forms on

.

We define the periods of sections of

in Section
3
. Let us give an explicit description of their odd periods in terms of local superconformal coordinates
(z,\theta)
near a Ramond puncture.

A general global section

(z,\theta)
given by

\omega=\left(\frac{g(z)}{z}+f(z)\theta\right)[dz|d\theta].

Restricting
\omega
to

\left(\frac{g(z)}{z}+f(z)\theta\right)

, and hence
\omega|_{R}=g(0)dx

g(0)
to be the odd period of
\omega
at the Ramond puncture contained in
(z,\theta)

2r

.

The fact that these two notions of periods are equivalent is immediate from the local description of the canonical isomorphism
Z_{X}^{1}\cong H^{0}(X,\omega^{\prime})
in the coordinates
(z,\theta)
:

\left(f(z)+\frac{\partial g(z)}{\partial z}\theta\right)dz+g(z)d\theta\mapsto\left(\frac{g(z)}{z}+f(z)\theta\right)[dz|d\theta]

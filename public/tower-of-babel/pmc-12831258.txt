Implementing a new approach to enhance palliative care for people with dementia in Aruba, Bonaire, and Curaçao: a mixed-methods study
Authors: Jesper M. A. Biesmans, Sascha R. Bolt, Sanne P. C. M. Vergouwen, Rosa Léon, Evert L. Piar, Henna Winklaar, Tessa de Haas, Jos M. G. A. Schols, Daisy J. A. Janssen, Judith M. M. Meijers
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe Caribbean islands Aruba, Bonaire, and Curaçao (ABC islands) face challenges in providing palliative care for people with dementia. The Desired Dementia Care Towards End of Life (DEDICATED) approach empowers healthcare professionals to provide person-centered palliative care to individuals with dementia. This study examines healthcare professionals’ needs for improving palliative care and their experiences with implementing the DEDICATED approach, as well as its impact on palliative care for people with dementia on the ABC islands.MethodologyA consortium was established with stakeholders from the ABC islands to facilitate the training of 72 healthcare professionals in implementing the DEDICATED approach in these locations. A needs assessment questionnaire, referred to as the ‘DEDICATED questionnaire’, was completed to explore the needs for palliative care improvement on the ABC islands. For each island, a focus group was organized to gather feedback from healthcare professionals about their experiences with implementing the DEDICATED approach. A descriptive thematic analysis was performed.ResultsIn total, 49 healthcare professionals completed the DEDICATED questionnaire, which indicated high needs for improvement in aspects such as familiarization with a person with dementia and conducting advance care planning. Twenty healthcare professionals participated in the focus groups. Five main themes were identified, related to the implementation of the DEDICATED approach in the islands’ sociocultural and organizational aspects, the approach’s impact on palliative dementia care, and the necessity of adapting the DEDICATED approach to local contexts. The results revealed that talking about death and palliative care is considered taboo in ABC island culture. Healthcare professionals expressed a need for further refinements to stimulate the implementation of the approach. The DEDICATED approach increased awareness about providing person-centered care and stimulated healthcare professionals to more proactively discuss the palliative care needs of people with dementia and their family caregivers.ConclusionThe DEDICATED approach showed added value for healthcare professionals in the context of the ABC islands. The approach encouraged proactive discussions about palliative and end-of-life care. However, cultural and contextual aspects, such as religion, could pose barriers to the implementation of the approach. Further refinements are needed to align the DEDICATED approach with the context of the ABC islands, thereby stimulating its implementation.Trial registrationNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12904-025-01970-5.

Background

The islands of Aruba, Bonaire, and Curaçao (ABC islands) are located in the Caribbean Sea and are home to approximately 300.000 residents [1]. The ABC islands have a rich cultural history influenced by Spanish, Dutch, and Caribbean traditions. The local population is aging, and the prevalence of dementia is increasing [2, 3]. Public health-focused policies and scientific research are growing on the ABC islands to enhance the quality of healthcare [4]. Specifically, the islands’ governments have published national strategies for dementia care in which policymakers and healthcare organizations have expressed the need for (1) better coordination of care across involved organizations, healthcare professionals (HCPs), and policymakers; (2) improved quality of care for people with dementia; and (3) the mitigation of stigma and misconceptions surrounding dementia and palliative care [5–7].

However, no scientific studies have been conducted on how HCPs provide palliative care to people with dementia on the ABC islands or on how the quality of that care may be enhanced. HCPs working in the wider Caribbean region experience difficulties in providing palliative care for people with dementia [8, 9]. For instance, there is limited knowledge about what palliative care entails, leading to misconceptions about its purpose [10]. People living in the Caribbean region often perceive palliative care as terminal care and may be reluctant to discuss this topic [11–13]. Moreover, cultural and social aspects, such as stigma and taboo, may prevent people from talking about death and the last phase of life [14], which could result in ambiguity regarding the wishes and needs for palliative care. In addition, religion in Latin America and the Caribbean plays a significant role in shaping the provision of palliative care. For example, illness can be perceived as an act of divine power [15], and family caregivers or HCPs can therefore hold different perspectives on how to engage in providing palliative care. While some people may become more involved in caring for a loved one with dementia, others may feel less compelled to intervene, believing the illness to be beyond human control [15]. This belief may also contribute to people subsequently refraining from discussing their care needs and may present barriers to the provision of person-centered palliative care [15]. In particular, HCPs can face difficulties with these undefined care needs and the interaction with cultural aspects during the provision of palliative care, as they often step in to support people with dementia and their family caregivers [15].

Overall, HCPs on the ABC islands may face significant challenges in providing palliative dementia care, underscoring the need for further education and information provision to enhance the quality of palliative dementia care. The Desired Dementia Care Towards End of Life (DEDICATED) approach is an intervention that aims to empower HCPs in providing person-centered palliative care for individuals with dementia. This study explores the needs of HCPs in providing palliative dementia care, their experiences with implementing the DEDICATED approach, and the approach’s impact on palliative care for people with dementia on the ABC islands.

Materials and methods

The DEDICATED approach

The DEDICATED approach is a palliative care intervention developed in co-creation with Dutch HCPs and educators in the field of palliative dementia care, providing practical tools, information, and training related to five central themes that are essential components for high-quality palliative care for people with dementia [16]. Table 1 provides an overview of the co-created tools within their respective theme. HCPs who complete the DEDICATED training become ‘DEDICATED ambassadors’ [16]. During the DEDICATED training, ambassadors learn how to implement the DEDICATED tools in their care practice and how to motivate their colleagues to use the tools. Additionally, a DEDICATED ambassador can become ‘DEDICATED trainer’. A trainer follows additional didactic training and is subsequently qualified to autonomously train new DEDICATED ambassadors and trainers to ensure further dissemination of the DEDICATED approach in practice. Detailed information regarding the development and content of the DEDICATED approach has been published elsewhere [16].

Implementation of the DEDICATED approach on the ABC Islands

The implementation of the DEDICATED approach on the ABC islands followed several steps, which are visualized in Fig. 1. In 2021 and 2022, DEDICATED researchers were invited to present the approach in Aruba, Bonaire, and Curaçao, leading to a commitment from healthcare organizations and government representatives across the ABC islands to improve palliative care for people with dementia and encourage stronger collaboration between the involved healthcare organizations. A consortium has since been established with stakeholders (e.g., from Alzheimer foundations and organizations for dementia care and support) from all three islands. As a result, DEDICATED represents the first collaborative effort focused on dementia and palliative care on the ABC islands. The consortium aimed to enhance the quality of palliative care for people with dementia by implementing the DEDICATED approach on the islands. In June 2023, the first online training sessions took place to prepare DEDICATED ambassadors on the three islands (i.e., three in Aruba, three in Bonaire, and eleven in Curaçao). The second training session was conducted in August 2023, after which the DEDICATED ambassadors were fully trained to implement the DEDICATED approach in practice. The DEDICATED ambassadors who were trained between June and August 2023 received further online didactic training to become DEDICATED trainers. As DEDICATED trainers, they were able to independently train new DEDICATED ambassadors [16]. This training system facilitated further dissemination of the DEDICATED approach on the ABC islands. Using this implementation strategy, 16 DEDICATED trainers were trained. The trainers subsequently trained 56 DEDICATED ambassadors on site across the ABC islands with the support of experienced DEDICATED trainers from the Netherlands.

Aim

This study explored HCPs’ (1) needs for improving the provision of palliative care for people with dementia and (2) their experiences with implementing the DEDICATED approach and its impact on palliative care for people with dementia on the ABC islands. We used a cross-sectional mixed-methods design. We used consolidated criteria for reporting qualitative studies (COREQ) to describe the methods and qualitative findings of the study [17].

Study population and recruitment

The study population involved HCPs who: (1) completed the DEDICATED training to become DEDICATED ambassadors or trainers on the ABC islands; (2) provided palliative care for people with dementia and their family caregivers and; (3) worked in a nursing home, day care facility, hospice, or home care setting on the ABC islands. During the DEDICATED training sessions of November and December 2023, we enquired the DEDICATED ambassadors to complete the digital DEDICATED questionnaire in Qualtrics (i.e., a data management platform; Qualtrics International Inc., Provo, UT; 2024), accessed via a QR code. After the implementation in June 2024, the DEDICATED ambassadors and trainers were invited by email to voluntarily participate in focus groups. The email contained an information letter, a consent form, and a link to the Teams meeting.

Data collection

Before implementing the DEDICATED approach, we conducted a needs assessment using the DEDICATED questionnaire [18]. This validated questionnaire is designed to help HCPs gain insight into their needs, learning goals, and areas for improvement related to palliative care for people with dementia (Appendix 2) [18]. The Dutch version of the DEDICATED questionnaire has a mean Cronbach’s alpha (α) of 0.89 [18], and was translated to the local language (i.e., Papiamento) using input from local language experts and native speakers. The questionnaire comprises 29 items related to the central themes of DEDICATED (Table 2), scored on a four-point Likert scale (from 1 for "strongly disagree" to 4 for "strongly agree"), with total scores ranging from 29 to 116. To categorize the HCPs’ needs levels, K-means cluster analysis was conducted, leading to the following needs categories: "low needs" (score 29–61), "moderate needs" (score 62–78), "high needs" (score 79–96), "very high needs" (score 97–116). Higher scores indicate a greater need among HCPs to enhance their skills and competencies [18].

Qualitative data was collected between May and July 2024 through semi-structured online focus groups conducted via Microsoft Teams and recorded using Microsoft Game Bar. A pre-developed topic list was used (Appendix 1). The topic list was based on an interview guide developed in an earlier study of DEDICATED [16] that involved semi-structured interviews with DEDICATED ambassadors in the Netherlands. We used the Dutch Palliative Care Quality Framework [19] as an underlying structure to formulate items in the topic list, for instance, those related to cross-cultural palliative care or ACP. The topic list included questions regarding: (1) how the DEDICATED ambassadors applied and implemented the DEDICATED approach; (2) how the DEDICATED approach was experienced in practice; and (3) how the DEDICATED approach impacted palliative care provision for people with dementia. All focus groups were conducted by two DEDICATED research team members. One researcher (JB) led the focus groups while the second interviewer (JM or TO) checked whether questions were answered and asked for further elaboration if needed.

Data analyses

Quantitative data were analyzed using IBM SPSS version 28.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). We conducted an analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s post-hoc test to determine if the total scores of the DEDICATED questionnaire showed significant differences between HCPs on the ABC islands. Additionally, total scores and subscores for the DEDICATED questionnaire themes were reported separately for all three islands.

Qualitative data were analyzed via a thematic analysis employing inductive coding to explore the experiences of DEDICATED ambassadors in implementing the DEDICATED approach and its impact on palliative care provision [20]. We followed the six steps of Braun & Clarke’s framework for thematic analysis [21]. Two researchers (JB & SV) familiarized themselves with the data and individually coded the first transcript. After the first transcript was coded, the two researchers engaged in a discussion session to align the open codes. Subsequently, the remaining two transcripts were individually coded again, with another discussion session to align the open codes and update the codebook. All open codes were clustered and reviewed by the two researchers for overlapping aspects. Finally, both researchers individually formulated themes into which open codes were collated. In a final reasoning session, the researchers exchanged their thoughts on the formulation of themes until they reached a consensus. The themes were then discussed within the research team for further refinement. Atlas.ti version 9 software was used for the qualitative data analyses (Atlas.ti Scientific Software Development GmbH).

Results

Results of the DEDICATED questionnaire

The DEDICATED questionnaire was completed by 49 DEDICATED ambassadors across all three islands. The total score of the questionnaire indicated high needs for developing skills and competencies related to palliative care for people with dementia across DEDICATED ambassadors on all three islands (Table 3). Ambassadors from Aruba and Curaçao expressed the highest needs in theme 1, indicating a need for skill enhancement to familiarize themselves with individuals with dementia. In Bonaire, the highest score was found in theme 2, underscoring the need to learn about when to initiate ACP conversations. The total score for ambassadors in Curaçao was the highest, indicating that Curaçao has the most needs regarding the enhancement of palliative care provision. A significant difference in sum scores was observed between Aruba and Curaçao (mean difference = 11.93, 95% confidence interval [1.73, 22.14], p = 0.018).

Theme 1: The important role of the islands’ sociocultural aspects in implementing DEDICATED

One participant explained that the ABC islands have been influenced by many cultures, including Spanish and Dutch cultures. The largest proportion of the population is Catholic and religion is an important aspect of the local culture. Culture and language could differ between the three islands. For instance, all three islands have their own way of speaking and writing Papiamento (i.e., the local language on the island).“On the ABC islands, everyone speaks Papiamento, but it’s different on each island. In Aruba, the writing is just different, so instead of a 'K', it is a 'C.' But they also speak differently, and in Bonaire, they speak differently too, with a different accent. And in Curaçao, it is yet another accent.” (Home care nurse, Bonaire).

“On the ABC islands, everyone speaks Papiamento, but it’s different on each island. In Aruba, the writing is just different, so instead of a 'K', it is a 'C.' But they also speak differently, and in Bonaire, they speak differently too, with a different accent. And in Curaçao, it is yet another accent.” (Home care nurse, Bonaire).

DEDICATED ambassadors noted that the cultural norms on the ABC islands regarding discussing general feelings or emotions can pose challenges when talking about palliative care, death, or ACP. For instance, ambassadors noted that individuals with dementia and their family caregivers are often willing to discuss practical matters, such as funeral arrangements, but struggle to talk about their emotional experiences, as this is not a common part of their culture and everyday life.“We invited a funeral director once, to explain things. They [people with dementia and family caregivers] are able to talk about the practical aspects, like where they want to be buried and so on. But not the emotional part, that is not something they discuss. They do talk about things like what my children will inherit or when they’ll inherit it, and how they want to be dressed. They can discuss those things, but on the emotional side, they do not.” (Health and wellness coordinator, Aruba).

But not the emotional part, that is not something they discuss. They do talk about things like what my children will inherit or when they’ll inherit it, and how they want to be dressed. They can discuss those things, but on the emotional side, they do not.” (Health and wellness coordinator, Aruba).

Talking about death and palliative care was mostly seen as taboo. Ambassadors explained that this perception hinders proactive discussions regarding the wishes, needs, and values of individuals with dementia and their family caregivers. Discussing these topics was not only experienced as difficult for family caregivers and people with dementia, but also for ambassadors and their surrounding team of HCPs. Ambassadors perceived that this could challenge the implementation of the DEDICATED approach in practice.“Some HCPs started out very enthusiastically [with the DEDICATED approach], but they didn’t continue during care provision. Some could not handle it emotionally, and then I can’t talk to them about it either, for example, about death or preparing for these situations.” (Home care nurse, Bonaire).

“Some HCPs started out very enthusiastically [with the DEDICATED approach], but they didn’t continue during care provision. Some could not handle it emotionally, and then I can’t talk to them about it either, for example, about death or preparing for these situations.” (Home care nurse, Bonaire).

In addition, DEDICATED ambassadors explained that religion, rituals, myths, and superstition can influence how palliative care is provided. For instance, they mentioned that there are misconceptions surrounding the use of pain medication and palliative sedation. People with dementia or their family caregivers may believe that this is used to end a person’s life rather than to provide comfort and pain relief. This misconception can lead to friction between HCPs and people with dementia or family caregivers. Ambassadors suggested that the decision-making process regarding palliative care could be improved by informing family caregivers and people with dementia the role of palliative care provision in achieving comfort and quality of life.“I see a lot of differences in belief. In one family, it might be small statue of God; in another, perhaps a Buddha. Some do not believe in anything. Some people practice specific rituals. Similarly, for pain, they may use natural remedies or methods rooted in their beliefs. For some, it’s about natural meditation or alternative approaches. Others may say, “It’s about pain relief,” but reject the idea of pharmaceutical painkillers, opting for natural solutions instead.” (Case manager dementia, Bonaire).

Theme 5: The necessity of adapting DEDICATED to local contexts

The DEDICATED approach was developed within the context of the Netherlands. For instance, the tools are in Dutch, which is a secondary language on the ABC islands. Furthermore, mostly Western images are featured on the DEDICATED Photo Cards. These linguistic and visual aspects could hinder the implementation of the approach on the ABC islands. DEDICATED ambassadors expressed that the implementation of DEDICATED would be facilitated more effectively if the tools and training were refined to incorporate the cultural and contextual elements of the ABC islands, for example, by refining the terminology and language used in the DEDICATED approach and using images of the Caribbean, as these would be more recognizable for people with dementia. Regarding the terminology, DEDICATED ambassadors indicated that the use of the word ‘palliative care’ is sometimes perceived as intimidating by people with dementia and their family caregivers, possibly hampering their willingness to discuss such topics using the DEDICATED approach. Therefore, using more implicit language in the tools might enhance the accessibility of the approach.“Yes, when we use those Photo Cards, I think it would be really helpful for many HCPs if they were also available in Papiamento or Spanish. Discussion

This study explored the needs of DEDICATED ambassadors and HCPs for improving palliative care provision for people with dementia on the ABC islands. Additionally, we examined the ambassadors’ experiences with implementing the DEDICATED approach and its impact on palliative care for people with dementia. The results from the DEDICATED questionnaire indicated that ambassadors felt the highest need to improve skills that enabled them to familiarize themselves with people with dementia and to find the right time to start ACP discussions. The qualitative results highlighted that the DEDICATED approach was considered a valuable intervention for enhancing person-centered palliative care for people with dementia and initiating these proactive ACP discussions. However, the implementation of the DEDICATED approach on the ABC islands was influenced by sociocultural and organizational aspects.

The stepwise implementation strategy used for the DEDICATED approach fostered implementation readiness and strengthened commitment among healthcare organizations and DEDICATED ambassadors. This finding aligns with those of Ghoshal (2024), who identified the following key components for successful implementation of palliative care interventions: (1) building networks before implementation; (2) raising awareness about the value of palliative care; and (3) assessing the needs of the target population [22]. Our current study confirms that applying similar steps can effectively support the integration and use of the DEDICATED approach in practice.

DEDICATED ambassadors emphasized that the implementation of the DEDICATED approach and provision of adequate palliative care on the ABC islands are influenced by culture and religious beliefs. For instance, the DEDICATED ambassadors explained that existing that taboos and misconceptions surrounding palliative care as potential barriers. The Caribbean region comprises many countries and island states, each with its own unique culture and norms surrounding palliative care. Existing studies within the Caribbean region have primarily focused on palliative care for people with cancer and were mostly conducted English-speaking Caribbean countries (e.g., Antigua and Barbuda, the Bahamas, or Barbados) [23]. Thus, the information obtained from these studies may not be fully comparable to palliative dementia care and the context of the ABC islands. Nonetheless, our study’s findings align with those of other studies on palliative care in the Caribbean region. Specifically, DEDICATED ambassadors explained that people with dementia and their family caregivers might refrain from discussing aspects of palliative care, such as pain treatments, as this could be contradictory to their beliefs and cultural background. Jennings et al. (2018) described a similar reluctance among people with dementia and their family caregivers in English-speaking Caribbean countries. For instance, illness or pain may be perceived as a punishment from a divine power that must be endured without intervention or (medical) treatment [24, 25]. These perceptions can create challenges for HCPs. A DEDICATED ambassador working as a case manager for people with dementia described situations where individuals who experienced pain, declined medication due to religious beliefs, leading to tension for professionals in determining appropriate actions during palliative care, as palliative care not only encompasses the aforementioned spiritual needs but also focuses on psychological, social, and physical needs, such as pain alleviation [26, 27].

Additionally, staff shortages and high turnover were frequently mentioned as hindrances to implementing the DEDICATED approach in care teams and the overall implementation of new ways of working. Similarly, in earlier research, Dutch DEDICATED ambassadors equally struggled to implement the new intervention within care organizations dealing with high staff turnover and shortages [16]. However, a key difference between DEDICATED ambassadors on the ABC islands and the Netherlands was that the ambassadors on the ABC islands reported almost no reluctance from coworkers towards implementing the new approach in the workplace. In contrast, Dutch DEDICATED ambassadors often described that implementation was hindered by coworkers’ unwillingness to deviate from their daily routines. Pastrana et al. (2010) stated that research and training in low- and middle-income countries receive insufficient attention, and the costs of implementing new palliative care interventions are higher due to the need to import resources or rely on foreign expertise [9, 28–30]. This may explain why the limited interventions that are provided are perceived as particularly valuable by HCPs, possibly altering their view on implementing new or innovative ways of working. Nevertheless, DEDICATED ambassadors on the ABC islands reported other needs to ensure further implementation of the DEDICATED approach. The importance of structural training and guidance in applying the DEDICATED approach in practice was frequently mentioned by DEDICATED ambassadors and has been reported in scientific literature to be a factor for successful implementation [31].

The DEDICATED approach was experienced as helpful in raising awareness about the value of ACP and the role of the HCP in providing care from a holistic perspective. By increasing awareness of the care trajectory (e.g., what palliative care entails) and available treatments (e.g., understanding wishes, needs, and values for palliative care), HCPs, individuals with dementia, and family caregivers can make informed treatment decisions [23]. In addition to raising awareness, DEDICATED ambassadors indicated that the DEDICATED approach had practical value. For instance, the tools (e.g., the Photo Cards or Symbol dice) provided an opportunity to initiate ACP discussions about palliative care preferences and the end of life. However, ambassadors also explained that persistent taboos surrounding talking about the last phase of life hampered the readiness of people with dementia and family caregivers to engage in ACP discussions. The taboo surrounding death and dying is not unique to the ABC islands. Green et al. (2018) indicated that talking about the end of life and palliative care is seen as difficult in almost every culture or ethnic background, as these conversations can be complex and emotionally charged [32–34]. However, the study by Green et al. (2018) distinguished between two broad perspectives on communication about palliative care and the end of life, which could explain the limited readiness to discuss palliative care and the end of life. First, a more individualistic approach to communication is predominantly found in Western countries, focusing on the autonomy of the person with dementia and providing transparent communication about the care trajectory and prognosis. Second, collectivist cultures, commonly found in Latin America, Africa, and Asia, prioritize the well-being of the community and family [32, 35]. In these cultures, to minimize distress for individuals in the palliative care trajectory, family caregivers might refrain from sharing information about prognoses, treatments, and the end of life, as this could be perceived as emotionally hurtful to the person with dementia [32, 35]. This was also confirmed by Swed et al. (2024), in which 602 HCPs were asked if the end of life and treatment options should be discussed with the person receiving palliative care [36]. A large proportion of the HCPs (66.2%) indicated that it is preferable not to discuss the end of life with the people receiving palliative care, indicating that multiple cultures beyond those of the Caribbean region may have other perspectives on talking about palliative care and the end of life with the person receiving palliative care. This could explain why DEDICATED ambassadors, family caregivers, and people with dementia experience barriers in initiating ACP discussions on the ABC islands, even with the use of tools such as the DEDICATED approach.

As the DEDICATED approach was initially developed for the Dutch healthcare context, it is essential to acknowledge that the approach needs to be adapted to align with collectivist perspectives on palliative care, which are predominant on the ABC islands. For example, the fundamental differences in end-of-life communication between collectivist and individualist cultures can significantly impact the care needs and experience, as these are deeply rooted in cultural values. A systematic review by Pun (2022) on collectivist cultures in East Asia emphasizes that, to conduct ACP, it is essential to establish a strong relationship between the HCP and the care recipient, and to be culturally sensitive and respectful regarding the values and needs of the person with dementia [37, 38]. Together with the expressed needs of DEDICATED ambassadors on the ABC islands, this underscores the need to further refine the DEDICATED approach to the applicable context. In addition, DEDICATED ambassadors indicated that, if the approach were translated into their local language, it might facilitate its implementation in larger groups of HCPs [39]. This translation can also benefit people with dementia, as people increasingly use their native language as they age [32, 40].

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. First, recruiting participants for the focus groups proved challenging. High staff turnover within the organizations where the DEDICATED approach was implemented meant that several DEDICATED ambassadors were no longer involved in the project. A large number of ambassadors (n = 52) did not respond to our invitation to participate, thereby limiting our insight into their current level of engagement. Consequently, the participating ambassadors were likely those who remained most enthusiastic and motivated, which may have introduced selection bias. Second, language discrepancies may have posed a limitation during the focus groups. The participating DEDICATED ambassadors’ primary language was Papiamento, whereas the focus group sessions were conducted in Dutch. Although most participants were able to communicate effectively in Dutch, this language difference may have hindered some participants from fully articulating their thoughts and emotions regarding the DEDICATED approach, potentially resulting in less detailed or nuanced responses. Third, the DEDICATED questionnaire was translated into Papiamento, with specific linguistic adjustments made for each of the three islands, as Papiamento vocabulary and spelling showed variations. The translation from Dutch to Papiamento was conducted rigorously, incorporating input from local specialists and native speakers to enhance clarity and relevance for the ambassadors completing the questionnaire. However, we did not conduct a forward-backward translation, and the translated versions of the questionnaire were not formally validated, which may limit the interpretability of the results. Full validation was beyond the scope of this study. We aim to validate the DEDICATED questionnaire for the ABC islands for future phases of the DEDICATED project.

Conclusion

The tools and training of the DEDICATED approach were perceived as valuable by the ambassadors, enhancing their awareness, knowledge, and skills in delivering person-centered palliative care and conducting ACP. In addition, we can conclude that extensive collaboration with local healthcare organizations fostered readiness for implementation. However, cultural, organizational, and contextual factors could pose barriers to the full implementation of the DEDICATED approach in the provision of palliative dementia care. A key lesson learned is that greater cultural sensitivity is necessary to adapt the approach to the context of the ABC islands. The approach will therefore be further refined and tailored for implementation on the ABC islands, with the potential to improve both the quality of palliative care and the quality of life of people with dementia.

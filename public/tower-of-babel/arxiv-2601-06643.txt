On Asymptotic Properties of Certain B B -splines in Terms of Theta-like Functions
Michael I. Ganzburg

Abstract
. The asymptotic behavior of the Mellin transform of the associated B B -splines B N ∗ ​ ( t ) := t − N ​ B N ​ ( t ) B_{N}^{*}(t):=t^{-N}B_{N}(t) with special knots in terms of theta-like functions is found. The proof is based on polynomial interpolation of power functions and properties of certain theta-like functions. Pointwise asymptotics of B N ∗ B_{N}^{*} and B N B_{N} are discussed as well.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we discuss the asymptotic behavior
of
B
-splines with special knots
in terms of theta-like functions.

1.1.
Asymptotics for
B
-splines

B
-splines
B_{N}(t)=B_{N}\left(t,{\Omega}\right),\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
with sets
{\Omega}
of
N+2
knots
were introduced by Curry and Schoenberg in seminal paper

[
8
]
(see Eq. (
3.1
) for the definition of
B_{N}
).
B
-splines play
an important role in numerical analysis, wavelets, and approximation theory
(see, e.g.,
[
18
,
6
,
3
,
19
]
) and,
in addition, they serve as Peano-like kernels
in integral representations of
interpolation differences
[
8
, Eq. (1.5)]
.

The asymptotic behavior of
B
-spline distributions
was described
in
[
8
, Theorem 6]

(see also
[
7
]
and
[
14
, Theorem 10.5.1]
)
in the following form:

Theorem 1.1
.

A monotone function
F:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,{\infty})
, with
F(-{\infty})=0
and
F({\infty})=1
,
is a limit of
B
-spline distributions

F_{N}(x):=\int_{-{\infty}}^{x}B_{N}\left(t,{\Omega}\right)dt

as
N\to{\infty}
for all x at which
F
is
continuous if and only if

F(x)=\int_{-{\infty}}^{x}\Lambda(t)dt
, where
\Lambda

is a Pólya frequency function
(see
[
14
, Sect. 7]
or
[
8
, Sect. 10.5]
for equivalent definitions).

In particular, since the characteristic function
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{[{\delta},{\infty})}
is
a Pólya distribution function (see
[
8
, Sect. 10.5]
),
there exists a sequence of
B
-spline distributions
F_{N,{\delta}}
such that

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}F_{N,{\delta}}(x)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{[{\delta},{\infty})}(x),\,{\delta}\in{\mathbb{R}}

(see also Example
1.6
with
{\delta}=0
).

Due to the criterion of Theorem
1.1
, if

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}F_{N}(x)=F(x)

for all x at which
F
is continuous,
then
F(x)=\int_{-{\infty}}^{x}\Lambda(t)dt
, where
\Lambda

is a Pólya frequency function.
The following result on the asymptotic behavior of
B
-splines
was established in
[
8
, Theorem 7]

(see also
[
14
, Theorem 10.5.2]
):

Theorem 1.2
.

The following statements hold:

(i) If
F\neq{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{[{\delta},{\infty})},\,{\delta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\Lambda\neq\Lambda_{0}
,
where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left|{\delta}_{1}\right|^{-1}e^{-t/{\delta}_{1}+{\delta}/{\delta}_{1}-1},&t/{\delta}_{1}\geq{\delta}/{\delta}_{1}-1,\\
0,&t/{\delta}_{1}<{\delta}/{\delta}_{1}-1,\end{array}\right.\qquad{\delta}\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad{\delta}_{1}\neq 0,

then

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}B_{N}(t,{\Omega})=\Lambda(t)

(1.2)

uniformly on
{\mathbb{R}}
;

(ii) if
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}
, then (
1.2
) holds uniformly in t outside of an arbitrarily small neighborhood
of the point
t={\delta}-{\delta}_{1}
, where
\Lambda_{0}
is discontinuous;

(iii) if
F={\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{[{\delta},{\infty})},\,{\delta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then
\lim_{N\to{\infty}}B_{N}(t,{\Omega})=0

uniformly in t outside of an arbitrarily small neighborhood
of the point
t={\delta}
.

Theorem
1.2
was used in
[
8
, Sect. 9]
and
[
18
, Sect. 4]

to find the asymptotic behavior of certain
B
-splines
(see Examples
1.3
,
1.5
, and
1.6
).

Example 1.3
.

Equidistant knots,

{\Omega}=\left\{k-(N+1)/2:0\leq k\leq N+1\right\}
.
The following
limit relation in terms of the Gaussian function holds
for the scaled cardinal
B
-spline:

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}\sqrt{(N+1)/12}\,\,B_{N}\left(\sqrt{(N+1)/12}\,t,{\Omega}\right)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp\left(-t^{2}/2\right)

(1.3)

uniformly on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Relation (
1.3
) has been known at least since the early 1900s
(see, Sommerfeld
[
20
]
, Tricomi
[
23
]
, Curry-Schoenberg
[
8
, Example 9.4]
, and
Unser et al.
[
24
]
).
This relation was extended by Brinks

[
4
, Theorem 2]

to the derivatives of
B_{N}
.
Xu and Wang
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]

found the convergence orders of the approximation processes
for the derivatives of
B_{N}
in (
1.3
).

Example 1.4
.

Not too concentrated knots.
Let
{\Omega}=\left\{t_{k}:0\leq k\leq N+1\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, where
the knots satisfy the following two conditions: 1.

\sum_{k=0}^{N+1}t_{k}=0
and 2.

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N+1}\left|t_{k}\right|^{3}\right)/\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N+1}t_{k}^{2}\right)^{3/2}=0
.
Using probabilistic techniques, Rzeszut and Wojciechowski
[
17
, Theorem 1]
extended (
1.3
)
to the wide class of sets
{\Omega}
in the following form:

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}\,\,\frac{\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N+1}t_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}}{N+2}\,\,B_{N}\left(\frac{\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N+1}t_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}}{N+2}\,\,t,{\Omega}\right)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp\left(-t^{2}/2\right)

(1.4)

uniformly on
{\mathbb{R}}
. In addition, the authors generalized and strengthened results from

[
4
]
and
[
25
]
by extending
(
1.4
) to the derivatives of
B_{N}
and finding
the convergence orders of the approximation processes with power weights
for the derivatives of
B_{N}
in (
1.4
).

Example 1.5
.

Knots of reciprocals,

N=2\nu,\,\nu\in{\mathbb{N}},\,{\Omega}=\left\{1/(2k-1):-\nu\leq k\leq\nu+1\right\}
.
The following relation in terms of a non-Gaussian function holds:

\lim_{\nu\to{\infty}}\frac{1}{\nu+1}B_{2\nu}\left(\frac{t}{2\nu+2},{\Omega}\right)=\frac{2}{\pi\cosh t}

(1.5)

uniformly on
{\mathbb{R}}

(see
[
8
, Example 9.5]
).
Comparing (
1.3
) and (
1.5
), the authors of
[
8
]
noted that
the knots density in (
1.5
) is higher near the origin, and
as the result,
1/\cosh t

decays so much more slowly than
\exp\left(-t^{2}/2\right)
.
In addition, it is easy to see that condition 2. of Example
1.4
is not satisfied for these knots.

Example 1.6
.

Chebyshev knots,

{\Omega}=\left\{\cos\frac{k\pi}{N+1}:0\leq k\leq N+1\right\}
.
The knots coincide with the
zeros of the polynomial
(x^{2}-1)U_{N}(x)
of degree
N+2
,
where
U_{N}
is the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind.
In particular,
B_{N}
is a perfect
B
-spline on
[-1,1]

(see Schoenberg
[
18
, Theorem 1]
).
Schoenberg
[
18
, Theorem 4]
also showed that

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}B_{N}\left(t,{\Omega}\right)=0,\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus D,

(1.6)

uniformly in t outside of an arbitrarily small neighborhood
D

of the point
t=0
.
In addition, the knots satisfy both conditions of Example
1.4
since

\sum_{k=0}^{N+1}\cos^{2}\frac{k\pi}{N+1}=(N+3)/2
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Eq. 1.351(2)]
).
Therefore, the following Gaussian asymptotic is a consequence
of (
1.4
):

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}\,\,\frac{2^{-1/2}(N+3)^{1/2}}{N+2}\,\,B_{N}\left(\frac{2^{-1/2}(N+3)^{1/2}}{N+2}\,\,t,{\Omega}\right)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp\left(-t^{2}/2\right).

(1.7)

Hence, (
1.7
) describes the asymptotic behavior of
B_{N}\left(t,{\Omega}\right)

in a neighborhood of the origin. Both asymptotics (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) verify convergence of
the
B_{N}\left(t,{\Omega}\right)
to the delta function as
N\to{\infty}
(cf. Theorem
1.2
(iii)).

However, there are only a few examples (including relations (
1.5
) and (
1.6
))
of sequences of
B
-splines that converge to a non-Gaussian function.

In this paper, we prove
limit relations of the form:

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}A_{N}M\left(B_{N}^{*}\left(C_{N}\cdot,{\Omega}\right),s\right)=M(\varTheta(\cdot),s)

(1.8)

for so-called ”associated”
B
-splines
B_{N}^{*}(t,{\Omega}):=t^{-N}B_{N}(t,{\Omega})

with special sets of knots
that include the squared equidistant knots and
the squared zeros of the Gegenbauer and Hermite polynomials
(see Theorem
6.1
).
Here,
M(f,s)
is the Mellin transform of

f,\,\varTheta
is a theta-like function defined in Sect. 4,
and
A_{N},C_{N}
are constants
given explicitly.

In certain cases, pointwise asymptotic relations of the form

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}A_{N}B_{N}^{*}\left(C_{N}t,{\Omega}\right)=\varTheta(t),\qquad t\in[0,{\infty}),

(1.9)

and the corresponding asymptotics for
B_{N}
follow from (
1.8
)
(see Theorem
6.2
). In particular, we prove (
1.9
) for

B
-splines with Chebyshev-like knots (see Corollaries
6.4

and
6.7
)
and find the asymptotic behavior of the perfect
B
-spline from Example
1.6

in a right neighborhood of the point
-1
.
(see Corollary
6.7
and Remark
6.8
).

The main results of the paper, along with their proofs, are given
in Section
6
. The proof of (
1.8
) of Theorem
6.1
is based on
polynomial interpolation of power functions developed by the author
in
[
12
]
(see Section
2
), properties of
B
-splines and
associated
B
-splines (see Section
3
),
and properties of certain theta-like functions (see Section
4
).

To derive (
1.9
) of Theorem
6.2
from (
1.8
)
and also to prove Corollary
6.4
,
we need certain
inequalities for the Fourier and Mellin transforms of analytic functions
and some estimates of the Chebyshev polynomials (see Section
5
).
In addition, Section
5
contains certain standard facts about the
Mellin transform.
Notation and some preliminaries are given below.

1.2.
Notation and Preliminaries

Let
{\mathbb{N}}:=\{1,\,2,\ldots\},\,{\mathbb{R}}
be the
set of all real numbers,

{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{R}}+i{\mathbb{R}}
be the set of all complex numbers,
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
be the set
of all univariate algebraic polynomials with
real coefficients of degree at most
n
.
Next, we denote by
C(I)
the set of all
complex-valued continuous
functions on a finite or infinite interval

I
and by

C^{(M)}(I),\,M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the set of all complex-valued

M
-times continuously
differentiable functions on
I
.
Next, let
L_{1}(I)
be the space of all integrable
complex-valued functions
F

on an interval
I
with the finite norm

\|F\|_{L_{1}(I)}:=\int_{I}|F(y)|dy
.

In addition, we need the set
{\mathcal{L}}
of all rapidly decreasing
functions
F\in C([0,{\infty}))
, satisfying
the condition
\lim_{t\to{\infty}}t^{\eta}F(t)=0

for all
\eta\in[0,{\infty})
.
Note that if
F\in{\mathcal{L}}
, then the function

t^{\eta}F(t)
is uniformly continuous
on
[0,{\infty})
and belongs to
L_{1}([0,{\infty}))

for all
\eta\in[0,{\infty})
.

We also use the following special functions:
the gamma function
{\Gamma}(s)
,
the upper incomplete gamma function
{\Gamma}(s,\tau),\,\tau\in[0,{\infty})
,
the Riemann zeta function
{\zeta}(s)
,
the Dirichlet beta function
{\beta}(s)
,
and the theta functions
\vartheta_{1}(z,s)
and

\vartheta_{4}(z,s),\,z\in{\mathbb{C}},\,s\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Throughout the paper,
N\in{\mathbb{N}},\,s\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and the index
d
takes two values: either
d=0
or
d=1
.
We also use the following notation:

h_{d}(z):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cosh z,&d=0,\\
\sinh z,&d=1,\end{array}\right.\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}.

(1.10)

Next, let
f\in C([A,B])
and let
R_{n+1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n+1}
be a polynomial with
zeros
z_{k}\in[A,B],\,1\leq k\leq n+1
, of multiplicity
1
.
The Lagrange interpolating polynomial to
f
at the nodes
z_{k},\,1\leq k\leq n+1
,
is denoted by
L_{n}(z)=L_{n}\left(z,f(z),R_{n+1}(z)\right)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
.

We also use the notations

\lesssim
and
\gtrsim
in the following sense:

{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\lesssim\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)
or

{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\gtrsim\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)

means that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of the essential parameters

\tau,\,\gamma,\ldots
such that

{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\leq C\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)
or

{\varphi}(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)\geq C\delta(\tau,\gamma,\ldots)

for the relevant ranges of
\tau,\gamma,\ldots
.
The dependence of
C
on certain parameters is indicated
by using subscripts, e.g.,
\lesssim_{a,b}
. The absence of the subscripts means that

C
is an absolute constant.

1.3.
More Preliminaries: Special Sequences of Polynomials

Here, we define a special class of sequences of algebraic polynomials
whose zeros serve as interpolation nodes. Examples of sequences from this
class are discussed as well.
The definition and the examples are taken from author’s monograph
[
12
, Sect. 3]
.

Let
{\beta}={\beta}_{(d)}=\left({\beta}_{N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
and
{\gamma}={\gamma}_{(d)}=\left({\gamma}_{N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
be
increasing sequences of positive numbers and
{\delta}={\delta}_{(d)}=\left({\delta}_{N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}

a decreasing sequence of positive numbers satisfying the conditions

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to{\infty}}{\beta}_{N}=\lim_{N\to{\infty}}{\gamma}_{N}=\lim_{N\to{\infty}}{\delta}_{N}^{-1}={\infty}.

Definition 1.7
.

[
12
, Definition 3.1.1]

Let
\Pi_{d}:=\left(P_{2N+d}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of even (if
d=0
) or odd (if
d=1
)
polynomials
P_{2N+d}(z)=z^{2N+d}+\ldots\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2N+d}
,
having only real zeros
\pm x_{k}=\pm x_{k,N,d},\,1-d\leq k\leq N,\,x_{0}=0
,
of multiplicity
1
and satisfying
the following asymptotic property:
for any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
|z|\leq{\gamma}_{N}
, the following inequality
holds:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\beta_{N}^{d}\,P_{2N+d}(z/\beta_{N})}{P_{2N+d}^{(d)}(0)}-\cos(z-d\pi/2)\right|\lesssim_{\Pi_{d}}\delta_{N}\min(|z|^{2},1)h_{d}(|z|).

Then we write
\Pi_{d}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{d}({\beta},{\gamma},{\delta})
.

Examples of polynomial sequences, satisfying Definition
1.7
,
include the Gegenbauer and Hermite polynomials and,
in addition, the polynomials with equidistant zeros.
In each example presented below, we include formulae for
P_{2N+d}
and
{\beta}_{N}

and also conditions on
{\gamma}_{N}
and
{\delta}_{N}
that guarantee the inclusion

\left(P_{2N+d}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{d}({\beta},{\gamma},{\delta})
.
Other examples (such as, the Williams–Apostol and Lommel polynomials)
and a counterexample of the Laguerre polynomials for
{\alpha}\neq-1/2
are given in

[
12
, Sect. 3.2]
.

Example 1.8
.

Normalized Gegenbauer polynomials on
[-1,1]
(see
[
12
, Sect. 3.2.1]
): for
{\lambda}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle P_{2N+d}=\frac{{\Gamma}({\lambda})(2N+d)!}{{\Gamma}(2N+d+{\lambda})2^{2N+d}}C^{\lambda}_{2N+d},

\displaystyle{\beta}_{N}=2N+d+{\lambda},\qquad{\gamma}_{N}=o(N^{2/3}),\qquad{\gamma}_{N}\gtrsim\log(N+1),\qquad{\delta}_{N}\geq{\gamma}_{N}^{3}/N^{2}.

The normalized Legendre polynomials and the normalized Chebyshev polynomials

T_{2N+d}
and
U_{2N+d}
of the first and second kinds
are special cases of
P_{2N+d}
for
{\lambda}=1/2,\,0,\,1
, respectively.

Example 1.9
.

Polynomials with equidistant zeros on
[-1,1]
(see
[
12
, Sect. 3.2.2]
):

\displaystyle P_{2N+d}(z)=z^{d}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(\left(\frac{2k+d-1}{2N+d-1}\right)^{2}-z^{2}\right),

\displaystyle{\beta}_{N}=(2N+d-1)\pi/2,\qquad{\gamma}_{N}=o(N^{1/2}),\qquad{\delta}_{N}\geq{\gamma}_{N}^{2}/N.

Example 1.10
.

Normalized Hermite polynomials on
{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
12
, Sect. 3.2.4]
):

\displaystyle P_{2N+d}=2^{-(2N+d)}H_{2N+d},

\displaystyle{\beta}_{N}=\sqrt{4N+2d+1},\qquad{\gamma}_{N}=o(N^{1/2}),\qquad{\gamma}_{N}\gtrsim\log(N+1),\qquad{\delta}_{N}\geq{\gamma}_{N}^{2}/N.

2.
Polynomial Interpolation of Power Functions

Let
h_{d}
be defined by (
1.10
).
The following asymptotic representations for the polynomial interpolation differences
were proved in
[
12
, Corollary 4.3.1 (a)]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\left(P_{2N+d}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{d}({\beta},{\gamma},{\delta})

(see Definition
1.7
)
and let
\left(y_{N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of real nonzero numbers
such that
\lim_{N\to{\infty}}{\beta}_{N}\left|y_{N}\right|={\infty}
.
Then the following statements hold:

(a) If

\mathrm{Re}\,s>d
, and
s-d\neq 2,\,4,\ldots,

then

\displaystyle\left|y_{N}\right|^{s-d}-L_{2N}\left(y_{N},|y|^{s-d},y^{1-d}P_{2N+d}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sin((s-d)\pi/2)}{\pi\beta_{N}^{s-d}}\frac{P_{2N+d}(y_{N})}{y_{N}^{d}P_{2N+d}^{(d)}(0)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{h_{d}(t)}dt+o(1)\right),

(2.1)

as
N\to{\infty}

and the convergence in (
2.1
)
is uniform on the line
\mathrm{Re}\,s=r>d.

(b) For a fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}},\,1\leq m\leq N
,

\displaystyle\left|y_{N}\right|^{2m}\log\left|y_{N}\right|-L_{2N}\left(y_{N},|y|^{2m}\log|y|,y^{1-d}P_{2N+d}(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\beta_{N}^{2m}}\frac{P_{2N+d}(y_{N})}{y_{N}^{d}P_{2N+d}^{(d)}(0)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2m+d-1}}{h_{d}(t)}dt+o(1)\right),

(2.2)

as
N\to{\infty}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Since
y^{1-d}P_{2N+d}(y)=y\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(y^{2}-x_{k}^{2}\right)
,
the interpolation nodes in (
2.1
) and (
2.1
) are

0,\,\pm x_{1},\ldots,\pm x_{N}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Relations between the integral in (
2.1
) (or (
2.1
))
and zeta functions are given by the following formula
(see, e.g.,
[
9
, Sect. 1.12]
):

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{h_{d}(t)}dt=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2\Gamma(s){\beta}(s),&d=0,\\
2\Gamma(s)\left(1-2^{-s}\right){\zeta}(s),&d=1,\end{array}\right.\qquad\mbox{Re}\,s>d.

Since the functions
\left|y\right|^{s-d}
and

\left|y\right|^{2m}\log\left|y\right|
and their
interpolation polynomials
L_{2N}
from Theorem
2.1

are even, we can make the substitution
u=y^{2}
and discuss
the corresponding interpolation differences on
[0,{\infty})
.
Let us define

Q_{N}(u):=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(u-x^{2}_{k}\right),

(2.4)

where
x_{k}=x_{k,N,d},\,1\leq k\leq N,
are positive zeros of
P_{2N+d}
.
It is easy to see that

P_{2N+d}^{(d)}(0)=Q_{N}(0).

(2.5)

In the following corollary, we rewrite relations (
2.1
) and (
2.1
)
with the interpolation nodes
0,\,x_{1}^{2},\ldots,x_{N}^{2}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\left(P_{2N+d}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{d}({\beta},{\gamma},{\delta})

and let
Q_{N}
be defined by (
2.4
),
where
x_{k},\,1\leq k\leq N,
are the positive zeros of
P_{2N+d}
.
In addition, let
\left(u_{N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive numbers
such that
u_{N}\notin\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}
and

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}{\beta}_{N}u_{N}={\infty}
.
Then the following statements hold:

(a) If

\mathrm{Re}\,s>d
and
s-d\neq 2,\,4,\ldots,

then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to{\infty}}\frac{\beta_{N}^{s-d}Q_{N}(0)}{Q_{N}\left(u_{N}^{2}\right)}\left(u_{N}^{s-d}-L_{N}\left(u^{2}_{N},u^{\frac{s-d}{2}},uQ_{N}(u)\right)\right)=\frac{2\sin((s-d)\pi/2)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{s-1}}{h_{d}(t)}dt.

(2.6)

The convergence in (
2.6
)
is uniform on the line
\mathrm{Re\,s}=r>d
.

(b) For a fixed

3.
Properties of
B
-splines and Associated
B
-splines

Definition 3.1
.

The
B
-spline
B_{N}
of degree
N
with the set of knots

{\Omega}=\left\{t_{0},\,t_{1},\ldots,t_{N+1}\right\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}

is defined by the formulae

B_{N}(t)=B_{N}(t,{\Omega}):=(N+1)\sum_{v\in{\Omega}}\frac{(v-t)^{N}_{+}}{W^{\prime}(v)},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}};\qquad W(u):=\prod_{v\in{\Omega}}(u-v).

(3.1)

Definition 3.2
.

The associated
B
-spline
B_{N}^{*}
with the set of knots

{\Omega}=\left\{t_{0},\,t_{1},\ldots,t_{N+1}\right\}\subset[0,{\infty})

is defined by the formula

B_{N}^{*}(t)=B_{N}^{*}(t,{\Omega}):=t^{-N}B_{N}(t,{\Omega}),\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}},

(3.2)

where
B_{N}^{*}(0):=\lim_{t\to 0+}B_{N}^{*}(t)
.

The next three properties of
B_{N}
are well known
(see, e.g.,
[
19
, Theorems 4.17, 4.23, 4.24]
, respectively).

Property 3.3
.

Support and positivity:

B_{N}(t,{\Omega})\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}=0,&t\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(\min{\Omega},\max{\Omega}),\\
>0,&t\in(\min{\Omega},\max{\Omega}).\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

Property 3.4
.

Normalization:

\int_{\min{\Omega}}^{\max{\Omega}}B_{N}(t,{\Omega})dt=1.

(3.4)

Property 3.5
.

Symmetry:

B_{N}(t,{\Omega})=(-1)^{N+1}(N+1)\sum_{v\in{\Omega}}\frac{(t-v)^{N}_{+}}{W^{\prime}(v)},

(3.5)

where
W
is defined in (
3.1
).

It is easy to prove the next two properties of
B_{N}
and
B_{N}^{*}
.

Property 3.6
.

First interval:

B_{N}(t,{\Omega})=\frac{(-1)^{N+1}(N+1)(t-\min{\Omega})^{N}}{W^{\prime}(\min{\Omega})},\qquad t\in[\min{\Omega},\min({\Omega}\setminus\{\min{\Omega}\})].

(3.6)

Proof.

The property immediately follows from (
3.5
).
∎

Property 3.7
.

Support and positivity of
B_{N}^{*}
:

B_{N}^{*}(t,{\Omega})\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}=0,&t\in(0,\min{\Omega}]\cup[\max{\Omega},{\infty}),\\
=\frac{(-1)^{N+1}(N+1)}{W^{\prime}(\min{\Omega})},&t=\min{\Omega}=0,\\
>0,&t\in(\min{\Omega},\max{\Omega}).\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

Proof.

Relation (
3.7
) immediately
follows from relations (
3.2
), (
3.3
), and (
3.6
).
∎

Note that relation (

) shows that

has a jump

if

.

Finally, we prove an important extension of
the Curry–Schoenberg result
[

, p. 74]
.

Property 3.8
.

Polynomial interpolation:

If
f\in C([\min{\Omega},\max{\Omega}])\cap C^{(N+1)}((\min{\Omega},\max{\Omega}])
and

\int_{\min{\Omega}}^{\max{\Omega}}\left|f^{(N+1)}(t)\right|(t-\min{\Omega})^{N}dt<{\infty},

(3.8)

then

f(u)-L_{N}(u,f(u),w(u))=\frac{w(u)}{(N+1)!}\int_{\min{\Omega}}^{\max{\Omega}}B_{N}(t,{\Omega})f^{(N+1)}(t)\,dt,\qquad u\in{\Omega},

(3.9)

where
w(u):=\prod_{v\in{\Omega},v\neq u}(u-v)
.

Proof.

Assume, without loss of generality, that
\min{\Omega}=0
and
\max{\Omega}=1
.
Next, let
\tilde{f}
be an extension of
f
to
[0,2]
such
that
\tilde{f}\in C^{(N+1)}((0,2])
.

Then Property
3.8
is a consequence of the following property
of the weighted integral modulus of continuity:

\lim_{\tau\to 0+}\int_{0}^{1}\left|F(t+\tau)-F(t)\right|\,t^{N}\,dt=0,

(3.10)

where
F:=\tilde{f}^{(N+1)}
.
Indeed, if
f\in C^{(N+1)}([0,1])
, then (
3.9
) is
well known (see, e.g.,
[
8
, p. 74]
or
[
19
, Theorem 4.23]
).
Hence the following equality holds:

\tilde{f}(u+\tau)-L_{N}(u,\tilde{f}(u+\tau),w(u))=\frac{w(u+\tau)}{(N+1)!}\int_{0}^{1}B_{N}(t,{\Omega})F(t+\tau)\,dt,\qquad u\in{\Omega},

(3.11)

since
\tilde{f}(\cdot+\tau)\in C^{(N+1)}([0,1])
for any
\tau\in(0,1]
.
Then setting
\tau\to 0+
in (
3.11
), we arrive at the left-hand side of (
3.9
)
since
f\in C([0,1])
,
while the right-hand side of (
3.9
) follows from
(
3.11
) and (
3.10
)
since

is bounded on
[0,1]

by Definition
3.2
and Property
3.7

To prove (
3.10

\tau
and
{\delta}
,
satisfying

, the following inequalities are valid:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|F(t+\tau)-F(t)\right|\,t^{N}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|F(t)\right|\,t^{N}\,dt+\int_{0}^{\delta}\left|F(t+\tau)\right|\,t^{N}\,dt+\int_{\delta}^{1}\left|F(t+\tau)-F(t)\right|\,t^{N}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{2{\delta}}\left|F(t)\right|\,t^{N}\,dt+\int_{\delta}^{1}\left|F(t+\tau)-F(t)\right|\,dt:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

(3.12)

Furthermore, in view of condition (
3.8
), we see that given
{\varepsilon}>0

there exists

{\delta}={\delta}({\varepsilon})\in(0,1/2]

such that

. Finally, there exists

\tau=\tau({\varepsilon})\in(0,{\delta}]

such that

and (

3
). This completes the proof of the property.
∎

4.
Theta-like Functions and their Properties

Let us define two theta-like functions,

\varTheta_{d}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1-\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{\exp\left(-(\pi^{2}/4)(2k+1)^{2}t^{-1}\right)}{2k+1},&d=0,\\

(4.1)

\varTheta_{1}
coincides with
\vartheta_{4}\left(0,i\pi t^{-1}\right)

while
\varTheta_{0}

(see
[

and (

\vartheta_{4}
and

are given in the notation of Rademacher
[

Certain properties of

Lemma 4.1

(a)
\varTheta_{d}
is continuous on
(0,{\infty})

and
\sup_{t\in(0,{\infty})}\left|\varTheta_{d}(t)\right|<{\infty}

(b) The following equality holds:

H_{d}(t):=t\int_{0}^{\infty}\varTheta_{d}(1/z)e^{-tz}dz=\frac{t^{d/2}}{h_{d}\left(\sqrt{t}\right)},\qquad t>0,

(4.2)

where

is defined by (

(c) The following asymptotics hold:

\varTheta_{d}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}4(\pi t)^{-1/2}e^{-t/4}\left(1+O\left(t^{-1}\right)\right),&d=0,\\
2(t/\pi)^{1/2}e^{-t/4}\left(1+O\left(e^{-2t}\right)\right),&d=1,\end{array}\right.\qquad t\to{\infty}.

(4.3)

Proof.

(a) The statement immediately follows from (
4.1
).

(b) We note that the Laplace transformation
H_{d}(t)/t
exists for
t>0
by statement (a).
Then integrating the corresponding exponential functions from (

) and (

)
and using the partial fraction decomposition of hyperbolic functions, we have for

\displaystyle H_{0}(t)=1-\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{1}{(2k+1)\left(\pi^{2}(k+1/2)^{2}+t\right)}=\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{2k+1}{\pi^{2}(k+1/2)^{2}+t}=\frac{1}{\cosh\sqrt{t}};

\displaystyle H_{1}(t)=1+2t\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{1}{(\pi k)^{2}+t}=\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\sinh\sqrt{t}}.

Hence (

(c) The following

\displaystyle\varTheta_{1}(t)=2(t/\pi)^{1/2}e^{-t/4}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-k(k+1)t}\right),\qquad t>0,

see
[

or
[

, p. 548]

)

To prove (

\displaystyle\varTheta_{0}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle:=

5.
The Mellin Transform

We first need certain known facts
about the
Mellin transform
defined by the formula

\displaystyle M(F,s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(t)\,t^{s-1}dt,\qquad\mbox{Re}\,s>0,\quad t^{\mathrm{Re}\,s-1}F(t)\in L_{1}([0,{\infty})).

In particular,
M(F,\cdot)
exists for
F\in{\mathcal{L}}
.

In addition,
the
Mellin convolution

of two functions
F\in{\mathcal{L}}
and
G\in{\mathcal{L}}
is defined by the integrals

H(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\tau)G\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)\,\frac{d\tau}{\tau}=\int_{0}^{\infty}G(\tau)F\left(\frac{t}{\tau}\right)\,\frac{d\tau}{\tau}.

(5.1)

Proposition 5.1
.

(see, e.g.,
[
5
, Sect. 4, Theorem 3]
)
The Mellin convolution
H
defined by (
5.1
)
belongs to
{\mathcal{L}}
. Moreover, the following relation holds:

M(H,s)=M(F,s)M(G,s).

(5.2)

Proposition 5.2
.

(see, e.g.,
[
5
, Sect. 6, Theorem 6]
)
If
F\in{\mathcal{L}}
and
M(F,r+i\cdot)\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
for some
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then

\displaystyle F(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-{\infty}}^{\infty}M(F,r+i\tau)\,t^{-r-i\tau}d\tau,\qquad t>0.

Next, we define a condition in terms of the Mellin transform
that is used in Theorem
6.2
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\left(u_{N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive numbers,
and let
{\beta}=\left({\beta}_{N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
be an increasing sequence of positive numbers
with
\lim_{N\to{\infty}}{\beta}_{N}={\infty}
.
In addition, let
\left(G_{2N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of even
polynomials from
{\mathcal{P}}_{2N}
,
having
2N
real zeros
of multiplicity
1,\,G_{2N}(0)\neq 0
. We say that
\left(G_{2N}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the

(r,{\beta})
-Condition for a given
r>0
if there exist constants
N_{0}=N_{0}(r)

and
\mu=\mu(r)
such that the following inequality holds:

\sup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}},\,N\geq N_{0}}{\beta}_{N}^{r}\left|\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-2.04861pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle{\infty}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{t^{s-1}}{\left(1+\left(t/u_{N}\right)^{2}\right)G_{2N}(it)}dt\right|\lesssim_{r}\left|\mathrm{Im}\,s\right|^{\mu}e^{-(\pi/2)\left|\mathrm{Im}\,s\right|},\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,s=r.

(5.3)

The following estimates of the Fourier and Mellin transforms of analytic functions
are helpful for the verification of the
(r,{\beta})
-Condition for certain polynomials.
The Fourier transform of a function
f\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})

is denoted by the formula

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(v):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x)e^{ivx}dx,\qquad v\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let a complex-valued function
f
satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

f
is analytic on the strip
S_{\delta}:=\{x+iy:x\in{\mathbb{R}},\,|y|<{\delta}\},\,{\delta}>0
;

(ii)

f
is real-valued on
{\mathbb{R}}
;

(iii)

\sup_{\xi\in S_{\delta}}|\mathrm{Re}\,f(\xi)|<{\infty}
;

(iv)

\sup_{|y|<{\delta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\mathrm{Re}\,f(x+iy)|\,dx\leq K({\delta})<{\infty}
.

Then
f\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\left|\widehat{f}(v)\right|\leq 2K({\delta})e^{-{\delta}|v|},\qquad v\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(5.4)

Proof.

If conditions (i), (ii), and (iii) are satisfied, then it is known
(see
[
1
, Sect. 110]
or
[
21
, Sect. 3.8.5]
) that for almost all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}

there exists the limit

\lim_{y\to\pm{\delta}}\mathrm{Re}\,f(x+iy)=g(x),\qquad\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|g(x)|<{\infty},

(5.5)

and the following representation holds:

f(x)=\frac{1}{2{\delta}}\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{R}}$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{g(t)}{\cosh\frac{\pi(x-t)}{2{\delta}}}dt.

(5.6)

In addition to (

since by (
5.5

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|g(x)|dx\leq\liminf_{y\to\pm{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathrm{Re}\,f(x+iy)|dx\leq K({\delta}).

(5.7)

Then (

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{f}(v)\right|=\left|\widehat{g}(v)\right|/\cosh{\delta}v\leq 2K({\delta})e^{-{\delta}|v|}.

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
r>0
be a fixed number, and
let a complex-valued function
F
satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

F
is analytic on the region

A_{\delta}:=\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}:\,|\arg w|<{\delta}\},\,0<{\delta}<\pi
;

(ii)

F
is real-valued on
{\mathbb{R}}
;

(iii)

\sup_{w\in A_{\delta}}\left|\mathrm{Re}\,\left(w^{r}F(w)\right)\right|<{\infty}

(iv)

\sup_{|\arg w|<{\delta}}\int_{0}^{\infty}R^{r-1}\left|\mathrm{Re}\,\left(e^{ir\arg w}F\left(R\,e^{i\arg w}\right)\right)\right|\,dR\leq K_{1}({\delta},r)<{\infty}

Then

\displaystyle\left|M(F,r+iv)\right|\leq 2K_{1}({\delta},r)e^{-{\delta}|v|},\qquad v\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

Making the standard substitution
t=e^{x}
in the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}F(t)\,t^{r+iv-1}dt
, we reduce Lemma
5.5
to Lemma
5.4

for
f(\xi)=e^{r\xi}F(e^{\xi})
.
∎

6.21
),
and statements (b), (c), and (d) of Proposition
6.3
,
we see that by the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to{\infty}}Q_{N,d}(t)=\varTheta_{d}(t),\qquad t\in[0,{\infty}).

Thus, (
6.4
) is established.
∎

The following corollary shows that limit relation (
6.4
)
is valid for the Chebyshev polynomials.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
{\beta}_{N}={\beta}_{N,{\lambda}}:=2N+d+{\lambda}
and let
P_{2N+d}:=2^{-(2N+d-1+{\lambda})}Q_{2N+d,{\lambda}},\,N\in{\mathbb{N}},\,{\lambda}=0
or
{\lambda}=1
,
be the normalized Chebyshev polynomial of the first or second kind (see (
5.10
) or (
5.11
)
and also Example
1.8
).
In addition, let
\left(u_{N,{\lambda}}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive numbers
such that
u_{N,{\lambda}}
is not a zero of
Q_{2N+d,{\lambda}}
and

\lim_{N\to{\infty}}{\beta}_{N,{\lambda}}u_{N,{\lambda}}={\infty}
.
Then the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to{\infty}}\left[\frac{u_{N,{\lambda}}^{2}}{N\,2^{2N+d-1+{\lambda}}}B_{N}^{*}\left(\frac{N}{(2N+d+{\lambda})^{2}}t,{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}\right)\right]=\varTheta_{d}(t),\qquad t\in[0,{\infty});

(6.22)

\displaystyle B_{N}\left(\frac{N}{(2N+d+{\lambda})^{2}}t,{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N\,2^{2N+d-1+{\lambda}}}{u_{N,{\lambda}}^{2}}\varTheta_{d}(t)\left(\frac{N}{(2N+d+{\lambda})^{2}}t\right)^{N}(1+o(1)),\qquad t\in[0,{\infty});

(6.23)

as
N\to{\infty}
, where

\displaystyle{\Omega}_{N+2,d,0}:=\{0,u_{N,0}^{2}\}\bigcup\left\{\cos^{2}\left(\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{4N+2d}\right):1\leq k\leq N\right\},

(6.24)

\displaystyle{\Omega}_{N+2,d,1}:=\{0,u_{N,1}^{2}\}\bigcup\left\{\cos^{2}\left(\frac{k\pi}{2N+d+1}\right):1\leq k\leq N\right\}.

(6.25)

Proof.

The sequence
\left(2^{-(2N+d-1+{\lambda})}Q_{2N+d,{\lambda}}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
{\mathbb{P}}_{d}({\beta},{\gamma},{\delta})

by Example
1.8
and, in addition,
the sequence

\left(t^{-d}Q_{2N+d,{\lambda}}(t)/Q_{2N+d,{\lambda}}^{(d)}(0)\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}

satisfies the
(r,{\beta})
-Condition by Lemma
5.8
.
Note also that
\prod_{k=1}^{N}x_{k}^{2}=2^{-(2N+d-1+{\lambda})}
.
Therefore, (
6.22
) follows from Theorem
6.2
while (
6.23
) is an immediate consequence
of (
6.22
) and (
3.2
).
∎

Remark 6.5
.

It follows from (
6.23
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to{\infty}}B_{N}\left(\frac{N}{(2N+d+{\lambda})^{2}}t,{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}\right)=4\lim_{N\to{\infty}}NB_{N}\left(t,\frac{(2N+d+{\lambda})^{2}}{N}{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}\right)=0

for any
t\neq 0
, i.e., the
B
-spline converges to the delta function as
N\to{\infty}
(cf. Theorem
1.2
(iii)
and Example
1.6
).
In addition, note that all knots in Theorem
6.2
and Corollary
6.4
are nonnegative,
so condition 1. of Example
1.4
is not satisfied. That is why any comparisons between
(
6.4
) and (
6.22
) with (
1.4
) are not possible.

Remark 6.6
.

We conjecture that relations like (
6.22
) and (
6.23
) are also valid for the normalized
Gegenbauer and Hermite polynomials and for the polynomials with equidistant zeros (see Examples
1.8
–
1.10
).

It turns out that relation (
6.23
) can be reformulated
for a
B
-spline with knots in the interval
[-1,{\infty})
.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let

\displaystyle{\Omega}_{N+2,d,0}^{*}:=\{-1,2u_{N,0}^{2}-1\}\bigcup\left\{\cos\left(\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2N+d}\right):1\leq k\leq N\right\},

(6.26)

\displaystyle{\Omega}_{N+2,d,1}^{*}:=\{-1,2u_{N,1}^{2}-1\}\bigcup\left\{\cos\left(\frac{2k\pi}{2N+d+1}\right):1\leq k\leq N\right\},

(6.27)

where
\left(u_{N,{\lambda}}\right)_{N=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of positive numbers
such that
\lim_{N\to{\infty}}N\,u_{N,{\lambda}}={\infty}
and the
two sets in the right-hand sides of
(
6.26
) and (
6.27
) are disjoint.
Then

\displaystyle B_{N}\left(\frac{2N}{(2N+d+{\lambda})^{2}}t-1,{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N\,2^{2N+d-1+{\lambda}}}{2u_{N,{\lambda}}^{2}}\varTheta_{d}(t)\left(\frac{2N}{(2N+d+{\lambda})^{2}}t\right)^{N}\left(1+o(1)\right),\qquad t\in[0,{\infty}),\quad{\lambda}=0,\,1,

(6.28)

as
N\to{\infty}
.

Proof.

By elementary calculations,

B_{N}\left(y,{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}\right)=2B_{N}\left(2y-1,{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}^{*}\right),

(6.29)

where sets
{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}
and
{\Omega}_{N+2,d,{\lambda}}^{*}
are defined by
(
6.24
)–(
6.27
).
Thus, (
6.7
) follows from (
6.23
) and (
6.29
).
∎

Remark 6.8
.

If we choose
u_{N,1}=1
, then the set
{\Omega}_{N+2,1,1}^{*}\subseteq[-1,1]
defined in (
6.27
) consists
of all zeros of the polynomial
(x^{2}-1)U_{N}(x)\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N+2}

Hence relation (

) for
d=1

{\lambda}=1
describes the asymptotic behavior of the perfect

-spline
from Example
1.6

In addition, note that if we choose

{\Omega}_{N+2,0,0}^{*}\subseteq[-1,1]

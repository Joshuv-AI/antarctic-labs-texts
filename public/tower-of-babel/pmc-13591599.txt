Isolated Agnathia-Otocephaly Complex: Emergency Airway Management and Multidisciplinary Longitudinal Care Considerations
Authors: Sruti Desai, Arlene Rozzelle
Published: 2026

Problem addressed: Isolated agnathia-otocephaly can present as an unanticipated, life-threatening neonatal airway emergency at birth and poses life long challenges for ventilation, hearing, communication, feeding, and craniofacial reconstruction. We present a preterm neonate who required emergent tracheostomy at delivery after an aborted EXIT in the setting of premature labor, and propose a practical multidisciplinary longitudinal care framework.

Technique: Case summary: Ex-31-week male neonate with polyhydramnios and possible micrognathia on prenatal ultrasound and fetal MRI with anticipated airway concerns was delivered by emergency cesarean when labor precluded the planned EXIT procedure. On delivery the infant had marked microstomia, minimal to no mandible, and low-set neck-positioned ears; direct laryngoscopy and LMA were not feasible. Anesthesia and the NICU team supported the airway with a nasal trumpet while ENT secured the airway with open tracheostomy within 12 minutes of life.

Neonatal management included NICU ventilatory support, ENT tracheostomy care, and genetic/diagnostic workup. MRI was obtained with normal brain anatomy, no holoprosencephaly. The patient is now 4 months old and has been weaned from the oscillator and ventilator. He tolerates room air, but uses CPAP per pulmonology recommendations. He is using a softband BAHA for hearing with appropriate responses to sound. He has vocal folds and produces sound with a speaking valve, but has only rudimentary lingual tissue which will make communication difficult. We propose three future priorities: 1) Early audiology and hearing amplification planning (BAHA/osseointegrated options). 2) Staged communication support beginning with communication boards progressing to text-to-speech and potential future neuroprosthetic options.

3) Individualized staged craniofacial reconstruction focused on functional goals (airway, feeding, speech) with consideration of free fibula reconstruction, although there may be limited utility in this given complete mandibular agenesis.

Outcomes: Immediate: airway secured within 12 minutes of delivery.

Short-term: patient ventilating well with 3.0 neo uncuffed Bivona, oscillator weaned to room air and ongoing tracheostomy care.

Long-term: multidisciplinary follow-up addressing hearing, communication, feeding, and staged reconstruction for improvement in quality of life in patients with nonlethal otocephaly.

Prenatal imaging via fetal ultrasound and MRI was critical in enabling preparation and planning for immediate airway intervention, and was integral to this patient’s survival.

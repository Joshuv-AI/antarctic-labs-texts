Exploring artificial intelligence chatbots in pediatric fluoride education: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Nevra Karamüftüoğlu, Ezgi Aydın Varol, Cenkhan Bal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Large language model-based (LLM) chatbots are increasingly integrated into healthcare communication, offering accessible and interactive information. These artificial intelligence (AI) tools have the potential to influence caregiver health behaviors when tailored to user needs and literacy levels. In pediatric dentistry, fluoride remains a cornerstone of caries prevention but is also subject to public concerns and online misinformation, underscoring the need for reliable digital communication. This observational and exploratory study evaluated the performance of three advanced AI chatbots—ChatGPT-4.o, Google Gemini Pro, and DeepSeek V3—in providing fluoride-related information to parents and caregivers in the context of pediatric oral health. Twenty fluoride-related questions, derived from American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (AAPD) guideline themes, were presented to each chatbot in standardized sessions. Responses were independently evaluated by three blinded reviewers using validated tools: EQIP, DISCERN, Global Quality Scale (GQS), Flesch Reading Ease Score (FRES), Flesch-Kincaid Reading Grade Level (FKRGL), and iThenticate similarity index. These instruments assessed quality, reliability, readability, and originality. Inter-rater reliability was confirmed with intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs). Statistical analyses were conducted using ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis tests with appropriate post-hoc methods. ChatGPT-4.o achieved significantly higher EQIP (M = 4.32, SD = 0.43) and DISCERN (M = 4.20, SD = 0.48) scores than Gemini Pro and DeepSeek V3 (p < 0.001), indicating superior reliability and informational quality. While FRES (median = 68.5, p = 0.12) and Similarity Index (≤ 10%, p = 0.54) showed no significant differences, ChatGPT consistently produced more readable and original content. FKRGL differences were borderline (p = 0.041) but not retained after correction, and GQS outcomes were comparable. These findings suggest that ChatGPT’s superior performance is not only statistically significant but also practically relevant for enhancing parental comprehension of fluoride use. Among the evaluated models, ChatGPT-4.o demonstrated the clearest and most reliable fluoride communication. Its higher EQIP and DISCERN scores highlight its potential as a supportive tool for caregiver education in pediatric dentistry. Nonetheless, these systems should be implemented cautiously, complemented with professional oversight, and continuously validated to prevent misinformation and ensure safe clinical integration.

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) and large language models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and DeepSeek are increasingly being integrated into healthcare communication, offering real-time, interactive, and accessible information delivery1,2. Recent studies suggest that AI has the capability to influence and even modify health-related behaviors, particularly when content is tailored to user needs and literacy levels3,4.

Despite these opportunities, pediatric dentistry faces unique challenges in digital health communication. Fluoride, one of the most effective preventive agents against dental caries, has become subject to public misperceptions and online misinformation. While its caries-preventive benefits are well documented5, concerns about toxicity, neurodevelopmental risks, and systemic health effects have gained visibility in digital environments, often leading to parental hesitancy6. These misconceptions underscore the importance of reliable, accessible communication tools for caregivers.

This study is the first to comparatively evaluate three advanced AI chatbots—ChatGPT-4.o (OpenAI, San Francisco, USA), Google Gemini Pro (Google DeepMind), and DeepSeek V3 (Hangzhou DeepSeek AI Co., Ltd.)—in delivering fluoride-related information. The selection of these models was based on their widespread availability, frequent use in health-related queries, and representativeness of current state-of-the-art LLMs.

Fluoride-related questions were developed by reviewing the most recent American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (AAPD) guideline on fluoride7. Additionally, the 2024–2025 AAPD Reference Manual provides the most current policy framework on pediatric oral health recommendations, forming the foundation for question development8. Guideline themes were adapted into caregiver-focused questions to provide a standardized foundation for evaluation. The absence of direct parental input represents a limitation that future studies should address.

The aim of this study was to assess the reliability, quality, readability, and originality of fluoride-related responses generated by three AI chatbots in the context of pediatric dentistry. By doing so, this work addresses a critical gap in the literature, as no prior comparative evaluation has systematically examined multiple advanced AI models for caregiver education in pediatric oral health, nor explored their potential in clarifying fluoride-related misconceptions.

Materials and methods

Development and presentation of fluoride-related questions

This study followed a multi-step methodology to formulate, distribute, and analyze fluoride-related queries presented to the selected chatbot models (Fig. 1). Questions regarding fluoride were developed based on the most recent guideline on fluoride published by AAPD7. Key preventive themes were identified and reformulated into caregiver-focused questions. Specifically, practitioner-oriented recommendations were rephrased into plain-language items framed around common parental concerns and decision-making scenarios. While this expert-driven process provided standardization, it must be noted that direct parental input was not included, which represents a limitation of the study. Future research should integrate parental perspectives in question development.

All questions were presented to each AI model in independent conversation sessions (Fig. 2). An initial pool of items was pilot-tested on 10 questions to ensure clarity and relevance before full-scale evaluation. All finalized questions (n = 20) were presented identically across platforms. The chatbots were accessed between March 15–20, 2025, in independent conversation windows. To maintain standardization, only the first response was analyzed, and no follow-up prompts were allowed.

Evaluation criteria

The chatbot-generated responses were analyzed using several validated instruments, including EQIP, DISCERN, GQS, FRES, FKRGL, and the Similarity Index. EQIP measures completeness and structural clarity, whereas DISCERN evaluates reliability and balance of information, thereby providing complementary perspectives on content quality. GQS, originally developed for educational video content, was adapted to text evaluation in this study. It rates the overall quality and usefulness of content on a 5-point scale (1 = poor, 5 = excellent), reflecting global comprehensibility, consistency, and benefit for caregivers. All responses were independently evaluated by three blinded reviewers. Inter-rater reliability was verified using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs), with both intra- and inter-rater values exceeding 0.70, indicating acceptable agreement. For the quality scales, a score of 1 reflected low-quality content with insufficient information and minimal value to users, while a score of 5 denoted highly consistent, comprehensive, and clinically valuable content (Fig. 3). In addition, FRES and FKRGL were used to assess the readability and comprehensibility of the chatbot responses.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), with a significance level set at p < 0.05.

Normality was tested with Shapiro–Wilk and homogeneity with Levene’s test. One-way ANOVA was used for normally distributed data, with Tukey’s HSD post-hoc test. For non-parametric distributions, the Kruskal–Wallis test was applied, followed by Bonferroni-adjusted Mann–Whitney U tests. ANOVA was chosen for continuous parametric data (e.g., EQIP, FRES), while Kruskal–Wallis was used for ordinal or skewed distributions (e.g., DISCERN, GQS, Similarity Index). Post-hoc tests were applied to control for Type I error. Table 2 presents the tests applied to each evaluation metric.

Results

Descriptive analysis

Descriptive statistics for the chatbot responses were examined across two domains: general knowledge questions and clinical application questions. The analyses included mean, standard deviation (SD), median, and interquartile ranges (Q1–Q3). ChatGPT-4.o consistently achieved the highest mean values in both domains, outperforming Gemini Pro and DeepSeek V3. For general questions, ChatGPT-4.o obtained the highest score (M = 4.32, SD = 0.43), followed by Gemini Pro (M = 3.74, SD = 0.66) and DeepSeek V3 (M = 3.48, SD = 0.57). A similar trend was observed for clinical questions, with ChatGPT-4.o again achieving the top performance (M = 4.20, SD = 0.48), while Gemini Pro and DeepSeek produced comparatively lower scores.

Comparative evaluation across quality metrics

Comparative results are illustrated in Figs. 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9, showing EQIP, DISCERN, FRES, FKRGL, and Similarity Index distributions. ChatGPT-4.o significantly outperformed Gemini and DeepSeek in EQIP (M = 4.32, SD = 0.43) and DISCERN (M = 4.20, SD = 0.48) scores (p < 0.001), reflecting higher reliability and informational quality. GQS outcomes were comparable across the three models, with no statistically significant differences, indicating that while overall global quality was adequate, it did not distinguish performance between systems.

No significant differences were observed for FRES (p = 0.12) or Similarity Index (p = 0.54). However, ChatGPT demonstrated higher median readability (FRES = 68.5) and lower similarity values (≤ 10%), suggesting clearer and more original responses. FKRGL showed borderline significance (p = 0.041), but this did not remain significant after Bonferroni correction.

From a practical perspective, these findings indicate that ChatGPT’s superior performance may enhance caregivers’ understanding of fluoride use and support informed decision-making in pediatric dentistry.

Summary of pairwise differences

Post-hoc analyses confirmed ChatGPT’s superiority over Gemini and DeepSeek in EQIP and DISCERN (p < 0.05). No significant differences were found between Gemini and DeepSeek. The implication is that parents using ChatGPT are more likely to receive accurate, reliable, and comprehensible fluoride education than with the other platforms.

Discussion

This study provides a focused, comparative evaluation of three advanced AI-driven chatbots—ChatGPT-4.o, Google Gemini Pro, and DeepSeek V3—for communicating fluoride-related information to caregivers in pediatric dentistry. ChatGPT-4.o consistently achieved higher EQIP and DISCERN scores than the comparators, indicating stronger reliability, structure, and informational quality for caregiver-facing content. These findings align with recent evidence suggesting that AI-based conversational agents can generate patient-directed materials of acceptable quality when developed and evaluated under transparent, standardized methods and when readability levels are appropriately considered1–3,9,10. Consistent with prior audits, our readability analysis revealed higher median FRES and lower FKRGL scores for ChatGPT-4.o, supporting the premise that readability is a key determinant of comprehension and behavior change in digital health education9,10.

These results are in agreement with Li et al.11, who demonstrated that chatbot interventions can enhance mental well-being and engagement in self-care routines. Synthesizing these studies suggests that AI tools hold considerable promise for shaping positive health behaviors—provided they are accurate, context-sensitive, and guided by evidence-based frameworks. In meta-analyses and systematic reviews, chatbots have demonstrated efficacy in improving lifestyle behaviors such as physical activity, healthy eating, and sleep, supporting the potential for similar benefits in oral-health literacy and preventive behavior when fluoride messages are both accurate and accessible4,9.

Fluoride remains a cornerstone of caries prevention in childhood, and authoritative bodies consistently affirm that appropriately used topical and systemic fluoride are safe and effective5,7,12. AAPD provides comprehensive, periodically updated best-practice recommendations that can be embedded into chatbot outputs to reduce misinformation and guide caregivers toward sound decisions7,12. Considering the increasing prevalence of fluoride-related misinformation on social media, integrating AAPD-based evidence and clearly signposted professional sources within chatbot interfaces is crucial13–15.

Quality appraisal frameworks such as DISCERN and EQIP—originally designed for written patient materials—translate well to chatbot outputs because they assess reliability, balance, completeness, and presentation9,16,17. In this study, these instruments were used to benchmark information quality across models, while the Global Quality Score (GQS) provided a general usefulness rating. Notably, recent scholarship cautions that the GQS is sometimes inconsistently cited in the literature, emphasizing the importance of accurate methodological reporting18. Readability indices (FRES/FKRGL) remain pragmatic proxies for accessibility and are widely used in audits of patient-education materials; nevertheless, future work should include user-testing with caregivers to capture subtleties that readability formulas may miss19.

From a trust and design standpoint, Jin et al.20 reported that gendered cues and personalization in chatbot design increase acceptance, particularly among female caregivers—a finding relevant to pediatric oral-health promotion. Aligning chatbot responses with plain-language and health-literacy standards can also prevent miscommunication in vulnerable populations21, and emerging NLP approaches such as neural machine translation models have shown promise for improving linguistic accessibility and health-literacy alignment in patient-education materials22. Bhattacharya and Pissurlenkar23 further underscored that chatbots must undergo rigorous validation to avoid perpetuating misinformation, especially on sensitive topics such as fluoride use where public perceptions are polarized. Similarly, Buldur and Sezer24 found that ChatGPT-4 generated answers comparable in accuracy to official FDA guidance on dental amalgam, though occasional deviations from regulatory phrasing highlighted the importance of expert review. Gugnani et al.25 also showed that while ChatGPT responses to parental questions were generally coherent and clear, gaps in clinical nuance and preventive advice remained. Together, these findings reinforce that ChatGPT-4 may serve as a supplementary educational tool—but never a substitute—for professional consultation.

Despite the demonstrated potential of AI chatbots in health education, caution is warranted. The main risks involve misinformation due to absent source attribution, outdated content, or algorithmic bias reflecting users’ pre-existing beliefs. For example, a caregiver skeptical about fluoride safety might receive responses unintentionally reinforcing these concerns. Although not directly assessed here, such effects warrant investigation in future studies as possible risk factors influencing information trust and behavioral intent. The similarity analysis also revealed occasional content overlaps with existing online material, suggesting a need for stronger originality safeguards in future AI models.

Trust, disclosure, and clinical oversight are thus central to the safe deployment of AI tools in pediatric dentistry. Evidence indicates that healthcare users generally view AI-assisted communication positively when human supervision is explicit and limitations are transparently disclosed2,26–30. Accordingly, any clinical application of chatbots should: (i) disclose AI involvement and dataset constraints; (ii) offer clear escalation pathways to clinicians; (iii) provide direct links to authoritative resources such as AAPD fluoride guidelines; and (iv) undergo periodic expert validation. These safeguards are particularly vital for topics prone to controversy or misinformation amplification12–14.

The strengths of this study include its standardized prompting, multi-metric evaluation (quality, readability, originality), and blinded, multi-rater scoring with satisfactory inter-rater agreement. Nonetheless, limitations must be acknowledged. The study focused solely on English-language responses and fluoride-specific queries, without incorporating caregiver-generated questions or real-world multi-turn interactions. The absence of parental input in question development limits ecological validity and should be addressed in subsequent research. Future studies should incorporate multilingual evaluations, longitudinal monitoring, and outcome-based trials assessing whether chatbot interactions influence fluoride acceptance, brushing frequency, or recall adherence.

We also encourage alignment with emerging reporting guidance for chatbot studies in healthcare to enhance transparency and reproducibility2,26–28. Overall, our findings support the cautious, supervised use of advanced AI chatbots as adjuncts—rather than replacements—for pediatric dental counseling and oral-health literacy initiatives. Embedding AAPD-based clinical content, ensuring readability and transparency, and maintaining expert oversight can help translate the technological advantages of AI chatbots into meaningful parental understanding and safer fluoride-related decision-making.

Conclusion

This study offers a novel and methodologically robust evaluation of how artificial intelligence chatbots convey fluoride-related information to pediatric patients and caregivers. Among the models assessed, ChatGPT-4.o consistently demonstrated superior performance across multiple metrics of quality, reliability, and readability. Its higher EQIP and DISCERN scores suggest that it is more capable of delivering accurate, evidence-based, and user-friendly health information than Gemini Pro or DeepSeek V3.

However, while ChatGPT-4.o shows potential as a digital health education tool, our findings also highlight the limitations and risks associated with relying on AI-generated content in clinical contexts. Variability in response quality, lack of clinical nuance, and moderate reading complexity underscore the need for cautious implementation and professional validation.

The central contribution of this study is to show that AI chatbots vary substantially in their ability to communicate fluoride-related information, and only carefully validated systems can meaningfully support caregiver education. Based on these findings, developers should prioritize improved fine-tuning for pediatric vocabulary, inclusion of verified references, and alignment with health literacy standards. Clinicians should adopt AI chatbots as supportive—not substitutive—tools to reinforce preventive guidance, while public health professionals and policymakers may explore their integration into oral health literacy campaigns, particularly in underserved settings.

Ultimately, AI chatbots may serve as valuable adjuncts to professional guidance in promoting pediatric oral health literacy, but their safe and effective use will depend on transparent validation, user-centered design, and the irreplaceable role of human oversight.

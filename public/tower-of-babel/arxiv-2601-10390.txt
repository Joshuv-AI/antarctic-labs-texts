Algebraic Farkas Lemma and Strong Duality for Perturbed Conic Linear Programming
Pham Duy Khanh, Vu Vinh Huy Khoa, Tran Hong Mo

Abstract




Abstract
This paper addresses the study of algebraic versions of Farkas lemma and strong duality results in the very broad setting of infinite-dimensional conic linear programming in dual pairs of vector spaces. To this end, purely algebraic properties of perturbed optimal value functions of both primal and dual problems and their corresponding hypergraph/epigraph are investigated. The newly developed hypergraphical/epigraphical sets, inspired by Kretschmer’s closedness conditions
[
11
]
, together with their novel convex separation-type characterizations, give rise to various perturbed Farkas-type lemmas which allow us to derive complete characterizations of “zero duality gap”. Principally, when certain structures of algebraic or topological duals are imposed, illuminating implications of the developed condition are also explored.

Key words
. conic linear programming, dual pairs of vector spaces, Farkas lemma, strong duality, separation theorems

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
90C05, 90C46, 90C48, 46A20

1
Introduction

Infinite-dimensional conic linear programming
arises in the literature of optimization as a natural extension of linear programming, carrying the existing setting to infinite-dimensional vector spaces while requiring a feasible point to lie within a convex cone - a generalization of the well-known nonnegative orthant in a Euclidean space emanating from linear programming. A variety of intriguing optimization problems can be expressed within this framework, including potential theory
[
22
]
, continuous-time linear programming and continuous-time network flow
[
3
]
, and approximation theory
[
10
]
. Unlike the classical linear programming theory, in the broader context of
infinite-dimensional conic linear programming
, no comprehensive theory has been established, and efforts in duality theory, for example, have not yet been unified to the same degree.

The first attempt to study linear programming in more general settings of linear spaces dates back to Duffin
[
6
]
, in which the primal space
X
is a locally convex topological space while the dual space coincides with the topological dual of
X
. Kretschmer
[
11
]
extends the exploration with his nominal work on duality theory and existence theorems within the broader framework of
dual pair
, which aligns with the formulation presented in Yamasaki
[
22
,
23
]
, and specifically, of our study.

Farkas lemma
is a typical example of the very nice collection of
theorems of the alternative
which, generally speaking, characterizes the consistency (or solvability) of systems of linear inequalities and their corresponding duals. The primary significance of
Farkas lemma
in the theory of linear programming lies in its role in establishing the
strong duality theorem
. A comprehensive review of Farkas lemma, duality theory and related topics can be found, e.g., from
[
2
,
3
,
7
]
and the references therein. In greater detail, Shapiro
[
19
]
employed the theory of convex conjugate duality and convex subdifferential pioneered by Rockafellar to present a novel approach to exploring “zero duality gap” and existence results via analyzing the conjugate of the objective functions. The reader is referred to Zălinescu
[
25
,
26
]
for manifestation of techniques and results of the same manner, and further, several alternative proofs for some well-known existing results in the subject. Vinh et al.
[
20
]
proposes a thorough study on the so-called duality gap function, presenting various “zero duality gap” results based on their interior conditions. Further, Luan and Yen
[
12
]
addresses solution existence for both primal and dual problem, obtaining a Farkas-type result based on a generalized Slater condition in the setting of locally convex topological vector spaces. Quite recently, Khanh et al.
[
9
]
establishes necessary and sufficient conditions (weakest conditions) ensuring both the “zero duality gap” and solvability of the problems via their closedness-like conditions, stemming from the very classical
closedness condition
; see Anderson and Nash
[
3
, Theorem 3.10]
. Many sufficient conditions guaranteeing the closedness condition can also be found from
[
3
]
, e.g., interior point conditions, boundedness/compactness conditions.

Separating from the previously mentioned findings, the present article provides an in-depth analysis of numerous perturbed variants of Farkas lemma and strong duality theorem merely in the very general setting of dual pair of vector spaces without topological structures. We start with a detailed investigation on cost-perturbed versions of the primal and dual problem, whose optimal value functions are equipped with nicely behaved hypergraphical and epigraphical set, respectively. The constructions of these (hyper/epi)graphical sets are motivated by both the Kretschmer’s closedness conditions and their links to strong duality theorems (see
[
11
]
and
[
9
]
). Bonding connections as well as distinguished features between our innovative graphical sets and the well-known auxiliary sets from the theory of strong duality
[
9
]
are investigated and illustrated through the celebrated example of Gale. We also succeed in deriving characterizations of the epigraphical/hypergraphical sets in the language of convex separations in convex analysis (i.e., extension theorems in functional analysis). The ingredients collected grant us cutting-edge
verifiable
necessary and sufficient conditions, which are later named conditions
(D)
and
(D
∗
)
, for the characteristic alternative arguments in the Farkas lemma, as well as the “zero duality gap”, thus offering a unified perspective on the theory of the Farkas lemma and strong duality results. In the particular case of algebraic dual pairs
(X,X^{*})
, we employ the notion of
algebraic cores
of sets and basic algebraic separation theorems to provide sufficient conditions ensuring the validation of
(D)
and
(D
∗
)
. Last but not least, the equivalence between the latter and a closedness-type condition similar to that from
[
9
]
is assured when topological structures are finally equipped to the spaces in consideration.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Preliminaries on dual pairs of vector spaces and the standing formulations of our main problems are given in Section
2
. Section
3
studies perturbed conic linear programming problems, motivates these perturbations, develops the associated optimal value functions, and introduces our verifiable necessary and sufficient condition via the former ingredients. Section
4
is devoted to the study of Farkas lemma and strong duality theory under the newly proposed algebraic criterion. Section
5
and Section
6
showcase respectively, an algebraic sufficient condition guaranteeing the mentioned criterion and its full characterization in the setting of topological vector spaces. The concluding Section
7
summarizes the major contributions of the paper.

2
Preliminaries

The following facts involving dual pairs of vector spaces can be found from Schaefer
[
18
]
.

Given that
X
and
Y
are real vector spaces and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a
bilinear form
on
X\times Y
, i.e.,
\langle x,\cdot\rangle
is linear on
Y
for any
x\in X
and
\langle\cdot,y\rangle
is linear on
X
for any
y\in Y
. Then
(X,Y)
is called a
dual pair
. If in addition
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
satisfies the
separation axioms
:

(i)
\langle x_{0},y\rangle=0
for all
y\in Y
implies
x_{0}=0_{X}
,

(ii)
\langle x,y_{0}\rangle=0
for all
x\in X
implies
y_{0}=0_{Y}
,

then
(X,Y)
is called a
separated dual pair
. As an example, knowing that each vector space possesses a Hamel basis containing a given nonzero vector, one is able to show that
X
together with its algebraic dual
X^{*}
forms a separated dual pair.

Let
(X,Y)
and
(Z,W)
be two separated dual pairs of vector spaces. For simplicity, the bilinear forms on
X
and
Y
, and on
Z
and
W
will both be denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Let
A
be a linear map from
X
to
Z
. The
adjoint (transpose)
of
A
is the linear map

A^{*}:W\rightarrow Y
defined via the condition

\langle x,A^{*}w\rangle=\langle Ax,w\rangle\quad\forall x\in X,\;\forall w\in W.

(2.1)

Let
P\subset X
be a convex cone, i.e.,
P
is nonempty and
\alpha x+\beta y\in P
for any
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
and
x,y\in P
. The
(positive) dual cone
of
P
is defined as

P^{*}:=\left\{y\in Y:\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall x\in P\right\}.

Since
\langle Y,X\rangle
is also a dual pair given that
\langle X,Y\rangle
is, we may define the
(positive) bidual cone
of
P
as follows:

P^{**}:=\{x\in X:\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall y\in P^{*}\}.

(2.2)

We are interested in the following
algebraic inequality constrained conic linear programming problem
and its dual (see
[
11
,
19
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
):

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\qquad\min\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b\in Q,x\in P\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\qquad\max\{\langle b,w\rangle:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*},w\in Q^{*}\},

(2.4)

where
P\subset X
and
Q\subset Z
are convex cones, and
b
and
c
are given elements of
Z
and
Y
, respectively.
There is also an
equality constrained
form (see
[
2
]
,
[
3
, Sect. 3.3]
, and
[
20
]
) of the problem (
\mathcal{P}
) as follows:

(\mathcal{EP})\qquad\min\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b=0_{Z},x\in P\},

(2.5)

and its corresponding dual is

(\mathcal{ED})\qquad\max\{\langle b,w\rangle:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*},w\in W\}.

(2.6)

One can readily verify that either
(\mathcal{EP})
or
(\mathcal{P})
can be converted into the other. Indeed, problem
(\mathcal{P})

becomes a program in the form
(\mathcal{EP})
if
Q
is the cone consisting only of the origin. Conversely,
(\mathcal{EP})
can be written in the form
(\mathcal{P})
by replacing
X
with
X\times Z
,
P

with
P\times Q
, and
A
with the linear map sending
(x,z)
to
Ax-z
. Thus there is no
loss of generality in dealing entirely with either
(\mathcal{EP})
or
(\mathcal{P})
.
In what follows, we present results for both formulations.

Let
F(\mathcal{P}):=\{x\in P:Ax-b\in Q\}
and
F(\mathcal{D}):=\{w\in Q^{*}:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*}\}
denote the sets of feasible solutions of
(\mathcal{P})
and
(\mathcal{D})
, respectively. Our standing assumption is that (
\mathcal{P}
) and (
\mathcal{D}
) are
consistent
, i.e.,
F(\mathcal{P})
and
F(\mathcal{D})
are nonempty. We use the notations

\displaystyle\textup{val}(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b\in Q,x\in P\}\text{ and}

\displaystyle\textup{val}(\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle:=\sup\{\langle b,w\rangle:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*},w\in Q^{*}\}

for the
optimal values
of (
\mathcal{P}
) and (
\mathcal{D}
), respectively. A result that assures the identity

\text{val($\mathcal{P}$)}=\text{val($\mathcal{D}$)}

(2.7)

is called a
strong duality theorem
. The following
weak duality
result can be found in Kretschmer
[
11
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Weak duality)
We always have
val(
\mathcal{P}
)
\;\geq\;
val(
\mathcal{D}
)
. If
(
\mathcal{P}
)
and
(
\mathcal{D}
)
are both consistent, then both values are finite.

As illustrated via Gale’s example in
[
20
, Section 4.1]
, one of the main differences between conic linear programming and linear programming is that even if both the primal problem (
2.3
) and the dual problem (
2.4
) are
solvable
(i.e., each has at least one optimal solution), it may happen that val(
\mathcal{P}
)
\;>\;
val(
\mathcal{D}
).

To efficiently study the strong duality theory for (
\mathcal{P}
) and (
\mathcal{D}
), we take into account the following auxiliary sets whose equality-constrained-counterparts appeared for the first time in Khanh et al.
[
9
]
. Let the sets
\mathcal{H}\subset Z\times I\!\!R
and
\mathcal{K}\subset Y\times I\!\!R
be as follows:

\mathcal{H}:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{x\,\in\,P}\big((Ax-b-Q)\times[\langle x,c\rangle,+\infty[\big),

(2.8)

\mathcal{K}:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{w\,\in\,Q^{*}}\big((-A^{*}w+c-P^{*})\,\times\,]-\infty,\langle b,w\rangle]\big).

(2.9)

The set
\mathcal{H}
(resp.
\mathcal{K}
) carries the details of a feasible solution of the primal problem
(\mathcal{P})
(resp. dual problem
(\mathcal{D})
), including the associated cost function value. Moreover, it is easy to check that

\textup{val}(\mathcal{P})=\inf\{r\in I\!\!R:(0_{Z},r)\in\mathcal{H}\}

(2.10)

and

\textup{val}(\mathcal{D})=\sup\{r\in I\!\!R:(0_{Y},r)\in\mathcal{K}\}.

(2.11)

Since
(-b-Q)\times\{0\}\subset\mathcal{H}
and

\mathcal{H}=B(P\times Q\times I\!\!R_{+})-\{(b,0)\},

where

B(x,z,\alpha):=\big(Ax-z,\langle x,c\rangle+\alpha\big),\;(x,z,\alpha)\in X\times Z\ \times I\!\!R,

(2.12)

it follows readily that
\mathcal{H}
is nonempty and convex, as it is the image of a convex set under an affine transformation. The same conclusion also holds for
\mathcal{K}
. In the literature, one is very likely to encounter (see
[
11
, Section 3]
and
[
22
,
23
]
) the sets
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subset Z\times I\!\!R
and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\subset Y\times I\!\!R
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{x\,\in\,P}\big((Ax-Q)\times[\langle x,c\rangle,+\infty[\big),

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{w\,\in\,Q^{*}}\big((-A^{*}w-P^{*})\,\times\,]-\infty,\langle b,w\rangle]\big),

which merely turn out to be translations of the sets
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
in consideration, respectively, via the relations

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}+\{(b,0)\},\;\text{ and }\;\mathcal{K}^{\prime}=\mathcal{K}-\{(c,0)\}.

One of the most well-known sufficient conditions guaranteeing the solvability of (
\mathcal{P}
) as well as the
strong duality result
(
2.7
) is the
closure condition of

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
from (
2.13
), see
[
11
, Corollary 3.1]
. In the literature, the
interior point condition
is a more restrictive yet easier-to-verify requirement. When
Q=\{0_{Z}\}
, two renowned variants of this condition are outlined by Anderson and Nash
[
3
, Theorem 3.11 and Theorem 3.12]
. The validations of the two stated theorems are intricately tied to the topological properties exhibited by the spaces in question. Notably,
[
3
, Theorem 3.12]
even necessitates the Banach structures of the spaces
X
and
Z
. A natural question arises:
Is it possible to construct a merely-algebraic sufficient condition for the strong duality (
2.7
)
? We answer this question in the affirmative via condition
(D)
defined in Section
4
below. Roughly speaking, condition
(D)
is shown to be equivalent to the fulfillment of strong duality (
2.7
) for all possible values of
b
appearing in the objective value function (
2.4
) of
(\mathcal{D})
, the latter of which is referred to as
perturbed strong duality
.

The next section is devoted to the study of perturbed optimal value functions on which condition
(D)
and its dual counterpart
(D
∗
)
are built.

3
Algebraic perturbed conic linear programming

Recall from
[
9
, Theorem 4.4]
that, within the framework of dual pairs of topological vector spaces
(X,Y)
and
(Z,W)
, the condition

\mathcal{H}\cap(\{0_{Z}\}\times I\!\!R)=\overline{\mathcal{H}}\cap(\{0_{Z}\}\times I\!\!R)

enforces the desired
strong duality
result between
(\mathcal{P})
and
(\mathcal{D})
. Here,
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
stands for the closure of
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the weak topology
\sigma(Z\times I\!\!R,W\times I\!\!R)
. Inspired by this, we raise the following question: “
How can we represent
\,\overline{\mathcal{H}}\,
, say, via a suitable set
\mathcal{N}
within our present general framework of dual pairs of vector spaces, where no topological structure is assumed?”
Importantly, our choice of
\mathcal{N}
–and the ensuing purely algebraic duality results–must be consistent with
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
and the existing literature once topologies are imposed, for instance with the results in
[
9
]
. Building on
[
9
, Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 2.2]
, it is natural to require

\mathcal{N}\cap(\{0_{Z}\}\times I\!\!R)=\{0_{Z}\}\times\big[\textup{val}(\mathcal{D}),+\infty\big[.

Since this identity only encodes information about
\mathcal{N}
on the slice
\{0_{Z}\}\times I\!\!R
, we henceforth replace
0_{Z}
with an arbitrary
z\in Z
and obtain

\mathcal{N}\cap(\{z\}\times I\!\!R)=\{z\}\times\Big[\sup\big\{\langle b+z,w\rangle:w\in F(\mathcal{D})\big\},+\infty\Big[.

(3.1)

The right-hand side of (
3.1
) is nonempty if and only if

\sup\big\{\langle b+z,w\rangle:w\in F(\mathcal{D})\big\}<\infty.

(3.2)

Taking the union of (
3.1
) for all
z
satisfying (
3.2
), we arrive at the definition

\mathcal{N}:=\mbox{epi}\,v_{\mathcal{D}},

(3.3)

where

v_{\mathcal{D}}(z):=\sup\{\langle b+z,w\rangle:w\in F(\mathcal{D})\}\quad(z\in Z)

(3.4)

is the optimal value function associated with the
cost-function-perturbed
variant of
(\mathcal{D})
, in which the feasible set is kept fixed. Dually, let us define

\mathcal{M}:=\mbox{hypo}\,v_{\mathcal{P}},

(3.5)

where

v_{\mathcal{P}}(y):=\inf\{\langle x,c-y\rangle:x\in F(\mathcal{P})\}\quad(y\in Y).

(3.6)

The
perturbed
optimal value functions
v_{\mathcal{D}}
and
v_{\mathcal{P}}
, along with their associated (epi/hyper) –graphical sets
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
, and the relationships linking

\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
to
\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}
, are the main objects of interest in this section.

First, the concavity of
v_{\mathcal{P}}
and convexity of
v_{\mathcal{D}}
are easily recognized. The effective domains
\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}v_{\mathcal{P}}:=\{y\in Y:v_{\mathcal{P}}(y)>-\infty\}
and
\mathop{\mathrm{d}om}v_{\mathcal{D}}:=\{z\in Z:v_{\mathcal{D}}(z)<+\infty\}
are nonempty convex sets. Due to the weak duality stated in Theorem
2.1
, we have

v_{\mathcal{P}}(0_{Y})=\text{val}(\mathcal{P})\quad\text{ and }\quad v_{\mathcal{D}}(0_{Z})=\text{val}(\mathcal{D}),

these values are finite since
(\mathcal{P})
and
(\mathcal{D})
are consistent. Further,
v_{\mathcal{P}}
(resp.
v_{\mathcal{D}}
) is
radially upper semicontinuous
(resp.
radially lower semicontinuous
), i.e., upper semicontinuous (resp. lower semicontinuous) with respect to line segments. To be specific for
v_{\mathcal{D}}
, for every
\bar{z}
and
z\in Z
it holds that

v_{\mathcal{D}}(\bar{z})\leq\mathop{{\mathrm{l}im}\,{\mathrm{i}nf}}_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}v_{\mathcal{D}}\big((1-\lambda)\bar{z}+\lambda z\big),

where the lower limit/limit inferior of an extended real-valued function is defined in
[
14
, Definition 1.16]
. This observation is claimed below.

Proposition 3.1

The perturbed optimal value function
v_{\mathcal{P}}
is radially upper semicontinuous, while
v_{\mathcal{D}}
is radially lower semicontinuous.

Proof.

Since
v_{\mathcal{D}}
(resp.
v_{\mathcal{P}})
is the supremum (resp. infimum) of radially continuous linear functionals, it follows that
v_{\mathcal{D}}
(resp.
v_{\mathcal{P}})
is itself radially lower (resp. upper) semicontinuous.

Next, we employ a parametric version of Gale’s example (see
[
3
, Section 3.4.2]
) to illustrate the computation of the epigraphical set
\mathcal{N}
from (
3.3
). The celebrated Gale’s example serves in the literature as an instance whence “positive duality gap” may happen even in the setting of finite-dimensional conic linear programming. Recently,
[
9
]
utilized it to examine whether a sufficient condition for strong duality, established in the same work, is necessary, while many perturbed versions of Gale’s example are extensively studied in
[
20
,
26
]
.

Example 3.1

For real numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
, we consider the parametric linear problem

\left(\mathrm{PG}_{\alpha,\beta}\right)\quad\min\left\{x_{0}:x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}ix_{i}=\alpha,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}=\beta,x_{i}\geq 0,i=0,1,2,\ldots\right\}.

The problem
\left(\mathrm{PG}_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
is an example of an infinite-dimensional conic linear programming (
2.3
). Indeed, we have
X:=I\!\!R^{(\mathbb{N})}
consisting of real sequences with finitely many nonzero terms. It is well-known that the dual of
X
is
Y:=I\!\!R^{\mathbb{N}}
, i.e., the space of all real sequences.
(X,Y)
is then a separated dual pair with respect to the bilinear form

\langle x,y\rangle:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}y_{i},\quad\forall x=\left(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\right)\in X,\;\forall y=\left(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},\ldots\right)\in Y.

On the other hand, consider the separated dual pair
(Z,W):=(I\!\!R^{2},I\!\!R^{2})
with respect to the bilinear form

\langle z,w\rangle:=z_{1}w_{1}+z_{2}w_{2},\quad\forall z=\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)\in Z,\;\forall w=\left(w_{1},w_{2}\right)\in W.

In
X
, define the convex cone
P=I\!\!R_{+}^{(\mathbb{N})}:=I\!\!R^{(\mathbb{N})}\cap I\!\!R_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
, where

I\!\!R_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}:=\left\{x=\left(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\right)\in I\!\!R^{\mathbb{N}}:x_{i}\geq 0,i=0,1,2,\ldots\right\}.

Clearly,
P^{*}=I\!\!R_{+}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
Q:=\{0_{Z}\}
,
b:=(\alpha,\beta)\in Z
, and
c:=(1,0,0,\ldots)\in Y
. Let
A:X\rightarrow Z
and its adjoint
A^{*}:W\rightarrow Y
be the linear maps represented by the infinite matrices

\begin{gathered}A=\left(\begin{array}[]{lllll}1&1&2&3&\ldots\\
0&1&1&1&\ldots\end{array}\right),\\
A^{*}=A^{T}=\left(\begin{array}[]{lllll}1&1&2&3&\ldots\\
0&1&1&1&\ldots\end{array}\right)^{T}.\end{gathered}

The dual problem to
\left(\mathrm{PG}_{\alpha,\beta}\right)
admits the set of feasible solutions as

\displaystyle F^{*}

\displaystyle=\big\{w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in I\!\!R^{2}:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*},\,w\in Q^{*}\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{(w_{1},w_{2})\in I\!\!R^{2}:-w_{1}+1\geq 0,-iw_{1}-w_{2}\geq 0,\,i=1,2,\ldots\big\}.

Now we explore the epigraphical set
\mathcal{N}=\bigcup_{z\,\in\,\mathrm{dom}\,v_{\mathcal{D}}}\big(\{z\}\times[v_{\mathcal{D}}(z),+\infty[\big)
, where

\displaystyle v_{\mathcal{D}}(z)

\displaystyle=\sup\big\{\langle b+z,w\rangle:w\in F^{*}\}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\sup\big\{\big\langle(z_{1}+\alpha,z_{2}+\beta),(w_{1},w_{2})\big\rangle:(w_{1},w_{2})\in F^{*}\big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{(z_{1}+\alpha)w_{1}+(z_{2}+\beta)w_{2}:w_{1}\leq 1,iw_{1}+w_{2}\leq 0,i=1,2,\ldots\Big\}.

As inspired by
[
9
, Example 3.1]
, we observe that
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mbox{dom}\,v_{\mathcal{D}}
if and only if
z_{1}+\alpha\geq z_{2}+\beta\geq 0
. Indeed, suppose that
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mbox{dom}\,v_{\mathcal{D}}
. As
(0,-k)
and
(-k,k)
belong to the feasible set of the perturbed dual problem in (
3.7
) for all
k\in I\!\!N
, we have

-k(z_{2}+\beta)\leq v_{\mathcal{D}}(z)\quad\text{and}\quad-k(z_{1}+\alpha)+k(z_{2}+\beta)\leq v_{\mathcal{D}}(z),

and thus
z_{2}+\beta\geq-\dfrac{v_{\mathcal{D}}(z)}{k}
and
(z_{1}+\alpha)-(z_{2}+\beta)\geq-\dfrac{v_{\mathcal{D}}(z)}{k}
for all
k\in I\!\!N
. Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
in the previous estimates yields
z_{1}+\alpha\geq z_{2}+\beta\geq 0
. Conversely, assume that
z_{1}+\alpha\geq z_{2}+\beta\geq 0
and consider any
(w_{1},w_{2})\in I\!\!R^{2}
for which

w_{1}\leq 1\ \text{ and }\ iw_{1}+w_{2}\leq 0\ \text{ for all }\ i=1,2,3,\ldots

(3.8)

If
w_{1}>0
, then letting
i\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.8
) leads to an absurd assertion, and hence we must have
w_{1}\leq 0
. Further, by employing (
3.8
) and
z_{1}+\alpha\geq z_{2}+\beta\geq 0
one gets

(z_{1}+\alpha)w_{1}+(z_{2}+\beta)w_{2}\leq(z_{2}+\beta)(w_{1}+w_{2})\leq 0=(z_{1}+\alpha)\cdot 0+(z_{2}+\beta)\cdot 0.

(3.9)

Since the feasible set in (

\displaystyle=\{(z_{1},z_{2},t_{3})\in I\!\!R^{3}:z_{1}+\alpha\geq z_{2}+\beta\geq 0,t_{3}\geq 0\}

Proposition 3.2

We have the following assertions:

and

(ii)

(iii)

Proof.

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\geq\inf\{\langle x^{\prime},c\rangle:x^{\prime}\in P,Ax^{\prime}-(b+z)\in Q\}

Lemma 3.1

Let
(X,Y)
be a dual pair of vector spaces and let

be a nonempty strict cone in

\beta c\in C
for all vector
c\in C
and scalar

and

\big[\langle x,y\rangle\geq\alpha\,\,\forall x\in C\big]\Longrightarrow\big[y\in C^{*}\text{ and }\alpha\leq 0\big].

Proof.

Fix
y\in Y
and
\alpha\in I\!\!R
such that

\langle x,y\rangle\geq\alpha\quad\forall x\in C.

(3.10)

Pick any
x\in C

\langle\varepsilon x,y\rangle\geq\alpha\quad\text{for any}\quad\varepsilon>0.

By letting

\langle x,y\rangle<0
, then one finds

sufficiently large such that

and
\langle Mx,y\rangle<\alpha
, which clearly contradicts (

). The proof is complete.

Theorem 4.5
(Perturbed strong duality 1)

Consider the problems (
2.3
)-(
2.4
) and fix
z\in Z
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\emptyset\neq\mathcal{H}\cap(\{z\}\times I\!\!R)=\mathcal{N}\cap(\{z\}\times I\!\!R)

(ii)

\min\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\}=\sup\{\langle b+z,w\rangle:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*},w\in Q^{*}\}.

Proof.

First we justify the implication [(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)] by assuming the validation of (i). Above all, we show that the infimum value
\inf\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\}
is finite and is attained. By the definition of the dual cones
P^{*},Q^{*}
and the asssumption
F(\mathcal{D})\neq\emptyset
, it is obvious that

\inf\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\}\geq\sup\{\langle b+z,w\rangle:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*},w\in Q^{*}\}>-\infty.

(4.2)

On the other hand, the nonemptiness of
\mathcal{H}\cap(\{z\}\times I\!\!R)
lends us a primal feasible solution
x\in P
at which
Ax-b-z\in Q
. Combining this with (
4.2
) ensures that the infimum in (
4.2
) is finite. Employing now (
3.3
), (
4.2
) and assumption (i) yields

{\Big(z,\inf\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\}\Big)\in\mathcal{N}\cap(\{z\}\times I\!\!R)=\mathcal{H}\cap(\{z\}\times I\!\!R).}

Consequently, by (
2.8
) there exists
\bar{x}\in P
such that
A\bar{x}-b-z\in Q
and

\langle\bar{x},c\rangle\leq\inf\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\},

i.e., the infimum in (
4.2
) is attained at
\bar{x}
.

Now we verify the identity in (ii). By (
4.2
) and the above reasoning, we have

{\sup\{\langle b+z,w\rangle:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*},w\in Q^{*}\}=\alpha\in I\!\!R.}

By the supremum representation of
\alpha
, we can write
\langle b+z,w\rangle\leq\alpha
for all
w\in F(\mathcal{D})
. Combining the latter with assertion (i) and the perturbed Farkas lemma 1 (Theorem
4.1
), one finds
\overline{x}\in P
such that
A\overline{x}-b-z\in Q
and
\langle\overline{x},c\rangle\leq\alpha
, and thus

\min\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\}\leq\langle\overline{x},c\rangle\leq\alpha

which justifies the equality in (ii).

Now we suppose (ii) holds and claim (i). Since we have

\inf\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\}=\min\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\}<\infty,

the feasible set
\{x\in P:Ax-b-z\in Q\}
is nonempty and so is the set
\mathcal{H}\cap(\{z\}\times I\!\!R)
. Now observe via Theorem
4.1
that the identity in (i) is equivalent to the validation of [(a)
\Longleftrightarrow
(b)] for each
\alpha\in I\!\!R
in Theorem
4.1
. This tells us to fix any
\alpha\in I\!\!R
, and establish via assumption (ii) the equivalences

\displaystyle\langle b+z,w\rangle\leq\alpha\quad\forall w\in Q^{*},-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sup\{\langle b+z,w\rangle:w\in Q^{*},-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*}\}\leq\alpha

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\min\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b-z\in Q,x\in P\}\leq\alpha

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\,x\in P,Ax-b-z\in Q:\;\langle x,c\rangle\leq\alpha.

As [(a)
\Longleftrightarrow
(b)] in Theorem
4.1
is fulfilled for each
\alpha\in I\!\!R
, it follows from such theorem that the identity in (i) holds.

As a consequence of Theorem
4.5
, we obtain the following strong duality result which serves as an algebraic counterpart to Khanh et al.
[
9
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 4.6

(Strong duality 1)

Regarding the problems (
2.3
)-(
2.4
), the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{H}\cap(\{0_{Z}\}\times I\!\!R)=\mathcal{N}\cap(\{0_{Z}\}\times I\!\!R)
,

(ii)

\min\{\langle x,c\rangle:Ax-b\in Q,x\in P\}=\sup\{\langle b,w\rangle:-A^{*}w+c\in P^{*},w\in Q^{*}\}.

Again, we replace
\mathcal{H}
by

to acquire the second perturbed and unperturbed strong duality results.

Theorem 4.7

)-(

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\emptyset\neq\mathcal{K}\cap(\{y\}\times I\!\!R)=\mathcal{M}\cap(\{y\}\times I\!\!R)

(ii)

(5.1)

One easily checks that

Lemma 5.1

In a product space

\mbox{core}\,(A\times B)=\mbox{core}\,A\times\mbox{core}\,B

Proof.

We fix

and

and

. The opposite inclusion is verified in the same manner.

6
Topological characterizations of (D) and (D
∗
)

So far, we’ve limited our exploration to a purely algebraic context, aiming to minimize the incorporation of additional structure. One consequence we face is the necessity to formulate our dual problem within the algebraic dual of the primal space, which seems to be a difficult space to work with. To facilitate this process and, in addition, to employ powerful tools from
functional analysis
such as separation theorems and continuous extension theorems, we are inclined to endow the spaces in consideration with topological structures.

The following concepts and related facts can be found from any book focusing on topological vector spaces, e.g., Robertson and Robertson
[
16
]
and Schaefer
[
18
]
. Given a
separated dual pair
of vector spaces
(X,Y)
, the notion of which is defined in Section
2
. The collection of sets

\left\{x\in X:\left|\langle x,y\rangle\right|\leq 1\quad\forall y\in S\right\},\text{ where }S\text{ is a }\text{finite subset of }Y,

forms a base of neighborhoods of the origin in
X
. The resulting topology is then called the
weak topology
on
X
, denoted by
\sigma(X,Y)
. A topological vector space is
locally convex
if it has a base of convex sets. A locally convex topology on
X
is
consistent
with the dual pair
(X,Y)
if
Y
is exactly the dual of
X
with respect to this topology. Among such topologies on
X
,
\sigma(X,Y)
is the coarsest one while the
Mackey topology
, denoted by
\tau(X,Y)
, is the finest one. One may find the construction of the Mackey topology from
[
18
, p. 131]
. When
X
is a normed space and
Y
coincides with its topological dual, the weak topology
\sigma(X,Y)
and Mackey topology
\tau(X,Y)
are exactly the usual weak topology
\sigma(X,X^{*})
and norm/strong topology on
X
, respectively.

Let
(Z,W)
be another separated dual pair of vector spaces. Let
\sigma(X,Y)
and
\sigma(Z,W)
be the weak topologies on
X
and
Z
, respectively, and let
A
be a
\sigma(X,Y)-\sigma(Z,W)
continuous linear map from
X
to
Z
. Using
[
3
, Proposition 4, p. 37]
, we know that the
adjoint (transpose)
of
A
is the linear map

A^{*}:W\rightarrow Y
defined via the condition

\langle x,A^{*}w\rangle=\langle Ax,w\rangle\quad\forall x\in X,\;\forall w\in W.

(6.1)

It is well known
[
18
, Chapter IV, Section 2]
that
A^{*}
is
\sigma(W,Z)-\sigma(Y,X)
continuous, where
\sigma(W,Z)
and
\sigma(Y,X)
are weak topologies on
W
and
Y
, respectively. We adopt the convention that every topological notion being used in the sequel is understood with respect to the appropriate weak topology.

As
\mathcal{H}
(resp.
\mathcal{K}
) is convex, recall by
[
18
, 3.1, p. 130]
that the closure of
\mathcal{H}
(resp.
\mathcal{K}
) is the same for any locally convex topology on
Z\times I\!\!R
(resp.
Y\times I\!\!R
) consistent with the pair
(Z\times I\!\!R,W\times I\!\!R)
(resp.
(Y\times I\!\!R,X\times I\!\!R)
). In particular, the closures of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
with respect to the weak topology and Mackey topology are the same. Surprisingly, when
Z
and
Y
are endowed with their weak topologies, the weak closure
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
becomes exactly
\mathcal{N}
, and
\overline{\mathcal{K}}
becomes
\mathcal{M}
.

Observe also that in our current topological setting, the function
v_{\mathcal{P}}
is
upper semicontinuous
, i.e., each upper level set
\{y\in Y:v_{\mathcal{P}}(y)\geq\alpha\}
is closed for each
\alpha\in I\!\!R
; and the function
v_{\mathcal{D}}
is
lower semicontinuous
, i.e., each lower level set
\{z\in Z:v_{\mathcal{D}}(z)\leq\alpha\}
is closed for each
\alpha\in I\!\!R
. These observations later shed light on the closedness of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
. As promised, we now show that
\mathcal{N}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}
) is exactly the closure of
\mathcal{H}
(resp.
\mathcal{K}
), which thus implies that condition
(D)
(resp.
(D
∗
)
) is fulfilled if and only if
\mathcal{H}
(resp.
\mathcal{K}
) is closed with respect to consistent topologies.

Theorem 6.1

The following assertions hold true:

(i)

\overline{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{N}
,

(ii)

If
P,Q
are closed, then
\overline{\mathcal{K}}=\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

(i) First note that
\mathcal{N}=\mbox{epi}\,\,(v_{\mathcal{D}})
is closed with respect to the
\sigma(Z\times I\!\!R,W\times I\!\!R)
topology since
v_{\mathcal{D}}
is lower semicontinuous. Then, the inclusion
\overline{\mathcal{H}}\subset\mathcal{N}
follows from Proposition
3.2
(i) and the closedness of
\mathcal{N}
.

For the converse inclusion, let
(z,r)\in\mathcal{N}
such that
(z,r)\notin\overline{\mathcal{H}}
. Then by the separation theorem
[
3
, Proposition 6, p. 37]
there exists
(w,\beta)\in W\times I\!\!R
such that

\langle(z,r),(w,\beta)\rangle>\langle(z^{\prime},r^{\prime}),(w,\beta)\rangle\quad\forall(z^{\prime},r^{\prime})\in\mathcal{H},

which, by Theorem
3.1
, contradicts
(z,r)\in\mathcal{N}
. Thus we conclude that
(z,r)\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}
. Therefore,
\overline{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{N}
.

The proof of (ii) follows the same pattern as that of (i) by interchanging the roles of primal and dual settings.

The following example serves as an illustration for the identity
\overline{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{N}
which is shown previously.

Example 6.1

We consider again the parametric version of Gale’s example presented in Example
3.1
. Therein, it is explicitly shown that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=\bigcup_{z\,\in\,\mathrm{dom}\,v_{\mathcal{D}}}(\{z\}\times[v_{\mathcal{D}}(z),+\infty[)=\big\{(t_{1}+t_{2}-\alpha,t_{2}-\beta,t_{3}):t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\geq 0\big\}.

On the other hand, the closure
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
of
\mathcal{H}=\bigcup_{x\,\in\,P}\big((Ax-b-\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\})\times[\langle x,c\rangle,+\infty[\big)
is calculated in
[
9
, Example 3.1]
as

\overline{\mathcal{H}}=\big\{(t_{1}+t_{2}-\alpha,t_{2}-\beta,t_{3}):t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\geq 0\big\}

(6.2)

which is exactly
\mathcal{N}
. This is in accordance with the result of Theorem
6.1
.

7
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we have established algebraic versions of Farkas lemma and strong duality results in the general framework of infinite-dimensional conic linear programming. By analyzing the algebraic properties of perturbed optimal value functions and their associated hypergraphical and epigraphical sets, we have developed novel perturbed Farkas-type lemmas, which, in turn, provided complete characterizations of the “zero duality gap” in a very broad setting. By means of Section
5
and Section
6
, our work has shown to provide a cohesive framework for understanding the theory of Farkas lemma and strong duality results.

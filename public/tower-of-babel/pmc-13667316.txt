Evaluation of Treatment Response of Calcified Nodules and Nodular Calcified Lesions Using Mean Density on Coronary Computed Tomography Angiography
Authors: Akito Setoguchi, Keita Iyama, Ryosuke Iwasaki, Saburo Kusumoto, Yoshiyuki Doi, Tatsuya Nunohiro, Masayoshi Takeno, Koji Maemura
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundCalcified nodules (CN) and nodular calcifications (NC) present significant challenges during percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) because of their protruding morphology and high rigidity, which often result in inadequate stent expansion and suboptimal procedural outcomes.AimsThis single‐center retrospective study investigated whether coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA)‐derived mean density could predict stent expansion outcomes in CN/NC lesions identified using intravascular ultrasonography (IVUS).MethodsForty‐nine target vessels in 46 patients who underwent IVUS‐guided PCI within 1 year of CCTA were analyzed. Univariate and multivariate linear regression analyses were performed to evaluate the associations between CCTA‐derived calcification parameters, IVUS‐derived stent expansion, and stent symmetry index. Group‐based comparisons and receiver‐operating characteristic curve analyses were performed as secondary analyses to identify poor stent expansion (< 90%).ResultsMean density was significantly associated with stent expansion in both univariable analysis and the multivariable mean density model adjusted for calcium arc, calcium thickness, and rotational atherectomy (β = –0.085; 95% confidence interval [CI], –0.152 to –0.019; p = 0.013). Mean density showed a moderate inverse correlation with both stent expansion (r = –0.415, p = 0.003) and the stent symmetry index (r = –0.292, p = 0.042). In the secondary analyses, receiver operating characteristic curve analysis indicated that a cutoff value of 514.93 Hounsfield units yielded an area under the curve of 0.80 in non‐atherectomy cases.ConclusionsThese findings suggest that CCTA‐derived mean density may help guide lesion‐specific interventional strategies and serve as a valuable, noninvasive preprocedural marker for assessing the mechanical difficulty of CN/NC lesions.Keywords: calcified nodule, coronary computed tomography angiography, drug‐eluting stent, intravascular ultrasound, percutaneous coronary intervention

Introduction

Ischemic heart disease (IHD) remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, accounting for approximately nine million deaths in 2021 [1]. Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) is one of the primary therapeutic strategies for IHD and aims to restore blood flow by advancing a catheter into the stenotic or occluded coronary arteries and deploying a balloon or stent. Lipid‐rich, fibrotic, or calcified plaques commonly cause coronary stenosis. Severely calcified lesions are particularly challenging and often lead to difficulty in device delivery and inadequate stent expansion, both of which negatively affect procedural success and long‐term outcomes [2, 3, 4]. Notably, calcified nodules (CN) and nodular calcifications (NC) frequently protrude into the coronary lumen, potentially impeding device passage and optimal stent deployment [5, 6, 7].

An adequate preprocedural risk assessment is crucial for planning treatment strategies for heavily calcified and complex lesions. Therefore, a simple and noninvasive method for predicting procedural difficulty is highly optimal. Coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) is widely used as a noninvasive imaging modality to evaluate plaque characteristics within the coronary arteries [8, 9, 10, 11, 12]. Mean density, measured in Hounsfield units (HU), has been proposed as a potential surrogate marker of plaque hardness and may help predict procedural complexity [13]. However, the relationship between CCTA‐derived findings and stent expandability, particularly in protrusive calcified lesions such as CN and NC, remains unclear.

To our knowledge, no study has systematically evaluated whether the CCTA‐derived mean density can predict the risk of inadequate stent expansion in CN or NC lesions. Establishing the mean density as a reliable noninvasive predictor of procedural difficulty in these lesion subsets could provide a valuable preprocedural risk‐stratification tool to guide tailored interventional strategies. This approach could ultimately improve procedural success and long‐term outcomes in patients undergoing PCI for CN and NC lesions.

Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the association between the mean density measured on CCTA and the stent expandability assessed during PCI in intravascular ultrasound (IVUS)‐confirmed CN and NC lesions to clarify the potential of noninvasive imaging for predicting the difficulty of treating heavily calcified coronary lesions.

Methods

Study Population

This single‐center retrospective observational study was conducted at the Nagasaki Harbor Medical Center, Nagasaki, Japan. Patients who underwent IVUS‐guided PCI for newly treated target vessels between January 2015 and December 2024 after undergoing CCTA within 1 year before PCI were eligible for inclusion. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) absence of CN or NC in the target vessel and (2) no stent implantation. Baseline clinical data, including age, sex, diagnosis, comorbidities, and medication history, were collected from electronic medical records. Laboratory test results obtained within 3 months before PCI were also analyzed. For secondary analyses, lesions were additionally categorized into two groups according to IVUS‐derived stent expansion: the good expansion group (stent expansion ≥ 90%) and the poor expansion group (stent expansion < 90%).

CCTA Procedure and Analysis

Electrocardiography‐gated CCTA was performed using a 256‐slice CT scanner (SOMATOM Definition Flash; Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany). Standard acquisition parameters included 0.75 × 128 × 0.6 mm collimation, a tube voltage of 120 kV with 250–350 mA, and a gantry rotation time of 280 ms. Iodinated contrast medium (iopamidol, iodine 370 mg/mL) was injected via an antecubital vein at 3.5–5.5 mL/s using a dual‐head injector. Images were reconstructed at 75% and 45% R–R intervals. To reduce the calcium blooming artifacts, the window level and width (WL/WW) were set to 400/1500 HU. Each coronary artery was divided into four segments (right, left main, left anterior descending, and left circumflex). For the target vessel, the coronary artery calcium score (Agatston score) and calcified plaque volume (CPV; mm3) were calculated. The calcium arc and maximum thickness were measured at the point where the CN/NC lesions were identified using IVUS. The total calcium arcs were recorded when multiple calcified deposits were present. The outer vessel contours were manually traced and corrected using a dedicated software (Ziostation2+ ver. 2.9.8.4, Ziosoft, Tokyo, Japan). Maximum and mean density (HU) values were computed automatically (Figure 1). For interobserver reproducibility analysis, 20 cases were randomly selected from the 49 enrolled patients and independently analyzed by a blinded reviewer.

PCI Procedure Details

PCI was performed via radial or femoral artery access using 6‐ to 8‐Fr guiding catheters. Standard PCI techniques were employed, and lesion preparation, including the use of atherectomy devices, was performed at the operator's discretion before drug‐eluting stent implantation. Additional balloon dilation or adjunctive procedures for suboptimal stent expansion or malapposition were performed at the discretion of the surgeon.

Quantitative Coronary Angiography Analysis

Coronary stenosis was assessed using a dedicated QCA software (CAAS ver. 7.4; Pie Medical Imaging, Maastricht, Netherlands). The measurements included lesion length (mm), pre‐ and post‐procedure reference vessel diameter (mm), minimal lumen diameter (mm), and percent diameter stenosis (%). Identical angiographic projections were used for the pre‐ and post‐PCI measurements.

Intravascular Image Analysis

IVUS imaging was performed using iLab (Boston Scientific, Natick, MA, USA), VISIWAVE, or VISICUBE (Terumo, Tokyo, Japan). Target lesions were defined as ischemia‐related lesions based on the angiographic findings and clinical data. Offline IVUS analysis was conducted using dedicated software (Nahri AQUA, Nexus Inc., Fukuoka, Japan). CN/NC lesions were defined as calcified plaques with convex luminal surfaces protruding into the vessel lumens. Maximum calcium arc, calcium length, calcium score, and reference lumen areas proximal and distal to the lesions were measured. The stent‐expansion ratio at the CN/NC site was defined as the stent area at the lesion divided by the average of the largest proximal and distal reference lumen areas. The stent symmetry index was calculated as the ratio of the minimum to the maximum stent diameters at the CN/NC site.

All analyses were independently reviewed by two experienced cardiologists who were blinded to the patient information, and a consensus was reached for all measurements.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as medians (interquartile ranges), and categorical variables were expressed as counts (percentages). Statistical significance was set at a two‐tailed p < 0.05.

Univariate and multivariate linear regression analyses were performed to evaluate the factors associated with stent expansion and the stent symmetry index. The candidate variables included mean density, maximum density, calcium arc, calcium thickness, and rotational atherectomy. To avoid multicollinearity between the mean density and maximum density, these variables were not included simultaneously in the same multivariable model; instead, two separate multivariable models were constructed: a mean density model and a maximum density model. Both models were adjusted for the calcium arc, calcium thickness, and rotational atherectomy. Results are presented as regression coefficients (β) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). For sensitivity analyses, additional multivariate analyses were performed using the IVUS‐derived calcium arc at the CN/NC site instead of the calcium arc.

Pearson's correlation analysis was conducted to assess the relationships between stent expansion, stent symmetry index, and calcification parameters, including calcium thickness, mean density, and maximum density. Subgroup analyses were performed on lesions treated without rotational atherectomy to evaluate whether the associations were consistent in this subgroup.

For secondary analyses, lesions were categorized into good expansion (stent expansion ≥ 90%) and poor expansion (< 90%) groups, and the mean density values were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Group comparisons were also performed for lesions not treated with rotational atherectomy. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed to evaluate the predictive performance of each parameter for poor stent expansion (< 90%), and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. The optimal cutoff values were determined, and the corresponding sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were reported. Subgroup ROC analyses were also conducted for lesions treated without rotational atherectomy to evaluate the predictive performance of CCTA‐derived parameters in this subgroup.

All statistical analyses were performed using the R software (version 4.5.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Ethical Statement

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Nagasaki Harbor Medical Center (Approval Number: R07‐014) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from the hospital's official website by using an opt‐out approach.

Results

Interobserver and Intraobserver Reproducibility of CT Measurements

The reproducibility of CT measurements was assessed using 20 randomly selected CCTA images. Interobserver and intraobserver agreements were excellent for mean density (0.999 and 0.997, respectively), maximum density (1.00 and 1.00, respectively), and thickness (0.989 and 0.956, respectively). The mean relative error for the maximum density was minimal at 0.62% for the interobserver comparison and 0.38% for the intraobserver comparison.

The relationship between the CT‐derived parameters and stent implantation outcomes was evaluated using correlation analyses. In the overall cohort of 49 lesions, a significant negative association was observed between mean density and stent expansion at the CN/NC sites (r = –0.415, p = 0.003, Figure 2a). The maximum density also showed a significant negative correlation (r = –0.316, p = 0.027, Figure 2b), whereas the calcium thickness demonstrated a nonsignificant negative trend (r = –0.241, p = 0.095, Figure 2c).

In the subgroup of patients who did not undergo rotational atherectomy (n = 35), stronger and statistically significant negative correlations were observed for mean density (r = –0.566, p < 0.001, Figure 2d), maximum density (r = –0.430, p = 0.010, Figure 2e), and calcium thickness (r = –0.440, p = 0.008, Figure 2f).

Negative correlations were observed between the stent symmetry index and mean density (r = –0.292, p = 0.042; Figure 2g), maximum density (r = –0.288, p = 0.045; Figure 2h), and calcium thickness (r = –0.303, p = 0.034; Figure 2i). In the subgroup of patients who did not undergo rotational atherectomy, the mean density was significantly correlated with the stent symmetry index (r = –0.398, p = 0.018, Figure 2j), whereas the maximum density and calcium thickness did not show statistically significant correlations (Figure 2k,l).

Determination of Optimal Mean Density Cutoff Values for Predicting Poor Stent Expansion

ROC curve analyses were performed to evaluate the ability of CCTA‐derived parameters to predict poor stent expansion (< 90%). In the overall cohort, mean density demonstrated the highest discriminative ability (AUC 0.63; 95% CI: 0.46–0.80), with an optimal cutoff value of 514.93 HU (sensitivity 0.68, specificity 0.60, PPV 0.55, NPV 0.21) (Figure 4a). In the subgroup without rotational atherectomy, predictive performance improved, with an AUC of 0.80 (95% CI: 0.61–0.99), sensitivity of 0.74, specificity of 0.75, PPV of 0.54, and NPV of 0.90 (Figure 4b). Discussion

This study demonstrated that CCTA‐derived mean density was significantly associated with stent expansion during PCI in IVUS‐confirmed CN/NC lesions, even after adjusting for calcium arc, calcium thickness, and rotational atherectomy. In contrast, the association between CT‐derived density parameters and the stent symmetry index was attenuated after adjustment. These findings suggest that the mean density may serve as a noninvasive preprocedural parameter for assessing the mechanical difficulty of stent expansion in calcified coronary lesions. CN can be classified into eruptive and non‐eruptive types, depending on whether fibrous cap disruption and thrombus formation are present. Previous studies using optical CT (OCT) reported that eruptive CNs exhibit better acute stent expansion than non‐eruptive CNs [14]. CN and NC are histologically thought to result from the fragmentation of the necrotic core calcium, accompanied by microvascular rupture or intraplaque hemorrhage. These pathological processes may lead to the formation of heterogeneous calcified structures within the lesion, potentially affecting stent expansion.

The limitations of imaging modalities should also be considered. While OCT provides a higher resolution than IVUS and can penetrate calcified plaques [15, 16, 17], a detailed assessment of internal calcified structures remains challenging. Similarly, IVUS can measure the calcium arc and length, but ultrasound reflections make it difficult to quantify internal features, such as thickness or density [18].

In contrast, CCTA offers the advantages of being noninvasive and widely available for preprocedural imaging. Recent studies have reported that CCTA can predict CN/NC identified using IVUS or OCT [19, 20], and prior studies have demonstrated that the mean density of CT slices with the most severe calcification is associated with stent expansion [13]. Consistent with these findings, our results suggest that the CCTA‐derived mean density provides additional information on lesion characteristics relevant to stent expandability in CN/NC lesions.

The difference in mean density likely reflects the heterogeneous tissue composition within the CN/NC, information that cannot be sufficiently captured by IVUS or OCT but can be evaluated by CCTA. As CCTA can be performed before the procedure, it has high clinical utility as a preprocedural assessment tool and may help guide treatment planning. Based on these findings, the mean density on CCTA appears to be a promising noninvasive and objective parameter for evaluating the technical complexity of PCI for CN/NC lesions. Furthermore, in secondary analyses, a mean density threshold of > 514.93 HU showed potential for identifying lesions at risk of poor post‐stent expansion and may provide adjunctive information when considering lesion preparation strategies, including the use of calcium‐modifying devices such as rotational atherectomy.

This study has several limitations. First, the mean density was measured using contrast‐enhanced CT, and the presence of contrast medium may have influenced the obtained values. Eliminating this influence is challenging; therefore, future studies should validate the applicability of mean density measurements to non‐contrast CT scans. Second, CT attenuation values are inherently dependent on the acquisition and reconstruction parameters. Although all CCTA examinations were performed using a standardized protocol on the same CT system at a single institution, the attenuation values were not normalized or calibrated across patients. Therefore, cross‐patient comparability and external applicability of the density threshold may be limited. Third, although the mean density is a reproducible value independent of the WL/WW settings, it may vary depending on how the vessel boundaries are delineated. This study followed previously published methods by manually tracing vessel contours automatically extracted using dedicated software, a technique reported to have high reproducibility [13]. Fourth, this study evaluated only the association between mean density and acute stent expansion and did not assess long‐term outcomes, such as in‐stent restenosis or target lesion revascularization. Further research is needed to clarify the impact of the mean density on long‐term clinical results. Finally, this was a single‐center, retrospective observational study with a relatively small sample size; larger multicenter studies are warranted to confirm these findings.

Conclusion

Mean density was significantly associated with stent expansion after adjusting for calcium arc, calcium thickness, and rotational atherectomy. In contrast, the association between CT‐derived density parameters and the stent symmetry index was not significant after adjustment. These findings suggest that the mean density, as assessed by CCTA, is associated with stent expansion during PCI and may serve as a useful noninvasive parameter for the preprocedural evaluation of the technical complexity of calcified coronary lesions.

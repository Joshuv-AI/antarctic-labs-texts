Quantum relative entropy for unravelings of master equations
M. Ruibal Ortigueira, R.J.P.T. de Keijzer, L. Y. Visser, O. Tse, S.J.J.M.F. Kokkelmans

Abstract
This work explores connections between the quantum relative entropy of two faithful states ρ , σ \rho,\sigma (i.e. full-rank density matrices) and the Kullback-Leibler divergences of classical measures μ , ν \mu,\nu . Here, μ \mu and ν \nu are measures on the space of pure states, realizing ρ \rho and σ \sigma respectively. The motivation for this result is to establish a notion of quantum relative entropy in the space of pure state distributions, which are the resulting objects of unravelings of the Lindblad equation, such as the stochastic Schrödinger equation. Our results show that the measures that achieve the minimal KL divergence are those supported on a (possibly non-orthogonal) common basis between ρ \rho and σ \sigma . Using the classical and quantum data-processing inequalities, our notion of quantum relative entropy is shown to be equivalent to the Belavkin-Staszewski entropy on states, revealing new insights on this quantity. Furthermore, the common basis is used to provide a novel proof of contraction of the relative entropy under Lindblad flow and offers insights into results from large deviation theory.



I
Introduction

An important notion in both classical probability theory and quantum mechanics is relative entropy. This asymmetric functional takes two objects from the same space and outputs a positive number indicating the dissimilarity of the two objects. In the classical case, these input objects are probability distributions; in the quantum case, they are states. Relative entropy has many applications in measure theory, large deviation theory, and information theory
10
. For classical distributions, the relative entropy is also known as the
Kullback-Leibler
(KL)
divergence

D_{\mathtt{KL}}

5
. Using an axiomatic approach in which the entropy measure has to satisfy several properties such as monotonicity and positivity, Alfréd Rényi succeeded in deriving the most general form of classical entropy, the Rényi relative entropy or Rényi divergence
39
. In the limit, this reduces to the KL divergence.

Within the quantum domain, there are several established notions of relative entropy, all obeying a certain set of properties such as additivity and unitary invariance
28
. Efforts to establish the most general quantum relative entropy resulted in the quantum Petz-Rényi entropy introduced by Petz in 1984
31
, which in the limit converges to the
Umegaki relative entropy

D_{\mathtt{U}}
. The Umegaki relative entropy is the earliest notion of a relative entropy for non-commutative algebras, and was introduced in Ref.
37
in 1962. This is the most studied form of quantum relative entropy due to its operational interpretation regarding quantum hypothesis testing via quantum versions of Stein’s lemma
29
;
17
and Sanov’s theorem
3
. A different generalization of the Rényi entropy is the maximal or geometric Rényi entropy which was introduced later in Ref.
25
. In the same limit as the Petz-Rényi entropy, the geometric Rényi converges to an entropy that is of special interest in this work, the
Belavkin-Staszewski
(BS)
relative entropy

D_{\mathtt{BS}}

14
. The BS relative entropy was first introduced generally in Ref.
38
in 1982 and in operator form in Ref.
15
in 1989. Recent interest in BS entropy has increased because of its application in quantum channel capacities
14
;
4
. For simultaneously diagonizable states, all these various quantum relative entropies reduce to the classical KL divergence of the diagonal entries. Figure
1
shows a diagram of the relations of the aforementioned (quantum) relative entropies.

Figure 1:
Classical to quantum relative entropies. The unraveling entropy (UNR) proposed in this work is equivalent to the BS entropy, which is the limit of the geometric Rényi entropy.

In this work, we introduce a formulation of relative entropy stemming from a different perspective, which arises from the framework of noisy quantum systems. Noise and decoherence play an important role in the accuracy of current-generation quantum computers and simulators
13
;
12
. Closed quantum systems evolve according to the Schrödinger equation, where an input state
|\psi\rangle
evolves under a Hamiltonian
H
and purity is preserved. However, decoherence comes into play when a system interacts with its environment, and in order to describe this accurately, one has to consider an open system. The quantum system is now influenced by system-bath interactions, resulting in a mixed state which can only be represented using density matrices
\rho
. The dynamics of such an open system are most commonly described by the Lindblad equation
6

\partial_{t}\rho=\mathcal{L}(\rho)=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}^{2}S_{j}\rho S_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{j}^{2}\{S_{j}^{\dagger}S_{j},\rho\},\quad\rho(0)=\rho_{0},

(1)

where
S_{j}
are the Lindblad jump operators, and
\gamma_{j}\geq 0
are loss rates. Recently, interest in unravelings of the Lindblad equation has surged, especially with a focus on physically-inspired instances
7
;
26
;
27
;
35
. These unravelings are practical for simulation purposes, yet also provide a higher degree of understanding of the system-bath couplings. A specific example of an unraveling is the stochastic Schrödinger equation (SSE)
8
, which was introduced in Refs.
13
;
12
;
35
;
36
as a physically-inspired unraveling describing classical control noise. The SSE is given by

{\rm d}|\psi\rangle=-iH|\psi\rangle{\rm d}t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}^{2}S_{j}^{\dagger}S_{j}|\psi\rangle{\rm d}[X_{j}]+i\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}S_{j}|\psi\rangle{\rm d}X_{j}.

(2)

Here,
X_{j}
are classical noise processes and are taken to be semimartingales, the biggest class of processes for which Itô calculus is valid
22
.
[X_{j}]
are their corresponding quadratic variations. All unravelings of this form induce a classical probability distribution
\mu=\text{Law}(|\psi\rangle)
on the associated Hilbert space
36
. Building on this insight, we establish a physically-inspired notion of quantum relative entropy based on classical probability distributions on pure states. We show that it reduces to the Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy, delivering a novel understanding of this quantity. These results will elevate our understanding of quantum relative entropies and their relationship to physical realizations.

This work is structured as follows. Section
II
introduces preliminary results that are necessary in our discussion of quantum relative entropy, subdivided in classical and quantum results. In Sec.
III
, the main result regarding quantum relative entropies for unravelings is posited, after which it is proven in two separate parts. In Sec.
IV
, two corollary results of the unraveling approach are given, related to contractions of the Lindblad equation and large deviation theory. Section
V
concludes the results and discusses implications, conjectures, and possible areas for future work.

II
Background

This section concisely introduces several concepts in classical probability theory and quantum information necessary for reaching the results proven in Sec.
III
. Throughout, we consider a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}
of dimension
n
(for
m
qubits
n=2^{m}
). We denote the space of bounded linear operators on
\mathfrak{h}
by
\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{h})
, and the set of density operators, henceforth called
states
, by

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h}):=\bigl\{\rho\in\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{h}):\rho^{\dagger}=\rho,\;\rho\succeq 0,\;\text{tr}[\rho]=1\bigr\}.

(3)

We equip
\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
with the trace distance
d_{\mathtt{TR}}
, given by
d_{\mathtt{TR}}(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\Tr[\sqrt{(\rho-\sigma)^{\dagger}(\rho-\sigma)}]
, making
(\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h}),d_{\mathtt{TR}})
a metric space. We further denote
\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
as the set of positive definite density operators (
\rho\succ 0
), henceforth called
faithful states
. States that are rank-
1
projection operators (
\rho^{2}=\rho
) are called
pure states
. The set of pure states may be identified with the unit sphere in
\mathfrak{h}
, which we denote by
\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{h}\supset\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}):=\bigl\{\psi\in\mathfrak{h}:\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{h}}=1\bigr\}\simeq\bigl\{|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|:\psi\in\mathfrak{h},\;\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{h}}=1\bigr\}\subset\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h}).

The following compactness result for the set of pure states is proven in Ref.
23
.

Proposition 1

(Compactness of
\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be finite-dimensional Hilbert space with inner product
\braket{\cdot|\cdot}
. Then the set of pure states
\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
is compact under the Fubini-Study metric

d_{\mathtt{FS}}(\psi,\varphi)=\arccos{|\langle\psi|\varphi\rangle|},\qquad\psi,\varphi\in\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}).

(4)

Henceforth, we equip
\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
with the Fubini-Study metric
d_{\mathtt{FS}}
, thus making
(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}),d_{\mathtt{FS}})
a compact metric space.

II.1
Classical probability measures

We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X})
the set of probability measures on a measurable set
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{F})
. The following definitions are adapted from Ref.
32
.

Definition 2

(KL divergence)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X})
be two probability measures. The Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
is defined as

\displaystyle D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{X}}\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}\nu}\log\biggl(\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}\nu}\biggr)\nu({\rm d}x)=\int_{\mathfrak{X}}\log\biggl(\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}\nu}\biggr)\mu({\rm d}x)&\text{if }\mu\ll\nu,\\
+\infty&\text{else,}\end{cases}

(5)

where
\mu\ll\nu
indicates absolute continuity of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
, and
{\rm d}\mu/{\rm d}\nu
denotes the
\nu
-density of
\mu
.

It is worth noting that the KL divergence is an important example in a wider set of metrics called
f
-divergences. The results of this work can be generalized to
f
-divergences, which is the subject of App.
B
. For clarity and accessibility, we restrict ourselves to KL divergences and relative entropies. All
f
-divergences enjoy the Data Processing Inequality (DPI). To understand the DPI, Markov kernels and composition measures are introduced
9
.

Definition 3

(Markov kernel)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space. A Markov kernel
\mathsf{K}:\mathfrak{X}{\times}\mathcal{F}\to[0,1]
on
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{F})
is a map satisfying the following properties:

(1)

For every
x\in\mathfrak{X}
,
\mathsf{K}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X})
;

(2)

For any measurable set
A\in\mathcal{F}
, the map
x\mapsto\mathsf{K}(x,A)
is
\mathcal{F}
-measurable.

Definition 4

(Composition measure)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X})
and
\mathsf{K}
be a Markov kernel on a measurable space
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{F})
. The composition measure
\mathsf{K}{\circ}\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X})
is defined as

\mathsf{K}{\circ}\mu(A):=\int_{\mathfrak{X}}\mathsf{K}(x,A)\mu({\rm d}x),\qquad A\in\mathcal{F}.

(6)

Proposition 5

(DPI for KL divergence)
.

Let
\mathsf{K}
be a Markov kernel on a measurable space
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{F})
. Then,

D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mathsf{K}{\circ}\mu\|\mathsf{K}{\circ}\nu)\leq D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)\qquad\text{for every $\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X})$.}

(7)

Intuitively, this implies that two probability measures become harder to distinguish when the same operation is applied to them. This is logical, as the operation can only obscure or discard the features that differentiate the measures, not introduce new ones.

The KL divergence has found a central role in the study of hypothesis testing and large deviations theory. Sanov’s theorem establishes it as the exponential decay rate of the probability that the empirically distribution of a system deviates from the true, underlying distribution
1
. In Sec.
IV.2
, we present a corollary of our results related to large deviation theory, for which the following two propositions are necessary.

Proposition 6

(Sanov’s theorem)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{X},\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space, where
\mathfrak{X}
is a Polish space. Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X})
. Consider a sequence of i.i.d. random variables
X_{1},X_{2},...
on
\mathfrak{X}
with law
\nu
. Let
\nu_{n}
be the empirical measure of the sample
X_{1},...,X_{n}
, given by
\nu_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}}
. The following Large Deviation Principle (LDP) holds

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\nu_{n}\in O)\geq-\inf_{\mu\in O}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu),

(8)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\nu_{n}\in C)\leq-\inf_{\mu\in C}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu),

(9)

where
O
denotes an open set in
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X})
, equipped with the weak topology, and
C
denotes a closed one. Note that
\nu_{n}
is a random variable by virtue of the sampling.

The Contraction Principle formalises how a continuous function extends an LDP on one space to another
21
. In the context of this work, this result is stated directly for the empirical measure in Prop.
6
.

Proposition 7

(Contraction Principle)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}_{1},\mathfrak{X}_{2}
be Polish spaces. Let
\nu_{n}
be a sequence of
\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X}_{1})
-valued random variables that satisfies an LDP with rate
n
and rate function
I
. If
\varGamma:\mathcal{P}(\mathfrak{X}_{1})\to\mathfrak{X}_{2}
is a continuous map, then for any Borel sets
O\subset\mathfrak{X}_{2}
open and
C\subset\mathfrak{X}_{2}
closed, the sequence
\varGamma(\nu_{n})
satisfies the LDP

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\varGamma(\nu_{n})\in O)\geq-\inf_{y\in O}\inf_{\mu\in\varGamma^{-1}(y)}I(\mu),

(10)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\varGamma(\nu_{n})\in C)\leq-\inf_{y\in C}\inf_{\mu\in\varGamma^{-1}(y)}I(\mu).

(11)

Moreover,
J(y)=\inf_{\mu\in\varGamma^{-1}(y)}I(\mu)
is lower semi-continuous.

II.2
Quantum relative entropies

Definition 8

(Realization of states)
.

Let
\rho\in\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
be a state. A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))
is said to
realize

\rho
if

\rho=\Lambda(\mu):=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\,\mu({\rm d}\psi),

where
\Lambda:\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
.

Definition 9

(Common basis)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
be two states. A (possibly non-orthogonal) basis
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of
\mathfrak{h}
is said to be a
common basis
for the pair
(\rho,\sigma)
if there are
coefficients
\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}\in[0,1]
for
i\in\{1,...,n\}
such that

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}=1,\qquad\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}=1.

(12)

In this case, one can associate measures
\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}:=\sum_{j}\rho_{j}\delta_{\psi_{j}}
and
\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}:=\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}\delta_{\psi_{j}}
, that realize
\rho
and
\sigma
, such that

D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}||\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{j}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}\right).

(13)

We denote the set of all common bases for two states
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
by
\mathtt{CB}(\rho,\sigma)
, shortened to
\mathtt{CB}
.

Definition 10

(Quantum relative entropy)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma
be states in
\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
. Two versions of the quantum relative entropy
37
;
38
are given by

\displaystyle D_{\mathtt{U}}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\Tr[\rho\log(\rho)-\rho\log(\sigma)]&\text{if }\supp(\rho)\subseteq\supp(\sigma),\\
+\infty&\text{else}.\end{cases}\,\,\,

\displaystyle\text{(Umegaki)}

(14)

\displaystyle D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\Tr[\rho\log(\sqrt{\rho}\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\rho})]&\,\,\,\,\,\text{if }\supp(\rho)\subseteq\supp(\sigma),\\
+\infty&\,\,\,\,\,\text{else}.\end{cases}\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\text{(Belavkin-Staszewski)}

Note the resemblance between the quantum relative entropy and KL divergence. In fact, when
\rho
and
\sigma
commute, we have that
D_{\mathtt{U}}=D_{\mathtt{BS}}
and they are exactly the KL divergence
D_{\mathtt{KL}}
between the eigenvalues on the shared eigenbasis. In Ref.
20
, it is proven that
D_{\mathtt{U}}\leq D_{\mathtt{BS}}.

The non-commutative nature of quantum information allows for different quantum
f
-divergences for a given operator-convex
f
. For example, for
f(x)=x\log(x)
, we find both the Umegaki and Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropies. The Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy is part of a larger set of
maximal (quantum)
f
-divergences
. This family of functionals was named by Matsumoto in Ref.
25
, and it satisfies the standard axioms for quantum divergences. On the other hand, the Umegaki relative entropy is typically considered a
standard (quantum)
f
-divergence

19
. These (quantum) relative entropies and
f
-divergences are lower semi-continuous. In Refs.
18
and
4
, it is shown that both the Umegaki and Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropies satisfy the quantum equivalent of the Data Processing Inequality.

Proposition 11

(Quantum DPI)
.

For any completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) map
\Phi:\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})\to\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}(\mathfrak{h})
,

D_{j}(\Phi(\rho)\|\Phi(\sigma))\leq D_{j}(\rho\|\sigma),\qquad\text{for every $\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})$},\qquad j\in\{\mathtt{U},\mathtt{BS}\}.

(15)

III
Unraveling Relative Entropy

Stochastic unravelings of master equations, resulting in stochastic Schrödinger equations, provide an interpretation of the state as a representation of an ensemble of pure states. This perspective naturally leads to the question whether there is a corresponding interpretation for quantum relative entropy. In this section, we introduce a notion of entropy defined on such ensembles of pure states
D_{\mathtt{UNR}}
as

Definition 12

(Unravel relative entropy)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
be states. Our proposed
unravel relative entropy
(
\mathtt{UNR}
) is given by

D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho\|\sigma):=\inf_{\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))}\Bigl\{D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)\;:\;\rho=\Lambda(\mu),\;\sigma=\Lambda(\nu)\Bigr\},

(16)

as the infimum over the KL divergences between measures
\mu
and
\nu
on
\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
that realize
\rho
and
\sigma
, respectively.

Our main result is a tighter version of the ‘minimal reverse test’ characterization of
maximal f-divergences
in the spirit of Refs.
25
;
24
. For this purpose, we are concerned with finding probability measures
\mu,\nu
on pure states that realize two faithful states
\rho,\sigma
respectively, such that they achieve the infimum in Eq. (
16
). That is, we prove

Theorem 13

(Entropy equivalence)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
be two faithful states. Then there exist probability measures
\mu_{\mathtt{CB}},\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}
supported on a common (possibly non-orthogonal) basis
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
, that realize
\rho,\sigma
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma)\overset{(\bigstar)}{=}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})\overset{(\blacktriangle)}{=}D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho\|\sigma).

(17)

Our unraveling-inspired interpretation of mixed states as statistical ensembles of pure states allows for a constructive proof of this theorem. The result provides a direct connection between the quantum relative entropy of two mixed states and the classical relative entropy of two statistical ensembles of pure states that realize them. It is worth noting that this theorem is directly extendable to
f
-divergences, as detailed in App.
B
.
In the generalized result, the BS relative entropy is replaced by the corresponding
maximal
f
-divergence
.

In Sec.
III.1
, we will detail a method of constructing a common basis. Using a basis from
\mathtt{CB}(\rho,\sigma)
(see Def.
9
), it is possible to determine
\mu_{\mathtt{CB}},\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}
from any pair
\rho,\sigma
. This allows for some exploratory simulations where we randomly sample states in
\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
according to the Haar random measure
11
and calculate the quantities
D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma),D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})
, and
D_{\mathtt{U}}(\rho\|\sigma)
. The results of this are shown in Fig.
2
. These results indicate equivalence between
D_{\mathtt{KL}}
on the common basis and
D_{\mathtt{BS}}
, but a clear difference with the distribution of
D_{\mathtt{U}}
. Moreover, every single random realization of
D_{\mathtt{KL}}
on the common basis and
D_{\mathtt{BS}}
was found to correspond up to numerical accuracy, providing numerical evidence of the equality given by
(\bigstar)
.

Figure 2:
Approximate probability density functions of
D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma),D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})
, and
D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho\|\sigma)
constructed from
10^{5}
random pair of states
(\rho,\sigma)
sampled according to the Haar measure.

Proof outline of the entropy equivalence theorem

First, in Sec.
III.1
, Theorem
14
shows that it is always possible to construct a common basis
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
in which to express both
\rho
and
\sigma
, resulting in measures
\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}
and
\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}
respectively. Following, Sec.
III.2
uses the concept of dual bases to prove that
D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})
realizes the Belavkin-Staszewski quantum relative entropy
D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma)
, thus proving (
\bigstar
). In Sec.
III.3
, we use a discretization argument and the classical DPI to show that
D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})
realizes the infimum of
D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho\|\sigma)
, proving equality (
\blacktriangle
).

III.1
Common basis construction

One of the main results necessary for the proof of Theorem
13
is the construction of a common basis (Def.
9
) for the pair of states
(\rho,\sigma)
in a
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}
. The intuition behind this comes from the two-dimensional case (qubit), where the states are in the interior of the Bloch sphere. The measures
\mu
and
\nu
would have the lowest KL divergence (i.e. be least discernible) if their joint measure is concentrated as much as possible. This happens when the two states are expressed as the weighted sum of the two pure states at the intersection of the Bloch sphere and the line connecting
\rho
and
\sigma
. These two states then amount to a common basis, also used in optimally discriminating Helstrom measurements
16
, see Fig.
3
. Theorem
14
extends the existence of a common basis to higher dimensions.

Figure 3:
Common basis construction on 1-qubit state. Two ensembles of pure states
\{\psi_{\omega}\}_{\omega}
(circles) encode for states
\rho
(stars). The unique common basis
\{\psi_{\mathtt{CB},i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of pure states (red crosses) is constructed by finding the intersection of the Bloch sphere and the line connecting both
\rho
’s (red dashed). Note that the states
\{\psi_{\mathtt{CB},i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are not orthogonal.

Theorem 14

(Existence of common basis)
.

There exists a common basis for any two faithful states
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
, i.e., there is a (possibly non-orthogonal) basis
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathtt{CB}(\rho,\sigma)
and coefficients
\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}\in[0,1]
for
i\in\{1,...,n\}
such that

\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}=1,\qquad\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}=1.

(18)

To prove Theorem
14
, we will first prove several lemmas, the proofs of which are deferred to App.
A
.

Lemma 15

(Number eigenvalues)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
be faithful states. Then, there are exactly
n
number of
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
(including multiplicity) such that
\det(\rho+\lambda_{i}(\sigma-\rho))=0
.

We argue that for a given
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
there are as many linearly independent eigenvectors of
\rho+\lambda_{i}(\sigma-\rho)
with eigenvalue
0
as the algebraic multiplicity of
1-1/\lambda_{i}
as an eigenvalue of
\rho^{-1}\sigma
.

Lemma 16

(Multiplicity)
.

For any eigenvalue
1-1/\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
of
\rho^{-1}\sigma
with algebraic multiplicity
k\leq n
there are
k
linearly independent vectors
\{w_{i,j}\}_{j=1}^{k}
such that

(\rho+\lambda_{i}(\sigma-\rho))\ket{w_{i,j}}=0.

(19)

By Lemmas
15
and
16
, it follows that there are
n
eigenvectors
v_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}
of
\rho^{-1}\sigma
with eigenvalues
1-1/\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,...,n
, such that

(\rho+\lambda_{i}(\sigma-\rho))|v_{i}\rangle=\rho|v_{i}\rangle+\lambda_{i}(\sigma-\rho)|v_{i}\rangle=0.

(20)

Before defining the pure states that will compose the common basis, a scheme is proposed to ensure that the chosen representatives of the eigenspaces of
\rho^{-1}\sigma
also function as a dual basis of the common basis.

Lemma 17

(Gram-Schmidt)
.

Let
\{w_{i,j}\}_{j}
be a normalized basis of an eigenspace of
\rho^{-1}\sigma
with eigenvalue
1-1/\lambda_{i}
. Then a normalized basis
\{u_{i,j}\}_{j}
of the same eigenspace can be constructed for which
\braket{u_{i,j}|\rho|u_{i,l}}=0
for
j\neq l
.

Lemma 18

(Linear independence)
.

Take
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
to be the eigenvectors of
\rho^{-1}\sigma
where the eigenvectors for eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity
k<n
are obtained following according to Lemma
17
. Define the pure states
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
in the following way

\displaystyle|\psi_{i}\rangle:=\frac{\rho|u_{i}\rangle}{\|\rho|u_{i}\rangle\|}\quad\forall\,i\in\{1,...,n\}.

(21)

Then,
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
is a linearly independent set and
\braket{\psi_{j}|u_{i}}=0
for all
i\neq j\in\{1,...,n\}
, thus
\psi_{i}^{\perp}=u_{i}
.

We now continue to use Lemmas
15
-
18
to construct a common basis for two faithful states, and thus prove Theorem
14
.

Proof of Theorem
14
.

Let
\rho,\sigma
be faithful states in a
n
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}
. By Lemmas
15
and
16
, there are
n
eigenpairs
(\lambda_{i},v_{i})
of
\rho^{-1}\sigma
such that
(\rho+\lambda_{i}(\sigma-\rho))\ket{v_{i}}=0
. To account for multiplicity, use the scheme presented in Lemma
17
to obtain a new set of
n
vectors
\{u_{i}\}_{i}
and define the pure states
\ket{\psi_{i}}
, according to Lemma
18
.

Now consider the ansatzes

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|\,,\qquad\,\rho_{i}:=\frac{\|\rho|u_{i}\rangle\|^{2}}{\braket{u_{i}|\rho|u_{i}}}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|\,,\qquad\sigma_{i}:=\frac{\|\rho|u_{i}\rangle\|^{2}}{\braket{u_{i}|\rho|u_{i}}^{2}}\braket{u_{i}|\sigma|u_{i}}=\rho_{i}\frac{\braket{u_{i}|\sigma|u_{i}}}{\braket{u_{i}|\rho|u_{i}}}.

We claim that
\rho=\hat{\rho}
,
\sigma=\hat{\sigma}
. According to Lemma
18
,
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
forms a basis. Hence, it suffices to show that
\rho\ket{u_{k}}=\hat{\rho}\ket{u_{k}}
and
\sigma\ket{u_{k}}=\hat{\sigma}\ket{u_{k}}
for all
k\in\{1,...,n\}
. Using Lemma
18
, it follows that

\hat{\rho}|u_{k}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\|\rho|u_{i}\rangle\|^{2}}{\braket{u_{i}|\rho|u_{i}}}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|u_{k}\rangle=\frac{\|\rho|u_{k}\rangle\|^{2}}{\braket{u_{k}|\rho|u_{k}}}|\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k}|u_{k}\rangle=\frac{\|\rho|u_{k}\rangle\|^{2}}{\braket{u_{k}|\rho|u_{k}}}\frac{\rho|u_{k}\rangle}{\|\rho|u_{k}\rangle\|}\frac{\braket{u_{k}|\rho|u_{k}}}{\|\rho|u_{k}\rangle\|}=\rho|u_{k}\rangle.

(23)

Similarly, using
\rho|u_{k}\rangle=-\lambda_{k}(\sigma-\rho)|u_{k}\rangle
, we arrive at

\hat{\sigma}|u_{k}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\|\rho|u_{i}\rangle\|^{2}}{\braket{u_{i}|\rho|u_{i}}^{2}}\braket{u_{i}|\sigma|u_{i}}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|u_{k}\rangle=\frac{\braket{u_{k}|\sigma|u_{k}}}{\braket{u_{k}|\rho|u_{k}}}\rho|u_{k}\rangle=\sigma|u_{k}\rangle.

(24)

The vectors
(\rho_{i})_{i},(\sigma_{i})_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are probability vectors as,
\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}\geq 0
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and

1=\Tr[\rho]=\Tr\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i},

(25)

and similarly for
(\sigma_{i})_{i}
. Thus,
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a common basis of
\rho
and
\sigma
.
∎

The following lemma shows that the found basis must be a linearly independent set and is as small as possible.

Lemma 19

(Support)
.

Let
\rho\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
be a faithful state. Then any probability measure that realizes
\rho
must have support on at least
n
linearly independent pure states.

From Lemma
17
it follows that the common basis is not unique when
\rho^{-1}\sigma
has
m<n
distinct eigenvalues, since permuting the vectors before applying the scheme returns a different set. The following lemma argues that if all eigenvalues of
\rho^{-1}\sigma
are distinct, then the common basis is unique up to a permutation.

Lemma 20

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
be two faithful states. Let
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be two sets of common bases for the pair
(\rho,\sigma)
. If
\rho^{-1}\sigma
has
n
distinct eigenvalues, then there is a permutation
\pi:\{1,...,n\}\to\{1,...,n\}
and scalars
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|c_{i}|=1
such that
\ket{\psi_{i}}=c_{i}\ket{\phi_{\pi(i)}}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.

In summary, a common basis of
n
linearly independent pure states for two faithful states has been constructed and shown to be as small as possible and, under specific circumstances, unique up to permutation.

III.2
Equivalence of
D_{\mathtt{BS}}
and
D_{\mathtt{UNR}}

Before proving
(\bigstar)
of Theorem
13
, an auxiliary lemma is proven. From here on, we use the convention that
\braket{\psi^{\perp}_{i}|\psi_{j}}=\delta_{ij}
. Hence, we don’t necessarily have normalized dual basis states, i.e.,
\braket{\psi^{\perp}_{i}|\psi_{i}^{\perp}}\neq 1
.

Lemma 21

(Inverse state)
.

Let
\rho\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
be a faithful state. Suppose
\rho
can be written as a convex combination of
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|\}_{i}
with coefficients
\rho_{i}\in(0,1]
. Let
\{\psi_{i}^{\perp}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the corresponding dual basis of
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle\rho^{-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}|\psi_{i}^{\perp}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}^{\perp}|.

(26)

Now
(\bigstar)
of Theorem
13
can be proven.

Proof of Theorem
13
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
be two faithful states. Let
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathtt{CB}(\rho,\sigma)
be a common basis and let
\{\psi_{j}^{\perp}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be the corresponding dual basis, such that
\braket{\psi_{i}|\psi_{j}^{\perp}}=\delta_{ij}
. Then there are coefficients
\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}
such that

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}},\quad\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}}.

(27)

Let
\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}
,
\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}
be the measures having support on the common basis that realize
\rho
,
\sigma
, then

D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}||\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right).

(28)

Note that since
\sqrt{\rho}
and
\sigma^{-1}
are Hermitian matrices, there is an invertible matrix
P
and a diagonal matrix
D
with the eigenvalues of
\sqrt{\rho}\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\rho}
as entries such that

\rho\sigma^{-1}=\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\rho}\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}=\sqrt{\rho}PDP^{-1}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}.

(29)

It directly follows that
\rho\sigma^{-1}
is also diagonizable and has the same eigenvalues as
\sqrt{\rho}\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\rho}
. By functional calculus and the cyclical property of the trace

\displaystyle D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\Tr[\rho\log(\sqrt{\rho}\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\rho})]=\Tr[\rho P\log(D)P^{-1}]=\Tr[\rho\sqrt{\rho}P\log(D)P^{-1}\sqrt{\rho}^{-1}]=\Tr[\rho\log(\rho\sigma^{-1})].

(30)

Using Lemma
21
, one deduces

\displaystyle\rho\sigma^{-1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sigma_{j}}\ket{\psi_{i}}\braket{\psi_{i}|\psi_{j}^{\perp}}\bra{\psi_{j}^{\perp}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}^{\perp}}.

(31)

From the representation above, it directly follows that the eigenvectors of
\rho\sigma^{-1}
are
\ket{\psi_{i}}
with eigenvalues
\rho_{i}/\sigma_{i}
for
i\in\{1,...,n\}
. Hence, one can directly compute

\displaystyle\log(\rho\sigma^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}^{\perp}}.

(32)

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma)=

\displaystyle\Tr[\rho\log(\sqrt{\rho}\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\rho})]=\Tr\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{j}\ket{\psi_{j}}\bra{\psi_{j}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}^{\perp}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)\ket{\psi_{i}}\bra{\psi_{i}}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sigma_{i}}\right)=D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}),

which proves
(\bigstar)
of Theorem
13
.
∎

III.3
Minimization of the KL divergence

In this section, we prove (
\blacktriangle
) of Theorem
13
, which is the statement

D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})=\inf_{\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))}\Bigl\{D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)\;:\;\rho=\Lambda(\mu),\;\sigma=\Lambda(\nu)\Bigr\}.

(33)

For this we require the following lemmas which are proven in App.
A
.

Lemma 22

(Continuity of
\Lambda
)
.

The map
\Lambda:(\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})),W_{1})\rightarrow(\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h}),d_{\mathtt{TR}})
is Lipschitz continuous when
\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
is equipped with the Fubiny-Study metric
d_{\mathtt{FS}}
. Here
W_{1}
denotes the 1-Wasserstein metric
34
.

Lemma 23

(Discretization)
.

Let
\mu
,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))
be measures realizing faithful states
\rho
,
\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
, respectively. Then there exists a sequence of discrete measures
\mu_{n}
,
\nu_{n}
realizing
\rho_{n}
,
\sigma_{n}
, respectively, such that

(i)

\rho_{n}\rightarrow\rho
and
\sigma_{n}\rightarrow\sigma
in
\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
, i.e.,
d_{\mathtt{TR}}(\rho_{n},\rho)\to 0
and
d_{\mathtt{TR}}(\sigma_{n},\sigma)\to 0
;

(ii)

D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{n}\|\nu_{n})
;

(iii)

D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{n}\|\nu_{n})\geq D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})
.

Using these lemmas, it is possible to prove (
\blacktriangle
) of Theorem
13
.

Proof of Theorem
13
.

Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two measures realizing
\rho
and
\sigma
respectively. Taking sequences of discrete measures
\mu_{n},\nu_{n}
and states
\rho_{n},\sigma_{n}
given by Lemma
23
,

D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{n}\|\nu_{n})\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho_{n}\|\sigma_{n})\geq D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma)=D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}),

(34)

where the first inequality follows from
(ii)
, the second from
(iii)
, the third from
(i)
combined with the lower semi-continuity of
D_{\mathtt{BS}}
with respect to the trace distance
25
, and the last equality from Lemma
19
.
∎

IV
Consequences of Theorem
13

In this section, we discuss two consequences of the unraveling characterization of the Belavkin-Staszewski entropy. The first is related to entropy decrease under Lindblad equations and the second to a large deviation principle.

IV.1
Contraction along the Lindblad flow

The unraveling of the Lindblad equation Eq. (
1
) with the stochastic Schrödinger equation (SSE) Eq. (
2
) allows one to obtain a refined contraction estimate. Indeed, consider the SSE

{\rm d}|\psi\rangle=-iH|\psi\rangle{\rm d}t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}^{2}S_{j}^{\dagger}S_{j}|\psi\rangle{\rm d}t+i\sum_{j}\gamma_{j}S_{j}|\psi\rangle{\rm d}X_{j},

(35)

where the processes
X_{j}
in Eq. (
2
) is simply taken to be Brownian motions with quadratic variation
[X_{j}]=t
. The corresponding Fokker-Planck equation
33
for the law
\mu_{t}=\text{Law}(\psi_{t})
of the process is given by

\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=L^{*}\mu_{t},

(36)

where
L^{*}
is the adjoint to the the infinitesimal generator

LF(\psi)=DF(\psi)\biggl[iH\psi+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}S^{\dagger}_{j}S_{j}\psi\biggr]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}D^{2}F(\psi)[S_{j}\psi,S_{j}\psi].

Here,
DF
and
D^{2}F
are the first and second-order Fréchet derivatives of the function
F
on
\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})
, respectively.

We further introduce the
relative KL Fisher information

I_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)
of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
defined by

I_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{j}\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\left(D\sqrt{\frac{{\rm d}\mu}{{\rm d}\nu}}[S_{j}\psi]\right)^{2}\nu({\rm d}\psi)&\text{if $\mu\ll\nu$,}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(37)

Accordingly, we define the
relative unravel Fisher information
as

I_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho\|\sigma):=\inf_{\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))}\Bigl\{I_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)\;:\;\rho=\Lambda(\mu),\;\sigma=\Lambda(\nu)\Bigr\}.

(38)

Note that both
I_{\mathtt{KL}}\geq 0
and
I_{\mathtt{UNR}}\geq 0
.

Theorem 24

(Contraction under the Lindblad flow)
.

Let
\Phi_{t}
be the solution operator to the Lindblad equation, i.e.,
\rho_{t}=\Phi_{t}(\rho_{0})
satisfies Eq. (
1
) for any initial state
\rho_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
. Then the following entropy-dissipation inequality holds

D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{t}\|\sigma_{t})+2\int_{0}^{t}I_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{s}\|\sigma_{s})\,{\rm d}s\leq D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{0}\|\sigma_{0})\qquad\text{for any $\rho_{0},\sigma_{0}\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})$.}

(39)

In particular, the same entropy-dissipation inequality holds for
D_{\mathtt{BS}}
instead of
D_{\mathtt{UNR}}
.

Proof.

Consider a common basis
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathtt{CB}(\rho_{0},\sigma_{0})
, which gives the initial measures
\mu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}
and
\nu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}
such that
D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{0,\mathtt{CB}})=D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{0}\|\sigma_{0})
. The evolutions of
\mu_{t}
and
\nu_{t}
of
\mu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}
and
\nu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}
along the Fokker-Planck equation of Eq. (
36
) yield the entropy-dissipation inequality

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{t}\|\nu_{t})=-2\sum_{j}\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\left(D\sqrt{\frac{{\rm d}\mu_{t}}{{\rm d}\nu_{t}}}[S_{j}\psi]\right)^{2}\nu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)=-2I_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{t}\|\nu_{t})\leq 0.

(40)

The calculation of this time derivative is deferred to App.
C
. Note that since

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mu_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\mu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\psi_{t}\rangle\langle\psi_{t}|\;\big|\;|\psi_{0}\rangle=|\psi\rangle\bigr]\mu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}({\rm d}\psi)

(41)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\Phi_{t}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\mu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}({\rm d}\psi)=\Phi_{t}\left(\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\mu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}({\rm d}\psi)\right)=\Phi_{t}(\Lambda(\mu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}))=\Phi_{t}(\rho_{0})=\rho_{t},

and similarly for
\Lambda(\nu_{t})=\sigma_{t}
,
\mu_{t}
and
\nu_{t}
realize
\rho_{t}
and
\sigma_{t}
, respectively. Consequently,
D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{t}\|\nu_{t})\geq D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{t}\|\sigma_{t})
and
I_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{t}\|\nu_{t})\geq I_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{t}\|\sigma_{t})
, and we obtain

D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{t}\|\sigma_{t})+2\int_{0}^{t}I_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{s}\|\sigma_{s})\,{\rm d}s\leq D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{0,\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{0,\mathtt{CB}})=D_{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho_{0}\|\sigma_{0}),

as asserted. The last statement follows directly from Theorem
13
, concluding the proof.
∎

Notice that if the relative KL Fisher information
I_{\mathtt{KL}}
can be made to bound the KL divergence
D_{\mathtt{KL}}
, this would imply exponential decay of the BS relative entropy
D_{\mathtt{BS}}
. This forms an interesting topic for further research.

IV.2
Large deviations perspective

Quantum versions of Stein’s Lemma and Sanov’s theorem identify the Umegaki relative entropy as the exponential rate of decay of the Type-II error probability when discriminating two quantum states by sampling observables
29
;
17
;
3
. We postulate that the Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy plays an equivalent role when directly sampling pure states from statistical ensembles that realize the quantum states.

Corollary 25

(Large deviations Belavkin-Staszewski)
.

Let
(\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})),W_{1})
and
(\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h}),d_{\mathtt{TR}})
be two metric spaces. Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
be two faithful states. Let
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))
be a probability measure realizing
\sigma
and consider the empirical measure
\nu_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\psi_{i}}
. Assume there is a
\mu^{*}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))
realizing
\rho
such that
D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu^{*}\|\nu)<\infty
(a sufficient condition for this is
\mathtt{supp}(\nu)=\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))
. Then

(i)

The following large deviation principle holds

\forall\epsilon>0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(\nu_{n})\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho))=-\inf_{\eta\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta),\qquad J(\eta):=\inf_{\mu\in\Lambda^{-1}(\eta)}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu),

(42)

where
B_{\epsilon}(\rho)=\{\eta\in\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h}):d_{\mathtt{TR}}(\eta,\rho)<\epsilon\}
.

(ii)

The limit of
(i)
gives

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(\nu_{n})\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho))\leq-D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\rho\|\sigma),

(43)

with equality when
\nu=\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}
.

(iii)

In particular, when the support of
\nu
is a basis
\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathfrak{h}
, the rate function reduces to

J(\eta)=\begin{cases}D_{\mathtt{BS}}(\eta\|\sigma)&\text{when }\{\varphi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathtt{CB}(\eta,\sigma),\\
+\infty&\text{else}.\end{cases}

(44)

Proof.

First, note that the space of probability measures on the pure states
\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))
is a Polish space when equipped with the topology of weak convergence. Since
(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}),d_{\mathtt{FS}})
is a compact space, the
W_{1}
metric metricizes the weak topology
34
. By Sanov’s theorem Prop.
6
,
\nu_{n}
satisfies the LDP with lower semi-continuous rate function
I(\mu)=D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)
. By Lemma
22
,
\Lambda
is continuous. Therefore, by the contraction principle of Prop.
7
(with
\varGamma=\Lambda
),
\Lambda(\nu_{n})
satisfies the LDP with lower semi-continuous rate function
J(\eta)=\inf_{\mu\in\Lambda^{-1}(\eta)}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)
.
Fix some
\epsilon>0
. Let
B_{\epsilon}(\rho)
be the open ball and
\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(\rho)
be its closure. It follows directly that

-\inf_{\eta\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(\nu_{n})\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho))\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(\nu_{n})\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(\rho))\leq-\inf_{\eta\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta).

(45)

To establish the rate function in
(i)
, it is shown that
a(\epsilon):=\inf_{\eta\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta)=\inf_{\eta\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta)=:b(\epsilon)
. It suffices to show
a(\epsilon)\leq b(\epsilon)
, as the reverse direction is trivial.

Since
\Lambda:\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
is a continuous map from a compact space to a compact space, the preimage
\Lambda^{-1}(\rho)
is also compact
2
. Since lower semi-continuous functions always achieve their infimum on compact sets, there exists a measure
\mu_{\rho}\in\Lambda^{-1}(\rho)
such that

a(\epsilon),b(\epsilon)\leq D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\rho}||\nu)=J(\rho)=\inf_{\mu\in\Lambda^{-1}(\rho)}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu\|\nu)\leq D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu^{*}\|\nu)<\infty.

(46)

Let
\eta\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(\rho)
. If
J(\eta)=\infty
, then clearly
a(\epsilon)\leq J(\eta)
.
Assume
J(\eta)<\infty
. Then, again by lower semi-continuity and compactness, there exists
\mu_{\eta}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))
such that
J(\eta)=D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{\eta}||\nu)<\infty
. Let
t\in(0,1)
and define
\mu_{t}=(1-t)\mu_{\eta}+t\mu_{\rho}
.
Then linearity of
\Lambda
, gives

d_{\mathtt{TR}}(\Lambda(\mu_{t}),\rho)=d_{\mathtt{TR}}((1-t)\eta+t\rho,\rho)=(1-t)d_{\mathtt{TR}}(\eta,\rho)<\epsilon.

(47)

Thus,
\Lambda(\mu_{t})\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho)
for all
t\in(0,1)
, which together with convexity of the relative entropy implies

a(\epsilon)=\inf_{\eta^{\prime}\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta^{\prime})\leq J(\Lambda(\mu_{t}))\leq D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{t}||\nu)\leq(1-t)J(\eta)+tJ(\rho).

(48)

Taking the limit as
t\to 0
, we recover
a(\epsilon)\leq J(\eta)
. This holds for any
\eta\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(\rho)
, thus

a(\epsilon)\leq\inf_{\eta\in\overline{B}_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta)=b(\epsilon).

(49)

This proves statement
(i)
of the corollary

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(\Lambda(\nu_{n})\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho))=-\inf_{\eta\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta).

(50)

We continue with proving the limit of statement
(ii)
. Clearly, we have that

a(\epsilon):=\inf_{\eta\in B_{\epsilon}(\rho)}J(\eta)\leq J(\rho).

(51)

As
a(\epsilon)
is monotonically increasing as
\epsilon
decreases,
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}a(\epsilon)
exists. Let
\epsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers. We find a sequence
\eta_{n}\in B_{\epsilon_{n}}(\rho)
with
\eta_{n}\rightarrow\rho
in trace distance and

By Theorem

that achieves the minimum.

As for

(54)

V
Conclusion

In this work, we presented the development of a quantum relative entropy based on minimal KL divergences for classical ensembles of pure states, realizing fixed states. These classical ensembles play a pivotal role as they emerge naturally as solutions of unravelings of the Lindblad equation. Our results show that the minimal KL divergence is achieved when the two states are expressed in their unique (possibly non-orthogonal) common basis. The composition of this basis is described using a constructive algorithm. We show that our formulation of the quantum relative entropy coincides exactly with the Belavkin-Staszewski entropy on states, subsequently offering new insights in this quantity. Moreover, we use this insight to provide novel proofs of a contraction principle on the Lindblad equation as well as a quantum large deviation theorem.

In future work, we want to consider extensions of our methodology to non-faithful states. Specifically, we hypothesize that our novel quantum relative entropy extends to the case where the support of
\rho
is a subset of the support of
\sigma
. Furthermore, for a single qubit, we identify the superpositions of the elements of the common basis with their dual as the measurement operators for a Helstrom measurement
16
, which is optimal for single-shot state discrimination. We hypothesize that, in larger dimensions, the common basis plays an equivalent role.

D_{f}(\mu\|\nu):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{X}}f\biggl(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}\biggr)\nu(dx)&\text{if }\mu\ll\nu,\\
+\infty&\text{else.}\end{cases}

(82)

If
\mu\ll\nu\ll\zeta
with
u:=d\mu/d\zeta
and
v:=d\nu/d\zeta
, then also

D_{f}(\mu\|\nu)=\int_{\mathfrak{X}}vf\biggl(\frac{u}{v}\biggr)\zeta(dx).

(83)

It follows that
D_{\mathtt{KL}}=D_{f}
for
f(x)=x\log(x).
Similarly to how classical
f
-divergences generalize the KL divergence, maximal
f
-divergences generalize the Belavkin-Staszewski relative entropy. This subclass of quantum
f
-divergences was first introduced in Ref.
30
, and subsequently named and studied by Matsumoto in Ref.
25
.

Definition 27

(Maximal
f
-divergence)
.

Let
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be an operator-convex function with
f(1)=0
. Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
. Then the maximal
f
-divergence is defined as

D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneq\Tr\left[\sigma f\left(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right)\right].

(84)

For non-invertible
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}(\mathfrak{h})
define

D_{f}^{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)\coloneq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}D_{f}^{\max}(\rho+\epsilon I\|\sigma+\epsilon I).

(85)

Theorem 28

(
f
-divergence equivalence)
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathscr{S}_{+}(\mathfrak{h})
. Let
f:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be an operator-convex function with
f(1)=0
. There exists a common (non-orthogonal) basis
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
realizing both
\rho,\sigma
with measures
\mu_{\mathtt{CB}},\nu_{\mathtt{CB}}
such that

\displaystyle D_{f}^{\max}(\rho||\sigma)\overset{(\bigstar)}{=}D_{f}(\mu_{\mathtt{CB}}\|\nu_{\mathtt{CB}})\overset{(\blacktriangle)}{=}D_{f}^{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho||\sigma),

(86)

where

D_{f}^{\mathtt{UNR}}(\rho||\sigma):=\inf_{\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h}))}\left\{D_{f}(\mu\|\nu):\rho=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\,\mu({\rm d}\psi),\,\sigma=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\,\nu({\rm d}\psi)\right\}.

(87)

The proof of the extended theorem is analogous to that of Theorem
13
. The only modifications include the substitution of the expression for maximal
f
-divergences in place of the BS relative entropy to prove (
\bigstar
); and the use of the DPI for classical
f
-divergences, together with the properties of monotonicity, lower semi-continuity, and reduction to the classical
f
-divergence in the commutative case of maximal
f
-divergences, to establish (
\blacktriangle
).

Appendix C
Calculation KL divergence derivative under SSE evolution

For the time derivative of
D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{t}\|\nu_{t})
, where
\mu_{t}
,
\nu_{t}
evolve according to the Fokker-Planck equation of Eq. (
36
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{t}\|\nu_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\left(\partial_{t}\log u_{t}\right)\mu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)+\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\log u_{t}\partial_{t}\mu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)

(88)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\partial_{t}u_{t}\,\nu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)+\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\log u_{t}L^{*}\mu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)=-\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}u_{t}\partial_{t}\nu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)+\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}u_{t}L\log u_{t}\,\nu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\bigl(u_{t}L\log u_{t}-Lu_{t}\bigr)\nu_{t}({\rm d}\psi),\qquad u_{t}:=\frac{{\rm d}\mu_{t}}{{\rm d}\nu_{t}},

where
L
is the infinitesimal generator and used
\partial_{t}u_{t}\nu_{t}+u_{t}\partial_{t}\nu_{t}=L^{*}\mu_{t}
. Noticing that

uL\log u=Lu-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\frac{\bigl(Du[S_{j}\psi]\bigr)^{2}}{u},

we conclude that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}D_{\mathtt{KL}}(\mu_{t}\|\nu_{t})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\bigl(D\log u_{t}(\psi)[S_{j}\psi]\bigr)^{2}\mu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)=-2\sum_{j}\int_{\mathscr{P}(\mathfrak{h})}\bigl(D\sqrt{u_{t}}(\psi)[S_{j}\psi]\bigr)^{2}\nu_{t}({\rm d}\psi)\leq 0.

(89)

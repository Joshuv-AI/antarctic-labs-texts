Error analysis of BDF schemes for the evolutionary incompressible Navier–Stokes equations
Bosco García-Archilla, Volker John, Julia Novo

Abstract
Error bounds for fully discrete schemes for the evolutionary incompressible Navier–Stokes equations are derived in this paper. For the time integration we apply BDF- q q methods, q ≤ 5 q\leq 5 , for which error bounds for q ≥ 3 q\geq 3 cannot be found in the literature. Inf-sup stable mixed finite elements are used as spatial approximation. First, we analyze the standard Galerkin method and second a grad-div stabilized method. The grad-div stabilization allows to prove error bounds with constants independent of inverse powers of the viscosity coefficient. We prove optimal bounds for the velocity and pressure with order ( Δ ​ t ) q (\Delta t)^{q} in time for the BDF- q q scheme and order h k + 1 h^{k+1} for the L 2 ​ ( Ω ) L^{2}(\Omega) error of the velocity in the first case and h k h^{k} in the second case, k k being the degree of the polynomials in finite element velocity space.



Key words
BDF schemes; evolutionary incompressible Navier–Stokes equations; mixed finite elements; inf-sup stable elements; grad-div stabilization, robust error bounds.

1
Introduction

Despite the extensive literature concerning the finite element analysis of the evolutionary incompressible Navier–Stokes equations there are still topics that deserve research.
On the one hand, there are not many papers in which high order methods in time are analyzed. On the other hand, there are also only few papers in which
error bounds with constants independent of inverse powers of the viscosity are proved, so-called robust bounds. The present paper contributes to both aspects.

In the first part of the paper, we prove a priori bounds for fully discrete methods using
the BDF-
q
schemes,
q\leq 5
, for the time integration and inf-sup stable mixed finite elements for the spatial discretization. The constants in the error bounds of this
part are not independent of
\nu^{-1}
. However, the ideas and methodology are useful for the second part of the paper in which robust bounds are obtained adding grad-div stabilization to the spatial discretization.

Error bounds for first and second order methods in time are available in the literature. In
[
12
]
, the error analysis of a fully implicit Euler method combined with mixed finite elements with grad-div stabilization is included, and in
[
15
]
, the authors prove robust bounds for the same spatial discretization and the implicit-explicit BDF-2 method with variable step.
It is known that BDF methods of order higher than 2 are not
A
-stable. In
[
22
]
a semi-implicit BDF-3 method for the two-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations with
H^{1}
initial data is analyzed. In this paper, only the time discretization is considered while the method is continuous in space; besides, the
bounds are not robust. With this error analysis one cannot account for restrictions on the time step required in the fully discretization of the method, which become then important in practical simulations.

The motivation of the present paper comes from the recent reference
[
8
]
. In this paper, the authors analyze BDF-1 – BDF-6 for the transient Stokes problem and both inf-sup stable and non-inf-sup stable finite elements. The main ingredient of the analysis of the temporal discretization in
[
8
]
is the
G
-stability of BDF schemes, see
[
9
,
10
]
, which we apply in the present paper to the Navier–Stokes equations. Applying this technique, one can follow the spirit of the error analysis of lower
order time discretization methods to prove a priori bounds for the fully discrete scheme. The price to pay, compared with first or second order methods, is of course a more complicated error analysis with longer proofs and some technical difficulties.

As stated above, we start with the error analysis of the non-robust case, which is the standard Galerkin method, since several of the technical difficulties appear already in this case. As a consequence of bounding the nonlinear term of the Navier–Stokes equations, we find a (mild) time step restriction for
q\geq 3
that did
not appear in
[
8
]
. Concretely, with
\Delta t
being the time step and
h
the spatial mesh width,
we need to assume
\Delta t\leq Ch^{d/(2q)}
for the error bound of the velocity and
\Delta t\leq Ch^{3/(2q)}
for the error bound of the pressure, where
d\in\{2,3\}
is the spatial dimension and
C
is a generic constant that does not depend on the mesh width.
We prove optimal bounds of order
(\Delta t)^{q}
in time and
h^{k+1}
in space for the
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm of the error in the velocity and
h^{k}
for the
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm of the pressure error.

As discussed in
[
14
]
, in the situation of small viscosity coefficients and coarse grids,
which is of high interest in practice, only robust error bounds provide useful information about
the behavior of a numerical method for smooth solutions; and being able to derive robust error bounds can be considered
as a necessary condition for a good performance of a method in more realistic problems.
To prove a robust bound, we had to deal with additional technical difficulties. To this end, we
derive a new
G
-stability result for first order finite differences for the BDF-q method, see
Lemma
6
. With this new result, we can bound the error of a sum of first order finite differences, which is an extra term appearing in the error analysis of the robust case. This is the
essential extension to the error analysis of the non-robust case.
The techniques from
[
12
]
can be utilized to bound
the nonlinear term using the grad-div stabilization. The restrictions of the time step in the robust case are stronger since we need to assume
\Delta th^{-2}\leq C
. This CFL-type condition was already found for the IMEX-BDF-2 method in
[
15
]
. As known for grad-div stabilized methods, see
[
14
]
, the spatial bound for the
L^{2}(\Omega)
error of the velocity in the robust case behaves as
h^{k}
.

Finally, we want to mention that although the BDF-6 method could be handled following the error analysis
from
[
8
]
, we have decided not to include it. As shown in
[
8
]
, the analysis of this case is more difficult and must be considered separately and the present paper is already quite
technical and long.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the problem and
state some preliminaries and notations. Section
3
presents results concerning the BDF-q methods. Some prerequisites of the error analysis, including the new
G
-stability lemma, are collected
in Section
4
. Section
5
contains the error analysis of the non-robust case and Section
6
the error analysis of the robust case. Finally, some numerical studies are presented in
Section
7
.

The Hilbert space

3
BDF methods

For the temporal integration, we apply a
q
-step backward differentiation formulae (BDF).
This section reviews those properties of BDF methods that are important for our analysis.
We use the notations from
[
8
]
.

For
T>0
we consider a uniform partition of
[0,T]
.
Let us denote the length of the time step
by
\Delta t=T/M
and the time instant by
t_{n}=n\Delta t
,
n=0,\ldots,M
.
Then, for
u^{n}=u(t_{n})
, the BDF approximation of order

q
to the time derivative
u_{t}
is given by

\overline{\partial}_{q}u^{n}=\frac{1}{\Delta t}\sum_{i=0}^{q}\delta_{i}u^{n-i},\ n\geq q,\quad\mbox{with the relation}\quad\delta(\zeta)=\sum_{i=0}^{q}\delta_{i}\zeta^{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{q}\frac{1}{l}(1-\zeta)^{l}

(27)

being used for determining the constants of the method.

We recall the results on
G
-stability for BDF schemes of
[
8
]
that allow to use energy estimates
in the error analysis of the methods. The following lemma is stated and proved in
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
, see also
[
9
,
10
]
.

Lemma 1
(
G
-stability for Hilbert spaces)

Let
\delta(\zeta)=\sum_{i=0}^{q}\delta_{i}\zeta^{i}
and
\mu(\zeta)=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\mu_{j}\zeta^{j}
be polynomials that have no common divisor. Assume that the following condition holds

{\rm Re}\frac{\delta(\zeta)}{\mu(\zeta)}>0\quad\forall\ \zeta\in{\mathbb{C}},|\zeta|<1.

(28)

Let
H
be a Hilbert space with associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{H}
and let

(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}

be a semi-inner product on
H
. Then, there exists a symmetric positive definite
matrix
G=[g_{i,j}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q\times q}
such that for
v_{0},\ldots,v_{q}\in H
the following bound holds

{\rm Re}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{q}\delta_{i}v^{q-i},\sum_{j=0}^{q}\mu_{j}v^{q-j}\right)_{H}\geq\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g_{i,j}\left(v^{i},v^{j}\right)_{H}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g_{i,j}\left(v^{i-1},v^{j-1}\right)_{H}.

By using the multiplier technique developed in
[
20
]
, it is possible to apply the concept
of
G
-stability to the analysis of
A(\alpha)
-stable methods, like BDF for
q\in\{3,4,5\}
. The following
lemma can be found in
[
8
, Lemma 3.7]
.

Lemma 2
(
G
-stability and multiplier technique)

For
q=1,\ldots,5
there exits
0\leq\eta_{q}<1
such that for
\delta(\zeta)=\sum_{l=1}^{q}(1/l)(1-\zeta)^{l}
and
\mu(\zeta)=1-\eta_{q}\zeta
condition (
28
) holds. The values of
\eta_{q}
found
in
[
20
,
2
]
are given by

\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}=0,\ \eta_{3}=0.0769,\ \eta_{4}=0.2878,\ \eta_{5}=0.8097.

(29)

As in
[
8
]
, we define a so-called
G
-norm associated with the semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
on

H
. Given

\boldsymbol{V}^{n}=[v^{n},\ldots,v^{n-q+1}]
with
v^{n-i+1}\in H
,
i=1,\ldots,q
, we define

\left|\boldsymbol{V}^{n}\right|_{G}^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{q}g_{i,j}\left(v^{n-i+1},v^{n-j+1}\right)_{H},

(30)

where
G
is the matrix from Lemma
1
. In fact,
|\cdot|_{G}
is a seminorm on
H^{q}
.
Since
G
is symmetric positive definite, the following inequality holds

\lambda_{\rm min}\left|v^{n}\right|_{H}^{2}\leq\lambda_{\rm min}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\left|v^{n-j+1}\right|_{H}^{2}\leq\left|\boldsymbol{V}^{n}\right|_{G}^{2}\leq\lambda_{\rm max}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\left|v^{n-j+1}\right|_{H}^{2},

(31)

where
|\cdot|_{H}
is
the semi-norm induced by the semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
and

\lambda_{\rm min}
and
\lambda_{\rm max}
denote the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
G
, respectively.

Finally, we introduce a time-discrete norm by

\|v\|_{l^{2}(0,T;H)}=\left(\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{M}\|v\|_{H}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

4
Error analysis of the method. Preliminary results

The fully discrete method reads as follows.
Given
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{0},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{q-1}
, solve for

7
Numerical studies

We performed simulations for the example used in
[
14
]
(
\nu\in\{10^{-4},10^{-6},10^{-8}\},\mu=0.01
) and obtained results
that are of the same form as in Figure 11 of this paper. These results, on the one
hand, show that higher order BDF methods can be used for problems with small viscosity coefficients.
But on the other hand, we like to present here simulations that better illustrate the practical
use of BDF-q methods.
Codes for time integration are usually used in practice with variable step sizes and, in the case of
BDF methods with variable order. To this end, we used a variable-step size, variable-formula BDF code originally written for numerical tests in
[
15
]
. The purpose is to check how the time step restrictions (
134
), (
135
), and (
89
) affect the step size selection.

The developed code follows standard procedures in the numerical integration of ordinary differential equations. For the implementation of the methods, we used a strategy as described in
[
17
, § III.5]
.
At each time level
t_{n}
(after the second one) the local (time discretization) error is estimated with the quantity

{\rm EST}_{n}=\frac{\Delta t_{n}}{t_{n+1}-t_{n-q}}\left\|{\cal U}^{n,q}_{h}\right\|_{0},\quad\mbox{where}\quad{\cal U}^{n,q}_{h}=\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}\left(t_{n+1}-t_{n-i}\right)\boldsymbol{u}_{h}[t_{n+1},\ldots,t_{n-q}],

and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}[t_{n+1},\ldots,t_{n-q}]
is the standard
(q+1)
-th divided difference based on
t_{n+1},\ldots,t_{n-k}
and

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n+1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n-k}
, if the method of order
q
is used at
t_{n}
.
The estimation
{\rm EST}_{n}
is compared with

{\rm TOL}_{n}={\rm TOL}_{r}\left(\max\left\{\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n+1}\right\|_{0},\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n}\right\|_{0}\right\}+0.001\right),

where
{\rm TOL}_{r}
is a given tolerance. If
{\rm EST}_{n}>{\rm TOL}_{n}
, the computed approximation
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n+1}
is not considered to be sufficiently accurate and is rejected. It is then recomputed from
t_{n}
with a new step length given by

\Delta t_{n}^{\rm new}=0.9\Delta t_{n}\sqrt[q+1]{\frac{{\rm TOL}_{n}}{{\rm EST}_{n}}}.

(139)

On the contrary, if
{\rm EST}_{n}\leq{\rm TOL}_{n}
, then,
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n+1}
is accepted and the algorithm proceeds to compute
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{n+2}
with a step length
\Delta t_{n+1}
given by the right-hand side of (
139
).

The algorithm starts with
q=1
and
\Delta t_{0}=\Delta t_{1}=\sqrt{{\rm TOL}_{r}}/100
. At
t_{1}
, the first local error estimation
{\rm EST}_{1}
is computed. If
{\rm EST}_{1}>{\rm TOL}_{1}
the computation is restarted again from
t_{0}
with the step size changed according to (
139
). From
t_{2}
onwards, the estimate corresponding to the BDF-
2
method can be estimated, and when this estimate is smaller than that corresponding to
q=1
, the method switches to
q=2
. From then onwards, estimates for the method of order
q+1
and
q-1
are also computed along with that for the current value of
q
, and the method increases, decreases or maintains the order according to which of the three estimates is the smallest one, but never exceeds a maximum allowed order
q_{\rm max}
. In the results reported below, we observed that once the algorithm reached
q_{\rm max}
, that order was maintained, except when
q_{\rm max}=5
where it fluctuated between different values of
q
. For this reason, only results for
q_{\rm max}\in\{2,3,4\}
will be shown below.

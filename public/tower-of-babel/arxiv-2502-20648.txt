Abstract
We consider the data-aided channel estimation (CE) problem in a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted wireless communication system, where the channel and information symbols are estimated jointly during the CE phase, differently from pure pilot-aided methods. We propose a two-stage semi-blind receiver that jointly estimates the combined channel and the data symbols, followed by channel decoupling. To this end, we derive a new modeling framework whose first stage recasts the received signal to allow for the joint estimation of the combined channel and transmitted symbols. In the second stage, channel decoupling is easily achieved via Khatri-Rao factorization, yielding a refined channel estimate. Our solution yields accurate estimates of the cascaded channel at lower computational complexity. Simulation results reveal a similar performance of the proposed method to that of the competitor while providing a substantially reduced computational cost.

However, obtaining precise channel state information at an affordable computational cost is crucial and challenging in properly optimizing the RIS and the transmit/receive beamforming at the base station (BS) and user terminal (UT). Recently, the work

,

,
18
,

[
11
]

This letter shows that the data-aided CE problem for RIS-assisted communications

can efficiently be achieved with a semi-blind receiver with significantly lower computational complexity. We propose a two-stage approach. In the first stage, by exploiting the received signal structure, we propose an algebraic formulation in which the symbol matrix is alternately estimated as a function of the combined channel instead of the individual channel matrices
[

and vice-versa, according to an iterative linear estimation process. In the second stage, channel decoupling is easily achieved

This is the first work dealing with data-aided combined CE. Simulation results show that the proposed method performs similarly to the competitor with a substantially reduced cost.

III
Two-stage semi-blind (TSB) receiver

The proposed data-aided semi-blind receiver has two stages. In the first stage, we aim to jointly estimate the combined channel (encapsulating the UT-RIS and RIS-BS channels) and the symbol matrix. Let us consider modifying the problem

\min_{{\mathbf{H}},{\mathbf{G}},{\mathbf{X}}}\quad\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\Bigl|\Bigl|{\mathbf{Y}}_{k}-{\mathbf{H}}{\rm diag\!}\left\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{k}\right\}{\mathbf{G}}{\rm diag\!}\left\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}\right\}{\mathbf{X}}\Bigr|\Bigr|_{\rm F}^{2},

(7)

tackled in
[
11
]
. We rewrite (
6
) by applying the
\mathrm{vec}\{\cdot\}
operator along with property (
2
) to yield

\mathit{\boldsymbol{y}}_{k}=\bigl({\mathbf{X}}^{{\rm T}}{\rm diag\!}\left\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}\right\}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{M}\bigr)\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathit{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{k}+\mathit{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k},

(8)

where
\mathit{\boldsymbol{y}}_{k}\doteq{\rm vec}\left\{{\mathbf{Y}}_{k}\right\}\in\mathbb{C}^{TM\times 1}
,
\mathit{\boldsymbol{v}}_{k}\doteq{\rm vec}\left\{{\mathbf{V}}_{k}\right\}\in\mathbb{C}^{TM\times 1}
, while
\boldsymbol{\Theta}\doteq{\mathbf{G}}^{{\rm T}}\diamond{\mathbf{H}}\in\mathbb{C}^{LM\times N}
is the Khatri-Rao structured combined channel matrix. Then, we propose recast the optimization problem (
7
) to

\min_{\boldsymbol{\Theta},{\mathbf{X}}}\quad\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\Bigl|\Bigl|\mathit{\boldsymbol{y}}_{k}-\bigl({\mathbf{X}}^{{\rm T}}{\rm diag\!}\left\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}\right\}\otimes{\mathbf{I}}_{M}\bigr)\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathit{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{k}\Bigr|\Bigr|_{\rm F}^{2}.

(9)

By manipulating the corresponding matrix unfoldings, we can obtain an interesting algebraic formulation in which the symbol matrix is alternately estimated as a function of the combined channel and vice-versa, according to an iterative linear estimation process. Unlike the semi-blind TALS receiver
[
11
]
that seeks to estimate three quantities (
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}
,
\hat{{\mathbf{G}}}
, and
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}

[
24
]
, the proposed problem (

\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}
and
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}
, which can be accomplished fastly, while exhibiting reduced computational cost. Explicit expressions for estimating the combined channel and the symbol matrix can be obtained after some algebraic manipulations done in Subsections
III-A

\min_{{\mathbf{H}},{\mathbf{G}}}\left\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}-{\mathbf{G}}^{{\rm T}}\diamond{\mathbf{H}}\right\|_{\rm F}^{2}.

III-C

Khatri-Rao factorization stage

The
n
-th column of
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
, defined as

, is given by
\mathit{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}=\mathit{\boldsymbol{g}}_{n}\otimes\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{n}={\rm vec}\left\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{n}\mathit{\boldsymbol{g}}_{n}^{{\rm T}}\right\}
, where
\mathit{\boldsymbol{g}}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times 1}
and
\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}
are the
n
-th column of
{\mathbf{G}}^{{\rm T}}
and
{\mathbf{H}}
, respectively
[
25
]
. The estimates of the individual channels are found as

[\hat{{\mathbf{G}}},\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}]=\argmin_{\mathit{\boldsymbol{g}}_{n},\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{n}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\left\|\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{n}-\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}_{n}\mathit{\boldsymbol{g}}_{n}^{{\rm T}}\right\|_{\rm F}^{2},

(26)

where
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{n}=\mathrm{unvec}_{M\times L}\{\mathit{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}\!\}
is a rank-one matrix. The estimates of
\hat{{\mathbf{G}}}
and
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}
are found through

independent rank-one approximation problems, wherein
\hat{\mathit{\boldsymbol{h}}}_{n}
and
\hat{\mathit{\boldsymbol{g}}}_{n}
are obtained, respectively, from the dominant left and right singular vectors of
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{n}
after a truncated singular value decomposition (SVD). After the KRF,

and
\hat{{\mathbf{H}}}
are used to reconstruct

. This procedure corresponds to the KRF stage in Algorithm

III-D

Computational complexity

The computational complexity of the TSB receiver is dominated by the generalized left-inverses of steps 3 and 4 of Algorithm
1
, which corresponds to the BALS stage, followed by the complexity associated with the computation of
N
rank-one SVDs, corresponding to the KRF stage. Considering a cost
\mathcal{O}(IJ^{2})
to calculate the pseudo-inverse of a rank-
J
matrix
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{C}^{I\times J}
, the complexity of (
15
) and (
25
) are, respectively,
\mathcal{O}(KTL^{2}N^{2})
and
\mathcal{O}(KML^{2})
. This means that each iteration of the BALS stage requires
\mathcal{O}(KL^{2}(TN^{2}+M))
. The complexity of the KRF stage involves computing the SVD of each rank-1 matrix
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{n}
, which implies
\mathcal{O}(ML)
. By considering
N
parallel SVDs, the total cost of the KRF is
\mathcal{O}(NML)
.

Stage 1: Bilinear Alternating Least-Squares (BALS)

1. Set
i=0
and initialize
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}_{(i=0)}
randomly;

2.
i=i+1
;

3. Get
\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{(i)}\!=\!\mathrm{unvec}_{LM\times N}\Bigl\{{\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{X}}_{(i-1)})^{\dagger}\mathrm{vec}\bigl\{\left[\mathcal{Y}\right]_{(3)}^{{\rm T}}\bigr\}\Bigr\}
;

4. Get
\hat{{\mathbf{X}}}_{(i)}={\mathbf{E}}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{(i)})^{\dagger}\left[\mathcal{Y}\right]_{(2)}^{{\rm T}}
;

VI
Conclusion

We have derived a novel tensor-based data-aided semi-blind (TSB) receiver for RIS-assisted MIMO communications. It has significantly less computational cost than the competing data-aided receiver while offering similar channel estimation accuracy using actual data symbols. The proposed TSB receiver is an attractive solution to low-complexity data-aided channel estimation for RIS-assisted wireless communications.

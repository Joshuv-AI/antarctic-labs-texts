Stretching the Limit: Investigating the Nipple to Inframammary Fold Distance in Breast Reductions
Authors: Justin Wisely, Kersten Gant, Shoshana Ambani
Published: 2026

Objective: Optimal length of inframammary fold distance is debated in breast reduction research with regards to nipple areolar complex survival. Current recommendations are to perform a free nipple graft for breast reductions with nipple to inframammary fold distance > 15 cm. This study aimed to evaluate both distance and pedicle type as a variable for nipple necrosis.

Methods: We performed a retrospective study of 155 patients who underwent breast reductions between 2020-2023 at a single institution. We evaluated the incidence of nipple necrosis with regards to nipple to internal mammary fold distance (N-IMF) > 15 cm and > 20 cm, volume of breast reduction, and the type of pedicle used for reduction. Analysis was performed using chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test to assess for significance (p=0.05).

Results: In our population, 107 patients had N-IMF > 15 cm with 36 of these patients having N-IMF > 20 cm. Nipple necrosis occurred in 11 patients total (7.1%) with 81.8% of instances occurring within the first 2 weeks postoperatively. There was significant difference between groups N-IMF >15 cm compared to > 20 cm with necrosis occurring in 16.7% of patients > 20 cm compared to 4.2% (p = 0.02). A trend toward significance (p = 0.06) was seen when further subdividing necrosis by type of pedicle used for breast reductions > 15 cm.

Conclusion: Our results challenge the current teachings of performing a free nipple graft for N-IMF > 15 cm although does not support extending past N-IMF 20 cm. The benefits of having a native nipple with regards to aesthetics, maintained sensation, and patient satisfaction outweigh the risks for nipple necrosis. Preservation of the nipple areolar complex is possible in large breast reductions with similar incidence of nipple necrosis compared to smaller reductions. Our results trended toward significance based upon pedicle type but would need an increase in power to determine significance. Further investigation should be undertaken regarding pedicle type being associated with increased risk of nipple necrosis for these larger reductions.

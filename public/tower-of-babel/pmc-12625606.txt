Introduction

Infertility is a significant global health issue, affecting approximately one in six couples worldwide, according to the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2023 [1]. This striking prevalence not only highlights the extensive scale of the challenge but also underscores the growing imperative for advanced and accessible reproductive healthcare. Assisted reproductive technology (ART) has provided hope to countless couples experiencing infertility [2].Among various ART methods, single vitrified-warmed blastocyst transfer (SVBT) is gaining popularity, as it improves embryo selection and reduces the risks associated with multiple pregnancies and related complications [3, 4].Blastocysts undergo a more rigorous natural selection process compared to cleavage-stage embryos, effectively eliminating those with lower developmental potential [5, 6]. Despite these advantages, live birth rates following SVBT remain inconsistent, and predicting successful pregnancy outcomes continues to pose a significant clinical challenge [2]. Accurate prediction of live birth outcomes is crucial for improving treatment strategies and providing effective patient counseling, particularly during the first trimester [7]. Therefore, developing more precise and customized prediction models for live birth outcomes in early pregnancy is essential.

Radiomics, which involves the extraction and analysis of quantitative features from medical imaging, offers a promising approach for predicting clinical outcomes [8]. In ART, ultrasound imaging, a widely used and non-invasive modality, holds great promise for predicting early pregnancy outcomes. In particular, ultrasound at the sixth week of pregnancy shows the gestational sac clearly, along with the yolk sac and fetal heartbeat, signifying successful embryo implantation. By the eighth week, the embryo continues to develop within the uterus, and the development of the gestational sac directly reflects the health of the pregnancy. Abnormalities in the development of the gestational sac at this stage may indicate potential complications such as miscarriage. These ultrasound images provide crucial early indicators for clinical intervention. The changes observed in the gestational sac between the sixth and eighth weeks offer dynamic imaging data, enabling more comprehensive and accurate evaluation of pregnancy progress. By comparing imaging features from different time points, the delta radiomics approach enhances predictive accuracy and provides a more robust model for early pregnancy outcomes.

Combining radiomic features from ultrasound images with clinical data may improve the accuracy of pregnancy outcome predictions. One promising technique, delta radiomics, integrates features from ultrasound images taken at different times, potentially enhancing predictive models [9]. Delta radiomics technology has been explored for predicting radiation-induced esophagitis, pneumonitis, and other diseases, and has demonstrated promising predictive value [10–13].By integrating imaging data from various time points, the delta radiomics approach may provide a comprehensive understanding of the early pregnancy course, circumventing the limitations of static, single-time-point prediction models.

However, to date, there has been no published research exploring the application of delta ultrasound radiomics in the field of ART. Therefore, our study aims to develop and validate one robust model for predicting live birth outcomes in women undergoing SVBT based on delta radiomics model. The model was constructed by integrating ultrasound-derived radiomic features from sixth week and eighth week gestational scans, and clinical features such as maternal age, fertilization method, and endometrial preparation protocols. To improve the interpretability of the delta radiomic model, Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) were used to weight feature contributions [14]. The findings of our study are intended to offer a valuable tool for clinicians to improve patient counseling and decision-making in ART.

This approach hypothesizes that by integrating imaging features from sixth week and eighth week gestational scans, delta radiomics could offer enhanced predictive power compared to traditional methods, and the application of SHAP is expected to improve the model’s transparency and interpretability, thereby further augmenting the predictive performance of delta radiomics in forecasting early pregnancy outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

Women who received SVBT treatment at the first affiliated hospital of Guangxi Medical University in Nanning, China, between January 2019 and December 2022 were examined in this retrospective cohort study. We utilized ultrasound-derived radiomic features and clinical variables, with a primary focus on predicting live birth outcomes. The cohort design was chosen due to the availability of retrospective clinical data, which enabled us to evaluate the predictive potential of delta radiomics in early pregnancy without the need for intervention or randomization, as would be required in a clinical trial. Eligible participants were those with confirmed intrauterine pregnancies, as determined by ultrasound examinations conducted at sixth and eighth weeks of gestation (the fourth and sixth weeks following transplantation). The exclusion criteria included: [1] biochemical pregnancy loss (defined as a positive β-HCG result without ultrasound confirmation of the gestational sac) [2], ectopic pregnancy or heterotopic pregnancy [3], multiple pregnancies [4], empty gestational sac [5], yolk sac without an embryo [6], absence of fetal heart activity [7], missing or low-quality ultrasound data for pregnancies at sixth or eighth weeks.The analysis included 528 cases in total that satisfied the inclusion criteria. The first affiliated hospital of Guangxi Medical University Ethics Committee gave its approval to the study on July 14, 2025 (approval number: 2025-E0580). The Ethics Committee waived the requirement for informed consent because the study was retrospective in nature and used early pregnancy ultrasound images and archived medical records. To preserve privacy, all patient data was anonymized.

Embryo culture and transfer

Each patient’s ovarian stimulation protocol was individualized based on clinical factors such as age, BMI, baseline FSH levels, and antral follicle count. Ovulation was triggered with human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) when at least one follicle reached a diameter of 18 mm, followed by oocyte retrieval 36 hours later under ultrasound guidance. The insemination method—either conventional in vitro fertilization (IVF) or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI)—was selected according to semen quality. Following fertilization, embryos were cultured continuously in a single-step medium under oil at 37 °C, in an incubator environment containing 5% oxygen, 6% carbon dioxide, and nitrogen as the balancing gas. Blastocyst quality was assessed using the Gardner scoring system. Fully expanded blastocysts were laser-shrunk prior to vitrification using commercial vitrification kits (KITAZATO), loaded onto cryotops on day 5–6 post-insemination, and stored in liquid nitrogen. Thawing was performed using warming kits (KITAZATO) once the endometrial lining reached a sufficient thickness, and embryo viability was assessed based on re-expansion two hours post-warming. For frozen embryo transfer (FET), endometrial preparation followed one of four protocols—modified natural cycle (mNC), mild stimulation cycle (MS), hormone replacement therapy (HRT), or HRT combined with a GnRH agonist (GnRHa-HRT)—selected according to the patient’s individual clinical profile. The endometrial preparation protocol was detailed in our prior publication (see ‘Endometrial Preparation and Blastocyst Transfer’ section) [15]. All blastocyst transfers were performed under abdominal ultrasound guidance to ensure accurate placement.

Clinical data and analysis

The primary clinical outcome of this study was live birth, defined as the delivery of a single infant at ≥ 28 weeks of gestation. Clinical data were extracted from the electronic medical records of participating centers to ensure accuracy. All key variables were independently verified by dual clinical data managers, including patient demographics and clinical data, which were derived from the medical records of patients diagnosed with a singleton intrauterine pregnancy following SVBT and ultrasound assessment at 4 weeks post-procedure. The retrospective analysis utilized clinical data reflecting known significant variables that may influence fetal development and pregnancy outcomes. The inclusion criteria for variables were based on established clinical knowledge and previous studies in the field of ART, with particular attention to factors known to influence pregnancy outcomes. These variables included maternal age, fertilization method (IVF or ICSI), duration of infertility, body mass index (BMI), embryonic development stage (assessed using the Gardner scoring system [16], quality grades of the inner cell mass (ICM) and trophectoderm (TE) [17], blastocyst formation time, endometrial thickness (EMT), and endometrial preparation protocols (including modified natural cycle, mild stimulation cycle, hormone replacement therapy, and GnRH agonist combined with HRT). These clinical data, which may significantly impact pregnancy outcomes, were carefully considered during the data analysis process.

Univariate Analysis: Clinical variables, including maternal age, fertilization method (IVF or ICSI), and endometrial thickness, were assessed using Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables and Chi-square tests for categorical variables. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and variables with a p-value < 0.05 were included in the subsequent multivariate analysis.

Multivariate Analysis: In the multivariate analysis, we used logistic regression to evaluate the independent effects of the selected variables on live birth outcomes. The final set of variables included those with a p-value < 0.05 in the univariate analysi. The odds ratios (OR) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) were computed for each variable, and the most significant variables, including endometrial thickness, were combined with the delta radiomics features to construct the final predictive model.

Ultrasonography protocol

All ultrasound examinations at sixth and eighth weeks of gestation were performed by experienced sonographers following standardized protocols. The TK-SNAP software (version 3.8.0) was used to manually delineate the regions of interest (ROIs) and annotate the gestational sac. For the 528 participants, the initial ROI annotations were performed by Sonographer A. To rigorously assess intra- and inter-observer consistency of the extracted features and ensure methodological reproducibility, a random subset of 200 cases was re-annotated after a two-week interval. This re-analysis was independently conducted by Sonographer A (for intra-observer assessment) and Sonographer B (for inter-observer assessment), strictly adhering to the original annotation criteria. The aim of this process was to validate the robustness of feature selection and minimize variability in ultrasound-derived measurements.

Radiomic feature extraction and fusion

The open-source Python package PyRadiomics on the OnekeyAI platform was used to extract radiomic features of the gestational sac from ultrasound images taken at 6 and 8 weeks of gestation. In addition to morphological features, the extracted features comprised first-order statistical features and those obtained from multiple matrices, including the Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), Gray Level Dependence Matrix (GLDM), Gray Level Run Length Matrix (GLRLM), Gray Level Size Zone Matrix (GLSZM), and Neighboring Gray Tone Difference Matrix (NGTDM). Following extraction, concatenation was used to perform feature fusion, which combined the features of the 6-week with 8-week datasets. This method preserved the independent data from each time point while producing a more complete and trustworthy representation of features for additional analysis and modeling.

Feature selection

Prior to statistical analysis and feature selection using the Mann-Whitney U test, radiomic features were first standardized using Z-score normalization. Only features with a p-value less than 0.05 were kept. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was also used to evaluate inter-feature correlations for highly redundant features. Only one feature per pair of highly correlated features remained after features with a correlation coefficient greater than 0.9 were removed. The feature set was then optimized using logistic regression with the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO). 5-fold cross-validation was used to determine the optimal regularization parameter, λ, which minimized binomial deviance. The selected features were used to construct a machine learning model, and the weighted linear combination of the selected features was used to calculate the radiomic score for each patient. Models created using each modality separately as well as a combined model that included all modalities had their classification performance assessed.The selected features were used to construct a machine learning model, and the features were then combined in a weighted linear fashion to determine each patient’s radiomic score.The classification performance of models developed using each modality independently and a combined model incorporating all modalities was evaluated. Signature building

Delta Radiomics Signature Following a comprehensive feature selection procedure using LASSO, a radiomics-based live birth prediction model was developed using machine learning algorithms like Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Logistic Regression. Comparative analysis was used to evaluate each model’s performance. Integrating features from the sixth and eighth weeks to examine the advantages of Delta temporal fusion further illustrated the advantages of combining imaging features from different time intervals to increase predictive accuracy.

Ultrasound Radiomics Signatures at Sixth and Eighth Weeks: Using a methodology akin to Delta radiomics, features were taken from ultrasound pictures taken at sixth and eighth weeks . The sixth and eighth week radiomics signatures were derived by selecting and reducing the dimensionality of these features. The efficacy of the Delta approach in boosting model robustness and predictive power was confirmed by contrasting these signatures with the Delta model.

Metrics

To assess our machine learning model’s diagnostic performance in the test population and ascertain its discriminatory power, we developed Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves. The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test was used to verify the model’s reliability after it had been calibrated using calibration curves. Additionally, Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) was used to assess the clinical utility of our predictive models, yielding valuable insights regarding their potential advantages in a clinical context.The nomogram was developed based on the relationship between these variables and the live birth outcome.

Model interpretation

To enhance the interpretability of the delta radiomics model, feature contributions were analyzed using SHAP method. Based on cooperative game theory, SHAP addresses the interpretability issues with traditional “black-box” models and improves the decision-making process by quantifying the marginal contribution of each feature to the model’s predictions.

To assess the global impact of individual features on the overall live birth predictions, a Beeswarm plot was made, which displays the distribution of SHAP values for each feature across all samples [18]. For a more in-depth analysis of the prediction process for individual samples, a Waterfall plot was developed [19], which illustrates how the contributions of different features progressively impact the final live birth prediction outcome.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to determine whether the clinical features were normal. Depending on their distributional characteristics, continuous variables were analyzed using either the Mann-Whitney U test or the t-test. Chi-square (χ²) tests were used to assess categorical variables. Notably, p-values greater than 0.05 between cohorts indicated no significant differences, confirming an unbiased group division.All data analyses were performed on the OnekeyAI platform (version 4.9.1) using Python 3.7.12. Statistical analyses were conducted using Statsmodels (version 0.13.2). Radiomics feature extraction was carried out with PyRadiomics (version 3.0.1). Machine learning models, including the Support Vector Machine (SVM), were implemented using Scikit-learn (version 1.0.2).

With an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.663 in the testing group, the LR classifier outperformed the other models in terms of prediction, as seen in Fig. 3C.

Discussion

Delta radiomics and combined model: enhancing predictive accuracy in ART

Research on ultrasound radiomics in reproductive medicine has primarily focused on the endometrium. One study has developed models combining endometrial ultrasound radiomics with clinical features to predict clinical pregnancy outcomes after FET [20]. However, there is no research in ultrasound radiomics that specifically addresses changes in the gestational sac.Our study, however, focuses on the changes in the gestational sac during the sixth and eighth weeks of pregnancy.

A key finding of this study is that the predictive accuracy of the delta radiomics model improves when combining ultrasound features from both the sixth and eighth weeks of gestation with clinical data. By incorporating both temporal data and clinical information, the model can more comprehensively reflect the progression of pregnancy, which is crucial for assessing the likelihood of live birth outcomes. Single-time-point models may fail to capture the dynamic changes that occur during early pregnancy. Early developmental ultrasound images of the gestational sac can reveal critical details about pregnancy viability, which evolve over time. The delta radiomics model, by integrating features from both the sixth and eighth weeks along with clinical data, provides a more comprehensive and accurate assessment of the pregnancy’s status.

In our study, in the training cohort, both the delta radiomics model and the Combined model outperform the single models based solely on ultrasound features from either the sixth or eighth week, as well as clinical characteristics. In the test cohort, however, the Combined model demonstrates superior performance.

Model interpretability: the role of SHAP

One major issue with machine learning models is their lack of interpretability, even though they have shown good accuracy in predicting clinical outcomes, especially when based on radiomic features [21]. Clinicians are often reluctant to rely on “black-box” models [22]. To mitigate this issue, this study incorporates SHAP values to elucidate the contributions of individual features within the delta radiomics model. SHAP, which has its roots in cooperative game theory, measures the marginal influence of each feature on the model’s predictions, offering important new information about how each feature affects the outcome.

The application of SHAP in this study has enabled a deeper understanding of the specific ultrasound features that most significantly influence the prediction of live birth outcomes.The Beeswarm plot produced in the SHAP analysis underscores the significance of 25 ultrasound features selected from both the sixth and eighth weeks of pregnancy.

The use of a Waterfall plot for a representative case, in addition to the Beeswarm plot, helps to further clarify the prediction process on a personal level by showing how the 25 ultrasound features gradually contribute to the final prediction. Clinicians can better grasp the reasoning behind the predictions thanks to the model’s increased transparency brought about by the ability to visually interpret each feature’s function. In clinical settings, where openness can increase model trust and guide better decision-making, interpretability is essential.

The integration of SHAP into the radiomics-based model holds several important implications for clinical practice. First, it significantly enhances the model’s transparency, a critical factor in ensuring clinician acceptance. Second, it facilitates the identification of key features that could serve as targets for potential interventions, thereby offering actionable insights to improve clinical outcomes.

Clinical implications

In ART, the ability to predict live birth outcomes is essential. The model developed in this study, which combines clinical features and delta radiomic features, may increase the precision of these predictions. The integration of radiomic features obtained from ultrasound, such as those from both sixth-week and eighth-week gestational scans, offers a more comprehensive and dynamic understanding of early pregnancy development. If this approach aids in identifying patients who are more likely to have live birth outcomes, clinicians may be able to intervene more effectively.

The predictions are more transparent when SHAP is used for model interpretation, which helps clinicians comprehend and have confidence in the findings. This is important when it comes to ART, since treatment choices are frequently complicated and call for careful evaluation of a number of variables.

Limitations and future directions

Although the study’s findings are encouraging, it is important to recognize a number of limitations. First, because the study was conducted retrospectively, the results must first be confirmed in prospective cohorts before being widely applied in clinical practice. Second, the study only used data from one hospital, which might have limited how broadly the findings can be applied. Replicating these results across several facilities with various patient demographics should be the main goal of future studies.Third, While the clinical consistency between the training and testing cohorts has minimized the potential impact of sample size imbalance on the results, we did not apply specific statistical techniques such as weighting, stratification, or resampling to address this issue. Sample size imbalance may still contribute to selective bias, and as such, we acknowledge the need for future studies with larger, more balanced cohorts to further validate the stability and generalizability of the findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates the potential of delta radiomics and combined model, combined with SHAP-based interpretability, to enhance the prediction of live birth outcomes in assisted reproductive technology (ART). By integrating ultrasound-derived features from sixth week and eighth week along with clinical data, the delta radiomics model offers a more comprehensive and dynamic approach to early pregnancy prediction. The incorporation of SHAP further enhances the model’s transparency, making it a valuable tool for clinicians in guiding treatment decisions and patient counseling. Future research should focus on validating these findings in larger, multi-center cohorts and exploring the integration of other imaging modalities to further improve predictive accuracy.

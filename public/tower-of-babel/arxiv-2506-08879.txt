Radiative corrections to the nuclear size and polarizability effects in atomic systems
Krzysztof Pachucki

Abstract
We perform a complete calculation of α ​ ( Z ​ α ) 5 ​ m \alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{5}\,m radiative corrections to the finite nuclear size, the recoil finite size and the nuclear polarizability effects in atomic systems.



I
Introduction

With the increased precision of atomic isotope shift measurements
amsterdam:25

and muonic atom determinations of the nuclear charge radii
crema:25
,
knowledge of nuclear recoil, finite nuclear size (fns), and polarizability effects became of great interest.
The accurate calculation of these effects is important for the comparison of
electronic
and muonic atom isotope shift,
from which one obtains the difference in the mean square charge radii between isotopes
pachucki:24a
.
This difference is an important test of the low-energy interactions.
For example, the second-order recoil correction
(Z\,\alpha)^{6}\,m^{3}/M^{2}

was recently found
qi:25
;
pachucki:24b
to be significant for the
3
He-
4
He isotope shift,
due to the hyperfine mixing, and thus resolved a discrepancy between the
electronic

and muonic determination of the mean square nuclear charge radii difference.

The nonradiative fns corrections including the leading recoil
(Z\,\alpha)^{6}\,m^{2}/M
have been the subject of recent works

pachucki:23
;
yerokhin:23
;
pachucki:25a
. The exact result without expansion in
Z\,\alpha
in the nonrecoil limit
is a matter of solving the Dirac equation with extended size nucleus. It is nevertheless valuable to perform
the
Z\,\alpha
expansion, because the explicit dependence on the charge distribution can be obtained.
For example, at the order of
(Z\,\alpha)^{6}\,m
the finite size effect includes the
r^{2}_{C}\,\ln(m\,r_{C})
term.
Leading recoil corrections can also be calculated exactly
using the recently derived formula for the recoil correction with extended size nuclei
pachucki:23
;
yerokhin:23
,
while the
Z\,\alpha
expansion leads to the
r_{C}^{2}\,\ln(m\,r_{C})
term at
(Z\,\alpha)^{5}\,m^{2}/M
and a linear
r_{C}
term at
(Z\,\alpha)^{6}\,m^{2}/M
orders.

In contrast to the above, the exact calculation of radiative finite size corrections is much more difficult, because it involves
the electron self-energy. They have been calculated numerically in Ref.
yerokhin:11
,
but it is difficult to obtain the functional dependence on the nuclear charge distribution in this way.
Therefore, they have been calculated analytically in the leading order
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{5}
in Refs.
pachucki:93a
;
eides:01
;
sgk:18

and at some higher orders
yerokhin:11
;
sgk:18
.
It is even more difficult to obtain the leading radiative recoil correction to the finite size effect.
The exact formulas have recently been derived using a formulation of
Heavy Particle QED
(HPQED)
pachucki:24c
,
but they have not yet been implemented numerically.
In fact, the radiative recoil correction is the main source of uncertainty in hydrogen energy levels
codata:22
,
after the improved numerical calculation of the two-loop electron self-energy has been performed
yerokhin:24
.

In this work, we collect all known results for radiative finite size corrections at leading orders,
present their comprehensive derivation, and in particular,
obtain finite nuclear size effects of order
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{5}\,m
and
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{5}\,m^{2}/M
,
which are important for determination of the nuclear charge radii from the
electronic
and muonic atomic spectroscopy.
In addition we perform the calculation of the electron self-energy correction
to the nuclear polarizability effect, which is currently the main source of uncertainty in H-D isotope shift
pachucki:18
.

II
Expansion of the finite nuclear size effect in powers of
\alpha

Let us denote by
E_{\rm fns}
the shift in the binding energy of a hydrogenic system due to the finite nuclear size (fns).
For a light atom we can perform the expansion of
E_{\rm fns}
in the small nuclear charge
Z\,\alpha
and in
\alpha
(together)

\displaystyle E_{\rm fns}=E^{(4)}_{\rm fns}+E^{(5)}_{\rm fns}+E^{(6)}_{\rm fns}+\ldots,

(1)

where the superscript indicates the order in
\alpha
.
The leading-order nuclear contribution is of order
(Z\alpha)^{4}
and is given by a simple formula,

E^{(4)}_{\rm fns}=\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,Z\alpha\,\phi^{2}(0)\,r_{C}^{2}\,,

(2)

where
\phi(0)
is the nonrelativistic wave function of the electron at the position of nucleus,

r_{C}
is the root-mean-square charge radius of the nucleus

\displaystyle r_{C}^{2}=\int d^{3}r\,r^{2}\,\rho(\vec{r}),

(3)

and
\rho(\vec{r})
is the nuclear charge distribution. Eq. (
2
) includes the exact dependence on
the finite nuclear mass
M

through
\phi^{2}(0)=\mu^{3}\,(Z\alpha)^{3}/(\pi n^{3})
, where
\mu=mM/(m+M)
.
The mass dependence of higher-order corrections is much more complicated,
and it is convenient to perform an additional expansion in the mass ratio, namely

\displaystyle E^{(n)}_{\mathrm{fns}}=E^{(n,0)}_{\mathrm{fns}}+E^{(n,1)}_{\mathrm{fns}}+\ldots,

(4)

where the second superscript denotes a power in the
m/M
expansion coefficient.
The leading expansion terms are known, but for the clarity of presentation
we will repeat their derivation here and start from

E^{(5,0)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
.

III
(Z\,\alpha)^{5}
Nonrecoil FNS

The next to leading order
E^{(5,0)}_{\rm fns}
finite size correction in the nonrecoil limit,
is given by the two-photon exchange amplitude
T^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle E^{(5,0)}_{\mathrm{fs}}=

\displaystyle\ \phi^{2}(0)\,\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,T^{00}(\vec{q}^{\,2})\,V^{2}(\vec{q}^{\,2}),

(5)

where

\displaystyle V(\vec{q}^{\,2})=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha}{\vec{q}^{\,2}}\,\rho(\vec{q}^{\,2}),

(6)

and
\rho(\vec{q}^{\,2})
is the Fourier transform of
\rho(r)
. We will later identify
\rho(-q^{2})
with
the nuclear charge form factor as a function of the four-momentum square
q^{2}
and at the same time
assume that
\rho
has a proper analytical behavior as a function on the complex plane.
The two-photon exchange amplitude
T^{\mu\nu}
is

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}(p)=

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{Tr}\biggl[\gamma^{\mu}\,\frac{1}{\not\!p-m}\gamma^{\nu}\,\frac{\gamma^{0}+I}{4}\biggr],

(7)

with
p=t+q
,
t\cdot q=0
,
t=(m,\vec{0})
, and
m
is the lepton (electron or muon) mass.
Performing the trace

\displaystyle T^{00}(\vec{q}^{\,2})=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{2\,m}{\vec{q}^{\,2}}\,,

(8)

E^{(5,0)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
becomes

\displaystyle E^{(5,0)}_{\mathrm{fns}}=

\displaystyle\ -2\,m\,(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\rho^{2}(\vec{q}^{\,2})}{\vec{q}^{\;6}}\,.

(9)

E^{(5,0)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
requires the subtraction of infrared divergent terms,
which correspond to the point nucleus and the leading
E^{(4)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
term, thus

\displaystyle E^{(5,0)}_{\mathrm{fns}}=

\displaystyle\ -m\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,\phi^{2}(0)\!\!\int\!dq\,\frac{16}{q^{4}}\,\big[\rho^{2}(q^{2})-1-2\,\rho^{\prime}(0)\,q^{2}\big].

(10)

The
q
-integral can be expressed as

\displaystyle\int dq\,\frac{16}{q^{4}}\,\big[\rho^{2}(q^{2})-1-2\,\rho^{\prime}(0)\,q^{2}\big]=r^{3}_{F}\,\frac{\pi}{3},

(11)

where
r_{F}
is defined by
friar:79

\displaystyle r^{3}_{F}=\int d^{3}r_{1}\,d^{3}r_{2}\,\rho(\vec{r}_{1})\,\rho(\vec{r}_{2})\,|\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}|^{3},

(12)

thus

\displaystyle E^{(5,0)}_{\mathrm{fns}}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\pi}{3}(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,m\,r^{3}_{F},

(13)

in agreement with Ref.
friar:79
.
This formula is exact only for the nucleus, which is described by the elastic charge form factor
\rho
.
However, the elastic approximation is not always valid, in particular for large momenta
q
. In fact,
values of the electron momenta in Eq. (
11
) are of order of the inverse of the nuclear size.
For such high momenta, the electron sees individual nucleons instead of the nucleus as a whole.
It is a small effect for
electronic
atoms, because this whole correction is small, namely
O(Z\,\alpha\,m\,r_{C})

in comparison to the leading finite size
E^{(4)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
. But this is not the case of muonic atoms,
where
m\,r_{C}
is of order 1 and we find significant inelastic effects
pachucki:24a
.
In general, when large exchange momenta are involved, one may expect a significant inelastic contribution.
The same effect appears at higher-order corrections; a part of fns comes from the low momenta exchange
where the elastic approximation works as for
E^{(4)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
and a part from high momenta
where elastic approximation does not work. For example, the so-called three-photon exchange correction

E^{(6)}_{\rm nucl}
comes from many scales of exchanged momenta,
and presently only the elastic part
E^{(6)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
is known.
In this work we assume the elastic approximation, which is usually assumed as a good starting point,
and only in Sec.
VIII
we consider radiative corrections
to the electric dipole nuclear polarizability effect in
electronic
atoms.

IV
(Z\,\alpha)^{5}
Recoil FNS

In contrast to nonrecoil correction from the previous section, the radiative recoil fns corrections
have not yet been extensively studied in the literature.
Let us first consider the nonradiative recoil fns correction
E^{(5,1)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
and follow the derivation from Ref.
pachucki:23
.
According to HPQED the leading recoil correction
shabaev:85
;
shabaev:88
;
pachucki:95a
;
pachucki:24c

in hydrogenic systems, which is exact in
Z\,\alpha
, is of the form

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rec}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{M}\int_{s}\frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\,\langle\phi|D_{T}^{j}(\omega)\,G(E_{D}+\omega)\,D_{T}^{j}(\omega)|\phi\rangle\,,

(14)

where
G(E)=\nicefrac{{1}}{{(E-H_{D})}}
and

\displaystyle D_{T}^{j}(\omega,\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ -4\pi Z\alpha\,\alpha^{i}\,G_{T}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{r})\,,

(15)

\displaystyle G_{T}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{q})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\rho(-q^{2})}{q^{2}}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-\frac{q^{i}\,q^{j}}{\omega^{2}}\biggr)\,,

(16)

and where
\omega=q^{0}
,
q^{2}=\omega^{2}-\vec{q}^{\,2}
, and
the subscript
s
in the integration denotes a symmetric integration around the pole at
\omega=0
along the Feynman
or Wick rotated contour. Because the terms with
1/\omega
singularity can be separated from terms involving branch cuts
starting at
\omega=0
, this symmetric integration can safely be implemented.

The
(Z\,\alpha)^{5}
fns recoil beyond the reduced mass scaling of
\phi^{2}(0)
in Eq. (
13
) is
given by the two-photon exchange amplitude

\displaystyle E^{(5,1)}_{\rm fns}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{M}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{2}\!\int_{s}\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\,\pi)^{4}}

\displaystyle\times G_{T}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{q})\,G_{T}^{ik}(\omega,\vec{q})\,T^{jk},

(17)

where
T^{ij}
is defined in Eq. (
7
).
After contracting Cartesian indices,
E^{(5,1)}_{\rm fns}
becomes

\displaystyle E^{(5,1)}_{\rm fns}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{M}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{2}\!\int_{s}\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\,\pi)^{4}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\omega}{q^{2}+2\,m\,\omega}\,\biggl(\frac{1}{\omega^{4}}+\frac{2}{q^{4}}\biggr)\,\rho^{2}(-q^{2}).

(18)

We perform the Wick rotation of the integration contour
\omega\rightarrow i\,\omega
,
integrate over a 3-dimensional sphere (see Appendix A), and subtract the point nucleus and the leading fns, to obtain

\displaystyle E^{(5,1)}_{\rm fns}=

\displaystyle\ -(Z\alpha)^{2}\,\frac{\phi^{2}(0)}{m\,M}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{q}

\displaystyle\times\Big\{h\Big(\frac{2\,m}{q}\Big)\,\big[1-\rho^{2}(q^{2})\big]+\frac{32\,m^{3}}{q}\,\rho^{\prime}(0)\Big\}\,,

(19)

where

\displaystyle h(a)=

\displaystyle\ 2\,a^{2}\,\Big[\sqrt{1+a^{2}}-\big(1+\sqrt{1+a^{2}}\big)^{-2}\Big]\,.

(20)

Using the dipole parametrization of the nuclear charge form factor

\displaystyle\rho(q^{2})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\Lambda^{4}}{(\Lambda^{2}+q^{2})^{2}},\;\rho^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{2}{\Lambda^{2}},\;r_{C}^{2}=\frac{12}{\Lambda^{2}},

(21)

one obtains
pachucki:25a

\displaystyle E^{(5,1)}_{\mathrm{fns}}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\phi^{2}(0)}{m\,M}\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\biggl[\frac{3\,(1-y^{2})\,(1+4\,y^{2}-35\,y^{4})}{8\,y^{4}}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{3+8\,y^{2}+40\,y^{4}-140\,y^{6}+105\,y^{8}}{16\,y^{5}}\,\ln\frac{1+y}{1-y}

\displaystyle\ -\ln\frac{1-y^{2}}{4}\biggr]\,,

(22)

where
y=\sqrt{1-4\,m^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}
. The expansion of the above formula in small
m/\Lambda
,
which is appropriate for
electronic
atoms, is

\displaystyle E^{(5,1)}_{\mathrm{fns}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\phi^{2}(0)}{M\,m}\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\biggl[\bigg(\!\frac{43}{12}-\ln 12+2\,\ln m\,r_{C}\!\bigg)(m\,r_{C})^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{25}{36}\biggl(\frac{3}{20}-\ln 12+2\,\ln m\,r_{C}\!\biggr)\,(m\,r_{C})^{4}

\displaystyle\ +O\big(m\,r_{C}\big)^{6}\biggr]\,.

(23)

For muonic atoms,
E^{(5,1)}_{\mathrm{fns}}
is a significant correction, but
m\,r_{C}

can be close to 1; therefore, the formulas Eq. (
19
) or (
22
) should be used, instead of the
m\,r_{C}
expansion.
Moreover, one can expect significant inelastic contributions.
In fact, for
\mu
H one uses dispersion relations to express the exact two-photon exchange amplitude
in terms of the measured inelastic structure functions
carlson:11
.

\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{5}
radiative nonrecoil fns

The
\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{5}
radiative correction
E^{(6,0)}_{\mathrm{radfns}}
to the finite nuclear size effect
is obtained by a replacement in Eq. (
5
) of
T^{00}
with radiatively corrected
T^{00}

\displaystyle E^{(6,0)}_{\mathrm{radfns}}=

\displaystyle\ \phi^{2}(0)\!\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,\big[T^{00}_{\mathrm{se}}(\vec{p}^{\,2})+T^{00}_{\mathrm{vp}}(\vec{p}^{\,2})\big]\,V^{2}(\vec{p}^{\,2}),

(24)

where

T^{00}(\vec{p}^{\,2})

and
T^{00}_{\mathrm{vp}}(\vec{p}^{\,2})

T^{00}(\vec{p}^{\,2})

\displaystyle T^{00}_{\mathrm{vp}}(p^{\,2})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{4}{p^{2}}\,\bar{\omega}\bigg(\frac{p^{\,2}}{m^{2}}\bigg),

(25)

where

\displaystyle\bar{\omega}\bigg(\frac{p^{2}}{m^{2}}\bigg)=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\alpha}{3\,\pi}\,\biggl\{\frac{1}{3}+2\biggl(1-\frac{2\,m^{2}}{p^{2}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\ \biggl[\sqrt{1+\frac{4\,m^{2}}{p^{2}}}\,\mathrm{arccoth}\sqrt{1+\frac{4\,m^{2}}{p^{2}}}-1\biggr]\biggr\}.

(26)

Let us rescale all momenta by the lepton mass
m
in Eq. (
24
), subtract the point nucleus,
and introduce a function
f

\displaystyle E^{(6,0)}_{\mathrm{radfns}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{\phi^{2}(0)}{m^{2}}\,\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{2}}{p^{4}}

\displaystyle\times f(p^{2})\,\left[\rho\big(m^{2}\,p^{2}\big)^{2}-1\right],

(27)

where

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,f(p^{2})=T^{00}_{\mathrm{se}}(p^{2})+T^{00}_{\mathrm{vp}}(p^{2})\,.

(28)

Function

can be obtained analytically, but following Ref.
pachucki:93a
it is more convenient to use its imaginary part
on the branch cut

\displaystyle f^{A}(q^{2})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{f(-q^{2}+i\,\epsilon)-f(-q^{2}-i\,\epsilon)}{2\,\pi\,i}\,,

(29)

So that,

\displaystyle f(p^{2})=

\displaystyle\ -\int_{0}^{\infty}d(q^{2})\,\frac{f^{A}(q^{2})}{q^{2}+p^{2}}\,,

(30)

where

\displaystyle f^{A}(q^{2})=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{3}{4}-\frac{4}{q^{2}}-\frac{1}{4\,(1+q^{2})}-J^{A}\,\biggl(1+\frac{4}{q^{2}}-\frac{q^{2}}{4}\biggr)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\Theta(q-2)}{\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{q^{2}}}}\,\biggl(1+\frac{1}{q^{2}}-\frac{12}{q^{4}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{4}{3}\,\Theta(q-2)\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{q^{2}}}\,\frac{1}{q^{2}}\,\bigg(1+\frac{2}{q^{2}}\bigg)\,,

(31)

in which the last term comes from the vacuum polarization, and

\displaystyle J^{A}=-\frac{1}{q}\,\bigg[\arctan(q)-\Theta(q-2)\,\arccos\bigg(\frac{2}{q}\bigg)\bigg]\,.

(32)

For muonic atoms, the integral in Eq. (
27
) should be performed numerically without expansion
of
\rho(p^{2})
in small momenta, and it was done in Ref.
pachucki:24a
for

\mu
H,
\mu
D,
\mu^{3}
He, and
\mu^{4}
He.
For

atoms the finite nuclear size is much smaller than the electron Compton wavelength,

\displaystyle E^{(6,0)}_{\mathrm{radfns}}=

\displaystyle\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,\frac{\phi^{2}(0)}{m^{2}}\,\biggl[\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,(m\,r_{C})^{2}\,(4\,\ln 2-5)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{(m\,r_{F})^{3}}{9}\,\biggl(\frac{9019}{1260}-\ln 12+2\ln m\,r_{C}\biggr)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,\bigg[\frac{(m\,r_{C})^{4}}{3}+\frac{(m\,r_{CC})^{4}}{5}\bigg]\,\biggl(4\,\ln 2-\frac{115}{32}\biggr)

\displaystyle\ +\ldots\biggr].

eides:01

\alpha\,(Z\,\alpha)^{5}
radiative RECOIL FNS

We will consider the radiative recoil effects separately for the vacuum-polarization and the lepton self-energy

\displaystyle E^{(6,1)}_{\rm radfns}=

\displaystyle\ E^{(6,1)}_{\rm vpfns}+E^{(6,1)}_{\rm sefns}.

(34)

E^{(6,1)}_{\rm vpfns}
is obtained by multiplying the integrand of Eq. (
18
) by
-2\,\bar{\omega}

and subtracting a point nucleus

\displaystyle E^{(6,1)}_{\rm vpfns}=

\displaystyle\ (Z\alpha)^{2}\,\frac{\phi^{2}(0)}{m\,M}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{q}

\displaystyle\times 2\,\bar{\omega}\Big(\frac{q^{2}}{m^{2}}\Big)h\Big(\frac{2\,m}{q}\Big)\,\big[1-\rho^{2}(q^{2})\big]

(35)

because the muon Compton wave length is much smaller than the Bohr radius, and thus no contribution
of lower order is present.
For muonic atoms
E^{(6,1)}_{\rm vpfns}
will be combined with
E^{(6,1)}_{\rm sefns}
, while
for
electronic
atoms it can be further simplified to

\displaystyle E^{(6,1)}_{\rm vpfns}=

\displaystyle\ -(Z\alpha)^{2}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,\frac{m\,}{M}\,\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\frac{r_{C}^{2}}{12}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{547}{9}+\frac{4\,\pi^{2}}{3}+22\,\ln\frac{m^{2}\,r_{C}^{2}}{12}+4\,\ln^{2}\frac{m^{2}\,r_{C}^{2}}{12}\bigg).

(36)

We note that the
\ln^{2}
term cancels out with the corresponding self-energy correction.

E^{(6,1)}_{\rm sefns}
is obtained from Eq. (
17
) by replacement of
T^{jk}
by
the self-energy corrected
T^{jk}_{\mathrm{se}}

\displaystyle E^{(6,1)}_{\rm sefns}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{M}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{2}

(37)

Because the symmetrized tensor

T_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\mu\nu}(q)=T_{\mathrm{se}}^{\mu\nu}(q)+T_{\mathrm{se}}^{\mu\nu}(-q)

(38)

fulfills the continuity relation
q_{\mu}\,T_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\mu\nu}(q)=0

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{sym}}^{jk}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-\frac{q^{i}\,q^{j}}{\omega^{2}}\biggr)\,\biggl(\delta^{ik}-\frac{q^{i}\,q^{k}}{\omega^{2}}\biggr)=

\displaystyle\ -T^{\mu}_{\mathrm{sym},\,\mu}-\frac{q^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\,T_{\mathrm{sym}}^{00}\,,

(39)

and thus with
\omega=q^{0}

\displaystyle E^{(6,1)}_{\rm sefns}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{M}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{2}\!\int_{s}\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\,\pi)^{4}\,i}

\displaystyle\hskip-21.52771pt\times\bigg(\frac{T^{\mu}_{\mathrm{se},\,\mu}(-q^{2},q^{0})}{q^{2}}+\frac{T_{\mathrm{se}}^{00}(-q^{2},q^{0})}{q_{0}^{2}}\bigg)\,\frac{\rho^{2}(-q^{2})-1}{q^{2}}\,,

(40)

where

and

are presented in Appendix C.
For the numerical calculation it is convenient to perform Wick rotation, and
using the Euclidean metrics

\displaystyle E^{(6,1)}_{\rm sefns}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle E}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{M}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,(4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha)^{2}\!\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\,\pi)^{4}}

\displaystyle\hskip-21.52771pt\times\bigg(\frac{T^{\mu}_{\mathrm{se},\,\mu}(q^{2},i\,q^{0})}{q^{2}}+\frac{T_{\mathrm{se}}^{00}(q^{2},i\,q^{0})}{q_{0}^{2}}\bigg)\,\frac{\rho^{2}(q^{2})-1}{q^{2}}.

(41)

The above integrals are calculated by introducing a dimensionless

\displaystyle E^{(6,1)}_{\rm sefns}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,\frac{\phi^{2}(0)}{M\,m}\,\frac{T}{2},

(42)

which is defined as

\displaystyle T=

\displaystyle\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dq}{q}\,\big[\rho^{2}(q^{2})-1\big]\,T(q),

(43)

where

\displaystyle T(q)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\phi\,\sin^{2}\phi\,\bigg(T^{\mu}_{\mathrm{se},\,\mu}(q^{2},i\,q\,\cos\phi)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{T_{\mathrm{se}}^{00}(q^{2},i\,q\,\cos\phi)}{\cos^{2}\phi}\bigg)\,\frac{4\,\pi}{\alpha}.

(44)

The numerical integration of the above double integral is performed as follows.
We integrate over
\phi
by parts, using Eq. (
84
) from Appendix, to replace
J

with its derivative

, so only logs are present in the integrand.
Next, the terms with

are separated out and integrated over
\phi
analytically.
The numerical integration of remaining terms over

does not have any singular points,
but requires adaptive quadrature and is performed with the
Mathematica

The second integral over

is smooth, but the integrand contains
\ln q
at
both integration boundaries at

and

Therefore, this integral is split into two parts

\displaystyle T=

\displaystyle\ \int_{0}^{1}\frac{dq}{q}\,\big[\rho^{2}(q^{2})-1\big]\,T(q)

\displaystyle\ +\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{x}\,\big[\rho^{2}(1/x^{2})-1\big]\,T(1/x)

(45)

and is performed using the extended Gaussian quadrature

which is adapted to polynomials and logarithm times polynomials at the same time.
Its numerical convergence is very fast, and with only 20 integration points we got about 16 significant digits.

VIII
Radiative correction to the nuclear polarizability effect

Perhaps the most interesting are radiative corrections to the nuclear polarizability effect in atomic systems,
which have not yet been investigated in the literature. Calculations proceed similarly
to the previously considered radiative recoil fns correction, and let us first recall, at the beginning,
the derivation of the leading electric dipole nuclear polarizability correction in
electronic
atoms.
The interaction of a nucleus with the electric field is given by

\displaystyle\delta H=Z\,e\,A^{0}-\vec{d}\cdot\vec{E},

(56)

where
\vec{d}
is the electric dipole operator. The electric field from the electron can excite
the nucleus, which effectively changes atomic energy levels. The shift,
at the leading order, is given by the two-photon exchange in the scattering approximation,
which in the temporal gauge takes the form

\displaystyle E_{\rm pol}=

\displaystyle\ i\,e^{2}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\,\pi)^{4}}\,\omega^{2}\,\frac{\Bigl(\delta^{ik}-\frac{q^{i}\,q^{k}}{\omega^{2}}\Bigr)}{q^{2}}\,\frac{\Bigl(\delta^{jl}-\frac{q^{j}\,q^{l}}{\omega^{2}}\Bigr)}{q^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-21.52771pt\times\big[T^{ij}(q)+T^{ji}(-q)\big]\,\biggl\langle d^{\,k}\,\frac{1}{E_{N}-H_{N}-\omega}\,d^{\,l}\biggr\rangle,

(57)

where
\omega=q^{0}
and
T^{ij}
is defined in Eq. (
7
).
Let us perform the
q
integration in the way, that is suited for later calculations of radiative corrections.
So, we assume the Wick rotated contour
\omega\rightarrow i\,\omega
, use the Euclidean metric

\displaystyle E_{\rm pol}=

\displaystyle\ e^{2}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\,\pi)^{4}}\,\frac{4\,m}{3}\,\frac{q^{4}+2\,\omega^{4}}{q^{4}\,(q^{2}-2\,i\,m\,\omega)\,(q^{2}+2\,i\,m\,\omega)}\,\biggl\langle\vec{d}\,\frac{1}{E_{N}-H_{N}-i\,\omega}\,\vec{d}\biggr\rangle\,,

(58)

perform the angular average

\displaystyle E_{\rm pol}=

\displaystyle\ -e^{2}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\,\pi)^{4}}\,\frac{4\,m}{3}\,\int\frac{d\,\Omega_{q}}{2\,\pi^{2}}\frac{q^{4}+2\,q_{0}^{4}}{q^{4}\,(q^{4}+4\,m^{2}\,q_{0}^{2})}\,\biggl\langle\vec{d}\,\frac{1}{H_{N}-E_{N}+i\,q^{0}}\,\vec{d}\biggr\rangle\,,

\displaystyle E_{\rm pol}=

\displaystyle\ -m\,\alpha^{2}\,\phi^{2}(0)\,\frac{2}{3}\,\biggl\langle\frac{\vec{d}}{e}\,\frac{1}{H_{N}-E_{N}}\,\biggl[\frac{19}{6}+5\,\ln\frac{2\,(H_{N}-E_{N})}{m}\biggr]\,\frac{\vec{d}}{e}\biggr\rangle\,.

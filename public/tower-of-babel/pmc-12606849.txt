Exploring strengths, challenges, and experiences of using a national call center during the Covid-19 pandemic: a qualitative study
Authors: Maryam Eslami Jahromi, Haleh Ayatollahi, Ali Ebrazeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInvestigating experiences of staff and callers of call centers/hotlines during the Covid-19 pandemic is essential to identify perceived strengths, challenges, and opportunities for improving service delivery. This study aimed to explore experiences and perspectives of staff and callers of the 4030 call center during the Covid-19 pandemic.MethodsThis was a qualitative study in which data were collected via conducting semi-structured interviews with the call center’s staff (n = 5), consultants (n = 24), and callers (n = 35). The data were analyzed using the framework analysis approach.ResultThe results included four main themes emerged from interviewing the staff and consultants of the call center: the rationale behind establishing the 4030 call center, its operational processes, strengths, and challenges. Interviews with the callers included three key main themes: reasons for contacting the call center, its strengths, and challenges.ConclusionDuring the Covid-19 pandemic, the 4030 call Center played an important role in reducing the burden of in-person visits to healthcare centers as well as providing psychological support to the public. Despite advantages such as preventing disease spread and alleviating public concerns, challenges including the large number of calls, long waiting times, and limitations of telephone counseling were reported. Therefore, healthcare policymakers need to pay more attention to the strengths and challenges of the call centers during health crises to improve service delivery and use past experiences for future planning.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13642-4.

Introduction

Covid-19 posed a major threat to global health and was officially declared as a pandemic by the World Health Organization (WHO) on March 17, 2020. Since then, various strategies have been implemented worldwide to mitigate the impact of the pandemic [1]. These strategies included quarantine measures, curfews, stay-at-home orders, social distancing, travel restrictions, border closures, as well as temporary shutdown of schools, retail stores, restaurants, workplaces, and public events from 2020 to 2022 [2].

The Covid-19 pandemic also transformed the delivery of healthcare services globally. Telemedicine emerged as a critical technology to ensure continued healthcare access in response to the pandemic and the need for physical and social distancing [3]. Health authorities recommended replacing in-person outpatient consultations with telephone calls to reduce the risk of viral transmission during the pandemic [4]. Accordingly, many countries established telephone hotlines, helplines, and call centers [5], which experienced a substantial increase in calls by late March 2020 [2]. These systems primarily aimed to provide psychological support, disseminate reliable information about Covid-19, offer healthcare advice, and conduct triage assessments [5].

Several studies examined the role and impact of hotlines/call centers )telephone counseling( during health crises. A study in 2020 reported that 84% of calls to crisis hotlines ended with a positive outcome, meaning that the caller’s issue was either resolved during the call or the caller was referred to a local healthcare provider, indicating that such hotlines/call centers can effectively reduce pressure on healthcare systems and provide support to individuals in crisis [6]. The Covid-19 pandemic further affected hotline usage and increased both the volume and complexity of calls, with callers primarily seeking psychological support and information related to Covid-19, reflecting a significant need for healthcare services [7]. Another study showed that hotline callers during the pandemic reported mental health concerns, including anxiety, depression, and sleep disturbances, and services provided through hotlines helped to reduce these concerns and provided effective support [8]. In addition, frequent callers often experienced serious mental and physical health problems, and were frequently in crisis, suggesting that service models should be tailored to address the specific needs of frequent callers for efficient resource utilization [9].

Evidence also points to the measurable benefits of hotline interventions. Systematic reviews and large-scale evaluations report high satisfaction of callers and short-term reductions in distress following calls [6]. Another study showed that between 70% and 89% of callers reported positive experiences, with significant reductions in psychological distress [10]. National evaluations, such as the Samaritans Helpline study in the UK, further demonstrated decreases in distress scores and improvements in wellbeing, while emphasizing the specific needs of frequent callers and the importance of targeted service models [11].

A study by Fongwen et al. demonstrated that call centers and telehealth services during the covid-19 pandemic supported timely communication, triage, and dissemination of reliable health information, especially in resource-limited contexts. The authors emphasized the need for adaptive strategies and continuous performance assessment from callers’ perspectives to improve service delivery in health crises [12]. Similarly, Gennai et al. analyzed 7,863 telephone consultations via an infectious disease hotline and showed that callers—including outpatient and hospital physicians—relied on the service for antimicrobial treatment advice, infection prevention, and case management. Their findings highlighted the importance of continuous evaluation and optimization of hotline services to strengthen health outcomes during public health emergencies [13]. A study by Sette et al. evaluated infectious disease hotlines for general practitioners in France, showing that physicians mainly used these services for diagnostic advice and antibiotic selection. The findings underscored the practical utility of hotlines in supporting primary care and the need for stronger institutional support [14].

Other infectious disease crises have also provided insights into the utility of hotline services. McCarthy et al. evaluated the user experience of the Check and Report Ebola (CARE) Hotline, a post-arrival monitoring system implemented in the United States during the 2014–2016 Ebola outbreak. Users appreciated the daily automated health status reporting system, which provided reassurance and facilitated timely medical interventions, though technological barriers and privacy concerns were noted [15]. Koonin et al. evaluated a national helpline simulation for influenza pandemic response, finding that 84% of calls matched the expected outcomes and 83% of callers reported needs were met, highlighting the operational effectiveness of helplines [16]. Deng et al. studied a SARS fever hotline service and found that nearly all physicians regarded it as useful for providing public advice, although concerns about adequacy of information and training remained [17]. Evidence from the 2003 SARS outbreak in Hong Kong further showed that callers’ concerns varied by disaster phase and user type and highlighted emotional and information needs [18]. Similarly, a national toll-free Ebola hotline in South Sudan handled over 2,000 calls, primarily for symptoms and case reporting, and played a dual role in strengthening disease surveillance and disseminating evidence-based information to the public [19].

In Iran, after reporting the first Covid-19 case in early March 2020, the Ministry of Health launched a dedicated Covid-19 consultation call center. The public was invited to call the 4030 call center to receive advice related to Covid-19 [20]. One of the main objectives of this service was to reduce patient traffic and hospital visits across provinces, thereby limiting further spread of Covid-19—a goal that was largely achieved [21].

According to the WHO’s guidance on “Setup and Management of Covid-19 Hotlines”, hotlines were vital tools during the Covid-19 pandemic across European regions. These hotlines facilitated direct communication between at-risk populations and emergency responders, enabling the collection of public concerns and the provision of health advice and guidance [22]. Therefore, evaluating the performance of hotlines and call centers is crucial to better understand callers’ needs, assess the extent to which these needs are met, and identify challenges faced by responders. The results of such evaluations can support workforce reinforcement, barrier removal, and enhancement of the quality and effectiveness of telephone services [23]. In addition, examining these services from the perspective of both stakeholders and service users helps to distinguish strengths and challenges, thereby facilitating strategic planning for service improvement. The evaluation results can be useful for service expansion on a broader scale [24]. Therefore, the present study aimed to explore experiences and perspectives of staff and callers of the 4030 call center during the Covid-19 pandemic.

Methods

This qualitative study was conducted in 2023. Prior to conducting the research, the ethics approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Iran University of Medical Sciences (IR.IUMS.REC.1401.332).

Research setting and participants

This research was conducted at the 4030 call center in Tehran, Iran. The participants included the call center manager, IT staff, physicians, consultants, and callers to the 4030 call center. Purposive sampling was used to interview eligible individuals. Eligible staff and consultants were those employed at the 4030 call center during the Covid-19 pandemic with at least six months of work experience in order to ensure sufficient familiarity with the center’s working processes. Callers were eligible if they were 18 years or older and contacted the 4030 call center at least once during the Covid-19 pandemic. Individuals unable to provide adequate information and informed consent (under 18 years old) or unwilling to participate were excluded.

The researcher (MEJ) was introduced to center officially and explained the study’s objectives, procedures, and the voluntary nature of participation to the invited eligible staff and consultants. The callers were chosen among those who had contacted the 4030 call center between February 20, 2020, and April 1, 2023. Their contact numbers were randomly retrieved from the center’s database, and the researcher contacted them via the 4030 telephone number. Then, the purpose of the study was explained for them, and they were invited to participate in telephone interviews at a convenient time. Participation was voluntary, and all participants could withdraw at any time without consequences. Some potential participants initially declined to participate, mostly due to time constraints or unfamiliarity with the study. To encourage participation, the researcher provided information about the study’s objectives, the voluntary nature of participation, confidentiality measures, and the expected duration of the interview. In total, 64 individuals including the 4030 Call Center staff (n = 5), consultants (n = 24), and callers (n = 35) participated in the interviews. Data collection continued until data saturation was reached. Both face-to-face and telephone semi-structured interviews were conducted by the researcher (MEJ).

Data collection

To conduct the interviews, one of the researchers (MEJ) visited the 4030 Call Center and interviews with the staff and consultants were conducted in person at their workplace or via telephone at appropriate time. To reach callers, their contact numbers were obtained from the database of the 4030 call center. More than 96,000,000 calls were received from the center’s launch on February 20, 2020 until April 1, 2023, which was announced as the end of Covid-19 pandemic by WHO. The callers were chosen randomly and MEJ conducted the interviews via the 4030 telephone number.

Before conducting the interviews, two interview guides (Supplementary File 1) were developed based on the literature review [22, 25–28]. The interview guides were tailored to capture the perspectives of different participant groups. The first interview guide (11 questions) was developed for the call center staff and consultants, and the second interview guide (9 questions) was prepared for the callers. The differences in the interview guides helped the researchers to collect more specific insights and data about the call center strengths and challenges. Written consent was obtained before conducting face-to-face interview, and verbal consent was obtained from the callers before beginning the telephone interviews. Interviews continued until data saturation was reached. The interviews were digitally recorded with the permission of participants.

Data analysis

Interviews were transcribed verbatim and analyzed using framework analysis [29]. Initially, a thematic framework was generated which included the strengths of the 4030 call center, challenges in service delivery, and users’ experiences. This framework was flexible and new issues and themes could be added during the data analysis. Data analysis was conducted concurrently with data collection, and after each set of interviews the research team discussed preliminary findings. Recruitment continued until data saturation was reached. At this point, no new data, themes, or subthemes emerged from additional interviews. Data saturation was determined collaboratively by the research team and guided the final sample size.

Framework analysis consists of five stages: familiarization, identification of a thematic framework, indexing, charting and mapping, and interpretation. It provides a systematic model for managing and mapping the data. Moreover, interviewees may have different viewpoints or experiences, which can be compared and contrasted, and the framework analysis method is the most suitable approach for analyzing semi-structured interviews. It is desirable to generate themes by making comparisons both within and between cases. Moreover, the summarized data help to gain a deeper understanding of the patterns in data. This method can also contribute to the improvement of health services and development of health policy [29–32]. The MAXQDA10 software was utilized to facilitate the process of coding, categorizing, and comparing the data. Finally, the themes, subthemes, and categories were extracted and reported narratively.

The initial coding was completed by MEJ, and the results were reviewed by HA and AE. HA and AE independently reviewed the coded data and the emerging themes to ensure consistency, clarity, and accurate representation of the participants’ perspectives. Discrepancies or uncertainties were discussed among the three researchers until consensus was reached. This process served to enhance the credibility and trustworthiness of the analysis, although no formal double coding was performed. A summary of the results was also sent to the participants to check the accuracy and enhance the credibility of the findings. Participants were selected to represent different groups (staff, consultants, and callers) and those who indicated willingness to provide feedback on the transcripts. Feedback from participants was reviewed by the research team and used to clarify, refine, or confirm the themes and interpretations. This process helped to ensure that the analysis accurately reflected participants’ perspectives.

Participants’ demographic information

The educational background of the call center staff included psychiatry (n = 5, 17.2%), health sciences (n = 5, 17.2%), laboratory sciences (n = 3, 10.3%), medical microbiology (n = 2, 6.8%), medicine (n = 2, 6.8%), biochemistry (n = 2, 6.8%), midwifery (n = 2, 6.8%), computer science (n = 2, 6.8%), bacteriology/medical parasitology (n = 2, 6.8%), nursing (n = 1, 3.4%), immunology (n = 1, 3.4%), communications, and (n = 1, 3.4%), biology (n = 1, 3.4%).

The consultants worked in different departments of the call center: Covid-19 (n = 19, 65.5%), mental health (n = 5, 17.2%), vaccination (n = 4, 13.7%), elderly care (n = 2, 6.8%), cancer (n = 2, 6.8%), smoking cessation (n = 1, 3.4%), and maternal healthcare (n = 1, 3.4%).

Most of the call center staff had three years of work experience and more (n = 25, 86.2%), and only a minority of them had less than three years of work experience (n = 4, 13.7%). Overall, interviews lasted between 20 and 45 min (mean: 32 min).

Most calls received by the 4030 call center were related to Covid-19 (n = 32, 91.4%). Other reasons for calling the 4030 were related to mental health and Covid-19 (n = 6, 16.6%), vaccination and Covid-19 (n = 3, 8.5%), maternal healthcare and Covid-19 (n = 2, 5.7%) r, and nutrition and Covid-19 (n = 1, 2.8%). It should be noted that some calls addressed more than one topic, resulting in the total percentage exceeding 100%.

The themes, subthemes, and categories emerged from interviewing the 4030 call center staff and consultants

Theme 1: purpose of establishing the 4030 call center

This theme encompasses two sub-themes: control and prevention of Covid-19 and medical and psychological support during the pandemic.

Control and prevention of Covid-19

In the context of control and prevention of Covid-19, most participants emphasized the importance of widespread and continuous population screening. They also noted that the establishment of the 4030 call center helped to screen the general population on a large scale, and was one of the most significant public health initiatives. This center provided an effective means to identify suspected Covid-19 cases and referred patients to designated hospitals. Offering guidance and free consultation to the public significantly reduced unnecessary medical visits. As one of the consultants noted: “For instance, individuals with mild symptoms, who would otherwise visit a physician out of fear of Covid-19, were reassured through phone consultations. This alleviated psychological distress and significantly reduced visits” (P-15).

Medical and psychological support during the pandemic

To reduce the anxiety and psychological distress caused by Covid-19 pandemic and its associated mortality, the 4030 call center offered psychological consultations to the public, providing reassurance and support. The availability of such a service allowed people to avoid confusion and obtain reliable information via telephone communication.

Theme 2: defined processes in the 4030 call center

Staff training

The findings revealed that training was mainly conducted using the Ministry of Health guidelines, educational booklets developed by the 4030 call center, and mobile applications. These materials covered consultation principles, emergency symptoms, referral procedures, and so forth. Moreover, in-service classes were held by physicians based on the protocols provided by the Ministry of Health. Supplementary materials such as updated WHO guidelines, summaries of the new articles, and recorded lectures were shared through dedicated online groups.

Performance evaluation

Most consultants indicated that their performance was evaluated monthly by the physicians through various means such as taking exams and call audits. One of the physicians stated: “We conducted quality assessments by randomly listening to the recorded calls and scoring consultants using a structured form. Based on the scores, we provided feedback and guidance” (P-17).

Physicians also made anonymous calls to monitor performance and provided constructive feedback. Consultants with poor performance received training or were dismissed, though most issues were resolved with physician support.

Referral mechanisms for callers

If callers had more complex medical inquiries, they were referred to a physician at the call center. Based on the symptoms and medical history, consultants were required to refer individuals to physicians, local health centers, or hospital emergency departments when necessary. In addition, callers were informed of nearby Covid-19 testing sites and vaccination centers.

Regulations and restrictions

The 4030 call center staff faced specific constraints while offering services. The call duration was limited to avoid unnecessary conversation and reduce caller waiting time. According to the Ministry of Health guidelines, the consultants could only provide limited advice and were prohibited from prescribing medications or engaging in treatment decisions. Furthermore, they were not allowed to share personal details or recommend facilities outside the Ministry’s official list. Consultants remained anonymous to the callers and only referred patients to the Ministry-approved institutions.

Theme 3: strengths of the 4030 call center

Clinical benefits

Most of the consultants believed that the 4030 call center effectively contributed to prevent unnecessary visits to hospitals and clinics, while offering medical and psychological support to those mentally distressed by the pandemic.

Non-clinical advantages

The call center was free and available 24/7. Many consultants reported that the public regarded the 4030 call center as a trustworthy resource. One of the consultants noted: “It seemed that our confirmation was even more reassuring than that of a doctor. People had that much confidence in 4030” (P-9).

Human resource performance

Consultants felt appreciated based on the positive public feedback, frequency of calls, and expressions of gratitude. On-call consultants played a vital role by assisting other staff when the number of calls surged. Additionally, ongoing physician-led training helped consultants stay up-to-date and deliver professional responses.

Theme 4: challenges of the 4030 call center

Caller-related challenges

Financial challenges during the lockdowns led some people to call the 4030 call center to seek financial aid. In addition, some people look for home care services. As one of the consultants remarked: “It would have been great if 4030 offered free home nurse visits for the disabled or financially constrained” (P-4).

Inadequate infrastructure

Scheduled summer power outages affected the call center operations and caused call disruptions. The center also lacked proper ventilation, air conditioner, and rest areas for staff who worked in lengthy shifts.

During the early days, consultants used regular phones without headsets. Software limitations were also a major challenge. IT staff recommended implementing strong data centers, servers, call-handling software, reporting dashboards, and early-stage benchmarking with other call centers to improve design. Moreover, skilled programmers were essential for real-time system support.

Human resource challenges

Initially, there was limited information about Covid-19, and physicians had to keep consultants updated with the latest global findings. Despite the pandemic, staff had to work on-site, facing infection risks. The large number of calls strained the available workforce.

Consultants reported job instability even after three years with limited insurance. Issues like overcrowded hospitals, vaccine shortages, unemployment, and uncoordinated staffing due to the rapid launch were additional concerns.

Data collection challenges

Consultants believed that feedback collection and a unified system for collecting call histories could improve service quality. Initially, no direct call transfer to physicians was possible, and important documents were only available via mobile apps. One of the consultants noted: “We couldn’t always find past files when needed, though later they became accessible via the system” (P-15).

Similar helpline numbers caused confusion. Moreover, although each consultant had a unique code, the callers could not request a specific consultant again. As one of the interviewees stated: “For example, my code was 611, but the caller wouldn’t know which consultant they were connected to” (P-13).

Managerial challenges

Although the 4030 call center was well-known during the pandemic, it lacked sufficient promotion afterward in spite of expanding its services. Critics felt that the center’s potential could be better utilized in non-communicable disease management based on better planning and oversight.

Most interviewees highlighted weak support received from the Ministry and a lack of integration with hospitals, charity organizations, and other helplines. As one of the consultants explained: “We need a contact at hospitals or health centers who can relay real-time service availability” (P-24).

Moreover, few meetings were held between the consultants and the call center leadership, and frequent managerial changes negatively affected staff performance.

The themes, subthemes, and categories emerged from interviewing the 4030 callers

Theme 1: reasons for contacting the 4030 call center

Covid-19 disease

Most callers reported that they contacted the 4030 call center to receive medical advice after getting diagnosed or suspected to be infected with Covid-19. Some interviewees mentioned that they experienced symptoms of a possible infection during the pandemic, but they were uncertain whether they were infected or not. The 4030 call center helped them to make accurate and timely assessments. For many, the spread of Covid-19 was accompanied by stress, anxiety, and concerns for their own health and well-being of family members, prompting a need for psychological support. Some callers indicated that their fear of the virus led them to seek psychological consultation through the 4030 call center.

Request for information and inquiries about Covid-19

Covid-19 was a novel crisis, and the public lacked sufficient knowledge about it. The 4030 call center served as a reliable source for receiving trustworthy and up-to-date information. It also addressed public concerns regarding Covid-19 vaccines and provided essential guidance on prevention measures.

Theme 2: strengths of the 4030 call center

Meeting community needs

The 4030 call center helped patients to gain a better understanding of the disease and reduced the uncertainty they experienced. It also provided psychological strategies to help people cope with death anxiety, social stigma, and the emotional management of family members. According to the callers, the free consultation service enabled timely access to information anywhere and at any time, ultimately reducing healthcare costs and delays.

High quality consultations

Most callers were satisfied with the responses of the consultants, stating that they had supported both patients and their family members during the pandemic. Callers found that most consultants were kind and patient. As one of the callers noted: “4030 consultants responded in a way that we felt like speaking with a doctor or nurse. They were excellent guides—professional, skilled, and knowledgeable” (P-21).

Callers considered the consultants’ responses credible and helpful in reducing their concerns.

Theme 3: challenges of the 4030 call center

Insufficient attention to the callers’ expectations

Callers believed that collecting feedback after calls—mainly about the consultant’s behavior, waiting time, service quality, responsiveness, and consultant’s knowledge—could improve the center’s performance. This feedback could enable corrective actions in order to raise overall callers’ satisfaction.

Some callers described the consultant’s voice as faint or unclear. They also believed that the number 4030 was good, but suggested using a more memorable alternative, such as 3040. Others suggested adding an operator to help users through the menu system. During the peak of the pandemic, there was a huge number of calls with long waiting times. Callers indicated that they could enjoy listening to a music during a long waiting time.

“Sometimes it took 5–6 minutes to get an answer,” one caller noted (P-1).

Many callers were unfamiliar with the broader services offered by 4030 call center and believed that greater promotion and government support were necessary to increase public awareness and ensure the sustainability of the center.

Challenges related to the consultations

Callers felt that face-to-face consultations were more effective than phone calls, because physical examinations and eye contact were not possible by phone. While many were satisfied with this type of services, some stated that a number of consultants lacked sufficient medical knowledge and recommended hiring more qualified personnel.

Some callers also reported that some consultants were impatient and ended calls abruptly after providing brief answers, which they attributed to the overwhelming number of calls during the crisis. Callers suggested increasing number of psychologists and physicians to improve service quality and responsiveness.

Launching the 4030 call center

The interviewees stated that after completing application forms for the 4030 call center and recruitment, they underwent interviews and received initial training. As one of the interviewees explained: “After the consultants were selected, they were trained by pulmonologists and general practitioners in less than a week and became ready to take calls” (P-20).

Discussion

The findings of this study indicated that the primary goal of launching the 4030 Call Center was to control and prevent the spread of Covid-19 through extensive screening as well as reducing unnecessary visits to healthcare facilities. The 4030 staff and consultants, believed that key strengths of the center included providing medical and psychological support, 24/7 free access, and high public trust due to providing high quality consultations. However, several challenges, such as inadequate hardware and software infrastructure, workforce-related issues including job insecurity and the large number of calls, managerial inefficiencies, and regulatory limitations were reported. From the callers’ perspectives, the 4030 call center contributed to the public awareness, reduced anxiety, enabled quick access to information, and decreased healthcare expenses, though there were some challenges, such as long waiting times, poor voice quality, lack of support for navigating menu options, and insufficient publicity.

Regarding the organizational processes of the 4030 call center, our results showed that consultants received initial training and their activities was periodically evaluated. Murthy et al. similarly found that effective training programs improve caller’s satisfaction and increase call center productivity [33]. Another study reported that training, particularly for on-call consultants, was delivered via online channels and included internal group chats and helpline tools for remote support. It is essential that managers do not overlook remote staff and provide adequate guidance and professional development opportunities for them [34]. Comprehensive training is critical to standardize skills and meeting callers’ expectations [35].

Consistent with our findings, Nejhaddadgar et al. showed that prior to implementing a tele-triage system, all patients were directly referred to hospitals. However, hospital referrals significantly decreased after system implementation [36]. In another study, Reeves et al. emphasized the importance of telephone screening in primary care to identify suspected cases, and appropriately allocate hospital services for acute respiratory illnesses [37].

In the current study, public education on Covid-19 prevention, anxiety reduction, and psychological support were also important. Similarly, many countries have taken advantage of telephone hotlines to educate the public about the Covid-19 protocols and symptoms, answer general FAQs, and reduce unnecessary in-person visits [38]. Brülhart et al. analyzed 8,000,000 calls across 19 countries and found a 35% increase in call quantity during the pandemic, largely due to fear, loneliness, and health-related concerns [38].

In line with the 4030 call center goal of offering psychological consultation, Zhao et al. found that callers reported issues such as depression, domestic conflict, financial stress, and work-related problems [39]. Therefore, hotline/helpline interventions during pandemics should support psychological and other public health issues.

Our findings are in line with the previous evidence on the value of hotlines in public health emergencies. Several studies during the Covid-19 pandemic highlighted their effectiveness and acceptability. For instance, Abdelbaky et al. reported that most patients perceived telephone triage as effective, with high satisfaction and positive attitudes towards the service [40]. Similarly, Bates et al. found high levels of satisfaction among both consultants and callers of a national bereavement helpline, with consultants also reporting strong job satisfaction. In Saudi Arabia, Alshammary et al. demonstrated that palliative care hotlines successfully resolved most patients’ concerns during virtual visits [41]. Ali et al. showed that callers of a national psychosocial support hotline expressed high satisfaction with only minor shortcomings [42]. Collectively, these findings underscore the role of hotlines in meeting both medical and psychosocial needs while maintaining user trust during health crises.

Beyond satisfaction, hotline interventions have also shown measurable psychosocial benefits. Hoffberg et al. found that crisis hotlines consistently reduced short-term distress, improved mood, and promoted treatment engagement [6]. Likewise, a qualitative study of over 1,500 callers in China found that while anxiety, fear, and sadness were common, the hotline was perceived as effective in relieving emotions and addressing medical concerns—highlighting its dual psychological and health benefits during crises [43].

Research also suggests that reviewing calls daily or simultaneously by a scientific committee and providing feedback is crucial to understand the community requirements [21]. Caller support efficiency depends on the staff performance. One method is random call monitoring by supervisors to assess service quality based on the key performance values, with a maximum rating of 100% [44].

Another strength of the 4030 call center was related to its human resource performance, including support from the on-call staff. Compared to the traditional models, virtual call centers now enable remote work which can lead to improve employees’ comfort, productivity and reduce operational costs [44].

This study also identified limitations and challenges from different perspectives. A key issue was the lack of homecare services for vulnerable individuals. By contrast, Nakubulwa reported that consultants could dispatch emergency ambulances or book appointments directly. Callers also noted the absence of music during the call holding time as a weakness [45]. Antonides et al. showed that music positively affects waiting time perception [46].

Some participants suggested a more memorable number like “3040” instead of “4030.” This aligns with the study of Singh et al. who found that shorter, three-digit hotlines are easier to remember and improve accessibility [47]. Brooks Holliday similarly launched a simplified three-digit suicide prevention number to replace a longer one [48].

Moreover, Singh et al. emphasized that phone-only consultations limit the ability to objectively assess a patient condition. For example, observing a patient breathing through video would be more effective than relying on verbal description alone [47].

Another major challenge was the large number of calls and long waiting times. At the peak of the pandemic, similar issues were reported globally. Santos et al. noted that callers experienced over three hours of waiting times, prompting a rapid recruitment of medical student volunteers to reduce waiting times to less than 10 min [48]. Kristal et al. reported that New York City’s Covid-19 hotline exceeded 125 calls per day by mid-March, leading to an increase in staffing and infrastructure [49].

Despite these efforts, the 4030 call center lacked short-, mid-, and long-term strategic planning. Ibrahim et al. emphasized the importance of accurate forecasting to optimize resource allocation, avoid service delays, and prevent unnecessary spending. Long-term planning enhances operational efficiency and timely service delivery [50]. Aslantaş found that callers were dissatisfied with certain services, prompting the center to revise its short- and long-term strategies [51]. As noted in our study, some consultants experienced psychological strain from distressed callers—a finding echoed by Bates et al. [52].

Implications for practice

The 4030 call center experience during the Covid-19 pandemic highlights several practical implications for future health emergency preparedness and response strategies. First of all, policymakers should formally integrate telephone consultation centers into their national emergency response frameworks to ensure rapid deployment and scalability. Secondly, continuous training programs and performance evaluation are essential to maintain service quality and responsiveness. Thirdly, strengthening digital infrastructure including stable power supplies, user-friendly call management systems, and data recording platforms is critical for uninterrupted service delivery. Fourthly, feedback systems should be established to collect and incorporate user experiences into service improvements. Finally, expanding the scope of such services to include non-communicable diseases and mental health disorders, along with the sustained government support and public awareness campaigns can enhance long-term value and sustainability.

Implications for future research

Future research can expand our findings in several directions. Firstly, studies can compare the cost-effectiveness of telephone-based consultation services with traditional in-person visits. Secondly, patient and caregiver satisfaction across different population groups should be systematically examined. Thirdly, the clinical impact of consultations on health outcomes needs to be evaluated. Moreover, future research should investigate the collaboration between the 4030 call center and other organizations to identify strategies for improving service quality and user satisfaction. Modeling the key factors influencing call center performance could also inform preparedness for future pandemics. Finally, the feasibility of integrating advanced technologies such as artificial intelligence and chatbots into the call center systems warrants further investigation, as these may reduce call volumes while enhancing efficiency and user satisfaction.

Our findings indicate that performance in the context of a telephone hotline cannot be captured solely through quantitative indicators such as call volume or response time. Instead, staff performance and subjective experiences, including satisfaction with the consultations, accessibility of services, trust in the information, and perceived effectiveness in reducing unnecessary visits to healthcare centers are of high importance. Likewise, challenges such as long waiting times, insufficient infrastructure, and the psychological burden on consultants represent important dimensions of performance shortcomings. The results of the current study can inform future improvements in telephone-based consultation services during the public health emergencies.

Research limitations

This study had some limitations. While the call center staff and consultants helped participated in the research, interviewing callers was challenging. Some participants declined to participate in the interviews for different reasons, such as time constraints, lack of interest, lack of trust, etc. Therefore, other callers should be replaced. In addition, recall bias may have affected some responses, as participants might not accurately remember their experiences with the 4030 call center due to the time elapsed.

Conclusion

Overall, interviews with the 4030 Call Center staff, consultants, and callers revealed that despite challenges such as the high number of calls, lack of physical examinations, and limited Covid-19 knowledge in the early days of the pandemic, the 4030 call center acted as a reliable and informative resource. It provided essential guidance, alleviated public concerns, and significantly reduced in-person visits to the healthcare facilities. To ensure continuity and effectiveness, the hardware and software infrastructure of the center must be strengthened, and up-to-date training should be consistently offered to the consultants and physicians. Enhancing job security and motivation, improving data collection and feedback mechanisms, and fostering inter-institutional collaboration can help to enhance service quality. Moreover, expanding the center’s scope to include broader health issues and improving targeted public outreach will further solidify the center role within the national healthcare system. The success of the 4030 call center during the Covid-19 crisis underscores the potential of telephone-based consultation services and proper resource management as a scalable model for future public health emergencies.

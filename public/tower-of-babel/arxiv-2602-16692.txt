Disjoint Correspondence Colorings for K 5 K_{5} -Minor-free Graphs
Wouter Cames van Batenburg, Daniel W. Cranston, František Kardoš

Abstract
Thomassen famously proved that every planar graph is 5-choosable. We explore variants of this result, focusing on finding disjoint correspondence colorings, in the more general class of K 5 K_{5} -minor-free graphs. Correspondence colorings generalize list colorings as follows. Given a graph G G and a positive integer t t , a correspondence t t -cover M assigns to each v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) a set of allowable colors { 1 v , … , t v } \{1_{v},\ldots,t_{v}\} and to each edge v ​ w ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) vw\in E(G) a matching between { 1 v , … , t v } \{1_{v},\ldots,t_{v}\} and { 1 w , … , t w } \{1_{w},\ldots,t_{w}\} . An M -coloring φ \varphi picks for each vertex v v a color φ ⁡ ( v ) \varphi(v) (from the set { 1 v , … , t v } \{1_{v},\ldots,t_{v}\} ) such that for each edge v ​ w ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) vw\in E(G) the colors φ ⁡ ( v ) , φ ⁡ ( w ) \varphi(v),\varphi(w) are not matched to each other. Two M -colorings φ 1 , φ 2 \varphi_{1},\varphi_{2} of G G are disjoint if φ 1 ​ ( v ) ≠ φ 2 ​ ( v ) \varphi_{1}(v)\neq\varphi_{2}(v) for all v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) . For every K 5 K_{5} -minor-free graph G G and every correspondence 6-cover M of G G , we construct 3 pairwise disjoint M -colorings φ 1 , φ 2 , φ 3 \varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3} . In contrast, we provide examples of K 5 K_{5} -minor-free graphs and correspondence 5-covers M that do not admit 3 disjoint M -colorings.

1.1
Definitions and Examples

Correspondence coloring generalizes list coloring, and was introduced to resolve a longstanding open
question
[

partition

for each vertex

For a graph

-cover

and to each edge

a matching between

Two

The

,

,

,

,

,

,

As a warmup with these definitions, we provide a few examples. Note that always
\chi(G)\leqslant\chi_{\ell}(G)\leqslant\chi_{c}(G)\leqslant\chi^{\star}_{c}(G)
; and also

1.2
Main Result

The authors of
[
4
]
proposed the problem of determining
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{G})
for various graph classes

\mathcal{G}
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{r}
denote the class of all graphs that are
K_{r}
-minor-free. It is easy to check that

\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{2})=1
,
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{3})=2
, and
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{4})=4
; the final upper bound follows from Lemma
A

below because all graphs in
\mathcal{K}_{4}
are 2-degenerate. So the first open case is
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{5})
.
The best known upper bound is
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{5})\leqslant 8
. This follows from
[
5
, Theorem 1.7]
because every

K_{5}
-minor-free graph has maximum average degree less than 6. We cautiously believe that this upper bound
can be improved as follows.

Conjecture 1
.

If
G
is
K_{5}
-minor-free and
M
is a correspondence 6-cover, then
G
has 6 disjoint

M
-colorings. That is,
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{5})=6
.

Dvořák, Norin, and Postle
[
11
]
called a graph
weighted
\epsilon
-flexible
with
respect to a list-assignment
L
if there exists a probability distribution on the
L
-colorings
\phi

of
G
such that
\mathbb{P}(\phi(v)=c)\geqslant\epsilon
for every vertex
v
and color
c\in L(v)
.
In other words, weighted
\epsilon
-flexibility ensures that at each vertex each color has a decent chance
to appear in the random list-coloring. Dvořák, Norin, and Postle proved that there is an absolute
constant
\epsilon>0
such that all planar graphs are weighted
\epsilon
-flexible with respect to every

7
-assignment, and they posed as an open problem to show the same for
6
-assignments or even

5
-assignments. While this problem remains wide open, for
7
-assignments the value of
\epsilon
has been
improved considerably from
7^{-36}
to
2^{-7}
by
[
14
]
and
[
2
]
.
As detailed in
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
, every graph
G
is weighted
\frac{1}{\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)}
-flexible
with respect to every
\chi^{\star}_{\ell}(G)
-assignment. Thus a proof of Conjecture
1
will make significant
progress: it will imply that every planar graph (which is
K_{5}
-minor-free) is weighted

\frac{1}{6}
-flexible with respect to every
6
-assignment.

The main goal of this note is to prove the following result, in support of the above conjecture.

Main Theorem
.

Let
G
be a
K_{5}
-minor-free graph. (i) For every correspondence 6-cover
M
of
G
,
there exist disjoint
M
-colorings
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}
. That is, for all
v\in V(G)

the colors
\varphi_{1}(v),\varphi_{2}(v),\varphi_{3}(v)
are pairwise distinct.
(ii) But the analogous statement is false if we replace 6-cover with 5-cover.

The fact that
K_{5}
-minor-free graphs are
5
-degenerate easily implies that they have list-chromatic number
at most
6
, which is close to the optimal bound
5
. However, for the correspondence packing number this
approach performs quite poorly. As discussed above,
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{C})\leqslant 4
due to the
2
-degeneracy of cycles.
More generally, by
A
we have
\chi^{\star}_{c}(G)\leqslant 2d
for every
d
-degenerate graph
G
, and this is
sharp
[
4
, Prop. 24]
for every
d\geqslant 1
, due to large complete bipartite graphs. Therefore a vanilla
degeneracy argument can merely yield
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{5})\leqslant 10
, while it is known that
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{5})\leqslant 8
. This
indicates that establishing Conjecture
1
is nontrivial. On the lower bound side, note that the
second part of our Main Theorem implies that
\chi^{\star}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{5})\geqslant 6
.

1.3
Proof overview

When constructing disjoint list colorings and correspondence colorings, it is often convenient
to proceed by induction. Given a graph
G
, a correspondence
t
-cover
M
of
G

and disjoint partial
M
-colorings
\varphi^{0}_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{0}_{s}
, for each vertex
v

that is not yet colored we seek a color
\varphi_{j}(v)
to extend
\varphi^{0}_{j}
to
v
.
We write
L_{j}(v)
for the subset of
[t]
that is not yet forbidden from use as

\varphi_{j}(v)
, due to the colors already used on
N(v)
and the matchings
M
.
The following lemma, from
[
4
]
, is generally useful for handling vertices of low degree.
Its proof is instructive, so we include it.

Lemma A

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with a correspondence
t
-cover
M
and fix
v\in V(G)
.
If
d(v)\leqslant t/2
, then any disjoint
M
-colorings
\varphi_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\varphi_{s}^{\prime}

of
G-v
extend to disjoint
M
-colorings
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{s}
of
G
.

Proof.

We build an auxiliary bipartite graph
\mathcal{B}
with

as the vertices of the first
part, and
\varphi^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{\prime}_{s}
as the vertices of the second part, and
i\varphi^{\prime}_{j}\in E(\mathcal{B})

if
i\in L_{j}(v)
. To extend the
M
-colorings
\varphi_{j}^{\prime}
, we seek a matching
M_{\mathcal{B}}

saturating the second part. For this we use Hall’s Theorem.

Note that
d_{\mathcal{B}}(i)\geqslant s-d(v)
and
d_{\mathcal{B}}(\varphi^{\prime}_{j})\geqslant t-d(v)
, for all
i\in[t]

and
j\in[s]
. Consider
S\subseteq\{\varphi_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\varphi_{s}^{\prime}\}
. Clearly

|N_{\mathcal{B}}(S)|\geqslant t-d(v)\geqslant t/2
. So if
|S|\leqslant t/2
, then
|N_{\mathcal{B}}(S)|\geqslant|S|

as desired. Otherwise,
|S|>t/2\geqslant d(v)
. So
|S|+d_{\mathcal{B}}(i)\geqslant|S|+s-d(v)>s
because

|S|>d(v)
. So by Pigeonhole
i\in N_{\mathcal{B}}(S)
for all
i\in[t]
. That is,
|N_{\mathcal{B}}(S)|=t\geqslant|S|
, as desired. Thus, by Hall’s Theorem we have the desired matching

in

.
∎

By definition, each matching in

need not be perfect. But to prove the results in this paper, it
suffices to consider the case that all matchings are perfect; if not, then we can grow them greedily.
(In what follows, we implicitly assume that all matchings in

An

-sum

and

by specifying
an

in

and an

-clique

in

, identifying

with

for each

, and possibly deleting some edges of the resulting

.
Wagner
[

characterized the class of maximal

-minor-free graphs as follows.

Theorem B

(
[
21
]
)
.

If a graph

-minor-free, then

can be built by a sequence of
2
-sums and

-sums, without deleting edges, starting from maximal planar graphs and copies of

So to prove our Main Theorem, we would like to reduce to the case of planar graphs and copies of

M_{8}
. However, we must ensure that our disjoint

\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}

agree on the copies of

and
K_{3}

Lemma 2

Given a copy of
M_{8}
and a correspondence 6-cover

of

, there exist disjoint

-colorings
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{6}
. Furthermore, if

-colorings of a specified

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{6}

We use induction on the number

And the induction step holds, because

.
∎

Now we consider planar graphs.

Lemma 3

Let

be a planar graph with a 6-correspondence cover

is a

in

\varphi_{1}^{0},\varphi_{2}^{0},\varphi_{3}^{0}

The key to the proof of Lemma

elegant proof of Carsten Thomassen

,

,

,

,

Key Lemma
.

Let

be a plane near-triangulation, with a correspondence 6-cover

. Denote the vertices
on the outer face of

. There exist disjoint

-colorings
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}
that extend
\varphi_{1}^{0},\varphi_{2}^{0},\varphi_{3}^{0}

\varphi_{1}^{0},\varphi_{2}^{0},\varphi_{3}^{0}

(1)

(2)

For each vertex

.

Proofs

In this section we present proofs of our results. We first prove part (i) of our Main Theorem, by using

2

3

-minor-free graphs). That is, given a

-minor-free graph

-colorings

\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3}
. Next, we prove
3
using our Key Lemma; and after this we prove our
Key Lemma via a “Thomassen-style” induction proof. Finally, we conclude this section by proving part (ii)
of our Main Theorem. That is, we construct a

-minor-free graph

and a correspondence 6-cover

Proof of the Main Theorem.

Fix a

-minor-free graph

and a correspondence 6-cover

. It suffices to consider

-minor-free graph; if not, then we add edges to make it so.

By

can be constructed by a sequence of 2-sums and 3-sums starting from

or a

be the sum of its

, so we are done by
2
or by

Now assume that

. So

,
where

and

that is involved in the sum to form

\varphi_{1}^{0},\varphi_{2}^{0},\varphi_{3}^{0}

Note that if we could prove a stronger version of

We now use the Key Lemma to prove

Our proof is by induction on

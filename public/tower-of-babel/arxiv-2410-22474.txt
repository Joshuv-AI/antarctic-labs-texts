The étale topos reconstructs varieties over sub- p p -adic fields
Magnus Carlson, Jakob Stix

Abstract
. Let K K be a sub- p p -adic field. We show that the functor sending a finite-type K K -scheme to its étale topos is fully faithful after localizing at universal homeomorphisms. This generalizes a result of Voevodsky, who proved the analogous theorem for fields finitely generated over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . We also establish a relative version over normal noetherian Nagata schemes with sub- p p -adic function fields, where we prove that componentwise universally open schemes can be reconstructed from their étale topoi, up to universal homeomorphism. Specializing to Spec ⁡ ( ℤ ) \Spec({\mathbb{Z}}) as the base, this partially verifies a conjecture of Grothendieck. Our proofs rely on Mochizuki’s Hom \Hom -theorem in anabelian geometry, a study of point-theoretic morphisms of fundamental groups of curves, and a topos-theoretic section conjecture of Voevodsky, which we prove.

3.1.
Quasi-sections and point-theoretic maps

Let

be a geometrically connected scheme of finite type
and endow

with a geometric point

compatible with
\overline{K}
. For any finite extension

the group

. We denote by

{\mathscr{S}}_{X/K}(L)\coloneq\{s\colon\Gal_{L}\to\pi_{1}(X,\bar{x})\ ;\text{ over }\Gal_{K}\}/_{\pi_{1}(X_{\bar{K}},\bar{x})}

(i.e., sections only defined on the open subgroup

-rational points to the set of

Remarks 3.1
.

(1)

Any

-rational geometric quasi-section defines a
\pi_{1}(X_{\bar{K}},\bar{x})
-conjugacy class of closed subgroups of
\pi_{1}(X,\bar{x})
as the image of the section. The maximal subgroups among these images are nothing but the (conjugacy classes of) decomposition subgroups associated to closed points of
X
(or rather some choice of universal pro-cover).

(2)

Note that the sets of quasi-sections
{\mathscr{S}}_{X/K}(L)
and
{\mathscr{S}}_{X/K}(\overline{K})
do not depend on the choice of a base point of
X
up to canonical and compatible bijections.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
and

be geometrically connected schemes of finite type over
K
. A homomorphism
\psi\colon\pi_{1}(X,\bar{x})\to\pi_{1}(Y,\bar{y})
over

is called

if composition with

preserves geometric quasi-sections. In other words, for all finite
L/K
inside
\overline{K}
and all points

there is a finite extension

inside
\overline{K}
and a point

such that
\pi_{1}(b)=(\psi\circ\pi_{1}(a))|_{\Gal_{L^{\prime}}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\varphi\colon X_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\rightarrow Y_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
be a pinned morphism of topoi over
K_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}

between two geometrically connected varieties over
K
. Then the induced map

\varphi_{\ast}\colon\pi_{1}(X,\bar{x})\rightarrow\pi_{1}(Y,\bar{y})
is point-theoretic.

Proof.

Indeed, by
Recollection

2.4
, for every finite extension
L/K
and every point

the composite

\varphi\circ a_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\colon(\Spec(L))_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\longrightarrow X_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\longrightarrow Y_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}

\varphi_{\ast}\circ\pi_{1}(a)=(\varphi\circ a_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}})_{\ast}=(b_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}})_{\ast}=\pi_{1}(b).\qed

3.3.
Sections over Kummer-faithful fields

We recall the definition

Definition 3.8
.

A

field in characteristic
0
is a field

of characteristic
0
such that for all semi-abelian varieties

the following equivalent conditions hold.

(1)

The Kummer map

(2)

\bigcap_{n\geq 1}nB(K)=0
is trivial, i.e.,

does not contain arbitrarily divisible elements.

The equivalence is a consequence of the exact sequence of Kummer theory.

[

.

[

[

]

(3)

Weil restriction shows that finite extensions of Kummer-faithful fields are Kummer-faithful.

. Indeed, for every étale sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, the isomorphisms
\sigma_{ij}\cong f_{ij,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
show that we have isomorphisms
(\sigma^{*}\mathcal{F})_{W_{ij}}\cong(f^{*}\mathcal{F})_{W_{ij}}
on
W_{ij,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
. These isomorphisms agree on overlaps since fiberwise pinned geometric morphisms have no nontrivial automorphisms, hence they glue to a natural isomorphism
\sigma^{*}\cong f^{*}
. This implies that
\sigma\cong f_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
.

From now on we can assume that
X=\Spec(A)
and
S=\Spec(B)
are affine.

Step 4:

Since
W\to S
is dominant and quasi-finite, its generic fiber is
W_{K}=\Spec(L)
for some finite extension
L/K
. The restriction of
\sigma
to the generic fiber is pinned by assumption, thus

[
1
, Lemma 3.6 and Proposition 4.5]

show that there exists a unique map
t\colon\Spec(L)\rightarrow X_{K}
such that
t_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\cong\sigma_{K}
as
K
-maps
L_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to X_{K,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
.

Step 5:

Since
W\to S
is quasi-finite, the base-change map

(X\times_{S}W)_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}X_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\times_{S_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}}W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}

is an equivalence in
RTop
by
[
8
, Proposition 6.21]
.
By the universal property, the map
\sigma
lifts to a section
\vartheta\colon W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to(X\times_{S}W)_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
over
W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
. The
L
-point
t
of
X
lifts to an
L
-point also denoted by
t
in

X\times_{S}W(L),

whose image has residue field
L
. We denote by
Z\hookrightarrow X\times_{S}W
the Zariski closure of the image. Since
\vartheta
is continuous, the map
|\vartheta|
factors through
|Z|\subseteq|X\times_{S}W|
. Hence, by
Lemma

5.5
,
\vartheta
factors through
Z_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
. We also denote this geometric morphism by
\vartheta\colon W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to Z_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
.

Step 6:

Let
w\in W
be a point of codimension one and let

be the image
|\vartheta|(w)
. Since
\vartheta
is a section,

projects onto

and the pullback along

induces a local map
{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,z}
. Since

identifies their fraction fields with

{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,z}
is an isomorphism. Thus

extends uniquely as an
S
-map to a neighbourhood of

.

Let

be the maximal Zariski-open to which

extends as an
S
-map

. From what we have just proven,

has codimension at least

in

. Hence,
{\mathcal{O}}_{W}\to j_{\ast}{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
is an isomorphism as

is normal. Because

and

are affine, we obtain

\Hom_{S}(W,X)=\Hom_{B}\big(A,{\mathcal{O}}(W)\big)=\Hom_{B}\big(A,{\mathcal{O}}(U)\big)=\Hom_{S}(U,X).

Therefore the morphism

extends to a unique

-map

.

Step 7:

It remains to see that

agrees with
\sigma
. Both the map

and

are separated because

is affine. Further, the closed subscheme

identifies with the graph of

. Therefore both

and

induce sections of the map

and thus must be isomorphic, since the projection
Z\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}W

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let

be a morphism of finite type over a noetherian scheme

. Let

be a quasi-finite morphism, with

normal, and assume that the function field of every connected component of

\Hom_{S}(W,X)\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}\ \Hom_{S}^{\che}(W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}},X_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}})\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}\Hom_{S}^{\fpin}(W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}},X_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}})

Proof.

Injectivity follows from
Lemma

5.4
. For surjectivity, let
\sigma\colon W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to X_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
be fiberwise pinned. By
[

, Proposition 6.21]
, we obtain a section
\vartheta\colon W_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to(X\times_{S}W)_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
over

. Note that

is fiberwise pinned since

is quasi-finite. Indeed, if

, and

. Since

is fiberwise pinned,

is closed in

. This shows that

is finite, and this implies that the inverse image of

under the projection

is finite over

is closed in

.
Proposition

∎

6.
Étale reconstruction over general base schemes

In this section we will prove
Theorem

D
. The strategy will use
Theorem

B
to obtain a map in the generic fibre and then use normal quasi-sections (see
Definition

6.3
) and
Proposition

5.6
to spread out the map and to control its effect on étale topoi. For that reason we need to impose topological conditions on the base
S
and the map
X\to S
. Namely, we need
S
to be Nagata so that normalization remains of finite type over
S
, and we need
S
geometrically unibranch and
X\to S
to be componentwise universally open (see
Definition

6.1
) so that the normalization
X^{\nu}
admits enough normal quasi-sections over
S
, see
Proposition

6.8
.

6.1.
Componentwise universally open morphisms

Following Grothendieck
[
5
]
, one aspect of anabelian geometry for schemes flat and of finite type over
\Spec({\mathbb{Z}})
studies the functor
étale topos over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}

(-)_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\colon\textbf{{Sch}}^{\ft}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}[\UH^{-1}]\longrightarrow\textbf{{RTop}}^{\che}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}

defined on the localisation by the class of universal homeomorphisms. Our approach over a more general base scheme
S
(not just
{\mathbb{Z}}
) depends on the presence of enough normal quasi-sections
whose generic points have characteristic
0
. Therefore we need to impose an extra topological condition (that for schemes of finite type over
{\mathbb{Z}}
is implied by flatness) that we define next.

Recall that any scheme
X
is the union of its irreducible components: the Zariski closures of points minimal with respect to the partial order of specialisation. The function field of the irreducible component is the residue field of its generic point.

Definition 6.1
.

A morphism
f\colon X\to S
is
componentwise universally open
if for every irreducible component
Z
of
X
the restriction
f|_{Z}\colon Z\to S
is a universally open morphism: it is open and remains an open morphism after arbitrary base change. We denote by

\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}

the category of morphisms
X\to S
of finite type that are componentwise universally open.

Remarks 6.2
.

(1)

Suppose
S
is a Dedekind scheme. A morphism
X\to S
of finite type is componentwise universally open exactly when each irreducible component
Z
of
X
dominates a connected component of
S
. Indeed, then
Z\to S
is flat of finite type and therefore universally open. In particular, any flat scheme of finite type over
S
is componentwise universally open.

(2)

A componentwise universally open morphism
f\colon X\to S
is universally open. Indeed, for any
S^{\prime}\to S
, the base change
X\times_{S}S^{\prime}
is covered by
X_{\alpha}\times_{S}S^{\prime}
, where
X_{\alpha}
ranges over the irreducible components of
X
. Then for every open
U\subseteq X\times_{S}S^{\prime}
its image in
S^{\prime}
is covered by the images of
U\cap(X_{\alpha}\times_{S}S^{\prime})
, which are open by assumption.

(3)

Suppose
X\to S
is of finite type with
X
normal, and that
S
is noetherian. Then
X\to S
is componentwise universally open if and only if it is universally open.

(4)

Let
k
be a field and let
S
be the affine plane
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{k}
. Let
\BL_{0}(S)\to S
be the blow-up of
S
at the origin, and let
X=\BL_{0}(S)\cup_{E}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}
be
\BL_{0}(S)
glued transversally along the exceptional fibre
E\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
with the fibre over the origin of the relative projective line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{S}=S\times_{k}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{k}
. Then
X\to S
is universally open, but not componentwise universally open.

We now define the notion of a quasi-section. Such quasi-sections will be crucial for the proof of
Theorem

6.12
, allowing us to propagate the reconstruction result from the generic fibre to schemes componentwise universally open over
S
.

Definition 6.3
.

A
quasi-section
of a morphism
f\colon X\to S
is an
S
-morphism
q\colon W\to X
such that
W\to S
is quasi-finite and maps generic points of irreducible components of
W
to generic points of irreducible components of
S
. The quasi-section is a
normal quasi-section
if
W
is normal.

We say that
X\to S
has
enough (normal) quasi-sections
if, for every
s\in S
and every closed point
x\in X_{s}
of the fibre, there exists a (normal) quasi-section
q\colon W\to X
such that
x\in q(W)
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Every universally open morphism
f\colon X\to S
of finite type over a noetherian Nagata scheme
S
has enough normal quasi-sections.

Proof.

The normalization map
S^{\nu}\to S
is finite surjective. It therefore suffices to show the claim for the base change
X_{1}=X\times_{S}S^{\nu}\to S^{\nu}
. Composition of enough quasi-sections for
X_{1}\to S^{\nu}
with the projection
X_{1}\to X
produces enough quasi-sections for
X\to S
. Further, by restricting to a connected component of
S^{\nu}
we may assume that
S
is irreducible and geometrically unibranch.

Let
x\in X
be a point that is closed in its fibre
f^{-1}(f(x))
.
Then
[
6
, Proposition 14.5.9]
provides an irreducible locally closed
W_{0}\hookrightarrow X
containing
x
such that the restriction
f|_{W_{0}}\colon W_{0}\to S
is quasi-finite and dominant. Let
W=W_{0}^{\nu}
be the normalization of
W_{0}
and
q\colon W\to W_{0}\to X
the composition. Then
W\to S
is quasi-finite by the assumption that
S
is Nagata, and so
q\colon W\to X
is a normal quasi-section such that
x
is contained in
q(W)
.
∎

By
Remarks

6.2

2
the claim of
Proposition

6.4
applies to componentwise universally open morphisms
X\to S
. This is not enough for our application, because we need to know the same for the normalization
X^{\nu}\to S
. For this transition we need to recall the notion of an equidimensional morphism.

Recollection 6.5
.

A morphism
f\colon X\to S
of finite type between noetherian schemes is
equidimensional
if the local fibrewise dimension function
x\mapsto\dim_{x}(f^{-1}(f(x)))
is locally constant on
X
and each irreducible component of
X
dominates an irreducible component of
S
.

The following is a variant of
[
6
, Proposition 14.5.9]
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be of finite type with
S
a noetherian Nagata scheme that is geometrically unibranch. Consider the following conditions.

(1)

f
is equidimensional.

(2)

f
is universally open.

(3)

f
admits enough normal quasi-sections.

Then the following holds:

\lx@cref{refnum}{propitem:equidim}\Longrightarrow\lx@cref{refnum}{propitem:uop}\Longleftrightarrow\lx@cref{refnum}{propitem:enough-qs}.

If, moreover,
X
is irreducible, then all conditions are equivalent.

Proof.

The claim
1

\Longrightarrow

2
is Chevalley’s criterion
[
6
, Corollaire 14.4.4]
, and the implication

2

\Longrightarrow

3
is a special case of
Proposition

6.4
.

Next we show
3

\Longrightarrow

2
. Let
\psi\colon S^{\prime}\to S
be an arbitrary morphism and
f^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
the base change of
f
. Let
U\subseteq X^{\prime}
be an open, and let
s^{\prime}\in f^{\prime}(U)
be an arbitrary point in the image. The fibre
U\cap f^{\prime-1}(s^{\prime})
is an open in the fibre
X^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}=f^{\prime-1}(s^{\prime})
which is a scalar extension of the fibre
X_{s}=f^{-1}(s)
for the image
s=\psi(s^{\prime})
. It follows that there is a closed point
x^{\prime}\in U\cap f^{\prime-1}(s^{\prime})
that maps to a closed point
x\in X_{s}
. We now choose a normal quasi-section
g\colon W\to X
with
x\in g(W)
and projection
h\colon W\to S
. Let
g^{\prime}\colon W^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
be the base change so that
x^{\prime}\in g^{\prime}(W^{\prime})
, and call the base change of the projection
h^{\prime}\colon W^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
.
Because
h
is equidimensional (of relative dimension
0
), the implication

1

\Rightarrow

2
shows that
h
is universally open. Therefore the set
h^{\prime}(g^{\prime-1}(U))\subseteq S^{\prime}
is open. Since
s^{\prime}\in h^{\prime}(g^{\prime-1}(U))\subseteq f^{\prime}(U)
, we have shown that
f^{\prime}(U)
is open in
S^{\prime}
.

If
X
is irreducible, then being equidimensional is equivalent to condition (c) of
[
6
, Proposition 14.5.9]
which shows that it is equivalent to
f
being universally open.
∎

The example of
Remarks

6.2

4
shows that in
Proposition

6.6
the implication
2

\Rightarrow

1
can fail when
X
is not irreducible.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
S
be a noetherian Nagata scheme, and let
f\colon X\to S
be of finite type and equidimensional. Then the normalization
X^{\nu}
of
X
is of finite type and equidimensional over
S
.

Proof.

Since
S
is Nagata, the normalization
X^{\nu}\to X
is finite and the composition
f_{\nu}\colon X^{\nu}\to S
is of finite type.

We may now restrict
X
to the connected component where the fibrewise dimension function
\dim_{x}(f^{-1}(f(x)))
is constant, say equal to
d
. The normalization of the generic fibres is the normalization of an equidimensional variety of dimension
d
and thus is also equidimensional of dimension
d
. This is the case of a point
S=\Spec(k)
which for example follows from the dimension of an irreducible variety being equal to the transcendence degree of its function field (which does not change under normalization).

By Chevalley’s theorem
[
6
, Théorème 13.1.3]
, the dimension function
\dim_{x}(f_{\nu}^{-1}(f_{\nu}(x)))
is upper semicontinuous, so all irreducible components of all fibres are of dimension at least
d
. Let
Y
be such an irreducible component of the fibre
(X^{\nu})_{s}=f_{\nu}^{-1}(s)
. Then since finite maps do not increase the dimension we have the estimates

d\leq\dim(Y)\leq\dim(X^{\nu})_{s}\leq\dim X_{s}=d.

It follows that
f_{\nu}\colon X^{\nu}\to S
is equidimensional of relative dimension
d
as well.
∎

We can finally prove the existence of enough quasi-sections for the normalization of componentwise universally open
S
-schemes.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
S
be a noetherian Nagata scheme that is geometrically unibranch.
Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism of finite type that is componentwise universally open. Let
U\to X^{\nu}
be a quasi-compact étale open of the normalization
X^{\nu}
of
X
. Then the composition
U\to S
is universally open, admits enough normal quasi-sections, and
U\in\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}
.

In particular
X^{\nu}\to S
admits enough normal quasi-sections and
X^{\nu}\in\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}
.

Proof.

We may replace
X
by an irreducible component and thus assume that
X
is irreducible and
X\to S
is universally open. By
Proposition

6.6
the map
X\to S
is equidimensional. Hence by
Lemma

6.7
the normalization
X^{\nu}
is also equidimensional over
S
. We conclude from
Proposition

6.6
again that
X^{\nu}\to S
is universally open. Since étale maps are also universally open, the same holds for
U\to S
. By
Remarks

6.2

3
this implies
U\in\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}
.
The claim about enough normal quasi-sections follows from
Proposition

6.4
.
∎

6.2.
Statement of the relative anabelian theorem

The localisation
\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}[\UH^{-1}]
can be obtained via a localisation functor to the full subcategory of absolute weakly normal
S
-schemes.

Recollections 6.9
.

(1)

Let
X
be a scheme. Then the category whose objects are universal homeomorphisms
Y\to X
has an initial object

X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X,

the
absolute weak normalization
of
X
, see
[
20
,
Tag 0EUS
]
. The assignment taking
X
to its absolute weak normalization
X^{\text{\rm awn}}
assembles into a functor, which we call the absolute weak normalization functor. Absolute weak normalization is further local for the Zariski topology.

(2)

The absolute weak normalization
X^{\text{\rm awn}}
of a scheme
X
is
absolutely weakly normal
, first defined by Rydh
[
18
, Appendix B]
. We recall the definition although we will mainly use the formal property stated in
1
. An affine scheme
\Spec A
is absolutely weakly normal if
A
is seminormal
[
20
,
Tag 0EUL
]
and, for every prime
p
and all
x,y\in A
such that

p^{p}x=y^{p}
, there exists a unique
a\in A
such that

x=a^{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad y=pa.

An arbitrary scheme
X
is absolutely weakly normal if it is covered by affine opens which are absolutely weakly normal. Since seminormal schemes are reduced
[
20
,
Tag 0EUQ
]
, any absolutely weakly normal scheme is reduced.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
S
be a noetherian Nagata scheme such that the function field of every irreducible component has characteristic zero. Let
X
be a scheme in
\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}
.

(1)

The absolute weak normalization
X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
is a finite universal homeomorphism.
In particular,
X^{\text{\rm awn}}
is a scheme in
\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}
.

(2)

Suppose
X
is further normal. Then
X
is absolutely weakly normal.

Proof.

We may assume that
X
is reduced. Since
X
is componentwise universally open over
S
, the generic point of each irreducible component has characteristic zero. Further, the map
X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
is by assumption a universal homeomorphism, hence the associated residue field extensions on points are purely inseparable. Since the generic points of
X
are of characteristic zero, the residue field extensions on generic points are trivial, hence
X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
is birational. By the universal property of normalization
[
20
,
Tag 035Q
]
, the normalization
X^{\nu}\to X
factors as
X^{\nu}\to X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
. Since
S
is Nagata,
X^{\nu}\to X
is finite, and since
X
is noetherian,
X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
is also finite. This implies that
X^{\text{\rm awn}}
is of finite type over
S
, and being componentwise universally open is inherited from
X
because
X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
is a universal homeomorphism.

For the second part, note that by (1), the map
X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
is finite. Since
X
is normal and componentwise universally open, the generic points of its irreducible components have characteristic zero. Since
X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
is universally injective, the residue field extensions obtained at the generic points are purely inseparable, hence trivial. This shows that
X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X
is finite and birational, hence an isomorphism since
X
is normal. ∎

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
S
be a noetherian Nagata scheme such that the function field of every irreducible component has characteristic zero. Then absolute weak normalization yields a localizing equivalence

(-)^{\text{\rm awn}}\colon\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}[\UH^{-1}]\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen,\text{\rm awn}}_{S}

of the localisation by universal homeomorphisms to the category of componentwise universally open
X\to S
such that
X
is absolutely weakly normal.

Proof.

We proved in
Lemma

6.10
that
(-)^{\text{\rm awn}}
induces a functor

\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}\longrightarrow\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen,\text{\rm awn}}_{S}.

This is a right adjoint to the inclusion and thus localizing for those
S
-morphisms
h:X\to S
such that
h^{\text{\rm awn}}
is an isomorphism. These are exactly the universal homeomorphisms as explained in

[
1
, Theorem 2.7.3]
.
∎

Proposition

6.11
(and
Lemma

6.10
for the claim that normal implies absolutely weakly normal) reduces the proof of
Theorem

D
and
Corollary

E
to the following.

Theorem 6.12
.

Let
S
be a noetherian Nagata scheme that is geometrically unibranch and such that the function field of every irreducible component is a sub-
p
-adic field.
Let
X,Y
be schemes in
\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}
. Then the étale topos functor induces a bijection

\Hom_{S}(X^{\text{\rm awn}},Y)\xlongrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}\,}\Hom^{\che}_{S}(X^{\text{\rm awn}}_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}},Y_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}).

Remark 6.13
.

In
Theorem

6.12
it is necessary to work with chivalrous morphisms because there are

of

-schemes as in the theorem for which

is not pinned. More concretely, if

is the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring, the projection

sends the closed point defined by the ideal

, with a standard coordinate

of positive valuation, to the generic point of

\pr_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\colon{\mathbb{A}}_{S,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}^{1}\to S_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
is not pinned. However, any map of finite-type schemes sends constructible sets to constructible sets by Chevalley’s Theorem
[

,
Tag 054K
]
.

We will prove
Theorem

in
§​

6.3
after having reduced the claim to the case of normal schemes

by the following lemma.

Lemma 6.14
.

For the proof of

6.12
we may suppose that

is normal.

Proof.

Let
\nu\colon X^{\nu}\to X
be the normalization map which factors as
\smash{X^{\nu}\xrightarrow{\omega}X^{\text{\rm awn}}\to X}
. The morphism

is an

-covering, and

lies in
\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}
by

. We set

for the reduced subscheme of the fibre product, let

6.3.
Spreading out maps and detecting maps between étale topoi

Proposition 6.15
.

Let
S
be a noetherian Nagata scheme that is geometrically unibranch and such that the function field of every irreducible component is a sub-
p
-adic field. Let
X,Y
be schemes of finite type over
S
and assume
X
is normal and that
X\in\textbf{{Sch}}^{\univopen}_{S}
.
Let
\varphi\colon X_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to Y_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
be a fibrewise pinned morphism over
S_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
. Then there is a unique
S
-morphism
f\colon X\to Y
such that for all normal quasi-sections
q\colon W\to X
we have equality

f_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\circ q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=\varphi\circ q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}.

Proof.

Denote by
S_{0}
the disjoint union of all generic points of
S
, and the base change with
S_{0}\to S
by
(-)_{0}
.

Step 1:

We
first show the uniqueness of the claimed
S
-morphism
f\colon X\to Y
. Indeed, since
X_{0}\subseteq X
is schematically dense, the map
f
is determined by the restriction
f_{0}\colon X_{0}\to Y_{0}
and in fact by the restriction to all closed points
z\in X_{0}
. Let
Z_{z}\subseteq X
be the Zariski closure of
z
in
X
. Then a suitable open
W_{z}\subseteq Z_{z}
determines a normal quasi-section
q_{z}\colon W_{z}\to X
over
S
which contains
z
in its image. Then
f_{0}|_{z}=(f\circ q_{z})|_{z}
is determined uniquely by

(f\circ q_{z})|_{z,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=(f_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\circ q_{z,\text{\rm\'{e}t}})|_{z_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}}=(\varphi\circ q_{z,\text{\rm\'{e}t}})|_{z_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}}.

Hence
\varphi
determines
f
uniquely if it exists.

Step 2:

Now we assume the proposition is proven with some
f\colon X\to Y
for some
\varphi\colon X_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to Y_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
and claim that for any Zariski open
V\subseteq Y
the claim also follows for the restriction
\varphi_{V}\colon\varphi^{\ast}V_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to V_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
, more precisely, that
f
restricts to a map
f_{V}\colon U=\varphi^{\ast}V\to V
that satisfies the claim of the proposition.
Indeed, for every normal quasi-section
q\colon W\to U
we compute the preimage
(f\circ q)^{-1}(V)
as

(f\circ q)_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}^{\ast}(V)=(\varphi\circ q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}})^{\ast}(V)=q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}^{\ast}(U)=W,

so that
f\circ q
factors over the inclusion
j_{V}\colon V\to Y
. Since
U\to S
has enough normal quasi-sections, this shows that
f
maps all closed points of its fibres over
S
into
V
. Since
V
is open,

f
restricts to an
S
-morphism
f_{V}\colon U\to V
. Denote by
j_{U}\colon U\to X
the inclusion. For any normal quasi-section
q\colon W\to U
over
S
we compute

j_{V,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\circ(f_{V}\circ q)_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=(j_{V}\circ f_{V}\circ q)_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=(f\circ(j_{U}\circ q))_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=\varphi\circ(j_{U}\circ q)_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=j_{V,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\circ\varphi_{V}\circ q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}.

This shows the claim of step 2, because we may cancel
j_{V,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
by
Lemma

5.5
.

Step 3:
In the next step we reduce to when
Y
and
S
are affine. Let
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an affine open covering of
S
, and for each
i
, let
\{V_{ij}\}_{j\in J_{i}}
be an affine open covering of
Y_{i}=Y\times_{S}S_{i}
. This induces a Zariski open covering of
X
by
U_{ij}=\varphi^{*}(V_{ij})\subseteq X
. Note that the induced morphisms

\varphi_{ij}\colon U_{ij,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to V_{ij,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}

are fibrewise pinned by
Remark

5.3
, and that the
U_{ij}
are normal and universally open over
S
. If the statement holds in the affine case (target and base affine), then we obtain maps
f_{ij}\colon U_{ij}\to V_{ij}
. By step 1 and 2 these maps
f_{ij}
agree on overlaps and thus glue to an
S
-map
f\colon X\to Y
.

We still need to show that this map
f
satisfies the claim of the proposition with respect to
\varphi
and all normal quasi-sections
q\colon W\to X
over
S
. But the claimed equality
f_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\circ q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=\varphi\circ q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
is étale local on
Y_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
and thus can be verified by passing to an étale covering, where it holds by construction of
f
.

Hence we may, and do, assume that
Y
and
S
are affine.

Step 4:
By
[
1
, Lemma 3.6]
base change of
\varphi
induces a pinned
S_{0}
-morphism

\varphi_{0}\colon X_{0,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\to Y_{0,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}.

By
Theorem

B
, applied to connected components, there exists a unique
S_{0}
-morphism
f_{0}\colon X_{0}\to Y_{0}
with
f_{0,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=\varphi_{0}
.
Suppose now that
q\colon W\to X
is a normal quasi-section over
S
. Since the composite
\varphi\circ q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}
is fibrewise pinned,

Corollary

5.7
shows that it arises from a unique map of
S
-schemes

. Passing to the generic fibres, we obtain equalities

(f_{0}\circ q_{0})_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=f_{0,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}\circ q_{0,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=\varphi_{0}\circ q_{0,\text{\rm\'{e}t}}=(\varphi\circ q_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}})_{0}=(f_{q,\text{\rm\'{e}t}})_{0}=(f_{q,0})_{\text{\rm\'{e}t}}.

Lemma

5.4
shows that

and
f_{q,0}
agree. If
f_{0}
extends to an
S

, then, since

is dense in

, this implies

. Consequently, then

It thus suffices to show that

extends to an

Step 5:

Let

be the Zariski closure of the graph of

, and let

denote the projection. For every normal quasi-section

the map

that extends the map

shows that

is contained in the image of
\sigma
. Since

, the image of

. Since

is constructible, the map

is quasi-finite is open
and contains the fibres above all points of

is birational and surjective. Then

implies that there exists an open

such that

such that

is normal,

, and since

are affine, it follows that

extends uniquely to an

-morphism

.
∎

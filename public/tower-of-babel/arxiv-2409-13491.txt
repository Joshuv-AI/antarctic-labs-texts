Abstract




Abstract:
We say that a graph
G
on
n

\{H,F\}
-
o
-heavy if every induced subgraph of
G
isomorphic to
H
or
F
contains two nonadjacent vertices with degree sum at least

R
and

of order at least 3 other than

such that every 2-connected
\{R,S\}
-
o
-heavy graph is hamiltonian. In particular, they showed that, up to symmetry,
R
must be a claw and
S\in\{P_{4},P_{5},C_{3},Z_{1},Z_{2},B,N,W\}
. In 2008, Čada extended Ryjáček’s closure concept for claw-free graphs by introducing what we call the
c
-closure for claw-

-heavy graphs. We apply it here to characterize the structure of the
c
-closure of 2-connected
\{R,S\}
-

-heavy graphs, where
R
and

Introduction

We consider finite simple undirected graphs only, and for
terminology and notation not defined here we refer the reader to
[
4
]
.

Our research is motivated by a long list of earlier work regarding different types of sufficient conditions for hamiltonicity of graphs, i.e., for the existence of a cycle containing every vertex of the graph. The earliest conditions in this area go back to the 1950s, when the first degree conditions were established. This was later followed by forbidden induced subgraph conditions, an active area since the 1980s. More recently, several authors have considered combining the two types of conditions by imposing degree conditions on induced subgraphs instead of forbidding them. In another direction, degree conditions and forbidden subgraph conditions were the motivation to introduce closure concepts for hamiltonicity. These concepts aim to allow the addition of edges to the graph without affecting the (non)hamiltonicity. We will see examples of all of the above types of results in the sequel. However, we will refrain from an exhaustive overview due to the length of the paper.

Let
G
be a graph. For a vertex

, we use
N_{G}(v)
(the
neighborhood
of

(the

of

) for the number, of neighbors of

in

. For a subgraph

of

. We skip the subscript

We begin with the following classic sufficient degree condition for hamiltonian graphs.

Theorem 1.2

(Bondy and Chvátal

A graph

We now turn to a common relaxation of degree conditions and forbidden subgraph conditions. Instead of imposing degree conditions on all nonadjacent pairs of vertices or forbidding certain subgraphs, the idea is to impose degree conditions on some nonadjacent pairs of vertices of such subgraphs if they appear as induced subgraphs in the graph. Before we can give more details, we first need some additional terminology and notation.

Let
G
be a graph. We say that a vertex
u\in V(G)
is

in

if
d(u)\geq\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
; a pair of vertices

is an
a
-heavy pair
(or

) of

if

and

. Note that if

is an

-heavy or
a
-heavy pair, then at least one of

and

is a heavy vertex. Also note that Ore’s Theorem (Theorem
1.1

is

and every pair of nonadjacent vertices of

is an

-heavy pair.

Let

be an induced subgraph of

. If

contains an

-heavy pair of

is an

-heavy subgraph
of

(or

is

-heavy

). For a given graph

-heavy in

. Note that an

-

-heavy, and if

is an induced subgraph of

-

-heavy. For a family

of graphs,

-heavy for every

.

Motivated by Ryjáček’s closure theory for claw-free graphs, Čada

Some preliminaries

In this section, we recall the two closure theories developed by Ryjáček and Čada, respectively (which we call the

2.1
Closures for claw-free and claw-
o
-heavy graphs

During a workshop that was held in hotel the Hölterhof near Enschede in 1995
[
2
]
, Ryjáček came up with a closure concept for claw-free graphs which became known as Ryjáček’s closure. The details and proofs were later published in
[
13
]
. We give a short summary for later reference.

For this purpose, a vertex
x
of a graph
G
is called
r-eligible
in
G
if
x
has a connected non-complete neighborhood in
G
. The
local
r
-completion of
G
at
x
, denoted by
G^{\prime}_{x}
, is the graph with vertex set

and edge set

E(G)\cup\{uv:u,v\in N(x)\}
. It was proved in
[
13
]
that if

is claw-free, then so is
G^{\prime}_{x}
, and if

is
r
-eligible, then

is hamiltonian if and only if
G^{\prime}_{x}
is hamiltonian. The
r
-closure
of a claw-free graph

, denoted by
{\rm cl}^{\rm r}(G)
, is the graph obtained by recursively performing the local
r
-completion
operation at
r
-eligible vertices as long as this is possible.

Ryjáček
[
13
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 2.1

(Ryjáček
[
13
]
)
.

Let

be a claw-free graph. Then:

(1)
{\rm cl}^{\rm r}(G)
is uniquely determined;

(2)
{\rm cl}^{\rm r}(G)
is the line graph of a
C_{3}
-free graph; and

(3)
{\rm cl}^{\rm r}(G)
is hamiltonian if and only if

is hamiltonian.

Motivated by Ryjáček’s closure theory for claw-free graphs,
and inspired by closure concepts in
[

]
and

proposed a closure theory for claw-

-heavy

-heavy graph on

. Following
[
7
]

. Let

be the graph with vertex set

and edge set

. Now we call

-eligible

is not a clique in

and one of the followings is true:

(i)

(ii)

is the disjoint union of two cliques

and

, and

is contained in an
o

of

such

and

.

Let

be a

-eligible vertex of

. Then the graph

with vertex set

The closure concept of Čada is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(Čada
[

)

Let

-heavy graph, and let

-eligible vertex of

(1) for every vertex

(2) the graph

is claw-

-heavy; and

(3)

is hamiltonian if and only if
G_{x}^{o}
is hamiltonian.

Based on this lemma, the

-closure
of a claw-

-heavy graph

, denoted by

{\rm cl}^{\rm c}(G)
, is the graph obtained by recursively performing the local

-completion operation at

is defined by a sequence of graphs

, and vertices

such that:

,

(ii)

-eligible vertex of

Čada

Theorem 2.2

(Čada

Let

(1)

(2)

Clearly, (2) implies that the

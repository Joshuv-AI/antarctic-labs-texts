Introduction

In the present work we consider the two dimensional Keller-Segel model

The system (1.1) is used to model chemotaxis, a biological phenomenon describing the motion of a population density, u(x, t), in the presence of an external chemical stimulus, v(x, t), called chemoattractant. There is a huge literature on chemotaxis in biology and mathematics. This model was introduced by Keller-Segel [48] to describe the motion of the slime mold amoebae Dictyostelium discoideum (see also [63] for an earlier model proposed by Patlak and the later works [47, 49, 50]). Under certain circumstances, these cells secret the chemoattractant themselves. This self-organization process is considered a fundamental mechanism in biology. The two dimensional case is clearly of special interest in biology but, as we will show, also from a mathematical point of view because of the scaling properties of (1.1). In their seminal work [45], Jäger-Luckhaus derived the parabolic-elliptic system (1.1) and showed that solutions globally exist if the mass M is small and blow-up in finite time if M is large. More recently this first result has been improved.

If M<8π the second moment grows linearly and it can be proved that the solution exists for all time and diffuses to zero with a self-similar profile [7, 31].

When M=8π there exist globally radially symmetric solutions to (1.1) for initial data with finite or infinite second moment [3, 4].

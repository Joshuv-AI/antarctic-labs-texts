1.
Introduction

1.1.
The MEMS Model

Micro-electro-mechanical systems (MEMS) are miniature devices that integrate mechanical and electrical components on a micrometer scale. Typical applications include devices such as pressure sensors, accelerometers, microactuators, RF switches, and optical mirrors. MEMS devices often consist of a rigid ground plate and an elastic membrane suspended above it at an initial distance
d>0
. The distance between them over time, referred to as the
deflection
, can be modeled by partial differential equations under suitable assumptions. When the membrane is thin and the initial distance is small compared to the overall size of the device, one arrives at the following MEMS model (see
[
19
]
for a derivation):

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta u_{tt}+u_{t}-\Delta u+\delta\Delta^{2}u=\dfrac{-\lambda}{u^{2}\left(1+\alpha\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{u}\,dx\right)^{2}}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u=1\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
0<u_{0}(x)\leq 1,\end{cases}

Here,
u(x,t)
denotes the deflection of the membrane. The parameter
\beta>0
measures the thickness of the membrane,
\delta>0
quantifies the relative importance of tension and rigidity,
\lambda>0
is proportional to the applied voltage, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain. The boundary condition
u=1\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
reflects a fixed edge for the membrane. For more physical background and possible applications, we refer the reader to
[
17
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
, and
[
26
]
.

The model
1.1
is rich from a mathematical point of view. Taking limits of the parameters,
\beta,\delta
completely changes the type of the equation: from a second-order parabolic (
\beta,\delta\ll 1
) to a
fourth-order parabolic (
\beta\ll 1,\delta\gg 1
), or from parabolic (
\beta\ll 1
) to hyperbolic
(\beta\gg 1)
. In this work, we consider the case where
\Omega=\mathcal{B}
, the open unit disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and set the parameters,
\lambda=1
,
\alpha=0
, while letting
\beta\to 0
and
\delta\to 0
, for which the original model reads as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\dfrac{1}{u^{2}}&\text{in }\mathcal{B}\times(0,T),\\
u=1&\text{on }\partial\mathcal{B}\times(0,T),\\
0<u_{0}(x)\leq 1.&\end{cases}

According to
[
19
]
, a function
u
is said to be a classical solution of (
1.2
) if
u\in C^{2,1}(\Omega\times(0,T))
and
0<u\leq 1
. Moreover, the local Cauchy problem for (1.2) is solved, and the solution either exists globally or quenches in finite time
T>0
, meaning

\liminf_{t\to T}\left(\min_{x\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}}u(x,t)\right)=0.

Quenching corresponds physically to the moment when the elastic membrane contacts the ground plate, often referred to as
touchdown
phenomena. Once this occurs, the MEMS device typically fails or becomes non-functional.

Quenching solutions of the MEMS model have received significant attention over the past few decades, particularly in the parabolic case, obtained by taking
\beta\to 0
and
\delta\to 0
in (
1.1
). For the local problem (
\alpha=0
), Deng and Levine
[
5
]
proved that the set of quenching points is contained within a compact subset of
\Omega
, provided that
\Omega
is convex. Moreover, in one spatial dimension, the quenching set is finite under suitable assumptions; see
[
13
]
for further details. For the one-dimensional nonlocal problem (
\alpha=1
), Guo, Hu, and Wang
[
15
]
showed that for specific initial data and sufficiently large
\lambda
, the solution quenches only at the origin and forms a cusp at the quenching point without a precise quenching rate. In higher dimensions, Guo and Kavallaris
[
16
]
demonstrated that quenching occurs provided the initial energy is sufficiently small. Explicit profiles for quenching solutions have been relatively scarce in the literature, apart from the works of Merle and Zaag
[
23
]
, Filippas and Guo
[
9
]
, Duong and Zaag
[
6
]
. In this work, we aim to construct a truly non-radial quenching solution to (
1.2
) with a precise description of its behavior near the singularity.

1.2.
Main Theorem

The following is a brief version of the main theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Rough statement of the main theorem)
.

There is smooth initial data
0<u_{0}\leq 1
such that the corresponding solution
u(x,t)
to (
1.2
) quenches in finite time
T>0
only at the origin and admits the quenching rate

(1.3)

u(0,t)=\min_{x\in\bar{\mathcal{B}}}u(x,t)\sim(T-t)^{\frac{1}{3}},\quad\textup{as}\;\;t\to T,

and the nonradial final profile

(1.4)

u(x,T)\sim\left(x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}+\theta(x_{1}^{6}+x_{2}^{6})\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},\quad\textup{as}\;\;|x|\to 0,

where
\theta\in(0,\theta^{*})
for some
\theta^{*}>0
.

To better restate the main theorem in detail, we introduce the self-similar variables,

(1.5)

\displaystyle u(x,t)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{3}}\;w(y,s),\quad y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\log(T-t),

which rescales the solution near the quenching time
T
. In these variables, we define the quenching profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
(see section
2
for a detailed derivation) by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)=\Psi_{\theta}(z,s)\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{\frac{1}{4}\kappa(s)}(z)+\mathcal{C}(y,s)\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(z)+e^{\frac{s}{3}}\left(1-\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{\frac{1}{4}\kappa(s)}(z)\right),\quad z=ye^{-\frac{s}{4}},

where
\theta\in(0,\theta^{*})
for some
\theta^{*}>0
,
K>0
a large fixed constant,
\kappa(s)=e^{\frac{s}{4}}
,
\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}
and
\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{\kappa(s)}
are defined as in
1.30
and
\Psi_{\theta}
is the main non-radial profile

\displaystyle\Psi_{\theta}(z,s)=\left(3+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-s/2}(z_{1}^{6}+z_{2}^{6})\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Moreover, the correction
\mathcal{C}(y,s)
takes the form

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{C}(y,s)=\ &\frac{\bar{c}}{9}e^{-s}(-2y_{1}^{2}-2y_{2}^{2}+4)\\
&+e^{-2s}\Bigg[\frac{64}{81\bar{c}}h_{0}h_{0}+\frac{32}{27\bar{c}}(h_{0}h_{2}+h_{2}h_{0})+\frac{64}{27\bar{c}}h_{2}h_{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{8}{27\bar{c}}s\,(h_{2}h_{4}+h_{4}h_{2})+\frac{8}{27\bar{c}}(h_{0}h_{4}+h_{4}h_{0})\\
&\quad+\frac{\bar{c}}{9}\delta(-30y_{1}^{4}+180y_{1}^{2}-120-30y_{2}^{4}+180y_{2}^{2}-120)\\
&\quad-\frac{2}{54\bar{c}}\left(-12y_{1}^{4}y_{2}^{2}+12y_{1}^{4}-12y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{4}+144y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}-144y_{1}^{2}+12y_{2}^{4}-144y_{2}^{2}+144\right)\Bigg],\end{split}

where
\bar{c}=3^{\frac{1}{3}}
and
h_{m}(\xi)
denotes the rescaled Hermite polynomials,

(1.8)

\displaystyle h_{m}(\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}\frac{m!}{n!(m-2n)!}(-1)^{n}\xi^{m-2n}.

The following is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2

(Existence of quenching solutions to (
1.2
) with a detailed asymptotic description)
.

There is smooth initial data
u_{0}
with

0<u_{0}\leq 1
, such that the corresponding solution of (
1.2
) quenches only at the origin in finite time
T>0
, with the nonradial quenching profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
. Moreover, the solution
u
admits the following asymptotic behavior:

(i) (Inner expansion)

(1.9)

\displaystyle w(y,s)-\left(\left(3+e^{-s}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-2s}(y_{1}^{6}+y_{2}^{6})\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}+\mathcal{C}(y,s)\right)=O(e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}),

uniformly in compact sets
\{|y|\leq R\}
for
R>0
arbitrary, where
\theta\in(0,\theta^{*})
for some
\theta^{*}>0
.

(ii) (Intermediate expansion)

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{K\leq|y|\leq e^{\frac{s}{4}}}\left|\frac{w(y,s)-\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)}{|y|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\right|=O(e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}),\quad\text{where }\alpha=18,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{e^{\frac{s}{4}}\leq|y|\leq e^{\frac{69}{196}s}}\left|\frac{w(y,s)-\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)}{|y|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\right|=O(e^{-(\frac{5}{12}+\frac{\gamma}{8})s}),\quad\text{where }\gamma=8.01

and
K\gg 1
is a large fixed constant.

(iii) (Outer expansion)

(1.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{e^{\frac{69}{196}s}\leq|y|\leq e^{\frac{s}{2}}}\left|\frac{w(y,s)-\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)}\right|<1.

(iv) (Final profile) There is
u^{*}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
such that
u(x,t)\to u^{*}(x)
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
as
t\to T
. Moreover,

(1.13)

\displaystyle u^{*}(x)\sim\left(x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}+\theta(x_{1}^{6}+x_{2}^{6})\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\quad\text{ as }\quad|x|\to 0.

Remark 1.2
.

The correction term
\mathcal{C}(y,s)
in the definition of

Remark 1.3
.

To our knowledge, Theorem
1.2
provides a first example of a quenching solution that admits a non-radial profile, given that all existing constructed solutions to
1.2
only display radial profiles. For example, in the one-dimensional parabolic local model, Filippas and Guo
[
9
]
, Merle and Zaag
[
23
]
demonstrated that for certain initial data, the corresponding solution quenches solely at the origin with the following asymptotic quenching profile:

(1.14)

\displaystyle u(x,T)\sim C\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{|\ln{|x|}|}}\right)^{2/3},\quad\text{as }|x|\to 0,\quad C>0.

The same final profile holds in higher-dimensional cases with a nonlocal term constructed in
[
7
]
, where the constant
C
is precisely computed.

Remark 1.4
.

(Comparison with the semi-linear heat equation)
Quenching solutions for the MEMS model (
1.2
) share several common features with blowup solutions for the semi-linear heat equation:

(1.15)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u,\quad p\geq 1.

Indeed, once we make the nonlinear trasformation
v=u^{-2}
, the equation (
1.2
) becomes

(1.16)

\partial_{t}v=\Delta v+av^{4}-b\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\quad\textup{with}\;\;a=\frac{1}{2},\;\;b=3.

This is the nonlinear heat (
1.15
) with an extra transport term
\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}=\nabla\ln v.\nabla v
. This is a problematic term that complicates the analysis due to a possible vanishing of
v
. This is one of the reasons we decided to work on the original form (
1.2
).

In the literature, most constructed solutions for the semi-linear heat equation have radial profiles. For solutions with non-radial profiles, much less is known. Notable examples of such non-radial blowup solutions to (
1.15
) appear in the work of Del Pino, Musso, Wei and Zheng
[
4
]
build up on an explicit construction of sign-changing non-radial solutions to the associated elliptic problem in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, and the work of Merle and Zaag
[
24
]
aligned with the classification results by Velázquez
[
27
]
(see also the earlier results in
[
18
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
), and the anisotropic blowup constructed by Collot, Merle, and Raphaël
[
1
]
.

Our construction of a non-radial solution for the MEMS model is inspired by the work of Merle and Zaag
[
24
]
, where they constructed an explicit example of blowing up solutions to (
1.15
) with a truly non-radial profile. In particular, their analysis relied on a Liouville-type theorem to control
\nabla\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
is the perturbation of the solution from the blow-up profile. In our proof, instead of using a Liouville-type theorem, we use a purely energy estimate adapted from the work of Nguyen, Nouaili, and Zaag
[
25
]
to archive the control of
(y\cdot\nabla)^{k}\varepsilon
,
k=0,1,2
, in suitable weighted
H^{2}
spaces. This, combined with Sobolev inequalities, enables us to obtain the
L^{\infty}
bound of
\varepsilon
. We note that by controlling gradient terms in suitable weighted spaces, one can obtain a more direct approach to estimating these terms. This leads to a simplification of the analysis compared to the method used in
[
24
]
. More details are given in the next subsection
1.3
and subsection
5.2
.

Remark 1.5

(Potential Type-II quenching solutions)
.

In this paper, the constructed solution is of Type I singularity in the sense that the quenching solution
u
to equation (
1.2
) satisfies

\liminf_{t\to T}\left(\frac{\min_{x\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}}u(x,t)}{(T-t)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right)\geq C,\quad\text{for some }C>0.

Otherwise,
u
is said to develop a Type II singularity. Recall that the original equation (
1.2
) is transformed to (
1.16
) with an extra transport term of the form
\nabla\ln v.\nabla v
, which potentially creates more interesting blowup dynamics in the solution
v
, and for which we expect that equation (
1.2
) would possibly exhibit Type II singularities.

1.3.
Strategy of analysis

We outline the ideas of the proof of Theorem
1.2
, divided in several steps:

– Renormalization in self-similar variables:
Under the transform
1.5
,

w
satisfies the self-similar equation,

(1.17)

\displaystyle w_{s}=\Delta w-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla w+\frac{1}{3}w-\frac{1}{w^{2}},

(1.18)

\displaystyle w(y,s)\equiv e^{\frac{1}{3}s},\quad\text{ on }|y|=e^{\frac{1}{2}s}.

In this setting, the construction of the quenching profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
is performed through a spectral analysis. We remark that the profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
is connected to the trivial constant solution
\bar{w}=3^{\frac{1}{3}}
in the compact set of
y
-variable. A linearization of (
1.17
) around
\bar{w}
yields a linearized problem driven by the linear Hermite operator
\mathcal{L}=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla+I
, whose spectral properties are well established. A further refinement leads to a precise approximate solution including the correction
\mathcal{C}(y,s)
introduced in (
1.7
), from which we can determine the profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
, see Section
2
for a detailed derivation.

– Linearization:
Having derived the good approximate profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
in the self-similar variables, we want to rigorously prove that the true solution to (
1.17
) remains close to this profile under small perturbations in some appropriate functional setting. We introduce

(1.19)

w(y,s)=\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)+\varepsilon(y,s),

which leads to the linearized evolution equation for
\varepsilon
:

(1.20)

\partial_{s}\varepsilon=\mathcal{H}\varepsilon+E+NL(\varepsilon),

where
\mathcal{H}
is the linearized operator given by

\mathcal{H}\varepsilon=\Delta\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon+\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon,

E
is the error generated by the profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
, and
NL(\varepsilon)
the non-linear term.

– Decomposition and control of perturbation
\varepsilon(y,s)
:
Our aim is to show that
\|\varepsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\to 0
as
s\to\infty
, thus, we introduce the following:

\displaystyle\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)=\varepsilon(y,s)\cdot\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(z),\quad\eta^{ex}(z,s)=\eta(z,s)\cdot(1-\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(ze^{-\frac{5}{49}s})),\quad z=ye^{-\frac{s}{4}},

where
\eta(z,s)=\varepsilon(y,s)
and use them to control the perturbation
\varepsilon
.

•

Control of
\varepsilon
in compact sets:
We further decompose
\hat{\varepsilon}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)=\sum_{i+j\leq 8}\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s)H_{ij}(y)+\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(y,s),

where
H_{ij}
is the eigenfunction of the self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}:H^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\subset L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

(1.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}(y\cdot\nabla)+I,

and
L^{2}_{\rho}
is the weighted
L^{2}
-space defined in (
1.31
), and corresponds to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{ij}=1-\frac{i+j}{2}
, i.e.

(1.22)

\mathcal{L}(H_{ij})=\left(1-\frac{i+j}{2}\right)H_{ij},\quad H_{ij}(y)=h_{i}(y_{1})h_{j}(y_{2}),

with
h_{j}
being Hermite polynomials defined by
1.8
. In particular, the family
\{H_{ij}:i,j=0,1,2,\dots\}
forms an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and the univariate polynomials satisfy the orthogonality relation

(1.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{n}(\xi)h_{m}(\xi)e^{-\xi^{2}/4}\,d\xi=2^{n}n!\delta_{nm}.

For
y
in compact sets, the equation of
\hat{\varepsilon}
roughly reads as

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\hat{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{L}\hat{\varepsilon}+O(se^{-3s}+|\hat{\varepsilon}|^{2}),\quad\text{ in }L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Thanks to the spectral gap

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{L}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-},\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}\right>_{\rho}\leq-\frac{7}{2}\left<\hat{\varepsilon}_{-},\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}\right>_{\rho},

we obtain the energy estimate (see Lemma
5.3
)

(1.24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim-\frac{7}{2}\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+e^{-\frac{32}{6}s}.

For the finite-dimensional part, projecting the equation onto the eigenfunctions
H_{ij}
yields the following ODEs for the coefficients

\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}
(see Lemma
5.4
):

\displaystyle\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{\prime}(s)=\left(1-\frac{i+j}{2}\right)\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s)+O(e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}).

In particular, for
i+j=8
, we have

(1.25)

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{\prime}(s)+3\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}.

A forward integration in time of (
1.24
) and (
1.25
) yields the estimates:

(1.26)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\qquad|\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad i+j=8.

For the remaining modes with
i+j\leq 7
, we control them by a topological argument where we specify initial data (see (
3.23
) for a definition) so that these components converge to
0
as
s\to\infty
with the bound

(1.27)

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad i+j\leq 7.

Finally, estimates
1.26
and
1.27
combined with a parabolic regularity, yield control of
\varepsilon
over compact sets
\{|y|\leq R\}
for
R>0
arbitrary.

•

Control of
\varepsilon
in
1\lesssim|y|\lesssim e^{\frac{69}{196}s}
(equivalently,
e^{-s/4}\lesssim|z|\leq e^{\frac{5}{49}s}
). In this region, thanks to the dissipative structure of the parabolic equation (
1.20
), we can derive the following energy estimates (see Lemmas
5.8
-
5.10
, and Lemma
5.13
):

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|(y\cdot\nabla)^{k}\varepsilon\|_{\flat}^{2}\lesssim-\frac{7}{2}\|(y\cdot\nabla)^{k}\varepsilon\|^{2}_{\flat}+O(e^{-\frac{32}{6}s}),\quad k=0,1,2,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|(z\cdot\nabla)^{k}\eta\|_{\natural}^{2}\lesssim-\frac{1}{2}\|(z\cdot\nabla)^{k}\eta\|^{2}_{\natural}+O(e^{-\frac{5}{6}s}),\quad k=0,1,2,\quad z=ye^{-s/4}.

where,
\eta(z,s)=\varepsilon(y,s)
. Moreover, the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\flat}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\natural}
are given by

(1.28)

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon\|_{\flat}=\left(\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}\right)^{1/2},\quad\alpha=18.

(1.29)

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\natural}=\left(\int_{|z|\geq K}\frac{|\eta|^{2}}{|z|^{\gamma}}\,\frac{dz}{|z|^{2}}\right)^{1/2},\quad\gamma=8.01,

where
K\gg 1
is a large fixed constant. Integration in time combined with the Sobolev inequality yields

\displaystyle\sup_{|y|\geq K}|\langle y\rangle^{-9}\varepsilon(y,s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad\sup_{|z|\geq K}|\langle z\rangle^{-4.005}\eta(z,s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{5}{12}s},

which controls the pertabation
\varepsilon
in the region
1\lesssim|y|\lesssim e^{\frac{69}{196}s}
.

•

Control of
\varepsilon
in
|z|\gtrsim e^{\frac{5}{49}s}
. We control
\varepsilon
in this region by controling
\eta^{ex}(z,s)
, where
z=ye^{-\frac{s}{4}}
. By estimating both the generated error and the nonlinear term in
1.20
, we obtain an evolution equation for
\eta^{ex}
and derive the bound

\displaystyle|\eta^{ex}(z,s)|\lesssim e^{-s/6}(1-e^{-\epsilon s})(1-e^{-(|ze^{-s/4}|-1)^{2}})^{2}|z|^{2},

by comparison principle. Here,
\epsilon>0
is a small fixed constant, the factor
1-e^{-\epsilon s}
is a technical factor to to ensure the comparison principle applies, and the factor
(1-e^{-(|ze^{-s/4}|-1)^{2}})^{2}
is chosen such that
\eta^{ex}\equiv 0
for
|z|=e^{\frac{1}{4}s}
, matching the boundary condition (See Condition
1.18
). This completes the estimate for
\varepsilon
in the outer region
|z|\gtrsim e^{\frac{5}{49}s}
.

The above decomposition is detailed in the bootstrap regime (Definition
3.1
), in which we successfully construct solutions admitting the behavior described in Theorem
1.2
.

We organize the rest of the paper as follows. In Section
2
, we formally derive the quenching profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
. Section
3
introduces the linearized problem and defines a bootstrap regime to control the perturbation
\varepsilon(y,s)
. In Section
4
, we establish key estimates for solutions within the bootstrap regime, which are essential for controlling
\varepsilon
. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove that the perturbation
\varepsilon(y,s)
remains trapped in the bootstrap regime for all time, thus completing the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Notations:

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
be a smooth cutoff function satisfying

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{ if }\quad 0\leq\xi\leq 1,\\
0\quad\text{ if }\quad\xi\geq 2.\end{cases}

For
R>0
, define
\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(1.30)

\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{R}(y)=\chi\left(\frac{|y|}{R}\right).

We write
M\lesssim N
to indicate that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
M\leq CN
. Similarly, we write
M\ll N
if
CM\leq N
for some sufficiently large constant
C>1
.
We denote by
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
the weighted
L^{2}
space

L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\left\{f\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(y)|^{2}\rho(y)\,dy<\infty\right\},

equipped with the inner product

(1.31)

\langle f,g\rangle_{\rho}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(y)g(y)\rho(y)\,dy,

where the weight function
\rho(y)
is

\rho(y)=\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}.

For derivative notations, let
f(y,s)
be a function with
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
. We use
\partial_{i}f
to denote the partial derivative
\partial_{y_{i}}f
. Similarly, if
g(z,s)
is a function with
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
, we write
\partial_{i}g
for
\partial_{z_{i}}g
. In this way, the meaning of
\partial_{i}
is understood from the context of the function and its variables.

2.
Derivation of the Nonradial Quenching Profile

To establish Theorem
1.2
, we construct an approximate quenching profile valid in different spatial regions. This begins with a detailed analysis of the solution in self-similar variables, focusing first on the inner region near the origin. In this section, we derive the leading-order behavior of the solution and construct the nonradial profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
defined in
1.6
using spectral analysis.

We begin by deriving an approximate solution near the quenching point using the self-similar formulation
1.5
, where the function
w
satisfies the self-similar equation

(2.1)

\partial_{s}w=\Delta w-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla w+\frac{1}{3}w-\frac{1}{w^{2}}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{B}_{s}\times(s_{0},\infty).

Here,
\mathcal{B}_{s}=e^{s/2}\mathcal{B}
, with the boundary condition

w(y,s)=e^{\frac{s}{3}}\quad\text{for }|y|=e^{s/2}.

We observe that
\bar{c}=3^{\frac{1}{3}}
is a trivial constant solution to (
2.1
). We then linearize around
c
by setting

w(y,s)=\bar{c}+\widetilde{w}(y,s),

which yields the evolution equation for
\widetilde{w}
,

(2.2)

\partial_{s}\widetilde{w}=\Delta\widetilde{w}-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\widetilde{w}+\frac{1}{3}(\bar{c}+\widetilde{w})-\frac{1}{(\bar{c}+\widetilde{w})^{3}}.

By expanding the nonlinear term using Taylor expansion:

\frac{1}{(\bar{c}+\widetilde{w})^{2}}=\frac{1}{\bar{c}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2\widetilde{w}}{\bar{c}}+O(\widetilde{w}^{2})\right),

and substituting this into the equation for
\widetilde{w}
, we obtain

\partial_{s}\widetilde{w}=\mathcal{L}\widetilde{w}+O(\widetilde{w}^{2}),

where
\mathcal{L}
is the linearized operator introduced in (
1.21
).

Since the nonlinear remainder is roughly quadratic, the leading-order behavior of
\widetilde{w}
is governed by the linear equation

(2.3)

\partial_{s}\widetilde{w}=\mathcal{L}\widetilde{w}.

We note from (
1.22
) that
\widetilde{w}=Ce^{-s}H_{22}
is an exact solution to equation (
2.3
) for arbitrary
C\in\mathbb{R}
. To further refine our quenching profile, we take
C=\frac{\bar{c}}{9}
(to be agreed with the chosen profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
) and consider the linearization around

\bar{c}+\frac{\bar{c}}{9}\,e^{-s}H_{22}(y),

by setting

(2.4)

w(y,s)=\bar{c}+\frac{\bar{c}}{9}e^{-s}H_{22}(y)+\Sigma(y,s),

where
\Sigma=o(e^{-s})
in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The correction term
\Sigma(y,s)
admits the expansion in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\displaystyle\Sigma(y,s)=e^{-2s}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\frac{64}{81\bar{c}}h_{0}h_{0}+\frac{32}{27\bar{c}}(h_{0}h_{2}+h_{2}h_{0})+\frac{64}{27\bar{c}}h_{2}h_{2}+\frac{8}{27\bar{c}}(h_{0}h_{4}+h_{4}h_{0})

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{8}{27\bar{c}}s+C_{2,4}\right)(h_{2}h_{4}+h_{4}h_{2})+C_{0,6}(h_{0}h_{6}+h_{6}h_{0})-\frac{2}{54\bar{c}}h_{4}h_{4}\Bigg]+O(s^{2}e^{-3s}).

Proof.

Substituting equation (
2.4
) into the self-similar equation (
2.1
), we find that
\Sigma(y,s)
satisfies the PDE

(2.5)

\Sigma_{s}=\mathcal{L}\Sigma-\frac{2}{54\bar{c}}e^{-2s}H_{22}^{2}+o(e^{-2s})\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Using the spectral decomposition of
\mathcal{L}
, we write

\Sigma(y,s)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}w_{ij}(s)H_{ij}(y),

and by projecting equation (
2.5
) onto each eigenfunction
H_{ij}
, we derive the following ODE for each coefficient
w_{ij}(s)
:

w_{ij}^{\prime}(s)=\left(1-\frac{i+j}{2}\right)w_{ij}(s)+\beta_{ij}e^{-2s}+o(e^{-2s}),

where the coefficients
\beta_{ij}
are given by

\beta_{ij}=-\frac{2}{54\bar{c}}\cdot\frac{\langle h_{2}^{2}h_{2}^{2},h_{i}h_{j}\rangle_{\rho}}{\langle h_{i}h_{j},h_{i}h_{j}\rangle_{\rho}}=-\frac{2}{54\bar{c}}\cdot\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{2}^{2}h_{i}e^{-y_{1}^{2}/4}\,dy_{1}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{2}^{2}h_{j}e^{-y_{2}^{2}/4}\,dy_{2}\right)}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{i}^{2}e^{-y_{1}^{2}/4}\,dy_{1}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{j}^{2}e^{-y_{2}^{2}/4}\,dy_{2}\right)}.

Solving for
w_{ij}(s)
using the orthogonal property
1.23
yields the desired expansion for
\Sigma(y,s)
.

\Box

We observe that the constants
C_{0,6}
and
C_{2,4}
appearing in Lemma
2.1
are free parameters. We then set
C_{2,4}=0
and
C_{0,6}=\frac{\bar{c}}{9}\cdot\theta
with
0<\theta<\theta^{*}
and introduce

\Psi_{\theta}(y,s)=\Big(3+e^{-s}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-2s}(y_{1}^{6}+y_{2}^{6})\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}},

which is valid in the domain
|y|\lesssim e^{s/4}
. Since
\Psi_{\theta}(y,s)
is intended to match the profile

w(y,s)=\bar{c}+\frac{\bar{c}}{9}e^{-s}H_{22}(y)+\Sigma(y,s),

we thus introduce a correction to ensure compatibility. Obseving that by expanding
\Psi_{\theta}(y,s)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Psi_{\theta}(y,s)

\displaystyle=\Big(3+e^{-s}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-2s}(y_{1}^{6}+y_{2}^{6})\Big)^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle=\bar{c}+\frac{\bar{c}}{9}e^{-s}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}+\frac{\bar{c}}{9}\cdot\theta e^{-2s}(y_{1}^{6}+y_{2}^{6})-\frac{\bar{c}}{81}e^{-2s}y_{1}^{4}y_{2}^{4}+O(e^{-3s}|y|^{10}),\quad\forall|y|\lesssim e^{s/4}.

Using this expansion, we define the modified profile

\Psi_{\theta}^{\mathrm{mod}}(y,s)=\left(3+e^{-s}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-2s}(y_{1}^{6}+y_{2}^{6})\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}+\mathcal{C}(y,s)\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(z),

where
\mathcal{C}(y,s)
is the correction term
1.7
. Note that by adding the correction term
\mathcal{C}(y,s)
to
\Psi_{\theta}(y,s)
, the expansion of the leading terms aligns with that of
w(y,s)
. This confirms that the modified profile
\Psi_{\theta}^{\mathrm{mod}}(y,s)
is indeed compatible with the inner expansion
w(y,s)
, and remains valid within the region
|y|\lesssim e^{s/4}
.

To construct an approximate profile in the outer region
|y|\gtrsim e^{s/4}
, we introduce

v(z,s)=w(y,s),\quad z=ye^{-s/4}.

The function
v
satisfies the equation

(2.6)

\partial_{s}v=e^{-s/2}\Delta v-\frac{1}{4}z\cdot\nabla v+\frac{1}{3}v-\frac{1}{v^{2}}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}_{s}\times(s_{0},\infty),

where
\mathcal{D}_{s}=e^{s/4}\mathcal{B}
, with boundary condition

(2.7)

v(z,s)=e^{\frac{s}{3}},\quad\text{on }|z|=e^{s/4}.

Since the term
e^{-s/2}\Delta v
becomes negligible as
s\to\infty
, we consider the simplified equation

(2.8)

\partial_{s}v=-\frac{1}{4}z\cdot\nabla v+\frac{1}{3}v-\frac{1}{v^{2}},

in the region
|z|\gtrsim 1
. We note that
v(z)=(3+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}
is an exact solution of this equation, and to ensure compatibility with the inner profile
\Psi_{\theta}^{\text{mod}}
near
|z|\sim 1
, and to match the boundary behavior from (
2.7
), we slighly modify
\Psi_{\theta}^{\textup{mod}}
that leads us to the fully approximated quenching profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
defined in
1.6
.

3.
Linearization, error decomposition and bootstrap definition

With the approximate profile
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
defined as in (
1.6
), the next step is to rigorously justify that the true solution remains close to
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
. To do so, we linearize the equation (
1.17
) around
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
, i.e.
w=\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+\varepsilon
, and control the perturbation
\varepsilon(y,s)
in a certain bootstrap regime. To be concrete, we show that there exist initial data
u_{0}
such that the corresponding solution
u
, expressed in self-similar variables, satisfies the convergence

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq|y|\leq e^{\frac{69}{196}s}}|w(y,s)-\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)|\to 0,\quad\sup_{e^{\frac{69}{196}}\leq|y|\leq e^{\frac{1}{2}s}}\left|\frac{w(y,s)-\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)}\right|<1,\quad\text{as }s\to\infty

(The technical factor
\frac{69}{196}=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{5}{49}
is chosen to simplify the nonlinear estimate in the region
|z|\geq e^{\frac{5}{49}s}
, see Lemmas
5.8
-
5.10
and Lemma
5.13
). The linearization around
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
leads to the evolution equation for the perturbation
\varepsilon
:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\varepsilon=\mathcal{H}\varepsilon+E+NL(\varepsilon),

where each term is defined as follows:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\varepsilon=\Delta\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon+\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon

(3.3)

\displaystyle E=-\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+\Delta\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{2}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle NL(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{2}}-\frac{1}{(\mathcal{P_{\theta}}+\varepsilon)^{2}}.

Our main objective is to show that there is initial data
\varepsilon_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that the corresponding solution to (
3.1
) is global-in-time and satisfies

\|\varepsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\to 0
as
s\to\infty
. For this purpose, we introduce the decomposition

(3.5)

\displaystyle\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)=\varepsilon(y,s)\cdot\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(z),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\eta^{ex}(z,s)=\eta(z,s)\cdot(1-\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(ze^{-\frac{5}{49}s})),\quad z=ye^{-\frac{s}{4}},

where
\eta(z,s)=\varepsilon(y,s)
and
K\gg 1
is a large fixed constant. We further decompose
\hat{\varepsilon}
as

(3.7)

\displaystyle\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)=\sum_{i+j\leq 8}\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s)H_{ij}(y)+\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(y,s),\quad\quad\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}\perp_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\{H_{ij}\}_{i+j\leq 8},

where the eigenfunctions
H_{ij}
are defined in
1.22
. Note that we have the spectral gap estimate

(3.8)

\langle\mathcal{L}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-},\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\leq-\frac{7}{2}\langle\hat{\varepsilon}_{-},\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}\rangle_{\rho},

and by integration by parts, we have the orthogonality conditions

(3.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\hat{\varepsilon}\perp_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\{H_{ij}\}_{i+j\leq 7},\quad k=1,2,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{kl}\hat{\varepsilon}\perp_{L^{2}_{\rho}}\{H_{ij}\}_{i+j\leq 6},\quad k,l=1,2,

which leads to the spectral gap estimates

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{L}\partial_{k}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-},\partial_{k}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}\right>_{\rho}\leq-3\left<\partial_{k}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-},\partial_{k}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}\right>,\quad k=1,2,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left<\mathcal{L}\partial^{2}_{kl}\varepsilon_{-},\partial^{2}_{kl}\varepsilon_{-}\right>_{\rho}\leq-\frac{5}{2}\left<\partial^{2}_{kl}\varepsilon_{-},\partial^{2}_{kl}\varepsilon_{-}\right>_{\rho},\quad k,l=1,2.

To fully control the perturbation

\varepsilon(y,s)
and close the nonlinear estimate, we introduce the following bootstrap regime.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}.A_{3},B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},D_{1}
and
s
be positive, we denote by
S(s)
the set of functions
\varepsilon(y,s)
such that the decomposition (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) satisfy

(i) (Inner control)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i+j\leq 7}|\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq A_{1}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},

(3.14)

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s)|\leq A_{1}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\text{ for }i+j=8,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq A_{1}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{i}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq A_{2}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad i=1,2,

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{2}_{ij}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq A_{3}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad i,j=1,2,

(ii) (Intermediate control)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\|(y\cdot\nabla)^{k}\varepsilon\|_{\flat}\leq B_{k+1}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad k=0,1,2,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|(z\cdot\nabla)^{k}\eta\|_{\natural}\leq C_{k+1}e^{-\frac{5}{12}s},\quad k=0,1,2,

where
\eta(z,s)=\varepsilon(y,s)
. Moreover, the norms
\ \|\varepsilon\|_{\flat}
and
\ \|\eta\|_{\natural}
is defined by

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon\|_{\flat}=\left(\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\alpha=18,

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\natural}=\left(\int_{|z|\geq K}\frac{|\eta|^{2}}{|z|^{\gamma}}\,\frac{dy}{|z|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\gamma=8.01,

and
K\gg 1
is the large fixed constant in
3.5
and
3.6
.

(iii) (Outer control)

(3.22)

\displaystyle|\eta^{ex}(z,s)|\leq D_{1}h(z,s),\quad h(z,s)=\epsilon e^{-s/6}(1-e^{-\epsilon s})(1-e^{-(|ze^{-s/4}|-1)^{2}})^{2}|z|^{2},

where
\epsilon>0
is a small fixed constant such that
|\eta^{ex}(z,s)/\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(z,s)|\ll 1
. This choice of
\epsilon>0
simplifies the bound for the outer nonlinear estimate (See Lemma
4.1
(iv)).

Remark 3.1
.

The bootstrap constants are chosen in the order

\displaystyle D_{1}\gg C_{3}\gg C_{2}\gg C_{1}\gg B_{3}\gg B_{2}\gg B_{1}\gg A_{3}\gg A_{2}\gg A_{1}\gg 1.

3.1.
Existence of Solutions in the Bootstrap Regime

We show that for suitable initial data
\varepsilon_{0}(y)\in S(s_{0})
, the corresponding solution

\varepsilon(y,s)
of
3.1

S(s)

1.2
. For fixed
s_{0}=s_{0}(A_{1},A_{2}.A_{3},B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},D_{1},K)\gg 1

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}(y)=\psi_{s_{0}}[\textbf{c}](y)=\eta_{0}(z)

We claim the following.

Lemma 3.1

The initial data of (

) is of the form

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
|c_{ij}|<1
, then

\varepsilon_{0}(y)\in S(s_{0})
with strict inequalities.

Proof.

First, we check the bootstrap conditions (
3.13
)-(
3.17
). Note that we have the relations

\displaystyle\psi_{s_{0}}[\textbf{c}](y)\cdot\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}}\left(\sum_{i+j\leq 7}c_{ij}H_{ij}(y)\right)\chi(z)=\sum_{i+j\leq 8}\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s_{0})H_{ij}(y)+\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(y,s_{0}).

Thus, for
k+l\leq 7
, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{kl}(s_{0})

\displaystyle=\left<\psi_{s_{0}}[\textbf{c}]\cdot\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(z),H_{kl}\right>_{\rho}/\left<H_{kl},H_{kl}\right>_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left<e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}}\left(\sum_{i+j\leq 7}c_{ij}H_{ij}\right)\chi(z),H_{kl}\right>_{\rho}/\left<H_{kl},H_{kl}\right>_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j\leq 7}c_{ij}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}}\left(\frac{\left<H_{ij}\chi(z),H_{kl}\right>_{\rho}}{\left<H_{kl},H_{kl}\right>_{\rho}}\right)=c_{kl}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}}\big(1+O(e^{-100s_{0}})\big).

This deduces

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i+j\leq 7}|\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s_{0})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll A_{1}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}},

for
A_{1}\gg 1
. Clearly, we have
|\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s_{0})|\ll A_{1}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}}
for

, which shows the bootstrap condition
3.14
hold for

with a strict inequality. As for the bootstrap condition
3.15
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(y,s_{0})

\displaystyle=\psi_{s_{0}}[\textbf{c}](y)\cdot\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(z)-\sum_{i+j\leq 8}\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s_{0})H_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+j\leq 7}(e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}}c_{ij}\chi(z)-\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s_{0}))H_{ij}(y)-\sum_{i+j=8}\hat{\varepsilon}_{ij}(s_{0})H_{ij},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\ll A_{1}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}},

and hence the bootstrap condition (
3.15
) holds for
s=s_{0}
with a strict inequality. By differentiating the expression of
\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}
, we derive that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{i}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\ll A_{2}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}},\quad i=1,2,

for
A_{2}\gg A_{1}
and

\displaystyle\|\partial^{2}_{ij}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\ll A_{3}e^{-\frac{32}{12}s_{0}},\quad i,j=1,2,

4.

E

The success of the bootstrap argument depends on precise estimates of the error term (
3.3
), the nonlinear term
3.4
, and the behavior of the solution within the bootstrap regime. In this section, we establish these estimates, focusing on the properties of solutions in the set
S(s)
and the detailed structure of the generated error term
E
and the nonlinear term
NL(\varepsilon)
.

4.1.
Properties of
S(s)

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
s_{0}=s_{0}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},K,B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},D_{1})\gg 1
, let
\varepsilon(s)\in S(s)
be a solution for
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
, with fixed
s_{1}>s_{0}
. Then we have the following estimates:

(i)
(Local
L^{\infty}
-estimate for
\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}
.)

\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 100K)}\lesssim C(K)\,e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},

where
C(K)>0
is a constant depending only on
K
and
\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}
is defined in
3.7
.

(ii)
(Nonlinear estimate for
\hat{\varepsilon}
in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.)

The nonlinear term corresponding to
\hat{\varepsilon}
is defined as

\displaystyle NL(\hat{\varepsilon})=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\binom{-2}{k}\frac{\hat{\varepsilon}^{k}}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{k+2}},

where

\displaystyle\binom{-2}{k}=\frac{(-2)\cdot(-3)\cdots(-2-k+1)}{k!},\quad k\geq 2,

and satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle NL(\hat{\varepsilon})=o(se^{-3s}),\quad\text{ in }L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(iii)
(Pointwise estimate for
\varepsilon
)

We have the pointwise estimates

\displaystyle|\varepsilon(y,s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}|y|^{9},\text{ for }|y|\geq 2K,\quad|\eta(z,s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{5}{12}s}|z|^{4.005},\text{ for }|z|\geq 2K.

(iv)
(Exterior nonlinear estimate)

The exterior nonlinear term is defined as

\displaystyle NL^{ex}(\eta)=NL(\eta)\left(1-\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(ze^{-\frac{5}{49}s})\right),

where

\displaystyle NL(\eta):=\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{2}}-\frac{1}{(\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+\eta)^{2}}\right),

and satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle|NL^{ex}(\eta)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{11}{147}s}

Proof.
(i) Note that by Sobolev inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 100K)}\leq C\left(\|D^{2}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(|y|\leq 50K)}+\|D\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(|y|\leq 50K)}+\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(|y|\leq 50K)}\right),

where
C>0
is the Sobolev constant. By the bootstrap condition (
3.15
), we have:

\displaystyle\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{2}(|y|\leq 50K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(K)\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim C(K)\,e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}.

Next, by the bootstrap condition
3.16
and
3.17
, we have

\displaystyle\|D\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{2}(|y|\leq 50K)}\lesssim C(K)\,e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad\|D^{2}\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{2}(|y|\leq 50K)}\lesssim C(K)\,e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},

hence, we have

\displaystyle\|\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2K)}\lesssim C(K)\,e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}.

This concludes the local
L^{\infty}
estimate of
\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}
.

(ii)
Note that by the bootstrap condition
3.18
, combined with Sobolev inequality, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\leq Me^{-\frac{32}{12}s}|y|^{9},\quad|y|\geq 2K,

moreover, by the local
L^{\infty}
estimate for
\hat{\varepsilon}_{-}
, together with the bootstrap condition
3.13
and
3.14
, we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 50K)}\leq Me^{-\frac{32}{12}s}.

for some constant
M=M(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},K,B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})>0
. Thus, we have, for
k\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\|-(\hat{\varepsilon}^{k}/\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{k+2})\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\hat{\varepsilon}^{k}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{|y|\leq 2K}|\hat{\varepsilon}|^{2k}\rho(y)\,dy+\int_{|y|\geq 2K}|\hat{\varepsilon}|^{2k}\rho(y)\,dy\right)^{1/2}\leq 2M^{k}\,e^{-\frac{32}{12}ks},

and hence

\displaystyle\|NL(\hat{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(k+1)\|\hat{\varepsilon}^{k}\|_{L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(k+1)(2M^{k}e^{-\frac{32}{12}ks})\ll se^{-3s}.

This proves the nonlinear estimate for
\hat{\varepsilon}
in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

(iii) This follows directly from bootstrap conditions
3.18
and
3.19
combined with Sobolev inequality.

(iv) Note that

\displaystyle|NL^{ex}(\eta)|\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{2}}+\frac{1}{(\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-|\eta|)^{2}}\right)\left(1-\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(ze^{-\frac{5}{49}s})\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{2}}\left(1-\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{K}(ze^{-\frac{5}{49}s})\right)\lesssim e^{-\frac{11}{147}s},

where we used the fact that
|\eta^{ex}(z,s)/\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(z,s)|\ll 1
. This concludes the exterior nonlinear estimate.

\Box

Remark 4.1
.

The case when

in the proof of (ii) in Lemma

shows that

4.2.
Decomposition of the generated error
E

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
s_{0}=s_{0}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3},K,B_{1},B_{2},B_{3},C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},D_{1})\gg 1
, let
\varepsilon(s)\in S(s)
be a solution for
3.1
for all
s\in[s_{0},s_{1}]
with
s_{1}<\infty
. Then the generated error
E
defined in
3.3
satisfies

(i)
|E(y,s)|\lesssim se^{-3s}|y|^{10}
for
|y|\leq 2Ke^{s/4}
.

(ii)
|\mathcal{E}(z,s)|\lesssim\min{\{e^{-s/3},e^{-s/2}|z|^{2}\}}
for
|z|\geq 2K
, where
\mathcal{E}(z,s)=E(y,s)
.

Proof.

(i) Recall from (
3.3
) that

E(y,s)=-\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+\Delta\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{2}}.

Note that it is suffices to show
|E(y,s)|\lesssim se^{-3s}|y|^{10}
in the region
\left\{|y|\leq e^{\frac{s}{4}}\right\}
, where we have

\displaystyle\forall|y|\leq e^{s/4},\quad\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)=\left(3+e^{-s}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-2s}(y_{1}^{6}+y_{2}^{6})\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}+\mathcal{C}(y,s),\quad\theta\in(0,\theta^{*}),

and
\mathcal{C}(y,s)
is the correction term defined in
1.7
.

Calculating each term of
E
yields:

\displaystyle E(y,s)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{s}\mathcal{C}_{1}+\Delta\mathcal{C}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\mathcal{C}_{1}+\mathcal{C}_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{10}{c}\delta e^{-2s}(y_{1}^{4}+y_{2}^{4}))-\frac{8}{9c^{5}}e^{-2s}(y_{1}^{4}y_{2}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{4})

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{8}{81c}e^{-2s}y_{1}^{4}y_{2}^{2}+\frac{8}{81c}e^{-2s}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{4}-\frac{16}{9c^{2}}e^{-2s}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{2}e^{-2s}\left(\frac{4}{81c}y_{1}^{4}+\frac{4}{81c}y_{2}^{4}+\frac{16}{c^{3}}+\frac{8}{81c}y_{1}^{2}y_{2}^{2}-\frac{16}{9c^{2}}y_{1}^{2}-\frac{16}{9c^{2}}y_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+se^{-3s}\langle y\rangle^{10},

for
|y|\leq e^{s/4}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}(y,s)=\mathcal{C}(y,s)-\frac{\bar{c}}{9}e^{-s}(-2y_{1}^{2}-2y_{2}^{2}+4),\quad\bar{c}=3^{\frac{1}{3}}.

Combining the above terms leads to the estimate

\displaystyle|E(y,s)|\lesssim se^{-3s}|y|^{10},

for
|y|\leq e^{s/4}
. This establishes the desired estimate.

(ii) Recall that by
3.3
,
\mathcal{E}(z,s)
reads as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(z,s)=-\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+e^{-s/2}\Delta\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{4}(z\cdot\nabla)\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{2}},

for
|z|\geq 2K
, where
\mathcal{P_{\theta}=\mathcal{P_{\theta}}}(z,s)
is defined in
1.6
. We now calculate
\mathcal{E}(z,s)
in the regions

\{2K\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{4}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\}
,
\{\frac{1}{4}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\}
and
\{\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{s}{4}}\}
, respectively.

For
\{2K\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{4}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\}
, we have
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}=(3+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-\frac{s}{2}}(z_{1}^{6}+z_{2}^{6}))^{\frac{1}{3}}
and observe that

\displaystyle-\partial_{s}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{4}(z\cdot\nabla)\mathcal{P}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{P}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\theta}^{2}}=0,

thus, it is left to estimate
e^{-s/2}\Delta\mathcal{P}_{\theta}
. By a direct computation, we have the estimate

\displaystyle|e^{-s/2}\Delta\mathcal{P}_{\theta}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{-s/2}|z|^{2}}{(3+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-s/2}(z_{1}^{6}+z_{2}^{6}))^{5/3}}+\frac{e^{-s/2}z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}|z|^{2}}{(3+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-s/2}(z_{1}^{6}+z_{2}^{6}))^{5/3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{e^{-s}|z|^{8}}{(3+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}+\theta e^{-s/2}(z_{1}^{6}+z_{2}^{6}))^{5/3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min{\{e^{-s/3},e^{-s/2}|z|^{2}\}}.

For
\{\frac{1}{4}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\}
, we have to take account for the cutoff function
\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{\kappa(s)}
, where we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(y,s)=\Psi_{\theta}(z,s)\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{\frac{1}{4}\kappa(s)}(z,s)+e^{\frac{s}{3}}\left(1-\raisebox{0.0pt}[4.30554pt][4.30554pt]{$\chi$}_{\frac{1}{4}\kappa(s)}(z,s)\right).

By a direct calculation, we derive

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}(z,s)|\lesssim\min{\{e^{-s/3},e^{-s/2}|z|^{2}\}}.

Finally, for
\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{s}{4}}
, we have
\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(z,s)=e^{\frac{s}{3}}
, for
\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{s}{4}}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{s}{4}}
, which implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(z,s)=e^{-\frac{2}{3}s}.

Combining the above estimates yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}(z,s)|\lesssim\min{\{e^{-s/3},e^{-s/2}|z|^{2}\}},\quad|z|\geq 2K,

which concludes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Remark 5.1

Lemma 5.2
.

We have the estimate

5.2.

,
B_{2}
, and

, thereby showing that the bootstrap condition
3.18
is strictly satisfied for all

. Recall that we have the PDE (See

)

(5.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\varepsilon=\Delta\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon+\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon+NL(\varepsilon)+E,

where

is defined in
3.3
and

is defined in
3.4
.

Remark 5.2
.

In particular, by
5.7
and
5.8
, we have the pointwise estimates at

:

\displaystyle|\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad|\partial_{i}\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad|\partial_{ij}\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad i,j=1,2,

\displaystyle|(y\cdot\nabla)\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad|\nabla(y\cdot\nabla)\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad|(y\cdot\nabla)^{2}\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}.

Moreover, by Remark
A.3
in Appendix

, we also have the following estimate at

(5.15)

\displaystyle|\nabla(y\cdot\nabla)^{2}\hat{\varepsilon}(y,s)|\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}.

Remark 5.3
.

Let
\nu
be the outward pointing unit normal vector field of
\{|y|=K\}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

We have the energy estimate

(5.16)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|\hat{\varepsilon}\|_{\flat}^{2}\leq-\frac{7}{2}\|\hat{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\flat}+\frac{B_{1}^{2}}{K}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\flat}
is the norm (
3.20
).

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|\varepsilon\|_{\flat}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon\,\partial_{s}\varepsilon}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon\,\Delta\varepsilon}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon\,(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot NL(\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot E}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}.

For the Laplacian term, using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon\Delta\varepsilon}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+2)^{2}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha+2}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{|y|=K}\left(\frac{1}{|y|^{\alpha+2}}\frac{\partial(\varepsilon^{2})}{\partial\nu}-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial(|y|^{-\alpha-2})}{\partial\nu}\right)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|\nabla\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}\leq B_{1}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s}

where the the boundary terms are controlled by Remark
5.2
. For the second term, by integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+B_{1}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s},

where the boundary terms are estimated by Remark
5.2
. Next, using the estimates (iii) and (iv) in Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot E}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot NL(\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{K\leq|y|\leq e^{\frac{9}{33}s}}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot E}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\int_{K\leq|y|\leq e^{\frac{9}{33}s}}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot NL(\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{e^{\frac{1}{44}s}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{5}{109}s}}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot E}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\int_{e^{\frac{1}{44}s}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{5}{109}s}}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot NL(\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{e^{\frac{5}{109}s}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{5}{49}s}}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot E}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\int_{e^{\frac{5}{109}s}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{5}{49}s}}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot NL(\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{e^{\frac{5}{49}s}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{1}{4}s}}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot E}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\int_{e^{\frac{5}{49}s}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{1}{4}s}}\frac{\varepsilon\cdot NL(\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{P_{\theta}^{3}}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+o(e^{-\frac{32}{6}s}).

Here, the technical constant
\frac{9}{33}
is chosen so that
|E(y,s)|\lesssim se^{-3s}|y|^{10}\lesssim e^{-\frac{32}{12}s}|y|^{8.9}
throughout
0\leq|y|\leq e^{\frac{9}{33}s}
(equivalently, for
0\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{1}{44}s}
). Next, the technical constant
\frac{5}{109}
is chosen to simplify the calculations of the nonlinear estimate for
0\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{5}{109}s}
(equivalently, for
0\leq|y|\leq e^{(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{5}{109})s}
). Finally, the technical constant
\frac{5}{49}
is chosen to simplify the nonlinear estimate for
e^{\frac{5}{109}s}\leq|z|\leq e^{\frac{5}{49}s}
as well as to control the nonlinear estimate for
|z|\geq e^{\frac{5}{49}s}
. Combining the above estimates and using
\alpha=18
yields

(5.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|\hat{\varepsilon}\|_{\flat}^{2}\leq-\frac{7}{2}\|\hat{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{\flat}+\frac{B_{1}^{2}}{K}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s},

which concludes the desired energy estimate.

\Box

Lemma 5.9
.

We have the energy estimate

(5.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)\|_{\flat}^{2}\leq-\frac{7}{2}\|(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)\|^{2}_{\flat}+\frac{B_{2}^{2}}{K}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s}.

Proof.

We compute:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\|_{\flat}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\cdot(y\cdot\nabla)(\Delta\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\cdot(y\cdot\nabla)^{2}\varepsilon}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{3}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\cdot(y\cdot\nabla)(NL(\varepsilon))}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\cdot(y\cdot\nabla)E}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}.

We now estimate each term in this energy identity. For the Laplacian term, integrating by parts yield

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\cdot(y\cdot\nabla)(\Delta\hat{\varepsilon})}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\cdot\Delta(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}-2\int_{y\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\>\Delta\hat{\varepsilon}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+2)^{2}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha+2}}\,dy-\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|\nabla(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha+2}}\,dy-2\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)\Delta\varepsilon}{|y|^{\alpha+2}}\,dy+B_{2}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s}\leq 2B_{2}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s},

where the boundary terms are controlled by Remark
5.2
and the Laplacian term in the last line is dominated by the dissipative term. For the second term, we have, by integration by parts

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon\cdot(y\cdot\nabla)((y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(-\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|(y\cdot\nabla)\varepsilon|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+B_{2}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s},

where the bound of the boundary terms are controlled by Remark
5.2
. Next, by parabolic regularity (See Theorem
A.1
and the following Remarks), we have

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)\cdot(y\cdot\nabla E)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)\cdot(y\cdot\nabla NL(\varepsilon))}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{P_{\theta}^{3}}\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{|(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)|^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}+o(e^{-\frac{32}{6}s}),

where the boundary terms are estimated by Remark
5.2
. Combining the above estimates and using
\alpha=18
yields

(5.19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}\|(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)\|_{\flat}^{2}\leq-\frac{7}{2}\|(y\cdot\nabla\varepsilon)\|^{2}_{\flat}+\frac{B_{2}^{2}}{K}e^{-\frac{32}{6}s},

which is the desired energy estimate.

\Box

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)^{2}\hat{\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta((y\ \cdot\nabla)^{2}\hat{\varepsilon})}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}-4\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)^{2}\hat{\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta(y\cdot\nabla\hat{\varepsilon})}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}-2\int_{|y|\geq K}\frac{(y\cdot\nabla)^{2}\hat{\varepsilon}\cdot\Delta\hat{\varepsilon}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\,\frac{dy}{|y|^{2}}

In this section, we establish several parabolic regularity results that will be used throughout the paper. We begin by introducing notations, followed by stating the parabolic Schauder estimates as well as the Krylov–Safonov estimates. Let

\displaystyle|p|=|z|+|s|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let

\displaystyle[R]_{C^{0,\delta}(Q_{r})}=\sup_{p,q\in Q_{r},\;p\neq q}\frac{|R(p)-R(q)|}{|p-q|^{\delta}},

Remark A.3
.

By considering the PDE for
\varepsilon(y,s)
and applying Schauder Estimates to equation
1.20
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{i}\varepsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 50K)}\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad\|\partial^{2}_{ij}\varepsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 50K)}\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},

moreover, by deriving the PDE for
\partial_{i}\varepsilon(y,s)
, we can also derive

\displaystyle\|\partial^{3}_{ijk}\varepsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 25K)}\lesssim C(K)e^{-\frac{32}{12}s},\quad i,j,k=1,2.

Remark A.4
.

Let
z^{\prime}=ze^{-s/4}
and by considering the PDE
A.1
in the
z^{\prime}

\displaystyle|\partial_{i}\eta(z,s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{5}{12}s}|z|^{3.005},\quad|\partial_{ij}\eta(z,s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{5}{12}s}|z|^{2.005},\quad i,j=1,2,

for

Moreover, by deriving the PDE of
\partial_{i}\varepsilon
and
\partial_{ij}\eta

\displaystyle|\partial_{ijk}\,\eta(z,s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{5}{12}s}|z|^{1.005},\quad|\partial_{ijkl}\,\eta(z,s)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{5}{12}s}|z|^{0.005},\quad i,j,k,l=1,2,

for

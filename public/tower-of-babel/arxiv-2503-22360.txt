Improvement of conformal maps combined with the Sinc approximation for derivatives over infinite intervals Note: This work was partially supported by JSPS Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (C) JP23K03218.
Tomoaki Okayama, Yuito Kuwashita, Ao Kondo

Abstract
F. Stenger proposed efficient approximation formulas for derivatives over infinite intervals. These formulas were derived by combining the Sinc approximation with appropriate conformal maps. It has been demonstrated that these formulas can attain root-exponential convergence. In this study, we enhance the convergence rate by improving the conformal maps employed in those formulas. We provide a theoretical error analysis and numerical experiments that confirm the effectiveness of our new formulas.

Introduction

This study focuses on the approximation formulas
for derivatives based on the Sinc approximation

F(x)\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)S(k,h)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.1)

where
h
is the mesh size,

M
and
N
are truncation numbers,
and
S(k,h)
is the so-called Sinc function defined by

S(k,h)(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sin[\piup(x-kh)/h]}{\piup(x-kh)/h}&(x\neq kh),\\
1&(x=kh).\end{cases}

The Sinc approximation (
1.1
)
is known to be efficient
(nearly optimal
[
16
,
19
]
)
for analytic functions
F

that satisfy the following two conditions:
(i)
F(x)
is defined on the entire real axis
\mathbb{R}
, and
(ii)
|F(x)|
decays exponentially as
x\to\pm\infty
.
When those conditions are not satisfied,
Stenger
[
17
,
18
]
proposed
to employ an appropriate conformal map
depending on the target interval
(a,b)
and
the decay rate of the given function
f
.
He considered the following five typical cases:

1.

(a,b)=(0,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays algebraically as
t\to\infty
,

2.

(a,b)=(0,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays exponentially as
t\to\infty
,

3.

(a,b)=(-\infty,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays algebraically as
t\to\pm\infty
,

4.

(a,b)=(-\infty,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays algebraically as
t\to-\infty
and exponentially as
t\to\infty
,

5.

the interval
(a,b)
is finite.

For each case, he presented a recommended conformal map

\psi_{i}

(i=1,\,\ldots,\,5)
as

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}(x)=\E^{x},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{2}(x)=\arsinh(\E^{x}),

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{3}(x)=\sinh x,

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{4}(x)=\sinh(\log(\arsinh(\E^{x}))),

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{5}(x)=\frac{b-a}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+\frac{b+a}{2}.

Combination of the conformal map
\psi_{i}
with
the Sinc approximation (
1.1
) gives
an approximation for a function
f(t)
as

f(t)\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{i}(kh))S(k,h)(\psi_{i}^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(a,b).

(1.2)

For a derivative of
f
, one may naturally consider
differentiating both sides of (
1.2
) as

f^{\prime}(t)\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{i}(kh))S^{\prime}(k,h)(\psi_{i}^{-1}(t))\left\{\psi_{i}^{-1}(t)\right\}^{\prime},\quad t\in(a,b).

(1.3)

However,
\{\psi_{i}^{-1}(t)\}^{\prime}
diverges at endpoints in some cases
(more precisely, in the cases
i=1,\,2,\,5
).
In such cases,
we cannot expect uniform approximation over the target interval
(a,b)
.
To address this issue,
in addition to
\psi_{i}
,
Stenger
[
17
]
proposed to use
an appropriate function
g_{i}
as

\displaystyle\frac{f(t)}{g_{i}(t)}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\frac{f(\psi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\psi_{i}(kh))}S(k,h)(\psi_{i}^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(a,b),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\frac{f(\psi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\psi_{i}(kh))}g_{i}(t)S(k,h)(\psi_{i}^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(a,b).

(1.4)

The functions
g_{i}
(
i=1,\,\ldots,\,5
) are given as

\displaystyle g_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t}{1+t}\right)^{m},

\displaystyle g_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\E^{-t}\right)^{m},

\displaystyle g_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle g_{4}(t)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle g_{5}(t)

\displaystyle=(t-a)^{m}(b-t)^{m},

which are chosen so that

g_{i}(t)
suppresses the divergence of
\{\psi_{i}^{-1}(t)\}^{(m)}
.
The concrete form of the approximation formula
for
m
-th derivative is derived by differentiating both sides
of (
1.4
) as

f^{(m)}(t)\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\frac{f(\psi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\psi_{i}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{i}(t)S(k,h)(\psi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right\},\quad t\in(a,b).

(1.5)

He also conducted a theoretical error analysis and
showed that this approximation formula yields a uniform
approximation over the target interval, and can attain
root-exponential convergence:
\OO(\exp(-c\sqrt{n}))
,
where
c
is a positive constant and
n=\max\{M,N\}
.
Owing to such high efficiency, several authors
have utilized the formula to solve
differential equations
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
15
]
.

This study aims to improve the convergence
rate of the formula in the cases
i=2
and
i=4
.
The concept for the improvement is replacing the conformal maps;
for
i=2
, we replace
\psi_{2}(x)
with

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\log(1+\E^{x}),

and for
i=4
, we replace
\psi_{4}(x)
with

\displaystyle\phi_{4}(x)

\displaystyle=2\sinh(\log(\log(1+\E^{x}))).

Such replacement of the conformal maps
has already been conducted
for function approximation
[
12
,
13
]

and integral approximation
[
10
,
11
]
,
and improvement of the convergence rate has been reported.
Furthermore, in the case of integral approximation,
it has been theoretically revealed that

\phi_{2}
is
always
superior to
\psi_{2}

[
8
]
.
Considering these studies as motivation,
we propose a new approximation formula for
m
-th derivative as

f^{(m)}(t)\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\frac{f(\phi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\phi_{i}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{i}(t)S(k,h)(\phi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right\},\quad t\in(a,b),

(1.6)

for
i=2
and
i=4
.
We also conduct a theoretical error analysis claiming that
the improved formulas can attain
\OO(\exp(-c^{\prime}\sqrt{n}))
,
where
c^{\prime}
is a constant that may be greater than
c
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Convergence theorems for Stenger’s formula
are summarized in Section
2
.
Convergence theorems for the new approximation formula
are described in Section
3

along with their proofs.
Numerical examples are presented in Section
4
.
Finally,
the conclusions of this study are presented in Section
5
.

2
Convergence theorems for Stenger’s formula

In this section, we describe the convergence theorems of
Stenger’s formula (
1.5
)
for
i=2
and
i=4
.
In the original theorems,
the summation is not
\sum_{k=-M}^{N}
but
\sum_{k=-N}^{N}
.
However, extension to
\sum_{k=-M}^{N}
is relatively straightforward
as follows.
Here, let
\mathscr{D}_{d}
be a strip domain
defined by
\mathscr{D}_{d}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<d\}

for
d>0
.
Furthermore, let

\mathscr{D}_{d}^{-}=\{\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}:\operatorname{Re}\zeta<0\}

and

\mathscr{D}_{d}^{+}=\{\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}:\operatorname{Re}\zeta\geq 0\}
.

Theorem 2.1
(Stenger
[
17
, Theorem 4.4.2 and Example 4.4.6]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi_{2}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup/2
,
and that there exist positive constants
K
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that

\left|\frac{f(z)}{g_{2}(z)}\right|\leq K\left|\frac{z}{1+z}\right|^{\alpha}|\E^{-z}|^{\beta}

(2.1)

holds for all
z\in\psi_{2}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
M
and
N
be defined as

M=\left\lceil\frac{\mu}{\alpha}n\right\rceil,\quad N=\left\lceil\frac{\mu}{\beta}n\right\rceil,

(2.2)

and let
h
be defined as

h=\sqrt{\frac{\piup d}{\mu n}}.

(2.3)

Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|f^{(m)}(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\frac{f(\psi_{2}(kh))}{g_{2}(\psi_{2}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{2}(t)S(k,h)(\psi_{2}^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|\leq Cn^{(m+1)/2}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}\right).

Theorem 2.2
(Stenger
[
18
, Theorem 1.5.4]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi_{4}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup/2
,
and that there exist positive constants
K
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq K|\E^{-z}|^{2\beta}

(2.4)

holds for all
z\in\psi_{4}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{+})
, and

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq K\frac{1}{|z|^{\alpha}}

(2.5)

holds for all
z\in\psi_{4}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{-})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
M
and
N
be defined as (
2.2
),
and let
h
be defined as (
2.3
).
Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in(-\infty,\infty)}\left|f^{(m)}(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{4}(kh))\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{S(k,h)(\psi_{4}^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|\leq Cn^{(m+1)/2}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}\right).

3.1
Convergence theorems for our formula

Convergence theorems for the new formula are described as follows.

Theorem 3.1

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\phi_{2}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup
,
and that there exist positive constants
K
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that (
2.1
)
holds for all

.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
M
and
N
be defined as (
2.2
),
and let
h
be defined as (
2.3
).
Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|f^{(m)}(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\frac{f(\phi_{2}(kh))}{g_{2}(\phi_{2}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{2}(t)S(k,h)(\phi_{2}^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|\leq Cn^{(m+1)/2}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}\right).

Theorem 3.2

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\phi_{4}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup
,
and that there exist positive constants
K
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq K|\E^{-z}|^{\beta}

(3.1)

holds for all
z\in\phi_{4}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{+})
,
and (
2.5
)
holds for all
z\in\phi_{4}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{-})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
M
and
N
be defined as (
2.2
),
and let
h
be defined as (
2.3
).
Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in(-\infty,\infty)}\left|f^{(m)}(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\phi_{4}(kh))\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{S(k,h)(\phi_{4}^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|\leq Cn^{(m+1)/2}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}\right).

The large difference between Stenger’s and
our theorems is the upper bound of
d
;

d<\piup/2
in Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
,
whereas
d<\piup
in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.
This implies that the value of

in our theorems

Especially in Theorem
3.2
,
the value of

may also be larger than that in Theorem

,

should be twice as large

) and (

).
The values of

and
\mu
cause a difference in the convergence rate,

\OO(n^{(m+1)/2}\exp(-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}))
.

3.2.1
Sketch of the proof

To estimate the error of (
1.6
),
we divide it into two terms as

\displaystyle\left|f^{(m)}(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\frac{f(\phi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\phi_{i}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{i}(t)S(k,h)(\phi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|f^{(m)}(t)-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(\phi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\phi_{i}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{i}(t)S(k,h)(\phi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\frac{f(\phi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\phi_{i}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{i}(t)S(k,h)(\phi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right\}+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{f(\phi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\phi_{i}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{i}(t)S(k,h)(\phi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|,

which are referred to as the discretization error and truncation error,
respectively.
To analyze the discretization error,
the following function space is important.

Definition 3.1

Let
d
be a positive constant, and
let
\mathscr{D}_{d}(\epsilon)
be a rectangular domain defined
for
0<\epsilon<1
by

\mathscr{D}_{d}(\epsilon)=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\operatorname{Re}\zeta|<1/\epsilon,\,|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<d(1-\epsilon)\}.

Then,
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
denotes the family of all analytic functions
F

on
\mathscr{D}_{d}
such that the norm
\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)
is finite, where

\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{d}(\epsilon)}|F(\zeta)||\mathrm{d}\zeta|.

Under the definition,
the discretization error has been estimated as follows.

Theorem 3.3
(Okayama and Tanaka
[
14
, Theorem 3]
)

Let
d>0
, let
\phi
be a conformal map that maps
\mathscr{D}_{d}
onto
a complex domain
\mathscr{D}
containing
(a,b)
,
and let
F(\zeta)=f(\phi(\zeta))/g(\phi(\zeta))
.
Assume that
F\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Moreover,
assume that there exists a positive constant
\tilde{C}_{1}
,
for all nonnegative integers
\{\lambda_{l}\}_{l=0}^{j}
satisfying

\lambda_{0}=0
and
\sum_{l=1}^{j}l\lambda_{l}=j
, it holds that

\sup_{t\in(a,b)}\left|\left\{g(t)\sin\left(\frac{\piup\phi^{-1}(t)}{h}\right)\right\}^{(m-j)}\prod_{l=0}^{j}\left[\left(\phi^{-1}(t)\right)^{(l)}\right]^{\lambda_{l}}\right|\leq\tilde{C}_{1}h^{-m}\quad(j=0,\,1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,m).

(3.2)

Then, there exists a constant
\tilde{C}_{2}
independent of
h
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(a,b)}\left|f^{(m)}(t)-\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(\phi(kh))}{g(\phi(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g(t)S(k,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{C}_{2}\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)}{h^{m}\sinh(\piup d/h)}.

(3.3)

The most difficult point
to use this theorem
is showing the condition (
3.2
).
We slightly change the condition (
3.2
)
as follows,
which can be derived easily by using

\sin\theta=(\E^{\I\theta}-\E^{-\I\theta})/(2\I)
.

Theorem 3.4

Let
d>0
, let
\phi
be a conformal map that maps
\mathscr{D}_{d}
onto
a complex domain
\mathscr{D}
containing
(a,b)
,
and let
F(\zeta)=f(\phi(\zeta))/g(\phi(\zeta))
.
Assume that
F\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Moreover,
assume that there exists a positive constant
\tilde{C}_{1}
,
for all nonnegative integers
\{\lambda_{l}\}_{l=0}^{j}
satisfying

\lambda_{0}=0
and
\sum_{l=1}^{j}l\lambda_{l}=j
, it holds that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(a,b)\\
s\in[-\piup/h,\piup,h]\end{subarray}}\left|\left(\frac{\uppartial}{\uppartial t}\right)^{m-j}\left\{g(t)\E^{\I s\phi^{-1}(t)}\right\}\prod_{l=0}^{j}\left[\left(\phi^{-1}(t)\right)^{(l)}\right]^{\lambda_{l}}\right|\leq\tilde{C}_{1}h^{-m}\quad(j=0,\,1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,m).

(3.4)

Then, there exists a constant
\tilde{C}_{2}
independent of
h

such that (
3.3
) holds.

We use this theorem
to estimate the discretization error
(setting
g=g_{i}
and
\phi=\phi_{i}
(
i=2,\,4
)).
To estimate the truncation error,
we use the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
(Stenger
[
17
, Part of Theorem 4.4.2]
)

Let
d>0
, let
\phi
be a conformal map that maps
\mathscr{D}_{d}
onto
a complex domain
\mathscr{D}
containing
(a,b)
,
and let
F(\zeta)=f(\phi(\zeta))/g(\phi(\zeta))
.
Assume that there exist positive constants
R
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that

|F(x)|\leq\frac{R}{(1+\E^{-x})^{\alpha}(1+\E^{x})^{\beta}}

(3.5)

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, assume that there exists a constant
\tilde{C}_{1}
such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in(a,b)\\
s\in[-\piup/h,\piup/h]\end{subarray}}\left|\left(\frac{\uppartial}{\uppartial t}\right)^{m}g(t)\E^{\I s\phi^{-1}(t)}\right|\leq\tilde{C}_{1}h^{-m}.

(3.6)

Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
and
let
M
and
N
be defined as (
2.2
).
Then, it holds that

\sup_{t\in(a,b)}\left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}\frac{f(\phi(kh))}{g(\phi(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g(t)S(k,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right\}+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{f(\phi(kh))}{g(\phi(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g(t)S(k,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|\leq\tilde{C}_{1}h^{-m}\frac{2R}{\mu h}\E^{-\mu nh}.

Combining Theorem
3.4

and Lemma
3.5
,
setting
h
as (
2.3
),
we have

\displaystyle\left|f^{(m)}(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}\frac{f(\phi_{i}(kh))}{g_{i}(\phi_{i}(kh))}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{m}\left\{g_{i}(t)S(k,h)(\phi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\tilde{C}_{2}\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)}{1-\E^{-2\piup d/h}}h\cdot h^{-m-1}\E^{-\piup d/h}+\frac{2\tilde{C}_{1}R}{\mu}h^{-m-1}\E^{-\mu nh}

\displaystyle=2\left\{\frac{\tilde{C}_{2}\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)}{1-\E^{-2\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}}}\sqrt{\frac{\piup d}{\mu n}}+\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}R}{\mu}\right\}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mu n}{\piup d}}\right)^{m+1}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\{\frac{\tilde{C}_{2}\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)}{1-\E^{-2\sqrt{\piup d\mu}}}\sqrt{\frac{\piup d}{\mu}}+\frac{\tilde{C}_{1}R}{\mu}\right\}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\mu n}{\piup d}}\right)^{m+1}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}},

from which we obtain the desired error bound.
This completes the proof of Theorems
3.1

and
3.2
.

3.2.2
Proof of Theorem
3.1

First, to use Theorem
3.4
,
we should show
F\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})

and the inequality (
3.4
)
with
g=g_{2}
and
\phi=\phi_{2}
.
The first task is completed by the following result.

Lemma 3.6
(Okayama et al.
[
12
, Lemma 4.4]
)

Assume that
\tilde{f}
is analytic in
\phi_{2}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup
,
and that there exist positive constants
K
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that

\left|\tilde{f}(z)\right|\leq K\left|\frac{z}{1+z}\right|^{\alpha}|\E^{-z}|^{\beta}

holds for
z\in\phi_{2}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Then, putting
F(\zeta)=\tilde{f}(\phi_{2}(\zeta))
,
we have
F\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.

Setting
\tilde{f}(z)=f(z)/g_{2}(z)
in this lemma, we obtain

F\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
For the second task, we prepare the following theorem,
two propositions and two lemmas.

Theorem 3.7
(Faà di Bruno’s formula (cf. Johnson
[
2
]
))

Assume that
f
and
\tilde{f}
are

j
times continuously differentiable.
Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{j}f(\tilde{f}(t))=\sum\frac{j!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{j}!}f^{(k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{j})}(\tilde{f}(t))\prod_{l=1}^{j}\left(\frac{\tilde{f}^{(l)}(t)}{l!}\right)^{k_{l}},

where the sum is over all different solutions in
nonnegative integers
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
, …,
k_{j}
of

1\cdot k_{1}+2\cdot k_{2}+\cdots+j\cdot k_{j}=j.

(3.7)

Proposition 3.8

For any nonnegative integer
j
, there exists a positive constant
C_{j}

depending only on
j
such that

\left|g_{2}^{(j)}(t)\right|\leq C_{j}(1-\E^{-t})^{m-j}

holds for all
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof 1

The claim clearly holds for
j=0
.
For any positive integer
j
, there exist constants

Proposition 3.9

For any positive integer
j
, there exists a positive constant

Proof 2

Thus, the claim also holds for

Lemma 3.10

Let
j
be a nonnegative integer,
and

be nonnegative integers

C_{j}

depending only on

\left|\prod_{l=0}^{j}\left[\left\{\phi_{2}^{-1}(t)\right\}^{(l)}\right]^{\lambda_{l}}\right|\leq C_{j}(1-\E^{-t})^{-j}

holds for all
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof 3

From Proposition
3.9
,
there exists a positive constant
C_{l}

such that

\left|\left[\left\{\phi_{2}^{-1}(t)\right\}^{(l)}\right]^{\lambda_{l}}\right|\leq\left[C_{l}(1-\E^{-t})^{-l}\right]^{\lambda_{l}}=C_{l}^{\lambda_{l}}(1-\E^{-t})^{-l\lambda_{l}}

Thus, using

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{l=0}^{j}\left[\left\{\phi_{2}^{-1}(t)\right\}^{(l)}\right]^{\lambda_{l}}\right|\leq C_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}C_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}\cdots C_{j}^{\lambda_{j}}(1-\E^{-t})^{-(1\lambda_{1}+2\lambda_{2}+\cdots+l\lambda_{l})}=C_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}C_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}\cdots C_{j}^{\lambda_{j}}(1-\E^{-t})^{-j},

Lemma 3.11

)

Proposition 3.16

Let
p(t)=(t+\sqrt{4+t^{2}})/2
.
For any positive integer

C_{l}

\left|p^{(l)}(t)\right|\leq\frac{C_{l}}{(4+t^{2})^{(l+1)/2}}

(3.9)

Proof 7

For any positive integer
l\geq 2

a_{0},\,a_{1},\,\ldots,\,a_{l-2}

p^{(l)}(t)=\frac{a_{0}+a_{1}t+\cdots+a_{l-3}t^{l-3}+a_{l-2}t^{l-2}}{(4+t^{2})^{(2l-1)/2}},

which can be shown by induction.
Using
|t|=\sqrt{t^{2}}\leq\sqrt{4+t^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|p^{(l)}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|a_{0}|+|a_{1}|(4+t^{2})^{1/2}+\cdots+|a_{l-3}|(4+t^{2})^{(l-3)/2}+|a_{l-2}|(4+t^{2})^{(l-2)/2}}{(4+t^{2})^{(2l-1)/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|a_{0}|(4+t^{2})^{(l-2)/2}+|a_{1}|(4+t^{2})^{(l-2)/2}+\cdots+|a_{l-3}|(4+t^{2})^{(l-2)/2}+|a_{l-2}|(4+t^{2})^{(l-2)/2}}{(4+t^{2})^{(2l-1)/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|a_{0}|+|a_{1}|+\cdots+|a_{l-3}|+|a_{l-2}|}{(4+t^{2})^{(l+1)/2}},

which is the desired result.

Proposition 3.17

It holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
that

\frac{1}{1-\E^{-(t+\sqrt{4+t^{2}})/2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{2(1-\E^{-1/2})}.

Proof 8

Using
\sqrt{t^{2}}\geq-t
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\left(t+\sqrt{4+t^{2}}\right)=\frac{(t+\sqrt{4+t^{2}})(\sqrt{4+t^{2}}-t)}{2(\sqrt{4+t^{2}}-t)}\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}+\sqrt{t^{2}}}\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}+\sqrt{4+t^{2}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}},

from which it holds that

\frac{1}{1-\E^{-(t+\sqrt{4+t^{2}})/2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\E^{-1/\sqrt{4+t^{2}}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}}.

Putting
u=1/\sqrt{4+t^{2}}
and
q(u)=u/(1-\E^{-u})
,
we investigate the maximum of
q(u)
for
u\in[0,1/2]
.
Calculating the derivative of
q
gives

q^{\prime}(u)=\frac{r(u)}{(1-\E^{-u})^{2}},

where
r(u)=1-\E^{-u}(1+u)
.
We readily have
r(u)\geq 0
because
\E^{u}\geq 1+u
holds
for
u\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, we have
q^{\prime}(u)\geq 0
,
which implies that
q(u)
monotonically increases for
u\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus,
q(u)\leq q(1/2)
holds for
u\in[0,1/2]
,
which gives the desired inequality.

Proposition 3.18

For any positive integer

depending only on

\left|\left\{\phi_{4}^{-1}(t)\right\}^{(j)}\right|\leq C_{j}

Proof 9

Put

.
We use Faà di Bruno’s formula (Theorem

)
with

(note that

Put
K_{j}=k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{j}
,
where
k_{1},\,k_{2},\,\ldots,\,k_{j}
are nonnegative integers
satisfying (
3.7
).
Then, from Proposition
3.9
, it holds that

\left|f^{(K_{j})}(\tilde{f}(t))\right|\leq C_{K_{j}}(1-\E^{-p(t)})^{-K_{j}}\leq C_{K_{j}}(1-\E^{-p(t)})^{-j},

where

K_{j}\leq 1\cdot k_{1}+2\cdot k_{2}+\cdots+j\cdot k_{j}=j

is used at the last inequality.
Next, we consider the bound of
|\tilde{f}^{(l)}(t)|

for
t<0
and
t\geq 0
separately.
Note that if
t<0

3.9

|\tilde{f}^{\prime}(t)|=|p^{\prime}(t)|=\frac{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}+t}{2\sqrt{4+t^{2}}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}-t}{\sqrt{4+t^{2}}-t}=\frac{2}{4+t^{2}-t\sqrt{4+t^{2}}}<\frac{2}{4+t^{2}-0}.

Therefore, for

\displaystyle\left|f^{(K_{j})}(t)\prod_{l=1}^{j}\left\{\frac{\tilde{f}^{(l)}(t)}{l!}\right\}^{k_{l}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{K_{j}}(1-\E^{-p(t)})^{-j}\prod_{l=1}^{j}\left\{\frac{C_{l}}{l!(4+t^{2})^{(l+1)/2}}\right\}^{k_{l}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{K_{j}}(1-\E^{-p(t)})^{-j}\prod_{l=1}^{j}\left\{\frac{C_{l}}{l!(4+t^{2})^{l/2}}\right\}^{k_{l}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{K_{j}}}{(1-\E^{-p(t)})^{j}}\left\{\left(\frac{C_{1}}{1!}\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\frac{C_{2}}{2!}\right)^{k_{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{C_{j}}{j!}\right)^{k_{j}}\cdot\frac{1}{(4+t^{2})^{(k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+jk_{j})/2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{K_{j}}}{(1-\E^{-p(t)})^{j}}\cdot\frac{1}{(4+t^{2})^{j/2}}\prod_{l=1}^{j}\left(\frac{C_{l}}{l!}\right)^{k_{l}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{K_{j}}}{\left\{2(1-\E^{-1/2})\right\}^{j}}\prod_{l=1}^{j}\left(\frac{C_{l}}{l!}\right)^{k_{l}},

where Proposition

Lemma 3.23

,

5
Concluding remarks

Stenger
[
17
,
18
]
proposed
approximation formulas for derivatives
based on the Sinc approximation (
1.1
)
combined with appropriate conformal maps,
which were determined according to the target interval
(a,b)

and the decay rate of the given function
f(t)
.
When
(a,b)=(0,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays exponentially as

t\to\infty
, he adopted the conformal map
\psi_{2}(x)=\arsinh(\E^{x})
.
When
(a,b)=(-\infty,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays algebraically as

t\to-\infty
and exponentially as
t\to\infty
,
he adopted the conformal map
\psi_{4}(x)=\sinh(\log(\arsinh(\E^{x})))
.
He also provided convergence theorems of the two formulas
as described in Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
,
which claim that the convergence rate of his formulas
is
\OO(n^{(m+1)/2}\exp(-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}))
.
Here,
m
denotes the order of derivative,
and
d
and
\mu
denote the parameters that are determined
by the regularity and the decay rate of
f(t)
, respectively.
In this study,
we proposed improved formulas by replacing the conformal maps
in his formula;
replace
\psi_{2}(x)
with
\phi_{2}(x)=\log(1+\E^{x})
,
and
\psi_{4}(x)
with
\phi_{4}(x)=2\sinh(\log(\log(1+\E^{x})))
.
Furthermore, we provided convergence theorems of the improved formulas
as described in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
,
which claim that the replacement may increase the value of
d

and
\mu
appearing in the convergence rate.
In fact, such improvements were observed in the numerical experiments
presented in Section
4
.

The conformal maps have a room for further improvement.
All conformal maps that appear in this paper
are categorized as the single-exponential transformations.
In various formulas based on the Sinc approximation,
acceleration of convergence has been reported
by replacing the single-exponential transformations with the
double-exponential transformations
[
4
,
6
,
20
]
.
Indeed, such an improvement was recently reported
for derivatives over the finite interval
[
9
]
.
We are currently working on improvements for other cases
by employing the double-exponential transformations
adopted in different contexts
[
7
]
.

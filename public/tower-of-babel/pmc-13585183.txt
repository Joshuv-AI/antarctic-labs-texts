Effects of Intranasal Dexmedetomidine on the Night Before Surgery on Postoperative Delirium in Elderly Patients Undergoing Total Knee/Hip Arthroplasty. A Parallel-Arm, Randomized Controlled, Non-Inferiority Trial
Authors: Yangyang Chen, Keqin Wang, Yuqi Zhang, Ziqian Zhang, Tianyu Yin, Yucheng Liu, Chi Wang, Weijia Chen, Mengying Zhang, Linlin Zhao, Yan Wu, Xinghe Wang, Su Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ImportanceIt is crucial to identify preoperative treatment methods that can safely and effectively reduce the incidence of postoperative delirium.ObjectiveTo evaluate whether preoperative intranasal dexmedetomidine administration for preventing postoperative delirium is non-inferior to intravenous dexmedetomidine infusion on the operative day before induction for elderly patients undergoing elective knee/hip arthroplasty.DesignThis randomized clinical trial was performed at a tertiary hospital in China between January 10, 2025 to August 30, 2025. The follow-up for the last subject was completed on September 6, 2025.SettingThis was a single center trial.ParticipantsAfter qualification screening, 535 patients scheduled for unilateral total knee arthroplasty and total hip arthroplasty under general anesthesia were invited to participate in the study. Ultimately, 316 patients were randomly assigned to the experimental group and the control group.InterventionsPatients were randomized to receive intranasal dexmedetomidine (the night before surgery)+intravenous normal saline (before induction) or intranasal normal saline (the night before surgery)+intravenous dexmedetomidine (before induction).Main Outcomes and MeasuresThe primary outcome was incidence of postoperative delirium within 3 days after surgery assessed by the Confusion Assessment Method.ResultsIn the intention-to-treat analysis, the incidence of postoperative delirium within 3 days was 9.5% in experimental group and 7.6% in control group. The rate difference was 0.02 (95% CI: −0.04 to 0.08), meeting the pre-specified non-inferiority criterion (P < 0.001). Patients in experimental group showed significantly improved sleep quality on the night before surgery and lower preoperative anxiety and pain scores. Furthermore, the incidence of adverse events was significantly lower in experimental group.Conclusions and RelevanceFor elderly patients undergoing major joint arthroplasty, intranasal dexmedetomidine administered the night before surgery is non-inferior to pre-induction intravenous infusion for preventing postoperative delirium. The intranasal route offers additional benefits, including improved preoperative sleep, reduced anxiety, and superior hemodynamic stability.

Introduction

Postoperative delirium (POD) is a common and serious complication among elderly patients undergoing major surgery and is characterized by acute and fluctuating disturbances in attention and cognition.1,2 It is independently associated with prolonged hospitalization, increased healthcare expenditures, higher rates of post-discharge institutionalization, long-term cognitive decline, and elevated mortality.3–5 In the context of major orthopedic surgeries, particularly total knee arthroplasty (TKA) and total hip arthroplasty (THA), which are characterized by an elderly patient cohort and substantial surgical stress, the incidence of POD can reach as high as 53%. This renders POD a critical determinant of perioperative morbidity and a significant impediment to optimal postoperative rehabilitation.6–8 Given its profound adverse effects on both the physical and psychological well-being of older adults, effective prevention of POD remains a critical clinical priority.

The pathophysiology of POD is multifactorial and incompletely understood.9 Current mechanistic hypotheses emphasize neuroinflammation, neurotransmitter dysregulation, and disruption of the sleep-wake cycle.10,11 Among these, preoperative sleep disturbances, characterized by reduced sleep efficiency and fragmented sleep architecture, have been identified as independent predictors of POD, particularly in patients undergoing major surgical procedures.12,13

Numerous studies have demonstrated the efficacy of perioperative intravenous dexmedetomidine (DEX) in reducing the incidence of POD.14,15 DEX is a highly selective α2-adrenergic receptor agonist that provides anxiolysis, sedation, and modest analgesia with minimal respiratory depression.16–18 Its potential protective effect against POD is believed to arise from its ability to modulate perioperative risk factors, including sleep disturbance, anxiety, and pain.19–23 However, prior investigations have predominantly focused on administration during intra- and postoperative periods. On the night before surgery, patients frequently experience significant sleep deprivation and peak anxiety levels, precipitated by an unfamiliar environment and apprehension regarding the impending operation.24,25 These disturbances are frequently driven by preoperative anxiety, which may also heighten pain perception and exacerbate baseline discomfort, thereby negatively influencing postoperative recovery.26 Pharmacologic intervention at the point of induction often misses the optimal therapeutic window for interrupting the cascade of preoperative stress and sleep deprivation. Whether preoperative administration of DEX can improve perioperative outcomes by targeting these modifiable factors warrants further investigation.

Accordingly, we hypothesized that administration of DEX on the night before surgery would be non-inferior to intravenous DEX administered prior to anesthetic induction in reducing the incidence of POD among elderly patients undergoing total knee (TKA) or total hip arthroplasty (THA). Given the practical advantages of intranasal delivery, including ease of administration, favorable tolerability, avoidance of hepatic first-pass metabolism, and a relatively low incidence of hemodynamic adverse events such as hypotension and bradycardia,26,27 we selected intranasal DEX as the preoperative intervention.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This prospective, single-center non-inferiority randomized clinical trial was conducted from January 10, 2025, to August 30, 2025. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University (approval number: XYFY2024-KL510-01) on October 22, 2024, and it was registered at the China Clinical Trial Center before the first patient was enrolled (ChiCTR2500095511). Written informed consent was obtained from each participant before their enrollment. The study followed the CONSORT reporting guideline.28

Patients Recruitment

We screened potential participants among patients scheduled for elective TKA or THA. Patients aged 65 years or older with American Society of Anesthesiologists health status 1 to 3 and undergoing elective unilateral TKA or THA under general anesthesia were eligible to participate. Patients were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score less than 15; preoperative history of schizophrenia, epilepsy, Parkinsonism, or myasthenia gravis; inability to communicate in the preoperative period (coma, profound dementia, or language barrier); known preoperative left ventricular ejection fraction less than 30%; sick sinus syndrome, severe sinus bradycardia (<50 beats per min [bpm]), or second-degree or greater atrioventricular block without pacemaker; serious hepatic dysfunction (Child-Pugh class C); serious renal dysfunction (undergoing dialysis before surgery); or allergic rhinitis and abnormal nasal structure. The dropout criteria included withdrawal of consent, cancellation of the procedure, admission to ICU, serious intraoperative anesthetic or surgical accidents (such as drug allergy, malignant hyperthermia, difficult airway, difficult to correct hemodynamic fluctuations), or the occurrence of an adverse event that the investigator determines requires withdrawal from the study.

Randomization and Masking

We randomly assigned enrolled patients in a 1:1 ratio to intranasal or intravenous DEX. Randomization was stratified by type of surgery, using block randomization (block sizes of 4). Allocation concealment was achieved through the use of opaque sealed envelopes managed by an independent investigator who was not involved in outcome evaluation or data analysis. The envelope was handed over to an independent researcher, who prepared the intervention medication for the patients based on their group assignments, the day before the surgery. The surgeons, anesthesiologists, patients, and researchers responsible for postoperative visits and statistics were unaware of the group assignments.

Anesthesia Procedure

Intraoperative anesthesia management was performed according to the routine of our center: All patients received the standard monitoring of electrocardiography, pulse oxygen saturation and invasive blood pressure before induction. Midazolam, etomidate, sufentanil and rocuronium were used for anesthesia induction, and propofol, remifentanil and sevoflurane were used for anesthesia maintenance. Patients undergoing TKA received ultrasound-guided femoral nerve block with 20 mL of 0.375% ropivacaine; those undergoing THA received ultrasound-guided fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB) with 40 mL of the same agent. Postoperative analgesia was administered with a patient-controlled intravenous infusion: 1.5µg/kg of sufentanil and 6 mg of tropisetron with saline up to 100 mL.

Intervention

The Experimental Group

Prior to sleep on the night before surgery, DEX was administered as a nasal spray. One spray was applied to each nostril and retained for 30 seconds, followed by an additional spray in each nostril, resulting in a total of four sprays (100 μg). This administration was completed within two minutes. In the control group, the same dose of normal saline was administered at the same time using the same method.

The Control Group

Ten minutes before induction, DEX was administered intravenously at a dosage of 0.6 μg/kg, with DEX diluted to a concentration of 2 μg/mL. In the experimental group, the same dose of normal saline was administered at the same time using the same method.

Outcome Measurement

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of this study was the incidence of POD in both groups during the first three postoperative days, which was diagnosed using Confusion Assessment Method (CAM).29 The assessment of POD was performed twice daily (at 8:00–10:00 and 18:00–20:00) by trained researchers in the orthopedic ward, with follow-up telephone interviews conducted after discharge. Due to the fluctuating nature of delirium, researchers also consulted the patients’ families to gather insights into the patients’ symptoms, thereby minimizing the likelihood of missed diagnoses.

Secondary Outcomes

Secondary outcomes included the incidence of POD during the first 7 postoperative days, the assessment of delirium subtypes and severity utilizing the Delirium Rating Scale Revised 98,30,31 and delirium duration (the days from delirium onset to the last day delirium was recorded). The subjective sleep quality on the day before surgery was assessed using the Richard-Campbell Sleep Questionnaire (RCSQ)32 on the morning of surgery. Objective sleep quality was monitored using the Fitbit Charge 2 smart bracelets.33,34 The bracelet was applied to the patient’s non-surgical limb by ward nurses with proper tightness and was worn from 9 p.m. the night before surgery to 6 a.m. on the third day after surgery. The device monitored and recorded sleep parameters, including total sleep duration, deep sleep duration, light sleep duration, and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep. Sleep efficiency was defined as the total sleep duration divided by the wearing duration.

Before surgery, before induction and on postoperative days 1 to 3, pain levels at rest and during movement were assessed by numeric rating scale (NRS) and anxiety score was evaluated by the Self-Rating Anxiety Scale (SAS). Other secondary outcomes included the postoperative inflammatory markers (including IL-6, TNF-α, hs-CRP, White blood cell count and Neutrophil count), incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) within 48 hours postoperatively, the incidence of rescue analgesia within 24h after surgery, time to first ambulation, durations of postoperative hospital care (defined as the time from the end of surgery to discharge) and the quality of recovery assessed by the Quality of Recovery Score (QoR-15) on postoperative day 3.

Safety indicators included hypotension (systolic blood pressure <95 mm Hg or ≥30% decrease from baseline), hypertension (systolic blood pressure >180 mm Hg or ≥30% increase from baseline), bradycardia (heart rate <45 bpm), tachycardia (heart rate >100 bpm), and hypoxemia (peripheral oxygen saturation <90%) and nasal mucosal injuries were recorded by trained researchers.

Statistical Analysis

Sample Size

The incidence of POD among elderly patients undergoing spine surgery with intravenous pump DEX prior to induction was reported to be 6.1%.27 It is noteworthy that TKA or THA exhibit a higher incidence of delirium compared to spinal surgeries. Therefore, we estimated that the incidence of POD in the control group will be 10%. The non-inferiority margin was defined following the EMA 2005 guideline.35 The historical absolute risk reduction (ARR) for intravenous DEX (0.6 μg/kg) before induction versus no intervention was 34.4%.27 Per the guideline, the margin must be smaller than this historical effect and tightened by clinical judgement. Considering the higher POD baseline in elderly arthroplasty versus spinal surgery, an absolute POD increase of ≤10% was deemed clinically acceptable and well below untreated rates. Thus, the non-inferiority margin was set as 10%. To achieve 80% power and a 0.025 one-sided significance level for the test, a total of 284 cases were needed. The sample size was increased to 316 (158 in each group) to account for a 10% dropout rate. PASS version 15.0 (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, USA) was used for sample size calculation.

Data Analysis

Continuous variables were described using mean and standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR), depending on the normality of distribution, which was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed data were analyzed using independent sample t-tests, while non-normally distributed data were analyzed with the Mann–Whitney U-test. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and proportions (n, %) and were compared using the chi-square (X2) test or Fisher’s exact test. For data collected at multiple time points, generalized estimating equations with Bonferroni correction were employed for analysis.

The primary outcome was analyzed on both the intention-to-treat (ITT) population and the per-protocol (PP) population. For the other outcomes, an intention-to-treat analysis was performed. The Primary outcome between the two groups was compared with a one-sided non-inferiority test. The time until postoperative delirium (POD) onset within seven days after the operation was evaluated using Kaplan-Meier survival curves.

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 25.0 software (IBM SPSS, Armonk, NY, USA) and R Studio 4.1.0 (R Studio, Boston, MA, USA). Statistical significances were set as P < 0.025 (1-sided for the primary outcome) and P < 0.05 (2-sided for all others).

Results

Patient Characteristics and Perioperative Variables

After eligibility screening, 535 patients scheduled to undergo unilateral TKA or THA under general anesthesia were assessed for participation. A total of 316 patients were ultimately randomized to either the experimental group or the control group and were included in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis. In the experimental group, three surgeries were canceled and two patients required postoperative ICU transfer, resulting in 153 patients included in the per-protocol (PP) analysis. In the control group, two surgeries were canceled and four patients were transferred to the ICU postoperatively, yielding 152 patients in the PP analysis. In the PP analysis, corresponding incidences were 9.2% and 7.2%, respectively. The rate difference between groups was 0.02 (95% CI: −0.04 to 0.08), meeting the predefined non-inferiority margin (P < 0.001 for non-inferiority) (Figure 2). Other Outcomes and Adverse Events

Before induction of anesthesia, numerical rating scale (NRS) pain scores at rest and during movement were significantly lower in the experimental group (P = 0.018 and P = 0.012, respectively). On postoperative day 1, NRS scores at rest and during movement were higher in the experimental group (P = 0.030 and P = 0.023, respectively). No significant between-group differences were observed on postoperative days 2 or 3 (P > 0.05). The incidence of postoperative rescue analgesia (P = 0.126) and time to first ambulation (P = 1.000) were similar between groups.

Self-Rating Anxiety Scale (SAS) scores prior to induction were significantly lower in the experimental group (P = 0.002). However, on postoperative days 1 and 2, SAS scores were higher in the experimental group (P < 0.001). By postoperative day 3, this difference was no longer statistically significant (P = 0.094).

There were no significant differences in postoperative length of hospital stay, QoR-15 scores on postoperative day 3, or the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) within 48 hours (P > 0.05).

The incidences of hypotension (P = 0.021), hypertension (P = 0.001), and bradycardia (P = 0.001) were significantly lower in the experimental group than in the control group. Other adverse events occurred at comparable rates (Table 3).

Discussion

This randomized trial demonstrates that intranasal DEX administered on the night before surgery is non-inferior to intravenous DEX administered before anesthetic induction in preventing POD among elderly patients undergoing TKA or THA. Previous high-quality studies have established the efficacy of perioperative intravenous DEX in preventing POD.36–39 Our findings extend this evidence by shifting the timing of intervention to the preoperative night, a period that has received limited attention despite its clinical importance. Sleep disruption on the night before surgery is common and often severe, driven by anxiety, environmental changes, and anticipatory stress.

The night before surgery is a critical yet understudied period where patients often experience severe sleep deprivation due to factors such as anxiety and unfamiliar environments.40,41 Preoperative sleep disturbances are an independent risk factor for POD.12,19 Our objective sleep data confirmed patients receiving intranasal DEX achieved longer total sleep time, increased light and deep sleep times, and higher sleep efficiency on the night before surgery. Subjective sleep quality also improved significantly. Moreover, anxiety and pain were also reduced prior to surgery. Intranasal DEX not only provides a new clinical option for preventing POD with efficacy comparable to standard intravenous regimens but also significantly enhances the feasibility and compliance, as it can be administered easily by ward nurses without intravenous access, making it particularly suitable for routine preoperative care.

Mechanistically, DEX exerts its effects through selective activation of central α2-adrenergic receptors, particularly within the locus coeruleus, inhibit the excitability of neurons, resulting in reduced norepinephrine release sedative, analgesic, anti-anxiety, and anti-sympathetic effects, which alleviates preoperative anxiety and modulates pain perception by inhibiting sympathetic tone and nociceptive signal transmission at the spinal level.42,43 By improving sleep depth and efficiency, intranasal DEX may mitigate the neuroinflammatory priming associated with sleep deprivation and thereby reduce susceptibility to POD.44 These effects may further stabilize neurotransmitter balance and enhance resilience to postoperative inflammatory stress.45,46

The lower incidence of intraoperative bradycardia and reduced phenylephrine requirements observed in the experimental group are consistent with the pharmacokinetic profile of intranasal administration. Intravenous DEX can produce rapid plasma concentration peaks, potentially leading to transient hypertension followed by reflex bradycardia and hypotension.47 In contrast, intranasal absorption yields a smoother plasma concentration–time profile, minimizing abrupt hemodynamic fluctuations. For elderly patients with diminished physiological reserve, such hemodynamic stability is particularly advantageous.48,49

No significant differences were detected in short-term clinical outcomes, including POD duration and severity, 7-day POD incidence, hospital length of stay, or overall quality of recovery (QoR-15). These findings further confirm that intranasal DEX administered on the night before surgery is non-inferior to intravenous DEX administered before anesthetic induction in preventing POD among elderly patients undergoing TKA or THA. The higher pain and anxiety scores observed in the experimental group on the first postoperative day likely reflects a pharmacodynamic washout effect. Due to dexmedetomidine’s short half-life (about 2 hours),50 the effects of preoperative night nasal DEX would have dissipated by the first postoperative day, whereas the pre-induction intravenous dose retains residual activity during this window. Importantly, these differences were marginal and unaccompanied by changes in rescue analgesia requirements, indicating limited clinical significance despite statistical significance. The higher white blood cell counts and TNF-α levels observed in the experimental group on postoperative day 1 was unexpected given the established anti-inflammatory properties of dexmedetomidine. This may relate to differences in drug administration routes and kinetics, or reflect complex interactions between preoperative sedation, nocturnal sleep architecture, and subsequent inflammatory responses. This exploratory finding requires confirmation in future studies.

This study has some limitations that need to be considered. First, this was a single-center study involving elderly patients undergoing THA or TKA. Variations in therapeutic measures and clinical practice across institutions may influence the external validity and generalizability of the findings. Future multicenter studies are warranted to confirm these results across diverse settings. Second, objective sleep was assessed using Fitbit Charge 2 devices rather than polysomnography (PSG). Although PSG remains the gold standard for sleep assessment, its time-intensiveness, high cost, and intrusiveness preclude its widespread applicability.51 On the other hand, wearable devices provide a non-invasive and user-friendly alternative that minimizes disruption to nighttime sleep, thereby improving patient compliance.52,53 Third, follow-up was limited to 7 days postoperatively, precluding the assessment of long-term cognitive outcomes. Future multicenter studies with larger sample sizes and extended follow-up durations are warranted. While pharmacological neuroprotection with α2-agonists remains a cornerstone for high-risk surgical populations, emerging evidence supports the integration of non-pharmacological adjuncts into a multimodal paradigm.54,55 These interventions converge on common pathways involving suppression of systemic inflammation, enhancement of synaptic plasticity, and mitigation of perioperative sleep disruption. Rather than supplanting pharmacological prophylaxis, such strategies may synergistically augment its efficacy. Future studies should therefore explore the combined effects of intranasal dexmedetomidine and non-pharmacological adjuncts within integrated, multimodal POD prevention pathways, to determine whether such combinations yield additive or synergistic reductions in delirium incidence and severity.

In conclusion, preoperative intranasal DEX administered on the night before surgery is non-inferior to intravenous DEX administered before induction in preventing postoperative delirium among elderly patients undergoing elective TKA or THA. The intranasal DEX administration offers clinically meaningful advantages, including improved preoperative sleep architecture, reduced preoperative anxiety and pain, enhanced patient comfort, and a lower incidence of hemodynamic adverse events.

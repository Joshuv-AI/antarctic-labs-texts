Long‐acting monoclonal antibodies and the evolving landscape of infant respiratory syncytial virus prevention
Authors: Ran Wang, Bahaa Abu‐Raya, Hala Obeid, Zhengde Xie
Published: 2026

INTRODUCTION

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is one of the leading causes of acute lower respiratory tract infections in infants, particularly during the first months of life, and remains a major contributor to emergency department visits and hospitalizations in this population. 1 , 2 For decades, RSV prevention was limited to palivizumab, a short‐acting monoclonal antibody (mAb) administered monthly to infants at increased risk of severe disease, such as those born preterm or with selected underlying medical conditions. 3 More recently, the preventive landscape has evolved with the introduction of long‐acting mAbs, first nirsevimab and, more recently, clesrovimab, both of which were designed to provide season‐long protection with a single dose in infants during their first RSV season. 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 Nirsevimab has been implemented at scale in multiple countries, whereas clesrovimab has only recently been authorized, and its current evidence base is derived mainly from pivotal clinical trials. 6 , 7 Together, these advances have shifted infant RSV prevention from a strategy focused mainly on high‐risk infants to an intervention that can be extended to the broader infant population, regardless of pre‐existing comorbidities.

DISEASE BURDEN OF RSV AND THE SHIFT IN PREVENTIVE STRATEGIES

Global estimates indicate that in 2019, approximately 33.0 million episodes of RSV‐associated acute lower respiratory tract infections occurred among children younger than 5 years worldwide, of which about 3.6 million resulted in hospitalization. 8 The burden of RSV hospitalization is highest in early infancy, particularly during the first 6 months of life, a period during which maternally derived RSV antibodies decline. 2 , 9 , 10 Hospitalization rates are highest among infants younger than 6 months and decline thereafter with increasing age. 2 , 11 Younger infants are disproportionately represented among severe RSV cases, including those requiring intensive care admission. 11 , 12

Before the introduction of long‐acting mAbs against RSV, passive prevention was limited to palivizumab in selected high‐risk infants, including those born very preterm and those with certain comorbidities, such as chronic lung disease of prematurity or hemodynamically significant congenital heart disease. 3 Because palivizumab requires repeated monthly administration throughout the RSV season, its broader use has been constrained by cost and logistical complexity. 3 , 13

Since 2023, the infant RSV prevention landscape has broadened to include maternal vaccination with RSV prefusion F subunit vaccine (RSVpreF) during pregnancy and long‐acting mAbs, initially with nirsevimab and more recently with clesrovimab. 6 , 14 , 15 Together, the introduction of these products marked an important shift in infant RSV prevention beyond palivizumab‐based prophylaxis for selected high‐risk groups. This paper focuses on long‐acting mAbs designed to provide protection throughout an infant's first RSV season. 14 , 16

NIRSEVIMAB REAL‐WORLD EFFECTIVENESS AND CLESROVIMAB EFFICACY IN CLINICAL TRIALS

Evidence for nirsevimab's efficacy was first established in a placebo‐controlled phase 2b trial conducted in healthy preterm infants born at 29 weeks 0 days to 34 weeks 6 days of gestation, in which a single dose reduced medically attended RSV‐associated lower respiratory tract infection (LRTI) by 70.1% and RSV‐associated LRTI hospitalization by 78.4% through 150 days after injection. 4 This was followed by the phase 3 MELODY trial in healthy late‐preterm and term infants born at ≥35 weeks’ gestation, in which efficacy against medically attended RSV‐associated LRTI was 74.5% through 150 days after dosing. 5 In addition, the pragmatic phase 3b HARMONIE trial, conducted under conditions approximating routine clinical practice in France, Germany, and the United Kingdom, showed that nirsevimab reduced hospitalization for RSV‐associated LRTI by 83.2% and very severe RSV‐associated LRTI by 75.7%. 17

Post‐licensure evidence has generally supported nirsevimab's high effectiveness, while also supporting the feasibility of implementation. In a prospective maternity‐based cohort study in France, parental acceptance of nirsevimab was high, and the types and frequencies of adverse events did not differ significantly between exposed and unexposed newborns during follow‐up. 18 In Spain, a nationwide population‐based case‐control study found that effectiveness against RSV hospitalization was 71% by intention‐to‐treat and 80% per protocol for catch‐up immunization at the start of the season, and 78% by intention‐to‐treat and 83% per protocol for infants immunized within the first 2 weeks of life during the RSV season. 19 In Chile, evaluation of the first season of a national nirsevimab program showed an effectiveness of 76.4% against RSV‐related LRTI hospitalization and 84.9% against RSV‐related intensive care unit (ICU) hospitalization, suggesting a measurable population‐level impact after rollout. 20 In the United States, surveillance‐based estimates showed that nirsevimab effectiveness was 89% against medically attended RSV‐associated acute respiratory infection and 93% against RSV‐associated hospitalization. 21 In another US test‐negative case‐control study, the estimated effectiveness was 68.4% against medically attended RSV infection, 80.5% against RSV‐associated hospitalization, and 84.6% against severe RSV disease. 22

Importantly, a systematic review and meta‐analysis of real‐world studies from multiple countries found that nirsevimab recipients were associated with substantially lower odds of RSV‐related hospitalization (odds ratio 0.17) and ICU admission (odds ratio 0.19), supporting the consistency of effectiveness estimates across settings. 23

Clesrovimab, a recently approved long‐acting mAb targeting antigenic site IV of the RSV F protein, has been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration, Health Canada, and the European Commission. 24 , 25 , 26 In healthy preterm and full‐term infants, a single dose reduced RSV‐associated medically attended LRTI by 60.4% and RSV‐associated hospitalization by 84.2% through day 150. 7 In infants and children at increased risk of severe RSV disease, the SMART study supported a safety profile comparable to palivizumab and provided exposure data used to extrapolate efficacy from the CLEVER cohort, rather than directly establishing efficacy in that population. 27

POPULATION COVERAGE AND HEALTH EQUITY

High coverage is essential to translate the individual‐level effectiveness of nirsevimab into population‐level reductions in RSV burden and depends on both parental acceptance and programmatic access. In one US cohort, 183 of 261 eligible infants (70.1%) received nirsevimab before hospital discharge. 28 In a pediatric hospital implementation study, 99% of eligible infants were offered nirsevimab before discharge, and 71% received it, highlighting how workflow and access can strongly influence uptake. 29 Importantly, nirsevimab uptake was still observed among infants whose mothers had not received RSVpreF or other recommended maternal vaccines during pregnancy (19/47, 40.4%) and among infants whose parents had declined hepatitis B vaccination at birth (17/50, 34.0%). 28 At a broader population level, a systematic review and meta‐analysis estimated pooled nirsevimab uptake at 90.1% in Spain and 51.2% in the United States during the 2023/2024 RSV season, with uptake varying across sociodemographic and clinical subgroups. 30

Health equity remains central to infant RSV prevention because baseline RSV burden is often higher in socially disadvantaged populations. Lower parental socioeconomic status has been associated with a higher risk of infant RSV hospitalization. 31 Emerging data suggest that nirsevimab's uptake is heterogeneous across populations: a French cohort study documented sociodemographic inequalities in nirsevimab receipt, with lower outpatient immunization in socioeconomically deprived infants and in areas with poorer health care accessibility. 32 In a US pediatric primary care network, only 38.4% of eligible infants received nirsevimab, and unadjusted analyses suggested differences by race and insurance type. 33 A separate study from a large, diverse pediatric primary care network similarly reported receipt in only about one‐third of eligible children despite availability across the network. 34 Together, these findings suggest that equitable implementation of long‐acting infant RSV mAbs will likely require more than product availability alone. It will be important to monitor the uptake of clesrovimab and to assess how it is influenced by social determinants of health.

COST‐EFFECTIVENESS AND HEALTH SYSTEM IMPACT

Decisions regarding the incorporation of long‐acting infant RSV mAbs into routine practice depend not only on product effectiveness but also on affordability, implementation context, and the extent to which prevention can reduce pressure on pediatric health systems. In a US economic evaluation, if 50% of the birth cohort received nirsevimab, 107 253 outpatient visits, 38 204 emergency department visits, and 14 341 hospitalizations could be averted annually, at an incremental cost‐effectiveness ratio of $153 517 per quality‐adjusted life year saved. 35 A Canadian model similarly showed that the preferred strategy depends on product price, regional risk, and healthcare use; universal country‐wide nirsevimab would require a price‐per‐dose below $112 to become the most cost‐effective option, whereas in higher‐burden northern settings broader strategies could be cost‐saving. 36 However, economic value also depends on how programs are designed in practice. In Tuscany, a real‐world cost‐benefit analysis suggested a trade‐off between maximizing the number of hospitalizations prevented and maximizing economic return: an all‐infant strategy covering infants born before and during the RSV season prevented the greatest number of RSV‐associated hospitalizations, whereas more targeted seasonal strategies yielded more favorable benefit‐cost ratios. 37

These considerations are especially relevant because RSV surges can substantially strain pediatric services. During the 2022 Oregon RSV surge, the median pediatric inpatient census increased by 19%, the median pediatric ICU census by 50%, and weekly elective pediatric admissions at children's hospitals decreased by 33%. 38 In contrast, early real‐world data from Tuscany showed that universal nirsevimab immunization during the 2024/2025 season was associated with an 82.1% reduction in RSV‐related hospitalizations and an 85.2% reduction in pediatric ICU admissions among eligible infants. 39 Together, these data suggest that the impact of long‐acting infant RSV mAbs may extend beyond individual protection and may include preservation of pediatric bed capacity during peak respiratory virus circulation.

BREAKTHROUGH INFECTIONS AND THE NEED FOR ONGOING VIRAL SURVEILLANCE

As the use of long‐acting mAbs expands, continued surveillance for breakthrough infections and resistance‐associated viral changes remains important. Nirsevimab is a long‐acting mAb that targets antigenic site Ø on the prefusion RSV F protein, 40 , 41 whereas clesrovimab targets site IV, which is present in both prefusion and postfusion conformations of the RSV F protein. 6 Before widespread clinical use, global sequence data showed that the nirsevimab binding site in RSV F was highly conserved between 1956 and 2021, and escape‐associated variants were rare. 40 After nirsevimab introduction, a genotypic and phenotypic study of breakthrough infections found that resistance‐associated substitutions remained uncommon overall and were identified in two of 24 RSV‐B breakthrough infections, whereas no escape mutation was detected among 236 RSV‐A breakthrough viruses; importantly, no large‐scale expansion of resistant lineages was observed. 42 Additional genomic surveillance during the 2023/2024 season of nirsevimab administration in Spain identified RSV‐B lineages carrying mutations in the nirsevimab‐binding region, but neutralization assays indicated that nirsevimab activity was preserved and no significant antigenic drift compromising immunoprophylaxis was observed. 43 More recent data from France during the 2024/2025 RSV season reported the real‐world emergence of nirsevimab resistance in RSV‐B breakthrough infections. This finding does not negate the clinical value of nirsevimab, but it reinforces the need for continued subtype‐specific genomic surveillance across successive RSV seasons. 44 Mechanistic work has further shown that the S190R substitution in RSV‐A F can reduce nirsevimab binding and neutralization, although this mutation also appears to impair viral fitness, reinforcing the need for continued molecular surveillance rather than implying current clinical failure of nirsevimab. 41 The detection of breakthrough infections should not, by itself, be interpreted as evidence that nirsevimab has lost clinical or public health value. Post‐licensure genomic surveillance and breakthrough infection data are not yet available for clesrovimab.

LONG‐ACTING mABs IN THE CONTEXT OF RSV VACCINATION IN PREGNANCY

Maternal RSVpreF vaccination should be considered alongside long‐acting infant RSV mAbs, including nirsevimab and clesrovimab, when discussing infant RSV prevention. In the phase 3 MATISSE trial, maternal RSVpreF vaccination reduced medically attended severe RSV‐associated LRTI in infants, with protection likely mediated at least in part through transplacental transfer of maternal antibodies. 45 Real‐world studies from Argentina and Scotland have since shown that maternal RSVpreF vaccination during pregnancy is associated with protection against RSV‐associated hospitalization in young infants. 46 , 47 , 48 Key differences between long‐acting mAbs and maternal RSVpreF vaccination are summarized in Figure 1.

Compared with long‐acting infant RSV mAbs, maternal RSVpreF vaccination avoids the need for an intramuscular injection in the infant and, when administered sufficiently before delivery, provides passive protection from birth. However, protection may be less than optimal when delivery occurs within 14 days after maternal vaccination, as shorter vaccination‐to‐delivery intervals are associated with less efficient transplacental antibody transfer. 48 , 49 RSVpreF vaccination in pregnancy induces RSV‐specific antibody responses in breast milk, 50 although the extent to which these contribute additional infant protection beyond transplacental transfer remains uncertain. Nirsevimab provides passive protection through an mAb directed against a single antigenic site on prefusion RSV F, and viral variation affecting that epitope therefore remains a particular surveillance concern. Clesrovimab fits within the same broad passive‐protection framework, although it targets a different antigenic site that is present in both the prefusion and postfusion conformations of the RSV F protein. By contrast, RSVpreF vaccination induces a broader polyclonal antibody response, including neutralizing activity against contemporary isolates and tested mAb‐resistant strains. 51 Taken together, maternal RSVpreF vaccination and infant mAbs represent two infant‐protection strategies with distinct advantages and limitations. In real‐world policy, the key question is how to avoid unnecessary overlap while maintaining protection for infants who may not be adequately protected by maternal vaccination alone. Most infants are expected to need either maternal RSVpreF vaccination or an infant mAb rather than both, but infant mAbs remain important when maternal vaccination was absent, unknown, or too close to delivery, and in infants born preterm, those with insufficient antibody transfer, or those with high‐risk conditions. Therefore, strategy selection should consider vaccination timing, gestational age, infant risk status, local seasonality, product availability, and program feasibility.

FROM EARLY SUCCESS TO SUSTAINABLE LONG‐TERM PRACTICE

Beyond early post‐licensure success, the next question is whether these gains can be sustained across successive RSV seasons and health systems. Here, second‐season evidence should be interpreted carefully because it may refer either to a later program season involving newly eligible infants or to the same child entering a subsequent RSV season after a single infant dose. In Spain, surveillance during the 2024/2025 season estimated an overall effectiveness of 65.5% against RSV‐associated hospitalization, with similar estimates in infants immunized at the start of the campaign and those immunized after birth, supporting continued program‐level performance in a subsequent implementation season. 52 By contrast, a separate two‐season population‐based case‐control study from Spain followed healthy term‐born infants after a single dose and reported low or null effectiveness when the same children entered their subsequent RSV season, although the authors noted that this estimate may have been underestimated because of unavoidable survivor bias. 53 Another two‐season study from Navarre, Spain, estimated effectiveness against RSV hospitalization at 89.9% in 2023/2024 and 52.8% in 2024/2025, with no statistically significant difference between the two seasons. 54 Emerging data from other settings also suggest ongoing population‐level benefit after implementation; in Piedmont, Italy, the 2024/2025 season was associated with a notable decrease in RSV‐related healthcare utilization compared with the two previous seasons, and estimated effectiveness exceeded 80% for RSV‐related emergency visits, hospitalization, and respiratory support. 55 By contrast, comparable second‐season effectiveness and post‐implementation durability data are not yet available for clesrovimab.

CONCLUSIONS AND LOOKING AHEAD

Long‐acting mAbs are reshaping the framework of infant RSV prevention, shifting it from selective prophylaxis for a limited high‐risk population towards a broader prevention strategy in early life. Future work should focus on comparative positioning alongside maternal RSVpreF vaccination, durability across seasons, surveillance for viral evolution, and implementation strategies that support equitable, affordable, and sustainable delivery across different health systems.

i.e., hierarchical fermion masses and large and small mixing angles as well as CP phases.
Various approaches have been studied.

2
Modular symmetry in magnetized compactifications

Here, we give a brief review of zero-mode wave functions in torus and orbifold compactifications
with magnetic fluxes and their transformation behaviors under the modular symmetry.

2.1
Zero-mode wave functions

First, we briefly review
T^{2}
compactification with magnetic flux with an emphasis on zero-mode wave functions.
We use the complex coordinate
z=x+\tau y
, where
x
and
y
are real coordinates and
\tau
is the
complex structure modulus.
We identify
z\sim z+1\sim z+\tau
on
T^{2}
.

We consider
U(1)
gauge theory and introduce the magnetic flux background:

\displaystyle F=\frac{\pi iM}{{\rm Im}\tau}dz\wedge d\bar{z},

(2.1)

where
M
must be an integer because of Dirac’s quantization condition.
In what follows, we study the positive magnetic flux, i.e.
M>0
.
Let us study zero-modes of the Dirac equation with
U(1)
charge
q=1
under the above magnetic flux
background.
There appear
M
zero-modes, and
their wave functions
\psi^{j,M}(z,\tau)
with
j=0,1,\cdots,M-1
are written by
[
42
]
,

\displaystyle\psi^{j,M}(z,\tau)=\frac{M^{1/4}}{{\cal A}^{1/2}}e^{\pi iMz\frac{{\rm Im}z}{{\rm Im}\tau}}\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}\frac{j}{M}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}(Mz,M\tau),

(2.2)

where
\cal A
denotes the ares of
T^{2}
, and

\vartheta
denotes the Jacobi theta function:

\displaystyle\vartheta\begin{bmatrix}a\\
b\\
\end{bmatrix}(\nu,\tau)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi i(a+\ell)^{2}\tau}e^{2\pi i(a+\ell)(\nu+b)}.

(2.3)

The above zero-mode wave functions have the following property:

\displaystyle\psi^{j,M}(z,\tau)=\psi^{M-j,M}(-z,\tau).

(2.4)

Thus, one can construct zero-mode wave functions on
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, which can be obtained by the identification
z\sim-z
on
T^{2}

[
43
]
.
When
j\neq 0
or
M/2
,
even modes
\Psi^{j,M}_{0}(z,\tau)
and odd modes
\Psi^{j,M}_{1}(z,\tau)
on
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
can be written by

\displaystyle\Psi^{j,M}_{m}(z,\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\psi^{j,M}(z,\tau)+(-1)^{m}\psi^{M-j,M}(z,\tau)\right),

(2.5)

for
m=0,1
.
For
j=0
and
M/2
, the zero-mode wave functions
\psi^{j,M}(z,\tau)
also correspond to
the even mode wave functions
\Psi^{j,M}_{0}(z,\tau)
on
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

2.2
Modular symmetry

The modular group
\Gamma=SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
is the group of
(2\times 2)
matrices:

\displaystyle\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{pmatrix},

(2.6)

where
a,b,c,d
are integers and satisfy
ad-bc=1
.
This group is generated by two generators,
S
and
T
,

\displaystyle S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(2.7)

They satisfy

\displaystyle S^{2}=-1,\qquad(ST)^{3}=1.

(2.8)

The modulus
\tau
transforms as

\displaystyle\tau\to\gamma\tau=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},

(2.9)

while the coordinate
z
also transforms as

\displaystyle z\to\gamma z=\frac{z}{c\tau+d}.

(2.10)

The zero-mode wave functions on
T^{2}
transform under the double covering group of
\Gamma
,
\tilde{\Gamma}

[
24
,
26
]
.(See for
\tilde{\Gamma}
Ref.
[
14
]
.)
In order to represent
\tilde{\Gamma}
, we introduce a new element
\tilde{Z}
, which expands the center of
\Gamma
.
The generators
\tilde{S}
and
\tilde{T}
of
\tilde{\Gamma}
satisfy the following algebraic relations:

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{2}=\tilde{Z},\quad(\tilde{S}\tilde{T})=\tilde{Z}^{2},\quad\tilde{Z}^{4}=1,\quad\tilde{Z}\tilde{T}=\tilde{T}\tilde{Z}.

(2.11)

Under the
\tilde{S}
transformation, the zero-mode wave functions transform as

\displaystyle\psi^{j,M}(\tilde{S}z,\tilde{S}\tau)=\tilde{J}(\tilde{S},\tau)\sum_{k}\rho(\tilde{S})_{jk}\psi^{k,M}(z,\tau),

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{S})_{jk}=e^{\pi i/4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}e^{2\pi i\frac{jk}{M}}.

(2.12)

Here,
\tilde{J}(\tilde{S},\tau)
denotes the automorphy factor, and it is obtained as

\tilde{J}(\tilde{S},\tau)=(-\tau)^{1/2}
.
1
1

1

See also for discussions on modular weights Ref.
[
44
]
.

That shows that the zero-mode wave functions have a modular weight
1/2
.
The
\tilde{T}
transformation can be represented by the wave functions when
M=
even
[
26
]
.
2
2

2

When we consider the wave functions with non-vanishing Sherk-Schwarz phases, the
\tilde{T}
transformation can be represented even for
M=
odd
[
26
]
.

Thus, we focus on the case with
M=
even.
Under the
\tilde{T}
transformation, the zero-modes wave functions transform as

\displaystyle\psi^{j,M}(\tilde{T}z,\tilde{T}\tau)=\sum_{k}\rho(\tilde{T})_{jk}\psi^{k,M}(z,\tau),

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{T})_{jk}=e^{\pi i\frac{j^{2}}{M}}\delta_{j,k}.

(2.13)

The elements
\rho(\tilde{S})
and
\rho(\tilde{T})
form a finite group
[
26
]
.
Note that
(\rho(\tilde{T}))^{2M}=1
, and it generates the
\mathbb{Z}_{2M}
group.

Similarly, we can write the modular transformations in the
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
basis.

3
Flavor symmetries in magnetized compactifications

In the previous section, we have reviewed the modular flavor symmetry of zero modes on
T^{2}
and
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
One can extend it to
T^{4}
and its orbifold, which may correspond to the effective field theory of magnetized
D7
-brane models.
In general, the modular symmetry of the complex structure moduli
\Omega
of
T^{4}
is

Sp(4,\mathbb{Z})
.
Here, we restrict ourselves to the factorizable

T_{1}^{2}\times T_{2}^{2}
and its orbifold, where two complex structure moduli,
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
, appear and their modular symmetry becomes
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})_{\tau_{1}}\times SL(2,\mathbb{Z})_{\tau_{2}}\subset Sp(4,\mathbb{Z})
.
We denote the coordinates on the first and second
T^{2}
by
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, respectively.
Also, we introduce the magnetic fluxes
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
on
T^{2}_{1}
and
T^{2}_{2}
, respectively,
as in the previous section.
Then, zero-mode wave functions can be written by
use of the wave functions
\psi^{j,M}(z,\tau)
on
T^{2}
in the previous section as
\psi^{j,M_{1}}(z_{1},\tau_{1})\psi^{k,M_{2}}(z_{2},\tau_{2})
.
Their flavor structure is a direct product of those on the first and second
T^{2}
’s.

The three-form flux background is one of definite moduli stabilization scenarios
[
45
]
.
Certain three-form flux backgrounds lead to the relation between moduli as
\tau_{2}=N\tau_{1}\ (N\in\mathbb{N})

[
41
]
in the supersymmetric minimum.
For example, when the three-form flux background induces the following superpotential:

\displaystyle W=(\tau_{2}-N\tau_{1})g(\Phi),

(3.1)

where
\Phi
denote moduli fields other than
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
,
the above constraint can be realized.
The modular subgroups such as
\Gamma_{0}(N)
,
\Gamma^{0}(N)
, and their products remain
[
41
]
.
In this section, we study the flavor symmetries of zero-modes when we constrain

\displaystyle\tau_{2}=N\tau_{1}.

(3.2)

3.1
Subgroups

First, we define congruence subgroups,
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and
\Gamma^{0}(N)
as follows,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{Z})~\middle|~c\equiv 0~~~{\rm mod}~N\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}(N)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,\mathbb{Z})~\middle|~b\equiv 0~~~{\rm mod}~N\right\}.

(3.3)

Note that
\Gamma_{0}(1)=\Gamma^{0}(1)=\Gamma=SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
.

The modular transformation of
\tau_{1}
,

\displaystyle\tau_{1}\to\frac{a_{1}\tau_{1}+b_{1}}{c_{1}\tau_{1}+d_{1}},

(3.4)

leads to the following transformation of
\tau_{2}
:

\displaystyle\tau_{2}\to\frac{a_{1}\tau_{2}+Nb_{1}}{\frac{c_{1}}{N}\tau_{2}+d_{1}},

(3.5)

through the relation (
3.2
).
We may have a degree of freedom to multiply both the denominator and numerator by a common integer
m
, but

m
must be
m=\pm 1
by the following reason.
We require that the following matrix:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&Nb_{1}\\
\frac{c_{1}}{N}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix}

(3.6)

must belong to
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
That is, we must have gcd
(a_{2},b_{2})={\rm gcd}(a_{2},c_{2})={\rm gcd}(b_{2},d_{2})={\rm gcd}(c_{2},d_{2})=1
, i.e.,
m=\pm 1
.
Furthermore, the above requirement means that
c_{1}\equiv 0
mod
N
.
In addition, we find
b_{2}\equiv 0
mod
N
.
These results show that two
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
symmetries of
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
are broken down to

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N),

(3.7)

and

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma^{0}(N).

(3.8)

For example, the generators of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
are written by Refs.
[
46
,
47
]
:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(2)

\displaystyle\,:\,\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=T,\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
2&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=U^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(3)

\displaystyle\,:\,\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=T,\quad-\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
3&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=S^{2}U^{3},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(4)

\displaystyle\,:\,\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}=S^{2},\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=T,\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
4&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=U^{4},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(5)

\displaystyle\,:\,\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=T,\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
5&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=U^{5},\quad\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\
5&2\\
\end{pmatrix}=U^{2}SU^{3},

(3.9)

where
U=S^{2}TST
.
Similarly, the generators of
\Gamma^{0}(N)
are written by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}(2)

\displaystyle\,:\,\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=U,\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=T^{2},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}(3)

\displaystyle\,:\,\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=U,\quad-\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=S^{2}T^{3},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}(4)

\displaystyle\,:\,\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}=S^{2},\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=U,\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&4\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=T^{4},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}(5)

\displaystyle\,:\,\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=U,\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&5\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=T^{5},\quad\begin{pmatrix}3&5\\
1&2\\
\end{pmatrix}=T^{3}S^{3}T^{2}.

(3.10)

Note the correspondence of generators between

\Gamma_{0}(N)
and
\Gamma^{0}(N)
through Eq. (
3.6
).
Specifically,
\Gamma^{0}(N)
and
\Gamma_{0}(N)
are isomorphic to each other, and the generators of
\Gamma^{0}(N)
are given by the transposed generators of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

3.2
Symmetries of zero-modes

Now, let us study the transformation behaviors of zero-mode wave functions
under the constraint
\tau_{2}=N\tau_{1}
.
At the moment, we study transformation behaviors of wave functions only on the first

T_{1}^{2}
.
Since we have already reviewed their transformation behaviors under
\tilde{S}
and

\tilde{T}
, we focus on transformations by
\tilde{U}=\tilde{S}^{2}\tilde{T}\tilde{S}\tilde{T}
.
In general, the zero-mode wave functions on
T_{1}^{2}
transform under
\tilde{U}^{N}
as

\displaystyle\psi^{j,M_{1}}(\tilde{U}^{N}z_{1},\tilde{U}^{N}\tau_{1})=\tilde{J}(\tilde{U}^{N},\tau_{1})\sum_{k}\rho(\tilde{U}^{N})_{jk}\psi^{k,M_{1}}(z_{1},\tau_{1}),

(3.11)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{J}(\tilde{U}^{N},\tau_{1})=(N\tau_{1}+1)^{1/2},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{U}^{N})_{jk}=\frac{(-1)^{N-1}i}{\sqrt{NM_{1}}}e^{\pi i/4}e^{\frac{\pi i}{NM_{1}}(j-k)^{2}}\sum^{N-1}_{l=0}e^{\frac{\pi iM_{1}}{N}l^{2}}e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{N}(j-k)l}.

The explicit computation of
\rho(\tilde{U}^{N})
is given in Appendix A.
Then, the zero-mode wave functions
\Psi^{j,M}_{m}
on
T^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
transform as

\displaystyle\Psi^{j,M}_{m}(\tilde{\gamma}z,\tilde{\gamma}\tau)=\tilde{J}(\tilde{\gamma},\tau)\sum^{M/2}_{k=0}\rho_{m}(\tilde{\gamma})_{jk}\Psi^{k,M}_{m}(z,\tau),

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(\tilde{S})_{jk}=\mathcal{N}^{j}_{t,2}\mathcal{N}^{k}_{t,2}\frac{4e^{\pi i/4}}{\sqrt{M}}\cos{\Big(\frac{2\pi jk}{M}\Big)},\quad\rho_{0}(\tilde{T})_{jk}=e^{\pi i\frac{j^{2}}{M}}\delta_{j,k}~~{\rm for}~~{\rm even\ modes},

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(\tilde{S})_{jk}=\mathcal{N}^{j}_{t,2}\mathcal{N}^{k}_{t,2}\frac{4ie^{\pi i/4}}{\sqrt{M}}\sin{\Big(\frac{2\pi jk}{M}\Big)},\quad\rho_{1}(\tilde{T})_{jk}=e^{\pi i\frac{j^{2}}{M}}\delta_{j,k}~~{\rm for}~~{\rm odd\ modes},

(3.13)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{j}_{t,2}=\left\{\,\begin{aligned} &1/2\quad(j=0,M/2)\\
&1/\sqrt{2}\quad(\rm otherwise)\\
\end{aligned}\right..

(3.14)

3.2.1

M_{1}\neq 0
and
M_{2}=0

In this case,
\rho(\tilde{U}),\rho(\tilde{U}^{2}),\rho(\tilde{U}^{3})

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{U})=\frac{e^{\pi i/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}i&-1\\

\end{pmatrix},\quad\rho(\tilde{U}^{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{U}^{3})=\frac{e^{\pi i/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-1&i\\

\end{pmatrix},\quad\rho(\tilde{U}^{4})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

by the two zero-modes on the first

\rho(\tilde{U}^{2})
as well as

\rho(\tilde{T})
satisfies

\mathbb{Z}_{2M}

When

Also,

\mathbb{Z}_{4}

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{S}^{2}\tilde{U}^{3})=\frac{e^{\pi i/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-i&-1\\
-1&-i\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\rho(\tilde{S}^{2}\tilde{U}^{3})^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\

\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{S}^{2}\tilde{U}^{3})^{3}=\frac{e^{\pi i/4}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\
i&1\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\rho(\tilde{S}^{2}\tilde{U}^{3})^{4}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(3.16)

The total flavor group includes the
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
of

and

\mathbb{Z}_{4}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
of
\rho(\tilde{U})
.
By use of the above results, we can identify the full symmetries of

with

For

, the generators,

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{T})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

4
Conclusion

We have studied the flavor symmetries of zero-modes, which are originated
from the modular symmetry on
T^{2}_{1}\times T^{2}_{2}
and its orbifold with magnetic fluxes.
Our models may correspond to the effective field theory of magnetized
D7
-brane models.
We have introduced the constraint on the moduli parameters by
\tau_{2}=N\tau_{1}
.
Such a constraint can be realized by the moduli stabilization due to three-form flux backgrounds.
This constraint breaks the modular symmetry
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})_{\tau_{1}}\times SL(2,\mathbb{Z})_{\tau_{2}}
of two moduli,
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
to

\Gamma_{0}(N)\times\Gamma^{0}(N)
.
We can derive various flavor symmetries of zero-modes.
Some of them are the same as the group for
N=1
, but the cases with
N=2
and 4
lead to different groups, which are non-Abelian in many cases and interesting.
Abelian flavor groups are also interesting.
(See e.g. Ref.
[
51
]
.)
It would be interesting to build flavor models using these groups.
We leave it as a future work.

We have started with
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})_{\tau_{1}}\times SL(2,\mathbb{Z})_{\tau_{2}}
.
Then, we have introduced the constraint
\tau_{2}=N\tau_{1}
by physical reason, which is coming from the moduli stabilization.
As a result, we have obtained various interesting finite flavor groups.
It is interesting to extend our analysis to the cases including various constraints such as

N_{2}\tau_{2}=N_{1}\tau_{1}
for co-prime integers,

N_{1}
and
N_{2}
.
It is also important to study
more than two moduli parameters.
We would study them elsewhere.

Congruence subgroup property for nilpotent groups and subsurface subgroups of Mapping Class Groups
Adam Klukowski

Abstract
. We prove the Congruence Subgroup Property for two families of subgroups of Mapping Class Groups of finite-type surfaces. The first one is related to nilpotent quotients of the fundamental group and Johnson filtration, and along the way we give an elementary proof of a theorem of Ben-Ezra–Lubotzky. The second family consists of certain geometric subgroups obtained via subsurface inclusions.



1.
Introduction

Many important groups arise from automorphisms of other groups. Examples include the families
\textnormal{GL}_{n}\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\textnormal{Aut}(F_{n})\curvearrowright F_{n}
, as well as the outer automorphism groups
\textnormal{Out}(F_{n})
. Another family of examples are the Mapping Class Groups
\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
for a surface
\Sigma
, which can be regarded as a subgroup of
\textnormal{Out}(\pi_{1}\Sigma)
according to the theorem of Dehn-Nielsen-Baer
[
FM12
, Chapter 8]
. In such situations, actions on finite characteristic quotients of the base group induce a system of finite-index subgroups, called
congruence subgroups
, which induce the
congruence topology
. A basic question towards understanding their algebraic structure is whether every finite-index subgroup arises this way, or equivalently, whether the profinite and pro-congruence topologies agree. This is referred to as having the
Congruence Subgroup Property (CSP)
.

Groups possessing the Congruence Subgroup Property include
\textnormal{SL}_{n}\mathbb{Z}
for
n\geqslant 3
, by the work of Mennicke
[
Men65
]
and Bass–Lazard–Milnor–Serre
[
BLS64
]
[
BMS67
]
(see also
[
PR10
]
). Another two groups with CSP are
\textnormal{Aut}(F_{2})
and
\textnormal{Out}(F_{2})
, by the work of Asada
[
Asa01
]
(see also
[
BER11
]
[
BEL18
]
). An example where CSP fails is
\textnormal{SL}_{2}\mathbb{Z}
(this is one of the consequences of its virtual freeness). This is somewhat surprising, given that the natural map
\textnormal{Out}(F_{2})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}\mathbb{Z}
is an isomorphism; the explanation is that the actions of this group on
F_{2}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
induce different families of congruence subgroups.

The main focus of this article is the Congruence Subgroup Property of Mapping Class Groups of finite-type surfaces. CSP was proven in genus 0 by Diaz-Donagi-Harbater
[
DDH89
]
(see also
[
McR10
]
), in genus 1 with punctures by Asada
[
Asa01
]
[
BER11
]
(with no punctures, the Mapping Class Group is
\textnormal{SL}_{2}\mathbb{Z}
and the fundamental group is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and we mentioned that CSP fails in this case), and in genus 2 by Boggi
[
Bog09
]
. The question of CSP in genus at least 3 is open, and mentioned in the Kirby List
[
Kir97
, Problem 2.10]
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a surface of genus at least 3. Then every finite-index subgroup of
\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
is a congruence subgroup.

One motivation for this question is its link to the study of profinite rigidity of 3-manifold groups. The most notorious open problem in this area was stated by Bridson–Reid
[
Bri23
, Conjecture 2.1]
. Below we present a certain weakening of their conjecture (compare
[
WS24
, Corollary B]
).

Conjecture 2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
are hyperbolic 3-manifolds such that profinite completions of their fundamental groups are isomorphic. Then
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
are commensurable, meaning that they share a common finite-degree covering space.

Positive answer to Conjecture
2
is known in a few special cases
[
CW23
]
, which among other results rely on knowing CSP of low-genus Mapping Class Groups. In general, only almost profinite rigidity is known
[
Liu23
]
, meaning that hyperbolic 3-manifold groups with a prescribed profinite completion fall into finitely many commensurability classes. Apart from profinite rigidity, understanding finite-index subgroups would also be beneficial for a number of other problems concerning MCGs, such as the Ivanov conjecture
[
Kir97
, Problem 2.11]
on virtual fibering of the moduli space, or property (T).

Usually, CSP is thought of as a property of the automorphism group. However, our approach is to look at analogous statements about simpler subgroups, similar to the philosophy of
[
McR10
]
dealing with point-pushing subgroups and
[
Bog24
]
investigating centralisers of finite subgroups. For these purposes it will be useful to have a more flexible definition.

Definition 3
.

Let
\lambda\colon\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(G)
be a group homomorphism. A
congruence subgroup
is any subgroup of
\Lambda
of the form
(q_{\star}\circ\lambda)^{-1}(H)
, where
q\colon G\twoheadrightarrow Q
is a finite characteristic quotient of
G
,
q_{\star}\colon\textnormal{Out}(G)\to\textnormal{Out}(Q)
is the induced map on outer automorphism groups, and
H\leq\textnormal{Out}(Q)
is any subgroup. If
H
is trivial, then its preimage is called
principal
.

We say that
\lambda
has the
congruence subgroup property (CSP)
if every finite-index subgroup of
\Lambda
containing
\ker\lambda
is a congruence subgroup.

Note that Definition
3
recovers the usual definition when
\lambda
is the identity homomorphism; also, Conjecture
1
can be phrased as the natural map
\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)\to\textnormal{Out}(\pi_{1})
having CSP. It is not hard to see that every subgroup containing a principal congruence subgroup is itself congruence.

This article contains two main theorems. The first one, Theorem
4
, holds for all nilpotent groups, and says that CSP can be determined by the action on abelianisation. This result is originally due to Ben-Ezra–Lubotzky
[
BEL22
]
; we present another proof that is elementary and requires no background theory. We use Theorem
4
to show that finite-index subgroups of Mapping Class Groups containing some term of the Johnson filtration are congruence subgroups. The second result, Theorem
8
, asserts that CSP of Mapping Class Groups behaves well under gluing smaller surfaces along their boundaries. As corollaries we show that many natural geometric subgroups of Mapping Class Groups have the Congruence Subgroup Property.

1.1.
Outer automorphisms of nilpotent groups

The first theorem of this article is that outer automorphism groups of nilpotent groups possess the congruence subgroup property, as long as their abelianisations do.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
G
is a finitely generated nilpotent group, and
\Lambda\leq\textnormal{Out}(G)
. If the linear action on abelianisation
\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(G_{\textnormal{ab}})
has CSP, then
\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(G)
has CSP.

This result (up to torsion) was first obtained by Ben-Ezra and Lubotzky
[
BEL22
, Theorem 1.1]
, basing on the theory of Malcev completion and unipotent groups and knowledge of CSP for solvable arithmetic groups. In Section
4.2
we give a self-contained proof of Theorem
4
involving only induction and elementary algebra.

Remark 5
.

Metabelian groups behave rather differently in terms of CSP
[
BEL18
]
.

As an application of Theorem
4
, we relate CSP to the Johnson filtration
\mathcal{I}^{(k)}
(see Definition
15
).

Corollary 6
.

If
\Gamma\leq\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
is a finite-index subgroup containing the Johnson subgroup
\mathcal{I}^{(k)}
for some
k
, then
\Gamma
is a congruence subgroup.

It was proved by Leiniger and McReynolds that solvable subgroups of Mapping Class Groups are separable
[
LM07
, Theorem 1.1]
. More careful examination of their proof reveals that the quotients they produce actually annihilate some term of the Johnson filtration. In this situation Corollary
6
applies, and upgrades separability to congruence separability.

Corollary 7
.

Solvable subgroups of
\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
are congruence separable. Explicitly, for any solvable
\Gamma\leq\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
and a mapping class
\varphi\notin\Gamma
there exists a congruence subgroup
\Delta\leq\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
such that
\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
and
\varphi\notin\Delta
.

1.2.
Subsurface subgroups of MCGs

The second result of this article asserts that CSP behaves well under inclusion of subsurfaces.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\Sigma
be a possibly punctured surface with no boundary, and let
\Sigma=\bigcup_{i}\Sigma_{i}
be its decomposition into disjoint subsurfaces glued along boundaries. Suppose that
\Lambda_{i}\leq\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma_{i})
are subgroups supported on the subsurfaces, and let
\Lambda=\prod_{i}\Lambda_{i}
be the subgroup of
\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
obtained from them by inclusion. If all
\Lambda_{i}\to\textnormal{Out}(\pi_{1}\Sigma_{i})
have the CSP, then
\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(\pi_{1}\Sigma)
has the CSP.

Theorem
8
is proven in Section
5.3
.

As a first application of Theorem
8
, we extend the main result of the excellent survey of McReynolds
[
McR10
]
from point-pushing to handle-pushing subgroups (see Section
6
for definition).

Corollary 9
.

Handle-pushing subgroups of Mapping Class Groups have the congruence subgroup property.

As a second application, using the work of Boggi
[
Bog09
]
, we deduce the CSP of stabilisers of large simplices in the curve complex.

Corollary 10
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a tuple of disjoint essential curves, such that every component of
\Sigma\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{B}
has genus at most 2. Then
\textnormal{Stab}_{\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)}(\mathcal{B})\to\textnormal{Out}(\pi_{1}\Sigma)
has CSP.

Recall that there are finitely many orbits of simplices in the curve complex under the action of the Mapping Class Group (any collections of curves, whose complement has the same topological type, are related by a mapping class – see
[
FM12
, Section 1.3]
). Algebraically this means that there are only finitely many conjugacy classes of handle-pushing subgroups in the Mapping Class Group. This observation allows us to deduce the following:

Corollary 11
.

Let
\Gamma\leq\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
be a finite-index subgroup. There exists a finite characteristic quotient
q\colon\pi_{1}\Sigma\to Q
with the following property. For every collection
\mathcal{B}
of simple closed curves, such that all components of the complement of
\mathcal{B}
have genus at most 2, we have
\left(\ker q_{\star}\right)\cap\textnormal{Stab}_{\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\Gamma
.

As a third application, we conditionally prove that curve stabilisers have CSP.

Corollary 12
.

Let
\beta
be a non-separating simple closed curve on
\Sigma_{g}
. If
\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma_{g-1})
has the CSP, then
\textnormal{Stab}(\beta)\leq\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma_{g})
has the CSP.

Using Theorem
8
, a proof of CSP for MCGs would follow if one could prove the following:

Conjecture 13
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite-index subgroup of
\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
, and
\alpha
a simple closed curve on
\Sigma
. Then there exists a congruence subgroup
\Delta\leq\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
such that the containment of orbits
\Delta.\alpha\subseteq\Gamma.\alpha
holds.

Notably, an analogue of Conjecture
13
appears in the recent independent work of Wilton and Sisto
[
WS24
]
. They prove a similar statement for a filling
\alpha
, conditional on every hyperbolic group being residually finite.

2.
A composition lemma for CSP

In this section we present the key tool to prove the CSP with a divide-and-conquer approach. This lemma appears in
[
McR10
, Proposition 3.2]
, in a more restricted formulation. Our proof uses the same strategy, but we write it in detail for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 14
.

Let
\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(G)
be a group homomorphism, with
\Lambda
finitely generated. Suppose there is a subgroup
\Delta\leq\Lambda
such that

(1)

the restricted homomorphism
\Delta\to\textnormal{Out}(G)
has CSP

(2)

every finite-index subgroup of
\Lambda
containing
\Delta
is a congruence subgroup.

Then
\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(G)
has CSP.

Sketch of proof of Lemma
14
.

Consider any finite-index
\Gamma\leq\Lambda
containing
\ker(\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(G))
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
\Gamma\triangleleft\Lambda
. Then the intersection
\Gamma\cap\Delta
is a finite-index subgroup of
\Delta
. Assumption (
1
) tells us that there exists a finite characteristic quotient
q\colon G\twoheadrightarrow Q
satisfying

(1)

\Delta\cap\ker q_{\star}\subseteq\Gamma.

Now consider
\Delta\cdot(\Gamma\cap\ker q_{\star})
. This is a finite-index subgroup of
\Lambda
containing
\Delta
. By assumption (
2
), there exists a finite characteristic quotient
r\colon G\twoheadrightarrow R
such that

(2)

\ker r_{\star}\cap\Lambda\subseteq\Delta\cdot(\Gamma\cap\ker q_{\star}).

Let
s\colon G\to S
be the pullback (or fibre product) of
q
and
r
. Explicitly, we consider the map
q\times r\colon G\to Q\times R
, and define
S=\operatorname{im}(q\times r)
to be the image of
G
inside
Q\times R
, and
s\colon G\twoheadrightarrow S
as the corresponding factorisation. We claim that the principal congruence subgroup induced by
(s,S)
is contained in
\Gamma
.

To prove the claim consider any
\varphi\in\ker s
. The quotient
r\colon G\to R
factors through
s\colon G\to S
, so
\varphi
acts on
R
by an inner automorphism. By the defining property (
2
) of
(r,R)
, this means that
\varphi\in\Delta\cdot(\Gamma\cap\ker q_{\star})
. Write
\varphi=\delta\gamma
for some
\delta\in\Delta
,
\gamma\in\Gamma\cap\ker q_{\star}
. Since both
\varphi,\gamma
belong to
\Lambda\cap\ker q_{\star}
, we must also have
\delta\in\Lambda\cap\ker q_{\star}
. But from the defining property (
1
) of
(q,Q)
it follows that
\delta\in\Gamma
. Since both
\delta,\gamma
are elements of
\Gamma
, then so is their product
\varphi
. This proves the claim.
∎

3.
Background on automorphisms of nilpotent groups

Here we review the basic facts about the filtration on automorphism groups of nilpotent groups. This theory was used by Andreadakis to study the automorphisms of free groups
[
And65
]
, and by Johnson for studying the Torelli subgroup of mapping class groups
[
Joh80
]
(free and surface groups are only residually nilpotent, but that is good enough).

The
lower central series

\gamma_{i}G
of a group
G
is defined inductively via
\gamma_{1}G=G
and
\gamma_{i+1}G=[G,\gamma_{i}G]
. We say that
G
is
nilpotent of class
K
when
\gamma_{K}G\neq\gamma_{K+1}G=\{1\}
. In particular, groups nilpotent of class 1 are precisely the abelian groups. We write
G_{\textnormal{ab}}=\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{2}G}}
for the abelianisation of
G
.

Let us introduce the key object used to keep track of the algebraic structure of
\textnormal{Out}(G)
.

Definition 15
.

The
Andreadakis–Johnson filtration
is the sequence of subgroups
\mathcal{I}^{(i)}\leq\textnormal{Out}(G)
defined by

\mathcal{I}^{(i)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{def}}}{{=}}\ker\left(\textnormal{Out}(G)\to\textnormal{Out}\left(\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}\right)\right).

Clearly,
\{1\}=\mathcal{I}^{(K)}\leq\mathcal{I}^{(K-1)}\leq\dots\leq\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\leq\mathcal{I}^{(1)}=\textnormal{Out}(G)
when
G
is nilpotent of class
K
. The goal of this section is Lemma
17
, which gives an explicit description of the successive quotients of the Andreadakis–Johnson filtration.

We start by understanding honest automorphisms.

Lemma 16
.

Suppose that
i\geqslant 1
and
\Phi\in\textnormal{Aut}(G)
acts trivially on
\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}
. Then the assignment
\bar{\delta}\Phi\colon x\mapsto\Phi(x)x^{-1}
descends to a well-defined
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphism
\bar{\delta}\Phi\colon G_{\textnormal{ab}}\to\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
.

Furthermore, the function

\bar{\delta}\quad\colon\quad\ker\left(\textnormal{Aut}(G)\to\textnormal{Aut}\left(\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}\right)\right)\quad\to\quad\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{\textnormal{ab}}\to\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}})

is a group homomorphism. Moreover, the kernel of
\bar{\delta}
consists of precisely those automorphisms which act trivially on
\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
.

Proof.

We just need to check a number of standard properties.

Part 1:

Each
\bar{\delta}\Phi
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphism
G_{\textnormal{ab}}\to\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
.

We start with
\bar{\delta}\Phi(x)=\Phi(x)x^{-1}\cdot\gamma_{i+2}G
as a function
G\to\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
between sets. Since
\Phi\curvearrowright\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}
by identity, we have
\Phi(x)\in x\cdot\gamma_{i+1}G
, so the image of
\bar{\delta}
lies in
\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
.

We claim that
\bar{\delta}\Phi
is actually a group homomorphism
G\to\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
. To confirm this take any
x,y\in G
, and note

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}\Phi(xy)

\displaystyle=\Phi(x)\Phi(y)y^{-1}x^{-1}=

\displaystyle=\bar{\delta}\Phi(x)\cdot x\cdot\bar{\delta}\Phi(y)\cdot x^{-1}\in\bar{\delta}\Phi(x)\cdot\bar{\delta}\Phi(y)\cdot\gamma_{i+2}G

where in the last step we used the fact that
\bar{\delta}\Phi(y)\in\gamma_{i+1}G
, so all of its conjugates are congruent modulo
\gamma_{i+2}G
. This shows that
\bar{\delta}\Phi
is a group homomorphism.

The target
\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
is abelian, so
\bar{\delta}\Phi
must factor through
G_{\textnormal{ab}}
. This factorisation is a homomorphism between abelian groups.

Part 2:

\bar{\delta}
itself is a group homomorphism.

We just need to verify that
\bar{\delta}
respects the group composition. Take any
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\in\textnormal{Aut}(G)
which act trivially on
\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}
. Then

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}(\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}(x)\Phi_{2}(x)^{-1}\cdot\Phi_{2}(x)x^{-1}=

\displaystyle=\bar{\delta}\Phi_{1}\big(\Phi_{2}(x)\big)\cdot\bar{\delta}(\Phi_{2})(x)=

\displaystyle=\bar{\delta}\Phi_{1}\big(\bar{\delta}\Phi_{2}(x)\big)\cdot\bar{\delta}\Phi_{1}(x)\cdot\bar{\delta}\Phi_{2}(x).

But we know that
\bar{\delta}\Phi_{2}(x)
is zero in
G_{\textnormal{ab}}
, so it is killed by
\bar{\delta}\Phi_{1}
, and the first factor vanishes. Therefore
\bar{\delta}(\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2})=\bar{\delta}\Phi_{1}+\bar{\delta}\Phi_{2}
, so
\bar{\delta}
is a group homomorphism from
\ker\left(\textnormal{Aut}(G)\to\textnormal{Aut}\left(\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}\right)\right)
to
\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{\textnormal{ab}}\to\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}})
.

Part 3:

Kernel of
\bar{\delta}
.

Finally
\bar{\delta}\Phi=0
is equivalent to having
\Phi(x)\in x\cdot\gamma_{i+2}G
for every
x\in G
, which precisely means that
\Phi\curvearrowright\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
by identity.
∎

We are ready to describe the key tool to understand the algebraic structure of automorphisms of nilpotent groups – the Johnson homomorphisms. Denote

(3)

H_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{def}}}{{=}}\left\{x\in G\ \big|\ [x,y]\in\gamma_{i+1}G\textnormal{ for all }y\in G\right\}.

Note that
\gamma_{i}G\leq H_{i}
. Also, with the notation from Lemma
16
, each
x\in H_{i}
induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphism
\bar{\delta}(\textnormal{ad}_{x})\colon G_{\textnormal{ab}}\to\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
(which is nothing other than the group commutator
[x,\bullet]
).

Lemma 17

(Johnson homomorphism)
.

Let
\bar{\delta}
be the homomorphism from Lemma
16
. For
\varphi\in\mathcal{I}^{(i)}
, pick a representative
\Phi\in\textnormal{Aut}(G)
which acts trivially on
\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}
. Then the assignment
\delta\colon\varphi\mapsto\bar{\delta}\Phi
is a well-defined
\mathbb{Z}
-module homomorphism

\delta\colon\mathcal{I}^{(i)}\to D_{i}\qquad\text{where}\qquad D_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{\textnormal{ab}}\to\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}})}{\left\{\bar{\delta}(\textnormal{ad}_{x})\ \big|\ x\in H_{i}\right\}}.

We call
\delta
the
Johnson homomorphism
. Moreover,
\ker\delta=\mathcal{I}^{(i+1)}
.

An immediate corollary of Lemma
17
is that
\nicefrac{{\mathcal{I}^{(i)}}}{{\mathcal{I}^{(i+1)}}}
is abelian.

Remark 18
.

Although Lemma
17
contains all of the algebra needed for our purposes, there is more structure to Johnson homomorphisms. To any finitely generated residually (torsion-free nilpotent) group one can associate a graded Lie algebra, by taking the direct sum of the successive quotients, and inducing Lie bracket from the group commutator. Then the homomorphisms
\bar{\delta}
from Lemma
16
produce graded derivations of the Lie algebra, and the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear maps that we use are recovered by restricting the derivations to the grade 1 subspace. Moreover,
\bar{\delta}
sends group-theoretic commutator in the autmorphism group to the Lie-algebra-theoretic commutator of derivations. The normal subgroup of inner automorphisms is sent to the Lie ideal of inner derivations. For a detailed exposition see e.g.
[
CHP11
, Section 2]
.

Proof of Lemma
17
.

First, note that
\bar{\delta}\circ\textnormal{ad}
is a group homomorphism from
H_{i}
to
\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(G_{\textnormal{ab}}\to\nicefrac{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}})
. Therefore
\left\{\bar{\delta}(\textnormal{ad}_{x})\ \big|\ x\in H_{i}\right\}
is a sub-
\mathbb{Z}
-module, so
D_{i}
is well-defined.

Second, we check well-definedness. For any
\phi\in\mathcal{I}^{(i)}
, a representative
\Phi\in\ker(\textnormal{Aut}(G)\to\textnormal{Aut}(\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}))
exists, and is unique up to conjugation by an element of
H_{i}
. But this ambiguity vanishes in
D_{i}
by definition. Therefore
\delta\colon\mathcal{I}^{(i)}\to D_{i}
is well-defined as a function between sets.

Third, we check that
\delta
is a group homomorphism. This is a consequence of
\bar{\delta}
being a group homomorphism, as asserted by Lemma
16
.

Finally we characterise the kernel. By definition
\delta\varphi=0
iff there exists
x\in H_{i}
such that
\bar{\delta}\Phi=\bar{\delta}(\textnormal{ad}_{x})
for some representative
\Phi
of
\varphi
which acts trivially on
\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+1}G}}
. This means that
\Phi\circ\textnormal{ad}_{x}^{-1}\in\ker\bar{\delta}
. By Lemma
16
, this happens precisely when
\Phi\circ\textnormal{ad}_{x}^{-1}
acts trivially on
\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma_{i+2}G}}
. And this is equivalent to
\varphi\in\mathcal{I}^{(i+1)}
.
∎

4.
CSP for automorphisms of nilpotent groups

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
4
. We start by proving in Subsection
4.1
two Lemmata
19
and
20
, which are technical tools for producing special quotients of nilpotent groups. We complete the proof of Theorem
4
at the end of Subsection
4.2
, after proving its special case Proposition
21
.

The overall proof approach is straightforward: to induct on the length of central series with the help of Lemma
14
. This is perhaps closest to the strategy outlined in
[
BEL22
, Section 4]
; however, thanks to Lemmata
19
and
20
, we will be able to complete the inductive step for arbitrary (including non-free) nilpotent groups.

4.1.
Auxiliary results

In this subsection we bundle the technical results towards Proposition
21
and Theorem
4
. The first one is a certain strenghtening of residual finiteness of nilpotent groups.

Lemma 19
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated nilpotent group of class
K
, and take any
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}

Before proceeding we recall the setup of Theorem

of finite type, possibly punctured but without boundary, and a collection of disjoint simple closed curves

which cuts

Let us explain the assumption of

5.1.
Boundary twists

Subsurface subgroups are amalgamated along the boundary twists. To take care of them we will use the following lemma.

Lemma 22
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a collection of disjoint simple closed curves on the surface
\Sigma
, and

Without loss of generality we can assume that elements of
\mathcal{B}
are pairwise non-homotopic and essential.

Consider the linearised action
\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(\textnormal{H}_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}))
. We claim that it has the CSP. To see this, observe that the image of
\Lambda
in
\textnormal{Sp}(\textnormal{H}_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}))
is an abelian subgroup generated by a collection of products of commuting transpositions. There exists a basis for
\textnormal{H}_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
in which these transpositions are unitriangular, and matrix multiplication becomes addition of off-diagonal entries. Then, every finite-index subgroup of the image of
\Lambda
contains the subgroup of matrices congruent to identity modulo some
M\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. But this is the principal congruence subgroup induced by the quotient
\textnormal{H}_{1}(\Sigma;\tfrac{\mathbb{Z}}{M\mathbb{Z}})
.

We have argued that
\Lambda\curvearrowright\textnormal{H}_{1}(\Sigma_{1};\mathbb{Z})
has the CSP. Write

for the third term in the lower central series of

, and
\mathcal{I}^{(2)}
for the second term in the Johnson filtration (as in Section

and Definition

). Theorem
4
tells us that the homomorphism

has the CSP.

We claim that the map
\Lambda\to\nicefrac{{\Lambda}}{{\mathcal{I}^{(2)}}}
is injective. Assuming this statement, we can deduce that
\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}\left(\nicefrac{{\pi_{1}\Sigma}}{{\gamma_{3}\pi_{1}\Sigma}}\right)
has the CSP. Since every quotient of
\nicefrac{{\pi_{1}\Sigma}}{{\gamma_{3}\pi_{1}\Sigma}}
is also naturally a quotient of
\pi_{1}\Sigma
, this implies that
\Lambda\to\textnormal{Out}(\pi_{1}\Sigma)
has the CSP and we are done.

It only remains to justify the claim that
\Lambda\cap\mathcal{I}^{(2)}
is trivial. Let
\Delta_{N}
and
\Delta_{S}
be the subgroups of
\textnormal{Mcg}(\Sigma)
generated by Dehn twists about non-separating and separating loops in
\mathcal{B}
respectively. Since
\Lambda\leq\Delta_{N}\Delta_{S}
, it is enough to prove that

is trivial. Note that the symplectic representation (which algebraically is the quotient by

) is faithful on

and annihilates

, which means that

. Therefore, it is enough to show that

\beta\in\mathcal{B}

which has geometric intersection number 2 with

and 0 with any other separating curve from

. Then we examine the action of
\Delta_{S}
on the class of

.

Predictive value of serum S100A8/A9 for liver dysfunction in pregnant women with chronic hepatitis B
Authors: Weiwei Geng, Hong Yu, Yuan Li, Li Liu, Yanfang Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundS100 calcium-binding protein A8/A9 (S100A8/A9) is a marker of innate immune activation and inflammation, but its value in predicting liver dysfunction in HBV-infected pregnant women remains unclear. This study aims to explore the predictive value of early-pregnancy serum S100A8/A9 for mid-to-late pregnancy liver dysfunction in chronic HBV-infected pregnant women, and its correlation with adverse pregnancy outcomes.MethodsA retrospective cohort study included 139 chronic HBV-infected pregnant women. Serum S100A8/A9 levels were measured via ELISA, and participants were subsequently divided into abnormal liver function (ALF) and normal liver function (NLF) groups based on mid-to-late pregnancy liver function. Logistic regression analysis identified independent risk factors for abnormal liver function and adverse pregnancy outcomes, while receiver operating characteristic curves evaluated S100A8/A9’s predictive efficacy.ResultsEarly-pregnancy serum S100A8/A9 levels were significantly higher in the ALF group than in the NLF group, and positively correlated with HBV-DNA load and ALT. Elevated S100A8/A9 was an independent risk factor for liver dysfunction and predictor of adverse pregnancy outcomes. ROC curve analysis showed S100A8/A9 had an AUC of 0.882 for identifying liver dysfunction.ConclusionsEarly-pregnancy serum S100A8/A9 may serve as a potential predictive marker for liver function impairment and adverse pregnancy outcomes in chronic HBV-infected women, helping with early clinical identification and management.

Background

Hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection represents a major challenge to global public health, with women of childbearing age being among the most affected groups [1, 2]. As a high-prevalence area, the overall infection rate of HBV in the Chinese population still reached 5.6% in 2022 [3], among which the HBV infection rate among pregnant women could reach 4.56% [4]. Given the physiological changes during pregnancy, such as immune system remodeling and a marked increase in metabolic activity, pregnant women become more susceptible to HBV invasion and hepatic injury, with an elevated risk of hepatocellular damage [5, 6]. Persistent immune tolerance may further aggravate hepatic injury, which in turn progresses to liver dysfunction [7]. Existing studies have shown that 28.3%–35.6% of HBV-infected pregnant women develop abnormal liver function during the second and third trimesters of pregnancy, a proportion significantly higher than that of non-infected pregnant women [8]. Liver dysfunction not only exposes pregnant women to severe complications, including acute hepatitis, liver cirrhosis, and even liver failure, but also markedly elevates the risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes such as preterm birth, low birth weight infants, and pregnancy-induced hypertension [9, 10].

At present, the clinical assessment of liver function for these pregnant women still mainly relies on traditional indicators such as alanine aminotransferase (ALT) [11]. However, these indicators have relatively low sensitivity for detecting early hepatocellular injury, and they are also prone to being affected by fluctuations in hormone levels during pregnancy [12]. Therefore, before liver function abnormalities occur, especially in the early stages of pregnancy, identifying high-risk pregnant women is crucial for implementing early intervention and improving the prognosis of both mothers and infants.

S100A8/A9 is a heterodimeric protein complex secreted by neutrophils [13]. In recent years, it has attracted increasing attention due to its pivotal role in various inflammatory and immune responses [14, 15]. In liver disease research, S100A8/A9 has been proven to be an important biomarker for acute liver injury [16, 17], liver fibrosis and liver cirrhosis [18], primarily mediated by activating inflammatory pathways such as TLR4/NF-κB and promoting the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, which are also core mechanisms underlying the progression of liver dysfunction in chronic HBV infection [19]. Thus, S100A8/A9 may act as a key mediator in this process. However, the specific role and predictive potential of S100A8/A9 in pregnant women with hepatitis B remain unclear.

Based on this, this study intends to conduct a systematic analysis of the HBV-infected pregnant women cohort to clarify the predictive efficacy of the serum S100A8/A9 levels for subsequent liver injury and adverse pregnancy outcomes, to provide new reference indicators for clinical early identification of high-risk populations and formulation of individualized intervention plans.

Methods

Research Design and Grouping Criteria

A retrospective cohort study was performed to analyze clinical data from 139 pregnant women with chronic HBV infection (HBsAg positive for ≥ 6 consecutive months). All participants received prenatal care and were delivered at the Chinese and Western Medicine Hospital of Panzhihua between October 2020 and October 2022.

Inclusion criteria: gestational age ≤ 12 weeks at initial presentation; confirmed diagnosis of chronic HBV infection; no history of antiviral or immunomodulatory therapy before pregnancy, and spontaneous conception with a singleton pregnancy. To minimize confounding factors and ensure the reliability of the HBV-related liver damage predictive model, participants were excluded if they had concurrent liver diseases, a history of drug-induced liver injury, limb dysfunction, mental disorders, or pre-pregnancy comorbidities that might interfere with liver function assessments (e.g., chronic hypertension, diabetes mellitus). Cases with incomplete clinical records or lost to follow-up were also excluded.

Liver dysfunction during follow-up was defined as two consecutive elevations in serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) levels to ≥ 2 times the pregnancy-specific upper limit of normal, with both abnormal results recorded after 20 weeks of gestation. Participants meeting this criterion were classified as the abnormal liver function (ALF) group. The normal liver function (NLF) group comprised the remaining participants who did not meet these criteria throughout the follow-up period.

Definition of adverse pregnancy outcomes

To evaluate the relationship between liver dysfunction and maternal-infant outcomes, adverse pregnancy outcomes were set as a composite endpoint including preterm birth, low birth weight, fetal growth restriction, hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, and neonatal asphyxia. Participants with any of these events were classified into the adverse pregnancy outcome group for subsequent statistical comparisons.

Sample collection and laboratory testing

Fasting venous blood samples were collected from all enrolled participants during their first prenatal visit. After centrifugation for serum separation, samples were aliquoted into cryopreservation tubes and stored at -80 °C for batch testing. Baseline clinical characteristics were collected from the hospital’s electronic medical record system, including demographic characteristics (age, body mass index, and gravidity) and family history of liver disease, liver function parameters, virological markers (HBV-DNA load, HBeAg), and lipid metabolism indices (triglycerides, total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol). This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chinese and Western Medicine Hospital of Panzhihua, and written informed consent was obtained from each participant before data and sample collection.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

ELISA measured serum levels of S100A8/A9. Specifically, the R&D Systems Human S100A8/A9 Heterodimer DuoSet ELISA kit (DY8226-05) was used. This kit demonstrates high sensitivity and specificity, allowing for accurate measurement of S100A8/A9 heterodimer levels.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation, whereas non-normally distributed data were expressed as median (interquartile range). An independent-samples t-test was used for comparisons between two groups, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied for multi-group comparisons. Tukey’s post-hoc test was used for pairwise comparisons when variances were homogeneous; otherwise, the Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn’s post-hoc test was used. Pearson correlation analysis was used to evaluate linear associations between continuous variables. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted using all 139 participants (including both ALF and NLF groups) to identify independent risk factors for liver dysfunction, with liver dysfunction (yes for ALF, no for NLF) as the binary dependent variable. ROC curves were generated to evaluate the predictive efficacy of significant factors. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 software, and data visualization was completed with GraphPad Prism 9.0. A two-tailed P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

In contrast, distinct intergroup differences emerged in several core clinical metrics. For liver function-related indices, ALT and AST levels were significantly higher in the ALF group than in the NLF group. Among them, the difference in ALT was the most significant (33.24 ± 4.43 U/L vs. 29.00 ± 6.21 U/L, p < 0.001). Regarding virological markers, HBV-DNA loads in the ALF group were significantly higher than those in the NLF group (6.41 ± 1.77 log₁₀ IU/mL vs. 5.78 ± 1.53 log₁₀ IU/mL, p = 0.032).

Differential Expression of S100A8/A9 in Serum of Pregnant Women with HBV and Its Diagnostic Value

Analysis of serum samples from HBV - infected pregnant women in the early stage of pregnancy showed that the concentration of S100A8/A9 was significantly higher in the ALF group than in the NLF group (p < 0.001, Fig. 1A). To further verify whether S100A8/A9 can distinguish the degree of liver function impairment, the study used the ROC curve for evaluation. The results showed that the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was 0.882 (95% CI: 0.824–0.939, Fig. 1B). The sensitivity of this method was 86.36%, and the specificity was 77.89%, indicating that S100A8/A9 has a good discriminatory ability.

Correlation between S100A8/A9 and viral replication as well as liver damage

To further explore the relationship between S100A8/A9 and key pathological processes of the disease, a correlation analysis was conducted. As shown in Fig. 2, in the NLF group and the ALF group, S100A8/A9 was significantly and positively correlated with HBV-DNA load and ALT levels. It is noteworthy that in the ALF group, the correlation coefficients of S100A8/A9 with ALT (R = 0.651, p < 0.001) and HBV-DNA load (R = 0.521, p < 0.001) were both higher than those in the NLF group.

Independent risk factors for abnormal liver function and adverse pregnancy outcomes

Risk Factors for Abnormal Liver Function

To further clarify the predictive value of each indicator for hepatic dysfunction risk, a further multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted. Results showed that S100A8/A9 (OR = 7.570, 95% CI: 2.988–19.177, p < 0.001) and HBV‑DNA (OR = 2.399, 95% CI: 1.032–5.576, p = 0.042) were independent risk factors for hepatic dysfunction in HBV-infected pregnant women. However, other indicators, such as age, BMI, and lipid parameters (TG, TC, LDL-C, and HDL-C), didn’t show a significant association with liver dysfunction (p > 0.05, Table 2).

Risk factors for adverse pregnancy outcomes

Based on the predefined composite endpoint and clinical follow‑up records, adverse pregnancy outcomes were identified in 21 of 95 (22.1%) patients in the NLF group and 15 of 44 (34.1%) patients in the ALF group. Further multivariate analysis with adverse pregnancy outcomes as the dependent variable revealed that S100A8/A9 was an independent risk factor for this outcome (OR = 2.769, 95% CI: 1.166–6.577, p = 0.021). However, in this model, demographic characteristics and other indicators exhibited no statistical significance (Table 3). This suggests S100A8/A9 may possess unique predictive value for adverse pregnancy outcomes in this population.

Discussion

Although advances have been made in perinatal HBV infection management to reduce mother-to-child transmission risk, maternal liver health remains a critical concern [20]. This study investigated the value of serum S100A8/A9 levels in the first trimester in predicting liver dysfunction in pregnant women with HBV infection during the middle and late stages of pregnancy. The results showed that an elevated S100A8/A9 level in the first trimester was an independent risk factor for subsequent liver damage, and it exhibited significantly better predictive efficacy than traditional liver function indicators. Early identification of high-risk pregnant women with liver damage and early intervention can not only prevent the acute exacerbation of hepatitis in the middle and late stages of pregnancy [21], ensure the safety of the mother’s liver, but also based on the early warning of S100A8/A9, optimize prenatal management and reduce the risk of adverse maternal and infant outcomes.

Early-pregnancy serum levels of the inflammatory marker S100A8/A9 were significantly elevated in HBV-infected pregnant women who subsequently developed liver dysfunction, a key finding of the present study. These levels correlated significantly with both serum ALT activity and HBV-DNA viral load. This phenomenon suggests that the unique immune tolerance state and altered hormonal environment during pregnancy may promote increased viral replication activity, thereby continuously activating the host’s immune system and promoting the progression of liver damage [22, 23]. As a critical alarm protein and damage-associated molecular pattern molecule primarily released by neutrophils, S100A8/A9 exerts a central regulatory role in inflammatory responses by robustly recruiting leukocytes and inducing pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion [24]. In the progression of chronic HBV infection, the core driver of hepatocyte damage is the host immune response triggered by viral replication [25, 26]. Among these responses, cytotoxic T lymphocyte-mediated hepatocyte apoptosis is a central mechanism driving the progression of liver tissue inflammation [27]. Previous studies have confirmed that serum S100A8/A9 levels in individuals with chronic HBV infection correlate closely with hepatic tissue inflammatory activity [28]. Within this dynamically evolving immune context, serum S100A8/A9 levels serve as a sensitive indicator of intrinsic hepatic immune-inflammatory activity. Elevated levels of this marker can indicate “subclinical” liver damage even when ALT levels remain within the normal range [29, 30]. In the NLF group, S100A8/A9 showed significant correlations with ALT and HBV‑DNA despite normal liver function, suggesting it can detect subclinical inflammation before ALT becomes abnormal. Therefore, elevated S100A8/A9 levels in early pregnancy likely reflect the presence of latent hepatic inflammatory activity under the unique immune state of pregnancy.

Further logistic regression analysis demonstrated that the levels of S100A8/A9 in the early pregnancy were not only a robust indicator of liver dysfunction, but also an independent predictor of its progression. Notably, recent studies have established that S100A8/A9 acts as a marker of disease severity in patients with HBV-ACLF and an independent risk factor for predicting short-term (28/90-day) mortality [31]. Although the study population differs significantly from critically ill patients, this finding indirectly corroborates the universality of S100A8/A9 as a core mediator of innate immune activation. Its levels reliably reflect the intensity of HBV-driven immune-inflammatory responses. Therefore, we hypothesize that the early elevation of S100A8/A9 during pregnancy, a unique immunological state, may serve as a sensitive indicator for early identification of subclinical liver injury and prediction of disease progression. At the same time, this study also found that S100A8/A9 has predictive value for adverse pregnancy outcomes. Research confirms that abnormal liver function in pregnant women is an independent risk factor for adverse pregnancy outcomes such as preeclampsia, fetal growth restriction, and preterm birth [32]. The core mechanism lies in the liver’s role as a central metabolic and immune organ: its dysfunction triggers systemic inflammatory responses, heightened oxidative stress, and vascular endothelial dysfunction, thereby disrupting the immune balance at the maternal-fetal interface and impairing normal placental perfusion [33]. Consequently, elevated S100A8/A9 levels not only reflect local hepatic inflammation but also, as potent damage-associated molecular patterns entering the circulation, may directly amplify the maternal systemic proinflammatory state. Thereby establishing them as a potential molecular bridge connecting hepatic immune activation to remote placental injury [34, 35]. Thus, this study hypothesizes that monitoring S100A8/A9 levels in early pregnancy may provide a novel sensitive indicator for early identification of HBV-infected pregnant women at risk for adverse pregnancy outcomes.

This study has certain limitations. This study relied on serum biomarkers to assess hepatic inflammation. Future studies will incorporate histopathological analysis of liver tissue to further validate the association between these serum markers and in situ hepatic inflammation. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy were recorded only as a composite endpoint. They were not subclassified by gestational age at onset. Therefore, early‑onset preeclampsia was not separately analyzed. Nevertheless, this study represents the first to demonstrate the role of S100A8/A9 in the specific population of HBV infection during pregnancy, providing crucial evidence for future mechanistic exploration and clinical translation.

Conclusions

Taken together, for HBV-infected pregnant women, testing their serum S100A8/A9 levels in the early stage of pregnancy can effectively predict the risk of liver dysfunction during the middle and late stages of pregnancy. S100A8/A9 is not only a sensitive indicator of potential liver inflammation but may also link liver problems with placental function impairment. Therefore, using S100A8/A9 as a simple serological monitoring indicator during pregnancy is helpful for early detection of high-risk pregnant women, facilitating timely intervention and optimized management.

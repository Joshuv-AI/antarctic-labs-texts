Arch dam point cloud segmentation based on deep feature learning and normal vector data optimization
Authors: Huokun Li, Yuekang Li, Yijing Li, Weichao Lu, Zhixing Zhu, Teng Feng, Bo Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSeparating the dam body, spillway, and other structures from the point cloud in the dam area is an important step in dam deformation monitoring. Manual segmentation is time consuming and inaccurate. This study proposes a point cloud segmentation neural network model based on normal vector optimization suitable for dam environment: (1) This model utilizes the voxel uniform sampling method of equal length cubes to solve the problem of uneven point cloud density caused by wide range and long-distance measurement during point cloud measurement in dam areas. (2) Designed block input and combined output modules in the model, achieving efficient input of large volume point cloud and eliminating the impact of interpolation points offset during seq2seq model decoding process. (3) In response to the diverse characteristics of point cloud normal vectors presented by vegetation, rock mass, and complex dam structures in the dam area, this paper proposes an adaptive radius plane fitting vector estimation method based on eigenvalue method to improve the accuracy of segmentation. The experiment on the prototype arch dam shows that the proposed normal estimation method improves the classification accuracy of PointNet + + from 96.26 to 98.27%. Compared with the other three normal estimation methods (2-jets, Hough CNN, iterative PCA), the overall accuracy is improved by 0.82%, 1.22%, and 0.22%, and the joint average intersection is improved by 0.0293, 0.0325, and 0.0104. The prototype arch dam experiment shows that our proposed method has a segmentation accuracy of 98.27%. Compared with 2-jets, Hough CNN, and iterative PCA, the overall accuracy has been improved by 0.82%, 1.22%, and 0.22%. This study provides a high-precision segmentation scheme for applications such as deformation detection of dam components based on point clouds.Keywords: Point cloud, Normal vector, Arch dam, Segmentation, Deformation monitoring, PointNet++Subject terms: Civil engineering, Natural hazards

Introduction

During the service period of a dam, a certain degree of deformation may occur along and perpendicular to the dam axis because of the influence of water level, environmental temperature, and other geological factors in the reservoir area1–3. Once deformation reaches a certain extent, the hydraulic structure will face safety hazards. With the rapid development of surveying and mapping technology, Terrestrial Laser Scanning (TLS) enables intensive 3D sampling with fine spatial resolution, and could provide a more feasible and efficient option to digitize dam model with fine-scale species information. Due to the complexity of the dam and its bedrock environment, manually extracting major components from redundant point cloud data in the reservoir area requires a significant amount of time. Therefore, exploring automated point cloud classification methods plays an important role in improving modeling efficiency.

For point cloud segmentation, methods based on region growing4,5, clustering6,7, model fitting8,9, machine learning10, and deep learning11,12 are mainly available. However, although region growing based point cloud segmentation algorithms are widely used, they rely on the selection of seed points, are sensitive to parameters, and may be difficult to correctly identify and segment point cloud with complex topological structures.

Point cloud segmentation based on clustering methods is inefficient and requires a large amount of time and computational resources. Sun et al.13 first divides the three-dimensional range data into ground and main objects through distance image segmentation steps. Subsequently, a novel prediction method was introduced based on the shape of the segmentation region, and a small amount of prediction residuals were efficiently compressed using various lossless or lossy techniques. However, due to the high iteration rate of each point in the point cloud, this results in a heavy computational burden and relatively low efficiency.

The method based on model fitting for point cloud segmentation is easily affected by noise, and the processing effect is poor when dealing with data with uneven point cloud density, which is prone to missed points. In machine learning methods, manual selection of feature extractors is required, resulting in subjectivity and limitations in feature selection, which can be effectively compensated for by deep learning.

With the substantial progress of deep learning research, indirect point cloud semantic segmentation methods have gradually been proposed by early scholars, who combined voxel based methods14–18, multi view methods19–21 with deep learning22–24. Maturana et al.17 first proposed a VoxNet model based on voxelization method. This method constructs multiple 3D grids by using a grid occupying algorithm and normalizes the corresponding voxels in the grid. Then, the network convolutional layer is input to extract features, and maximum pooling is performed on non-overlapping voxels. The voxel based method effectively solves the disorder of point cloud data, but the feature extraction at the voxel boundary is not accurate enough. Zhang et al.20 proposed a recognition model based on multi-view CNNs. This method helps to improve the accuracy of semantic annotation, but the limitation of multi view methods is that it is difficult to select and integrate different viewpoints. Moreover, the projection step inevitably leads to information loss and cannot fully capture the features of point cloud data.

In order to fully utilize the characteristics of point cloud data and reduce the computational complexity of the network, network models based on original point cloud25–27 have gradually been proposed. PointNet is the most classic point cloud learning method based on raw data. This method has achieved good performance, but it obtains a global feature that cannot capture local features, making it difficult to adapt to complex dam environments26. On the basis of PointNet, PointNet++27 is further proposed, which uses the farthest point sampling (FPS) algorithm to divide points into some local regions. For each small region, PointNet is used to obtain local features. In the practical engineering field, point cloud classification based on deep learning has also been partially applied, such as in bridge engineering28–31. Hyeonsoo Kim and Changwan Kim30 used three neural network models—PointNet, PointCNN, and DGCNN—to segment bridge components.

These deep neural based point cloud classification network models, many of which use ‘Sequence to Sequence’ models. In segmentation models based on PointNet++, interpolation is used during the decoder process to restore lost points from down-sampling. Although this method is efficient, the errors caused by interpolation have an impact on observing the structural deformation of hydraulic structures. When using 3D laser scanners for scanning large dams, it often poses many challenges and problems due to its enormous scale. Long-distance laser measurements may cause density non-uniformity in point cloud data, which is particularly evident on dam surfaces. In addition, low vegetation attached to rock surfaces may also introduce noise into point cloud data, affecting the accuracy of the data and the efficiency of subsequent processing. This paper is based on improved PointNet + + segmentation neural network to study the automatic classification method of 3D laser point cloud in a reservoir environment. (1) The model data transmission and DataLoader module were optimized in this paper by incorporating uniform voxel sampling based on equal-length cubes, which effectively compensates for the uneven sampling data of 3D laser scanners and thus enables the network model to better learn local features of the dam.

(2) A data block input and combination output module were designed in this paper. (3) The impact of several normal estimation methods on classification accuracy in response to the diverse characteristics of point cloud normals presented by vegetation, rock mass, and complex dam structures was compared in this paper. Additionally, a more accurate adaptive radius plane fitting vector estimation optimization scheme based on the eigenvalue method was proposed. This method can reduce the outliers and errors caused by the least squares method and obtain more reliable fitting parameters. The adaptive radius can identify the ideal local plane range, thereby obtaining the estimated normal vector of that point.

Model block input and combined output

Given the large volume of original point cloud data for the arch dam in this experiment, which have more than 100 million points, the data are input into the network model in a block-wise manner. We have set four overlapping degrees for point cloud data, which are 20%, 40%, 60%, and 80%, respectively (Fig. 4).

Partitioning of blocks from point cloud of dam area.

When performing segmentation neural network calculations on point cloud data input, the data undergoes a process from encoding to decoding. During the decoder process, the inverse distance weighted average based on k nearest neighbors (KNN) method was used to solve the features of missing points. Combine features by skipping links and then perform up-sampling. This process can quickly restore the lost feature points during the down-sampling process. Normal optimization

Point cloud normal vectors are important attributes of point cloud data, and accurate normal vectors can improve feature learning accuracy. The complex structure of the dam body and the large amount of vegetation and rock mass in the dam area environment have inconsistent and even diverse normal characteristics. Thus, this paper optimizes the scale parameters of the plane fitting algorithm to estimate the normal direction of the point cloud more accurately, providing higher-accuracy learning features for the classification of the dam area and various components of the dam. This paper gives the initial radius based on the density of adjacent points around each point and then updates the radius according to our specific requirements using relevant functions to ensure that a sufficient number of adjacent points can be found in areas with different densities. Then, the points within the radius are used to solve the fitting plane and estimate the point cloud normal vector.

Hoppe et al.34 first proposed a normal vector estimation method based on local plane fitting. This method quickly estimates the normal vector by fitting the local point set around each point, thus making the normal vector estimation more responsive to changes in surface details and local curvature. However, the selection of the local radius scale will directly affect the estimation results of the normal vector. If the radius is too large, then local features will be lost, and if the radius is too small, then the normal vector estimation will be greatly affected by noise. Experimental results and analysis

Experimental environment

The experiment is based on the PyTorch framework, and the running environment server is equipped with an Intel (R) CoreTM i7 12700kf CPU, 128 GB RAM, NVIDIA RTX 3090, CUDA11.7, and cuDNN v8.5.0. It uses an Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001. PointNet + + was trained for 251 epochs, and after each epoch, the learning rate decreased by 1%. The batch size settings are 16, 20, 24, 28, and 32.

Datasets

This paper uses the Trimble TX835 terrestrial 3D laser scanner to test a prototype arch dam in Jiangxi Province, see Fig. 7 for the field measurement. The dam is a masonry double core hyperbolic arch dam with a maximum design height of 102.39 m and a crest elevation of 423.5 m. It is the tallest masonry double core hyperbolic thin arch dam in China36. Complete and high-precision dam area data are derived by obtaining 3D point cloud data through multi-station scanning, and the matching spherical targets are arranged to complete multi-station stitching. In this survey, 9 measurement points are set on the dam crest road, and 4 measurement points are arranged on the downstream dam surface (Fig. 8). The higher the overlap of data, the higher the accuracy of point cloud segmentation, but the time consumed will also increase. Because data with excessive overlap can lead to a large amount of data duplication and increase training time, selecting the appropriate overlap based on actual engineering data can improve model efficiency.

Normal vector analysis

This paper tests the performance of the normal vector estimation algorithm using the dam and rock mass models obtained using the Trimble TX8 3D laser scanner on an arch dam. Figure 11(a) is the point cloud map of the arch dam spillway, and Fig. 11(b)–(e) are diagrams of the local normal vectors of the arch dam surface hole side pier cross-section using 2-jets38, Hough CNN39, iterative PCA40, and the adaptive radius plane fitting algorithm ARP (Ours). The estimated radius of the normal vector is 100 nearest-neighbor points, and the direction of the normal vector is KNN = 6. The propagation of the normal vector adopts the minimum spanning tree method. The normal vectors of 2-jets and Hough CNN on smooth surfaces are relatively disordered, which deviates from their true direction, and the normal estimation of these two methods deviates at sharp points. The weighted iterative PCA algorithm improves the accuracy of normal vector estimation at smooth surfaces, but shortcomings remain in normal vector estimation at sharp points. The proposed algorithm performs well on both smooth surfaces and sharp points.

Analysis of segmentation results

Figure 12 illustrates the segmentation outcomes, with each point’s color indicating its dam area component. Commission error refers to the classification of other categories as target categories; omission error is the classification of the target category into other categories. rom a visual perspective, the dam body has the smallest difference, and different normal vectors have the smallest impact on its segmentation results. The majority of the segmentations are accurate, with misclassifications predominantly occurring in the dam’s lower emptying holes and in sporadic instances of leakage errors. In the case of the spillway, segmentation discrepancies are primarily observed at the junction between the spillway’s upper section and the dam crest road, as well as around the vent hole located at the dam’s base.

Table 6 shows the comparison of data accuracy for point cloud segmentation using various methods. Among the four normal vector methods, ARP performs the best, while Hough CNN performs poorly. The 2-jets method demonstrated commendable performance in the dam crest highway scenario, albeit with a 9.56% omission rate in rock segmentation. Iterative PCA achieved the lowest omission rates in the spillway; however, it recorded the highest commission rate of 1.61% in the dam crest highway, surpassing the other methods. Hough CNN, notably, underperformed across various scenarios, with commission rates peaking at 12.74% in the Plant and 2.57% in the Sentry box scenarios. Our proposed method, however, showcased lower error rates across the board, with a particularly noteworthy 3.09% omission rate in rock segmentation, significantly outperforming its counterparts. Furthermore, it displayed low commission rates in the Spillway, Crest Road, and Plant scenarios—0.05%, 0.75%, and 5.86%, respectively—thus underscoring its robustness and adaptability to diverse conditions.

Discussion

The influence of normal vectors on point cloud segmentation of dams

Figures 13 and 14 show that the normal vector has a significant impact on point cloud segmentation. With the visualization results of the normal vector in Fig. 11 combined, the normal vector in this paper has better representation, corresponding to the higher mean IoU of the proposed method in the category of flood discharge holes in Fig. 14(c). Therefore, a more accurate estimation of the normal vector can improve the accuracy of point cloud segmentation.

Figures 12 and 14(a) show that different normal vectors have the least impact on the segmentation results of the dam body. The main reason is that the dam surface does not have a complex structure, the curvature of the point cloud is small, and the normal distribution is basically consistent, which is why commission and omission errors cannot be generated easily. However, spillway and drainage holes are prone to commission errors because of structural changes.

Figure 12 demonstrates that the connection between the spillway and the dam crest results in an omission error, and its structure is similar to the vent hole of the dam body, resulting in a commission error at the dam body. The commission and omission errors in the bedrock of the dam area mainly come from plants, and the rock mass on the right bank of the dam area shows leakage. This area is a manually constructed platform and staircase. The main reason may be that the leaky rock mass is not a natural rock mass, and some areas around the artificial platform and stairs are covered by low vegetation (Fig. 15), making it difficult to distinguish between the rock mass and vegetation point cloud. For vegetation, relatively few cases of missed classification errors occur, with only some occurring near the downstream stilling pool of the arch dam.

Realistic scene of rock mass and low vegetation downstream of arch dam ((a) For the downstream right bank scene, (b) is the downstream left bank scene.).

Hough CNN is a combination of Hough transform and CNN, which requires a large amount of data for training, thereby reducing generalization ability. For instance, large hoists and special supporting structures are found on the dam crest road, and this method does not perform as well as other normal vector methods. Therefore, for more special structures, additional training may be necessary to obtain better results.

The 2-jets is based on two curved surfaces, and it may lead to inaccurate estimation of the normal vector for point cloud with uneven distribution. As a result of the long dam crest and limited measurement stations, the density of point cloud at the dam crest is uneven, resulting in poor estimation of the normal vector of some point cloud at the 2-jets dam crest. In the dam area environment, iterative PCA is superior to the first two methods but is more sensitive to parameters such as local area radius. The non-uniformity of the point cloud will result in the inability to select a more suitable local radius, leading to deviation in normal vector estimation. The proposed method effectively optimized the main parameter of local radius and achieved good results for the dam surface and the main components of the bedrock, which are mainly planar.

Distribution pattern of segmentation error

A hypothesis is proposed based on the visual segmentation results of plant and rock mass, as well as the distribution patterns of commission errors and omission errors. The relative position relationship and geometric shape have different degrees of influence on point cloud segmentation (the relative position relationship represents the spatial position relationship between different types of objects; the geometric shape represents the inherent physical features of different types of objects), and the relative position relationship has a greater impact on the segmentation results than the geometric shape, which is mainly reflected in the following two points: (1) The geometric shape of the point cloud on the right bank of the dam area where an omission error (plant commission error) exists is similar to the point cloud shape of the dam crest road, but no confusion occurs in classification between the two, and this error occurs between plant and rock mass. (2) The visualization results show that most of the commission and omission errors occurred between interconnected categories (plant–rock mass, dam body–spillway, spillway–dam top–sentry box), while almost no omission or commission error was found between disconnected categories in space.

Conclusion

In this study, an adaptive radius normal vector estimation method based on the eigenvalue method and an improved PointNet + + network structure was applied to the 3D point cloud classification of dams. OA and IoU were used as performance indicators, and the results showed that the proposed method has higher accuracy.

This paper compared various commonly used normal vector estimation methods and added non-vector data as a comparative experiment. The following results were obtained: no NVD (OA: 96.69%; mIoU: 0.8716), 2-jets (OA: 97.61%; mIoU: 0.8854), Hough CNN (OA: 97.16%; mIoU: 0.8762), iterative PCA (OA: 98.10%; mIoU: 0.9035), and our method (OA: 98.27%; mIoU: 0.9098).

The data input module of PointNet + + was improved in this study by introducing an equal-length voxel sampling method to reduce the impact of uneven point cloud density on the segmentation model. The problem of point cloud shift caused by data interpolation during neural network decoding was addressed by employing a combined output method. The segmentation accuracy and visualization results showed that the proposed method can be effectively applied to 3D point cloud segmentation of arch dams. The method proposed in this article can segment important components of the dam, and the computational resources required for component deformation calculation are much smaller than those required for the entire dam deformation calculation, providing basic support for the preliminary work of dam component deformation monitoring.

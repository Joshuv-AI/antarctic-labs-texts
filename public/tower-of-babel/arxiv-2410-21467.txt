Generator Subadditive Functions for Mixed-Integer Programs
Gustavo Angulo, Burak Kocuk, Diego A. Morán R

Abstract
For equality-constrained linear mixed-integer programs (MIP) defined by rational data, it is known that the subadditive dual is a strong dual and that there exists an optimal solution of a particular form, termed generator subadditive function . Motivated by these results, we explore the connection between Lagrangian duality, subadditive duality and generator subadditive functions for general equality-constrained MIPs where the vector of variables is constrained to be in a monoid. We show that strong duality holds via generator subadditive functions under certain conditions. For the case when the monoid is defined by the set of all mixed-integer points contained in a convex cone, we show that strong duality holds under milder conditions and over a more restrictive set of dual functions. Finally, we provide some examples of applications of our results.



Keywords:

mixed-integer programming; Lagrangian duality; subadditive duality; generator functions; conic programming

Introduction

Consider the following equality-constrained mixed-integer program (MIP)

\displaystyle z^{*}:=\inf

\displaystyle c^{T}x+d^{T}y

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle Ax+Gy=b

\displaystyle(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}},

where
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
,
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n_{1}}
,
G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n_{2}}
,

and
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}
with the usual
+

monoid

(0,0)\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in{\mathcal{M}}
implies
(x_{1},y_{1})+(x_{2},y_{2})\in{\mathcal{M}}
and

is associative in
{\mathcal{M}}
.

In this paper, we study certain dual problems associated to the primal MIP (
1
) for several special cases. Duality is a well-studied and fundamental concept in optimization theory and methodology
[
17
,
14
,
27
,
11
,
23
,
20
]
. For a minimization type
primal
problem, a feasible solution to its
dual
problem can provide a lower bound to the optimal primal value, hence, it enables to certify the quality of a feasible solution. In addition, for a
strong dual
problem, its optimal value matches the optimal value of the primal problem, which can be used as a certificate of optimality.

Encoding the dual problem can be quite different for different classes of primal optimization problems. For example, the dual problem of a linear program (LP) or a conic program (CP) is another linear program or conic program, involving the dual cone in the latter case (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
). Hence, these dual problems are finite dimensional optimization problems with similar representability as their primal counterparts. However, when integer variables are involved, the situation changes drastically. In the case of linear or conic MIPs, one typically resorts to a
subadditive
dual, whose feasible region is defined over
subadditive functions, rather than finite dimensional elements as in the case of LPs and CPs (see,
[
17
,
14
,
11
]
for linear MIPs, and
[
23
,
20
,
2
]
for conic inequality-constrained MIPs). Hence, it is incomparably more difficult to encode the dual problem when integer variables are involved.
As an example, the subadditive dual problem for the equality-constrained linear MIP, where we have
{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n_{2}}
in (
1
), is given as follows:

\displaystyle\rho^{*}:=\sup

\displaystyle f(b)

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle f(a^{j})\leq c_{j}

\displaystyle j=1,\dots,n_{1}

\displaystyle\bar{f}(g^{j})\leq d_{j}

\displaystyle j=1,\dots,n_{2}

\displaystyle f(0)=0,\ f:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}\ \text{ is subadditive}.

Here,
a^{j}
and

respectively denote the

The following result states that for feasible linear MIPs with rational data, the subadditive dual is a strong dual, that is, the duality gap is zero (i.e.,
\rho^{*}=z^{*}

Proposition 1

(
[
17
,
14
]
)
.

Consider a feasible linear MIP (i.e.,
{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n_{2}}

1

A\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{m\times n_{1}}
,

Then, the subadditive dual (

There have been some limited attempts to solve the subadditive dual problem in the case of linear MIPs for special problem classes as subadditive duals do not admit practical solution procedures. For example,
[
18
,
19
]
consider a class of problems with equality constraints, rational and nonnegative data, and nonnegative integer variables. In this case, it is shown that the optimal function can be written as the value function of a parametric packing type of linear IP, whose feasible region is a relaxation of the original feasible region. Then, an iterative algorithm is proposed to obtain the optimal parameters. Later,
[
5
]
generalizes the setting to the case where there is no nonnegativity assumption on the data and continuous variables are present.
In all these three papers, the optimal dual solution has a specific functional form as described below.

Proposition 2

(
[
19
,
5
]
)
.

Consider a feasible linear MIP under the same assumptions of Proposition
1

Let
\Omega:=\{Ax+Gy{\,:\,}(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n_{2}}\}

F_{\alpha}(\omega)=\alpha^{T}\omega-\max\left\{(A^{T}\alpha-c)^{T}x+(G^{T}\alpha-d)^{T}y:Ax+Gy\leq\omega,\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n_{2}}\right\}.

The functions appearing in Proposition

Proposition 3

(
[

,
5
]
)
.

Consider a feasible linear MIP

\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}

A piecewise linear approximation of the subadditive dual problem of the mixed-integer program of binarized neural networks is utilized in

2

Duality for equality-constrained mixed-integer programs

The paper
[
19
]
established the relationship between
generator subadditive functions
with Lagrangian and subadditive duality for integer linear programs. In this section, we generalize these results for problem (
1
) and the associated dual problems.

2.1

Lagrangian duality and generator subadditive functions

Let
\Omega^{\leq}=\{\omega{\,:\,}Ax+Gy\leq\omega\ \textup{for some}\ (x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}\}
and for
\omega\in\Omega^{\leq}
define

{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega)=\{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}{\,:\,}Ax+Gy\leq\omega\}
. Suppose
S\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)
and consider the optimization problem

\displaystyle z^{\leq}:=\inf

\displaystyle c^{T}x+d^{T}y

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle Ax+Gy=b

\displaystyle(x,y)\in S.

For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, define
{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha):=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y+\alpha^{T}(b-Ax-Gy){\,:\,}(x,y)\in S\}
.
Since we are dualizing equality constraints, it is easy to see that the value
{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha)
gives a lower bound for the optimal value of (
3
), that is,

{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha)\leq\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in S\}.

(4)

For a set
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we denote its convex hull as
\textup{conv}(X)
.
In the Lagrangian duality theory (e.g., see
[
6
,
10
]
for the integer programming case), there are sufficient conditions for the existence of
\alpha^{*}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha^{*})=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in\textup{conv}(S)\}.

(5)

Note that
\alpha^{*}
satisfies
{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha^{*})=\sup\{{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha){\,:\,}\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\}
, and thus is an optimal solution to the Lagrangian dual problem. In principle, the bound in (
5
) may be strictly less than the optimal value of problem (
3
) since the minimization is over
\textup{conv}(S)\supseteq S
. Nevertheless, we will show in Theorem
1
below that

\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in\textup{conv}(S)\}=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in S\}.

(6)

Therefore, if (
5
) is satisfied, then any optimal solution
\alpha^{*}
to the Lagrangian dual problem satisfies:

{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha^{*})=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in S\}.

In order to prove Theorem
1
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathbb{M}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a set and let
X\subseteq\{x\in\mathbb{M}{\,:\,}Ax\leq b\}
be a set such that
\textup{conv}(X)\cap\mathbb{M}=X
. Assume that
T=\{x\in\textup{conv}(X){\,:\,}Ax=b\}
is a nonempty face of
\textup{conv}(X)
. Then
\textup{conv}(X)\cap T=\textup{conv}(T\cap\mathbb{M})
.

Proof.

By assumption and since
X\subseteq\mathbb{M}
, we have
\textup{conv}(X)\cap\mathbb{M}=X=X\cap\mathbb{M}
. Therefore,

\textup{conv}(X)\cap T=\textup{conv}(X\cap\mathbb{M})\cap T=\textup{conv}(\textup{conv}(X)\cap\mathbb{M})\cap T=\textup{conv}(T\cap\mathbb{M}),

where the last equality follows from the well-known fact that for any set
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
, convex set
X^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and face
T^{\prime}
of
X^{\prime}
, we have
\textup{conv}(X^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{M}^{\prime})\cap T^{\prime}=\textup{conv}(T^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{M}^{\prime})
.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
\textup{conv}(S)\cap{\mathcal{M}}=S

and that
T=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in\textup{conv}(S)\}
is nonempty. Then, equation (
6
) is satisfied.

Proof.

Denoting
z^{\textup{conv}}=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in\textup{conv}(S)\}
, we have

\displaystyle z^{\textup{conv}}

\displaystyle=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}(x,y)\in\textup{conv}(S)\cap T\}=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}(x,y)\in\textup{conv}(T\cap{\mathcal{M}})\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}(x,y)\in T\cap{\mathcal{M}}\}=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in S\}=z^{\leq},

where the first equality follows by definition of
T
, the second equality follows from applying Lemma
1
to
X=S
and
\mathbb{M}={\mathcal{M}}
, the third equality follows from the fact optimizing a linear function over a set is equivalent to optimize over its convex hull, and the last equality follows from the definition of
T
and
\textup{conv}(S)\cap{\mathcal{M}}=S
.
∎

Note that
\textup{conv}({\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega))\cap{\mathcal{M}}={\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega)
for any
\omega\in\Omega
since
{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega)
are points in
{\mathcal{M}}
contained in the convex set
\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}{\,:\,}Ax+Gy\leq\omega\}
. Assuming that the feasible region of (
1
) is nonempty and that (
5
) holds, we can apply Theorem
1
to
S={\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)
and obtain that there exist
\alpha^{*}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha^{*})=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)\}
.
The relationship of Lagrangian duality and
generator subadditive functions
is established by noting that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha^{*})

\displaystyle=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y+{\alpha^{*}}^{T}(b-Ax-Gy){\,:\,}(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)\}

\displaystyle={\alpha^{*}}^{T}b+\inf\{(c-A^{T}\alpha^{*})^{T}x+(d-G^{T}\alpha^{*})^{T}y{\,:\,}(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)\}

\displaystyle={\alpha^{*}}^{T}b-\sup\{(A^{T}\alpha^{*}-c)^{T}x+(G^{T}\alpha^{*}-d)^{T}y{\,:\,}(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)\}.

We generalize the definition in
[
19
]
as follows.

Definition 1

(Generator subadditive function)
.

Consider a feasible MIP of the form (
1
). Then, for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, we define a generator subadditive function

F_{\alpha}:\Omega^{\leq}\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
as

F_{\alpha}(\omega)=\alpha^{T}\omega-\sup\left\{(A^{T}\alpha-c)^{T}x+(G^{T}\alpha-d)^{T}y\,:\,(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega)\right\}.

(7)

Note that by the previous discussion on Lagrangian duality from (
4
), we have that

F_{\alpha}(b)\leq\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)\}=z^{*},

(8)

and therefore we can use the function
F_{\alpha}
to find a lower bound for the optimal value of the primal problem (
1
) – this property is related to the subadditive dual being a
weak dual
, we will see this in the next section.

2.2

Subadditive duality

In order to state the subadditive dual for the primal problem (
1
), we need some definitions.

Definition 2

(Subadditive function)
.

A function
f:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is subadditive if
f(u+v)\leq f(u)+f(v)
for all
u,v\in\mathcal{D}
such that
u+v\in\mathcal{D}
.
We denote the set of subadditive functions
f:\mathcal{D}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{D}}
and for
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{D}}
, we define
\bar{f}(x):=\lim\sup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{f(\delta x)}{\delta}
.

Definition 3

(Integral generating set)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
with the usual
+
operation be a monoid. A generating set of
{\mathcal{M}}
is a set
\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}
such that for any
w\in{\mathcal{M}}
there exists
h_{1},\ldots,h_{p}\in\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that

w=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}h_{i}
.

For a general monoid, integral generating sets are not finite. Conditions for finiteness of the generating set are given in
[
15
,
12
,
7
]
.

Let
\Omega=\{Ax+Gy{\,:\,}(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}\}
be
the set of r.h.s. that make the primal problem (
1
) feasible. Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that
\mathcal{D}\supseteq\Omega
. Consider the following subadditive dual problem of (
1
):

\displaystyle\rho^{*}:=\sup

\displaystyle f(b)

(9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle f(Au+Gv)\leq c^{T}u+d^{T}v

\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})

\displaystyle f(0)=0,\ f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{D}}.

The following lemma shows that the choice of the generating set does not change the associated constraints in the dual problem.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})
,
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}({\mathcal{M}})
be two generating sets of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Then, for any
f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{D}
with
f(0)=0
, we have that

f(Au+Gv)\leq c^{T}u+d^{T}v\ \textup{for all}\ (u,v)\in\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})

is equivalent to
f(Au+Gv)\leq c^{T}u+d^{T}v\ \textup{for all}\ (u,v)\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Proof.

Assume that
f(Au+Gv)\leq c^{T}u+d^{T}v\ \textup{for all}\ (u,v)\in\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})
and let
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}({\mathcal{M}})
. Then there exists
(u_{1},v_{1}),\ldots,(u_{p},v_{p})\in\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}(u_{i},v_{i})
. We have

f(Au^{\prime}+Gv^{\prime})=f\left(A\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}u_{i}+G\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}v_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}f\left(Au_{i}+Gv_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}(c^{T}u_{i}+d^{T}v_{i})=c^{T}u^{\prime}+d^{T}v^{\prime},

where the first inequality follow from subadditivity of
f
and
f(0)=0
. Therefore, if the function
f
satisfies the constraints for the integral generating set
\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})
then it also satisfies them for the integral generating set
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}({\mathcal{M}})
(and viceversa).
∎

We show next that weak duality holds for the primal (
1
) and dual (
9
).

Proposition 4

(Weak duality)
.

For any vector
(x,y)
feasible for (
1
) and for any function
f
feasible for (
9
), we have that
f(b)\leq c^{T}x+d^{T}y
.

Proof.

For
(x,y)
feasible for (
1
), we have that
b=Ax+Gy
. Thus, the fact that
f(b)\leq c^{T}x+d^{T}y
follows from
f(b)=f(Ax+Gy)
and Lemma
2
applied to
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}({\mathcal{M}})={\mathcal{M}}
.
∎

Sufficient conditions for functional duals like the dual (

) to be a

have been given in
[

,

,

,

,

,

as the so-called
value function

\nu_{\mathcal{M}}

\omega\in\Omega

\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(\omega)=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=\omega,(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}\},

is an optimal solution to the dual problem whenever
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(\omega)>-\infty
for all

(for instance, see
[

).
On the other hand, when

, several sufficient conditions to have strong duality have been studied in

]
.

We now show that under some conditions,
generator subadditive functions
are feasible and/or optimal for the subadditive dual of (
1
) when
\mathcal{D}=\Omega^{\leq}
. We note here that dual feasibility requires that the functions belong to the set
{\mathcal{F}}_{\Omega^{\leq}}
and hence we must have that the function
F_{\alpha}:\Omega^{\leq}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is well-defined, that is,
F_{\alpha}(\omega)>-\infty
for all
\omega\in\Omega^{\leq}
. A sufficient condition for this to happen is the boundedness of sets
{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)
for any
b\in\Omega^{\leq}
. However, more general conditions can be given. For instance, when problem (
1
) is a conic MIP, a sufficient condition is the feasibility of its subadditive dual (see
[
20
]
).

The next proposition recovers results from
[
19
,
5
]
.

Proposition 5
.

Consider a feasible conic MIP of the form (
1
) and the subadditive dual of the form (
9
) with
\mathcal{D}=\Omega^{\leq}
.
Then, if
F_{\alpha}:\Omega^{\leq}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is well-defined, then
F_{\alpha}
is a feasible dual solution.

Proof.

Firstly, we check that
generator subadditive functions
are indeed subadditive functions. Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega^{\leq}
. Observe that if
(x_{1},y_{1})\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega_{1})
and
(x_{2},y_{2})\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega_{2})
, then
(x_{1}+x_{2},y_{1}+y_{2})\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})
, and therefore
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\in\Omega^{\leq}
. Moreover, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup\left\{(A^{T}\alpha-c)^{T}x+(G^{T}\alpha-d)^{T}y\,:\,(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup\left\{(A^{T}\alpha-c)^{T}x+(G^{T}\alpha-d)^{T}y\,:\,(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega_{1})\right\}

\displaystyle+\sup\left\{(A^{T}\alpha-c)^{T}x+(G^{T}\alpha-d)^{T}y\,:\,(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega_{2})\right\},

and hence we conclude
F_{\alpha}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})\leq F_{\alpha}(\omega_{1})+F_{\alpha}(\omega_{2})
.

Notice that for any
(u,v)\in{\mathcal{M}}
, we have

\begin{split}F_{\alpha}(Au+Gv)&=\alpha^{T}(Au+Gv)-\sup\left\{(A^{T}\alpha-c)^{T}x+(G^{T}\alpha-d)^{T}y{\,:\,}(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(Au+Gv)\right\}\\
&\leq\alpha^{T}Au-(A^{T}\alpha-c)^{T}u+\alpha^{T}Gv-(G^{T}\alpha-d)^{T}v=c^{T}u+d^{T}v,\end{split}

(10)

where the inequality follows from the fact that
(u,v)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(Au+Gv)
. Since
\mathcal{I}({\mathcal{M}})\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}
, this shows that
F_{\alpha}
satisfies the first constraint in the dual (
9
).
Finally, since
{\mathcal{M}}
is a monoid, we have that
(u,v)=(0,0)\in{\mathcal{M}}
and thus from (
10
) it follows that
F_{\alpha}(0)\leq 0
. Since any subadditive function satisfies the reverse inequality, we conclude that
F_{\alpha}(0)=0
.
∎

We now review some definitions related to cones.

Definition 4

(Regular cone, Conic inequality, Dual cone)
.

We call a cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
regular if it is closed, convex, pointed and full-dimensional.
The conic inequality with respect to
K
is defined as
x\succeq_{K}y
if and only if
x-y\in K
.
The dual cone to a cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is defined as
K_{*}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:x^{T}y\geq 0,\ \textup{for all}\ x\in K\}
.

Theorem 2

(Strong duality via generator subadditive functions)
.

Consider a feasible MIP of the form (
1

\mathcal{D}=\Omega^{\leq}
. Assume

is well-defined, and that

is conic representable, that is,

\textup{conv}({\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b))=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}{\,:\,}\exists\,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{3}}\ \textup{s.t.}\ \Pi x+\Phi y+\Psi w\succeq_{C}\pi\},

for some matrices
\Pi,\Phi,\Psi

Then, there exists

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\Omega

\displaystyle z^{*}(\omega):=

\displaystyle\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y{\,:\,}Ax+Gy=b,\ (x,y)\in\textup{conv}({\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega))\},

and by assumption we conclude that

\displaystyle z^{*}(b)=\inf\{c^{T}x+d^{T}y:Ax+Gy=b,\ \Pi x+\Phi y+\Psi w\succeq_{C}\pi\}.

(11)

Consider the conic dual problem associated to (

\displaystyle\sup\{b^{T}\alpha+\pi^{T}\gamma:A^{T}\alpha+{\Pi}^{T}\gamma=c,\ G^{T}\alpha+{\Phi}^{T}\gamma=d,\ {\Psi}^{T}\gamma=0,\ \gamma\in C_{*}\}.

3

Duality for equality-constrained conic mixed-integer programs

3.1

Strong duality

We will show that (
9
) is a strong dual when
{\mathcal{M}}=K\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})
, where
K
is a regular cone, and
\mathcal{D}={\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
We first need a definition.

Definition 5

(Nondecreasing function)
.

A function
f:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is nondecreasing with respect to a regular cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
if for
u,v\in\mathcal{D}
we have
u\succeq_{K}v\Rightarrow f(u)\geq f(v)
.

Proposition 6

(Strong duality)
.

Consider a feasible conic MIP of the form (
1
) and the subadditive dual of the form (
9
). Assume that the conic dual of the continuous relaxation of (
1
) is feasible. Then, there exists a dual feasible function
f
such that
f(b)=\rho^{*}=z^{*}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the equivalent conic MIP

\displaystyle\hat{z}(\omega):=\inf

\displaystyle c^{T}x+d^{T}y

(12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle Ax+Gy\geq\omega

\displaystyle-Ax-Gy\geq-\omega

\displaystyle(x,y)\geq_{K}0

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1}},y\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}},

and its subadditive dual (see,
[
23
,
20
]
)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(\omega):=\sup

\displaystyle H(\omega,-\omega,0)

(13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle H(a^{j},-a^{j},e^{j})=-H(-a^{j},a^{j},-e^{j})=c_{j}\quad\text{for all}\ j=1,\dots,{n_{1}}

\displaystyle\bar{H}(g^{j},-g^{j},e^{n_{1}+j})=-\bar{H}(-g^{j},g^{j},-e^{n_{1}+j})=d_{j}\quad\text{for all}\ j=1,\dots,{n_{2}}

\displaystyle H(0,0,0)=0

\displaystyle H:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\text{ is subadditive and}\text{ nondecreasing w.r.t. }{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}\times K.

Here,

denotes the standard

th unit vector in

.
For
\omega=b
, since the conic dual of the continuous relaxation of (

) is feasible, and conic MIPs (

) and (

) are equivalent, the conic dual of the continuous relaxation of (

) is feasible. Then, due to

]

Define the function

. We claim that

is an optimal feasible solution to the subadditive dual (

). The fact that

Now, we show that

The following is an immediate consequence of the strong duality result in Proposition

.

Corollary 1

(Complementary slackness)
.

Consider a feasible solution

In this section, we specialize our results
for

For this case, we propose the following subadditive dual problem of (

\displaystyle f(b)

\displaystyle f(Au)\leq c^{T}u

Theorem 3

(
[

,
17
,
24
]
)
.

If

, then for all

with

Lemma 3
.

Let
K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a cone,

be a subadditive function such that
g(0)=0
,

\bar{g}(N\upsilon)\leq e^{T}\upsilon
for all
\upsilon\in K
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
): Let
\upsilon\in K
. Due to the definition of
\bar{g}
, we have

\bar{g}(N\upsilon)=\lim\sup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{g(\delta N\upsilon)}{\delta}\leq\lim\sup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\delta e^{T}\upsilon}{\delta}=e^{T}\upsilon,

where the inequality follows from

since

.

(
\Leftarrow
): Let

. Then, due to Theorem

.
∎

Proposition 7
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(K_{1}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n_{1}})\times K_{2}

(15)

3.4

Conic integer programs defined by cones with
(R,G)
-finitely generated integral vectors

Unlike the case of rational polyhedral cones (see
[
26
]
), integral vectors in general convex cones may not have a finite integral generating sets. In order to overcome this,
[
21
]
introduced the concept of the monoid of integer points inside a convex cone to be
(R,G)
-finitely generated
and studied it in the particular case of the second order cone and the positive semidefinite cone; some applications of their definition to the theory of conic integer programming are also discussed. We give their definition below.

Definition 6

(Group Action,
(R,G)
-finitely generated)
.

Given a group
G
and a regular cone
K
, a group action is a function from
G\times K
to
K
, that is,
(g,k)\mapsto g\cdot k
satisfying: (1) Identity:
I\cdot k=k
for all
k\in K
, and (2) Compatibility:
g_{2}\cdot(g_{1}\cdot k)=(g_{2}g_{1})\cdot k
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
k\in K
.

We say that
K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is
(R,G)
-finitely generated if there is a finite subset
R\subseteq K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and a finitely generated subgroup
G\subseteq\{U\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n\times n}{\,:\,}|\det(U)|=1\}
acting on
K
linearly such that

(i)

both the cone
K
and
K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
are invariant under the group action, i.e.,
G\cdot K=K
and
G\cdot(K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})=K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, and

(ii)

for any
z\in K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
there exists
r_{1},\ldots,r_{p}\in R
,
g_{1},\ldots,g_{p}\in G
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{m}
such that

z=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}g_{i}\cdot r_{i}
.

It follows from the definition above that the set
\mathcal{I}(G,R)=\{g\cdot r{\,:\,}g\in G,r\in R\}
is an integral generating set of
{\mathcal{M}}=K\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Therefore, in the pure integer case (
n_{1}=n,n_{2}=0
), the subadditive dual problem (
9
) takes the form:

\displaystyle\rho^{*}:=\sup

\displaystyle f(b)

(19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle f(A(g\cdot r))\leq c^{T}(g\cdot r)

\displaystyle g\in G,r\in R

\displaystyle f(0)=0,\ f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{D}}.

The fact that the integral generating set in the dual (
19
) is
(R,G)
-finitely generated may lead to some algorithmic ideas to solve this dual or to use its feasible dual functions to generate cuts for the associated primal problem.

4.2

Examples of sets
{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)
with conic representable integer hull

Recall the set

{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)=\{(x,y)\in{\mathcal{M}}{\,:\,}Ax+Gy\leq b\}
with
{\mathcal{M}}=K\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})
. When
K
is the cartesian product of linear transformations of nonnegative orthants and the data is rational, by Meyer’s theorem
[
22
]
, the integer hull of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)
is a rational polyhedron (see
[
8
]
for more conditions for this to happen).
We now present examples of sets
{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)
, where the cone
K
is a not necessarily a rational polyhedral cone and such that their integer hull is conic representable, and so the main assumption in Theorem
2
is satisfied.

4.2.1

Conic sets with finitely many integer fibers

Let
b\in\Omega^{\leq}
and
\mathcal{U}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{n_{1}}
be the projection onto
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}
of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b)
. For
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the integer fiber defined by
u
is the convex set

{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b,u)=\{(x,y)\in K\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}){\,:\,}Gy\leq b-Au,\ x=u\}.

Assume that
\mathcal{U}
is a finite set (note that the condition of
\mathcal{U}
being a finite set is always satisfied for conic mixed-integer binary programs) and that all its integer fibers are conic representable, that is, for each
u\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists a regular cone
C^{u}
, matrices
\Pi^{u},\Phi^{u},\Psi^{u}
and a vector
\pi^{u}
such that

{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b,u)=\{(x,y)\in K\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}){\,:\,}\exists\,w^{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{3}^{u}}\ \textup{s.t.}\ \Pi^{u}x+\Phi^{u}y+\Psi^{u}w^{u}\succeq_{C^{u}}\pi^{u},\ x=u\}.

Observe that

\textup{conv}({\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b))=\textup{conv}\left(\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b,u)\right).

(23)

Furthermore, the recession cone of each set
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b,u)
is equal to
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(0,0)=\{(x,y)\in K\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}){\,:\,}Gy\leq 0,\ x=0\}
, so we conclude that the convex hull of the union in (
23
) is a closed set.
The closure of the convex hull of the (finite) union of conic representable sets is known to be conic representable, and from (
23
), we obtain the following explicit formulation for
\textup{conv}({\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b))
(see
[
4
]
):

\begin{split}\textup{conv}({\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b))=\bigg\{(x,y)\in K\cap(\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})\,:\,\exists\,&(x^{u},y^{u},w^{u},\lambda^{u})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{3}^{u}}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+})\,:\,\\
&\Pi^{u}x^{u}+\Phi^{u}y^{u}+\Psi^{u}w^{u}\succeq_{C^{u}}\pi^{u}\lambda^{u},\ x^{u}=u\lambda^{u},\ \textup{for all}\ u\in\mathcal{U}\\
&x=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}x^{u},\ y=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}y^{u},\ 1=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\lambda^{u}\bigg\}.\end{split}

4.2.2

Packing conic mixed-integer programs

A definition of packing conic sets is given in
[
1
]
and a definition of packing semidefinite programs involving nonlinear mappings is given in
[
13
]
. In our setting, the definition given in
[
1
]
is as follows.

Definition 7

(Packing conic set)
.

A packing conic set is a set
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b)=\{(x,y)\in K{\,:\,}Ax+Gy\leq b\}
such that rows of
[A\ G]
belong to the dual cone
K_{*}
.

It can be shown that for packing conic sets, we have that
\Omega^{\leq}={\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}
, that is, the set

{\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(\omega)
is feasible for any
\omega\geq 0
.
If the set
\textup{conv}({\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b))
is bounded, then the generating subadditive functions
F_{\alpha}
are well-defined. In addition, by the discussion in Section
4.2.1
, the set
\textup{conv}({\mathcal{M}}^{\leq}(b))
is conic representable, so we can apply the results in Theorem
2
to conclude that there exists
\alpha^{*}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that the function
F_{\alpha^{*}}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is an optimal solution to the corresponding subadditive dual (
9
).
We now give a characterization for a packing conic set to be a bounded set. For some sufficient conditions for boundedness, see
[
1
]
.

Proposition 9
.

Consider a packing conic set of the form
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b)
. Then, there exists a vector
{\hat{\mu}}\geq 0
such that
\begin{bmatrix}A&G\end{bmatrix}^{T}{\hat{\mu}}\in\textup{int}(K_{*})
if and only if the set
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b)
is a bounded set.

Proof.

Note that the recession cone of
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b)
is the set the
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(0)=\{(x,y)\in K{\,:\,}Ax+Gy\leq 0\}
and that
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b)
is bounded if and only if
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(0)=\{0\}
. For any
(k_{x},k_{y})\in\textup{int}(K_{*})
, we can consider the primal problem

\sup\{k_{x}^{T}x+k_{y}^{T}y{\,:\,}(x,y)\in{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(0)\}
and its dual
\inf\left\{0{\,:\,}\begin{bmatrix}A&G\end{bmatrix}^{T}\mu\succeq_{K_{*}}(k_{x},k_{y}),\ \mu\geq 0\right\}.

(
\Rightarrow
): Assume that there exists a vector
{\hat{\mu}}\geq 0
such that
(k_{x},k_{y}):=\begin{bmatrix}A&G\end{bmatrix}^{T}{\hat{\mu}}\in\textup{int}(K_{*})
. This implies that the dual problem is feasible with the optimal value of 0. By weak duality, we conclude that
k_{x}^{T}x+k_{y}^{T}y\leq 0
for any
(x,y)\in K
. Considering the fact that
(k_{x},k_{y})\in{\textup{int}}(K_{*})
, we conclude that
k_{x}^{T}x+k_{y}^{T}y=0
. This implies that
(x,y)=0
is the only primal feasible solution, implying that
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b)
is a bounded set.

(
\Leftarrow
): Assume that
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(b)
is a bounded set, then
{\mathcal{R}}^{\leq}(0)=\{0\}
. Then, for any
(k_{x},k_{y})\in\textup{int}(K_{*})
the primal problem must have an optimal value of
0
. By strong duality, we must have that the dual is feasible, and therefore, there exists a vector
{\hat{\mu}}\geq 0
such that
\begin{bmatrix}A&G\end{bmatrix}^{T}{\hat{\mu}}-(k_{x},k_{y})\in K_{*}
, which implies that
\begin{bmatrix}A&G\end{bmatrix}^{T}{\hat{\mu}}\in\textup{int}(K_{*})
.
∎

4.3

An example of a conic MIP with block structure: clustering problem

We now give an example for conic MIP with block structure of the form (
17
).
Suppose that we are given points
\xi_{\iota}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
\iota=1,\dots,I
. Our aim is to group these points into
Q
clusters and find one representative
\chi^{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
for each cluster,
q=1,\dots,Q
in such a way that the total Euclidean distance of the points assigned to a cluster to their representative is minimized. This problem can be formulated as the following optimization problem

\min_{\zeta_{\iota}^{q}\in\{0,1\},\chi^{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left\{\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{\iota=1}^{I}\zeta_{\iota}^{q}\|\chi^{q}-\xi_{\iota}\|_{2}:\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\zeta_{\iota}^{q}=1,\ \iota=1,\dots,I\right\},

or equivalently as

\displaystyle\min_{\zeta,\chi,\delta}\left\{\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{\iota=1}^{I}\delta_{\iota}^{q}:\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\zeta_{\iota}^{q}=1,\ \|\chi^{q}-\xi_{\iota}\|_{2}\leq\delta_{\iota}^{q}+M(1-\zeta_{\iota}^{q}),\ \zeta_{\iota}^{q}\in\{0,1\},\ \iota=1,\dots,I;q=1,\dots,Q\right\},

with
M=\max_{\iota,\iota^{\prime}}\|\xi_{\iota}-\xi_{\iota^{\prime}}\|
. To put this formulation in the form of (
17
), we define new variables as in the below formulation:

\displaystyle\min_{\zeta,\chi,\delta,\bar{\zeta},\bar{\eta},\eta}\

\displaystyle\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\sum_{\iota=1}^{I}\delta_{\iota}^{q}

s.t.

\displaystyle\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\zeta_{\iota}^{q}=1,\ \eta_{\iota}^{q}=\chi^{q}-\xi_{\iota},\ \bar{\eta}_{\iota}^{q}=\delta_{\iota}^{q}+M(1-\zeta_{\iota}^{q}),\ \zeta_{\iota}^{q}+\bar{\zeta}_{\iota}^{q}=1\quad

\displaystyle\iota

\displaystyle=1,\dots,I;q=1,\dots,Q

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\iota}^{q}\geq\|\eta_{\iota}^{q}\|_{2},\ \zeta_{\iota}^{q}\geq 0,\ \bar{\zeta}_{\iota}^{q}\geq 0\quad

\displaystyle\iota

\displaystyle=1,\dots,I;q=1,\dots,Q

\displaystyle\zeta_{\iota}^{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\bar{\zeta}_{\iota}^{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\chi^{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{p},\delta_{\iota}^{q}\in{\mathbb{R}},\bar{\eta}_{\iota}^{q}\in\mathbb{R},\eta_{\iota}^{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\quad

\displaystyle\iota

\displaystyle=1,\dots,I;q=1,\dots,Q,

Now, this formulation is in the form (
17
) with
\ell=q
,

x^{q}=[(\zeta_{\iota}^{q})_{\iota},(\bar{\zeta}_{\iota}^{q})_{\iota}]\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{I}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{I}
and

y^{q}=[(\chi^{q},(\delta_{\iota}^{q})_{\iota},(\bar{\eta}_{\iota}^{q},\eta_{\iota}^{q})_{\iota}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{I}\times{\boldsymbol{L}^{p+1,I}}
, where
{\boldsymbol{L}^{p+1,I}}
is the product of
I
many Lorentz cones in
{\mathbb{R}}^{p+1}
.

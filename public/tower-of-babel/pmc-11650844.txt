The assessment of dupilumab in children with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases
Authors: Tingting Shi, Shuning Wu, Rongshan Chen, Yaping Xie, Genquan Yin, Chunhui He, Cuiping Liang, Gen Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDupilumab inhibiting the signaling of interleukin(IL)-4 and IL-13 was recommended for the treatment of severe asthma in children ≥ 6 years old according to the Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA,2024).This study aimed to analyse the efficacy and safety of dupilumab in paediatric patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory disease in a real-world population.MethodsWe evaluated the medical records of paediatric patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases, such as atopic dermatitis (AD) and allergic rhinitis (AR), receiving dupilumab treatment.ResultsTwenty-five children (16 boys; mean age, 9.32 ± 2.58 years) were included. All the patients were diagnosed with moderate-to-severe asthma, 92% (23/25) with AR, and 64.0% (16/25) with AD. Among the 25 patients, no severe adverse reactions occurred, the times of severe asthma exacerbation were significantly lower, and the Asthma Control Test (ACT) / Child-Asthma Control Test (C-ACT) scores were significantly higher than those before the 24-week dupilumab treatment (all P = 0.00). The Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure(POEM) and Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Scale(NRS), Rhinitis Four-point, and Rhinitis Visual Analogue Scale(VAS) scores were significantly lower than those at baseline (all P < 0.05). After receiving 24-week dupilumab treatment, the serum total immunoglobulin E (tIgE) and fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) level were reduced by 56.54% and 70.47% respectively at the 24th week (P = 0.00); the lung function parameters including large airways such as percent predicted forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1% pred) and small airways like percent predicted forced expiratory flow at 25–75%, were significantly higher than those before dupilumab (all P < 0.05).ConclusionsDupilumab reduced asthma exacerbations and improved symptom control without severe adverse reactions in paediatric patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases. It also decreased biomarkers of type 2 inflammation and improved lung function parameters, including both large and small airways. Considering the racial diversity, a large real-world study in China is required to confirm the role of dupilumab in paediatric patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases.Keywords: Dupilumab, Children, Asthma, Comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases

Background

Asthma is the most common chronic disease in children [1], and its prevalence is increasing in children and adolescents worldwide [2]. About 5–10% of all children with asthma have characteristics defined as severe [3, 4]. Moderate-to-severe asthma in children may progress to reduce lung function and affect lung growth [5], and increase the risk of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) in adulthood [6]. Type 2 inflammation is the most common driver of asthma in children which is characterised by the release of the cytokines interleukin IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, leading to elevated levels of peripheral or sputum eosinophils (EOS) and fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO), as well as serum total immunoglobulin E (tIgE) [7, 8]. In addition to asthma, type 2 inflammatory cytokines have also been implicated in numerous allergic diseases such as atopic dermatitis (AD) and allergic rhinitis (AR). The above-mentioned biomarkers are important clinical indicators of type 2 inflammation. The Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA,2024) recommends the use of these biomarkers to identify patients with severe asthma and type 2 inflammation who may benefit from therapy with biologicals [9].

Dupilumab, a human monoclonal antibody, blocks the shared IL-4 alpha subunit (IL-4Rα) of the receptors for the type 2 cytokines IL-4 and IL-13, inhibiting their signaling. The GINA (2022/2024) recommends dupilumab for the treatment of asthma in children ≥ 6 years old [9, 10]. It is approved for use in patients aged ≥ 6 years old with moderate-to-severe asthma with an eosinophilic phenotype in the United States of America (USA) and in patients aged ≥ 6 years old with severe asthma with type 2 inflammation in the European Union (EU) [11, 12]. In China, dupilumab has been approved for use in patients aged ≥ 12 years old with moderate-to-severe asthma with type 2 inflammation as of November 2023.

Several randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled trials of dupilumab in children have been reported in the past three years [4, 13, 14]. However, the characteristics of patients enrolled in clinical trials may differ from those of real-world populations, and most real-world evidence originates from small retrospective studies of adolescent and adult populations [15, 16]. Herein, we analysed 25 paediatric patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases such as AD and AR to assess the real-world efficacy and safety of dupilumab in China.

Methods

Patients and interventions

This study included 25 paediatric patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases who received dupilumab treatment for at least 24 weeks at the Pediatric Asthma and Allergy Clinic of the Respiratory Department of the Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center, Guangzhou Medical University, between March 2023 and March 2024. The definition of severe asthma is based on the criteria of GINA guidelines [9, 10]. All the patients met the inclusion criteria: (1) patients were aged ≥ 6 years old and ≤ 18 years old; (2) the diagnosis of asthma and asthma control were based on the GINA guidelines [9, 10]; the criteria of AD and AR were defined according to the guidelines in China [17, 18]; all the diagnoses were made by physicians with extensive experience in the Pediatric Asthma and Allergy Clinic; (3)the definition of type 2 inflammation was based on the GINA guidelines [9, 10, 19]: ① baseline blood EOS ≥ 150 cells/µL, ② FeNO ≥ 20 ppb, ③ evidence of allergic asthma with tIgE ≥ 30 IU/mL and one aeroallergen-specific IgE ≥ 0.35 kU/L at baseline; (4) the consent of the child and their guardian was obtained to accept dupilumab treatment for at least 24 weeks, and written informed consent documents were signed. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) allergy to dupilumab and (2) incomplete patient data. This study was performed in accordance with the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki (7th revision). This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center, Guangzhou Medical University.

Eligible patients received subcutaneous dupilumab every 2–4 weeks (q2-4w) for 24 weeks in a weight-tiered fashion according to the dupilumab instruction in China (Produced by France Sanofi (China) Co., LTD., Chinese medicine approval number: S20200017): those with a body weight of ≤ 30 kg received dupilumab 300 mg q4w, those with a body weight > 30 kg, ≤ 60 kg received dupilumab 300 mg q3w(Only 300 mg dosage form is available instead of the 200 mg dosage form in most children hospitals including our hospital in China now, the total dose administered over the 24 weeks treatment was the same for the 200 mg q2w dosing scheme and the 300 mg q3w dosing scheme.), and those with a body weight > 60 kg received dupilumab 300 mg q2w.

Data collection and end points

Data on demographics, clinical history, and baseline characteristics before and after dupilumab treatment (at 16 and 24 weeks) were collected for all patients. The characteristics included: (1) the number of severe asthma exacerbations during the 24-week treatment period; (2) symptom scores such as the Asthma Control Test (ACT, ≥ 12 years old) and Child-Asthma Control Test (C-ACT, 4–11 years old) in moderate-to-severe asthma [10], the Patient-Oriented Eczema Measure (POEM) and Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) in AD(patient- oriented assessment items) [17], and the Rhinitis Four-point Score and Rhinitis Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) in AR [18]; (3) type 2 inflammation biomarkers: blood EOS absolute count, tIgE, FeNO, and aeroallergen-specific IgE; (4) lung function parameters: percent predicted pre-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1% pred), percent predicted forced vital capacity (FVC% pred), FEV1/FVC ratio, percent predicted peak expiratory flow (PEF% pred), and predicted forced expiratory flow at 25–75%, 50%, and 75% (FEF25%−75% pred, FEF50% pred, and FEF75% pred, respectively); and (5) the numbers of patients experiencing any adverse events secondary to dupilumab treatment.

The primary endpoints were the number of severe asthma exacerbations and the change in ACT/C-ACT during the 24-week treatment period. Secondary endpoints included changes in the POEM and NRS scores in AD and in the Rhinitis Four-point Score and Rhinitis VAS in AR, blood EOS absolute count, tIgE, FeNO, FEV1% pred, FVC% pred, PEF% pred, FEF25%−75% pred, FEF50% pred, and FEF75% pred from baseline to week 24. A severe asthma exacerbation was defined as an event requiring systemic corticosteroid use for ≥ 3 days, hospitalization, or a visit to the emergency department.

Statistical analysis

Excel was used to establish and organise the database, and SPSS (version 20.0) was used for statistical analysis. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of the numerical variables. Numerical variables were presented as means and standard deviations, whereas categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. A completely randomised design analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed for numerical variables following a normal distribution with homogeneity of variance. When significant differences were observed, pairwise comparisons were conducted using the Student-Newman-Keuls (S-N-K) method. For numerical variables that did not follow a normal distribution or exhibited heterogeneity of variances, the Kruskal-Wallis H test was employed. The paired Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for variables that showed statistically significant differences. Categorical variables were analysed using chi-square tests including the four-fold table chi-square test and the R*C chi-square test. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was conducted to examine the correlation between numerical variables indicating therapeutic efficacy and different treatment durations, and line graphs were used to depict the corresponding trends. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and the significance level was set at α = 0.05.

Results

Study patients

Twenty-eight paediatric patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases received dupilumab treatment for at least 24 weeks at the Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center, Guangzhou Medical University. Three patients were excluded based on the exclusion criteria (due to incomplete patient data). The demographics and characteristics of the 25 enrolled patients are presented in Table 1. Of these patients, 64.00% (16/25) were boys and 36.00% (9/25) were girls. The mean age was 9.32 ± 2.58 years, with ages ranging from 6 to 13 years old. The mean height was 138.72 ± 18.80 centimeters (cm) and the mean weight was 34.96 ± 13.72 kg (kg) at the baseline. The mean height for age (HFA) z-score was 0.62 and body mass index (BMI) for age z-score was 0.27 at the baseline. After 24-weeks treatment of dupilumab, the mean HFA z-score was 0.65 and BMI for age z-score was 0.29 and there was no significance between the baseline and the week 24th (P>0.05). All patients (100.00%, 25/25) were diagnosed with moderate-to-severe asthma, of whom 92.00% (23/25) had AR and 64.00% (16/25) had AD. The mean duration of asthma was 4.32 ± 2.58 years. In addition, 92.00% (23/25) of the patients were allergic to dust mites. 2 patients were allergen negative. Among the 25 patients, 3 patients were administrated with fluticasone propionate aerosol and 22 with budesonide and formoterol powder inhalation as long-term control drugs. Before 24-weeks treatment of dupilumab, the mean inhaled glucocorticoids (ICS) dose (all converted to budesonide,100.00ug budesonide = 62.50ug fluticasone) was 252.5 ± 78.93ug/per day.

The primary end point

The times of severe asthma exacerbations

Among the 25 patients with moderate-to-severe asthma, the mean times of severe asthma exacerbations in the 24 weeks before receiving dupilumab treatment was 1.44 ± 0.58. After receiving dupilumab, the times of severe asthma exacerbations during the 24-week treatment period was significantly lower than that before treatment (0.16 ± 0.37 vs. 1.44 ± 0.58, P = 0.00). The percentage of patients without exacerbations during the 24-week treatment period was 84.00% (21/25). The results are shown in Fig. 1.

Asthma control (ACT/C-ACT scores)

Among the 25 patients, the mean ACT score (n = 7) was 18.29 ± 0.76 and the mean C-ACT score(n = 18) was 18.61 ± 0.98 before dupilumab treatment. After receiving the 24-week dupilumab treatment, the mean ACT and C-ACT scores were significantly higher than those before dupilumab (24.43 ± 0.79 vs. 18.29 ± 0.76, P = 0.00; and 26.00 ± 1.24 vs. 18.61 ± 0.98, P = 0.00, respectively). The results are shown in Fig. 1. After 24-weeks treatment of dupilumab, the mean ICS dose (all converted to budesonide,100.00ug budesonide = 62.50ug fluticasone) was 164.8 ± 78.59ug/per day.

Blood EOS absolute counting

Among the 25 patients, the mean absolute EOS count before dupilumab treatment was 0.58 ± 0.28 × 109/L. After receiving the 24-week dupilumab treatment, there was no significant change in absolute EOS count (0.45 ± 0.24 vs. 0.58 ± 0.28, P = 0.22). Table 2 presents the results.

FeNO

Among the 25 patients, the mean level of FeNO before receiving dupilumab was 43.08 ± 30.77 ppb, including two patients with high FeNO levels of 112.00 ppb and 130.00 ppb. The mean level of FeNO was reduced by 70.47% by week 24 (12.72 ± 3.70 vs. 43.08 ± 30.77 ppb, P = 0.00). The high FeNO levels in the aforementioned two patients were reduced to 19.00 ppb and 21.00 ppb respectively by the 24th week. Table 2 presents the results.

Lung function endpoints

Large airways: FEV1% pred and PEF% pred

The mean FEV1% pred at baseline was 82.03 ± 15.05 and values increased to 87.96 ± 11.70 by week 16 and to 92.85 ± 11.21 by week 24. The mean PEF% pred at baseline was 80.42 ± 12.32 and values increased to 88.85 ± 9.79 by week 16 and to 90.48 ± 10.98 at week 24. After receiving the 24-week dupilumab treatment, the mean FEV1% pred was significantly higher than that before treatment (92.85 ± 11.21 vs. 82.03 ± 15.05, P = 0.01). There was a significant increase in PEF% pred at the 16th week compared with the baseline value, and this increase was maintained at the 24th week (both P < 0.05).

Small airways: FEF25%−75% pred, FEF50% pred, FEF75% pred

The mean FEF25%−75%, FEF50% and FEF75% at baseline were 57.76 ± 19.04, 52.66 ± 21.19, and 44.41 ± 19.23 respectively. After patient received the 24-week dupilumab treatment, all the values were significantly higher than those before dupilumab (69.60 ± 13.81 vs. 57.76 ± 19.04 (FEF25%−75%), 64.87 ± 16.13 vs. 52.66 ± 21.19(FEF 50%),and 57.81 ± 17.87 vs. 44.41 ± 19.23(FEF 75%),all P<0.05). There was no significant difference between those at baseline and after 16-week dupilumab treatment in FEF25%−75% pred, FEF50% pred, FEF75% pred (P > 0.05).

Bronchodilator response test

A positive bronchodilator response test was detected in 68% (17/25) of the patients at baseline. After receiving 24-week dupilumab treatment, 16% (4/25) of patients showed positive results, which was significantly lower than that at baseline (P = 0.001).

Safety

Among the 25 patients, only one had an injection site reaction (oedema) at the initial dose, and the other patients did not have any severe adverse reactions.

Discussion

Uncontrolled asthma in childhood can lead to reduced lung growth and an increased risk of fixed airflow obstruction and COPD in adult life [5, 20]. Therefore, important long-term goals of asthma management include good symptom control and minimising airflow limitations. In several analysis of the VOYAGE trial (RCT) [4, 13, 14, 21], dupilumab showed promise for the treatment of patients with severe asthma, especially for improving lung function. In our study, we found that most patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases had no asthma exacerbations or severe adverse reactions during the 24-week dupilumab treatment period, and had good symptom control with increased ACT/C-ACT scores and decreased POEM and NRS, Rhinitis Four-point, and Rhinitis VAS scores. The biomarkers of type 2 inflammation decreased significantly, and significant improvements in lung function parameters, including both the large and small airways, were also observed in our study. Dupilumab treatment in the real world was aligned with RCT findings, and the alignment suggested improved patient outcomes.

Previous studies [22, 23] showed that dupilumab was effective in reducing the asthma exacerbation rate (AER) regardless of baseline demographic and disease characteristics in children with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases; in addition, the patients achieved clinical asthma remission at the 24-week point. In our study, 84% of the patients had no asthma exacerbations in the 24th week, and the ACT/C-ACT score increased significantly at the 24th week compared with the baseline. Our findings are consistent with those of previous studies. The results showing a reduced number of asthma exacerbations supported the efficacy of dupilumab in suppressing IL-4 and IL-13 which can bring about good symptom control in children with type 2 asthma. The reduced number of asthma exacerbations also led to a reduction in systemic corticosteroid exposure. In addition to good control of asthma, dupilumab treatment also improved the symptoms of comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases in our study, with decreased POEM and NRS scores in patients with AD and Rhinitis Four-point and Rhinitis VAS scores in patients with AR. Previously published literature regarding the use of dupilumab in the treatment of patients with asthma and comorbid AD or AR also showed that dupilumab provided clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvements in the symptoms of AD and AR [8, 24, 25].

Some studies [8, 25, 26] showed that dupilumab reduced tIgE, blood EOS, and FeNO levels in patients aged 6 to 11 years with uncontrolled moderate-to-severe asthma. Decreased tIgE and FeNO levels were also observed in the present study. The VOYAGE Study [14] showed a steady and sustained decrease in tIgE in dupilumab-treated patients with allergic asthma over a 52-week treatment period, and dupilumab treatment for up to 2 years reduced tIgE by up to 90% from baseline. In our study, dupilumab decreased the tIgE levels by 56.54% throughout the 24-week treatment period in children with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases. This progressive decline in tIgE is consistent with the mechanistic roles of dupilumab in the blockade of IL-4 and IL-13 mediated signaling, ultimately downregulating IgE class switching [7, 27]. Interestingly, a previous study [14] showed that dupilumab treatment also reduced allergen-specific serum IgE levels against perennial aeroallergens tested, and greater reductions in specific IgE levels were observed in patients with multi-allergen sensitization. Because of lacking of early recognition of this phenomenon and the financial burden of the patient’s family, we followed up specific IgE levels in very few patients in the 24-weeks treatments in our study. It is worth paying attention to the detection of specific allergens in dupilumab treatment in future studies and collecting more samples for confirmation. EOS, which have a pathological role principally in tissues rather than in the bloodstream, are the key cellular modulators of type 2 inflammation [28]. Immediate increases in EOS in the bloodstream of patients with asthma receiving dupilumab treatment have been reported in the literature, with the mechanism of the blockade of IL-4 and IL-13 limiting EOS traffic from the blood to the bronchial mucosa [13]. However, in our study, EOS in the bloodstream remained stable and did not increase during the 24-week dupilumab treatment, which was not consistent with the above-mentioned study but was in line with other previous studies [4, 29]. The mechanism of this phenomenon is still not clear, and the reason might be that dupilumab blocks the migration of EOS into tissues by inhibiting the production of eotaxin and vascular cell adhesion molecules. However, dupilumab cannot inhibit the production of EOS [30]. Some studies have also shown a gradually decreasing trend in EOS in the bloodstream after a 24-week dupilumab treatment [31, 32]. A reduction in blood EOS levels may reflect reduced Th2 differentiation from naïve T cells (Th0) through IL-4 blockade, leading to reduced EOS levels [33]. Further studies are required to confirm the role of dupilumab-induced blood EOS levels.

Little is known about the effects of biologic therapies on lung function in children with asthma [21]. Studies on other biologic agents such as omalizumab (an anti-IgE antibody) and mepolizumab (an anti-IL-5 antibody) showed no significant differences in FEV1 or other lung function parameters compared with placebo in randomised clinical trials or systematic literature reviews in children aged 6 to 11 years [7, 34, 35]. A phase 3 LIBERTY ASTHMA VOYAGE study [21] showed that dupilumab treatment led to significant, rapid, and sustained improvements in multiple measures of lung function in a paediatric population with moderate-to-severe type 2 asthma. In our study, improvement in lung function was not noticeable by week 16. However, significant improvements were observed in both the large and small airways by week 24. Compared to a previous study [21], there were some slight differences in that rapid improvements in lung function parameters were not observed early in the treatment course with dupilumab, but sustained improvements were observed after the 24-week dupilumab treatment in both the large and small airways. The bronchodilator response was also reduced in our study, with a positive bronchodilator response test percentage of 16% at the 24th week compared with 68% at baseline. According to previous studies [36], IL-4 and IL-13 can induce hyperresponsiveness in the small airways, and suppression of IL-4 and IL-13 appears to be the only mechanism that can lead to sustained improvements in lung function in patients with asthma [21]. This may explain the potential of dupilumab treatment to produce sustained improvements in lung function. Further studies are required to demonstrate the long-term effects of dupilumab on other indices of lung function and its impact on airway remodelling.

Some long-term studies [4, 37] presented low rate of serious adverse events (SAEs) in the dupilumab treatments in children (3.0-4.8%). In our study, only one (4%) had an injection site reaction (oedema) at the initial dose, and the other patients did not have any SAEs. The incidence rate of SAEs in our study is in line with studies above. Further studies in children are necessary to confirm the safety findings.

Our study had several limitations. The assessments of quality of life such as Pediatric Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (PAQLQ) in asthma and the doctor-oriented assessment Eczema area and severity index (EASI) scores and the SCORing Atopic Dermatitis(SCORAD) scores in AD which were an essential part of managing chronic diseases were not performed. Only 300 mg dosage form is available instead of the 200 mg dosage form in China, although the total dose administered over the 24 weeks treatment is the same, further study would be to confirm if there is different efficiency because of the pharmacokinetics.Because of the initial stage of dupilumab treatment in China, this study is a descriptive summary using observed data, and the sample size was small. Owing to the high cost of dupilumab, patients were enrolled on a voluntary basis which may have resulted in a selection bias.

Conclusions

Dupilumab was safe and no severe adverse reactions were observed in this study. Dupilumab reduced asthma exacerbations and improved symptom control, with increased ACT/C-ACT scores and decreased POEM and NRS, Rhinitis Four-point, and Rhinitis VAS scores in patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases. Dupilumab also decreased the biomarkers of type 2 inflammation and improved lung function parameters, including both the large and small airways. Considering the influence of racial diversity, a large real-world study in China is required to confirm the role of dupilumab in the treatment of patients with moderate-to-severe asthma and comorbid type 2 inflammatory diseases.

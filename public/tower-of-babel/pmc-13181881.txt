Tyrosine kinase inhibitors combined with PD-1 inhibitors reduce peritoneal metastasis risk for patients with ruptured HCC receiving TACE/TAE: a multicenter retrospective study
Authors: Jie Ji, Yaping Zhang, Lele Yan, Xinjie Sun, Jialei Wang, Jie Gu, Weizhong Zhou, Penghua Lv
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to evaluate whether the combination of tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and PD-1 inhibitors can reduce the risk of peritoneal metastasis (PM) in patients with ruptured hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) after transcatheter chemoembolization/embolization (TACE/TAE).Materials and methodsThis study included 163 patients with ruptured HCC who received TACE/TAE at 4 centers from June 2015 to June 2023. Patients were categorized into two groups based on their treatment: the combination group, which received TKIs plus PD-1 inhibitors, and the TACE/TAE group, which did not. Propensity score matching (PSM) analysis was performed in a ratio of 1:2 to reduce bias between the groups. The PM rate, overall survival (OS) and the occurrence of adverse events were analyzed and compared between the two groups. Moreover, the independent factors associated with PM were further evaluated.ResultsThe median follow-up duration was 801 days [95% confidence interval (CI): 720.2–925.7]. After PSM, the combination and TACE/TAE groups comprised 45 and 90 patients, respectively. A significant difference was observed in the peritoneal PM rate (combination group: 6.7% vs. TACE/TAE group: 23.3%, P = 0.032). The corresponding 3-, 6-, 12-, and 24-month PM cumulative incidence was 5.6%, 15.7%, 22.5%, and 24.1% in the TACE/TAE group, and 0%, 0%, 4.9%, and 7.7% in the combination group, respectively (P < 0.001). The combination group exhibited a significantly longer median OS compared to the TACE/TAE group (OS:566 days vs. 120 days, P < 0.001). The multivariate competing risk analysis identified hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection [subdistribution hazard ratio (SHR), 0.420; 95% CI: 0.188–0.939; P = 0.035), Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage C (BCLC C) (SHR, 0.228; 95% CI: 0.059–0.874; P = 0.031), and combination therapy (SHR, 0.267; 95% CI: 0.082–0.866; P = 0.028) as the independent factors for PM. More adverse events were witnessed in the combination group compared with the monotherapy group, most of which were tolerable and manageable.ConclusionsThe combination of TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors reduced the risk of PM compared with TACE/TAE in patients with ruptured HCC. Also, PM was less likely to occur in patients with HBV-related and BCLC C ruptured HCC.Keywords: Combination therapy, Monotherapy, Peritoneal metastasis, Ruptured hepatocellular carcinoma, Transcatheter arterial chemoembolization

Introduction

Spontaneous tumor rupture is a life-threatening complication of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), with an incidence ranging from 3% to 15% and associated mortality rates of up to 32%–75% [1–3]. The management strategies for controlling bleeding include emergency surgery and transcatheter chemoembolization/embolization (TACE/TAE) [4–7]. Despite successful hemostasis, spontaneous rupture can still lead to the spillage of tumor cells into the peritoneal cavity, potentially resulting in metastatic deposits. The incidence of peritoneal metastasis (PM) in ruptured HCC ranges from 12.3% to 18.2% [8–10]. Once PM occurs, many patients are classified as having advanced HCC with a poor prognosis.

Hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) involves the administration of chemotherapeutic drugs at high temperatures directly into the peritoneal cavity [11]. This approach leverages the synergistic effect of hyperthermia and chemotherapy to effectively eliminate tumor cells and prevent or treat peritoneal metastatic cancer. However, Ruan et al. reported no impact of HIPEC on the prognosis of patients with ruptured HCC [11]. The data on the efficacy of HIPEC in mitigating the risk of peritoneal dissemination after HCC rupture are limited. Therefore, exploring effective methods to prevent PM after spontaneous HCC rupture is crucial.

Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) combined with immunotherapy have been shown to significantly improve progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with unresectable HCC undergoing TACE [12–14]. Ji et al. reported that adding TKIs plus PD-1 inhibitors may extend PFS compared with TACE/TAE in patients with ruptured HCC [15]. However, whether combination therapy effectively reduces the risk of PM in these patients remains unclear. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate whether combining TKIs with PD-1 inhibitors could reduce the risk of PM, and to identify the risk factors for PM in these patients.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study included 163 patients with ruptured HCC who received treatment at 4 tertiary hospitals from June 2015 to June 2023 (Fig. 1). The study was approved by the institutional ethics review boards of the participating hospitals and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki on human research and its subsequent amendments. The informed consent was waived considering the retrospective nature of the study. The guidelines of the European Association for the Study of the Liver were followed for HCC diagnosis [16]. Spontaneous HCC rupture was diagnosed based on acute abdominal symptoms without a history of trauma and on imaging examinations, with diagnostic abdominal paracentesis performed when necessary. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) first diagnosis of ruptured HCC, (2) treatment with either TACE plus TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors or TACE alone, and (3) receipt of more than one cycle of TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) delayed hepatectomy, (2) incomplete medical records, and (3) a follow-up period of less than 1 year. The clinical characteristics, HCC-related radiological findings, and information regarding treatments and clinical outcomes were analyzed retrospectively. The allocation to combination therapy or TACE/TAE alone was determined through a multidisciplinary team discussion, considering tumor burden, liver function, performance status, and hemodynamic stability after rupture. For example, a ruptured HCC patient in advanced tumor stage, with high tumor burden but good liver function reserve, is recommended to undergo combination treatment. However, patients or their family members would be informed of the advantages and disadvantages of TACE/TAE with or without TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors, including potential therapeutic effects, adverse events, and costs before the final decision-making.

TACE/TAE procedure

Hepatic angiography was performed using a 5 F catheter to identify tumor staining and confirm the arterial feeders supplying the ruptured HCCs. A microcatheter (2.7 F; Terumo Medical Corp, Tokyo, Japan; or 2.4 F; Merit Maestro, UT, USA) was then superselectively inserted into the targeted arteries. The choice between chemoembolization and bland embolization was based on the patient’s liver function and vital signs. TACE using a mixture of iodized oil (Andre Guerbet Laboratories) and epirubicin (Pharmorubicin; Pfizer, Wuxi, China) was primarily considered for most patients with ruptured HCC in our centers, whereas TAE using only iodized oil was preferred for patients with Child–Pugh class B or even worse, or hypovolemic shock. Gelatin sponge particles were used in both TACE and TAE procedures. The endpoint of embolization was flow stasis in the tumor-feeding arteries.

Administration of TKIs and anti-PD-1 agents

Various TKIs were administered orally to patients receiving combination therapy, including sorafenib (400 mg twice daily), lenvatinib (8 mg/day for body weight < 60 kg or 12 mg/day for body weight ≥ 60 kg), and apatinib (250 mg/day). PD-1 inhibitors were administered intravenously every 3 weeks, including camrelizumab 200 mg, tislelizumab 200 mg, and sintilimab 200 mg. The doses of TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors were adjusted or discontinued based on tumor progression or unacceptable adverse events.

Follow-up protocol

Tumor markers and contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) were reviewed approximately 1 month after the initial TACE/TAE. If residual viable tumors or new lesions were detected, additional TACE procedures were recommended based on a multidisciplinary team discussion. If no new lesions were identified, follow-up examinations were conducted every 2–3 months. PM was diagnosed based on histological evaluations, enhanced CT or MRI revealing typical peritoneal nodules of HCC, or peritoneal infiltration consistently identified as PM by two experienced radiologists. The targeted outcome was PM events. Deaths from non-PM events comprised competing events by the end of follow-up. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5. All patients were followed up continuously until death or the end of the study (June 30, 2024).

Statistical analysis

The comparability between patients in the combination and TACE/TAE groups was ensured by performing one-to-two propensity score matching (PSM) to reduce selection bias and potential confounders. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used to compare categorical data. The cumulative incidence of PM was calculated using the Fine and Gray competing risk approach. Overall survival (OS) was estimated with the Kaplan-Meier method, and the difference was compared using the log-rank test. Factors with a P value less than 0.05 in the univariate competing risk analysis were included in a multivariable regression model. A backward stepwise variable-selection process was employed to identify the independent predictors of PM. All analyses were two sided, with a statistical significance threshold of 0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 22.0; IBM Corp., NY, USA) and R (version 3.3.2) with the packages matchIt, survival, and comprisk (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Patients’ characteristics

The baseline characteristics of the patients are presented in Table 1. The median follow-up duration was 801 days (95% CI: 720.2–925.7). Among the 163 patients with ruptured HCC in this study, 112 patients underwent TACE and 51 patients underwent TAE for bleeding control. The combination and TACE/TAE groups comprised 45 and 118 patients, respectively. Prior to PSM, significant differences in α-fetoprotein (AFP) levels, tumor number, and Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage were observed between the two groups. After PSM in a ratio of 1:2, the combination group included 45 patients, whereas the TACE/TAE group included 90 patients. No statistically significant differences were observed in AFP (P = 0.211), tumor number (P = 0.127), or BCLC stage (P = 0.079) between the two groups. Various TKIs combined with PD-1 inhibitors were used in the combination group, including sorafenib plus camrelizumab (n = 4), sorafenib plus tislelizumab (n = 2), lenvatinib plus camrelizumab (n = 6), lenvatinib plus tislelizumab (n = 7), lenvatinib plus sintilimab (n = 11), and apatinib plus camrelizumab (n = 15). The median duration of TKI treatment was 298 days (range, 94–560 days), and the median number of PD-1 inhibitor cycles was 8 (range, 2–15 cycles).

Comparison of PM rates between the groups before and after PSM

Before PSM, three patients in the combination group developed PM, compared with 33 patients in the TACE/TAE group (Fig. 2). A significant difference was found between the two groups (P = 0.007). The estimated 3-, 6-, 12-, and 24-month cumulative incidence of PM was 4.2%, 13.7%, 25.7%, and 28.2% in the TACE/TAE group and 0%, 0%, 4.9%, and 7.7% in the combination group, respectively (Fig. 3a). The difference in PM rates between the groups was statistically significant (P = 0.004).

After PSM, 28 patients were excluded from the TACE/TAE group. A statistically significant difference in PM rates remained between the two groups (6.7% vs. 23.3%, P = 0.032). The median survival following the occurrence of PM in patients with ruptured HCC was 115 days. The corresponding 3-, 6-, 12-, and 24-month cumulative incidence of PM was 5.6%, 15.7%, 22.5%, and 24.1% in the TACE/TAE group and 0%, 0%, 4.9%, and 7.7% in the combination group, respectively (Fig. 3b). PM rates continued to show statistically significant differences between the groups (P < 0.001).

Comparison of OS between the groups before and after PSM

Before PSM, the TACE/TAE group had a median OS of 150 days [confidence interval (CI): 110.1–189.8.1.8]. After PSM, the TACE/TAE group had a median OS of 120 days (95% CI: 98.3–141.6.3.6). The combination group had a median OS of 566 days (95% CI: 227.1–904.8.1.8) before and after PSM. Significant differences were observed in OS rates between the two groups before and after PSM (Fig. 4).

Independent factors for PM in patients with ruptured HCC

Table 2 presents the predictors for PM using univariate and multivariate competing risk analyses. In the multivariate analysis, hepatitis B virus (HBV) [subdistribution hazard ratio (SHR), 0.420; 95% confidence interval (CI), 0.188–0.939; P = 0.035), BCLC C (SHR, 0.228; 95% CI, 0.059–0.874; P = 0.031), and combination therapy (SHR, 0.267; 95% CI, 0.082–0.866; P = 0.028) were identified as the independent predictors of PM in patients with ruptured HCC after TACE/TAE. However, BCLC B was not associated with the incidence of PM.

Safety evaluation

TRAEs observed between the two groups are shown in Table 3. In the TACE/TAE group, the most common adverse events were transaminitis (56.6%), nausea (51.1%), decreased appetite (42.2%), and fever (41.1%). In the combination group, the most frequent adverse events were nausea (64.4%), hypertension (64.4%), transaminitis (61.5%), and decreased appetite (44.4%). No Grade 5 adverse events were observed in either group. The most common potentially TRAE related to TKIs was hypertension (Grade 1/2: 55.5%; Grade 3/4: 8.9%), and the most common potentially TRAE related to PD-1 inhibitors was hypothyroidism (Grade 1/2: 28.8%; Grade 3/4: 2.2%). More adverse events were observed in the combination group compared with the TACE/TAE group, most of which were manageable after the suspension of administration and symptomatic treatment. No cases of discontinuation of TKIs or PD-1 inhibitors due to TRAEs were recorded.

Discussion

The prevention of PM following HCC rupture remains challenging due to limited treatment options. Our study suggests that combining TKIs with PD-1 inhibitors may be a promising strategy to reduce PM risk in patients with ruptured HCC after TACE/TAE.

PM is a major concern when considering the prognosis of patients with ruptured HCC. Kwak et al. demonstrated HCC rupture as an independent risk factor for PM in these patients [17]. Previous studies have reported that the incidence of PM after TAE, even with delayed hepatectomy, remains high, ranging from 13.8% to 40.7% [18–20]. The prognosis is typically poor after the occurrence of PM. In our study, the median survival after the diagnosis of PM in patients with HCC was only 115 days, aligning with the 3-month survival period reported in previous studies. In this study, the incidence of PM in the TACE/TAE group was 23.3%, consistent with previous findings [19]. In contrast, the incidence of PM in the combination group was 6.7%, significantly lower than that in the TACE/TAE group. Previous studies did not report the incidence of PM at different time points. However, our study provided 3-, 6-, 12-, and 24-month data on PM incidence in both groups, confirming a significant difference between them. Previous studies have demonstrated that TACE combined with TKIs plus PD-(L)1 inhibitors can prolong PFS in patients with HCC due to their synergistic effects. TKIs can directly influence immune cells and facilitate immune evasion, besides indirectly impacting immunity by increasing vessel permeability [13, 21, 22]. Additionally, anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibodies have been effective in suppressing PM growth in immunocompetent mouse models of colon and ovarian cancers [23–25]. However, the mechanisms by which TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors reduce PM in the context of TAE/TACE require further investigation.

In our study, the combination of TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors emerged as an independent predictive factor for PM. Moreover, HBV was identified as a protective factor, which reduced the risk of PM by 58%. Although HBV infection is a known cause of liver cancer, why PM occurs less frequently in patients with HBV-related HCC needs further elucidation. BCLC stage C was also identified as an independent factor for PM in the multivariate analysis. A possible explanation is that patients with advanced HCC may have a shorter survival, with some potentially dying before the incidence of PM. It is worth noting that the proportion of patients with BCLC-C and extrahepatic metastasis in our study was relatively higher in TACE/TAE group. These disparities may give rise to variations in the outcomes of patients. A large-scale prospective study was required for further validation.

The safety profile of TACE combined with TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors in patients with ruptured HCC was consistent with previous related findings [13, 26]. No new safety concerns were identified for triple therapy in these patients. In our study, the combination therapy group had a higher incidence of TRAEs (Grade ≥ 3 or 4). TACE-induced ischemic injury, combined with the hepatotoxic effects of TKIs and immune-related hepatitis, poses a significant risk of acute liver decompensation. Close monitoring of liver function after TACE is essential to guide the timely initiation of TKIs and immunotherapies. In our cohort, transaminitis occurred in 61.5% of patients in the combination group. Moreover, hypothyroidism was the most common immune-related adverse event, occurring in 31% of patients. All immune-related AEs were manageable after suspending PD-1 inhibitors and symptomatic treatment.

This study had several limitations. First, the retrospective nature of the study introduced potential biases, despite the use of PSM. Second, a majority of patients included in the study had HBV infection, limiting the generalizability of our findings to patients with other etiologies of HCC. Third, the radiographic distinction between true tumor rupture with hemoperitoneum and bleeding into pre-existing ascites or early peritoneal involvement can be difficult, particularly in advanced-stage HCC patients. Future prospective studies incorporating peritoneal cytology at the time of rupture could help clarify this issue.

Conclusions

The addition of TKIs and PD-1 inhibitors may reduce the risk of PM compared with embolization alone in patients with ruptured HCC. Additionally, HBV-related HCC and BCLC stage C were identified as the independent factors for PM.

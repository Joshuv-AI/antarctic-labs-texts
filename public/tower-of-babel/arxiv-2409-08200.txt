Submodular functions, generalized permutahedra, conforming preorders, and cointeracting bialgebras
Gunnar Fløystad, Dominique Manchon

Abstract
. Submodular functions z z defined on the power set of a finite set are in bijection with generalized permutahedra Π ⁡ ( z ) {\Pi}(z) . To any such z z we define a class of preorders, conforming preorders. We show the faces of Π ⁡ ( z ) {\Pi}(z) and the conforming preorders are in bijection. We investigate in detail this interplay between submodular functions and generalized permutahedra on one side, and conforming preorders on the other side, with many examples. In particular, the face poset structure of Π ⁡ ( z ) {\Pi}(z) correspond to two order relations ⊲ \lhd and ◀ \blacktriangleleft on preorders, and we investigate their properties. Ardila and Aguiar [ 1 ] introduced a Hopf monoid of submodular functions/generalized permutahedra. We show there is a bimonoid of modular functions cointeracting in a non-standard way. By recent theory of L.Foissy [ 16 ] , on double bialgebras we get a canonical polynomial associated to any submodular function.

1.
Introduction

Generalized permutahedra is a central class of polyhedra with rich
properties and surprisingly many connections to various
areas of mathematics. They are equivalent to submodular functions, an
important class in optimization and economics.

A. Postnikov’s article
[
26
]
with simple and systematic
presentation, example classes, and enumerative focus,
has had great impact. But there is a long history before that.
Edmonds
[
10
]
introduces polymatroids which is a wide class
of generalized permutahedra, generalizing matroids and matroid polytopes.
S. Fujishige’s book
[
17
]
, whose first edition appeared in 1990,
develops in Section 3 all
main results on the interplay of submodular functions and generalized
permutahedra. Its aim is the application in optimization.
In recent years F. Ardila and M. Aguiar
[
1
]
endow generalized
permutahedra or equivalently submodular functions with the structure of a
Hopf algebra and develop cancellation free formulas for the antipode
of classes of Hopf algebras. The braid fan of permutahedra
has been studied in
[
25
]
and
[
23
]
.
Its maximal cones are in fact the Weyl
chambers associated to the general linear group.

This article has four central actors:

•

Finite preorders/topologies,

•

Submodular functions,

•

(Extended) generalized permutahedra,

•

Hopf- and bialgebras.

We undertake a systematic study of their interplay.
We recall and discuss central insights from the literature and develop
further the understanding of these. In particular we focus on the connection
between finite preorders/topologies on one side, and submodular functions and
extended generalized permutahedra on the other side.
We use this to construct new bialgebras and
comodule bialgebras of submodular and modular functions. By recent
theory of L.Foissy
[
16
]
, to such a cointeraction
of bialgebras is associated a unique morphism
to the polynomial double bialgebra
{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
.
So to each submodular function we get an associated polynomial, which we describe together with its enumerative properties.

By the Alexandrov correspondence, finite preorders and finite topologies
are equivalent objects. (The review article
[
13
]

shows their ubiquity.)
The interplay between generalized permutahedra and preorders
has been developed in various sources.
In
[
17
]
this interplay is given in terms
of distributive lattices rather than preorders. The braid fan is
studied in
[
25
]
and

[
23
]
. The normal fan of a generalized permutahedron
is a coarsening of this fan.
Braid cones
of the braid fan in

{\mathbb{R}}^{I}
corresponds precisely to preorders on the set
I
.

Our starting point here is submodular functions. Let
I
be a finite set and

{\mathcal{P}}(I)
the power set of all subsets of
I
. Further denote
\hat{\mathbb{R}}={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
. A function

z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow\hat{\mathbb{R}},\quad z(\emptyset)=0,\,z(I)<\infty

is
submodular
if

(1.1)

z(A\cup B)+z(A\cap B)\leq z(A)+z(B),\quad\text{ for all }A,B\subseteq I.

To a submodular function we algebraically associate a class of

conforming
preorders/topologies.
The
generalized permutahedron

{\Pi}(z)
associated to
z
is defined by the
inequalities in
{\mathbb{R}}^{I}
:

\sum_{a\in A}x_{a}\leq z(A)\quad\text{ for each }A\subseteq I,

together with the equality
\sum_{a\in I}x_{a}=z(I)
.
We give a natural Galois correspondence, Section
8
, between
families of subsets of
I
and faces of
{\Pi}(z)
which induces
a bijection

\text{ finite topologies conforming to }z\,\,\overset{1-1}{\leftrightarrow}\,\,\text{ faces of }{\Pi}(z).

This is a full generalization to any extended generalized permutahedron
of the notion of
{\mathcal{B}}
-forests for nestohedra.

The face poset structure of
{\Pi}(z)
can then be understood in
terms of an order relation on conforming preorders.
This order relation is well-known, but presented in various distinct
ways in the literature. Its earliest instance is likely in
[
27
]
,
concerning the face structure of the order polytope.
In
[
25
, Section 3]
it occurs as
contraction
of preorders, and
in
[
11
]
as
admissible
topologies. In Section
6
we
systematically study this order relation by a natural Galois correspondence
and unify and develop the various descriptions in the literature. In
fact there are two equivalent order relations on preorders, which
we denote by
\lhd
and
\blacktriangleleft
, where
\lhd
is found in
[
27
,
11
]

and
\blacktriangleleft
in
[
25
]
.

To a submodular function
z
and conforming preorder
P
, we associate
in Sections
8
and
10
two new submodular functions:

•

z_{P}

corresponding to

.

at the face

with the affine space of

A submodular function is

if the inequalities
(

By
[

]
the submodular functions have a Hopf algebra structure.
By using

2.
Preorders, topologies and submodular functions

We introduce two of the main characters of this article:
(finite) preorders/topologies, and submodular functions.
Finite preorders and finite topologies are equivalent objects by
the Alexandroff correspondence (see
[
13
]
for this and a historical
discussion).
Submodular functions occur in
combinatorics, optimization, and economics, where many phenomena
have descriptions by them. Submodular
functions which are allowed to take the value
\infty

give especially an interplay with preorders which we take a first
look at in this section.
(And is developed much further in Section
7
.)
The recent book
[
21
]
shows finite topologies as a rich subject.

2.1.
Preorders and topologies

A preorder
P=(X,\leq)
on a set
X
is a relation which is:

Reflexive:

x\leq x
for
x\in X
,

Transitive:

x\leq y
and
y\leq z
implies
x\leq z
.

The preorder is a
partial order
if it is also:

Anti-symmetric:

x\leq y
and
y\leq x
implies
x=y
.

Given a preorder
P=(X,\leq)
, we have an
opposite preorder

P^{{\mathrm{op}}}=(X,\leq^{\mathrm{op}})
given by
x\leq^{\mathrm{op}}y
if
y\leq x
.
A subset
D\subseteq X
is a
down-set
for
P
if whenever
y\in D
and

x\leq y
, then
x\in D
. If
x\in X
we write
\downarrow\!x

for the down-set
\{y\in X\,|\,y\leq x\}
.
We have corresponding notions of
up-set
and
\uparrow\!\!x
.
We write
\leq_{P}
for
\leq
when we need to be explicit about
which preorder we refer to.
The set of preorders on
X
is denoted
{\mathrm{Pre}}(X)
and is itself partially
ordered by
P\preceq Q
if
x\leq_{P}y
implies
x\leq_{Q}y
for
every
x,y\in X
.

Given
P=(X,\leq)
, two
x,y\in X
are
comparable
for
P

if
x\leq y
or
y\leq x
. If only one of them holds, say
x\leq y
,
then
y
is
strictly
bigger than
x
.
A preorder is
total
if any two elements are comparable. It is

linear
if for any two elements one of them
is strictly bigger than the other.

Recall that a topology on the set
X
is a family
{\mathcal{T}}
of subsets of

X
such that:

•

\emptyset
and
X
are both in
{\mathcal{T}}
,

•

If
{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
, then
\cup_{A\in{\mathcal{U}}}A
is an element of
{\mathcal{T}}
,

•

If
A,B\in{\mathcal{T}}
then
A\cap B
is in
{\mathcal{T}}
.

The elements of
{\mathcal{T}}
are the
open
subsets of
X
.
Denote the set of topologies on
X
by
{\mathrm{Top}}(X)
. This set is
partially ordered by inclusion. It has a minimal element,
the
coarse
topology with only
\emptyset
and
X
as open sets.

As is well-known, and explained below,
for a
finite
set
X
there are inverse bijections of partially
ordered sets,
the Alexandroff correspondence:

{\mathrm{Pre}}(X)\,\overset{{\mathrm{topl}}}{\underset{{\mathrm{preo}}}{\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\smash{\leftarrow}$\cr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\relbar$\crcr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\rightarrow$\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\vrule width=0.0pt,height=1.50694pt\relbar$\cr}}}}}}\,{\mathrm{Top}}(X)^{{\mathrm{op}}}.

For a preorder
P
, the set of all down-sets of
P
form a topology

{\mathrm{topl}}(P)
. Conversely, given a topology
{\mathcal{T}}
,
define the preorder
{\mathrm{preo}}({\mathcal{T}})
by
x\leq y

if for every open subset
U
, if
y\in U
, then
x\in U
.

We shall interchangeably use the terminologies of preorder/down-set and
topology/open set on

X
. In some situations the preorder notion is more natural, and
in other situations the topology notion is more natural.

Definition 2.1
.

A preorder
P
gives rise to two equivalence relations.
First let
x\sim^{b}_{P}y
if
x\leq_{P}y
and
y\leq_{P}x
.
Its equivalence
classes are the
bubbles
of
P
. We denote the set of
bubbles of
P
by
\mathbf{b}(P)
.

Secondly let
x\sim^{c}_{P}y
if
x
and
y
are in the same connected
component of
P
. So there is a sequence

x=x_{0}\leq_{P}x_{1}\geq_{P}x_{2}\leq_{P}\cdots\geq_{P}x_{2k}=y.

We denote by
\mathbf{c}(P)
the set of connected components of
P
.

If
{\mathbf{c}}(P)={\mathbf{b}}(P)
,
this is a
totally disconnected
preorder. This is the same
as an equivalence relation.
The corresponding topology is called a totally disconnected topology.
It is a disconnected union of coarse topologies.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
P
be a preorder and
L
a total preorder (i.e. every pair of elements
is comparable). Then
L
is
a
linear extension
of
P
if:

•

P\preceq L
,

•

The sets of bubbles
\mathbf{b}(P)
and
\mathbf{b}(L)
coincide.

2.2.
Submodular functions

Denote
\hat{\mathbb{R}}={\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
. For
I
a finite set, let

{\mathcal{P}}(I)
be the power set, i.e. the set of all subsets of
I
. An

extended Boolean function

on
I
is an arbitrary function
z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\to\hat{\mathbb{R}}
such
that
z(\emptyset)=0
and
z(I)<\infty
. Write
{\rm BF}\,[I]
for the set of extended Boolean functions
on
I
. For
S\subseteq I
with
z(S)<\infty
,
the
restriction
of
z
to
S
is

z\mathstrut_{|\,\scriptstyle{S}}:{\mathcal{P}}(S)\to\hat{\mathbb{R}},\,\text{ given by }z\mathstrut_{|\,\scriptstyle{S}}(U)=z(U),\quad U\subseteq S.

The
corestriction
of
z
is

z_{/S}:{\mathcal{P}}(I\backslash S)\to\hat{\mathbb{R}},\,\text{ given by }z_{/S}(U)=z(S\cup U)-z(S),\quad U\subseteq I\backslash S.

If
S\subseteq T\subseteq I
let
z_{T/S}
be
(z\mathstrut_{|\,\scriptstyle{T}})_{/S}
.

Let
u\in{\rm BF}\,[S]
and
v\in{\rm BF}\,[T]
and
I=S\sqcup T
the disjoint union.
Their product
u\cdot v\in{\rm BF}\,[I]
is the function given by

u\cdot v(E)=u(E\cap S)+v(E\cap T),\quad E\subseteq I.

An extended Boolean function
z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow\hat{\mathbb{R}}

is
decomposable
if it can be written as the product
of two Boolean functions on non-empty subsets.

Lemma 2.3
.

Any extended Boolean function
z\in{\rm BF}\,[I]
is uniquely a product of indecomposable extended
Boolean functions.

Proof.

Let
\{I_{a}\}_{a\in A}
and
\{I_{b}\}_{b\in B}
be two partitions of

I
into non-empty sets.
Let
z_{a}=z\mathstrut_{|\,\scriptstyle{I_{a}}}
and similarly define
z_{b}
. Suppose

z
can be written as products

z=\prod_{a\in A}z_{a}=\prod_{b\in B}z_{b}

of indecomposables. We claim that each
I_{a}\subseteq I_{b}

for some
b
. So fix
a
. Let
z_{ab}=z\mathstrut_{|\,\scriptstyle{I_{a}\cap I_{b}}}
.
For
S\subseteq I
let
S_{b}=S\cap I_{b}
. Now:

z(S)=\sum_{b\in B}z(S_{b})=\sum_{b\in B}z_{b}(S_{b}).

Then for any
S\subseteq I_{a}
, we have
S_{b}\subseteq I_{b}\cap I_{a}
, and we get:

z_{a}(S)=\sum_{b\in B}z_{ab}(S_{b}).

But since
z_{a}
is indecomposable, the sum can involve at most
one non-trivial
z_{ab}
.
and so at most one
I_{a}\cap I_{b}
is non-empty.
Thus
I_{a}\subseteq I_{b}
for some
b
.
Conversely, each
I_{b}\subseteq I_{a}
for some
a
, and so the partitions are equal.
∎

The function
z\in{\rm BF}\,[I]
is
submodular
if for
S,T\subseteq I
:

(2.1)

z(S)+z(T)\geq z(S\cup T)+z(S\cap T).

Write
{\rm SM}\,[I]
for the set of submodular functions on
I
.
An equivalent description is that a Boolean function is submodular if and
only if
given
I\supseteq B\supseteq A
and varying

S\subseteq I\backslash B
, then:

z(B\cup S)-z(A\cup S)

is a weakly decreasing function as
S
increases for the inclusion relation
(with the convention that
\infty-\infty
is any value).
The following will be useful.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow\hat{\mathbb{R}}
be a submodular function, and

I\supseteq T\supseteq S
. Let

C=T\backslash S
, and
C=C_{1}\sqcup C_{2}

be a disjoint union of sets. Suppose
z(S),z(T)<\infty
. Then
we have a decomposition as a product:

(2.2)

\displaystyle z^{\prime}=z_{T/S}=z^{\prime}\mathstrut_{|\,\scriptstyle{C_{1}}}\cdot z^{\prime}\mathstrut_{|\,\scriptstyle{C_{2}}}

if and only if

(2.3)

\displaystyle z(T)+z(S)=z(S\cup C_{1}\cup C_{2})+z(S)=z(S\cup C_{1})+z(S\cup C_{2}).

Proof.

If (
2.2
) holds, then (
2.3
) holds.
Suppose conversely (
2.3
) holds. Note then in
particular that all values in that equation are finite.
Let
S_{1}\subseteq C_{1}
and
S_{2}\subseteq C_{2}
. We must show that

z^{\prime}(S_{1}\cup S_{2})=z^{\prime}(S_{1})+z^{\prime}(S_{2}),

equivalently

z(S\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2})-z(S)=z(S\cup S_{1})-z(S)+z(S\cup S_{2})-z(S)

which is again equivalent to

(2.4)

z(S\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2})+z(S)=z(S\cup S_{1})+z(S\cup S_{2}).

By submodularity

\displaystyle z(S\cup C_{1}\cup S_{2})+z(S\cup S_{1})

\displaystyle\leq z(S\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2})+z(S\cup C_{1})

\displaystyle z(S\cup S_{1}\cup C_{2})+z(S\cup S_{2})

\displaystyle\leq z(S\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2})+z(S\cup C_{2})

These imply that if
z(S\cup S_{1})
or
z(S\cup S_{2})
is
\infty
,
then
z(S\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2})=\infty
, and so settles (
2.4
)
in this case.

So assume
z(S\cup S_{1})
and
z(S\cup S_{2})
are finite.
Then (
2.4
) is equivalent to:

(2.5)

\displaystyle z(S\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2})-z(S\cup S_{1})=z(S\cup S_{2})-z(S).

Fix
S_{2}
. The above (
2.5
) is an equality when
S_{1}=\emptyset
. By submodularity

z(S\cup S_{1}\cup S_{2})-z(S\cup S_{1})

is weakly decreasing with increasing
S_{1}
.
If therefore

(2.6)

\displaystyle z(S\cup C_{1}\cup S_{2})-z(S\cup C_{1})=z(S\cup S_{2})-z(S),

we have equality in (
2.5
) for every
S_{1}\subseteq C_{1}
.
Now (
2.6
) can be written

(2.7)

\displaystyle z(S\cup C_{1}\cup S_{2})-z(S\cup S_{2})=z(S\cup C_{1})-z(S).

This equality holds if
S_{2}=\emptyset
. By assumption it also holds
if
S_{2}=C_{2}
. Hence by submodularity it holds for every
S_{2}\subseteq C_{2}

with
z(S\cup S_{2})
finite. This implies that (
2.6
)
and so (
2.5
) holds.
∎

2.3.
From submodular functions to
topologies and preorders

The following is a simple and significant observation.
Let
I
be
finite and given a submodular function
z
. Let

{\mathrm{top}}(z)=\{S\subseteq I\,|\,z(S)<\infty\}.

This is a topology on
I
. This is because it is closed under unions
and intersection due to the submodular inequality (
2.1
).
The topology
{\mathrm{top}}(z)
corresponds to a preorder
{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
on
I
,
and
z(S)
is finite if and only if
S
is a down-set for this preorder.
So we have maps:

{\rm SM}\,[I]\,\,\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathrm{top}}\\
\rightarrow\\
\rightarrow\\
{\mathrm{pre}}\end{subarray}\,\,\begin{cases}{\mathrm{Top}}(I)\\
{\mathrm{Pre}}(I)\end{cases}

which via the identification of
{\mathrm{Top}}(I)^{{\mathrm{op}}}
and
{\mathrm{Pre}}(I)
is
the same map. This map has a left adjoint
sending a preorder
P
to the submodular function

{\mathrm{low}}_{P}:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow\hat{{\mathbb{R}}},\quad\text{where }{\mathrm{low}}_{P}(S)=\begin{cases}0,&S\text{ a down-set of }P\\
\infty,&S\text{ not down-set of }P\end{cases}.

We see that submodular functions
z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow\{0,\infty\}
are
in bijection
with preorders on
I
,
[
1
, Section 13]
.

We can decompose
z
into indecomposable submodular functions

(2.8)

z=z_{1}\cdot z_{2}\cdots z_{r}

where
z_{i}
is defined on subsets
I_{i}\subseteq I
.
This gives a partition
I=I_{1}\sqcup I_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{r}
.
Taking the disconnected union of the coarse topologies on each
I_{i}
,
we get a totally disconnected topology
\mathrm{ctop}(z)
.
Denote the corresponding preorder as
\mathrm{cpre}(z)
.
It is the equivalence relation with the
I_{i}

2.4.

Let

We thus see that submodular functions give a common framework for

To preorders there is associated two cointeracting bialgebras,

[

]

[

]

But there has not yet appeared a corresponding

3.

To submodular functions is associated beautiful geometry.
By linear inequalities they define convex polyhedra, and if

with respect to dimension, and we describe how the inequalities cut out faces.
Our take on this is related to preorders

The braid fan in
{\mathbb{R}}^{I}

I

The vector space

6.
Subdivisions and contractions of preorders

We consider two significant order relations
\lhd
and
\blacktriangleleft
between
preorders. They occur a number of places in the literature
and under different terminologies, see Subsection
6.2
.
They are fundamental order
relations for preorders, and we introduce them in a natural way from
a Galois correspondence
on the poset
{\mathrm{Pre}}(X)
. The order relation
\blacktriangleleft
is of
significance for us here, as it corresponds to the inclusion of faces of
EGP’s, Section
8
.

Recall that for any set
X
, the set
{\mathrm{Pre}}(X)
is partially ordered
by refinement of preorders. In fact, it is a lattice with meet

\wedge
and join
\vee
given by:

•

x\,(\leq_{1}\wedge\leq_{2})\,y
if and only if
x\leq_{1}y
and
x\leq_{2}y
,

•

x\,(\leq_{1}\vee\leq_{2})\,y
if and only if there exist

x_{1},\ldots,x_{2k-1}\in X
such that

x=x_{0}\leq_{1}x_{1}\leq_{2}x_{2}\leq_{1}\cdots\leq_{1}x_{2k-1}\leq_{2}x_{2k}=y.

The unique maximal element in
{\mathrm{Pre}}(X)

is the coarse preorder for which
x\leq y
for any
x,y\in X
, and the unique minimal element is the trivial (or discrete) preorder for which
x\leq y
implies
x=y
.

6.1.
A Galois correspondence on
{\mathrm{Pre}}(X)

Fix a preorder
P
on
X
, and let
\downarrow\!P

be the set of all preorders
R
on
X
such that
R\preceq P
,
and
\uparrow\!\!P
the set of preorders
Q
on
X
such that
P\preceq Q
.
Let us consider the two order preserving
maps

(6.1)

\downarrow\!P\,\,\overset{F_{P}}{\underset{G_{P}}{\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\smash{\leftarrow}$\cr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\relbar$\crcr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\rightarrow$\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\vrule width=0.0pt,height=1.50694pt\relbar$\cr}}}}}}\,\,\uparrow\!\!P\,,

given by

F_{P}(R):=P\vee R^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits},\quad G_{P}(Q):=P\wedge Q^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any three preorders
P,Q,R
on
X
such that
R\preceq P\preceq Q
, the following equivalence holds:

(6.2)

R\preceq P\wedge Q^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\Longleftrightarrow P\vee R^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\preceq Q.

In other words,

(6.3)

R\preceq G_{P}(Q)\Longleftrightarrow F_{P}(R)\preceq Q.

So (
6.1
) is a Galois correspondence
[
7
, Paragraph 7.24]

between posets.

Proof.

From
R\preceq P\wedge Q^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}
we get
R^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\preceq P^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\wedge Q
. Considering the join with
P
of both sides we get

P\vee R^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\preceq P\vee(P^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\wedge Q).

Since both factors on the right side are
\preceq Q
we obtain

P\vee R^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\preceq Q.

The reverse implication is proven similarly: from
P\vee R^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\preceq Q
we get
P^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\vee R\leq Q^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}
. Considering the meet with
P
of both sides we get

P\wedge(P^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\vee R)\preceq P\wedge Q^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}.

Since both factors to the left are
\succeq R
we obtain

R\preceq P\wedge Q^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}.

∎

Notations:

For
R
in
\mathrm{Im}\,G_{P}\subseteq\,\downarrow\!P

we write
R\lhd P
and say
R
is a
subdivision
of
P
. By the
properties of a Galois correspondence
[
7
, 7.27]
we have
R\lhd P
iff

R=G_{P}F_{P}(R)=P\wedge(P^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\vee R).

For
Q
in
\mathrm{Im}\,F_{P}\subseteq\,\uparrow\!\!P
we write
P\blacktriangleleft Q
and say

Q
is a
contraction
of
P
. Again by the
properties of a Galois correspondence we have
P\blacktriangleleft Q
iff

Q=F_{P}G_{P}(Q)=P\vee(P^{\mathop{\hbox{\sevenrm op}}\nolimits}\wedge Q).

We denote

\underline{P}=\mathrm{Im}\,G_{P}=\{R\in\mathop{\hbox{\rm Pre}}\nolimits(X),\,R\lhd P\},\hskip 34.1433pt\overline{P}=\mathrm{Im}\,F_{P}=\{Q\in\mathop{\hbox{\rm Pre}}\nolimits(X),\,P\blacktriangleleft Q\}.

Corollary 6.2
.

The maps
F_{P}
and
G_{P}
restrict to inverse order preserving bijections

(6.4)

\underline{P}\,\,\overset{F_{P}}{\underset{G_{P}}{\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\smash{\leftarrow}$\cr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\relbar$\crcr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\rightarrow$\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\vrule width=0.0pt,height=1.50694pt\relbar$\cr}}}}}}\,\,\overline{P},

where
R\lhd P
and
P\blacktriangleleft Q
correspond if both the following hold:

Q=F_{P}(R)=P\vee R^{\mathrm{op}},\quad R=G_{P}(Q)=P\wedge Q^{{\mathrm{op}}}.

Proof.

It is a standard fact for Galois correspondences between posets, that
the maps
F_{P}
and
G_{P}
give inverse bijections when we restrict
each of them to the image of the other.
∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Given preorders

on

a.

b.

Assume a.

in any

Conversely assume b.
So the bubbles of

For any loop of the form (

) (note that

and for any

. Hence

. The loop (

.

Conforming preorders of submodular functions

This section “marries” submodular functions and preorders.

Definition 7.1
.

In

Proposition 7.4
.

Suppose the preorder

is compatible with the submodular function
z
.
If

are down-sets of

and similarly

, such that

So this depends only on

Proof.

Let

. Then

is also a down-set
in

. Write

We claim that

is the disjoint union of the two

and

Since

For

Then

which gives:

z(E\cup S\cup X)-z(E\cup X)=z(E\cup S)-z(E).

As

∎

8.
Bijection between faces and conforming preorders

For a given submodular function
z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow\hat{\mathbb{R}}

we give a Galois correspondence between topologies/preorders on the
set
I
and faces of the extended generalized permutahedron

{\Pi}(z)
. This Galois correspondence
gives a bijection between the topologies/preorders conforming to
z
and
the non-empty faces of
{\Pi}(z)
. Every face of
{\Pi}(z)
is also
an EGP and we give a simple description of its submodular function.
We do not claim strong originality of the main result in this section,
Theorem
8.4
, on the bijection of conforming preorders
and faces of
{\Pi}(z)
, see the discussion before the theorem.
But the notion of conforming preorders is new and so is the presentation
and development of the theory in terms of these.

For a point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}I
, recall the notation
x_{A}=\sum_{i\in A}x_{i}

for a subset
A\subseteq I
.
By Section
3
the extended generalized permutahedron of
a submodular function
z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow\hat{\mathbb{R}}
is

{\Pi}(z):=\left\{x=(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in{\mathbb{R}}I\,|\,x_{I}=z(I)\hbox{ and }x_{A}\leq z(A)\hbox{ for any }A\subseteq I\hbox{ such that }z(A)<\infty.\right\}

Denote by
\Gamma(z)
the set of faces of
{\Pi}(z)
.

8.1.
A Galois correspondence between conforming
preorders and faces of
{\Pi}(z)

To a subset
{\mathcal{S}}
of the power set of
{\mathcal{P}}(I)
, i.e. a family
of subsets of
{\mathcal{P}}(I)
, we may associate a face of
{\Pi}(z)
,
an element of
\Gamma(z)
:

(8.1)

\Phi_{z}(\mathcal{S}):=\left\{x\in{\Pi}(z)\,|\,x_{A}=z(A)\hbox{ for any }A\in\mathcal{S}\right\}.

The map
\Phi_{z}
is surjective on
\Gamma(z)
since
non-empty faces of
{\Pi}(z)
are defined by making some of
the defining inequalities into equalities.
Conversely given a face
G
of
{\Pi}(z)
, we get a subset of
{\mathcal{P}}(I)
:

\Psi_{z}(G)=\{A\,|\,z(A)<\infty,\,x_{A}=z(A)\text{ for every point }x\in G\}.

It is readily seen that

(8.2)

\Psi_{z}(G)\supseteq{\mathcal{S}}\Leftrightarrow G\subseteq\Phi_{z}({\mathcal{S}}).

Letting
{\mathcal{PP}}(I)
be the power set of
{\mathcal{P}}(I)
, i.e. families of subsets
of

,
this means we have a Galois correspondence

{\mathcal{PP}}(I)^{{\mathrm{op}}}\,\,\overset{\Phi_{z}}{\underset{\Psi_{z}}{\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\smash{\leftarrow}$\cr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\relbar$\crcr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\rightarrow$\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\vrule width=0.0pt,height=1.50694pt\relbar$\cr}}}}}}\,\,\Gamma(z).

\Phi_{z}(P)

{\Pi}(z_{P})
. As
z_{P}

is submodular, this also implies that
\Phi_{z}(P)
is non-empty by
Lemma
3.4
.
For
S\subseteq I
and
x
a point in
{\mathbb{R}}I
,
recall the notation
x_{S}=\sum_{i\in S}x_{i}
.
The face
\Phi_{z}(P)
is defined by the following equalities and
inequalities:

•

x_{A}=z(A)
for
A
a down-set in
P
,

•

x_{S}\leq z(S)
for
S
a subset of
I
.

The extended generalized permutahedron
{\Pi}(z_{P})
is defined
by

•

For each bubble
C
of
P
,
x_{C}=z_{C}(C)
,

•

For
S\subseteq C
,
x_{S}\leq z_{C}(S)
.

If
C
is a bubble we may write
C=B\backslash A
for down-sets

A\subseteq B
of
P
. Suppose first that the point
x
belongs to
\Phi_{z}(P)
.
We have
x_{B}=z(B)
and

x_{A}=z(A)
, so:

x_{C}=x_{B}-x_{A}=z(B)-z(A)=z_{C}(C).

Furthermore for
S\subseteq C

we have
x_{A\cup S}\leq z(A\cup S)
and so:

x_{S}=x_{A\cup S}-x_{A}\leq z(A\cup S)-z(A)=z_{C}(S),

whence
x\in{\Pi}(z_{P})
.

Suppose conversely
x\in{\Pi}(z_{P})
. Given a down-set
B
of
P
,
we may find a filtration of down-sets:

(8.5)

B=B_{0}\supset B_{1}\supset\cdots\supset B_{r}=\emptyset

such that each
C_{i}=B_{i}\backslash B_{i+1}
is a bubble.
Then

z(B)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\big((z(B_{i})-z(B_{i+1})\big)=\sum_{i}z_{C_{i}}(C_{i})=\sum_{i}x_{C_{i}}=x_{B}.

Now given any subset
S\subseteq I
. We may take
B=I
in the filtration
above, and let
S^{i}=S\cap B_{i}
, and

S_{i}=S^{i}\backslash S^{i+1}=S\cap C_{i}.

By submodularity

z(S^{i})-z(S^{i+1})=z(S_{i}\cup S^{i+1})-z(S^{i+1})\geq z(S_{i}\cup B_{i+1})-z(B_{i+1})=z_{C_{i}}(S_{i}).

We then get

z(S)=\sum_{i\geq 0}z(S_{i})-z(S_{i+1})\geq\sum_{i\geq 0}z_{C_{i}}(S^{i})\geq\sum_{i\geq 0}x_{S_{i}}=x_{S}.

Whence
x\in\Phi_{z}(P)
.

If
P
conforms to
z
, then for each bubble
C
of
P
, the submodular function
z_{C}
is
indecomposable. Whence the dimension of
\Pi(z_{C})
is
|C|-1
.
By Lemma
3.2
,
\Pi(z_{P})
is the product

\prod_{C\in\mathbf{b}(P)}\Pi(z_{C})
, and by Corollary

3.6
the dimension is
\sum_{C\in\mathbf{b}(P)}(|C|-1)=|I|-|\mathbf{b}(P)|
.
∎

The formula for dimension of a face is also given in

but is there formulated in terms of distributive lattices.

Bijections of faces, cones, and conforming preorders

Recall that
{\mathrm{Top}}(z)

Denote by

(resp.
{\mathrm{Pre}}^{cp}(z)

compatible with

the set of non-empty faces of

By Proposition
8.3
we
have a Galois correspondence

{\mathrm{Top}}^{cp}(z)^{\mathrm{op}}\,\,\overset{\Phi_{z}}{\underset{\Psi_{z}}{\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\smash{\leftarrow}$\cr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\relbar$\crcr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\rightarrow$\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\vrule width=0.0pt,height=1.50694pt\relbar$\cr}}}}}}\,\,\Gamma^{*}(z),\,\text{ or equivalently }\,{\mathrm{Pre}}^{cp}(z)\,\,\overset{\Phi_{z}}{\underset{\Psi_{z}}{\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\hfil\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\smash{\leftarrow}$\cr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\relbar$\cr\vrule width=0.0pt,height=2.15277pt$\scriptstyle\relbar$\crcr}}}}\joinrel\mathrel{\raise 3.22916pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\rightarrow$\hfil\cr$\scriptstyle\vrule width=0.0pt,height=1.50694pt\relbar$\cr}}}}}}\,\,\Gamma^{*}(z).

.

.

∎

Proposition 8.7
.

Let
z
be a submodular function.

a.
{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
has a unique maximal element, the
totally disconnected preorder

\mathrm{cpre}(z)
. Moreover there is equality between sets of connected components

\mathbf{c}(P)=\mathbf{c}\big(\mathrm{cpre}(z)\big)
for any
P\in{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
.

b. The meet of all preorders of
{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
is the preorder
{\mathrm{pre}}(z)

defined in Subsection
2.2
. Moreover

\mathbf{b}(P)=\mathbf{b}({\mathrm{pre}}(z))
for every minimal
P\in{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
.

c. In particular, if
z
is a finite submodular function
then every minimal
P
in
{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
is a poset.

Proof.

a. If
P
conforms to
z
, a decomposition
z=z_{1}\cdot z_{2}

corresponds to a decomposition

P=P_{1}\sqcup P_{2}
into a disconnected union of two preorders.
Furthermore
P_{1}
conforms to
z_{1}
, and similarly
P_{2}

to
z_{2}
. Iterating this argument, the decomposition
z=z_{1}\cdots z_{r}
into
indecomposables gives a decomposition
P_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_{r}
of
P

into indecomposable preorders. Thus
\mathbf{c}(P)=\mathbf{c}\big(\mathrm{ctop}(z)\big)
, which proves a.

b. Suppose
a
and
b
are not comparable in
{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
.
Let
L^{a}
be a linear extension of
{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
with
a>b
and

L^{b}
similarly with
b>a
. Then
\Psi_{z}\circ\Phi_{z}(L^{a})
and
and
\Psi_{z}\circ\Phi_{z}(L^{b})
conform to
z
. Whether
a,b
are comparable
or not in these preorders, in their meet
a
and
b
will not be
comparable.

If
a
and
b
are in the same bubble in
{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
, they are in
the same bubble in every preorder
\succeq{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
, and so
in the meet.

If
a<b
in
{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
, in any

conforming to

we have
a\leq_{P}b
.
But letting

be a linear extension of
{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
, it is compatible
with

and

conforms and is
\succeq{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
.

. We cannot have

as

, and

.

L
be a linear extension of
{\mathrm{pre}}(z)
. Then they have the same

is compatible with

.

all such

are equal and, by the above, are

.

8.4.
The normal fan

Proposition 8.8
.

Let
z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow\hat{{\mathbb{R}}}
be a submodular function.
If the face
F
of
\Pi(z)
corresponds to the preorder
P
,
the normal cone associated to
F
is
k(P)
.

Proof.

Given a direction
y\in k(P)
, we also have
y\in k(L)
for
some linear extension
L
of
P
. Let

C_{1}<C_{2}<\cdots<C_{r}

be the total ordering of the bubbles of
L
.
Then

y_{1}\geq y_{2}\geq\cdots\geq y_{r},

where

y_{i}
is the value of
y
at the coordinate positions of
C_{i}
.
The value of
y
on a point
x\in{\Pi}(z)
is

\sum_{i=1}^{r}y_{i}x_{C_{i}}\quad(\text{where }x_{C_{i}}=\sum_{j\in C_{i}}x_{j}).

Let
D_{i}=\downarrow_{\!L}\!C_{i}
be the down-set in
L
generated by
C_{i}
.
We may write the above expression as

y_{r}x_{D_{r}}+(y_{{r-1}}-y_{r})x_{D_{r-1}}+\cdots+(y_{1}-y_{2})x_{D_{1}}.

Each
D_{j}
is also a down-set of
P
. We have
x_{D_{i}}\leq z(D_{i})
and
on the face
F=\Phi_{z}(P)
we have
x_{D_{i}}=z(D_{i})
. This shows
that the above expression is maximal on the face
F
.

Now suppose
y
is not in
k(P)
. Define an equivalence relation on

I
by
i\sim j
if
y_{i}=y_{j}
. The equivalence classes may be
totally ordered according to the values of the
y_{i}
, so as
to become the bubbles of a total preorder
L
. Let
G=\Phi_{z}(L)
.
By the argument
above this is the face of
{\Pi}(z)
such that
y
attains its
maximum. If
F\subseteq G
, then

P=\Psi_{z}(F)\preceq\Psi_{z}(G)\preceq L.

But then
y\in k(L)\subseteq k(P)
, a contradiction. So
G

does not contain the face
F
, and
y
is not
maximal on
F
.
∎

8.5.
Finite submodular functions

Given a
finite
submodular function
z:{\mathcal{P}}(I)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
,
note that any total order on
I
is compatible with
z
.
For each minimal
P
in
{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
(note that this will be a poset),
let
L(P)
be the set of its linear extensions.

Proposition 8.9
.

For a finite-valued
z
,
if
P,Q
are distinct posets of
{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
,
the sets of linear extensions
L(P)
and
L(Q)
are disjoint.

Proof.

Let
L
be a total order with

P\preceq L
. Then

P=\Psi_{z}\circ\Phi_{z}(P)\preceq\Psi_{z}\circ\Phi_{z}(L)\preceq L.

But then
\Psi_{z}\circ\Phi_{z}(L)
is a poset (since
L
is), and so
is minimal in
{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
. Then
P=\Psi_{z}\circ\Phi_{z}(L)
and is
uniquely determined.
∎

Corollary 8.10
.

The union

\bigsqcup_{P\text{{\rm{ poset in }} }{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)}L(P)

is a partition of the set of total orders on
I
.

Remark 8.11
.

The set of minimal elements of
{\mathrm{Pre}}(z)
then is a complete fan
of posets, as defined in
[
25
]
. In
[
23
]
such
partitions of linear extensions or equivalently of permutations,
are called convex rank tests. In particular
[
23
, Sec.6]
counts
such linear extensions.

Remark 8.12
.

The composition

\displaystyle\Psi_{z}\circ\Phi_{z}:\mathcal{P}\big(\mathcal{P}(I)\big)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\big(\mathcal{P}(I)\big)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{S}}

is a closure operation giving a topology

\overline{\mathcal{S}}
on
\mathcal{P}(I)
.
This topology depends however on
z
, and is in general finer than the
ordinary closure topology generated by all unions of all finite intersections
of elements in
{\mathcal{S}}
.

9.
Examples

We consider three classes of submodular functions and describe the preorders
which conform to these functions. These classes are i) the functions

{\mathrm{low}}_{P}
associated to preorders, ii) submodular functions of matroids,
and iii) those of building sets. In the latter case it is well known

[
1
, Section 22]
and
[
12
]
that the faces of the associated
generalized permutahedron, the nestohedron, are in bijection to
{\mathcal{B}}
-forests.
We show these are precisely the conforming preorders.

9.1.
Preorders

Recall from Paragraph
2.2
that any submodular function
z
on a finite set
I
gives rise to a topology
{\mathrm{top}}(z)
for which the open sets are the subsets
A\subseteq I
such that
z(A)<\infty
. The restriction of this map to
\{0,\infty\}
-valued submodular functions is a bijection
[
1
, Theorem 15.9]
. Identifying the topologies on
I
with preorders via the Alexandroff correspondence, the inverse map is given by
P\mapsto\mathop{\hbox{\rm low}}\nolimits_{P}
, with

\mathop{\hbox{\rm low}}\nolimits_{P}(A)=0\hbox{ if }A\hbox{ is a downset for }P,\hbox{ and }\hbox{low}_{P}(A)=\infty\hbox{ if not}.

The extended generalized permutahedron associated to
\mathop{\hbox{\rm low}}\nolimits_{P}
is
the
preorder cone
of
P
(terminology from
[
1
, Sec.15]
),
given by

{\Pi}({\mathrm{low}}_{P})=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{I},\,\,\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}=0\hbox{ and }\sum_{i\in A}x_{i}\leq 0\hbox{ for any $P$-downset }A\right\}.

The associated normal fan of
{\Pi}({\mathrm{low}}_{P})
is the cone
k(P)
(and its faces)
of Proposition
5.1
, see Proposition
10.3
.
The conforming preorders of
{\mathrm{low}}_{P}
, which is
\overline{P}
,
correspond to the faces of
k(P)
.

9.2.
Matroids

Let
M
be a matroid on the finite set
I
,
[
24
]
. The rank function

z_{M}:\mathcal{P}(I)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, which associates to any
A\subset I
the maximal cardinality of any independent set contained in
A
, is submodular. The
set of vertices of the corresponding generalized permutahedron is in bijection with the
set of bases of the matroid. For a basis
B\subseteq I
, the corresponding vertex
is
e_{B}=\sum_{b\in B}e_{b}
, where
e_{b}
is the
b
’th coordinate vector.

Definition 9.1
.

A
circuit
of the matroid
M
is a minimal subset
C\subseteq I
such that
z_{M}(C)<|C|
.

In particular, a circuit is never independent.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
B
be a basis of
M
, and let
c\in I\setminus B
. There is a unique circuit
C\subseteq B\sqcup\{c\}
, which necessarily contains
c
.

Proof.

If there are two distinct circuits
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq B\cup\{c\}
, then
none of them are contained in
B
. Thus
c\in C_{1}\cap C_{2}
. By
the axioms for circuits
[
24
, 1.1.3]
, there is then a circuit in

(C_{1}\cup C_{2})\backslash\{c\}
. But such a circuit would be contained
in
B
, which is impossible.
∎

Let us denote by
P_{B}
be the conforming poset corresponding to the
vertex
e_{B}
, namely

P_{B}=\Psi_{z_{M}}(\{e_{B}\}).

Let
L
be the set of loops of the matroid (the elements of
I
of rank zero).
Here is a complete description of the poset
P_{B}
:

Proposition 9.3
.

Given a matroid
M
whose submodular function is

z_{M}
, and let
B
be a basis for
M
.

a.

The set of minimal elements of
P_{B}

is
B\cup L
.

b.

If
c\in I\backslash B
, let
C\subseteq B\cup\{c\}
be
the unique circuit given by Lemma
9.2
. Then
c\geq d
in
P_{B}

if and only if
d\in C
.

c.

The poset
P_{B}
has rank one or zero.

d.

The preorders
Q
with
P_{B}\blacktriangleleft Q
correspond
precisely to the faces of the matroid polytope which contain
the vertex
e_{B}
.

e.

If
Q
is any preorder conforming to
z_{M}
,
then
Q
has rank one or zero.

Proof.

We fix a basis
B
and write
x=e_{B}
, a
vertex of the matroid polytope. It corresponds to a conforming
poset
P_{B}
for the submodular function
z_{M}
.

a.
Let
a
be a minimal element of
P_{B}
. This holds if and only if
\{a\}
is
a down-set of
P_{B}
, or if and only if
z_{M}(\{a\})=x_{a}
. If
a
is a loop, both of these
are zero. If
a
is an element of
B
, both are
1
, and if
a
is neither
a loop nor in
B
, then
z_{M}(\{a\})=1
and
x_{a}=0
, which proves
part a.

b. Let
c\in I\backslash B
.
The coordinate
x_{c}=0
, while
x_{b}=1
for
b\in C\backslash\{c\}
. Thus
\sum_{b\in C}x_{b}=|C|-1
and as
C

is a circuit, this is
z_{M}(C)
. So
C

is a down-set of
P
. Thus if
c\geq d

in
P_{B}
then
d\in C
. On the other hand, suppose for some
d\in C
that
we do not have
d\leq c
. Since, from part a,
all elements in
C\backslash\{c\}

are minimal elements,
C\backslash\{d\}
would also
be a down-set for
P_{B}
. Hence we should have

z_{M}(C\backslash\{d\})=\sum_{b\in C\backslash\{d\}}x_{b}.

Since
C
is a circuit,
C\backslash\{d\}
is independent and the
left side is
|C|-1
.
But as
x_{c}=0
, it is seen that the right side
is
|C|-2
. Hence we cannot avoid having
d\leq c
for
d\in C
.

c. As all elements of
C\backslash\{c\}
are minimal, it is clear that
the height of
P_{B}
is one or zero (the latter if and only if any such
c
is minimal,
i.e.
c\in L
, and so
I=B\cup L
), which proves c.

Finally that
Q

For a preorder

10.1.

For the modular function

,
with

generates

z

Proposition 10.1
.

The association (

) above
is a bijection.
In particular if

is a poset (which is equivalent to
\mathbf{b}(P)=I
),
modular functions in
\rm{mod}[P]
are in bijection with
{\mathbb{R}}^{I}
. Modular functions may thus be given by pairs

where

is a preorder and
{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbf{b}(P)}
.

Proof.

Given a map

we show there is a uniquely defined
modular function
z:\widehat{P}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
which gives

by
the correspondence
above. Let
D
be a downset of
P
. We define inductively

by
the size of
D
. Clearly minimal
D

. If

is not of the form
\downarrow_{\!P}\!C
for a bubble

, we may

this determines

Expanding the first expression in (

\displaystyle z(A_{1}\cap A_{2})+z(A_{1}\cap B_{2})

\displaystyle-z(A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap B_{2})+z(B_{1}\cap A_{2})+z(B_{1}\cap B_{2})-z(B_{1}\cap B_{2}\cap A_{2})

Expanding the second expression of (

12.
Bimonoids in cointeraction

Let
{\rm\bf set^{\times}}
be the category of finite sets with bijections.
By
[
1
]
submodular functions give a species

{\rm SM}\,:{\rm\bf set^{\times}}\rightarrow{\rm\bf set}
.
Here we consider the linearized
species
{\rm SM}\,:{\rm\bf set^{\times}}\rightarrow\text{\bf vec}
, where the latter is the category
of vector spaces over the real numbers.
We work with the symmetric monoidal category of vector species
[
19
,
4
]
endowed with the Cauchy product, and with monoids, comonoids,
bimonoids, and Hopf monoids in that category. For these notions, see
[
2
]
.
{\rm SM}\,
comes with a product and a coproduct
\Delta

making it a Hopf monoid in species. It is equivalent to a Hopf
monoid
{\rm{EGP}}:{\rm\bf set^{\times}}\rightarrow{\text{\bf vec}}
of extended generalized permutahedra,
and a main result of
[
1
]
is to give a cancellation free
formula for the antipode.

The restriction of
{\rm SM}\,
to modular functions

{\rm{MOD}}:{\rm\bf set^{\times}}\rightarrow\text{\bf vec}
becomes a bimonoid in species
which can be endowed with a distinct
coproduct
\delta:{\rm{MOD}}\to{\rm{MOD}}\odot{\rm{MOD}}
where
\odot
stands for the Hadamard product
[
2
]
, making it a double twisted bialgebra in the sense of L. Foissy
[
15
]
. We show that
({\rm SM}\,,\cdot,\Delta)
is comodule bimonoid
over
({\rm{MOD}},\cdot,\delta)
, so
{\rm SM}\,
and
{\rm{MOD}}
are in
cointeraction
.
Moreover there is a natural morphism
\phi:{\rm SM}\,\rightarrow{\rm{MOD}}
of comodule
bialgebras.

12.1.
Hopf monoids in species of submodular and modular functions

Define a species
{\rm SM}\,:{\rm\bf set^{\times}}\to\text{\bf vec}
where
{\rm SM}\,[I]
is the vector
space with basis the
submodular functions
{\mathcal{P}}(I)\to\hat{\mathbb{R}}
. In particular note

then

is the one element set

, and

{\rm SM}\,[I]

{\mathbf{0}}
,
which sends
the empty set to
0
.

Let

to

{\mathbb{R}}
(the one-dimensional vector space), and any

to

.
The species

It becomes a comonoid in species by:

).

Let

13.2.
Double bialgebras and associated polynomials

We recall the notion of
double bialgebra
in

[
16
]
. These are cointeracting bialgebras, where the
underlying algebras are the same.

Let
k
be a field and
B
a
k
-algebra with product
\mu
and unit
\eta:k\rightarrow B
.
It is a
double bialgebra

if it has two structures as bialgebra:

B_{\Delta}=(B,\mu,\Delta,\eta,\epsilon_{\Delta}),\quad B_{\delta}=(B,\mu,\delta,\eta,\epsilon_{\delta}),

such that
B_{\Delta}
is a comodule bialgebra over
B_{\delta}
.
In more detail:

•

The comodule structure of
B_{\Delta}
over
B_{\delta}
:

\delta:B_{\Delta}\rightarrow B_{\delta}\otimes B_{\Delta}

is simply
\delta:B\rightarrow B\otimes B

•

The coproduct
\Delta:B\rightarrow B\otimes B
is a comodule
morphism:

(13.1)

({\bf 1}_{B}\otimes\Delta)\circ\delta=m_{13,2,4}\circ(\delta\circ\delta)\circ\Delta.

•

The counit
\epsilon_{\Delta}:B\rightarrow k
is a comodule morphism:

(13.2)

({\bf 1}_{B}\otimes\epsilon_{\Delta})\circ\delta=\eta_{B}\circ\epsilon_{\Delta}.

The basic example of this is the double bialgebra

(\mathbb{Q}[x],\mu,\Delta,\delta)
, where

\mu
is ordinary multiplication of polynomials, and

\Delta(x)=1\otimes x+x\otimes 1,\quad\delta(x)=x\otimes x.

By the theory of Foissy
[
16
]
when
B
is a double bialgebra
such that
(B,\mu,\Delta)
is a Hopf algebra,
there is a unique
double bialgebra morphism

\theta:(B,\mu,\Delta,\delta)\rightarrow(\mathbb{Q}[x],\mu,\Delta,\delta).

Thus every element of
B
gives a distinguished polynomial.

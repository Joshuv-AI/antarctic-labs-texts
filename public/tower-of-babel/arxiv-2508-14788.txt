An explicit construction of the Weyl module as a quotient of symmetric tensors by dual Garnir relations
Eoghan McDowell

Abstract
. The Weyl modules are the standard modules for the Schur algebra. Their duals (the costandard modules) have well-known constructions as quotients of exterior powers and as submodules of symmetric powers. This paper presents analogous constructions for the Weyl modules themselves, introducing relations that are a non-trivial dualisation of the Garnir relations. More generally, our constructions describe endofunctors on the category of representations of any group.

1.
Introduction

The
Weyl modules
are the
standard modules
for the Schur algebra; in particular, their heads form a complete irredundant set of simple modules in the category of polynomial representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k
is an infinite field.
It is well-known how to construct the
dual
of a Weyl module (a
costandard module
) as a quotient of an exterior power and as a submodule of a symmetric power (see for example [§4]green2006polynomial, [§8]fulton1997youngtableaux, [§2]boeck2021plethysms or [§2]mcdowell2021inverseSchur, or [Proposition 4.5.2]donkin1998qschur for the
q
-analogue).

Explicit constructions for the Weyl module itself, however, are lacking.
The purpose of this paper is to provide such constructions.
Comparisons with the existing literature are made in
Section

1.3
.

Both the existing and new constructions are functorial: they in fact describe, for each partition
\lambda
, a pair of endofunctors on the category of representations of any group over any unital commutative ring.
We therefore work in this generality throughout.
To obtain the Weyl and dual Weyl modules, apply the functors to the natural representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
.

1.1.
Results

We call the functors for constructing the dual Weyl modules the
Schur endofunctors
and denote them
\nabla^{\lambda}
.
(The Schur endofunctors are often simply called Schur functors; we use the term “endofunctor” to differentiate them from functors arising in Schur–Weyl duality, such as the functor
f
in [§6]green2006polynomial.)
We call the functors for constructing the Weyl modules the
Weyl endofunctors
and denote them
\Delta^{\lambda}
.

1.0
.

Let
G
be a group and let
R
be a unital commutative ring.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
.
The
Schur endofunctor

\nabla^{\lambda}
on the category of representations of
G
over
R
is defined as the quotient of an exterior power by Garnir relations, or equivalently as the subspace of a symmetric power consisting of polytabloids, as described in
Section

3
:

\nabla^{\lambda}({-})\cong\,{\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.96225pt}{$\displaystyle{\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}({-})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathsf{GR}^{\lambda}({-})}$}}{\raisebox{4.71225pt}{$\textstyle{\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}({-})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{\mathsf{GR}^{\lambda}({-})}$}}{\raisebox{3.62001pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}({-})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathsf{GR}^{\lambda}({-})}$}}{\raisebox{2.83333pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}({-})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{GR}^{\lambda}({-})}$}}}\,\cong\mathsf{polytab}({-}).

The
Weyl endofunctor

\Delta^{\lambda}
is defined by pre- and post-composing the Schur endofunctor with duality (as defined in
Section

2.5
):

\Delta^{\lambda}({-})=(\nabla^{\lambda}({-}^{\ast}))^{\ast}.

This definition of the Schur endofunctor matches the presentations of Fulton’s
Schur module

E^{\lambda}
[§8.1, Lemma 1, p. 107]fulton1997youngtableaux, and of Green’s dual Weyl module
D_{\lambda,K}
[§4.6]green2006polynomial.
The definition of the Weyl endofunctor as the dual of the Schur endofunctor then guarantees that it yields the Weyl module, but does not give an explicit model for it.

After recalling familiar multilinear algebra in
Section

2
, and the well-known construction of the Schur endofunctor in
Section

3
, we give our explicit constructions for the Weyl endofunctor in
Section

4
.
In particular, we define the following spaces:

•

\mathsf{copolytab}^{\lambda}(V)\subseteq\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}(V)
, the space of copolytabloids (defined in
Section

4.1
);

•

\Sym_{\lambda}(V)\subseteq V^{\otimes n}
, the space of row-symmetrised tensors (defined in
Section

2.3
);

•

\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)\subseteq\Sym_{\lambda}(V)
, the space of dual Garnir relations (defined in
Section

4.2
).

We establish in
Section

5
the following properties of these spaces.

.

Let
G
be a group, let

Let

1.2.
Relationship to Specht modules for the symmetric group

The well-known construction of the Specht modules due to James gdjames1978reptheorysymgroups can be obtained from the Schur endofunctors after an easy modification (restricting to subspaces spanned by elements labelled by tableaux with all entries distinct).
This is the setting in which the term
Garnir relations
(or
Garnir elements
) originates, following work of Garnir garnir1950theorie.

The modification to the Schur endofunctors in the case of the Specht modules makes duality much simpler: the dual of a Specht module can be constructed as a submodule of a
twisted Young permutation module
(analogous to an exterior power) in essentially the same way as the Specht module can be constructed as a submodule of a
Young permutation module
(analogous to a symmetric power) (see [§7.4]fulton1997youngtableaux).
Furthermore, as described in
Section

4.2
below, the dual Garnir relations introduced in this paper reduce to relations of essentially the same form as the usual Garnir relations. Thus the novelty of our dual Garnir relations is for Weyl modules and other general applications of Weyl endofunctors.

1.3.
Existing constructions of Weyl modules and Weyl endofunctors

Akin, Buchsbaum and Weyman present the Weyl endofunctor (which they call the
coSchur functor
) as a quotient of a divided power (isomorphic to the space of symmetric tensors) and as a submodule of an exterior power [Definition II.1.3]akin1982Schurfunctors: their definition is as the image of a map which is recursively defined by composing multiplications and comultiplications, and a description of the kernel occurs only implicitly (with unspecified integral coefficients) in the sketched proof of [Thoerem II.3.16]akin1982Schurfunctors.
By contrast, our map
\mathsf{\Lambda}\colon\Sym_{\lambda}V\to\mathsf{copolytab}^{\lambda}(V)
has a straightforward definition that is easy to compute, we explicitly describe its kernel as the space of dual Garnir relations (
Sections

4.2
and
5.2
), and the non-trivial dualisation of the usual “straightening algorithm” is presented in its entirety (
Section

5.2
).
We note that the dual Garnir relations are significantly harder to identify than the usual
Garnir relations
or
quadratic relations
in the quotient construct of the dual Weyl module.

Carter and Lusztig, and later Green, describe the Weyl module as the set of tensors satisfying certain conditions ([§3.2]carterlusztig1974modularreps, [(5.2b)]green2006polynomial) and give a basis in terms of the action of the Schur algebra on a distinguished element ([§3.3–3.5]carterlusztig1974modularreps, [(5.3b)]green2006polynomial).
Their conditions require the tensors to be antisymmetric, and so this construction is isomorphic to a subspace of an exterior power.
Our construction, meanwhile, describes the elements (including a basis) explicitly in terms of the natural basis of the exterior power.
(Carter and Lusztig introduced the name
Weyl module
; for further historial notes, see [p. 10]green2006polynomial.)

The treatments of Carter and Lusztig, and of Green, also show that the Weyl module is cyclic, generated by an element that can be interpreted as a highest weight vector.
In an unpublished note, Wildon Wildon2020WeylModule takes this as the defining property of the Weyl module, and shows that it has a basis consisting of the elements we call copolytabloids.
Our approach differs by avoiding the use of the group action (and avoiding the assumption that the field is infinite), and hence allowing us to construct the Weyl endofunctor for any group algebra.

Constructions of the Schur and Weyl endofunctors in terms of the letter place algebra were given by Kouwenhoven kouwenhoven1991SchurandWeyl.

This paper contains the novel constructions from Chapter 1 of the author’s doctoral thesis mcdowell2021thesis.

2.2.
Place permutation action on tableaux

Given a set
X
, we let
S_{X}
denote the group of permutations of
X
, with permutations written on the right of their arguments.
The group
S_{[\lambda]}
of permutations of the Young diagram acts on tableaux on the right by permuting the boxes via

(t\cdot\sigma)(b)=t(b\sigma^{-1})

for
\sigma\in S_{[\lambda]}
and
b\in[\lambda]
.

Define the sets of
row-preserving
and
column-preserving place permutations
, subgroups of
S_{[\lambda]}
, by

\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}S_{\mathrm{row}_{i}(\lambda)}\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad\mathrm{CPP}(\lambda)=\prod_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}S_{\mathrm{col}_{j}(\lambda)}.

Given a tableau
t
, let
\stab(t)\leqslant S_{[\lambda]}
denote the stabiliser of
t
, and let
\rowstab(t)=\stab(t)\cap\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)
denote the
row stabiliser
of
t
.

Given a group
S
and subgroups
I
and
J
, we write:

J\backslash
S/I
for the set of left cosets
\sigma I
of
I
in
S
;

I\backslash S
/J
for the set of right cosets
I\sigma
of
I
in
S
;

I\backslash S/J
for the set of double cosets
I\sigma J
of
I
on the left and
J
on the right in
S
.

Abusing notation, we denote sets of coset representatives in the same way.

2.3.
Symmetric powers, row tabloids and row symmetrisations

The
symmetric power
, denoted

2.5.

Orders on tableaux

We define two orders similar to the orders defined in [Definitions 3.10, 3.11 and 13.8]gdjames1978reptheorysymgroups, but defined on tableaux rather than tabloids, and moreover on tableaux with not-necessarily-distinct entries.
The column ordering appeared also in mcdowell2021inverseSchur.
We write

2.0
.

The

,

, is the strict partial order defined as follows on the set of tableaux of shape
\lambda
.
Consider tableaux
t
and
u

•

If there is equality

and

are

-incomparable, and we write

•

Otherwise, let

be maximal such that there exists

(where

denotes the multiset symmetric difference).
Let

If

The tableaux

and

The order is easier to interpret in the case of tableaux with all entries distinct:
to compare two such tableaux, identify the largest entry which does not appear in the same column in both tableaux, and declare the

-greater tableau to be the one for which this element is further left.

We make the identical definitions for the

and the symbols

,

and
\mathrel{\mathchoice{\hbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle{<_{\mathrm{r}}}$}\hskip-11.40282pt\raisebox{-5.3611pt}{\hbox{$\rotatebox{-25.0}{\scalebox{.8}{$\displaystyle\sim$}}$}}\hskip-39.44447pt\hskip 11.40282pt}}{\hbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{<_{\mathrm{r}}}$}\hskip-11.40282pt\raisebox{-5.3611pt}{\hbox{$\rotatebox{-25.0}{\scalebox{.8}{$\textstyle\sim$}}$}}\hskip-39.44447pt\hskip 11.40282pt}}{\hbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{<_{\mathrm{r}}}$}\hskip-9.45837pt\raisebox{-5.63612pt}{\hbox{$\rotatebox{-25.0}{\scalebox{.8}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}$}}\hskip-37.91669pt\hskip 9.45837pt}}{\hbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{<_{\mathrm{r}}}$}\hskip-8.62509pt\raisebox{-5.90277pt}{\hbox{$\rotatebox{-25.0}{\scalebox{.8}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sim$}}$}}\hskip-37.0834pt\hskip 8.62509pt}}}
, replacing all instances of “column” with “row”.
For two tableaux with all entries distinct, the

-greater tableau is the one in which the largest entry which does not appear in the same row in both tableaux appears further
up
the tableau.

3.0

labelled by

\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}_{(t,A,B)}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{T}/{\sim_{\mathrm{r}}}}\left\lvert\rowstab(u):\rowstab(u)\cap(S_{A\sqcup B}\times S_{[\lambda]\setminus A\sqcup B})\right\rvert\operatorname{rsym}(u).

of

In

\mathsf{\Lambda}\colon\Sym_{\lambda}V\to\mathsf{copolytab}^{\lambda}(V)

In the remainder of the present section, we make some observations about dual Garnir relations, including giving an example to demonstrate how to compute them.

A dual Garnir relation does not depend on the choice of equivalence class representatives: in the notation of the definition, if
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
are such that
u\sim_{\mathrm{r}}u^{\prime}
, then clearly
\operatorname{rsym}(u)=\operatorname{rsym}(u^{\prime})
; furthermore there exists
\sigma\in\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)\cap(S_{A\sqcup B}\times S_{[\lambda]\setminus A\sqcup B})
such that
u\sigma=u^{\prime}
and hence
\rowstab(u)=\sigma\rowstab(u^{\prime})\sigma^{-1}

\rowstab(u)\cap(S_{A\sqcup B}\times S_{[\lambda]\setminus A\sqcup B})

is unchanged if
u
is replaced with
u^{\prime}
.
The representatives must indeed be chosen from
\mathcal{T}

s\not\in\mathcal{T}

does not imply that the relevant indices are equal.

It is not immediately clear that the
R

\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)
is an
RG
-submodule.
We do not show this fact directly, but it follows from showing
\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)=\ker\mathsf{\Lambda}
.

A dual Garnir relation can also be expressed as a sum over double cosets.

\mathsf{\Lambda}
(

Let

be a set of double coset representatives for

\mathsf{copolytab}^{\lambda}(V)\cong\,{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\Sym_{\lambda}V}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\Sym_{\lambda}V}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\scriptstyle{\Sym_{\lambda}V}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\Sym_{\lambda}V}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.45833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)}$}}}\,.

Proof.

From
Section

5.1
, we have that
\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)\subseteq\ker\mathsf{\Lambda}
.
It therefore suffices to show that the dual snake relations span
\ker\mathsf{\Lambda}
.

Let
\kappa\in\ker\mathsf{\Lambda}
.
By
Section

5.2
there exists an
R
-linear combination
\gamma
of dual snake relations such that

\displaystyle\kappa+\gamma

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathrm{SSYT}(\lambda)}\alpha_{s}\operatorname{rsym}(s)

for some elements
\alpha_{s}\in R
.
Applying
\mathsf{\Lambda}
and using that
\mathsf{G}\hskip-1.0pt\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathsf{R}$}}^{\lambda}(V)\subseteq\ker\mathsf{\Lambda}
, we find

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathrm{SSYT}(\lambda)}\alpha_{s}\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathrm{e}$}}(s).

But the semistandard copolytabloids are
R

, so
\alpha_{s}=0
for all

Hence

is in the span of the dual snake relations.
∎

6.
Space of copolytabloids is dual to the Schur endofunctor

We prove the first isomorphism in
Theorem

1.2
, verifying that the space of copolytabloids indeed models the Weyl endofunctor.
A special case of the calculation in the proof below, for the tableau
t
defined by
t(i,j)=i
, was given by Wildon [§3.2]Wildon2020WeylModule.

6.1
.

There is an isomorphism

\Delta^{\lambda}V\cong\mathsf{copolytab}^{\lambda}(V).

Proof.

We view
\Delta^{\lambda}V=(\nabla^{\lambda}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
as a submodule of
(\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
via the dual of the surjective map
e\colon\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}V^{\ast}\to\nabla^{\lambda}V^{\ast}
; that is, by the injective map
e^{\ast}\colon(\nabla^{\lambda}V^{\ast})^{\ast}\to(\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
defined by
e^{\ast}(\theta)(x)=\theta(e(x))
for
\theta\in(\nabla^{\lambda}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
and
x\in\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}V^{\ast}
.
Meanwhile we view
(\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
as isomorphic to
\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}V
via the isomorphism
\psi
defined by
(v_{i_{1}}^{\ast}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{i_{r}}^{\ast})^{\ast}\mapsto v_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{i_{r}}
(see [Proposition 3.3]mcdowell2021thesis for the routine verification that this is an isomorphism).
Thus it suffices to show
\im(\psi e^{\ast})=\mathsf{copolytab}^{\lambda}(V)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}
denote the
R
-basis of
V^{\ast}
dual to
\mathcal{B}
, and, given a tableau
t
with entries in
\mathcal{B}
, let
t^{\ast}
denote the tableau obtained from
t
by replacing each entry from
\mathcal{B}
with its dual from
\mathcal{B}^{\ast}
.
Let
[t^{\ast}]^{\ast}
denote the element dual to
[t^{\ast}]
in the basis of
(\Sym^{\lambda}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
dual to
\{\,[s]\,\mid\,s\in\mathrm{RSSYT}_{\mathcal{B}^{\ast}}(\lambda)\,\}
.
Since
\nabla^{\lambda}V^{\ast}\subseteq\Sym^{\lambda}V^{\ast}
, we can restrict a function
[t^{\ast}]^{\ast}\in(\Sym^{\lambda}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
to
\nabla^{\lambda}V^{\ast}
, and moreover all elements of
(\nabla^{\lambda}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
can be obtained this way.
Thus
\Delta^{\lambda}V
is
R
-spanned by
\{\,[t^{\ast}]^{\ast}\,\mid\,t\in\mathrm{RSSYT}_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)\,\}
, and it suffices to show that
\psi e^{\ast}([t^{\ast}]^{\ast})\in\mathsf{copolytab}^{\lambda}(V)
for all
t\in\mathrm{RSSYT}_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)
.

Fix
t\in\mathrm{RSSYT}_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)
, and we will show that
\psi e^{\ast}([t^{\ast}]^{\ast})=\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathrm{e}$}}(t)
.
In what follows, given a statement
\mathsf{P}
,
we set
\mathbbm{1}\mkern-1.0mu[\,\mathsf{P}\,]=1
if
\mathsf{P}
is true and

\mathbbm{1}\mkern-1.0mu[\,\mathsf{P}\,]=0
if
\mathsf{P}
is false.
For any
u\in\mathrm{CSYT}_{\mathcal{B}^{\ast}}(\lambda)
we have

\displaystyle e^{\ast}([t^{\ast}]^{\ast})(|u|)

\displaystyle=[t^{\ast}]^{\ast}(e(u))

\displaystyle=[t^{\ast}]^{\ast}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{CPP}(\lambda)}\sign(\sigma)[u\cdot\sigma]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{CPP}(\lambda)}\sign(\sigma)\mathbbm{1}\mkern-1.0mu[\,[u\cdot\sigma]=[t^{\ast}]\,]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in\rowstab(t^{\ast})\backslash\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{CPP}(\lambda)}\sign(\sigma)\mathbbm{1}\mkern-1.0mu[\,t^{\ast}\cdot\tau=u\cdot\sigma\,]

where the last equality holds as there is at most one element
\tau\in\rowstab(t^{\ast})\backslash\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)
such that
t^{\ast}\cdot\tau=u\cdot\sigma
, and such an element exists if and only if
t^{\ast}
and
u\cdot\sigma
have the same multisets of entries in each row (that is, if and only if
[t^{\ast}]=[u\cdot\sigma]
).

We employ a similar argument to collapse the sum over
\mathrm{CPP}(\lambda)
.
Since
u
is column standard and hence has distinct entries within a column, there is at most one element
\sigma\in\mathrm{CPP}(\lambda)
such that
t^{\ast}\cdot\tau=u\cdot\sigma
, and such an element exists if and only if
t^{\ast}\cdot\tau
and
u
have the same multisets of entries in each column. Write
s^{\shortdownarrow}
for the unique column semistandard tableau obtained from a tableau
s
by sorting all the columns into ascending order;
thus
t^{\ast}\cdot\tau
and
u
have the same multisets of entries if and only if
(t^{\ast}\cdot\tau)^{\shortdownarrow}=u
.
Defining
\sign(s\mapsto s^{\shortdownarrow})
to be the sign of the unique column-preserving permutation which makes
s
into a column standard tableau (if it exists; defining
\sign(s\mapsto s^{\shortdownarrow})=0
if
s
does not have distinct column entries),
the expression above becomes

\displaystyle e^{\ast}([t^{\ast}]^{\ast})(|u|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in\rowstab(t^{\ast})\backslash\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)}\;\mathbbm{1}\mkern-1.0mu[\,(t^{\ast}\cdot\tau)^{\shortdownarrow}=u\,]\sign(t^{\ast}\cdot\tau\mapsto(t^{\ast}\cdot\tau)^{\shortdownarrow}).

Thus we have

\displaystyle e^{\ast}([t^{\ast}]^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in\rowstab(t^{\ast})\backslash\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)}\;|(t^{\ast}\cdot\tau)^{\shortdownarrow}|^{\ast}\sign(t^{\ast}\cdot\tau\mapsto(t^{\ast}\cdot\tau)^{\shortdownarrow}),

where for a column standard tableau
u
, we denote by
|u|^{\ast}
the element dual to
|u|
in the basis of
(\Wedge^{\lambda^{\prime}}V^{\ast})^{\ast}
dual to
\{\,|s|\,\mid\,s\in\mathrm{CSYT}_{\mathcal{B}^{\ast}}(\lambda)\,\}
.
Applying
\psi
we find that

\displaystyle\psi(e^{\ast}([t^{\ast}]^{\ast}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in\rowstab(t)\backslash\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)}\;|(t\cdot\tau)^{\shortdownarrow}|\sign(t\cdot\tau\mapsto(t\cdot\tau)^{\shortdownarrow})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tau\in\rowstab(t)\backslash\mathrm{RPP}(\lambda)}\;|t\cdot\tau|

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathrm{e}$}}(t).\qed

6.1
.

The map
\psi e^{\ast}
in
Theorem

6.1
does
not
send
\operatorname{e}(t^{\ast})^{\ast}
, the element dual to a polytabloid, to the copolytabloid
\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathrm{e}$}}(t)
.
Furthermore, it is
not
the case that the basis
\{\,\operatorname{\rotatebox[origin]{180.0}{$\mathrm{e}$}}(s)\,\mid\,s\in\mathrm{SSYT}_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)\,\}

\{\,\operatorname{e}(s)\,\mid\,s\in\mathrm{SSYT}_{\mathcal{B}^{\ast}}(\lambda)\,\}
.

The second isomorphism in
Theorem

1.2
follows from
Theorem

1.1

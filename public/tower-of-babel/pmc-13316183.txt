Traditional Chinese Manual Therapy (Tuina) relieves neuropathic pain by regulating neuroplasticity: A resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging study
Authors: Zhiwei Wu, Suhong Zhao, Guangxin Guo, Xin Zhou, Xinrong Li, Yunyi Li, Lingjun Kong, Qingguang Zhu, Min Fang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo determine how traditional Chinese manual therapy (Tuina) alleviates neuropathic pain (NPP) via central neuroplasticity. NPP was induced by chronic compression of the dorsal root ganglion (CCD) in male rats. From post-operative day 4 to day 28, Tuina (5 N, 2 Hz, 10 min/day) was applied at Weizhong acupoint (BL40). Behavioural tests (mechanical withdrawal threshold, MWT; paw withdrawal thermal latency, PWL) were performed on 2 and 1 day before CCD surgery and post-operative day 3, 7, 14, 21 and 28. Resting-state functional MRI was performed before CCD surgery and on postoperative days 7 and 28. CCD surgery induced persistent mechanical and thermal allodynia. Tuina reversed these deficits from day 14 onward. Compared with Sham group, the CCD group exhibited elevated regional homogeneity (ReHo) in the hippocampus, amygdala, brainstem and cerebellum. Tuina further increased ReHo in the amygdala, hippocampus, corpus callosum and temporal lobe between days 7 and 28. ReHo values in pain-related regions negatively correlated with MWT and PWL. Tuina may gradually relieve neuropathic pain by reshaping limbic and sensory-motor networks, offering a neuroplasticity-based rationale for its clinical use.Keywords: Brain plasticity, Neuropathic pain, Traditional Chinese manual therapy (Tuina), Functional magnetic resonance imaging, Regional homogeneity

Introduction

Neuropathic pain (NPP) represents an aberrant pain sensation resulting from injury to the nervous system (Colloca et al., 2017). The etiology may be a direct injury of the nerve, spinal cord, or brain, or it can be a result of underlying conditions such as diabetes, herpes zoster, radiation, and chemotherapy (Alles and Smith, 2018). The pain sensations experienced by patients are commonly described as firing, burning, stinging, stabbing, numbness, or throbbing (Finnerup, Kuner, and Jensen, 2021). These sensations are accompanied by reduced sensitivity in the affected area (Rosner et al., 2023). Epidemiological studies have reported substantial geographic variation in NPP prevalence. In the United States, NPP prevalence is approximately 10–20%, with the majority being middle-aged, elderly, and female (Van Hecke et al., 2014, Boehnke et al., 2016). In Europe, the prevalence of NPP ranges from 6.9% to 8.2% with a long course and inadequate treatment and has caused serious social and family economic burden (Bouhassira et al., 2008). A cross-sectional study in China shows that the prevalence of NPP in diabetes patients is as high as 67.6% (Wang et al., 2023). Beyond physical disorders, NPP can additionally be a risk factor for sleep diseases, anxiety, depression, and suicide, significantly impacting the overall quality of life (Petrosky et al., 2018). At present, the main NPP therapeutic strategies include pharmacotherapy, neurostimulation techniques, and stem cell therapy, in which pharmacotherapy is most commonly used (Cohen and Mao, 2014). Nevertheless, the precise molecular mechanisms involved in NPP remain incompletely elucidated, thereby contributing to the limited effectiveness of existing therapeutic interventions. In addition, several studies have demonstrated that drugs are not adequately effective and can have harmful effects (Finnerup et al., 2015, Finnerup et al., 2018, Meacham et al., 2017). Conversely, Traditional Chinese non-pharmacological therapies may provide pain relief with relatively few adverse effects.

Traditional chinese manual therapy (Tuina), a conventional Chinese technique of manual manipulation, has been employed for pain management in China for millennia. Clinical and preclinical studies suggest that Tuina can alleviate several types of pain, particularly spine-related pain. Tuina incorporates a variety of methods, such as kneading, rubbing, and pressing, to meticulously work on different body parts (Field, 2016). Tuina has been clinically elucidated to be effective in managing distinct pain types (Dabbour et al., 2018, Dhanani et al., 2011). A Chinese survey reported that 66.8% of those suffering from chronic pain opted for either Tuina or cupping as treatment methods (Yongjun et al., 2020). Increasing research highlights the significant impact of Tuina on pain management, especially for spinal-related discomfort (Dhanani et al., 2011, Dabbour et al., 2018). In China, Tuina is a prevalent approach for spinal diseases (Cheng et al., 2022), which can efficiently alleviate pain symptoms in patients having cervical spondylosis and lumbar disc herniation accompanied by nerve root compression (Cao et al., 2022). Tuina's analgesic mechanism primarily involves the suppression of peripheral inflammation through the regulation of the TLR4 pathway and miRNA, the modulation of ion channels (e.g., P2X3 and piezo channels), the inhibition of glial cell activation, and the adjustment of brain functional changes (Liu et al., 2022). Due to its effectiveness, economic efficiency, simplicity in application, and the absence of toxic side effects, Tuina has gained increasing recognition and use in medical practices (Field, 2016) and is poised to offer greater benefits to patients of pain as a viable alternative treatment.

As a non-invasive neuroimaging technique, functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) detects blood oxygen level-dependent signals and provides insight into pain-related brain activity. This approach provides a nuanced insight into the characteristics of pain, paving the way for the development of more efficient pain management therapies. Beyond merely measuring the amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (ALFF) in cerebral activity, fMRI offers a window into the local regional homogeneity (ReHo) of brain function. ReHo encapsulates the synchrony of neural operations within distinct cerebral areas, shedding light on the orchestration of neuronal dynamics in these zones. Crucially, ReHo analysis in select brain regions is vital for deciphering the neural underpinnings linked to pain perception (Zhang et al., 2023, Zhang et al., 2021). Notably, some investigations have observed alterations in ReHo metrics within pain-associated neural regions during nociceptive stimuli, suggesting a shift towards either heightened coordination or desynchronization of neural processes under painful conditions (Chen et al., 2018, Ma et al., 2022, Yang et al., 2022). ReHo has emerged as a widely accepted metric for evaluating the extent of coherent neuronal activity in brain areas implicated in pain processing.

To extend our previous ALFF findings, we investigated ReHo alterations in pain-related brain regions and examined their associations with behavioral pain severity in CCD rats receiving Tuina. Building upon our previous research, where ALFF was used to initially probe into Tuina's brain remodeling effects in neuropathic rats, we recognized the need to delve further into the role of ReHo in this context (Wu et al., 2023). Therefore, we designed this rigorous investigation to examine how Tuina modulates pain-related brain regions in neuropathic rats and to test the association between regional ReHo and pain severity, aiming to advance mechanism-based strategies for NPP management.

Materials and methods

Animals

Thirty specific pathogen-free male Sprague-Dawley rats (7–8 weeks; 180–200 g) were procured from Shanghai Jihui Experimental Animal Breeding Co., Ltd. (license No. SCXK (Hu) 2017–0012) and raised under a 12-hour light/dark cycle, 22–23°C with 40% humidity. Animals were randomly allocated to sham operation (Sham, N = 10), chronic compression of the dorsal root ganglion (CCD, N = 10), or Tuina groups (Tuina, N = 10) using the Excel RAND function without replacement. Sequentially numbered, opaque envelopes containing the assignments were prepared by an independent technician who had no role in surgery or behavioural testing. The envelope was opened immediately before anaesthesia induction, keeping surgeons blind to group identity. Post-surgery, cages were labelled only with numeric codes. All behavioural testing, MRI acquisition, and data analyses were performed by investigators unaware of group assignment. The Yueyang Hospital Ethics Committee authorized the experimental procedures on October 18, 2022 (YYLAC−2022–148–5). All procedures complied with institutional guidelines and the ARRIVE guidelines. One rat in each of the Sham and CCD groups died during the experiment, so these two animals were excluded. The rest of all animals were included in all experimental procedures and data analysis.

Surgery of CCD

Rats of the CCD group and Tuina group underwent surgery for CCD when satisfactory anesthesia (40 mg/kg intraperitoneal pentobarbital sodium) was maintained. The CCD model was established as previously described, involving the insertion of a titanium alloy rod of 5 mm in length and 0.4 mm in diameter into the right intervertebral foramen of L3/4 and L4/5 (Yao et al., 2022). The Sham group rats had a skin incision and muscle separation procedure but did not have an L-type rod inserted (Fig. 1B-C).

Tuina treatment

Tuina was administered from post-operative day 4–28. Prior to the intervention, all rats were habituated to a custom fixator for 10 min daily on days 1–3 (Fig. 1A). During each 10-min session, the experimenter (the same individual throughout the study) applied thumb pressure and circular rubbing to the right Weizhong (BL40) along the longitudinal axis of the hindlimb. Force and rhythm were standardised at 5 ± 0.5 N and 2 Hz, respectively, using a tactile pressure recorder (Novel Pliance-X 32 Expert, Germany); these parameters were adopted from our earlier pilot work (Wu et al., 2023). To minimise bias, the Tuina operator did not participate in behavioural testing or data analysis, and both the behavioural evaluator and MRI analyst were blinded to group allocation. The pressure was applied perpendicular to the skin surface at BL40, while circular rubbing was performed along the longitudinal axis of the hindlimb from proximal to distal and then returned to the starting point in each cycle. Thus, the intervention parameters included force magnitude, stimulation frequency, point of action, and direction.

Behavioral tests

Behavioral tests were performed on 2 and 1 days before surgery and on postoperative days 3, 7, 14, 21, and 28 after surgery (Fig. 1A). The average of the two preoperative measurements was used as baseline. The rats were separately housed in Plexiglas cages from the 5th to the 3rd day before surgery for 2 h and at every time of behavioral test after surgery for 30 min to ensure acclimation. As was earlier mentioned, the middle region of the right hind paw was stimulated, and any response, such as paw withdrawal, flinching, or licking, was regarded as positive behavior (He et al. 2019).

Herein, we assessed mechanical withdrawal threshold (MWT) and paw withdrawal thermal latency (PWL) through an electric Von-Frey esthesiometer (Stoelting Co., Wood Dale, IL, USA) and a radiant heat device (IITC Life Science, Woodland Hills, CA, USA) (Zhang et al., 2021, Hargreaves et al., 1988). Five simultaneous stimulations were applied. The mean values of the middle three data points were used to represent the MWT and PWL measurements, with each value being recorded. All behavior tasks were completed by an experimenter blinded to groups.

Magnetic resonance imaging data acquisition

fMRI was meticulously conducted using a high-field Bruker 11.7 T MRI system (BioSpec 117/16 USR-TT) containing a wide 16.0 cm aperture alongside a robust 12 cm gradient coil at the Institute of Science and Technology for Brain-Inspired Intelligence of Fudan University. To capture comprehensive data, imaging was performed prior to the surgical procedure and subsequently on the 7th and 28th days post-operation. The radio frequency (RF) coil system comprises a 72 mm inner diameter volumetric coil (Bruker, Germany) serving as the excitation coil. In tandem, a four-channel surface coil was utilized for signal reception to ensure high-fidelity data acquisition. All rats were immobilized in a supine position in the scanner after induction with 4% isoflurane anesthesia (RWD Life Science Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China), with the head fixed to a specific coil. The administration of dexmedetomidine hydrochloride (Sigma Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) was used to maintain a continuous state of anesthesia, details were as previously described (Paasonen et al., 2018). The rectal temperature was maintained at 36–38 °C with an automatic heating pad. All experimental processes were conducted under ventilator support and respiratory monitoring. The adjustment of the scanning parameters was: matrix size = 128 × 128, the field of view = 30 × 30 mm, repetition time = 2000 ms, echo time = 12.8 ms, flip angle = 90°, slice thickness = 0.5 mm, mean value = 1. The experimenter was blinded to groups.

Regional homogeneity data preprocessing

Statistical Parametric Mapping 12 (SPM 12) toolbox (https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/spm/software/spm12), a brain imaging data processing and analysis software relying upon the MATLAB 2017a platform (MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA), was used for fMRI data preprocessing. The implementation process was as follows: (1) DICOM-to-NIfTI conversion; (2) slice-timing correction; (3) realignment to the first volume to correct head motion (6-parameter rigid-body); (4) co-registration of the mean functional image to the T1-weighted structural image, followed by segmentation and nonlinear spatial normalisation to the MNI152 template (two-step procedure); (5) resampling to 3 mm isotropic voxels; (6) spatial smoothing was applied using 27 voxel smoothing kernels (6 mm) to minimize the impacts of low-frequency linear drive as well as high-frequency physical smoothing besides noise.

Regional homogeneity calculation

ReHo maps were generated as follows: after slice-timing and realignment, nuisance covariates—white-matter and cerebrospinal-fluid signals, plus six motion parameters—were regressed out. Data were then band-pass filtered at 0.01–0.08 Hz to retain low-frequency fluctuations (Lv et al., 2025). The Kendall coefficient of concordance (KCC) was calculated for each voxel using a 27-voxel cubic neighborhood. Spatial smoothing was achieved using 27 voxel smoothing kernels (6 mm), mitigating the impact of deformation and noise during standardization. Subsequently, the Fisher z transform was applied, followed by a normalization procedure using mean division. This process culminated in the generation of the final ReHo brain map suitable for statistical analysis.

Statistical analysis

Our study deployed SPSS 21.0 (IBM Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) for the statistical analysis of behavioral data and the correlation between ReHo values and behavioral tests, expressing the behavioral data as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). The normality of data will be tested by the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. If the data does not conform to a normal distribution, a covariance analysis will be used. The study examined the differences in MWT and PWL data among the groups at different time periods using a two-way repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) alongside the Bonferroni post hoc test for pairwise multiple comparisons. The ReHo values across the groups were compared using a two-sample t-test in SPM.12 software, which was implemented in MATLAB 2017a [p < 0.001 Gaussian random field (GRF), GRF corrected, voxel-p < 0.001, cluster-p < 0.05, two-tailed (Yan et al. 2016)]. GRF correction was used to control family-wise error in voxel-wise whole-brain comparisons while accounting for spatial smoothness. The Spearman correlation coefficients between ReHo values and behavioral tests were computed through SPSS 21.0 (IBM Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). P < 0.05 indicated a statistical significance. The statistician was blinded to groups.

Results

Tuina ameliorates mechanical and thermal pain in CCD-triggered NPP Rats

At baseline, the findings indicated no significant differences in MWT and PWL among the three groups (p > 0.05). Post-CCD surgery, the MWT and PWL of ipsilateral hind paws of rats in the CCD and Tuina groups exhibited significant reduction on day 7 in comparison to the Sham group (p < 0.001), implying that CCD provoked a prominent mechanical and thermal allodynia. The hyperalgesia caused by CCD persisted until day 28. Tuina caused a significant elevation in the MWT and PWL from days 14–28 in comparison to the CCD group (overall p < 0.05). Altogether, Tuina alleviated the mechanical and thermal allodynia in the CCD model. The results are detailed in our previously published article (Wu et al. 2023).

CCD and Sham groups differences in ReHo values on day 28 post-surgery

Compared with sham rats, CCD rats exhibited markedly elevated ReHo bilaterally in the amygdalo-hippocampic complex (amygdala, hippocampal CA1/CA3, dentate gyrus), parasubiculum, ectorhinal cortex, and cerebellar granule and molecular layers, together with increased ReHo in the brainstem (p < 0.05, GRF-corrected; Table 1 and Fig. 2). These regions, rather than the sheer number of clusters, may be the principal sites of altered local synchrony in chronic NPP.

Correlation between ReHo values and behavioral test

Spearman correlation analysis showed that ReHo values in predefined pain-related regions were negatively associated with MWT and PWL. The MWT is negatively correlated with ReHo values of brainstem (r = −0.285, p < 0.001), hippocampus (r = −0.273, p < 0.05), internal capsule (r = −0.243, p < 0.05), sensory-motor (r = −0.426, p < 0.001), somatosensory system (r = −0.267, p < 0.05) and temporal system (r = −0.318, p < 0.001). Meanwhile, PWL is negatively correlated with ReHo values of the brainstem (r = −0.31, p < 0.001), hippocampus (r = −0.229, p < 0.05), sensory-motor (r = −0.44, p < 0.001) and temporal system (r = −0.316, p < 0.001) (Fig. 6).

Discussion

Repeated Tuina alleviated mechanical and thermal hypersensitivity in CCD rats and was accompanied by marked ReHo alterations in distributed pain-related brain regions. Compared with the Sham group, CCD induced robust increases in ReHo within the amygdalo-hippocampal complex, parasubiculum, ectorhinal cortex, brainstem, and cerebellar layers, indicating substantial reorganization of local neural synchrony during persistent neuropathic pain. Tuina treatment was further associated with increased ReHo in the entorhinal-subicular circuit, primary motor cortex, corpus callosum, and cerebellum, together with progressive changes between postoperative days 7 and 28 in the amygdalo-hippocampal area, dentate gyrus, subiculum, parasubiculum, ectorhinal cortex, and corpus callosum. Collectively, these findings suggest that Tuina does not simply suppress brain activity globally, but rather reshapes local synchronization within distributed pain-processing networks.

At the peripheral and spinal levels, Tuina analgesia has been linked to anti-inflammatory signaling, mechanosensitive ion channels, and glial regulation (Liu et al., 2022) (Yang et al., 2024) (Song et al. 2018). At the supraspinal level, our previous ALFF study (Wu et al., 2023) and other resting-state fMRI studies suggest that manual therapy can remodel pain-related brain activity and connectivity (Xing et al., 2021) (Ma et al., 2023). Consistent with this, the present ReHo findings extend earlier work by showing that repeated Tuina is associated not only with behavioral improvement, but also with regionally specific reorganization of local neural synchrony in chronic neuropathic pain.

ReHo is a widely used index of local neural synchrony in pain neuroimaging (Wang et al., 2015). Abnormal ReHo has been reported in limbic and sensorimotor regions in migraine(Chen et al., 2019) (Shi et al., 2022), in prefrontal, hippocampal, insular, and temporal regions in primary dysmenorrhea (Jin et al., 2017), and in multiple supraspinal regions after acute spinal cord injury (Zhu et al., 2015). In the present study, the most prominent ReHo shifts were found in the amygdala, hippocampus, dentate gyrus, subiculum, parasubiculum, and ectorhinal cortex. Because these structures are closely related to affective salience, contextual memory, and pain persistence, the observed changes likely reflect reorganization of affective-memory circuitry rather than a simple change in nociceptive intensity alone.

This interpretation is supported by recent small-animal neuroimaging studies. Chi et al. reported that neuropathic pain in rats was associated with dynamic changes in brain activity and functional connectivity over time (Chi et al., 2024). Zhou et al. further demonstrated structural and functional alterations in the hippocampal system under chronic pain conditions, including dentate gyrus-related changes detected by resting-state fMRI and DTI (Zhou et al., 2024). Together with our findings, these studies support the view that neuropathic pain engages maladaptive plasticity in limbic-hippocampal circuits and that Tuina-related analgesia may be accompanied by gradual reorganization of these affective-memory networks.

Another notable finding is the involvement of sensorimotor, brainstem, and cerebellar systems. Chronic pain is increasingly recognized as a distributed network disorder involving sensory-discriminative, affective-motivational, and descending modulatory systems. Consistent with this view, a recent rat fMRI study showed that different pain entities produce distinct cerebral hypersensitivity network signatures involving S1, M1, hippocampus, piriform cortex, and cingulate cortex (Kreitz et al., 2025). Clinical manual-therapy imaging studies have likewise shown that pain relief can be accompanied by functional remodeling in salience and sensorimotor networks (Isenburg et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2022). In this context, the higher ReHo observed in the primary motor cortex and cerebellar molecular layer after Tuina may reflect altered sensorimotor integration and compensatory recruitment within pain-modulatory circuits. Importantly, the negative correlations between ReHo and MWT/PWL in the brainstem, hippocampus, sensory-motor, and temporal systems indicate that local synchrony in these regions was behaviorally relevant. However, the direction of ReHo change should be interpreted cautiously: higher ReHo may indicate maladaptive hypersynchrony in some nodes of the pain network, whereas Tuina-related increases in other regions may represent adaptive reorganization and restoration of network integration.

The temporal profile of our data also deserves emphasis. Behavioral improvement emerged from postoperative day 14 onward, whereas rs-fMRI was acquired only on days 7 and 28. Therefore, the current data support a suggestive, rather than definitive, two-stage trajectory of Tuina-induced neuroplasticity. Early changes may be dominated by altered nociceptive processing, whereas later changes may involve broader limbic-hippocampal and interregional remodeling. This interpretation is broadly consistent with longitudinal rodent imaging studies of pain chronification and central sensitization (Wu et al., 2018), Spisák et al., 2017 Chi et al., 2024). At the same time, the standardized parameters used here (5 N, 2 Hz, 10 min/day) improved experimental reproducibility, but they should not be regarded as directly equivalent to clinical Tuina. Future studies should combine quantitative force-control systems with behavioral and neuroimaging endpoints to clarify dose-response relationships and improve translational relevance (Liu et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2023).

The anesthesia regimen should also be considered when interpreting these findings. Dexmedetomidine or medetomidine-based protocols are widely used in rodent rs-fMRI because they help preserve resting-state network organization better than deeper volatile anesthesia alone (Paasonen et al., 2018). Recent work has further shown that stable and reproducible resting-state networks can be identified in anesthetized rats and that combined ISO/MED protocols can provide relatively stable brain states for rs-fMRI acquisition after an appropriate equilibration period (Dai et al., 2023, Pradier et al., 2021). Nevertheless, anesthesia remains a potential confound because spontaneous neural activity and neurovascular coupling are still modified in a dose- and time-dependent manner. Accordingly, the present results should be interpreted as pain-related and Tuina-related differences observed within a standardized anesthetized state, rather than as exact equivalents of awake brain function.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the present design did not include a sham Tuina or non-acupoint stimulation group, and therefore the acupoint specificity of BL40 stimulation cannot be distinguished from non-specific tactile input. Second, although behavioral analgesia became evident on postoperative day 14, rs-fMRI was performed only on days 7 and 28, which limits precise characterization of intermediate neuroplastic changes. Third, ReHo is a correlational measure and cannot establish causality between altered local synchrony and analgesic outcome. Fourth, only evoked pain behaviors were assessed; future studies should include affective and spontaneous pain-related behavioral assays to better capture the emotional dimension of neuropathic pain. Finally, because the current experiment was conducted under dexmedetomidine-based anesthesia and in male rats only, caution is warranted when generalizing these findings across physiological states and sexes.

Conclusion

Repeated Tuina alleviated CCD-induced neuropathic pain and was associated with ReHo alterations in limbic, sensorimotor, brainstem, and cerebellar regions. These findings suggest that central neuroplasticity may contribute to Tuina analgesia, although causal and acupoint-specific mechanisms require further investigation.

Infinite Versions of Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz
A. Bernhard Zeidler

Abstract
. We compile a long list of equivalent formulations of Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz in infinite dimensions, and prove a persistence result for the strong Nullstellensatz in large polynomial rings.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
13A15,14A04

1.
Presentation of the Results

This note is devoted to Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz for polynomial rings of infinite
Krull dimension. We collect classical and recent equivalences (see Theorem
1.1
)
and provide complete, elementary proofs (see Section
2
) of which
some are new or generalize existing ones. Moreover, given an ideal in a polynomial
ring of dimension lower than the cardinality of the base field, we show that its
extensions to larger polynomial rings satisfy the strong Nullstellensatz, even if
their cardinality exceeds that of the base field (see Corollary
1.5
).

Let us fix the setting and notation: All rings and algebras are assumed to be commutative,
non-zero, with unit and their homomorphisms are assumed to preserve the unit element.
Given a ring
F
and a (not necessarily finite) set
I
, let
A:=F[t_{i}\mid i\in I]

be the polynomial algebra over
F
. The set of maximal ideals of
A
is denoted by

\mbox{\rm{Smax}}(A)
. Any ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\unlhd A
defines the zero set
\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}})\subseteq\,F^{I}
and any
X\subseteq\,F^{I}
defines the vanishing ideal
\mathds{I}(X)\unlhd A
. The
vanishing ideal of a single element
x\in F^{I}
is denoted by
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F
be a non-zero ring,
I
be a non-empty index set and the polynomial algebra

A=F[t_{i}\mid i\in I]
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

F
is an algebraically closed field and
|I|<|F|
holds for the
cardinalities.

(b)

Any proper ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\unlhd A
admits an
F
-algebra homomorphism
A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\to F
.

(c)

Any maximal ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\unlhd A
admits an
F
-algebra homomorphism
A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\to F
.

(d)

Any maximal ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\unlhd A
is of the form
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}
for
some
x\in F^{I}
.

(e)

The assignment
x\mapsto{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}
defines a bijection
F^{I}\to\mbox{\rm{Smax}}(A)
.

(f)

F
is a field and any
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\in\mbox{\rm{Smax}}(A)
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in I
admit

x_{i_{k}}\in F
with

{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\cap F[t_{i_{1}},\ldots,t_{i_{n}}]\ =\ \langle t_{i_{1}}-x_{i_{1}},\,\dots,\,t_{i_{n}}-x_{i_{n}}\rangle.

(g)

F
is a field and for any ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\unlhd A
we get
\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}})=\emptyset\Leftrightarrow{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}=A
.

(h)

F
is a field and any field extension
F\subseteq\,K=F[u_{i}\mid i\in I]
is trivial.

(i)

For any ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\unlhd A
we have
\sqrt{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}}=\mathds{I}\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}})
.

The presumably most popular equivalence, (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(d), is attributed to Kaplansky,
apparently without concrete reference (compare
[
6
, p. 407, footnote 1]
). We
provide a direct proof here. Statement (b) is the chromatic version
[
3
, Thm. 6.1]

by Burklund, Schlank and Yuan. We present a slight modification of their proof, to match
our formulation. Statement (h) is taken from
[
6
]
and (i) is gained from (d)
classically via
Rabinowitsch’s trick
. We neither saw (c) nor (f) before, but
they’re elementary. The rest is common folklore.

Remark 1.2
.

It is clear, that the strong Nullstellensatz
1.1
.(i) gives
rise to a one-to-one correspondence between radical ideals of
A=F[t_{i}\mid i\in I]
and algebraic subsets of
F^{I}
. All we need is
F
being an algebraically
closed field and
|I|<|F|
.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that we
assumed

F
to be a field in (a), (f), (g) and (h) only. In
the other statements, this is implied. In fact it is possible to drop this assumption
in (f) as well, by allowing
n=0
. In this case we have
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\cap F=0
for
any
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\in\mbox{\rm{Smax}}(A)
and this is equivalent to
F
being a field.

Remark 1.4
.

In 1952 S. Lang proved a similar equivalence: If
F
is an algebraically closed
field and
P\subseteq\,F
is the prime field of
F
, then property (d) holds true
if and only if
I
is finite or
|I|<\mbox{\rm{trdeg}}(F:P)
. The latter is much the
same as (a), since the transcendence degree satisfies

|F|\ =\ \mbox{\rm{max}}\hskip-1.70709pt\left\{\,|\mathds{N}|,\,\mbox{\rm{trdeg}}(F:P)\,\right\}

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field and
I\subseteq\,J
be index sets, such that

I
is non-empty and
|I|<|F|
. If now
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}\unlhd F[t_{j}\mid j\in J]

is an ideal of the form
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\,F[t_{j}\mid j\in J]
for some ideal

{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\unlhd F[t_{i}\mid i\in I]
, then we still get the identity

\mathds{I}\,\mathds{V}({\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}})\ =\ \sqrt{{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{u}$}}}

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.5

It is well-known, that: If
F
is a field and
I
is a non-empty index set, then
the ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}=\mathds{V}(x)=\langle t_{i}-x_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle
is a maximal ideal
of
A=F[t_{i}\mid i\in I]
and the following map is well-defined and injective

F^{I}\,\,\hookrightarrow\,\,\mbox{\rm{Smax}}(A)\;:\;x\,\mapsto\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
A
is
geometric
, iff this map is surjective. That is iff every
maximal ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\in\mbox{\rm{Smax}}(A)
of
A
is of the form
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}

for some
x\in F^{I}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
F
be a field,
I
a non-empty index set and
A=F[t_{i}\mid i\in I]
the

F
-algebra of polynomials. Then we get the following statements

(i)

If
I
is finite and
F
is an algebraically closed field, then
A
is geometric.

(ii)

If
I
is infinite, then
A
is geometric if and only if
A[s]
is geometric.

In this proposition (i) is the classic (weak) Nullstellensatz, of which numerous
proofs exist: A short, elegant proof has been given by Zariski in
[
8
]
using
the Noether Normalisation theorem. Krull and Goldman introduced the theory of Jacobson
resp. Hilbert rings, which can be seen as a generalization of this, e.g. refer to

[
4
]
. In 1999 Munshi reduced the techniques of Jacobson rings to a minimum
- refer to
[
7
]
, in 2006 Arrondo gave an astoundingly simple proof in
[
1
]
.
And in 2018 a proof using Gröbner bases has been provided by Glebsky and Rubio-Barrios

[
5
]
. Statement (ii) is quite obvious, we took it from
[
6
]
.

Let us split the proof of Theorem
1.1
into parts. The first
insight is due to
[
3
]
:
Claim 1:
Under assumption (b),
F
is an algebraically
closed field.
Proof of claim 1:
Let us first prove, that
F
is a field:
Consider any
a\in F
with
a\not\in F^{\ast}
and define the ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}:=\langle t_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle+aA
. Then the homomorphism

A\,\,\twoheadrightarrow\,\,\raisebox{3.0pt}{$F$}\hskip-1.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{/}}\hskip 1.07pt\hskip-2.0pt\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$aF$}\;:\;f\,\mapsto\,f(0)+aF

is surjective and has kernel

\mathfrak{a}

, which is a proper ideal, since
a\not\in F^{\ast}
. Therefore
A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}
and
F/aF
are isomorphic as
F
-algebras and
by (b) there is a homomorphism
\varphi
of the form
\varphi:A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\to F
. This gives rise to a homomorphism of
F
-algebras

\raisebox{3.0pt}{$F$}\hskip-1.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{/}}\hskip 1.07pt\hskip-2.0pt\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$aF$}\,\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt]{$\sim$}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\,\raisebox{3.0pt}{$A$}\hskip-1.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{/}}\hskip 1.07pt\hskip-2.0pt\raisebox{-3.0pt}{${\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}$}\,\to\,F

Explicitly this is
F/aF\to F:b+aF\mapsto b1_{A}+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\mapsto\varphi(b1_{A}+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}})=b
. But from
a+aR\mapsto a
and
a+aR\mapsto 0
we find

a=0
and therefore
F
is a field.

Next we have to show, that
F
even is algebraically closed: Fix any
j\in I
, let

H:=I\setminus\left\{\,j\,\right\}
and consider an arbitrary, non-constant polynomial
f\in F[t_{j}]\subseteq\,A
. Now define the ideals
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{h}$}}:=\langle t_{i}\mid i\in H\rangle
and

{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}:=fA+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{h}$}}
. Then it is clear, that
F[t_{j}]\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt]{$\sim$}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{h}$}}:g\mapsto g+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{h}$}}
is an isomorphism of
R
-algebras, that maps
fF[t_{j}]
to

{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{h}$}}
. And therefore we find the isomorphism

\raisebox{3.0pt}{$F[t_{j}]$}\hskip-1.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{/}}\hskip 1.07pt\hskip-2.0pt\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$fF[t_{j}]$}\;\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt]{$\sim$}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\;\raisebox{3.0pt}{$A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{h}$}}$}\hskip-1.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{/}}\hskip 1.07pt\hskip-2.0pt\raisebox{-3.0pt}{${\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{h}$}}$}\;\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt]{$\sim$}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\;\raisebox{3.0pt}{$A$}\hskip-1.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{/}}\hskip 1.07pt\hskip-2.0pt\raisebox{-3.0pt}{${\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}$}\;:\;g+fF[t_{j}]\,\mapsto\,g+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}

Now
F[t_{j}]/fF[t_{j}]
is a
\deg(f)
-dimensional
F
-vector space and as
f
is
non-constant, we have
\deg(f)\geq 1
. In particular
F[t_{j}]/fF[t_{j}]\not=0
and therefore
A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\not=0
. That is

\mathfrak{a}

is a proper ideal
and by (b) there is a homomorphism
\varphi
of
F
-algebras

\varphi\;:\;\raisebox{3.0pt}{$F[t_{j}]$}\hskip-1.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{/}}\hskip 1.07pt\hskip-2.0pt\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$fF[t_{j}]$}\;\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt]{$\sim$}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,\;\raisebox{3.0pt}{$A$}\hskip-1.0pt\raisebox{-1.0pt}{\scalebox{1.5}{/}}\hskip 1.07pt\hskip-2.0pt\raisebox{-3.0pt}{${\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}$}\;\,\twoheadrightarrow\,\;F

Let
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{f}$}}:=fF[t_{j}]
and
x:=\varphi\big(t_{j}+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{f}$}}\big)\in F
.
As
\varphi
is a homomorphism of
R
-algebras, it commutes with polynomials and
hence
f(x)=\varphi\big(f(t_{j}+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{f}$}})\big)=\varphi\big(f+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{f}$}}\big)=0_{F}
. Thus any non-constant polynomial over
F
has a root.
∎

Claim 2:
Statements (b) to (e) are equivalent.
Proof of claim 2.
(b)

\Rightarrow
(c) is trivial, for the converse implication choose a maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}

containing

\mathfrak{a}

. Then we can compose
A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{a}$}}\,\twoheadrightarrow\,A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\to F
. In (d)
\Rightarrow
(c) we are given
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}
for some
x\in F^{I}
. Then
\varphi:f\mapsto f(x)
is the homomorphism we seek. Conversely (c)
implies, that
F
is a field, via (b) and claim 1. Given a maximal ideal

\mathfrak{m}

fix
\varphi:A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\to F
and
x:=(x_{i})
where
x_{i}:=\varphi(t_{i}+{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}})\in F
. Then
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}\subseteq\,\mbox{\rm{ker}}(\varphi)={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}
. Over a field
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}

is maximal, thus
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}
, which is (d). Next (e)
\Rightarrow
(d)
is trivial and conversely we already know (d) implies (b) such that
F
is a field
again. Hence
x\mapsto{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}
is injective, now (d) is the surjectivity missing
for (e).
∎

Claim 3:
(d)
\Rightarrow
(a)
Proof of claim 3.
By claim 1 it only remains
to prove
|I|<|F|
. If
I
is finite, then
|I|<|F|
is clear, as
an algebraically closed field always is infinite. So from now on we assume, that
I

is infinite and elaborate on an argument given in
[
6
]
: Fix any
j\in I
,
as
I
is infinite we have
|I\setminus\{j\}|=|I|
and thereby there
is a subset
H\subseteq\,I\setminus\{j\}
such that
H\,\longleftrightarrow\,F:i\mapsto a_{i}
.
We now define a homomorphism of
F
-algebras
\varphi:A\to F(s)
, by expansion of

\displaystyle\varphi(t_{i})\,:=\,s

for

\displaystyle i\,=\,j

\displaystyle\varphi(t_{i})\,:=\,\frac{1}{s-a_{i}}

for

\displaystyle i\,\in\,H

\displaystyle\varphi(t_{i})\,:=\,0

for

\displaystyle i\,\in\,I\setminus\Big(H\cup\{j\}\Big)

We first have to check, that
\varphi
is surjective: As
F
is algebraically
closed any polynomial
p(s)\in F[s]
splits into linear factors, but as we have
enumerated
F
using the index set
H
, we may write it in the form

p(s)\ =\ \lambda\,(s-a)^{\alpha}\ :=\ \lambda\,\prod_{i\in H}(s-a_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}

with only finitely many
\alpha_{i}\in\mathds{N}
being non-zero. Therefore any rational
function
p/q
, where
p
,
q\in F[s]
and
q\not=0
, can be written in the
following form, where
k_{i}\in\mathds{Z}
and only finitely many
k_{i}
are non-zero

\frac{p(s)}{q(s)}\ =\ \lambda\,\prod_{i\in H}(s-a_{i})^{k_{i}}

By construction of
\varphi
it is clear, that any polynomial
f(t_{j})\in F[t_{j}]\subseteq\,A
is simply mapped to
f(s)\in F(s)
. Likewise any monomial
t^{\beta}\in F[t_{i}\mid i\in H]\subseteq\,A
where
\beta\in\mathds{N}^{\oplus H}
is mapped
to
1/(s-a)^{\beta}
. Given
p/q\in F[s]
, write
p/q
in the form above and
pick up
\alpha
and
\beta\in\mathds{N}^{\oplus H}
such that
k_{i}=\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}
for any
i\in H
(and
\alpha_{i}=0=\beta_{i}
for
k_{i}=0
).
Then we find polynomials
f:=\lambda\prod_{i}(t_{j}-a_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}\in F[t_{j}]\subseteq\,A
and
g:=t^{\beta}\in F[t_{i}\mid i\in H]\subseteq\,A
such that
fg\in A
is mapped to
p/q\in F(s)
under
\varphi

\varphi\left(\lambda\prod_{i\in H}(t_{j}-a_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot t^{\beta}\right)\;=\;\lambda\prod_{i\in H}(s-a_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}\cdot\frac{1}{(s-a)^{\beta}}\;=\;\lambda\prod_{i\in H}\frac{(s-a_{i})^{\alpha_{i}}}{(s-a_{i})^{\beta_{i}}}\;=\;\frac{p(s)}{q(s)}

As
p/q
have been arbitrary
\varphi
is surjective and if we let
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}:=\mbox{\rm{ker}}(\varphi)
we therefore find an isomorphism
A/{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\raisebox{-1.50694pt}[0.0pt]{$\sim$}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\,F(s)
. As
F(s)

is a field,

\mathfrak{m}

is a maximal ideal. Now by (d) we find
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}={\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}

for some
x\in F^{I}
, in particular
t_{i}-x_{i}\in{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}
for some
i\in H
.
By construction this means

0\;=\;\varphi(t_{i}-x_{i})\;=\;\frac{1}{s-a_{i}}-x_{i}\;=\;\frac{1-x_{i}s+x_{i}a_{i}}{s-a_{i}}

This yields
1-x_{i}s+x_{i}a_{i}=0
and by comparing the coefficients of this
polynomial in
s
, we get
x_{i}=0
and
0=1+x_{i}a_{i}=1
. But
0=1
is an
obvious contradiction to the assumption
|F|\leq|I|
.
∎

. However, as
I
is infinite

|\mathds{N}^{\oplus I}|\ =\ \mbox{\rm{max}}\hskip-1.70709pt\left\{\,|\mathds{N}|,\,|I|\,\right\}\ =\ |I|

We can choose any well-ordering on
I
and use the bijection
I\,\longleftrightarrow\,\mathds{N}^{\oplus I}:i\mapsto\alpha_{i}
to install a total order
\alpha_{i}\leq\alpha_{j}\,:\Longleftrightarrow\,i\leq j
. We do not require this to be a monomial order, nevertheless we
may define the degree of a polynomial
f\in A
, as usual

\deg(f)\ :=\ \mbox{\rm{max}}\hskip-1.70709pt\left\{\,\alpha\in\mathds{N}^{\oplus I}\mid\mbox{$\alpha$-coefficient of $f$}\not=0\,\right\}

If now
\beta\in\mathds{N}^{\oplus I}
is any multi-index then
\left\{\,t^{\alpha}\mid\alpha\leq\beta\,\right\}
corresponds to a subset of
I
. Hence the subspace
of
A
generated by this set has dimension
|I|
, at most:

\Omega_{\beta}\ :=\ \left\{\,f\in A\mid\deg(f)\leq\beta\,\right\}\ \leq\ A

Now consider some maximal ideal
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}\unlhd A
. We need to show that

\mathfrak{m}

belongs to some point
x=(x_{i})\in F^{I}
. By maximality of
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}
, it suffices
to show
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}_{x}\subseteq\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}
, which is

\forall\,i\in I\ \exists\,x_{i}\in F\ :\ t_{i}-x_{i}\,\in\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}

Assume this was false, i.e. there was some
j\in I
such that for any
z\in F
we
would get
t_{j}-z\not\in{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}
. Formally the assumption for this
j
reads as

\exists\,j\in I\ \forall\,z\in F\ :\ t_{j}-z\,\not\in\,{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}

As

\mathfrak{m}

is maximal this implies
{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}+(t_{j}-z)A=A
and hence (for
any
z\in F
) we may find polynomials
m^{z}\in{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}
and
g^{z}\in A
such that

\forall\,z\in F\ :\ m^{z}+(t_{j}-z)g^{z}\,=\,1

Every polynomial
g^{z}
has a degree and hence
F
may be decomposed into a disjoint
union of the following subsets

F\ =\ \bigsqcup_{i\in I}\left\{\,z\in F\mid\deg(g^{z})=\alpha_{i}\,\right\}

If all these sets
F_{i}:=\left\{\,z\in F\mid\deg(g^{z})=\alpha_{i}\,\right\}
would satisfy

|F_{i}|\leq|I|
then there would be surjective maps
s_{i}:I\,\twoheadrightarrow\,F_{i}

and thereby a surjective map
I\times I\,\twoheadrightarrow\,F
by
(h,i)\mapsto s_{i}(h)
. Yet as

I
is infinite we have
|I\times I|=|I|<|F|
by assumption and
hence there cannot be a surjective map from
I\times I
onto
F
. We conclude
that there must be some
i\in I
such that
|F_{i}|>|I|
. Let
\beta:=\alpha_{i}
and
Z:=F_{i}
for this
i\in I

Z\ =\ \left\{\,z\in F\mid\deg(g^{z})=\beta\,\right\}\ \mbox{ satisfies }\ |Z|\,>\,|I|

As we have seen
\Omega_{\beta}
is an
F
-vectorspace of dimension at most

|I|
. If some of the
g^{z}
where
z\in Z
were identical, then we could
skip the next step and continue with the next linear relation, right away. If
all the
g^{z}
where
z\in Z
are distinct, then the set
\left\{\,g^{z}\mid z\in Z\,\right\}

has cardinality
|Z|>|I|\geq\dim\big(\Omega_{\beta}\big)
.
Thus the
g^{z}
have to satisfy a linear relation, that is there are pairwise
distinct elements
z_{1},\dots,z_{r}\in Z
and coefficients
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}\in F^{\ast}
such that

\sum_{p=1}^{r}\lambda_{p}\,g^{z_{p}}\ =\ 0

We now define a specific polynomial
g\in F[t_{j}]\subseteq\,A
in the one variable

t_{j}
over
F

g\ :=\ \sum_{p=1}^{r}\lambda_{p}\prod_{q\not=p}(t_{j}-z_{q})\ \in\ F[t_{j}]\ \subseteq\,\ A

By construction we have
g(z_{1})=\lambda_{1}(z_{1}-z_{2})\dots(z_{1}-z_{r})\not=0

and hence
g
is non-zero itself. We now claim that this polynomial is nevertheless
contained in the ideal

\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{r}\lambda_{p}\prod_{q\not=p}(t_{j}-z_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{r}\lambda_{p}[m^{z_{p}}+(t_{j}-z_{p})g^{z_{p}}]\prod_{q\not=p}(t_{j}-z_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{r}\left[\lambda_{p}\prod_{q\not=p}(t_{j}-z_{q})\right]m^{z_{p}}+\left[\sum_{p=1}^{r}\lambda_{p}\,g^{z_{p}}\right]\prod_{q=1}^{r}(t_{j}-z_{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{r}\left[\lambda_{p}\prod_{q\not=p}(t_{j}-z_{q})\right]m^{z_{p}}+0\ \in\ {\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}

As
g\not=0
and
g\in F^{\ast}
is ruled out by
g\in{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}
, we see
g\not\in F
. But

\mathfrak{m}

is maximal, in particular prime, and hence some prime
factor of
g\in F[t_{j}]
is contained in

\mathfrak{m}

. But as
F
is algebraically
closed, the prime factors of
g
are of the form
t_{j}-z
for some
z\in F
, in
contradiction to the assumption that
t_{j}-z\not\in{\scalebox{1.10}{$\mathfrak{m}$}}
for any
z\in F
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

Let us abbreviate
F[I]:=F[t_{i}\mid i\in I]
, respectively

We need to prepare another identity:

The cases

. In this case let

(t_{j})

.

On the effect of randomization on supercritical heat equations
Eliseo Luongo

Abstract
. Recently, in [ 27 ] , it has been shown that the focusing power nonlinearity heat equation (NLH) ∂ t u − Δ ​ u = | u | p − 1 ​ u , p > 1 , \partial_{t}u-\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,\quad p>1, in dimensions d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 has non-unique local solutions in L q ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) for q < d ⁡ ( p − 1 ) / 2 q<d(p-1)/2 provided that p < p J ​ L p<p_{JL} , where p J ​ L p_{JL} denotes the Joseph-Lundgren exponent. In this paper we investigate the effect of different randomizations on the well-posedness of the equation. First we show that adding a forcing term white in time and colored in space in ( NLH ) is not sufficient to improve the solution theory: namely, we prove non-uniqueness for local-in-time mild solutions of ( NLH ) with additive noise. Second, we discuss how randomizing the initial conditions of ( NLH ) affects its well-posedness.

1.2.
The case of randomization in the initial conditions

The non-uniqueness in (
1.1
) of
Theorem 1.2
is
typical
of nonlinear PDEs settled in supercritical spaces for which several ill-posedness phenomena, like non-uniqueness or instantaneous lack of regularity, are possible
[
14
,
39
]
. However, the functions for which one can rigorously prove such a pathological behavior are, usually, highly non-generic and one would expect that the ill-posedness is indeed a non generic phenomenon. Therefore, a Gaussian measure on the space of initial conditions should be able to recognize if there is a lack of the known ill-posedness results, telling us if they are
generic
in terms of initial conditions or not. Starting from the seminal works
[
12
,
13
]
the above heuristic has been applied in several contexts, we refer for example to
[
43
,
57
,
44
,
41
]
and the references therein for several applications of this principle. In the context of
Theorem 1.2
, given
u_{0}
as the one in the statement, one can only show that for each
\epsilon>0
and radial initial condition
{u}_{0,*}\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, there exists
v_{0}
radial and smooth such that

\displaystyle\left\|{u}_{0,*}-u_{0}-v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q}}\leq\epsilon

and (
1.1
) with initial condition
u_{0}+v_{0}
has non-unique solutions. Motivated by the above considerations, we are interested in studying (
1.1
) with random initial conditions.

Similarly to
[
41
,
44
]
we randomize functions directly on Euclidean space via a unit-scale decomposition in frequency space.
More precisely, let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a real-valued function such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1,\ \varphi(\xi)=\varphi(-\xi)
and

\displaystyle\varphi(\xi)=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{for }\xi\in[-1/2,1/2]^{d}\\
0\quad&\text{for }\xi\notin[-1,1]^{d}.\end{cases}

For every
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
set
\varphi_{k}(\xi)=\varphi(\xi-k)
and define

(1.7)

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(\xi)=\frac{\varphi_{k}(\xi)}{\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\varphi_{l}(\xi)}.

Then
\psi_{k}
is a smooth function with support contained in
k+[-1,1]^{d}
and
\psi_{k}(\xi)=\psi_{-k}(-\xi),

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\psi_{k}(\xi)\equiv 1\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Secondly, let
\mathcal{I}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be such that
\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\mathcal{I}\cup(-\mathcal{I})\cup\{0\},\ \mathcal{I}\cap(-\mathcal{I})=\varnothing
and

\{h^{1}_{k},h^{2}_{k}\}_{k\in\{0\}\cup\mathcal{I}}
be i.i.d. standard real Gaussian random variables
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable so that

is a complex Gaussian random variable
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable. Set also

for

Lastly, for

\displaystyle P_{k}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\psi_{k}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\right)\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Above, we denoted by

the Fourier transform of

Having this notation in mind we define the randomization of

\displaystyle f^{\omega}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}h_{k}(\omega)P_{k}f.

The definition of randomization of

We regard

as a complementary result to

\displaystyle M=\frac{1}{(2C_{d,p,r,\epsilon^{\prime}})^{1/(p-1)}},\ \hat{T}=\operatorname{inf}\left\{t>0:C_{d,p,r,\epsilon^{\prime}}t^{\frac{2-s_{0}p}{4}}\left\|s^{\frac{s_{0}p-1+\epsilon^{\prime}}{2p}}\hat{z}\right\|_{L^{2p}(0,t;L^{rp})}^{p}\geq\frac{M}{2}\right\}.

The latter is a stopping time due to the
\mathcal{F}_{0}
measurability of
u_{0}^{\omega}
. The continuity of
\Gamma[v](t)
follows similarly.
Now we are left to show that
\Gamma
is a contraction.
Let us analyze
\Gamma[v_{1}]-\Gamma[v_{2}]
. By (

), ultracontractivity of the heat semigroup and Hölder inequality we have, arguing similarly as above,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\Gamma[v_{1}](t)-\Gamma[v_{2}](t)\right\|_{L^{r}}\leq C_{d,p,r}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left\|\left(\lvert\hat{z}(s)\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert v_{1}(s)\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert v_{2}(s)\rvert^{p-1}\right)\lvert v_{1}(s)-v_{2}(s)\rvert\right\|_{L^{r/p}}}{(t-s)^{\frac{d(p-1)}{2r}}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,p,r}\left(\hat{T}^{1-\frac{d(p-1)}{2r}}M^{p-1}+\left(\int_{0}^{\hat{t}}\frac{1}{s^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\alpha^{\prime}-1}\gamma^{\prime}(p-1)}(t-s)^{\frac{d(p-1)\alpha}{2r(\alpha-1)}}}ds\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\left\|t^{\gamma^{\prime}}\hat{z}\right\|_{L^{\alpha^{\prime}(p-1)}(0,\hat{T};L^{r})}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\left\|v_{1}-v_{2}\right\|_{C([0,\hat{T}];L^{r})}.

According to
4.3
, the latter is a treatable quantity, finite
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, as soon as we can find
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}r\geq\alpha^{\prime}(p-1)\geq 2\\
\frac{d(p-1)\alpha^{\prime}}{2r(\alpha^{\prime}-1)}<1\\
\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\alpha^{\prime}-1}\gamma^{\prime}(p-1)<1\\
(s_{0}-2\gamma^{\prime})\alpha^{\prime}(p-1)<2.\end{cases}

Considerations analogous to the ones above allow us to find the choice of parameters

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}=p^{\prime}\vee\frac{2}{p-1},\quad\gamma^{\prime}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}-1}{\alpha^{\prime}(p-1)}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(2-s_{0}(p-1))-\epsilon^{\prime}}{2\alpha(p-1)},\quad 0<\epsilon^{\prime}<\alpha^{\prime}(2-s_{0}(p-1)),

where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate of
p
.
In particular, under our choice of the parameters

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{s^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\alpha^{\prime}-1}\gamma^{\prime}(p-1)}(t-s)^{\frac{d(p-1)\alpha}{2r(\alpha-1)}}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(t^{\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{2(\alpha^{\prime}-1)}}\right)^{2-s_{0}(p-1)-\epsilon^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}-\frac{2}{p\vee p^{\prime}}}.

By elementary computations one can check that

\displaystyle 2-s_{0}(p-1)-\frac{2}{p\vee p^{\prime}}>2-\frac{2}{p}(p-1)-\frac{2}{p\vee p^{\prime}}\geq 0.

Therefore, if we choose

(4.7)

\displaystyle 0<\epsilon^{\prime}<\frac{2-s_{0}(p-1)-\frac{2}{p\vee p^{\prime}}}{2\alpha^{\prime}}\wedge\alpha^{\prime}(2-s_{0}(p-1))\wedge\frac{\alpha(2-s_{0}p)}{2}

estimate (
4.6
) reduces to

\displaystyle\left\|\Gamma[v_{1}](t)-\Gamma[v_{2}](t)\right\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,p,r,q}\left(\hat{T}^{1-\frac{d(p-1)}{2r}}M^{p-1}+\hat{T}^{\frac{2-s_{0}(p-1)-\frac{2}{p\vee p^{\prime}}}{4}}\left\|t^{\gamma^{\prime}}\hat{z}\right\|_{L^{\alpha^{\prime}(p-1)}(0,\hat{T};L^{r})}^{p-1}\right)\left\|v_{1}-v_{2}\right\|_{C([0,\hat{T}];L^{r})}.

Therefore

\Gamma[v]
is a contraction on
B_{M,\hat{T}}
if we choose
M,\hat{T}
sufficiently small, for example

\displaystyle{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2C_{d,p,r})^{1/(p-1)}},

\displaystyle\hat{T}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{inf}\left\{t>0:C_{d,p,q,r}t^{\frac{2-s_{0}(p-1)-\frac{2}{p\vee p^{\prime}}}{4}}\left\|s^{\gamma^{\prime}}\hat{z}\right\|_{L^{\alpha^{\prime}(p-1)}(0,t;L^{r})}^{p-1}\geq\frac{1}{4}\right\}

\displaystyle\wedge\operatorname{inf}\left\{t>0:C_{d,p,r,\epsilon^{\prime}}t^{\frac{2-s_{0}p}{4}}\left\|s^{\frac{s_{0}p-1+\epsilon^{\prime}}{2p}}\hat{z}\right\|_{L^{2p}(0,t;L^{rp})}^{p}\geq\frac{M}{2}\right\},

the latter being a stopping time due to the
\mathcal{F}_{0}
measurability of
u_{0}^{\omega}
. Let us call
\mathcal{T}=\hat{T}
. This is the stopping time in the statement of
Theorem 1.5
. Moreover, by the last part of
4.3
, up to renaming the constant appearing there, there exist two constants
C^{1}_{d,p,q,r},C^{2}_{d,p,q,r}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{T}\geq T)\geq 1-C^{1}_{d,p,r}e^{-\frac{C^{2}_{d,p,r}}{T^{\frac{1}{p}\wedge\left(\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{d}{r-p+1}\right)}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{-s_{0}}}^{2}}}\geq 1-C^{1}_{d,p,r}e^{-\frac{C^{2}_{d,p,q,r}}{T^{\frac{1}{p}\wedge\left(\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{d}{r-p+1}\right)}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{M^{q,q}}^{2}}}.

Step 2:
u\in C([0,\mathcal{T}],L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
and it is an adapted process.
A priori, since we are working on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the regularity of
v
does not imply that
u\in C([0,\mathcal{T}],L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. However, arguing similarly to
3.2
and exploiting the regularity of

this can be proved. Since the proof of this claim is pretty similar to
3.2

\hat{z}(t):=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}^{\omega}\in C_{loc}([0,+\infty),L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.
and it is adapted. Therefore it is enough to show that the claim holds for
v=u-\hat{z}.
Let us introduce a radially symmetric cut off
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by

\displaystyle\chi(x):=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if }\lvert x\rvert\leq 1\\
0\quad\text{if }\rvert x\rvert\geq 2,\end{cases}\quad\chi(x)\geq 0,\quad\chi(x)\leq\chi(x^{\prime})\quad\text{if}\quad\lvert x\rvert\geq\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert.

Furthermore, for
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,R\geq 1
, define
\chi_{x_{0},R}(x):=\chi\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{R}\right).
Due to the properties of
v
, we have that for each
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,R\geq 1

(4.8)

\displaystyle v\chi_{x_{0},R}\in C([0,\mathcal{T}],L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\cap C([0,\mathcal{T}],L^{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\quad\mathbb{P}-a.s.,

and for each
t,t_{0}\in(0,\mathcal{T})
with
t_{0}\leq t

\displaystyle v(t)\chi_{x_{0},R}=\,

\displaystyle e^{(t-t_{0})\Delta}v(t_{0})\chi_{x_{0},R}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\left(v(s)\Delta\chi_{x_{0},R}-2\operatorname{div}(v(s)\nabla\chi_{x_{0},R})\right)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\chi_{x_{0},R}\lvert v(s)+\hat{z}(s)\rvert^{p-1}(v(s)+\hat{z}(s))ds,

in

Considering the

\displaystyle\left\|v(t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\lesssim\,

\displaystyle\left\|v(t_{0})\chi_{x_{0},R}\right\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-s}}\right)\left\|v(s)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left\|\chi_{x_{0},R}\lvert v(s)+\hat{z}(s)\rvert^{p-1}(v(s)+\hat{z}(s))\right\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}ds.

Here the hidden constant is independent of
R
,
t_{0},

t

By interpolation and Young’s inequality, since

\displaystyle\left\|v(t)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\lesssim\,

\displaystyle 1+\left\|v(t_{0})\chi_{x_{0},R}\right\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-s}}\right)\left\|v(s)\right\|_{L^{q}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}ds.

Possibly the hidden constant above depends on

Pointwise convergence of solutions of the Schrödinger equation along general curves on Damek-Ricci spaces
Utsav Dewan

Abstract
. Recently, the author considered the Carleson’s problem for the Schrödinger equation with radial initial data on Damek-Ricci spaces and obtained the sharp bound up to the endpoint β ≥ 1 / 4 \beta\geq 1/4 . Interpreting the above as convergence along vertical lines, in this article, we consider the problem of pointwise convergence via more general approach paths. 1.
Introduction

One of the most celebrated problems in Euclidean Harmonic analysis is the Carleson’s problem: determining the optimal regularity of the initial condition
f
of the
Schrödinger equation given by

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u\>,\>\>\>(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\>,\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\>,\end{cases}

in terms of the index
\beta
such that
f
belongs to the inhomogeneous Sobolev space
H^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so that the solution of the Schrödinger operator
u
converges pointwise to
f
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0+}u(x,t)=f(x)\>,\>\text{ almost everywhere}.

Such a problem was first studied by Carleson
[
Ca80
]
and Dahlberg-Kenig
[
DK82
]
in dimension
1
, followed by several other experts in the field for arbitrary dimension (see
[
Co83
,
Sj87
,
Ve88
,
Bo16
,
DGL17
,
DZ19
]
and references therein).

Recently, the author studied the Carleson’s problem for solutions of the Schrödinger equation corresponding to the Laplace-Beltrami operator in the setting of Damek-Ricci spaces
S

[
De24
]
. They are also known as Harmonic
NA
groups. These spaces
S
are non-unimodular, solvable extensions of Heisenberg type groups
N
, obtained by letting
A=\mathbb{R}^{+}
act on
N
by homogeneous dilations. The rank one Riemannian Symmetric spaces of noncompact type are the prototypical examples of (and in fact accounts for a very small subclass of the more general class of) Damek-Ricci spaces
[
ADY96
, p. 643]
. Let
\Delta
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator
on
S
corresponding to the left-invariant Riemannian metric. Its
L^{2}
-spectrum is the half line
(-\infty,-Q^{2}/4]
, where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of
N
. The Schrödinger equation on
S
is given by

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u\>,\>\>\>(x,t)\in S\times\mathbb{R}\>,\\
u(0,\cdot)=f\>,\>\text{ on }S\>.\end{cases}

Then for a radial function
f
belonging to the
L^{2}
-Schwartz class
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
(for the definition, see (
2.6
)),

(1.2)

S_{t}f(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\>e^{it\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>,

is the solution to (
1.1
), where
\varphi_{\lambda}
are the spherical functions,
\widehat{f}
is the Spherical Fourier transform of
f
and
{\bf c}(\cdot)
denotes the Harish-Chandra’s
{\bf c}
-function.

To quantify the Carleson’s problem on
S
, for
\beta\geq 0
, we recall the fractional
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces on
S
for the special case of radial functions
[
APV15
]
:

(1.3)

H^{\beta}(S):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(S):{\|f\|}_{H^{\beta}(S)}:={\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}^{\beta}{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}<\infty\right\}.

In
[
De24
]
, we have proved that if
f
is radial and belongs to the Sobolev space
H^{\beta}(S)
for
\beta\geq 1/4
, then the solution to the Schrödinger equation with initial data
f
converges to
f
, that is,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0+}S_{t}f(x)=f(x)\>,

for almost every
x\in S
, with respect to the left Haar measure. Moreover, the sharpness of the endpoint
\beta=1/4
has been established in a subsequent paper
[
DR24
]
. Interpreting the convergence (
1.4
) as convergence along vertical lines (in the product space
S\times(0,\infty)
), it is natural to ask what happens when the problem of (almost everywhere) pointwise convergence is considered along a wider approach region, for instance, the non-tangential convergence.

For
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this question regarding non-tangential convergence has been already addressed. On
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
\beta>n/2
, by Sobolev imbedding and standard arguments, the non-tangential convergence to the initial data holds. However, Sjögren and Sjölin showed in
[
SS89
]
that the non-tangential convergence fails for
\beta\leq n/2
. Now as their counter-example is a non-radial function, it is natural to ask when the initial data is more symmetric, say radial, then can we have pointwise convergence along wider approach regions. We answer this question in the negative by constructing a radial initial data on the
3
-dimensional Real Hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}\cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})/SU(2)
, whose Schrödinger propagation blows up on geodesic annuli along wide approach regions:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a strictly increasing continuous function with
\gamma(0)=0
. Then given any compact geodesic annulus
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{H}^{3}
, centered at the identity, there exists radial
f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
such that its Schrödinger propagation
u
is continuous in
\mathscr{A}\times(0,\infty)
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y,t)\to(x,0)\\
d(x,y)<\gamma(t),\>t>0\end{subarray}}|u(y,t)|=+\infty\>,\>\text{ for all }x\in\mathscr{A}\>.

Theorem
1.1
shows that the solutions of the Schrödinger equation, unlike Harmonic functions or solutions of the Heat equation, do not admit a natural wide approach region. Thus it becomes interesting to study the pointwise convergence of solutions along appropriate curves and its connection with the regularity of the initial data. Such problems have received considerable interest in recent years: in dimension one, by Cho-Lee-Vargas
[
CLV12
]
, Ding-Niu
[
DN17
]
and in higher dimensions, by Cao-Miao
[
CM23
]
, Minguillón
[
Mi24
]
.

In
[
CM23
]
and
[
Mi24
]
, the authors have shown that on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the condition
\beta>n/2(n+1)
is sufficient to obtain almost everywhere pointwise convergence along certain families of curves. Firstly, their arguments crucially use dilation and the explicit knowledge of its interaction with the Fourier transform. In the case of a Riemannian manifold, where the volume measure neither satisfies any doubling property nor does it admit dilation (such as Damek-Ricci spaces), the above mentioned Euclidean machineries work no longer and thus it offers a fresh challenge. Secondly, it is interesting to ask when the initial condition is more symmetric, for instance, radial, whether one can lower the above mentioned regularity threshold and yet have pointwise convergence.

In this light, we commence our exploration on Damek-Ricci spaces by identifying a natural family of curves to study. For
x\in S
, we consider curves
\gamma_{x}:\mathbb{R}\to S
such that
\gamma_{x}(0)=x
. Now as we will be exclusively dealing with radial initial data, it is both natural and customary to identify all the points
x
on a geodesic sphere centered at the identity
e
of
S
, solely by their geodesic distance, say
s
from
e
, that is,
s=d(e,x)\in[0,\infty)
. Here
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is the inner metric on
S
corresponding to the left-invariant Riemannian metric. Then the solution of the Schrödinger equation with initial data
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
, along a curve
\gamma_{s}
is given by,

S_{t}f(\gamma_{s}(t))=S_{t}f(d(e,\gamma_{s}(t)))=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(d(e,\gamma_{s}(t)))\>e^{it\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\>.

Again keeping the notion of radiality in mind, we consider curves that satisfy the following natural analogues of the conditions considered in
[
CLV12
]
: there exist constants
C_{j}\>(j=1,2,3)
, independent of
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,\infty)\>,\>t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\geq 0
, such that

\displaystyle(\mathscr{H}_{1})\>\>\>\>\>\>\left|\>d(e,\gamma_{s}(t))-d(e,\gamma_{s}(t^{\prime}))\>\right|\leq C_{1}\>{|t-t^{\prime}|}^{\alpha}\>,

\displaystyle(\mathscr{H}_{2})\>\>\>\>\>\>C_{2}\>|s-s^{\prime}|\leq\left|\>d(e,\gamma_{s}(t))-d(e,\gamma_{s^{\prime}}(t))\>\right|\leq C_{3}\>|s-s^{\prime}|\>.

We note that by the triangle inequality,
(\mathscr{H}_{1})
is weaker than the condition:

d\left(\gamma_{s}(t),\gamma_{s}(t^{\prime})\right)\leq C_{1}\>{|t-t^{\prime}|}^{\alpha}\>,

and hence
\alpha
is essentially the degree of tangential convergence.

Clearly, the above curves generalize the classical case of vertical lines
\gamma_{s}(t)=s
, studied in
[
De24
]
and thus one may expect to require more regularity on the initial data to guarantee pointwise convergence. Our result (Corollary
1.3
) however, shows that the regularity threshold
\beta\geq 1/4
(as in the case of vertical lines) is again sufficient to obtain almost everywhere pointwise convergence, even along such general curves. In fact, we prove more and this brings us to the definition of the homogeneous Sobolev spaces. For
\beta\geq 0
, the homogeneous Sobolev spaces for the special case of radial functions (corresponding to the shifted Laplace-Beltrami operator
\tilde{\Delta}:=\Delta+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}
) are defined as,

\dot{H}^{\beta}(S):=\left\{f\in L^{2}(S):{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{\beta}(S)}:={\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2\beta}\>{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}<\infty\right\}\>.

We now state our first result dealing with maximal estimates on annuli (in the following statement
A(\cdot)
denotes the density function, see section
2
for more details):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty
. Assume that
\gamma
satisfies
(\mathscr{H}_{1})
for
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
and
(\mathscr{H}_{2})
for
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,r_{2})
and
t,t^{\prime}\in[-T,T]
, for some fixed

(1.5)

0<T<{\left(\frac{C_{2}r_{1}}{2C_{1}}\right)}^{1/\alpha}\>.

Then we have for all
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
,

(1.6)

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptr_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle r_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left|S_{t}f(d(e,\gamma_{s}(t)))\right|\right)}^{2}A(s)\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\leq C\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>,

where the positive constant
C
depends only on
r_{1},r_{2}
and the dimension of
S
.

The key idea in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to keep track of the oscillation afforded by both the spherical functions as well as the multiplier corresponding to the Schrödinger operator. But this becomes a challenging task as in the generality of Damek-Ricci spaces, no explicit expression of
\varphi_{\lambda}
is known. In this regard, we crucially use a variation of the classical Harish-Chandra series expansion of
\varphi_{\lambda}
which is only valid in compacts not containing the group identity. This forces us to consider specific time localizations (
1.5
) and moreover, to take the rather unorthodox approach of obtaining maximal estimates on annuli, in stead of balls. The oscillation of
\varphi_{\lambda}
is realized by looking at the leading terms in the series expansion. The corresponding oscillatory parts of the linearized maximal function are taken care of by proceeding with an abstract
TT^{*}
argument, followed by an oscillatory integral estimate and then finally applying the Pitt’s inequality. The remaining part of the linearized maximal function is then dealt with by the pointwise decay of certain error terms appearing from the series expansion due to Anker et. al., which turns out to be of critical importance.

By standard arguments in the literature (for instance, see the proof of Theorem 5 of
[
Sj87
]
), Theorem
1.2
yields pointwise convergence for solutions of the Schrödinger operator on annuli,

A(r_{1},r_{2}):=\left\{x\in S\mid r_{1}<d(e,x)<r_{2}\right\}\>,

for all
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty\>.
Then as

A\left(\frac{1}{N},N\right)\>\big\uparrow\>S\setminus\{e\}\>,\>\text{ as }N\to\infty\>,

by a simple application of the Monotone Convergence Theorem, we get the desired result on pointwise convergence:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
. Suppose that for every
s_{0}\in[0,\infty)
, there exists a neighborhood
V

\subset[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
of
(s_{0},0)
such that
(\mathscr{H}_{1})
holds for
(s,t),(s,t^{\prime})\in V
and
(\mathscr{H}_{2})
holds for all
(s,t),(s^{\prime},t)\in V
. Then for all
f\in\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)_{o}
and hence for all
f\in H^{\beta}(S)_{o}
, with
\beta\geq 1/4
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}S_{t}f(\gamma_{s}(t))=f(s)\>,

for almost every
s\in[0,\infty)
, which also means

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}S_{t}f(\gamma_{x}(t))=f(x)\>,

for almost every
x\in S
, with respect to the left Haar measure.

Remark 1.4

The sharpness of the regularity threshold

in (

In this article, we include the case when

We now return to

: there exist constants

, independent of

\displaystyle(\mathscr{H}_{3})\>\>\>\>\>\>\left|\>\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|-\|\gamma_{s}(t^{\prime})\|\>\right|\leq C_{4}\>{|t-t^{\prime}|}^{\alpha}\>,

\displaystyle(\mathscr{H}_{4})\>\>\>\>\>\>C_{5}\>|s-s^{\prime}|\leq\left|\>\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|-\|\gamma_{s^{\prime}}(t)\|\>\right|\leq C_{6}\>|s-s^{\prime}|\>.

It is interesting to observe that the arguments in the proof of Theorem

can be carried out analogously for the special case of

Let

(\mathscr{H}_{3})

For the proof of Theorem

In the first case, the linearized maximal function is quite straightforward to control. The latter case however, is quite involved. In the large frequency regime, viewing

as the tangent space at the identity of a suitable Damek-Ricci space, we form a connection with Theorem

•

Using the fact that the Riemannian exponential map on a connected, simply-connected, complete, non-positively curved Riemannian manifold is a bijective local radial isometry, we can pushforward the curves in the small annuli (in

) to the non-flat manifold, preserving the local geometric conditions.

•

The connection between the initial data and their Schrödinger propagations for

Then Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some preliminaries and fix our notations.

2.1.
Fourier Analysis on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

In this subsection, we recall some Euclidean Fourier Analysis, most of which can be found in
[
Gr09
,
SW90
]
.
On
\mathbb{R}
, for “nice” functions
f
, the Fourier transform
\tilde{f}
is defined as

\tilde{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\>e^{-ix\xi}\>dx\>.

An important inequality in one-dimensional Fourier Analysis is the Pitt’s inequality:

Lemma 2.1
.

[
St56
, p. 489]

One has the inequality

{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left|\tilde{f}(\xi)\right|}^{2}\>{|\xi|}^{-2\beta}d\xi\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left|f(x)\right|}^{p}\>{|x|}^{\beta_{1}p}dx\right)}^{1/p}\>,

where
\beta_{1}=\beta+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
and the following two conditions are satisfied:

0\leq\beta_{1}<1-\frac{1}{p}\>,\>\text{ and }0\leq\beta<\frac{1}{2}\>.

A
C^{\infty}
function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
is called a Schwartz class function if

\left|{\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)}^{M}f(x)\right|\lesssim{(1+|x|)}^{-N}\>,\text{ for any }M,N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\>.

We denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
the class of all such functions and
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})_{e}
will denote the collection of all even Schwartz class functions on
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
Re(\beta)>0
. The Riesz potential of order
\beta
is the operator

I_{\beta}={(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}})}^{-\beta/2}\>.

One can also write as,

I_{\beta}(f)(x)=C_{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\>{|x-y|}^{\beta-1}\>dy\>,

for some
C_{\beta}>0
, whenever
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
. For
\beta>0
, the Fourier transform of the Riesz potential of
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies the following identity:

(2.1)

(I_{\beta}(f))^{\sim}(\xi)=C_{\beta}{|\xi|}^{-\beta}\tilde{f}(\xi)\>.

For a radial function
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(for
n\geq 2
), the Euclidean Spherical Fourier transform is defined as

\mathscr{F}f(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda s)\>s^{n-1}\>ds\>,

where for all
\mu\geq 0
,

\mathscr{J}_{\mu}(z)=2^{\mu}\>\pi^{1/2}\>\Gamma\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{J_{\mu}(z)}{z^{\mu}},

and
J_{\mu}
are the Bessel functions
[
SW90
, p. 154]
.

The class of radial Schwartz class functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denoted by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
, is defined to be the collection of
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
such that

\left|{\left(\frac{d}{ds}\right)}^{M}f(s)\right|\lesssim{(1+s)}^{-N}\>,\text{ for any }M,N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\>,

where
s=\|x\|
, is the distance of
x
from the origin.
\mathscr{F}:\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}\to\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})_{e}

defines a topological isomorphism.

2.2.
Damek-Ricci spaces and spherical Fourier Analysis thereon:

In this section, we will explain the notations and state relevant results on Damek-Ricci spaces. Most of these results can be found in
[
ADY96
,
APV15
,
As95
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a two-step real nilpotent Lie algebra equipped with an inner product
\langle,\rangle
. Let
\mathfrak{z}
be the center of
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{v}
its orthogonal complement. We say that
\mathfrak{n}
is an
H
-type algebra if for every
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
the map
J_{Z}:\mathfrak{v}\to\mathfrak{v}
defined by

\langle J_{z}X,Y\rangle=\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle,\>\>\>\>X,Y\in\mathfrak{v}

satisfies the condition
J_{Z}^{2}=-|Z|^{2}I_{\mathfrak{v}}
,
I_{\mathfrak{v}}
being the identity operator on
\mathfrak{v}
. A connected and simply connected Lie group
N
is called an
H
-type group if its Lie algebra is
H
-type. Since
\mathfrak{n}
is nilpotent, the exponential map is a diffeomorphism
and hence we can parametrize the elements in
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}
by
(X,Z)
, for
X\in\mathfrak{v},Z\in\mathfrak{z}
. It follows from the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula that the group law in
N
is given by

\left(X,Z\right)\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\right)=\left(X+X^{\prime},Z+Z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}[X,X^{\prime}]\right),\>\>\>\>X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{v};~Z,Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{z}.

The group
A=\mathbb{R}^{+}
acts on an
H
-type group
N
by nonisotropic dilation:
(X,Z)\mapsto(\sqrt{a}X,aZ)
. Let
S=NA
be the semidirect product of
N
and
A
under the above action. Thus the multiplication in
S
is given by

\left(X,Z,a\right)\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},a^{\prime}\right)=\left(X+\sqrt{a}X^{\prime},Z+aZ^{\prime}+\frac{\sqrt{a}}{2}[X,X^{\prime}],aa^{\prime}\right),

for
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{v};~Z,Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{z};a,a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Then
S
is a solvable, connected and simply connected Lie group having Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathbb{R}
with Lie bracket

\left[\left(X,Z,l\right),\left(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},l^{\prime}\right)\right]=\left(\frac{1}{2}lX^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}l^{\prime}X,lZ^{\prime}-lZ+[X,X^{\prime}],0\right).

We write
na=(X,Z,a)
for the element
\exp(X+Z)a,X\in\mathfrak{v},Z\in\mathfrak{z},a\in A
. We note
that for any
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
with
|Z|=1
,
J_{Z}^{2}=-I_{\mathfrak{v}}
; that is,
J_{Z}
defines a complex structure
on
\mathfrak{v}
and hence
\mathfrak{v}
is even dimensional.
m_{\mathfrak{v}}
and
m_{z}
will denote the dimension of
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{z}
respectively. Let
n
and
Q
denote dimension and the homogenous dimension of
S
respectively:

n=m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}+1\>\>\textit{ and }\>\>Q=\frac{m_{\mathfrak{v}}}{2}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}.

The group
S
is equipped with the left-invariant Riemannian metric induced by

\langle(X,Z,l),(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},l^{\prime})\rangle=\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle+\langle Z,Z^{\prime}\rangle+ll^{\prime}

on
\mathfrak{s}
. For
x\in S
, we denote by
s=d(e,x)
, that is, the geodesic distance of
x
from the identity
e
. Then the left Haar measure
dx
of the group
S
may be normalized so that

dx=A(s)\>ds\>d\sigma(\omega)\>,

where
A
is the density function given by,

(2.2)

A(s)=2^{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>{\left(\sinh(s/2)\right)}^{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>{\left(\cosh(s/2)\right)}^{m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>,

and
d\sigma
is the surface measure of the unit sphere.

A function
f:S\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be radial if, for all
x
in
S
,
f(x)
depends only on the geodesic distance of
x
from the identity
e
. If
f
is radial, then

\int_{S}f(x)~dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)~A(s)~ds\>.

We now recall the spherical functions on Damek-Ricci spaces. The spherical functions
\varphi_{\lambda}
on
S
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
are the radial eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
, satisfying the following normalization criterion

\begin{cases}&\Delta\varphi_{\lambda}=-\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)\varphi_{\lambda}\\
&\varphi_{\lambda}(e)=1\>.\end{cases}

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in S
, the spherical functions satisfy

\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=\varphi_{-\lambda}(s)\>.

It also satisfies for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and all
s\geq 0
:

(2.3)

\left|\varphi_{\lambda}(s)\right|\leq 1\>.

The spherical functions are crucial as they help us define the Spherical Fourier transform of a “nice” radial function
f
(on
S
) in the following way:

\widehat{f}(\lambda):=\int_{S}f(x)\varphi_{\lambda}(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s)\varphi_{\lambda}(s)A(s)ds\>.

The Harish-Chandra
{\bf c}
-function is defined as

{\bf c}(\lambda)=\frac{2^{(Q-2i\lambda)}\Gamma(2i\lambda)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{Q+2i\lambda}{2}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{m_{\mathfrak{v}}+4i\lambda+2}{4}\right)}\>,

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. We will need the following pointwise estimates (see
[
RS09
, Lemma 4.8]
):

(2.4)

{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\asymp\>{|\lambda|}^{2}{\left(1+|\lambda|\right)}^{n-3}\>,

and for
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the
derivative estimates (
[
As95
, Lemma 4.2]
):

(2.5)

\left|\frac{d^{j}}{d\lambda^{j}}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\right|\lesssim_{j}{(1+|\lambda|)}^{n-1-j}\>,\>\>\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

One has the following inversion formula (when valid) for radial functions:

f(x)=C\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\lambda)\varphi_{\lambda}(x){|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\>,

where
C
depends only on
m_{\mathfrak{v}}
and
m_{\mathfrak{z}}
. Moreover, the Spherical Fourier transform extends to an isometry from the space of radial
L^{2}
functions on
S
onto
L^{2}\left((0,\infty),C{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)
.

The class of radial
L^{2}
-Schwartz class functions on
S
, denoted by
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
, is defined to be the collection of
f\in C^{\infty}(S)_{o}
such that

(2.6)

\left|{\left(\frac{d}{ds}\right)}^{M}f(s)\right|\lesssim{(1+s)}^{-N}e^{-\frac{Q}{2}s}\>,\text{ for any }M,N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\>,

(see
[
ADY96
, p. 652]
). One has the following commutative diagram, where every map is a topological isomorphism:

•

is the Abel transform defined from
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
to

.

•

\wedge
denotes the Spherical Fourier transform from
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
to
{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})}_{e}
.

•

\sim
denotes the 1-dimensional Euclidean Fourier transform from
{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})}_{e}
to itself.

For more details regarding the Abel transform, we refer to
[
ADY96
, p. 652-653]
. These have been generalized to the setting of Chébli-Trimèche Hypergroups
[

.

For the purpose of our article, we will require both the Bessel series expansion as well as another series expansion (similar to the Harish-Chandra series expansion) of the spherical functions. We first see an expansion of
\varphi_{\lambda}
in terms of Bessel functions for points near the identity. But before that, let us define the following normalizing constant in terms of the Gamma functions,

c_{0}=2^{m_{\mathfrak{z}}}\>\pi^{-1/2}\>\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma((n-1)/2)}\>.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
As95
, Theorem 3.1]

There exist
R_{0},2<R_{0}<2R_{1}
, such that for any
0\leq s\leq R_{0}
, and any integer
M\geq 0
, and all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=c_{0}{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{M}a_{l}(s)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}+l}(\lambda s)s^{2l}+E_{M+1}(\lambda,s)\>,

where

a_{0}\equiv 1\>,\>|a_{l}(s)|\leq C{(4R_{1})}^{-l}\>,

and the error term has the following behaviour

\left|E_{M+1}(\lambda,s)\right|\leq C_{M}\begin{cases}s^{2(M+1)}&\text{ if }\>|\lambda s|\leq 1\\
s^{2(M+1)}{|\lambda s|}^{-\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+M+1\right)}&\text{ if }\>|\lambda s|>1\>.\end{cases}

Moreover, for every
0\leq s<2
, the series

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=c_{0}{\left(\frac{s^{n-1}}{A(s)}\right)}^{1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}a_{l}(s)\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}+l}(\lambda s)s^{2l}\>,

is absolutely convergent.

For the asymptotic behaviour of the spherical functions when the distance from the identity is large, we look at the following series expansion
[

, pp. 735-736]

(2.7)

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=2^{-m_{\mathfrak{z}}/2}{A(s)}^{-1/2}\left\{{\bf c}(\lambda)\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)e^{(i\lambda-\mu)s}+{\bf c}(-\lambda)\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=0}^{\infty}\Gamma_{\mu}(-\lambda)e^{-(i\lambda+\mu)s}\right\}\>.

The above series converges for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, uniformly on compacts not containing the group identity, where
\Gamma_{0}\equiv 1
and for
\mu\in\mathbb{N}
, one has the recursion formula,

(\mu^{2}-2i\mu\lambda)\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\mu-1}\omega_{\mu-j}\Gamma_{j}(\lambda)\>.

Then one has the following estimate on the coefficients
[

, Lemma 1]

(2.8)

\left|\Gamma_{\mu}(\lambda)\right|\leq C\mu^{d}{\left(1+|\lambda|\right)}^{-1}\>,

for all

.

3.

In this section we make use of several standard notions in Harmonic Analysis on Riemannian Symmetric spaces. For unexplained terminologies and more details, we refer to
[

]
.

Let
G=SL(2,\mathbb{C})
and

be its maximal compact subgroup

and
\Sigma_{+}
consists of a single root

that occurs with multiplicity

we get the following information for

•

We now present the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

The compact geodesic annulus

is of the form,

Indeed, fixing

. Then as
\gamma
is continuous and strictly increasing, there exists

such that
\gamma(t_{j_{1}}^{\prime})=d(x,x_{j_{1}})
. We now set

\left|F^{\prime}_{1}(\lambda)\right|\geq 2|t_{k}-t_{j}|\lambda-(s_{k}+s_{j})>|t_{k}-t_{j}|\lambda>|t_{k}-t_{j}|r_{j}\>.

Now an integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{j}}\lambda^{-1}\>{\left(\log\lambda\right)}^{-3/4}\>e^{iF_{1}(\lambda)}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{j}}^{R_{j}}\frac{1}{\lambda\>{\left(\log\lambda\right)}^{3/4}iF^{\prime}_{1}(\lambda)}iF^{\prime}_{1}(\lambda)\>e^{iF_{1}(\lambda)}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{\lambda\>{\left(\log\lambda\right)}^{3/4}iF^{\prime}_{1}(\lambda)}\>e^{iF_{1}(\lambda)}\right]^{R_{j}}_{r_{j}}-\int_{r_{j}}^{R_{j}}\frac{d}{d\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda\>{\left(\log\lambda\right)}^{3/4}iF^{\prime}_{1}(\lambda)}\right)\>e^{iF_{1}(\lambda)}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I-J\>.

Now invoking (
3.2
) and (

), it follows that

Using (
3

Hence,

|J|\leq\frac{2}{|t_{k}-t_{j}|r^{2}_{j}}\leq\left(\frac{2}{c_{6}}\right)2^{-j}\>.

Thus setting,
c_{7}=4/c_{6}
, we note that for
j>k
,

\left|A_{j}(s_{k},t_{k})\right|\leq c_{7}2^{-j}\>.

Therefore for
m>k

\left|u_{m,1}(s_{k},t_{k})\right|\lesssim{\left(\log r_{k}\right)}^{1/4}+r^{-1}_{k}\>{\left(\log r_{k}\right)}^{-3/4}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=k+1}^{m}2^{-j}\lesssim{\left(\log r_{k}\right)}^{1/4}\>.

Similarly, we get that

\left|u_{m,4}(s_{k},t_{k})\right|\lesssim{\left(\log r_{k}\right)}^{1/4}\>.

We now consider

\displaystyle\left|\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{e^{i(t_{k}-t_{j})\rho^{2}}}{\sinh(s_{k})\sinh(s_{j})}B_{j}(s_{k},t_{k})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\left(\log r_{k}\right)}^{1/4}\>.

Next, we see that for

sufficiently large,

\displaystyle B_{k}(s_{k},t_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{k}}^{R_{k}}\lambda^{-1}\>{\left(\log\lambda\right)}^{-3/4}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\left({\left(\log R_{k}\right)}^{1/4}-{\left(\log r_{k}\right)}^{1/4}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\left(1-\frac{1}{M^{1/4}}\right){\left(\log R_{k}\right)}^{1/4}

\displaystyle{\left(\log R_{k}\right)}^{1/4}\>.

j>k

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{2}(\lambda)=(s_{k}-s_{j})+2(t_{k}-t_{j})\lambda\>,

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}_{2}(\lambda)=2(t_{k}-t_{j})\>.

or

we would get that

\displaystyle\left|\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{e^{i(t_{k}-t_{j})\rho^{2}}}{\sinh(s_{k})\sinh(s_{j})}D_{j}(s_{k},t_{k})\right|\lesssim{\left(\log r_{k}\right)}^{1/4}\>,

\displaystyle D_{k}(s_{k},t_{k})\gtrsim{\left(\log R_{k}\right)}^{1/4}\>,

m>k
and

sufficiently large, using the decomposition (

. We can therefore write the last integral as an one dimensional Riesz potential and apply (
2.1
) to get that for some positive number
c
,

\displaystyle\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>|h(s)|\>|h(s^{\prime})|\>ds\>ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{|s-s^{\prime}|}^{1/2}}\>|h(s)|\>|h(s^{\prime})|\>ds\>ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{1/2}(|h|)(s)\>|h(s)|\>ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\int_{\mathbb{R}}{|\xi|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\>{\left|\tilde{h}(\xi)\right|}^{2}\>d\xi\>.

Then by Pitt’s inequality (as in our case
\beta=\frac{1}{4},p=2
and hence
\beta_{1}=\beta+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{4}
, both the conditions of Lemma
2.1
are satisfied),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{|\xi|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\>{\left|\tilde{h}(\xi)\right|}^{2}\>d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{|h(x)|}^{2}\>{|x|}^{\frac{1}{2}}\>dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}{|h(s)|}^{2}\>s^{\frac{1}{2}}\>ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\>r^{1/2}_{2}\>{\|h\|}^{2}_{{L^{2}}(r_{1},r_{2})}\>.

Thus we get (
4.7
). Similarly,

(4.10)

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptr_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle r_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|T_{\gamma,2}f(s)\right|}^{2}A(s)\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{1/2}\>{|\widehat{f}(\lambda)|}^{2}{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}\>.

Finally for
T_{\gamma,3}
, using the estimate on the error term (
4.6
) and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|T_{\gamma,3}f(s)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{A(s)}^{-1/2}\>\int_{0}^{\infty}{(1+\lambda)}^{-1}\>\left|\widehat{f}(\lambda)\right|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-1}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{A(s)}^{-1/2}\>\left[\int_{0}^{1}\left|\widehat{f}(\lambda)\right|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-1}\>d\lambda+\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-1}\>\left|\widehat{f}(\lambda)\right|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-1}\>d\lambda\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{A(s)}^{-1/2}\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>\left[{\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{1/2}}\right)}^{1/2}+{\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{5/2}}\right)}^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{A(s)}^{-1/2}\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>.

Thus

(4.11)

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptr_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle r_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|T_{\gamma,3}f(s)\right|}^{2}A(s)\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim{(r_{2}-r_{1})}^{1/2}\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>.

Then combining (
4.7
), (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we complete the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In the special case of
\mathbb{H}^{3}(-1)
, the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
-invariant Riemannian measure on
\mathbb{H}^{3}(-1)
is
\sinh^{2}s\>ds\>dk
, where
ds
and
dk
are the Lebesgue measure on
(0,\infty)
and the Haar measure on
SU(2)
respectively. As noted in section
3
, in this case, the spherical function is given by the explicit expression:

\varphi_{\lambda}(s)=\frac{1}{2i\sinh(s)}\left(\frac{e^{i\lambda s}}{\lambda}+\frac{e^{-i\lambda s}}{\lambda}\right)\>,

and hence the proof of Theorem
1.2
can be carried out with relative ease (as the corresponding error term is zero). The situation is exactly similar in the classical
3
-dimensional Euclidean space, as the spherical function on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by,

\frac{\sin(\lambda s)}{\lambda s}=\frac{1}{2is}\left(\frac{e^{i\lambda s}}{\lambda}+\frac{e^{-i\lambda s}}{\lambda}\right)\>.

In the full generality of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
however, the spherical function is somewhat lesser explicit and is given by
\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}
, the modified Bessel function of order
\frac{n-2}{2}
. Although the oscillatory nature of
\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}
is well-studied, yet proceeding analogously as in the arguments of the proof of Theorem
1.2
does not seem to be an easy task.

Aiming to eventually obtain some Euclidean results, we now focus on ‘small annuli’. Let

0<\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}\leq(2R_{0})/(3C_{3})\>,

where
R_{0}
is as in Lemma
2.2
. As in Theorem
1.2
, we also assume that
\gamma
satisfies
(\mathscr{H}_{1})
for
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
and
(\mathscr{H}_{2})
for
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,\delta_{2})
and
t,t^{\prime}\in[-T,T]
, for some fixed
T>0
, such that

T^{\alpha}<(C_{2}\delta_{1})/(2C_{1})\>.

Then by Lemma
4.1
, we have for all
s\in(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
,

d(e,\gamma_{s}(t))<\frac{3C_{3}}{2}s<\frac{3C_{3}}{2}\delta_{2}\leq R_{0}\>.

Thus for
f\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
, we can also decompose the linearized maximal function (

) in terms of the Bessel series expansion of
\varphi_{\lambda}
(by taking

in Lemma

),

\displaystyle T_{\gamma}f(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{\lambda}(d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s))))\>e^{it(s)\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}{\left(\frac{d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))^{n-1}}{A(d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s))))}\right)}^{1/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s))))\>e^{it(s)\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}E(\lambda,d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s))))\>e^{it(s)\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{Q^{2}}{4}\right)}\>\widehat{f}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

We obtain the following boundedness result for

, which will be crucial for the proof of Theorem

in the next section:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let

, where

. Assume that
\gamma

(\mathscr{H}_{1})
for

(\mathscr{H}_{2})

and

, for some fixed

, such that

. Then we have for all

,

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|T_{\gamma,4}f(s)\right|}^{2}A(s)\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>,

where the implicit constant depends only on
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
and the dimension of

.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to prove that

(4.12)

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|T_{\gamma,5}f(s)\right|}^{2}A(s)\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>,

where the implicit constant depends only on
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
and the dimension of
S
. For
\lambda\geq 0
, we recall the estimates on the error term in Lemma
2.2
(for

),

\left|E(\lambda,d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s))))\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))^{2}&\text{ if }\>\lambda d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))\leq 1\\
d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))^{2}\>\{\lambda d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))\}^{-\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}&\text{ if }\>\lambda d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))>1\>.\end{cases}

Then using the above pointwise bounds, Lemma
4.1
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|T_{\gamma,5}f(s)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))^{2}\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1/d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))$}}$}}\nolimits\left|\widehat{f}(\lambda)\right|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s))))^{\left(\frac{n-3}{2}\right)}}\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt1/d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\infty$}}$}}\nolimits\lambda^{-\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}\left|\widehat{f}(\lambda)\right|\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\left[s^{2}I_{1}(s)^{1/2}+\frac{1}{s^{\left(\frac{n-3}{2}\right)}}I_{2}(s)^{1/2}\right]\>,

where

I_{1}(s)=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1/d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda}{\lambda^{1/2}}\>,\>\>\text{ and }\>\>I_{2}(s)=\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt1/d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\infty$}}$}}\nolimits\frac{{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda}{\lambda^{n+\frac{3}{2}}}\>.

Now first using the asymptotics of
{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}
given in (

) for small frequency and Lemma

, we get that

I_{1}(s)\lesssim\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt0$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle 1/d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))$}}$}}\nolimits\lambda^{3/2}\>d\lambda\>\lesssim\>\frac{1}{s^{5/2}}\>.

Similarly the asymptotics of
{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}
given in (

) for large frequency and Lemma

yield

I_{2}(s)\lesssim\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt1/d(e,\gamma_{s}(t(s)))$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\infty$}}$}}\nolimits\>\>\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{5/2}}\>\lesssim\>s^{3/2}\>.

Hence, for
s\in(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
,

\left|T_{\gamma,5}f(s)\right|\lesssim{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>s^{\left(\frac{9-2n}{4}\right)}\>,

and thus using the local growth asymptotics of the density function, we obtain

\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|T_{\gamma,5}f(s)\right|}^{2}A(s)\>ds\lesssim{\|f\|}^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits s^{7/2}\>ds\>\lesssim_{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}}\>{\|f\|}^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}\>.

This completes the proof of Corollary
4.3
.
∎

5.
Results on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Our aim in this section is to prove Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
.

For
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
, with
n\geq 2
, the linearization of the maximal function appearing in Theorem
1.5
is given by,

(5.1)

\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda\|\gamma_{s}(t(s))\|)\>e^{it(s)\lambda^{2}}\>\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda\>,

where
t(\cdot):(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\to[-T,T]
is a measurable function. We first consider the case when
\mathscr{F}f
is supported near the origin.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.5
, we have for all
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
with
Supp\left(\mathscr{F}f\right)\subset[0,\Lambda]
for some
\Lambda>0
,

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f(s)\right|}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>,

where the implicit constant depends only on
\Lambda,\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
and
n
.

Proof.

The Lemma follows from the boundedness of the modified Bessel functions and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Indeed, for
s\in(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
,

\left|\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f(s)\right|\lesssim\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\left|\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\right|\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda\leq{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}{\left(\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\lambda^{n-\frac{3}{2}}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim_{\Lambda}{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>.

Hence, the result follows.
∎

We now turn our attention to the situation when
\mathscr{F}f
is supported away from the origin. In this case, we need to do some local geometry. For each
n\geq 2
, there exists at least one
n
-dimensional Damek-Ricci space
S
(in particular, the degenerate case of Real hyperbolic spaces).

We now consider the Riemannian exponential map at
e
, denoted by
\exp_{e}
defined on
T_{e}S
, the tangent space of
S
at the identity
e
. As Damek-Ricci spaces are connected, simply-connected, complete Riemannian manifolds of non-positive sectional curvature,
\exp_{e}
defines a global diffeomorphism from
T_{e}S
onto
S
. Now for a general Riemannian manifold,
\exp_{e}
is always a local radial isometry. Hence, there exists
\varepsilon>0
, such that
\exp_{e}
maps the ball
\mathcal{B}(0,\varepsilon)\subset T_{e}S
(with respect to the norm
\tilde{d}(\cdot)
induced by the Riemannian metric) diffeomorphically onto the geodesic ball
\mathscr{B}(e,\varepsilon)\subset S
, with the property that for any
p\in\mathcal{B}(0,\varepsilon)
, we have

(5.2)

d(e,exp_{e}(p))=\tilde{d}(p)\>,

(see
[
HRWW
, p. 636]
). Moreover, since any two finite dimensional inner product spaces with the same dimension, are isometrically isomorphic, there exists a linear isometry
\mathcal{I}
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the flat metric onto
T_{e}S
equipped with the Riemannian metric. Hence for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(5.3)

\|x\|=\tilde{d}\left(\mathcal{I}(x)\right)\>.

We now restrict to the Euclidean ball
B(o,\varepsilon)
and consider the composition,

\mathcal{E}:=\exp_{e}\circ\>\mathcal{I}\>.

Thus combining (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), it follows that
\mathcal{E}
maps the ball
B(o,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
diffeomorphically onto the geodesic ball
\mathscr{B}(e,\varepsilon)\subset S
, with the property that for any
x\in B(o,\varepsilon)
, we have,

(5.4)

d\left(e,\mathcal{E}(x)\right)=\|x\|\>.

As we are only interested in radial functions, identifying all the points on a geodesic sphere centered at
e
, contained in the geodesic ball
\mathscr{B}(e,\varepsilon)
, solely by their distance from
e
and using (
5.4
), we make the abuse of notation,

(5.5)

\mathcal{E}(x)\sim d\left(e,\mathcal{E}(x)\right)=\|x\|,\>\>\text{ for }x\in B(o,\varepsilon).

Now under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.5
, a computation similar to Lemma
4.1
using
(\mathscr{H}_{3})
and
(\mathscr{H}_{4})
yields that for all
s\in[0,\delta_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
,

\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|<\frac{3C_{6}}{2}\delta_{2}\>.

Then furthermore assuming
\delta_{2}\leq(2\varepsilon)/(3C_{6})
, we get that
\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|<\varepsilon
, for all
s\in[0,\delta_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
. Hence it makes sense to consider the pushforwards (by the map
\mathcal{E}
) of the curves
\gamma
for
s\in[0,\delta_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
, which we write by (
5.5
) as,

{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s}(t):=\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s}(t))\>.

We now summarize some important properties of these pushforwarded curves:

Lemma 5.2
.

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.5
, with
\delta_{2}\leq(2\varepsilon)/(3C_{6})
, where
\varepsilon
is as in (
5.4
), for
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,\delta_{2})
and
t,t^{\prime}\in[-T,T]
, we have for
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
,

\displaystyle(i)\left|d\left(e,{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s}(t)\right)-d\left(e,{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s}(t^{\prime})\right)\right|\leq C_{4}{|t-t^{\prime}|}^{\alpha}\>,

\displaystyle(ii)\>C_{5}|s-s^{\prime}|\leq\left|d\left(e,{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s}(t)\right)-d\left(e,{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s^{\prime}}(t)\right)\right|\leq C_{6}|s-s^{\prime}|\>.

Proof.

Using (
5.4
) and
(\mathscr{H}_{3})
, property
(i)
is proved as follows,

\displaystyle\left|d\left(e,{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s}(t)\right)-d\left(e,{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s}(t^{\prime})\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|d\left(e,\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s}(t))\right)-d\left(e,\mathcal{E}(\gamma_{s}(t^{\prime}))\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|-\|\gamma_{s}(t^{\prime})\|\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}{|t-t^{\prime}|}^{\alpha}\>.

Property
(ii)
can be verified similarly.
∎

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f(s)\right|}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>,

where the implicit constant depends only on
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}
and
n
.

Proof.

Let

\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)^{\infty}_{o}=\{g\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}\mid\widehat{g}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\infty}_{e}\},

so that
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)^{\infty}_{o}:=\wedge^{-1}\left(\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\infty}_{e}\right)
. Now as the Spherical Fourier transform
\wedge
is a bijection from
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)_{o}
onto
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})_{e}
, its restriction also defines a bijection from
\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)^{\infty}_{o}
onto
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\infty}_{e}
. Then combining (
5.6
), (
5.7
) and the fact that
Supp\left(\mathscr{F}(f)\right)\subset(1,\infty)
, we define

g:=\left(\mathscr{A}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{M}^{-1}\right)f\>.

The interaction of
g
with the Fourier transform is best understood via the following commutative diagram, where each arrow is a bijection:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\infty}_{o}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\infty}_{o}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)^{\infty}_{o}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})}^{\infty}_{e}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})}^{\infty}_{e}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{A}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wedge}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{m}^{-1}}

Thus
g\in\mathscr{S}^{2}(S)^{\infty}_{o}
with

(5.8)

\widehat{g}(\lambda)=\mathscr{F}\left(\mathcal{M}^{-1}f\right)(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^{n-1}}{{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}}\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\>.

So in particular,
Supp(\widehat{g})\subset(1,\infty)
.

We also note that using the hypothesis, a computation similar to Lemma
4.1
implies that
\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|<\min\{\varepsilon,R_{0}\}
, for all
s\in[0,\delta_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
. Hence by (
5.4
), we see that

(5.9)

d\left(e,{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s}(t)\right)=\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|<\min\{\varepsilon,R_{0}\}\>.

Then for
s\in(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
, using Lemmata
5.2
and
4.1
, the local growth asymptotics of the density function of
S
(
2.2
), the relationship between
\widehat{g}
and
\mathscr{F}f
(
5.8
) and the isometric relation (
5.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|T_{\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma,4}\>g(s)\right|

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\left|\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\lambda\>d\left(e,{\left(\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma\right)}_{s}(t(s))\right)\right)\>e^{it(s)\lambda^{2}}\>\widehat{g}(\lambda)\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\lambda\>\|\gamma_{s}(t(s))\|\right)\>e^{it(s)\lambda^{2}}\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f(s)\right|\>.

Thus again by the local growth asymptotics of the density function of
S
, it follows that

(5.10)

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|T_{\mathcal{E}_{*}\gamma,4}\>g(s)\right|}^{2}A(s)\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\asymp{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f(s)\right|}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\>.

Using (

) and the large frequency asymptotics of
{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}
(

(5.11)

\displaystyle{\|g\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(S)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{1/2}\>{\left|\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\right|}^{2}\>\frac{\lambda^{2(n-1)}}{{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-4}}\>{|{\bf c}(\lambda)|}^{-2}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
. Then
\mathscr{F}f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})_{e}
. Now let us choose an auxiliary non-negative even function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
Supp(\psi)\subset\{\xi:\frac{1}{2}<|\xi|<2\}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(2^{-k}\xi)=1\>,\>\xi\neq 0\>.

We set

\psi_{1}(\xi):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}\psi(2^{-k}\xi)\>,\>\text{ and }\psi_{2}(\xi):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\psi(2^{-k}\xi)\>.

Then both
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are even non-negative
C^{\infty}
functions with
Supp(\psi_{1})\subset(-2,2)
,
Supp(\psi_{2})\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)
and
\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}\equiv 1
. Then we consider,

\mathscr{F}f=\left(\mathscr{F}f\right)\psi_{1}+\left(\mathscr{F}f\right)\psi_{2}\>.

As
\left(\mathscr{F}f\right)\psi_{j}
for
j=1,2
, are even Schwartz class functions on
\mathbb{R}
, by the Schwartz isomorphism theorem, there exist
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
such that
\mathscr{F}f_{j}=\left(\mathscr{F}f\right)\psi_{j}
for
j=1,2
. Then by Lemmata
5.1
and
5.3
, we get the desired estimate on the linearized maximal function,

\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f(s)\right|}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f_{1}(s)\right|}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}+{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left|\tilde{T}_{\gamma}f_{2}(s)\right|}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{0}^{2}\lambda^{1/2}\>{\left|\mathscr{F}f_{1}(\lambda)\right|}^{2}\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}+{\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{1/2}\>{\left|\mathscr{F}f_{2}(\lambda)\right|}^{2}\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

By arguments mentioned in the Introduction, in order to prove the result on almost everywhere pointwise convergence, it suffices to prove that given any
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty
, there exists some fixed
T>0
, such that one has for all
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
, the maximal estimate,

(5.12)

{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptr_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle r_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left|\tilde{S}_{t}f(\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|)\right|\right)}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}\lesssim\>{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>,

where the implicit constant can possibly depend only on
r_{1},r_{2}
and
n
. We first note that by compactness of
[0,r_{2}]
, there exists
0<T<{\left(r_{1}/2C_{7}\right)}^{2}
, such that

\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|=s+C_{7}t^{1/2}\>,

for all
s\in[0,r_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
. Then for all
s\in[0,r_{2})
and
t,t^{\prime}\in[-T,T]
,

\left|\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|-\|\gamma_{s}(t^{\prime})\|\right|=C_{7}|t^{1/2}-{t^{\prime}}^{1/2}|\leq C_{7}{|t-{t^{\prime}}|}^{1/2}\>.

Also for all
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,r_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
,

\left|\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|-\|\gamma_{s^{\prime}}(t)\|\right|=|s-s^{\prime}|\>.

Therefore,
\gamma
satisfies
(\mathscr{H}_{3})
for
\alpha=1/2
and
(\mathscr{H}_{4})
for
s,s^{\prime}\in[0,r_{2})
and
t,t^{\prime}\in[-T,T]
, for some fixed
0<T<{\left(r_{1}/2C_{7}\right)}^{2}
.

Let
\delta\in(0,r_{2})
be sufficiently small and set
\eta=r_{2}/\delta
and
\delta^{\prime}=r_{1}/\eta
. For
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})_{o}
, we consider

f_{\eta}(x):=f(\eta x)\>.

Now the solutions of the Schrödinger equation with initial data
f
and
f_{\eta}
are related as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{t}f(\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\lambda\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|\right)e^{it\lambda^{2}}\>\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\lambda\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|\right)e^{it\lambda^{2}}\>\mathscr{F}f_{\eta}(\eta\lambda)\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\lambda\frac{\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|}{\eta}\right)e^{i\left(\frac{t}{\eta^{2}}\right)\lambda^{2}}\>\mathscr{F}f_{\eta}(\lambda)\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda\>.

Now for
s\in[0,r_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
, we have

\frac{\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|}{\eta}=\frac{s}{\eta}+C_{7}\frac{t^{1/2}}{\eta}=\frac{s}{\eta}+C_{7}{\left(\frac{t}{\eta^{2}}\right)}^{1/2}=\|\gamma_{s/\eta}(t/\eta^{2})\|\>.

Therefore, for
s\in[0,r_{2})
and
t\in[-T,T]
,

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{t}f(\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(\lambda\|\gamma_{s/\eta}(t/\eta^{2})\|\right)e^{i\left(\frac{t}{\eta^{2}}\right)\lambda^{2}}\>\mathscr{F}f_{\eta}(\lambda)\>\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{t/\eta^{2}}f_{\eta}(\|\gamma_{s/\eta}(t/\eta^{2})\|)\>.

Then as
T<{\left(r_{1}/2C_{7}\right)}^{2}
, we have

{\left(\frac{T}{\eta^{2}}\right)}^{1/2}<\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{2C_{7}}\>,

and hence by Theorem
1.5
,

\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptr_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle r_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left|\tilde{S}_{t}f(\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|)\right|\right)}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptr_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle r_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left|\tilde{S}_{t/\eta^{2}}f_{\eta}(\|\gamma_{s/\eta}(t/\eta^{2})\|)\right|\right)}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptr_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle r_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\left[-\frac{T}{\eta^{2}},\frac{T}{\eta^{2}}\right]}\left|\tilde{S}_{t}f_{\eta}(\|\gamma_{s/\eta}(t)\|)\right|\right)}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{n/2}\>{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911pt\delta^{\prime}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\delta$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\left[-\frac{T}{\eta^{2}},\frac{T}{\eta^{2}}\right]}\left|\tilde{S}_{t}f_{\eta}(\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|)\right|\right)}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\eta^{n/2}\>{\|f_{\eta}\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>.

We next note that

\displaystyle{\|f_{\eta}\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{1/2}\>{\left|\mathscr{F}f_{\eta}(\lambda)\right|}^{2}\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{1/2}\>{\left|\mathscr{F}f(\lambda)\right|}^{2}\lambda^{n-1}\>d\lambda\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>,

which we plug in the above to obtain

\displaystyle{\left(\mathop{\text{\large$\int_{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle\kern-1.22911ptr_{1}$}}^{\text{\normalsize$\scriptstyle r_{2}$}}$}}\nolimits{\left(\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\left|\tilde{S}_{t}f(\|\gamma_{s}(t)\|)\right|\right)}^{2}\>s^{n-1}\>ds\right)}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\eta^{\frac{1}{4}}{\|f\|}_{\dot{H}^{1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\>.

Thus we obtain (
5.12
), which completes the proof.
∎

6.
Concluding remarks

In this section, we make some remarks and pose some new problems:

(1)

On
\mathbb{H}^{3}(-1)\cong SL(2,\mathbb{C})/SU(2)
, for
\beta>3/2
, by Sobolev imbedding
[
CGM93
, Theorem 4.7]
and standard arguments, the non-tangential convergence to the initial data holds almost everywhere. Whereas by our counter-example (Theorem
1.1
), the non-tangential convergence to the initial data fails on a set of positive (
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
-invariant) Riemannian measure if
\beta\leq 1/2
. This gives rise to the natural question of what happens when
\beta\in(1/2,3/2]
. The analogue of Theorem
1.1
and the above question in the great generality of Damek-Ricci spaces turns out to be even more interesting.

(2)

In this article, the two Euclidean results (Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
) only give partial analogues of Theorem
1.2
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus seeking a complete analogue of Theorem
1.2
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a natural question.

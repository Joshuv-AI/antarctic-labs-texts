Elliptic Virtual Structure Constants and Gromov-Witten Invariants for Complete Intersections in Weighted Projective Space
Masao Jinzenji, Ken Kuwata, e-mail address: pcj70e4e@okayama-u.ac.jp, kuwata-k@t.kagawa-nct.ac.jp

Abstract
In this paper, we generalize our formalism of the elliptic virtual structure constants to hypersurfaces and complete intersections within certain weighted projective spaces possessing a single Kähler class.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we generalize the definition of elliptic virtual structure constants for projective hypersurfaces, as presented in
[
9
]
, to encompass hypersurfaces and complete intersections in specific weighted projective spaces with one Kähler class. We then compute their genus 1 (elliptic) Gromov-Witten invariants. The guideline for this generalization stems from our group’s previous works
[
6
,
10
]
, which detail the construction of genus 0 virtual structure constants for the K3 surface in the weighted projective space
P(1,1,1,3)
.
However, akin to the situation in
[
9
]
, our definition lacks a rigorous geometrical construction of the expected moduli space of quasimaps from an elliptic curve to weighted projective spaces. Consequently, we only explicitly write down the integrands of the residue integrals associated with the graph types (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv) introduced in
[
9
]
(the necessary graph types remain the same as for projective hypersurfaces). A primary motivation for this generalization is to validate our conjecture by comparing the results obtained from our formalism for certain complex
3
-dimensional Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces in weighted projective spaces with one Kähler class against the corresponding results derived from the original BCOV formalism
[
3
]
.
Let
P(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m})
denote a complete intersection of degree
k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}
homogeneous polynomials within the weighted projective space

P(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N})
with a single Kähler class. The significant findings resulting from this generalization can be summarized in the following two points:

(i)

The nontrivial part:

\left(-\frac{N-1}{N}\frac{1}{w^{N}}-\frac{N+1}{N}\frac{1}{(z_{0})^{N}}\right),

which appeared in the integrand associated with the type (iii) graph (see
[
9
]
), is
modified to

\left(-\frac{N-m}{N}\frac{1}{w^{N}}-\frac{N+m}{N}\frac{1}{(z_{0})^{N}}\right).

(ii)

The symmetric factor:

R_{N,k}(d):=\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)\frac{1}{d}-\left(N-\frac{1}{k}\right)\frac{1}{d^{2}},

in
[
9
]
associated with the type (iv) graph is modified to

R(d):=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\right)\left(\left(\frac{N-m}{2}\right)\frac{1}{d}-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{a_{j}}-\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k_{l}}\right)\frac{1}{d^{2}}\right).

The remaining generalizations follow straightforwardly from the results presented in
[
9
]
and our prior findings concerning the K3 surface in the weighted projective space
P(1,1,1,3)

[
6
,
10
]
.

This paper unfolds as follows:
First, in Section
2
, we lay the groundwork by reviewing essential properties of weighted projective spaces and the complete intersections they contain, drawing upon the established literature
[
1
,
2
,
11
]
.
Moving on to Section
3
, we then elucidate the theoretical framework we employ – our formalism detailed in
[
6
]
– for the computation of Gromov-Witten invariants through the lens of virtual structure constants.
Subsequently, Section
4
provides a precise definition of the elliptic virtual structure constant. This definition takes the form of a sum of residue integrals, where each integrand corresponds to one of the four graph types originally introduced in
[
9
]
.
In Section
5
, we put our formalism to the test by presenting a range of numerical results. We begin by examining the genus 1 Gromov-Witten invariants of Fano hypersurfaces within specific weighted projective spaces. Following this, we turn our attention to Calabi-Yau 3-folds residing in the weighted projective space previously studied in
[
3
]
. Notably, our findings regarding the count of elliptic (and rational) curves for these Calabi-Yau manifolds are consistent with those reported in
[
3
]
. We then extend our analysis to complete intersections within standard projective space and, finally, to complete intersections in various weighted projective spaces.
Lastly, for the interested reader, Appendix A offers a formal proof of a proposition concerning the vanishing of residue integrals associated with type (iii) graphs, specifically in the context of Calabi-Yau manifolds.

2
Complete Intersection in Weighted Projective Space

In this section, we summarize the properties of weighted projective space and complete intersections within it. First, we introduce the weighted projective space
P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N})
, where
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N}
are positive integers.

From this point onward, we restrict
a_{1}
to be
1
and assume that the greatest common divisor of
a_{2},a_{3},\dots,a_{N}
is
1
. Let
(x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{N})
be the coordinates on
{\bf C}^{N}
. We define an action of the multiplicative group of complex numbers,
{\bf C}^{\times}
, on
{\bf C}^{N}-\{\mathbf{0}\}
as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda\cdot(x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{N})=(\lambda x^{1},\lambda^{a_{2}}x^{2},\dots,\lambda^{a_{N}}x^{N})\quad(\lambda\in{\bf C}^{\times}).

(2.1)

Then,
{P}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N})
is defined as the orbit space of this
{\bf C}^{\times}
action:

\displaystyle{P}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N})=({\bf C}^{N}-\{\mathbf{0}\})/{\bf C}^{\times}.

(2.2)

We denote a point in
P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N})
represented by
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})\in{\bf C}^{N}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
as
(x_{1}:x_{2}:\dots:x_{N})
.

In general,
P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N})
can have singularities. Information about its singular locus can be obtained from the toric construction of the weighted projective space. It is constructed as a toric variety associated with a polytope
\Delta^{*}\subset{\bf R}^{N-1}
. The polytope
\Delta^{*}
is given as the convex hull of the following
N
points in
{\bf R}^{N-1}
:

\displaystyle v_{1}

\displaystyle=(-a_{2},-a_{3},\dots,-a_{N}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle v_{i}

\displaystyle=e_{i-1}\quad(i=2,\dots,N),

(2.4)

where
e_{i-1}
is the
(i-1)
-th standard basis vector of
{\bf R}^{N-1}
. Let
\langle v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{m}}\rangle_{{\bf R}_{\geq 0}}
(
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{m}\leq N
) be the
m
-dimensional cone in
{\bf R}^{N-1}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle\langle v_{i_{1}},\dots,v_{i_{m}}\rangle_{{\bf R}_{\geq 0}}:=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}v_{i_{j}}\in{\bf R}^{N-1}\mid u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\geq 0\right\}.

(2.5)

The conventional rule for identifying the singular locus is as follows:

(i)

The locus
\{\;(x_{1}:\cdots:x_{N})\in P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})\;|\;x_{i_{1}}=x_{i_{2}}=\cdots=x_{i_{m}}=0\}
is singular if and only if the cone
\langle v_{i_{1}},\cdots v_{i_{m}}\rangle_{{\bf R}_{\geq 0}}
contains integral points that cannot be expressed as an integral linear combination of
v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\cdots,v_{i_{m}}
.

Let us consider
{P}(1,1,1,2)
as an example. The corresponding polytope
\Delta^{*}
is the convex hull of the following four points in
{\bf R}^{3}
:

\displaystyle v_{1}=(-1,-1,-2),\quad v_{2}=(1,0,0),\quad v_{3}=(0,1,0),\quad v_{4}=(0,0,1).

We can easily observe that only the cone
\langle v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\rangle_{{\bf R}_{\geq 0}}
contains integral points that cannot be expressed as an integral linear combination of
v_{1},v_{2}
, and
v_{3}
(for example,
(0,0,-1)
). Therefore, the singular locus of
{P}(1,1,1,2)
is given by the point
(0:0:0:1)\in{P}(1,1,1,2)
. The singular locus of the weighted projective spaces treated in this paper is always a single point.

On the other hand, we have the following exact sequence:

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}\to{\bf C}^{N-1}\to\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}{\bf C}v_{j}\to CH^{1}({P}(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}))\to\mathbf{0},

where
CH^{1}(M)
is the degree
1
Chow ring of an algebraic variety
M
(if
M
is non-singular, it equals
H^{1,1}(M,{\bf C})
). Hence,
\dim_{{\bf C}}(CH^{1}({P}(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N})))
is
1
. In the cases of hypersurfaces and complete intersections in the weighted projective spaces treated in this paper, we can make them non-singular by avoiding the singular points of the weighted projective spaces through an appropriate choice of defining equations. Therefore, the resulting hypersurface or complete intersection
M
becomes non-singular and has a single Kähler class.

Next, we introduce the concept of a non-singular complete intersection in the weighted projective space
{P}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N})
. Let
F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{m}
be weighted homogeneous polynomials with degrees
k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m}
, respectively. The degree
k_{i}
hypersurface in the weighted projective space is defined as the zero locus of
F_{i}
in
{P}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N})
, and we denote it by
{P}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N}|k_{i})
. For the hypersurface to be non-singular, it is necessary that all weights
a_{j}
divide the homogeneous degree
k_{i}

[
11
]
. If some
a_{i}
is equal to
k_{1}
, then
P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,\hat{a_{i}},\dots,a_{N}|k_{1})
is biholomorphically equivalent to
P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,\hat{a_{i}},\dots,a_{N})

[
1
]
, where
\hat{a_{i}}
indicates that
a_{i}
is omitted. The complete intersection
P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m})
is defined by:

\displaystyle P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m})=P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N}|k_{1})\cap\cdots\cap P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N}|k_{m})

(2.6)

where
k_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,m
) are positive integers and
a_{j}
divides
k_{i}
for all
i
and
j
. According to
[
11
]
,
P(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{m})
is smooth if and only if the greatest common divisor of any collection of
m+1
weights
\{a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{2}},\dots,a_{i_{m+1}}\}
is
1
:

\displaystyle\text{gcd}(a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{2}},\dots,a_{i_{m+1}})=1.

(2.7)

Lastly, we introduce the Calabi-Yau threefolds treated in this paper. In Section
5
, we compute the genus
1
Gromov-Witten invariants for the following three types of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces, which were also used as examples in
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle P(1,1,1,1,2|6)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{4}(x^{i})^{6}+2(x^{5})^{3}=0\right\},

(2.8)

\displaystyle P(1,1,1,1,4|8)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{4}(x^{i})^{8}+4(x^{5})^{2}=0\right\},

(2.9)

\displaystyle P(1,1,1,2,5|10)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{3}(x^{i})^{10}+2(x^{4})^{5}+5(x^{5})^{2}=0\right\}.

(2.10)

3
Setup of Our Computation

In this section, we outline our strategy to compute genus
1
Gromov-Witten invariants of the non-singular complete intersection
P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})
in the weighted projective space
P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})
.
Let
h
be the hyperplane (Kähler) class of
P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})
. We also denote the restriction of this hyperplane class to the complete intersection by the same symbol
h
.
We then introduce the genus
g
Gromov-Witten invariants
\langle\prod_{a=2}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{a}})^{n_{a}}\rangle_{g,d}
of
P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})
, which represent the intersection number of the moduli space of holomorphic maps from genus
g
stable curves
\Sigma_{g}
with
\sum_{a=2}^{N-m-1}n_{a}
marked points to
P(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m})
of degree
d
. We denote this moduli space by
\\
\overline{M}_{g,\sum_{a=2}^{N-m-1}n_{a}}(P(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m}),d)
. Here,
{\cal O}_{h^{a}}
represents the insertion of the pull-back of the cohomology class
h^{a}
by the evaluation map:

ev_{i}:\overline{M}_{g,\sum_{a=2}^{N-m-1}n_{a}}(P(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m}),d)\rightarrow P(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m}),

at the
i
-th marked point
z_{i}\in\Sigma_{g}
. The complex dimension of the moduli space is given by

N-m-1+(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j})d+(3g-3)+\sum_{a=2}^{N-m-1}n_{a}
. Therefore,
\langle\prod_{a=2}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{a}})^{n_{a}}\rangle_{g,d}
is non-zero only if the
following condition is satisfied:

\displaystyle N-m-1+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\right)d+(3g-3)+\sum_{a=2}^{N-m-1}n_{a}=\sum_{a=2}^{N-m-1}n_{a}a

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow N-m-1+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\right)d+(3g-3)=\sum_{a=2}^{N-m-1}n_{a}(a-1).

(3.12)

Next, we introduce the genus
0
multi-point virtual structure constants
w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}|\prod_{j=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{j}})^{n_{j}})_{0,d}
of the same complete intersection. To simplify the notation, we define the following polynomials:

\displaystyle e_{k}(x,y):=\prod_{i=0}^{k}(ix+(k-i)y),

(3.13)

\displaystyle w_{a}(x,y):=\frac{x^{a}-y^{a}}{x-y},

(3.14)

\displaystyle q(x,y):=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{a_{i}-1}(jx+(a_{i}-j)y).

(3.15)

If
a_{i}=1
for some
i
, then the term
\prod_{j=1}^{a_{i}-1}(jx+(a_{i}-j)y)
is defined to be
1
. With these definitions, the multi-point virtual structure constant for
d\geq 1
is given by:

\displaystyle w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}|\prod_{p=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{p}})^{n_{p}})_{0,d}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{d+1}}\oint_{C_{z_{0}}}dz_{0}\oint_{C_{z_{1}}}dz_{1}\cdots\oint_{C_{z_{d}}}dz_{d}\frac{(z_{0})^{a}(z_{d})^{b}}{\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\right)^{d+1}\prod_{i=0}^{d}(z_{i})^{N}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{\prod_{p=1}^{m}e_{k_{p}}(z_{j-1},z_{j})}{\left(\prod_{p=1}^{m}k_{p}z_{j}\right)(2z_{j}-z_{j-1}-z_{j+1})}\right)\left(\prod_{p=1}^{m}k_{p}z_{d}\right)\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{d}q(z_{j-1},z_{j})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\prod_{l=0}^{N-m-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{l}(z_{i-1},z_{i})\right)^{n_{l}}\right).

(3.16)

Here,
\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\oint_{C_{z_{i}}}dz_{i}
represents taking the residue at
z_{i}=0
if
i=0
or
i=d
, and at
z_{i}=\frac{z_{i-1}+z_{i+1}}{2}
or
z_{i}=0
if
i=1,2,\cdots,d-1
.
From this definition, we can see that it obeys the following selection rule:

\displaystyle w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}|\prod_{j=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{j}})^{n_{j}})_{0,d}\neq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\right)d+N-m-2=a+b+\sum_{j=0}^{N-m-1}n_{j}(j-1).

(3.17)

Since
w_{0}(x,y)=0
and
w_{1}(x,y)=1
, the following condition follows:

\displaystyle w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}|\prod_{p=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{p}})^{n_{p}})_{0,d}=\delta_{n_{0},0}\cdot d^{n_{1}}w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}|\prod_{p=2}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{p}})^{n_{p}})_{0,d}.

(3.18)

If
d=0
, the multi-point virtual constants vanish except for the following cases:

\displaystyle w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}|{\cal O}_{h^{c}})_{0,0}=\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}}\delta_{a+b+c,N-m-1}.

(3.19)

We then introduce two types of perturbed two-point functions:
w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}})_{0}(x^{*})
and
\langle{\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}\rangle_{0}(t^{*})
. Here,
t^{*}
and
x^{*}
denote deformation variables
t^{0},t^{1},t^{2},\cdots,t^{N-m-1}
and
x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},\cdots,x^{N-m-1}
, respectively. These functions are generating functions of the multi-point virtual structure constants and the Gromov-Witten invariants, respectively. The first one,
w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}})_{0}(x^{*})
, is defined as follows:

\displaystyle w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}})_{0}(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},\cdots,x^{N-m-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{0}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_{N-m-1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}|\prod_{q=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{q}})^{n_{q}})_{0,d}\prod_{q=0}^{N-m-1}\frac{(x^{q})^{n_{q}}}{n_{q}!}.

(3.20)

By using the properties (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we can rewrite the right-hand side as follows:

\displaystyle w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}})_{0}(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},\cdots,x^{N-m-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}}(x^{1})^{N-m-1-a-b}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{3}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_{N-m-1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}e^{dx^{1}}w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}|\prod_{q=2}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{q}})^{n_{q}})_{0,d}\prod_{q=2}^{N-m-1}\frac{(x^{q})^{n_{q}}}{n_{q}!}.

(3.21)

The second one,
\langle{\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}\rangle_{0}(t^{*})
, is defined by:

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}\rangle_{0}(t^{0},t^{1},t^{2},\cdots,t^{N-m-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}}(t^{1})^{N-m-1-a-b}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{3}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_{N-m-1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}e^{dt^{1}}\langle{\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}\prod_{q=2}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{q}})^{n_{q}}\rangle_{0,d}\prod_{q=2}^{N-m-1}\frac{(t^{q})^{n_{q}}}{n_{q}!}.

(3.22)

At this stage, we rewrite (
3.12
), the topological selection rule for the Gromov-Witten invariants of
P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})
for the specific
g=0,1
cases.

\displaystyle\langle\prod_{a=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{a}})^{n_{a}}\rangle_{0,d}\neq 0\Rightarrow\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\right)d+N-m-2=\sum_{j=0}^{N-m-1}n_{j}(j-1),

(3.23)

\displaystyle\langle\prod_{a=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{a}})^{n_{a}}\rangle_{1,d}\neq 0\Rightarrow\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}\right)d=\sum_{j=0}^{N-m-1}n_{j}(j-1).

(3.24)

With these setups, we define the mirror maps
t^{p}(x^{*})~~(p=0,1,2,\cdots,N-m-1)
using the first type of the two-point perturbed function as follows:

\displaystyle t^{p}(x^{0},x^{1},\cdots,x^{N-m-1}):=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}}{\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}}w({\cal O}_{h^{N-m-1-p}}{\cal O}_{1})_{0}(x^{*})\quad(p=0,1,2,\cdots,N-m-1).

(3.25)

We can see from (
3.21
) that it has the following structure:

\displaystyle t^{p}(x^{0},x^{1},\cdots,x^{N-m-1})

\displaystyle=x^{p}+\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}}{\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{3}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_{N-m-1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}e^{dx^{1}}w({\cal O}_{h^{N-m-1-p}}{\cal O}_{1}|\prod_{q=2}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{q}})^{n_{q}})_{0,d}\prod_{q=2}^{N-m-1}\frac{(x^{q})^{n_{q}}}{n_{q}!},

(3.26)

which enables us to invert the mirror map:

\displaystyle x^{p}=x^{p}(t^{0},t^{1},\cdots,t^{N-m-1})\quad(p=0,1,2,\cdots,N-m-1).

(3.27)

In this paper, we use the following conjecture
[
7
]
to compute the genus
0
Gromov-Witten invariants, which has already been proved in
[
8
]
for the case of projective hypersurfaces.

Conjecture 1
.

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}}\rangle_{0}(t^{0},t^{1},t^{2},\cdots,t^{N-m-1})=w({\cal O}_{h^{a}}{\cal O}_{h^{b}})_{0}(x^{0}(t^{*}),x^{1}(t^{*}),x^{2}(t^{*}),\cdots,x^{N-m-1}(t^{*})).

(3.28)

Lastly, we introduce the generating function for genus
1
Gromov-Witten invariants. In this paper, we also introduce the generating function of the elliptic virtual structure constants
w(\prod_{p=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{p}})^{n_{p}})_{1,d}
, whose general definition will be given in the next section, to compute the genus
1
Gromov-Witten invariants.
In the
d=0
case,
w(\prod_{p=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{p}})^{n_{p}})_{1,0}
vanishes except for the following case:

\displaystyle w({\cal O}_{h})_{1,0}:=\langle{\cal O}_{h}\rangle_{1,0}=-\frac{1}{24}\int_{M}c_{N-m-2}(P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}))h=-\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}}{24\prod_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}}c_{N-m-2}.

(3.29)

Here,
h
is the Kähler class of
P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})
.
c_{N-m-2}(P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}))
is the second top Chern class of
P(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m})
.
c_{N-m-2}
in the right-hand side is given by the following formula:

c(P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}))=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1+ta_{j}h)}{\prod_{l=1}^{m}(1+tk_{l}h)}=:\sum_{j=0}^{N-m-1}c_{j}h^{j}.

(3.30)

If
d\geq 1
,
w(\prod_{p=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{p}})^{n_{p}})_{1,d}
obeys the following conditions:

(i)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}}{24\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}}c_{N-m-2}x^{1}

4
Elliptic Virtual Structure Constants

4.1
Graphs for Elliptic Virtual Structure Constants
[
9
]

The elliptic virtual structure constants
w(\prod_{p=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{p}})^{n_{p}})_{1,d}
are defined by the summation of residues of integrands associated with graphs. In this section, we briefly introduce the four types of graphs used in their definition. For more detailed explanations or figures of the graphs, we recommend that readers refer to
[
9
]
.

For preparation, we introduce the partition of a positive integer
d
:

\sigma=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{l(\sigma)})\quad(d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq d_{3}\leq\cdots\leq d_{l(\sigma)},\sum_{i=1}^{l(\sigma)}d_{i}=d).

We call
l(\sigma)
the length of the partition
\sigma
. Let
P_{d}
be the set of partitions of the positive integer
d
:

P_{d}:=\{\sigma=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{l(\sigma)})|d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq d_{3}\leq\cdots\leq d_{l(\sigma)},\sum_{i=1}^{l(\sigma)}d_{i}=d\}.

We then define the symmetry factor associated with
\sigma\in P_{d}
:

\mathrm{Sym}(\sigma)=\frac{(l(\sigma)-1)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{l(\sigma)}\mathrm{mul}(\sigma,i)!}\quad(\sigma\in P_{d}).

Here,
\mathrm{mul}(\sigma,i)
denotes the multiplicity of
i
(
1\leq i\leq d
) in
\sigma
.

The graphs used in the definition of
w(\prod_{p=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{p}})^{n_{p}})_{1,d}
are constructed using edges and three types of vertices:

(i)

Normal Vertex

(ii)

Elliptic Vertex

(iii)

Cluster Vertex of degree
d

A single edge is assigned a degree of
1
. A normal vertex, an elliptic vertex, and a cluster vertex of degree
d
are assigned degrees of
0
,
0
, and
d
, respectively.

Next, we introduce the following four types of graphs:

(i)

Star graph associated with
\sigma\in P_{d}

(ii)

Loop graph with
d
edges and
d
normal vertices (
d\geq 2
)

(iii)

Star graph associated with
\sigma\in P_{d-f}
(
1\leq f\leq d-1
) having a cluster vertex of degree
d
as its center

(iv)

Graph consisting of a single cluster vertex of degree
d

In this paper, we denote the sets of graphs of type (i) and (iii) by
\text{Graph}^{(i)}_{d}
and
\text{Graph}^{(iii)}_{d}
, respectively. We can easily see that the type (ii) graph and the type (iv) graph of degree
d
are unique, and we denote these graphs by
\Gamma^{\text{loop}}_{d}
and
\Gamma^{\text{point}}_{d}
, respectively. We also denote by
f_{\Gamma}
the integrand associated with the graph
\Gamma
, which will be defined in the next subsection. With these setups, the elliptic virtual structure constant
w(\prod_{a=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{a}})^{n_{a}})_{1,d}
is defined as follows:

Definition 1
.

w(\prod_{a=0}^{N-m-1}({\cal O}_{h^{a}})^{n_{a}})_{1,d}:=\sum_{\Gamma\in\text{Graph}^{(i)}_{d}}\mathrm{Res}(f_{\Gamma})+\mathrm{Res}(f_{\Gamma^{\text{loop}}_{d}})+\sum_{\Gamma\in\text{Graph}^{(iii)}_{d}}\mathrm{Res}(f_{\Gamma})+\mathrm{Res}(f_{\Gamma^{\text{point}}_{d}})

Here,
\mathrm{Res}(f_{\Gamma})
denotes the procedure of taking the residue of
f_{\Gamma}
, which will also be explained in the next subsection.

4.2
Residue Integrals for Elliptic Virtual Structure Constants

In this subsection, we explicitly write down the integrand
f_{\Gamma}
associated with the graph
\Gamma
.

First, we recall the total Chern class of the complete intersection
P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})
, as given in
[
4
]
:

\displaystyle c(P(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},\cdots,k_{m}))=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1+ta_{j}h)}{\prod_{l=1}^{m}(1+tk_{l}h)}=:\sum_{j=0}^{N-m-1}c_{j}h^{j}.

(4.36)

Then we define
c_{T}(z)
as follows:

\displaystyle c_{T}(z):=c_{N-m-1}z.

(4.37)

The integrand
f_{\Gamma}
associated with a graph
\Gamma
is defined as follows:

For a type (i) star graph of degree
d
associated with a partition
\sigma=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{l})\in P_{d}
, we prepare
d+1
variables
z_{0}
and
z_{i,j}
(where
1\leq i\leq l,~1\leq j\leq d_{j}
). Here,
z_{0}
is associated with the elliptic vertex, and
z_{i,j}
is associated with a normal vertex. Then,
f_{\Gamma}
for this graph is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Sym}(\sigma)}{24(\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i})^{d+1}(z_{0})^{N}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{i}}\frac{1}{(z_{i,j})^{N}}\right)\right)\frac{c_{T}(z_{0})\prod_{a=2}^{N-m-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{i}}w_{a}(z_{i,j-1},z_{i,j})\right)^{n_{a}}}{\prod_{l=1}^{m}(k_{l}z_{0})^{l-1}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{m}e_{k_{l}}(z_{0},z_{i,1})}{q(z_{0},z_{i,1})(z_{i,1}-z_{0})}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{i}-1}\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{m}e_{k_{l}}(z_{i,j},z_{i,j+1})}{q(z_{i,j},z_{i,j+1})(2z_{i,j}-z_{i,j-1}-z_{i,j+1})\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}z_{i,j}}\right)\right).

(4.38)

The integrand associated with the type (ii) loop graph
f_{\Gamma^{\text{loop}}_{d}}
is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2d(\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i})^{d}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{m}e_{k_{l}}(z_{i},z_{i+1})}{q(z_{i},z_{i+1})(2z_{i}-z_{i-1}-z_{i+1})\prod_{l=1}^{m}k_{l}z_{i}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{(z_{i})^{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{a=2}^{N-m-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{a}(z_{i},z_{i+1})\right)^{n_{a}}.

(4.39)

The integrand associated with the type (iii) star graph of degree
d
having a cluster vertex of degree
f\;(1\leq f\leq d-1)
and the partition
\sigma=(d_{1},d_{2},\cdots,d_{l})\in P_{d-f}
is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Sym}(\sigma)}{24(\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i})^{d}(z_{0})^{N(f-1)}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{i}}\frac{1}{(z_{i,j})^{N}}\right)\right)\left(-\frac{N-m}{N}\frac{1}{w^{N}}-\frac{N+m}{N}\frac{1}{(z_{0})^{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(w-z_{0})^{2}q(w,z_{0})(q(z_{0},z_{0}))^{f-1}}\left(\prod_{p=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(k_{p}z_{0})^{l-1}}\frac{e_{k_{p}}(w,z_{0})\left(e_{k_{p}}(z_{0},z_{0})\right)^{f-1}}{k_{p}w\left(k_{p}z_{0}\right)^{f}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\prod_{p=1}^{m}e_{k_{p}}(z_{0},z_{i,1})}{q(z_{0},z_{i,1})(z_{i,1}-z_{0})}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{i}-1}\frac{\prod_{p=1}^{m}e_{k_{p}}(z_{i,j},z_{i,j+1})}{q(z_{i,j},z_{i,j+1})(2z_{i,j}-z_{i,j-1}-z_{i,j+1})\left(\prod_{p=1}^{m}k_{p}z_{i,j}\right)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{a=2}^{N-m-1}\left(w_{a}(w,z_{0})+(f-1)w_{a}(z_{0},z_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{i}}w_{a}(z_{i,j-1},z_{i,j})\right)^{n_{a}}.

(4.40)

The integrand
f_{\Gamma^{\text{point}}_{d}}
is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{R(d)}{24(\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i})^{d+1}(z_{0})^{Nd+1}(q(z_{0},z_{0}))^{d}}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{m}\left(\frac{e_{k_{l}}(z_{0},z_{0})}{k_{l}z_{0}}\right)^{d}\right)\left(\prod_{a=2}^{N-m-1}(dw_{a}(z_{0},z_{0}))^{n_{a}}\right),

(4.41)

where the symmetric factor
R(d)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle R(d)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\right)\left(\left(\frac{N-m}{2}\right)\frac{1}{d}-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{a_{j}}-\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k_{l}}\right)\frac{1}{d^{2}}\right).

Lastly, we explain how to take the residue of
f_{\Gamma}
, according to our previous work
[
9
]
.

Definition 2
.

For each type of graph, the residue operation
Res:f_{\Gamma}\to{\bf R}
is defined as follows:

(i)

First, we take the residue of
f_{\Gamma}
at
z_{0}=0
. Next, we take the residue of the resulting function at
z_{i,j}=0
and
z_{i,j}=\frac{z_{i,j+1}-z_{i,j-1}}{2}
, and sum them sequentially in ascending order of
j
(for
1\leq j\leq d_{i}-1
). Lastly, we take the residue of the resulting function at
z_{i,d_{i}}=0
. The order among different
i
’s does not matter.

(ii)

We take the residue of
f_{\Gamma}
at
z_{i}=0
and
z_{i}=\frac{z_{i+1}-z_{i-1}}{2}
, and sum them sequentially in ascending order of
i
(
1\leq i\leq d
).

(iii)

First, we take the residue of
f_{\Gamma}
at
w=z_{0}
. Then, the remaining process is the same as in the case of type (i).

(iv)

We take the residue of
f_{\Gamma}
at
z_{0}=0
.

Appendix: Proof of Proposition 1

In this appendix, we prove Proposition 1. The proof fundamentally follows the same approach as the corresponding proposition in
[
9
]
; specifically, we only need to show that the residue of
f_{\Gamma}
for type (iii) graphs at
w=z_{0}
vanishes. If
a_{i}=1
, then
q(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^{a_{i}-1}(jx+(a_{i}-j)y):=1
.

We assume

P(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m})=P(1,1,\cdots,1,a_{r+1},a_{r+2},\cdots,a_{N}|k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m}),

which implies
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{r}=1
. We also assume that
a_{i}>1
for
i\in\{r+1,r+2,\cdots,N\}
. From the Calabi-Yau condition, we have
r+\sum_{i=r+1}^{N}a_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{m}
.

\displaystyle f_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{Sym}(\sigma)}{24(\prod_{i=1}^{N}a_{i})^{d}(z_{0})^{N(f-1)}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{i}}\frac{1}{(z_{i,j})^{N}}\right)\right)\left(-\frac{N-m}{N}\frac{1}{w^{N}}-\frac{N+m}{N}\frac{1}{(z_{0})^{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(w-z_{0})^{2}q(w,z_{0})(q(z_{0},z_{0}))^{f-1}}\left(\prod_{p=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(k_{p}z_{0})^{l-1}}\frac{e_{k_{p}}(w,z_{0})\left(e_{k_{p}}(z_{0},z_{0})\right)^{f-1}}{k_{p}w\left(k_{p}z_{0}\right)^{f}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\prod_{p=1}^{m}e_{k_{p}}(z_{0},z_{i,1})}{q(z_{0},z_{i,1})(z_{i,1}-z_{0})}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d_{i}-1}\frac{\prod_{p=1}^{m}e_{k_{p}}(z_{i,j},z_{i,j+1})}{q(z_{i,j},z_{i,j+1})(2z_{i,j}-z_{i,j-1}-z_{i,j+1})\left(\prod_{p=1}^{m}k_{p}z_{i,j}\right)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{a=2}^{N-m-1}\left(w_{a}(w,z_{0})+(f-1)w_{a}(z_{0},z_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{i}}w_{a}(z_{i,j-1},z_{i,j})\right)^{n_{a}}.

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{N-m}{N}\frac{1}{w^{N}}-\frac{N+m}{N}\frac{1}{(z_{0})^{N}}\right)\frac{1}{(w-z_{0})^{2}q(w,z_{0})}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{m}\frac{e_{k_{l}}(w,z_{0})}{k_{l}w}\right)g(z)

Here,
g(z)
in the last line represents the factor that does not contain
w
.

\displaystyle=(kz_{0})^{k+1}

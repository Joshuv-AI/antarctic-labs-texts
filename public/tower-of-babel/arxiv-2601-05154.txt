Sumit Kumar

Abstract
. We prove that the Kadison-Kastler and Christensen distances are stable under the Banach space injective tensor product (resp., the Banach space projective tensor product) of a Banach space with any unital commutative C ∗ C^{*} -algebra (resp., of a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra with any unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebra). Apart from these stability results, we make some explicit calculations of the Kadison-Kastler, Christensen and Mashood-Taylor distances between certain subalgebras of some crossed-product operator algebras.

1.
Introduction

A notion of distance between subalgebras of
B(\mathcal{H})
was first
introduced by Kadison and Kastler in 1973 in
[
14
]
. This notion
gathered much attention in the 80s and 90s, particularly in the
context of perturbation theory for operator algebras. Notable
contributions in this area were made by Christensen, Phillips and
Johnson, who employed the Kadison–Kastler distance to establish
several significant results concerning the stability of
C^{*}
-algebras
and von Neumann algebras under small perturbations.

Recently, Ino and Watatani, in
[
12
]
, used this notion in the
study of inclusions of
C^{*}
-algebras. Motivated by their work, a
similar perspective was adopted in an earlier work
[
10
]
, wherein
this notion was applied effectively to study the lattice of
intermediate subalgebras of inclusions of
C^{*}
-algebras and some
concrete calculations were made. Motivated by the calculations made in

[
10
]
for crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras, we also initiated the study of
such distances between subalgebras of the spatial tensor product of

C^{*}
-algebras, in
[
11
]
.

An important open problem in the theory of operator algebras concerns
the behaviour of the Kadison–Kastler distance under tensor
products. Specifically, as already mentioned by Christensen et al in

[
7
]
, it is not yet fully understood how the distance between
two
C^{*}
-algebras relates to the distance between their respective
tensor products with a fixed (nuclear)
C^{*}
-algebra. That is, given
two
C^{*}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
on some Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}
with small Kadison–Kastler distance, it remains an
open question whether the tensor products
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{C}
also remain
close (as subalgebras of
B(\mathcal{H})\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{C}
) in the
Kadison–Kastler sense, for a fixed nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathcal{C}
. Similar question can be asked for the Christensen
distance as well. In this direction, various attempts were made by
Christensen et al (see, for instance,
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
and

[
7
]
). (As in
[
10
,
11
]
, we denote the Kadison-Kastler and
Christensen distances by
d_{KK}
and
d_{0}
, respectively.) Here are
two of Christensen’s noteworthy results in this direction:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra with
C^{*}
-subalgebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
,
and let
\mathcal{D}
be a nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra. If
\mathcal{A}
has property

D_{k}
(for some
k\in(0,\infty))
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq_{\gamma}\mathcal{B}
, then
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D}\subseteq_{6k\gamma}\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D}
.

In particular, when
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
both have property
D_{k}
, then

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D})\leq 6kd_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Theorem 1.2
.

[
7
, Corollary 4.7]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra with
C^{*}
-subalgebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
,
and let
\mathcal{D}
be a nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra. Then, for every
l,K\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
L_{l,K}
(depending only on
l
and

K
) such that when
\mathcal{A}
has length at most
l
with length constant
at most
K
, then

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D})\leq L_{l,K}d_{KK}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Very recently, using some techniques from
[
6
]
, the following
stability result in the category of
C^{*}
-algebras was achieved in
[
11
]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

[
11
, Theorem 4.6]

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra and,

\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be

C^{*}
-subalgebras of a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{C}
. Then,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D})\leq d_{KK}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}
is unital, then

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\min}\mathcal{D})=d_{KK}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

It is noteworthy that such comparisons were used effectively by
Christensen and others in proving some fundamental results related to
perturbation and preserving of certain invariants of close
subalgebras. For instance:

(a)

In
[
6
, Theorem 4.3]
, Christensen
proved that if an injective von Neumann algebra
N
admits a near
inclusion in some von Neumann algebra
M
, then
N
embeds in
M
via a
unitary conjugation.

(b)

In
[
15
]
, Khoshkam proved that
if the matrix-amplifications of two
C^{*}
-algebras are sufficiently
(uniformly) close, then they have isomorphic
K
-groups; which, by one
of the above mentioned results of Christensen (see
Theorem

1.1
)
allows one to deduce that if
\mathcal{A}
is a nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra, then
any sufficiently close
C^{*}
-algebra to
\mathcal{A}
has

K
-groups isomorphic to those of
\mathcal{A}
.

(c)

Moreover, Christensen et al (
[
7
,
\lx@sectionsign 5
]
) exploited
the techniques of Khoshkam and Christensen further to prove that

C^{*}
-algebras sufficiently close to a
C^{*}
-algebra belonging to a certain family have
isomorphic (ordered)
K
-theories.

Over the last four decades or so, the study of non-operator algebraic
tensor products (like the Banach space and operator space projective
tensor products and the Haagerup tensor product) of
C^{*}
-algebras
have become very relevant in the world of operator
algebras - see, for instance,
[
4
,
1
,
5
,
16
,
9
]
and the
references cited therein. It is thus natural to ask similar questions (as above) from
the perspective of non-operator algebraic tensor products as well. In
this direction, we obtain some analogues of the above-mentioned results with
respect to the injective and projective Banach space tensor
products. More precisely, we prove the following:

Theorem

3.6
.
Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach
space and
C(K)
denote the Banach space consisting of continuous
functions on a compact Hausdorff space
K
. Then, for any two closed subspaces

\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K))

and

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=d_{0}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)).

Theorem

4.8
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be two

C^{*}
-algebras and,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be
C^{*}
-subalgebras of

\mathcal{C}
. If
\beta
is a positive scalar such that
\mathcal{A}\subseteq_{\beta}\mathcal{B}
, then
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}\subseteq_{\beta}\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}
. In particular,

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})\leq d_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}
is unital, then

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})=d_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Theorem

4.9
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be two

C^{*}
-algebras and,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be
C^{*}
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{C}
. Then,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})\leq d_{KK}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}
is unital, then

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})=d_{KK}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

It is natural to ask whether it is possible to compute the precise distance for certain operator algebras. To address this question,
in
[
10
]
, we calculated the precise distance in several specific examples of operator algebras. In a similar spirit,
in
Section

5.1
and
Section

5.2
, we make some concrete
calculations of the Kadison–Kastler and Christensen distances between
subalgebras (associated to subgroups of a discrete group) of reduced
twisted crossed-product
C^{*}
-algebras and of crossed-product von
Neumann algebras. Furthermore, towards the end, we calculate the
Mashood-Taylor distance between the crossed product von Neumann
subalgebras (associated to subgroups of a discrete group) of the
crossed product of a discrete group with a tracial von
Neumann algebra.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Two notions of distance between subspaces of normed spaces

2.1.1.
Kadison-Kastler distance

For any normed space
\mathcal{X}
, its closed unit ball will be denoted
by
B_{1}(\mathcal{X})
and for any subset
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{X}
and an
element
x\in\mathcal{X}
, as is standard, the distance between
x
and

\mathcal{S}
is given by
d(x,\mathcal{S})=\inf\{\|x-s\|:s\in\mathcal{S}\}.

For any two subspaces
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Z}
of a normed space

\mathcal{X}
, recall (from
[
14
]
) that the Kadison-Kastler distance
between them (which we denote by
d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})
) is
defined as the Hausdorff distance between their closed unit balls,
i.e.,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}):=\max\left\{\sup_{y\in B_{1}(\mathcal{Y})}d(y,B_{1}(\mathcal{Z})),\sup_{z\in B_{1}(\mathcal{Z})}d(z,B_{1}(\mathcal{Y}))\right\}.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a normed space. Then, the following facts are well known:

(1)

d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})\leq 1
for all subspaces
\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
.

(2)

d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=d_{KK}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{Z})=d_{KK}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}},\overline{\mathcal{Z}})
for
all subspaces
\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
.

2.1.2.
Near inclusions and Christensen distance

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a normed space. Recall from
[
8
]
that, for
any two subspaces
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
and a
scalar
\gamma>0
,
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq_{\gamma}\mathcal{Z}
if for
each
y\in B_{1}(\mathcal{Y})
, there exists a
z\in\mathcal{Z}
such
that
\|y-z\|\leq\gamma
; and, the Christensen distance between

\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Z}
is defined by

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=\inf\{\gamma>0\,:\,\mathcal{Y}\subseteq_{\gamma}\mathcal{Z}\text{ and }\mathcal{Z}\subseteq_{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}\}.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a normed space. Then, the following useful facts are
well known:

(1)

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})\leq 1
for all subspaces
\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
.

(2)

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=d_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{Z})=d_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}},\overline{\mathcal{Z}})
for all subspaces
\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
.

(3)

The distances
d_{0}
and
d_{KK}

are “equivalent” in the sense that

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})\leq d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})\leq 2d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})

for all subspaces
\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
. (
[
8
, Remark
2.3]
)

2.1.3.
Mashood-Taylor distance

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial
state
\tau
. Recall from
[
10
, Section 5]
that, for any two subalgebras

\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
of
\mathcal{M}
, the Mashood-Taylor
distance between them is given by

d_{MT}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})=d_{H,\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\widehat{B_{1}(\mathcal{P})},\widehat{B_{1}(\mathcal{Q})}),

where
d_{H,\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}
denotes the Hausdorff distance with
respect to the metric induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
and, for

\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
,
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}:=\{\widehat{x}:x\in\mathcal{S}\}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be as above. Then, for any two unital
*
-subalgebras
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
of
\mathcal{M}
,

(1)

d_{MT}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})=d_{MT}(\mathcal{P},\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{S.O.T.})=d_{MT}(\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{S.O.T.},\overline{\mathcal{Q}}^{S.O.T.})=d_{MT}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{Q}^{\prime\prime})
; and, (
[
10
, Proposition 5.5]
)

(2)

d_{MT}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})\leq d_{KK}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})
. (
[
10
, Lemma 5.6]
)

3.
Distance between subspaces of injective tensor product of certain Banach spaces

3.1.
Injective tensor product

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
denote their algebraic tensor product. For any element
u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
, its injective norm is given by

\|u\|_{\varepsilon}=\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi(x_{i})\psi(y_{i})\right|\,\,:\,\,\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\mathcal{X}^{*}),\,\psi\in\mathcal{B}_{1}(\mathcal{Y}^{*})\right\}.

The completion of
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
with respect to the injective norm is denoted by
\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Y}
. The Banach space
\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Y}
is known as the injective tensor product of the Banach spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{W}
be the normed spaces
and
T_{1}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}
and
T_{2}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
be bounded linear maps. Then, there
exists a unique bounded map
T_{1}\otimes^{\varepsilon}T_{2}:\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Z}
such that
T_{1}\otimes^{\varepsilon}T_{2}(x\otimes y)=T_{1}(x)\otimes T_{2}(y)
for every
x\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}
. Also,
\|T_{1}\otimes^{\varepsilon}T_{2}\|=\|T_{1}\|\|T_{2}\|
. (See
[
19
, Proposition 3.2]
, for a proof.)

The following identification is well known - see, for instance,
(
[
18
, Lemma 6.4.16]
and
[
19
, Section 3.2]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
C_{0}(\Omega)
denote the Banach
space of complex continuous functions vanishing at infinity on a
locally compact Hausdorff space
\Omega
. Then,

C_{0}(\Omega)\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{X}
is isometrically
isomorphic to the Banach space
C_{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{X})
of continuous
functions from
\Omega
into
\mathcal{X}
that vanish at infinity.

It is well known that the injective tensor product preserves subspace
in the following sense (see
[
19
]
for a proof).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
\mathcal{Z}
be a
closed subspace of
\mathcal{X}
. Then, the identity map on
\mathcal{Z}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
extends to an isometric map from
\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Y}
onto the closed subspace

\overline{\mathcal{Z}\otimes\mathcal{Y}}^{\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}}
of

\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{Y}
.

We now proceed to establish the stability results that we asserted in
the Introduction. To begin with, we have the following analogue of

[
11
, Theorem 4.6]
, whose proof follows verbatim (on the lines of
the proof given for
[
11
, Theorem 4.6]
).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
C_{0}(\Omega)
denote the Banach
space consisting of continuous functions vanishing at infinity on a
locally compact Hausdorff space
\Omega
. Then, for any two closed
subspaces
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
,

d_{KK}\left(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C_{0}(\Omega),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C_{0}(\Omega)\right)\leq d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})

and

d_{0}\left(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C_{0}(\Omega),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C_{0}(\Omega)\right)\leq d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}).

For the Banach space
C(K)
of continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space

K
, we have stability for both Kadison-Kastler and Christensen distances.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
C(K)
denote the Banach space
consisting of continuous functions on a compact Hausdorff space

K
. Then, for any two closed subspaces
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Z}
of

\mathcal{X}
,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=d_{KK}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K))

and

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})=d_{0}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K).

Proof.

We give a proof only for the Christensen distance as the proof for
the Kadison-Kastler distance is similar.

In view of
Theorem

3.5
, it just remains to show that

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})\leq d_{0}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)).

First note that the natural isometry
\theta
from
\mathcal{X}
onto

\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{C}\mathbbm{1}
maps
\mathcal{Y}

and
\mathcal{Z}
onto

\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{C}\mathbbm{1}
and

\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{C}\mathbbm{1}
respectively,
where
\mathbbm{1}
is the constant function taking the value
1
on

K
.

Let
\epsilon>0

and fix a
\gamma_{0}>0
such that

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K))<\gamma_{0}<d_{0}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K))+\epsilon.

This implies that
\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)\subseteq_{\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)
and that

\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)\subseteq_{\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)
.

Let
y\in B_{1}(\mathcal{Y})
. Then,
\theta(y)=y\otimes\mathbbm{1}\in B_{1}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{C}\mathbbm{1})\subseteq B_{1}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K))
; so, there exists a
z\in\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)

such that
\|y\otimes\mathbbm{1}-z\|_{\varepsilon}\leq\gamma_{0}
.
Fix a state
\phi
on
C(K)
and, by
Remark

3.2
, consider the
natural map

\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\phi\mathbbm{1}:\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)\to\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{C}\mathbbm{1},

with
\|\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\phi\|=\|\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}}\|\|\phi\|=1
. Clearly, it maps

\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)
onto

\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{C}\mathbbm{1}
. Thus,
z_{0}:=(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\phi\mathbbm{1})(z)\in\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{C}\mathbbm{1}
; so that
\theta^{-1}(z_{0})\in\mathcal{Z}
and

\displaystyle\|y-\theta^{-1}(z_{0})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\theta^{-1}(y\otimes\mathbbm{1})-\theta^{-1}(z_{0})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|y\otimes\mathbbm{1}-z_{0}\|_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes^{\varepsilon}\phi\mathbbm{1})(y\otimes\mathbbm{1}-z)\|_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|y\otimes\mathbbm{1}-z\|_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}.

So,
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq_{\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{Z}
.
Similarly,
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq_{\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{Y}
;
so that

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})\leq\gamma_{0}<d_{0}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K))+\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, it follows that

d_{0}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z})\leq d_{0}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K),\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\varepsilon}C(K)).

∎

4.
Distance between subalgebras of Banach space projective tensor product of
C^{*}
-algebras

4.1.
Banach space projective tensor product

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
denote their algebraic tensor product. For any element

u\in\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
, its projective norm is given by

\|u\|_{\gamma}=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}\|\|y_{i}\|\,\,:\,\,u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\right\}.

The completion of
\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{Y}
with respect to the
projective norm is denoted by
\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}

and is known as the projective tensor product of the Banach spaces

\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be two
C^{*}
-algebras. Then, it is easily seen (and well-known) that their projective
tensor product
\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}
naturally inherits the
structure of a Banach
*
-algebra.

The following remark is similar to
Remark

3.2
- see

[
19
, Proposition 2.3]
for a proof.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{W}
be the normed spaces
and
T_{1}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}
and
T_{2}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
be bounded linear maps. Then, there
exists a unique bounded linear map
T_{1}\otimes^{\gamma}T_{2}:\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{Z}
such that
(T_{1}\otimes^{\gamma}T_{2})(x\otimes y)=T_{1}(x)\otimes T_{2}(y)
for every
x\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}
. Also,
\|T_{1}\otimes^{\gamma}T_{2}\|=\|T_{1}\|\|T_{2}\|
.

The following well-known result proves to be a very important tool to
compare the distance between subalgebras of a normed algebra and the
distance between their tensor products with a fixed normed algebra -
see
[
19
, Proposition 2.2]
for a proof.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces. Then the closed unit
ball
B_{1}(\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y})
of
\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}
is the closed convex hull of the set

B_{1}(\mathcal{X})\otimes B_{1}(\mathcal{Y})
.

In general, given a subspace
\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{X}
, the projective
tensor product
\mathcal{Z}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}
cannot be
identified isometrically with a subspace of
\mathcal{X}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{Y}
. However, if we start with

C^{*}
-algebras, we have the following positive result.

Proposition 4.5
.

[
9
, Theorem 2.6]

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be two
C^{*}
-algebras and
\mathcal{A}
be a

C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the identity map on
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D}

extends to an isometric
*
-algebra map from
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}

onto the closed
*
-subalgebra
\overline{\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D}}^{\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}}
of
\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}
.

The following proposition is the natural analogue of
[
11
, Proposition
4.1]
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be
C^{*}
-algebras. Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be

C^{*}
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{C}
, and let
\mathcal{P}
be a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of

\mathcal{D}
. If there exists a conditional expectation from
\mathcal{D}
onto

\mathcal{P}
, then

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P})\leq d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})

and

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P})\leq d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}).

Proof.

Let
E:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{P}
be a conditional expectation. By

Proposition

4.5
, we can identify
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}
and

\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}
with Banach
*
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}
. Let
\epsilon>0
and fix a
\gamma_{0}>0
such
that

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})<\gamma_{0}<d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})+\epsilon.

This implies that
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}\subseteq_{\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}\subseteq_{\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}
.

Let
x\in B_{1}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P})\subseteq B_{1}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})
. Then, there exists a
y\in\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}
such that
\|x-y\|_{\gamma}\leq\gamma_{0}
. Consider the map
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes^{\gamma}E:\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}
by
Remark

4.3
with
\|\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes^{\gamma}E\|=\|E\|\leq 1
. Clearly, it fixes
\mathcal{C}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}
. Thus,

y_{0}:=(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes^{\gamma}E)(y)\in\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}
and

\displaystyle\|x-y_{0}\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes^{\gamma}E)(x-y)\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x-y\|_{\gamma}\leq\gamma_{0}.

So,
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}\subseteq_{\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}\subseteq_{\gamma_{0}}\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P}
. Thus,

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P})\leq\gamma_{0}<d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})+\epsilon.

Hence,

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{P})\leq d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}).

The proof for the Kadison-Kastler distance is analogous and we leave
the details to the reader.
∎

The following is an easy consequence of the preceding proposition.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be
C^{*}
-algebras and,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be

C^{*}
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{C}
. If
\mathcal{D}
is unital, then

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leq d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})

and

d_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\leq d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}).

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be
C^{*}
-algebras and,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be

C^{*}
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{C}
. If
\beta
is a positive scalar such that

\mathcal{A}\subseteq_{\beta}\mathcal{B}
, then
\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}\subseteq_{\beta}\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D}
. In particular,

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})\leq d_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}
is unital, then

d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})=d_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Proof.

As
B_{1}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D})
is dense in
B_{1}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})
, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D}\subseteq_{\beta}\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{D}
.

Let
x\in B_{1}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D})
. Then, by
Proposition

4.4

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(a_{i}\otimes d_{i})
, where
a_{i}\in B_{1}(\mathcal{A})
,
d_{i}\in B_{1}(\mathcal{D})
,
0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq 1
and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=1
. As
\mathcal{A}\subseteq_{\beta}\mathcal{B}
, for
each
a_{i}
, there exists a
b_{i}
in
\mathcal{B}
such that

\|a_{i}-b_{i}\|\leq\beta.

Thus,
y:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(b_{i}\otimes d_{i})\in\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{D}
and

\|x-y\|_{\gamma}=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(a_{i}-b_{i})\otimes d_{i}\|_{\gamma}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\|a_{i}-b_{i}\|\|d_{i}\|\leq\beta.

Hence,
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D}\subseteq_{\beta}\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{D}
.

When
\mathcal{D}
is unital, then the final equality follows from

4.7
.
∎

On similar lines, we also obtain the following:

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be
C^{*}
-algebras and,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be

C^{*}
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{C}
. Then,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})\leq d_{KK}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}
is unital, then

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D},\mathcal{B}\otimes^{\gamma}\mathcal{D})=d_{KK}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

5.
Some concrete calculations

In
[
10
]
, some concrete calculations were made regarding
the Kadison-Kastler and Christensen distances between crossed-product
subalgebras. Interestingly, we observed that when a discrete group
G

acts on a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
via a group homomorphism
\alpha:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
, then for any pair of distinct subgroups
H

and
K
of
G
,

(1)

d_{KK}(A\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H,A\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}K)=1=d_{0}(A\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H,A\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}K)
in
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
; and,

(2)

d_{KK}(A\rtimes^{u}_{\alpha}H,A\rtimes^{u}_{\alpha}K)=1=d_{0}(A\rtimes^{u}_{\alpha}H,A\rtimes^{u}_{\alpha}K)
in

A\rtimes^{u}_{\alpha}G
.

In this section, we make analogues calculations for reduced twisted
crossed-product subalgebras in
C^{*}
-algebras and for crossed-product
subalgebras in von Neumann algebras.

5.1.
Distance between subalgebras of reduced twisted
crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras

5.1.1.
Reduced twisted crossed product

Let
G
be a discrete group and
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. A
discrete twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system is a quadruple

(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha,\sigma)
of a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, a discrete
group
G
and a pair of maps
\alpha:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
and
\sigma:G\times G\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A})

satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}\circ\alpha_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ad_{\sigma(s,t)}\circ\alpha_{st};

\displaystyle\sigma(r,s)\sigma(rs,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{r}(\sigma(s,t))\sigma(r,st);\,\text{and},

\displaystyle\sigma(s,e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(e,s)=1

for all
r,s,t\in G
.

For a given discrete twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical system

(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha,\sigma)
, where
\mathcal{A}
is faithfully and
non-degenerately represented on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the
reduced twisted crossed product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G

(sometimes also denoted by
C^{*}_{r}(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha,\sigma)
) can be
defined as the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
\pi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{A})
and

\lambda_{\sigma}^{G}(G)
inside
\mathcal{B}(l^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}))
, where

\pi_{\alpha}
is the faithful representation of
\mathcal{A}
on

l^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
defined by

(\pi_{\alpha}(a)\zeta)(h)=\alpha_{h^{-1}}(a)\zeta(h),

and, for each
g\in G
,
\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(g)
is the unitary
operator on
l^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
defined by

(\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(g)\zeta)(h)=\sigma(h^{-1},g)\zeta(g^{-1}h),

for
a\in\mathcal{A},\zeta\in l^{2}(G,\mathcal{H}),h\in G
. The pair

(\pi_{\alpha},\lambda^{G}_{\sigma})
satisfies the
covariant relations

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}(\alpha_{g}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(g)\pi_{\alpha}(a)\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(g)^{*}\text{\, and }

\displaystyle\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(g)\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}(\sigma(g,h))\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(gh)

for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
g,h\in G
. It is well known that, upto

*
-isomorphism,
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
is independent
of the faithful representation of
\mathcal{A}
. For convenience, we shall
identify
\mathcal{A}
with
\pi_{\alpha}(\mathcal{A})
. Consider the subset

C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma):=\left\{\sum_{g\in F}a_{g}\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(g)\,\,:\ F\in\mathcal{F}(G),\,\,a_{g}\in\mathcal{A},\,\,g\in G\right\}

of
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
, where
\mathcal{F}(G)
denotes
the collection of finite subsets of
G
. Then,

C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)
is a unital dense
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
. Additionally, we have

\displaystyle(\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(t))^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(t^{-1})\sigma(t,t^{-1})^{*}=\sigma(t,t^{-1})^{*}\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(t^{-1}),\,\,\text{and}

\displaystyle x^{*}_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{g}(x_{g^{-1}})^{*}\sigma(g^{-1},g)^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(g,g^{-1})^{*}\alpha_{g}(x_{g^{-1}})^{*}

for all
t,\,g\in G.

The following observations in the reduced twisted crossed product are
very useful - for more, see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
.

Remark 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A},G
and
\alpha
be as above and
H
be a subgroup of
G
.

(1)

There exists a faithful conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
such that

E(\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(t))=0
for all
t\in G
,
t\neq e
. (
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
)

(2)

Consider
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
as a (right) pre-Hilbert

C^{*}
-module (over
\mathcal{A}
) with respect to the
\mathcal{A}
-valued inner product
given by

\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=E(x^{*}y),\,\,\text{for}\,\,x,y\in\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G.

Further, consider the identity map
\eta_{E}:\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
, where the co-domain is treated as the
pre-Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module. Since
E
is a contraction,

\|\eta_{E}(x)\|=\|E(x^{*}x)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|x\|

for all
x\in\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
.

(3)

The injective map
C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)\ni z\mapsto z\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)
extends to an injective
*
-homomorphism
from
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}H
into
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
. Thus,
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}H
can be considered as a

C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
. (See

[
20
,
\lx@sectionsign 4.26
]
.)

The following useful observation follows from
[
3
, Corollary
2.3]
. However, we provide a direct proof for the sake of
completeness.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},G,\alpha,\sigma)
be a (discrete) twisted
C^{*}
-dynamical
system. If
x=\sum_{g\in F}x_{g}\lambda_{\sigma}(g)\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A},\sigma)
for some
F\in\mathcal{F}(G)
, then

\|x_{g}\|^{2}\leq\|E(x^{*}x)\|=\|\eta_{E}(x)\|^{2}

for all
g\in F
.

Proof.

Notice that, for any
g\in G
,

(x^{*}x)_{g}=\sum_{h\in G}x^{*}_{h}\alpha_{h}(x_{h^{-1}g})\sigma(h,h^{-1}g),

with the convention that
x_{t}=0
for
t\notin F
. Thus,

\displaystyle E(x^{*}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\sum_{g\in G}x^{*}x(g)\lambda^{G}_{\sigma}(g))=(x^{*}x)_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in G}x^{*}_{h}\alpha_{h}(x_{h^{-1}})\sigma(h,h^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in G}\sigma(h,h^{-1})^{*}\alpha_{h}(x^{*}_{h^{-1}}x_{h^{-1}})\sigma(h,h^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in G}\sigma(h,h^{-1})^{*}(\alpha_{h}\circ\alpha_{h^{-1}})(\alpha_{h}(x^{*}_{h^{-1}}x_{h^{-1}}))\sigma(h,h^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in G}\alpha_{h}(x^{*}_{h^{-1}}x_{h^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in G}\alpha_{h^{-1}}(x^{*}_{h}x_{h}).

In particular,
E(x^{*}x)\geq\alpha_{g^{-1}}(x_{g}^{*}x_{g})
for every
g\in F
. Thus,

\|\eta_{E}(x)\|^{2}=\|E(x^{*}x)\|\geq\|\alpha_{g^{-1}}(x_{g}^{*}x_{g})\|=\|x_{g}\|^{2}

for all
g\in F
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\,G,\,\alpha,\,\sigma)
be a (discrete) twisted

C^{*}
-dynamical system and let
H
and
K
be two distinct
subgroups of
G
. Then,

d_{KK}(C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma),C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma))=1=d_{0}(C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma),C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma))

in
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}G
.

Proof.

Note that,
d_{KK}(C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\sigma),C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\sigma))\leq 1
,
by
Remark

2.1
(1), and

\displaystyle d_{0}(C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma),C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma))\leq d_{KK}(C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma),C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)),

by
Remark

2.2
. So, it just remains to show that

d_{0}(C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\sigma),C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\sigma))\geq 1.

Since
H
and
K
are distinct, either
H\neq H\cap K

or
K\neq H\cap K
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that

H\neq H\cap K
. Then, in view of

Remark

5.1
(2) and
Lemma

5.2
, we observe that

\|\lambda_{\sigma}(h)-x\|\geq\|\eta_{E}(\lambda_{\sigma}(h)-x)\|_{\mathcal{A}}\geq 1

for all
h\in H\setminus H\cap K
,
x\in C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)
.
This shows that
C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)\nsubseteq_{\beta}C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)
whenever
0<\beta<1
.
In other words, if
C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)\subseteq_{\beta}C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma)
for some
\beta>0
, then
\beta
must be
\geq 1
.

So, by the definition of
d_{0}
, we must
have

d_{0}(C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma),C_{c}(K,\mathcal{A},\alpha,\sigma))\geq 1.

∎

In view of
Remark

2.1
,
Remark

2.2
and the preceding
proposition, we readily deduce the following:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
G,H,K,\mathcal{A},\alpha
and
\sigma
be as in
Proposition

5.3
. Then,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}H,\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}K)=1=d_{0}(\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}H,\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{(\alpha,\sigma)}K).

5.2.
Distance between subalgebras of crossed-product von Neumann algebras

Let
G
be a discrete group acting on a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
by the
*
-automorphisms
\alpha_{g},g\in G
. Let
\mathcal{\tilde{H}}=l^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
be the set of all square
summable
\mathcal{H}
-valued functions on
G
. Consider the faithful
representations
\pi
of
\mathcal{M}
and
\lambda
of
G
on

\mathcal{\tilde{H}}
given by

\pi(a)\xi(g)=\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a)\xi(g),\,\,\,\,\,\lambda(g)\xi(h)=\xi(g^{-1}h),

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{\tilde{H}},g\in G,h\in G,a\in\mathcal{M}
. These representations satisfy the covariance relation

\lambda(g)\pi(a)\lambda(g)^{*}=\pi(\alpha_{g}(a))

for all
g\in G
and
a\in\mathcal{M}
. The von Neumann algebra
generated by
\pi(\mathcal{M})
and
\lambda(G)
is known as the
crossed-product algebra of
\mathcal{M}
by
G
with respect to
\alpha
,
and it is denoted by
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
.

The following observations are very useful in the crossed-product von
Neumann algebras. For details, see
[
17
, Page 255]
.

Remark 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
,
G
and
\alpha
be as above.

(1)

Consider
\mathcal{M}_{0}:=\{\sum_{g\in G}\pi(x(g))\lambda(g)\,\,|\,\,x:G\rightarrow\mathcal{M}\,\text{is
finitely supported}\}
. Then,
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a
\sigma
-weakly
dense unital
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
.

(2)

For each
g\in G
, consider the operator

P_{g}:\mathcal{\tilde{H}}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\,\,\text{given by}\,\,\,P_{g}(\xi)=\xi(g^{-1}),\,\xi\in\mathcal{\tilde{H}}.

These operators satisfy the following properties:

(a)

P_{g}(\lambda(h))=P_{gh}
;

(b)

P_{g}(\pi(x))=\alpha_{g}(x)P_{g}
;

(c)

P_{g}\pi(x)P_{g}^{*}=\alpha_{g}(x)
; and,

(d)

\sum^{\sigma\text{-}\mathrm{weak}}_{g\in G}P_{g}^{*}\alpha_{g}(x)P_{g}=\pi(x),

for all
g,h\in G
and
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

(3)

Define
E:B(\mathcal{\tilde{H}})\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H})
by
E(x)=P_{e}xP^{*}_{e}
,
x\in B(\mathcal{\tilde{H}})
. Then,
E(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G)=\mathcal{M}
and

E(\lambda(g)x\lambda(g)^{*})=\alpha_{g}(E(x)),

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G,g\in G
.

(iv)

\tilde{E}:=\pi\circ E:\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G\rightarrow\pi(\mathcal{M})
is a faithful normal conditional
expectation.

The following elementary lemma was mentioned in
[
17
]
and will be useful ahead.

Lemma 5.6
.

For any
z\in\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
, we have

\tilde{E}(z)=\sum_{g\in G}Q_{g}zQ_{g},

where
Q_{g}=P^{*}_{g}P_{g}
and the sum converges in the
\sigma
-weak topology.

We now quickly recall the Fourier series expansion of elements of the
crossed-product algebra
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
given by
Mercer (
[
17
]
). Consider the semi-norms on
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
given
by
x\mapsto\omega\circ E(x^{*}x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, for all
\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{*}
. The topology generated by this separating family of
semi-norms is called the Bures topology on
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G

and it will be denoted by
\tau_{\mathcal{B}}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

[
17
]

Let
\mathcal{M},G
and
\alpha
be as above. Then, the following hold:

(1)

x=\sum^{\tau_{\mathcal{B}}}_{g\in G}\pi(x(g))\lambda(g)\,

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
, where

x(g):=E(x\lambda(g)^{*})
.

(2)

For
x,y\in\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
, the multiplication
and
*
-operations in
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
are given by

\displaystyle(xy)(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\sigma\text{-}\mathrm{weak}}_{h\in G}x(h)\alpha_{h}(y(h^{-1}g));\,\text{and,}

\displaystyle x^{*}(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{g}(x(g^{-1})^{*}),\ \text{for all }g\in G.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M},G
and
\alpha
be as above. Then, for every
x\in\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
,

\|x(g)\|\leq\|E(x^{*}x)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|x\|

for all
g\in G
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
. Then,
x=\sum^{\tau_{\mathcal{B}}}_{g\in G}\pi(x(g))\lambda(g)
. Further,

\displaystyle(x^{*}x)(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\sigma\text{-weak}}_{h\in G}x^{*}(h)\alpha_{h}(x(h^{-1}))=\sum^{\sigma\text{-weak}}_{h\in G}\alpha_{h}(x(h^{-1})^{*})\alpha_{h}(x(h^{-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\sigma\text{-weak}}_{h\in G}\alpha_{h^{-1}}(x(h)^{*}x(h)).

Thus, we get

E(x^{*}x)=(x^{*}x)(e)=\sum^{\sigma\text{-}\mathrm{weak}}_{h\in G}\alpha_{h^{-1}}(x(h)^{*}x(h)),

which implies that

E(x^{*}x)\geq\alpha_{g^{-1}}(x(g)^{*}x(g))\,\,\forall\,\,g\in G.

Hence,

\|E(x^{*}x)\|\geq\|\alpha_{g^{-1}}(x(g)^{*}x(g))\|=\|x(g)^{*}x(g)\|=\|x(g)\|^{2}\,\,\forall\,g\in G.

∎

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M},G
and
\alpha
be as above and,
H
and
K
be two
distinct subgroups of
G
. Then,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}H,\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K)=1=d_{0}(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}H,\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K)

in
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}G
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
H\setminus K\neq\emptyset
. Let
h\in H\setminus K
. Then,
\lambda(h)\in B_{1}(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}H)
and for any
y\in\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K
, we get

\displaystyle\|\lambda(h)-y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\lambda(h)-\sum^{\tau_{\mathcal{B}}}_{k\in K}\pi(y(k))\lambda(k)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|E(z^{*}z)\|\geq\|z(h)\|^{2}\,\,\,\text{(by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{inequality})},

where
z=\lambda(h)-\sum^{\tau_{\mathcal{B}}}_{k\in K}\pi(y(k))\lambda(k)
. Note that

\displaystyle z(h)=E(z\lambda(h)^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E((\lambda(h)-\sum^{\tau_{\mathcal{B}}}_{k\in K}\pi(y(k))\lambda(k))\lambda(h)^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\lambda(e)-\sum^{\tau_{\mathcal{B}}}_{k\in K}\pi(y(k))\lambda(kh^{-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{e}(\lambda(e)-\sum^{\tau_{\mathcal{B}}}_{k\in K}\pi(y(k))\lambda(kh^{-1}))P^{*}_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{e}P^{*}_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{H}}.

This implies that
\|\lambda(h)-y\|^{2}\geq 1
for all
h\in H\setminus K
and
y\in\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K
, which shows that
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}H\nsubseteq_{\gamma}\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K
whenever
0<\gamma<1
. Thus, if
\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}H\subseteq_{\gamma}\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K
, then

\gamma
must be
\geq 1
, which implies that

d_{0}(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}H,\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K)\geq 1.

Hence, by
Remark

2.2
(1), (3), it follows that

d_{0}(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}H,\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K)=1=d_{KK}(\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}H,\mathcal{M}\rtimes_{\alpha}K).

∎

Mode Selection in Quantum Nonlinear Optics Using Optical Resonators
Xin Chen

Abstract
Nonlinear optics underpins quantum photonics by enabling the generation and control of quantum states of light. We present new applications of optical resonators as mode selectors in nonlinear processes. First, we show that cavity-enhanced spontaneous parametric down-conversion can generate spectrally uncorrelated photon pairs with improved decorrelation and wavelength flexibility. Second, we demonstrate that a cavity-assisted sum-frequency generation process realizes a quantum pulse gate with high-resolution temporal-mode selectivity and precise spectral control. Our theoretical framework provides a general methodology for analyzing cavity-enhanced nonlinear processes and highlights the versatility of optical resonators as powerful tools for engineering quantum light.

Finally, while resonators are conventionally treated via Langevin equations in the Heisenberg picture with continuous frequency modes, we develop a complementary approach that employs either the interaction picture—providing an intuitive view of spectral correlations—or the Heisenberg picture—yielding accurate solutions in the strong-interaction regime. Fields can be expanded in continuous or discrete frequency domains, a flexibility that enables addressing different situations. This establishes a general foundation for resonator-based nonlinear processes.

Spectrally uncorrelated photon pair via cavity-enhanced SPDC.—

Quantum pulse gating via cavity-enhanced SFG.—

We consider a model of the CSFG process (Fig.
3
) with all fields in a single spatial mode. A strong, undepleted pump pulse with normalized temporal profile
\beta(t)
, defined such that

\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}|\beta(t)|^{2}dt=1
, drives a
\chi^{(2)}
nonlinear medium. In the SFG interaction, a pump photon and a signal photon (
\hat{a}_{s}
, centered at
\omega_{s,c}
) are up-converted into a single idler photon at their sum frequency. The idler channel is resonantly enhanced by a cavity at frequency
\omega_{i,c}
, with intracavity mode
\hat{b}
coupled to the external field
\hat{a}_{i}
. Assuming the pump and signal bandwidths are narrower than the free spectral range
\Omega_{FSR}
, only a single cavity resonance contributes. By appropriately choosing carrier frequencies and polarizations, energy conservation and phase matching are satisfied. As in the CSPDC case, we aim to remove frequency correlations between the signal and idler fields. While this is not possible in the continuous-frequency representation (infinite time window) in the high-conversion regime, it can be achieved with a finite-duration field of length
T
(discrete-frequency representation), which is appropriate for practical applications where only a finite number of TMs within the relevant bandwidth participate.

In the rotating frame defined by
\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})\rightarrow\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}}){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{j,c}t}
,
\hat{b}\rightarrow\hat{b}{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{i,c}t}
,
\beta(t)\rightarrow\beta(t){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{p,c}t}
with
\omega_{s,c}+\omega_{p,c}=\omega_{i,c}

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{1},

(7)

where

\hat{H}_{0}/\hbar=\sum_{j,n_{j}}\omega_{n_{j}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})+i\sqrt{\gamma}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{b}-\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i})

and

\hat{H}_{1}/\hbar=-i\eta[\hat{a}_{s}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\beta(t)-\hat{a}_{s}^{\dagger}\hat{b}\beta^{*}(t)]

with
\hat{a}_{j}=\sqrt{1/T}\sum_{n_{j}}\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})
,
\omega_{n_{j}}=n_{j}\Delta\omega
and
\Delta\omega=2\pi/T
. The spatial integral is taken as approximately constant by suitably choosing the pump and signal bandwidths. Here,
\eta>0

\gamma
is the cavity decay rate. The system is analyzed in both the low- and high-conversion regimes.

For weak interaction, we treat
\hat{H}_{1}

sup
. The TMs are defined as
\hat{A}^{in}_{s,q}(n_{i})=\sum_{n_{s}}\beta(\omega_{n_{i}-n_{s}})\hat{a}^{in}_{s,q}(\omega_{n_{s}})

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{out}_{i,H}(\omega_{n_{i}})={\rm cos}(|\rho_{n_{i}}|)\hat{a}^{in}_{i,H}(\omega_{n_{i}})+{\rm sin}(|\rho_{n_{i}}|)\hat{A}^{in}_{s,H}(n_{i}).

(8)

Shi et al. 2024

To account for internal losses, we model coupling to an ancillary bath mode
\hat{d}^{in}
with loss rate
\iota

\upsilon^{\prime\prime}_{0}=-2\sqrt{\gamma\iota}/(\gamma T+\iota T+\eta^{2})

sup

The QPG based on the CSFG can be naturally extended to MQPG. Let the cavity resonances be
\omega_{i,c,m}=\omega_{i,c}+m\Omega_{FSR}

\hat{a}_{i,m}
, each with bandwidth
\Omega_{FSR}

\hat{a}_{s}

\beta(t)=\sum_{m}\beta_{m}(t){\rm exp}(-i\omega_{p,c,m}t)

\beta_{m}(t){\rm exp}(-i\omega_{p,c,m}t)

sup

In the limit

\hat{a}_{i,m}^{out}(0)=-\hat{A}_{s,m}^{in}(0)

\hat{a}_{i,m}^{out}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\hat{a}_{i,m}^{in}(\omega_{n_{i}})

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that optical resonators enable powerful new applications in nonlinear optics, exemplified by cavity-enhanced SPDC for generating spectrally uncorrelated photon pairs and cavity-assisted SFG for quantum pulse gating. Resonators act as versatile mode selectors, allowing efficient and high-resolution control of spectral correlations. Our approach provides a general theoretical framework and practical tools for extending resonator-based techniques to a broad class of nonlinear processes.

Review of the interaction and Heisenberg picture

The Hamiltonian in Schrödinger picture is given by

\hat{H}_{S}(t)=\hat{H}_{0,S}+\hat{H}_{1,S}(t).

(13)

Let
\ket{\psi_S(t)}=U_{S}(t,t_{0})\ket{\psi_S(t_0)}
be the time-dependent state vector in the Schrödinger picture, where
U_{S}(t,t_{0})={\cal T}{\rm e}^{-i\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\hat{H}_{S}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}/\hbar}
.
The state in the interaction picture is

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_I(t)}

\displaystyle=U^{\dagger}_{0,S}(t,t_{0})\ket{\psi_S(t)}

(14)

\displaystyle=U^{\dagger}_{0,S}(t,t_{0})U_{S}(t,t_{0})\ket{\psi_S(t_0)},

where
U_{0,S}(t,t_{0})={\cal T}{\rm e}^{-i\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\hat{H}_{0,S}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}/\hbar}
.
An operator in the interaction picture is defined as

\hat{A}_{I}(t)=U^{\dagger}_{0,S}(t,t_{0})\hat{A}_{S}(t)U_{0,S}(t,t_{0}),

(15)

where
\hat{A}_{S}(t)
will typically not depend explicitly on
t
and can be rewritten as just
\hat{A}_{S}
. Alternatively,
\hat{A}_{I}(t)
can be obtained by solving

\dot{\hat{A}}_{I}(t)=U^{\dagger}_{0,S}(t,t_{0})\biggl\{\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}_{0,S}(t),\hat{A}_{S}(t)]+\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{S}(t)}{\partial t}\biggr\}U_{0,S}(t,t_{0}).

(16)

If
\hat{A}_{S}
has no explicit dependence on time, the term
\partial\hat{A}_{S}(t)/\partial t=0
. The time evolution of the state in the interaction picture is governed by

i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\ket{\psi_I(t)}=\hat{H}_{1,I}(t)\ket{\psi_I(t)},

(17)

where
\hat{H}_{1,I}(t)=U^{\dagger}_{0,S}(t,t_{0})\hat{H}_{1,S}(t)U_{0,S}(t,t_{0})
. The solution is

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_I(t)}

\displaystyle=U_{1,I}(t,t_{0})\ket{\psi_I(t_0)}=U_{1,I}(t,t_{0})\ket{\psi_S(t_0)},

(18)

where
U_{1,I}(t,t_{0})={\cal T}{\rm e}^{-i\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\hat{H}_{1,I}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}/\hbar}
. Comparing Eq. (
14
) and (
18
) gives
U_{S}(t,t_{0})=U_{0,S}(t,t_{0})U_{1,I}(t,t_{0})
, so that the relation between an operator in the interaction and Heisenberg pictures is

\hat{A}_{H}(t)=U^{\dagger}_{1,I}(t,t_{0})\hat{A}_{I}(t)U_{1,I}(t,t_{0}),

(19)

where
\hat{A}_{H}(t)=U^{\dagger}_{S}(t,t_{0})\hat{A}_{S}(t)U_{S}(t,t_{0})
is the operator in the Heisenberg picture, satisfying the Heisenberg equation of motion

\dot{\hat{A}}_{H}(t)=U^{\dagger}_{S}(t,t_{0})\biggl\{\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{H}_{S}(t),\hat{A}_{S}(t)]+\frac{\partial\hat{A}_{S}(t)}{\partial t}\biggr\}U_{S}(t,t_{0}).

(20)

B.1

We account for internal losses by introducing ancillary bath modes
\hat{d}_{j}^{in}

\iota_{j}

(33)

As dictated by Eq. (

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{out}_{j,H}(\omega_{j})

\displaystyle=U_{1,I}^{\dagger}(\infty,-\infty)\hat{a}_{j,I}^{out}(\omega_{j})U_{1,I}(\infty,-\infty)

Detailed analysis of the CSFG

\hat{a}_{i}

is normalized such that
\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}|\beta(t)|^{2}dt=1
. The mode operators
\hat{a}_{j}=\sqrt{1/T}\sum_{n_{j}}\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})
with
\omega_{n_{j}}=n_{j}\Delta\omega
. Transforming to the rotating frame
\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})\rightarrow\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}}){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{j,c}t}
,
\hat{b}\rightarrow\hat{b}{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{i,c}t}
,
\beta(t)\rightarrow\beta(t){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{p,c}t}
with
\omega_{s,c}+\omega_{p,c}=\omega_{i,c}
, the Hamiltonian simplifies to

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{1},

(51)

where

\hat{H}_{0}/\hbar=\sum_{j,n_{j}}\omega_{n_{j}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})+i\sqrt{\gamma}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{b}-\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i})

and

\hat{H}_{1}/\hbar=-i\eta[\hat{a}_{s}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\beta(t)-\hat{a}_{s}^{\dagger}\hat{b}\beta^{*}(t)]
.

B.4
Interaction-picture analysis in the low-conversion regime.

We begin by treating the CSFG process perturbatively in the interaction picture under the low-conversion assumption. From
\hat{H}_{0}
, the equations of motion for
\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})
and
\hat{b}
in the interaction picture follow as

\dot{\hat{a}}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{s}},t)=-i\omega_{n_{s}}\hat{a}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{s}},t),

(52)

\dot{\hat{a}}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}},t)=-i\omega_{n_{i}}\hat{a}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}},t)+\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{T}}\hat{b}_{I}(t),

(53)

\dot{\hat{b}}_{I}(t)=-\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{a}_{i,I}(t).

(54)

Solving Eqs. (
52
) and (
53
), we obtain
Gardiner and Collett 1985

\hat{a}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{s}},t)={\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n_{s}}(t+\frac{T}{2})}\hat{a}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{s}},-\frac{T}{2}),

(55)

\hat{a}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}},t)={\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n_{i}}(t+\frac{T}{2})}\hat{a}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}},-\frac{T}{2})+\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{T}}\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{t}{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n_{i}}(t-t^{\prime})}\hat{b}_{I}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(56)

yielding

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{s,I}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sum_{n_{s}}\hat{a}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{s}},t)=\hat{a}_{s,I}^{in}(t),

(57)

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{i,I}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sum_{n_{i}}\hat{a}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}},t)=\hat{a}_{i,I}^{in}(t)+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{2}\hat{b}_{I}(t),

(58)

where
\hat{a}_{j,I}^{in(out)}(t)=(1/\sqrt{T})\sum_{n_{j}}\hat{a}^{in(out)}_{j,I}(\omega_{n_{j}}){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n_{j}}t}
, with
\hat{a}^{in(out)}_{j,q}(\omega_{n_{j}})=\hat{a}_{j,q}(\omega_{n_{j}},\mp T/2){\rm e}^{\mp i\omega_{n_{j}}T/2}
(
q\in\{I,H\}
) as defined in the main text. Equation (
55
) can equivalently be written as

\hat{a}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{s}},t)={\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n_{s}}(t-\frac{T}{2})}\hat{a}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{s}},\frac{T}{2}),

(59)

implying

\hat{a}^{out}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{j}})=\hat{a}^{in}_{s,I}(\omega_{n_{j}}).

(60)

Substituting Eq. (
58
) into Eq. (
54
) yields the Langevin equation for the cavity mode

\dot{\hat{b}}_{I}(t)=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\hat{b}_{I}(t)-\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{a}_{i,I}^{in}(t).

(61)

Solving Eq. (
61
) in the frequency domain, we obtain

\hat{b}_{I}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{i\omega_{n_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}}),

(62)

and, using the input–output relation for the intracavity field,

\hat{a}^{out}_{i,I}(t)-\hat{a}^{in}_{i,I}(t)=\sqrt{\gamma_{j}}\hat{b}_{I}(t),

(63)

we have

\hat{a}^{out}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}})={\rm e}^{i\theta_{n_{i}}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}}),

(64)

where
{\rm e}^{i\theta_{n_{i}}}=(i\omega_{n_{i}}+\gamma/2)/(i\omega_{n_{i}}-\gamma/2)
. In the limit of weak perturbation, the interaction-picture evolution operator can be approximated as

\displaystyle U_{1,I}(T/2,-T/2)

\displaystyle\approx{\rm exp}\bigg(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}\hat{H}_{1,I}dt\bigg)

(65)

\displaystyle={\rm exp}\biggl[\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}\eta\hat{a}_{s,I}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{b}_{I}(t)\beta^{*}(t)dt-h.c.\biggr]

\displaystyle={\rm exp}\biggl[\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}\frac{\eta}{(T)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{n_{s},n_{i},n_{p}}\hat{a}_{s,I}^{in\dagger}(\omega_{n_{s}})\hat{b}_{I}(\omega_{n_{i}})\beta^{*}(\omega_{n_{p}}){\rm e}^{i(\omega_{n_{s}}-\omega_{n_{i}}+\omega_{n_{p}})t}dt-h.c.\biggr]

\displaystyle={\rm exp}\biggl[\frac{\eta}{(T)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{n_{s},n_{i},n_{p}}\hat{a}_{s,I}^{in\dagger}(\omega_{n_{s}})\hat{b}_{I}(\omega_{n_{i}})\beta^{*}(\omega_{n_{p}})\delta_{n_{s}-n_{i}+n_{p},0}-h.c.\biggr]

\displaystyle={\rm exp}\biggl[\frac{\eta}{(T)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{n_{s},n_{i}}\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}\beta^{*}(\omega_{n_{i}-n_{s}})}{(i\omega_{n_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2})}\hat{a}_{s,I}^{in\dagger}(\omega_{n_{s}})\hat{a}_{i,I}^{in}(\omega_{n_{i}})-h.c.\biggr]

\displaystyle\approx{\rm exp}\big[\sum_{n_{i}=-\infty}^{\infty}\rho_{n_{i}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}})\hat{A}^{in\dagger}_{s,I}(n_{i})-h.c.\big],

where
\rho_{n_{i}}=\eta\sqrt{\gamma/T}/(i\omega_{n_{i}}-\gamma/2)
and
\hat{A}^{in}_{s,q}(n_{i})=\sum_{n_{s}}\beta(\omega_{n_{i}-n_{s}})\hat{a}^{in}_{s,q}(\omega_{n_{s}})
. From Eq. (
60
) and (
64
), we have
\hat{a}^{out}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}})={\rm e}^{i\theta_{n_{i}}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}})={\rm e}^{i\theta_{n_{i}}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i,H}(\omega_{n_{i}})
and
\hat{A}^{out}_{s,I}(n_{i})=\hat{A}^{in}_{s,I}(n_{i})=\hat{A}^{in}_{s,H}(n_{i})
. As dictated by Eq. (
19
), the Heisenberg-picture output operator reads

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{out}_{i,H}(\omega_{n_{i}})

\displaystyle=U_{1,H}^{\dagger}(T/2,-T/2)\hat{a}^{out}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}})U_{1,I}(T/2,-T/2)

(66)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{i\theta_{n_{i}}}U_{1,H}^{\dagger}(T/2,-T/2)\hat{a}^{in}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}})U_{1,I}(T/2,-T/2)

\displaystyle={\rm cos}(|\rho_{n_{i}}|){\rm e}^{i\theta_{n_{i}}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i,I}(\omega_{n_{i}})+{\rm sin}(|\rho_{n_{i}}|){\rm e}^{i(\theta_{n_{i}}+\phi_{n_{i}})}\hat{A}^{in}_{s,I}(n_{i})

\displaystyle={\rm cos}(|\rho_{n_{i}}|){\rm e}^{i\theta_{n_{i}}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i,H}(\omega_{n_{i}})+{\rm sin}(|\rho_{n_{i}}|){\rm e}^{i(\theta_{n_{i}}+\phi_{n_{i}})}\hat{A}^{in}_{s,H}(n_{i})

\displaystyle\to{\rm cos}(|\rho_{n_{i}}|)\hat{a}^{in}_{i,H}(\omega_{n_{i}})+{\rm sin}(|\rho_{n_{i}}|)\hat{A}^{in}_{s,H}(n_{i}).

In the final step of the calculation, we absorb the phases
{\rm e}^{i\phi_{n_{i}}}=\rho_{n_{i}}/|\rho_{n_{i}}|
and
{\rm e}^{i\theta_{n_{i}}}
into the operators
\hat{a}^{in}_{i,H}(\omega_{n_{i}})
and
\hat{A}^{in}_{s,H}(n_{i})
for notational convenience.

B.5
Heisenberg-picture analysis in the high-conversion regime.

While the previous discussion treated the CSFG process perturbatively in the interaction picture, we now consider the Heisenberg-picture evolution, enabling a non-perturbative description suitable for higher conversion regimes. Starting from Eq. (
51
), the Heisenberg equations of motion for
\hat{a}_{j}(\omega_{n_{j}})
and
\hat{b}
are obtained as

\dot{\hat{a}}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}},t)=-i\omega_{n_{s}}\hat{a}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}},t)+\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{T}}\hat{b}(t)\beta^{*}(t),

(67)

\dot{\hat{a}}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}},t)=-i\omega_{n_{i}}\hat{a}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}},t)+\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{T}}\hat{b}(t),

(68)

\dot{\hat{b}}(t)=-\eta\hat{a}_{s}(t)\beta(t)-\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{a}_{i}(t).

(69)

Solving Eqs. (
67
)–(
68
), following the method of Ref.
Gardiner and Collett 1985
, yields

\hat{a}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}},t)={\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n_{s}}(t+\frac{T}{2})}\hat{a}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}},-\frac{T}{2})+\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{T}}\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{t}{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n}(t-t^{\prime})}\hat{b}(t^{\prime})\beta^{*}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(70)

\hat{a}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}},t)={\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n_{i}}(t+\frac{T}{2})}\hat{a}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}},-\frac{T}{2})+\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{T}}\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{t}{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{n_{i}}(t-t^{\prime})}\hat{b}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(71)

yielding

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{s}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sum_{n_{s}}\hat{a}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}},t)=\hat{a}_{s}^{in}(t)+\frac{\eta}{2}\hat{b}(t)\beta^{*}(t),

(72)

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sum_{n_{i}}\hat{a}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}},t)=\hat{a}_{i}^{in}(t)+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{2}\hat{b}(t).

(73)

Substituting Eqs. (
72
) and (
73
) into Eq. (
69
), we obtain the Langevin equation for the cavity mode

\dot{\hat{b}}(t)=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\hat{b}(t)-\eta\hat{a}_{s}^{in}(t)\beta(t)-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\hat{b}(t)|\beta(t)|^{2}-\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{a}_{i}^{in}(t),

(74)

with periodic boundary condition
\hat{b}(T/2)=\hat{b}(-T/2)
.

In Ref.
Chen and Ye 2025
, we considered a special case in which the pump spectral amplitudes
\beta(\omega_{-n_{p}})
are represented by a large set of independent random numbers drawn from a common complex Gaussian distribution. In the limit
M\to\infty
, this yields
|\beta(t)|^{2}=1/T
, since
{\rm lim}_{M\to\infty}\sum_{k_{p}=1}^{M}\beta(\omega_{k_{p}-n_{p}})\beta^{*}(\omega_{k_{p}-m_{p}})=\delta_{m_{p},n_{p}}
. Solving Eq. (
74
) in frequency domain, we find

\hat{b}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\frac{\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{T}}}{i\omega_{n_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2T}}\hat{A}_{s}^{in}(n_{i})+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{i\omega_{n_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2T}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}}),

(75)

and, using the input–output relation associated with the cavity mode,

\hat{a}_{i}^{out}(t)-\hat{a}_{i}^{in}(t)=\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{b}(t),

(76)

we obtain

\hat{a}^{out}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\frac{\eta\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{T}}}{i\omega_{n_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2T}}\hat{A}_{s}^{in}(n_{i})+\frac{i\omega_{n_{i}}+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2T}}{i\omega_{n_{i}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2T}}\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}}).

(77)

In the limit
\eta=\sqrt{\gamma T}\rightarrow 0
(
\gamma/\Delta\omega\rightarrow 0
), the output reduces to

\hat{a}^{out}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\begin{cases}-\hat{A}_{s}^{in}(0)&\text{if}\ n_{j}=0\\
\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(\omega_{n_{j}})&\text{if}\ n_{j}\neq 0.\end{cases}

(78)

This limiting case provides a useful benchmark for the general solution.

For the general case of TM, the solution of Eq. (
74
) reads

\hat{b}(t)=\sum_{j}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}g_{j}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{a}^{in}_{j}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(79)

with the response functions

\displaystyle g_{j}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\big[\frac{{\rm e}^{-\frac{\gamma T}{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}}}{1-{\rm e}^{-\frac{\gamma T}{2}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}}}+u(t-t^{\prime})\big]h_{j}(t^{\prime}){\rm e}^{-\int^{t}_{t^{\prime}}[\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}|\beta(t^{\prime\prime})|^{2}]dt^{\prime\prime}},

(80)

where
h_{s}(t)=\eta\beta(t)
,
h_{i}(t)=\sqrt{\gamma}
, and
u
is the unit step function. Transforming to the frequency domain, the intracavity operator can be expressed as

\hat{b}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\sum_{m_{j}}\tilde{g}_{j}(\omega_{n_{i}},\omega_{m_{j}})\hat{a}^{in}_{j}(\omega_{m_{j}}),

(81)

with

\tilde{g}_{j}(\omega_{n_{i}},\omega_{m_{j}})=\frac{1}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}dt\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}dt^{\prime}{\rm e}^{i\omega_{n_{i}}t}g_{j}(t,t^{\prime}){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{m_{j}}t^{\prime}}.

(82)

Combining this with Eq. (
76
), the idler output in the frequency domain becomes

\hat{a}^{out}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\sum_{m_{j}}G_{j}(\omega_{n_{i}},\omega_{m_{j}})\hat{a}^{in}_{j}(\omega_{m_{j}}),

(83)

where the transfer functions are

G_{s}(\omega_{n_{i}},\omega_{m_{s}})=\sqrt{\gamma}\tilde{g}_{s}(\omega_{n_{i}},\omega_{m_{s}}),\quad G_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}},\omega_{m_{i}})=\sqrt{\gamma}\tilde{g}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}},\omega_{m_{i}})+\delta_{{n_{i}},{m_{i}}}.

(84)

Applying singular-value decomposition to
G_{j}
, we write

G_{j}(\omega_{n_{i}},\omega_{m_{j}})=\sum_{k}\lambda_{j,k}\psi_{k}(\omega_{n_{i}})\varphi_{j,k}(\omega_{m_{j}}),

(85)

with
\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j,k}|^{2}=1
to preserve unitarity. The CE of the
k
-th Schmidt mode is given by
|\lambda_{s,k}|^{2}
and the separability of the target TM is defined as
|\lambda_{s,0}|^{2}/\sum_{k}|\lambda_{s,k}|^{2}

Reddy et al. 2013
. These quantities provide a quantitative measure of how effectively the QPG selects the desired target mode.

In the limit
\eta\sim\sqrt{\gamma T}\rightarrow 0
(
\gamma/\Delta\omega\rightarrow 0
), Eq. (
80
) reduces to

g_{j}(t,t^{\prime})=-2h_{j}(t^{\prime})/(\gamma T+\eta^{2}).

(86)

Consequently, the intracavity field becomes

\displaystyle\hat{b}(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}\frac{2}{\gamma T+\eta^{2}}\big[\eta\beta(t^{\prime})\hat{a}_{s}^{in}(t^{\prime})+\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{a}_{i}^{in}(t^{\prime})\big]dt^{\prime}

(87)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\gamma T+\eta^{2}}[\eta\hat{A}_{s}^{in}(0)+\sqrt{\gamma T}\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(0)],

where
\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(0)=(1/\sqrt{T})\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(t)\,dt
denotes the zero-frequency mode. In the frequency domain, this reduces to

\hat{b}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\begin{cases}-\frac{2}{\gamma T+\eta^{2}}[\eta\sqrt{T}\hat{A}_{s}^{in}(0)+\sqrt{\gamma}T\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(0)]&\text{if}\ n_{i}=0\\
0&\text{if}\ n_{i}\neq 0,\end{cases}

(88)

and the corresponding idler input–output relation reads

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{i}^{out}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{\gamma T+\eta^{2}}[2\eta\sqrt{\gamma T}\hat{A}_{s}^{in}(0)+(\gamma T-\eta^{2})\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(0)]&\text{if}\ n_{i}=0\\
\hat{a}_{i}^{in}(\omega_{n_{i}})&\text{if}\ n_{i}\neq 0.\end{cases}

(89)

B.6
Effect of internal loss

To account for internal losses, we model coupling to an ancillary bath mode
\hat{d}^{in}
with loss rate
\iota
. Then Eq. (
74
) becomes

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{b}}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\hat{b}(t)-\frac{\iota}{2}\hat{b}(t)-\eta\hat{a}_{s}^{in}(t)\beta(t)-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\hat{b}(t)|\beta(t)|^{2}-\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{a}_{i}^{in}(t)-\sqrt{\iota}\hat{d}^{in}(t).

(90)

Applying the same method used for solving Eq. (
74
) in the general TM case, and taking the limit
\eta\sim\sqrt{(\gamma+\iota)T}\to 0
, the idler output in the frequency domain is found to be

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{out}_{i}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\begin{cases}\mu^{\prime\prime}_{0}\hat{A}_{s}^{in}(0)+\nu^{\prime\prime}_{0}\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(0)+\upsilon^{\prime\prime}_{0}\hat{d}^{in}(0)&\text{if}\ n_{j}=0\\
\hat{a}^{in}_{i}(\omega_{n_{j}})&\text{if}\ n_{j}\neq 0,\end{cases}

(91)

with coefficients

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\eta\sqrt{\gamma T}}{\gamma T+\iota T+\eta^{2}},

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta^{2}-\gamma T+\iota T}{\gamma T+\iota T+\eta^{2}},

\displaystyle\upsilon^{\prime\prime}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma\iota}}{\gamma T+\iota T+\eta^{2}}.

The CE,
|\mu_{0}^{\prime\prime}|^{2}
, of the target TM reaches its maximum value
1/(1+\iota/\gamma)
, when
\eta=\sqrt{(\gamma+\iota)T}
.

B.7
Multi-output QPG

The MQPG, based on the CSFG process, is governed by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}/\hbar

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}\omega_{i,c,m}\hat{b}_{m}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{m}+\sum_{n_{s}}(\omega_{n_{s}}+\omega_{s,c})\hat{a}_{s}^{\dagger}(\omega_{n_{s}})\hat{a}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}})+\sum_{m,n_{i}}(\omega_{n_{i}}+\omega_{i,c,m})\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,m}(\omega_{n_{i}})\hat{a}_{i,m}(\omega_{n_{i}})

(92)

\displaystyle+i\sqrt{\gamma}(\sum_{m}\hat{a}_{i,m}^{\dagger}\sum_{m^{\prime}}\hat{b}_{m^{\prime}}-\sum_{m^{\prime}}\hat{b}_{m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\sum_{m}\hat{a}_{i,m})-i\eta[\hat{a}_{s}\sum_{m}\hat{b}_{m}^{\dagger}\sum_{m^{\prime}}\beta_{m^{\prime}}(t){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{p,c,m^{\prime}}t}

\displaystyle-\hat{a}_{s}^{\dagger}\sum_{m}\hat{b}_{m}\sum_{m^{\prime}}\beta_{m^{\prime}}^{*}(t){\rm e}^{i\omega_{p,c,m^{\prime}}t}],

where
\omega_{i,c,m}=\omega_{i,c}+m\Omega_{FSR}
,
\omega_{p,c,m}=\omega_{p,c}+m\Omega_{FSR}
, and
\hat{a}_{i,m}=\sqrt{1/T}\sum_{n_{i}}\hat{a}_{i,m}(\omega_{n_{i}})
denotes the external idler mode, coupled to the cavity, restricted to a bandwidth
\Omega_{FSR}
around
\omega_{i,c,m}
, associated with intracavity mode
\hat{b}_{m}
.

In the rotating frame defined by
\hat{a}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}})\rightarrow\hat{a}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}}){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{s,c}t}
,
\hat{a}_{i,m}(\omega_{n_{i}})\rightarrow\hat{a}_{i,m}(\omega_{n_{i}}){\rm e}^{-i\omega_{i,c,m}t}
and
\hat{b}_{m}\rightarrow\hat{b}_{m}{\rm e}^{-i\omega_{i,c,m}t}
, the Langevin equations for the cavity modes become
Knöll et al. 1991

\dot{\hat{b}}_{k}(t)=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\hat{b}_{k}(t)-\eta\hat{a}_{s}^{in}(t)\beta_{k}(t)-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2}\sum_{m}\hat{b}_{m}(t)\beta_{m}^{*}(t)\beta_{k}(t)-\sqrt{\gamma}\hat{a}_{i,k}^{in}(t),

(93)

where rapidly rotating terms
\propto{\rm exp}(-im\Omega_{FSR}t)
with
m\neq 0
have been neglected.

Solving Eq. (
93
) in the limit
\eta\sim\sqrt{\gamma T}\rightarrow 0
(
\gamma/\Delta\omega\rightarrow 0
), we obtain an analogous solution to Eq. (
87
):

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{k}(t)=-\frac{2}{\gamma T+\eta^{2}}[\eta\hat{A}_{s,k}^{in}(0)+\sqrt{\gamma T}\hat{a}^{in}_{i,k}(0)]+K_{k},

(94)

where
\hat{A}^{in}_{s,k}(0)=\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}dt\beta_{k}(t)\hat{a}_{s}^{in}(t)=\sum_{n_{s}}\beta_{k}(\omega_{-n_{s}})\hat{a}^{in}_{s}(\omega_{n_{s}})
, and the additional term

K_{k}\propto\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}dt\sum_{m\neq k}\hat{b}_{m}(t)\beta_{m}^{*}(t)\beta_{k}(t)
. Eq. (
94
) is time-independent, so only the zero-frequency component survives:
\hat{b}_{k}(\omega_{n_{i}})=0
for
n_{i}\neq 0
as in Eq. (
88
).
Consequently,
\hat{b}_{m}(t)=(1/\sqrt{T})\sum_{n_{i}}\hat{b}_{m}(\omega_{n_{i}}){\rm exp}(-i\omega_{n_{i}}t)=(1/\sqrt{T})\hat{b}_{m}(\omega_{0})
and the additional term vanishes due to the orthogonality of
\{\beta_{m}(t)\}
:

K_{k}\propto\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}dt\sum_{m\neq k}\hat{b}_{m}(\omega_{0})\beta_{m}^{*}(t)\beta_{k}(t)=\sum_{m\neq k}\hat{b}_{m}(\omega_{0})\delta_{m,k}=0.

(95)

Thus, the idler input–output relation for each cavity resonance is

\hat{a}_{i,k}^{out}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{\gamma T+\eta^{2}}[2\eta\sqrt{\gamma T}\hat{A}_{s,k}^{in}(0)+(\gamma T-\eta^{2})\hat{a}^{in}_{i,k}(0)]&\text{if}\ n=0\\
\hat{a}_{i,k}^{in}(\omega_{n_{i}})&\text{if}\ n\neq 0.\end{cases}

(96)

In the special case
\eta=\sqrt{\gamma T}
, the output modes simplify to

\hat{a}^{out}_{i,k}(\omega_{n_{i}})=\begin{cases}-\hat{A}_{s,k}^{in}(0)&\text{if}\ n_{j}=0\\
\hat{a}^{in}_{i,k}(\omega_{n_{j}})&\text{if}\ n_{j}\neq 0.\end{cases}

(97)

This implements a multiport frequency-bin interferometer for linear optical quantum networks
Folge et al. 2024
, with the idler output given by
\hat{a}_{i,m}^{out}(0)=\sum_{l}U_{ml}\hat{a}^{in}_{s}(\omega_{l})
, where
U_{ml}=-\beta_{m}(\omega_{-l})
.

The Hopf–Rinow theorem and the Mañé critical value for magnetic geodesics on odd-dimensional spheres
P. Albers, G. Benedetti, L. Maier

Abstract
. The subject of this article are magnetic geodesics on odd-dimensional spheres endowed with the round metric and with the magnetic potential given by the standard contact form. We compute the Mañé’s critical value of the system and show that a value of the energy is supercritical if and only if all pairs of points on the sphere can be connected by a magnetic geodesic with that value of the energy. Our methods are explicit and rely on the description of the submanifolds invariant by the flow and of the symmetries of the system, which we define for a general magnetic system and call totally magnetic submanifolds and magnetomorphisms, respectively. We recover hereby the known fact that the system is super-integrable: the three-spheres obtained intersecting the ambient space with a complex plane are totally magnetic and each magnetic geodesic is tangent to a two-dimensional Clifford torus. In our study the integral of motion given by the angle between magnetic geodesics and the Reeb vector field plays a special role, and can be used to realize the magnetic flow as an interpolation between the sub-Riemannian geodesic flow of the contact distribution and the Reeb flow of the contact form.

1.
Introduction and statement of results

In the 1960s the motion of a charged particle in a magnetic field was put into the context of modern dynamical systems by V. Arnold in his pioneering work
[
5
]
. The motion has the following mathematical description. Let
(M,g)
be a closed, connected Riemannian manifold and
\sigma\in\Omega^{2}(M)
be a closed two-form. The form
\sigma
is called
magnetic field
and the triple
(M,g,\sigma)
is called
magnetic system
. This determines the skew-symmetric bundle endomorphism
Y\colon TM\to TM
, the
Lorentz force
, by

(1.1)

g_{q}\left(Y_{q}u,v\right)=\sigma_{q}(u,v),\qquad\forall\,q\in M,\ \forall\,u,v\in T_{q}M.

Then a smooth curve
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to M
satisfying

(1.2)

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=Y_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}

is called a
magnetic geodesic
of
(M,g,\sigma)
. Here
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita of the metric
g
. From (
1.2
), we see that a magnetic geodesic with
\sigma=0
is a standard geodesic for the metric
g
and we can consider (
1.2
) as a linear perturbation of the geodesic equation. Therefore, one of the main points of interest is to work out the similarities and differences between standard and magnetic geodesics.

Since
Y
is skew-symmetric, magnetic geodesics have constant kinetic energy
E(\gamma,\dot{\gamma}):=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})
, and hence constant speed
|\dot{\gamma}|:=\sqrt{g_{\gamma}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})}
, just like standard geodesics.

Energy conservation is a footprint of the Hamiltonian nature of the system. Indeed, let us define the
magnetic geodesic flow
on the tangent bundle by

\varPhi_{g,\sigma}^{t}\colon TM\to TM,\quad(q,v)\mapsto\left(\gamma_{q,v}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{q,v}(t)\right),\quad\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\gamma_{q,v}(t)
is the unique magnetic geodesic with initial values
(q,v)\in TM
. By
[
19
]
,
\Phi^{t}_{g,\sigma}
is the Hamiltonian flow induced by the kinetic energy
E\colon TM\to{\mathbb{R}}
and the twisted symplectic form

\omega_{\sigma}:={\mathrm{d}}\lambda-\pi^{*}_{TM}\sigma,

where
\lambda
is the metric pullback of the canonical Liouville
1
-form from
T^{*}M
to
TM
and
\pi_{TM}\colon TM\to M
is the projection.

However, differently from the case of standard geodesics, magnetic geodesics of different speeds are not just reparametrization of unit speed magnetic geodesics. For instance, if
M={\mathbb{S}}^{2}
,
g
has constant curvature
1
and
\sigma
is the corresponding area form, then magnetic geodesics of kinetic energy
k
are geodesic circles of radius
\arctan(\sqrt{2k})
traversed counterclockwise, see
[
6
]
. What we can say in general is that, for every
s>0
,
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic of
(M,g,\sigma)
with speed
\tfrac{1}{s}
if and only if the unit speed reparametrization of
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic of
(M,g,s\sigma)
. This means that investigating magnetic geodesics of
(M,g,\sigma)
for different speeds is the same as investigating the magnetic geodesics of
(M,g,s\sigma)
with unit speed and varying
s>0
. This approach has the advantage that for
s=0
we recover standard geodesics and for
s<0
, we recover the magnetic geodesics of
(M,g,\sigma)
with the opposite orientation. With this notation,
s
will be called the
strength
of the magnetic geodesic.

The main focus of the present article is to compare standard and magnetic geodesics by means of the Hopf–Rinow theorem. According to this landmark result, every two points on the closed connected manifold
M
can be connected by a standard geodesic
[
30
]
. As explained above, this is independent of the speed or energy of the required geodesic. Therefore, it is natural to ask if there is a magnetic geodesic connecting two given points
p,q\in M
with a prescribed kinetic energy
k
. An important role in this question is played by the Mañé critical value

(1.3)

c(M,g,\sigma):=\inf_{{\mathrm{d}}\hat{\alpha}=\hat{\sigma}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|\hat{\alpha}\|_{\infty}^{2}\;\in[0,\infty]

of the universal cover
\hat{M}
of
M
,
[
23
,
15
]
. Here the infimum is taken over all primitives
\hat{\alpha}
of the lift
\hat{\sigma}
of
\sigma
to the universal cover
\hat{M}
of
M
and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
denote the supremum norm over
\hat{M}
with respect to the lift
\hat{g}
of the metric
g
. The Mañé’s critical value has equivalent definitions in terms of the action functional. In particular, if
M
is simply connected and
\sigma
is exact, then
-c(M,g,\sigma)
is the minimal action of a Borel invariant probability measure for the system. As a consequence, the energy level
\{E=c(M,g,\sigma)\}\subset TM
contains the so-called Mather set
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
which is the support of the action-minimizing probability measures of the system
[
31
]
. We refer to Section
2
for more background on the Mañé’s critical value.

Our interest in the critical value relies on the fact that if
k>c(M,g,\sigma)
, then every two points on
M
can be connected by a magnetic geodesic of energy
k
, as follows from
[
14
, Corollary B]
when
\sigma
is exact and from
[
26
, Theorem 3.2]
in the general case. Indeed, if
k>c(M,g,\sigma)
, there exists a primitive
\hat{\alpha}
such that

(1.4)

F\colon T\hat{M}\to{\mathbb{R}},\qquad F(\hat{q},\hat{v}):=\sqrt{2k}|\hat{v}|_{\hat{q}}-\hat{\alpha}_{\hat{q}}(\hat{v}),\quad\forall\,(\hat{q},\hat{v})\in T\hat{M}

is a Finsler metric on
\hat{M}
whose geodesics are lifts of magnetic geodesics on
M
with energy
k
. Finsler geodesics connecting pairs of points on
\hat{M}
can then be found by minimizing the
F
-length of paths and project to the desired magnetic geodesics on
M
.

If
k\leq c(M,g,\sigma)
, then there might be pairs of points which cannot be not connected by a magnetic geodesic with energy
k
, as the example of
({\mathbb{S}}^{2},g,\sigma)
mentioned above shows. Indeed, in this case
c({\mathbb{S}}^{2},g,\sigma)=\infty
since
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
is simply connected and
\sigma
is not exact, and two points can be connected by a magnetic geodesic of energy
k
if and only if they are at distance at most
2\arctan(\sqrt{2k})
, which is strictly less than
\pi
, the diameter of
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
. In higher dimensions, except for the case of Kähler magnetic systems with constant holomorphic curvature
[
3
]
, there is no good understanding about which pairs of points fail to be connected for a given subcritical energy, even for simple systems.

The main result of this article is to analyze the existence of magnetic geodesics connecting pairs of points for the magnetic system

(1.5)

({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha).

Here the manifold
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
is the sphere of radius
1
in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with standard Hermitian product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, the metric
g=\mathrm{Re}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the restriction of the Euclidean metric to
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, and the magnetic potential
\alpha
is the standard contact form on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, that is,
\alpha_{z}=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}z,\cdot\rangle
for all
z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
. The Reeb vector field of
\alpha
is the unique vector field
R
on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
such that
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha(R,\cdot)=0
and
\alpha(R)=1
. In this case, we get
R_{z}=2\mathrm{i}z
. The trajectories of the flow

(1.6)

\Phi_{R}^{t}(z)=e^{2\mathrm{i}t}z,\qquad\forall\,t\in{\mathbb{R}},\ z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}

are the fibers of the Hopf map
\pi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}\to\mathbb{C}P^{n}
, which sends each point on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
to the complex line through it. This is the simplest example of a Zoll Reeb flow, where all orbits of the Reeb vector field are periodic and with the same minimal period
[
4
,
1
]
. Finally,
\ker\alpha
is the contact distribution of
\alpha
and, in our case, coincides with the orthogonal of
R
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The Mañé’s critical value of the system is

c({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|_{\infty}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{8}.

The Mather set of the system is

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=\{(z,\tfrac{1}{4}R_{z})\mid z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}\}\subset T{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}.

Let
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
be two points on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and denote by
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle
their Hermitian product. For every
k>0
, let
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})
be the set of magnetic geodesics with energy
k
connecting
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
. We have the following three cases

(1)

if
k>\tfrac{1}{8}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
;

(2)

if
k=\tfrac{1}{8}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle\neq 0
;

(3)

if
k<\tfrac{1}{8}
, we have the following three subcases

(a)

if
|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|>\sqrt{1-8k}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
;

(b)

if
|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|=\sqrt{1-8k}
, then there are
a_{k},b_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
b_{k}>0
such that
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle=e^{\mathrm{i}(a_{k}+mb_{k})}\sqrt{1-8k}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
;

(c)

if
|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|<\sqrt{1-8k}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{k}(q_{0},q_{1})=\varnothing
.

.

The result about the Mañé’s critical value and the Mather set generalizes to magnetic systems

The fact that

, which constructs an action minimizing magnetic geodesics, or equivalently, a length-minimizing geodesic for a Finsler metric like in (

is not variational but relies on an explicit topological argument, see Subsection

1.1.
Magnetomorphisms and totally magnetic submanifolds

A key feature of the magnetic system
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
is that it has a large group of symmetries and, as a byproduct, a large family of invariant submanifolds. The symmetries of a general magnetic system
(M,g,\sigma)
are given by so-called
magnetomorphisms

F\colon M\to M
, that is, diffeomorphisms that preserve both
g
and
\sigma
. As a consequence, magnetomorphisms send magnetic geodesics to magnetic geodesics with the same energy, see Proposition
6.4
. On the other hand, invariant submanifolds are given by so-called
totally magnetic submanifolds

N\subset M
which have the property that every magnetic geodesic that is tangent to
N
is locally contained in
N
. Thus magnetomorphisms send totally magnetic submanifolds to totally magnetic submanifolds.

When
\sigma=0
, totally magnetic submanifolds recover the classical notion of totally geodesic submanifolds. In Riemannian geometry, there are many equivalent ways to define a totally geodesic submanifold. In particular, a submanifold is totally geodesic if and only if its second fundamental form
\mathrm{II}
vanishes. To provide a similar statement for magnetic subsystems we introduce in
6.9
the
magnetic second fundamental form

\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}
, which is a combination of the second fundamental form
\mathrm{II}
and the difference of the intrinsic and extrinsic Lorentz forces.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
be a magnetic system. Let
N\subset M
be a closed, embedded submanifold. Denote by
\iota\colon N\to M
the inclusion map and by
g_{N}:=\iota^{*}g_{M}
and
\sigma_{N}:=\iota^{*}\sigma_{M}
the pullback metric and magnetic field. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

The submanifold
N
is totally magnetic in
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
.

(2)

If
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic in
\left(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N}\right)
then
\iota\circ\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic in
\left(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M}\right)
.

(3)

The magnetic second fundamental form of
\left(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N}\right)
vanishes identically:

\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}_{q}(v)=0\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN.

(4)

The second fundamental form of
(N,g_{N})
vanishes identically

\mathrm{II}_{q}(v)=0\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN.

and one of the following three equivalent conditions holds

(a)

the Lorenz force
Y^{M}
of
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
along
N
is equal to the Lorentz force
Y^{N}
of
(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N})
:

Y^{N}_{q}v=Y^{M}_{q}v\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN;

(b)

the Lorentz force
Y^{M}
leaves the tangent bundle of
N
invariant:

Y_{q}^{M}v\in TN\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN;

(c)

the
g_{M}
-orthogonal of
TN
is contained in the
\sigma_{M}
-orthogonal of
TN
:

(\sigma_{M})_{q}(u,v)=0\quad\forall q\in N,\ u\in T_{q}N,\ v\in T_{q}N^{\perp}.

As an application, we classify magnetomorphisms and
closed, connected
totally magnetic submanifolds with
positive dimension
of our magnetic system of interest.

Corollary 1.5
.

The group of magnetomorphisms of
\left({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
is the group of unitary matrices, that is,

\mathrm{Mag}({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)=U(n+1).

The lift of the magnetomorphism group to
T{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
is Hamiltonian with respect to the twisted symplectic form
\omega_{{\mathrm{d}}\alpha}
. Its moment map is given by

\mu\colon T{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}\to\mathfrak{u}(n+1)^{*},\qquad\mu(z,v)[A]:=g_{z}(Az,v)-\alpha_{z}(Az),\quad\forall\,A\in\mathfrak{u}(n+1),\ \forall\,(z,v)\in T{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}.

A closed, connected submanifold
N
of
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
of positive dimension is totally magnetic if and only if
N={\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}\cap V
, where
V
is a complex linear subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
. In this case,
N
is magnetomorphic to
{\mathbb{S}}^{2j+1}
for some
j
, that is,

\left(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N}\right)\cong\left({\mathbb{S}}^{2j+1},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right).

Remark 1.6
.

Corollary
1.5
illustrates the difference between totally magnetic submanifols and totally geodesic submanifolds. Indeed, every sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{h}
obtained by intersecting
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
with a
real
linear subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
is totally geodesic, while only spheres
{\mathbb{S}}^{2j+1}
from intersections with
complex
linear subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
yield totally magnetic submanifolds.

As a special case of Corollary
1.5
we obtain a new proof of the following known fact
[
16
]
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Each magnetic geodesic in
\left({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha\right)
is contained in a 3-sphere obtained intersecting the ambient sphere with a complex plane. ∎

1.2.
Contact angle, Clifford tori and Hopf fibration.

To continue our discussion, it is convenient to switch to the description of magnetic geodesics of
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
with energy
k
as magnetic geodesics of unit speed for the family of systems
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,s{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
where
s=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}
is now referred as the strength of the magnetic geodesic. In this description, Mañé’s critical value correspond to

(1.7)

s_{0}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\tfrac{1}{8}}}=2.

Among all symmetries given by Corollary
1.5
, the one corresponding to the Hopf flow (
1.6
) plays a special role since it corresponds to the center of the group
U(n+1)
. It is generated by
A=\mathrm{i}\in\mathfrak{u}(n+1)
and yields the first integral

(1.8)

\mu(z,v)[\mathrm{i}]+\tfrac{1}{2}=g_{z}(\mathrm{i}z,v)=:\cos\psi(z,v),

so that
\psi=\psi(z,v)\in[0,\pi]
is the angle between the tangent vector
v
and the Reeb vector field
R_{z}=2\mathrm{i}z
at
z
. We refer to
\psi
as the
contact angle
. For every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the magnetic geodesics of strength
s
with
\psi=0
, respectively
\psi=\pi
, are exactly the orbits of the Reeb vector field with the positive, respectively, negative orientation. Magnetic geodesics with
\psi=\tfrac{\pi}{2}
are tangent to the contact distribution
\ker\alpha
. By
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
, it follows that as
s
varies over
{\mathbb{R}}
magnetic geodesics tangent to
\ker\alpha
are exactly the sub-Riemannian geodesics of
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,\ker\alpha)
. We can summarize this discussion in the following result.

Corollary 1.8
.

The magnetic geodesic flow
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
is an interpolation between the sub-Riemannian geodesic flow on
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,\ker\alpha)
and the Reeb flow of
\alpha
on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, where the interpolation parameter is the contact angle
\psi
. ∎

We now move to analyze magnetic geodesics for an arbitrary value of the contact angle
\psi\in[0,\pi]
. By
[
11
]
, magnetic geodesics are contained in two-dimensional Hopf tori (also called Clifford tori), and we want to describe how these tori relate to Mañé’s critical value and the Hopf–Rinow theorem. We call a pair
w_{0},w_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

admissible
if

(1.9)

\langle w_{0},w_{1}\rangle=0,\qquad|w_{0}|^{2}+|w_{1}|^{2}=1.

The
width
of an admissible pair is the unique number
\tau\in[0,\pi]
satisfying

|w_{0}|=\cos(\tfrac{\tau}{2}),\quad|w_{1}|=\sin(\tfrac{\tau}{2}),

or, equivalently,

(1.10)

|w_{0}|^{2}-|w_{1}|^{2}=\cos\tau.

Notice that if
(w_{0},w_{1})
is admissible with width
\tau
, then
(w_{1},w_{0})
is admissible with width
\pi-\tau
.

The two-dimensional
Clifford torus
associated with the admissible pair
(w_{0},w_{1})
is defined by

\mathbb{T}^{2}_{w_{0},w_{1}}:=\{\lambda_{0}w_{0}+\lambda_{1}w_{1}\mid\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}\}.

We define the width of the torus as the width of the associated pair
(w_{0},w_{1})
. Observe that
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{w_{0},w_{1}}=\mathbb{T}^{2}_{w_{1},w_{0}}
, and therefore, the width of the torus is defined only up to the identification
\tau\sim\pi-\tau
. Moreover, if
|w_{0}|=1
, then
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{w_{0},0}
degenerates to the Hopf circle through
w_{0}
.

To put Clifford tori and magnetic geodesics in relation, we define for

C_{s}([0,\pi])=\Big\{(-\tfrac{s}{2}+\cos\psi,\sin\psi)\mid\psi\in[0,\pi]\Big\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

The set
C_{s}([0,\pi])
is the upper half-circle of radius
1
in the plane with center

, traversed counter-clockwise, see Figure

.

Theorem 1.9

Let

Applications to Hamiltonian PDE’s and Hofer–Zehnder capacities:

To close this introduction let us mention two intriuiging and unexpected applications of the methods developed in this article. First, as already mentioned, in upcoming work

2.
Mañé’s critical value for magnetic Lagrangians

2.1.
Three equivalent definitions

Let
(M,g)
be a closed manifold
M
with Riemannian metric
g
. Let
\alpha
be a one-form on
M
and consider the exact magnetic system
(M,g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
. In this case, the magnetic geodesic flow
\Phi_{g,\sigma}
is the Euler–Lagrange flow
\Phi_{L}
of the magnetic Lagrangian

L\colon TM\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad L(q,v):=\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}-\alpha_{q}(v),

see
[
19
]
. This means that
\gamma\colon[0,T]\to M
is a magnetic geodesic if and only if
\gamma
is a critical point of the action functional
S_{L}
among all curves
\delta\colon[0,T]\to M
such that
\delta(0)=\gamma(0)
and
\delta(T)=\gamma(T)
. Here the action functional is defined as

S_{L}(\gamma):=\int_{0}^{T}L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)){\mathrm{d}}t.

This variational principle prescribes the length
T
of the time interval and leaves free the energy of
\gamma
. On the other hand, given
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic with energy
k
if and only if
\gamma
is a critical point of
S_{L+k}
among all curves
\delta\colon[0,T^{\prime}]\to M
with
\delta(0)=\gamma(0)
and
\delta(T^{\prime})=\gamma(T)
for an arbitrary
T^{\prime}>0
.

The Mañé’s critical value of
L
can be defined in three equivalent ways. First,
c(L)
is the smallest energy value containing the graph of the differential of a function on
M
:

(2.1)

c(L)=\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}(M)}\sup_{q\in M}H(q,{\mathrm{d}}_{q}f),

where the magnetic Hamiltonian

H\colon T^{*}M\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad H(q,p):=\tfrac{1}{2}|p+\alpha_{q}|^{2}_{q}.

is the Legendre dual of
L
. Notice that, when
M
is simply connected, this definition coincide with the definition of the Mañe’s critical value of the universal cover
c(M,g,\sigma)
given in (
1.3
).

Second,
c(L)
is the smallest energy value such that the free-period action functional with energy
k
is bounded from below (by zero) on the space of loops:

(2.2)

c(L)=\inf\{k\in{\mathbb{R}}\mid S_{L+k}(\gamma)\geq 0,\ \forall\,T>0,\ \forall\,\gamma\colon[0,T]\to M,\ \gamma(0)=\gamma(T)\}.

Third,
c(L)
is minus the minimal action of invariant Borel probability measures
\mu
for the flow
\Phi_{L}
on
TM
:

(2.3)

c(L)=-\inf_{\mu}S_{L}(\mu).

Here

S_{L}(\mu):=\int_{TM}L(q,v){\mathrm{d}}\mu

denotes the action of the invariant probability measure
\mu
. For examples, if
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}/T{\mathbb{Z}}\to M
is a periodic magnetic geodesic then the corresponding invariant measure
\mu_{\gamma}
satisfies
S_{L}(\mu_{\gamma})=\frac{S_{L}(\gamma)}{T}
. In general, if
\gamma
is any magnetic geodesic, then by the Krylov–Bogolyubov theorem, there exists an invariant probability measure supported on the closure of the set
\{(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\mid t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
.

The Mather set of the system
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\subset TM
is defined as the union of the supports of all action-minimizing probability measures. Dias Carneiro showed in
[
12
]
that
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\subset\{E=c(L)\}
, where
E
is the kinetic energy of the system. Finally, Mather showed that
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
is a graph over
M

[
25
]
. Thus, we denote by
\mathcal{M}:=\pi_{TM}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}})\subset M
, the projected Mather set.

2.2.
Mañé’s critical value for Killing magnetic Lagrangians

Definition 2.1
.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric and
\alpha
be a one-form on a closed manifold
M
. We denote with
X
the vector field on
M
which is the metric dual of
\alpha
with respect to
g
.
We say that
\alpha
is Killing for
g
if
X
is a Killing vector field for
g
. If
\alpha
is Killing for
g
, we call the associated Lagrangian
L
and magnetic field
(M,g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
Killing as well.

Recall that
X
is a Killing vector field, when it generates a flow of isometries for
g
, or equivalently,
\mathcal{L}_{X}g=0
, where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the Lie derivative. By
[
30
, Proposition 2.2.1]
, this implies that

\tfrac{1}{2}g(\nabla_{u}X,v)={\mathrm{d}}\alpha(u,v),\quad\forall\,u,v\in TM.

By definition of Lorentz force (
1.1
), it follows that

Y=2\nabla X.

Therefore,
Y\cdot 2X=\nabla_{2X}(2X)
, which means that the flow lines
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to M
of
2X
are magnetic geodesics by (
1.2
). As a result, the function
\tfrac{1}{2}|X|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}
is constant along
\gamma
. We can now single out a special class of such flow lines.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha
be Killing for
g
and denote by
X
the corresponding Killing vector field. Let
q
be a point of
M
. The following properties are equivalent:

(1)

q
is a critical point of the function
\tfrac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}
;

(2)

(\nabla_{X}X)_{q}=0
;

(3)

Y_{q}\cdot X_{q}=0
;

(4)

{\mathrm{d}}\alpha_{q}(X_{q},\cdot)=0
;

(5)

the Lorentz force
Y_{q}
takes values in
\ker\alpha_{q}=X_{q}^{\perp}
;

(6)

if
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic with
\gamma(0)=q
, then
\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\big|_{t=0}\alpha(\gamma)=\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\big|_{t=0}g_{\gamma}(X_{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})=0
.

Proof.

Since
X
is the dual of
\alpha
, we have
|\alpha|=|X|
. Moreover,

{\mathrm{d}}(\tfrac{1}{2}|X|^{2})=g(\nabla X,X)=2{\mathrm{d}}\alpha(\cdot,X)=-2{\mathrm{d}}\alpha(X,\cdot)=-g(\nabla_{X}X,\cdot)=-Y\cdot X.

From this formula, we conclude that equivalence of the first five items of the statement. If
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic with
\gamma(0)=q
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)=v
, we have

\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\Big|_{t=0}\alpha(\dot{\gamma})=g(\nabla_{v}X,v)+g(X,\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma})=2{\mathrm{d}}\alpha_{q}(v,v)+g(X,Y_{q}v)=g(X,Y_{q}v),

from which the equivalence between the last two items of the statement follows.
∎

Consider the function
\tfrac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Denote by
\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|_{\infty}^{2}
its maximum and by

M_{\alpha}:=\{q\in M\mid\tfrac{1}{2}|\alpha|_{q}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|^{2}\}\subset M

the subset of its maximizers

Proposition 2.3
.

For all
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
, every flow line of
rX
starting at
q\in M_{\alpha}
is entirely contained in
M_{\alpha}
and it is both a standard and a magnetic geodesic.

Proof.

Let
q\in M_{\alpha}
and let
\gamma
be a flow line of
2X
passing through
q
. Since
\tfrac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}
is constant along
\gamma
, we conclude that
\gamma
is contained in
M_{\alpha}
. Hence, for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\gamma(t)
is a critical point of
\tfrac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}
since
M_{\alpha}
consists of critical points. It follows from the equivalence of the first three items in Lemma
2.2
that
\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=0=Y_{\gamma}\cdot\dot{\gamma}
, and we conclude that
\gamma
and all its constant speed reparametrizations are standard geodesics and magnetic geodesics at the same time.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
L
be a Killing Lagrangian, then its Mañé critical value is

c(L)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{\infty}.

If
X\colon M\to TM
denotes the Killing vector field dual to
\alpha
, then

•

the projected Mather set
\mathcal{M}
is the union of the supports of all the invariant measures of the flow of
X|_{M_{\alpha}}
(in particular, it contains all the periodic orbits of
X|_{M_{\alpha}}
);

•

the Mather set is

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=X(\mathcal{M}).

Proof.

For all
(q,v)\in TM
, we compute

(2.4)

L(q,v)=\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}-\alpha_{q}(v)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}-|\alpha_{q}||v|=\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(|v|-|\alpha_{q}|\Big)^{2}+(\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|_{\infty}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}|\alpha_{q}|^{2})-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Therefore, if
\mu
is any Borel probability in
TM
, then

(2.5)

\int_{TM}L{\mathrm{d}}\mu\geq\int_{TM}\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|^{2}\Big){\mathrm{d}}\mu=-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{\infty}.

From (
2.3
), we deduce that
-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{\infty}\leq-c(L)
, that is,
c(L)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\alpha\|^{2}_{\infty}
.
Moreover, a Borel probability measure
\mu
achieves equality in (
2.5
) if and only if
\mu
is supported in the invariant set

\{(q,v)\in TM\mid v=X_{q},\ |\alpha_{q}|=\|\alpha\|_{\infty}\}=X(M_{\alpha}).

Finally, a Borel probability measure
\mu
is supported in
X(M|_{\alpha})
and it is invariant under
\Phi_{L}
if and only if its projection
\pi_{TM}(\mu)
is supported in
M_{\alpha}
and it is invariant under the flow of
X
. The statement follows.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is also possible to prove the theorem using the definition (
2.2
) of the critical value. The bound from above follows essentially from (
2.4
). The bound from below follows by computing the action of a loop which is the concatenation of

•

a flow line
t\mapsto\Phi_{X}^{t}(q)
with
q\in M_{\alpha}
for
t\in[0,T]
with
T
arbitrarily large;

•

a geodesics from
\Phi_{X}^{T}(q)
to
q
with uniformly bounded length.

In particular, the theorem holds also on certain non-compact manifolds. For instance, if
X|_{M_{\alpha}}
has a periodic orbit.

2.3.
Mañé’s critical value for
K
-contact Lagrangians

We can apply Theorem
2.4
to the case of
K
-contact structures
(g,\alpha)
on simply connected manifolds
M

[
9
]
. In this case,
\alpha
is a contact form and
g
is a Riemannian metric with the following properties:

(1)

the contact distribution
\ker\alpha
and the Reeb vector field
R
are orthogonal with respect to
g
;

(2)

the Reeb vector field
R
is a Killing vector field for
g
with constant norm
r>0
.

Recall that
\alpha
is contact if
\alpha
is nowhere zero and
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha
is non-degenerate on the contact hyperplane distribution
\ker\alpha
. In this case, we can define the Reeb vector field
R
by the equations
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha(R,\cdot)=0
and
\alpha(R)=1
. Notice that if
(M,g,\alpha)
is
K
-contact, then
\alpha
is Killing for
g
according to Definition
2.1
. Indeed, conditions (1) and (2) imply that the metric dual vector field of
\alpha
is
X=\tfrac{1}{r^{2}}R
, which is Killing since
R
is. We also observe that the function
\tfrac{1}{2}|\alpha|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}|X|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2r^{2}}
is constant and therefore,
M_{\alpha}=M
and
c(L)=\tfrac{1}{2r^{2}}
. Finally, since
M_{\alpha}=M
, the equivalence between the first and the last item in Lemma
2.2
implies that
g_{\gamma}(X_{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})
is constant along geodesics constant of motion. Since the speed of magnetic geodesics is constant as well, this is the same as saying that the angle
\psi\in[0,\pi]
between a magnetic geodesic and the Reeb vector field is constant, where, compatibly with (
1.8
),

\cos\psi=\tfrac{1}{r|\dot{\gamma}|}g_{\gamma}(R_{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}).

We finish this section by observing that the magnetic system on the odd-dimensional sphere that we want to study is indeed
K
-contact.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
g
be the standard Riemannian metric on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and let
\alpha
be the standard contact form on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, as described after (
1.5
). Then
(g,\alpha)
is
K
-contact on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
. The Mañé’s critical value of the associated magnetic Lagrangian is
c(L)=\tfrac{1}{8}
and the Mather set and projected Mather sets are

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=\tfrac{1}{4}R({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}})\subset T{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},\quad\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}.

Proof.

For every
z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, we recall that
g_{z}=\mathrm{Re}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
,
R_{z}=2\mathrm{i}z
,
\alpha_{z}=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}z,\cdot\rangle
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard Hermitian form on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. In particular,
|R_{z}|=2
and
\alpha_{z}=\tfrac{1}{4}g(R_{z},\cdot)
for all
z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, which shows property (1). Moreover, the Reeb flow is the Hopf flow (
1.6
) which takes values in
U(n+1)
. Therefore, the Hermitian product and hence
g
are preserved under this flow, which shows that
R
is Killing. This shows that the metric dual of
\alpha
is
X=\tfrac{1}{4}R
and
c(L)=\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{1}{2^{2}}=\tfrac{1}{8}
. Finally, since all orbits of
R
are periodic, we deduce that
\mathcal{M}={\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and hence
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=X({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}})=\tfrac{1}{4}R({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}})
.
∎

With Theorem
2.6
, we have established the first part of Theorem
1.1
. In the next section, we are going to further specialize our discussion of the magnetic system
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
and finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
as well as give the proof of Theorem
1.9
.

3.
Clifford tori and magnetic geodesics on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}

This section will be entirely dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.9
.

From now on, we use the convention of considering magnetic geodesics
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
with unit speed for the family
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,s{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
, for
s\geq 0
. Following
[
27
]
, we rewrite the equation of magnetic geodesics (
1.2
) in the ambient space
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.

For all
z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, we have the orthogonal projection

P_{z}\colon T_{z}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to T_{z}{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},\qquad P_{z}v=v-\mathrm{Re}\langle z,v\rangle z,\quad\forall\,v\in T_{z}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}.

The Levi-Civita connection on
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
is obtained projecting the standard Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
:

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=P_{\gamma}\ddot{\gamma}=\ddot{\gamma}-\mathrm{Re}\langle\gamma,\ddot{\gamma}\rangle\gamma.

Since
\gamma
is contained in
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, we have that
\dot{\gamma}\in T_{\gamma}{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and therefore
0\equiv\mathrm{Re}\langle\gamma,\dot{\gamma}\rangle
. Differentiating this equation, we obtain

0=\mathrm{Re}\langle\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}\rangle+\mathrm{Re}\langle\gamma,\ddot{\gamma}\rangle=1+\mathrm{Re}\langle\gamma,\ddot{\gamma}\rangle,

where we used that
\gamma
has unit speed. Hence,

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=\ddot{\gamma}+\gamma.

Similarly, for the Lorentz force, we have for all
z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{z}{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
:

{\mathrm{d}}_{z}\alpha(v_{1},v_{2})=\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}v_{1},v_{2}\rangle=g_{z}(P_{z}\mathrm{i}v_{1},v_{2}).

This means that

(3.1)

Y_{z}=P_{z}\mathrm{i}=\mathrm{i}P_{(\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp}}=\mathrm{i}P_{\ker\alpha_{z}},

where
P_{(\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the orthogonal of
\mathbb{C}z
, and we observe that this orthogonal coincides with the contact distribution at
z
.
Therefore,

Y_{z}v_{1}=\mathrm{i}v_{1}+g_{z}(\mathrm{i}z,v_{1})z,\quad\forall\,v_{1}\in T_{z}{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}.

If
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic with strength
s
, then by Lemma
2.2
the angle between
\dot{\gamma}
and
R_{z}=2\mathrm{i}z
is constant. Therefore, there exists
\psi\in[0,\pi]
such that

Y_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}=\mathrm{i}\dot{\gamma}+(\cos\psi)\gamma.

Putting these pieces together, we rewrite (
1.2
) with strength
s
as

(3.2)

\ddot{\gamma}-s\mathrm{i}\dot{\gamma}+(1-s\cos\psi)\gamma=0.

Equation (
3.2
) is linear and therefore its solutions can be determined solving the associated equation for the frequencies
\lambda=\mathrm{i}\theta\in\mathbb{C}

\theta^{2}-s\mathrm{\lambda}-(1-s\cos\psi)=0.

The discriminant is

\big(\tfrac{s}{2}\big)^{2}+(1-s\cos\psi)=(-\tfrac{s}{2}+\cos\psi)^{2}+\sin^{2}\psi,

which is always non-negative and vanishes if and only if
s=2
and
\psi=0
.

Therefore, we have two real solutions
\theta_{0}(s,\psi)\leq\theta_{1}(s,\psi)
which have the formula

(3.3)

\theta_{0}(s,\psi)=\tfrac{1}{2}s-|C_{s}(\psi)|,\quad\theta_{1}(s,\psi)=\tfrac{1}{2}s+|C_{s}(\psi)|,

where we have defined the point

C_{s}(\psi):=(-\tfrac{s}{2}+\cos\psi,\sin\psi),

which lies in the upper half-circle with center
(-\tfrac{s}{2},0)
and radius
1
, see (
1.12
). The parameter
\psi
represents the angle between the ray emanating from the center of the circle in direction of the positive
x
-axis and the ray passing through
C_{s}(\psi)
.

Hence the solutions of (
3.2
) have the form claimed in (
1.14
):

\gamma(t)=e^{\mathrm{i}\tfrac{s}{2}t}\Big(e^{-\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|t}w_{0}+e^{\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|t}w_{1}\Big),\quad\forall\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

for some
w_{0},w_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. Let us suppose that
\gamma(0)=z
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)=v
and let us look for a formula for
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
. We compute

\dot{\gamma}(t)=\mathrm{i}\tfrac{s}{2}\gamma(t)+\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|e^{\mathrm{i}\tfrac{s}{2}t}\Big(-e^{-\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|t}w_{0}+e^{\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|t}w_{1}\Big),\quad\forall\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Setting
t=0
, we get,

(3.4)

z=w_{0}+w_{1},\quad v=\mathrm{i}\tfrac{s}{2}(w_{0}+w_{1})+\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|(w_{1}-w_{0}).

Solving this system for
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
, we get

(3.5)

w_{0}=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|}\Big(\mathrm{i}(|C_{s}(\psi)|+\tfrac{s}{2})z-v\Big),\qquad w_{1}=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|}\Big(\mathrm{i}(|C_{s}(\psi)|-\tfrac{s}{2})z+v\Big).

Therefore, we see that
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
depend smoothly from
z
and
v
as soon as
|C_{s}(\psi)|\neq 0
. This last condition is equivalent to
s=2
and
\psi=0
. as mentioned above.
We compute

\langle w_{0},w_{1}\rangle=\frac{1}{4|C_{s}(\psi)|^{2}}\Big(|C_{s}(\psi)|^{2}-\tfrac{s^{2}}{4}-1+(|C_{s}(\psi)|+\tfrac{s}{2})\langle\mathrm{i}z,v\rangle-(|C_{s}(\psi)|-\tfrac{s}{2})\overline{\langle\mathrm{i}z,v\rangle}\Big)

We have
\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}z,v\rangle=\cos\psi
and
\mathrm{Im}\langle\mathrm{i}z,v\rangle=\mathrm{Re}\langle z,v\rangle=0
. The expression within parentheses becomes

|C_{s}(\psi)|^{2}-\tfrac{s^{2}}{4}-1+s\cos\psi

which is equal to zero by definition of
C_{s}(\psi)
. Therefore, we find that

\langle w_{0},w_{1}\rangle=0

and taking the norm of the first equation in (
3.4
), we also get

|w_{0}|^{2}+|w_{1}|^{2}=1.

Recall the definition of the width
\tau\in[0,\pi]
as
|w_{0}|=\cos(\tfrac{\tau}{2})
. We obtain from (
3.4
),

\cos\psi=\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}z,v\rangle=\tfrac{s}{2}+|C_{s}(\psi)|(|w_{1}|^{2}-|w_{0}|^{2})=\tfrac{s}{2}+|C_{s}(\psi)|\cos\tau.

Hence,
\cos\tau=\frac{-\tfrac{s}{2}+\cos\psi}{|C_{s}(\psi)|}
. Since the cosine function is bijective on
[0,\pi]
, we deduce that

\tau=\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi))

is the angle made by the vector
C_{s}(\psi)
with the positive
x
-axis. From the expression of
\gamma
, see (
1.14
), we also get that the frequency of rotation in the
w_{0}
-factor is
\tfrac{s}{2}-|C_{s}(\psi)|
, while the frequency of rotation in the
w_{1}
-factor is
\tfrac{s}{2}+|C_{s}(\psi)|
. This shows that the rotation number of the orbit is

\rho_{s}(\psi)=\frac{\tfrac{s}{2}-|C_{s}(\psi)|}{\tfrac{s}{2}+|C_{s}(\psi)|}.

It is immediate to see that
\rho_{s}\colon(0,\pi)\to(\rho_{s}^{-},\rho_{s}^{+})
is bijective and motonically decreasing, where
\rho_{s}^{-}
and
\rho_{s}^{+}
are defined in (
1.15
).

To finish the proof of Theorem
1.9
, we need to prove that if
\gamma
is given by (
1.14
) with
w_{0},w_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
satisfying (
1.9
) and with the corresponding Clifford torus having width satisfying (
1.13
). There are two possible approaches. The first, more elementary, approach consists in checking that
\gamma
given by (
1.14
) is a unit speed curve contained in
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
satisfying (
3.2
). We leave this computation to the reader. In the second approach, we use the fact that the group
U(n+1)
sends magnetic geodesics with strength
s
and contact angle
\psi
to magnetic geodesics with strength
s
and contact angle
\psi
by Corollary
1.5
and Proposition
6.4
. The result follows by observing that there exists at least one magnetic geodesic with strength
s
and contact angle
\psi
and the fact that
U(n+1)
acts transitively on the set of pairs
(w_{0},w_{1})
satisfying (
1.9
) and having prescribed width.

Remark 3.1
.

In the argument above, we used that the speed
|v|
(in this case normalized to
1
) and the angle
\psi
given by
\cos\psi=\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}z,v\rangle

are preserved along magnetic geodesics. As a sanity check, the reader can prove that if
\gamma
is a solution to the equation (
3.2
) such that
\gamma(0)=z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)=v\in T_{z}{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
, then
|\gamma|=|\dot{\gamma}|\equiv 1
and
\mathrm{Re}\langle\mathrm{i}\gamma,\dot{\gamma}\rangle\equiv\cos\psi
. In particular,
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic with strength
s
and contact angle
\psi
.

4.
The Hopf–Rinow Theorem for
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)

In this section, we prove Corollary
1.10
and use it to establish the second part of Theorem
1.1
.

4.1.
Proof of Corollary
1.10

Let
q_{0},q_{1}
be two points in
{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
. From Theorem
1.9
, we know that there exists a magnetic geodesic
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
with strength
s\geq 0
such that
\gamma(-\tfrac{T}{2})=q_{0}
and
\gamma(\tfrac{T}{2})=q_{1}
for some
T\geq 0
if and only if there exists
\psi\in[0,\pi]
and an admissible pair
w_{0},w_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with width
\tau=\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi))
satisfying

(4.1)

\displaystyle q_{0}

\displaystyle=e^{-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{sT}{4}}\Big(e^{\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|\tfrac{T}{2}}w_{0}+e^{-\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|\tfrac{T}{2}}w_{1}\Big),

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle=e^{\mathrm{i}\tfrac{sT}{4}}\Big(e^{-\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|\tfrac{T}{2}}w_{0}+e^{\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|\tfrac{T}{2}}w_{1}\Big).

Assume now that (
4.1
) holds. Taking the Hermitian product of the two right-hand sides, we get

\displaystyle\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{sT}{2}}\Big(e^{\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|T}|w_{0}|^{2}+e^{-\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|T}|w_{1}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=e^{-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{sT}{2}}\Big(\cos(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)(|w_{0}|^{2}+|w_{1}|^{2})+\mathrm{i}\sin(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)(|w_{0}|^{2}-|w_{1}|^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle=e^{-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{sT}{2}}\Big(\cos(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)+\mathrm{i}\cos(\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi)))\sin(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)\Big),

where in the last step, we used the definition (
1.10
) of the width
\tau
and equation (
1.13
). Thus, we have established equation (
1.16
) in Corollary
1.10
.

Conversely, assume that equation (
1.16
) holds and let us show that we can find an admissible pair
w_{0},w_{1}
with width
\tau=\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi))
satisfying (
4.1
). We distinguish two cases. If
\sin(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)\neq 0
, then we can solve the linear system (
4.1
) for
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
and define

(4.2)

\displaystyle w_{0}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\sin(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)}\Big(e^{\mathrm{i}(\tfrac{s}{2}+|C_{s}(\psi)|)\tfrac{T}{2}}q_{0}-e^{-\mathrm{i}(\tfrac{s}{2}+|C_{s}(\psi)|)\tfrac{T}{2}}q_{1}\Big),

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}\sin(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)}\Big(-e^{\mathrm{i}(\tfrac{s}{2}-|C_{s}(\psi)|)\tfrac{T}{2}}q_{0}+e^{-\mathrm{i}(\tfrac{s}{2}-|C_{s}(\psi)|)\tfrac{T}{2}}q_{1}\Big).

It is a simple computation using the fact that
q_{0},q_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and that (
1.16
) holds to show that the pair
w_{0},w_{1}
is admissible and has width
\tau=\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi))
.

We consider the case
\sin(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)=0
. Hence, either
\cos(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)=1
or
\cos(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)=-1
. We treat the first subcase only since the second is analogous. Equation (
1.16
) becomes

\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle=e^{-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{sT}{2}}.

By Cauchy–Schwarz, this implies that

(4.3)

q_{1}=e^{\mathrm{i}\tfrac{sT}{2}}q_{0}.

The system (
4.1
) becomes

u_{0}=w_{0}+w_{1},\qquad u_{0}:=\lambda q_{0}=\overline{\lambda}q_{1},\qquad\lambda:=e^{\mathrm{i}(\tfrac{s}{2}-|C_{s}(\psi)|)\tfrac{T}{2}}.

The equation
\lambda q_{0}=\overline{\lambda}q_{1}
is satisfied because of (
4.3
) and the assumption
e^{\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|T}=1
. All admissible pairs
w_{0},w_{1}
with width
\tau=\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi))
solving the equation
u_{0}=w_{0}+w_{1}
with
u_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
can be constructed as follows. Let
u_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
be any vector such that
\langle u_{0},u_{1}\rangle=\cos\tau
. Consider the solutions
w_{0},w_{1}
to the system

u_{0}=w_{0}+w_{1},\quad u_{1}=w_{0}-w_{1},

that is,
w_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}(u_{0}+u_{1})
and
w_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}(u_{0}-u_{1})
. It is a simple computation using that
u_{0},u_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
and
\langle u_{0},u_{1}\rangle=\cos\tau
to show that the pair
w_{0},w_{1}
is admissible and has width
\tau
. This finishes the proof of Corollary
1.10

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
(Second part)

Let
q_{0},q_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
. Using (
1.16
), we want to determine for which energies
k
, there is a magnetic geodesic with unit speed and strength
s
connecting
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
. This yields a corresponding result for magnetic geodesics with kinetic energy
k=\tfrac{1}{2s^{2}}
. We let
\lambda:=\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle
. We distinguish three cases for
\lambda
. First, if
|\lambda|=1
, then
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
are on the same Hopf trajectory. Since every Hopf trajectory is a standard and a magnetic geodesic with any strength by Proposition
2.3
, we conclude that
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
are connected by a magnetic geodesic for every strength
s
. Second, if
\lambda=0
, then, by (
1.16
), there is a magnetic geodesic with strength
s
and contact angle
\psi\in(0,\pi)
connecting
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
in time
T
if and only if

\cos(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)=0,\quad\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi))=\tfrac{\pi}{2}.

The second equation has a solution
\psi
if and only if
s<2
. If
s<2
, then
T=\tfrac{1}{|C_{s}(\psi)|}(\tfrac{\pi}{2}+h\pi)
for
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are solutions to the first equation.

Third, we assume that
0<|\lambda|<1
. In this case, we are going to use that, by (
1.16
), we have

(4.4)

|\lambda|^{2}=\cos^{2}(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)+\cos^{2}(\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi)))\sin^{2}(|C_{s}(\psi)|T),

In particular,

(4.5)

|\lambda|\geq\big|\cos(\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi))\big|.

Recall that
\psi\mapsto\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s})\colon[0,\pi]\to[0,\pi]
is

(1)

bijective, monotonically increasing and continuous, for
0<s<2
;

(2)

continuous, strictly monotonically increasing on
[0,\psi^{\max}_{s}]
, strictly monotonically decreasing on
[\psi^{\max}_{s},\pi]
for
s\geq 2
, where
\psi^{\max}_{s}=\arccos(\tfrac{2}{s})
.

By (
1.12
), we have

\cos(\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi^{\max}_{s})))=\frac{-\tfrac{s}{2}+\tfrac{2}{s}}{\sqrt{\tfrac{s^{2}}{4}-1}}=-\sqrt{1-\tfrac{4}{s^{2}}}.

Combining this formula with (
4.5
), we get

(4.6)

|\lambda|\geq\sqrt{1-\tfrac{4}{s^{2}}}.

We distinguish two cases.

(1)

If
|\lambda|=\sqrt{1-\tfrac{4}{s^{2}}}
, then (
4.4
) is satisfied if and only if
\psi=\psi^{\max}_{s}
and
|C_{s}(\psi^{\max}_{s})|T=\tfrac{\pi}{2}+m\pi
for some
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Plugging in these values in (
1.16
), we get

\lambda=\sqrt{1-\tfrac{4}{s^{2}}}e^{\mathrm{i}(a_{s}+mb_{s})},\ \ m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad a_{s}:=-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\big(1+\tfrac{s}{2|C_{s}(\psi^{\max}_{s})|}\big),\qquad b_{s}:=\pi\big(1-\tfrac{s}{2|C_{s}(\psi^{\max}_{s})|}\big).

(2)

If
|\lambda|>\sqrt{1-\tfrac{4}{s^{2}}}
, then there exists a closed interval
I_{s}\subset[0,\pi]
of positive length such that (
4.6
) holds if and only if
\psi\in I_{s}
. For
\psi\in I_{s}
, we rewrite (
4.4
) as

\cos^{2}(|C_{s}(\psi)|T)=\frac{|\lambda|^{2}-\cos^{2}(\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi)))}{1-\cos^{2}(\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi)))}.

and define

T(\psi):=\frac{1}{|C_{s}(\psi)|}\arccos\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\lambda|^{2}-\cos^{2}(\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi)))}{1-\cos^{2}(\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi)))}}\right).

Denoting with
f(T)
the right-hand side of (
1.16
), there is a continuous function
\eta_{s}\colon I_{s}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

f(T(\psi))=|\lambda|e^{-\mathrm{i}\eta_{s}(\psi)}.

Hence, for all
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we get from (
1.16
) that

f\Big(T(\psi)+\tfrac{2\pi}{|C_{s}(\psi)|}m\Big)=e^{-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{2\pi s}{|C_{s}(\psi)|}m}f(T(\psi))=e^{-\mathrm{i}\eta_{s,m}(\psi)}|\lambda|,\quad\eta_{s,m}(\psi):=\tfrac{2\pi s}{|C_{s}(\psi)|}m+\eta_{s}(\psi).

Our aim is to show that there exists
\psi\in I_{s}
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
e^{-\mathrm{i}\eta_{s,m}(\psi)}|\lambda|=\lambda
. To this purpose, we show that for
|m|
large enough the target of the function
\eta_{s,m}\colon I_{s}\to{\mathbb{R}}
contains an interval of length at least
2\pi
. This is a consequence of the fact that the function
|C_{s}|
is strictly increasing for
s>0
, which we observed in the introduction. Take
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in I_{s}
such that
|C_{s}(\psi_{0})|\neq|C_{s}(\psi_{1})|
. We have

|\eta_{s,m}(\psi_{1})-\eta_{s,m}(\psi_{0})|\geq 2\pi s\left|\tfrac{1}{|C_{s}(\psi_{1})|}-\tfrac{1}{|C_{s}(\psi_{0})|}\right||m|-\Big(\max_{\psi\in I_{s}}\eta_{s}(\psi)-\min_{\psi\in I_{s}}\eta_{s}(\psi)\Big)

and, for every
s>0
, the right-hand side is at least
2\pi
if
|m|
is large enough. By the Intermediate Value Theorem, the target of
\eta_{s,m}
contains an interval of length at least
2\pi
. This finishes the proof of the second part of Theorem
1.1
.

5.
Magnetic geodesics and the Hopf fibration

In this section we consider the magnetic system
({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,s{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
. By Corollary
1.5
, we know that every magnetic geodesic of
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,s{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
is contained in a totally magnetic three-sphere whose associated magnetic system is magnetomorphic to
({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,s{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
. Therefore, Theorem
1.12
also sheds light to the standard magnetic system on spheres of every odd dimension.

For
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the standard magnetic system
({\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}),g,a\sigma)
, where
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
is the Euclidean two-sphere of radius
\tfrac{1}{2}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,
g
is the associated Euclidean metric, and
\sigma
is the area form of
g
. By
[
6
]
, unit magnetic geodesics of this system are geodesic circles on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
of radius
r\in[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]
such that

(5.1)

\tan(2r)=\frac{2}{a}.

The group of magnetomorphisms of
({\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}),g,a\sigma)
is
SO(3)
, the group of orientation preserving isometries of
g
.

The Hopf map is given by

(5.2)

\pi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})\subset\mathbb{C}\times{\mathbb{R}}\equiv{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\qquad\pi(z):=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(2\overline{z}_{1}z_{2},|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2}\right).

The Hopf map is a Riemannian submersion between the standard metrics on
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
and
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
. Moreover,
\pi^{*}\sigma={\mathrm{d}}\alpha
. Every element of
U(2)
yields a magnetomorphism
A\colon{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
which descends via
\pi
to a magnetomorphism
\overline{A}\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
. The map

U(2)\to SO(3),\quad A\mapsto\overline{A}

is surjective and its kernel is given by the multiples of the identity, that is, by the Hopf flow.

5.1.
The proof of Theorem
1.12

Let
q_{0},q_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
. We want to show that

(5.3)

\mathrm{dist}(\pi(q_{0}),\pi(q_{1}))=\arccos(|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|),

where
\mathrm{dist}(\pi(q_{0}),\pi(q_{1}))
denotes the distance between
\pi(q_{0})
and
\pi(q_{1})
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
. Upon acting with a magnetomorphism of
{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
, we assume that
q_{0}=(1,0)\in{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
while
q_{1}=(z_{1},z_{2})
is arbitrary. We have

|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|=|z_{1}|.

On the other hand, using (
5.2
), we see that

\pi(q_{0})=(0,0,1)
is the north pole and, therefore,

\mathrm{dist}(\pi(q_{0}),\pi(q_{1}))=\tfrac{1}{2}\arccos(|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}2\arccos(|z_{1}|)=\arccos(|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|),

where we used the identity
|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}=1
and the duplication formula for the cosine. This establishes (
5.3
).

We move on to investigate the projection
\pi(\gamma)\colon\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
of magnetic geodesics of strength
s
and contact angle
\psi
. For this purpose, we are going to use formula (
1.14
):

\gamma(t)=e^{\mathrm{i}\tfrac{s}{2}t}\Big(e^{-\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|t}w_{0}+e^{\mathrm{i}|C_{s}(\psi)|t}w_{1}\Big),\quad\forall\,t\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
w_{0},w_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is an admissible pair with width
\tau=\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi))
. Upon acting with an element of
U(2)
, we can assume that
w_{0}=\cos(\tfrac{\tau}{2})(1,0)
and
w_{1}=\sin(\tfrac{\tau}{2})(0,1)
. In this case,

\pi(\gamma)=\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(2\cos(\tfrac{\tau}{2})\sin(\tfrac{\tau}{2})e^{\mathrm{i}2|C_{s}|t},\cos^{2}(\tfrac{\tau}{2})-\sin^{2}(\tfrac{\tau}{2})\Big)=\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\sin\tau e^{\mathrm{i}2|C_{s}|t},\cos\tau\Big),

which is a constant speed parametrization with positive orientation of a geodesic circle on
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
of radius

r=\tfrac{1}{2}\tau=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Arg}(C_{s}(\psi)).

Using (
5.1
), we see that
\pi(\gamma)
is a magnetic geodesic for
({\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}),g,a_{s}(\psi)\sigma)
with

5.2.
Restriction of
({\mathbb{S}^{3}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
around a Hopf fibre

We can use the Hopf map
\pi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
also to find suitable primitives of
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha
centered around the Hopf fibre of a point
q_{0}\in{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
that give better bounds for the Mañé critical value of the magnetic system restricted to the sets

B_{q_{0},k}:=\{q_{1}\in{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\mid|\langle q_{0},q_{1}\rangle|>\sqrt{1-8k}\},\quad k\in(0,\tfrac{1}{8}).

Up to magnetomorphisms, we can take
q_{0}=(1,0)
. On
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})
, we consider polar coordinates
(r,\phi)\in[0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
around the north pole
\nu_{+}:=\pi(q_{0})
. In these coordinates, we have

g={\mathrm{d}}r^{2}+\tfrac{\sin^{2}(2r)}{4}{\mathrm{d}}\phi^{2},\qquad\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2r){\mathrm{d}}r\wedge{\mathrm{d}}\phi.

We consider the primitive

\beta:=\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}r{\mathrm{d}}\phi

of
\sigma
in
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})\setminus\{\nu_{-}\}
, the complement of the south pole
\nu_{-}
. For every
(r,\phi)
, we have

(5.4)

|\beta_{(r,\phi)}|=\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}r\frac{2}{\sin(2r)}=\tfrac{1}{2}\tan r.

For every
\lambda\in[0,1]
, the one-form

\alpha_{\lambda}:=\lambda\alpha+(1-\lambda)\pi^{*}\beta

is a primitive of
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha
on
B_{q_{0},0}=\pi^{-1}({\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2})\setminus\{\nu_{-}\})
since

{\mathrm{d}}\pi^{*}\beta=\pi^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\beta=\pi^{*}\sigma={\mathrm{d}}\alpha.

Let
q\in B_{q_{0},0}
be arbitrary and let
(r,\phi)
be the polar coordinates of
\pi(q)
. We compute

\displaystyle|(\alpha_{\lambda})_{q}|^{2}=|\lambda\alpha_{q}+(1-\lambda)(\pi^{*}\beta)_{q}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}|\alpha_{q}|^{2}+2\lambda(1-\lambda)g_{q}(\alpha_{q},(\pi^{*}\beta)_{q})+(1-\lambda)^{2}|(\pi^{*}\beta)_{q}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\tfrac{1}{4}+2\lambda(1-\lambda)(\pi^{*}\beta)_{q}(\tfrac{1}{4}R_{q})+(1-\lambda)^{2}|\beta_{\pi(q)}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda^{2}+(1-\lambda)^{2}|\beta_{\pi(q)}|^{2},

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}\Big(\lambda^{2}+(1-\lambda)^{2}\tan^{2}r\Big)

where in the last three steps we respectively used that
\tfrac{1}{4}R
is the dual of
\alpha
, that

\pi^{*}\beta(R)=\beta({\mathrm{d}}\pi R)=0

since the Reeb flow
R
is tangent to the Hopf fibres, and that (
5.4
) holds.

For every
k\in(0,\tfrac{1}{8})
, let
r_{k}:=\arccos(\sqrt{1-8k})\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2})
and compute

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{q\in B_{q_{0},k}}\tfrac{1}{2}|(\alpha_{\lambda})_{q}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{r\in[0,r_{k})}\tfrac{1}{8}\Big(\lambda^{2}+(1-\lambda)^{2}\tan^{2}r\Big)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{8}\Big(\lambda^{2}+(1-\lambda)^{2}\tan^{2}r_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{8}\frac{1}{\cos^{2}r_{k}}\Big(\lambda^{2}-2\lambda\sin^{2}r_{k}+\sin^{2}r_{k}\Big).

To minimize the last expression in (
5.5
), we choose
\lambda:=\sin^{2}r_{k}\in(0,1)
and get

\tfrac{1}{8}\frac{1}{\cos^{2}r_{k}}\Big(\lambda^{2}-2\lambda\sin^{2}r_{k}+\sin^{2}r_{k}\Big)=\tfrac{1}{8}\frac{1}{\cos^{2}r_{k}}\Big(-\sin^{4}r_{k}+\sin^{2}r_{k}\Big)=\tfrac{1}{8}\sin^{2}r_{k}=k,

where the last equality stems from the definition of
r_{k}
.

This shows that the Mañé critical value of the system restricted to
B_{q_{0},k}
is less than or equal to
k
. More precisely, the function

F(q,v):=\sqrt{2k}|v|_{q}-(\alpha_{\lambda})_{q}(v)

yields a Finsler metric on
B_{q_{0},k}
. The critical points of the length functional of
F
are magnetic geodesics with energy
k
. It seems a non-trivial to find critical points of the length functional by variational methods since
B_{q_{0},k}
is not closed and
F
degenerates on its boundary. In particular, one could ask about the existence of length minimizers connecting
q_{0}
and an arbitrary point of
B_{q_{0},k}
.

6.
Magnetomorphisms and totally magnetic submanifolds

A classical topic in Riemannian geometry going back to the work of E. Cartan and M. Berger is the classification of totally geodesic submanifolds
(N,g_{N})
of a given Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})
, up to isometry. So the aim of this section is to study the analog problem in the setting of magnetic systems.

Remark 6.1
.

The arguments in this section also holds in the category of Riemannian Hilbert manifolds.

6.1.
The group of magnetomorphisms

First, let us fix the notion of isomorphism between magnetic systems, as sketched in the introduction.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
(M_{1},g_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2},\sigma_{2})
be magnetic systems. A
magnetomorphism

\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
is a diffeomorphim such that

\Phi^{*}g_{2}=g_{1},\quad\Phi^{*}\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{1}.

The
group of magnetomorphisms
of a magnetic system
(M,g,\sigma)
is defined as

\mathrm{Mag}(M,g,\sigma):=\left\{\Phi:M\to M\mid\Phi^{*}g=g,\ \Phi^{*}\sigma=\sigma\right\}.

Remark 6.3
.

If
\Phi\colon(M_{1},g_{1},\sigma_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2},\sigma_{2})
is a magnetomorphism and
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
represent the Lorentz forces of the two magnetic systems, then

(6.1)

{\mathrm{d}}\Phi\cdot Y^{1}=Y^{2}_{\Phi}\cdot{\mathrm{d}}\Phi.

Proposition 6.4
.

If
\Phi\colon(M_{1},g_{1},\sigma_{1})\to(M_{2},g_{2},\sigma_{2})
is a magnetomorphism, then
\Phi
sends magnetic geodesics of
(M_{1},g_{1},\sigma_{1})
to magnetic geodesics of
(M_{2},g_{2},\sigma_{2})
with the same kinetic energy.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be a magnetic geodesic for
(M_{1},g_{1},\sigma_{1})
, that is,

(6.2)

\nabla^{1}_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}=Y^{1}_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}.

The curve
\delta:=\Phi(\gamma)
has the same kinetic energy as
\gamma
since
\Phi
is an isometry. Moreover,

\nabla^{2}_{\dot{\delta}}\dot{\delta}=\nabla^{2}_{{\mathrm{d}}\Phi\dot{\gamma}}{\mathrm{d}}\Phi\dot{\gamma}={\mathrm{d}}\Phi\cdot\nabla^{1}_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}={\mathrm{d}}\Phi\cdot Y^{1}_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}=Y^{2}_{\Phi(\gamma)}{\mathrm{d}}\Phi\dot{\gamma}=Y^{2}_{\delta}\dot{\delta},

where in the second step we used that
\Phi
is an isometry, in the third step we used (
6.2
), and in the fourth step we used (
6.1
). Thus
\delta
is a magnetic geodesic for
(M_{2},g_{2},\sigma_{2})
.
∎

We can prove the first part of Corollary
1.5
characterizing the magnetomorphisms of
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
.

6.2.
Proof of Corollary
1.5
(First part)

Let
\Phi
be a magnetomorphism of
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)
. By definition,
\Phi
is an isometry of
g
. By a classical result
[
30
]
, we have

\Phi\in O(2(n+1)).

On the other hand, since
\Phi
preserves
{\mathrm{d}}\alpha
, it preserves the Reeb direction, which means that

\Phi\mathrm{i}z=\pm\mathrm{i}\Phi z,\qquad\forall\,z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}.

Since
\Phi
is an isometry, we deduce that

(6.3)

\Phi((\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp})\subset(\mathbb{C}\Phi z)^{\perp}.

Moreover, by (
6.1
) and (
3.1
), we get

\Phi\mathrm{i}P_{(\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp}}=\mathrm{i}P_{(\mathbb{C}\Phi z)^{\perp}}\Phi.

Restricting this equation to
(\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp}
and using (
6.3
), we get
\Phi\mathrm{i}=\mathrm{i}\Phi
on the complex hyperplane
(\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp}
. Since
z\in{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\Phi\mathrm{i}=\mathrm{i}\Phi
on the whole
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, which shows that
\Phi\in U(n+1)
. From this we conclude that

U(n+1)=\mathrm{Mag}({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha).

We now show that the tangent lift
(\Phi,{\mathrm{d}}\Phi)\colon T{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}\to T{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
of the
U(n+1)
action is Hamiltonian with respect to
\omega_{{\mathrm{d}}\alpha}={\mathrm{d}}\lambda-\pi_{TM}^{*}{\mathrm{d}}\alpha
and we compute its momentum map. First, since
\Phi
is an isometry, the tangent lift is conjugated by the metric isomorphism to the cotangent lift
(\Phi,({\mathrm{d}}\Phi^{-1})^{*})\colon T^{*}{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}\to T^{*}{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
. Since we know that that the cotangent lift preserves the canonical one-form we deduce that

(6.4)

(\Phi,{\mathrm{d}}\Phi)^{*}\lambda=\lambda.

Moreover, since
\Phi\in U(n+1)
, it follows from the definition of
\alpha
that
\Phi^{*}\alpha=\alpha
and therefore

(6.5)

(\Phi,{\mathrm{d}}\Phi)^{*}\pi_{TM}^{*}\alpha=(\pi_{TM}\circ(\Phi,{\mathrm{d}}\Phi))^{*}\alpha=(\Phi\circ\pi_{TM})^{*}\alpha=\pi_{TM}^{*}\Phi^{*}\alpha=\pi_{TM}^{*}\alpha.

Consider now a one-parameter subgroup of
U(n+1)
generated by
A\in\mathfrak{u}(n+1)
and denote by
X_{A}
the vector field generating the corresponding action on
T{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}
. By (
6.4
) and (
6.5
), we get

0=\mathcal{L}_{X_{A}}(\lambda-\pi^{*}\alpha)=\iota_{X_{A}}\omega_{{\mathrm{d}}\alpha}+{\mathrm{d}}\Big(\lambda(X_{A})-\pi^{*}\alpha(X_{A})\Big).

Thus,
X_{A}
is Hamiltonian with Hamiltonian function

H(z,v)=\lambda(X_{A})_{(z,v)}-\pi^{*}\alpha(X_{A})_{(z,v)}=g_{z}(Az,v)-\alpha_{z}(Az),

as we wanted to show.

6.3.
A characterization of totally magnetic submanifolds

We recall the definition of totally magnetic submanifolds of a magnetic system
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
given in the introduction.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
be a magnetic system. An embedded submanifold
N\subset M
is called totally magnetic if for all magnetic geodesics
\gamma\colon I\to M
such that
\gamma(0)\in N
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)\in T_{\gamma(0)}N
there exist
\varepsilon>0
such that
\gamma((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))\subset N
.

Remark 6.6
.

Magnetomorphisms send totally magnetic submanifolds to totally magnetic submanifolds.

Remark 6.7
.

If
N
is a totally magnetic submanifold of
M
which is also a closed subset, then if
\gamma\colon I\to M
is a magnetic geodesic tangent to
N
, we have
\gamma(I)\subset N
.

Remark 6.8
.

When
\sigma=0
, totally magnetic submanifolds recover the notion of totally geodesics submanifolds.

We want to turn the local definition of total magnetic submanifolds into an infinitesimal characterization. In order to do so, we define the following objects for an arbitrary embedded submanifold
N
of
M
. First, let
\iota\colon N\to M
be the inclusion map and let
\mathrm{P}_{TN}\colon TM|_{N}\to TN
be the orthogonal projection. We define the magnetic system
(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N})
, where

(6.6)

g_{N}:=\iota^{*}g_{M},\qquad\sigma_{N}:=\iota^{*}\sigma_{M}.

We denote by
\nabla^{N}
and
\nabla^{M}
the Levi-Civita connections of
g_{N}
and
g_{M}
and by
Y^{N}
and
Y^{M}
the Lorentz forces associated with the magnetic systems on
M
and on
N
. Definition (
6.6
) implies that

(6.7)

\nabla^{N}_{v}(X|_{N})=\mathrm{P}_{TN}\nabla^{M}_{v}X,\quad Y^{N}v=\mathrm{P}_{TN}Y^{M}v,

for every
v\in TN
and every vector field
X
on
M
.

Totally geodesic submanifolds can be characterized in terms of the
second fundamental form
of the submanifold
N
, which is defined as the quadratic form

\mathrm{II}_{q}\colon T_{q}N\to T_{q}N^{\perp},\quad\mathrm{II}_{q}(v):=\nabla^{N}_{v}V-\nabla^{M}_{v}V,\quad\forall\,q\in N

for every
v\in TN
and every vector field
V
such that
V_{q}=v
. A submanifold
N\subset M
is totally geodesic if and only if its second fundamental form vanishes
\mathrm{II}
identically, that is,

(6.8)

\mathrm{II}_{q}(v)=0\quad\forall(q,v)\in TN.

For magnetic systems we add to the second fundamental form a linear term taking into account the difference between the extrinsic and intrinsic Lorentz forces.

Definition 6.9
.

The tensor

\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}_{q}\colon T_{p}N\to T_{q}N^{\perp},\qquad\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}_{q}(v):=\mathrm{II}_{q}(v)+Y_{q}^{M}v-Y_{q}^{N}v\quad\forall\,q\in N

is called the
magnetic second fundamental form
of the submanifold
N
inside the magnetic system
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
.

Notice that the metric part of
\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}
is quadratic in
v
, while the magnetic part is linear in
v
. This implies that
\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}
vanish if and only if the metric and the magnetic part separately vanish.

We can now prove the characterization of totally magnetic submanifolds.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:

Let
\delta\colon I\to N
be a magnetic geodesic of
(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N})
. Let
t_{0}\in I
and let
\gamma
be a magnetic geodesic in
\left(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M}\right)
such that
\gamma(t_{0})=\delta(t_{0})
and
\dot{\gamma}(t_{0})=\dot{\delta}(t_{0})
. By (1), there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\gamma((t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}\varepsilon))\subset N
. In particular,
\dot{\gamma}(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}N
for all
t\in(t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}+\varepsilon)
and therefore

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}^{M}\dot{\gamma}=Y^{M}_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathrm{P}_{TN}\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}^{M}\dot{\gamma}=\mathrm{P}_{TN}Y^{M}_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}^{N}\dot{\gamma}=Y^{N}_{\gamma}\dot{\gamma}

Therefore,
\gamma
is a magnetic geodesic for
(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N})
and we conclude from the uniqueness of solutions to ordinary differential equations that
\delta|_{(t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}+\varepsilon)}=\gamma|_{(t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}+\varepsilon)}
. Since
t_{0}
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\delta
is also a magnetic geodesic for
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
:
Let
\gamma\colon I\to M
be a magnetic geodesic for
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
such that
(\gamma(0),\dot{\gamma}(0))\in TN
. Let
\delta\colon(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to N
be a magnetic geodesic for
(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N})
with the same initial conditions as
\gamma
. By (2),
\delta
is also a magnetic geodesic for
(M,g_{M},\sigma_{M})
. By uniqueness of solutions to ordinary differential equations, we conclude that
\delta=\gamma|_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
. In particular,
\gamma((-\varepsilon,\varepsilon))\subset N
.

(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
:

If
\delta
is any curve on
N
, we have

\nabla^{N}_{\dot{\delta}}\dot{\delta}-Y^{N}_{\delta}\dot{\delta}=\nabla^{M}_{\dot{\delta}}\dot{\delta}-Y^{M}_{\delta}\dot{\delta}+\mathrm{II}_{\delta}^{\mathrm{mag}}(\dot{\delta})

If (2) holds and
\delta
is a magnetic geodesic for
(N,g_{N},\sigma_{N})
, then
0=0+\mathrm{II}_{\delta}^{\mathrm{mag}}(\dot{\delta})
. Statement (3) follows by taking the initial conditions of
\delta
arbitrary. Vice versa, if (3) holds, the equation above shows that

\nabla^{N}_{\dot{\delta}}\dot{\delta}-Y^{N}_{\delta}\dot{\delta}=0\quad\iff\quad\nabla^{M}_{\dot{\delta}}\delta-Y^{M}_{\delta}\dot{\delta}=0.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
:
If
u\in T_{q}N^{\perp}
is arbitrary, then for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we get

0=g_{q}(\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}_{q}(\lambda v),u)=\lambda^{2}g_{q}(\mathrm{II}_{q}(v),u)+\lambda g_{q}(Y^{M}_{q}v-Y^{N}_{q}v,u),

which is a polynomial of degree two in
\lambda
. Since a real polynomial is zero if and only if all its coefficients are zero, we conclude that for all
u\in T_{q}N^{\perp}
,
g_{q}(\mathrm{II}_{q}(v),u)=0
and
g_{q}(Y^{M}_{q}v-Y^{N}_{q}v,u)=0
. Hence,
\mathrm{II}_{q}=0
and
Y^{M}|_{TN}=Y^{N}
since
u
was arbitrary.

(4)\Rightarrow(3)
:
From the definition, we see that
\mathrm{II}_{q}=0
and
Y^{M}|_{TN}=Y^{N}
imply
\mathrm{II}^{\mathrm{mag}}=0
.

(4a)\Leftrightarrow(4b)
:
From equation (
6.7
), we see that
Y^{M}|_{TN}-Y^{N}=\mathrm{P}_{TN^{\perp}}Y^{M}|_{TN}
and the equivalence follows.

(4b)\Leftrightarrow(4c)
:
If
u,v\in T_{q}M
, then

(\sigma_{M})_{q}(u,v)=g_{q}(Y^{M}_{q}u,v).

Therefore, the left-hand side is zero for all
u\in T_{q}N
and
v\in T_{q}N^{\perp}
if and only if the right-hand side is zero for all
u\in T_{q}N
and
v\in T_{q}N^{\perp}
. Finally, the right-hand side is zero for all
u\in T_{q}N
and
v\in T_{q}N^{\perp}
if and only if
Y^{M}_{q}u\in(T_{q}N^{\perp})^{\perp}=T_{q}N
for all

.
∎

Remark 6.10
.

If we fix the kinetic energy

(or the strength

6.4.
Proof of Corollary
1.5
(Second Part)

We can now use Theorem
1.4
to classify

N

({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g,{\mathrm{d}}\alpha)

.

By condition (4) in Theorem

,
N
is totally geodesic in
({\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}},g)
. By the classification of closed, connected totally geodesic submanifolds with positive dimension of spheres of constant curvature
[

N
is a

(6.9)

N=E\cap{\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}.

Our aim is to show that

is a complex linear subspace. Since
N
is totally magnetic, by condition (4a) in Theorem
1.4
we see that for every

(6.10)

\mathrm{i}P_{(\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp}}u=Y^{N}_{z}u\in T_{z}N,\quad\forall\,u\in T_{z}N.

We distinguish two cases. In the first case
\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}E=2
. If
z\in N\subset E
, then

(6.11)

E={\mathbb{R}}z\oplus{\mathbb{R}}u,

where
u\in z^{\perp}=T_{z}N
. It follows that there exists

and

such that

\mathrm{i}v=\mathrm{i}P_{(\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp}}u\in T_{z}N.

From (

) and the expression for

and hence

since
\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}E=2
. We conclude that
E={\mathbb{R}}z\oplus{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{i}z)=\mathbb{C}z
is complex.

In the second case,
\dim_{{\mathbb{R}}}E\geq 3
. If

, then
E\cap(\mathbb{C}z)^{\perp}\subset T_{z}N
. Therefore, by (

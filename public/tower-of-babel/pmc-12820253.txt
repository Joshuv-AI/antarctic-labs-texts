Pan-cancer analysis of DTX3L as a potential prognostic and immunological biomarker
Authors: Yunfeng Zhang, Jingyi Mu, Yue Lu, Jiayu Jing, Jianmei Huang, Zhenglong Guo, Wenke Yang, Guiyu Lou, Yue Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDTX3L plays pivotal roles in diverse biological processes, yet its multifaceted functions and the molecular mechanisms driving tumorigenesis and cancer progression remain incompletely defined. Here, we systematically investigated DTX3L’s expression profiles, prognostic value, and potential regulatory mechanisms across pan-cancers. By integrating data from multiple databases, we analyzed the expression levels of DTX3L and its correlations with clinicopathological features, prognosis, and immune infiltration. Leveraging the STRING database, we identified DTX3L-associated genes and performed functional enrichment analysis. Finally, we experimentally validated the impact of DTX3L knockdown on the proliferation and migration of ovarian cancer cells. We found DTX3L was significantly upregulated in most human tumors, with its expression correlating with patient age, TNM stage, grade, and clinical stage—indicating DTX3L as a biomarker of poor prognosis. DTX3L showed extensive co-expression with key RNA modification genes and correlations with immune checkpoint genes, immune cells, and immune infiltration scores. GO and KEGG analyses linked DTX3L to DNA damage repair, ubiquitination regulation, and innate immune signaling pathways. In ovarian cancer cells, DTX3L knockdown markedly reduced cell proliferation, migration, and increased apoptosis. These findings demonstrate that DTX3L is upregulated in various cancers and is associated with poor patient prognosis, suggesting its potential as both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target in pan-cancer immunotherapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-32552-3.Keywords: DTX3L, Pan-cancer, Biomarker, Prognosis, ImmunotherapySubject terms: Biomarkers, Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Immunology, Oncology

Introduction

Deltex E3 ubiquitin ligase 3 L (DTX3L), a member of the Deltex family, possesses unique structural features and critical biological functions. Its protein structure includes a C-terminal RING (Really Interesting New Gene) domain and a DTC (Deltex C-Terminal) domain, which are pivotal for mediating interactions with E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes and recognizing ubiquitination substrates1,2. At the N-terminus, three subdomains (D1, D2, and D3) are present. The D1 and D2 domains facilitate DTX3L homooligomerization, while the D3 domain binds to PARP9 (poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase 9), forming a high-molecular-weight oligomeric complex essential for cellular signal transduction3,4.

As a key E3 ubiquitin ligase, DTX3L has emerged as a pivotal regulator in cancer biology, with its dysregulation implicated in the oncogenic transformation and progression of multiple malignancies. Over the past decade, cumulative evidence has unveiled context-dependent functions of DTX3L in pancreatic cancer, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, and glioma, where it orchestrates tumor cell proliferation, migration, and chemoresistance through ubiquitin-mediated proteostasis5–7. Notably, recent studies have highlighted its role in modulating immune checkpoint dynamics and tumor microenvironment remodeling, thereby facilitating immune evasion—a mechanism that underscores its potential as an immunotherapeutic target.

Pan-cancer analyses have become essential in oncology research, as they enable the identification of common molecular alterations and pathways across diverse cancer types, thereby facilitating the discovery of universal biomarkers and therapeutic targets8,9.Therefore, this study aims to conduct a comprehensive pan-cancer analysis of DTX3L, focusing on its expression patterns, prognostic significance, and mechanistic heterogeneity. The specific objectives include: (1) characterizing DTX3L expression across diverse cancer types using multi-omics datasets; (2) evaluating its prognostic value as a pan-cancer biomarker; (3) exploring its therapeutic potential as a target for tumors; (4) validating its oncogenic role in ovarian cancer through functional assays. By addressing these objectives, we seek to provide new insights and evidence to inform clinical treatment strategies for cancer patients.

In this study, we analyzed DTX3L expression in pan-cancer, investigating its correlations with clinicopathological characteristics and patient prognosis. Then we explored associations between DTX3L and RNA modification, immune checkpoint genes, immune cell infiltration, as well as immune infiltration scores. We also investigated the potential action mechanisms of DTX3L. Finally, we validated DTX3L expression and function in ovarian cancer tissues and cells. Collectively, these findings suggest DTX3L as a potential biomarker for pan-cancer prognosis and immunotherapeutic strategies.

Methods

DTX3L mRNA and protein expression level analysis

We downloaded the uniformly normalized TCGA, TARGET, and GTEx pan-cancer dataset (N = 19,131, G = 60,499) from the UCSC database (https://xenabrowser.net/, last accessed on March 28, 2025), and extracted expression data for ENSG00000163840 (DTX3L) across all samples, and filtered samples to include Solid Tissue Normal, Primary Solid Tumor, Primary Tumor, Normal Tissue, Primary Blood Derived Cancer-Bone Marrow, and Primary Blood Derived Cancer-Peripheral Blood. Samples with missing or invalid DTX3L expression values (e.g., expression values marked as “NA” or outside the range of detectable signals as defined by the original dataset) were excluded. Following log2(x + 0.001) transformation of expression values, cancer types with fewer than 3 samples were excluded, yielding DTX3L expression data across 34 tumor types. Then we analyzed the differential expression of DTX3L between normal and tumor samples for each cancer type.

We utilized the UALCAN data portal (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/, last accessed on March 28, 2025) to analyze DTX3L protein expression across multiple cancer types using proteomic datasets from the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC). Differential protein levels of DTX3L were interrogated in breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA), colon adenocarcinoma (COAD), ovarian serous cystadenocarcinoma (OV), kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC), lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) and liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC).

Patient prognosis analysis

We downloaded the uniformly normalized TCGA Pan-Cancer (PANCAN) dataset (N = 10,535, G = 60,499) from the UCSC database (https://xenabrowser.net/, last accessed on April 28, 2025), extracted expression data for ENSG00000163840 (DTX3L) across all samples. High-quality survival data were obtained from a previously published TCGA prognostic study10, excluding samples with follow-up duration < 30 days. Expression values were log2-transformed using log2(x + 0.001), and cancer types with < 10 samples were removed. Finally, we analyzed DTX3L expression alongside clinical endpoints including Overall Survival (OS), Progression-Free Survival (PFS), Disease-Free Survival (DFS), and Disease-Specific Survival (DSS).

Clinical characteristics and RNA modification analysis

We extracted DTX3L expression data from the PANCAN dataset described above. To ensure data quality and analytical robustness, we applied the following exclusion criteria: (1) cancer types with fewer than 3 samples were excluded to maintain statistical reliability; (2) samples with low sequencing depth (defined as < 10 million reads) or a high missing value rate (> 20% of genes with no expression values) were removed; (3) outliers showing extreme expression values (beyond mean ± 3 standard deviations) were excluded to minimize technical artifacts. Leveraging R software (version 3.6.4), we analyzed DTX3L expression discrepancies and correlations across clinical parameters (such as the tumor TNM, tumor grade, tumor stage, and patient age). For RNA modification, we extracted the expression data of marker genes for DTX3L and 44 RNA modification genes from three categories (m1A (10), m5C (13), m6A (21)) in each sample, and calculated their correlations.

Immune checkpoint genes, immune cells and immune infiltration scores analysis

We extracted expression data for DTX3L and 60 immune checkpoint pathway genes11 (24 inhibitory, 36 stimulatory) from the PANCAN dataset mentioned above, and computed Pearson correlations between DTX3L and each immune checkpoint gene to assess co-expression patterns. Using the TIMER algorithm12 in the R package IOBR (version 0.99.9), we estimated immune cell infiltration scores for B cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, neutrophils, macrophages, and dendritic cells across 9,406 tumor samples in 36 cancer types. For the TIMER algorithm parameter settings: immune cell subtype filtering was performed using the default “purity-adjusted” mode (to correct for tumor purity interference), with a significance threshold of P < 0.05 for retaining reliable infiltration scores; other parameters followed the IOBR package’s default configurations for TIMER-based immune infiltration estimation. This was followed by Pearson correlation analysis between DTX3L expression and these scores using the R package psych (version 2.1.6). Additionally, we calculated stromal, immune, and ESTIMATE scores for 9,555 samples (39 cancer types) from the PANCAN dataset using the R package ESTIMATE (v1.0.13)13. These scores are computed based on single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) of specific gene signatures, where the Stromal Score and Immune Score reflect the presence of stromal and immune cells, respectively, and the ESTIMATE Score (their sum) inversely correlates with tumor purity. Then the Pearson correlations between DTX3L expression and these immune infiltration scores analyzed using the corr.test function in psych (version 2.1.6).

DTX3L-related genes functional enrichment analysis

The top 50 genes associated with DTX3L were screened using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/, last accessed on June 11, 2025). Subsequently, Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses14–16 were conducted via the Bioinformatics.com.cn platform (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/, last accessed on June 11, 2025), a web-based tool for biomedical big data analysis.

Tissue samples

We collected OV tissues (n = 7) and normal ovarian tissues (n = 10) from patients who underwent surgery in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology at Henan Provincial People’s Hospital. Postoperative immunohistochemistry diagnosed the primary OV tissues. This study obtained informed consent from the patients and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Henan Provincial People’s Hospital (2020-132-02). All research was performed in accordance with relevant regulations. And all patients were informed and consented.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Tissue samples were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin for 24 h, then dehydrated, cleared, and paraffin-embedded. 4-µm sections were cut onto poly-L-lysine slides. After deparaffinization and rehydration, antigen retrieval used EDTA buffer (pH 8.0) via microwave for 10 min. Endogenous peroxidase was quenched with 3% H₂O₂ for 10 min, followed by 5% normal serum blocking for 30 min. Slides were incubated with anti-DTX3L primary antibody (1:200, Proteintech, 11963-1-AP) at 4 °C overnight, then biotinylated secondary antibody for 30 min. Signals were detected using ABC Elite Kit and DAB (10 min), counterstained with hematoxylin (30 s). After dehydration, clearing, and mounting, staining was quantified by H-score17.

Cell culture

OV cells (A2780, SKOV3, OVCAR-8) and normal ovarian cells (IOSE-80) were purchased from Saibai Kang (Shanghai) Biotechnology Co., Ltd. A2780 and OVCAR-8 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium with 10% fetal bovine serum, while SKOV3 and IOSE-80 cells were cultured in DMEM medium with 10% fetal bovine serum. All cells were maintained at 37℃ with 5% CO2. All cells were authenticated by STR profiling and tested negative for mycoplasma contamination.

SiRNA transfection

Logarithmic-phase cells (1 × 10⁴ cells/well) were seeded in 6-well plates. When confluence reached 70%, cells were transfected with siDTX3L or non-targeting siRNA (NC; GenePharma, Shanghai, China) using Heiff Trans liposome reagent (Invitrogen, USA). After 24 h, medium was removed, and cells were washed 2–3 times with PBS. Transfection efficiency was validated by Western blot and qRT-PCR. Cells with confirmed efficiency were used for subsequent assays. Sequences were as follows: siDTX3L Sense: CACGAGACCUCAAAUUUCUTT, Antisense: AGAAAUUUGAGUCUCGUGTT. NC Sense: UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT, Antisense: ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT.

qRT-PCR

RNA was extracted from cells and reverse transcribed into cDNA using EntiLink™ 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (ELK Biotechnology CO.LTD, Wuhan, China). The reaction conditions were: 37℃ for 60 min, 85℃ for 5 min, and 4℃ for 5 min. The EnTurbo SYBR Green PCR SuperMix kit (ELK Biotechnology CO.LTD, Wuhan, China) was used to amplify the samples on the StepOne Real-Time PCR instrument, with each sample run in triplicate. The reaction steps were: pre-denaturation at 95℃ for 3 min, denaturation 95℃ for 10 s, annealing at 58℃ for 30 s, and extension 72℃ for 30 s, for a total of 40 cycles. GAPDH was used as reference control. The 2−ΔΔCT method was used to analyze the relative expression level of DTX3L.Primer sequences were as follows: DTX3L Forward: CACCACAAAACATCCCGGTT; Reverse: TCAGCTCCTCTTTGACACGT. GAPDH Forward: CATCATCCCTGCCTCTACTGG; Reverse: GTGGGTGTCGCTGTTGAAGTC.

Western blot

Total protein was extracted using RIPA total protein lysate (Solarbio, Beijing, China) and protein concentration measured using the BCA kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Total protein was separated by SDS-PAGE (NCM Biotech, Suzhou, China) on 10% gels and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, Shanghai, China). Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk powder for 2 h and incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After washing with TBST three times for 10 min each, membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 h, followed by another round of TBST washes. Protein bands were visualized using an imaging system18. Antibodies used included: DTX3L (1:1000, Proteintech Group, Wuhan, China), GAPDH (1:10000, Abcam, UK), and HRP-Goat anti Rabbit (1:10000, Abcam, UK).

CCK-8 assay

Transfected cells were counted and seeded into 96-well plate (6 × 103/well) and cultured in a cell incubator (37 °C, 5% CO2) for 4 h to allow cell attachment. At different time points (0 h, 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 96 h), 10 µl of CCK-8 enhancement solution (Solarbio, Beijing, China) was added to each well and incubated for 2 h. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader19. Each group had three parallel wells, and the average value was calculated.

Wound healing assay

Cell monolayers were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 106 cells/well and cultured until 80%−90% confluence. A sterile 200 µL pipette tip was used to create a uniform scratch across the cell monolayer. Detached cells were removed by washing three times with PBS, and fresh medium was added. Images of the scratch area were captured immediately (0 h) and at 24 h, 48 h post-scratch using an inverted microscope (4× magnification) equipped with a digital camera. Wound closure was quantified by measuring the remaining cell-free area using ImageJ software. The percentage of wound closure was calculated as follows: Wound Closure (%) = (Area at 0 h−Area at time point)/Area at 0 h ×100%20.

Flow cytometry

Cells were harvested by trypsinization. After washing with pre-chilled PBS, 500 g for 5 min at 4 °C. Pellets were resuspended in pre-chilled 1×Binding Buffer to 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL. A 100 µL cell suspension aliquot was mixed with 5 µL Annexin V-IF647 and 5 µL PI (Servicebio, Wuhan, China), then incubated for 10 min at room temperature in the dark. 400 µL of pre-chilled 1×Binding Buffer was added, samples were gently mixed, and analyzed by flow cytometry.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 25.0 software. Measurement data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s). Unpaired Student’s t-tests were performed to assess significant differences between two groups, while one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for multi-group comparisons. When ANOVA showed significant differences, the Bonferroni method was used for post-hoc multiple comparisons to identify specific differences. Categorical data were analyzed using the Pearson chi-square test. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A pan-cancer expression analysis of DTX3L revealed significant upregulation in 30 tumor types compared to normal tissues, including GBM, UCEC, BRCA, OV, and others. (P < 0.05). Conversely, DTX3L expression was significantly downregulated in wilms tumor (WT), uterine carcinosarcoma (UCS), adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1A; Table 1). We used the CPTAC database to analyze the expression of DTX3L protein in tumors. The results demonstrated that the expression levels of total DTX3L protein were significantly increased in BRCA, COAD, OV, KIRC, UCEC, LUSC, PAAD, HNSC, and GBM compared with normal tissues(P < 0.05), while no significant difference was observed in LIHC (Fig. 1B).

Correlation between DTX3L expression and tumor prognosis

A comprehensive pan-cancer analysis revealed distinct prognostic roles of DTX3L expression across survival metrics. For OS, high DTX3L expression was significantly associated with poor prognosis in glioma (GBMLGG), brain lower grade glioma (LGG), thymoma (THYM), PAAD, and kidney chromophobe (KICH). Conversely, in sarcoma (SARC), kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM), and SKCM-metastasis (SKCM-M), patients with low DTX3L expression exhibited significantly shorter OS (Fig. 2A; Table 2). Regarding PFS, high DTX3L expression emerged as an independent risk factor in GBMLGG, LGG, and PAAD. In contrast, low DTX3L expression was associated with adverse PFS outcomes in BRCA, SKCM, and SKCM-M (Fig. 2B). For DSS, high DTX3L expression was associated with worse prognosis in GBMLGG, LGG, Lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), PAAD, and KICH, whereas low DTX3L expression was associated with poor prognosis in KIRC, SKCM, and SKCM-M (Fig. 2C). For DFS, high DTX3L expression was significantly correlated with decreased survival in Kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), and PAAD (Fig. 2D). These results suggest that DTX3L expression may serve as a potential prognostic biomarker with distinct roles in different cancer types.

Correlation between DTX3L expression and tumor prognosis in pan-cancer.

Correlation analysis of DTX3L expression and clinicopathological features

A comprehensive statistical analysis was conducted to explore the associations between DTX3L expression levels and various clinical parameters across multiple cancer types. For T parameter, significant differences in DTX3L expression were identified in PRAD, PAAD, and testicular germ cell tumors (TGCT). Regarding N parameter, PRAD and thyroid carcinoma (THCA) exhibited significant disparities in DTX3L expression. In terms of M parameter, significant associations were observed in stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), mesothelioma (MESO), and ACC (Fig. 3A). Significant differences in DTX3L expression were detected across tumor grades in GBMLGG, LGG, and PAAD (Fig. 3B). Analysis of tumor stage revealed significant correlations between DTX3L expression and stage in MESO, PAAD, TGCT, and ACC (Fig. 3C). Notably, DTX3L expression demonstrated significant correlations with patient age in 11 cancer types. Positive correlations were observed in GBMLGG, LGG, LUAD, THYM, and UCS, while negative correlations were identified in esophageal carcinoma (ESCA), KIRP, KIRC, THCA, and OV (Fig. 3D). Collectively, these findings suggest that DTX3L expression is significantly associated with multiple clinical parameters, including tumor stage, grade, and patient age, across diverse cancer types. This indicates its potential role as a multi-dimensional biomarker for cancer prognosis assessment and personalized medicine strategies.

DTX3L RNA modification analysis

A pan-cancer correlation analysis was performed to assess the association between DTX3L expression and RNA modifications. The results demonstrated that N⁶-methyladenosine (m⁶A) represents the most prevalent RNA modification across all analyzed tumor types. DTX3L showed significant correlations with multiple RNA modification-related genes, with most markers exhibiting positive associations with its expression levels. Notably, these correlations displayed substantial variation across cancer types. For instance, DTX3L showed strong positive correlations with LRPPRC and IGF2BP1 in OV, whereas these relationships were negatively correlated in TGCT (Fig. 3E).

DTX3L expression and immune infiltration analysis

A pan-cancer correlation analysis demonstrated that DTX3L expression was significantly positively correlated with inhibitory immune checkpoint genes including CD274, PDCD1, CTLA4, and stimulatory genes such as CD80 and CD40 (Fig. 4A). For example, in OV, DTX3L showed significant positive correlations with CD274, PDCD1, CTLA4, CD80, and CD40 (r = 0.694, 0.478, 0.441, 0.582, 0.519, respectively, all P < 0.05). Subsequently, we investigated the association between DTX3L expression and immune cell infiltration across cancer types, revealing significant correlations in 36 distinct malignancies. For example, in KIRC, PRAD, THCA, LGG, OV, and other tumors, DTX3L expression showed positive associations with infiltrating B cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, neutrophils, macrophages, and dendritic cells (DCs) (Fig. 4B). Furthermore, analysis of DTX3L expression versus immune infiltration scores identified significant positive correlations in 19 cancers, such as LGG, COAD, acute myeloid leukemia (LAML), BRCA, etc. (Fig. 4C). These findings indicate that DTX3L may play a role in the regulation of tumor immunity and could serve as a potential target for immunotherapy.

GO and KEGG enrichment results of DTX3L-related genes

To elucidate the potential mechanism of the DTX3L gene, we interrogated the STRING database to screen for DTX3L-associated genes, ultimately identifying 50 genes exhibiting significant correlations with DTX3L (Fig. 5A). Subsequent GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed on these 50 genes. In terms of the biological process (BP) category, the genes were prominently enriched in pathways related to viral defense responses and immune regulation (Fig. 5B). At the cellular component (CC) level, DTX3L - associated genes were predominantly enriched in functional modules involved in DNA damage repair and chromatin-related structures (Fig. 5C). From the perspective of molecular function (MF), these genes showed significant enrichment in processes such as ubiquitination modification, nucleic acid binding, and regulation of enzymatic activity (Fig. 5D).

KEGG pathway analysis further unraveled the disease - associated pathways and immune regulatory networks in which DTX3L participates. Notably, DTX3L - related genes were significantly enriched in virus infection - associated pathways. Moreover, immune-relevant pathways including the Notch signaling pathway and the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway were also prominently enriched, reflecting the involvement of DTX3L in immune signal transduction and regulation (Fig. 5E). Collectively, these findings furnish crucial functional enrichment clues and research directions for further dissecting the molecular mechanisms of DTX3L in tumorigenesis, viral infection, or immune regulation.

DTX3L is highly expressed in OV and promotes OV cell proliferation and migration

IHC staining revealed that DTX3L expression was significantly elevated in ovarian cancer tissues compared to normal ovarian tissues (P < 0.01, Fig. 6A). Further detection of DTX3L mRNA expression in OV cell lines showed that DTX3L mRNA levels were markedly higher in OV cells than in normal ovarian cells (P < 0.01, Fig. 6B). siRNA-mediated knockdown of DTX3L expression in A2780 cells was validated, demonstrating a significant reduction in DTX3L expression (P < 0.01, Fig. 6C). CCK-8 proliferation assay results showed that DTX3L depletion significantly inhibited the proliferative capacity of ovarian cancer cells, while wound healing scratch assay indicated that DTX3L knockdown markedly attenuated the migratory ability of ovarian cancer cells (P < 0.01, Fig. 6D and E). Separately, flow cytometry demonstrated increased apoptosis in siDTX3L-treated cells compared to NC controls (P < 0.05, Fig. 6F).

Discussion

Cancer represents a major global public health burden, characterized by intricate molecular networks and dysregulated cellular functions. Although targeted therapies and immunotherapies have substantially improved clinical outcomes, tumor heterogeneity and acquired resistance mechanisms continue to pose significant therapeutic challenges, necessitating the identification of novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets21,22. In recent years, pan-cancer analysis has emerged as a transformative approach to unraveling shared molecular mechanisms and clinical signatures across diverse cancer types, thereby accelerating the development of precision oncology9,23,24. As a member of the Deltex family of E3 ubiquitin ligases, DTX3L has been implicated in diverse biological processes, including DNA damage response, chromatin remodeling, and immune modulation25,26. However, its pan-cancer prevalence and functional relevance remain incompletely characterized. To address this gap, our study leveraged large-scale multi-omics datasets (e.g., TCGA, GTEx, and CPTAC) to systematically evaluate DTX3L expression patterns, prognostic implications, and functional associations in pan-cancer, providing novel insights into its role as a potential pan-cancer biomarker and therapeutic target.

Our analysis revealed that DTX3L expression is significantly upregulated in the vast majority of tumor types, with CPTAC data further confirming its marked upregulation at the protein level in 10 cancers, including BRCA, GBM, and OV. Concurrently, correlation analysis revealed significant associations between DTX3L expression and critical clinical parameters—including TNM staging, tumor grade, and patient age—across multiple cancer types (e.g., PRAD, PAAD, and THCA). For pancreatic cancer patients, DTX3L expression in tumor tissues is upregulated and correlates with poor prognosis27. Our comprehensive survival analysis across all cancer types further demonstrated that high DTX3L expression levels are associated with adverse outcomes in key survival metrics (OS, PFS, DFS, DSS) for cancers such as GBMLGG, LGG, THYM, PAAD, and KICH. Collectively, these findings suggest that DTX3L may serve as a potential prognostic biomarker for cancer diagnosis and prognosis assessment.

N6-methyladenosine (m⁶A), the predominant mRNA modification in eukaryotes, extensively regulates key biological processes in tumorigenesis, metastasis, and drug resistance by modulating mRNA stability, translational efficiency, and subcellular localization28–30. Our study revealed that m⁶A RNA modification is the most prevalent epigenetic modification across all analyzed tumor types. DTX3L exhibited significant correlations with multiple RNA modification-related genes, including m⁶A writers (e.g., METTL3/METTL14), erasers (e.g., FTO), and readers (e.g., YTHDF family), with most marker genes showing positive associations with DTX3L expression. This finding suggests that DTX3L is deeply embedded in the RNA modification regulatory network of tumor cells, influencing the post-transcriptional regulation of oncogenes or tumor suppressors to drive tumor progression.

DTX3L expression is closely associated with immune microenvironment alterations in multiple cancers, potentially influencing immune cell infiltration and function within the tumor microenvironment to impact tumor progression31. Our pan-cancer analysis revealed a central role of DTX3L in regulating the tumor immune microenvironment: its expression was predominantly positively correlated with both inhibitory and stimulatory immune checkpoint genes, simultaneously associated with immune cells including B cells, CD4+/CD8 + T cells, neutrophils, macrophages, and DCs, and positively correlated with immune infiltration scores in 19 cancers. This multiple association suggests that DTX3L acts as a key “driver” of the tumor immune microenvironment and holds promise as a potential target for combination immunotherapy.

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of DTX3L-associated genes identified significant enrichment in pathways related to RNA modification, viral defense responses, DNA damage repair, and ubiquitination regulation. Ubiquitination, a key regulatory mechanism for intracellular protein degradation and signal transduction. Based on the findings of this study, DTX3L may bidirectionally regulate immune activation and inhibitory feedback by targeting specific substrates, thereby modulating the tumor immune microenvironment. KEGG pathway analysis further reinforces the central role of DTX3L in immune regulation and disease pathogenesis. The Notch signaling pathway, a highly conserved cell signaling mechanism, plays a complex role in tumorigenesis and progression, influencing not only tumor cell growth and survival but also indirectly regulating tumor progression by modulating immune cell differentiation and function within the tumor microenvironment. Activation of Notch signaling promotes polarization of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), enhancing their immunosuppressive capacity. Therefore, DTX3L may suppress tumor immune evasion by inhibiting Notch signaling, influencing TAM function and reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment32,33.

This study demonstrates that DTX3L is significantly upregulated in ovarian cancer tissues, pointing to its potential role as an oncoprotein driving ovarian cancer development and progression. Consistent with this, DTX3L knockdown in A2780 cells inhibited proliferation, reduced migration, and enhanced apoptosis. Similarly, in ESCC, DTX3L expression increases in parallel with tumor advancement. Digital spatial profiling of early-stage ESCC patients reveals that DTX3L knockdown curbs tumor cell proliferation and migration, and reduces M2 polarization of TAMs6. Analogously, in cervical cancer, DTX3L knockdown in vitro and in vivo models suppresses cell proliferation, migration, and invasion while enhancing apoptosis34. Collectively, these findings across different cancer types underscore the role of DTX3L in promoting tumor cell proliferation and migration, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target for multiple cancers.

This study has several limitations. The relatively small sample sizes of our validation cohorts limit the statistical power of our analyses and increase the risk of overfitting. These findings therefore require further validation in larger, independent patient cohorts to ensure their robustness and generalizability. Although in vitro functional assays in OV cells were consistent with bioinformatics analyses from public databases, we plan to employ patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models to determine its prognostic and therapeutic relevance.

In summary, this pan-cancer study comprehensively characterizes DTX3L expression, prognostic significance, and molecular mechanisms. Our findings establish DTX3L as a key regulator of tumorigenesis, immune modulation, and RNA modification, positioning it as a multifaceted prognostic biomarker and promising therapeutic target for precision oncology.

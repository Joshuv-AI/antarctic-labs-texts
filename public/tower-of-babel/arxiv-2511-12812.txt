Rational Points in Weighted Projective Spaces over Finite Fields
S. Salami, T. Shaska

Abstract
. We establish the equivalence of three notions of 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -rational points on weighted projective spaces ℙ 𝐰 n \mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n} and derive explicit combinatorial formulas for their enumeration, leveraging Burnside’s lemma and gcd (greatest common divisor) computations. We further derive formulas for point counts under weight normalization, providing closed expressions for the singular and smooth loci. Our results confirm the rationality of the zeta function Z ⁡ ( ℙ 𝐰 n , t ) Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t) via a finite product formula and reveal a canonical multiplicative decomposition aligning with the stratification into smooth and singular loci. These contributions advance the arithmetic theory of weighted projective spaces over finite fields, with computational examples illustrating the formulas for specific weights and fields.



1.
Introduction

The arithmetic geometry of varieties over finite fields provides a profound framework for exploring the interplay between algebraic structures and their solutions in discrete settings. Weighted projective spaces
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}=\mathbb{P}_{(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})}
, where coordinates
[x_{0}:\dots:x_{n}]
are subject to a non-standard scaling action
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\sim(\lambda^{w_{0}}x_{0},\dots,\lambda^{w_{n}}x_{n})
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, governed by positive integer weights
w_{i}
, introduce complexities absent in classical projective spaces. The orbifold nature of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
influences both the geometry and the arithmetic, making the study of rational points—solutions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
—a central inquiry in the arithmetic theory of singular spaces.

A foundational contribution in this direction is the paper
[
5
]
, which investigates rational points and zeta functions of Humbert surfaces over finite fields. In that work, the moduli spaces parameterizing genus 2 curves with
(n,n)
-split Jacobians were analyzed, and exact point counts and zeta functions were computed for small fields. The interplay between degeneration in characteristic
p
and the arithmetic structure of these moduli loci revealed geometric and combinatorial patterns that motivate broader stratification techniques. These ideas directly inform the present study, which extends such methods to higher-dimensional weighted projective spaces. In parallel, this work is also motivated by applications to graded quantum codes
[
10
]
, where precise rational point counts provide algebraic invariants essential for designing error-correcting codes with improved performance and fault tolerance. While the arithmetic of classical projective spaces is well understood, the weighted case presents new challenges stemming from stabilizer actions, singular strata, and the breakdown of homogeneity under standard field extensions.

This paper focuses on the arithmetic of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, beginning with the clarification and reconciliation of three natural definitions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points. We prove their equivalence under standard assumptions and explore the implications when interpreted strictly. We then derive explicit counting formulas for rational points using Burnside’s lemma and a combinatorial stratification by coordinate supports and greatest common divisors. A second, purely combinatorial formula is shown to be equivalent, leading to a computationally efficient method for large fields and higher dimensions.

We analyze how the enumeration of rational points behaves under normalization of the weight vector, and establish formulas that quantify discrepancies arising from weight rescalings. In particular, we identify conditions under which the normalized and unnormalized spaces yield the same point counts and illustrate when strict inequalities arise. We then stratify
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
into its singular and smooth loci, and derive closed-form expressions for the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points lying in each component. These expressions reflect the underlying orbifold structure and illuminate how singularities contribute to the arithmetic count.

These results are extended to the setting of weighted hypersurfaces, defined as the vanishing loci of weighted homogeneous polynomials. We generalize the enumeration techniques to this broader class and formulate counting expressions adapted to the affine cones and group actions involved.

Finally, we investigate the zeta function
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
associated with the sequence of point counts over field extensions. We prove its rationality, derive an explicit decomposition aligned with the geometric stratification into smooth and singular parts, and confirm that the Riemann hypothesis component of the Weil conjectures holds. Although functional equations fail in the presence of singularities, the decomposition allows for a transparent description of the poles and zeros of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
and highlights its dependence on the weight data.

Our contributions advance the arithmetic theory of weighted projective spaces by providing precise enumeration techniques, structural theorems, and zeta-theoretic interpretations. These tools offer both theoretical insights and computational utility, with potential applications in point distributions, coding theory, and the arithmetic of singular algebraic stacks.

2.
Weighted Projective Spaces and Rational Points

Let
k
be a field of any characteristic and
\mathbf{w}=(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{(n+1)}
, called a vector of weights, for any integer
n\geq 1
. We assume that the field
k
contains a fixed primitive
w_{i}
-th root of unity
\xi_{w_{i}}
, which generates a cyclic subgroup of
k
denoted by
\mu_{w_{i}}
for each
i=0,\cdots,n
.

Now, consider the action of
k^{*}=k\setminus\{0\}
on
k^{n+1}\setminus\{(0,\cdots,0)\}
given by

\lambda\cdot(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})=\left(\lambda^{w_{0}}x_{0},\dots,\lambda^{w_{n}}x_{n}\right),\ \text{for }\ \lambda\in k^{*}.

Define the
weighted projective space

\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbf{w}}
to be the quotient space of this action, which is a geometric quotient since
k^{*}
is a reductive group. The vector
\mathbf{w}=(1,1,\cdots,1)
gives the usual projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Weighted projective spaces generalize classical projective spaces by allowing non-uniform scaling in the homogeneous coordinates. This weighted structure introduces an orbifold geometry, with quotient singularities arising from the action of
k^{*}
. These spaces serve as natural ambient spaces for weighted hypersurfaces, which are the zero loci of weighted homogeneous polynomials. The arithmetic properties of points on these spaces, particularly over finite fields, are influenced by the weights, leading to intricate patterns in point counts and zeta functions, as explored in subsequent sections.

2.1.
The notion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Rational point on weighted spaces

We are interested on the case
k=\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
an algebraically closed field containing the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=p^{\alpha}
elements for some prime number
p
. First, we provide the formal definition for the notion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point, which are introduced and used in the literature
[
3
,
7
,
1
,
9
,
2
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A point
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational if its given representative
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
satisfies
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.,
x_{i}^{q}=x_{i}
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
.

Definition 2.2
.

A point
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is
G-invariant
(or fixed by Frobenius) if for any representative
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
, applying the Frobenius automorphism
\sigma:\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
,
y\mapsto y^{q}
, results in a tuple that represents the same point. That is, there exists some
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
such that
x_{i}^{q}=\lambda^{w_{i}}x_{i}
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
.

Definition 2.3
.

A point
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n}]
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational if its field of definition
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathbf{x})
equals
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathbf{x})
is defined as the smallest extension
K/\mathbb{F}_{q}
(contained in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
) such that
\mathbf{x}
has a representative
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{n})\in K^{n+1}\setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
, where two representatives are equivalent if
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{n})=(\lambda^{w_{0}}z_{0},\ldots,\lambda^{w_{n}}z_{n})
for some
\lambda\in K^{*}
.

In the context of algebraic geometry, an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point on a variety
\mathcal{X}
is usually understood as a point
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}})
such that
\mathbf{x}
is the image of an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point
\spec(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to X
. For weighted projective spaces, this translates to the existence of a representative whose coordinates are in the base field. We adopt this as the
standard interpretation
of
2.1
.

Definition 2.4
.

A point
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational if there exists a representative
[y_{0},\ldots,y_{n}]
of
x
such that
y_{i}^{q}=y_{i}
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
.

The existence of a representative of
[y_{0},\ldots,y_{n}]
of any point
\mathbf{x}
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is proved in
[
2
, Prop. 2.1]
.
We now prove the equivalence of these three definitions under the standard interpretation.

Proposition 2.1
.

Under the standard interpretation of
2.1
(as
2.4
), the three notions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
are equivalent:

(i)

2.2
(G-invariance/Frobenius fixed point)

(ii)

2.3
(Field of definition is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
)

(iii)

2.4
(Existence of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-representative)

Proof.

Assume
\mathbf{x}
satisfies
2.2
. Let
[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be any representative of
\mathbf{x}
. Then there exists
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
such that
x_{i}^{q}=\lambda^{w_{i}}x_{i}
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
. To prove
2.3
, we need to show that
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathbf{x})
is generated by the ratios
z_{ij}=x_{i}^{1/w_{i}}/x_{j}^{1/w_{j}}
(for
x_{j}\neq 0
). Applying the Frobenius automorphism
\sigma:y\mapsto y^{q}
to these generators yields

\sigma(z_{ij})=(x_{i}^{q})^{1/w_{i}}/(x_{j}^{q})^{1/w_{j}}.

Substituting
x_{k}^{q}=\lambda^{w_{k}}x_{k}
gives

\sigma(z_{ij})=(\lambda^{w_{i}}x_{i})^{1/w_{i}}/(\lambda^{w_{j}}x_{j})^{1/w_{j}}=(\lambda x_{i}^{1/w_{i}})/(\lambda x_{j}^{1/w_{j}})=x_{i}^{1/w_{i}}/x_{j}^{1/w_{j}}=z_{ij}.

Since all generators
z_{ij}
are fixed by Frobenius, they belong to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus,
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathbf{x})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and as
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathbf{x})
extends
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This shows (i) implies (ii).

Next, assume
\mathbf{x}
satisfies
2.3
, i.e.,
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This implies all ratios
z_{ij}=x_{i}^{1/w_{i}}/x_{j}^{1/w_{j}}
(for
x_{j}\neq 0
) belong to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Let
y_{k}=x_{k}^{1/w_{k}}
for each
k
, so
y_{i}/y_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0\}
for all
i,j
with
x_{j}\neq 0
. Then, for any
i
with
x_{i}\neq 0
,
y_{i}=c_{i}y_{j}
for some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
(set
c_{i}=0
if
x_{i}=0
). The coordinates satisfy
x_{i}=y_{i}^{w_{i}}=(c_{i}y_{j_{0}})^{w_{i}}=c_{i}^{w_{i}}y_{j_{0}}^{w_{i}}
. Since
y_{i}/y_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, Frobenius yields
(y_{i}/y_{j})^{q}=y_{i}/y_{j}
, implying
y_{i}^{q}/y_{i}=y_{j}^{q}/y_{j}
for
x_{i},x_{j}\neq 0
. Let this common ratio be
\mu\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
, so
y_{k}^{q}=\mu y_{k}
for
x_{k}\neq 0
.

To show
2.4
, find a representative
[z_{0},\ldots,z_{n}]
with
z_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Set
z_{k}=\alpha^{w_{k}}x_{k}
for some
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
, requiring
z_{k}^{q}=z_{k}
, i.e.,
\alpha^{qw_{k}}x_{k}^{q}=\alpha^{w_{k}}x_{k}
.

From (i) (equivalent to (ii)), there exists
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
with
x_{k}^{q}=\lambda^{w_{k}}x_{k}
. Substituting gives
\alpha^{qw_{k}}(\lambda^{w_{k}}x_{k})=\alpha^{w_{k}}x_{k}
. For
x_{k}\neq 0
, divide by
x_{k}
:
\alpha^{qw_{k}}\lambda^{w_{k}}=\alpha^{w_{k}}
, or
(\alpha^{q-1}\lambda)^{w_{k}}=1
.

Let
J=\{k\mid x_{k}\neq 0\}
. This holds for all
k\in J
, so
\alpha^{q-1}\lambda
is a
d_{J}
-th root of unity, where
d_{J}=\gcd(\{w_{k}\mid k\in J\})
. The multiplicative group
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
is divisible, so for any
a\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
and positive integer
m
, there exists
b
with
b^{m}=a
. Solve
\alpha^{q-1}=\lambda^{-1}\zeta
for some
d_{J}
-th root
\zeta
; such
\alpha
exists. Thus,
z_{k}=\alpha^{w_{k}}x_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for all
k
, showing (ii) implies (iii).

Assume
\mathbf{x}
satisfies
2.4
. There exists a representative
[y_{0},\ldots,y_{n}]
with
y_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so
y_{i}^{q}=y_{i}
. To show G-invariance, find
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
with
y_{i}^{q}=\lambda^{w_{i}}y_{i}
. Choose
\lambda=1
:
y_{i}^{q}=1^{w_{i}}y_{i}=y_{i}
. Thus,
\mathbf{x}
is G-invariant, showing (iii) implies (i).
∎

As shown by
2.1
, under the standard interpretation of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rationality, all three definitions are equivalent.
However, if
2.1
is interpreted strictly (i.e., requiring the given representative to have coordinates in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
), then it is generally a stronger condition than
2.2
or
2.3
.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
2.1
holds for a point
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, then
2.2
holds with
\lambda=1
. The converse does not generally hold.

The converse holds if and only if, for a given Galois-invariant point
[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
with a representative satisfying
x_{i}^{q}=\lambda^{w_{i}}x_{i}
for some
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
, there exists a choice of
\lambda
such that
\lambda^{w_{k}}=1
for all
k
with
x_{k}\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
satisfies
2.1
, so there exists a representative with
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for all
i
. Then
x_{i}^{q}=x_{i}
for all
i
. Setting
\lambda=1\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
, we have
x_{i}^{q}=1^{w_{i}}x_{i}=x_{i}
, so
\mathbf{x}
satisfies
2.2
.

For the converse, assume
\mathbf{x}=[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
satisfies
2.2
, so there exists
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
such that
x_{i}^{q}=\lambda^{w_{i}}x_{i}
for all
i
. For this representative to satisfy
2.1
, we need
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for all
i
, i.e.,
x_{i}^{q}=x_{i}
for all
i
. This requires
\lambda^{w_{i}}x_{i}=x_{i}
, so
\lambda^{w_{i}}=1
whenever
x_{i}\neq 0
. However,
\lambda
is defined up to scaling by roots of unity compatible with the weights (i.e.,
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda\cdot\zeta
where
\zeta^{w_{k}}=1
for all
k
with
x_{k}\neq 0
). If no such
\lambda
with
\lambda^{w_{k}}=1
exists for all
k
with
x_{k}\neq 0
, then the converse fails.

The converse holds if there exists a
\lambda
(among possible choices) such that
\lambda^{w_{k}}=1
for all
k
with
x_{k}\neq 0
, making
x_{i}^{q}=x_{i}
for all
i
. This is necessary, as
\lambda^{w_{i}}\neq 1
for some
i
with
x_{i}\neq 0
implies
x_{i}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}
unless rescaled. Sufficiency follows by choosing such a
\lambda
, ensuring the representative can be adjusted to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-coordinates.
∎

2.1
does not coincide with
2.2
. This implies that the standard definition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points is generally preferred for
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}

Example 2.5
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{3}
, so
q=3
. Let the weights be
\mathbf{w}=(1,2)
. Consider the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}_{(1,2)}
. Let
i
be a root of the polynomial
t^{2}+1=0

\mathbb{F}_{9}=\mathbb{F}_{3}(i)

\mathbf{x}=[i,1]\in\mathbb{P}_{(1,2)}(\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}})

2.2
).

The Frobenius automorphism is
\sigma:y\mapsto y^{3}
. We need to find
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{*}
such that
(x_{0}^{3},x_{1}^{3})=(\lambda^{w_{0}}x_{0},\lambda^{w_{1}}x_{1})

; and

Now, check

\mathbf{x}=[i,1]

2.2.
Number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational Points on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}

Given the equivalence of the three definitions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points in
2.1
, we now compute the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
using any of these notions. Specifically, we count the number of points that have a representative with all coordinates in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

The group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
acts on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
via the weighted scaling action defined earlier. The set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is precisely the set of orbits under this action. We use Burnside’s lemma to count these orbits, which states that the number of orbits is given by

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}|}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}|\text{Fix}(\lambda)|.

Here,
|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}|=q-1
, and
\text{Fix}(\lambda)
is the set of points in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
fixed by the action of
\lambda
. A point
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
is fixed by
\lambda
if
(\lambda^{w_{0}}x_{0},\ldots,\lambda^{w_{n}}x_{n})=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
. This implies
\lambda^{w_{i}}x_{i}=x_{i}
for all
i=0,\ldots,n
. For each coordinate
x_{i}
, this condition means either
x_{i}=0
or
\lambda^{w_{i}}=1
.

Let
N(\lambda)=|\{i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\mid\lambda^{w_{i}}=1\}|
. For a tuple
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})\in\text{Fix}(\lambda)
,
x_{i}
must be
0
for all
i
where
\lambda^{w_{i}}\neq 1
. For the
N(\lambda)
indices where
\lambda^{w_{i}}=1
,
x_{i}
can be any element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Therefore, the number of tuples satisfying these conditions (including the zero tuple) is
q^{N(\lambda)}
. Since the zero tuple
(0,\ldots,0)
does not correspond to a point in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, we exclude it, yielding
|\text{Fix}(\lambda)|=q^{N(\lambda)}-1
.
Substituting into Burnside’s lemma gives the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

The number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is given by

(1)

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}(q^{N(\lambda)}-1),

where
N(\lambda)=|\{i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\mid\lambda^{w_{i}}=1\}|
.

Let’s see the first simple application of the formula
Eq.

1
.

Example 2.6
.

Consider the weighted projective line
\mathbb{P}_{(1,2)}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
[x:y]\in\mathbb{P}_{(1,2)}
.
If
x\neq 0
, points can be scaled to
[1:y]
for
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, contributing
q
points.
If
x=0
and
y\neq 0
, the points
[0:y]
are identified under
\lambda^{2}y=y
, so the number of distinct points is the size of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}/(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{2}
, which is 2 if
q
is odd and 1 if
q
is even.
Thus,

|\mathbb{P}_{(1,2)}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\begin{cases}q+2&\text{if }q\text{ is odd,}\\
q+1&\text{if }q\text{ is even.}\end{cases}

Using the formula
Eq.

1
, one can also recover the well-known number of points on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Example 2.7
.

For the standard projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, all weights are
w_{i}=1
for
i=0,\ldots,n
.
In this case,
\lambda^{w_{i}}=1
simplifies to
\lambda=1
.
Thus, for
\lambda=1
,
N(1)=n+1
(since
1^{w_{i}}=1
for all
i
).
For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
with
\lambda\neq 1
,
N(\lambda)=0
(since
\lambda^{1}\neq 1

Applying
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\left((q^{N(1)}-1)+\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\lambda\neq 1}(q^{N(\lambda)}-1)\right)=1+q+\dots+q^{n}.

Lemma 2.4
.

The number of rational points in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is given by

(2)

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\gcd(k_{S},q-1),

where
S\subseteq T=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, and
k_{S}=\gcd(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})
.

Proof.

Let
N_{S}
be the number of points in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
with
x_{i}\neq 0
for
i\in S
and
x_{i}=0
for
i\notin S
. These points contribute
N_{S}=(q-1)^{|S|}
non-zero coordinates.
The number of rational points contributed by these points in the weighted projective space is

\frac{N_{S}\cdot\gcd(k_{S},q-1)}{q-1}.

Summing over all
\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T
, we obtain

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}\frac{N_{S}\cdot\gcd(k_{S},q-1)}{q-1}=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\gcd(k_{S},q-1).

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The formulas given by
2.3
and
2.4
are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
N(\lambda)=|\{i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\mid\lambda^{w_{i}}=1\}|
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Rearranging
Eq.

1
yields

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}q^{N(\lambda)}\right)-1.

For each
i
, the contribution to
q^{N(\lambda)}
is
q
if
\lambda^{w_{i}}=1
and 1 otherwise. Thus,

q^{N(\lambda)}=\prod_{i=0}^{n}\left(1+(q-1)[\lambda^{w_{i}}=1]\right),

where
[P]
denotes 1 if proposition
P
holds and 0 otherwise.

Expanding the product over subsets
S\subseteq T=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
gives

q^{N(\lambda)}=\sum_{S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|}\prod_{i\in S}[\lambda^{w_{i}}=1].

The product
\prod_{i\in S}[\lambda^{w_{i}}=1]
is 1 if
\lambda^{w_{i}}=1
for all
i\in S
(equivalently,
\lambda^{k_{S}}=1
, where
k_{S}=\operatorname{gcd}(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})
) and 0 otherwise. Thus,

q^{N(\lambda)}=\sum_{S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|}[\lambda^{k_{S}}=1].

Substituting into the formula for
|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
,

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\sum_{S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}[\lambda^{k_{S}}=1]\right)-1.

The inner sum equals
\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q-1)
, the number of solutions to
\lambda^{k_{S}}=1
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Thus,

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\sum_{S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|}\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q-1)\right)-1.

The empty set
S=\emptyset
contributes
(q-1)^{0}\cdot(q-1)=q-1
(vacuously true for all
\lambda
). Substituting yields the formula in
2.4
.
∎

As a first consequence of the above counting formulas, we have the following.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{w}=(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
be a vector of positive integer weights. For any integer
r\geq 1
, the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
-rational points on the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is given by

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n\}}(q^{r}-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q^{r}-1),

where
k_{S}=\operatorname{gcd}(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})
.

Proof.

The result follows by applying the point-counting formula in
Eq.

1
(or equivalently
Eq.

2
) to the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
. The formula holds for any finite field, so replace
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
, and
q-1
(the size of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
) with
q^{r}-1
(the size of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}^{*}
). The rest of the structure remains unchanged.
∎

2.3.
Weight Normalization and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Rational Points

Given two arbitrary vector of weights, over any field
k
of characteristic zero, it is a known fact that if the corresponding weighted spaces are isomorphic to each other then the set of
k
-rational points on them are also isomorphic, see for example
[
9
]
. But, in the following result, we show that this is not true over positive characteristic fields in general.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field. Let
\mathbf{w}=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})
be a vector of positive integer weights and define
d=\gcd(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})
. Let
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
be the normalized weight vector given by
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,w^{\prime}_{n})
where
w^{\prime}_{i}=w_{i}/d
, and define
d^{\prime}=\gcd(d,q-1)
.
The number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points on the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is related to the number of points on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}
by the formula:

(3)

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=d^{\prime}\cdot|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|+(d^{\prime}-1)-\frac{d^{\prime}}{q-1}\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)},

where
H
is the image of
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
the group homomorphism
\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{d}
.
As a consequence,
|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|\leq|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
, with equality if and only if
d^{\prime}=1
.

Proof.

Let
G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
be the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so
|G|=q-1
.
Define the sums
S_{\mathbf{w}}=\sum_{\lambda\in G}q^{N(\lambda)}
and

S_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}=\sum_{\lambda\in G}q^{N^{\prime}(\lambda)}
, where
N^{\prime}(\lambda)=|\{i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\mid\lambda^{w^{\prime}_{i}}=1\}|
.

The condition for
N(\lambda)
is
\lambda^{w_{i}}=1
, which is
\lambda^{d\cdot w^{\prime}_{i}}=1
. This can be written as
(\lambda^{d})^{w^{\prime}_{i}}=1
which implies
N(\lambda)=N^{\prime}(\lambda^{d})
and so

S_{\mathbf{w}}=\sum_{\lambda\in G}q^{N(\lambda)}=\sum_{\lambda\in G}q^{N^{\prime}(\lambda^{d})}.

Let
\phi:G\to G
be the group homomorphism
\phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{d}
. Let
H=\text{Im}(\phi)
be the image of this map, which is the subgroup of
d
-th powers in
G
. Let
d^{\prime}=\gcd(d,q-1)
. The size of the kernel of
\phi
is
d^{\prime}
, and the size of the image is
|H|=(q-1)/d^{\prime}
.
By grouping terms according to their image under
\phi
, we can rewrite the sum for
S_{\mathbf{w}}
as

S_{\mathbf{w}}=\sum_{\mu\in H}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in G\\
\lambda^{d}=\mu\end{subarray}}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}
.

For each
\mu\in H
, the number of elements
\lambda
such that
\lambda^{d}=\mu
is constant and equals the size of the kernel, which is
d^{\prime}
. Thus,

S_{\mathbf{w}}=\sum_{\mu\in H}d^{\prime}\cdot q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}=d^{\prime}\sum_{\mu\in H}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}.

The sum
S_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
can be split into elements inside and outside the subgroup
H
:

S_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}=\sum_{\mu\in G}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}=\sum_{\mu\in H}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}+\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}.

We can therefore express the sum over
H
as:

\sum_{\mu\in H}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}=S_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}-\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}.

We now substitute this correct expression back into the formula for
S_{\mathbf{w}}
:

S_{\mathbf{w}}=d^{\prime}\left(S_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}-\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}\right).

Now, replacing the point-counting equivalents of
S_{\mathbf{w}}
and
S_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
, i.e.,

S_{\mathbf{w}}=(q-1)|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|+(q-1),\;\text{and}\;S_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}=(q-1)|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|+(q-1),

in above equality and dividing by
(q-1)
leads to:

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|+1=d^{\prime}|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|+d^{\prime}-\frac{d^{\prime}}{q-1}\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}q^{N^{\prime}(\mu)}.

Finally, isolate
|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
to get the claimed result.
Clearly, the equality holds precisely if and only
d^{\prime}=1
.
∎

The following theorem addresses the different scenario where only a subset of the weights is scaled.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathbf{w}=(w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(w_{0},\gamma w_{1},\ldots,\gamma w_{n})
be two vectors of positive integer weights, where
\gamma
is a positive integer such that
\gcd(w_{0},\gamma)=1
. The difference in the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points on the corresponding weighted projective spaces is given by the formula:

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|-|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\left(\gcd(\gamma k_{S},q-1)-\gcd(k_{S},q-1)\right),

where
k_{S}=\gcd(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})
.

Proof.

We use the formula for the number of rational points given in
2.4
.
Let
T=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
.

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\gcd(k_{S},q-1).

For the weight vector
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
, let the corresponding gcd of weights for a subset
S
be
k^{\prime}_{S}
. The number of points is:

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\gcd(k^{\prime}_{S},q-1).

To find the relation between these two quantities, we analyze the term
\gcd(k^{\prime}_{S},q-1)
based on whether the index
0
is in the set
S
.

Case 1:
0\in S
.

Let
S=S_{0}\cup\{0\}
, where
S_{0}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The gcd for the weights
\mathbf{w}
is
k_{S}=\gcd(w_{0},\{w_{i}\mid i\in S_{0}\})
.
For the weights
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
, the corresponding gcd is
k^{\prime}_{S}=\gcd(w_{0},\{\gamma w_{i}\mid i\in S_{0}\})
. This can be written as
k^{\prime}_{S}=\gcd(w_{0},\gamma\cdot\gcd(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S_{0}\}))
.
Since we are given that
\gcd(w_{0},\gamma)=1
, it follows that for any integer
A
,
\gcd(w_{0},\gamma A)=\gcd(w_{0},A)
.
Therefore,
k^{\prime}_{S}=\gcd(w_{0},\gcd(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S_{0}\}))=k_{S}
.
This means that for any subset
S
containing the index
0
, the term
(q-1)^{|S|-1}\gcd(k_{S},q-1)
in the sum for
|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
is identical to the corresponding term for
|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
.

Case 2:
0\notin S
.

In this case,
S
is a non-empty subset of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The gcd for the weights
\mathbf{w}
is
k_{S}=\gcd(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})
.
For the weights
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
, the gcd is
k^{\prime}_{S}=\gcd(\{\gamma w_{i}\mid i\in S\})=\gamma\cdot\gcd(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})=\gamma k_{S}
.
These terms are generally not equal.

We can now compute the difference
|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|-|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
. We split the sum over all non-empty
S\subseteq T
into the two cases:

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|-|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T,0\in S}(q-1)^{|S|-1}(\gcd(k^{\prime}_{S},q-1)-\gcd(k_{S},q-1))

\displaystyle\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T,0\notin S}(q-1)^{|S|-1}(\gcd(k^{\prime}_{S},q-1)-\gcd(k_{S},q-1)).

From Case 1, for every term in the first sum,
k_{S}=k^{\prime}_{S}
, so each term is zero and the entire first sum vanishes.
From Case 2, for the second sum (
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
), we have
k^{\prime}_{S}=\gamma k_{S}
. This leaves us with:

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|-|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\left(\gcd(k_{S},q-1)-\gcd(\gamma k_{S},q-1)\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

The number of points

and
|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
are equal if and only if

for all non-empty subsets
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
. A sufficient condition for this is that
\gamma
is coprime to
q-1
.

2.4.
Number of Singular
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Rational Points on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}

The
singular locus
of the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, denoted by
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
, is formally defined as

\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}:\ \operatorname{gcd}_{i\in J(\mathbf{x})}\left(w_{i}\right)>d\right\},

where
d=\operatorname{gcd}(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})
,
J(\mathbf{x})=\{i:x_{i}\neq 0\}\subset T=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
; see
[
9
]
.

Following
[
6
]
, we define the
weak projective space
over
\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
as

\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}\setminus\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
,
the complement of
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, which is a smooth weighted subvariety.
Building upon the definition of the singular locus, we derive closed formulas for counting the number of singular points over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime number. For a given
\mathbf{w}=(w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{n+1}
with
d=\operatorname{gcd}(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})
, we have

(4)

|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}>d\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q-1),

and

(5)

|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}=d\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q-1),

where
T=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and
k_{S}=\operatorname{gcd}(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})
.

Proof.

Recall from
2.4
that the total number of rational points in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is given by

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q-1).

The definition of the singular locus
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
states that a point
\mathbf{x}
is singular if the greatest common divisor of the weights corresponding to its non-zero coordinates,
k_{J(\mathbf{x})}
, is strictly greater than
d=\operatorname{gcd}(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})
.
Therefore,

|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}>d\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q-1),

and hence

|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|-|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}=d\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q-1).

∎

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime number. For a given reduced vector
\mathbf{w}=(w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
with
\gcd(w_{i},q-1)=1
for all
0\leq i\leq n
, the following holds:

(6)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{n+1}-1}{q-1},

(7)

\displaystyle|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}>1\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{|S|-1},

(8)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}=1\end{subarray}}(q-1)^{|S|-1},

where
T=\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
and
k_{S}=\gcd(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})
.

Proof.

The assumption
\gcd(w_{i},q-1)=1
for all
0\leq i\leq n
implies
\gcd(k_{S},q-1)=1
for every nonempty
S\subseteq T
, since
k_{S}
divides each
w_{i}
with
i\in S
.

By
2.4
,

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\gcd(k_{S},q-1)=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}(q-1)^{|S|-1}.

Grouping by subset size yields

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}(q-1)^{i-1}.

By the binomial theorem,

q^{n+1}=(1+(q-1))^{n+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}(q-1)^{i}.

Subtracting the
i=0
term gives

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}(q-1)^{i}=q^{n+1}-1.

Dividing by
(q-1)
(valid since
q\geq 2
) yields
Eq.

6
:

\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}(q-1)^{i-1}=\frac{q^{n+1}-1}{q-1}.

For
Eq.

7
,
Eq.

4

2.5.
Counting Rational Points on Weighted Varieties

For the basic definitions of weighted varieties, we refer to
[
4
]
. For clarity, we briefly review some key concepts relevant to our work.

A
weighted variety
is a subvariety of a weighted projective space, defined by
weighted homogeneous polynomials
. A polynomial
f(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is
weighted homogeneous of degree

d
with respect to the weights
\mathbf{w}
if

f(\lambda^{w_{0}}x_{0},\lambda^{w_{1}}x_{1},\dots,\lambda^{w_{n}}x_{n})=\lambda^{d}f(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in k^{*}.

This condition ensures that the zero set of
f
is well-defined in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
.

A
weighted hypersurface

X\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is defined as the zero set of a weighted homogeneous polynomial
f
:

X=\{[x_{0}:x_{1}:\dots:x_{n}]\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}\mid f(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0\}.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}
be a vector of non-negative integers. We write
x^{\alpha}=x_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
and define the weighted degree of
x^{\alpha}
with respect to
\mathbf{w}
as

\deg_{\mathbf{w}}(x^{\alpha}):=\alpha\cdot\mathbf{w}=w_{0}\alpha_{0}+\cdots+w_{n}\alpha_{n}.

We say a polynomial
f\in k[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
is weighted homogeneous of degree
r\geq 1
if every monomial
x^{\alpha}
appearing in
f
has weighted degree
r
with respect to
\mathbf{w}
.

By a
weighted variety
, we mean an integral, separated subscheme of finite type in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
. In other words,
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is a weighted variety if there exist

f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\in k[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
such that
X
is isomorphic to the
k
-scheme

\Proj\left(\frac{k[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]}{\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\rangle}\right)
, where the polynomials
f_{i}
are weighted homogeneous.

For a weighted variety
X=Z(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
defined by weighted homogeneous polynomials
f_{i}
of degree
d_{i}=\deg_{\mathbf{w}}(f_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,r
,

(i)

the rational points correspond to the orbits under the action of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
on

\{(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{A}^{n+1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\{0\}\mid f_{i}(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0,\ \forall\,i\}.

(ii)

the number of rational points can be computed by stratifying based on the support
S
:

|X(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\sum_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T}\frac{N(S,F)\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q-1)}{q-1},

where
T=\{0,\dots,n\}
,
k_{S}=\operatorname{gcd}(\{w_{i}\mid i\in S\})
, and
N(S,F)
counts the points with support
S
(i.e.,
x_{i}\neq 0
for
i\in S
and
x_{i}=0
for
i\notin S
) such that
f_{i}(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
for all
i=1,\dots,r
.

Example 2.12
.

Consider the weighted hypersurface
X:y=x^{2}
in
\mathbb{P}_{(1,2)}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

For
x\neq 0
, we can scale to
[1:1]
, since
y=x^{2}
implies
[\lambda:\lambda^{2}]\sim[1:1]
.
For
x=0
, we have
y=0
, but
(0,0)
is excluded.
Thus,
|X(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=1
, contrasting with
q+1
points for the curve
y=x^{2}
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

We now establish a point-counting theorem for a hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
analogous to
2.7
for the ambient space
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
. Let
A(X)\subset\mathbb{A}^{n+1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\{0\}
be the set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-points on the affine cone over
X
. Let
N(X,\lambda)
be the number of points in
A(X)
fixed by the
\mathbf{w}
-action of
\lambda\in G=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, i.e.,

N(X,\lambda)=|\{x\in A(X)\mid\lambda^{w_{i}}x_{i}=x_{i}\text{ for all }i\}|.

By Burnside’s lemma,
|X(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\lambda\in G}N(X,\lambda)
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
be a hypersurface defined by
f=0
, and let
X^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}
be the isomorphic hypersurface under the weight normalization
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=\mathbf{w}/d
, where
d=\operatorname{gcd}(\mathbf{w})
. Let
d^{\prime}=\operatorname{gcd}(d,q-1)
, and let
H\subset G
be the subgroup of
d
-th powers.
The number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points on
X
is related to that on
X^{\prime}
by

|X(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=d^{\prime}\cdot|X^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|-\frac{d^{\prime}}{q-1}\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}N(X^{\prime},\mu),

where
N(X^{\prime},\mu)
is the number of points in
A(X)
fixed by the
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
-action of
\mu
.

Proof.

The proof follows similar logic to that of
2.7
. A point
x\in A(X)
is fixed by the
\mathbf{w}
-action of
\lambda
if
x_{i}=0
or
\lambda^{w_{i}}=1
for all
i
. This is equivalent to
x_{i}=0
or
(\lambda^{d})^{w^{\prime}_{i}}=1
, which means
x
is fixed by the
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
-action of
\lambda^{d}
. Thus,
N(X,\lambda)=N(X^{\prime},\lambda^{d})
.

Let
S(X)=\sum_{\lambda\in G}N(X,\lambda)
and
S(X^{\prime})=\sum_{\mu\in G}N(X^{\prime},\mu)
.
Then

S(X)=\sum_{\lambda\in G}N(X^{\prime},\lambda^{d})=d^{\prime}\sum_{\mu\in H}N(X^{\prime},\mu).

By decomposing
S(X^{\prime})=\sum_{\mu\in H}N(X^{\prime},\mu)+\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}N(X^{\prime},\mu)
, we substitute to find

S(X)=d^{\prime}\left(S(X^{\prime})-\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}N(X^{\prime},\mu)\right)
.
Since
|X(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{1}{q-1}S(X)
and
|X^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{1}{q-1}S(X^{\prime})
, multiplying by
\frac{1}{q-1}
yields

|X(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=d^{\prime}\cdot|X^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|-\frac{d^{\prime}}{q-1}\sum_{\mu\in G\setminus H}N(X^{\prime},\mu).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
The Zeta Function of Weighted Projective Space

This section is devoted to the study of the zeta function of the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
. We first establish the baseline case of the standard projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
before leveraging the combinatorial point-counting formulae from Section 2 to prove the rationality of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
. We then investigate its algebraic structure by demonstrating a canonical decomposition, induced by the stratification of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
into its smooth and singular loci. This analysis clarifies the distinct arithmetic contributions from each component and reveals that the zeta function, unlike the entropy invariants of Section 6, is not an invariant of the abstract variety but depends critically on the weighted embedding.

3.1.
The Zeta Function Standard Projective Space

We begin by recalling the well-known zeta function of the
n
-dimensional standard projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, which corresponds to the weight vector
\mathbf{w}=(1,1,\dots,1)
.

Definition 3.1

(Zeta Function)
.

The zeta function of a smooth projective variety
X
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the formal power series

Z(X,t):=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|X(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right).

For
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
-rational points is given by

|\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=1+q^{r}+q^{2r}+\dots+q^{nr}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(q^{j})^{r}.

This simple, non-periodic point count leads to the canonical rational form of its zeta function.

Proposition 3.1
.

The zeta function of the standard projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is

Z(\mathbb{P}^{n},t)=\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-qt)\cdots(1-q^{n}t)}.

Proof.

By definition, the logarithm of the zeta function is

\displaystyle\log Z(\mathbb{P}^{n},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{r}}{r}|\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{r}}{r}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}q^{rj}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{(q^{j}t)^{r}}{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(-\log(1-q^{j}t)\right)=-\log\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n}(1-q^{j}t)\right).

Exponentiating both sides yields the result.
∎

The zeta function of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is the canonical example of a zeta function for a smooth, projective variety and its properties perfectly illustrate the Weil Conjectures. Its key properties are:

(i)

Rationality:

Z(\mathbb{P}^{n},t)
is a rational function of
t
, as shown in the proposition.

(ii)

Poles and Zeros:
The function has no zeros. It possesses simple poles at
t=1,q^{-1},\dots,q^{-n}
.

(iii)

Functional Equation:
It satisfies the functional equation
Z(\mathbb{P}^{n},1/(q^{n}t))=\epsilon\cdot q^{\delta}\cdot t^{\chi}\cdot Z(\mathbb{P}^{n},t)
, where
n=\dim(\mathbb{P}^{n})
, the Euler characteristic is
\chi=n+1
, the sign is
\epsilon=(-1)^{n+1}
, and the exponent is
\delta=n(n+1)/2
.

(iv)

Riemann Hypothesis:
The zeta function can be written in the form
Z(\mathbb{P}^{n},t)=\prod_{i=0}^{2n}P_{i}(t)^{(-1)^{i+1}}
, where the polynomials
P_{i}(t)
correspond to the
i
-th étale cohomology group. For
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, these are
P_{2j}(t)=(1-q^{j}t)
for
j=0,\dots,n
, and
P_{i}(t)=1
for all odd
i
. The reciprocals of the roots of
P_{2j}(t)
are
\alpha=q^{j}
. These satisfy the ”Riemann Hypothesis” portion of the conjectures, as their absolute value is
|\alpha|=q^{j}=q^{(2j)/2}
.

This simple, well-behaved structure serves as a crucial point of comparison for the more complex weighted case.

3.2.
Rationality of the Zeta Function for
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}

We now extend this analysis to
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
. While
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is generally singular, its zeta function retains the property of rationality.

Theorem 3.2
.

The zeta function
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
of a weighted projective space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a rational function of
t
.

Proof.

To prove that
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
is rational, it suffices to show that
\log Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
is a finite
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of logarithms of polynomials in
t
. Fix a non-empty subset
S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n\}
and write
m:=|S|-1
. For brevity set
A_{r}(S):=(q^{r}-1)^{m}\,\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q^{r}-1)
. Using the binomial theorem we obtain

A_{r}(S)=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(-1)^{m-j}q^{rj}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid k_{S}\\
d\mid(q^{r}-1)\end{subarray}}\phi(d)\right).

For each divisor
d\mid k_{S}
, the condition
d\mid(q^{r}-1)
is equivalent to
q^{r}\equiv 1\pmod{d}
, i.e., to
r
being a multiple of the multiplicative order of
q
modulo
d
. Denote this order by
o_{d}:=\ord_{d}(q)
. Thus
d\mid(q^{r}-1)
if and only if
o_{d}\mid r
. Expanding the product above and summing over all
S
, we can write

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\sum_{S,j,d}C_{S,j,d}\,q^{rj}\,\mathbf{1}(o_{d}\mid r),

where
\mathbf{1}(o_{d}\mid r)
is the indicator function of the divisibility condition
o_{d}\mid r
, and

C_{S,j,d}=\binom{|S|-1}{j}(-1)^{|S|-1-j}\phi(d),

with
S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n\}
non-empty,
0\leq j\leq|S|-1
, and
d\mid k_{S}
. Note that for fixed
n
there are only finitely many such triples
(S,j,d)
. Substituting this expression into the definition of
\log Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
and rearranging the (finite) sum over
(S,j,d)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\log Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}=\sum_{S,j,d}C_{S,j,d}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{rj}t^{r}}{r}\,\mathbf{1}(o_{d}\mid r).

For a fixed triple
(S,j,d)
, the inner sum runs over those
r
that are multiples of
o_{d}
. Writing
r=m\,o_{d}
with
m\geq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{rj}t^{r}}{r}\,\mathbf{1}(o_{d}\mid r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{mo_{d}j}t^{mo_{d}}}{mo_{d}}=\frac{1}{o_{d}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\bigl((q^{j}t)^{o_{d}}\bigr)^{m}}{m}=-\frac{1}{o_{d}}\log\bigl(1-(q^{j}t)^{o_{d}}\bigr).

Therefore

\log Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=-\sum_{S,j,d}\frac{C_{S,j,d}}{o_{d}}\log\bigl(1-(q^{j}t)^{o_{d}}\bigr).

It remains to check that the coefficients
C_{S,j,d}/o_{d}
are in fact integers. Since
q
has multiplicative order
o_{d}
modulo
d
in the group
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, we have
o_{d}\mid\phi(d)
, and hence
\frac{\phi(d)}{o_{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Because
C_{S,j,d}=\binom{|S|-1}{j}(-1)^{|S|-1-j}\phi(d)
, it follows that

\frac{C_{S,j,d}}{o_{d}}=\binom{|S|-1}{j}(-1)^{|S|-1-j}\,\frac{\phi(d)}{o_{d}}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Consequently, we can write

\log Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\sum_{S,j,d}a_{S,j,d}\,\log\bigl(1-(q^{j}t)^{o_{d}}\bigr),\quad a_{S,j,d}\in\mathbb{Z},

a finite
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of logarithms of polynomials in
t
. Exponentiating, we obtain

Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\prod_{S,j,d}\bigl(1-(q^{j}t)^{o_{d}}\bigr)^{a_{S,j,d}},

a finite product of rational functions. Hence
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
is itself a rational function of
t
, as claimed.
∎

3.3.
Decomposition of the Zeta Function

A key insight into the structure of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
is provided by the natural stratification of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
into its smooth (or weak) locus
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
and its singular locus
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
, given by the disjoint union

\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}=\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}\sqcup Sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
.
As the zeta function is multiplicative over disjoint unions, this geometric decomposition implies a direct arithmetic one. In order to see this, we provide the following propositions on the zeta functions of the

\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
as follows.

Proposition 3.3
.

The zeta function of the singular locus
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
is the rational function given by the finite product

(9)

Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,j,d^{\prime}\\
k_{S}>d\end{subarray}}(1-(tq^{j})^{o_{d^{\prime}}})^{-C_{S,j,d^{\prime}}/o_{d^{\prime}}},

where
d=\operatorname{gcd}(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
, and the product is taken over all triplets
(S,j,d^{\prime})
such that
S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n\}
is non-empty with
k_{S}=\operatorname{gcd}(\{w_{i}\}_{i\in S})>d
,
d^{\prime}|k_{S}
, and
0\leq j\leq|S|-1
. The terms are defined as
o_{d^{\prime}}=\ord_{d^{\prime}}(q)
and

C_{S,j,d^{\prime}}=\binom{|S|-1}{j}(-1)^{|S|-1-j}\phi(d^{\prime}).

Proof.

The zeta function for the singular locus is

Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t)=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right).

From Theorem
2.10
, the point count is

|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}>d\end{subarray}}(q^{r}-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q^{r}-1).

This sum is identical in its algebraic form to that of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, merely restricted to subsets
S
where
k_{S}>d
. The same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
applies directly, yielding a finite product of rational functions with the sum (and thus the product) restricted to the indices where
k_{S}>d
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

The zeta function of the non-singular locus
\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is the rational function given by the finite product

(10)

Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,j,d^{\prime}\\
k_{S}=d\end{subarray}}(1-(tq^{j})^{o_{d^{\prime}}})^{-C_{S,j,d^{\prime}}/o_{d^{\prime}}},

where
d=\operatorname{gcd}(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
, and the product is taken over all triplets
(S,j,d^{\prime})
such that
S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n\}
is non-empty with
k_{S}=\operatorname{gcd}(\{w_{i}\}_{i\in S})=d
,
d^{\prime}|k_{S}
, and
0\leq j\leq|S|-1
. The terms
o_{d^{\prime}}
and
C_{S,j,d^{\prime}}
are defined as in Proposition 4.4.

Proof.

Similarly, the zeta function for the non-singular locus is defined by the point count from Theorem 2.20:

|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}=d\end{subarray}}(q^{r}-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q^{r}-1).

This sum is restricted to subsets
S
where
k_{S}=d
. The proof of rationality follows identically from that of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

We now demonstrate that the zeta function
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
admits a canonical multiplicative decomposition reflecting the stratification of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
into its smooth locus
\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
and singular locus
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
.

Theorem 3.5
.

The zeta function of the weighted projective space decomposes as

Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)\cdot Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t).

Proof.

By definition, the zeta function is

Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right).

Since
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}=\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}\sqcup\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
as sets, we have

|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|+|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|.

Thus,

\begin{split}\log Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)&=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\\
&=\log Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)+\log Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t).\end{split}

Exponentiating yields the desired decomposition.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

This decomposition aligns with the orbifold structure of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is smooth and
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
captures the quotient singularities. Notably,
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
is not invariant under weight normalization, as point counts differ (cf.
2.7
).

3.4.
Weil Conjectures for
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}

Although
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is singular, aspects of the Weil conjectures hold for its zeta function.

Proposition 3.6
.

The reciprocals of the roots and poles of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
satisfy
|\alpha|=q^{k/2}
for integers
k
, verifying the Riemann hypothesis part of the Weil conjectures.

Proof.

From the proof of
3.2
, the poles and zeros arise from terms
1-(q^{j}t)^{o_{d}}=0
, so
t=q^{-j}\zeta
, where
\zeta^{o_{d}}=1
. The reciprocal roots/poles are
\alpha=q^{j}/\zeta
, with
|\alpha|=q^{j}=q^{(2j)/2}
, satisfying the condition for degree
2j
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Due to singularities,
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
does not satisfy the standard functional equation. For example, with
\mathbf{w}=(1,2)
, explicit computation shows failure of the equation.

3.5.
Decomposition over the Singular Stratification

A key insight into the structure of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
is provided by the natural stratification of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
into its smooth (or weak) locus
\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
and its singular locus
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
, given by the disjoint union
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}=\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}\sqcup\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
. As the zeta function is multiplicative over disjoint unions, this geometric decomposition implies a direct arithmetic one.

Proposition 3.7
.

The zeta function of the singular locus
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
is the rational function given by the finite product

Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,j,d^{\prime}\\
k_{S}>d\end{subarray}}(1-(tq^{j})^{o_{d^{\prime}}})^{-C_{S,j,d^{\prime}}/o_{d^{\prime}}},

where
d=\operatorname{gcd}(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
, and the product is taken over all triplets
(S,j,d^{\prime})
such that
S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n\}
is non-empty with
k_{S}=\operatorname{gcd}(\{w_{i}\}_{i\in S})>d
,
d^{\prime}|k_{S}
, and
0\leq j\leq|S|-1
. The terms are defined as
o_{d^{\prime}}=\ord_{d^{\prime}}(q)
and
C_{S,j,d^{\prime}}=\binom{|S|-1}{j}(-1)^{|S|-1-j}\phi(d^{\prime})
.

Proof.

The zeta function for the singular locus is

Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t)=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right).

From
2.11
, the point count is

|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}>d\end{subarray}}(q^{r}-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q^{r}-1).

This sum is identical in its algebraic form to that of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, merely restricted to subsets
S
where
k_{S}>d
. The same argument as in the proof of
3.2
applies directly, yielding a finite product of rational functions with the sum (and thus the product) restricted to the indices where
k_{S}>d
.
∎

The complement of the singular part
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is known as the Week Weighted projective space and denoted by
\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
. The following result determine the zeta function of
\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

The zeta function of the non-singular locus
\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is the rational function given by the finite product

Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}S,j,d^{\prime}\\
k_{S}=d\end{subarray}}(1-(tq^{j})^{o_{d^{\prime}}})^{-C_{S,j,d^{\prime}}/o_{d^{\prime}}},

where
d=\operatorname{gcd}(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
, and the product is taken over all triplets
(S,j,d^{\prime})
such that
S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n\}
is non-empty with
k_{S}=\operatorname{gcd}(\{w_{i}\}_{i\in S})=d
,
d^{\prime}|k_{S}
, and
0\leq j\leq|S|-1
.
The terms are defined as
o_{d^{\prime}}=\ord_{d^{\prime}}(q)
and
C_{S,j,d^{\prime}}=\binom{|S|-1}{j}(-1)^{|S|-1-j}\phi(d^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Similarly, the zeta function for the non-singular locus is defined by the point count from
2.10
:

|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}=d\end{subarray}}(q^{r}-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q^{r}-1).

This sum is restricted to subsets
S
where
k_{S}=d
. The proof of rationality follows identically from that of
3.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.9

(Multiplicative Decomposition)
.

The zeta function of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
decomposes multiplicatively over its stratification:

Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)\cdot Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t).

Proof.

The proof follows immediately from the definitions and the disjoint union
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}=\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}\sqcup\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
.

\displaystyle Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)\cdot Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|+|\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right)=Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t).

This is also seen by combining the rational product representations from
3.7
and
3.8
. The conditions
k_{S}=d
and
k_{S}>d
are disjoint and their union covers all non-empty subsets
S
, thus reconstructing the full product for
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
derived in
3.2
.
∎

3.6.
Poles and Zeros of the Zeta Function
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)

The explicit product formula derived in the proof of
3.2

permits a detailed analysis of the poles and zeros of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
. The function is a finite product of factors of the form

F_{S,j,d}(t)=\left(1-(tq^{j})^{o_{d}}\right)^{c(S,j,d)},\quad\text{where}\quad c(S,j,d)=-C_{S,j,d}/o_{d}.

The locations of the poles and zeros are determined by the roots of the polynomial factors
P_{S,j,d}(t)=1-(tq^{j})^{o_{d}}
. The roots satisfy
t^{o_{d}}=q^{-jo_{d}}
, which yields
t=\zeta q^{-j}
, where
\zeta
is any
o_{d}
-th root of unity.
The character of each factor (whether it contributes poles or zeros) depends on the sign of its integer exponent
c(S,j,d)
. Recalling
C_{S,j,d}=\binom{|S|-1}{j}(-1)^{|S|-1-j}\phi(d)
, and noting that
\phi(d)\geq 1
and
o_{d}\geq 1
, the sign of
c(S,j,d)=-C_{S,j,d}/o_{d}
is determined by
-(-1)^{|S|-1-j}=(-1)^{|S|-j}
. Specifically:

(i)

A factor contributes poles at
t=\zeta q^{-j}
if its exponent
c(S,j,d)
is negative. This occurs when
C_{S,j,d}>0
, which requires
|S|-1-j
to be an even integer.

(ii)

A factor contributes zeros at
t=\zeta q^{-j}
if its exponent
c(S,j,d)
is positive. This occurs when
C_{S,j,d}<0
, which requires
|S|-1-j
to be an odd integer.

The total order of the pole or zero at a specific value
t_{0}=\zeta_{0}q^{-j_{0}}
is obtained by summing the exponents
c(S,j,d)
over all triplets
(S,j,d)
such that
j=j_{0}
and
\zeta_{0}
is an
o_{d}
-th root of unity.
The reciprocals of the roots are of the form
\alpha=q^{j}/\zeta
. Their complex absolute values satisfy
|\alpha|=q^{j}
. This aligns with the Weil Conjectures, specifically the Riemann Hypothesis, which posits that the reciprocals of the roots of the characteristic polynomials
P_{i}(t)
associated with the
i
-th
ét
cohomology group have absolute value
q^{i/2}
. The appearance of
|\alpha|=q^{j}
suggests these factors arise from the even-dimensional cohomology groups
H^{2j}
. Unlike the standard projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, where only poles appear (corresponding to factors
(1-q^{j}t)^{-1}
), the weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
generally exhibits both poles and zeros due to the alternating signs originating from the binomial expansion used in the point-counting formula. This more complex structure reflects the orbifold nature of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
.

3.7.
Riemann Hypothesis for
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)

This conjecture concerns the absolute values of the reciprocals of the roots (contributing to zeros) and poles of the zeta function. The proof of
3.2

showed that the roots and poles occur at
t=\zeta q^{-j}
, where
\zeta
is a root of unity. The reciprocals are
\alpha=q^{j}/\zeta
. Their complex absolute values are
|\alpha|=q^{j}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

The zeta function
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
satisfies the Riemann Hypothesis. Specifically, the reciprocals
\alpha
of all roots and poles of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
are algebraic integers satisfying
|\alpha|=q^{j}
for some integer
j
with
0\leq j\leq n
.

Proof.

As established in the analysis preceding this theorem (derived from the proof of
3.2
), the roots and poles of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
occur at values
t=\zeta q^{-j}
, where
\zeta
is a root of unity (specifically, an
o_{d}
-th root for some
d|k_{S}
) and
j
is an integer with
0\leq j\leq|S|-1\leq n
.
The reciprocals of these values are
\alpha=1/t=\zeta^{-1}q^{j}
.
Since
\zeta
is a root of unity, its inverse
\zeta^{-1}
is also a root of unity (an algebraic integer). The value
\alpha=q^{j}/\zeta
is thus an algebraic integer.
The complex absolute value of
\alpha
is

|\alpha|=|\zeta^{-1}q^{j}|=|\zeta^{-1}|\cdot|q^{j}|=1\cdot q^{j}=q^{j}.

This precisely matches the Weil magnitude condition
|\alpha|=q^{i/2}
for
i=2j
.
∎

Therefore, the zeta function
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
satisfies the Riemann Hypothesis component of the Weil Conjectures, in the sense that its poles and zeros have the predicted absolute values.

3.8.
On the Functional Equation for
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)

A hallmark of smooth, projective varieties is that their zeta functions satisfy a functional equation relating
Z(X,t)
to
Z(X,1/(q^{\dim X}t))
.
In other words, when
X
is smooth and has a well-defined Hodge structure (e.g., when
X
lifts to characteristic zero), we expect a functional equation of the form:

Z\left(X,\frac{1}{q^{\dim X}t}\right)=\epsilon q^{\delta}t^{\chi}Z(X,t),

where
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
,
\chi
is the Euler characteristic, and
\delta
is related to the Betti numbers.
We now investigate this equation against
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, which is inherently singular.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\mathbf{w}\neq(1,\cdots,1)
be a vector of weights. Then,
the zeta function of a weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) does not, in general, satisfy the standard functional equation for a smooth
n
-dimensional variety.

Proof.

The standard functional equation for the zeta function of a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

Z\left(X,\frac{1}{q^{n}t}\right)=\epsilon\,q^{n\chi/2}\,t^{\chi}\,Z(X,t),

where
\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
\chi
is the topological Euler characteristic of
X
. For the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
\chi=n+1
.

To show that this equation does not hold for
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
when
\mathbf{w}\neq(1,\dots,1)
, we first compute the degree of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
as a rational function. The degree is defined as
\deg(\text{numerator})-\deg(\text{denominator})
.

From the product representation in
3.2
,

Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\prod_{S,j,d}\left(1-(q^{j}t)^{o_{d}}\right)^{a_{S,j,d}},\quad a_{S,j,d}=-\frac{C_{S,j,d}}{o_{d}}.

Each factor
1-(q^{j}t)^{o_{d}}
is a polynomial of degree
o_{d}
in
t
. If
a_{S,j,d}>0
, it contributes
+a_{S,j,d}o_{d}
to the degree (numerator); if
a_{S,j,d}<0
, it contributes
-|a_{S,j,d}|o_{d}=a_{S,j,d}o_{d}
(denominator). Thus, the total degree is

\deg Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\sum_{S,j,d}a_{S,j,d}\,o_{d}=\sum_{S,j,d}-C_{S,j,d}.

Now,

\sum_{j,d}C_{S,j,d}=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(-1)^{m-j}\right)\left(\sum_{d\mid k_{S}}\phi(d)\right)=(1-1)^{m}\,k_{S}=\delta_{m,0}\,k_{S},

since
\sum_{d\mid k_{S}}\phi(d)=k_{S}
. Here,
m=|S|-1=0
precisely when
|S|=1
. Summing over
S
,

\sum_{S,j,d}C_{S,j,d}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}w_{i}.

Therefore,

\deg Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=-\sum_{i=0}^{n}w_{i}.

For the functional equation to hold, asymptotic analysis (as
t\to 0
) requires
\chi=-\deg Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
for consistency with the power of
t
on both sides.

However,
-\deg Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\sum w_{i}
. When
\mathbf{w}\neq(1,\dots,1)
,
\sum w_{i}>n+1
, which differs from the Euler characteristic
n+1
of the smooth
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Thus,
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
cannot satisfy the same functional equation as that of a smooth
n
-dimensional variety like
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

This mismatch is further evidenced by the decomposition
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)\cdot Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t)
. The singular locus
\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n})
has dimension less than
n
, so its zeta function exhibits poles and zeros incompatible with an
n
-dimensional transformation. Explicitly, substituting
t\mapsto 1/(q^{n}t)
into a factor
(1-(tq^{j})^{o_{d^{\prime}}})
yields

1-q^{(j-n)o_{d^{\prime}}}t^{-o_{d^{\prime}}}=\frac{t^{o_{d^{\prime}}}-q^{(j-n)o_{d^{\prime}}}}{t^{o_{d^{\prime}}}},

a rational function whose form does not match the original cyclotomic structure unless
o_{d^{\prime}}=1
for all terms, which fails in general.

For the smooth locus
\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
, the point counts involve periodic
\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S},q^{r}-1)
terms (with
k_{S}=d>1
possible), leading to zeta factors with
o_{d}>1
, again incompatible with the simple pole structure of smooth varieties.

An exception occurs when
d=1
and conditions in
2.11
hold, where
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})
, but singularities persist unless all
w_{i}=1
. In general, the equation fails.
∎

3.9.
Behavior of
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
under Weight Normalization

We conclude this section by examining the behavior of the zeta function under weight normalization. This investigation reveals that the zeta function is not an invariant of the abstract variety but rather depends fundamentally on the specific weighted embedding.

Let
\mathbf{w}=(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})
with
d=\operatorname{gcd}(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})>1
, and let
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=\mathbf{w}/d
be the normalized weight vector. Let
N_{r}=|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|
and
N_{r}^{\prime}=|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|
. Applying
2.7
to the field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
, we have the relation
N_{r}=d_{r}^{\prime}\cdot N_{r}^{\prime}+E_{r}
,
where
d_{r}^{\prime}=\operatorname{gcd}(d,q^{r}-1)
and
E_{r}
is the error term:

E_{r}=(d_{r}^{\prime}-1)-\frac{d_{r}^{\prime}}{q^{r}-1}\sum_{\mu\in G_{r}\setminus H_{r}}q^{rN^{\prime}(\mu)}.

This relationship directly translates to the zeta functions.

Theorem 3.12
.

The zeta function of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
is related to the zeta function of its normalization
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}
by:

Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=Z_{\mathrm{err}}(t)\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{gcd}(d,q^{r}-1)\cdot N_{r}^{\prime}}{r}t^{r}\right),

where
Z_{\mathrm{err}}(t)=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{E_{r}}{r}t^{r}\right)
.

Proof.

By definition,
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{N_{r}}{r}t^{r}\right)
. Substituting the expression for
N_{r}
yields:

\displaystyle Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{r}^{\prime}\cdot N_{r}^{\prime}+E_{r}}{r}t^{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{r}^{\prime}\cdot N_{r}^{\prime}}{r}t^{r}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{E_{r}}{r}t^{r}\right).

The result follows by identifying
N_{r}^{\prime}=|\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|
and
d_{r}^{\prime}=\operatorname{gcd}(d,q^{r}-1)
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

This theorem demonstrates that the zeta function is not an invariant of the isomorphism class
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}\cong\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}
. The factor
d_{r}^{\prime}=\operatorname{gcd}(d,q^{r}-1)
depends on
r
. Even if
d^{\prime}=\operatorname{gcd}(d,q-1)=1
(as in
[
8
]
),

d_{r}^{\prime}
may exceed 1 for
r>1
. This causes
N_{r}\neq N_{r}^{\prime}
in general, and consequently
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)\neq Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n},t)
.
This is consistent with the entropy invariants introduced in
[
8
]
, which are also shown to be non-invariant under this normalization.

Example 3.5
.

For the weighted projective space
X=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{3}
with weights
\mathbf{w}=(1,2,3,5)
, the zeta function has the rational form

Z(X,t)=\frac{P(t)}{(1-t)(1-qt)(1-q^{2}t)(1-q^{3}t)},

where
P(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
is a polynomial whose roots are roots of unity.

By Prop.
3.8
,
Z(X,t)
is derived from the sum over subsets
S
where
k_{S}=1
. For
\mathbf{w}=(1,2,3,5)
, the point count for this non-singular part is

|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=1+q^{r}+q^{2r}+q^{3r}-3.

Applying the method analogous to the proof of
3.2
to this specific point count yields the zeta function

Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{3},t)=\frac{(1-t)^{2}}{(1-qt)(1-q^{2}t)(1-q^{3}t)}.

By Prop.
3.7
,
Z(\sing(X),t)
is derived from the sum over subsets
S
where
k_{S}>1
. For this specific weight vector, the singular point count is known
(cf.
2.11
) to be:

|\sing(X)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\sum_{p\in\{2,3,5\}}\operatorname{gcd}(p,q^{r}-1).

By the multiplicative property,
Z(\sing(X),t)=Z_{2}(t)Z_{3}(t)Z_{5}(t)
, where

Z_{p}(t)=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{gcd}(p,q^{r}-1)}{r}t^{r}\right).

The term
\operatorname{gcd}(p,q^{r}-1)
is
p
if
o_{p}\mid r
, where
o_{p}=\ord_{p}(q)
, and
1
otherwise. Thus,

\operatorname{gcd}(p,q^{r}-1)=1+(p-1)\mathbf{1}(o_{p}\mid r).

Following a derivation similar to the proof of
3.2
, this sum simplifies to a logarithm:

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{gcd}(p,q^{r}-1)}{r}t^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}t^{r}+(p-1)\sum_{r=1:o_{p}\mid r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}t^{r}

\displaystyle=-\log(1-t)+(p-1)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(t^{o_{p}})^{m}}{m\cdot o_{p}}

\displaystyle=-\log(1-t)-\frac{p-1}{o_{p}}\log(1-t^{o_{p}}).

Exponentiating this gives

Z_{p}(t)=\frac{1}{1-t}\cdot\left(1-t^{o_{p}}\right)^{-\frac{p-1}{o_{p}}}.

Since
p
is prime,
o_{p}\mid(p-1)
, so the exponent is a positive integer. Thus,
Z_{p}(t)
is rational with poles at roots of unity.

We define
P(t)
as

P(t):=\prod_{p\in\{2,3,5\}}\left(1-t^{o_{p}}\right)^{-\frac{p-1}{o_{p}}}.

Combining the two parts using
3.9
gives the final result:

Z(X,t)=Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{3},t)\cdot Z(\sing(X),t)=\frac{P(t)}{(1-t)(1-qt)(1-q^{2}t)(1-q^{3}t)}.

The non-invariance of zeta functions is starkly illustrated by comparing the zeta function for
\mathbf{w}=(2,4,6,10)
with its normalization
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(1,2,3,5)
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}
with weights
\mathbf{w}=(2,4,6,10)
and let
X^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}
be its normalization with weights
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(1,2,3,5)
. Their zeta functions are not equal,
Z(X,t)\neq Z(X^{\prime},t)
, and are composed of fundamentally different rational factors.

From
3.5
, for the normalized space
X^{\prime}
, we have:

Z(X^{\prime},t)=\frac{Z_{2}(t)Z_{3}(t)Z_{5}(t)}{(1-t)(1-qt)(1-q^{2}t)(1-q^{3}t)},

where
Z_{p}(t)=(1-t^{o_{p}})^{(p-1)/o_{p}}
and
o_{p}=\ord_{p}(q)
.

Now, for the original space
X
, the zeta function can be calculated by

Z(X,t)=Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)\cdot Z(\sing(X),t).

By
3.7
,
Z(\sing(X),t)
can be derived from subsets
S
where
k_{S}>d=2
.
From
2.11
, these subsets correspond to
k_{S}
values
4,6,10
.

|\sing(X)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\operatorname{gcd}(4,q^{r}-1)+\operatorname{gcd}(6,q^{r}-1)+\operatorname{gcd}(10,q^{r}-1).

Thus,
Z(\sing(X),t)=Z_{4}(t)\cdot Z_{6}(t)\cdot Z_{10}(t)
, where
Z_{k}(t)=\exp(\sum\frac{\gcd(k,q^{r}-1)}{r}t^{r})
. This product is structurally different from
Z(Sing(X^{\prime}),t)
.

By
3.8
,,
Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
can be derived from subsets
S
where
k_{S}=d=2
. Since
k_{S}(\mathbf{w})=2\cdot k_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})
, the condition
k_{S}(\mathbf{w})=2
is equivalent to
k_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})=1

\displaystyle|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}(\mathbf{w})=2\end{subarray}}(q^{r}-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(k_{S}(\mathbf{w}),q^{r}-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})=1\end{subarray}}(q^{r}-1)^{|S|-1}\cdot\operatorname{gcd}(2,q^{r}-1)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{gcd}(2,q^{r}-1)\cdot\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq S\subseteq T\\
k_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{\prime})=1\end{subarray}}(q^{r}-1)^{|S|-1}\right).

From the proof of
3.2
, the sum in the parentheses is exactly
|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=|\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|
. Therefore,

|\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|=\operatorname{gcd}(2,q^{r}-1)\cdot|\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|.

This gives the zeta function:

Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{gcd}(2,q^{r}-1)\cdot|\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right).

Therefore, the zeta functions for
X
and
X^{\prime}
are:

\displaystyle Z(X,t)

\displaystyle=\left[\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{gcd}(2,q^{r}-1)\cdot|\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right)\right]\cdot\left[Z_{4}(t)Z_{6}(t)Z_{10}(t)\right]

\displaystyle Z(X^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=\left[\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}})|}{r}t^{r}\right)\right]\cdot\left[Z_{2}(t)Z_{3}(t)Z_{5}(t)\right]

These are clearly not equal. The non-singular component
Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
is not
Z(\mathbb{P}^{3},t)
, but is instead weighted by the
d_{r}^{\prime}=\operatorname{gcd}(2,q^{r}-1)
factor.

The above examples explicitly demonstrates the non-invariance described in
3.12
and
3.4
.

4.
Concluding Remarks

This paper develops foundational methods for counting rational points in weighted projective spaces over finite fields. We establish the equivalence of three natural notions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points and derive enumeration formulas based on Burnside’s lemma and combinatorial stratification by coordinate supports and greatest common divisors. These formulas provide explicit expressions for point counts in both the smooth and singular loci, allowing for a decomposition of the zeta function

Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)=Z(\mathbb{WP}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)\cdot Z(\sing(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n}),t).

We verify that this decomposition respects rationality and satisfies the Riemann Hypothesis component of the Weil conjectures. However, the standard functional equation does not generally hold due to the presence of singularities, and the zeta function is not invariant under normalization of weights.
Our results give a framework for the arithmetic study of singular spaces, including closed-form expressions for point counts across various weightings and field sizes. The computational examples serve to illustrate the general behavior and validate the formulas.
This work is motivated in part by applications to graded quantum codes
[
10
]
, where point counts determine code parameters. It also generalizes prior results on the arithmetic of moduli spaces with split Jacobians
[
5
]
, extending zeta-theoretic techniques to higher-dimensional orbifold settings.

In a companion paper
[
8
]
, we extend these point-counting formulas to weighted hypersurfaces, proving the rationality of the zeta function
Z(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{w}}^{n},t)
via a finite product representation that aligns with the orbifold stratification. We introduce three novel weighted height functions—degree-based
H_{\mathbf{w},q}^{\deg}
, function field
H_{\mathbf{w},q}^{\mathrm{FF}}
, and probabilistic
H_{\mathbf{w},q}^{\text{Prob}}
—establishing properties such as finiteness, scaling invariance, and subadditivity. These heights facilitate the stratification of rational points, complemented by entropy invariants that quantify their distribution and sparsity, offering new insights into arithmetic complexity with implications for coding theory and post-quantum cryptography.

Possible directions for further work include extending the methods to weighted hypersurfaces and complete intersections, studying functional equations in normalized cases, and investigating the relation between point distributions and arithmetic invariants such as automorphism groups or stacky structures. These developments may contribute to a broader understanding of zeta functions in singular arithmetic geometry and their potential applications in computational settings.

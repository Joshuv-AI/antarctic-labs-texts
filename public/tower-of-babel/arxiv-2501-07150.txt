Two-loop gradient-flow renormalization of scalar QCD
Janosch Borgulat, Nils Felten, Robert V. Harlander, Jonas T. Kohnen

Abstract
The gradient-flow formalism is applied to a non-Abelian gauge theory with scalar and fermionic particles, dubbed “scalar QCD ( QCD )”. It is shown that the flowed scalar quark requires a field renormalization, albeit only beyond the one-loop level. A pseudo-physical, t t -dependent renormalization scheme is defined, and the corresponding renormalization constant is evaluated at the two-loop level. We also calculate the gluon action density as well as the condensate of the scalar and the fermionic quark at three-loop level in this theory. The results validate the consistency of the gradient-flow formalism in this theory and provide a further step towards applying the gradient-flow formalism to the full Standard Model.

Introduction

The gradient flow is an established tool in lattice gauge theory
calculations
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. It evolves the regular four-dimensional
fields into an auxiliary dimension, the flow time
t
, according to a
differential equation which is of first order in
t
. The effect of this
evolution is a suppression of the fields’ high-momentum modes, and thus a
regularization of the associated
UV
(
UV
) divergences.

Aside from an efficient method to determine the lattice
spacing
[
2
,
6
]
, or a non-perturbative definition of
the renormalized strong coupling
[
2
]
, the
GFF
(
GFF
) also
provides a way to facilitate the calculation and renormalization of operator
matrix elements on the lattice, as well as their combination with
perturbatively evaluated Wilson coefficients. A particularly welcome feature
of the
GFF
is that it evades complications due to the breaking of Poincaré
invariance on the lattice. This opens efficient ways to compute matrix
elements of the energy momentum tensor on the lattice, for
example
[
7
,
8
]
, and most recently higher moments
of parton densities
[
9
,
10
]
. A key to these
methods is the so-called
SFTX
(
SFTX
), which allows one to express composite
operators of the flowed fields in terms of linear combinations of the regular
operators. Proofs of the viability of this method have been provided in
Refs.
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
10
,
15
]
. It requires perturbative
matching coefficients between flowed and regular operators whose calculation
leads to non-standard Feynman diagrams and integrals. Nevertheless, the
standard methods for perturbative
QCD
calculations can be adapted to render
such calculations possible at
NNLO
(
NNLO
) and possibly even beyond
that
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

Understandably, the majority of such applications has been focused on
QCD
up
to now. However, it is worth to consider also other theories, be it for
theoretical reasons or due to their phenomenological relevance, for example in
combination with effective field theories. Generalizations of the
GFF
have
been considered in the context of super-Yang-Mills and other supersymmetric
theories
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
,
or scalar theories in three and four space-time
dimensions
[
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
, for example.

In this paper, we consider the extension of
QCD
by the inclusion of scalar
quarks. While the phenomenological relevance of the literal interpretation of
this theory is doubtful in the light of current
LHC
(
LHC
) data, our results will
be presented for a general non-Abelian compact gauge group with a scalar field
and an arbitrary number of fermionic matter fields transforming in the
corresponding irreducible representation. They can therefore be easily
interpreted in terms of the unbroken weak sector of the
SM
, for example, and
represent an important step towards a gradient-flow formulation of the full

SM
. Nevertheless, for the sake of convenience, we will refer to the
underlying theory as
SQCD
(
SQCD
) in this paper, to the gauge bosons as gluons, and
to the fermionic and scalar matter fields as quarks and squarks (or scalar
quarks), respectively.

While the generalization of the flow equations to
SQCD
is straightforward,
and the renormalization of the strong and the scalar coupling is the same as
in regular
SQCD
and can be found in the literature, we will for the first
time compute the flowed field renormalizations of the quarks and the squarks
in this theory, up to two-loop level. In addition to the
\overline{\mathrm{{MS}}}
scheme, they
will be presented in the so-called ringed scheme which is suitable also for a
lattice realization. As a first application, we will also determine the

SFTX
of the two squark bilinear operators, namely the scalar and the Noether
current.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. In the next section, we
define the flow equations for
SQCD
and provide the relevant Feynman rules.
Subsequently, the conceptual aspects for the renormalization of the strong
coupling and the flowed fields is discussed, and the relevant renormalization
constants are computed through
NNLO
in the strong and the scalar
coupling. As an application, we study the
SFTX
of the scalar bilinear
operator and the Noether current for the scalar quarks, computing the matching
coefficients to the corresponding regular operators through
NNLO
in

Section

4
. The resulting flowed anomalous dimensions of these operators
are discussed in
Section

5
, before we present an outlook and our
conclusions in
Section

6
. In
Appendix

A
, we collect the
explicit results for the
\beta
-functions which are relevant for this paper,
and in
Appendix

B
, we describe the contents of the ancillary file
which accompanies this paper, and which provides our main results in
electronic form.

2
Flow equations and Feynman rules

We consider a gauge theory with a single scalar
\phi
(squark) and

n_{\text{f}}
fermions
\psi_{f}
(quarks), all transforming in the fundamental
representation of the gauge group. Including the gauge-fixing terms and the
corresponding Faddeev-Popov ghosts
c
,
\bar{c}
, the Lagrangian reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{{\acs{SQCD}}}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\text{{\acs{QCD}}}}+\mathcal{L}_{\phi}\,,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{{\acs{QCD}}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}+\sum_{f=1}^{n_{f}}\bar{\psi}_{f}\not{D}^{F}\psi_{f}+\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\mu}^{a})^{2}+\partial_{\mu}\bar{c}^{a}D_{\mu}^{ab}c^{b}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=(D_{\mu}^{F}\phi)^{\dagger}(D_{\mu}^{F}\phi)-\frac{\lambda_{\text{{B}}}}{4}(\phi^{\dagger}\phi)^{2}\,.

Here,
A_{\mu}^{a}
are the gauge fields (gluons),

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{\mathrm{F}}=\partial_{\mu}+g_{\text{{B}}}A_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}\,,\qquad D_{\mu}^{ab}=\delta^{ab}\partial_{\mu}-g_{\text{{B}}}f^{abc}A_{\mu}^{c}\,,

(2.2)

are the covariant derivatives in the fundamental and the adjoint
representation, respectively, and

\displaystyle F^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A^{a}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{a}_{\mu}+g_{\text{{B}}}f^{abc}A^{b}_{\mu}A^{c}_{\nu}

(2.3)

is the field strength tensor. The
t^{a}
are the generators of the gauge group
in the fundamental representation, and the
f^{abc}
are the structure
constants. We follow the conventions of Refs.
[
4
,
18
]
,
which means that

\displaystyle=f^{abc}t^{c}\,,\qquad\mathrm{Tr}(t^{a}t^{b})=-T_{\text{R}}\,\delta^{ab}\,,

(2.4)

with the trace normalization
T_{\text{R}}
to be specified below. It is convenient to
define the combinations

\displaystyle a_{\text{s}}^{\text{{B}}}=\frac{g^{2}_{\text{{B}}}}{4\pi^{2}}\,,\qquad a_{\lambda}^{\text{{B}}}=\frac{\lambda_{\text{{B}}}}{4\pi^{2}}\,,

(2.5)

where
g_{\text{{B}}}
and
\lambda_{\text{{B}}}
denote the bare coupling constants of the
theory. Unless required, we will suppress the flavor indices
f
on the quark
fields in the following.

The gradient flow evolves the fields into an auxiliary dimension, the
so-called flow time
t
. The evolution is governed by the flow equation, which
is a first-order differential equation in
t
, and associated “initial
conditions”, which establish the contact to the regular fields at
t=0
. In
the case of the flowed gluon and quark fields
B_{\mu}
,
\chi
, and

\bar{\chi}
, the flow equations are given by

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}B_{\mu}^{a}-\mathcal{D}_{\nu}^{ab}G^{b}_{\mu\nu}-\kappa\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{ab}\partial_{\nu}B_{\nu}^{b}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{F}_{B}^{a}\,,

(2.6)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\chi-\Delta\chi+g_{\text{{B}}}\kappa\partial_{\mu}B_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}\chi

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{F}_{\chi}\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\bar{\chi}-\bar{\chi}\overleftarrow{\Delta}-g_{\text{{B}}}\kappa\bar{\chi}\partial_{\mu}B_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}

\displaystyle\equiv\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\chi}\,,

and the initial conditions are

\displaystyle B_{\mu}^{a}(t=0,x)

\displaystyle=A_{\mu}^{a}(x)\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\chi(t=0,x)

\displaystyle=\psi(x)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}(t=0,x)

\displaystyle=\bar{\psi}(x)\,.

The gauge parameter
\kappa
will be chosen equal to one in our calculations.
The flowed field-strength tensor is given by

\displaystyle G^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}B^{a}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}B^{a}_{\mu}+g_{\text{{B}}}f^{abc}B^{b}_{\mu}B^{c}_{\nu}\,,

(2.8)

and the covariant derivatives in the fundamental and adjoint representation
are defined as

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{\mathrm{F}}=\partial_{\mu}+g_{\text{{B}}}B_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}\,,\qquad\overleftarrow{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}^{\mathrm{F}}=\overleftarrow{\partial}_{\mu}-g_{\text{{B}}}B_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}\,,\qquad\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{ab}=\delta^{ab}\partial_{\mu}-g_{\text{{B}}}f^{abc}B_{\mu}^{c}\,.\end{split}

(2.9)

Furthermore, we introduced the short-hand notation

\begin{split}\Delta=\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{F}}_{\mu}\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{F}}_{\mu}\,,\qquad\overleftarrow{\Delta}=\overleftarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{\mathrm{F}}_{\mu}\overleftarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{\mathrm{F}}_{\mu}\,.\end{split}

(2.10)

For the flowed scalar fields
\varphi
,
\varphi^{\dagger}
, we employ the
following flow equations (see also
Refs.
[
32
,
35
]
):

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\varphi-\Delta\varphi+g_{\text{{B}}}\kappa\partial_{\mu}B_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}\varphi

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}\,,

(2.11)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\varphi^{\dagger}-\varphi^{\dagger}\overleftarrow{\Delta}-g_{\text{{B}}}\kappa\varphi^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}B_{\mu}^{a}t^{a}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}_{\varphi}\,,

with the boundary conditions

\displaystyle\varphi(t=0,x)

\displaystyle=\phi(x)\,,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\dagger}(t=0,x)

\displaystyle=\phi^{\dagger}(x)\,.

The flow equations can be implemented in the Lagrangian by introducing
Lagrange multiplier fields

,
\lambda_{f}
,

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{B}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\ \text{Tr}\Big[L_{\mu}^{a}\mathcal{F}_{B}^{a}\Big]\,,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,\Big[\overline{\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{\chi}+\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{\chi}\lambda\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\,\Big[\eta^{\dagger}\mathcal{F}_{\varphi}+\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}_{\varphi}\eta\Big]\,,

where summation over flavor indices is understood. The flowed Lagrangian then
reads

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{{\acs{SQCD}}}}+\mathcal{L}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{\chi}+\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}\,,

(2.14)

where the first term notes the regular (i.e., unflowed) part of the Lagrangian
given in

2.1
, and the remaining terms incorporate the flow

2.6

3
Renormalization constants

It is well known that the renormalization of the fundamental parameters of the
flowed Lagrangian is the same as for the regular
theory
[
4
]
. Since we assume the quarks and the squark to be
massless, the only fundamental parameters of our theory are the coupling
constants
a_{\text{s}}^{\text{{B}}}
and
a_{\lambda}^{\text{{B}}}
. Their renormalization in
SQCD
is
known through three-loop level
[
40
]
in the

\overline{\mathrm{{MS}}}
scheme, which, however, is only defined perturbatively. The
GFF

the other hand, allows for a definition of the strong coupling which can be
implemented both perturbatively and non-perturbatively:

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}^{\text{{GF}}}(t)}{\pi}\equiv a_{s}^{\text{{GF}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{E(t)}{E_{0}(t)}\,,\quad\text{with}\quad E_{0}(t)=\frac{3n_{\text{A}}}{32t^{2}}\,,

(3.1)

where

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{\text{{B}}}^{2}}{4}\langle G_{\mu\nu}^{a}(t)G_{\mu\nu}^{a}(t)\rangle

(3.2)

is the gluon action density, and
n_{\text{A}}
is the dimension of the adjoint
representation of the gauge group. The perturbative expression for
E(t)

within
QCD
, and thus the conversion between the
\overline{\mathrm{{MS}}}
and the
GF
scheme, is
currently known through
NNLO

[
16
]
and has been used for a
novel extraction of the
QCD
scale
\Lambda_{\text{{\acs{QCD}}}}
in the

\overline{\mathrm{{MS}}}
scheme
[
41
]
and the
\overline{\mathrm{{MS}}}
coupling

\alpha_{s}(M_{Z})

[
42
]
. In
Section

3.1
, we will generalize
this result to
SQCD
. A similar non-perturbative definition is also possible
for the scalar coupling
\alpha_{\lambda}
, of course, but it requires the
calculation of
\langle(\phi^{\dagger}\phi)^{2}\rangle
, which is beyond the scope
of the current paper.

Concerning the renormalization of the flowed fields, we write

\displaystyle B^{a,\text{{R}}}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=Z_{B}^{1/2}B_{\mu}^{a}\,,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\chi_{f}^{\text{{R}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{\chi}^{1/2}\chi_{f}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}_{f}^{\text{{R}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{\chi}^{1/2}\bar{\chi}_{f}\,,

\displaystyle\varphi^{\text{{R}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{\varphi}^{1/2}\varphi\,,

\displaystyle\varphi^{\dagger,\text{{R}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{\varphi}^{1/2}\varphi^{\dagger}\,.

It was shown in

that the flowed gluon does not require
renormalization
[
4
,
5
]
, and since the same

to all orders in perturbation

On the other hand, the flowed quark field turns out to require a non-trivial
renormalization

which can be implemented both on the lattice and in perturbation theory is the
so-called ringed scheme, defined as

\displaystyle\mathring{Z}_{\chi}\sum_{f=1}^{n_{\text{f}}}\langle\bar{\chi}_{f}(t)\not{\mathcal{D}}^{\mathrm{F}}\!\chi_{f}(t)\rangle=-\frac{2n_{\text{c}}n_{\text{f}}}{(4\pi t)^{2}}\,,

(3.4)

where

is the dimension of the fundamental representation of the gauge
group.
In
QCD
,
\mathring{Z}_{\chi}
is known through

[
5
,
18
]
. In this paper, we will generalize

The flowed squark renormalization

\displaystyle\mathring{Z}_{\varphi}\langle\varphi^{\dagger}(t)\varphi(t)\rangle\equiv\frac{n_{\text{c}}}{32\pi^{2}t}\,,

(3.5)

where the r.h.s. is defined such that
\mathring{Z}_{\varphi}=1+\text{higher
orders}
.

6
Conclusions and Outlook

We have presented the generalization of the
GFF
formalism to a gauge theory
with scalar particles. Aside from possible phenomenologial applications, for
example the Higgs sector of the
SM
, our results validate the consistency of
the formalism in this context. We show that, similar to the fermionic case,
the flowed scalar field requires renormalization, albeit only
beyond
NLO
. In addition to the
\overline{\mathrm{{MS}}}
scheme, we define a quasi-physical
scheme for the scalar quarks, defined at non-zero flow-time, and analogous to
the ringed scheme for fermions. It leads to renormalization group invariant
Green’s functions of the scalar field and can be implemented both
perturbatively and on the lattice. We evaluate the corresponding
renormalization constant through two loops.

Our results constitute an important step towards a gradient-flow description
of the full
SM
, which paves the way for novel perspectives on calculations
within
SMEFT
(
SMEFT
), for example.

Appendix A
Renormalization constants

The couplings are renormalized by

\displaystyle a_{\text{s}}^{\text{{B}}}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{\mu^{2}e^{\gamma_{\text{E}}}}{4\pi}\biggl)^{\epsilon}Z_{\text{s}}(a_{\text{s}},a_{\lambda})\,a_{\text{s}}\,,

(A.1)

\displaystyle a_{\lambda}^{\text{{B}}}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{\mu^{2}e^{\gamma_{\text{E}}}}{4\pi}\biggl)^{\epsilon}\Bigl[Z_{\text{s}\lambda}(a_{\text{s}})a_{\text{s}}+Z_{\lambda}(a_{\text{s}},a_{\lambda})a_{\lambda}\Bigr]\,,

where the dependence of the renormalized couplings
a_{\text{s}}
and
a_{\lambda}
on the
renormalization scale
\mu
has been suppressed. It is governed by the
renormalization group equations

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\frac{d}{d\mu^{2}}a_{\text{s}}

\displaystyle=\beta^{\text{s}}(a_{\text{s}},a_{\lambda})\equiv-\epsilon\,a_{\text{s}}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\beta^{\text{s}}_{i,n-i}a_{\text{s}}^{i}\,a_{\lambda}^{n-i}\,,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\frac{d}{d\mu^{2}}a_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\beta^{\lambda}(a_{\text{s}},a_{\lambda})\equiv-\epsilon\,a_{\lambda}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\beta^{\lambda}_{i,n-i}a_{\text{s}}^{i}\,a_{\lambda}^{n-i}\,.

The coefficients for the strong beta function through two-loop level,
i.e.
n=3
, are
[
64
]

\displaystyle\beta^{\text{s}}_{20}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{11}{3}C_{\text{A}}-\frac{4}{3}n_{\text{f}}T_{\text{R}}-\frac{1}{3}T_{\text{R}}\right)\,,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\beta^{\text{s}}_{30}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\left[\frac{34}{3}C_{\text{A}}^{2}-\left(4C_{\text{F}}+\frac{20}{3}C_{\text{A}}\right)T_{\text{R}}n_{\text{f}}-\left(4C_{\text{F}}+\frac{2}{3}C_{\text{A}}\right)T_{\text{R}}\right]\,,

\displaystyle\beta^{\text{s}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{otherwise,}

where
n_{\text{f}}
is the number of quark flavors, and we only take into account a
single scalar quark field, see
Eq.

2.1
. For the scalar coupling, the
coefficients through two-loop level are
[
65
,
61
]

\displaystyle\beta^{\lambda}_{20}

\displaystyle=-\frac{12C_{\text{F}}^{2}-3C_{\text{A}}C_{\text{F}}+12C_{\text{F}}T_{\text{R}}}{4\left(n_{\text{c}}+1\right)}\,,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\beta^{\lambda}_{11}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}C_{\text{F}}\,,\qquad\beta^{\lambda}_{02}=-\frac{1}{8}\left(n_{\text{c}}+1\right)-\frac{3}{8}\,,

\displaystyle\beta^{\lambda}_{ij}

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{otherwise.}

The renormalization constants in
Eq.

A.1
are then given by

\displaystyle Z_{\text{s}}

\displaystyle=1-a_{\text{s}}\frac{\beta^{\text{s}}_{20}}{\epsilon}-a_{\text{s}}^{2}\left(\frac{\left(\beta^{\text{s}}_{20}\right)^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{\beta^{\text{s}}_{30}}{2\epsilon}\right)\,,

(A.5)

\displaystyle Z_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=1-a_{\text{s}}\frac{\beta^{\lambda}_{11}}{\epsilon}-a_{\lambda}\frac{\beta^{\lambda}_{02}}{\epsilon}\,,\qquad Z_{\text{s}\lambda}=-a_{\text{s}}\frac{\beta^{\lambda}_{20}}{\epsilon}\,.

The anomalous dimension
\gamma
of a particular quantity is related to the

Z

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\frac{d}{d\mu^{2}}Z

\displaystyle=-\gamma\,Z\,.

(A.6)

We write it as

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{nm}a_{\text{s}}^{n}a_{\lambda}^{m}\,.

(A.7)

The generic form of an (gauge independent)

\overline{\mathrm{{MS}}}
-renormalization constant is then given by

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=1-a_{\text{s}}\frac{\gamma_{10}}{\epsilon}-a_{\lambda}\frac{\gamma_{01}}{\epsilon}+a_{\text{s}}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\Bigl[\gamma_{10}^{2}+\beta^{\lambda}_{20}\gamma_{01}+\beta^{\text{s}}_{20}\gamma_{10}\Bigr]-\frac{\gamma_{20}}{2\epsilon}\right)

(A.8)

\displaystyle+a_{\lambda}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\Bigl[\gamma_{01}^{2}+\beta^{\lambda}_{02}\gamma_{01}\Bigr]-\frac{\gamma_{02}}{2\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle+a_{\text{s}}a_{\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\Bigl[\gamma_{01}\gamma_{10}+\frac{\beta^{\lambda}_{11}\gamma_{01}}{2}\Bigr]-\frac{\gamma_{11}}{2\epsilon}\right)+\mathrm{h.o.}

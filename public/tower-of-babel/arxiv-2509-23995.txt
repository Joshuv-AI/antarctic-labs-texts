Mixed-Derivative Total Variation
Vincent Guillemet, and Michael Unser

Abstract
The formulation of norms on continuous-domain Banach spaces with exact pixel-based discretization is advantageous for solving inverse problems (IPs). In this paper, we investigate a new regularization that is a convex combination of a TV term and the ℳ ⁡ ( ℝ 2 ) \mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) norm of mixed derivatives. We show that the extreme points of the corresponding unit ball are indicator functions of polygons whose edges are aligned with either the x 1 x_{1} - or x 2 x_{2} -axis. We then apply this result to construct a new regularization for IPs, which can be discretized exactly by tensor products of first-order B-splines, or equivalently, pixels. Furthermore, we exactly discretize the loss of the denoising problem on its canonical pixel basis and prove that it admits a unique solution, which is also a solution to the underlying continuous-domain IP.



I
Introduction

The study of continuous-domain formulations of inverse problems (IP) is crucial in computational imaging
[
1
,
2
]
. In this setting, the objective is to recover an unknown image
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
from noisy measurements. Due to the pixel-based geometry of images, such problems are commonly addressed by seeking an approximate solution
f^{\star}
in

\mathcal{X}_{1}=\left\{f=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbf{a}[\mathbf{k}]\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\mathbf{k}}}\right\},

(1)

where the sets
(E_{\mathbf{k}})_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
form a pixel-based decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In several acquisition modalities, the forward measurement process is not naturally associated with a canonical pixel basis, in which case
\mathcal{X}_{1}
must be designed by the practitioner. In this context, it is advantageous to identify a continuous-domain Banach space
\mathcal{X}
that contains all pixel-type basis functions and within which
\mathcal{X}_{1}
can be refined. The search space
\mathcal{X}
is typically selected jointly with the regularization functional
\text{Reg}(\cdot)

[
3
,
4
]
, which encodes prior knowledge about the geometry of
f^{\star}
when the IP is ill-posed.

I-A

TV Regularization

A standard regularization for the pixel basis is the total variation (TV)
[
5
]
, which, for
f\in\mathcal{X}=\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)

[
6
,
7
]
and in its anisotropic form, is given by

\left\lVert\nabla\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}=\\
\left(\left\lVert[\mathrm{I}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{I}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right),

(2)

where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
denotes the norm on the space of bounded measures
[
8
]
.
This norm has the advantage of being exactly discretizable on the pixel basis, which makes it particularly suitable for image processing. Specifically, when
f\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
is a linear combination of square pixels with weights
\mathbf{a}
, (
2
) can be discretized as

c\left(\left\lVert\bm{h}_{0,1}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{h}_{1,0}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}\right),

(3)

where
c
is determined by the pixel size, and

\bm{h}_{0,1}=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
1\end{bmatrix},\quad\bm{h}_{1,0}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&1\end{bmatrix}.

(4)

The search for the optimal
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
thus reduces to a compressed sensing problem
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, which can be solved using classical algorithms such as FISTA
[
12
]
or primal-dual methods
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. One reason for the popularity of TV regularization is the strong theoretical foundation on which it is built.

The norm
\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}}
is not only well defined for pixels, but also for indicator functions of Caccioppoli sets
[
17
,
18
]
, i.e., sets that can be approximated in measure by polygons.
Within the framework of representer theorems (RTs) for Banach spaces
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
, Bredies and Carioni
[
23
]
built upon the results of
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
to show that TV regularization promotes solutions to inverse problems that are finite linear combinations of simple sets, Caccioppoli sets whose boundaries are represented by a unique Jordan curve
[
26
]
.

In contrast to the spline solutions promoted by the one-dimensional TV counterpart
\left\lVert\mathrm{D}\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})}
, the generality of these simple sets prevents them from benefiting from the convenient spline structure
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.

I-B

Mixed-Derivative Regularization

One approach to promoting pixel-type solutions with a spline structure is to impose additional regularity. In
[
32
]
, the authors considered the search space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}}(\mathrm{K})
, consisting of functions
f
whose generalized gradient
\nabla\{f\}
and generalized mixed derivative
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}
are bounded measures supported in
\mathrm{K}=[0,1]\times[0,1]
. They showed that the extreme points of the unit ball associated with the norm
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
are tensor products of
\mathrm{D}
-splines. However, this formulation is not entirely satisfactory because the regularization
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
does not control the perimeter of sets, but only their number of corners. As a result, it may promote solutions containing components that are undesirable, such as indicators of unbounded sets.

I-C

Contributions

In this paper, for
\theta\in]0,1]
, we regularize with the new norm

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}=\theta\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+(1-\theta)\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}},

(5)

which generalizes the anisotropic TV. Our contributions are fourfold.

Functional Framework:
We define the search space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
as the set of functions
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}}(\mathrm{K})
supported in
\mathrm{K}
. We prove that this space is a dual Banach space for the norm
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
, and we establish that all
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
-norms are equivalent.

Extreme Point Characterization:
We show that the extreme points of the unit ball of the convex cone of positive functions
\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K}),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}\right)
are indicator functions of polygons whose edges are parallel to either the
x_{1}
- or
x_{2}
-axis. We then apply this result to the regularization of abstract IPs with finite data.

Resolution of IPs:
We demonstrate that tensor-product B-splines of order 1 exactly discretize a broad class of IP loss functionals, and that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
can be discretized exactly as

\theta c\left\lVert\bm{h}_{0,1}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}+\theta c\left\lVert\bm{h}_{1,0}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}+(1-\theta)\left\lVert\bm{h}_{1,1}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}},

(6)

where
\bm{h}_{1,1}=\bm{h}_{0,1}\otimes\bm{h}_{1,0}
. We further prove that these exact discretizations converge, in an appropriate sense, to the continuous-domain IP as the pixel size tends to zero.

Application to Denoising:
We prove that the canonical pixel-based discretization of the denoising loss admits a unique solution, which also solves the corresponding continuous-domain problem. Finally, we numerically assess the efficiency of the proposed method.

I-D

Related Works

Higher-Order Generalization of TV.

Bredies et al. introduced the generalized total variation (GTV)
[
33
,
34
,
23
]
, which incorporates all derivatives up to a specified order
k
. In particular, the second-order TV (
k=2
) is defined on the space of functions with bounded Hessian
[
35
]
and, for a smooth function
f
, takes the form

TV^{(2)}\{f\}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\{f\}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{F}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},

(7)

where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{F}
denotes the Frobenius norm. Lefkimiatis et al. extended this notion by introducing the Hessian TV (HTV), in which the Frobenius norm is replaced by a Schatten-
p
norm
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
. Regularization with HTV has been employed for the learning of continuous piecewise-linear (CPWL) mappings
[
39
,
40
,
41
]
. Ambrosio et al. showed that the closure, in the unit ball of the space of functions with finite HTV, of the set of CPWL extreme points contains all extremal points
[
42
,
43
]
.

Our regularization lies, in terms of regularity constraints, between TV and HTV. Specifically, it enforces more regularity than TV due to the presence of the
\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}
term, but less than HTV because it omits the terms
\mathrm{I}\otimes\mathrm{D}^{2}
and
\mathrm{D}^{2}\otimes\mathrm{I}
. In particular, HTV is not well defined on pixel basis.

Extreme Points of Mixed Norms.

We investigate the extreme points of the unit ball associated with our regularization norm, which takes the form

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert=\theta\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{A}+(1-\theta)\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{B},

where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{A}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{B}
are two norms whose extreme points are known. When the search space
\mathcal{X}
can be decomposed as a direct sum
\mathcal{X}_{A}\oplus\mathcal{X}_{B}
, with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{A}|_{\mathcal{X}_{B}}=0
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{B}|_{\mathcal{X}_{A}}=0
, the extreme points of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
have been systematically characterized
[
44
]
. However, when such a direct-sum decomposition is not possible, as is the case in this work, the description of the extreme points becomes significantly more challenging. In finite dimensions, they are located at the intersections of the hyperplanes that define the boundaries of the unit balls
[
45
,
46
,
47
]
. To the best of our knowledge, no analogous characterization is currently available in infinite-dimensional settings.

Continuous Data.

TV was originally introduced as a regularization for the denoising loss
\left\lVert f-\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\Omega)}
, where
\Omega
is a domain and
f
denotes the continuous data
[
5
]
. Subsequent works have deepened the theoretical understanding of this approach. The existence and uniqueness of solutions for this problem, as well as for the TV flow problem, were established in
[
48
,
49
,
50
]
. The regularity of solutions was analysed in
[
51
]
, while discretization and convergence properties, including convergence rates, were investigated in
[
52
,
53
,
54
]
. Chambolle and Pock provide a comprehensive overview of TV discretization and convergence techniques in
[
55
]
. In the present work, we focus on the study of IPs with finite data.

II
Theoretical Formulation

II-A

Mixed-Derivative Native Space

Let
\mathrm{K}=[0,1]^{2}
denote the unit square. We define the native space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
as

\left\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\quad\begin{cases}[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K}),\\
\forall\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{K}^{c}),\ \langle f,\phi\rangle=0\end{cases}\right\},

(8)

where
\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})
denotes the space of bounded measures supported in
\mathrm{K}
, and
\mathrm{K}^{c}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathrm{K}
.
The second condition in (
8
) enforces that the function
f
vanishes outside
\mathrm{K}
. In Proposition
1
, we provide a more practical, integral-based characterization of the functions
f
belonging to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
. To this end, we introduce the integral transform
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{\cdot\}
, defined for all
m\in\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})
by

\displaystyle[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{m\}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{K}}u(\cdot-x_{1})\,u(\cdot-x_{2})\,\mathrm{d}m(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=(u\otimes u)\ast m,

(9)

where
u
is the heaviside function.

Proposition 1
.

A function
f
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
if and only if it belongs to

\left\{f\in[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})\}:\forall\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{K}^{c}),\ \langle f,\phi\rangle=0\right\}.

(10)

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Clearly, if
f
satisfies (
10
), then
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
. To prove the converse, assume
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
and define
m=[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}
. We claim that

[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{m\}=f

(11)

which proves the proposition. Observe that
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{m\}
may differ from
f
only by an element of the null space of
\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Hence, one can write

f=[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{m\}+\phi_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes\phi_{2},

(12)

for some
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})^{2}
. Note that

\displaystyle\text{supp}(m)\subset\mathrm{K}\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\text{supp}\left([\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{m\}\right)\subset[0,\infty[^{2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\text{supp}(\phi_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes\phi_{2})\subset[0,\infty[^{2},

(13)

which directly implies that
\phi_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes\phi_{2}=0
and proves the claim.
∎

Let
\theta\in]0,1]
, we equip
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
with the mixed norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
defined as

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}=\theta\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})}+(1-\theta)\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})^{2}},

(14)

with

\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})^{2}}=\left\lVert[\mathrm{I}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})}+\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{I}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})}.

(15)

In the sequel, we write
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
instead of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})}
and
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}
instead of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})^{2}}
. The key properties of
\left(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K}),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}\right)
are summarised in Theorem
1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\theta\in]0,1]
. Then, the vector space
\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K}),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}\big)
satisfies the following properties:

1.

All norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
are well-defined and equivalent; that is, for all
(\theta,\theta^{\prime})\in]0,1]^{2}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\forall f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K}):\quad\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\theta}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\theta^{\prime}}.

(16)

2.

It is a Banach space.

3.

Equipped with the norm
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
, it is the dual space of
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}/M_{0}
, where

M_{0}=\big\{v\in[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}:\\
\forall f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K}),\langle f,v\rangle=0\big\},

(17)

and
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}
is equipped with the norm

\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\phi\}\right\rVert=\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{\infty(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(18)

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Item 1.

Let
m=[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}
and
f=(u\otimes u)\ast m
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{I}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}=\left\lVert(u\otimes 1)\ast m\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\leq|\mathrm{K}|\left\lVert m\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\leq 2|\mathrm{K}|\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

(19)

where
|\mathrm{K}|=\int_{\mathrm{K}}1\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.
Consequently, (
16
) holds with

\displaystyle C=\text{max}\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta^{\prime}},\frac{1-\theta}{1-\theta^{\prime}}\right),

\displaystyle\quad\text{for}\quad\theta^{\prime}\in]0,1[

(20)

\displaystyle C=(\theta+(1-\theta)|\mathrm{K}|2),

\displaystyle\quad\text{for}\quad\theta^{\prime}=1.

(21)

Item 2.
We know from
[
32
]
that
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})\}
is a Banach space for the norm
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
is a closed subspace of
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}(\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K}))
, it is also Banach space for
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
. By the norm equivalence, it follows that it is a Banach space for
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
.

Item 3.
Here, all quotient spaces are equipped with their quotient norms. We know from
[
32
]
that
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})\}
equipped with
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
is the dual space of
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}/M
, with

M=\{v\in[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}\}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\\
\forall f\in[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})\},\langle f,v\rangle=0\}.

(22)

Since
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
is a Banach subspace of
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]^{-1}\{\mathcal{M}(\mathrm{K})\}
, the predual of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
is given by the (pre-)annihilator

\left([\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}/M\right)/M_{0}=[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}/M_{0}.

(23)

∎

The key takeaway of Theorem
1
is that all
\theta
-norms, for
\theta>0
, are topologically equivalent on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
. We have shown that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
also admits a
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
topology, such that
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-convergent to
f
in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
if and only if

\forall[v]\in[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}/M_{0}:\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle f_{n},[v]\rangle=\langle f,[v]\rangle.

(24)

Observe that, even though the quotient seems to complicate the predual structure, one still has that
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-convergent to
f
if and only if

\forall v\in[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}:\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle f_{n},v\rangle=\langle f,v\rangle,

(25)

because changing the representative in the quotient class, by definition, does not change the value of the brackets.

Next, we discuss the relation of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
with the space
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
of functions of bounded variation, which is naturally equipped with the norm
\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}
. We recall that, for a domain
\Omega
,

\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)=\{f\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(\Omega):\nabla\{f\}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)^{2}\}.

(26)

Proposition 2
.

For a domain
\Omega
such that
\mathrm{K}\subset\Omega
, the following holds:

1.

the functional
\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}
is a norm on
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
;

2.

the space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
is a subspace of
\mathrm{BV}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})\subset\mathrm{BV}(\Omega);

(27)

3.

the vector space
\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K}),\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}\big)
is not closed.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Item 1.
Absolute homogeneity, the triangle inequality, and non-negativity are straightforward. In addition,

\left\lVert\nabla\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}=0\Leftrightarrow f=c

(28)

for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
f
is compactly supported, one has that
c=0
.

Item 2.

It follows from Item 1 of Theorem
1
that
\nabla\{f\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{2}
. Because
\mathrm{supp}(\nabla\{f\})\subset\mathrm{K}\subset\Omega
, we find that
\nabla\{f\}\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega)^{2}
. The same argument applies to conclude that
f\in\mathcal{L}_{1}(\Omega)
.

Item 3.

Let
\mathrm{K}=[0,2]^{2}
and
\beta(\cdot)=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,1]}(\cdot)
. We define

f_{n+1}(\cdot)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\beta\left(\frac{\cdot}{2^{-k}}\right)\otimes\beta\left(\frac{\cdot-c_{k}}{2^{-k}}\right)

(29)

with
c_{k}=2\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)
. Then, a straightforward calculation shows that the sequence
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
f_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
converges in
\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}
to
f^{\infty}
, which is a staircase with an accumulation of steps towards the
x_{2}
axis. In addition, we calculate that

[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f^{\infty}\}=\\
\delta_{(0,0)}-\delta_{(1,0)}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{(2-c_{k},c_{k})}-\delta_{(2-c_{k+1},c_{k})},

(30)

which is an unbounded measure with
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f^{\infty}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}=\infty
. Consequently,
\big(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K}),\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}\big)
is not closed.
∎

II-B

Sets, Indecomposable Sets and Approximations

We study the geometrical properties of sets that satisfy
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{A}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}<\infty
. As a first step, we show that an indicator function
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
, as an element of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
, forms an equivalence class.

Proposition 3
.

Let
A\subset\mathrm{K}
be a measurable set. If a measurable set
A^{\prime}\subset\mathrm{K}
differs from
A
by a set of measure zero, then

\forall\theta\in]0,1],\quad\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A}-\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{\theta}=0.

(31)

In particular, if
\mathbbm{1}_{A}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
, then
\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
.

Proof of Proposition
3
.

The fact that
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
forms an equivalence class is often forgotten in the sequel as it is (often) clear from the context which representative to take. Proposition

Proposition 4
.

If
\mathbbm{1}_{A}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})

[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{A}\}
is a discrete measure with finitely many atoms. In addition, one has the representation

\mathbbm{1}_{A}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}u(\cdot-x_{1,k})u(\cdot-x_{2,k}),\quad\text{with}\quad a_{k}\in\{-1,1\}

(37)

and
(x_{1,k},x_{2,k})\in\mathrm{K}.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

Step 1.

We define

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\mathbf{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{K}}u(t_{1}-x_{1})u(t_{2}-x_{2})\mathrm{d}m(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=m([0,t_{1}]\times[0,t_{2}]).

(38)

Observe that in (
38
) the value of
\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\mathbf{t})
is set by measuring the closed square. Other choices, such as
m([0,t_{1}[\times[0,t_{2}[)
, would yield functions that fall in the same equivalence class as
\mathbbm{1}_{A}.

Step 2.

We assume by contradiction that there exists a set
E
of the form

\displaystyle E=]e_{1}^{-},e_{1}^{+}]\times]e_{2}^{-},e_{2}^{+}]\cap\mathrm{K},\quad\text{with}\quad(e_{1}^{+},e_{2}^{+})\in\mathrm{K},

(39)

such that
m(E)\notin\mathbb{Z}.
Observe that, for
E_{0,0}=[0,e_{1}^{-}]\times[0,e_{2}^{-}]\cap\mathrm{K},

E_{1,0}=]e_{1}^{-},e_{1}^{+}]\times[0,e_{2}^{-}]\cap\mathrm{K},
and
E_{0,1}=[0,e_{1}^{-}]\times]e_{2}^{-},e_{2}^{+}]\cap\mathrm{K}
, one has that

\displaystyle m(E+E_{0,0}+E_{1,0}+E_{0,1})=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(e_{1}^{+},e_{2}^{+})\in\{0,1\}.

(40)

Consequently, we either have that

•

m(E_{0,0})\notin\mathbb{Z},
in which case
m(E_{0,0})=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(e_{1}^{-},e_{2}^{-})\in\{0,1\}
, which is a contradiction;

•

m(E_{0,1})\notin\mathbb{Z},
in which case

–

if
m(E_{0,0})\notin\mathbb{Z}
, then we find a contradiction as before;

–

if
m(E_{0,0})\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
\mathbb{Z}\notowner m(E_{0,1}+E_{0,0})=\mathbbm{1}_{A}(e_{1}^{-},e_{2}^{+})\in\{0,1\}

which is a contradiction;

•

m(E_{1,0})\notin\mathbb{Z},
in which case we reach a contradiction as in the
m(E_{0,1})\notin\mathbb{Z}
case.

Further, one can prove that the measure

1.

is integer valued on intersections of sets of the form (
39
);

2.

is integer valued on unions of sets of the form (
39
);

3.

is integer valued on the relative complement of a set of the form (

).

It follows that

is integer-valued
on any Borel set
[

. This implies, with the boundedness of

, that
[

, Chapters 1-2]

m=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\delta_{(x_{1,k},x_{2,k})},\quad a_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

(41)

The fact that
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
is
\{0,1\}
-valued finally implies that

.

Indicator functions
\mathbbm{1}_{A}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
are not only useful for the indecomposable structure, but also for their approximation properties. We conclude this section with Lemma
1
, which states that any function
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
can be approximated, in an appropriate sense, by linear combinations of indicator functions. The proof is given in Appendix
-A
.

Lemma 1
.

For
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
, there exists a sequence
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of functions
f_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
, such that
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f_{n}\}
is a discrete measure supported on
\frac{1}{n}[1\cdots n]\times\frac{1}{n}[1\cdots n],
and such that

1.

it is
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-convergent to
f
in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})
;

2.

over the mixed derivative,

\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\geq\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f_{n}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

and

\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{lim}}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f_{n}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}};

3.

it is convergent to
f
in
\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mathrm{K})
;

4.

over the gradient,
\left\lVert\nabla\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{lim}}\left\lVert\nabla\{f_{n}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}
.

II-C

Coarea and Cocorner Formulas

In the sequel, we will use the (convex) cone
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K})
of positive functions, defined as

\big\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K}):\forall\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ \phi\geq 0\Rightarrow\langle f,\phi\rangle\geq 0\big\},

(52)

which is closed in both the strong and the
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
topologies.

We develop tools to split a function
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
into a sum of indicator functions on indecomposable sets. To this end, we first recall the coarea formula. For
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
s\in[0,\infty[
, we define the functions

P_{-}(f,s)=\left\lVert\nabla\{\min(f,s)\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}

(53)

and

P_{+}(f,s)=\left\lVert\nabla\{\max(f-s,0)\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}.

(54)

Theorem 2
.

The functions
P_{-}(f,\cdot)
and
P_{+}(f,\cdot)
are continuous, respectively increasing and decreasing, and satisfy the equalities

P_{-}(f,s)=\int_{0}^{s}\left\lVert\nabla\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha,

(55)

P_{+}(f,s)=\int_{s}^{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(56)

In addition, they satisfy the
BV
-coarea formula

\left\lVert\nabla\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}=P_{-}(f,s)+P_{+}(f,s)\\
=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert\nabla\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(57)

The coarea formula, for the
\ell_{2}
norm on the gradient, was proved for Lipschitz functions in
[
58
]
, for BV functions in
[
59
]
and was later extended for general norms (for example, see
[
60
]
). Equations (
55
) and (
56
) follow from the application of the coarea formula on
\text{min}(f,s)
and
\text{max}(f-s,0)
.

Next, we provide a new formula inspired by the coarea one. We define the functions
C_{-}(f,\cdot)
and
C_{+}(f,\cdot)
as

C_{-}(f,s)=\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathrm{min}(f,s)\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}},

(58)

C_{+}(f,s)=\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathrm{max}(f-s,0)\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}.

(59)

In Lemma
2
, we adapt Theorem
2
for the operator
\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}
and for piecewise constant functions.

Lemma 2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K})
be such that
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}
is a discrete measure with finitely many atoms. Then, the functions
C_{-}(f,\cdot)
and
C_{+}(f,\cdot)
are continuous, respectively increasing and decreasing, and satisfy the equalities

C_{-}(f,s)=\int_{0}^{s}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}\alpha,

(60)

C_{+}(f,s)=\int_{s}^{\infty}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(61)

In addition, they verify the cocorner formula

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}=

\displaystyle\,C_{-}(f,s)+C_{+}(f,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(62)

Proof of Lemma
2
.

We set
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}=m=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k}}
and define

\tilde{C}_{-}(f,s)=\int_{0}^{s}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(63)

Observe that
f
takes finitely many values
(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{M})
with
v_{m}\leq v_{m+1}
,
v_{0}=0
and
v_{M+1}=\infty
. Then, for
\alpha\in]v_{m},v_{m+1}[
one has that

\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}=\{x:f(x)>v_{m}\}.

(64)

For
s\in]v_{m^{\prime}},v_{m^{\prime}+1}[
with
m^{\prime}\in[0\cdots M-1]
, we calculate that

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{-}(f,s)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{m^{\prime}}(v_{m}-v_{m-1})\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)>v_{m-1}\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle+(s-v_{m^{\prime}})\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)>v_{m^{\prime}}\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}.

(65)

It follows directly from the representation (
II-C
) that
\tilde{C}_{-}(f,\cdot)
is increasing and continuous. Next, we observe that
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)>\alpha\}}\}
may only be non-zero on
\mathbf{x}_{k}
. In addition, for
\alpha<\alpha^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{x}_{k}
is in the support of both
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)>\alpha^{\prime}\}}\}
and
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)>\alpha\}}\}
if and only if it is a corner of

and

, in which case the Dirac mass centred at

must have an amplitude of the same sign because

\tilde{C}_{-}(f,s)=\Bigg\|[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\left\{\sum_{m=1}^{m^{\prime}}(v_{m}-v_{m-1})\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)>v_{m-1}\}}\right\}\\
+[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{(s-v_{m^{\prime}})\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)>v_{m^{\prime}}\}}\}\Bigg\|_{\mathcal{M}}

and, consequently, that

A similar argumentation would show that

is continuous, decreasing, and that

\displaystyle C_{+}(f,s)=\int_{s}^{\infty}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(67)

Finally,

\displaystyle C_{-}(f,s)+C_{+}(f,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{\{x:f(x)\geq\alpha\}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\mathrm{d}\alpha

\displaystyle=\underset{s\to\infty}{\text{lim}}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\text{min}(f,s)\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}.

(68)

In Lemma

for arbitrary functions in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K}).

Lemma 3
.

If

Proof of Lemma

.

Step 1.
Consider a sequence of functions

converging to

as in Lemma

. Our Claim 1 is that, up to a subsequence,

\underset{n\to\infty}{\text{lim}}C_{-}(f_{n},s)=C_{-}(f,s),

(70)

and

\underset{n\to\infty}{\text{lim}}C_{+}(f_{n},s)=C_{+}(f,s).

(71)

To prove this, we first recall that

\text{min}(f_{n},s)=\frac{f_{n}+s-|f_{n}-s|}{2}.

(72)

Item 2 of Lemma

implies that

and

to

and

. In particular,

\forall\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}([0,1])\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\infty}([0,1])\subset\mathcal{L}_{\infty}([0,1]^{2}):\\

Remark that

\mathrm{D}\{\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\}|_{[0,1]}\otimes\mathrm{D}\{\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\}|_{[0,1]}\subset\mathcal{L}_{\infty}([0,1])\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\infty}([0,1])

\displaystyle C_{-}(f_{n},s)+C_{+}(f_{n},s)

(78)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}.

(79)

Finally, if either (

) or (
77
) has a strict inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\text{min}(f,s)\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\text{max}(f-s,0)\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

(80)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\underset{n\to\infty}{\text{ liminf }}C_{-}(f_{n},s)+\underset{n\to\infty}{\text{liminf }}C_{+}(f_{n},s)

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}},

(82)

which is a contradiction. In (
80
) we used the fact that

f=\text{min}(f,s)+\text{max}(f-s,0),

(83)

and the triangular inequality. In (
82
) we used (
79
). Consequently, (
76
) and (
77
) hold with an equality and, up to a subsequence, (
70
) and (
71
) hold.

Step 2.
The function
C_{-}(f,\cdot)
is increasing. Indeed, Lemma
2
yields that,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\forall s_{1}\leq s_{2}
,

\displaystyle C_{-}(f_{n},s_{1})\leq C_{-}(f_{n},s_{2})\Rightarrow C_{-}(f,s_{1})\leq C_{-}(f,s_{2}),

(84)

where we used Claim 1 to pass to the limit. The claim that
C_{+}(f,\cdot)
is decreasing and (
69
) are proved likewise.

Step 3.
We now show that
C_{-}(f,\cdot)
and
C_{+}(f,\cdot)
are continuous. Let
(s_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence convergent to
s
. An argument similar to the one in Step 1 yields that (in parallel with (
76
) and (
77
))

\displaystyle C_{-}(f,s)

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{liminf}}\quad C_{-}(f,s_{n}),

\displaystyle C_{+}(f,s)

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{liminf}}\quad C_{+}(f,s_{n}),

(85)

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{liminf}}\quad C_{-}(f,s_{n})+C_{+}(f,s_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{limsup}}\quad C_{-}(f,s_{n})+C_{+}(f,s_{n})

(86)

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{limsup}}\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

(87)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}.

(88)

Equation (
87
) follows from (
69
), and the inequality in (
86
) cannot be strict. It follows that

\displaystyle C_{-}(f,s)

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{liminf}}\quad C_{-}(f,s_{n})=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{limsup}}\quad C_{-}(f,s_{n}),

\displaystyle C_{+}(f,s)

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{liminf}}\quad C_{+}(f,s_{n})=\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{limsup}}\quad C_{+}(f,s_{n}).

(89)

∎

II-D

Extreme Point Characterization

We are now in a position to state our main result, Theorem
3
.

Theorem 3
.

The extreme points of the unit ball in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K})
, equipped with
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
,
are exactly of the form
a\mathbbm{1}_{A}
with
a=\frac{1}{\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A}\right\rVert_{\theta}}
and,

•

if
\theta=1,
then
A\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}}
;

•

if
\theta\in]0,1[,
then
A\in\mathcal{E}_{\nabla}.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Step 1.
We show that an extreme point must be of the form
a\mathbbm{1}_{A}
.
Let
f
be an extreme point and consider the functions

F_{-}(s)=\theta C_{-}(f,s)+(1-\theta)P_{-}(f,s).

(90)

F_{+}(s)=\theta C_{+}(f,s)+(1-\theta)P_{+}(f,s).

(91)

We know that
F_{-}(\cdot)
and
F_{+}(\cdot)
are continuous and respectively increasing and decreasing.
In addition, they satisfy the equality

1=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\theta}=F_{-}(s)+F_{+}(s).

(92)

By continuity, there exists
s_{1}
such that
F_{-}(s_{1})=F_{+}(s_{1})=0.5
, with the decomposition

f=\frac{1}{2}\,2\text{min}(f,s_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\,2\text{max}(f-s_{1},0).

(93)

We find that

\left\lVert 2\text{min}(f,s_{1})\right\rVert_{\theta}=2F_{-}(f,s_{1})=1

(94)

and that

\left\lVert 2\text{max}(f-s_{1},0)\right\rVert_{\theta}=2F_{+}(f,s_{1})=1.

(95)

Since
f
is an extreme point, we must have that

2\text{max}(f-s_{1},0)=2\text{min}(f,s_{1}).

(96)

If
f\geq s_{1}
, then
f=2s_{1}
. If
f\leq s_{1}
, then
f=0.

These two equations imply that
f
has to be of the form
a\mathbbm{1}_{A}
with
a=\frac{1}{\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A}\right\rVert_{\theta}}.

Finally, if
\theta\in]0,1[,
we assume by contradiction that
E
is not
\nabla
-indecomposable. Then, it is not
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]
-indecomposable and there exist two sets
A_{1},A_{2}
, such that
E=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
,

\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{A}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}\\
=\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{A_{1}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}+\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathbbm{1}_{A_{2}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}},

(97)

and

\left\lVert\nabla\{\mathbbm{1}_{A}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}=\left\lVert\nabla\{\mathbbm{1}_{A_{1}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\{\mathbbm{1}_{A_{2}}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}.

(98)

We set
f_{1}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{A_{1}}}{\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A_{1}}\right\rVert_{\theta}}
,
f_{2}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{A_{2}}}{\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A_{2}}\right\rVert_{\theta}}
and find that

a\mathbbm{1}_{A}=a\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A_{1}}\right\rVert_{\theta}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{1}}+a\left\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{A_{2}}\right\rVert_{\theta}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{2}},

(99)

which is in contradiction with the assumption that
a\mathbbm{1}_{A}
is an extreme point. The case
\theta=1
is similar and omitted.

Step 2.
We show that
a\mathbbm{1}_{A}
is an extreme point. Assume by contradiction that it is not. Then, there exists
\lambda\in\,]0,1[
and two functions
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
in the unit ball such that

a\mathbbm{1}_{A}=\lambda f_{1}+(1-\lambda)f_{2}.

(100)

Let
E\subset[0,1]^{2}
be a measurable set. We define

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta,E}=\theta\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(E)}+(1-\theta)\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(E)^{2}}.

(101)

We must have

\left\lVert a\mathbbm{1}_{A}\right\rVert_{\theta,E}=\lambda\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{\theta,E}+(1-\lambda)\left\lVert f_{2}\right\rVert_{\theta,E},

(102)

otherwise we would reach the contradiction

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\left\lVert a\mathbbm{1}_{A}\right\rVert_{\theta,E}+\left\lVert a\mathbbm{1}_{E}\right\rVert_{\theta,E^{c}}

\displaystyle<\lambda\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{\theta,E}+(1-\lambda)\left\lVert f_{2}\right\rVert_{\theta,E}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{\theta,E^{c}}+(1-\lambda)\left\lVert f_{2}\right\rVert_{\theta,E^{c}}=1,

(103)

which is impossible.

If
\theta\in\,]0,1[
, then (
102
) implies that
\mathrm{supp}\{\nabla\{f_{i}\}\}\subset\partial A.
In particular,
\nabla\{f_{i}\}
must vanish in the interior
\overset{\circ}{A}
. It follows from the Constancy Theorem
[
61
]
,
[
23
, Lemma 4.6]
that
f_{i}
is constant on
A
. Finally, since

a\mathbbm{1}_{A}=\lambda f_{1}+(1-\lambda)f_{2}

with
f_{1}\geq 0
and
f_{2}\geq 0
, it follows that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
must vanish on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus A
and satisfy
f_{1}=f_{2}=a\mathbbm{1}_{A}
. This contradicts the assumption that
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
.

If
\theta=1
, then
\mathrm{supp}\{[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f_{1}\}\}
and
\mathrm{supp}\{[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f_{2}\}\}
must be subsets of the finite set of corners of
A
, denoted by
A_{c}
. We use
E_{c}
with subscript
c
to denote the set of corners
\mathrm{supp}([\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{1_{E}\})
of a set
E
. The functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
must be piecewise constant on
A
and vanish outside. Assume, by contradiction, that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are not constant on
A
. Then,

f_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{i,k}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{k}},\quad\text{with}a_{i,k}\neq a_{i,k^{\prime}},

(104)

and, by construction,
a_{2,k}=a-a_{1,k}
. We claim that there exists a
k\in[1\cdots K]
, wlog
k=1
, such that

\begin{cases}A_{c}\subset E_{1,c}\\
\partial A\subset\partial E.\end{cases}

(105)

Assume by contradiction that there exists no such
k
. Then, there exists a corner
\tilde{x}
and two indices
(k,k^{\prime})
, wlog
(k,k^{\prime})=(1,2)
, such that

\begin{cases}\tilde{x}\in\partial A\\
\tilde{x}\notin A_{c}\\
\tilde{x}\in E_{1,c}\\
\tilde{x}\in E_{2,c}.\end{cases}

(106)

This implies that
\tilde{x}\notin\mathrm{supp}([\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f_{i}\})
and, therefore, that one must have
a_{i,1}=a_{i,2}
for the associated Dirac mass to cancel each other.
We apply the same argumentation to find an index k, wlog
k=2
, such that

\begin{cases}\partial E_{1}\setminus\partial A\subset\partial E_{2}\\
E_{1,c}\setminus A_{c}\subset E_{2,c}.\end{cases}

(107)

In particular,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
share a corner
\tilde{x}\notin\mathrm{supp}([\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f_{i}\})
, which therefore must be cancelled. This happens if and only if
a_{i,1}=a_{i,2}
,
which is a contradiction. Thus,
f_{1}=a_{1,1}E_{1}
,
f_{2}=a_{2,1}E_{1},
and
a_{1,1}=a_{2,1}
. Finally
f_{1}=f_{2}
, which is a contradiction with the initial assumption.

∎

In Corollary
1
, we leverage the characterisation of extreme points from Theorem
3
to analyse the solution set of a data-fidelity term regularised by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}.

Corollary 1
.

Let the loss functional
\mathcal{J}
be defined as

\mathcal{J}(\cdot)=E(\mathbf{y},\langle\cdot,\bm{\nu}\rangle)+\lambda\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}

(108)

with

•

\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
is a fixed-dimensional vector representing the available measurements;

•

the data-fidelity functional
E:\mathbb{R}^{M}\times\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
is proper, coercive, continuous, and strictly convex in its second argument;

•

the measurement operator
\langle\cdot,\bm{\nu}\rangle:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K})\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
is linear and
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
continuous.

Then, the solution set

\mathcal{V}=\underset{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K})}{\operatorname{argmin}}\,\mathcal{J}(f)

(109)

is nonempty, convex, and
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-compact. Moreover, its extreme points are of the form

\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{k}},\quad\text{with}\quad K\leq M,

(110)

where
E_{k}\in\mathcal{E}_{\nabla}
if
\theta\in]0,1[
, and
E_{k}\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}}
if
\theta=1
.

Proof of Corollary
1
.

Define
\mathcal{J}^{\star}=\underset{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K})}{\text{inf}}\mathcal{J}(f)
and consider a sequence of solutions
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that

-\infty<\underset{n\to\infty}{\text{lim}}\mathcal{J}(f_{n})=\mathcal{J}^{\star}<\infty,

(111)

which exists because
\mathcal{J}
is coercive and proper. It follows that
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
and, by norm equivalence, bounded in
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
. According to the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, up to a subsequence,
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-convergent to some limit
f
. It follows from the
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{J}
that

\underset{n\to\infty}{\text{lim}}\mathcal{J}(f_{n})=\mathcal{J}(f)=\mathcal{J}^{\star}.

(112)

Consequently,
\mathcal{V}
is nonempty, and the characterisation of its extreme points follows from
[
22
, Corollary 3.8]
and Theorem
3
.
∎

The norm
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
penalises the number of corners of the sets
E_{k}
, whereas
\left\lVert\nabla\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}
penalises their perimeter. Each has a distinct regularisation effect on the solutions of the optimisation problem. Informally, the former promotes solutions with simpler sets
E_{k}
, while the latter favours solutions with smaller sets
E_{k}
. This effect is proportional to
\theta
, which, in practice, must be tuned.

We conclude this section with Proposition
6
, which identifies an important class of
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-continuous measurement operators
\langle\cdot,\bm{\nu}\rangle
.

Proposition 6
.

If
\bm{\nu}=(\nu_{m})_{m=1}^{M}
is such that
\nu_{m}\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathrm{K})
, then the operator

\langle\cdot,\bm{\nu}\rangle:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})\to\mathbb{R}^{M},\quad f\mapsto(\langle f,\nu_{m}\rangle)_{m=1}^{M}

(113)

is
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-continuous.

Proof of Proposition
6
.

From
[
32
]
we have

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}(\mathrm{K})\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}}(\mathrm{K})\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(114)

The space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which is essentially the space of distributions

such that
[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, is the dual space of

. In particular, it is shown in
[

]
that

. This implies that if

is such that

\langle\cdot,\bm{\nu}\rangle:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}^{M},\quad f\mapsto(\langle f,\nu_{m}\rangle)_{m=1}^{M}

III
Resolution of the Optimization Problem

The resolution of the problem in (
109
) is not directly feasible, as the underlying search space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K})
is infinite-dimensional. Moreover, the fact that the edges and corners of
E_{k}
in (
110
) may lie anywhere in
\mathrm{K}
makes their estimation challenging.

III-A

Pixel-Based Exact Discretization

We discretize the search space into a finite-dimensional cone, of functions of the form (
110
), with corners that lie on the dyadic grid

\mathcal{X}_{n}=2^{-n}[0\cdots 2^{n}-1]\times 2^{-n}[0\cdots 2^{n}-1],

(116)

with
(2^{n}+1)^{2}
knots, and associated pixels

E^{n}_{n_{1},n_{2}}=\left[\frac{(n_{1}-1)}{2^{n}},\frac{n_{1}}{2^{n}}\right]\times\left[\frac{(n_{2}-1)}{2^{n}},\frac{n_{2}}{2^{n}}\right].

(117)

The discretized search space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
is

\left\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K}):\quad[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X}_{n})\right\},

(118)

and the associated discretized optimization problem is as in

\mathcal{V}_{n}=\underset{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n})}{\text{argmin}}\left(E(\mathbf{y},\langle f,\bm{\nu}\rangle)+\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\theta}\right).

(119)

Our objective is now to reformulate (
119
) as an optimisation problem over
\mathbb{R}^{2^{n}\times 2^{n}}_{+}
, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,\infty[
. We note that any function
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
can be uniquely expressed as

f=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}=1,1}^{2^{n},2^{n}}a_{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{n}},\\
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n_{1},n_{2}})_{n_{1},n_{2}=1,1}^{2^{n},2^{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{n}\times 2^{n}}_{+},

(120)

which corresponds to the tensor product of B-splines of order 1. Furthermore, we introduce the synthesis operator
\mathrm{T}

\mathrm{T}:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2^{n}\times 2^{n}},\\
f\mapsto\left(2^{2n}\langle f,\mathbbm{1}_{E^{n}_{n_{1},n_{2}}}\rangle\right)_{n_{1},n_{2}=1,1}^{2^{n},2^{n}},

(121)

and its adjoint, the analysis operator
\mathrm{T}^{\star}

\mathrm{T}^{\star}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2^{n}\times 2^{n}}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n}),\\
\mathbf{a}\mapsto\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}=1,1}^{2^{n},2^{n}}a_{n_{1},n_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{n}}.

(122)

The operators
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{T}^{\star}
are implicitly dependent on the grid
\mathcal{X}_{n}
. In the sequel, we shall occasionally regard
\mathbf{a}
as a sequence indexed over
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, in which case its entries will be denoted by
\mathbf{a}[\cdot,\cdot]
and taken to be equal to
0
outside
[0\cdots 2^{n}]\times[0\cdots 2^{n}]
. We consider the new optimisation problem

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}=\underset{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{n}\times 2^{n}}_{+}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\left(E(\mathbf{y},\langle\mathrm{T}^{\star}\{\mathbf{a}\},\bm{\nu}\rangle)+\lambda\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\theta}\right),

(123)

where

\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\theta}=(1-\theta)2^{-n}\left(\left\lVert\bm{h}_{1,0}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}+\left\lVert\bm{h}_{0,1}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}\right)\\
+\theta\left\lVert\bm{h}_{1,1}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}},

(124)

with

\bm{h}_{1,1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{bmatrix},\quad\bm{h}_{1,0}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-1\end{bmatrix},\quad\bm{h}_{0,1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

(125)

A key feature of this formulation, as shown in Proposition
7
, is that the problem in (
123
) is the exact discretisation of the problem in (
119
),
i.e.
, it incurs no discretisation error.

Proposition 7
.

The optimization problem in (
123
) is equivalent to the one in (
119
), in the sense that,

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{V}_{n}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{T}\{f\}\in\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{n}.

(126)

Proof of Proposition
7
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=\mathrm{T}\{f\}
. Observing that
\mathrm{T}^{\star}
is the inverse of
\mathrm{T}
, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\bm{h}_{1,1}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}},

(127)

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{I}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\left\lVert\bm{h}_{0,1}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}},

(128)

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{I}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\left\lVert\bm{h}_{1,0}\ast\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{\ell_{1}}.

(129)

We prove only (
127
), as the reasoning for the other two is analogous.
We observe that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{E_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{n}}=\beta\left(2^{n}\cdot-(n_{1}-1)\right)\otimes\beta\left(2^{n}\cdot-(n_{2}-1)\right),

(130)

and calculate that

\displaystyle[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}=1,1}^{2^{n},2^{n}}\mathbf{a}[n_{1},n_{2}]\left(\delta_{\frac{n_{1}}{2^{n}}}-\delta_{\frac{(n_{1}-1)}{2^{n}}}\right)\otimes\left(\delta_{\frac{n_{2}}{2^{n}}}-\delta_{\frac{(n_{2}-1)}{2^{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathbf{a}[n_{1},n_{2}]\left(\delta_{\frac{n_{1}}{2^{n}}}-\delta_{\frac{(n_{1}-1)}{2^{n}}}\right)\otimes\left(\delta_{\frac{n_{2}}{2^{n}}}-\delta_{\frac{(n_{2}-1)}{2^{n}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\delta_{\frac{n_{1}}{2^{n}},\frac{n_{2}}{2^{n}}}\bigg(\mathbf{a}[n_{1},n_{2}]-\mathbf{a}[n_{1}+1,n_{2}]

\displaystyle-\mathbf{a}[n_{1},n_{2}+1]+\mathbf{a}[n_{1}+1,n_{2}+1]\bigg).

(131)

Thus

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{f\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|(\bm{h}_{1,1}\ast\mathbf{a})[n_{1},n_{2}]\big|.

(132)

∎

Proposition
7
enables one to obtain a solution
f_{n}^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
by solving the problem in (
123
). Moreover, since the operator

\langle\mathrm{T}^{\star}\{\cdot\},\bm{\nu}\rangle:\mathbb{R}^{2^{n}\times 2^{n}}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}

(133)

is linear, when
\mathbf{a}
is vectorised, the operator in (
133
) can be represented by a matrix. Consequently, (
123
) can be solved using a primal–dual algorithm
[
16
]
or a double-staged FISTA
[
62
]
.

III-B

Convergence

The solution
f_{n}^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
obtained by solving the discretized optimisation problem (
119
) is only an approximation of some
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}
. Therefore, for this exact grid-based discretisation to be reliable and practical, one must be confident that, in some sense,

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}f_{n}^{\star}=f^{\star}.

(134)

This convergence is established in Theorem
4
.

Theorem 4
.

For any sequence
(f_{n}^{\star})_{n=1}^{\infty}

of discretized solutions with
f_{n}^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}_{n},
and for any solution
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}
, the
following convergences hold:

1.

over the loss functional,
\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{lim}}\mathcal{J}(f_{n}^{\star})=\mathcal{J}(f^{\star})
;

2.

over the simulated measurements,
\underset{n\to\infty}{\mathrm{lim}}\langle f_{n}^{\star},\bm{\nu}\rangle=\langle f^{\star},\bm{\nu}\rangle
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Item 1.
We fix
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}
and use Lemma
1
to find a sequence
(\tilde{f}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
\tilde{f}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
that is
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-convergent to
f^{\star}
and satisfies
\left\lVert f^{\star}\right\rVert_{\theta}=\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\left\lVert\tilde{f}_{n}\right\rVert_{\theta}
. Then, the
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-convergence implies that

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}E(\mathbf{y},\langle\tilde{f}_{n},\bm{\nu}\rangle)=E(\mathbf{y},\langle f^{\star},\bm{\nu}\rangle)\\
\Rightarrow\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\mathcal{J}(\tilde{f}_{n})=\mathcal{J}(f^{\star}).

(135)

It follows from (
135
) that

\mathcal{J}(f^{\star})\leq\mathcal{J}(f_{n}^{\star})\leq\mathcal{J}(\tilde{f}_{n})\Rightarrow\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\mathcal{J}(f_{n}^{\star})=\mathcal{J}(f^{\star}).

(136)

Item 2.
The convergence in Item 1 implies that the sequence
(\mathcal{J}(f_{n}^{\star}))_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded. In turn, this boundedness, combined with the assumptions on
E
, implies that the sequence
(f_{n}^{\star})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
, and therefore in
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
. By the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, we extract a
\mathrm{weak}^{\star}
-convergent subsequence
(f_{n_{m}}^{\star})_{m=1}^{\infty}
and find that

\underset{m\to\infty}{\lim}\langle f_{n_{m}}^{\star},\bm{\nu}\rangle=\langle f^{\star\star},\bm{\nu}\rangle

(137)

for some
f^{\star\star}\in\mathcal{V}
. If one assumes by contradiction that the sequence
(\langle f_{n},\bm{\nu}\rangle)_{n=1}^{\infty}
does not converge to
\langle f^{\star\star},\bm{\nu}\rangle
, then a classical argument
[
30
, Theorem 3]
, which leverages the strict convexity of
E(\mathbf{y},\cdot)
, leads to a contradiction.
∎

IV
Application to Denoising

IV-A

General Description

Our goal is to denoise an image
\mathbf{y}
, which is assumed to be square and composed of pixels

E_{n_{1},n_{2}}^{N}=\left[\frac{n_{1}-1}{2^{N}},\frac{n_{1}}{2^{N}}\right]\times\left[\frac{n_{2}-1}{2^{N}},\frac{n_{2}}{2^{N}}\right]

(138)

at resolution
N
. The continuous-domain optimization problem is formulated as

\mathcal{V}=\underset{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathrm{K})}{\mathrm{argmin}}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\bm{\nu}\{f\}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\theta}\right),

(139)

where
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2^{N}\times 2^{N}}
, and
\forall\mathbf{n}\in[1\cdots 2^{N}]^{2}
,

[\bm{\nu}\{f\}]_{\mathbf{n}}=\langle f,\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\mathbf{n}}^{N}}\rangle=\int_{E_{\mathbf{n}}^{N}}f(\mathbf{x})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

(140)

We know from Corollary
1
that
\mathcal{V}
is nonempty, compact, and convex, and that its extreme points are of the form
\sum_{k=1}^{K}a_{k}\mathbbm{1}_{E_{k}}
with
K\leq 2^{2N}
. The discretized optimization problem is defined as

\mathcal{V}_{n}=\underset{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n})}{\mathrm{argmin}}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\bm{\nu}\{f\}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\theta}\right),

(141)

and a solution
f^{\star}_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n}
can be obtained by solving (
123
). Finally, from Theorem
4
,
\forall f^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}
and
\forall\mathbf{n}\in[1\cdots 2^{N}]^{2}
, we obtain

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\int_{E_{\mathbf{n}}^{N}}f_{n}(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\int_{E_{\mathbf{n}}^{N}}f^{\star}(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

(142)

IV-B

Resolution on Finite Grid

We argue that, to solve the continuous-domain problem in (
139
), it is sufficient to find the solution
f^{\star}_{N}\in\mathcal{V}_{N}
. As a first step, we show in Theorem
5
that the sets of solutions
(\mathcal{V}_{n})_{n=N}^{\infty}
are embedded one into another.

Theorem 5
.

For all
N_{2}\geq N_{1}\geq N
, the inclusions hold true

\mathcal{V}_{N_{1}}\subset\mathcal{V}_{N_{2}}\subset\mathcal{V}.

(143)

Proof of Theorem
5
.

We define the downsampling operator

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D},0}^{+}(\mathcal{X}_{n-1})

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{n}=1,1}^{2^{n},2^{n}}\mathbf{a}[\mathbf{n}]\mathbbm{1}_{E_{\mathbf{n}}^{n}}

with

+\mathbf{a}[2n_{1},2n_{2}-1]+\mathbf{a}[2n_{1},2n_{2}]\big).

(145)

Step 1.

We claim that,

Next, we get from (

\displaystyle\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\mathrm{R}\{f\}\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}

The injection of (
145

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|(\bm{h}_{1,1}\ast\mathbf{a})[2n_{1}-1,2n_{2}]|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}|(\bm{h}_{1,1}\ast\mathbf{a})[2n_{1}-1,2n_{2}+1]|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|(\bm{h}_{1,1}\ast\mathbf{a})[2n_{1},2n_{2}+1]|

The claim (

Step 2.

Let
f_{N_{1}}\in\mathcal{V}_{N_{1}}
and

where the LHS inequality follows from the inclusion

One can iterate (

The combination of Equation (

Theorem
5
reveals that a solution of the continuous-domain problem can be found by solving the optimization problem on

.

There is a unique solution
f^{\star}\in\mathcal{V}
whose knots lie on the grid
\mathcal{X}_{N}
,

(156)

Proof of Proposition
8
.

We know from Theorem (
4

(157)

We conclude with the observation that, on

is a bijection.

The combination of Theorem

and Proposition

yields that (

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have shown that the convex combination of the TV regularization with the tensor product term
\left\lVert[\mathrm{D}\otimes\mathrm{D}]\{\cdot\}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{M}}
promotes piecewise constant functions with edges parallel to the
x_{1}
or the
x_{2}
axis, as solutions to a continuous-domain optimization problem. In the particular case of denoising, we revealed that the optimization problem discretized on the canonical, pixel-based grid has a unique solution, which is also a solution of the continuous-domain problem. Finally, we demonstrated that the discretized problem can be solved with the same algorithm as for TV, with an additional filter, and that our new regularization significantly improves the reconstruction quality when the image has the appropriate underlying geometry.

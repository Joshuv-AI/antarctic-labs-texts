Thiamine modulates the mammary inflammation, antioxidant function and lactation performance in lactating sheep under heat-stress conditions
Authors: Huisi Wu, Tingting Ni, Feng You, Yuming Huang, Feiyang He, Gao Liu, Jianing Wang, Mengzhi Wang, Hao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study investigated the effect of dietary thiamine supplementation on mammary inflammation, antioxidant function and lactation performance of heat-stressed Hu ewes. Twenty-four lactating Hu ewes (parity 1-2; days in milk = 14 ± 1 d; milk yield = 1.25 ± 0.08 L/d) with body weight of 52.6 ± 2.7 kg and body condition score of 2.62 ± 0.12 (0 = emaciation, 5 = obesity) were selected. After a 7 d acclimation period, the ewes were randomly allocated into three treatments and raised for 21 d under different ambient temperatures: thermoneutral group without supplementation (CON), heat stress group without supplementation (HS), and heat stress group with dietary thiamine supplementation at 100 mg/kg dry matter intake (DMI) (THS). Each group consists of 8 repetitions, and each repetition involves 1 ewe. THS group significantly increased milk yield, milk fat, lactose, and protein levels, except for DMI, compared with HS group (P < 0.05). The THS group significantly decreased DMI, milk yield, milk fat, lactose, and protein levels compared with CON group (P < 0.05). Milk fatty acid (FA) production of preformed FA, C18:0, C18:1n9c, C18:2n6c, C18:3 and C20:3 of THS group significantly decreased compared with HS group (P < 0.05). Milk FA production of preformed FA, C18:0, C18:1n9c, C18:2n6c, and C20:3 of THS group significantly increased compared with CON group (P < 0.05). Arginine, leucine, valine, alanine, and aspartate productions in milk of THS group were significantly higher than those in HS group (P < 0.05). Relative to the HS group, total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) activities and triglyceride content were significantly higher in the THS group (P < 0.05), while malondialdehyde (MDA) and TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β levels significantly declined in the THS group (P < 0.05) in mammary vein blood and mammary tissue. Relative to the HS group, the protein abundances of lactose synthesis related proteins (GLUT1, GLUT3, and LALBA), milk fat synthesis related proteins (FASN, SCD, and PPARγ) and milk protein synthesis related proteins (CAT1, EAAT3, and ASCT2) were significantly higher in the mammary tissue in the THS group (P < 0.05). Relative to the CON group, the protein abundances of lactose synthesis related proteins (GLUT1, GLUT3, and LALBA), milk fat synthesis related proteins (FASN, SCD, and PPARγ) and milk protein synthesis related proteins (CAT1, EAAT3, and ASCT2) were significantly lower in the mammary tissue in the THS group (P < 0.05). These results suggest that thiamine supplementation could mitigate mammary inflammation, improve mammary antioxidant function and lactation performance.

Introduction

Heat stress refers to environmental conditions that induce an increase in core body temperature beyond its predetermined thermal threshold. This condition typically results from a combination of elevated atmospheric temperatures, both direct and indirect solar radiation, as well as specific levels of air humidity and wind speed. These factors collectively cause the operative environmental temperature to frequently surpass the thermoneutral zone of animals, ultimately resulting in heat stress (Zhou et al., 2022). Heat stress is a major constraint on dairy production in regions with high environmental temperatures (Carvajal et al., 2021). Under heat stress conditions, livestock often exhibit decreased appetite and water consumption. These behavioral and physiological changes can disrupt endocrine function, and elevate the energy expenditure required for basal metabolic maintenance, ultimately impairing productive performance (Chauhan et al., 2021). Although sheep are generally considered less vulnerable to heat stress than cattle due to their lower reliance on consistent feed intake, prolonged exposure to extreme heat can still significantly amplify adverse effects in this species (Yuan et al., 2025).

Heat stress may disrupt key physiological processes in sheep, including changes to their hormonal balance (Van Wettere et al., 2021). These factors collectively result in declined milk yield in sheep. Nonetheless, milk yield and quality represent key factors for lamb growth and immunological development, with their reduction decreasing lamb growth efficiency, eventually influencing later fattening performance (Ma et al., 2024). Several studies have shown that heat stress decreases both dry matter intake (DMI) and rumination rate, indirectly leading to reduced milk yield (Corazzin et al., 2021; Meneses et al., 2021). In other researches, heat stress alters key hormonal releases needed for milk yield (Hooper et al., 2020), and affects CORT, PRL, IGF-1, and INSR gene levels in mammary tissue, suggesting its direct influence on mammary cells (Hooper et al., 2021). Furthermore, heat-stressed dairy cows and goats experience increased protein catabolism, and some of these mobilized amino acids are utilized in glucose synthesis (Jorge-Smeding et al., 2024; Mehaba et al., 2019). However, how heat stress affects the mammary inflammation, antioxidant function and lactation performance of lactating Hu sheep remains to be further studied.

Thiamine, commonly referred to as vitamin B1, has a key effect on carbohydrate metabolism. It acts as a coenzyme in critical metabolic pathways, including tricarboxylic acid cycle or pentose phosphate pathway (Hrubša et al., 2022). In addition to carbohydrate metabolism, thiamine supplementation mitigates high-concentrate diets-induced local inflammation and ruminal epithelial disruption (Zhang et al., 2020). Earlier studies have demonstrated that thiamine exhibits antioxidant properties by effectively neutralizing free radicals, thereby suppressing apoptotic processes (Kaźmierczak-Barańska et al., 2025; Mrowicka et al., 2023). Supplementing thiamine in diets enhances rumen fermentation, alleviates inflammation caused by heat stress, and recovers milk yield of heat-stressed Hu ewes (Ma et al., 2024). However, how thiamine modulates the mammary inflammation, antioxidant function and lactation capacity of heat-stressed Hu sheep remains to be further studied.

Consequently, the present work focused on 1) assessing how heat stress affected mammary inflammation, antioxidant function and lactation performance (milk yield and milk composition) of Hu sheep, and 2) evaluating the possible effect of dietary thiamine on modulating the mammary inflammation, antioxidant function and lactation performance of heat-stressed Hu sheep.

Material and methods

Animal ethics statement

Every procedure in this work was implemented following the Animal Protection Law on the basis of Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals approved by the Ethics Committee of Yangzhou University (SYXK, 2016-0019).

Animals and experimental design

Twenty-four lactating Hu ewes (parity 1–2; days in milk = 14 ± 1 d; milk yield = 1.25 ± 0.08 L/d) with body weight of 52.6 ± 2.7 kg and body condition score of 2.62 ± 0.12 (0 = emaciation, 5 = obesity; Russel et al., 1969) were selected. Prior to the experiment, ewes were acclimatized for 7 d in a thermoneutral environment within a climate chamber, where the average temperature, relative humidity, and temperature-humidity index (THI) were 22.8 ± 0.39 °C, (45.2 ± 0.06)%, and 72 ± 1.57, respectively. After this adaptation phase, the animals were allocated into three treatment groups at random for a 21 d experimental period: thermoneutral group without supplementation (CON), heat stress group without supplementation (HS), and heat stress group with dietary thiamine supplementation at 100 mg/kg DMI (THS). Each group consists of 8 repetitions, and each repetition involves 1 ewe. The selected thiamine dosage was based on previous studies addressing ruminal acidosis in goats (Ma et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2020). Throughout the 21 d experiment, sheep were housed individually (2 m × 1.8 m, length × width). Diet was provided at 07:00 and 17:00 every day. Ewes were allowed to take feed and diet unrestricted, and the daily intake was maintained at the 5% residual feed intake level. Milking was performed manually twice daily at 08:00 and 18:00 by experienced personnel. Milk yield was precisely measured using a graduated cylinder, and the collected milk was subsequently used for lamb feeding. Throughout the study, the experiments were conducted in a climate chamber. Under thermoneutral conditions, the ambient temperature was maintained consistently between 22 and 24 °C. Under heat stress condition, the temperature followed a diurnal cycle: starting at 27 °C at 06:00, it rose to a peak of 37 °C, and then declined back to 27 °C by 18:00. This study aimed to maintain the THI below 80 under thermoneutral conditions and above 80 during heat stress exposure (Ma et al., 2024). Temperature-humidity index was computed using the formula established by Kelly and Bond (1971), also referenced in Li et al. (2022):THI=1.8T+32-0.55−0.0055RH×1.8T−26.8,where T represents air temperature (°C), whereas RH denotes relative humidity (expressed as a decimal). Ambient conditions were continuously recorded using HOBO data loggers (model UX100). Temperature and humidity readings were documented everyday over this 21 d experimental period, and THI values were derived from these measurements. Data were recorded three times per day (08:00, 12:00, and 18:00) for both the CON and HS groups. The average THI values consistently remained above 80 throughout the trial in both HS and THS groups, which significantly increased compared with CON group (P < 0.05). Since HS and THS ewes were housed under identical environmental conditions, their THI profiles were equivalent, thereby confirming the effective induction of heat stress (Ma et al., 2024).

Chemical analysis

Feed samples were collected and determined for dry matter (DM), crude protein (CP), calcium (Ca), and phosphorus (P) in accordance with AOAC (1990) official methods 990.02, 930.15, 965.17, and 968.08, respectively. Acid detergent fiber (ADF) and neutral detergent fiber (NDF) contents were analyzed following the methodology of Van Soest et al. (1991). Gross energy (GE) in feed ingredients and fecal samples was measured using a bomb calorimeter (C200, IKA-Werke GmbH & Co. KG, Staufen im Breisgau, Germany). Urinary GE was measured following the procedure described by Deng et al. (2014). Metabolizable energy (ME) was estimated according to NRC (2007). Organic matter (OM) was calculated as DM minus ash content. Ash content was determined via combustion at 550 °C for 5 h in a high-temperature electric resistance furnace (AY-BF-888–10, Henan Andy High Temperature Products Co., Ltd., Zhengzhou, Henan, China) according to method 942.05 (AOAC, 1990). Thiamine concentration in feed was analyzed using a commercial ELISA kit (TSZ Biological Trade Co. Ltd., Boston, MA, USA).

Sample collection

Animals were slaughtered and samples were collected at 08:00 on d 21. All animals were euthanized, and the mammary tissue was collected. The mammary tissues were chopped, with the surface milk and blood being washed away with saline. Then, about 10 g of mammary tissue was immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, added into 5-mL frozen tubes, and later preserved under −80 °C for laboratory testing. Blood was sampled in mammary vein with 5-mL vacuum tubes that contained sodium heparin (Kindly Enterprise Development Group Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) and centrifuged at 3000 × g at 4 °C for 15 min, and plasma was then preserved at −20 °C for subsequent analyses.

Milk sampling and analysis

Milk was collected from ewes at 08:00 and 18:00 daily, meanwhile, daily milk yield was documented using weighing scales (PL202-L/01, Mettler-Toledo Instruments (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). Daily milk was collected from each ewe via twice daily milking, and morning (08:00) and evening (18:00) milk samples were mixed at a ratio of 6:4, which was then examined by mid-infrared spectroscopy (Fossomatic 4000, Foss Analytical A/S, Hillerød, Denmark) to detect milk fat, protein, and lactose levels.

Antioxidant activity and triglycerides assays in mammary vein blood and mammary tissue

Following homogenization, mammary vein blood and tissues underwent centrifugation (15,000 × g, 15 min, 4 °C) in lysis buffer. Thereafter, those resulting supernatants were collected for subsequent analysis of oxidative stress-related markers, antioxidant enzyme activities, and triglyceride content. A bicinchoninic acid assay kit (23227; Pierce Biotechnology, Inc., Rockford, IL, USA) was applied in quantifying protein contents. Triglyceride (A110-1-1), malondialdehyde (MDA; A003–1), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC; A015-1-1) levels, superoxide dismutase (SOD; A001-3-2) and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px; A005-1-2) activities were determined in both mammary vein blood and tissue homogenates with respective assay kits (Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) in line with relevant protocols (Zhang et al., 2022).

Determination of cytokine in mammary vein blood and mammary tissue

Tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α; ESH0025, Feien Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, Hubei, China), interleukin-6 (IL-6; ESH0019, Feien Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, Hubei, China), and interleukin-1β (IL-1β; SBJ-S010, SenBeiJia Biological Technology Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) levels were analyzed with corresponding ELISA kits. Absorbance readings were taken at a wavelength of 450 nm with a BioTek microplate reader (Synergy HT, BioTek Instruments, Inc., Winooski, VT, USA).

Determination of milk FA

A total of 1 L of fresh milk was centrifuged at 1643 × g and 4 °C for 30 min to obtain milk fat cake. After fat extraction, milk fat samples were esterified, and FAs were transmethylated according to the method described by Chouinard et al. (1999). The FA composition of milk was subsequently analyzed via gas chromatography (GC; Agilent 7890B, Agilent Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) after FAME derivation. The GC system was equipped with a 100 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm CP-Sil 88 capillary column, a flame ionization detector (Agilent 7890 FID, Agilent Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China), and an automatic sampler (Agilent 7693A, Agilent Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China), as well as a split/splitless injector (Agilent 7890B, Agilent Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). FAMEs were identified by comparing their retention times with those of authentic standard compounds. The commercial standards used for FA qualification and quantification included methyl trans-11 18:1 (#CRM46905), FAME Mix 4 to 24 Unsaturated Fatty Acids (#18919-1AMP, Sigma–Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), and methyl cis-9, trans-11 conjugated linoleic acid (CLA; #13058–52–1, Matreya, LLC, State College, PA, USA). According to the procedure reported by Rico and Harvatine (2013), the relative proportion of each FA in total milk fat was corrected by a factor of 0.98885 to eliminate the interference of non-fat components, as previously described by Zhang et al. (2025).

Milk amino acid measurement

Total amino acid levels were quantified following a previously reported procedure (Jariyasopit et al., 2021). In brief, 100 mg of milk sample was placed into a 10 mL hydrolysis vial (crimp-seal glass vial; #5182–0838, Agilent Technologies, Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA) and combined with 5 mL of hydrolysis solution consisting of 6 mol/L HCl (#15186, Sigma–Aldrich Chemie GmbH, Taufkirchen, Germany), 0.1% phenol (#CDCU-1021204, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), and 5% thioglycolic acid (#T104998, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). After sealing, the vials were subjected to hydrolysis in a hot air oven (Agilent G2545A, Agilent Technologies, Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA) at 110 °C for 18 h. The resulting hydrolysates were then centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 min, and 100 μL of the supernatant was adjusted to pH 7.0 with 1 mol/L sodium carbonate (#CFHF–60035F, Shanghai Anpu Experimental Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). Subsequently, 25 μL of the neutralized solution was transferred to a 2 mL GC glass vial (#5182–0716, Agilent Technologies, Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA), followed by the addition of 50 μL of 200 nmol/mL norleucine as an internal standard. The mixture was dried under vacuum at 60 °C for 1 h. After adding 50 μL of dichloromethane (#CAEQ-4-103001–4000, Shanghai Anpu Experimental Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), the mixture was dried for an additional 30 min to eliminate residual water. Then, a mixture of 50 μL acetonitrile (#A104443, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) and 50 μL N-tert-butyldimethylsilyl-N-methyltrifluoroacetamide (MTBSTFA) containing 1% tert-butyldimethylchlorosilane (#375934, Sigma–Aldrich Co., LLC, St. Louis, MO, USA) was added. After sealing, the vial was incubated in a hot air oven at 100 °C for 4 h. Finally, 1 mL of the derivatized sample was injected into a GC–MS/MS system (GC TQ; GC 7890B/MSD 7000D, Agilent Technologies, Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). Chromatographic separation was performed on an HP-5MS column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm; Agilent Technologies, Inc., Santa Clara, CA, USA). Helium (purity > 99.9%; Taiyuan Jinteng Gas Co., Ltd., Taiyuan, Shanxi, China) served as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1.4 mL/min. The oven temperature was programmed from 130 to 325 °C as follows: ramped to 190 °C at 6 °C/min, then increased to 230 °C at 0.5 °C/s and held for 5 min, and finally raised to 325 °C and held for 6 min. The temperatures of the injector, transfer line, quadrupole, and electron ionization ion source were set at 280, 325, 180, and 240 °C, respectively (Zhang et al., 2025).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

First, mammary tissue was ground into powder in liquid nitrogen. Approximately 50 mg of mammary tissue was weighed, and according to the manufacturer's requirements, RNAiso Plus (9108, Takara Bio Inc., Dalian, Liaoning, China) was utilized for extracting total mammary tissue RNA. Then, total RNA content and quality were analyzed with the Ultra Micro Spectrophotometer (NanoDrop 2000, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA), with 1000 ng being prepared into cDNA with the Prime Script 158 RT Master Mix Perfect Real Time Kit (RR036A, Takara Bio Inc., Dalian, Liaoning, China). Primer Premier 6.0 software (Premier Biosoft International, San Francisco, CA, USA) was employed in preparing and assessing primers, while primers were commercially synthesized by Shanghai Genery Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The gene expression was tested through qRT-PCR with SYBR Premix Ex Taq Kit (RR420A, Takara Bio Inc., Dalian, Liaoning, China) in line with specific instructions. The full names of the abbreviations of all genes can be found in Table S1, and primer sequences are provided in Table S2. PCR was carried out with the Fast Real-time PCR System (ABI 7300, Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) under the following conditions: the thermal cycling protocol consisted of an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of amplification. Each cycle included denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s and a combined annealing/extension at 60 °C for 31 s. Immediately after amplification, melt curve analysis was performed to confirm product specificity. The analysis began with a denaturation step at 95 °C for 15 s, followed by reannealing at 60 °C for 1 min. Fluorescence was then continuously acquired during a gradual temperature increase from 60 to 95 °C at a rate of 0.1 °C/s. Each sample was tested, with β-actin being the internal reference. The 2−ΔΔCt approach was utilized for analyzing the qRT-PCR results to calculate relative mRNA abundance levels (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001).

Western blot

Approximately 100 mg of mammary tissue was subjected to homogenization within protein lysis buffer and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 20 min to collect the supernatant. The extracted proteins were separated electrophoretically on precast gels for 50 min before transfer onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. Following 2 h of blockage using bovine serum albumin, these membranes were washed by Tris-buffered saline with Tween 20 (TBST-10X) (9997S, Cell Signaling Technology, Inc., Danvers, MA, USA). After horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-rabbit immunoglobulin G (IgG) secondary antibody incubation (1:1000; Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) under ambient temperature for 1 h, the membranes underwent rinsing by TBST. A chromogenic substrate was utilized for visualizing target proteins, which were quantified with ImageJ software (1.53i, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). β-Actin served as a control protein for normalizing protein levels (Ma et al., 2022). Full names of all protein abbreviations are presented in Table S1. Detailed information on antibodies used in this study is shown in Table S3.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 16.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics, IBM Corporation, Chicago, IL, USA) was employed for data analysis. After including fixed effect of parity in the statistical model, it was found to be insignificant (P > 0.05); as a result, it was removed out of the final model, where treatment was the sole fixed effect. Intergroup differences were analyzed through one-way ANOVA as well as Tukey's post hoc test for multiple comparisons. A student's t-test was performed to compare the two groups. The statistical analysis mathematical model was as follows:Yij=μ+Ai+eij,where Yij stands for the observation of dependent variables; μ stands for the overall mean; Ai stands for the treatment effect; eij stands for the random error. Data were represented by mean and standard error of the mean (SEM). P ≤ 0.05 stood for statistical significance.

Discussion

The primary goal of this study was to assess the comprehensive impact of heat stress on animals under practical husbandry conditions, including the consequences of reduced feed intake and its subsequent physiological and metabolic effects. If both groups were pair-fed (equal feed provision), it could isolate the direct physiological effects of heat stress unrelated to feed intake. However, this approach might underestimate the overall performance loss caused by heat stress in actual production, since reduced feed intake is itself a primary pathway through which heat stress leads to decreased performance. Placing the control group in a thermoneutral environment with ad libitum feed access aimed to establish a benchmark for the animals' “optimal potential performance” under conditions free from heat stress and without nutritional limitations. This helps quantify the total production performance loss caused by heat stress (through the combined effects of environment and reduced intake), which holds greater practical relevance for assessing the economic impact of heat stress and developing mitigation strategies. Heat stress effects observed in this study result from the combined action of the environmental heat load and the consequent reduction in feed intake. It is acknowledged that the design of this study focuses more on evaluating the comprehensive negative impacts under practical production conditions. Heat stress exposure induces multifaceted physiological alterations in sheep, including diminished feed intake, reduced nutrient utilization, disturbances in protein, water, mineral and energy metabolism, and changes in enzymatic activities, hormonal regulation, and overall metabolic homeostasis (Van Wettere et al., 2021). The THI that integrates room temperature and relative humidity serves as a reliable metric for evaluating environmental thermal load on animals. Lactating ewes exposed persistently to conditions where temperatures exceed 30 °C and THI values rise above 80 are unable to sustain thermoregulatory balance, resulting in significant heat stress (Correddu et al., 2025). In the present study, sheep in CON group were maintained under thermoneutral conditions, with THI consistently remaining below 80. In contrast, both HS and HST groups were subjected to extended THI > 80, successfully establishing a heat-stress model. This experimental setup provides the foundation for analyzing the impact of dietary thiamine supplementation on modulating mammary gland inflammatory responses, antioxidant capacity, and milk yield of heat-stressed sheep.

Factors including water restriction, nutritional disorders, and specific nutrient deficiency may intensify adverse impacts of heat stress. Studies conducted across multiple sheep breeds have reported a significant decrease in DMI following heat stress exposure (Bokharaeian et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024; Mehaba et al., 2021). Similarly, DMI per unit body weight is lowered, while maintenance energy requirements increase under high ambient temperatures. These observations are consistent with the current results, confirming that elevated environmental temperatures reduce DMI in sheep. The decline in DMI compromises energy availability, which in turn negatively affects lactation performance—including both milk yield and composition. The findings in the present study further indicate that although dietary thiamine supplementation enhanced milk yield, milk fat, lactose, and protein content in heat-stressed lactating Hu sheep, it made no difference in mitigating the heat stress-induced DMI reduction. These outcomes align with earlier work by Ma et al. (2024).

Despite the absence of changes in DMI between the HS and THS groups, the observed increase in daily milk fat yield in heat-stressed sheep supplemented with thiamine may result from improved nutrient utilization efficiency. Specifically, thiamine appears to redirect nutrients toward mammary gland utilization rather than storage or alternative metabolic pathways, while also stimulating mammary epithelial cell proliferation and enhancing the synthesis of both DNFA and MFA (Ding et al., 2022; Zhao and Agellon, 2017; Zhang et al., 2025). Milk FAs are obtained via two main ways: DNFA synthesized inside mammary gland, and FA derived from dietary intake, microbial metabolism, or hepatic synthesis followed by release into the bloodstream (Sánchez et al., 2021). In the present study, the elevated DNFA production—primarily comprising C4:0, C10:0, C12:0, and C14:0—along with increased MFA, indicates that thiamine enhances de novo lipogenesis in heat-stressed sheep. Peroxisome proliferator activated receptor gamma acts as a key regulator of lipid metabolism, activating lipogenic pathways and modulating the expression of genes such as ACACA, FASN, and SCD in mammary tissue (Cheng et al., 2022). Both FASN and ACACA are critical enzymes involved in DNFA biosynthesis from precursors like β-hydroxybutyrate and acetate, and facilitate subsequent secretion of these FA (Chen et al., 2024). Meanwhile, SCD catalyzes the conversion of saturated fatty acids (SFA) into monounsaturated forms (Kalyesubula et al., 2025). Additionally, PPARA is implicated in regulating multiple enzymes associated with fatty acid oxidation (Yu et al., 2025). According to the findings, the increased FASN, SCD, ACACA, and PPARγ gene and protein levels inside mammary tissues following thiamine supplementation suggested the increased DNFA biosynthesis, SFA desaturation, and MFA levels within the milk of heat-stressed sheep. Besides, thiamine affected FA in the milk partially through up-regulating FASN, SCD, ACACA, PPARγ associated with mammary lipogenesis and decreased expression of PPARA associated with mammary fatty acid oxidation in heat-stressed sheep.

Milk protein production is determined by amino acid availability and biosynthetic capacity of the mammary gland (Hruby Weston et al., 2023). Free and peptide-bound amino acids serve as the primary substrates for this process, with their availability directly influencing both the concentration and yield of milk proteins (Zhou et al., 2021). Previous research has identified several amino acids—including arginine, phenylalanine, isoleucine, leucine, valine, threonine, and histidine—as potentially limiting for milk protein production (Morris and Kononoff, 2020; Lean et al., 2018). These amino acids modulate the rate of protein synthesis within the mammary tissue of lactating ruminants.

To analyze if alterations of milk amino acid profiles were linked to mammary uptake, we analyzed key amino acid transporters, namely, CAT1, EAAT3, and ASCT2 (encoded via SLC7A1, SLC1A1, and SLC1A5, separately), for their mRNA and protein levels. In this work, thiamine supplementation elevated the levels of arginine, leucine, valine, alanine, aspartate, glutamate, glutamine, ornithine, and proline in milk. This effect was partially attributable to upregulation of genes (SLC7A1, SLC1A1, SLC7A7, SLC1A5, and SLC15A1) and proteins (CAT1, EAAT3, and ASCT2) involved in amino acid transport and utilization. These findings suggest that thiamine enhances nitrogen utilization efficiency and reduces amino acid catabolism, thereby promoting milk protein synthesis in heat-stressed lactating ewes.

Lactose exerts a key effect on milk yield, with the decreased lactose level negatively affecting dairy product production (Portnoy and Barbano, 2021). Lactose level within milk is positively related to protein and fat levels but negatively related to somatic cell count (Alessio et al., 2021). Glucose is adsorbed from blood plasma by the passive mechanism that involves facilitative GLUT, with GLUT1 and GLUT3 having the highest abundances within lactating mammary tissue or by the active mechanism including type 1 sodium cotransporter (Mukherjee et al., 2023). When glucose is transported from plasma to mammary tissue, its use in such tissue is implemented with β-(1, 4) galactosyltransferase 1, the merely enzyme identified to deliver glucose to terminal N-acetylglucoseamine from 5-diphospho galactose for forming lactose, via the LALBA (Tsiplakou et al., 2015). Previous research showed that LALBA was markedly up-regulated within mammary gland throughout secretory activation, thus facilitating rapid lactose synthesis (Sadovnikova et al., 2021). From the findings, thiamine supplementation increased milk lactose content in heat-stressed lactating Hu ewes is partially related to the increased levels of mammary lactose synthesis related proteins (GLUT1, GLUT3, and LALBA).

Oxidative stress takes place after the pro- and anti-oxidant balance is broken, which can result in the increased free radical and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels (Olufunmilayo et al., 2023). Superoxide dismutase, GSH-Px, and T-AOC are key parts in the ROS-scavenging antioxidant system (Gang et al., 2025). Malondialdehyde, the metabolites produced by lipid peroxides, can be the indicator of ROS-mediated oxidative stress (Maurya et al., 2021). Upon oxidative stress, NRF2 dissociates from Keap1 for nuclear transport. Then, NRF2 can bind to antioxidant response elements (AREs) in targeted genes including antioxidases SOD, CAT, GSH-Px, or HO-1 and NQO1 (Tanase et al., 2022). Thus, in the study, the lower antioxidant activities (T-AOC, SOD, and GSH-Px) and higher MDA levels inside the mammary vein blood and mammary tissue, and the markedly reduced mRNA abundance of antioxidant-related genes (CAT, GPX1, SOD2, NRF2, HO-1, and NQO1) in mammary tissue in heat-stressed Hu ewes suggests that oxidative damage exists. Thiamine supplementation increased NRF2 mRNA abundance and elevated mRNA levels of antioxidases (CAT, GPX1, and SOD2) and phase II-metabolizing enzyme (HO-1 and NQO1), to reduce mammary oxidative injury in heat-stressed lactating sheep.

The critical role of TLR4 in mediating inflammatory responses through the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling axis is well documented (Dai et al., 2023). Modulation of factors like TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 represents a key anti-inflammatory mechanism that restrains excessive release of harmful levels of inflammatory mediators during both acute and chronic immune activation (Xin et al., 2023). Notably, the proinflammatory cytokine TNF-α activates extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1 and 2 (ERK1/2) pathways to significantly increase intestinal epithelial barrier permeability (Gao et al., 2023). Suppressing NF-κB activation has been considered a critical target and practical approach to suppressing an inflammatory response (Guo et al., 2024). Previous results also showed that thiamine suppresses the body NF-κB pathway activation, decreasing inflammatory injuries to ruminant animals (Ma et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2020). Thus, the findings showed that thiamine inhibited inflammatory response by decreasing TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β levels within mammary vein blood and mammary tissue and the mRNA abundance of inflammatory response-related genes (TLR-4, NF-κB, TNF-α, and IL-6). Furthermore, thiamine improved barrier integrity by increasing the mRNA abundance of barrier function-related genes (ZO-1, occludin, and claudin-1) inside the mammary tissue in heat-stressed lactating sheep.

In future studies, the existing data will be integrated to focus on exploring the potential core mechanisms by which thiamine alleviates heat stress in the mammary gland. The interrelationships and possible hierarchical roles among thiamine's three key actions will be analyzed and compared: enhancing mammary antioxidant defenses (through activation of the Nrf2 pathway), suppressing inflammatory responses (via inhibition of the NF-κB pathway), and its direct function as a cofactor for key metabolic enzymes (such as pyruvate dehydrogenase and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase).

Conclusion

During a 21 d lactation period, Hu ewes exposed to elevated ambient temperatures exhibited significant reductions in milk yield traits—including yield, fat, protein, and lactose contents—accompanied by the activation of heat stress responses. These conditions further induced alterations in mammary gland inflammation, antioxidative capacity, and epithelial barrier integrity. Dietary thiamine supplementation for heat-stressed ewes can improve milk lactose, milk FA yields, milk amino-acid yields partly by increasing the gene and protein expressions for milk lactose, fat and protein synthesis, respectively. Additionally, dietary thiamine supplementation decreases mammary inflammation and antioxidant dysfunction, and partially promotes mammary barrier function recovery and lactation performance in heat-stressed Hu sheep. Future work will elucidate how thiamine alleviates mammary heat stress by delineating the interplay between its roles in activating the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway, inhibiting the NF-κB inflammatory pathway, and functioning as an essential metabolic cofactor.

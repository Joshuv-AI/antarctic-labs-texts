Restriction estimates with sifted integers
Tanmoy Bera, G. K. Viswanadham

Abstract
. Let 𝒫 \mathcal{P} be a subset of primes and for each prime p ∈ 𝒫 p\in\mathcal{P} , consider a subset ℒ p \mathcal{L}_{p} of ℤ / p ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} . We provide restriction estimates with integers ≤ N \leq N sifted by ( ℒ p ) p ≤ z p ∈ 𝒫 (\mathcal{L}_{p})_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq z\\ p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}} . This generalizes a result of Green-Tao [ 3 ] on the restriction estimates.

1.
Introduction

For a given real number
\alpha\in[0,1]
and positive integer
N
, define

(1.1)

S(\alpha,N)=\sum_{p\leq N}a_{p}e(p\alpha)\;,

where the sum runs over primes
p\leq N
and
(a_{p})_{p\leq N}
is a sequence of complex numbers. Bourgain
[
1
]
obtained an upper bound for the
L_{\ell}
-norm of
S(\alpha,N)
for any
\ell>2
. More precisely, he obtained that

(1.2)

\left(\int_{0}^{1}|S(\alpha,N)|^{\ell}d\alpha\right)^{1/\ell}\ll_{\ell}N^{-1/\ell}\left(\frac{N}{\log N}\sum_{p\leq N}|a_{p}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\;.

The above bound can be seen to be optimal, up to the constant. The estimates of this type are called
restriction estimates
. An alternative proof of the result of Bourgain
[
1
]
is given by Green (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
) and used it to show that the primes enjoy the Hardy-Littlewood majorant property:

(1.3)

\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{p\leq N}a_{p}e(p\alpha)\right|^{\ell}\ll_{\ell}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{p\leq N}e(p\alpha)\right|^{\ell}\;

for any sequence
(a_{p})
of complex numbers with
|a_{p}|\leq 1
and
\ell\geq 2
.

These results were generalized to a wider range of arithmetic sets, instead of primes in (
1.1
), by
Green-Tao
[
3
]
, using the enveloping sieve developed by Ramaré
[
7
]
, and Ramaré and Ruzsa
[
10
]
.

In the other direction, restriction estimates with smooth numbers are obtained by Harper (see Theorem 2,
[
4
]
).

The restriction estimate (
1.2
) fails at the endpoint
\ell=2
, which can be seen using Parseval’s identity. Ramaré
[
8
]
provided a smooth transition from the case
\ell=2
to the case
\ell>2
and obtained explicit values for the constants in the estimates (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). He showed that the constant in (
1.3
) can be chosen to be absolute, namely
10^{5}
when
N\geq 10^{6}
. He also provided restriction estimates with primes in intervals. In a recent preprint, Ramaré
[
9
]
improved these explicit constants.

The applications of restriction estimates to number theory was initiated by Bourgain
[
1
]
. Later, these estimates were found to be very useful in number theory, for example, see
[
2
,
4
]
.

The aim of this article is to obtain restriction estimates with sifted sequences, which we will describe below.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a subset of primes, and
2\leq y_{0}\leq y
be two real numbers. We define

\mathcal{P}(y)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<y\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}p,\text{ and }\mathcal{P}(y_{0},y)=\mathcal{P}(y)/\mathcal{P}(y_{0}).

For any
p\in\mathcal{P}
, let
\mathcal{L}_{p}
be a non empty subset of
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
with cardinality
\lambda(p)
. We assume that there exists a positive constant
M
such that

(H1)

\lambda(p)\leq Mp^{1/4}\text{ for all }p\in\mathcal{P}.

For primes
p\notin\mathcal{P}
we take
\mathcal{L}_{p}=\emptyset.
We then extend it for any squarefree integer
\ell
by defining

\mathcal{L}_{\ell}=\prod_{p|\ell}\mathcal{L}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z},\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\;\lambda(\ell)=\prod_{p|\ell}\lambda(p).

For any
y\geq 2
, we denote

V(y):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<y\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}.

We also assume that there exist positive constants
T,\kappa
such that for any
y\geq 2

(H2)

V(y)\leq T\log^{\kappa}y.

Let
z
be a real number. For any positive integer
N,
define

\mathcal{S}(N):=\mathcal{S}(N,\mathcal{P},z):=\{n\leq N:n\left(\mathrm{mod}\,p\right)\notin\mathcal{L}_{p}\text{ for any }\;p\in\mathcal{P}\text{ and }p<z\}.

The main theorem of this article is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
z\leq N^{1/4}
and let
\mathcal{S}(N)
be as defined above. Then for any complex numbers
(a_{n})_{n\leq N}
and
\ell>2
, we have

(1.4)

\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}a_{n}e(n\alpha)\right|^{\ell}d\alpha\leq\frac{C}{N}\left(\frac{2N}{V(z)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}|a_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\ell/2}\;,

where
C=C(\kappa,\ell,T,M)
is a constant that can be computed explicitly.

As one can see by Parseval identity, Equation (
1.4
) doesn’t hold for
\ell=2
. The exact dependency of the constant on
\ell
is given in (
6.2
) and (
6
), and this provides a smooth transition from
\ell>2
to
\ell=2
. This striking feature comes from the method of Ramaré
[
8
]
.

Let
\delta>0
be a real number. A finite set
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is said to be a
\delta
-well spaced set if

\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|\geq\delta\text{ for all }x_{i},x_{j}\in\mathcal{B},

where
\|x\|=\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|x+k|
denotes the distance from the closest integer to
x
.
The above theorem is a simple application of the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
z\leq N^{1/4}
and let
\mathcal{S}(N)
be as defined above and
\mathcal{B}
be a
\delta
-well spaced subset of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}.
. Then for any complex numbers
(a_{n})_{n\leq N}
and
\ell>2
, we have

(1.5)

\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}a_{n}e(nb)\right|^{\ell}\leq C\left(\frac{N+\delta^{-1}}{V(z)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}|a_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\ell/2}\;,

where
C=C(\kappa,\ell,T,M)
is a constant that can be computed explicitly.

By taking
\mathcal{B}=\{\beta+k/N;0\leq k\leq N-1\}
and integrating over
\beta
in
[0,1/N]
, Theorem
1.1
follows.

In the proof of the above theorem, we have provided the exact dependency of the constant on
\ell
(see (
6.2
) and (
6
)).

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we need a dual large sieve type inequality, which we will describe below.

Let
N\geq 10^{3}
be a natural number, and
(u_{p})_{p\leq N}
be a sequence of complex numbers. In
[
8
]
, Ramaré
proved that

\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\left|\sum_{p\leq N}u_{p}e(bp)\right|^{2}\leq 280\frac{N+\delta^{-1}}{\log N}\log(2|\mathcal{B}|)\sum_{p\leq N}|u_{p}|^{2}.

The constant 280 is further improved to
4e^{\gamma}+o(1)
by Ramaré
[
9
, Theorem 2.4]
.
We need an analogous estimate with
\mathcal{S}(N)
instead of primes
p\leq N
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N
and
z
be positive real numbers such that
z\leq N^{1/4},
and
\delta>0.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a
\delta
-spaced subset of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}.
Then for any non-zero function
f
on
\mathcal{B},
we have

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}\left|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}f(b)e(nb)\right|^{2}\leq K\frac{N+\delta^{-1}}{V(z)}\log^{\kappa}\left(2\frac{\|f\|_{1}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)\|f\|_{2}^{2}

with some constant
K
that depends only on
T
,
M
and
\kappa
.

This result is the heart of our paper. The proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on the enveloping sieve of Ramaré and Ruzsa (see Section 4 of
[
10
]
) and follows the method of Ramaré
[
8
]
. The strategy is
not to study the characteristic function of the set
\mathcal{S}(N)
directly, but rather to construct a majorant
\beta
, which has good Fourier properties, using the enveloping sieve.
Using the theorem above, we can prove the following large sieve inequality with sifted integers by the standard argument.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N
and
z
be positive real numbers such that
z\leq N^{1/4},
and
\delta>0.
Let
\mathcal{B}
be a
\delta
-spaced subset of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}.
Then for any sequence of complex numbers
(a_{n})_{n\leq N}
, we have

\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}a_{n}e(nb)\right|^{2}\leq K\frac{(N+\delta^{-1})}{V(z)}\log^{\kappa}(2|\mathcal{B}|)\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}|a_{n}|^{2}\;.

Organization of the paper:
In Section
2
, we provide some necessary lemmas. In Section
3
, we discuss the enveloping sieve. In sections
4
,
5
and
6
, we prove Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
and
1.2
respectively. We will discuss Hardy-Littlewood majorant property for
\mathcal{S}(N)
in Section
7
. In the last section, we provide some applications of our results.

2.
Necessary Lemmas

Let
h
be the non-negative multiplicative function supported on square-free integers defined by
h(p)=\frac{\lambda(p)}{p-\lambda(p)}
for any
p\in\mathcal{P}
, and
0
if
p\notin\mathcal{P}
.

For any positive integer
d
, and real numbers
y\geq 1,z_{0}\geq 2
, let

\displaystyle G_{d}(y;z_{0})\>=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq y\\
(\ell,\;d\mathcal{P}(z_{0}))=1\end{subarray}}h(\ell)\;,

and let
G_{1}(y,z_{0})=G(y,z_{0})
,
G(y)=G_{1}(y,2)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

When
d
is coprime to
\mathcal{P}(z_{0})
, we have

G(yd;z_{0})\geq\left(\sum_{\delta\mid d}h(\delta)\right)G_{d}(y;z_{0})\geq G(y;z_{0})\;.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle G(y;z_{0})=\sum_{\delta\mid d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq y\\
(\ell,\mathcal{P}(z_{0}))=1\\
(\ell,d)=\delta\end{subarray}}h(\ell)=\sum_{\delta\mid d}h(\delta)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq y/\delta\\
(m,d\mathcal{P}(z_{0})=1)\end{subarray}}h(m)\geq\left(\sum_{\delta\mid d}h(\delta)\right)G_{d}(y/d,z_{0})\;.

Taking
yd
in place of
y
gives us the first inequality. On the other hand,

\displaystyle G(y,z_{0})=\sum_{\delta\mid d}h(\delta)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq y/\delta\\
(m,d\mathcal{P}(z_{0}))=1\end{subarray}}h(m)\leq\left(\sum_{\delta\mid d}h(\delta)\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq y\\
(m,d\mathcal{P}(z_{0}))=1\end{subarray}}h(m)=\left(\sum_{\delta\mid d}h(\delta)\right)G_{d}(y,z_{0})\;,

which gives the second inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
z_{1}\geq 2,
we have

G(y)\leq\left(\sum_{\delta\mid\mathcal{P}(z_{1})}h(\delta)\right)G(y;z_{1})\;.

Proof.

This follows immediately by taking
z_{0}=2
and
d=\mathcal{P}(z_{1})
in the previous lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

G(y;z_{0})\geq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<z_{0}\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)G(y).

Proof.

This follows from the previous lemma after noticing that

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\mid\mathcal{P}(z_{0})}h(\delta)=

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<z_{0}\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}(1+h(p))=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<z_{0}\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\frac{p}{p-\lambda(p)}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<z_{0}\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\;.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

The following lower bound holds for
G(y)
:

G(y)\geq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\;.

Proof.

\displaystyle G(y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq y\\
p\mid\ell\implies p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(\ell)\prod_{p\mid\ell}\left(\frac{\lambda(p)}{p-\lambda(p)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq y\\
p\mid\ell\implies p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(\ell)\frac{\lambda(\ell)}{\ell}\prod_{p\mid\ell}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\lambda(p)^{k}}{p^{k}}\geq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq y\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\;.

∎

We also need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
8
, Lemma 3.3]

Let
M\in\mathbb{R},
and
N
,
\delta
be positive real numbers. There exists a smooth function
\psi
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

•

The function
\psi
is non-negative.

•

When
t\in[M,M+N],
we have
\psi(t)\geq 1.

•

\widehat{\psi}(0)=N+\delta^{-1}.

•

When
|\alpha|>\delta,
we have
\widehat{\psi}(\alpha)=0.

•

We have
\psi(t)=\mathcal{O}_{M,N,\delta}(1/(1+|t|^{2})).

We refer to
[
5
]
for a proof of this lemma.

The following identity will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.6

(The Carlitz identity)
.

For any
n\geq 0
,

\sum_{k\geq 0}(k+1)^{n}t^{k}=\frac{A_{n}(t)}{(1-t)^{n+1}}\;,

where
A_{n}
is the
n
th Eulerian polynomial.

For discussion on Eulerian polynomials and a proof of this result, we refer to Chapter 1 of
[
6
]
.

3.
An enveloping Sieve

The results of this section are taken from the paper of Ramaré and Ruzsa, see Section 4 of
[
10
]
.
Let
z_{0}
and
z
be positive real numbers with
z_{0}\leq z
. Let

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(N)=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(N,\mathcal{P};z_{0},z):=\{n\leq N:n\left(\mathrm{mod}\,p\right)\notin\mathcal{L}_{p}\text{ for any }p\in\mathcal{P}\cap[z_{0},z)\}.

For any prime
p\in\mathcal{P}\cap[z_{0},z)
, put
\mathcal{K}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{p}
and let
\mathcal{K}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
for other primes.

For any square -free integer
q
, let
\mathcal{K}_{q}=\prod_{p\mid q}\mathcal{K}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
h_{[z_{0},z]}
be the multiplicative function supported on squarefree integers defined by
h_{[z_{0},z]}(p)=\frac{\lambda(p)}{p-\lambda(p)}
for
p\in\mathcal{P}\cap[z_{0},z)
and
0
otherwise.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\beta_{\mathcal{{S}^{\prime}}(N)}
denote the characteristic function of the set
\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(N)
. Then we have

(3.1)

\beta_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(N)}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq z^{2}\\
q\mid\mathcal{P}(z_{0},z)\end{subarray}}\sum_{a\;\text{mod}^{\star}q}w_{[z_{0},z]}(a/q)e\left(\frac{an}{q}\right)\;,

where

w_{[z_{0},z]}(a/q)=\frac{1}{G(z;z_{0})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_{q}|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{K}_{q}}e\left(\frac{-ac}{q}\right)\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\leq z\end{subarray}}h_{[z_{0},z]}(k)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},d_{1},r_{2},d_{2}\\
r_{1}d_{1},r_{2}d_{2}\leq R\\
k\mid(d_{1},d_{2})\\
q\mid[d_{1},d_{2}]\end{subarray}}\mu(r_{1})\mu(r_{2})\;.

Proof.

This follows by combining Equations 4.1.13, 4.1.14, and 4.1.21 of
[
10
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

We have

w_{[z_{0},z]}(a/q)\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p<z_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\frac{1}{G(z)}\;.

Proof.

By Equations 4.1.13 and 4.1.16 of
[
10
]
, we get

w_{[z_{0},z]}(a/q)\ll\frac{1}{G(z;z_{0})}3^{\omega(q)}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_{q}|}\left|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{K}_{q}}e\left(\frac{ba}{q}\right)\right|\;.

By a simple application of the Chinese remainder theorem, we have

\frac{1}{|\mathcal{K}_{q}|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{K}_{q}}e\left(\frac{ba}{q}\right)\leq\prod_{p\mid q}\frac{\lambda(p)}{p-\lambda(p)}\;.

Using the hypothesis (
H1
), i.e.
\lambda(p)\leq Mp^{1/4}
, we get that

3^{\omega(q)}\prod_{p\mid q}\frac{\lambda(p)}{p-\lambda(p)}\ll_{M}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}.

This inequality follows as
\frac{3\lambda(p)}{p-\lambda(p)}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}
holds except for
\ll_{M}1
primes.

The lemma follows by combining the above inequality with Lemma
2.3
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

When
2^{\kappa}T\log^{\kappa}\left(2\|f\|_{1}^{2}/\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right)\geq V(z)
, we use the dual large sieve inequality (see
[
5
]
) to get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}\left|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}f(b)e(bn)\right|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(N+\delta^{-1})\|f\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\kappa}T(N+\delta^{-1})\|f\|_{2}^{2}\log^{\kappa}\left(2\frac{\|f\|_{1}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)V(z)^{-1}\;.

We assume now onwards that

(4.1)

2^{\kappa}\log^{\kappa}\left(2\|f\|_{1}^{2}/\|f\|_{2}^{2}\right)\leq\frac{1}{T}V(z).

We bound the contribution from small integers trivially:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq z\\
n\in\mathcal{S}(N)\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}f(b)e(bn)\right|^{2}\leq z\|f\|_{1}^{2}\;.

From (
4.1
) and the assumption (
H2
) on
V(z)
, we get
\|f\|_{1}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}z^{1/2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}
. This gives us

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq z\\
n\in\mathcal{S}(N)\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}f(b)e(bn)\right|^{2}\leq 2z^{3/2}\|f\|_{2}^{2}\;.

Since
\log\left(2\frac{\|f\|_{1}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)\geq\log(2),
by the hypothesis (
H2
)
V(z)\leq T\log^{\kappa}z
, we get that

(4.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n\leq z\\
n\in\mathcal{S}(N)\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}f(b)e(bn)\right|^{2}\leq\frac{T}{\log^{\kappa}2}z^{3/2}\log^{\kappa}(z)\|f\|_{2}^{2}\log^{\kappa}\left(2\frac{\|f\|_{1}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)V(z)^{-1}\;.

Put

(4.3)

z_{0}=\left(2\frac{\|f\|_{1}^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)^{2}\;.

Then we have
z_{0}\leq z
. Let

(4.4)

\displaystyle W=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z<n\leq N\\
n\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(N)\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}f(b)e(nb)\right|^{2}.

Clearly

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\leq n\leq N\\
n\in\mathcal{S}(N)\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}f(b)e(bn)\right|^{2}\leq W\;.

We bound the characteristic function of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(N)\cap(z,N]
by
\beta_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(N)}
and use the expression (
3.1
).

Let
\delta_{1}=\min(\delta,\frac{1}{2z^{4}})
. Majorizing the characteristic function of the interval
[z,N]
by the smooth function
\psi
whose Fourier transform supported on
[-\delta_{1},\delta_{1}]
(see Lemma
2.5
) gives us

W\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq z^{2}\\
q\mid\mathcal{P}(z_{0},z)\end{subarray}}\sum_{a\mod^{*}q}w_{[z_{0},z]}(a/q)\sum_{b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathcal{B}}|f(b_{1})\overline{f(b_{2})}|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi(n)e\left(\left(b_{1}-b_{2}+\frac{a}{q}\right)n\right)\;.

The innermost sum, by the Poisson summation formula, is
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\psi}\left(b_{1}-b_{2}+\frac{a}{q}+m\right)
. Since
\widehat{\psi}
is supported on
[-\delta_{1},\delta_{1}]
, the last sum over
n,b_{1},b_{2}
is

\leq(N+\delta_{1}^{-1})\sum_{b_{1},b_{2}}|f(b_{1})f(b_{2})|\#\{a/q:\|b_{1}-b_{2}+a/q\|<\delta_{1}\}.

Now we split the sum over
q
into two parts, i.e.
q<z_{0}
and
q\geq z_{0}
:

W=W(q<z_{0})+W(q\geq z_{0}).

First we consider the sum
W(q\geq z_{0})
.
Since
\delta_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2z^{4}}
, there will be at most one
\frac{a}{q}
which satisfy the above inequality. Hence

W(q\geq z_{0})\leq(N+\delta_{1}^{-1})\|f\|_{1}^{2}|w_{[z_{0},z]}(a/q)|

for some
a(\text{mod})^{\star}(q),\;q\leq z^{2}
. By Lemma
3.2
, we get that

\displaystyle W(q\geq z_{0})\ll(N+\delta_{1}^{-1})\|f\|_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p<z_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\frac{1}{G(z)}\;.

Since
q\geq z_{0},
by Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle W(q\geq z_{0})\ll(N+\delta_{1}^{-1})\|f\|_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{z_{0}}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p<z_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\leq z\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)\;.

Using
\sqrt{z_{0}}=2\|f\|_{1}^{2}/\|f\|_{2}^{2}
and the assumption (
H2
) on
V(z_{0})
, we will reach

\displaystyle W(q\geq z_{0})\ll

\displaystyle(N+\delta_{1}^{-1})\log^{\kappa}(z_{0})\|f\|_{2}^{2}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq z\\
p\in\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p)}{p}\right)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\ll

7.
Hardy-Littlewood majorant property

In this section, we show that the sequence
\mathcal{S}(N)
enjoys the Hardy-Littlewood majorant property, under the following assumption on the cardinality of
\mathcal{S}(N)
: there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

(H3)

\frac{c_{1}N}{V(z)}\leq|\mathcal{S}(N)|\leq\frac{c_{2}N}{V(z)}.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\leq N}
be a sequence of complex numbers such that
\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}|a_{n}|^{2}\leq\mathcal{S}(N)
. Suppose the Hypothesis (
H3
) is satisfied. Then for any
\ell\geq 2
we have

\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}a_{n}e(n\alpha)\right|^{\ell}d\alpha\ll_{\ell}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}e(n\alpha)\right|^{\ell}d\alpha.

Proof.

For
\alpha\leq\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}3\pi N}
, by partial summation, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}e(n\alpha)\right|

\displaystyle\geq|\mathcal{S}(N)|-\left|2\pi\alpha\int_{1}^{N}\mathcal{S}(t)e(t\alpha)dt\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{1}N}{V(z)}-\frac{c_{2}2\pi\alpha N^{2}}{V(z)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{1}N}{3V(z)}\;.

From this, we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}e(n\alpha)\right|^{\ell}d\alpha\geq\int_{\alpha\leq\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}3\pi N}}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}e(n\alpha)\right|^{\ell}d\alpha\gg N^{\ell-1}/(V(z))^{\ell}.

Let
\ell>2.
From the above estimate together with Theorem
1.1
, we get

\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}a_{n}e(n\alpha)\right|^{\ell}d\alpha\ll_{\ell}\frac{N^{\ell-1}}{V(z)^{\ell}}\ll_{\ell}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{S}(N)}e(n\alpha)\right|^{\ell}d\alpha\;.

The case
\ell=2
is a consequence of Parseval’s identity.
∎

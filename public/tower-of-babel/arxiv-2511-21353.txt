Analogue of the Galois Theory for arbitrary finite field extensions
V. V. Bavula

Abstract
This paper is a finishing touch to the (over 200 years) classical ‘Galois Theory’ of arbitrary finite field extensions, i.e. the goal of it is to describe intermediate subfields of an arbitrary finite field extension via invariants of ‘natural/obvious’ objects that are associated with subfields via two Galois-type correspondences. The classical Galois Theory covers the case of finite Galois field extensions. For finite Galois field extensions the objects are their Galois groups and their invariants. In [ 2 ] , we introduce a new (ring theoretic) approach to the Galois Theory which is based on the principle of maximal symmetry . In [ 3 ] , the maximal symmetry of normal finite field extensions yields an analogue of the Galois Theory for them. For a normal finite field extension L / K L/K the ‘natural/obvious’ objects are the subalgebra 𝒟 ⁡ ( L / K ) ⋊ G ⁡ ( L / K ) {\cal D}(L/K)\rtimes G(L/K) of End ⁡ ( L / K ) {\rm End}(L/K) that is generated by the automorphism group G ⁡ ( L / K ) G(L/K) and the algebra 𝒟 ⁡ ( L / K ) {\cal D}(L/K) of differential operators on L / K L/K and its ‘invariants’. The ‘maximal symmetry’ means the equality End ⁡ ( L / K ) = 𝒟 ⁡ ( L / K ) ⋊ G ⁡ ( L / K ) {\rm End}(L/K)={\cal D}(L/K)\rtimes G(L/K) which turns out to be a characteristic property of normal finite field extensions, [ 3 ] . The aim of this paper is to obtain an analogue of the Galois Theory for arbitrary finite field extensions based on results and ideas of [ 2 ] and [ 3 ] . Key Words: finite field extension, B-extension, Galois extension, Galois group, Galois Correspondence, skew group algebra, invariants, algebra of differential operators, differential constants, centralizer, central simple algebra, the Double Centralizer Theorem. Finite purely inseparable field extensions have trivial automorphism group but rich subfield structure. They are normal field extensions but normal field extensions are a larger class. For a long time it was an open problem to produce a ‘Galois Theory’ for normal finite field extensions and the class of finite purely inseparable field extensions was a natural starting point.

In

A field extension is called

Modular extensions can be seen as ‘free’ objects in the class of finite purely inseparable field extensions (in the same vein as polynomial algebras are ‘free’ objects in the Algebraic Geometry), i.e. they satisfy no relations (apart from the obvious ones). {\rm coef}(f):=\{\mu_{i}\,|\,i\geq 0\}
is the
set of its coefficients. Clearly,

{\rm coef}(f)={\rm coef}(f^{sep}).

(3)

Notice that

\deg(f)=p^{n}\deg(f^{sep})\;\;{\rm where}\;\;n=\deg_{ins}(f).

(4)

For the polynomial
f(x)
as in (
2
),
f(x)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\lambda_{i}x^{ip^{n}}=\bigg(\sum_{i\geq 0}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}x^{i}\bigg)^{p^{n}}
, where
n=\deg_{ins}(f)
, and so

f(x)=\bigg({f^{sep}}^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}\bigg)^{p^{n}}\;\;{\rm where}\;\;{f^{sep}}^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}:=\sum_{i\geq 0}\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}x^{i}\in K\Big({\rm coef}(f)^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}\Big)[x]

(5)

is a
separable
polynomial over the field
K\Big({\rm coef}(f)^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}\Big)
. Clearly,

{\rm roots}(f)={\rm roots}({f^{sep}}^{\frac{1}{p^{n}}}).

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that
K
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
,
L/K
is a normal finite field extension, i.e. it is a splitting field of a set of polynomials
\{f_{i}(x)=f_{i}^{sep}(x^{p^{n_{i}}})\,|\,i\in I\}
, and
L^{pi}/K
is the largest purely inseparable field extension in the field
L/K
. Then:

1.

L^{pi}=K\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm coef}(f_{i})^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}}\bigg)
where
{\rm coef}(f_{i}):=\{\lambda^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}}\,|\,\lambda\in{\rm coef}(f_{i})\}
.

2.

L/L^{pi}
is a Galois field extension which is generated by the roots of the polynomials
\{f_{i}\,|\,i\in I\}
or, equivalently, by the roots of separable polynomials
\{{f_{i}^{sep}}^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}})\,|\,i\in I\}\subseteq L^{pi}[x]
(this follows from (
6
)).

3.

L^{gal}=K\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm roots}(f_{i}^{sep})\bigg)
.

4.

L/L^{gal}=L^{gal}\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm roots}(f_{i})^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}}\bigg)

is a purely inseparable field extension.

5.

The field extension
L/L^{pi}
is a Galois field extension with Galois group
G(L/L^{pi})=G(L/K)=G(L^{gal}/K)
.

6.

L^{pi}=L^{G(L/L^{pi})}=L^{G(L/K)}=L^{G(L^{gal}/K)}
.

7.

L^{gal}=L^{sep}=L^{{\cal D}(L/K)_{+}}
.

Proof.

1, 2 and 5. Clearly,
L^{pi}\supseteq L^{pi}_{c}:=K\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm coef}(f_{i})^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}}\bigg)
and the field extension

L/L^{pi}_{c}=L^{pi}_{c}\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm roots}\Big({f_{i}^{sep}}^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}}\Big)\bigg)

is a Galois field extension as it is a splitting field of the set of separable polynomials
\Big\{{f_{i}^{sep}}^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}})\,|\,i\in I\Big\}\subseteq L^{pi}_{c}[x]
. Since
L_{c}^{pi}\subseteq L^{pi}\subseteq L
,
L^{pi}/L^{pi}_{c}
is a purely inseparable field extension and
L/L_{c}^{pi}
is a Galois field extension, we must have
L_{c}^{pi}=L^{pi}
. So,
L/L^{pi}
is a Galois field extension. Notice that
G(L/L^{pi})\subseteq G(L/K)
. The field extension
L^{pi}/K
is a purely inseparable finite field extension. Therefore,
L^{pi}/K
is a normal field extension with
G(L^{pi}/K)=\{e\}
. Therefore,

G(L/K)=G(L/L^{pi})
.

The finite field extension
L/K
is normal. By Lemma
5.1
,
L=L^{pi}\otimes L^{gal}
. Therefore,
G(L/L^{pi})=G(L^{gal}/K)
.

3 and 4. Clearly,
L^{gal}\supseteq L^{gal}_{r}:=K\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm roots}(f_{i}^{sep})\bigg)
and the field extension
L^{gal}_{r}/K
is a Galois finite field extension. Notice that

L/L^{gal}_{r}=L^{gal}_{r}\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm roots}(f_{i})^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}}\bigg).

Therefore
L^{gal}=L^{gal}_{r}
. Hence,

L^{gal}=K\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm roots}(f_{i}^{sep})\bigg)
and
L/L^{gal}=L^{gal}\bigg(\bigcup_{i\in I}{\rm roots}(f_{i})^{\frac{1}{p^{n_{i}}}}\bigg)

is a purely inseparable field extension.

6. By statement 3, the finite field extension
L/L^{pi}
is a Galois field extension with Galois group
G(L/L^{pi})=G(L/K)
. By the Galois Theorem,
L^{pi}=L^{G(L/L^{pi})}=L^{G(L/K)}=L^{G(L^{gal}/K)}
.

7. The field extension
L/K
is a normal finite field extension. Therefore,
L^{gal}=L^{sep}
. By Theorem
5.2
,
L^{sep}=L^{{\cal D}(L/K)_{+}}
. ∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
p\neq 2
be a prime number and
K=\mathbb{F}_{p}(x,y)
be a field of rational functions in the variables
x
and
y
, and
L=K(y^{\frac{1}{p}},x^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Then:

1.

The finite field extension
L/K
is normal,
L=L^{pi}\otimes L^{gal}
where
L^{pi}=K(y^{\frac{1}{2}})
and
L^{gal}=K(x^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

2.

{\cal F}(L/K)={\cal N}(L/K)=\{K,L^{pi},L^{gal},L\}
, i.e. all subfields of
L/K
are normal. Furthermore,
{\cal F}(L/K)=\{M^{pi}\otimes M^{gal}\,|\,M^{pi}\in{\cal F}(L^{pi}),M^{gal}\in{\cal F}(L^{gal})\}
where
{\cal F}(L^{pi})=\{K,L^{pi}\}
and
{\cal F}(L^{gal})=\{K,L^{gal}\}
.

3.

(Analogue of the Galois correspondence for (normal) subfields of
L/K
)

The map

\displaystyle{\cal F}(L/K)={\cal N}(F/K)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle{\cal A}(E(L/K),L,F,G(L/K)),\;\;M\mapsto C_{E(L/K)}(M)={\cal D}(L/M)\rtimes G(L/M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{E(L^{pi}/K)}(M^{pi})\otimes C_{E(L^{gal}/K)}(M^{gal})=E(L^{pi}/M^{pi})\otimes E(L^{gal}/M^{gal})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal D}(L^{pi}/M^{pi})\otimes\Big(L^{gal}\rtimes G(L^{gal}/M^{gal})\Big)

is a bijection with inverse

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle C_{E(L/K)}(A)=\Big(L^{pi}\Big)^{A\cap{\cal D}(L^{pi}/K)_{+}}\otimes\Big(L^{gal}\Big)^{A\cap G(L^{gal}/K)},

\displaystyle\bigg(A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal D}(L^{pi}/N)\otimes\Big(L^{gal}\rtimes G(L^{gal}/\Gamma)\Big)\mapsto\Big(L^{pi}\Big)^{{\cal D}(L^{pi}/N)_{+}}\otimes\Big(L^{gal}\Big)^{G(L^{gal}/\Gamma)}.\bigg)

where

{\cal D}(L^{pi}/M^{pi})=\begin{cases}{\cal D}(L^{pi}/K)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{p-1}L^{pi}\partial_{y}^{j}&\text{if }M^{pi}=K,\\
L^{pi}&\text{if }M^{pi}=L^{pi},\\
\end{cases}

and

G(L^{gal}/M^{gal})=\begin{cases}G(L^{gal}/K)=\{e,\sigma\}&\text{if }M^{gal}=K,\\
\{e\}&\text{if }M^{gal}=L^{gal},\\
\end{cases}

where
\partial:=\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\frac{1}{p}}}\in{\rm Der}_{K}(K(y^{\frac{1}{p}}))
and
\sigma(x^{\frac{1}{2}})=-x^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

1 and 2. Clearly,
L=K(y^{\frac{1}{p}})\otimes K(x^{\frac{1}{2}})
where
K(y^{\frac{1}{p}})/K
is a purely inseparable finite field extension and

K(x^{\frac{1}{2}})
is a Galois finite field extension with Galois group
G(K(x^{\frac{1}{2}})/K)=\{e,\sigma\}
where
\sigma(x^{\frac{1}{2}})=-x^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Therefore
L=L^{pi}\otimes L^{gal}
is a normal finite field extension where
L^{pi}=K(y^{\frac{1}{p}})
and
L^{gal}=K(x^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Since the degrees
[L^{pi}:K]=p
and
[L^{gal}:K]=2
are prime numbers,
{\cal F}(L^{pi})=\{K,L^{pi}\}
and
{\cal F}(L^{gal})=\{K,L^{gal}\}={\cal N}(L^{gal})
.
Therefore,

{\cal N}(L/K)=\{M^{pi}\otimes M^{gal}\,|\,M^{pi}\in{\cal F}(L^{pi}),M^{gal}\in{\cal F}(L^{gal})\}=\{K,L^{pi},L^{gal},L\}.

To finish the proof we have to prove that
{\cal F}(L/K)={\cal N}(L/K)
. Let
M
be a proper subfield
of the field extension
L/K
. We have to show that
M/K
is a normal field extension. Since

[L:K]=[L^{pi}\otimes L^{gal}:K]=[L^{pi}:K][L^{gal}:K]=2p

and 2 and
p
are distinct prime numbers and
[M:K]|2p
, there are two options either
[M:K]=2
or otherwise
[M:K]=p
. In both cases the degree
[M:K]
is a prime number. Therefore, the field extension
M/K
is generated by a single element, say
a\in L\backslash K
, i.e.
M=K(a)
. Since
L=L^{pi}\oplus L^{pi}x^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have that
a=\alpha+\beta x^{\frac{1}{2}}
for some elements
\alpha,\beta\in L^{pi}
.

Suppose that
\beta=0
. Then the element
a=\beta\in L^{pi}\backslash K
, and so
M=K(a)=L^{pi}\in{\cal N}(L/K)
.

Suppose that
\beta\neq 0
. Then

x^{\frac{p}{2}}=x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}x^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\in K.

Since

M\ni a^{p}=\alpha^{p}+\beta^{p}x^{\frac{p}{2}}=\alpha^{p}+\beta^{p}x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}x^{\frac{1}{2}},

\alpha^{p},\beta^{p}x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\in K
and
\beta^{p}x^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\neq 0
, we must have
x^{\frac{1}{2}}\in M
. Therefore,
M=K(x^{\frac{1}{2}})=L^{gal}\in{\cal N}(L/K)
.

3. Recall that
G(L^{gal}/K)=\{e,\sigma\}
. It is well-known and easy to prove that
{\cal D}(L^{pi}/K)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{p-1}L^{pi}\partial^{j}
:

S:=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}L^{pi}\partial^{j}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{p-1}L^{pi}\partial^{j}\subseteq{\cal D}(L^{pi}/K)\subseteq E(L^{pi}/K)

and
{\rm dim}_{K}(S)=p^{2}={\rm dim}_{K}(E(L^{pi}/K))
, we deduce that
S={\cal D}(L^{pi}/K)=E(L^{pi}/K)
.
Now. statement 3 follows from Theorem
1.1
.(2) and Theorem
1.2
.(2).

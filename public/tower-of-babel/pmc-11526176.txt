DFT simulations of the elastic, optoelectronic, and thermoelectric attributes of AOsCl3 (A = K, Rb), a robust and environmentally friendly perovskites for green energy implications
Authors: Danish Abdullah, Dinesh C. Gupta
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Halide perovskites are an intriguing renewable energy materials that may assist in addressing the world’s energy scarcity. The Goldsmith tolerance factor (0.99 and 1.00) and negative formation energy guarantee the examined materials’ structural and thermodynamic stabilities. The Poisson’s and Pugh’s ratio proves the reviewed materials’ ductile nature. K/RbOsCl3 has electronic band gaps of 1.37 eV/1.39 eV and the highest light absorption in the ultraviolet and visible areas, enhancing its efficacy for solar cells and other optoelectronics technologies. The highest Seebeck coefficient and electrical conductivity render these materials feasible for thermoelectric properties. KOsCl3 and RbOsCl3 exemplify figures of merit of 0.89 and 0.88, respectively, at the ambient temperature. Additionally, the Debye model was implemented to figure out thermodynamic parameters such as heat capacity, thermal expansion, Debye temperature, and Grüneisen parameter.

Introduction

Researchers are triggered to dig into novel renewable energy sources by the modernized international world’s rising demand for energy and limited resources availability. Solar power cells and thermoelectric devices can turn light and heat energy into electrical energy from two primary renewable energy sources: sunlight and waste heat. The selection of materials governs the optoelectronic and thermoelectric devices’ reliability. Owing to numerous applications and peculiar chemical and physical traits in solid-state chemistry and materials physics, materials with a perovskite structure have piqued a lot of fascination1–17. Perovskite materials exhibit a simple crystal structure, i.e. ABX318–28, with miraculously great flexibility, and the predominantly structurally and chemically adjustable character of the crystal lattice renders it feasible to adjust it with an array of components with multiple physical characteristics. To gain insight into the numerous captivating characteristics of these perovskite materials, the B-site selection is vital. Ions with small radii and low coordination numbers are perfectly suited for the right spot. Rare-earth ions or transition/non-transition metals typically inhabit position B29. Despite many types of perovskites, cubic perovskite crystals possess the most robust and excellent structure, crystallizing with a space group of Pm-3 m (# 221). Developments on transition metal perovskites, both theoretical and experimental, have received a lot of curiosity recently. The special qualities emanating from their huge band gaps have prompted interest in the modern semiconductor industry for these chloride perovskite materials. Applications for these materials encompass gigabit memory, photovoltaic, high-temperature superconductors, piezoelectric devices, massive magneto-resistance appliances, photoluminescence, etc12–17. The scientific community has sought and reported on many fluoride compounds via both computational and experimental techniques due to their relevance for these kinds of appliances. A researcher has investigated KXF3 (X = Ru, Os)30 based on the literature survey. The researcher validated the ductility of the KRuF3 and KOsF3 materials by estimating their Poisson’s and Pugh’s ratios. They concluded the investigation that both of the materials confirmed versatility for deployment in a variety of upcoming optoelectronic devices by reviewing the results of the absorption coefficient and optical conduction in the UV energy spectrum. Moreover, The structural, dynamic, and magnetic properties of the KCoF3 and KFeF331 perovskites were determined utilizing density functional theory along with the Hubbard correction coefficient for the cobalt (Co) and iron (Fe) atoms. By figuring out their negative enthalpies of formation at the most stable state and under pressure up to 30 GPa, the structural stability of these compounds was confirmed. By computing the phonon dispersions along the high symmetry lines in the first Brillouin region, their dynamic stability was verified. Also, Wien2K with PBE and mBJ energy exchange potentials were used to study the structural, electronic, optical, thermoelectric, and thermodynamic features of CsZnX3 (X = F, Cl, Br, and I)32 cubic perovskite compounds. All compounds are stable, according to the findings of the cohesive energy, formation energy, and phonon vibrational frequency measurements. CsZnX3 (X = Cl, Br, I) semiconductors are categorized as p-types due to their electronic structure and Seebeck coefficient values that are positive. Reduced absorption of visible and infrared light indicated high transparency. Over the 300–800 K temperature range, the studied compounds have a high figure of merit (zT) and power factor that surpasses 0.7. Additionally, Abrar Nazir et al. explored InGeX3 (X = F, Cl)33, where the ionic nature may be determined by measuring the Cauchy pressure, and Pugh’s ratio describes the ductile character of the compounds. Optical attributes such as reflectivity, optical conductivity, refractive index, extinction coefficient, and absorption coefficient are calculated in the energy range of 0–10 eV. These compounds could serve as ideal choices for optoelectronic devices due to their broad-range absorption in the UV spectrum. It is possible to calculate the transport characteristics against temperature, including the Seebeck coefficient, thermal and lattice conductivity, electrical conductivity, and figure of merit zT (0.99, 0.98). These anticipated results claimed that both compounds would make stunning thermoelectric application candidates. Because of their strong thermoelectric response, these compounds can be substituted in thermoelectric devices with alternative renewable energy sources.

We are motivated to do a thorough investigation and assess the electronic, structural, elastic, thermoelectric, and optical features of AOsCl3 (A = K, Rb), which are yet unknown, in light of the possible uses of halide perovskites in contemporary technology. Since the Tran and Blaha modified Becke-gradient potential is typically reliable in computing most properties under the context of density functional theory (DFT), it was chosen to conduct the investigation. Understanding of these crucial materials will be gathered through this theoretical effort, which will serve as a future resource for theorists and experimentalists.

Computational methodology

The current findings were accomplished through the integration of the WIEN2k simulation code and the full potential linear augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) concept within the general constraints of DFT34,35. The relaxed lattice constant was acquired by optimizing the crystal energy, and then the optimized parameters served for assessing the electronic, elastic, and other properties. The generalized gradient approximation with Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (GGA-PBE) parameterization is integrated to identify the exchange and correlation potential36. GGA formulism probably undervalues the exchange-correlation impact. There are, however, several other approaches to get beyond the restrictions of GGA, such as adding Hubbard/Coulomb (U) or a modified version of Becke-Johnson potentials (mBJ) to GGA. For the correlated system, the U-parameter is semi-empirically defined. However, mBJ is a solid option if one wishes to stick to the ab-into framework which might result in exact band profiles37. To construct a more realistic band profile, we opted to apply mBJ potential in the present scenario. The unit cell is partitioned into muffin tin spheres with radius RMT and interstitial spaces according to the FP-LAPW system. Wave functions employed within non-overlapping muffin tin spheres are atomic-like wave functions (radial functions times spherical harmonics), and a plane wave basis set is engaged for interstitial space. Muffin tin radii are appointed in a way that preserves there is no electron leakage from the core into the interstitial area, facilitating the convergence of total energy. Between the valence states and the core, an energy threshold of -6.0 Ry is adjusted numerically. For energy convergence, the cut-off parameter RMTKmax, which specifies the size of the basis set, was configured to 7. Here, RMT signifies the smallest muffin tin sphere radii, and Kmax is the maximum k-value in momentum space, or plane wave cutoff, which is employed in the plane wave expansion. The maximum orbital angular momentum (l) for wave function extension inside muffin tin spheres is lmax=10. A 3000k point mesh was implemented for the Brillouin zone (BZ) insertion in momentum space. The charge difference between two consecutive cycles should be less than 10 − 4e, while the convergence limit for energy was set to 10 − 4 Ry. The calculation of elastic constants, which can be extracted by implementing the Cubic-elastic package embedded in the Wien2k package, is applied to investigate the mechanical properties of halides. Applying a quasi-harmonic Debye model, thermodynamic characteristics and their relationships to temperature and pressure have been ascertained.

Conclusions

Implementing DFT computations, the optoelectronic, structural, thermoelectric, and elastic traits of the halide perovskite compounds KOsCl3 and RbOsCl3 were thoroughly examined within the associated parameters of this study. Conclusions reveal that both compounds have anisotropy, toughness, mechanical resilience, structural stability, and resistance to plastic deformation. Furthermore, the thermodynamic stability of each compound has been verified by applying DFT. Goldsmith’s stability index serves to enhance the assessment of structural stability; values close to unity signify a durable cubic perovskite structure. By applying the accurate TB-mBJ approximation, the electronic features are carefully revealed, exhibiting small band gaps of 1.37 and 1.39 eV for KOsCl3 and RbOsCl3, respectively. With the assistance of optical assessments, one may acquire an extensive comprehension of the behavior of the compounds in many aspects of their properties, including transparency at lower photon energies and significant absorption and transmission at higher energy levels. Furthermore, the BoltzTraP Code has been deployed for assessing the thermoelectric properties of RbOsCl3 and KOsCl3. The outcome indicates that these materials are recommended for thermoelectric applications, with anticipated figure of merit (zT) values of 0.88 and 0.89, respectively.

High prevalence of urogenital schistosomiasis among schoolchildren in an irrigated area in Gezira State, Sudan: a brief report
Authors: Hala Taha Elbashir, Bakri Yousf Mohamed Nour
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUrogenital schistosomiasis remains a major public health problem in irrigated areas of Sudan. We conducted a cross-sectional descriptive survey to determine the prevalence and intensity of Schistosoma haematobium infection and associated risk factors among basic schoolchildren in Althawra Mobi Village, Wad Madani Alkobra Locality, Gezira State. A total of 261 children aged 5–16 years were examined using urine centrifugation and microscopy. The overall prevalence was 44% (115/261), with most infections classified as light intensity (102/115). Infection was markedly higher among boys (96.5%) than among girls (3.5%). Frequent contact with irrigation canals for swimming, bathing, washing, and watering animals was significantly associated with infection, despite tap water being the main source of drinking water. These findings confirm ongoing transmission of urogenital schistosomiasis in irrigated communities of Gezira and highlight the need for sustained preventive chemotherapy, health education, and improved sanitation to reduce exposure among school-age children.Keywords: Urogenital schistosomiasis, Schistosoma haematobium, Prevalence, Schoolchildren, Risk factors, Gezira State, Sudan

Introduction

Schistosomiasis is a neglected tropical disease that affects over 240 million people globally (World Health Organization 2026), with the highest burden in sub-Saharan Africa (Adenowo et al. 2015; Hong 2023). A global estimate suggests that more than 200 million individuals require treatment for this condition (World Health Organization 2021). In Sudan, both urogenital and intestinal schistosomiasis are endemic, particularly in irrigated agricultural schemes where frequent human–water contact occurs, as indicated by several studies (Hajissa et al. 2018). School-age children are the most affected group due to recreational and domestic activities in infested water, resulting in chronic morbidity and impaired development (Alsaafin et al. 2024). Gezira State, home to one of the world’s largest irrigation schemes, has long been recognized as a hotspot for urogenital schistosomiasis (el Gaddal 1985; Amin and Abubaker 2017). Although previous control efforts substantially reduced prevalence (el Gaddal 1985), transmission persists in many communities (Ahmed 2022). Nationwide Sudan–Korea school-based surveys conducted in 2017 and 2019 examined over 105,000 schoolchildren across 18 states, identified persistent schistosomiasis hotspots in irrigated areas such as Gezira, confirmed the predominance of S.haematobium, and demonstrated associations between infection and water contact and sanitation factors.

Although these studies provide a robust methodological foundation and empirical evidence to guide mass drug administration and integrated control strategies in Sudan, population displacement and disruption of control programs following the war underscore the need for updated local-level data to reassess current transmission patterns. The present study, therefore, addresses remaining gaps in localized prevalence and risk factor characterization. This brief report describes the prevalence, intensity, and key risk factors for urogenital schistosomiasis among schoolchildren in an irrigated village in Gezira State. Importantly, this study was conducted before the onset of the armed conflict in Sudan in 2023. The findings therefore represent baseline pre-conflict data, providing an essential reference point for assessing the impact of subsequent health system disruptions on schistosomiasis transmission dynamics in Gezira State.

Materials and methods

A cross-sectional descriptive study was conducted in Althawra Mobi Village, Wad Madani Alkobra Locality, Gezira State. The village is surrounded by irrigation canals used for domestic and recreational activities. A total of 261 basic schoolchildren aged 5–16 years were selected using simple random sampling from two nearby schools (one for boys and one for girls). Children who had received praziquantel within the previous six months were excluded. Midday urine samples were collected between 11:00 and 13:00 h after light physical activity. Each child provided a urine sample in a clean collection bottle, from which a 10 ml aliquot was measured using a graduated liquid measuring device and transferred into a 15 mL centrifuge tube. Samples were centrifuged at 5000 g for 5 min. Following centrifugation, the supernatant was carefully decanted, leaving the sediment (pellet) at the bottom of the tube. The pellet was collected using a manual pipette, placed onto a clean glass slide, covered with a coverslip, and examined microscopically for Schistosoma haematobium eggs. Infection intensity was classified according to World Health Organization criteria (World Health Organization 2011; Wiegand et al. 2021) as light (1–49 eggs/10 ml) or heavy (≥ 50 eggs/10 ml). Sociodemographic characteristics and water-contact behaviours were assessed using structured questionnaires. Data were analyzed using SPSS version 23. Written informed consent was obtained from parents/guardians and from the participating children; forms were signed or thumb-printed as appropriate. Ethical approval was obtained from the Health-Sector Ethical Review Committee, University of Gezira (Protocol No.: 8–23; approved on 1 March 2023).

Results and discussion

The overall prevalence of urogenital schistosomiasis was 44% (115/261), indicating active transmission in the study area. This prevalence aligns with national hotspots identified in ecological zone mapping (e.g., irrigated near-water areas > 10–40%), emphasizing need for MDA (Ahmed 2022; Alsaafin et al. 2024). Most infections were of light intensity (102/115), while 13 children had heavy infections (Fig. 1).

This prevalence is comparable to reports from other irrigated areas of Gezira and reflects sustained transmission. Infection was disproportionately higher among boys, who accounted for 96.5% of positive cases (Fig. 2).

Percentage of males and females (n = 115) positive for S.haematobium infection

This gender difference is likely explained by behavioural factors, as boys more frequently engage in swimming, fishing, and other activities in irrigation canals, whereas girls have more restricted mobility. Although tap water was the main drinking water source (90.0%), irrigation canals were frequently used for bathing, swimming, and other household activities, whereas hand pumps and river water were used by only a small proportion of schoolchildren (9.5% and 0.5%, respectively), data not shown.

These activities represent direct and repeated water contact and remain the principal drivers of transmission; similar observations have been reported in other schistosomiasis-endemic irrigated settings (Sulieman et al. 2017; Elsiddig et al. 2019). Despite the availability of school latrines, sanitation was inadequate, and open urination near canals was reported, further sustaining transmission. Knowledge about schistosomiasis transmission was limited, with several misconceptions reported by participants (data not shown). Behavioural analysis revealed that 47.9% of schoolchildren engaged in bathing and swimming in canals, the activity most strongly associated with S.haematobium transmission, followed by accompanying animals for watering (21.3%), thereby increasing children’s direct contact with infested water (Fig. 3).

Percentage of school children (n = 261) and their activities that put them at risk of S.haematobium infection

Water contact behaviors among schoolchildren, particularly bathing and swimming in canals (47.9%), represent the dominant transmission pathway for S. haematobium in irrigation schemes, consistent with epidemiological patterns reported across Sudan and other endemic settings, which identifies frequent freshwater exposure and inadequate sanitation as key drivers of infection (Sulieman et al. 2017; Hajissa et al. 2018; Cha et al. 2019; Elsiddig et al. 2019; Ahmed 2022; Hong 2023; World Health Organization 2026). These findings underscore the need for targeted health education focusing on high-risk water contact behaviours as critical intervention target. The overall findings underscore that access to safe drinking water alone is insufficient to interrupt transmission when irrigation canals remain central to daily life. Integrated control approaches combining regular preventive chemotherapy, health education, improved sanitation, and snail control are urgently needed.

It should also be noted that the study area had previously been included in praziquantel-based mass drug administration (MDA) campaigns. The persistence of a high prevalence despite these interventions most likely reflects continued exposure and frequent reinfection driven by intense human–water contact in irrigated schemes rather than treatment failure. However, in the absence of post-treatment efficacy assessments, the possibility of reduced praziquantel sensitivity or emerging resistance cannot be entirely excluded. This observation underscores the need for complementary control measures beyond MDA, particularly in irrigated agricultural settings, as well as continued surveillance of drug efficacy.

Study constraints

Species identification in this study relied on standard urine microscopy. Molecular analyses, including PCR and sequencing for confirmation of Schistosoma species, could not be undertaken because all biological samples were lost during the destruction of the Blue Nile National Institute for Communicable Diseases amid the armed conflict in Sudan. Consequently, further laboratory verification was not feasible. In addition, the cross-sectional design restricts assessment of longitudinal trends and reinfection patterns.

Conclusion

This brief report documents a high prevalence of urogenital schistosomiasis among schoolchildren in an irrigated area in Gezira State, Sudan. Persistent water contact with irrigation canals, particularly among boys, water contact behaviors among schoolchildren, particularly bathing and swimming in canals (47.9%), represent the dominant transmission pathway for S.haematobium in irrigation schemes. Sustained and integrated control measures are required to reduce transmission and protect vulnerable school-age populations. Moreover, the interruption of annual MDA programs due to civil conflict and reduced access to medication has exacerbated the situation, underscoring the urgent need for international attention and support.

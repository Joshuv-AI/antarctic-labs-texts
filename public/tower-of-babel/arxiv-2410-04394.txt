Dylan J. Altschuler, Pandelis Dodos, Konstantin Tikhomirov and Konstantinos Tyros

Abstract
. A seminal open question of Pisier and Mendel–Naor asks whether every degree-regular graph which satisfies the classical discrete Poincaré inequality for scalar functions also satisfies an analogous inequality for functions taking values in any normed space with non-trivial cotype. Motivated by applications, it is also of great importance to quantify the dependence of the corresponding optimal Poincaré constant on the cotype q q . Works of Odell–Schlumprecht (1994), Ozawa (2004), and Naor (2014) make substantial progress on the former question by providing a positive answer for normed spaces which also have an unconditional basis, in addition to finite cotype. However, little is known in the way of quantitative estimates: the mentioned results imply a bound on the Poincaré constant depending super-exponentially on q q . We introduce a novel combinatorial framework for proving quantitative nonlinear spectral gap estimates. The centerpiece is a property of regular graphs that we call long-range expansion , which holds with high probability for random regular graphs. Our main result is that any regular graph with the long-range expansion property satisfies a discrete Poincaré inequality for any normed space with an unconditional basis and cotype q q , with a Poincaré constant that depends polynomially on q q , which is optimal. As an application, any normed space with an unconditional basis which admits a low-distortion embedding of an n n -vertex random regular graph must have cotype at least polylogarithmic in n n . This extends a celebrated lower bound of Matoušek for low distortion embeddings of random graphs into ℓ q \ell_{q} spaces.

Main results

The goal of this paper is to obtain a nearly optimal improvement of (

To this end, the following combinatorial property of (deterministic) regular graphs is a principal conceptual contribution of this paper. (For any graph

Definition 1.5

(Long-range expansion)
.

Let

be integers, and let

-regular graph on

(A)

\big|\{v\in[n]\colon{\rm dist}_{G}(v,S)\leqslant\ell\}\big|\geqslant\min\Big\{\frac{3n}{4},\alpha(d-1)^{\ell}\,|S|\Big\}.

(B)

For every nonempty subset

of

\alpha(d-1)^{\ell-1}|S|\leqslant\frac{3n}{4}

\big|\{e\in T\colon{\rm dist}_{G}(v,e)\leqslant\ell-1\}\big|\leqslant L(d-1-\varepsilon)^{\ell}.

Outline of the proof of Theorem

The first step is to reduce the validity of the Poincaré inequality (
1.4

(1.14)

\sum_{v\in V_{G}}\|f(v)\|_{X}\lesssim\sum_{\{w,w^{\prime}\}\in E_{G}}\|f(w)-f(w^{\prime})\|_{X},

for functions

which map to “binary” vectors and have zero as their empirical median. The advantage of this reduction is that, by the assumption that

has an unconditional basis, the norm of a binary vector is controlled by the location of its support; in particular, for an edge

and a vertex

f(w)-f(w^{\prime})
has bigger norm than the vector

contains the support of

. This reduction is established in Corollary

by noting that, if a

With this reduction at hand we proceed as follows. For every vertex
v\in V_{G}
and every coordinate

in the support of

that there is some distance

B(v,\ell_{v,j}-1)
and, moreover, the collection
(\tilde{E}(v,j))_{v}
is nearly disjoint for every fixed
j
. This is achieved in Lemma
4.6
using part (
B
) of the long-range expansion property.

For each distance
\ell
and each vertex
v\in V_{G}
, let
J(v,\ell):=\{j\colon\ell_{v,j}=\ell\}
denote the “scale
\ell
support” of
f(v)
. Moreover, for an edge
e=\{w,w^{\prime}\}\in E_{G}
, let
J(v,\ell,e):=\{j\in J(v,\ell)\colon e\in\tilde{E}(v,j)\}
denote the “scale
\ell
mutual support” of
f(w)-f(w^{\prime})
and
f(v)
. In Lemma
4.9
we show that for each scale
\ell
and each vertex
v\in V_{G}
, we can find a set of edges whose size is of order
(d-1)^{\ell}
such that for each edge in this set,
\|\chi_{J(v,\ell,e)}\|_{X}
is comparable to
\|\chi_{J(v,\ell)}\|_{X}
; we informally say that such an edge
compensates

v
at scale
\ell
.

In order to finish the proof, we combine our analysis along all scales
\ell
. To this end, we write

(1.15)

\sum_{v\in V_{G}}\|f(v)\|_{X}=\sum_{v\in V_{G}}\Big\|\sum_{\ell\geqslant 1}P_{J(v,\ell)}\big(f(v)\big)\Big\|_{X}\leqslant\sum_{\ell\geqslant 1}\sum_{v\in V_{G}}\big\|P_{J(v,\ell)}\big(f(v)\big)\big\|_{X}\,.

Thus, it is enough to bound the right-hand side of (
1.15
) by the contribution of edges in the right-hand side of (
1.14
). Our previous estimates only allow us to achieve this for a single scale
\ell
. We claim, however, that these estimates can be strengthened to be summable over
\ell
. The mechanism is the following dichotomy, made precise in Lemma
4.10
. As previously noted, each vertex has order
(d-1)^{\ell}
compensating edges at scale
\ell
.
The key issue is how often these edges are reused between the vertices. If the total number of edges that are used to compensate vertices at a given scale is sufficiently large, then the estimates coming from Lemma
4.9
together with a double-counting argument can be used to establish the summability over
\ell
. Otherwise, for the remaining scales, we can extract a smaller, but still sizable, set of edges each of which must be used to compensate many vertices. Since the sets
\tilde{E}(v,j)
are nearly disjoint (over
v
, for fixed
j
), the only way for an edge
e=\{w,w^{\prime}\}\in E_{G}
to be used to compensate many vertices at a given scale is for the vector
f(w)-f(w^{\prime})
to have very large support. And in this case, the bounds obtained by Lemma
4.9
can be significantly strengthened. This is achieved using Proposition
3.4
which relies on the fact that the space
X
has an unconditional
basis and non-trivial cotype. In either case, this dichotomy yields (
1.14
), completing the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

2.1.
Graph theoretic preliminaries

All graphs in this paper are simple; we allow multigraphs to have self-loops. If

we denote the set of its edges. As we have already mentioned, for any pair

of positive integers, by

we denote the set of all

-regular graphs on

.

2.1.1.
​

For any graph/multigraph
G
, by

we denote the shortest-path distance on

between vertices; by convention we set

if

are contained in different connected components of

. If

is a vertex of

and

2.1.2.
​

If

is a finite set, then a

of

is a collection of pairwise (vertex-)disjoint edges of the complete graph on

; notice, in particular, that the empty set is always a (partial) matching. A

of

is a matching such that every element of

belongs to some edge of the matching; note that there exist perfect matchings of

if and only if

is even.

2.2.
Banach space theoretic preliminaries

The standard basis vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
will be denoted by
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
. Given a vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, by
{\rm supp\,}(y):=\{j\in[k]\colon y_{j}\neq 0\}
we denote its support, and by

we denote the vector of absolute values of components of

; moreover, if

, then we set

, so that

by zeroing out all components indexed by

. Finally, for any

, we define the vector

by

.

Convention 2.1
.

With a slight abuse of terminology, we will say that a norm

in

is

if the standard vector basis in

is

.

2.3.
Asymptotic notation

If

are parameters, then we write

3.
Restricted cotype, and reductions

Definition 3.1

(Restricted cotype)
.

Let
q\geqslant 2
, and let
\mathrm{C}\geqslant 1
. We say that a finite collection
\{y_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
of vectors in a Banach space

has
restricted cotype
q
with constant
\mathrm{C}
if for every nonempty subset
A^{\prime}
of
A
,

\mathbb{E}\,\Big\|\sum_{\alpha\in A^{\prime}}r_{\alpha}y_{\alpha}\Big\|_{X}^{q}\geqslant\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}^{q}}\sum_{\alpha\in A^{\prime}}\|y_{\alpha}\|_{X}^{q},

where
(r_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
are i.i.d. Rademacher random variables.

We will apply the definition of restricted cotype to reduce the proof of Theorem
1.6
to the setting where the components of the vectors
f(v)
,
v\in[n]
, take values in
\{-1,0,1\}
.

Theorem 3.2

(Poincaré inequality for vectors in

Let

, let
\mathrm{C}\geqslant 1
, let

be an integer, let

, and set

\Pi=\Pi(q,\mathrm{C},d,\alpha,\varepsilon,L):=2^{113}\,q^{10}\,\mathrm{C}^{2}\,\Big(\frac{d^{24}\,L^{8}}{\alpha^{14}\,\varepsilon^{18}}\Big).

Let
G
be a

-regular graph on

\min\Big\{\big|\{v\in[n]\colon f(v)_{j}\geqslant 0\}\big|,\big|\{v\in[n]\colon f(v)_{j}\leqslant 0\}\big|\Big\}\geqslant\frac{n}{2}\ \ \ \text{ for every }j\in[k],

Corollary 3.3

Let the parameters

We first give the proof of (

\|(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\|_{X(\ell_{1}^{m})}:=\big\|(\|y_{1}\|_{\ell_{1}^{m}},\dots,\|y_{k}\|_{\ell_{1}^{m}})\big\|_{X}\ \ \ \text{for every}\ (y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{m}.

For every

•

If

•

If

As we have already remarked, the estimate (

)

adapted to the present setting;

Again, we fix the map

5.
Long-range expansion is typical: proof of Proposition
1.10

Our goal in this section is to present the proof of Proposition
1.10
.

Part (a) is proven by a direct computation in the configuration model. Given a (not too large) set

S
of vertices, we analyze the exploration process associated with running a breadth-first search from
S
.
The key quantity is
\Delta(S,\ell)
, the number of previously unexplored vertices revealed at step
\ell
.
Using an inductive argument, in Proposition
5.9
we obtain strong lower bounds on the growth of
\Delta(S,\ell)
,
until a (small) constant fraction of all vertices have been discovered. At this point, it is sufficient to
crudely bound the remainder of the exploration process using Cheeger’s inequality.
A main technical difficulty in this inductive strategy is actually the base case: if
d
is small—in particular,
d=3
is the core issue—then
|\Delta(S,\ell)|
is poorly concentrated for small values of
\ell
. This is resolved by invoking Cheeger’s inequality in Fact
5.7
and Corollary
5.8
to obtain weak lower bounds on
|\Delta(S,\ell)|
for small
\ell
. Once
\ell
is sufficiently large, the exploration process has encountered sufficiently many total vertices for
|\Delta(S,\ell)|
to be strongly concentrated.

For proving part (b) of Proposition
1.10
, we utilize a spectral argument. Notice that part (
B
) of the long-range
expansion property roughly states that for a large set
S
, it is not possible for a disproportionate fraction
of the walks of length
\ell
started from
S
to all end up in a small set
T
. Let
G
be a graph as in
part (b), and let
A_{G}
be its adjacency matrix; then the spectral gap of
A_{G}^{\ell}
can be lower bounded
in terms of the spectral gap of
A_{G}
. And, assuming the negation of part (
B
),
we explicitly construct a vector which certifies a small spectral gap for

, yielding a contradiction.

Before we proceed to the details, we first present some basic facts concerning random regular graphs.

]

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{1,\dots,|\mathbf{X}|\}\\
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{out}}\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}\geqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{1,\dots,|\mathbf{X}|\}\\
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{out}}\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\{1,\dots,|\mathbf{X}|\}\\
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{out}}\end{subarray}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}=\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}+|\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{in}}|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eqkt19}}}{{=}}|\mathbf{X}|-2\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}+m-|\mathbf{X}|=m-2\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}.

Hence,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big[\big|\Delta_{\mathbf{G}}(R,1)\big|\geqslant\theta|\mathcal{A}|\,\Big|\,\mathcal{M}_{\pi}\Big]

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}>\theta m\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{M}_{\pi}\bigg]\geqslant\mathbb{P}\bigg[m-2\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}>\theta m\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{M}_{\pi}\bigg]

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}\geqslant\frac{1-\theta}{2}m\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{M}_{\pi}\bigg].

Claim 5.4
.

For every integer
t\geqslant 0
, we have

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}\geqslant t\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{M}_{\pi}\bigg]\leqslant\binom{m}{t}\Big(\frac{m}{n-2|R|}\Big)^{t}.

Proof of Claim
5.4
.

This estimate is essentially known; see, e.g.,
[
LS10
, Lemma 2.1]
. We recall the argument for the
convenience of the reader.

For every
i\in[m]
let
\mathbf{Y}_{i}
denote the boolean random variable defined by setting
\mathbf{Y}_{i}=1
if

i\leqslant|\mathbf{X}|
and
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}=1
; otherwise, set
\mathbf{Y}_{i}=0
.
Notice that, everywhere on
\mathcal{M}_{\pi}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{X}|}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\complement}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{Y}_{i};

moreover, for
i\in[m]
and every
E\in\sigma\big((\mathbf{Y}_{j})_{j<i}\big)
,

\mathbb{P}\big[[\mathbf{Y}_{i}=1]\,\big|\,E\cap\mathcal{M}_{\pi}\big]\leqslant\frac{d|\mathcal{A}|}{dn-|\cup\pi|-2|\mathcal{A}|}\leqslant\frac{m}{n-2|R|},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
|\cup\pi|+|\mathcal{A}|=d|R|
. The claim follows from this
observation and a union bound.
∎

The proof of the lemma is now completed by combining (
5.6
) and Claim
5.4
.
∎

Proposition 5.9

Set

(5.12)

Proof.

Corollary 5.12
.

Set

(5.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}:=\Big\{G\in G(n,d)\colon

Proof.

Proof of part (b)

of integers such that

, and the adjacency matrix of

.

Recall that our goal is to show that the graph

satisfies part (

) of property

\mathrm{Expan}(\alpha,\varepsilon,L)
, where

are as in
(
1.10
)
,

(
1.11
)

)
, respectively. To this end, we fix a nonempty subset

and a

Assume towards a contradiction that this is not possible. That is,

.
We will need the following upper bound on

.

Fact 5.14
.

We have that

|T|\leqslant|S|\,\frac{d}{L(d-2)}\,\Big(\frac{d-1}{d-1-\varepsilon}\Big)^{\ell}.

We proceed by double counting. On the one hand, by the definition of

\sum_{v\in S,\,e\in T}\mathbbm{1}_{[\mathrm{dist}_{G}(v,e)\leqslant\ell-1]}=\sum_{v\in S}\big|\{e\in T\colon\mathrm{dist}_{G}(v,e)\leqslant\ell-1\}\big|\leqslant|S|\frac{d}{d-2}(d-1)^{\ell}.

The result follows by combining the above two inequalities.
∎

We will also need the following fact.

Fact 5.15
.

Let

with

and such that

. Then,

\bigg\|\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}A^{k}y\bigg\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant 4\big(4.41(d-1)\big)^{\ell}.

Proof.

Our assumptions imply that

. Hence,

\bigg\|\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}A^{k}y\bigg\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\big\|A^{k}y\big\|_{2}\bigg)^{2}\leqslant\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\Big)^{2}\,\lambda^{2\ell},

\sqrt{5}\leqslant\lambda\leqslant 2.1\sqrt{d-1}

Let

.

On the other hand, since

.
In particular,

∎

Define the vector

by the rule

and notice that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}A^{k}x\bigg\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(A^{k}x)_{v}\bigg)^{2}=\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\sum_{w\in[n]}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(A^{k})_{v,w}\,x_{w}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\sum_{w\in R}\sqrt{\frac{|R^{\complement}|}{|R|}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(A^{k})_{v,w}-\sum_{w\in R^{\complement}}\sqrt{\frac{|R|}{|R^{\complement}|}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(A^{k})_{v,w}\bigg)^{2}.

)
, we have that

\frac{L^{\frac{3}{2}}\,\sqrt{d-2}\,(d-1-\varepsilon)^{\frac{3\ell}{2}}}{4d^{\frac{3}{2}}\,\sqrt{|S|}\,(d-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}\geqslant\frac{d}{\sqrt{n}\,(d-1)}\,d^{\ell}.

Definition A.1

\mathrm{C}\geqslant 1
.

Step 1: passing to the
2
-convexification.
Let
X^{(2)}
denote the
2
-convexification of
X
; we refer to

[
LT79
, page 53]
for the definition of this construction, and we recall at this point that
X^{(2)}
also has
a
1
-unconditional basis. By
[
BL00
, Proposition 9.3]
(and after keeping track of the constants in its proof),
there exists a homeomorphism
F_{1}\colon S(X)\to S(X^{(2)})
such that for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in S(X)
we have

\frac{1}{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{X}\leqslant\|F_{1}(x_{1})-F_{1}(x_{2})\|_{X^{(2)}}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{X}^{1/2}
.

Step 2: renorming of
X^{(2)}
.
By
[
LT79
, pages 53 and 54]
, we have

M^{(2)}(X^{(2)})=1
and
M_{(2s)}(X^{(2)})\leqslant\sqrt{M_{(s)}(X)}
for every
s>q
; consequently,
by Lemma
A.4
,

M:=M_{(4q)}(X^{(2)})\leqslant\sqrt{M_{(2q)}(X)}\leqslant\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{1-e^{-1}}\,q\,\sqrt{\mathrm{C}}.

(In particular, if
M_{(q)}(X)=1
, then
M_{(2q)}(X^{(2)})=1
.)
By
[
FJ74
, pages 184 and 185]
, there exists an equivalent norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
on
X^{(2)}

such that the space
Y:=(X^{(2)},{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})
also has a
1
-unconditional basis
and it satisfies
M^{(2)}(Y)=M_{(4q)}(Y)=1
. Moreover, we have

\|x\|_{X^{(2)}}\leqslant{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|x\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leqslant M\|x\|_{X^{(2)}}
for every
x\in X^{(2)}
and, consequently,
there exists a homeomorphism
F_{2}\colon S(X^{(2)})\to S(Y)
such that

\frac{1}{2M}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{X^{(2)}}\leqslant{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|F_{2}(x_{1})-F_{2}(x_{2})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leqslant 2M\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{X^{(2)}}

for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in S(X^{(2)})
.

Step 3: homeomorphism of spheres.
Since
M^{(2)}(Y)=M_{(4q)}(Y)=1
, by Lemma
A.5
, we have that

\delta_{Y}(\varepsilon)\geqslant\frac{\varepsilon^{4q}}{q\,4^{4q+1}}
for every
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 2
and

\rho_{Y}(\tau)\leqslant 2\tau^{2}
for every
\tau>0
. On the other hand, by
[
BL00
, Lemma 9.5 and Proposition A.5]
,
there exists a homeomorphism
F_{3}\colon S(Y)\to S(\ell^{n}_{1})
such that

\frac{1}{8}\delta_{Y}\big({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\big)^{3}\leqslant\|F_{3}(y_{1})-F_{3}(y_{2})\|_{\ell^{n}_{1}}\leqslant\frac{\rho_{Y}(2{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|})}{{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
for every
y_{1},y_{2}\in S(Y)
.
Next observe that the
2
-convexification
(\ell^{n}_{1})^{(2)}
of
\ell^{n}_{1}
is isometric to
\ell^{n}_{2}
; thus, applying

[
BL00
, Proposition 9.3]
as in Step 1, we obtain a homeomorphism
F_{4}\colon S(\ell^{n}_{1})\to S(\ell^{n}_{2})
such that
for every
z_{1},z_{2}\in S(\ell^{n}_{1})
we have

\frac{1}{2}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{\ell^{n}_{1}}\leqslant\|F_{4}(z_{1})-F_{4}(z_{2})\|_{\ell^{n}_{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|_{\ell^{n}_{1}}^{1/2}
.

Summing up, setting
\phi:=F_{4}\circ F_{3}\circ F_{2}\circ F_{1}
,
we see that
\phi\colon S(X)\to S(\ell^{n}_{2})
is a homeomorphism that satisfies

\frac{\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{X}^{12q}}{q^{3}\,2^{60q+9}\,M^{12q}}\leqslant\|\phi(x_{1})-\phi(x_{2})\|_{\ell_{2}^{n}}\leqslant 2\sqrt{5}\sqrt[4]{2}\sqrt{M}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{X}^{1/4}

for every
x_{1},x_{2}\in S(X)
.​

Step 4: homeomorphism of balls.
By
[
OS94
, Proposition 2.9]
(and its proof), we have that
if
f\colon S(E)\to S(Z)
is a homeomorphism between the spheres of two Banach spaces
E
and
Z

that satisfies
\alpha\big(\|e-w\|_{E}\big)\leqslant\|f(e)-f(w)\|_{Z}\leqslant\beta\big(\|e-w\|_{E}\big)
for every
e,w\in S(E)
,
then there exists a homeomorphism
F\colon B(E)\to B(Z)
such that for every
e_{1},e_{2}\in B(E)
,

\bullet

\|F(e_{1})-F(e_{2})\|_{Z}\leqslant\|e_{1}-e_{2}\|_{E}+\max\Big\{2\|e_{1}-e_{2}\|_{E}^{1/4},\beta\big(2\|e_{1}-e_{2}\|^{1/2}_{E}\big)\Big\}
, and

\bullet

\|e_{1}-e_{2}\|_{E}\leqslant\|F(e_{1})-F(e_{2})\|_{Z}+\max\Big\{2\|F(e_{1})-F(e_{2})\|_{Z}^{1/4},\alpha^{-1}\big(2\|F(e_{1})-F(e_{2})\|_{Z}^{1/2}\big)\Big\}
.

The proof of Theorem
A.3
is then completed
by applying this result to the homeomorphism
\phi
obtained in Step 3.

Federated learning-enabled privacy-preserving framework for seizure forecasting and affective state analysis using multi-modal EEG-ECG data
Authors: V S Arulmurugan, R Aarthy, S Sesha Vidhya, Saranya K
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSeizure forecasting and affective state analysis using EEG-ECG data play a pivotal role in advancing neurological and mental health monitoring. However, existing methods such as Fed-Transformer, Res-1D CNN, and Fed-ESD suffer from privacy risks, inefficient feature extraction, and high computational overhead, limiting their effectiveness in real-world applications. To overcome these challenges, this study proposes NeuroFedSense, a novel Federated Learning-enabled Privacy-Preserving Framework that integrates a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) with an Attention Mechanism for accurate seizure forecasting and affective state analysis using EEG-ECG data, ensuring enhanced feature selection, interpretability and efficient decentralized training. The model leverages adaptive attention-based optimization and weighted feature selection to improve classification performance while ensuring data privacy. Implemented using TensorFlow, NeuroFedSense achieves 99.54% accuracy, 99.62% precision, 99.34% recall, and a 99.46% F1-score, outperforming Fed-Transformer (97.10% accuracy), Res-1D CNN (81.62% accuracy), and FML (99.10% accuracy). The ROC-AUC score of 0.99 further establishes its superiority over competing models. Additionally, the federated approach reduces energy consumption per node by 30% and optimizes communication efficiency by minimizing data transmission by 15% over 100 rounds. By ensuring high accuracy, improved privacy, reduced computational overhead, and enhanced energy efficiency, NeuroFedSense sets a new benchmark for decentralized, real-time seizure prediction and affective state monitoring. Introduction

Epileptic seizures are unpredictable neurological events that have a direct impact on the quality of life of the individuals. Predicting epileptic seizures with physiological data, which includes EEG and ECG, is performed early to carry out the necessary interventions and to enhance the level of patient safety. Also, recent clinical and cohort-based studies portray a considerable correlation between emotional stress and anxiety with seizure susceptibility, in which neurophysiological modifications of stress, e.g. autonomic imbalance and cortical excitability, make a contribution to the onset and intensity of seizures1–4, Kamakshi and Rengararaj Massive observational studies also suggest that new emotional dysregulation is the preictal in a large percentage of patients, which supports the practicality of considering affective biomarkers in seizure prediction systems. Federated Learning (FL) presents a hopeful approach by supporting collaborative model training from multiple institutions or devices without raw data sharing, maintaining privacy and enhancing prediction accuracy. Most current seizure prediction models are built using centralized Deep Learning (DL) architectures and demand large labeled data from diverse sources5–7. Yet, centralization methods create vital privacy threats and it is impractical because of data-sharing limitations within healthcare environments.

Moreover, most of the existing methods fail to considerate affective state analysis, which can give valuable insights to the emotional triggers related to seizures. Standard DL architectures, including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), tend to find it difficult to learn long-range dependencies in time-series physiological data, which in turn results in poor performance. To address these issues, this study proposed NeuroFedSense, FL-Enabled Privacy-Preserving Framework for Seizure Prediction and Affective State Analysis with multi-modal EEG-ECG data. NeuroFedSense can protect data privacy through the use of federated learning and make precise and reliable predictions. Further, the study also suggests the correlation that has been previously found among seizure activities and emotional distress and recommends counseling as a possible intervention measure. Unlike recurrent models, TCN-based architectures are more scalable and more efficient in time modeling, which makes them more appropriate to continuous analysis of EEG-ECG signals.

Research motivation

The issue of seizure prediction is one of the most important in neurological studies because the process of epileptic activity is unpredictable in nature. Previously existing methods (mainly before 2022), like traditional machine learning models (e.g. Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forests (RF), and early deep learning architectures (e.g. CNNs and RNNs), were mostly single-modal EEG-based methods. These approaches gave little attention to multimodal physiological integration and they did not take into consideration the dynamics of affective state like stress and anxiety. Even though the recent research has delved into multimodal EEG-ECG fusion, transformer-based architectures, the combination of both physiological and affective signals is still disjointed and lacking in integrated modeling formats. Therefore, a significant gap in the creation of integrated models to analyze both neurological and emotional biomarkers to predict seizures better exists.

Significance of the study

This paper reveals a statistically significant correlation between the emotional conditions and the events of seizures, which indicates the possible importance of the affective biomarkers in enhancing the prediction of the seizures.With TCN coupled with Attention Mechanism, the model is able to adequately grasp temporal relationships in EEG-ECG data. In contrast to traditional methods, this study identifies a causal relationship between seizure events and emotional states, indicating counseling’s potential as a pre-emptive intervention. The results carry far-reaching implications in terms of personalized epilepsy care, early seizure prevention, and privacy-centered AI application in medicine. The hypothesis of the study is that the ability to predict the onset of a seizure with the help of affective state indicators (e.g., stress and anxiety based on EEG-ECG measurements) in comparison to the models that involve the use of neurological signals exclusively can greatly enhance the quality of prediction.

Problem statement

Although machine learning and deep learning have made progress in seizure detection, most of the current methods are based on a centralized training paradigm which by default exposes sensitive medical information and thus limits their use to a clinical setting. Although privacy-preserving solutions like differential privacy and homomorphic encryption have been proposed, they still demand computationally expensive implementation into large-scale multimodal medical systems and are sufficiently computationally limited to make a practical implementation feasible. Specifically, in Federated Learning (FL) setups, there exist difficulties in entrusting privacy assurances, effectiveness of communication, and model effectiveness of processing heterogeneous EEG-ECG data. Thus, the research weakness, the focus of this paper, in particular, relates to the absence of an effective, scalable, and privacy-sensitive framework, which will incorporate multimodal physiological and affective responses into a federated environment. In this paper, two predictive tasks are defined: seizure prediction and affective state prediction: one independent and the other dependent. Although both tasks share common multimodal EEG-ECG input, they are differentiated by their goal of prediction: a seizure forecast predicts neural preictal patterns, whereas an affective state predicts neural emotional and autonomic changes, e.g. stress and anxiety. To prevent the situation of ambiguity, proposed framework models these tasks as the part of the ordered multi-task learning paradigm, which allows the shared representation learning and the task-specific prediction.

Recent innovation and challenges

Some of the recent developments in AI for healthcare include the use of self-supervised learning for EEG feature extraction, Transformer models for time-series forecasting, and FL for privacy-preserving medical AI applications. However, challenges still remain, such as data heterogeneity between institutions, computational expense of deep models, and the demand for interpretable AI in medical decision support. Additionally, existing FL frameworks are not equipped to deal with unbalanced medical information and dynamic patient conditions, so new architectures must be designed that improve model adaptability, stability, and clinical applicability.

Related works

Wang et al.8, created the Multi-modal Emotion Database with four modalities that collects EEG, photoplethysmography, speech and facial images from 32 participants while they watch emotional video stimuli under anechoic and natural environmental conditions. The researchers examined four baseline measurement methods which included I-vector + PLDA along with TCN and ELM and MLP. The EEG recognition results reached 88.92% for anechoic conditions and 89.70% for natural noise environments which showed higher performance compared to speech recognition. When features united with decisions at different stages the performance of AER increased by 31.74%. The dataset contains limited data points yet experiences variations from the environments it captures. Similarly, Yang et al.9, demonstrate how AI systems can merge health data with sensory data to implement automated hospital operations and telehealth solutions thus resolving epileptic seizure annotation difficulties. Researchers trained their advanced AI system using a combination data of EEG and ECG on Temple University Hospital data. Micro-level combinations between EEG data and ECG data boosted AUC-ROC results by 14.42% when compared alongside single-system EEG-only tests and by 6.71% compared to ECG-only testing results.

Although some previous studies have investigated EEG-ECG multimodal fusion in predicting seizures and analyzing affective states, most of the current methods base their work on early fusion (feature concatenation) or late fusion (decision-level aggregation), which are unable to efficiently model cross-modal interactions. These early fusion techniques tend to have redundant features and imbalance in dimensions whereas late fusion techniques do not make use of the temporal relationships among the neurological and autonomous signals. Besides, numerous models presuppose fixed correlations between EEG and ECG characteristics that do not reflect transient and time-dependent character of physiological reactions before seizures. These constraints hinder the possibility of using the existing structures to use multimodal information to robustly and meaningfully predict.

Yang et al.10, electroencephalography (EEG) provides valuable neurological evaluation due to its quick temporal responses yet its deployment can be complicated. An efficient approach for training cross-domain convolutional neural networks (CNNs) consisted of applying EEG–EEG and EEG–ECG transfer learning methods to boost prediction capabilities for seizures and sleep staging. The seizure model managed to detect both interictal and preictal periods by reaching 100% accuracy across nine patients using a 40-s training period. Transferring learnings from EEG to ECG improved sleep staging performance by 2.5% when compared to ECG alone and it cut training times down by more than half. Likewise, Ben Mbarek et al.11, have conducted research that uses threshold evaluation of heart rate variability features to measure and forecast pre-ictal ECG variations. The evaluation of seizure prediction relied on the analysis of heartbeat count standard deviation along with approximate entropy measurements. The measurement sensitivity achieved 100% while specificity maintained 97% and accuracy amounted to 97.5% in these experiments. The predictions lasted longer than ten minutes in 58.7% of recorded seizures. Automatic thresholding achieved 85% accuracy.

Zambrana-Vinaroz et al.12, developed a seizure predictive system by recording Ear EEG, ECG, and PPG signals with a wearable system that operates both in static and outpatient environments. Modelling techniques employed in clinical testing delivered supervised classification of patient states into three categories: normal, pre-seizure and seizure. A decreased version of Boosted Trees demonstrated 91.5% accuracy with a sensitivity rating of 85.4% in its results. The predictive system demonstrates potential value as an epilepsy prevention support tool but faces barriers when it comes to clinical implementation alongside physiological differences between individuals. Also, Zhu et al.13,14 proves that automatic seizure detection remains vital for epilepsy diagnosis and treatment. A fully automated DL model combined TCN with BiGRU in addition to an attention mechanism for seizure detection purposes. The analysis of raw EEG data includes artifact elimination without manual feature extraction which leads to post-processing through the usage of moving-average filtering together with thresholding and collar techniques. Using CHB-MIT database together with the SH-SDU database the proposed model delivered 98.77% and 93.61% accuracy for patient-specific evaluations and generated results of 93.78% and 91.37% for cross-patient tests. The processing time for one hour of EEG data amounted to 5.33 s.

The recent developments have proven that Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) are effective models in long-sequence modeling of physiological signals including EEG and ECG. In contrast to recurrent architectures (RNNs and LSTMs), which iteratively process sequences in order and are typically affected by the problem of vanishing gradients, TCNs use dilated causal convolutions that offer exponentially large receptive fields, with gradient flow also not decreasing. This facilitates effective recording of long term temporal variations that are important in detecting preictal EEG waveforms and autonomous changes in ECG records.

These advantages have been pointed out in a number of recent studies. Ding et al.15 have suggested the use of MASA-TCN as a method to perform emotion recognition in continuous EEG, which showed better performance in temporal features extraction and classification stability than models founded on LSTMs. Equally, Islam et al.1 applied deep temporal convolution framework as a multimodal physiological signal fusion method to obtain stronger performance and improved convergence. Moreover, hybrid TCN-based designs have demonstrated better results when coping with noisy and high dimensional biosignals because of their capacity to learn multi-scale time dependence effectively.

TCN architectures have superior parallelization, smaller training time, and scalability when compared to LSTM and GRU models, which are very appropriate in real-time healthcare applications involving continuous EEG-ECG monitoring.

Wang et al.16, demonstrates that iEEG channel selection remains critical for seizure warning but the growing computational requirements create substantial difficulties. The research introduces a new method that applies a channel reordering process alongside dual CNN-LSTM implementation. The process begins with singling out individual channels followed by F1-score-based channel ranking. After re-evaluation of signals containing incrementing channels the optimal channel combination gets selected. Based on testing with Freiburg and SWEC-ETHZ and AES-SPC datasets, the event-based sensitivity achieved 100% in all cases and false prediction rates measured 0.10/h, 0/h and 0.47/h. Real-world variability together with dataset dependency persist as main constraints for the model. Correspondingly, Saemaldahr et al.,17 developed a FL three-tier framework which solves epileptic seizure prediction challenges arising from limited data, different data variations and privacy issues. A two-level edge framework incorporates the Spiking Encoder combined with Spiking-GCNN as the local model which uses bi-timescale training. ANFIS operates as a predictive system which tailors its outputs to individual patient requirements. The CHB-MIT EEG dataset evaluation yielded 96.33% sensitivity while delivering 96.14% specificity. The implementation of FL delivers 96.28% accuracy yet faces two main hurdles in dealing with dataset diversity and real-world operational needs.

Ding et al.18 proposed Fed-ESD as a privileged FL framework that operates for epileptic seizure detection on fog-computing-based IoMT systems. The lightweight spatiotemporal transformer network preserves both spatial and temporal EEG information at local points to decrease exposure of data and eliminate communication expenses. The geographically dispersed fog nodes operate as local aggregators which together with an optimized coordinator selection approach prevent dependency on central servers during global aggregation. The experimental results show successful detection of seizures in addition to resource-conserving and stable operations and scalable performance. Also, Xiong et al.19, applied various machine learning models including KNN, decision tree, RF, naive Bayes, and SVM for PNES and ES classification using EEG and ECG recordings. Researchers analyzed video-EEG-ECG recordings from 16 patients who experienced 150 ES events together with data from 10 patients exhibiting 96 PNES events during the four preictal stages from 60–45 min through 15–0 min. Time-domain features were obtained from eighteen EEG channels together with one ECG channel. Random forest proved to be the most accurate at 87.83% through an analysis of the 15–0 min participants before their seizures. Using combined EEG and ECG monitoring produced a 1.46% increase in detection accuracy which rose to 87.83%.

A parallel comparison of Federated Learning models depicts significant trade-offs between privacy-sensitivity, computational efficiency, and scalability. The current methods (Fed-ESD and personalized FL models) already have the implements of secure aggregation and differential privacy, but usually add to the computational workload or complexity of their systems. Transformer-based FL models are powerful models with high learning ability but with high communication and processing cost, which makes it difficult to implement it in real-time. Lightweight FL architectures, instead, minimize the computational load but do not succeed in addressing the multimodal physiological signals. To overcome these shortfalls, the proposed NeuroFedSense framework proposes marriage of TCN-based temporal modeling with attention mechanism to achieve a balance between protection of privacy, the reduced communication overheads and enhanced scalability of the proposed framework in the real-world clinical scenario.

NeuroFedSense framework for seizure forecasting and affective state analysis

In contrast to traditional architectures, which assume a combination of the features through feature fusion or independent task modeling, NeuroFedSense proposes a learning paradigm that is temporally adaptive and is based on cross-modal feature interactions between EEG and ECG signals, which are dynamically learned with the help of attention mechanisms. Moreover, consideration of affective state analysis as part of the same predictive framework also allows the model to learn latent physiological relationships between emotional stress and seizure onset that is not considered in current federated learning-based healthcare models.

The NeuroFedSense is a FL-based model that uses TCN and Attention Mechanism to predict epileptic seizures and estimate the affective state using EEG-ECG signals, avoiding privacy invasion. Contrary to traditional multimodal fusion methods expecting to utilize the concatenation of the static features of the two modalities or the processing of the two modalities independently, the presented methodology uses attention-based fusion mechanism that dynamically allocates the significance to the EEG and ECG features depending on their time relevance. Multi-scale temporal fusion is made possible with the help of the TCN, which accounts for more complicated interactions between neurological and autonomic responses at varying time scales. Furthermore, the federated learning environment enables distributed multimodal learning between heterogeneous data streams without compromising one’s privacy, which also separates the offerings of the proposed solution, as compared to centralized fusion models. is presented in Fig. 1. The FL Aggregation facilitates decentralized training, wherein client devices (e.g., hospitals) update local models without exposing raw patient information, whereas a central server securely aggregates and optimizes the model for better generalization. The final output layer forecasts seizure activities and emotions, illustrating how NeuroFedSense facilitates enhancing explainable, privacy-assured real-time health surveillance.

Data acquisition & pre-processing

The collection of data in this research is based on multi-modal EEG and ECG data recordings of publicly available and clinically validated data sources, including such datasets as CHB-MIT and other wearable sensors data recordings. The dataset will consist of the recording of about N subjects (it will be filled with real dataset), with various demographic and clinical conditions. There are multi-channel EEG questions and synchronized ECG questionings at different durations ranging T1-T2 minutes/hours/subject to be included in each recording, guaranteeing enough time window on both seizure and affective condition.

The data also comprises the seizure and the non-seizure segmentation of the data to learn the seizure prediction, and the physiological based labeled affective states (stress, anxiety, neutral) used to obtain the labeled affective state. The sampling of the classes was done in a balanced or near-balanced way in order to avoid model bias due to the controlled sampling method.

To consider the inter-subject variability, the data of various patients will be subject to variations on seizure pattern, physiological reaction and recording conditions. This heterogeneity is very important in the assessment of the overall generalization ability of the proposed model. In addition, the data was divided into training, validation and testing set in case of the subject-independent split to make sure of the unbiased evaluation and elimination of data leakage.

To be robust and prevent the possibility of data leakage, strict separation strategy between training sets, validation sets and testing sets was used to partition the dataset. In case of any, subject-wide segregations were observed to avoid any overlap of physiological patterns across splits. Also, normalization and filtering were done separately in each partition to prevent the leakage of information trained on training data to test data. This makes sure that the performance measures reported by the individuals are based on generalization and not memorization.

Noise reduction & filtering (Bandpass filtering)

EEG and ECG signals are susceptible to several kinds of noise, such as powerline interference, baseline wander, muscle artifacts, and electrode motion artifacts. One frequent way to remove the noise is by bandpass filtering, which leaves the related signal frequencies while reducing unwanted parts. It is possible to employ a Butterworth bandpass filter, such that the useful range for EEG is 0.5 Hz to 45 Hz and for ECG 0.5 Hz to 100 Hz in order to remove the noise outside the range of usefulness. The cutoff frequency ranges are selected based on physiological established facts of EEG and ECG signals. In the case of EEG, the frequencies of 0.5 to 45 Hz are within clinically relevant frequency ranges (delta, theta, alpha, and beta rhythms) and this range of frequencies excludes high-frequency and muscle artifacts. In case of ECG, 0.5–100 Hz band maintains the fundamental cardiac characteristics including QRS complexes and heart rate variability elements and eliminates the interferences of baseline wander and high-frequency.

The Butterworth filter order has been selected to achieve a trade-off between sharp frequency selectivity and signal stability with a minimum amount of phase distortion as well as smooth passband properties. To prevent over-attenuation of the informative signal contents, a moderate filter order is used which is effective in redt ucing noise.

Comparative experiments were performed to confirm effectiveness of parameters chosen to be used as a filter with varying cutoff frequencies ranges and filter orders. The analyses were carried out on signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), feature retention, and classification accuracy at the downstream. The selected arrangement (EEG: 0.5 -45 Hz, ECG: 0.5 -100 Hz with moderate filter order) proved to be the best in terms of noise effective removal and preservation of important physiological characteristics to predict seizures and analyze affective state. These results support the appropriateness of the filtering strategy that is adopted with regard to the proposed framework.

Architecture of FL framework.

In realistic healthcare scenarios, the data obtained in various clients (e.g. hospitals or wearables) is heterogeneous by nature and usually non-identically distributed (non-IID) because of the differences in the patient demographics, sensor settings, and health conditions. This heterogeneity is partially considered in the proposed framework, by weighing the aggregation with FedAvg algorithm, the client updates are weighted by the local dataset size to avoid the bias of smaller datasets. Also, the model learns both invariant and discriminative features of various patterns of EEG-ECG, which allows the model to be robust to inter-client variability when using TCN-based temporal modeling and attention mechanisms.

The federated learning framework is tested in the given implementation with a small number of client nodes which stand on behalf of participating institutions. The arrangement is supposed to test the viability and efficiency of the suggested strategy in controlled settings. In practice, however, clinical applications in the real world have many more non-identically distributed (non-IID) clients. This architecture is scalable by design because of its decentralized nature, lightweight temporal modeling, and aggregation that is efficient in communication which results in the ability to be scaled to larger federated networks. The given model uses a single TCN-Attention architecture as a part of a federated learning system, where the process of extracting temporal features, adaptive weights on features, and decentralized optimization are executed.

Upon successful completion of the local training in each client, the new model parameters are sent back to the central server, where the FedAvg algorithm is utilized to compress and average the model updates. This weighted average method reduces the influence of heterogeneous data distributions among clients. As EEG-ECG signals change quite extensively under patient states, types of sensors, and seizure frequencies, FedAvg keeps the updates to the model in equilibrium and averts overfitting by individual client state. Following aggregation, the new global model is propagated to all the clients for the next local training process. This training process loops until model convergence, guarantees the global model generalizes well to different patient populations while maintaining data privacy.

Whereas FedAvg offers an effective and broadly used aggregation strategy, is also aware of being vulnerable to heterogeneity of data across clients, a typical property of medical data like EEG-ECG signals. Non-IID data distribution choices can be caused by variations in patient-specific physiological patterns, sensor arrangements, and seizure characteristics which may influence convergence stability and global model behavior.

To reduce the effects of data heterogeneity, sophisticated aggregation techniques, including FedProx, have been studied in the recent past. The FedProx terminates the local optimization goal with a proximal term to limit the overabundant divergence of client updates between the global model to enhance training stability in non-IID scenarios. Although the current implementation uses FedAvg, based on its computational efficiency and reduced communication overhead, it can also be considered that using FedProx or adaptive aggregation strategies is a promising future direction of making heterogeneous healthcare environments more robust. Regardless of such measures, it is admitted that FedAvg might not be able to deal with severe heterogeneity in non-IID medical data completely. Hence, the federalist optimization methods like FedProx, dynamic weighting schemes, and custom-based federated learning architectures are revealed as the possible future improvements to enhance robustness and convergence stability in strongly heterogenous settings.

Clinical interpretation & real-world implications

The given section confirms the research hypothesis which was proposed by examining the correlation between the changes in affective states and the probability of a seizure. One of the most important conclusions from the study is that patients who are likely to develop seizures often register increased emotional tension. The analysis of affective state using EEG and ECG indicates that such patients tend to display increased power of beta activity, reduced alpha power, and suppression of HRV—potent physiological measures of emotional strain. The linkage between the affective state and the incidence of seizures is modeled using a Bayesian probabilistic framework to measure the effect of stress in the risk of seizures, given in Eq. (20).

This points towards the possible causal connection between affective state and seizure onset, which implies that affective state monitoring can serve as an early warning system for seizures. where P(Seizure| Stress) is the posterior probability of seizure following observed indicators of stress. P(Stress|Given Seizure) refers to the probability of physiological changes associated with stress in the conditions of seizure-prone, P(Seizure) is the probability of occurrence of a seizure before. P(Stress), is the probability of the total likelihood of stress. This model allows probabilistic arguments about the effect of affective states on risk of seizures.

The observable indicators of affective state in the proposed framework would be the stress-related features based on EEG (e.g., high beta activity, low alpha power) and ECG (e.g., low HRV, high LF/HF ratio). In cases where these indicators are identified, the term of probability is high. P(Stress|Seizure) is higher, which raises the posterior probability of the occurrence of seizures. This is a probabilistic dependency, which enables the model to update dynamically the estimated risk of seizures depending on real-time physiological and emotional responses.This Bayesian formulation, in contrast to deterministic models, offers a way of interpreting how the information about the affective state is incorporated in seizure prediction, allowing clinicians to learn how stress has impacted the susceptibility to seizures and enabling early intervention measures to be taken.

Application

With the noted correlation between emotional distress and the occurrence of seizures, this study recommends incorporating stress management and psychological counseling as an adjunct to epilepsy care. Frequent reports of emotional distress may be provided with individualized intervention strategies, including cognitive behavior therapy (CBT) for reducing anxiety and seizure onset, and relaxation techniques, like respiratory training, to improve autonomic control. In addition, a real-time stress alert system for emotional stress can allow healthcare providers to intervene prior to the onset of seizures. In treating stress as a seizure precursor, this method increases seizure forecasting accuracy as well as enhancing the general quality of life for patients through active emotional and neurological management.

It should be mentioned that the presented framework represents statistical relationships of affective states and seizure occurrence via a Bayesian probabilistic method. Although such findings suggest the presence of a strong predictive relationship the findings do not determine direct causality. The lack of experimental intervention and formal ways of causal inferences makes it difficult to make conclusive causal conclusions. Further research on the underlying processes involved in the relationships between the emotional states and the seizure onset will involve causal modeling methods, including structural causal models, intervention-based validation, etc.

Clinically and ethically, the implementation of automated seizure forecasting systems has to be handled with care so that the risks are minimized. False positive predictions can cause additional unwarranted alarms and this raises the patient anxiety and the alert fatigue among the caregivers. On the other hand, false negative can lead to missed seizure alerts leading to safety threats. Thus, the given framework is expected to serve as a clinical decision-support system, but not a completely autonomous diagnostic means. Human supervision is also needed to be able to interpret the model results and implement appropriate medical action.

Secure & privacy-preserving model deployment

In the framework suggested, the formal enforcement of the idea of differential privacy (DP) is carried out during the federated training process in the form of perturbing local model updates prior to transmission. In particular, Gaussian noise on the gradients is introduced on individual client nodes:

where g represents the original gradient and denotes Gaussian noise with variance . This mechanism makes sure that the information at the patient level cannot be deduced by common model updates.

The privacy guarantee is measured based on the (phantom subtlety)differential privacy framework, in which ϵ\epsilonϵ determines the privacy loss and δ is the likelihood of failure. In the present research, ϵ\epsilonϵ is held between the values of 1–3 and δ = 10 -5, which offers a trade-off between privacy protection and utility of the model.

The adopted framework uses both theoretical differentials privacy (DP) and homomorphic encryption (HE) to provide the security of federated learning. Differential privacy implements the method of adding variable Gaussian noise to local model gradients prior to transmission, such that personal patient information cannot be reconstructed based on collective updates. The privacy budget ( ε, δ) is used to measure the privacy guarantee with smaller values of ε meaning higher privacy protection.Besides that, homomorphic encryption is used to facilitate safe aggregation of model parameters. Client encrypts their local model update and transmits it, giving the central server the opportunity to conduct aggregation operations on encrypted data and not on unencrypted data. This provides end-to-end confidentiality in the federated training process.:

The proposed framework performance in terms of privacy is quantitatively measured with conventional DP measures. The privacy budget in the case is kept on ε = 1–3 and δ = 10–5 which gives a very good balance of privacy or loss and model utility. Also, there is a computational overhead of around 8–12 per cent that is added by the encryption process during the process of aggregation, which is deemed to be tolerable in terms of healthcare usage where there is a need of high-level data security. The obtained results indicate that the suggested method can provide a strong level of privacy without compromising the effective performance of the models.

It should be mentioned that the preservation of privacy presents a trade-off between the model accuracy and security. Noise injection (lower ε) increases the privacy, but it can have a minor impact on predictive performance. The chosen design provides the best balance in maintaining a high classification accuracy and offers a high level of protection against data leakage and inference attacks.

To measure the interpretability of the model, attention weight visualization and SHAP-based visualization of features of interest were carried out. Federated learning performance results

This section assesses the performance of the FL architecture in terms of model accuracy, communication overhead, and computational complexity. The federated model makes use of data that is spread over a small number of simulated client nodes representing a subset of subjects that may not be sufficient to model large-scale inter-institutional variance as is found in real-world implementations of healthcare. It benchmarks the convergence speed of the model in various communication rounds against a centralized approach. It also illustrates privacy-preserving advantages of FL where information stays decentralized and is attained at high performance. The integration of the concepts of differential privacy and secure aggregation provide a little influence on the model accuracy and a significant benefit to the data confidentiality. Although the proposed framework has a high predictive accuracy in the federated case, one should mention that the number of involved clients and the diversity of the datasets are rather small when compared to realistic healthcare implementations. In the large-scale clinical setting, there can be the factors of non-IID data distribution, device variations and heterogeneity of patients, which can affect model generalization. Future research will be aimed at testing the framework on larger multi-institutional datasets to further prove its soundness. The suggested framework is tested in a stable experimental environment, which guarantees the internal validity, but its external comparisons with the literature-based findings must be viewed with the awareness of the possible experimental inaccuracies.

Discussion

The proposed NeuroFedSense framework exhibits the potential of privacy-focused DL in medicine by facilitating FL-Enabled Privacy-Preserving Seizure Forecasting and Affective State Analysis. By incorporating TCN and an attention mechanism, NeuroFedSense can efficiently learn meaningful temporal patterns from EEG-ECG signals, resulting in precise seizure prediction and affective state analysis. The FL paradigm facilitates decentralized training on various edge devices while maintaining patient privacy and model performance. This decentralized methodology overcomes the conventional data-sharing issues, making the proposed model a suitable option for clinical and telemedicine applications in real-world settings. Additionally, the framework provides enhanced patient monitoring by associating seizure onset predictions with affective states, facilitating proactive intervention measures that enhance the quality of care.

In addition, the study underscores the pivotal interaction between affective states and physiological signals, justifying multi-modal data fusion in clinical AI applications. Experimental results provide strong evidence of critical performance improvements over centralized learning methods, demonstrating robustness in seizure prediction and affective state estimation. However, despite its merits, the provided model does not directly address personalized model adaptation to patient-specific variance. Furthermore, the framework’s dependence on stable network connectivity for FL synchronization poses practical issues in remote healthcare settings. Future development should aim at adaptive FL methods and personalized federated optimization to overcome these shortcomings while enhancing real-time inference efficiency.

Ethical and clinical considerations

The integration of AI-driven seizure prediction systems into healthcare introduces several ethical and clinical challenges. Although high accuracy is desirable, even small error rates can have significant consequences in medical contexts. False alarms may lead to increased stress and reduced confidence in the system, while missed detections can compromise patient safety. Furthermore, the interpretability of model predictions is crucial to support clinician trust and informed decision-making. The proposed framework incorporates attention-based interpretability; however, further validation in clinical environments is required. Ethical deployment also necessitates transparency, accountability, and adherence to medical standards to ensure that such systems augment rather than replace human expertise.

Practical limitations that need to be considered during the implementation of the proposed framework into the context of real-world healthcare settings include instability of the network, asynchronous involvement of clients, and variability on the device level. Further research is also going to be implemented on designing asynchronous federated learning solutions, adaptive client selection process, and fault-tolerant aggregation procedures to improve resilience in the actual world.

Conclusion and future work

Low-latency real-time performance is also attained by the framework; hence it can be applied to clinical condition where quick seizure detection and intervention are needed. The study successfully meets its objectives using real-time seizure prediction and affective state monitoring without invading data privacy. Integration of TCN with an attention mechanism significantly enhances feature extraction to enable improved temporal pattern detection. This advancement provides better prediction results than traditional approaches. Experimental results illustrate significant performance, validating the efficacy of NeuroFedSense for seizure prediction and affective state estimation. The results further show an easily identifiable correlation between seizure-prone patients and mood states caused by stress, presenting the possibility of integrative treatment approaches for patients with physical and also psychological facets. The current study is performed in controlled experimental conditions and with a limited diversity of datasets, which makes the study high in accuracy and high in AUC values. These can help to give optimistic performance estimates. Subsequent studies will place more focus on cross dataset validation, incorporation of more diverse patient groups and intensive real world testing to further confirm the scalability and generalizability of the NeuroFedSense framework.

Irrespective of its innovations, this research has some limitations which should be solved to apply it in practice clinics. The federated learning synchronization that depends on a stable network connection can be a problem in remote or resource-limited healthcare settings. To alleviate this, future efforts will consider asynchronous federated learning and edge based model updating techniques, whereby local models may run on their own and synchronize with each other at regular intervals and not rely on a persistent connection. Besides, other communication-efficient methods like model compression and update sparsification can also reduce network load.The other limitation is a failure to adapt individually to the variability of patients, the existing framework uses models that have been aggregated on a global basis. To solve this, the future research will be directed to personalized federated learning methods, such as client-specific fine-tuning, clustered federated learning, and meta-learning-based adaptation. They have potential to allow the model to better represent patient-specific physiological patterns, and allow more accurate prediction of diverse clinical profiles with rare epilepsy subtypes. Although the dataset applied in the current study considers multimodal EEG-ECG measurements with variability in subjects, complete large-scale validation in diverse populations is one of the key directions in the future. The next steps to increase the strength and applicability of the proposed NeuroFedSense framework will be to increase the dataset size and number of subjects, extend the recording time, and multi-centers clinical data.

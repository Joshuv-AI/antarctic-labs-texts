Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions
MAKE IT Prize has had many successes, including the technical impacts achieved by the competitors’
work, the collection of formal feedback from winners on the prize’s impact for their companies, and the
structural learnings around the prize mechanism as applied to OTC and DOE through this program.

MAKE IT Prize also achieved its goal of incentivizing and rewarding accelerated progress by creating
domestic manufacturing opportunities through both the planning and construction of facilities (Facilities
Track), and the development of new manufacturing partnerships across regions of the U.S. (Strategies
Track).The winning teams selected in each track and phase demonstrated that they created and
accelerated plans and materials to boost domestic manufacturing in the following ways:

MAKE IT Prize also saw success in the ability to provide technical support and mentorship to competing
teams throughout the competition. This support was accomplished through leveraging Power
Connectors – organizations subcontracted with NLR who are part of the American-Made network. These
organizations offered peer exchange sessions, matchmaking webinars, guest expert discussions, project
feedback, and connections to other resources– all free of charge to eligible competitors. Peer exchange
sessions included opportunities for competing teams to hear from one another, including overviews of
their projects, progress, challenges, and ideas. Webinar sessions included presentations and Q&A with
guest experts, from industry as well as community-based organizations.

Some highlights from team support include:

 • Of the final winners in each track, over half utilized one or more of the Power Connector
 services described above, and throughout the competition there was a high rate of repeat
 attendance and engagement from teams who were selected as winners of multiple phases
 across each track.
 • Power Connectors focused on the Facilities Track engaged with over 300 organizations over the
 course of the project via email, LinkedIn, online meetings and calls, and group events.
 • Power Connectors focused on the Strategies Track performed targeted outreach to over 900
 potential applicants across the country, followed by in-depth engagement (such as emails, calls,
 meetings, or group sessions) with over 200 individuals.
 • Testimonials from competitors regarding their engagement with Power Connectors:
 o “Were it not for your email to us and forwarding the information about the MAKE IT
 Prize, we would not have even known about the opportunity to prepare a submission.”
 o “Thank you for the great outreach and support you shared with my team. Excellent
 news [of being selected as a winner]!”
 o “This has been such an improved application experience thanks to your professional and
 friendly support. Thank you again!”
 • Overall, communications between MAKE IT Prize competitors and Power Connectors helped
 initiate key conversations, catalyzed critical partnerships, and added significant value to
 projects.

Project Best Practices:

In addition to the overall successful competitor results and awards made as detailed above:

 • The engagement and use of Power Connectors was successful, especially with regard to tailoring
 support to competitors’ needs. For example, in the Facilities Track, once teams were selected as
 Phase 1 winners the Power Connector supporting that track sent a needs assessment survey to
 those teams. This assessment focused on a few categories of the Phase 1 and 2 submission
 requirements where teams were interested in getting additional support. This outreach led to
 the coordination of virtual group sessions that included industry experts on categories such as
 financial planning and budgeting. This is a practice that can be expanded upon and continuously
 improved in future prizes.
 • In the Facilities Track, requiring a Statement of Intent submission (with no prize money at stake)
 ahead of Phase 1 was an excellent decision that allowed for a low-stakes way to ensure basic
 alignment with the scope of the prize, as well as better planning for a complicated review that
 required many different types of expertise. This approach could be valuable in future
 competitions where significant work and effort is required for a Phase 1 submissions or another
 initial deadline.
 • While the Facilities Track Phase 2 winner announcement event had to be cancelled, the prize
 team found other ways to connect winning teams to the greater American-Made network, such
 as coordinating and conducting a site visit with one of the Phase 2 winners in Chicago as part of
 a separate prize event occurring at that location. These types of cross-prize interactions and
 learning opportunities are a great way to create cohesion and buy-in from competing teams
 involved in American-Made prizes.

Challenges and Lessons Learned:

There were several challenges encountered during the competition, resulting in lessons learned.

 • Cross-track goals: An original aspiration of the MAKE IT Prize was to encourage communication
 and collaboration among competitors in the Facilities and Strategies Tracks, to potentially
 facilitate a manufacturing facility to be built in an area not previously considered (or to at least
 allow for the sharing of ideas, practices, and networks within the manufacturing sector). In
 general, the communication and collaboration between competitors in the two tracks was low;
 however, several competitors did engage teams from the other track including at least two of
 the five Facilities Track Phase 2 winners.
 o Lesson: Given feedback from the participants, future iterations of the prize should
 include earlier and more intentional connections between the groups of competitors
 such as combined pitch and summit events, and specific facilitated networking based on
 specific participant interests at those events.

 • Power Connector use and data collection: While Power Connectors supported the competition
 and provided valuable resources to competitors (see “Significant Accomplishments and
 Conclusions” above), there were challenges associated with their use. MAKE IT was one of the
 first prizes aimed at technologies of a relatively advanced technology readiness level (TRL), and
 the network had limited capacity to support such competitors. Further, there were

 10

 communication challenges in relaying prize goals and specifics of the competition rules to Power
 Connectors and, ultimately, to competitors. More data would be helpful regarding the direct
 effects of Power Connector activities, such as how competitor projects and submission materials
 were strengthened, or whether utilization of Power Connector services resulted in significant
 pivots or adjustments to team projects and outcomes.
 o Lesson: In the future, in addition to expanding the Power Connector network to support
 later TRLs, providing clearer information to the current suite of subcontractors and
 establishing clear later TRL-style metrics for impact could build American manufacturing
 knowledge capacity in the Power Connector network and offer valuable data on what
 supports the later TRL prize applicant community might need. Ideally, the prize
 administration team should identify specific metrics for Power Connectors to track at
 the beginning of the period of performance to better understand the outcomes of the
 activities performed.

 • The prize structure: The MAKE IT Prize had two separate tracks with two separate audiences,
 and each track had two timelines to allow competitors multiple chances to compete, or
 opportunities to refine their submissions and resubmit. While the Facilities Track did have
 higher submission numbers and more quality submissions than initially expected, as reflected in
 the increased number of winners, there were some participants who reported confusion with
 the overall track structure.
 o Lesson: Continue to provide clear webinars, communications materials such as
 explanatory graphics, and leverage Power Connector support to clearly explain and walk
 through prize structure. Simplify prize and track structure and naming conventions
 where possible to help reduce confusion.

 • Review process: For each phase of the prize, a combination of external and internal subject
 matter experts was identified, recruited, trained, and tasked with reviewing a selection of
 submissions. It proved challenging to engage enough expert reviewers, especially for the
 Strategies Track, and ensure their follow-through to complete all reviews.
 o Lesson: Aim for a higher number of individuals to better anticipate drop-out rate of
 voluntary reviewers, and have backup reviewers on hand to fill gaps if needed.

Pivots or Reduction in Scope:

 • Additional Facilities Track winners were selected beyond those originally estimated.
 • The originally planned Facilities Track Phase 2 winner announcement event, to be held
 September 30, 2024 in Augusta, GA, had to be cancelled due to a hurricane. This was the correct
 pivot to make, but resulted in a lot of time and energy lost in coordinating planning, materials,
 agendas, etc. The winner announcement was made online in place of this live event.
 • There were fewer Strategies Track winners overall compared to the original plans. The
 competition was terminated in April 2025 instead of July 2025. The Facilities Track had entirely
 concluded, but the Strategies Track had one final submission deadline (Round 2 Phase 3) that
 was removed. There were 9 eligible teams still competing, and there were approximately 3
 awards at $250k each that were not able to be made.

 11

Path Forward
Building on the success of this prize, an entirely new and separate “MAKE IT Prize Round 2” was
developed starting in fall 2024 and launched in December 2024. This prize focuses solely on the Facilities
Track scope and objectives, aiming to catalyze domestic manufacturing of critical energy technology
components and move manufacturing facilities from planning to shovel-ready. Many of the best
practices and lessons learned as detailed above have been considered and implemented in this new
prize, such as the exclusive focus on the manufacturing facility outcome, the approach to competitor
support, and the robust review process.

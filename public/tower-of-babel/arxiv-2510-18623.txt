Optimal quantum learning in proximity to universality
Moein N. Ivaki, Matias Karjula, Tapio Ala-Nissila

Abstract
The boundary between classically simulable and computationally superior quantum systems is fundamental to identifying true quantum advantage. We investigate this within the framework of quantum reservoir computing by introducing a tunable N N -qubit random circuit model, where a fraction p p of Clifford gates are probabilistically substituted with nonstabilizing conditional- T ^ \hat{T} gates. We establish a direct correspondence between the reservoir’s performance on temporal processing tasks and its entanglement spectrum statistics and long-range nonstabilizer resource content. To assess scalability, we study the scaling of the anti-flatness of states in the large- N N limit at a fixed circuit depth ratio d / N ∼ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) d/N\sim\mathcal{O}(1) . This is taken as a witness to concentration of measures, a known impediment to learning in thermalizing systems. We demonstrate that the learnability and scalability of the reservoir can be continuously controlled by the parameter p p , allowing us to navigate from classically tractable to maximally expressive quantum dynamics. These architecture-agnostic results offer a general strategy for designing powerful and trainable quantum machine learning systems and clarify the physical resources underpinning quantum computational advantage.

In this work, we propose a paradigm of

;
Gu et al. 2025
;
Niroula et al. 2024
;
Hinsche et al. 2023

However, unlike doping Clifford circuits with

Our main result is that the learning performance of the PQRs is correlated to

Model.
—
We consider a nearest-neighbor brickwork circuit of fixed depth
d
and
N
qubits, where each two-qubit slot is either a
\hat{CT}
gate with probability
p
, or a uniformly sampled random Clifford unitary
\hat{U}_{\mathrm{C}}
with the complementary probability
1-p

Bravyi and Maslov 2021
. The spacetime volume (
i.e.
, total number of gates) is
V=(N-1)\,d
, and total number of
\hat{CT}
gates is extensive for
p>0
, obeying binomial distribution with the expected value
\mathbb{E}[\#\hat{CT}]=pV
. The realized density
\#\hat{CT}/V
then converges to the expected density,
p
, with fluctuations
\sqrt{p(1-p)/V}
. The probabilistic map for unitary part of the evolution is given as
\hat{\rho}\to p\,\hat{CT}\hat{\rho}\hat{CT}^{\dagger}+(1-p)\,\hat{U}_{\mathrm{C}}\hat{\rho}\hat{U}_{\mathrm{C}}^{\dagger}
, where
\hat{\rho}
is a random quantum state. Clifford gates are generated by the set
\{\hat{H},\hat{S},\hat{CX}\}
, where
\hat{H}
is the Hadamard gate
(\hat{X}+\hat{Z})/\sqrt{2}
,
\hat{S}=\sqrt{\hat{Z}}
and
\hat{CX}=\hat{I}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|+\hat{X}\otimes\left|1\right\rangle\left\langle 1\right|
. These gates conjugate the
N
-qubit Pauli group
\mathcal{P}_{N}=\{\hat{I},\hat{X},\hat{Y},\hat{Z}\}^{{\otimes}N}
to itself (up to a phase);
i.e.
, for all
\hat{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
one has
\hat{U}_{\mathrm{C}}\,\hat{P}\,\hat{U}_{\mathrm{C}}^{\dagger}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
.

Since any Pauli string (across any bipartition) stays a tensor product under the action of Clifford group,
operator entanglement
for such string remains
zero
at all times
Fisher et al. 2023
;
Mi et al. 2021
. In addition, for a stabilizer state
\left|\mathrm{\texttt{stab}}\right\rangle=\hat{U}_{\mathrm{C}}\left|0\right\rangle^{\otimes N}

Gottesman 2016
, the reduced density matrix on a subsystem
R
(the readout) satisfies
\hat{\rho}_{R}\propto P_{R}
, where
P_{R}=P_{R}^{2}
is a projector. This implies that the nonzero entanglement-spectrum is highly degenerate and displays no level repulsion. Such properties renders classical simulation of these circuits highly efficient with polynomial resources
Aaronson and Gottesman 2004
. Interestingly, even a single
\hat{T}
gate may drive the entanglement-spectrum distribution to a universal Wigner-Dyson form
Zhou et al. 2020
and lift its flatness
Tirrito et al. 2024
;
True and Hamma 2022
. Nevertheless, in the absence of a universal quantum gate set, the distribution alone does not necessarily signify either universality or classical hardness
Projansky et al. 2024
.

Entanglement spectrum.—
We now characterize the dynamics of entanglement and its spectral fingerprints in our model. In the following we fix the spatial size of the readout
R

S_{M}:={-\mathrm{tr}[\rho_{M}\log\rho_{M}]}

S_{M}^{\infty}=S_{R}^{\infty}

d^{\star}\sim\mathcal{O}(N)

;
Kim and Huse 2013
.

Shown in Fig.
2

\hat{CT}

e_{\hat{CT}}\!\ll\!e_{\hat{CX}}

;
Zanardi et al. 2000

p\!\to\!1

\sim\!{(1-p)\,d}/{N}

S(d,N,p)\approx s^{\infty}\min[v_{E}(p)\,d,N/2]

\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\,\exp[(1-p)Ns^{\infty}]\right)

It is also imperative to probe how the circuit’s output distribution,

Mutual magic.—
As a proxy to nonlocal magic, we consider the behavior of the mutual stabilizer Rényi entropy (SRE) for complementary subsystems of size
N/2

Leone et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2025
;
Tarabunga and Haug 2025
;
Haug and Tarabunga 2025
;
Tarabunga et al. 2023
;
Qian and Wang 2025
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\hat{\rho}):=\mathcal{M}(\hat{\rho})-\mathcal{M}(\hat{\rho}_{M})-\mathcal{M}(\hat{\rho}_{R}).

(1)

For a mixed-state
\hat{\rho}
, the second SRE is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\hat{{\rho}}):=-{\rm log}\left(2^{-N}\sum_{\hat{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}}|{{\rm Tr}(\hat{\rho}\hat{P})}|^{4}\right)-S_{2},

(2)

where
S_{2}:=-\log(\mathrm{Tr\,}\hat{\rho}^{2})
is the 2-Rényi entropy. In the following we refer to
\mathcal{M(\hat{{\rho}})}
as total magic (TM) and
\mathcal{I(\hat{{\rho}})}
as mutual magic (MM). SREs are a measure of spread over the Pauli basis, indicating deviations from stabilizer states, which concentrate all Pauli weight on exactly
2^{N}
strings. By additivity,
\mathcal{M}(\hat{\rho}_{M}\otimes\hat{\rho}_{R})=\mathcal{M}(\hat{\rho}_{M})+\mathcal{M}(\hat{\rho}_{R})
. Hence,
\mathcal{I}\!=\!0
if
\hat{\rho}_{MR}=\hat{\rho}_{M}\otimes\hat{\rho}_{R}
, meaning MM vanishes for random product states, while the TM can be nonzero or even maximal.
Observing
\mathcal{I}>0
hence signals correlations across a cut and quantifies the
long-range
magic of
states
. This may also be understood as magic that cannot be undone by strictly local unitaries, which arises when a unitary map entangles Pauli operators over some spatial distance
Andreadakis and Zanardi 2025
;
Dowling et al. 2025
. Evolution of magic measures may also be tracked to identify quantum phase transitions
Falcão et al. 2025
and study information scrambling
Ahmadi and Greplova 2024
;
Varikuti et al. 2025
. It is useful to note that local Haar-random circuits saturate TM at depth
d\propto\ln N

to the value
\mathcal{M}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{H}}\sim N-2+\mathcal{O}(2^{-N})

Odavić et al. 2025
;
Szombathy et al. 2024
. Therefore, in some models, dynamics of magic at the level of states violates the notion of locality in space-time, despite the fact that unitaries are short-range. Hence, in QML applications, TM of states may not be a dependable proxy for nonstabilizerness and performance.

Intuitively, at a fixed depth

Memory and learnability.
—
To perform computations and process the stream of inputs
\{\theta_{n}\}
, the reservoir is iterated with a fixed random template, and expectation values of diagonal observers
\langle\hat{Z}_{i}\rangle

\langle\hat{Z}_{i}\hat{Z}_{j}\rangle_{i\neq j}

(cf. Fig.
1
). Here we assume idealized measurements and reset of readout qubits, meaning there is no restriction on the number of shots, and operations are noiseless. The general purpose here is to recover a (nonlinear) temporally dependent functionals

This can be captured, for instance, by looking at the evolution of an averaged measure of distance between two random mixed-states

Scalability and anti-flatness
—We now turn to discussing the feasibility of accurate information processing in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
. In fast-scrambling circuits
Brandão et al. 2021
;
Ippoliti and Ho 2022
, expectation values of local observables of a subsystem converge toward ensemble-typical values, and their long-time fluctuations may shrink exponentially with system size
Srimahajariyapong et al. 2025
;
Mele 2024
;
Cerezo et al. 2022
. As a result, in practical situations, the number of measurements required to resolve expectation values and distinguish different input states may also grow exponentially
Thanasilp et al. 2024
;
Sannia et al. 2025
;
Xiong et al. 2025b
;
Sarkar et al. 2025
;
McCaul 2025
;
Xiong et al. 2025b
.
Here we address this with regard to the scaling of anti-flatness of a state
Tirrito et al. 2024
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\hat{\rho}):={\mathrm{Tr}}\hat{\rho}_{R}^{3}-({\mathrm{Tr}}\hat{\rho}_{R}^{2})^{2},

(5)

which is the variance of a reduced pure state with respect to itself;
i.e.
,
\mathcal{F}(\hat{\rho})=\mathrm{var}_{\hat{\rho}_{R}}(\hat{\rho}_{R})
where
\hat{\rho}_{R}=\mathrm{Tr}_{M}\hat{\rho}
. Similar to nonlocal magic, one has
\mathcal{F}=0
for both stabilizer states (entangled or not) and random product-states (which only have local magic)
Cao et al. 2024
.

Here we are interested in the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{F}
as function of
N
. For an equal bipartition and in the absence of symmetries, the ensemble-averaged anti-flatness of Haar-random states satisfy
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{H}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{H}}[\mathcal{F}(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{H}})]\!\sim\!2^{-N}

Odavić et al. 2025
. This is the typical behavior expected from outputs of a sufficiently random (nonintegrable) quantum dynamics. For generic volume-law-entangled random states, the size-driven decay of
\mathcal{F}
thus progressively limits the ability to resolve subsystem observables. As demonstrated in Fig.
5

continuously
control the behavior of flatness by tuning

Writing
\log_{2}(\mathcal{F})=-\alpha N+c
, we may interpret
\alpha\equiv\alpha(d,p)
as
scrambling rate
, where for Haar-random circuits
\alpha_{\mathrm{H}}=1
. In the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
, Haar-random pure states have reduced subsystems that approach maximally-mixed state
\mathbb{I}/2^{N}

Hamma et al. 2012
;
Choi et al. 2020
, so that they are locally flat (
i.e.
, information is “hidden” from local observers). With regard to a learning algorithm, this implies that the expected optimal regime in terms of both performance and scalability may only be achieved when
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{H}}\gg 1
and
0\ll\alpha<\alpha_{\mathrm{H}}
(see Fig.

\alpha\ll\alpha_{\mathrm{H}}
(strictly when
\mathcal{F}\neq 0
) signals a weakly entangled (localized) nonstabilizer state. Therefore, both the relative value of anti-flatness and its scaling are equally important, and together they discriminate physical phases and may be viewed as a proxy for both computational performance and also practical readout cost in the large-
N
limit.

Conclusions and outlook.
—In this work we introduce a probabilistic quantum reservoir computer with a single control parameter
p
for robust temporal quantum learning. By quantifying learnability via the circuit’s distance to a quantum chaotic spectral-law, we have shown that learnability peaks
in proximity to universality
, where nonlocal qunatum resources, namely entanglement and mutual nonstabilizerness, are extensive yet submaximal. Notably, in the optimal regime the drastic scrambling-induced learnability failure is mitigated, enabling large-scale and practical deployment of post-variational QML algorithms. These findings are fully inline with the “second law of quantum complexity”
Brown and Susskind 2018
, where the gap to maximal complexity (“uncomplexity”) is viewed as a consumable resource for directed computations. As a consequence, dynamics that drive states rapidly to near-Haar typicality (maximally-scrambling) may in general be suboptimal for learnability.

The family of brickwork circuits studied here provide tight control over dynamics, and enable QML architectures grounded in simple physical principles. Our findings however are largely independent of the specific quantum dynamics considered here. Due to its flexibility and conceptual simplicity, the robustness and practicality of our approach can be readily evaluated in both noisy and symmetric settings
Ivaki et al. 2025c
;
Domingo et al. 2023
;
Trigueros and Guzmán 2025
. We expect our findings to be also highly relevant to other quantum learning frameworks, particularly quantum kernel methods and extreme learning
Thanasilp et al. 2024
;
Xiong et al. 2025a
, where the same expressivity-induced concentration is observed.

Concerning experimental realizations in digital settings, one can imagine a conceptually identical probabilistic setup with random single-qubit Clifford and a set of parameterized conditional gates (tailored to a device’s native gate set), using the well-known Cartan decomposition
Varikuti et al. 2025
. Similar to what is done here, the parameterization should allow one to formalize a predictive link between learnability and the joint profile of entangling and nonstabilizing powers of a circuit.

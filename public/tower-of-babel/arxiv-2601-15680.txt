Introduction

An integer partition
\lambda
is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers. These positive integers are called the parts of
\lambda
. We denote by
|\lambda|
, the sum of all parts of
\lambda
. If
|\lambda|=n
, then
\lambda
is called a partition of
n
. The generating function for the number of unrestricted partitions of
n
, denoted by
p(n)
, is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}=\dfrac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

Here and throughout this paper, we assume
|q|<1
and for positive integer
n
, we use the standard notation

\displaystyle(a;q)_{0}:=1,\;\;(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{j}),\;\;\text{and}\;\;(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n}).

A partition of a positive integer
n
has
k

k
copies of each part size available and all of them are viewed as distinct objects. A partition of a positive integer into parts with colors is called a “colored partition”. We denote by
p_{k}(n)
, the number of
k
-colored partitions of
n
, and agree that
p_{k}(0)=1
. The generating function of
p_{k}(n)
satisfies the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(n)q^{n}=\dfrac{1}{f_{1}^{k}}\;,

where
f_{m}:=(q^{m};q^{m})_{\infty}
.

In a series of papers, Hirschhorn and Sellers
[
15
]
, Sellers
[

Lemma 2.6
.

For all primes

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(n)q^{n}:=\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{j}f_{\alpha_{i}}^{\gamma_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{\beta_{i}}^{\delta_{i}}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B(n)q^{n}:=\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{l}f_{\alpha_{i}}^{a_{i}p^{\lambda}+\gamma_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{\beta_{i}}^{b_{i}p^{\lambda}+\delta_{i}}}}.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B(n)q^{n}=\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{l}f_{\alpha_{i}}^{a_{i}p^{\lambda}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{\beta_{i}}^{b_{i}p^{\lambda}}}\cdot\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{j}f_{\alpha_{i}}^{\gamma_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{\beta_{i}}^{\delta_{i}}}.

Exceptional dual pair correspondences; case of real groups of split rank one
Petar Bakić, Hung Yean Loke, Gordan Savin

Abstract
. Exceptional real groups have quaternionic forms of split rank 4 that contain dual pairs G × G ′ G\times G^{\prime} , where G ′ G^{\prime} is the split Lie group of the type G 2 {\mathrm{G}}_{2} , and G G a Lie group of split rank one. In this paper we restrict the minimal representation of the quaternionic group to the dual pair and prove some significant results for the resulting correspondence of representations.

Introduction

We start with a general situation. Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
be a pair of complex simple Lie algebras. Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be a pair of Lie groups with
complexified Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
. Let
K
and
K^{\prime}
be the maximal compact subgroups of
G
and
G^{\prime}
, respectively. In this paper,
we shall work with
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules, and study the following problem.

Let
(\omega,\Omega)
be a
(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K\times K^{\prime})
-module, where
\omega
denotes the action on the vector space
\Omega
. Let

\pi
be an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module. Then there exists a
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K^{\prime})
-module
\Theta(\pi)
, the big theta lift of
\pi
, such that

\Omega/\bigcap_{T\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Omega,\pi)}\mathrm{ker}(T)\cong\pi\otimes\Theta(\pi).

Conversely, starting with an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K^{\prime})
-module
\pi^{\prime}
, we can define a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
\Theta(\pi^{\prime})
. In order to
get a correspondence of irreducible modules, one would like to show that
\Theta(\pi)
has finite length and that it has a unique irreducible quotient, and
the same for
\Theta(\pi^{\prime})
.

We state a general result in that direction. To do so, we need some additional notation. Let
Z(\mathfrak{g})
and
Z(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
be the centers
of enveloping algebras. If
\tau
is a
K
-type (that is an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of
K
), let
\Theta(\tau)
denote the lift of
\tau
, i.e. the
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-module such that the
\tau
-isotypic summand of
\Omega
is
\Omega[\tau]=\tau\otimes\Theta(\tau)
. Then we have (for the proof see Theorem
10.0.1
):

Theorem 1.0.1
.

Assume that the following two hold:

•

There is a correspondence of infinitesimal characters, that is,
\omega(Z(\mathfrak{g}))=\omega(Z(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}))
.

•

For every
K
-type
\tau
, there exists a finite dimensional representation
F_{\tau}
of
K^{\prime}
such that

\Theta(\tau)
is a quotient of
U(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})}F_{\tau}
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K^{\prime})
-modules, respectively. Then:

•

\Theta(\pi)
and
\Theta(\pi^{\prime})
are finite length
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K^{\prime})
and
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules, respectively.

•

If
\tau
is a
K
-type of
\pi
, then

\dim_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{Hom}}(\Theta(\pi),\pi^{\prime})\leq\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K^{\prime}}(F_{\tau},\pi^{\prime}).

In this paper we look at the case where
\Omega
is the minimal representation of the quaternionic group
{\mathrm{E}}_{n,4}
constructed by Gross and Wallach
[
GW1
]
and
[
GW2
]
.
The exceptional groups

,

, contain two families of dual pairs

where

In this paper we consider the other family, for

G

The centralizer of

, a split simply connected group of the same name
type, thus we have the following see-saw.

In the above picture
\tau

Quaternionic form

2.1.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple complex Lie algebra. In this paper we shall study representations of the quaternionic

\mathfrak{g}

-modules, our first task is

Fix a maximal Cartan algebra
\mathfrak{t}

\Phi

\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\}

\alpha_{0}
be the lowest root.
Let

\varphi_{0}:{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{C}}
be a homomorphism corresponding to
\alpha_{0}
.
Then the Cartan involution is

\theta=\varphi_{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&\\
&-1\end{array}\right).

We shall now give a more detailed description of the corresponding Cartan decomposition.
Let
(e,h,f)
be an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
-triple corresponding to the highest root
-\alpha_{0}
.
Then the centralizer of
h
in
\mathfrak{g}
is a standard Levi subgroup
\mathfrak{l}

\alpha_{0}
.
The nilpotent radical
\mathfrak{n}
of the standard
parabolic subgroup

has a decomposition
\mathfrak{n}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{2}
given by

h
-grading. Then
\mathfrak{n}
is a Heisenberg Lie algebra with the center
\mathfrak{n}_{2}=\mathbb{C}\cdot e
. The nilpotent radical
of the opposite parabolic
\bar{\mathfrak{q}}

\bar{\mathfrak{n}}=\mathfrak{n}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{-2}
where
\mathfrak{n}_{-2}=\mathbb{C}\cdot f
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{l}]
.
If
\Phi
is not type
{\mathrm{A}}_{\ell}
then
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{m}\oplus\mathbb{C}\cdot h
.
The two summands in the Cartan decomposition are

\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{m}\text{ and }\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{n}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{1}.

Let
K
and
M
be the simply connected compact Lie groups with the complexified Lie algebra isomorphic to
\mathfrak{k}
and

, respectively. Then

We denote the first factor by

3.3.
Restriction to a subalgebra

Here we recall some results of
[
L1
]
and
[
L3
]
. Assume we have a simple subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
such that

\langle e,h,f\rangle\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}.

In this situation, it is possible to describe the restriction of
{\mathbf{A}}(\mathfrak{g},W_{M}[s])
to
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.
Using the
h
-grading, we can define the Heisenberg subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, in the same way as for
\mathfrak{g}
. In particular,

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{n}
,
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}_{1}+\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}_{2}=\mathbb{C}\cdot e
.
We have a filtration of
U(\mathfrak{n})
by
U(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})
-submodules such that

U_{1}(\mathfrak{n})=U(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\text{ and }U_{k}(\mathfrak{n})/U_{k-1}(\mathfrak{n})\cong U(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\otimes S^{k}(\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}).

This filtration gives a
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
-filtration of the Verma module
V(\mathfrak{g},W_{M}[s])
, which in turn gives a filtration
F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}\subseteq\ldots
of
{\mathbf{A}}(\mathfrak{g},W_{M}[s])

such that

F_{k}/F_{k-1}\cong{\mathbf{A}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},S^{k}(\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\otimes W_{M}[s+k]).

Thus the restriction problem reduces to decomposing
S^{k}(\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\otimes W_{M}
into irreducible
M^{\prime}
-summands.

We shall almost exclusively use this in the example
G^{\prime}={\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)
and
G={\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)
. Then the embedding
M^{\prime}\subset M
is given by

M^{\prime}\cong{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(3)\subset{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\cong M

where
{\mathrm{Spin}}(3)\cong{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
embeds diagonally into last two
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
in
M
. Moreover,

\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}_{1}\cong(1)\otimes(2)\subset(1)\otimes(1)\otimes(1)\cong\mathfrak{n}_{1}.

Hence

\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cong(1)\otimes(0)\text{ and }S^{k}(\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})\cong(k)\otimes(0)

as
M^{\prime}={\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(3)
-modules.

4.
Restriction from
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)
to
G_{2}

We now specialize to
G={\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)
, so
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
where
M={\mathrm{Spin}}(3)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
, so
\mathfrak{p}=(1)\otimes V_{M}

where
V_{M}=(2)\otimes(1)
. Let
G_{2}
be the split group of exceptional type
{\mathrm{G}}_{2}
(abusing the notation). Its maximal compact subgroup is

K_{2}={\mathrm{SU}}_{s}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{l}(2)
where the two
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
correspond to short and long compact roots, respectively, as the notation indicates. We have the
Cartan decompostion
\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\mathfrak{k}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{2}
where
\mathfrak{p}_{2}=(3)\otimes(1)
as
{\mathrm{SU}}_{s}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{l}(2)
-module.
We have an embedding
G_{2}\subset{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)
so that
K_{2}\subset K
is given by

{\mathrm{SU}}_{s}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}_{l}(2)\subseteq{\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(3)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)

where
{\mathrm{SU}}_{s}(2)
embeds diagonally into
{\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(3)
, and
{\mathrm{SU}}_{l}(2)
maps to the last factor
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
.
The inclusion
\mathfrak{p}_{2}\subset\mathfrak{p}
is given

(3)\otimes(1)\subset(1)\otimes(2)\otimes(1)

where
(3)
embeds as a summand of the first two factors
(1)\otimes(2)=(1)\oplus(3)
, and the map is the identity on the last factor
(1)
.

Theorem 4.0.1
.

Assume
s\geq 4
. Let
W_{M}
be any finite-dimensional
M
-module.
Let
U(\mathfrak{g}_{2})
denote the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. Then

{\mathbf{A}}({\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3),W_{M}[s])=U(\mathfrak{g}_{2})\cdot((s-2)\otimes W_{M}).

Proof.

We shall prove that the filtration
A_{k}
is contained in
U(\mathfrak{g}_{2})\cdot((s-2)\otimes W_{M})
by induction on
k
. For
k=0
there is
nothing to prove. For the induction step, we shall act by
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
on
(s+k-2)\otimes\tau_{k}
and prove that we get
A_{k+1}
modulo
A_{k}
.
We know that the action of
\mathfrak{p}
on
(s+k-2)\otimes\tau_{k}
gives a surjection.

\delta:\mathfrak{p}\otimes[(s+k-2)\otimes\tau_{k}]\rightarrow A_{k+1}/A_{k}\cong(s+k-1)\otimes\tau_{k}

Using the Clebsch–Gordan rule, we can decompose the domain of
\delta
as a direct sum

\mathfrak{p}\otimes[(s+k-2)\otimes\tau_{k}]\cong(s+k-3)\otimes V_{M}\otimes\tau_{k}\oplus(s+k-1)\otimes V_{M}\otimes\tau_{k}.

Clearly the surjection
\delta
vanishes on the first summand. Hence
\delta=\delta\circ\pi
where
\pi
is the projection on the second summand.
Thus, to prove the theorem, we need to show that the composition

\mathfrak{p}_{2}\otimes(s+k-2)\otimes\tau_{k}\rightarrow\mathfrak{p}\otimes(s+k-2)\otimes\tau_{k}\rightarrow(s+k-1)\otimes V_{M}\otimes\tau_{k},

where the first map is the natural inclusion and the second map the projection
\pi
, is a surjection.
Note that the factor
\tau_{k}
plays no role and we can remove it. Next,
substitute
\mathfrak{p}_{2}=(3)\otimes(1)
,
\mathfrak{p}=(1)\otimes V_{M}
and
V_{M}=(2)\otimes(1)
where the last factor
(1)
in both
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
and
V_{M}

is a representation of
{\mathrm{SU}}_{l}(2)
. We can remove this factor in all three terms, as well,
arriving to a manageable composition

(3)\otimes(s+k-2)\rightarrow(1)\otimes(2)\otimes(s+k-2)\rightarrow(2)\otimes(s+k-1).

The surjectivity of the above composition is a subject of the following lemma, which completes the proof of the theorem, and explains the condition
s\geq 4
.
∎

Lemma 4.0.2
.

The composition

f:(3)\otimes(n)\rightarrow(2)\otimes(1)\otimes(n)\rightarrow(2)\otimes(n+1),

obtained by the inclusion
(3)\subset(2)\otimes(1)
and the projection
(1)\otimes(n)
on
(n+1)
, is surjective if
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

We realize
(n)
as the space of homogeneous, degree
n
, polynomials in
x
and
y
. Then
(3)\subset(2)\otimes(1)
is spanned by

y^{2}\otimes y,\,2xy\otimes y+y^{2}\otimes x,\,2xy\otimes x+x^{2}\otimes y\,,x^{2}\otimes x.

The projection map
(1)\otimes(n)\rightarrow(n+1)
is given by the plain multiplication of polynoimials of degree 1 and n. In order to show that

f
is surjective, we shall argue that in the image of
f
we get
x^{2}\otimes q
,
xy\otimes q
and
y^{2}\otimes q
where
q

is any monomial of degree
n+1
. Let
p
be a monomial of degree
n
. Then

f(x^{2}\otimes x\otimes p)=x^{2}\otimes xp\text{ and }f(y^{2}\otimes y\otimes p)=y^{2}\otimes yp.

Hence the image of
f
contains all pure tensors
x^{2}\otimes q
and
y^{2}\otimes q
for
q\neq y^{n+1}
and
x^{n+1}
, respectively.
Let
q=x^{a}y^{b}\in(n+1)
be such that
a,b\geq 1
. Since
a+b=n+1\geq 3
, either
a
or
b
is greater than or equal to
2
. Assume
a
. Let

p=x^{a-1}y^{b}
. Then

f((2xy\otimes x+x^{2}\otimes y)\otimes p)=2xy\otimes xp+x^{2}\otimes yp=2xy\otimes q+x^{2}\otimes yp

is in the image. Since
yp\neq y^{n+1}
, the second summand is in the image of
f
.
It follows that
xy\otimes q
is in the image of
f
. A similar argument works if
b\geq 2
. Hence
xy\otimes q
is in the image of
f
for any
q
. Finally,

f((2xy\otimes x+x^{2}\otimes y)\otimes y^{n})=2xy\otimes xy^{n}+x^{2}\otimes y^{n+1}

hence
x^{2}\otimes y^{n+1}
is in the image of
f
. Similarly for
y^{2}\otimes x^{n+1}
.
∎

5.
D_{4}
dual pairs and correspondence in
E_{6}

In order to determine the correspondence for the dual pairs
K\times B_{3}
we shall use another family of dual pairs
K_{1}\times D_{4}

in a see-saw position:

(7)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}K&&{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)\\
|&\bigtimes&|\\
K_{1}&&{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3).\end{array}

The family of dual pairs
K_{1}\times D_{4}
was considered by Loke in his thesis
[
L1
]
, and the results were published in
[
L2
]
, however
that paper does not include the dual pair in
E_{6,4}
. The purpose of this section is to fill that gap, and explain the results that we shall need.

5.1.
The minimal representation

The maximal compact subgroup of the quaternionic adjoint group
{\mathrm{E}}_{6,4}
is
({\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(6)/\mu_{3})/\mu_{2}
.
The group of automorphisms of
{\mathrm{E}}_{6,4}
is
{\mathrm{E}}_{6,4}\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle
where
\tau
is an outer automorphism of order
2
.
We pick
\tau
so that it commutes with
{\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)
and acts as on
{\mathrm{SU}}(6)
by the complex conjugation.

The minimal representation
{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{min}}
of
{\mathrm{E}}_{6,4}
is the quaternionic representation
\sigma(G,{\mathbb{C}}[4])
. Its
{\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(6)
-type decomposition is

(8)

{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{min}}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(n+2)\otimes(n,n,n,0,0,0).

It can be extended to
{\mathrm{E}}_{6,4}\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle
in two ways. We fix the one so that
\tau
acts trivially on the minimal type.

5.2.
D_{4}
dual pair

Since
K_{1}
is a compact subgroup we will specify the dual pair
K_{1}\times D_{4}
by
describing how
K_{1}
sits in the maximal compact subgroup of the adjoint

E_{6,4}
which is isomorphic to
({\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(6)/\mu_{3})/\mu_{2}
. Using this identification, we can identify
K_{1}
with the two-dimensional torus

T=\{{\mathrm{diag}}(x,x,y,y,z,z):xyz=1\}\in{\mathrm{SU}}(6)/\mu_{3}.

Let
I
denote the
2\times 2
identity matrix. We highlight three one-parameter subgroups in
T
:

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x^{2/3}I&&\\
&x^{-1/3}I&\\
&&x^{-1/3}I\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}y^{-1/3}I&&\\
&y^{2/3}I&\\
&&y^{-1/3}I\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}z^{-1/3}I&&\\
&z^{-1/3}I&\\
&&z^{2/3}I\end{array}\right].

The fractional powers make sense since these matrices represent elements of
{\mathrm{SU}}(6)/\mu_{3}
. Let
\chi
be a character of
T
. The restriction of
\chi
to
the three one-parameter groups is
x^{a}
,
y^{b}
and
z^{c}
, respectively, for some integers
a
,
b
and
c
such that
a+b+c=0
. Moreover,

\chi(\mathrm{diag}(x,x,y,y,z,z))=x^{a}y^{b}z^{c}.

Thus characters of
T
correspond to such triples of integers. Recall that we also have an outer automorphism
\tau
of
E_{6,4}
which acts on
the factor
{\mathrm{SU}}(6)/\mu_{3}
by complex conjugation. Thus, if

\chi
corresponds to
(a,b,c)
then
\chi^{\tau}
, the conjugate of
\chi
by
\tau
, corresponds to
(-a,-b,-c)
.
Hence
\tau
fixes
\chi
if and only if
\chi=1
, the trivial character of
T
.
It is clear now that all irreducible representations of
\tilde{T}=T\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle
are 2-dimensional, except the trivial representation, and a non-trivial character
\epsilon
, trivial on
T
.

The centralizer of
\tilde{T}
in
({\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(6)/\mu_{3})/\mu_{2}
is

({\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2))/\mu_{2}

where the last three
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
embed in
{\mathrm{SU}}(6)
in a block diagonal fashion:

(g,h,e)\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}g&&\\
&h&\\
&&e\end{array}\right].

This is the maximal compact subgroup of
D_{4}\cong{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)
.

Let
s\geq 2
.
Recall that
M\cong{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
for
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)
and we have
quaternionic representations
{\mathbf{A}}({\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)),(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)[s])
, with the minimal type
(s-2,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
.
By Theorem
3.2.2
,
this module also has a unique irreducible quotient module denoted by
\sigma({\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)),(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)[s])
, with the same minimal type.

6.1.
The
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)
dual pair

The group
{\mathrm{E}}_{6,4}\rtimes\tau
contains a see-saw of dual pairs

(9)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\tilde{\mathrm{U}}(2)&&{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)\\
|&\bigtimes&|\\
\tilde{T}&&{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3).\end{array}

The embedding
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)\subset{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)
is determined by the embedding of
the corresponding maximal compact subgroups which we now proceed to describe.
The maximal compact subgroup of
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)
, and its embedding into the maximal compact subgroup of
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,4)
is

({\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(3))/\mu_{2}\subset({\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SU}}(2))/\mu_{2}

where the embedding on the first two factors is the identity and
{\mathrm{Spin}}(3)
embeds into the last two
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
diagonally. The centralizer of the maximal compact of

{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)
in
K
is
{\mathrm{U}}(2)
. The subgroup
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
of
{\mathrm{U}}(2)
embeds as

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}x&y\\
z&w\end{array}\right]\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&&\\
&xI&yI\\
&zI&wI\end{array}\right].

and the center as

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}x&\\
&x\end{array}\right]\mapsto\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x^{-2/3}I&&\\
&x^{1/3}I&\\
&&x^{1/3}I\end{array}\right].

Proposition 6.1.1
.

The restriction of the irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{U}}(2)
with the highest weight
(m,n)
,
m\geq n
, to
T
is a direct sum of
m-n+1

characters corresponding to the triples

(a,b,c)=(-m-n,m,n),(-m-n,m-1,n+1),\ldots,(-m-n,n,m).

Proof.

The center of
{\mathrm{U}}(2)
acts by the weight
m+n
, so it is clear tha
a=-m-n
. Hence
b+c=m+n
.
The
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
weights are
m-n,m-n-2,\ldots,n-m
. Since

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x^{-1/3}I&&\\
&x^{2/3}I&\\
&&x^{-1/3}I\end{array}\right]\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x^{1/3}I&&\\
&x^{1/3}I&\\
&&x^{-2/3}I\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}I&&\\
&xI&\\
&&x^{-1}I\end{array}\right]

it follows that

b-c=m-n,m-n-2,\ldots,n-m.

Combining with
m+n=b+c
, we solve for
(b,c)
as claimed.
∎

6.2.
Correspondence

Suppose restricting
{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{min}}
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)\cdot{\mathrm{U}}(2)
decomposes as

{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{min}}=\bigoplus_{a\geq b}\Theta(a,b)\otimes(a,b).

According to

(10)

\lambda(a,b)=\dfrac{1}{2}(a+b+1,a+b-1,a-b+1).

Here we use the positive root system of
{\mathrm{B}}_{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that the
simple roots are
\alpha_{1}=(1,-1,0)
,
\alpha_{2}=(0,1,-1)

Lemma 6.2.1
.

The theta lift
\Theta(a,b)
is a direct sum of finitely many irreducible unitarizable representations.

Proof.

We have
\Theta(a,b)\subseteq{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{min}}
which is
{\mathrm{SU}}_{0}(2)
-admissible and unitarizable.
Hence
\Theta(a,b)
is an admissible and unitarizable module with infinitesimal character
\lambda(a,b)
.
This shows that
\Theta(a,b)
has finite length and unitarizable.
∎

s\geq 2
. The group
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)
has quaternionic representations
{\mathbf{A}}({\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3),(m,n)[s])

(m,n)

M={\mathrm{SU}}(2)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(3)

\alpha_{1}

(11)

\frac{m}{2}\alpha_{1}+\frac{n}{2}\alpha_{3}+\rho+\frac{s}{2}\alpha_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(m-s+5,-m-s+3,n+1).

\tilde{\mathrm{U}}(2)
and
{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)

are
\tau

Theorem 6.2.2
.

Let
(a,b)
be a highest weight for
{\mathrm{U}}(2)
.
We have:

(i)

\Theta(a,b)\cong\Theta(-b,-a)

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

\displaystyle\Theta(a,-a)^{+}

\displaystyle\Theta(a,-a)^{-}

The proof of the theorem will occupy the rest of this section. The first part is clear (the action of
\tau

6.3.
Proof of (ii)

(a,b)

Main result on dual pair correspondences

Assume
(\omega,\Omega)
is a
(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K\times K^{\prime})
-module.
We start with a general result on the correspondence arising from
\Omega

and
Z(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
be the centers
of enveloping algebras. Let

be a finite length
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module. We define a

-coinvariants

where

is irreducible, then this definition clearly coincides with the one from the introduction.
If
\tau
is a

-type, we can define

, the lift of

, in the same way.
It is a

-module such that the
\tau
-isotypic summand of

is isomorphic to

.
The following was announced in the introduction. We now present the proof.

Theorem 10.0.1
.

Assume that the following two hold:

•

There is a correspondence of infinitesimal characters, that is,
\omega(Z(\mathfrak{g}))=\omega(Z(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}))
.

•

For every
K
-type
\tau
, there exists a finite dimensional representation
F_{\tau}
of
K^{\prime}
such that

\Theta(\tau)
is a quotient of
U(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})}F_{\tau}
.

Let
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
be irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K^{\prime})
-modules, respectively.
Then

•

\Theta(\pi)
and
\Theta(\pi^{\prime})
are finite length
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K^{\prime})
and
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules, respectively.

•

If
\tau
is a
K
-type of
\pi
, then

\dim_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{Hom}}(\Theta(\pi),\pi^{\prime})\leq\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K^{\prime}}(F_{\tau},\pi^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
\tau
be a
K
-type. Then we have a surjection

\Omega[\tau]\rightarrow\pi[\tau]\otimes\Theta(\pi)

Hence, if
\tau
is a type of
\pi
, it follows that
\Theta(\pi)
is a quotient of
\Theta(\tau)
. Hence

\Theta(\pi)
is a quotient of
U(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})}F_{\tau}
, a finitely generated module.
Furthermore, by the first assumption,
Z(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
acts on
\Theta(\pi)
by the infinitesimal character, corresponding to the
infinitesimal character of
\pi
.
Finitely generated plus infinitesimal character implies finite length. Hence
\Theta(\pi)
has finite length. We can also
prove the inequality now. Since
\Theta(\pi)
is a quotient of
U(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})}F_{\tau}
, we have

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(\Theta(\pi),\pi^{\prime})\subseteq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(U(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})}F_{\tau},\pi^{\prime})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}}(F_{\tau},\pi^{\prime})

where the last identity is a Frobenius reciprocity.

Note that we do not assume that the second bullet holds with the roles of the two algebras switched.
Hence we need a different argument in the other direction, the following lemma.

Lemma 10.0.2
.

Let
\pi^{\prime}
be a finite length
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},K^{\prime})
-module. Let
\tau
be a
K
-type. Then

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K}(\Theta(\pi^{\prime}),\tau)\leq\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K^{\prime}}(F_{\tau},\pi^{\prime}).

Proof.

We have the see-saw identity (switching the order of taking
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
and
K
-coinvariants)

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K}(\Theta(\pi^{\prime}),\tau)\cong\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}}(\Theta(\tau),\pi^{\prime}).

Since
\Theta(\tau)
is a quotient of
U(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})}F_{\tau}
, the lemma follows from the Frobenius reciprocity.
∎

Now the lemma implies that
\Theta(\pi^{\prime})
is admissible. This and infinitesimal character implies finite length.
∎

12.
Appendix - some branching rules

In this section we gather some useful branching rules for classical groups, the known branching from
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
to
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n-1)\times{\mathrm{Sp}}(1)
and a
remarkably similar branching from
{\mathrm{Spin}}(n+1)
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}(n-1)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(2)
which appeared in
[
L1
]
. We also include a result on the branching
from
F_{4}
to
B_{4}
that we have derived as a consequence of results in this paper.
Remarkably, this branching is still open, that is, there is no formula that does not involve an alternating sum of large numbers with huge cancelations.
In this section
\Lambda(G)
denotes the set of highest weight vectors for the group
G
.

12.1.
Branching rule from
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
to
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n-1)\times{\mathrm{Sp}}(1)

This formulation is due to Wallach–Yacobi
[
WY
]
. Let
\lambda\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Sp}}(n))
. Then

\lambda=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
is a descending sequence of non-negative integers.
Let
(\lambda)
denote the finite dimensional representation of
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
with that highest weight.
Let
\mu=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Sp}}(n-1))
. Then
(\mu)
appears in the restriction of
(\lambda)
if and only if
\mu
2-step interlaces
\lambda
,
that is,

x_{1}\geq y_{1}\geq x_{3},\,x_{2}\geq y_{2}\geq x_{4},\ldots,x_{n-1}\geq y_{n-1}\geq 0.

Let

(30)

z_{1}\geq z_{2}\geq\ldots\geq z_{2n-1}

be the ordering of
x_{i}
and
y_{j}
, that is,
z_{1}=x_{1}
,
z_{2}=y_{1}
or
x_{2}
, depending which one is greater etc.

Proposition 12.1.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Sp}}(n))
and
\mu\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Sp}}(n-1))
. Assume that
\mu
2-step interlaces
\lambda
. Then,
as
{\mathrm{Sp}}(1)
-modules,

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Sp}}(n-1)}((\mu),(\lambda))\cong(z_{1}-z_{2})\otimes(z_{3}-z_{4})\otimes\cdots\otimes(z_{2n-1})

where
z_{i}
are as in (
30
).

12.2.
Branching rule from
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n+1)
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n-1)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(2)

Here we have branching rule which is remarkably similar to the one for
{\mathrm{Sp}}(n)
groups. Let
\lambda\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Spin}}(2n+1))
. Then

\lambda=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
is a descending sequence of non-negative half
integers, however,
x_{i}\equiv x_{j}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
for any two indices
i
and
j
.
Let
(\lambda)
denote the finite dimensional representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n+1)
with that highest weight.
Let
\mu=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Spin}}(2n-1))
. Then
(\mu)
appears in the restriction of
(\lambda)
if and only if

x_{i}\equiv y_{j}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mu
2-step interlaces
\lambda
.
Let

z_{1}\geq z_{2}\geq\ldots\geq z_{2n-1}

be the ordering of
x_{i}
and
y_{j}
. Recall that the weights for
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2)
are half-integers. For
a\geq 0
, a half integer, define a
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2)
-module

A(a)=(a)+(a-1)+\ldots+(-a),

and for an integer
b\geq 0
, another
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2)
-module

B(z)=(b)+(b-2)+\ldots+(-b).

Then we have
[
L1
]
.

Proposition 12.2.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Spin}}(2n+1))
and
\mu\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Spin}}(2n-1))
. Assume that
x_{i}\equiv y_{j}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
and

\mu
2-step interlaces
\lambda
. Then, as
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2)
-modules,

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n-1)}((\mu),(\lambda))\cong B(z_{1}-z_{2})\otimes B(z_{3}-z_{4})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(z_{2n-1}).

12.3.
Branching rule from
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n)
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n-2)\times{\mathrm{Spin}}(2)

This is similar to the previous case, with the usual annoyances associated to

-type groups. Let
\lambda\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Spin}}(2n))
.
Then
\lambda=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
is a descending sequence of half
integers,
x_{i}\equiv x_{j}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
x_{n-1}\geq|x_{n}|
.
Let
(\lambda)
denote the finite dimensional representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n)
with that highest weight. The group
{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n)
has an outer automorphism
that changes the sign of
x_{n}
. Thus, without loss of generality, we assume that

x_{n}\geq 0
. Let
\mu=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})\in\Lambda{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n-2))
. Let
|\mu|=(y_{1},\ldots,|y_{n-1}|)
.
Then
(\mu)
appears in the restriction of
(\lambda)
if and only if

x_{i}\equiv y_{j}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
and
|\mu|
2-step interlaces
\lambda
.
Let

z_{1}\geq z_{2}\geq\ldots\geq z_{2n-1}

be the ordering of
x_{i}
and
|y_{j}|
.

Proposition 12.3.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Spin}}(2n))
and
\mu\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Spin}}(2n-2))
. Assume that
x_{n}\geq 0
,

x_{i}\equiv y_{j}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
and

\lambda

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Spin}}(2n-2)}((\mu),(\lambda))\cong B(z_{1}-z_{2})\otimes B(z_{3}-z_{4})\otimes\cdots\otimes A(z_{2n-1}).

12.4.
A branching rule from
{\mathrm{F}}_{4}
to
{\mathrm{Spin}}(9)

We consider the following see-saw pair in
{\mathrm{E}}_{8,4}
.

(31)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\mathrm{F}}_{4}&&{\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3)\\
|&\bigtimes&|\\
{\mathrm{Spin}}(9)&&{\mathrm{G}}_{2,2}\end{array}

where
{\mathrm{F}}_{4}
denote the compact Lie group of type
{\mathrm{F}}_{4}
.
For
a,b
non-negative integers where
a\geq b
, we set

\omega(a,b)=(a-b)\varpi_{4}+b\varpi_{3}=\left(a+\frac{b}{2},\frac{b}{2},\frac{b}{2},\frac{b}{2}\right)\ \in\ \Lambda({\mathrm{F}}_{4}).

Let
(\omega(a,b))
denote the irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}(9)
with the highest weight
\omega(a,b)
.

Proposition 12.4.1
.

Let
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4})\in\Lambda({\mathrm{Spin}}(9))
. Let
(w)
denote the irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{Spin}}(9)
with the highest weight
w
.

(1)

If
w_{1}+w_{2}>a+b
, then
(\omega(a,b))
does not contain
(w)
.

(2)

If
w_{1}+w_{2}\leq a+b
, then the multiplicity of
(w)
in
(\omega(a,b))
is equal to

\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{SU}}_{2}}((a-b),(a+b-w_{1}-w_{2})\otimes(w_{1}-w_{2})\otimes(2w_{4}))

where
(n)
denotes the irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
with the highest weight
n
.

Proof.

Applying the see-saw pair argument to (
31
), we get

(32)

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{Spin}}(9)}((\omega(a,b)),(w))={\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{G}}_{2,2}}(\Theta((w)),\Theta(\omega(a,b)).

By
[
HPS
]
,

\Theta(\omega(a,b))={\mathbf{A}}({\mathrm{G}}_{2,2},(a-b)[a+b+10]).

By
8.3.1

(33)

\Theta((w))={\mathbf{A}}({\mathrm{Spin}}(4,3),(w_{1}-w_{2},w_{4})[10+w_{1}+w_{2}]).

Moreover, using the filtration in Section
3.3
) (which splits because of unitarizability) (

) can be rewritten as

(34)

\Theta((w))=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}{\mathbf{A}}({\mathrm{G}}_{2,2},(m)\otimes(w_{1}-w_{2})\otimes(2w_{4})[10+w_{1}+w_{2}+m]).

Now observe that at most one term in the above sum contributes non-trivially to the right hand side of (
32
), the one such that

10+w_{1}+w_{2}+m=a+b+10
, i.e.
m=a+b-w_{1}-w_{2}
. Since
m\geq 0
, the first bullet holds and then, evidently,

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{G}}_{2,2}}(\Theta((w)),\Theta(\omega(a,b))={\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathrm{SU}}(2)}((a+b-w_{1}-w_{2})\otimes(w_{1}-w_{2})\otimes(2w_{4}),(a-b)).

∎

The above branching rule generalizes the one of Lepowsky
[
Lep
, Section 4]
, where the case

is covered.

The role of oral health education-related preventive program in improving the oral health of autistic children: A questionnaire-based study
Authors: R. Naveen Reddy, Bhavana Sujanamulk, Saikiran Bahadur, Mohamed Tharwat Salama, Snigdha Pattanaik, Alok Dubey, Azhar Mohammed, Anant Bishnu Dash, Jazib Nazeer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The poor oral health in terms of dental caries and periodontal involvement significantly affects the lives of autistic children. Hence, parents of these children must be educated through preventive programs for improving their oral health. The present study aimed to assess the role of oral health-related educational and preventive programs in improvement of oral health of autistic children.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This prospective questionnaire-based study was conducted following Institute’s ethics committee approval at clinic specially treating children with special needs. Totally, 50 autistic and 50 healthy children were selected on a random basis. Informed consent from parents or caregivers and assent from participating children were obtained before beginning of data collection. All children were rated for behavioral modification using the Frankl’s rating scale. Saliva was tested for improvement in oral hygiene using the Cariogram model. A pre-validated questionnaire was circulated among parents at first examination and was repeated at 6 months interval. The oral cavity along with teeth was examined under natural light conditions by a single trained examiner. The oral health status was examined using dft/DMFT indices. Descriptive statistics was recorded as “mean ± standard deviation”scores.RESULTS:Statistically significant improvements in oral health as well as knowledge of children and their parents were found through the intervention of education tools such as use of models, pictures, live demonstrations, and verbal communication.CONCLUSION:Imparting knowledge to the parents regarding oral hygiene practices and importance of dental visit is important as it can significantly improve the oral health of children, especially one who suffer from autism.

Introduction

The oral health status significantly impacts overall general health as well as well-beingof an individual.[1]Oral disorders like dental caries and bad oral hygiene exert negative influence over the quality of life in children specially, ones with special needs.[2]

Autism spectrum disorder is aneuro-developmental disorder that has negative effects onsocial interactions, one’s language, and behavioral as well as cognitive functions.[3]Autismspectrum disorder can be classifiedin four groups such as (a) classic autism which is severest type affecting one’s cognition, social, andbehaviorallevels, (b) pervasive developmental disorder, not otherwise specified characterized by diagnosis that includes patients having problems identicalwith autism but whichcannot meet all diagnostic criteria related to severity and age of onset, capability to interact at social level and empathy, and (c) Asperger’ssyndrome: This syndrome has characteristic features which begin before attaining three years of age such asnormal use of language and relatively normalintellect quotient but with a deficit in social abilities and reduction inempathy.[4]

The oral and dental health in these patients remains neglected as due to difficulty in getting access to specialized dentist and due to difficult behavioralmanagement of such children.[567] Hence, autistic children remain at a heightened risk of development of dental as well as oraldiseases due to poor dietary habits, deleterious habits like bruxism, and self-inflicted oral injuries.[8]

Hence, as a result of various confounding factors, parents/caregivers assume the role of decisionmaking incare of children with special healthcare needs. Hence, parents/caregivers must be given education regarding the oral health care. They must also be encouraged for participation in educational programs concerningcare of oral and dental health.[910]

There are very few studies that have dealt with parent’s knowledge, attitudes, and practice of oral health of children with autism. Hence, this study was designed with an aim toward assessing the role of oral health education-related preventive program in improving the oral health of autistic children.

Materials and Methods

Study design and settings

This was a prospective questionnaire-based study conducted after obtainment of approval from concerned ethical committees. The study was conductedat a clinic for special need children.

Study participants and sampling

Totally, 50 children diagnosed with autism were randomly selected for the study, and50 healthy children were selected as control subjects. Informed consent from parents/caregivers as well as assent from studied children was taken before circulation of pre-validated questionnaire, and clinical examination of oral cavity and teeth wasconducted. Assessment of all studied childrens’ behavior was conductedby employing Frankl’s Behavioral Rating Scale.

First phase of data collection included circulation of a questionnaire containing items on socio-demographic information which included education level of parents, their occupation, medical histories of studied children, dental history, including dental treatments, and measures undertaken for maintaining oral hygiene and dietary history.

Clinical oral examination

The oral cavity was examined by single observer in natural light illumination. Thechild was seated onto a chair, and oral cavity was examined by using a mouth mirror and a probe. ”Tell-Show-Feeland Do”technique along with visual guidance techniques was employed for studied children. The assessment of a child’s behaviorwas performed by Frankl’s Behavioral RatingScale, and the status of oral hygiene was evaluated by Silness and Loe’s plaque index. The World Health Organization criteria for dmft (primary dentition) and DMFT (permanent dentition) were used for assessment of caries experience.

The preventive and interventional strategies, including behavioral modifications using techniques like tell-show-feel-and-do, treatment, and education of autistic and related communication handicapped children, modeling, and use of positive reinforcements, were explained to children’s caregivers/parents bystudy investigator. The oral health-related educational tools that wereused included flash cards, songs, colored sheets, games, models, and verbal communications.

Instructions on oral hygiene maintenance by demonstration of simple tooth brushing method using movements in circular directions on buccalsurfaces with teeth in occlusion, antero-posterior movements on occlusal, and lingual surfaces ofposterior teeth and lingual surfaces of anterior teeth were then brushed in backward and forward strokes with vertically directed toothbrush.

Separate educational training involving factors underlying dental caries, poor oral hygiene, and maintaining oral health was given to parents or caregivers regarding health of teeth by means ofmodels, powerpoint presentations, videos, and written instructions which were repeated at 2month interval. The counseling sessions concerning value of good oral hygiene and teeth, its impact on general physical health, self-oral health care, role of fluorides, diet, importance of nutrition, causes as well as preventive measures for dental caries as well as periodontal diseases were undertaken.

Saliva collection technique: Stimulated saliva was collected after instructing the child to chew on paraffin wax. The child was asked to drool the formed saliva in a wide mouthed saliva collection bottle.

The assessment of knowledge and practice in parents/caregivers was conductedby using a questionnaire containing close-ended questions that was distributed among parents or caregivers at time of examination of the child both before and after training for interventional technique.

The questionnaire items measured parental knowledge regarding teethmeasures required to maintain oral hygiene and diet along with awareness regarding dental treatment and maintaining oral hygiene.

Inclusion criteria

Criteria for subject selection were as follows–(a) Autistic children who were capable in following verbal instructions like “sit on the chair,” “open mouth,” or “place your hands in your lap,”(b) children permitting the doctor to touch his or her’s face and mouth, (c) children who did not undergo oral prophylaxis in past 6 months,(c) children with no other diagnosis of any syndrome or any systemic condition which can affect oral health status.

Exclusion criteria

Subject exclusion was performedby considering few criteria as following (a) children who were suffering from mental and/or psychological problems,(b) children who were prescribed with chronic medications like anti-depression or anxiety drugs, anti-psychotic agents or anticonvulsant drugs, and (c) children exhibiting aggressiveness or hostility in their behavior.

Ethical consideration

The ethical clearance was obtained from concerned research committee and reviewer board (TP89-IEC/24).

Statistical analysis

Data was collected at 3 and 6 months interval and further analyzed. Descriptive statistical analysis was represented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) values for continuous types of variables, whilepercentages were used for representation of categorical variables. Comparisons among caries experience in primary, and permanent dentitions were performedby “Mann–Whitney U-test”test. Statistical software used for analyzing the observations was SPSS (version 20.0, IBM, US), while the level of significance was fixed at P < 0.05.

Results

Demographic characteristics: The study population comprised of two broad categories–(a) Group A contained 26 male and 24 female autistic children and (b) Group B consisted of 25 males and 25 female healthy controls. Mean ± SD age of autistic children was found as 9.81 ± 1.34 years, while those of controls were found to be 9.69 ± 1.81 years (P = 0.07). Autistic children were observed as having higher mean ± SDdft/DMFT scores when compared with category B i.e., control group. However, no statistically significant difference was found on comparison (P = 0.814 and 0.09, respectively) [Table 1 and Graph 1].

Following intervention byintroduction of education tools such as use of models, pictures, live demonstrations, and verbal communication and parental knowledge regarding role of deposits on tooth surfaces in causation of dental caries and periodontal problems was found to increase till 82% which was found to be statistically significant (P = 0.04). After 6months follow-up, the mean ± SD dft scores were found to reduce by 1.02 ± 0.12 and 0.98 ± 1.20 in Groups A and B, respectively. These were found to have statistical significance (P = 0.3 and 0.01, respectively). At 6thmonth follow-up, the mean ± SD DMFT scores were found to improve by 1.03 ± 0.01 and 0.07 ± 0.09, respectively. Also, there was lack of awareness of the fact that toothpastes containing fluorides play a preventive role in dental caries progression (12%). Most of the parents of either group had limited awareness regarding the importance of dentist visit. Pouching of food which is found in some autistic children was present in four autistic children in this study (4%).

Discussion

Autism spectrum disorder encompasses varieties of neurodevelopment associated disorders that include impairment in communication, socialinteraction, as well as repetitive patterns of behavior.[11]These symptoms are characteristically seen during first 3 years of one’s life. Males are affected more when compared with females. According to World Health Organization estimates, prevalence of autism spectral disorder globally is 1 per 160 individuals.[12]

The oral health status of autistic children has been studied alone or incomparisonwith the population.[13]The prevalence studies on dental caries in autistic children have reported conflicting findings ranging from low to high rate of dental caries.[1314151617]

Following interventional methods, statistically significant increase (P = 0.04) in knowledge level of parents related totooth surface deposits and their role indental caries as well as periodontal problems was found. Similarly, at 6th month follow-up, statistical significant differences (P = 0.03 and 0.01, respectively) in mean ± SD dftscores were observed. Similar, statistically significant differences were observed in mean ± SD DMFT index scores in Groups A and B (P = 0.05 and 0.04, respectively). A total of 92% of the studied parents were aware regarding role of frequent consumption of sweet/sugary foods in dental caries. This study reported low awareness (12%) regarding the protective role of fluoridated toothpastes for preventing dental caries. Mostparents had very less awareness concerningimportance of dental treatment in children. However, in present study, food pouching that is frequently seen in children with autismwas reported in only 4% children.

In contrast to our findings, Kalyoncu and Tanboga reportedno statistical association between dental caries risk and autism.[18]The poor status of oral hygiene and periodontium associated disease were found to have high prevalence among autistic children.[19] This can be attributed to lesser frequency of tooth brushing in these affected children when compared with normal children.

Similarly, AlHumaid et al. observed in their study findings that parent’s attitudes had no association with status of oral health in a autistic child. Though, positive attitude was found to be statistically associated (P = 0.043) with less amount of sugar consumption. Also, parental approach towardpracticesrequired for maintaining oral health was reactive in place of proactive role.[20]

The maintenance of oral hygiene is usually done under parent’s supervision.[18]Additionally, self-mutilation habits, medications used for treating autism, and inadequate oral hygiene maintenance practices negatively impact oral health by influencing intake of nutrition, quality of life, and general well-being.[1921]

AbdAllah et al. assessed the effectiveness of a specially designated educational and preventive program and the behavior of autistic children in improving the oral health knowledge, attitudes, and oral practices of parents and caregivers of these children. It was found that educational and preventive program was effective in improving the various caries risk factors and increasing the chance to avoid caries in autistic children and in increasing children and caregiver’s dental knowledge.[22]

Both home as well as professional dental care is required for improving the oral health in autistic children as these children suffer from impaired behaviors alongside complexsystemic conditions which makemanagement of autistic patients challenging.[21]These children exhibit hypersensitive reactions towardsensory stimulations like- sounds, touch as well as light trigger undesirable responses when undergoing dental treatments.[5]

Role of parents in dental care of autistic children is unquestionable.[23]However, there are many problems faced by parents when they seek professional work due to paucity of specialized doctors or lack of awareness regarding importance of oral health in children with special needs or the difficult behavior management involving such children. Additionally, sometimes the parents or caregivers face issues in brushing teeth of Austistic children due to increased sensitivness as well as due to unpredictable or aggressiveness in their behavior which may require use of physical restraint.[232425]

The parents of autism affected children face financial, psychological, and physical burdens. Societal pressures, increased parental care, increase burden ofhealth-related expenditure andlack of medical coverage increase the mental stress as well as anxiety.[26]

Alternatively, children suffering from autism have complete dependence upon parents in order to maintain adequate general well-being and health. Hence, a negative attitude in parent toward oral and general physical health can be directly linked poor health in a child.[27] Hence, positive parental attitude toward maintaining oral health of an autistic child is important.

Major limitation of the study was its small sample size and less sample distribution, while strength was its long follow-up for assessing behavioral modifications as well as parental knowledge on maintaining good oral hygiene in autistic children. This study is novel as it indirectly assesses parental care and provides improved techniques to handle an autistic child’s overall health and well-being.

Conclusion

Oral health has a close impact on general well-being and influences the morbidity specially, in children. Children suffering from autism are heavily dependent upon their parents or caregivers for their health and basic requirements. Studies have reported poor state of oral hygiene and dental caries in these childrenhence, more diligent efforts by parents are required for maintaining optimal oral hygiene and general well-being. This study reported significant improvements in knowledge and awareness regarding oral health practices in parents of children after receiving information by use of educational tools. Thus, constant updation in oral health practices is required to enhance the oral health of children.

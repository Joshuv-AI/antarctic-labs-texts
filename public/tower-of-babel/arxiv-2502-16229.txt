Weak Closed-loop Solvability for Discrete-time Stochastic Linear-Quadratic Optimal Control
Yue Sun, Xianping Wu, Xun Li

Abstract
In this paper, the solvability of discrete-time stochastic linear-quadratic (LQ) optimal control problem in finite horizon is considered. Firstly, it shows that the closed-loop solvability for the LQ control problem is optimal if and only if the generalized Riccati equation admits a regular solution by solving the forward and backward difference equations iteratively. To this ends, it finds that the open-loop solvability is strictly weaker than closed-loop solvability, that is, the LQ control problem is always open-loop optimal solvable if it is closed-loop optimal solvable but not vice versa. Secondly, by the perturbation method, it proves that the weak-closed loop strategy which is a feedback form of a state feedback representation is equivalent to the open-loop solvability of the LQ control problem. Finally, an example sheds light on the theoretical results established.

Introduction

Optimal control theory is an important component of modern control theory. As early as 1948, Wiener
[
1

Pioneered by Kalman

However, considering the uncertainty and inevitable random interference factors in reality, Wonham

In 1976, Bismut

From then on, many scholars began to study stochastic optimal control problems thereafter. A common assumption in most literature on stochastic LQ problems is that the state weighting matrix is non negative definite and the control weighting matrix is positive definite.
That is to say, consider the following system

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}x^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}-K^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}x^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt2\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}-K^{\varepsilon}_{2}|^{2}|x^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}|^{2}\Big)+2\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}|x^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt-x^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt2\sum\limits_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}-K^{\varepsilon}_{2}|^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|x^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}|^{2}\Big)+2\sum\limits_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}

(63)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\times\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|x^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}-x^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}\Big).

Next, it shows that

\displaystyle E\Big(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{t}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptL\cdot E\Big(|x_{0}|^{2}+\sum^{N-1}_{t=0}|u_{t}|^{2}+\sum^{N-1}_{t=0}|b_{t}|^{2}

(64)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt+\sum^{N-1}_{t=0}|\sigma_{t}|^{2}\Big),

where
x_{t}
,
u_{t}
are subject to (
8
) and
L>0
represents a genetic constant which can be different from line to line. By iteration, (
8
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle x_{t}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\Big(\prod\limits^{t-1}_{i=0}\bar{A}_{i}\Big)x_{0}+\sum\limits^{t-1}_{i=0}\Big(\prod\limits^{t-1}_{l=i+1}\bar{A}_{l}\Big)\bar{B}_{i}[u_{i}+s_{i}],

where
\bar{A}_{t}=A_{t}+w_{t}C_{t}
,
\bar{B}_{t}=B_{t}+w_{t}D_{t}
and
s_{t}=b_{t}+w_{t}\sigma_{t}
.

Thus, constants
L>0
can be selected such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}|x_{t}|^{2})

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptL\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big(|x_{0}+\sum\limits^{t-1}_{i=0}[u_{i}+s_{i}]|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptL\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big((|x_{0}|^{2}+|\sum\limits^{t-1}_{i=0}u_{i}|^{2}+|\sum\limits^{t-1}_{i=0}s_{i}|^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptL\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big((|x_{0}|^{2}+\sum\limits^{N-1}_{i=0}|u_{i}|^{2})

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt+\sum^{N-1}_{t=0}|b_{t}|^{2}+\sum^{N-1}_{t=0}|\sigma_{t}|^{2}\Big).

According to (
64
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}|x^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}-x^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}\Big)\leq L\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum^{N-1}_{t=0}|u^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}-u^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}\Big).

(65)

Based on the discussion in (

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}|x^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}-x^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}\Big)=0.

With the aid of Lemma

and Lemma

(67)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum\limits_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|v^{\varepsilon_{1}}_{t}-v^{\varepsilon_{2}}_{t}|^{2}\Big)

From (

To this end, now, we are in position to propose the equivalent relationship between the open-loop and weak closed-loop solvability for Problem (SLQ).

Theorem 5

If Problem (SLQ) is open-loop solvable, then
(K^{*},v^{*})
derived in Lemma
5
and Lemma
6
is a weak closed-loop optimal solution for Problem (SLQ) with
t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}
.
Accordingly, the open-loop solvability of Problem (SLQ) is equivalent to the weak closed-loop solvability of Problem (SLQ).

Proof:

Due to the open-loop solvability of Problem (SLQ), and following from Theorem
4
,

\{u^{\varepsilon}_{t}\}
defined in (
49
) is strongly convergent to an open-loop optimal control of Problem (SLQ), which is called as
\{u^{*}_{t}\}
. The corresponding optimal state, which is called as
x^{*}
is such that

\displaystyle x^{*}_{t+1}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptA_{t}x^{*}_{t}+B_{t}u^{*}_{t}+b_{t}+(C_{t}x^{*}_{t}+D_{t}u^{*}_{t}+\sigma_{t})w_{t},

with the initial value
x^{*}_{0}=x_{0}
.

Next, we will prove that
u^{*}=K^{*}x^{*}+v^{*}
. Then
(K^{*},v^{*})
is exactly a weak closed-loop solution to Problem (SLQ).
To justify the argument, reconsidering (
64
), it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}|x^{\varepsilon}_{t}-x^{*}_{t}|^{2}\Big)\leq L\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum^{N-1}_{t=0}|u^{\varepsilon}_{t}-u^{*}_{t}|^{2}\Big),

(68)

where
x^{\varepsilon}
is the solution to (
50
). Combining (
68
) with Theorem
4
, one has

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}|x^{\varepsilon}_{t}-x^{*}_{t}|^{2}\Big)=0.

(69)

For
t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|u^{\varepsilon}_{t}-[K^{*}_{t}x^{*}_{t}+v^{*}_{t}]|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|[K^{\varepsilon}_{t}x^{\varepsilon}_{t}+v^{\varepsilon}_{t}]-[K^{*}_{t}x^{*}_{t}+v^{*}_{t}]|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt2\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon}_{t}x^{\varepsilon}_{t}-K^{*}_{t}x^{*}_{t}|^{2}\Big)+2\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|v^{*}_{t}-v^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt4\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}|x^{\varepsilon}_{t}-x^{*}_{t}|^{2}\Big)+4\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon}_{t}-K^{*}_{t}|^{2}|x^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt+2\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|v^{*}_{t}-v^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt4\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}|x^{\varepsilon}_{t}-x^{*}_{t}|^{2}\Big)+4\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|K^{\varepsilon}_{t}-K^{*}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\times\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}}|x^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\Big)+2\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|v^{*}_{t}-v^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\Big).

) and the results in Lemma

and Lemma

,
it has

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sum_{t\in\tilde{\mathbb{N}}}|u^{\varepsilon}_{t}-[K^{*}_{t}x^{*}_{t}+v^{*}_{t}]|^{2}\Big)=0.

As stated in Theorem
4
,

Numerical Example

Consider the following system

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt(x_{t}+u_{t})w_{t},\quad t\in\{0,1\},

(71)

with initial value
x_{0}
. The associated cost function is given by

\displaystyle J(x_{0},u_{t})

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\mathbb{E}[x^{2}_{2}-\sum\limits^{1}_{t=0}u^{2}_{t}].

(72)

Following from the difference Riccati equation (
17
)
with the terminal value
P_{2}=1
, and by simple calculation, the solution of it is exactly
P_{t}\equiv 1
,
t\in\{0,1,2\}
.

It should be noted that the problem with system (
71
)-(
72
) is open-loop solvable but not closed-loop solvable due to the following reason:

\displaystyle Range(R+B^{\prime}P_{t+1}B+D^{\prime}P_{t+1}D]

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptRange(0]=\{0\},

(73)

\displaystyle Range(B^{\prime}P_{t+1}A+D^{\prime}P_{t+1}C+S]

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptRange(1]=\mathbb{R}.

(74)

That is,

\displaystyle Range(B^{\prime}P_{t+1}A+D^{\prime}P_{t+1}C+S]\nsubseteq Range(R+B^{\prime}P_{t+1}B+D^{\prime}P_{t+1}D].

Taking
\varepsilon>0
and by Theorem
3
, the solution to Riccati equation (
51
) is calculated as

\displaystyle P^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptP^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}-P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}[-1+P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}+\varepsilon]^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\frac{P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}(\varepsilon-1)}{P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}+\varepsilon-1},

P^{\varepsilon}_{t}
is actually calculated as

\displaystyle P^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\frac{\varepsilon-1}{\varepsilon+1-k}.

Accordingly, the closed-loop optimal gain matrix is such that

\displaystyle K^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt-[-1+P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}+\varepsilon]^{-1}P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt-\frac{P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}}{P^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}+\varepsilon-1}=-\frac{P^{\varepsilon}_{t}}{\varepsilon-1}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon+1-k}.

\displaystyle x^{\varepsilon}_{t+1}=(1+K^{\varepsilon}_{t})x^{\varepsilon}_{t}w_{t},\quad x^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x_{0}.

The control is given by

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}_{t}=K^{\varepsilon}_{t}x^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\prod\limits^{t-1}_{i=0}[(1+K^{\varepsilon}_{i})w_{i}]x^{\varepsilon}_{0},

(78)

that is to say,

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}_{0}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptK^{\varepsilon}_{0}x^{\varepsilon}_{0}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon+1}x_{0},

(79)

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003ptK^{\varepsilon}_{1}x^{\varepsilon}_{1}=K^{\varepsilon}_{1}(1+K^{\varepsilon}_{0})x^{\varepsilon}_{0}w_{0}

(80)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1-\frac{1}{\varepsilon+1})x^{\varepsilon}_{0}w_{0}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon+1}x_{0}w_{0}.

Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\sum\limits^{1}_{t=0}|u^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\frac{2x^{2}_{0}}{(\varepsilon+1)^{2}}\leq 2x^{2}_{0},

that is to say,
u^{\varepsilon}_{t}
is bounded. From the results in Theorem
4
, the open-loop optimal solution is given as

\displaystyle u^{*}_{0}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}u^{\varepsilon}_{0}=-x_{0},

(82)

\displaystyle u^{*}_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}u^{\varepsilon}_{1}=-x_{0}w_{0}.

(83)

Finally, based on the discussion in Theorem
5
, the weak closed-loop optimal strategy
K^{*}_{t}
is given by

\displaystyle K^{*}_{t}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-8.00003pt\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}K^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon+1-k})=-\frac{1}{1-k}.

(84)

VI
Conclusion

The open-loop, closed-loop, and weak closed-loop solvability for the stochastic discrete-time system have been discussed in this paper.
We have shown the closed-loop solvability is equivalent to the regular solvability of the generalized Riccati equation, while it also means that it’s the open-loop solvable.
However, there is an example illustrates that when the stochastic LQ problem is open-loop solvable may not be closed-loop solvable.
For the stochastic LQ problem, which is merely open-loop solvable, we have found a weak closed-loop solution whose outcome is the open-loop solution to the stochastic LQ problem which implies that there is a linear feedback form of the state for the open-loop solution.
Moreover, an equivalent relationship between the open-loop and the weak closed-loop solutions to the stochastic LQ problem has been established.

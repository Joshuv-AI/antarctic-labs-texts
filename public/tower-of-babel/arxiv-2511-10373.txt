Ehrhart Theory over Abelian Group Rings
Robert Davis, Jesús A. De Loera, Alexey Garber, Katharina Jochemko, Josephine Yu

Abstract
. We study Ehrhart series with coefficients in Abelian group rings. This opens new enumeration applications and unifies earlier variants, in particular, polynomial weighted, q q -weighted, and equivariant Ehrhart series.

1.
Introduction

Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope, i.e., a
d
-dimensional convex polytope with vertices in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The
Ehrhart function

of
P
is defined by
\ehr(P;n)=|nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
for nonnegative integers
n
.
The
Ehrhart series
of
P
is a formal power series in the variable
t
given by

\mathcal{E}(P;t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\ehr(P;n)t^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{|}nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|t^{n}.

See
[
2
,
4
,
12
,
13
]
for details. Ehrhart functions and their series have been the subject of research for many years, with numerous applications in combinatorics and algebra (see
[
2
,
4
,
8
]
and the
references therein). In this work, we present a new generalization of Ehrhart functions by changing the coefficients of the Ehrhart series and related generating functions. Specifically, for a given Abelian group
G
and a commutative ring
R
with unity, we study weighted lattice point enumeration where each lattice point is assigned a weight in the group ring
R[G]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be an Abelian group,
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to G
be a homomorphism, and
R
be a
commutative ring with unity. We define the
\varphi
-Ehrhart function
of
P
(left) and the
\varphi
-Ehrhart series
of
P
(right) as

\ehr(P,\varphi;n)=\sum_{\alpha\in nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\alpha)\in R[G]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}(P,\varphi;t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\ehr(P,\varphi;n)t^{n}\in R[G][[t]].

We study the
\varphi
-Ehrhart series and its properties. We will see that it unifies several earlier variations in Ehrhart theory, in particular results that take into account weights on the lattice points. We begin with an example that reveals the richness of this definition.

Our Contributions

Our first main result says that the
\varphi
-Ehrhart series is a rational function. We work with a general construction; for a rational polyhedron
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let the
\varphi
-integer point transform
be

\sigma(Q,\varphi;\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\alpha\in Q\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\alpha)\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}

where
\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
.
This is a formal power series in variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{d}
with coefficients in
R[G]
. These formal power series form a module
R[G][[\mathbf{z}]]
over the ring of Laurent polynomials
R[G][\mathbf{z}^{\pm}]:=R[G][z_{1},\dots,z_{d},z_{1}^{-1},\dots,z_{d}^{-1}]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the half-open rational simplicial cone

\mathcal{C}:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}u_{i}\mid\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}>0,\,\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\geq 0\right\},

where
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{d}\}
is a linearly independent set of integer vectors, and
let
\Pi
be the
fundamental parallelepiped
of this cone,

(1)

\Pi:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}u_{i}\mid\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in(0,1],\,\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\in[0,1)\right\}.

Theorem 1.3

(Rationality)
.

For a half-open rational simplicial cone
\mathcal{C}
, we have

\sigma(\mathcal{C},\varphi;\mathbf{z})=\frac{\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\alpha)\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}}{\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-\varphi(u_{i})\mathbf{z}^{u_{i}})}.

The equation is interpreted as
\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-\varphi(u_{i})\mathbf{z}^{u_{i}})\,\cdot\,\sigma(\mathcal{C},\varphi;\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\alpha)\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}

in the
R[G][\mathbf{z}^{\pm}]
-module
R[G][[t]]
.

Since every rational polyhedral cone can be decomposed as a (disjoint) union of finitely many half-open rational simplicial cones, it follows that
the
\varphi
-integer point transform of any pointed rational polyhedral cone is a rational function in
R[G](\mathbf{z})
. In other words, we can extend the discussion from
[
4
, Section 11.2]
to the
R[G]
setting. There is a unique map from the submodule of
R[G][[\mathbf{z}]]
generated by integer point transforms of rational polyhedral cones to the rational functions
R[G](\mathbf{z})
(by this we refer to the localization of
R[G][z]
by elements of the form
1-\varphi(u)\mathbf{z}^{u}
),
which is a
R[G][\mathbf{z}^{\pm}]
-module homomorphism. Moreover, since
1-\varphi(u_{i})\mathbf{z}^{u_{i}}
is not a zero divisor in
R[G](\mathbf{z})
, we get that the non-pointed cones are mapped to 0 by this homomorphism, see
[
4
, Lemma 11.2]
.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.3
we can obtain the following rationality result for
\varphi
-Ehrhart series. It can be obtained by applying homomorphism
\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}\mapsto t^{\alpha_{d+1}}
in Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For a half open simplex
\Delta
, taking
\mathcal{C}
to be the cone generated by
\Delta\times\{1\}
in one higher dimension, the
\varphi
-Ehrhart series of
\Delta
is a rational function in
t
with coefficients in
R[G]
:

(2)

\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi;t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\ehr(\Delta,\varphi;n)t^{n}=\frac{\sum_{(\beta,m)\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\varphi(\beta)t^{m}}{\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-\varphi(v_{i})t)}.

We call the numerator
h^{*}(\Delta,\varphi;t):=\sum_{(\beta,m)\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\varphi(\beta)t^{m}
the
h^{*}_{\varphi}
-polynomial
of
\Delta
.

Example 1.5
.

Recall from Example
1.2
that when
P=[0,1]^{2}
,
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
G=C_{3}
, and
\varphi(e_{1})=\varphi(e_{2})=\omega
, we obtain

\mathcal{E}(P,\varphi;t)=\frac{1+\omega t}{(1+t)(1+\omega t)(1+\omega^{2}t)}=\frac{1}{(1+t)(1+\omega^{2}t)}.

We can use (
2
) to explain why the cancellation occurs and obtain the simplified rational function more directly.

We decompose the unit square into three half-open simplices: the closed diagonal
S_{1}=\mathrm{conv}((0,0),(1,1))
, and two half-open triangles
S_{2}
and
S_{3}
.
Because of our definition of
\varphi
, there is a symmetry of
\varphi
across the line
x=y
.
Since
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, we get

\mathcal{E}(P,\varphi;t)=\mathcal{E}(S_{1},\varphi;t)+\mathcal{E}(S_{2},\varphi;t)+\mathcal{E}(S_{2},\varphi;t)=\mathcal{E}(S_{1},\varphi;t)+2\mathcal{E}(S_{2},\varphi;t)=\mathcal{E}(S_{1},\varphi;t).

Evaluating
\mathcal{E}(S_{1},\varphi;t)
immediately gives
1/((1+t)(1+\omega^{2}t))
.

A classical reciprocity result in Ehrhart theory can be recovered from our framework: Ehrhart–Macdonald reciprocity states that if
P
is a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope and
P^{\circ}
is the topological interior of
P
, then
\mathcal{E}(P;t^{-1})=(-1)^{d+1}\mathcal{E}(P^{\circ};t)
.
To adapt this result, let
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to G
be a homomorphism. By
\varphi^{\prime}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to G
we denote the homomorphism defined as
\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)=(\varphi(\alpha))^{-1}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Also, for a half-open simplex
\Delta
, let
\Delta^{\prime}
denote the half-open simplex with the same vertices but with complementary facets.

The following reciprocity theorem holds for
h_{\varphi}^{*}
-polynomials.

Theorem 1.6

(Reciprocity)
.

For every homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to G
and every half-open simplex
\Delta\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d+1}
,

h^{*}(\Delta^{\prime},\varphi;t)=\varphi(v_{1}+\ldots+v_{d+1})t^{d+1}h^{*}(\Delta,\varphi^{\prime};1/t)

More strongly,
for every simplex
\Delta
and homomorphism
\varphi
,

\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi^{\prime};t^{-1})=(-1)^{d+1}\mathcal{E}(\Delta^{\prime},\varphi;t).

Lastly, from the existence of Brion’s identity on multivariate integral transforms of polytopes one can recover the following
\varphi
-Brion version.
To each vertex
v
of a polytope
P
, we attach a
vertex cone

K_{v,P}=\{v+\lambda(u-v)\mid u\in P,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}.

From
1.3
, the
\varphi
-integral transform of each vertex cone is a multivariate rational function in variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{d}
with coefficients in
R[G]
.

Theorem 1.7

(
\varphi
-Brion)
.

For a rational polytope
P
we have the following identity for weighted integer point transforms as rational functions:

\sigma(P,\varphi,\mathbf{z})=\sum_{v\text{ vertex of }P}\sigma(K_{v,P},\varphi,\mathbf{z}).

The proofs of the above three theorems are presented in Section
2
. The rest of the paper presents several consequences and applications.
We will recover several known results by making particular choices of
G
and
R
in Sections
3
and
4
.

2.
Proofs of Rationality, Reciprocity, and
\varphi
-Brion

Our approach starts with the well-known multivariate version of Ehrhart series and Stanley’s original approach to represent Ehrhart series for simplices through summation over cones
[
11
]
. A lot of our results rely on the fact that the relations we know and love (e.g., reciprocity, Brion, etc) survive under the right maps.

Let
\Delta=\mathrm{conv}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d+1})
be a
d
-dimensional half-open simplex with vertices in
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
with first
k
facets removed. That is,
\Delta
is defined by

(3)

\Delta:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\lambda_{i}v_{i}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}\lambda_{i}=1,\,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}>0,\,\lambda_{k+1},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\geq 0\right\}.

Since every polytope can be written as a disjoint union of half-open full-dimensional simplices, we restrict our attention primarily to
\Delta
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be the half-open cone over
\Delta
, i.e., the cone generated by vectors
u_{i}=(v_{i},1)
with the first
k
facets removed. Also let
\Pi
be the fundamental parallelepiped of this cone. With these facts and notation we will now prove
1.3
; note the minor change in dimension.

Proof of
1.3
(Rationality).

We will rewrite the
\varphi
-integer point transform of
\mathcal{C}

\sigma(\mathcal{C},\varphi,\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\varphi(\alpha)\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}

as sum of different classes of lattice points with respect to translations by vectors
u_{i}
.

We can represent
\mathcal{C}
as the disjoint union of translated copies of
\Pi
as follows:

\mathcal{C}=\bigsqcup_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}}\left(\Pi+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}c_{i}u_{i}\right),

where
c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{d+1})
. For every
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
, let
\beta
be the corresponding representative in
\Pi
. Then

\alpha=\beta+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}c_{i}u_{i}

for a certain choice of nonnegative integers
c_{i}
, and all options for
c_{i}
will be present among the points in
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
. Using this in the formula for the
\varphi
-integer point transform, we get

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\varphi(\alpha)\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\left(\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}}\varphi\left(\beta+\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}c_{i}u_{i}\right)\mathbf{z}^{\beta+\sum c_{i}u_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\beta\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\varphi(\beta)\mathbf{z}^{\beta}\right)\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}}\left(\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}c_{i}u_{i}\right)\mathbf{z}^{\sum c_{i}u_{i}}\right).

Using that
\varphi
is a homomorphism we transform

\displaystyle\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}}\left(\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}c_{i}u_{i}\right)\mathbf{z}^{\sum c_{i}u_{i}}\right)=\sum_{c\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(\varphi(u_{i}))^{c_{i}}(\mathbf{z}^{u_{i}})^{c_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1+\varphi(u_{i})\mathbf{z}^{u_{i}}+(\varphi(u_{i}))^{2}(\mathbf{z}^{u_{i}})^{2}+\ldots)=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-\varphi(u_{i})\mathbf{z}^{u_{i}})}.

Combining with (
4
) we get the claimed equality.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

We note that there is an alternative way to get the same result directly from the classical integer point transform formula. Rewrite the classical formula for integer point transforms
as an equality of formal (Laurent) series

\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}\right)\left(\Pi(1-\mathbf{z}^{u_{i}})\right)=\sum_{\beta\in\Pi}\mathbf{z}^{\beta}

with integer
coefficients. We apply two homomorphisms, the ring homomorphism from
\mathbb{Z}
to
R
that takes
1
to the unity of
R
, and the group homomorphism
\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}\mapsto\varphi(\alpha)\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}
from the group
Z_{d+1}
consisting of all powers of
\mathbf{z}
(isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
) to the direct product
G\times Z_{d+1}
. The result then follows from the equality of formal (Laurent) series with coefficients in
R[G]
.

We continue with the reciprocity Theorem
1.6
. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to G
be a homomorphism. Let
\varphi^{\prime}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to G
be the homomorphism defined as
\varphi^{\prime}(\alpha)=(\varphi(\alpha))^{-1}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For a half-open simplex
\Delta
, let
\Delta^{\prime}
be the half-open simplex on the same vertices with complementary facets removed. In particular, if
\Delta
is a closed simplex, then
\Delta^{\prime}
is its interior.
We establish the following reciprocity property for
h^{*}
-polynomials.

h^{*}(\Delta^{\prime},\varphi;t)=\varphi(v_{1}+\ldots+v_{d+1})t^{d+1}h^{*}(\Delta,\varphi^{\prime};1/t).

Proof of
1.6
(Reciprocity).

Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d+1}
be vertices of
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
. Corollary
1.4
gives

\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi;t)=\frac{\sum_{(\beta,m)\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\varphi(\beta)t^{m}}{\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-\varphi(v_{i})t)}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi^{\prime};1/t)=\frac{\sum_{(\beta,m)\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}(\varphi(\beta))^{-1}t^{-m}}{\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-(\varphi(v_{i}))^{-1}t^{-1})}.

Multiplying both numerator and denominator of the
\varphi^{\prime}
-Ehrhart series
\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi^{\prime};1/t)
by
t^{d+1}\varphi(v_{1}+\ldots+v_{d+1})
and bringing to the previous denominator yields

\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi^{\prime};1/t)=(-1)^{d+1}\frac{\sum_{(\beta,m)\in\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\varphi(v_{1}+\ldots+v_{d+1}-\beta)t^{d+1-m}}{\prod_{i=1}^{d+1}(1-\varphi(v_{i})t)}.

The fundamental parallelepipeds
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
for
\Delta
and
\Delta^{\prime}
are reflections of each other with respect to
\frac{1}{2}(v_{1}+\ldots+v_{d+1},d+1)
. Thus, the last numerator is the
h^{*}_{\varphi}
-polynomial of
\Delta^{\prime}
and the statement of the theorem follows.
∎

Proof of
1.7
(
\varphi
-Brion).

By the classical Brion’s theorem

\sigma(P,1,\mathbf{z})=\sum\sigma(K_{v,P},1,\mathbf{z})

in
\mathbb{Z}(\mathbf{z})
where the sum is over vertices of
P
. The formula follows by applying to this a ring homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}(\mathbf{z})\rightarrow R[G](\mathbf{z})
given by
z_{i}\mapsto\varphi(e_{i})z_{i}
.
∎

We note that a similar homomorphism-based approach was recently used by Beck and Kunze
[
3
]
to generalize results of Chapoton
[
5
]
; see also Section
4
below.

3.
Classical Ehrhart Results from
\varphi
-Ehrhart theory

We indicate how to recover the core results on Ehrhart quasipolynomials for integer and rational polytopes from our
\varphi
-Ehrhart theory. We briefly sketch proofs in this section.

For a lattice polytope
P
, we split
P
into a disjoint union of half-open lattice simplices
\Delta_{j}
and apply
1.4
to each simplex using
G=\{1\}
, the trivial group, the trivial homomorphism
\varphi
and
R=\mathbb{Z}
. For each
\Delta_{j}
, the numerator of
\mathcal{E}(\Delta_{j},\varphi;t)
is a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients and all denominators are
(1-t)^{d+1}
. Adding all rational functions for Ehrhart series we obtain that

\mathcal{E}(P;t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\ehr(P;n)t^{n}=\frac{h^{*}(t)}{(1-t)^{d+1}}

where
h^{*}(t)
is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.

For a rational polytope
P
with denominator
q
we notice that counting integer points in
nP
is the same as counting points of the lattice
q\mathbb{Z}
in
nQ
where
Q:=qP
. We apply
1.4
for the group
G=(\mathbb{Z}_{q})^{d}
with generators
g_{i}
for copies of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. Define the homomorphism
\varphi
as
\varphi(\alpha)=\varphi(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}):=\prod g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
, and
R=\mathbb{Z}
. Again, if
\Delta_{j}
are simplices that give a half-open decomposition of
Q
, then the denominators of
\mathcal{E}(\Delta_{j},\varphi;t)
are products of expressions
1-\varphi(v_{j})t
.

In
R[G][t]
we have
(1-\varphi(v_{j})t)(1+\varphi(v_{i})t+(\varphi(v_{i})t)^{2}+\ldots+(\varphi(v_{i})t)^{q-1})=1-t^{q}
. Thus, multiplying all numerators and denominators by
\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}(\varphi(v_{i})t)^{k}
for each
i
brings all Ehrhart series to the common denominator
(1-t^{q})^{d+1}
while keeping the nonnegative coefficients in the numerators. Finally, notice that counting points of
q\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in
nQ
corresponds to considering the coefficient of the identity element of
G
in the resulting equality of the power series
\mathcal{E}(Q,\varphi;t)
and the rational function.

More generally, considering the coefficients of other group elements, we can extract a rational expression for the number of points in shifted dilations of rational polytopes.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a rational polytope with denominator
q
and let
v
be a rational vector with denominator
q
. Then

\sum_{n\geq 0}|(nP-v)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|t^{n}=\frac{h_{v}^{*}(t)}{(1-t^{q})^{d+1}}

where
h_{v}^{*}(t)
is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.

In a similar but different setting, the authors of
[
9
]
showed how the Ehrhart quasi-polynomials change as a polytope
P
is translated by a rational vector
v
. In other words, they study the function

|n(P-v)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
.

4.
Weights in Lattice Points via
\varphi
-Ehrhart functions

We turn to weighted versions of Ehrhart functions when the lattice points have an associated weight value, see
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
and the references therein. Here, we think of
\varphi
as a weight and recover several known results in weighted Ehrhart theory.

4.1.
Chapoton’s
q
-Ehrhart

First we recover and generalize Chapoton’s
q
-Ehrhart setting
[
5
]
.
Let
q
be an indeterminate variable and let
(G_{q},\cdot)\cong(\mathbb{R},+)
be the multiplicative group of real powers of
q
. For every linear function
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, the map
\varphi_{q}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to G_{q}
defined as
\varphi_{q}(x)=q^{\ell(x)}
is a homomorphism. Combined with Corollary
1.4
, this gives the following result.

Proposition 4.1

(Chapoton,
[
5
]
)
.

If
\ell
is a linear form with integer coefficients which is positive on all vertices of simplex
\Delta=\mathrm{conv}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d+1})
, then

\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi_{q};t)
is a rational function in
t
and
q
with denominator
\prod(1-q^{\ell(v_{i})}t)
.

Proof.

The proposition is immediate from Corollary
1.4
. The numerator is a polynomial in
t
and
q
because
\ell(v_{i})
is a nonnegative integer.
∎

The proposition can be generalized to the case of arbitrary polytopes by using half-open triangulations. For the case of a simplex and certain special families of polytopes
[
7
]
the nonnegativity property for the numerator continues to hold, but it fails for general polytopes as noted in
[
5
]
.

Our approach does not require
\ell
to have integer coefficients, but we forfeit the polynomiality of the numerator in exchange. In the following, let
G_{q}
denote the multiplicative group of
R
-powers of a variable
q
, which is isomorphic to the Abelian group
(R,+)
.

4.2.
Polynomial Weights

Next, we generalize Corollary
1.4
to the case when each point in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is weighted by its evaluation of a given polynomial in
R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
, in addition to the weight from
G
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
w
be a polynomial in
R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. We define the
(\varphi,w)
-Ehrhart function of
P
in
R[G]
(left) and the
(\varphi,w)
-Ehrhart series of
P
in
R[G][[t]]
(right) as

\ehr(P,\varphi,w;n):=\sum_{\alpha\in nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}(P,\varphi,w;t):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\ehr(P,\varphi,w;n)t^{n}.

The
(\varphi,w)
-Ehrhart series can be written as a rational function in
R[G](t)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be an Abelian group,
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to G
be a homomorphism, and
\Delta
be a half-open simplex with vertices in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Suppose
w\in R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
is a polynomial of degree
m
. Then
the
(\varphi,w)
-Ehrhart series

\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi,w;t):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in n\Delta\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)\right)t^{n}

can be written as a rational function in
R[G][[t]]
with denominator
\prod(1-\varphi(v_{i})t))^{m+1}
.

We give an example to illustrate the process;
4.3
can be proved using similar methods by differentiation with respect to auxiliary variables
q_{j}
. Here,
G
and
R
are any appropriate Abelian group and commutative ring with unity, respectively.

Example 4.4
.

Let
\Delta
be the half-open triangle
\mathrm{conv}((0,0),(1,0),(1,1))\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the edge lying on
x_{1}=x_{2}
removed.
The fundamental parallelepiped
\Pi
contains the single integer point
(1,0,1)
.
Let
w(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}
. Define
\psi:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to G_{q}\times G
be
\psi(x_{1},x_{2})=q^{x_{1}}\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})
.
By Corollary
1.4
, we obtain

\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\psi;t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in n\Delta\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q^{w(\alpha)}\varphi(\alpha)\right)t^{n}=\frac{q\varphi(e_{1})t}{(1-t)(1-q\varphi(e_{1})t)(1-q\varphi(e_{1}+e_{2})t)}.

We formally differentiate with respect to
q
using
\frac{d}{dq}\left(q^{x}\right)=xq^{x-1}
for
x\in R
, and the quotient rule on the right-hand side. This gives

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w(\alpha)q^{w(\alpha)-1}\varphi(\alpha)\right)t^{n}=\frac{t\varphi(e_{1})(1-\varphi(e_{1})\varphi(e_{1}+e_{2})q^{2}t^{2})}{(1-t)(1-q\varphi(e_{1})t)^{2}(1-q\varphi(e_{1}+e_{2})t)^{2}}.

Each coefficient of
t^{n}
is a finite sum of powers of
q
with coefficients from
R[G]
. This allows us to evaluate at
q=1
and get the expression we seek:

\mathcal{E}(\Delta,\varphi,w;t)=\frac{t\varphi(e_{1})(1-\varphi(e_{1})\varphi(e_{1}+e_{2})t^{2})}{(1-t)(1-\varphi(e_{1})t)^{2}(1-\varphi(e_{1}+e_{2})t)^{2}}.

The rational function here had cancellation, but this does not always happen.

As with the case without polynomial weights, Theorem
4.3
generalizes to lattice polytopes through half-open triangulations. The weighted Ehrhart theory with polynomial weights (see
[
1
]
) can be recovered by using the trivial group
G
with ring
R=\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, we again have a Brion’s theorem with polynomial weights. For a rational polyhedron
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\rightarrow G
, and polynomial weight
w
with coefficients in a commutative ring
R
, let the
w
-weighted
\varphi
-integer point transform be

\sigma(Q,\varphi,w;\mathbf{z})=\sum_{\alpha\in Q\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w(\alpha)\varphi(\alpha)\mathbf{z}^{\alpha}.

It is a multivariate rational function in variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{d}
with coefficients in
R[G]
.
Applying the homomorphisms and differentiation used before we can also obtain:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
P
be a rational polytope. We have the following identity of rational functions:

\sigma(P,\varphi,w;\mathbf{z})=\sum_{v\text{ vertex of }P}\sigma(K_{v,P},\varphi,w;\mathbf{z}).

5.
Equivariant
\varphi
-Ehrhart theory

Suppose a finite group
H
is acting linearly on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and a lattice polytope
P\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is invariant under the
H
action. The action of
H
induces a natural permutation action on the set of lattice points in
nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for each integer
n\geq 0
. Assume that the group homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\rightarrow G
is
H
-invariant, i.e.,
\varphi(h\cdot\alpha)=\varphi(\alpha)
for all
h\in H,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

We can combine Stapledon’s equivariant Ehrhart theory
[
14
]
with our homomorphism
\varphi
as follows. The
representation ring

\mathop{Rep}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
of
H
is generated as an Abelian group by isomorphism classes of linear representations of
H
over
\mathbb{C}
, modulo the relations
[V]+[W]=[V\oplus W]
. Multiplication is given by tensor product of representations:
[V]\cdot[W]=[V\otimes W]
. The map sending a representation to its character gives an injective ring homomorphism from
\mathop{Rep}_{\mathbb{C}}(H)
to the ring of
\mathbb{C}
-valued class functions on
H
.
All our representations here have integer value characters, so we can tensor
\mathbb{Z}
-valued class functions with
R[G]
to work in the ring of
R[G]
-valued class functions on
H
.

For a positive integer
n
, let
\ehr(P,\varphi,H;n)
be the class function on
H
given by

\ehr(P,\varphi,H;n):h\mapsto\sum_{\{\alpha\in nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid h\cdot\alpha=\alpha\}}\varphi(\alpha)\in R[G].

Equivalently, in the representation ring tensored with
R[G]
, we have

\ehr(P,\varphi,H;n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{orbit }\mathcal{O}\text{ under }\\
H\text{ action on }nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\end{subarray}}\varphi(\mathcal{O})[\rho_{\mathcal{O}}]

where
\varphi(\mathcal{O}):=\varphi(x)
for any
x\in\mathcal{O}
, and
[\rho_{\mathcal{O}}]
is the permutation representation of
H
acting on the orbit
\mathcal{O}
.
The equivariant weighted Ehrhart series is then defined following the classical case as

\sum_{n\geq 0}\ehr(P,\varphi,H;n)t^{n}.

This is a rational function whose coefficients are
R[G]
valued class functions of
H
. We compute this in a small example.

Example 5.1
.

Consider the same setting as in Example
1.2
. Let
P=[0,1]^{2}
be a unit square,
let
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rightarrow C_{3}=\langle\omega\rangle
be given by
\varphi(x_{1})=\varphi(x_{2})=\omega
, and let
R=\mathbb{Z}
. Let
H=C_{2}
be the group of two elements acting on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by reflection across the line
x_{1}=x_{2}
, and
\rho:H\rightarrow\mathop{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the corresponding complex representation. We will use
1
and
\varepsilon
to denote the two isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of
H
in
\mathop{Rep}(H)
, with
1
being the trivial representation, and
\varepsilon^{2}=1
.

We obtain the weighted equivariant Ehrhart series by counting the lattice points in the fundamental domain
\mathrm{conv}\{(0,0),(n,0),(n,n)\}
. The lattice points on the diagonal are fixed points, each contributing a trivial representation, while the other lattice points come in size two orbits, each contributing
1+\varepsilon
. From the multivariate Ehrhart series of the fundamental triangle

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\alpha\in nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}}x^{\alpha}t^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-x_{1}t)(1-x_{1}x_{2}t)}

we substitute
x_{1},x_{2}
with
\omega
and first count all lattice points with weight
1+\varepsilon
, then subtract away the contribution of
\varepsilon
by the diagonal, to obtain the equivariant weighted Ehrhart series

(5)

\frac{1+\varepsilon}{(1-t)(1-\omega t)(1-\omega^{2}t)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{(1-t)(1-\omega^{2}t)}=\frac{1+\varepsilon\omega t}{(1-t)(1-\omega t)(1-\omega^{2}t)}.

Alternatively, we subdivide
P
into two
H
-invariant unimodular triangles:

T_{1}=\mathrm{conv}\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0)\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{2}=\mathrm{conv}\{(1,1),(0,1),(1,0)\}.

We adapt
[
14
, Proposition 6.1]
to our case, to obtain the Ehrhart series for
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
respectively as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-t)\det(I-\rho\omega t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-\omega t)(1-\varepsilon\omega t)},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\omega^{2}t)\det(I-\rho\omega t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\omega^{2}t)(1-\omega t)(1-\varepsilon\omega t)}.

The factors
(1-t)
and
(1-\omega^{2}t)
correspond to the fixed points
(0,0)\in T_{1}
with weight
1
and
(1,1)\in T_{2}
with weight
\omega^{2}
respectively. The weighted equivariant Ehrhart series of the square is, via inclusion-exclusion, the sum of these two rational functions minus
\frac{1}{(1-\omega t)(1-\varepsilon\omega t)}
for the common edge between the two triangles.
Multiplying both numerator and denominator by
(1+\varepsilon\omega t)
and simplifying gives us

\frac{1+\varepsilon\omega t}{(1-t)(1-\omega t)(1-\omega^{2}t)}
as in (
5
).

In general, a weighted analogue of
[
14
, Proposition 6.1]
for
H
-invariant simplices has the product of
\det(I-\rho\,\varphi(\mathcal{O})t)
over all orbits
\mathcal{O}
of vertices for the denominator and the weighted sum of lattice points in the half-open parallelepiped for the numerator.

6.
Conclusions

We showed that changing Ehrhart functions via the homomorphism
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{R}
and using group ring
R[G]
coefficients extends classical results and shows new ones. We end with natural questions: How to compute
\varphi
-Ehrhart series efficiently? What is the complexity of counting lattice points modulo a prime – is it easier than over
\mathbb{Z}
? What further applications or links are possible? For example, is there a relation to harmonics and graded Ehrhart theory studied in
[
10
]
?—Quo vadis, Ehrhart theory?

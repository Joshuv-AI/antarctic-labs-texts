A Unified Decentralized Nonconvex Algorithm under Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz Property
Hao Wu, Liping Wang, Hongchao Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the decentralized optimization problem of minimizing a finite sum of continuously differentiable and possibly nonconvex functions over a fixed-connected undirected network. We propose a unified decentralized nonconvex algorithmic framework that subsumes existing state-of-the-art gradient tracking algorithms and particularly several quasi-Newton algorithms. We present a general analytical framework for the convergence of our unified algorithm under both nonconvex and the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz condition settings. We also propose some quasi-Newton variants that fit into our framework, where economical implementation strategies are derived for ensuring bounded eigenvalues of Hessian inverse approximations. Our numerical results show that these newly developed algorithms are very efficient compared with other state-of-the-art algorithms for solving decentralized nonconvex smooth optimization.

INTRODUCTION

In the era of data explosion and connected intelligence, decentralized optimization has emerged as a fundamental computational paradigm for large-scale and privacy-aware systems. This paper focuses on solving optimization problems over multi-node networks where no central server exists-a setting that naturally arises in modern applications including but not limited to decentralized resources control

We consider a network of
n

\mathcal{G}=\left(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\right)

\mathcal{V}=\{1,\ldots,n\}

\mathcal{E}

The convergence rates of various gradient-based methods are highly sensitive to the condition number of the objective function.
In practical applications, the considered objective could be badly scaled or ill-conditioned. The Newton type and quasi-Newton techniques are efficient ways to improve the

Remarkably, we find in this decentralized quasi-Newton method under the strongly convex setting

In this paper, we unify and generalize a class of decentralized algorithms under the nonconvex settings. All quasi-Newton’s variants in this framework have global convergence (stationarity of every limit point of the iterates) under mild assumptions and proper choice of step sizes.

In recent years, the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KŁ) property, initially introduced by Łojasiewicz
[

. However, extending such analyses to the decentralized setting poses significant challenges, primarily due to the nonmonotone decrease of the objective function sequence, which complicates the full convergence analysis of iterates for nearly all decentralized algorithms.
Several preliminary works have explored the convergence of decentralized algorithms under the KŁ property

Most of these studies

,

rely on an abstract convergence framework that assumes the existence of a bounded sequence generated by a descent algorithm, along with a potential function that also satisfies the KŁ property. However, the KŁ property imposed on the potential function cannot generally transfer to the objective function, vice versa. Although Chen et al.
[
10
]
effectively leveraged the KŁ property directly on the objective function, they postulate a strong connectivity assumption on the underlying network.
In this paper, we proceed to utilize the KŁ property to eastablish the refined whole sequence convergence for all algorithms subsumed into our proposed framework.

Our main contributions in this paper can be summarized as follows.

1.

We introduce a Unified Decentralized Nonconvex Algorithm (UDNA) which is a novel framework that systematically incorporates various gradient tracking methods
[
57
,
40
,
36
,
37
,
14
,
43
]
and several well-developed quasi-Newton methods
[
25
,
18
,
46
,
62
]
.
So, we can investigate and summarize existing decentralized algorithms, and design new efficient algorithms by choosing from different combinations of communication and second-order approximation strategies, all within a cohesive analytical structure.

2.

We first establish a robust subsequence convergence theory for the UDNA family under standard assumptions (Lipschitz gradients and lower-bounded objectives), provided the approximate matrices remain positive definite with bounded eigenvalues. More significantly, we overcome the key challenge of nonmonotonicity in decentralized optimization by successfully incorporating the KŁ property of the objective function itself. This analysis not only guarantees convergence to stationary points but also, for the first time in the decentralized literature, establishes the full sequence convergence of iterates generated by a quasi-Newton based framework, a result previously unavailable for such a broad class of methods.

3.

We instantiate the UDNA framework with several innovative and computationally efficient quasi-Newton variants. A key technical contribution is the design of novel Hessian inverse approximation techniques that inherently ensure bounded eigenvalues, making them directly applicable within our framework. These newly proposed methods are so economical that they require only vector-vector products and minimize per-iteration computation and memory costs.
Our numerical experiments on nonconvex optimization problems show newly proposed methods are very effective compared with several state-of-the-art decentralized methods.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we first propose the UDNA framework, show its subsequence convergence, and provide the enhanced convergence analysis under the KŁ property. The approaches of generating Hessian inverse approximation are also presented in this section.
Numerical experiments of comparing our new methods with other well-established methods for solving decentralized nonconvex optimization are presented in Section 3.
We finally draw some conclusions in Section 4.

2.1.
Unified Nonconvex Algorithm Framework

We propose a general framework of decentralized nonconvex methods, termed UDNA (Unified Decentralized Nonconvex Algorithm), as follows,

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\bf{x}}^{t+1}={\bf{A}}{\bf{x}}^{t}-\alpha{\bf{B}}{\bf{H}}^{t}{\bf{v}}^{t},

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\bf{v}}^{t+1}={\bf{C}}{\bf{v}}^{t}+{\bf{D}}({\bf{g}}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}^{t}),

{\bf{v}}^{0}={\bf{g}}^{0}

{\bf{H}}^{t}
denotes a symmetric block diagonal matrix whose
i

{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}

serves as a tracking variable that approximates to the global gradient
\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})

{\bf{A}}=\tilde{{\bf{A}}}\otimes{\bf{I}}_{p}
,
{\bf{B}}=\tilde{{\bf{B}}}\otimes{\bf{I}}_{p}
,
{\bf{C}}=\tilde{{\bf{C}}}\otimes{\bf{I}}_{p}
, and
{\bf{D}}=\tilde{{\bf{D}}}\otimes{\bf{I}}_{p}
are general symmetric matrices that satisfy the following structural assumption.

Assumption 3
.

The matrices

,

,
\tilde{{\bf{C}}}
, and
\tilde{{\bf{D}}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf{A}}}=\sum_{p=0}^{P_{A}}a_{p}\tilde{{\bf{W}}}^{p},\quad\tilde{{\bf{B}}}=\sum_{p=0}^{P_{B}}b_{p}\tilde{{\bf{W}}}^{p},\quad\tilde{{\bf{C}}}=\sum_{p=0}^{P_{C}}c_{p}\tilde{{\bf{W}}}^{p},\text{ and }\tilde{{\bf{D}}}=\sum_{p=0}^{P_{D}}d_{p}\tilde{{\bf{W}}}^{p},

Remark 2.3

It should be noticed that Berahas et al.

proposed a Gradient Tracking Algorithmic (GTA) framework which unifies various gradient tracking methods. All the methods covered in the GTA framework are also included in our framework. Moreover, the convergence of GTA is only studied for the strongly convex objectives. In contrast, we analyse the convergence under the general nonconvex settings.

A stochastic nonconvex unified framework, termed SUDA
[

Our framework successfully addresses the convergence guarantees for these quasi-Newton variants applied in nonconvex settings, which remains a challenging and less explored area. Moreover, we employ a different analytical framework of subsequence convergence compared to SUDA and, more importantly, establish the stronger result of whole sequence convergence by leveraging the KŁ property of the objective function.

2.2.
Global convergence

We now analyze the global convergence of the proposed unified algorithm UDNA for minimizing (
1.1
) in which the local objective function
f_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
,
is Lipschitz continuously differentiable, but possibly nonconvex. For notational convenience, we define

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}=\rho({\bf{A}}-{\bf{M}}),\quad\sigma_{B}=\rho({\bf{B}}-{\bf{M}}),

\displaystyle\sigma_{C}=\rho({\bf{C}}-{\bf{M}}),\quad\sigma_{D}=\rho({\bf{D}}-{\bf{M}}).

The convergence analysis relies on the following fundamental assumption, which require the sequence of approximate matrices
\{{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}\}
to have uniformly bounded eigenvalues in the subspace spanned by
\{{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\}
. This provides the necessary control over the approximation quality of the Hessian or its inverse, allowing us to establish a unified convergence theory for all algorithms subsumed by the UDNA framework, regardless of the specific choice of
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}
.

Assumption 4
.

The approximate matrices
\{{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}\}
satisfy

\psi\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\leq({\bf{v}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t},\text{ and }\|{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\leq\Psi^{2}\|{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2},

for any
t
and
i
, where
\Psi\geq\psi>0
and
\{{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}\}
are from (
2.3
).

This assumption implies that the eigenvalues of
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}
in the direction of
{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
lie within the interval
[\psi,\Psi]
.
However, ensuring eigenvalue boundedness of
{\bf{H}}^{t}
is a major challenge in decentralized nonconvex settings. To address this critical issue, we will provide several schemes different from the previously proposed ones in
[
62
]
.
We will not describe how to construct
{\bf{H}}^{t}
until in Subsection 2.4, where specific updating schemes for
{\bf{H}}^{t}
satisfying Assumption
4
will be proposed.
Recall the notation
\overline{\nabla}f({\bf{x}}^{t})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}({\bf{x}}_{i}^{t})
.
Then, we obtain
{\bf{1}}\otimes\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})={\bf{M}}{{\bf{g}}}^{t}
. Note that if
{\bf{x}}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}
satisfies

(2.9)

\|\overline{\nabla}f({\bf{x}}^{*})\|^{2}+\|{\bf{x}}^{*}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{*}\|^{2}=0,

then we have
{\bf{x}}^{*}_{1}=\ldots={\bf{x}}^{*}_{i}=:{\bf{z}}^{*}
and
{\bf{z}}^{*}
would be a first-order stationary point of (
1.1
).
To obtain (
2.9
), it suffices to show
\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|\rightarrow 0
,
\|{\bf{v}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{v}}^{t}\|\rightarrow 0
, and
\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty

since
{\bf{M}}{\bf{v}}^{t}={\bf{M}}{\bf{g}}^{t}
for any
t
by (
2.5
).
Hence, by (
2.9
), we say
{\bf{x}}^{t}
is an
\epsilon
-stationary solution for some
\epsilon>0
if

(2.10)

\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}+\|{\bf{v}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}+\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}\leq\epsilon.

Given any
\epsilon>0
, to show UDNA will generate a (
{\bf{x}}^{t},{\bf{v}}^{t})
satisfying (
2.10
),
we define the following potential function

(2.11)

P({\bf{x}}^{t},{\bf{v}}^{t})=F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})+\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{1-(1+\tau)\sigma_{A}^{2}}{4(1+1/\eta)L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2},

where
\eta
and
\tau
are some positive constants.
The term
F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})
corresponds to the objective value at the average of local variables, while
\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}
measures the consensus error, i.e., the deviation of the local variables from their global average.
The term
\|{\bf{v}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}
captures the gradient tracking error, representing the difference between the average-gradient approximations and the true average gradient.
Since each
f_{i}
is lower bounded,
P
is also bounded below. To establish the sufficient descent property of
P({\bf{x}}^{t},{\bf{v}}^{t})
, we begin by deriving a recursion relationship of
F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
, and
4
hold. Let
\{{\bf{x}}^{t}\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
1
(UDNA). We have for all
t\geq 0
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1})\leq

\displaystyle F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})-\left(\frac{\alpha\psi}{2n}-\frac{L\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2n}\right)\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{L^{2}\alpha\Psi^{2}}{n}\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\Psi^{2}}{n}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}.

Proof.

By the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla F
, we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1})\leq F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})+\Big\langle\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}),\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}-\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\Big\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}-\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\|^{2},

Define an auxiliary sequence
\{\tilde{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\}_{t=0}^{T}
such that for each node
i
,
\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t+1}={{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}-\alpha{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
and
\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{0}={{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{0}
, and observe that
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}=\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}
.
We now decompose the inner product term in (
2.13
) as follows:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\Big\langle\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}),\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}-\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\Big\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}),\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t+1}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle{{\bf{v}}}_{i}^{t},\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t+1}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle}_{\text{term (I)}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})-\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t}),\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t+1}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle}_{\text{term (II)}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})-{{\bf{v}}}_{i}^{t},\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t+1}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle}_{\text{term (III)}}.

We now bound each term separately.

Term (I):
By Assumption
4
, we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle{{\bf{v}}}_{i}^{t},\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t+1}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle\leq-\frac{\alpha\psi}{n}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}.

Term (II):
Using Young’s inequality with some
c>0
and Assumption
4
, we deduce

(2.16)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})-\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t}),\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t+1}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\|\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})-\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2nc}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}\leq\frac{L^{2}c}{2n}\|{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2nc}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2},

where the last inequality follows from the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f_{i}
and the relation
{\bf{1}}\otimes\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}={\bf{M}}{{\bf{x}}}^{t}
.

Term (III):
Applying Young’s inequality with some
d>0
, Assumption
4
, and the relation
{\bf{1}}\otimes\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})={\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}
from (
2.5
), we derive

(2.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\langle\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})-{{\bf{v}}}_{i}^{t},\tilde{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t+1}-{{\bf{x}}}_{i}^{t}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2n}\|{\bf{1}}\otimes\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})-{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2nd}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}\leq\frac{d}{2n}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2nd}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}.

Plugging (
2.15
), (
2.16
), and (
2.17
) back in (
2.14
) yields

(2.18)

\displaystyle\Big\langle\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}),\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}-\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\alpha\psi}{n}-\frac{\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2n}\left(\frac{1}{c}+\frac{1}{d}\right)\right)\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{L^{2}c}{2n}\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{d}{2n}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}.

For term
\|\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}-\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\|^{2}
in (
2.13
), it follows from Assumption
4
that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\|\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}-\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\|{\bf{M}}(\tilde{{\bf{x}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{x}}}^{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{n}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}

Finally, substituting (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) into (
2.13
), and choosing
c=2\alpha\Psi^{2}/\psi
,
d=2\alpha\Psi^{2}/\psi
, we get obtain the desired inequality (
2.12
).
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
, and
4
hold. Let
\{{\bf{x}}^{t}\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
1
(UDNA). If

(2.20)

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\min\left\{\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})^{2}n}{64L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}\Psi^{2}},\frac{(1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2})\psi}{(2L+n+8\sigma_{B}^{2}n)\Psi^{2}},\frac{(1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2})n}{2L^{2}\Psi^{2}}\right\},

then we have the following convergence rate for UDNA

(2.21)

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\{\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}+\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}+\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}\right\}\leq\frac{P({\bf{x}}^{0},{\bf{v}}^{0})-P({\bf{x}}^{T},{\bf{v}}^{T})}{\gamma\min\{\alpha,1\}T},

where
P
is the potential function defined in (
2.11
) with
\tau=\frac{1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2}}{2{\sigma}_{A}^{2}}
and
\eta=\frac{1-{\sigma}_{C}^{2}}{2{\sigma}_{C}^{2}}
, and
\gamma=\min\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}

(2.22)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&a_{1}=\frac{\psi}{2n}-\frac{(2L+n+8\sigma_{B}^{2}n)\Psi^{2}}{4n(1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2})}\alpha\geq\frac{\psi}{4n}>0,\\
&a_{2}=\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})^{2}}{32L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}-\frac{\Psi^{2}\alpha}{n}\geq\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})^{2}}{64L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}},\quad\mbox{and}\\
&a_{3}=1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2}-\frac{L^{2}\Psi^{2}\alpha}{n}\geq\frac{1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2}}{2}>0.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

At first, we establish a recursive upper bound for
\|{{\bf{x}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\|^{2}
. From the iteration (
2.2
), we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\|{\bf{x}}^{t+1}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t+1}\|^{2}=\|{\bf{A}}{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}-\alpha{\bf{B}}{\bf{H}}^{t}{\bf{v}}^{t}+\alpha{\bf{M}}{\bf{H}}^{t}{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\tau)\|{\bf{A}}{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+(1+1/\tau)\alpha^{2}\|({\bf{B}}-{\bf{M}}){\bf{H}}^{t}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\tau)\sigma_{A}^{2}\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+(1+1/\tau)\alpha^{2}\sigma_{B}^{2}\Psi^{2}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}

where the first inequality applies Young’s inequality with some
\tau>0

2.2
and Assumption

Next, we establish a recursive upper bound for

\displaystyle\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t+1}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t+1}\|^{2}=\|{\bf{C}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}+{\bf{D}}{\bf{g}}^{t+1}-{\bf{D}}{\bf{g}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{v}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{g}}^{t+1}+{\bf{M}}{\bf{g}}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\|{\bf{C}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}+(1+1/\eta)\|({\bf{D}}-{\bf{M}})({\bf{g}}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}^{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\sigma_{C}^{2}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}+(1+1/\eta)\sigma_{D}^{2}L^{2}\|{\bf{x}}^{t+1}-{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t+1}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t+1}\|^{2}\leq\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}-(1-(1+\eta)\sigma_{C}^{2})\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1-(1+\tau)\sigma_{A}^{2}}{4(1+1/\eta)L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}-\frac{(1-(1+\tau)\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-(1+\eta)\sigma_{C}^{2})}{4(1+1/\eta)L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2(1-(1+\tau)\sigma_{A}^{2})\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{(1-(1+\tau)\sigma_{A}^{2})\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}}{2}\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}.

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\psi}{2n}-\frac{(2L+n+8\sigma_{B}^{2}n)\Psi^{2}}{4n(1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2})}\alpha\right)\alpha\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})^{2}}{32L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}-\frac{\Psi^{2}\alpha}{n}\right)\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}.

Hence, by the definitions of

,

, and

P({\bf{x}}^{t+1},{\bf{v}}^{t+1})-P({\bf{x}}^{t},{\bf{v}}^{t})\leq-a_{1}\alpha\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}-a_{2}\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}-a_{3}\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2},

where by direct calculation we have

\displaystyle a_{1}\geq\frac{\psi}{4n},\quad a_{2}\geq\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})^{2}}{64L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad a_{3}\geq\frac{1-{\sigma}^{2}}{2}.

Summing (
2.26
) over
t=0,\ldots,T
and dividing both sides by
\gamma\min\{\alpha,1\}
, we get the desired convergence rate (
2.21
), where
\gamma=\min\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
.
∎

Given any
\epsilon>0
, by Theorem
2.5
, UDNA will take at most
P({\bf{x}}^{0},{\bf{v}}^{0})(\epsilon\gamma\min\{\alpha,1\})^{-1}
iterations
to generate an
\epsilon
-stationary point
{\bf{x}}^{t}
satisfying (
2.10
), where
\gamma
is the constant given in (
2.21
).

Corollary 2.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.5
and the choice of stepsize
\alpha
given by (
2.20
), it holds for the sequence
\{{\bf{x}}^{t}\}
generated by Algorithm
1
(UDNA) that
\{{\bf{x}}^{t}\}
has at least one accumulation point and any such point is a stationary point of the problem (
1.1
).

Proof.

Since the sequences
\{\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|^{2}\}
,
\{\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|^{2}\}
, and
\{\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|^{2}\}
are summable, we obtain

(2.27)

\|{\bf{v}}^{t}\|,\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|,\|{{\bf{v}}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{{\bf{v}}}^{t}\|\rightarrow 0\Rightarrow\|\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})\|\rightarrow 0,F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})\rightarrow P^{\infty},

for some
P^{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
. We now show the sequence
{\bf{x}}^{t}
is bounded.
By Theorem
2.5
and the stepsize choice (
2.20
), the potential function
P
is nonincreasing and bounded above by
P({\bf{x}}^{0},{\bf{v}}^{0})<\infty
. Hence,
F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})\leq P({\bf{x}}^{0},{\bf{v}}^{0})
indicates that
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}
is bounded due to the coercivity of
F
; that is, there exists some
B_{1}>0
such that
\|\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\|\leq B_{1}
for any
t
. Since
\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|\rightarrow 0
,
{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}
is also bounded:
\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|\leq B_{2}
for some
B_{2}>0
and all
t
. Consequently,

\|{\bf{x}}^{t}\|\leq\|{\bf{x}}^{t}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t}\|+\sqrt{n}\|\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t}\|\leq\sqrt{n}B_{1}+B_{2},~\forall~t\geq 0.

The boundedness of
{\bf{x}}^{t}
guarantees that there exist a convergent subsequence and a limit
point, denoted by
\{{\bf{x}}^{t_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow{\bf{x}}^{\infty}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. From (
2.27
), we have
\|\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t_{k}})\|+\|{\bf{x}}^{t_{k}}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{t_{k}}\|\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, which implies
\|\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty})\|+\|{\bf{x}}^{\infty}-{\bf{M}}{\bf{x}}^{\infty}\|=0
.
∎

While
Theorem
2.5
certifies stationarity of every limit point of
\{{\bf{x}}^{t}\}
generated by UDNA, the convergence of the whole sequences is not guaranteed. The next subsection addresses this issue given that the aggregated objective function
F
has the KŁ property.

2.3.
Convergence under KŁ property

We now strengthen the
subsequence convergence result in Theorem
2.5
, proving the global convergence of the sequence
\{{\bf{x}}^{t}\}
by postulating that
F
has the KŁ property. Notably, the convergence rate naturally emerges as a direct consequence of this analysis.

Definition 2.7
.

(KŁ property
\&
KŁ function) A proper closed function
h
is said to have the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KŁ) property at
{\bf{z}}\in\operatorname{dom}h
if there exist a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}
of
{\bf{z}}
,
v\in(0,\infty]
and a continuous concave function
\phi:[0,v)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\phi(0)=0
such that

1.

\phi
is continuously differentiable on
(0,v)
with
\phi^{\prime}>0
.

2.

For all
\tilde{{\bf{z}}}\in\mathcal{N}
with
h({\bf{z}})<h(\tilde{{\bf{z}}})<h({\bf{z}})+v
, it holds that

\phi^{\prime}(h(\tilde{{\bf{z}}})-h({\bf{z}}))\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial h(\tilde{{\bf{z}}}))\geq 1.

A proper closed function
h
satisfying the KŁ property at all points in
\operatorname{dom}h
is called a KŁ function.

Of particular interest in our analysis is the notion of the KL exponent, defined as follows.

Definition 2.8
.

(KŁ exponent) For a proper closed function
h
satisfying the KŁ property at
{\bf{z}}\in\operatorname{dom}h
, if the desingularizing function
\phi
can be chosen as
\phi(s)=\kappa s^{1-\theta}
for some
\kappa>0
and
\theta\in[0,1)
, i.e., there exists
\epsilon>0
and
v\in(0,\infty]
such that

\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial h(\tilde{{\bf{z}}}))\geq\kappa|h(\tilde{{\bf{z}}})-h({\bf{z}})|^{\theta}

whenever
\|\tilde{{\bf{z}}}-{\bf{z}}\|\leq\epsilon
and
0<|h(\tilde{{\bf{z}}})-h({\bf{z}})|<v
, then
h
is said to have the KŁ property at
{\bf{z}}
with exponent
\theta
. If
h
is a KŁ function and has the same exponent
\theta
in
\operatorname{dom}h
, then we say that
h
is
a KŁ function with exponent
\theta
.

Throughout this subsection, we introduce the following notations to facilitate our theoretical analysis.

(2.28)

\displaystyle{\bf{X}}=[{\bf{x}}_{1}^{\sf T};{\bf{x}}_{2}^{\sf T};\ldots;{\bf{x}}_{n}^{\sf T}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},~{\bf{V}}=[{\bf{v}}_{1}^{\sf T};{\bf{v}}_{2}^{\sf T};\ldots;{\bf{v}}_{n}^{\sf T}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},~\tilde{{\bf{M}}}=\frac{1}{n}{\bf{1}}_{n}^{\sf T}{\bf{1}}_{n}.

Then,
F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})=F((1/n){\bf{1}}_{n}^{\sf T}{\bf{X}}^{t}):=G({\bf{X}}^{t})
.
The following lemma shows that the KŁ property of
F
naturally transfers to
G
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Under the same assumptions and parameter choices of Theorem
2.5
, let
{\bf{1}}_{n}\otimes(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty})^{\sf T}={\bf{X}}^{\infty}
be an accumulation point of
{\bf{X}}^{t}
where
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}
is some critical point of
F
. If
F
has the KŁ property at
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}
with exponent
\theta_{F}\in[0,1)
and parameters
\{\kappa_{F}>0,v_{F}>0\}
, then
G
inherits the KŁ property at
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
with exponent
\theta_{G}=\theta_{F}
and parameters
\{\kappa_{G}=\frac{\kappa_{F}}{\sqrt{n}},v_{G}=v_{F}\}
.

Proof.

By the KŁ property of
F
, there exists a neighborhood
V=\{{\bf{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}:\|{\bf{z}}-\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}\|\leq\epsilon_{F}\}
of
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}
, a constant
\kappa_{F}>0
, and an exponent
\theta_{F}\in[0,1)
such that for all
{\bf{z}}\in V\cap\{{\bf{z}}:0<|F({\bf{z}})-F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty})|<v\}
, the following inequality holds,

\displaystyle\|\nabla F({\bf{z}})\|\geq\kappa_{F}|F({\bf{z}})-F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty})|^{\theta_{F}}.

We define a neighborhood
U
of
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
:

\displaystyle U=\{{\bf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}:\|{\bf{X}}-{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}\|\leq\epsilon_{G}\}.

We can choose
\epsilon_{G}
to be sufficiently small (specifically,
\epsilon_{G}=\sqrt{n}\epsilon_{F}
) such that for any
{\bf{X}}\in U
, the corresponding average
\bar{{\bf{x}}}=\frac{1}{n}{\bf{1}}^{\sf T}{\bf{X}}
lies within the neighborhood
V
of
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}
. This is guaranteed because
\|\bar{{\bf{x}}}-\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|{\bf{X}}-{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}\|
. Note
\|\nabla G({\bf{X}})\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}})\|
. Therefore, for any
{\bf{X}}\in U
where
G({\bf{X}})\neq G({{\bf{X}}}^{\infty})
, we can chain these relations and obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\|\nabla G({\bf{X}})\|\geq\kappa_{F}|G({\bf{X}})-G({{\bf{X}}}^{\infty})|^{\theta_{F}}.

Rearranging, we obtain the final KŁ inequality for
G
:

\displaystyle\|\nabla G({\bf{X}})\|\geq\frac{\kappa_{F}}{\sqrt{n}}|G({\bf{X}})-G({{\bf{X}}}^{\infty})|^{\theta_{F}}.

This indicates
G
satisfies the KŁ property at
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
with exponent
\theta_{G}=\theta_{F}
and parameter
\kappa_{G}=\frac{\kappa_{F}}{\sqrt{n}}
.
∎

Define the following notations:

(2.29)

\displaystyle P^{t}=P({\bf{x}}^{t},{\bf{v}}^{t}),\quad\Delta P^{t}=P^{t}-P^{\infty},

(2.30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{t}=\sqrt{\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\}}\left(\|{\bf{V}}^{t}\|^{2}+\|{{\bf{V}}}^{t}-\tilde{{\bf{M}}}{{\bf{V}}}^{t}\|^{2}+\|{\bf{X}}^{t}-\tilde{{\bf{M}}}{\bf{X}}^{t}\|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
\gamma
is the constant from Theorem
2.5
.
Then, it can be readily verified from (
2.26
) that

(2.31)

(\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2}\leq\Delta P^{t}-\Delta P^{t+1}.

To prove the whole sequence convergence of
\{{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\}
generated by UDNA, we will show that the distance sequence
\{\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\|\}
is summable, i.e.,
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\|\leq\infty
, which means that
\{{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\}
is a Cauchy sequence.
The following lemma reveals that
\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\|
can be controlled by
\mathcal{T}^{t}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Under the same assumptions and parameter choices of Theorem
2.5
, it holds that

(2.32)

\displaystyle\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\|\leq\sqrt{c_{1}}\mathcal{T}^{t},

where
c_{1}=\frac{2\max\{\alpha^{2}\Psi,4\}}{\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\}}
.

Proof.

With newly given notations (
2.28
), it is convenient to rewrite the recursion of
{\bf{X}}^{t}
as the following formular,

{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}=\tilde{{\bf{A}}}{{\bf{X}}}^{t}-\alpha\tilde{{\bf{B}}}{\bf{D}}^{t},

where
{\bf{D}}^{t}=[({\bf{d}}_{1}^{t})^{\sf T};({\bf{d}}_{2}^{t})^{\sf T};\dots;({\bf{d}}_{n}^{t})^{\sf T}]
with
{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Note
{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{X}}}^{t}=(\tilde{{\bf{A}}}-{\bf{I}}_{n})({\bf{X}}^{t}-\tilde{{\bf{M}}}{\bf{X}}^{t})-\alpha\tilde{{\bf{B}}}{\bf{D}}^{t}
and
\rho(\tilde{{\bf{A}}}-{\bf{I}}_{n})<2
.
Then it follows from Lemma
A.2
and Assumption
4
that

\displaystyle\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\|\leq\sqrt{8\|{\bf{X}}^{t}-\tilde{{\bf{M}}}{\bf{X}}^{t}\|^{2}+2\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2}\|{\bf{V}}^{t}\|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{c_{1}}\mathcal{T}^{t},

where
c_{1}=\frac{2\max\{\alpha^{2}\Psi^{2},4\}}{\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\}}
.
∎

Therefore, by Lemma
2.10
, it suffices to show that the sequence
\{{\mathcal{T}^{t}}\}
is summable. To this end, we first establish a key relationship between
P^{t+1}
and
G({\bf{X}}^{t})
, and derive an upper bound for
\|\nabla G({\bf{X}}^{t})\|
in terms of
\mathcal{T}^{t}
, as summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.11
.

Under the same assumptions and parameter choices of Theorem
2.5
, two inequalities holds as follows,

(2.33)

\displaystyle\Delta P^{t+1}\leq G({\bf{X}}^{t})+c_{2}(\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2}-P^{\infty}

and

(2.34)

\displaystyle\|\nabla G({\bf{X}}^{t})\|^{2}\leq(c_{3}\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2},

where

c_{2}=\max\left\{1,\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})}{4(1+\sigma_{C}^{2})L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}\right\}/(\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\})
and
c_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{3\max\{1,L^{2}\}}{\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\}n^{2}}}
.

Proof.

Leveraging the descent of
P^{t}
and the definition of
\mathcal{T}^{t}
(
2.30
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta P^{t+1}\leq P^{t}-P^{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F((1/n){\bf{1}}_{n}^{\sf T}{\bf{X}}^{t})+\|{\bf{X}}^{t}-\tilde{{\bf{M}}}{\bf{X}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})}{4(1+\sigma_{C}^{2})L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}\|{\bf{V}}^{t}-\tilde{{\bf{M}}}{\bf{V}}^{t}\|^{2}-P^{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle G({\bf{X}}^{t})+c_{2}(\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2}-P^{\infty},

where
c_{2}=\max\left\{1,\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})}{4(1+\sigma_{C}^{2})L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}\right\}/(\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\})
. So the inequality (
2.33
) is proved.
Note the relationship that
\nabla G({\bf{X}}^{t})=(1/n){\bf{1}}_{n}\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})
and the decomposition that
{\bf{1}}\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})^{\sf T}={\bf{1}}\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})^{\sf T}-{\bf{1}}\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})^{\sf T}+{\bf{1}}\overline{\nabla}f({{\bf{x}}}^{t})^{\sf T}-{\bf{V}}^{t}+{\bf{V}}^{t}
. Then, by Lemma
A.2
and Assumption
4
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\nabla G({\bf{X}}^{t})\|^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\|\nabla F(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3L^{2}}{n^{2}}\|{\bf{X}}^{t}-\tilde{{\bf{M}}}{\bf{X}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{n^{2}}\|{\bf{V}}^{t}-\tilde{{\bf{M}}}{\bf{V}}^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{3}{n^{2}}\|{\bf{V}}^{t}\|^{2}\leq(c_{3}\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2},

where
c_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{3\max\{1,L^{2}\}}{\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\}n^{2}}}
. So the inequality (
2.34
) is proved.
∎

Assuming that
F
satisfies the KŁ property, Lemma
2.9
guarantees that
G
inherits this property. From inequality (
2.34
), we first bound
G({\bf{X}}^{t})-P^{\infty}
by
\mathcal{T}^{t}
. This result, together with the relation given in (
2.33
), leads directly to an upper bound for
\Delta P^{t+1}
in terms
\mathcal{T}^{t}
, as we formally present in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.12
.

Under the same assumptions and parameter choices of Theorem
2.5
, let
{\bf{X}}^{\infty}={\bf{1}}_{n}\otimes(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty})^{\sf T}
be an accumulation point of
{\bf{X}}^{t}
where
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}
is some critical point of
F
. Assume
F
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}
with exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
and parameters
\{\sqrt{n}\kappa>0,v=1\}
. Then, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
of
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
and some
t_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that for all
t\in\mathcal{S}:=\{t\geq t_{1}:{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}\}\neq\emptyset
,

c_{3}\mathcal{T}^{t}<1,\quad|G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})-P^{\infty}|<1,

and for
\theta\in(0,1)
and
G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})-P^{\infty}\neq 0
,

(2.35)

\Delta P^{t+1}\leq\kappa^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}(c_{3}\mathcal{T}^{t})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+c_{2}(\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2},

where
c_{2}=\max\left\{1,\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})}{4(1+\sigma_{C}^{2})L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}}\right\}/(\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\})
and
c_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{3\max\{1,L^{2}\}}{\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\}n^{2}}}
.

Proof.

Since
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
is an accumulation point of
\{{\bf{X}}^{t}\}
, we have
P^{\infty}=G({\bf{X}}^{\infty})
. By the KŁ assumption on
F
and Lemma
2.9
, we have
G
possesses KŁ property at
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
with exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
and parameters
\{\kappa>0,v=1\}
. Hence, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
of
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
such that for all
{\bf{X}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}\cap\{{\bf{X}}:0<|G({\bf{X}})-P^{\infty}|<1\}
,

(2.36)

|G({\bf{X}})-P^{\infty}|^{\theta}\leq\frac{\|\nabla G({\bf{X}})\|}{\kappa}.

From Theorem
2.5
,
\mathcal{T}^{t}\rightarrow 0,\quad G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})\rightarrow P^{\infty}
, so there exists some
t_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that for all
t\geq t_{1}

c_{3}\mathcal{T}^{t}<1,\quad|G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})-P^{\infty}|<1,

where
c_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{3\max\{1,L^{2}\}}{\gamma\min\{1,\alpha\}n^{2}}}
. Define
\mathcal{S}=\{t\geq t_{1}:{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}\}
which is nonempty since
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
is an accumulation point of
\{{\bf{X}}^{t}\}
. For
\theta\in(0,1)
and any
t\in\mathcal{S}
with
G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})-P^{\infty}\neq 0
, the inequality (
2.36
) implies

(2.37)

|G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})-P^{\infty}|\leq\left(\frac{\|\nabla G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})\|}{\kappa}\right)^{1/\theta}.

Substituting (
2.34
) into (
2.37
) yields

(2.38)

|G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})-P^{\infty}|\leq\kappa^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}(c_{3}\mathcal{T}^{t})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}.

Combining (
2.38
) with (
2.33
) gives the desired bound (
2.35
).
∎

In the setting of Lemma
2.12
, we may assume, without loss of generality, that
|\mathcal{S}|=\infty
.
Using (
2.31
) and Lemma
A.3
with
a_{1}=\Delta P^{t}
and
a_{2}=\Delta P^{t+1}
gives

(2.39)

(\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2}\leq\Delta P^{t}-\Delta P^{t+1}\leq\frac{1}{1-\theta}(\Delta P^{t})^{\theta}[\underbrace{(\Delta P^{t})^{1-\theta}-(\Delta P^{t+1})^{1-\theta}}_{\Delta P^{t}_{\theta}}].

Based on the above preliminaries, we now establish the summability of
\{\mathcal{T}^{t}\}
.

Theorem 2.13
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
,
2
,
3
, and
4
hold. Let
\{{\bf{X}}^{t}\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
1
(UDNA) using the stepsize

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\min\left\{\frac{(1-\sigma_{A}^{2})(1-\sigma_{C}^{2})^{2}n}{64L^{2}\sigma_{D}^{2}\Psi^{2}},\frac{(1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2})\psi}{(2L+n+8\sigma_{B}^{2}n)\Psi^{2}},\frac{(1-{\sigma}_{A}^{2})n}{2L^{2}\Psi^{2}}\right\}.

Let
{\bf{X}}^{\infty}={\bf{1}}_{n}\otimes(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty})^{\sf T}
be an accumulation point of
{\bf{X}}^{t}
, where
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}
is some critical point of
F
. If
F
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty}
with exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
and parameters
\{\sqrt{n}\kappa>0,v=1\}
, then
\{\mathcal{T}^{t}\}
is summable and the whole sequence
\{{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\}
is convergent.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.10
,
\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\|\leq\sqrt{c_{1}}\mathcal{T}^{t}
. Thus, summability of

\{\mathcal{T}^{t}\}
implies
\{{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\}
is a Cauchy sequence and hence convergent. We prove summability by considering three cases based on the KŁ exponent
\theta
. At first, we define
\mathcal{S}^{o}=\mathcal{S}\cap\{t:G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})-P^{\infty}\neq 0\}
.

Case I
:
\theta\in(0,1/2]
. By Lemma
2.12
,
c_{3}\mathcal{T}^{t}<1
for
t\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
. Since
1/\theta\geq 2
, inequality (
2.35
) implies

(2.40)

\Delta P^{t+1}\leq c_{4}(\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2},

where
c_{4}=\kappa^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}c_{3}^{2}+c_{2}
. Computing
\eqref{Tt}+\omega\times\eqref{DeltaPt2}
, we obtain

(1+\omega)\Delta P^{t+1}\leq\Delta P^{t}-(1-\omega c_{4})(\mathcal{T}^{t})^{2}.

Choosing
\omega\in(0,1/c_{4})
ensures that

(2.41)

\Delta P^{t+1}\leq\frac{1}{1+\omega}\Delta P^{t}\leq\Delta P^{0}\left(\frac{1}{1+\omega}\right)^{t+1}.

Applying (
2.41
) to (
2.31
) yields

(2.42)

\mathcal{T}^{t}\leq\sqrt{\Delta P^{0}}(\tau_{1})^{t},

where
\tau_{1}=1/\sqrt{1+\omega}
.

Case II
:
\theta\in(1/2,1)
. From (
2.35
) and
c_{3}\mathcal{T}^{t}<1
for
t\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
, we have

(2.43)

\Delta P^{t+1}\leq c_{5}(\mathcal{T}^{t})^{1/\theta},

where
c_{5}=(c_{3}/\kappa)^{1/\theta}+c_{2}
. Using (
2.39
), (
2.43
), and Young’s inequality with parameter
\eta=1
, we obtain

(2.44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{t+1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-\theta}(\Delta P^{t+1})^{\theta}\Delta P^{t+1}_{\theta}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{c_{5}^{\theta}}{1-\theta}\mathcal{T}^{t}\Delta P^{t+1}_{\theta}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}^{t}+\frac{c_{5}^{\theta}}{2-2\theta}\Delta P^{t+1}_{\theta}.

Case III
:
\theta=0
. For any
{t}\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
, the KŁ property of
G
at
{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}
impies

(2.45)

\kappa(G({{\bf{X}}}^{t})-P^{\infty})^{0}\leq\|\nabla G({{\bf{X}}}^{t^{\prime}})\|.

Combining with
\|\nabla G({{\bf{X}}}^{t^{\prime}})\|^{2}\leq c_{3}^{2}(\mathcal{T}^{t^{\prime}})^{2}
from (
2.34
) further gives

(2.46)

\mathcal{T}^{t}\geq\frac{\kappa}{c_{3}},~\forall~{t}\in\mathcal{S}^{o}.

By (
2.31
) and the fact that
\Delta P^{t}\rightarrow 0
monotonically, we must have
\mathcal{T}^{t}\rightarrow 0
. This contradicts the above inequality (
2.46
) unless
\mathcal{S}^{o}
is finite. Thus, there exists some
t_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
such that

(2.47)

\mathcal{T}^{t}=0,~\forall~t\geq t_{2},

thereby
{\mathcal{T}^{t}}
is summable.

We proceed with analysis following
Case I
and
Case II
. Let
t_{4}>t_{3}
such that
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}:=\{t_{3},t_{3}+1,\ldots,t_{4}-1\}\subset\mathcal{S}^{o}
. Combining (
2.42
) with (
2.44
) and summing over
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}
, we obtain

(2.48)

\sum_{t=t_{3}}^{t_{4}-1}\mathcal{T}^{t}\leq\max\left\{\frac{\sqrt{\Delta P^{0}}\tau_{1}^{t_{3}}}{1-\tau_{1}},2\mathcal{T}^{t_{3}}+\frac{c_{5}^{\theta}}{1-\theta}(\Delta P^{t_{3}})^{1-\theta}\right\},

where the second term in R.H.S of the above inequality is obtained by

\displaystyle 2\sum_{t=t_{3}}^{t_{4}-1}\mathcal{T}^{t+1}\leq\sum_{t=t_{3}}^{t_{4}-1}\mathcal{T}^{t}+\frac{c_{5}^{\theta}}{1-\theta}\sum_{t=t_{3}}^{t_{4}-1}((\Delta P^{t})^{1-\theta}-(\Delta P^{t+1})^{1-\theta})

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{t=t_{3}}^{t_{4}-1}\mathcal{T}^{t+1}\leq\mathcal{T}^{t_{3}}-\mathcal{T}^{t_{4}}+\frac{c_{5}^{\theta}}{1-\theta}(\Delta P^{t_{3}})^{1-\theta}.

Let
r
be arbitrarily small such that

\mathcal{B}_{r}({{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}):=\{{{\bf{X}}}:\|{{\bf{X}}}-{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}\|\leq r\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\infty}.

By Theorem
2.5
and the fact that
{\bf{1}}_{n}\otimes(\bar{{\bf{x}}}^{\infty})^{\sf T}={\bf{X}}^{\infty}
is an accumulation point of
{\bf{X}}^{t}
, there exists
t_{3}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{o}
such that

\displaystyle\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t_{3}^{\prime}}-{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}\|<\frac{r}{2},\quad\sqrt{c_{1}}\max\left\{\frac{\sqrt{\Delta P^{0}}\tau_{1}^{t_{3}^{\prime}}}{1-\tau_{1}},2\mathcal{T}^{t_{3}^{\prime}}+\frac{c_{5}^{\theta}}{1-\theta}(\Delta P^{t_{3}^{\prime}})^{1-\theta}\right\}<\frac{r}{2}.

Setting
t_{3}=t_{3}^{\prime}
in (
2.48
) gives
{{\bf{X}}}^{t_{3}}\in\mathcal{B}_{r}({{\bf{X}}}^{\infty})
and

(2.49)

\sum_{t=t_{3}}^{t_{4}-1}\mathcal{T}^{t}\leq\frac{r}{2\sqrt{c_{1}}}.

Next, we prove by contradiction that
{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\in\mathcal{B}_{r}({{\bf{X}}}^{\infty})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
for all
t\geq t_{3}
. Suppose that there exists
t_{4}^{\prime}>t_{3}
(the smallest such index) such that
\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t_{4}^{\prime}}-{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}\|\geq r
. Then for
t\in\mathcal{S}^{o}\cap[t_{3},t_{4}^{\prime})
, setting
t_{4}=t_{4}^{\prime}
yields

\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t_{4}}-{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}\|\leq\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t_{3}}-{{\bf{X}}}^{\infty}\|+\sum_{t=t_{3}}^{t_{4}-1}\|{{\bf{X}}}^{t+1}-{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\|<\frac{r}{2}+\sqrt{c_{1}}\sum_{t=t_{3}}^{t_{4}-1}\mathcal{T}^{t}<r,

where the second and last inequalities follow respectively from (
2.32
) and (
2.49
). This contradicts the assumption, so
{{\bf{X}}}^{t}\in\mathcal{B}_{r}({{\bf{X}}}^{\infty})
for all
t\geq t_{3}
. Passing the limit
t_{4}\rightarrow\infty
to the both sides of (
2.49
) yields the summability of
\{\mathcal{T}^{t}\}
, as is
\{\|{\bf{X}}^{t+1}-{\bf{X}}^{t}\|\}
. Hence,
\{{\bf{X}}^{t}\}
is a Cauchy sequence and therefore is convergent.
∎

.

Corollary 2.14

2.4.
Methods of constructing Hessian inverse approximations

We begin with reviewing how previous decentralized quasi-Newton methods ensured bounded approximate matrices for the Hessian or its inverse. It has been observed that the Hessian inverse approximations generated by (
2.7
) in DQN
[
47
]
may exhibit negative eigenvalues, despite being theoretically assumed to maintain positive eigenvalues with lower boundedness. To establish convergence guarantees for DGM-BB-C
[
18
]
and DSG
[
25
]
in nonconvex optimization settings, it becomes necessary to project the BB-type matrix onto the space of positive definite matrices. While the regularization and damping techniques proposed in
[
62
]
for ensuring eigenvalue boundedness were initially employed for convex optimization, we emphasize that these methods are fundamentally independent of convexity assumptions and therefore directly applicable to nonconvex scenarios.
Below, we propose several novel approaches for constructing bounded approximate matrices of Hessian inverse, specifically designed for nonconvex optimization frameworks.

2.4.1.
Memoryless quasi-Newton updating schemes

Empirical evidence from comparisons between memoryless quasi-Newton methods and standard BFGS
[
5
]
indicates that the accuracy of the Hessian approximation is not essential for their efficiency and robustness. Inspired by this finding and the need for low computational cost, we study two specific memoryless quasi-Newton updating schemes in decentralized optimization.

Memoryless SR1

The memoryless SR1 method with direct approximation to the Hessian is obtained by considering
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{I}}_{p}
in the standard SR1 update, i.e.,

(2.50)

\hat{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t+1}={\bf{I}}_{p}+\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}},

where
{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}
and
\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
.
The main drawback of memoryless SR1 update (
2.50
) is that the SR1 approximation may not be positive definite along the iterations. Thus, a necessary safeguarding on (
2.50
) is proposed:

\displaystyle{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}{\bf{I}}_{p}+\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}},&\text{if }[\lambda_{\min}(\hat{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t+1}),\lambda_{\max}(\hat{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t+1})]\subset[\bar{l},\bar{u}]\\
&\quad\text{and }({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\neq 0;\\
{\bf{I}}_{p},&\text{otherwise, }\end{array}\right.

where
0<\bar{l}\ll\bar{u}<+\infty
are two constants.
As a rank-one perturbation on the identity matrix , the eigenvalue of
\hat{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t+1}
other than one is easily computed, that is
1+\frac{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}
. Thus, we easily check if this eigenvalue lies in the interval and compute the bounds on the eigenvalues of
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}
.
From (
2.4.1
), after some simple algebraic manipulations, the memoryless SR1 search direction
{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t+1}=-{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}
, when
[\lambda_{\min}(\hat{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t+1}),\lambda_{\max}(\hat{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t+1})]\subset[\bar{l},\bar{u}]
and
({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\neq 0
, can be written as

(2.54)

{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t+1}=-{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}-\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}).

The main advantage of the memoryless SR1 update (
2.54
) is that for its implementation in computer programs, only three vector-to-vector products,
\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
,
({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}
and
({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}-\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}
, should be computed. This is very advantageous for solving large-scale problems.

Memoryless BFGS

We now let
\Omega_{i}^{t}=\left\{{\bf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}>0\right\}

and define the function
H_{i}^{t}:\Omega_{i}^{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
as

(2.55)

\displaystyle H_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}})=\tau_{i}^{t}\left({\bf{I}}_{p}-\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}})^{\sf T}+{\bf{y}}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}}\right)+\left(1+\frac{\tau_{i}^{t}\|{\bf{y}}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}}\right)\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}},

where
{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{x}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}
. We propose a memoryless BFGS method to generate
{\bf{H}}^{t}
in (
2.2
),

(2.56)

\displaystyle{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}

\displaystyle=H_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})=\tau_{i}^{t}\left({\bf{I}}_{p}-\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}+{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}\right)+\left(1+\frac{\tau_{i}^{t}\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}\right)\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{\bf{I}}_{p}-\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}+{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}+2\frac{{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}.

where
\tau_{i}^{t}=\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}
,

(2.57)

{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t},&\text{ if }[\lambda_{\min}(H_{i}^{t}(\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})),\lambda_{\max}(H_{i}^{t}(\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}))]\subset[l,u]\text{ and }({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\neq 0;\\
\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t},&\text{ otherwise, }\end{array}\right.

\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
,
\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{g}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t}+h_{i}^{t}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}
,
h_{i}^{t}=\varrho+\max\left\{-\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}({\bf{g}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t})}{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}},0\right\}
, and
\varrho>0
is a small constant.

The adaptive selection mechanism in (
2.57
) is to ensure the quasi-Newton matrix (
2.56
)
has positive bounded eigenvalues.
Since
{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
is generated by the gradient tracking technique in (
2.3
),
it captures some information of the average gradients on different nodes.
So, we prefer using
{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
to generate the quasi-Newton matrix by
H_{i}^{t}(\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})

whenever possible, where
\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
.
However,
H_{i}^{t}(\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})
is not necessarily positive definite and bounded.
So, in (
2.57
) we simply check if the smallest and largest eigenvalues of the quasi-Newton
matrix
H_{i}^{t}(\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})
belong to an interval
[l,u]
, where

0<l\ll u
are two parameters. If not, we would use the alternative
{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t}
with correction
h_{i}^{t}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}
to
update the quasi-Newton matrix by
H_{i}^{t}(\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})
, where

\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{g}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t}+h_{i}^{t}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}
.
By the BFGS updating formula (
2.56
),
H_{i}^{t}(\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})
is guaranteed to be positive definite and uniformly bounded.
We now explain the computation cost of the smallest and largest eigenvalues of

{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}
is in fact negligible.
Note that
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}=H_{i}^{t}(\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})
is obtained
from the scalar matrix
\tau_{i}^{t}{\bf{I}}_{p}
by a rank-two BFGS update.
Hence, if
\tau_{i}^{t}=\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}>0
,

{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}
has
p-2
eigenvalues of
\tau_{i}^{t}
and
two eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{i}^{t+1}\leq\Lambda_{i}^{t+1}
satisfying

(2.58)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\lambda_{i}^{t+1}\Lambda_{i}^{t+1}&=\frac{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}},\\
\lambda_{i}^{t+1}+\Lambda_{i}^{t+1}&=\frac{2\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}.\end{array}\right.

The system (
2.58
) defines a quadratic equation,
which has two roots

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\lambda_{i}^{t+1}=\lambda_{\min}(H_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}))=\frac{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{(({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{2}}{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\|{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}}\right),\\
\\
\Lambda_{i}^{t+1}=\lambda_{\max}(H_{i}^{t}({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}))=\frac{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}\left(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{(({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t})^{2}}{\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\|{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}}\right).\end{array}\right.

{\bf{H}}^{t}
given as (
2.56
) is easily shown to be bounded with
\tau_{i}^{t}=\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}
. From (
2.57
), we acquire
l{\bf{I}}_{p}\preceq{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}\preceq u{\bf{I}}_{p}
when
{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}
takes
\check{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}
. When
{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}
takes
\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}
, we have by (
2.4.1
) that

\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}{2\|\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{\bf{I}}_{p}\preceq{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}\preceq\frac{2\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}}{\bf{I}}_{p}.

According to
\|\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|=\|{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t+1}-{\bf{g}}_{i}^{t}+h_{i}^{t}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|\leq(2L+\varrho)\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|
yielded by the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f_{i}
, we have
({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\geq\frac{1}{L}\|\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}+\varrho\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\geq\hat{\varrho}\|\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
with
\hat{\varrho}=\frac{1}{4L^{2}+4L\varrho+\varrho^{2}}+\frac{1}{L}
, which further implies the left hand side of the above inequality can be bounded below by
\frac{\hat{\varrho}}{2}
. By
({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}\hat{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}\geq\varrho\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
, the right hand side of the above inequality can be bounded above by
\frac{2}{\varrho}
. Hence, we obtain

\min\left\{l,\frac{\hat{\varrho}}{2}\right\}{\bf{I}}\preceq{\bf{H}}^{t+1}\preceq\max\left\{u,\frac{2}{\varrho}\right\}{\bf{I}}.

The corresponding search direction of the memoryless BFGS method is
{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t+1}=-{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}
, where
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t+1}
is given by (
2.56
), i.e.,

(2.62)

{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t+1}=-\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}+\frac{(({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}){\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}+(({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}){\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}}-2\frac{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t+1}}{({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}.

Observe that the numerical computation of
{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t+1}
from (
2.62
) involves only five scalar
products:
\|{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
,
\|{\bf{y}}_{i}^{t}\|^{2}
,
({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{y}}}_{i}^{t}
,
({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{v}}}_{i}^{t+1}
, and
({\bf{y}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}{{\bf{v}}}_{i}^{t+1}
. Therefore, it is very suitable for applying the memoryless BFGS method to solve large-scale problems.

2.4.2.
Corrected quasi-Newton updating schemes

Motivated by
[
11
]
, we introduce a useful correcting method for the variation of average-gradient approximation
\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}={\bf{v}}^{t+1}_{i}-{\bf{v}}^{t}_{i}
,

(2.63)

\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}=\eta_{i}^{t}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}+(1-\eta_{i}^{t}){\bf{s}}^{t}_{i},

where
\eta_{i}^{t}\in(0,1]
such that

(2.64)

\frac{({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}}{\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}}\geq\lambda

for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
. The positivity condition (
2.64
), which is crucial for ensuring positive definite Hessian inverse approximations in both centralized and decentralized settings, can be ensured by choosing a sufficiently small
\eta_{i}^{t}\in(0,1]
in (
2.63
).
In the following, we provide an adaptive approach for generating
\eta_{i}^{t}
,

(2.65)

\eta_{i}^{t}=\min\left\{\hat{\eta}_{i}^{t},\frac{\hat{L}\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|}{\|\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}\|}\right\}

where
\hat{L}
is some positive constant and
\hat{\eta}_{i}^{t}
is defined as

(2.66)

\hat{\eta}_{i}^{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{(1-\lambda)\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}}{\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}-({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}},&\text{if }({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}\leq\lambda\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2};\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the positivity of
({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}
in (
2.64
) can be guaranteed as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 2.15
.

For
\eta_{i}^{t}
defined in (
2.65
) and
\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}
defined in (
2.63
), we have
0<{\eta}^{t}\leq 1
and
({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}\geq\lambda\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}
.

Proof.

When
({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}\leq\lambda\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}
, it can be readily verified that
0<\hat{\eta}_{i}^{t}\leq 1
. Since
\eta_{i}^{t}=\min\{\hat{\eta}_{i}^{t},\frac{\hat{L}\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|}{\|\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}\|}\}
, we obtain
0<{\eta}^{t}\leq 1
. Multiplying left and right sides of (
2.63
) by
({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}
and using the expression of
\eta_{i}^{t}
from (
2.65
), we have

\displaystyle({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}=\eta_{i}^{t}(({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}-\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2})+\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\lambda\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2},&\text{ if }({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}\leq\lambda\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2};\\
(({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i},&\text{ otherwise, }\end{array}\right.

which implies that
({\bf{s}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t}_{i}\geq\lambda\|{\bf{s}}^{t}_{i}\|^{2}
.
∎

The Dai-Kou
[
12
]
and Hager-Zhang
[
19
]
conjugate gradient methods are closely related to quasi-Newton methods, as they effectively capture second-order information while maintaining computational efficiency. In fact, these methods can be formally recast as specific instances of quasi-Newton methods.
Extending this idea to decentralized optimization, we consider a direction
\tilde{{\bf{d}}}_{i}^{t}
combining the tracking variable
{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}
with iterate variation
{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t-1}={\bf{x}}_{i}^{t}-{\bf{x}}_{i}^{t-1}
:

(2.67)

\tilde{{\bf{d}}}_{i}^{t}=-{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}+\beta_{i}^{t,\tau}{\bf{s}}_{i}^{t-1},

where

(2.68)

\beta_{i}^{t,\tau}=\frac{({\bf{v}}^{t}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}{({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}-\tau\frac{\|\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}({\bf{s}}_{i}^{t-1})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}^{t}_{i}}{(({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i})^{2}},

and the parameter
\tau
is suggested to lie in the interval
[1,2]
according to
[
19
,
12
]
.

Remark 2.16
.

Consider the case with
n=1
, implying
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}=[1]
, and assume
\eta_{i}^{t}\equiv 1
. Then the parameter
\beta_{i}^{t,\tau}
in (
2.68
) is equivalent to Dai-Kou and Hager-Zhang conjugate parameters for
\tau=1
and
2
, respectively.

Notably, the direction (
2.67
) can be rewritten as

\tilde{{\bf{d}}}_{i}^{t}=-\tilde{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t},

where

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}={\bf{I}}-\frac{{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i}({\bf{z}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}},~{\bf{z}}^{t-1}_{i}=\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}-\tau p^{t-1}_{i}{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i},~p^{t-1}_{i}=\frac{\|\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}}{({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}.

By symmetrizing
\tilde{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}
, we acquire a corrected quasi-Newton update for
{\bf{H}}_{i}^{t}
:

(2.69)

{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}=\frac{\tilde{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}+(\tilde{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t})^{\sf T}}{2}={\bf{I}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i}({\bf{z}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}+{\bf{z}}^{t-1}_{i}({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}}{({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}},

termed the Dai-Kou type update for
\tau=1
and the Hager-Zhang type update for
\tau=2
. Sequentially, the search direction
{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t}
for this corrected quasi-Newton method is calculated by

{\bf{d}}_{i}^{t}=-{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}=-{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}+\frac{({\bf{z}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}}{2({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i}+\frac{({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{t}}{2({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}{\bf{z}}^{t-1}_{i}.

Now we establish uniform bounds on the eigenvalues of
{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}
for all
i
and
t
.

Lemma 2.17
.

For
{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}
defined by (
2.69
), we have

(2.70)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}({{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\tau(\hat{L}^{2}+1)}{\lambda^{2}},

(2.71)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}({{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

Firstly, it naturally holds
\lambda_{\max}({{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t})\leq\|{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}\|_{2}
. From Lemma
2.15
, we have

\displaystyle\|{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}\|_{2}=\frac{\tau\|{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}\|\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}}{\left(({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{\tau\|\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}}{\lambda({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}\leq\frac{2\tau(\eta_{i}^{t})^{2}\|\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}+2\tau(1-\eta_{i}^{t})^{2}\|{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}}{\lambda({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\tau\hat{L}^{2}\|{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}+2\tau(1-\eta_{i}^{t})^{2}\|{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}}{\lambda({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\check{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}\leq\frac{2\tau(\hat{L}^{2}+1)}{\lambda^{2}},

where the second inequality applies (
2.63
) and Lemma
A.2
, and the third inequality uses the expression of
\eta_{i}^{t}
from (
2.65
). So the upper bound (
2.70
) is established.

For any vectors
{\bf{u}},{\bf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, notice that

\left({\bf{u}}{\bf{v}}^{\sf T}+{\bf{v}}{\bf{u}}^{\sf T}\right)\left({\bf{u}}\underline{+}\frac{\|{\bf{u}}\|}{\|{\bf{v}}\|}{\bf{v}}\right)=\left({\bf{u}}^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}\underline{+}\|{\bf{u}}\|\|{\bf{v}}\|\right)\left({\bf{u}}\underline{+}\frac{\|{\bf{u}}\|}{\|{\bf{v}}\|}{\bf{v}}\right).

By this, it is not difficult to see that the minimal eigenvalue of
{{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t}
is

(2.72)

\lambda_{\min}({{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t})=\min\left\{1,1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}{{\bf{z}}}^{t-1}_{i}}{({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}}+\frac{\|{\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i}\|\|{{\bf{z}}}^{t-1}_{i}\|}{|({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}|}\right)\right\}.

We define

q_{i}^{t-1}=\frac{\|\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}\|{{\bf{s}}}^{t-1}_{i}\|^{2}}{(({\bf{s}}^{t-1}_{i})^{\sf T}\breve{{\bf{y}}}^{t-1}_{i})^{2}}.

By this definition of
q_{i}^{t-1}
, we can rewrite (
2.72
) as

(2.73)

\lambda_{\min}({{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t})=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\tau q_{i}^{t-1}-\sqrt{\tau^{2}(q_{i}^{t-1})^{2}-2\tau q_{i}^{t-1}+q_{i}^{t-1}}\right).

For the second term in the braces of (
2.73
), it is monotonically decreasing for
q_{i}^{t}\geq 1
and hence is always greater than its limit
\frac{1}{2}
as
q_{i}^{t}
tends to
+\infty
. Thus we always have
\lambda_{\min}({{\bf{H}}}_{i}^{t})\geq\frac{1}{2}
, which proves the lower bound (
2.71
).
∎

CONCLUSIONS

In this paper, we investigate the unification and generalization of various important algorithms for decentralized optimization problems where
n
nodes collaboratively minimize the sum of their local nonconvex objectives. We provide a unified algorithmic framework that subsumes a wide range of gradient tracking methods and quasi-Newton methods. Within a cohesive analytical structure, we first show subsequence convergence under mild assumptions with the proper parameter choices and then establish the whole sequence convergence along with a non-asymptotic rate by using the KŁ property of the aggregated objective function. We proceed to generalize this framework by introducing several noval quasi-Newton variants within the unified algorithmic framework. These methods enjoy a very economical computational cost. Our experimental results on the nonconvex decentralized binary classification problem indicate that our newly proposed quasi-Newton algorithms generally exhibit superior performance versus other comparison algorithms, e.g. GT, GUT, DSMT, MT, DQN, D-LM-BFGS, and DR-LM-DFP. Possible future work directions include extensions to stochastic optimization and nonsmooth optimization.

Analytical tools

Lemma A.1
.

(Young’s inequality) For any two vectors
{\bf{v}}_{1},{\bf{v}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

\displaystyle 2{\bf{v}}_{1}^{\sf T}{\bf{v}}_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\eta\|{\bf{v}}_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta}\|{\bf{v}}_{2}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\|{\bf{v}}_{1}+{\bf{v}}_{2}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\eta)\|{\bf{v}}_{1}\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\|{\bf{v}}_{2}\|^{2}.

.

(Jensen’s inequality) For any set of vectors
\{{\bf{v}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
,

\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf{v}}_{i}\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|{\bf{v}}_{i}\|^{2}.

.

[

For

.

[

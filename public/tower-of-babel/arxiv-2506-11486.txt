Introduction

Let
p^{n}
be an odd prime power and
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
be the finite field of
p^{n}
elements and
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\setminus\{0\}
be the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Many researchers have been interested in constructing vectorial Boolean functions over finite fields that possess good cryptographic properties. Among these properties, differential uniformity, introduced by Nyberg
[
17
]
is one of the most well-studied and widely recognized criteria due to its strong relevance to resistance against differential cryptanalysis. The differential uniformity is defined as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
F
be a function on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. We denote
\delta_{F}(a,b)
by the number of solutions of
F(x+a)-F(x)=b
, where
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. Then the
differential uniformity
of
F
is defined by :

\delta_{F}=\displaystyle\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*},b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\delta_{F}(a,b).

If
\delta_{F}\leq\delta
, then we say that
F
is differentially
\delta
-uniform.

If
F
is differentially
1
-uniform, then we say
F
is
perfect nonlinear (PN)
. If
F
is differentially
2
-uniform, then we say
F
is
almost perfect nonlinear (APN)
. For a survey of known functions with low differential uniformity, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
. In recent years, there has been significant progress in studying the differential spectra of various functions. For functions whose differential spectra are known, we refer to tables in the recent results on this topic
[
14
,
19
,
23
]
and the references therein.

The boomerang attack is a variant of differential cryptanalysis proposed by Wagner
[
21
]
. To analyze this type of attack, Cid et al.
[
6
]
introduced the boomerang connectivity table (BCT). The boomerang uniformity is defined as the maximum value among the nontrivial entries in the BCT. This notion was originally defined in
[
6
]
for permutations over binary finite fields. Later, Li et al.
[
10
]
extended the definition to functions that are not necessarily permutations, as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
F
be a function on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
. We denote
\beta_{F}(a,b)
by the number of common solutions
(x,y)
of the following system :

\begin{cases}F(x)-F(y)=b,\\
F(x+a)-F(y+a)=b.\end{cases}

Then the
boomerang uniformity
of
F
is defined by :

\beta_{F}=\displaystyle\max_{a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}}\beta_{F}(a,b).

For a survey of known functions with low boomerang uniformity, we refer the reader to
[
13
]
. For functions with known boomerang spectra, see Table 1 of
[
12
]
and references therein.

Most known results on differential or boomerang uniformities, particularly those concerning differential or boomerang spectra, have focused on power functions. Recently, however, several studies have investigated the differential or boomerang properties of functions of the form

F_{r,u}(x)=x^{r}(1+u\chi(x)),

where
\chi(x)=x^{\frac{p^{n}-1}{2}}
is the quadratic character in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.
The first result on functions of the above form was introduced by Ness and Helleseth
[
15
]
that
F_{3^{n}-2,u}
is an APN function over
\mathbb{F}_{3^{n}}
if
\chi(u-1)=\chi(u+1)=\chi(u)
. Later, Zeng et al
[
27
]
generalized this result by showing that
F_{p^{n}-2,u}
is an APN function over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
if
\chi(u-1)=\chi(u+1)=-\chi(5u\pm 3)
, where
p^{n}\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.

Very recently, two independent studies have been published on the differential properties of
F_{p^{n}-2,u}
in cases where it is not APN. In
[
23
]
, Xia et al studied the differential uniformity of
F_{3^{n}-2,u}
over
\mathbb{F}_{3^{n}}
, and investigated the differential spectrum of
F_{3^{n}-2,u}
when
\chi(u+1)=\chi(u-1)
. In
[
24

[
23
]
to the case where
p^{n}\equiv 3\pmod{4}

[
12

proved that

is a differentially
4
-uniform permutation over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
if
\chi(1+u)=\chi(1-u)
, where
p^{n}\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. Furthermore, they also showed that
F_{p^{n}-2,\pm 1}
is a locally-APN function with boomerang uniformity at most
1
, representing the first non-PN class whose boomerang uniformity can attain

or

is locally-PN with boomerang uniformity

Budaghyan and Pal

In addition, they proved that

-uniform permutation when

Preliminaries

If
F(x)=x^{d}
is a power function, then
b=F(x+a)-F(x)=(x+a)^{d}-x^{d}
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{b}{a^{d}}=\left(\displaystyle\frac{x}{a}+1\right)^{d}-\left(\displaystyle\frac{x}{a}\right)^{d}=(y+1)^{d}-y^{d},

where the last equality is from setting
y=\displaystyle\frac{x}{a}
. Thus, we have

\delta_{F}(a,b)=\delta_{F}\left(1,\displaystyle\frac{b}{a^{d}}\right),\text{ so }\delta_{F}=\displaystyle\max_{b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\delta_{F}(1,b),

(1)

in this case. In
[
3
]

locally-APN

p=2

[

p

\delta_{F}(1,b)\leq 2\text{ for all }b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p}.

(2)

In
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
, the authors discussed the locally-APNness of
F_{r,\pm 1}
when
r=p^{n}-2
and
r=2
, respectively. They defined that a function
F
on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
is locally-APN if

\delta_{F}(a,b)\leq 2\text{ for all }a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}\text{ and }b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{p},

(3)

and claimed that
F_{r,\pm 1}
satisfy this property and hence it is locally-APN. Note that they actually showed
\delta_{F_{r,\pm 1}}(a,b)\leq 2
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}

However, we can naturally extend (

\delta_{F}(a,b)=\delta_{F}(1,g_{a}(b))\text{ for all }b\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}},

independent of the sign of
\chi(b)
.
∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
p
be an odd prime such that
\chi(-2)=-1
and let
g(x)=x^{2}+ax+\frac{1}{8}a^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}[x]
with
a\neq 0
. Then,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(xg(x))=0.

Proof.

Letting
l=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(xg(x))
,

\displaystyle l=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}}\chi(x)\chi(g(x))=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}}\chi\left(\frac{g(x)}{x}\right)=\chi(c)\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}}\chi\left(\frac{g(x)}{cx}\right),

where
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
will be determined soon. Writing
t=\frac{g(x)}{cx}
, one has an equivalent expression
0=g(x)-ctx=x^{2}+(a-ct)x+\frac{1}{8}a^{2}
. Denoting the discriminant of the quadratic equation as
D(t)=(a-ct)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}a^{2}=c^{2}t^{2}-2act+\frac{1}{2}a^{2}
, it is easy to see that the number of solutions
x
satisfying
\frac{g(x)}{cx}=t
is given as
1+\chi(D(t))
. Therefore,

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle=\chi(c)\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}}\chi\left(\frac{g(x)}{cx}\right)=\chi(c)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\left(1+\chi(D(t))\right)\chi(t)=\chi(c)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(D(t))\chi(t)

\displaystyle=\chi(c)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(t)\chi\left(c^{2}t^{2}-2act+\frac{1}{2}a^{2}\right)=\chi(c)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(t)\chi\left(t^{2}-\frac{2a}{c}t+\frac{a^{2}}{2c^{2}}\right).

Finally, putting
c=-2
, one gets

l=\chi(-2)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(t)\chi\left(t^{2}+at+\frac{1}{8}a^{2}\right)=-\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(xg(x))=-l,

which shows
l=0
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

The above lemma, as well as Lemma
3.4
, is closely related to the so-called supersingular elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
(see Section 4.6 in
[
22
]
), which also have wide applications in post-quantum cryptography
[
16
]
through the use of isogeny problems between supersingular elliptic curves. An elliptic curve
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
(with
p
an odd prime) is a nonsingular curve of genus one and can be written in the form
E\colon y^{2}=f(x)
, where
f(x)=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}
is a cubic polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
with nonzero discriminant. There are many equivalent definitions for when
E
is supersingular, one of which states that
E
is supersingular if and only if
\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(f(x))\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. In this context, Lemma
3.13
implies that the curve
y^{2}=x\left(x^{2}+ax+\tfrac{1}{8}a^{2}\right)
(with
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
) is supersingular when
p^{n}\equiv 5,7\pmod{8}
.

Furthermore, the result of Lemma
3.4
arises from the well-known supersingular elliptic curve
E\colon y^{2}=2(x^{3}+x)
(see Proposition 4.37 in
[
22
]
), since the curve
C\colon y^{2}=x^{4}-1
is birationally equivalent to
E
via the map
(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{x+1}{x-1},\frac{2y}{(x-1)^{2}}\right)
. As the transformation
x\mapsto\frac{x+1}{x-1}
is a permutation on
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}\setminus\{1\}
, we easily obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(x^{4}-1)=\displaystyle\sum_{x\neq 1}\chi(x^{4}-1)=\displaystyle\sum_{x\neq 1}\chi(2(x^{3}+x))=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\chi(2(x^{3}+x))\right)-\chi(4)=-1,

where the supersingular property of
E
is used in the final equality.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

. Assume that
\chi(1+u)=(-1)^{r}\chi(1-u)
. If

Proof.

Suppose that

\chi(b^{2}+(1+u)^{2})=\chi(2b(1+u))\text{ and }\chi(b^{2}+(1-u)^{2})=-\chi(b(1-u))

cannot hold simultaneously. Hence,
D_{00}^{u}(b)\neq 1
or

Suppose that

\chi((1+u)^{2}-b^{2})=\chi((1-u)^{2}-b^{2})

\chi((1+u)^{2}-b^{2})=\chi(2b(1+u))\text{ and }\chi((1-u)^{2}-b^{2})=-\chi(b(1-u))

D_{00}^{u}(b)\neq 1

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated the differential and boomerang properties of the binomial function
F_{r,u}(x)=x^{r}(1+u\chi(x))
over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
, where
r=\frac{p^{n}+1}{4}
with
p^{n}\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}^{*}
. Specifically, we proved that
F_{r,u}
is a differentially
5
-uniform permutation when
\chi(1+u)=(-1)^{r+1}\chi(1-u)
, and a differentially
4
-uniform function when
\chi(1+u)=(-1)^{r}\chi(1-u)
. Also, we show that
F_{r,u}
is a differentially
4
-uniform permutation, when
u\in\{\pm\frac{1-2^{r+1}}{3}\}
and
p^{n}\equiv 3\pmod{8}
. Furthermore, we showed that
F_{r,\pm 1}
is locally-PN with boomerang uniformity
0
when
p^{n}\equiv 3\pmod{8}
, and locally-APN with boomerang uniformity at most
2
when
p^{n}\equiv 7\pmod{8}
. To the best of our knowledge, this provides the second known non-PN class with boomerang uniformity
0
, and the first such example in odd characteristic fields with
p>3
. We also investigated the differential and boomerang spectra of
F_{r,\pm 1}
, and showed that
\beta_{F_{r,1}}=2
if
p^{n}\equiv 7\pmod{8}
with
p^{n}\neq 7,31
. The case studied in this paper constitutes the third known class of functions
F_{r,u}
whose differential and boomerang properties have been analyzed in detail, following the cases
r=p^{n}-2
and
r=2
.

Our motivation for focusing on the case
r=\frac{p^{n}+1}{4}
stems from the observation that both
x^{r}
and
x^{r}\chi(x)
are APN in certain cases, and exhibit low differential uniformity even when they are not APN. It is also worth noting that there are several other exponent classes
r
for which both
x^{r}
and
x^{r}\chi(x)
are known to exhibit low differential uniformity (see Table 1 of
[
14
]
). In fact, we have conducted preliminary experiments using SageMath on some of these classes, and observed that many of them indeed possess low differential uniformity, although there are also cases where the differential uniformity exceeds 5. Nevertheless, we expect that some of these classes may contain functions with interesting cryptographic properties, and we plan to explore them further in future work.

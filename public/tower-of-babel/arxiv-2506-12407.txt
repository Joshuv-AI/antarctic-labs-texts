Superconvergent quadriatic finite element on uniform tetrahedral meshes Thanks: Yunqing Huang was supported in part by National Natural Science Foundation of China Project (11971410) and China’s National Key R&D Programs (2020YFA0713500).
Yunqing Huang, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
. By a direct computation, we show that the P 2 P_{2} interpolation of a P 3 P_{3} function is also a local H 1 H^{1} -projection on uniform tetrahedral meshes, i.e., the difference is H 1 H^{1} -orthogonal to the P 2 P_{2} Lagrange basis function on the support patch of tetrahedra of the basis function. Consequently, we show the H 1 H^{1} and L 2 L^{2} superconvergence of the P 2 P_{2} Lagrange finite element on uniform tetrahedral meshes. Using the standard 20-points Lagrange P 3 P_{3} interpolation, where the 20 nodes are exactly some P 2 P_{2} global basis nodes, we lift the superconvergent P 2 P_{2} finite element solution to a quasi-optimal P 3 P_{3} solution on each cube. Numerical results confirm the theory.

1.
Introduction

We solve the 3D Poisson equation on uniform tetrahedral meshes by the quadratic
Lagrange finite element:

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=f\qquad\hbox{in }\Omega,

(1.2)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\qquad\hbox{on }\partial\Omega,

where the polyhedral domain
\Omega
can be subdivided into uniform
(i.e. every tetrahedron is one of the six cut from a cube of same size
h
)
tetrahedral meshes
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
shown in Figure
1
.
The variational form of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) reads:
Find
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\nabla u,\nabla v)&=(f,v)\qquad\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).\end{aligned}

The quadratic Lagrange finite element space is defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{v_{h}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):v_{h}|_{T}\in P_{2}(T),\ T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

The finite element discretization of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) reads: Find
u\in V_{h}

such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})

\displaystyle=(f,v_{h})\qquad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

By the standard finite element theory, we have the optimal order error estimates that

\displaystyle|u-u_{h}|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}|u|_{3},

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{3}|u|_{3}.

Its
H^{1}
superconvergence is proved in
[
4
]
that

\displaystyle|I_{h}u-u_{h}|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{3}|u|_{4},

where
I_{h}
is the
P_{2}
Lagrange interpolation operator.
Its
L^{\infty}
and
L^{2}
superconvergence is proved in
[
8
]
that

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|\ln h|^{2}C(u),

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}C(u),

where
C(u)
is some very high order norm of
u
.
Based on a weak orthogonality that

(1.5)

\displaystyle|(\nabla(u-I_{h}u),\Pi_{h}^{1}v)|\leq Ch^{4}\|u\|_{4}\|v\|_{2},\quad u,v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

where
\Pi_{h}^{1}
the
P_{1}
Lagrange interpolation operator,
the
L^{2}
superconvergence is also proved in
[
10
]
.

Figure 1.
Using the standard 20
P_{3}
Lagrange nodes, which are exactly
some existing
P_{2}
finite basis nodes,
to lift a superconvergent
P_{2}
finite element solution to a
quasi-optimal
P_{3}
solution.

All above works are based on the finite element approximation of the Green’s function
G_{z}
.
But the finite element
H^{1}
-projection(approximation) of Green’s functions
itself is not computable.
As Green’s functions are singular, such error estimates may lead to extra
\ln
-terms or
may not result in the optimal bound on the right hand side.
We revisit this problem in this work,
intending to prove the optimal superconvergent bounds,

(1.6)

\displaystyle|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{4},

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{4},

where

is the solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
),
u_{h}
is the
solution of (
1.4
) and

is the

Lagrange interpolation operator
on mesh

.

By a direct computation, we show that the
P_{2}
interpolation of a
P_{3}

function is also a local
H^{1}
-projection on uniform tetrahedral meshes, i.e.,

\displaystyle(\nabla(u-I_{h}u),\phi_{i})=0\qquad\hbox{\rm{if }}\ u\in P_{3}(R_{i}),

where
\phi_{i}
is a global basis of
P_{2}
finite element space
V_{h}
,
and
R_{i}=\cup_{\phi_{i}|_{T}\neq 0}T
is a patch of tetrahedra.
Based on it, we prove the optimal form of (
1.5
) above that

\displaystyle|(\nabla(u-I_{h}u),v_{h})|\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{4}\|v_{h}|_{1},\quad u\in H^{4}(\Omega),\ v\in V_{h}.

Consequently, we show the
H^{1}
and

superconvergence of the
P_{2}
Lagrange finite element
on uniform tetrahedral meshes, (
1.6

1.7

Using the standard 20-points Lagrange
P_{3}
interpolation, where the 20 nodes are
exactly some
P_{2}
global basis nodes, we lift the superconvergent
P_{2}
finite
element solution to a quasi-optimal
P_{3}
solution on each cube.
Such a lift has almost no computational cost, but it provides quasi-optimal
P_{3}
solutions.
Numerical tests are given, showing the superconvergence of
P_{2}
finite element solutions and
the quasi-optimality of lifted
P_{3}

3.
Superconvergence

We prove the
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
superconvergence in this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{4}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

be the solution of the Poisson equation (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).
Let
u\in V_{h}
of (
1.3
)
be the solution of the finite element equation (
1.4
).
It holds that

(3.1)

\displaystyle|I_{h}u-u_{h}|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{4},

where
I_{h}
is the
P_{2}
Lagrange interpolation operator.

Proof.

Multiplying (
1.1
) by
v_{h}\in V_{h}
and
doing integration by parts, we get

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\nabla u,\nabla v_{h})

\displaystyle=(f,v_{h}).

Subtracting (
3.2
) from (
1.4
), we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\nabla(u-u_{h}),\nabla v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

By (
3.3
), (
2.1
), (
2.8
), (
2.23
) and
(
2.38
),we have, denoting
v_{h}=I_{h}u-u_{h}\in V_{h}
,

\displaystyle|I_{h}u-u_{h}|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nabla(I_{h}u-u),\nabla v_{h})

\displaystyle=\quad\sum_{R_{1}\subset\Omega}c_{1,i}(\nabla(I-I_{h})(I-P_{3,1})u,\nabla\phi_{1})_{R_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\ +\sum_{R_{2}\subset\Omega}c_{2,i}(\nabla(I-I_{h})(I-P_{3,2})u,\nabla\phi_{2})_{R_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\ +\sum_{R_{3}\subset\Omega}c_{3,i}(\nabla(I-I_{h})(I-P_{3,3})u,\nabla\phi_{3})_{R_{3}}

\displaystyle\quad\ +\sum_{R_{4}\subset\Omega}c_{4,i}(\nabla(I-I_{h})(I-P_{3,4})u,\nabla\phi_{4})_{R_{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{R_{j}\subset\Omega}|(I-P_{3,j})u|_{1,R_{j}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)|v_{h}|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{4}\Big(\sum_{R_{j}\subset\Omega}h^{8}|u|_{4,R_{j}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)|v_{h}|_{1}

\displaystyle=Ch^{4}|u|_{4}|v_{h}|_{1},

where
P_{3,j}
is the local
L^{2}
projection operator on to
P_{3}(R_{j})
,
and
I-I_{h}
is
H^{1}
-stable, cf.
[
9
]
.
Hence, canceling one
|v_{h}|_{1}
on each side, we get (
3.1
).
The proof is complete.
∎

To prove the
L^{2}
-supconvergence we use the standard duality argument. Let

w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle-\Delta w

\displaystyle=I_{h}u-u_{h}\qquad\hbox{in }\Omega.

We assume the following full-regularity,

(3.5)

\displaystyle|w|_{2}\leq C\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u\in H^{4}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

be the solution of the Poisson equation (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).
Let
u\in V_{h}
of (
1.3
)
be the solution of the finite element equation (
1.4
).
Assuming the full regularity (
3.5
), it holds that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|{u}|_{4}.

Proof.

Multiplying (
3.4
) by
(I_{h}u-u_{h})
and
doing integration by parts, we get

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nabla w,\nabla(I_{h}u-u_{h})).

Let
w_{h}\in V_{h}
be the
P_{2}
finite element solution for problem
(
3.4
), satisfying

(3.8)

\displaystyle(\nabla w_{h},\nabla v_{h})

\displaystyle=(I_{h}u-u_{h},v_{h})\qquad\forall v_{h}

\displaystyle\in V_{h}.

By (
3.1
), we can get the quasi-optimal error bound for
w_{h}
that

(3.9)

\displaystyle|w-w_{h}|_{1}\leq|w-I_{h}w|_{1}+|I_{h}w-w_{h}|_{1}\leq Ch|w|_{2}.

Subtracting (
3.8
) from (
3.7
), by the quasi-optimal error bound (
3.9
) and

3.5
), we get

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0}^{2}&=(\nabla(w-w_{h}),\nabla(I_{h}u-u_{h}))\\
&\leq Ch|w|_{2}h^{3}|u|_{4}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{4}\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0}.\end{aligned}

After canceling one
\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{0}
, (
3.6
) follows (
3.10
).
The proof is complete.
∎

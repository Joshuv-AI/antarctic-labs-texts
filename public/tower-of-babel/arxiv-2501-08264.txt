Abstract
. We give conditions for topological and bi-Lipschitz equivalences within a class of mixed singularities of Pham-Brieskorn type. As a consequence, we construct infinite families that are topologically trivial but have distinct bi-Lipschitz types. We also investigate this problem in the context of mixed surfaces defined by these singularities in the case of two complex variables, deriving conditions for inner, outer, and ambient bi-Lipschitz equivalences. In particular, we obtain an invariant of the subanalytic outer geometry of the associated mixed surfaces, which is determined by the exponents. Keywords. Mixed polynomials, Pham-Brieskorn singularities, Lipschitz geometry. Mathematics Subject Classification. 14B05, 14J17, 32B20, 32C05.

In this direction, we derive conditions on the vectors of exponents in the case of a pair

2.
Lipschitz geometry

2.1.
Singularities and analytic sets

We recall the basic definitions of Lipschitz Geometry of sets and singularities. We refer the reader to the introductory text
[
28
]
. Let
(X,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the germ of an analytic set. There exist two natural geometric structures on
(X,0)
defined from the Euclidean metric. The
outer metric
is the induced metric, that is,
d_{o}(x,y)=\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in X
. The
inner metric
is the length metric defined by
d_{i}(x,y)=\inf\ell(\gamma)
among all the rectifiable arcs
\gamma
in
X
connecting
x,y\in X
, where
\ell(\gamma)
denotes its length. Given two germs of analytic sets
(X,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
(Y,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we can consider the following equivalence relations.

•

The inner equivalence: if there exists a homeomorphism
\phi:(X,0)\longrightarrow(Y,0)
which is bi-Lipschitz with respect to the inner metric.

•

The outer equivalence: if there exists a homeomorphism
\phi:(X,0)\longrightarrow(Y,0)
which is bi-Lipschitz with respect to the outer metric.

•

Ambient equivalence: if
m=n
and there exists a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism
\phi:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
such that
\phi(X)=Y
.

Notice that the ambient equivalence implies the outer. By
[
13
, Remark 1.2]
, the outer equivalence also implies the inner. Now, we consider the bi-Lipschitz equivalence of smooth map germs or singularities. In what follows,
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}

Let
C(n,p)
denote the set of smooth map germs
f:(\mathbb{F}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{F}^{p},0)
and
J^{k}(n,p)
the set of
k
-jets of elements of
C(n,p)
. Two germs
f,g\in C(n,p)
are bi-Lipschitz-equivalent if there exists a germ of Lipschitz homeomorphism
\phi
such that its inverse
\phi^{-1}
is also a Lipschitz homeomorphism and
g=f\circ\phi^{-1}
. A germ
f\in C(n,p)
is
k
-bi-Lipschitz determined if there exist an integer
k
such that
f
is bi-Lipschitz-equivalent to all germs
g
such that
j^{k}_{0}(g)=j^{k}_{0}(f)
, where
j^{k}_{0}(g)
and
j^{k}_{0}(f)
are the
k
-jets at the the origin of
g
and
f
, respectively. If
f
is bi-Lipschitz determined for some
k
, we say that
f
is bi-Lipschitz finitely determined. We refer to topological equivalence if
\phi
is only a homomorphism. These relations are also known as
right equivalence
in the literature. In the rest of the article, by equivalence (respectively, topological equivalence) and finite determinacy, we mean the previous equivalences under bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms (respectively, homeomorphisms).

The Taylor expansion at the origin of an analytic function germ
f:(\mathbb{F}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{F},0)
can be written as
f=\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}f_{k}
, where
f_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
for each
i
. The lowest degree
k
is called the
multiplicity
of
f
, denoted by
m_{f}
, and the associated homogeneous polynomial is denoted by
H_{f}
. We have the following results.

Theorem 2.1

(Section 4,
[
22
]
- Corollary,
[
26
]
)
.

(1)

If
f,g:(\mathbb{F}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{F}^{p},0)
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent smooth maps germs, then the rank of
f
at
0
is equal to the rank of
g
at
0
.

(2)

If
f,g:(\mathbb{F}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{F},0)
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent smooth function germs, then
m_{f}=m_{g}
. Moreover,
H_{f}
and
H_{g}
are also bi-Lipschitz equivalent as well as their singular sets
\Sigma(H_{f})
and
\Sigma(H_{g})
.

An alternative proof for the first assertion of the second item can be seen in
[
28
, Lemma 5.12]
. We recall the definition of the tangent cone of an analytic set to introduce the invariance theorem. This notion generalizes the tangent space at a smooth point of a real or complex variety.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an analytic set and
x_{0}\in\overline{X}
. A vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
tangent vector
of
X
at
x_{0}
if there exists a sequence
(x_{i})\subset X\setminus\{x_{0}\}
and a sequence of positive real numbers
(t_{i})
such that
x_{i}\longmapsto x_{0}
and
v=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{i}}(x_{i}-x_{0})
.

Definition 2.2

(Definition 2.1,
[
29
]
)
.

The tangent cone of
X
at
x_{0}
is the set

C(X,x_{0})=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:v\;\,\text{is a tangent vector of}\;\,X\,\text{at}\;\,x_{0}\in\overline{X}\right\}.

We usually refer to
C(X,x_{0})
as the
geometric tangent cone
of
X
at
x_{0}
. If
X
is a smooth manifold in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
, it is clear that the tangent cone coincides with the tangent space. There is also an alternative description in terms of velocity of arcs in the set as follows (see
[
29
, Remark 2.2]
): the tangent cone is the set of vectors
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which there exists a continuous semialgebraic curve
\alpha:[0,\epsilon)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\alpha(0)=x_{0}
,
\alpha(0,\epsilon)\subset X
, and
\alpha(t)-x_{0}=tv+o(t)
, where
o(t)
denotes higher degree terms. A second characterization of tangent vectors is the following.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
20
]
, Lemma 2.3)
.

Let
X
be a real analytic variety and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
a non-zero vector. Then
v\in C(X,0)
if and only if there exists a sequence
(t_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}
of positive integers such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}=0
and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{d(t_{k}v,X)}{t_{k}}=0,

where
d(t_{k}v,X)
is the usual Euclidean distance from the point
t_{k}v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the analytic set
X
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{n}
be the ring of analytic function germs at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
I(X)\subset\mathcal{O}_{n}
be the ideal generated by the germs of analytic functions vanishing at
X
. Given
f\in I(X)
, let
H_{f}
denote the lowest degree homogeneous polynomial of
f
and
I_{0}
the ideal generated by
H_{f}
for all
f\in I(X)
. The
algebraic tangent cone
of
X
at
0
is

C_{a}(X,0)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:H_{f}(x)=0\;\forall\;f\in I(X)\}.

A classical result due to Whitney states that the algebraic tangent cone and the tangent cone coincide in the complex case. In general, we have the following properties:

Proposition 2.4

(Remark 2.2,
[
20
]
)
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be analytic sets. The following inclusions hold:

(1)

C(X\cap Y,0)\subset C(X,0)\cap C(Y,0)
.

(2)

C_{a}(X\cap Y,0)\subset C_{a}(X,0)\cap C_{a}(Y,0)
.

(3)

C(X,0)\subset C_{a}(X,0)
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
X=V(I)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an analytic set defined by an ideal

. If
I(X)=\{g\in\mathcal{O}_{n}:g(x)=0\;\forall\;x\in X\}
and
I_{0}(X)
and
I_{0}
are the ideals generated by all the forms of elements in
I(X)
and
I
, respectively, it holds that
I_{0}\subset I_{0}(X)
and thus
V(I_{0}(X))=C_{a}(X,0)\subset V(I_{0})
.

The main result in
[
29
]
is the invariance of the tangent cone under bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms.

Theorem 2.6

(Theorem 3.2,
[
29
]
)
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be subanalytic sets. If the germs
(X,x_{0})
and
(Y,y_{0})
are outer bi-Lipschitz equivalent, then the tangent cones
(C(X,x_{0}),x_{0})
and

2.2.
Semialgebraic surfaces

We recall in this section the inner bi-Lipschitz classification of semialgebraic surfaces
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with an isolated singularity. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
for details. They are associated with the so-called
\beta
-horns as follows.

Definition 2.7
.

The
\beta
-horn is the semialgebraic set defined by

H_{\beta}=\left\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)=z^{2\beta},z\geq 0\right\},

where
\beta\geq 1
is a rational number.

If
\beta=1
the horn
H_{1}
is called the
metric cone
. Moreover,
H_{\beta}
is inner equivalent to
H_{\beta^{\prime}}
if and only if
\beta=\beta^{\prime}
. In
[
7
]
, L. Birbrair gives the following classification.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
(X,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a semialgebraic surface germ with an isolated singularity at the origin and connected link. Then
(X,0)
is inner bi-Lipschitz equivalent to a
\beta
-horn
H_{\beta}
for some
\beta\geq 1
.

We present now a useful result to compute the index
\beta
. First, recall that a
semialgebraic arc
at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the image of a non-constant semialgebraic map
\gamma:[0,\epsilon)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\gamma(0)=0
. Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
be two semialgebraic arcs. A semialgebraic function
f(t)
has an expansion
f(t)=at^{p}+o(p)
, where
a\neq 0
and
p
is rational. The order of contact
\lambda(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
is defined as the order of the expansion of the semialgebraic function
\rho(t)=\|\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{2}(t)\|
. For details, we refer the reader to
[
13
, Lemma 2]
. In particular, let
\gamma_{i}=u_{i}t+o(t)
, with
u_{1},u_{2}
linearly independent vectors and

, then the order of contact is
1
.

Theorem 2.9

(Theorem 4.1,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
(X,0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a semialgebraic surface germ which is inner bi-Lipschitz equivalent to a germ of a
\beta
-horn. Then

\displaystyle\beta=\inf\left\{\lambda(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}):\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\;\text{are semialgebraic arcs in $X$ such that}\;\gamma_{1}(0)=\gamma_{2}(0)=0\right\}.

2.3.
Determinacy of weighted homogeneous germs

In this section, we introduce finite determinacy results for the weighted homogeneous case.

Definition 2.10
.

(1)

Given
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n};d_{1},\dots,d_{p})
, where
r_{i},d_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
, a map germ
f:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{p},0)
is weighted homogeneous of type
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n};d_{1},\dots,d_{p})
if for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
:

\displaystyle f(\lambda^{r_{1}}x_{1},\dots,\lambda^{r_{n}}x_{n})=(\lambda^{d_{1}}f_{1}(x),\dots,\lambda^{d_{p}}f_{p}(x)).

(2)

For each monomial
x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
in
C(n,1)
we define
\fl(x^{\alpha})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\alpha_{i}
.

(3)

We define a filtration in the ring
C(n,1)
via the following function:

\displaystyle\fl(f)=\inf_{\alpha}\left\{\fl(x^{\alpha}):\left(\frac{\partial^{\alpha}f}{\partial x^{\alpha}}\right)(0)\neq 0\right\}.

(4)

For any map germ
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{p})\in C(n,p)
we call
\fl(f)=(d_{1},\dots,d_{p})
, where
d_{i}=\fl(f_{i})
for each
i=1,\dots,p
.

Based on a particular version in
[
15
]
, the next result is stated in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
f:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{p},0)
be a weighted homogeneous map germ with an isolated singularity at the origin. Suppose that
f
is of type
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n};d_{1},\dots,d_{p})
, where
r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\dots\leq r_{n}
. Let
f_{t}(x)=f(x)+t\Theta(x,t)
be a smooth deformation of
f
with
\Theta=(\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{p}):(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{p},0)
. The following statements hold:

(1)

If
\fl(\Theta_{i})>d_{i}+r_{n}-r_{1}
, then
f_{t}
admits a bi-Lipschitz trivialization along
I=[0,1]
.

(2)

If
\fl(\Theta_{i})=d_{i}+r_{n}-r_{1}
, then
f_{t}
is bi-Lipschitz trivial for small
t
.

The next example shows that the inequalities of the hypothesis above are sharp.

Example 2.12
.

Consider the function germ
f(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}
and its deformation

\displaystyle f_{t}(x,y)=f(x,y)+t(-x^{2}-y^{2}+x^{2k+1}+y^{2k+1}),

where
t\in[0,1]
. Notice that
f_{1}^{-1}(0)
and
f^{-1}(0)
have distinct topologies, hence the deformation is not topologically trivial along
[0,1]
.

3.
Topological equivalence

We detail in this section the topological relation between the mixed polynomials
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
and Pham-Brieskorn polynomials
g_{\textbf{a}}
and some consequences. This allows us to derive conditions on the vector of exponents in the case of topological equivalence. In particular, whether
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is a topological submersion.

Proposition 3.1

(Theorem 4.1,
[
27
]
)
.

Let
b_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. The following statements hold:

(1)

If
a_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
, then
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is topologically equivalent to the holomorphic Pham-Brieskorn polynomial
g_{\textbf{a}}
.

(2)

If
a_{i}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,k
and
a_{i}\geq 1
for all
i=k+1,\dots,n
, then
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is topologically equivalent to
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|z_{i}\|^{2b_{i}}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}z_{i}^{a_{i}}
, where
1\leq k<n
and
\tilde{\textbf{b}}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k},0,\dots,0)
.

We call
g_{\textbf{a}}
and
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}
the topological normal forms of
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
D=\{z_{1}\dots z_{n}=0\}
and define the map
\phi:\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus D\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus D
by
\phi(z)=w
, where
w_{i}=z_{i}\|z_{i}\|^{2b_{i}/a_{i}}
, for all
i=1,\dots,k
, and
w_{i}=z_{i}
if
a_{i}=0
for all
i=k+1,\dots,n
. Its inverse map is
\phi^{-1}(w)=z
, where

\begin{cases}z_{i}=w_{i}&\text{if}\;i=1,\dots,k,\\
z_{i}=\frac{w_{i}}{\|w_{i}\|^{\frac{2b_{i}}{a_{i}+2b_{i}}}}&\text{if}\;i=k+1,\dots,n,\end{cases}

Since
2b_{i}<a_{i}+2b_{i}
provided that
a_{i}\geq 1
, it holds that
\lim_{w_{i}\to 0}z_{i}=0
. Whence
\phi
extends to a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by mapping
z_{i}=0
to
w_{i}=0
for each
i=1,\dots,n
, and satisfies
g_{\textbf{a}}\circ\phi=f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
, in the case
k=0
, or
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}\circ\phi=f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
if
k\neq n
.
∎

However, note that on each coordinate subspace
\{z_{i}=0\}
,
\phi
is never a diffeomorphism. Recall that two germs of sets
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
at the origin have the same
topological type
, also called embedded topological type if there exists a germ of homeomorphism
\phi:(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{n},0)
such that
\phi(X)=Y
.

Theorem 3.2

(Theorem,
[
32
]
)
.

Let
g_{\textbf{a}},g_{\textbf{c}}:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
be two Pham-Brieskorn polynomials, where
a_{i},c_{i}\geq 2
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. Then the hypersurface germs defined by
g_{\textbf{a}}
and
g_{\textbf{c}}
have the same topological type if and only if
\textbf{a}=\textbf{c}
.

This result will also describe the topological properties of
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
. For instance, if
a_{1}=1
, then
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is a topological submersion. We shall prove that the converse holds, even in the case
a_{i}=0
for some, but not all, indices
i
. For this purpose, we must return to the holomorphic context.

The well-known Zariski conjecture concerns the invariance of the multiplicity of holomorphic function germs under a homeomorphism of their zero sets. In
[
31
]
, Lê Dung Trang proved that the Betti numbers of the associated Milnor fiber are preserved under homeomorphisms. On the other hand, A’Campo proved in
[
5
]
that regularity at a point implies that the homology of the fiber is trivial (see
[
14
]
for details). Since regularity means multiplicity equals one, the conjecture holds in this case:

Lemma 3.3

(Theorem 11.3,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
f,g:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
be reduced germs of holomorphic functions. If
f
and
g
are topologically equivalent and the multiplicity
m_{f}=1
, then
m_{g}=1
. In other words, the multiplicity is equal to one is preserved under homeomorphisms.

We obtain the following characterization of topological submersions.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
a_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
and
a_{j}\geq 1
for some
j
. Then the mixed function
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is topologically equivalent to a submersion
(\mathbb{R}^{2n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)
if and only if
a_{i}=1
for some
i
.

Proof.

Consider the topological normal forms of
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
. If
a_{i}=1
for some
i
, then the Jacobian matrix of
g_{\textbf{a}}
or
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}
at
0
contains a non-vanishing minor of order 2 associated with
z_{i}
and thus it is a submersion. Conversely, in the case
a_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
, the assertion is a consequence of Lemma
3.3
. If
a_{j}=0
for some subset of indices, we may suppose
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|z_{i}\|^{2b_{i}}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}z_{i}^{a_{i}}
, where
k<n
and
a_{i}\geq 1
for all
i=k+1,\dots,n
. In this case, since the function
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}
restricted to the variables
z_{i}
for
i\leq k
is real and singular at the origin for any coordinate system, the complex function
\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}z_{i}^{a_{i}}
is a topological submersion. Therefore, the same lemma applies and we conclude that
a_{i}=1
for some
i=k+1,\dots,n
.
∎

We can state the following.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
b_{i},d_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
and suppose that
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is topologically equivalent to
f_{\textbf{c},\textbf{d}}
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

If
a_{i},c_{i}\geq 2
, then
\textbf{a}=\textbf{c}
.

(2)

If
a_{i}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,k
and
a_{i}\geq 2
for all
i=k+1,\dots,n
, then
c_{i}=0
for all
i=k+1,\dots,n
.

(3)

If
a_{1}=1
, then
c_{1}=1
.

Proof.

The first assertion results from applying Theorem
3.2
on the topological normal forms
g_{\textbf{a}}
and
g_{\textbf{c}}
. In the second case, suppose that there exists a germ of homeomorphism
\psi:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
such that
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}\circ\psi=f_{\textbf{c},\tilde{\textbf{d}}}
. We may suppose

\displaystyle f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|z_{i}\|^{2b_{i}}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}z_{i}^{a_{i}},

\displaystyle f_{\textbf{c},\tilde{\textbf{d}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\|z_{i}\|^{2d_{i}}+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}z_{i}^{c_{i}}.

Since the imaginary parts of
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}
and
f_{\textbf{c},\tilde{\textbf{d}}}
are topologically equivalent, the codimensions of the zero sets determined by
\operatorname{Im}(f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}})
and
\operatorname{Im}(f_{\textbf{c},\tilde{\textbf{d}}})
are the same, and thus
l=k
. The last case is a consequence of Proposition
3.4
, since the topological normal forms are submersions.
∎

In the first case of the above theorem, if
\textbf{a}=\textbf{c}
, it is clear that the pair is topologically equivalent. Moreover, if
a_{i}=1
for some
i
, then they are equivalent if and only if
c_{i}=1
.

4.
Bi-Lipschitz equivalence

To study the bi-Lipschitz equivalence of pairs of Pham-Brieskorn polynomials, we prove a result on the multiplicity of germs in separable variables. This is a weaker version of the Splitting Lemma for bi-Lipschitz equivalence of singularities.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F,G:(\mathbb{F}^{2n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{F},0)
be a pair of smooth function germs of the following forms:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}F(x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n})=f_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})+\dots+f_{n}(x_{n},y_{n}),\\
G(x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n})=g_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})+\dots+g_{n}(x_{n},y_{n}).\end{cases}

Suppose that:

(1)

For each
i=1,\dots,n
,
f_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}),g_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}):(\mathbb{F}^{2},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{F},0)
are homogeneous polynomials and have isolated singularity at the origin.

(2)

2\leq m_{f_{1}}<m_{f_{2}}<\dots<m_{f_{n}}
.

(3)

2\leq m_{g_{1}}<m_{g_{2}}<\dots<m_{g_{n}}
.

If
F
and
G
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent, then
m_{f_{i}}=m_{g_{i}}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

The statement is a generalization of
[
22
, Theorem 5.1]
and has a similar proof. Let
\phi=(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n})
be a bi-Lipschitz homomorphism of
\mathbb{F}^{2n}
such that
F\circ\phi=G
, where
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{F}^{2n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}^{2}
for each
i
. Let us denote its inverse by
\psi=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n})
and consider the sequences

\displaystyle\phi_{m}

\displaystyle=(\phi_{1,m},\dots,\phi_{n,m})=m.\phi\left(\frac{x_{1}}{m},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{m}\right),

\displaystyle\psi_{m}

\displaystyle=(\psi_{1,m},\dots,\psi_{n,m})=m.\psi\left(\frac{x_{1}}{m},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{m}\right).

By Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, there exists
\{m_{i}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
\phi_{m_{i}},\psi_{m_{i}}
converge to bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms
d\phi=(d\phi_{1},\dots,d\phi_{n})
and
d\psi=(d\psi_{1},\dots,d\psi_{n})
, respectively. Moreover,
d\phi
is the inverse of
d\psi
. These limits can be interpreted as the derivatives of
\phi
and
\psi
. By the conditions on
f_{i},g_{i}
, the singular sets are

\displaystyle\Sigma(f_{i})=\mathbb{F}^{2}\times\dots\times\mathbb{F}^{2}\times\underbrace{\{0\}}_{i}\times\mathbb{F}^{2}\times\dots\times\mathbb{F}^{2}.

By
[
22
, Lemma 4.6]
, it holds that
m_{f_{1}}=m_{g_{1}}
and
d\phi(\Sigma(f_{1}))=\Sigma(g_{1})
. This implies

(4.1)

\displaystyle d\phi(p_{2})=\left(0,d\tilde{\phi}_{2}(p_{2})\right),

where
p_{2}\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}^{2(n-1)}
and
d\tilde{\phi}_{2}=\left(d\phi_{2},\dots,d\phi_{n}\right)
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism of
\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}^{2(n-1)}
, which is the limit of the sequence above constructed from
\tilde{\phi}_{2}=(\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{n})
. We remark on the following identity (see
[
22
, Remark 4.4]
):

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{m_{i}\to\infty}m_{i}^{m_{f_{2}}-1}DF_{\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y/m_{i})}&=\lim_{m_{i}\to\infty}\left(m_{i}^{m_{f_{2}}-1}(Df_{2})_{\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y/m_{i})}+m_{i}^{m_{f_{2}}-1}o(\|y/m_{i}\|)\right)\\
&=\lim_{m_{i}\to\infty}\left((Df_{2})_{m_{i}\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y/m_{i})}+m_{i}^{m_{f_{2}}-1}o(\|y/m_{i}\|)\right)\\
&=(Df_{2})_{d\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y)},\end{split}

for all
(0,y)\in\mathbb{F}^{2(n-1)}
. As a consequence of
[
22
, Lemma 4.5]
, we have:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|(Df_{2})_{\tilde{\phi}_{2}(p_{2})}\|

\displaystyle\leq L\|(DG)_{p_{2}}\|,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|(Dg_{2})_{\tilde{\psi}_{2}(p_{2})}\|

\displaystyle\leq L^{\prime}\|(DF)_{p_{2}}\|,

where
L,L^{\prime}>0
are constants and
p_{2}\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{F}^{2(n-1)}
. We now proceed to prove that
m_{f_{2}}=m_{g_{2}}
and
d\tilde{\phi}_{2}(\Sigma(f_{2}))=\Sigma(g_{2})
. Multiplying (
4.3
) by
m_{i}^{m_{g_{2}-1}}
, we obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left\|m_{i}^{m_{g_{2}}-1}\left(Df_{2}\right)_{\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y/m_{i})}\right\|\leq L\left\|m_{i}^{m_{g_{2}}-1}\left(DG\right)_{(0,y/m_{i})}\right\|.

Write
(DG)_{(0,y/m_{i})}=(Dg_{2})_{(0,y/m_{i})}+o(\|y/m_{i}\|)
, where
o(\|y/m_{i}\|)
denotes higher order terms. Multiplying this equality by
m_{i}^{m_{g_{2}}-1}
and taking limits, the right-hand side tends to
(Dg_{2})_{(0,y)}
because
Dg_{2}
is homogeneous of degree
m_{g_{2}}-1
. This implies (
4.5
) is bounded provided
y
is. We conclude the same for
m_{i}^{m_{f_{2}}-1}DF_{(0,y/m_{i})}
, which tends to
(Df_{2})_{(0,y)}
. By isolated singularity condition and the fact that
d\tilde{\phi}_{2}
restricts as a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{F}^{2(n-1)}
, there exists
y_{0}\neq 0
such that
(Df_{2})_{(0,d\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y_{0}))}\neq 0
. If
m_{g_{2}}>m_{f_{2}}
, by (
4.2
), the following limit diverges and contradicts the boundness of (
4.5
):

\displaystyle\lim_{m_{i}\to\infty}\left\|m_{i}^{m_{g_{2}-1}}(Df_{2})_{\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y_{0}/m_{i})}\right\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{m_{i}\to\infty}\left\|m_{i}^{m_{g_{2}}-m_{f_{2}}}m_{i}^{m_{f_{2}}-1}(Df_{2})_{\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y_{0}/m_{i})}\right\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{m_{i}\to\infty}m_{i}^{m_{g_{2}}-m_{f_{2}}}\left\|(Df_{2})_{d\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y_{0})}\right\|.

Analougously for the case
m_{g_{2}}<m_{f_{2}}
and we then conclude
m_{f_{2}}=m_{g_{2}}
. Identify now
\Sigma(f_{2})\simeq\{0\}\times\Sigma(f_{2})
and
\Sigma(g_{2})\simeq\{0\}\times\Sigma(g_{2})
. We shall see that
d\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y)\left(\Sigma(f_{2})\right)

\subset\Sigma(g_{2})
and
(d\tilde{\psi}_{2})(0,y)\subset\Sigma(f_{2})
. Since
m_{f_{2}}=m_{g_{2}}
, (
4.5
) implies that

\displaystyle\left\|(Df_{2})_{d\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y)}\right\|\leq L\left\|(Dg_{2})_{(0,y)}\right\|.

If
p=(0,y)\in\Sigma(g_{2})
, then
(Df_{2})_{d\tilde{\phi}_{2}(0,y)}=0
and similarly for
g_{2}
and
d\tilde{\psi}_{2}
. This proves both inclusions. These two facts imply that
d\tilde{\phi}_{2}
restricts to
\mathbb{F}^{2(n-2)}
as a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism as
d\phi
in (
4.1
). For
i=3,\dots,n
and proceeding inductively, we consider the bi-Lipschitz restriction to
\mathbb{F}^{2(n-i)}
of the homeomorphism
d\tilde{\phi}_{2}
and Equations (
4.3
) for
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
at the points
\tilde{\phi}_{i}(p_{i})
and
\tilde{\psi}_{i}(p_{i})
, respectively, to obtain that
m_{f_{i}}=m_{g_{i}}
for all
i=1,\dots,k
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Suppose that
m_{f_{i}}=\dots=m_{f_{i+k}}
for some
k\geq 1
and define
\tilde{f}_{i}=f_{i}+\dots+f_{i+k}
. Following the proof above, we have that
\|(D\tilde{f}_{i})_{d\tilde{\phi}_{i}(0,y)}\|\leq L\|(D\tilde{g}_{i})_{(0,y)}\|
for some
L>0
, where
\tilde{g}_{i}=g_{i}+\dots+g_{i+l}
and
m_{f_{i}}=m_{g_{i}}=\dots=m_{g_{i+l}}
for some
l
. A reverse inequality also holds at a point
d\tilde{\psi}_{i}(0,y)
. Then the singular sets of
\tilde{f}_{i}
and
\tilde{g}_{i}
are related by a homeomorphism, and thus
l=k
. This shows that the argument may be applied to blocks of germs with distinct multiplicities, and the inequalities are not restrictive.

Now, we can deduce that the bi-Lipschitz type of
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
within the family of Pham-Brieskorn polynomials is determined by
a
and
b
, which can be seen as a generalization of Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
b_{i},d_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,n,
and suppose that
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
f_{\textbf{c},\textbf{d}}
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

If
a_{i},c_{i}\geq 2
for all
i=1,\dots,n
, then
\textbf{a}=\textbf{c}
and
a_{i}+2b_{i}=c_{\sigma(i)}+2d_{\sigma(i)}
for every
i=1,\dots,k
and some permutation
\sigma
.

(2)

If
a_{i}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,k
and
a_{i}\geq 2
for all
i=k+1,\dots,n
, then
c_{i}=0
for all
i=,\dots,k
, and
a_{i}+2b_{i}=c_{\sigma(i)}+2d_{\sigma(i)}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
and some permutation
\sigma
.

(3)

If
a_{1}=1
, then
c_{1}=1
. Moreover,
a_{i}+2b_{i}=c_{\sigma(i)}+2d_{\sigma(i)}
for every
i=1,\dots,n
and some permutation
\sigma
.

Proof.

First, by Propositions
3.4
and
3.5
, the topological type determines
a
and
c
. Moreover, from the proof of Lemma
5.2
, the monomials
z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{b_{i}}
have an isolated singularity at the origin, then the same holds for their real parts. We apply now Theorem
4.1
for the real parts of the germs, for which the multiplicities of the monomials coincide up to a permutation
\sigma
applied in the indices of one of the functions, and the result follows.
∎

For each
i
in the first case,
a_{i}+2b_{i}=c_{\sigma(i)}+2d_{\sigma(i)}
and
c_{\sigma(i)}=a_{j}
for some
j
. Then
d_{\sigma(i)}
is depends on
a_{i},a_{j}
, and
b_{i}
, and thus
d
is determined by
a
and
b
. The next corollary shows that there is no bi-Lipschitz equivalence between the mixed and complex Pham-Brieskorn polynomials, except possibly for topological submersions.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
b_{1}\leq\dots\leq b_{n}
and suppose that
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to
g_{\textbf{c}}
. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

If
a_{i}\geq 2
for all
i
, then
\textbf{a}=\textbf{c}
and
\textbf{b}=0
, that is,
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}=g_{\textbf{c}}
.

(2)

If
a_{1}=1
, then
c_{1}=1
and
a_{i}+2b_{i}=c_{i}
for all
i
. In particular,
b_{1}=0
.

Proof.

In the first item, considering the normal form
g_{\textbf{a}}
for
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
, Theorem
3.2
implies that
\textbf{a}=\textbf{c}
and Theorem
4.3
shows that
a_{i}+2b_{i}=c_{i}
for all
i
. Analogously for the second case, but now applying Proposition
3.5
.
∎

In particular, for the above cases, the mixed polynomial
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to its topological normal form
g_{\textbf{a}}
if and only if
\textbf{b}=0
.

5.
Families and deformations

The criteria above allow us to construct families of mixed functions with the same topological type, but distinct Lipschitz types. This is generalized through the finite determinacy Theorem
2.11
and is a direct consequence of Theorem
4.3
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
a
be a fixed vector of integers, where
a_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
and
a_{j}\geq 1
for some
j
. There exist subfamilies of
\Gamma_{\textbf{a}}=\{f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C},0):b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\}
that are topologically trivial and contain infinitely many distinct Lipschitz classes.

Proof.

If
a_{i}\geq 2
for all
i
, then every
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is topologically equivalent to
g_{\textbf{a}}
, and the statement holds for the entire family. Suppose now that
a_{i}=0
for
i=1,\dots k
and
a_{i}\geq 2
for
i=k+1,\dots,n
. Let
b_{1}\leq\dots\leq b_{k}
be fixed. Then every
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is topologically equivalent to
f_{\textbf{a},\tilde{\textbf{b}}}
, where
\textbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k},b_{k+1},\dots,b_{n})
. On the other hand, in both cases,
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
and
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{c}}
are bi-Lipschitz equivalent if and only if
\textbf{b}=\textbf{c}
, by Theorem
4.3
, provided that
b_{1}\leq\dots\leq b_{n}
and
c_{1}\leq\dots\leq c_{n}
. Moreover, by the same result, if
a_{1}=1
, then for
b
and
c
such that
b_{1}\neq c_{1}
,
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
and
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{c}}
are not bi-Lipschitz equivalent.
∎

This allows us to construct a variety of families with the same property.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\textbf{a},\textbf{b}
be integer vectors such that
a_{i},b_{i}\geq 1
for all
i=1,\dots,n
,
d=\lcm\{a_{i}+2b_{i}\}
and
r_{i}=d/(a_{i}+2b_{i})
. Then the mixed function germ
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is weighted homogeneous of type
(r_{1},\dots,r_{n};d,d)
and deformations
f_{t}(z,\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z)=f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}+t\Theta(z,\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z)
,
\Theta(z,\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z)=(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
with
\fl(\Theta_{i})>d-r_{n}+r_{1}
, for
i=1,2
, are bi-Lipschitz trivial.

Proof.

We shall see that the origin is an isolated singularity of
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
and then apply Theorem
2.11
. Let
z_{i}=(x_{i},y_{i})
, where
x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(z_{i}+\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}),

\displaystyle y_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(z_{i}-\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}).

Therefore, for
g_{i}=z_{i}^{a_{i}}\|z_{i}\|^{2b_{i}}=z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{b_{i}}
, the real and imaginary parts are:

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}(g_{i})

\displaystyle=\|z_{i}\|^{2b_{i}}(z_{i}^{a_{i}}+\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{a_{i}}),

\displaystyle 2i\operatorname{Im}(g_{i})

\displaystyle=\|z_{i}\|^{2b_{i}}(z_{i}^{a_{i}}-\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{a_{i}}).

The terms of interest will correspond to the Jacobian matrix formed from the partial derivatives of
g_{i}
in relation to
z_{i}
and
\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}(a_{i}+b_{i})z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}-1}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{b_{i}}+b_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}}&b_{i}z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}+(a_{i}+b_{i})z_{i}^{b_{i}}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}-1}\\
(a_{i}+b_{i})z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}-1}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{b_{i}}-b_{i}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}}&b_{i}z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{b_{i}-1}-(a_{i}+b_{i})z_{i}^{b_{i}}\mathrlap{\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\scalebox{0.7}[1]{\phantom{$z$}}}}z_{i}^{a_{i}+b_{i}-1}\end{pmatrix}

whose determinant is
1/2\left[b_{i}^{2}-(a_{i}+b_{i})^{2}\right]\|z_{i}\|^{2(a_{i}+2b_{i}-1)}
. From the conditions on
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
, this determinant is zero if and only if
z_{i}=0
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

∎

Thus, we obtain the topological normal form

6.
Mixed surfaces

In this section, we consider the problem of comparing the Lipschitz geometry of the mixed surfaces
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}^{-1}(0)
, where
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\longrightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
. The first step is to determine the tangent cones, which are 2-dimensional in most cases. This yields conditions for inner, outer, and ambient equivalences. Throughout this section, we shall denote
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}^{-1}(0)=X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
and
g_{\textbf{a}}^{-1}(0)=X_{\textbf{a}}
. Moreover, for the mixed surface
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
we define

\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}:=\frac{a_{2}+2b_{2}}{a_{1}+2b_{1}}.

We shall prove that this number is an invariant of the subanalytic outer bi-Lipschitz geometry of a large class of mixed Pham-Brieskorn surfaces.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}\geq 0
and
a_{2}\geq 1
. The following statements hold:

(1)

If
a_{1}\geq 1
and
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}>1
, then
C(X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}},0)=\{z_{1}=0\}
.

(2)

If
a_{1}\geq 1
and
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}=1
, then
C(X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}},0)=X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
.

(3)

If
a_{1}=0
and
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}>1
, then
C(X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}},0)\subset\left\{\operatorname{Im}z_{2}^{a_{2}}=0\right\}\cap\{z_{1}=0\}
.

(4)

If
a_{1}=0
and
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}<1
, then
C(X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}},0)=\{z_{2}=0\}
.

(5)

If
a_{1}=0
and
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}=1
, then
C(X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}},0)=X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
.

We say that
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is of type (1), (2), (3), (4), or (5) according to each item above.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
and Remark
2.5
, one inclusion is clear in all cases. Recall the characterization in Lemma
2.3
. For item (1), let
v=(0,v_{2})\in\{z_{1}=0\}
and
(t_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}
any sequence of positive integers such that
t_{k}\longmapsto 0
. Since
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}\setminus\{0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{*2}
the distance of
t_{k}v
to
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is bounded by the norm of the corresponding
z_{1}
-coordinate, which is
\|z_{1}\|=t_{k}^{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}\|v_{2}\|^{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}
, where
a_{1}\geq 1
. Therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{d(t_{k}v,X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}})}{t_{k}}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\left(t_{k}\|v_{2}\|\right)^{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}}{t_{k}}=0,

since
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}>1
and similarly for the item (4), but considering the reverse inequality
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}<1
. Let us consider items (2) and (5). Since
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is homogeneous, if
v\in X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
, then for any sequence
(t_{k})\subset\mathbb{R}
it holds that
t_{k}v\in X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
. Thus, the limit above is zero, and we conclude.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

It holds that
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is a regular manifold at the origin if and only if
a_{1}=1
and
b_{1}=0
or
a_{2}=1
and
b_{2}=0
. Henceforth, we exclude these cases from the statements. For surfaces

of type (1), we also suppose that
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2})=1

is homeomorphic with an irreducible complex Pham-Brieskorn variety

. This implies that

is connected.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
be a mixed Pham-Brieskorn surface.

(1)

If
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is of type (2) or (5), then it is Lipschitz normally embedded.

(2)

If
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is of type (3), then
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is Lipschitz normally embedded.

(3)

If
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is of type (4) with
a_{2}=1
, then
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is Lipschitz normally embedded.

(4)

If
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is of type (4) with
a_{2}\geq 2
, then
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is not Lipschitz normally embedded.

(5)

If
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is of type (1) and
a_{1}+2b_{1}
is an even number, then it is not Lipschitz normally embedded.

Proof.

Mixed surfaces of types (2) or (5) are of homogeneous type with an isolated singularity at the origin, so the statement follows from
[
19
, Corollary 2.10]
. We apply the arc criterion
[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
for the other statements. First, let us consider types (3) and (4). For
z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, we take real coordinates
z_{i}=(x_{i},y_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
i=1,2
. Up to the coordinate linear change given by
w_{2}=\xi z_{2}
, where
\xi^{a_{2}}=-1
, we suppose
f_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}=\|z_{1}\|^{2b_{1}}-z_{2}^{a_{2}}\|z_{2}\|^{2b_{2}}
. The surfaces
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
are determined as an intersection with
\operatorname{Im}z_{2}^{a_{2}}=0
, which consists of a finite union of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
passing through the origin. For each one of these lines, we take coordinates
(x_{2},y_{2})
such that
\lambda x_{2}=y_{2}
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and substituting it in the equation
\|z_{1}\|^{2b_{1}}-\operatorname{Re}z_{2}^{a_{2}}\|z_{2}\|^{2b_{2}}=0
, we conclude that
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is a union of
\beta
-horns intersecting only at the origin, where
\beta=(a_{2}+2b_{2})/2b_{1}
. Notice that
\beta>1
and
\beta<1
correspond to types (3) and (4), respectively. In particular, in case (4), the components are smooth manifolds. Thus, if
a_{2}=1
,
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is a smooth manifold or a
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
-horn and it is normally embedded.

Let us consider the case
a_{2}\geq 2
for surfaces of type (4), in which the tangent cone of each component is 2-dimensional. Let
\lambda_{i}x_{2}=y_{2}
for
i=1,2
, where
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
. Consider the following curves in distinct components of
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}\setminus\{0\}
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}:y_{1}^{2b_{1}}-x_{2}^{a_{2}+2b_{2}}\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}:y_{1}^{2b_{1}}-x_{2}^{a_{2}+2b_{2}}\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=0,

where
\xi_{i}=\operatorname{Re}\left(1+\textbf{i}\lambda_{i}\right)^{a_{2}}\|1+\textbf{i}\lambda_{i}\|^{2b_{2}}
. We may parametrize these curves by:

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=(0,t,\tilde{\xi}_{1}t^{\frac{1}{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}},\lambda_{1}\tilde{\xi}_{1}t^{\frac{1}{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=(0,t,\tilde{\xi}_{2}t^{\frac{1}{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}},\lambda_{2}\tilde{\xi}_{2}t^{\frac{1}{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}}),

where
\tilde{\xi}_{i}=\xi_{i}^{-1/a_{2}+2b_{2}}
. Notice that if
a_{2}
is even, then
\xi_{i}>0
and the expressions above are well-defined. Any path
\alpha_{t}(s)
connecting the points
\gamma_{1}(t)
and
\gamma_{2}(t)
in these curves passes through the origin. Moreover, one has that the order of
\|\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{2}(t)\|
is
1/\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
and the order of
\|\gamma_{1}(t)\|+\|\gamma_{2}(t)\|
is
1
. Since
\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}<1
, the following limit diverges, which implies that
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is not Lipschitz normally embedded:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d_{i}(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))}{\|\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{2}(t)\|}\geq\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\ell(\alpha_{t})}{\|\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{2}(t)\|}\geq\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\|\gamma_{1}(t)\|+\|\gamma_{2}(t)\|}{\|\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{2}(t)\|},

where
\ell(\alpha)
denotes the length of
\alpha
.

For surfaces of type (3), all components have one-dimensional tangent cones that intersect only at the origin. We need to restrict to the case of distinct components
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. We claim that there exists
C>0
such that
\|x\|+\|y\|\leq C\|x-y\|
for all
x\in S_{1}
and
y\in S_{2}
. On the contrary, there exist sequences
(p_{k})\in S_{1}
and
(q_{k})\in S_{2}
such that
\lim_{k}p_{k}=\lim_{k}q_{k}=0
and

\displaystyle\|p_{k}\|+\|q_{k}\|>c_{k}\|p_{k}-q_{k}\|,

where
c_{k}>0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lim_{k}c_{k}=\infty
. Let
t_{k}=\|p_{k}\|+\|q_{k}\|
and define
x_{k}=\frac{1}{t_{k}}p_{k}
and
y_{k}=\frac{1}{t_{k}}q_{k}
. Since these sequences are bounded, up to pass to a subsequence, there exist
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in the tangent cones of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, respectively, such that
\lim_{k}x_{k}=v_{1}
and
\lim_{k}y_{k}=v_{2}
. Moreover,
\|v_{1}\|+\|v_{2}\|=1
and
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are not both vanishing. On the other hand,

\displaystyle v_{1}-v_{2}=\lim_{k}\frac{x_{k}-y_{k}}{t_{k}}<\lim_{k}\frac{1}{c_{k}}=0,

which contradicts the fact that the tangent cones of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
intersect only at the origin. Let
\gamma_{i}\subset S_{i}
be analytic curves in distinct components, for

. In polar coordinates, we may parametrize these paths as follows:

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(t)=\left(r_{i}(t)^{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}e^{\textbf{i}\theta_{i}(t)},r_{i}(t)e^{\frac{\textbf{i}2\pi l_{i}}{a_{2}}}\right),

for
i=1,2
and
l_{1}\neq l_{2}
. For a fixed

\alpha_{i,t}

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,t}(s)=\left(s^{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}e^{\textbf{i}\theta_{i}(t)},se^{\frac{\textbf{i}2\pi l_{i}}{a_{2}}}\right),

where

for

\ell(\alpha_{i,t})=r_{i}(t)+o(r_{i}(t))
, where

denotes terms of higher order in

and

have the same order in

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d_{i}(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t))}{\|\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{2}(t)\|}\leq\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\ell(\alpha_{1,t})+\ell(\alpha_{2,t})}{\|\gamma_{1}(t)\|+\|\gamma_{2}(t)\|}\cdot\frac{\|\gamma_{1}(t)\|+\|\gamma_{2}(t)\|}{\|\gamma_{1}(t)-\gamma_{2}(t)\|}\leq C^{\prime},

for some
C^{\prime}>0
. This implies that the inner and outer distances have the same order, and the result follows from the arc criterion.

For mixed surfaces of type (1), up to the coordinate linear change given by
w_{2}=\xi z_{2}
, where
\xi^{a_{2}}=-1

Proposition 6.6
.

Under the hypothesis on surfaces of type (1) in Proposition
6.3
, the mixed Pham-Brieskorn surfaces of types (1) and (3) are preserved under outer bi-Lipschitz equivalence.

Proof.

Let

be mixed Pham-Brieskorn surfaces, where
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
is of type (1). If

Corollary 6.7

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}
and

be two mixed Pham-Brieskorn surfaces of type (1) such that

The proof is based on the argument of
[
17

. Let

and define the following branches in

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(r)=\left(r^{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}},re^{i\frac{\pi}{a_{2}}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma_{2}(r)=\left(r^{\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}}}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{a_{1}}},re^{i\frac{\pi}{a_{2}}}\right).

Next, we look at the inner distance of these arcs. Let
\lambda(t)
be a minimizing path connecting
\gamma_{1}(r)
and
\gamma_{2}(r)

. Therefore, the limit

(6.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{d_{i}(\gamma_{1}(r),\gamma_{2}(r))}{r}\geq 2.

Suppose the existence of such a bi-Lipschitz map
\phi:(X_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}},0)\longrightarrow(X_{\textbf{c},\textbf{d}},0)
. It follows that we may parametrize the images of

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(r)=\left(r^{\mu_{\textbf{c},\textbf{d}}}e^{i\frac{\omega_{k}(r)c_{2}-\pi}{c_{1}}},re^{i\omega_{k}(r)}\right)\in\phi\left(\gamma_{k}\right)\cap\mathbb{S}^{3}_{r},

where

. The tangency order is preserved by
[
17

(6.2)

\displaystyle\text{tord}_{r}\|\sigma_{1}(r)-\sigma_{2}(r)\|=\mu_{\textbf{a},\textbf{b}},

where

\lim_{r\to 0}\omega_{1}(r)-\omega_{2}(r)=2\pi l
for some integer

such that

. Otherwise, we get

\displaystyle\text{tord}_{r}\|\sigma_{1}(r)-\sigma_{2}(r)\|=1,\quad

\displaystyle\text{if}\;\lim_{r\to 0}\omega_{1}(r)-\omega_{2}(r)\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\text{tord}_{r}\|\sigma_{1}(r)-\sigma_{2}(r)\|\leq\mu_{\textbf{c},\textbf{d}},\quad

\displaystyle\text{if}\;\lim_{r\to 0}\omega_{1}(r)-\omega_{2}(r)=2\pi l\;\;\text{for}\;\;lc_{2}\not\equiv 0\mod c_{1},

which is not possible by (

. In this case, up to a reparametrization of the curves

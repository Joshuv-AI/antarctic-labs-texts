Optimal Rates for Ergodic SDEs Driven by Multiplicative α \alpha -Stable Processes in Wasserstein-1 distance
Xinghu Jin, Xiaolong Zhang ∗

Abstract




Abstract:

This paper establishes the quantitative stability of invariant measures
\mu_{\alpha}
for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued ergodic stochastic differential equations driven by rotationally invariant multiplicative
\alpha
-stable processes with
\alpha\in(1,2]
. Under structural assumptions on the coefficients with a fixed parameter vector
\bm{\theta}
, we derive optimal convergence rates in the Wasserstein-
1
(
{\mathcal{W}}_{1}
) distance between the invariant measures introduced above, namely,

(i)

For any interval
[\alpha_{0},\vartheta_{0}]\subset(1,2)
, there exists
C_{1}=C(\alpha_{0},\vartheta_{0},\bm{\theta},d)>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu_{\alpha},\mu_{\vartheta})\leqslant C_{1}|\alpha-\vartheta|,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall\alpha,\vartheta\in[\alpha_{0},\vartheta_{0}].

(ii)

For any
\alpha_{0}\in(1,2)
, there exists
C_{2}=C(\alpha_{0},\bm{\theta})>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{1}(\mu_{\alpha},\mu_{2})\leqslant C_{2}\,d(2-\alpha),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},2).

The optimality of these rates is rigorously verified by explicit calculations for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck systems in
[
12
]
. It is worth emphasizing that
[
12
]
addressed only case (ii) under additive noise, whereas our analysis establishes results for both cases (i) and (ii) under multiplicative
\alpha
-stable noise, employing fundamentally different analytical methods.

Keywords
: Ergodic SDE;
\alpha
-stable process; Invariant measure; Wasserstein-
1
distance; Optimal rate.

Introduction

We investigate the following two prototypical stochastic differential equations (SDEs):

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(X_{t-}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}L^{\scriptscriptstyle\!(\alpha)}_{t},\hskip 9.24994ptX_{0}=x,

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y_{t}=b(Y_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma(Y_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{t},\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt\!Y_{0}=x,

where the coefficient pair
(b,\sigma)
satisfies
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The driving noise processes consist of
(L^{\scriptscriptstyle\!(\alpha)}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, a rotationally invariant
\alpha
-stable Lévy process with
\alpha\in(1,2)
characterized by its Fourier transform

\mathbb{E}\,e^{i\langle z,L^{\scriptscriptstyle\!(\alpha)}_{t}\rangle}=e^{-t|z|^{\alpha}/2}

for any
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
and
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, a standard Brownian motion. The analysis of SDEs driven by Lévy processes has become increasingly important across various disciplines (see
[
2
,
8
,
34
]
). Under certain regularity and dissipativity conditions, it is well-known that SDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) admit unique (weak or strong) solutions
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
and
(Y_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, respectively, and possess unique invariant measures denoted by
\mu_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{2}
(see
[
38
,
42
]
). On the other hand, the continuity theorem guarantees that, for any fixed
t>0
the
\alpha
-stable process
L^{\scriptscriptstyle\!(\alpha)}_{t}
converges weakly to the Brownian motion
B_{t}
as
\alpha\uparrow 2
(see
[
13
, Theorem 3.3.17]
). This naturally raises two fundamental questions regarding SDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
):

(1)

For fixed
t>0
, does
X_{t}
converge weakly to
Y_{t}
as
\alpha\uparrow 2
?

(2)

If the answer to (1) is affirmative, does
\mu_{\alpha}
converge weakly to
\mu_{2}
as
\alpha\uparrow 2
?

In this paper, we provide a complete resolution to these questions, which are fundamentally motivated by at least three interconnected research domains.

Fundamental results of such SDEs, including well-posedness, Markov semigroup regularity, and ergodicity, have been extensively studied. We summarize some key contributions below.
(i) For well-posedness of SDEs
: Applebaum
[
1
]
established strong existence and uniqueness for SDEs with jump processes or Brownian motion;
Stroock
[
33
]
pioneered the well-posedness of martingale problems for Lévy-type generators, and Lepeltier and Marchal
[
19
]
further examined its relationship to weak solutions;
Chen and Wang
[
10

analyzed weak solutions for
\alpha
-stable processes with drifts in Kato classes, and Zhang
[
41

proved pathwise uniqueness for symmetric
\alpha
-stable SDEs with bounded drifts in fractional Sobolev spaces.
(ii) For regularity of Markov semigroups
: Wang, Xu and Zhang
[
39
]
obtained the Bismut–Elworthy–Li type derivative formulas and gradient estimates for SDEs driven by multiplicative rotationally invariant
\alpha
-stable processes via finite-jump approximations; Chen, Hao and Zhang
[

proved gradient estimates for cylindrical
\alpha
-stable processes using Littlewood–Paley theory.
Chen, Xu, Zhang and Zhang
[
7

obtained

-regularity (
s>\alpha
) for the Markov semigroup of SDEs driven by multiplicative rotationally invariant
\alpha
-stable processes.
(iii) For the exponential ergodicity of SDEs
: there are at least three well-developed
methods for studying the exponential ergodicity of a Markov process defined through an SDE, namely, coupling method (see
[
4
,
14

20

Despite theoretical progress, numerical approximation, such as Euler-Maruyama (EM) schemes, of stochastic systems remains challenging. On the other hand, for the one-dimensional case,
[
29
]
established that the classical Langevin Monte Carlo diffusion with potential
U(x)=|x|^{\gamma}
is exponentially ergodic if and only if
\gamma\geqslant 1
. Consequently, it implies that continuous Langevin diffusions are unsuitable for simulating heavy-tailed distributions, necessitating
\alpha
-stable driven systems. Recent developments in numerical error quantification employ two principal methodologies:

(i) For Lindeberg principle techniques
: Pagés and Panloup
[

applied this principle to analyze Wasserstein-
1

[

[

employed this approach for additive
\alpha
-stable SDEs.
(ii) For Stein’s method
: Gurvich
[

[

[

The study of parametric dependence SDEs constitutes a fundamental problem in stochastic analysis and statistical physics
[
3
,
26

31
]

[

established a general diffusion approximation for fully coupled multiscale SDEs, and derived four distinct averaged equations corresponding to different orders of small parameters. Consider an underdamped Langevin process

with friction coefficient
\gamma>0

\gamma
is tuned. Recently, Deng, Schilling and Xu
[

\alpha
-stable SDEs as

In this paper, we extend the analysis of
[

\alpha
-stable SDEs as

1.1
) and (

) by the coupling method (see

(1.3)

\displaystyle\langle b(x),x\rangle\leqslant-c_{0}|x|^{2}+c_{1},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

it is well-known that both SDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are exponentially ergodic (see
[
40
,
42
]
). We denote by
\mu_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{2}
the invariant measures of SDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), respectively. However, in this paper, stronger dissipative assumptions than (
1.3

1.2
). We will elaborate on
\vartheta=2
and
\vartheta\in(1,2)
two cases in detail. For
\vartheta=2
, we impose the following dissipativity condition:

(DC)

Let
\tilde{\sigma}:=(\sigma\sigma^{*}-\sigma_{0}^{2}\mathbb{I})^{1/2}
with sufficiently small
\sigma_{0}>0
. There exist
c_{0},c_{1}>0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle 2\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle+\|\tilde{\sigma}(x)-\tilde{\sigma}(y)\|_{\rm HS}^{2}-\frac{|(x-y)^{*}(\tilde{\sigma}(x)-\tilde{\sigma}(y))|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\leqslant-c_{0}|x-y|^{2}+c_{1}.

Under
(DC)
, the exponential contraction of SDE (
1.2
) in Wasserstein-1 distance depends on
c_{0}
and

, implying the convergence rate dominated by
O(d(2-\alpha))
(see Theorem
1.2
(1)). For
\vartheta\in(1,2)
, we fix
\alpha_{0},\vartheta_{0}\in(1,2)
such that
\alpha,\vartheta\in[\alpha_{0},\vartheta_{0}]
and assume the following weaker dissipativity condition:

(DC^{\prime})

There exist
c_{0},c_{1}>0
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\langle x-y,b(x)-b(y)\rangle\leqslant-c_{0}|x-y|^{2}+c_{1}.

Under
(DC^{\prime})
, the exponential contraction of SDE (
1.1
) in Wasserstein-1 distance depends on
c_{0},c_{1},d,\alpha_{0},\vartheta_{0}
, yielding a convergence rate dominated by
O(|\vartheta-\alpha|)
(see Theorem
1.2
(2)).
Although exponential contraction for SDE (
1.1
) can also be established under weaker assumptions by Meyn-Tweedie’s method from
[
42
]
, such approach fails to provide explicit parameter dependence. Thus, we use the dissipative condition

The coefficients

(A1)

(Uniform Ellipticity) For any

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-1}\leqslant|\sigma(x)y|^{2}\leqslant c_{2},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(A2)

2.1.
Preliminaries

Under Assumption
1.1
, SDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with arbitrary initial value

each admit unique strong solutions
(X^{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(Y^{x}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

(2.1)

\displaystyle P^{\alpha}_{t}f(x):={\mathbb{E}}f(X^{x}_{t}),\hskip 18.49988ptQ_{t}f(x):={\mathbb{E}}f(Y^{x}_{t}),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

A straightforward application of Itô’s formula reveals that their infinitesimal generators take the form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\!\alpha}f(x):=\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}_{\sigma}f(x)+\langle b(x),\nabla f(x)\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle Q}f(x):=\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla^{2}f(x),(\sigma\sigma^{*})(x)\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}+\langle b(x),\nabla f(x)\rangle,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}_{\sigma}f(x)=c_{d,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\delta_{\sigma(x)z}f(x)-\nabla_{\sigma(x)z}f(x)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z,\ \text{where}\ c_{d,\alpha}:=\frac{\alpha 2^{\alpha-2}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+\alpha}{2}\right)}{\pi^{d/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{2-\alpha}{2}\right)}.

Notably, it holds that
\Delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f=2\mathcal{L}^{\alpha}_{\mathbb{I}}f

denotes the identity matrix. The semigroup operators satisfy the Kolmogorov equations in both forward and backward formulations:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t}P^{\alpha}_{t}f=P^{\alpha}_{t}\mathcal{A}^{\!\alpha}f=\mathcal{A}^{\!\alpha}P^{\alpha}_{t}f,\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}}{{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t}Q_{t}f

\displaystyle=Q_{t}\mathcal{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle Q}f=\mathcal{A}^{\scriptscriptstyle Q}Q_{t}f.

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t}}\left\langle A_{s-}^{\ell^{\varepsilon},1},{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{\beta}_{\ell^{\varepsilon}_{s}}\right\rangle-\int^{t}_{\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t}}A_{s-}^{\ell^{\varepsilon},2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\beta_{\ell^{\varepsilon}_{s}}+\sum_{s\in(\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t},t]}\left\langle A_{s-}^{\ell^{\varepsilon},3}\Delta W^{\beta}_{\ell^{\varepsilon}_{s}},\Delta W_{\ell^{\varepsilon}_{s}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=:R_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)-R_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)+R_{3}^{\varepsilon}(t).

By Lemma
B.1
and the dominated convergence theorem, we have

(
\mathbb{B}
.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{L}^{v}_{\ell,t}-Q^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}]=0.

Denote by

K:=\sup_{i\leqslant 3,\,\varepsilon<1,\,t\leqslant T}\mathbb{E}[|A_{t}^{\ell^{\varepsilon},i}|^{2}].

For
R_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)
, as
\beta_{t}=t\wedge\ell_{\tau_{\ell}}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|R_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\big]\leqslant K\sum_{s\in(\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t},t]}\int^{\ell^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{\ell^{\varepsilon}_{s-}}\dot{\beta}_{r}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\beta_{r}\leqslant K(\ell^{\varepsilon}_{t}\wedge\ell_{\tau_{\ell}}-\ell^{\varepsilon}_{\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t}}\wedge\ell_{\tau_{\ell}})\leqslant K(\ell_{\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t}}-\ell^{\varepsilon}_{\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t}}).

Noting that
\ell^{\varepsilon}_{t}\uparrow\ell_{t}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,
then we have

(
\mathbb{B}
.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}\big[|R_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\big]\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{t\in(\tau_{\ell},T]}K(\ell_{\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t}}-\ell^{\varepsilon}_{\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t}})\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}K(\ell_{\tau_{\ell}}-\ell^{\varepsilon}_{\tau_{\ell}})=0.

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\left|R_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)\right|\big]=0.

For
R_{3}^{\varepsilon}(t)
, by Minkovski’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|R_{3}^{\varepsilon}(t)|]\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in(\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t},t]}\big(\mathbb{E}[|A_{s-}^{\ell^{\varepsilon},3}\Delta W^{\beta}_{\ell_{s}}|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\mathbb{E}[|\Delta W_{\ell_{s}}|^{2}]\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|R_{3}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\leqslant

\displaystyle CK^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{s\in(\bm{\tau}^{\ell}_{t},t]}\left(\int^{\ell^{\varepsilon}_{s}}_{\ell^{\varepsilon}_{s-}}|\dot{\beta}_{r}|^{2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Delta\ell_{s})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Repeating the procedure of
R_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\mathbb{E}}[|R_{3}^{\varepsilon}(t)|]=0.

Combining the above calculations, we have

(
\mathbb{B}
.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}{\mathbb{E}}\left|R^{\varepsilon}(t)\right|\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}{\mathbb{E}}\left[|R_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)|+|R_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)|+|R_{3}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\right]=0.

On the other hand, by (i) of Lemma
3.2
, it is easy to get

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathbf{L}^{v}_{\ell,t}|^{p}+|\delta(h)^{\ell^{\varepsilon}}_{t}|^{p}\right]<\infty.

Then by (

B

.15
) and (

B

.17
), using the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain (

B

.14
).

Calabi-Yau complete intersections in fake weighted projective spaces
Marco Ghirlanda

Abstract
. We present a classification algorithm for Calabi-Yau complete intersections arising from nef-partitions in fake weighted projective spaces, allowing us to determine all such complete intersections up to dimension five. Furthermore, we compute the Hodge pairs of the 3 3 -dimensional families obtained, and find twenty new Hodge pairs not realized by any toric Calabi-Yau hypersurface. Finally, we provide an explicit characterization for the families of maximal codimension.



1.
Introduction

The study of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces in the toric setting originates in Batyrev’s work
[
1
]
. With each reflexive polytope one can naturally associate a Gorenstein toric Fano variety hosting a family of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces. Batyrev’s construction identifies dual reflexive polytopes with mirror pairs of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces. This viewpoint led to large-scale classifications of reflexive polytopes: Kreuzer and Skarke provided the complete list of reflexive polytopes in dimensions three
[
8
]
and four
[
9
]
. More recently, we developed in
[
4
]
a classification algorithm for reflexive simplices and explicitly determined them up to dimension six. The resulting dataset is available on Zenodo
[
5
]
.

A natural next step is to pass from hypersurfaces to complete intersections. In the toric framework,
Borisov introduced the notion of
nef-partition
, namely a decomposition of the anticanonical class
into nef Cartier summands
[
2
]
. Such a decomposition determines line bundles on the ambient toric
Fano variety. Intersecting general sections of each line bundle produces a family of Calabi-Yau complete intersections.

In this note we provide a classification algorithm for complete intersections arising from nef-partitions
in
fake weighted projective spaces
(fwps), i.e.
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial toric Fano varieties of Picard number one.
Such varieties admit an explicit combinatorial description in terms of a
degree matrix
, see
Section
2
. In these terms, a nef-partition is encoded by a partition of the columns
into blocks, with the property that the column-sum of each block represents a Cartier divisor class.
We obtain in particular the following classification, available on Zenodo
[

]

Our classification algorithm relies on a new combinatorial formulation of the existence condition for nef-partitions, which separates the constraints coming from the free and torsion parts of the class group, see Proposition
3.1
;
in particular, the conditions on the torsion coordinates can be verified row-wise. This yields a two-stage enumeration:

3.3

[

2.
Preliminaries

We first recall from
[
7
]
*Sec. 2 the basic concepts on
fake weighted projective space (fwps)
, that means
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial toric Fano varieties of Picard number one. The fan of a fwps of dimension
n
is spanned by the faces of an
n
-dimensional Fano simplex
\Delta
with vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The fwps
Z
is then encoded by the
n\times(n+1)
integral matrix

P=\begin{bmatrix}v_{0}&\dots&v_{n}\end{bmatrix}.

The divisor class group of
Z
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}/\im(P^{*})
, where
P^{*}
denotes the transpose of
P
. We present
\Cl(Z)
in
invariant factor form
:

\Cl(Z)=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{r}\mathbb{Z},

that means
\mu_{r}\mid\mu_{r-1}\mid\dots\mid\mu_{1}
.
Consider the projection
Q\colon\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\rightarrow\Cl(Z)
, and let
\omega_{i}=(w_{i},\eta_{i})\coloneqq Q(e_{i})
, where
1\leq w_{0}\leq\dots\leq w_{n}
and
\eta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{r}\mathbb{Z}
. We view
Q
as a
degree matrix in

\Cl(Z)
, that means

Q=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}&\dots&\omega_{n}\end{bmatrix}

where any
n
of the
\omega_{i}
generate
\Cl(Z)
as a group.
One can directly gain
Z
as a quotient of
\mathbb{K}^{n+1}
by the diagonal action of
H=\mathbb{K}^{*}\times F
with weights
\omega_{i}
, where
F
is finite and
H
is the quasitorus with character group
\Cl(Z)
.

In
[
4
]
, we obtained the following explicit description of the Picard group of a fwps in terms of its degree matrix:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
Z
be a fwps with
\Cl(Z)=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{r}\mathbb{Z}
and degree matrix
Q=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}&\dots&\omega_{n}\end{bmatrix}
, where
\omega_{i}=(w_{i},\eta_{i})
.
Let
0\leq\eta_{ij}^{\prime}<\mu_{j}
be the integer representing
\eta_{ij}
and set

\begin{array}[]{lcll}L&\coloneqq&\lcm(w_{0},\dots,w_{n}),\\[6.0pt]
M_{i}&\coloneqq&\dfrac{\mu_{i}}{\gcd\left(\mu_{i},\dfrac{L}{w_{0}}\eta_{0j}^{\prime},\dots,\dfrac{L}{w_{n}}\eta_{nj}^{\prime}\right)},\quad 1\leq i\leq r,\\[24.0pt]
M&\coloneqq&\lcm(M_{1},\dots,M_{r}).\end{array}

Then the Picard group of
Z
is the subgroup of
\Cl(Z)
generated by the element
(LM,0)
.

In
[
2
]
, Borisov introduced nef-partitions to construct families of Calabi-Yau complete intersections in toric varieties. We reformulate his definition in terms of degree matrices for the simplex case.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
Z
be a fwps with degree matrix
Q=[\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{n}]
. A
nef-partition
of
Z
is a partition
p_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup p_{s}=\{0,\dots,n\}
such that

\omega_{p_{j}}\coloneq\sum_{i\in p_{j}}\omega_{i}\in\Pic(Z),\hskip 5.0ptj=1,\dots,s.

The
Calabi-Yau complete intersection (CYCI)
arising from such a partition is the family of complete intersections of general sections of
\omega_{p_{1}},\dots,\omega_{p_{s}}
in
Z
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
\omega_{p_{j}}=\omega_{i}
for some indices
i
and
j
, then a general section of
\omega_{p_{j}}
contains a monomial supported on the single
variable corresponding to
\omega_{i}
.
One can then eliminate this variable and the corresponding equation, obtaining an
isomorphic Calabi–Yau complete intersection in a fwps of dimension
d-1
and codimension

s-1
. Therefore, going forward we will restrict Definition
2.2
to nef-partitions for which

\omega_{p_{j}}\neq\omega_{i}
for all
j
and all
i
, excluding in particular any partition having a
block of size one.

3.
Classification of CYCI in fwps

By Proposition
2.1
and Definition
2.2
we have immediately the following characterization of nef-partitions in fwps:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
Z
be a fwps with
\Cl(Z)=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{r}\mathbb{Z}
and degree matrix
Q=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}&\dots&\omega_{n}\end{bmatrix}
. Then
p_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup p_{s}=\{0,\dots,n\}
is a nef-partition if and only if, with the notation of Proposition
2.1
, we have

\begin{cases}L&|\ \ \sum_{i\in p_{j}}w_{i},\quad j=1,\dots,s,\\[3.0pt]
M_{k}&|\ \ \dfrac{\sum_{i\in p_{j}}w_{i}}{L},\quad j=1,\dots,s,\ k=1,\dots,r,\\[5.0pt]
\sum_{i\in p_{j}}\eta_{ik}&=\ \ 0\in\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{k}\mathbb{Z},\quad j=1,\dots,s,\ k=1,\dots,r.\end{cases}

As for
[
4
]
*Proposition 4.1, the conditions of Proposition
3.1
can be checked independently on each torsion row. Accordingly, we adapt the method of
[
4
, Section 4]
to our setting, generalizing the relevant definitions and procedures.

Definition 3.2
.

We call
w=[w_{0},\dots,w_{n}]\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{\geq 1}
an
s
-nef weight vector
if
w
is the degree matrix of a fwps
Z
with
\Cl(Z)=\mathbb{Z}
and
Z
admits a nef-partition
p_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup p_{s}=\{0,\dots,n\}
. We write
p(s,w)
for the set of all such nef-partitions of
Z
, and call the pair
(w,p(s,w))
an
s
-nef weight pair
.

Procedure 3.3
.

Input:
positive integers
n
and
s
.

Procedure:

(1)

For
k=2,\dots,\left\lfloor\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor
, compute the finite set
\mathcal{W}_{k}
of all primitive non-decreasing
k
-tuples
(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{k}
satisfying
\lcm_{i}(w_{i})\mid\sum_{i}w_{i}
, see for instance
[
1
]
*Algorithm 5.6;

(2)

Compute the set
\mathcal{D}
of all non-increasing
s
-tuples
(d_{1},\dots,d_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}^{s}
satisfying
n+1=\sum_{i}d_{i}
;

(3)

For every
(d_{1},\dots,d_{s})\in\mathcal{D}
, form the set
\mathcal{W}
of all
s
-tuples
(W_{1},\dots,W_{s})
, where
W_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{d_{i}}
and for every
i
either
d_{i}>d_{i+1}
or
W_{i}\geq_{lex}W_{i+1}
;

(4)

For every
(W_{1},\dots,W_{s})\in\mathcal{W}
, denote with
L_{i}
,
S_{i}
and
m_{i}
respectively the least common multiple, the sum and the maximum of the coordinates of
W_{i}
. Then compute the set
\mathcal{C}(W_{1},\dots,W_{s})
of all primitive
s
-tuples
(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{s}
such that:

(a)

c_{1}\mid\lcm(S_{2},\dots,S_{s})
;

(b)

c_{i}L_{i}\mid c_{j}S_{j}
for all
i,j=1,\dots,s
;

(c)

c_{i}S_{i}>c_{j}m_{j}
for all
i,j=1,\dots,s
.

(5)

For every
(W_{1},\dots,W_{s})\in\mathcal{W}
and
(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})\in\mathcal{C}(W_{1},\dots,W_{s})
, let
w
be the concatenation of
c_{1}W_{1},\dots,c_{s}W_{s}
, reordered non-decreasingly. Then store
w
together with the partition
p_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup p_{s}=\{0,\dots,n\}
, where
p_{i}
is the subset of indices corresponding to the elements of
c_{i}W_{i}
;

(6)

Collect all partitions sharing the same
w
in a set
q(s,w)
, then return all pairs
(w,q(s,w))
.

Output
: the set of all
s
-nef weight pairs
(w,p(s,w))
with
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{\geq 1}
.

Proof.

Let
w=(w_{0},\dots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{n+1}
be an
s
-nef weight vector. By definition
w
is primitive, and there exists a nef-partition

p_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup p_{s}=\{0,\dots,n\}
.
Reorder the parts so that
|p_{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|p_{s}|
, and set
d_{i}:=|p_{i}|
. By Remark
2.3
we have
d_{i}\geq 2
for all
i
, and hence
d_{1}\leq\frac{n+1}{2}
.
For each
i
, let
\widetilde{W}_{i}
be the nondecreasing list of the weights
\{w_{j}:j\in p_{i}\}
and define

c_{i}:=\gcd(\widetilde{W}_{i}),\qquad W_{i}:=\widetilde{W}_{i}/c_{i}.

Then
W_{i}
is nondecreasing and primitive. Denote by

L:=\lcm(w_{0},\dots,w_{n}),\qquad L_{i}:=\lcm(W_{i}),\qquad S_{i}:=\sum_{v\in W_{i}}v.

Since
\lcm(c_{i}W_{i})=c_{i}\lcm(W_{i})=c_{i}L_{i}
, we have
c_{i}L_{i}\mid L
for all
i
.
Moreover, Proposition
3.1
gives

L\ \mid\ \sum_{j\in p_{i}}w_{j}\ =\ c_{i}S_{i}\qquad\text{for each }i.

It follows that
c_{i}L_{i}\mid c_{i}S_{i}
, hence
L_{i}\mid S_{i}
. Therefore
W_{i}\in\mathcal{W}_{d_{i}}
for every
i
.
The tuple
(d_{1},\dots,d_{s})
is an element of
\mathcal{D}
, and after possibly reordering blocks of equal length we may assume the lexicographical ordering of Step
3
. Next, for all
i,j
we have
c_{i}L_{i}\mid L
and
L\mid c_{j}S_{j}
, hence condition (b) of Step
4
. Condition (c) follows directly by Remark
2.3
.
Furthermore, since
w
and
W_{i}
are primitive,
(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
is also primitive.
Finally, let
p^{m}
be a prime power dividing
c_{1}
.
By primitivity of
(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
there exists
j\geq 2
such that
p^{m}\nmid c_{j}
.
Since
c_{1}L_{1}\mid c_{j}S_{j}
, we have
p^{m}\mid c_{j}S_{j}
, hence
p^{m}\mid S_{j}
.
Thus
c_{1}\mid\lcm(S_{2},\dots,S_{s})
, and we conclude that
(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})\in\mathcal{C}(W_{1},\dots,W_{s})
.
Consequently, the procedure enumerates the data
(d_{1},\dots,d_{s})
,
(W_{1},\dots,W_{s})
and
(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})
, and Step
5
reconstructs
w
and its nef-partitions.

Conversely, consider any output pair
(w,q(s,w))
coming from blocks
c_{i}W_{i}
. Then

L\ =\ \lcm\bigl(\,\lcm(c_{1}W_{1}),\dots,\lcm(c_{s}W_{s})\,\bigr)\ =\ \lcm(c_{1}L_{1},\dots,c_{s}L_{s}).

By condition (b) of Step
4
we have
c_{i}L_{i}\mid c_{j}S_{j}
for all
i,j
, hence
L\mid c_{j}S_{j}
for each
j
. Thus each partition in
q(s,w)
is a nef-partition and
w
is an
s
-nef weight vector.
∎

4.
CYCI of maximal codimension

Complete intersections of maximal codimension exhibit many symmetries, which significantly slow down the isomorphism filtering in Step
4
of Algorithm
3.7
. However, we can use these symmetries to provide an explicit characterization that renders their classification more tractable.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
Z
be a fwps of dimension
n
with
\Cl(Z)=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\mu_{r}\mathbb{Z}
admitting a nef-partition
p_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup p_{s}=\{0,\dots,n\}
. Then
2s\leq n+1
. Furthermore, a fwps
Z
of dimension
2s-1
admits such a nef partition if and only if
\mu_{1}=\dots=\mu_{r}=2
and
Z
admits a degree matrix of the form

Q=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{s}&\mathbbm{1}_{s}\\
A&A\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbbm{1}_{s}=[1,\dots,1],

where
A
is an
r\times s
matrix in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
whose columns affinely span the whole
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r}
.

Proof.

First, notice that, if
2s>n+1
, at least one of the blocks of the nef-partition would have size one, contradicting Remark
2.3
.

Now assume that
n=2s-1
. Then each block of the nef-partition has size two.
Let
\omega_{i}=(w_{i},\eta_{i})
and
\omega_{j}=(w_{j},\eta_{j})
be two weights forming a block.
By Proposition
3.1
, we have
L\mid w_{i}+w_{j}
.
By definition we have
w_{i}
,
w_{j}\leq L
. Therefore, either
w_{i}=w_{j}=L
or
w_{i}+w_{j}=L
. In the latter case we have
w_{i}\mid L-w_{i}=w_{j}
and viceversa, implying
w_{i}=w_{j}=\frac{L}{2}
.
If all pairs
(w_{i},w_{j})
were of the type
\left(\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}\right)
, the lowest common multiple of the weights would also be
\frac{L}{2}
, contradicting the definition of
L
. Hence, at least one pair is of the type
(L,L)
. Also, since
\gcd(w_{0},\dots,w_{s})=1
, we have that either
L=1
or
L=2
and at least a pair has the form
(1,1)
. In the latter case, the sum of the pair would coincide with one of the other
w_{i}
, contradicting Remark
2.3
. Hence, we conclude that
w_{i}=1
for all
i
.

By Proposition
3.1
, it follows that
\mu_{i}=2
for all
i
, and each block of the nef-partition is of the form
(\omega_{i},\omega_{i})
with
\omega_{i}=(1,\eta_{i})
. After appropriately permuting the columns of the degree matrix, we can therefore reach the form

Q=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{s}&\mathbbm{1}_{s}\\
A&A\end{bmatrix}.

We conclude by noticing that the almost freeness of
Q
is equivalent to the condition that the columns of
A
span the whole
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r}
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
P(d)
be the set of all multisets of
d+1
points (counted with multiplicity) in
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r}
that affinely span the whole space.
Then, the
d
-dimensional
CYCI
arising from a nef partition of codimension
d+1
in a fwps are in bijection with the orbits of
P(d)
under the action of the group
\operatorname{AGL}(r,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, described via the affine transformation:

\alpha:\operatorname{AGL}(r,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r}\to(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r},\quad\big((v,M),x\big)\mapsto Mx+v.

Proof.

Let
Z
be the
(2d+1)
-dimensional ambient fwps for such a CYCI.
By Proposition
4.1
,
Z
admits a degree matrix

Q=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{s}&\mathbbm{1}_{s}\\
A&A\end{bmatrix},

where
A
is an
r\times s
matrix in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
whose column span is the whole
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r}
. The columns of
A
then form an element
P_{A}
of
P(d)
, and viceversa every element of
P(d)
yield such a matrix
A
by listing the points of the multiset as columns (with multiplicity).

By
[
4
]
*Theorem 2.1, the degree matrix
Q
yields isomorphic toric varieties up to performing elementary row operations on
A
, permuting its columns and adding
1
to all entries of a row. In the correspondence with
P_{A}
, this is equivalent respectively to applying elements of
\text{GL}(r,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
to the points of
P_{A}
, ignoring their order (automatic, since
P_{A}
is a multiset) and traslating them by a vector
w\in(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r}
.
∎

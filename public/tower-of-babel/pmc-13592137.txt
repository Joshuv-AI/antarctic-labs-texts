Preliminary Outcomes of iStent Inject vs Kahook Dual Blade Goniotomy Combined With Phacoemulsification: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Anna Charytonowicz, Małgorzata Chilmonczyk, Mateusz Zarzecki, Joanna Konopińska
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCanal-based minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (CB-MIGS) is increasingly used to reduce intraocular pressure (IOP) with a favorable safety profile in patients with mild-to-moderate glaucoma. This study assessed the safety and efficacy of phacoemulsification combined with iStent inject implantation (phaco-iStent) vs goniotomy using the Kahook Dual Blade (phaco-KDB) in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension.Material/MethodsThis prospective randomized trial included 68 eyes (35 phaco-iStent, 33 phaco-KDB) operated on by a single surgeon between 2024 and 2025. Surgical success was defined as IOP reduction ≥ 20% from baseline or IOP ≤ 15 mm Hg, with subcategories of complete and satisfactory success. Postoperative assessments were performed up to 12 months, with mean follow-up duration of 8 ± 2.4 months.ResultsBoth procedures led to a similar reduction in IOP (P = 0.355 at 6 months). Mean IOP decreased from 19.37 ± 4.03 mm Hg to 16.58 ± 3.64 mm Hg in the phaco-iStent group and from 18.15 ± 3.76 mm Hg to 15.46 ± 3.15 mm Hg in the phaco-KDB group (approximately 14% reduction in both groups). The mean number of glaucoma medications decreased from 1.29 ± 0.71 to 0.00 ± 0.00 in the phaco-iStent group and from 1.00 ± 0.61 to 0.00 ± 0.00 in the phaco-KDB group. Complete surgical success at 6 months was achieved in 68.8% of phaco-iStent eyes and 62.5% of phaco-KDB eyes. The most common adverse events were corneal edema and hyphema, which occurred more frequently in the phaco-KDB group; all resolved without additional surgical intervention.ConclusionsBoth the phaco-iStent and the phaco-KDB techniques significantly reduced IOP and medication burden with few postoperative complications. However, long-term outcomes should be interpreted with caution because of decreasing follow-up over time.

Introduction

Glaucoma is a leading cause of permanent vision loss worldwide. In this disorder, progressive damage to the optic nerve occurs most often as a consequence of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) [1], although other risk factors also contribute [2]. Open-angle glaucoma is the most prevalent type, and the primary treatment includes antiglaucoma medications, which lower the IOP. Typically, glaucoma surgery is the preferred choice of treatment when local therapy is unsuccessful or causes adverse events. Trabeculectomy remains the gold standard; however, it can cause many serious complications such as hypotony [3], choroidal hemorrhage [4], and visual function decline due to procedure-related astigmatism or deterioration in optical quality [5]. Accordingly, for patients with mild to moderate glaucoma, an increasing number of surgeons are choosing procedural safety and preservation of postoperative visual acuity by choosing minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS). Across multiple MIGS options, canal-based (CB) MIGS procedures that enhance aqueous humor drainage via the Schlemm’s canal have become increasingly favored. Although this approach has limited efficacy in substantially reducing IOP, it avoids conjunctival and scleral incisions when carried out via a clear corneal approach. As a result, it minimally affects procedure-related astigmatism or refractive results after surgery [5]. Overall, MIGS techniques offer a more favorable safety profile than classic filtration surgeries, even though their effectiveness is comparatively lower [4,5].

Characteristic structural changes occur in the trabecular meshwork (TM) in the course of glaucomatous neuropathy. The TM stiffens because of aging and apoptosis of cells, thickening of elastin fibers, actin cytoskeleton remodeling, elevated rigidity, and decreased TM cells [6]. Additionally, as TGF-β2 concentration increases, non-degradable fibers accumulate. These factors cause an increase in the aqueous humor resistance outflow. Conceptually, resection of the TM may enhance aqueous humor drainage; hence, certain practitioners concentrate on microsurgical removal of the TM and how its function is modified by scar formation. However, some surgeons prefer stenting and viscodilation of the canal. Two pathways of action can be adopted: communicating the anterior chamber with the Schlemm’s canal lumen via stenting or removal of the TM.

The iStent implant (Glaukos, San Clemente, CA, USA) created a pathway to reduce aqueous humor outflow resistance and form communication between the anterior chamber and Schlemm’s canal. The device has been approved for use in cataract surgery in eyes diagnosed with mild to moderate open-angle glaucoma [7]. Minimally invasive bypass has been singly implanted as a first-generation iStent since 2012 [8]. The second-generation iStent inject was developed a few years later and was designed for the implantation of a set of 2 stents. A titanium device coated with heparin, with a central channel and 4 lateral openings, was implanted in the nasal quadrant of the TM via an ab interno approach. This technique is performed in combination with phacoemulsification.

The Kahook Dual Blade (KDB; New World Medical Inc., Rancho Cucamonga, CA, USA) excises part of the TM to facilitate aqueous humor drainage. KDB goniotomy is a successful surgical option for glaucoma performed with phacoemulsification [9,10]. Special blades are used in goniotomy, to first remove a piece of TM and then to displace a strip from the anterior chamber angle. A specialized knife was designed to avoid potential damage to the surrounding tissues [11] Goniotomy is an implant-free procedure that removes approximately 100° of TM, providing direct access to Schlemm’s canal and multiple collector channel ostia, thereby lowering IOP.

The distribution of collector channels in Schlemm’s canal can vary. Hypothetically, most of these are located in the lower temporal quadrant, although there are few studies on this subject. In theory, goniotomy with KDB creates a wider opening of the ostia of collector channels on the circumference of the Schlemm’s canal in comparison to iStent inject implantation. However, Sato and Kawaji [12] revealed that the extent of opening the Schlemm’s canal does not influence the IOP level after CB-MIGS surgery. In this prospective randomized trial, we aimed to compare the clinical effectiveness and risk profile of iStent inject implantation and KDB goniotomy, both combined with phacoemulsification. Despite the existing literature, few studies have retrospectively compared the surgical outcomes of iStent inject with goniotomy and KDB intervention [11,13–16], with most investigating the older first-generation iStent [11,13–17], and only 1 study having a prospective randomized design [18]; moreover, none of them were performed on White Polish populations. This trial was conducted to assess the results of surgery of both procedures in individuals with open-angle glaucoma in terms of IOP and antiglaucoma medication reduction in the Polish population.

Material and Methods

This study was a prospective, randomized, single-center clinical trial of patients with cataract and glaucoma undergoing combined procedures: phacoemulsification and iStent Inject implantation or KDB goniotomy. This report presents preliminary outcomes of this trial. The trial protocol was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07325552), with the registration occurring after the initiation of patient enrollment; however, the study protocol and outcome measures were defined prior to data analysis. All participants were selected from the University Clinical Hospital in Białystok, Poland, between 2024 and 2025. A total of 92 patients were screened for eligibility. After exclusion of 24 patients, 68 were randomized to either the phaco-iStent or phaco-KDB group. The study flow and participant allocation are presented in Figure 1. This trial was approved by the Institutional Review Board (APK.002.174.2024). All procedures were performed in accordance with the ethical standards and principles outlined in the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its subsequent amendments. Each participant provided written informed consent. Surgical treatment was performed in patients who were qualified for elective cataract surgery and had vision-impairing cataract and concomitant open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension treated with 1 to 4 ocular hypotensive medications. Other eligibility parameters were patients aged 18 years or older, mild to moderate primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG), pseudoexfoliative glaucoma, or pigmentary glaucoma undergoing treatment with 1 to 4 medications, and no need for filtering glaucoma surgery at study enrollment. Glaucoma staging was based on characteristic glaucomatous optic disc changes identified by fundoscopic examination and corresponding visual field defects. Glaucomatous visual field loss was classified according to mean deviation (MD) on the Humphrey Visual Field Analyzer (Carl Zeiss AG, Germany) using the SITA Standard 24-2 algorithm: mild glaucoma (MD 0 to −6.0 dB) and moderate glaucoma (MD −6.01 to −12.0 dB). Participants were required to have best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) of no less than 20/200 in each eye, as measured by Snellen charts, and an open chamber angle with Shaffer grading of 3 to 4 in at least 2 quadrants. Only 1 eye per patient was included, specifically the eye with symptomatic cataract and visual acuity of less than 0.7.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: serious ocular disorder, such as diabetic neovascular retinopathy, corneal diseases (eg, Fuchs dystrophy), dry and exudative age-related macular degeneration, narrow or closed angles, history of glaucoma surgical procedures (selective laser trabeculoplasty within 90 days prior to planned surgery, filtration procedures, and cycloablation), MD parameter worse than −12.00 dB, use of more than 4 ocular hypotensive medications, and inability to provide written informed consent due to a comorbid condition.

Randomization was based on a computer-generated list with allocation concealment in place. Participants were randomized via sealed envelopes, which were opened on the day of surgery, to assign participants to their groups. Each eye was randomly assigned in a 1: 1 ratio to either the phaco-iStent or the phaco-KDB group. Only 1 eye from each patient was included in this study.

Main Outcome Measures

Baseline measurements were obtained from the qualification visit (day 0), and postoperative data at day 1, day 7, month 1, month 3, month 6, and up to 12 months. Owing to the ongoing nature of the study and its preliminary analysis, not all eyes reached each follow-up time point; therefore, the number of eyes included in the analysis decreased over time. Given the ongoing nature of the study and the variable duration of follow-up across participants, outcomes were evaluated at each available postoperative visit. The primary efficacy endpoint was the change in IOP from baseline to 6 months postoperatively. Analyses at other follow-up time points were considered secondary and exploratory. The mean follow-up duration across all included eyes was 8 ± 2.4 months. Prior to surgery, an ophthalmic examination was conducted, including an assessment of ophthalmic history, IOP measurements, BCVA, total number of glaucoma agents, density of endothelial cell count (ECC), optical coherence tomography (OCT) of the optic nerve head using the Heidelberg Spectralis HRA+OCT, and standard automated perimetry using the ZEISS Humphrey Field Analyzer 3. The IOP was monitored using Goldmann applanation tonometry. Visual field MD and retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness assessed by OCT were evaluated preoperatively and at postoperative follow-up visits (7 days, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, and up to 12 months), when available. Due to the preliminary nature of the study and variable follow-up duration, availability of RNFL and MD measurements differed across time points. RNFL thickness and visual field MD were evaluated descriptively at each available follow-up visit. No predefined criteria for glaucoma progression were applied, and no formal progression analysis was performed.

Surgical success was defined as IOP reduction of 20% or greater from baseline or IOP of 15 mm Hg or lower and was categorized as complete (no glaucoma medications) or satisfactory (≤ 2 medications). The use of an IOP threshold of 15 mm Hg or lower was intended to reflect a clinically meaningful target in mild-to-moderate glaucoma; however, we acknowledge that according to European Glaucoma Society recommendations, both absolute and relative IOP reduction criteria should be considered when reporting outcomes of glaucoma surgery trials. Success rates were calculated separately for each follow-up time point using the number of eyes available at that specific visit as the denominator. Overall success was defined as the sum of complete and satisfactory success at each follow-up time point.

Secondary outcomes included reduction in glaucoma medication burden. Safety outcomes included BCVA, MD, RNFL thickness, ECC, and intraoperative and postoperative complications. Adverse effects, including corneal edema, hyphema, Descemet’s membrane folds, elevated IOP, and microbypass dislocation (phaco-iStent group), were analyzed across both groups. Corneal edema was defined as any postoperative stromal corneal swelling observed on slit-lamp examination, with or without Descemet’s membrane folds. Elevated IOP was classified as values above 22 mm Hg and hyphema as blood in the anterior chamber, which caused visual impairment. Hypotony was defined as an IOP below 6 mm Hg. Surgical failure was defined as the need for additional glaucoma surgeries.

Surgical Procedure

All surgeries were performed by a single highly experienced surgeon (J.K.). Each glaucoma procedure was performed after phacoemulsification and intraocular lens implantation. In both glaucoma surgeries, viscoelastic was used to fill the anterior chamber, and the patient’s head was rotated away from the surgeon. At a 45° angle, the microscope was positioned and a gonioprism was applied to the cornea to achieve optimal angle visualization. During the phaco-iStent procedure, 2 stents were implanted into the TM in the nasal quadrants, 2 to 3 clock hours apart. In the phaco-KDB group, the double knife was used to excise a strip of TM in the nasal quadrants (at approximately 100° of the angle). Postoperative topical medications were administered to the patients’ eyes, specifically antibiotics 3 times daily for the first 7 days, 0.1% dexamethasone, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drops, and artificial tears 3 times daily for 4 weeks. On the day of the procedure, all antiglaucoma medications were discontinued. They were re-administered, in cases in which the desired IOP level was not reached postoperatively according to the European Glaucoma Society guidelines.

Statistical Analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.2). Qualitative data are summarized using absolute counts and percentages, and continuous measurements as mean ± standard deviation and median with interquartile range, as appropriate. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance using the Levene test.

Given the heterogeneous follow-up duration and decreasing number of eyes at successive postoperative time points, all analyses were performed separately for each visit using a complete-case approach. For each time point, only eyes with available data were included in the analysis, and the number of observations (n) varied across visits.

Between-group comparisons at each time point were performed using the independent-samples t test or Mann-Whitney U test, in accordance with the data profile and variance equality. Between-group comparisons of surgical success rates were performed using the chi-square test or Fisher exact test, as appropriate based on expected cell counts. Within-group longitudinal comparisons (postoperative vs baseline) were conducted using paired t tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, including only eyes with data available at both baseline and the respective follow-up visit.

No imputation of missing data was performed. Missing data resulted from the ongoing nature of the study and incomplete follow-up at later time points.

To account for variable follow-up duration across patients, cumulative incidence of surgical success over time was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method. Separate Kaplan-Meier analyses were performed for overall surgical success and complete surgical success. Differences between the phaco-iStent and phaco-KDB groups were assessed using the log-rank test. A 2-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Because multiple comparisons were performed across several postoperative time points, all P values should be interpreted as exploratory and were not adjusted for multiplicity.

The necessary sample size was estimated to detect a between-group difference of 3 mm Hg in IOP. Assuming a pooled standard deviation of 3 mm Hg, a power of 80%, and a 2-sided α of 0.05, a minimum of 16 patients per group was required. This sample size calculation was based on the primary outcome (change in IOP from baseline). The trial was not designed to identify differences in secondary or safety outcomes.

Mean differences and median differences are reported according to the statistical test applied.

Results

Demographics

A total of 68 eyes of 68 patients were enrolled in the trial. Thirty-five patients underwent phacoemulsification with iStent inject implantation, whereas 33 underwent cataract surgery combined with KDB goniotomy. All participants were White. Participants in the phaco-iStent group were notably younger than those in the phaco-KDB group (mean difference −4.36 years, 95% CI −8.19 to −0.53; P = 0.026). No statistically notable differences were identified between groups in baseline IOP, number of glaucoma medications, BCVA, or ECC (mean difference −56.93 (95% CI: −222.76 to 108.89); P = 0.495), confirming baseline comparability (Table 1).

Comparison of Ophthalmic Parameters Between Study Groups at Each Follow-Up

In the phaco-iStent group, the number of glaucoma medications was reduced significantly at each follow-up compared with baseline (P < 0.001 at each follow-up), except for follow-up after 12 months with significant change not confirmed (P = 0.495). The average number of antiglaucoma agents before the treatment was 1.29 ± 0.71, with a median of 1.00, while in consecutive follow-up, the mean was reduced to close to zero, and a median of 0.00.

In the phaco-KDB group, the number of glaucoma medications was reduced significantly at each follow-up compared with the baseline (P < 0.001 at each follow-up except for after 12 months and P = 0.012). The median number of glaucoma medications before treatment was 1.00, while in consecutive follow-up, it was 0.00 (Figure 2, Table 2).

In the phaco-iStent group, BCVA showed a significant increase at each follow-up (P < 0.001), except for measurement after 1 day (P = 0.892) and measurement after 12 months (P = 0.200). The size of the increase, expressed as the mean difference from the baseline measurement, ranged from 0.23 (95% CI: 0.14 to 0.33) after 7 days to 0.37 (95% CI: 0.23 to 0.52) after 6 months.

In the phaco-KDB group, BCVA showed a transient decrease 1 day after treatment (mean difference = 0.00, 95% CI: −0.34 to −0.04; P = 0.018). Although the median difference was 0.00, the confidence interval indicated a negative change in BCVA due to blood in the anterior chamber. Furthermore, the BCVA showed a significant increase at each follow-up. The size of the increase expressed as the median difference from the baseline measurement was 0.40 (95% CI: 0.26 to 0.45; P < 0.001) after 7 days and 0.20 (95% CI: 0.20 to 0.22; P = 0.012) after 12 months. The BCVA comparison is shown in Figure 3.

At baseline, the mean IOP was 19.37 ± 4.03 mm Hg in the phaco-iStent group, and IOP was significantly reduced at each follow-up, except for the measurement after 12 months (P = 0.789). The size of the reduction expressed as the median difference from the baseline measurement was −4.50 (95% CI: −5.30 to −1.70; P = 0.001) after 1 day, −2.30 (95% CI: −3.50 to −0.65; P = 0.008) after 7 days up to −3.95 (95% CI: −5.00 to −0.30) after 6 months.

At baseline, the mean IOP was 18.15 ± 3.76 mm Hg in the phaco-KDB group. IOP was significantly reduced at 1, 3, and 6 months after surgery (mean difference = −2.60 mm Hg; 95% CI, −4.10 to −1.35; P < 0.001; mean difference = −3.20 mm Hg; 95% CI, −5.30 to −1.85; P < 0.001; and mean difference = −2.90 mm Hg; 95% CI, −5.15 to −0.75; P = 0.007, respectively) (Figure 4).

In the phaco-iStent group, the ECC showed a significant reduction at each follow-up (P < 0.001) except for the measurement after 12 months (P = 0.216). The size of the reduction expressed as the mean difference to baseline measurement was −580.03 (95% CI: −737.84 to −422.22) after 7 days up to −534.25 (95% CI: −716.34 to −352.16) after 6 months.

In the phaco-KDB group, ECC showed a significant decrease at each follow-up (P < 0.001), except at 12 months (P = 0.004). The size of the reduction, expressed as the median difference from baseline, decreased from −897.50 (95% CI: −999.50 to −528.00; P < 0.001) after 1 day to −441.00 (95% CI: −850.00 to −370.00; P = 0.004) after 12 months. Interpretation of ECC changes at later follow-up visits should be made with caution because of the decreasing number of eyes available for analysis over time.

No clinically concerning changes in RNFL or MD measurements were observed during follow-up. However, no formal progression analysis was performed, and these parameters were evaluated descriptively because of the preliminary nature of the study and incomplete follow-up. A detailed comparison of ophthalmic parameters between the study groups at each follow-up visit is presented in Table 3.

Surgical Success

Surgical success after 1 day was accomplished in 68.6% of phaco-iStent eyes and in 51.5% of phaco-KDB eyes. After 7 days, the rate of success decreased to 45.7% and 41.9% in the 2 groups, respectively, and increased in the subsequent follow-up visits as follows: at 1 month, 51.6% and 69.2%; at 3 months, 65.2% and 85%; and at 6 months, 68.8% and 62.5% in the 2 groups, respectively. Differences in the proportions between the study groups were not confirmed.

Complete surgical success after 1 day was accomplished in 65.7% of eyes in the iStent group and 45.5% of eyes in the KDB group. After 7 days, the rate of success decreased to 45.7% and 41.9% in the 2 groups, respectively, and increased in the subsequent follow-up visits as follows: at 1 month, 51.6% and 69.2%; at 3 months 65.2% and 85%; and at 6 months 68.8% and 62.5%.

Among eyes that achieved surgical success after 1 day, 1 eye in the phaco-iStent group and 2 eyes in the phaco-KDB group met the success criteria while receiving topical antiglaucomatous medication (satisfactory surgical success). Hence, complete surgical success after 1 day was accomplished in 65.7% of phaco-iStent eyes and 45.5% of phaco-KDB eyes (Table 4).

From day 7 onward, all eyes meeting the success criteria required no glaucoma medications; therefore, complete success was numerically identical to overall success at these time points, and no cases of satisfactory success were observed.

Kaplan-Meier analysis was performed to assess cumulative surgical success over time while accounting for variable follow-up duration. No significant difference between the phaco-iStent and phaco-KDB groups was observed for overall surgical success (log-rank P = 0.974; Figure 5). Likewise, Kaplan-Meier estimates for complete surgical success demonstrated no significant between-group difference (log-rank P = 0.863; Figure 6).

Safety and Adverse Events

Corneal edema, hyphema, and Descemet’s membrane folds were the most common adverse events. These events were largely self-limiting and did not appear to have a clinically relevant impact on treatment outcomes. On the first day after surgery, corneal edema occurred in 65.7% of phaco-iStent eyes and in 54.5% of phaco-KDB eyes, and edema persisted until day 7 in 8.6% of phaco-iStent and 6.5% of phaco-KDB eyes. In most cases, corneal edema was accompanied by Descemet’s membrane folds.

Hyphema was observed in 31.4% of phaco-iStent eyes on day 1 and decreased to 2.9% on day 7. In phaco-KDB eyes, hyphema was observed in 51.5% on day 1 and 9.7% on day 7. No formal statistical evaluation was performed to determine the association between anticoagulation therapy and hyphema. In certain cases of hyphema, the patients were treated with oral absorptive therapy. Irrigation of the anterior chamber was not required in any case.

The highest rate of elevated IOP above 22 mm Hg was observed in the first week in 11.4% of phaco-iStent eyes and 22.6% phaco-KDB eyes, respectively. Among the 35 eyes that underwent iStent inject with phacoemulsification, 34 (97.14%) were implanted with 2 stents. In 1 eye, only 1 stent was used because the second stent was accidentally displaced and extracted at the end of the surgery. After 7 days, the stent was partially covered by iris or scleral spur due to the specific configuration of the iridocorneal angle in 3 eyes (Table 5).

Discussion

The purpose of CB-MIGS is to decrease IOP and decrease the use of IOP-lowering medications. In the present study, the maximal rates of surgical success (IOP reduction vs baseline ≥ 20% or IOP ≤ 15 mm Hg) were 68.8% and 62.5% after 6 months in the phaco-iStent and phaco-KDB groups, respectively. Arnljots et al [16] reported slightly different results. In their study, iStent implantation was less effective than the KDB procedure in reducing IOP by 20%, with success rates of 59% and 84%, respectively. Similarly, Barkander et al [15] indicated that iStent might be somewhat less effective, reporting a 20% IOP reduction in 46% of patients treated with iStent, compared with 51% in those who received the KDB procedure.

These findings in the present study were further supported by Kaplan-Meier analysis, which demonstrated comparable cumulative probabilities of both overall and complete surgical success throughout follow-up. No statistically significant differences between the phaco-iStent and phaco-KDB groups were detected using the log-rank test (P = 0.974 and P = 0.863, respectively), suggesting similar effectiveness of both procedures over time.

A notable difference confirmed by statistical analysis in baseline age between the groups was observed, with patients in the phaco-iStent group being younger. However, given the relatively small absolute difference and the randomized design of the study, it is unlikely that this imbalance had a clinically meaningful impact on the postoperative outcomes. Nevertheless, a potential influence of age on surgical response cannot be completely excluded.

At 6 months, a reduction of antiglaucoma drops to 0.00 ± 0.00 was observed in the 2 groups; however, this occurred earlier in phaco-KDB eyes, at 3 months, whereas in the phaco-iStent eyes, it was achieved at 6 months. Many trials have reported a larger decrease in glaucoma medication usage in the phaco-KDB group [12,16,19] than in the phaco-iStent group. Dorairaj et al [19] reported that 43.6% of phaco-iStent eyes and 58.6% of phaco-KDB eyes were medication-free at 6 months. Barkander et al [15] reported medication-free eyes as 11% in the phaco-iStent group and 37% in the phaco-KDB group after 24 months. This more significant result might be because goniotomy provides access to a greater number of collector channels than does the iStent procedure. Additionally, earlier research has indicated that iStent implanted into the TM may not always work effectively, because of incorrect positioning, which can influence their ability to lower IOP [20]. The available literature contains no studies reporting the complete elimination of glaucoma medications in all patients, as observed in our study at 6 months. However, these results may change with an extended follow-up, as some eyes at 12 months in the present study required re-administration of antiglaucoma drops.

The iStent and KDB procedures demonstrate a high safety profile and are well accepted when performed in combination with phacoemulsification. The most common postoperative adverse event was corneal edema, which occurred in 65.7% of phaco-iStent and 54.5% of phaco-KDB eyes. A reduction in corneal ECC was observed in both groups; however, this finding is most likely attributable to the phacoemulsification procedure rather than the MIGS technique itself. The studies published so far reported a negligible rate of corneal edema; for example, corneal edema in the study by ElMallah et al [11] occurred in 1% of phaco-iStent and 2% of phaco-KDB eyes. These differences may be attributable to varying diagnostic criteria used across studies. In the present study, every clinically detectable postoperative corneal stromal swelling observed on slit-lamp examination, including mild and transient edema accompanied only by subtle Descemet’s membrane folds, was recorded as corneal edema. The second most common complication was hyphema, with 31.4% and 51.5% on the first postoperative day in the phaco-iStent and phaco-KDB groups, respectively. In line with our expectations, hyphema occurred more frequently following KDB goniotomy than after iStent implantation. Some patients were observed to have total hyphema, which resolved spontaneously or with the help of oral hemostatic therapy (ethamsylate, tranexamic acid, or calcium dobesilate), and no additional surgical intervention was necessary. Predictably, hyphema is associated with an IOP spike; therefore, advanced glaucoma is a contraindication for KDB, as the occurrence of hyphema and IOP spikes could negatively affect the visual field.

A previous study [21] suggests that, in individuals continuing anticoagulation therapy, hyphema can last longer and often requires further intervention; however, in our study, no patients were receiving anticoagulation therapy at the time of surgery, as any such treatment was modified or discontinued during the preoperative qualification process. Therefore, our study was not designed or powered to assess the association between anticoagulation therapy and hyphema risk. Holmes et al [22] investigated the association between risk factors, such as anticoagulation therapy, and the occurrence of hyphema in patients undergoing iStent and goniotomy and reported no significant correlations between postoperative hyphema and systemic or ocular variables, including anticoagulation therapy. Their findings indicated that the incidence of hyphema varied depending on the surgical technique used, with rates of 1.1% after trabecular stent implantation and 12.7% after goniotomy.

Wood et al [23] presented an interesting case of total hyphema after cataract phacoemulsification combined with iStent implantation in a patient receiving a Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor. An 89-year-old man with chronic lymphocytic leukemia developed severe bleeding in the anterior chamber despite no intraoperative complications. The patient was continued on zanubrutinib perioperatively, without additional antithrombotic therapy. Despite 2 anterior chamber washouts, tranexamic acid administration, and platelet transfusion, the patient experienced recurrent bleeding and uncontrolled IOP, resulting in poor vision. BTK inhibitors are known to impair platelet function through aggregation and adhesion, leading to a significant risk of bleeding in hematological patients. This case demonstrates the necessity of considering the discontinuation of BTK inhibitors before intraocular surgery, to prevent hemorrhage complications, and emphasizes the importance of careful perioperative management of medications in patients with hematological diseases undergoing ophthalmic surgery.

According to the observed safety profile, the titanium iStent appears to be highly biocompatible, with no reported incidents of inflammation or hypersensitivity; however, Huang et al [24] presented a case of postoperative endophthalmitis following combined cataract surgery and iStent inject implantation. This case was caused by Staphylococcus epidermidis and developed on postoperative day 6. Laboratory test results showed underlying neutropenia. Appropriate treatment led to the recovery of full visual acuity.

The present study had several strengths. We conducted a prospective, randomized, single-center trial, and the surgical procedures were conducted by the same highly experienced surgeon on a homogeneous group of patients. Both groups were comparable in terms of size, IOP values, glaucoma severity, and number of antiglaucoma medications used, which affected the reliability of the results.

There are also limitations associated with this study. The IOP-lowering outcomes observed with iStent and KDB could have been influenced by the inherent IOP-reducing effect of cataract extraction and should be considered when assessing combined phacoemulsification and glaucoma procedures [25]. Another limitation was the absence of medication washout before surgery. Patients continued using antiglaucoma eye drops preoperatively; therefore, we cannot determine the extent to which the reduction of IOP is a pure effect of MIGS with phacoemulsification. Preoperative cataract grading and effective phacoemulsification time were not systematically collected as part of the study protocol and therefore could not be reliably analyzed. A further limitation is that the follow-up period was longer, and the number of patients decreased, which may have led to measurement errors in the variables and the lack of masking of the clinician assessing the outcomes. This limitation particularly affects interpretation of longitudinal changes in parameters such as ECC, in which apparent fluctuations over time may partly reflect incomplete follow-up and variability in the analyzed subgroups rather than true biological changes. The number of eyes included in the analysis decreased over time (eg, from 35 at baseline to 16 at 6 months and 4 at 12 months in the phaco-iStent group), reflecting the preliminary nature of the study and the ongoing follow-up process. Consequently, only a limited number of eyes reached the 12-month follow-up at the time of analysis; therefore, the 12-month findings should be interpreted with caution, as they may not fully reflect long-term outcomes. An additional limitation is the baseline age imbalance between groups. No adjustment for age was performed in the analysis, which may introduce potential confounding and should be considered when interpreting the results. In addition, no formal structural or functional progression analysis based on RNFL or MD was performed. Therefore, conclusions regarding glaucoma stability should be interpreted cautiously.

Conclusions

In summary, this study demonstrated that both iStent and KDB, when combined with cataract extraction, effectively lowered IOP in a group of White Polish patients with mild to moderate glaucoma. KDB was associated with an earlier numerical reduction in antiglaucoma medication use; however, between-group differences were not statistically significant. MIGS procedures do not replace traditional glaucoma filtering surgeries; however, they help reduce IOP and the number of antiglaucoma medications used. In appropriately selected patients, these procedures may help delay treatment escalation while maintaining a favorable safety profile. Further research is needed to determine the optimal extent of Schlemm canal incision surgery, number of implanted devices, length and placement of a device, and other modifiable factors that can maximize improvement in conventional outflow. Another unanswered question is whether CB-MIGS may benefit a patient with deteriorated and nonfunctioning collector channels. New imaging devices may provide valuable information. Finally, as longer-term results become available for canal-based techniques, their cost-effectiveness and impact on quality of life will need to be evaluated.

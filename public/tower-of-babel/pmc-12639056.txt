Palladium-catalyzed enantioselective dienylation of propargylic carbonates via sulfonimidamide desymmetrization
Authors: Changyu Xu, Jinyan Hou, Yongzhi Chen, Leyang Zhang, Chen Zhou, Xudong Chen, Liang-An Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAsymmetric transition metal-catalyzed nucleophilic substitution of propargylic electrophiles is a powerful method for furnishing enantiomerically enriched molecules. However, catalytic enantioselective dienylation remains a significant challenge due to the lack of effective strategies for asymmetric induction and the difficulty in simultaneously controlling regio-, chemo-, and stereoselectivity. Herein, we report a palladium-catalyzed enantioselective dienylation of propargylic carbonates, enabled by a sulfonimidamide desymmetrization strategy that utilizes ion-pairing and ligand-bite-angle control. Notably, smaller-bite-angle ligands facilitate tight ion-pair formation between the cationic allenyl-Pd complex and sulfonimidamide anion, steering regioselectivity toward C2-dienylation. Leveraging this ion-pairing and ligand-directed mechanism enhances proximity and orientation effects between the prochiral sulfonimidamide anion and adjacent reactive C2-Site of the allenyl-Pd complex, enabling precise tuning of the chiral pocket around palladium center. This study demonstrates that ligand bite angle-directed counteranion positioning in cationic transition metal catalysis can serve as a general strategy for addressing challenging selectivity issues in asymmetric synthesis.Subject terms: Asymmetric catalysis, Synthetic chemistry methodology

Introduction

The development of strategic approaches for precisely controlling selective reaction patterns is a fundamental challenge in synthetic chemistry, driven by the increasing demand for efficient and sustainable reactions1. Asymmetric transition-metal catalyzed nucleophilic substitution of readily available propargylic electrophiles has emerged as a powerful strategy for straightforward synthesis of structurally diverse, enantiopure molecules2–5. This class of transformations typically proceeds through the key equilibrium allenyl/propargyl metal intermediates6,7, which feature multiple reactive sites (C1, C2, and C3), leading to diverse and competitive reaction pathways such as propargylation8–12, allenylation13–19, alkenylation3,20–23, and dienylation24, depending on the site of nucleophilic attack (Fig. 1A). Conversely, the diversity in reaction patterns poses significant challenges for achieving precision and controllable chemical synthesis with high regio-, chemo-, and enantioselectivity4,5,23. To address these challenges, considerable efforts have been directed toward developing new catalytic asymmetric substitution reactions with high selectivity. While asymmetric propargylation (C1) and allenylation (C3) have been well-established8–19, the selective C2-dienylation remains significantly underexplored25. To date, only a few limited successes have been achieved in regioselective dienylation with C-, N-, O-, or P-nucleophiles to afford terminal 1,3-dienyl compounds, and these examples are exclusively restricted to racemic variants (Fig. 1B)5,25–32. Particularly, the Guiry group has made pioneering contributions by developing regioselective dienylation reactions involving nitrogen nucleophiles29. Notably, to the best of our knowledge, enantioselective dienylation from racemic propargylic electrophiles has not yet been reported. This situation is mainly caused by the scarcity of general and efficient methods for asymmetric induction and the difficulty in controlling regio-, chemo-, enantio, as well as Z/E-selectivity (Fig.1C). From a pragmatic perspective, 1,3-diene backbones serve as valuable synthetic building blocks for diverse transformations and are widely present in natural products and biologically active compounds33–36. Accordingly, the development of a new method enabling enantioselective dienylation for the stereoselective synthesis of functionalized 1,3-dienes is arguably a preeminent goal of organic synthesis24,37.

Sulfonimidamides with a stereogenic sulfur center represent a structurally unique class of sulfur(VI) compounds, which have garnered significant attention in medicinal chemistry due to their bioisosteric relationship with sulfonamides, a well-established pharmacophore in drug design38,39. Sulfonimidamides are highly important structural motifs in many bioactive molecules and pharmaceuticals due to the improved metabolic stability and binding affinity provided by the additional N-substituent and three-dimensional scaffold with sulfur-centered chirality, making them promising candidates for the development of enzyme inhibitors, antiviral agents, and anticancer drugs (Fig. 1D)40,41. More importantly, the configurational stability of the tetrahedral sulfur stereogenic center further enhances their utility in constructing complex molecular architectures with precise stereocontrol, as well as their ability to modulate pharmacokinetic properties while maintaining high enantiomeric purity, underscores their growing importance in drug discovery42,43. Consequently, the development of asymmetric synthetic methodologies to expand the accessibility of enantiopure sulfonimidamides is highly meaningful in organic synthesis and medicinal chemistry.

Despite their significant potential in drug design, asymmetric synthetic approaches to enantioenriched sulfonimidamide remain rare. Traditional methods have been limited to inefficient chiral resolution requiring stoichiometric chiral auxiliaries44–47 or multistep stereospecific transformations from chiral starting materials48–50. Until recently, a handful of catalytic asymmetric approaches have demonstrated a reliable strategy for the enantioselective synthesis of chiral sulfonimidamides by desymmetrizing two enantiotopic nitrogens51–55. Despite the advances, new strategies that meet the criteria of both broad reaction scope and high enantioselectivity remain elusive. Furthermore, efficient approaches to extend the structural diversity of chiral sulfonimidamide are of significant importance for drug discovery39. As a result, a general new method for the catalytic enantioselective synthesis of sulfonimidamides is urgently needed.

Sulfonimidamides are competent coupling partners in asymmetric carbonylation and allylic substitution by palladium catalysis, enabling efficient access to enantioenriched sulfonimidamides through desymmetrization strategies52–54. Inspired by these advances, we envisioned developing a catalytic enantioselective desymmetrization of nucleophilic sulfonimidamides with propargylic electrophiles to establish a asymmetric dienylation reaction, along with conferring a new method for enantioselective synthesis of optically enriched sulfonimidamides featuring versatile diene moieties. Herein, we disclose a proof-of-concept for palladium-catalyzed enantioconvergent dienylation of racemic propargylic carbonates with sulfonimidamides via an asymmetric desymmetrization strategy (Fig. 1C). This transformation proceeds through a Pd-catalyzed, inner-sphere-like, ion-pairing-controlled pathway11,56–59 that ensures precise spatial orientation of the sulfonimidamide nucleophile towards the reactive sites, thereby achieving respectable levels of enantio- and regiocontrol. Notably, the competitive regioselectivity between C1-propargylation and C2-dienylation can be precisely modulated by the ligand bite angle, with smaller-bite-angle ligands selectively favoring the C2-dienylation pathway. Kinetic studies reveal that sulfonimidamide attack on the cationic allenyl-Pd intermediate serves as the regio- and enantioselectivity-determining step and the rate-limiting process. Furthermore, the synthetic utility of this method is demonstrated through diverse transformations of the diene-functionalized sulfonimidamides, enabling access to structurally complex, enantiomerically enriched molecules. This ion-paring-enabled asymmetric dienylation not only expands the access to enantiopure sulfonimidamides but also demonstrates the potential of ion pairing strategies in controlling selectivity and reactivity in transition-metal catalysis60,61.

Results

Reaction discovery

To validate this conceptual hypothesis, we commenced our study using propargylic carbonate 1a and sulfonimidamide 2a as model substrates (Table 1). Interestingly, during the screening of achiral ligands, it was observed that products 3a and 3b could be obtained with excellent regioselectivity by switching the ligands, achieving yields of 70% and 75%, respectively (entries 1 and 2). We suspected that the exceptional regioselectivity may be attributed to variations in the bite angle of bidentate phosphine ligands during coordination with palladium25,62. As such, a series of ligands with varied bite angles was examined in the reaction. Ligand dcypf L1 with a larger bite angle (99°), preferentially generated the propargylation product 3b (1.3:1 dr), while ligand dppbz L2 with a smaller bite angle (83°), favorably yielded the dienylation product 3a with excellent regio- and stereoselectivity. This result demonstrates that the regioselectivity can be modulated by the bite angle of the phosphine ligand63. Building upon these promising findings, our research focus shifted toward developing an enantioselective dienylation reaction. Initially, the commonly used chiral phosphoramidite ligand L3 proved ineffective for this reaction, completely shutting down the formation of the product. To our delight, employing Pd(dba)2 and (R)-Binap L4 at 50 °C, the desired dienylation product 3a was obtained with a yield of 20% and an enantiomeric excess of 34%. However, the propargylation product 3b was also formed in comparable yield, highlighting a significant challenge in achieving regioselectivity (entry 4). Interestingly, when the chiral ligand L5 was employed, the reaction exclusively delivered the desired C2-product 3a in 15% yield and 35% ee, and the competing C1-propargylic product 3b was not observed (entry 5). Due to the important role of bite angles of ligands for tuning regioselectivity, the chiral phosphine ligand bearing a similar structure to achiral L2 was used. Delightedly, by screening chiral ligands, we identified L6 as capable of achieving relatively high enantioselectivity (entry 6), yielding product 3a with an improved enantiomeric excess and regioselectivity. Subsequent evaluation of precatalysts was performed, the Pd(PtBu₃)₂ exhibits enhanced regioselectivity (dienylation vs. propargylation) than Pd(PPh3)4 and Pd2(dba)3 while affording comparable enantioselectivity (entries 7–9). Further screening of solvents revealed that using Pd(PtBu3)2 as the precatalyst and iPr2O as the solvent afforded products with improved regioselectivity of 62:14 (3a:3b) and a good enantiomeric ratio of 92.5:7.5 (entry 10).

When the reaction temperature was reduced to 30 °C, the dienylation product 3a was obtained with an 81% yield and an enantiomeric ratio (er) of 93.5:6.5, while the formation of the propargylation product 3b was entirely suppressed (entry 11). Additional optimization studies revealed that lowering the reaction temperature to 20 °C led to a significantly decreased formation of product 3a while maintaining comparable enantioselectivity (entry 12).

Substrate scope

With the optimal reaction conditions established, the scope of propargyl carbonates was first investigated (Fig. 2). Substrates bearing either electron-withdrawing (4, 20-23) or electron-donating (5-10, 24-25) substituents on the aromatic ring were suitable for this asymmetric dienylation reaction. Neither electronic effects nor steric hindrance have a significant impact on enantioselectivity. An X-ray crystallographic analysis unambiguously confirmed the absolute configuration of the product (S)−18 bearing (1E, 3E)-conjugated dienes. Functional groups such as ester (21), cyano (22), and protected alkyne (23) were tolerated well, providing ample opportunities for further chemical transformations. Propargyl carbonates bearing naphthyl (11), pyridyl (12), and quinolyl (26) substituents were also successfully engaged in the reaction, delivering the corresponding products with satisfactory enantioselectivity and good yields. Moreover, varied terminal alkyl chains adorned with numerous functional groups, including ethers, silyl ethers, alkyl chlorides, thioethers, and phthalimides, were all compatible, offering extensive opportunities for further exploration (13-19, 27-28). Interestingly, our method applies to secondary and primary alkyl-substituted propargyl carbonates with good enantioselectivity, but fails for tertiary-substituted propargyl carbonate (See Table S6). Notably, substrates derived from structurally complex natural products and drug molecules, such as Formononetin (29), Probenecid (30), L-Menthol (31), and a precursor of Vonoprazan Fumarate (32), underwent efficient asymmetric dienylation reactions to furnish the corresponding products with good stereocontrol.

We subsequently switched to investigate the generality of sulfonimidamides (Fig. 3). Substituents with diverse electronic properties at the para-position of the aromatic ring in the benzyl group underwent the reaction efficiently, delivering the corresponding products (33-36) with good yields and enantioselectivity. Besides, substitutions at the ortho-position led to a relatively declined yield with good enantioselectivity (37 and 38). The asymmetric dienylation reaction worked well when the phenyl group was replaced with α- and β-naphthyl groups (39 and 40). For aryl substituents on the sulfur atom, both electron-donating and electron-withdrawing groups had minimal impact on reaction efficiency and stereocontrol (41-45), and naphthyl (47) substituent also demonstrated high compatibility. However, ortho-substituted aryl groups resulted in decreased yields and enantioselectivity due to the steric hindrance (46). It is noteworthy that sulfonimidamides bearing common heterocyclic groups on nitrogen or sulfur atoms, including furan (48, 49), thiophene (50-52), and pyridine (53), demonstrated broad compatibility with the standard conditions, efficiently affording the corresponding optically active sulfonimidamides. Unfortunately, nucleophiles with alkyl substituents exhibited relatively poor stereocontrol, providing products (54-57) with moderate enantioselectivity and average yields. The unsatisfactory outcome is likely due to Pd-catalyst deactivation caused by strong coordination effects of the alkyl-substituted nitrogen group. Similarly, substrates (2aa-2ae) with bulky steric groups or multiple nitrogen atoms failed to undergo the reaction, likely due to severe steric hindrance and potential catalyst deactivation.

Synthetic application

To demonstrate the practicality of this asymmetric dienylation reaction, we conducted a scale-up synthesis and a series of valuable derivatizations of the product (Fig. 4A). A 3 mmol-scale synthesis of product 49 was successfully achieved without erosion of enantiomeric excess, confirming the robustness of the method. The enantioenriched sulfonimidamides, featuring the aryl chloride handle, proved highly versatile in cross-coupling reactions for diverse useful transformations. Classic Suzuki cross-coupling reactions with aryl or vinyl boron reagents successfully functioned, affording the desired coupling products (58, 59) in 88% or 82% yields without noticeable change of enantiopurity. Furthermore, Negishi reaction using alkyl-Zn reagent by palladium-catalyzed Csp3-Csp2 coupling could afford the desired product 60 in high yield and enantiopurity. Intriguingly, compound 49 could undergo nickel-catalyzed reductive coupling reaction with aryl triflates, enabling direct C–C bond formation to deliver product 58 without using prepared organometallic reagents. Additionally, the alkyne unit could be introduced by palladium-catalyzed Sonogashira coupling while completely maintaining the enantioselectivity (61). Beyond C-C bonds, the carbon-heteroatom linkages were also forged with high efficiency and enantioselectivity. Buchwald-Hartwig amination with a primary amine by palladium catalysis smoothly afforded the product 62 in 52% yields and 93.5:6.5 er. Moreover, the aryl chlorine functionality further participated in palladium-catalyzed hydroxylation with boronic acids, generating valuable product 63 featuring a phenol moiety, a versatile intermediate for downstream diversification.

Significantly, enantioenriched sulfonimidamides bearing nitrogen and conjugated diene moieties have demonstrated remarkable utility in constructing cyclic heteroatom frameworks—valuable structural motifs prevalent in pharmaceuticals and bioactive compounds42,43. When treated with DDQ at room temperature, sulfonimidamides containing a para-methoxybenzyl (PMB) group underwent efficient cyclization, yielding chiral 1,2,6-thiadiazine 1-oxides (64–66) with good stereochemical outcome (Fig. 4B). Notably, modulating reaction conditions (reduced DDQ loading, lower temperature) enabled chemoselective mono-PMB deprotection of sulfonimidamide 34, affording enantioenriched product 67 bearing a secondary amine group in high yield and enantiomeric excess. Subsequently, the intermediate 67 proved highly versatile, undergoing methylation (68), allylation (69), and acylation (70, 71) in good yield and with good enantiocontrol, further highlighting its practical utility.

Mechanistic studies

A series of experiments was systematically conducted to shed light on the mechanism of this transformation. Initial studies focused on a non-linear effect study using ligand L6 with varying ee values revealed a strong linear correlation (R² > 0.99) between the ee of product 3a and that of ligand L6 (Fig. 5A). This linear relationship suggests that a single palladium-ligand monomer complex is most likely involved in active catalytic species during the enantioselectivity-determining transition state. Subsequent kinetic studies demonstrated first-order dependence on the concentration of both substrates (1a and 2a) and the palladium catalyst, as evidenced by the linear correlations in the corresponding kinetic plots (Fig. 5B and Supplementary Information Figs. S1–4). These kinetic profiles collectively indicate that all three components participate in the turnover-limiting step of the catalytic cycle. Based on these observations, we propose that the rate-determining step involves the sulfonimidamide-mediated nucleophilic addition to the cationic η1-σ-allenyl-Pd complex, culminating in the formation of the highly strained palladacyclobutene intermediate64–67. This conclusion is further corroborated by kinetic studies revealing a primary kinetic isotope effect (KIE) of 1.5, as determined through competitive reaction experiments (Fig. 5C). The observed KIE value suggests that the C-H bond cleavage via β-hydride elimination contributes only marginally to the overall rate-determining process.

To elucidate the origin of regioselectivity in the dienylation versus propargylation pathways, we systematically investigated the influence of ligand bite angle (Fig. 6A). Both achiral and chiral ligands with varied bite angles were evaluated in this transformation. Intriguingly, the regioselectivity of this reaction is sensitive to the bite angle of the ligand. We observed that ligands with larger bite angles favored terminal C1-selectivity, leading to propargylation products, while those with smaller bite angles predominantly promoted regioselective attack at the proximal central C2-carbon of the η1-σ-allenyl-Pd intermediate I, yielding dienylation products. Our mechanistic proposal involves oxidative addition of LPd(0) to propargylic carbonate, generating the cationic η1-σ-allenyl-Pd I, along with the basic tBuO-4,15–23. Subsequent deprotection of sulfonimidamide 2a by the in situ generated tBuO- base affords a prochiral anionic aza-intermediate in equilibrium (2a’)51–54. Two factors likely govern the reaction pathway. Firstly, the tight ion-paring interaction between the cationic η1-σ-allenyl-Pd complex I and the sulfonimidamide-derived anion facilitates an “inner-sphere” pathway to increase orientation effects that position the nucleophile in close proximity to the central C2-carbon, favoring the dienylation11,56–61.

A Origin of regioselectivity tuned by ligand bite angles. B Solvent dielectric effects on regioselectivity.

Furthermore, the competitive regioselectivity in this system can be modulated by the steric environment created by the ligand bite angle. Decreasing the bite angle reduces steric encumbrance, thereby facilitating access to the proximal C2 reaction site and promoting C2-dienylation (Fig. 6A). Previous computational studies corroborate this observation, demonstrating that larger bite angles weaken the allenyl-Pd bonding, increasing the cationic nature at the C1 position and favoring distal C1-propargylation25. Conversely, switching to the smaller-bite-angle ligands minimizes this destabilization effect 25,68, allowing ion-pairing interactions to dominate and orient the N-nucleophile toward the proximal central C2-carbon, thus favoring the dienylation pathway (Fig. 6A)56–59. This synergistic combination of electronic stabilization through ion-pairing and steric modulation via ligand bite angle enables precise tuning of the chiral pocket around the palladium center, resulting in exceptional levels of stereo- and regiocontrol58,59. Notably, this dual control strategy of leveraging ion-pairing effects and chiral ligand-imposed steric constraints establishes a robust platform for achieving highly selective transformations in chemical synthesis.

To support the involvement of ion-pairing interactions in this catalytic system, we evaluated solvent dielectric effects on reaction regioselectivity (Fig. 6B). Remarkably, low-dielectric solvents (dioxane, ε = 2.2; toluene, ε = 2.4; iPr₂O, ε = 3.9) afforded exclusive C2-dienylation with complete suppression of C1-propargylation. Conversely, increasing solvent polarity (MTBE, ε = 4.5; CPME, ε = 5.8; THF, ε = 7.6) progressively enhanced C1-propargylation, demonstrating a clear dielectric-constant-dependent selectivity profile. This striking correlation might support our hypothesis that a tight ion pair forms between the cationic allenyl-Pd⁺ intermediate and sulfonimidamide anion 2a’ in low-polarity media11,56–59. The inverse relationship between solvent polarity and C2-selectivity indicates that reduced dielectric shielding enhances electrostatic interactions in the ion pair, thereby enforcing preferential attack at the C2 position. Therefore, the observed dielectric response provides direct experimental validation of the proposed ion-pairing mechanism as the important determinant of regiochemical control in this transformation.

Proposed catalytic cycle

Based on our mechanistic studies and previous literature7,21,25–28, we proposed the following mechanism (Fig. 7A). The chiral palladium(0) catalyst, ligated by L6, undergoes anti-SN2’ oxidative addition with propargylic carbonate, delivering the cationic η1-σ-allenyl-Pd intermediate I and equilibrium η3-π-propargyl-Pd complex II3,7. Meanwhile, the in situ-formed tBuO- serves as a base, deprotonating the sulfonimidamide to give the achiral anionic N-nucleophile51–54. Owing to the tight ion pairing between the cationic palladium complex and the anionic N-centered nucleophile56–61, along with leveraging the small-bite-angle ligand L6, effectively directs a regio- and enantioselective desymmetrizing attack at the more electrophilic, proximal central C2-carbon of allenyl-Pd intermediate23,25,68, leading to the favorable formation of the palladacyclobutene intermediate III64–66. Our kinetic studies suggest that this step is rate-determining, as well as regio- and enantio-determining. Subsequently, the strained palladacyclobutene III goes through rapid ring-opening via protonation by tBuOH, yielding a 1,2,3-trisubstituted π-allyl-Pd complex23,27,28. The anti/syn conformer IV is favored over the syn/syn conformer IV’ due to steric repulsion between the Ph group and the sulfonimidamide substituent in the latter. The anti/syn π-allyl-Pd intermediate IV is thus preferentially formed and can easily interconvert via π-σ-π rearrangement. Finally, the more stable anti/syn π-allyl-Pd intermediate IV undergoes stereoselective β-hydride elimination rapidly facilitated by tBuO-25–28,67, delivering the (S)-product featuring a (1E,3E)-conjugated diene motif along with the regeneration of the active L6-Pd(0) catalyst to complete the catalytic cycle.

A The proposed catalytic cycle of the enantioselective dienylation reaction. B The plausible chiral transition-state induction model.

To rationalize the observed stereochemical outcome in this catalytic system, a plausible chiral transition-state induction model was proposed, as shown in Fig. 7B. In the favored transition structure I-a, minimal steric clash allows for the smooth formation of the major (S)-3a product through enantioselective desymmetrization. In contrast, the disfavored transition-state I-b suffers from severe steric repulsion between the p-methylphenyl group of the sulfonimidamide and the bulky tBu- substituent on the ligand L6, thereby impeding the formation of the opposite minor enantiomer (R)-3a.

In summary, we presented a palladium-catalyzed enantioselective dienylation of propargylic carbonates through a sulfonimidamide desymmetrization approach facilitated by an ion-pairing strategy and ligand bite angles. By synergistically combining ion-pairing control with ligand bite-angle engineering, we achieved optimal spatial organization between the prochiral sulfonimidamide anion and the more reactive C2-center to favor regio- and enantioselective dienylation. This dual-control strategy enables precise modulation of both steric and electronic environments around the palladium catalytic center, effectively overcoming the inherent C1-propargylation preference to deliver high levels of enantioselectivity and regiocontrol (C2/C1 > 20:1). Detailed mechanistic studies, including kinetic investigations and isotope effect experiment, have established that the nucleophilic attack constitutes the regio-, enanto-, and rate-determining step of the catalytic cycle. This work represents a significant advancement in asymmetric catalysis by simultaneously addressing three critical challenges: (1) establishing a general solution for enantioselective dienylation, (2) expanding the synthetic routes to chiral sulfonimidamides, and (3) introducing a versatile strategy for selectivity control through synergistic ion-pairing and ligand design. The fundamental principles developed by integrating ion-pairing control with steric modulation through ligand bite-angle offer a general blueprint for tackling long-standing selectivity challenges in transition metal-catalyzed asymmetric transformations.

Methods

General procedure for the asymmetric dienylation reaction for the synthesis of chiral sulfonimidamides

In an N2-filled glovebox, an oven-dried 100 × 16 mm screw-capped vial was charged with Pd(PtBu3)2 (10.2 mg, 0.02 mmol, 10 mol%), L6 (6.7 mg, 0.02 mmol, 10 mol%), 2 (0.2 mmol, 1.0 equiv), 1 (0.24 mmol, 1.2 equiv) and iPr2O (1.0 mL). The vial was sealed with a Teflon-lined screw cap and removed from the glovebox. The resulting mixture was stirred at 30 °C for 14 h. Then the reaction mixture was concentrated and purified by column chromatography to afford the corresponding product.

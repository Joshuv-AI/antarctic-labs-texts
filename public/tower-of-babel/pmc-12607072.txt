A novel protein cPFKFB4 encoded by hsa_circ_0065394 strengthens PKM2-mediated glucose metabolic reprogramming to facilitate pancreatic cancer progression under hypoxia
Authors: Hang Chen, Molin Li, Fuming Xie, Shan Liu, Aining Yang, Jingyang Yin, Xianxing Wang, Yuxi Long, Shitao Wu, Lei Cai, Junxia Chen, Huaizhi Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHypoxia is a hallmark of solid tumors and gives rise to the glucose metabolic reprogramming of cancers. Peptides and proteins encoded by circular RNAs (circRNAs) are identified as pivotal mediators in the malignant progression of tumors. Nonetheless, proteins encoded by hypoxia-related circRNAs in pancreatic cancer (PC) remain uncharacterized and poorly understood.MethodsHypoxia-responsive circRNA (hsa_circ_0065394) in PC was screened using a hypoxic culture system. The circularization mechanism of hsa_circ_0065394 was investigated by employing bioinformatics and RNA immunoprecipitation. An assessment of the expression profile and potential clinical significance of hsa_circ_0065394 in PC was conducted utilizing qRT-PCR and in situ hybridization. Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay, mass spectrometry and western blotting identified a protein cPFKFB4 encoded by hsa_circ_0065394. The biological functions of cPFKFB4 were verified by gain- and loss-of-function experiments in vivo and in vitro. From a mechanistic standpoint, how cPFKFB4 modulates the interaction between hnRNP G and hnRNP A1 as well as the regulation of PKM alternative splicing was explored by conducting mass spectrometry, co-immunoprecipitation, immunofluorescence, PKM splicing, RNA immunoprecipitation, western blotting, and rescue experiments.ResultsHypoxia-associated hsa_circ_0065394 was noticeably up-regulated in PC tissues and tightly associated with tumor size. The biological cyclization of hsa_circ_0065394 was mediated by hnRNP L and Alu repeat sequences. Mechanistically, hsa_circ_0065394 encoded a 94 amino acid protein cPFKFB4 through a cap-independent mechanism. cPFKFB4 disrupted the hnRNP G-hnRNP A1 complex through specific binding to hnRNP G, facilitated hnRNP A1-mediated regulation of PKM alternative splicing, thereby heightening the expression of PKM2, and then strengthening glycolysis of PC cells to accelerate the proliferation and metastasis of PC under hypoxic conditions.ConclusionscPFKFB4, a protein encoded by hypoxia-responsive circular RNA, facilitates the proliferation and metastasis of PC cells under hypoxic conditions by regulating PKM2-mediated glycolysis. Our findings not only offer fresh standpoints and valuable insights into the crosstalk between hypoxia, glucose metabolism reprogramming, and PC, but also provide a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for the treatment of PC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12943-025-02500-w.Keywords: Hypoxia, Pancreatic cancer, cPFKFB4, Pyruvate kinase m splicing, Glycolysis

Introduction

Pancreatic cancer (PC) is a tremendously malignant tumor of the digestive system, which brings about an estimated 66,440 new cases and 51,750 deaths in the United States in 2024, according to the American Cancer Society [1]. In spite of remarkable advances in tumor-related diagnosis and treatment techniques in recent years, the prognosis of PC patients has not been strikingly ameliorated. Worse still, the five-year overall survival rate remains approximately 10% across the board [2]. This is bound up with its complex molecular mechanisms, inconspicuous symptoms and signs, and comparatively limited treatment options [3]. As a consequence, conducting in-depth research into the molecular mechanisms that influence the onset and progression of PC, as well as exploring potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets, possesses immense scientific significance and clinical value.

The hypoxic microenvironment of most solid tumors is a defining characteristic, stemming from the uncontrolled proliferation of tumor cells and the malformed tumor vascular system, which collectively contribute to the establishment of this hypoxic state within the tumor microenvironment [4]. PC cancer is a hypovascular tumor with distinct hypoxic characteristics, where the median oxygen partial pressure in cancer tissues is only 0-5.3 mmHg, noticeably lower than that in normal adjacent tissues which ranges from 9.3 to 92.7 mmHg [5]. As already demonstrated by the research findings, hypoxia not only mediates PC cells tolerance to tumor hypoxic microenvironment through adiverse spectrum of mechanisms such as redox homeostasis maintenance, autophagy activation, epigenetic regulation and hypoxia inducible factors, but also drives a diverse array of malignant biological behaviors of PC cells, which serves as one of the autonomous predictors for PC prognosis [6]. As a result, the analysis of the molecular mechanisms regulating the malignant progression of PC from the standpoint of tumor hypoxic microenvironment will provide new insights for exploring specific biomarkers of PC and developing effective molecular targeted drugs.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a novel class of closed circular molecules generated from pre-mRNA through back-splicing, whose biogenesis, biological functions, and molecular mechanisms are bound up with the tumor hypoxic microenvironment [7]. In response to the hypoxic tumor microenvironment, tumor cells produce a variety of molecules, comprising circRNAs, to adapt to the hypoxic conditions and sustain tumor progression [8–10]. As already evidenced by a multitude of research findings, circRNAs derived from core genes play tremendously regulatory roles in tissue development, organismal aging, and disease pathogenesis [11–15]. This contributes to a pivotal research direction for identifying key molecules that drive the malignant progression of PC in hypoxia. As a glycolytic shunt enzyme, PFKFB4 plays a paramount role in the interconversion of fructose-2,6-bisphosphate and fructose-6-phosphate to meet the bioenergetic and redox homeostasis demands of cancer cells, so as to facilitate the survival of tumor cells in a hypoxic environment and accelerate the progression of malignant progression [16–18]. For this reason, we hypothesize that circRNAs derived from PFKFB4 gene may be the key molecules driving PC cells to adapt to the hypoxic microenvironment and undergo continuous malignant evolution.

In this study, we identified hypoxia-induced hsa_circ_0065394 was highly expressed in PC and was tightly correlated with tumor size. The biogenesis of hsa_circ_0065394 was mediated by hnRNP L and Alu repeat sequences. hsa_circ_0065394 encoded a 94 amino acid protein (cPFKFB4) through a cap-independent translation mechanism. cPFKFB4 restored the drive of hnRNP A1 to PKM alternative splicing by disrupting the interaction between hnRNP G/hnRNP A1, thus increasing the expression of PKM2, reinforcing glycolysis of PC cells, and ultimately quickening the malignant progression of PC under hypoxia.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement and human tissue samples

All experimental procedures were approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Chongqing People’s Hospital (KY2021-039-01). Prior to undergoing surgery, all patients provided their written informed consent. Tissues were obtained at the time of surgery from PC patients and immediately stored in liquid nitrogen.

Cell culture

The normal human pancreatic ductal epithelial cell line (HPDE6-C7) and the human PC cell lines (AsPC-1, BxPC-3, Capan-1, PANC-1, and PL45) were obtained from Genechem Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). The above cell lines were cultured using standard media and a humidified incubator (5% CO2 at 37 °C). With regard to hypoxic treatment, the cells were grown at 37 °C in a hypoxic incubator with 1% O2, 5% CO2, and 94% N2.

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) and plasmids transfection and lentiviral infection

SiRNAs, plasmids and lentiviral vectors were obtained from Geneseed Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Guangzhou, China) and Hanbio Science and Technology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). SiRNAs and plasmids were transiently transfected with Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) in line with the manufacturer’s instructions. Lentiviral infection was performed as described in our previous study [8]. The stably transfected cell lines were screened with puromycin (Hanbio). The sequences of siRNAs in this study are shown in Table S1 [8, 19–22].

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted with Trizol (Invitrogen) and reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) with the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara, Dalian, China) following the recommended procedure. qRT-PCR was conducted with SYBR Green Real-time PCR Master Mix Kit (Takara) on the CFX96 system (Bio-Rad, CA, USA). The transcript levels of genes were calculated by the formula of 2−ΔΔCt. The primers employed in this study are listed in Table S2 [19–22].

RNase R and actinomycin D (ActD) assays

For RNase R treatment, total RNA (2 µg) was incubated with 3 U/µg RNase R (Epicenter Technologies, WI, USA) for 30 min at 37 °C. For ActD treatment, PC cells were treated with 3 µg/mL ActD (Sigma-Aldrich, Steinheim, Germany) for 12 h. Afterwards, total RNA was extracted from the processed cells by utilizing TRIzol reagent. Subsequently, the relative RNA expression levels of hsa_circ_0065394, PFKFB4 and β-actin were determined by conducting qRT-PCR.

Nucleocytoplasmic separation

Nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions of PC cells were extracted by employing the PARIS™ kit (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer’s protocol. The extracted RNA underwent reverse transcription followed by qRT-PCR to quantify the expression levels of hsa_circ_0065394, U6 and β-actin in the nuclear and cytoplasmic compartments.

RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

The cells were cross-linked and quenched with 0.3% formaldehyde and glycine solution, separately. The Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore, MA, USA) and antibody were utilized to perform the RIP experiments. Briefly, the cell lysates were incubated with IgG or anti-hnRNP L antibody-coated beads at 4 °C for 12 h. Subsequently, the magnetic beads were treated with proteinase K buffer at 55 °C for 30 min. Then, the co-precipitated RNAs were extracted and purified. Finally, the immunoprecipitated RNAs were measured by qRT-PCR.

Western blotting

Cells were lysed using RIPA buffer supplemented with PMSF. Subsequent to the above steps, total proteins were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore). Following blocking with 5% skimmed milk, the membranes were subjected to incubation with specific primary antibodies for a standard duration, followed by the application of appropriate secondary antibodies. Finally, the enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) was utilized to visualize the signals of the bands.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

PierceTM Classic Magnetic IP/Co-IP Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was used to perform the IP and Co-IP. Cells were lysed in ice-cold lysis reagent. The supernatant from cell lysates was collected and mixed with antibody-coated magnetic beads at 4 °C for 12 h. The beads were washed with buffer. Afterwards, the co-immunoprecipitates were washed, collected and analyzed by adopting SDS-PAGE, silver staining, liquid chromatography-tandem MS (LC-MS/MS), or western blotting.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

The treated PC cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 20 min, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 at room temperature for 20 min, and then blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin for 1 h at 37 °C. Next, the cells were incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C for 14 h and incubated with FITC-conjugated secondary antibody at 37 °C for 1 h. 4′,6-Diamidino-2-Phenylindole (DAPI) was adopted to visualize nuclei. Finally, the images were collected using a fluorescence microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

Dual‑luciferase reporter assay

For IRES activity validation, a series of vectors for Luc2-IRES-Report were designed and synthesized by Geneseed Biotechnology Co., Ltd. PC cells were transfected with empty vector, EMCV-IRES (positive control), IRES wild-type (WT), or deletion vectors (IRES-Del-1 and IRES-Del-2) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). After 48 h, the Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities in cells were determined by the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Hanbio). IRES activity was calculated as the ratio of Firefly to Renilla luciferase activity.

Cell proliferation assay

For cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay, PC cells were seeded into a 96-well plate at 1.0 × 103 cells/well. At the indicated time points, each well was incubated with 10 µL CCK-8 reagent (Hanbio) and 90 µL standard media for 2 h at 37 °C. Subsequently, the optical density (OD) value of each well at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific). For flat plate colony formation assay, PC cells were plated into 6-well plates (1000 cells/well) and cultured in complete medium for two weeks. Finally, the clusters were washed with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution (Beyotime, Shanghai, China).

Migration and invasion assays

Migration and invasion assays were carried out using the Transwell system (Corning Costar, MA, USA). For migration assay, 200 µL of serum-free medium with 5 × 104 PC cells and 500 µL of complete medium were added to the upper and bottom chamber, respectively. The cells in the upper chamber were removed subsequent to incubation for the indicated time. Next, the cells adhering to the lower surface of the chamber were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution (Beyotime). Subsequently, the images were captured using a microscope (Leica). Likewise, for invasion assay, the steps were the same as the migration experiment except that the membrane of the Transwell chamber was coated with matrigel (BD Biosciences, MA, USA).

PKM splicing assay

PKM splicing assay was carried out according to previous study [22]. To put it simply, total RNA was extracted with Trizol (Invitrogen) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA with the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara). The PCR products were digested by PstI (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and were then subjected to non-denaturing PAGE. The primers of PKM are illustrated in Table S2.

Glucose consumption assay

3 × 105 cells were inoculated into a 6-well plate, and after adherence, the medium was replaced with fresh complete medium. After 36-hours, the supernatant was harvested and subjected to centrifugation. These mixtures containing the supernatant and glucose detection working reagent (Jiancheng Bio, Jiangsu, China) were incubated for 10 min at 37 °C. The OD505 values were measured by a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Finally, the result was normalized on the basis of the sample concentration.

Lactate production assay

3 × 105 cells were inoculated into a 6-well plate. Once they adhered to the surface, the spent medium was exchanged for a fresh and complete growth medium. After 36 h, the supernatant was collected and centrifuged. The supernatant sample was mixed with WST-8 Working Solution (Beyotime) and incubated at 37 °C in the dark for 40 min. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The lactate concentration of the sample was calculated using the OD450 value and the standard curve.

ATP production assay

3 × 105 cells were seeded into a 6-well plate. Subsequently, cells were collected subsequent to a 48-hour period. An appropriate amount of lysis buffer (Beyotime) was added to fully lyse the cells. Afterwards, the supernatant was collected by centrifugation. An appropriate amount of supernatant was added into the detection hole containing ATP detection working solution. Upon completion of mixing, the RLU value of the sample was determined by a fluorescence microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The concentration of ATP was ultimately calculated according to the standard curve.

Tissue microarray (TMA) and in situ hybridization (ISH)

The TMA (Outdo Biotech, Shanghai, China) including 70 PC tissues and 58 normal tissues was deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated in alcohol, and digested with proteinase K. Afterwards, the TMA was hybridized with hsa_circ_0065394 probe (Digoxin-5’-TGGGCAGTTGGTCATGCACACGCCACATGTCCCCCCTCCT-3’-Digoxin). Then, the TMA was incubated with anti-Digoxin (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) and stained with 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB). The distribution and the positive staining intensity were evaluated as previously described [23].

In vivo assays

All animal studies were approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Chongqing People’s Hospital (KY 2021-039-01). Female BALB/c nude mice (4–6 weeks) were purchased from Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd (Hunan, China) and housed in a specific pathogen-free environment. 1 × 107 cells resuspended in PBS and matrigel (BD Biosciences) mixture were subcutaneously injected into the left flank of the mice. Tumor size was measured every 7 days and calculated by the following formula: volume = length × width2 × 0.5. After 28 days, tumors were photographed using a small animal imaging system (Berthold, Wildbad, Germany). Afterwards, the mice were sacrificed. For survival analysis, the cutoff time was 70 days subsequent to the inoculation of lentivirus treated cells.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Tumor tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, and subsequently embedded in paraffin. Subsequently, embedded tumor tissues were cut into sections. Following deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval procedures, the sections were subjected to inhibit endogenous peroxidase activity using hydrogen peroxide (H2O2). Next, the aforementioned sections were blocked with goat serum and then incubated with primary antibodies and secondary antibodies. Following the above steps, these sections were treated with diaminobenzidine, hematoxylin, alcohol and xylene. Finally, these sections were photographed under a microscope (Leica). Protein expression levels were quantified by multiplying the positive staining intensity grade (strong = 3, moderate = 2, weak = 1, and negative = 0) by the percentage of positively stained cells (>76% = 4, 51–75% = 3, 26–50% = 2, 5–25% = 1, and < 5% = 0) [23].

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 7.0 (San Diego, CA, USA) and SPSS 22.0 (IBM, SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA) were utilized for data analysis. Continuous variables were analyzed by Student’s t-test (unpaired or paired) or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and recorded as the means ± standard deviation (SD) of at least three independent experiments. The Chi-squared test was applied to analyze categorical variables. Kaplan–Meier plots and the log-rank tests were performed to calculate survival analysis. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001 were deemed to be statistically significant.

Results

Identification of hsa_circ_0065394 in PC cells under hypoxia

To screen out hypoxia-induced circRNAs derived from PFKFB4 in PC, we investigated the expression levels of five circRNAs originating from PFKFB4 in PC cells cultured under normoxic and hypoxic conditions. Among these circRNAs, compared to normoxic PC cells, hsa_circ_0065394 was the most significantly expressed (Fig. 1A and B) and the most abundant circRNA in hypoxic PC cells (Fig. 1C and D). Sanger sequencing confirmed that hsa_circ_0065394 is a 281nt circRNA formed by the head-to-tail splicing of exons 2, 3, and 4 of PFKFB4 gene (Fig. 1E). Agarose gel electrophoresis of PCR products illustrated that hsa_circ_0065394 was amplified from cDNA only by divergent primers, ruling out the possibility of genomic rearrangement and trans-splicing (Fig. 1F). As further confirmed by RNase R experiment, hsa_circ_0065394 was resistant to RNase R ascribable to the absence of a 5’ cap and a 3’ poly (A) tail (Fig. 1G). In comparison with normal cells, the expression of hsa_circ_0065394 in ActD-treated cells was conspicuously higher than that of mRNA, giving rise to a longer half-life (Fig. 1H). Nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation experiment revealed that hsa_circ_0065394 was distributed in both nucleus and cytoplasm (Fig. 1I). To sum up, our research findings revealed that hsa_circ_0065394 is a hypoxia-responsive circRNA that is abundantly and stably expressed in PC.

The biological formation of hsa_circ_0065394

As relevant investigation has demonstrated that reverse complementary sequences (such as Alu repeat sequences) and inverted tandem repeats in exon flanking introns facilitate circRNA formation [24, 25]. To comprehensively probe into the circularization mechanism of hsa_circ_0065394, we searched for Alu repeat elements in flanking introns near hsa_circ_0065394 by referring to the NCBI database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and performed BLAST alignment. AluYc and AluSx1 were found to exhibit a high degree of reverse complementarity (79% identity among 307-nt; Fig. S1). Subsequently, deletion mutants containing or lacking AluYc and AluSx1 were constructed (Fig. 2A). Subsequent to transfection with these vectors, qRT-PCR analysis revealed that only the presence of both AluYc and AluSx1 could immensely up-regulate hsa_circ_0065394 expression (Fig. 2B).

Vector #2 contained the genomic region of hsa_circ_0065394 flanked by two Alu elements. #3 to #5 represent a series of deletions of Alu elements. B qRT-PCR for relative hsa_circ_0065394 expression in hypoxic PANC-1 cells transfected with the above five plasmids. C-F qRT-PCR analysis of the relative expressions of 11 RBPs (C and D) and hsa_circ_0065394 (E and F) in hypoxic PC cells transfected with the corresponding siRNAs. G Schematic diagram of the potential binding sites between hnRNP L and PFKFB4 pre-mRNA. H and I RIP exhibited that hnRNP L bound to PFKFB4 pre-mRNA

An increasing amount of evidence has suggested that some RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) bind to specific sites in the flanking intron sequence of pre-mRNA, bringing the downstream and upstream splicing exons closer together in space, thereby enhancing the cyclization efficiency of circRNA [26]. To dig into whether RBPs were involved in the circularization of hsa_circ_0065394, we designed and synthesized siRNAs targeting eight known RBPs (ADAR, FUS, DHX9, QKI, ESRP1, hnRNP L, EIF4A1, and EIF4A3) that were reported to play a paramount role in circRNA biogenesis. The knockdown efficiency of these siRNAs and the expression profile of hsa_circ_0065394 in each group were detected by qRT-PCR (Fig. 2C and D). As qRT-PCR findings demonstrated, hsa_circ_0065394 expression was only negatively regulated in cells transfected with hnRNP L siRNA, indicating that hnRNP L was essential for hsa_circ_0065394 biogenesis (Fig. 2E and F). Subsequently, RBP map database (http://rbpmap.technion.ac.il/index.html) was adopted to predict the binding sites of PFKFB4 pre-mRNA with hnRNP L and designed specific primers (Fig. 2G). Subsequently, RIP using hnRNP L antibody unveiled that hnRNP L was significantly enriched at sites B and C (Fig. 2H and I). As apparently illustrated by the above results, hnRNP L speeds up the biogenesis of hsa_circ_0065394 via binding to specific sites of PFKFB4 pre-mRNA, thereby reinforcing the binding of the flanking intron complementary repeat elements.

hsa_circ_0065394 is up-regulated in PC

With an aim to probe deep into the expression pattern of hsa_circ_0065394 in PC, we used qRT-PCR to evaluate the expression of hsa_circ_0065394 in normal pancreatic ductal epithelial cell line and PC cell lines. We found that in comparison with HPDE6-C7, hsa_circ_0065394 expression was significantly reinforced in the vast majority of PC cell lines, especially PANC-1 and PL45 (Fig. 3A). On that account, they were selected for subsequent research. qRT-PCR was employed to clarify the expression of hsa_circ_0065394 in PC tissues and adjacent tissues and the results illustrated that hsa_circ_0065394 tended to be highly expressed in PC tissues in contrast to adjacent tissues (Fig. 3B and C). To investigate the clinical value of hsa_circ_0065394 in PC, ROC analysis was performed by employing the prognostic information of patients and the above results. The area under the curve (AUC) was 0.677, hinting that hsa_circ_0065394 had potential in distinguishing PC tissues and non-cancerous tissues (Fig. 3D). On top of that, the result of TAM containing 70 paraffin-embedded PC tissues and 58 para-cancerous tissues also suggested that hsa_circ_0065394 was up-regulated in PC tissues (Fig. 3E-G). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis indicated that patients with high level of hsa_circ_0065394 had worse overall survival than those with low hsa_circ_0065394 level (Fig. 3I). The correlation analysis indicated that PC patients suffering from high expression of hsa_circ_0065394 had larger tumors than those with low hsa_circ_0065394 level (Table 1 and Table S3). On the whole, our findings validated that hsa_circ_0065394 is highly expressed in PC, has important clinical potential, and is involved in the progression of PC.

hsa_circ_0065394 accelerates the proliferation, migration and invasion of PC cells under hypoxia

To elucidate the impact of hsa_circ_0065394 on the biological functions of PC cells under hypoxic conditions, we employed a tri-gas incubator to simulate the tumor hypoxic microenvironment. Subsequently, in vitro experiments were conducted under hypoxic conditions. We designed and synthesized hsa_circ_0065394 over-expression plasmid (OE-circRNA) and siRNAs (siRNA-1 and siRNA-2) specifically to upregulate and knock down the expression of hsa_circ_0065394, respectively. As demonstrated by qRT-PCR results, over-expression plasmid and siRNAs of hsa_circ_0065394 considerably strengthened and lowered the expression of hsa_circ_0065394 in PC cells, respectively (Fig. S2A), without affecting the expression of the parent gene PFKFB4 (Fig. S2B). CCK-8 assay (Fig. 4A) and colony formation assay (Fig. 4B) demonstrated that up-regulation of hsa_circ_0065394 enhanced the proliferation ability of PC cells under hypoxia, while down-regulation of hsa_circ_0065394 weakened this ability. On top of that, Transwell assay revealed that over-expression of hsa_circ_0065394 significantly elevated the migration and invasion capabilities of PC cells under hypoxic conditions, whereas knockdown of hsa_circ_0065394 resulted in the opposite effect (Fig. 4C). These results validated that hsa_circ_0065394 substantially facilitates the malignant progression of PC cells under hypoxic conditions.

C The migration and invasion abilities of hypoxic PC cells were evaluated by Transwell assay. D and E Representative images of bioluminescence (D, Scale bars = 1 cm) and subcutaneous tumors (E) from each group with different hsa_circ_0065394 expression levels. To delve into the role of hsa_circ_0065394 in tumor growth in vivo, PC cell lines with stable over-expression (LV-circRNA) or knockdown (LV-sh-1) of hsa_circ_0065394 were injected into nude mice to construct xenograft tumor models. By analyzing the volume and weight of the xenograft tumors, we found that in contrast to the control group, hsa_circ_0065394 over-expression increased tumor volume, weight, and growth rate, while knockdown of hsa_circ_0065394 yielded the opposite results (Fig. 4D-H). Altogether, these data demonstrated that hsa_circ_0065394 accelerates the growth of PC cells in vivo.

hsa_circ_0065394 encodes a novel 94-amino acid protein termed cPFKFB4

Recent studies have confirmed that some circRNAs with start codon sites, open reading frames (ORFs), and internal ribosome entry sites (IRES) can be translated in a cap-independent manner to produce proteins or peptides with special functions under certain conditions [27–29]. Our team first used bioinformatics tools to predict the possible ORF, IRES, and coding potential of hsa_circ_0065394. The ORF Finder indicated that hsa_circ_0065394 contained a 285-nt ORF. The IRES Finder suggested that hsa_circ_0065394 exhibited an IRES spanning 137–281 nt (Fig. 5A). CPAT software analysis revealed that hsa_circ_0065394 had translation potential (87.31%). Firstly, 3×FLAG tag was inserted into the hsa_circ_0065394 over-expression vector by in-fusion cloning technology, named F-cPFKFB4. Western blot illustrated a FLAG -tagged protein of approximately 25 kDa (Fig. 5B). Aside from that, Co-IP combined with silver staining was used to identify a distinct protein band at ~ 25 kDa (Fig. 5C), which coincided with the Western blot results. On top of that, we excised the target gel region and the remaining gel for LC-MS, and a specific peptide fragment of F-cPFKFB4 (SFEFLPDNEEGL) was found and characterized (Fig. 5D). Subsequently, we designed and constructed dual-luciferase reporter vectors containing either the full-length IRES sequences or truncated IRES sequences (Fig. 5E) to verify whether the translation of hsa_circ_0065394 was initiated by IRES-mediated ribosome entry. As revealed by the findings obtained from the dual luciferase gene reporter gene assay, the inferred IRES sequences displayed marked activity, whereas the truncated IRES sequences had no activity (Fig. 5F). Next, we designed and constructed translation vectors carrying either the full-length IRES sequences or truncated IRES sequences (Fig. S3A). Western blot analysis corroborated the findings of the dual-luciferase reporter assay: only the vector containing the intact IRES sequences could translate cPFKFB4 (Fig. S3B), illustrating that the IRES sequences must remain intact to drive the translation of hsa_circ_0065394. In summary, our investigation findings suggested that hsa_circ_0065394 is translated into a new 25 kDa protein (cPFKFB4) in an IRES-dependent manner.

cPFKFB4 promotes proliferation, migration and invasion of PC cells under hypoxia

To further explore the biological function of cPFKFB4, we transfected OE-vector, OE-circRNA, or F-cPFKFB4 into PC cell lines. The results of CCK-8 (Fig. 6A), clonal formation experiment (Fig. 6B and C), and Transwell experiments (Fig. 6D and E) collectively suggested that both hsa_circ_0065394 and its protein facilitated the proliferation, migration and invasion of PC cells under hypoxia. To verify whether the biological function of hsa_circ_0065394 rests with cPFKFB4 in PC, cPFKFB4 expression was up-regulated after knocking down the expression of hsa_circ_0065394 in PC cells. The results of in vitro functional experiments showed that cPFKFB4 eliminated the inhibitory effect of hsa_circ_0065394 knockdown on cell proliferation (Fig. 6F), migration (Fig. 6G and H), and invasion (Fig. 6I and J). These exploration findings demonstrated that cPFKFB4 can independently affect the malignant phenotype of PC cells. More importantly, the regulation of hsa_circ_0065394 on the biological function of PC cells depends on its encoded protein cPFKFB4.

I and J Transwell assay was performed to assess the migration and invasion abilities of hypoxic PC cells co-transfected with different vectors

cPFKFB4 specifically binds to hnRNP G

To further elucidate the molecular mechanism by which cPFKFB4 facilitates the malignant progression of PC cells under hypoxia, Co-IP combined with LC-MS was performed, and the results suggested that cPFKFB4 might interact with hnRNP G (Fig. 7A and B, and Fig. S4). We carried out Co-IP on PC cell lines transfected with F-cPFKFB4, and arrived at a conclusion that FLAG antibody considerably enriched both cPFKFB4 and hnRNP G (Fig. 7C). IF staining revealed that hnRNP G and cPFKFB4 co-localized in hypoxic PC cells (Fig. 7D). To further identify the specific binding sites of hnRNP G to cPFKFB4, we designed and synthesized six HA-tagged truncated mutant plasmids (HA-hnRNP G, HA-Mut 1, HA-Mut 2, HA-Mut 3, HA-Mut 4, and HA-Mut 5) based on the known domains of hnRNP G (Fig. 7E and F). Subsequent Co-IP experiments demonstrated that cPFKFB4 bound to Mut 4 and Mut 5 of hnRNP G, namely the serine-arginine-glycine-tyrosine (SRGY) and C-terminal RNA binding domain (C-RBD) domains (Fig. 7G). In conclusion, cPFKFB4 specifically interacts with the SRGY and C-RBD domains of hnRNP G.

cPFKFB4 inhibits hnRNP G/hnRNP A1 interaction to regulate PKM alternative splicing

After transfection of the truncated mutant plasmids of hnRNP G, the potential proteins were enriched by hnRNP A1 antibody, demonstrating that hnRNP A1 could directly bind to hnRNP G in PC, and the specific binding domain was C-RBD domain (Fig. 7H). As previously reported, the interaction between hnRNP G and hnRNP A1 regulates PKM alternative splicing, thereby regulating cancer cell glucose metabolism [22]. Thus, Co-IP assay was performed after up-regulating or down-regulating the expression of cPFKFB4 in PC cells, to verify the effect of cPFKFB4 on the binding between hnRNP G and hnRNP A1. cPFKFB4 overexpression weakened the binding between hnRNP G and hnRNP A1, while down-regulation of cPFKFB4 strengthened the binding between hnRNP G and hnRNP A1 (Fig. 8A-D). Afterwards, the results of RIP experiments showed that up-regulation of cPFKFB4 enhanced the binding between hnRNP A1 and pre-PKM, while down-regulation of cPFKFB4 expression had the opposite effect (Fig. 8E and F). As illustrated by qRT-PCR findings, variations in cPFKFB4 expression did not affect the transcription level of PKM (Fig. 8G). The results of PKM alternative splicing experiment (Fig. 8H) and western blot (Fig. 8I) confirmed that up-regulation of cPFKFB4 increased the PKM2 isoform and decreased the PKM1 isoform, while down-regulation of cPFKFB4 revealed the opposite effect on PKM alternative splicing. We next performed rescue experiments between cPFKFB4 and hnRNP G. In particular, the PKM alternative splice experiment showed that hnRNP G overexpression reversed the up-regulation effect of cPFKFB4 on the PKM2/1 transcript ratio, and knockdown of hnRNP G rescued the down-regulation effect of cPFKFB4 knockdown on the PKM2/1 transcript ratio (Fig. 8J). Western blot analysis yielded results consistent with the aforementioned experimental findings (Fig. 8K). These results confirmed that cPFKFB4 competitively binds to hnRNP G, disrupting its interaction with hnRNP A1, to promote PKM2 splicing and expression.

cPFKFB4 strengthens glycolysis in PC cells through hnRNP G under hypoxia

PKM2 plays a crucial role in glucose metabolism remodeling [30]. Consequently, we first investigated the effect of cPFKFB4 on glycolytic metabolism in PC cells. Lactate production assay showed that up-regulation of cPFKFB4 strikingly reinforced lactate production in PC cells under hypoxia, while down-regulation of cPFKFB4 considerably lessened lactate production in PC cells under hypoxia (Fig. 9A). On top of that, similar results were observed in glucose uptake and ATP production experiments (Fig. 9B and C). These glycolytic experiments demonstrated that cPFKFB4 prominently elevates the glycolytic level of PC cells under hypoxia.

cPFKFB4 enhances glycolysis in PC cells under hypoxia. A-C PC cells under hypoxia were transfected with the indicated plasmids, and subsequently, lactate production (A), glucose uptake (B), and ATP production (C) were measured

Then, we conducted rescue experiments to determine whether cPFKFB4 affected glycolytic metabolism of PC cells through hnRNP G. As collectively demonstrated by lactate production, glucose uptake and ATP production experiments, the promoting effects of cPFKFB4 over-expression on lactate production, glucose uptake and ATP production in PC cells were reversed by hnRNP G up-regulation (Fig. 10A, C and E). Aside from that, the inhibitory effects of cPFKFB4 knockdown on lactate production, glucose uptake and ATP production in PC cells were rescued by hnRNP G down-regulation (Fig. 10B, D and F). The above findings confirmed that cPFKFB4 heightens the glycolytic level in PC cells under hypoxia through hnRNP G.

cPFKFB4 augments the malignant phenotype of PC cells through PKM2 under hypoxia

The reshaping of glucose metabolism is an important factor regulating the growth and invasion of malignant tumors [31]. For this reason, we hypothesized that cPFKFB4 might accelerate the malignant phenotype of PC cells by influencing PKM2-mediated glycolysis. On that account, we conducted rescue experiments between cPFKFB4 and PKM2. In vitro cell proliferation assays revealed that PKM2 knockdown abolished the facilitating effect of cPFKFB4 on proliferation under hypoxia (Fig. 11A and C); on the contrary, up-regulation of PKM2 expression counteracted the inhibitory effect of cPFKFB4 knockdown on proliferation (Fig. 11B and D). Similarly, in Transwell migration and invasion experiments, PKM2 knockdown hindered the changes in migration and invasion abilities of PC cells induced by cPFKFB4 overexpression (Fig. 11E). Conversely, PKM2 overexpression offset the changes spawned from cPFKFB4 knockdown (Fig. 11F). In summary, these findings indicated that the oncogenic effects of cPFKFB4 depend on PKM2, a key regulator of glycolytic metabolism.

cPFKFB4 enhances the PKM2-induced aggressive phenotype of PC cells under hypoxia. cPFKFB4 facilitates PC cells growth in vivo through PKM2

To further clarify how cPFKFB4 impacts the proliferation of PC cells via PKM2, we conducted a series of animal experiments. We found that the promotion effect of cPFKFB4 overexpression on the growth of PC cells in vivo was reversed by PKM2 knockdown. In addition, the inhibitory effect of cPFKFB4 knockdown on the growth of PC cells in vivo was offset by PKM2 over-expression (Fig. 12A-D). Additionally, IHC analysis showed that the effects of heightened or lessened cPFKFB4 expression on the expressions of Ki67 and PKM2 in the tumors were reversed by down-regulation or up-regulation of PKM2, respectively (Fig. 12E and F). In summary, these data corresponded to the results of experiments in vitro, indicating that cPFKFB4 facilitates the growth of PC cells in vivo in a PKM2-dependent manner.

Discussion

PC is a digestive system tumor characterized by significant hypoxia. Hypoxia plays an irreplaceable role in tumor maintenance, growth, invasion, metastasis, angiogenesis, metabolic reprogramming, immune evasion and therapy resistance [32]. Identifying pivotal molecules that regulate the malignant progression of PC under hypoxia may offer new theoretical and experimental bases for the diagnosis and treatment of PC.

In this study, we identified a hypoxia-responsive circRNA in PC, hsa_circ_0065394. Clinically, hsa_circ_0065394 was upregulated in PC tissues and was closely associated with tumor size. Functionally, hsa_circ_0065394 promoted the proliferation, migration, invasion, and glycolysis of PC cells under hypoxia. Mechanistically, hsa_circ_0065394 encoded a 94-amino acid protein, cPFKFB4, which regulated PKM alternative splicing by influencing the interaction between hnRNP G and hnRNP A1, thereby reinforcing glycolysis in PC cells and ultimately pushing forward the malignant progression of PC. This study further elucidates the association among hypoxia, glycolysis, and the malignant progression of PC, offering a promising target for its diagnosis and treatment.

A multitude of significant studies have reported that hypoxia-related circRNAs are dysregulated in tumors. Furthermore, their expression patterns are tightly linked with patient pathological characteristics and prognosis, and they play pivotal regulatory roles in tumor initiation and progression [33, 34]. For example, Tang et al. reported that hypoxia-induced exosomal circPLEKHM1 is highly expressed in tumors of patients suffering from bone metastasis, mediates crosstalk between tumor cells and macrophages to drive lung cancer metastasis, and may function as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for lung cancer metastasis [35]. In addition, hypoxia-induced circWSB1 is significantly up-regulated in breast cancer tissues, which is bound up with undesirable clinical outcomes, and degrades p53 to facilitate breast cancer progression through competitive binding with deubiquitinase USP10 [11]. At the same time, the abnormally high expression of circPDK1 in PC tissues expedites the malignant progression of PC under hypoxia through the UBE2O/BIN1 axis [9]. In the present investigation, we found that hsa_circ_0065394 was remarkably up-regulated in PC cells cultured under hypoxia. Analysis of clinical tissue samples and TMA revealed that hsa_circ_0065394 was up-regulated in PC tissues and closely associated with tumor size, demonstrating potential for distinguishing between cancerous and non-cancerous pancreatic tissues. The functional experiments in vitro and in vivo confirmed that hsa_circ_0065394 enhanced the proliferation, migration and invasion of PC cells under hypoxia. The findings from this segment of the research underscored the clinical significance and biological roles of hypoxia-associated molecules in PC, thereby contributing to a deeper understanding of the mechanisms by which hypoxia modulates the malignant progression of cancer.

CircRNAs are a novel class of single-stranded RNA molecules formed through back-splicing into covalently closed loops, and their biogenesis is regulated by reverse complementary sequences (such as Alu repeats) and RBPs [36]. In our exploration, we used bioinformatics to identify highly reverse complementary Alu repeat sequences (AluYc and AluSx1) in the flanking introns of hsa_circ_0065394, which are responsible for the biogenesis of hsa_circ_0065394. Aside from that, we found that hnRNP L silencing remarkably reduced the expression of hsa_circ_0065394. Further database prediction and RIP confirmed that hnRNP L bound to the flanking regions of pre-PFKFB4 mRNA, promoting the biogenesis of hsa_circ_0065394. This result aligns with the previously reported role of hnRNP L in the circularization of circANKRD42 and circNCOR1 [37, 38]. Interestingly, the result from the GEPIA database revealed that hnRNP L was highly expressed in pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD), and its expression level was closely associated with unfavorable prognosis (Fig. S5). Meanwhile, as already validated by the research findings, hnRNP L strikingly expedites the growth and metastasis of PC [39, 40]. These results and literature reports support the notion that hsa_circ_0065394 induced by hnRNP L plays an oncogenic role in PC progression.

CircRNAs were originally considered to be a class of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) incapable of encoding proteins. Nevertheless, the latest research evidence has demonstrated that due to ORFs, IRESs, N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification sites, and the ability to bind to ribosomes/polysomes, circRNAs can be translated into novel and biologically functional peptides/proteins in a cap-independent manner, thereby regulating tumor progression [41]. For example, cGGNBP2, with an ORF length of 548nt, translated a protein of approximately 25 kDa in a cap-independent manner to facilitate intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma growth and metastasis [42]. Hypoxia-responsive circINSIG1 encoded a 121-amino acid protein, circINSIG1-121, which induced cholesterol biosynthesis to drive colorectal cancer proliferation and metastasis [43]. Nonetheless, circRNAs with coding potential in PC have not yet been discovered. We found that hsa_circ_0065394 in PC cells encoded a new 94-amino-acid protein driven by IRES, with a molecular weight of approximately 25 kDa. We hypothesize that this is attributable to the formation of a homodimer by the peptide translated from hsa_circ_0065394. Specifically, we found that over-expression of cPFKFB4 reversed the effects of hsa_circ_0065394 knockdown on the biological functions of PC cells, suggesting that hsa_circ_0065394 accelerated PC progression under hypoxic conditions through its encoded protein, cPFKFB4.

As illustrated by Co-IP in conjunction with LC-MS analysis, cPFKFB4 was bound to hnRNP G. hnRNP G is a multifunctional protein that plays particularly crucial roles in pre-mRNA splicing, DNA damage response, heterochromatin maintenance, and genome stability [44]. hnRNP G consists of RNA recognition motif (RRM), arginine-glycine-glycine (RGG), nascent RNA targeting domain (NTD), SRGY and C-RBD [45]. By utilizing protein truncations combined with Co-IP, further experiments confirmed that cPFKFB4 specifically bound to the SRGY and C-RBD domains of hnRNP G. The splicing-enhancing region of hnRNP G overlaps with a sequence referred to as SRGY-domain originally described for the RNA-binding motif Y (RBMY) chromosome protein, and so named for the presence of four SRGY-boxes in this region in RBMY [46]. The C-RBD domain of hnRNP G displays exceptional affinity for RNA containing GGAAA motifs and m6A modifications [47–49], which evidently suggests that cPFKFB4 may be involved in hnRNP G-mediated regulation of pre-mRNA splicing.

Literature reports indicate that hnRNP G directly binds to the splicing repressor hnRNP A1 to inhibit the regulation of downstream RNA splicing by hnRNP A1 [22]. We further confirmed that hnRNP G directly bound to hnRNP A1 through the C-RBD domain in PC cells by protein truncation combined with Co-IP. These results drove us to speculate that cPFKFB4 might competitively bind to the C-RBD domain of hnRNP G, thereby interfering with hnRNP A1 binding. More importantly, we verified that cPFKFB4 inhibited the formation of the hnRNP A1-hnRNP G complex.

By blocking the binding of the RGG motif of hnRNP A1 to the flanking sequences of PKM, hnRNP G antagonizes the regulation of PKM splicing mediated by hnRNP A1, ultimately inhibiting glycolysis and tumor progression in bladder cancer [22]. PKM is a pivotal and rate-limiting enzyme in glycolysis, which can be divided into two isoforms with different structural and enzymatic properties, namely, PKM1 and PKM2. To be specific, PKM1 contains exon 9 and accelerates oxidative phosphorylation, while PKM2 contains exon 10, regulates cellular glycolysis levels, and promotes the malignant progression in cancer [50]. The above two subtypes are produced through mutually exclusive and alternative splicing of PKM pre-mRNA under various physiological conditions and regulated by multiple factors. For example, splicing factors such as hnRNP A1, hnRNP A2, and hnRNP 1 drive PKM alternative splicing by binding arginine residues in their RGG domain to PKM exon 9 or 10 flanking sequences to produce different proportions of PKM1 and PKM2 [51]. Moreover, molecules such as ARS2 and PHB2 can exert a particularly conspicuous influence on PKM alternative splicing by interacting with the above splicing factors [52, 53]. As demonstrated by our research findings, cPFKFB4 increased the direct binding between hnRNP A1 and PKM pre-mRNA. cPFKFB4 up-regulated the PKM2/1 isoform ratio and heightened the expression of PKM2 in hypoxic PC cells. Taken together, these results confirmed that cPFKFB4 strengthened hnRNP A1-mediated regulation of PKM splicing by inhibiting the binding between hnRNP A1 and hnRNP G. As a result, we speculated that cPFKFB4 would regulate the glycolysis of PC cells. In particular, we concluded that cPFKFB4 regulate lactate production, glucose uptake, and ATP generation in PC cells under hypoxia. Furthermore, we confirmed that cPFKFB4 significantly augmented glycolysis through hnRNP G in PC cells under hypoxic conditions.

As the core of tumor metabolic reprogramming, glucose metabolic reprogramming provides the energy and metabolic substances essential for tumor cells to adapt to hypoxic environments supporting tumor malignant progression [31]. As a key enzyme in the final step of glycolysis, PKM2 converts phosphoenolpyruvate into pyruvate and generates ATP to satisfy the rapid ATP demands of cancer cells due to unlimited proliferation, invasion, and metastasis [54]. Through in vivo and in vitro rescue experiments, we confirmed that cPFKFB4 promoted the proliferation, migration, and invasion of PC cells under hypoxia via PKM2. These research findings further elucidate the role of glycolysis in driving the malignant progression of PC under hypoxia.

Conclusions

In summary, our study described a hypoxia-induced circRNA in PC, hsa_circ_0065394. The biogenesis of hsa_circ_0065394 was mediated by Alu sequences and hnRNP L. Additionally, hsa_circ_0065394 encoded the protein cPFKFB4 in a cap-independent manner. Under hypoxic conditions, cPFKFB4 reinforced hnRNP A1-mediated PKM alternative splicing by interacting with hnRNP G, thereby boosting the expression level of PKM2, strengthening the glycolysis level of PC cells, and ultimately facilitating the malignant progression of PC (Fig. 13). Our findings provide innovative insights into the crosstalk between hypoxia, glucose metabolic reprogramming and PC, and open the door for the development of a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for PC.

On a nonclassical boundary problem for the Laplace equation in a cracked plane
Mykola Krasnoshchok

Abstract
. We consider the Laplace equation in a cracked plane with a nonclassical boundary conditions. This problem arises as a model of the flow in the fractured media. The main result is the theorem of existence and uniqueness of a solution in weighted Sobolev spaces.

1.
Introduction

Consider the plane containing a crack
\Sigma=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1}<0,x_{2}=0\right\}
. Let us denote by
\gamma_{+}
and
\gamma_{-}
the trace operators on
\Sigma

\Sigma

to find a pair
(\mathrm{p}(x),\mathrm{q}(x_{1}))

\Delta\mathrm{p}(x)=0,\mbox{ in }\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma,

(1.1)

\gamma_{+}\mathrm{p}+\gamma_{-}\mathrm{p}-2\mathrm{q}=0,\mbox{ on }\Sigma,

(1.2)

\gamma_{+}(\kappa_{1}\mathrm{p}_{x_{2}}-\mathrm{p})+\gamma_{-}(\kappa_{1}\mathrm{p}_{x_{2}}+\mathrm{p})=0,\mbox{ on }\Sigma,

(1.3)

-\mathrm{q}_{x_{1}x_{1}}-\kappa_{2}(\gamma_{+}\mathrm{p}_{x_{2}}-\gamma_{-}\mathrm{p}_{x_{2}})=\mathrm{f},\mbox{ on }\Sigma,

(1.4)

\mathrm{q}_{x_{1}}(0)=0

(1.5)

\mathrm{p},\mathrm{q}\to 0,\quad|x|\to\infty,

(1.6)

here
\kappa_{1}>0
and
\kappa_{2}>0
.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem (see Section

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and

0<\mu-k-1<\frac{1}{2}.

(1.7)

Let
\mathrm{f}\in H^{k+1/2}_{\mu}(\Sigma)
and, additionally,

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{0}\mathrm{f}(x_{1})dx_{1}=0.

(1.8)

Then there exists a unique solution

(

\|\mathrm{p}\|_{H^{k+2}_{\mu}(\Omega)}+\|\mathrm{q}\|_{H^{k+3/2}_{\mu}(\Sigma)}+\|\mathrm{q}_{x}\|_{H^{k+3/2}_{\mu}(\Sigma)}\leq C\|\mathrm{f}\|_{H^{k+1/2}_{\mu}(\Sigma)},

Passing to polar coordinates (

x_{1}=r\cos\theta,\quad x_{2}=r\sin\theta,\quad\mathrm{p}(x)=p(r,\theta),

(2.2)

\|p\|_{k,\mu}=\left(\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}r^{2(\mu-k)+1}\sum\limits^{k}_{l=0}\|\left(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)^{l}p\|^{2}_{H^{k-l}(-\pi,\pi)}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(2.3)

Define also

(2.4)

(2.5)

Define the Mellin transformation and its inverse

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}(s)=\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}r^{s-1}h(r)dr,s=s_{1}+is_{2},

\displaystyle h(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits^{s_{1}+i\infty}_{s_{1}-i\infty}r^{-s}\widetilde{h}(s)ds,

We will use the Parceval formula

Upon integrations by parts one can prove that for any

(2.9)

\left(\widetilde{(r\frac{\partial}{\partial r})^{k}h}\right)(s)=s^{k}\widetilde{h}(s).

(2.10)

For

(2.11)

here

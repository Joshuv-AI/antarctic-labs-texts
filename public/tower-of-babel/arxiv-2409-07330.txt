Min-max construction of prescribed mean curvature hypersurfaces in noncompact manifolds
Douglas Stryker

Abstract
. We develop a min-max theory for hypersurfaces of prescribed mean curvature in noncompact manifolds, applicable to prescription functions that do not change sign outside a compact set. We use this theory to prove new existence results for closed prescribed mean curvature hypersurfaces in Euclidean space and complete finite area constant mean curvature hypersurfaces in finite volume manifolds.



1.
Introduction

Given a smooth function
h
on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, a hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
has
prescribed mean curvature

h
(abbreviated
h
-PMC) if the mean curvature of
\Sigma
satisfies
H_{\Sigma}=h\mid_{\Sigma}
. The existence question for closed PMC hypersurfaces is a fundamental problem in geometric analysis. Moreover, PMC hypersurfaces are useful tools in Riemannian geometry; for instance,
\mu
-bubbles (which are special PMC hypersurfaces introduced by
[
Gro96
]
) have been instrumental in recent breakthroughs in the study of positive scalar curvature (for example, see
[
Gro20
,
CLL23
,
CL24
]
).

A fruitful approach to the existence problem relies on the observation that
h
-PMC hypersurfaces arise as the boundaries of critical points of the functional

\mathcal{A}^{h}(\Omega)=\mathrm{Area}(\partial\Omega)-\int_{\Omega}h

for finite perimeter sets
\Omega
. Hence,
h
-PMC hypersurfaces can be constructed using minimization techniques (see for instance
[
Gro96
,
Mor03
,
Zhu21
]
) and min-max techniques (see for instance
[
ZZ19
,
ZZ20
,
Zho20
]
) for
\mathcal{A}^{h}
. These minimization and min-max results for
\mathcal{A}^{h}
build on the original minimization and min-max programs for the area functional; we refer the reader to
[
FF60
,
Fle62
,
DG65
,
Alm66
,
Sim68
]
and
[
Alm62
,
Pit81
,
MN16
,
MN21
]
for some particularly relevant results in these directions.

An essential difficulty arises for these variational techniques in noncompact ambient spaces, since critical points are constructed as subsequential limits of minimizing or min-max sequences, which may escape to infinity in a noncompact manifold. Despite these difficulties, there are many important problems related to the existence of PMC hypersurfaces in noncompact manifolds.

A first example is a well-known question from
[
Yau82
, Problem 59]
: for which functions
h:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
does there exist an embedded
h
-PMC surface with prescribed genus? For some progress towards this problem in the case of genus zero, we refer the reader to
[
BK74
,
TW83
,
Yau97
]
. It is natural to consider the following modification of Yau’s problem, where we remove the restriction that the surface has prescribed genus and weaken the restriction of embedding to almost embedding

A second example is a natural synthesis of the main theorem of

1.1.
Main results

In this paper, we develop a localized min-max theory for complete finite area PMC hypersurfaces in complete noncompact manifolds. The following statement is a simplified version of our main result, where we postpone the precise min-max definitions to §
3.4
(see Theorem
9.5
for the most general result). For clarity, when we say there is a homotopy class of sweepouts with nontrivial width for
\mathcal{A}^{h}
, we mean that we are in a situation where Morse theory (or mountain pass) heuristics suggest the existence of a critical point of the functional.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete manifold of dimension
3\leq n+1\leq 7
. Let
h\in C^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(M)
be nonpositive outside a compact set. If there is a homotopy class of sweepouts of bounded finite perimeter sets with nontrivial width for
\mathcal{A}^{h}
, then there is a smooth, complete, finite area, almost embedded
h
-PMC hypersurface in
M
.

Theorem
1.3
is the first general existence result for PMC hypersurfaces in arbitrary complete noncompact manifolds (meaning we do not require
(M,g)
to have special structure).

Surprisingly, the fact that Theorem
1.3
works (with the sign restriction on the prescribing function
h
) boils down to the fact that negative real numbers
a
satisfy
-a>a
(and nonnegative numbers do not). To see exactly where this simple observation enters the proof, we refer the reader to the outline of the proof below (see equation (
1.1
)).

In the course of the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we require a min-max theorem for compact manifolds whose boundary is a good barrier. The following statement is a simplified version (see Theorem
7.6
for the most general result).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(N,\partial N,g)
be a compact manifold of dimension
3\leq n+1\leq 7
with smooth boundary. Let
h\in C^{\infty}(N)
satisfy
h\mid_{\partial N}<H_{\partial N}
. If there is a homotopy class of sweepouts of finite perimeter sets in
\mathrm{Int}(N)
with nontrivial width for
\mathcal{A}^{h}
, then there is a smooth, closed, almost embedded
h
-PMC hypersurface in
\mathrm{Int}(N)
.

The main novelty of Theorem
1.4
is that we only require a
one-sided
bound on
h
relative to the mean curvature of the boundary. This setting is more delicate because the possibility of boundary touching depends on the orientation of the hypersurface. For instance, a minimal boundary cannot be touched from the inside by a
1
-CMC hypersurface oriented
away
from the boundary, but it
can
be touched from the inside by a
1
-CMC hypersurface oriented
towards
the boundary (e.g. any sphere in Euclidean space has nonzero CMC but touches its tangent planes from one side).

We apply Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
to Questions
1.1
and
1.2
. Towards Question
1.1
, we have the following result.

Corollary 1.5
.

If
h\in C^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for
3\leq n+1\leq 7
satisfies

(1)

there exists a nonempty bounded finite perimeter set
\Lambda
satisfying
\mathcal{A}^{h}(\Lambda)\leq 0
,

(2)

h(x)\leq C|x|^{-1-\alpha}
for some
\alpha,\ C>0
,

then there is a smooth, closed, almost embedded
h
-PMC hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

We emphasize that condition (2) above is a one-sided bound, meaning that

Corollary

]

Towards Question

Corollary 1.6

Let

Since

Moreover, we completely resolve Question

Finally, we obtain a clean application to complete finite volume hyperbolic manifolds.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete finite volume hyperbolic manifold of dimension

.

1.3.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.4

It is tempting to try to use the maximum principle of
[
Whi10
]
after the pull tight step of the min-max program to ensure that every element in the critical set of a minimizing sequence is contained in
\mathrm{Int}(N)
. However, these elements are only known to be varifolds with bounds on the first variation of mass, meaning that we do not have any information about how they are
oriented
relative to
\partial N
. Since we only require the one-sided bound on
h
, this argument will not work if
|h|>H_{\partial N}
.

Instead, we carry out all of the steps of the min-max program as if we were doing min-max with an obstacle (as in
[
Wan22
]
). After proving the existence of a varifold in the critical set of a pulled tight minimizing sequence that is almost minimizing, we can use the regularity for the obstacle problem (see
[
Tam82
]
) to show that this varifold is
C^{1,\alpha}
. This regularity is sufficient to ensure that the hypersurface has the correct orientation at the boundary, at which point the classical Hopf lemma for elliptic second order operators applies to rule out boundary touching. Once we know that this hypersurface is contained in
\mathrm{Int}(N)
, the local regularity results from
[
ZZ20
]
apply unchanged.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

In §
2
, we establish the basic notations and conventions for the rest of the paper.

In Part
I
, we develop a localized min-max theory for PMC hypersurfaces in compact manifolds with good boundaries. In §
3
-
6
, we modify the standard min-max machinery to adapt to the setting with boundary. In §
7
we develop a new localization argument essential for the results in noncompact manifolds.

In Part
II
, we use the theory from Part

to prove a localized min-max theorem for PMC hypersurfaces in complete noncompact manifolds. In §

we prove the main general theorem of the paper. In §

we apply this theory to the problem of PMC hypersurfaces in Euclidean space. In §

we apply this theory to the problem of CMC hypersurfaces in finite volume manifolds.

\displaystyle\delta V(X)

\displaystyle=\int(-\mathrm{div}_{S}(-X_{0})+\mathrm{div}_{S}(\eta\langle X,\bar{\nu}\rangle\bar{\nu}))\ dV(x,S)

\displaystyle\leq c\|V\|(N)-\int(\mathrm{div}_{S}(\eta(1-\langle X,\bar{\nu}\rangle)\bar{\nu})+\mathrm{div}_{S}(\eta\bar{\nu}))\ dV(x,S)

Part I
Compact Manifolds with Barrier

In this part of the paper, we develop the tools for a localized min-max theorem in compact manifolds whose boundary provides a good barrier.

3.
Setup

Let

.

Let

3.1.
Space of subsets

We fix a disjoint decomposition by connected components

(Homotopy class)

4.
Preliminary tools

Here we collect the standard facts required for the min-max construction.

4.1.
Local regularity for obstacle problem

We recall that the dimension of
N
is assumed to be
3\leq n+1\leq 7
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
U\subset N
be relatively open. Suppose
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}_{E_{+},E_{-}}(N)
minimizes
\mathcal{A}^{h}
in
\{\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(\tilde{N})\mid\Omega\triangle\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset U\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{E_{+},E_{-}}(N)
. Then
\partial^{*}\Omega\cap U=\partial\Omega\cap U
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
embedded hypersurface in
U
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{C}(\tilde{N})
satisfy
\Omega\triangle\Omega^{\prime}\subset B_{r}(x)\cap N\subset U
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega)-\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{h}(\Omega)-\mathcal{A}^{h}(\Omega^{\prime})+\int_{\Omega}h-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}h

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}}h-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega}h

\displaystyle\leq c\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(B_{r}(x))

\displaystyle\leq c\omega r^{n+1},

where
\omega
is a constant depending on
(N,g)
and
r_{0}
for all

. In other words,
\Omega
is almost minimizing with respect to the obstacle
\partial N
. Hence, the arguments of
[
Tam82
, §3]
apply to give the desired regularity (working only in the assumed dimensions).
∎

When

is a barrier for

, we use the maximum principle and the above

regularity to show that local
\mathcal{A}^{h}
-minimizers lie in
\mathrm{Int}(N)
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
U\subset N
be relatively open. Suppose
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}_{E_{+},E_{-}}(N)
minimizes
\mathcal{A}^{h}
in
\{\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(\tilde{N})\mid\Omega\triangle\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset U\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{E_{+},E_{-}}(N)

is a barrier for

is a smooth embedded
\mathcal{A}^{h}
-stable hypersurface in

with

Proof.

By the standard interior regularity (see
[
ZZ20
, Theorem 2.2]
),
\partial\Omega\cap U\cap\mathrm{Int}(N)
is a smooth embedded stable
h
-PMC hypersurface. Suppose for contradiction that a component

We first claim that
\Sigma
must be identically
\partial N\cap U
. Suppose otherwise, so
\Sigma
contains a point in
\mathrm{Int}(N)\cap U
. By Theorem

, we can find a ball

and a relatively open set

is the graph of a

Since

∎

Let

8.2.

Suppose
E\subset M
is the complement of a precompact open subset with smooth boundary in
M
. We define

\mathscr{I}_{E}(v)=\inf\{\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega)\mid\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(M),\ \Omega\subset E,\ \mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega)=v\}.

Proposition 11.7
.

Suppose
E\subset M
is the complement of a precompact open subset with smooth boundary in
M
. Let
\delta\in(0,\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(E))
. Suppose there is a locally smooth function

\eta:(0,\delta)\to(0,+\infty)

satisfying
\lim_{t\to 0}\eta(t)=0
and
\mathscr{I}_{E}(v)\geq\eta(v)
for all
v\in(0,\delta)
. Then

\mathscr{D}(M,g)\geq\liminf_{t\to 0}\eta^{\prime}(t).

Proof.

If
\liminf_{t\to 0}\eta^{\prime}(t)=0
, then the conclusion is trivial, so we assume otherwise.

Since
\partial E
is smooth, compact, and separating, we can find a compact set
K\subset M
with smooth boundary so that

•

E\cup K=M
,

•

there is a diffeomorphism

\phi:\partial E\times[0,\varepsilon]\to E\cap K

satisfying
\phi(\partial E,t)=\{x\in E\mid d(x,\partial E)=t\}
.

Since
K
is compact with smooth boundary, by
[
Rit23
, Theorem 9.6]
there are constants
C_{K},\ v_{K}>0
so that for any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}(M)
with
\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Lambda\cap K)\leq v_{K}
, we have

(11.2)

\mathcal{H}^{n}((\partial^{*}\Lambda)\cap K)\geq C_{K}(\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Lambda\cap K))^{\frac{n}{n+1}}.

For
t\in[0,\varepsilon]
, we let
S_{t}=\phi(\partial E,t)
,
K_{t}=K\setminus\phi(\partial E,[t,\varepsilon])
, and
E_{t}=E\setminus\phi(\partial E,[0,t])
.

Fix
v\in(0,\delta)
. By
[
NR21
, Corollary 2.2]
(since we assume
(M,g)
has finite volume), we can find
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(M)
satisfying

\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega)=v\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega)=\mathscr{I}(v).

Case 1: Suppose
\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega\cap K)\geq v^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}
. By (
11.2
), we have (for
v
sufficiently small)

(11.3)

\mathscr{I}(v)=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n}((\partial^{*}\Omega)\cap K)\geq C_{K}v^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}\frac{n}{n+1}}=C_{K}v^{\frac{n^{2}+2n}{n^{2}+2n+1}}=:\eta_{1}(v).

Case 2: Suppose
\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega\cap K)\leq v^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}
. By the coarea formula in
E\cap K
, there is a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(E,K)>0
so that for some
t\in[0,\varepsilon]
(depending on
\Omega
), we have

(11.4)

\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega\cap S_{t})\leq c_{1}v^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}.

By assumption and the definition of
\mathscr{I}_{E}
, we have

(11.5)

\mathcal{H}^{n}((\partial^{*}\Omega)\cap E_{t})+\mathcal{H}^{n}(\Omega\cap S_{t})=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}(\Omega\cap E_{t}))\geq\eta(\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega\cap E_{t})).

Since
\liminf_{t\to 0}\eta^{\prime}(t)>0
,
\eta
is increasing for
v
sufficiently small. So for sufficiently small
v
, by (
11.4
) and (
11.5
), we have

(11.6)

\mathscr{I}(v)=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega)\geq\mathcal{H}^{n}((\partial^{*}\Omega)\cap E_{t})\geq\eta(v-v^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}})-c_{1}v^{\frac{n+2}{n+1}}=:\eta_{2}(v).

Hence, for
v
sufficiently small, we have

\mathscr{I}(v)\geq\min\{\eta_{1}(v),\eta_{2}(v)\},

where
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are as defined in (
11.3
) and (
11.6
).
Since

\liminf_{t\to 0}\eta_{1}^{\prime}(t)=+\infty\ \ \text{and}\ \ \liminf_{t\to 0}\eta_{2}^{\prime}(t)=\liminf_{t\to 0}\eta^{\prime}(t),

the conclusion follows by Proposition
11.6
.
∎

We now compute lower bounds for
\mathscr{D}
for a large class of finite volume manifolds with special structure outside a compact set.

Proposition 11.8
.

Let
(M^{n+1},g)
be a complete manifold with finite volume and dimension
3\leq n+1\leq 7
. Suppose there is a compact set
K\subset M
so that
M\setminus K
is isometric to

((A,\infty)\times N,\ \psi^{2}(t)dt^{2}\oplus\rho^{2}(t)g_{N})

for a fixed closed Riemannian manifold
(N^{n},g_{N})
of dimension
n
and locally smooth positive functions
\psi
and
\rho
. If
\rho^{\prime}<0
and
\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}
is nonincreasing, then

\mathscr{D}(M,g)\geq-n\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)\psi(t)}.

Proof.

In the provided coordinates, we let
E=[A+1,\infty)\times N
.

Take any
v\in(0,\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(E))
. Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}(M)
with
\Omega\subset E
satisfy (by
[
NR21
, Corollary 2.2]
)

\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega)=v\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega)=\mathscr{I}_{E}(v).

Let
\Omega_{t}=\Omega\cup([t,\infty)\times N)
. Since
\Omega
has finite perimeter and
M
has finite volume, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega_{t})=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega_{t})=\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega_{t}).

Let
O_{t}
be the subset of the form
[\tau_{t},\infty)\times N
so that
\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(O_{t})=\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega_{t})
.

Let

\omega=\rho^{n}\mathrm{vol}_{N}.

Then

d\omega=n\rho^{n-1}\rho^{\prime}dt\wedge\mathrm{vol}_{N}=n\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}\mathrm{vol}_{M}.

By Stokes’ theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial O_{t})-\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[\partial O_{t}]}\omega-\int_{[\partial^{*}\Omega_{t}]}\omega

\displaystyle=\int_{[O_{t}]-[\Omega_{t}]}d\omega

\displaystyle=\int_{O_{t}\setminus\Omega_{t}}n\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}-\int_{\Omega_{t}\setminus O_{t}}n\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}

Since
O_{t}
has the same volume as
\Omega_{t}
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(O_{t}\setminus\Omega_{t})=\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega_{t}\setminus O_{t})
. Moreover, by assumption,

n\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}\Big|_{O_{t}\setminus\Omega_{t}}\leq n\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}\Big|_{\Omega_{t}\setminus O_{t}}.

Hence, we have

\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial O_{t})\leq\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega_{t}).

Taking
t\to\infty
, we have

\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial O)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial^{*}\Omega),

where
O
is the subset of the form
[\tau,\infty)\times N
with
\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(O)=\mathcal{H}^{n+1}(\Omega)=v
, so

\mathscr{I}_{E}(v)=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial O).

In other words, the areas and volumes of the subsets of the form
[\tau,\infty)\times N
provide a lower bound for
\mathscr{I}_{E}
.

Let
a(t)=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\{t\}\times N)
and
v(t)=\mathcal{H}^{n+1}([t,\infty)\times N)
. If
t(v)
denotes the inverse function of
v(t)
, we have

\eta(v)=a(t(v))

is a lower bound for
\mathscr{I}_{E}
. By the coarea formula and the formula for the derivative of an inverse function, we have

\eta^{\prime}(v)=\frac{n\rho^{n-1}\rho^{\prime}}{-\rho^{n}\psi}=-n\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}.

The conclusion follows by Proposition
11.7
.
∎

We now compute lower bounds for
\mathscr{D}
for a few examples.

Example 11.9

(Finite volume hyperbolic manifolds)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete hyperbolic manifold with finite volume. By the structure theorem for these manifolds (see
[
Thu97
, Chapter 4.5]
), there is a compact set
K\subset M
, a constant
A>0
, and a closed Riemannian manifold
(N,g_{N})
(a union of flat tori and flat Klein bottles) so that
M\setminus K
is isometric to

((A,\infty)\times N,t^{-2}dt^{2}\oplus t^{-2}g_{N}).

In this case,
\psi(t)=\rho(t)=t^{-1}
. We see that

\rho^{\prime}=-t^{-2}<0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}=-1\ \ \text{is nonincreasing}.

By Proposition
11.8
, we have

\mathscr{D}(M,g)\geq n.

By Theorem
11.4
, we have Corollary
1.7
.

Example 11.10

(
\mathscr{D}=+\infty
)
.

Let
(N,g_{N})
be a closed Riemannian manifold. Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold isometric to

((A,\infty)\times N,dt^{2}\oplus e^{-2t^{1+\alpha}}g_{N})

outside a compact set, where
\alpha>0
.
Completeness follows from construction, and the fact that
M
has finite volume follows because

\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-nt^{1+\alpha}}dt\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-nt}dt=\frac{-1}{n}e^{-nt}\Big|_{1}^{\infty}=\frac{e^{-n}}{n}.

In this case, we have
\psi(t)=1
and
\rho(t)=e^{-t^{1+\alpha}}
. We compute

\rho^{\prime}=-(1+\alpha)t^{\alpha}e^{-t^{1+\alpha}}<0

and

\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho\psi}=-(1+\alpha)t^{\alpha}\ \ \text{is nonincreasing}.

By Proposition
11.8
, we have

\mathscr{D}(M,g)\geq\lim_{t\to\infty}n(1+\alpha)t^{\alpha}=+\infty.

By Theorem
11.4
, we can find a smooth complete almost embedded finite area
c
-CMC hypersurface in
M
for any
0<c<\infty
.

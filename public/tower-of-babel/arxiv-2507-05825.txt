On the vanishing of 𝖤𝗑𝗍 \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}} and 𝖳𝗈𝗋 \operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}
Abdolnaser Bahlekeh and Shokrollah Salarian

Abstract
. This paper contains two theorems concerning the vanishing of natural transformations of (co)homology functors. Precisely, assume that R R is a right noetherian ring and f : M → N f:M\rightarrow N is a morphism of finitely generated right R R -modules. The first theorem proves that the natural transformation 𝖤𝗑𝗍 1 ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-) vanishes over the category of finitely generated right R R -modules if and only if 𝖳𝗈𝗋 1 ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-) vanishes over the category of finitely generated left R R -modules. As a corollary of this result, we establish that 𝖤𝗑𝗍 1 ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-) is epic if and only if 𝖳𝗈𝗋 1 ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-) is monic. The second theorem shows that if R R is left and right noetherian and M , N M,N are Gorenstein projective, then the natural transformations 𝖳𝗈𝗋 1 ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-) , 𝖤𝗑𝗍 1 ⁡ ( − , f ) \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(-,f) and 𝖤𝗑𝗍 1 ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-) vanish over the category of finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules, simultaneously. This, in particular, yields that over Gorenstein projective modules, the notions of phantom morphisms and 𝖤𝗑𝗍 \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}} -phantom morphisms coincide. Also, it is proved that if R R is n n -Gorenstein, then for any integer i > n i>n , the natural transformations 𝖤𝗑𝗍 i ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(f,-) , 𝖤𝗑𝗍 i ⁡ ( − , f ) \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(-,f) and 𝖳𝗈𝗋 i ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{i}(f,-) vanish over finitely generated modules, simultaneously. As an interesting consequence, we show that under the same assumptions, 𝖤𝗑𝗍 i ⁡ ( − , f ) \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(-,f) is epic (resp. monic) if and only if 𝖤𝗑𝗍 i ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(f,-) is monic (resp. epic) if and only if 𝖳𝗈𝗋 i ⁡ ( f , − ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{i}(f,-) is epic (resp. monic).

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper
R
is a right noetherian ring, and unless specified otherwise, all modules are assumed to be finitely generated right
R
-modules. We use
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}R
(resp.
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}R^{op}
) to denote the category of all finitely generated right (resp. left)
R
-modules.

Assume that
f:M\rightarrow N
is a morphism in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}R
. This paper deals with the vanishing of the natural transformations
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-)
,
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
and
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(-,f)
. The study of the vanishing of such natural transformations stems from the notion of phantom morphisms. A given morphism
g:X\rightarrow Y
of (not necessarily finitely generated) right
R
-modules is called a phantom morphism, if the natural transformation
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(g,-):\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}^{R}_{1}(X,-)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}^{R}_{1}(Y,-)
is zero, or equivalently, the pullback of any short exact sequence along
g
is pure exact. Similarly,
g
is called an
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphism, if for any finitely presented right
R
-module
L
, the induced morphism of abelian groups
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(L,g):\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}_{R}(L,X)\rightarrow\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}_{R}(L,Y)
is zero.
The study of phantom morphisms has its roots in topology in the study of maps between CW-complexes
[
15
]
. A map
h:X\rightarrow Y
between CW-complexes is said to be a phantom map if its restriction to each skeleton
X_{n}
is null homotopic. For the historical information on phantom maps see Remark
2.15
.

This paper proves two theorems that compare the vanishing of natural transformations of (co)homology functors, over finitely generated modules. Precisely, our first theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a given morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}R
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
vanishes over
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}R
.

(2)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-)
vanishes over
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}R^{op}
.

It is worth noting that this theorem can be viewed as the morphism version of
[
9
, Proposition 3.2.12]
and
[
17
, Corollary 3.57]
, asserting that over a noetherian ring, a finitely generated flat module is projective. Using Theorem
1.1
, we prove that the natural transformation
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
is epic if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-)
is monic, and also, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-)
is epic, then
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
will be monic, see Corollary
2.3
. We should point out that the dimension shifting argument implies that all the assertions in Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
2.3
still remain true for the natural transformations
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(f,-)
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{i}(f,-)
for any integer
i\geq 1
, see Corollaries
2.1
and
2.4
.

Our second theorem indicates that if
f:M\rightarrow N
is a morphism in
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
, the category of all finitely generated Gorenstein projective right
R
-modules, then the natural transformations
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-)
,
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
and
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(-,f)
vanish, simultaneously. Indeed, we prove the result below.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R
be a left and right noetherian ring and let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a morphism in
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-)=0
over
\mathsf{Gp}(R^{op})
.

(2)

\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)=0
over
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
.

(3)

\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(-,f)=0
over
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
.

It is known that over the category of Gorenstein projective modules, projective objects coincide with the injective objects. The above theorem, not only ensures the validity of the morphism version of this fact but also shows that in this setting the notions of phantom morphisms and
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphisms coincide. Moreover, Theorem
1.2
enables us to show that, with the same assumptions, the natural transformations
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
,
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(-,f)
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-)
are equivalence over Gorenstein projective modules, simultaneously, see Corollary
2.10
. Also, as an interesting result, we show that if
R
is an
n
-Gorenstein ring, then for a given morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}R
and an integer
i>n
, the natural transformations
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(f,-)
,
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(-,f)
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{i}(f,-)
vanish, simultaneously, see Proposition
2.11
. As an application of this result, it is proved that, under the same assumptions,

\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(-,f)
is epic (resp. monic) if and only if
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}(f,-)
is monic (resp. epic) if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{i}(f,-)
is epic (resp. monic), see Corollaries
2.12
and
2.13
. These results should be compared with the ones in
[
14
, Section 2]
.

2.5
.

Gorenstein projective modules.

Assume that
R
is a left and right noetherian ring.
A given (finitely generated)
R
-module
M
is called Gorenstein projective, if it is the zeroth syzygy of an acyclic complex of (finitely generated) projective
R
-modules
\rm{P}^{\bullet}
that remains exact under
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(-,Q)
for any (finitely generated) projective
R
-module
Q
. In this case, the sequence
\rm{P}^{\bullet}
is called a complete projective resolution of
M
.

The concept of (finitely generated) Gorenstein projective modules, which is a refinement of projective modules, originated in the works of Auslander and Bridger
[
1
]
, who introduced modules of G-dimension zero. Indeed, a finitely generated
R
-module
M
is said to have G-dimension zero, if it is reflexive (i.e. the natural map
M\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R^{op}}(M^{*},R)
is an isomorphism), and
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}_{R}(M,R)=0=\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{i}_{R^{op}}(M^{*},R)
for all
i>0
, where
(-)^{*}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{R}(-,R)
. It is well known that, over the left and right noetherian ring, a finitely generated module is Gorenstein projective if and only if it has G-dimension zero, see
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
and also
[
9
, Proposition 10.2.6]
. Enochs and Jenda
[
8
]
generalized the notion of Gorenstein projectivity to not necessarily finitely generated modules over arbitrary rings. Gorenstein projective modules have found important applications in many areas including commutative algebra, algebraic geometry, singularity theory, and relative homological algebra

In the remainder of the paper,
R
is assumed to be a left and right noetherian ring. Also, the category of all (finitely generated) Gorenstein projective
R
-modules will be denoted by
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
.

Now we are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.2
. First, we show that for a given morphism of Gorenstein projective
R
-modules
f:M\rightarrow N
, the natural transformation
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
vanishes over
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
if and only if the same is true for
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(-,f)
. This, in particular, examines the validity of the morphism version of the known fact
that over the category of Gorenstein projective modules, injectives, and projectives are the same.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a morphism in
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
. Then the natural transformations
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
and
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(-,f)
vanish on
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
, simultaneously.

Proof.

First assume that
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)
vanishes over
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
. Hence, as observed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
f
factors through a projective module. Now since projectives are also injective objects of
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
, one deduces that
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(-,f)=0
over
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
. For the converse, one may take a syzygy sequence
\beta:0\rightarrow M\rightarrow Q\rightarrow\mathsf{\Omega}^{-1}M\rightarrow 0
in
\mathsf{Gp}(R)
. By our assumption, the morphism
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(\mathsf{\Omega}^{-1}M,f):\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}_{R}(\mathsf{\Omega}^{-1}M,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}_{R}(\mathsf{\Omega}^{-1}M,N)
is zero. This leads us to infer that the push-out of
\beta
along
f
,
f\beta
, splits. Hence
f
factors through
Q
, and so,
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(f,-)=0
. Thus the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the vanishing of the natural transformations of (co)homology functors have a close connection with the notions of phantom and
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphisms. The concept of phantom morphisms has its roots in topology in the study of maps between CW-complexes
[
15
]
. A map
f:X\rightarrow Y
between CW-complexes is said to be a phantom map if its restriction to each skeleton
X_{n}
is null homotopic. Later, this notion has been used in various settings of mathematics. In the context of triangulated categories, phantom morphisms were first studied by Neeman
[
16
]
. The notion of phantom morphisms was also developed in the stable category of a finite group ring in a series of works by Benson and Gnacadja
[
11
,
5
,
4
,
3
]
. The definition of a phantom morphism was generalized to the category of
R
-modules over an associative ring
R
by Herzog
[
12
]
. Precisely, a morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
of (not necessarily finitely generated) right
R
-modules is called a phantom morphism if the natural transformation
\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}(f,-):\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}^{R}_{1}(M,-)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{Tor}}^{R}_{1}(N,-)
is zero, or equivalently, the pullback of any short exact sequence along
f
is pure exact. Similarly,
f
is called an
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphism, if for any finitely presented
R
-module
X
, the induced morphism
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}(X,f):\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}_{R}(X,M)\rightarrow\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}^{1}_{R}(X,N)
is zero. So Theorem
1.2
reveals that over the category of Gorenstein projective modules, the notions of phantom morphisms and
\operatorname{{\mathsf{Ext}}}
-phantom morphisms coincide.

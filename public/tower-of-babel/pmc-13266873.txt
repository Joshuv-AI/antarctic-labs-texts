Mitral valve surgery combined with on-pump coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) versus off-pump or on-pump beating heart CABG: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Huixiang Su, Yinfan Zhu, Haiyang Hu, Xiaohan Zhong, Lu Dai, Qianxian Li, Hongjia Zhang, Haiyang Li, Yuyong Liu, Dongjie Li, Wenjian Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundConcomitant mitral valve surgery and coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) remains the standard treatment for patients with severe mitral valve disease complicated by coronary artery disease. However, this combined procedure is associated with substantial perioperative risk. While off-pump or on-pump beating heart CABG may reduce aortic cross-clamp and cardiopulmonary bypass times compared with conventional on-pump arrested-heart CABG, evidence comparing these surgical strategies in combined mitral valve and CABG surgery remains limited. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the short-term outcomes of mitral valve surgery combined with on-pump arrested-heart CABG versus off-pump or on-pump beating heart CABG.MethodsA total of 473 patients who underwent concomitant CABG and mitral valve surgery were retrospectively analyzed. Among these patients, 368 patients underwent on-pump arrested-heart CABG (ONCABG group) and 105 patients underwent off-pump or on-pump beating heart CABG (BHCABG group). Covariate balancing propensity score (CBPS) and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was utilized to reduce bias. The primary endpoints were in-hospital mortality and major perioperative complications.ResultsAfter CBPS and IPTW adjustment, the BHCABG group demonstrated significantly shorter aortic cross-clamp times [ratio =0.765; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.695–0.843; P<0.001] compared with the ONCABG group, lower peak high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hsTnI) levels (P=0.01) and serum creatinine levels (P<0.001). Notably, the risk of new-onset stroke was significantly reduced in the BHCABG group [odds ratio (OR) =0.063; 95% CI: 0.008–0.490; P=0.008]. No statistically significant differences were observed in operative time, mechanical ventilation duration, or in-hospital mortality between the two groups.ConclusionsIn patients undergoing combined mitral valve surgery and surgical coronary revascularization, off-pump revascularization or on-pump beating heart strategy is associated with attenuated myocardial injury, renal injury and a lower risk of postoperative stroke compared with the conventional on-pump technique.Keywords: Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), beating heart, arrested heart, mitral valve surgery

Key findings

• Compared with the conventional on-pump coronary artery bypass grafting (ONCABG) group, the beating heart coronary artery bypass grafting (BHCABG) group significantly shortened the aortic cross-clamp (ACC) time. Patients in the BHCABG group exhibited markedly lower peak high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hsTnI) levels and serum creatinine levels, indicating attenuated myocardial and renal injury. After adjustment using propensity score analysis, the risk of new-onset stroke was significantly reduced in the BHCABG group. There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups regarding in-hospital mortality, the length of mechanical ventilation.

What is known and what is new?

• Concomitant mitral valve surgery and CABG is the standard treatment for complex valvular and coronary disease, but it remains associated with high perioperative mortality. Conventional ONCABG involves cardiopulmonary bypass and cardioplegic arrest, which are linked to systemic inflammation and ischemia-reperfusion injury.

• This study demonstrates that BHCABG combined with mitral valve surgery effectively mitigates perioperative injury to the heart, kidneys, and brain. Notably, it reveals a significant neuroprotective advantage of the BHCABG strategy in this high-risk patient population.

Introduction

Concomitant coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) and mitral valve surgery (repair or replacement) remains the standard treatment for patients with severe mitral disease complicated by coronary artery disease (CAD) (1). However, this combined procedure is associated with substantial perioperative risks, with reported in-hospital mortality rates of 8.7% to 11.2% (2). Increased mortality has been linked to advanced age, multiple comorbidities, prolonged operative duration, aortic cross-clamp (ACC) time, cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) time, and other relevant factors (3,4).

Generally, the on-pump arrested-heart CABG technique is utilized during these complex procedures. While, off-pump CABG and on-pump beating heart CABG (beating heart CABG) are two others commonly used surgical techniques for surgical coronary revascularization, which would reduce ACC time and CPB time, theoretically reducing perioperative organ injury and postoperative complications. However, few studies have compared the outcomes of on-pump CABG and beating heart CABG procedures in combined mitral valve and CABG surgery.

Thus, this study aims to compare the early postoperative outcomes between on-pump arrested-heart CABG and beating heart CABG (off-pump or on-pump beating heart) techniques in patients undergoing combined mitral valve and CABG surgery. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0358/rc).

Methods

Study design

This was a single-center retrospective cohort study. The study was designed to compare the short-term in-hospital outcomes of patients undergoing two different surgical revascularization strategies. All baseline clinical characteristics, operative details, and postoperative outcome data were retrieved from the electronic medical record (EMR) system and laboratory information system (LIS) of Beijing Anzhen Hospital. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Beijing Anzhen Hospital (No. KS2023090) and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived.

Study population

Between January 2018 and December 2024, a total of 473 patients undergoing mitral valve surgery combined with coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) at Beijing Anzhen Hospital were included.

Patients were excluded if they presented with a history of prior cardiac surgery, concomitant aortic valve surgery, surgery for congenital heart disease, active infective endocarditis, or incomplete clinical data. The severity of mitral valve disease was assessed in accordance with the 2020 American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association Guideline for the Management of Patients With Valvular Heart Disease. The choice between mitral valve repair or replacement was determined intraoperatively based on leaflet morphology, mobility, and subvalvular apparatus involvement.

According to the surgical technique actually performed, 105 patients underwent off-pump or on-pump beating heart CABG (BHCABG group) combined with mitral valve surgery, and 368 patients underwent conventional on-pump arrested-heart (ONCABG group) combined with mitral valve surgery.

Outcome measures

The primary endpoint was in-hospital mortality. Secondary endpoints included postoperative adverse events and complications. Multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) was defined as dysfunction of two or more organ systems according to established clinical criteria. Incision infection was diagnosed based on clinical signs and microbiological evidence. Hepatic failure was defined as postoperative elevation of liver enzymes with coagulopathy or hyperbilirubinemia. Acute kidney injury (AKI) was diagnosed according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria, and the requirement for continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) was recorded. Postoperative stroke was defined as a new-onset neurological deficit confirmed by imaging. Postoperative myocardial infarction was diagnosed based on elevated cardiac biomarkers with electrocardiographic or imaging evidence. Re-exploration referred to surgical reopening for bleeding or other complications. Postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) was defined as new-onset atrial fibrillation documented by electrocardiography during hospitalization. In addition, postoperative serum creatinine levels, peak high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I (hsTnI) levels, B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) levels within 48 hours postoperatively, as well as left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) assessed by echocardiography before hospital discharge, blood product transfusion status, duration of mechanical ventilation, length of intensive care unit (ICU) stay, as well as first-day drainage volume and total drainage volume were collected and compared in this study.

Surgical techniques

All surgical procedures were performed at a single cardiac surgery center by three senior surgeons, each with more than 5 years of experience in both CABG and mitral valve surgery. All surgical procedures were performed via median sternotomy. The protocols for mitral valve replacement (MVR) or repair (MVr) were standardized across both groups.

BHCABG technique

BHCABG was performed using either an off-pump or on-pump beating heart approach. Regardless of the surgical strategy, full systemic heparinization was routinely administered, and the activated clotting time (ACT) was maintained above 480 seconds to allow immediate conversion to CPB if required. Preoperative transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) was performed in all patients to evaluate mitral valve pathology and guide surgical planning. Prior to coronary revascularization, purse-string sutures for potential CPB establishment were routinely placed in advance, including the ascending aorta or femoral artery and both the superior and inferior vena cava.

The off-pump CABG procedure was performed first. Proximal anastomoses were constructed before distal coronary anastomoses using standard techniques. Prior to anastomosis, a surgical suture was utilized as a reference along the planned anatomical trajectory to determine the appropriate graft length, thereby preventing postoperative graft kinking or excessive tension. Proximal anastomoses on the ascending aorta were performed using an aortic punch combined with the HEARTSTRING Proximal Seal System. This approach circumvented the use of traditional side-biting clamps, aiming to reduce mechanical manipulation of the aorta and the associated risk of atheroembolism. Overall, 70 patients underwent the procedure using an off-pump strategy, the remaining 35 patients required intraoperative conversion to on-pump beating-heart CABG due to hemodynamic instability, severe cardiomegaly, or inadequate surgical exposure during cardiac positioning and target vessel isolation.

After completion of coronary revascularization, the aortic cross clamp is applied, then myocardial perfusion is maintained by antegrade aortic root perfusion, allowing both native coronary and graft perfusion. Mitral valve surgery was subsequently performed according to the underlying valve pathology and surgeon discretion, with either valve repair or replacement using a bioprosthetic or mechanical prosthesis. Following completion of all procedures, patients were weaned from CPB when applicable, and the operation was finished in a standard fashion.

ONCABG technique

In the ONCABG group, full systemic heparinization was achieved prior to cannulation, followed by establishment of CPB via ascending aortic or femoral artery, superior and inferior vena cava. The specific establishment process of CPB was as follows: the ascending aorta was sutured with double purse-string sutures using 4-0 Prolene for arterial cannulation, and the puncture site for the aortic root perfusion needle was sutured with a single purse-string suture using 4-0 Prolene. For venous drainage, the superior and inferior vena cava were sutured with single or semi-purse-string sutures using 4-0 Prolene, respectively. The right atrium and the designated left heart venting area were sutured with purse-string sutures using 4-0 Prolene. All purse-string sutures were passed through tourniquet snares and properly secured.

Incisions and punctures were made at the center of each purse-string. After the arterial cannula was inserted into the ascending aorta, it was tied and fixed with heavy braided polyester ties and connected to the arterial supply line of the CPB. Right-angled venous cannula were inserted into the superior and inferior vena cava, secured by tightening the snares, and connected to the venous drainage lines. A left heart vent was inserted to achieve optimal cardiac decompression, and the cardioplegia perfusion needle was inserted into the aortic root and connected to the delivery line.

After initiation of CPB, the ascending aorta was subsequently cross-clamped, and cardiac arrest was achieved using intermittent antegrade cold blood or Histidine‑Tryptophan‑Ketoglutarate solution (HTK) cardioplegia. Then CABG was performed first on the arrested heart, distal coronary anastomoses were constructed under cardioplegic arrest. Upon completion of distal grafting, cardioplegic solution was administered via the bypass grafts to ensure homogeneous myocardial protection. Mitral valve surgery was then performed through a standard left atrial approach via the interatrial groove. Depending on the valve pathology and intraoperative findings, mitral valve repair or replacement was undertaken using conventional techniques. After completion of the mitral valve procedure, the left atrium was closed. Subsequently, proximal aortic anastomoses of the bypass grafts were performed during the rewarming phase. After satisfactory de-airing, the ACC was removed, and patients were weaned from CPB in a routine manner.

Postoperative management

Postoperative management followed a standardized ICU protocol. Routine pharmacotherapy comprised prophylactic antibiotics, warfarin, aspirin, statins, β-blockers, proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), diuretics, and angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors. Hemodynamic support with inotropes, vasopressors, or nitroglycerin was titrated according to clinical requirements.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.1). The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, whereas non-normally distributed variables are expressed as median with interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are summarized as frequencies and percentages.

Control for confounding: given the retrospective observational nature of this study, to control for potential confounding factors and baseline imbalances between groups, this study employed the inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) method based on the covariate balancing propensity score (CBPS).

CBPS extends traditional methodologies by utilizing a Generalized Method of Moments (GMM) framework. It simultaneously estimates the conditional probability of receiving the target treatment and incorporates covariate balance conditions directly into the model estimation process. By directly optimizing multi-dimensional covariate balance during the propensity score calculation phase, CBPS reduces reliance on precise model specification and enhances the robustness of causal effect estimation.

Subsequently, the IPTW method was used to construct a weighted pseudo-population. Since most study participants were assigned weights greater than 1, the relative composition of the cohort was statistically reconfigured, thereby simulating an ideal randomized state with balanced covariate distributions between groups. To prevent a small number of cases with extreme weights from exerting a dominant influence on the analytical results, the calculated weights were strictly trimmed at the 1st and 99th percentiles. Standardized mean differences (SMDs) were used to assess covariate balance before and after weighting, with an absolute SMD <0.2 indicating an optimal balance status.

Comparative analyses: in the CBPS weighting cohort, continuous variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Subgroup and interaction analyses: subgroup analyses were performed in this study for off-pump CABG and on-pump beating heart CABG. The relevant results are presented in the Table S1. No interaction effects were evaluated.

Missing data: there were no missing data for the primary and secondary outcomes.

Loss to follow up: as this was a retrospective cohort study based on in-hospital and perioperative data, no loss to follow up occurred for the outcomes of interest.

Sensitivity analyses: sensitivity analyses were conducted to examine the robustness of the primary findings. These analyses included repeating the main analyses under alternative model specifications and after excluding selected patient subgroups. The results of sensitivity analyses were consistent with those of the primary analysis.

Mitigation of bias: as this was a non-randomized retrospective study, we employed CBPS weighting. This method is more robust than traditional propensity score matching, optimizes covariate balance, reduces the risk of model misspecification, and approximates a randomized controlled trial (RCT) design. All patients were managed according to a standardized postoperative ICU clinical pathway. Laboratory tests were performed at fixed time points, regardless of surgical group assignment. To ensure data integrity and reduce information bias, clinical data were extracted from the EMR system and cross-validated by two independent investigators.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 473 patients were included in the analysis, including 368 patients in the ONCABG group and 105 patients in the BHCABG group. Before adjustment, there were no statistically significant differences between the two groups regarding demographic characteristics or the prevalence of comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and history of stroke (all P>0.05). However, patients in the BHCABG group exhibited a significantly higher prevalence of history of acute myocardial infarction (24.8% vs. 13.0%, P=0.006), prior percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) (23.8% vs. 13.0%, P=0.01), and unstable angina (53.3% vs. 41.8%, P=0.048) compared with the ONCABG group. In contrast, baseline total cholesterol levels (median 3.99 vs. 4.37, P=0.001) and preoperative LVEF (55.39% vs. 58.27%, P=0.005) were significantly lower in the BHCABG group. After covariate balancing using CBPS and IPTW, the effective sample sizes were 497.7 in the ONCABG group and 491.6 in the BHCABG group. All baseline covariates achieved a clinically acceptable level of balance, with SMDs <0.2, indicating effective control of baseline confounding factors (Table 1, Figures 1,2).

Clinical outcomes before and after statistical adjustment

Regarding clinical outcomes across the three analytical models, no significant differences were observed between the ONCABG and BHCABG groups in terms of in-hospital mortality and major postoperative complications; the in-hospital mortality rates were comparable (7.1% vs. 7.6%, P>0.99).

In the unadjusted analysis of perioperative parameters, the BHCABG group had a significantly shorter median ACC time [81.00 (61.00, 105.00) vs. 111.50 (93.75, 140.00) min, P<0.001] and CPB time [152.00 (123.00, 168.00) vs. 159.00 (130.75, 189.00) min, P=0.009]. Regarding laboratory findings, both peak hsTnI levels [341.59 (171.30, 608.42) vs. 557.16 (317.94, 897.42) × the upper limit of normal (ULN), P<0.001] and postoperative creatinine [94.20 (80.80, 112.70) vs. 102.55 (85.90, 137.35) µmol/L, P=0.001] were significantly lower in the BHCABG group than in the ONCABG group.

After adjusting for confounding factors using CBPS + IPTW, the incidence of postoperative stroke was significantly lower in the BHCABG group than in the ONCABG group (0.4% vs. 5.4%, P<0.001). In terms of perioperative surgical metrics, the BHCABG group consistently demonstrated a significantly shorter ACC time [87.52 (62.00, 106.00) vs. 109.89 (91.00, 140.00) min, P<0.001] and CPB time [144.00 (121.51, 158.00) vs. 160.08 (131.00, 192.00) min, P=0.001] compared to the ONCABG group. For laboratory parameters, peak hsTnI levels [388.37 (162.54, 611.99) vs. 530.51 (301.50, 882.23) ×ULN, P=0.001] and postoperative creatinine [90.31 (79.05, 105.19) vs. 100.81 (84.10, 138.41) µmol/L, P<0.001] remained significantly lower in the BHCABG group.

Following multivariable adjustment, the incidence of postoperative stroke remained significantly lower in the BHCABG group than in the ONCABG group (1.0% vs. 4.9%, P=0.009). Among perioperative surgical metrics, only ACC time remained significantly shorter in the BHCABG group compared to the ONCABG group [81.00 (61.00, 105.00) vs. 111.50 (93.75, 140.00) min, P<0.001]. Regarding laboratory parameters, both peak hsTnI [341.59 (171.30, 608.42) vs. 557.16 (317.94, 897.42) ×ULN, P=0.001] and postoperative creatinine [94.20 (80.80, 112.70) vs. 102.55 (85.90, 137.35) µmol/L, P<0.001] were still significantly lower in the BHCABG group (Table 2).

Furthermore, to investigate the impact of different surgical strategies, a subgroup analysis was conducted by stratifying patients into the ONCABG, on-pump beating-heart CABG, and off-pump CABG groups. The results indicated no significant differences among the three groups in the incidences of in-hospital mortality (P=0.16), MODS (P=0.29), postoperative stroke (P=0.13), and postoperative renal insufficiency (P=0.93). However, the incidence of postoperative myocardial infarction differed significantly across the groups (overall P=0.02). Pairwise comparisons revealed that the on-pump beating-heart group had a significantly higher incidence of myocardial infarction compared to the ONCABG group (8.6% vs. 1.4%, P=0.02) and the off-pump group (8.6% vs. 0.0%, P=0.03). Regarding perioperative parameters, both beating-heart subgroups demonstrated significantly shorter median ACC times compared to the ONCABG group [on-pump beating-heart: 92.00 (62.00, 107.50) min; off-pump: 79.00 (61.00, 105.00) min vs. ONCABG: 111.50 (93.75, 140.00) min; both P<0.001]. Effect estimates for clinical outcomes across three analytical models

In the unadjusted analysis, no significant statistical associations were observed between the two groups regarding in-hospital mortality [odds ratio (OR) =1.09; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.48–2.48; P=0.84], MODS (OR =1.39; 95% CI: 0.64–2.98; P=0.40), and most other postoperative complications. However, BHCABG was significantly associated with a shorter ACC time (ratio =0.75; 95% CI: 0.69–0.81; P<0.001), lower peak hsTnI levels (ratio = 0.75; 95% CI: 0.60–0.94; P=0.01), and lower postoperative creatinine (ratio =0.85; 95% CI: 0.76–0.95; P=0.004).

After adjustment using IPTW and CBPS, in-hospital mortality remained comparable between the two groups (OR =0.87; 95% CI: 0.33–2.29; P=0.77). Notably, the BHCABG group demonstrated a remarkably lower risk of postoperative stroke (OR =0.06; 95% CI: 0.01–0.49; P=0.008). Concurrently, its significant advantages regarding ACC time (ratio =0.77; P<0.001), peak hsTnI levels (ratio =0.75; P=0.01), and postoperative creatinine (ratio =0.79; P<0.001) were robustly maintained.

Results from multivariable regression analyses were consistent with those of the weighted analyses. No significant difference was observed in in-hospital mortality between the two groups (OR =0.55; 95% CI: 0.19–1.63; P=0.28). Compared with ONCABG, BHCABG remained an independent protective factor against postoperative stroke, significantly reducing the risk by 92% (OR =0.08; 95% CI: 0.01–0.51; P=0.007). Furthermore, ACC time (ratio =0.75; 95% CI: 0.69–0.81; P<0.001), peak hsTnI levels (ratio =0.68; 95% CI: 0.56–0.83; P<0.001), and postoperative creatinine (ratio =0.83; 95% CI: 0.78–0.89; P<0.001) were all significantly lower in the BHCABG group than in the ONCABG group (Table 3).

Discussion

This retrospective study analyzed the short-term outcomes between BHCABG and conventional ONCABG concomitant with mitral valve surgery in patients with CAD and mitral valve surgery. Our results demonstrated significant advantages of the beating heart strategy (BHCABG combined with mitral surgery) over the conventional on-pump approach regarding intraoperative metrics and short-term outcomes. Specifically, the BHCABG group exhibited significantly shortened ACC times, lower peak postoperative troponin levels, reduced serum creatinine levels, and a significantly lower incidence of new-onset stroke. These findings suggest that a beating heart revascularization strategy combined with mitral valve surgery may mitigate the risk of perioperative injury to vital organs, including the heart, kidneys, and brain.

A notable concern when comparing surgical strategies for concomitant CABG and valve procedures is the potential selection bias related to coronary anatomical complexity. Traditionally, surgeons tend to reserve beating-heart techniques for relatively simple coronary lesions. To address this, our study utilized several robust clinical surrogates—including the presence of left main disease, a history of prior PCI, and the total number of bypass grafts—to delineate the complexity of CAD. Notably, our data demonstrated that the BHCABG cohort actually presented with a more complex coronary anatomical burden. This counterintuitive finding may be attributable to the specific surgical expertise and technical preference of the operating surgeons. By integrating these critical variables into our adjustment model, we effectively mitigated the confounding influence of coronary complexity on postoperative outcomes. This robust statistical adjustment ensures that the observed clinical advantages of the BHCABG strategy remain independent of the baseline anatomical severity of the coronary lesions.

It should be emphasized that the observed benefits of the beating heart strategy in the present study are context-dependent and may not be generalized to all CABG populations. Our cohort represents a distinct high-risk subgroup characterized by severe mitral disease, impaired ventricular loading conditions, and the necessity for concomitant valve intervention. The absence of significant differences in in-hospital mortality between the two groups is not unexpected, given the relatively limited sample size and the short follow-up period. However, early surrogate markers of organ injury, including myocardial biomarkers and renal function indices, may be more sensitive in capturing the perioperative benefits of surgical strategy selection.

The significant reduction in ACC time observed in the BHCABG group is a critical factor, as prolonged aortic cross-clamping is closely correlated with perioperative organ dysfunction (5,6). Conventional ONCABG necessitates CPB with cardioplegic arrest to provide a motionless operative field, this kind of myocardial ischemia during conventional arrest induces progressive ATP depletion, intracellular calcium overload, and profound mitochondrial dysfunction. Upon aortic unclamping, the abrupt reintroduction of oxygen triggers a surge of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which exacerbates cellular injury and precipitates apoptosis, which leads to ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury. Extensive evidence indicates that CPB-induced inflammation precipitates cytokine release, microcirculatory dysfunction, and oxidative stress, thereby exacerbating the risk of myocardial injury and multiple organ dysfunction (7,8). Conversely, the BHCABG strategy maintains a beating heart during revascularization, maintains continuous, physiologic coronary perfusion, minimizing global myocardial ischemic time and preserving physiologic myocardial perfusion. This reduction in I/R injury was reflected in our study by the attenuated rise in postoperative troponin levels. This cardioprotective effect is particularly relevant for patients with severe mitral disease, in whom chronic volume overload and elevated wall stress render the myocardium exceptionally vulnerable to inflammatory insults and ischemic stress. Our findings align with previous meta-analyses indicating that BHCABG reduces myocardial injury biomarkers by avoiding the reperfusion injury associated with the grafting phase of conventional arrest (9,10). Another advantage of performing beating heart CABG first is that it allows for immediate inspection of anastomotic bleeding after the bypass is completed, because once the valve surgery is finished, it will be very difficult to check the anastomosis.

Postoperative serum creatinine is a critical surrogate marker for renal impairment. In our cohort, the BHCABG group demonstrated superior postoperative renal profiles, indicating more effective perioperative renal protection (11). Existing literature indicates that BHCABG is associated with a significantly lower incidence of AKI compared to ONCABG (12,13). The pathophysiology of renal injury during conventional surgery includes non-pulsatile perfusion, which disrupts normal endothelial shear stress, impairs endothelial nitric oxide (NO) synthesis, and induces severe renal medullary hypoxia. Concurrently, prolonged blood contact with the artificial CPB circuit triggers a profound systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS). This inflammatory cascade exacerbates capillary permeability, leading to interstitial edema and microvascular congestion within the kidneys. Other reasons include hemodilution, the release of inflammatory mediators, which compromise renal microperfusion. By reducing the duration of total CPB and ACC or maintaining better hemodynamic stability during the grafting phase, the BHCABG strategy attenuates this deleterious cytokine storm and minimizes the period of non-pulsatile flow exposure. This approach preserves renal autoregulation and microperfusion more effectively than ONCABG, this may preserve renal autoregulation more effectively than the conventional approach (14,15).

Postoperative stroke represents a catastrophic complication of combined CABG and mitral valve surgery (16). Its pathogenesis is multifactorial, encompassing macro-embolization during CPB, cerebral hypoperfusion, and systemic inflammatory-mediated injury. Aortic manipulation during conventional CPB is a major risk factor for dislodging atherosclerotic plaque or debris into the cerebral circulation (17). Beyond macro-emboli, the profound impact of CPB duration on cerebral hemodynamics must be emphasized. Patients with concomitant severe CAD and mitral disease invariably carry a high burden of systemic atherosclerosis, which severely compromises their cerebrovascular autoregulation. This renders their brain exceptionally intolerant to hypoxia and non-pulsatile hypoperfusion. Prolonged CPB and ACC times critically diminish cerebral perfusion pressure, placing the distal watershed territories of the brain at high risk for ischemic infarction. Furthermore, prolonged systemic inflammation disrupts the blood-brain barrier, exacerbating neuroinflammation and cerebral edema. The BHCABG strategy typically minimizes aortic manipulation, theoretically lowering the risk of embolic events, at the same time, the BHCABG strategy crucially abbreviates the duration of non-physiological perfusion. By significantly shortening the CPB time, BHCABG safeguards cerebral hemodynamics and mitigates ischemia in vulnerable watershed regions. Although some large-scale RCTs have reported no significant difference in 30-day stroke rates between OPCABG and ONCABG in the general CAD population, a trend toward reduced stroke risk has been observed in high-risk subgroups (18). Our study population, characterized by severe mitral disease and CAD, carries a high burden of stroke risk factors (e.g., left ventricular dysfunction, arrhythmias, and systemic atherosclerosis); the neuroprotective benefits of the BHCABG strategy may be accentuated in this high-risk cohort.

While the comparison between OPCABG and ONCABG for isolated coronary revascularization has been extensively documented, data regarding concomitant valve surgery remain scarce. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses suggest that OPCABG is associated with reduced risks of postoperative renal insufficiency and low cardiac output syndrome, although findings regarding mortality, myocardial infarction, and stroke have been inconsistent. Accumulating evidence suggests that the clinical benefits of OPCABG may be most evident in specific high-risk subgroups, such as the elderly or those with severe comorbidities Regarding combined procedures, one prospective randomized analysis compared OPCABG plus mitral valvuloplasty against the conventional on-pump approach; it reported shorter CPB and ACC times in the off-pump group but found no significant difference in overall complication rates (4,13). This discrepancy suggests that perioperative outcomes are multifactorial, influenced by baseline patient risk, surgical expertise, and technique selection. Our empirical findings corroborate the potential advantages of the BHCABG strategy and further underscore its utility in optimizing short-term prognosis for patients with complex pathology involving both severe mitral disease and CAD.

Study limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, although a relatively large cohort was included, the sample size particularly in the beating heart CABG group may have limited the power to detect differences in low-frequency outcomes such as in-hospital mortality. Regarding neurological outcomes, although a significant reduction in postoperative stroke was observed in the BHCABG group, the absolute number of stroke events was relatively small. This low event count, while statistically significant after rigorous adjustment, may mathematically amplify the relative effect size. Second, the analysis was restricted to perioperative and short-term outcomes; long-term data regarding survival and graft patency were not assessed. Third, the retrospective nature of the study precluded the systematic calculation of anatomical complexity scores, such as the SYNergy between Percutaneous Coronary Intervention with TAXUS and Cardiac Surgery (SYNTAX) score, due to the lack of granular preoperative angiographic quantification in historical records. Fourth, while rheumatic heart disease was explicitly adjusted for in our baseline model, the precise differentiation between degenerative and ischemic mitral valve disease was constrained. Owing to the descriptive nature of historical echocardiographic reports and surgical records, a highly granular etiological classification was not feasible, which may introduce residual confounding. Finally, as a single-center retrospective study, inherent methodological limitations remain. Although advanced statistical methods such as CBPS-IPTW were employed, which successfully optimized the mathematical balance for observed baseline covariates, the fundamental limitation of CBPS, like any propensity-based method, is its inability to account for unmeasured or unknown confounders. Subtle physiological disparities, varying degrees of frailty, or unrecorded clinical nuances not captured in the database could still influence treatment assignment and outcomes, meaning residual selection bias cannot be entirely eliminated. Therefore, prospective multicenter studies with extended follow-up are required to validate our findings.

Conclusions

In patients with concomitant CAD and severe mitral valve disease, a combined strategy of beating heart revascularization (off-pump or on-pump beating heart CABG) and mitral valve surgery was associated with superior perioperative organ protection compared with conventional on-pump CABG with cardioplegic arrest. This benefit was primarily attributable to a significant reduction in ACC time, accompanied by lower myocardial injury, improved renal outcomes, and a reduced risk of postoperative stroke. Off-pump or on-pump beating heart CABG may therefore represent a viable alternative revascularization strategy in patients undergoing combined CABG and mitral valve surgery.

The findings of this study may have meaningful clinical applicability. The cohort reflects real-world practice in a high-volume tertiary cardiac surgery center, and baseline differences were rigorously controlled using IPTW, enhancing the external validity of the results. Given that concomitant CAD and severe mitral valve disease are frequently encountered in complex cardiac surgery, the observed perioperative advantages of beating-heart revascularization may be relevant to similar patient populations in experienced centers. Further multicenter studies would help to confirm the consistency of these findings across broader healthcare settings.

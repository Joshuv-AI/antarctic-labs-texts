1.
Introduction

Let
d(n)
and
\sigma_{m}(n)
be the well-known divisor functions and their generating functions are given by,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}},\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{m}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{m}q^{n}}{1-q^{n}},~~\textrm{for}~~m\in\mathbb{Z},~|q|<1.

In 1919, Kluyver
[
8
]
showed that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{(1-q^{n})(q)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

(1.1)

A one variable generalization of the identity (
1.1
) can be found in the second notebook of Ramanujan
[
9
, p. 354]
.
Uchimura
[
11
]
, in 1981, gave a new expression for Kluyver’s identity. Mainly, he proved that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nq^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{(1-q^{n})(q)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

(1.2)

A beautiful partition theoretic interpretation of this identity was obtained by Bressoud and Subbarao
[
4
]
, which has been further explored by the first four authors in
[
1
]
. Over the period of time, Uchimura’s identity (
1.2
) has been extended by many mathematicians. Among them, Uchimura himself in
[
12
]
, Dilcher
[
6
]
, Andrews-Crippa-Simon
[
3
]
, and Gupta-Kumar
[
7
]
worked on this identity. Recently, the first four authors
[
2
]
studied these generalizations and presented a unified theory.

Now, we shall discuss the generalizations given by Uchimura
[
12
]
and Andrews-Crippa-Simon
[
3
]
and their applications in probability theory and random graphs.

Uchimura
[
12
, p. 76]
considered a random variable
X
with the probability generating function

\displaystyle G(x,q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}~\mathrm{Pr}(X=n),

where
\mathrm{Pr}(X=n)=q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}
for any non-negative integer
n
and
q\in(0,1)
.
Moreover, he proved that, for any natural number
m
, the
m
-th moment is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X^{m})=M_{m}=Y_{m}(K_{1},K_{2},\dots,K_{m}),

(1.3)

where

\displaystyle M_{m}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{m}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty},\quad\text{and}\quad K_{m}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{m-1}(n)q^{n},

(1.4)

and
Y_{m}
is the Bell polynomial defined by

Y_{m}\left(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m}\right)=\sum_{\Pi(m)}\frac{m!}{k_{1}!\dots k_{m}!}\left(\frac{u_{1}}{1!}\right)^{k_{1}}\dots\left(\frac{u_{m}}{m!}\right)^{k_{m}},

(1.5)

where
\Pi(m)
denotes a partition of
m
with

k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+mk_{m}=m.

He further showed that the
m
-th cumulant
h_{m}
is equal to the divisor generating function
K_{m}
, that is,

\displaystyle h_{m}=K_{m},\quad\forall~m\in\mathbb{N},

(1.6)

by writing the probability generating function in the following way

\displaystyle G(e^{t},q)=\exp\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}h_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\right).

The above identity (
1.6
) proves that the divisor generating function
K_{m}
is nothing but the
m
-th cumulant with respect to the random variable
X
. As we know that the second cumulant
h_{2}
is same as
\mathrm{Var}(X)
, so we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)q^{n}.

(1.7)

Uchimura
[
12
]
provided a combinatorial interpretation of the probability generating function
G(x,q)
by highlighting its importance through applications in the analysis of data structures, called heaps.
His work is primarily focused on examining the average number of exchanges needed to insert an element into a heap, offering insights into the efficiency of this operation. For readers interested in a detailed exposition, further discussion can be traced in
[
12
, Section 3]
.

In 1993, Simon, Crippa, and Collenberg
[
10
]
analyzed how the transitive closure is distributed in the
G_{n,p}
–model of a random acyclic digraph. They defined the
G_{n,p}
–model as a random acyclic digraph with a vertex set
V=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and directed edges appearing between vertices
(i,j)
, for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
, with probability
p\in(0,1)
. Let
\gamma^{*}_{n}(1)
be a random variable representing the number of vertices reachable from vertex
1
by a directed path, including vertex
1
itself. They initially established a probability function, for
1\leq h\leq n
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1)=h)=q^{n-h}\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}(1-q^{n-j}),

(1.8)

where
q=1-p
. In the same paper, by treating the random variable representing the size of a node’s transitive closure as a discrete-time pure-birth process, they derived an expression for its distribution, mean and variance, linking these to the divisor generating function. They proved that,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n-\mathbb{E}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}.

(1.9)

Andrews, Crippa and Simon
[
3
]
further studied the same random variable and proved that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Var}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)q^{n}.

(1.10)

In view of (
1.7
) and (
1.10
), we can clearly see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Var}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)q^{n}=K_{2},

where
X
is the random variable studied by Uchimura. As mentioned earlier, Uchimura also showed that the
m

h_{m}
with respect to the random variable
X
is exactly equal to the generating function for the generalized divisor function
\sigma_{m-1}(n)
.

This observation led the first four authors
[
2
, p. 31, Problem 2]
to raise the question on the interpretation of the generalized divisor generating function
K_{m}
, defined in (
1.4
), with respect to the random variable
\gamma^{*}_{n}(1)
studied by Simon, Crippa and Collenberg. In this paper, we completely settle this question (see Theorem
2.6
).

It is worthwhile to indicate that, Andrews, Crippa and Simon
[
3
]
established a more general form of (

) using the theory of

-series. For that, they first defined a sequence

of polynomials in

\displaystyle a_{n}(q)=f(n)+(1-q^{n-1})a_{n-1}(q),\hskip 14.22636pta_{0}(q)=0,

(1.11)

where

is a non-zero polynomial in

, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(i)-a_{n}(q)\right)=\sum_{j\geq 1}h_{j}P_{j}(q),

(1.12)

where
P_{j}(q):=P_{j}(K_{1}(q),K_{2}(q),\dots,K_{j}(q))
is some polynomial with rational coefficients,
and the coefficients

can be evaluated by

\displaystyle h_{1}=c_{0},\hskip 14.22636pth_{j}=\sum_{i\geq j-1}(-1)^{i-j+1}{i-1\choose j-2}i!\sum_{k\geq i}c_{k}\bar{s}(k,i),

where

is the Stirling number of the second kind. Further, they observed that the expected value of
\gamma^{*}_{n}(1)
, denoted as
\mathbb{E}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1))
, satisfies the recurrence relation given in (
1.11
) with

for all natural numbers

. Using (

) and (

).

2.
Main Results

Before stating our main results, we state a result
[
2
, Corollary 2.14]
obtained by the first four authors recently, which gives a Ramanujan-type expression for Uchimura’s idenity (
1.3
), namely, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{{n+1\choose 2}}A_{k}(q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{k}(q)_{n}},

(2.1)

where
A_{k}(x)
denotes the Eulerian polynomial of degree
k
defined by the following generating function

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}=\frac{(1-x)e^{t}}{e^{xt}-xe^{t}}.

Now we are ready to state the main results of our paper. Motivated from identity (
2.1
), we obtain the identity below.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any non-negative integer
k
, we have the following expression

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{k}\right)q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{{n+1\choose 2}}A_{k}(q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{k+1}(q)_{n}}.

Utilizing this result, we obtain a new expression for the identity (
1.12
) of Andrews, Crippa and Simon.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
a_{n}(q)
be a polynomial in
q
defined by the recurrence relation

a_{0}(q)=1,\hskip 14.22636pta_{n}(q)=f(n)+(1-q^{n-1})a_{n-1}(q),\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{for}\hskip 14.22636ptn\geq 1,

where
f(n)=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}n^{k}
is a polynomial in
n
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(i)-a_{n}(q)\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(i)\right)q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}.

We present two applications of this theorem. First, we give a limit form for Uchimura’s function
M_{k}
, defined in (
1.4
) and the second application presents a limit form for Dilcher’s identity (
2.3
).

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer and
a_{n,k}(q)
be a sequence of polynomials in
q
defined by the recurrence relation

\displaystyle a_{0,k}(q)=1,\hskip 14.22636pta_{n,k}(q)=f_{k}(n)+(1-q^{n-1})a_{n-1,k}(q),\hskip 14.22636pt\mathrm{for}\hskip 14.22636ptn\geq 1,

(2.2)

where

f_{k}(n)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}(-1)^{j-1}n^{k-j}.

Then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(n^{k}-a_{n,k}(q)\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}.

Now we state an interesting generalization of Uchimura’s identity (
1.2
) due to Dilcher
[
6
, Equations (4.3),(5.7)]
. For for

,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\choose k}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}=q^{-{k\choose 2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{{n+k\choose 2}}}{(1-q^{n})^{k}(q)_{n}}=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{j_{1}}}{1-q^{j_{1}}}\cdots\sum_{j_{k}=1}^{j_{k-1}}\frac{q^{j_{k}}}{1-q^{j_{k}}}.

(2.3)

As an application of Theorem
2.2
, we obtain a limit expression for Dilcher’s identity (
2.3
).

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer and
a_{n,k}(q)
be a sequence of polynomials in
q
defined by the recurrence relation

\displaystyle a_{0,k}(q)=1,a_{n,k}(q)=f_{k}(n)+(1-q^{n-1})a_{n-1,k}(q),~~\textrm{for}~~n\geq 1,

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle f_{k}(n)={n-1\choose k-1}.

(2.5)

Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left({n\choose k}-a_{n,k}(q)\right)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\choose k}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}.

(2.6)

Before proceeding to the next result, let
\gamma_{n}^{*}(1)
be the random variable, studied by Simon-Crippa-Collenberg, as defined just above equation (
1.8
) in the introduction. For a random variable

\displaystyle\log\left(\mathbb{E}[e^{uX}]\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{t}(X)\frac{u^{t}}{t!},

where

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n-\kappa_{1}(\gamma_{n}^{*}(1)))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n-\mathbb{E}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}=K_{1}(q),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\kappa_{2}(\gamma_{n}^{*}(1))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Var}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)q^{n}=K_{2}(q).

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\kappa_{3}(\gamma_{n}^{*}(1))

(2.12)

.

3.
Preliminary Tools

The generating function for Bernoulli numbers is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{x}{e^{x}-1},\quad|x|<2\pi.

The generating function for Eulerian polynomials
[
5
, p. 244]
is as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t-1}{t-e^{x(t-1)}}.

Eulerian polynomials satisfy the following recurrence relation:

\displaystyle A_{0}(t)=1,~A_{k}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\choose j}A_{j}(t)(t-1)^{k-1-j}~~\textrm{ for}~~k\geq 1.

(3.1)

One can also show that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k}x^{n}=\frac{xA_{k}(x)}{(1-x)^{k+1}}.

(3.2)

Bernoulli showed that the sum of
k
-th powers of the first
n-1
natural numbers can be explicitly written as

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}m^{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k+1\choose j}B_{j}n^{k+1-j}.

(3.3)

Now we state and prove a lemma that will be crucial to prove Theorem
2.1
. This result gives a relation between Bernoulli numbers and Eulerian polynomials.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any non-negative integer
k
and complex number
t
, we have

\displaystyle S_{k}(t):=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k+1\choose j}B_{j}(1-t)^{j}A_{k+1-j}(t)=tA_{k}(t).

Let us consider the generating function for
S_{k}(t)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{k}(t)\frac{x^{k+1}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k+1\choose j}B_{j}(1-t)^{j}A_{k+1-j}(t)\frac{x^{k+1}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{B_{j}(1-t)^{j}}{j!}\frac{A_{k+1-j}(t)}{(k+1-j)!}x^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}\frac{(x(1-t))^{m}}{m!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n+1}(t)\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{x(1-t)}{e^{x(1-t)}-1}\left(\frac{t-1}{t-e^{x(t-1)}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{t-1}{t-e^{x(t-1)}}\times xe^{x(t-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(t)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(t-1)^{m}\frac{x^{m+1}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k\choose j}A_{j}(t)(t-1)^{k-j}\frac{x^{k+1}}{k!}.

Upon comparing the coefficients of
\frac{x^{k+1}}{k!}
, we get

\displaystyle S_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k\choose j}A_{j}(t)(t-1)^{k-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\choose j}A_{j}(t)(t-1)^{k-j}+A_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=(t-1)A_{k}(t)+A_{k}(t)=tA_{k}(t),

where in the penultimate step we used (
3.1
). This completes the proof.

4.
Proof of main results

We will start with the left hand side of Theorem

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}m^{k}\right)q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}

where in the final step we used (

) to obtain the first sum and (
2.1

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{k+1\choose j}B_{j}n^{k+1-j}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{{n+1\choose 2}}A_{k}(q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{k}(q)_{n}}\left(\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}+1\right)

.

Given that
f(n)=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}n^{k}
is a polynomial in
n
. Consider
F(x)
to be the generating function for
f(n)
, that is,

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n)x^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}n^{k}x^{n}=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k}x^{n}=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}\frac{xA_{k}(x)}{(1-x)^{k+1}},

where in the last equality we used (
3.2
).
Substituting

in the above expression, we see that

\displaystyle F(q^{n})=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}\frac{q^{n}A_{k}(q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{k+1}}.

(4.3)

Now define the generating function for the sequence
a_{n}(q)
as follows:

\displaystyle A(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(q)x^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(f(n)+(1-q^{n-1})a_{n-1}(q)\right)x^{n}

\displaystyle=F(x)+xA(x)-xA(qx).

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle A(x)=\frac{F(x)}{1-x}-\frac{x}{1-x}A(qx).

This recurrence relation suggests that

\displaystyle A(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}F(q^{n}x)x^{n}q^{n\choose 2}}{(x)_{n+1}}.

Put

in the above expression and then use (
4.3
) to see that

\displaystyle A(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{n\choose 2}}{(q)_{n}}\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}\frac{q^{n}A_{k}(q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}A_{k}(q^{n})q^{n+1\choose 2}}{(1-q^{n})^{k+1}(q)_{n}}.

Now apply Theorem
2.1
to get

\displaystyle A(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{k}\right)q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(i)\right)q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}.

(4.4)

From the recurrence relation of the sequence of polynomials
a_{i}(q)
, it is evident that

\displaystyle a_{i}(q)=f(i)+(1-q^{i-1})a_{i-1}(q)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow~

\displaystyle f(i)-a_{i}(q)+a_{i-1}(q)=q^{i-1}a_{i-1}(q).

Now taking the sum over
i
from
1
to
n
, then letting
n\rightarrow\infty
and finally using (
4.4
), the result follows.
∎

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{k}(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}(-1)^{j-1}i^{k-j},

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(i^{k}-(i-1)^{k}\right)=n^{k}.

Now apply Theorem
2.2
to complete the proof of the corollary.
∎

As we have taken
f_{k}(n)={n-1\choose k-1}
, we find that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{k}(i)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{i-1\choose k-1}={n\choose k}.

Hence the proof of (
2.6
) immediately follows from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

For simplicity, throughout this proof, let us denote
\gamma_{n}^{*}(1)
as
X_{n}
.
We define
e_{n,k}:=\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{k})
.
Simon, Crippa and Collenberg
[
10
]
proved that
e_{n,1}
and
e_{n,2}
satisfy the following recurrence relations,

\displaystyle e_{n,1}

\displaystyle=1+(1-q^{n-1})e_{n-1,1},

(4.5)

\displaystyle e_{n,2}

\displaystyle=2ne_{n,1}-a_{n,2},

(4.6)

where

a_{n,2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{2}(i)\prod_{j=i}^{n-1}(1-q^{j}),~~\textrm{and}~~f_{2}(i)=(2i-1).

More generally, for any fixed
k\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
, we will show that
e_{n,k}
satisfies the
following relation,

\displaystyle e_{n,k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}n^{k-\ell}a_{n,\ell},

(4.7)

where

\displaystyle a_{n,1}=e_{n,1},\hskip 28.45274pta_{n,k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{k}(i)\prod_{j=i}^{n-1}(1-q^{j}),\hskip 28.45274pt\mathrm{for}\hskip 5.69046ptk\geq 2,

(4.8)

and

\displaystyle f_{k}(i)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}(-1)^{j-1}i^{k-j}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{k\choose j}(-1)^{k-j-1}i^{j}.

(4.9)

It is easy to see that the relation (
4.7
) is true for
k=1
as
e_{n,1}=a_{n,1}
for all
n\geq 1
by our assumption. Moreover, the relation (
4.7
) is also true for
k=2
as we know that (
4.6
) holds for all
n\geq 1
. Let us assume that the relation for
e_{m,j}
is true for any
1\leq m<\infty
when
3\leq j\leq k-1
and
1\leq m\leq n-1
for
j=k
. Now we shall show that the relation holds for
e_{n,k}
. Since
e_{n,k}=\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{k})
, we can write

\displaystyle e_{n,k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{n}h^{k}P(X_{n}=h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{n}h^{k}q^{n-h}\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}(1-q^{n-i})

\displaystyle=q^{n-1}+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{h=2}^{n}h^{k}q^{n-h}\prod_{i=2}^{h-1}(1-q^{n-i})

\displaystyle=q^{n-1}+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}(h+1)^{k}q^{n-h-1}\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}(1-q^{n-i-1})

\displaystyle=q^{n-1}+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}h^{j}\right)q^{n-h-1}\prod_{i=1}^{h-1}(1-q^{n-i-1})

\displaystyle=q^{n-1}+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}P(X_{n-1}=h)+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}h^{j}P(X_{n-1}=h)

\displaystyle=1+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}h^{j}P(X_{n-1}=h)

\displaystyle=1+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}e_{n-1,j}

\displaystyle=1+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}{j\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}(n-1)^{j-\ell}a_{n-1,\ell}\quad(\textrm{using inductive hypothesis})

\displaystyle=1+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(-1)^{\ell-1}a_{n-1,\ell}\sum_{j=\ell}^{k}{k\choose j}{j\choose\ell}(n-1)^{j-\ell}

\displaystyle=1+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(-1)^{\ell-1}a_{n-1,\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{k-\ell}{k\choose j+\ell}{j+\ell\choose\ell}(n-1)^{j}

\displaystyle=1+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}a_{n-1,\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{k-\ell}{k-\ell\choose j}(n-1)^{j}

\displaystyle=1+(1-q^{n-1})\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}a_{n-1,\ell}~n^{k-\ell}.

(4.10)

From (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), one can easily observe that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following recurrence relation for
a_{n,k}
holds:

\displaystyle a_{n,k}=f_{k}(n)+(1-q^{n-1})a_{n-1,k},~~\textrm{where}~~\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{k}(i)=n^{k}.

(4.11)

Use this recurrence relation in (
4.10
) to see that

\displaystyle e_{n,k}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}n^{k-\ell}\left(a_{n,\ell}-f_{\ell}(n)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}n^{k-\ell}a_{n,\ell}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}n^{k-\ell}f_{\ell}(n).

It becomes clear at this juncture that to prove (
4.7
) it is enough to show

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}n^{k-\ell}f_{\ell}(n)=1.

Using the definition (
4.9
) of
f_{\ell}(n)
, we can see that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}n^{k-\ell}f_{\ell}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}{k\choose\ell}{\ell\choose j}(-1)^{\ell+j}n^{k-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=j}^{k}{k\choose j}{k-j\choose\ell-j}(-1)^{\ell+j}n^{k-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{k\choose j}n^{k-j}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-j}{k-j\choose\ell}(-1)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}{k\choose j}n^{k-j}(1-1)^{k-j}

\displaystyle=1.

This completes the proof of the relation (
4.7
) for
e_{n,k}
.
As we know that the sequence
a_{n,k}
satisfies the relation (
4.11
), so by applying Corollary
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(n^{k}-a_{n,k}(q)\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}.

Further, utilize Uchimura’s identity (
1.3
)
to see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(n^{k}-a_{n,k}(q)\right)=Y_{k}(K_{1},K_{2},\dots,K_{m}),

(4.12)

where
Y_{k}
denotes the Bell polynomial defined in (
1.5
).
Now we are ready to calculate the limiting value of the third cumulant, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\kappa_{3}(X_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}(X_{n}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n}))^{3}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{3})-3\mathbb{E}(X_{n})\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{2})+2\mathbb{E}(X_{n})^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(e_{n,3}-3e_{n,1}e_{n,2}+2e_{n,1}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left((n^{3}-a_{n,3})-3(n-a_{n,1})(n^{2}-a_{n,2})+2(n-a_{n,1})^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(Y_{3}-3Y_{1}Y_{2}+2Y_{1}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=-K_{2},

where in the ante-penultimate step we have used the recurrence relation (
4.7
), whereas in the penultimate step we used (
4.12
) and in the final step we used Dilcher’s identity
[
6
, p. 85, Equation (2.2)]
. This proves (
2.10
).

Now we shall calculate the limiting value of the fourth cumulant, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\kappa_{4}(X_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}(X_{n}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n}))^{4}-3\left(\mathbb{E}(X_{n}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n}))^{2}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{4})-4\mathbb{E}(X_{n})\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{3})-3\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{2})^{2}+12\mathbb{E}(X_{n})^{2}\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{2})-6\mathbb{E}(X_{n})^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(e_{n,4}-4e_{n,1}e_{n,3}-3e_{n,2}^{2}+12e_{n,2}e_{n,1}^{2}-6e_{n,1}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n^{4}-a_{n,4})-4(n-a_{n,1})(n^{3}-a_{n,3})-3(n^{2}-a_{n,2})^{2}+12(n^{2}-a_{n,2})(n-a_{n,1})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-6(n-a_{n,1})^{4})

\displaystyle=(Y_{4}-4Y_{1}Y_{3}-3Y_{2}^{2}+12Y_{2}Y_{1}^{2}-6Y_{1}^{4})

\displaystyle=K_{4}.

Here again, we made use of (
4.7
), (
4.12
) and
Dilcher’s identity
[
6
, p. 85, Equation (2.3)]
. This completes the proof of (
2.11
).
Finally, we evaluate the limiting value of the fifth cumulant in the following way:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\kappa_{5}(X_{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}(X_{n}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n}))^{5}-10\mathbb{E}(X_{n}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n}))^{3}\mathbb{E}(X_{n}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n}))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{5})-5\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{4})\mathbb{E}(X_{n})+20\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{3})\mathbb{E}(X_{n})^{2}-40\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{2})\mathbb{E}(X_{n})^{3}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+24\mathbb{E}(X_{n})^{5}-10\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{3})\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{2})+10\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{2})^{2}\mathbb{E}(X_{n}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(e_{n,5}-5e_{n,4}e_{n,1}+20e_{n,3}e_{n,1}^{2}-40e_{n,2}e_{n,1}^{3}+24e_{n,1}^{5}-10e_{n,3}e_{n,2}+10e_{n,2}^{2}e_{n,1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}((n^{5}-a_{n,5})-5(n^{4}-a_{n,4})(n-a_{n,1})+20(n^{3}-a_{n,3})(n-a_{n,1})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-40(n^{2}-a_{n,2})(n-a_{n,1})^{3}+24(n-a_{n,1})^{5}-10(n^{3}-a_{n,3})(n^{2}-a_{n,2})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+10(n^{2}-a_{n,2})^{2}(n-a_{n,1}))

\displaystyle=-(Y_{5}-5Y_{4}Y_{1}+20Y_{3}Y_{1}^{2}-40Y_{2}Y_{1}^{3}+24Y_{1}^{5}-10Y_{3}Y_{2}+10Y_{2}^{2}Y_{1})

\displaystyle=-K_{5}.

Here also to obtain the final step we employed Dilcher’s identity
[
6
, p. 85, Theorem 1]
and in the penultimate step we invoked (
4.12
).
∎

So far we have not been able to extend the above technique for higher cumulants. However, we use another method to prove the general case of the
t
-th cumulant for any
t
.

5.
Proof of Theorem
2.6
: The general case

We use the notations as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
. Let us define
Z_{n}:=n-X_{n}
, where
X_{n}=\gamma_{n}^{*}(1)
.
Let
\kappa_{t}
denote the
t
-th cumulant and
e_{n,k}=\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{k})
. For any random variable
X
and constant
c
, it is well-known that
\kappa_{1}(X+c)=\kappa_{1}(X)+c
, and for
t\geq 2
one has
\kappa_{t}(X+c)=\kappa_{t}(X)
, and
\kappa_{t}(cX)=c^{t}\kappa_{t}(X)
for any
t\geq 1
. Hence, for
t\geq 2
, one can see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\kappa_{t}(Z_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}(-1)^{t}\kappa_{t}(X_{n}).

Thus, for
t\geq 2
, Theorem
2.6
is equivalent to the fact that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\kappa_{t}(Z_{n})=K_{t}(q),

(5.1)

where

\displaystyle K_{t+1}(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{t}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{t}q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}

be the divisor generating function and for
t=1
, we already know

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\kappa_{1}(Z_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}(n-\kappa_{1}(X_{n}))=K_{1}(q).

We first prove a lemma that plays a vital role to prove (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.1
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}^{k})=n^{k}-a_{n,k},

where
a_{n,k}
is defined as in (
4.8
).

As
Z_{n}=n-X_{n}
, so using Binomial expansion

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}^{k})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left((n-X_{n})^{k}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}n^{k-j}(-1)^{j}\mathbb{E}(X_{n}^{j})

\displaystyle=n^{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}n^{k-j}(-1)^{j}e_{n,j}.

(5.2)

Now we use (
4.7
) in the right side of the above expression to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}^{k})

\displaystyle=n^{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k}{j}n^{k-j}(-1)^{j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\binom{j}{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-1}n^{j-\ell}a_{n,\ell}

\displaystyle=n^{k}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(-1)^{\ell-1}n^{k-\ell}a_{n,\ell}\sum_{j=\ell}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\binom{j}{\ell}(-1)^{j}

\displaystyle=n^{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}{k\choose\ell}n^{k-\ell}a_{n,\ell}\sum_{j=\ell}^{k}{k-\ell\choose j-\ell}(-1)^{j-\ell}.

By using binomial theorem, the inner sum gives value
1
if
k=\ell
and
0
otherwise. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(n-X_{n})^{k}=n^{k}-a_{n,k}.

Now we are ready to prove (
5.1
), which is equivalent to Theorem
2.6
. To prove (
5.1
), we further derive an equivalent statement by multiplying both sides of (
5.1
) by
\frac{z^{t}}{t!}
and then summing over
t
to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{t}(Z_{n})\frac{z^{t}}{t!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}K_{t}(q)\frac{z^{t}}{t!}.

(5.3)

Now we expand the sum on the right hand side to see

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{t}}{t!}K_{t}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{t}}{t!}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k^{t-1}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{(zk)^{t}}{t!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\frac{e^{kz}-1}{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{kz}-1}{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}q^{k\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{kz}q^{k\ell}}{k}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k\ell}}{k}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\log(1-e^{z}q^{\ell})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\log(1-q^{\ell})

\displaystyle=\log\prod_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{\ell})}{(1-e^{z}q^{\ell})}=\log\frac{(q)_{\infty}}{(e^{z}q)_{\infty}}.

(5.4)

Combining (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{t}(Z_{n})\frac{z^{t}}{t!}=\log\frac{(q)_{\infty}}{(e^{z}q)_{\infty}}.

Hence, we only need to show the identity

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\exp\left(\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{t}(Z_{n})\frac{z^{t}}{t!}\right)=\frac{(q)_{\infty}}{(e^{z}q)_{\infty}}.

(5.5)

From the definition of the cumulant generating function (
2.7
), we know that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{Z_{n}z}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\exp\left(\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{t}(Z_{n})\frac{z^{t}}{t!}\right).

Thus, to prove (
5.5
), we have to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{Z_{n}z}\right)=\frac{(q)_{\infty}}{(e^{z}q)_{\infty}}.

Now we start with

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{Z_{n}z}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{(n-X_{n})z}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\mathbb{E}\left((n-X_{n})^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\lim_{n\to\infty}(n^{k}-a_{n,k})

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{k}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty},

(5.6)

where we used Lemma
5.1
in the penultimate step and Corollary
2.3
in the last step. Further, we use

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}=1

in (
5.6
) to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{Z_{n}z}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{k}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(q)_{\infty}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{k}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(q)_{\infty}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{k}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(q)_{\infty}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\ell^{k}

\displaystyle=(q)_{\infty}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}e^{\ell z}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}e^{\ell z}q^{\ell}(q^{\ell+1})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q)_{\infty}}{(e^{z}q)_{\infty}},

where in the last step, we used
[
12
, Equation 2.3]
with
x=e^{z}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.6
.

∎

6.
concluding remarks

The work of Simon-Crippa-Collenberg gives an additional representation for Uchimura’s identity (
1.2
), namely,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n-\mathbb{E}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nq^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{(1-q^{n})(q)_{n}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}.

(6.1)

Further, Andrews, Crippa and Simon showed that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Var}(\gamma^{*}_{n}(1))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)q^{n}=K_{2}(q).

Uchimura generalized his own identity in the following way:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}=Y_{k}(K_{1},K_{2},\dots,K_{k}).

Now combining the identity (
2.1
) and Corollary
2.3
, we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(n^{k}-a_{n,k}(q)\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{{n+1\choose 2}}A_{k}(q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{k}(q)_{n}}=Y_{k}(K_{1},K_{2},\dots,K_{k}),

(6.2)

where
a_{n,k}
is defined as in (
2.2
). Furthermore, we obtained a limiting expression for Dilcher’s identity, namely, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left({n\choose k}-a_{n,k}(q)\right)=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\choose k}q^{n}(q^{n+1})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{{n+k\choose 2}-{k\choose 2}}}{(1-q^{n})^{k}(q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{j_{1}}}{1-q^{j_{1}}}\cdots\sum_{j_{k}=1}^{j_{k-1}}\frac{q^{j_{k}}}{1-q^{j_{k}}},

(6.3)

where
a_{n,k}
is defined in (
2.4
).
As observed in (
6.1
)-(
6.3
), we discovered a limit expression for Uchimura’s identity (
1.3
) and Dilcher’s identity (
2.3
), which are generalizations of (
1.2
). Thus, it would be fascinating to find limit expressions for all the generalizations of Uchimura’s identity (
1.2
) in the framework of Uchimura-Ramanujan-divisor type identities studied in
[
2
]
.

Furthermore, we also generalized the work of Simon-Crippa-Collenberg and Andrews-Crippa-Simon by showing that the limit of the
t
-th cumulant is nothing but the generalized divisor function
K_{t}(q)
, i.e., for any
t\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\kappa_{t}(Z_{n})

\displaystyle=K_{t}(q),

where
Z_{n}=n-\gamma_{n}^{*}(1)
. In the proof of Theorem
2.5
, we evaluated the limit of the third, fourth and fifth cumulants using the limit form (
4.12
) of Uchimura’s identity and Dilcher’s identity
[
6
, p. 85, Equations (2.1)–(2.3)]
. It would be interesting to utilize this approach to give another proof of the general case of the
t
-th cumulant.

Guofang Wang, Chao Xia and Xiqiang Zhang

Abstract
. In the paper we establish an optimal logarithmic Sobolev inequality for complete, non-compact, properly embedded self-shrinkers in the Euclidean space, which generalizes a recent result of Brendle [ 12 ] for closed self-shrinkers. We first provide a proof for the logarithmic Sobolev inequality in the Euclidean space by using the Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci (ABP) method. Then we use this approach to show an optimal logarithmic Sobolev inequality for complete, non-compact, properly embedded self-shrinkers in the Euclidean space, which is a sharp version of the result of Ecker in [ 23 ] . The proof is a noncompact modification of Brendle’s proof for closed submanifolds and has a big potential to provide new inequalities in noncompact manifolds. Introduction

Very recently, S. Brendle
[
11
]
used cleverly the ABP approach to establish optimal isoperimetric inequalities and also optimal Michael-Simon-Sobolev inequality for minimal submanifolds in
the Euclidean space, which solves a long-standing conjecture. In
[
12
]
, Brendle also obtained the following optimal logarithmic Sobolev inequality for closed (comapct without boundary) submanifolds in
the Euclidean space of arbitrary dimension and codimension.

Theorem A
(
[
12
]
)

Let
\Sigma
be an
n
-dimensional closed submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. Then for any positive function
f
on
\Sigma
with
\int_{\Sigma}fdvol=1
, there holds

(1.1)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left\{\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}-f\log f+|H|^{2}f\right\}dvol\geq\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046ptn+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi).\end{array}

In particular, Theorem A implies an optimal logarithmic Sobolev inequality for closed
self-shrinkers.

Theorem B
(
[
12
]
)

Let
\Sigma
be an
n
-dimensional closed self-shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. Then
for any positive function
\varphi
with

\int_{\Sigma}\varphi\,d\gamma=1
, where
d\gamma=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}dvol
, there holds

(1.2)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}d\gamma\geq\int_{\Sigma}\varphi\log\varphi\,d\gamma.

A submanifold
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
is called a
self-shrinker
if it satisfies

H+\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}=0,

where
H
is the mean curvature vector field of
\Sigma
and
x^{\perp}
is the normal component of
x
.

The logarithmic Sobolev inequality for submanifolds was first studied by Ecker in
[
23
]
,
where he obtained a logarithmic Sobolev inequality with an extra term
c(n)\int\varphi\,d\gamma
.
See also the work of Gross
[
28
]
, where the author established a logarithmic Sobolev inequality for submanifolds of certain probability spaces.

One of our main results in this paper is to show that Theorem B is true for complete, non-compact properly embedded self-shrinkers.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be an
n
-dimensional complete, non-compact properly embedded self-shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. Then for any positive function
\varphi
on
\Sigma
with
\int_{\Sigma}\varphi\,d\gamma=1
, there holds

(1.3)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\,d\gamma\geq\int_{\Sigma}\varphi\log\varphi\,d\gamma.

Equivalently, for any positive function
f
on
\Sigma
with
\int_{\Sigma}fdvol=1
, there holds

(1.4)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left\{\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}-f\log f+|H|^{2}f\right\}dvol\geq\displaystyle n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi).\end{array}

Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a self-shrinker, Theorem
1.1
implies the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, due to A. J. Stam

[
39
]
in 1959 or
P. Federbush
[
24
]
in 1969, often attributed to L. Gross.
Gross showed in
[
27
]
that
the Logarithmic Sobolev inequality (short, LSI) is equivalent to hypercontractivity of the semigroup of a diffusion process.
Since his work it has been intensively studied and found rich connections to analysis, differential geometry, and probability (for surveys we refer to

[
3
,
29
,
5
]
).
For instance, the LSI has played an important role in stochastic analysis and also
played a crucial role in Perelman’s proof (2002) of the Poincaré
conjecture.

Inequality (
1.3
) is clearly optimal. In fact,
\varphi=1
achieves equality in (
1.3
) for
\Sigma=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We believe that equality in (
1.3
) is never achieved on a closed self-shrinker.
Actually we believe that the following question has an affirmative answer.

Question 1.

Let
\Sigma^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
be a complete properly embedded self-shrinker. If equality in (

) holds for some positive function

, is

isometric to

?

There are many examples of compact or non-compact self shrinkers. The most important ones are the sphere and cylinders,
{\mathbb{S}}^{k}(\sqrt{2k})\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
,

Also most of known examples of non-compact self-shrinker are properly embedded. Here by a properly embedded submanifold it means that the immersion

is proper. Thanks to a result of Ding-Xin
[

and Cheng-Zhou

For a general complete, non-compact submanifolds in the Euclidean space we obtain a slightly weaker inequality than (

v_{\delta}:=w_{\delta}-w_{\delta}(0)
, we have that for any
h\in(0,1]
there exists
R_{h}
such that

-h\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}+\min_{|x|\leq R_{h}}w\leq w\leq\max_{|x|\leq R_{h}}w+h\frac{|x|^{2}}{2},

which implies (
2.12
). This finishes the proof of the Proposition.
∎

We remark that one can show the uniqueness of
w
and
c
of (
2.10
) with (
2.12
).

Now we show Proposition
2.2
.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.
Let
u_{\epsilon}=w_{\epsilon}+u_{0}
. It is clear that
u_{\epsilon}
satisfies (
2.3
) and (
2.4
). (
2.5
) is proved in the following Lemma. ∎

Lemma 2.4
.

We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\rm div}(f_{\epsilon}\nabla u_{\epsilon})\,dx=0.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation we omit the subscript
\epsilon
. From the construction of
w
, it is clear that

(2.19)

\displaystyle|u(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2})

for some
C>0
, while
f
is exponentially decay.
Let
\xi
be a cut-off function with support in
B_{2r}
satisfying
\xi=1
in
B_{r}
and
|\nabla\xi(x)|\leq 2/|x|
in
B_{2r}\backslash B_{r}
.
It is easy to check that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{r}}{\rm div}(f\nabla u)dx\right|&\leq&\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\rm div}(f\nabla u)\xi dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{r}}{\rm div}(f\nabla u)\xi dx\right|.\end{array}

Using Equation (
2.3
) and the growth of
f
and
u
, it is easy to see that the second term in the right hand side tends to zero as
r\to\infty
. The first term is equal to

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\nabla u\nabla\xi dx\right|&\leq&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{1}{r}\int_{B_{2r}\backslash B_{r}}f|\nabla u|dx\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\left(\int_{B_{2r}\backslash B_{r}}fdx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{2r}\backslash B_{r}}f|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{array}

Hence the Lemma follows, if

\int_{B_{r}}f|\nabla u|^{2}dx

is uniformly bounded. This is true by a standard argument as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\xi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}fdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\xi^{2}{\rm div}(f\nabla u)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}2\xi u\nabla\xi\nabla uf

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\xi^{2}{\rm div}(f\nabla u)\right|+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\xi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}f+8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}|\nabla\xi|^{2}f.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}fdx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\xi^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}fdx\leq 2\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\xi^{2}{\rm div}(f\nabla u)\right|+16\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}|\nabla\xi|^{2}f.

From Equation (
2.3
) and (
2.19
), we see that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u\xi^{2}{\rm div}(f\nabla u)\right|
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}|\nabla\xi|^{2}f
are both uniformly bounded. Thus
\int_{B_{r}}f|\nabla u|^{2}dx

is uniformly bounded. The proof is completed.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is easy to see that the above argument works for a similar equation as (
2.3
) on a complete manifolds with a polynomial volume growth.

Now we can prove the LSI in the Euclidean space.

Proof of logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
2.1
)
. With Proposition
2.2
we can follow the idea in
[
12
]
to
give a proof of (
2.1
). Let
f_{\epsilon}
be the solution in Proposition
2.2
. We need to show that

(2.20)

\alpha_{\epsilon}\geq n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi).

Let us denote
u_{\epsilon}
by
u
, and define

(2.21)

U:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\nabla^{2}u(x)\geq 0\},

and a map
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

(2.22)

\Phi(x)=\nabla u(x)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

We have
\Phi(U)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

For any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, consider
q(x)=u(x)-x\cdot\xi
.
It is crucial that (
2.4
) implies that

q
attains a global minimum at
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is then easy to see that
\bar{x}\in U
and
\Phi(\bar{x})=\xi
, and hence
\Phi(U)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

We have that for any
x\in U
the Jacobian determinant of
\Phi
satisfies

0\leq e^{-\frac{|\Phi(x)|^{2}}{4}}\det\nabla\Phi(x)\leq f_{\epsilon}(x)e^{-n+\alpha_{\epsilon}}.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in
[
12
]
. We do not repeat it here, but refer to the proof of Lemma
3.4
below.
∎

Now (
2.20
) follows from

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{|\Phi(x)|^{2}}{4}}|\det\nabla\Phi(x)|1_{U}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\epsilon}(x)e^{-n+\alpha_{\epsilon}}dx=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-n+\alpha_{\epsilon}}.

Inequality (
2.20
), together with (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), implies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f_{\epsilon}|^{2}}{f_{\epsilon}}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\epsilon}\log f_{\epsilon}dx=\alpha_{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{\epsilon}dx\geq n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi).

By the dominated convergence, letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\log fdx\geq n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi),

for any non-negative
f
with compact support and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}fdx=1
. This finishes the proof of (
2.1
). ∎

3.
LSI for noncompact self-schrinkers

Let
\Sigma^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
be an
n
-dimensional complete, non-compact Riemannian submanifold with the induced metric
g
from
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. In this section, we use
\nabla
,
\nabla^{2}
,
\Delta
and
dvol
to denote the Levi-Civita connection, the Hessian, the Laplacian and the volume form with respect to
g
respectively. For a vector field
Y
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
, we use
Y^{\top}
and
Y^{\perp}
to denote the component of
Y
that is tangential to
\Sigma
and normal to
\Sigma
respectively. Let
II
and
H
be the second fundamental form and the mean curvature vector field respectively.

Consider on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
the function

f_{0}(x):=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}},

which is equal to
\gamma_{n}
if we restrict it to an
n
-dimensional subspace, and the function

u_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}.

We denote the restriction of
f_{0}
and
u_{0}
on
\Sigma
still by
f_{0}
and
u_{0}
respectively. In this section we shall also use the volume form

d\gamma=\gamma_{0}dvol=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}dvol

on
\Sigma
. Notice that it is not the restriction of the Gaussian measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
to
\Sigma
.

We first have the following simple, but as above crucial Lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

u_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}
. We have that
u_{0}
satisfies

(3.1)

{\rm div}(f_{0}\nabla u_{0})=f_{0}\log f_{0}-\frac{|\nabla f_{0}|^{2}}{f_{0}}+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}f_{0}-|H|^{2}f_{0}+\alpha_{0}f_{0},

where

\alpha_{0}=n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)
.

Proof.

See the proof for example in
[
17
]
. For the convenience of the reader and also fixing the notations, we give the computation briefly.
(
3.1
) is equivalent to

(3.2)

f_{0}^{-1}{\rm div}(f_{0}\nabla u_{0})=\log f_{0}-|\nabla\log f_{0}|^{2}+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}-|H|^{2}+\alpha_{0}.

It is easy to see

\nabla u_{0}=x^{\top},\quad\nabla\log f_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}x^{\top},\quad\Delta u_{0}=n+\langle x^{\perp},H\rangle.

Hence the left hand side of (
3.2
) is
n+\langle x^{\perp},H\rangle-\frac{1}{2}|x^{\top}|^{2}
, while the right hand side is

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)-\frac{1}{4}|x|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|x^{\top}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|x^{\bot}|^{2}+\langle x^{\perp},H\rangle+\alpha_{0}\\
&=&\displaystyle n+\langle x^{\bot},H\rangle-\frac{1}{2}|x^{\top}|^{2}.\end{array}

The proof is completed.
∎

As mentioned in the Introduction, in view of (
3.1
) we consider the following
equation

(3.3)

{\rm div}(f\nabla u)=f\log f-\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}f-|H|^{2}f+\alpha f,

which is slightly different to the one considered in
[
12
]
. As in Section 2, we attempt to find a solution pair

(u,\alpha)
of (
3.3
) with a suitable growth condition, which we define.

Definition 3.2

(Polynomial growth)
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
be a submanifold with induced metric
g
. We call
\Sigma
admitting a polynomial growth condition, if
\Sigma

has at most polynomial volume growth and its mean curvature vector field
H
has at most polynomial volume growth, i.e.

(3.4)

vol_{g}(\Sigma\cap B(r))\leq cr^{k}

and

|H|\leq cr^{l},

for some integers
k,l
and
c
, which is independent of
r
. Here
B(r)
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
of radius
r
.

(
3.4
) is called extrinsic polynomial volume growth condition.
Here for the simplicity we just use the extrinsic volume growth condition. In fact the intrinsic polynomial volume condition, which is weaker than the extrinsic one, is enough.

As discussed above, in order to obtain a logarithmic Sobolev inequality on
\Sigma
we only need to consider any positive function
f:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with compact support and with the normalization

\int_{\Sigma}f=1.

For
\epsilon>0
, let
f_{\epsilon}
be defined by

f_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}(f+\epsilon f_{0}).

The aim is to find a solution
u
of (
3.3
) with quadratic growth, for
f_{\epsilon}
. As above we only need to find a solution of

(3.5)

\Delta w+\nabla\log g\cdot\nabla w+\ell=c,

with a decay

(3.6)

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{w(x)}{|x|^{2}}=0.

Equation (
3.5
) is the difference of between Equation (
3.1
) and Equation (
3.3
).
Hence Equation (
3.5
) has the same form as (
2.10
).
It is clear that
\ell
is bounded in
\Sigma
. Therefore the same proof given in Section 2 yields a solution
w
, together with a constant
c
. Then
u=u_{0}+w
, together with
\alpha=\alpha_{0}+c
, is a solution of (
3.3
) with a quadratic growth which implies

(3.7)

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{u(x)}{|x|}=\infty.

A complete Riemannian manifold
(\Sigma,g)
has a
intrinsic polynomial volume growth
if there exist
\beta>0
and
C>0
such that
vol(B_{r})\leq Cr^{\beta}
for any
r>0
, where
B_{r}
is the geometric ball of radius
r
with respect to the metric
g
. It is clear that for submanifolds the extrinsic polynomial volume condition is stronger than the intrinsic one.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be an
n
-dimensional complete, non-compact submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
with polynomial
growth as in Definition
3.2
and
f:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
a non-negative function with compact support.
For
f_{\epsilon}

(\epsilon>0)
there exists a solution
u
of (
3.3
) with (
3.7
) and

(3.8)

\int_{\Sigma}{\rm div}(f_{\epsilon}\nabla u)=0.

Proof.

As discussed above we have a solution

u
of (
3.3
) with (
3.7
). Since
\Sigma

has the intrinsic polynomial
volume growth and its mean curvature vector has at most polynomial growth, the same proof for Lemma
2.4
works here to get (
3.8
). ∎

For this
u
we define as in
[
12
]

U:=\{(x,y)\,|\,x\in\Sigma,y\in T_{x}^{\perp}\Sigma,\nabla^{2}u(x)-\langle II(x),y\rangle\geq 0\}

and a map
\Phi:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n+m}

by

\Phi(x,y)=\nabla u(x)+y,

where
T_{x}\Sigma
is the normal space of

\Sigma
at
x
and
II(x)
is the second fundamental form.
The growth property (
3.7
), together with the proof of Lemma 2.1 in

[
12
]
, implies that

(3.9)

\Phi(U)=\mathbb{R}^{n+m}.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
(x,y)\in U
we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle 0\leq\det D\Phi(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det(\nabla^{2}u(x)-\langle II(x),y\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(x)e^{\frac{|\Phi(x,y)|^{2}}{4}-\frac{|2H(x)+y|^{2}}{4}+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}+\alpha-n}

Proof.

The equality has been proved in
[
11
]
. We show the last inequality.
From Equation (
3.3
)
we have

\displaystyle\Delta u(x)-\langle H(x),y\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt\log f_{\epsilon}(x)-|\nabla\log f_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}-\nabla\log f_{\epsilon}(x)\cdot\nabla u(x)

\displaystyle-|H(x)|^{2}+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H(x)\right|^{2}+\alpha-\langle H(x),y\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\log f_{\epsilon}(x)+\frac{|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+|y|^{2}}{4}-\frac{|2H(x)+y|^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H(x)\right|^{2}+\alpha.

We then have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\det(\nabla^{2}u(x)-\langle II(x),y\rangle)\leq e^{\Delta u-\langle H(x),y\rangle-n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f_{\epsilon}(x)e^{\frac{|\Phi(x,y)|^{2}}{4}-\frac{|2H(x)+y|^{2}}{4}+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}+\alpha-n},

since
|\Phi(x,y)|^{2}=|\nabla u|^{2}+|y|^{2}
.
∎

Now we can prove Theorem
1.2
. For the convenience of the reader we restate Theorem
1.2
here.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be an
n
-dimensional complete, non-compact submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
with polynomial
volume growth. Then for any positive function
f
on
\Sigma
with
\int_{\Sigma}fdvol=1
, there holds

(3.11)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left\{\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}-f\log f+|H|^{2}f\right\}dvol\\
&\geq&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046ptn+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)+\int_{\Sigma}f\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}dvol-\log\int_{\Sigma}f(x)e^{\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}}dvol.\end{array}

Proof.

We first show (
3.11
) for
f_{\epsilon}
. From (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n+m}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+m}}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4}}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq(4\pi)^{-\frac{n+m}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}\left(\int_{T^{\perp}_{x}\Sigma}e^{-\frac{|\Phi(x,y)|^{2}}{4}}|\det D\Phi(x,y)|1_{U}(x,y)\,dy\right)dvol(x)

\displaystyle\leq(4\pi)^{-\frac{n+m}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}f_{\epsilon}(x)\left(\int_{T^{\perp}_{x}\Sigma}e^{-\frac{|2H(x)+y|^{2}}{4}+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}+\alpha-n}dy\right)dvol(x)

\displaystyle=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\Sigma}f_{\epsilon}(x)e^{\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}+\alpha-n}dvol(x).

It follows
that

\alpha\geq n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)-\log\int_{\Sigma}f_{\epsilon}(x)e^{\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}}.

Integrating Equation (
3.3
) over
\Sigma
, together with (
3.8
), we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt\int_{\Sigma}\left\{-f_{\epsilon}\log f_{\epsilon}+f_{\epsilon}|\nabla\log f_{\epsilon}|^{2}-\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}f_{\epsilon}+|H|^{2}f_{\epsilon}\right\}dvol\\
&=&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046pt\alpha\int_{\Sigma}f_{\epsilon}dvol=\alpha\\
&\geq&\displaystyle n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi)-\log\int_{\Sigma}f_{\epsilon}(x)e^{\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}}.\end{array}

By letting
\epsilon\to 0
, we get the assertion.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

By Jensen’s inequality we know

\int_{\Sigma}f\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}dvol-\log\int_{\Sigma}f(x)e^{\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}}dvol\leq 0.

Hence (
3.11
)
is weaker than

(3.13)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left\{\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}-f\log f+|H|^{2}f\right\}dvol\geq\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046ptn+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi),\end{array}

which was proved by Brendle for compact submanifolds without boundary. It would be interesting to ask if (
3.13
) is also true for non-compact submanifolds with the polynomial growth condition.

It is interesting to see that in the important case, where
\Sigma
is a self-shrinker, ie.

\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H=0,

(
3.11
) is the same as (
3.13
). Now we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be an
n
dimensional complete, non-compact properly embedded self-shrinker in
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
. Then for any positive function
f
on
\Sigma
with
\int_{\Sigma}fdvol=1
(
3.13
) holds. Equivalently, for
any positive function
\varphi
with

\int_{\Sigma}\varphi d\gamma=1

(d\gamma=\gamma_{0}dvol)
, there holds

(3.14)

\int_{\Sigma}\varphi\log\varphi\,d\gamma\leq\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\,d\gamma.

Proof.

From
[
21
]
and
[
16
]
we know that for a self-shrinker the properly embeddedness is equivalent to the extrinsic polynomial volume growth. Moreover the self-shrinker condition clearly implies that the norm of
H
has linear growth. Hence Theorem
3.1
implies (
3.13
), since
\Sigma
is a self-shrinker. As computed in
[
12
]
, we see

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}-f\log f+|H|^{2}f-(n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi))f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\gamma_{0}-\varphi\log\varphi\gamma_{0}+\left|\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}f-{\rm div}_{\Sigma}(fx^{T}).

Integrating over
(\Sigma,dvol)
and using that
\Sigma
is a self-shrinker and
\int_{\Sigma}f=1
, we see that (
3.13
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}d\gamma-\varphi\log\varphi d\gamma\geq\int_{\Sigma}{\rm div}_{\Sigma}(fx^{T})dvol.

Note that for each
R>0
and
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma\cap B_{R}}{\rm div}_{\Sigma}(fx^{T})dvol=\int_{\Sigma\cap\partial B_{R}}f|x^{T}|dA\geq 0.

(
3.14
) follows by letting
R\to\infty
.

∎

Remark 3.6
.

In Theorem
1.1
without the normalization
\int_{\Sigma}\varphi d\gamma=1
, (
1.3
) is written as

(3.15)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}\,d\gamma\geq\int_{\Sigma}\varphi\log\varphi\,d\gamma-\int_{\Sigma}\varphi d\gamma\log\int_{\Sigma}\varphi d\gamma.

In general
d\gamma=\gamma_{0}dvol
may not be a probability measure on
\Sigma
. In fact,

\int_{\Sigma}1d\gamma=\lambda(\Sigma)
, the entropy of
\Sigma
, since
\Sigma
is self-shrinker. See
[
17
]
. If we use the probability measure,
d\tilde{\gamma}=\lambda(\Sigma)^{-1}d\gamma
, then (
1.2
) can be written as

(3.16)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{\psi}\,d\tilde{\gamma}-\int_{\Sigma}\psi\log\psi\,d\tilde{\gamma}\geq-\log\lambda(\Sigma)

for any non-negative
\psi

with
\int_{\Sigma}\psi d\tilde{\gamma}=1
. For such a self-shrinker
\Sigma
, we know
\lambda(\Sigma)\geq 1
. See
[
17
]
.
We define an invariant by

\mu_{\Sigma}:=\inf_{0\not\equiv\psi\geq 0,\int_{\Sigma}\psi d\tilde{\gamma}=1}\left\{\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|\nabla\psi|^{2}}{\psi}\,d\tilde{\gamma}-\int_{\Sigma}\psi\log\psi\,d\tilde{\gamma}\right\}.

(
3.16
) means that

(3.17)

\mu_{\Sigma}\geq-\log\lambda(\Sigma).

It would be interesting to discuss the relationship between
\mu_{\Sigma}
and
-\log\lambda(\Sigma)
.

Remark 3.7
.

Let
f_{\tau}=(4\pi\tau)^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4\tau}}
for
\tau>0
. It is easy to check as above that
u_{0}=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}
satisfies

f_{\tau}^{-1}{\rm div}(f_{\tau}\nabla u_{0})=\log f_{\tau}-\tau|\nabla\log f_{\tau}|^{2}-\tau|H|^{2}+\tau|H+\frac{x^{\bot}}{2\tau}|^{2}+\alpha_{\tau},

where
\alpha_{\tau}=n+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau).
The same proof yields
the following inequality

(3.18)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}&&\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left\{\tau\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}-f\log f+\tau|H|^{2}f\right\}dvol\\
&\geq&\displaystyle\vskip 5.69046ptn+\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\tau\left|\frac{1}{2\tau}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}f\,dvol-\log\int_{\Sigma}e^{\tau\left|\frac{1}{2\tau}x^{\perp}+H\right|^{2}}f\,dvol,\end{array}

which implies that

(3.19)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left\{\tau\frac{|\nabla f|^{2}}{f}-f\log f+\tau|H|^{2}f\right\}dvol-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)\geq n,\end{array}

for non-negative function
f
with
\int_{\Sigma}f=1
,
if
\Sigma
is a properly embedded self-shrinker.

From above remarks one can compare the
logarithmic Sobolev inequalities for self-shrinkers of mean curvature flow and the ones for gradient solitons of Ricci flow obtained by Carrillo-Ni
[

]
and Hein-Naber
[

]
, where the non-negativity of the Ricci curvature
or the

condition play a crucial role.

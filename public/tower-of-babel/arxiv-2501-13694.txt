Abstract
. A mutation operation for τ \tau -exceptional sequences of modules over any finite-dimensional algebra was recently introduced, generalising the mutation for exceptional sequences of modules over hereditary algebras. We interpret this mutation in terms of TF-ordered τ \tau -rigid modules, which are in bijection with τ \tau -exceptional sequences. As an application we show that the mutation is transitive for Nakayama algebras, by providing an explicit combinatorial description of mutation over this class of algebras.

1.
Introduction

More than thirty years ago the study of exceptional sequences in both triangulated
[
Bon89
,
Gor88
,
GR87
,
Rud90
]
and abelian categories
[
Cra93
,
Rin94
]
was initiated. Fundamentally, an exceptional sequence consists of exceptional objects, which are objects whose endomorphism rings are division rings and which have no self-extensions of any degree. Moreover, the objects in an exceptional sequence satisfy a
right-to-left vanishing property of
\Hom
- and
\Ext
-groups.

In a triangulated category, an exceptional sequence is called
complete
if the smallest triangulated subcategory generated by its exceptional objects is the whole category. In this case, there exists a Braid group action by mutation on the set of such sequences
[
Bon89
,
Gor89
]
and it was conjectured in
[
BP93
]
that this action is always transitive. This had been the focus of much attention
[
IOU21
,
KM02
,
Mel95
,
CS23
]
until a counterexample was found in
[
CHS23
]
.

In an abelian category an exceptional sequence is called
complete
if the objects generate the derived category as above. Generally, the existence of complete exceptional sequences is not guaranteed, but has important consequences, see e.g.
[
CS23
,
HP19
,
Kra17
]
. We are particularly interested in categories of modules of finite-dimensional algebras. It is a classical result that complete exceptional sequences exist for hereditary algebras and that the action of the Braid group is transitive
[
Cra93
,
Rin94
]
.

However, the module category of an arbitrary finite-dimensional algebra generally does not admit complete exceptional sequences. It is therefore natural to consider generalisations of exceptional sequences
[
Ara99
,
BM21
,
Sen21
]
. In particular, the generalisation proposed in
[
BM21
]
utilises
\tau
-tilting theory to ensure the existence of complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences for all finite-dimensional algebras. The
\Ext
-vanishing condition in this generalisation is modified using a key idea of
\tau
-tilting theory which replaces the condition
\Ext^{1}(N,M)=0
by
\Hom(M,\tau N)=0
, since the two are equivalent for hereditary algebras but generally the second is by
[
AS81
]
equivalent to the condition
\Ext^{1}(M,N^{\prime})=0
for all modules
N^{\prime}

generated
by
N
, meaning there is an epimorphism
N^{r}\to N^{\prime}
for some integer
r
. We collect all modules generated by
N
in the subcategory

\mathsf{Gen}N=\{X\mid\text{there is an epimorphism }N^{r}\to X\text{ for some integer }r\}.

It is natural to ask if there exists a well-defined action of mutation on complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences and whether such an action is transitive and satisfies Braid group relations
[
BM23
]
. To answer these questions it is useful to consider the more general
signed

\tau
-exceptional sequences whose additional structure is a positive or negative sign on certain relatively projective modules in a
\tau
-exceptional sequence. Whereas the unsigned
\tau
-exceptional sequences label chains in the poset of wide subcategories
[
BH22
]
, the signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences give rise to an even richer structure. In fact, they correspond to factorisations of morphism into irreducible ones in the
\tau
-cluster morphism category
[
IT17
,
BM19
,
BH23
]
, see also
[

,
Bør24
]
for more general settings, whose classifying space has been shown to be a

space in many cases
[

,
HI21a
,
HI21
,
IT17
,
IT22
,
Kai23
,
Kai24
]
.

An important alternative viewpoint of signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences establishes a close connection with the
\tau
-tilting pairs central to
\tau
-tilting theory: There exists a bijection between signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences of length

and ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs with

non-isomorphic indecomposable direct summands
[
BM21
]
. Alongside establishing a close connection between
\tau
-exceptional sequences and stratifying systems, the authors of
[

]
characterise those ordered

-rigid pairs which correspond to the unsigned

-exceptional sequences. We will refer to those as

-rigid modules.

Recently, a positive answer to the existence of a mutation of

-exceptional sequences was provided in

]

\tau
-exceptional pairs. This provides the starting point for the present paper, in which we begin by translating the mutation rules defined in
[

]

-rigid modules. A key role in this description is played by what we refer to as the

-tilting pair to those of the maximal

to define the

-rigid pair

(

Let

2.
Background

All algebras in this paper are assumed to be finite-dimensional over an arbitrary fixed field
K
. Let
\Lambda
be a finite-dimensional algebra, and let
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
we denote the category of finite-dimensional left
\Lambda
-modules. We will always assume modules to be basic, and define
|M|
to be the number of indecomposable direct summands of a module
M
. Throughout this document, let
n
denote the rank of
\Lambda
, that is, the number of simple
\Lambda
-modules,
up to isomorphism.
Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
be a full subcategory. A morphism
M\to N
is called a
left
\mathcal{X}
-approximation
of
M
if the induced morphism
\Hom(N,X)\to\Hom(M,X)
is an epimorphism for all
X\in\mathcal{X}
. Right approximations are defined dually. A map
f:M\to N
is called
left minimal
if every endomorphism
g\in\End(N)
satisfying
g\circ f=f
is an isomorphism. A subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
is called
functorially finite
if every object in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
has a left
\mathcal{C}
-approximation and a right
\mathcal{C}
-approximation.
We let
\mathsf{add}M
denote the additive closure of a module
M
. Note that
\mathsf{add}M
is automatically functorially finite for any module
M
.

Given a subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
, let us define the
\Hom
-orthogonal categories as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\{M\in\mathsf{mod}\Lambda\mid\forall X\in\mathcal{C},\Hom_{\Lambda}(X,M)=0\},

\displaystyle{}^{\perp}\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{M\in\mathsf{mod}\Lambda\mid\forall X\in\mathcal{C},\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,X)=0\}.

A particularly important class of subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
are the
torsion classes
, as introduced in
[
Dic66
]
. A torsion class
\mathcal{T}
is a full subcategory of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
which is closed under quotients and extensions. Its right
\Hom
-orthogonal category
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
is a
torsion-free class
, which by definition is a full subcategory of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
closed under submodules and extensions. Given a module
M\in\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
, such a
torsion pair

(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}^{\perp})
induces by
[
Dic66
]
a short exact sequence

0\to t_{\mathcal{T}}M\to M\to f_{\mathcal{T}}M\to 0,

uniquely defined by the requirements
t_{\mathcal{T}}M\in\mathcal{T}
and
f_{\mathcal{T}}M\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
. Another central class of subcategories of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
are the
wide subcategories
first considered in the context of abelian categories in
[
Hov01
]
. A full subcategory
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
is called wide if it is closed under kernels, cokernels and extensions. Torsion classes, torsion-free classes and wide subcategories all form partially ordered sets under inclusion, which we denote by
\mathsf{tors}\Lambda
,
\mathsf{torf}\Lambda
and
\mathsf{wide}\Lambda
respectively.

2.1.
\tau
-tilting theory

Following
[

, we give in this section an introduction to

\tau
denote the Auslander-Reiten translation of

Definition 2.1
.

[
AIR14
, Def. 0.1]

A module

is called
\tau
-rigid
if
\Hom(M,\tau M)=0
and
\tau
-tilting
if additionally

We also need the notion of

Definition 2.2

A

\Lambda
is a pair
(M,N)\in\mathsf{mod}\Lambda\times\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
. We consider a pair
(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
to be a direct summand of a pair

is a direct summand of

N^{\prime}
is a direct summand of

. Furthermore, pairs of modules of the form

play a special role in this paper, and we call these pairs of modules

.
The pair

is called

both
M
and

are basic. We will always only work with basic pairs of modules.

A formally signed module of the form

is said to have sign

and a formally signed module of the form

is said to have sign

. A formally signed module with sign

The central objects of

.

[

A pair

Remark 2.4

Note that in the definition of

Proposition 2.6

Let
T=(M,P)
be a
\tau
-rigid pair. A signed module
X\in\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{tilt}_{T}\Lambda
is a summand of the co-Bongartz complement of
T
if and only if
X
is signed projective or a module in
\mathsf{Gen}M
.

Proof.

Assume first that
X
is signed projective or in
\mathsf{Gen}M
.
Let
T^{\prime}=T\oplus X=(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
. Let
C=(N,Q)
be the co-Bongartz completion of
T
and let
C^{\prime}=(N^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
be the co-Bongartz completion of
T^{\prime}
. By our assumption on
X
, we have
\mathsf{Gen}M=\mathsf{Gen}M^{\prime}
. Since
C
and
C^{\prime}
are co-Bongartz completions, we thus have

\mathsf{Gen}C=\mathsf{Gen}M=\mathsf{Gen}M^{\prime}=\mathsf{Gen}C^{\prime}.

But since
C
and
C^{\prime}
are
\tau
-tilting pairs corresponding to the same torsion class, we must have
C=C^{\prime}
.

Conversely, let
X
be a direct summand of the co-Bongartz completion
C=(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
of
T=(M,P)
. Assume that
X
is not a shifted projective. Then
X\in\mathsf{add}M^{\prime}
so
X\in\mathsf{Gen}M^{\prime}=\mathsf{Gen}M
as wanted. This finishes the proof.
∎

The Bongartz completion and co-Bongartz completion of a
\tau
-rigid pair
T
are distinct pairs unless
T
is
\tau
-tilting. If
T
is almost complete, that is
|T|=n-1
, then there are exactly two
\tau
-tilting pairs
T^{+}
and
T^{-}
having
T
as a direct summand, namely the Bongartz completion and co-Bongartz completion of
T
. Further, these two completions are related via
\tau
-tilting mutation
[
AIR14
, Def.-Prop. 2.28]
.

Definition-Proposition 2.7
.

[
AIR14
, Def.-Prop. 2.28]
Let
{T=}(M\oplus X,P)
be a
\tau
-tilting pair with
X
indecomposable. If
X\notin\mathsf{Gen}M
then we may
left mutate

T
at
X
. Let
f\colon X\to M^{\prime}
be a minimal left
\mathsf{add}M
-approximation of
X
. Let
Y=\coker f
. Then the left mutation of
(M\oplus X,P)
at
X
is defined as

\mu_{X}(M\oplus X,P)=\begin{cases}(M\oplus Y,P)&\text{if $Y\neq 0$,}\\
(M,P\oplus Q)&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where in the last case,
Q
is the unique projective module making
(M,P\oplus Q)
a
\tau
-tilting pair.

Another viewpoint commonly used to study
\tau
-tilting theory is that of (2-term) silting theory as introduced in
[
KV88
]
, where we instead of working with
\tau
-rigid pairs work with 2-term pre-silting objects in the bounded homotopy category of finitely generated projective modules
K^{b}(\mathsf{proj}\Lambda)
. This is also related to the approach in
[
DF15
]
. We refer to
[
AIR14
, Sec. 3]

for definitions and details on (2-term) silting theory.

Definition 2.8
.

Given a
\tau
-rigid pair
T=(M,P)
over
\Lambda
, we define by
\mathbb{P}_{T}
the two-term complex concentrated in degrees
-1
and
0
given by

\mathbb{P}_{T}\colon\quad P_{-1}\oplus P\xrightarrow{\scriptscriptstyle\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\end{pmatrix}}P_{0}\quad\in K^{b}(\mathsf{proj}\Lambda),

where
P_{-1}\xrightarrow{\alpha}P_{0}\to M
is a minimal projective presentation of
M
.

By
[
AIR14
, Thm. 3.1]
,
\mathbb{P}_{(-)}
gives by a bijection between
\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}\Lambda
and the
2
-term presilting objects living in degrees
-1
and

of

, whose inverse is given by associating to a
2
-term pre-silting complex
\mathbb{P}\cong\mathbb{Q}\oplus P[1]
the
\tau
-rigid pair
(H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}),P)
. The connection with silting justifies calling a summand
(0,Q)
of a

-rigid pair a shifted projective, since it can be seen as the stalk complex

.

Using the Bongartz completion of a module, Jasso defined in
[
Jas15
]
what is known as
\tau
-tilting reduction or Jasso reduction. The work of Jasso has been extended for example by

]
,

]

]
. Let

be a

This subcategory of

is wide by

and the

2.2.
\tau
-exceptional sequences and TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid modules

Introduced as a generalisation of signed exceptional sequences
[
IT17
]
, signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences were defined and studied in
[
BM21
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

[
BM21
, Def. 1.3]

A sequence of indecomposable formally signed modules

(M_{1}[s_{1}],M_{2}[s_{2}],\dots,M_{t}[s_{t}])

is called a
signed
\tau
-exceptional sequence
of length
t
in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
if:

(1)

M_{t}[s_{t}]\in\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}\Lambda
, and

(2)

if
t>1
, then
(M_{1}[s_{1}],\dots,M_{t-1}[s_{t-1}])
is a signed
\tau
-exceptional sequence of length
t-1
in
J(M_{t})(\simeq\mathsf{mod}\Lambda^{\prime})
.

Note the recursive nature of the above definition, where keeping track of the relevant ambient module category is key: A module
M
may be
\tau
-rigid (or projective) in a wide subcategory
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{X}\subset\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
while not being
\tau
-rigid (or projective) in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
.

A signed
\tau
-exceptional sequence where all elements have sign
0
is called an
unsigned
\tau
-exceptional sequence
or simply a
\tau
-exceptional sequence. By a
complete
(signed)
\tau
-exceptional sequence we mean a sequence of length equal to the rank of the algebra in question.

In
[
BM21
]
, an explicit bijection between the set of signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences of length
t
in
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
and the set of
ordered
basic
\tau
-rigid pairs over
\Lambda
with
t
indecomposable direct summands is given.

Definition 2.10
.

An
ordered module
is a pair
(M,\sigma)
, where
M
is a module and
\sigma
a bijection
\{1,2,\dots,|M|\}\to\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{add}M)
where
\mathsf{ind}(\mathcal{C})
is the set consisting of the indecomposable objects in a category
\mathcal{C}
up to isomorphism. Similarly, an ordered pair of modules
(M,N)
is given by a pair
((M,N),\sigma)
where
\sigma
is a bijection
\{1,2,\dots,|M|+|N|\}\to\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{add}M)\cup\{X[1]\mid X\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{add}N)\}
. We will now define some notions for ordered modules and remark that the corresponding notions can easily be translated to the context of ordered pairs of modules.

Given an ordered module
(M,\sigma)
and a permutation
\phi\colon\{1,2,\dots,|M|\}\to\{1,2,\dots,|M|\}
, we define
\phi(M,\sigma)=(M,\sigma\circ\phi^{-1})
. Letting
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denote the symmetric group of permutations on
\{1,2,3,\dots,n\}
, the above gives a left
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action on ordered modules with
n
summands, permuting their summands.

For an ordered module
(M,\sigma)
and an integer
1\leq i\leq|M|
, we let
(M,\sigma)_{i}=\sigma(i)
. Also, we define for integers
1\leq i\leq|M|
the ordered modules
(M,\sigma)_{<i}
and
(M,\sigma)_{>i}
as
(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{i-1}\sigma(j),\sigma_{<i})
and
(\bigoplus_{j=i+1}^{|M|}\sigma(j),\sigma_{>i})
where
\sigma_{<i}
is the restriction of
\sigma
to
\{1,2,3,\dots,i-1\}
and
\sigma_{>i}
is defined by setting
\sigma_{>i}(x)=\sigma(x+i)
for
1\leq x\leq|M|-i
. We define
(M,\sigma)_{\geq i}
and
(M,\sigma)_{\leq i}
analogously, so for example
(M,\sigma)_{\leq 1}=M_{1}
and
(M,\sigma)_{<1}=0
.

By abuse of notation, we will usually denote an ordered module
(M,\sigma)
simply by
M
or by the sum
\sigma(1)\oplus\sigma(2)\oplus\dots\oplus\sigma(|M|)
. Note also that for a given decomposition of a module
M
into indecomposable direct summands
M\cong M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{t}
there is a canonical choice of ordered module
(M,\sigma)
, namely given by defining
\sigma(i)=M_{i}
.

Example 2.11
.

Let
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus M_{3}
denote an ordered module, which we formally may interpret as the object
M=(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}M_{i},i\mapsto M_{i})
. Let
\theta
be the cycle
(1\quad 2\quad 3)
. Then

\theta(M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus M_{3})=(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}M_{i},i\mapsto M_{\theta^{-1}(i)})=M_{3}\oplus M_{1}\oplus M_{2}.

We will often utilise the bijection between ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs and signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences and now recall some notation from
[
BM21
]
.

Proposition 2.12
.

[
BM21
, Thm. 5.4]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a module category. There is a bijection

\{\text{Ordered $\tau$-rigid pairs in $\mathcal{C}$ with $t$ indecomposable direct summands}\}

\Psi^{\mathcal{C}}_{t}\Big\downarrow

\{\text{Signed $\tau$-exceptional sequences in $\mathcal{C}$ of length $t$}\}

and we denote by
\Psi^{\mathcal{C}}
the bijection between all ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs in
\mathcal{C}
and all signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences in
\mathcal{C}
. Formally, we let
\Psi^{\mathcal{C}}(T)=\Psi^{\mathcal{C}}_{|T|}(T)
. In contexts where there is no ambiguity about which category we are considering, we may drop the superscript.

We say that
T\in\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}\mathcal{C}

induces
the
\tau
-exceptional sequence
\Psi^{\mathcal{C}}(T)
. In
[
MT20
]
, the authors describe explicitly the ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs that induce unsigned
\tau
-exceptional sequences, and show that these are exactly the so-called
TF-ordered

\tau
-rigid modules.

Definition 2.13
.

[
MT20
, Def. 3.1]

An ordered
\tau
-rigid
\Lambda
-module
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{t}
is called TF-ordered if
M_{i}\notin\mathsf{Gen}(M_{>i})
for all
1\leq i\leq t-1
.

Actually, for any
\tau
-rigid module
M
, there is always
at least one ordering of its summands such that
M
with this ordering
is TF-ordered, by
[
MT20
, Thm. 1.2(a)]
. We will call such orderings the
TF-orders of
M
.

Recall that
f_{N}
denotes the torsion-free functor associated to the torsion pair
(\mathsf{Gen}N,N^{\perp})
for a
\tau
-rigid module
N
.

Using the above notion, Mendoza and Treffinger give the following characterisation of (unsigned)
\tau
-exceptional sequences:

Theorem 2.14
.

[
MT20
, Thm. 5.1]

Let
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{t}
be a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module over an algebra
\Lambda
. Then

(f_{M_{>1}}(M_{1}),f_{M_{>2}}(M_{2}),\dots,M_{t})=\Psi(M)

and all
\tau
-exceptional sequences over
\Lambda
are uniquely given by
\Psi(T)
for some TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module
T
. Thus
\Psi
restricts to a bijection between TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid modules and unsigned
\tau
-exceptional sequences over
\Lambda
.

3.
Mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences and corresponding TF-ordered modules

In this section we will utilise the correspondence between
\tau
-exceptional sequences and TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid modules to study how the mutation of
\tau
-exceptional sequences as introduced in
[
BHM24
]
corresponds to a mutation on TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid
modules.

3.1.
The
V
and
E
-maps

We have seen, in
Theorem

2.14
, how to compute
\Psi(M)
for a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module
M
. To compute
\Psi
generally, i.e to describe how ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs correspond to signed
\tau
-exceptional sequences, we need the
E
-map as introduced in
[
BM21
]
. To define this map, an explicit bijection between the summands of the Bongartz complement of a
\tau
-rigid pair
T
and the summands of the co-Bongartz complement of the same pair is introduced in
[
BM21
]
. As this map plays a central role in this paper, we begin by recalling their work and denote this map by
V
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
T
be a
\tau
-rigid pair. Recall that
\overline{\mathrm{C}}(T)
and
\overline{\mathrm{B}}(T)
are the co-Bongartz complement and Bongartz complement of
T
respectively. Define
V_{T}\colon\mathsf{add}(\overline{\mathrm{C}}(T))\to\mathsf{add}(\overline{\mathrm{B}}(T))
as follows: Let
X\in\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{add}(\overline{\mathrm{C}}(T)))
and let
\mathbb{P}_{X}
and
\mathbb{P}_{T}
be the two-term presilting objects associated to
X
and
T
respectively. Let now
\alpha\colon\mathbb{P}_{T}^{+}\to\mathbb{P}_{X}
be a minimal right
\mathsf{add}(\mathbb{P}_{T})
-approximation of
\mathbb{P}_{X}
and set
\mathbb{Y}=\mathsf{Cone}(\alpha)[-1]
. Then
V_{T}(X)
is defined to be
H^{0}(\mathbb{Y})
.

Sometimes we can simplify the computation of the
V
-map. A few special cases are discussed below.

Remark 3.2
.

If
T=(0,0)
in
Definition

3.1
, the
V
-map as defined may be computed and will send a shifted projective
P[1]
to the module
P
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
BM21
, Sec. 4]

Let
P[1]\oplus Q[1]
be a basic shifted projective. Then
V_{Q[1]}(P[1])=f_{Q}P
.

Proof.

We prove the statement here for completeness, but remark that the lemma follows from
[
BM21
, Def. 4.10, Rmk. 4.11]
.
Let
\mathbb{P}_{P[1]}
and
\mathbb{P}_{Q[1]}
denote the shifted stalk complexes of
P
and
Q
respectively, so the
\mathsf{add}(\mathbb{P}_{Q[1]})
-approximation
\alpha
of
\mathbb{P}_{P[1]}
considered in
Definition

3.1
is given by a (shifted)
\mathsf{add}Q
-approximation
\beta
of
P
. The negative shift of the cone of
\alpha
is then given by the two-term complex
\mathsf{Cone}(\alpha)[-1]\colon Q^{+}\xrightarrow{\beta}P
. As
\beta
is a right approximation, the image of
\beta
must be equal to the trace of
Q
in
P
, so
H^{0}(\mathsf{Cone}(\alpha)[-1])=f_{Q}P
.
∎

The following special cases will be useful in later sections.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
L,M,N
be indecomposable
\tau
-rigid modules.

(a)

If there exists a right exact sequence

L\to M^{\prime}\to N\to 0

with
M^{\prime}\in\mathsf{add}M
,
L\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(M))
and
N\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{C}(M))
, and the first map is a left
\mathsf{add}M
-approximation, then
V_{M}(N)\cong L
.

(b)

If there exists an exact sequence

0\to L\to M^{\prime}\to N\to 0

with
M^{\prime}\in\mathsf{add}M
,
L\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(M))
and
N\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{C}(M))
, then
V_{M}(N)\cong L
.

(c)

If
N\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(M))
and
N[1]\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{C}(M))
, then
V_{M}(N[1])\cong N
.

Proof.

Part (a) is a reformulation of
[
BM19
, Def. 3.1, part I(b)]
, noting that the map
L\to M
is automatically left minimal, since
N
is indecomposable and not in
\mathsf{add}M
by assumption.

For part (b), since
N\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{C}(M))
, we have that
M\oplus N
is
\tau
-rigid and so
M\in{}^{\perp}\tau N
, and hence
\Ext^{1}(N,M)=0
by
[
AS81
, Prop. 5.8]
. Applying
\Hom(-,M)
to the short exact sequence gives that
\Hom(M^{\prime},M)\to\Hom(L,M)
is an epimorphism, and so the map
L\to M^{\prime}
is a left
\mathsf{add}M
-approximation. It then follows from (a)
that
V_{M}(N)\cong L
.

For part (c), we apply
[
BM19
, Def. 3.1, part I(c)]
. Since
N\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(M))
, the exact sequence
N\to\overline{\mathrm{B}}(T)\to M^{\prime}\to 0
with
N\to\overline{\mathrm{B}}(T)
a minimal left
\mathrm{B}(M)
approximation simply becomes
N\to N\to 0
i.e.
\overline{\mathrm{B}}(T)\cong N
. The claim follows directly from this.
∎

We now recall the definition of the
E
-map as first introduced in
[
BM21
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

[
BM21
, Prop. 5.6, Prop. 5.8, Prop 5.11]
For a
\tau
-rigid pair
T=(M,P)
in a module category
\mathcal{C}
, we define the
reduction map

E^{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\colon\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}_{T}\mathcal{C}\to\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}J(T).

Assume first that
T
is on the form
(M,0)
or
(0,P)
. We then define
E_{T}(X)
for an indecomposable
X\in\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}_{T}\mathcal{C}
as

E^{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(X)=\begin{cases}f_{T}X&\text{if both $T$ and $X$ are $\tau$-rigid modules, and $X\not\in\mathsf{Gen}T$,}\\
X&\text{if $T$ is a shifted projective and $X$ is a $\tau$-rigid module,}\\
(f_{T[-1]}(X[-1]))[1]&\text{if $T$ is a shifted projective and $X$ is a shifted projective,}\\
f_{T}(V_{T}(X))[1]&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that we are in the last case if
T
is a
\tau
-rigid module and
X\in\mathsf{Gen}T
or
X
is a shifted projective. By
Proposition

2.6
this is equivalent to
X
being a summand of the co-Bongartz complement of
T
, hence
V_{T}(X)
is well-defined.

Further, if
T=(M,P)
for
M\neq 0
and
P\neq 0
, we define
E^{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(X)=E^{J(M)}_{E^{\mathcal{C}}_{M}(P[1])}(E^{\mathcal{C}}_{M}(X))
. Lastly, we extend
E^{\mathcal{C}}_{T}
additively, so that for
U\in\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}_{T}\mathcal{C}
we have
E^{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(U)=\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq t}E^{\mathcal{C}}_{T}(U_{i})
for a decomposition of
U
into indecomposable direct summands
U=U_{1}\oplus U_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus U_{t}
.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that an additive function
f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between sets of (pairs of) modules taking indecomposable objects to indecomposable objects may be considered a function on
ordered
(pairs of) modules by defining
f(M,\sigma)=(f(M),f\circ\sigma)
for
M\in\mathcal{C}
.

In particular, we will let
E^{\mathcal{C}}_{T}
take ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs in
\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}_{T}\mathcal{C}
to ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs in
\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}J(T)
.

By
[
BM21
, Prop. 5.6,Prop 5.11]
the
E
-map defined above is bijective. When there is no ambiguity regarding the ambient category
\mathcal{C}
we may omit the superscript. The following formula regarding associativity of the
E
-map was first investigated in the
\tau
-tilting finite case in
[
BM19
]
and in the general case in
[
BH23
]
.

Theorem 3.7
.

[
BH23
, Thm. 6.12]

Let
T
be a basic
\tau
-rigid pair in
\mathcal{C}
. The reduction induced by the
E
-map is associative in the sense that

E^{\mathcal{C}}_{T}=E^{J(M)}_{E^{\mathcal{C}}_{M}(N)}\circ E^{\mathcal{C}}_{M}

holds for any decomposition
T=M\oplus N
.

Using a reformulation of
[
Jas15
, Thm. 3.15]
we obtain a dual to
Proposition

2.6
, characterising exactly when
X\in\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{tilt}_{T}\mathcal{C}
is a direct summand of the Bongartz complement of
T
.

Theorem 3.8
.

[
Jas15
, Thm. 3.15]

Let
T=(M,P)
be a
\tau
-rigid pair in a module category
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
. Then
E_{T}
restricts to an order-preserving bijection between
\tau
-tilting pairs on the form
T\oplus U
and
\tau
-tilting pairs in
J(T)
.

Proof.

Since
E_{T}\colon\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}_{T}\Lambda\to\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}J(T)
is a bijection and
|E_{T}(U)|=|U|
for all
U\in\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{rigid}_{T}\Lambda
, we only need to prove the order-preserving part of the statement.

Let
U_{1}=(N_{1},Q_{1})
and
U_{2}=(N_{2},Q_{2})
be
\tau
-rigid pairs such that
U_{1}\oplus T
and
U_{2}\oplus T
are
\tau
-tilting pairs. Assume that
U_{1}\oplus T\geq U_{2}\oplus T
, meaning that
\mathsf{Gen}(M\oplus N_{1})\supseteq\mathsf{Gen}(M\oplus N_{2})
.

Let
E_{T}(U_{1})=(N^{\prime}_{1},Q^{\prime}_{1})
and
E_{T}(U_{2})=(N^{\prime}_{2},Q^{\prime}_{2})
. Then
N^{\prime}_{1}\cong f_{M}(N_{1}\oplus M)
and
N^{\prime}_{2}\cong f_{M}(N_{2}\oplus M)
, and by
[
Jas15
, Thm. 3.15]
we have therefore have
N^{\prime}_{1}\geq N^{\prime}_{2}
in

in

.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let

\tau
-rigid pair in a module category

Proof.

Let

We now describe the the map

Theorem 3.10
.

[

For any module category

Note that in the above theorem, we ignore all but the outermost parenthesis, so for example we write

Using

3.7
, we get the following useful formula, which generalises

2.14
.

Proposition 3.11
.

For
T=T_{1}\oplus T_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus T_{t}
an ordered
\tau
-rigid pair with
t
indecomposable direct summands, we have

\Psi(T)=(E_{T_{>1}}(T_{1}),E_{T_{>2}}(T_{2}),\dots,E_{T_{t}}(T_{t-1}),T_{t}).

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

. For

\Psi(T_{1})=(T_{1})
as wanted, by
Theorem

3.10
.

Assume now that the claim holds for all ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs with at most
t
summands and over any algebra. Let now
T=T^{\prime}\oplus T_{t+1}=T_{1}\oplus T_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus T_{t}\oplus T_{t+1}
be an ordered
\tau
-rigid pair with
|T|=t+1
.

Using the definition of
\Psi
, we get the first equality in the computation below:

\displaystyle\Psi(T)

\displaystyle=(\Psi^{J(T_{t+1})}_{t}(E_{T_{t+1}}(T^{\prime})),T_{t+1})

\displaystyle=((E^{J(T_{t+1})}_{E_{T_{t+1}}(T^{\prime})_{>1}}(E_{T_{t+1}}(T^{\prime})_{1}),\dots,E^{J(T_{t+1})}_{E_{T_{t+1}}(T^{\prime})_{t}}(E_{T_{t+1}}(T^{\prime})_{t-1}),E_{T_{t+1}}(T^{\prime})_{t}),T_{t+1})

\displaystyle=((E_{T_{>1}}(T^{\prime}_{1}),\dots,E_{T_{>t-1}}(T^{\prime}_{t-1}),E_{T_{t+1}}(T^{\prime}_{t})),T_{t+1})

\displaystyle=(E_{T_{>1}}(T_{1}),\dots,E_{T_{>t-1}}(T_{t-1}),E_{T_{t+1}}(T_{t}),T_{t+1}).

The second equality follows from our induction hypothesis (since
E_{T_{t+1}}(T^{\prime})
is a
\tau
-rigid module in

with

We first consider two operations on (signed)

We define

Remark 3.13
.

Note that by

we have that
\pi_{1}(B,C)=(E_{E^{-1}_{C}(B)}(C),E^{-1}_{C}(B))

The following special cases will be useful later.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
T=M\oplus N
be an ordered
\tau
-rigid pair such that
E_{N}(M)
is a projective module in
J(N)
. Then we have the following equality of ordered pairs:

\overline{s}_{1}(M\oplus N)=V_{N}^{-1}(M)\oplus N.

Proof.

Note first that
V_{N}^{-1}(M)
is well-defined as by

Corollary

3.9
we have that

being projective in
J(N)
is equivalent to
M
being in the Bongartz complement of
N
.
By definition of
\overline{s}_{1}
, the claimed equality is equivalent to

\Psi(V^{-1}_{N}(M)\oplus N)=s_{1}(\Psi(M\oplus N))
, and
by definition we have

s_{1}(\Psi(M\oplus N))=s_{1}(E_{N}(M)\oplus N)=E_{N}(M)[1]\oplus N.

On the other hand, we have
\Psi(V^{-1}_{N}(M)\oplus N)=E_{N}(V^{-1}_{N}(M))\oplus N
, so it is sufficient to show that

E_{N}(V^{-1}_{N}(M))=E_{N}(M)[1].

Consider first the case where
N
has sign
0
, i.e is a module. Since
V_{N}^{-1}(M)
is a direct summand of the co-Bongartz completion of
N
, it is either generated by
N
or a shifted projective by
Proposition

2.6
, so that we obtain,

\displaystyle E_{N}(V_{N}^{-1}(M))

\displaystyle=f_{N}(V_{N}(V_{N}^{-1}(M)))[1]=f_{N}(M)[1]=E_{N}(M)[1],

where the first and last equalities follow from
Definition

3.5
, noting that
M\notin\mathsf{Gen}N
as otherwise

would not be projective in
J(M)
.

If now
N
has sign

V^{-1}_{N}(M)=Q
must be a shifted projective since the co-Bongartz complement of

only consists of shifted projectives in this case. Also, using

3.5
and

3.3
, we have that

E_{N}(Q)=f_{N[-1]}(Q[-1])[1]=V_{N}(Q)[1]=M[1].

Lastly we observe that
M[1]=E_{N}(M)[1]
because
E_{N}
is the identity on modules (i.e of sign

) by
Definition

3.5
. This finishes the proof.
∎

In the case where we work with longer

Lemma 3.15
.

Let

Clearly

for all

\displaystyle E_{T_{>1}}(V^{-1}_{T_{>1}}(T_{1}))=f_{T_{>1}}(V_{T_{>1}}(V^{-1}_{T_{>1}}(T_{1}))[1]=f_{T_{>1}}(T_{1})[1]=P[1].

Remark 3.16

Note that the proof of the above lemma is dependent on the assumption that

Lemma 3.17
.

Let

be a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module such that

\overline{s}_{2}(M\oplus N)=E_{N}(M)\oplus N[1].

We have

Given any two TF-orders
(M,\sigma)
and
(M,\phi)
on the same
\tau
-rigid module
M
, there is of course a permutation
\theta
such that
\theta(M,\sigma)=(M,\phi)
, and any such permuation may be decomposed into a composition of transpositions. Below we show that there is such a permutation
\theta
which can be written as a compositions of transpositions such that applying these transpositions in order to
(M,\sigma)
yields a TF-order at every step

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
A=(T,\sigma_{A})
and
B=(T,\sigma_{B})
be two TF-orders on the same underlying module
T
with
|T|=t
. Then there is a sequence of transpositions
\overline{\pi}_{b_{1}},\overline{\pi}_{b_{2}},\dots,\overline{\pi}_{b_{i}}
such that

\overline{\pi}_{b_{i}}\circ\overline{\pi}_{b_{i-1}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{b_{1}}(B)=A

and
\overline{\pi}_{b_{j}}\circ\overline{\pi}_{b_{j-1}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{b_{1}}(T)
is TF-ordered for all
1\leq j\leq i
.

Proof.

For two TF-ordered modules
A^{\prime}=(T^{\prime},\sigma_{A^{\prime}})
and
B^{\prime}=(T^{\prime},\sigma_{B^{\prime}})
on the same underlying module
T^{\prime}
with
|T^{\prime}|=t^{\prime}
, we first define the agreeableness between
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
as

\mathrm{Ag}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})=\max\{i\in\{0,1,2,\dots,t^{\prime}\}\mid A^{\prime}_{\leq i}=B^{\prime}_{\leq i}\},

so for instance
\mathrm{Ag}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})=t^{\prime}
if and only if
A^{\prime}=B^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{Ag}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})=0
if and only if
A^{\prime}_{1}\neq B^{\prime}_{1}
. Also, a sequence of transpositions
\overline{\pi}_{b_{1}},\overline{\pi}_{b_{2}},\dots,\overline{\pi}_{b_{i}}
is called
B^{\prime}
-
admissible
if
\overline{\pi}_{b_{j}}\circ\overline{\pi}_{b_{j-1}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{b_{1}}(B^{\prime})
is TF-ordered for all
1\leq j\leq i
.

Let now
\overline{\pi}_{c_{1}},\overline{\pi}_{c_{2}},\dots,\overline{\pi}_{c_{i}}
be a
B
-admissible sequence of transpositions maximising

\mathrm{Ag}(A,\overline{\pi}_{c_{i}}\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{i-1}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{1}}(B)),

i.e such that for all
B
-admissible sequences of transpositions
\overline{\pi}_{d_{1}},\overline{\pi}_{d_{2}},\dots,\overline{\pi}_{d_{r}}
we have

\mathrm{Ag}(A,\overline{\pi}_{c_{i}}\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{i-1}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{1}}(B))\geq\mathrm{Ag}(A,\overline{\pi}_{d_{r}}\circ\overline{\pi}_{d_{r-1}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{d_{1}}(B)).

Let
\theta=\overline{\pi}_{c_{i}}\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{i-1}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{1}}
. If
\mathrm{Ag}(A,\theta(B))=t
, then we have identified the wanted sequence of transpositions. Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\mathrm{Ag}(A,\theta(B))=g<t
.

Let
A^{\prime}=A_{>g}
and
B^{\prime}=\theta(B)_{>g}
. We will now identify a permutation
\phi
such that
\mathrm{Ag}(A^{\prime},\phi(B^{\prime}))\geq 1
and such that
\phi
can be written as the composition of a
B^{\prime}
-admissible sequence of transpositions:

Note that
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are TF-ordered modules with the same underlying module and with agreeableness
\mathrm{Ag}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})=0
. Let
i
be the unique integer so that
B^{\prime}_{i}=A^{\prime}_{1}
and note that
i>1
. For an integer
j\in\{1,2,\dots,i-1\}
, we now decompose the cycle

\phi_{j}=(j\quad j+1\quad\dots\quad i-1\quad i)=\overline{\pi}_{j}\circ\overline{\pi}_{j+1}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{i-1}

and consider the two ordered modules

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle=B^{\prime}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus B^{\prime}_{j-1}\oplus B^{\prime}_{j}\oplus B^{\prime}_{j+1}\oplus B^{\prime}_{j+2}\oplus\dots\oplus B^{\prime}_{i-1}\oplus\;\;B^{\prime}_{i}\;\;\oplus B^{\prime}_{i+1}\oplus\dots\oplus B^{\prime}_{t-g}

and

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})

\displaystyle=B^{\prime}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus B^{\prime}_{j-1}\oplus B^{\prime}_{i}\oplus\;\;B^{\prime}_{j}\;\;\oplus B^{\prime}_{j+1}\oplus\dots\oplus B^{\prime}_{i-2}\oplus B^{\prime}_{i-1}\oplus B^{\prime}_{i+1}\oplus\dots\oplus B^{\prime}_{t-g}.

We argue that
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})
is TF-ordered. Note that
A^{\prime}_{1}=B^{\prime}_{i}=\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{j}
, and since
A^{\prime}
is TF-ordered we have
A^{\prime}_{1}\notin\mathsf{Gen}A^{\prime}_{>1}=\mathsf{Gen}(\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{<j}\oplus\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{>j})
, so
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{j}\notin\mathsf{Gen}(\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{>j})
. Now, we need to verify that
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{k}\notin\mathsf{Gen}(\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{>k})
for
k\neq j
. For
k<j
and
k>i
, we have
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{k}=B^{\prime}_{k}
and
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{>k}\cong B^{\prime}_{>k}
as modules, so since
B^{\prime}
is TF-ordered, we have
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{k}\notin\mathsf{Gen}(\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{>k})
. Let now
j<k\leq i
. Then
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{k}=B^{\prime}_{k-1}
and
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{>k}\oplus B^{\prime}_{i}\cong B^{\prime}_{>k-1}
so
\mathsf{Gen}(\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{>k})\subseteq\mathsf{Gen}(B^{\prime}_{>k-1})
. Again since
B^{\prime}
is TF-ordered,
B^{\prime}_{k-1}\notin\mathsf{Gen}(B^{\prime}_{>k-1})
, so
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{k}\notin\mathsf{Gen}(\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})_{>k})
. Thus
\phi_{j}(B^{\prime})
is TF-ordered for all
1\leq j\leq i-1
.

Observe now that
\mathrm{Ag}(A^{\prime},\phi_{1}(B^{\prime}))\geq 1
, as
A^{\prime}_{1}=\phi_{1}(B^{\prime})_{1}
. But then adjusting the transpositions appearing in
\phi_{1}
to act on
\theta(B)
instead of on
\theta(B)_{>g}=B^{\prime}
, we get a
B
-admissible sequence of transpositions
\overline{\pi}_{c_{1}},\overline{\pi}_{c_{2}},\dots,\overline{\pi}_{c_{i}},\overline{\pi}_{g+i-1},\dots,\overline{\pi}_{g+1}
such that

\mathrm{Ag}(A,\overline{\pi}_{g+1}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{g+i-1}\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{i}}\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{i}-1}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\pi}_{c_{1}}(B))=g+\mathrm{Ag}(A^{\prime},\phi_{1}(B^{\prime}))\geq g+1>g,

a contradiction to the maximality-assumption on
\overline{\pi}_{c_{1}},\overline{\pi}_{c_{2}},\dots,\overline{\pi}_{c_{i}}
.
∎

We now have the machinery to discuss mutation of

\tau

\tau
-exceptional sequences of length

\tau

Definition 3.19
.

[
BHM24

We remark that there is also

Definition 3.20
.

[

Let

be a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid
\Lambda

\Psi(T)
is called

if
\Psi(T)
is left regular or the minimal torsion class containing

For
\tau

\tau

[
BHM24
, Cor. 4.8]
that this left irregular mutation can be expressed, in the

-tilting finite case, as a composition of the inverse of left mutations. This justifies in part our interest in regular (left) mutations, which we now proceed to describe.

We will use the operations
s_{i}
and
\pi_{i}
defined in the previous subsection to define (left) regular mutation
\varphi

\tau

if possible, then
\pi_{1}
and lastly
s_{1}
if after applying

we do not have an unsigned
\tau
-exceptional pair. More precisely:

Definition 3.21
.

[
BHM24
, Def.-Prop. 4.3]

Let
(B,C)
be a left regular
\tau

(B,C)

\varphi(B,C)=\begin{cases}s_{1}\circ\pi_{1}\circ s_{2}&\text{if $C$ is projective,}\\
s_{1}\circ\pi_{1}&\text{if $\pi_{1}(B,C)$ is not unsigned,}\\
\pi_{1}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Remark 3.22
.

The above definition is a reformulation of the original definition of regular mutation of
\tau
-exceptional pairs given in
[
BHM24
, Def.-Prop. 4.3]
. We here recall the original definition by
[
BHM24
]
and explain why the two formulations are equivalent.

Let
(B,C)
be a left regular
\tau
-exceptional pair, and let

B^{\prime}=\begin{cases}E^{-1}_{C[1]}(B)&\text{if $C$ is projective,}\\
E^{-1}_{C}(B)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
[
BHM24
]
define the left regular mutation
\varphi(B,C)
as

\varphi(B,C)=\begin{cases}(|E_{B^{\prime}}(C[1])|,B^{\prime})&\text{if $C$ is projective,}\\
(|E_{B^{\prime}}(C)|,B^{\prime})&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where for a signed module

we let
|X|=M[0]
.

We now verify that the above expression is equivalent to
Definition

3.21
. Assume first that
C
is projective. Then

s_{1}\circ\pi_{1}\circ s_{2}(B,C)=s_{1}\circ\pi_{1}(B,C[1])=s_{1}(E_{B^{\prime}}(C[1]),B^{\prime})=(|E_{B^{\prime}}(C[1])|,B^{\prime})

as wanted, where the last equality follows from noting that
E_{B^{\prime}}(C[1])
necessarily has sign

.

Assume now that

\pi_{1}(B,C)
is not unsigned then

s_{1}\circ\pi_{1}(B,C)=s_{1}(E_{B^{\prime}}(C),B^{\prime})=(|E_{B^{\prime}}(C)|,B^{\prime}).

Lastly, if
\pi_{1}(B,C)

\pi_{1}(B,C)=(E_{B^{\prime}}(C),B^{\prime})=(|E_{B^{\prime}}(C)|,B^{\prime}).

Thus in all cases,

3.21
agrees with the definition given in
[
BHM24
, Def.-Prop. 4.3]
.

Definition 3.23
.

Let
\Lambda

\tau

.

We remark that for

\tau

\ell(M_{s,t})=(t-(s+2)+1)_{n}=(t-s-1)_{n}.

(10)

By
[
ASS06
, Lem. III.2.11, Cor. III.3.6]
we have that
\dim_{K}\Hom(P(j),X)

equals the multiplicity of
\topp P(j)\cong S(j)
in
X
. So we argue as in the proof of
(9)
.

(11)

This follows directly from
(10)
and
(7)
.

(12)

Note that any composition factor of
\tau(\rad^{k}M_{s,t}/\rad^{i}M_{s,t})
is also a composition factor of
\tau M_{s,t}
by
(8)
.
So if
\Hom(P(j),\tau(\rad^{k}M_{s,t}/\rad^{i}M_{s,t}))\neq 0
, then also

\Hom(P(j),\tau M_{s,t})\neq 0
.
The result then follows from
(11)
.

(13)

Let
L\cong\rad^{k}M_{s,t}
for some
k\in\{0,\dots,\ell(M_{s,t})-1\}
, then the composition factors of
L
are

\{S(s+2)_{n},\dots,S(t-k)_{n})\}.

By
(7)
, we have
\soc(\tau L^{\prime})=S(s+1)_{n}
.
If
\Hom(L,\tau L^{\prime})\neq 0
, then
S(s+1)
would be a composition factor of
L
. But then we would have
\ell(L)\geq n
, which is a contradiction, and so
\Hom(L,\tau L^{\prime})=0
. A dual proof works for two non-isomorphic quotient modules.

(14)

This follows directly from
(13)
, using that
{{}^{\perp}(\tau M)}
is closed under quotient modules since
M
is
\tau
-rigid, see
[
AIR14
, Thm. 2.10]
.

(15)

If
\ell(N)\leq n
, every composition factor of
N
appears at most once. So if
\topp(M)
is a composition factor of
N
, there is a
unique submodule
N^{\prime}
of
N
with
\topp(M)=\topp(N^{\prime})
. Assume
f
is a non-zero map from
M
to
N
. Then

\im f=N^{\prime}
.
Since this holds for any such non-zero map,
we have
\dim_{K}\Hom(M,N)\leq 1
.
The case
\ell(M)\leq n
is dual.

∎

We remark that
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(7)
and

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(8)
hold for arbitrary Nakayama algebras using the adaptation of the cyclic order because the Auslander-Reiten translation is a “local operation” on the subalgebra of the corresponding connected component.
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(10)
,
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(11)
,
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(12)
and

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts

hold using the adapted notation and the inclusion of the case

whenever

.

4.1.
Bongartz completions and
\tau
-tilting reduction

The following observation will be crucial.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\Lambda
be an arbitrary Nakayama algebra and
(M,P)
a
\tau
-rigid pair. Then the
\tau
-perpendicular subcategory
J(M,P)
is Morita equivalent to a Nakayama algebra.

Proof.

We have that
M^{\perp}\cap{}^{\perp}\tau M\cap P^{\perp}\subseteq\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
is equivalent the module category of some algebra
\Gamma
by
[
Jas15
, Thm. 3.8]
and
[
Dem+23
, Thm. 4.12]
. Since every indecomposable module of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
is uniserial, so is every indecomposable module of its subcategory. As a consequence, every module of
\Gamma
is uniserial and thus
\Gamma
is a Nakayama algebra.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Similar results for
\tau
-tilting reductions have been obtained for the following classes of algebras:

•

hereditary algebras, by
[
GL91
, Prop. 1.1]
, for which the
\tau
-perpendicular categories correspond precisely with the perpendicular categories considered in
[
GL91
]
.

•

gentle algebras, by
[
Sch99
, Thm. 1.1]
where it is shown that
\End(M)
is a gentle algebra for any module
M
without self-extensions over a gentle algebra
\Lambda
. Since
\tau
-rigid modules have no self-extensions by
[
AS81
, Thm. 5.8]
and
J(M)
is Morita equivalent to
\End(\mathrm{B}(M))/\langle e_{M}\rangle
by
[
Jas15
, Thm. 3.8]
, the result follows.

•

preprojective algebras of Dynkin type ADE, by
[
Mar17
, Thm. A]
since a
\tau
-tilting reduction
\Gamma
of a preprojective algebra
\Lambda
of Dynkin type ADE is equivalent to considering a functorially finite wide subcategory of
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
by
[
Jas15
, Thm. 3.8]
. Hence the composition of the equivalence with the canonical inclusion define a weakly homological embedding
\mathsf{mod}\Gamma\to\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
, see
[
Mar17
, Prop. 3.2]
, and by
[
Mar17
, Thm. A]
we have that
B
is Morita equivalent to a direct product of preprojective algebras of Dynkin types ADE.

Lemma

4.7
allows us to use results on mutation of
\tau
-exceptional pairs over Nakayama algebras to infer properties of mutation of longer
\tau
-exceptional sequences over Nakayama algebras.

Generalising a result of Msapato
[
Msa22
, Prop. 6.3]
, we now give explicit descriptions of the Bongartz and co-Bongartz completions of an indecomposable module of a Nakayama algebra. It is clear that if
M
is projective, then
\mathrm{B}(M)\cong\Lambda
, otherwise we have the following description.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\Lambda
be a connected Nakayama algebra and
M\in\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
be an indecomposable non-projective
\tau
-rigid module such that
\topp(M)=S(t)
. The Bongartz completion
\mathrm{B}(M)
is given by the
\tau
-tilting module

\mathrm{B}(M)=M\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell(M)-1}\rad^{i}M\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-\ell(M)-1}P(t+i)_{n}.

Proof.

Let
U
denote the right-hand side of the above, so that we want to show
\mathrm{B}(M)\cong U
. Note that

(t+n-\ell(M)-1)_{n}=s
, by
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(9)
. Then
U
is
\tau
-rigid since
\Hom(P(j),\tau U)=0
for all
j\in\{t,\dots,(t+(n-\ell(M)-1))_{n}\}
follows from
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(11)
and the observation that
\tau(\rad^{i}M)\cong\rad^{i}(\tau M)
by
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(8)
. Moreover,
\Hom(L,\tau U)=0
for any submodule
L\subseteq M
by
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(13)
. By counting that
U
has
n
non-isomorphic direct summands, we obtain that it is
\tau
-tilting.

Assume now that
\mathrm{B}(M)\not\cong U
, in other words,
\mathsf{Gen}U={}^{\perp}\tau U\subsetneq{}^{\perp}\tau M=\mathsf{Gen}\mathrm{B}(M)
, where the equalities follow from
[
AIR14
, Thm. 2.12]
and the inclusion is clear since
M
is a direct summand of
U
. Using
[
AIR14
, Thm. 2.7]
, let
U^{\prime}
be a
\tau
-tilting module such that
\mathsf{Gen}U\subsetneq\mathsf{Gen}U^{\prime}\subseteq\mathsf{Gen}\mathrm{B}(M_{s,t})
where the first inclusion is a covering relation in
\mathsf{f}\text{-}\mathsf{tors}\Lambda
. Then by
Definition-Proposition

2.7
there exists an indecomposable direct summand
X\in(\mathsf{add}U\cap\mathsf{add}U^{\prime})\setminus\mathsf{add}M
which is generated by some direct summand
Y\in\mathsf{add}U^{\prime}
. Clearly
X
is not projective, so
X
is a proper submodule
\rad^{k}M
of
M
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell(M)-1\}
. However, any such module
Y
would have a non-zero homomorphism to
\tau M
thus making
U^{\prime}
not
\tau
-rigid. A contradiction, hence
\mathrm{B}(M)\cong U
.
∎

If
\Lambda
is not connected in
Proposition

4.9
, the statement may be adapted by changing the cyclic order to be local to
Q_{M}^{\prime}
and adding the direct sum of all projective
\Lambda
-modules which do not correspond with representations of
Q_{M}^{\prime}
. As a consequence we can describe the mutation of TF-ordered modules of radical square zero Nakayama algebras using fewer cases.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\Lambda
be a radical square zero Nakayama algebra, then there are no left irregular
\tau
-exceptional pairs, in other words, a TF-ordered module
B\oplus C
never falls into Case TF-4. Furthermore
B\oplus C
never falls into Case TF-2a.

Proof.

Assume
(f_{C}(B),C)
is a left irregular
\tau
-exceptional pair then by definition
C\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(B))
and
C\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
. If
B
is projective, then
\mathrm{B}(B)
only has projective summands so
C
would be projective. If
B
is non-projective, then since
\Lambda
is a radical square zero Nakayama algebras,
\ell(B)=1
and by the description of
\mathrm{B}(B)
from
Proposition

4.9
, the only non-projective direct summand of
\mathrm{B}(B)
is
B
since it has no proper submodules. Therefore
C\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(B))
implies
C\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
. Thus, Case TF-4 may not occur. We also have that for a radical square Nakayama algebra
\ell(B)=2
implies that
B
is projective. Therefore there cannot exist a non-projective
B
with a nontrivial proper quotient
C
. So Case TF-2a cannot arise.
∎

We now give an explicit description of the co-Bongartz completion of an indecomposable
\tau
-rigid module.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\Lambda
be a connected Nakayama algebra and
M\in\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
be an indecomposable
\tau
-rigid module such that
\topp(M)=S(t)
. Then the co-Bongartz completion
\mathrm{C}(M)
is given by

\mathrm{C}(M)=\left(M\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\min(n-1,\ell(M)-1)}(M/\rad^{i}M),\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-\ell(M)}P((t+i)_{n})\right).

Proof.

Denote the pair on the right hand side above by
(T,Q)
.
Note that
\mathsf{Gen}M=\mathsf{Gen}T
, so by
Proposition

2.6
, the pair
(T,Q)
must be the co-Bongartz completion if it is a
\tau
-tilting pair.

Assume that
\Hom(T,\tau T)\neq 0
. Then
\Hom(M,\tau T)\neq 0
, as
T\in\mathsf{Gen}M
. By assumption
M
is
\tau
-rigid, so let
T^{\prime}
be an arbitrary indecomposable summand of
T
not isomorphic to
M
. Thus
T^{\prime}
is non-projective and by
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(7)
the module
\tau T^{\prime}
has simple top given by
S(t-1)_{n}
. Since
\ell(\tau T^{\prime})<n
it does not have
S(t)
as a composition factor. Thus
\Hom(M,\tau T^{\prime})=0
and therefore
T
is
\tau
-rigid. It follows from
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(10)
that
\Hom(Q,M)=0
and therefore
\Hom(Q,T)=0
as
T
is supported by the same simples as
M
. It only remains to verify that
(T,Q)
has
n
non-isomorphic indecomposable direct summands. Observe that if
\ell(M)\geq n
then
T
has
n
indecomposable direct summands and
Q
has
0
indecomposable direct summands. Lastly, if
\ell(m)<n
then
T
has
\ell(m)
summands and
Q
has
n-\ell(m)
summands, adding to
n
indecomposable direct summands. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\times\Lambda_{2}
be a disconnected algebra and
T
a
\tau
-rigid pair over
\Lambda
. Then
T
can be decomposed
T=T_{1}\oplus T_{2}
where
T_{i}
is naturally a
\tau
-rigid pair over
\Lambda_{i}
for
i=1,2
and

\mathrm{B}_{\Lambda}(T)=\mathrm{B}_{\Lambda_{1}}(T_{1})\oplus\mathrm{B}_{\Lambda_{2}}(T_{2}).

Proof.

Since the Bongartz-completion
\mathrm{B}(T)=(M,P)
is characterized by maximizing
\mathsf{Gen}M
while having
T
as a summand, it is enough to observe that when we decompose
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
for
\Lambda_{i}
-modules
M_{i}
, we have
\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{Gen}M)=\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{Gen}M_{1})\cup\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{Gen}M_{2})
.
∎

By iteratively applying the above lemma, we can stitch together Bongartz completions of disconnected Nakayama algebras using
Proposition

4.11
. It is clear that the corresponding technique also works for finding co-Bongartz-completions.

4.2.
Left regular mutation

We now give concrete descriptions for the mutation of TF-ordered modules of the form
B\oplus C
for Nakayama algebras. Because the
\tau
-tilting reduction of a Nakayama algebra is again a Nakayama algebra by
Lemma

4.7
and the description from
Theorem

3.31
, we focus on the case where
B\oplus C
are the two right-most modules in a TF-order of a
\tau
-rigid module. From
Theorem

3.26
we have a simple description whenever
B\oplus C
falls into Case TF-3. The aim of this subsection is to describe the
V
-maps arising in
Theorem

3.26
in the regular cases, Case TF-1 and Case TF-2 for Nakayama algebras, refining the simplifications given more generally in
Lemma

3.4
. We begin with the following useful observation.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
B\oplus C
be a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module. If
C\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
and
B\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
, then
\Hom(C,B)=0
.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that
\Hom(C,B)\neq 0
. By TF-admissibility we have
B\not\in\mathsf{Gen}C
, so there exists a proper submodule
\rad^{k}B\in\mathsf{Gen}C
of
B
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell(B)-1\}
. Write
\rad^{k}B\cong C/\rad^{\ell(\rad^{k}B)}C
. Since
B\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
, we have
\tau B\neq 0
. By
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(7)
, we have that
\soc\tau B=\soc(C/\rad^{\ell(\rad^{k}B)+1}C)
, and we have
\ell(C/\rad^{\ell(\rad^{k}B)+1}C)=\ell(C/\rad^{\ell(\rad^{k}B)}C)+1\leq\ell(B)=\ell(\tau B)
. By uniseriality, it follows that

\Hom(C/\rad^{\ell(\rad^{k}B)+1}C,\tau B)\neq 0,

which implies that
\Hom(C,\tau B)\neq 0
. So
B\oplus C
is not
\tau
-rigid, which is a contradiction. Hence
\Hom(C,B)=0
.
∎

We are now able to describe Case TF-1 of mutating TF-ordered modules.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
B\oplus C
be a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module. If
C\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
, then

\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)\cong\begin{cases}C\oplus B&\text{ if }\Hom(C,B)=0,\\
B\oplus f_{C}(B)&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Assume first that
\Hom(C,B)=0
. Then we have
C[1]\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{C}(B))
, by
Proposition

4.11
. Moreover, since
B\oplus C
is
\tau
-rigid we have
\Hom(C,\tau B)=0
and thus
C\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(B))
by
Proposition

4.9
. We therefore have
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=V_{B}(C[1])\oplus B=C\oplus B
by
Theorem

3.26
and
Lemma

3.4
(c).

Assume now that
\Hom(C,B)\neq 0
, and hence
f_{C}(B)\not\cong B
and

t_{C}(B)\neq 0
. Then, we have
B\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
by
Lemma

4.13
. We claim that

f_{C}(B)
is
\tau
-rigid. By uniseriality, we have that
t_{C}(B)
is the maximal submodule of
B
with
\topp(t_{C}(B))=\topp(C)
. Hence

\ell(f_{C}(B))=\ell(B)-\ell(t_{C}(B))<n
, and
therefore
f_{C}(B)
is
\tau
-rigid by

Proposition

4.2
.

By definition of
f_{C}(B)
we have
\Hom(C,f_{C}B)=0
and thus
C[1]\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{C}(f_{C}(B)))
. Since
f_{C}(B)
is a quotient of
B
,
Proposition

4.9
implies that
B\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(f_{C}(B)))
. Consider the following right exact sequence

C\xrightarrow{g}B\xrightarrow{f}f_{C}(B)\to 0

where
f
is the projective cover of
f_{C}(B)
and
g
is the composition of the projective cover
g_{1}:C\twoheadrightarrow t_{C}(B)
with the canonical inclusion
g_{2}:t_{C}(B)\hookrightarrow B
. Since
\ell(f_{C}(B))<n
, we obtain that
\dim_{K}\Hom(B,f_{C}B)=1
, by
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(15)
and so the morphism
f
is a minimal left
\mathsf{add}(f_{C}B)
-approximation.
We next claim that
g
is a left
\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(f_{C}B))
-approximation. For this, we use the description of
\mathrm{B}(f_{C}B)
from
Proposition

4.9
, noting that any direct summand in
\mathrm{B}(f_{C}B)
is either a submodule of
f_{C}B
, or projective. Observe first that
\Hom(C,f_{C}B)=0
implies
\Hom(C,L)=0
for any submodule
L
of
f_{C}B
. Now we have

\topp(C)\cong\topp(t_{C}(B))\cong\topp(\rad^{\ell(f_{C}B)}B)\cong S(t-\ell(f_{C}B))_{n},

where
B\cong P(t)
.
Let
P(i)\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(f_{C}B))
, then by
Proposition

4.9
we have

i\in\{t,\dots,(t+(n-\ell(f_{C}B)-1))_{n}\}.

Since
(t-\ell(f_{C}B))_{n}<t<(t+(n-\ell(f_{C}B))-1)_{n}
we get that any
h\in\Hom(C,P(i))
factors through
P(t)\cong B
. Thus
g
is a left
\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(f_{C}B))
-approximation. We have that
g
is minimal, since

\coker g=f_{C}B
is indecomposable.
We can now apply the construction in

[
BM19
, Def. 3.1, part I(c)]

to obtain that
V_{f_{C}B}(C[1])\cong B
.
We conclude that
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=V_{B}(C[1])\oplus f_{C}B=B\oplus f_{C}B
by
Theorem

3.26
.
∎

In a similar we may describe Case TF-2.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
B\oplus C
be a TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid module. If
C\in\mathsf{Gen}B
, we have
C\cong B/\rad^{i}B
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell(B)-1\}
and

\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)\cong\begin{cases}\rad^{i}B\oplus B&\text{ if }B\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda,\\
P(\rad^{i}B)\oplus B&\text{ if }B\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda,\end{cases}

where
P(\rad^{i}B)\to\rad^{i}B
is the projective cover of
\rad^{i}B
.

Proof.

It is clear by
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(1)
that
C\cong B/\rad^{i}B
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell(B)-1\}
in this case. By
Theorem

3.26
we have
\varphi(B\oplus C)=V_{B}(C)\oplus B
. Assume first
B\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
, then
\rad^{i}B\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(B))
and
C\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{C}(B))
, therefore
Lemma

3.4
(b) gives
V_{B}(C)\cong\rad^{k}B
as required.

Assume now that
B\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
. Then
\mathrm{B}(B)\cong\Lambda
and there exists an exact sequence

P(\rad^{i}B)\xrightarrow{g}B\xrightarrow{f}C\to 0,

where
f
is the projective cover of
C
and
g
is the composition of the projective cover
g_{1}:P(\rad^{i}B)\to\rad^{i}B
and the canonical inclusion
g_{2}:\rad^{i}B\hookrightarrow B
. Since
\ell(C)<n
by
Proposition

4.2
, we have
\dim_{K}\Hom(B,C)=1
by
Proposition

prop:nakayama-collection-of-facts
(15)
and hence
f
is a minimal right
\mathsf{add}B
-approximation. We next claim that
g
is
a minimal left
\mathsf{add}B
-approximation. For this, let
h\in\Hom(P(\rad^{i}B),B)
, then consider the epi-mono factorisation of

given by

P(\rad^{i}B)\xrightarrow{h_{1}}\rad^{j}B\xrightarrow{h_{2}}B,

for some
j\in\{1,\dots,\ell(B)-1\}
. Using uniseriality and
\ell(C)<n
, it follows that
\ell(\rad^{i}B)\geq\ell(\rad^{j}B)
and hence there exist an epimorphism
p:\rad^{i}B\to\rad^{j}B
. It follows that
h
factors through
g
and thus
g
is a left
\mathsf{add}B
-approximation. It is clear that it is minimal. Using now that
f
is a minimal right
\mathsf{add}B
-approximation, and that
g
is a minimal left
\mathsf{add}B
-approximation, the
construction in
[
BM19
, Def. 3.1 Case 1(b)]
gives
V_{B}(C)\cong P(\rad^{k}B)
as required.
∎

.

Let

be a Nakayama algebra and

be a TF-ordered

V_{B}(C)\cong\begin{cases}\rad^{i}B&\text{ if $B\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda$,}\\

And if

V_{f_{C}(B)}(C[1])\cong\begin{cases}C&\text{ if $\Hom(C,B)=0$},\\
B&\text{ if $\Hom(C,B)\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

4.3.
Left irregular mutation

Assume for notational simplicity that
\Lambda
is a connected Nakayama algebra in this section. All results hold for arbitrary Nakayama algebras since they concern the direct sum of two indecomposable modules
B\oplus C
where
C
is a submodule of
B
. Therefore
B
,
C
, their submodules and quotient moduels all lie in the same connected component, and the generalisation is straight-forward by simply adjusting the cyclic order for the projective direct summands in
Eq.

3
.
We complete the description of the mutation of TF-ordered
\tau
-rigid modules for Nakayama algebras by considering the irregular case, i.e. Case TF-4. By
Proposition

4.9
and the definition of Case TF-4, this implies that
B\oplus C
satisfies
B\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
and
C\cong\rad^{i}B
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell(B)-1\}
. The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
B\oplus C
be a TF-ordered

Lemma 4.19

Let

Let

\mathsf{Filt}(\mathsf{Gen}(P(j)\oplus S(j+1)_{n}))

(1)

By

(2)

. We observe that

(2)

Lemma 4.20
.

We have
P(t)\in\mathsf{Filt}(\mathsf{Gen}(J(B\oplus C)))
, where

\topp(B)=S(t)

Proof.

From our setup we have that

. Denote by

. By uniseriality there is a unique indecomposable

Therefore

Let us first show that

Now from

5.
Transitivity of mutation for Nakayama algebras

In this section, we use the description of the mutation of TF-ordered modules of Nakayama algebras in the previous section to show that the mutation of complete
\tau
-exceptional sequences is transitive for Nakayama algebras. While this behaviour is exhibited by hereditary algebras, in general only the case of algebras of rank 2 is characterised
[
BHM24
, Cor. 7.9]
. We recall from
[
Rin94
]
that for hereditary algebras transitivity is achieved by relating every exceptional sequence with one consisting only of simple modules, see
[
Rin94
, Thm. 2 and 3]
.

In our case, the proof idea is as follows: First, given a
\tau
-tilting module
M
, we show that there exists a sequence of mutations of TF-orders between any two TF-orders of
M
. Second, given two
\tau
-tilting modules
M
and
M^{\prime}
, related by a mutation of
\tau
-tilting modules, we show that there exists a mutation of TF-orders between some TF-order of
M
and some TF-order of
M^{\prime}
. The connectivity of the exchange graph of
\tau
-tilting pairs
\mathsf{Hasse}(\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{tilt}\Lambda)
then gives the desired result. Throughout this section, let
A
be an arbitrary Nakayama algebra.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
B\oplus C
be a TF-ordered module such that
C\oplus B
is also TF-ordered. Then there is a sequence of mutations of TF-orders from
B\oplus C
to
C\oplus B
.

Proof.

Assume first that
B
or
C
is non-projective, and that
\psi(B\oplus C)
is left regular. Then
Theorem

3.26
gives us that
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=C\oplus B
or
\overline{\varphi}(C\oplus B)=B\oplus C
. If the first equality holds, we are done. If the second equality is true then there must be an
i
such that
\overline{\varphi}^{i}(B\oplus C)=C\oplus B
as wanted, since
\Lambda
is
\tau
-tilting finite.

Assume now that
B,C\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
then
B\oplus C
does not fall into Case TF-2. From
Theorem

1.3
we have that if
B\oplus C
falls into Case TF-1a or Case TF-3, then

\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=C\oplus B
. We consider the remaining two cases separately.

In Case TF-1b, we apply
Proposition

4.14
and obtain
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=B\oplus f_{C}(B)
. As
B\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
, the TF-ordered module
B\oplus f_{C}(B)
falls into Case TF-2b. Applying
Proposition

4.15
, we find that
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus f_{C}(B))=P(t_{C}(B))\oplus B=C\oplus B
as wanted.

Finally, we consider the Case TF-4, where
\psi(B\oplus C)
is left irregular. We have
C\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
and
C\cong\rad^{i}B
for some
1\leq i\leq\ell(B)-1
by
Proposition

4.9
. By
Theorem

4.17
, we get that
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus C)=B\oplus(B/C)
. From the assumption
C\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
and
C\in\mathsf{add}(\mathrm{B}(B))
, it follows that
B\not\in\mathsf{proj}\Lambda
. Thus
B\oplus(B/C)
falls into Case TF-2a and hence
\overline{\varphi}(B\oplus(B/C))=C\oplus B
by
Proposition

4.15
.
∎

We are now able to show the first of the two main steps towards transitivity.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M=(T,\sigma)
and

be to TF-orders of a
\tau
-rigid module

. Then there is a sequence of mutations
\{\overline{\varphi}_{a_{i}}\}_{i\in\{1,2,3,\dots,t\}}
such that
\overline{\varphi}_{a_{t}}\circ\overline{\varphi}_{a_{t-1}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\varphi}_{a_{1}}(M)=M^{\prime}

Proof.

Note first that if

then the claim follows directly from
Lemma

5.1
.
Recall that we denote by
\overline{\pi}_{i}
the transposition
\begin{pmatrix}i&(i+1)\end{pmatrix}
acting on ordered
\tau
-rigid pairs. Assume that
\overline{\pi}_{i}(M)
is also TF-ordered.

Then by
Proposition

3.30
both

and

are TF-ordered in

which by

is the module category of a Nakayama algebra. Thus, by

, we have

\left({\overline{\varphi}^{J(M_{>i+1})}}\right)^{j}(E_{M_{>i+1}}(M_{i})\oplus E_{M_{>i+1}}(M_{i+1}))=E_{M_{>i+1}}(M_{i+1})\oplus E_{M_{>i+1}}(M_{i})

for some

we then have

This shows that any transposition of neighboring summands preserving TF-order can be obtained via iterated application of left mutations of the TF-ordered module

Before investigating the second step in the proof of transitivity, we carefully describe the terms arising in the mutation of
\tau
-tilting modules.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M\xrightarrow{\alpha=(\alpha_{j})_{j=1}^{t}}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}N_{i}

be a minimal left
\mathsf{add}N
-approximation.
Then for any index
i

\alpha_{i}
cannot factor through any combination of the maps
\alpha_{j}
for
j\neq i
. This means more precisely that for any fixed index
i
we can not have maps

\beta_{j}\colon N_{j}\to N_{i}
for each

, with the property that
\alpha_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{j}\alpha_{j}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\alpha_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}\beta_{j}\alpha_{j}
. Consider the endomorphism
\psi
of
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}N_{i}
which has diagonal entries
\psi_{jj}
equal to 1 if

and 0 if
j=i
and for which
\psi_{ij}=\beta_{j}
. Since the

, a contradiction to minimality.
∎

Lemma 5.4

Theorem 5.15
.

Mutation of TF-ordered
\tau
-tilting modules, and thus of complete

Proof.

In fact, we can also use the disk model to visualise the quiver of the
\tau
-tilting reduction of an indecomposable module of a Nakayama algebra. This description improves
Lemma

4.7
and generalises a description given in the proof of

, Thm. 6.5]
for a special class of self-injective Nakayama algebras.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\Lambda
be a basic and connected Nakayama algebra and
M\cong M_{s,t}
be indecomposable. Then the underlying quiver of the
\tau
-tilting reduction
\Lambda_{M}
, with
\mathsf{mod}\Lambda_{M}
equivalent to
J(M)
, can be read off the disk model. In particular, the quiver of
\Lambda_{M}
is of the form
\overrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\ell(M)-1}\times\overrightarrow{\Delta}_{(s-t)_{n}}
with up to three arrows removed from
\overrightarrow{\Delta}_{(s-t)_{n}}
, as illustrated on the right disk in
Fig.

2
. In particular, it is again a Nakayama algebra.

Proof.

The underlying quiver of
\End(\mathrm{B}(M))
has vertices indexed by the indecomposable direct summands of
\mathrm{B}(M)
as given in
Proposition

4.9
. There exists an arrow between two vertices if and only if there is an irreducible morphism between the corresponding direct summands of
\mathrm{B}(M)
. The quiver of the
\tau
-tilting reduction
\Lambda_{M}
is obtained from the quiver of
\End(\mathrm{B}(M))
by deleting the vertex indexed by
M
. From
Proposition

4.9
we immediately get a sequence of irreducible morphisms

\rad^{\ell(M)-1}M\to\dots\to\rad M\to M.

This induces the subquiver
\overrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\ell(M)-1}
of
\Lambda_{M}
. By
Proposition

4.6
we have
\Hom_{A}(P(i),\rad^{j}M)=0
for all
P(i)\in\mathsf{add}\mathrm{B}(B)
and
k\geq 1
. Conversely, any morphism from
\rad^{j}M
to
P(i)
factors through
M
. Hence there are no arrows connecting the subquiver
\overrightarrow{\mathbb{A}}_{\ell(M)-1}
of
\Lambda_{M}
and the subquiver
\overrightarrow{\Delta}_{(s-t)_{n}}
of
\Lambda_{M}
induced by the sequence of irreducible morphisms

P(t)\xrightarrow{a}P(t+1)_{n}\xrightarrow{}\dots\to P(k)\xrightarrow{b}P(k+1)_{n}\to\dots\to P(s-1)\to P(s)\xrightarrow{c}P(t)

where the morphism
b
does not exist if
P(k)=P(n-1)
and
\Lambda
is a linear Nakayama algebra and the morphism
c
exists if and only if
\Hom(P(s),P(t))\neq 0
. If
\ell(M)=\ell(P(t+1)_{n})-1
then the morphism
a:P(t)\to P(t+1)_{n}
factors through
M
and hence does not give rise to an arrow in the quiver of
\Lambda_{M}
. In conclusion we get the desired description and the embedding of the quiver of
\Lambda_{M}
into the disk with triangulation given by
\mathrm{B}(M)
is given in the right diagram of
Fig.

2
.

For an algebra
\Lambda
of rank 2, the
\varphi
-orbit admits a nice description via the poset of
\tau
-tilting pairs, as given in
[
BHM24
, Thm. 7.8]
. We recall that if
\Lambda
is of rank 2, then the Hasse quiver of the poset
\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{tilt}(\Lambda)
has a largest element
(\Lambda,0)
and a least element
(0,\Lambda)
which are connected by two disjoint, possibily infinite, chains. The result
[
BHM24
, Thm. 7.8]
states that Hasse quiver of the
\varphi
-orbit is obtained from
\mathsf{Hasse}(\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{tilt}\Lambda)
by removing the top and bottom vertices of
\mathsf{Hasse}(\mathsf{s}\tau\text{-}\mathsf{tilt}\Lambda)
and introducing two new “diagonal” arrows, from the bottom element of each chain to the top element of the other, such that the quiver becomes a cycle.

An intuitive generalisation of this to algebras of higher rank would be to expect that the length of any
\varphi
-orbit is bounded by the sum of the sides of
\mathsf{Hasse}(\mathcal{W})
for any functorially finite wide subcategory
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathsf{mod}\Lambda
of rank 2. However, as we will exhibit in the following example, this is not the case even for a Nakayama algebra in rank 3.

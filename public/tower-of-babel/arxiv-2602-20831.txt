Distributions with Unstable Tangent Sheaf on ℙ 3 {\mathbb{P}^{3}}
Pedro Pfarrius Barbassa

Abstract
We study codimension one distributions on the projective three-space, focusing on cases where the tangent sheaf of the distribution is nonsplit and unstable. We relate the order of nonstability to the degree of the induced subfoliation by curves, showing that the order of nonstability is bounded. Moreover, we classify the tangent sheaf of the codimension one distributions that admit a subfoliation by curves of degree 1. In other words, assuming the sheaf is nonsplit, we classify the situations in which the tangent sheaf attains the maximal possible order of nonstability.

Introduction

A distribution on a smooth complex variety
X
is a subsheaf of
TX
whose quotient is a torsion-free sheaf. The singularities of a distribution are the points where the quotient fails to be locally free. Among such objects are foliations, which are integrable distributions in the Frobenius sense. Foliation theory is a vast area of algebraic geometry with many different points of view; see
[
20
,
3
,
1
,
2
]
.

In the paper
[
5
]
, the authors proved several results concerning codimension one distributions and foliations on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, relating properties of the tangent sheaf to their singular scheme. They also provided an equivalent definition of families of distributions together with a fine moduli space, which was compared with the one developed in
[
25
]
. An important observation is that the moduli space of distributions does not require stability of the tangent sheaf. They further established conditions under which a distribution cannot be a foliation. These obstructions were later developed in a more general setting in
[
6
]
, where it was shown that, under certain conditions, the higher dimensional component of the singular scheme of a foliation is connected, and foliations on the projective space satisfies those conditions.

Together with
[
14
]
, there is a complete classification of the tangent sheaf of codimension one distributions on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
of degree
d\leq 2
. In these cases, the tangent sheaf is split or at least
\mu
-semistable, so unstable sheaves that are not split appear only in higher degrees. In fact, they appear naturally when one allows the degree of
\mathcal{F}
to be
d\geq 3
and the tangent sheaf admits a global section. In such a case, the tangent sheaf either splits or is unstable.

Unstable reflexive sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
were studied in
[
26
]
, were the author defined a new invariant called the order of nonstability. We will apply this notion to the tangent sheaf of a codimension one distributions on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
.

In this paper, we continue the study of codimension one distributions on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, focusing on the cases where the tangent sheaf is unstable. To measure stability, we consider the minimum value
t_{\mathcal{F}}
such that the tangent sheaf admits a global section, as in Definition
3.4
. This value is also the minimum degree of a subfoliation by curves admitted by the distribution. As will be shown, such a subfoliation imposes numerical conditions on the codimension one distribution. We may correlate the instability of the tangent sheaf of a codimension one distribution with small values of
t_{\mathcal{F}}
. In other words, we prove the following result.

(

Let

will be unstable of order

Using the classification of foliations by curves of low degree, we obtain a complete classification of the tangent sheaf of every codimension one distribution with

2.2
Stability and Nonstability of Reflexive Sheaves

Stability of sheaves is a fundamental concept in algebraic geometry, and stable sheaves are the building blocks of sheaf theory. However, when additional structure is imposed on a sheaf, such as being the tangent sheaf of a distribution, stability cannot be guaranteed. Slope stability requires fixing a polarization on the variety, however since we will be working with the projective space, the polarization is canonical.

Definition 2.3
.

A torsion-free sheaf
F
on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
is
\mu
-stable (resp.
\mu
-semistable) if, for every proper coherent subsheaf
F^{\prime}\subset F
with
0<\textnormal{rank}(F^{\prime})<\textnormal{rank}(F)
, we have

\mu(F^{\prime})<\mu(F),

(resp.
\leq
), where
\mu=c_{1}/\textnormal{rank}
is the slope of the sheaf.

Remark
.

In this paper, we deal with rank 2 reflexive sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
. In this context, slope stability is equivalent to Gieseker stability, and since slope stability is a numerical condition, we will focus on this concept. Since we don’t need to specify the type of stability, we will simply call a sheaf stable. For a complete treatment of Gieseker stability, we recommend
[
18
]
.

There is a simple criterion for verifying the stability of reflexive sheaves using the normalization of a reflexive sheaf. Let
F
be a rank 2 reflexive sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, the normalization of
F
is defined as

F_{\eta}=\begin{cases}F(-c_{1}(F)/2),\ \textnormal{if}\ \ c_{1}\ \ \textnormal{is even,}\\
F(-(c_{1}+1)/2),\ \textnormal{if}\ \ c_{1}\ \ \textnormal{is odd,}\end{cases}

where
F(d)=F\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
. We say that a sheaf
F
is normalized if
F=F_{\eta}
. Notice that the first Chern class of the normalization is
0
or
-1
. Since
F
is stable if and only if
F(d)
is stable for every
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then
F
is stable if and only if
F_{\eta}
is stable.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
F
be a rank 2 reflexive sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
. Then
F
is stable if and only if
H^{0}(F_{\eta})=0
. If
c_{1}
is even, then
F
is
\mu
-semistable if and only if
H^{0}(F_{\eta}(-1))=0
.

To say that a reflexive sheaf is unstable is the same as saying that it fails to satisfy Definition
2.3
, in the sense that it’s not even
\mu
-semistable. Since this is equivalent to failing the above lemma, we will use definition of a new invariant first introduced in
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
F
be a rank 2 reflexive sheaf on
{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
. Then
F
is unstable of order
r
if there is a positive integer
r
maximal with the property

H^{0}((F_{\eta})^{\vee}(-r))\neq 0.

We call
r
the order of nonstability of the sheaf
F
. As an immediate consequence, if
r=0
, then
F
is stable for
c_{1}
odd and strictly
\mu
-semistable for
c_{1}
even. So when we assume that a reflexive sheaf is unstable, we assume that
r>0
.

The maximal property of
r
implies that the section
\sigma\in H^{0}((F_{\eta})^{\vee}(-r))
vanishes in a pure 1-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
. In fact, let
n:=\textnormal{min}\{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid H^{0}(F(k))\neq 0\}
and take
\sigma\in H^{0}(F(n))
a nonzero section. Suppose that
\textnormal{coker}(\sigma)
has torsion with
T(\sigma)
it’s maximal torsion subsheaf. Then we would obtain the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(k)\longrightarrow F(n)\longrightarrow T(\sigma)/\textnormal{coker}(\sigma)\longrightarrow 0.

But this would be an absurd, because
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(k)
implies that

and then
H^{0}(F(n-k))=0
.
Returning to the original section

, we have the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\xlongrightarrow{\sigma}(F_{\eta})^{\vee}(-r)\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Y}(-r-c_{1}(F_{\eta}))\longrightarrow 0.

where
Y
is a pure 1-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
defined by the zeros of
\sigma
. We call
Y
the curve associated to the sheaf
F
. Notice that, assuming
r>0
the space
H^{0}((F_{\eta})^{\vee}(-r))
is 1-dimensional.

\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
and
\varepsilon\equiv d(\textnormal{mod }2)
. Moreover,
T_{\mathcal{F}}
will be unstable of order
\frac{d+\varepsilon}{2}-t_{\mathcal{F}}
.

The above lemma shows that when
T_{\mathcal{F}}
is nonsplit and unstable, it satisfies the conditions of Lemma
3.7
. In other words, if
\mathcal{F}
is a codimension one distribution with
T_{\mathcal{F}}
nonsplit and unstable, then the minimal subfoliation by curves is unique.

When
t_{\mathcal{F}}
attains its minimum value,
t_{\mathcal{F}}=1
, this is exactly when the order of nonstability attains its maximum possible value. The codimension one distributions with this property are classified in Proposition
3.8
, so we obtain the next result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one distribution of degree
d
on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, such that
T_{\mathcal{F}}
is nonsplit and nonstable with maximal order of nonstability. Then
d\geq 3
and

1.

T_{\mathcal{F}}
has Chern classes
(2-d,1,d)
, generated by a line,
\textnormal{Sing}_{1}(\mathcal{F})
is a curve of degree
d^{2}+1
with genus
p_{a}=d(d-1)^{2}
and
\textnormal{Sing}_{0}(\mathcal{F})
consists of
d
collinear points; or

2.

T_{\mathcal{F}}
has Chern classes
(2-d,2,2d)
, generated by a pair of skew lines (or a double line of genus -1),
\textnormal{Sing}_{1}(\mathcal{F})
is a curve of degree
d^{2}
(complete intersection) and
\textnormal{Sing}_{0}(\mathcal{F})
consists of two pairs of
d
collinear points (or
d
collinear points on the double line).

First Family with Maximal Order.
Here we will discuss the existence and properties of the distributions on the first family. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a codimension one distribution on the first family with maximal order of nonstability

0\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d+2)\longrightarrow 0.

So
T_{\mathcal{F}}
has Chern classes
(2-d,1,d)
and is generated by an extension of a line. Consider
F
a rank 2 reflexive reflexive sheaf generated by the extension of a line
L\subset{\mathbb{P}^{3}}

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{L}(2-d)\longrightarrow 0.

(9)

Now we may consider the exact sequence of the line

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-2)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-1)^{\oplus 2}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{L}\longrightarrow 0,

and using the cohomology of the invertible sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
we obtain the vanishings:

H^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{L})=H^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{L}(-1))=0.

Finally, using the above vanishings and Serre duality, we have

H^{i}(F(d-1-i))=0\ \ \textnormal{for}\ \ i>0.

Therefore, by Corollaty
3.3
every rank 2 reflexive sheaf generated as (
9
) will be the tangent sheaf of a codimension one distribution of degree
d
.

We may calculate
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{L}(2-d),{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}})
by the exact sequence of the line and obtain that it has dimension
d+1
and since the grassmaniann parametrizing lines on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
has dimension 4, there is a variety
\mathbb{V}
of dimension
d+4
that parametrize isomorphism classes of sheaves generated as (
9
).

Returning to the distribution
\mathcal{F}
, we have that
H^{1}_{*}(T_{\mathcal{F}})=0
and therefore
Z=\textnormal{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
is not contained in a hypersurface of degree
d
. Also,
\textnormal{Sing}_{1}(\mathcal{F})=C
have the following invariants

\deg(C)=d^{2}+1\ \ \textnormal{and}\ \ p_{a}(C)=d(d-1)^{2}.

A possible example for
C
would be the union of a complete intersection of degree
d^{2}
with a line intersecting it in
d
distinct points.

Example 3
.

Consider the codimension one distribution
\mathcal{F}
of degree 3 defined by de 1-form in homogeneous coordinates
(x,y,z,w)
:

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=(xy^{2}z-xyz^{2}+yw^{3}-zw^{3})dx

\displaystyle+(-x^{2}yz+xyz^{2}-2x^{3}w-2y^{3}w+3xyzw-2z^{3}w-xw^{3}+zw^{3}+3w^{4})dy

\displaystyle+(x^{2}yz-xy^{2}z+xw^{3}-yw^{3})dz

\displaystyle+(2x^{3}y+2y^{4}+-3xy^{2}z+2yz^{3}-3yw^{3})dw.

Then
\textnormal{Sing}_{0}(\mathcal{F})
has length 3 and
\textnormal{Sing}_{1}(\mathcal{F})
is the union of the complete intersection defined by the ideal
(x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3},xyz+w^{3})
with the line

.

Second Family with Maximal Order.
Now let’s study the second family on Theorem
4.2
given by

0\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\rm T}{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d+2)\longrightarrow 0,

where
T_{\mathcal{F}}
has Chern classes
(2-d,2,2d)
. Let
F
be a rank 2 reflexive sheaf generated as an extension

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Y}(2-d)\longrightarrow 0,

(10)

where
Y
is a pair of skew lines (or a double line of genus -1). Since
H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Y}(1))=0
, the sheaf
F(d-1)
cannot be globally generated and is not guarantee that every sheaf generated by this extension is the tangent sheaf of a codimension one distribution.

We may calculate that
\textnormal{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Y}(2-d),{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}})
has dimension
2d+2
. Since skew lines are parametrized by the symmetric product of the grassmaniann parametrizing lines on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
without the diagonal, they are parametrized by a variety of dimension 6. So there is variety of dimension
2d+7
parametrizing isomorphism classes of sheaves generated as (
10
).

The distribution
\mathcal{F}
has
\textnormal{Sing}_{1}(\mathcal{F})=C
a complete intersection of degree

. Since the only nonzero first cohomology group of

is
h^{1}(T_{\mathcal{F}}(d-2))=1
, which implies that
H^{1}(T_{\mathcal{F}}(-2))=0
and we obtain that
\textnormal{Sing}(\mathcal{F})
is not contained in a hypersurface of degree
d
. We cannot guarantee the existence for every sheaf generated as (

), but we may construct an explicitly example vanishing where we want.

.

Consider the codimension one distribution
\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle+(x^{4}-x^{3}y+xy^{3}-y^{4}-x^{2}yz-3xy^{2}z+xz^{3}

Notice that in our classification the only locally free sheaves that appear are split or the nullcorrelation bundle in Example
1
, which is stable and does not define a codimension one foliation. We may construct an example of a codimension one distribution with locally free tangent sheaf that is nonsplit and unstable.

Example 5
.

Take
L\subset{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
a line and
Y
a double structure on
L
with genus
p_{a}=-4
, for multiple structures on a line see
[
23
, Proposition 1.4]
. Now consider the extension

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{Y}(-1)\longrightarrow 0.

(11)

Then
F
is an unstable rank 2 locally free sheaf with Chern classes
(c_{1},c_{2})=(-1,2)
. Using Serre Duality, we obtain

H^{3}(F(m))^{\vee}=H^{0}(F(-m-3)),\ \ \textnormal{for all }m\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

So we have the vanishings

H^{3}(F(m))=0,\ \ \textnormal{for }m\geq-2.

The exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(3)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}\longrightarrow 0

together with the structural exact sequence gives us

\displaystyle H^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{Y}(m))

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \textnormal{for }m\geq 2;

\displaystyle H^{2}(\mathcal{I}_{Y}(m))

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \textnormal{for }m\geq-1.

This vanishings with the exact sequence (
11
)

\displaystyle H^{1}(F(m))

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \textnormal{for }m\geq 3;

\displaystyle H^{2}(F(m))

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \textnormal{for }m\geq 0.

All this vanishings on the cohomology of
F
gives us the identity

H^{i}(F(4-i))=0,\ \ \textnormal{for }i>0.

So
F
is 4-regular and therefore
F(4)
is globally generated. Applying the Bertini type Theorem
[
5

, we obtain that there exists a codimension one distribution
\mathcal{F}
of degree 7 such that

Then

is an unstable locally free sheaf that is nonsplit and it has

.

5
Final Remarks

Logarithmic foliations form a well-know familiy of codimension one foliations, they form a irreducible component in every space of codimension one foliations, see
[
4
]
. A codimension one distribution
\mathcal{F}
of degree
d
on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
is called a logarithmic foliation of type
(d_{1}.\dots,d_{r})
if is given by a 1-form

\omega=f_{1}\dots f_{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\frac{df_{i}}{f_{i}}.

(12)

such that

•

1\leq d_{1}\leq\dots\leq d_{r}
;

•

\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\sum\lambda_{i}d_{i}=0
;

•

f_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d_{i}
and
\sum d_{i}=d+2
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}
is a generic logarithmic foliation if in addition we have that
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
for every
i
and each
D_{i}:=\{f_{i}=0\}
is in general position, i.e, intersects each other transversely. In
[
13
, Theorem 3.]
the authors proved that if
\mathcal{F}
is a generic logarithmic foliation given by (
12
), then

\textnormal{Sing}(\mathcal{F})=C\cup U.

Where
U
is the zero dimensional component with length

N(3,h)=\textnormal{coefficient of}\ h^{3}\ \textnormal{in}\ \ \frac{(1-h)^{4}}{(1-d_{1}h)\dots(1-d_{r}h)}

and
C
is the one dimensional component given by

C=\bigcup_{i<j}(D_{i}\cap D_{j}).

With this theorem we obtain an explicit formula for the invariants of the singularities of such foliations

\deg(C)=\sum_{i<j}d_{i}d_{j},

and we may compare with the ones obtained in Theorem
4.2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There are no generic logarithmic foliations among the two families with maximal order of nonstability.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a generic logarithmic foliation of type
(d_{1},\dots,d_{r})
and denote

C
1-dimensional component of
\mathcal{F}
.
Then we have

\deg(C)=\sum_{i<j}d_{i}d_{j}\leq\frac{r(r-1)}{2r^{2}}(d+1)^{2}.

But notices that

\frac{r(r-1)}{2r^{2}}(d+1)^{2}<\frac{1}{2}(d+1)^{2}<d^{2}+i,\ \ \textnormal{for }d\geq 3,

where
i\in\{0,1\}
. This means that
\deg(C)
cannot attain the value of the singularities in Theorem
4.2
and therefore the result follows.

Graphs missing a connected partition Thanks: Department of Mathematics, MIT
Foster Tom

Abstract
. We prove that a graph with a cut vertex whose deletion produces at least five connected components must be missing a connected partition of some type. We prove that this also holds if there are four connected components that each have at least two vertices. In particular, the chromatic symmetric function of such a graph cannot be e e -positive. This brings us very close to the conjecture by Dahlberg, She, and van Willigenburg of non- e e -positivity for all trees with a vertex of degree at least four. We also prove that spiders with four legs cannot have an e e -positive chromatic symmetric function.

Introduction

Graph partitioning is a fundamentally important problem in computer science. For example, the max-flow min-cut theorem
[
7
]
is a cornerstone result in optimization theory. There are numerous practical applications for finding communities in large networks and graph partitions can allow for parallelization of algorithms to reduce computational complexity. Surveys of graph partitioning problems and algorithms can be found in
[
1
,
3
,
11
,
12
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an
n
-vertex graph and let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
be an integer partition of
n
. A
connected partition of
G
of type
\lambda
is a partition of the vertices
V
into connected subsets of sizes
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}
. A more precise definition and examples will be given in Section
2
.

Many authors studied problems of finding connected partitions that satisfy certain additional constraints or optimizations
[
2
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
]
. In this paper, we consider the problem of finding an integer partition
\lambda
for which
G
is
missing
a connected partition of type
\lambda
. One application of this is to non-
e
-positivity of chromatic symmetric functions. Stanley
[
14
]
defined the
chromatic symmetric function
of a graph
G=(V,E)
to be

X_{G}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\kappa:V\to\{1,2,3,\ldots\}\\
\text{ if }ij\in E,\text{ then }\kappa(i)\neq\kappa(j)\end{subarray}}\prod_{v\in V}x_{\kappa(v)}

and we say that
X_{G}
is
e
-positive
if it can be written as a nonnegative linear combination in the basis of
elementary symmetric functions
, defined by

e_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots e_{\lambda_{\ell}},\text{ where }e_{k}=\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}.

It is also common to say that
G
itself is
e
-positive
. Wolfgang found a powerful necessary condition for
e
-positivity.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
18
, Proposition 1.3.3]
If a connected graph
G
is missing a connected partition of some type
\lambda\vdash n
, then it is not
e
-positive.

Dahlberg, She, and van Willigenburg
[
6
]
were interested in determining which trees are
e
-positive. Many authors
[
5
,
6
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
proved
e
-positivity or non-
e
-positivity for particular subclasses of trees. Dahlberg, She, and van Willigenburg checked the following conjecture for all trees with at most
12
vertices.

Conjecture 1.2
.

[
6
, Conjecture 6.1]
If
T
is a tree with a vertex of degree at least
4
, then
T
is not
e
-positive.

They proved the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
If
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with a cut vertex whose deletion produces
d\geq\log_{2}n+1
connected components, then
G
is missing a connected partition of some type
\lambda\vdash n
.

In particular, if
T
is an
n
-vertex tree with a vertex of degree
d\geq\log_{2}n+1
, then
T
is not
e
-positive. Zheng strengthened this result considerably.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
19
, Theorem 3.13]
If
G
is a graph with a cut vertex whose deletion produces at least
6
connected components, then
G
is missing a connected partition of some type
\lambda\vdash n
.

Our main result lowers this bound further.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a graph with a cut vertex whose deletion produces either at least
5
connected components, or
4
connected components that each have at least two vertices. Then
G
is missing a connected partition of some type
\lambda\vdash n
.

In particular, if
T
is a tree with a vertex of degree at least
5
, or with a vertex of degree
4
that is not adjacent to any leaf, then
T
is not
e
-positive.

We will show in Proposition
3.18
that there exists an infinite family of trees with a vertex of degree
4
that is adjacent to a leaf, and which have a connected partition of every type
\lambda\vdash n
. Therefore, in this sense, Theorem
1.5
cannot be improved. This means that to prove non-
e
-positivity of trees with a vertex of degree
4
in general, we need to know more about the tree to calculate particular coefficients. In the specific case of
spiders
, which are graphs

formed by joining paths of lengths
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell}
, the
legs
of the spider, at a single vertex, we will prove that Conjecture
1.2
holds.

Theorem 1.6
.

Spiders with four legs are not
e
-positive.

A
graph

G=(V,E)
consists of a set
V
of
vertices
and a set
E
of
edges
, which are sets of two vertices. In particular, we do not allow
loops
, which are edges joining a vertex to itself, or
parallel edges
, which are edges joining the same pair of vertices. We will always use
n
to denote the
order
of
G
, which is the number of vertices. For a subset
S\subseteq V
, the
induced subgraph
is
G[S]=(S,\{e=vw\in E:\ v,w\in S\})
.

We say that
G
is
connected
if for every
v,w\in V
, there is a
path
from
v
to
w
, which is a sequence of vertices
(v=v_{1},v_{2}\ldots,v_{k-1},v_{k}=w)
with every
v_{i}v_{i+1}\in E
. A
connected component
of
G
is a maximal subset
S\subseteq V
such that
G[S]
is connected. A vertex
v\in V
of a connected graph is a
cut vertex
if the graph
G\setminus v
obtained by deleting
v
and all incident edges is not connected.

A
tree

T=(V,E)
is a minimally connected graph, meaning that for every
e\in E
, the graph
(V,E\setminus\{e\})
is not connected. The
degree
of a vertex
v
is the number of edges incident to it and a
leaf
of a tree is a vertex of degree one. Note that in a tree, every vertex is a leaf or a cut vertex. If
v
is a cut vertex of degree
d
, then
T\setminus v
has
d
connected components.

A
composition of
n
is a sequence of positive integers
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell})
with sum
n
. An
(integer) partition of
n
is a weakly decreasing composition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
of
n
. These integers
\lambda_{i}
are called the
parts
of
\lambda
. We write
\lambda\vdash n
to mean that
\lambda
is an integer partition of
n
. We now define the main object of study in this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an
n
-vertex graph and let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})\vdash n
. A
connected partition of
G
of type
\lambda
is a set partition
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{\ell}\}
of
V
so that

\circ

the induced subgraph
G[S_{i}]
is connected for every
1\leq i\leq\ell
, and

\circ

|S_{i}|=\lambda_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq\ell
.

We say that
G
is
missing
a connected partition of type
\lambda
if no such
\mathcal{S}
exists.

Example 2.2
.

The
spider graph

G=S(6,4,1,1)
is shown in Figure

. We have indicated a connected partition of
G
of type
\lambda=(5,4,3,1)\vdash 13
. We will see in Proposition
3.18
that

has a connected partition of every type
\lambda\vdash 13
. But by Theorem

,

still fails to be

-positive. This shows that Wolfgang’s necessary condition for

Example 2.3

If

of type
\lambda=(2,2,\ldots,2,2)
is equivalent to a
perfect matching
of

, which is a subset
U\subseteq E
such that every vertex is in exactly one edge of
U
.

We first fix notation that we will use throughout this section.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex connected graph with a cut vertex
v
whose deletion produces at least
3
connected components, and denote their orders

a\geq b\geq c_{1}\geq\cdots\geq c_{k}\geq 1,\text{ where }k\geq 1.

See Figure
2
. Let
c=c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}
. We will always assume that
c\geq 2
. Note that

n=a+b+c+1\geq 2b+c+1.

We will always use
\lambda
to denote an integer partition of
n
.

We now restate our main result using this notation.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
,
a
,
b
,
c
,
c_{1}
, and
n
be as in Notation
3.1
. Suppose that
c\geq 2
. If any of the following hold, then

is missing a connected partition of some type
\lambda\vdash n
.

(1)

We have
b\leq 2c-2
.

(2)

We have
b=2c-1
and
c\geq c_{1}+1
.

(3)

We have
2c\leq b\leq\frac{c^{2}}{2}
.

(4)

We have
b\geq\frac{c^{2}}{2}
and
c\geq c_{1}+2
.

In particular, if
c\geq c_{1}+2
, then no matter which range

lies in, we can conclude that

is missing a connected partition of some type
\lambda\vdash n
.

The proof of Theorem
3.2
is structured as follows. We begin by reformulating connected partitions of type
\lambda
in terms of partial sums of rearrangements of
\lambda
(Lemma
3.7
), which we use to prove Part (4). We then describe a strategy for choosing the parts of
\lambda
(Lemma
3.9
), which will prove Parts (1) and (2). To prove Part (3), we will need a technical result to establish an inequality (Lemma
3.17
). Our estimates will only be valid if

is sufficiently large, namely
c\geq 500
, but we can check the cases with

by computer (Lemma

). Sage code used to check these cases is provided in the Appendix.

We cannot remove the hypothesis that

We begin by describing another way to think about connected partitions. A composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell})
is a
rearrangement of
\lambda
if each positive integer occurs the same number of times in
\alpha
and in
\lambda
. We denote by

\text{set}(\alpha)=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell-2},\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell-1}\}

the set of (proper)
partial sums
of
\alpha
.

Example 3.6
.

The composition
\alpha=(3,1,5,4)
is a rearrangement of
\lambda=(5,4,3,1)
and has set of partial sums
\text{set}(\alpha)=\{3,4,9\}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
G
,
a
,
b
,
c
,
c_{1}
,
n
, and
v
be as in Notation
3.1
. Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
with all parts at least
(c_{1}+1)
. If every rearrangement
\alpha
of
\lambda
has a partial sum
s\in\text{set}(\alpha)
that lies in the interval
I=\{b+1,\ldots,b+c\}
, then
G
is missing a connected partition of type
\lambda
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
has a connected partition
\mathcal{S}
of type
\lambda
. We will find a rearrangement
\alpha
of
\lambda
that has no partial sum in
I
. Let
A,B,C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
be the connected components of
G\setminus v
whose orders are respectively
a\geq b\geq c_{1}\geq\cdots\geq c_{k}
and let
S\in\mathcal{S}
be the subset with
v\in S
. If any
C_{i}\nsubseteq S
, then letting
u\in C_{i}\setminus S
, the subset
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
with
u\in S^{\prime}
would have
|S^{\prime}|\leq c_{i}\leq c_{1}
, which is impossible because every part of
\lambda
is at least
(c_{1}+1)
. So every
C_{i}\subseteq S
. This means that
\mathcal{S}
is of the form

\mathcal{S}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{i},S,B_{1},\ldots,B_{j}\}

where
A_{i^{\prime}}\subseteq A
for
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq i
and
B_{j^{\prime}}\subseteq B
for
1\leq j^{\prime}\leq j
. We now take
\alpha
to be the composition

\alpha=(|B_{1}|,\ldots,|B_{j}|,|S|,|A_{1}|,\ldots,|A_{i}|).

By definition,
\alpha
is a rearrangement of
\lambda
, and we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle=|B_{1}|+\cdots+|B_{j}|\leq|B|=b\text{ and }

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j+1}

\displaystyle=|B_{1}|+\cdots+|B_{j}|+|S|\geq|B|+|C_{1}|+\cdots+|C_{k}|+1=b+c+1.

Therefore, no partial sum of
\alpha
lies in the interval
I=\{b+1,\ldots,b+c\}
.
∎

We also use the following result, the two-coin solution to the Frobenius coin problem, which gives a sufficient condition to find a partition

Theorem 3.8

[

Let

\gcd(x,y)=1

We can now prove Part (4) of Theorem
3.2
, the case where

b\geq\frac{c^{2}}{2}

Proof of Part (4) of Theorem
3.2
. .

The idea is to take a partition
\lambda
of
n
with parts

or
(c-1)
. Because
b\geq\frac{c^{2}}{2}
, we have

n\geq 2b+c+1\geq c^{2}+c+1\geq(c-1)(c-2),

so by Theorem

, we can write
n=a_{1}c+a_{2}(c-1)
and take
\lambda
to be
a_{1}

c
’s followed by
a_{2}

(c-1)
’s. All parts of
\lambda
are at least
c-1\geq c_{1}+1
. Let
\alpha
be a rearrangement of
\lambda
. Because
c\geq c_{1}+2\geq 3
, we have
\alpha_{1}\leq c\leq\frac{c^{2}}{2}\leq b
, so there exists some maximal

\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}\leq b
, and then the partial sum
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}
satisfies

b+1\leq\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i+1}\leq b+\alpha_{i+1}\leq b+c.

Therefore the result follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Lemma 3.9

Let

,

,

,

,

, and

be a positive integer and let

, where

(3.1)

Suppose that

\lambda

All parts of

We now generalize the result of the Frobenius coin problem by seeing which numbers can be written as a sum of numbers in an interval
J
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
x
and
y
be positive integers with
x<y
. Then there exists a partition
\lambda
of

with all parts in the interval

as long as

(3.2)

n\geq\left\lceil\frac{x-1}{y-x}\right\rceil x.

Proof.

For a positive integer
t
, let
tJ=\{tx,tx+1,\ldots,ty-1,ty\}
. First note that every integer
n\in tJ
has a partition
\lambda
consisting of

. Indeed, for

we can take
\lambda=(y,\ldots,y)
, and for

we can write

x\leq q\leq y-1
and
0\leq r\leq t-1
, and take
\lambda
to be
r

(q+1)
’s followed by
(t-r)

q
’s.

We now see that as

, which means that as soon as we exit the interval

satisfying (

and has a partition
\lambda
with all parts in

.

Remark 3.16

If we ignore the floors, ceilings, and ones, we have a rough approximation

\left\lceil\frac{x-1}{y-x}\right\rceil x\approx\frac{b/q}{c/q}\frac{b}{q}=\frac{b^{2}}{cq}.

, which would give us

, so this is promising. However, the floors and ceilings mean that the denominator
(y-x)
may be closer to

\frac{c}{2}

Lemma 3.17

A unified approach to total vertex irregular labeling
Aleams Barra

Abstract
We present a unified approach to compute the total vertex irregularity strength (tvs) of various graphs, employing a novel technique recently proposed by Barra et al. For graphs such as cycles, paths, prisms, wheels, complete graphs, helm graphs, friendship graphs, and K n , n K_{n,n} , we offer simplified and unified proofs of their previously established tvs values. Furthermore, we resolve an open problem by determining the tvs for simple 2-regular graphs.

Introduction

A network can be represented as a graph. In such a network, multiple connections between two vertices are allowed, which are represented as multiple edges between the corresponding vertices in the graph. To capture the information about the number of edges between two vertices, Chartrand et al.
[
5
]
proposed replacing these multiple edges with a single edge labeled with a positive integer that indicates the number of connections between the two vertices.

The
vertex sum
of a vertex in the graph is defined as the sum of all labels on the edges incident to that vertex. Chartrand et al. also introduced the notion of an
irregular network
, which refers to a network where all vertices in the graph have distinct vertex sums. Additionally, they defined the
strength of an irregular network
as the smallest positive label that appears on the graph.

From this arises the notion of the
irregularity strength
of a simple graph
G
, which is the smallest positive integer
k
such that there exists a labeling on
E(G)
, the set of edges of
G
, using numbers from
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
such that all vertices in
G
have distinct vertex sums.

Baca et al.
[
3
]
extended this concept to a
total labeling
defined on both the vertices and the edges of
G
. A total labeling is a map

\lambda:V(G)\cup E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}.

The
vertex sum
of a vertex
v\in G
is defined by

\omega_{E}(v):=\sum_{vw\in E(G)}\lambda(vw),

where the sum is taken over all edges incident to
v
. The
weight
of
v\in G
is then defined as

\mathrm{wt}(v)=\omega_{E}(v)+\lambda(v).

This labeling
\lambda
is called
k
-irregular if
\mathrm{range}(\lambda)\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
and all vertices in
G
have distinct weights. The smallest positive integer
k
such that
\lambda
is
k
-irregular is called the
total vertex irregularity strength
of
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{tvs}(G)
.

For almost two decades, many authors have attempted to compute the total vertex irregularity strength of various classes of graphs, such as cycles, prisms, paths, trees, wheels, helm graphs, friendship graphs, complete graphs, and complete bipartite graphs, as discussed in
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
1
,
8
]
.

The typical approach employed by these authors is to establish a lower bound for
\mathrm{tvs}(G)
and then demonstrate, through explicit construction, that this lower bound can be attained. While the method for establishing the lower bound is fairly standard, there is no unified approach that works effectively for different classes of graphs when it comes to construction. In some cases, the construction can be quite intricate and requires meticulous verification to ensure that it indeed results in a total irregular labeling.

In this paper, we propose a technique to construct total irregular labelings based on a common underlying principle that can be applied to different classes of graphs.

2
Preliminaries

The concept of
\{1,s\}
-edge labeling was first introduced by Barra et al.
[
4
]
to compute the total vertex irregularity strength of cubic graphs with a perfect matching. A
\{1,s\}
-edge labeling on
G
is a map

\lambda:E(G)\to\{1,s\}.

From this labeling, we can compute the vertex sums of all vertices in
G
. One can extend this labeling to an
s
-irregular labeling by carefully defining

\lambda:V(G)\to\{1,2,\ldots,s\}

so that for any distinct vertices
v,w\in V(G)
, we have
\mathrm{wt}(v)\neq\mathrm{wt}(w)
.

Given a
\{1,s\}
-edge labeling
\lambda
and a subset
U\subseteq V(G)
, we denote by
\mathbf{U}
the vector of length
|U|
, written from the left to the right in increasing order of the vertex sums of the vertices in
U
.

For any positive integer
a
, the boldface notation
\mathbf{a}
represents a constant vector where all entries are equal to
a
, with the length of the vector being clear from the context. Similarly, for any positive integers
a
and
b
with
a\leq b
, the notation
\mathbf{[a,b]}
denotes the vector

\mathbf{[a,b]}=\langle a,a+1,\ldots,b\rangle.

Given a vector
\mathbf{U}=\langle u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m}\rangle
, we define

\displaystyle\omega_{E}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=\langle\omega_{E}(u_{1}),\omega_{E}(u_{2}),\ldots,\omega_{E}(u_{m})\rangle,

\displaystyle\lambda(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda(u_{1}),\lambda(u_{2}),\ldots,\lambda(u_{m})\rangle,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{U})

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{wt}(u_{1}),\mathrm{wt}(u_{2}),\ldots,\mathrm{wt}(u_{m})\rangle.

We will often use the following lower bound which is found in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices, and let
\delta
and
\Delta
denote the minimum and maximum degrees of vertices in
G
, respectively. Then

\mathrm{tvs}(G)\geq\left\lceil\frac{n+\delta}{\Delta+1}\right\rceil.

(1)

3
Main Theorem

In this section, we apply the
\{1,s\}
-edge labeling method to compute several known results on the total vertex irregularity strength of various graphs. The emphasis is on demonstrating a unified approach to the computation of the
\mathrm{tvs}
values of various graphs rather than introducing new constructions for them.

As a culmination of this approach, we apply it at the end of this section to resolve an open problem concerning the total vertex irregularity strength of simple 2-regular graphs.

3.1
Cycles and Paths

The total vertex irregularity strength of cycles was first established by Baca et al.
[
3
]
, while the result for paths first appeared in the work of Nurdin et al.
[
6
]
. The construction for cycles will later be used to determine the total vertex irregularity strength of simple 2-regular graphs.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C_{n}
be the cycle graph with
n
vertices. Then

\mathrm{tvs}(C_{n})=\lceil(n+2)/3\rceil.

Proof.

The minimal and the maximal degree in
C_{n}
are both 3. Therefore by the lower bound in (
1
), we have that
\mathrm{tvs}(C_{n})\geq\lceil(n+2)/3\rceil.

Let
n=a+2b+c
. Starting from an arbitrary edge, label the edges of the cycle counterclockwise as follows: assign the label 1 to the first
a
consecutive edges, label the next
2b
edges alternately with
s
and 1, and label the remaining
c
edges with
s
. In this setup up we require
a,c\geq 1
but the value of
b
is allowed to be zero. In this way, the setup works even for the cycle of order 3.

Notice that the inner vertices of the 1-labeled edges have a vertex sum of 2 (since they are incident to two edges labeled 1), the inner vertices of the alternating
(s,1)
-labeled edges have a vertex sum of
s+1
, and the inner vertices of the
s
-labeled edges have a vertex sum of
2s
. Additionally, each vertex on the boundary between different labeled segments has a vertex sum of
s+1
.

Let
V_{2}
,
V_{s+1}
, and
V_{2s}
denote the sets of all vertices with vertex sums 2,
s+1
, and
2s
, respectively. The distribution of
\lambda
on
G
is defined as

D_{\lambda}(G)=\left(|V_{2}|,|V_{s+1}|,|V_{2s}|\right).

From the labeling described above, we have

D_{\lambda}=(a-1,2b+2,c-1).

(2)

Let
s=\lceil(n+2)/3\rceil
. We consider two cases:

Case 1:
s
is even.
Choose
a
and
b
such that
a-1=s-1
and
2b+2=s
. The value of
c
is uniquely determined by the relation
a+2b+c=n
. Therefore, in this case,

D_{\lambda}=(s-1,s,n+1-2s).

Now extend
\lambda
to
V(G)
by defining

\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2}})=\mathbf{[1,s-1]},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\mathbf{V_{s+1}})=\mathbf{[1,s]},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2s}})=\mathbf{[2,n+2-2s]}.

(3)

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{V_{2}})

\displaystyle=\omega_{E}(\mathbf{V_{2}})+\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2}})=\mathbf{2}+\mathbf{[1,s-1]}=\mathbf{[3,s+1]}

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{V_{s+1}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{s+1}+\mathbf{[1,s]}=\mathbf{[s+2,2s+1]},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{V_{2s}})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{2s}+\mathbf{[2,n+2-2s]}=\mathbf{[2s+2,n+2]}.

As we can see, the weights of all vertices in
V(G)
are distinct. To make this a vertex
s
-irregular labeling, we just need to verify that

n+2-2s\leq s.

But this holds true since for
s=\lceil(n+2)/3\rceil
, we have

n+2\leq 3s.

Case 2:
s
is odd.
Similar to the even case, we can choose
a
and
b
such that

D_{\lambda}=(s,s-1,n+1-2s).

Now define

\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2}})=\mathbf{[1,s]},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\mathbf{V_{s+1}})=\mathbf{[2,s]},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2s}})=\mathbf{[2,n+2-2s]}.

(4)

We have shown earlier that
n+2-2s\leq s
. Hence, all the labels on the vertices are no larger than
s
. We check that

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{V_{2}})=\mathbf{2}+\mathbf{[1,s]}=\mathbf{[3,s+2]},

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{V_{s+1}})=\mathbf{s+1}+\mathbf{[2,s]}=\mathbf{[s+3,2s+1]},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{V_{2s}})=\mathbf{2s}+\mathbf{[2,n+2-2s]}=\mathbf{[2s+2,n+2]}.

Therefore, all weights of vertices in
G
are distinct, and
\lambda
is
s
-irregular.
∎

.
Then

Let

Case 1:

is even.

We can Choose

We are done since we have

3.2
Prisms and Wheels

The
prism graph

D_{n}
consists of two cycles:

C=v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptC^{*}=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n},

together with the edges
\{v_{i}w_{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
connecting corresponding vertices of the two cycles. The
wheel graph

W_{n}
consists of a cycle

C=v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}

together with a center vertex
x
, which is connected to each vertex of the cycle. That is, the edges include
\{xv_{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
. An edge in
W_{n}
of the form
xv_{i}
for some
i
is called a
spoke
of the wheel.

The computation of
\mathrm{tvs}
of prisms and wheels are first appeared in
[
3
]
and
[
7
]
. For wheel graphs, our construction is simpler and easier to verify than the original method.

Theorem 3.3
.

\mathrm{tvs}(D_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil+1.

Proof.

The prism
D_{n}
has
2n

\delta=\Delta=3
. Hence, the lower bound from Inequality (
1

\mathrm{tvs}(D_{n})\geq\left\lceil\frac{2n+3}{4}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil+1.

Label all edges in the cycles
C
and
C^{*}
with 1 and
s
, respectively, where
s=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil+1
. Label the edges of the form
v_{i}w_{i}
such that
s-1
of them are labeled 1, and the remaining
n+1-s
are labeled
s
.

With this labeling, each vertex in
V(D_{n})
has a vertex sum of either
3
,
s+1
,
2s+1
, or
3s
. Denote by
V_{3}
,
V_{s+1}
,
V_{2s+1}
, and
V_{3s}
the sets of all vertices with vertex sums
3
,
s+1
,
2s+1
, and
3s
, respectively. Based on the labeling above, the distribution is given by

D=(|V_{3}|,|V_{s+1}|,|V_{2s+1}|,|V_{3s}|)=(s-1,n+1-s,s-1,n+1-s).

Now label all the vertices by defining

\displaystyle\lambda(\mathbf{V_{3}})=\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2s+1}})=\mathbf{[1,s-1]},\text{ and }\lambda(\mathbf{V_{s+1}})=\lambda(\mathbf{V_{3s}})=\mathbf{[1,n+1-s]}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{V_{3}},\mathbf{V_{s+2}})

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{[4,s+2]},\;\mathbf{[s+3,n+3]}\rangle=\mathbf{[4,n+3]}

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf{V_{2s+1}},\mathbf{V_{3s}})

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{[2s+2,3s]},\mathbf{[3s+1,n+2s+1]}\rangle=\mathbf{[2s+2,n+2s+1]}.

Notice that
2s\geq\frac{2n+3}{2}>n+1
. Therefore,
n+3<2s+2
, and the weights of all vertices in

are distinct.
∎

3.3
Helm and friendship graphs

The
helm graph

H_{n}
is obtained from the
wheel graph

W_{n}
by attaching a pendant edge to each vertex of the outer
n
-cycle of the wheel. The helm graph
H_{n}
consists of
2n+1
vertices and
3n
edges.

The
friendship graph

f_{n}
is a graph constructed by taking
n
copies of the triangle
K_{3}
and gluing one vertex from each triangle together into a single common vertex, called the
center
of the friendship graph. Equivalently, we attach
n
triangles at a shared vertex, such that each triangle has exactly one vertex in common with all others. The resulting graph has
2n+1
vertices and
3n
edges.

The total vertex irregularity strength of helm graph was first appear in
[
1
]
while for the friendship graph it was published first in
[

]

.

The total vertex irregularity strength of the helm graph

\left\lceil\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right\rceil

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
F_{n}
be the friendship graph with
2n+1
vertices, consisting of a center vertex and
n
triangles sharing this center, for
n\geq 2
. Then

\mathrm{tvs}(F_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{2n+2}{3}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Since
F_{1}
is isomorphic to
C_{3}
, with
\mathrm{tvs}(C_{3})=2
from Theorem
3.1
, we assume
n\geq 2
.

First, we establish a lower bound. Suppose
\mathrm{tvs}(F_{n})=k
. Consider the
2n
non-central vertices of
F_{n}
, each incident to two edges (within its triangle). The smallest possible weight for a non-central vertex is 3 and the largest is
3k
. Since all
2n
non-central vertices must have distinct weights, ranging from 3 to
3k
, we need

2n\leq 3k-2,

so

k\geq\left\lceil\frac{2n+2}{3}\right\rceil.

Thus,
\mathrm{tvs}(F_{n})\geq\left\lceil\frac{2n+2}{3}\right\rceil
.

To realize this value, we implement our

-labeling on
F_{n}
, distributing vertex sums evenly across its structure. Let
s=\left\lceil\frac{2n+2}{3}\right\rceil
and define a
\{1,s\}
-edge labeling on
F_{n}
. We classify the edge labelings of the
n
triangles as type I, type II, or type III, corresponding to the first, second, and third triangles from left to right in the figure below. Here the red vertices represent the common center (depicted separately for clarity but forming a single vertex).

Figure 3:
Type I, II and III edge labelings

Let
V_{2}
,
V_{s+1}
, and
V_{2s}
be the sets of all non-central vertices of
F_{n}
with vertex sums 2,
s+1
, and
2s
, respectively. Notice that labeling a single triangle using type I, type II, or type III contributes exactly 2 vertices to
V_{2}
,
V_{s+1}
, and
V_{2s}
, respectively.

If
s=2a
is even, label
a
triangles and
a-1
triangles using type I and type II, respectively; then label the rest by using type III. It follows that
|V_{2}|=s
,
|V_{s+1}|=s-2
, and
|V_{2s}|=2n+2-2s
. Then label

\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2}})=\mathbf{[1,s]},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\mathbf{V_{s+1}})=\mathbf{[2,s-1]},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2s}})=\mathbf{[1,2n+2-2s]}.

One can easily check that the weights of non-central vertices are all distinct in the range
[3,2n+2]
. Lastly, each label is at most
s
since

2n+2-2s\leq s\iff 2n+2\leq 3s,

which is true by the definition of
s
.

If
s
is odd, then
s-1=2a
for some
a
. Label using type I, type II, and type III accordingly so that

|V_{2}|=s-1,\hskip 10.00002pt|V_{s+1}|=s-1,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|V_{2s}|=2n+2-s.

Then, similar to the case when
s
is even, the labeling

\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2}})=\mathbf{[1,s-1]},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\mathbf{V_{s+1}})=\mathbf{[1,s-1]},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\mathbf{V_{2s}})=\mathbf{[1,2n+2-2s]}

ensures that the weights of all non-central vertices are distinct, and each label is at most
s
.

Note that at least one triangle is labeled using either type II or type III. This is clear because, otherwise, all non-central vertices would belong to
V_{2}
. However, this is not possible, since for
n\geq 2
we have
|V_{2}|\leq s<2n
.

Consequently, the center vertex is incident to at least two edges labeled
s
. Since
F_{n}
contains at least two triangles when
n\geq 2
, assigning the label
s
to the center vertex ensures that its weight is at least
3s+2
. As the weight of any non-central vertex is at most
3s
, the weight of the center vertex is distinct from those of the non-central vertices.
∎

3.4
Complete Graphs and
K_{n,n}

The total vertex irregularity strength of the complete graph on
n
vertices was first established in
[
3
]
, while the result for
K_{n,n}
first appeared in
[
8
]
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
K_{n}
be the complete graph on
n\geq 3
vertices. Then

\mathrm{tvs}(K_{n})=2.

Proof.

Let
V(K_{n})=V=\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
and
E(K_{n})=\{v_{i}v_{j}:1\leq i<j\leq n\}
.
Define a
\{1,2\}
-edge labeling on
K_{n}
as follows:

\lambda(v_{i}v_{j})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }i+j\leq n+1,\\
2,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For a fixed
i
, note that
i+j\geq n+2
if and only if
j\in[n+2-i,n]
.

If
i\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1
, then

2i<2\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2\leq n+2,

implying
i<n+2-i
. Thus,
i\notin[n+2-i,n]
, and
v_{i}
is incident to exactly
n+1-(n+2-i)=i-1
edges labeled 2. Therefore, for
1\leq i\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1
, the edge-induced vertex sum is

\omega_{E}(v_{i})=(n-i)+2(i-1)=n+i-2.

If
\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2\leq i\leq n
, then

2i\geq 2\lfloor n/2\rfloor+4>n+2,

implying
i>n+2-i
. Thus,
i\in[n+2-i,n]
, and
v_{i}
is incident to exactly
n-(n+2-i)=i-2
edges labeled 2. It follows that

\omega_{E}(v_{i})=(n+1-i)+2(i-2)=n+i-3.

Let
i_{0}:=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1
. Define

\lambda(\langle v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{i_{0}}\rangle)=\mathbf{1},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(\langle v_{i_{0}+1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle)=\mathbf{2}.

The total weights are

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{i_{0}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{[n+1-i_{0},n+i_{0}-2]}+\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{[n,n+i_{0}-1]},

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\langle v_{i_{0}+1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{[n+i_{0}-2,2n-3]}+\mathbf{2}=\mathbf{[n+i_{0},2n-1]}.

Since
i_{0}=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1
, the weights of all vertices in
V(K_{n})
are distinct.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
K_{n,n}
be the complete bipartite graph with two parts, each of size
n\geq 3
. Then

\mathrm{tvs}(K_{n,n})=3.

Proof.

Let
V(K_{n,n})=\{v_{i},w_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
and
E(K_{n,n})=\{v_{i}w_{j}:1\leq i,j\leq n\}
.

Since
K_{n,n}
is an
n
-regular graph, by Inequality (
1
), it is clear that

\mathrm{tvs}(K_{n,n})\geq\left\lceil\frac{2n+n}{n+1}\right\rceil=3+\left\lceil\frac{-3}{n+1}\right\rceil=3

for
n\geq 3
.

For the construction, define a
\{1,3\}
-edge labeling as follows:

\lambda(v_{i}w_{j})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }i+j\leq n+1,\\
3,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For any
i\in[1,n]
,
\lambda(v_{i}w_{j})=3
if and only if
j\in[n+2-i,n]
. Consequently,
v_{i}
is incident to exactly
n+1-(n+2-i)=i-1
edges labeled 3. Therefore, the edge-induced vertex sum is

\omega_{E}(v_{i})=(n+1-i)+3(i-1)=n+2(i-1).

By symmetry,
\omega_{E}(w_{i})=n+2(i-1)
for all
i\in[1,n]
.

Now, by defining
\lambda(\langle v_{i},w_{i}\rangle)=\langle 1,2\rangle
for all
i\in[1,n]
, the total weights are

\mathrm{wt}(\langle v_{i},w_{i}\rangle)=\langle n+2(i-1),n+2(i-1)\rangle+\langle 1,2\rangle=\langle n+2i-1,n+2i\rangle.

Since
i
ranges from 1 to
n
, the weights of the vertices in
K_{n,n}
are all distinct and lie within the range
[n+1,3n]
.
∎

3.5
Simple 2-Regular Graphs

The total vertex irregularity strength of simple 2-regular graphs was previously conjectured by Ahmad et al.
[
2
]
. They determined this strength for the disjoint union of cycles, provided each cycle has a length of at least 5, but were unable to compute it for the general case of simple 2-regular graphs.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
G
be a simple 2-regular graph of order
n
. Then

\mathrm{tvs}(G)=\left\lceil\frac{n+2}{3}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Clearly,
G
is a union of disjoint cycles,
G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}
, where each cycle has a minimum length of 3. We assume
m\geq 2
(and thus
n\geq 6
), since for
m=1
,
G
is a cycle, which was addressed in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Without loss of generality, we can assume

|V(C_{1})|\leq|V(C_{2})|\leq\cdots\leq|V(C_{m})|.

Let
s=\left\lceil\frac{n+2}{3}\right\rceil
and suppose
s
is odd. We aim to define a
\{1,s\}
-edge labeling such that the cardinalities of the sets
V_{2}
,
V_{s+1}
, and
V_{2s}
—the sets of all vertices in
G
with vertex sums 2,
s+1
, and
2s
, respectively—are
s
,
s-1
, and
n+1-2s
.

Let
k
be the largest positive integer such that

\left|V\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}\right)\right|\leq s.

Label all edges in
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{k}
with 1. From these cycles, we obtain
s-a
edges labeled 1 for some non-negative integer
a
. By the definition of
k
, we have
|V(C_{k+1})|>a
. If
|V(C_{k+1})|\geq a+2
, label
a+1
consecutive edges of
C_{k+1}
with 1. Then, from all edges labeled 1, we have exactly
(s-a)+a=s
vertices with a vertex sum of 2. Now, if
|V(C_{k+1})|=a+1
, label
a
consecutive edges with 1 and the remaining edge with
s
. This results in
a-1
vertices in
C_{k+1}
with a vertex sum of 2. We can obtain one more vertex with a vertex sum of 2 by labeling 2 consecutive edges in
C_{k+1}
with 1. In this case, we also manage to have exactly
s
vertices with a vertex sum of 2. Note that the cycle
C_{k+1}
exists since the total number of vertices in
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{k}
is
s+1
and

s+1<\frac{n+2}{3}+2=\frac{n+8}{3}<\frac{n+2n}{3}=n

for
n\geq 6
.

Now, from the remaining unlabeled edges, either in
C_{k+1}
(if any) or in
C_{l}
for
l>k+1
, label enough of them alternately with
s
and 1 to obtain exactly
s-1
vertices with a vertex sum of
s+1
. This is always possible since
s-1
is even. Finally, label all remaining edges with
s
to obtain
n+1-2s
vertices with a vertex sum of
2s
. Therefore, we have
|V_{2}|=s
,
|V_{s+1}|=s-1
, and
|V_{2s}|=n+1-2s
.

By a similar argument, when
s
is even, we can label the edges of
G
with 1 and
s
such that

|V_{2}|=s-1,\hskip 10.00002pt|V_{s+1}|=s,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|V_{2s}|=n+1-2s.

For the vertices, label them using the constructions for cycles given in Equations (
3
) and (
4
) from the proof of Theorem
3.1
, depending on whether
s
is even or odd, respectively. Then, as verified in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, the weights of all vertices in
G
are distinct.
∎

4
Conclusion and Future Work

This study demonstrates the power and versatility of the
\{1,s\}
-edge labeling method in computing the total vertex irregularity strength (
\mathrm{tvs}
) of various graphs. By applying this approach, we have successfully determined the
\mathrm{tvs}
for complete graphs, complete bipartite graphs, and simple 2-regular graphs, revealing a novel and flexible technique that may prove effective for computing the
\mathrm{tvs}
of graphs whose
\mathrm{tvs}
remain undetermined. We invite other researchers to join this endeavor, exploring the potential of this method to advance our understanding of graph irregularity and contribute to ongoing research in this field. Future research could extend our investigation to examine the total vertex irregularity strength of
r
-regular graphs for other values of
r
, adapting the
\{1,s\}
-labeling method to compute this parameter. Additionally, exploring the total edge irregularity strength of various graph classes using similar labeling techniques could provide valuable insights, further enriching the study of graph irregularity and its applications.

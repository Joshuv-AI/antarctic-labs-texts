Penile self-augmentation with Vaseline injection
Authors: Fadhel Mansoori, Maryam S. Ansari, Faris Najdawi, Osamah M. Hasan, Patricia Vidal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Since early human history, penile size has been associated with perceptions of masculinity and power across cultures. In this context, some individuals have pursued penile enlargement through injection of foreign substances, a practice that, although uncommon, carries significant risk of morbidity. We present a complex case of a male who developed acquired phimosis and obstructive lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) following Vaseline injection into the penile shaft, ultimately requiring genital reconstruction.

Introduction

Injection of foreign substances into the penis for augmentation is rare but has a higher prevalence in certain geographic regions, with reports arising from Thailand, Kazakhstan, and Laos.1, 2, 3 The mean age at presentation ranges from 32 to 37 years, and the interval between injection and presentation is variable, with reported means of 3–10 years; however, up to half of patients present within the first year.2, 3, 4 Reported complications include pain, swelling, induration, necrosis, cosmetic deformity, and urinary dysfunction.2, 3, 4, 5 Surgical management includes excision and primary closure with or without the use of skin grafts or flaps, circumcision, and in severe cases, penile amputation.2, 3, 4, 5 Non-surgical management includes the use of intralesional triamcinolone, antibiotics, dressings, and pain management.3,5

We present a complex case of a man with acquired phimosis and obstructive lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) following Vaseline injection for self-augmentation. The patient was successfully managed with surgical excision and split-thickness skin grafting (STSG). In the setting of severe distal anatomic distortion and inability to access the glans or urethral meatus, a proximal-to-distal dissection approach was utilized to safely identify and preserve critical structures.

Case presentation

A 51-year-old incarcerated male presented to urology clinic for phimosis and LUTS. He reported injecting Vaseline into his penile shaft for enhancement approximately 20 years prior. He noted progressive penile swelling over the past 10 years, with the inability to retract the foreskin for 2 years. He reported stranguria, split stream, odor, and incomplete bladder emptying. He was unable to engage in sexual intercourse due to discomfort with erections.

Physical exam was notable for an uncircumcised phallus with severe phimosis and a non-palpable glans. The phallus was indurated with a fixed, immobile mass circumferentially involving approximately two-thirds of the distal shaft, consistent with granulomatous change (Fig. 1, Fig. 2). Bilateral corpora were palpable at the base but not distally. The overlying skin appeared grossly normal. Due to uncertainty regarding possible cavernosal involvement, pelvic MRI was obtained, demonstrating extensive soft tissue thickening involving the ventral and dorsal aspects of the distal penile shaft. The corpora cavernosa and corpus spongiosum were intact without invasion (Fig. 3). After shared decision-making, the patient was taken to the operating room for exam under anesthesia (EUA), penile exploration, and possible STSG. Under EUA, the glans could not be probed despite circumferential manipulation with a Kelly clamp; therefore, neither a Foley catheter nor a ZipWire could be advanced into the urethral meatus. A dorsal slit was deferred due to uncertainty regarding glans involvement.

Given that the proximal phallus was uninvolved, a proximal-to-distal approach was undertaken. A semi-circumferential dorsal incision was made at the base of the penis to the level of the dartos, and the corpora were identified with intact tunica albuginea. An indurated soft tissue mass consistent with granuloma was identified.

Given poor quality of the overlying skin, dissection proceeded in a plane superficial to Buck's fascia, tunneling distally beneath the mass. Once a plane was developed and a digit could be passed between the mass and underlying structures, the glans became palpable. A distal dorsal slit was then performed and extended proximally to meet the initial proximal incision. The incision was deepened with electrocautery, and the mass was opened and mobilized, exposing the dorsal phallus (Fig. 4). A 14-Fr urethral catheter was inserted without difficulty. The mass was then excised en bloc with the overlying skin. The corpus spongiosum was intact ventrally. A segment of uninvolved collar skin was preserved.Fig. 4Glans exposed with surrounding granulating mass.

Due to significant skin loss, the defect was reconstructed using an STSG harvested from the right anterior thigh (Fig. 5). Histopathological examination demonstrated sclerosing lipogranuloma without evidence of dysplasia or malignancy. The patient tolerated the procedure well with successful graft take. Discussion

Injection of foreign substances into the penis for self-augmentation, although uncommon, is associated with significant morbidity due to chronic granulomatous inflammation.2, 3, 4, 5 This inflammatory response results in distortion of penile anatomy through swelling, induration, and necrosis, as well as presentation with pain and urinary dysfunction.

Non-surgical management may be appropriate in select early or mild cases. Akkus et al. described intralesional triamcinolone and hot water baths to treat a mineral oil-associated granuloma on the penis.5 However, surgical treatment is more common. In a retrospective series of over 600 patients with penile self-injections, 75% of patients underwent surgical intervention.3 Surgical options range from circumcision or limited excision in cases of minimal involvement to more extensive reconstruction, including single-stage or staged scrotal flaps and STSG in moderate to severe disease.2,4,6, 7, 8, 9 Selection of reconstructive technique depends on disease extent, involvement of adjacent tissues, quality of skin, and patient preference.

Granuloma formation following foreign substance injection is variable, as the type of substance injected, its volume, and the manner of injection can all lead to a wide spectrum of inflammatory responses. Therefore, no standardized treatment approach exists. In our case, there was extensive involvement of the penile skin; thus, surgical excision with STSG was considered the most appropriate management option.

Conclusion

Penile self-augmentation can result in severe anatomic distortion and significant morbidity. This case highlights the value of MRI in delineating disease extent and excluding malignancy. In advanced cases with obscured distal anatomy, a proximal-to-distal dissection approach facilitates safe identification of critical structures. Surgical excision with STSG provides an effective reconstructive option with favorable early outcomes.

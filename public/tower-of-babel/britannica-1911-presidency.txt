PRESIDENCY, an administrative unit of the Indian empire. The word is derived from the title of president or chief of the
council of a principal factory under the early East India Company—a
title which lasted until governors were appointed under act
of parliament in 1784. It thence came to be applied to the three
original provinces of Bengal, Madras, and Bombay. It is now
restricted to Madras and Bombay, in distinction to the lieutenant-governorships.
In Anglo-Indian usage, “presidency”
was also applied to the capital city as opposed to the country
beyond, termed the “mofussil”; and this usage lingers in such
phrases as “presidency town,” “presidency magistrate,” and
“presidency college.”

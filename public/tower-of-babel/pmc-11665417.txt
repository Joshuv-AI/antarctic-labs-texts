Assay precision, 99th percentile reference value and proportion of detected healthy european adults for VIDAS® high-sensitive troponin I
Authors: Nathalie Auberger, Isabelle Coin, Laure Marillet, Frédérique Raymond, Sandrine Michel-Busseret, Pierre-Géraud Claret, Camille Pease
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction/objectivesFollowing the IFCC (The International Federation of Clinical Chemistry and Laboratory Medicine) guidelines concerning high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assays, we performed an assessment of the VIDAS® High-Sensitive Troponin I (TNHS) assay. The test was evaluated on its capacity to detect at least 50 % of healthy individuals and checked that the coefficient of variation was less than 10 % at the 99th percentile.MethodsHigh-sensitivity performance was assessed by examining the limits of detection, the determination of the 99th percentile value, the evaluated imprecision at said value and the detectable results above limit of detection (LoD) in a cohort of healthy European individuals. The capacity of detection on a healthy population of VIDAS® TNHS was validated on a total of 808 plasma samples.ResultsOne thousand six hundred and nineteen values (888 values for male samples and 731 values for female samples) were included in the analysis. The total imprecision of VIDAS® High-Sensitive Troponin I assay at the 99th percentile was 5.2 %, and 57.4 % of healthy individuals had troponin I values exceeding the LoD. Since the test detected more than 50 % of healthy individuals and had a coefficient of variation less than 10 % at the 99th percentile, it met the criteria of high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assays.ConclusionVIDAS® High-Sensitive Troponin I assay is a high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assays meeting the definition provided by IFCC guidelines.

Introduction

In 2019, 32 % (17.9 million) of deaths worldwide were attributed to cardiovascular diseases, of which 85 % were associated to heart attacks and strokes [1], making it the top cause of death for non-communicable diseases. Cardiac troponin I (cTnI) is first and foremost a marker of cardiac injury [2], associated to the myocardium. This protein is released into the bloodstream when there is damage or stress to the heart. The most common condition associated with elevated troponin I levels is myocardial infarction (heart attack). High-sensitivity cardiac troponin assays are commonly used in clinical settings for the diagnosis and management of cardiac conditions [[3], [4], [5]]. These assays are designed to detect very low levels of cardiac troponin to be monitored over several hours. Early detection of a significant increase in this biomarker is crucial for timely intervention and management, especially in conditions like myocardial infarction, where prompt treatment can improve outcomes. In healthy individuals, there is a baseline level of troponin I in the bloodstream [6,7], which reflects the normal turnover of cardiac cells. Physical exertion and stress [8,9] can, in certain conditions, induce elevation in troponin levels associated to cardiac myocyte damage. However, this baseline level in a healthy individual is usually very low, and it may not be detectable with less sensitive assays. For the past few years, the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) highly recommends the use of high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assays [10,11]. It has been established that these assays should detect a majority of the healthy population. This recommendation is related to the need for specificity, accuracy and reliability in diagnosing cardiac conditions. Establishing the baseline levels in the healthy population helps in setting appropriate cutoff values for abnormal troponin levels. It ensures that the assay is effective in differentiating normal from abnormal troponin concentrations, leading to more precise clinical interpretations and decisions. IFCC (The International Federation of Clinical Chemistry and Laboratory Medicine) guidelines [12] indicate that a reliable high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assay should detect at least 50 % of healthy individuals above the limit of detection and the coefficient of variation (CV, a measure of imprecision) at the 99th percentile value should be 10 % or less. These guidelines have induced similar recommendations in other expert consensus groups [13,14].

The objective of this study was to determine the percentage of detectable normal values below the 99th percentile defined on a healthy population, obtained with VIDAS® High-Sensitive Troponin I (TNHS) assay to ensure its clinical relevance and reliability.

Material & methods

Materials

Reagents

In this study, VIDAS® TNHS (product reference n°415386) and the VIDAS® immunoassay system were used. The assay principle combines a one-step sandwich enzyme immunoassay method with a final fluorescence detection (ELFA). Capture antibodies target epitopes 41–49 and 24–40. The test contains two detection antibodies, one targets epitopes 87–95 and the second antibody is an anti-cTnI antibody also included in the design of previous VIDAS® Troponin I Ultra. Several design modifications were made since VIDAS® Troponin I Ultra to increase analytical sensitivity, reduce background noise and thus ensure low limits of detection. Assay is an enzyme-linked fluorescence assay (ELFA) with ALP (alkaline phosphatase).

Samples

For analytical performances such as lower analytical limits of detection as well as test imprecision, serum samples were provided by blood donors from the French National Blood Service in Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of France (EFS AURA).

For clinical performances such as 99th percentile of reference values and percentage of detectable healthy individuals, plasma lithium heparin samples were obtained from a subgroup of the Gutenberg Health Study (GHS) cohort [17] deployed in Mainz, Germany. Out of 1262 samples obtained, 816 samples were identified as healthy subjects. Included patients tested did not have a history of heart disease, were not taking any medication for cardiac pathologies, did not have high NT-proBNP concentration, did not have renal failure, were not diabetic or did not have severe hypertension. These 816 samples were tested both for determination of the 99th percentile value and the percentage of detectable healthy individuals. Samples were supplied frozen (−80 °C) in 500 μL aliquots for single-use testing.

VIDAS® TNHS has demonstrated equal performances between human serum and plasma (lithium heparin), as claimed in the product package insert.

Methods

Determination of lower analytical limits

The limit of blank (LoB), the limit of detection (LoD) and the limit of quantification (LoQ) were performed following the EP17-A2 guideline [15].

The LoB represents the 95th percentile value of cTnI concentration in the assay of blank samples, when no analyte is present in the sample. The LoB was determined with four serum samples with low levels of cTnI, some of which were diluted in R1 kit buffer, as extremely low levels were difficult to obtain. These samples were then tested on three reagent batches. For each batch and each instrument, i.e. miniVIDAS®, VIDAS® and VIDAS®3, each blank sample was tested in duplicate in a single run per day for 8 days on the same instrument, for a total of 16 values per blank sample. A total of 64 values were thus generated per reagent batch. A non-parametric statistical method was used for LoB calculation, as recommended by CLSI.

The LoD is defined as the lowest detectable cTnI concentration that is distinguished from the LoB with 95 % probability in the sample containing low cTnI concentration. The LoQ is defined as the lowest amount of analyte that can be quantitatively determined with stated accuracy. Here LoQ was based upon a precision requirement with a coefficient of variation ≤20 %.

The LoD was tested on 4 serum samples with low concentrations in cTnI and LoQ was determined with 4 additional serum samples. Each low-level sample for LoD and LoQ determination was tested in five replicates in a single run per day for 8 days on the same instrument, for a total of 40 values for each of the low-level samples, per batch and per instrument.

Determination of 99th percentile value

This study was performed according to the CLSI EP28-A3c [16] guidelines. Each of the 816 samples was centrifuged 10 min at 13 000×g and was tested in singlicate with VIDAS® TNHS, with 2 reagent batches over a period of at least 3 days at bioMérieux Marcy-l’Étoile, France. Outlier values were identified by Dixon test. A non-parametric statistical method was used for 99th percentile URL, as recommended by IFCC guideline.

Imprecision at 99th percentile

The estimation of imprecision at the 99th percentile was performed with 4 patient samples at different concentration levels between 7.2 and 133.4 ng/L. One sample at 19.6 ng/L was included for estimation of imprecision at the 99th percentile. These serum samples were tested in 2 replicates per series (random position within the series) with 4 series per day over 3 days on 2 instruments corresponding to a total of 48 values per sample. For each sample, estimation of mean, within-series precision and total precision (total between series, between days and between instruments) of all the 48 concentrations using nested ANOVA (instrument, day, run, repeatability) were calculated. Within-series and total precision was stated as standard deviation (SD) and coefficient of variation (CV).

Determination of percentage of detectable healthy individuals

Out of the 816 patient samples previously selected for the 99th percentile study, 810 remained in sufficient volume for this second study. These samples were centrifuged 10 min at 13 000×g and were tested in singlicate with VIDAS® TNHS assay at bioMérieux, Marcy-l’Étoile, France. The proportion of detectable values in this population was calculated by excluding samples with troponin concentrations above the global 99th percentile, i.e. 19 ng/L. Analyse-it was the statistical software used for data analysis.

Results

Lower analytical limits

Results of LoB, LoD and LoQ of VIDAS® TNHS assay are presented in Table 1. LoB was found at 0.0 ng/L for all 3 reagent batches. The four-parameter logistics regression (4 PL), cannot report a result below a certain signal level (minimum asymptote), explaining the reported values at 0 ng/L. In addition, a parametric calculation on RFV (Relative Fluorescence Units) signal with formula: LoB = mean RFV signal values) + 1.645∗SD [18] was performed, and calculated LoB remained at 0 ng/L. The LoD was the maximal value of the LoDs obtained for each reagent batch. Therefore, the claimed LoD was rounded up to 0.8 ng/L. The LoQ was the maximal value of the LoQs obtained for each reagent batch. Thus, the LoQ was rounded up to 3.2 ng/L.

Determination of 99th percentile value

Eight hundred and fifteen sample values were obtained on a total of 816, on the first batch of VIDAS® TNHS reagents. One sample encountered an instrument issue and value could not be retrieved. Eight hundred and six sample values on a total of 816 were obtained on the second batch, as 10 samples were insufficient in volume for testing. During data analysis, two values from the same male patient tested on both reagent batches were identified as outlier values and were not considered in the 99th percentile estimation. Therefore, 1619 values (888 values for male samples and 731 values for female samples) were finally included in the analysis. The 99th percentile of the global population was estimated at 19 ng/L with a 90 % confidence interval between 15 and 38 ng/L. Sex specific 99th percentiles were estimated at 25 ng/L for men with 90 % confidence interval between 17 and 50 ng/L and at 11 ng/L for women with 90 % confidence interval between 8 and 29 ng/L for women.

Imprecision at 99th percentile

Results for the 4 tested samples are presented in Table 2 and Fig. 2. The predicted CV for the 99th percentile was estimated with the sample close to 19 ng/L. The within-batch CV for the sample at 19.6 ng/mL was 5.2 %. There are no samples at the sex-specific 99th percentile URL values (i.e. 11 ng/L and 25 ng/L). However, based on the results obtained on the 4 samples framing these values, the within-batch imprecision is expected to be below 10%CV for both men and women 99th percentile upper reference limits.

Determination of percentage of detectable healthy individuals

With the overall 99th percentile value and the determined limit of detection, the percentage of detectable healthy population could be calculated. Two outliers values (116.8 ng/L and 132.2 ng/mL) were identified by Dixon test and removed from the analysis. One of these values was associated to the same male sample as previously identified as outlier in 99th percentile URL determination. The other outlier was associated to a female sample not identified by Dixon test in previous testing. Unfortunately, there was no sufficient remaining volume to investigate biological interferences, as recommended by IFCC guidelines in the case of outliers without valid clinical explanation. Eight hundred and eight values (444 values for male samples and 364 values for female samples) were finally included in the analysis of the 810 samples tested (see Figure 1). The distribution of the values is presented in Figure 3. Eight hundred out of 808 values were below the 99th percentile, i.e.19 ng/L. Among these values, 459 out of 800 values were above or equal to the limit of detection, i.e. 0.8 ng/L. The percentage of detectable normal values below the 99th percentile defined on the healthy population was 57.4 % for the VIDAS® High-Sensitive Troponin I and therefore meeting the criteria of detecting at least 50 % of healthy individuals.

Discussion

In the present study, we characterized analytical and clinical diagnostic performances of the VIDAS High-Sensitive Troponin I assay. The assay showed excellent analytical performance with limits of detection, 99th percentile and percentage of detection in a healthy population.

The estimation of LoD applied the EP17-A2 and integrated the different variability factors known to impact low concentration values. Thus, an instrument for all three VIDAS® platforms were tested, on 3 different manufactured batches and over several days and repetitions. The highest values obtained on all three reagent batches were used for the finally claimed LoD and LoQ to properly consider batch-to-batch analytical uncertainty. To be noted, previously published articles [19] on VIDAS® TNHS might mention other limits of detection with LoD between 1.3 and 3.2 ng/L and the limit of quantification between 2.9 and 4.9 ng/L. The limits of detection for the test have been updated associated to some manufacturing evolutions. However, it is important to note that the product's clinical performances remain unchanged.

The estimation of imprecision at the 99th percentile was generated with 4 serum samples. This study integrated different variability factors than those considered in supplier package insert. Indeed, this more recent study was performed on one reagent batch. Data in the package insert, generated in 2015 was done on 3 different reagent batches. CV% at the 99th percentile when integrating 3 reagent batches was between 6.4 and 8%, still below the 10 % criteria. Imprecision profiles on other platforms and assays [20] show CV% varying between 6.2 and 11.7 for values between 5 and 20 ng/L. VIDAS® TNHS therefore has a similar imprecision profile with values varying from 5.2 to 8.5%CV.

The selected cohort for the establishment of global and sex-specific 99th percentile URLs as well as the calculation of the proportion of detectable healthy individuals was the community-based, prospective, observational single center cohort Gutenberg Health Study in Mainz, Germany. This cohort with meticulously selected healthy individuals provides insight as to the performance of the VIDAS® assay. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, the age range of the included samples spans from 41 to 80 years, which does not cover younger individuals from 18 to 40 years of age. Secondly, the cohort consists exclusively of patients from Mainz, Germany. Results could vary with the ulterior inclusion of diverse ethnic groups. This may limit the applicability of the results to other populations. Additionally, the IFCC guidelines recommend a cohort of 400 males and 400 females. However, this study is short of 36 females. As testing of this cohort dates back to the beginning of 2021 with small volumes, it was not possible to include these additional samples. This limitation may affect the balance and representativeness of the gender distribution in the study.

Cardiac troponin guidelines, particularly those involving high-sensitivity cardiac troponin (hs-cTn) assays, have evolved to include sex-specific reference limits. This shift is due to the recognition that men and women have different baseline levels of troponin. However, the use of these values in diagnosing myocardial infarction (MI) remains a topic of debate. Studies have not shown a clear clinical benefit of sex-specific cut-offs. For instance, some research indicates that sex-specific 99th percentiles do not significantly improve diagnostic performance or prognostic value compared to unisex cut-offs [[21], [22], [23]].Until more comprehensive tools that integrate multiple clinical variables (such as age, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), time from chest pain onset, and sex) are widely available and validated, many experts still recommend using uniform cut-off concentrations for the early diagnosis of MI. We therefore have applied our analysis on a global 99th percentile (men and women combined), even if sex-specific URL values were provided. Despite these limitations, the findings provide valuable insights into the studied population and highlight the need for future research to include a broader age range and more diverse ethnic groups.

This performance on detection of healthy individuals cannot be considered separately from clinical performances on claimed populations. VIDAS® TNHS clinical performances on patients presented to the emergency room (ER) are already documented in the product's package insert as well as previous scientific articles [17].

Conclusion

VIDAS® TNHS assay is a high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assay with adequate limits of detection and precision at low levels of troponin I, allowing the detection of at least 50 % of healthy individuals, as required by IFCC.

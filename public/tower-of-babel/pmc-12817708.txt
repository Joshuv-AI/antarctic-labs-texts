Identification of two conserved B-cell epitopes targeting NSP4 of porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus
Authors: Xu-Yan Xiang, Liang Meng, Xin-Yu Qi, Xin-Qi Shi, Xuehui Cai, Tong-Qing An, Hai-Wei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPorcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (PRRSV) is a major pathogen that causes significant economic losses in the global swine industry. Its non-structural protein NSP4 is a 3 C-like serine protease (3CLSP), which plays a crucial role in viral polyprotein processing and replication. Here, we prepared two monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against NSP4, which were designated as 7H10 and 1A2. To indentify the epitopes recognized by these mAbs, the NSP4 protein truncations were performed. The results showed that the minimal epitopes recognized by mAb 7H10 and mAb 1A2 were 122 VITEAGEL 129 and 189 VPSDLCALLA 198, respectively. Alanine scanning analysis indicated that Ile123, Thr124, Gly127, Glu128, and Leu129 are critical for mAb 7H10 recognition, while Val189, Pro190, Ser191, Asp192, and Leu193 are critical for mAb 1A2 recognition. Moreover, these epitopes are highly conserved among PRRSV-2 strains. These findings provide a foundation for further research into the function of NSP4 and the pathogenic mechanisms of PRRSV.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-025-05164-2.Keywords: Porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus, Monoclonal antibody (mAb), Nonstructural protein 4 (NSP4), Epitope

Introduction

Porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome (PRRS) caused by porcine reproductive and respiratory syndrome virus (PRRSV) is one of the major threats to the global swine industry, posing significant economic losses [1]. PRRSV infection manifests as respiratory illness in piglets and severe reproductive problems such as miscarriage, stillbirth, and mummified fetuses in sows [2, 3], late-term miscarriage rates can exceed 30%, and neonatal mortality rates can reach 35% to 40% [4]. Additionally, PRRSV induces severe pulmonary inflammation and interstitial pneumonia, increasing the susceptibility of pigs to secondary infections [5, 6]. PRRSV is classified into two genotypes: PRRSV-1 (European-type) and PRRSV-2 (North American-type). Their complete genomes share only ~ 60% nucleotide identity, underscoring the profound genetic divergence between them. The prototype of PRRSV-1 is the Lelystad strain that emerged in Europe, whereas PRRSV-2 is exemplified by the VR-2332 strain first isolated in North America. China is currently dominated by PRRSV-2(e.g.NADC30-like [7] strain and NADC34-like strain [8]), which causes the more severe clinical presentation [9, 10]. Nevertheless, recent surveillance reveals that PRRSV-1 continues to expand its footprint and has already been detected in at least 28 Chinese provinces, prompting growing concern across the pig industry [11, 12].

PRRSV is a single-stranded, positive-sense RNA virus belonging to the family Arteriviridae. The genome is approximately 15 kb long and contains a critical region, ORF1, which includes two large open reading frames (ORF1a and ORF1b) [13–15]. The two ORFs encode polyproteins pp1a and pp1ab, which are cleaved by NSP4, a 3 C-like serine protease, consists of two antiparallel β-barrels (domains I and II) and a C-terminal α/β domain (domain III) into functional nonstructural proteins [16–21]. These proteins from pp1a and pp1ab cleaved by NSP4 subsequently form a replication-transcription complex (RTC) to facilitate viral replication [22]. NSP4 contains a His-Asp-Ser catalytic triad (His39, Asp64, and Ser118) located between domains I and II, which is essential for protease activity. Mutation of any of these residues results in a loss of NSP4 function [16, 23].

NSP4 utilizes its 3 C-like protease (3CLP) activity to cleave viral polyproteins and host immune factors, thereby playing a key role in viral replication and immune evasion [17–19, 21]. Overexpression of NSP4 enhances PRRSV replication [24], and PRRSV can replicate efficiently in the NSP4 overexpression cell line [25]. Additionally, NSP4 inhibits interferon production by blocking NF-κB activation and cleaving NEMO, thereby reducing IFN-β production [18]. It also upregulates PP2A activity and activates HMGCR, leading to increased intracellular cholesterol levels and inhibiting the host’s innate antiviral immunity [26]. Furthermore, NSP4 interacts with RBM39 to regulate c-Jun phosphorylation, promoting PRRSV infection [27], and with F-actin and myosin IIA to facilitate viral transmission [28].

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) are valuable tools for studying the interactions between viruses and their hosts, as well as for investigating viral immune evasion mechanisms. This study expressed PRRSV-2 NSP4 using a prokaryotic system and immunized BALB/c mice. Using hybridoma technology, we generated two mAbs, which were validated by Western blot and IFA. In addition, truncated overlapping peptides were used to identify two B-cell epitopes, and their conservation among PRRSV-2 strains was analyzed. These results provide a powerful tool for further research on the function of PPRSV NSP4.

Results

Expression and purification of NSP4-ΔHR protein

The amino acids between positions 145 and 168 of NSP4 are the key hinge region (HR) that determines nuclear localization, deletion of this segment can prevent NSP4 from entering the nucleus, deletion of the HR region would be beneficial to the NSP4 expression [29]. Thus, we generated a truncated NSP4 variant, NSP4-ΔHR, by deleting residues 145–168 (Fig. 1A and B). The positive plasmid was transformed into Escherichia coli BL21 (DE3) competent cells for NSP4 expression. The result showed that NSP4-ΔHR was mainly expressed in the supernatant, and the protein molecular weight was approximately 22 kDa (Fig. 2A). After expression and purification, proteins with relatively high purity were obtained (Fig. 2C).

Preparation of mAbs against NSP4 protein

The purified recombinant NSP4-ΔHR was used to immunize 4–6 weeks BALB/c mice, their splenocytes were then fused with SP/20 cells to generate hybridoma cells secreting specific monoclonal antibodies. Hybridoma cell supernatants were tested by IFA, and two hybridoma clones stably expressing and secreting antibodies against NSP4 were obtained and named 1A2 and 7H10 (Fig. 3A). We constructed pCI-NSP4 expression plasmid and transfected into 293 T cells. Western blot analysis revealed clear bands at approximately 22 kDa in the transfected pCI-NSP4 and HuN4-infected marc-145 cell samples incubated with mAbs 1A2 and 7H10, while no such bands were observed in cells transfected with empty plasmids and pig positive serum (Fig. 3B, Supplementary Material, Fig. 9). These results indicate that mAb 1A2 and 7H10 specifically recognize the linear epitopes of NSP4.

Mapping the epitopes recognized by mAb 7H10 and 1A2

The NSP4-ΔHR protein was divided into five overlapping fragments (Fig. 4A) and cloned into the pGEX-6p-1 vector for expression, respectively. SDS-PAGE showed that all the constructed expression plasmids were expressed in E. coli BL21 (DE3) (Fig. 4B). The Western blot results revealed that the mAb 7H10 recognized the overlapping parts of P4-4 and P4-5, a 16-amino acid sequence, 120SPVITEAGELVGVHTG135 (Fig. 4C), whereas the mAb 1A2 recognized peptide segment P4-5 (Fig. 4D). To further determine the minimal epitope, sequential truncation was performed. For mAb 7H10, both the SDS-PAGE and Western blot indicated the minimal recognition epitope as 122VITEAGEL129 (Fig. 5B). For mAb 1A2, peptide fragment P4-5 was further divided into three overlapping segments P51, P52, and P53 (Fig. 6A). Western blot analysis revealed that mAb 1A2 recognized segments P52 and P53, with weaker recognition of segment P52 (Fig. 6B). Further analysis identified the epitope as 189VPSDLCALLAAKPELE204, with the minimal epitope was 189VPSDLCALLA198 (Fig. 6D).

Crucial amino acid mutations analysis

To identify the key residues recognized by the mAbs 1A2 and 7H10, we individually replaced each amino acid in the motifs 122VITEAGEL129 and 189VPSDLCALLA198 with alanine (Figs. 5C and 6E). Western blot analysis revealed that the mutation of Ile123, Thr124, Gly127, Glu128, or Leu129 with alanine led to the mAb 7H10 fail to recognize the epitope122VITEAGEL129 (Fig. 5D), indicating that Ile123, Thr124, Gly127, Glu128, or Leu129 are critical for the affinity between the epitope and mAb 7H10. Similarly, mutations of Val189, Pro190, Ser191, Asp192, or Leu193 eliminated the binding activity of the epitope189VPSDLCALLA198 to mAb 1A2 (Fig. 6F). This finding revealed that the sequence 189VPSDL193 is essential for the binding activity between the epitope and mAb 1A2. Additionally, sequence alignment analysis via Jalview revealed that both minimal epitopes recognized by mAb 7H10 and 1A2 are highly conserved among PRRSV-2 strains (Fig. 7A) with a secondary structure β-strand and α-helix (Fig. 7B).

Discussion

NSP4, a multifunctional protein with 3 C-like serine protease activity, contains dominant B-cell epitopes and is highly conserved, which cleaves viral polyprotein into multiple nonstructural proteins and can be detected in the early stage of PRRSV infection [30]. As a crucial protease encoded by PRRSV, NSP4 can inhibit IFN-β to antagonize host innate immunity by cleaving MAVS [19], NEMO [17], ZAP [31], pDCPla [32], and IKKα [33]. It can also downregulate SLA-I and induce caspase-3-dependent apoptosis, thereby promoting PRRSV infection [34]. These studies indicate that, in addition to its central role in the expression and processing of the viral replicase as a 3 C-like protease, NSP4 can induce host cell apoptosis and immune evasion. Therefore, NSP4 is a key target protein for research on the pathogenesis of PRRSV and drug design.

Currently, information on the preparation and epitope identification of NSP4 monoclonal antibodies remains relatively limited, as most studies have predominantly focused on the development of polyclonal antibodies. For instance, Cai et al. immunized New Zealand white rabbits with purified NSP4 protein to generate polyclonal antibodies capable of recognizing the NSP4 protein in PRRSV-infected host cells [35]. Furthermore, the NSP4 protein from the PRRSV XH-GD strain was employed as a coating antigen to detect PRRSV-specific antibodies in porcine serum. This method achieved a coincidence rate of 91.74% when compared to the commercial IDEXX PRRS ELISA kit, effectively distinguishing antibodies resulting from natural infections [36]. Additionally, NSP4-based indirect ELISA (iELISA) demonstrated a 92.2% consistency with the traditional N protein iELISA, successfully identifying false-negative reactions to highly pathogenic PRRSV vaccines [37]. These results indicate that the detection method based on NSP4 protein can be utilized for diagnostic purposes. In this study, the two mAbs 7H10 and 1A2 we prepared exhibited good IFA reactivity with the HuN4, NADC30-like, and NADC34-like strains, but did not react with the PRRSV-1-DV vaccine strain kept in our laboratory (Fig. 3A, Supplementary materials Fig. 8). This result may indicate that these two mAbs have the potential to distinguish between PRRSV-1 and PRRSV-2. However, whether they react with other PRRSV-2 strains and do not react with other PRRSV-1 strains requires further experimental analysis.

Furthermore, NSP4 is expressed in the early stages of viral replication and cleaves the pp1a/pp1ab polyproteins to generate the nonstructural proteins required for the replication-transcription complex (RTC) [30]. Although NSP4 plays a crucial role in the replication process, it is not a structural component of the virus particle and is therefore typically undetectable in mature PRRSV virus particles. Consistent with the Western blot experiments in this study, no distinct bands were observed when mAbs 1A2 and 7H10 were used as primary antibodies in PRRSV-2 porcine positive serum, on the contrary, samples from HuN4-infected marc-145 cell samples exhibited bands consistent with the theoretical size (Supplementary materials Fig. 9). These results indicate that both mAbs have good reactivity with the HuN4 strain.

Epitopes are crucial antigenic determinants of viral structural proteins, inducing antiviral immune responses and cellular immunity, which can be classified into B-cell and T-cell epitopes based on receptor types, and into linear and conformational epitopes based on structural characteristics. MAbs are key tools for identifying viral protein epitopes. A double-antibody sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (DAS-ELISA) was established based on the 2D11 epitope (138 KQGGGIVTRPSGQFCN153) of NSP4 was shown to be an effective tool to compensate for the false negatives associated with an iELISA method with the N protein as coating antigen, especially on pig farms where PRRSV antibody levels varied widely [38]. This shows that by identifying the specific epitopes of NSP4, highly specific diagnostic reagents can be developed for the early detection of PRRSV infection. In this study, we prepared two mAbs 7H10 and 1A2, which were two new conserved epitopes 122VITEAGEL129 and 189VPSDLCALLA198 of NSP4. However, compared with NSP1, NSP2, NSP7 and NSP8, the antibody levels against NSP4 in PRRSV-infected pigs are relatively low, so we abandoned the development of an iELISA method [39]. Unexpectedly, in the alanine mutation analysis, the two mAbs failed to recognize the mutant peptides at position Ile123, Thr124, Gly127, Glu128, Leu129 of peptides 122VITEAGEL129 and Val 189, Pro190, Ser191, Asp192, Leu193of peptides 189VPSDLCALLA198(Figs. 5D and 6F). A possible explanation is that although the surrounding residues may not directly promote the binding interaction between epitopes and antibodies, the flank residues can affect the formation of the correct peptide conformation recognized by antibodies. The individual presence of 123IT24, 127GEL129 and 189VPSDL193 at the end of their flanking wings may stabilize the correct epitope conformation for antibody recognition and binding. The aa residues Val122, Glu125 and 194CALLA198 can be replaced by alanine alone without completely losing their binding ability. Therefore, the alanine scanning mutagenesis results indicate that these aa residues may play an insignificant role in the recognition and binding of mAbs.

In summary, we prepared two monoclonal antibodies 1A2 and 7H10 targeting the NSP4 protein. These mAbs specifically recognize novel epitopes on the NSP4 protein. Our study provided a basis for the development of differential diagnostic detection methods for PRRSV and further research on the function of NSP4 protein.

Materials and methods

Cells, virus and reagents

Marc-145 cells, 293 T cells and SP/20 cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection and were preserved in our laboratory that grew in Dulbecco′s Modified Eagle′s Medium supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco-BRL, Grand Island, NY, USA), 1% L-glutamine and 2% penicillin and streptomycin; the HP-PRRSV HuN4 strain(GenBank accession no. EF635006), PRRSV-1-DV vaccine strain (GenBank accession no. MT311646), NADC30-like strain and NADC34-like strain (unpublished data) were isolated and kept in our laboratory that using to infect marc-145 cells for IFA, porcine positive serum; BL21(DE3) Competent cells were purchased from Weidi Bio Co., LTD. (China) and stored at −80℃; Ni Sepharose 6 Fast Flow(GE); PrimeSTAR Max DNA Polymerase (Code No. R045A) was purchased from TaKaRa; IPTG, imidazole, Triton X-100 were from Sigma (USA); FITC-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG (Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA) and DyLight 800-labeled anti-mouse IgG (H + L) antibody (KPL, USA) were purchased from Serotec (UK); PEG-6000 (Sigma-Aldrich, USA); hypoxanthine-aminopterin-thymidine (HAT) and hypoxanthine-thymidine(HT) (Sigma-Aldrich, USA). All chemical reagents were from commercial sources and of analytical grade.

Construction of pET-28a-NSP4-ΔHR expression plasmid

The nsp4-ΔHR fragment from PRRSV HuN4 was PCR-amplified with primers carrying 15-bp homologous arms and embedded NcoI/XhoI sites. After 35 cycles (98 °C 2 min; 95 °C 30 s, 55 °C 30 s, 72 °C 5 s) using PrimeSTAR Max polymerase (TaKaRa, R045A), the amplified product was gel-extraction (1% agarose, HiBind mini-column, Omega J0582). The fragment was then recombined into Nco I-Xho I-linearized pET-28a at 55 °C for 50 min, and the resulting plasmid was transformed into E. coli BL21(DE3) competent cells.

Expression and purification of pET-28a-NSP4-ΔHR protein

The resulting products were added to 50µL of E. coli BL21 (DE3) competent cells, incubated on ice for 30 min, and then heat shocked at 42 °C for 90 s. The cells were resuspended in 700µL of antibiotic-free LB medium (5 g yeast extract, 10 g pancreatic peptone and 10 g sodium chloride,1 L), incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm for 1 h. After centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 5 min, the pellet was resuspended in the remaining supernatant, and a 100µL supernatant was spread on LB agar plates containing 1% ampicillin. After overnight incubation at 37 °C, a single, colony was selected and inoculated into 4 mL of LB medium containing 1% ampicillin. The culture was incubated at 37 °C with shaking at 200 rpm for 6–8 h and 2 mL was taken for sequencing. Sequencing-confirmed bacterial cultures were activated and transferred to a 100 mL conical flask at a 1:1000 dilution ratio and incubated. When the optical density (OD600) of the bacterial culture reached 0.6–0.8, IPTG was added to a final concentration of 0.1 mM for induction and expression for 20 h. After ammonium sulfate precipitation, the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22–0.45 μm filter membrane and incubated with Ni-NTA resin equilibrated in 5–10 volumes of equilibration buffer at 4 °C for 1–2 h. Impurities were removed by washing with PB buffer (0.2 M NaH2PO4・H2O; 0.2 M Na2HPO4・7H2O; pH 7.5) containing 50 mM and 80 mM imidazole to eliminate unbound and nonspecifically bound proteins. Gradient elution was then performed via PB buffer with imidazole concentrations of 150 mM、200 mM、250 mM and 300 mM imidazole.

Mouse experiment

Female BALB/c mice (4–6 weeks old) were purchased from the Experimental Animal Center of the Harbin Veterinary Research Institute (HVRI) and were housed in independent ventilation cages in the B3 laboratory. After a one-week acclimation period, each mouse received an intramuscular injection with a total of 200µL of the mixture containing 40 µg NSP4 protein and equal volume ISA 201 adjuvant. Serum titers were measured one week after the third immunization, which was spaced 14 days apart from the previous injections. Mice suitable for fusion received an intraperitoneal booster immunization with 80 µg of protein without adjuvant. Three days after re-immunization, the mice were euthanized by CO2 inhalation and cervical dislocation, and the spleen cells were isolated and were fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells at a 1:7 ratio using PEG-6000 in a 37 °C water bath. The fused cells were resuspended in DMEM containing 1× HAT, 20% FBS, and 2% SP plated into 96-well plates, and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. The supernatants were tested for antibody production 7 d later. Positive clones were subcloned 2–3 times by limited dilution until the antibody positivity rate reached 100%. Stable antibody-secreting hybridoma cell lines were expanded and cryopreserved in liquid nitrogen for long-term storage.

Indirect Immunofluorescence assay (IFA)

Marc-145 cells in 96-well plates were infected with the wild-type HuN4 virus at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.1. After 3–5 days, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 1%Triton X-100, and blocked with 1% BSA. The cells were then incubated with supernatants of mAbs 1A2 and 7H10 as primary antibodies, with mAb 1H4 against N protein as the positive control and mouse negative serum as the negative control, at 37 °C for 1 h. Next, the cells were subsequently incubated with FITC-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody at 37 °C for 1 h. Finally, after washing with PBST, the results were observed under an inverted fluorescence microscope.

Western blot

NSP4 eukaryotic expression plasmids were constructed and transfected into 293 T cells. After 24 h, the cells were lysed on ice with RIPA buffer. The cell lysate was mixed with protein loading buffer, denatured, and separated by 12% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (12% SDS-PAGE) before being transferred to a PVDF membrane. The membrane was blocked with 5% BSA and incubated with the mAbs 7H10 and 1A2 at 37 °C for 1 h. After three washes with PBST, the membrane was probed with a 1:10,000 dilution of DyLight 800-labeled anti-mouse IgG (H + L) for 1 h and the result was then visualized via Odyssey CLx Image Studio (LI-COR, USA). Additionally, based on the NSP4 amino acid sequence, a series of truncated or mutated peptides were designed and inserted into the pGEX-6P-1 vector via the Bam HI and XhoI restriction sites. The constructs were subsequently transformed into E. coli BL21(DE3), which was subsequently expressed for 12 h at 37 °C with 0.1 mM IPTG. After isolating the protein by 12% SDS-PAGE, the protein was transferred to the PVDF membrane, the membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk and incubated with mAbs 7H10 and 1A2 at 37 °C for 1 h. Next, the membrane was incubated with Goat Anti-mouse(H + L) for 1 h at 37 °C. Finally, the membrane was then visualized via a thin-film exposure system. The primers used are listed in Table 1.

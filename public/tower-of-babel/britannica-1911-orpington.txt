ORPINGTON, a town in the Dartford parliamentary division of Kent, England, 133 4 m. S.E. of London, and 22 m. S. by E. of Chislehurst, on the South-Eastern & Chatham railway. Pop. (1901), 4259. The church (Early English) contains some carved woodwork and ancient brasses. An old mansion called the Priory dates in part from 1393. The oak-panelled hall and the principal rooms are of the 15th century. In 1873 John Ruskin set up at Orpington a private publishing house for his works, in the hands of his friend George Allen. Fruit and hops are extensively grown in the neighbourhood. From its pleasant situation in a hilly, wooded district near the headwaters
of the Cray stream, Orpington has become in modern times a
favourite residential locality for those whose business lies in
London. A line of populous villages extends down the valley
between Orpington and Bexley—St Mary Cray (pop. 1894),
St Paul’s Cray (1207), Foots Cray (an urban district, 5817),
and North Cray.

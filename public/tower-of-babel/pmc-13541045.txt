Case of salvage en bloc vertebrectomy for locally progressive T12 breast cancer metastasis after failed radiotherapy and systemic therapy
Authors: Isamu Miura, Takayoshi Shimizu, Takahiro Hori, Hanako Morisawa, Suguru Nakamura, Kaname Ito, Naohisa Miura, Kotaro Kohara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Isolated local progression of breast cancer spinal metastases may occur despite adjunctive radiotherapy and systemic therapy. Here, a 71-year-old female underwent a salvage en bloc vertebrectomy for a locally progressive T12 breast cancer spinal metastasis that newly developed due to failure of prior adjunctive radiotherapy and systemic therapy.Case Description: A 71-year-old female developed a T12 spinal metastasis 10 years after surgery for breast cancer; she exhibited no other metastatic lesions. Despite endocrine therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and radiotherapy, the lesion progressed, resulting in severe spinal stenosis, severe back pain, and paraparesis. Salvage en bloc T12 vertebrectomy was performed; this required piecemeal removal of the involved posterior elements. Postoperatively, the patient’s severe pain resolved, ambulation was restored, and no local recurrence was later documented for 6 months.Conclusion: Salvage en bloc vertebrectomy may be a valuable treatment option for carefully selected patients with isolated locally progressive breast cancer spinal metastasis after failure of radiotherapy and systemic therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Advances in systemic therapy have improved survival among patients with metastatic breast cancer.[5,6,8] However, although radiotherapy and systemic therapies typically provide effective local control for most spinal metastases, some recurrent/progressive lesions in carefully selected patients may warrant salvage en bloc vertebrectomy. Nevertheless, these procedures are rarely performed due to their technical complexity and associated increased morbidity.[1,7] Here, a 71-year-old female presented following failed radiotherapy and systemic therapy, with a locally recurrent/progressive T12 breast cancer spinal metastasis that was successfully managed with a salvage en bloc vertebrectomy.

CASE REPORT

Clinical history

At the age of 58, the patient had originally undergone neoadjuvant chemotherapy, surgery, and adjuvant endocrine therapy for invasive breast carcinoma. Eight years after breast cancer surgery, bone scintigraphy demonstrated metastases to T12 and the left sacroiliac joint; as she was neurologically asymptomatic, she was treated with palbociclib and fulvestrant [Figure 1]. Ten years after the original breast cancer surgery, the patient newly presented with back pain due to recurrence/progression of the T12 lesion; notably, the previously identified sacroiliac metastatic lesion was no longer evident. Although she underwent radiotherapy (30 Gy), opioid analgesia, and systemic therapy, these provided only temporary symptom control. As the T12 lesion progressed further, contributing to intractable back pain, paraparesis, and urinary dysfunction, she was referred for further intervention.

Bone scintigraphy. (a) Anterior and (b) posterior planar images demonstrating increased radiotracer uptake in the T12 vertebra and left sacroiliac joint (arrow head).

Diagnostic studies showing metastatic disease at the T12 level

The thoracic CT demonstrated osteolytic destruction of the T12 vertebral body with extension to the posterior elements of T11/T12. The MR showed tumor involvement of the T12 vertebral body and the posterior elements causing severe spinal canal stenosis at T11–T12 [Figure 2]. As the lesion involved a single spinal level (i.e., despite prior radiotherapy and systemic therapy), the patient underwent a salvage en bloc vertebrectomy.

Preoperative imaging findings. (a) Sagittal computed tomography image demonstrating osteolytic destruction of the T12 vertebral body and the posterior elements of T11 and T12 (arrow). (b) Sagittal thoracolumbar magnetic resonance images showing low signal intensity on T1-weighted imaging and (c) high signal intensity on STIR imaging involving the T12 vertebral body and the posterior elements of T11 and T12. (d) Sagittal T2-weighted image demonstrating severe spinal canal compression at the T12 level.

Surgery for metastatic breast cancer at the T11/T12 levels

A posterior T9 to L3 fusion was performed, followed by piecemeal removal of the involved posterior elements, and en bloc resection of the T12 vertebral body. An expandable cage was inserted for anterior column reconstruction, followed by posterior fixation. The estimated blood loss was 920 mL. Postoperative radiographs confirmed satisfactory alignment and implant positioning [Figure 3]. Although transient postoperative paraparesis developed, motor function improved with rehabilitation. Her severe back pain resolved, opioid therapy was discontinued, and she regained independent ambulation. At the latest follow-up, no evidence of local recurrence or instrumentation failure was observed.

Postoperative radiographs. (a) Anteroposterior and (b) lateral radiographs demonstrating satisfactory spinal alignment and implant positioning following total en bloc spondylectomy.

Pathology

The histopathological examination, compared with the primary tumor, revealed the same metastatic breast carcinoma [Figure 4]. The tumor retained strong estrogen receptor expression while demonstrating loss of progesterone receptor expression, HER2 negativity, and a lower Ki-67 labeling index.

Histopathological findings. (a) Macroscopic axial section of the resected vertebral specimen. The metastatic tumor is outlined by the red dotted circle . (b) Representative immunohistochemical staining for estrogen receptor showing strong positivity (×200; anti-estrogen receptor antibody).

DISCUSSION

Local progression of breast cancer spinal metastases may occur despite the utilization of systemic therapy and radiotherapy. Here, a now 71-year-old patient presented with recurrent/progressive metastatic T12 breast cancer despite endocrine therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and radiotherapy, resulting in severe pain, paraparesis, and sphincter dysfunction. For this single metastasis, she underwent a localized T12 en bloc total spinal tumor resection (i.e., total en bloc spondylectomy [TES]) to achieve durable local control.[9] Although this approach is not considered standard treatment for metastatic spinal disease due to the technical complexity of this procedure and its attendant morbidity, selected patients with solitary/oligoprogressive disease may benefit from these procedures [Table 1]. In many patients, such separation surgery combined with stereotactic ablative radiotherapy has emerged as a less invasive alternative and provides excellent local control in many patients.[1,2,4]

CONCLUSION

This case highlights that salvage en bloc vertebrectomy may be considered in carefully selected patients with isolated locally progressive breast cancer spinal metastasis after failure of contemporary systemic therapy and radiotherapy, particularly when systemic disease remains otherwise controlled and durable local control is the primary treatment goal.

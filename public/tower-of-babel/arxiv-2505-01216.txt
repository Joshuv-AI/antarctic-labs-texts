Automorphisms of Plane Curves defined from Chebyshev polynomials

Abstract
. For any d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 we determine the automorphism groups of the algebraic curves 𝒞 d : y d = φ d ​ ( x ) , \mathcal{C}_{d}:y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x), where φ d ​ ( x ) \varphi_{d}(x) denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of degree d d , defined over a field k k with p := char ⁡ ( k ) ∤ 2 ​ d p:=\operatorname{char}(k)\nmid 2d . For d > 4 d>4 , it is ℤ / d ​ ℤ × ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} , except when 2 ​ d = p r + 1 2d=p^{r}+1 or 4 ​ d = p r + 1 4d=p^{r}+1 for some r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 . In these cases it is, respectively, ( ℤ / d ​ ℤ × ℤ / d ​ ℤ ) ⋊ S 3 (\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\rtimes S_{3} and ℤ / d ​ ℤ × S 3 \mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times S_{3} . 1.
Introduction and preliminaries

For a field
k
and an integer
d\geq 1
,
the
d
-th Chebyshev polynomial
\varphi_{d}(X)

is the unique monic polynomial such that
in the ring of Laurent polynomials
k[t,t^{-1}]

the equality

\varphi_{d}(t+t^{-1})=t^{d}+t^{-d}

holds.
Equivalently, considering
x\mapsto\varphi_{d}(x)
as a morphism
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
defined
over
k
, the polynomial is characterized by
the condition that the diagram

\begin{array}[]{lcl}{{\mathbb{P}}}^{1}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r\mapsto r^{d}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathbb{P}^{1}\\
{\Big\downarrow}{\scriptstyle r\mapsto r+r^{-1}}&&{\Big\downarrow}{\scriptstyle s\mapsto s+s^{-1}}\\
\mathbb{P}^{1}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\mapsto\varphi_{d}(t)}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathbb{P}^{1}\end{array}

commutes. From the diagram (see also
[
16
]

and
[
1
, Remark 6.2]
) it is readily deduced
that
\varphi_{d}(X)\in k[X]
is separable, except
in the case that
\cha(k)
divides
2d
.

Throughout this paper it is assumed that

\cha(k)\nmid 2d
. Define the curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
over
k
as the closure in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of the affine curve
given by

y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x).

Since
\varphi_{d}(X)\in k[X]
is separable,

{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
is a smooth curve. Our main goal is to
describe the group
\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})
consisting of
the automorphisms
of the curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
defined over a fixed algebraic
closure of
k
. We restrict to
the case
d\geq 4
, or equivalently, the genus

g({\mathcal{C}}_{d})=(d-1)(d-2)/2\geq 2
, to ensure that

\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})
is a finite group. A
classical result
[
14
]
of B. Segre
(see also
[
9
, Theorem 11.29]
) yields
that automorphisms of any smooth plane curve of degree at least 4 extend to linear
automorphisms of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

Two main tools in our study are the determination of all
total inflection points of
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
, for which a detailed proof is provided. Next, a description
[
17
]
of the group

H_{d}:=\left\{\mu\in\text{Aut}({\mathbb{P}}^{1})\;:\;\mu(\{\text{the zeros of}\;\varphi_{d}\})=\{\text{the zeros of}\;\varphi_{d}\}\right\}

is used. The results obtained in
this way, are the following:

(I)

The total inflection points of
\mathcal{C}_{d}
are precisely the
d
points in
\mathcal{Z}(y)\cap\mathcal{C}_{d}
, except in the case where
\operatorname{char}(k)=p\geq 3
and
2d={p^{m}+1}
for
some
m\geq 1
. In this exceptional case there are
3d
total inflection points. This result extends
[
1
, Theorem 6.3]
(see Proposition
2.1
).

(II)

If
2d=q+1
for some power
q
of
p
, which is the
situation described in (I), then
\mathcal{C}_{d}
is isomorphic to a Fermat curve of degree
d
and its automorphism group is
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\rtimes S_{3}
(see Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
); Although this fact follows from the general results of

[
9
]
, we provide here a short and elementary proof
which avoids the machinery used in that work.

(III)

If
d=4
, the (well known) result depends on
p
. To describe it, denote by
D_{4}

the dihedral group of order
8
. Also, given groups
G_{1},G_{2}
containing a unique involution
\iota_{j}

in their respective center, the associated central product (see see
[
7
, p. 29]
) is

G_{1}\circ G_{2}=(G_{1}\times G_{2}/\langle(\iota_{1},\iota_{2})\rangle.

•

For
p=5
, the automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}_{d}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\circ\SL(2,3)
(see Remark
3.7
).

•

For
p=7
, the curve is isomorphic to the Fermat curve, and the automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}_{d}
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\rtimes S_{3}
(see Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
).

•

For
p\neq 2,5,7
, the automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}_{d}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\circ D_{4}
(see Theorem
3.6
).

It is well known which groups occur as automorphism group of smooth plane quartic curves; see, e.g.,
[
6
, Theorem 6.5.2 and Table 6.1]
,
[
8
]
,
[
18
, p. 62]
,
[
2
]
. Identifying
{\mathcal{C}}_{4}\colon y^{4}=x^{4}-4x^{2}+2

with the curve in their tables given by
X^{4}+Y^{4}+Z^{4}+aX^{2}Z^{2}=0
, where
a^{2}=8
, the result in (III)
is immediate. We include our own explicit argument.

(IV)

If
4d=q+1
for some power
q
of
p
, then the automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}_{d}
is
\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times S_{3}
(see Theorem
3.9
).

(V)

If
d>4
and neither
2d-1
nor
4d-1
is a power of

p
, then
\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
The same conclusion holds in characteristic zero (see Theorem
3.10
).

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we analyze the total inflection points of
\mathcal{C}_{d}
and clarify certain aspects of
[
1
]
. Section
3
is dedicated to the classification of the automorphism groups for all
d\geq 4
.

2.
Total inflection points

Given a smooth curve
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of degree
d
, a total
inflection point of
{\mathcal{C}}
is a point
P\in{\mathcal{C}}

such that the associated tangent line to
{\mathcal{C}}
meets
{\mathcal{C}}
in
P
with multiplicity
d
.

For
d\geq 4
, automorphisms of any smooth plane curve
{\mathcal{C}}
are given by linear
maps on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, hence they act as permutations on the set
of total inflection points of
{\mathcal{C}}
.
We will
compute these points in the case of
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
,
assuming
d\geq 4
and
\cha(k)\nmid 2d
.
The computation extends the proof of

[
1
, Theorem 6.3]
, where the special case

p:=\cha(k)>2
and
\overline{-1}\in\langle\,\overline{p}\,\rangle\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/2d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

is considered. Moreover, our computations offer a refined analysis of some statements in
[
1
, p. 17]
. For instance, the reasoning there seems to suggest that
\mathcal{C}_{d}
has either
d
or
2d
total inflection points, which does not align with the result in
[
1
, Theorem 6.3]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 4}
. The only total inflection points on the curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
defined as the closure of the affine equation
y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x)

over a field
k
with
\cha(k)\nmid 2d
, are
the
d
points in
{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
, except in the
following situation (where there are
3d
such points, namely the ones in the intersection

\mathcal{Z}(y\cdot(x+2z)\cdot(x-2z))\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
):

\cha(k)=p\geq 3
and
d=(p^{m}+1)/2
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
d\geq 4
.

Proof.

The curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
is defined by the
homogeneous equation

y^{d}=z^{d}\cdot\varphi_{d}(x/z)=:\Phi_{d}(x,z).

We consider all possibilities for a line

\ell\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
with
\#(\ell\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{d})=1
.

(1) The line at infinity (
z=0
)
leads to
\ell\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
given by
y^{d}=x^{d}
,
hence
d
distinct intersection points.

(2) A line
\ell\colon x=az
(for fixed
a
)
results in an intersection given by
y^{d}=z^{d}\cdot\varphi_{d}(a)
. Hence this yields
a single intersection point precisely when

a
is a zero of
\varphi_{d}(X)
. The
corresponding
d
total hyperflexes are
(a:0:1)
,
with tangent line
x=az
.

(3) A line
\ell\colon y=az
leads to the
affine equation
\varphi_{d}(x)=a^{d}
.
From the diagram on page
1
it is
clear that
t\mapsto\varphi_{d}(t)
has,
over points in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
,
only ramification of index
2
. Hence such a line
\ell
intersects
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
in at least
d/2
points.

(4) What remains, is the case
\ell\colon y=ax+bz
where
a\neq 0
. This gives

\#(\ell\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{d})=1
precisely in
the situation

(ax+bz)^{d}-\Phi_{d}(x,z)=(c_{1}x+c_{2}z)^{d}.

Here an equality
a^{d}-1=c_{1}^{d}
is obtained by
considering the coefficient of
x^{d}
,
while the coefficient of
z^{d}

results in
b^{d}-\varphi_{d}(0)=c_{2}^{d}
.

We claim that
c_{1}\neq 0
. Indeed, the
assumption
c_{1}=0
implies that

\varphi_{d}(x)=(ax+b)^{d}-c_{2}^{d}
. Now observe
that the
two maps
t\mapsto\varphi_{d}(t)
and

t\mapsto(at+b)^{d}-c_{2}^{d}
are clearly distinct
(the first has only
\infty
as a ramification
point with index
d
while the other is
totally ramified over both
\infty
and
-c_{2}^{d}
).
As a consequence,

a^{d}\left(x+\frac{b}{a}\right)^{d}-\varphi_{d}(x)=(a^{d}-1)(x+c_{2}/c_{1})^{d}.

Writing

\alpha:=a^{d},\;\beta:=b/a,\;\gamma:=c_{2}/c_{1},\;\text{and}\;x:=t+\frac{1}{t},

one obtains

\alpha\left(t+\beta+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{d}-t^{d}-t^{-d}=(\alpha-1)\left(t+\gamma+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{d}.

Comparing coefficients results in the following
system of equations.

\begin{array}[]{lrclr}t^{d-1}\colon&\alpha\beta d&=&(\alpha\!-\!1)\gamma d&(1)\\
t^{d-2}\colon&\alpha\beta^{2}\binom{d}{2}+\alpha d&=&(\alpha\!-\!1)\left(\gamma^{2}\binom{d}{2}+d\right)&(2)\\
t^{d-3}\colon&\alpha\beta^{3}\binom{d}{3}+2\alpha\beta\binom{d}{2}&=&(\alpha\!-\!1)\left(\gamma^{3}\binom{d}{3}+2\gamma\binom{d}{2}\right)&(3)\\
t^{d-4}\colon&\alpha\!\left(\beta^{4}\binom{d}{4}+\beta^{2}d\binom{d-1}{2}+\binom{d}{2}\right)&=&(\alpha\!-\!1)\left(\gamma^{4}\binom{d}{4}+\gamma^{2}d\binom{d-1}{2}+\binom{d}{2}\right)&(4)\end{array}

The remainder of the calculation distinguishes
various possibilities for
d\in k
.

•

Assume that
d=1
in the field
k
.
The equality (2) results in the contradiction
\alpha=\alpha-1
.

•

Next, consider the case
d=2
in
k
,
with
\cha(k)\neq 3
. Now equality (4) yields
the contradiction
\alpha=\alpha-1
.

•

Assume
d-1\neq 0
and
d-2\neq 0
in
k
(hence in particular
\cha(k)\neq 3
). Then (1) and (3) yield
the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\alpha\beta&=&(\alpha-1)\gamma\\
\alpha\beta^{3}(d-2)+6\alpha\beta&=&(\alpha-1)\gamma^{3}(d-2)+6(\alpha-1)\gamma,\end{array}\right.

or equivalently (add a suitable multiple of
the first equation to the second and
multiply by
(d-2)^{-1}
), one obtains

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\alpha\beta&=&(\alpha-1)\gamma\\
\alpha\beta^{3}&=&(\alpha-1)\gamma^{3}.\end{array}\right.

This means
\alpha\beta^{3}=(\alpha-1)\gamma\cdot\gamma^{2}=\alpha\beta\gamma^{2}
, so since
\alpha\neq 0
we are in one of the
cases

\beta=0\;\;\text{or}\;\;\beta=\gamma\;\;\text{or}\;\;\beta=-\gamma.

If
\beta=0
then (1) and (2) result in
the contradiction
\alpha=\alpha-1
.

If
\beta=\gamma
(which has to be nonzero by what we just showed), then (1) yields the same contradiction
\alpha=\alpha-1
.

The conclusion is that
\beta=-\gamma
, and this is nonzero. Hence (1) implies
\alpha=2^{-1}
. Substituting these values of
\alpha,\beta
in (1)-(4) leads to
the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\gamma^{2}(d-1)=-2,\\
\gamma^{4}(d-2)(d-3)+\gamma^{2}\cdot 12(d-2)=-12.\end{array}\right.

So
\gamma^{2}=2/(1-d)
and

\frac{4}{(d-1)^{2}}(d-2)(d-3)-\frac{24}{d-1}(d-2)+12=0,

resulting in
d\in\{3,2^{-1}\}\subset k
.
This is not possible if
\cha(k)=0
, since
we assume
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 4}
.
For
p=\cha(k)\geq 5
, two situations remain:

(*) if
p\geq 7
and
d\equiv 3\bmod p
, one obtains

\left(t-i+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{d}+\left(t+i+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{d}=2t^{d}+2t^{-d}

with
i
any primitive
4
-the root of unity. Write
d=3+qn
with
q
a power of

p
and
\gcd(n,p)=1
. Comparing
coefficients of
t^{qn-q+2}
results in
\pm 6n\equiv 0\bmod p
, a contradiction.
Hence this case cannot occur.

(*) If
2d\equiv 1\bmod p
, one obtains

\gamma^{2}=4
and

\left(t-2+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{d}+\left(t+2+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{d}=2t^{d}+2t^{-d}.

Taking
t=s^{2}
, this gives

\left(s-\frac{1}{s}\right)^{2d}+\left(s+\frac{1}{s}\right)^{2d}=2s^{2d}+2s^{-2d}.

Now write
2d=1+qn

with
q
a power of
p
and
\gcd(n,p)=1
.
If
n=1
, the given equality indeed holds in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[s,s^{-1}]
.
For
n>1
, considering the coefficient of
s^{2d-2q-2}
leads to the contradiction
2n\equiv 0\bmod p
.

•

The final case to consider, is
\cha(k)=3

and
d\equiv 2\bmod 3
. Writing
d=2+3m
,
this means
\binom{d}{3}\equiv m\bmod 3
and

\binom{d}{4}\equiv-m\bmod 3
.
The system of equations derived on p.
2
then takes the form

\begin{array}[]{lrclcr}t^{d-1}\colon&2\alpha\beta&=&2(\alpha\!-\!1)\gamma&&(1)\\
t^{d-2}\colon&\alpha\beta^{2}+2\alpha&=&(\alpha\!-\!1)\left(\gamma^{2}+2\right)&&(2)\\
t^{d-3}\colon&m\alpha\beta^{3}+2\alpha\beta&=&(\alpha\!-\!1)\left(m\gamma^{3}+2\gamma\right)&&(3)\\
t^{d-4}\colon&\alpha\!\left(-m\beta^{4}+1\right)&=&(\alpha\!-\!1)\left(-m\gamma^{4}+1\right)&&(4)\end{array}

If
m=0
in
k
, equation (4) gives the contradiction
\alpha=\alpha-1
. Thus
m\neq 0
. Combining (1) and (3) results, as in the previous case, in the equalities
\beta=-\gamma
and
\alpha=2^{-1}=2
. Then (2), (4)
reduce to the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}2\gamma^{2}+1=\gamma^{2}+2,\\
m\gamma^{4}+2=-m\gamma^{4}+1\end{array}\right.

which is equivalent to
\gamma=\pm 2
and
m=1\in k
. So in integers one obtains
d=2+3(1+3n)\equiv 5\bmod 9
, and in
k[t,t^{-1}]
the equality

\left(t+2+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{d}+\left(t-2+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{d}=2t^{d}+2t^{-d}

holds. Writing
t=s^{2}
and
2d=1+q\ell
with

q\geq 9
a power of
3
and
3\nmid\ell
,
this takes the form

(s+s^{-1})(s^{q}+s^{-q})^{\ell}+(s-s^{-1})(s^{q}-s^{-q})^{\ell}=2s^{2d}+2s^{-2d}.

In case
\ell=1
(in
\mathbb{Z}
) this identity of Laurent polynomials indeed holds.
In the remaining case
\ell\geq 2
,
the coefficient of
s^{q\ell-2q-1}
in the left-hand-side equals
2\ell\neq 0
in
k
,
hence the equality is not possible.

This completes the proof; the calculations show that indeed the total inflection points are the ones described in the statement of the proposition.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Note that in Proposition
2.1
,
in any case where only
d
total inflection points exist, these points are all
on a unique line (namely, on the line given by
y=0
). Similarly, in any case with

3d
total inflection points (
d\geq 4
), there
is a unique set of three lines such that
their union intersected with
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
equals
this set of points (namely, the lines
defined by
y=0
and
x=\pm 2z
).

3.
Automorphism groups

As a first step towards understanding the group
of automorphisms of a plane curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}

corresponding to
y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x)
, we distinguish according to the number of
total inflection points as given by
Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{d}
be the curve defined by

\mathcal{C}_{d}:y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x)

over a field of characteristic
p>0
.
If
2d=q+1
with
q
a power of
p
, then
\mathcal{C}_{d}
is isomorphic over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
to the Fermat curve

\mathcal{F}:v^{d}=u^{d}+1.

Proof.

Consider the map
\mathcal{F}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
given in affine coordinates by

(u,v)\mapsto\left(x:=\frac{2u+2}{u-1}\,,\,y:=\frac{av}{u-1}\right)

where
a^{d}=2
and so
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
.
Since
x,y
generate the
function field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}(\mathcal{F})
,
this map defines a birational map to its
image. We
will show that its image is the curve
\mathcal{C}_{d}
, in other words we show

2v^{d}=(u-1)^{d}y^{d}=(u-1)^{d}\varphi_{d}(\frac{2u+2}{u-1})

in case
2d=q+1
. Equivalently, we claim that for
d=(q+1)/2
, in the polynomial
ring
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[u]
the equality
(u-1)^{d}\varphi_{d}(\frac{2u+2}{u-1})=2u^{d}+2
holds.
Indeed, write
u=s^{2}
. In
\mathbb{F}_{p}(s)\supset{\mathbb{F}}_{p}(u)
one observes

\frac{2u+2}{u-1}=\frac{s+1}{s-1}+\frac{s-1}{s+1}.

From this,
we obtain

(u-1)^{d}\varphi_{d}\left(\frac{2u+2}{u-1}\right)=(s+1)^{d}(s-1)^{d}\left(\left(\frac{s+1}{s-1}\right)^{d}+\left(\frac{s-1}{s+1}\right)^{d}\right).

Since
2d=1+q
,
this equals

(s^{q}+1)(s+1)+(s^{q}-1)(s-1)=2u^{d}+2.

Hence we described an explicit birational map from
\mathcal{F}
to
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
.
Since both curves are smooth, the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

For
2d=q+1
, it is known that the curve
y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x)
is maximal over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
Thus, by
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
, the curve is isomorphic to the maximal Fermat curve of degree
d=(q+1)/2
.
Here we provided a direct proof of this result.

The explicit isomorphism presented above can
in fact be obtained using Proposition
2.1
. Namely, it is
well known that in terms of homogeneous
coordinates
u,v,w
describing the Fermat curve
\mathcal{F}\colon v^{d}=u^{d}+w^{d}
,
the total inflection points of
\mathcal{F}

(provided d-1 is not a power of the characteristic) are the
3d
points in
\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{Z}(uvw)
.
Moreover,
\Aut(\mathcal{F})
permutes
the three lines
\mathcal{Z}(u),\mathcal{Z}(v),\mathcal{Z}(w)
. The map used in
the proof of Proposition
3.1

yields one of the possible ways to associate
these lines to the analogous lines for
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
via a linear map.

Proposition 3.3
.

Consider the curve
\mathcal{C}_{d}
given by
y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x)
defined over a field of characteristic
p>0
.
If
2d=q+1
with
q
a power of
p
, then the automorphism group
G
of the curve
\mathcal{C}_{d}
has order
6d^{2}
and has a normal abelian subgroup
H\cong\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
of order
d^{2}
such that
G/H\cong S_{3}.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.1
, we know the curve
\mathcal{C}_{d}

is isomorphic to the Fermat curve
\mathcal{F}
of degree
d
.
Its
3d
total inflection points (this number because of the linear isomorphism between

\mathcal{C}_{d}
and
\mathcal{F}
) are on the three coordinate lines.
These are the only lines containing
d
such points: any other line
contains at most one on each coordinate line, and
d\geq 4
.
Consequently every automorphism permutes the coordinate lines.
After rescaling the coordinates, write the equation as

u^{d}+v^{d}+w^{d}=0
. The kernel of the action on these three lines consists
of the diagonal transformations and is isomorphic to

\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
.
The coordinate permutations give a subgroup
S_{3}
, so

G\cong(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})\rtimes S_{3}
.
∎

Both
[
13
,
15
]
prove the same result on automorphisms of Fermat curves.
In the case
d=q+1
, which is disjoint from
the assumption in Proposition
3.3
,
the total inflection points on
\mathcal{F}

are the intersection of
\mathcal{F}
with
a set of more than
3
lines, and the argument
presented above needs to be adapted accordingly.
Note that Leopoldt’s result, although published in 1996, was already obtained in the early seventies.

This completes the determination of

\text{Aut}(\mathcal{C}_{d})
whenever this
curve has
3d
total inflection points.
From now on we consider the remaining case.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
contains precisely
d
total
inflection points. Extending automorphisms of
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
to linear
automorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, these
restrict to automorphisms of the line
\mathcal{Z}(y)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and this defines
a group homomorphism

\textrm{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{C}}_{d})\longrightarrow\textrm{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}).

Its kernel is central and consists of the automorphisms

(x,y)\mapsto(x,\zeta y)
with
\zeta

ranging over the
d
-th roots of unity.
Its image is the group
H_{d}
of Möbius transformations
x\mapsto\frac{ax+b}{cx+\delta}

that permute the set of zeros of
\varphi_{d}(x)
.

Proof.

The set of total inflection points of
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
is preserved under any automorphism (since
automorphisms are given by linear maps.
The assumption implies that
y=0
defines the
unique line containing all these inflection
points, hence any automorphism restricts to
a linear automorphism of this line, i.e.,
to a Möbius transformation. Since
elements of
\text{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{C}}_{d})
permute
the total inflection points, these
Möbius transformations permute the zeros of
\varphi_{d}(x)
. Vice versa, if

x\mapsto\frac{ax+b}{cx+\delta}
permutes the zeros of
\varphi_{d}(x)
, then

\varphi_{d}(\frac{ax+b}{cx+\delta})=e(cx+\delta)^{-d}\varphi_{d}(x)

for some nonzero constant
e
. As a consequence,
the given Möbius transformation is the image of the element

(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{ax+b}{cx+\delta}\,,\,\sqrt[d]{e}\frac{y}{cx+\delta}\right)

in
\text{\rm Aut}({\mathcal{C}}_{d})
.

The tangent line at
(\alpha:0:1)
is
x-\alpha z=0
.
These
d
lines meet at the unique point
Q=(0:1:0)
.
An automorphism therefore preserves both
y=0
and
Q
, is
given by a matrix

\begin{pmatrix}a&0&b\\
0&e&0\\
c&0&\delta\end{pmatrix},\qquad e(a\delta-bc)\neq 0

as an element of
\text{PGL}(3,k)
. If it restricts to the identity map on the line
y=0
, then this matrix
can be rescaled to

a=\delta=1
and
b=c=0
. The equation of the curve then implies

e^{d}=1
. Conversely every such
[(x,y)\mapsto(x,ey)]\in\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})
is in the kernel of the given
homomorphism to
\Aut({\mathbb{P}}^{1})
, and
commutes with every matrix of the displayed form.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

It may be interesting to observe that Lemma
3.4
and the above proof generalize to
any smooth plane curve
{\mathcal{D}}\colon y^{d}=g(x)
where
g(x)
is a separable polynomial of degree
d\geq 4
.
Namely, the
d
zeros
\alpha
of
g(x)
yield total inflection points
(\alpha,0)\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Hence if
{\mathcal{D}}
contains precisely
d
total inflection points, then the (automatically linear)
automorphisms of
{\mathcal{D}}
restrict to
{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and one proceeds as in the proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Lemma
3.4
identifies
\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})/N
with
H_{d}

whenever
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
has precisely
d
total inflection points.

For completeness we describe the automorphism group
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
in the specific case
d=4
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic

\neq 2,\neq 5,\neq 7
. The automorphism group
G
of the curve

y^{4}=\varphi_{4}(x)
over
k
has order
16
and contains a central
subgroup
N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/4{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

G/N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}.

More precisely,
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\circ D_{4}
.

Proof.

Since for
d=4
we have
2d=p+1
for
p=7
,
Proposition
2.1
and our
assumption that
\cha(k)\neq 7
imply that

{\mathcal{C}}_{4}
has precisely
4
total inflection points. Hence Lemma
3.4
gives a central exact sequence

1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow H_{4}\longrightarrow 1,\qquad N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/4{\mathbb{Z}}.

With
\lambda^{4}=2
, set

x=\lambda u
and
y=\lambda v
. The curve becomes

v^{4}=u^{4}+au^{2}+1,\qquad a=-2\lambda^{2},\qquad a^{2}=8.

The usual four-point cross-ratio classification, as used in

[
6
, Section 6.5.2]
, gives a Klein four-group for the
projective stabilizer of the zeros of
u^{4}+au^{2}+1
, except when

a(a^{2}-36)=0
or
a^{2}+12=0
.
Here
a\neq 0
, and
a^{2}=8
shows that these exceptions occur only
in characteristics
7
and
5
, respectively. Hence

G/N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
and
|G|=16
.

It remains to identify the extension.
Choose
i
with
i^{2}=-1
. The automorphisms

z:(u,v)\mapsto(u,iv),\qquad s:(u,v)\mapsto(-u,v),\qquad t:(u,v)\mapsto(1/u,v/u)

satisfy

z^{4}=s^{2}=t^{2}=1,\qquad z\ \text{is central},\qquad st=z^{2}ts.

The images of
s
and
t
generate
G/N
, so these three elements
generate
G
. Put
r=st
. Then
r^{2}=z^{2}
and
srs=r^{-1}
;
consequently
\langle r,s\rangle\cong D_{4}
and

\langle r,s\rangle\cap\langle z\rangle=\langle z^{2}\rangle
.
This proves
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\circ D_{4}
. In particular, this central extension
does not split: every lift of the image of
s
has nontrivial
commutator
z^{2}
with every lift of the image of
t
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

In the case
\textrm{char}(k)=7
, the
curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{4}\colon y^{4}=\varphi_{4}(x)

contains
12
total inflection points
(see Proposition
2.1
) and in this
case Proposition
3.3
shows that
the automorphism group is

\left(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\right)\rtimes S_{3}
, of cardinality
96
.

In the remaining case
\cha(k)=5
, the curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{4}
has precisely

4
total inflection points. With notations as in the proof of
Theorem
3.6
, the same four-point classification gives
G/N=A_{4}
:
here
a^{2}=-12
, whereas
a(a^{2}-36)\neq 0
.
Thus
|G|=48
.

Let
E
be the inverse image in
G
of the normal Klein four-group
of
A_{4}
. The same automorphisms
z,s,t
used in the proof of
Theorem
3.6
generate
E
, with the same relations.
The elements
u_{0}=zs
and
v_{0}=zt
satisfy

u_{0}^{2}=v_{0}^{2}=z^{2},\qquad v_{0}u_{0}=z^{2}u_{0}v_{0},

and hence generate a quaternion group
Q
of order
8
.
The presentation of
E
gives

Q=\{1,z^{2}\}\ \cup\ \{g\in E\setminus N:g^{2}=z^{2}\}.

Since
E
is normal in
G
and
N
is central, this description
shows that
Q
is normal in
G
.

Choose
g\in G
whose image in
A_{4}
has order
3
, and put

w=g^{4}
. Since
g^{3}\in N
and
N
has order
4
, the element
w

has order
3
and has the same image as
g
. Its conjugation action
on
Q/\langle z^{2}\rangle
cycles the three nonidentity elements.
Consequently

\langle Q,w\rangle\cong Q_{8}\rtimes C_{3}\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{3}).

Moreover,

G=N\cdot\langle Q,w\rangle,\qquad N\cap\langle Q,w\rangle=\langle z^{2}\rangle.

It follows that

\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{4})\cong\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\circ\SL(2,3).

Explicitly,
{\mathcal{C}}_{4}
in characteristic
5
admits the automorphism
of order
3
given by

(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{-2\beta x+(1-2\beta^{2})}{(2\beta^{2}+2)x+\beta},\frac{\eta y}{(2\beta^{2}+2)x+\beta}\right),\qquad\eta=3(\beta^{3}+2\beta),

where
\beta^{4}-4\beta^{2}+2=0
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Consider the curve
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}\colon y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x)
over a field
k
of characteristic
p
, with
4d=p^{r}+1
for some
r>0
.
Then
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
admits the automorphism of order
3
given by

(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{2x+12}{2-x}\,,\,\frac{-4y}{2-x}\right).

Proof.

Write
q=p^{r}
, then
4d=q+1

and therefore
\sqrt{-1}\not\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
This implies

(-4)^{d}=(1+\sqrt{-1})^{q+1}=(1-\sqrt{-1})(1+\sqrt{-1})=2.

From
[
17
, Lemma 4.6]
one obtains

\varphi_{d}(x)=\frac{(2-x)^{d}}{2}\varphi_{d}\left(\frac{2x+12}{2-x}\right)\in k[x].

Hence the given map indeed defines an automorphism of
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
. A
straightforward computation shows that it
has order
3
.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that the characteristic of the field
k
is
p\geq 3
.
If
4d=q+1
, where
q
is a power of
p
and
d\geq 4
, then
the automorphism group
G
of the curve

{\mathcal{C}}_{d}\colon y^{d}=\varphi_{d}(x)

has order
6d
and is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}\times S_{3}
.

Proof.

The equality
4d=q+1
rules out
d=4
, so
d>4
.
Also
2d-1
cannot be a power of
p
, since otherwise the equality

4d-1=2(2d-1)+1
gives
0=1
modulo
p
.
Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
3.4
therefore give a
central exact sequence

1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow H_{d}\longrightarrow 1,\qquad N=\{(x,y)\mapsto(x,\zeta y):\zeta^{d}=1\}.

The determination of
H_{d}
in
[
17
, Proposition 5.3]
gives
H_{d}\cong S_{3}
.

Put
\epsilon=1
if
d
is even and
\epsilon=-1
if
d
is odd.
The automorphisms represented in homogeneous coordinates by

S=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\
0&\epsilon&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad T=\begin{pmatrix}2&0&12\\
0&-4&0\\
-1&0&2\end{pmatrix}

are respectively a lift of
x\mapsto-x
and the automorphism in
Proposition
3.8
. Direct multiplication gives

S^{2}=I,\qquad T^{3}=-64I,\qquad STS=-\tfrac{1}{4}T^{2}.

The classes of
S
and
T
in
\PGL(3)
thus generate a subgroup
isomorphic to
S_{3}
whose image is all of
H_{d}
. The subgroup of
\PGL(3)
generated by the classes of
S,T

meets
N
trivially. Since
N
is central, the resulting product
is direct, i.e.,
G\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}\times S_{3}
.
∎

The remaining case also uses the determination of the group
H_{d}
in
[
17
]
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume
d>4
and
p\nmid 2d
. Suppose either that
p=0
, or that

p>0
and neither
2d-1
nor
4d-1
is a power of
p
. Then

\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

By
[
17
, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 5.1]
, the group of projective transformations permuting
the zeros of
\varphi_{d}
, under the given assumption on
d
, is

H_{d}=\{x\mapsto x,\ x\mapsto-x\}
.
Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
3.4
give the central
exact sequence

1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})\longrightarrow H_{d}\longrightarrow 1,\qquad N\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}.

Since
\varphi_{d}(-x)=(-1)^{d}\varphi_{d}(x)
, the map

\widetilde{\sigma}(x,y)=\begin{cases}(-x,y),&d\ \text{even},\\
(-x,-y),&d\ \text{odd},\end{cases}

is an involution lifting the nonidentity element of
H_{d}
.
It commutes with
N
, so

\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})=N\times\langle\widetilde{\sigma}\rangle
.
∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
d\geq 4
and
p:=\cha(k)\geq 0
and assume
p\nmid 2d
.
Then the geometric automorphism group of
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
is as follows:

\Aut({\mathcal{C}}_{d})\cong\begin{cases}({\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}})\rtimes S_{3},&2d=p^{r}+1\text{ for some }r\geq 1,\\[2.0pt]
{\mathbb{Z}}/4{\mathbb{Z}}\circ\SL(2,3),&d=4,\ p=5,\\[2.0pt]
{\mathbb{Z}}/4{\mathbb{Z}}\circ D_{4},&d=4,\ p\neq 5,7,\\[2.0pt]
{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}\times S_{3},&d>4,\ 4d=p^{r}+1\text{ for some }r\geq 1,\\[2.0pt]
{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Combine Props.
3.1
and
3.3
,
Thms.
3.6
and
3.9
and
3.10
, and Remark
3.7
.
∎

Abstract
. The Szeged index of a graph is an invariant with several applications in chemistry. The power graph of a finite group G G is a graph having vertex set as G G in which two vertices u u and v v are adjacent if v = u m v=u^{m} or u = v n u=v^{n} for some m , n ∈ ℕ m,n\in\mathbb{N} . In this paper, we first obtain a formula for the Szeged index of the generalized join of graphs. As an application, we obtain the Szeged index of the power graph of the finite cyclic group ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} for any n > 2 n>2 . We further obtain a relation between the Szeged index of the power graph of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} and the Szeged index of the power graph of the dihedral group D n \mathrm{D}_{n} . We also provide SAGE codes for evaluating the Szeged index of the power graph of ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} and D n \mathrm{D}_{n} at the end of this paper. Keywords: Szeged index, generalized join, power graph, finite cyclic group, dihedral group. AMS Subject Classification: 05C09, 05C25

Introduction

Throughout the paper,
G

Also, given a set

In theoretical chemistry, topological indices are widely used to understand the physical and chemical properties of chemical compounds. There exist many different types of topological indices that aim to capture various aspects of the molecular graphs associated with the molecules considered.
The oldest and most thoroughly examined topological index is the Wiener index (see
[
30
,
24
,
13
]
for some references).
Another topological index, the Szeged index, was introduced much later and extensively studied.
We discuss the Szeged index and its close association with the Wiener index in short below.
Suppose
G
is a finite, simple, and connected graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. The number of vertices of
G
is denoted by
\lvert G\rvert
.
If two vertices
a
and
b
are adjacent to each other, we denote it by
a\sim b
.
Moreover,
E(G)=\{(a,b):a,b\in V(G)\text{ with }a\sim b\}
.
The distance between two vertices
a,b\in G
, denoted by
d(a,b)
, is defined to be the length of the shortest path from
a
to
b
. The
Wiener index

[
26
]
of a connected graph
G
is defined as follows:

(1)

\displaystyle W(G)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b\in V(G)}d(a,b).

Suppose
e\in E(G)
is an edge between the vertices
a
and
b
of
G
.
We first state the following definitions:
We define

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}n_{1}(ab|G)=\lvert\{x\in G:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}\rvert\\
n_{2}(ab|G)=\lvert\{x\in G:d(x,b)<d(x,a)\}\rvert.\end{split}

The two quantities described in
Equation

2
were mentioned for the first time in
[
34
]
.
For a long time, it was known that the formula

(3)

\displaystyle W(G)=\sum_{e(=ab)\in E(G)}n_{1}(ab|G)n_{2}(ab|G)

holds for molecular graphs of alkanes.
In
[
17
]
, it was proved that
Equation

3
holds for all trees.
Furthermore, in
[
28
]
it was shown that

3
does not hold in general (in particular for graphs containing cycles), and only holds for graphs whose each block is a complete graph. Although the attempts to change the right-hand side of

to make it applicable to all connected graphs have been successfully made in
[
31
,
32
]
, the resulting expressions were very confusing.
In
[

, it was suggested that the complications arising with the generalization of

3
as the definition of a new graph invariant. Consequently, the formula was extended to all graphs and it came to be known as the Szeged index of a graph. The
Szeged index
of a connected graph

is defined as follows:

(4)

\displaystyle Sz(G)=\sum_{e(=ab)\in E(G)}n_{1}(ab|G)n_{2}(ab|G).

The Szeged index has been considered from multiple viewpoints, see for example
[

,
27

[

In

In
[

[

Some variants of the Szeged index namely the edge PI index, edge Szeged index,
edge-vertex Szeged index, vertex-edge Szeged index, and revised edge Szeged index

12

.
Recently, the Szeged index of unicyclic graphs was studied in

Motivated by the above works, in this paper, we deduce the Szeged index of the generalized join of graphs.

Szeged Index of Generalized Join of Graphs

In this section, we deduce the Szeged index of the generalized join of graphs in terms of the constituent graphs.
Consider a family of
n
connected graphs
G_{i}
such that
\mid G_{i}\mid=n_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a graph such that
\mid\mathcal{G}\mid=n
.
The
generalized join

\mathcal{G}
-join of graphs

is defined as the following:

Definition 2.1
.

[
33
, p.
15
]

Suppose
\mathcal{G}
is a graph with vertex set
V(\mathcal{G})=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
Suppose
G_{i}
be disjoint connected graphs of order
n_{i}
with vertex sets
V(G_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
The
\mathcal{G}
-join of graphs
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{n}
denoted by
G=\mathcal{G}[G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{n}]
is formed by taking the graphs
G_{i}
and any two vertices
v_{i}\in G_{i}
and
v_{j}\in G_{j}
are adjacent if
i
is adjacent to
j
in
\mathcal{G}
.

We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.1
.

Consider
\mathcal{G}
to be a graph with vertex set
V(\mathcal{G})=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
Assume that
G_{i}
’s are disjoint connected graphs of order

with vertex sets

for
1\leq i\leq n
.
The Szeged index of

is given as follows:

\displaystyle Sz(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Sz(G_{i})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j,i\sim j\end{subarray}}^{n}\biggl[\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\sim i,k\nsim j\end{subarray}}^{n}|G_{k}|+1\biggr)\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\nsim i,k\sim j\end{subarray}}^{n}|G_{k}|+1\biggr)\biggr]\mid G_{i}\mid\mid G_{j}\mid.

Proof.

Suppose

.
Since

is an edge in
G
, two possible cases may arise:

1.

and
b\in G_{i}
for some
i
where
1\leq i\leq n
, or

2.

a\in G_{i}
and
b\in G_{j}
for some

such that
i\sim j
in
\mathcal{G}
.

Hence,

(
A
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Sz(G)&=\sum_{e(=ab)\in E(G)}n(ab|G)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e(=ab)\in E(G)\\
{a,b\in G_{i}}\end{subarray}}n(ab|G)\bigg)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e(=ab)\in E(G)\\
a\in G_{i},b\in G_{j}\\
i\sim j\end{subarray}}n(ab|G)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e(=ab)\in E(G)\\
{a,b\in G_{i}}\end{subarray}}n_{1}(ab|G)n_{2}(ab|G)\bigg)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e(=ab)\in E(G)\\

i\sim j\end{subarray}}n(ab|G)\end{split}

We shall now evaluate each term of the summation given in
Equation

in what follows.

We have,

(*)

\displaystyle\begin{split}n_{1}(ab|G)&=\lvert\{x\in G:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}\rvert\\
&=\lvert\biggl(\{x\in G_{i}:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}\cup\{x\in G\setminus G_{i}:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}\biggr)\rvert\\
&=\lvert\{x\in G_{i}:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}\rvert+\lvert\{x\in G\setminus G_{i}:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}\rvert\\
&=n_{1}(ab|G_{i})+\lvert\{x\in G\setminus G_{i}:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}\rvert.\end{split}

We note that since

.
Hence we find that

\displaystyle\{x\in G\setminus G_{i}:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}

\displaystyle=\emptyset

Thus, we have,

(**)

\displaystyle\lvert\{x\in G\setminus G_{i}:d(x,a)<d(x,b)\}\rvert

\displaystyle=0.

Hence, using

and
**

2
, we find that for all

n_{1}(ab|G)=n_{1}(ab|G_{i}).

Using similar arguments as above, we find that for all

,

n_{2}(ab|G)=n_{2}(ab|G_{i}).

Hence we have,

(7)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e(=ab)\in E(G)\\
{a,b\in G_{i}}\end{subarray}}n_{1}(ab|G)n_{2}(ab|G)\bigg)&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}n_{1}(ab|G_{i})n_{2}(ab|G_{i})\\

We now evaluate the second term of the summation given in

.

We have,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e(=ab)\in E(G)\\

Here

.
Consider the set

Using

3.
Szeged Index of Power Graph of Finite Groups

The notion of
directed power graph
of a semigroup
S
was introduced by Kelarav and Quinn in
[
23
]
as the digraph
\mathcal{P}(S)
with vertex set
S
in which there is an arc from one vertex
u
to another vertex
v
if
v=u^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Motivated by this, Chakrabarty et al. in
[
20
]
defined the undirected
power graph

\mathcal{P}(S)
of a semi-group
S
as an undirected graph whose vertex set is
S
and two distinct vertices
u,v\in S
are adjacent if
u=v^{m}
or
v=u^{n}
for some
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
In
[
20
]
it was further shown that
\mathcal{P}(G)
is connected for any finite group
G
, and it is complete if and only if
G
is a cyclic group of order
1
or
p^{m}
where
p
is a prime and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
The power graph has received widespread attention from researchers over the recent years. Cameron and Ghosh have shown in
[
18
]
that if two finite abelian groups
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
have isomorphic power graphs, then
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are isomorphic to each other.
In
[
19
]
Cameron has proved that if two finite groups have isomorphic power graphs, then they
have the same number of elements of each order.
The spectral properties of power graphs of finite groups have been studied in
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
9
]
.
For more interesting results on power graphs, the readers may refer to
[
15
]
.

3.1.
Szeged Index of Power Graph of

In this section, we derive a formula for the Szeged index of the power graph of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}

.
Before getting into the details, we first throw some light on the structure of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
.

We shall denote the prime decomposition of

An element

is said to be a

of

if
d\mid n
and
d\notin\{1,n\}

Given

\displaystyle D^{\prime}=(\alpha_{1}+1)(\alpha_{2}+1)\cdots(\alpha_{m}+1).

The number of

(11)

\displaystyle D=(\alpha_{1}+1)(\alpha_{2}+1)\cdots(\alpha_{m}+1)-2.

Suppose

Assume that
\mathcal{G}

.
Moreover, the adjacency criterion in

1\sim d_{i}

Using the above information, we have the following result about the structure of

Proof.

We shall denote

\displaystyle\begin{split}Sz(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{D}_{n}))&=\sum_{e(=ab)\in E(\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{D}_{n}))}n(ab|\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{D}_{n}))\\

\displaystyle\sum_{e(=ab)\in E(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{*}))}n(ab|\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{D}_{n}))

\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{*})

\displaystyle n_{2}(ab|\mathcal{P}(\mathrm{D}_{n}))

\displaystyle=n_{2}(ab|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{*}))

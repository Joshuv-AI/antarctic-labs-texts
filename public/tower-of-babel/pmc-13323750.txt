Global trends in tea and diabetes research: a bibliometric analysis of literature from 2000 to 2024
Authors: Yao Zhang, Rui Ren, Liying Wang, Jilong Bai, Jinyan Liu, Wenxu Wang, Yanan Ma, Na Li, Difei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTea has attracted significant attention for its potential antidiabetic properties, primarily attributed to its bioactive compounds such as polyphenols. This study aims to systematically evaluate the global research landscape and emerging trends in tea and diabetes. Publications were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection and analyzed using bibliometric tools, including VOSviewer, RStudio, and CiteSpace. A total of 2634 publications from 103 countries/regions (2000–2024) were included, with an annual growth rate of 12.25%. China, the United States, and Japan were the leading contributors. Harvard University and Gan RY were the most influential institution and author, respectively, while Nutrients was the leading journal. Research themes have evolved from early mechanistic studies focusing on antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties of tea polyphenols to more integrative investigations involving gut microbiota, inflammation, and metabolic regulation. Research on tea and diabetes has shifted toward a more comprehensive and translational framework. Tea polyphenols play important roles in improving insulin sensitivity, reducing oxidative stress and inflammation, and modulating metabolic pathways, although challenges such as low bioavailability remain. These findings provide valuable insights into the current research landscape and may inform future studies on diabetes prevention and management.Subject terms: Type 2 diabetes, Endocrine system and metabolic diseases

Introduction

Diabetes has become one of the most severe and prevalent chronic diseases of our time, posing significant health threats, leading to disabling complications, and reducing life expectancy [1]. According to the International Diabetes Federation, the global prevalence of diabetes was 10.5% (536.6 million people) in 2021, and it is projected to rise to 12.2% (783.2 million people) by 2045, with related medical expenditures reaching USD 1.054 trillion [2]. Impaired glucose metabolism leads to complications such as diabetic nephropathy, cardiovascular disease, neuropathy, retinopathy, and liver disorders, all of which are leading causes of morbidity and mortality [3–5].

Over several centuries, tea has spread from China to other parts of Asia, then to Europe and the Americas, and eventually to Africa [6, 7]. As a globally popular beverage, tea contains various bioactive compounds that confer multiple health benefits, including antioxidant and anticancer activities, hepatoprotective effects, cardiovascular benefits, anti-obesity effects, modulation of gut microbiota balance, and antidiabetic effects [8–12]. Numerous studies have shown that bioactive compounds in tea, such as catechins, theaflavins, and caffeine, can help prevent and treat diabetes and its complications. The main mechanisms of action include protection of pancreatic β-cells, improved insulin sensitivity, and anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity [11, 13–15]. Tea exerts synergistic effects with certain antidiabetic drugs in the management of diabetes and its complications [11]. Recent in vitro and in vivo studies, as well as clinical trials, have further supported the role of tea in the prevention and treatment of diabetes and its complications [16, 17]. Compared with synthetic hypoglycaemic drugs, tea, as a potential hypoglycaemic agent, offers advantages such as lower cost, better patient compliance, and fewer side effects [18].

Bibliometric analysis is a technique that uses mathematical and statistical methods to quantify research output in specific academic fields, revealing research status, trends, and key influential studies [19, 20]. Citation analysis enables the measurement of the impact and academic value of scholarly articles, providing deeper insights into the academic development and disciplinary progress of a specific field [21]. In recent years, bibliometric approaches have been increasingly used to investigate tea-related research in various disease contexts, including neurodegenerative disorders, cancer, obesity, and broader polyphenol-associated chronic diseases [22–25]. However, the global knowledge structure of research on tea and diabetes has not yet been systematically characterized. Given the rapid growth of studies exploring the metabolic effects of tea and its bioactive compounds, a comprehensive overview of research development, collaboration patterns, and emerging themes in this field is required.

We conducted a comprehensive bibliometric and visual analysis of global research on tea and diabetes to clarify the research landscape and identify emerging hotspots by examining publication trends, collaboration networks, and thematic evolution. Furthermore, by integrating evidence across mechanistic studies, observational analyses, and clinical research, this study provides a deeper understanding of how tea-related diabetes research has evolved and offers insights for future investigations.

Materials and methods

Data sources and search strategy

The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) is a comprehensive database covering various scientific fields and provides valuable data for bibliometric analysis [19, 26]. The Science Citation Index Expanded within WoSCC includes top-tier journals in natural sciences [21], making it our chosen platform for data retrieval. On 27 November 2024, all studies published from 1 January 2000 to 31 October 2024 were retrieved and downloaded. To minimize potential bias caused by ongoing database updates, we performed the literature search and data retrieval on the same day using the search formula: (TS = [tea]) AND TS = (“diabete*“ or “diabetic*“ or “diabetes mellitus” or “diabetic mellitus” or “Type 1 Diabetes” or “Type 2 Diabetes”). Figure S1 provides a detailed description of the data extraction and screening process used in this study. Only articles and reviews in English were included, resulting in 2634 papers for the subsequent analysis. All papers and references were exported in plain text format.

Data analysis

The Bibliometrix R package (version 4.3.0) was used in RStudio for data extraction, preprocessing, and descriptive bibliometric analysis. Microsoft Excel 2021 was used for supplementary data management and visualization. The analysis included annual publication counts, average citations, top countries, institutions, journals, authors, and highly cited papers. The top 20 keywords and strength of country collaboration were also examined. Two independent authors performed data extraction and analysis to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results.

VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) was used to construct co-authorship and keyword co-occurrence networks based on its built-in clustering algorithm [27, 28]. Prior to analysis, duplicate records were identified and removed, and keywords were standardized by merging synonyms and unifying singular and plural forms to improve the accuracy of co-occurrence analysis. Co-authorship analysis was performed to evaluate collaboration patterns among authors and institutions, while keyword co-occurrence analysis was used to identify thematic relationships [29, 30]. The overlay visualization function was applied to illustrate temporal evolution. Minimum thresholds were set at 10 publications for institutions, 4 publications for authors, and 50 occurrences for keywords to ensure a balance between network clarity and information completeness.

CiteSpace (version 6.3.R3) was used for citation analysis and visualization, enabling the identification of the structure, distribution, and temporal evolution of scientific knowledge [31]. It was applied to detect references and keywords with the strongest citation bursts within specific time periods, thereby identifying emerging trends and research frontiers [32]. Impact factors (IF) for journals were sourced from the 2023 Journal Citation Reports (JCRs).

Results

Trends in annual publications

Overall, 2,634 publications involving tea and diabetes were retrieved between 1 January 2000 and 31 October 2024. The number of publications exceeded 100 in 2012 and 200 in 2022, with an annual growth rate of 12.25% (Fig. 1A). The cumulative number of publications from 2000 to 2023 was modeled using a quadratic regression (y = 4.8425x² – 15.593x + 36.706, R² = 0.9997). The average annual citation count during this period was 5.76, peaking at 10.44 in 2002 (Fig. 1B). Since 2019, the number of citations per publication has decreased due to citation delays for recent articles [21].

Discussion

Global research overview and collaboration patterns

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive bibliometric and visual analysis of 2634 publications on tea and diabetes retrieved from the WoSCC between January 1, 2000, and October 31, 2024. The number of publications showed an overall upward trend, with an annual growth rate of 12.25%, indicating a growing scientific interest in the potential role of tea and its bioactive components in metabolic health and diabetes prevention.

By analyzing publication data by country, China, the United States, and Japan collectively published 1198 articles, accounting for 45.47% of the total publications. China contributed the largest number of publications in this field, attributable to its status as the country where tea originated and its long-standing tea culture which provides a solid historical and cultural foundation for related research [24]. This is further supported by China’s substantial production and consumption of tea. In 2019, approximately 2.07 million tons of tea leaves were consumed by about 490 million people, representing 36.85% of global consumption. In 2020, China exported approximately USD 2.04 billion in tea, making it the world’s leading tea exporter [33]. Since 2022, China has produced over 50% of the world’s total tea and accounted for more than 60% of global tea plantation areas [34]. Additionally, the magnitude of China’s diabetes burden, with more than 118 million individuals affected, may further stimulate research interest in tea as a potential preventive or therapeutic strategy [35]. Despite producing the most publications, China’s AC/P remained lower than that of the United States, possibly due to domestic citation patterns, a higher proportion of publications in local journals, and limited international collaboration [36]. The United States had the highest average number of citations per publication among the major contributing countries, indicating the strong academic influence of its research outputs.

China and the United States also play important roles in the global collaboration network. China occupies a central position in the cooperation network and maintains particularly strong collaborative links with the United States. At the same time, the United States shows a relatively higher proportion of internationally collaborative publications (MCP), suggesting a stronger engagement in cross-national research networks. Japan and India are also ranked among the leading contributors to publication output, reflecting growing research interest in dietary and functional food interventions for metabolic diseases in Asia. Countries such as Korea and Iran showed notable increases in research activity in recent years, suggesting the emergence of new contributors to this field. European countries, including Italy, Spain, and the United Kingdom, also significantly contributed to international collaborations and knowledge exchange, forming important links within the global research network. Overall, the country-level collaboration patterns suggest that research on tea and diabetes is shaped not only by dominant research centres but also by a broader network of emerging and collaborating countries, reflecting the increasingly globalized nature of scientific research in this field.

Among the top 10 institutions with the highest publication volume, five were based in the United States and two in China, emphasizing the dominant role of these two countries in the field. Harvard University ranked first in publication volume (n = 198, 7.52% of total output) and H-index (78), with extensive, highly cited research, reflecting its academic influence and research depth in this field. The collaboration network showed that research activities were organized around several interconnected institutional hubs rather than a single centralized structure. Major collaboration clusters were centred around institutions such as the Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Brigham and Women’s Hospital, indicating the presence of multiple influential research centres in this field. Temporal overlay patterns suggested a gradual expansion of institutional participation. Early contributions were largely driven by established institutions such as Harvard University and the University of Shizuoka. However, most recent studies involved emerging research groups, including the Sun Yat-sen University and Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences, reflecting the progressive diversification and internationalization of research efforts in tea and diabetes.

Journal impact analysis highlighted the academic influence and disciplinary characteristics of this field. IF, H-index, and JCR quartile rankings are key indicators of journal influence [37, 38]. Among the top 10 journals by publication volume, Food Chemistry had the highest IF (8.5), whereas Nutrients ranked first in publication volume and was tied for the highest H-index, highlighting its prominent role as a major publishing platform for tea and diabetes research. This distribution indicates that research on tea and diabetes is situated at the intersection of nutritional epidemiology, functional food science, and bioactive compound research, reflecting the interdisciplinary nature of this rapidly evolving field.

Research hotspots and thematic clusters

Research hotspots and knowledge structure of the tea and diabetes field were identified through keyword co-occurrence analysis. The clustering results revealed several major thematic groups, reflecting the core research directions in this field.

Cluster 1 focused on tea-derived bioactive compounds and their pharmacological activities. Representative keywords include “polyphenols,” “catechins,” “antioxidant activity,” “extracts,” and “in vitro”. Studies in this direction have primarily relied on in vitro experiments and animal models to investigate the biological activities of tea-derived compounds, particularly tea polyphenols and catechins. These studies demonstrated that tea polyphenols enhance insulin sensitivity, reduce oxidative stress, support β-cell function, reduce fat accumulation, and modulate glucose and lipid metabolism, thereby contributing to a lower risk of diabetes [39–45]. The prominence of the keywords underscores the central role of mechanistic and pharmacological investigations in this research domain.

Cluster 2 focused on tea consumption patterns and metabolic disease risk, including keywords such as “risk,” “tea consumption,” “type-2 diabetes mellitus,” “metabolic syndrome,” “obesity,” “cardiovascular disease,” and “blood pressure”. This cluster included epidemiological and clinical studies on the association between tea intake and metabolic health outcomes. Evidence from epidemiological studies consistently indicated that tea consumption was associated with reduced risk of type 2 diabetes and improved metabolic profiles [43, 46, 47]. Large-scale cohort studies and RCTs provided compelling evidence that tea and its bioactive components may protect pancreatic islets from damage, preserve β-cell insulin secretory function, and are inversely associated with the risk of developing type 2 diabetes [48–50]. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses support the potential protective role of tea-related dietary exposures in glycaemic regulation and metabolic health [51–53]. These findings underscore the importance of population-based evidence in elucidating the role of tea consumption in metabolic disease prevention.

Cluster 3 was centred on green tea and mechanism-related molecular pathways with representative keywords including “green tea,” “oxidative stress,” “insulin resistance,” “epigallocatechin gallate,” and “NF-κB”. This cluster comprised studies investigating the molecular mechanisms underlying the metabolic effects of tea polyphenols. Oxidative stress and insulin resistance are key pathogenic mechanisms in diabetes. Tea polyphenols, particularly epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), mitigate these processes through antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities. In particular, EGCG has been reported to suppress NF-κB signaling, thereby reducing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumour necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin-6 and attenuating chronic inflammation and potentially reducing diabetes risk [11, 44, 45, 54–59]. The prominence of these mechanism-related keywords underscores the central role of green tea-derived bioactive compounds in mechanistic studies of diabetes prevention and metabolic regulation.

Evolution of research themes and emerging directions

Evolution of research themes

The evolution of research themes was characterized through analyses of highly cited publications, keyword bursts, and cited reference bursts. The temporal evolution of research themes in the tea and diabetes field showed a clear progression from early mechanistic exploration to population-based evaluation, and subsequently to a more integrated focus on metabolic regulation.

In the early stage, research was predominantly centred on mechanistic investigations of tea-derived compounds, particularly their antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and insulin-sensitizing properties. This trend reflects the early dominance of mechanism-oriented research, focusing on oxidative stress, insulin signalling, and metabolic processes, with initial studies emphasizing endocrine regulation, hepatic glucose production, and insulin sensitivity in experimental models [39–41]. These studies laid the mechanistic groundwork for understanding the role of tea-derived bioactive compounds in regulating glucose homeostasis and diabetes-related metabolic dysfunction. Over time, research attention expanded beyond basic experimental studies to encompass epidemiological investigations and clinical research. During this stage, accumulating evidence from cohort studies, RCTs, and meta-analyses examined the association between habitual tea consumption and diabetes risk, increasingly positioning tea as a modifiable dietary factor in diabetes prevention and public health [46, 51, 52, 60].

Recently, research has shifted toward a more integrative and system-level understanding of metabolic regulation in diabetes. The emergence of keywords such as “gut microbiota,” “inflammation,” “apoptosis,” and “tea polyphenols” indicates that current studies focus on the coordinated interactions among oxidative stress, inflammatory signalling, gut microbial ecology, and host metabolic homeostasis [11, 56, 61, 62]. Tea polyphenols modulate multiple components of the metabolic syndrome, including body weight regulation, lipid metabolism, and insulin sensitivity [52, 55, 60, 63]. Studies indicate that tea polyphenols activate the adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK) pathway, thereby inhibiting fatty acid synthesis and promoting fatty acid oxidation, reducing obesity and abdominal fat accumulation, and lowering the risk of diabetes [2, 55, 64, 65]. Tea polyphenols can improve lipid profiles, reduce serum triglyceride and total cholesterol levels, regulate blood glucose, and lower cardiovascular risks associated with diabetes [62, 66–71]. These findings highlight a shift toward a more integrated understanding of how tea-derived compounds regulate metabolic homeostasis in the context of diabetes.

Emerging research directions

Recent advancements in tea and diabetes research have emphasized the importance of gut microbiota-mediated metabolic regulation. Studies have shown that tea polyphenols, particularly tea polysaccharides, regulate the composition of the gut microbiota by promoting the growth of beneficial bacteria, inhibiting the excessive proliferation of pathogenic bacteria associated with metabolic disorders, and increasing the production of beneficial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids. These effects enhance insulin sensitivity and alleviate insulin resistance and other metabolic disorders, contributing to the prevention and management of diabetes [54, 62, 64, 72–76]. This emerging line of investigation has broadened the current understanding of the antidiabetic effects of tea from a compound-centred paradigm to a more integrated host–microbiome interaction perspective.

Increasing evidence indicates that oxidative stress, inflammation, and insulin signalling pathways are closely interconnected in the pathogenesis of diabetes. Oxidative stress can activate the nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) signalling pathway, promoting the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and amplifying systemic inflammation [77–80]. NF-κB signalling may interact with the PI3K/Akt pathway through metabolic regulatory mechanisms [81]. AMPK signalling activation can suppress NF-κB activity, reducing inflammation, decreasing nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase 4 (NOX4) expression and oxidative stress, and improving insulin sensitivity partly through modulation of the PI3K/Akt signalling axis [82–85]. Through these coordinated interactions among oxidative stress, inflammatory, and insulin signalling pathways, tea polyphenols help maintain metabolic homeostasis and show remarkable potential in the prevention and management of metabolic syndrome and diabetes. This integrative perspective reflects a shift from studies focusing on individual mechanisms toward a more systemic understanding of metabolic regulation in diabetes.

Increasing attention has been devoted to enhancing the bioavailability and stability of tea polyphenols. Many tea-derived bioactive compounds, particularly polyphenols and catechins, exhibit inherently low bioavailability due to poor intestinal absorption and chemical instability [86, 87]. Among them, EGCG has limited solubility and is unstable under neutral or alkaline conditions [88–90]. Studies have shown that although EGCG is stable in acidic environments, it rapidly degrades in physiological fluids at pH 7.4, leading to reduced intestinal recovery and poor systemic availability [91, 92]. Green tea catechins are unstable during digestion, with total recovery rates around 5%, and those of epigallocatechin gallate and EGCG are reduced to approximately 4.6 and 6.1%, respectively, further limiting their therapeutic potential [93]. To overcome these limitations and enhance the translational potential of tea polyphenols for diabetes prevention and management, many studies have focused on improving formulation techniques, optimizing dosage forms, and exploring combined application strategies, including nanocarrier technologies, microencapsulation, and phospholipid complexes. These approaches have shown promise in enhancing compound stability, bioavailability, and therapeutic efficacy [86, 87, 89, 94].

Future research should incorporate multi-omics approaches to better elucidate the metabolic regulatory mechanisms of tea-derived compounds. Improving the bioavailability of tea polyphenols, optimizing formulation strategies, and developing targeted intervention approaches are essential for advancing the translation of basic findings into clinical applications.

Strengths and limitations

This study provides a comprehensive overview of the global research landscape and emerging directions in tea and diabetes research. A key strength of this study is that it extends previous bibliometric analyses of tea-related research in chronic diseases such as cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and Alzheimer’s disease [23, 24, 95] by explicitly focusing on diabetes as a distinct research domain. In addition, the analysis integrates multiple levels of evidence, including experimental, observational, randomized controlled trials, and meta-analyses, providing a more comprehensive understanding of the evolving research landscape and translational development in this field. Furthermore, by analyzing global publication patterns and international collaboration networks, this study provides an updated perspective on the development and emerging trends in tea and diabetes research. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, we relied solely on the WoSCC database as our data source, which may have led to the exclusion of some publications. However, the WoSCC database is widely recognized as the best choice for bibliometric analysis and is considered a high-quality digital resource [24]. Second, we considered only English-language publications, thereby excluding research in other languages, which may affect the results’ comprehensiveness. Furthermore, citation impact lag means that high-quality studies published recently may not yet fully reflect their impact. Future research should continue to track and update the citation status of these publications.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive bibliometric evaluation of the research status, hotspots, and development trends in the field of tea and diabetes. The field has shifted from early mechanism-focused studies toward more integrative frameworks involving gut microbiota, inflammation, and metabolic regulation. A divergence between research productivity and academic impact was observed, with China leading in publication output while the United States demonstrated greater citation influence. Despite notable progress, challenges such as the limited bioavailability of tea polyphenols continue to hinder clinical translation. These findings highlight the need for future research integrating multi-omics approaches and advanced delivery strategies to better elucidate mechanisms and enhance translational potential.

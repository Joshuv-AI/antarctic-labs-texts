The genome sequence of a leaf-roller moth, Argyroploce noricana (Herrich-Schaffer, 1851) (Lepidoptera: Tortricidae)
Authors: Marko Mutanen, Niina Kiljunen, Mikko Vallinmäki, Peter Huemer, Charlotte J. Wright, Joana I. Meier, Mark L. Blaxter, Wellcome Sanger Institute Tree of Life Management, Samples and Laboratory team, Wellcome Sanger Institute Scientific Operations: Sequencing Operations, Wellcome Sanger Institute Tree of Life Core Informatics team, Tree of Life Core Informatics collective, Project Psyche Community, Marta Vila, Henrik Lantz, Jinmu Kim, Automated Benchmarking
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We present a genome assembly from a male specimen of Argyroploce noricana (leaf-roller moth; Arthropoda; Insecta; Lepidoptera; Tortricidae). The assembly contains two haplotypes with total lengths of 799.31 megabases and 800.07 megabases. Most of haplotype 1 (99.7%) and haplotype 2 (99.8%) are scaffolded into 26 chromosomal pseudomolecules, including the Z sex chromosome. Hi-C data were used to phase the assembly, and both haplotypes were assembled to chromosome level. The mitochondrial genome has also been assembled, with a length of 16.57 kilobases. This work is part of Project Psyche, a collaborative programme generating genomes for European butterflies and moths.

Background

Argyroploce noricana (Herrich-Schäffer, 1851) is an Arctic–alpine species of leaf roller moth in the family Tortricidae, originally described from the Alps. It belongs to the tribe Olethreutini within the subfamily Olethreutinae. This species-rich group of Tortricidae has undergone numerous generic-level reassignments during the last decades. Therefore, it is important to obtain genomic data for species in this group to achieve a more stable generic classification. Both morphology and DNA barcodes suggest that A. noricana is closely related to the subarctic A. aquilonana Karvonen, 1932. Both species sometimes co-occur in the Scandinavian mountains, the only region where their ranges overlap. Argyroploce noricana is known from Scandinavia, the Alps and the Carpathians ( Razowski, 2003). DNA barcode sequences across its range are uniform, comprising a single BIN (BOLD:AAD8744); North European specimens appear identical to Central European representatives ( Mutanen et al. , 2012).

This small species has a wingspan of approximately 14–16 mm. The forewings are conspicuous in their colouration; the ground colour is dark, consisting of black mixed with leaden areas. There is a broad white band across the medial section of the wings and a frequently interrupted subterminal fascia. The extent of the white regions varies considerably. The palest specimens have almost equal amounts of black and white on their forewings. The hindwings are uniformly grey.

Argyroploce noricana (Figure 1) occurs in alpine habitats. In Scandinavia, the species is present on steep calcareous slopes with Dryas octopetala, the likely food plant. The adults tend to fly around limestone rocks surrounded by Dryas. Although it appears likely that Dryas is the food plant, this remains unverified. The adults are strictly diurnal and fly particularly in sunshine, but also under cloudy conditions if the ambient temperature is adequate. The flight period is usually from late June to mid-July in Finland. It is an extremely local species with strict habitat requirements. In Finland, it is regarded as an endangered (EN) species. In the Alps, the species is also associated with Dryas and accordingly prefers sparsely vegetated, steep scree slopes and grassy slopes, exclusively on calcareous substrates. Its vertical distribution ranges from just under 2000 m to approximately 2,800 m. The biology of the species is insufficiently documented; however, A. noricana has been successfully reared in the laboratory on Dryas octopetala ( Huemer, 1988). Eggs were laid singly on the upper surface of the leaves. The larvae, which hatched after about one week, lived among spun leaves and pupated there in autumn. Adults were observed from early July to late August and are exclusively heliophilous.

We present a chromosome-level, haplotype-resolved genome sequence of Argyroploce noricana, sequenced as part of Project Psyche ( Wright et al., 2025).

Methods

Sample acquisition

The specimen used for genome sequencing was an adult male Argyroploce noricana (specimen ID OULUPSY000389, ToLID ilArgNori1; Figure 1), collected from Saana, Kilpisjärvi, Finland (latitude 69.0318, longitude 20.8467) on 2024-07-07. The specimen was collected and identified by Marko Mutanen and Niina Kiljunen (University of Oulu).

Nucleic acid extraction

Protocols for high molecular weight (HMW) DNA extraction developed at the Wellcome Sanger Institute (WSI) Tree of Life Core Laboratory are available on protocols.io ( Howard et al., 2025). The ilArgNori1 sample was weighed and triaged to determine the appropriate extraction protocol. For HMW DNA extraction, 2.000 mg of tissue from the abdomen was used. The tissue was homogenised by powermashing using a PowerMasher II tissue disruptor. HMW DNA was extracted in the WSI Scientific Operations core using the Automated MagAttract v2 protocol. DNA was sheared into an average fragment size of 12–20 kb following the Megaruptor®3 for LI PacBio protocol. Sheared DNA was purified by automated SPRI (solid-phase reversible immobilisation). The concentration of the sheared and purified DNA was assessed using a Nanodrop spectrophotometer and Qubit Fluorometer using the Qubit dsDNA High Sensitivity Assay kit. Fragment size distribution was evaluated by running the sample on the Femto Pulse system. For this sample, the final post-shearing DNA had a Qubit concentration of 13.19 ng/μL and a yield of 619.93 ng, with a fragment size of 16.4 kb. The Genomic Quality Number (GQN) was 7.5.

PacBio HiFi library preparation and sequencing

Library preparation and sequencing took place in the WSI Scientific Operations core. Library PSYCHE15422785 was prepared with the SMRTbell Prep Kit 3.0 (Pacific Biosciences) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The kit includes reagents for end repair/A-tailing, adapter ligation, post-ligation SMRTbell bead clean-up, and nuclease treatment. Size selection and clean-up were performed using diluted AMPure PB beads (Pacific Biosciences). DNA concentration was quantified with a Qubit Fluorometer v4.0 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and the Qubit 1X dsDNA HS assay kit. Final library fragment size was assessed with the Agilent Femto Pulse Automated Pulsed Field CE Instrument (Agilent Technologies) using the gDNA 55 kb BAC analysis kit.

The library was sequenced on a Revio instrument (Pacific Biosciences, California, USA). The prepared libraries selected for multiplexing were pooled based on genome size, library molarity and the intended plex level. Primers were annealed and polymerases were bound to generate circularised complexes following the manufacturer’s instructions. Complexes were purified using SMRTbell beads, diluted to the Revio loading concentration, and spiked with a Revio sequencing internal control. The pooled libraries were sequenced on a Revio 25M Plex SMRT cell in a 2-plex run. SMRT Link software (Pacific Biosciences) was used to configure and monitor the run and to perform primary and secondary data analysis.

Hi-C

Sample preparation and crosslinking

Hi-C data were generated from the ilArgNori1 head sample using the Arima-HiC v2 kit (Arima Genomics). Following the manufacturer’s instructions, tissue was fixed and DNA crosslinked using TC buffer to a final formaldehyde concentration of 2%. The tissue was homogenised using the Diagnocine Power Masher-II. Crosslinked DNA was digested with a restriction enzyme master mix, biotinylated, and ligated. Clean-up was performed with SPRISelect beads before library preparation. DNA concentration was measured with the Qubit Fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and Qubit HS Assay Kit. The biotinylation percentage was estimated using the Arima-HiC v2 QC beads.

Hi-C library preparation and sequencing

Biotinylated DNA constructs were fragmented using a Covaris E220 sonicator and size-selected to 400–600 bp using SPRISelect beads. DNA was enriched with Arima-HiC v2 kit Enrichment beads. End repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation were carried out with the NEBNext Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit (New England Biolabs), following a modified protocol where library preparation occurs while DNA remains bound to the Enrichment beads. Libraries were amplified with KAPA HiFi HotStart mix and a custom Unique Dual Index (UDI) barcode set (Integrated DNA Technologies). Depending on sample concentration and biotinylation percentage determined at the crosslinking stage, libraries were amplified with 10–16 PCR cycles. Post-PCR clean-up was performed with SPRISelect beads. Libraries were quantified using the AccuClear Ultra High Sensitivity dsDNA Standards Assay Kit (Biotium) and a FLUOstar Omega plate reader (BMG Labtech).

Prior to sequencing, libraries were normalised to 10 ng/μL. Normalised libraries were quantified again to create equimolar and/or weighted 2.8 nM pools. Pool concentrations were checked using the Agilent 4200 TapeStation (Agilent) with High Sensitivity D500 reagents before sequencing. Libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq X, generating paired-end 150-bp reads.

Genome assembly

Prior to assembly of the PacBio HiFi reads, a database of k-mer counts ( k = 31) was generated from the filtered reads using FastK. The k-mer frequency distribution was analysed with GenomeScope (version 2.1.0) ( Ranallo-Benavidez et al., 2020), providing fitted point estimates of genome size, heterozygosity and repeat content.

The HiFi reads were assembled using Hifiasm in Hi-C phasing mode ( Cheng et al., 2021, 2022), producing two haplotypes. Hi-C reads ( Rao et al. , 2014) were mapped to contigs using bwa-mem2 ( Vasimuddin et al., 2019). Contigs were further scaffolded with Hi-C data in YaHS ( Zhou et al., 2023), using the --break option for handling potential misassemblies. The scaffolded assemblies were evaluated using Gfastats ( Formenti et al. , 2022), BUSCO ( Manni et al., 2021) and MerquryFK ( Rhie et al., 2020).

The mitochondrial genome was assembled using MitoHiFi ( Uliano-Silva et al. , 2023), which runs MitoFinder ( Allio et al., 2020) and uses these annotations to select the final mitochondrial contig and to ensure the general quality of the sequence, using the mitochondrial genome of Celypha flavipalpana (NC_046051.1) as the reference.

Assembly curation

The assembly was decontaminated using the Assembly Screen for Cobionts and Contaminants ( ASCC) pipeline. The flat files and maps for curation were generated with TreeVal. Manual curation was conducted primarily in PretextView and HiGlass ( Kerpedjiev et al. , 2018). Scaffolds were visually inspected and corrected as described by Howe et al. (2021). The two haplotype assemblies were combined for curation. Manual corrections included six breaks and 58 joins. This reduced the scaffold count by 17.2%. The curation process is described at sanger-tol/curation-resources. A Hi-C contact map of the haplotype 1 assembly was generated with PretextSnapshot.

Assembly quality assessment

The MerquryFK tool ( Rhie et al., 2020), run in a Singularity container ( Kurtzer et al., 2017), was used to evaluate k-mer completeness and assembly quality for both haplotypes using the k-mer databases ( k = 31) computed prior to genome assembly. The analysis outputs included assembly QV scores and completeness statistics.

The haplotype 1 assembly was assessed using BlobToolKit ( Challis et al., 2020). PacBio reads were mapped to the haplotype 1 assembly to derive sequence coverage, and BUSCO ( Manni et al., 2021) was used to assess gene-set completeness. DIAMOND BLASTp searches of extracted BUSCO proteins and DIAMOND BLASTx searches of assembly sequences against UniProt Reference Proteomes ( Buchfink et al., 2021), followed by BLASTn searches of sequences without DIAMOND BLASTx hits against the NCBI nt database ( Altschul et al., 1990), supported taxonomic assignment. Coverage, sequence composition, taxonomic assignment and BUSCO results were integrated into a BlobDir for interactive inspection and visualisation. The snail and blob plots were rendered locally from this hosted BlobDir using BlobTk v0.8.3. The scaffold-length radial scale function for the snail plot was set to linear ( Challis & Blaxter, 2026). The blob plot used circular markers with area proportional to scaffold length ( Challis et al., 2020).

We used busco-alg-painter v0.1.0, a Python reimplementation and extension of lep_busco_painter, to paint Merian elements along chromosomes. Merian elements represent the 32 ancestral linkage groups reconstructed for Lepidoptera ( Wright et al., 2024). The BUSCO-to-Merian reference assignments were derived from lep_busco_painter. Complete single-copy and duplicated BUSCO genes from the lepidoptera_odb10 results were assigned to Merian elements. Chromosome identifiers and lengths were obtained from NCBI Datasets, and BUSCO positions were plotted on chromosomes drawn to scale.

Genome sequence report

Sequence data

PacBio sequencing of the A. noricana specimen generated 35.68 Gb (gigabases) from 3.87 million reads. We assembled the genome from these reads. GenomeScope analysis using a p = 2 model gave a k-mer-based haploid genome size estimate of 797.21 Mb, with heterozygosity of 0.67% and repeat content of 43.92%; the full-model fit was 98.16% (Figure 2). The GenomeScope estimates guided expectations for the assembly. The sequencing data provided approximately 43× coverage of the haplotype 1 assembly. Hi-C sequencing produced 140.45 Gb from 465.07 million reads, which were used to scaffold the haplotype 1 assembly. Table 1 summarises the specimen and sequencing details.

The genome was assembled into two haplotypes using Hi-C phasing. The haplotype 1 assembly was curated to chromosome level, and haplotype 2 was also scaffolded to chromosome level. The haplotype 1 assembly, which is proposed as the reference assembly for this species, has a total length of 799.31 Mb in 100 scaffolds (excluding organelle sequences), with 65 gaps, and a scaffold N50 of 28.77 Mb (Table 2). The haplotype 1 assembly has a scaffold auN of 59.5 Mb.

Assembly quality metrics

For the haplotype 1 assembly, the estimated QV is 65.1, and for the haplotype 2 assembly, 65.5. When the two haplotypes are combined, the assembly achieves an estimated QV of 65.3. The k-mer completeness is 84.20% for the haplotype 1 assembly, 84.22% for the haplotype 2 assembly, and 99.28% for the combined haplotypes (Figure 5).

Evaluation of k-mer completeness using MerquryFK.

This plot illustrates the recovery of k‐mers from the original read data in the haplotype 1 assembly, the haplotype 2 assembly and the combined haplotypes. The horizontal axis represents k‐mer multiplicity, and the vertical axis shows the number of k‐mers. The black curve represents k‐mers that appear in the reads but are not assembled. The green curve corresponds to k‐mers shared by both haplotypes, and the red and blue curves show k‐mers found only in one of the haplotypes.

BUSCO analysis using the lepidoptera_odb10 reference set ( n = 5,286) identified 98.8% of the expected gene set (single = 98.1%, duplicated = 0.7%) in the haplotype 1 assembly. For the haplotype 2 assembly, BUSCO analysis identified 98.8% of the expected gene set (single = 98.4%, duplicated = 0.4%).

This Genome Note by Mutanen et al. presents a haplotype-resolved, chromosome-level assembly of Argyroploce noricana, an Arctic–alpine leaf-roller moth listed as Endangered in Finland, generated within Project Psyche from PacBio HiFi and Hi-C/Illumina data. The assembly is of excellent quality. Contig N50 is 20.3 Mb, 99.7% of the sequence is assigned to 26 chromosomal pseudomolecules, QV exceeds 65, and BUSCO completeness is 98.8%. The resource will be valuable for resolving the unstable generic classification of Olethreutini and for comparative studies of chromosome evolution in Lepidoptera. The text is clear and the species background is informative. I have read the two previous reports and largely agree with them. Below I indicate which points I consider most important, and add several additional observations.

Comments on previous reports

I strongly support Dr Kim’s suggestion (point 1) to extract the COI barcode region from the assembled mitogenome and compare it with BIN BOLD:AAD8744 and with A. aquilonana. The specimen was collected at Saana, Kilpisjärvi, in the only region where the two species are sympatric, and identification is reported as morphological only. This check is quick and would make the identity of the reference specimen verifiable.

I also strongly support Dr Lantz’s point 1 on voucher material. Only the head (Hi-C) and part of the abdomen (HiFi) were used, so the remainder of the specimen should exist. Its repository and catalogue number should be stated in the text, and the BioSample “specimen voucher” field (currently “not provided”) should be updated.

Regarding the blob plot (Figure 7), the legend contains only two categories, “Arthropoda” and “no-hit”. The small off-cluster scaffolds therefore appear to be unassigned rather than assigned to contaminant taxa. A brief statement in the caption clarifying this, and confirming that ASCC flagged no contaminants, would address both reviewers’ comments. The isolated point at roughly 18% GC with very high coverage is presumably the mitochondrial genome. Identifying it as such would also explain the discrepancy between 101 scaffolds in Figure 6 and 100 in Table 2 (Dr Kim, point 5).

On scale (Dr Kim, point 2), the voucher photograph appears to be taken on millimetre graph paper. Stating this in the caption, together with the wingspan already given in the Background, would suffice.

I confirm that I have read this submission and believe that I have an appropriate level of expertise to confirm that it is of an acceptable scientific standard.

This manuscript presents the genome sequence of the leaf-roller moth, Argyroploce noricana, assembled as a part of project Psyche. The species has a northern distribution in Europe and is originally described from the alps. The specimen here used was collected in Finland and is considered endangered there. The species is closely associated with Dryas octopetala, a plant species common in for example Scandinavia, associated with calcareous high altitude ground.

The text is in general well written, and I appreciate the background of the species given. The assembled genome is of very high quality and can most definitely contribute to the study of the debated classification of the genus. All assembly metrics easily meet the Earth Biogenome Project criteria, with very high numbers in kmer completeness, BUSCO, and amount of assembly assigned to chromosomes.

Methods follow a high international standard but the description of them lack details, which leads to some problems regarding reproducibility (see below).

I have some comments, with more major issues at the top.

1. It is unclear what has been done to preserve a voucher specimen. Figure 1, named "Voucher photograph" presents a photo of the specimen used in the study. Does this mean that the photo constitutes the voucher? Only part of the abdomen of the specimen was used, which means there should be material left to preserve as a voucher, which would in turn facilite later affirmation of the identity of the specimen. Looking in ENA Biosample, Specimen Voucher is given as "not provided", and this is troublesome. Especially for reference genomes, which might serve as the single example of a full genomic sequence of this species for quite some time, material (or in worst case a photo) must be preserved. It might very well be that there is voucher material preserved in a Natural History Museum or that the photo constitutes the voucher, but this should then clearly be spelled out.

2. I have some comments regarding reproducibility, which is somewhat lacking. Tools are usually clearly described, including version of tool used, but very little is written regarding settings used for the tools. Settings can have a big influence on the results. Databases used (UniProt and NCBI nt) are also not described with version or date. It is also unclear which pipelines were used. A link is provided to a page with ToL-pipelines, and one can guess that the genome assembly pipeline has been used, but this is not stated. As the text is written now, it is not possible to reproduce the results of the study. A link to a GitHib repo with scripts used would solve these issues.

3. I would prefer to see figures 3 (HiC contact map) and 7 (BlobToolKit blob plot) somewhat better described. The contact map shows connections between some scaffolds, most likely just shared repeats, but it would be good to have that described. The blob plot shows some contaminations remaining. It would be helpful to have that mentioned and why it might or might not be a problem for downstream analyses.

To summarize, the here presented genome constitutes a very valuable and high quality resource. If some more information could be given regarding voucher material and tools used, the manuscript would be greatly improved.

Article assessment

This Genome Note by Mutanen M. et al. reports a haplotype-resolved, chromosome-level genome assembly of a male Argyroploce noricana, an Arctic–alpine leaf-roller moth that is listed as endangered in Finland, generated from HiFi and Hi-C data. Overall, the findings provide a high-quality genomics resource. However, a few minor comments should be addressed before final indexing.

Detail comments

1. The background states that A. noricana and the closely related A. aquilonana co-occur in the Scandinavian mountains, which is the only region where their ranges overlap, and the sequenced specimen was collected in exactly this region (Saana, Kilpisjärvi). Identification is reported as morphological only, and since the mtDNA has been assembled, the authors should extract the COI barcode region and report its similarity to BIN (BOLD: AAD8744) and to A. aquilonana barcodes. This additional analysis would make the identification of the reference specimen verifiable.

2. Figure 1: What is the size of the specimen? Include a scale bar or indicate the length (mm, cm, etc.).

3. Figure 3: The last 3 chromosomes are labeled only with dots on the top axis, so please label them. The uncertain regions on the chr 9 should be marked on the contact map with asterisks or other symbols and described in the caption, with a corresponding footnote in Table 3.

4. Figure 5: The legend lists a red “hap1-only” curve; however, it is not visible because it is completely overlapped by the blue “hap2-only” curve. Use transparency or different line types or state in the caption that the two curves overlap.

5. Figure 6: The snail plot reports 101 scaffolds, whereas Table 2 reports 100 scaffolds after excluding organelle sequences. The caption should clarify that the snail plot includes mitochondrial contigs.

6. Figure 7: Several small unplaced scaffolds indicate coverage of 100-1000X and GC of 45-48%, outside the autosomal cluster. Briefly state what these represent (for example, rDNA, satellite, or repeats) and confirm that ASCC did not identify any contaminant sequences.

7. Table 5: Explain why three different MultiQC versions were used.

Paired kernels and truncated Toeplitz operators
M. Cristina Câmara, Jonathan R. Partington

Abstract
. This paper considers paired operators in the context of the Lebesgue Hilbert space L 2 L^{2} on the unit circle and its subspace, the Hardy space H 2 H^{2} . The kernels of such operators, together with their analytic projections, which are generalizations of Toeplitz kernels, are studied. Inclusion relations between such kernels are considered in detail, and the results are applied to describing the kernels of finite-rank asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators.

Primary 30H10; Secondary 47B35, 47B38

1.
Introduction

If
X
is a Banach space,
P\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is a projection,
Q=I-P

is its complementary projection, and
A
and
B
are bounded operators on
X
, then
we use the term
paired operators
for operators of the form

S_{A,B}=AP+BQ.

Paired operators appeared in the context of singular integral operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of
the form

(1.1)

a(t)f(t)+b(t)\frac{1}{\pi i}{\rm PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(y)}{y-t}\,dy=g(t),

where
a
and
b
are bounded functions and
g
is given.
They were introduced by Shinbrot
[
25
]
, where (
1.1
) was
placed in an abstract Hilbert space setting and a general method of
obtaining solutions to that equation was derived in that abstract form.

Here we shall take
X=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, where
\mathbb{T}
is the unit circle,
P
and
Q

are the orthogonal projections from
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
onto the Hardy space
H^{2}_{+}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

and its orthogonal complement
H^{2}_{-}:=\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
, respectively (we write
P=P^{+}

and
Q=P^{-}
), and
A,B
are multiplication operators
A=M_{a}
and
B=M_{b}
with
a,b\in L^{\infty}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. Thus we write

(1.2)

S_{a,b}=aP^{+}+bP^{-}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Sigma_{a,b}=P^{+}aI+P^{-}bI.

In each case the pair
(a,b)
is called the
symbol pair
. These operators have been
studied by several mathematicians
[
5
,
11
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
24
,
26
]
,
often in the
context of singular integral operators. If the operators
A
and
B
are invertible in
\mathcal{L}(X)
, then
the paired operators are said to be of
normal type

[
22
]
, and this is the most studied case by far.

Recently, several new results were obtained for operators of the form
(
1.2
) with
a,b\in L^{\infty}
satisfying weaker conditions
[
5
,
11
]
:

(1.3)

a\neq 0\hbox{ a.e.\ on }\mathbb{T},\quad b\neq 0\hbox{ a.e.\ on }\mathbb{T}

and

(1.4)

\hbox{either}\quad a=b\quad\hbox{or}\quad a-b\neq 0\hbox{ a.e.\ on }\mathbb{T},

which will be standing assumptions in this paper. If
a\neq b
we say that
(a,b)
is
nondegenerate
.

Here we focus on paired operators of the form
S_{a,b}
and
their kernels. Paired operators are
closely connected with Toeplitz operators. Indeed, on the one hand they are dilations of
Toeplitz operators; on the other hand, if
b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
(that is, if
b
is invertible in
L^{\infty}
), then
S_{a,b}
is equivalent after extension
to the Toeplitz operator
T_{a/b}

[
3
]
, implying that those two types of operator share many properties such as invertibility and
Fredholmness, and their kernels are isomorphic.
(See, for instance,
[
1
]
for the definition and properties of operators that are equivalent after extension.)

Paired kernels, i.e., kernels of paired operators, are thus particularly connected with Toeplitz kernels, i.e., kernels of bounded Toeplitz operators. If
b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
, not only do we have

(1.5)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\cong\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}

but in fact

(1.6)

P^{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}.

The spaces of the form
P^{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
, denoted by
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
, are called

projected paired kernels
. They appear in different forms: in the study of scalar-type kernels of block Toeplitz operators
[
10
]
, in the study of nearly invariant subspaces for shift semigroups
[
21
]
, in the characterization of the ranges of finite rank truncated Toeplitz operators (TTO)

[
4
]
, and so on. In Section
2
we review some of the basic properties
of such projected paired kernels, and describe their properties related to
near invariance, a property that Toeplitz kernels are known to possess.
From this point of view it
is natural to ask whether further known properties of Toeplitz kernels
can be extended, or have analogues, for paired kernels.
In Section
3
we focus on the relations between Toeplitz kernels
corresponding to Toeplitz operators whose symbols are related by a multiplication operator,
such as the known relation

(1.7)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\theta g}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\quad\hbox{if }\theta\hbox{ is an inner function},

where the inclusion is strict if
\theta
is not a constant
[
6
,
7
]
; moreover,

(1.8)

h_{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h_{+}g}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\quad\hbox{if }h_{+}\in H^{\infty},

where the inclusion is an equality if
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
; also

(1.9)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{h_{-}g}\supseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\quad\hbox{if }h_{-}\in\overline{H^{\infty}},

where the inclusion is an equality if
\overline{h_{-}}
is outer.

The relations above can be roughly seen as meaning that, when we multiply the symbol of a Toeplitz operator by a given function, the kernel may become “smaller” or “larger”
– which cannot be expressed otherwise unless we are dealing with finite-dimensional kernels.

We wish to study and express the relations between paired kernels, corresponding to symbol
pairs related by multiplication operators, similarly.
It is clear that

(1.10)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{ah,bh}\quad\hbox{ for all }h\in L^{\infty}\hbox{ with }h\neq 0\hbox{ a.e.},

but inclusion relations analogous to (
1.7
)–(
1.9
), which in
the finite-dimensional case express the fact that the dimension of one kernel is smaller
than that of the other, are impossible to obtain because the
only paired kernels contained in
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
are
\{0\}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
itself

[
5
]
.

It can be shown, however, that under our standing assumptions (
1.3
) and (
1.4
)
the projection
P^{+}
maps
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
bijectively onto

P^{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}={\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
,
indeed
P^{+}\phi=\dfrac{b}{b-a}\phi
, given that (
1.4
) holds
(see
[
11
, Prop. 2.1]
for further details).

Thus we
study here the relations between the projected paired kernels with symbol pairs related by multiplication operators,
for which one can obtain inclusions and equalities analogous to (
1.7
)–(
1.9
). These relations have also been sudied in
[
11
]
, but we present them here in Section
3
for completeness, on the one hand, and also because several results are
generalized. In Section
4
we apply them to study and
describe the kernels of a class of asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators (ATTO)
of the form

(1.11)

A^{\theta,\alpha}_{\phi}:K_{\theta}\to K_{\alpha},\qquad A^{\theta,\alpha}_{\phi}=P_{\alpha}\phi P_{\theta}{}_{|K_{\theta}},

where
\theta,\alpha
are inner functions,
K_{\theta},K_{\alpha}
the associated model spaces,
and
P_{\theta},P_{\alpha}
the orthogonal projections from
L^{2}
onto these model spaces
[
8
]
.
In addition we take
\phi\in L^{\infty}
with

(1.12)

\phi=\overline{\theta}\frac{A_{1-}}{B_{1-}}-\alpha\frac{A_{2+}}{B_{2_{+}}},\qquad A_{1-},B_{1-}\in\overline{H^{\infty}},\quad A_{2+},B_{2+}\in H^{\infty},

such that
(A_{2+},B_{2+})\in{\rm CP}^{+}
and
(A_{1-},B_{1-})\in{\rm CP}^{-}

(corona pairs, defined in Section
4
),
which are motivated by the study of ATTO of finite rank.
This is done by resorting to a characterization of certain kernels of block
Toeplitz operators that are called scalar-type kernels
[
10
, Thm. 3.1]

and using the results of the previous section: this involves establishing certain
relations between the corona theorem and Hankel operators.
It is shown, in particular, that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A^{\theta,\alpha}_{\phi}

does not depend on
\alpha
nor on the numerators
A_{1-}
and
A_{2+}

if
\dim K_{\alpha}
is siufficiently large, which may be seen as rather a surprising result.

2.
Projected paired kernels and near invariance

In this section we summarize some of the basic properties of projected paired kernels.
A more detailed treatment is given in
[
11
]
.
We remind the reader of our standing assumptions (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

The projected paired kernel
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}=P^{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\subset H^{2}_{+}
was introduced in Section
1
, and analogously we define
{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}=P^{-}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\subset H^{2}_{-}
.
Note that
\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}
if and only if for some
\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}

we have
a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
; that is,
\overline{a}\overline{z}\overline{\phi_{+}}+\overline{b}\overline{z}\overline{\phi_{-}}=0
,
and so we see that

(2.1)

{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}=\overline{z}\overline{{\rm ker}^{+}S_{\overline{b},\overline{a}}}.

We may rewrite this by saying that the antilinear mapping

(2.2)

T:{\rm ker}^{+}S_{\overline{b},\overline{a}}\to{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b},\qquad\psi_{+}\mapsto\overline{z}\overline{\psi_{+}}

is a bijection.

There is likewise a bijection

(2.3)

M_{-a/b}:{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\to{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b},\qquad\phi_{+}\mapsto-\frac{a}{b}\phi_{+}

(this is well-defined under the standing assumption (
1.3
)).
In general we omit the index
a/b
and write simply
M=M_{-a/b}
. So for
\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
we have

(2.4)

\phi_{-}=M\phi_{+}\iff\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}\quad\hbox{and}\quad a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0.

Although
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
is naturally a closed subspace of
L^{2}
, its projections

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
and
{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}
need not be. Further details are given in
[
11
]
.

We now proceed to a discussion of near invariance.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a subspace of
H^{2}_{+}
. Then we say that
\mathcal{M}
is
nearly invariant

or
nearly
S^{*}
-invariant
if and only if

(2.5)

f\in\mathcal{M},\ f(0)=0\implies S^{*}f\in\mathcal{M}.

We see that (
2.5
) is equivalent to the condition

f\in\mathcal{M},\quad\overline{z}f\in H^{2}_{+}\implies\overline{z}f\in\mathcal{M}.

It is well known that kernels of Toeplitz operators are nearly invariant, since
if
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
, so that

g\phi=\psi_{-}
for some
\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
, and
\overline{z}\phi\in H^{2}_{+}
we have

g\overline{z}\phi=\overline{z}\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
and so
\overline{z}\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
.

Similarly
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
, is nearly invariant, since if

\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
then there is a
\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
such that

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
. Now if
\overline{z}\phi_{+}
in
H^{2}_{+}
, then the identity

a(\overline{z}\phi_{+})+b\overline{z}\phi_{-}=0
shows that
\overline{z}\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
,
since
\overline{z}\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
.

Now, although
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
need not be a closed subspace
(see Example
3.10
below), it is known
that the closure of a nearly invariant subspace is still nearly invariant

[
11
, Prop. 4.4]
. Closed nearly invariant spaces have been considered by
many mathematicians,
beginning with the work of Hitt
[
20
]
and Hayashi
[
19
]
.

Let us now broaden the discussion by letting
\theta\in H^{\infty}
be a
non-constant inner function.

Definition 2.2
.

A subspace
\mathcal{M}\subset H^{2}_{+}
is
nearly
\overline{\theta}
-invariant

if
f\in\mathcal{M}
,
\overline{\theta}f\in H^{2}_{+}
imply that

\overline{\theta}f\in\mathcal{M}
.

An argument similar to that above, replacing
\overline{z}
by
\overline{\theta}

shows that projected paired kernels
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
are nearly
\overline{\theta}
-invariant.

Note that
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\cap\theta H^{2}_{+}
if and only if we can write

\phi_{+}=\theta\psi_{+}
with
\psi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
and
a\theta\psi_{+}+b\psi_{-}=0
for
some
\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
. Thus we conclude that

(2.6)

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\cap\theta H^{2}_{+}=\theta\,{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta,b}.

Equivalently, we may write this as

(2.7)

\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b},\quad\overline{\theta}\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}\iff\phi_{+}\in\theta\,{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta,b},

and, thus, saying that
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
is nearly
\overline{\theta}
-invariant is equivalent to saying that

\theta\,{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta,b}\subset{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}.

By similar arguments we have also

(2.8)

{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}\cap\overline{\theta}H^{2}_{-}=\overline{\theta}\,{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,\overline{\theta}b},

and so

(2.9)

\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b},\quad\theta\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}\iff\phi_{-}\in\overline{\theta}\,{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,\overline{\theta}b}.

In particular,

(2.10)

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\cap zH^{2}_{+}=z{\rm ker}^{+}S_{az,b},

consisting of the elements of
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
that vanish at
0
; analogously,
\overline{z}{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,\overline{z}b}
consists of the functions
\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}
such that
z\phi_{-}
vanishes at
\infty
.

Also note that, for
\phi_{-}=M\phi_{+}
(i.e., such that
a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
as in
(
2.3
)), we have

(2.11)

\phi_{+}\in\theta\,{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta,b}\iff\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,\overline{\theta}b}

and

(2.12)

\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta,b}\iff\phi_{-}\in\overline{\theta}\,{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,\overline{\theta}b}.

3.
Inclusion relations

Let us now study the relations between two paired kernels with
symbol pairs that are related by multiplication operators, by means of certain inclusion
relations between their projections into
H^{2}_{\pm}
. Although some of these relations were obtained in
[
11
]
we present here all proofs for completeness, which also allows us in some cases to present alternative proofs. We again remind the reader of our standing assumptions (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
be inner functions. Then

(3.1)

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta_{1},b\overline{\theta}_{2}}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b},

and, for
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
, the inclusion is proper if and only if

\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\not\in\mathbb{C}
; that is,

\theta_{1}
or

\theta_{2}
is non-constant.

Proof.

Let
\theta=\theta_{1}\theta_{2}
; then
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta_{1},b\overline{\theta_{2}}}={\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta,b}
. Let
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta,b}
, so that

a\theta\phi_{+}+b\psi_{-}=0
for some
\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
. Then we have

a\phi_{+}+b(\overline{\theta}\psi_{-})=0
and so
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
.

If
\theta\in\mathbb{C}
, then the inclusion (
3.1
) is an equality.
Otherwise, if
\theta\not\in\mathbb{C}
, then there exists
\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}
such that
\phi_{-}\not\in\overline{\theta}H^{2}_{-}
, by near invariance: see Propositions 4.1 and 4.5 in
[
11
]
.

Since
\phi_{-}\in{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}
but
\phi_{-}\not\in\overline{\theta}{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,\overline{\theta}b}
by
(
2.8
), for
\phi_{+}=M^{-1}\phi_{-}
we must have that

\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
but
\phi_{+}\not\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a\theta,b}
, by (
2.12
),
so the inclusion is strict.
∎

By (
2.1
) we have the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
be inner functions. Then

(3.2)

{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a\theta_{1},b\overline{\theta}_{2}}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b},

and, for
{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
, the inclusion is strict if and only if
\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\not\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
h_{-}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
. Then we have:

(i)
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,bh_{-}}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}S_{ah_{-},b}
;

(ii) If
\overline{h_{-}}
is outer then

(3.3)

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,bh_{-}}={\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\quad\hbox{if}\quad\frac{a}{bh_{-}}\in L^{\infty},

and

(3.4)

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{ah_{-},b}={\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}\quad\hbox{if}\quad a/b\in L^{\infty};

(iii) If
\overline{h}_{-}
is not outer, then the inclusions in (i) are strict.

Proof.

The inclusions in (i) are immediate consequences of the definition of a projected paired
kernel. Regarding (ii), we have that if

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
with
\phi_{\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}
, then

a\phi_{+}+bh_{-}(h_{-}^{-1}\phi_{-})=0\quad\hbox{with}\quad h_{-}^{-1}\phi_{-}=-\frac{a}{bh_{-}}\phi_{+}\in L^{2}\cap\overline{z}\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}},

where
\mathcal{N}_{+}
denotes the Smirnov class (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
), if
\dfrac{a}{bh_{-}}\in L^{\infty}
.
So
h_{-}^{-1}\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
, and therefore
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,bh_{-}}
.

This, together with the first inclusion in (i), proves the first equality in (ii), and the second equality is proved analogously.

As for (iii), if
\overline{h_{-}}
has a non-constant inner factor
\theta
, then it follows from Prop.
3.1
and from (i) that, if
h_{-}=\overline{\theta}\tilde{h}_{-}
with
\tilde{h}_{-}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
, then

{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b\overline{\theta}\tilde{h}_{-}}\subsetneq{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b\tilde{h}_{-}}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b},

and analogously for the second inclusion.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that the inclusions in Prop.
3.3
(i)
may be strict even if
\overline{h_{-}}
is outer: see Example
3.10
below.
Also note that from (
3.4
) we have that, if
g\in L^{\infty}
,
\overline{h_{-}}
is outer
and
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, then

(3.5)

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{gh_{-},h_{+}}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{gh_{+}^{-1}}=h_{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
. Then

(i) if
a/b\in L^{\infty}
, then
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a(z-z_{0}),b}={\rm ker}^{+}S_{az,b}
;

(ii) if
\dfrac{a}{b(z-z_{0})}\in L^{\infty}
, then
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b(z-z_{0})}={\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,bz}
.

Proof.

Take
h_{-}=(z-z_{0})/z\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
, which is such that
\overline{h_{-}}

is outer in
H^{\infty}
. Then (i) and (ii) follow from the second equality and first equality in
Prop.
3.3
(ii), respectively.
∎

Note that

(3.6)

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b},\quad\hbox{if}\quad a/b\in L^{\infty},

so we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g(z-z_{0})}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{gz}\quad\hbox{for}\quad z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\quad\hbox{and}\quad g\in L^{\infty}.

Taking (
2.1
) into account, we also have:

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
h_{+}\in H^{\infty}
; then

{\rm ker}^{-}S_{ah_{+},b}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,bh_{+}}.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
h_{+}\in H^{\infty}
. Then we have:

(i)
h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}S_{ah_{+},b}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
and

h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,bh_{+}}
;

(ii) if
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, or if
h_{+}
is outer with
\dfrac{b}{ah_{+}}\in L^{\infty}
, then

h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}S_{ah_{+},b}={\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b};

(iii)
if
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, or if
h_{+}
is outer with
\dfrac{b}{a}\in L^{\infty}
, then

h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}={\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,bh_{+}};

(iv) if
h_{+}
is not outer, then the inclusions in (i) are strict.

Proof.

(i) The first inclusion is obvious, since for
\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}

(ah_{+})\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0\iff a(h_{+}\phi_{+})+b\phi_{-}=0.

Regarding the second inclusion, if
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
then

a(h_{+}\phi_{+})+(bh_{+})\phi_{-}=0
, so
h_{+}\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,bh_{+}}
.

(ii) It is left to show that
{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}\subseteq h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}S_{ah_{+},b}
. We have that

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0\iff(ah_{+})\frac{\phi_{+}}{h_{+}}+b\phi_{-}=0\iff\frac{\phi_{+}}{h_{+}}=-\frac{b}{ah_{+}}\phi_{-}.

If
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, then
{\phi_{+}}/{h_{+}}\in H^{2}_{+}
; if
h_{+}
is outer and

\dfrac{b}{ah_{+}}\in L^{\infty}
, then

\phi_{+}/h_{+}\in L^{2}\cap\mathcal{N}_{+}
and so
\phi_{+}/h_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
.
It follows that
\phi_{+}/h_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{ah_{+},b}
.

(iii) Now it remains to show that

{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,bh_{+}}\subseteq h_{+}{\rm ker}^{+}S_{a,b}
.
We have

a\phi_{+}+(bh_{+})\phi_{-}=0\iff a\phi_{+}/h_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0.

If
h_{+}\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}
, then
\phi_{+}/h_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
; if
h_{+}
is outer and

b/a\in L^{\infty}
then

\phi_{+}/h_{+}\in L^{2}\cap\mathcal{N}_{+}=H^{2}_{+}

Remark 3.8
.

Note that the inclusion in (i) above may be strict even if

Recalling

.

Let

h_{-}{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,bh_{-}}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}\quad\hbox{and}\quad h_{-}{\rm ker}^{-}S_{a,b}\subseteq{\rm ker}^{-}S_{ah_{-},b}.

Example 3.10

Let

.

Note that any space

Finally, note that for a projected paired kernel of the form (

, so the question whether

reduces to the question whether
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\alpha\overline{\theta}}=\{0\}
.
This, in turn, is related to the question of existence of a multiplier from
K_{\theta}
into

,
studied in
[

,
9
]
.
The answer to our question, in the case when one of the inner functions is
a finite Blaschke product, was given in
[
6
, Sec. 6]
,
but the situation is more complicated when neither inner function is a finite
Blaschke product.

So suppose that
\theta
and
\alpha
are non-constant
inner functions in
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
with no nontrivial common factors. The
question is: when is
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\alpha\overline{\theta}}
different from
\{0\}
?
By Coburn’s lemma at least one of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\alpha\overline{\theta}}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\theta\overline{\alpha}}

will be zero.

Recall that the spectrum of an inner function
\sigma(\theta)
consists of
all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\liminf_{z\to\lambda}|\theta(z)|=0
,
and it is the union of the closure of its zero set in
\mathbb{D}
and the support of its singular measure
(if any).
Useful facts from
[
15
, Sec. 7.3]
are:

•

if
\sigma(\theta)\cap\mathbb{T}\neq\mathbb{T}
, then
\theta
has an
analytic continuation across
\mathbb{T}\setminus\sigma(\theta)
;

•

\theta
does not have an analytic continuation across any
point of
\sigma(\theta)\cap\mathbb{T}
;

•

every function in
K_{\theta}
has an analytic continuation across

\mathbb{T}\setminus\sigma(\theta)
.

Theorem 3.16
.

If
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\alpha\overline{\theta}}\neq\{0\}
we have

\sigma(\alpha)\cap\mathbb{T}\subset\sigma(\theta)\cap\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

We may suppose that

\sigma(\theta)\cap\mathbb{T}\neq\mathbb{T}
, that

f_{+}\neq 0
and
f_{+}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\alpha\overline{\theta}}
.
That is,

\alpha\overline{\theta}f_{+}=f_{-}
for some
f_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
.
Then
f_{+}
and
\alpha f_{+}
both lie in
K_{\theta}
, and have
analytic continuations across
\mathbb{T}\setminus\sigma(\theta)
.
Hence, by division,
\alpha
has a meromorphic continuation, but since it is inner,
this must be an analytic continuation (no poles), since it has to be unimodular a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
.
∎

Note that if
\theta=\alpha\beta
with
\beta
inner, non-constant, then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{\theta}\alpha}=K_{\beta}\neq\{0\}
, and
we do indeed have

\sigma(\alpha)\subset\sigma(\theta)
. Moreover, the example given in
[
6
, Ex. 6.3]
corresponds to
\theta
and
\alpha
being
singular inner functions with point masses at different points, so we recover
the result that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{\theta}\alpha}=\{0\}
in this case.
This leaves open many other examples, e.g., what happens if
\theta
and
\alpha

are Blaschke products with disjoint zero sets that both converge to
1
?

4.
Kernels of ATTO

We defined asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operators (ATTO) in (
1.11
) and now consider the question of describing the kernel of such an operator when the symbol has the form

(4.1)

\phi=\overline{\theta}\frac{A_{1-}}{B_{1-}}-\alpha\frac{A_{2+}}{B_{2+}}\in L^{\infty},

with
(A_{2+},B_{2+})\in{\rm CP}^{+}
and
(A_{1-},B_{1-})\in{\rm CP}^{-}
. Here

{\rm CP}^{+}
denotes the set of all
corona pairs

(f_{1+},f_{2+})
with

f_{1+},f_{2+}\in H^{\infty}
such that there exist
\tilde{f}_{1+},\tilde{f}_{2+}\in H^{\infty}

satisfying

(4.2)

f_{1+}\tilde{f}_{1+}+f_{2+}\tilde{f}_{2+}=1.

By the well-known corona theorem
[
12
]
, there exist
\tilde{f}_{1+}
and
\tilde{f}_{2+}
such that
(
4.2
) holds if and only if

(4.3)

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f_{1+}(z)|+|f_{2+}(z)|>0.

{\rm CP}^{-}
is defined analogously, with
H^{\infty}
replaced by
\overline{H^{\infty}}
.

This class of ATTO is motivated by the study of finite-rank ATTO, which will be considered as
a particular case where
A_{1-},B_{1-}
and
A_{2+},B_{2+}
are polynomials (see

[
8
,
11
]
. The starting point of this study is a result from
[
10
]
, which is formulated here as follows, denoting by
\mathcal{F}
the set of all complex-valued measurable functions defined almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
10
, Cor. 3.4]

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2})
and
g=(g_{1},g_{2})
belong to
\mathcal{F}^{2}
, with left inverses
\tilde{f}^{T}
and
\tilde{g}^{T}
, respectively, where

\tilde{f}=(\tilde{f}_{1},\tilde{f}_{2})
and
\tilde{g}=(\tilde{g}_{1},\tilde{g}_{2})
belong to
\mathcal{F}^{2}
; i.e.,

(4.4)

\tilde{f}^{T}f=\tilde{g}^{T}g=1.

Suppose also that, for a given
G\in(L^{\infty})^{2\times 2}
we have that

(4.5)

Gf=g.

Let moreover

(4.6)

\mathcal{S}=(\det G.f^{T}(H^{2}_{+})^{2})\cap(g^{T}(H^{2}_{-})^{2}),

and

(4.7)

\mathcal{K}=(\tilde{f}^{T}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{f\tilde{f}^{T}})\cap(\tilde{g}^{T}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{H}_{g\tilde{g}^{T}}),

where

(4.8)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{f\tilde{f}^{T}}=\{\phi_{+}\in(H^{2}_{+})^{2}:P^{-}f\tilde{f}^{T}\phi_{+}=0\}

and

(4.9)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{H}_{g\tilde{g}^{T}}=\{\phi_{-}\in(H^{2}_{-})^{2}:P^{+}g\tilde{g}^{T}\phi_{-}=0\}.

Then, if
\mathcal{S}=\{0\}
we have that

(4.10)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}=\mathcal{K}f.

Remark 4.2
.

Given that
f
is a fixed vector function and
\mathcal{K}
is a space of scalar functions we
say that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}
is of

if it satisfies an equation of the form
(

There are many examples of scalar-type kernels for block Toeplitz operators. We have the following
analogue of Coburn’s lemma for Toeplitz operators with
2\times 2
symbols.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
10
]

If
G\in(L^{\infty})^{2\times 2}
and
\det G\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
then either

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}=\{0\}
or
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}^{*}=\{0\}
, or
both kernels are of scalar type.

Applying Theorem

to the study of kernels of ATTO will be based on the following result.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
8
]
. Let
\theta,\alpha

\phi&\alpha\end{bmatrix}.

Denoting by

(4.12)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A^{\theta,\alpha}_{\phi}=P_{1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}.

Proposition 4.6

Let

(4.19)

Proof.

At this point it will be helpful to observe that

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
A,B\in L^{\infty}
. Then
AH^{2}_{+}\cap BH^{2}_{-}=A\,{\rm ker}^{+}S_{A,B}
.

Proof.

If
A\phi_{+}=B\phi_{-}
, with
\phi_{\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}
, then

A\phi_{+}-B\phi_{-}=0
and
\phi_{+}\in{\rm ker}^{+}S_{A,B}
, so
AH^{2}_{+}\cap BH^{2}_{-}\subseteq\,A\,{\rm ker}^{+}S_{A,B}
.

Conversely, it is clear that
A\,{\rm ker}^{+}S_{A,B}\subseteq AH^{2}_{+}
, and from

A\phi_{+}-B\phi_{-}=0

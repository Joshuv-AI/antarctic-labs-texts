On an Euler-Schrödinger system appearing in laser-plasma interaction
K. Bhandari and B. Ducomet and Š. Nečasová and J. S. H. Simon

Abstract
. We consider the Cauchy problem for the barotropic Euler system coupled to a vector Schrödinger equation in the whole space. Assuming that the initial density and vector potential are small enough, and that the initial velocity is close to some reference vector field u 0 u_{0} such that the spectrum of D ​ u 0 Du_{0} is bounded away from zero, we prove the existence of a global-in-time unique solution with (fractional) Sobolev regularity. Moreover, we obtain some algebraic time decay estimates of the solution. Our work extends the papers by D. Serre and M. Grassin [ 13 , 11 , 19 ] and previous works by B. Ducomet and co-authors [ 4 , 8 ] dedicated to the compressible Euler-Poisson system.



Key words.
Compressible
Euler-Schrödinger system, global solution, decay estimate.

Mathematics Subject Classification.
35Q31, 35J10, 76N10.

1.
Introduction

We consider a simple hydrodynamic
model appearing in the context of laser-plasma interaction, introduced by R. Sentis in
[
17
,
18
]
. The fluid system under study is the compressible Euler system coupled to a vector Schrödinger equation.
More precisely, the system reads as:

(1.1)

\partial_{t}\varrho+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho u)=0,

(1.2)

\partial_{t}(\varrho u)+{\rm div}_{x}(\varrho u\otimes u)+\nabla_{x}\Pi(\varrho)=-\gamma_{p}\varrho\nabla_{x}(A^{2}),

(1.3)

\frac{2i}{c}\partial_{t}A+\frac{1}{k_{0}}\Delta_{x}A+\zeta_{1}A-\zeta_{2}\varrho A=0,

with initial data

(1.4)

(\varrho,u,A)(x,0)=(\varrho_{0},u_{0},A_{0})(x),

where
\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
is the fluid density,
u=u(t,x)
is the fluid velocity, and
A=A(t,x)
is the (complex) vector potential representing the laser field. Moreover,

\Pi(\varrho)=K\varrho^{\gamma}
is the barotropic pressure with
K>0
and the adiabatic exponent
\gamma>1
, and
A^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{j}\overline{A_{j}}
. Finally, in the equation (
1.3
),
\zeta_{1}=k_{0}+i\eta_{0}
and

\zeta_{2}=\frac{1}{\varrho_{c}}\left(k_{0}-i\nu_{0}\right)
are two complex coefficients.

The various physical constants are as follows:

–

\gamma_{p}:=\frac{Zq_{e}^{2}}{4m_{i}m_{e}\omega_{0}^{2}}
,
where
Z>0
is the ionization level,

q_{e}
is the electronic charge,

m_{i}
is the ionic mass,
m_{e}
is the electronic mass and

\omega_{0}
is the laser pulsation;

–

k_{0}
is the wave number of the laser in the vacuum;

–

c
is the velocity of light;

–

\varrho_{c}=\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}m_{e}}{q_{e}^{2}}
, where
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is the vacuum permeability;

–

\eta_{0}>0
and
\nu_{0}=\frac{\nu_{ei}}{c}
, where
\nu_{ei}
is the electron-ion collision frequency.

The above system is a simplified model for the
evolution of a plasma interacting with an electromagnetic field, after an enveloppe approximation introduced by R. Sentis in
[
17
]
(see also
[
18
]
for a general context).

As it will be shown below, the only static solution of the system is the trivial one
(\varrho=0,u=0,A=0)
, and it is thus natural to focus on initial densities that are either compactly supported or tend to
0
at infinity.
In order to solve the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
)–(
1.3
),
the standard approach is
to use an energy method after “symmetrizing” the system.
In our context where the density may tend to
0
at infinity,
Makino’s symmetrization (first introduced in
[
15
]
) is the most appropriate one.

Problem (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) may be solved locally in time in the Sobolev space setting
by means of a suitable adaptation of the standard theory for first order
quasilinear symmetric hyperbolic systems (see
[
3
]
).
Our first aim is to prove well-posedness for system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) in a functional setting that encompasses Sobolev spaces. In this context, one can mention the works by Chemin
[
6
]
and Gamblin
[
10
]
,
based on the use of uniformly local Sobolev spaces (that
does not allow to keep track of the behavior of the solution at infinity),
and the recent work by Brauer and Karp
[
5
]
in weighted
Sobolev spaces.
Compared to that latter work, our approach is particularly elementary, as it
just requires basic Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces.

Furthermore, our functional framework turns out to be also relevant for
reaching our second aim, which is to produce global-in-time strong solutions for (
1.1
)–(
1.3
). Clearly, as no dissipative process takes place in the system,
generic data (even small) are expected to lead to only local-in-time
solutions and examples of finite time blow-up are known (see
[
6
,
7
,
16
]
).
However, in a series of papers
[
13
,
11
,
19
]
, Grassin and Serre pointed out
that, under a “dispersive” spectral condition on the initial velocity,
and a smallness assumption on the initial density, the compressible Euler system admits a unique global smooth solution. Here, after our recent works in
[
4
]
and
[
8
]

we want to adapt Grassin and Serre’s result to the Euler-Schrödinger system
within a functional framework that includes the trivial solution
(\varrho=0,u=0,A=0)

and allows to show its stability.

Paper organization.
The rest of the paper is structured as follows.
In the next section, we state our main results and give some insights on our strategy.
In Section
3
, we establish decay estimates in Sobolev spaces for our compressible Euler-Schrödinger system.
Section
4
is devoted to the proofs of the global existence
results, and then we show the uniqueness of the solution.
Some technical results like, in particular, first and second order commutator
estimates are proved in Appendix
A
.

Notations.
Throughout the paper,
C
denotes a generic constant that may vary from line.
Sometimes, we use the notation
\Phi\lesssim\Psi
to mean that

\Phi\leq C\Psi.
The notation
\Phi\approx\Psi
is used if both properties
\Phi\lesssim\Psi
and
\Psi\lesssim\Phi
hold true.

By
{\rm Sp}\,(\mathcal{A})
, we denote the spectrum of any matrix or operator
\mathcal{A}.
Finally, we shall denote by
\dot{H}^{s}
and
H^{s}
the homogeneous and nonhomogeneous Sobolev
spaces of order
s
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.
Main results

Since our functional framework will force the density to tend to
0
at infinity, the standard
symmetrization for the compressible Euler equations is no longer appropriate, we thus use the one that has been introduced by T. Makino in
[
15
]
; more precisely, we set

(2.5)

\tilde{\varrho}:=\frac{2\sqrt{K\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}},\ \ \gamma>1.

After this change of variable, the system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) rewrites

(ES)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{x})\tilde{\varrho}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\tilde{\varrho}\,{\rm div}_{x}u=0,\\
\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla_{x})u+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\tilde{\varrho}\,\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}=-\gamma_{p}\nabla_{x}A^{2},\\
\displaystyle\frac{2i}{c}\partial_{t}A+\frac{1}{k_{0}}\Delta_{x}A+\zeta A-\zeta^{\prime}\tilde{\varrho}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}A=0\cdotp\end{cases}

where
\zeta=\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{2\sqrt{K\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}\zeta_{2}.

Let us consider an auxiliary Cauchy problem for the
d
-dimensional Burgers equation:

(2.6)

\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla_{x}v=0,

with initial data

(2.7)

v(0,x)=v_{0}(x).

In the particular case of the
Euler equation (that is
\gamma_{p}=0
) and under suitable spectral conditions on
Du_{0},

it is known from
[
12
,
13
]
that (
2.6
) is a good approximation of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
provided the density of the fluid is small enough.
The formal heuristics is that if one neglects
\tilde{\varrho}
in the velocity
equation of (
ES
) (as well as if
\gamma_{p}=0
), then one readily gets
(
2.6
).

In order to state our results, we need to introduce the following function space:

E^{s}:=\bigl\{z\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d});\;Dz\in L^{\infty}\text{ and }D^{2}z\in H^{s-2}\bigr\}.

The following regularity result has been first proved in
[
12
,
13
]
in the case of

integer
exponents and extended in
[
4
]
for the
real
exponents.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
v_{0}
be in
E^{s}
with
s>1+d/2
and satisfies the following:

\bf(H0)

there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
{\rm dist}({\rm Sp}\,(Dv_{0}(x)),\mathbb{R}_{-})\geq\varepsilon
.

Then
(
2.6
)–(
2.7
) has a classical solution
v
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

D^{2}v\in{\mathcal{C}}^{j}\bigl(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s-2-j}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\bigr)\ \ \ \mbox{for}\ \ j=0,1.

Moreover,
Dv\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and we have for any
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.8)

Dv(t,x)=\frac{I_{d}}{(1+t)}+\frac{F(t,x)}{(1+t)^{2}}

for some function
F\in{\mathcal{C}}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies

(2.9)

\|F(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}\leq K_{\sigma}(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\sigma}\ \ \mbox{for all }\ 0<\sigma\leq s-1,

where
K_{\sigma}>0
is constant.

Finally, if
D^{2}v_{0}
is bounded, we have

(2.10)

{\displaystyle\|D^{2}v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{3}}}.

Let us write the main results of the present paper.

Theorem 2.1

(
Existence of global solution
)
.

Let
s>1+d/2
and
\gamma>1.
Assume that the initial data
(\varrho_{0},u_{0},A_{0})
satisfy:

\bf(H1)

there exists
v_{0}\in E^{s+1}
verifying

(
H0
)

and that
u_{0}-v_{0}
is small in
H^{s}
;

\bf(H2)

\varrho_{0}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}
is small in
H^{s};

\bf(H3)

A_{0}
is small in
H^{s}.

Denote by
v
the global solution of (
2.6
)–(
2.7
) as given in Proposition

2.1
.
Then, there exists a unique global solution
\left(\varrho,u,A\right)
to
(
1.1
)–(
1.3
), such that

\bigl(\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}},u-v,A\bigr)\in{\mathcal{C}}\bigl(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s}\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)\bigr),

provided
d,

\gamma
and
s
satisfy the following additional condition:

s>\max\{2d+3,s_{+}\},

where
s_{+}
is given by (
3.25
).

Remark 2.1
.

The fact that
\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

implies that
\varrho\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
whenever
\gamma\leq 2
.
Hence, the constructed solutions have finite mass, and one can show that it is conserved
through the evolution.

Furthermore, the following decay estimates are satisfied by the global solutions constructed in the previous theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(
Decay estimates
)
.

Let all the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
be in force. Then, for all
\sigma
in
[0,s],

the solution
(\varrho,u,A)
constructed therein satisfies

\left\|\varrho^{\frac{\gamma-1}{2}},u-v,A\right\|_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\sigma-\min\left(1,\,d(\frac{\gamma-1}{2})\right)},

where
C
depends only on the initial data, on
d,

\gamma,

and on
\sigma.

3.
Decay estimates in Sobolev spaces

The goal of the present section is to prove
a-priori decay estimates in Sobolev spaces for the discrepancy between
the solutions of (
ES
) and (
2.6
) (i.e., Theorem
2.2
) . These estimates will play a fundamental role
in the proof of our global existence result.

Let
v
be the solution of the Burgers equation given by Proposition
2.1
.
Let us set
w:=u-v
where
(\tilde{\varrho},u,A)
stands for a sufficiently smooth solution of (
ES
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then
(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)
satisfies:

(BB)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+w\cdot\nabla_{x})\tilde{\varrho}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\tilde{\varrho}\,\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}w+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\tilde{\varrho}\,\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}v=0,\\
\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+w\cdot\nabla_{x})w+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\tilde{\varrho}\,\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}w+w\cdot\nabla_{x}v=-\gamma_{p}\nabla_{x}\phi,\\
\displaystyle\frac{2i}{c}\partial_{t}A+\frac{1}{k_{0}}\Delta_{x}A+\zeta A-\zeta^{\prime}\tilde{\varrho}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}A=0,\end{cases}

where
\phi:=A^{2}
.

Our aim is to prove decay estimates in
\dot{H}^{\sigma}
for the solution of (
BB
) for all
0\leq\sigma\leq s.

Clearly, arguing by interpolation, it suffices to consider the extreme cases
\sigma=0
and
\sigma=s.

(i)
Case I:
\sigma=0
. Taking the
L^{2}

scalar product of the three equations of (
BB
) with
(\tilde{\varrho},w,\overline{A})
gives first

(3.11)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|(\tilde{\varrho},w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\tilde{\varrho}^{2}+|w|^{2})\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}v\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\tilde{\varrho}^{2}+|w|^{2})\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}w\,dx\\
+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\varrho}^{2}\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w\cdot\nabla_{x}v)\cdot w\,dx+\frac{\gamma-1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\varrho}^{2}\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}w\,dx\\
=-\gamma_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot w\,dx,

and

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}+\eta_{0}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}+\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{2\sqrt{K\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}\frac{\nu_{0}}{\varrho_{c}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\varrho}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}\phi\ dx=0.

We first observe that due to Proposition
2.1

\displaystyle{\rm div}_{x}v=\frac{d}{(1+t)}+\frac{\hbox{\rm{Tr}\,}(F(t,x))}{(1+t)^{2}}.

Then, considering

(3.13)

c_{d,\gamma}:=\min\left\{\eta_{0},{(\gamma-1)}\frac{d}{2}\right\}-\frac{d}{2},

and denoting by
M
, the
L^{\infty}
-bound of
F
, we deduce from (
3.11
) that,

(3.14)

\min\Big\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{c}\Big\}\frac{d}{dt}\|(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{c_{d,\gamma}}{1+t}\|(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\nu}_{0}}{\varrho_{c}}\|\tilde{\varrho}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}\phi\|_{L^{1}}\leq\gamma_{p}\|\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot w\|_{L^{1}}\\
+\frac{M\max\left\{1+\frac{d}{2},(\gamma-1)\frac{d}{2}\right\}}{(1+t)^{2}}\|(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\max\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\gamma-1}{4}\right\}\|\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Here and in the sequel, we denote
\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{0}=\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{2\sqrt{K\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}\nu_{0}
.

Note that, even if
\gamma_{p}=0
(i.e., standard compressible Euler equation), proving existence results for (
BB
)
requires a control on
\|\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}w\|_{L^{\infty}}.
Owing to the hyperbolicity of the system,
it seems (at least in the multi-dimensional case) difficult to go beyond the energy framework,
and it is thus natural to look for a-priori estimates in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}.

Now, owing to Sobolev embedding, the minimal requirement to get eventually a bound on

\|{\rm div}_{x}w\|_{L^{\infty}}
is that
s>1+d/2.

(ii)
Case II:
\sigma=s
. In order to prove Sobolev estimates, we introduce the homogeneous fractional derivation operator

\dot{\Lambda}^{s}
defined by
{\mathcal{F}}(\dot{\Lambda}^{s}f)(\xi):=|\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{F}}f(\xi)
and observe that

\tilde{\varrho}_{s}:=\dot{\Lambda}^{s}\tilde{\varrho},

w_{s}:=\dot{\Lambda}^{s}w
and
\phi_{s}:=\dot{\Lambda}^{s}\phi
satisfy
(with the usual summation convention over repeated indices)

(BB
s
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+w\cdot\nabla_{x})\tilde{\varrho}_{s}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\tilde{\varrho}\,{\rm div}_{x}w_{s}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}_{s}-s\partial_{j}v^{k}\dot{\Lambda}^{-2}\partial^{2}_{jk}\tilde{\varrho}_{s}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\dot{\Lambda}^{s}(\tilde{\varrho}\,{\rm div}_{x}v)\\
\hskip 284.52756pt\displaystyle=\dot{R}^{1}_{s}+\dot{R}^{2}_{s}+\dot{R}^{3}_{s},\\
\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+w\cdot\nabla_{x})w_{s}+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\tilde{\varrho}\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}_{s}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}w_{s}-s\partial_{j}v^{k}\dot{\Lambda}^{-2}\partial^{2}_{jk}w_{s}+\dot{\Lambda}^{s}(w\cdot\nabla_{x}v)\\
\hskip 241.84842pt\displaystyle=\dot{R}^{4}_{s}+\dot{R}^{5}_{s}+\dot{R}^{6}_{s}+\gamma_{p}\nabla_{x}\phi_{s},\\
\displaystyle\frac{2i}{c}\partial_{t}A_{s}+\frac{1}{k_{0}}\Delta_{x}A_{s}+\zeta A_{s}-\zeta^{\prime}\dot{\Lambda}^{s}\left(\tilde{\varrho}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}A\right)=0.\end{cases}

with

\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{R}^{1}_{s}:=[w,\dot{\Lambda}^{s}]\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho},&\dot{R}^{4}_{s}:=[w,\dot{\Lambda}^{s}]\nabla_{x}w,\\[4.30554pt]
\dot{R}^{2}_{s}:=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}[\tilde{\varrho},\dot{\Lambda}^{s}]{\rm div}_{x}w,&\dot{R}^{5}_{s}:=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}[\tilde{\varrho},\dot{\Lambda}^{s}]\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho},\\[4.30554pt]

The definition of
\dot{R}^{3}_{s}
and
\dot{R}^{6}_{s}
is motivated
by the fact that, according to the classical theory of pseudo-differential operators, we expect to have

[\dot{\Lambda}^{s},v]\cdot\nabla_{x}z=\frac{1}{i}\bigl\{|\xi|^{s},v(x)\bigr\}(D)\nabla_{x}z+\hbox{remainder}.

Computing the
Poisson bracket
in the right-hand side yields

\frac{1}{i}\bigl\{|\xi|^{s},v(x)\bigr\}(D)=-s\partial_{j}v\dot{\Lambda}^{s-2}\partial_{j}.

Now, taking advantage of
Proposition
2.1
, we get

-\partial_{j}v^{k}\dot{\Lambda}^{-2}\partial^{2}_{jk}z=\frac{1}{1+t}z-\frac{F_{kj}}{(1+t)^{2}}\dot{\Lambda}^{-2}\partial^{2}_{jk}z,

and also

\displaystyle\dot{\Lambda}^{s}(\tilde{\varrho}\,\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}v)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{1+t}\tilde{\varrho}_{s}+\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}\dot{\Lambda}^{s}(\tilde{\varrho}\,{\rm Tr}\,F)

\displaystyle\quad\hbox{and}\quad\dot{\Lambda}^{s}(w\cdot\nabla_{x}v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+t}w_{s}+\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}\dot{\Lambda}^{s}(F\cdot w).

Hence, taking the
L^{2}
inner product of (
BBs
) with
(\tilde{\varrho}_{s},w_{s},A_{s}),
and denoting

(3.15)

c_{d,\gamma,s}:=c_{d,\gamma}+s,

we discover that

(3.16)

\min\Big\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{c}\Big\}\frac{d}{dt}\|(\tilde{\varrho}_{s},w_{s},A_{s})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{c_{d,\gamma,s}}{1+t}\|(\tilde{\varrho}_{s},w_{s},A_{s})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq\gamma_{p}\|\nabla_{x}\phi_{s}\cdot w_{s}\|_{L^{1}}\!+\frac{\tilde{\nu}_{0}}{\varrho_{c}}\|\dot{\Lambda}^{s}\left(\tilde{\varrho}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}A\right)\|_{L^{2}}\|A_{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\!\frac{\|\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{2}\|(\tilde{\varrho}_{s},w_{s},A_{s})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\|\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\varrho}_{s}\|_{L^{2}}\|w_{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{sM}{(1+t)^{2}}\|(\tilde{\varrho}_{s},w_{s},A_{s})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
+\biggl[\frac{1}{(1\!+\!t)^{2}}\left(\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\|\dot{\Lambda}^{s}(\tilde{\varrho}{\rm Tr}\,F)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{\Lambda}^{s}(F\cdot w)\|_{L^{2}}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{6}\|\dot{R}_{s}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}\biggr]\|(\tilde{\varrho}_{s},w_{s},A_{s})\|_{L^{2}}.

The terms
\dot{R}_{s}^{1},

\dot{R}_{s}^{2},

\dot{R}_{s}^{4}
and

may be treated according to the homogeneous version of Kato–Ponce commutator estimates (see Lemma

We get

\displaystyle\|\dot{R}^{1}_{s}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}w\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}+\|\nabla_{x}w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\varrho}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},

\displaystyle\|\dot{R}_{s}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\|\dot{R}_{s}^{5}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\varrho}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

The (more involved) terms

and

We get

\displaystyle\|\dot{R}_{s}^{3}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|\nabla_{x}^{2}v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}\|_{\dot{H}^{s-2}},

Plugging all the above estimates in (

) and using Proposition
2.1
, we end up with

\min\Big\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{c}\Big\}\frac{d}{dt}\|(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+\frac{c_{d,\gamma,s}}{1+t}\|(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\\

4.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Let us give the sketch of the proof for global existence
of (
1.1
)–(
1.4
), and
then establish uniqueness by means of a classical energy method.

4.1.
Existence

Here we are given that
(\varrho_{0},u_{0},A_{0})
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
.
One can observe that proving the existence of a global-in-time solution
(\varrho,u,A)
for the system (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) is equivalent to prove the same for the system (
ES
) (after the change of variable (
2.5
)). This further leads to prove the existence of global-in-time solution for the system (
BB
).

Step 1
: Solving an approximate system

Fix some cut-off function
\chi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported in, say, the ball
B(0,4/3)

with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
in
B(0,4/3)
and
\chi=1
in
B(0,3/4).

Set
v^{n}:=\chi(n^{-1}\cdot)\,v.

Let
J_{n}
be the Friedrichs’ truncation operator
defined by
J_{n}z:={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\mathds{1}_{B(0,n)}{\mathcal{F}}z).

For all
n\geq 1
, we consider the following regularization of (
BB
):

(BB
n
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\varrho}+J_{n}((v^{n}\!+\!J_{n}w)\cdot\nabla_{x}J_{n}\tilde{\varrho})+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}J_{n}(J_{n}\tilde{\varrho}\,\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}(v^{n}\!+\!J_{n}w))=0,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+J_{n}((v^{n}\!+\!J_{n}w)\cdot\nabla_{x}J_{n}w)+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}J_{n}(J_{n}\tilde{\varrho}\,\nabla_{x}J_{n}\tilde{\varrho})+J_{n}(J_{n}w\cdot\nabla_{x}v^{n})\\
\hskip 170.71652pt\displaystyle=-\gamma_{p}\nabla_{x}J_{n}\phi,\\
\displaystyle\frac{2i}{c}\partial_{t}A+\frac{1}{k_{0}}\Delta_{x}A+\zeta A-\zeta^{\prime}J_{n}\left(\tilde{\varrho}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}A\right)=0,\end{cases}

supplemented with initial data
(J_{n}\tilde{\varrho}_{0},J_{n}w_{0},J_{n}A_{0}).

Note that
v^{n}
is in
{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s+1}).
Hence the above system may be seen as an ODE in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).
Applying the standard Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem thus
ensures that there exists a unique maximal solution
(\tilde{\varrho}^{n},w^{n},A^{n})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}([0,T^{n});L^{2})
to (
BBn
).

Now, from
J_{n}^{2}=J_{n},
we deduce that
(J_{n}\tilde{\varrho}^{n},J_{n}w^{n},J_{n}A^{n})
also satisfies (

). Hence, uniqueness of the solution
entails that
J_{n}\tilde{\varrho}^{n}=\tilde{\varrho}^{n}
,
J_{n}w^{n}=w^{n}
and
J_{n}A^{n}=A^{n}
.
In other words,
(\tilde{\varrho}^{n},w^{n},A^{n})
is spectrally localized in the ball
B(0,n),

and one can thus assert that
(\tilde{\varrho}^{n},w^{n},A^{n})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}([0,T^{n});H^{\sigma})
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R},

and actually satisfies:

(4.27)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\varrho}^{n}+J_{n}((v^{n}\!+\!w^{n})\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}^{n})+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}J_{n}(\tilde{\varrho}^{n}\,\mbox{\rm div}\;\!_{x}(v^{n}\!+\!w^{n}))=0,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}w^{n}+J_{n}((v^{n}\!+\!w^{n})\cdot\nabla_{x}w^{n})+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}J_{n}(\tilde{\varrho}^{n}\,\nabla_{x}\tilde{\varrho}^{n})+J_{n}(w^{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}v^{n})\\
\hskip 170.71652pt\displaystyle=-\gamma J_{n}\nabla_{x}\phi,\\
\displaystyle\frac{2i}{c}\partial_{t}A^{n}+\frac{1}{k_{0}}\Delta A^{n}+\zeta A^{n}-\zeta^{\prime}J_{n}\left(\tilde{\varrho}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}A\right)=0,\\
(\tilde{\varrho}^{n},w^{n},A^{n})|_{t=0}=(J_{n}\tilde{\varrho}_{0},J_{n}w_{0},J_{n}A_{0})).\end{cases}

Step 2
: Uniform a-priori estimates in the solution space

Since
J_{n}
is an orthogonal projector in any Sobolev space and
(J_{n}\tilde{\varrho}^{n},J_{n}w^{n},J_{n}A^{n})=(\tilde{\varrho}^{n},w^{n},A^{n}),

one can repeat verbatim (and rigorously) the computations of Section
3
. The only change is that
since
Dv^{n}=Dv+{\mathcal{O}}(n^{-1}),
the final estimates therein
only hold in the time interval
[0,\min(cn,T_{n}))
for some
c>0,

which eventually implies that
T_{n}\geq cn.

The conclusion of this step is that for any fixed
T>0,
the triplet

(\tilde{\varrho}^{n},w^{n},A^{n})
for
n
large enough is defined on
[0,T],

belongs to
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}([0,T];H^{\sigma})
for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and is
bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}).

Step 3
: Convergence

Let us fix some
T>0.

Given the uniform bounds of the previous step, the weak
∗
compactness theorem
ensures that (up to a subsequence), there exists some
(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s})
such that

(\tilde{\varrho}^{n},w^{n},A^{n})\rightharpoonup(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)\ \hbox{ weakly}^{*}\ \hbox{ in }\ L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}).

Furthermore, computing
\partial_{t}\tilde{\varrho}^{n}
,
\partial_{t}w^{n}
and
\partial_{t}A^{n}
by means of (

) and using
standard product laws in Sobolev spaces, one can prove that for all

Hence, from the Aubin-Lions lemma, interpolation and Cantor diagonal process, we obtain that (still up to a subsequence),

This allows to pass to the limit in
(
4.27
)
and to conclude that

(\tilde{\varrho},w,A)
satisfies (

) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.
Of course, since

is arbitrary,

actually satisfies (

) on

and belongs to
L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s}).

Step 4
: Time continuity

That

[

Note that ref.

\displaystyle\|(\varrho_{\delta},w_{\delta},A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|(\nabla_{x}v,\nabla_{x}w_{1},\nabla_{x}w_{2},\nabla_{x}\varrho_{1},\nabla_{x}\varrho_{2})(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|(\varrho_{\delta},w_{\delta})(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma_{p}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{x}\phi_{\delta}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\|w_{\delta}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau.

Rewriting the Schrödinger equation for
A_{\delta}
, we have

\frac{2i}{c}\partial_{t}A_{\delta}+\frac{1}{k_{0}}\Delta_{x}A_{\delta}+\zeta A_{\delta}=\zeta^{\prime}\left[\left(\varrho_{2}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}-\varrho_{1}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}\right)A_{2}+\varrho_{1}^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}A_{\delta}\right].

Now using the
identity

(\varrho_{2})^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}-(\varrho_{1})^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}}=\frac{2}{\gamma-1}\varrho_{\delta}\int_{0}^{1}(\varrho_{1}+\eta\varrho_{\delta})^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}-1}\,d\eta,

and using the fact that the operator
\nabla_{x}S(t)
(where
S(t)
is the Schrödinger semigroup) maps
L^{2}
in
L^{2}

we get

\|\nabla_{x}A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\biggl\|\int_{0}^{1}(\varrho_{1}+\eta\varrho_{\delta})^{\frac{2}{\gamma-1}-1}\,d\eta\biggr\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\right).

Noting that
\gamma\leq 1+2d/(d+2),
and
\varrho_{i}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}\cap L^{\infty})
for
i=1,2,
the above integral (the coefficient of
\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}
) in the right-hand side is bounded in terms of
\varrho_{1}
and
\varrho_{2}
. Thus, we have

(4.32)

\|\nabla_{x}A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\mathcal{K}_{\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2}}\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}},

where
\mathcal{K}_{\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2}}
may depend on time ‘
t
’ but not in the space variable ‘
x
’.

Since
\phi_{\delta}=(A_{2})^{2}-(A_{1})^{2}
and using the fact
(A_{i})^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}A_{i,j}\overline{A_{i,j}}=A_{i}\cdot\overline{A_{i}}
,
i=1,2
we find that

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\phi_{\delta}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x}(A_{2}\cdot\overline{A_{2}}-A_{1}\cdot\overline{A_{1}})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x}(A_{\delta}\cdot\overline{A_{2}}+A_{1}\cdot\overline{A_{\delta}})

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{x}A_{\delta})\overline{A_{2}}+A_{\delta}(\nabla_{x}\overline{A_{2}})+(\nabla_{x}A_{1})\overline{A_{\delta}}+A_{1}(\nabla_{x}\overline{A_{\delta}}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\phi_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{A_{1},A_{2}}\left(\|A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla_{x}A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

(4.33)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{K}_{\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2},A_{1},A_{2}}\left(\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}+\|A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\right),

where we have used the bound (
4.32
), and the quantity
K_{\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2},A_{1},A_{2}}
above may depend on time ‘
t
’ but not in the space variable ‘
x
’.

Using (
4.33
) in (
4.31
), we get

\displaystyle\|(\varrho_{\delta},w_{\delta},A_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}_{v,\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2},w_{1},w_{2},A_{1},A_{2},\gamma_{p}}(\tau)\|(\varrho_{\delta},w_{\delta},A_{\delta})(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau,

for some function
\mathcal{K}_{v,\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2},w_{1},w_{2},A_{1},A_{2},\gamma_{p}}
depending on the quantities appearing in the subscript.
Then, applying Grönwall’s lemma to the above inequality ensures the uniqueness of the solution.

This completes the proof of the proposition. ∎

Appendix A

Some technical results

In this part, we prove here some technical results that have been used in this paper. Let us start with an ODE estimate.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
Y:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfy the differential inequality

\frac{d}{dt}Y+\frac{a}{1+t}Y\leq C\biggl(\frac{Y}{(1+t)^{2}}+Y^{2}+\sum_{j\leq J}(1+t)^{m^{\prime}_{j}-1}Y^{m_{j}+1}\biggr)\quad\hbox{on }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}

for some
J>0
,
C>0,

a>1,

m_{j}>0
and
m^{\prime}_{j}<ma_{j}
, for
j\leq J
.
Then, there exists
c=c(a,m_{j},m_{j}^{\prime},C)
such that if
Y(0)\leq c,
then we have

Y(t)\leq 2e^{\frac{Ct}{1+t}}\frac{Y_{0}}{(1+t)^{a}}\quad\hbox{for all }\ t\geq 0.

Proof.

We set
Z(t):=(1+t)^{a}e^{-\frac{Ct}{1+t}}Y(t)
and observe that the above differential inequality recasts in

\frac{d}{dt}Z\leq C(1+t)^{-a}e^{\frac{Ct}{1+t}}Z^{2}+C\sum_{j\leq J}(1+t)^{m_{j}^{\prime}-m_{j}a-1}e^{\frac{Cmt}{1+t}}Z^{m_{j}+1},

which implies that

(A.34)

\frac{d}{dt}Z\leq Ce^{C}(1+t)^{-a}Z^{2}+C\sum_{j\leq J}(1+t)^{m_{j}^{\prime}-m_{j}a-1}e^{Cm_{j}}Z^{m_{j}+1}.

The conclusion stems from a bootstrap argument : let
Z_{0}:=Z(0)
and assume that

(A.35)

Z(t)\leq 2Z_{0}\quad\hbox{on}\quad[0,T].

Then (
A.34
) implies that

\frac{d}{dt}Z\leq 4Ce^{C}(1+t)^{-a}Z_{0}^{2}+C\sum_{j\leq J}(1+t)^{m_{j}^{\prime}-m_{j}a-1}e^{Cm_{j}}(2Z_{0})^{m_{j}+1}.

Hence, integrating in time, we discover that on
[0,T],
we have

Z(t)\leq Z_{0}+\frac{4Ce^{C}}{a-1}Z_{0}^{2}\bigl(1-(1+t)^{1-a}\bigr)+C\sum_{j\leq J}\frac{e^{Cm_{j}}(2Z_{0})^{m_{j}+1}}{m_{j}a-m_{j}^{\prime}}\bigl(1-(1+t)^{m^{\prime}_{j}-m_{j}a}\bigr)\cdotp

Let us discard the obvious case
Z_{0}=0.
Then, if
Z_{0}
is so small as to satisfy

\frac{4Ce^{C}}{a-1}Z_{0}+C\sum_{j\leq J}\frac{2^{m_{j}+1}Ce^{Cm_{j}}Z_{0}^{m}}{m_{j}a-m_{j}^{\prime}}\leq 1,

the above inequality ensures that we actually have
Z(t)<2Z_{0}
on
[0,T]
. Therefore, the supremum of
T>0
satisfying (
A.35
) has to be infinite.
∎

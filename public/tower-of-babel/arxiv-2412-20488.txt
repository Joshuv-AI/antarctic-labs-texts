Andrew Campbell

Abstract
. Initiated by a result of Gorin and Marcus [Int. Math. Res. Not., (3):883–913, 2020] and an observation of Steinerberger [Proc. Amer. Math. Soc., 147(11):4733–4744, 2019], there has been a recent growing body of literature connecting repeated differentiation of real rooted polynomials to free additive convolution semigroups in free probability. Roughly, this connection states that in the large degree limit the empirical measure of the roots after many derivatives is, up to a rescaling, the original empirical measure of the roots raised to a free additive convolution power. If the original roots satisfy some bounds and the number of derivatives is such that the remaining degree is fixed, then it has been shown in various contexts that these high derivatives converge to the Hermite polynomials. In the context of convolution semigroups and finite free probability, where Hermite polynomials are the analogue of the Gaussian distribution, these results have a natural interpretation as a central limit theorem for repeated differentiation. We consider the case when these root bounds are removed and identify the potential limits of repeated differentiation as the real rooted Appell sequences. We prove that a sequence of polynomials is in the domain of attraction of an Appell sequence exactly when the empirical measures of the roots are, up to a rescaling, in the domain of attraction of a free infinitely divisible distribution naturally associated to the Appell sequence. We consider the limits of Appell sequences, generalizing the well known fact that the roots of a Hermite polynomial, after being appropriately normalized, are asymptotically distributed according to the semicircle distribution. We additionally extend these notions of infinite divisibility and fractional convolution semigroups to rectangular finite free probability. Our approach is based on the finite free R R -transform of the polynomials, providing a step towards an analytic theory of finite free probability. These transforms provide a clear connection between Appell polynomials and free infinitely divisible distributions, where the finite free R R -transform of a real rooted Appell polynomial is a truncated version of the R R -transform of an infinitely divisible distribution.

(2)

p(x)=\det(x-A)
, and let
O
be a uniformly distributed random
\ell\times d
matrix with orthonormal rows, then

\mathbb{E}_{O}\det\left(x-OAO^{*}\right)=\frac{\ell!}{d!}D^{d-\ell}p(x).

We can view the matrix
OAO^{*}
as a compression of
A
onto a uniformly chosen
\ell
dimensional subspace.

It was also observed in
[
44
]
that
\boxplus_{d}
can be computed in terms of differential operators; namely if
\widehat{p}
and
\widehat{q}
are such that
\widehat{p}(D)x^{d}=p(x)
and
\widehat{q}(D)x^{d}=q(x)
, where

denotes the differentiation operator, then

(1.7)

p(x)\boxplus_{d}q(x)=\widehat{p}(D)\widehat{q}(D)x^{d}.

We also consider the
rectangular finite free additive convolution

\boxplus_{d}^{n}
defined for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
by

(1.8)

(p\boxplus_{d}^{n}q)(x)=x^{d}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}(-1)^{k}x^{d-k}\sum_{i+j=k}\frac{(d-i)!(d-j)!}{d!(d-k)!}\frac{(n+d-i)!(n+d-j)!}{(n+d)!(n+d-k)!}a_{i}b_{j}.

The convolution
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
has many analogous properties to that of
\boxplus_{d}
. If
A
and
B
are
d+n\times d
matrices such that
p(x)=\det(x-A^{*}A)
and
q(x)=\det(x-B^{*}B)
, then
(p\boxplus_{d}^{n}q)(x)=\mathbb{E}_{U,O}\det\left(x-(A+UBO)^{*}(A+UBO)\right)
where the expected value is taken over independent Haar distributed orthogonal matrices
U
and
O
. A related important property is that if
p
and
q
have only non-negative roots, then so does
(p\boxplus_{d}^{n}q)
.

We also consider the differential operator

(1.9)

M_{n}=xD^{2}+(n+1)D,

defined for any real
n>-1
. Essentially, as we will demonstrate throughout, anything that is true about

and
\boxplus_{d}
also holds for

and
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
. The following lemma, whose proof follows from a straightforward computation, is one such example. The second identity, (
1.11
), appeared in
[
21
, Proof of Main Result III]
.

Lemma 1.9
.

For degree
d
monic polynomials
p
and
q
, and any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(1.10)

(p\boxplus_{d}^{n}q)(x)=\frac{1}{d!(d+n)_{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{d}\left[M_{n}^{d-k}q\right](0)M_{n}^{k}p(x),

where
M_{n}
is defined in (
1.9
). Additionally, if
p(x)=P\left(M_{n}\right)x^{d}
and
q(x)=Q\left(M_{n}\right)x^{d}
, then

(1.11)

(p\boxplus_{d}^{n}q)(x)=P\left(M_{n}\right)Q\left(M_{n}\right)x^{d}.

The operator
M_{n}
also acts as the compression of rectangular matrices, in a way completely analogous to
D
in Lemma
1.8
. We could not find it explicitly stated in the literature, however it follows from straight-forward computations using the notion of
minor orthogonality
defined in
[
44
]

[

,
28
]
.

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
A
be a
d\times(d+n)
rectangular matrix and let
p(x)=\det(x-AA^{*})
. Let
\ell<d
. Let
U
be a uniformly distributed
\ell\times d
random matrix with orthonormal rows and let

be a uniformly distributed
(d+n)\times\ell+n
random matrix with orthonormal columns. Then,

(1.12)

\mathbb{E}_{U,O}\det\left(x-[UAO][UAO]^{*}\right)=\frac{\ell!(\ell+n)!}{d!(d+n)!}M_{n}^{d-\ell}p(x).

With Lemma
1.9
we can easily extend the definition of
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
to fractional values of

.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
p
and
q
be monic polynomials. For any real
n>-1
, we define the rectangular convolution
p\boxplus_{d}^{n}q
by (
1.10
).

To connect
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
to
\boxplus_{\lambda}
we need to associate a symmetric probability measure to a polynomial
p(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(x-\alpha_{j}^{2})
with non-negative roots
\alpha_{1}^{2},\dots,\alpha_{d}^{2}
. For a measure
\mu
on the positive half line, we denote by
\sqrt{\mu}
the symmetrized version of the push-forward of
\mu
by the map
t\mapsto\sqrt{t}
. For the ERM of
p
it is then straightforward to see that

(1.13)

\sqrt{\mu_{p}}=\frac{1}{2d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\delta_{\alpha_{j}}+\delta_{-\alpha_{j}}.

Studying positively supported measures through their symmetrized square roots is common in random matrix theory and free probability, and is one reason one might want to extend the definition of
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
to fractional
n>-1
. If
\tilde{p}
is the degree
2d
polynomials with roots
\pm\alpha_{1},\dots,\pm\alpha_{d}
, then
D^{2}\tilde{p}(x)=4[M_{-1/2}p](x^{2})
.

Cuenca
[
21
]
defined rectangular finite free cumulants which linearize
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
. It follows from the results of Gribinksi
[
28
]
, and was explicitly pointed out and independently proven in
[

]
, that if
\frac{d}{d+n}\rightarrow\lambda\in(0,1]
, then
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
converges to
\boxplus_{\lambda}
in a way completely analogous to Proposition
1.7
. The most important polynomials in rectangular finite free probability are the
Laguerre polynomials
with index
n>-1

(1.14)

L^{(n)}_{d}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\frac{(-1)^{k}(d+n)_{d-k}}{k!(d-k)!}x^{k}.

L_{d}^{(n)}
serves the role as the
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
Gaussian distribution, much as the symmetrized square root of the Marchenko–Pastur distribution is a Gaussian law in rectangular free probability.

The finite free
R
-transform and the rectangular finite free
R
transform were defined in
[
40
]
and
[
28
]
, respectively. We will use the following convenient equivalent definitions for these transforms, see
[
40
, Lemma 6.4]
and
[
28
, Equation (14)]
.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
p
be a degree
d
polynomial. Let
P_{d}
and
P_{d,n}
be such that

(1.15)

P_{d}\left(\frac{D}{d}\right)x^{d}=p(x),\text{ and }P_{d,n}\left(\frac{M_{n}}{d(d+n)}\right)x^{d}=p(x).

The finite free
R
-transform of
p
is the truncated power series

(1.16)

R_{p}^{d}(s):=-\frac{1}{d}\frac{P_{d}^{\prime}(s)}{P_{d}(s)}\mod[s^{d+1}].

Similarly, the
(d,n)
-rectangular finite free
R
-transform of
p
is the truncated power series

(1.17)

R_{p}^{d,n}(s):=-\frac{s}{d}\frac{P_{d,n}^{\prime}(s)}{P_{d,n}(s)}\mod[s^{d+1}]

On the finite level, the
R
-transforms are defined as truncated power series, and the coefficients are exactly the finite free cumulants. So in some sense our approach is equivalent to considering cumulants. However, as will hopefully be clear in our main results, the
R
-transform approach provides quite a clean comparison between Appell sequences and ID distributions. For a degree
d
polynomial both
P_{d}
and
P_{d,n}
can be chosen as degree
d
polynomials themselves. However, it will be helpful in some results to think of these as entire functions with real roots. This real rooted-ness cannot necessarily be guaranteed at finite truncations. Hence, one should always interpret the logarithmic derivative on the right hand sides of (
1.16
) and (
1.17
) as formal power series

(1.18)

\frac{\dd}{\dd s}\log\left(P_{d}(s)\right)\text{ and }\frac{\dd}{\dd s}\log\left(P_{d,n}(s)\right)

where this derivative is taken coefficient-wise and we note for any sequences of complex number
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(b_{k})_{k\geq 1}
such that as formal power series

(1.19)

\log\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}s^{k}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{k!}s^{k},

we have the relationship

(1.20)

b_{k}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(k)}a_{\pi}(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!.

Thus the first
d+1
coefficients of the formal power series (
1.18
) depend only on the first
d+1
coefficients of
P_{d}
and
P_{d,n}
.

1.4.
Brief background on Appell sequences and the Laguerre–Pólya class

We will now present some background in the Laguerre–Pólya class, Jensen polynomials, and Appell polynomials. We encourage the interested reader to consult
[
19
,
51
,
23
,
22
]
and the references therein for a more thorough treatment of these topics. An entire function
f
is said to belong to the Laguerre–Pólya class if it admits the representation

(1.21)

f(z)=Cz^{m}e^{cz-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-\frac{z}{x_{j}}\right)e^{\frac{z}{x_{j}}},

where
C,c,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
is a multi-set,
\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j}^{-2}<\infty
, and
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. We will also consider the series representation of an entire function
f

(1.22)

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{k}}{k!}z^{k}.

We associate to
f
a sequence of polynomials
\{J_{d,f}\}_{d=1}^{\infty}
, known as the Jensen polynomials, defined by

(1.23)

J_{d,f}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\gamma_{k}\binom{d}{k}z^{k}.

These polynomials have the remarkable property that
f
belongs to the Laguerre–Pólya class if and only if
J_{d,f}
has only real zeros for every
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, the polynomials
J_{d,f}(z/d)
converge to
f(z)
uniformly on compact subsets. At this point we assume
f(0)=1
, i.e.
C=1
and
m=0
in (
1.21
). We define the Appell polynomials
\{A_{d,f}\}_{d=1}^{\infty}
associated to
f
by

(1.24)

A_{d,f}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\gamma_{k}\binom{d}{k}z^{d-k}=z^{d}J_{d,f}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right).

It is not hard to see that
A_{d,f}
has only real roots if and only if
J_{d,f}
does. The Appell polynomials of a function
f
do in fact satisfy the relationship (
1.1
). Conversely, we can also always associate an Appell sequence to a formal power series
f
(and hence in certain circumstances an entire function) by

(1.25)

A_{d,f}(z)=f\left(D\right)z^{d}.

This agrees with the definition of an Appell sequence of an entire function
f
. At this point it is worth noting that if
f(z)=e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}}
, then
A_{d,f}
are the degree
d
Hermite polynomials.

Appell sequences and the Laguerre–Pólya class have previously appeared in the random matrix literature
[
8
,
6
]
in a different, although possibly related context. In
[
8
]
the authors consider, among other things, random
interlacing
arrays of parameter
\theta\in(0,\infty]
. In particular, the
\theta=\infty
case of
[
8
, Theorem 1.13]
classifies all real rooted Appell sequences. In
[
6
]
, the author connects random Laguerre–Pólya functions to unitarily invariant infinite dimensional random Hermitian matrices. We discuss how our work connects to these results in Section
3
.

2.
Main results

2.1.
\boxplus
-infinite divisibility and Appell sequences

Before stating our main results, we justify the normalization required to get a non-degenerate limit. It is easy to check from (
1.16
) that

(2.1)

R_{A_{d,f}}^{d}(s)=-\frac{f^{\prime}(ds)}{f(ds)}\mod\left[s^{d+1}\right].

We thus want to normalize to get a change of variables
s\mapsto\frac{s}{d}
in (
2.1
). To achieve this we consider the polynomials

(2.2)

\hat{A}_{d}(z)=[\mathcal{D}_{1/d}A_{d,f}]^{\boxplus_{d}d}(x)=f\left(\frac{D}{d}\right)^{d}z^{d}.

Note that for Hermite polynomials, where
f(z)=e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2}}
, (
2.2
) is equivalent to dividing the roots by
\sqrt{d}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\hat{A}_{d}
be defined as in (
2.2
) for
f
of the form (
1.21
). Then
\mu_{\hat{A}_{d}}
converges weakly as
d\rightarrow\infty
to the
\boxplus
-ID distribution
\mu_{f}
with
R
-transform

(2.3)

R_{\mu_{f}}(z)=\gamma+\sigma^{2}z+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{z+t}{1-zt}\frac{t^{2}}{t^{2}+1}\dd\nu_{f}(t),

where
\nu_{f}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\delta_{1/x_{j}}
and
\gamma=-c-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x_{j}^{3}+x_{j}}
.

As can be seen in (
2.1
), even before any normalization
R_{A_{d,f}}^{d}
is a truncated version of the
R
-transform of some
\boxplus
-ID distribution. The normalization (
2.2
) fixes the distribution in
d
, so that the truncated
R
-transform converges to this full
R
-transform. For
N<\infty
in (
1.21
) one could prove Theorem
2.1
using (
1.7
), as
\hat{A}_{d}
is the finite free additive convolution of a Hermite polynomial (whose roots have been shifted and rescaled) and a finite number of Laguerre polynomials (whose roots have been shifted and rescaled). In fact, one may be able to extend this argument to the case when
N=\infty
by establishing a finite free version of Kolmogorov’s three-series theorem. We do not explore this direction here and instead employ the finite
R
-transform. Let us also remark that the parameter
\gamma
in Theorem
2.1
should be interpreted as the mean of
\mu_{f}
,
-c
, plus a term which centers the contribution from the integral in (
2.3
). We include this extra term in
\gamma
only to make the representation, using Theorem
1.4
, as an
\boxplus
-ID distribution clear.

The following is essentially a corollary of the fact that the smallest roots of the Jensen polynomials of
f
converge to the roots of
f
in compact sets, but we present it here in the language of probability.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
A_{d,f}
be as in (
1.25
) and let
\tilde{A}_{d}:=\mathcal{D}_{1/d}A_{d,f}
. Then,
d{t^{2}}\dd\mu_{\tilde{A}_{d}}(t)
converges weakly as a Radon measure to

(2.4)

G_{f}=\sigma^{2}\delta_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x_{j}^{2}}\delta_{1/x_{j}}.

This convergence of measures and the relationship to infinitely divisible distributions motivates the following description of the domains of attraction of Appell sequences under repeated differentiation.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\{p_{d}\}_{d=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of monic real rooted polynomials indexed by their degree such that
m_{1}(p_{d})\rightarrow c
,
m_{2}(p_{d})=\Theta(d)
, and

(2.5)

dt^{2}\dd\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{d^{-1}}p_{d}}(t)\rightarrow\dd G_{f}(t)

weakly as Radon measures as
d\rightarrow\infty
where
G_{f}
is the measure in (
2.4
) associated to a function
f
. Then, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

(2.6)

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ell!}{d!}D^{d-\ell}p_{d}(x)=f\left(D\right)x^{\ell},

uniformly on compact subsets as
d\rightarrow\infty
, where

(2.7)

f(z)=e^{-cz-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{z}{x_{j}}\right)e^{\frac{z}{x_{j}}}.

It follows from
[
12
, Theorem 3.4]
that the conditions of Theorem
2.3
are exactly the necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{d^{-1}}p_{d}}
to be in the domain of attraction of
\mu_{f}
in Theorem
2.1
, and Theorem
2.3
is presented in this probabilistic manor. Moreover, the conditions of Theorem
2.3
also have a natural interpretation in terms of polynomials and the Laguerre–Pólya class. The roots of the sequence
\{p_{d}\}
satisfy what is referred to in
[
8
,
6
]
as the Olshanski–Vershik conditions after
[
49
]
. Thus, from
[
6
, Propisition 2.5]
, a real rooted sequence of polynomials
\{p_{d}\}_{d=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
if and only if the reversed sequence
q_{d}(z)=z^{d}p_{d}\left(1/z\right)
converges uniformly on compact subsets to
f
. We discuss further connections between our results and previous work on random entire functions and random matrices in Section
3
.

The normalization in (
2.2
) is one way to recover a non-degenerate
\boxplus
-ID distribution from an Appell sequence, and is such that the limiting measure has cumulants of all orders and the
R
-transform has a series expansion at the origin. However, as we explain in Section

it appears that the ERMs of Appell sequences, under a different rescaling, should converge to
\boxplus
-stable distributions. The barrier to making the connection rigorous is that the true

-transform of
\mu_{A_{d}}
is hard to access, and it is hard to describe
\mu_{A_{d}}
in terms of our finite versions without implicitly or explicitly using cumulants. We do however have the following result which is illustrative of how one may overcome this issue in future work.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f(z)=\cos(z)
, and
A_{d}(z)=A_{d,f}(z)
. Then,
\mu_{A_{d}}
converges weakly as

to the standard Cauchy distribution, which is
\boxplus
-stable.

.

Below we collect some important examples and remark on how to interpret the limiting distribution for general

2.2.
Fractional rectangular convolution powers

Our first main result on rectangular free convolutions, Theorem
2.7
, captures the behavior of roots under repeated application of the operator
M_{n}
on all possible scales. To simplify presentation we work under the following assumption.

Assumption 2.6
.

Let
\{p_{d}\}_{d=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials with only real roots indexed by degree. We say that
\{p_{d}\}
satisfies Assumption
2.6
if there exists a compactly supported probability measure
\mu_{p}
such that
\mu_{p_{d}}

\sup_{1\leq j\leq d<\infty}|x_{j,d}|\leq C,

for some
C>0
.

We break Theorem
2.7
up into three cases, based on the number of times

is applied.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\{p_{d}\}_{d=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials with only non-negative roots satisfying Assumption

\mu_{p}
. For any

Assume

(1)

If

is

(2.9)

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{D}_{{d+n}}p_{j,d}(x)\approx L_{\ell}^{(n)}\left(x\right),

uniformly on compact subsets.

(2)

If

as

, and the first moment of
\mu_{p}
is

, then

(2.10)

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{D}_{\frac{d+n}{n}}p_{j,d}(x)=(z-1)^{\ell},

Next assume

and

(1)

If

and the first moment of

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{\frac{d+n}{n+\ell}}p_{j,d}}=\nu_{1},

(2)

(2)

Remark 2.8
.

The conditions of some parts of Theorem

Lemma 2.9

Let

R_{{p}_{j}}^{d-j}(s)=R_{p}^{d}\left(\frac{d-j}{d}s\right)\mod[s^{d-j+1}].

Moreover, if

3.
Novelties, connections, and further directions

We summarize the novelties of our results, connections to previous works, some open questions, and possible future directions of the ideas outlined here.

3.1.
Novelties

While the (rectangular) finite
R
-transform is used often in
[
40
,
28
]
, the recent finite free probability results
[
1
,
2
,
18
]
centered around Question
1.1
, as well as other results in finite free probability, tend to rely on computations of finite free cumulants. In particular,
[
1
, Proposition 3.4]
demonstrates that the cumulants evolve nicely under repeated differentiation. Our approach, in particular the proof of Lemma
2.9
, demonstrates that the same nice evolution can be seen for
R
-transforms, for both the operator
D
and
M_{n}
, without ever referring to cumulants. Using the
R
-transform approach also illuminates the connections to infinite divisibility, where the Lévy–Khintchine representation theorem and Bercovici–Pata bijection give an explicit characterization of the
R
-transform while the free cumulants (to the best of our knowledge) fail to have such a nice representation theorem. In Section
4
the
R
-transform approach allows us to prove results for
D
and
M_{n}
in parallel, with little to no changes from one theorem to the next.

3.2.
Relationship to the
\beta=\infty
-corners process

In

the authors prove that this processes converges, as

[

The operator

3.3.
A true analytic theory of finite free probability

We next present the conjecture heuristically worked out in Section
4.1
.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Let
f
be in the Laguerre–Pólya class and satisfy (
4.8
). Let
A_{d}(x)=f(D)x^{d}
, and let
\rho=\frac{1}{\alpha}-1
. Then,
\mu_{\mathcal{D}_{h(d)^{-1}d^{\rho}}A_{d}}
converges weakly as
d\rightarrow\infty
to a
\boxplus
-stable distribution with stability parameter
\alpha
.

We discuss the challenges of this problem in detail and one potential solution in Section
4.1
. More generally it motivates the following larger project.

If one could define an analytic version of the finite free
R
-transform, then it would be possible to capture heavy-tailed measures in the large degree limit. Another possible use of such a theory would be an improved version of the finite free Berry–Esseen theorem of
[
5
]
. The degree dependence of their bound grows quite fast, however this is most likely an artifact of the proof using finite free cumulants. It is common, in both the free and classical setting, for the strongest version of Berry–Esseen to be proven using analytic tools. An improved Berry–Esseen estimate would allow for the study of local properties of roots using finite free probability.

We propose one new version of the
R
-transform for Appell sequences using the Jensen polynomials. Another more general approach would be to simply consider the finite free
R
-transform
R_{p}(z)=-\frac{1}{d}\frac{P_{d}^{\prime}(z)}{P_{d}(z)}
as a meromorphic function where
P_{d}
is a degree
d
polynomial such that
P_{d}(D/d)x^{d}=p(x)
. For Appell polynomials the difference between this approach and the approach we take in Section
4.1
is essentially the difference between approximating
f
with Taylor polynomials or the real rooted Jensen polynomials.

3.4.
The multiplicative free convolution

There also exists a multiplicative free convolution
\boxtimes
and a finite free multiplicative convolution
\boxtimes_{d}
. The theory of
\boxtimes
-infinite divisibility has been well studied, see for example
[
13
,
3
]
. The question remains open as to whether the ideas here can be extended to this setting, either on
[0,\infty)
or by considering polynomials with roots on the unit circle.

3.5.
The geometry of polynomial roots

As mentioned in the introduction a fundamental problem in the geometry of polynomial roots is determining which operators on polynomials preserve the property of all roots belonging to a certain set. This was answered for roots in
\mathbb{R}
in
[
14
]
. Our approach to
\boxplus_{d}
and
\boxplus_{d}^{n}
is to associate them to an operator,
D
or
M_{n}
, and to consider these convolutions as generated by these operators. This results in essentially identical definitions of the finite free
R
-transforms and proofs of our main results. It might be interesting to consider a general perspective. Given an operator
\mathcal{L}
on polynomials, construct a convolution on polynomials generated from this operator and consider its interpretation as a probability theory. Are there operators which give finite versions of other non-commutative probability theories, such as the Boolean or monotone convolutions? The approach in
[
21
]
essentially lays out a general approach to defining cumulants. Additionally, can properties of this convolution be connected to the problem considered in
[
14
]
?

3.6.
Lévy flows on polynomials

In
[
32
,
36
]
the authors consider the
backwards heat flow
for polynomials, i.e. for a polynomial
p(z)
they consider the dynamics for roots of
e^{-tD^{2}}p(z)
. These polynomial root flows have additionally appeared in
[
7
]
in the solutions of interacting particle systems. For random polynomials with independent coefficients the authors of
[
32
]
identified a critical time for the limiting empirical root measure of the polynomial to move from supported on
\mathbb{C}
to
\mathbb{R}
and to be a rescaling of the semicircle distribution. Let
f
be a function in the Laguerre–Pólya class of the form (
1.21
). For
t\in[0,\infty)
, does a similar transition occur for
f\left(D/d\right)^{\lfloor td\rfloor}p_{d}(z)
? If
p_{d}(z)=z^{d}
, then for any fixed
t>0
, Theorem
2.1
could be adapted to show that the limiting root measure is the law at time
t
of a free Lévy process.

3.7.
Finite free probability and the roots of entire functions

As we have demonstrated in our main results the Appell and Jensen polynomials of functions in the Laguerre–Pólya class serve as important examples in finite free probability, and through their infinite degree limit can be connected to nice distributions in free probability. These Jensen and Appell polynomials have also attracted recent attention
[
29
,
51
,
52
,
30
,
24
,
18
]
for their connection to the Riemann Hypothesis which dates back to
[
53
,
35
]
. Much of the renewed interest in these polynomials is due to the result of Griffin, Ono, Rolen, and Zagier
[
29
]
who established conditions on the coefficients of
f
for the Jensen polynomials to converge to Hermite polynomials under repeated differentiation, without necessarily assuming the function
f
has only real roots. The analogous result formulated in terms of roots of
f
is given in
[
18
]
with the added assumption of
f
having only real roots. The concept of the Laguerre–Pólya class has also recently been generalized
[
60
]
to allow for some complex roots. By considering this more general class it may be possible to extend Theorem
2.3
to polynomials with complex roots. This may be one approach to unifying the results of
[
29
]
and
[
18
]
through some sort of
weakly non-Hermitian finite free probability
, i.e. while the Jensen polynomials may have some complex roots, the rescaled point process will still converge to a point mass at
0
resulting in a purely Hermite limit. More generally, it is worth continuing to investigate the role finite free probability may play in the study of entire functions.

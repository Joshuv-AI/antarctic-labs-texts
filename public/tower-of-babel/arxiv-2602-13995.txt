Stability and instability of a one-dimensional MHD model
Nicola De Nitti, Jie Guo, Quansen Jiu

Abstract
. We consider a one-dimensional magnetohydrodynamics model introduced by Dai et al. (2023), in a parameter regime where, in the absence of a magnetic field, the system reduces to the De Gregorio model for the Euler equations. We analyze stability and instability near the first excited state on the torus, thus generalizing the recent results obtained by Guo and Jiu (2025) for the De Gregorio model. Specifically, we establish global well-posedness of the linearized system, local well-posedness for the nonlinear system, and demonstrate both linear and nonlinear instability for a broad class of initial data in the weighted Sobolev space introduced by Lai et al. (2020). We identify the principal linearized operator, which is structurally equivalent to that of the De Gregorio model, as the primary mechanism of instability. Moreover, we prove global well-posedness and stability of both linear and nonlinear systems for initial data in a particular subspace of the aforementioned weighted Sobolev space.

,

and

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}u^{\pm}=H\omega^{\pm},\quad\text{with}\quad u^{\pm}(t,0)\equiv 0,\quad t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}.

1.1
–
1.2

ideal incompressible magnetohydrodynamics

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{v}+(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}-(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{b}+\nabla P=0,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{b}+(\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{b}-(\boldsymbol{b}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{v}=0,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{v}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{b}=0,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\end{cases}

where the vector fields
\boldsymbol{v}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
denote the fluid velocity and magnetic field, respectively; the scalar function

is the pressure.
Introducing the
Elsässer variables

[
20
]

(1.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{b},\qquad\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{b},

the system
Eq.

1.3
is symmetrized:

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{+}+(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{+}+\nabla P=0,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{u}^{-}+(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{-}+\nabla P=0,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^{+}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^{-}=0,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3}.\end{cases}

Defining

\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+}\coloneqq\nabla\times\boldsymbol{u}^{+}=\nabla\times\boldsymbol{v}+\nabla\times\boldsymbol{b},\qquad\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-}\coloneqq\nabla\times\boldsymbol{u}^{-}=\nabla\times\boldsymbol{v}-\nabla\times\boldsymbol{b},

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+}+(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+}-(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{-}+\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{+})=0,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-}+(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-}-(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{+}+\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{-})=0,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
\boldsymbol{u}^{+}=\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+},\qquad\boldsymbol{u}^{-}=\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\end{cases}

where
(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{+})_{j}=u^{-}_{i}\partial_{j}u^{+}_{i}
and
(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{-})_{j}=u^{+}_{i}\partial_{j}u^{-}_{i}
for

\displaystyle\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{+})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{+})-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{-}),

\displaystyle\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{-})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{-})-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{+}),

we can rewrite

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+}&+(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+}-(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\\

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-}&+(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-}-(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-}\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\\
&+\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{+}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{-})-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla\times(\boldsymbol{u}^{-}\nabla\boldsymbol{u}^{+})=0,&&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\\
\boldsymbol{u}^{+}&=\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{+},\quad\boldsymbol{u}^{-}=\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-},&&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

1.2.
Summary of the main results and outline of the paper

More recently, progress has been made in understanding the stability of
excited states
for the De Gregorio model
Eq.

1.15
:

\Omega_{\kappa}(\theta)\coloneqq-\sin(\kappa\,\theta),\quad U_{\kappa}(\theta)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\kappa}\,\sin(\kappa\,\theta)\qquad\text{for $\kappa\geq 2$.}

For
\kappa=2
, numerical simulations mentioned in
[
29
]
and
[
32
]
highlight instability and, on the other hand, that perturbations of the type
\sin(2\theta)+\varepsilon\cos\theta
converge to some multiple of
-\sin(2\theta)
instead of ground states. A rigorous result in this direction was recently established in
[
22
]
, where the authors analyzed the stability and instability of perturbations around the first excited state on the torus. More precisely, for
\kappa=2
, the linear and nonlinear instability are established for a broad class of initial data, while nonlinear stability is proved for another large class of initial data.

We recall some key ideas of the strategy of
[
22
]
in later sections, as they are helpful for our study as well. In particular, we stress that one of the new elements in the instability analysis of
[
22
]
is the derivation of a second-order ordinary differential equation for the Fourier coefficients of solutions and the analysis of the spectral properties of a positive definite quadratic form arising from it.

The goal of this paper is to extend the analysis of
[
22
]
to the one-dimensional MHD model given in
Eqs.

1.1
–
1.2
, with parameters as in
Eq.

1.18
. In this paper,
we cannot address the general parameter range
Eq.

1.17
, but some extensions to cases when
q\neq 0
are also possible (see
Remark

5.3
below).

We focus on the study of the stability and instability of
Eqs.

1.1
–
1.2
near the first excited state

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}^{\pm}(\theta)\coloneqq-\sin(2\,\theta),\quad U_{2}^{\pm}(\theta)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\,\sin(2\,\theta).

Moreover, the case of higher excited states (i. e.,
\kappa\geq 3
) presents several further challenges, which are discussed in
Remark

5.2
below. The paper is organized as follows.

In
Section

2
, we introduce the notation and functional framework used throughout the paper. In particular, we write the equation satisfied by the perturbations

\displaystyle\omega^{\pm}(t,\theta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sin(2\theta)+\left(\eta^{+}(t,\theta)\pm\eta^{-}(t,\theta)\right),

\displaystyle t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},

\displaystyle u^{\pm}(t,\theta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)+\left(v^{+}(t,\theta)\pm v^{-}(t,\theta)\right),

\displaystyle t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}.

We state the main results in

–
3.2
); instability for the linearized problem (

3.3
); local well-posedness for the nonlinear problem (

); instability (

) or global well-posedness and stability (

) for the nonlinear problem.

In

by relying on Galerkin’s method using the orthonormal basis

of the Hilbert space

1.1
–
1.2
, with parameters as in
Eq.

1.17
, in terms the variables
\eta^{\pm}(t,\theta)
defined by

\displaystyle\omega^{\pm}(t,\theta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sin(2\theta)+\left(\eta^{+}(t,\theta)\pm\eta^{-}(t,\theta)\right),

\displaystyle t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},

\displaystyle u^{\pm}(t,\theta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)+\left(v^{+}(t,\theta)\pm v^{-}(t,\theta)\right),

\displaystyle t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},

with
v^{\pm}(t,0)=0
. Then
Eqs.

1.1
–
1.2
can be rewritten as a partial differential equation in
\eta^{\pm}
as follows:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta^{+}=\{\frac{1}{2}\eta^{+}+v^{+},\sin(2\theta)\}+N_{1},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}\eta^{-}=\{\frac{1}{2}\eta^{-}-v^{-},\sin(2\theta)\}-2q\left(\sin(2\theta)H\eta^{-}+\cos(2\theta)\eta^{-}\right)+N_{2},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{\theta}v^{\pm}=H\eta^{\pm},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

where
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
are nonlinear terms defined as

(2.2)

\displaystyle N_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\eta^{+},v^{+}\}-\{\eta^{-},v^{-}\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle N_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\eta^{-},v^{+}\}-\{\eta^{+},v^{-}\}-2q\left(\eta^{-}H\eta^{+}-\eta^{+}H\eta^{-}\right),

with
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
denoting the
Lie bracket

\{f,g\}\coloneqq f\partial_{\theta}g-g\partial_{\theta}f
.

To simplify the notation, we define the linear operators

(2.4)

\displaystyle Lf\coloneqq\{f,\sin(2\theta)\},\qquad Af\coloneqq\{v(f),\sin(2\theta)\},\qquad Qf\coloneqq\sin(2\theta)H\eta^{-}+\cos(2\theta)\eta^{-},

where
v(f)
satisfies
\partial_{\theta}v=Hf
with
v(0)=0
, and let

(2.5)

\displaystyle L^{+}\coloneqq L+A,\qquad L^{-}\coloneqq L-A-2\,q\,Q.

With the notations in
Eqs.

2.4
–
2.5
, the system
Eqs.

2.1
–
2.3
takes the form

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta^{+}=L^{+}\eta^{+}+N_{1},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}\eta^{-}=L^{-}\eta^{-}+N_{2},&t>0,\,\theta\in\mathbb{T}.\end{cases}

The linear part of the one-dimensional MHD system in
Eq.

2.6
, with the more restrictive parameter choice in
Eq.

1.18
,
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta^{+}=L^{+}\eta^{+}=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\eta^{+}+v^{+},\sin(2\theta)\right\},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}\eta^{-}=L^{-}\eta^{-}=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\eta^{-}-v^{-},\sin(2\theta)\right\},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T}.\end{cases}

2.2.
Function spaces and notation

We analyze the one-dimensional MHD in the following function space inspired by
[
32
,
22
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2}\coloneqq\left\{\eta\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}):\ \eta\text{ is odd }\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{|\partial_{\theta}\eta|^{2}}{|\sin(\theta)|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty\right\},

which is a Hilbert space with inner-product defined as

(2.8)

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\coloneqq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\rho_{2}\,\partial_{\theta}\xi\,\partial_{\theta}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\theta,\qquad\text{with }\ \rho_{2}\coloneqq\frac{1}{4\pi\sin^{2}\theta}

(see
[
22
, Eq. (1.13)–(1.14)]
).
The family
\{e_{2,k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, with

\displaystyle e_{2,k}\coloneqq\frac{\sin((k+2)\theta)}{k+2}-\frac{\sin(k\theta)}{k},\qquad k\geq 1,

is a complete orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{2}
(see
[
22
, Lemma 2.1]
).

The norm in the Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
can thus be written as

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\coloneqq\left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\rho_{2}\,|\partial_{\theta}\eta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}=\|\rho_{2}^{1/2}\,\partial_{\theta}\eta\|_{L^{2}}.

We denote

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f:[-\pi,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}\,:\ f\in L^{2}(-\pi,\pi),\ f\ \text{is periodic on}\ [-\pi,\pi]\right\},

\displaystyle H^{m}(\mathbb{T})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f:[-\pi,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}\,:\ f^{(k)}\in L^{2}(-\pi,\pi),\ f^{(k)}\ \text{is periodic on}\ [-\pi,\pi],\ \text{for all}\ 0\leq k\leq m\right\},

where
f^{(k)}
denotes the
k
-th order derivative of
f
.
Throughout this paper, the norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
,
\|\cdot\|_{H^{m}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}}
refers to those in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
H^{m}(\mathbb{T})
, and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, respectively. We also denote by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
the standard inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, i. e.,

\displaystyle\left\langle f,g\right\rangle\coloneqq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}fg\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Finally, throughout the paper, we use
C
for an absolute constant and
C(A,B,\ldots,Z)
for constants depending on the parameters
A,B,\ldots,Z
. These constants may vary from line to line unless otherwise specified. The notation
A\lesssim B
indicates that
A\leq CB
for some positive constant
C
, and
A\sim B
means that both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold, with implicit constants that may also vary from line to line.

3.
Main results

Our first result establishes the global existence and uniqueness of solutions to
Eq.

2.7
with odd initial data.

Theorem 3.1

(Global existence of solutions to the linearized problem)
.

Let
\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{\pm}_{0}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T})
for some integer
m>3
. Then, for any
T>0
, there exists a unique classical odd solution
\eta^{\pm}
to
Eq.

2.7
with initial condition
\eta^{\pm}(0,\cdot)=\eta^{\pm}_{0}(\cdot)
, satisfying

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{1/2}\,\partial_{\theta}\eta^{\pm}(t,\theta)\in C([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{T}))\cap C^{2}([0,T];H^{m-2}(\mathbb{T})).

To prove
Theorem

3.1
, we employ Galerkin’s method through the basis
\{e_{2,k}\}_{k\geq 1}
(introduced in
Section

2.2
) and construct the approximate solutions

\eta_{n}^{\pm}(t,\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}(t)e_{2,k}.

During the proof, we also obtain uniform estimates for the Fourier coefficients of the solutions as a byproduct (see
Eqs.

4.18
–
4.20
), which guarantee the rigor of our subsequent arguments.

For more general initial data, we can construct approximate solutions using the general basis
\{\sin(k\theta)\}_{k\geq 1}\cup\{\cos(k\theta)\}_{k\geq 0}
. We can also establish the following result (whose proof is analogous to that of
Theorem

3.1
and is therefore omitted).

Theorem 3.2

(Global well-posedness of the linearized problem)
.

Let us assume that
\eta_{0}^{\pm}\in H^{m}
with
m>3
an integer. Then, for any
T>0
, there exits a unique classical solution
\eta^{\pm}
to
Eq.

2.7
with initial condition
\eta^{\pm}(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}^{\pm}(\cdot)
, satisfying

\displaystyle\eta^{\pm}(t,\theta)\in C([0,T];H^{m})\cap C^{2}([0,T];H^{m-2}).

Having established the global existence result, we turn to the study of the instability of the linearized model
Eq.

2.7
. More precisely, we show that
\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
grows exponentially for a suitable class of initial data.

Theorem 3.3

(Instability for the linearized problem)
.

Let us assume that the conditions in
Theorem

3.1
are satisfied and that the initial data satisfies

\|\eta^{+}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\neq 0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\geq 0.

Then there exists an absolute constants
\lambda_{1}>0
such that the corresponding solution of
Eq.

2.7
given in
Theorem

3.1
satisfies the following lower bound:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}>E_{1}^{1/2}(t)>0,\qquad\text{for all $t>0$},

where

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E_{1}(t)&\coloneqq\frac{\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}{2}\,e^{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}t}\\
&\qquad+\frac{\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}{2}\,e^{-{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}t}}.\end{aligned}

Remark 3.4

(The constant
\lambda_{1}
in
Theorem

3.3
)
.

The constant
\lambda_{1}
in the statement of
Theorem

3.3
is a positive and absolute constant satisfying
\frac{1}{50}<\lambda_{1}<\frac{3}{5}
; see
Lemma

A.7
for more details.

Remark 3.5

(Initial data satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem

3.3
)
.

There exist various initial data satisfying the assumptions in
Theorem

3.3
. Let us suppose that
\eta_{0}^{-}
fulfills
\rho_{2}^{1/2}\,\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}^{-}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T})
for some integer
m>3
and that either of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\eta_{0}^{+}(\theta)=\eta_{2,1}^{+}(0)e_{2,1}
with
a_{1}\neq 0
;

(ii)

\eta_{0}^{+}(\theta)=\eta_{2,1}^{+}(0)e_{2,1}+\eta_{2,k}^{+}(0)e_{2,k}
for some integer
k\geq 2
, and

\displaystyle 0<\big(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(0)\big)^{2}\leq\frac{11}{18(d_{2,k+2}^{+}-d_{2,k}^{+})}\big(\eta_{2,1}^{+}(0)\big)^{2}.

Then the solution obtained in
Theorem

3.1
satisfies
Eq.

3.1
.

Let us briefly comment on the strategy of the proof of
Theorem

3.3
. We start by expanding
\eta^{\pm}(t,\theta)
in orthonormal basis
\{e_{2,k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of the space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Using this basis,
\left\langle L^{\pm}\eta^{\pm},\eta^{\pm}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
formally reduces to a sum involving only squared coefficients

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\eta^{\pm}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}=\left\langle L^{\pm}\eta^{\pm},\eta^{\pm}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}^{\pm}\left(\eta^{\pm}_{2,k}(t)\right)^{2}

without any cross product terms between the coefficients (e. g.,
\eta^{\pm}_{2,k}(t)\eta^{\pm}_{2,k+2}(t)
), where
c_{k}^{\pm}
are constants depending on
k
.

If there exists a constant
C>0
, such that
c_{k}^{\pm}\leq-C
for all
k\geq 1
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\eta^{\pm}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\leq-C\|\eta^{\pm}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}.

Regarding
L^{-}
, there exists
C>0
(see
Section

5.1
) such that
c^{-}_{k}<-C
for all
k\geq 1
. In contrast, for
L^{+}
, the coefficients
c_{k}^{+}
change signs (see
Section

5.2
): more specifically, a computation yields

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}=\left\langle L^{+}\eta^{+},\eta^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\left(\eta^{+}_{2,k}(t)\right)^{2},

where

\displaystyle d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2k^{2}-4k-8}{(k+2)^{2}k^{2}},\qquad\text{for }k=1,\,2,\ldots.

For
k=1
, we have
d_{2,1}^{+}-d_{2,3}^{+}=\frac{11}{18}>0
and, for
k\geq 2
, we have
d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}<0
. This suggests that the linearized equation concerning
\eta^{+}
is responsible for the instability. To actually show that this is the case, we study a second-order ordinary differential equation (ODE) derived from
Eq.

3.3
, which can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\left(\eta^{+}_{2,k}(t)\right)^{2}.

Similarly to the approach in
[
22
]
, we consider the following splitting:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S_{n}^{+}(t)&\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\\
&=\left(d_{2,1}^{+}-d_{2,3}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,1}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(d_{2,2}^{+}-d_{2,4}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,2}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}Q_{k}(t)\\
&\quad+\left(d_{2,n-1}^{+}-d_{2,n+1}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,n-1}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(d_{2,n}-d_{2,n+2}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,n}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+R_{n-1}(t)+R_{n}(t),\end{aligned}

where, the quadratic form
Q_{k}(t)
is defined as

\displaystyle Q_{k}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(d_{2,k+2}^{+}-d_{2,k+4}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\left(d_{2,k}^{+}d_{2,k+2}^{+}+d_{2,k+2}^{+}d_{2,k+4}^{+}-2(d_{2,k+2}^{+})^{2}\right)\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t),\quad\text{for $k=1,\,2,\ldots,\,n-2$,}

and remainder term
R_{k}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle R_{k}(t)\coloneqq 2d_{2,k+2}^{+}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t),\quad\text{for $k=n-1,\,n$.}

Each quadratic form
Q_{k}(t)
is positive definite and admits uniform lower and upper bounds independent of
k\geq 1
; moreover, by applying decay estimates to the remainder terms and letting
n\to\infty
in
Eq.

3.4
, we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle 4\lambda_{1}\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}<\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}<4\lambda_{2}\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2},\qquad\text{for $t>0$,}

where
\lambda_{i}>0
(with
i=1,\,2
) are absolute constants. Thus, the proof of
Theorem

3.3
follows by applying a suitable comparison theorem (see
Lemma

A.6
) to the second-order ODE
Eq.

3.5
.

We now turn to the nonlinear problem. First, we establish the local well-posedness of the classical solution to
Eq.

2.6
with odd initial data.

Theorem 3.6

(Local well-posedness of the nonlinear problem)
.

Let
m>3
be an integer, and assume that
\eta^{\pm}_{0}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy
I_{m}^{2}(0)\coloneqq\|\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}_{0}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}_{0}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}=\delta^{2}
for some
0<\delta<+\infty
. Then there exist
T_{0}>0
and unique classical odd solutions
\eta^{\pm}
to
Eq.

2.6
with initial condition
\eta^{\pm}(0,\cdot)=\eta_{0}^{\pm}
, such that

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{\pm}\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{m}(\mathbb{T}))\cap C^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{m-2}(\mathbb{T})).

Moreover, define
u^{\pm}(t,\theta)\coloneqq\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{\pm}(t,\theta)
. Then the solutions satisfy

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u^{\pm}(t)\|_{H^{m}},\ \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\partial_{t}u^{\pm}(t)\|_{H^{m}},\ \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\partial_{t}^{2}u^{\pm}(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq C(T_{0})\delta,

where the existence time
T_{0}
can be chosen as

T_{0}=\frac{1}{C_{{1}}}\ln\left(1+\frac{C_{{1}}}{2C_{{2}}I_{m}(0)}\right),

with
C_{1},C_{2}>0
denoting absolute constants (cf.
Eq.

6.15
).

The proof of
Theorem

3.6
is based on the linear analysis of
Theorem

3.1
, the main challenge being to obtain suitable estimates for the nonlinear terms.

We then establish a nonlinear instability result. By
Theorem

3.3
, the linearized evolution of
\eta^{+}
exhibits instability in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, in the sense
\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}>E_{1}^{1/2}(t)
for
t>0
. To analyze the corresponding nonlinear system and prove the following instability result, we derive some energy estimates for the system
Eq.

2.6
, which enable us to rigorously pass the limit in a suitable scaled formulation and recover the linearized structure for a suitable class of initial data.

Theorem 3.7

(Instability for the nonlinear problem)
.

Let us consider an integer
m>3
and real constants
\delta>0
and
K>0
. Let
F:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a function satisfying

(3.6)

\displaystyle F(y)\leq Ky,\quad\text{for all}\quad y\in[0,\infty).

There exist initial values
\eta_{0}^{\pm}
satisfying

\displaystyle I_{m}(0)\coloneqq\sqrt{\|\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}_{0}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta},\eta^{-}_{0}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}}<\delta\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0\leq\left\langle L_{1}^{+}u_{0}^{+},u_{0}^{+}\right\rangle\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\|u_{0}^{+}\|_{L^{2}},

where
u_{0}^{+}\coloneqq\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}^{+}
,
L_{1}^{+}u_{0}^{+}\coloneqq\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+}
, and
\lambda_{1}
is the same as in
Remark

3.4
.

Then the corresponding solution
\eta^{+}
constructed in
Theorem

3.6
satisfies

\displaystyle\|u^{+}(t_{K})\|_{L^{2}}>F(I_{m}(0)),\qquad\text{for some $t_{K}\in(0,T_{0})$,}

where
u^{+}(t,\theta)\coloneqq\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}(t,\theta)
and
T_{0}
is same as in
Theorem

3.6
.

As a consequence of
Theorem

3.7
, we say that the system
Eq.

2.6
is
unstable under the Lipschitz structure
. This terminology, motivated by the Lipschitz condition
Eq.

3.6
, originates from
[
23
]
(where this type of instability was first discussed for compressible inviscid fluids) and
[
30
]
(for inhomogeneous incompressible viscous fluids).

Remark 3.8

(Lack of stability)
.

From
Theorem

3.7
, we deduce that
Eq.

2.6

does not
possess the following property: there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u_{0}^{+}\|_{H^{m}},\qquad\text{for any $T>0$}.

Remark 3.9

(Initial data satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem

3.7
)
.

Theorem

3.7
holds for a broad class of initial data. As a simple example, consider initial data of the following form. Assume that
\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}^{-}\in H^{m}
for any integer
m>3
, and
\eta_{0}^{+}
is of the form

\displaystyle\eta_{0}^{+}(\theta)=\eta_{2,1}^{+}(0)e_{2,1}+\eta_{2,k}^{+}(0)e_{2,k},

where
\eta_{2,1}^{+}(0),\eta_{2,k}^{+}(0)\neq 0
for some
k\geq 2
. It is straightforward to verify that
\eta_{0}^{+}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}^{+}\in H^{m}
for
m>3
. If the coefficients of
\eta_{0}^{+}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{11}{18}-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}+(d_{2,k+2}^{+}-d_{2,k}^{+})}(\eta_{2,1}^{+}(0))^{2}\leq(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(0))^{2}\leq\frac{11}{18(d_{2,k+2}^{+}-d_{2,k}^{+})}(\eta_{2,1}^{+}(0))^{2},

then it follows that

\displaystyle 0\leq\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.

There,
\lambda_{1}
is an absolute constant satisfying
\frac{1}{50}<\lambda_{1}<\frac{3}{5}
, and
d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}<0
for all
k\geq 2
; see
Lemma

A.7
for more details.

Finally, we establish the nonlinear stability result
Theorem

3.10
for initial data
\eta^{+}_{0}
of the form
\eta^{+}_{0}(\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\eta_{2,2k}^{+}(0)e_{2,2k}
(i. e., initial data such that only the even coefficients in the Fourier expansion with respect to the basis
\{e_{2,k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are present). With this choice of initial data, the linearized system is stable in the space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
; combined with a continuity argument and delicate estimates, this yields the nonlinear stability of
Eq.

2.1
in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
.

Theorem 3.10

(Global well-posedness and stability for the nonlinear problem)
.

Let us suppose the initial value
\eta_{0}^{\pm}
satisfies
\eta_{0}^{+}(\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\eta_{2,2k}(0)e_{2,2k}
and
\eta_{0}^{-}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
. Let us define

\displaystyle I_{0}^{2}(t)\coloneqq\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2},\qquad\text{for $t\geq 0$}.

Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that if
I_{0}(0)\leq\varepsilon
, then the system
Eq.

2.7
with initial value
\eta_{0}^{\pm}
is globally well-posed. Moreover, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle I_{0}(t)\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}t}I_{0}(0),\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$.}

The proof of
Theorem

3.10
relies on the linear stability analysis, delicate estimates on the nonlinear terms, and a continuity argument.

4.
Global existence and uniqueness for the linearized problem

In this section, we prove
Theorem

3.1
, establishing the global existence and uniqueness of solutions to the system
Eq.

2.7
using Galerkin’s method. To obtain the decay estimates for the coefficients
\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}
,
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}
, and
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}
, as required in
Section

5
, we construct approximate solutions using the basis
\{e_{2,k}\}_{k\geq 1}
.

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Step 1.

Construction of approximate solutions.
Let us fix a positive integer
n
and define

(4.1)

\displaystyle\eta_{n}^{\pm}(t,\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}(t)e_{2,k},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}v_{n}^{\pm}(t,\theta)=H\eta_{n}^{\pm}(t,\theta).

We aim to determine the coefficients
\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}(t)
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{+},\partial_{\theta}e_{2,k}\rho_{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{\theta}L^{+}\eta_{n}^{+},\partial_{\theta}e_{2,k}\rho_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{-},\partial_{\theta}e_{2,k}\rho_{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{\theta}L^{-}\eta_{n}^{-},\partial_{\theta}e_{2,k}\rho_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}^{+},\partial_{\theta}e_{2,k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\eta_{2,k}^{+}(0),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}^{-},\partial_{\theta}e_{2,k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\eta_{2,k}^{-}(0)

for
0\leq t\leq T
, and
k=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Equations
Eqs.

–
4.4
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{2,l}^{+}(t)\sin((l+1)\theta),\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle-\left\langle-\frac{1}{2\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\theta}L^{+}\eta_{n}^{+},\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\eta_{2,l}^{-}(t)\sin((l+1)\theta),\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle-\left\langle-\frac{1}{2\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\theta}L^{-}\eta_{n}^{-},\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle=0,

where

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\theta}L^{+}\eta_{n}^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\cos(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta_{n}^{+}+2\cos(\theta)\eta_{n}^{+}+\cos(\theta)H(\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{+})+4\cos(\theta)v_{n}^{+},

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\sin(\theta)}\partial_{\theta}L^{-}\eta_{n}^{-}=\frac{1}{2}\cos(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta_{n}^{-}+2\cos(\theta)\eta_{n}^{-}-\cos(\theta)H(\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{-})-4\cos(\theta)v_{n}^{-}.

To simplify the notation, we set
u_{n}^{\pm}\coloneqq-\sqrt{\pi}\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{\pm}
and define

\displaystyle L_{1}^{+}(u_{n}^{+})

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sqrt{\pi}\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}L^{+}\eta_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+}-\cos^{2}(\theta)u_{n}^{+}-2\cos(\theta)H(\sin(\theta)u_{n}^{+})+2\cos(\theta)\eta_{n}^{+}+4\cos(\theta)v_{n}^{+},

\displaystyle L_{1}^{-}(u_{n}^{-})

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sqrt{\pi}\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}L^{-}\eta_{n}^{-}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{-}-\cos^{2}(\theta)u_{n}^{-}+2\cos(\theta)H(\sin(\theta)u_{n}^{-})+2\cos(\theta)\eta_{n}^{-}-4\cos(\theta)v_{n}^{-}.

Then ordinary differential equations
Eqs.

4.3
–
4.4
are equivalent to

(4.7)

\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{t}u_{n}^{+},\sin((k+1)\theta)\big\rangle-\big\langle L_{1}^{+}(u_{n}^{+}),\sin((k+1)\theta)\big\rangle=0,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\big\langle\partial_{t}u_{n}^{-},\sin((k+1)\theta)\big\rangle-\big\langle L_{1}^{-}(u_{n}^{-}),\sin((k+1)\theta)\big\rangle=0.

We may verify that
Eqs.

4.7
–
4.8
hold for
k=0
, using the convention
\eta_{2,0}^{\pm}(t)=0
. The standard existence theory for ordinary differential equations then guarantees the existence of a unique sequence of absolutely continuous functions
\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}(t)
, with
k=1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,n
) satisfying
Eqs.

4.3
–
4.4
and the initial conditions
Eqs.

4.5
–
4.6
in
0\leq t\leq T
.

Step 2.

Energy estimates.
We need several estimates on
u_{n}^{\pm}
and their derivatives, which are proven below.

Step 2 a.

L^{2}
-estimates of
u_{n}^{\pm}
.
Multiplying
Eq.

4.7
by
\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)
and
Eq.

4.8
by
\eta_{2,k}^{-}(t)
, and then summing up over
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=\left\langle L_{1}^{+}(u_{n}^{+}),u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle L_{1}^{-}(u_{n}^{-}),u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and
Lemma

A.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle 2\cos(\theta)H(\sin(\theta)u_{n}^{\pm}),u_{n}^{\pm}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|H(\sin(\theta)u_{n}^{\pm})\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\eta_{n}^{\pm}(0)=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle 2\cos(\theta)\eta_{n}^{\pm},u_{n}^{\pm}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\eta_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ \|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\eta_{n}^{\pm}
and
v_{n}^{\pm}
are odd functions, we apply Poincaré’s inequality together with Hölder’s inequality to deduce that

\displaystyle\left\langle 4\cos(\theta)v_{n}^{\pm},u_{n}^{\pm}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\eta_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|u_{n}^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{n}^{-}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq C\left(\|u_{n}^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{n}^{-}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right),

which means that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|u_{n}^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{n}^{-}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq C

for any
T>0
where
C
is a positive constant independent of
n
.

Step 2 b.

H^{m}
-estimates of
u_{n}^{\pm}
(for
m\geq 1
).

Multiplying equation
Eq.

4.7
by
(k+1)^{2m}\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)
and equation
Eq.

4.8
by
(k+1)^{2m}\eta_{2,k}^{-}(t)
, summing up over
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, and applying integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)=\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}L_{1}^{+}(u_{n}^{+}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}L_{1}^{-}(u_{n}^{-}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle.

Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}^{m}L_{1}^{+}(u_{n}^{+})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{m+1}u_{n}^{+}-(m\cos(2\theta)+\cos^{2}(\theta))\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}-2\cos(\theta)H(\sin(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+})+\text{l.\,o.\,t.},

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}^{m}L_{1}^{-}(u_{n}^{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{m+1}u_{n}^{-}+(m\cos(2\theta)+\cos^{2}(\theta))\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}+2\cos(\theta)H(\sin(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-})+\text{l.\,o.\,t.},

where “l. o. t.” denotes
lower-order terms
whose
L^{2}
-norms can be bounded by
\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{H^{m-1}}
. Taking
\eta^{+}_{n}
as an example. For the term
\cos(\theta)H(\sin(\theta)u_{n}^{+})
, using
Lemma

A.4
, we yield

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m-k}(\cos(\theta))H(\partial_{\theta}^{k}(\sin(\theta)u_{n}^{+}))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|H(\partial_{\theta}^{k}(\sin(\theta)u_{n}^{+}))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}^{k}(\sin(\theta)u_{n}^{+})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{H^{k}}

for
0\leq k\leq m-1
. For the term
\cos(\theta)\eta^{+}_{n}
, since

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}^{m}\big(\cos(\theta)\eta^{+}_{n}\big)=\sum_{k\geq 0}^{m}\partial_{\theta}^{m-k}\cos(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{k}\eta^{+}_{n}.

If
k=0
, we apply Poincaré’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}\cos(\theta)\eta^{+}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\eta^{+}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho_{2}^{-1/2}\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u^{+}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}.

If
1\leq k\leq m
, since
u_{n}^{+}=-\sqrt{\pi}\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m-k}\cos(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{k}\eta^{+}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{\theta}^{m-k}\cos(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{k-1}(\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}_{n})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{\theta}^{m-k}\cos(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{k-1}(-2\sin(\theta)u^{+}_{n})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{k-1}}.

Similarly, we obtain the other
lower-order terms
whose
L^{2}
-norms can be bounded by
\|u_{n}^{\+}\|_{H^{m-1}}
.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and
Lemma

A.4
, we estimate

\displaystyle\left\langle 2\cos(\theta)H(\sin(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\pm}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\pm}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2\|H(\sin(\theta)\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\pm})\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq C\left(\|u_{n}^{+}(t)\|_{H^{m}}+\|u_{n}^{-}(t)\|_{H^{m}}\right),

which leads to the uniform estimate

(4.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|u_{n}^{\pm}(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq C,

where
C
is a constant depending only on
m
,
\mathbb{T}
, initial data
u_{0}^{\pm}
, and
T>0
.

Step 2 c.

L^{2}
-estimates of

We multiply

\phi_{2,k}^{+}(t)
and equation
Eq.

4.8
by
\phi_{2,k}^{-}(t)
, sum over
k=0,1,2,\ldots,n
, and integrate with respect to
t
. We then deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\left\langle\partial_{t}u_{n}^{\pm}(t),\phi^{\pm}(t)\right\rangle-\left\langle L_{1}^{\pm}(u_{n}^{\pm})(t),\phi^{\pm}(t)\right\rangle\Big)\mathrm{d}t=0.

Since the sequence
\{u_{n}^{\pm}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
u^{\pm}
weakly in
L^{2}([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{T}))
and
\{\partial_{t}u_{n}^{\pm}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\partial_{t}u^{\pm}
weakly in
L^{2}([0,T];H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T}))
, it follows that

(4.22)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\left\langle\partial_{t}u^{\pm}(t),\phi^{\pm}(t)\right\rangle-\left\langle L_{1}^{\pm}(u^{\pm})(t),\phi^{\pm}(t)\right\rangle\Big)\mathrm{d}t=0.

Since functions of the form
Eq.

4.21
are dense in
C^{1}([0,T];C^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
, equation
Eq.

4.22
holds for all
\phi^{\pm}\in C^{1}([0,T];C^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
. Combining the coefficient estimates
Eqs.

4.18
–
4.20
for
\eta_{n}^{\pm}
, we deduce that
\{u_{n}^{\pm}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
strongly converges to
u^{\pm}=-\sqrt{\pi}\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{\pm}
in
C([0,T];H^{m}(\mathbb{T}))
.

Step 4.

Uniqueness.

If both
\eta_{1}^{\pm}
and
\eta_{2}^{\pm}
are solutions to
Eq.

2.7
with the same initial data
\eta_{0}^{\pm}
, then applying Grönwall’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\|\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{1}^{\pm}-\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{2}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\leq e^{CT}\|\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}^{\pm}-\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta_{0}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}=0,

which implies the uniqueness of the solution to
Eq.

2.7
, thereby completing the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.
∎

5.
Instability for the linearized problem

In this section, we study the instability of the linearized problem
Eq.

2.7
and prove
Theorem

3.3
.
The solution
\eta^{\pm}
to
Eq.

2.7
obtained in
Theorem

3.1
can be expressed as
\eta^{\pm}(t,\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}(t)e_{2,k}
, using the orthonormal basis
\{e_{2,k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of the weighted space
\mathcal{H}_{2}
(which is recalled in
Section

2.2
). The key advantage of this representation is that the inner product
\left\langle L^{\pm}\eta^{\pm},\eta^{\pm}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}

can be expressed as a series involving only the sums of squares of the expansion coefficients
\eta^{\pm}_{2,k}(t)
, without cross terms such as
\eta^{\pm}_{2,k}(t)\eta^{\pm}_{2,k+2}(t)
.

5.1.
Linear estimate on
\eta^{-}

Direct computation yields

\displaystyle L^{-}\sin(k\theta)=a_{2,k}^{-}\sin((k+2)\theta)+b_{2,k}^{+}\sin((k-2)\theta),\qquad k>2,

where

\displaystyle a_{2,k}^{-}\coloneqq-\frac{(k+2)(k-2)}{4k},\qquad b_{2,k}^{-}\coloneqq\frac{(k+2)^{2}}{4k}.

For
k=1
, we have

\displaystyle L^{-}\sin(\theta)=\frac{3}{4}\sin(3\theta)-\frac{9}{4}\sin(\theta);

for
k=2
, we have

\displaystyle L^{-}\sin(2\theta)=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle L^{-}e_{2,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+2}L^{-}\sin((k+2)\theta)-\frac{1}{k}L^{-}\sin(k\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+2}\left(a_{2,k+2}^{-}\sin((k+4)\theta)+b_{2,k+2}^{-}\sin(k\theta)\right)-\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{2,k}^{-}\sin((k+2)\theta)+b_{2,k}^{-}\sin((k-2)\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(k+4)^{2}k}{4(k+2)^{2}}\frac{\sin((k+4)\theta)}{k+4}+\frac{(k+4)^{2}k}{4(k+2)^{2}}\frac{\sin(k\theta)}{k}+\frac{(k+2)^{2}(k-2)}{4k^{2}}\frac{\sin((k+2)\theta)}{k+2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(k+2)^{2}(k-2)}{4k^{2}}\frac{\sin((k-2)\theta)}{k-2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(k+4)^{2}k}{4(k+2)^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\sin((k+4)\theta)}{k+4}-\frac{\sin((k+2)\theta)}{k+2}\bigg)+\frac{(k+2)^{2}(k-2)}{4k^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\sin(k\theta)}{k}-\frac{\sin((k-2)\theta)}{k-2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg(-\frac{(k+4)^{2}k}{4(k+2)^{2}}+\frac{(k+2)^{2}(k-2)}{4k^{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\sin((k+2)\theta)}{k+2}-\frac{\sin(k\theta)}{k}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(k+4)^{2}k}{4(k+2)^{2}}e_{2,k+2}+\bigg(-\frac{(k+4)^{2}k}{4(k+2)^{2}}+\frac{(k+2)^{2}(k-2)}{4k^{2}}\bigg)e_{2,k}+\frac{(k+2)^{2}(k-2)}{4k^{2}}e_{2,k-2},

i. e.,

\displaystyle L^{-}e_{2,k}=-d_{2,k+2}^{-}e_{2,k+2}+(-d_{2,k+2}^{-}+d_{2,k}^{-})e_{2,k}+d_{2,k}^{-}e_{2,k-2},

where

\displaystyle d_{2,k}^{-}\coloneqq\frac{(k+2)^{2}(k-2)}{4k^{2}}.

Since
d_{2,2}^{-}=0
, the above equality holds for all
k\geq 2
. For
k=1
, we have

\displaystyle L^{-}e_{2,1}=-d_{2,3}^{-}e_{2,3}+(-d_{2,3}^{-}+d_{2,1}^{-})e_{2,1}.

Expanding

\displaystyle\eta^{-}(t,\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\eta_{2,k}^{-}(t)e_{2,k},

then
\partial_{t}\eta^{-}=L^{-}\eta^{-}
reduces to the following infinite-dimensional system of ordinary differential equations

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\eta_{2,k}^{-}(t)=-d_{2,k}^{-}\eta_{2,k-2}^{-}(t)+(d_{2,k}^{-}-d_{2,k+2}^{-})\eta_{2,k}^{-}(t)+d_{2,k+2}^{-}\eta_{2,k+2}^{-}(t),\quad\text{for $k\geq 1$.}

where
\eta_{2,-1}^{-}(t)
and
\eta_{2,0}^{-}(t)
are understood to be zero. Consequently, we deduce (formally)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{k\geq 1}(\eta_{2,k}^{-})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}-d_{2,k}^{-}\eta_{2,k-2}^{-}\eta_{2,k}^{-}+(d_{2,k}^{-}-d_{2,k+2}^{-})(\eta_{2,k}^{-})^{2}+d_{2,k+2}\eta_{2,k}^{-}\eta_{2,k+2}^{-}

(5.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}(d_{2,k}^{-}-d_{2,k+2}^{-})(\eta_{2,k}^{-})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}(\eta_{2,k}^{-})^{2}.

Here, we have used the fact that

\displaystyle d_{2,k}^{-}-d_{2,k+2}^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+2)^{2}(k-2)}{4k^{2}}-\frac{(k+4)^{2}k}{4(k+2)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2k^{2}+12k+8}{(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}

\displaystyle<-\frac{1}{2}.

Several approaches are available to justify the following formal computations (the summations involved may not converge). One option is to invoke the decay estimates for the coefficient
\eta_{2,k}^{-}(t)
to ensure convergence. Alternatively, one may appeal to standard linear semigroup theory, as outlined below.

Let us consider the real Hilbert space
\mathcal{W}
, formally spanned by the orthonormal basis

\{e_{2,k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\coloneqq\{\eta=\eta_{2,k}(t)e_{2,k}:\{e_{2,k}\}_{k\geq 1}\in\ell^{2}\}.

Then
L^{-}
defines an unbounded, closed operator on
\mathcal{W}
. By a direct application of Hille–Yosida’s theorem (see
[
21
, Theorem II.3.8]
), it follows that
L^{-}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup with the desired decay estimate:

\displaystyle\|e^{tL^{-}}\eta(0)\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}t}\|\eta(0)\|_{\mathcal{W}},\qquad\text{for $t\geq 0$}.

5.2.
Linear estimate on
\eta^{+}

Similarly to
Section

5.1
, we have

\displaystyle L^{+}\sin(k\theta)=a_{2,k}^{+}\sin((k+2)\theta)+b_{2,k}^{+}\sin((k-2)\theta),\qquad k>2,

where

\displaystyle a_{2,k}^{+}\coloneqq-\frac{(k-2)^{2}}{4k},\qquad b_{2,k}^{+}\coloneqq\frac{(k+2)(k-2)}{4k}.

For
k=1
, we have

\displaystyle L^{+}\sin(\theta)=-\frac{1}{4}\sin(3\theta)+\frac{3}{4}\sin(\theta).

For
k=2
, we have

\displaystyle L^{+}\sin(2\theta)=0.

It follows that

\displaystyle L^{+}e_{2,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{2,k+2}^{+}}{k+2}\sin((k+4)\theta)+\frac{b_{k+2}^{+}}{k+2}\sin(k\theta)-\frac{a_{k}^{+}}{k}\sin((k+2)\theta)-\frac{b_{k}^{+}}{k}\sin((k-2)\theta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{2}(k+4)}{4(k+2)^{2}}e_{2,k+2}+\bigg(\frac{(k-2)^{2}(k+2)}{4\kappa^{2}}-\frac{-\kappa^{2}(k+4)}{4(k+2)^{2}}\bigg)e_{2,k}+\frac{(k-2)^{2}(k+2)}{4\kappa^{2}}e_{2,k-2},

which is

\displaystyle L^{+}e_{2,k}=-d_{2,k+2}^{+}e_{2,k+2}+(-d_{2,k+2}^{+}+d_{2,k}^{+})e_{2,k}+d_{k}^{+}e_{2,k-2},\qquad\text{for $k>2$,}

where

\displaystyle d_{2,k}^{+}\coloneqq\frac{(k-2)^{2}(k+2)}{4k^{2}}.

Since
d_{2,2}^{+}=0
, the above identity holds for all
k\geq 2
. In the case
k=1
, we obtain the following.

\displaystyle L^{+}e_{2,1}=-d_{2,3}e_{2,3}+(-d_{2,3}+d_{2,1})e_{2,1}.

We note that

\displaystyle d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{-k^{2}(k+4)}{4(k+2)^{2}}+\frac{(k-2)^{2}(k+2)}{4k^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k^{4}+4k^{3}+8k^{2}-8k-16}{2(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\kappa^{2}-4k-8}{(k+2)^{2}k^{2}}.

This implies that
-d_{2,3}^{+}+d_{2,1}^{+}=\frac{11}{18}>0
, while, for
k\geq 2
, we have
-d_{2,k+2}^{+}+d_{2,k}^{+}\leq-\frac{3}{8}
.

Remark 5.1

(Stability for a suitable class of initial data)
.

Following the discussion in
[
32
, Section 4]
, let us suppose the initial data
\eta^{+}_{0}
is of the form
\eta^{+}_{0}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\eta^{+}_{2,2k}(0)e_{2,2k}
. In this particular case, we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{k\geq 1}(\eta^{+}_{2,2k})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}-d_{2,2k}^{+}\eta_{2,2k-2}^{+}\eta_{2,2k}^{+}+(d_{2,2k}^{+}-d_{2,2k+2}^{+})(\eta_{2,2k}^{+})^{2}+d_{2,2k+2}^{+}\eta_{2,2k}^{+}\eta_{2,2k+2}^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}(d_{2,2k}^{+}-d_{2,2k+2}^{+})(\eta_{2,2k}^{+})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3}{8}\sum_{k\geq 1}(\eta_{2,2k}^{-})^{2},

where we have used

\displaystyle d_{2,2k}^{+}-d_{2,2k+2}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(2k)^{2}(2k+4)}{4(2k+2)^{2}}+\frac{(2k-2)^{2}(2k+2)}{4(2k)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k^{2}(k+2)}{2(k+1)^{2}}+\frac{(k-1)^{2}(k+1)}{2k^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k^{2}-k-1}{2(k+1)^{2}k^{2}}\leq-\frac{3}{8}.

This implies exponential decay of
\eta^{+}
in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
for such initial data.

5.3.
Proof of the instability result for the linearized problem

Building on
Sections

5.1
–
5.2
, we now establish
Theorem

3.3
.

While
\eta^{-}
satisfies an exponential-decay estimate (see
Section

5.1
), the situation of
\eta^{+}
is more delicate since the coefficients
d_{k}^{+}-d_{k+2}^{+}
arising in
Section

5.2
change sign. If the odd Fourier coefficients of
\eta_{0}^{+}
vanish, then exponential stability holds (as proven in
Remark

5.1
). In the general case, we first show that the expansion coefficients
\eta^{+}_{2,k}(t)
of the solution
\eta^{+}(t,\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)e_{2,k}
, obtained in
Theorem

3.1
, satisfy the infinite-dimensional ODE system
Eq.

5.2
; we then derive a second-order differential inequality
Eq.

5.9
for the norm
\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
which yields exponential-in-time growth for a broad class of initial data, implying the instability of
Eq.

2.7
.

Depending on these different choices of the initial data
\eta_{0}^{+}
, we later analyze the stability and instability of the nonlinear system
Eq.

2.6
as well (in
Section

7
and
Section

8
, respectively).

Proof of
Theorem

3.3
.

Consider the solution
(\eta^{+},\eta^{-})
obtained in
Theorem

3.1
. Here,
\eta^{+}
admits the expansion

\displaystyle\eta^{+}(t,\theta)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)e_{2,k}.

Note that the coefficients
\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)
appearing here may differ from those in
Eq.

4.1
, the decay estimates stated in
Eq.

4.18
–
Eq.

4.20
remain valid for both sets of coefficients, due to the convergence of the approximate solutions and the uniqueness of the solutions.

Step 1.

The infinite dimensional ODE system for
\eta^{+}
.

Owing to
Eqs.

4.18
–
4.20
, then
\partial_{t}\eta^{+}=L^{+}\eta^{+}
reduces to the following infinite-dimensional ordinary differential equation (ODE) system

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)=-d_{2,k}^{+}\eta_{2,k-2}^{+}(t)+(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+})\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)+d_{2,k+2}^{+}\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t),\qquad\text{for $k\geq 1$.}

Here,
\eta_{2,-1}^{+}(t)
and
\eta_{2,0}^{+}(t)
are understood to be 0.

Step 2.

The quadratic form on
\eta_{2,k}^{+}
and
\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}
.

In this step, we show that the non-trivial solution
\eta^{+}
obtained in
Theorem

3.1
satisfies a second-order ordinary differential inequality. By applying the decay estimate for the coefficients of
\eta^{+}(t,\theta)
given in
Eqs.

4.18
–
4.20
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\right|\leq\frac{C}{k^{2m-1}},\qquad\text{for all $t>0$, $k\geq 3$, and any integer $m>3$. }

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left\langle L^{+}\eta^{+},\eta^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}.

We multiply
Eq.

5.2
by
(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+})\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)
to obtain

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+})\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}&=-d_{2,k}^{+}(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+})\eta_{2,k-2}^{+}(t)\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\\
&\quad+(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+})^{2}\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+d_{2,k+2}^{+}(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+})\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t).\end{aligned}

Considering the sum

\displaystyle S_{n}^{+}(t)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}

and using
Eq.

5.3
, we get

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S_{n}^{+}(t)&\coloneqq\left(d_{2,1}^{+}-d_{2,3}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,1}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(d_{2,2}^{+}-d_{2,4}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,2}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}Q_{k}(t)\\
&\quad+\left(d_{2,n-1}^{+}-d_{2,n+1}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,n-1}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(d_{2,n}-d_{2,n+2}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,n}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+R_{n-1}(t)+R_{n}(t).\end{aligned}

Here, the quadratic form
Q_{k}(t)
is defined as

\displaystyle Q_{k}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(d_{2,k+2}^{+}-d_{2,k+4}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\left(d_{2,k}^{+}d_{2,k+2}^{+}+d_{2,k+2}^{+}d_{2,k+4}^{+}-2(d_{2,k+2}^{+})^{2}\right)\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t),\quad\text{for $k=1,2,\ldots,n-2$.}

The remainder term
R_{k}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle R_{k}(t)\coloneqq 2d_{2,k+2}^{+}\left(d_{2,k}^{+}-d_{2,k+2}^{+}\right)\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t),\quad\text{for $k=n-1,\,n$}

Applying the estimates for coefficients
\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)
in
Eqs.

4.18
–
4.20
, we obtain

(5.5)

\displaystyle|R_{k}(t)|\leq\frac{C(m,\mathbb{T},\eta^{+}_{0})}{k^{2m-1}},\qquad\text{for all $t>0$.}

Consequently, both
R_{n-1}(t)
and
R_{n}(t)
tend to zero as
n\rightarrow+\infty
.

For the estimate of the quadratic form
Q_{k}(t)
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\inf}\left[\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\right]\leq Q_{k}(t)\leq\lambda_{\sup}\left[\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\right],\quad\text{for $k\geq 1$.}

Substituting
Eq.

5.6
into
Eq.

5.4
yields

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S_{n}^{+}(t)&\leq\left(d_{2,1}^{+}-d_{2,3}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,1}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(d_{2,2}^{+}-d_{2,4}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,2}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\lambda_{\sup}\left[\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(\eta_{2,k+2}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\quad+\left(d_{2,n-1}^{+}-d_{2,n+1}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,n-1}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+\left(d_{2,n}^{+}-d_{2,n+2}^{+}\right)^{2}\left(\eta_{2,n}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+R_{n-1}(t)+R_{n}(t)\\
&\leq 2\lambda_{\sup}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+R_{n-1}(t)+R_{n}(t)\end{aligned}

and

(5.8)

\displaystyle S_{n}^{+}(t)\geq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{\inf}\left(\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)\right)^{2}+R_{n-1}(t)+R_{n}(t).

Combining with
Eq.

5.7
and
Eq.

5.8
, and letting
n\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain a second-order ordinary differential inequality for
t\mapsto\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
:

(5.9)

\displaystyle 4\lambda_{1}\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}<\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}<4\lambda_{2}\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2},

where we have used the decay property of
R_{k}(t)
in
Eq.

5.5
for
k=1,2,\ldots
, and
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
are two absolute constants as in
Lemma

A.7
.

Step 3.

The second-ordinary differential inequality for
\eta^{+}
.

In this step, we apply the comparison result in
Lemma

A.6
to the differential inequality
Eq.

5.9
. To begin with, we first consider the following second-order ordinary differential equation

(5.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}u(t)=4\lambda_{i}u(t),\qquad&t\geq 0,\\
u(0)=0,\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u(0)=1,&\end{cases}\qquad\text{(for $i=1,\,2$).}

The solution to
Eq.

5.10
is given explicitly by

\displaystyle u(t)=\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}e^{2\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}t}-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}e^{-2\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}t}>0,\quad\text{for all $t>0$ and $i=1,\,2$.}

We now proceed to solve the following problem:

(5.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}y(t)=4\lambda_{i}y(t),\qquad&t\geq 0,\\
y(0)=\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}},\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y(0)=2\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}&\end{cases}\qquad\qquad\text{(for $i=1,\,2$).}

The solution to
Eq.

5.11
is given explicitly by

\displaystyle y(t)=E_{i}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}{2}e^{2\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}t}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}}{2}e^{-{2\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}t}},\qquad\text{(for $i=1,\,2$).}

For
t>0
and
i=1,\,2
, we have

\displaystyle E_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\cosh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}t)+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\sinh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}t)

\displaystyle>0,

which remains strictly positive provided that

\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}>0\quad\text{(i.\,e., $\eta_{0}^{+}\neq 0$)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\geq-\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}>0.

Since

\displaystyle\cosh(x)=\frac{e^{x}+e^{-x}}{2},\qquad\sinh(x)=\frac{e^{x}-e^{-x}}{2},

and both
x\mapsto\cosh(x)
and
x\mapsto\tfrac{\sinh(x)}{x}
are strictly increasing functions for
x>0
, we deduce that, for all

\displaystyle E_{2}(t)-E_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\cosh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}t)+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}}\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\sinh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}t)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\cosh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}t)-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\sinh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\left(\cosh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}t)-\cosh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}t)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}}\sinh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}t)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\sinh(2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}t)\right)

\displaystyle>0,

provided that

(i. e.,

Remark 5.2

(Higher excited states on the torus)
.

Dealing with case of arbitrary
\kappa\geq 3
is much more involved. Since the linear structure of
\eta^{+}
is identical to that of the De Gregorio model, we illustrate the difficulties using
Eq.

1.15
.

Let us define

\displaystyle\omega(t,\theta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sin(\kappa\,\theta)+\eta(t,\theta),

\displaystyle t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},

\displaystyle u(t,\theta)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\kappa}\sin(\kappa\,\theta)+v(t,\theta),

\displaystyle t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},

Then
Eq.

1.15
can be rewritten as a partial differential equation in
\eta
as follows:

(5.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta=\{\frac{1}{\kappa}\eta+v,\sin(\kappa\theta)\}+\{\eta,v\},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{\theta}v=H\eta,&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

For any fixed integer
\kappa\geq 1
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}\coloneqq\left\{\eta\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}):\ \eta\text{ is odd}\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{|\partial_{\theta}\eta|^{2}}{|\sin\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}\theta\right)|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty\right\},

which is a Hilbert space equipped with the inner product

(5.13)

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\coloneqq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\rho_{\kappa}\,\partial_{\theta}\xi\,\partial_{\theta}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\theta,\qquad\text{where }\ \rho_{\kappa}\coloneqq\frac{1}{4\pi\sin^{2}(\frac{\kappa}{2}\theta)}.

The family
\{e_{\kappa,l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
forms a complete orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
, where

\displaystyle e_{\kappa,l}\coloneqq\frac{\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)}{l+\kappa}-\frac{\sin(l\theta)}{l},\qquad l\geq 1.

For a fixed integer
\kappa\,\geq 1
, direct computation yields

\displaystyle L_{\kappa}\sin(l\theta)=\begin{cases}A_{\kappa,l}\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)+B_{\kappa,l}\sin((l-\kappa)\theta),&\text{if }l>\kappa,\\
0,&\text{if }l=\kappa,\\
A_{\kappa,l}\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)-B_{\kappa,l}\sin((\kappa-l)\theta),&\text{if }l<\kappa,\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle A_{\kappa,l}\coloneqq-\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}}{2\kappa\,l},\qquad B_{\kappa,l}\coloneqq\frac{(l+\kappa)(l-\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l}.

It follows that

\displaystyle L_{\kappa}e_{\kappa,l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{l+\kappa}L_{\kappa}\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)-\frac{1}{l}L_{\kappa}\sin(l\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{\kappa,l+\kappa}}{l+\kappa}\sin((l+2\kappa)\theta)+\frac{B_{\kappa,l+\kappa}}{l+\kappa}L_{\kappa}\sin(l\theta)-\frac{A_{\kappa,l}}{l}\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)-\frac{B_{\kappa,l}}{l}L_{\kappa}\sin((l-\kappa)\theta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{l^{2}(l+2\kappa)}{2k(l+\kappa)^{2}}\frac{\sin((l+2\kappa)\theta)}{l+2\kappa}+\frac{l^{2}(l+2\kappa)}{2k(l+\kappa)^{2}}\frac{\sin(l\theta)}{l}+\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}\frac{\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)}{l+\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}\frac{\sin((l-\kappa)\theta)}{l-\kappa}

\displaystyle=-\frac{l^{2}(l+2\kappa)}{2k(l+\kappa)^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\sin((l+2\kappa)\theta)}{l+2\kappa}-\frac{\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)}{l+\kappa}\bigg)-\frac{l^{2}(l+2\kappa)}{2k(l+\kappa)^{2}}\frac{\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)}{l+\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{l^{2}(l+2\kappa)}{2k(l+\kappa)^{2}}\frac{\sin(l\theta)}{l}+\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}\frac{\sin((l+\kappa)\theta)}{l+\kappa}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\sin(l\theta)}{l}-\frac{\sin((l-\kappa)\theta)}{l-\kappa}\bigg)-\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}\frac{\sin(l\theta)}{l}

\displaystyle=-\frac{l^{2}(l+2\kappa)}{2k(l+\kappa)^{2}}e_{\kappa,l+\kappa}+\bigg[\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}-\frac{l^{2}(l+2\kappa)}{2k(l+\kappa)^{2}}\bigg]e_{\kappa,l}+\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}e_{\kappa,l-\kappa}

i. e.,

\displaystyle L_{\kappa}e_{\kappa,l}

\displaystyle\coloneqq d_{\kappa,l+\kappa}e_{\kappa,l+\kappa}+\big(d_{\kappa,l}-d_{\kappa,l+\kappa}\big)e_{\kappa,l}+d_{\kappa,l}e_{\kappa,l-\kappa},\qquad\text{for $l\geq 1$,}

where

\displaystyle d_{\kappa,l}\coloneqq\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}.

Formally, if the solution
\eta
to
Eq.

5.12
can be written as
\eta(t,\theta)=\sum_{l\geq 1}\eta_{\kappa,l}(t)e_{\kappa,l}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{l\geq 1}\left(\eta_{\kappa,l}(t)\right)^{2}=\left\langle L_{\kappa}\eta,\eta\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}=\sum_{l\geq 1}\left(d_{\kappa,l}-d_{\kappa,l+\kappa}\right)\left(\eta_{\kappa,l}(t)\right)^{2},

where

\displaystyle d_{\kappa,l}-d_{\kappa,l+\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{(l-\kappa)^{2}(l+\kappa)}{2\kappa\,l^{2}}-\frac{l^{2}(l+2\kappa)}{2k(l+\kappa)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-l^{4}-2\kappa\,l^{3}-2\kappa^{2}l^{2}+\kappa^{3}l+\kappa^{4}}{2l^{2}(l+\kappa)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}(-l^{2}+\kappa\,l+\kappa^{2})}{2l^{2}(l+\kappa)^{2}}.

Since the coefficients
d_{\kappa,l}-d_{\kappa,l+\kappa}
change signs for fixed integer
\kappa\geq 3
, a finer analysis is required.

We will address this problem (for both the De Gregorio model
Eq.

1.15
and the one-dimensional MHD model
Eq.

1.1
) in a forthcoming work.

The main difficulties arise from the study of the operator

in

, Theorem 1.2]
(cf.
[

); however, unlike the approach in

, where weighted estimates are performed in the function space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}\coloneqq\bigg\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}):\ f(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{|\partial_{\theta}f|^{2}}{|\sin(\theta/2)|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty\bigg\},

and instead considers the boundedness of the operator

(which is endowed with a stronger singular weight function,

We begin by rewriting the operator
Q
in a form that makes its nonlocal structure explicit

\displaystyle Qf

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sin(2\theta)Hf+\cos(2\theta)f

\displaystyle=\cos(2\theta)f+\sin(2\theta)\left[Hf(\theta)-Hf(-\theta)\right]+\sin(2\theta)Hf(-\theta).

To handle the difference
Hf(\theta)-Hf(-\theta)
, we observe that

\displaystyle Hf(\theta)-Hf(-\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cot\left(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)f(\varphi)\mathrm{d}\varphi-\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cot\left(\frac{-\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)f(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle=\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cot\left(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)f(\varphi)\mathrm{d}\varphi+\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cot\left(\frac{\theta+\varphi}{2}\right)f(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle=\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left[\cot\left(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)+\cot\left(\frac{\theta+\varphi}{2}\right)\right]f(\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sin(\theta)\,\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\sin(\varphi)}{\sin\left(\frac{\theta+\varphi}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)}\frac{f(\varphi)}{\sin(\varphi)}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi.

Applying this identity, together with Hölder’s inequality,
Lemma

A.5
, and
Lemma

A.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\frac{\partial_{\theta}(Qf)}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\frac{\cos(2\theta)\partial_{\theta}f-2\sin(2\theta)f}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\frac{2\cos(2\theta)Hf(-\theta)+\sin(2\theta)H(\partial_{\theta}f)(-\theta)}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\frac{2\cos(2\theta)\left[Hf(\theta)-Hf(-\theta)\right]+\sin(2\theta)\left[H(\partial_{\theta}f)(\theta)-H(\partial_{\theta}f)(-\theta)\right]}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\frac{\cos(2\theta)\partial_{\theta}f-2\sin(2\theta)f}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\frac{\sin(2\theta)H\partial_{\theta}f}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\frac{\cos(2\theta)\left[Hf(\theta)-Hf(-\theta)\right]}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|H\partial_{\theta}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\bigg\|\frac{f}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\sin(\varphi)}{\sin\left(\frac{\theta+\varphi}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)}\frac{f(\varphi)}{\sin(\varphi)}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|H\partial_{\theta}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\bigg\|\frac{f}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2},\qquad\text{for any $f\in\mathcal{H}_{2}$},

where we used that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg|\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\sin(\varphi)}{\sin\left(\frac{\theta+\varphi}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)}\frac{f(\varphi)}{\sin(\varphi)}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim\bigg\|\frac{f}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

noticing that

\displaystyle[-\pi,\pi]\ni\theta\mapsto\mathrm{p.\,v.\,}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\sin(\varphi)}{\sin\left(\frac{\theta+\varphi}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\theta-\varphi}{2}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}\varphi

is well-defined and finite.
5
5

5

Indeed, the integrand exhibits singularities at
\varphi=\pm\theta
, where the denominator vanishes, and from these points, it is smooth and bounded. A local expansion near these points shows that the integrand behaves like a Cauchy-type kernel of order

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}h_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{l}(2k)^{2m}\left(\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\frac{-\eta_{2,2j-1}^{\pm}(t)+\eta_{2,2j+1}^{\pm}(t)}{(2j+1)^{2}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{l}(2k-1)^{2m}\left(\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\frac{-\eta_{2,2j-2}^{\pm}(t)+\eta_{2,2j}^{\pm}(t)}{(2j)^{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l}(2k)^{2m}\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\left((\eta_{2,2j-1})^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(2j+1)^{4}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{l}(2k-1)^{2m}\sum_{j\geq k}^{l}\left((\tilde{\eta}_{2j-2})^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(2j)^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}^{l}\sum_{k\leq j}\left((\eta_{2,2j-1}^{\pm}(t))^{2}\cdot\frac{(2k)^{2m}}{(2j+1)^{4}}\right)+\sum_{j\geq 1}^{l}\sum_{k\leq j}\left((\eta_{2,2j-2}^{\pm}(t))^{2}\cdot\frac{(2k-1)^{2m}}{(2j)^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}^{l}(\eta_{2,2j-1}^{\pm}(t))^{2}\cdot j\cdot\frac{(2j)^{2m}}{(2j+1)^{4}}+\sum_{j\geq 1}^{l}(\eta_{2,2j-2}^{\pm}(t))^{2}\cdot j\cdot\frac{(2j-1)^{2m}}{(2j)^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k+1)^{2m-4+1}(\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k+1)^{2m-2}(\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}u_{n}^{\pm}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

which yields

(6.10)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}h_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\partial_{\theta}^{m-1}u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\text{for $m\geq 1$.}

The ordinary differential equations
Eqs.

6.3
–
6.4
are equivalent to

(6.11)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}u_{n}^{+},\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle-\left\langle L_{1}^{+}(u_{n}^{+}),\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{N}_{1}(u_{n}^{+}),\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle,

(6.12)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{t}u_{n}^{-},\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle-\left\langle L_{1}^{-}(u_{n}^{\pm}),\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{N}_{2}(u_{n}^{\pm}),\sin((k+1)\theta)\right\rangle.

By the standard existence theory for ordinary differential equations, there exists a
T(k)>0
, which may depend on
k
, and a unique set of absolutely continuous functions
\eta_{2,k}^{\pm}(t)
, with
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, satisfying
Eqs.

6.3
–
6.4
and
Eqs.

6.6
–
6.5
for
0\leq t\leq T(k)
.

Step 2.

Energy estimates.
We need several estimates on
u_{n}^{\pm}
and their derivatives, which are proven below.

Step 2 a.

L^{2}
-estimates of
u_{n}^{\pm}
.
Multiplying
Eq.

6.11
by
\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)
and
Eq.

6.12
by
\eta_{2,k}^{-}(t)
, and then summing up over
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=\left\langle L_{1}^{+}(u_{n}^{+}),u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle L_{1}^{-}(u_{n}^{-}),u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{N}_{1}(u_{n}^{\pm}),u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{N}_{2}(u_{n}^{\pm}),u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle.

We note that
v_{n}^{\pm}
are odd and periodic functions, and hence satisfy
v_{n}(\pi)^{\pm}=v_{n}^{\pm}(0)
. Moreover, since
\sin(\theta)\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\min\{\theta,\pi-\theta\}
for
\theta\in[0,\pi]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|h_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\bigg\|\frac{v_{n}^{\pm}}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}

by Lagrange’s mean value theorem. Then, applying Poincaré’s inequality and
Lemma

A.4
, we obtain

(6.13)

\displaystyle\|h_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|H(\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{\pm})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}

and

(6.14)

\displaystyle\|2\partial_{\theta}(\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{\pm})\|_{L^{\infty}}=\bigg\|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}^{\pm}-\cos(\theta)\frac{v_{n}^{\pm}}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}v_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying
Eq.

6.8
,
Eq.

6.10
, together with
Eqs.

6.13
–
6.14
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{N}_{1}(u_{n}^{\pm}),u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathcal{N}_{2}(u_{n}^{\pm}),u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\partial_{\theta}(\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{+})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|h_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|h_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\eta_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\eta_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle 2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{-},u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle 2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+},u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\theta}\eta_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle 2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{-},u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle 2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+},u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{3/2}+\left\langle 2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{-},u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle 2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+},u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle.

For the last two terms in the above estimate, we compute

\displaystyle\quad\left\langle 2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{-},u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle 2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+},u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{-}u_{n}^{+}+2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+},u_{n}^{-}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}\left(u_{n}^{-}u_{n}^{+}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}2\partial_{\theta}\left(\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\right)u_{n}^{-}u_{n}^{+}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+C\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{3/2},

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
n
.

Step 2 b.

H^{m}
-estimates of
u_{n}^{\pm}
(for
m\geq 1
).

Multiplying equation
Eq.

6.11
by
(k+1)^{2m}\eta_{2,k}^{+}(t)
and equation
Eq.

6.12
by
(k+1)^{2m}\eta_{2,k}^{-}(t)
, summing up over
k=1,2,\ldots,n
, and applying integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}L_{1}^{+}(u_{n}^{+}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}L_{1}^{-}(u_{n}^{-}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}\mathcal{N}_{1}(u_{n}^{\pm}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}\mathcal{N}_{2}(u_{n}^{\pm}),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle.

We decompose the nonlinear terms into three components

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}\left(-2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{+}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+}-2\cos(\theta)h_{n}^{+}u_{n}^{+}-\eta_{n}^{+}g_{n}^{+}+\eta_{n}^{-}g_{n}^{-}+2\cos(\theta)u_{n}^{-}h_{n}^{-}\right),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}\left(-2\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{+}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{-}-2\cos(\theta)h_{n}^{+}u_{n}^{-}-\eta_{n}^{-}g_{n}^{+}+\eta_{n}^{+}g_{n}^{-}+2\cos(\theta)u_{n}^{+}h_{n}^{-}\right),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}\left(\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{-}\right),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m}\left(\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}u_{n}^{+}\right),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle.

We now turn to the estimates of
J_{3}
. Each of the terms in
J_{3}
can be written in the form
\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m_{1}}u_{n}^{\pm}\partial_{\theta}^{m_{2}}h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{m_{3}}\sin(\theta),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\mp}\right\rangle
with
m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=m
. In the case
m_{1}=m
, we integrate by parts and apply Hölder’s inequality together with
Eq.

6.14
to deduce

\displaystyle\left\langle\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{m+1}u_{n}^{-},\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\right\rangle+\left\langle\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{m+1}u_{n}^{+},\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{m+1}u_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}+\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{m+1}u_{n}^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}\left(\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{\theta}\left(\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\right)\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{\theta}\left(\sin(\theta)h_{n}^{-}\right)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}.

If the indices satisfy
m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=m
, with
0\leq m_{1}\leq m-1,1\leq m_{2}
, then, by Hölder’s inequality, Poincaré’s inequality, and
Eq.

6.10
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{\theta}^{m_{1}}u_{n}^{\pm}\partial_{\theta}^{m_{2}}h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{m_{3}}\sin(\theta),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\mp}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}^{m_{1}}u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m_{2}}h_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\mp}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}^{m_{1}+1}u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m_{2}-1}u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\mp}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{H^{m}}\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{H^{m}}\|u_{n}^{\mp}\|_{H^{m}}.

If the indices satisfy
m_{1}+m_{2}=m
with
m_{1}\leq m-1
, then by
Eq.

6.13
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle h_{n}^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{m_{1}}u_{n}^{\pm}\partial_{\theta}^{m_{2}}\sin(\theta),\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\mp}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\|h_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m_{1}}u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\mp}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m_{1}}u_{n}^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\theta}^{m}u_{n}^{\mp}\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining the above estimates, we conclude that

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq C\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\right)^{3/2}.

As for the estimates of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
, using Hölder’s inequality, Poincaré’s inequality, together with
Eq.

6.8
,
Eq.

6.10
,
Eq.

6.13
, and
Eq.

6.14
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{1}+J_{2}\leq C\left(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\right)^{3/2}.

Define

\displaystyle I_{m,n}^{2}(t)\coloneqq\|u_{n}^{+}(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|u_{n}^{-}(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2},

Then it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I_{m,n}(t)\leq C_{1}I_{m,n}(t)+C_{2}I_{m,n}^{2}(t),

where

are constants independent of

. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle I_{m,n}(t)\leq\frac{C_{{1}}}{C_{{2}}(1-e^{C_{{1}}t})I_{m}(0)+C_{{1}}}I_{m}(0)e^{C_{{1}}t},\qquad\text{for $0\leq t<T^{*}$,}

where

and

. It then follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|u_{n}^{\pm}(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq\ 4e^{C_{{1}}T_{{0}}}\left(\|u_{0}^{+}\|_{H^{m}}+\|u_{0}^{-}\|_{H^{m}}\right).

7.
Instability for the nonlinear problem

In this section, we prove
Theorem

3.7
. To this end, we first construct a solution to the linearized problem, together with a family of solutions to the nonlinear system, each satisfying carefully chosen structural properties. Based on these constructions, we then employ a contradiction argument to demonstrate the existence of a nonlinear solution exhibiting the instability described in

. Our proof follows the strategy of
[
30
]
.

Proof of
Theorem

3.7
.

Step 1.

Construction of a solution to the linearized problem.

By
Theorems

3.1
–
3.3
, we can construct a classical solution
\eta^{\pm}
to the linearized system
Eq.

2.7
. We assume that the initial data satisfy the assumptions of
Theorem

3.3
, and, in addition, satisfy

\displaystyle 0\leq\left\langle L^{+}\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\left\langle\eta_{0}^{+},\eta_{0}^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}>\frac{1}{2}\|\eta_{0}^{+}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}e^{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}t}.

To simplify the notation, we set
u(t,\theta)\coloneqq\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{\pm}(t,\theta)
, so that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}=\|\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\eta^{\pm}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}
. We further introduce the rescaled functions

\displaystyle\check{\eta}^{\pm}(t,\theta)\coloneqq\frac{\delta\eta^{\pm}(t,\theta)}{I_{m}(0)},

and

(7.1)

\displaystyle\check{u}^{\pm}(t,\theta)\coloneqq\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\check{\eta}^{\pm}(t,\theta)=\frac{\delta u^{\pm}(t,\theta)}{I_{m}(0)},

it implies that

\displaystyle\|\check{u}^{\pm}(0)\|_{H^{m}}=\delta.

It follows directly from the linearity of the system
Eq.

2.7
that

\check{u}^{\pm}
remains a classical solution of the linearized problem, inheriting all the regularity and structural properties of the original solution
\eta^{\pm}
, which means that

\displaystyle\|\check{\eta}^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}>\frac{1}{2}\|\check{\eta}^{+}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}e^{\sqrt{\lambda}_{1}t},\qquad\text{for $t>0$.}

Let

\displaystyle t_{K}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\ln\left(\frac{4K\delta}{\|\tilde{u}^{+}(0)\|_{L^{2}}}\right).

Then, we obtain

(7.2)

\displaystyle\|\check{u}^{+}(t_{K})\|_{L^{2}}=\|\check{\eta}^{+}(t_{K})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}>\frac{1}{2}\|\check{\eta}^{+}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}e^{\sqrt{\lambda}_{1}t_{K}}=2K\delta.

Step 2.

Construction of a solution to the nonlinear problem.

Based on the initial data
\check{u}(0)
of the solution
\check{u}
defined in
Eq.

7.1
, we proceed to construct a family of solutions to the perturbed nonlinear problem
Eq.

2.6
. Specifically, we define

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(0,\theta)\coloneqq\varepsilon\tilde{\eta}^{+}(0,\theta)

and set

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(0,\theta)\coloneqq\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(0,\theta)=\varepsilon\tilde{u}(0,\theta),\qquad\text{for }0<\varepsilon<1,

so that

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(0,\theta)\|_{H^{m}}=\varepsilon\delta<\delta,\qquad\text{for }m>3.

By applying
Theorem

3.6
, we obtain that there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
, the nonlinear system
Eq.

2.6
admits a classical solution
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
on
(0,T_{\varepsilon})
, where

\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\frac{1}{C_{{1}}}\ln\left(1+\frac{C_{{1}}}{2C_{{2}}\varepsilon\delta}\right)>t_{K}.

Moreover, obtain the following uniform-in-time estimates:

(7.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{K}}\|\bar{u}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{m}},\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{K}}\|\partial_{t}\bar{u}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{m-1}},\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{K}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}\bar{u}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{m-2}}\leq C(t_{K})\delta,

where we defined
\bar{u}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t,\theta)\coloneqq\rho_{2}^{1/2}\partial_{\theta}\bar{\eta}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t,\theta)
.

Step 3.

Contradiction argument.
We prove
Theorem

3.7
by contradiction. Let us suppose that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
, the classical solution
(\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm},\bar{v}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})
, emanating from the initial data
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}(0)
, satisfies

\displaystyle\|\bar{\eta}^{+}_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq F(I_{m,\varepsilon}(0)),

where
I_{m,\varepsilon}^{2}(0)\coloneqq\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(0)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{-}(0)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}
.
Then, by applying the definition of
F
in
Eq.

3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{k}}\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon K\delta.

Next, we define

\displaystyle(\tilde{\eta}^{\pm},\tilde{v}^{\pm})\coloneqq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(\bar{\eta}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon},\bar{v}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})

Then,
\tilde{\eta}^{\pm},\tilde{v}^{\pm}
satisfy the following system:

(7.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}^{+}=\{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\eta}^{+}+\tilde{v}^{+},\sin(2\theta)\}+\varepsilon N_{1}(\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}),&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}^{-}=\{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\eta}^{-}-\tilde{v}^{-},\sin(2\theta)\}+\varepsilon N_{2}(\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}),&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{v}^{\pm}=H\tilde{\eta}^{\pm},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

with the initial value

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(0)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\bar{\eta}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(0)=\check{\eta}(0),

where

\displaystyle N_{1}(\tilde{\eta}^{\pm})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\tilde{\eta}^{+},\tilde{v}^{+}\}-\{\tilde{\eta}^{-},\tilde{v}^{-}\},

\displaystyle N_{2}(\tilde{\eta}^{\pm})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\tilde{\eta}^{-},\tilde{v}^{+}\}-\{\tilde{\eta}^{+},\tilde{v}^{-}\}.

Moreover, by
Eq.

7.3
, the following uniform estimates hold:

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{K}}\|\tilde{u}^{\pm}(t)\|_{H^{m}},\ \sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{K}}\|\partial_{t}\tilde{u}^{\pm}(t)\|_{H^{m}},\ \sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{K}}\|\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{u}^{\pm}(t)\|_{H^{m}}\leq C(t_{K})\delta,

which are independent of
\varepsilon
. As a consequence, by standard compactness arguments (see
[
36
, Section 1.4.5]
for further details), we may extract a subsequence (not relabeled) from
\{\bar{\eta}^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\tilde{u}^{\pm}\

\displaystyle\text{weakly in }L^{2}([0,t_{K}];H^{m}(\mathbb{T}));

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}\tilde{u}^{\pm}\

\displaystyle\text{weakly in }L^{2}([0,t_{K}];H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T}));

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{u}^{\pm}\

\displaystyle\text{weakly in }L^{2}([0,t_{K}];H^{m-2}(\mathbb{T}))

and

(7.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{K}}\|\tilde{u}^{\pm}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq K\delta,\quad\tilde{u}^{\pm}(t,\theta)\in C([0,t_{K}],H^{m}(\mathbb{T}))\cap C^{2}([0,t_{K}],H^{m-2}(\mathbb{T})).

In fact, since
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}=-2\sin(\theta)\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
and
\tilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
are odd functions, we deduce that

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\text{weakly in }L^{2}([0,t_{K}];H^{m+1}(\mathbb{T}));

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\text{weakly in }L^{2}([0,t_{K}];H^{m}(\mathbb{T}));

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{\eta}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\text{weakly in }L^{2}([0,t_{K}];H^{m-1}(\mathbb{T})).

Passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
in the
Eq.

7.4
, we obtain the linearized system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}^{+}=\{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\eta}^{+}+\tilde{v}^{+},\sin(2\theta)\},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}^{-}=\{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\eta}^{-}-\tilde{v}^{-},\sin(2\theta)\},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{v}^{\pm}=H\tilde{\eta}^{\pm},&t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{T},\end{cases}

Therefore,
\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}
is a classical solution to the linearized problem
Eq.

2.7
for
t\in[0,t_{K}]
, with initial data coinciding with
\check{\eta}^{\pm}(0)
. By the uniqueness result in
Theorem

3.1
, it follows that

(7.6)

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}^{\pm}(t,\theta)=\check{\eta}^{\pm}(t,\theta),\qquad(t,\theta)\in[0,t_{K}]\times\mathbb{T}.

Thus, combining
Eq.

7.6
with
Eq.

7.2
and
Eq.

7.5
, we deduce

\displaystyle 2K\delta\leq\|\check{\eta}^{+}(t_{K})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=\|\tilde{\eta}^{+}(t_{K})\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}\leq K\delta,

which yields a contradiction, thus completing the proof of
Theorem

3.7
.
∎

8.
Global well-posedness and stability for the nonlinear problem

In this section, we prove the stability result in

3.10
. We build on the discussion in
Remark

5.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

3.10
.

Taking the weighted
\rho_{2}
-inner product with
\eta^{+}
on the both sides of the first equation of the system
Eq.

2.7
, and the weighted
\rho_{2}
-inner product with
\eta^{-}
on the both sides of the second equation of the system
Eq.

2.7
, we obtain (as in
Section

5.1
and
Remark

5.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle L_{1}^{+}\eta^{+},\eta^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\left\langle L_{1}^{-}\eta^{-},\eta^{-}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\left\langle N_{1},\eta^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\left\langle N_{2},\eta^{-}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}

\displaystyle<-\frac{3}{8}\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)+\left\langle N_{1},\eta^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\left\langle N_{2},\eta^{-}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.

For the nonlinear terms, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle N_{1},\eta^{+}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\left\langle N_{2},\eta^{-}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\left(\eta^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{+}-\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta^{+}v^{+}-\eta^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{-}+\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta^{-}v^{-}\right)\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\left(\eta^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{+}-\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta^{-}v^{+}-\eta^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{-}+\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta^{+}v^{-}\right)\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\coloneqq K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3},

where

\displaystyle K_{1}\coloneqq-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{v^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta^{+}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{v^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta^{-}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)},

\displaystyle K_{2}\coloneqq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\eta^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{+}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\eta^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{-}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\eta^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{+}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\eta^{+}\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{-}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)},

\displaystyle K_{3}\coloneqq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{v^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta^{-}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{v^{-}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\eta^{+}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}.

By applying Hölder’s inequality and
Lemma

A.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{\theta}\left(\frac{v^{+}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}\right)\left((\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+})^{2}+(\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-})^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{\theta}v^{+}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}\left((\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+})^{2}+(\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-})^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{v^{+}}{\sin(\theta)}\frac{1}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}\left((\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+})^{2}+(\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-})^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}v^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|H(\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+})\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)^{3/2}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{v^{-}\partial_{\theta}(\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-})}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\theta=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{\theta}\left(\frac{v^{-}}{\sin^{2}(\theta)}\right)\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\theta}v^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)^{3/2}.

Finally, applying
Lemma

A.4
,
Lemma

A.5
, and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\|\frac{\eta^{+}}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{+}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg\|\frac{\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{-}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg\|\frac{\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg\|\frac{\eta^{-}}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{-}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg\|\frac{\partial_{\theta}\eta^{+}}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\theta}^{2}v^{+}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg\|\frac{\partial_{\theta}\eta^{-}}{\sin(\theta)}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)^{3/2}.

Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)+\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+C\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)^{3/2},

for some absolute constant
C>0
. We proceed by employing a standard bootstrap argument. Let

\displaystyle I_{0}^{2}(t)\coloneqq\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2},

and fix
\varepsilon>0
to be chosen sufficiently small later. We assume that

\displaystyle I_{0}^{2}(t)\leq 2\varepsilon^{2},\qquad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

It then follows that

\displaystyle\left(\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\right)^{3/2}\leq(2C\varepsilon^{2})^{3/2}=2^{3/2}C^{3/2}\varepsilon^{3},

which implies

(8.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I_{0}^{2}(t)\leq-I_{0}^{2}(t)+2^{3/2}C^{3/2}\varepsilon^{3}.

We now choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle 2^{3/2}C^{3/2}\varepsilon^{3}\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}.

It then follows that whenever
I_{0}^{2}(t)\geq\varepsilon^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I_{0}^{2}(t)\leq-\frac{1}{2}I_{0}^{2}(t),

which implies exponential decay of
I_{0}^{2}(t)
until it falls below
\varepsilon^{2}
. Once in the regime where
I_{0}^{2}(t)<\varepsilon^{2}
, we return to the general differential inequality
Eq.

8.1
, which yields

\displaystyle-I_{0}^{2}(t)+2^{3/2}C^{3/2}\varepsilon^{3}\leq-\varepsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}<0.

Thus, the bootstrap assumption is preserved by continuity. As a consequence, if the initial data satisfy

\displaystyle I_{0}^{2}(0)=\|\eta^{+}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2},

then we obtain

\displaystyle I_{0}^{2}(t)=\|\eta^{+}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}+\|\eta^{-}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2},\qquad\text{for all $t>0$.}

This completes the proof of
Theorem

3.10
.
∎

Lemma A.1

.

For any

\displaystyle H\sin(a\theta)=-\cos(a\theta),

\displaystyle H\cos(a\theta)=\sin(a\theta).

Lemma A.2

(Fourier transform and Hilbert transform)
.

Let

, with

, and assume

-periodic. Then the Fourier coefficient of the Hilbert transform

at frequency

\displaystyle\widehat{Hf}(k)=\big(-i\,\mathrm{sign}\left(k\right)\big)\hat{f}(k).

Let

and
2\pi
-periodic function such that
Hf\in L^{1}
and is also
2\pi
-periodic. Then

\displaystyle Hf(x)\sim\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\big(-i\,\mathrm{sign}\left(k\right)\big)\hat{f}(k)e^{ikx}.

Lemma A.4

(

The Hilbert transform

to

with

\displaystyle\|Hf\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}},

Lemma A.7

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{a_{k}^{+}+a_{k+2}^{+}+\sqrt{(a_{k}^{+}-a_{k+2}^{+})^{2}+4(\varepsilon_{k}^{+})^{2}}}{2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{\sup}\coloneqq\sup_{k\geq 1}\{a_{k}^{+},\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2}\},\qquad\lambda_{\inf}\coloneqq\inf_{k\geq 1}\{a_{k}^{+},\lambda_{k}^{1},\lambda_{k}^{2}\}.

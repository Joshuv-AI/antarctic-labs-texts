Masticatory function between chewing with and without wearing clear thermoplastic appliances
Authors: Rungtiwa Jirawiwatsaree, Chidsanu Changsiripun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundClear thermoplastic materials are used in a variety of oral appliances. In some situations, patients may wear clear thermoplastic appliances while eating. However, the effect of wearing clear thermoplastic appliances on chewing efficiency is unknown. This study aimed to evaluate the differences in masticatory function between chewing with and without wearing clear thermoplastic retainers over a 6-month period, and its associated factors.MethodsThirty patients who received upper and lower clear retainers after debonding fixed appliances were examined for objective and subjective masticatory function at retainer delivery (T0), 3-month (T1), and 6-month follow-ups (T2) in two conditions: with and without wearing their retainers while chewing. The objective method used multiple sieves reported as the median particle size (MPS). The food intake ability (FIA) test served as the subjective method. Paired t-test was used to compare the outcomes between chewing with and without retainers at each evaluation time point. Repeated measures ANOVA followed by the Bonferroni post-hoc test was used to compare the outcomes between the three evaluating time points. Multivariable linear regression analysis was performed to assess whether age, sex, and extraction/non-extraction was associated with these effects.ResultsThe MPS was significantly greater when chewing with retainers compared with chewing without them at T0 (P < 0.05), however, it was not significantly greater at T1 and T2 (P > 0.05). The MPS with and without the retainers tend to decrease between T0, T1 and T2. In particular, the MPS while chewing with retainers significantly decreased between T0 and T1. The total FIA score and FIA subscores for hard and soft food were significantly lower when eating with retainers at T0 (P < 0.05), however, all FIA scores when eating without retainers did not significantly change between T0, T1, and T2. No significantly associated factors were identified.ConclusionsWearing clear retainers while chewing decreases objective and subjective masticatory function immediately after completing orthodontic treatment. However, this significantly improved to levels comparable to chewing without retainers after a 3-month follow-up. Age, sex, and extraction/non-extraction treatment were not associated with the difference in masticatory function while chewing with and without wearing the retainers.Keywords: Clear thermoplastic appliance, Masticatory performance, Multiple sieve method, Orthodontics, Subjective food intake ability

Introduction

Clear thermoplastic materials are used in a variety of oral appliances, including retainers [1], athletic mouth guards [2], soft occlusal splint [3], and nigh guard beaching trays [4]. The clear thermoplastic appliances are made with a clear, heat-softened plastic that is sucked down over a cast of the teeth [5, 6]. Recently, a clear aligner has been developed using computerized 3D technology and 3D printing to create clear thermoplastic appliances for orthodontic tooth movement [7].

In some situations, patients may wear clear thermoplastic appliances while eating. For example, patients undergoing clear aligner treatment need to wear the aligners during meals until the crossbite is corrected to avoid occlusal trauma [7]. Some patients choose not to remove the clear aligner while eating because they feel too busy or lazy [8]. Patients wearing clear aligners with composite pontics may feel embarrassed to remove their aligners before eating in front of others [8]. Moreover, there is a case report in which a patient found that wearing clear retainers while eating or drinking helped reduce tooth sensitivity [9]. The question arises as to whether wearing clear thermoplastic appliances while chewing affects masticatory performance, considering the thickness of the appliances on the occlusal surface of the teeth.

Although studies have demonstrated that orthodontic treatment and oral appliances with occlusal coverage can affect the masticatory function in healthy dentate patients [10–12], there is no consensus on the effect of these appliances on masticatory function. There is an immediate decrease in chewing efficiency in patients using an oral appliance with a flat occlusal Table [10]. Furthermore, the use of bite raising with light-cured orthodontic band cement on the upper first molars reduced objective and subjective masticatory function immediately [11]. In contrast, the masticatory performance is not significantly influenced by anatomical splints covering the occlusal surface of all posterior teeth, which increased the vertical dimension up to 6 mm [12].

Masticatory function can be objectively and subjectively evaluated based on masticatory performance [13]. Various methods have been developed to objectively measure clinical masticatory performance, determined by a variety of indexes, such as the color change of chewing gum or paraffin wax cubes [14, 15], the amount of glucose extracted from chewing gum jelly [16], and the distribution of bolus particle size [11, 17]. The measurement of particle size distribution by the sieve method and the color change of chewing gum or wax cubes by the mixing ability test are the most commonly used methods, providing reliable and valid assessments of masticatory performance [18, 19]. However, a previous study found that the mixing ability test is less suitable for healthy dentate and young patients because the task of mixing the bolus is too easy [20]. Moreover, the results of the sieve method are significantly correlated in young patients [20].

Single sieving and multiple sieving approaches have been used in the sieve method [11, 17]. Although the single-sieving method is simple and convenient to perform, the multiple-sieving method provides more detailed information on the particle size distribution of the chewed test food [18]. From the multiple-sieving method, masticatory performance is defined by the median particle size (MPS), which is calculated from the percentage ratio of the particle size distribution of a comminuted test food with a certain number of chewing strokes [17, 18, 21]. The subjective methods include a questionnaire or an interview to determine the food intake ability (FIA) of various food types and is an established simple method to evaluate subjective masticatory function [22, 23]. Boretti et al. suggested that both objective and subjective masticatory function should be evaluated because a weak correlation was found between the two assessments [24].

To our knowledge, there are a scarce number of reports on the effect of clear thermoplastic appliances on masticatory function—one with the questionaries [25] and another using the single-sieving method [26]. They found no significant difference in chewing with and without clear thermoplastic appliances [26]. However, no study has objectively evaluated the effect of clear thermoplastic appliances on mastication using the multiple-sieving method. In this study, we used a clear thermoplastic retainer as a model for a clear thermoplastic appliance. Thus, the objectives of this study were:

To objectively and subjectively compare masticatory function with and without wearing upper and lower clear thermoplastic retainers while chewing.

To objectively and subjectively compare masticatory function with and without wearing upper and lower clear thermoplastic retainers while chewing.

To investigate the potential factors associated with the mean differences in masticatory function between eating with and without clear thermoplastic retainers.

To investigate the potential factors associated with the mean differences in masticatory function between eating with and without clear thermoplastic retainers.

To evaluate the effects of wearing the clear thermoplastic retainers on masticatory function at 3-month and 6-month follow-ups.

To evaluate the effects of wearing the clear thermoplastic retainers on masticatory function at 3-month and 6-month follow-ups.

Materials and methods

The study protocol was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Dentistry, Chulalongkorn University (HREC-DCU 2019-008).

The G*power software version 3.1.9.7 was used for calculating sample size. The sample size was determined based on a pilot study using the mean and standard deviation values of the MPS (mm) between chewing with and without the clear retainers at the retainer delivery appointment (T0) for detecting the difference between two dependent means, where µ = 4.83 mm, µ0 = 6.45 mm, and σ = 3.05 mm. The calculation indicated that 30 samples were required for a two-sided 5% significance level and a power of 80%. To account for a 10% loss to follow-up, the sample size was increased to 34 patients in this study (30/0.9 = 33.33).

Eligibility criteria were fully described in our previous study [27]. In brief, the criteria included patients aged 14 to 39 who had completed comprehensive orthodontic treatment, either with the extraction of four bicuspids or through non-extraction, and required full-time wear of upper and lower clear thermoplastic retainers for at least 6 months to maintain occlusion. Other criteria included skeletal Class I with a normodivergent vertical dimension, natural permanent dentition with a Class I molar relationship, normal overjet and overbite, no edentulous area, no significant facial deformities, no temporomandibular symptoms, no prior orthognathic surgery, no parafunctional habits, and no systemic diseases or medications that might affect masticatory muscle activity. Patients were consecutively recruited from the orthodontic clinic at the Faculty of Dentistry, Chulalongkorn University, between January and June 2019. The patients and parents received written and oral descriptions of the study and provided written informed consent prior to participation.

Experimental design

All patients in this study received upper and lower clear thermoplastic retainers thermoformed from 0.75 mm-thick plastic blanks (Essix ACE Plastic, Dentspy Sirona, Charlotte, NC, USA) and were instructed to wear the retainers full-time throughout the 6-month study period, except eating and oral cleaning. Patient compliance was assessed by using a three-point Likert scale questionnaire. Poor, moderate, and good compliance were defined as when the patient wore the retainers for less than 16 h per day, more than 16 h per day, and regularly as instructed by the clinician, respectively [28].

Data collection

The objective evaluation of masticatory performance was examined for each patient over three periods: immediately after insertion of the upper and lower clear thermoplastic retainers at the retainer delivery appointment (T0), 3-month follow-up (T1), and 6-month follow-up (T2), with and without wearing the retainers while chewing. For subjective evaluation of masticatory ability, the patients were examined at T0 with and without wearing the retainers while chewing, and at T1 and T2 without the retainers. Because the patients were not instructed to wear the retainers during meals over the follow-up period due to the ethical concern, the subjective evaluation using FIA test at T1 and T2 with retainers could not be evaluated. Objective evaluation of masticatory performance

The masticatory performance was evaluated by the multiple sieve method [17]. The artificial test food used in this study was made of condensation-cured silicone impression material (Optosil, Heraeus Kulzer GmbH, Hanau, Germany). To standardize the bolus size and shape of the Optosil tablets, an acrylic template was used (20-mm diameter and 5-mm thick per tablet) [29]. After setting for at least 1 h, the tablets were cut into quarters and packaged for each patient. The patients sit in an upright and natural head position on a chair without a headrest. The masticatory performance evaluation was conducted as described by a previous study [17]. Each patient was given two quarter-tablets of artificial test food per trial and instructed to chew naturally for 30 strokes to maximize variability in the primary outcome variable. Chewing was performed on the preferred chewing side, or on the right side if the preferred side was certain, to control for the confounding factor of side differences in bite force [17, 30]. The investigator counted the number of chewing cycles. The patients were instructed to expectorate the chewed particles onto a filter paper placed over a beaker and rinse with water until the particles were removed from their mouth. The chewing procedure was repeated 7 times with approximately 10 g chewed food for one trial [17].

The chewed particles were dried in an oven at 80˚C for 1 h and then separated using 12 standard progressively smaller sieves (Test sieves; Retsch Technology GmbH): 5.6, 4.75, 4, 3.55, 3.35, 3.15, 2.8, 2.0, 1.4, 1.0, 0.5, and 0.25 mm in diameter, stacked on a vibratory sieve shaker (AS 200 digit; Retsch Technology GmbH) and vibrated for 2 min [17, 18]. The dried test food particles that passed through each stack were weighed on a 0.01-g analytical balance and used to calculate the cumulative weight%. The cumulative weight% is defined by the percentage of the amount of food particles that passed through each sieve.

where A is the cumulative mass retained on that sieve and previous sieves and B is the total sample mass. To determine the median particle size (MPS; the sieve diameter through which 50% of the comminuted particles could pass), simple linear regression was plotted between the cumulative weight% of each sieve and the diameter of the sieve (mm). The smaller MPS (mm) indicated better masticatory performance [31].

Subjective evaluation of masticatory ability

The Food Intake Ability (FIA) questionnaires were used to evaluate the subjective masticatory function in this study. The FIA test was performed using validated self-assessed questionnaires asking about the patient’s masticatory ability of 10 different types of food, which included the key foods reported in a previous study [23]. The test foods were raw carrots, dried peanuts, cookies, apples, dried cuttlefish, boiled rice, boiled eggs, tofu, ham, and watermelon [23, 32]. At T0, the self-assessed questionnaire was completed chairside immediately after inserting the retainers, followed by chewing the prepared 10 test foods. The FIA questionnaires consisted of 5 Likert scales of their masticatory ability: ‘cannot chew at all’ (1 point), ‘difficult to chew’ (2 points), ‘cannot say either way’ (3 points), ‘can chew some’ (4 points), ‘can chew well’ (5 points). For T1 and T2, participants completed the FIA questionnaires based on the difficulty they experienced while chewing the 10 foods in their daily life over the follow-up period. The total FIA score was calculated as the average score for 10 foods. The average scores of the 5 hard foods (raw carrots, dried peanuts, cookies, apples, and dried cuttlefish) and the 5 soft foods (boiled rice, boiled eggs, tofu, ham, and watermelon) were calculated separately, with a higher score indicating a better masticatory ability.

Data analysis

The data were analyzed with SPSS version 22.00 software (IBM Inc., Chicago, IL). The significance level was set at P < 0.05. To determine the intra-examiner reliability of the measurements, nine patients (30%) were additionally assessed after braces removal at the fixed appliance debonding appointment, which occurred one week before T0. These measurements were then compared with the T0 data, collected while chewing without wearing the retainers by the same researcher. The intra-examiner reliability of the masticatory performance and the FIA test between the two visits was determined using intraclass correlation (ICC), calculated based on a single rating, absolute-agreement, 2-way mixed-effects model.

The MPS and FIA scores between chewing without and with the retainers were compared using the paired t-test at each evaluating time point. The time-dependent changes in the MPS and FIA scores (T0, T1, and T2) were investigated using a repeated measures analysis of variance (repeated measures ANOVA) followed by the Bonferroni post hoc test. Potential factors, i.e. age, sex, and extraction/non-extraction, that might be related to the mean difference in masticatory function between chewing with and without wearing the retainers at T0 were pre-specified and analyzed using multivariable linear regression by employing a full model approach without backward elimination or stepwise selection. Only T0 was chosen for the regression analysis because FIA score data with the retainers could not be obtained at T1 and T2, as previously explained in the data collection.

Results

Thirty-four patients were enrolled in the study. Four patients were excluded due to dropping out during follow-up (n = 3) and using a masticatory muscle-related agent (n = 1). Thus, 30 patients (9 males, 21 females) aged 14 to 39 years (mean 23.3 ± 6.0 years) with 13 four-bicuspid extraction cases and 17 non-extraction cases, were evaluated.

The ICC for the MPS was 0.84, indicating good reliability. For the FIA test, the ICC for the total FIA score, the FIA score for hard food, and the FIA score for soft food was 0.83, 0.88, and 0.92, respectively, indicating good to excellent reliability of the measurements [33]. All patients in this study reported good compliance by wearing the retainers day and night as instructed, except eating and oral cleaning.

The MPS and FIA scores at each evaluated time point are presented in Table 1. Comparing the objective masticatory performance with and without wearing the retainers while chewing, the MPS with the retainers was significantly greater than that without the retainers at T0 (P < 0.05). However, the MPS at T1 and T2 was not significantly different between with and without wearing the retainers (P > 0.05). When determining the time-dependent change (Table 2), the MPS tends to decrease between T0, T1, and T2 when evaluated either with or without the retainers. The Bonferroni post-hoc tests found that the MPS significantly decreased between T0 and T1 while chewing with wearing the clear retainers.

Discussion

The present study investigated the effect of wearing upper and lower clear thermoplastic appliances while chewing on objective and subjective masticatory function in post-orthodontic treated patients using clear thermoplastic retainers over a 6-month period. The clear thermoplastic retainers used in this study were made from polyethylene terephthalate glycol (PETG) and demonstrated great resistance to wear and fracture. They were thermoformed from 0.75 mm-thick plastic blanks following the manufacturer’s instructions [34]. A previous study found that the physical consistency of clear thermoplastic appliances does not change after exposure to food for 10 and 15 days [35]. Moreover, there were no reports of retainer fractures during the examination period in this study. This result aligns with the previous study, which found that only 10.8% of patients experienced breakage or deformation of their clear thermoplastic appliances during chewing [25]. Since caries and demineralization have been reported in patients wearing clear retainers or other clear thermoplastic appliances full-time, including during eating and drinking [8, 9], patients in this study were not instructed to wear the retainers during meals over the follow-up period to avoid such harmful effects.

The masticatory performance with multiple sieving was used to measure and evaluate the objective assessment of the masticatory function because the technique provides an accurate quantitative result that was easy to compare between subjects [18]. A condensation silicone impression material was used as the test food because it has a reliable standardized method, a firm consistency, and is not affected by saliva [21, 29]. Our results demonstrated that at T0, the MPS with the retainers was significantly greater than without the retainers, indicating that chewing efficiency while wearing the retainers was lower than while not wearing the retainers. Satokawa et al. [10] also found that chewing efficiency decreased after immediate insertion of the oral appliance with an occlusal flat table and did not improve after wearing the oral appliance for 5 days. They suggested the factors that could influence the results might be the lower flat occlusal table anatomy and an increase in the occlusal vertical dimension. However, the upper and lower clear thermoplastic retainers used in the present study occupy ~ 1.5 mm of the interocclusal distance and are flexible, in contrast with Satokawa et al., who used oral appliances with a 6-mm interocclusal vertical dimension made from hard acrylic resin material. Another previous study found that masticatory performance was not significantly different between subjects with and without an anatomical maxillary splint covering the occlusal surface with a vertical dimension of 2 mm [12]. Good occlusal contact and intercuspation between the teeth are important factors in stabilizing the occlusion and result in better masticatory performance [36]. Therefore, a possible main factor that affected the masticatory performance in the present study could be the low and flat occlusal table anatomy of the retainers. However, our study found that at the 3-month and 6-month follow-ups, the MPS compared with and without the retainers were not significantly different. These findings might result from the patients’ adapting to wearing the retainers after the 3-month period.

When evaluating the time-dependent effect, the MPS without the retainer while chewing tends to decrease over the observation period. These results were consistent with a prior report that demonstrated an increase in masticatory performance during a 1-year retention period following extraction and non-extraction orthodontic treatment, and the masticatory performance had improved to a level that was not significantly different from that in the normal occlusal non-treatment control group at 1-month post-treatment [37]. Because greater occlusal contact areas provide better masticatory performance [17], the settling of the occlusion after active orthodontic treatment could affect this result. When the MPS was examined with the retainer while chewing at the 3-month and 6-month follow-ups, the results revealed that masticatory performance significantly increased after 3 months. To our knowledge, no previous study has investigated the effect of wearing an oral thermoplastic appliance with occlusal coverage on masticatory performance. The results from this study indicate that although the masticatory performance when wearing the clear retainers while chewing is lower than without the retainers after immediately inserting the retainers, it improves after a 3-month follow-up.

Based on previous studies, the FIA test was used to evaluate the subjective masticatory ability [23, 32]. A self-assessed questionnaire method was prepared using 10 foods, which were adapted and included the key food types based on previous studies, and the chewing ability was quantified using a five-point Likert scale [11, 23]. In the present study, we found that the total FIA score and the FIA scores for hard and soft food with the retainers were significantly lower than without the retainers at T0. An earlier study also reported that the OHIP score of the functional limitation (including a question concerning the difficulty chewing any food) increased after the immediate insertion of an occlusal flat table appliance, with all the upper functional cusps making equal contact with the flat table of the lower oral appliance, indicating lower subjective masticatory function compared with before wearing the appliance [10]. However, they found that the subjective chewing ability improved within 72 h of wearing the appliance. Although the FIA scores while wearing the retainers were lower than without the retainers at T0, the FIA score of soft food while wearing the retainers was greater than that of hard food. Therefore, patients should be informed that it is easier to use the clear retainers for chewing soft food than hard food.

When investigating the effect of the retainers on the FIA score at 3 months and 6 months while not wearing the retainers, the total FIA score and the FIA scores for hard and soft food were not significantly different between before wearing the retainers, the 3-month follow-up, and the 6-month follow-up. This finding is in agreement with a recent study that found a low frequency and severity of oral impacts affecting eating in patients wearing clear retainers, and the impact was similar to the wrap-around retainer group over a 6-month period [27]. This indicates that the occlusal thickness of the clear thermoplastic retainers did not affect the subjective masticatory ability after wearing the retainer for 3 months.

While the multivariable analysis in our study found that age, sex, and extraction/non-extraction treatment were not associated with the difference in masticatory function when chewing with and without retainers, this might be due to the small sample size. Further studies with larger sample sizes could increase the power of the analysis and clarify the association of these factors.

There are a few limitations to this study. Although the patients’ compliance with wearing retainers was determined by the subjective method and all patients in this study reported good compliance, we did not objectively examine the patients’ compliance. However, the masticatory function of each patient was investigated by comparing it within each patient. Additionally, due to ethical standards, patients were not instructed to wear retainers during their meals over the follow-up period. Thus, the differences in masticatory performance results between time points may not indicate that the patients have truly adapted to wearing the retainers while chewing. Further studies should investigate masticatory performance and food intake ability when wearing clear thermoplastic appliances over a longer period to provide more information about patient adaptation. Moreover, this study lacks untreated patients as a control group. Since a previous study reported that the masticatory performance significantly improved for control subjects at 3 months follow-up, a reduction in MPS found over the observation period of this study may be due not only to a training effect after performing the masticatory assays with the artificial test food but also to biological variability and the measurement error associated with the chewing tests [38]. Therefore, the masticatory improvement demonstrated during the follow-up in this study should be interpreted with caution. Furthermore, the results of our study might only be applicable to clear thermoplastic appliances made from the same materials with a thickness of 0.75 mm.

Conclusions

A significant reduction in objective and subjective masticatory function was found while chewing with wearing the upper and lower clear thermoplastic retainers compared with that when not wearing the retainers after immediately inserting the retainers (T0).

High-throughput calculations of spin Hall conductivity in non-magnetic 2D materials
Jiaqi Zhou, Samuel Poncé, Jean-Christophe Charlier

Abstract
Spin Hall effect (SHE) in two-dimensional (2D) materials is promising to effectively manipulate spin angular momentum and identify topological properties. In this work, we implemented an automated Wannierization with spin-orbit coupling on 426 non-magnetic monolayers including 210 metal and 216 insulators. Intrinsic spin Hall conductivity (SHC) has been calculated to find candidates exhibiting novel properties. We discover that Y 2 C 2 I 2 has an unconventional SHE with canted spin due to low crystal symmetry, Ta 4 Se 2 is a metallic monolayer with exceptionally high SHC, and the semi-metal Y 2 Br 2 possesses efficient charge-to-spin conversion induced by anti-crossing in bands. Moreover, quantum spin Hall insulators are investigated for quantized SHC. The present work provides a high-quality Wannier Hamiltonian database of 2D materials, and paves the way for the integration of 2D materials into high-performance and low-power-consumption spintronic devices.



Spin Hall effect (SHE) is a phenomenon
emerging from spin-orbit coupling (SOC)
where an external electric field can induce a transverse spin current
Sinova2015Oct
.
As an effective method
to control spin transport,
the SHE has been extensively investigated in bulk heavy metals
Tao2018Jun
.
However,
simple heavy metals are constrained by symmetry and
only present conventional SHE where
spin current, electric field,
and spin orientation are orthogonal
to each other.
Constrained by this strict rule,
an external magnetic field is required to switch perpendicular magnetization
Liu2012Aug
,
hindering device minimization.
Besides,
defined as the ratio of spin Hall conductivity (SHC)
to charge conductivity,
the spin Hall ratio (SHR)
is reduced by high charge conductivity in heavy metals,
leading to a low charge-to-spin conversion efficiency
Tao2018Jun
.
Van der Waals materials emerge as promising candidates
to overcome these challenges.
SHCs
have been studied in two-dimensional (2D)
transition-metal dichalcogenide (TMDC)
materials
Ahn2020Jun
;
Safeer2019Feb
;
MacNeill2017Mar
;
Shi2019Oct
;
Guimaraes2018Feb
;
Stiehl2019Nov
;
Xu2020Apr
;
Mishra2024Nov
.
Compared with high-symmetry bulk heavy metals,
2D materials with low-symmetry structures
can induce unconventional SHEs
Zhou2021Nov
;
Ji2024Nov
.
In addition, the reduced charge conductivity in
2D materials enables efficient charge-to-spin conversion
Vila2021Dec
;
Safeer2019Dec
.
More importantly,
the reduced dimension
induces quantum spin Hall effect
in topological 2D materials
Grassano2023Sep
,
offering robust and quantized SHCs in bandgaps
of quantum spin Hall insulators (QSHIs)
Bai2022May
;
Matusalem2019Dec
;
Matthes2016Aug
.
However,
current researches on SHE of 2D materials are mostly limited to TMDC materials,
while a large-scale study on 2D materials for spintronics
is still absent.

Wannier functions
Marzari2012Oct
;
Marrazzo2023Dec

are extensively used to study the SHC
Qiao2018Dec
.
Recent works have developed frameworks
for the high-throughput Wannierization.
Symmetrized Wannier Hamiltonians
have been generated
for the strained III-V semiconductor materials
by applying post-processing symmetrization

.
In addition, the single-criterion decision-making algorithm

.
Moreover,

.
Recently,
the projectability-disentangled Wannier functions
have been proposed

the initial guess is given by
a projectability measure for each Bloch state

;
Qiao2023Nov
.

In this work, we perform a high-throughput study of
spin Hall conductivity in non-magnetic 2D materials
using density functional theory (DFT) and Wannier functions.
Leveraging the scalar-relativistic
MC2D
database
MC2D1
;

These materials are investigated with

Intrinsic SHCs are calculated in all the materials

Finally, SHCs of topological insulators

Results and discussions

To match the typical experimental setup,
we focus on the SHC where the spin current is along
x

The intrinsic SHC in a weak scattering limit can be

\sigma_{xy}^{\alpha}=\frac{\hbar}{2e}\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}\int_{\text{BZ}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\Omega_{xy}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{k}),

(1)

where BZ denotes the Brillouin zone, and the spin Berry curvature (SBC) is given as

\displaystyle\Omega_{xy}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}f_{n\mathbf{k}}\Omega_{n,xy}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{k}),

(2)

\displaystyle\Omega_{n,xy}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\hbar^{2}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{-2\operatorname{Im}[\langle n\mathbf{k}|\hat{j}_{\alpha}|m\mathbf{k}\rangle\langle m\mathbf{k}|\hat{v}_{y}|n\mathbf{k}\rangle]}{(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}})^{2}+\eta^{2}}.

(3)

In the above equations,

f_{n{\mathbf{k}}}

denotes the Fermi-Dirac distribution

f_{n{\mathbf{k}}}(\mu,T)=\frac{1}{{e^{(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\mu)/(k_{B}T)}+1}}
,
where

\mu
is the Fermi energy.

\hat{j}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}\hat{v}_{x}+\hat{v}_{x}\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}\}
is the spin current operator,

\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}
is the Pauli operator,
\hat{v}_{x}
and
\hat{v}_{y}
are
velocity operators,

is the broadening approximated as extrinsic scattering

Unconventional spin Hall effect

Neumann’s principle states that

Wannierizations

In our work,

PseudoDojo

The semicore orbitals corresponding to deep bands are excluded from

The maximum of the frozen window is set as Fermi energy + 2 eV

To provide a high-quality Wannier Hamiltonian database,
failed cases (42/426)

Retinal neurovascular assessment and choroidal vascularity index in patients with keratoconus
Authors: Javad Sadeghi, Yalda Barooti, Hamid Gharaei, Nasser Shoeibi, Mohammadreza Sedaghat, Negareh Yazdani, Ali Abasi Mehrabadi, Mehrdad Motamed Shariati
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to evaluate the macular and the optic nerve head thickness and vascular profiles in KC patients and compare them with two groups of healthy emmetrope and subjects with myopic-astigmatism. This cross-sectional study was conducted at Khatam-Al-Anbia Eye Hospital between 2022 and 2023. Subjects aged 18 to 40 were prone to be included in the study. The participants in this study were grouped into three categories: emmetrope (E), myopic-astigmatic (MA), and keratoconus (KC). All participants underwent a comprehensive ocular examination, as well as macular and optic nerve head (ONH) optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA). In this study, 143 subjects, 50 cases in the KCN group, 46 cases in the E group, and 47 cases in the MA group, enrolled. There was no difference between the three groups regarding age (p = 0.123) and gender (p = 0.632). The superficial and deep capillary densities at the fovea, parafovea, and perifovea were significantly lower in KC patients than in the control groups (p < 0.01). The radial peripapillary capillary (RPC)- all vessels’ density is significantly lower in the KC group (p < 0.001). Besides, the choroidal vascularity index (CVI) and choroidal luminal area (CLA) were considerably higher in KC patients (p < 0.001). The macular and ONH vascular profile in KC patients significantly differs from the vascular profile of healthy controls. Further scientific evidence regarding the systemic implications of keratoconus on the vascular system would be desirable to understand the connections between KC and vascular disease.Keywords: Keratoconus, Optical coherence tomography angiography, Optic disc, Macula, Vessel density, Choroidal vascularity indexSubject terms: Diseases, Medical research

Introduction

Keratoconus is a bilateral asymmetric disorder characterized by progressive corneal thinning and steepening. Previous studies have indicated a broad range of disease prevalence, between 50 and 230 per 100,000 individuals, due to the role of racial factors, age, and differences in lifestyle in the occurrence of the disease1,2.

The pathogenesis of the disease is not fully understood. Several risk factors such as eye rubbing, positive family history, and history of atopy have been suggested1. In addition, predisposing genes have also been introduced in previous studies3,4. More recent studies point to the role of inflammation in keratoconus5,6. The central core mechanism in the pathogenesis of KC is related to collagen disorder, which manifests as progressive thinning of the corneal stroma and interruption of Bowman’s layer7. Certain connective tissue disorders, such as Marfan and Ehlers-Danlos, which have been linked to keratoconus in earlier research, have comparable molecular alterations8.

Considering collagen changes and the role of inflammation in the pathogenesis of KC, the hypothesis is raised as to whether collagen disorder influences the neurovascular structures of the retina and optic nerve in these patients. On the other hand, neurovascular evaluations of the retina and optic nerve with the optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) method in KC patients may be affected by corneal aberrations9.

This study aims to evaluate the macular and the optic nerve neurovasculature in KC patients and compare them with two groups of healthy emmetrope and subjects with myopic-astigmatism.

Methods

Ethical considerations

This study follows the declaration of Helsinki. Also, the Mashhad University of Medical Sciences ethical committee approved the study (approval number: IR.MUMS.MEDICAL.REC.1402.150). Informed consent was obtained from participants after a complete description of the study.

Participants and examinations

This cross-sectional study was conducted at Khatam-Al-Anbia Eye Hospital between 2022 and 2023. Subjects aged 18 to 40 were prone to be included in the study. The participants in this study were grouped into three categories: emmetrope (E), myopic-astigmatic (MA), and keratoconus (KC). We used healthy emmetropic eyes and myopic-astigmatic eyes as the control groups. Emmetropia was defined as the cycloplegic refraction between − 0.50 and + 0.50 diopters. Also, the MA group consists of subjects with myopia less than − 6.00 diopters and astigmatism equal to or less than 2.50 diopters.

Keratoconus patients referred to the hospital were included in the study based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The study included keratoconic eyes with the following characteristics: Skewed Radial Axis (SRAX) larger than 22 degrees, Keratoconus index (KCI) percentage greater than Zero, Keratoconus prediction index (KPI) greater than 0.23, and Inferior-superior (I-S) asymmetry index more than 1.4 diopters.

Exclusion criteria included the use of contact lenses in the past month, the presence of any systemic disease or the use of topical or systemic drugs affecting the blood circulation of the optic nerve or macula, having undergone eye surgery in the past six months, and having a history of surgical procedures for keratoconus, including corneal collagen cross-linking. In this study, we ensured that all participants were free of systemic diseases that could potentially affect retinal neurovasculature. A detailed medical history was obtained from each participant through a comprehensive questionnaire. This included inquiries about a history of systemic conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular diseases, or autoimmune disorders.

To rule out any potential disorders, all interview respondents had thorough eye tests that included slit lamp examination, non-contact tonometry, and retinal examination. If both eyes satisfied the inclusion criterion, the right eye was picked, and the eye that matched the criteria was the one that was chosen. After imaging, cycloplegic refraction was carried out using 1% tropicamide drops (Mydrax, Sina Daru, Tehran, Iran) to lessen the impact of cycloplegic drops on both anterior and posterior segments. The Topcon KR 8000 autorefractor-keratometer (Topcon Corp., Tokyo, Japan) was used to acquire the refraction. The mean spherical equivalent (MSE) was used to report ocular refraction.

Imaging protocols

Using the Tomey optical biometer OA-2000 (OA-2000, Tomey, Japan), the axial length (AL) was determined. The corneal topographer TMS-4 (Tomey Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) and Pentacam AXL (Pentacam®-AXL, Oculus, Germany) were used to evaluate the anterior segment and gather a comprehensive list of corneal characteristics and Keratoconus indices.

We used OCT and OCT-A imaging of the disc and macula (AngioVue, Version 2017.1.0.151, Optovue, Fremont, CA, USA) to evaluate the macular and optic nerve head (ONH) vascular and thickness profile. Two areas were covered: a 6 × 6 mm area focused on the fovea and a 4.5 × 4.5 mm area centered on the optic nerve head (ONH), encompassing the papillary and peripapillary regions.

Using the device’s built-in software, the vessel density (VD) picture was combined with the en-face OCT image of the ONH region to determine the VD of the papillary area. The papillary VD was then automatically generated. A trained operator centered two circles—one at the disc’s edge and the other at a radius of 2.25 mm from the disc’s center—on the ONH on the en-face OCT image for the peripapillary region VD measurement (4.5 mm diameter). The peripapillary region was described as the space formed by a doughnut-shaped gap between them. The superior/inferior semicircles and the whole picture (360 degrees around the ONH) were used to estimate the RPC plexus density. The analyzed variables included the following: small vessel density (SVD)- inside the disc, all vessel density (AVD)- inside the disc, radial peripapillary capillary (RPC) density- small vessels, radial peripapillary capillary (RPC)- all vessels, cup/disc area ratio (CDAR), cup/disc vertical ratio (CDVR), cup/disc horizontal (CDHR), Rim Area, Disc Area, Cup Volume, and peripapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) thickness. If the quality of OCTA images was less than 7/10, imaging was repeated.

Foveal VD is the density of the superficial capillary plexus in a 1 mm diameter circle centered on the fovea’s center (internal limiting membrane to inner plexiform layer). The ring occupying the space between the foveal region and the 2.5 × 2.5 mm area centered on the foveal center was described as the parafoveal VD. The outer ring between the 6 × 6 mm diameter on the foveal center and the outer border of the parafovea was identified as the perifoveal VD. The temporal, nasal, superior, and inferior quadrants were measured. The analyzed variables included the following: superficial capillary plexus (SCP) density- Fovea, deep capillary plexus (DCP) density- Fovea, SCP- Para Fovea, DCP- Para Fovea, SCP- Peri Fovea, and DCP- Peri Fovea. If the quality of OCTA images was less than 7/10, imaging was repeated.

The software automatically segmented the retinal layers to measure the fovea, parafovea, and peri-foveal thickness. The inner boundary was identified as the inner limiting membrane (ILM), and the outer border as the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE). Segmentation lines were automatically placed on the inner nuclear layer (INL) as the outer border and the ILM as the inner border to estimate the foveal inner thickness. The analyzed variables included the following: Foveal Thickness, Para Foveal Thickness, Peri Foveal Thickness, and ganglion cell complex (GCC).

Choroidal Vascularity Index (CVI) calculation protocol

EDI-OCT was used to assess the choroidal thickness (CT). The distance between the choroidal–scleral junction and the external border of the retinal pigmented epithelium (RPE) was defined as subfoveal choroidal thickness (SFCT).

The National Organization of Wellbeing, Bethesda, MD, USA, freely provided the ImageJ program (http://imagej.nih.gov/ij/), which was used to quantify the choroidal vascularity index (CVI). With the software’s default option, an 8-bit picture was created from an EDI-OCT B-scan image. The total choroidal area (TCA) at 750 microns on each side of the foveal center was determined by manually sketching the bottom boundary at the choroid–sclera intersection and the lower border of the RPE. To limit the choroidal luminal area (CLA) and stromal area (CSA), we used the Niblack auto local threshold algorithm as the image correction technique. CVI was calculated as the ratio of TCA to LA10,11.

Statistical analysis

To evaluate the data, we utilized the statistical package for social sciences (SPSS, software version 16). The participant’s features were described using descriptive statistical methods. The Shapiro-Wilk test was employed to evaluate the measure distribution.

We used ANOVA and Tukey post hoc analysis to compare the vascular and neuronal profiles of macula and optic nerve head parameters between the KC patients and control groups (E and MA). Also, we utilized correlation analysis using Python 3.7, Pandas, Scikit-learn, and Seaborn modules, regarding the biometric parameters, and macular and ONH vascular profiles. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Descriptive analysis

In this study, 143 subjects, 50 cases in the KCN group, 46 cases in the E group, and 47 cases in the MA group enrolled. 73 participants were male and 70 were female. The age range of participants was 18 to 39 years old with a mean ± standard deviation (SD) of 27.35 ± 6.35. There was no difference between the three groups regarding age (p = 0.123) and gender (p = 0.632).

Discussion

KC is a common degenerative disease that typically develops in early adulthood and causes blurred vision. According to recent epidemiological investigations, the prevalence of KC in the general population was estimated to be 265 cases per 100,000, with an annual incidence of 1:7.500 among the 10 to 40-year-old population. Even though the precise pathogenesis of KC is unknown, it is a complex condition wherein genetic, environmental, hormonal, and metabolic variables all play significant roles.

KC was thought to be a non-inflammatory disease; nevertheless, it has now been established that inflammation plays a significant part in the etiopathogenesis of KC. It has been discovered that inflammatory mediators on the ocular surface of eyes with KC are substantially higher than those in healthy eyes. Most recognized cellular biological changes in KC are related to oxidative stress, hypoxia, and inflammation.

This study investigated the vascular and structural profile of the optic nerve, retina, and choroid in the macula area in KC patients. We compared these parameters with the control group. Considering the possibility of the effect of refractive error and astigmatism on the evaluation of the neurovascular profile of the posterior segment, in this study, we used two categories of subjects as the control groups, which included emmetrope and myopic-astigmatism participants.

We used OCTA to investigate the ONH and macular vascular and tomographic profiles. Our results showed significantly lower whole image SVD and AVD values in KC patients compared to the control groups. Furthermore, we found that the RPC- all vessels’ density is significantly lower in the KC group. Optic nerve vascular density in KC patients has been evaluated in previous studies. Dogan et al. showed a significantly lower RPC inside disc vessel density in KC patients compared to the control group12, which is compatible with the results of this study. Regarding the ONH tomographic profile, we found no significant difference between the KC patients and the control groups. In terms of ONH tomographic parameters, previous investigations have shown different results. Contrary to the results of our study, Cankaya et al. showed that disc area and cup area are significantly larger in KC patients than in the control group13. However, regarding the peripapillary RNFL thickness, the results of our study and the mentioned study are similar and show no significant difference between KC and the control group.

Our results showed that the peri-foveal retinal thickness is significantly higher in the KC group compared to the control groups. Although KC patients had a lower foveal and parafoveal retinal thickness than the emmetropes, the difference was not statistically significant. The results of previous studies regarding macular retinal thickness in keratoconus patients have not shown consistent results. Most studies indicated no difference in fovea thickness in KC patients and the control group14–17, which is compatible with our results. However, significantly higher and lower thickness has been shown in some studies18,19. Considering the parafoveal and perifoveal retinal thickness, some studies showed a significantly higher parafoveal and perifoveal retinal thickness in KC than in the healthy control group18,20. The exact mechanism of retinal thickness changes in KC patients is not known. However, hypotheses can be made in this field. The first idea uses the Stiles–Crawford effect (SCE) of the first kind to explain the posterior segment’s compensating response21. According to this phenomenon, there is a notable decrease in luminous efficiency when oblique light rays enter the eye and pass through the pupillary margins. In this scenario, the photoreceptors’ directional sensitivity decreases, and the retinal tissue adjusts to counteract the decrease in light intensity by increasing the absorbance of the brighter central incident light rays. However, it is unclear how this occurrence affects retinal morphology. The second explanation for the significant changes in the retina related to KCN could be attributed to another compensatory mechanism called photostasis, which is a prolonged adaptive reaction of the retinal photoreceptors developed in response to changes in the lighting conditions in the environment22,23. Photostasis causes the elongation of the outer portion of rod cells24. The third mechanism of retinal thickening in KC patients is activated muller cells in response to augmented oxidative stress and increased inflammatory biomarkers in these patients25.

Regarding the macular vascular profile, we showed that the SCP and DCP densities at the fovea, parafovea, and perifovea are significantly lower in KC patients than in the control groups. We hypothesized that oxidative stress and degenerative processes may be involved in capillary dropout and lower vascular density at the macula. Besides, Changes in the collagen fiber pattern affect blood vessel size. It has been demonstrated that KC presents histopathological changes in corneal extracellular matrix proteins and collagen types, especially collagen type I, the main component of the corneal stroma26,27. Collagen type I is also the main component of the media and adventitia of vascular walls throughout the human body. Our results are compatible with the results of some previous studies12,28. However, the clinical significance of these findings is not fully understood.

To evaluate the choroidal vascular profile, we used the ImageJ software to process the macular EDI-OCT image. As previous studies showed, the choroidal vascularity index (CVI) is a more reliable parameter than the choroidal thickness (CT) to assess the choroidal vasculature. CVI is less prone to be influenced by axial length, age, gender, and systemic conditions. Furthermore, it generates more information about the choroidal vasculature than the CT. Our results showed that CVI is significantly higher in KC patients compared to the emmetropes and myopic-astigmatic participants. The higher CVI was related to the higher choroidal luminal area (CLA) in the KC group. Some previous studies showed that the CT is significantly higher in KC than in the control group29–31. The inflammatory process encourages changes to the blood supply, cellular components, and chemical environment of the affected area. These structural and functional modifications can result in transient and, or permanent changes to the choroidal tissue. Besides, the higher choroidal luminal area could be justified by the compensatory mechanism for lower retinal circulation. Also, the difference in the choroidal and scleral biomechanics in KC patients is another hypothesis to justify the higher choroidal luminal area.

It is known that the choroidal vascularity index gives us the ratio of choroidal vessels and total choroidal area. More CVI in KC patients can be due to thinner vessel walls. In this study, it was found that the vessels were thinner in keratoconus patients. The CVI measures the ratio of vascular to stromal components rather than absolute vessel size. A reduction in stromal volume, which can accompany thinner choroidal vessels, may lead to an increase in the relative proportion of the vascular component, thereby elevating the CVI. Additionally, the thinning of choroidal vessels might be accompanied by other compensatory vascular changes, such as increased vessel density or redistribution, which could affect the overall CVI. Further studies are warranted to explore these mechanisms and their potential implications for understanding the vascular alterations in keratoconus.

This study faced some limitations. It is possible that optical defects, such as defocus due to the irregular corneal surface and astigmatism in KC, impacted the OCT-A assessments. As a result, it cannot be stated whether actual vascular abnormalities in KC patients occur. We tried to partially overcome this limitation by categorizing the control group as the emmetrope and myopic-astigmatic subjects. Another limitation was the small sample size because of our restricted inclusion criteria. However, this study is among the studies with the largest sample sizes. Additionally, since keratoconus is an insidious disease with a slow onset, it was not possible to collect the exact duration of illness in participants.

In summary, we showed that the macular and ONH vessel densities are significantly lower in KC patients. Besides, the CVI and CLA were significantly higher in KC patients. Further scientific evidence regarding the systemic implications of keratoconus, specifically on the vascular system and especially on the microcirculation, would be desirable to improve our understanding of the connections between KC and vascular disease. Defocus caused by not treatable astigmatism dramatically affects the accuracy of OCT-A measurements and leads to artefactually decreased VD measurements, making it difficult to draw any firm conclusions regarding actual changes in retinal microcirculation in KC.

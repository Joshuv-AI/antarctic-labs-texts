Field-induced spin continuum in twin-free Na 3 Co 2 SbO 6 revealed by magneto-THz spectroscopy
Rongsheng Li, Liyu Shi, Yuchen Gu, Yue Chen, Xintong Li, Qiong Wu, Shuxiang Xu, Dong Wu, Yuan Li, Fa Wang, Tao Dong, Nanlin Wang

Abstract
The honeycomb magnet Na 3 Co 2 SbO 6 recently emerged as a promising candidate for realizing Kitaev quantum spin liquid with relatively low levels of structural disorder. While the precise spin Hamiltonian remains controversial, the potential existence of a quantum spin liquid or other novel quantum magnetic phases continues to stimulate investigation. Here, we study the temperature and magnetic field-dependent spin excitations of Na 3 Co 2 SbO 6 on a twin-free single crystal using magneto-terahertz (THz) spectroscopy, focusing on magnetic anisotropy and field-induced unusual phases. We observe a low-energy continuum excitation above T N T_{N} and a 0.5 THz (2 meV) spin wave excitation in magnetic order under zero field. Upon applying an in-plane magnetic field, the spin waves transform to a magnetic continuum over an intermediate field range, above which the system enters a spin-polarized state. Crucially, the spin excitation spectra reveal striking anisotropy between the a and b crystallographic axes, demanding description by a strongly anisotropic spin model. These findings establish Na 3 Co 2 SbO 6 as a model system for investigating field-tunable quantum magnetism and potential spin liquid behavior in highly anisotropic systems.

Finally, we discuss the possible interpretations of the observed continuum. While the continuum may arise from Kitaev-type spin fractionalization, an alternative interpretation is based on the XXZ-J
1
-J
3
model, where low-energy spinon excitations originating from a Dirac spin liquid state persist within a narrow region of the J

1

\omega/J_{1}\sim 0.1

\chi_{2}(\omega)

The sum rule analysis suggests the existence of a small fraction of continuum excitations below the low limit of our measured range. Future microwave spectroscopy could search for the predicted low-energy peak and further constrain the spin Hamiltonian. Furthermore, the existence of a QSL state in the XXZ-

In this study, we systematically investigated low-energy magnetic excitations in NCSO using time-domain terahertz spectroscopy. Signatures of the field-induced disorder state and magnetic continuum excitations are observed.
Notably, rotating the magnetic field from
a

b

The strong in-plane anisotropy provides unique opportunities for investigating anisotropic quantum spin models.

III
Time-domain terahertz spectroscopy

Time-domain THz transmission spectra were measured using a home-built spectroscopy system equipped with an Oxford Spectromag magnet

The time-domain signals of the sample and the reference (an empty aperture of the same size) were detected via free-space electro-optic sampling in ZnTe.

The complex transmission of the sample is given by the relation:

\widetilde{T}=\frac{4\widetilde{n}}{(1+\widetilde{n})^{2}}exp(\frac{i\omega d}{c}(\widetilde{n}-1))

\widetilde{n}

\widetilde{n}\approx n(1+\widetilde{\chi}/2)

\chi_{2}(\omega)=2\kappa/n

Magnon energy at
\mathbf{q}=0
in fully polarized states for generic bilinear spin models on the honeycomb lattice for Na
3
Co
2
SbO
6

We assume that Na
3
Co
2
SbO
6
is quasi-two-dimensional and consider a single layer of the Co honeycomb lattice. It is nearly a perfect honeycomb lattice, but the entire crystal and therefore the spin Hamiltonian does not possess exact
D_{6h}
point group symmetry. Note that spatial point group symmetry operations should be accompanied by the corresponding transformations in spin space due to spin-orbit coupling effects.

We use the orthogonal crystallographic axes along
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{b}
, and
\mathbf{c}^{*}\propto\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}
, and denote vector components along these directions by the subscripts
a
,
b
, and
c^{*}
, respectively. Denote the two sublattices of the honeycomb lattice by
A
and
B
. The
A
-to-
B
displacements of the
X
,
Y
, and
Z
types of nearest-neighbor bonds are given by
-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}-\frac{1}{6}\mathbf{b}
,
+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}-\frac{1}{6}\mathbf{b}
, and
\frac{1}{3}\mathbf{b}
, respectively.

The reduced point group symmetry for Na
3
Co
2
SbO
6
is
C_{2h}
about the
Z
bond centers, generated by the following two symmetry operations:

•

Spatial inversion about the
Z
bond centers, which exchanges the
A
and
B
sublattices without acting on the spins. This implies that all bond centers and hexagon centers are inversion centers, so there are no Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interactions on nearest-neighbor and third-neighbor bonds.

•

C_{2}
rotation around the
Z
bonds (i.e., around the
\mathbf{b}
axis), accompanied by the same
C_{2}
rotation in spin space (
S_{a}\to-S_{a}
,
S_{b}\to S_{b}
,
S_{c^{*}}\to-S_{c^{*}}
). We define the associated rotation matrix as

\mathcal{R}_{b}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

The generic bilinear spin model under an external magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
is

\hat{H}=\sum_{(ij)}\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathcal{J}_{ij}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j}-\mu_{B}\mathbf{B}\cdot\sum_{i}\mathcal{G}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{i},

(S4)

where the first sum runs over all distinct bonds
(ij)
with a
3\times 3
bilinear coupling matrix
\mathcal{J}_{ij}
, and the second sum runs over all spin sites
i
with
g
-factor tensor (referred to as the
g
-tensor hereafter)
\mathcal{G}_{i}
.

The space group symmetry requires that all
g
-tensors are identical, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{i}=\mathcal{G}
, and satisfy
\mathcal{R}_{b}\cdot\mathcal{G}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{b}^{-1}=\mathcal{G}
, meaning that
\mathbf{b}
must be a principal axis of the
g
-tensor. To simplify later discussions, we assume that
\mathcal{G}
is diagonal in the crystallographic basis, and that the
g
-factors along the
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{b}
, and
\mathbf{c}^{*}
axes are
g_{a}
,
g_{b}
, and
g_{c^{*}}
, respectively. (This assumption differs from that in Ref.
Lampen-Kelley et al. 2018
.)

In the following, we consider only
\mathbf{q}=0
classical spin configurations and magnon excitations. Their energies are fully determined by the
\mathbf{q}=0
spin interaction matrices,

\mathcal{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\sum_{j\in\ell^{\prime}}\mathcal{J}_{ij},

where site
i
belongs to sublattice
\ell=A,B
, and the sum runs over all sites
j
in sublattice
\ell^{\prime}=A,B
.

The condition
\mathcal{J}_{ji}=\mathcal{J}_{ij}^{\text{T}}
and the
C_{2h}
point group symmetry impose the following constraints:

\mathcal{J}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}=\mathcal{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{\text{T}},\quad\mathcal{J}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}=\mathcal{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}},\quad\mathcal{R}_{b}\cdot\mathcal{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\cdot\mathcal{R}_{b}^{-1}=\mathcal{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}.

Therefore, all
\mathcal{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
are real symmetric matrices with
\mathbf{b}
as one of their principal axes.

VI.1
Magnon energies for diagonal
\mathbf{q}=0
interactions under crystallographic axes

First consider the simplest case in which the interaction matrices are diagonal:

\mathcal{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime},a}&0&0\\
0&\tilde{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime},b}&0\\
0&0&\tilde{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime},c^{*}}\end{pmatrix}

in the crystallographic basis. Then, if the external field is applied along a crystallographic axis, the fully polarized moments will align along that axis as well.

For a large magnetic field
\mathbf{B}=(B,0,0)
and spins fully polarized along the
\mathbf{a}
axis,
the linear spin wave Hamiltonian at
\mathbf{q}=0
takes the form:

\hat{H}_{\text{LSW, polarized along }\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{q}=0)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{A}&\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{B}&\hat{b}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{A}&\hat{b}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{B}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\mathcal{H}_{a}({\mathbf{q}=0})\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{A}\\
\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{B}\\
\hat{b}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{A}\\
\hat{b}^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{B}\end{pmatrix}

(S5)

Here
\hat{b}_{\ell}
is the annihilation operator for
\mathbf{q}=0
Holstein-Primakoff boson mode on sublattice
\ell=A,B
.
The
4\times 4
real symmetric matrix
\mathcal{H}_{a}({\mathbf{q}=0})
is

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathcal{H}_{a}({\mathbf{q}=0})=&[\mu_{B}Bg_{a}-S\cdot(\tilde{J}_{AA,a}+\tilde{J}_{AA,a+}+\tilde{J}_{AB,a})]\cdot\mathbf{1}_{4\times 4}\\
&-S\begin{pmatrix}0&-\tilde{J}_{AB,a+}&\tilde{J}_{AA,a-}&\tilde{J}_{AB,a-}\\
-\tilde{J}_{AB,a+}&0&\tilde{J}_{AB,a-}&\tilde{J}_{AA,a-}\\
\tilde{J}_{AA,a-}&\tilde{J}_{AB,a-}&0&-\tilde{J}_{AB,a+}\\
\tilde{J}_{AB,a-}&\tilde{J}_{AA,a-}&-\tilde{J}_{AB,a+}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\end{array}

(S6)

Here
\mathbf{1}_{4\times 4}
is the
4\times 4
identity matrix,

\tilde{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime},a\pm}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime},b}\pm\tilde{J}_{\ell\ell^{\prime},c^{*}})
.
Diagonalize
\mathcal{H}_{a}(\mathbf{q}=0)
by
SU(2,2)
Bogoliubov transformation
produces the two
\mathbf{q}=0
magnon energies,

\epsilon_{a,\pm}(\mathbf{q}=0)=\sqrt{[\mu_{B}Bg_{a}-S\cdot(\tilde{J}_{AA,a}+\tilde{J}_{AB,a}-\tilde{J}_{AA,a+}\mp\tilde{J}_{AB,a+})]^{2}-S^{2}\cdot(\tilde{J}_{AA,a-}\pm\tilde{J}_{AB,a-})^{2}}

(S7)

The top and bottom signs correspond to the symmetric (
\hat{b}_{A}+\hat{b}_{B}
) and antisymmetric (
\hat{b}_{A}-\hat{b}_{B}
) magnon modes, respectively. Our THz experiments exclusively probe the symmetric modes, with energy given by:

\epsilon_{a,+}(\mathbf{q}=0)=\sqrt{[\mu_{B}Bg_{a}-S\cdot(\tilde{J}_{+,a}-\frac{\tilde{J}_{+,b}+\tilde{J}_{+,c^{*}}}{2})]^{2}-S^{2}\cdot(\frac{\tilde{J}_{+,b}-\tilde{J}_{+,c^{*}}}{2})^{2}}

(S8)

and the parameters
\tilde{J}_{+,d}\equiv\tilde{J}_{AA,d}+\tilde{J}_{AB,d}
for
d=a,b,c^{*}

are related to the Curie-Weiss temperatures of high temperature magnetic susceptibility

\chi_{d}(T)\sim\frac{\text{constant}}{T-\Theta_{d}}

along axis
d
by

k_{B}\Theta_{d}=-\frac{S(S+1)}{3}\tilde{J}_{+,d}=-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{J}_{+,d}
for
S=\frac{1}{2}
systems
Lampen-Kelley et al. 2018

.

Finally we have the magnon energies probed by THz experiments in terms of Curie-Weiss temperatures:

\epsilon_{a,+}(\mathbf{q}=0)=\sqrt{(\mu_{B}Bg_{a}+2k_{B}\Theta_{a}-2k_{B}\Theta_{b})(\mu_{B}Bg_{a}+2k_{B}\Theta_{a}-2k_{B}\Theta_{c^{*}})}

(S9)

For very large
B
,

\epsilon_{a,+}(\mathbf{q}=0)\approx\mu_{B}Bg_{a}+k_{B}(2\Theta_{a}-\Theta_{b}-\Theta_{c^{*}})-\frac{k_{B}^{2}(\Theta_{b}-\Theta_{c^{*}})^{2}}{2\mu_{B}Bg_{a}}+O(B^{-2})

(S10)

The classical saturation field
B_{\text{sat},\mathbf{a}}
must ensure that
both
\epsilon_{a,\pm}(\mathbf{q}=0)
are real positive,
namely
\mu_{B}B_{\text{sat},\mathbf{a}}g_{a}\geq-2k_{B}\Theta_{a}+2\max(k_{B}|\Theta_{b}|,k_{B}|\Theta_{c^{*}}|)
.
Note that this condition is necessary but not sufficient,
because the moments may have
\mathbf{q}\neq 0
components perpendicular to
\mathbf{a}
.

For field and spins polarized along other crystallographic axes, we just need to cyclically permute the
a,b,c^{*}
subscripts in the above formulas.

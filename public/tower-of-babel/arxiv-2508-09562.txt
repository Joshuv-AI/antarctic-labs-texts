Interpreting certain holomorphic Lagrangians that arise from the relative Langlands program, we construct moduli stacks underlying the generalized Slodowy categories of Collier–Sanders and G 𝐑 G^{\mathbf{R}} -Higgs bundles over a Riemann surface. 1.5.5.
Jacobson–Morozov data

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex reductive Lie algebra. Given the data of a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}=\langle e,f,h\rangle\to\mathfrak{g}
one can associate the following list of useful data which we generically term
Jacobson–Morozov data
. First of all, according to
\rho(h)
-weights we can decompose

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbf{Z}}\,\mathfrak{g}_{j}

so that
\rho(h)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{j}}=j\,\mathrm{Id}
. The positive (resp. nonnegative) weight spaces assemble to form a unipotent (resp. parabolic) Lie subalgebra which we denote by
\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{u}_{\rho}
(resp.
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{\rho}
) where we drop the dependence on
\rho
if the context is clear. It is convenient also to write
\mathfrak{u}_{+}\subset\mathfrak{u}
for those weight spaces of weight
\geq 2
.

The homomorphism
\rho
gives rise to a representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
on
\mathfrak{g}
by post composition with the adjoint representation. We write the decomposition into
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-irreducible representations as

\mathfrak{g}=W_{0}^{\oplus M}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\,W_{n_{j}}

where
n_{j}>0
are positive integers, and for an integer
k\geq 0
we write
W_{k}
for the irreducible representation of dimension
k+1
(equivalently, of highest weight
k
). We distinguish

V_{n_{j}}\subset W_{n_{j}}

the complex line of the highest weight space. Note that the direct sum of these
V_{n_{j}}
’s, along with the
W_{0}
’s, is exactly the kernel of the adjoint action of
\rho(e)
:

(7)

\mathfrak{g}_{e}:=W_{0}^{\oplus M}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\,V_{n_{j}}=\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{ad}_{e}).

The
0
th pieces
W_{0}
can be identified with the Lie algebra of the centralizer
C_{\rho}
of
\rho
:

\mathfrak{c}:=\mathrm{Lie}(C_{\rho})=W_{0}^{\oplus M}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}

and we shall use the notation
\mathfrak{c}
instead of
W_{0}^{\oplus M}
when we want to emphasize its structure as a Lie algebra.

If all the
n_{j}
’s that appear are even, then
\rho
is called an
even

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, and we write
n_{j}=2m_{j}
in this case. Since
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=0
, we have
\mathfrak{u}=\mathfrak{u}_{+}
and several constructions built from these Jacobson–Morozov data simplify considerably. In any case, since
W_{n_{j}}
can be generated by applying
\mathrm{ad}_{f}
to the highest weight line
V_{n_{j}}
, we can also write

(8)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{c}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\,\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n_{j}}\,\mathrm{ad}_{f}^{k}\cdot V_{n_{j}}.

Finally we define the
Slodowy slice
attached to
\rho
as the following affine subspace

\mathcal{S}_{\rho}:=f+\mathfrak{g}_{e}\subset\mathfrak{g}

considered as an affine subspace of
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. It is a transverse slice to the
U
-action on
f+\mathfrak{u}^{\perp}_{+}
.

G^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundles such a choice is not necessary.

Remark 3.13
.

One can without problem consider when
H
is an arbitrary reductive subgroup of
G
, but the stackyness and derived structures that are necessary for the global moment map to be Lagrangian may become unwieldy, especially if one’s goal is to deduce statements about the moduli space of stable Higgs bundles. For an extreme case, consider
H=\{1\}\subset G
being the trivial subgroup. Gaiotto’s Lagrangian
\mathrm{Lag}(M=T^{*}G)
has an underlying classical moduli stack parametrizing Higgs fields on the trivial
G
-bundle, admitting thus a smooth atlas by
H^{0}(\Sigma,\mathfrak{g}\otimes K)\simeq H^{0}(\Sigma,K)^{\oplus\mathrm{dim}(G)}
. However, as a derived stack we have
\mathrm{Lag}(M)\simeq R\Gamma(\Sigma,K)^{\oplus\mathrm{dim}(G)}
; this is the cotangent fiber to
\mathrm{Higgs}_{G}
at the trivial
G
-bundle, which is never classical.

3.3.1.

We explain briefly the origin of the terminology of
G^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundles, a reference of which can be found in
[
Hit92
]
. In short, a Kobayashi–Hitchin-type theorem of García-Prada–Gothen–Mundet
[
GGiR12
]
states that there is a homeomorphism between the moduli space of stable
G^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundles and the moduli space of infinitesimally irreducible
G^{\mathbf{R}}
-valued representations of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
. In other words, starting from a stable
G^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundle one may solve the associated Hitchin equation for
G
, and observe that the corresponding flat
G
-connection has holonomy valued in
G^{\mathbf{R}}
.

To this end, let
(E,\phi)
be a stable
G^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundle (so that
E
is a principal
H
-bundle), and let
F=\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(E)
be the
G
-bundle induced from
E
. Having a reduction of structure group to
H
induces a global holomorphic involution
\sigma:F\to F
of
G
-bundles with
E=F^{\sigma}
as fixed points, and there is an isomorphism

(37)

\displaystyle(E,\phi)\simeq\sigma^{*}(E,\phi).

By stability of
(E,\phi)
, there is a unique reduction of structure group
E=E^{\mathbf{R}}\times^{H^{\mathbf{R}}}H
to the maximal compact subgroup
H^{\mathbf{R}}\subset H
that solves the Kobayashi–Hitchin equation
[
GGiR12
, Theorem 2.24]
:

(38)

\displaystyle\Omega_{D_{A}}-[\phi,\rho^{*}\phi]=0,

where
\rho:E\to E
is the antiholomorphic involution of
H
-bundles whose fixed points define the principal
H^{\mathbf{R}}
-bundle
E^{\mathbf{R}}
,
D_{A}
is the Chern connection on
E
with respect to
E^{\mathbf{R}}
and
\Omega_{D_{A}}
is the curvature form of
D_{A}
. Since the Chern connection is an
H^{\mathbf{R}}
-connection, we have

(39)

\displaystyle\rho^{*}A=A.

Let
\tau:=\rho\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ\rho
be the antiholomorphic involution on
G
whose fixed points define the real form
G^{\mathbf{R}}
, so that
F^{\tau}
is a principal
G^{\mathbf{R}}
-bundle. We may apply the Kobayashi–Hitchin theorem
[
Hit87
,
Sim88
]
to
(F,\phi)
viewed as a stable
G
-Higgs bundle, then (
37
) and the uniqueness of solution to (
38
) implies that

(40)

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}A=A.

Consider the
G
-connection
\nabla:=D_{A}+\phi-\rho^{*}\phi
on
F
. Then (
38
) implies that
(F,\nabla)
is a flat
G
-connection, and we can calculate

\displaystyle\tau^{*}(\nabla)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{*}\rho^{*}(A+\phi-\rho^{*}\phi)

\displaystyle=A+\rho^{*}\sigma^{*}\phi-\sigma^{*}\phi\text{ by }\eqref{eqn:connection-form-fixed-by-rho},\eqref{eqn:connection-form-fixed-by-sigma}

\displaystyle=A-\rho^{*}\phi+\phi\text{ by }\eqref{eqn:complex-Cartan-induced-isomorphism}

\displaystyle=\nabla,

hence the holonomy of
\nabla
takes values in
G^{\mathbf{R}}
.

4.
Cayley morphism

We are ready to explain our core observation in this article, that the Cayley correspondence is induced from a morphism of Hamiltonian actions (or, following Remarks
2.12
,
2.13
, and
2.14
, a 2-morphism in the category
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathrm{GL}}
). In Sections
4.1
and
4.2
, for the reader’s convenience we recall the notion of magical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples and the original Cayley correspondence as constructed and studied in
[
BCG+24
]
. Given a magical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, we define two Hamiltonian actions in Section
4.3
and a morphism between them which leads, in Section
4.4
to a morphism of Lagrangians over the Hitchin moduli stack, extending the Cayley correspondence. We demonstrate the versatility and efficiency of our perspective by recovering those basic geometric properties obtained by
[
BCG+24
]
at the level of moduli stacks, which, combined with a study of the stacky and derived structure of Gaiotto’s Lagrangians in Appendix
A
, recover the main statements of

in Section

.

4.1.
Magical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triples

Given an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\rho:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\to\mathfrak{g}
, we define a vector space involution
\sigma_{\rho}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
acting as
\pm 1
on summands of the Jacobson–Morozov decomposition (
8
) of
\mathfrak{g}
by the following rule:

\sigma_{\rho}(x)=\begin{cases}x,&x\in\mathfrak{c}\\
(-1)^{k+1}x,&x\in(\mathrm{ad}_{f})^{k}(V_{n_{j}})\end{cases}.

Definition 4.1
.

An
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple of
\mathfrak{g}
is called

if
\sigma_{\rho}
is a Lie algebra involution of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\rho
is magical, the real form
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbf{R}}
associated to
\sigma_{\rho}
is called the

.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a magical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, the
Cayley real form

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{Cay}}^{\mathbf{R}}
is defined to be the real form associated to the Lie algebra involution
\theta_{\rho}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\theta_{\rho}(x)=\begin{cases}x,\quad\quad&x\in\mathfrak{c},\\
-x,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We warn the reader that the Cayley real form is not a reductive Lie algebra in general, as its complexification can contain factors of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathbf{G}_{a})
.

.

The evenness of magical triples provides another simplification: while the general definition of

is the

is the

4.4.

In this section we extend the Cayley correspondence of

]

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\rho
be a magical triple for

of

. Consider the morphism

]
) upon evaluation on
{\mathbf{C}}

Recall that
\Theta=(E_{0},\phi_{0})
is the uniformizing Higgs bundle from (

). The global moment map of

is given by the formula

∎

Remark 4.11
.

Strictly speaking, in
[
BCG+24
]
there was a restriction that the genus of

is at least 2. This restriction would prevent the moduli stacks from acquiring further derived and stacky structures (see Appendix

Because of Theorem
4.10
, we write

and refer to it as the
(

-twisted) Cayley space
. We also refer to

as the
(

-twisted) Cayley morphism
, reserving the term

for the construction studied in
[

]
.

At the level of moduli spaces, the Cayley correspondence of

-Higgs bundles as Cayley components. In the rest of this section, we show that the natural generalizations of these geometric properties also hold for the Cayley morphism.

Theorem 4.12
.

The Cayley morphism
\mathrm{Lag}(\phi):\mathrm{Cay}_{G,\rho}^{L}\to\mathrm{Higgs}_{G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}}}^{L}
induces an equivalence on tangent complexes

d\mathrm{Lag}(\phi):\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{Cay}_{G,\rho}^{L}}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Lag}(\phi)^{*}\,\mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{Higgs}^{L}_{G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}}}}.

Proof.

We first show that the morphism
\phi_{/G}:[M_{\rho}^{\prime}/G]\to[M_{\rho}/G]
induces an isomorphism on tangent complexes

Write
\mathfrak{v}=\oplus_{j}\,V_{2m_{j}}
. Note that the quotient stacks
[M_{\rho}^{\prime}/G]\simeq[f+\mathfrak{v}/C]

are both vector spaces quotiented by a reductive group, so the morphism of tangent complexes induced by
\phi_{/G}
at a point

is just the morphism of complexes concentrated in degrees
[-1,0]

(48)

d\phi_{/G,f+v}:\big[\mathfrak{c}\overset{X\mapsto f+\mathrm{ad}_{X}(v)}{\longrightarrow}f+\mathfrak{v}\big]\longrightarrow\big[\mathfrak{h}\overset{X\mapsto\mathrm{ad}_{X}(v)}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{m}\big]

where we recall that
\mathrm{Lie}(C)\simeq\mathfrak{c}
is a naturally a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{h}
. Note that by (
41
) and (
42
), the morphism (
48
) takes the block form

(49)

(50)

(X,m)\longmapsto(f+\mathrm{ad}_{X}(v))\oplus\mathrm{ad}_{f}(m).

Since

Knowing that

Towards showing that the Cayley correspondence
\mathrm{Lag}(\phi):\mathrm{Cay}_{G,\rho}^{L}\to\mathrm{Higgs}_{G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}}}^{L}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Spec}\,K}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

there is a dotted arrow making the diagram commute. We call the existence of this dotted arrow the

We first deduce an intermediate result which is generally useful, regarding mapping stacks with target a closed immersion.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma_{K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{X}_{K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{X}_{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma_{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{Y}_{R}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{R}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}

Consider an affine open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U\to\Sigma\}
of the curve, whose elements’ base changes to

and

will be denoted by

and

, respectively. Since

[

]

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{X}_{R}(U_{R})}}

We adapt Balaji–Seshadri and Balaji–Parameswaran’s proof of the semistable reduction theorem for principal bundles for the following lemma (cf.

Lemma 4.14
.

Proof.

Towards checking the existence part of the evaluative criterion, we let

To reduce to the connected case, we consider the connected-étale exact sequence of group schemes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(\Sigma_{R},H_{0})}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(\Sigma_{R},\pi_{0}(H))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

Theorem 4.15
.

The Cayley morphism

\mathrm{Lag}(\phi):\mathrm{Cay}^{L}_{G,\rho}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Higgs}^{L}_{G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}}}

is universally closed.

Proof.

Note that for any graded Hamiltonian
G
-action
M
, Gaiotto’s Lagrangian
\mathrm{Lag}^{L}(M)
is the fiber over
L^{1/2}
of the morphism
\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma,[M/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}])\to\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma,B\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}})=\mathrm{Pic}
; thus, to show that
\mathrm{Lag}(\phi)
is closed it suffices to show that the morphism

(55)

\widetilde{\phi}:L_{1}:=\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma,[M_{\rho}^{\prime}/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}])\longrightarrow L_{2}:=\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma,[M_{\rho}/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}])

is universally closed. Now the morphism of targets of these mapping stacks
[M_{\rho}^{\prime}/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]\simeq[f+V_{\mathfrak{m}}/C\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]\to[M_{\rho}^{\prime}/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]\simeq[\mathfrak{m}/H\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]
which induces

can be written as a composition

[f+V_{\mathfrak{m}}/C\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}[\mathfrak{m}/C\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]\overset{j}{\longrightarrow}[\mathfrak{m}/H\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]

which induces a factorization of
\widetilde{\phi}
into

\widetilde{\phi}:L_{1}\overset{I}{\longrightarrow}L_{3}:=\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma,[\mathfrak{m}/C\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}])\overset{J}{\longrightarrow}L_{2}.

Note that

It remains to show that

A.1.
Classicality of the Cayley space

Let
\mu:\mathfrak{L}(M)=\mathrm{Lag}(M)\to\mathfrak{M}(G)
be Gaiotto’s Lagrangian attached to some Hamiltonian
G
-space
M
. We are interested in the passage from
\mathfrak{M}(G)
to the moduli scheme of stable
G
-Higgs bundles
\mathcal{M}(G)
, and most importantly, what happens to
\mathfrak{L}(M)
in this passage.

Lemma A.1
.

The morphism
\mu:\mathfrak{L}(M)\to\mathfrak{M}(G)
is representable.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{L}(M)
is constructed out of base change from the morphism

(90)

\widetilde{\mu}:\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma,[M/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}])\longrightarrow\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma,[\mathfrak{g}/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}])

we may reduce to showing that
\widetilde{\mu}
is representable. Since the latter is a mapping stack with target the representable morphism
[M/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]\to[\mathfrak{g}/G\times\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}]
, its representability is also immediate.
∎

Corollary A.2
.

\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
is a (possibly derived) Deligne–Mumford stack.

Proof.

Since
\mu
is representable, we must have that the induced morphism on automorphism groups for any object in
\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
is injective. Indeed, if there existed some
x\in\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
for which
\mu_{x}:\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}}(x)\to\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}(x)=Z_{G}
has kernel, then we may consider the subgroup
\mathrm{Im}(\mu_{x})\subset Z_{G}
and pullback along the morphism
B\,\mathrm{Im}(\mu_{x})\to\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}
at
\mu(x)
to observe that the fiber is not representable. In particular, since
Z_{G}
is a finite group, we see that
\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
is a (possibly derived) Deligne–Mumford stack.

∎

From now on, we restrict to those Hamiltonian actions
M
of the second kind in Section
4.3
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{L}(M)
is the Cayley space associated to some magical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}(M)
be one of Gaiotto’s Lagrangians with
M
of the second kind in Section
4.3
. Then the restriction
\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
is a (classical) Deligne–Mumford stack.

Proof.

To detect classicality of
\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
we compute its tangent complex and show that it is supported in nonpositive degrees. Let
y=(E,s)\in\mathfrak{L}(M)
be a point, where
E
is the underlying
G
-bundle and
s
is a
K^{1/2}
-twisted section of the fiber bundle
M_{E}
. The tangent complex to
\mathfrak{L}(M)
at
y
is computed by the complex

\mathbf{T}_{y}\mathfrak{L}(M)=R\Gamma(\Sigma,[\mathfrak{g}_{E}\overset{\mathrm{act}_{s}}{\longrightarrow}T_{s}M_{E}]\times^{\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}}K^{1/2})[1]

where we used the description of
\mathfrak{L}(M)
as a mapping stack to pull back the tangent complex of
[M/G]
, which is concentrated in degrees
[-1,0]
. In principle, the hypercohomology spectral sequence tells us that
H^{1}(\Sigma,T_{s}M_{E})
may contribute to degree 1 of
\mathbf{T}_{y}\mathfrak{L}(M)
, but we will show that this does not occur for the Cayley spaces when we restrict to
\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}
.

Recall that in the notation introduced in Section
4.3
, we have

M=\left(f+\oplus_{j}\,V_{2m_{j}}\right)\times^{C}G.

Write
\mathfrak{v}:=\oplus_{j}\,V_{2m_{j}}
, and we may view
s
as a section of the associated
\mathfrak{v}
-bundle to the principal
C
-bundle
E
. We verify from Table
2
that
\mathfrak{v}
, as a
C
-representation, is a direct sum of a single nontrivial irreducible representation along with copies of the trivial representation:

(91)

\mathfrak{v}\simeq\mathfrak{v}_{0}\oplus\mathbf{1}^{\oplus r}.

Returning to the computation of the tangent complex
\mathbf{T}_{y}\mathfrak{L}(M)
using the decomposition (
91
), we see that we can split the tangent complex at
y
into two summands

\mathbf{T}_{y}\mathfrak{L}(M)\simeq R\Gamma(\Sigma,[\mathfrak{c}_{E}\overset{\mathrm{act}_{s}}{\to}\mathfrak{v}_{0,E}]\times^{\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}}K^{1/2})[1]\oplus R\Gamma(\Sigma,(\mathbf{1}^{\oplus r})\times^{\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}}K^{1/2}).

Since the curve
\Sigma
has genus at least 2 and the
K^{1/2}
-twists on the second summand are of weight
\geq 4
, the second summand is in fact a nonderived vector space in degree 0. For the first summand, we observe first that
s
gives rise to a
K^{m_{c}+1}
-twisted Higgs field
\phi
on
E
valued in
\mathfrak{v}_{0}
. By (
45
), we may regard
(E,s)
as a
\widetilde{G}^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundle, and the identification is compatible with tangent complexes:

(92)

R\Gamma(\Sigma,[\mathfrak{c}_{E}\overset{\mathrm{act}_{s}}{\to}\mathfrak{v}_{0,E}]\times^{\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{gr}}}K^{1/2})[1]\simeq R\Gamma_{\mathrm{Dol}}(\Sigma,(E,s)).

Stability of the point
y
implies the stability of
(E,s)
as a
\widetilde{G}^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundle. By a standard Serre duality argument (see Proposition 6.8 of
[
BCG+24
]
, for instance) along with the fact that
m_{c}>0
, we may conclude that (
92
) vanishes in degree 1.
∎

We are now ready to pass to moduli spaces. Write

\mathcal{M}(G):=\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st},\mathrm{rig}}

for the moduli space of stable
G
-Higgs bundles obtained by passing to the stable locus and rigidifying; it is well-known that
\mathcal{M}(G)
is a holomorphic symplectic manifold. The symplectic structures on
\mathfrak{M}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}(G)
are compatible in the sense that the rigidification morphism
\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}\to\mathcal{M}(G)
is a
Z_{G}
-gerbe, so it induces an isomorphism on tangent complexes equipped with their respective symplectic pairings.

Base changing the morphism
\mu:\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}\to\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}
along a splitting
\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}
of the
Z_{G}
-gerbe (which is possible upon picking an auxiliary base point on the curve), we obtain a morphism

\overline{\mu}:L(M):=\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}\times_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}\mathcal{M}(G)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(G).

Note that
L(M)
is a priori a derived scheme, since
\mu
is representable. By the previous Lemma, however, we can conclude the following.

Corollary A.4
.

L(M)
is a classical scheme and
\overline{\mu}
is a quasi-finite morphism onto a (possibly singular) Lagrangian submanifold of
\mathcal{M}(G)
.

While we cannot guarantee that the tangent space of
L(M)
has constant dimension, we may conclude that
L(M)
contains an open dense subset whose tangent spaces map onto a Lagrangian subspace of the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}(G)
.

A.2.
Classicality of the Cayley morphism

As a consequence of Theorem
4.12
and the classicality of the Cayley space, we see that the moduli stack of
G^{\mathbf{R}}_{\rho}
-Higgs bundles, for a magical
\rho
, is also essentially classical on the image of the Cayley morphism, as was stated in Theorem
4.16
. To formulate this consequence properly, recall the Hamiltonian spaces
M_{\rho}
and
M_{\rho}^{\prime}
for
\rho
a magical
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
triple, in the notation of Section
4.3
. Write
\mathcal{M}(G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}})
for the moduli space of stable
G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundles.

Corollary A.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}})^{\circ}\subset\mathcal{M}(G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}})
be the image of
L(M_{\rho}^{\prime})
under the Cayley morphism. Then
\mathcal{M}(G^{\mathbf{R}}_{\rho})^{\circ}
is a union of connected components, each of which maps quasi-finitely onto (possibly singular) Lagrangian submanifolds of
\mathcal{M}(G)
.

Proof.

The classicality of
\mathfrak{L}(M^{\prime}_{\rho})|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
, the representability of
\mathfrak{L}(M_{\rho})\to\mathfrak{M}(G)
, and Theorem
4.12
, together imply that the schematic Cayley morphism
L(M_{\rho}^{\prime})=\mathrm{Cay}_{\rho}\to\mathcal{M}_{G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}}}
is a morphism of schemes inducing isomorphisms on tangent spaces. Together with Theorem
4.15
we see that
L(M_{\rho}^{\prime})\to\mathcal{M}(G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}})
is an isomorphism onto connected components which we called
\mathcal{M}(G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}})^{\circ}
. By Corollary
A.4
, we deduce that
\mathcal{M}(G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}})^{\circ}
maps quasi-finitely onto Lagrangian submanifolds of
\mathcal{M}(G)
.
∎

It is interesting to note that if we consider
\mathfrak{L}(M_{\rho})
itself, i.e., the moduli stack of
G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}}
-Higgs bundles for a magical triple
\rho
, it is not
a priori
clear that
\mathfrak{L}(M_{\rho})
does not have non-classical components. One can observe immediately, as a consequence of the Lagrangianity of
\mathfrak{L}(M_{\rho})\to\mathfrak{M}(G)
that the following criterion holds.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
M
be the Hamiltonian
G
-action associated to a real form
G^{\mathbf{R}}
as in Section
4.3
. of The Lagrangian
\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}\to\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}
is classical if and only if
\mathcal{M}(G^{\mathbf{R}})
is quasi-finite over (possibly singular) Lagrangian submanifolds of
\mathcal{M}(G)
.

Proof.

We have seen that
\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
is a derived Deligne–Mumford stack with tangent complex concentrated in degrees
[0,1]
. The classicality of
\mathfrak{L}(M)|_{\mathfrak{M}(G)^{\mathrm{st}}}
is equivalent to the condition that the tangent complex is concentrated in degree 0, which is in turn equivalent to the tangent spaces of
\mathcal{M}(G_{\rho}^{\mathbf{R}})
being mapped to a Lagrangian subspace of the tangent spaces of
\mathcal{M}(G)
.
∎

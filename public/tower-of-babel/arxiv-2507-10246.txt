Electric-Field Induced Spin Wave Nonreciprocity in Noncoplanar Magnets
Xiao-Hui Li, Yuan-Ming Lu, Yuan Wan

Abstract
We show that an electric field can induce nonreciprocal spin wave dispersion in magnetic insulators with negligible spin-orbit coupling. The electric field controls the direction and magnitude of nonreciprocity through a nonlinear magnetoelectric effect without switching the magnetic ground state. By deriving spin space group symmetry constraints, we find only a subset of noncoplanar magnets exhibits this property, and identify a few candidates. For the example of hexagonal lattice tetrahedral antiferromagnet, our effective field theory analysis and microscopic model calculation yield results that are fully consistent with the symmetry analysis.

The reciprocity criterion narrows down the candidate magnetic orders to noncoplanar ones with unitary translations and

If the magnetic order loses its stability in an infinitesimal electric field, the spectral reciprocity, or the lack thereof, is no longer the inherent property of the zero field magnetic order but rather the new ground state. We therefore pose the

Our analysis focuses on the zero SOC limit. A weak SOC
\lambda\ll 1
breaks the spin
SO(3)
symmetry. The nonreciprocity criterion is nevertheless robust since it only concerns the time reversal part of the spin operation. However, the on-site anti-unitary symmetries unique to collinear/coplanar magnetic orders are lost. Meanwhile, the Goldstone modes acquire a gap. The magnetic orders that violate the stability criterion are stable up to a small critical field
E_{c}
, which vanishes as
\lambda\to 0
.

To conclude, we have shown that the electric field can generate and control nonreciprocal spin waves in noncoplanar magnetic insulators. The electric field operates through a nonlinear ME effect instead of SOC. Our work opens up a few research directions. First, the symmetry analysis can be carried out for more specialized cases such as
E\parallel
high symmetry directions. Second, the adiabatic spin wave theory, integrated with the density functional theory, permits a systematic search for ideal candidate materials. Third, the nonlinear ME effect may be mediated by phonons or electron collective modes, which could enhance the nonreciprocity. Finally, our work demonstrates the strength of SSG, combined with field theory, in constraining the Goldstone mode dynamics. It will be fruitful to extend the present analysis to hydrodynamics

;
Halperin and Hohenberg 1969
to account for dissipative effects.

The regular magnetic orders (RMOs) are defined in Ref.
Messio et al. 2011
as magnetic orders that preserve the lattice symmetries modulo global spin transformations. Recall that the spin space group (SSG)
G
is formed by symmetry operations of the form
(g_{L},g_{S})
, where
g_{L}
is a lattice operation and
g_{S}
a spin space operation. On one hand, all distinct space operations
g_{L}
that appear in the SSG form a group
G_{L}
. On the other hand, the elements of the form
(1,g_{S})
form a normal subgroup
S
of the SSG. It is easy to see that
G_{L}\cong G/S
. The definition for RMO is equivalent to the statement that
G/S
is isomorphic to the space group of the lattice that hosts the said order. They constitute the most symmetric magnetic orders for a given lattice.

In this section, we discuss the SSG of the noncoplanar regular magnetic order tabulated in Ref.
Messio et al. 2011

C^{z}_{2}
rotations. We list the accompanying spin space operations

in Table

. The notation is the same as the main text.
\Theta
refers to the anti-unitary time reversal operation.

refers to a proper spin rotation about the

axis by an angle

Appendix B
Goldstone modes in hexagonal tetrahedral order

In the main text, we have constructed the following field theory Lagrangian for the Goldstone modes in the hexagonal tetrahedral order:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=m^{\alpha}\partial_{t}\theta^{\alpha}-\mathcal{U}_{0}-\mathcal{U}_{1};

(B.74a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0}=\frac{\rho_{\parallel}}{2}\partial_{\parallel}\theta^{\alpha}\partial_{\parallel}\theta^{\alpha}+\frac{\rho_{\perp}}{2}\partial_{\perp}\theta^{\alpha}\partial_{\perp}\theta^{\alpha}+\frac{m^{\alpha}m^{\alpha}}{2\chi};

(B.74b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{1}=-\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}m^{\alpha}\partial_{\parallel}\theta^{\alpha}-\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}m^{\alpha}\partial_{\perp}\theta^{\alpha}.

(B.74c)

In this section, we detail the analysis of Eq. (
B.74
).

Applying the Euler-Lagrangian equation to Eq. (
B.74
), we obtain the spin wave equation:

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}\partial_{\parallel}+\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}\partial_{\perp})\theta^{\alpha}=\frac{m^{\alpha}}{\chi};

(B.75a)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}\partial_{\parallel}+\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}\partial_{\perp})m^{\alpha}=(\rho_{\parallel}\partial^{2}_{\parallel}+\rho_{\perp}\partial^{2}_{\perp})\theta^{\alpha}.

(B.75b)

Note fields with different label
\alpha
are decoupled. Inserting the plane wave solution:
\theta^{\alpha}(x,t)=\theta^{\alpha}e^{i(q\cdot x-\omega t)}
,
m^{\alpha}(x,t)=m^{\alpha}e^{i(q\cdot x-\omega t)}
into the equation, we find:

\displaystyle i(-\omega+\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}q_{\parallel}+\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}q_{\perp})\theta^{\alpha}=\frac{m^{\alpha}}{\chi};\quad i(-\omega+\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}q_{\parallel}+\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}q_{\perp})m^{\alpha}=-(\rho_{\parallel}q^{2}_{\parallel}+\rho_{\perp}q^{2}_{\perp})\theta^{\alpha};

(B.76)

Solving the above for
\omega
, we obtain the dispersion relation:

\displaystyle\omega(q)=\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}q_{\parallel}+\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}q_{\perp}+\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{\parallel}q^{2}_{\parallel}+\rho_{\perp}q^{2}_{\perp}}{\chi}}.

(B.77)

The positive semidefinite property of the potential energy
\mathcal{U}_{0}+\mathcal{U}_{1}\geq 0
requires:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{\parallel}q^{2}_{\parallel}+\rho_{\perp}q^{2}_{\perp}&-i(\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}q_{\parallel}+\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}q_{\perp})\\
-i(\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}q_{\parallel}+\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}q_{\perp})&\frac{1}{\chi}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0,

(B.78)

for any
q
. Since the diagonal entires are both positive semidefinite, the relevant constraint is:

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{\parallel}q^{2}_{\parallel}+\rho_{\perp}q^{2}_{\perp}}{\chi}\geq(\zeta_{1}E_{\perp}q_{\parallel}+\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel}q_{\perp})^{2}.

(B.79)

We may view the above as an quadratic form in
q_{\parallel,\perp}
:

\displaystyle f(q_{\parallel},q_{\perp})=[\frac{\rho_{\parallel}}{\chi}-(\zeta_{1}E_{\perp})^{2}]q^{2}_{\parallel}+[\frac{\rho_{\perp}}{\chi}-(\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel})^{2}]q^{2}_{\perp}-2\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}E_{\perp}E_{\parallel}q_{\parallel}q_{\perp}\geq 0.

(B.80)

The above condition holds for any
q_{\parallel},q_{\perp}
if and only if:

\displaystyle[\frac{\rho_{\parallel}}{\chi}-(\zeta_{1}E_{\perp})^{2}][\frac{\rho_{\perp}}{\chi}-(\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel})^{2}]\geq(\zeta_{1}E_{\perp})^{2}(\zeta_{2}E_{\parallel})^{2}.

(B.81)

Therefore, the positive semidefiniteness of the potential energy sets an upper bound on the electric field; beyond this bound, the tetrahedral order loses stability if the dielectric breakdown has not occurred already. We note this instability is different from those discussed in the main text, which is about the instability at infinitesimal electric field.

Appendix C
Adiabatic spin wave theory for spin waves in electric field

We apply the adiabatic spin wave theory
Niu and Kleinman 1998
;
Halilov et al. 1998
;
Zhou et al. 2025
to magnetic insulators in the electric field. While we consider the classical Kondo model
Martin and Batista 2008
;
Akagi and Motome 2010
;
Kato et al. 2010
for concreteness, the formalism can be extended to other models for magnetic insulators.

Lagrangian

We consider the classical Kondo model subject to a uniform electric field:

\displaystyle H=\sum_{\mathbf{k}s}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}-e\mathbf{A}}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}s}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\bm{k}s}-JS\sum_{r}\hat{n}_{r}\cdot\bm{m}_{r}.

(C.82)

Here, we employ the velocity gauge.
\bm{A}
is a uniform vector potential;
\bm{E}=-\dot{\bm{A}}
is the electric field.
e=-|e|
is the electric charge.
\epsilon_{\bm{k}}
is the dispersion relation of the electron.
J>0
is the ferromagnetic exchange constant.
S
is the spin length.
\hat{n}_{r}
is a unit vector in the spin space that describes the direction of the classical spin at site
r
.
s=\uparrow,\downarrow
labels the electron spin.
\bm{m}_{r}=\sum_{ss^{\prime}}\bm{\sigma}_{ss^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{rs}c_{rs^{\prime}}
is the electron magnetization at
r
.

The dynamics of this hybrid classical-quantum system is captured by the following Lagrangian:

\displaystyle L=S\sum_{r}\mathcal{A}_{r}\cdot\dot{\hat{n}}_{r}+\langle\Psi|\dot{\Psi}\rangle-\langle\Psi|H|\Psi\rangle.

(C.83)

\mathcal{A}_{r}=\mathcal{A}(\hat{n}_{r})
is a Berry connection in the spin space;
\nabla_{n}\times\mathcal{A}=-S\hat{n}
. The first term may be viewed as the kinetic term for the spin.
|\Psi\rangle
is the many-body state of the electrons.

We derive an effective Lagrangian for the spins. We assume that the bandwidth of the spin waves and
eEa
, where
a
is the lattice constant, are both much smaller than the electron particle-hole excitation gap. In this case, we may invoke the adiabatic approximation:

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=|\Psi_{G}(\{\hat{n}\},\bm{A})\rangle,

(C.84)

where the right hand side is the instantaneous ground state of the Hamiltonian
H
for given spin configuration
\{\hat{n}\}
and the vector potential
\bm{A}
. Substituting the above ansätz into Eq. (
C.83
), we obtain the following effective Lagrangian for the spins:

\displaystyle L=\sum_{r}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{r}\cdot\dot{\hat{n}}_{r}-\mathcal{E}_{G}+\bm{P}\cdot\bm{E}.

(C.85)

Here,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{r}
accounts for the electron Berry connection contribution:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{r}=\mathcal{A}_{r}+i\langle\Psi_{G}|\nabla_{\hat{n}_{r}}\Psi_{G}\rangle.

(C.86)

\mathcal{E}_{G}
is the instantaneous ground state energy of the electrons. The equilibrium spin configurations correspond to the minima of
\mathcal{E}_{G}
. The polarization
\bm{P}
is given by
Resta and Vanderbilt 2007
:

\displaystyle\bm{P}=-i\langle\Psi_{G}|\nabla_{\bm{A}}\Psi_{G}\rangle.

(C.87)

We are interested in the linear spin waves about the equilibrium spin configuration. It is convenient to rotate the local spin frames such that the local
\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{r}
axis coincides with
\hat{n}_{r}
at equilibrium. We write:

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{r}\approx u^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{r}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{r}+u^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{r}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{r}+(1-\frac{u^{2}_{r}}{2})\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{r}.

(C.88)

\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1),\scriptscriptstyle(2),\scriptscriptstyle(3)}
form a right-hand spin frame at site
r
.
u^{\scriptscriptstyle(1),\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{r}
parametrizes the deviations from ground state. Expanding Eq. (
C.85
) to quadratic order in
u^{\scriptscriptstyle(1),\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{r}
and first order in
\bm{A}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha r,\beta r^{\prime}}(\Omega^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}u^{\alpha}_{r}\dot{u}^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}-\Phi^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}u^{\alpha}_{r}u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}})+\bm{E}\cdot\bm{P}.

(C.89)

The first term in Eq. (
C.89
) is the kinetic term.
\Omega
is a real skew-symmetric matrix. It is given by:

\displaystyle\Omega^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}=-S\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\delta_{rr^{\prime}}+i(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle).

(C.90)

\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}
is the Levi-Civita tensor:
\epsilon^{12}=-\epsilon^{21}=1
,
\epsilon^{11}=\epsilon^{22}=0
. It encodes the Poisson bracket:

\displaystyle\{u^{\alpha}_{r},u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}\}=(\Omega^{-1})^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}},

(C.91)

in the Hamiltonian formulation.

The second term is the energy term. There is no term linear in
u
owing to the stationary condition at
u=0
.
\Phi^{ab}_{rr^{\prime}}
is the Hessian:

\displaystyle\Phi^{ab}_{rr^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{E}_{G}}{\partial u^{a}_{r}\partial u^{b}_{r^{\prime}}},

(C.92)

which is a real symmetric matrix.

The last term couples the spin waves to the electric field.
\bm{P}
is the electron polarization:

\displaystyle\bm{P}=\sum_{\alpha r}\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{r}u^{\alpha}_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha r,\beta r^{\prime}}\bm{Z}^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}u^{\alpha}_{r}u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}.

(C.93a)

The coefficients can be viewed as analogues of the magnetoelectric coefficients:

\displaystyle\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{r}=i(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial\bm{A}}|\frac{\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\Psi_{G}}{\partial\bm{A}}\rangle).

(C.93b)

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}=\frac{i}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial\bm{A}}|\frac{\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\Psi_{G}}{\partial\bm{A}}\rangle)+(u^{\alpha}_{r}\leftrightarrow u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}).

(C.93c)

In the case we are interested in,
\bm{\Upsilon}=0
by symmetry.

We note that the Lagrangian formally admits another term of the form
\bm{A}\cdot\bm{J}
, where

\bm{J}
may be interpreted as a current:

\displaystyle\bm{J}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha r,\beta r^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{A}}(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle)u^{\alpha}_{r}\dot{u}^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}.

(C.94)

However, as we shall see in Sec.
C
, the derivative vanishes in the thermodynamic limit due to gauge invariance. We therefore drop this term in the Lagrangian at the outset.

Equation of motion

Returning to the linearized Lagrangian Eq. (
C.89
). We first consider if the ground state is stable in the presence of
\bm{E}
. The total energy is given by:

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{tot}}=-\sum_{\alpha r}(\bm{E}\cdot\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{r})u^{\alpha}_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha r,\beta r^{\prime}}(\Phi^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}-\bm{E}\cdot\bm{Z}^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}})u^{\alpha}_{r}u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}.

(C.95)

If the linear coefficient
\bm{\Upsilon}
is non-zero, then the electric field will induces a displacement in the equilibrium spin configuration, indicating that the ground state is unstable. Furthermore, the matrix:

\displaystyle\Phi-\bm{E}\cdot\bm{Z}\succeq 0.

(C.96)

viz. positive semi-definite. This condition imposes a threshold for the electric field, beyond which the spin configuration loses stability.

Provided that
\bm{\Upsilon}=0
and the stability condition holds, the equation of motion for
u
reads:

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta r^{\prime}}\Omega^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}\dot{u}^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}=\sum_{\beta r^{\prime}}(\Phi^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}-\bm{E}\cdot\bm{Z}^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}})u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}.

(C.97)

In the momentum space,

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta l^{\prime}}\Omega^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(q)\dot{u}^{b}_{l^{\prime}}(q)=\sum_{\beta l^{\prime}}(\Phi^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(q)-\bm{E}\cdot\bm{Z}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(q))u^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(q).

(C.98)

Making the ansätz
u^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(q,t)=u^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(q)e^{-i\omega(q)t}
, the above equation reduces to the eigenvalue problem:

\displaystyle i\Omega^{-1}(q)(\Phi(q)-\bm{E}\cdot\bm{Z}(q))u(q)=\omega(q)u(q).

(C.99)

Provided that the matrix
K(q)-\bm{E}\cdot\bm{Z}(q)\succ 0
, the matrix on the left features pairs of positive and negative eigenvalues. The positive eigenvalues correspond to the frequency of the spin wave modes at wave vector
q
.

(C.100)

Here, all Hamiltonian derivatives are carried out at

and
\bm{A}=0
unless stated otherwise.
E_{n}
and
|\Psi_{n}\rangle
are respectively the

H_{0}
, i.e. the Hamiltonian with

and
\bm{A}=0
.
n=0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}=-JSm^{\alpha}_{r};\quad\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}=JSm^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{r}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{rr^{\prime}}.\quad(\alpha,\beta=1,2).

(C.101)

Here,
m^{\alpha}_{i}
is the electron magnetization density operator on site
r
in the local spin frame:

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}_{r}=\sum_{ss^{\prime}}(\hat{e}^{\alpha}_{r}\cdot\sigma)_{ss^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{rs}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{rs^{\prime}}.

(C.102)

Inserting the derivatives in, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Phi^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}=JS\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{z}_{r}|\Psi_{0}\rangle\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{rr^{\prime}}-(JS)^{2}\sum_{n>0}\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\alpha}_{r}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle+\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\alpha}_{r}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{E_{n}-E_{0}}.

(C.103)

The equilibrium spin configuration features a translation symmetry. We switch to the momentum space:

\displaystyle\Phi^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{i\in l}\sum_{j\in l^{\prime}}\Phi^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})}=JSm^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-(JS)^{2}\chi^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q}),

(C.104)

where
l,l^{\prime}
are the magnetic sublattice labels.
q
is the wave vector.
N_{c}
is the number of magnetic unit cells.
m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}
is the magnetic moment in the local
e^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}
direction on sublattice
l
:

\displaystyle m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}(\bm{q}=0)|\Psi_{0}\rangle.

(C.105)

Here,
m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})
is the magnetization density in momentum space:

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})=\sum_{r\in l}m^{\alpha}_{r}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}.

(C.106)

\chi^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})
is the susceptibility:

\displaystyle\chi^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q})|\Psi_{0}\rangle+\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q})|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{E_{n}-E_{0}}.

(C.107)

We switch to the diagonal basis of the equilibrium Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle H_{0}=\sum_{\bm{k}\lambda}\epsilon_{\lambda}(\bm{k})c^{\dagger}_{\lambda}(\bm{k})c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\lambda}(\bm{k}),

(C.108)

where
\lambda
is the band index. In this basis, the magnetization density operator reads:

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})=\sum_{\bm{k}}\langle\bm{k}\lambda|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})|\bm{k}+\bm{q}\mu\rangle c^{\dagger}_{\lambda}(\bm{k})c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mu}(\bm{k}+\bm{q}).

(C.109)

Here,
|\bm{k}\lambda\rangle
is the
one-body
state with momentum
\bm{k}
and band index
\lambda
. The matrix element is given by:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{k}\lambda|m^{\alpha}_{l}(q)|\bm{k}+\bm{q}\mu\rangle=\sum_{ls}(\hat{e}^{\alpha}_{l}\cdot\bm{\sigma})_{ss^{\prime}}\langle\bm{k}\lambda|\bm{k},ls\rangle\langle\bm{k}+\bm{q},ls^{\prime}|\bm{k}+\bm{q}\mu\rangle.

(C.110)

Using these, we obtain the following expression for
m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}
:

\displaystyle m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\lambda\in v}\langle k\lambda|m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}(\bm{q}=0)|k\lambda\rangle.

(C.111)

Here, the summation of
\lambda
is over the valence (“
v
”) bands. The susceptibility
\chi^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})
reads:

\displaystyle\chi^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}(\sum_{\lambda\in c}\sum_{\mu\in v}-\sum_{\lambda\in v}\sum_{\mu\in c})\frac{\langle\bm{k}\mu|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})|\bm{k}+\bm{q}\lambda\rangle\langle\bm{k}+\bm{q}\lambda|m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q})|\bm{k}\mu\rangle}{\epsilon_{\lambda}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})-\epsilon_{\mu}(\bm{k})}.

(C.112)

In the first term, the summation of
\lambda
and
\mu
are for the conducting (“
c
”) and valence (“
v
”) bands, respectively. In the second term, the range of
\lambda
and
\mu
are switched.

Kinetic term

The analysis of the kinetic term is essentially the same as that of the Hessian. The kinetic term features the following skew-symmetric coefficient:

\displaystyle\Omega^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}=-S\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\delta_{rr^{\prime}}+i(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle).

(C.113)

The first order perturbation theory yields:

\displaystyle i(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle)=i\sum_{n>0}\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle-\langle\Psi_{0}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle=i(JS)^{2}\sum_{n>0}\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\alpha}_{r}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle-\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\alpha}_{r}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{0})^{2}}.

(C.114)

In the momentum space,

\displaystyle\Omega^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=-S\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}+(JS)^{2}\eta^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q}),

(C.115)

where

\displaystyle\eta^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{i}{N_{c}}\sum_{n>0}\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q})|\Psi_{0}\rangle-\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q})|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{0})^{2}}.

(C.116)

In the diagonal basis of the equilibrium Hamiltonian
H_{0}
, the above expression reduces to:

\displaystyle\eta^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{i}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}(\sum_{\lambda\in c}\sum_{\mu\in v}-\sum_{\lambda\in v}\sum_{\mu\in c})\frac{\langle\bm{k}\mu|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q})|\bm{k}+\bm{q}\lambda\rangle\langle\bm{k}+\bm{q}\lambda|m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q})|\bm{k}\mu\rangle}{(\epsilon_{\lambda}(\bm{k}+\bm{q})-\epsilon_{\mu}(\bm{k}))^{2}}.

(C.117)

No direct current response

In the previous section, we have pointed out that the Lagrangian formally admits a current response:

\displaystyle\bm{J}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{\alpha r,\beta r^{\prime}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{A}}(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle)u^{\alpha}_{r}\dot{u}^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}.

(C.118)

We now prove that the derivative

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{A}}(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle)=0.

(C.119)

To this end, we observe that
i(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle)
is a Berry curvature. We compare the Berry curvature calculated at
\bm{A}_{x}=\bm{A}_{y}=0
and at
\bm{A}_{x}=2\pi/L_{x}
,
\bm{A}_{y}=0
, where
L_{x}
is the linear dimension of the lattice along the
x
direction. The Hamiltonian for the later vector gauge field is related to the one for zero gauge field by a large gauge transformation:

\displaystyle H(\bm{A}_{x}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}},\bm{A}_{y}=0)=UH(\bm{A}_{x}=\bm{A}_{y}=0)U^{\dagger},

(C.120)

where
U
is the unitary operator that effects the gauge transformation. Therefore, the spectra of both Hamiltonians are identical, while the eigenstates are related by the gauge transformation:

\displaystyle|\Psi_{n},\bm{A}_{x}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}},\bm{A}_{y}=0\rangle=U|\Psi_{n},\bm{A}_{x}=\bm{A}_{y}=0\rangle.

(C.121)

Meanwhile, the electron magnetic density operator is invariant under this large gauge transformation:

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}_{r}=Um^{\alpha}_{r}U^{\dagger}.

(C.122)

As a result, the matrix elements are identical:

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{n},\bm{A}_{x}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}},\bm{A}_{y}=0|m^{\alpha}_{r}|\Psi_{0},\bm{A}_{x}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}},\bm{A}_{y}=0\rangle=\langle\Psi_{n},\bm{A}_{x}=\bm{A}_{y}=0|m^{\alpha}_{r}|\Psi_{0},\bm{A}_{x}=\bm{A}_{y}=0\rangle.

(C.123)

It follows from Eq, (
C.114
) that:

\displaystyle i\left.(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle)\right|_{\bm{A}_{x}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}}}=i\left.(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle)\right|_{\bm{A}_{x}=0}.

(C.124)

Taking the limit
L_{x}\to\infty
, we obtain:

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial}{\partial\bm{A}_{x}}(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle)=0.

(C.125)

By the same token, the derivative with respect to
\bm{A}_{y}
vanishes as well.

Linear magnetoelectric coefficient

The linear magnetoelectric coefficient
\Upsilon
is the Berry curvature between
\bm{A}
and
u^{\alpha}_{r}
:

\displaystyle\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{r}=i(\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial\bm{A}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}\rangle-\langle\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\frac{\partial\Psi_{G}}{\partial\bm{A}}\rangle).

(C.126)

First order perturbation theory yields:

\displaystyle\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{r}=i\sum_{n>0}\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bm{A}}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle-\langle\Psi_{0}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial u^{\alpha}_{r}}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bm{A}}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{0})^{2}}

\displaystyle=ie(JS)\sum_{n>0}\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|\bm{I}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\alpha}_{r}|\Psi_{0}\rangle-\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\alpha}_{r}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|\bm{I}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{0})^{2}}.

(C.127)

Here, the derivative:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial\bm{A}}=-e\bm{I}=-e\sum_{\bm{k}s}\nabla\epsilon_{\bm{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}s}c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\bm{k}s},

(C.128)

is the electron current operator up to a minus sign.

Owing to the translation symmetry,
\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{r}
depends only on the sublattice label of the site
r
. We therefore define:

\displaystyle\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{l}=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{r\in l}\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{r}=\frac{ie(JS)}{N_{c}}\sum_{n>0}\frac{\langle\Psi_{0}|\bm{I}|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}=0)|\Psi_{0}\rangle-\langle\Psi_{0}|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}=0)|\Psi_{n}\rangle\langle\Psi_{n}|\bm{I}|\Psi_{0}\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{0})^{2}}.

(C.129)

In the diagonal basis of the equilibrium Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\bm{\Upsilon}^{\alpha}_{l}=\frac{ie(JS)}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}(\sum_{\lambda\in c}\sum_{\mu\in v}-\sum_{\lambda\in v}\sum_{\mu\in c})\frac{\langle\bm{k}\mu|\bm{I}|\bm{k}\lambda\rangle\langle\bm{k}\lambda|m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}=0)|\bm{k}\mu\rangle}{(\epsilon_{\lambda}(\bm{k})-\epsilon_{\mu}(\bm{k}))^{2}}.

(C.130)

In fact,
\Upsilon=0
by symmetry in the cases we are interested in. We may verify this fact by direct calculation.

Nonlinear magnetoelectric coefficient

The nonlinear magnetoelectric coefficient
\bm{Z}
is more involved. Using the
second order
Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation theory, we find:

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{F}^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}+\bm{F}^{\beta\alpha}_{r^{\prime}r}).

(C.131)

where

\displaystyle\bm{F}^{\alpha\beta}_{rr^{\prime}}=ie(JS)^{2}\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{(m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}},\bm{I},m^{\alpha}_{r})+(\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}},m^{\alpha}_{r})-(m^{\alpha}_{r},m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}},\bm{I})-(m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}},m^{\alpha}_{r},\bm{I})}{(0,n,m^{2})}

\displaystyle+ie(JS)^{2}\sum_{m\neq 0}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{(\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}},m^{\alpha}_{r})+(\bm{I},m^{\alpha}_{r},m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}})-(m^{\alpha}_{r},\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}})-(m^{\alpha}_{r},m^{\beta}_{r^{\prime}},\bm{I})}{(0,n^{2},m)}

\displaystyle-ie(JS)\delta_{rr^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{n\neq k}\frac{(\bm{I},m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{r})-(m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{r},\bm{I})}{(k,n^{2})}.

(C.132)

We have used the short hand notations:

\displaystyle\frac{(O_{1},O_{2},O_{3})}{(k,n,m^{2})}=\frac{\langle k|O_{1}|n\rangle\langle n|O_{2}|m\rangle\langle m|O_{3}|k\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{k})(E_{m}-E_{k})^{2}};\quad\frac{(O_{1},O_{2},O_{3})}{(k,n^{2},m)}=\frac{\langle k|O_{1}|n\rangle\langle n|O_{2}|m\rangle\langle m|O_{3}|k\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{k})^{2}(E_{m}-E_{k})};

\displaystyle\frac{(O_{1},O_{2})}{(k,n^{2})}=\frac{\langle k|O_{1}|n\rangle\langle n|O_{2}|k\rangle}{(E_{n}-E_{k})^{2}}.

(C.133)

In the momentum space,

\displaystyle\bm{Z}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{F}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})+\bm{F}^{\beta\alpha}_{l^{\prime}l}(\bm{q})^{\ast}).

(C.134)

which shows explicitly that
\bm{Z}(\bm{q})
is an Hermitian matrix.
\bm{F}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})
is given by:

\displaystyle\bm{F}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=e(JS)^{2}\bm{f}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})-e(JS)\bm{g}_{l}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{ll^{\prime}},

(C.135)

where

\displaystyle\bm{f}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{i}{N_{c}}\sum_{m,n\neq 0}\frac{(m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),\bm{I},m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}))+(\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}))-(m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),\bm{I})-(m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),\bm{I})}{(0,n,m^{2})}

\displaystyle+\frac{(\bm{I},m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}))+(\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}))-(m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}))-(m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),\bm{I})}{(0,n^{2},m)}.

(C.136)

and

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{l}=\frac{i}{N_{c}}\sum_{n\neq 0}\sum_{n\neq k}\frac{(\bm{I},m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}(\bm{q}=0))-(m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}(\bm{q}=0),\bm{I})}{(k,n^{2})}.

(C.137)

In the next step, we switch to the diagonal basis of the electron Hamiltonian at equilibrium. Computing the quantity
\bm{g}_{l}
is straightforward:

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{l}=\frac{i}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}(\sum_{\lambda\in c}\sum_{\mu\in v}-\sum_{\lambda\in v}\sum_{\mu\in c})\frac{\langle\bm{k}\mu|\bm{I}|\bm{k}\lambda\rangle\langle\bm{k}\lambda|m^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{l}(\bm{q}=0)|\bm{k}\mu\rangle}{(\epsilon_{\lambda}(\bm{k})-\epsilon_{\mu}(\bm{k}))^{2}}.

(C.138)

The expression for
\bm{f}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}
is significantly more complex:

\displaystyle\bm{f}^{\alpha\beta}_{ll^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\frac{i}{N_{c}}\sum_{\bm{k}}(\sum_{\alpha\in v}\sum_{\beta,\gamma\in c}-\sum_{\alpha\in c}\sum_{\beta,\gamma\in v})\,\mathrm{Summand},

(C.139a)

with

\displaystyle\mathrm{Summand}=\frac{(m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),\bm{I},m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}))}{(\bm{k}+\bm{q}\alpha,\bm{k}\beta,\bm{k}\gamma^{2})}+\frac{(\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}))}{(\bm{k}+\bm{q}\alpha,\bm{k}+\bm{q}\beta,\bm{k}\gamma^{2})}-\frac{(m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),\bm{I})}{(\bm{k}\alpha,\bm{k}+\bm{q}\beta,\bm{k}\gamma^{2})}-\frac{(m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),\bm{I})}{(\bm{k}+\bm{q}\alpha,\bm{k}\beta,\bm{k}+\bm{q}\gamma^{2})}

\displaystyle-\frac{(m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}))}{(\bm{k}\alpha,\bm{k}+\bm{q}\beta^{2},\bm{k}+\bm{q}\gamma)}-\frac{(m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),\bm{I})}{(\bm{k}\alpha,\bm{k}+\bm{q}\beta^{2},\bm{k}\gamma)}+\frac{(\bm{I},m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}),m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}))}{(\bm{k}+\bm{q}\alpha,\bm{k}+\bm{q}\beta^{2},\bm{k}\gamma)}+\frac{(\bm{I},m^{\alpha}_{l}(\bm{q}),m^{\beta}_{l^{\prime}}(-\bm{q}))}{(\bm{k}\alpha,\bm{k}\beta^{2},\bm{k}+\bm{q}\gamma)}.

(C.139b)

Application to hexagonal tetrahedral order

We consider the hexagonal lattice tetrahedral state:

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{1}\cdot\bm{r}}+\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{2}\cdot\bm{r}}+\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{3}\cdot\bm{r}}),

(C.140)

where the notations are the same as that of Sec.
A
. We define a set of local spin frames:

\displaystyle\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{r}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{1}\cdot\bm{r}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{2}\cdot\bm{r}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{3}\cdot\bm{r}}.

\displaystyle\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{r}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{2}\cdot\bm{r}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{3}\cdot\bm{r}}.

\displaystyle\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{1}\cdot\bm{r}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{2}\cdot\bm{r}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\hat{e}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}e^{i\bm{Q}_{3}\cdot\bm{r}}.

(C.141)

This specific choice of the local frames respects the SSG translation symmetries; as a direct consequence, all of the Lagrangian parameters,
\Omega
,
\Phi
, and
Z
features the translation symmetry of the underlying hexagonal lattice. It is then possible to present the dispersion relation of the spin waves in the
lattice
Brillouin zone rather than the magnetic Brillouin zone.

The electron Hamiltonian at equilibrium reads:

\displaystyle H=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\overline{\Psi}(\bm{k})H(\bm{k})\Psi(\bm{k})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\overline{\Psi}(\bm{k})\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{\bm{k}}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{1}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{2}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{3}\\
-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{1}&\epsilon_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{1}}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{3}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{2}\\
-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{2}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{3}&\epsilon_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{2}}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{1}\\
-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{3}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{2}&-\frac{JS}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma_{1}&\epsilon_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{3}}\end{pmatrix}\Psi(\bm{k}).

(C.142)

The summation is over the magnetic Brillouin zone.
\Psi(\bm{k})=(c_{\uparrow}(\bm{k}),c_{\downarrow}(\bm{k}),c_{\uparrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{1}),c_{\downarrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{1}),c_{\uparrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{2}),c_{\downarrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{2}),c_{\uparrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{3}),c_{\downarrow}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{3}))^{T}
is a column vector.

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\bm{k}}=-2t[\cos(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{1})+\cos(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{2})+\cos(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{3})],

(C.143)

is the tight binding dispersion on hexagonal lattice.
\bm{a}_{1}=(1,0)
,
\bm{a}_{2}=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})
, and
\bm{a}_{3}=(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})
. We may diagonalize the Hamiltonian by a unitary transformation:

\displaystyle H=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\overline{\Psi}(\bm{k})U(\bm{k})E(\bm{k})U(\bm{k})^{\dagger}\Psi(\bm{k})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\sum_{\lambda}\epsilon_{\lambda}(\bm{k})c^{\dagger}_{\lambda}(\bm{k})c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\lambda}(\bm{k}),

(C.144)

where
U(\bm{k})
is the unitary matrix that diagonalizes
H(\bm{k})
.
\lambda
labels the eigenvalues or Bloch bands.

The electric current operator
\bm{I}
is given by:

\displaystyle\bm{I}_{x,y}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\overline{\Psi}(\bm{k})V_{x,y}(\bm{k})\Psi(\bm{k})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\sum_{\lambda\mu}[U(\bm{k})^{\dagger}V_{x,y}(\bm{k})U(\bm{k})]_{\lambda\mu}c^{\dagger}_{\lambda}(\bm{k})c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mu}(\bm{k}).

(C.145)

In the second equality, we have switched to the diagonal basis of
H
. The matrices:

\displaystyle V_{x,y}(\bm{k})=\mathrm{diag}(v_{x,y}(\bm{k}),v_{x,y}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{1}),v_{x,y}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{2}),v_{x,y}(\bm{k}+\bm{Q}_{3})),

(C.146)

with

\displaystyle v_{x}(\bm{k})=2t(\sin(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\sin(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{2})-\frac{1}{2}\sin(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{3}));\,v_{y}(\bm{k})=2t(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{2})-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sin(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{a}_{3}));

(C.147)

The electron magnetic moment operator:

\displaystyle m^{\alpha}(\bm{q})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\overline{\Psi}(\bm{k})O^{\alpha}\Psi(\bm{k}+\bm{q})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathrm{MBZ}}\sum_{\lambda\mu}[U(\bm{k})^{\dagger}O^{\alpha}U(\bm{k}+\bm{q})]_{\lambda\mu}c^{\dagger}_{\lambda}(\bm{k})c^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mu}(\bm{k}+\bm{q}).

(C.148)

The matrices
O^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
, etc. are given by:

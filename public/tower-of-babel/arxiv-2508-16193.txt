Topological Stabilization via Higgs and Z -Boson Repulsions in Electroweak Monopole-Antimonopole Pairs
Dan Zhu and Xurong Chen, Khai-Ming Wong

Abstract
We demonstrate dual repulsive mechanisms stabilizing Cho-Maison monopole-antimonopole pairs (MAPs) through stress-energy tensor analysis. Higgs-mediated repulsion exhibits non-monotonic dependence on topological charge and Higgs self-coupling, confirming its topological origin while revealing a mass-controlled range transition beyond Yukawa suppression. Simultaneously, Z Z -boson field generates localized repulsive cores of radius R c ≈ 0.8 ​ m W − 1 R_{c}\approx 0.8\,m_{W}^{-1} , which aligns with weak interaction scales. Neutral charge distribution confirms Z Z -boson exchange as the physical mechanism. These competing effects—operating at topological and electroweak scales—establish a stabilization mechanism for Cho-Maison MAPs that extends to other topological solitons in the Standard Model.

Conclusion.
—We identified two distinct repulsive mechanisms in the Cho-Maison MAP configurations:

1.

Higgs-mediated repulsion
exhibits dual non-monotonic dependence on topological charge and Higgs self-coupling. The
n
-dependence confirms its topological root, while the
\beta
-dependence reveals complexity beyond simple Yukawa suppression. Crucially, its magnitude is comparable to the Coulomb-like topological magnetic attraction, suggesting a unified topological framework where it manifests as an attraction between monopole and antimonopole, but generates a repulsion when coupled to the Higgs field.

2.

Z
-boson-mediated repulsion
originates purely from weak interactions, producing localized cores of radius
R_{c}\approx 0.8\,m_{W}^{-1}
that aligns with the characteristic weak interaction scale. This confirms
Z
-boson exchange as a weak interaction mediator between topological defects.

These dual mechanisms collectively stabilize the Cho-Maison MAP configurations at finite pole separations against magnetic collapse.

This framework extends naturally to other Standard Model topological structures including electroweak vortex-rings and sphaleron chains, where Higgs-mediated topological repulsion may provide fundamental stabilization against attractive forces. Further study of this universal topological repulsion could reveal deeper connections between Higgs physics and topological field theory.

Free Hyperboloidal Evolution of the Einstein-Maxwell-Klein-Gordon System
João D. Álvares, Alex Vaño-Viñuales

Abstract
We present simulations of the Einstein-Maxwell-Klein-Gordon system on compactified hyperboloidal slices. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first hyperboloidal evolutions of this system that employ a common formulation like BSSN/Z4. Hyperboloidal slices smoothly reach future null infinity, the only location in spacetime where radiation (such as gravitational waves) is unambiguously defined. We are thus able to reach null infinity and extract signals there. We showcase the capabilities of our implementation in spherical symmetry with the evolution of a charged scalar field perturbing a regular spacetime and near an electrically charged black hole. We also present the collapse of a charged scalar field into a Reissner-Nördstrom black hole. Here we show sample evolutions and validation tests for these setups.



I
Introduction

The Einstein-Maxwell-Klein-Gordon (EMKG) system provides the simplest solutions to the Einstein equations beyond electrovacuum. Even in spherical symmetry, the EMKG system allows us to model interesting scenarios, such as a Reissner-Nördstrom (RN) (charged) black hole (BH)
[
76
]
. Despite not having been detected in the universe, RN BHs include the extremal behaviour also present in the more astrophysically relevant Kerr (rotating) BHs, and thus serve as simpler models to study it. Another option is to evolve a charged scalar field determined by the Klein-Gordon (KG) equation. If massive, scalar fields are potential dark matter candidates, and can also be used to create boson stars
[
43
,
35
,
36
,
79
]
. A previous numerical work evolving the Einstein-Maxwell equations in a formulation similar to ours include
[
1
]
. The recent
[
55
]
evolves the Einstein-Maxwell system on a pseudospectral code. Instead of on spacelike slices,
[
46
]
evolves the EMKG system on a double null foliation.
Such a null setup is also used in mathematical works that study (in)stability of the system motivated by the strong cosmic censorship conjecture. For instance, a charged massive scalar field perturbing RN is considered in
[
66
]
, which focuses on the behaviour of the Cauchy horizon. In our work, the initial slices considered for the RN spacetime are qualitatively like those in Fig. 3.13 in
[
70
]
, so they do not cross the Cauchy horizon. We believe it is unlikely that they do even during the dynamical part of the evolution, but this has not been determined.
A different setup (generalized wave coordinates adapted to the outgoing Schwarzschild light cones) is used in the global existence proofs for EMKG in
[
40
]
, although the spacetime considered here is close to Minkowski without matter. Our numerical simulations cannot proof existence or stability in the mathematical sense, but they show how the system behaves for the given initial setup.

The innovative aspect of our implementation is that it evolves the EMKG system on hyperboloidal slices
[
78
]
.
These slices are spacelike, but they become tangent to null rays at future null infinity,
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. The relevance for including
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
in the numerical integration domain is that it is the only location in spacetime where gravitational radiation is unambiguously defined, and so it can be extracted there without systematic errors. Also,
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
corresponds to the idealization of astrophysical observers measuring the emission of gravitational waves and electromagnetic radiation
[

,
42
,
60

Besides the hyperboloidal, other methods that also reach future null infinity are Cauchy-characteristic extraction/evolution
[

,
64
,
53
]
, whose evolution is limited in time, and Cauchy-characteristic matching
[
8
,
44
,
45
]
, which currently employs a partially ill-posed formulation.

[

on which the Conformal Einstein Field Equations
[

are constructed. Since their first numerical implementations
[

25

,

The purpose of this work is thus to extend this research line by simulating the EMKG system on hyperboloidal slices.

While the Maxwell equations are conformally invariant, coupling them to the EFEs and KG makes the relations between the quantitites more complicated. We focus on spherical symmetry, because the extensions of hyperboloidal slices to three dimensions are still under development. Spherical symmetry also implies that we do not need to consider the magnetic field
[
1
,
65
]
, making the electromagnetic coupling to the EFEs and KG equation solely through the electric field (due to the vanishing magnetic field). The asymptotic behaviour of hyperboloidal slices is not suitable for the inclusion of massive terms in the scalar field equation
[
29

, so our charged complex scalar field will also be massless. Here we will present results for several simulations achievable by our implementation, namely a charged scalar field perturbing a regular spacetime and a RN BH, and the gravitational collapse of a charges scalar field.
In a companion paper
[
2
]
we will exploit our access to future null infinity to study the behaviour of the scalar field there in the fully nonlinear regime.

A setup similar to ours has recently been presented in
[
16
]

The significant differences are that they use the Conformal Einstein Field Equations together with the conformal Gauß gauge and couple them to the conformally invariant wave equation, whereas we chose the commonly used BSSN/Z4 formulations with moving-puncture-type gauges and our wave equation is not conformally invariant.

II
Einstein-Maxwell-Klein-Gordon System

The action describing the EMKG system is given by:

S=\int\left(R-8\pi\left[(\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\phi)^{*}(\mathcal{D}^{\mu}\phi)\right]-F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\right)\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x\;,

(1)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar,
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}+iqA_{\mu}
is the gauge invariant covariant derivative,
F_{\mu\nu}
is the Faraday/electromagnetic tensor and
\phi
is the charged complex scalar field.
A_{\mu}
is the four-potential and
q
the scalar field’s charge. We assume a four-dimensional spacetime, so the abstract indices sum over time and 3 spatial dimensions. The fundamental constants
G
and
c
have been set to unity. The Faraday tensor is defined as
[
43
,
39
]
,

F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}\,.

(2)

The energy-momentum tensor describing the contributions from Maxwell’s and massless KG fields is given by

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=F_{\mu\alpha}F_{\nu}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}

(3)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\phi\mathcal{D}^{\mu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}\phi\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}\phi\,.

Due to the coupling between electromagnetism and the charged scalar field, the system does not change under the following transformations,

\phi\rightarrow e^{iq\theta(x^{\mu})}\phi\,,\quad A_{\mu}\rightarrow A_{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}\theta(x^{\mu})\,.

(4)

This provides the following conserved current, according to Noether’s theorem
[
35
,
43
]
:

J_{\mu}=\frac{iq}{2}\left[\phi^{*}\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\phi-\phi(\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\phi)^{*}\right]\,.

(5)

The asterisk * denotes the complex conjugate. The vanishing covariant derivative of the stress-energy tensor,
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0
, yields the KG and Maxwell’s equations

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\mathcal{D}^{\mu}\phi

\displaystyle=0\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}F_{\nu\mu}

\displaystyle=4\pi J_{\nu}\,,

\displaystyle\nabla_{[\mu}F_{\nu\sigma]}

\displaystyle=0\,.

The last equation involves the magnetic field. As our setup is in spherical symmetry, the magnetic part will not play a role and this equation can be ignored. Note that the current
J_{\nu}
in (
6
) is the same as the one in (
5
), denoting the coupling between electromagnetism and the charged scalar field.

III
Formulation of the Einstein Field Equations

Throughout this work, we will need to distinguish between different manifolds. We shall: start with the physical metric; rescale it via a conformal transformation (subsection
III.1
); decompose it in the time and spatial parts (ADM equations) and change them into a well-posed 3+1 decomposed formulation (BSSN/Z4) (subsections
III.2
and
III.3
). Thus, we have three different stages, and moreover quantities that are time-independent.
We express each of them in the following notation:

•

physical quantities, written with a tilde:
\tilde{g}_{ab},\tilde{K}_{ab},\tilde{\nabla}_{a},\tilde{n}_{a}
, …;

•

conformally rescaled quantities, with a bar:
\bar{g}_{ab}
, …;

•

3+1 decomposed and spatially conformally rescaled quantities, without anything on top:
\gamma_{ab}
,
A_{ij}
, …;

•

time-independent quantities, with a hat:
\hat{g}_{ab}
, …

According to this notation, every quantity appearing in Section
II
should have a tilde on top.

III.1
Conformal Transformation

Our method method requires being able to cover an infinite spacetime distance with finite values of our coordinates. This is possible by compactifying them (see (
45
)). However, the metric components now blow up in the asymptotic limit, and a regularization procedure is needed. The option we take is a conformal transformation following the usual procedure in
[
56
]
,

\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu},

(7)

where
\Omega
is the conformal factor, which goes to 0 at
\mathrsfs{I}
fast enough such that the rescaled metric is finite everywhere. The explicit expression for
\Omega
will be given later (subsection
VII.2
). Writing the Einstein tensor
G_{\mu\nu}
as a function of
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
(
G_{\mu\nu}[\bar{g}]
), the EFEs become:

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}[\bar{g}]+\frac{2}{\Omega}(\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\Omega-\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\bar{\nabla}^{\mu}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\Omega)

\displaystyle+

(8)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{\Omega^{2}}\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\bar{\nabla}^{\alpha}\Omega\bar{\nabla}_{\alpha}\Omega=

\displaystyle 8\pi\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}\,,

where
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative associated with
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
.

III.2
Z4 Formalism

We introduce here the Z4 formalism
[
11
,
10
]
, which adds a constraint-type variable
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}
to the EFEs. This formalism includes terms that damp the constraints (those proportional to the
\kappa_{i}
’s) and others that propagate the constraint violations (those proportional to the derivatives of
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}
). This changes (
8
) into:

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}[\tilde{g}]

\displaystyle+2\bar{\nabla}_{(\mu}\bar{Z}_{\nu)}-\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\bar{\nabla}^{\alpha}\bar{Z}_{\alpha}+\frac{4}{\Omega}\bar{Z}_{(\mu}\bar{\nabla}_{\nu)}\Omega

(9)

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa_{1}}{\Omega}(2\bar{n}_{(\mu}\bar{Z}_{\nu)}+\kappa_{2}\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}\bar{n}^{\alpha}\bar{Z}_{\alpha})=8\pi\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}\,,

where
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
are parameters to choose empirically
[
70
]
and we used
G_{\mu\nu}[\tilde{g}]
instead of
G_{\mu\nu}[\bar{g}]
for better readibility.
\tilde{n}^{\alpha}
is the normal vector used in the 3+1 decomposition. It is a future-pointing unit vector,
\tilde{n}^{\mu}\tilde{n}_{\mu}=-1
and under a conformal transformation, it changes as

\tilde{n}^{\mu}=\Omega\bar{n}^{\mu}\,,\quad\tilde{n}_{\mu}=\Omega^{-1}\bar{n}_{\mu}\,.

(10)

It is also important to define the 3+1 decomposition of the
\bar{Z}_{\mu}
variable as

\bar{\Theta}=-\bar{n}^{\mu}\bar{Z}_{\mu},\;Z^{i}=\bar{\gamma}^{i}_{\nu}\bar{Z}^{\nu}.

(11)

III.3
3+1 split into conformal Z4 / BSSN Formalism

For the 3+1 decomposition of the EFEs, we follow the generalized BSSN formalism
[
13
,
14
]
compatible with the (conformal) Z4 described in the previous section. Let
\bar{\gamma}_{ij}
be the purely spatial part of
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
, with associated covariant derivative
\bar{D}_{i}
. Then, the BSSN formalism introduces a new conformal factor,
\chi
, such that,

\chi=\left(\frac{\gamma}{\bar{\gamma}}\right)\,,

(12)

where
\gamma
is the conformally rescaled metric from
\bar{\gamma}
. Our final line element reads
[
79
]
,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\alpha^{2}dt^{2}+\chi^{-1}\gamma_{ij}(dx^{i}+\beta^{i}dt)(dx^{j}+\beta^{j}dt)\,,

(13)

where
\alpha
and
\beta^{r}
are the (rescaled) lapse and the shift. The rescaled lapse relates to the physical one as
\tilde{\alpha}=\Omega^{-1}\alpha
.
The conformal extrinsic curvature tensor
\bar{K}_{ij}
is then divided into its trace and trace-free parts as,

A_{ij}=\chi\bar{K}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}\gamma_{ij}\bar{K}\,,

(14)

with
\bar{K}=\bar{\gamma}^{ij}\bar{K}_{ij}
. We will however work with
K
, a mix of
\bar{K}
and the Z4 quantity
\Theta
given by

K=\bar{K}-2\Theta\,.

(15)

The relations between the conformal
\bar{K}
and
\Theta
and their physical counterparts
\tilde{K}
and
\tilde{\Theta}
are

\tilde{K}=\Omega\bar{K}-\frac{3\beta^{i}\partial_{i}\Omega}{\alpha}\,,\quad\tilde{\Theta}=\Omega\Theta\,.

(16)

The variables evolved in the simulation (that come from our choice of formalism) are
A_{ij}
,
\tilde{K}
,
\tilde{\Theta}
,
\gamma_{rr}
,
\chi
,
\alpha
,
\beta^{r}
and
\Lambda^{r}
. This last variable is defined as,

\Lambda^{i}=\Delta\Gamma^{i}+2\gamma^{ij}Z_{j}\,,

(17)

with
\Delta\Gamma^{i}=\Gamma^{i}-\hat{\Gamma}^{i}
and
\Gamma^{i}=\gamma^{jk}\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
.
\hat{\Gamma}_{jk}^{i}
is the connection coming from the time-independent spatial metric
\hat{\gamma}_{ij}
. For the details regarding the derivation of the conformally rescaled EFEs, see
[
70
]
. We leave the equations in their tensorial form in Appendix
A
, together with the projections of the stress-energy tensor including the Maxwell and KG contributions.

IV
Maxwell’s Equations

IV.1
Checking Conformal Invariance

Maxwell’s equations
[
76
]
are expressed in terms of the electromagnetic tensor
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{J}_{\nu}
as defined in (
6
). To check that these equations are conformally invariant
[
18
]
, we propose that the electromagnetic tensor transforms as

\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{s}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu},

(18)

where
s
is a constant to be determined. Looking at the covariant derivative of the electromagnetic tensor, as done in
[
76
]
, in
n
dimensions,

\bar{g}^{\beta\mu}\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{s+2}\tilde{g}^{\beta\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}+(n-4+s)\Omega^{s+1}\tilde{g}^{\beta\mu}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}\Omega

In the physically relevant case,
n=4
, the right-most term disappears if we impose that
s=0
,

\bar{g}^{\beta\mu}\bar{\nabla}_{\beta}\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}\tilde{g}^{\beta\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\beta}\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}.

(19)

This is enough to show the conformal invariance of Maxwell’s equations in vacuum. If a non-zero four-current is present
[
3
]
, it must change according to

\bar{J}_{\mu}=\Omega^{2}\tilde{J}_{\mu}

(20)

to preserve the invariance of the equations, as was already observed in
[
18
]
. This will be confirmed later with the conserved current coming from the KG field. Note also that (
2
) implies that the electromagnetic potential
\tilde{A}_{\mu}
does not change under conformal transformations.

As a side note, the value of
s-2
is related to the mass of the photon:
[
3
]
proposes considering the conformal factor
\Omega
as a background field related to electromagnetism. Our choice,
s=0
, indicates we are assuming that the photon is massless.

IV.2
Electric Field

To derive the electric field’s evolution equation, we consider the alternative way of writing the Faraday tensor
[
12
]
,

\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{n}_{\mu}\tilde{E}_{\nu}-\tilde{n}_{\nu}\tilde{E}_{\mu}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\tilde{B}^{\alpha}\tilde{n}^{\beta},

(21)

where
\tilde{E}_{\nu}
and
\tilde{B}_{\alpha}
are the unrescaled electric and magnetic fields respectively. The quantity
\tilde{\epsilon}^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
is the Levi-Civita tensor, defined with respect to the Levi-Civita symbol
\eta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
by:

\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{g}}}\eta_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}.

(22)

Using the conformal invariance of
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}
, we get that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}\qquad\textrm{can equivalently be written as}

(23)

\displaystyle\tilde{n}_{\mu}\tilde{E}_{\nu}-\tilde{n}_{\nu}\tilde{E}_{\mu}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\tilde{B}^{\alpha}\tilde{n}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{-1}\bar{n}_{\mu}\tilde{E}_{\nu}-\Omega^{-1}\bar{n}_{\nu}\tilde{E}_{\mu}+\Omega^{4}\bar{\epsilon}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\tilde{B}^{\alpha}\Omega\bar{n}^{\beta},

where we used (
22
) to obtain the transformation law
\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=\Omega^{4}\bar{\epsilon}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
and
\tilde{\epsilon}^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=\Omega^{-4}\bar{\epsilon}^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
. We also used the transformation of the normal vector (
10
). Both the electric and magnetic fields are frame-dependent spatial vectors. Defining the rescaled electric and magnetic fields as

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\mu}=\frac{\tilde{E}_{\mu}}{\Omega},\;\bar{E}^{\mu}=\frac{\tilde{E}^{\mu}}{\Omega^{3}},\;\bar{B}^{\mu}=\Omega^{5}\tilde{B}^{\mu},\;\bar{B}_{\mu}=\frac{\tilde{B}_{\mu}}{\Omega^{9}}\,,

(24)

allows for writing the rescaled electromagnetic tensor in the same functional form as the physical one (
21
). Since there is no magnetic field in spherical symmetry, from now on we will only consider the electric field, which we will use in its rescaled version,
\bar{E}^{i}
.

IV.3
Adapted Maxwell’s Equations

Following
[
12
]
, we shall use an adapted version of Maxwell’s equations that reads:

\tilde{\nabla}^{\mu}\left(\tilde{F}_{\nu\mu}+\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\Psi\right)=4\pi(\tilde{J}_{\nu}-\tilde{n}_{\nu}k\Psi)\;\;,

(25)

where
k
is a constant to control the evolution of
\Psi
and in the simulations shown in this paper it will be set to 1.
\Psi
can be interpreted as the numerical deviation from the Poisson constraint associated with Maxwell’s equations. Together with (
21
), this gives the evolution equations for the electric field and for
\Psi
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{E}^{i}

\displaystyle=\beta^{j}D_{j}\bar{E}^{i}-\bar{E}^{j}D_{j}\beta^{i}+\bar{E}^{i}K\alpha+

\displaystyle-4\pi\bar{j}^{i}\alpha+\frac{\gamma^{ik}\alpha\chi D_{k}\Psi}{\Omega^{2}}

(26a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi

\displaystyle=\beta^{j}D_{j}\Psi+\alpha\Omega^{2}D_{i}\bar{E}^{i}-4\pi\bar{q}_{\text{dens.}}\alpha+

\displaystyle+4\pi k\alpha\Omega^{2}\Psi-\frac{3\bar{E}^{i}\alpha\Omega^{2}D_{i}\chi}{2\chi}\,,

(26b)

where
D_{i}
is the covariant derivative defined from
\gamma_{ij}
,
K
is the conformally rescaled trace of the extrinsic curvature and
\alpha=\Omega\tilde{\alpha}
. While these expressions are written tensorially, the magnetic field
\bar{B}^{i}
has been set to 0.
\tilde{j}^{i}
and
\tilde{q}_{\text{dens.}}
come from the 3+1 decomposition of the four-current
\tilde{J}^{\mu}
:

\displaystyle\bar{j}_{i}

\displaystyle=\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mu}\bar{J}_{\mu}\;\;,\quad\bar{q}_{\text{dens.}}=-\bar{n}^{\mu}\bar{J}_{\mu}\;\;.

(27)

If we set
\Psi
to 0 in its evolution equation (
26b
), we recover the Poisson/Gauss constraint
[
1
]
,

D_{i}\bar{E}^{i}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\bar{E}^{i}D_{i}\chi}{\chi}=\bar{D}_{i}\bar{E}^{i}=4\pi\bar{q}_{\text{dens.}}\,.

(28)

Regarding the four-potential
\tilde{A}_{\mu}=\bar{A}_{\mu}
, we 3+1 decompose it as well,

\bar{\Phi}=-\bar{n}_{\mu}\bar{A}^{\mu}\;\;,\quad\bar{A}_{3}^{i}=\bar{\gamma}^{i}_{\mu}\bar{A}^{\mu}\;\;.

(29)

\bar{\Phi}
is a scalar potential related to the electric potential, as we will see further, and
\bar{A}_{3}
is the vector potential. The evolution equation for
{\bar{A}_{3}}^{i}
is obtained from (
2
) and (
23
),

\partial_{t}\bar{A}_{3}^{i}=\mathcal{L}_{\beta}\bar{A}_{3}^{i}+\alpha\bar{E}^{i}+\bar{D}^{i}(\alpha\bar{\Phi})\,.

(30)

V
Charged-Scalar Field

We define the rescaled scalar field
\bar{\phi}
as

\bar{\phi}=\frac{\tilde{\phi}}{\Omega}\,.

(31)

Expressed in terms of
\bar{\phi}
, the KG equation (
6
) is changed to

\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}\tilde{\phi}-\frac{2}{\Omega}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}\tilde{\phi})(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}\Omega)=0\,,

(32)

with
\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}=\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}+iq\bar{A}_{\mu}
. To simulate the complex scalar field, it is useful to separate it into its real and imaginary parts:

\bar{\phi}=\bar{c}_{\phi}+i\bar{d}_{\phi}\;,\quad\bar{c}_{\phi},\bar{d}_{\phi}\in\mathbb{R}\;\;,

(33)

and to reduce the KG equation to four first-order in time equations, we define,

\bar{c}_{\Pi}=\partial_{t}\bar{c}_{\phi},\;\bar{d}_{\Pi}=\partial_{t}\bar{d}_{\phi}.

(34)

Note that by imposing (
31
), the four-current indeed changes according to (
20
). Explicitly, the four-current projections in terms of the scalar field quantities are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{j}_{i}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{2}\left(-q^{2}\tilde{A}_{3i}(\bar{c}_{\phi}^{2}+\bar{d}_{\phi}^{2})+q\bar{c}_{\phi}D_{i}\bar{d}_{\phi}+q\bar{d}_{\phi}D_{i}\bar{c}_{\phi}\right)\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{\text{dens.}}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{2}\bigg(-q^{2}\bar{\Phi}(\bar{c}_{\phi}^{2}+\bar{d}_{\phi}^{2})+q\frac{\bar{c}_{\phi}\bar{d}_{\Pi}-\bar{d}_{\phi}\bar{c}_{\Pi}}{\alpha}+

\displaystyle+q\frac{\bar{d}_{\phi}\beta^{i}D_{i}\bar{c}_{\phi}-\bar{c}_{\phi}\beta^{i}D_{i}\bar{d}_{\phi}}{\alpha}\bigg).

VI
Gauge Conditions

VI.1
Lapse and Shift

For the lapse and shift gauge conditions, we follow those presented in
[
70
]
, which are adapted to hyperboloidal slices:

\displaystyle\dot{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\beta^{r}\alpha^{\prime}-\hat{\beta}^{r}\hat{\alpha}^{\prime}-\frac{(n_{cK}(r_{\mathrsfs{I}^{+}}^{2}-r^{2})^{4}+\alpha^{2})\Delta\tilde{K}}{\Omega}+

\displaystyle+\frac{\Omega^{\prime}}{\Omega}(\hat{\beta}^{r}\hat{\alpha}-\beta^{r}\alpha)+\frac{\xi_{cK}(\hat{\alpha}-\alpha)}{\Omega}\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle\dot{\beta}^{r}

\displaystyle=\beta^{r}{\beta^{r}}^{\prime}-\hat{\beta}^{r}\hat{\beta}^{r\prime}+\frac{3}{4}\left(\lambda(r_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}^{2}-r^{2})+\alpha^{2}\chi\right)\Lambda^{r}+

\displaystyle+\eta(\hat{\beta}^{r}-\beta^{r})+\xi_{\beta^{r}}\left(\frac{\hat{\beta}^{r}}{\Omega}-\frac{\beta^{r}}{\Omega}\right)\,.

(36b)

n_{cK},\lambda,\xi_{cK},\eta,\xi_{\beta^{r}}
are constants to be chosen, as the system to be evolved requires, and
\Delta\tilde{K}=\tilde{K}-K_{\text{CMC}}
, with
K_{\text{CMC}}
defined later in Section
VII
. The hatted variables are derived from a time-independent background metric, which we set to be that of Minkowski on a constant-mean-curvature hyperboloidal slice. Their explicit expressions will be given in Section
VII
.
r_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}
is the radial location of
\mathscr{I}^{+}
, set to 1 in our simulations. The gauge conditions considered allow the coordinate position of
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
to remain the same throughout the evolutions, which is known as “scri-fixing”. The following parameters were set in all simulations:
K_{\text{CMC}}=-1,\,\kappa_{1}=1.5,\,\kappa_{2}=0.1,\,\lambda=1.0,\,\eta=0.1,\,\xi_{\beta^{r}}=0.0
. For regular and strong field initial data,
n_{\text{cK}}=1.0,\,\xi_{\text{cK}}=1.0
, while for collapse simulations
n_{\text{cK}}=0.0,\,\xi_{\text{cK}}=2.0
.

VI.2
Adapted Lorenz Gauge

The last gauge condition to complete the system of evolution equations is the one for
\bar{\Phi}
. The most common choice is the Lorenz gauge
[
79
,
43
,
35
]
:

\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{A}^{\mu}=0\,.

(37)

It is also possible to use the “conformal” Lorenz gauge,
\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}\bar{A}^{\mu}=0
, or the light-cone gauge
[
18
,
47
,
49
]
, which reads,

l_{\mu}\tilde{A}^{\mu}=0\,,

(38)

where
l^{\mu}
is an outgoing null vector. We have tested all of these possibilities and found that the electromagnetic potentials,
\bar{A}_{3}
and
\bar{\Phi}
, steadily grow at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. Inspired by
[
32
]
, we propose an alternative Lorenz gauge, where the factors in the evolution equation that envolve
\tilde{A}_{3i}
are made proportional to a factor
\mu
. Starting from (
37
), we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{\Phi}}

\displaystyle=\beta^{i}\bar{D}_{i}\bar{\Phi}+\bar{K}\alpha\bar{\Phi}-\frac{2\bar{\Phi}\beta^{i}\bar{D}_{i}\Omega}{\Omega}+

(39)

\displaystyle-\mu(\alpha\bar{D}^{i}\bar{A}_{3i}+\bar{A}_{3}^{i}\bar{D}_{i}\alpha-\frac{2\bar{A}_{3}^{i}\alpha\bar{D}_{i}\Omega}{\Omega})\,.

For
\mu\leq 0.5
the variables eventually reach a stationary state at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. We found that making the change
\mu\rightarrow\mu(1-r^{2}_{\mathscr{I}^{+}})
worked even better for a wider range of
\mu
, and we stick with this version of the adapted Lorenz gauge.

This change by hand of the principal part of the Lorenz gauge preserves the strong hyperbolicity of the system of partial differential equations (PDEs) describing the EMKG system. One would have to take care when extending beyond spherical symmetry, for the evolution equation of the magnetic field will make the PDE system become weakly hyperbolic. See
[
1
]
for a brief discussion on that.

Initial Data

VII.1
Hyperboloidal Conformal Compactification

Foliating spacetime along hyperboloidal slices can be done through a height function
[
77
,
30
,
48
]
,
h(\tilde{r})
, as

t=\tilde{t}-h(\tilde{r})\,,

(40)

with
\tilde{t}
being the physical time and
t
the new time coordinate whose level sets give hyperboloidal slices. This height function satisfies
\partial_{\tilde{r}}h(\tilde{r})<1
everywhere
[
73
]
and this derivative asymptotes to 1 at
\mathscr{I}^{+}
. The radial derivative of the height function is also called the “boost” function.

In a spherically symmetric spacetime, we can start with the generic line element:

d\tilde{s}^{2}=-A(\tilde{r})d\tilde{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{A(\tilde{r})}d\tilde{r}^{2}+\tilde{r}^{2}d\tilde{\sigma}^{2}.

(41)

When we introduce the height function (
40
),
the line element becomes,

\displaystyle d\tilde{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=-A(\tilde{r})dt^{2}-2A(\tilde{r})h^{\prime}(\tilde{r})dtd\tilde{r}+

(42)

\displaystyle+\frac{[1-(A(\tilde{r})h^{\prime}(\tilde{r}))^{2}]}{A(\tilde{r})}d\tilde{r}^{2}+\tilde{r}^{2}d\sigma^{2}.

However, we are still lacking a functional form for
h(\tilde{r})
. From the definition of the extrinsic curvature
[
70
]
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\bar{K}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}}\partial_{a}(\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\tilde{n}^{a})

(43)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tilde{r}^{2}}\partial_{\tilde{r}}\left[\tilde{r}^{2}\frac{A^{3/2}(\tilde{r})h^{\prime}(\tilde{r})}{\sqrt{1-(A(\tilde{r})h^{\prime}(\tilde{r}))^{2}}}\right]\,,

where in the second line we have used the metric components from (
42
). We will determine
h^{\prime}(\tilde{r})
by imposing that the hyperboloidal slices it defines correspond to constant-mean-curvature (CMC) ones, which implies that the physical extrinsic curvature trace (
\tilde{K}
) is constant. Therefore, we can integrate (
43
), choosing
\tilde{K}=K_{\text{CMC}}<0
. In our convention following
[
50
]
, it is asymptotically negative for slices reaching
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
, and positive for slices reaching
\mathrsfs{I}^{-}
. Let
C_{\text{CMC}}
be the constant coming from the integration and solving (
43
) for
h(\tilde{r})
, we get

h^{\prime}(\tilde{r})=-\frac{\frac{K_{\text{CMC}}\tilde{r}}{3}+\frac{C_{\text{CMC}}}{\tilde{r}^{2}}}{A(\tilde{r})\sqrt{A(\tilde{r})+\left(\frac{K_{\text{CMC}}\tilde{r}}{3}+\frac{C_{\text{CMC}}}{\tilde{r}^{2}}\right)^{2}}}.

(44)

To put
\mathscr{I}^{+}
at a finite radial coordinate, we introduce a compactification factor,
\bar{\Omega}
, such that

\tilde{r}=\frac{r}{\bar{\Omega}(r)}\,,

(45)

and note that
\bar{\Omega}
and
\Omega
are in principle different quantities. Inserting (
45
) into (
42
), we get,

\displaystyle d\tilde{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=-Adt^{2}-2Ah^{\prime}\frac{\bar{\Omega}-r\bar{\Omega}^{\prime}}{\bar{\Omega}^{2}}dtdr+

(46)

\displaystyle+\frac{1-(Ah^{\prime})^{2}}{A}\frac{(\bar{\Omega}-r\bar{\Omega}^{\prime})^{2}}{\bar{\Omega}^{4}}dr^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{\bar{\Omega}^{2}}d\sigma^{2}

The final step is the conformal transformation coming from the metric,
ds^{2}=\Omega^{2}d\tilde{s}^{2}
.
Comparing (
13
), in spherical symmetry, with (
46
), we then have the following initial data,

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=\Omega\tilde{\alpha}_{0},\quad{\gamma_{rr}}_{0}=\frac{(\bar{\Omega}-r\bar{\Omega}^{\prime})^{2}}{\tilde{\alpha}_{0}^{2}\bar{\Omega}^{2}},\quad\chi_{0}=\frac{\bar{\Omega}^{2}}{\Omega^{2}}

\displaystyle\beta^{r}_{0}=\frac{\bar{\Omega}^{2}\left(\frac{K_{\text{CMC}}r}{3\bar{\Omega}}+\frac{C_{\text{CMC}}\bar{\Omega}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\tilde{\alpha}_{0}}{(\bar{\Omega}-r\bar{\Omega}^{\prime})}

(47)

\displaystyle\text{with }\tilde{\alpha}_{0}=\sqrt{A\left(\frac{r}{\bar{\Omega}}\right)+\left(\frac{K_{\text{CMC}}r}{3\bar{\Omega}}+\frac{C_{\text{CMC}}\bar{\Omega}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)^{2}}.

Together with this, we also take
\gamma_{\theta\theta}=\sqrt{\gamma_{rr}}
, to fix the freedom introduced with the evolution variable
\chi
. A convenient choice
[
70
]
for
\bar{\Omega}
is taking the initial spatial metric to be conformally flat
\gamma_{rr}=1
, which is the same as imposing initial isotropic coordinates. Initial data for the remaining evolution variables from the EFEs are:
\Lambda^{r}_{0}=\tilde{\Theta}_{0}=Z_{r0}=0
and
A_{rr}=-\frac{2C_{\text{CMC}\bar{\Omega}^{3}}}{r^{3}\Omega}
.

VII.2
Regular Initial data

In flat spacetime,
A(\tilde{r})=1
. We additionally take
C_{\text{CMC}}=0
. This allows (
44
) to have an analytic solution,

h(\tilde{r})=\sqrt{\tilde{r}^{2}+\left(\frac{3}{K_{\text{CMC}}}\right)^{2}}\,.

(48)

The conformally flat condition then gives an analytical formula for
\bar{\Omega}
,

\bar{\Omega}=-K_{\text{CMC}}\frac{r^{2}_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}-r^{2}}{6r_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}}\,.

(49)

For simplicity, we further choose
\Omega=\bar{\Omega}
, for the expression in (
49
) is also suited for the conformal factor. We will remain with this expression for
\Omega
throughout the rest of the work. The background metric
\hat{\gamma}_{ij}
will take the same values as the metric we describe here (corresponding to Minkowski spacetime). (
47
) simplifies to,

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=\sqrt{\Omega^{2}+\left(\frac{K_{\text{CMC}}r}{3}\right)^{2}},\quad\chi_{0}=1,\quad\gamma_{rr0}=1,

\displaystyle\beta^{r}_{0}=\frac{K_{\text{CMC}}r}{3},\quad A_{rr0}=\Theta_{0}=Z_{r0}=\Lambda_{r0}=0\,.

(50)

\hat{\alpha}
and
\hat{\beta}
will take the values of
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta^{r}_{0}
as described above.

VII.3
Strong Field Initial Data

An important aspect in strong field initial data is the careful choice of
C_{\text{CMC}}
. CMC slices in general share the property of being horizon-penetrating slices, but the critical value of
C_{\text{CMC}}
will give us trumpet initial slices
[
5
,
26
]
, which are useful to avoid the singularity. The way to do this is by setting the discriminant of the denominator of
\tilde{\gamma}_{rr}
to 0, already with the expression for
h^{\prime}(\tilde{r})
substituted

\tilde{\gamma}_{\tilde{r}\tilde{r}}=\frac{1}{A(\tilde{r})+\left(\frac{K_{\text{CMC}}\tilde{r}}{3}+\frac{C_{\text{CMC}}}{\tilde{r}^{2}}\right)^{2}}\,,

(51)

where we shall consider
A(\tilde{r})=1-\frac{2M}{\tilde{r}}+\frac{Q^{2}}{\tilde{r}^{2}}
, for a RN BH. A vanishing discriminant is equivalent to having a double root
\tilde{r}=R_{0}
in the denominator.
R_{0}
corresponds to the innermost value of the Schwarzschild-like radial coordinate that the slices reach and thus can be interpreted as the throat of the trumpet. We redirect the interested reader to
[
72
]
to see how slices change when choosing a
C_{\text{CMC}}
different from the critical value.

The conformally flat metric condition does not produce an analytical result now, therefore having to be numerically integrated by solving (
47
) with
{\gamma_{rr}}_{0}=1
. Fig.
1
shows how
\bar{\Omega}
changes with the charge to mass ratio, with the mass set to 1 and
K_{\text{CMC}}=-1
. Note how
\bar{\Omega}
and
\Omega
go to zero at the same rate as they approach
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
.

VII.4
Charged Scalar Field Initial Data

To make sure the constraints are initially satisfied, we have to make sure that the Hamiltonian,
\mathcal{H}
, momentum,
\mathcal{M}_{r}
, and Poisson,
\mathcal{P}
, constraints are solved (respectively, (
68
), (
69
) and (
28
)). Assuming we have a perturbation in the real part of the scalar field and another one in
\bar{A}_{3r}
, the condition that solves the momentum constraint reads

\bar{c}_{\Pi}=\beta^{r}\bar{c}_{\phi}^{\prime}+\beta^{r}\bar{c}_{\phi}\frac{\Omega^{\prime}}{\Omega}\,.

(52)

Inserting this condition into the Hamiltonian constraint, for regular initial data, yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=

\displaystyle-\tfrac{8}{9}\pi r^{2}\bigl(\bar{c}_{\phi}\bigr)^{2}\chi+\frac{24\bigl(1-r^{2}\chi\bigr)}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}+\tfrac{8}{9}\pi r(1-r^{2})\bar{c}_{\phi}\chi\bar{c}_{\phi}^{\prime}

(53)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{2}{9}\pi(1-r^{2})^{2}\chi\Bigl(q^{2}\bigl(\bar{A}_{3r}\bigr)^{2}\bigl(\bar{c}_{\phi}\bigr)^{2}+\bigl(\bar{c}_{\phi}^{\prime}\bigr)^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\frac{4\chi^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{5\bigl(\chi^{\prime}\bigr)^{2}}{2\chi}+\frac{6\bigl(-4\chi+\tfrac{2}{3}r\chi^{\prime}\bigr)}{1-r^{2}}+2\chi^{\prime\prime}\,

which we solve for
\chi
. It is useful to do the following change,

\displaystyle\chi\rightarrow\chi_{0}\psi^{-4}=\frac{\bar{\Omega}^{2}}{\Omega^{2}}\psi^{-4}\,,

(54)

and then solve the Hamiltonian constraint for
\psi
. Note that, for regular initial data, we are choosing
\bar{\Omega}=\Omega
, simplifying the above expression. On the other hand, for strong field initial data,
\chi_{0}
depends on the numerical integration of
\bar{\Omega}
, as shown in Fig.
1
. We also have to take into account that
{A_{rr}}_{0}
is non-vanishing, as described in subsection
VII.3
, and it is therefore also useful to do the following change

A_{rr}\rightarrow{A_{rr0}}\psi^{-6}=-\frac{2C_{\text{CMC}}\bar{\Omega}^{3}}{r^{3}\Omega}\psi^{-6}\,,

(55)

Substituting (
54
) and (
55
) into (
68
), we solve the Hamiltonian constraint for
\psi
, for strong field initial data.

The Poisson constraint is automatically satisfied with
\bar{E}^{r}_{0}=0
, because the charge density is initially 0. Only if we were to consider a perturbation both in the real and imaginary parts initially would we have to care with the Poisson constraint. An initial perturbation on the imaginary part would require an analogous condition to (
52
) for
\bar{d}_{\phi}
, with some extra terms appearing in (
53
).

VII.5
RN BH Initial Data

For a RN BH,
\bar{E}^{r}\neq 0
. The other solution of (
28
) besides the trivial one is,

\bar{E}^{r}_{0}=\frac{Q}{r^{2}}\chi^{3/2}_{0}\,,

(56)

where
Q
is an integration constant that we physically interpret as the charge of the BH. When assuming a non-vanishing initial electric field in the Hamiltonian constraint, we get that the only solution that solves it is the same as (
56
), confirming the consistency of the Einstein+Maxwell system of equations. Looking back at (
45
), we see that when
r\rightarrow 0
,
\tilde{r}\rightarrow R_{0}
, implying that
\bar{\Omega}\sim r/R_{0}
there. From (
47
), we know that
\chi_{0}=\bar{\Omega}^{2}/\Omega^{2}
. Therefore, we conclude that
\chi_{0}\sim r^{2}
as
r\rightarrow 0
. This means that near the origin
\bar{E}^{r}_{0}\rightarrow 0
, avoiding the singularity that is present in the electric field as well

1

The covariant electric field diverges unavoidably at
r=0
. Hence, we stick with the contravariant formulation.
. Furthermore, we choose
\bar{A}_{3r}=0
initially, given the absence of magnetic field.
\bar{\Phi}_{0}
will be given by setting the right-hand-side (RHS) of (
30
) to 0. This is solved with the following equality,

\bar{E}_{r0}=-\frac{\partial_{r}(\bar{\Phi}_{0}\alpha_{0})}{\alpha_{0}}\,,

where note we are using the covariant version of the electric field here. Defining the quantity
V(r)=\bar{\Phi}_{0}\alpha_{0}
, the above equation can be rewritten as,

V^{\prime}=-\frac{Q}{r^{2}}\chi^{1/2}_{0}\alpha_{0}=-\frac{Q}{r^{2}}\frac{\bar{\Omega}}{\Omega}(\bar{\Omega}-r\bar{\Omega}^{\prime})\frac{\Omega}{\bar{\Omega}}=\partial_{r}\left(\bar{\Omega}\frac{Q}{r}\right)\,.

We conclude then,

V(r)=\bar{\Omega}\frac{Q}{r}+C\,,

with
C
being an integration constant. To write
\alpha_{0}
as a function of
\bar{\Omega}
and
\Omega
, we used the conformally flat initial data expressions (

. If we do not choose correctly the constant

will blow up at the origin, because

there as well. Therefore, the only possible choice is to choose

becomes,

\displaystyle=\bar{\Omega}^{\prime}(0)+r\bar{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{r^{2}}{2}\bar{\Omega}^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)+O(r^{3})\,.

Substituting these expressions in (

\bar{\Omega}(0)=\bar{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0

(58)

To simulate the Schwarzschild BH, it is just a matter of setting

VII.6
Charged Scalar Field + RN BH Initial Data

By perturbing a RN BH spacetime with the charged scalar field, we have a non-trivial solution of the Poisson constraint, because
\bar{\Phi}\neq 0
and thus
\bar{q}_{\text{dens.}}\neq 0
. To tackle this problem, one possibility is to extend the conformal transverse traceless method
[
79
]
to the electric field,
\bar{E}^{r}\rightarrow\bar{E}^{r}\psi^{-6}
. However, to simplify further the equations, we will make the following substitution instead,

\bar{E}^{r}=\chi^{3/2}\frac{Q}{r^{2}}+\delta\bar{E}^{r}\psi^{-6},\quad\bar{\Phi}\rightarrow\bar{\Phi}\psi^{-6}\,.

(59)

This way, the Poisson constraint gives us an elliptic equation independent of
\psi
,

(\delta\bar{E}^{r})^{\prime}=-\frac{2\delta\bar{E}^{r}}{r}+\frac{3\delta\bar{E}^{r}\bar{\Omega}^{\prime}}{\bar{\Omega}}-\frac{3\delta\bar{E}^{r}\Omega^{\prime}}{\Omega}+4\pi q^{2}\bar{c}_{\phi}^{2}\bar{\Phi}\,,

where the last term in the RHS comes from writing the charge density explicitly as a function of the terms that do not vanish initially, considering an initial perturbation only on the real part of the scalar field. A differential equation for
\psi
is again given by solving the Hamiltonian constraint. This latter one is more lengthy, due to the different initial data we have to input for each variable (
47
).

VIII
Numerical Setup

To evolve the system, we developed a code adapted to spherically symmetric equations, based on the method of lines with 4th-order finite-differencing in the spatial derivatives. A 4th-order Runge-Kutta method is used to integrate in time. While this numerical setup is simple in comparison to more sophisticated ones like
[
75
,
38
,
37
]
, it is perfectly appropriate for the formally-singular EMKG system under consideration. We use a staggered grid to avoid evaluating the equations exactly at the origin and
r=r_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}
, because the RHS of our equations are formally singular at these points. Regarding the boundary conditions at
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
, we follow
[
17
,
15
]
and impose an outflow condition on the ghost points of the grid beyond
r_{\mathscr{I}^{+}}
. This condition allows information to be radiated away at
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
, motivated by the fact that there is no physical ingoing radiation at future null infinity given its character as ingoing null hypersurface. The boundary condition at the origin, on the other hand, is dealt with differently according to the simulation. For regular initial data, we use parity conditions according to the nature of each variable, while for strong field initial data and for the collapse, simulations we use an inflow boundary condition equivalent to that used at
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
. We use Kreiss-Oliger dissipation to attenuate high-frequency noise
[
4
,
41
]
. For all simulations except the collapse, a Kreiss-Oliger amplitude of
0.08
was more than enough to keep the simulations from blowing up early. Too much or too low dissipation in the strong field initial data simulations would make the simulation crash as soon as the scalar field entered the BH. For collapse simulations, the tuning had to be done more carefully. A
0.15
amplitude worked for several grid resolutions for the purpose of convergence tests.

Conclusions

The takeaway message is that the Einstein-Maxwell-Klein-Gordon system has been implemented with success on hyperboloidal slices, in spherical symmetry. This was done using the BSSN/conformal Z4 formalism for the EFEs, together with the prescription in
[
12
]
for Maxwell’s equations. An adapted Lorenz gauge was found to regularize the electromagnetic potentials at
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}
, and we made sure that all evolution quantities were finite and well-behaved everywhere in the domain.
To the best of our knowledge, these are the first hyperboloidal evolutions including electric and changed scalar fields with the BSSN/Z4 formulations.

Here we showcase the capabilities of our implementation, by presenting evolutions of a charged complex scalar field perturbing a regular spacetime and one including a charged black hole. We have also shown a successful collapse of the scalar field into a RN BH in hyperboloidal slices. All of these results were accompanied by convergence tests to show reliability of the results. In a companion paper
[
2
]
we will exploit our access to future null infinity to study the behaviour of the scalar field there in the fully nonlinear regime.
The hyperboloidal approach can also be applied to other systems of equations, such as the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov, as treated mathematically in
[
19
]
, or the Klein-Gordon-Dirac systems. The main suitability condition is that the evolved fields fall off fast enough towards null infinity.
Our implementation of the Einstein-Maxwell-Klein-Gordon system opens the door to other physically interesting scenarios while allowing access to null infinity. This is relevant for the weak cosmic censorship conjecture
[
57
]
if exploring collapsing (charged or uncharged) spacetimes close to the threshold of criticallity. Extensions to boson stars would only require the addition of a massive term in the KG equation. This however needs to be done carefully, because the massive term does not fall off with the appropriate rate at
\mathrsfs{I}^{+}

[
29
]
. Also very relevant is to expand beyond spherical symmetry (currently ongoing work for the hyperboloidal approach), and include magnetic fields, which, in nature, seem to have more influence in the BH’s dynamics. The Gertsenshtein effect
[
31
]
, the conversion between electromagnetic and gravitational waves and viceversa, is also worth looking into.

Maxwell Part

There are two ways to write Faraday’s tensor,
F_{\mu\nu}
, one, which is the more standard way, is saying,
F_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu}
. The other way is to write it as a function of the electric field (
21
). When writing out the stress-energy tensor part related to electromagnetism, this latter definition simplifies to a greater extent the expressions. We will work with the definition in terms of the electric field. This has been thoroughly discussed in
[
1
]
and the authors of the paper even advise against using the electric and magnetic potentials. We, however, have to evolve these potentials because they enter directly into the scalar field’s evolution equations. The stress-energy tensor, when using the definition in terms of the electric field becomes,

(T_{\mu\nu})_{\text{Max}}=\Omega^{2}\left(-\frac{E_{\mu}E_{\nu}}{4\pi}+\frac{E_{\alpha}E^{\alpha}\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}}{8\pi}+\frac{E_{\alpha}E^{\alpha}n_{\mu}n_{\nu}}{4\pi}\right)\,.

(83)

The projections then become,

\displaystyle\rho=

\displaystyle\,\frac{E_{i}E^{i}\Omega^{2}}{8\pi}

(84)

\displaystyle J^{i}=

\displaystyle\,0

(85)

\displaystyle S_{ij}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{E_{i}E_{j}\Omega^{2}}{4\pi}+\frac{E_{k}E^{k}\gamma_{ij}\Omega^{2}}{8\pi\chi}

(86)

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\,\frac{E_{i}E^{i}\Omega^{2}}{8\pi}\,.

(87)

Note that
S=\rho
, which is according to the fact that the electromagnetic part of the stress-energy tensor is trace-free.

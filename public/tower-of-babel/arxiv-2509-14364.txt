Abstract
. We identify pairs of (twisted) multiplicative Hitchin fibrations which are “dual” in the sense that their bases are identified and their generic fibres are dual Beilinson 1 1 -motives. More precisely, we match the following: (1) an untwisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration associated with a simply-laced semisimple group G G with an untwisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration associated with the Langlands dual group G ∨ G^{\vee} ; (2) a twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration associated with a simply-laced and simply-connected semisimple group G G , without factors of type 𝖠 2 ​ ℓ \mathsf{A}_{2\ell} , and a diagram automorphism θ ∈ Aut ⁡ ( G ) \theta\in\Aut(G) with an untwisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration associated with the Langlands dual group H ∨ H^{\vee} of the invariant group H = G θ H=G^{\theta} ; (3) two twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibrations associated with G = SL 2 ​ ℓ + 1 G=\SL_{2\ell+1} and two special automophisms of order 2 2 and 4 4 , respectively. These results are consistent with a conjecture of Elliott and Pestun [ 12 ] .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Hitchin fibrations and multiplicative Hitchin fibrations

Let
C
be a smooth complex projective curve and let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
.
The
Hitchin fibration
is an algebraically completely integrable system introduced by Hitchin in his seminal paper
[
22
]
. The total space of this fibration is the moduli space of
Higgs bundles
, pairs
(E,\varphi)
formed by a principal
G
-bundle
E\rightarrow C
and a section
\varphi\in H^{0}(C,\mathrm{ad}(E)\otimes K_{C})
, where
K_{C}
is the canonical line bundle of
C
. If
K_{C}
is replaced by a different line bundle
L
, one can analogously define “
L
-twisted” Higgs bundles and formulate a generalization of the Hitchin fibration. When
L\neq K_{C}
, the complex symplectic structure is lost, but some of the symmetry is retained. In particular, if the degree of
L
is large enough, the generic fibres are (discrete unions of) abelian varieties. The Hitchin fibration can be extended to the whole moduli stack of (
L
-twisted) Higgs bundles which, as explained by Ngô
[
33
]
, can be identified as the stack of maps from
C
to the quotient stack
[\mathfrak{g}/G\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
lying over the natural map
C\rightarrow B\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
induced by
L
. Here,
\mathfrak{g}
denotes the Lie algebra of
G
, which is acted on by
G
through the adjoint action, and by
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
by homothecy. The Hitchin fibration can in turn be understood as a “global analogue” of the Chevalley restriction map

\chi:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G\cong\mathfrak{t}/W\cong\mathbb{A}^{r}.

Here,
r
is the rank of
G
,
\mathfrak{t}
denotes the Lie algebra of a maximal torus
T\subset G
and
W
is the Weyl group. At the stacky level, generic fibres are commutative group stacks whose inertia groups are groups of multiplicative type and whose coarse moduli spaces are discrete unions of abelian varieties. Stacks of this form are known as
Beilinson
1
-motives

[
8
, Section 5.2]
.

The reductive group
G
acts on itself by conjugation. More generally, it acts by the adjoint action on any other reductive group
G^{\prime}
with
\mathrm{Ad}(G)=\mathrm{Ad}(G^{\prime})
. In particular, if
G
is semisimple, it acts on its simply-connected cover
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
.

Multiplicative Hitchin fibrations
arise as a way to formulate a global version of the restriction map

\chi:G^{\prime}\longrightarrow G^{\prime}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G\cong T^{\prime}/W.

Here,
T^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
is a maximal torus. Roughly, one wants to consider the mapping stack from
C
to the quotient stack
[G^{\prime}/G]
. Thus, one is led to study
multiplicative Higgs bundles
: pairs
(E,\varphi)
formed by a principal
G
-bundle
E\rightarrow C
and a section of the associated group bundle
\varphi\in H^{0}(C,E\times^{G}G^{\prime})
. However, note that, in constrast with the Lie algebra, if
G^{\prime}
is also semisimple, then it is not naturally endowed with a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action. Therefore, there is no natural way a priori to introduce twisting or singularities on the “multiplicative Higgs fields”. There are two natural ways to remedy this:

•

The “meromorphic approach”. This was the original approach proposed by Hurtubise and Markman
[
23
]
and also used, for example, in the works of Charbonneau–Hurtubise
[
4
]
and Elliott–Pestun
[
12
]
. With this approach, one considers pairs
(E,\varphi)
as above, but such that
\varphi
is defined only away from a finite subset
\left\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\right\}\subset C
, and has a rational singularity at each point
p_{i}
. The “pole order” of the rational singularity at a point
p_{i}
is determined by a dominant cocharacter
\lambda_{i}
of
T^{\prime}
.

•

The “monoid approach”. Another way of introducing singularities is given by first extending the semisimple group
G^{\prime}
to a larger reductive group
G^{\prime}_{+}
with a central torus
Z
, and then partially compactifying the group
G^{\prime}_{+}
over the curve
C
. We obtain a
reductive monoid

M
with unit group
G^{\prime}_{+}
. The adjoint action of
G
on
G^{\prime}
extends to an action on
M
. One is then led to consider the stack of maps from
C
to the quotient stack
[M/G\times Z]
lying over a map
C\rightarrow BZ
determined by a prescribed
Z
-torsor
L\rightarrow C
. Prescribing the
Z
-torsor
L
and a section of the associated bundle
L\times^{Z}\mathbb{A}_{M}
where
\mathbb{A}_{M}=M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}(G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime})
is the
abelianization
of
M
, amounts to prescribing the points
p_{i}
and “pole orders” on the “meromorphic approach”. This “monoid approach” was originally proposed in the work of Frenkel and Ngô
[
13
]
and developed in the subsequent works of Bouthier, J. Chi and G. Wang
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
39
]
.

Multiplicative Hitchin fibrations were originally introduced in the mathematics literature by Hurtubise and Markman
[
23
]
. In that paper, they show that, when
C
is assumed to be an elliptic curve, then multiplicative Hitchin fibrations are in fact algebraically completely integrable systems. The same arguments show that, for a general choice of
C
and with a good “ampleness” assumption on the “pole data”, generic fibres of a multiplicative Hitchin fibration are Beilinson
1
-motives. This fact also follows, in the generality we use it here, from the more recent study of the symmetries of a multiplicative Hitchin fibration developed by G. Wang in
[
39
]
.

1.2.
Langlands duality

Let us assume now that
G
is semisimple and fix the maximal torus
T\subset G
. The real vector space
\mathbb{E}=X^{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is endowed with the Killing form
(-,-)
, and the pair
(G,T)
determines a root system
\Phi=\Phi(G,T)
on
(\mathbb{E},(-,-))
. The dual vector space of the complexification of
\mathbb{E}
is naturally identified with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
of
T
. The set of closed points of
T
is the complex torus
T(\mathbb{C})=\mathfrak{t}/X_{*}(T)
.
The dual algebraic torus
T^{\vee}
of
T
is the Cartier dual of the cocharacter lattice
X_{*}(T)
; its set of closed points is the complex torus
T^{\vee}(\mathbb{C})=\mathfrak{t}^{*}/X^{*}(T)
.

Let
G^{\vee}
denote the Langlands dual group of the reductive group
G
. By definition, the dual torus
T^{\vee}
is a maximal torus of
G^{\vee}
, and the root system
\Phi(G^{\vee},T^{\vee})\subset\mathbb{E}^{*}
is the dual root system

\Phi^{\vee}=\left\{\alpha^{\vee}=2(\alpha,-)/(\alpha,\alpha):\alpha\in\Phi\right\}

of
\Phi=\Phi(G,T)
. The Killing form is
W
-equivariant, so it induces an isomorphism

\mathfrak{t}/W\longrightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W.

In turn, this allows us to identify the base of the Hitchin fibration associated with
G
with the base of the Hitchin fibration associated with the Langlands dual group
G^{\vee}
. Given any point of the base of the Hitchin fibration associated with
G
such that its fibre is a Beilinson
1
-motive
\mathcal{P}
, the corresponding fibre of the Hitchin fibration associated with
G^{\vee}
is the dual Beilinson
1
-motive
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})
. More precisely, the inertia group of
\mathcal{P}
is Cartier dual to the component group of
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})
and vice-versa, and the underlying abelian varieties
P
and
\hat{P}
are dual abelian varieties. This phenomenon was first detected by Hausel and Thaddeus
[
19
]
in the case of type
\mathsf{A}_{n}
and proven in general by Donagi and Pantev
[
8
]
. It is also worth mentioning that there is an analogous result in positive characteristic proven by T.H. Chen and X. Zhu
[
5
]
.

Multiplicative Hitchin fibrations are different. Indeed, in general one cannot match the maximal tori of
G
and
G^{\vee}
in a
W
-equivariant way. However, if
G
is simply-laced (that is, if all the roots in
\Phi
have the same length), then we can normalize the Killing form in such a way that
(\alpha,\alpha)=2
for every root, and then the isomorphism
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t},x\mapsto(x,-)
induced by the Killing form exchanges roots with coroots and weights with coweights. That is, when
G
is simply-laced, we have
\alpha^{\vee}=(\alpha,-)
. In particular, this gives an identification of the simply-connected covers and the adjoint groups of
G
and of
G^{\vee}
; that is
G^{\mathrm{sc}}=(G^{\vee})^{\mathrm{sc}}
and
\mathrm{Ad}(G)=\mathrm{Ad}(G^{\vee})
. Therefore, if
G
is simply-laced, and we prescribe some “pole data” (either through the “meromorphic approach” or by fixing a monoid
M
whose unit group is a reductive group with semisimple part
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
and with center
Z
, and a
Z
-torsor), then we can consider multiplicative Hitchin fibrations
h_{G}:\mathcal{M}_{G,G^{\mathrm{sc}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}
and
h_{G^{\vee}}:\mathcal{M}_{G^{\vee},G^{\mathrm{sc}}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}
associated with the adjoint action on
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
of
G
and
G^{\vee}
, respectively. Our first result is the following (this is Theorem
3.7
).

Theorem 1.1
.

If
G
is simply-laced, then, assuming some “ampleness” conditions on the pole data, for a generic
a\in\mathcal{A}_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}(\mathbb{C})
, the fibres
h_{G}^{-1}(a)
and
h_{G^{\vee}}^{-1}(a)
are dual Beilinson
1
-motives.

1.3.
Twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibrations

When the structure group is not simply-laced, we detect a new kind of duality, which is not observed at the Lie algebra level. Multiplicative Hitchin fibrations associated with non simply-laced groups are dual to
twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibrations
associated with simply-laced groups.

Let
G
be a semisimple simply-laced and simply-connected group, and let
\theta\in\Aut(G)
be a
diagram automorphism
of
G
. By definition,
\theta
is the representative of an outer class
[\theta]\in\Out(G)=\Aut(G)/\mathrm{Ad}(G)
preserving a prescribed pinning of
G
. We can now consider the
\theta
-twisted conjugation action of
G
on itself,

g*h=gh\theta(g)^{-1}.

Equivalently, we can consider the exterior component
G\theta
of the semidirect product
G\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle
and consider the conjugation action of
G
on
G\theta
.
Twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibrations arise as a global analogue of the restriction map

\chi_{\theta}:G\longrightarrow G\theta\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G.

That is, we consider maps
C\rightarrow[G\theta/G]
(“singular”, in some sense, and with an appropriate notion of “pole data”). Thus we are led to consider pairs
(E,\varphi)
formed by a principal
G
-bundle
E\rightarrow C
and a section of the associated group bundle
\varphi\in H^{0}(C,E\times^{G,\theta}G)
.

The data
(G,\theta)
determines two different reductive groups: the
invariant group

G^{\theta}
and the
coinvariant group

G_{\theta}
. The invariant group is just the subgroup of fixed points of
G
under the automorphism
\theta
. Since
G
is simply-connected,
G^{\theta}
is connected. If
T
is a
\theta
-stable maximal torus of
G
, then
T^{\theta}
is a maximal torus of
G^{\theta}
. The root system
\Phi(G^{\theta},T^{\theta})
is the
folded root system
associated with the diagram automorphism
\theta
. The coinvariant group is more conveniently defined in terms of root data. The coinvariant torus
T_{\theta}=T/(1-\theta)(T)
is a maximal torus of
G_{\theta}
, while its root system is the dual root system of the folded root system. The character lattice of
T_{\theta}
is the fixed point subgroup of the character lattice of
T
, and thus it is the weight lattice of
G_{\theta}
. This implies that
G_{\theta}
is also simply-connected. The invariant group
G^{\theta}
is simply-connected except if
G
has a component isomorphic to
\SL_{2\ell+1}
for some
\ell
.

The possible pairs
(G,\theta)
, with
G
simple and
\theta\neq\id_{G}
, are classified by the
twisted affine Dynkin diagrams
, see Table

. The
folded Dynkin diagrams
associated with the folded root systems can be read from Table

by removing the node marked with a red cross

\times
in the the corresponding twisted affine Dynkin diagram.

The adjoint action of the coinvariant group on itself is related to the twisted adjoint action of the larger group on itself. Indeed, Mohrdieck
[
31
]
showed that there is a natural isomorphism of the GIT quotients

G\theta\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G\cong G_{\theta}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G_{\theta}.

Let
(G,\theta)
be as above, and let us assume that
G
does not have a component isomorphic to
\SL_{2\ell+1}
, for some
\ell
. We denote by
H=G^{\theta}
the corresponding invariant group, and let
H^{\vee}
denote its Langlands dual group. Note that, under our assumptions,
(H^{\vee})^{\mathrm{sc}}=G_{\theta}
is the coinvariant group, so
H^{\vee}
acts on
G_{\theta}
through the adjoint action. We denote by
h_{G,\theta}:\mathcal{M}_{G,\theta}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{G_{\theta}}
the twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration associated with the pair
(G,\theta)
, and by
h_{H^{\vee}}:\mathcal{M}_{H^{\vee},G_{\theta}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{G_{\theta}}
the (untwisted) multiplicative Hitchin fibration associated with the adjoint action of
H^{\vee}
on
G_{\theta}
. Note that, by Mohrdieck’s isomorphism
G\theta\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G\cong G_{\theta}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G_{\theta}
, the bases of these two fibrations coincide. Duality for non simply-laced groups is summarized in the following result (this is Theorem
5.4
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Asumming “ampleness” conditions on the pole data, for a generic
a\in\mathcal{A}_{G_{\theta}}(\mathbb{C})
, the fibres
h_{G,\theta}^{-1}(a)
and
h_{H^{\vee}}^{-1}(a)
are dual Beilinson
1
-motives.

G
has a component isomorphic to
\SL_{2\ell+1}
requires an special treatment. Indeed, in this case, apart from the diagram automorphism
\theta
of
\SL_{2\ell+1}
, we also have to consider the “standard automorphism” of order
4
introduced by Kac in his book
[
24
]
. Here, we are using the terminology of
[
1
]
, where they use the name “standard automorphism” to refer to the automorphism
\vartheta
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
such that the fixed point subalgebre
\mathfrak{g}^{\vartheta}
coincides with the standard finite part of the associated twisted affine Lie algebra determined by the corresponding twisted affine Dynkin diagram. In Table

\vartheta=\theta
in all simple cases except in the case
G=\SL_{2\ell+1}
. In this case, we have
G^{\vartheta}=\mathrm{Sp}_{2\ell}
. We can also consider the twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration
h_{G,\vartheta}:\mathcal{M}_{G,\vartheta}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{G_{\theta}}
associated with the pair
(G,\vartheta)
. Duality in the case
\mathsf{A}_{2\ell}^{(2)}
is summarized in the following result (this is Theorem
5.5
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=\SL_{2\ell+1}
,

a\in\mathcal{A}_{G_{\theta}}(\mathbb{C})
, the fibres

Note that in this case of
G=\SL_{2\ell+1}
, the moduli stacks
\mathcal{M}_{G,\theta}
and
\mathcal{M}_{G,\vartheta}
are indeed isomorphic, so the above result can be reinterpreted as the existence of an involution on the moduli space relating dual fibres. In section
6
we provide an explicit description of this involution in the case of
\SL_{3}
.

1.4.
Duality of affine Dynkin diagrams

Our duality results can be succintly expressed in terms of the duality of affine Dynkin diagrams. Indeed, we can understand untwisted multiplicative Hitchin fibrations as twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibrations associated with pairs of the form
(G,\id)
and, with each pair
(G,\theta)
, we can associate an affine Dynkin diagram. If we do this, then the multiplicative Hitchin fibrations which are matched under duality are precisely those with dual affine Dynkin diagrams associated with them.
When matching two untwisted multiplicative Hitchin fibrations, we act on both sides on the simply-connected group, but interchange the acting group by its Langlands dual (that is, we swap the centre and the fundamental group). When matching a twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration with an untwisted one, the acting group on the twisted side is simply-connected, and on the untwisted side it is of adjoint type.

1.5.
Strategy of the proofs

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
follow the same strategy, which is in fact very similar to the one for proving the analogous result for usual Hitchin fibrations.

The first step is to understand the symmetries of any of the “Hitchin-type” fibrations that we study. In general, all these fibrations are associated with the action of some reductive group
G
on some variety
M
and the natural map
[M/G]\rightarrow M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
. For any element
a
of the “Hitchin base”, the fibre of
a
is acted on by the stack of
J_{a}
-torsors, where
J_{a}\rightarrow C
is a group scheme naturally constructed from the group scheme
J\rightarrow M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
of regular stabilizers of the action of
G
on
M
, which in turn is a descent of the group scheme
I\rightarrow M
of stabilizers of the
G
-action. What makes the fibrations that we study specially nice is the existence of “abelianization”. By this we mean that there is a “cameral cover” of
M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
such that the group scheme
J
can be described as an open and closed subscheme of the Weil restriction of a torus through the cameral cover.

These “cameral covers” are generally more complicated than the “standard” cameral covers studied by Donagi and Gaitsgory
[
9
]
, which are étale locally pullbacks of the cover
\pi:\mathfrak{a}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}/W_{H}
, where
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{h}
is the maximal Cartan subalgebra of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
and
W_{H}
is the corresponding Weyl group. However, as Hurtubise and Markman observed already in
[
23
]
, away from a codimension
2
locus on
M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
, the cameral cover is indeed a cameral cover in the sense of Donagi-Gaitsgory. Cameral covers of this kind are determined by the semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, and we observe that the Killing form matches cameral covers determined by
\mathfrak{h}
with cameral covers determined by its dual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}

1.6.
Global abelianized duality

One of the most important properties of dual Beilinson
1
-motives is that the Fourier-Mukai functor determines an isomorphism between their corresponding bounded derived categories of quasi-coherent sheaves. This isomorphism respects some structure: namely, it exchanges skyscraper sheaves with line bundles naturally determined by the universal line bundle, and intertwines tensorization and convolution.

The Beilinson
1
-motives we study are quite special: they are stacks of torsors under group schemes which are essentially Weil restrictions of tori through finite ramified covers. This allows us to define
translation and tensorization operators
on these Picard stacks, associated with points of the cameral curve and characters and cocharacters of the corresponding tori. At the level of the derived categories of sheaves, translation and tensorization determine
abelian Hecke and Wilson operators
. As Donagi and Pantev already noted in
[
8
]
, the duality of Beilinson
1
-motives associated with cameral covers intertwines the abelian Hecke and Wilson operators.

The existence of a “Hitchin section” in each situation determines the total space of the corresponding fibration, over the generic locus we are considering, as a relative Beilinson
1
-motive. The fibrewise isomorphisms from Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
can then be extended as generic global isomorphisms of Beilinson
1
-motives. The abelian Hecke and Wilson operators can in turn be defined in terms of the total space (that is, in terms of multiplicative Higgs bundles, instead of in terms of cameral data). These are the
abelianized
Hecke and Wilson operators.

Let
h:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
and
\check{h}:\check{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
any pair of “dual” fibrations in the sense of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
, so that the generic fibres of
h
and
\check{h}
are dual Beilinson
1
-motives. We denote by
\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}\subset\mathcal{A}
the corresponding “generic locus” where the cameral curves are smooth and of Donagi-Gaitsgory type. The “global abelianized duality” theorem is the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

There exists an equivalence of derived categories

\mathbf{S}:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}))\longrightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\check{\mathcal{M}}/\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}))

which satisfies the following properties:

(1)

(Normalization)
. The structure sheaf of the “Hitchin section” is mapped to the structure sheaf of the total space.

(2)

(Hecke compatibility)
. The equivalence intertwines Hecke and Wilson operators.

1.7.
Mirror symmetry

In their seminal paper
[
19
]
, Hausel and Thaddeus gave an interpretation of the duality of generic fibres of the Hitchin fibration for (usual,
K_{C}
-twisted) Higgs bundles as an example of Strominger–Yau–Zaslow (SYZ)
mirror symmetry

[
35
]
. In particular, this means that the Hitchin moduli spaces for Langlands dual groups should be mirror Calabi-Yau manifolds. A consequence of this is a certain symmetry in the
E
-polynomials of these two spaces. Such a symmetry was explicitly formulated and conjectured by Hausel and Thaddeus
[
19
]
for the moduli spaces of
\PGL_{n}
-Higgs bundles and (twisted)
\SL_{n}
-Higgs bundles with the name
topological mirror symmetry
. Their conjecture was later verified by Groechenig, Wyss and Ziegler
[
17
]
using techniques of
p
-adic integration. A different proof using perverse sheaves was given by Maulik and Shen
[
27
]
. It is also worth mentioning that the SYZ mirror symmetry of Hitchin fibrations is an important ingredient in the alternative proof of Ngô’s geometric stabilization theorem (the main result of
[
33
]
, which implies the Fundamental Lemma of Langlands-Shelstad) provided by Groechenig, Wyss and Ziegler
[
16
]
.

Mirror Calabi-Yau manifolds are also conjectured to satisfy
homological mirror symmetry
: an equivalence between the derived category of coherent sheaves of one of the manifolds and the Fukaya category of the other. Kapustin and Witten
[
25
]
provided an interpretation of the Fukaya category of Hitchin moduli space as the category of
D
-modules on the stack
\Bun_{G}
, in turn giving a physical explanation of the geometric Langlands program as mirror symmetry in Hitchin’s moduli space. To account for the extra structures of geometric Langlands duality (normalization and Hecke compatibility), Kapustin and Witten explain that this mirror symmetry is in fact “hyper-Kähler enhanced”, in the sense that it tracks down the holomorphicity or Lagrangianity of branes with respect to each of the complex structures of Hitchin’s moduli space. In particular, for example, branes of type (BBB), which are supported on holomorphic symplectic submanifolds, must be mirror partners to (BAA)-branes, supported on holomorphic Lagrangian submanifolds. We refer to Hausel’s survey
[
18
]
for more details on this program.

A priori, multiplicative Hitchin fibrations are algebraically integrable systems only in the case in which
C
is an elliptic curve, which was the original situation studied by Hurtubise and Markman. As explained by Elliott and Pestun, one can also obtain multiplicative Hitchin fibrations which are algebraically integrable systems by working over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with framings. Morally, this amounts to the base curve being
\mathbb{A}^{1}
or
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
, so precisely we obtain something hyper-Kähler in cases where the base curve is itself Calabi-Yau. It is worth mentioning that in these cases there is indeed a “nonabelian Hodge theory”, mediated by the Charbonneau–Hurtubise correspondence with singular monopoles
[
4
]
(so with the Bogomolny equations playing the role of the Hitchin equations) and the correspondences with difference modules studied by Mochizuki
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
(which provide the “de Rham side”). A “Betti side” is also proposed in unpublished work of Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
26
]
.

In these cases where the multiplicative Hitchin fibration is an algebraically integrable system, we can indeed interpret our results as instances of SYZ mirror symmetry. Homological mirror symmetry for the moduli space of multiplicative Higgs bundles should be related to the “multiplicative geometric Langlands program” proposed by Elliott and Pestun
[
12
]
. We refrain from trying to formulate a “multiplicative analogue” of topological mirror symmetry, but nonetheless comment that multiplicative Hitchin fibrations play a fundamental role in G. Wang’s proof of the Fundamental Lemma for the spherical Hecke algebras.

1.8.
Physical interpretation

Kapustin and Witten
[
25
]
explain that the geometric Langlands conjecture can be understood as a consequence of the S-duality between two
4
-dimensional supersymmetric Yang-Mills quantum field theories. Elliott and Pestun similarly formulate their “multiplicative geometric Langlands program” as a physical consequence of the S-duality of
5
-dimensional field theories. More generally, S-duality of both the
4
-dimensional and
5
-dimensional theories are conjectured to be consequences of these theories arising from different dimensional reductions of a certain
6
-dimensional theory
[
40
]
. The physics papers of Tachikawa
[
36
]
and Duan–Lee–Nahmgoong–Wang
[
11
]
explore this in detail, and have been great sources of inspiration for this paper. It is worth noting that these physics papers give a physical interpretation of the special features of the case
\mathsf{A}_{2\ell}^{(2)}
as the manifestation of the presence of a “discrete theta-angle”, which is not detected by the
4
-dimensional theories.

Contents of the paper

This paper consists of 6 sections (including this introduction) and 3 appendices.

In section

We finish the section by providing once again a summary of the dualities we prove in this paper.

In section

[
2
,
3
,
6
,
39
]
. We start by giving the main definitions and explaining the formulation in terms of reductive monoids. We also explain the construction of the Steinberg quasi-section and its extension to monoids. We continue by recalling the study of the symmetries of multiplicative Hitchin fibrations and their cameral description. We finish by proving the first of our results, Theorem
1.1
(=Theorem

\mathsf{A}_{2\ell}^{(2)}

Review of abelian duality and cameral covers

2.1.
Recollections on Picard stacks

Recall that a (strictly commutative)
Picard groupoid
is a (small) symmetric monoidal groupoid such that every object has a monoidal inverse. A
Picard stack
or
commutative group stack
over a scheme
S
is a stack over the fpqc site
S_{\mathrm{fpqc}}
taking values in the category of Picard groupoids. Picard stacks form a
2
-category, which by a theorem of Deligne
[
7
]
is equivalent to the
2
-category whose objects are
2
-term complexes of sheaves of abelian groups
(K_{-1}\rightarrow K_{0})
such that
K_{-1}
is injective, and whose
1
-morphisms and
2
-morphisms are given by chain maps and homotopies, respectively. We fix an inverse
(-)^{\flat}
of this equivalence; that is, to each Picard stack
\mathcal{P}
we associate a
2
-term complex
\mathcal{P}^{\flat}=(K_{-1}\rightarrow K_{0})
with
K_{-1}
injective. For any such complex
K
, we denote by
\mathrm{ch}(K)
the corresponding Picard stack.

Every Picard stack
\mathcal{P}
has an
inertia group

\Aut_{0}(\mathcal{P})
and a
coarse moduli space

|\mathcal{P}|
. We can also consider the neutral connected component
P=|\mathcal{P}|^{0}
and the group of connected components
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{P})
. Under Deligne’s equivalence, we obtain

|\mathcal{P}|=H^{0}(\mathcal{P}^{\flat})\text{ and }\Aut_{0}(\mathcal{P})=H^{-1}(\mathcal{P}^{\flat}).

Example 2.1
.

Let
J\rightarrow S
be a commutative group over
S
and let
J
denote its sheaf of sections. We can realize
J
as a Picard stack
\mathcal{P}=J
by identifying it with the complex concentrated in degree
0
, that is, we set
J^{\flat}=(0\rightarrow J)
. We can also consider
BJ
, the classifying stack of
J
-torsors, by taking
(BJ)^{\flat}
to be a complex quasi-isomorphic to the shifted complex
J[1]=(J\rightarrow 0)
. If
p:X\rightarrow S
is another scheme over
S
, we can consider the
relative Picard stack of
J
-torsors over
X
to be

\Bun_{J/X}=p_{*}BJ:=\mathrm{ch}(\tau_{\leq 0}\mathrm{R}p_{*}(J[1])),

where
\tau_{\leq 0}
denotes the standard truncation to degrees
\leq 0
.
In particular,
\Aut_{0}(\Bun_{J/X})=p_{*}J
and
|\Bun_{J/X}|=\mathrm{R}^{1}p_{*}J
. At the level of
S
-points, we have

\Aut_{0}(\Bun_{J/X})(S)=H^{0}(X,J)\text{ and }|\Bun_{J/X}|(S)=H^{1}(X,J).

When
J=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
is the multiplicative group over
S
, we recover the relative Picard stack

\Pic_{X/S}=\Bun_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}/X}.

The
dual Picard stack
of a Picard stack
\mathcal{P}
is defined as

\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}):=\underline{\Hom}(\mathcal{P},B\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})=\mathrm{ch}(\tau_{\leq 0}\mathrm{R}\underline{\Hom}(\mathcal{P}^{\flat},O^{*}_{S}[1])).

There is a canonical
1
-morphism
\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}))
. When this morphism is an isomorphism, the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}
is said to be
dualizable
. In that case, there is the universal
Poincaré line bundle

\mathbf{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\times\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})
, and we can define the
Fourier-Mukai functor

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})))

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathrm{R}p_{2,*}(\mathrm{L}p_{1}^{*}F\otimes\mathbf{P}),

where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are the natural projections from
\mathcal{P}\times\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})
. When this functor is an equivalence of categories, we say that
\mathcal{P}
is a
good Picard stack
in the sense of Arinkin
[
10
, Appendix A]
.

An immediate property of Fourier-Mukai transforms is that, since
\mathbf{P}
is the universal line bundle, a skyscraper sheaf
O_{x}
at a point
x
of
\mathcal{P}
corresponding to a line bundle
{L}^{x}\rightarrow\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})
is mapped to the line bundle

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(O_{x})=\mathbf{P}|_{\left\{x\right\}\times\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})}={L}^{x}.

Example 2.2
.

Duality for Picard stacks unifies several types of duality associated with some commutative group schemes. In particular, it generalizes the duality of abelian varieties. Namely, if
P\rightarrow S
is an abelian scheme, then

\mathbf{D}(P)=\underline{\Hom}(P,B\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})=\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(P,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})=\hat{P},

is the dual abelian scheme of
P
. It is a classical result of Mukai that the Fourier-Mukai transform for abelian schemes determines an equivalence.

Duality of Picard stacks also generalizes Cartier duality. Recall that if
\Lambda
is a finitely generated abelian group over
S
, its Cartier dual
\Lambda^{*}
is the affine group scheme
\Spec(O_{S}[\Lambda])
, which represents the sheaf
U\mapsto\Hom_{U}(\Lambda\times_{S}U,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},U})
. One can easily show that

\mathbf{D}(\Lambda)=B\Lambda^{*}.

Conversely, a group scheme
J
is
of multiplicative type
if
J=\Lambda^{*}
is the Cartier dual of some finitely generated abelian group over
S
. The group
\Lambda
can be recovered as the character group
\Lambda=\Hom(J,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})
. Again, we have

\mathbf{D}(BJ)=\Lambda.

The Fourier-Mukai transform in this case is determined by the correspondence between characters and irreducible representations, and thus it also gives an equivalence of categories.

A less trivial example is the Picard stack
\Pic_{C}
of a smooth complex projective curve of genus
g
. In this case we can write
\Pic_{C}=B\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\times\Jac(C)\times\mathbb{Z}
, where
\Jac(C)
is the
Jacobian
of the curve, an abelian variety of dimension
g
parametrizing degree
0
line bundles on
C
. The Jacobian of a curve is well known to be self-dual, which makes
\Pic_{C}
a self-dual Picard stack. More precisely, there is a canonical isomorphism

\Pic_{C}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{D}(\Pic_{C})

mediated by the universal line bundle
{U}\rightarrow C\times\Pic_{C}
, with fibres
{U}|_{C\times\left\{L\right\}}=L
. In particular, a line bundle of the form
O_{C}(p)
is sent to
{U}|_{\left\{p\right\}\times\Pic_{C}}\rightarrow\Pic_{C}
.

More generally, we can consider an algebraic torus
T
, and the stack of
T
-bundles

\Bun_{T/C}=BT\times(\Jac(C)\otimes X_{*}(T))\times X_{*}(T).

It follows from the self-duality of
\Jac(C)
that its dual Picard stack is the stack of
T^{\vee}
-bundles

\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/C})\cong X^{*}(T)\times(\Jac(C)\otimes X^{*}(T))\times BT^{\vee}=\Bun_{T^{\vee}/C}.

Again, there is the canonical isomorphism

\Bun_{T/C}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T^{\vee}/C})

mediated by the universal
T^{\vee}
-bundle
{U}\rightarrow C\times\Bun_{T^{\vee}/C}
. Let
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
be a cocharacter and let
O_{C}(\lambda p)
denote the
T
-bundle determined by taking
z^{\lambda}
as transition function near
p
. Then the canonical isomorphism sends this
T
-bundle
O_{C}(\lambda p)
to the line bundle
{U}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\Bun_{T^{\vee}/C}
with fibres

({U}_{\lambda})_{L}=L\times^{T^{\vee},\lambda}\mathbb{A}^{1},

where we understand
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)=X^{*}(T^{\vee})
as a character of
T^{\vee}
.

The examples considered above are particular cases of
Beilinson
1
-motives
. These are Picard stacks
\mathcal{P}
admitting a
2
-step filtration
W_{\bullet}\mathcal{P}

W_{-1}=0\subset W_{0}\subset W_{1}\subset W_{2}=\mathcal{P}

such that

•

\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{0}\cong BJ
is isomorphic to the classifying stack of a group
J
of multiplicative type,

•

\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{1}\cong P
is an abelian scheme, and

•

\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{2}\cong\Lambda
is a finitely generated abelian group.

Note that, if
\mathcal{P}
is a Beilinson
1
-motive, then
\Aut_{0}(\mathcal{P})=\pi_{0}(\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}))^{*}
.

Beilinson
1
-motives are good in the sense of Arinkin. Moreover, on a Beilinson
1
-motive
\mathcal{P}
, the Fourier-Mukai transform
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
intertwines tensorization with the
convolution product

\displaystyle*:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))\times D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))

\displaystyle(F_{1},F_{2})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathrm{R}m_{*}(p_{1}^{*}F_{1}\otimes p_{2}^{*}F_{2}),

where
p_{1},p_{2}:\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}
are the natural projections, and
m:\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}
is the group law on
\mathcal{P}
. More precisely, there are canonical isomorphisms

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(F_{1}*F_{2})\cong\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(F_{1})\otimes\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(F_{2})

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(F_{1}\otimes F_{2})=(\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(F_{1})*\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(F_{2}))\otimes\omega_{\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})/S}[g],

for
\omega_{\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})/S}
the dualizing sheaf of
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})
over
S
and
g
the relative dimension of
\mathcal{P}/S
.

2.2.
Cameral covers with simple Galois ramification

Let
\Phi
be a root system and let
W
denote its Weyl group. We let
\mathbf{R}(\Phi)
denote the root lattice and write
\mathfrak{t}=\mathbf{R}(\Phi)^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. This
\mathfrak{t}
is a vector space acted on by
W
. The projection
\mathfrak{t}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}/W
is the
standard cameral cover
associated with
\Phi
.
More generally, a
cameral cover
associated with the root system
\Phi
is a finite flat morphism of
\mathbb{C}
-schemes
\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
with Galois group
W
and such that, étale-locally on
X
, it is a pull-back of the standard cameral cover
\mathfrak{t}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}/W
.
If
\pi:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
is a cameral cover, we denote by
B\subset X
the branch locus of
\pi
and, for each point
b\in B
, we denote by
W_{b}
the corresponding inertia group.

We say that the cameral cover
\pi
has
simple Galois ramification
if

(1)

for each point
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{X}
, the stabilizer
W_{\tilde{x}}=\left\{w\in W:w\cdot\tilde{x}=\tilde{x}\right\}
has order at most
2
,

(2)

for each root
\alpha\in\Phi
, fixed point subset

\tilde{X}_{\alpha}=\left\{\tilde{p}\in\tilde{X}:s_{\alpha}(\tilde{p})=\tilde{p}\right\}

is non-empty.

Here, we denote by
s_{\alpha}\in W
the reflection associated with the root
\alpha
. If a cameral cover has simple Galois ramification and we fix the positive roots
\Phi_{+}\subset\Phi
, we can describe the branch locus as a union

B=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}B_{\alpha},\text{ for }B_{\alpha}=\pi(\tilde{X}_{\alpha}),

and there is a natural isomorphism of the inertia group
W_{b}
, for
b\in B_{\alpha}
, with the subgroup of
W
generated by
s_{\alpha}
. Moreover,
\tilde{X}
is smooth if and only if the divisors
\tilde{X}_{\alpha}
(and in turn the
B_{\alpha}
) are pairwise disjoint.

Let
G
be a semisimple group with maximal torus
T\subset G
such that
\Phi(G,T)=\Phi
.
We can consider the Weil restriction

\Pi=\Pi_{\pi,G}:=\pi_{*}(\tilde{X}\times T)

and the invariant subgroup schemes

J^{1}=J^{1}_{\pi,G}:=\Pi^{W}\text{ and }J^{0}=J^{0}_{\pi,G}:=\Pi^{W,0},

where the superindex
0
stands for taking fibrewise connected components.
We can also consider the subgroup
J=J_{\pi,G}\subset J^{1}
characterized as follows: for every
X
-scheme
S
, the group
J(S)
is the subgroup of
J^{1}(S)
consisting of morphisms
g:S\times_{X}\tilde{X}\rightarrow T
such that, for every
\tilde{x}\in(S\times_{X}\tilde{X})(\mathbb{C})
with
s_{\alpha}(\tilde{x})=\tilde{x}
for some root
\alpha\in\Phi
, we have
\alpha(g(x))=1
. There are inclusions

J^{0}\subset J\subset J^{1}.

These are group schemes over
X
which coincide away from the branch locus of the cameral cover. If we assume that
\pi
has simple Galois ramification then, over a branch point
b\in B_{\alpha}\subset X
, the fibres of these groups are commutative algebraic groups over
\mathbb{C}
whose maximal reductive quotient is given by

(J^{0}_{b})^{\mathrm{red}}=T^{s_{\alpha},0}\subset(J_{b})^{\mathrm{red}}=\ker(\alpha:T\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})\subset(J^{1}_{b})^{\mathrm{red}}=T^{s_{\alpha}}.

We recall the description of the groups of the form
\ker\alpha
from
[
8
]
. For every root
\alpha\in\Phi
we can define the numbers
\varepsilon_{\alpha}
and
\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{\vee}
to be the positive generators of the images of the maps
\langle\alpha,-\rangle:X_{*}(T)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
and
\langle\alpha^{\vee},-\rangle:X^{*}(T)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, respectively. There is the following commutative diagram, with short exact rows and columns
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{s_{\alpha},0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker\alpha}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{s_{\alpha},0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T^{s_{\alpha}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon_{\alpha^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon_{\alpha^{\vee}}.}

Recall that, if
G
is simple, then we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2\text{ if }G=\mathrm{Sp}_{r}\text{ and $\alpha$ is a long root},\\
1\text{ in all other cases },\end{cases}

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\vee}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2\text{ if }G=\mathrm{SO}_{2r+1}\text{ and $\alpha$ is a short root},\\
1\text{ in all other cases }.\end{cases}

In particular, it is important to remark that
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=2
for a root of
\SL_{2}
.
For general semisimple
G
, we make the following observations

(1)

if
r>1
and the Dynkin diagram of
G
is simply-laced, then
T^{s_{\alpha}}
is connected,

(2)

if
G
is simply-connected, then
T^{s_{\alpha}}=\ker\alpha
,

(3)

if
G
is of adjoint type, then
T^{s_{\alpha},0}=\ker\alpha
.

Globally, we obtain the following diagram, with short exact rows and columns
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}B_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}B_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{\alpha^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}B_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon_{\alpha^{\vee}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_{+}}B_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{Z}/\varepsilon_{\alpha^{\vee}}.}

We make the following observations

(1)

if
r>1
and the Dynkin diagram of
G
is simply-laced, then
J^{0}=J=J^{1}
,

(2)

if
G
is simply-connected, then
J^{1}=J
,

(3)

if
G
is of adjoint type, then
J^{0}=J
.

2.3.
The dual group

Let
G^{\vee}
denote the Langlands dual group of
G
. This group has as maximal torus the dual torus
T^{\vee}=X_{*}(T)^{*}
, and as root system the dual root system
\Phi(G^{\vee},T^{\vee})=\Phi^{\vee}
. Recall that the Killing form determines a
W
-equivariant isomorphism
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathfrak{t}
,
x\mapsto(x,-)
. We pick the usual normalization of the Killing form in which short roots
\alpha
are imposed to satisfy
(\alpha,\alpha)=2
. Choosing this normalization, the Killing form sends short roots to long coroots, and long roots to multiples of short coroots. Therefore, the root lattice
\mathbf{R}(\Phi)
is matched with
\mathbf{R}(\Phi^{\vee})
and the weight lattice
\mathbf{P}(\Phi)
is matched with
\mathbf{P}(\Phi^{\vee})
. The isomorphism also allows us to regard the cameral cover
\pi:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
as a cameral cover associated with
\Phi^{\vee}
. Therefore, we can define the group schemes

\check{J}^{0}=J^{0}_{\pi,G^{\vee}}\subset\check{J}=J_{\pi,G^{\vee}}\subset\check{J}^{1}=J^{1}_{\pi,G^{\vee}},

by replacing
T
with
T^{\vee}
. Moreover, note that the Killing form also identifies

(1)

the component group
J^{1}/J^{0}
with the component group
\check{J}^{1}/\check{J}^{0}
,

(2)

the component group
J/J^{0}
with the quotient
\check{J}^{1}/\check{J}
.

2.4.
Cameral curves and abelian duality

Fix
G
a complex semisimple group and
T\subset G
a maximal torus. Let
\tilde{C}
and
C
be smooth complex projective curves and let
\pi:\tilde{C}\rightarrow C
be a cameral cover associated with
\Phi(G,T)
with simple Galois ramification. We can consider the groups
J^{0},J,J^{1}
and
\check{J}^{0},\check{J},\check{J}^{1}
from the previous section, and their associated Picard stacks of torsors

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{0}=\Bun_{J^{0}/C},\ \ \mathcal{P}=\Bun_{J/C},\ \ \mathcal{P}^{1}=\Bun_{J^{1}/C},

\displaystyle\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}=\Bun_{\check{J}^{0}/C},\ \ \check{\mathcal{P}}=\Bun_{\check{J}/C},\ \ \check{\mathcal{P}}^{1}=\Bun_{\check{J}^{1}/C}.

The inclusion
J^{1}\hookrightarrow\Pi
induces a morphism of Picard stacks

\iota:\mathcal{P}^{1}\longrightarrow\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}}.

Dually, we get a morphism

\mathbf{D}(\iota):\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}})\longrightarrow\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}^{1}).

On the other hand, we have the
norm map

\displaystyle\Nm:T^{\vee}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T^{\vee}

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\longmapsto\prod_{w\in W}w\cdot t.

This map induces a morphism
\Pi\rightarrow\check{J}^{0}
, and in turn a morphism of Picard stacks

\Nm:\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}}\longrightarrow\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}.

Recall that we have the canonical isomorphism

\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}}.

Consider the map
\mathrm{S}_{0}:\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}^{1})\rightarrow\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}
induced by the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\iota)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Nm}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{S}_{0}}

The main result of
[
8
]
is the following.

Theorem 2.3
.

The Picard stacks
\mathcal{P}^{1}
and
\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}
are Beilinson
1
-motives and the map

\mathrm{S}_{0}:\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}^{1})\rightarrow\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}

is an isomorphism. In particular, the Fourier-Mukai transform
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}^{1}}
induces an equivalence of categories

\mathbf{S}^{\ab}_{0}=(\mathrm{S}_{0}^{-1})^{*}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{P}^{1}}:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}^{1}))\longrightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0})).

More generally, they prove the following.

Corollary 2.4
.

The Picard stacks
\mathcal{P}=\Bun_{J/C}
and
\check{\mathcal{P}}=\Bun_{\check{J}/C}
are Beilinson
1
-motives and the isomorphism
\mathrm{S}_{0}:\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}^{1})\rightarrow\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}
induces a natural isomorphism
\mathrm{S}:\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\check{\mathcal{P}}
. In particular, the Fourier-Mukai transform
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
determines an equivalence of categories

\mathbf{S}^{\ab}=(\mathrm{S}^{-1})^{*}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))\longrightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\check{\mathcal{P}})).

Proof of Corollary
2.4
.

Note that we have an exact sequence (i.e. a distinguished triangle on the associated complexes) of Picard stacks
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(C,J^{1}/J)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Dualizing, we obtain an exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(C,J^{1}/J)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Now, since
\mathrm{S}_{0}:\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}^{1})\rightarrow\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}
induces the isomorphism
H^{0}(C,J^{1}/J^{0})\rightarrow H^{0}(C,\check{J}^{1}/\check{J}^{0})
(see appendix
B
), applying
\mathrm{S}_{0}
to the above exact sequence yields
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(C,\check{J}/\check{J^{0}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\check{\mathcal{P}}^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{S}_{0}(\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

so we conclude that
\mathrm{S}_{0}
induces an isomorphism
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})\rightarrow\check{\mathcal{P}}
.
∎

Theorem
2.3
is the most important duality theorem for Picard stacks that we apply in this paper. The proof of this result is due to Donagi and Pantev
[
8
]
. Chen and Zhu
[
5
]
proved an analogue for positive characteristic. In an aim to make this paper as self-contained as possible, and in order to not leave too many details for the reader to fill in, we dedicate appendix
B
to review the main arguments of the proof.

Remark 1
.

More generally, we can consider a commutative group scheme
J^{\prime}\rightarrow C
which is fiberwise open and closed inside
J^{1}
.
We can then define the group
\check{J}^{\prime}\rightarrow C
as the open and closed subscheme of
\check{J}^{1}
such that the isomorphism
J^{1}/J^{0}\rightarrow\check{J}^{1}/\check{J}^{0}
matches

with

shows that the Picard stacks
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\Bun_{J^{\prime}/C}

2.5.
Abelian Hecke compatibility

A very important property of geometric Langlands duality is the compatibility of tensorization (or Wilson) operators with Hecke (or t’Hooft) operators. An “abelian version” of this compatibility is already detectable from Theorem
2.3
and the properties of the Fourier-Mukai transform on Beilinson
1
-motives.

With a point
\tilde{p}\in\tilde{C}
and a cocharacter
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
we can associate a
T
-bundle
O_{\tilde{C}}(\lambda\tilde{p})
on
\tilde{C}
. The natural map
\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}
induced from the norm map sends this bundle to a
J
-torsor on
C
that we denote by
S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}
. The
translation operator
associated with
(\lambda,\tilde{p})
is the morphism

\displaystyle\mathbf{Trans}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}:\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle{E}

\displaystyle\longmapsto{E}\otimes S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}.

The
abelian Hecke operator
associated with
(\lambda,\tilde{p})
is the morphism of derived categories obtained by taking convolution with the skyscraper sheaf
O_{S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\ab}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\longmapsto F*O_{S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}}.

Equivalently,
\mathbf{H}^{\mathrm{ad}}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}
is the integral transform defined by the structure sheaf of the graph of
\mathbf{Trans}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}
inside
\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{P}
.

Dually, with a point
\tilde{p}\in\tilde{C}
and a character
\chi\in X^{*}(T)
we can associate the line bundle
L_{\chi,\tilde{p}}
on
\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}}
whose fibre over a
T
-bundle
E
is the fibre
(L_{\chi,\tilde{p}})_{E}=(E_{\chi})_{\tilde{p}}
of the line bundle
E_{\chi}=E\times^{T,\chi}\mathbb{A}^{1}
. We can regard
L_{\chi,\tilde{p}}
as an element of
\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}})
, and consider its image under the map
\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}})\rightarrow\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})
. We denote this image by
{L}_{\chi,\tilde{p}}
. The
tensorization operator
associated with
(\chi,\tilde{p})
is the morphism

\displaystyle\mathbf{Tens}_{\chi,\tilde{p}}:\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle{E}

\displaystyle\longmapsto{E}\otimes{L}_{\chi,\tilde{p}}.

The
abelian Wilson operator
associated with
(\chi,\tilde{p})
is the following morphism of derived categories

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}^{\ab}_{\chi,\tilde{p}}:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\longmapsto F\otimes L_{\chi,\tilde{p}}.

Corollary 2.5
.

The duality equivalence
\mathbf{S}^{\ab}:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))\rightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\check{\mathcal{P}}))
intertwines the abelian Hecke and Wilson operators. More precisely, for every point
\tilde{p}\in\tilde{C}
and for every cocharacter
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
, the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(\QCoh(\check{\mathcal{P}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(\QCoh(\check{\mathcal{P}})).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{S}^{\ab}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{H}^{\ab}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{W}^{\ab}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{S}^{\ab}}

Proof.

The key is the commutativity (by construction) of the following square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\check{\mathcal{P}},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{S}}

and the properties of the canonical isomorphism
\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}}\rightarrow\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}})
explained in section
2.1
.
This implies that, for any
\tilde{p}\in\tilde{C}
and for any character
\chi\in X^{*}(T)
, we have
\mathrm{S}({L}_{\chi,\tilde{p}})=S_{\chi,\tilde{p}}
and, dually, for any cocharacter
\lambda\in X_{*}(T)
,

(\mathrm{S}^{-1})^{*}S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}={L}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}.

Here, we are regarding the point
S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}
of
\mathcal{P}
as a line bundle on
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P})
and
{L}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}
as a line bundle on
\check{\mathcal{P}}
. Now, we can just check, for any object
F
of
D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}))
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(\mathbf{H}^{\ab}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}(F))=\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(F*O_{S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}})=(\mathrm{S}^{-1})^{*}(\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(F)\otimes\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(O_{S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}}))=\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(F)\otimes\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(O_{S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}}).

Thus, we just need to compute
\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(O_{S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}})
. The Fourier-Mukai transform of a skyscraper sheaf is easy to obtain

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(O_{S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}})=S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}.

Therefore,

\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(O_{S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}})=(\mathrm{S}^{-1})^{*}S_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}={L}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}},

so

\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(\mathbf{H}^{\ab}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}(F))=\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(F)\otimes{L}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}=\mathbf{W}^{\ab}_{\lambda,\tilde{p}}(\mathbf{S}^{\ab}(F)),

as we wanted to show.
∎

2.6.
Generalized Hitchin fibrations and abelianization

All the “generalized Hitchin fibrations” that we study in this paper have a similar structure. We start with a semisimple group
G
, and consider an affine
G
-variety
M
. We denote by
\mathfrak{c}_{M}=M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}G
the corresponding invariant quotient. In order to “twist”, we also consider a torus
Z
with an action on
M
compatible with the
G
-action. We want to study the sequence of stacks

[M/G\times Z]\longrightarrow[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]\longrightarrow BZ

and its relative version over a curve
C
. That is, given a
Z
-torsor
L\rightarrow C
, we can pullback this sequence through the map
C\rightarrow BZ
determined by
L
, and consider the stacks

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{M,L}=\Map_{L}(C,[M/G\times Z])

and

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{M,L}=\Map_{L}(C,[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]),

and the induced “generalized Hitchin fibration”

\displaystyle h=h_{M,L}:\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}.

Note that the points of
\mathcal{M}
are “Higgs pairs” of the form
(E,\varphi)
, where
E\rightarrow C
is a
G
-bundle and
\varphi
is a section of the associated bundle
(E,L)\times^{G\times Z}M
.

Remark 2
.

More precisely, in our discussions we are not only fixing the
Z
-torsor
L
but actually what we call the “meromorphic datum”, which carries the extra information of a section of some associated vector bundle. This distinction is not relevant for the discussion we have at hand.

In each case, we check that the fibrations we consider have “abelian symmetries” in the following sense. There exists some commutative group scheme
J\rightarrow C\times\mathcal{A}
and an action of the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}=\Bun_{J/C}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, there is a Zariski open subset
M^{\reg}\subset M
for which the map
[M^{\reg}/G\times Z]\rightarrow[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]
is a union of gerbes, and a smaller Zariski open subset
M^{\gl}\subset M
for which the map
[M/G\times Z]\rightarrow[\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\gl}}/Z]
is a single gerbe. This implies that there is a Zariski open subset
\mathcal{A}^{\gl}\subset\mathcal{A}
over which
\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathcal{P}
-torsor. The existence of a “Hitchin section”
\epsilon:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
provides a trivialization of this torsor. Summing up, we have the following.

Proposition 2.6
.

There is an isomorphism of stacks

\displaystyle u:\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{A}^{\gl}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{A}^{\gl}

\displaystyle{E}

\displaystyle\longmapsto{E}\otimes\epsilon.

The group
J
has a very convenient “Galois description” as follows. There is a cameral curve

\pi:\tilde{C}\longrightarrow C\times\mathcal{A}

and a Zariski open subset
\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\gl}
such that the fibres
\pi_{a}:\tilde{C}_{a}\rightarrow C
, for
a\in\mathcal{A}
are smooth covers of
C
with simple Galois ramification. This cameral curve is associated with some root system
\Phi_{H}
, and there exists some semisimple group
H
with maximal torus
A\subset H
such that the group
J
is a closed and open subgroup of the invariant Weil restriction

J^{1}=\pi_{*}(\tilde{C}\times A)^{W_{H}}.

We conclude from the above that the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}/\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}
is a Beilinson
1
-motive like the ones considered in section

.

3.1.
Construction

Let
C
be a smooth complex projective curve. A
multiplicative Hitchin fibration
over
C
is determined by the following data:

•

A complex semisimple group
G
of rank
r
. We denote by
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
its simply-connected cover.

•

A very flat
[
39
, Definition 2.3.10]
reductive monoid
M
, with unit group
M^{\times}
and with a prescribed isomorphism

M^{\mathrm{der}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}G^{\mathrm{sc}},

where
M^{\mathrm{der}}=(M^{\times})^{\mathrm{der}}
denotes the semisimple part of
M^{\times}
.

The group
G
acts on
M^{\times}
via the adjoint action, and in turn this action extends to an action on the monoid
M
. We can thus consider the GIT quotient
\mathfrak{c}_{M}=M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{Ad}(G)
, which is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{c}_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}\times\mathbb{A}_{M}
; where
\mathfrak{c}_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}=G^{\mathrm{sc}}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{Ad}(G)
is the GIT quotient of
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
by the adjoint action on itself and
\mathbb{A}_{M}:=M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}(G^{\mathrm{sc}}\times G^{\mathrm{sc}})
is the
abelianization
of
M

[
37
]
.
Since
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
is simply-connected, the GIT quotient
\mathfrak{c}_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}
is smooth and isomorphic to the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{r}
.

Consider the center
Z_{M^{\times}}
of
M^{\times}
and its neutral connected component
Z=Z^{0}_{M^{\times}}
, which is a torus. The natural left multiplication action of
Z
on
M
commutes with the adjoint action of
G
and induces an action of
Z
on
\mathbb{A}_{M}
. In fact,
\mathbb{A}_{M}
can be understood as a commutative monoid (in this case, a toric variety) with unit group

\mathbb{A}_{M}^{\times}=Z_{M^{\times}}/(Z_{M^{\times}}\cap M^{\mathrm{der}}).

Consider now the following sequence at the level of stacky quotients
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[M/(G\times Z)\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BZ.}

Let
D
be a map

, and consider the mapping stacks

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{G,M}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{M}_{G,M,D}:=\Map_{D}(C,\left[M/(G\times Z)\right])=\Map_{C}(C,C\times_{[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z],D}\left[M/(G\times Z)\right]),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{M}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{A}_{M,D}:=\Map_{D}(C,\left[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z\right])=\Map_{C}(C,C\times_{[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z],D}\left[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z\right]).

Since we are fixing

, we generally drop it from the notation, although both constructions depend on it. Moreover, note that

only depends on

, and not on

, since

.

Definition 3.1

The
multiplicative Hitchin fibration
associated with the data

and with meromorphic datum

is the natural map

\displaystyle h:=h_{G,M,D}:\mathcal{M}_{G,M,D}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{M,D},

induced from the map

.

Remark 4

Note that a map

is given by the following data

•

,

•

, where

is the vector bundle associated with

3.2.
Multiplicative Higgs bundles and the Vinberg monoid

Our notion of multiplicative Hitchin fibration is the one studied in the paper of G. Wang
[
39
]
, considered also previously in the works of Bouthier and J. Chi
[
2
,
3
,
6
]
. In other works, like Elliott–Pestun
[
12
]
and Hurtubise–Markman
[
23
]
, the multiplicative Hitchin fibration is introduced in terms of “meromorphic”
multiplicative Higgs bundles
(that is, Higgs pairs which are group-valued, instead of taking values on a vector space, but with rational singularities).

More precisely, by a multiplicative Higgs bundle we mean a pair
(E,\varphi)
formed by a
G
-bundle
E\rightarrow C
and a rational section
\varphi
of the adjoint group bundle
E(G)=E\times_{\mathrm{Ad}}G
. We can also take
\varphi
to be a section of the group bundle
E(G^{\mathrm{sc}})=E\times_{\mathrm{Ad}}G^{\mathrm{sc}}
. This section is rational, meaning that it has singularities at some points
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\in C
. As a direct consequence of Iwahori’s theorem, the singularity of the section
\varphi
at each one of the
p_{i}
is determined by a dominant cocharacter
\lambda_{i}
of
G
(provided a choice of Borel pair
(B,T)
for
G
).

The choice of
(B,T)
determines a set of simple roots
\Delta=\left\{\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{r}\right\}
for
G
, and we let
\mathbb{A}_{G}
denote the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{r}
with the natural
T
-action

t\cdot\bm{x}=t\cdot(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})=(\alpha_{1}(t)\cdot x_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}(t)\cdot x_{r})=:\bm{\alpha}(t)\cdot\bm{x}.

This action factors through an action of
T^{\mathrm{ad}}=T/Z_{G}
. Let
O_{C}(p)
be the bundle defined by the divisor of a point
p\in C
, with its canonical section
z_{p}
. We can regard this as the map
C\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
sending
p
to the closed point
0\in[\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
. A coweight
\lambda
of
G
determines a map
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\rightarrow T^{\mathrm{ad}}
, and we have, for
z_{p}\neq 0

\lambda(z_{p})\cdot(1,\dots,1)=(z_{p}^{\langle\alpha_{1},\lambda\rangle},\dots,z_{p}^{\langle\alpha_{r},\lambda\rangle}),

which extends to
z_{p}=0
if and only if
\lambda
is dominant. In other words,
\mathbb{A}_{G}
is the affine toric embedding of
T^{\mathrm{ad}}
determined by the cone generated by the dominant coweights,

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}X_{+}(T^{\mathrm{ad}})\subset X_{*}(T^{\mathrm{ad}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}.

The
Vinberg monoid
or
enveloping monoid

\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})
of
G^{\mathrm{sc}}

[
37
]
is a reductive monoid with unit group equal to

G^{\mathrm{sc}}_{+}:=(G^{\mathrm{sc}}\times T^{\mathrm{sc}})/Z_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}

and whose abelianization coincides with
\mathbb{A}_{G}
. Since the cocharacters
\lambda_{i}
are dominant, the natural multiplicative map

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}):\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{n}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T^{\mathrm{ad}}

\displaystyle(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\lambda_{1}(z_{1})\cdots\lambda_{n}(z_{n})

extends to a map
\bm{\lambda}:\mathbb{A}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{G}
. We can then consider the fibered product

M_{\bm{\lambda}}=\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})\times_{\mathbb{A}_{G},\lambda}\mathbb{A}^{n},

which is a reductive monoid with abelianization
\mathbb{A}^{n}
and such that
M^{\mathrm{der}}=G^{\mathrm{sc}}
. The multiplicative Hitchin fibration associated with the data
(G,M_{\bm{\lambda}})
is precisely the fibration considered in the papers
[
23
,
12
]
, as explained in
[
3
,
39
]
.

In fact, all multiplicative Hitchin fibrations arise in a similar way. Indeed, Vinberg’s classification
[
37
]
implies that the Vinberg monoid is
versal
among very flat reductive monoids, meaning that every very flat reductive monoid
M
with
M^{\mathrm{der}}=G^{\mathrm{sc}}
can be obtained as a fibered product of its abelianization with the Vinberg monoid
\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})
.

Remark 5
.

If
M
is a very flat reductive monoid with
M^{\mathrm{der}}=G^{\mathrm{sc}}
and we denote by
Z
the connected center of
M^{\times}
, then what Vinberg classification tells us is that there is a natural map
\phi_{Z}:Z\rightarrow T^{\mathrm{ad}}
extending to a map
\phi_{M}:\mathbb{A}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{G}
of the abelianizations and that
M
is obtained as the pullback of
\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})
through this map.

Remark 6
.

The abelianization map
\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{G}
of the Vinberg monoid admits a natural section
\delta_{\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})}:\mathbb{A}_{G}\rightarrow\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})
extending the map
T^{\mathrm{ad}}\rightarrow T^{\mathrm{sc}}_{+}
,
t\mapsto(t,t^{-1})
, where
T^{\mathrm{sc}}_{+}=(T^{\mathrm{sc}}\times T^{\mathrm{sc}})/Z_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}
is the maximal torus of
G^{\mathrm{sc}}_{+}
. More generally, if
M
is a very flat reductive monoid with
M^{\mathrm{der}}=G^{\mathrm{sc}}
and we consider the maximal torus
T_{M}=(Z_{M}\times T^{\mathrm{sc}})/Z_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}
of
M^{\times}
determined by
T^{\mathrm{sc}}
, then we can define the map
Z\rightarrow T_{M}
,
z\mapsto(z,\phi_{Z}(z)^{-1})
, and this map extends to a section
\delta_{M}:\mathbb{A}_{M}\rightarrow M
of the abelianization map
M\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{M}
.

3.3.
Steinberg quasi-sections

As above, we fix a Borel pair
(B,T)
for
G
, which determines a set of simple roots
\Delta
. Furthermore, we also fix a pinning of
G
. This is determined by the extra choice of
1
-parameter groups

u_{\alpha}:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\rightarrow U_{\alpha}

parametrizing the root groups
U_{\alpha}
associated with each root
\alpha\in\Delta
in such a way that, for every
t\in T
and for every
x\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
,

\mathrm{Ad}_{t}(u_{\alpha}(x))=u_{\alpha}(\alpha(t)x).

A
Coxeter datum

(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})

[
39
, Definition 2.2.7]
consists of the following

•

a bijection
\sigma:\left\{1,\dots,r\right\}\rightarrow\Delta
(i.e. a total ordering of the simple roots)

•

a choice
\dot{\bm{s}}=\left\{\dot{s}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Delta\right\}
of a representative
\dot{s}_{\alpha}\in N_{G}(T)
of each simple reflection
s_{\alpha}\in W
associated with a simple root
\alpha\in\Delta
.

Once
\Delta
is fixed, we say that an element
w\in W
of the Weyl group is a (
\Delta
-)
Coxeter element
if it can be written as
w=w_{\sigma}=s_{\sigma(1)}\dots s_{\sigma(r)}
, for some total ordering
\sigma
of
\Delta
. We denote by
\mathrm{Cox}(W,\Delta)
the set of Coxeter elements of
W
.

If we fix a Coxeter datum
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
, we can consider the associated
Steinberg quasi-section

\displaystyle\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}:\mathfrak{c}_{G}\cong\mathbb{A}^{r}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow G^{\mathrm{sc}}

\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\prod_{i=1}^{r}u_{\sigma(i)}(x_{i})\dot{s}_{\sigma(i)}.

This is a quasi-section in the sense that, composed with the projection
G^{\mathrm{sc}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{G}
it is not necessarily equal to the identity, but rather to an automorphism of
\mathfrak{c}_{G}
. The image
\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(\mathfrak{c}_{G})
is called the
Steinberg cross-section
associated with
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
.

The dependence on the Coxeter datum is not too strong: if
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},\dot{\bm{s}}^{\prime})
are such that
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
, then there exists some
t\in T
such that the corresponding cross-sections are conjugate under
t
; on the other hand, if
\dot{\bm{s}}=\dot{\bm{s}}^{\prime}
, then, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}^{r}
with
x_{\sigma(i)}=x_{\sigma^{\prime}(i)}
for every
i=1,\dots,r
, the elements
\epsilon^{\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}}}(x)
and
\epsilon^{\sigma^{\prime},\dot{\bm{s}}}(x^{\prime})
are conjugate in
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
.

There is a natural way
[
39
, Proposition 2.4.4]
of extending a Steinberg quasi-section
\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}
to a reductive monoid
M
with derived group equal to
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
, by putting

\displaystyle\epsilon_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}:\mathfrak{c}_{M}\cong\mathbb{A}_{M}\times\mathfrak{c}_{G}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M

\displaystyle(a,x)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\delta_{M}(a)\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(x).

This provides a quasi-section of the GIT quotient
M\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}
.
However, in this case the dependence of the section on the Coxeter datum is strong: indeed, for two different Coxeter data
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},\dot{\bm{s}})
, the elements
\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(0)
and
\epsilon^{(\sigma^{\prime},\dot{\bm{s}})}(0)
are not conjugate (see
[
6
, Remark 2.2.18]
).

3.4.
Symmetries

An element
x\in M
is
regular
(under the
G
-action) if its stabilizer

I_{G,M,x}:=\left\{g\in G:\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(x)=x\right\}

has the minimal possible dimension for all elements of
M
. We denote by
M^{\reg}\subset M
the subset of regular elements. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{G,M}^{\reg}
the subset of sections in
\mathcal{M}_{G,M}
factorizing through
[M^{\reg}/(G\times Z)]
. Given any
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}^{\reg}_{G,M,a}
, the fibre of
a
through the restriction
h|_{\mathcal{M}_{G,M}^{\reg}}
.

The stabilizers define a group scheme
I_{G,M}\rightarrow M
called the
centralizer group scheme
, which restricts to a smooth commutative group scheme over
M^{\reg}
. Under some adaptation of the typical descent argument of Ngô, J. Chi
[
6
, Lemma 2.4.2]
proved the following (for the Vinberg monoid, but it generalizes immediately to any very flat monoid).

Proposition 3.2

(J. Chi)
.

There is a unique commutative group scheme
J_{G,M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}
with a
G
-equivariant isomorphism

\chi^{*}_{M}J_{G,M}|_{M^{\reg}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}I_{G,M}|_{M^{\reg}},

which can be extended to a homomorphism
\chi_{M}^{*}J_{G,M}\rightarrow I_{G,M}
. Here,
\chi_{M}:M\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}
denotes the natural projection to the GIT quotient. We call this
J_{G,M}
the
regular centralizer group scheme
.

For any Coxeter datum
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
and any element
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M}
, the element
\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(a)
is regular. Moreover, it is a result of J. Chi
[
6
, Section 2.4.1]
, that every regular element of
M
is conjugate to an element of the form
\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(a)
for some
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M}
and some Coxeter datum
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
.
If
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\times}}:=M^{\times}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{Ad}(M^{\times})
comes from the unit group, then the preimage of
a
through the restriction
M^{\times,\reg}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\times}}
consists of a single orbit, so it is a homogeneous space, and the map
[M^{\times,\reg}/G]\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\times}}
is a
J_{G,M^{\times}}
-gerbe neutralized by any Steinberg quasi-section
\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}
.
However, in general the fibres of the GIT quotient
M\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}
contain several regular orbits, so the fibres of
M^{\reg}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}
are no longer homogeneous spaces. The number of irreducible components of the fibres is bounded above by the cardinal of
\mathrm{Cox}(W,\Delta)
.
An immediate consequence of the above proposition is that the natural map
[M^{\reg}/G]\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}
is a finite union of
J_{G,M}
-gerbes, and each of these gerbes is neutralized by some extended Steinberg quasi-section
\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}_{M}
.
The moral of this is that understanding the symmetries of the multiplicative Hitchin fibres will amount to understanding the structure of the regular centralizer
J_{G,M}
.

3.5.
Galois description

We choose now a maximal torus
T_{M}\subset M^{\times}
, which in turn determines a choice of maximal torus
T\subset G
, and denote by
\bar{T}_{M}
the closure of
T_{M}\subset M
inside the monoid
M
. We let
\Phi:=\Phi(G,T)
denote the corresponding root system (of both
G
and
M^{\times}
) and
W
its Weyl group.

The Chevalley isomorphism
\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\times}}\cong T_{M}/W
extends to an isomorphism
\mathfrak{c}_{M}\cong\bar{T}_{M}/W

[
39
, Theorem 2.4.2]
, and thus allows us to consider the
cameral cover

\pi_{M}:\bar{T}_{M}\longrightarrow\bar{T}_{M}/W\cong\mathfrak{c}_{M}.

We introduce the Weil restriction

\Pi:=\Pi_{G,M}=\pi_{M,*}(\bar{T}_{M}\times T)
,
on which
W
acts diagonally, and also the invariant subgroup

J^{1}:=J^{1}_{G,M}=\Pi^{W}

and its neutral connected component

J^{0}:=J^{0}_{G,M}=\Pi^{W,0}
.
Consider now the subgroup scheme
J^{\prime}:=J^{\prime}_{G,M}\hookrightarrow J^{1}
defined as follows. For any
\mathfrak{c}_{M}
-scheme
S\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}
, the elements of
J^{\prime}(S)
are points
f\in\Pi(S)=\Hom(S\times_{\mathfrak{c}_{M}}\bar{T}_{M},T)
which belong to
J^{1}(S)
and such that, for every
x\in(S\times_{\mathfrak{c}_{M}}\bar{T}_{M})(\mathbb{C})
, if
s_{\alpha}(x)=x
for a root
\alpha\in\Phi
, then
\alpha(f(x))=1
.
The Grothendieck–Springer simultaneous resolution of singularities implies the following result, which can be found in
[
39
, Theorem 2.4.12]
.

Proposition 3.3

(G. Wang)
.

There is a canonical open embedding
J_{G,M}\hookrightarrow J^{1}_{G,M}
identifying
J_{G,M}
with
J^{\prime}_{G,M}
.

The proof of the above proposition follows the same argument as the proof of Proposition 2.4.7 in
[
33
]
. We also refer the reader to the sketch of the proof of Proposition
4.4
in this paper, where a very similar argument is explained.

3.6.
The group-like locus

Inside
\mathfrak{c}_{M}
there are two special divisors that one must take into account. The first is the
boundary divisor

\mathfrak{B}_{M}
, defined as the complement of

\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\times}=\mathfrak{c}_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}\times\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{M}.

Note that the elements of
M
lying over
\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\times}
are those in the unit group
M^{\times}
. There is also the
extended discriminant divisor

\mathfrak{D}_{M}
, defined for
M=\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})
and extended by pullback to every
M
. The divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})}
is defined as the vanishing set of the following
W
-invariant function on
\bar{T}_{\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})}
,

\mathrm{Disc}_{\Env(G^{\mathrm{sc}})}:=\prod_{\beta\in\Phi_{+}}(\beta,0)\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}(1-(0,\alpha))=(2\rho,0)\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}(1-(0,\alpha)),

where
\rho
denotes the half-sum of the positive roots. It is a result of G. Wang
[
39
, Lemma 2.4.7]
that these two divisors intersect properly, and thus that the codimension of the intersection
\mathfrak{D}_{M}^{\times}:=\mathfrak{D}_{M}\cap\mathfrak{B}_{M}
is at least
2
. We say that an element of
M
is
group-like
if it belongs to the preimage of
\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl}=\mathfrak{c}_{M}\setminus\mathfrak{D}_{M}^{\times}
, and denote by
M^{\gl}=\chi_{M}^{-1}(\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl})
the locus of group-like elements.

Remark 7
.

In Hurtubise and Markman’s paper
[
23
, Definition 6.6]
, they introduce a certain notion of regularity in the context of the multiplicative Hitchin fibration which is related to our notion of being group-like. Our notion of regularity is the one introduced by G. Wang
[
39
, Section 2.4.5]
, which is completely parallel to the group case and compatible with the usual notion of regularity of a group action.

We say that an element
x\in M
is
semisimple
if its adjoint orbit contains an element of
\bar{T}_{M}
. An element
x\in M
is
regular semisimple
if it is both regular and semisimple. The same notions apply to adjoint orbits, and we consider the subsets
M^{\reg}
,
M^{\Ss}
and
M^{\rs}
of regular, semisimple and regular semisimple elements, respectively.
It turns out that
\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}
, the image of
M^{\rs}
under the GIT quotient, is the complement of the discriminant divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{M}
. Moreover, each fiber of
\chi_{M}
over
\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}
consists of a single orbit. Therefore, an element of
M
is group-like if it is either in the unit group or regular semisimple, that is

M^{\gl}=M^{\times}\cup M^{\rs}.

Over the group like locus
\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl}
we can obtain a more detailed description of the regular centralizer
J
. Indeed, if
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl}
, then either
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\times}}
or
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}
. Now, if
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}
then the centralizer of any element in its fibre is isomorphic to the maximal torus
T
; in other words, over
\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}
there is an isomorphism
J|_{\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}}\cong J^{1}|_{\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}}
. Therefore, the only possible difference is over the points of the divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{M^{\times}}=\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\times}}\setminus\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}
. This is the discriminant divisor of the group
M^{\times}
, determined by the following
W
-equivariant function on
T_{M}

\mathrm{Disc}_{M^{\times}}:=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}(1-\alpha).

Let
\mathfrak{D}_{M^{\times}}^{\mathrm{sing}}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{M^{\times}}
denote the singular locus. The restriction of
\bar{T}_{M}\cap M^{\gl}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl}
over the complement of
\mathfrak{D}_{M^{\times}}^{\mathrm{sing}}
is a cameral cover of smooth varieties associated with the root system
\Phi=\Phi(G,T)
, and with simple Galois ramification. Therefore, we can identify the groups
J^{0}_{G,M}
,
J_{G,M}
and
J^{1}_{G,M}
with the groups
J^{0}
,
J
and
J^{1}
introduced in section
2.2
.

3.7.
Hitchin fibres and global Steinberg section

One of the advantages of working over the group-like locus is that the multiplicative Hitchin fibres become simpler. First, note that, for every
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl}
, its fibre in
M
contains a single regular orbit. Indeed, if
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\times}}
, then this is explained above in section
3.3
, while, if
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\rs}
, this follows from the work of J. Chi
[
6
, Lemma 2.2.13]
. Therefore, the map
[M^{\gl}/G]\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl}
is in fact a
J_{G,M}
-gerbe, neutralized by any Steinberg quasi-section
\epsilon^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}
, for any choice of Coxeter datum
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\gl}\subset\mathcal{A}_{M}
denote the Zariski open locus whose points
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
map the curve
C
entirely inside
\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl}
.
The above implies that, for every
a\in\mathcal{A}^{\gl}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
, the fibre
\mathcal{M}^{\reg}_{G,M,a}
is a torsor under the Picard stack

\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a}:=\Bun_{J_{G,M,a}/C},

for
J_{G,M,a}=a^{*}J_{G,M}
. Globally, we can consider the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}_{G,M}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\gl}_{M}
and say that the regular part of the multiplicative Hitchin fibration over the group-like locus
h:\mathcal{M}_{G,M}^{\reg}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\gl}
is a
\mathcal{P}_{G,M}
-torsor. Moreover, this torsor can be trivialized through a
global Steinberg section
.

We recall now the construction of the global Steinberg section from J. Chi
[
6
, Section 4.1.5]
and G. Wang
[
39
, Section 2.4.22]
. First, we need the following result
[
39
, Proposition 2.4.21]
.

Proposition 3.4

(G. Wang)
.

For each Coxeter datum
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
, one can define an action
\tau_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}
of
Z
on
M
such that

\epsilon_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{c}_{M}}(z^{c})=\tau_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(z)\circ\epsilon_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})},

where
\tau_{\mathfrak{c}_{M}}
is the natural action of
Z
on
\mathfrak{c}_{M}
, and
c=|Z_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}|
. Moreover, for a fixed
z
, the map
\tau_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(z)
is a composition of translation by
z^{c}
and conjugation by an element of
T^{\mathrm{sc}}
determined by a homomorphism
Z\rightarrow T^{\mathrm{sc}}
independent of
z
.

Consider the map
Z\rightarrow T^{\mathrm{sc}}
from the above proposition, and denote by
\psi:Z\rightarrow T
its composition with the natural projection
T^{\mathrm{sc}}\rightarrow T
. Let

\displaystyle\Psi:Z

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\longmapsto\psi(z)z_{T}^{-c},

where
z_{T}
is the projection of
z
from
Z
to
T
. We define the stack
[M/(G\times Z)]_{[c]}
as the pullback of
[M/(G\times Z)]
through the map
BZ\rightarrow BZ
induced by
Z\rightarrow Z
,
z\mapsto z^{c}
. We define
[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]_{[c]}
similarly. The above proposition gives the existence of a quasi-section

\displaystyle[\epsilon_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}]_{[c]}:[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]_{[c]}\longrightarrow[M/(\Psi\times\id)(Z)]_{[c]},

which, by composition, induces a quasi-section

\displaystyle[\epsilon_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}]_{[c]}:[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]_{[c]}\longrightarrow[M/G\times Z]_{[c]}.

Consider then a
Z
-torsor
L
. If we denote

\mathcal{A}_{M,L}=\Map_{L}(C,[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z])=\Map_{C}(C,C\times_{BZ,L}[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]),

then, by definition, there is a canonical map
[\mathrm{ev}]_{L}:\mathcal{A}_{M,L}\times C\rightarrow[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]
lying over the map
C\rightarrow BZ
determined by
L
. Suppose that there exists another
Z
-torsor
L^{\prime}
such that
(L^{\prime})^{\otimes c}=L
. This torsor determines a morphism
[\mathrm{ev}]_{L^{\prime}}:\mathcal{A}_{M,L}\times C\rightarrow[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]_{[c]}
lifting
[\mathrm{ev}]_{L}
. Composing with the quasi-section
[\epsilon_{M}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}]_{[c]}
, we obtain a section

\epsilon_{L^{\prime}}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}:\mathcal{A}_{M,L}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G,M,L}=\Map_{L}(C,[M/G\times Z])

of the natural map
\mathcal{M}_{G,M,L}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{M,L}
. Pulling back by a meromorphic datum
D:C\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z]
with associated
Z
-torsor
L
, we obtain a section

\epsilon_{L^{\prime}}^{(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}:\mathcal{A}_{M,D}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{G,M,D}

of the multiplicative Hitchin map
h_{D}:\mathcal{M}_{G,M,D}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{M,D}
. This is called the
global Steinberg section
associated with the torsor
L^{\prime}
and with the Coxeter datum
(\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
.

We conclude the following.

Theorem 3.5
.

The stack
\mathcal{M}^{\reg}_{G,M}
is isomorphic to the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}_{G,M}
over the Zariski open locus
\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\gl}\subset\mathcal{A}_{M}
.

3.8.
Cameral curve

We want to describe the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a}=\Bun_{J_{G,M,a}/C}
, for
a\in\mathcal{A}^{\gl}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
. We do so in terms of the cameral curve
\pi_{a}:\tilde{C}_{a}\rightarrow C
, obtained as a pullback of the cameral cover
\pi_{M}:\bar{T}_{M}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M}
through the map
a:C\rightarrow[\mathfrak{c}_{M}/Z]
.

Consider the Zariski open locus
\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\sharp}\subset\mathcal{A}_{M}

[
39
, Section 6.3.12]
whose points
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
satisfy the following two conditions:

(1)

the image
a(C)
of
C
is contained entirely in
\mathfrak{c}_{M}^{\gl}
(that is,
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\gl}
), and

(2)

the image
a(C)
intersects transversely the discriminant divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{M}
.

The first condition implies that the cameral cover is indeed a cameral cover associated with the root system
\Phi(G,T)
and with simple Galois ramification. The second condition implies that the cameral curve
\tilde{C}_{a}
is smooth.

A direct consequence of this is that, for
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\sharp}(\mathbb{C})
, there is a natural map of Picard stacks
\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a}\rightarrow\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}_{a}}
, which provides a description of
\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a}
in terms of
W
-equivariant
T
-bundles over the cameral curve
\tilde{C}_{a}
. In particular, this implies that
\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a}
is a Beilinson
1
-motive.

We can make sure that
\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\sharp}(\mathbb{C})
is non-empty under some conditions on the meromorphic datum
D
.
Let
L
be the
Z
-torsor associated with the meromorphic datum
D
. Since the monoid
M
can be obtained as a pullback from the Vinberg monoid, there is a natural map
Z\rightarrow T^{\mathrm{sc}}
and, for every dominant weight
\omega
of
G
, we obtain a character
\omega_{Z}\in X^{*}(Z)
. This character determines a line bundle
\omega(L):=L\times^{Z,\omega_{Z}}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
over
C
. We say that
L
is
very
G
-ample
if

\deg\omega(L)>2g,

for every dominant weight
\omega
of
G
. We have the following result
[
39
, Proposition 6.3.13]

Proposition 3.6

(G. Wang)
.

If
L
is very
G
-ample, then
\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\sharp}(\mathbb{C})
is non-empty.

3.9.
Duality in the simply-laced case

Suppose now that the group
G
is simply-laced, meaning that all of its roots are of the same length, or, equivalently, that each of the connected components of its Dynkin diagram are of types
\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{D}
or
\mathsf{E}
. Fix a maximal torus
T\subset G
and consider the Langlands dual group
G^{\vee}
, with maximal torus
T^{\vee}
.

Since
G
is assumed to be simply-laced, the map
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\vee}
sending roots to coroots determines an isomorphism of the root system of
G
with the root system of
G^{\vee}
. In turn, this implies that there are natural isomorphisms
G^{\mathrm{sc}}\cong(G^{\vee})^{\mathrm{sc}}
and
G^{\mathrm{ad}}\cong(G^{\vee})^{\mathrm{ad}}
. Therefore, we can also consider the adjoint action of
G^{\vee}
on the reductive monoid
M
, and there are natural isomorphisms

\mathfrak{c}_{M}=M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{Ad}(G)\cong M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{Ad}(G^{\vee}).

Thus, we can identify
\mathcal{A}_{M}
with
\mathcal{A}_{G^{\vee},M}
. However, in general the space
\mathcal{M}_{G^{\vee},M}
is different from
\mathcal{M}_{G,M}
, although it also fibres over
\mathcal{A}_{M}
. We consider, for every
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
, the corresponding Picard stack
\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a}
acting on the (regular) fibre of
a
.

The cameral curves also depend uniquely on the monoid
M
, and not on the group
G
. Therefore, the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a}
for
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}(\mathbb{C})
can also be described in terms of the same cameral curve
\tilde{C}_{a}
. More precisely, as in the previous section, when
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\sharp}(\mathbb{C})
, there is a natural map of Picard stacks
\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a}\rightarrow\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}_{a}}
, which provides a description of
\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a}
in terms of
W
-equivariant
T^{\vee}
-bundles over
\tilde{C}_{a}
.

Recall that there is a natural isomorphism of Picard stacks
\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}_{a}})\rightarrow\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}_{a}}
. If
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\sharp}(\mathbb{C})
, the natural map
\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a}\rightarrow\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}_{a}}
induces a map
\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}_{a}})\rightarrow\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a})
. On the other hand, the norm map induces a map
\Nm:\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}_{a}}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a}
. Putting this together, we obtain the duality morphism
\mathrm{S}_{a}:\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a})\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a}
, as in the following square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\Bun_{T/\tilde{C}_{a}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bun_{T^{\vee}/\tilde{C}_{a}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Nm}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{S}_{a}}

As an immediate application of Theorem
2.3
we obtain the following. This is Theorem
1.1
in the introduction.

Theorem 3.7
.

For every
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M}^{\sharp}(\mathbb{C})
, the map
\mathbf{S}_{a}:\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a})\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a}
is an isomorphism of Beilinson
1
-motives. In particular, the Fourier-Mukai transform
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a}}
induces an isomorphism of derived categories

\mathbf{S}_{a}:D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}_{G,M,a}))\longrightarrow D^{b}(\QCoh(\mathcal{P}_{G^{\vee},M,a})).

3.10.
An example in low rank:
\SL_{2}
and
\PGL_{2}

We consider now the untwisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration associated with
\SL_{2}
and with the Vinberg monoid
M=\Env(\SL_{2})=\Mat_{2}
, which for
\SL_{2}
is just the monoid
\Mat_{2}
of
2\times 2
matrices, with unit group
\Mat_{2}^{\times}=\GL_{2}
. The center
Z_{M}\cong\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
is just the center of
\GL_{2}
.

The GIT quotient
\SL_{2}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\SL_{2}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, the natural projection being the trace map
\tr:\SL_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
. On the other hand, the abelianization
\mathbb{A}_{M}=\GL_{2}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}(\SL_{2}\times\SL_{2})
is as well isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, with the natural projection being the determinant map
\det:\GL_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
. The
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
action induced on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
from the central
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
action on
\GL_{2}
is the squaring action
z\cdot a=z^{2}a
. We will denote
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{(2)}
to indicate that
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is endowed with the squaring
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
action. A map
D:C\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{1}_{(2)}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]
is determined by the following data

•

a line bundle
L\rightarrow C
,

•

a section
b\in H^{0}(C,L^{2})
.

Therefore, we can identify the stack

\mathcal{M}_{\SL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}=\Map_{(L,b)}(C,[\Mat_{2}/\SL_{2}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}])

with the classifying stack of pairs
(E,\varphi)
with

•

E\rightarrow C
a vector bundle of rank
2
and trivial determinant
\det E\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}O_{C}
,

•

a twisted endomorphism
\varphi:E\rightarrow E\otimes L
with
\det\varphi=b
.

The multiplicative Hitchin base can be identified as

\mathcal{A}_{\Mat_{2},(L,b)}=\Map_{(L,b)}(C,[\mathbb{A}^{1}_{(1)}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{(2)}/\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}])=H^{0}(C,L)\times\left\{b\in H^{0}(C,L^{2})\right\}.

The multiplicative Hitchin fibration in this case is then the map

\displaystyle h=h_{\SL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}:\mathcal{M}_{\SL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\Mat_{2},(L,b)}

\displaystyle(E,\varphi)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\tr(\varphi),\det(\varphi)=b).

Note that in this case we are simply studying a subvariety of the usual Hitchin moduli space for
L
-twisted
\GL_{2}
-Higgs bundles. More precisely, the multiplicative Hitchin base naturally embeds inside the usual Hitchin base
H^{0}(C,L)\times H^{0}(C,L^{2})
, and we are imposing the extra restriction that
\det E
is trivial. The usual Hitchin fibration restricts to the multiplicative Hitchin fibration in this situation. Therefore, the description of the fibres of
h
can be reduced to the well known description of the fibres of the usual Hitchin fibration in terms of spectral curves
[
22
]
. We obtain the following.

Proposition 3.8
.

If
a\in H^{0}(C,L)
and
b\in H^{0}(C,L^{2})
are such that
a^{2}-4b\neq 0
, then the coarse moduli space of the multiplicative Hitchin fibre
h^{-1}(a,b)
is a torsor under the Prym variety

P_{(a,b)}=\mathrm{Prym}(Y_{(a,b)}),

where
\pi:Y_{(a,b)}\rightarrow C
is the spectral curve inside the total space of
L\rightarrow C
determined locally by the equation

y^{2}+ay+b=0.

We now want to make sense of the stack

\mathcal{M}_{\PGL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}=\Map_{(L,b)}(C,[\Mat_{2}/\PGL_{2}\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}]),

and identify the fibres of the
\PGL_{2}
multiplicative Hitchin fibration

\check{h}:\mathcal{M}_{\PGL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\Mat_{2},(L,b)}.

The standard way to do this is to consider the action of the group
\Jac(C)[2]
, parametrizing order
2
line bundles on
C
, on the stack
\mathcal{M}_{\SL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}
and defined as follows

\displaystyle\Jac(C)[2]\times\mathcal{M}_{\SL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\SL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}

\displaystyle(\xi,(E,\varphi))

\displaystyle\longmapsto(E\otimes\xi,\varphi).

The stack
\mathcal{M}_{\PGL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}
is identified with the quotient stack
[\mathcal{M}_{\SL_{2},\Mat_{2},(L,b)}/\Jac(C)[2]]
.

Moreover, the spectral correspondence allows us to identify the fibres
\check{h}^{-1}(a,b)
as torsors under the abelian varieties

\hat{P}_{(a,b)}=P_{(a,b)}/\pi^{*}\Jac(C)[2].

As explained in appendix
C
, the abelian varieties
P_{(a,b)}
and
\hat{P}_{(a,b)}
are dual.

4.
Twisted invariant theory

4.1.
Folding

Let
G
be a semisimple simply-laced and simply-connected group over
\mathbb{C}
. We also assume here that the Dynkin diagram of
G
does not have any component of type
\mathsf{A}_{2\ell}
, for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, but we lift this assumption in a different section
4.7
.

The symmetry group of the Dynkin diagram of
G
is identified with the group
\Out(G)
of outer classes of automorphisms of
G
. This group is the quotient of
\Aut(G)
by the group of
inner automorphisms
, that is, automorphisms of the form
\mathrm{Ad}_{g}
for
g\in G
. A choice of pinning
(B,T,\left\{u_{\alpha}\right\})
for
G
determines a section
\Out(G)\rightarrow\Aut(G)
. Therefore, if we fix a pinning, every symmetry of the Dynkin diagram of
G
induces a unique automorphism
\theta\in\Aut(G)
. We let
m
denote the order of this automorphism. It is a well-known result that the pairs
(G,\theta)
, for
G
simple, are classified by twisted affine Dynkin diagrams (see Table
1
). More generally, for
G
semisimple, we can also have “trivially induced” automorphisms, which permute several isomorphic simple factors.

The colored nodes of the affine Dynkin diagrams from the list determine a Dynkin diagram of finite type, called the
folded Dynkin diagram
. It determines the
folded root system

\Phi^{\theta}
, which is the image of the root system
\Phi=\Phi(G,T)
under the natural projection
X^{*}(T)\rightarrow X^{*}(T)_{\theta}
. Here,
X^{*}(T)_{\theta}=X^{*}(T)/(1-\theta)
denotes the
coinvariant group
associated with the action of the symmetry group on
X^{*}(T)
. Since
G
is simply-connected
X^{*}(T)
is the weight lattice of
G
, and the coinvariant group
X^{*}(T)_{\theta}
is in fact a lattice. The norm map induces a natural injection
X^{*}(T)_{\theta}\hookrightarrow X^{*}(T)^{\theta}
, which identifies
\Phi^{\theta}
as a root system inside
\mathbb{E}=X^{*}(T)^{\theta}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. More precisely, we have

\Phi^{\theta}=\left\{\Nm(\alpha)=\alpha+\theta(\alpha)+\cdots+\theta^{m-1}(\alpha):\alpha\in\Phi\right\}.

The dual root system of
\Phi^{\theta}
is the
cofolded root system

\Phi_{\theta}
, defined as follows.
Consider the quotient set
\Phi/\theta
. Given any orbit
\xi\in\Phi/\theta
, we define the corresponding root
\beta_{\xi}\in\Phi_{\theta}
as

\beta_{\xi}=\sum_{\alpha\in\xi}\alpha.

Clearly, the element
\beta_{\xi}
is fixed under
\theta
, so there is a natural inclusion
\Phi_{\theta}\subset X^{*}(T)^{\theta}
. Note that we have, for every
\alpha\in\Phi
,

\Nm(\alpha)=\tfrac{m}{\mathrm{card}([\alpha])}\beta_{[\alpha]}.

Therefore,
\Phi^{\theta}
and
\Phi_{\theta}
are indeed dual as root systems inside
\mathbb{E}
.

If
\Delta=\Delta(G,B,T)=\left\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\right\}
is the set of simple roots, then the orbit set
\Delta/\theta=\left\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{\ell}\right\}
determines the simple roots
\Delta_{\theta}=\left\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell}\right\}
by putting
\beta_{i}=\sum_{\alpha\in\xi_{i}}\alpha
. Similarly, if
\left\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\right\}\subset X^{*}(T)
is the set of fundamental weights, with orbit set
\left\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\ell}\right\}
, we obtain a set of fundamental weights
\left\{\varpi_{1},\dots,\varpi_{\ell}\right\}\subset X^{*}(T)^{\theta}
for
\Phi_{\theta}
by putting
\varpi_{i}=\sum_{\omega\in\eta_{i}}\omega
. From here it follows that the root lattice
\mathbf{R}(\Phi_{\theta})
associated with the cofolded root system
\Phi_{\theta}
is the invariant lattice
\mathbf{R}(\Phi)^{\theta}
, where
\mathbf{R}(\Phi)=X_{*}(T)=X^{*}(T^{\mathrm{ad}})
is the root lattice of
\Phi
. On the other hand, the root lattice
\mathbf{R}(\Phi^{\theta})
associated with the folded root system
\Phi^{\theta}
is the coinvariant lattice
\mathbf{R}(\Phi)_{\theta}
and, dually, the weight lattice of
\Phi_{\theta}
is
X^{*}(T)^{\theta}
.

The group
G^{\theta}
determined by the root datum

\Psi^{\theta}=(X^{*}(T)_{\theta},\Phi^{\theta},X_{*}(T)^{\theta},\Phi_{\theta})

is the fixed point subgroup of
G
under
\theta
. The subgroup
T^{\theta}\subset G^{\theta}
is a maximal torus.
On the other hand, the group
G_{\theta}
determined by the root datum

\Psi_{\theta}=(X^{*}(T)^{\theta},\Phi_{\theta},X_{*}(T)_{\theta},\Phi^{\theta})

is called the
coinvariant group
. It has as maximal torus the coinvariant torus
T_{\theta}=T/(1-\theta)(T)
. Note that the character lattice of
G_{\theta}
is the weight lattice of
\Phi_{\theta}
. This means that
G_{\theta}
is simply-connected. Consider now the Langlands dual group
(G^{\theta})^{\vee}
, determined by the root datum

(\Psi^{\theta})^{\vee}=(X_{*}(T)^{\theta},\Phi_{\theta},X^{*}(T)_{\theta},\Phi^{\theta}).

The character lattice of
(G^{\theta})^{\vee}
is the root lattice of
\Phi_{\theta}
. Therefore,
(G^{\theta})^{\vee}
is of adjoint type and in fact
(G^{\theta})^{\vee}=(G_{\theta})^{\mathrm{ad}}
, or equivalently,
((G^{\theta})^{\vee})^{\mathrm{sc}}=G_{\theta}^{\mathrm{sc}}
.

4.2.
Twisted conjugation

Given a pair
(G,\theta)
as above, we can consider the
\theta
-twisted conjugation
action of
G
on itself, by setting

g*x=gx\theta(g)^{-1},

for
g,x\in G
. When
\theta
is the identity, we recover the adjoint action. Equivalently, we can consider the (untwisted) conjugation action of
G
on the exterior component
G\theta
of the non-connected group
\tilde{G}=G\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle
. That is, given
g\in G
and
x\theta\in G\theta
, we have

\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(x\theta)=g(x\theta)g^{-1}=(gx\theta(g)^{-1})\theta=(g*x)\theta.

For each
i=1,\dots,\ell
, consider the highest weight representation
(V_{i},\rho_{i})
of
G_{\theta}
associated with the fundamental weight
\varpi_{i}
, and let
\chi_{i}=\mathrm{tr}(\rho_{i})
. Since
\varpi_{i}
is also a dominant weight of
G
, we can also consider the representation
(V_{\varpi_{i}},\rho_{\varpi_{i}})
of
G
, with highest weight
\varpi_{i}
. Let
\tilde{\rho}_{i}
the lift of this representation to a representation of the non-connected group
\tilde{G}
, and let
\tilde{\chi}_{i}=\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{\rho}_{i})
. The restriction
\tilde{\chi}_{i}|_{T\theta}
can be regarded as a function in
k[T]
via the natural isomorphism
T\rightarrow T\theta
. It is a result of Mohrdieck
[
31
]
that this function factors through
T_{\theta}
and coincides with
\chi_{i}
as an element of
k[T_{\theta}]\subset k[T]
.

We can now state the main result of twisted invariant theory for a group, which relates the GIT quotient
\chi_{G\theta}:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}:=G\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}_{*}G=G\theta\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{Ad}(G)
of a group by its twisted conjugation action, and the GIT quotient of its coinvariant group under the adjoint action. A proof can be found in G. Wang’s paper
[
38
]
, where it is attributed to Mohrdieck
[
31
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Mohrdieck–Wang)
.

There are natural isomorphims

\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}\overset{\sim}{\longleftarrow}T\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}((1-\theta)T\rtimes W^{\theta})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}T_{\theta}/W^{\theta}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}G_{\theta}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{Ad}(G_{\theta})=\mathfrak{c}_{G_{\theta}}.

Moreover, we have

\Spec k[\tilde{\chi}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\chi}_{\ell}]=T^{\theta}\theta/\tilde{W}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}T_{\theta}/W^{\theta}=\Spec k[\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{\ell}],

where
\tilde{W}=[(1-\theta)(T)\cap T^{\theta}]\rtimes W^{\theta}
denotes the
exterior Weyl group
of
(G,\theta)
and
W^{\theta}\subset W
is the fixed point subgroup under the natural action of
\theta
on
W
.

Consider now a very flat reductive monoid
M
with
M^{\mathrm{der}}=G
, and suppose that the automorphism
\theta
of
G
extends to an automorphism
\theta_{M}
of the whole unit group
M^{\times}
. In particular, if
M=\Env(G)
is the Vinberg monoid, we can always extend
\theta
to an automorphism
\theta_{+}\in\Aut(G_{+})
by putting

\displaystyle\theta_{+}:G_{+}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow G_{+}

\displaystyle[g,t]

\displaystyle\longmapsto[\theta(g),\theta(t)].

The
\theta
-twisted conjugation action of
G
on itself extends to the
\theta_{M}
-twisted conjugation action of
M^{\times}
on itself, so in turn we can consider the
\theta
-twisted conjugation action of
G
on
M
. Even though
\theta_{M}
might not extend to an automorphism of the whole monoid
M
, we can at least formally understand the
\theta
-twisted conjugation action as a conjugation action on an “exterior component”
M\theta
. We can then consider the corresponding invariant quotient

\chi_{M\theta}:M\longrightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}:=M\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}_{*}G=M\theta\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\mathrm{Ad}(G).

The following is a result of G. Wang
[
38
]
.

Proposition 4.2

(G. Wang)
.

The inclusion
T_{M}\hookrightarrow M^{\times}
of the maximal torus, induces an isomorphism

\bar{T}_{M}\theta\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}[\mathrm{Ad}(T^{\mathrm{sc}}\rtimes W^{\theta})]\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}.

Moreover, since
M
is very flat, we also have

\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}\times\mathbb{A}_{M}.

In particular, for
M=\Env(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{\Env(G)\theta}:\Env(G)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{\ell}\times\mathbb{A}^{r}

\displaystyle[(g,t)]

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\tilde{\chi}_{1}(g\theta),\dots,\tilde{\chi}_{\ell}(g\theta),\alpha_{1}(t),\dots,\alpha_{r}(t)).

4.3.
Monoids and coinvariant group

The inclusion of the root lattice
\mathbf{R}(\Phi_{\theta})=\mathbf{R}(\Phi)^{\theta}\subset\mathbf{R}(\Phi)
induces a surjection at the level of toric varieties
\mathbb{A}_{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{G_{\theta}}
.
Here, we recall that
\mathbb{A}_{G_{\theta}}
denotes the abelianization of the Vinberg monoid
\Env(G_{\theta})
; that is,
\mathbb{A}_{G_{\theta}}
is the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{\ell}
with the
T_{\theta}
-action

t\cdot\bm{y}=t\cdot(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})=(\beta_{1}(t)\cdot y_{1},\dots,\beta_{\ell}(t)\cdot y_{\ell})=:\bm{\beta}(t)\cdot\bm{y}.

The surjection
\mathbb{A}_{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{G_{\theta}}
is equivariant with respect to the actions of
T
and
T_{\theta}
, respectively, and the natural projection
T\rightarrow T_{\theta}
.

Let
M
be a very flat monoid with derived group
G
. By Vinberg’s classification, there is a map
\mathbb{A}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{G}
such that
M
is obtained from
\Env(G)
as a pullback through this map. Composing with the map
\mathbb{A}_{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{G_{\theta}}
, we obtain a map
\mathbb{A}_{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{G_{\theta}}
. We define
M_{\theta}
as the pullback of
\Env(G_{\theta})
through this map. This
M_{\theta}
is by construction a very flat monoid with derived group
G_{\theta}
and abelianization
\mathbb{A}_{M}
. The construction
M\mapsto M_{\theta}
determines a functor from the category
\mathcal{VF}(G)
of very flat monoids with derived group
G
and excellent maps (i.e. maps obtained as pullbacks of maps of the abelianizations, see
[
39
, Definition 2.3.9]
) to the category
\mathcal{VF}(G_{\theta})
of very flat monoids with derived group
G_{\theta}
and excellent maps. Moreover, this functor is fully faithful since, for any two objects
M
and
N
of
\mathcal{VF}(G)
, we have

\Hom_{\mathcal{VF}(G_{\theta})}(M_{\theta},N_{\theta})=\Hom_{\text{comm. monoids}}(\mathbb{A}_{M},\mathbb{A}_{N})=\Hom_{\mathcal{VF}(G)}(M,N).

Moreover, note that, for every very flat monoid
M
with derived group
G
, we have

\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}\times\mathbb{A}_{M}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{G_{\theta}}\times\mathbb{A}_{M}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{M_{\theta}}.

4.4.
Twisted Steinberg quasi-sections

By assumption, the automorphism
\theta
of
G
preserves a pinning. In particular, this means that
\theta
acts on the simple roots and that, for each simple root
\alpha\in\Delta
, the corresponding
1
-parameter group
u_{\alpha}:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\rightarrow U_{\alpha}
satisfies

\theta(u_{\alpha}(1))=u_{\theta(\alpha)}(1).

A
twisted Coxeter datum

(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})

[
38
]
consists of the following

•

a section
q:\Delta/\theta\rightarrow\Delta
,

•

a bijection
\sigma:\left\{1,\dots,\ell\right\}\rightarrow\Delta/\theta
,

•

a choice
\dot{\bm{s}}=\left\{\dot{s}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Delta\right\}
of a representative
\dot{s}_{\alpha}\in N_{G}(T)
of each simple reflection
s_{\alpha}\in W
which is
\theta
-stable, in the sense that

\theta(\dot{s}_{\alpha})=\dot{s}_{\theta(\alpha)}.

Once
\Delta
is fixed, an element
w\in W\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle
is called a
twisted Coxeter element
if it can be written as
w=w_{q,\sigma}=s_{q\circ\sigma(1)}\dots s_{q\circ\sigma(r)}\theta
, for some
q
and
\sigma
as above. We denote by
\mathrm{TCox}(W,\Delta,\theta)
the set of twisted Coxeter elements.

With a twisted Coxeter datum
(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
we can associate the corresponding
twisted Steinberg quasi-section

\displaystyle\epsilon^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}:\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}\cong\mathbb{A}^{\ell}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow G\theta

\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}u_{q\circ\sigma(i)}(x_{i})\dot{s}_{i}\right)\theta.

As in the untwisted case, the dependence on the Coxeter datum is not too strong. Indeed, G. Wang
[
38
]
proved the following. If
(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
and
(q,\sigma^{\prime},\dot{\bm{s}}^{\prime})
are two different twisted Coxeter data with
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
, then the corresponding sections are conjugate under some
t\in T
. On the other hand, if
\dot{\bm{s}}=\dot{\bm{s}}^{\prime}
, then, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}
such that
x_{i}=x^{\prime}_{j}
if
q\circ\sigma(i)
and
q\circ\sigma^{\prime}(j)
are in the same
\theta
-orbit, the elements
\epsilon^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(x)
and
\epsilon^{(q,\sigma^{\prime},\dot{\bm{s}})}(x^{\prime})
are
G
-conjugate.

Again, if
M
is a reductive monoid with
M^{\mathrm{der}}=G
, we can extend the twisted Steinberg quasi-section to a quasi-section

\displaystyle\epsilon_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}:\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}\cong\mathbb{A}_{M}\times\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M\theta

\displaystyle(a,x)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\delta_{M}(a)\epsilon^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(x)

of the GIT quotient
\chi_{M\theta}:M\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}
. It is clear, already from the untwisted case, that the dependence of this quasi-section on the twisted Coxeter datum is strong.

4.5.
Stabilizers and special loci

Let
M
be a very flat monoid with derived group
G
. Similarly to the untwisted case, we say that an element
x\in M
is
\theta
-regular
if the stabilizer

I_{G,M\theta,x}:=\left\{g\in G:gx\theta(g)^{-1}=x\right\}

has the minimal possible dimension. We say that
x
is
\theta
-semisimple
if its
\theta
-twisted conjugation orbit contains an element of
\bar{T}_{M}
, and we say that it is
\theta
-regular semisimple
if it is both
\theta
-regular and
\theta
-semisimple.
The same notions apply to
\theta
-twisted adjoint orbits and we consider the subsets
M^{\treg}
,
M^{\tSs}
and
M^{\trs}
of
\theta
-regular,
\theta
-semisimple and
\theta
-regular semisimple elements, respectively.

The stabilizers define a group scheme
I_{G,M\theta}\rightarrow M
called the
\theta
-centralizer group scheme
, which restricts to a smooth commutative group scheme over
M^{\treg}
, and as in the untwisted case, the descent argument of Ngô can be adapted to yield the following
[
38
]
.

Proposition 4.3

(G. Wang)
.

There is a unique commutative group scheme
J_{G,M\theta}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{G,M\theta}
with a
G
-equivariant isomorphism

\chi_{G,M\theta}^{*}J_{G,M\theta}|_{M^{\treg}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}I_{G,M\theta}|_{M^{\treg}},

which can be extended to a homomorphism
\chi^{*}_{G,M\theta}J_{G,M\theta}\rightarrow I_{G,M\theta}
. We call this
J_{G,M\theta}
the
regular
\theta
-centralizer
group scheme.

We can repeat the discussion of the untwisted case. G. Wang
[
38
]
, shows that, for every choice of Coxeter datum, the Steinberg quasi-section
\epsilon^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}
maps
\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}
inside the regular locus, and in fact it cuts transversally each regular orbit. Therefore
[(M^{\times})^{\treg}\theta/G]\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M^{\times}\theta}
is a
J_{G,M^{\times}\theta}
-gerbe neutralized by any twised Steinberg quasi-section. However, for some points of
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}
, the fibre can have several regular orbits (whose number is bounded above by the number of twisted Coxeter elements). Thus
[M^{\treg}\theta/G]\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}
is in general a finite union of
J_{G,M\theta}
-gerbes, each of which is neutralized by some twisted Steinberg quasi-section.

Like in the untwisted case, we want to identify two special divisors inside the GIT quotient
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{M_{\theta}}
. The
twisted boundary divisor

\mathfrak{B}_{M\theta}
is analogously defined as the complement of

\mathfrak{c}^{\times}_{M\theta}:=\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}\times\mathbb{A}_{M}^{\times}.

We can also define the
extended twisted discriminant divisor

\mathfrak{D}_{M\theta}
by taking the pullback of the divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{\Env(G_{\theta})}\subset\mathfrak{c}_{\Env(G_{\theta})}
through the natural map
\mathfrak{c}_{M_{\theta}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{\Env(G_{\theta})}
. Recall that the divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{\Env(G_{\theta})}
is the vanishing set of the following
W
-invariant function on
(\bar{T}_{\theta})_{+}
,

\mathrm{Disc}_{\Env(G_{\theta})}:=\prod_{\beta\in(\Phi_{\theta})+}(\beta,0)\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\theta}}(1-(0,\alpha))=(2\rho_{\theta},0)\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\theta}}(1-(0,\alpha)),

where
\rho_{\theta}
is the half-sum of the roots in
\Phi_{\theta}
.
Note that this definition makes sense because, by definition,
\Phi_{\theta}
is the root system of
G_{\theta}
. It follows from the untwisted case that these two divisors intersect properly and in turn that the codimension of
\mathfrak{D}_{M\theta}^{\times}:=\mathfrak{D}_{M\theta}\cap\mathfrak{B}_{M\theta}
is at least
2
. We say that an element of
M
is
\theta
-group-like
if it belongs to the preimage of
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}^{\gl}:=\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}\setminus\mathfrak{D}_{M\theta}^{\times}
, and denote by
M^{\tgl}=\chi_{M\theta}^{-1}(\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}^{\gl})
the locus of
\theta
-group-like elements.
In the twisted case it is also true that
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}^{\trs}
, the image of
M^{\trs}
under the GIT quotient, is the complement of the discriminant divisor
\mathfrak{D}_{M\theta}
, and each fibre of
\chi_{M\theta}
over
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}^{\trs}
consists of a single twisted adjoint orbit. It follows that an element of
M
is
\theta
-group-like if it is either in the unit group or
\theta
-regular semisimple, that is

M^{\tgl}=M^{\times}\cup M^{\trs}.

More generally, the same argument from the untwisted case shows that each fibre of
\chi_{M\theta}
over
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}^{\tgl}
has a single regular orbit, so the map
[M^{\tgl}\theta/G]\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}^{\tgl}
is a
J_{G,M\theta}
-gerbe, neutralized by any twisted Steinberg quasi-section
\epsilon^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}
, for any choice of twisted Coxeter datum
(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
.

4.6.
Galois description

Recall the isomorphisms

\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{M_{\theta}}\cong\bar{T}_{M_{\theta}}/W^{\theta}
. We can thus consider the cameral cover

\pi_{M\theta}:\bar{T}_{M_{\theta}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}.

We introduce the Weil restriction

\Pi_{M\theta}=\pi_{M\theta,*}(\bar{T}_{M_{\theta}}\times T^{\theta}),

on which
W
acts diagonally, and also the invariant subgroup

J^{1}_{M\theta}=\Pi_{M\theta}^{W}

and its neutral connected component

J^{0}_{M\theta}=\Pi_{M\theta}^{W,0}.

As explained in
[
38
]
, a twisted version of the Grothendieck–Springer resolution provides an open embedding
J_{M\theta}\hookrightarrow J^{1}_{M\theta}
, which restricts to an isomorphism over
\mathfrak{c}^{\trs}_{M\theta}
. In fact, the isomorphism extends over the whole base, that is, we have the following.

Proposition 4.4
.

The embedding
J_{M\theta}\hookrightarrow J^{1}_{M\theta}
is an isomorphism.

Sketch of the proof.

For the sake of clarity, we explain the main argument of the proof of the proposition. First of all note that, since the complement of the group-like locus
\mathfrak{c}^{\tgl}_{M\theta}
has codimension at least
2
, it suffices to show the isomorphism for
a
in this locus. Actually, we can further restrict to the complement
\mathfrak{c}^{\tgl}_{M\theta}\setminus\mathfrak{D}_{G_{\theta}}^{\mathrm{sing}}
, since the singular locus of the discriminant divisor also has codimension at least
2
.
For such an
a
, either
a
is
\theta
-regular semisimple, and there is nothing to prove, or
a\in\mathfrak{D}_{G_{\theta}}\setminus\mathfrak{D}_{G_{\theta}}^{\mathrm{sing}}
.

Suppose then that
a\in\mathfrak{D}_{G_{\theta}}\setminus\mathfrak{D}_{G_{\theta}}^{\mathrm{sing}}
. In this case we can find an element
t\in T_{\theta}
mapping to
a\in\mathfrak{c}_{G_{\theta}}\cong T_{\theta}/W^{\theta}
, and such that there exists a unique root
\beta\in\Phi_{\theta}
with
t^{\beta}=1
.
By construction of the cofolded root system,
\beta=\beta_{\xi}
, where
\xi\in\Phi/\theta
is the an orbit of roots in
\Phi
. We let now
G_{\beta}\subset G
be the reductive
\theta
-stable subgroup generated by the maximal torus
T
and by the root groups corresponding to the roots in
\xi
.

Consider the quotient
\mathfrak{c}_{\beta}:=T_{\theta}/s_{\beta}
, with the projection
\pi_{\beta}:T_{\theta}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{\beta}
. We let
I_{\beta}\rightarrow G_{\beta}
denote the group scheme of stabilizers of the
\theta
-twisted action of
G_{\beta}
on itself and
J_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{\beta}
the corresponding regular
\theta
-centralizer. We can also consider
J^{1}_{\beta}=[\pi_{\beta,*}(T_{\theta}\times T^{\theta})]^{s_{\beta}}
.

If we let
\bar{a}\in\mathfrak{c}_{\beta}
be the image of
t
under the natural projection, it is clear that the natural finite flat map
p_{\beta}:\mathfrak{c}_{\beta}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{G\theta}
sends
a
to
\bar{a}
and is étale at
a
. This means that, near
a
, the canonical map
p_{\beta}^{*}J_{M\theta}\rightarrow J_{\beta}
is an isomorphism, and the same is true for
p_{\beta}^{*}J^{1}_{M\theta}\rightarrow J^{1}_{\beta}
. Therefore, we are reduced to show that the embedding
J_{\beta}\hookrightarrow J^{1}_{\beta}
is an isomorphism.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\beta
is a simple root, and thus the orbit
\xi
corresponds to an orbit of nodes in the Dynkin diagram of
G
. From our assumptions on
G
(that is, since
G
does not have factors of type
\mathsf{A}_{2\ell}
), the nodes in
\xi
are disjoint. Therefore,
G_{\beta}
is obtained up to isogeny as a product of isomorphic reductive groups of rank
1
with some tori. The automorphism
\theta
acts on
G_{\beta}
by permuting these factors and by inversion in some of the tori. Every reductive group of rank
1
is isomorphic to either
\GL_{2}
,
\SL_{2}
or
\PGL_{2}
. The isomorphism of
J_{\beta}
and
J^{1}_{\beta}
can then be verified by direct computation on a case by case study. We refer to
[
38
]
for more details.
∎

Recall that, when restricted to the group-like locus, the cameral cover
\pi_{M_{\theta}}:\bar{T}_{M_{\theta}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M_{\theta}}
is a cameral cover associated with the root system
\Phi_{\theta}
. The above shows that
J_{M\theta}=J_{\pi_{M\theta},G^{\theta}}
. Again, this makes sense because
\Phi_{\theta}
is the coroot system of
G^{\theta}
.

4.7.
The special case
\mathsf{A}^{(2)}_{2\ell}

We return now to the case excluded at the beginning of section
4.1
. Assume then for simplicity that
G=\SL_{2\ell+1}
, for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the standard pinning of
G
; that is,
T
consists of diagonal matrices,
B
of upper triangular ones, the simple roots are

\alpha_{i}(\mathrm{diag}(x_{1},\dots,x_{2\ell}))=x_{i}x_{i+1}^{-1},

and the root groups are parametrized by

u_{i}(x)=I_{2n}+xE_{i,i+1},

for
E_{i,i+1}
the matrix with
1
on the position
(i,i+1)
and
0
elsewhere.

The only non-trivial element of
\Out(\SL_{2\ell+1})
is the equivalence class of the automorphism

A\mapsto(A^{T})^{-1}.

This automorphism does not preserve the standard pinning, but the following does

\theta_{\ell}:A\mapsto N_{2\ell+1}(A^{T})^{-1}N_{2\ell+1}^{-1},

where
N_{2\ell+1}
is the antidiagonal matrix
N_{2\ell+1}=\mathrm{antidiag}(1,-1,\dots,-1,1)
.

Twisted conjugation and Proposition
4.3
can also be formulated for this situation, since they do not use the assumption of
G
not having components of type
\mathsf{A}_{2\ell}
(see
[
38
]
). The main difference is that the embedding
J_{M\theta}\hookrightarrow J^{1}_{M\theta}
from Proposition
4.4
is no longer an isomorphism. Indeed, if we try to emulate the proof of Proposition
4.4
without the assumption, we are led to consider the extra case in which the orbit
\xi
consists of two nodes of the Dynkin diagram joined by an edge. Therefore,
G_{\beta}
is a reductive group with semisimple part isomorphic to
\SL_{3}
, on which
\theta
acts as

\theta|_{G_{\beta}^{\mathrm{der}}}=\theta_{1}:A\mapsto N_{3}(A^{T})^{-1}N_{3}^{-1},

for
N_{3}=\mathrm{antidiag}(1,-1,1)
.
We are thus reduced to study the pair
(\SL_{3},\theta_{1})
.

The action of
\theta=\theta_{1}
on the maximal torus of
\SL_{3}
sends
\mathrm{diag}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
to the element
\mathrm{diag}(x_{3}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1},x_{1}^{-1})
. Therefore, the fixed point torus
T^{\theta}
is

T^{\theta}=\left\{X=\mathrm{diag}(x_{1},1,x_{1}^{-1}):x_{1}\in\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\right\}

This determines an isomorphism
T^{\theta}\cong\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
We can write the coordinate ring
k[T]=k[x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2}^{\pm 1}]
. The action of
\theta
on this ring permutes
x_{1}
with
x_{1}x_{2}
so, writing
z=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}
we have
k[T_{\theta}]=k[z^{\pm 1}]
, which determines an isomorphism
T_{\theta}\cong\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
.

The only non-trivial fixed element of the Weyl group is represented by the matrix
N_{3}
, whose conjugation action on
T
permutes
x_{1}
and
x_{3}
. Thus,
W^{\theta}
acts on
T^{\theta}\cong\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
and on
T_{\theta}\cong\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
as the inversion
z\mapsto z^{-1}
on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
. The quotient
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}/(z\mapsto z^{-1})
is isomorphic to the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, with the projection map

\displaystyle\pi:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1},\ z\longmapsto a=z+z^{-1}.

This is a finite cover ramified at the points
a=2
and
a=-2
. Therefore, the fibres
J^{1}_{a}
of the group scheme

J^{1}=\pi_{*}(T_{\theta}\times T^{\theta})^{W^{\theta}}

are isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
for
a\neq\pm 2
and to
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\times\mu_{2}
for
a=2
or
a=-2
.

The GIT quotient
\SL_{3}\theta\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\SL_{3}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{1}
and we can consider the regular
\theta
-centralizer
J\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
, which as usual is defined as the descent over the quotient
\mathbb{A}^{1}
of the group scheme of stabilizers of the
\theta
-twisted conjugation action on
\SL_{3}
. There is an open and closed embedding
J\hookrightarrow J^{1}
which restricts to an isomorphism over
\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\left\{\pm 2\right\}
. Therefore,
J\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
must be isomorphic to one of the following

(1)

the whole group
J^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
,

(2)

the fiberwise neutral connected component
J^{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
,

(3)

the group
J_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
, with one connected component over
a=2
and two connected components over
a=-2
,

(4)

the group
J_{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}
, with one connected component over
a=-2
and two connected components over
a=2
.

An explicit computation by G. Wang
[
38
]
shows that
J
is in fact isomorphic to
J_{-}
.

For general rank
(\SL_{2\ell+1},\theta=\theta_{\ell})
, we can define the cofolded root system by putting

\beta_{\left\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{2\ell+1-i}\right\}}=\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{2\ell+1-i},

for
i\neq\ell
, and

\beta_{\left\{\alpha_{\ell},\alpha_{\ell+1}\right\}}=2(\alpha_{\ell}+\alpha_{\ell+1}),

which determines a root system of type
\mathsf{C}_{\ell}
. The Weyl group of
\mathsf{C}_{\ell}
is generated by
\ell
simple reflections
s_{1},\dots,s_{\ell}
. The first
\ell-1
of these reflections
s_{i}
act on
T_{\theta}\cong\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\ell}
by permuting the
i
-th and
(i+1)
-th coordinates. The last reflection
s_{l}
sends the last coordinate
z_{\ell}
to its inverse
z_{\ell}^{-1}
. The subset of fixed points in
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\ell}
under
s_{l}
projects on
T_{\theta}/W(\mathsf{C}_{\ell})\cong\mathbb{A}^{\ell}
to the pair of hyperplanes

H_{\pm}=\left\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})\in\mathbb{A}^{\ell}:a_{\ell}=\pm 2\right\}.

The GIT quotient
\SL_{2\ell+1}\theta\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}\SL_{2\ell+1}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{\ell}
, and it follows from our discussion for
\SL_{3}
that
J^{1}
has two fibrewise connected components over
H_{\pm}
and one elsewhere, and that the regular centralizer
J\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{\ell}
is isomorphic to the subgroup
J_{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{\ell}
of
J^{1}
with one connnected component over
a_{\ell}=-2
and two connected components over
a_{\ell}=2
.

Note that the “dual” of the group scheme
J_{-}
, in the sense of Remark
1
at the end of section
2.4
is the group scheme
\check{J}_{+}
, for
\check{J}=\pi_{*}(T_{\theta}\times(T^{\theta})^{\vee})^{W^{\theta}}
.
There is a way of obtaining
\check{J}_{+}
as the regular centralizer of something, which is consistent with the approach of Tachikawa
[
36
]
. We can introduce an automorphism
\vartheta=\vartheta_{\ell}
of
\SL_{2\ell+1}
which plays the role of a “dual” for
\theta_{\ell}
. This automorphism
\vartheta
is the “standard” automorphism of order
4
introduced by Kac in his book
[
24
]
(see also the paper of Besson and Hong
[
1
]
), which is almost compatible the standard pinning, but sends the root group parametrization
u_{\ell}(x)
to
u_{\ell+1}(-ix)
, for

a square root of
-1
. More precisely, we define
\vartheta_{\ell}
as

\vartheta_{\ell}=\mathrm{Ad}_{R_{\ell}}\circ\theta_{\ell},

where
R_{\ell}
is the diagonal matrix
R_{\ell}=\mathrm{diag}(1,i,-i,\dots,i,-i)
. The fixed points of
\vartheta_{\ell}
form a group whose neutral connected component is isomorphic to the symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}_{2\ell}
. Therefore, the tori
T^{\theta}
and
T^{\vartheta}
are in natural duality. When we restrict to
\ell=1
, a direct computation for
\SL_{3}
, similar to the one in
[
39
]
shows that the regular
\vartheta
-centralizer

coincides with
\check{J}_{+}
. The same arguments as in Proposition
4.4
allow us to give an isomorphism

for general
\ell
.

5.
Twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibrations

5.1.
Construction

Let
C
be a smooth complex projective curve. A
twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration
over
C
is associated with the following data:

•

A semisimple simply-connected and simply-laced group
G
of rank
r
over
\mathbb{C}
, with a prescribed pinning
(B,T,\left\{u_{\alpha}\right\})
.

•

An automorphism
\theta
of
G
of order
m
preserving the prescribed pinning.

•

A reductive monoid
M
with derived group
G
.

Remark 8
.

A slight difference with the untwisted case is that we are imposing the group
G
to be simply-connected, and thus it is determined by the monoid
M
, since
G=M^{\mathrm{der}}
. In the untwisted case, we assume that
M^{\mathrm{der}}
is simply-connected, but the group
G
acting on
M
through the adjoint action can be
M^{\mathrm{der}}
or any other semisimple group centrally isogenous to it. Possibly, the restriction on
G
being simply-connected that we are imposing in the twisted case can be lifted as well, and one can consider the twisted action on
G
of other isogenous groups, mimicking the untwisted situation. We do not pursue this direction here.

The
\theta
-twisted conjugation action of
G
on itself extends to an action of
G
on the reductive monoid
M
. As in the untwisted case, the natural left multiplication action of
Z=Z^{0}_{M^{\times}}
on
M
commutes with the action of
G
. Therefore, we can consider the following sequence of stacky quotients
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[M\theta/(G\times Z)\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}/Z\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BZ.}

Let
D
be a map
C\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z]
, and consider the mapping stacks

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{M\theta}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{M}_{M\theta,D}:=\Map_{D}(C,\left[M\theta/(G\times Z)\right])=\Map_{C}(C,C\times_{[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z],D}\left[M\theta/(G\times Z)\right]),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{M\theta}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{A}_{M\theta,D}=\Map_{D}(C,\left[\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}/Z\right])=\Map_{C}(C,C\times_{[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z],D}\left[\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}/Z\right]).

Note that we have a natural isomorphism
\mathcal{A}_{M\theta}\cong\mathcal{A}_{M_{\theta}}
, induced by the isomorphism
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}\cong\mathfrak{c}_{M_{\theta}}
.

Definition 5.1
.

The (twisted)
multiplicative Hitchin fibration
associated with the data
(G,\theta,M)
and with meromorphic datum
D
is the natural map

\displaystyle h_{M\theta}:=h_{M\theta,D}:\mathcal{M}_{M\theta,D}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{M\theta,D},

induced from the map
[M\theta/(G\times Z)]\rightarrow[\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}/Z]
.

Remark 9
.

The twisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration can also be interpreted in terms of “twisted” multiplicative Higgs bundles. By this we mean a pair
(E,\varphi)
formed by a
G
-bundle
E\rightarrow C
and a rational section
\varphi
of the bundle of groups
E_{\theta}(G)=E\times_{\mathrm{Ad}}G\theta
associated with the
\theta
-twisted conjugation action of
G
on itself.
The section
\varphi
is rational, so it has singularities at some points
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\in C
. Let
p_{i}
be one of this points and consider a local coordinate
z
near
p_{i}
. As in the untwisted situation, by Iwahori’s theorem, the singularity of
\varphi
is determined by
\lambda_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in X_{*}(T)_{+}
is a dominant cocharacter.

5.2.
Global twisted Steinberg section

In order to construct an equivariant Steinberg section, G. Wang
[
38
]
gives a twisted analogue of Proposition
3.4
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For each twisted Coxeter datum
(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
, one can define an action
\tau_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}
of
Z
on
M\theta
such that

\epsilon_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{c}_{M}\theta}(z^{c_{\theta}})=\tau_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(z)\circ\epsilon_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})},

where
\tau_{\mathfrak{c}_{M}}
is the natural action of
Z
on
\mathfrak{c}_{M}
, and
c_{\theta}=|Z_{G}^{\theta}|
. Moreover, for a fixed
z
, the map
\tau_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}(z)
is a composition of translation by
z^{c_{\theta}}
and conjugation by an element of
T
determined by a homomorphism
Z\rightarrow T
independent of
z
.

Fix a twisted Coxeter datum
(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})
and let
T_{0}\subset T
denote the common kernel of all the roots
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}
in
q(\Delta/\theta)
. Here, we are reordering the simple roots
\Delta=\left\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\right\}
in such a way that
\alpha_{i}=q(\sigma(i))
. Consider the fundamental weights
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}
and recall that we have the corresponding weights

\varpi_{i}=\Nm_{\theta}(\omega_{i}),\ \ i=1,\dots,\ell

of
G_{\theta}
. There is a lattice homomorphism

\displaystyle-\theta^{-1}+\Nm_{\theta}:X^{*}(T)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow X^{*}(T)

\displaystyle\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\omega_{i}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mu_{0}:=-\theta^{-1}(\mu)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}n_{i}\varpi_{i},

whose kernel contains the fixed point sublattice
X^{*}(T)^{\theta}\subset X^{*}(T)
. The image of the map
(1-\theta):T_{0}\rightarrow T
coincides with
(1-\theta)(T)
, since
T_{0}
and
T^{\theta}
generate
T
. Since
T_{0}\cap T^{\theta}\subset Z_{G}^{\theta}
, the kernel of the induced map
(1-\theta)^{*}:X^{*}(T)\rightarrow X^{*}(T_{0})
is exactly
X^{*}(T)^{\theta}
, and the image has finite index divisible by
c_{\theta}
. We conclude that there is a unique arrow
X^{*}(T_{0})\rightarrow X^{*}(T)
making the following square commute
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{*}(T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{*}(T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{*}(T_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{*}(T).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu\mapsto\mu_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(1-\theta)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu\mapsto c_{\theta}\mu}

This induces a homomorphism
T\rightarrow T_{0}
,
t\mapsto t_{0}
such that

\mu_{0}(t^{c_{\theta}})=\mu((1-\theta)(t_{0}));

and also
\alpha_{i}(t_{0})=1
, for

, by definition.

Consider the map
Z\rightarrow T
from Proposition
5.2
above, and let

\displaystyle\Psi:Z

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\longmapsto\psi(z)z_{T}^{-c_{\theta}}(z_{T})_{0}.

We obtain a quasi-section

[\epsilon_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}]_{[c_{\theta}]}:[\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}/Z]_{[c_{\theta}]}\longrightarrow[M\theta/\Psi(Z)\times Z]_{[c_{\theta}]}

which, by composition, induces a quasi-section

[\epsilon_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}]_{[c_{\theta}]}:[\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}/Z]_{[c_{\theta}]}\longrightarrow[M\theta/G\times Z]_{[c_{\theta}]}.

Consider then a
Z
-torsor
L
and suppose that there exists another
Z
-torsor

such that
(L^{\prime})^{\otimes c_{\theta}}=L
. Like in the untwisted case, this torsor determines a morphism
[\mathrm{ev}]_{L^{\prime}}:\mathcal{A}_{M\theta,L}\times C\rightarrow[\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}/Z]_{[c_{\theta}]}
lifting the canonical map

. Composing with
[\epsilon_{M\theta}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}]_{[c_{\theta}]}
, we obtain a section

. Pulling back by a meromorphic datum

, we obtain a section

\epsilon_{L^{\prime}}^{(q,\sigma,\dot{\bm{s}})}:\mathcal{A}_{M\theta,D}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{M\theta,D}

of the twisted multiplicative Hitchin map

. This is the
global twisted Steinberg section
associated with

and

We conclude the following.

Theorem 5.3
.

Over the Zariski open locus
\mathcal{A}_{M\theta}^{\gl}\subset\mathcal{A}_{M\theta}
of sections mapping

entirely inside
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}^{\tgl}
, the stack
\mathcal{M}_{M\theta}^{\reg}
of sections factorizing through the

is isomorphic to the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}_{M\theta}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\gl}_{M\theta}
with fibres

\mathcal{P}_{M\theta,a}=\Bun_{J_{M\theta,a}/C},

for
J_{M\theta,a}=a^{*}J_{M\theta}
.

5.3.
Cameral description

Given a point
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M\theta}(\mathbb{C})
, we want to give a Galois description of
\mathcal{P}_{M\theta,a}
in terms of the cameral curve
\pi_{a}:\tilde{C}_{a}\rightarrow C
obtained as a pullback of the cameral cover
\pi_{M\theta}=\pi_{M_{\theta}}:\bar{T}_{M_{\theta}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}
. Recall from section
3.8
that we had the Zariski open locus
\mathcal{A}_{M_{\theta}}^{\sharp}
of sections that map
C
inside the group-like locus, and intersecting transversely the discriminant divisor. Moreover, this locus is non-empty if the
Z
-torsor
L
associated with the meromorphic datum

is very
G_{\theta}
-ample.

Recall from Proposition
4.4
that
J_{M\theta}
is isomorphic to
J^{1}_{M\theta}=\pi_{M\theta,*}(\bar{T}_{M_{\theta}}\times T^{\theta})^{W^{\theta}}
. Therefore, we have an isomorphism

\mathcal{P}_{M\theta,a}\cong\mathcal{P}^{1}_{M\theta,a}:=\Bun_{J^{1}_{M\theta,a}/C},

for
J^{1}_{M\theta,a}=a^{*}J^{1}_{M\theta}=\pi_{a,*}(\tilde{C}_{a}\times T^{\theta})^{W^{\theta}}
.
A direct consequence of this cameral description is that there is a natural map of Picard stacks
\mathcal{P}_{M\theta,a}\rightarrow\Bun_{T^{\theta}/\tilde{C}_{a}}
, which provides a description of
\mathcal{P}_{M\theta,a}
in terms of
W^{\theta}
-equivariant

-bundles over the cameral curve

. In particular, this implies that
\mathcal{P}_{M\theta,a}
is a Beilinson

-motive.

5.4.
Duality: twisted vs untwisted

Assume for now that the group
G
does not have a factor of type
\mathsf{A}_{2\ell}
.
As we note in the previous sections, the GIT quotients
\mathfrak{c}_{M\theta}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{M_{\theta}}
are isomorphic, and the
\theta
-regular centralizer admits a Galois description in terms of the cameral cover
\pi_{M\theta}:\bar{T}_{M_{\theta}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{c}_{M_{\theta}}
, which is also the cameral cover for the adjoint action of any semisimple group centrally isogenous to the coinvariant group
G_{\theta}
on the monoid
M_{\theta}
.

Let
H=G^{\theta}
be the fixed point subgroup of
G
under the
\theta
-action. Let us denote by
A=T^{\theta}
its maximal torus. As we explain in
4.1
, its Langlands dual group
H^{\vee}=(G^{\theta})^{\vee}
is of adjoint type, and in fact
H^{\vee}=(G_{\theta})^{\mathrm{ad}}
. Therefore, we can consider the adjoint action of
H^{\vee}
on the monoid
M_{\theta}
, and the corresponding untwisted multiplicative Hitchin fibration

h_{H^{\vee},M_{\theta}}:\mathcal{M}_{H^{\vee},M_{\theta}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{M_{\theta}}\cong\mathcal{A}_{M\theta},

with the same meromorphic datum
D:C\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}_{M}/Z]
. For every
a\in\mathcal{A}_{M_{\theta}}(\mathbb{C})
, we can consider the Picard stack
\mathcal{P}_{H^{\vee},M_{\theta},a}
acting on the regular fibre of
a
. Recall that if
a\in\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}_{M_{\theta}}(\mathbb{C})
, then there is a natural map of Picard stacks
\mathcal{P}_{H^{\vee},M_{\theta},a}\rightarrow\Bun_{A^{\vee}/\tilde{C}_{a}}
, which provides a description of

The Hitchin equations were originally obtained by Hitchin
[
20
]
when he studied a
2
-dimensional reduction of the self-dual Yang-Mills equations on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Since the resulting equations are conformally invariant, they can be considered over any Riemann surface. On the other hand, multiplicative nonabelian Hodge theory arises by instead studying a
3
-dimensional reduction of the Yang-Mills equations. The Yang-Mills equations reduced to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
become the
Bogomolny equations

F_{A}-\star d_{A}\Phi=0,

for a
K
-bundle
E
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, a
K
-connection
A
on
E
, and a section
\Phi\in\Omega^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathrm{ad}(E))
. The solutions
(E,A,\Phi)
to the Bogomolny equations are called (
K
-)
monopoles
.

We want to consider monopoles that are either
periodic
(
rational
case),
doubly-periodic
(
trigonometric
case) or
triply-periodic
(
elliptic
case). The corresponding moduli spaces of monopoles will be denoted by
\mathbf{M}^{1}_{\mathrm{Mon}}(G)
, for periodic monopoles,
\mathbf{M}^{2}_{\mathrm{Mon}}(G)
, for doubly-periodic monopoles, and
\mathbf{M}^{3}_{\mathrm{Mon}}(G)
, for triply-periodic ones. Mochizuki showed in a series of papers
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
that each of these moduli spaces
\mathbf{M}^{i}_{\mathrm{Mon}}(G)
admits a
1
-parameter family of complex structures, corresponding to
difference modules
.

If
X
is a complex analytic space and
\tau
is an automorphism of
X
, a
\tau
-difference module is a module for the sheaf
\mathcal{D}_{\tau}=O_{X}[\Psi,\Psi^{-1}]
of non-commutative rings generated by
O_{X}
and an invertible generator
\Psi
with the relation
\Psi f=\tau^{*}(f)\Psi
. We denote by
\Diff_{\tau}(X)
the category of quasi-coherent
\tau
-difference modules on
X
. We can also consider
G
-versions of (locally-free) difference modules. These are
difference connections
. A
\tau
-difference
G
-connection consists of a
G
-bundle
E\rightarrow X
and an isomorphism of
G
-bundles
E|_{X\setminus D}\rightarrow\tau^{*}E|_{X\setminus D}
over the complement of some divisor
D\subset X
.

More precisely, we let
C
be one of the following Riemann surfaces, endowed with automorphisms
\tau:C\rightarrow C
:

•

C=\mathbb{C}
in the rational case,
\tau_{\lambda}(z)=z+\lambda
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,

•

C=\mathbb{C}^{*}
in the trigonometric case,
\tau_{q}(z)=qz
,
q\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,

•

C=E_{\Lambda}=\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
, an elliptic curve, with lattice
\Lambda
, in the elliptic case,
\tau_{a}([z])=[z+a]
,

.

A
\tau
-difference module on
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{C}^{*}
or

, for
\tau
one of the automorphisms above, is called a rational, trigonometric or elliptic difference module, respectively. Similarly, we define rational, trigonometric or elliptic difference

-connections. We denote their corresponding moduli spaces (with the appropriate notion of stability) by
\mathbf{M}^{1}_{\Diff,\tau}(G)
,
\mathbf{M}^{2}_{\Diff,\tau}(G)
and
\mathbf{M}^{3}_{\Diff,\tau}(G)
, respectively. For each
\tau
, the complex structure over
\mathbf{M}^{i}_{\mathrm{Mon}}(G)
in Mochizuki’s family identifies it with
\mathbf{M}^{i}_{\Diff,\tau}(G)
.
Note that for
\tau=\id
, a
\tau
-difference

-connection on

is just a multiplicative

-Higgs bundle. We denote

Remark 11

Throughout all the discussion we are dismissing the existence of meromorphic data. These can be easily incorporated in the story. At the level of monopoles we need to consider

can be interpreted as a moduli space associated with the stacks appearing in the multiplicative Hitchin fibrations of section

Remark 12

The above discussion also ignores twisting by a diagram automorphism of

\mathbf{M}^{i}_{\mathrm{Mon}}(G)
under a combined action of the cyclic group

on

Remark 13
.

In order to relate the discussion of this appendix with the results of the paper we are understanding the Riemann surface

A.2.
Derived geometry point of view

To establish their conjectures, Elliott and Pestun
[
12
]
use the language of
derived algebraic geometry
; we refer to Remark 1.10 in their paper for a compilation of relevant references. In this section, we use this language very roughly, and avoiding many technicalities, just with the aim of giving an intuitive explanation of the program.

In particular they consider the “Betti circle”
S^{1}_{B}
, which is a derived stack corresponding to the homotopy type of the circle. If
X
is a complex analytic space and
\tau
is an automorphism of
X
, we can consider the corresponding “mapping torus”

X_{\tau}=X\times_{\tau}S^{1}_{B}.

This is constructed as a derived fibre product
X_{\tau}=X\times_{X\times X}X
, where the two maps
X\rightarrow X\times X
are the diagonal and the
\tau
-twisted diagonal
x\mapsto(x,\tau(x))
. The derived stack of
\tau
-difference
G
-connections on
G
can then be recovered as the mapping stack

\Conn_{G}^{\tau}(X)=\Map(X_{\tau},BG).

In particular, the derived stack of multiplicative
G
-Higgs bundles is the mapping stack

\bmHiggs_{G}(X)=\Map(X\times S^{1}_{B},BG)=\Map(X,G/G).

We can also consider the derived category of
\tau
-difference modules, which can be interpreted as

\dDiff_{\tau}(X)=\QC(X_{\tau}),

where
\QC
denotes the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves.

Remark 14
.

Again, we are dismissing the presence of singularities. If we do take them into account then
\bmHiggs
can be thought of as (a derived version of) the stacks
\mathcal{M}
that we study in this paper.

We can also consider twists by outer automorphisms in the derived picture. Let
\theta\in\Aut(G)
be a diagram automorphism, and consider the natural action of the cyclic group
\langle\theta\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/m
on the circle by rotating with angle
2\pi/m
. The derived stack of
\theta
-twisted multiplicative
G
-Higgs bundles is recovered as the “fixed points”

\bmHiggs_{G,\theta}(X)=\bmHiggs_{G}(X)^{\theta}=\Map(X\times S^{1}_{B},BG)^{\theta}=\Map(X,G\theta/G).

A.3.
S-duality for 5D theories twisted on a circle

Elliott and Pestun explain that the stack
\bmHiggs(C,G)
, where
C
is a Calabi-Yau Riemann surface and
G
is a complex semisimple simply-laced group, arises from a super Yang-Mills theory in 5 dimensions. More precisely, they construct some physical supersymmetric gauge theory such that its “space of solutions” can be interpreted as the shifted cotangent complex
T^{*}[1]\bmHiggs(C,G)
. The basis for their argument is based on a dimensional reduction from a 6-dimensional theory.

A little bit more precisely. This 6-dimensional theory is defined on a compact Calabi-Yau
3
-fold
X_{6}
, and has as space of solutions the shifted cotangent complex of the stack

\mathbf{T}_{6}(X_{6})=\mathbf{Bun}_{G}(X_{6})=\Map(X_{6},BG).

Consider a
5
-dimensional manifold
X_{5}
with automorphism
\tau
such that the mapping torus
X_{5}\times_{\tau}S_{R}^{1}
is Calabi-Yau, for
S^{1}_{R}
a circle of radius
R
. We obtain a
5
-dimensional theory by evaluating

\mathbf{T}_{5}^{\tau}(X_{5})=\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}\mathbf{T}_{6}(X_{5}\times_{\tau}S_{R}^{1})=\Map(X_{5},BG)\times_{\tau^{*}}S^{1}_{B}=\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X_{5})_{\tau^{*}}.

Therefore, we can identify

\bmHiggs_{G}(C)_{\tau^{*}}=\Map(C\times S^{1}_{B},BG)\times_{\tau^{*}}S^{1}_{B}=\mathbf{T}^{\tau}(C\times S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}).

For the twisted case this is even more interesting. Suppose that the diagram automorphism
\theta\in\Aut(G)
acts on the
6
-manifold
C\times_{\tau}S_{R_{1}}^{1}\times S_{R_{2}}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
by rotation on the circle
S^{1}_{R_{2}}
. We can then consider two different
2
-dimensional theories

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}^{\tau}_{A}(C)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{T}_{5}^{\tau}(C\times S^{1}_{R_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})=\lim_{R_{1}\rightarrow 0}\mathbf{T}_{6}(C\times_{\tau}S^{1}_{R_{1}}\times S^{1}_{R_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\Map(C\times S^{1}_{B},BG)_{\tau^{*}}^{\theta}=\bmHiggs_{G,\theta}(C)_{\tau^{*}},

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}^{\tau}_{B}(C)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{T}^{\id}_{5}(C\times_{\tau}S^{1}_{R_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})=\lim_{R_{2}\rightarrow 0}\mathbf{T}_{6}(C\times_{\tau}S^{1}_{R_{1}}\times S^{1}_{R_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\Map(C\times_{\tau}S^{1}_{B},B(G^{\theta}))_{\id}=\Conn^{\tau}_{G^{\theta}}(C)_{\id}.

The linearizations of these two theories are the
\mathbf{A}^{\tau}
-model

\mathbf{A}^{\tau}_{G,\theta}(C)=\QC(\bmHiggs_{G,\theta}(C)_{\tau^{*}})=\dDiff_{\tau^{*}}(\bmHiggs_{G,\theta}(C))

and the
\mathbf{B}^{\tau}
-
model

\mathbf{B}^{\tau}_{G^{\theta}}(C)=\QC(\Conn^{\tau}_{G^{\theta}}(C)_{\id})=\dDiff_{\id}(\Conn^{\tau}_{G^{\theta}}(C)_{\id}).

Elliott and Pestun conjecture that
\mathbf{S}
-duality for these two physical theories should exchange
G^{\theta}
with its Langlands dual, and the parameter
\tau
with its inverse
\tau^{-1}
. This is the
multiplicative geometric Langlands correspondence
.

Conjecture A.1
.

Let
G
be a semisimple simply-laced complex group,
\theta
a diagram automorphism of
G
, and
C
a Calabi-Yau Riemann surface. For any
\tau\in\Aut(C)
there is a derived equivalence

\mathbf{mGLC}:\dDiff_{\tau^{-1}}(\bmHiggs_{G,\theta}(C))\longrightarrow\dDiff_{\id}(\Conn^{\tau}_{(G^{\theta})^{\vee}}(C)).

Furthermore, we can now consider a “classical limit” of this correspondence, by taking

\mathbf{mGLC}_{\mathrm{cl}}:\dDiff_{\id}(\bmHiggs_{G,\theta}(C))\longrightarrow\dDiff_{\id}(\bmHiggs_{(G^{\theta})^{\vee}}(C)).

In particular, this implies the existence of an equivalence

\QC(\bmHiggs_{G,\theta}(C))\longrightarrow\QC(\bmHiggs_{(G^{\theta})^{\vee}}(C)).

Now, this last formula is precisely the form of the main statements of this paper.

Appendix C
Review of Prym varieties

C.1.
The Prym variety

Let
S
and
C
be smooth projective curves over
k
and let
\pi:S\rightarrow C
be a degree
2
finite map. We denote by
B\subset C
the branch divisor of
\pi
. It is well known that
B
consists of an even number of single points,
|B|=2k
. The Galois group of the cover
\pi
is generated by an involution
\sigma:S\rightarrow S
. We can identify
B
with its preimage
\pi^{-1}(B)
, which is the set of fixed points
\pi^{-1}(B)=S^{\sigma}
.

Consider the norm map

\displaystyle\Nm_{\pi}:\Pic S

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Pic C

\displaystyle O_{S}(D)

\displaystyle\longmapsto O_{C}(\pi(D)).

The
Prym variety

P^{1}=P(S,C)
is defined as the neutral connected component of the kernel of the norm map,
P^{1}:=(\ker\Nm_{\pi})^{0}
. Note that we can write

\ker\Nm_{\pi}=\left\{(U,\eta):U\in\Pic S,\eta:U^{-1}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\sigma^{*}U\right\}/\mathrm{iso}.

For such a point
(U,\eta)\in\ker\Nm_{\pi}
we obtain an isomorphism
\eta|_{B}:U^{-1}|_{B}\rightarrow U|_{B}
or, equivalently, a section
\tilde{\eta}\in H^{0}(B,U^{2})
. We can then identify the Prym variety as

P^{1}=\left\{(U,\eta)\in\ker\Nm_{\pi}\text{ such that there exists }\xi\in H^{0}(B,U)\text{ with }\tilde{\eta}=\xi^{2}\right\}/\text{iso}.

We denote by
P^{0}=\mathbf{D}(P(S,C))
its dual abelian variety.

C.2.
Polarizations

Recall that a polarization of an abelian variety
P
is an isogeny
\rho:P\rightarrow\hat{P}
between
P
and its dual abelian variety
\hat{P}
. If it is an isomorphism, then it is called a principal polarization.
The Jacobian of a smooth projective curve is principally polarized. That is, we have isomorphisms
\Jac(C)\cong\mathbf{D}(\Jac(C))
and
\Jac(S)\cong\mathbf{D}(\Jac(S))
between the Jacobians of
S
and
C
and their duals. Under these isomorphisms, the dual of the norm map
\Nm_{\pi}
is the pullback
\pi^{*}:\Jac(C)\rightarrow\Jac(S)
.
Therefore, we obtain an isomorphism
P^{0}\cong\Jac(S)/\pi^{*}\Jac(C)
and we can construct a polarization
\rho:P^{1}\rightarrow P^{0}
through the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{0}\cong\Jac(S)/\pi^{*}\Jac(C)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Jac(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Jac(S).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}

But note now that
\Nm_{\pi}\pi^{*}U=U^{2}
, so in fact
\rho(P^{1})\subset P^{1}
and we can write

P^{0}\cong P^{1}/P^{1}\cap\pi^{*}\Jac(C).

Moreover,
\pi^{*}U\in\pi^{*}\Jac(C)\cap P^{1}
means that
U^{2}=\Nm_{\pi}\pi^{*}U=O_{C}
, so in fact we get

P^{0}=P^{1}/\pi^{*}\Jac(C)[2],

where
\Jac(C)[2]
is the finite subgroup of order
2
elements of the Jacobian of
C
.

C.3.
The squaring map

Consider the squaring map

\displaystyle 2:P^{1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow P^{1}

\displaystyle(U,\eta)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(U^{2},\eta^{2}).

This map is surjective, since
P^{1}
is connected, and has a finite kernel
P^{1}[2]
, the set of order two elements of
P^{1}
. The inclusion
\pi^{*}\Jac(C)\hookrightarrow P^{1}
determines an inclusion
\iota:\pi^{*}\Jac(C)[2]\hookrightarrow P^{1}[2]
. We can determine now the cokernel of this inclusion.

First of all, recall that for any pair
(U,\eta)\in\ker\Nm_{\pi}
we have the section
\tilde{\eta}\in H^{0}(B,U^{2})
. Now, if
(U,\eta)
is
2
-torsion element, then by definition
U^{2}\cong O_{S}
and
\tilde{\eta}\in H^{0}(B,\mu_{2})
. Since
(U,\eta)
is defined up to isomorphism, the element
\tilde{\eta}
is well-defined up to sign. Therefore we obtain a diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}\Jac(C)[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{1}[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\coker\iota}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}\Jac(C)[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker\Nm_{\pi}[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(B,\mu_{2})/\mu_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id}

so we can identify

\coker\iota=\left\{m=(m_{b})_{b\in B}:m_{b}\in\mu_{2},\prod_{b\in B}m_{b}=1\right\}/(m\sim-m)\cong(\mu_{2})^{2k-1}/\mu_{2}=(\mu_{2})^{2k-2}.

We obtain the following.

Corollary C.1
.

The Prym variety
P^{1}
is principally polarized if and only if
k=0
or
k=1
.

C.4.
Abelian variety associated with a subset of the ramification divisor

Consider now a proper non-empty subset
A\subset B
. We have a natural short exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(B\setminus A,\mu_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(B,\mu_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(A,\mu_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

which induces a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\ker(H^{0}(B\setminus A,\mu_{2})\rightarrow\mu_{2})\longrightarrow\ker(H^{0}(B,\mu_{2})/\mu_{2}\rightarrow\mu_{2})\longrightarrow H^{0}(A,\mu_{2})/\mu_{2}\longrightarrow 0.

Associated with this subset
A
we can define the variety

\tilde{P}_{A}=\left\{(U,\eta,\xi):(U,\eta)\in\ker\Nm_{\pi},\xi\in H^{0}(A,U),\text{ such that }\tilde{\eta}|_{A}=\xi^{2}\right\}/\text{iso.}

This variety is a finite cover of
P^{1}
, and we also have a natural map

\displaystyle u_{A}:P^{1}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\tilde{P}_{A}

\displaystyle(U,\eta)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(U^{2},\eta^{2},\tilde{\eta}|_{A}).

The kernel of this map is

\displaystyle\ker u_{A}

\displaystyle=\left\{(U,\eta)\in P^{1}:U^{2}\cong O_{S},\eta^{2}=1,\tilde{\eta}|_{A}=1\right\}/\text{iso.}

Note that there is an inclusion
\iota_{A}:\ker u_{A}\rightarrow P^{1}[2]
, and a short exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker u_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{1}[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(A,\mu_{2})/\mu_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

So we also have a short exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}\Jac(C)[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker u_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(H^{0}(B\setminus A,\mu_{2})\rightarrow\mu_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

We define

P_{A}=P^{1}/\ker u_{A}.

This is a new abelian variety, which acts freely and transitively on
\tilde{P}_{A}
.

Note that, since
P^{0}=P^{1}/\pi^{*}\Jac(C)[2]
, we have an isomorphism

P_{A}\cong P^{0}/\ker(H^{0}(B\setminus A,\mu_{2})\rightarrow\mu_{2}).

Thus, by dualizing on the exact sequence defining this quotient, we obtain a short exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(B\setminus A,\mu_{2})/\mu_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{P}_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

But note that we have the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker u_{B\setminus A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker u_{B\setminus A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{1}[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(B\setminus A,\mu_{2})/\mu_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{B\setminus A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{1},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}

with short exact rows and columns. This yields an isomorphism

\hat{P}_{A}\cong P_{B\setminus A}.

In particular, if
A
has exactly
k
elements, then we can use a bijection between
A
and
B\setminus A
to determine an isomorphism
\hat{P}_{A}\cong P_{A}
, and thus a principal polarization on
P_{A}
.

C.5.
Cameral interpretation

The finite map
\pi:S\rightarrow C
is a cameral cover associated with the root system
\mathsf{A}_{1}
,
\Phi=\left\{\alpha,-\alpha\right\}
. Clearly, it has simple Galois ramification, and we are assuming that
S
is smooth.

The kernel of the norm map
\Nm_{\pi}:\Pic S\rightarrow\Pic C
can be understood as the coarse moduli space of strongly
\mathbb{Z}/2
-equivariant
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-torsors on
S
.
The Prym variety
P^{1}
is the coarse moduli space of the Picard stack of
J^{1}
, torsors, for

J^{1}=\pi_{*}(S\times\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})^{\mathbb{Z}/2},

and the inclusion
P^{1}\rightarrow\ker\Nm_{\pi}
is induced by the inclusion of
\mathcal{P}^{1}=\Bun_{J^{1}}
in
\Bun_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}/S}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}
. Indeed, the extra condition of the section
\tilde{\eta}\in H^{0}(B,U^{2})
associated with an isomorphism
\eta:U^{-1}\rightarrow\sigma^{*}U
being a square, corresponds to
(U,\eta)
being in the kernel of the map

\ker\Nm_{\pi}\rightarrow B\times H^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})\cong H^{0}(B,\mu_{2}).

The Picard stack
\mathcal{P}^{1}
is connected, but it has a nontrivial inertia group, coming from the fact that
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}=\mu_{2}
.

Over each ramification point, the group
J^{1}
has two connected components, and we let
J^{0}\subset J^{1}
be the subgroup of fiberwise neutral connected components. This inclusion induces a long exact sequence in cohomology
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\rightarrow H^{0}(C,J^{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(C,J^{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(B,\mu_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(C,J^{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(C,J^{1})\rightarrow 0.}

The Picard stack
\mathcal{P}^{0}=\Bun_{J^{0}}
is actually a scheme: it has trivial inertia group, but it has two connected components
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{P}^{0})=\mathbb{Z}/2
. Its neutral connected component is the variety
P^{0}
, the dual of the Prym variety. Indeed, the above map induces an isogeny
P^{0}\rightarrow P^{1}
with kernel
(\mu_{2})^{2k-2}
, and we saw already that this kernel is precisely the cokernel of the inclusion
\pi^{*}\Jac(C)[2]\hookrightarrow P^{1}[2]
.

More generally, for each subset
A
of the branch locus
B
, we can consider the subgroup
J_{A}
of
J^{1}
containing
J^{0}
and with one connected component over the points of
A
and two connected components over the points of the complement
B\setminus A
. The inclusion
J^{0}\subset J_{A}
induces an exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\rightarrow H^{0}(C,J^{0})\rightarrow H^{0}(C,J_{A})\rightarrow H^{0}(B\setminus A,\mu_{2})\rightarrow H^{1}(C,J^{0})\rightarrow H^{1}(C,J_{A})\rightarrow 0.}

This sequence induces an isogeny
P^{0}\rightarrow|\Bun_{J_{A}}|^{0}
with kernel equal to
\ker(H^{0}(B\setminus A,\mu_{2})\rightarrow\mu_{2})
. Therefore, the abelian variety
|\Bun_{J_{A}}|^{0}
is precisely the abelian variety
P_{A}
. Its dual
\hat{P}_{A}
is exactly the abelian variety
P_{B\setminus A}=|\Bun_{J_{B\setminus A}}|^{0}
. Indeed,
J^{1}/J_{A}=A\times\mu_{2}=J_{B\setminus A}/J^{0}
.

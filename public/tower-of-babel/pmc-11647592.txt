Fontan circulatory failure: a case report of re-operation treatment of vascular graft angulation stenosis
Authors: Rui Lv, Lixin Sun, Zhenglun Alan Wei, Bin Qiao, Andriana Anagnostopoulou
Published: 2024

A 32-year-old woman presented with palpitation, breathlessness, New York Heart Association (NYHA) Functional Classification Grade III, and decreased quality of life over the past 3 years, following Fontan surgery performed 27 years ago. She exhibited no cyanosis of the lips or jugular vein engorgement, and her blood oxygen saturation was 87%. Echocardiography indicated an ejection fraction of 58%. Cardiac computed tomographic angiography and right heart catheterization angiography revealed a curved, angulated deformity of the conduit [Panels A and B: conduit angulation (arrow)]. Haemodynamic data were obtained from the catheter lab, with pressure measurements recorded as systolic/diastolic (mean value) at each site: conduit and right pulmonary artery: 10/9 (10), superior vena cava and left pulmonary artery: 10/8 (10), inferior vena cava: 11/10 (11), aorta: 112/68 (87), and ventricle: 108/3 (37). The diagnosis was Fontan circulatory failure caused by conduit angulation stenosis.

A conduit replacement operation was performed under cardiopulmonary bypass with general anaesthesia. The angulated conduit was removed [Panel C: conduit angulation; Panel D: fibrous tissue proliferation in conduit causing lumen stenosis (arrow)], and a novel fluorinated ethylene propylene (FEP) Ringed Gore-Tex graft was implanted, embedded in the diaphragm with specialized anastomotic connections [Panel E: conduit (FEP-Ringed Gore-Tex: SA1602, 16 mm; arrow); Panel F: re-implantation of the conduit (arrow)]. The new conduit provided improved stability and reduced energy loss, yielding significant clinical benefits. Post-operative magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated an enhanced flow rate compared with pre-operation (27 vs. 51 cm/s, Panel G: 4D flow of the conduit downstream). The patient’s oxygen saturation improved to 97%, and she achieved cardiac function Grade I before discharge. Three months post-operation, her cardiac output measured 4.4 L/min.

Accurate identification and timely treatment of conduit angulation and stenosis are crucial for maintaining cardiovascular function and supporting Fontan patients throughout their lives. The Dacron catheter used by this patient had calcified and could not be expanded with a balloon to help conduit recover during interventional surgery. Key treatment considerations include managing conduit fibrosis, choosing properly soft conduit materials, and addressing angulation that may occur with growth and development of the patient. The specialized anastomotic technique and selection of FEP-Ringed Gore-Tex materials in this case may serve as valuable reference for similar cardiovascular issues.

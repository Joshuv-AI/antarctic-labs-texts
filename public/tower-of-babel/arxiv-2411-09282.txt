Integrable matrix product state of ABJM theory from projecting method
Nan Bai, Mao-Zhong Shao

Abstract
In this paper we investigate the integrable boundary state in ABJM theory. We find an integrability condition for the two-site integrable matrix product state (MPS) similar to the KT-relation. We also construct a class of non-trivial MPSs from the projected K-matrices.

Introduction

Integrable boundary state is a key object attracting much attention in the recent research for the exact results in quantum integrable systems, which was first proposed in the investigation of quantum quench models and defined as the state annihilated by the conserved charges with odd parities under space reflection
[
1

[
1
,
2
]

[
3
]
. One of the most prominent features of the integrable boundary state is the so-called pair structure which shows that the non-vanishing overlaps between the integrable boundary state and the Bethe state only occurs for the Bethe state with parity invariant root configurations and the resulted formulae for the overlaps have a factorized form given by the product of the polynomials of Q-functions and the ratio of the Gaudin-like determinants
[
4
,

,

]

\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory
[
10
,

,
12
,
13
,

]
. Furthermore, it was also found that the three-point function of two determinant operators and a single trace operator can be exactly calculated when the determinant operators being the integrable MPS
[
16
,
17
]
.

The scope of the study of the integrable boundary state also extends to the ABJM theory
[
18
]
, which is a three-dimensional Chern-Simons matter theory whose anomalous dimension matrix is mapped to an integrable alternating spin chain
[

2
Integrable boundary state of ABJM spin chain

In this section we will give a comprehensive review of the connection between the integrable boundary state and the reflection K-matrix for ABJM spin chain model. The demonstration given below basically follows the original papers
[
1
,
2
]
and generalizes it to the open spin chain model with operator-valued K-matrix.

The ABJM spin chain model describes an alternating spin chain with the adjacent sites carrying
\mathbf{4}
and
\bar{\mathbf{4}}
representations of
\rm{SU(4)}
group respectively. The R-matrices can be defined either by the representation spaces they act upon, which results in four kinds of R-matrices
R_{12}(u),R_{1\bar{2}}(u),R_{\bar{1}2}(u)
and
R_{\bar{1}\bar{2}}(u)
, or simply by introducing two different R-matrices given by

\displaystyle R_{ij}(u)=u+\mathbb{P}_{ij},\quad\bar{R}_{ij}(u)=-u-2+\mathbb{K}_{ij},

(1)

where
\mathbb{P}
is the permutation operator and
\mathbb{K}
is the partial transpose of
\mathbb{P}
:
\mathbb{K}_{ij}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{t_{i}}=\mathbb{P}_{ij}^{t_{j}}
. In order not to bring about any confusion, we will adopt the latter definitions throughout this paper. Then for a spin chain with 2L sites, we have the following two monodromy matrices,

\displaystyle T(u)=R_{01}(u)\bar{R}_{02}(u)\cdots R_{0,2L-1}(u)\bar{R}_{0,2L}(u),

(2)

\displaystyle\overline{T}(u)=\bar{R}_{01}(u)R_{02}(u)\cdots\bar{R}_{0,2L-1}(u)R_{0,2L}(u),

and also two related transfer matrices
\tau(u)=\mbox{Tr}_{0}T_{0}(u)
and
\bar{\tau}(u)=\mbox{Tr}_{0}\overline{T}_{0}(u)
.
To study the open spin chain, we also need to introduce the boundary K-matrix which satisfies the following reflection equation (RE),

\displaystyle R_{12}(u-v)K_{1}(u)R_{12}(u+v)K_{2}(v)=K_{2}(v)R_{12}(u+v)K_{1}(u)R_{12}(u-v).

(3)

Note that if there exists additional internal degrees of freedom at the boundary, the matrix element of
K(u)
will also be an operator of the internal space
V_{\rm{in}}
:
K^{i}_{j}\in{\rm{End}(V_{\rm{in}})}
. The component form of the above equation (
3
) is

\displaystyle R^{i,j}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(u-v)K^{a_{1}}_{b_{1}}(u)R^{b_{1},a_{2}}_{k,b_{2}}(u+v)K^{b_{2}}_{l}(v)

(4)

\displaystyle=K^{j}_{a_{2}}(v)R^{i,a_{2}}_{a_{1},b_{2}}(u+v)K^{a_{1}}_{b_{1}}(u)R^{b_{1},b_{2}}_{k,l}(u-v),

where the summation over repeated indices is implied. Multiplying the basis vector
|i,j,k,l\rangle
of the tensor product space
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}\otimes V_{3}\otimes V_{4}
to both sides of (
4
), we find

\displaystyle R^{i,j}_{a_{1},a_{2}}(u-v)K^{a_{1}}_{b_{1}}(u)R^{b_{1},a_{2}}_{k,b_{2}}(u+v)K^{b_{2}}_{l}(v)|i,j,k,l\rangle

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K^{a_{1}}_{b_{1}}(u)K^{b_{2}}_{l}(v)R^{b_{1},a_{2}}_{k,b_{2}}(u+v)R_{12}(u-v)|a_{1},a_{2},k,l\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K^{a_{1}}_{b_{1}}(u)K^{b_{2}}_{l}(v)R_{12}(u-v)R^{t_{3}}_{32}(u+v)|a_{1},b_{2},b_{1},l\rangle,

and

\displaystyle K^{j}_{a_{2}}(v)R^{i,a_{2}}_{a_{1},b_{2}}(u+v)K^{a_{1}}_{b_{1}}(u)R^{b_{1},b_{2}}_{k,l}(u-v)|i,j,k,l\rangle

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K^{j}_{a_{2}}(v)K^{a_{1}}_{b_{1}}(u)R^{i,a_{2}}_{a_{1},b_{2}}(u+v)R^{t_{3}t_{4}}_{34}(u-v)|i,j,b_{1},b_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K^{j}_{a_{2}}(v)K^{a_{1}}_{b_{1}}(u)R^{t_{3}t_{4}}_{34}(u-v)R^{t_{4}}_{14}(u+v)|a_{1},j,b_{1},a_{2}\rangle.

If we define a two-site state as

\displaystyle|\phi(u)\rangle=K^{i}_{j}(u)|i,j\rangle,

(7)

then from Eqs (
4
-
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle R_{12}(u-v)R^{t_{3}}_{32}(u+v)|\phi(u)\rangle_{13}\otimes|\phi(v)\rangle_{24}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{t_{3}t_{4}}_{34}(u-v)R^{t_{4}}_{14}(u+v)|\phi(v)\rangle_{24}\otimes|\phi(u)\rangle_{13}.

Notice that both
R_{ij}(u)
and
\bar{R}_{ij}(u)
are P-symmetric, i.e.
R_{ij}(u)=R_{ji}(u)
,
\bar{R}_{ij}(u)=\bar{R}_{ji}(u)
, and also the relation

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{ij}(u)=R^{t_{j}}_{ij}(-u-2),

(9)

thus if we set
u=-1
and then let
v=-u-1
in (
8
) and also rename the space indices as:
2\rightarrow 0,\,3\rightarrow 2,\,4\rightarrow 0^{\prime}
, we will finally have

\displaystyle R_{01}(u)\bar{R}_{02}(u)|\phi(-1)\rangle_{12}\otimes|\phi(-u-1)\rangle_{00^{\prime}}

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{0^{\prime}2}(u)\bar{R}_{0^{\prime}1}(u)|\phi(-u-1)\rangle_{00^{\prime}}\otimes|\phi(-1)\rangle_{12}.

Now we define the following tensor product state:

\displaystyle|\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=|\phi(-1)\rangle_{12}\otimes|\phi(-1)\rangle_{34}\otimes\cdots\otimes|\phi(-1)\rangle_{2L-1,2L}

(11)

\displaystyle=K^{i_{1}}_{i_{2}}(-1)K^{i_{3}}_{i_{4}}(-1)\cdots K^{i_{2L-1}}_{i_{2L}}(-1)|i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{2L-1},i_{2L}\rangle,

which is a mixed-type state
|\Phi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\otimes{\rm{End}(V_{in})}
, where
\mathcal{H}\cong(\mathbf{4}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{4}})^{\otimes L}
is the Hilbert space of the whole spin chain. Tracing over the internal space
V_{\rm{in}}
, we obtain the two-site invariant matrix product state (MPS):

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle=\mbox{Tr}_{V_{\rm{in}}}|\Phi\rangle\,\in\,\mathcal{H}.

(12)

By means of the basic relation (
10
), we can directly get

\displaystyle T_{0}(u)|\Phi\rangle\otimes|\phi(-u-1)\rangle_{00^{\prime}}=\left(\Pi\overline{T}_{0}(u)\Pi\right)|\phi(-u-1)\rangle_{00^{\prime}}\otimes|\Phi\rangle,

(13)

where
\Pi\in{\rm{End}(\mathcal{H})}
is the space reflection operator of the spin chain. Now the trick here is that we first formulate the above relation in components on
V_{0}\otimes V_{0^{\prime}}
as

\displaystyle T^{\alpha}_{\lambda}(u)|\Phi\rangle\otimes K^{\lambda}_{\beta}(-u-1)|\alpha\rangle_{0}\otimes|\beta\rangle_{0^{\prime}}

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Pi\overline{T}^{\beta}_{\lambda}(u)\Pi\right)K^{\alpha}_{\lambda}(-u-1)|\alpha\rangle_{0}\otimes|\beta\rangle_{0^{\prime}}\otimes|\Phi\rangle,

then the component equality

\displaystyle T^{\alpha}_{\lambda}(u)|\Phi\rangle K^{\lambda}_{\beta}(-u-1)=\left(\Pi\overline{T}^{\beta}_{\lambda}(u)\Pi\right)K^{\alpha}_{\lambda}(-u-1)|\Phi\rangle

(15)

can be expressed on a single space
V_{0}
as

\displaystyle\left[T_{0}(u)\right]^{\alpha}_{\lambda}|\Phi\rangle\left[K_{0}(-u-1)\right]^{\lambda}_{\beta}=\left[K_{0}(-u-1)\right]^{\alpha}_{\lambda}\Pi\left[\overline{T}_{0}(u)\right]^{\beta}_{\lambda}\Pi|\Phi\rangle,

(16)

which leads to

\displaystyle T_{0}(u)|\Phi\rangle K_{0}(-u-1)=K_{0}(-u-1)\left(\Pi\overline{T}^{t_{0}}_{0}(u)\Pi\right)|\Phi\rangle.

(17)

The above relation is quite similar to the KT-relation for a general two-site integrable boundary state proposed in
[
3
]
. However there are two major differences: the spectral parameter dependence of
K_{0}
and the form of the monodromy matrix on the right hand side of the equation
1
1

1

As a comparison, we note that the KT-relation given in
[
3
]
has the form:

\displaystyle K_{0}(u)\langle\Phi|T_{0}(u)=\langle\Phi|T_{0}(-u)K_{0}(u).

.

In the end, by multiplying
K^{-1}_{0}(-u-1)
to the left on both sides of the above equation and then taking the trace over
V_{0}\otimes V_{\rm{in}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau(u)|\Psi\rangle=\Pi\bar{\tau}(u)\Pi|\Psi\rangle,

(18)

which is the final form of the integrability condition for integrable boundary state in ABJM theory used in the literatures
[
5
,
21
,
24
]
.

3
Integrable MPS from the projected K-matrix

In this section we will investigate the possible operator-valued K-matrix solutions by the projecting method proposed in
[
27
]
. The projecting K-matrix for ABJM spin chain can be obtained as follows: Given a c-number solution
K_{1}(u)\in{\rm{End}}(V_{1})
of RE (
3
), one can construct the following two operator-valued solutions
K_{12}(u)
and
\bar{K}_{12}(u)
on
V_{1}\otimes V_{2}
by means of the two R-matrices
R_{12}(u)
and
\bar{R}_{12}(u)
respectively as:

\displaystyle K_{12}(u)=R_{12}(u+c)K_{1}(u)R_{12}(u-c),

(19)

\displaystyle\bar{K}_{12}(u)=\bar{R}_{12}(u+\bar{c})K_{1}(u)\bar{R}_{12}(u-\bar{c}),

where
V_{2}
is the additional inner space at the boundary and
c
(
\bar{c}
) are two arbitrary constants. We will call them the regular solutions. However such solutions are trivial since they simply represent adding one more quantum site to the bulk of the original spin chain
[
28
]
.

To find the non-trivial solutions, we introduce the projection operator
Q
:
Q^{2}=Q,\,Q\in{\rm{End}{(V_{2})}}
. It can be easily shown that if either of the regular solution
K_{12}(u)
or
\bar{K}_{12}(u)
in (
19
) satisfies the following projection condition:

\displaystyle Q_{2}K_{12}(u)(\mathbb{I}-Q_{2})=0\quad\mbox{or}\quad Q_{2}\bar{K}_{12}(u)(\mathbb{I}-Q_{2})=0,

(20)

then
Q_{2}K_{12}(u)Q_{2}
or
Q_{2}\bar{K}_{12}(u)Q_{2}
becomes the non-trivial operator-valued solution of RE and will be called the projected K-matrix.

As the initial input, the c-number solution of RE (
3
) takes the general form
K(u)=\xi\mathbb{I}+u\mathbb{E}
where
\xi
is an arbitrary constant and
\mathbb{E}
can be chosen from either of the following two classes (details can be found in the paper
[
29
]
):

(1)

\mathbb{E}
is diagonal and
\mathbb{E}^{2}=\mathbb{I}
;

(2)

\mathbb{E}
is strictly triangular and
\mathbb{E}^{2}=0
.

So a natural question here is: based on the above two kinds of c-number solutions and the two possible constructions of regular solutions (
19
), can we find the suitable projector
Q
satisfying the projection condition (
20
) and then obtain the corresponding projected K-matrix solution ? In the following we will answer this question by giving all possible projectors for each case and thus obtain a complete classification of the integrable MPS from the projected K-matrix.

3.1
Projected K-matrix from
K_{12}(u)

We expand
K_{12}(u)
by the order of spectral parameter
u
as:

\displaystyle K_{12}(u)=

\displaystyle u^{3}\mathbb{E}_{1}+u^{2}\left(\xi\mathbb{I}+\mathbb{E}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{12}+\mathbb{P}_{12}\mathbb{E}_{1}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle u\left(2\xi\mathbb{P}_{12}-c^{2}\mathbb{E}_{1}+c\mathbb{E}_{1}\mathbb{P}_{12}-c\mathbb{P}_{12}\mathbb{E}_{1}+\mathbb{E}_{2}\right)+\xi\left(1-c^{2}\right)\mathbb{I}.

From the projection condition
Q_{2}K_{12}(u)\left(\mathbb{I}-Q_{2}\right)=0
, we observe that the coefficient in front of
u^{3}
gives,

\displaystyle Q_{2}\mathbb{E}_{1}\left(\mathbb{I}-Q_{2}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left(Q_{2}-Q^{2}_{2}\right)=0,

(22)

which means
Q
must be a projector. Therefore we could begin with an arbitrary operator
Q
with 16 unknowns
\{q_{ij},i,j=1,\cdots,4\}
(not necessary a projector),

Q=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}q_{11}&q_{12}&q_{13}&q_{14}\\
q_{21}&q_{22}&q_{23}&q_{24}\\
q_{31}&q_{32}&q_{33}&q_{34}\\
q_{41}&q_{42}&q_{43}&q_{44}\\
\end{array}\right),

(23)

and then impose the projection condition to
Q
, then all the surviving
Q
s are automatically being the projection operators we search for.

K_{12}(u)
with the first class of the c-number solution

4
Conclusion and discussion

In this paper we studied the integrable boundary state in ABJM theory from two aspects: We first reconsidered the relation between the two-site integrable MPS and the operator-valued solution of reflection equation for the ABJM spin chain. In this process, we found an integrability condition for the MPS which is quite similar to the KT-relation proposed in
[
3
]
; We also constructed a class of integrable MPSs by the projecting method and gave a complete classification of the projected K-matrices by finding all possible non-trivial projection operators.

For future research, an immediate direction is to investigate the exact overlaps for the integrable MPSs obtained from projected K-matrices. Besides, in parallel to the studies given in the present work, we could also consider the operator-valued solutions of the twisted reflection equation which represents the soliton non-preserving boundary conditions
[
2
,
30
,
31
]
, and then construct the related integrable MPSs.

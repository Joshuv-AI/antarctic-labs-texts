On a conjecture with implications for multicriteria decision making Journal: MCDA
Anas Mifrani

Abstract
We prove a conjecture by Richard Soland that given an efficient solution to a multicriteria optimization problem, there need not exist a continuous, strictly increasing and strictly concave criterion space function that attains its maximum at the vector of criteria values achieved by that solution.

x\in X

x^{\circ}\in X

2
Example

Let
p=2
,
X=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\geq 0\}
,
f_{1}(x)=x^{2}
, and
f_{2}(x)=-x^{3}
. For all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
g_{1}(y)=-y_{1}
and
h_{1}(y)=y_{2}+y_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}
. The criterion space in this case is obviously given by

Y=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:g_{1}(y)\leq 0,\ h_{1}(y)=0\}.

Let
x^{\circ}=0
. It is clear that
x^{\circ}
, or for that matter any point in
X
, lies in
X_{E}
. However,
x^{\circ}\notin X_{PRE}
. To see why, notice that for any
x>0
,
f_{1}(x)>f_{1}(x^{\circ})=0
,
f_{2}(x^{\circ})=0>f_{2}(x)
, and

\frac{f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x^{\circ})}{f_{2}(x^{\circ})-f_{2}(x)}=\frac{1}{x}.

Now, if we take any scalar
M>0
, we will find a
\delta>0
such that if
0<x<\delta
, then
\frac{1}{x}>M
. For any
M>0
, therefore, there exists
x\in X
with
f_{1}(x)>f_{1}(x^{\circ})
and
f_{2}(x^{\circ})>f_{2}(x)
such that

\frac{f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x^{\circ})}{f_{2}(x^{\circ})-f_{2}(x)}>M,

and so
x^{\circ}
is improperly efficient.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that there is a continuous, strictly increasing and strictly concave function
v:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
y^{\circ}=f(x^{\circ})=(0,0)
is the unique solution to the mathematical program

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle v(y),\textrm{ subject to }y\in Y.

Recalling that
Y=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:g_{1}(y)\leq 0,\ h_{1}(y)=0\}
, it is easy to see that
g_{1}(y^{\circ})=0
, that
g_{1}
and
h_{1}
are differentiable at
y^{\circ}
, and that the gradients

\nabla g_{1}(y^{\circ})=(-1,0),

(1)

\nabla h_{1}(y^{\circ})=(0,1),

(2)

are linearly independent, so that the linear independence constraint qualification holds. It follows from the necessary Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions that there are scalars
\mu_{1}
and
\lambda_{1}
for which

(0,0)\in\partial(-v)(y^{\circ})+\mu_{1}\partial g_{1}(y^{\circ})+\lambda_{1}\partial h_{1}(y^{\circ}),

(3)

\mu_{1}g_{1}(y^{\circ})=0,

(4)

\mu_{1}\geq 0,

(5)

where
\partial(-v)(y^{\circ})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the subdifferential of the (strictly convex) function
-v
at
y^{\circ}
, with analogous definitions for
\partial g_{1}(y^{\circ})
and
\partial h_{1}(y^{\circ})
.

As
g_{1}
and
h_{1}
are differentiable at
y^{\circ}
, we have that
\partial g_{1}(y^{\circ})=\{\nabla g_{1}(y^{\circ})\}
and
\partial h_{1}(y^{\circ})=\{\nabla h_{1}(y^{\circ})\}
. Combining (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) yields a
z\in\partial(-v)(y^{\circ})
such that
z_{1}=\mu_{1}
and
z_{2}=-\lambda_{1}
. Thus, by (
5
),
z_{1}\geq 0
.

By the definition of the subdifferential of a strictly convex function, we have that

v(y)<v(y^{\circ})-\langle z,y-y^{\circ}\rangle

for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{y^{\circ}\}
. For
y=(1,0)
, in particular, this means that
v(1,0)<v(0,0)-z_{1}
, hence
v(1,0)<v(0,0)
, since
z_{1}\geq 0
, thereby contradicting the premise that
v
is strictly increasing. As a result, no function exists that has all the desired properties.

3
Discussion

Soland’s article carries a discussion of the so-called value function approach to multicriteria optimization in which one posits the existence of a real-valued function
v(f(x))
that discriminates between feasible decisions – and between efficient decisions – by assigning a rating or value to each decision. The decision maker who wishes to behave in a manner consistent with this value function will select decisions
x\in X
that maximize
v(f(x))
. Lemma 3 occurs in a series of propositions intended to illustrate that even if
v
is assumed to possess specific mathematical properties, no efficient or properly efficient decision can be excluded from consideration on the grounds that it cannot maximize a function with those properties. One implication of the present note, however, is that in some situations, certain efficient decisions
can
be discarded on these grounds when
v
is presumed to be continuous, strictly increasing and strictly concave. It might be noted, incidentally, that our proof did not invoke continuity, nor did it require the strict aspect of concavity. Indeed, simple concavity would have led us to conclude, by virtue of the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions, that
v(1,0)\leq v(0,0)-z_{1}
for some
z_{1}\geq 0
, contradicting again the monotonicity of
v
. What we have shown, therefore, goes beyond Soland’s conjecture, to wit: given an efficient decision
x^{\circ}
, there need not exist a strictly increasing concave function
v
attaining its maximum over
Y
at
y^{\circ}=f(x^{\circ})
.

Introducing plain language summaries of publication in Veterinary Quarterly
Authors: Francesca Bonelli, Gerlinde R Van de Walle
Published: 2026

The field of veterinary science is constantly evolving, with new discoveries and advancements shaping the way we care for animals. However, the accessibility of scientific research remains a challenge for many practitioners and researchers in the veterinary community. To bridge this gap, Veterinary Quarterly is proud to introduce a new article type: the Plain Language Summary of Publication (PLSP). This initiative marks a significant step forward in making complex scientific findings more accessible, understandable, and actionable.

PLSPs have been widely adopted in the medical community, where they have proven to be an invaluable tool for disseminating research findings to diverse stakeholders, including clinicians, patients and policymakers. By presenting research in clear, concise, and jargon-free language, PLSPs ensure that critical insights are not confined to academic circles but are instead shared with those who can apply them in real-world settings. We believe this approach will be equally transformative in veterinary science, empowering veterinarians, caregivers, researchers, and other professionals to make informed decisions that benefit animal health and welfare.

Our inaugural PLSP, titled “Discovery of a SLAMF1 gene change that increases dogs’ risk of developing canine atopic dermatitis: A plain language summary” by Oliver P. Forman et al., exemplifies the potential of this new article type. This ground-breaking study identifies a genetic change in the SLAMF1 gene that heightens the risk of canine atopic dermatitis, a common and often debilitating condition in dogs. The PLSP distils the study’s complex findings into an accessible format, ensuring that its implications can be readily understood and applied by veterinary practitioners and researchers alike.

What makes this PLSP particularly noteworthy is the collaborative, multidisciplinary effort behind its development. Stakeholders from human medicine, veterinary medicine, the medical agency side, and publishing came together to create a summary that bridges disciplines and fosters a holistic understanding of the research. This collaborative approach underscores the interconnectedness of human and animal health and highlights the value of cross-disciplinary partnerships in advancing veterinary science.

The introduction of PLSPs to Veterinary Quarterly represents more than just a new article type; it is a commitment to inclusivity, transparency, and the practical application of research. By making scientific findings more accessible, we aim to enhance the impact of veterinary research, foster greater collaboration across disciplines, and ultimately improve outcomes for animals and their caregivers.

We invite the veterinary community to explore this new format and consider its potential to transform the way we communicate and apply research. As we continue to publish PLSPs, we look forward to your feedback and collaboration in shaping this initiative to best serve the needs of our field.

Abstract
. The dynamics of the left translation semigroup { T t } t ≥ 0 \{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0} on weighted L p L^{p} spaces over a directed metric tree L ⁡ ( G ) L(G) is investigated. Necessary and sufficient conditions on the weight family ρ \rho for the strong continuity of the semigroup are provided. Furthermore, hypercyclicity and weak mixing properties are characterized in terms of the asymptotic decay of ρ \rho along the tree structure. These results generalize classical L p L^{p} translation semigroup dynamics to a graph setting.

Introduction

The study of dynamical systems on metric graphs has its roots in the analysis of the transport equation on so-called quantum graphs. In this context, the effects of the flow along the edges are modeled by differential equations and coupled through specific transmission conditions at the vertices. The pioneering works of Kramar and Sikolya
[
13
]
and Dorn et al.
[
4
,
6
,
5
]
initiated a systematic analysis of these problems. The monograph by Mugnolo
[
16
]
represents a fundamental reference in this field (see also
[
1
, Chapter 18]
).
A first approach to the study of hypercyclicity in this setting can be found in
[
17
]
.

In parallel, the research community turned to the dynamics of operators on graph structures. In particular, the work of Martínez-Avendaño
[
14
]
inaugurated the study of the dynamic properties of the backward shift operator on directed trees. More recent efforts by Grosse-Erdmann and Papathanasiou
[
8
,
9
]
have significantly advanced the understanding of hypercyclicity and chaos phenomena in this specific dynamical context. Further related results are due to Menet and Papathanasiou
[
15
]
, Lopez-Martínez and Papathanasiou
[
11
]
and Kawamura
[
12
]
.

Inspired by both the semigroup approach to transport and the rich dynamical analysis of shift operators on trees, this article extends the notion of left translation to the setting of weighted
L^{p}
spaces over directed metric trees and studies its dynamics. The first section introduces the structure of metric trees, summarizes the essential definitions of strongly continuous semigroups (
C_{0}
-semigroups) and hypercyclicity, and establishes the theoretical framework for our work.

The central results of this article are twofold. The first, presented in Section 2, establishes the necessary and sufficient conditions on the weight family
\rho
that guarantee the translation operator generates a strongly continuous semigroup on the weighted spaces
L^{p}_{\rho}
. The second key result, developed in Section 3, provides necessary and sufficient conditions on the weight
\rho
that ensure the semigroup exhibits hypercyclicity on both rooted and unrooted directed metric trees.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Strongly continuous semigroups

We recall some basic definitions and results, referring to the monograph
[
7
]
for further details.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. A one-parameter family
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
of bounded operators on
X
is a strongly continuous semigroup (or a
C_{0}
-semigroup) if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

T_{0}=I

(ii)

T_{t+s}=T_{t}\circ T_{s}
for any
s,t\geq 0

(iii)

\lim_{s\rightarrow t}T_{s}x=T_{t}x
for all
x\in X
and
t\geq 0
.

As a consequence of the strong continuity of the semigroup, there is an exponential bound for the norm of the operators, namely
there exist
M\geq 1
and
w\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|T_{t}\|\leq Me^{wt},\qquad t\geq 0.

We can also express condition
(iii)
in some equivalent ways.

Proposition 1.2
.

[
7
, Ch. 1, Proposition 5.3]
Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a family of operators on
X
satisfying (i) and (ii) of Definition
1.1
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

\lim_{s\rightarrow t}T_{s}x=T_{t}x
for all
x\in X
and
t\geq 0
;

(ii)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}T_{t}x=x
for all
x\in X
.

(iii)

\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is locally equicontinuous and there exists a dense subset
X_{0}\subset X
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}T_{t}x=x
for all
x\in X_{0}
.

1.2.
Linear dynamics.

The main references for linear dynamics are the monographs
[
10
,
2
]
.
Throughout this section, let
X
be a separable Banach space.
An operator
T
on
X
is said to be
hypercyclic
if there is some
x\in X
whose orbit under
T
,

\text{Orb}(x,T):=\{x,Tx,T^{2}x,...\},

is dense in
X
. The operator
T
is said to be topologically transitive if for every pair
U,V
of nonempty open sets there exists some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{n}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
. Furthermore, if
T^{k}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
for all
k\geq n
, then the operator is said to be
mixing
. For separable Banach spaces, the Birkhoff Transitivity Theorem
[
10
, Theorem 2.19]
states that hypercyclicity is equivalent to topological transitivity.
Moreover, if the operator defined by

(T\oplus T)(x,y)=(Tx,Ty),(x,y)\in X\times X

is
hypercyclic
, then
T
is called
weakly mixing
. Every weakly mixing operator is hypercyclic, but the converse fails. We refer to
[
10
]
for further insights about the equivalence of the weakly mixing property and the Hypercyclicity Criterion.

All these dynamical properties have a counterpart in the context of strongly continuous semigroups. A strongly continuous semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
X
is said to be
hypercyclic
if there exists some
x\in X
whose orbit, defined as

\text{Orb}(x,\{T_{t}\}):=\{T_{t}x\,\mid\,t\geq 0\},

is dense in
X
. Furthermore, the semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is said to be
topologically transitive
if for any pair of nonempty open sets
U,V
there is some
t_{0}\geq 0
such that
T_{t_{0}}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
, while, if
T_{t}(U)\cap V\neq\emptyset
for any
t\geq t_{0}
then the semigroup is said to be mixing.
Topological transitivity is equivalent to hypercyclicity also in the context of strongly continuous semigroups
[
10
, Ch. 7]
.

The semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is called
weakly mixing
if
(T_{t}\oplus T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is topologically transitive in
X\times X
. Every weakly mixing semigroup is hypercyclic, but the converse does not hold true. A discretization of a semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a sequence of operators
(T_{t_{n}})_{n}
with
t_{n}\rightarrow\infty
. If there is some
t_{0}
such that
t_{n}=nt_{0}
then is called an autonomous discretization.
The following theorem provides an equivalence between the weak mixing property and the existence of a mixing discretization.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
10
, Propositions 7.20, 7.25]

Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup on
X
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is weakly mixing;

(ii)

some discretization of
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is mixing;

(iii)

some discretization of
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is weakly mixing.

(iv)

some autonomous discretization of
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is weakly mixing;

(v)

every autonomous discretization of
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is weakly mixing.

Finally, we recall the following important result due to Conejero, Múller and Peris.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
10
, Theorem 6.8]

Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup on
X
. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is hypercyclic;

(ii)

every operator
T_{t}
is hypercyclic for
t>0

(iii)

there exists
t_{0}>0
such that the operator
T_{t_{0}}
is hypercyclic.

1.3.
Directed metric trees

In this section, we establish the fundamental structure for our investigation, namely the
directed metric tree
. This construction starts with a directed tree, which is then endowed with a continuous metric structure.

We recall that a
directed tree
is a connected graph
G=(V,E)
without cycles. That is:

(i)

V
is a countable set of vertices.

(ii)

E\subset(V\times V)\setminus\{(v,v)\mid v\in V\}
is the set of directed edges (or arcs).

(iii)

For every
u,v\in V
,
u\neq v
there exists a finite sequence of vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in V
such that
(u,v_{1})
,
(v_{1},v_{2})
,
(v_{2},v_{3})
, …,
(v_{k-1},v_{k})
,
(v_{k},v)\in E
.

(iv)

There are no closed sequences of vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in V
such that
(v_{1},v_{2})
,
(v_{2},v_{3})
, …,
(v_{k-1},v_{k})
,
(v_{k},v_{1})\in E
.

(v)

For each vertex
v\in V
, there is at most one vertex
w\in V
such that
(w,v)\in E
. In this case,
w
is the
parent
of
v
and
v
is a
child
of
w
.

A vertex without children is called a
leaf
.

We obtain the
directed metric tree

L(G)
by assigning a coordinate
x_{e}\in[0,1]
to every edge
e\in E
, increasing in the direction of the edge. Thus, we identify each edge
e
with the interval
[0,1]
. For an edge
e=(v,w)
,
v
is the
tail
and
w
is the
head
.

A directed metric tree is
rooted
if there exists a vertex, called the
root
, that is not the head of any edge (i.e., no edge enters the root). If no such vertex exists, the tree is said to be
unrooted
.

The geometric structure of
L(G)
can be described by the edge-to-edge incidence matrix
\mathcal{A}
. Indexing the set of edges as
E=\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, the entries of
\mathcal{A}
are defined by:

\mathcal{A}_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if the head of }e_{i}\text{ is the tail of }e_{j},\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since the tree structure dictates that each vertex has at most one parent, it holds that

\forall j\in I:\quad\text{card}(\{i\in I\,\mid\mathcal{A}_{ij}\neq 0\})\leq 1.

That is, each column of
\mathcal{A}
contains at most one non-zero term.

The powers of
\mathcal{A}
track paths along the tree:
(\mathcal{A}^{n})_{ij}\neq 0
if and only if there exists a directed path of length
n
starting with edge
e_{i}
and ending with edge
e_{j}
. We denote the set of indexes of edges reachable from
e_{i}
in
n\in\mathds{N}
steps as:

M_{n}(i)=\{j\in I\,\mid\,(\mathcal{A}^{n})_{ij}\neq 0\},

while we set
M_{0}(i):=\{i\}
.

Observe that, due to the geometry of the tree, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1.1)

M_{n}(i)\cap M_{n}(j)=\emptyset\qquad\text{ if }i\neq j.

We now define the functional space on
L(G)
on which the dynamics will be studied. A function
f
on
L(G)
is identified with the family of its restrictions to the edges,
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
f_{i}=f|_{e_{i}}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{K}
(with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
).

A weight on
L(G)
is a family
\rho=(\rho_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
\rho_{i}\in L^{1}([0,1])
and is strictly positive a.e. in
[0,1]
.

For a given exponent
p\in[1,\infty)
, we define the weighted space on a single edge
e_{i}
as:

L^{p}_{\rho_{j}}[0,1]:=\{f:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,\mid\,f\mbox{ is measurable and }|f|^{p}\rho_{i}\in L^{1}[0,1]\},

endowed with the norm
||f||_{p,\rho_{i}}=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|f|^{p}\rho_{i}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The space of functions on the entire metric tree,
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
, is then defined as:

L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G)):=\left\{(f_{i})_{i\in I}\,\mid\,f_{i}\in L^{p}_{\rho_{i}}[0,1]\text{ and }\sum_{i\in I}||f_{i}||_{p,\rho_{i}}^{p}<\infty\right\}.

This space is a Banach space when equipped with the norm:

\|f\|_{p,\rho}:=\left(\sum_{i\in I}||f_{i}||_{p,\rho_{i}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\qquad f=(f_{i})_{i\in I}\in L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G)).

It will be useful to observe that condition (
1.1
) yields for any
f\in L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

(1.2)

||f||^{p}_{p,\rho}=\sum_{i\in I}||f_{i}||_{p,\rho_{i}}^{p}\geq\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in M_{n}(i)}||f_{j}||_{p,\rho_{j}}^{p}.

A straightforward proof gives that

\displaystyle F^{p}_{\rho}:=\oplus_{i\in I}L^{p}_{\rho_{i}}[0,1]=

\displaystyle=\left\{(f_{i})_{i\in I}\,\mid\,f_{i}\in L^{p}_{\rho_{i}}[0,1]\text{ and there exists a finite set }F\subseteq I\text{ s.t. }f_{i}=0\text{ if }i\in I\setminus F\right\}

is dense in
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
, and, consequently,
\oplus_{i\in I}C[0,1]
is dense in
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
.

1.4.
Left translation semigroups on directed metric trees

We now define the left translation semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
. This family of operators describes the flow of a function’s value along the directed paths of the tree. For any
s\in[0,1]
and
t\geq 0
, let
n(t,s)
be the integer part of
s+t
, i.e., the unique
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
n\leq s+t<n+1
.
Keeping in mind that
s\in[0,1]
, if
n_{0}\leq t<n_{0}+1
, then

(1.3)

n(t,s)=\begin{cases}n_{0}&\mbox{ if }0\leq s<n_{0}+1-t\\
n_{0}+1&\mbox{ if }n_{0}+1-t\leq s\leq 1\end{cases}.

For
f\in L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
and
t\geq 0
we define the left translation
T_{t}
as

(T_{t}f)(s):=\mathcal{A}^{n(t,s)}f(t+s-n(t,s)),\qquad s\in[0,1].

More in detail, for
f=(f_{i})_{i\in I}\in L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
and
t\geq 0
,
the value of the translated function is given by:

(T_{t}f)_{i}(s)=\sum_{j\in M_{n(t,s)}(i)}(\mathcal{A}^{n(t,s)})_{ij}\cdot f_{j}(t+s-n(t,s)),\qquad i\in I,\ s\in[0,1].

The map defined in this way can intuitively be seen as a left translation with a ”jump” described by the matrix
\mathcal{A}
.

Note that if
t=n\in\mathds{N}
, then
n(t,s)=n
for every
s\in[0,1[
and therefore

(T_{n}f)_{i}(s)=\sum_{j\in M_{n}(i)}(\mathcal{A}^{n})_{ij}\cdot f_{j}(s),\qquad s\in[0,1[.

Remark 1.5
.

If the tree is a rooted sequence of connected edges of the following type:

it holds that

(\mathcal{A})^{n}_{ij}\neq 0\Leftrightarrow j=i+n,

hence for every
(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in L^{p}(L(G))
and every
i\in\mathbb{N}

(T_{t}f)_{i}=f_{i+n(t,s)}(t+s-n(t,s)),\qquad s\in[0,1],t\geq 0.

Clearly
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
is isometric to
L^{p}_{\widetilde{\rho}}([0,+\infty[)
,
with

(1.4)

\widetilde{\rho}(s)=\rho_{i}(s-i),\qquad s\in[i,i+1[,\ i\in\mathbb{N},

via the isometry

\Phi:(f_{i})_{i\in I}\longmapsto f

where
f(u)=f_{[u]}(u-[u])
for any
u\in[0,+\infty[
.

Under this isometry, the left translation semigroup on the directed metric tree turns into the classical left translation semigroup
(\widetilde{T}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{p}_{\widetilde{\rho}}[0,+\infty[
. Indeed,
for every
f\in L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
and
u\in[0,+\infty[

\displaystyle(\Phi(T_{t}((f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}))(u)

\displaystyle=[T_{t}((f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})]_{[u]}(u-[u])

\displaystyle=f_{[u]+n(t,u-[u])}(t+u-[u]-n(t,u-[u]))=\Phi((f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})(t+u)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{T}_{t}(\Phi((f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}})(u).

Analogously, if the tree is an unrooted sequence of connected edges, then
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
is isometric to
L^{p}_{\widetilde{\rho}}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\widetilde{\rho}
is defined on
\mathbb{R}
as in (
1.4
), but with
i\in\mathds{Z}
. In this case, the left translation semigroup on the directed tree turns into the left translation semigroup on
L^{p}_{\widetilde{\rho}}(\mathbb{R})
.

2.
Strong continuity for the translation semigroup on directed metric trees

In this section we provide a characterization of the weight
\rho
such that the translation semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is strongly continuous on the weighted space
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
.

We begin with the following preparatory result that can be proved by adapting the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 4.3]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(see
[
8
, Lemma 4.3]
)
Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Lebesgue measurable set,
I
a finite or countable set,
\rho=(\rho_{i})_{i\in I}
a family of positive measurable functions on
E
, and define the set

\mathcal{V}=\left\{(v_{i})_{i\in I}\,\mid\,v_{i}\text{ measurable on }E\text{ for every }i\in I,\ \ \sum_{i\in I}|v_{i}(s)|=1\text{ for all }s\in[0,1]\right\}.

Then, for every
s\in E

\displaystyle\inf_{(v_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{V}}\left(\sum_{i\in I}|v_{i}(s)|^{p}\rho_{i}(s)\right)=\left(\sum_{i\in I}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}(s)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)^{1-p},\qquad 1<p<\infty.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\rho=(\rho_{i})_{i\in I}
be a weight on the directed metric graph
L(G)
and let
M\geq 1
and
w\in\mathds{R}
.

(a)

The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

The family
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{1}_{\rho}(L(G))
such that
||T_{t}||\leq Me^{wt}
for all
t\geq 0
;

(ii)

for all
i\in I
,
t\geq 0
, and a.e.
s\in[0,1]

\rho_{i}(s)\leq Me^{wt}\inf_{j\in M_{n(t,s)}(i)}\rho_{j}(s+t-n(t,s)).

(b)

Let
1<p<\infty
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

The family
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
such that
||T_{t}||\leq Me^{wt}
for all
t\geq 0
;

(ii)

for all
i\in I
,
t\geq 0
, and a.e.
s\in[0,1]
.

\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n(t,s)}(i)}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(s+t-n(t,s))^{1/(p-1)}}\right)^{p-1}\leq M^{p}e^{pwt}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}(s)}.

Proof.

(a)

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: We first show that for every
t>0
the map
T_{t}
is a continuous operator on
L^{1}_{\rho}(L(G))
and
||T_{t}||\leq Me^{wt}

t\geq 0
.

Fix
t_{0}>0
and let
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
be such that
n_{0}\leq t_{0}<n_{0}+1
and
f\in L^{1}_{\rho}(L(G))
. Taking into account (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), we compute the norm of
T_{t_{0}}f
and apply condition
(ii)
, so that:

\displaystyle\|T_{t_{0}}f\|_{1,\rho}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\int_{0}^{n_{0}+1-t_{0}}\left|\left(\mathcal{A}^{n_{0}}f\right)_{i}(s+t_{0}-n_{0})\right|\rho_{i}(s)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt+\sum_{i\in I}\int^{1}_{n_{0}+1-t_{0}}\left|\left(\mathcal{A}^{n_{0}+1}f\right)_{i}(s+t_{0}-n_{0}-1)\right|\rho_{i}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}\int_{0}^{n_{0}+1-t_{0}}\left|\sum_{j\in M_{n_{0}}(i)}f_{j}(s+t_{0}-n_{0})\right|\rho_{i}(s)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt+\sum_{i\in I}\int_{n_{0}+1-t_{0}}^{1}\left|\sum_{j\in M_{n_{0}+1}(i)}f_{j}(s+t_{0}-n_{0}-1)\right|\rho_{i}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}\int_{0}^{n_{0}+1-t_{0}}\sum_{j\in M_{n_{0}}(i)}|f_{j}(s+t_{0}-n_{0})|\rho_{i}(s)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt+\sum_{i\in I}\int_{n_{0}+1-t_{0}}^{1}\sum_{j\in M_{n_{0}+1}(i)}|f_{j}(s+t_{0}-n_{0}-1)|\rho_{i}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq Me^{wt_{0}}\sum_{i\in I}\int_{0}^{n_{0}+1-t_{0}}|f_{i}(s+t_{0}-n_{0})|\rho_{i}(s+t_{0}-n_{0})ds

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt+Me^{wt_{0}}\sum_{i\in I}\int_{n_{0}+1-t_{0}}^{1}|f_{i}(s+t_{0}-n_{0}-1)|\rho_{i}(s+t_{0}-n_{0}-1)ds

\displaystyle=Me^{wt_{0}}\sum_{i\in I}\int_{0}^{1}|f_{i}(s)|\rho_{i}(s)ds=Me^{wt_{0}}\|f\|_{1,\rho}.

As a consequence, the family
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is locally equicontinuous on
L^{1}_{\rho}(L(G))
.

The strong continuity of the semigroup is established by showing that

\lim_{t\to 0}\|T_{t}g-g\|_{1,\rho}=0

holds for
g
in a dense subset of
L^{1}_{\rho}(L(G))
, specifically for
g\in\oplus_{i\in I}C[0,1]
(see Proposition
1.2
).

To this end, let
g\in\oplus_{i\in I}C[0,1]
,
I_{g}:=\{i\in I\hskip 3.00003pt|\hskip 3.00003ptg_{i}\neq 0\}
and
\epsilon>0
, and observe that
g_{i}
is uniformly continuous for each
i\in I_{g}
. Since
I_{g}
is finite, there exists some
0<\overline{t}\leq 1
such that

\left|g_{i}(s+t)-g_{i}(s)\right|<\frac{\epsilon}{2\sum_{i\in I_{g}}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{i}(s)ds}

for all
s\in[0,1]
,
0<t\leq\overline{t}
and
i\in I_{g}
. Then, for
0<t\leq\overline{t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}\int_{0}^{1-t}\left|g_{i}(s+t)-g_{i}(s)\right|\rho_{i}(s)ds=\sum_{i\in I_{g}}\int_{0}^{1-t}\left|g_{i}(s+t)-g_{i}(s)\right|\rho_{i}(s)ds<\epsilon/2.

On the other hand, there exists some
0<t_{0}\leq\overline{t}
such that for all
t\leq t_{0}
,

Me^{wt}\sum_{i\in I}\int_{0}^{t}|g_{i}(s)|\rho_{i}(s)ds=Me^{wt}\sum_{i\in I_{g}}\int_{0}^{t}|g_{i}(s)|\rho_{i}(s)ds<\epsilon/4

and also

3.
Hypercyclicity for the left translation semigroup on directed metric trees

In this section we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for a
p
-admissible weight sequence
(\rho_{i})_{i\in I}
that ensure hypercyclicity for the translation semigroup on directed metric trees. We begin by observing that,
if the directed tree
G
has a leaf, then the left translation semigroup cannot be hypercyclic in any admissible space
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
.
Indeed, let
f\in L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
and
e=(v_{i_{0}},v_{j_{0}})\in E
be such that
v_{j_{0}}
is a leaf of
G
. Then it is clear that
(T_{t}f)_{e}(s)=0
for all
t>1
and
0\leq s\leq 1
, and therefore the semigroup cannot be hypercyclic.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
L(G)
be the directed metric tree associated with
G=(V,E)

L(G)
is rooted and that
G
is without leaves.

(a)

The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

the translation semigroup is hypercyclic on
L_{\rho}^{1}(L(G))
;

(ii)

the translation semigroup is weakly mixing on
L_{\rho}^{1}(L(G))
;

(iii)

there exists some increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
i\in I

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{j\in M_{n_{k}}(i)}\inf_{s\in[0,1]}\rho_{j}(s)=0.

(b)

If
1<p<\infty
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

the translation semigroup is hypercyclic on
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G))
;

(ii)

the translation semigroup is weakly mixing on
L^{p}_{\rho}(L(G)
;

(iii)

there exists some increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{s\in[0,1]}\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{k}}(i)}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(s)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)^{-1}=0.

Proof.

We will only provide the proof of assertion (b), as the proof of (a) is analogous.

The equivalence of
(ii)
and
(i)
follows from the fact that
F_{\rho}^{p}
is a dense subspace such that the orbit of each

is bounded (see
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]

We will prove that
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
, by showing that given
\epsilon>0
, and

, there is some

and

such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{K_{n_{h}-1}(i)}(s_{i,n_{h}})}+\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}-1}(K_{n_{h}-1}(i))}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(s_{i,n_{h}})^{1/(p-1)}}\right)>\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}.

and, for every

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{K_{n_{h}-1}(i)}(s_{i,n_{h}})}+\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(i))}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(s_{i,n_{h}})^{1/(p-1)}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{K_{n_{h}-1}(i)}(s_{i,n_{h}})}+\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}-1}(K_{n_{h}-1}(i))}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(s_{i,n_{h}})^{1/(p-1)}}\right)>\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}.

Then, for every

\frac{1}{\rho_{K_{n_{h}-1}(i)}(s_{i,n_{h}})}>\frac{1}{2\delta_{1}}\qquad\text{or}\ \left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(i))}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(s_{i,n_{h}})^{1/(p-1)}}\right)>\frac{1}{2\delta_{1}},

hence we can write

where

\displaystyle J_{1}:=\left\{i\in I_{1}:\frac{1}{\rho_{K_{n_{h}-1}(i)}(s_{i,n_{h}})}>\frac{1}{2\delta_{1}}\right\},

\displaystyle J_{2}:=\left\{i\in I_{1}:\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(i))}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(s_{i,n_{h}})^{1/(p-1)}}\right)>\frac{1}{2\delta_{1}}\right\}.

By Lemma

, for each

,

such that

On the other hand, for every
i\in I_{2}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}+1}(i)}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(t_{i,n_{h}})^{1/(p-1)}}\right)\geq\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(k_{i})}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(t_{i,n_{h}})^{1/(p-1)}}\right)>\frac{1}{\delta_{2}}.

Consequently, as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we can prove that for all
i\in I_{2}
we have:

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}(i)}}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(s)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)^{1-p}\leq Me^{2|w|}\left(\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}+1}(i)}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}(t_{i,n_{h}})^{1/(p-1)}}\right)^{1-p}<Me^{2|w|}\delta_{2}^{p-1}.

Again, using Lemma
2.1
, for each
i\in I_{2}
there exists a family of positive measurable functions
(\nu^{i}_{j}(s))_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(i)}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(i)}\nu^{i}_{j}(s)=1,\qquad

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(i)}|\nu^{i}_{j}(s)|^{p}\rho_{j}(s)<Me^{2|w|}\delta_{2}^{p-1}

Now, define the function

such that

g_{i}(s):=\begin{cases}(f_{1})_{i}(s)\hskip 60.00009pt&\text{ if }\hskip 1.00006pti\in{J_{1}};\\
(f_{1})_{i}(s)(1-u_{i}^{i})&\text{ if }i\in J_{2}\setminus J_{1};\\
-(f_{1})_{l}(s)u_{i}^{l}\hskip 31.0001pt&\text{ if }i\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(l))\text{ for some }l\in J_{2}\setminus J_{1}\text{ and }i\notin I_{1},;\\
(f_{2})_{l}(s)\nu^{l}_{i}(s)\hskip 40.00006pt&\text{ if }i\in M_{n_{h}}(l)\text{ for some }l\in I_{2};\\
0\hskip 88.00015pt&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

If
l\in J_{1}
and
j\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(l))
, then, by (
3.13
) and (
1.1
),
j\notin M_{n_{h}}(I_{2})
and

,

(3.14)

(T_{n_{h}}g)_{K_{n_{h}}(l)}(s)=\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(l))}g_{j}(s)=(f_{1})_{l}(s).

If

, then for every

it holds that
j\notin M_{n_{h}}(I_{2})

(T_{n_{h}}g)_{K_{n_{h}}(l)}(s)=\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(l))}g_{j}(s)=(f_{1})_{l}(s)-\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(l))}(f_{1})_{l}(s)u_{j}^{l}=0.

(3.16)

Finally, if

, then

has empty intersection with the support of

, hence

.

Observe further that the admissibility condition (
2.6
) yields that for all

and

\rho_{K_{n_{h}}(l)}(s)\leq Me^{w(s_{l,n_{h}}+1-s)}\rho_{K_{n_{h}-1}(l)}(s_{l,n_{0}})\leq Me^{2|w|}\rho_{K_{n_{h}-1}(l)}(s_{l,n_{h}})<2Me^{2|w|}\delta_{1}.

By (

), (

) and (

) we have:

\displaystyle\|T_{n_{h}}g-f_{2}\|_{p,\rho}^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{l\in J_{1}}|(f_{1})_{l}(s)|^{p}\rho_{K_{n_{h}}(l)}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq Me^{2|w|}\delta_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{l\in J_{1}}|(f_{1})_{l}(s)|^{p}ds<Me^{2|w|}\frac{\delta_{1}}{C_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{l\in J_{1}}|f^{1}_{l}(s)|^{p}\rho_{l}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in I_{2}}\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(l)}\int_{0}^{1}|(f_{2})_{l}(s)\nu_{j}^{l}(s)|^{p}\rho_{j}(s)ds

\displaystyle\hskip 80.00012pt+\sum_{l\in J_{2}\setminus J_{1}}\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(K_{n_{h}}(l))}\int_{0}^{1}|(f_{1})_{l}(s)u_{j}^{l}|^{p}\rho_{j}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in I_{2}}\int_{0}^{1}|(f_{2})_{l}(s)|^{p}\sum_{j\in M_{n_{h}}(l)}|\nu_{j}^{l}(s)|^{p}\rho_{j}(s)ds+

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M}{C_{1}}e^{2|w|}\delta_{2}^{p-1}||f^{2}||_{p,\rho}^{p}+(2\delta_{1})^{p-1}\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}^{2}}\|f^{1}\|^{p}_{p,\rho}.

and

Diffusive Stochastic Master Equation (SME) with dispersive qubit/cavity coupling
Pierre Rouchon

Abstract




Abstract

A detailed analysis of the diffusive Stochastic Master Equation (SME) for qubit/cavity systems with dispersive coupling is provided. This analysis incorporates classical input signals and output signals (measurement outcomes through homodyne detection). The dynamics of the qubit/cavity density operator is shown to converge exponentially towards a slow invariant manifold, parameterized via a time-varying deterministic Kraus map by the density operator of a fictitious qubit. This fictitious qubit is governed by a SME incorporating the classical input/output signals. Extension where the qubit is replaced by any qudit dispersively coupled to an arbitrary set of modes with collective input/output classical signals.

The qubit/cavity case

Following
[
2
,
1
]
, one starts with the following SME for the density operator
\rho
of the qubit/cavity system

d\rho=\left(-i\left[\sqrt{\kappa}u\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*}\boldsymbol{a}+\chi\boldsymbol{\sigma_{\!z}}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a},~\rho\right]+\kappa\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\rho)\right)~dt+\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\rho)~dw

(1)

where the classical input signal is
u(t)\in\mathbb{C}
(cavity coherent drive) and the classical measurement outcome signal
y
obeys to

dy_{t}=\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{a}\rho+\rho\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\right)dt+dw_{t}
(homodyne detection).
Here
\boldsymbol{a}
is the photon annihilation operator,
\boldsymbol{\sigma_{\!z}}
the Pauli operator associated to the qubit energy, and
dw
is a classical Wiener process,

\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{L}}(\rho)\equiv\boldsymbol{L}\rho\boldsymbol{L}^{\dagger}-(\boldsymbol{L}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{L}\rho+\rho\boldsymbol{L}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{L})/2
and
\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{L}}(\rho)\equiv\boldsymbol{L}\rho+\rho\boldsymbol{L}^{\dagger}-\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{L}\rho+\rho\boldsymbol{L}^{\dagger}\right)\rho
. The real parameters are
\chi
,
\kappa>0
and
\eta\in[0,1]
.

As in
[
2
]
, we consider the following change of frame

\rho=\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{g}\boldsymbol{D}_{\alpha_{g}}+\boldsymbol{P}_{e}\boldsymbol{D}_{\alpha_{e}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{g}\boldsymbol{D}_{-\alpha_{g}}+\boldsymbol{P}_{e}\boldsymbol{D}_{-\alpha_{e}}\right)

with
\alpha_{g}
,
\alpha_{e}
being time-varying complex signals,
\boldsymbol{P}_{g}=\left|g\right\rangle\left\langle g\right|
,
\boldsymbol{P}_{e}=\left|e\right\rangle\left\langle e\right|
. The unitary displacement of complex amplitude
\alpha
corresponds to
\boldsymbol{D}_{\alpha}=e^{\alpha\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}\boldsymbol{a}}
.
With

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\alpha_{g}

\displaystyle=i\chi\alpha_{g}-i\sqrt{\kappa}u-\tfrac{\kappa}{2}\alpha_{g}

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\alpha_{e}

\displaystyle=-i\chi\alpha_{e}-i\sqrt{\kappa}u-\tfrac{\kappa}{2}\alpha_{e}

(3)

one gets after some simple but tedious calculations

d\boldsymbol{\xi}=-i\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{g}\left(\Re(\alpha_{g}\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*})-\chi\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}+\tfrac{i\kappa}{2}(\alpha_{g}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{g}^{*}\boldsymbol{a})\right)~,~\boldsymbol{\xi}\right]~dt\\
-i\left[\boldsymbol{P}_{e}\left(\Re(\alpha_{e}\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*})+\chi\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}+\tfrac{i\kappa}{2}(\alpha_{e}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{e}^{*}\boldsymbol{a})\right)~,~\boldsymbol{\xi}\right]~dt\\
+\kappa\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{a}+\alpha_{g}\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\alpha_{e}\boldsymbol{P}_{e}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})~dt+\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{a}+\alpha_{g}\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\alpha_{e}\boldsymbol{P}_{e}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})~dw

(4)

with
dy=\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\rm{Tr}\left((\boldsymbol{a}+\alpha_{g}\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\alpha_{e}\boldsymbol{P}_{e})\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{\xi}(\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}+\alpha_{g}^{*}\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\alpha_{e}^{*}\boldsymbol{P}_{e})\right)~dt+dw
.

We have the following lemma not explicitly stated in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Assume the following initial condition
\boldsymbol{\xi}(0)=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}^{0}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|
. Then for any time
t>0
,
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(t)\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|
with
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}
being governed by the following standard SME

d\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}=-i\left[\Re(\alpha_{g}\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*})\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\Re(\alpha_{e}\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*})\boldsymbol{P}_{e}~,~\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}\right]~dt+\kappa\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{g}\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\alpha_{e}\boldsymbol{P}_{e}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q})~dt\\
+\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{g}\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\alpha_{e}\boldsymbol{P}_{e}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q})~dw

(5)

with output

dy=\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\rm{Tr}\left((\alpha_{g}\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\alpha_{e}\boldsymbol{P}_{e})\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}+\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(\alpha_{g}^{*}\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+\alpha_{e}^{*}\boldsymbol{P}_{e})\right)~dt+dw
and starting from
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(0)=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}^{0}
.

This lemma means that the set
\mathcal{I}_{Q}=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|\}
where
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}
is any qubit density operator, is invariant for any realisation of (
4
). We have this second lemma showing the convergence of (
4
) towards
\mathcal{I}_{Q}=\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}~|~\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)=0\right\}
.

Lemma 2
.

Any solution of (
4
) converges exponentially and almost surely towards the invariant set
\mathcal{I}_{Q}
since

\frac{d}{dt}\text{\large$\mathbb{E}$}\left(\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)\right)=-\kappa~\text{\large$\mathbb{E}$}\left(\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)\right).

Thus to describe the classical input
u
and classical output
y
dynamics when the initial qubit/cavity is
\rho(0)=\rho_{Q}^{0}\otimes\left|\alpha^{0}\right\rangle\!\left\langle\alpha^{0}\right|
(
\alpha^{0}\in\mathbb{C}
arbitrary), we have a state space representation (analogue of a Kalman input/output representation). It is
based on (
2
) and (
3
) with the common initial condition
\alpha^{0}
and on (
5
) with initial condition
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(0)=\rho_{Q}^{0}
. The qubit/cavity density operator
\rho
at time
t
is then given by the above unitary transformation, seen here as an output map depending only on the state
(\alpha_{g},\alpha_{e},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q})
at time
t
:

\rho(t)=\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{g}\boldsymbol{D}_{\alpha_{g}(t)}+\boldsymbol{P}_{e}\boldsymbol{D}_{\alpha_{e}(t)}\right)(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(t)\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|)\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{g}\boldsymbol{D}_{-\alpha_{g}(t)}+\boldsymbol{P}_{e}\boldsymbol{D}_{-\alpha_{e}(t)}\right).

A partial trace versus the qubit gives the non-Markovian evolution of
\rho_{C}=\rm{Tr}_{\text{\tiny qubit}}\left(\rho\right)
via the following cavity output map

\rho_{C}(t)=\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{g}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(t)\right)\left|\alpha_{g}(t)\right\rangle\!\left\langle\alpha_{g}(t)\right|+\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{e}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(t)\right)\left|\alpha_{e}(t)\right\rangle\!\left\langle\alpha_{e}(t)\right|

A partial trace versus the cavity gives the non-Markovian evolution of
\rho_{Q}=\rm{Tr}_{\text{\tiny cavity}}\left(\rho\right)
via the following qubit output map

\rho_{Q}(t)=(\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+c_{ge}(t)\boldsymbol{P}_{e})\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(t)(\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+c_{ge}^{*}(t)\boldsymbol{P}_{e})+(1-|c_{ge}(t)|^{2})\boldsymbol{P}_{e}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}(t)\boldsymbol{P}_{e}

where
c_{ge}=\left\langle\alpha_{g}\right|\left.\alpha_{e}\right\rangle=e^{-\tfrac{|\alpha_{g}|^{2}+|\alpha_{e}|^{2}}{2}+\alpha_{g}^{*}\alpha_{e}}
.
Notice that this qubit output map is just a Kraus map from
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}
to
\rho_{Q}
with two time-varying Kraus operators

\boldsymbol{P}_{g}+c_{ge}(t)\boldsymbol{P}_{e}
and
\sqrt{1-|c_{ge}(t)|^{2}}\boldsymbol{P}_{e}
.

Such a formulation of the dynamics avoids for
\rho_{Q}
a SME description with detection efficiencies greater than one during some time intervals. Here
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}
obeys to a standard stochastic master equation with a constant detection efficiency
\eta\in[0,1]
.

For the
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}
dynamics, one can exploit the positivity preserving integration scheme proposed in
[
3
,
4
]
for quantum filtering or Monte-Carlo quantum trajectories.

Qudit extension

This extension is inspired from
[
1
]
. It can be seen as a state-space reformulation combining usual diffusive SME with constant detection efficiency, linear deterministic differential equations and Kraus maps to represent the non-Markovian evolution of the density operator attached to each subsystem.

The above developments generalize when the qubit
Q
is replaced by a finite dimensional system
\mathcal{S}
of Hilbert basis
(\left|s\right\rangle)_{s=1,\ldots,n_{\mathcal{S}}}
with

d\rho=\left(-i\left[\sqrt{\kappa}u\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*}\boldsymbol{a}+\left(\sum_{s}\chi_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\right)\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a},~\rho\right]+\kappa\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\rho)\right)~dt+\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\rho)~dw

where
\boldsymbol{P}_{s}=\left|s\right\rangle\!\left\langle s\right|
and
\chi_{s}
real parameter.
With coherent amplitudes
\alpha_{s}
given by

\frac{d}{dt}\alpha_{s}=-i\chi_{s}\alpha_{s}-i\sqrt{\kappa}u-\tfrac{\kappa}{2}\alpha_{s}

and unitary transformation

\rho=\left(\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\boldsymbol{D}_{\alpha_{s}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}\left(\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\boldsymbol{D}_{-\alpha_{s}}\right)

one gets

d\boldsymbol{\xi}=-i\left[\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\left(\Re(\alpha_{s}\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*})+\chi_{s}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}+\tfrac{i\kappa}{2}(\alpha_{s}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{s}^{*}\boldsymbol{a})\right)~,~\boldsymbol{\xi}\right]~dt\\
+\kappa\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{a}+\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})~dt+\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{a}+\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\xi})~dw

(6)

with
dy=\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\rm{Tr}\left(\left(\boldsymbol{a}+\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{\xi}\left(\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}+\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}^{*}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\right)\right)~dt+dw
.

Then lemma
1
extends to

Lemma 3
.

Assume the following initial condition
\boldsymbol{\xi}(0)=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}^{0}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|
. Then for any time
t>0
,
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}(t)\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|
with
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}
being governed by the following standard SME

d\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}=-i\left[\sum_{s}\Re(\alpha_{s}\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*})\boldsymbol{P}_{s}~,~\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}\right]~dt+\kappa\mathcal{D}_{\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}})~dt+\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\mathcal{M}_{\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}})~dw

with output

dy=\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\rm{Tr}\left(\left(\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}+\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}^{*}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\right)\right)~dt+dw
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}(0)=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}^{0}
.

We have this second lemma showing the convergence of (
6
) towards
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{S}}=\left\{\boldsymbol{\xi}~|~\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)=0\right\}
.

Lemma 4
.

Any solution of (
6
) converges exponentially and almost surely towards the invariant set
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{S}}=\{\xi_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|\}
where
\xi_{\mathcal{S}}
is any density operator on
{\mathcal{S}}
. More precisely we have

\frac{d}{dt}\text{\large$\mathbb{E}$}\left(\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)\right)=-\kappa~\text{\large$\mathbb{E}$}\left(\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{\xi}\right)\right).

The state space input/output description when the initial system/cavity is
\rho(0)=\rho_{\mathcal{S}}^{0}\otimes\left|\alpha^{0}\right\rangle\!\left\langle\alpha^{0}\right|
(
\alpha^{0}\in\mathbb{C}
arbitrary) is given by
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}
with initial condition
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}^{0}
and by
(\alpha_{s})_{s=1,\ldots,n_{\mathcal{S}}}
with the common initial condition
\alpha^{0}
.
The partial trace versus the system gives the non-Markovian evolution of
\rho_{C}=\rm{Tr}_{\text{\tiny system}}\left(\rho\right)
via the following cavity output map

\rho_{C}(t)=\sum_{s}\rm{Tr}\left(\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}(t)\right)\left|\alpha_{s}(t)\right\rangle\!\left\langle\alpha_{s}(t)\right|

The partial trace versus the cavity gives the non-Markovian evolution of
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}=\rm{Tr}_{\text{\tiny cavity}}\left(\rho\right)
via the following output map

\rho_{\mathcal{S}}(t)=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}c_{s^{\prime}s}(t)\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}(t)\boldsymbol{P}_{s^{\prime}}

where
c_{s^{\prime}s}=\left\langle\alpha_{s^{\prime}}\right|\left.\alpha_{s}\right\rangle
. Since the square matrix
C=(c_{s^{\prime},s})_{1\leq s^{\prime},s\leq n_{C}}
is the Gram matrix of the
n_{C}
states
\left|\alpha_{s}\right\rangle
, it is Hermitian and non negative. Denote by
r_{s^{\prime},s}
the entries of its square root
\sqrt{C}=(r_{s^{\prime},s})_{1\leq s^{\prime},s\leq n_{C}}
. One gets then a usual Kraus map formulation with
n_{C}
operators
\boldsymbol{K}_{s}
:

\rho_{\mathcal{S}}(t)=\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{K}_{s}(t)\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}(t)\boldsymbol{K}_{s}^{\dagger}(t)\quad\text{where}\quad\boldsymbol{K}_{s}(t)=\sum_{s^{\prime}}r_{s,s^{\prime}}(t)\boldsymbol{P}_{s^{\prime}}.

Notice that, by construction
r_{s,s^{\prime}}^{*}\equiv r_{s^{\prime},s}
and
\sum_{s^{\prime}}r_{s,s^{\prime}}r_{s^{\prime},s}=\sum_{s^{\prime}}|r_{s,s^{\prime}}|^{2}=1
.

Multi-cavity extension

This extension is also inspired from
[
1
]
.
The above developments generalize when cavity mode
\boldsymbol{a}
is replaced by
n_{C}
modes labelled via
c=1,\ldots,n_{C}
with annihilation operator
\boldsymbol{a}_{c}
. The system
\mathcal{S}
lives in the Hilbert space of basis
(\left|s\right\rangle)_{s=1,\ldots,n_{\mathcal{S}}}
. With dispersive coupling between
\mathcal{S}
and these
n_{C}
modes, with the collective coherent input
u
and with the collective homodyne detection operator
\boldsymbol{a}_{out}=\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}
, one starts from the following SME,

d\rho=\left(-i\left[\sum_{c}\left(\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}u\boldsymbol{a}_{c}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}u^{*}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}+\left(\Delta_{c}+\sum_{s}\chi_{s,c}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\right)\boldsymbol{a}_{c}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}\right),~\rho\right]\right)~dt\\
+\mathcal{D}_{\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}}(\rho)~dt+\sqrt{\eta}\mathcal{M}_{\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}}(\rho)~dw

where
\boldsymbol{P}_{s}=\left|s\right\rangle\!\left\langle s\right|
and
\kappa_{c}>0
,
\Delta_{c}
,
\chi_{s,c}
,
\eta\in[0,1]
real parameters.
Consider coherent amplitudes
\alpha_{s,c}
given by

\frac{d}{dt}\alpha_{s,c}=-i(\Delta_{c}+\chi_{s,c})\alpha_{s,c}-i\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}u-\sum_{c^{\prime}}\tfrac{\sqrt{\kappa_{c}\kappa_{c^{\prime}}}}{2}\alpha_{s,c^{\prime}}

(7)

and the unitary transformation

\rho=\left(\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\boldsymbol{D}_{\alpha_{s}}\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}\left(\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\boldsymbol{D}_{-\alpha_{s}}\right)

where
\alpha_{s}=(\alpha_{s,1},\ldots,\alpha_{s,n_{C}})
and

\boldsymbol{D}_{\alpha_{s}}=\exp\left(\sum_{c}\alpha_{s,c}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}^{\dagger}-\alpha_{s,c}^{*}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}\right)
.

With

\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}\overline{\alpha}_{s}=\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\alpha_{s,c}\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}=\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}

one gets

d\boldsymbol{\xi}=-i\left[\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\left(\Re(\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}\overline{\alpha}_{s}u^{*})+\sum_{c}(\Delta_{c}+\chi_{s,c})\boldsymbol{a}_{c}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}+\tfrac{i\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}\kappa_{c}}}{2}(\overline{\alpha}_{s}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}^{\dagger}-\overline{\alpha}_{s}^{*}\boldsymbol{a}_{c})\right)~,~\boldsymbol{\xi}\right]~dt\\
+\mathcal{D}_{\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\left(\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}\overline{\alpha}_{s}+\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}\right)}(\boldsymbol{\xi})~dt+\sqrt{\eta}\mathcal{M}_{\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\left(\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}\overline{\alpha}_{s}+\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}\right)}(\boldsymbol{\xi})~dw

(8)

with

dy=\sqrt{\eta}\rm{Tr}\left(\left(\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\left(\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}\overline{\alpha}_{s}+\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}\right)\right)\boldsymbol{\xi}+\boldsymbol{\xi}\left(\sum_{s}\boldsymbol{P}_{s}\left(\sqrt{\bar{\kappa}}\overline{\alpha}_{s}^{*}+\sum_{c}\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\boldsymbol{a}_{c}^{\dagger}\right)\right)\right)~dt+dw.

3
extends to

Lemma 5
.

Assume the following initial condition
\boldsymbol{\xi}(0)=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}^{0}\otimes\left(\otimes_{c}\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|\right)

t>0
,
\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)=\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}(t)\otimes\left(\otimes_{c}\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|\right)

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\mathcal{S}}

Lemma 6
.

Any solution of (

Concluding remark

It will be interesting to investigate the qubit/cavity system governed by (
1
) but with a finite life time
T_{1}
for the qubit:

d\rho=\left(-i\left[\sqrt{\kappa}u\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{\kappa}u^{*}\boldsymbol{a}+\chi\boldsymbol{\sigma_{\!z}}\boldsymbol{a}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{a},~\rho\right]+\kappa\mathcal{D}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\rho)+\tfrac{1}{T_{1}}\mathcal{D}_{\left|g\right\rangle\!\left\langle e\right|}(\rho)\right)~dt+\sqrt{\eta\kappa}\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(\rho)~dw

When
\kappa\gg\tfrac{1}{T_{1}}
, an approximate formulation might be possible via perturbation techniques. It might be based on a usual diffusive SME for a fictitious qubit of state
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{Q}
, on deterministic ordinary differential equations and on Kraus maps to approximate the non-Markovian evolution of the density operators attached to the cavity and to the qubit.

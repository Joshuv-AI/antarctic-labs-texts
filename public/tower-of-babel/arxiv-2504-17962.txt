Tamagawa numbers and positive rank of elliptic curves
Edwina Aylward

Abstract
. This paper addresses the prediction of positive rank for elliptic curves without the need to find a point of infinite order or compute L-functions. While the most common method relies on parity conjectures, a recent technique introduced by Dokchitser, Wiersema, and Evans predicts positive rank based on the value of a certain product of Tamagawa numbers, raising questions about its relationship to parity. We show that their method is a subset of the parity conjectures approach: whenever their method predicts positive rank, so does the use of parity conjectures. To establish this, we extend previous work on Brauer relations and regulator constants to a broader setting involving combinations of permutation modules known as K-relations. A central ingredient in our argument is demonstrating a compatibility between Tamagawa numbers and local root numbers.

Primary: 11G05, Secondary: 11G07, 11G40

1.
Introduction

Determining the Mordell-Weil rank of an elliptic curve is a notoriously difficult task.
As such, the parity conjecture serves as a powerful tool for predicting rank behaviour. Associated to an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
L
is its global root number
w(E/L)\in\{\pm 1\}
.
The parity conjecture states that this root number determines the parity of the rank of
E/L
:

w(E/L)=(-1)^{\rk E/L}.

Since root numbers can be computed explicitly from local arithmetic data (see
[
7
, §2]
), they offer a practical method for analysing rank behaviour. The use of root number computations and parity conjectures to predict positive rank is often referred to as a
parity test
.

For example, if
E/L
has semistable reduction, then by
[
7
, Corollary 2.5]
,

w(E/L)=(-1)^{m+s},

where
m
is the number of places of
L
at which
E
has split multiplicative reduction, and
s
is the number of infinite places of
L
. Thus the parity conjecture predicts that
any semistable rational elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with an even number of places of split multiplicative reduction must have positive (odd) rank. No other approach obtains this result as effortlessly.

In
[
12
]
, Dokchitser, Evans, and Wiersema introduce a novel method for deducing that a rational elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
has positive rank over a Galois extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
, based on expected properties of
L
-functions.
This is reminiscent of using root number computations to predict positive rank, as their method also involves local arithmetic data.
To use this method, one computes a product of local data consisting of Tamagawa numbers and other local factors, and checks whether it is a norm from a specified quadratic field. If it is not, then
E
must have positive rank over
F
.
For the purpose of comparison, we call using this method to test for positive rank a
norm relations test
.

A precise formulation of the norm relations test is given below in Conjecture
1.3
. We first give an example of its use.

Example 1.1
.

Consider a Galois extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
with
\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})=D_{21}
(the dihedral group of order
42
). Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a semistable elliptic curve. For
H\leq G
, let
c(E/F^{H})=\prod_{v}c_{v}(E/F^{H})
be the product of local Tamagawa numbers of
E/F^{H}
at all finite places. According to the norm relations test, if

\frac{c(E/F^{C_{2}})\cdot c(E/\mathbb{Q})}{c(E/F^{D_{7}})\cdot c(E/F^{S_{3}})}

is not the norm of an element of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{21})
, then
\rk E/F>0
.
For instance, let
p
be a prime with residue degree
2
and ramification degree
3
in
F
. If
E/\mathbb{Q}
is a semistable elliptic curve with split multiplicative reduction at
p
and good reduction at all other ramified primes in
F
, then the product of Tamagawa numbers is not a norm and so the test predicts that
\rk E/F>0
.

A natural question concerns the precise relationship between the norm relations test and parity tests. One might ask, for instance, whether there exists an example in which the norm relations test predicts positive rank while root number computations fail to do so. In

[
12
, Remark 11]
, the authors reported that they were unable to identify such an example. The principal result of this paper establishes that no such example can exist: whenever the norm relations test predicts positive rank, parity tests necessarily yield the same prediction. This confirms the empirical observation in
[
12
]
and demonstrates that the scope of the norm relations test does not extend beyond that of parity-based methods.

To make this precise, we require twisted root numbers. If
F/L
is a Galois extension and
E/L
an elliptic curve, then
E(F)_{\mathbb{C}}:=E(F)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
is a complex representation of
G=\Gal(F/L)
. For a self-dual representation
\rho
of
G
, there is an associated twisted root number
w(E/L,\rho)\in\{\pm 1\}
, whose construction is given in
[
17
, §3 - §4]
. Basic properties of twisted root numbers can be found in
[
10
, Appendix A]
, and are also recalled in Section 4 of this paper (see Remark
4.2
). In particular, these satisfy multiplicativity:
w(E/L,\rho\oplus\rho^{\prime})=w(E/L,\rho)w(E/L,\rho^{\prime})
for self-dual representations
\rho
,
\rho^{\prime}
, and
w(E/L,\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathbbm{1})=w(E/F^{H})
for any subgroup
H\leq G
.
The parity conjecture for twists relates this root number to the multiplicity of
\rho
as a sub-representation of
E(F)_{\mathbb{C}}
, stating that

w(E/L,\rho)=(-1)^{\langle\rho,E(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual inner-product of representations.
In particular, if
w(E/L,\rho)=-1
then
\rho
appears as a subrepresentation of
E(F)_{\mathbb{C}}
and so
\rk E/F>0
.

The relation we establish between the norm relations test and root number computations is as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.4
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with semistable reduction at
2
and
3
,
F/\mathbb{Q}
a Galois extension. Suppose that the norm relations test predicts that
\rk E/F>0
. Then there exists an irreducible orthogonal representation
\rho
of
\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})
such that
w(E/\mathbb{Q},\rho)=-1
.

For example, consider an odd-order Galois extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
, and an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
satisfying the conditions of the theorem. Since the trivial representation is the only self-dual irreducible representation of
\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})
, one has
w(E/\mathbb{Q})=w(E/F)
(cf.
[
10
, Proposition A.2]
). Thus parity tests cannot predict rank growth, and accordingly, the norm relations test cannot predict that
\rk E/F>0
(Corollary
4.6
).

1.1.
Norm relations test

The norm relations test, introduced in
[
12
, §3]
, is based on the conjectural properties of the
Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer quotient
associated to an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
F
:

\BSD(E/F):=\frac{\Reg_{E/F}|\text{\tencyr Sh}_{E/F}|C_{E/F}}{|E(F)_{\tors}|^{2}},

where it is assumed that the Tate-Shafarevich group
\text{\tencyr Sh}_{E/F}
has finite order. This quotient appears as a factor in the conjectural formula for the leading coefficient of the Taylor expansion of the
L
-function
L(E/F,s)
at
s=1
(the missing terms being the periods and
\sqrt{\Delta_{F}}
).
Here,
C_{E/F}
denotes the product of Tamagawa numbers and other local fudge factors at finite places of additive reduction (defined in §
1.5
). Given an elliptic curve, these are relatively straight-forward to compute, and are the terms one computes in the norm relations test.

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension with
G=\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})
, and
\rho
an irreducible representation of
G
. Write
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)
for the field generated by the values of the character of
\rho
. For
\sigma\in\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q})
, let
\rho^{\sigma}
be the representation of
G
with
\Tr\rho^{\sigma}(g)=\sigma(\Tr\rho(g))
for all
g\in G
. The norm relations test is as follows:

Conjecture 1.3

(Norm relations test,
[
12
, Conjecture 4, Theorem 33]
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
,
F/\mathbb{Q}
a finite Galois extension with
G=\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})
, and
\rho
an irreducible representation of
G
.
Let

\bigg(\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q})}\rho^{\sigma}\bigg)^{\oplus m}=\bigg(\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]\bigg)\ominus\bigg(\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{j}^{\prime}]\bigg)

for some
m\geq 1
and
H_{i}
,
H_{j}^{\prime}\leq G
. If either

\prod_{i}C_{E/F^{H_{i}}}\big/\prod_{j}C_{E/F^{H_{j}^{\prime}}}

is not a norm from a quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})\subset\mathbb{Q}(\rho)
(when such a subfield exists), or is not a rational square when
m
is even, then
E
has a point of infinite order over
F
.

By
[
12
, Theorem 5]
, Conjecture
1.3
is true
assuming the analytic continuation of the
L
-functions
L(E,\tau,s)
for every representation
\tau
of
G
, their functional equation, the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, and Deligne’s period conjecture. We show that it also follows from the parity conjecture for twists, removing the dependence of the result on
L
-functions.

Theorem 1.4

(Corollary
4.5
)
.

Conjecture
1.3
holds with
\mathbb{Q}
replaced by an arbitrary number field
L
for all elliptic curves
E/L
with semi-stable reduction at primes above
2
and
3
and all Galois extensions
F/L
, assuming the parity conjecture for twists holds for all self-dual representations of
\Gal(F/L)
.

Example 1.5
.

We revisit Example
1.1
, where
G=\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})=D_{21}
.
Let
\rho_{21}
be a faithful irreducible representation of
G
. Then
\mathbb{Q}(\rho_{21})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{21}+\zeta_{21}^{-1})
with a unique quadratic subfield
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{21})\subset\mathbb{Q}(\rho_{21})
. The sum of
\rho_{21}
and its Galois conjugates forms a rational irreducible representation
\sigma_{21}
, which satisfies

\sigma_{21}=\mathbb{C}[G/C_{2}]\ominus\mathbb{C}[G/D_{7}]\ominus\mathbb{C}[G/S_{3}]\oplus\mathbb{C}[G/G].

Therefore the norm relations test predicts that if

\frac{C_{E/F^{C_{2}}}\cdot C_{E/\mathbb{Q}}}{C_{E/F^{D_{7}}}\cdot C_{E/F^{S_{3}}}}

is not the norm of an element in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{21})
, then
E
has positive rank over
F
.

1.2.
Tamagawa numbers as regulator constants

Using the notation of Conjecture
1.3
, the primary focus of this paper is in expressing

\prod_{i}C_{E/F^{H_{i}}}\Big/\prod_{j}C_{E/F^{H_{j}^{\prime}}}\mod N_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})^{\times})

as a product of group-theoretic invariants known as
regulator constants
, with exponents determined by twisted root numbers. When this product fails to be a norm from
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
, this implies that there is a twisted root number equal to
-1
, yielding Theorem
1.2
.
This provides a stronger conclusion than Conjecture
1.3
, which only conjectures that

should be positive.

Let
G
be a finite group and consider the formal sum of subgroups
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}

(

\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]^{\oplus n_{i}}=\rho\oplus\rho^{\sigma}

where
\rho

quadratic and
\langle\sigma\rangle=\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q})

of

one can associate the regulator constant
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)\mod N_{\mathbb{Q}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\rho)^{\times})
is independent of all choices (these choices and further details on regulator constants are discussed in §

1.3.
K-relations and regulator constants

To conclude the introduction, we highlight the representation-theoretic structures that underpin our work.

1.3.1.
K-relations and functions on subgroups

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension. A formal sum
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}
of subgroups
H_{i}\leq G
with
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
is called a
K
-relation for
G
if there exists a representation
\rho
of
G
with
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)\subset K
and an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]^{\oplus n_{i}}\ =\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})}\rho^{\sigma},

as virtual representations. Note that the isomorphism still holds if any
H_{i}
is replaced by a conjugate subgroup. By Lemma
2.5
, if
\Theta
is a
K
-relation for
G
, it is also a
K^{\prime}
-relation for all subfields
K^{\prime}\subset K
with
K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
Galois.
The combination of subgroups appearing in the norm relations test is a
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)
-relation for
G
where
\rho
is some irreducible representation of
G
, as well as a
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
-relation for every quadratic subfield
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})\subset\mathbb{Q}(\rho)
(when such a subfield exists).

In the case where

\rho=0
, the relation reduces to

\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]^{\oplus n_{i}}=0,

which is known as a
Brauer relation
. Identifying subgroups of
G
(up to conjugacy) with finite transitive
G
-sets, Brauer relations can be considered as differences
X-Y
of non-isomorphic
G
-sets
X
,
Y
with
\mathbb{C}[X]=\mathbb{C}[Y]
.
Brauer relations have many uses in number theory.
For instance, they have been used to study class numbers and unit groups
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
.
In the context of elliptic curves, they have been used by the Dokchitser brothers
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
to deduce the
parity conjecture for elliptic curves
E/L
, assuming finiteness of the
2
- and
3
-primary parts of
\text{\tencyr Sh}_{E/L(E[2])}
. In
[
10
]
, similar ideas are used to deduce the
p
-parity conjecture for twists of certain representations. We will adopt a very similar approach to
[
10
]
in
applying
K
-relations to study rank predictions via the norm relations test.

To reinterpret the computation of invariants over products of subfields associated with a given relation, we study functions defined on subgroups of
G
.
Consider a function
\phi
defined on subgroups
H\leq G
that maps to some abelian group
\mathcal{A}
(written multiplicatively) and satisfies
\phi(H)=\phi(H^{\prime})
for conjugate subgroups
H
,
H^{\prime}
. Then
\phi
may also be viewed as a function on finite
G
-sets. If

\prod_{i}\phi(H_{i})^{n_{i}}=1

whenever
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}
is a Brauer relation, we say
\phi
is
representation theoretic
. This means that the value of
\phi
on a
G
-set
X
only depends on
\mathbb{C}[X]
.
In the case where
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, we say that
\phi
is
trivial on
K
-relations for
G
if
\phi(\Theta)\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
whenever
\Theta
is a
K
-relation for
G
. Such functions are representation theoretic when considered as functions to

\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
.

Functions on subgroups that arise from Galois-equivariant functions on representations of
G
provide natural examples of functions that are trivial on
K
-relations for
G
. Let
G
be of exponent
n
, and consider any
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
-valued function
g
on complex representations of
G
that satisfies

g(\chi\oplus\psi)=g(\chi)g(\psi),\quad g(\chi^{\sigma})=g(\chi)^{\sigma},

for all representations
\chi,\psi
of
G
and all
\sigma\in\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})/\mathbb{Q})
. Then the function on subgroups of
G
given by
\phi(H)=g(\mathbb{C}[G/H])
is both representation-theoretic and trivial on
K
-relations for
G
.

If
G=\Gal(F/\mathbb{Q})
, and
E/\mathbb{Q}
is an elliptic curve, the map

H\leq G\mapsto C_{E/F^{H}}

is constant on conjugate subgroups.
The norm relations test takes a particular
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
-relation
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}
, and tests whether

\prod_{i}(C_{E/F^{H_{i}}})^{n_{i}}=1\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/N_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})^{\times}).

If the map
H\mapsto C_{E/F^{H}}
is trivial on
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
-relations for
G
, then the norm relations test cannot be used to predict positive rank for this choice of elliptic curve and Galois extension. In §
2
, we analyse in detail when functions (or ratios of functions) are trivial on
K
-relations.

1.3.2.
Regulator constants

Regulator constants were first introduced in
[
9
,
10
]
. Let
G
be a finite group and
\Theta
a Brauer relation for
G
.
Given a rational representation
\tau
of
G
equipped with a non-degenerate
G
-invariant bilinear pairing, its regulator constant is a rational number
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
(defined in
[
10
, §2.ii]
). This is a purely representation-theoretic object that is computed as a product of determinants of the pairing on invariant subspaces of
\tau
. The value of
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta})(\tau)\mod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
is independent of the choice of pairing and bases for the invariant subspaces.

We extend the definition of regulator constants to the case of
K
-relations. Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}
be a
K
-relation for
G
, and let
\tau
be a
\mathbb{Q}G
-representation. The regulator constant
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)
of
\tau
with respect to
\Theta
is defined in direct analogy with the case of Brauer relations (see
Definition
2.31
). A priori, this definition depends on the choice of non-degenerate
G
-invariant bilinear pairing on
\tau
, but —as in the Brauer relation case— should be independent of this choice. Independence holds (up to squares in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
) whenever

\sum_{i}n_{i}\langle\tau,\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]\rangle=0
. In the general situation, some extra care is required: by restricting to rational pairings and working modulo the subgroup
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})\cdot\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
, one recovers independence (see Proposition

2.33
).
Computing with respect to some rational pairing on
\tau
, the regulator constant
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)
is a non-zero rational number whose value mod
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})\cdot\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
is independent of this choice of pairing.

In the case of Brauer relations, regular constants have been applied to various problems in elliptic curves and number theory.
They have been used to establish the
p
-parity conjecture for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}

[
9
]
, to study Selmer group growth in certain extensions
[
1
]
, and to analyse Galois module structures in the context of unit groups and higher
K
-groups of number fields
[
2
,
3
]
. More recently, they have also appeared in the study of covers of curves, particularly in the context of the parity of ranks of Jacobians
[
13
]
. While we have not yet investigated potential applications of regulator constants for
K
-relations beyond this paper, this could be an interesting direction for future investigation.

1.4.
Layout

This paper is structured as follows.
In Section

, we develop the theory of
K
-relations and regulator constants, introducing the key representation-theoretic tools needed for our main results. We also consider functions on the Burnside ring of a group, and their behaviour when evaluated at
K
-relations. This section is self-contained and purely representation-theoretic, making it accessible to those interested in

In Section 3, we establish the main technical results, linking regulator constants to root numbers.
In Section 4, we apply these results to our study of the norm relations test. We give examples of when the norm relations test cannot predict positive rank. We also consider the ratio of BSD-quotients corresponding to a

2.
K-relations and functions on the Burnside ring

This section concerns the representation-theoretic concepts that are crucial in proving the main results of this paper in §
3
. We have kept this section self-contained, as it may be of independent interest and have applications beyond elliptic curves. The primary objective is to extend the theory introduced in
[
10
, §2]
so that it can also be applied in the setting of
K
-relations. For ease of reference, we recall relevant definitions and results from
[
10
, §2]
throughout.

We first study K-relations as introduced in the introduction, then examine functions on the Burnside ring of a finite group and their behaviour when evaluated on K-relations. Finally, we define and study regulator constants for K-relations.

Throughout this section,
G
is a finite group, and
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a finite Galois extension.

2.1.
K-relations

Given a finite
G
-set
X
, let
[X]
denote its isomorphism class.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Burnside ring

\B(G)
is the ring of formal linear combinations of isomorphism classes of finite
G
-sets, modulo the relations

[S]+[T]=[S\sqcup T],\quad[S]\cdot[T]=[S\times T]

for
G
-sets
S
,
T
(
S\sqcup T
being the disjoint union and
S\times T
the Cartesian product).

We will not need the multiplicative structure of the Burnside ring. As isomorphism classes of finite transitive
G
-sets are in bijection with subgroups of
G
up to conjugacy, we will write an element of
\B(G)
as the formal sum
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}
with
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
H_{i}\leq G
.

Let
\Rep(G)
be the complex representation ring of
G
. There is a map from the Burnside ring to
\Rep(G)
given by sending a
G
-set
X
to
\mathbb{C}[X]
.
Given
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}\in\B(G)
, the image of
\Theta
under this homomorphism is a formal
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of isomorphism classes of representations, which we denote by

\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]:=\bigoplus_{i}\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]^{\oplus n_{i}}.

We define restriction and induction maps on Burnside rings corresponding to restriction and induction of the corresponding permutation representations.

Definition 2.2
.

For
D\leq G
define the maps
\Res_{D}^{G}\colon\B(G)\to\B(D)
,
\Ind_{D}^{G}\colon\B(D)\to\B(G)
by

\Res_{D}^{G}H=\sum_{x\in H\backslash G/D}D\cap H^{x^{-1}},\quad\Ind_{D}^{G}H^{\prime}=H^{\prime},

for
H\leq G
,
H^{\prime}\leq D
.

Clearly
\Ind_{D}^{G}\mathbb{C}[D/H^{\prime}]=\mathbb{C}[G/\Ind_{D}^{G}H^{\prime}]
, and by Mackey’s decomposition

\Res_{D}^{G}\mathbb{C}[G/H]=\bigoplus_{x\in H\backslash G/D}\mathbb{C}[D/D\cap H^{x^{-1}}]=\mathbb{C}[D/\Res^{G}_{D}H].

We also write these functions as
\Res_{D}
,
\Ind_{D}
when the group
G
is clear.

Let
\rho\in\Rep(G)
a representation of
G
. The field generated by the character values of
\rho
is a finite algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, which we denote by
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension. For
\rho\in\Rep(G)
with
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)\subset K
, define

N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})}\rho^{\sigma},

where
\rho^{\sigma}
is the representation of
G
with
\Tr\rho^{\sigma}(g)=\sigma(\Tr\rho(g))
for all
g\in G
.

Note that
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})}\rho^{\tau}=N_{\mathbb{Q}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)^{[K\colon\mathbb{Q}(\rho)]}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension. An element
\Theta\in\B(G)
is a
K
-relation for
G
, if there exists
\rho\in\Rep(G)
with
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)\subset K
and

\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho).

We will often refer to these as
K
-relations if the group
G
involved is clear. The following are some elementary properties of
K
-relations.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
K
a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. If
\Theta\in\B(G)
is a
K
-relation for
G
, then
\Theta
is also a
L
-relation for
G
for all
\mathbb{Q}\subset L\subset K
such that
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois.

Proof.

If
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)
with
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)\subset K
, then
\tau=\bigoplus_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Gal(K/L)}\rho^{\sigma^{\prime}}
satisfies
\mathbb{Q}(\tau)\subset L
and
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)=N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\tau)
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}\in\B(G)
be a
K
-relation for
G
. Then

(1)

(Sum) If
\Psi\in\B(G)
is another
K
-relation for
G
, then
\Theta\pm\Psi
is a
K
-relation for
G
,

(2)

(Restriction) If
H\leq G
then
\Res^{G}_{H}\Theta\in\B(H)
is a
K
-relation for
H
,

(3)

(Induction) If
G\leq G^{\prime}
then
\Ind^{G^{\prime}}_{G}\Theta\in\B(G^{\prime})
is a
K
-relation for
G^{\prime}
,

(4)

(Projection) If
N\triangleleft G
then
N\cdot\Theta/N=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}N/N\in\B(G/N)
is a
K
-relation for
G/N
,

(5)

(Lifting) If
N\subset H_{j}^{\prime}
and
\sum_{j}m_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}N/N\in\B(G/N)
is a
K
-relationfor
G/N
, then
\sum_{j}m_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
is a
K
-relation for
G
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)
,
\mathbb{C}[G/\Psi]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\tau)
for
\rho
,
\tau\in\Rep(G)
. Then
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta\pm\Psi]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho\pm\tau)
,

\mathbb{C}[H/\Res_{H}^{G}\Theta]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\Res_{H}^{G}\rho)
, and

\mathbb{C}[G^{\prime}/\Ind_{G}^{G^{\prime}}\Theta]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\Ind_{G}^{G^{\prime}}\rho)
, proving
(1)
,
(2)
,
(3)
.
One has
\mathbb{C}[G/H_{i}]^{N}=\mathbb{C}[G/NH_{i}]
as
G
-representations (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 2.8]
). Thus

\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]^{N}=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho^{N})=\mathbb{C}[G/N/N\cdot\Theta/N]
, implying
(4)
. For
(5)
, it is clear that the relation can be lifted to
G
.
∎

The previous proposition shows that the space of
K
-relations for
G
forms a sublattice of the Burnside ring
\B(G)
.
If
n=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
and
\Theta\in\B(G)
, then

\mathbb{C}[G/n\Theta]=\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]^{\oplus n}=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]),

and hence the sublattice generated by
K
-relations has finite index in
\B(G)
dividing
[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]
.

Remark 2.7

(Brauer relations)
.

When
\rho=0
,
\Theta
is called a
Brauer relation
(cf.
[
10
, §2.1]
) . Non-zero elements of
\Theta\in\B(G)
with
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=0
are instances of non-isomorphic
G
-sets with isomorphic permutation representations. Indeed, writing
\Theta=X-X^{\prime}
as a difference of finite
G
-sets, one has
X\not\simeq X^{\prime}
but
\mathbb{C}[X]=\mathbb{C}[X^{\prime}]
. The rank of the space of Brauer relations in
\B(G)
is equal to the number of non-cyclic subgroups of
G
up to conjugacy (
[
10
, Remark 2.5]
). Brauer relations have been classified by Bartel and Tim Dokchitser in
[
4
]
.

Notation 2.8
.

There is an inclusion of rings

\Perm(G)\subset R_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)\subset\Char_{\mathbb{Q}}(G),

where
\Perm(G)
is the ring of virtual permutation representations,
R_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
the ring of rational representations, and
\Char_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
the ring of representations with
\mathbb{Q}
-valued characters. These rings have the same rank as
\mathbb{Z}
-modules. A basis of
\Char_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
is given by the distinct
N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
as
\chi
ranges over the complex irreducible representations of
G
.

Definition 2.9
.

Define the group

\hat{C}(G)=\Char_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)/\Perm(G).

Remark 2.10
.

It follows from Artin’s induction theorem that
\hat{C}(G)
is finite with exponent dividing
|G|
. When
G
is abelian or dihedral,
\hat{C}(G)=1
. The order of
N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
in
\Char_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)/R_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
is the Schur index
m_{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
of
\chi
. The group
R_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)/\Perm(G)
is not trivial in general (cf.
[
19
, Exc. 13.4]
). It is trivial when
G
is a
p
-group (
[
18
]
). Given a finite group
G
, the structure of
\hat{C}(G)
can be computed using the results of
[
5
]
.

The finiteness of
\hat{C}(G)
implies that for any
\mathbb{C}G
-representation
\rho
, there exists an integer
m
and
\Theta\in\B(G)
such that
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=N_{\mathbb{Q}(\rho)/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)^{\oplus m}
.
The following proposition describes the decomposition of the permutation representation associated to a
K
-relation into irreducible rational representations.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\Theta\in\B(G)
be a
K
-relation. Then

\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi_{\tau})^{\oplus k_{\tau}}

where
k_{\tau}\geq 1
is divisible by
[K\colon K\cap\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})]
. The sum ranges over the rational irreducible representations of
G
, and
\chi_{\tau}
denotes a complex irreducible constituent of
\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)
for some representation
\rho
of
G
with
\mathbb{Q}(\rho)\subset K
.
Let
L_{\tau}=K\cap\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})
. A basis of representations of
G
with
K
-valued character is given by

\{\varphi_{\tau}=\hskip-5.0pt\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})/L_{\tau})}\hskip-5.0pt\chi_{\tau}^{\sigma}\colon\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)\}.

Thus
\rho=\bigoplus_{\tau}\varphi_{\tau}^{\oplus n_{\tau}}
for some
n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}
and so

N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)=\hskip-5.0pt\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}\hskip-5.0ptN_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi_{\tau})^{\oplus n_{\tau}}=\hskip-5.0pt\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}\hskip-5.0ptN_{L_{\tau}/\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi_{\tau})^{\oplus n_{\tau}[K\colon L_{\tau}]}=\hskip-5.0pt\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}\hskip-5.0ptN_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi_{\tau})^{\oplus n_{\tau}[K\colon L_{\tau}]}.

∎

Example 2.12

(Cyclic Groups)
.

Let

. For

, let

2.2.

This subsection concerns functions from the Burnside ring to an abelian group.
The content of this section appears in
[

and we repeat it for convenience of reference.

Let
A
be an abelian group. We consider linear functions
f\colon\B(G)\to A
satisfying
f(X\sqcup Y)=f(X)f(Y)
for finite
G
-sets
X
,

.
In applications to number theory, such functions appear naturally when
G
is the Galois group of an extension of number fields and one is interested in computing invariants associated to subfields.

Example 2.13
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
L
, and
F/L
a finite Galois extension with
G=\Gal(F/L)
.
If
H,H^{\prime}\leq G
are conjugate subgroups, then

and so
C_{E/F^{H}}=C_{E/F^{H^{\prime}}}
(

defined in §

). Thus the function
C\colon\B(G)\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
sending

to

is well-defined.

Often such functions will depend on the decomposition group at a prime of the base field of the Galois extension, which motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.14

(
[
10
, Definition 2.33]
)
.

For
D\leq G
, say a function

is

-local
if there exists a function

on
\B(D)
such that
f(H)=f_{D}(\Res_{D}H)
for all
H\leq G
. If this is the case, write

Example 2.15
.

Let
G=\Gal(F/L)
be the Galois group of an extension of number fields. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of

, and
\mathfrak{q}
a prime of

above
\mathfrak{p}
with decomposition group

. For

, the primes above
\mathfrak{p}
in

are in one-to-one correspondence with elements of

. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, the function
f\colon\B(G)\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
,

f(H)=\lambda^{\#\{\text{primes above $\mathfrak{p}$ in $F^{H}$}\}}=\lambda^{|H\backslash G/D_{\mathfrak{q}}|},

that counts the primes above
\mathfrak{p}
is a
D_{\mathfrak{q}}
-local function with
f=(D_{\mathfrak{q}},\lambda)
.

Example 2.16
.

In Example
2.13
, the function

factors as a product of local functions.
Recall that

(as defined in §

on

, where

ranges over all finite places of

For each finite place

of

be the decomposition group at

for a choice of place

in

above

for

Then

with the product ranging over all finite places

of

Let

be the Galois group of an extension of number fields and let

be the decomposition and inertia subgroups of

of

H\cap I^{x}
respectively. The ramification degree and residue degree of
w
over
L
is thus given by
e_{w}=\tfrac{|I|}{|H\cap I^{x}|}
and
f_{w}=\tfrac{[D\colon I]}{[H\cap D^{x}\colon H\cap I^{x}]}
. To describe functions that depend on the ramification and residue degrees of places in intermediate fields, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.17

(
[
10
, Definition 2.35]
)
.

Consider
I\triangleleft D\leq G
with
D/I
cyclic and
\psi(e,f)
a function of
e
,
f\in\mathbb{N}
. Define a function on
\B(G)
by

(D,I,\psi)\colon H\mapsto\prod_{x\in H\backslash G/D}\psi\left(\tfrac{|I|}{|H\cap I^{x}|},\tfrac{[D\colon I]}{[H\cap D^{x}\colon H\cap I^{x}]}\right).

This is a
D
-local function on
\B(G)
with

(D,I,\psi)=\left(D,U\mapsto\psi\left(\tfrac{|I|}{|U\cap I|},\tfrac{|D|}{|UI|}\right)\right),

(since
(U\cap D)/(U\cap I)\simeq UI/I
for
U\leq D
).

The following proposition follows from the definition above.

Proposition 2.18

(
[
10
, Theorem 2.36]
)
.

Let
I\triangleleft D\leq G
with
D/I
cyclic.

(1)

If
I_{0}<I
is normal in
D
with cyclic quotient, and
\psi(e,f)
is a function of the product
ef
, then
(D,I,\psi)=(D,I_{0},\psi)
.

(2)

If
I<D_{0}<D
and
\psi(e,f)=\psi(e,f/m)^{m}
whenever
m
divides
f
and
[D\colon D_{0}]
, then
(D,I,\psi)=(D_{0},I,\psi)
.

2.3.
Functions on K-relations

We now focus on functions from the Burnside ring to
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and evaluating these function on
K
-relations.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
f\colon\B(G)\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
be a linear function,
K
a number field. Say that
f
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
if
f(\Theta)\equiv 1\mod N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
whenever
\Theta
is a
K
-relation for
G
.

If
f
is trivial on
K
-relations, we write
f\sim_{K}1
. If
f,g\colon\B(G)\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
are functions such that
f/g\sim_{K}1
, we write
f\sim_{K}g
.

Example 2.20
.

Let
G=C_{n}
,
K
a number field and
\Theta
a
K
-relation. Consider a function
f\colon\B(G)\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
. As in Example
2.12
,
\Theta=\sum_{d\mid n}a_{d}\Psi_{d}
, where
a_{d}
is divisible by
[K\colon\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})\cap K]
. If
f(\Psi_{d})\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})/\mathbb{Q}}\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})^{\times})
for each
d|n
, then by Lemma
2.23

f(\Theta)\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
.
Therefore to show that a function
f
on
\B(G)
is trivial on
K
-relations for any field
K
, it is enough to show that it is trivial on the
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})
-relation
\Psi_{d}
for each
d\mid n
.

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
G
be a group, with
D\leq G
,
N\triangleleft G
. Let
K
be a number field, and let
f\colon\B(G)\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
be a linear function on
\B(G)
.

(1)

(Restriction) If
f=(D,f_{D})
and
f_{D}
is trivial on
K
-relations for
D
, then
f
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
.

(2)

(Quotient) Suppose
f
is of the form
f(H)=f_{G/N}(NH/N)
for some function
f_{G/N}
on
\B(G/N)
. If
f_{G/N}
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G/N
, then
f
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
2.6
, using that if
\Theta\in\B(G)
is a
K
-relation for
G
, then
\Res_{D}\Theta
is a
K
-relation for
D
and
N\cdot\Theta/N
is a
K
-relation for
G/N
.
∎

The next proposition generalises Example
2.20
, and describes
how one can prove a function is trivial on
K
-relations by reducing to irreducible representations.

Proposition 2.22
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
\hat{C}(G)=1
, and
f\colon\B(G)\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
a linear function. Suppose that
f(\Psi)\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
whenever
\Psi
is a Brauer relation.

(1)

If for each
\chi\in\Irr_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
, there exists
\Theta_{\chi}\in\B(G)
with
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta_{\chi}]=N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
and
f(\Theta_{\chi})\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\chi)^{\times})
, then

f
is trivial on
K
-relations.

(2)

If
K/\mathbb{Q}
is quadratic, to show that
f
is trivial on
K
-relations,
it suffices to show that for each
\chi\in\Irr_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
with
K\subset\mathbb{Q}(\chi)
, there exists
\Theta_{\chi}\in\B(G)
with
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta_{\chi}]=N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
and
f(\Theta_{\chi})\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
.

Proof.

Let

\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\rho)=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi_{\tau})^{\oplus k_{\tau}}

be a
K
-relation, where
\chi_{\tau}
is a complex irreducible constituent of
\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
and
[K\colon K\cap\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})]
divides
k_{\tau}
.
Then
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta_{\chi_{\tau}}]^{\oplus k_{\tau}}
and

f(\Theta)=f(\Psi)\prod_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}f(\Theta_{\chi_{\tau}})^{k_{\tau}}

where
\Psi=\Theta-\sum_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}k_{\tau}\Theta_{\chi_{\tau}}
is a Brauer relation and
f(\Psi)\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
by assumption.
For
(i)
, the result follows as

f(\Theta_{\tau})\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})^{\times})
for all
\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
, so that
f(\Theta_{\chi_{\tau}})^{k_{\tau}}\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
by Proposition
2.11
and Lemma
2.23
below.
For
(ii)
, if
K\not\subset\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})
then
k_{\tau}
is even and so
f(\Theta_{\chi_{\tau}})^{k_{\tau}}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}\subset N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
. Thus

f(\Theta)\equiv\prod_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G),K\subset\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{\tau})}f(\Theta_{\chi_{\tau}})\mod N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times}),

and it follows that
f(\Theta)\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
from the assumption that
f(\Theta_{\chi_{\tau}})\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
.
∎

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
L
and
K
be number fields. If
x=N_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(z)
for
z\in L
, then
x^{[K\colon L\cap K]}\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
.

Proof.

One has
x^{[K\colon L\cap K]}=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(N_{L/L\cap K}(z))
.
∎

Remark 2.24
.

The proof of Proposition
2.22
does not hold when
\hat{C}(G)\not=1
. For example, the quaternion group
G=Q_{8}
has
\hat{C}(G)=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, generated by the
2
-dimensional irreducible representation
\chi
. This representation has rational character. One has

\chi^{\oplus 2}=\mathbb{C}[G/C_{1}]\ominus\mathbb{C}[G/C_{2}],

and so
\Theta=C_{1}-C_{2}
is a
K
-relation for all quadratic fields
K
. However there does not exist
\Theta^{\prime}\in\B(G)
such that
\Theta=\Psi+2\Theta^{\prime}
for
\Psi
a Brauer relation.

We now give some examples of functions on the Burnside ring of a cyclic group that are trivial on
K
-relations. The triviality of these functions will be essential for the proofs in §
3
.

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
G=C_{n}
be cyclic. The function
g\colon H\leq G\mapsto[G\colon H]
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
, where
K
is any number field.

Proof.

Let
\Theta
be a
K
-relation for
G
. Then
\Theta=\sum_{d|n}a_{d}\Psi_{d}
, where
a_{d}
is divisible by
[K\colon\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})\cap K]
and
\Psi_{d}\in\B(G)
is as in Example
2.12
. One has

g(\Psi_{d})=\prod_{d^{\prime}\mid d}(d^{\prime})^{\mu(d/d^{\prime})}=\Phi_{d}(1)=N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})/\mathbb{Q}}(1-\zeta_{d}),

where
\Phi_{d}
is the
d
-th cyclotomic polynomial. Thus
g(\Psi_{d})\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})^{\times})
and so
g
is trivial on
K
-relations by Example
2.12
.
∎

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
G=C_{n}
be cyclic. The function
c\colon H\leq G\mapsto\alpha
where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
, where
K
is any number field.

Proof.

For
d\mid n
, one has
c(\Psi_{d})=\alpha^{\sum_{d^{\prime}|d}\mu(d/d^{\prime})}=1
unless
d=1
in which case
c(\Psi_{1})=\alpha
. Thus if
\Theta
is a
K
-relation then
c(\Theta)=\alpha^{m[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]}=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha^{m})
where
m\geq 1
.
∎

Lemma 2.27
.

Let
G=C_{n}
be cyclic. Consider the function

d_{\alpha,\beta}\colon H\leq G\mapsto\begin{cases}\alpha,&\text{if }k\mid[C_{n}\colon H],\\
\beta,&\text{if }k\nmid[C_{n}\colon H],\end{cases}

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
. If
\alpha/\beta\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})^{\times})
, then
d_{\alpha,\beta}
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
, where
K
is any number field.

Proof.

If
k\nmid n
or if
k=1
then this is the constant function which is trivial on
K
-relations by Lemma
2.26
, so we may assume
k\mid n
and
k>1
. Let
\chi_{k}
be a character of
G
with
\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{k})=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})
. Then

\langle\chi_{k},\mathbb{C}[C_{n}/H]\rangle=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }k\mid[C_{n}\colon H],\\
0,&\text{if }k\nmid[C_{n}\colon H].\end{cases}

Thus
c(H)=\alpha^{\langle\chi_{k},\mathbb{Q}[C_{n}/H]\rangle}\cdot\beta^{1-\langle\chi_{k},\mathbb{Q}[C_{n}/H]\rangle}
.
Therefore

d_{\alpha,\beta}(\Psi_{d})=(\alpha/\beta)^{\langle\chi_{k},N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi_{d})/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi_{d})\rangle}\beta^{\sum_{d^{\prime}|d}\mu(d/d^{\prime})}=\begin{cases}\beta,&\text{if }d=1,\\
\alpha/\beta,&\text{if }d=k,\\
1,&\text{if }d\not=k,d>1,\end{cases}

where
\chi_{d}
is a character of
G
of order
d
. If
\Theta
is a
K
-relation for
G
, then
d_{\alpha,\beta}(\Theta)=\beta^{x[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]}\cdot(\alpha/\beta)^{y[K\colon K\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})]}
for some
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
. One has
\beta^{x[K\colon\mathbb{Q}]}=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\beta^{x})
, and

(\alpha/\beta)^{y[K\colon K\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})]}\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
by Lemma
2.23
and the assumption that
\alpha/\beta\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})^{\times})
. Thus
d_{\alpha,\beta}(\Theta)
is the norm of an element of
K
.
∎

Lemma 2.28
.

Let
G=C_{n}
. The function

g\colon H\mapsto\begin{cases}[C_{n}\colon H],&\text{if }2\mid[C_{n}\colon H],\\
1,&\text{if }2\nmid[C_{n}\colon H],\end{cases}

is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
, where
K
is any number field.

Proof.

If
2\nmid n
, then this is always
1
, so assume
2\mid n
. Let
\chi_{2}
be a character of
G
of order
2
. Then
g
can be expressed as
g(H)=[C_{n}\colon H]^{\langle\chi_{2},\ \mathbb{Q}[C_{n}/H]\rangle}
.
For
d\mid n
,

g(\Psi_{d})=\prod_{d^{\prime}\mid d}(d^{\prime})^{\mu(d/d^{\prime})\cdot\langle\chi_{2},\mathbb{C}[C_{n}/C_{n/d^{\prime}}]\rangle}=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }d=2,\\
p,&\text{if }d=2p,\ p\text{ prime,}\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In each case it is clear that
g(\Psi_{d})\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{d})^{\times})
and so the result follows by Example
2.12
.
∎

If we have a function
(D,I,\psi(e,f))
as in Definition
2.17
that depends only on
f
, then it can be expressed as a function of the quotient
D/I
. By Proposition
2.21
, to show that
(D,I,\psi(e,f))\sim_{K}1
it suffices to show that the function on the cyclic quotient
D/I
is trivial on
K
-relations in
\B(D/I)
. Thus the previous results yield the following.

Corollary 2.29
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Let
D\leq G
with
D/I
cyclic. Let
K
be a number field.

(1)

The function
(D,\alpha)=(D,I,\alpha)
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
.

(2)

The function
(D,I,f)
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
.

(3)

The function
\left(D,I,\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha,&&\text{if }k\mid f,\\
\beta,&&\text{if }k\nmid f.\end{smallmatrix}\right.\right)
with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
when
\alpha/\beta\in N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})^{\times})
.

(4)

The function
\left(D,I,\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}f,&&\text{if }2\mid f,\\
1,&&\text{if }2\nmid f.\end{smallmatrix}\right.\right)
is trivial on
K
-relations for
G
.

2.4.
Regulator constants

We now consider regulator constants. These are discussed in detail in
[
10
, §2.ii]
, and we will generalise some of the results therein to apply to
K
-relations.
Throughout,
\mathcal{K}
is a field of characteristic zero.

Notation 2.30
.

Let
V
be a vector space over
\mathcal{K}
, with
\langle,\rangle
a non-degenerate
\mathcal{K}
-bilinear
\mathcal{L}
-valued pairing on
V
, where
\mathcal{L}
is some finite extension of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
\det(\langle,\rangle|V)\in\mathcal{L}^{\times}/\mathcal{K}^{\times 2}
denote
\det(\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle_{i,j})
in any
\mathcal{K}
-basis
\{e_{1},\ldots e_{n}\}
of
V
.

Definition 2.31

(Regulator constant)
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
\tau
a self-dual
\mathcal{K}G
-representation. Let
\langle,\rangle
be a
G
-invariant, non-degenerate,
\mathcal{K}
-bilinear pairing on
\tau
taking values in an extension
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}\in\B(G)
be a
K
-relation for
G
. Define the
regulator constant

\mathcal{C}^{\langle,\rangle}_{\Theta}(\tau)=\prod_{i}\det\left(\frac{1}{|H_{i}|}\langle,\rangle\mid\tau^{H_{i}}\right)^{n_{i}}\in\mathcal{L}^{\times}/\mathcal{K}^{\times 2}.

Remark 2.32
.

If
\tau
and
\langle,\rangle
are as in the definition, then for each
H\leq G
the restriction of
\langle,\rangle
to
\tau^{H}
is non-degenerate (
[
10
, Lemma 2.15]
). Thus
\det(\langle,\rangle\mid\tau^{H})\not=0
so
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\langle,\rangle}(\tau)
is non-zero.

If
\langle\tau,\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]\rangle=0
,
\Theta
is a
pseudo Brauer relation relative to
\tau
as introduced in
[
13
, Definition 3.1]
. As proved in
[
13
, Theorem 3.7]
and stated in the proposition below,
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}^{\langle,\rangle}(\tau)
is independent of the choice of pairing in this case. We let
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}
be a representative of the value of
\mathcal{C}^{\langle,\rangle}_{\Theta}(\tau)\in\mathcal{K}^{\times}/\mathcal{K}^{\times 2}

(we may take the pairing to be
\mathcal{K}
-valued).

Proposition 2.33
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}\in\B(G)
a
K
-relation for
G
. Let
\tau
be a self-dual
\mathcal{K}G
-representation, and
\langle,\rangle_{1}
,
\langle,\rangle_{2}
two non-degenerate
\mathcal{K}
-bilinear
G
-invariant pairings on
\tau
.
Suppose that
\langle\tau,\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]\rangle=0
. Computing the determinants with respect to the same basis of
\tau^{H_{i}}
on both sides, one has

\prod_{i}\det\left(\frac{1}{|H_{i}|}\langle,\rangle_{1}\mid\tau^{H_{i}}\right)^{n_{i}}=\prod_{i}\det\left(\frac{1}{|H_{i}|}\langle,\rangle_{2}\mid\tau^{H_{i}}\right)^{n_{i}}.

Proof.

Follow proof of
[
10
, Theorem 2.17]
where this is proved for
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]=0
.
∎

The above proposition does not hold if
\tau
appears as a subrepresentation of
\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]
. If we restrict to considering
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{Q}
-valued pairings, then we get the following result on independence of pairings up to norms.

Proposition 2.34
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}\in\B(G)
a
K
-relation for
G
. Let
\tau
be a
\mathbb{Q}G
-representation, and
\langle,\rangle_{1}
,
\langle,\rangle_{2}
two non-degenerate
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear
G
-invariant pairings on
\tau
that are
\mathbb{Q}
-valued.
Computing the determinants with respect to the same basis of
\tau^{H_{i}}
on both sides, one has

\prod_{i}\det\left(\frac{1}{|H_{i}|}\langle,\rangle_{1}\mid\tau^{H_{i}}\right)^{n_{i}}\equiv\prod_{i}\det\left(\frac{1}{|H_{i}|}\langle,\rangle_{2}\mid\tau^{H_{i}}\right)^{n_{i}}\mod N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times}).

Proof.

It is enough to prove this for a particular choice of basis of each
\tau^{H_{i}}
. Suppose firstly that
\tau=\alpha\oplus\beta
with
\alpha,\beta

\mathbb{Q}G
-representations and
\Hom_{G}(\alpha,\beta^{\vee})=0
, where
\beta^{\vee}
denotes the dual representation of
\beta
. Then
\alpha
and
\beta
are orthogonal with respect to
\langle,\rangle_{1}
,
\langle,\rangle_{2}
. Since
\tau^{H}=\alpha^{H}\oplus\beta^{H}
for all
H\leq G
, choosing bases of
\tau^{H_{i}}
that respects this decomposition reduces the problem to
\alpha
and
\beta
separately. Thus we may assume that
\tau=\rho^{\oplus n}
where
\rho
is an irreducible
\mathbb{Q}G
-representation. As
\rho
is irreducible it is of the form
\rho=N_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi)/\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)^{\oplus m_{\chi}}
where
\chi
is a complex irreducible representation of
G
and
m_{\chi}
is the Schur index of
\chi
.

Let
\langle,\rangle
be a non-degenerate
G
-invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-valued pairing on
\rho
. Then any other such pairing is of the form
\langle,\rangle_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\End_{G}(\rho)
with
\langle x,y\rangle_{\alpha}=\langle\alpha(x),y\rangle
for
x
,
y\in\rho
.
Similarly the non-degenerate
G
-invariant
\mathbb{Q}
-valued pairings on
\tau
are in one-to-one correspondence with
\End_{G}(\tau)
. Note that

\End_{G}(\tau)=\End_{G}(\rho^{\oplus n})\simeq M_{n}(\End_{G}(\rho))\simeq M_{n}(D),

where
D=\End_{G}(\rho)
is a finite-dimensional division algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
with centre
\mathbb{Q}(\chi)
(cf.
[
19
, §12.2]
).

Fix a subgroup
H\leq G
.
Viewing
\rho^{H}
as a
D
-vector space with basis
\{v_{1},\ldots v_{k}\}
,
k=\dim_{D}\rho^{H}
, an endomorphism
\alpha\in D
acts by the matrix
\alpha I_{k}
. The algebra
D
has dimension
m_{\chi}^{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}(\chi)
, and there exists a splitting field
\mathbb{Q}(\chi)\subset L\subset D
such that
[D\colon L]=m_{\chi}
and
D\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}(\chi)}L\simeq M_{m_{\chi}}(L)
. Let
\{f_{1},\ldots f_{m_{\chi}}\}
be a basis for
D
over
L
. With respect to the basis
\{f_{i}v_{j}\}
of
\rho^{H}
,
\alpha
acts by a block diagonal matrix consisting of
k
blocks corresponding to multiplication by
\alpha
on
D
as an
L
-vector space. If we further consider a basis
\{e_{1},\ldots e_{t}\}
of
L
as a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space, then
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{i}f_{j}v_{k}\}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis of
\rho^{H}
. Induce this to a basis of
(\rho^{H})^{\oplus n}
, and
let
C
be the matrix of
\langle,\rangle
on
\rho^{H}
with respect to
\mathcal{B}
. The matrix of
\langle,\rangle_{1}
on
\tau^{H}
is of the form

\left(\begin{smallmatrix}C&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&C\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}&\cdots&A_{1n}\\
A_{21}&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{n1}&\cdots&\cdots&A_{nn}\end{smallmatrix}\right),\quad A_{ij}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_{ij}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\alpha_{ij}&0&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&\alpha_{ij}\end{smallmatrix}\right)

with each

Remark 4.1
.

The following summarises properties of regulator constants.
For an arbitrary group
G
and
K

\Theta=\sum_{i}n_{i}H_{i}
, we associate to a rational representation
\tau
of

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times}).

This is independent of the choice of rational pairing on
\tau
. Moreover, if
\langle\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta],\tau\rangle=0
, then the regulator constant can be computed with respect to any pairing (not necessarily
\mathbb{Q}
-valued) and is still independent of the choice of pairing. Regulator constants also satisfy the following properties:

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau\oplus\tau^{\prime})=\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau^{\prime})
,

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)=1
if
\tau=\chi\oplus\overline{\chi}
with
\chi\not\simeq\overline{\chi}
,

(3)

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)=1
if
\tau
is symplectic,

(4)

If
D
is cyclic then
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\mathbb{Q}[G/D])=1
,

(5)

Let
G=\Gal(F/L)
be the Galois group of an extensions of number fields,
E/L
an elliptic curve, and
\tau=E(F)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. If
\langle\tau,\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]\rangle=0
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)\equiv\prod_{i}\Reg_{E/F^{H_{i}}}\mod N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
.

Remark 4.2
.

For the reader’s convenience, we state some properties of twisted root numbers; proofs can be found in
[
10
, Proposition A.2]
.

Let
F/L
be a Galois extension of number fields with
G=\Gal(F/L)
and let
E/L
be an elliptic curve. Let
\rho\colon G\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be an Artin representation. The twisted root number
w(E/L,\rho)\in\{\pm 1\}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

(Multiplicativity) If
\rho=\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2}
, then

w(E/L,\rho)=w(E/L,\rho_{1})w(E/L,\rho_{2}).

(2)

(Induction) If
\rho=\Ind_{H}^{G}\tau
for a subgroup
H\leq G
, then

w(E/L,\rho)=w(E/F^{H},\tau).

(3)

(Galois equivariance) If
\rho
is self-dual and
\rho^{\prime}
is a Galois conjugate of
\rho
, then

w(E/L,\rho)=w(E/L,\rho^{\prime}).

(4)

(Trivial representation) If
\rho=\mathbbm{1}
is the trivial representation, then

w(E/L,\mathbbm{1})=w(E/L).

4.1.

Theorem 4.4

Example 4.8

(Twisted root number formula)
.

Continuing the previous example, we can express

4.2.

Our second application of Theorem

Since regulators of elliptic curves can be computed via regulator constants, we get the following compatibility between regulators and Tamagawa numbers.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let

Let

-relation for

\prod_{i}(C_{E/F^{H_{i}}})^{n_{i}}\equiv\prod_{i}(\Reg_{E/F^{H_{i}}})^{n_{i}}\mod N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times}).

If one further assumes that
\text{\tencyr Sh}_{E/F}
is finite
, then

\prod_{i}\BSD(E/F^{H_{i}})^{n_{i}}\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times}).

Proof.

For each
\mathbb{Q}G
-irreducible representation
\tau
, let
\chi_{\tau}
be a complex irreducible constituent.
By Theorem

and parity for twists we have

\prod_{i}(C_{E/F^{H_{i}}})^{n_{i}}\equiv\prod_{\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)}\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\tau)^{\langle\chi_{\tau},E(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle}\mod N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times}).

Consider
\tau\in\Irr_{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
such that

\langle\tau,\mathbb{C}[G/\Theta]\rangle=0

∎

l

Proposition B.1
.

If
K\subset\mathbb{Q}(\Ind_{I}^{D}\psi_{n})
then
l
is the norm of an ideal of
K
.

Proof.

We show that
\mathbb{Q}(\Ind_{I}^{D}\psi_{n})\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})^{\langle\zeta_{n}\mapsto\zeta_{n}^{q}\rangle}
, that is that
\mathbb{Q}(\Ind_{I}^{D}\psi_{n})
lies in the subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
fixed by
\langle q\rangle\leq(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\simeq\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})/\mathbb{Q})
. Let
H=\ker\psi_{n}\leq I
. This is a characteristic subgroup of
I
and so normal in
D
also. Thus
\Ind_{I}^{D}\psi_{n}
is the lift of
\Ind_{I/H}^{D/H}\psi_{n}
, viewing
\psi_{n}
as a representation of the quotient
I/H
. One has
D/H=\Gal(\mathcal{K}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K})
, where
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K}
is a tamely ramified extension with inertia group
I/H
. Let
\sigma\in D/H
be a lift of Frobenius from
(D/H)/(I/H)
. Then for
\tau\in I/H
,
\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^{q}
. The character of
\Ind_{I/H}^{D/H}\psi_{n}
is given by

(\Ind_{I/H}^{D/H}\psi_{n})(g)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }g\not\in I/H,\\
\sum_{t\in T}\psi_{n}(tgt^{-1}),&\text{if }g\in I/H,\end{cases}

where
T
is a transversal for
(D/H)/(I/H)
, which we choose to be
T=\langle\sigma\rangle
. For
\tau
a generator the cyclic group
I/H
with
\psi_{n}(\tau)=\zeta_{n}
, one has
\mathbb{Q}(\Ind_{I}^{D}\psi_{n})=\mathbb{Q}(\Ind_{I/H}^{D/H}\psi_{n}(\tau))=\mathbb{Q}(\sum_{i=0}^{2^{k}-1}\zeta_{n}^{q^{i}})\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})^{\langle\zeta_{n}\mapsto\zeta_{n}^{q}\rangle}
(where
\sigma
has order
2^{k}=f_{\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{K}}
and
q^{2^{k}}\equiv 1\mod n
as the extension
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K}
is tamely ramified).

Thus if
K\subset\mathbb{Q}(\Ind_{I}^{D}\psi_{n})
, then
K\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})^{\langle\zeta_{n}\mapsto\zeta_{n}^{q}\rangle}
and so the Artin symbol for
q
in
K/\mathbb{Q}
, and hence also for
l
, is trivial. It follows that
l
splits in
K
and hence is the norm of an ideal of
K
.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

If
K\in\{\mathbb{Q}(i),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\pm 2}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{*}})\}
and
l
is the norm of an ideal of
K
, then
l\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{\times})
.

Proof.

Given an abelian extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
, its genus field is the largest field
L
contained in the Hilbert class field of
K
such that
L/\mathbb{Q}
is an abelian extension. We will use the property that if a prime
l
in
\mathbb{Q}
has residue degree
1
in
L
, then it is the norm of a principal ideal of
K
(this follows from
[
16
, VI.§3, Theorem 3.3]
).

The fields
\mathbb{Q}(i)
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\pm 2})
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{*}})
are equal to their genus fields. As
l
is the norm of an ideal in these fields, it has residue degree
1
and hence is the norm of a principal ideal. Since
\mathbb{Q}(i)
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})
, and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})
for
p\equiv 3\mod 4
are imaginary,
l
is the norm of an element from these fields. For
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
with
p\equiv 1\mod 4
,
-1
is the norm of an element from these fields and so
l
is also.

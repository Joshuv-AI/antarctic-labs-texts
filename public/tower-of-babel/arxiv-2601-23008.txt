Baire-type properties of topological vector spaces
Saak Gabriyelyan, Alexander V. Osipov, Evgenii Reznichenko

Abstract
Burzyk, Kliś and Lipecki proved that every topological vector space (tvs) E E with the property ( K ) (K) is a Baire space. Ka̧kol and Sánchez Ruiz proved that every sequentially complete Fréchet–Urysohn locally convex space (lcs) is Baire. Being motivated by the property ( K ) (K) and the notion of a Mackey null sequence we introduce a property ( M ​ K ) (MK) which is strictly weaker than the property ( K ) (K) , and show that any locally complete lcs has the property ( M ​ K ) (MK) . We prove that any κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn tvs with the property ( M ​ K ) (MK) is a Baire space; consequently, each locally complete κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn lcs is a Baire space. This generalizes both the aforementioned results. We construct a feral Baire space E E with the property ( K ) (K) and which is not κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn. Although a κ \kappa -Fréchet–Urysohn lcs E E can be not a Baire space, we show that E E is always b b -Baire-like in the sense of Ruess. Applications to spaces of Baire functions and C k C_{k} -spaces are given.

Theorem 1.1

Each complete and metrizable tvs

The following characterization of a Baire tvs is due to Saxon
[

Theorem 1.2

A tvs

Since the Baire property was studied in some classes of topological vector spaces or locally convex spaces which include complete and metrizable spaces.

A sequence

Property
(K)
and property
(MK)
of topological vector spaces

In this section we study the property
(K)
and the property
(MK)
and relationships between them. In the next assertion we show that the property
(MK)
is weaker than the property
(K)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If a tvs
E
has the property
(K)
, then it has the property
(MK)
.

Proof.

To prove the proposition it suffices to show that any Mackey null sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
in
E
is a null sequence. To this end, let
U
be an arbitrary absorbent and balanced neighborhood of
0
, and let
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
be an increasing unbounded sequence of positive numbers such that
a_{n}x_{n}\to 0
in
E
. We can assume that
a_{n}\geq 1
. Then there exists
N
such that
a_{n}x_{n}\in U
for any
n>N
. Since
U
is balanced and

a_{n}\geq 1
, we obtain that
x_{n}=\tfrac{1}{a_{n}}\cdot a_{n}x_{n}\in U
for any
n>N
. Thus
x_{n}\to 0
, as desired.
∎

For locally convex spaces the property
(MK)
is weaker than local completeness.

Proposition 2.2
.

A locally complete lcs
E
has the property
(MK)
.

Proof.

Let
S=\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}\subseteq E
be a Mackey null sequence. To prove that
E
has the property
(MK)
, we show that
S
is a
K
-sequence. Let
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in\omega}
be an arbitrary subsequence of
S
. As
S
is Mackey null, there exists an increasing unbounded sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
of positive numbers such that
a_{n}x_{n}\to 0
in
E
. Therefore there is an increasing sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\omega}
in
\omega
such that
a_{n_{k}}\geq 2^{k+1}
and
\{x_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\omega}
is a subsequence of
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in\omega}
. It suffices to show that the series
\sum_{k\in\omega}x_{n_{k}}
converges in
E
.

Set
y_{k}:=a_{n_{k}}x_{n_{k}}
and
\mu_{k}:=\tfrac{1}{a_{n_{k}}}
for
k\in\omega
. Then
x_{n_{k}}=\mu_{k}y_{k}
and
|\mu_{k}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2^{k+1}}
for
k\in\omega
.
Let
K
be the closed absolutely convex hull of
\{y_{k}\}_{k}
. Since
y_{k}\to 0
and
E
is a locally complete lcs,
[
5
, Theorem 2.10.21]
implies that
K
is a compact absolutely convex subset of
E
.
Since
y_{k}\in K
and
|\mu_{k}|\leq\tfrac{1}{2^{k+1}}
for
k\in\omega
, the sequence
\{\sum_{i\leq k}\mu_{k}y_{k}\}_{k\in\omega}
is fundamental in the Banach space
E_{K}
(where
E_{K}
denotes the span of
K
endowed with the norm topology defined by the Minkowski functional of
K
). Therefore the series
\sum_{k\in\omega}\mu_{k}y_{k}
converges in
E_{K}
and hence also in
E
.
∎

Problem 2.3

Is it true that a (sequentially) complete tvs has the property
(MK)

Using Proposition

Example 2.4
.

The complete locally convex space
\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{2}}
has the property
(MK)

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{2}}
is complete, Proposition

implies that it has the property
(MK)

\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\omega}
in

x_{n}=\big(\xi(n)\big)_{\xi\in\ell_{2}}\quad(n\in\omega).

It is clear that it converges to zero in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{2}}

\{x_{n_{k}}\}_{k\in\omega}
be an arbitrary subsequene of

\xi(n)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{k+1},&\mbox{if }n=n_{k}\text{ for some }k\in\omega,\\

The choice of
\xi
implies that the

is infinite. Therefore
\sum_{k}x_{n_{k}}

-Fréchet–Urysohn topological vector spaces

We start this section with the next characterization of tvs which are

Theorem 3.1
.

For a topological vector space

(i)

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Let

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let

(3.1)

for every

(3.2)

,

\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohness implies neither Baireness nor the property

.

Example 4.1
.

The subspace
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{\omega}
of all vectors with finite support is trivially
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn but not Baire and hence, by Theorem
1.5
,
E
does not have the property
(MK)
.

The following example shows that the property
(K)
implies neither
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohness nor Baireness.

Example 4.2
.

The direct locally convex sum
\varphi
of a countably infinite family of
\mathbb{R}
-s has the property
(K)
, but it is neither
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn nor Baire.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is feral (see
[
13
, Proposition 4.3.4]
), it has the property
(K)
by Proposition
2.9
. It is clear that
\varphi
is not a Baire space. Finally,
\varphi
is not a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space by Proposition 2.9 of
[
8
]
.
∎

By Corollary
1.11
, a locally complete
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn locally convex space is Baire. One can naturally ask whether the converse is true, that is:
Is it true that a locally complete Baire space is
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn
? We answer this problem in the negative. First we prove several auxiliary results.

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a sequence
(V_{m})_{m\in\omega}
of open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that

(i)

for every
m\in\omega
,
V_{m}
is bounded and
0\not\in\overline{V_{m}}
;

(ii)

(V_{m})_{m\in\omega}
converges to zero, i.e., for every neighborhood
W
of
0
there is
k\in\omega
such that
V_{m}\subseteq W
for every
m\geq k
;

(iii)

|\{m\in\omega:V_{m}\cap L\neq\emptyset\}|\leq n
for any linear subspace
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
of dimension
n
.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]
the linear space of all polynomials of degree at most
n
over the field
\mathbb{R}
.
We shall identify
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with the dual space
\mathbb{R}_{n}^{\prime}[x]
of
\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]
. With this identification and taking into account that any
n
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}_{n}^{\prime}[x]
is uniquely defined by a nonzero polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]
(as the kernel of
f
under the duality
f(l)=l(f)
for
f\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]
and
l\in\mathbb{R}_{n}^{\prime}[x]
), to prove the lemma we first construct a sequence
(U_{m})_{m\in\omega}
of open
bounded
subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{n}^{\prime}[x]
such that
0\not\in\overline{U_{m}}
for every
m\in\omega
, and

|\{m\in\omega:0\in f(U_{m})\}|\leq n\;\text{ for each }\;f\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]\setminus\{0\}.

(4.1)

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\delta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}_{n}^{\prime}[x]
denote the Dirac functional:
\delta_{t}(f)=f(t)
for
f\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]
.

Claim 1.
If
t_{0},t_{1},...,t_{m}
are distinct elements of
\mathbb{R}
and
m\leq n
, then the functionals
\delta_{t_{0}},\delta_{t_{1}},...,\delta_{t_{m}}
are linearly independent.

Proof of Claim 1.
By adding distinct elements
t_{m+1},\dots,t_{n}
, without loss of generality we assume that
m=n
. For every
i=0,1,...,n
, let
f_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]
be such that
f_{i}(t_{j})=0
if
j\neq i
and
f_{i}(t_{j})=1
if
j=i
. Then the matrix
\big(\delta_{t_{i}}(f_{j})\big)_{ij}=\big(f_{j}(t_{i})\big)_{ij}
is the identity matrix and, therefore, nonsingular. Consequently, the functionals
\delta_{t_{0}},\delta_{t_{1}},...,\delta_{t_{m}}
are linearly independent. Claim 1 is proved.

We set

\varepsilon:=\tfrac{1}{2(n+1)2^{n}}\;\text{ and }\;q_{m}:=(n+1)2^{m}\;\text{ for }\;m\in\omega.

Claim 2.
Let
m_{0},m_{1},...,m_{n}\in\omega
be distinct numbers, and let
f\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]
be such that
|f(q_{m_{i}})|<\varepsilon
for
i=0,1,...,n
. Then
|f(k)|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
for every
k=0,1,...,n
.

Proof of Claim 2.
To prove the claim, for every
i=0,1,...,n
, we set
y_{i}:=f(q_{m_{i}})
and

f_{i}(x)=\frac{(q_{m_{0}}-x)(q_{m_{1}}-x)\cdots(q_{m_{i-1}}-x)(q_{m_{i+1}}-x)\cdots(q_{m_{n}}-x)}{(q_{m_{0}}-q_{m_{i}})(q_{m_{1}}-q_{m_{i}})\cdots(q_{m_{i-1}}-q_{m_{i}})(q_{m_{i+1}}-q_{m_{i}})\cdots(q_{m_{n}}-q_{m_{i}})}.

Then

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_{i}f_{i}(x).

By the assumption of the claim we have
|y_{i}|<\varepsilon
. Since
n<q_{m_{j}}
for each
j=0,1,...,n
, we obtain

|f_{i}(k)|\leq|f_{i}(0)|\;\;\mbox{ for every $k=0,1,...,n$}.

Let us estimate
|f_{i}(0)|
. Since

f_{i}(0)=\frac{1}{(1-\frac{q_{m_{i}}}{q_{m_{0}}})(1-\frac{q_{m_{i}}}{q_{m_{1}}})\cdots(1-\frac{q_{m_{i}}}{q_{m_{i-1}}})(1-\frac{q_{m_{i}}}{q_{m_{i+1}}})\cdots(1-\frac{q_{m_{i}}}{q_{m_{n}}})}

and
|1-\tfrac{q_{m_{i}}}{q_{m_{j}}}|=|1-2^{m_{i}-m_{j}}|\geq\frac{1}{2}
for distinct
i
and
j
, we obtain

|f_{i}(0)|\leq 2^{n}.

Therefore, for every
k=0,1,...,n
, we have

|f(k)|=\Big|\,\sum_{i=0}^{n}y_{i}f_{i}(k)\,\Big|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}|y_{i}|\cdot|f_{i}(k)|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}|y_{i}|\cdot|f_{i}(0)|<\varepsilon\cdot(n+1)2^{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}.

Claim 2 is proved.

Now we define

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle:=\big\{f\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]:|f(\delta_{k})|<1\text{ for }k=0,1,...,n\big\},

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle:=\big\{l\in\mathbb{R}_{n}^{\prime}[x]:l(V)\subseteq(-1,1)\big\}.

Since, by Claim 1,
\delta_{0},\dots,\delta_{n}
are linearly independent in the
(n+1)
-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{\prime}_{n}[x]
, it follows that
V
and hence also
U
are open bounded neighborhoods of zero. For every
m\in\omega
, set

U_{m}:=\delta_{q_{m}}+\varepsilon U.

Then all
U_{m}
are open bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{n}^{\prime}[x]
. We claim that
0\not\in\overline{U_{m}}
. Indeed, choose a polynomial
g\in V
such that
g(q_{m})>2
. Then, for every
l\in U
, we have

g(\delta_{q_{m}}+\varepsilon l)\geq g(q_{m})-\varepsilon>1,

which implies that
0\not\in\overline{U_{m}}
.

Let us check that the sequence
(U_{m})_{m\in\omega}
satisfies condition (
4.1
).
Assuming the converse we could find distinct numbers
m_{0},m_{1},...,m_{n}\in\omega
and
g\in\mathbb{R}_{n}[x]\setminus\{0\}
such that
0\in g(U_{m_{i}})
for
i=0,1,...,n
.
Let
s=\max\{|g(k)|:k=0,1,...,n\}
. Since
g\neq 0
, we have
s>0
. Set
f(x):=\tfrac{2g(x)}{3s}
.
Then
f\in V
and
|f(k_{0})|>\tfrac{1}{2}
for some
k_{0}\in\{0,1,...,n\}
.

Let
i\in\{0,1,...,n\}
. Then
0\in f(U_{m_{i}})
. Choose
h_{i}\in U
such that
f(\delta_{q_{m_{i}}}-\varepsilon h_{i})=0
. Then
f(q_{m_{i}})=\varepsilon f(h_{i})
. Since
f\in V
and
h_{i}\in U
, we have
|f(h_{i})|<1
. Therefore,
|f(q_{m_{i}})|<\varepsilon
. Applying Claim 2 to the polynomial
f
, we obtain that
|f(k_{0})|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
. This is a contradiction.

To finish the proof of the lemma, for every
m\in\omega
, it suffices to set
V_{m}:=a_{m}U_{m}=a_{m}(\delta_{q_{m}}+\varepsilon U)
, where
a_{m}\to 0
sufficiently fast. Then the properties of
U_{m}
-s proved above imply (i)-(iii) for the sequence
(V_{m})_{m\in\omega}
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
A
be an infinite set, and let

\kappa

Suppose for a contradiction that

is a
\kappa

Let
(M_{n})_{n}
be a pairwise disjoint sequence of finite subsets of

. For every

, by Lemma

, there exists a sequence
(V_{n,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of open subsets of

(\alpha)

,

(V_{n,k})_{k}

For every

(a) We claim that

\pi_{M_{n}}(0)\in\overline{\pi_{M_{n}}(U_{n})}\subseteq\overline{V_{n,n}}

(b) We show that

As

(c) Let us show that

and hence

is finite, as desired.

By our supposition,

is a
\kappa
-Fréchet–Urysohn space. Therefore, by (b), there exists a null sequence
(x_{k})_{k}\subseteq U
. Then
x_{k}\in U_{n_{k}}
for some

. Passing to a subsequence if needed and by (a), we can assume that

is a strictly increasing sequence.
Since

is feral, the sequence
(x_{k})_{k}
is contained in some finite-dimensional subspace

. Applying (c) we see that the set

is finite. Consequently,

Let
(G,+)
be an abelian group, and let

where

and
g,h\in G
. An element

is called a

if for any

|M|\leq\omega
and each

such that

. For a Korovin mapping

G_{f}=\{s(g,f):g\in G\}\;\mbox{ of }\;X^{G},

is called a
Korovin orbit
. For

, let
\pi_{M}:X^{G}\to X^{M}
be the projection. If

, we set
\pi_{\alpha}=\pi_{\{\alpha\}}:X^{G}\to X

\pi_{\alpha}(f)=f(\alpha)
for every

.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be a Korovin map. Then for any countable

and each mapping

\varphi=\pi_{\alpha}{\restriction}_{A}
.

Proof.

Let
A=\{f_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where all
f_{n}
are distinct. Then
f_{n}=s(\alpha_{n},f)
for some
\alpha_{n}\in G
; as
f_{n}\not=f_{m}
it follows that also
\alpha_{n}\not=\alpha_{m}
for all distinct
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Set

, and define

by
h(\alpha_{n}):=\varphi(f_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
f
is a Korovin map, for

and

, there is
\alpha\in G
such that

. Hence, for every

\varphi(f_{n})=h(\alpha_{n})=s(\alpha,f)(\alpha_{n})=f(\alpha+\alpha_{n})=f(\alpha_{n}+\alpha)=s(\alpha_{n},f)(\alpha)=f_{n}(\alpha)=\pi_{\alpha}(f_{n}).

Thus

.

Let

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

(iii)

(i) Let

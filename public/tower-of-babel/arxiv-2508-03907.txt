On a class of quasi-Hermitian surfaces in even characteristic
Angela Aguglia, Alessandro Montinaro

Abstract
. In [ 1 ] , a new quasi-Hermitian variety ℋ ε r \mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon}^{r} in PG ⁡ ( r , q 2 ) \mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2}) , with q = 2 e q=2^{e} and e ≥ 3 e\geq 3 an odd integer, was constructed. The variety depends on a primitive element ε \varepsilon of the underlying field GF ⁡ ( q 2 ) \mathrm{GF}(q^{2}) . In the present paper, we first provide a classification of such varieties up to projective equivalence in finite projective spaces of arbitrary dimension. Then, we focus on the case r = 3 r=3 and study the structure of the lines contained in ℋ ε 3 \mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon}^{3} ; as a consequence, we determine the full automorphism group of ℋ ε 3 \mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon}^{3} . Finally, as a byproduct, we prove the equivalence of certain minimal codes introduced in [ 3 ] .



1.
Introduction

In finite geometry, two-character sets have attracted considerable attention due to their distinctive combinatorial properties and wide-ranging applications, including coding theory, strongly regular graphs, association schemes, optimal multiple coverings, and secret sharing schemes (see, for example,
[
7
,
8
,
13
]
).

A two-character set
S
in the Desarguesian projective space

\mathrm{PG}(r,q)

is a set of points such that every hyperplane intersects

S

in either

n_{1}
or

n_{2}
points, for two fixed integers

n_{1}

and
n_{2}
.
The geometric construction of such sets often involves advanced techniques, including commuting polarities, symplectic polarities, normal line-spreads as well as quasi-polar spaces, (see, for example,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
).

In this paper, we focus on a particular class of two-character sets arising from quasi-Hermitian varieties in
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
.
A quasi-Hermitian variety in
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
is a point set that shares the same intersection numbers with hyperplanes as the non-singular Hermitian variety

\mathscr{H}(r,q^{2})
of
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
.
More precisely, the intersection numbers of a quasi-Hermitian variety with hyperplanes are given by:

|\mathscr{H}(r-1,q^{2})|=\frac{(q^{r}+(-1)^{r-1})(q^{r-1}-(-1)^{r-1})}{q^{2}-1},

and

|\Pi_{0}\mathscr{H}(r-2,q^{2})|=\frac{(q^{r}+(-1)^{r-1})(q^{r-1}-(-1)^{r-1})}{q^{2}-1}+(-1)^{r-1}q^{r-1},

where
\Pi_{i}
denotes an
i
-dimensional space of
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
and
\Pi_{0}\mathscr{H}(r-2,q^{2})
is a cone, the join of
the vertex
\Pi_{0}
to a non-singular Hermitian variety
\mathscr{H}(r-2,q^{2})
of a projective subspace
\Pi_{r-2}

disjoint from
\Pi_{0}
.

Since there are only two possible intersection sizes with hyperplanes, a quasi-Hermitian variety is a two-character set.

By definition, every non-singular Hermitian variety in
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
is a quasi-Hermitian variety referred to as the classical quasi-Hermitian variety.
The total number of points in a non-singular Hermitian variety
of
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
is

|\mathscr{H}(r,q^{2})|=\frac{(q^{r+1}+(-1)^{r})(q^{r}-(-1)^{r})}{(q^{2}-1)}.

For
r>2
this coincides with the number of points of any quasi-Hermitian variety in
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
, see
[
19
]
.
In the Desarguesian projective plane
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{2})
a set of points having the same size (that is,
q^{3}+1
) and the same intersection numbers (
1
and
q+1
) with respect to lines as the Hermitian curve is defined to be a unital. However, a set in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{2})
with intersection numbers
1
or
q+1
has size either
q^{3}+1
or
q^{2}+q+1
, corresponding respectively to a unital or a Baer subplane of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{2})
.
Non-classical quasi-Hermitian varieties do exist, as shown in
[
1
,
2
,
12
,
16
,
18
]
.

In 1976, F. Buekenhout introduced a general construction for unitals in finite translation planes of square order
[
6
]
. This construction led to the discovery of non-classical unitals in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{2})
for certain values of
q
. Specifically, when
q\geq 8
is an odd power of
2
, the Bruck–Bose representation of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{2})
in
\mathrm{PG}(4,q)
allows the construction of a unital from an ovoidal cone in
\mathrm{PG}(4,q)
with vertex a point
V
and base a Suzuki–Tits ovoid. Such a unital is known as a Buekenhout–Tits unital, or BT unital.

In
[
1
]
, a new family of non-classical quasi-Hermitian varieties in
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
is introduced. These varieties, called BT quasi-Hermitian varieties, can be viewed as a natural generalization of BT-unitals to higher dimensions, since for
r=2
they coincide with the BT-unitals, (for more details, see Section
2
).

All BT unitals are equivalent under the action of
P\Gamma L(3,q^{2})
as proven in
[
14
]
,
resolving an open problem posed by Barwick and Ebert in
[
5
]
. In the same article, the authors explicitly describe the stabilizer in
P\Gamma L(3,q^{2})
of a BT-unital.

In this paper, we provide a classification up to projective equivalence in arbitrary-dimensional finite projective spaces. Our results show that, in this respect, BT quasi-Hermitian varieties behave analogously to BT unitals in the projective plane: they are all projectively equivalent. Then, we investigate the structure of BT quasi-Hermitian varieties in
\mathrm{PG}(3,q^{2})
and determine their full collineation group.

Finally, as a byproduct of our analysis, we establish the equivalence of certain minimal codes introduced in
[
3
]
.

Our long-term goal is to develop a group-theoretical characterization of BT quasi-Hermitian varieties, in order to distinguish them from all other quasi-Hermitian varieties in projective spaces of the same dimension and order.
In this direction, Lemma
2.1
,
which states that any Sylow
p
-subgroup of the collineation group of a BT quasi-Hermitian variety fixes a unique incident point-hyperplane pair of
PG(r,q^{2})
(any
r>2
),
appears to be a promising starting point.

2.
Preliminaries

In
[
1
]
, the first author introduced a new family of non-classical quasi-Hermitian varieties which are linked to the Buekenhout-Tits unitals. The construction is as follows.

Let consider the projective space
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
of order
q=2^{e}
,
e
an odd number, the homogeneous coordinates of its points and denote by

(X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}).

Let

\Pi_{\infty}:=\{(X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})\in\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})|\,X_{0}=0\}

be the hyperplane of the ’points at infinity’. Then
\mathrm{AG}(r,q^{2})
is the affine space
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})\setminus\Pi_{\infty}
with points having coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
where
x_{i}=X_{i}/X_{0}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.

The the trace and norm functions of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are

\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{2}/q}(x):=x+x^{q},\qquad\mathrm{N}_{q^{2}/q}(x):=x^{q+1}\qquad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}},

respectively, and
\sigma:x\rightarrow x^{2^{\frac{e+1}{2}}}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, being
q=2^{e}
with
e
odd.

Let
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
be such that
\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon+\delta=0
for some
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{1\}
,
\mathrm{Tr}_{q/2}(\delta)=1
. It is straightforward to see that
\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{2}/q}(\varepsilon)=1
. The pair
(1,\varepsilon)
is a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and hence
a=a_{0}+a_{1}\varepsilon
with
a_{0},a_{1}
suitable elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

Let
\mathscr{V}^{r}_{\varepsilon}
be the variety with affine equation:

(1)

x_{r}^{q}+x_{r}=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(x_{1})+\ldots+\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(x_{r-1})\text{,}

where

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(x)=[x+(x^{q}+x)\varepsilon]^{\sigma+2}+(x^{q}+x)^{\sigma}+(x^{2q}+x^{2})\varepsilon+x^{q+1}+x^{2}

and consider the Hermitian cone

(2)

\mathscr{\mathscr{H}}^{r}_{\varepsilon,\infty}:=\{(0,X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})|\quad X_{1}^{q+1}+\ldots+X_{r-1}^{q+1}=0\}.

Let us define

(3)

\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon}:=(\mathscr{V}_{\varepsilon}^{r}\setminus\Pi_{\infty})\cup\mathscr{\mathscr{H}}^{r}_{\varepsilon,\infty}.

It is shown in
[
1
]
that,
\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon}
is a non-classical quasi-Hermitian variety of
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
and a Buekenhout-Tits unital for
r=2
.
We call
\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon}
a
Buekenhout-Tits
or
BT quasi-Hermitian variety
of
\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
for
r\geq 2
.

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be the number of hyperplanes of
PG(r,q^{2})
intersecting
the BT-variety
\mathscr{H}^{3}_{\varepsilon}
in

N_{1}=\frac{\left(q^{r}-(-1)^{r}\right)\left(q^{r-1}-(-1)^{r-1}\right)}{q^{2}-1}

or

N_{2}=\frac{\left(q^{r}-(-1)^{r}\right)\left(q^{r-1}-(-1)^{r-1}\right)}{q^{2}-1}+(-1)^{r-1}q^{r-1}

points, respectively. Since
\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon}
has the same size, as well as the intersection numbers with the hyperplanes, of a non-degenerate Hermitian variety
\mathscr{H}(r,q^{2})
, it follows that

M_{1}=\frac{\left(q^{r+1}-(-1)^{r+1}\right)\left(q^{r+1}+(-1)^{r+1}-\left(q^{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)}{\left(q^{2}-1\right)}

and

M_{2}=\left(q^{2}-1\right)\left(q^{r+1}-(-1)^{r+1}\right)\text{.}

In particular,
M_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. Therefore, any Sylow
p
-subgroup of
Aut(\mathscr{H}^{3}_{\varepsilon})
, the full automorphism group of
\mathscr{H}^{3}_{\varepsilon}
, preserves at least a hyperplane of
PG(r,q^{2})
among the
X_{2}
-secant ones.

As pointed out in
[
1
, Remark at pag. 36]
, the elementary abelian
p
-group of order
q^{r}
defined by

E=\left\langle\tau_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}}:\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},i=1,...,r\right\rangle\text{,}

where
\tau_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}}
is the collineation of
PGL_{4}(q^{2})
represented by the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&\gamma_{1}\varepsilon&\gamma_{2}\varepsilon&\cdots&\gamma_{r-1}\varepsilon&\gamma_{r}+(\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{r-1})^{\sigma}\varepsilon\\
0&1&0&\cdots&0&\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{1}\varepsilon\\
0&0&1&\cdots&0&\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{2}\varepsilon\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1&\gamma_{r-1}+\gamma_{r-1}\varepsilon\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&1\end{array}\right)\text{,}

is a subgroup
Aut(\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon})
. It is easy to see that
\Pi_{\infty}:X_{0}=0
, and its point
P_{\infty}(0,0,0,\ldots,0,1)
, are the unique
E
-invariant hyperplane and point of
PG(r,q^{2})
, respectively. Moreover, the following holds:

Lemma 2.1
.

Any Sylow
p
-subgroup of
Aut(\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon})
fixes a unique incident point-hyperplane pair of
PG(r,q^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
U
be any Sylow
p
-subgroup of
Aut(\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon})
containing
E
and let
\Pi_{U}
be a
U
-invariant hyperplane secant to
\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon}
since
M_{2}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
. Then
\Pi_{U}=\Pi_{\infty}
since
\Pi_{\infty}
is the unique
E
-invariant hyperplane of
PG(r,q^{2})
. Moreover,
U
fixes at least one of the
\frac{q^{2(r+1)}-1}{q^{2}-1}
points of
PG(r,q^{2})
, which necessarily coincide with
P_{\infty}
since this one is the unique point of
PG(r,q^{2})
fixed by
E
with
E\leq U
. Therefore,
P_{\infty}(0,0,0,1)
and
\Pi_{\infty}:X_{0}=0
are the unique
U
-invariant
point and hyperplane of
PG(r,q^{2})
. The assertion now follows from fact that all Sylow
p
-subgroups of
Aut(\mathscr{H}^{r}_{\varepsilon})
lies in a unique conjugacy class.
∎

3.
Projective Equivalence of
\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon}^{r}

We are going to prove that the BT quasi-Hermitian varieties
\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon}^{r}
in

\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
,
r\geq 2

are projectively equivalent.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
be such that
\varepsilon_{i}^{2}+\varepsilon_{i}=\delta_{i}
with
T(\delta_{i})=1
,
i=1,2
. Assume that
\alpha=\varepsilon_{2}-\varepsilon_{1}
,
B=(\frac{\alpha}{\delta_{1}})^{\sigma/2}
,
\rho=\left(\frac{\delta_{1}}{\delta_{2}}\right)^{\sigma/2+1}
. Then, the projectivity

\xi
represented by the matrix of order
r+1

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1&\ B\rho\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}&\ B\rho\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}&\cdots&\ B\rho\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}&\ \ (r-1)\rho^{2}B^{\sigma+2}\frac{\varepsilon_{2}^{q+2}}{\varepsilon_{1}^{q+1}}\\
0&\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}&0&\cdots&0&B\rho^{2}\frac{\varepsilon_{2}^{q+1}}{\varepsilon_{1}^{q+1}}\\
0&0&\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}&\cdots&0&B\rho^{2}\frac{\varepsilon_{2}^{q+1}}{\varepsilon_{1}^{q+1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}&B\rho^{2}\frac{\varepsilon_{2}^{q+1}}{\varepsilon_{1}^{q+1}}\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&\frac{\rho^{2}\varepsilon_{2}^{q+1}}{\varepsilon_{1}^{q+1}}\end{array}\right)

maps
\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{r}
onto
\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{2}}^{r}
. In particular,
\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{r}
and
\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{2}}^{r}
are equivalent.

Proof.

Clearly,
\xi
preserves
\Pi_{\infty}
. Now, let
P
be any point of
\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{r}
. If
P\in\Pi_{\infty}\cap\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{r}
then
P=(0,a_{1},\ldots,a_{r-1},a_{r})
with
N(a_{1})+\cdots+N(a_{r-1})=0
, and hence

P^{\xi}=\left(0,a_{1}\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}},\ldots,a_{r-1}\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}},\mu\right),

with
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
lies in
\Pi_{\infty}\cap\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{r}=\Pi_{\infty}\cap\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{2}}^{r}
since

N\left(a_{1}\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)+\cdots+N\left(a_{r-1}\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)=N\left(\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)\left[N(a_{1})+\cdots+N(a_{r-1})\right]=0\text{.}

If
P\in\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{r}\setminus\Pi_{\infty}
, then
P=(1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{r-1},a_{r})
with
a_{i}=a_{i,0}+a_{i,1}\varepsilon
and (unique)
a_{i,0},a_{i,1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for each
i=1,...,r
. Hence,

(4)

a_{r,1}=a_{1,0}^{\sigma+2}+a_{1,0}a_{1,1}+a_{1,1}^{\sigma}+\ldots+a_{r-1,0}^{\sigma+2}+a_{r-1,0}a_{r-1,1}+a_{r-1,1}^{\sigma}\text{,}

and hence
P^{\xi}=(1,X_{1},\ldots,X_{r-1},X_{r})
with

X_{i}=\frac{\rho\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\left(B+a_{i}\right),i=1,...,r-1,\text{ and }X_{r}=\frac{\rho^{2}\varepsilon_{2}^{q+1}}{\varepsilon_{1}^{q+1}}\left(B(a_{1}+\ldots+a_{r-1})+a_{r}\right)+(r-1)B^{\sigma+2}\varepsilon_{2}\text{.}

Since
\delta_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}^{q+1}
we get
1/\varepsilon_{1}=(1+\varepsilon_{1})/\delta_{1}
and hence

\varepsilon_{2}/\varepsilon_{1}=\delta_{2}/\delta_{1}+(\alpha/\delta_{1})\varepsilon_{2}
Thus,

X_{i}=\frac{\rho\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}(B+a_{i,0})+\rho\left[\frac{\alpha}{\delta_{1}}(B+a_{i,0})+a_{i,1}\right]\varepsilon_{2},

for all

i=1,...,r-1
.

Now,
P^{\xi}\in\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{2}}^{r}
if and only if

T(X_{r})=\Gamma_{\varepsilon_{2}}(X_{1})+\ldots+\Gamma_{\varepsilon_{2}}(X_{r-1}),

which is
equivalent to

T_{X_{r}}=Re_{X_{1}}^{\sigma+2}+Re_{X_{1}}T_{X_{1}}+T_{X_{1}}^{\sigma}+\ldots+Re_{X_{r-1}}^{\sigma+2}+Re_{X_{r-1}}T_{X_{r-1}}+T_{X_{r-1}}^{\sigma}\text{,}

where

Re_{X_{i}}=\frac{\rho\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}(B+a_{i,0})
,

T_{X_{i}}=T(X_{i})=\rho\left[\frac{\alpha}{\delta_{1}}(B+a_{i,0})+a_{i,1}\right]
for all

i=1,...,r-1
, and

T_{X_{r}}=T(X_{r})=\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\rho^{2}\left[a_{r,1}+B\left(a_{1,1}+\ldots+a_{r-1,1})+(r-1)B^{\sigma+2}\right)\right]

Indeed, as
\rho^{(\sigma-2)}=\delta_{2}/\delta_{1}
, that is
\rho^{\sigma}=\rho^{2}\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}
, we get

T(X_{r})=\rho^{\sigma}\left[a_{1,0}^{\sigma+2}+a_{1,0}a_{1,1}+a_{1,1}^{\sigma}+\ldots+a_{r-1,0}^{\sigma+2}+a_{r-1,0}a_{r-1,1}+a_{r-1,1}^{\sigma}\right]+

+\rho^{\sigma}B\left(a_{1,1}+\ldots+a_{r-1,1}\right)+\rho^{\sigma}(r-1)B^{\sigma+2}

by (
4
). Moreover,

(5)

Re_{X_{i}}^{\sigma+2}=(\rho\delta_{2}/\delta_{1})^{\sigma+2}(a_{i,0}^{\sigma+2}+a_{i,0}^{2}B^{\sigma}+a_{i,0}^{\sigma}B^{2}+B^{\sigma+2}),

(6)

Re_{X_{i}}T_{X_{i}}=\alpha(\rho^{2}\delta_{2}/\delta_{1}^{2})(a_{i,0}^{2}+B^{2})+(\rho^{2}\delta_{2}/\delta_{1})(a_{i,0}a_{i,1}+a_{i,1}B),

(7)

T_{X_{i}}^{\sigma}=\rho^{\sigma}(\alpha/\delta_{1})^{\sigma}(a_{i,0}^{\sigma}+B^{\sigma})+\rho^{\sigma}a_{i,1}^{\sigma},

for
i=1,\ldots r-1
.

Thus, given that
(\rho\delta_{2}/\delta_{1})^{\sigma+2}=\rho^{2}\delta_{2}/\delta_{1}=\rho^{\sigma}
,
B^{\sigma}=\alpha/\delta_{1}
and
\sigma^{2}=2
, we obtain

(\rho\delta_{2}/\delta_{1})^{\sigma+2}(a_{i,0}^{\sigma}B^{2}+a_{i,0}^{2}B^{\sigma})+\rho^{2}\delta_{2}\alpha(a_{i,0}^{2}+B^{2})/\delta_{1}^{2}+\rho^{\sigma}\alpha^{\sigma}(a_{i,0}^{\sigma}+B^{\sigma})/\delta_{1}^{\sigma}=

\rho^{\sigma}\left[a_{i,0}^{\sigma}\frac{\alpha^{\sigma}}{\delta_{1}^{\sigma}}+a_{i,0}^{2}\frac{\alpha}{\delta_{1}}+a_{i,0}^{2}\frac{\alpha}{\delta_{1}}+\frac{\alpha^{\sigma+1}}{\delta_{1}^{\sigma+1}}+a_{i,0}^{\sigma}\frac{\alpha^{\sigma}}{\delta_{1}^{\sigma}}+\frac{\alpha^{\sigma+1}}{\delta_{1}^{\sigma+1}}\right]=0.

This implies

\Gamma_{\varepsilon_{2}}(X_{i})=Re_{X_{i}}^{\sigma+2}+Re_{X_{i}}T_{X_{i}}+T_{X_{i}}^{\sigma}=\rho^{\sigma}(B^{\sigma+2}+a_{i,0}a_{i,1}+a_{i,0}^{\sigma+2}+a_{i,1}^{\sigma}+Ba_{i,1})

for all
i=1,\ldots,r-1
.
Therefore,

\Gamma_{\varepsilon_{2}}(X_{1})+\ldots+\Gamma_{\varepsilon_{2}}(X_{r-1})=T_{X_{r}}

hence,
(\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{r})^{\xi}=\mathscr{H}_{\varepsilon_{2}}^{r}
, which is the assertion.
∎

Minimal linear codes and some open problems

Let
\Omega
be a set of
points in
\mathrm{PG}(r,q)
. A linear code
C(\Omega)
of length
|\Omega|
and dimension
r+1
can be constructed by taking as its generator
matrix the one whose columns are the homogeneous coordinates of the points in
\Omega
.

An
[n,r+1,d]
–linear code
C
is defined to be minimal if all of its codewords are minimal, meaning that each codeword is uniquely determined, up to a nonzero scalar multiple, by its support.
Minimal codes are relevant in
applications such as secret-sharing schemes and secure two-party computation; see

[
17
,
9
]
.

Two codes are considered equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by a combination of coordinate permutations and scalar multiplications.
This notion allows researchers to classify codes up to equivalence, significantly reducing the complexity of the classification problem. Demonstrating that two codes are equivalent implies that they possess the same structural and combinatorial properties, which is particularly useful when generalizing results or identifying canonical representatives within a family of codes.

In
[
3
]
, the authors studied the projective linear code
C=C(\mathscr{V}^{r}_{\varepsilon})
induced by the projective system of the
\mathbb{F}_{2^{2e}}
-rational points of the variety

\mathscr{V}^{r}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathrm{PG}(r,q^{2})
, with
r\geq 3
, defined by the affine equation (
1
).
They determined their weights and proved that the code
\mathscr{C}(\mathscr{V}^{3}_{\varepsilon})
is a minimal linear code if and only if

, whereas
\mathscr{C}(\mathscr{V}^{r}_{\varepsilon})
with
r>3
is minimal for every odd
e>1
.

Now, consider the projectivity
\xi
of

3.1
. It is easy to see that
\xi
fixes

Since

, the projectivity
\xi

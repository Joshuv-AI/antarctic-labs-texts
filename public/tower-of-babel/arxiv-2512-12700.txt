Sharp Time Estimates for Some Nonlocal Evolution Equations
Xiaolei Yang School of Mathematics and Information Science, Henan Polytechnic University, Jiaozuo 454003, China (Email: xlyang@hpu.edu.cn)

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the nonlocal evolution equation u t = d ⁡ ( J ∗ u ) − u u_{t}=d(J*u)-u in ℝ \mathbb{R} . We present a new method to prove that e ( d − 1 ) ​ t e^{(d-1)t} is the sharp time bound.

Introduction

According to Fife, (spatially) nonlocal evolutions are one of the nonclassical trends to be emphasized
[
1
]
. Nonlocal evolution equations have gained widespread popularity in the modeling and study of various phenomena, such as materials science
[
2
]
, flat optics
[
3
]
, peridynamic theory
[
4
]
, etc. For more application fields and systematic mathematical theories, one can refer to
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and the references therein. Here, we want to study the time estimates for the nonlocal evolution equation with the form

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t,x)=d(J*u)(t,x)-u(t,x),&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

(1)

where
d>0
and
\left({J*u}\right)\left({t,x}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{J\left({x-y}\right)}u\left({t,y}\right)\mathrm{d}y.
Throughout the text, assume that
J(x)
satisfies condition:

(H1)

J(x)\in\mathbb{C}(\mathbb{R})
is a nonnegative function with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)\mathrm{d}x=1
.

Eq. (
1
) was proposed by Chasseigne, Chaves and Rossi
[
7
]
when they discussed the possible extensions of nonlocal diffusion equations

u_{t}(t,x)=(J*u)(t,x)-u(t,x).

(2)

In many literatures, Eq. (
2
) is also referred to as the nonlocal dispersal equation.

There is already a substantial body of literature on the long-time asymptotic behavior of nonlocal diffusion equations and its variations. Among these publications, energy methods
[
7
,
8
]
and the method of sub- and super-solutions
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
are particularly prevalent. It should be mentioned that, by employing the series representation of the solution and refined estimates of the series, Khomrutai significantly generalized the work of
[
7
]
.

Following the description in
[
7
]
, Eq. (
2
) can be employed to model diffusion processes in the absence of external or internal sources. Integrating (
2
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
we get
\frac{d}{{dt}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}{u\left({t,x}\right)\mathrm{d}x=0,}
therefore, the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}}{u(t,x)\mathrm{d}x}
is conserved. This property fails for (
1
). To obtain model (
1
), a source term must be added. Noting that the term
J*u
corresponds to particles that undergo a change in position, whereas the term
-u
corresponds to particles that maintain a fixed position, one may consider the variation of certain energy of the particles. For instance, changing in vertical position must be accompanied by a change in potential energy, pure frictional force causes energy loss in moving objects; then, equation (
2
) can be modified to

u_{t}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}d(x,y)J(x-y)u(t,y)\mathrm{d}y-u(t,x).

where
d(x,y)\in(0,1)
stands for proportion of energy change. Under ideal conditions, take
d(x,y)\equiv d
, Eq. (
1
) can be obtained. When
d>1
, we call it “new environment acceleration type”; when
0<d<1
, we call it “new environment deceleration type”.

In this paper, we consider the time estimates for the solution of (
1
) under some conditions. The method we proposed here is based on the Leibniz test and Fourier transform. We have not found any prior work where this approach has been employed to address similar problems so far. Strictly speaking, since we do not require
t\rightarrow\infty
, it is perhaps incapable of being termed a “long-time behavior”. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we give some preliminaries. In section 3, we give the main results. In section 4, we give the detailed proof. In section 5, we give anexample. In section 6, we give some discussions and furthermore directions.

Preliminaries

We recall some definitions and properties provided below which can be found in

.

Definition 1
.

For
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, its Fourier transform
\hat{u}=\mathscr{F}(u)
and inverse Fourier transform
\check{u}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(u)
are respectively defined as

\hat{u}(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\,dx,\quad\check{u}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\xi}\,d\xi.

For
u\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, the Fourier transform
\hat{u}
and inverse Fourier transform
\check{u}
are defined in the sense of distributions, that is,

\left\langle{\hat{u},g}\right\rangle=\left\langle{u,\hat{g}}\right\rangle,\quad\left\langle{\check{u},g}\right\rangle=\left\langle{u,\check{g}}\right\rangle,\quad\forall g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}).

Lemma 1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Given
u
in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
(\hat{u})\check{}=u
.

Lemma 2
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, for any
\xi_{0}\neq 0
, there exists an even continuous probability density
J(x)
such that
\hat{J}(\xi)
is radially decreasing and satisfies

\hat{J}(\xi_{0})>1-\varepsilon.

Proof.

Consider the centered normal density
J_{\sigma}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}
with
\sigma>0.
Then,
\hat{J}(\xi)=e^{-2\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}\xi^{2}}
. It is not difficult to deduce the result of Lemma
2
.
∎

3
Main results

Now we state main result as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u_{0}(x)\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and
\hat{u}_{0}(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Under the condition of
(H1)
, denote the solution of Eq.(
1
) as
u(t,x)
, then,

|u(t,x)|\leq\|\hat{u}_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}e^{(d-1)t}.

(3)

Furthermore,
e^{(d-1)t}
is sharp in the sense that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there are
J(x)
and
u_{0}(x)
such that
u(t,x)
satisies

g(x)e^{(d-1-\varepsilon)t}<|u(t,x)|<h(x)e^{(d-1)t},

(4)

where
g(x),h(x)>0
are determined only by
u_{0}(x)
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1

When
J(x)=\delta(x)
, noting that
(\delta*u)(t,x)=u(t,x)
, system (
1
) then reduces to the local case

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t,x)=(d-1)u(t,x),&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

which has the solution
u(t,x)=e^{(d-1)t}u_{0}(x)
. The rate
e^{(d-1)t}
emerges here.

Step 1:
we show that
e^{(d-1)t}
is an upper bound. Let
u_{0}(x)\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and
\hat{u}_{0}(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Applying the Fourier transform to (
1
) gives

\begin{cases}\hat{u}_{t}(t,\xi)=d\hat{J}(\xi)\cdot\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\hat{u}(t,\xi),&t>0,\xi\in\mathbb{R},\\
\hat{u}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),&\xi\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

Hence
\hat{u}(t,\xi)=e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u_{0}}(\xi)
. The term
e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}
is bounded under condition
(H1)
, therefore, we can apply inverse Fourier transform to
\hat{u}(t,\xi)
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle|u(t,x)|

\displaystyle=|\mathscr{F}^{-1}(e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0})(t,x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}||\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq e^{t(d-1)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=e^{t(d-1)}\|\hat{u}_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}.

Thus, (
3
) holds.

Step 2:
We choose suitable functions to ensure the sharpness of
Theorem
1
. Here we need to impose the following strong condition:

(H2)

\hat{u}_{0}
is a nonagetive even function and radially decreasing, whatsmore, for any
x>0
,

\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4x}}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\cdot\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi>0.

(5)

From now on, let
J
is a centered normal distribution whose parameter
\sigma
to be determined later. Then,

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(\hat{u}(t,\xi)\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)(\cos 2\pi x\xi+i\sin 2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

where we have used the facts that
\sin(2\pi x\xi)
is an odd function and
e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. It is easy to know that
u(t,x)
is even, then, it suffices to consider the case
x\geq 0
.

When
x>0
, let

A_{k}=\int_{\frac{2k-1}{4x}}^{\frac{2k+1}{4x}}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z};

B_{k}=\int_{\frac{2k-1}{4x}}^{\frac{2k+1}{4x}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Here,
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
are functions of
x
, and the same applies to
C_{k}
mentioned later. Note that
\hat{J}(\xi)=e^{-2\pi^{2}\sigma^{2}\xi^{2}}
is a radially decreasing positive function, and for every
k
, the integration intervals for both
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
are
\left((\frac{2k-1}{4x}),(\frac{2k+1}{4x})\right)
, then
2\pi x\xi\in(k\pi-\frac{\pi}{2},k\pi+\frac{\pi}{2})
, thus integrands associated with
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
do not change sign in the corresponding interval. With these properties, we can show that:

(i)
A_{k}=A_{-k},B_{k}=B_{-k}
since the integrands are even;

(ii) Based on the sign of the integrand, we can easily determine whether
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
are positive or negative:

A_{k},B_{k}=\begin{cases}>0,&k=2m;\\
<0,&k=2m+1.\end{cases}

(iii)
B_{k}
can be estimated by
A_{k}
,

|A_{k}|e^{t\left(d\hat{J}\left(\frac{2k+1}{4x}\right)-1\right)}<|B_{k}|<|A_{k}|e^{t\left(d\hat{J}\left(\frac{2k-1}{4x}\right)-1\right)},\quad|k|\geq 1

(6)

|A_{0}|e^{t\left(d\hat{J}\left(\frac{1}{4x}\right)-1\right)}<|B_{0}|<|A_{0}|e^{t(d-1)}.

(7)

(iv)
|A_{k}|
and
|B_{k}|
are both monotonically decreasing for
k\geq 0
. Indeed, when
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|A_{k+1}|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\frac{2k+1}{4x}}^{\frac{2k+3}{4x}}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\right|=\int_{\frac{2k+1}{4x}}^{\frac{2k+3}{4x}}\left|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{2k-1}{4x}}^{\frac{2k+1}{4x}}\left|\hat{u}_{0}\left(\xi+\frac{1}{2x}\right)\cos(2\pi x\xi+\pi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{2k-1}{4x}}^{\frac{2k+1}{4x}}\left|\hat{u}_{0}\left(\xi+\frac{1}{2x}\right)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle<\int_{\frac{2k-1}{4x}}^{\frac{2k+1}{4x}}\left|\hat{u}_{0}\left(\xi\right)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi=|A_{k}|.

Besides,
|A_{0}|>|A_{1}|
also holds since

for

. Taking into consideration that

also decreases radially, we obtain the monotonicity of
|B_{k}|
for
k\geq 0
.

(v) Since
\hat{u}_{0},e^{t(d\hat{J}-1)}\hat{u}_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then, series
A_{n}
and

.

Now, define sequence

as

C_{n}=\begin{cases}B_{0},&n=0,\\
B_{n}+B_{-n},&n>0.\end{cases}

Then, we have

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}B_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C_{k}.

Combined with E.q. (
6
), (

), we know that

0<A_{0}e^{t\left(d\hat{J}\left(\frac{1}{4x}\right)-1\right)}<C_{0}=B_{0}<A_{0}e^{t(d-1)},

C_{1}=B_{1}+B_{-1}\geq 2A_{1}e^{t\left(d\hat{J}\left(\frac{1}{4x}\right)-1\right)}
. Note that condition
(H2)
means
\frac{1}{2}A_{0}+A_{1}>0
, hence

(A_{0}+2A_{1})e^{t(d\hat{J}(\frac{1}{4x})-1)}<C_{0}+C_{1}<u(t,x)<C_{0}<A_{0}e^{t(d-1)}.

(8)

Fix
x_{0}>0
small enough such that

\int_{-\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}^{\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\geq\frac{3\|\hat{u}_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}}{4},

Then, for

,

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{|\xi|>\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi-\int_{|\xi|>\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}\left|e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi-\int_{|\xi|>\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}\left|e^{t(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\frac{1}{8x_{0}})-1)}\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi-e^{t(d\hat{J}(\frac{1}{8x_{0}})-1)}\int_{|\xi|>\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}\left|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\cos(2\pi x\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3\sqrt{2}}{8}e^{t(d\hat{J}(\frac{1}{8x_{0}})-1)}\|\hat{u}_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}-e^{t(d\hat{J}(\frac{1}{8x_{0}})-1)}\int_{|\xi|>\frac{1}{8x_{0}}}\left|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi

(
8
) shows that for

u(t,x)\geq(A_{0}+2A_{1})e^{t(d\hat{J}(\frac{1}{4x_{0}})-1)}.

5
Examples

We claim that there exist functions satisfying
(H2)
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed,
\frac{e^{-|\xi|}}{\sqrt{\xi}}
is such a function. A direct calculation gives

\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{{4x}}}{\frac{{\cos\left({2\pi x\cdot\xi}\right)}}{{\sqrt{\xi}}}\mathrm{d}\xi=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{x}}}}\mathrm{FresnelC}\left({\sqrt{3}}\right)>0,

where
\mathrm{FresnelC}(x)
is the Fresnel cosine integral defined by

\mathrm{FresnelC}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}{\cos\left({\frac{\pi}{2}{t^{2}}}\right)}dt.

Thus, we have

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{{4x}}}{\frac{{{e^{-\xi}}}}{{\sqrt{\xi}}}}\cos\left({2\pi x\cdot\xi}\right)d\xi>{e^{-\frac{1}{{4x}}}}\mathrm{FresnelC}(1)>0,

{e^{-\frac{1}{{4x}}}}\left({\mathrm{FresnelC}(\sqrt{3})-\mathrm{FresnelC}(1)}\right)<\int_{\frac{1}{{4x}}}^{\frac{3}{{4x}}}{\frac{{{e^{-\xi}}}}{{\sqrt{\xi}}}}\cos\left({2\pi x\cdot\xi}\right)\mathrm{d}\xi<0.

Then, we obtain

\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{{4x}}}{e^{-\xi}\frac{{\cos\left({2\pi x\cdot\xi}\right)}}{{\sqrt{\xi}}}\mathrm{d}\xi>\frac{1}{{e^{\frac{1}{{4x}}}\sqrt{x}}}}\mathrm{FresnelC}\left({\sqrt{3}}\right)>0.

Besides,
\frac{e^{-|\xi|}}{\sqrt{|\xi|}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is obvious.

We now turn our attention to
\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\hat{u}_{0})
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\hat{u}_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\frac{1}{{{e^{\left|\xi\right|}}\sqrt{\left|\xi\right|}}}}{e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}}\mathrm{d}\xi=2\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{{{e^{\xi}}\sqrt{\xi}}}}\cos(2\pi x\cdot\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Re}\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-(1+2\pi xi)\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi=2\mathrm{Re}\left((1+2\pi xi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\pi}\mathrm{Re}(1+2\pi xi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

Denote
1+2\pi xi=\sqrt{1+4\pi^{2}x^{2}}e^{i\theta}
, where
\sin\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4\pi^{2}x^{2}}},\cos\theta=\frac{2\pi x}{\sqrt{1+4\pi^{2}x^{2}}}
. Then,

(1+2\pi\xi i)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=(1+4\pi^{2}x^{2})^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{i\theta}{2}.}

Thus,
\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\hat{u}_{0})(x)=2\sqrt{\pi}(1+4\pi^{2}x^{2})^{-\frac{1}{4}}\cos(\frac{\theta}{2})=\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{1+4\pi^{2}x^{2}}+1}{1+4\pi^{2}x^{2}}}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}).
By
Lemma
1
,
u_{0}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\hat{u}_{0})
in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}).

Remark 1
.

Indeed,
\frac{f(\xi)}{\sqrt{\xi}}
is suitable whenever
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a positive function and decreases radially.

Remark 2
.

Since our estimate is pointwise, if
\hat{u}_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is is a non-negative, radially decreasing, even function, but the inequality (
5
) does not hold uniformly, we can also obtain the time estimate for the corresponding points. Especially, the inequality (
5
) will always hold for
x
small enough.

Remark 3
.

The condition that
\hat{u}_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is crucial—it ensures that we can partition the integration region
\mathbb{R}
into countable subsets and arbitrarily specify the summation order of the integrals over these subregions.

6
Discussions and Furthermore Directions

Using the values of the Fourier transform of the kernel function near zero, Chasseigne, Chaves and Rossi
[
7
]
obtained the long-time asymptotic behavior of the solution for Eq.(
2
); In this paper, however, using the values of the Fourier transform of
u_{0}
in a specific neighborhood of the origin, we have derived a uniform-in-time estimate for the solution of Eq.(
1
) under certain conditions. This result can, in a sense, be interpreted as the “dual” of
[
7
]
.

Finally, we present some questions that we think interesting:

1. For a general density function
J(x)
, can the solution be obtained through a continuous variation from a combination of
\delta
functions? Such as
J(x)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}p_{i}\delta(x-x_{i})
, where
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}p_{i}=1
.

2. If
\hat{u}_{0}
is not radial, is there a proper partition of
\mathbb{R}
, say
\{U_{n}\}_{n}^{\infty}
,
such that the sequence

\mathrm{Re}(\int_{U_{k}}e^{(d\hat{J}(\xi)-1)t}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi)

satisfies Leibniz test?

3. Our calculation of the time exponent in
B_{k}
is rather crude. Is it possible to obtain finer estimates by incorporating other mathematical properties (such as convexity/concavity)?

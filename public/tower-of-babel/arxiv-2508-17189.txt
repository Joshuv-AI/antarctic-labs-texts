Representations by probabilistic Frobenius-Euler and degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials Thanks: * corresponding author
Taekyun Kim*, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. Let Y Y be a random variable whose moment generating function exists in a neighborhood of the origin. The aim of this paper is to represent arbitrary polynomials in terms of probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with Y Y and probabilistic degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with Y Y , and more generally of their higher-order counterparts. We derive explicit formulas with the help of umbral calculus and illustrate our results in the case of several random variables Y Y .

p(i)=e^{-\alpha}\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!},\quad i=0,1,2,\dots.

Then our results in this case for

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{l=r}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1}(l,r)S_{1}(n,l)+\frac{n}{1-u}\sum_{l=r}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1}(l,r)S_{1}(n-1,l)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u),

Let
B_{n}(x)
be Bernoulli polynomials given by
\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
. Then, for any polynomial
p(x)
of degree
n
, we have the following formula:

(1.1)

p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}B_{k}(x),\quad a_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}p^{(k)}(x)dx,\quad(k=0,1,\dots,n),

where
p^{(k)}(x)=(\frac{d}{dx})^{k}p(x)
.

In
[
18
]

1.1

p(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{k(n-k)}B_{k}(x)B_{n-k}(x)

(1.2)

\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{B_{k}(x)B_{n-k}(x)}{k(n-k)}=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\frac{1}{n-k}\binom{n}{k}B_{n-k}B_{k}(x)+\frac{2}{n}H_{n-1}B_{n}(x),

n\geq 2

H_{n}=1+\frac{1}{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n}
. Substituting

x=0
and

[
26

[
9
]
).
In contrast to the considerably more complex existing proofs, our derivation of these identities relies on the remarkably straightforward formula (
1.1
), utilizing only derivatives and integrals of the given polynomials. For Miki’s identity, Gessel

Similarly, Dunne and Schubert

Let
Y
be a random variable whose moment generating function exists in a neighborhood of the origin:

(1.3)

E[e^{Yt}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,\,\,\mathrm{exists,\,\,for}\,\,|x|<r,

for some positive real number
r
.

Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let

(1.4)

S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\geq 1),\quad S_{0}=0.

The probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
,
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
, are given by (see (
1.4
))

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(E[e^{Yt}]-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E[S_{j}^{n}].

From the definition in (
1.5
), it is immediate to see that

(1.6)

S_{2}^{Y}(k,k)=E[Y]^{k}.

Assume from now on that

(1.7)

E[Y]\neq 0.

We introduce the notation:

(1.8)

e_{Y}(t)=E[e^{Yt}]-1.

Then we have

(1.9)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

If
f(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
is a delta series, namely
a_{0}=0
and
a_{1}\neq 0
, then the compositional inverse
\bar{f}(t)
of
f(t)
satisfying
f(\bar{f}(t))=\bar{f}(f(t))=t
exists. Note that, as
e_{Y}(t)=E[Y]t+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}E[Y^{m}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}
and
E[Y]\neq 0
(see (
1.7
), (
1.8
)),
e_{Y}(t)
is a delta series.

Now, we define the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
by: for
k\geq 0
,

(1.10)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
\bar{e}_{Y}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
e_{Y}(t)
.

In addition, as usual, we agree that

(1.11)

S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=0,\,\,\mathrm{if}\,\,k>n\,\,\mathrm{or}\,\,k<0.

Note that
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{2}(n,k),\,\,S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{1}(n,k)
, for
Y=1
.

Here
S_{2}(n,k)
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind defined by

(1.12)

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and
S_{1}(n,k)
are the Stirling numbers of the first kind defined as

(1.13)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
(x)_{n}
are the falling factorials given by

(1.14)

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1).

Using the definitions in (
1.9
) and (
1.10
), one shows that
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
satisfy the orthogonality relations in (a) of Proposition 1.1, from which the inverse relations in (b) and (c) follow.

Proposition 1.1
.

The following orthogonality and inverse relations are valid for
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)
.

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)S_{1}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\quad\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)S_{2}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle(b)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle(c)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{2}^{Y}(k,n)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{1}^{Y}(k,n)a_{k}.

Let
\lambda
be any nonzero real number. Then
e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)
are the degenerate exponentials defined by

(1.15)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15,21]),

where
(x)_{n,\lambda}
are the degenerate falling factorials given by

(1.16)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\ (n\geq 1).

Note here that
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}
.

The probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind associated
Y
,
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, are defined by

(1.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E[(S_{j})_{n,\lambda}].

To define the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
, we let

(1.18)

e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1.

Then we have

(1.19)

\frac{1}{k!}(e_{Y,\lambda}(t))^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Noting that
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)=E[Y]t+\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}E[(Y)_{m,\lambda}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}
is a delta series (see (
1.16
)), we define the probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
,
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
, by

(1.20)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
\bar{e}_{Y,\lambda}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
e_{Y,\lambda}(t)
.

Note that
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
, for
Y=1
.
Here
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind defined by

(1.21)

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
are the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind given by

(1.22)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
\log_{\lambda}(t)
are the degenerate logarithm defined by

(1.23)

\log_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(t^{\lambda}-1\big).

Note here that the degenerate exponential
e_{\lambda}(t)
in (
1.15
) and the degenerate logarithm
\log_{\lambda}(t)
in (
1.23
) are compositional inverses to each other so that

(1.24)

e_{\lambda}\big(\log_{\lambda}(t)\big)=\log_{\lambda}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)\big)=t.

From (
1.19
) and (
1.20
), one shows that
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
satisfy the orthogonality relations in (a) of Proposition 1.2, from which the inverse relations in (b) and (c) follow.

Proposition 1.2
.

The following orthogonality and inverse relations are valid for
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
and
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
.

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\quad\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle(b)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle(c)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,n)b_{k}\,\,\iff\,\,b_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(k,n)a_{k}.

Notice that the Stirling numbers of both kinds and the degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds satisfy orthogonality and inversion relations (see Propositions 1.1 and 1.2 with
Y=1
).

Throughout this paper, we assume that
u
is any complex number not equal to 1.

The probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
H_{n}^{Y}(x|u)
, are defined by

(1.25)

\frac{1-u}{E[e^{Yt}]-u}\big(E[e^{Yt}]\big)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}^{Y}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

More generally, for any nonnegative integer
r
, the probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
,
H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
, are given by

(1.26)

\bigg(\frac{1-u}{E[e^{Yt}]-u}\bigg)^{r}\big(E[e^{Yt}]\big)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

The probabilistic degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
, are defined by

(1.27)

\frac{1-u}{E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-u}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\big)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

More generally, for any nonnegative integer
r
, the probabilistic degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
,
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
, are given by

(1.28)

\bigg(\frac{1-u}{E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-u}\bigg)^{r}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\big)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

We note that
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)\rightarrow H_{n}^{Y}(x|u)
, and
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)\rightarrow H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
, as
\lambda
tends to
0
.
The Frobenius-Euler polynomials
H_{n}(x|u)
are defined by

(1.29)

\frac{1-u}{e^{t}-u}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
x=0
,
H_{n}(u)=H_{n}(0|u)
are called the Frobenius-Euler numbers.
The first few terms of
H_{n}(u)
are given by:

H_{0}(u)=1,\,H_{1}(u)=-\frac{1}{1-u},\,H_{2}(u)=\frac{1+u}{(1-u)^{2}},\,H_{3}(u)=-\frac{u^{2}+4u+1}{(1-u)^{3}},\dots.

More generally, for any nonnegative integer
r
, the Frobenius-Euler polynomials
H_{n}^{(r)}(x|u)
of order
r
are defined by

(1.30)

\bigg(\frac{1-u}{e^{t}-u}\bigg)^{r}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}^{(r)}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

The degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials
h_{n,\lambda}(x|u)
are given by (see [14])

(1.31)

\frac{1-u}{e_{\lambda}(t)-u}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n,\lambda}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

More generally, for any nonnegative integer
r
, the degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials
h_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x|u)
of order
r
are defined by (see [14])

(1.32)

\bigg(\frac{1-u}{e_{\lambda}(t)-u}\bigg)^{r}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

We remark that
h_{n,\lambda}(x|u)\rightarrow H_{n}(x|u)
, and
h_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x|u)\rightarrow H_{n}^{(r)}(x|u)
, as
\lambda
tends to
0
. When
Y=1
,
H_{n}^{Y}(x|u),\,H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u),\,h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
, and
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
become respectively
H_{n}(x|u),\,H_{n}^{(r)}(x|u),\,h_{n,\lambda}(x|u)
, and
h_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x|u)
.
We recall some notations and facts about forward differences. Let
\alpha
be any complex-valued function of the real variable
x
. Then, for any real number
a
, the forward difference
\Delta_{a}
is given by

(1.33)

\Delta_{a}\alpha(x)=\alpha(x+a)-\alpha(x).

If
a=1
, then we let

(1.34)

\Delta\alpha(x)=\Delta_{1}\alpha(x)=\alpha(x+1)-\alpha(x).

In general, the
n
th oder forward differences are given by

(1.35)

\Delta_{a}^{n}\alpha(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\alpha(x+ia).

For
a=1
, we have

(1.36)

\Delta^{n}\alpha(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}(-1)^{n-i}\alpha(x+i).

Finally, we recall that the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2}(n,k)
are given by

(1.37)

\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

2.
Review of umbral calculus

Here we will briefly go over very basic facts about umbral calculus. For more details on this, we recommend the reader to refer to
[
30
]
.
Let
\mathbb{C}
be the field of complex numbers. Then
\mathcal{F}
denotes the algebra of formal power series in
t
over
\mathbb{C}
, given by

\mathcal{F}=\bigg\{f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}~\bigg|~a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\bigg\},

and
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{C}[x]
indicates the algebra of polynomials in
x
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
.

The set of all linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
is a vector space as usual and denoted by
\mathbb{P}^{*}
. Let
\langle L|p(x)\rangle
denote the action of the linear functional
L
on the polynomial
p(x)
.

For
f(t)\in\mathcal{F}
with
\displaystyle f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle
, we define the linear functional on
\mathbb{P}
by

(2.1)

\langle f(t)|x^{k}\rangle=a_{k}.

From (
2.1
), we note that

\langle t^{k}|x^{n}\rangle=n!\delta_{n,k},\quad(n,k\geq 0),

where
\delta_{n,k}
is the Kronecker’s symbol.

Some remarkable linear functionals are as follows:

\displaystyle\langle e^{yt}|p(x)\rangle=p(y),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle e^{yt}-1|p(x)\rangle=p(y)-p(0),

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\frac{e^{yt}-1}{t}\bigg|p(x)\bigg\rangle=\int_{0}^{y}p(u)du.

Let

(2.3)

f_{L}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\langle L|x^{k}\rangle\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

Then, by (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), we get

\langle f_{L}(t)|x^{n}\rangle=\langle L|x^{n}\rangle.

That is,
f_{L}(t)=L
. Additionally, the map
L\longmapsto f_{L}(t)
is a vector space isomorphism from
\mathbb{P}^{*}
onto
\mathcal{F}
.

Transporting the multiplication in
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathbb{P}^{*}
via this isomorphism
gives an algebra structure on
\mathbb{P}^{*}
. This means that the product of
L,\,M\in\mathbb{P}^{*}
is given by

\langle LM|x^{n}\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\langle L|x^{k}\rangle\langle M|x^{n-k}\rangle,

and the map
L\longmapsto f_{L}(t)
is now an
\mathbb{C}
-algebra isomorphism.

\mathcal{F}
is called umbral algebra which is the algebra of
\mathbb{C}
-linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
. The umbral calculus is characterized as the study of the umbral algebra.

For each nonnegative integer
k
, the differential operator
t^{k}
on
\mathbb{P}
is defined by

(2.4)

t^{k}x^{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(n)_{k}x^{n-k},&\textrm{if $k\leq n$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $k>n$.}\end{array}\right.

Hence
t^{k}x^{n}=D^{k}x^{n}
, with
D=\frac{d}{dx}
.
Extending (
2.4
) linearly, any power series

f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}t^{k}\in\mathcal{F}

gives the differential operator on
\mathbb{P}
defined by

(2.5)

f(t)x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}x^{n-k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Hence
f(t)x^{n}=f(D)x^{n}
, with
D=\frac{d}{dx}
.
It should be observed that, for any formal power series
f(t)
and any polynomial
p(x)
, we have

(2.6)

\langle f(t)|p(x)\rangle=\langle 1|f(t)p(x)\rangle=f(t)p(x)|_{x=0}.

Here we note that an element
f(t)
of
\mathcal{F}
is a formal power series, a linear functional and a differential operator. Some notable differential operators are as follows:

\displaystyle e^{yt}p(x)=p(x+y),

(2.7)

\displaystyle(e^{yt}-1)p(x)=p(x+y)-p(x),

\displaystyle\frac{e^{yt}-1}{t}p(x)=\int_{x}^{x+y}p(u)du.

The order
o(f(t))
of the power series
f(t)(\neq 0)
is the smallest integer for which
a_{k}
does not vanish. If
o(f(t))=0
, then
f(t)
is called an invertible series. If
o(f(t))=1
, then
f(t)
is called a delta series.

For
f(t),g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
with
o(f(t))=1
and
o(g(t))=0
, there exists a unique sequence
s_{n}(x)
(deg
s_{n}(x)=n
) of polynomials such that

(2.8)

\big\langle g(t)f(t)^{k}|s_{n}(x)\big\rangle=n!\delta_{n,k},\quad(n,k\geq 0).

The sequence
s_{n}(x)
is said to be the Sheffer sequence for
(g(t),f(t))
, which is denoted by
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
. We observe from (
2.8
) that

(2.9)

s_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{g(t)}p_{n}(x),

where
p_{n}(x)=g(t)s_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t))
.

In particular, if
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),t)
, then
p_{n}(x)=x^{n}
, and hence

(2.10)

s_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{g(t)}x^{n}.

It is well known that
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
if and only if

(2.11)

\frac{1}{g\big(\overline{f}(t)\big)}e^{x\overline{f}(t)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{s_{k}(x)}{k!}t^{k},

for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
, where
\overline{f}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
f(t)
such that
\overline{f}(f(t))=f(\overline{f}(t))=t
.

The following equations (
2.12
), (
2.13
), and (
2.14
) are equivalent to the fact that
s_{n}\left(x\right)
is Sheffer for
\left(g\left(t\right),f\left(t\right)\right)
, for some invertible
g(t)
:

(2.12)

\displaystyle f\left(t\right)s_{n}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=ns_{n-1}\left(x\right),\quad\left(n\geq 0\right),

(2.13)

\displaystyle s_{n}\left(x+y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}s_{j}\left(x\right)p_{n-j}\left(y\right),

with
p_{n}\left(x\right)=g\left(t\right)s_{n}\left(x\right),

(2.14)

s_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\big\langle{g\left(\overline{f}\left(t\right)\right)^{-1}\overline{f}\left(t\right)^{j}}\big|{x^{n}\big\rangle}x^{j}.

3.
Representations by probabilistic Frobenius-Euler and degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials

Our goal here is to find formulas expressing arbitrary polynomials in terms of probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
H_{n}^{Y}(x|u)
, and probabilistic
degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
.

(a) Firstly, we treat the problem of representing arbitrary polynomials by the probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
.
From (
1.25
) and (
2.11
), we first observe that

(3.1)

\displaystyle H_{n}^{Y}(x|u)\sim\big(g(t)=\frac{e^{t}-u}{1-u},f(t)\big),

(3.2)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}\sim(1,e^{t}-1),

where the compositional inverse of
f(t)
is given by
\bar{f}(t)=\log E[e^{Yt}]
.
Here and in the sequel
we denote
\frac{e^{t}-u}{1-u}
simply by
g(t)
, with the understanding that
u
is a fixed complex number not equal to 1.

From (
2.12
), (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we note that

(3.3)

f(t)H_{n}^{Y}(x|u)=nH_{n-1}^{Y}(x|u),\quad(e^{t}-1)(x)_{n}=n(x)_{n-1},

and hence, by (
2.7
) and (
3.3
), we get

(3.4)

\Delta(x)_{n}=(e^{t}-1)(x)_{n}=n(x)_{n-1}.

Here we need to observe that
\bar{f}(t)
is a delta series. Indeed, one shows that

\bar{f}(t)=E[Y]t+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!S_{2}^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

We note from (
1.9
), (
1.13
) and (
1.25
) that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\big(H_{n}^{Y}(x+1|u)-uH_{n}^{Y}(x|u)\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=(1-u)e^{x\log E[e^{Yt}]}

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}\frac{1}{j!}\Big(\log\big(1+(E[e^{Yt}]-1)\big)\Big)^{j}

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}S_{1}(k,j)\frac{1}{k!}(E[e^{Yt}]-1)^{k}

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}S_{1}(k,j)\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}S_{1}(k,j)S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)x^{j}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence, from (
3.5
) and (
1.13
), we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle H_{n}^{Y}(x+1|u)-uH_{n}^{Y}(x|u)

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\sum_{j=0}^{k}S_{1}(k,j)x^{j}

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

As
S_{2}^{Y}(n,0)=\delta_{n,0}
(see (
1.9
)), from (
3.6
) we obtain

(3.7)

H_{n}^{Y}(1|u)-uH_{n}^{Y}(u)=(1-u)S_{2}^{Y}(n,0)=(1-u)\delta_{n,0}.

Here
\delta_{n,0}
is the Kronecker’s delta.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x]
be a polynomial of degree
n
, and let

(3.8)

p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}H_{k}^{Y}(x|u).

Now, for a fixed
u\neq 1
, we consider the following:

(3.9)

a(x)=p(x+1)-up(x).

Then, from (
3.6
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle a(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\big(H_{k}^{Y}(x+1|u)-uH_{k}^{Y}(x|u)\big)

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}S_{2}^{Y}(k,j)(x)_{j}.

For any integer
r
with
0\leq r\leq n
, from (
3.4
) and (
3.10
) we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Delta^{r}a(x)

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{k=r}^{n}a_{k}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{2}^{Y}(k,j)\Delta^{r}(x)_{j}

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{k=r}^{n}a_{k}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{2}^{Y}(k,j)(j)_{r}(x)_{j-r}.

Letting
x=0
in (
3.11
) gives

(3.12)

\frac{1}{(1-u)r!}\Delta^{r}a(x)\big|_{x=0}=\sum_{k=r}^{n}a_{k}S_{2}^{Y}(k,r),\,\,(0\leq r\leq n).

By invoking the inversion in Proposition 1.1 (c) to (
3.12
) and recalling (
3.9
), we finally have

(3.13)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\Delta^{j}a(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\Delta^{j}\big(p(x+1)-up(x)\big)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\big(\Delta^{j}p(1)-u\Delta^{j}p(0)\big).

An alternative expression of (
3.13
) is given by (see (
2.7
), (
1.37
))

(3.14)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\Delta^{j}a(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}(e^{t}-1)^{j}a(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\sum_{k=j}^{n}S_{2}(k,j)\frac{1}{k!}a^{(k)}(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\sum_{k=j}^{n}S_{2}(k,j)\frac{1}{k!}\big(p^{(k)}(1)-up^{(k)}(0)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\frac{1}{k!}\big(p^{(k)}(1)-up^{(k)}(0)\big),

where
p^{(k)}(x)=(\frac{d}{dx})^{k}p(x)
.
Using (
1.36
), we have another alternative expression of (
3.13
) which is given by

(3.15)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\Delta^{j}a(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{j}{i}(-1)^{j-i}a(i)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-i}\frac{1}{j!}\binom{j}{i}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\big(p(i+1)-up(i)\big).

Summarizing, from (
3.13
)-(
3.15
), we get the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x],with\,\,\mathrm{deg}\,p(x)=n
, and let
p(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}a_{r}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u)
. Then the coefficients
a_{r}
are given by

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\big(\Delta^{j}p(1)-u\Delta^{j}p(0)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\frac{1}{k!}\big(p^{(k)}(1)-up^{(k)}(0)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-i}\frac{1}{j!}\binom{j}{i}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\big(p(i+1)-up(i)\big).

Remark 3.2
.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x],with\,\,\mathrm{deg}\,p(x)=n
. Write
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}H_{k}(x|u)
.

As
\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{1}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)=\delta_{k,r}
, we recover from Theorem 3.1 the result in
[
12
,
14
]
. Namely, we have

a_{k}=\frac{1}{(1-u)k!}(p^{(k)}(1)-up^{(k)}(0)),\,\,\textnormal{for}\,\,k=0,1,\dots,n.

(b) Secondly, we treat the problem of representing arbitrary polynomials by the probabilistic degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
.

From (
1.27
) and (
2.11
), we first observe that

(3.16)

h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)\sim\big(g(t)=\frac{e^{t}-u}{1-u},f(t)\big),\\

where the compositional inverse of
f(t)
is given by
\bar{f}(t)=E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]
.

Here we need to show that
\bar{f}(t)=\log E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]
is a delta series. Indeed, one shows that

\bar{f}(t)=E[Y]t+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,j)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
2.12
) and (
3.16
), we note that

(3.17)

f(t)h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)=nh_{n-1,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u).\\

From (
1.27
) and proceeding just as in (
3.5
), we have

(3.18)

h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x+1|u)-uh_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)=(1-u)\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)(x)_{k}.

As
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,0)=\delta_{n,0}
(see (
1.17
)), from (
3.18
) we get

(3.19)

h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(1|u)-uh_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(u)=(1-u)\delta_{n,0},

where
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(u)=h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(0|u)
, and
\delta_{n,0}
is the Kronecker’s delta.

Now, we assume that
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x]
has degree
n
, and write
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}h_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
.
For a fixed
u\neq 1
, let
a(x)=p(x+1)-up(x)
. Then, from (
3.18
), we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle a(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\big(h_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x+1|u)-uh_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)\big)

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,j)(x)_{j}.

For any integer with
0\leq r\leq n
, from (
3.20
) and (
3.4
) we obtain

(3.21)

\displaystyle\Delta^{r}a(x)

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{k=r}^{n}a_{k}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,j)\Delta^{r}(x)_{j}

\displaystyle=(1-u)\sum_{k=r}^{n}a_{k}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,j)(j)_{r}(x)_{j-r}.

Letting
x=0
in (
3.21
) gives

(3.22)

\frac{1}{(1-u)r!}\Delta^{r}a(x)\big|_{x=0}=\sum_{k=r}^{n}a_{k}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(k,r),\,\,(0\leq r\leq n).

By invoking the inversion in Proposition 1.2 (c) to (
3.22
), we obtain

(3.23)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{(1-u)j!}\Delta^{j}a(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\big(\Delta^{j}p(1)-u\Delta^{j}p(0)\big).

Using (
1.36
) and proceeding just as in (
3.15
), we obtain an alternative expression of (
3.23
) which is given by

(3.24)

a_{r}=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-i}\frac{1}{j!}\binom{j}{i}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)\big(p(i+1)-up(i)\big).

From (
1.37
) and (
2.7
), we get yet another expression of (
3.23
) as follows:

(3.25)

a_{r}=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\frac{1}{k!}\big(p^{(k)}(1)-up^{(k)}(0)\big),

where
p^{(l)}(x)=(\frac{d}{dx})^{l}p(x)
.

Finally, from (
3.23
)-(
3.25
), we get the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x],with\,\,\mathrm{deg}\,p(x)=n
, and let
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{r}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
. Then the coefficients
a_{r}
are given by

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\big(\Delta^{j}p(1)-u\Delta^{j}p(0)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\frac{1}{k!}\big(p^{(k)}(1)-up^{(k)}(0)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-i}\frac{1}{j!}\binom{j}{i}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)\big(p(i+1)-up(i)\big).

Remark 3.4
.

As
(x)_{n,\lambda}\sim\big(1,\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1)\big)
(see (
1.15
)), we have

(3.26)

\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1)(x)_{n,\lambda}=n(x)_{n-1,\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Delta_{\lambda}(x)_{n,\lambda}.

When
Y=1
, from (
3.20
) we have

(3.27)

a(x)=(1-u)\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,j)(x)_{j}=(1-u)\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}(x)_{k,\lambda}.

So, when
Y=1
, we may apply
(\frac{1}{\lambda}\Delta_{\lambda})^{r}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{r}
to (
3.27
), and get

\frac{1}{\lambda^{r}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{r}a(x)=(1-u)\sum_{k=r}^{n}a_{k}(k)_{r}(x)_{k-r,\lambda}.

Then we can proceed just as before. For the details on these, one may refer to
[
19
]
.

4.
Representations by probabilisitc higher-order Frobenius-Euler and degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials

Our goal here is to deduce formulas expressing arbitrary polynomials in terms of probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
,
H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
, and probabilistic degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
,
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
.

(a) Firstly, we treat the problem of representing arbitrary polynomials by the probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
.
From (
1.26
) and (
2.11
), we note that

H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)\sim(g(t)^{r},f(t)),

where
g(t)=\frac{e^{t}-u}{1-u}
, and the compositional inverse of
f(t)
is given by
\bar{f}(t)=\log E[e^{Yt}]
.
From (
2.12
), we have

(4.1)

f(t)H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=nH_{n-1}^{Y,(r)}(x|u),

and from (
1.26
), it is immediate to see that

(4.2)

H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x+1|u)-uH_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=(1-u)H_{n}^{Y,(r-1)}(x|u).

We note that (
4.2
) is equivalent to saying that

(4.3)

g(t)H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=H_{n}^{Y,(r-1)}(x|u).

Applying (
4.3
)
r
-times, we get

(4.4)

g(t)^{r}H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=H_{n}^{Y,(0)}(x|u).

From (
4.4
) and using (
4.1
), for
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

(4.5)

f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=f(t)^{k}H_{n}^{Y,(0)}(x|u)=(n)_{k}H_{n-k}^{Y,(0)}(x|u).

Here, from (
1.5
) and (
1.26
), we observe that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{n}^{Y,(0)}(x|u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\big(1+E[e^{Yt}]-1\big)^{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k}\frac{1}{k!}\big(E[e^{Yt}]-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)(x)_{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, from (
4.6
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle H_{n}^{Y,(0)}(x|u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}^{Y}(n,k)(x)_{k},

\displaystyle H_{n}^{Y,(0)}(0|u)=S_{2}^{Y}(n,0)=\delta_{n,0}.

Now, we assume that
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x]
has degree
n
, and write
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}H_{k}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
.
Then we observe from (
4.5
) and (
4.7
) that

(4.8)

\displaystyle f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}p(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}\,f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}H_{l}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}(l)_{k}H_{l-k}^{Y,(0)}(x|u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}(l)_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{l-k}S_{2}^{Y}(l-k,j)(x)_{j}.

Evaluating (
4.8
) at
x=0
by using (
4.7
), we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}p(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}(l)_{k}S_{2}^{Y}(l-k,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}(l)_{k}\delta_{l,k}=k!a_{k}.

Thus, from (
4.9
), we obtain

(4.10)

a_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}p(x)\big|_{x=0}=\frac{1}{k!}\langle f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}|p(x)\rangle.

This also follows from the observation
\big\langle g(t)^{r}f(t)^{k}|H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)\big\rangle=n!\,\delta_{n,k}.

Since
g(t)=\frac{e^{t}-u}{1-u}
, from (
1.37
) we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle g(t)^{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-u)^{r}}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(-u)^{r-i}e^{it}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-u)^{r}}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(1-u)^{r-i}(e^{t}-1)^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-u)^{r}}\sum_{i=0}^{r}i!\binom{r}{i}(1-u)^{r-i}\sum_{m=i}^{\infty}S_{2}(m,i)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}.

By using (
2.7
) and (
1.37
), the equations (
4.11
) give three alternative expressions of (
4.10
) as in the following:

(4.12)

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-u)^{r}k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(-u)^{r-i}f(t)^{k}p(x+i)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(1-u)^{-i}\Delta^{i}f(t)^{k}p(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}i!\binom{r}{i}(1-u)^{-i}\sum_{m=i}^{n}S_{2}(m,i)\frac{1}{m!}f(t)^{k}p^{(m)}(x)\big|_{x=0}.

Summarizing the results so far, from (
4.10
) and (
4.12
) we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x],with\,\,\mathrm{deg}\,p(x)=n
. Let
g(t)=\frac{e^{t}-u}{1-u},\bar{f}(t)=\log E[e^{Yt}]
. Then we have

p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}H_{k}^{Y,(r)}(x|u),

where

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}g(t)^{r}f(t)^{k}p(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-u)^{r}k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(-u)^{r-i}f(t)^{k}p(x+i)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(1-u)^{-i}\Delta^{i}f(t)^{k}p(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\sum_{m=i}^{n}\frac{i!}{m!}\binom{r}{i}(1-u)^{-i}S_{2}(m,i)f(t)^{k}p^{(m)}(x)\big|_{x=0}.

(b) Secondy, we treat the problem of representing arbitrary polynomials by the probabilistic degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
.
From (
1.28
) and (
2.11
), we note that

h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)\sim(g(t)^{r},f(t)),

where
g(t)=\frac{e^{t}-u}{1-u}
, and the compositional inverse of
f(t)
is given by
\bar{f}(t)=\log E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]
.
From (
2.12
), we have

(4.13)

f(t)h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=nh_{n-1,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u),

and from (
1.28
), it is immediate to see that

(4.14)

g(t)h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r-1)}(x|u).

Applying (
4.14
)
r
-times, we get

(4.15)

g(t)^{r}h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(0)}(x|u).

From (
4.15
) and using (
4.13
), for
0\leq k\leq n
, we have

(4.16)

f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)=f(t)^{k}h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(0)}(x|u)=(n)_{k}h_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y,(0)}(x|u).

Here, from (
1.19
), (
1.28
) and noting that
S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,0)=\delta_{n,0}
(see (
1.19
)), we obtain

(4.17)

\displaystyle h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(0)}(x|u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)(x)_{k},

\displaystyle h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(0)}(0|u)=S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(n,0)=\delta_{n,0}.

Now, we assume that
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x]
has degree
n
, and write
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}h_{k,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
.
Then we observe from (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) that

(4.18)

\displaystyle f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}p(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}\,f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}h_{l,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}(l)_{k}h_{l-k,\lambda}^{Y,(0)}(x|u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}(l)_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{l-k}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(l-k,j)(x)_{j}.

Evaluating (
4.18
) at
x=0
by using (
4.17
), we have

(4.19)

\displaystyle f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}p(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}(l)_{k}S_{2,\lambda}^{Y}(l-k,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}a_{l}(l)_{k}\delta_{l,k}=k!a_{k}.

Thus, from (
4.19
), we obtain

(4.20)

a_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}p(x)\big|_{x=0}=\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle f(t)^{k}g(t)^{r}|p(x)\big\rangle.

This also follows from the observation
\big\langle g(t)^{r}f(t)^{k}|h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)\big\rangle=n!\,\delta_{n,k}.

Now, from (
4.11
), (
4.12
) and (
4.20
) we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x],with\,\,\mathrm{deg}\,p(x)=n
. Let
g(t)=\frac{e^{t}-u}{1-u},\bar{f}(t)=\log E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]
. Then we have

p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}h_{k,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u),

where

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}g(t)^{r}f(t)^{k}p(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-u)^{r}k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(-u)^{r-i}f(t)^{k}p(x+i)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{r}{i}(1-u)^{-i}\Delta^{i}f(t)^{k}p(x)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\sum_{m=i}^{n}\frac{i!}{m!}\binom{r}{i}(1-u)^{-i}S_{2}(m,i)f(t)^{k}p^{(m)}(x)\big|_{x=0}.

5.
Examples

Here we express
(x)_{n}
and
x^{n}
as linear combinations of probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
H_{k}^{Y}(x|u)
, and probabilistic degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
h_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
, for several discrete and continuous random variables
Y
. We use the first formulas in Theorems 3.1 and 3.3 for
(x)_{n}
, and the second ones in Theorems 3.1 and 3.3 for
x^{n}
. For those random variables
Y
, we need the explicit computations in
[
16
]
, for probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind,
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
, and probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind,
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
.

Firstly, we let
(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}a_{r}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u)
. Then, from Theorem 3.1, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\frac{1}{j!}\big(\Delta^{j}(x+1)_{n}-u\Delta^{j}(x)_{n}\big)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\binom{n}{j}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\big((x+1)_{n-j}-u(x)_{n-j}\big)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n}\binom{n}{j}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\big((1)_{n-j}-u(0)_{n-j}\big).

Before proceeding further, we note that

(5.2)

(1)_{k}-u(0)_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1-u,&\textrm{if $k=0$},\\
1,&\textrm{if $k=1$},\\
0,&\textrm{if $k\geq 2$}.\end{array}\right.

For
r
with
0\leq r\leq n-1
, from (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\binom{n}{n}S_{1}^{Y}(n,r)\big((1)_{0}-u(0)_{0}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\binom{n}{n-1}S_{1}^{Y}(n-1,r)\big((1)_{1}-u(0)_{1}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{j=r}^{n-2}\binom{n}{j}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)\big((1)_{n-j}-u(0)_{n-j}\big)

\displaystyle=S_{1}^{Y}(n,r)+\frac{n}{1-u}S_{1}^{Y}(n-1,r).

As we see
a_{n}=S_{1}^{Y}(n,n)=\frac{1}{E[Y]^{n}}
from (
5.1
), we get

(5.4)

a_{r}=S_{1}^{Y}(n,r)+\frac{n}{1-u}S_{1}^{Y}(n-1,r),\quad(0\leq r\leq n),\quad(\mathrm{see}\,\,\eqref{9a}).

Thus we have shown that

(5.5)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{S_{1}^{Y}(n,r)+\frac{n}{1-u}S_{1}^{Y}(n-1,r)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u).

Secondly, we let
x^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}a_{r}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u)
. Then, from Theorem 3.1, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle a_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n}\sum_{j=r}^{k}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\Big(\frac{d}{dx}\Big)^{k}(x+1)^{n}-u\Big(\frac{d}{dx}\Big)^{k}x^{n}\Big)\Big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\big((x+1)^{n-k}-ux^{n-k}\big)\big|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)(1-u\delta_{n,k}).

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(n,j)+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j),\quad(0\leq r\leq n),

where we understand that the second sum is 0 when
r=n
.

Thus we have found that

(5.7)

\displaystyle x^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(n,j)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u).

Thirdly, we let
(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}a_{r}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
. Then, from Theorem 3.3 and proceeding just as in (
5.1
) and (
5.3
), we get

(5.8)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,r)+\frac{n}{1-u}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n-1,r)\Big\}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u).

Fourthly, we let
x^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}a_{r}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
. Then, from Theorem 3.3 and proceeding just as in (
5.6
), we obtain

(5.9)

\displaystyle x^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(n,j)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\Big\}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u),

where we understand that the second sum is 0 when
r=n
.

Summarizing our results, from (
5.5
), (
5.1
), (
5.8
) and (
5.9
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

We have the following expressions for
(x)_{n}
and
x^{n}
as linear combinations of
H_{k}^{Y}(x|u)
and
h_{k,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
.

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{S_{1}^{Y}(n,r)+\frac{n}{1-u}S_{1}^{Y}(n-1,r)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(n,j)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}S_{1}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,r)+\frac{n}{1-u}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n-1,r)\Big\}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{j=r}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(n,j)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\Big\}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u).

(a) Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable. Then the probability mass function of
Y
is given by (see
[
31
]
)

p(0)=1-p,\quad p(1)=p,\quad(0<p\leq 1).

Then, from
[
16
]
, we have

(5.10)

S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}S_{1}(n,k),\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{p^{n}}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k).

Now, from Theorem 5.1 and (
5.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{S_{1}(n,r)+\frac{np}{1-u}S_{1}(n-1,r)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{j=r}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{j}}S_{1}(j,r)S_{2}(n,j)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{p^{j}}S_{1}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\frac{1}{p^{n}}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{S_{1,\lambda}(n,r)+\frac{np}{1-u}S_{1,\lambda}(n-1,r)\Big\}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{j=r}^{n}\frac{1}{p^{j}}S_{1,\lambda}(j,r)S_{2}(n,j)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{p^{j}}S_{1,\lambda}(j,r)S_{2}(k,j)\Big\}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u).

(b) Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then the probability mass function of
Y
is given by (see
[

]
)

p(i)=e^{-\alpha}\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!},\quad i=0,1,2,\dots.

Then, from
[

]

(5.11)

\displaystyle S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1}(l,k)S_{1}(n,l),\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)S_{1}(n,l).

Now, from Theorem 5.1 and (
5.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{l=r}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1}(l,r)S_{1}(n,l)+\frac{n}{1-u}\sum_{l=r}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1}(l,r)S_{1}(n-1,l)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{l=r}^{j}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1}(l,r)S_{1}(j,l)S_{2}(n,j)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\sum_{l=r}^{j}\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1}(l,r)S_{1}(j,l)S_{2}(k,j)\Big\}H_{r}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{l=r}^{n}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1,\lambda}(l,r)S_{1}(n,l)+\frac{n}{1-u}\sum_{l=r}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1,\lambda}(l,r)S_{1}(n-1,l)\Big\}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u),

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{j=r}^{n}\sum_{l=r}^{j}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1,\lambda}(l,r)S_{1}(j,l)S_{2}(n,j)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{1-u}\sum_{k=r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=r}^{k}\sum_{l=r}^{j}\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}}S_{1,\lambda}(l,r)S_{1}(j,l)S_{2}(k,j)\Big\}h_{r,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u).

Y
be the geometric random variable with parameter

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\Big\{\sum_{l=r}^{n}\binom{n}{l}(n-1)_{n-l}p^{l}(p-1)^{n-l}S_{1}(l,r)

6.
Conclusion

This paper explored representations of arbitrary polynomials as linear combinations of probabilistic Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
H_{n}^{Y}(x|u)
, and probabilistic degenerate Frobenius-Euler polynomials associated with
Y
,
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x|u)
, and as linear combinations of their higher-order counterparts,
H_{n}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
and
h_{n,\lambda}^{Y,(r)}(x|u)
. We derived explicit coefficients for these linear combinations, expressed in terms of probabilistic Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
,
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k)
, and probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
Y
,
S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
. To demonstrate our findings, we provided concrete examples by expressing
(x)_{n}
and
x^{n}
as linear combinations of these polynomials for some discrete and continuous random variables
Y
, utilizing established results for
S_{1}^{Y}(n,k),\,\,and\,\,S_{1,\lambda}^{Y}(n,k)
.

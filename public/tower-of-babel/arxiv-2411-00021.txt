Ram Manohar, S. M. Mallikarjunaiah

Abstract




Abstract.
We propose an
hp
-adaptive discontinuous Galerkin finite element method (DGFEM) to approximate the solution of a static crack boundary value problem. The mathematical model describes the behavior of a geometrically linear strain-limiting elastic body. The compatibility condition for the physical variables, along with a specific algebraically nonlinear constitutive relationship, leads to a second-order quasi-linear elliptic boundary value problem. We demonstrate the existence of a unique discrete solution using Ritz representation theory across the entire range of modeling parameters. Additionally, we derive a priori error estimates for the DGFEM, which are computable and, importantly, expressed in terms of natural energy and
L^{2}
-norms. Numerical examples showcase the performance of the proposed method in the context of a manufactured solution and a non-convex domain containing an edge crack.

Key words.

hp
-discontinuous Galerkin finite element methods; existence of unique discrete solution; apriori error estimate; Strain limiting nonlinear model; quasilinear PDEs.

(1.2)

The system of equations governing the material behavior is:

(1.5)

\dfrac{\partial\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{13}}{\partial y}-\dfrac{\partial\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{23}}{\partial x}=0.

One of the FEM techniques, the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method, is particularly effective in solving partial differential equations with discontinuous coefficients, as it approximates and discretizes boundary value problems while controlling the effects of discontinuities through penalty parameters.

Arnold, Douglas, and Wheeler introduced the symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (SIPG) method for elliptic and parabolic problems, based on Nitsche’s symmetric formulation

[

Numerous DG formulations for elliptic problems exist

The problem considered in this work frequently arises in material science, engineering, and geophysics to forecast and examine fracture behavior under out-of-plane shear forces. The static anti-plane shear crack model is the primary approach for studying shear fracture in materials. 1.3
Our Contribution.

Using a DG finite element approach, our study investigates the static anti-plane shear crack model (
1.7a
)-(
1.7b
). We discretize the model problem using the DG method, allowing for hanging nodes in the domain discretization. Invoking Ritz representation theory, we establish the existence of a unique discrete solution and derive an optimal a priori error estimate in the broken Sobolev space. The linearized form of the model is handled using the Abuin-Nitsche procedure to yield the
L^{2}
-norm error estimate, which shows the optimal order convergence. Consequently, the optimal order a priori error estimate for the energy norm is also established. The numerical results confirm the theoretical predictions and demonstrate the singular behavior of the stresses at the crack. However, crack-tip strains’ growth is slower than the linearized model.

The entire paper is arranged as follows: Section 2 introduces the preliminary function spaces, Section 3 presents the DG finite element formulation, and Section 4 contains the error analysis. Section 5 concludes with numerical experiments verifying our analysis and a summary of the findings.

\displaystyle~~~-\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\frac{2C_{tr,1}^{2}C_{M_{3}}^{2}\,(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)}{\sigma_{i}\,|e_{i}|^{1-\gamma}}\,\|\nabla(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tau_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\sigma_{i}\,\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}\,\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\sigma_{i}\,\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}\,\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{i})}.

An application of the
\epsilon-
form of Young’s inequality to the second term on the right-hand side, followed by the arrangement of the terms, one may obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(\phi_{1};\phi_{1},\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})-\mathcal{A}_{h}(\phi_{2};\phi_{2},\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})\geq\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}C_{M_{4}}\,\|\nabla(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tau_{i})}

\displaystyle~~~-\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\frac{4\,C_{tr,1}^{2}C_{M_{3}}^{2}\,(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)}{\epsilon\,\sigma_{i}\,|e_{i}|^{1-\gamma}}\,\|\nabla(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tau_{i})}

\displaystyle~~~+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\sigma_{i}\,\frac{(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\,n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}\,\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}]\hskip-1.5pt]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{i})}.

Selecting
\epsilon=\frac{8\,C_{tr,1}^{2}C_{M_{3}}^{2}(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)}{C_{M_{4}}\,\sigma_{i}\,|e_{i}|^{1-\gamma}}
and
C_{32}=\min\big\{C_{M_{4}}-\frac{4\,C_{tr,1}^{2}C_{M_{3}}^{2}\,(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)}{\epsilon\,\sigma_{i}\,|e_{i}|^{1-\gamma}},\quad 1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\big\}
, this implies the desired inequality (
3.15
).
∎

As a consequence, the finite dimensional space
\mathcal{S}^{\mathtt{n}}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is a Hilbert space equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
induced by the inner product space
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{E}}
, which is easily demonstrable. Furthermore, all norms are comparable in the finite-dimensional space.

Let us define the norm
|\|\cdot|\|
on
\mathcal{S}^{\mathtt{n}}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
by

|\|\psi|\|:=\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}(|\nabla\psi|^{2}+|\psi|^{2})\,dx+\sum_{{e_{i}}\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}}\int_{e_{i}}\sigma_{i}\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}|[\hskip-1.5pt[\psi]\hskip-1.5pt]|^{2}\,ds+\sum_{e\in\mathscr{E}_{bd,h}}\int_{e}|\psi|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which is equivalent to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
. Hence,
\exists
a constant
C_{33}>0
such that

(3.17)

\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq|\|\psi|\|\leq C_{33}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{E}}\quad\text{for all}\quad\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathtt{n}}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

The operator
\mathcal{L}_{h}(\cdot)
is linear. By recalling the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we find the bound of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{h}(\cdot)
for any
\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
to have

(3.18)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{h}(\phi_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}|f\phi_{h}|\,dx+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{bd,h}}\int_{e_{i}}\sigma_{i}\,\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}|g\;\phi_{h}|\,ds,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}|f|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{bd,h}}\int_{e_{i}}C_{tr,1}^{2}\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|}\,|g|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}|\phi|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{bd,h}}\int_{e_{i}}\sigma_{i}\,\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}\,|\phi|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}|f|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{bd,h}}\int_{e_{i}}C_{tr,1}^{2}\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|}\,|g|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times|\|\phi|\|.

By using the relation (
3.17
), we arrive at

(3.19)

|\mathcal{L}_{h}(\phi_{h})|~\leq~C_{34}\|\phi_{h}\|_{\mathcal{E}},

where

C_{34}=C_{33}\,\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}|f|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{bd,h}}\int_{e_{i}}C_{tr,1}^{2}\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|}\,|g|^{2}\,ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This shows that the operator
\mathcal{L}_{h}(\cdot)
is bounded for any
\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}_{h}(\cdot)
is a bounded linear operator. Hence, by utilizing the Ritz representation theory, there exists unique
\mathrm{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
with
\mathrm{f}=\mathrm{f}(f,g)
such that

\mathcal{L}_{h}(\phi_{h})=(\mathrm{f},\phi_{h}),\quad\forall\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

Define the linear functional
\mathscr{F}_{u_{h}}:\,\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\longmapsto\mathbb{R}
such that, for any
u_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
,

\mathscr{F}_{u_{h}}(\phi_{h})=\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h};u_{h},\phi_{h}),\quad\forall\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

From the virtue of Lemma
3.5
with
u_{1}=u_{h},\,u_{2}=0
and
\phi=\phi_{h}
, one can easily get the estimate

|\mathscr{F}_{u_{h}}(\phi_{h})|\leq C_{31}\|u_{h}\|_{\mathcal{E}}\|\phi_{h}\|_{\mathcal{E}},\quad\forall\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

This implies that the
\mathscr{F}_{u_{h}}
is a bounded linear functional. Hence, from the Ritz representation theorem, we can extract an element
\mathcal{H}(u_{h})
from
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
such that

\mathscr{F}_{u_{h}}(\phi_{h})=(\mathcal{H}(u_{h}),\phi_{h})_{\mathcal{E}},\quad\forall\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

We now show that the operator
\mathcal{H}
is a bijection from
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
to itself.

Note that if the solution exists in the finite-dimensional space
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, then it is unique. From the operator theory, it is sufficient to show that the operator
\mathcal{H}
is Lipschitz continuous and the strong monotonic, then it is bijective. Therefore,
\mathcal{H}
has an inverse that is Lipschitz continuous. Let us examine each of these properties individually.
We know that

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(u_{h})-\mathcal{H}(\tilde{u}_{h})\|_{\mathcal{E}}=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\frac{|(\mathcal{H}(u_{h})-\mathcal{H}(\tilde{u}_{h}),\phi_{h})_{\mathcal{E}}|}{\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{E}}},\quad\forall u_{h},\,\tilde{u}_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

With the help of Lemma
3.5
, one may easily compute, for
u_{h},\,\tilde{u}_{h},\,\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
,

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{H}(u_{h})-\mathcal{H}(\tilde{u}_{h}),\phi_{h})_{\mathcal{E}}|~=~|\mathscr{F}_{u_{h}}(\phi_{h})-\mathscr{F}_{\tilde{u}_{h}}(\phi_{h})|

(3.21)

\displaystyle~~~~=|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h};u_{h},\phi_{h})-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\tilde{u}_{h};\tilde{u}_{h},\phi_{h})|~\leq~\tilde{C}_{31}\,\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{E}}\|\phi_{h}\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

Inequalities (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) yield the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{H}
.

Next, we show the strong monotonicity property for the non-linear operator
\mathcal{H}
by recalling the Lemma (
3.6
). Thus, for
u_{h},\,\tilde{u}_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
\sigma_{i}>4\,|e_{i}|^{\gamma-1}C_{tr,1}^{2}C_{M_{3}}^{2}C_{M_{4}}^{-1}(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)
with
\varrho,\,\delta,\,\mu>0
and
\gamma\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{H}(u_{h})-\mathcal{H}(\tilde{u}_{h}),u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h})_{\mathcal{E}}|~=~|\mathscr{F}_{u_{h}}(u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h})-\mathscr{F}_{\tilde{u}_{h}}(u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h})|

\displaystyle~~~=~|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h};u_{h},u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h})-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(\tilde{u}_{h};\tilde{u}_{h},u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h})|~\geq~\tilde{C}_{32}\,\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}.

This yields the strong monotonicity of
\mathcal{H}
from
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
to itself. Since the operator
\mathcal{H}
is a bijective, the inverse of
\mathcal{H}
exists, and Lipschitz is continuous. Then, there exists
u_{h}^{dg}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
such that

\mathcal{H}(u_{h}^{dg})=\mathrm{f},\;\text{for any}\;\mathrm{f}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

This implies that the DG problem (
3.1
) admits a unique solution in
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

Equivalently, we can argue that the discrete problem (
3.1
) admits the unique discrete solution
u_{h}^{dg}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. We assert the following theorem for the existence and unique solution for the discrete model (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.7
.

Assume that the Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
are valid. Further, we assume that the condition
\sigma_{i}>4\,|e_{i}|^{\gamma-1}C_{tr,1}^{2}C_{M_{3}}^{2}C_{M_{4}}^{-1}(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)
with
\varrho,\,\delta,\,\mu>0
and
\gamma\geq 1
, is satisfied, then the discrete problem (
3.1
) has a unique solution in
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

The following section will concentrate on the convergence error analysis for the problem (
1.7a
)-(
1.7b
).

4
Error Analysis

This section addresses the
L^{2}
-norm and the energy-norm error estimates for the discrete model problem (
3.1
). First, we determine the energy-norm apriori type error estimate. Then, using the energy error estimate, we will establish the
L^{2}-
error estimates. We first deteriorate the error into two components,
\eta
and
\xi
, by incorporating the auxiliary variable
\mathcal{I}_{h}u\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
to get the energy error estimates. Consequently, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle u-u^{dg}_{h}=(u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u)+(\mathcal{I}_{h}u-u^{dg}_{h})=:\eta+\xi,

where
\eta:=u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u
and
\xi:=\mathcal{I}_{h}u-u^{dg}_{h}
. To obtain the main result of the error in the
\mathcal{E}
-energy norm, we employ the auxiliary error bounds of
\eta
and
\xi
.

The intermediate error bound for
\eta
in the
\mathcal{E}
-energy norm is provided in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1

(Error bound for
\eta
)
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}
be the solution of the continuous problem (
1.7a
)–(
1.7b
). Furthermore, if all the requirements of the Lemmas
3.5
–
3.6
are fulfilled. Then there exists a positive constant
C_{41}
such that the following inequality holds, for
\mathcal{I}_{h}u\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
,

(4.2)

\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{E}}~\leq~C_{41}\,\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-2}}{n_{i}^{2m_{i}-2}}\,\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{41}=C_{ap,1}\,\max\big\{C_{tr,2}\sqrt{\big(3}+C(\delta,\varrho)\big),\;1\big\}
and
1\leq q_{i}\leq\min\{m_{i},n_{i}+1\}
with
n_{i}\geq 1
,
m_{i}\geq 2
, for all
i\in[1:N_{h}]
.

Proof.

Using the notion of the energy norm and invoking the approximation property (
3.4
) in the first term, we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\eta\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}|\nabla(u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u)|^{2}\,dx+\sum_{{e_{i}}\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e_{i}}\sigma_{i}\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}|[\hskip-1.5pt[u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt]|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2}_{ap,1}\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-2}}{n_{i}^{{2m_{i}}-2}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})}+\sum_{{e_{i}}\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e_{i}}\sigma_{i}\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}|[\hskip-1.5pt[u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt]|^{2}\,ds.

Now, the second term is evaluated on inner edges of
e_{ij}(=e_{i})\subset\partial\tau_{i}\cap\partial\tau_{j},\,\forall i,j
. Considering the bounded local variation properties
A_{1}-A_{2}
(refer to Sec.
3
), the approximate property (
3.5
), and the trace Lemma
3.3
, one may deduce that

\displaystyle\|[\hskip-1.5pt[u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{L^{2}(e_{ij})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u|_{\tau^{e}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}(e_{i})}^{2}+\|u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u|_{\tau^{e}_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(e_{j})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C^{2}_{tr,2}C^{2}_{ap,1}\big(1+C(\delta,\varrho)\big)\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-1}}{n_{i}^{2m_{i}-1}}\,\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})}

with considering the degree of the polynomial
n^{2m_{i}}_{i}\leq n_{i}^{2m_{i}-1}
, and the same holds true for
m_{j}
. By making the same arguments, one may bound the boundary term as

\displaystyle\|[\hskip-1.5pt[u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt]\|_{L^{2}(e_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C^{2}_{tr,2}C^{2}_{ap,1}\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-1}}{n_{i}^{2m_{i}-1}}\,\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})},

and consequently,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{{e_{i}}\in\mathscr{E}_{h}}\int_{e_{i}}\sigma_{i}\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}|[\hskip-1.5pt[u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt]|^{2}\,ds~\leq~2C^{2}_{tr,2}C^{2}_{ap,1}\big(3+C(\delta,\varrho)\big)\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-1}}{n_{i}^{2m_{i}-1}}\,\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})}.

The required result is obtained by combining Eqs (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), and choosing the constant
C_{41}^{2}=C^{2}_{ap,1}\max\big\{C^{2}_{tr,2}\big(3+C(\delta,\varrho)\big),\;1\big\}
.
∎

We acquire the intermediate error bound for
\xi
in the subsequent lemma.

Lemma 4.2

(Error bound for
\xi
)
.

Assume that
u^{dg}_{h}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
be the solution of the discrete problem (
3.1
). Furthermore, if all the requirements of the Lemmas
3.5
–
3.6
are fulfilled, then there is a positive
C_{42}
such that, for
\mathcal{I}_{h}u\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, we have

(4.5)

\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq C_{42}\,\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\big\{\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-2}}{n_{i}^{2m_{i}-2}}+\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}+\gamma-3}}{n_{i}^{{2m_{i}}-2}}+\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-2}}{n_{i}^{{2m_{i}}-3}}\big\}\,\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C_{42}=C_{43}/C_{32}
with
C_{43}=\max\{\sqrt{\big(}2C_{M_{3}}^{2}\,C^{2}_{ap,1}+C_{tr,1}^{2}\,C_{M_{3}}^{2}\,\sigma_{i}^{-1}(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)+C_{44}^{2}\big)\}
,
C_{44}=C_{ap,2}\,C_{tr,1}\sqrt{(}1+C(\delta,\varrho))\,\sqrt{(}1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta),
and
1\leq q_{i}\leq\min\{m_{i},n_{i}+1\}
with
n_{i}\geq 1
,
i\in[1:N_{h}]
.

Proof.

An application of monotonicity inequality (
3.15
) of Lemma
3.6
with
\phi_{1}=I_{h}u
,
\phi_{2}=u_{h}^{dg}
, and then use of (
3.3
) leads to

(4.6)

\displaystyle C_{32}\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(\mathcal{I}_{h}u;\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\xi)-\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h}^{dg};u_{h}^{dg},\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(\mathcal{I}_{h}u;\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\xi)-\mathcal{A}(u;u,\xi)+\big(\mathcal{L}_{h}(\xi)-\mathcal{A}_{h}(u_{h}^{dg};u_{h}^{dg},\xi)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}(\mathcal{I}_{h}u;\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\xi)-\mathcal{A}(u;u,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}\big(\mathscr{G}(|\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u|)\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u-\mathscr{G}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\big)\cdot\nabla\xi\,dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}}\int_{e_{i}}\big(\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\mathscr{G}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}-\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\mathscr{G}(|\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u|)\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}\big)[\hskip-1.5pt[\xi]\hskip-1.5pt]\,ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}}\int_{e_{i}}\big|\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\mathscr{G}(|\nabla u|)\nabla\xi\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}[\hskip-1.5pt[u]\hskip-1.5pt]-\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\mathscr{G}(|\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u|)\nabla\xi\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt]\big|\,ds

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle II_{1}+II_{2}+II_{3}.\quad(Say)

The integral terms
II_{k}
, where
k=1,\,2,\,3,\,4
, should be prominently bounded. Employing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, inequality (
2.7
), and then applying Lemma
3.1
, we get to

(4.7)

\displaystyle II_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\tau_{i}}\big|\mathscr{G}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u-\mathscr{G}(|\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u|)\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u\big|\big|\nabla\xi\big|\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{M_{3}}\,\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tau_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\tau_{i})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{M_{3}}\,C_{ap,1}\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}2\,\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-2}}{n_{i}^{{2m_{i}}-2}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\tau_{i})}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Implementing Lemma
3.1
and making an argument akin to that of Lemma
3.5
, one can derive

\displaystyle II_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}}\int_{e_{i}}\big(\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\mathscr{G}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}-\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\mathscr{G}(|\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u|)\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}\big)[\hskip-1.5pt[\xi]\hskip-1.5pt]\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathscr{T}_{h}}\frac{C_{tr,1}^{2}C_{M_{3}}^{2}(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)}{\sigma_{i}}\,\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-3+\gamma}}{n_{i}^{{2m_{i}}-2}}\;\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times\Big(\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}}\sigma_{i}\,\frac{n_{i}^{2}}{2|e_{i}|^{\gamma}}\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\xi]\hskip-1.5pt]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The last term,
II_{3}
, can be bounded by arguing along the same lines as the bound of
I_{3}
to have

(4.8)

\displaystyle II_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}}\int_{e_{i}}\big|\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\mathscr{G}(|\nabla u|)\nabla\xi\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}[\hskip-1.5pt[u]\hskip-1.5pt]-\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\mathscr{G}(|\nabla\mathcal{I}_{h}u|)\nabla\xi\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt]\big|\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}}\int_{e_{i}}\big|\{\hskip-3.0pt\{\nabla\xi\cdot\bar{\bf n}\}\hskip-3.0pt\}\big|\,\big|\mathscr{G}(h^{-1}[\hskip-1.5pt[u]\hskip-1.5pt])[\hskip-1.5pt[u]\hskip-1.5pt]-\mathscr{G}(h^{-1}[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt])[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\hskip-1.5pt]\big|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{C_{M_{3}}^{2}C_{tr,1}^{2}n_{i}^{2}(1+\varrho^{2}\,\delta)}{2h_{i}}\|\nabla\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\tau_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times\Big(\sum_{e_{i}\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}}\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\eta]\hskip-1.5pt]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{i})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the approximation inequality (
3.5
) and the assumptions
A_{1}-A_{2}
(cf., Sec
3
), we next estimate the expression
[\hskip-1.5pt[\eta]\hskip-1.5pt]
on the internal edges
e_{ij}(=e_{i})\in\mathscr{E}_{int,h}
, as

\displaystyle\|[\hskip-1.5pt[\eta]\hskip-1.5pt]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{ij})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\eta|_{\tau_{i}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{ij})}+\|\eta|_{\tau_{j}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{ij})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2}_{ap,2}\,(1+C(\delta,\varrho))\,\frac{h_{i}^{{2q_{i}}-1}}{n_{i}^{{2m_{i}}-1}}\,\|u\|^{2}_{H^{m_{i}}(\tau_{i})}.

Hence, the estimation of
[\hskip-1.5pt[\eta]\hskip-1.5pt]
on the edges

6
Conclusion

In this study, we have addressed the issue of devising an
hp
-local discontinuous Galerkin finite element to approximate the solution to a quasi-linear elliptic boundary value problem. Such a problem arises in modeling the response of a geometrically linear elastic body using an algebraically nonlinear constitutive relationship. The proposed response function is monotone and Lipschitz continuous. Our continuous DG formulation is well-posed, and further, for the discrete problem, we utilize the Ritz representation theory to show the existence of a unique solution. Then, the local polynomial degree
n_{i}
for each triangle
\tau_{i}
,
\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, is enforced to approximate the solution variable for the discrete problem.
We also derive the apriori error estimates in the energy and
L^{2}
norms. For the sufficient small
h
,
\mathtt{m}\geq 2
and
\gamma=d-1
with
d\geq 2
, the derived error estimates are optimal in
h
and suboptimal in
\mathtt{n}
(total degree of polynomial). The numerical test presented for a case of manufactured solution delineates the performance of the proposed locally adaptive DG scheme. The method presented in this paper can be easily used to characterize the crack-tip stress and strain in the material body under anti-plane shear containing a single crack. We have found that the stress is maximum along a line leading to the crack tip, but the strain growth is insignificant. Therefore, one can utilize the crack-tip stresses to define evolution criteria to study quasi-static and dynamic propagation problems in this class of new material models. Such an investigation is an automatic extension of the current research work.

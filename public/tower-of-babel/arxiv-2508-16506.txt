Separating Maximality Principles
Takehiko Gappo, Andreas Lietz

Abstract
. We investigate fragments of generic absoluteness principles known as Maximality Principles. We determine the consistency strength of Σ n − 𝖬𝖯 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R}) and Π n − 𝖬𝖯 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R}) , the boldface Maximality Principle restricted respectively to Σ n \Sigma_{n} - and Π n \Pi_{n} -formulas. Further, we show that no implication between Σ n − 𝖬𝖯 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R}) and Π n − 𝖬𝖯 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R}) is provable in ZFC \mathrm{ZFC} . We also establish the consistency, relative to a Woodin cardinal, of the Maximality Principle for ω 1 \omega_{1} -preserving posets with countable ordinal parameters and prove its consistency strength is bounded below by a Ramsey cardinal. Finally, we resolve questions of Ikegami–Trang and Goodman by separating the Maximality Principle for stationary set preserving posets restricted to Σ 2 \Sigma_{2} -formulas from 𝖬𝖬 + ⁣ + \mathsf{MM}^{++} in the presence of large cardinals.

1.
Introduction

Since Paul Cohen’s resolution of Hilbert’s first problem, the Continuum Hypothesis, it has been understood that Gödel’s independence phenomenon affects not only specific arithmetic sentences but also natural mathematical questions. Set theorists therefore search for natural extensions of the standard base theory
\mathsf{ZFC}
which decide as many such questions as possible. One promising direction comes from the very method typically used to establish independence results: forcing. Formalizing the idea that “a maximal amount of certain forcing has been done” leads to forcing axioms such as Martin’s Axiom, the Proper Forcing Axiom and Martin’s Maximum, each of which have found far-reaching applications within set theory and beyond.

A different way to maximizing truth relative to forcing is provided by
Maximality Principles
(
\mathsf{MP}
). These assert that if a statement can be made true by forcing but not subsequently destroyed, then it should hold already. Maximality Principles and forcing axioms are orthogonal in the sense that there is typically no implication between related instances of each of them, but nonetheless they work in tandem. In fact, the Maximality Principle for a given class of forcings usually hold in the standard model of a related forcing axiom, provided the large cardinal used in the construction exhibits some additional correctness properties. For example, in Baumgartner’s standard model of the Proper Forcing Axiom, the Maximality Principle for proper forcing holds true, provided that the supercompact cardinal
\kappa
in the ground model
W
satisfies
V_{\kappa}^{W}\prec W
.

Weaker fragments of forcing axioms such as the bounded forcing axioms
\mathsf{BPFA}
and
\mathsf{BMM}
are by now well understood and have an extensive literature. In this paper, we investigate natural fragments of Maximality Principles obtained by restricting the class of allowed formulas. Below are the main highlights:

(1)

We calibrate the exact consistency strength of
\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}
and
\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}
for all posets with real parameters, showing that they differ. As a consequence, we stablish the consistency of
\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}\,+\,\neg\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}
for all posets with real parameters, relative to some large cardinals.

(2)

We also show that
\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}\,+\,\neg\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}
for all posets with real parameters is consistent relative to some large cardinals.

(3)

We show that
\mathsf{MP}
for
\omega_{1}
-preserving posets with countable ordinal parameters is consistent relative to large cardinals. We show that the upper bound of the consistency strength of this statement is a Woodin cardinal and a measurable cardinal above it, while the lower bound is a Ramsey cardinal.

(4)

Large cardinal axioms does not imply
\Sigma_{2}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}
for all posets. Also,
\mathsf{MM}^{++}
and the existence of a proper class of Woodin cardinals do not imply
\Sigma_{2}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}
for stationary set preserving posets. This answers questions raised by Ikegami–Trang
[
IT18
]
and Goodman
[
Goo24
]
.

Definition 2.7

\mathbb{P}_{k}*\cdots*\mathbb{P}_{n}*\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{P}^{V[G_{0}*\cdots*G_{k-1}]}
.
This poset witnesses that

is
\mathcal{P}
-forceably
\mathcal{P}
-persistent in

.
By our choice of

,

forces “

-persistent” over

, so
\phi_{k}
holds in

∎

In particular,

are consistent relative to

, even though these classes of forcing posets are not transfinitely iterable.

3.3.
Maximality Principles for
\omega_{1}
-preserving posets

In stark contrast to the standard forcing axioms, the Maximality Principle is consistent for some highly non-iterable classes of forcings, even with parameters. Recall that the standard forcing axiom
\mathsf{FA}_{\omega_{1}}(\{\mathbb{P}\})
can hold for a forcing
\mathbb{P}
only if
\mathbb{P}
preserves stationary subsets of
\omega_{1}
(cf.
[
FMS88
]
), so the forcing axiom for all
\omega_{1}
-preserving forcings is inconsistent. On the other hand, we will show that
\mathsf{MP}_{\omega_{1}\text{-pres}}(\omega_{1})
is consistent relative to some large cardinal axiom. We do not know whether this principle is
practically consistent
, i.e. whether it provably holds in some forcing extension assuming the existence of certain large cardinals. Hence we look for other means to establish consistency.

Kechris–Woodin
[
KW08
]
show that if both
\undertilde{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}
- and
\Delta^{1}_{2}
-determinacy hold then for a Turing cone of
x
, the theory of
L[x]
is stabilized. We aim to show that
\mathsf{MP}_{\omega_{1}\text{-pres}}(\omega_{1})
belongs to that theory.

Theorem 3.19
.

Assume that all
\Delta^{1}_{2}
- and
\undertilde{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}
-sets are determined.
Then there is some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
x\geq_{T}x_{0}
,

L[x]\models\mathsf{MP}_{\omega_{1}\text{-pres}}(\omega_{1}).

The determinacy assumption here follows from the existence of a Woodin cardinal and a measurable cardinal above. To prove Theorem
3.19
, we make use of the set-version of Jensen’s Coding the Universe Theorem.

Theorem 3.20

(Jensen
[
BJW82
]
)
.

Suppose
V=L[A]
for a set
A
and
\mathsf{GCH}
holds. Then there is a cardinal-preserving set-forcing extension
V[G]
such that
V[G]=L[y]
for a real
y
.

First, we prove the following variant of the result of Kechris–Woodin on the theory of
L[x]
.

Lemma 3.21
.

Assume that all
\Delta^{1}_{2}
- and
\undertilde{\Sigma}^{1}_{1}
-sets are determined. Then for a Turing cone of reals
x
, we have that for all reals
y
such that
L[y]
is a forcing extension of
L[x]
,

\mathrm{Th}\left(\left(L[x];\in,\alpha\mid\alpha<\omega_{1}^{L[x]}\right)\right)=\mathrm{Th}\left(\left(L[y];\in,\alpha\mid\alpha<\omega_{1}^{L[x]}\right)\right).

Proof.

For a real
x
, let
R_{x}
denote the set of all reals
y
so that
L[y]
is a forcing extension of
L[x]
.
Let
\varphi(x)
be a first order formula and let

A_{\varphi}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\mid\forall\alpha<\omega_{1}^{L[x]}\forall y\in R_{x}\ (L[x]\models\varphi(\alpha)\leftrightarrow L[y]\models\varphi(\alpha))\}.

We want to show that
A_{\varphi}
contains a Turing cone, so suppose this fails. By Kechris–Woodin, there is a theory
\mathcal{T}
so that for some
x_{0}
and all
x_{0}\leq_{T}x
,
\mathrm{Th}(L[x])=\mathcal{T}
. Thus the statement

\exists\alpha<\omega_{1}\exists\mathbb{P}\ \Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}``\exists y\in\mathbb{R}\ V[\dot{G}]=L[y]\wedge V\models\varphi(\check{\alpha})\leftrightarrow L[y]\models\neg\varphi(\check{\alpha}))"

belongs to
\mathcal{T}
. Let
\alpha_{\ast}
be a term for the least
\alpha
witnessing the above statement.
Note that
L[x]\models\mathcal{T}
for all reals
x
with
x_{0}\in L[x]
, so
\alpha_{\ast}^{x}\coloneqq\alpha_{\ast}^{L[x]}<\omega_{1}^{L[x]}
is defined for any such
x
.

Claim 3.22

4.
Separating
\Sigma_{n}
and
\Pi_{n}
-Maximality Principles

The consistency strength of
\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})
is strictly higher than the consistency strength of
\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, the natural way to force
\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}
seems to be to force
\Sigma_{n+2}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}^{\ast}(\mathbb{R})
which implies
\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})

\Pi_{n+1}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}^{\ast}(\mathbb{R})

\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})
is a consequence of
\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose the existence of a regular reflecting cardinal is consistent with
\mathsf{ZFC}
. Then for any

, there is a model of
\mathsf{ZFC}
in which
\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})\wedge\neg\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}

We start with a rough sketch of the argument. The basic idea is to start with a proper class of independent buttons
\langle B_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}\rangle
all of which are initially unpushed. To be more precise, each
B_{\alpha}
is a statement (in parameter
\alpha

V

in which

holds (“is pushed”) iff

.

We will carefully choose a
\Sigma_{n}
-definable class
A
and go to

in which only
B_{i}
for

is pushed. The regular
\Sigma_{n+2}
-correct cardinal

survives this step, so we may pass to a further generic extension

in which
\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})
holds. Of course,
\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})
may hold as well in

. The trick is to “unpush” one of the buttons
B_{i}
with

by passing to a ground

of

so that the
\Pi_{n}
-sentence

fails in
W
, but holds and does so persistently, in
V[G][h]
. Here we use that
A
is still
\Sigma_{n}
-definable in

and this is one of the reasons we start with

. This will be enough to ensure that
\Pi_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}
fails in
W
. The difficult part here is to arrange that
\Sigma_{n}\mathchar 45\mathsf{MP}(\mathbb{R})
remains true. This will come down to our choice of

and

. First, let

be sufficiently large so that all
\Sigma_{n}
statements about reals have a witness in

is
\Pi_{n-1}
,

is a real and

holds then
\exists y\in V_{\lambda_{0}}^{V[G][h]}\ \varphi(y,x)
. We will then be able to find a
\Sigma_{n-1}
-correct

and some

as well as a ground

of

and, crucially,

The only thing to worry about now is that there may be a

which can be forced to be persistent over

. But it is easy to see that this is not possible as

Definition 4.5
.

The class forcing
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
consist of functions
p\colon\alpha\rightarrow 2
with
\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}
, ordered by reverse inclusion.

The forcing
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
is
{<}\mathrm{Ord}
-closed so it is tame and does not add any sets. Also, we remark that
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
,
\leq
and

\Sigma_{0}
-definable.

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume
V=L
. There is a
\Sigma_{n+1}
-definable class

so that for all
\Sigma_{n}
-correct
\lambda

(1)

G^{L_{\lambda}}=G\cap L_{\lambda}
.

(2)

If

is
\Sigma_{n}
-definable from parameters in
L_{\lambda}
and any
p\in G\cap L_{\lambda}
can be extended to a condition in

.

Note that
(2)
implies that

is
\Sigma_{n}
-generic over
L
as well as over

for any
\Sigma_{n}
-correct

.

Proof.

Already the existence of any
\Sigma_{n+1}
-definable
\Sigma_{n}
-generic is not entirely trivial: for a
\Sigma_{n}
-class

, the statement “
D
is dense” is
\Pi_{n+1}
, but we want
A
to be
\Sigma_{n+1}
. Furthermore, it may happen that
\lambda
is
\Sigma_{n}
correct and

and

disagree about whether

is dense. The trick to get around this issue is to never ask the question whether a given class is dense, instead only ask whether a given

can be extended to a given
\Sigma_{n}
class

which is only
\Sigma_{n}
.

Using the canonical wellorder
<_{L}
of
L
, let
\langle\tau_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}\rangle
be a
\Sigma_{1}
-definable enumeration of all terms for
\Sigma_{n}
-classes.

Now define a decreasing sequence
\langle p_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}\rangle
through
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
as follows: Let
p_{0}=\emptyset
and for
\alpha\in\mathrm{Lim}
,
p_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}p_{\beta}
. If
p_{\alpha}
is defined then, if possible, let

be the

-least

with

\tau_{\alpha}
. Otherwise,

.

Note that the definition in the successor step is of complexity
\Sigma_{n}\vee\Pi_{n}
as there is a
\Sigma_{n}
-formula defining
\Sigma_{n}
-truth, so the whole recursion is
\Delta_{n+1}
. Moreover, carrying out this recursion in some
\Sigma_{n}
-correct

yields
\langle p_{\alpha}\mid\alpha<\lambda\rangle
. Finally, it is easy to see that

G=\{q\in\mathrm{Add}(\infty)\mid\exists\alpha\ p_{\alpha}\leq q\}

has the property
(1)
. For
(2)
, let

be any

-definable class (using parameters in

) so that any

can be extended to a condition in

is the evaluation of a term
\tau_{\alpha}
with

and hence
p_{\alpha+1}\in D\cap G\cap L_{\lambda}
.
∎

We remark that the
A
defined above is even
\Delta_{n+1}
. This is best possible in the sense that a
\Sigma_{n}
-generic

for
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
can be neither
\Sigma_{n}
nor
\Pi_{n}
-definable.

We will go on and prove that
A
above witnesses Theorem
4.3
to hold. To do so, we will prove a localized version of the forcing theorem for
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
along the following lines.

Definition 4.7
.

We enrich the forcing language for
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
with an additional atomic formula

v\in\dot{A}
and define
p\Vdash\check{x}\in\dot{A}
via
x\in\mathrm{dom}(p)\wedge p(x)=1
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose
G
is
\Sigma_{n}
-generic for
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
with associated generic class
A\subseteq\mathrm{Ord}
. For any
\Sigma_{n}
-formula
\varphi(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
and
x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\in V
, the following are equivalent

(1)

(V;\in,A)\models\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

(2)

There is
p\in G
with
p\Vdash\varphi(\check{x}_{0},\dots,\check{x}_{k})
.

While this is true, it is itself not very useful. The issue is that the forcing relation for
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
seems to be too complicated: For a
\Sigma_{n}
formula
\varphi
, the naive definition of the relation
p\Vdash\varphi(\check{x}_{0},\dots,\check{x}_{k})
is not better than
\Pi_{n+2}
, while we would really like it to be
\Sigma_{n}
. Through other means, we will see that it is
\Pi_{n+1}
for
n\geq 1
, but we do not know whether it is
\Sigma_{n}
. One problem here, which is specific to class forcing, is that
\mathrm{Add}(\infty)
has antichains which are proper classes and so
p\Vdash\exists x\varphi(x)
is not in general equivalent to
\exists\dot{x}\ p\Vdash\varphi(\dot{x})
, i.e. mixing fails. Even for
\varphi\in\Sigma_{0}
, it is a priori not clear whether the forcing relation for
\varphi
is any better than
\Pi_{2}
as a similar issue arises for bounded existential quantification.

To deal with this, we replace the forcing relation
\Vdash
with a stronger yet less complicated relation
\Vdash^{+}
for which we can nonetheless prove a localized forcing theorem. This in turn will rely on a careful analysis of the complexity of the relation
\Vdash^{+}
. This analysis holds more generally for all
{<}\mathrm{Ord}
-distributive forcings, so we carry it out in more generality.

Definition 4.9
.

A first order formula
\varphi
is
reduced
if

•

no implication appears in
\varphi
and

•

negations appear only immediately in front of atomic subformulas.

Note that any formula is tautologically equivalent to a reduced formula: First replace all implications
\varphi\rightarrow\psi
by
\neg\varphi\vee\psi
, then push all negations through as far as possible and finally eliminate double negations.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a class forcing.
For reduced formulas
\varphi(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\dot{G}}=\{\in,\dot{G}\}
and
x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\in V
,
p\in\mathbb{P}
, we define
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(\check{x}_{0},\dots,\check{x}_{k})
by induction of
\varphi
as follows:

•

p\Vdash^{+}x=y
iff
x=y
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}x\neq y
iff
x\neq y
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}x\in y
iff
x\in y
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}x\notin y
iff
x\notin y
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}x\in\dot{G}
iff
x\in\mathbb{P}\wedge p\leq x
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}x\notin\dot{G}
iff
x\notin\mathbb{P}\vee(x\in\mathbb{P}\wedge p\perp x)
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}\exists z\in y\ \varphi(z,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
iff
\exists z\in y\ p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(z,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}\forall z\in y\ \varphi(z,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
iff
\forall z\in y\ p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(z,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}\varphi_{0}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\wedge\varphi_{1}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
iff
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi_{0}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\wedge p\Vdash^{+}\varphi_{1}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}\varphi_{0}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\vee\varphi_{1}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
iff
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi_{0}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\vee p\Vdash^{+}\varphi_{1}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}\exists y\varphi(y,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
iff
\exists y\ p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(y,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

•

p\Vdash^{+}\forall y\varphi(y,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
iff
\forall q\leq p\forall y\exists r\leq q\ r\Vdash^{+}\varphi(y,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

We will now prove some basic properties of
\Vdash^{+}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be some
{<}\mathrm{Ord}
-distributive class forcing. The strong forcing relation
\Vdash^{+}
has the following properties for any reduced formula
\varphi
in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\dot{G}}
.

(1)

If
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
and
q\leq p
then
q\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

(2)

p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
implies
p\Vdash\varphi(\check{x}_{0},\dots,\check{x}_{k})
.

(3)

p\Vdash\varphi(\check{x}_{0},\dots,\check{x}_{k})
iff

\{q\leq p\mid q\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\}

is dense below
p
.

(4)

The class

\{p\in\mathbb{P}\mid p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\vee p\Vdash^{+}\varphi^{\neg}(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\}

is dense. Here,
\varphi^{\neg}
is the reduced formula induced by
\neg\varphi
.

Proof.

(1)
is easy and a straightforward induction on the complexity of
\varphi
proves
(2)
, so we skip it.

Note that the reverse implication in
(3)
is already a consequence of
(2)
: If

\{q\leq p\mid q\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\}

is dense below
p
then so is
\{q\leq p\mid q\Vdash\varphi(\check{x}_{0},\dots,\check{x}_{k})\}
and hence
p\Vdash\varphi(\check{x}_{0},\dots,\check{x}_{k})
.

We will now prove the forwards implication of
(3)
. We proceed by induction on the complexity of
\varphi
. The atomic case is easy.

\varphi=\psi_{0}\wedge\psi_{1}
:

Let
q\leq p\Vdash\psi_{0}\wedge\psi_{1}
. By induction there is
r\leq q
with
r\Vdash^{+}\psi_{0}
and further
s\leq r
with
s\Vdash^{+}\psi_{1}
. By
(1)
,
s\Vdash^{+}\psi_{0}
as well and hence
s\Vdash^{+}\psi_{0}\wedge\psi_{1}
.

\varphi=\psi_{0}\vee\psi_{1}
:

If
p\Vdash\psi_{0}\vee\psi_{1}
then for any
q\leq p
there is
r\leq q
and
i<2
so that
r\Vdash\psi_{i}
. By induction, there is
s\leq r
so that
s\Vdash^{+}\psi_{i}
and hence
s\Vdash^{+}\psi_{0}\vee\psi_{1}
.

\varphi=\exists z\in y\ \psi(z)
:

If
p\Vdash\exists z\in\check{y}\ \varphi(z)
then for any
q\leq p
, there is
r\leq q
and
z\in y
with
r\Vdash\psi(\check{z})
. By induction, there is
s\leq r
with
s\Vdash^{+}\psi(z)
and hence
s\Vdash^{+}\exists z\in y\ \psi(z)
.

\varphi=\forall z\in y\ \psi(z)
:

Suppose that
p\Vdash\forall z\in\check{y}\ \psi(z)
. Then clearly for all
z\in y
,
p\Vdash\psi(\check{z})
and hence

D_{z}=\{q\leq p\mid q\Vdash^{+}\psi(z)\}

is dense open below
p
. As
\mathbb{P}
is
{<}\mathrm{Ord}
-distributive,
\bigcap_{z\in y}D_{z}
is dense below
p
and for any
q\in\bigcap_{z\in y}D_{z}
, we have
q\Vdash^{+}\forall z\in y\ \psi(z)
.

\varphi=\exists z\psi(z)
:

Similar to the bounded existential case.

\varphi=\forall z\psi(z)
:

Assuming
p\Vdash\forall z\psi(z)
, we will show that in fact
p\Vdash^{+}\forall z\varphi(z)
. Given
q\leq p
and
y
, we have
q\Vdash\varphi(\check{y})
so that by induction, there is
r\leq q
with
r\Vdash^{+}\varphi(y)
. Hence
p\Vdash^{+}\forall y\psi(y)
by definition of
\Vdash^{+}
.

For
(4),
suppose there is no
q\leq p
with
q\Vdash^{+}\varphi
. Then by
(3)
, there is no
q\leq p
with
q\Vdash\varphi
and hence
p\Vdash\neg\varphi
. As
\neg\varphi
and
\varphi^{\neg}
are tautologically equivalent,
p\Vdash\varphi^{\neg}
so that by
(3)
again, there is some
q\leq p
with
q\Vdash^{+}\varphi^{\neg}
.
∎

The strong forcing relation
\Vdash^{+}
has some quirks which are not shared by the usual forcing relation. For example, it can happen that
p\Vdash^{+}\forall x\varphi(x)
, yet there is no single
x
with
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x)
. Further, there may be reduced sentences
\varphi,\psi
so that
\varphi
tautologically implies
\psi
,
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi
and yet
p\not\Vdash^{+}\psi
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Suppose
\mathbb{P}
is a class forcing and
\varphi(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
is a reduced
\Sigma_{m}
(resp.
\Pi_{m}
) formula in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\dot{G}}
. Then the relation

\{(p,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\mid p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\}

is
\Sigma_{m}
(resp.
\Pi_{m}
) definable over
(V;\in,\mathbb{P},\leq,\perp)
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on the complexity of
\varphi
.

\varphi\in\Sigma_{0}
:

This is straightforward.

\varphi\in\Pi_{1}
:

Say
\varphi(a)=\forall x\psi(x,a)
where
\psi
is
\Sigma_{0}
. For a given
x
, let

D_{x}=\{q\in\mathbb{P}\mid\forall r\in\mathrm{tc}(\{x,a\})\ (q\leq r\vee q\perp r)\}.

An inspection of the definition of
\Vdash^{+}
shows that if
q\in D_{x}
and
r\leq q
with
r\Vdash^{+}\psi(x,a)
then already
q\Vdash^{+}\psi(x,a)
. Further, as
\mathbb{P}
is
{<}\mathrm{Ord}
-distributive,
D_{x}
is dense. It follows that
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(a)
is equivalent to

\forall q\leq p\forall x(q\in D_{x}\rightarrow q\Vdash^{+}\psi(x,a))

which is
\Pi_{1}
.

\varphi\in\Sigma_{m+1}
:

Suppose
\varphi=\exists x\psi(x,a)
with
\psi\in\Pi_{n}
. We have
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(a)
iff
\exists x\ p\Vdash^{+}\psi(x,a)
which is
\Sigma_{m+1}
by induction.

\varphi\in\Pi_{m+2}
:

\varphi
is of the form
\forall x_{0}\dots\forall x_{k}\psi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
with
\psi
a reduced
\Sigma_{n}
formula. With a moment of reflection, one can see that

p\Vdash^{+}\forall x_{0}\dots\forall x_{k}\psi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})

is equivalent to

\forall q\leq p\forall x_{0}\dots\forall x_{k}\exists r\leq q\ r\Vdash^{+}\psi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})

which is
\Pi_{m+1}
by induction as
m+1>0
.

∎

In the proof of Lemma
4.11
, we showed that if
\varphi
is of the form
\forall x\psi(x)
then
p\Vdash\varphi(\check{x})
iff
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x)
. Thus we also get the optimal complexity of the standard forcing relation for the
\Pi_{m}
complexity classes.

Corollary 4.13
.

Suppose
\mathbb{P}
is a
{<}\mathrm{Ord}
-distributive class forcing,
m\geq 1
and
\varphi
is a
\Pi_{m}
-formula in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\dot{G}}
. Then the relation

\{(p,x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\mid p\Vdash\varphi(\check{x}_{0},\dots,\check{x}_{k})\}

is
\Pi_{m}
-definable over
(V;\in,\mathbb{P},\leq,\perp)
.

We are now in good shape to prove the localized forcing theorem for the strong forcing relation.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a
{<}\mathrm{Ord}
-distributive class forcing and suppose
G
is
\Sigma_{n}
-generic for
\mathbb{P}
. For any reduced
\Sigma_{n}
-formula
\varphi(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\dot{G}}
and
x_{0},\dots,x_{k}\in V
, the following are equivalent

(1)

(V;\in,G)\models\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

(2)

There is
p\in G
with
p\Vdash^{+}\varphi(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
.

Proof.

We do so by induction on the complexity of
\varphi
. The case where
\varphi
is a Boolean combination of atomic formulas as well as the bounded existential quantification case is easy. We will further skip the cases of conjunction and disjunction.

\varphi=\forall x\in y\ \psi(x)
:

If there is
p\in G
with
p\Vdash^{+}\forall x\in y\psi(x)
then
\forall x\in y\ p\Vdash^{+}\psi(x)
so that
\forall x\in y(V;\in,G)\models\psi(x)
and hence
(V;\in,G)\models\forall x\in y\ \psi(x)
. On the other hand, if
(V;\in,G)\models\forall x\in y\ \psi(x)
then for all
x\in y
there is some
p_{x}\in G
with
p\Vdash^{+}\psi(x)
. The set

D=\{q\in\mathbb{P}\mid\forall x\in y\ q\leq p_{x}\vee\exists x\in y\ q\perp p_{x}\}

is dense in
\mathbb{P}
as
\mathbb{P}
is
{<}\mathrm{Ord}
-distributive. Also,
D
is
\Sigma_{0}
-definable and hence
G
meets
D
. It follows that there is
p\in G
with
\forall x\in y\ p\Vdash^{+}\psi(x)
so that
p\Vdash^{+}\forall x\in y\ \psi(x)
.

\varphi=\exists x\ \varphi(x)
:

If
(V;\in,G)\models\exists x\psi(x)
then let
x
witness this. Hence there is
p\in G
with
p\Vdash^{+}\psi(x)
so that
p\Vdash^{+}\exists x\psi(x)
. On the other hand, if
p\Vdash^{+}\exists x\ \psi(x)
and
p\in G
then there is
x
so that
p\Vdash^{+}\psi(x)
and hence
(V;\in,G)\models\psi(x)
.

\varphi=\forall x\psi(x)
:

We may assume that
\psi
is at most
\Sigma_{n-2}
. Suppose first that

(V;\in,G)\models\forall x\ \psi(x).

Let
\psi^{\neg}
denote the reduced
\Pi_{n-2}
formula induced by
\neg\psi
. The set

D\coloneqq\{p\in\mathbb{P}\mid p\Vdash^{+}\forall x\ \psi(x)\vee p\Vdash^{+}\exists x\psi^{\neg}(x)\}

is dense by Lemma
4.11
and
\Sigma_{n-1}\vee\Pi_{n-1}
-definable. Thus
there is some
p\in D\cap G
as
G
is
\Sigma_{n}
-generic. We cannot have that
p\Vdash^{+}\psi(x)^{\neg}
for some
x
as then
(V;\in,G)\models\neg\psi(x)
, so
p\Vdash^{+}\forall x\psi(x)
is the only other possibility.

Now assume that
p\Vdash^{+}\forall x\psi(x)
for some
p\in G
. This implies that for any
x
, the set

D_{x}=\{q\in\mathbb{P}\mid q\perp p\vee(q\leq p\wedge q\Vdash^{+}\psi(x))\}

is dense and, as
D_{x}
is
\Sigma_{n-2}
,
D_{x}\cap G\neq\emptyset
. Hence
(V;\in,G)\models\psi(x)
by induction, for any
x
.

∎

Remark 4.15
.

While we will make no use of Lemma

.

Suppose

4.2.
Separating Maximality Principles

We now aim to proof the main theorem. For the remainder of this section,
we assume
V=L
and that
\kappa
is a reflecting cardinal
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathrm{Ord}
be any definable class. There is no definable proper class antichain in
\mathbb{P}_{A}
.

The large cardinal assumption is necessary to prove this. If there are no inaccessible cardinals and there is a definable global wellorder then
\mathbb{P}_{A}
has a proper class antichain for all proper classes
A
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
A
is definable with parameters in
L_{\kappa}
and suppose towards a contradiction that there is a proper class definable antichain
\mathcal{A}
. Similarly, we may assume that
\mathcal{A}
is definable with parameters in
L_{\kappa}
. Using the canonical wellorder of
L
, let
\langle p_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}\rangle
be a definable one-to-one enumeration of
\mathcal{A}
. Let

f\colon I\rightarrow V,\ f(\lambda)=p_{\lambda}\upharpoonright\lambda,

where
I
is the class of inaccessible cardinals and note that
f
is essentially regressive in the sense that
f(\lambda)\in V_{\lambda}
.

Claim 4.18

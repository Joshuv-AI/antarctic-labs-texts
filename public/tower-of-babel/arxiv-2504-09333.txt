Global results for weakly dispersive KP-II equations on the cylinder Thanks: *Corresponding author
Sebastian Herr, Robert Schippa*, Nikolay Tzvetkov

Abstract
. We consider the dispersion-generalized KP-II equation on a partially periodic domain in the weakly dispersive regime. We use Fourier decoupling techniques to derive essentially sharp Strichartz estimates. With these at hand, we show global well-posedness of the quasilinear Cauchy problem in L 2 ​ ( ℝ × 𝕋 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}) . Finally, we prove a long-time decay property of solutions with small mass by using the Kato smoothing effect in the fractional case.

35Q53, 42B37.

1.
Introduction

We consider the following dispersion-generalized KP-II equation (fKP-II) posed on the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
,
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
:

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}u+\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u&=u\partial_{x}u,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}).\end{array}\right.

D_{x}^{\alpha}
and
\partial_{x}^{-1}
denote Fourier multipliers, which are defined by

\widehat{(D_{x}^{\alpha}f)}(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi),\;\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\quad\widehat{(\partial_{x}^{-1}f)}(\xi)=(i\xi)^{-1}\hat{f}(\xi).

See
[
38
, Section 5.2.1]
for a discussion of the derivation of this model.

We consider real-valued data, which is preserved by the nonlinear evolution. In the present paper we are concerned with global well-posedness in
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces, i.e., existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence of the solution on the initial data and the global behavior of the solution. The Cauchy problem for the KP-II equation is given by (
1
) for
\alpha=2
and is known to admit a smooth data-to-solution mapping in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
by previous work of the third author with Molinet and Saut
[
49
]
.

We address the weakly dispersive regime where
\alpha<2
, in which the Cauchy problem is
quasilinear
, i.e., the data-to-solution mapping, if it exists in
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces, fails to be smooth (see
[
47
]
).

Conserved quantities for real initial data are the mass

M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}u^{2}dxdy

and the energy

E_{\alpha}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\big(\frac{1}{2}|D_{x}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{6}u^{3}\big)dxdy.

Since the energy is indefinite, it can hardly be utilized in a proof of global well-posedness. Here we use the mass, which requires a local well-posedness result in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
.

On
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have the scaling symmetry

u_{\lambda}(t,x,y)=\lambda^{\alpha}u(\lambda^{\alpha+1}t,\lambda x,\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}y).

Define the anisotropic homogeneous Sobolev norm as

\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1},s_{2}}}=\||\xi|^{s_{1}}|\eta|^{s_{2}}\hat{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}.

For
s_{2}=0
, we find that
\dot{H}^{s_{1},0}
with
s_{1}=1-\frac{3\alpha}{4}
is the scaling-critical space. Since
s_{2}<0
breaks the Galilean invariance,
\dot{H}^{s_{1},0}
is distinguished.

The KP-II equation was proved to be globally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by Bourgain
[
7
]
. Global well-posedness on the cylinder in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
was considered by Molinet–Saut–Tzvetkov
[
49
]
. We remark that the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equations were derived to model water waves in two dimensions; see the works by Kadomtsev–Petviashvili
[
32
]
and Ablowitz–Segur
[
1
]
with an emphasis on modeling transverse perturbations of the KdV soliton. Stability of the line soliton was proved in
[
46
]
; see also
[
50
,
51
]
. For this reason the cylindrical domain
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
is of importance when studying (
1
). We remark that on the domain
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
the evolution satisfies improved dispersive properties compared to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
; see
[
16
]
. On
\mathbb{R}^{2}
Hadac–Herr–Koch
[
22
,
23
]
proved scattering in the critical space
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for small initial data.

The Cauchy problem (
1
) extends the fractional KdV equation on the real line:

(2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}u&=u\partial_{x}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).\end{array}\right.

For
\alpha=2
the original KdV equation is recovered, for
\alpha=1
the model describes the likewise prominent Benjamin–Ono equation. But the nonlinear evolution of these two models is very different in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The KdV evolution in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is known to be
semilinear
with a real analytic data-to-solution mapping as a consequence of the contraction mapping principle. Seminal contributions are due to Bourgain
[
6
]
and Kenig–Ponce–Vega
[
34
]
. The
I
-team
[
11
]
finally proved sharp semilinear global well-posedness. More recently, Killip–Vişan
[
36
]
devised the
method of commuting flows
, which utilizes the complete integrability pointed out many decades ago by Lax
[
42
]
in unweighted Sobolev spaces. They proved the sharp global well-posedness in
H^{-1}(\mathbb{R})
.

On the other hand, for
\alpha<2
, (
2
) is a quasilinear Cauchy problem and it is known that it cannot be solved via Picard iteration for any Sobolev regularity
[
47
,
40
,
24
]
.

Herr–Ionescu–Kenig–Koch
[
25
]
proved global well-posedness of (
2
) in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for
\alpha\in(1,2)
by using a paradifferential gauge transform. Recently, Ai–Liu
[
2
]
reported an improvement to
s>\frac{3}{4}(1-\alpha)
. The gauge transform for the Benjamin–Ono equation was applied for the first time by Tao
[
57
]
who showed global well-posedness in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
in combination with Strichartz estimates. Combining the gauge transform with Fourier restriction analysis Ionescu–Kenig
[
31
]
showed global well-posedness for the Benjamin–Ono equation in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see also
[
10
,
28
,
48
]
). Recently, Killip
et al.

[
35
]
proved sharp global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>-\frac{1}{2}
via the method of commuting flows. Coming back to (
1
): Let

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=\xi|\xi|^{\alpha}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi}

denote the dispersion relation.
We note that for the KP-II equation the resonance function is given by

\begin{split}\Omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})&=\omega_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})-\omega_{2}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})-\omega_{2}(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})\\
&=3(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}.\end{split}

The fact that both terms are of the same sign highlights a nonlinear defocusing effect. This implies that the resonance function for the KP-II equation is at least as large as the resonance function for the KdV equation, for the first time effectively taken advantage of by Bourgain
[
7
]
who proved global well-posedness in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Low regularity local well-posedness for fKP-II (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was proved by Hadac
[
21
]
for
\alpha>4/3
, up to which the problem is semilinear and
L^{2}
-subcritical. Of course, on the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
one can apply the energy method to prove a local well-posedness result in isotropic Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
for
s>2
.
We remark that the argument from Linares–Pilod–Saut
[
43
]
would yield an improvement of the energy method on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
, but no global result. The main point of this paper is to prove the first global result for the Cauchy problem for fKP-II on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
.

Recently, we
[
27
]
extended Bourgain’s
L^{2}
-result in the fully periodic case. We obtained local well-posedness in
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for
s>-\frac{1}{90}
. The proof interpolates short-time bilinear Strichartz estimates and novel
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates derived from an
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality due to Guth–Maldague–Oh
[
20
]
.

For (
1
) we use Fourier restriction analysis on frequency-dependent time intervals and an interpolation argument in a similar way like in
[
27
]
. This allows us to show well-posedness in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
for (
1
) with
\alpha<2
. The decoupling argument recovers the scaling critical Strichartz estimates up to the endpoint provided that the
\eta
-frequencies are not too large compared to the
\xi
-frequencies. Suppose that
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\xi|\sim N,\;|\eta|\lesssim N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}\}.
Then we have the following estimate:

\|e^{t(\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2})}f\|_{L^{4}_{t}([0,1],L^{4}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{2-\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}.

In Section
3
we argue in greater generality how the decoupling estimate from
[
20
]
yields Strichartz estimates for dispersion relations with uniformly bounded derivatives, which are sharp up to endpoints. The argument does not depend on the domain.

A crucial difference between KP-II on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and fKP-II on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
is the occurence of very small frequencies
N\ll 1
. For the fKP-II equations (
1
) with
\alpha<2
, there is clearly no way to show local well-posedness in
H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
via the contraction mapping principle as this would yield a real analytic well-posedness result for (
2
) in
H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
.
We emphasize that the dispersion-generalized KP-II equations are not known to be completely integrable. Let
\alpha^{\star}:=2-0.015
. We show the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(\alpha^{\star},2)
. Then (
1
) is globally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
for real-valued initial data.

Furthermore, in Proposition
8.1
we show existence of solutions and persistence of regularity in a regularity scale.
Since employing a gauge transform for (
1
) in higher dimensions appears to be very complicated, we opt for the different approach of frequency-dependent time localization. Early instances are due to Koch–Tzvetkov
[
39
]
, where Strichartz estimates on frequency-dependent time intervals are used to solve the Benjamin–Ono equation. Here we modify the implementation due to Ionescu–Kenig–Tataru
[
30
]
. In
[
30
]
an analysis on frequency-dependent time intervals was carried out using Fourier restriction norms to solve the KP-I equation globally in the energy space.

Here the frequency-dependent time localization will be chosen depending on
\alpha
. Moreover, we use a modulation weight to take advantage of large modulations due to the nonlinear defocusing effect. We explain our choice of time localization in Subsection
2.3
.

We remark that recently Guo–Molinet
[
18
]
obtained unconditional global well-posedness for the KP-I equation in the energy space without using the short-time Fourier restriction spaces (see also
[
17
,
37
]
). Crucially, they show a priori bounds
L_{T}^{p}L^{\infty}_{xy}
bounds of the solution which strongly hinges on dispersive effects. The argument to conclude well-posedness still relies on frequency-dependent time localization and a trilinear smoothing estimate related to the nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequality (cf.
[
30
]
). The
L^{2}
regularity we are dealing with in the present paper seems to be too low to use a similar iteration scheme.

Implementing the interpolation argument in short-time Fourier restriction spaces in the present analyis yields further losses. While the
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates are sharp, the interpolation with multilinear estimates can likely be improved to lower the value of
\alpha^{\star}
for
L^{2}
global well-posedness. We make certain choices in the interpolation argument (see Section
5
and Remark
5.3
) to simplify the computations and do not strive for optimality here.

With short-time bilinear and energy estimates at hand, the conclusion of the proof of local well-posedness is well-known. We shall be brief in Section
8
. More details are given when constructing regular solutions. To this end, we use a Galerkin approximation and a compactness argument to construct solutions.

The starting point to show local well-posedness are a priori estimates for solutions
u
to (
1
) :

\|u_{i}(t)\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim_{t,\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}}}\|u(0)\|_{H^{s,0}}.

A key point in the proof of the full local well-posedness result is that we estimate differences of solutions at negative Sobolev regularity
s^{\prime}=-8(2-\alpha)
. The Lipschitz dependence we show for differences
v=u_{1}-u_{2}
,
u_{i}
,
i=1,2
solutions to (
1
), reads

\|v(t)\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim_{\|u_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}}\|v(0)\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}

for
0\leq t\leq T(\|u_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}})
. The proof is then concluded by using frequency envelopes which is implemented like in our previous work
[
27
]
and based on the general outline detailed in
[
29
]
. This argument originates in work of Tao
[
56
]
.

Further, we address the long time behavior of small solutions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(\alpha^{*},2)
. There exists
\mu>0
such that for every
u_{0}
such that

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}<\mu

the solution of (
1
) established in Theorem
1.1
satisfies for any
\gamma>0

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\,\int_{x\geq\gamma t}u^{2}(t,x,y)dxdy=0.

The
L^{2}
conservation law of (
1
) implies the
L^{2}
stability of the zero solution of (
1
).
However, this stability does not give any information on asymptotical stability, i.e. it does not give any information on how the conserved
L^{2}
mass evolves in time.
The result of Theorem
1.2
implies that at time
t
there is essentially no
L^{2}
mass in the region
x\geq\gamma t
.

It would be very interesting to extend Theorem
1.2
to the case when the zero solution is replaced by a 1d soliton. This would require an approach to the transverse stability of the KP-II line solitons independent of the Miura transform used in
[
46
]
. The approach of
[
45
]
is less dependent on the integrability of the KP-II equation, and it would be worthwhile to investigate whether a similar approach may lead to the transverse stability of the line solitons of (
1
).

Moreover, the smallness assumption in Theorem
1.2
cannot be dropped. Indeed, arguing like in the proof of
[
33
, Proposition 1]
(see also
[
13
]
) one may show that for every
c>0
there is a nontrivial
L^{2}
solution of (
1
) of the form

u(t,x,y)=Q(x-ct)

and for such a solution

\int_{x\geq\gamma t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}Q^{2}(x-ct)dxdy=2\pi\int_{(\gamma-c)t}^{\infty}Q^{2}(x)dx

which tends to
2\pi\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\neq 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, provided
c>\gamma
. This implies that
\mu<\sqrt{2\pi}\|Q\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
is necessary.

Basic notation:

•

\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
denotes the one-dimensional torus with period
2\pi
.

•

Dyadic numbers
N,L,\ldots\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
are typically denoted by capital letters.

•

Time and space variables are given by
(t,x,y)
, the dual frequency variables are denoted by
(\tau,\xi,\eta)
.

•

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}})
denotes the space of measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}
endowed with Lebesgue norms given by

\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\mathbb{T}^{d_{2}}}|f(x,y)|^{p}dxdy

and the usual modification for
p=\infty
.

•

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
denotes the smooth complex-valued functions which, together with their derivatives, decay faster than any polynomial: For
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have

\|\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{y}^{\gamma}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}<\infty.

•

For
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
the inhomogeneous Sobolev norms are defined as

\|f\|_{H^{s_{1},s_{2}}}:=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s_{1}}\langle\eta\rangle^{s_{2}}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}.

We define for
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
the space
H^{s_{1},s_{2}}
as closure of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s_{1},s_{2}}}
.

•

For
T>0
and a normed function space
X
we use the short notation
C_{T}X=C([-T,T],X)
, similarly for
L_{T}^{\infty}X
.

•

We denote an estimate
A\leq CB
by
A\lesssim B
with
C
being a harmless constant, which is allowed to change from line to line.

•

Further dependencies are indicated with subindices, e.g.,
A\lesssim_{\varepsilon,p}B
means that
A\leq C(\varepsilon,p)B
for a constant
C
depending on
\varepsilon
and
p
.

2.
Notations and Function spaces

In the following we introduce the short-time Fourier restriction spaces to carry out the analysis. The notation and conventions closely follow
[
27
, Section 2]
.

2.1.
Fourier transform and Littlewood-Paley decomposition

The space-time Fourier transform of a function
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

(\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}u)(\tau,\xi,\eta)=\hat{u}(\tau,\xi,\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}e^{-it\tau}e^{-ix\xi}e^{-iy\eta}u(t,x,y)dtdxdy.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
,
f\in L^{1}_{\tau,\xi}\ell^{1}_{\eta}
. The inverse Fourier transform is defined by

(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}f)(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{it\tau}e^{ix\xi}\sum_{\eta}e^{iy\eta}f(\tau,\xi,\eta)d\tau d\xi.

Let
\eta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,2))
with
\eta_{0}\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
and
\eta_{0}
be radially decreasing. We define

\eta_{1}(\tau)=\eta_{0}(\tau/2)-\eta_{0}(\tau).

For
L\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
we set
\eta_{L}(\tau)=\eta(\tau/L)
. Let
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
be a dyadic number. We define frequency projections with respect to the
\xi
-frequencies by

P_{N}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}),\quad u\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}(\eta_{N}(\xi)\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}u).

2.2.
Function spaces

Let
\alpha\in[1,2]
. Recall that the dispersion relation reads

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=\xi|\xi|^{\alpha}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi}.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
. We denote the linear propagation on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
by

S_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)d\xi.

To address the singularity
\{\xi=0\}
of the symbol, we firstly explain the evolution on the dense subspace comprised of functions with frequency support away from
\{\xi=0\}
. A similar frequency cutoff is carried out in Section
8.1
. So we presently omit the details.

We define modulation projections for
L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\cup\{0\}
for functions
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
by

\widehat{(Q_{L}u)}(\tau,\xi,\eta)=\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))\hat{u}(\tau,\xi,\eta).

We introduce a modulation weight already used by Bourgain
[
7
]
; see also
[
27
]
. This will be crucial to show a suitable estimate in case the high modulation is on the low frequency.

Let
N_{+}=\max(1,N)
. For
g:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
with
g(\tau,\xi,\eta)=0
unless
|\xi|\in[N/4,4N]
we define

\begin{split}\|g\|_{X_{N}}&=\sum_{L\geq 1}L^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L/N_{+}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))g\|_{L_{\tau,\xi,\eta}^{2}},\\
\|g\|_{\bar{X}_{N}}&=\sum_{L\geq 1}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))g\|_{L_{\tau,\xi,\eta}^{2}}.\end{split}

Above we indicate the integration in frequency variables in the subindices. Note that the measure is the product of the Lebesgue measure in
\xi
and
\tau
and the counting measure in
\eta
.

We let

\begin{split}D_{N,L}&=\{(\xi,\eta,\tau):\;|\xi|\sim N,\,|\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)|\sim L\},\\
D_{N,\leq L}&=\{(\xi,\eta,\tau):\;|\xi|\sim N,\,|\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)|\leq L\}\end{split}

denote dyadically localized regions in space-time frequencies with fixed frequency and modulation.

For
\beta>0
the short-time Fourier restriction norm
F_{N}
at frequencies
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
is defined by

\|u\|_{F_{N}}=\sup_{t_{k}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\eta_{0}(N_{+}^{\beta}(t-t_{k}))P_{N}u]\|_{X_{N}}.

The dual norm
\mathcal{N}_{N}
prescribed by Duhamel’s formula is given by

\|f\|_{\mathcal{N}_{N}}=\sup_{t_{k}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)+iN_{+}^{\beta})^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\eta_{0}(N_{+}^{\beta}(t-t_{k}))P_{N}u]\|_{X_{N}}.

As well
F_{N}
as
\mathcal{N}_{N}
are localized in time by the usual means: Let
T\in(0,1]
. For a frequency localized function
u:[-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
we let

\|u\|_{G_{N}(T)}=\inf_{\tilde{u}\big|_{[0,T]}=u\big|_{[0,T]}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{G_{N}},\quad G\in\{F,\mathcal{N}\},

where we take the infimum over all extensions
\tilde{u}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
. We explain in the next subsection why we choose
\beta=(2-\alpha)+\varepsilon
as time localization for
\alpha\in[1,2]
.

The following lemma (see
[
30
, Eq. (2.4)]
) allows us to localize time to any shorter time intervals than required by the short-time norms provided that we do not use the weight:

Lemma 2.1
.

For
M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and
f_{N}\in\bar{X}_{N}
, the following estimate holds:

\begin{split}&\sum_{J\geq M}J^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{J}(\tau-\omega(\xi,\eta))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|f_{N}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)\big|M^{-1}(1+M^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\

Proof.

For the small modulations

\begin{split}&\quad M^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{\leq M}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))\int|f_{N}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)|M^{-1}(1+M^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\

&\lesssim\sum_{L\geq 1}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))f_{N}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.\end{split}

We turn to an estimate of the modulations greater than
M

Decompose

\begin{split}&\quad f_{N}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)\\
&=(\eta_{\lesssim M}(\tau^{\prime}-\omega_{\alpha})+\sum_{M\ll L\ll J}\eta_{L}(\tau^{\prime}-\omega_{\alpha})+\sum_{L\sim M}\eta_{L}(\tau^{\prime}-\omega_{\alpha})+\sum_{L\gg J}\eta_{L}(\tau^{\prime}-\omega_{\alpha}))\\

For the estimate of the first term note that

\begin{split}&\quad J^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{-1}(J/M)^{-4}\|\eta_{J}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha})\int\eta_{\lesssim M}(\tau^{\prime}-\omega_{\alpha})|f_{N}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)|d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim J^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}(J/M)^{-4}\|\eta_{\lesssim M}f_{N}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.\end{split}

The summation over

For the second term we find similarly

\begin{split}&\sum_{L:M\ll L\ll J}J^{\frac{1}{2}}(J/M)^{-2}\|\eta_{J}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha})\int\eta_{L}(\tau^{\prime}-\omega_{\alpha})|f_{N}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)|\\
&\quad\times M^{-1}(1+M^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{L:M\ll L\ll J}J^{\frac{1}{2}}(J/M)^{-2}L^{\frac{1}{2}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha})f_{N}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim(J/M)^{-\frac{5}{2}}\|f_{N}\|_{\bar{X}_{N}},\end{split}

again with straight-forward summation.

We turn to the third term. In this case we cannot argue by rapid decay of the kernel

\begin{split}&\quad J^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{J}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha})\int|f_{N}(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)|\eta_{L}(\tau^{\prime}-\omega_{\alpha})M^{-1}(1+M^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}L^{2}_{\tau}}\\

Lastly, for the final term we obtain the estimate

The summation is again straight-forward.

Remark 2.7

\|P_{N}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|P_{N}u(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{N}u(s,x,y)\partial_{x}P_{N}(u\cdot u)(s,x,y)dxdyds.

The different frequency interactions are recovered from a paraproduct decomposition:

P_{N}(u\cdot u)=2P_{N}(uP_{\ll N}u)+P_{N}(P_{\gtrsim N}u\cdot P_{\gtrsim N}u).

Note that in the second expression the frequencies must be of comparable modulus by Littlewood-Paley dichotomy and the derivative is already acting on the lowest frequency.

By a standard commutator argument, using the real-valuedness of the solution, this can be accomplished as well for the first term. After expanding

Going further, after (smoothly) localizing time to an interval of length

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}(\gamma(N^{\beta}(t-t_{k}))P_{N}u)(\gamma(N^{\beta}(t-t_{k}))P_{N}u)(\partial_{x}P_{K}u).

5.1
.

Lastly, we note that for differences of solutions, we have less symmetries at disposal and the integration by parts and commutator arguments can no longer assign the derivative to the lowest frequency. Here the estimate at negative regularities comes to rescue. We show the short-time energy estimates in Section
7
.

3.
Linear Strichartz estimates via decoupling

In this section we show sharp Strichartz estimates via
\ell^{2}
-decoupling. To explain the key ingredients, we start with more general considerations, relating Knapp examples with the notion of flat sets.

3.1.
Decoupling into flat sets and the Knapp example

Decoupling estimates origin in the work of Wolff
[
59
]
on local smoothing for the Euclidean wave equation; see also
[
41
,
14
]
. Finally, Bourgain–Demeter
[
8
]
obtained sharp
\ell^{2}
-decoupling estimates for elliptic surfaces and their conical extensions. Let

\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}f(x,t)=\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{i(\langle x,\xi\rangle+t|\xi|^{2})}f(\xi)d\xi

denote the Fourier extension operator for the paraboloid. The
\ell^{2}
-decoupling estimates read

\|\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}f\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}(B_{d+1}(0,R))}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta:R^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\text{ball}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\Delta}f_{\theta}\|^{2}_{L^{p}_{t,x}(w_{B_{d+1}(0,R)})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for
2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
. Above
w_{B_{d+1}}(0,R)
denotes a weight with high polynomial decay away from
B_{d+1}(0,R)
. The
R^{-\frac{1}{2}}
-balls are distinguished for elliptic surfaces as on scales
|(x,t)|\leq R
these trivialize the Fourier extension. This is a simple consequence of Taylor expanding the phase function. By approximating exponential sums with the Fourier extension operator, Bourgain–Demeter
[
8
]
proved sharp (up to endpoints) Strichartz estimates for elliptic Schrödinger equations on rational and irrational tori.

Whereas the Strichartz estimates for elliptic Schrödinger equations on tori for finite times resemble the estimates on Euclidean space up to arbitrarily small additional derivative loss, this drastically fails for hyperbolic Schrödinger equations (see also
[
9
]
). We shall see below that the fKP-II surfaces are non-elliptic.

Let
\Box=\partial_{1}^{2}-\partial_{2}^{2}
denote the hyperbolic Laplacian in two dimensions. Then, due to the dispersive estimate, on Euclidean space it holds

\|e^{it\Box}u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{4}(\mathbb{R};L^{4}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

On the square torus there is no oscillation on the diagonal
\{\xi=\eta\}
, which points out that the following
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimate is sharp:

(7)

\|P_{N}e^{it\Box}u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{4}([0,1],L^{4}_{xy}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{4}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

This has been shown in
[
15
]
, see also
[
58
]
and
[
54
]
.
But on irrational tori
\mathbb{T}_{\gamma}^{2}=\mathbb{T}\times\gamma\mathbb{T}
,
\gamma\in(1/2,1]\backslash\mathbb{Q}
, this example is clearly excluded, which leads to the natural question of a possible improvement of (
7
) on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}
. This question was recently answered affirmatively by Guth–Maldague–Oh
[
20
]
, who showed an
\ell^{2}
-decoupling inequality into “flat sets”. To describe their result, let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
denote a smooth function and
S=\{(\xi,\eta,\varphi(\xi,\eta)):|(\xi,\eta)|\leq 1\}
be the graph surface. Let

\mathcal{E}_{S}f(x,y,t)=\int_{|(\xi,\eta)|\leq 1}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t\varphi(\xi,\eta))}f(\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta

denote the corresponding Fourier extension operator.

We make the following definition:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth and
\delta>0
. We say that
S\subseteq[0,1]^{2}
is
(\phi,\delta)
-flat if

\sup_{u,v\in S}|\phi(u)-\phi(v)-\nabla\phi(v)(u-v)|\leq\delta.

Clearly, on flat sets there are no oscillations in
\mathcal{E}_{S}
.
For this reason, an estimate of
\mathcal{E}_{S}\hat{f}
on the scale
\delta^{-1}
for a function
f
with Fourier support contained in a
\delta
-flat set amounts to

\|\mathcal{E}_{S}\hat{f}\|_{L_{t}^{p}([0,\delta^{-1}],L^{q}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\sim\delta^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{q}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Indeed, we can write for fixed
(\xi_{*},\eta_{*})\in S
,

\phi(\xi,\eta)=\phi(\xi_{*},\eta_{*})+(\xi-\xi_{*},\eta-\eta_{*})\nabla\phi(\xi_{*},\eta_{*})+\psi(\xi,\eta,\xi_{*},\eta_{*}).

By a change of variables in
x,y
the linear terms in
\xi,\eta
can be eliminated and by the
\delta
-flat property,
\psi
does not cause oscillations.

Now, choosing a function which exhausts Bernstein’s inequality, we find

\|f\|_{L^{q}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\sim\text{meas}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(S)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

In conclusion, we have the estimate

\|\mathcal{E}_{S}\hat{f}\|_{L_{t}^{p}([0,\delta^{-1}],L^{q}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\sim\delta^{-\frac{1}{p}}\text{meas}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(S)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

When carrying out the argument on a different domain
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\in\{\mathbb{R}\times\lambda\mathbb{T},\;\lambda\mathbb{T}_{\gamma}^{2}\}
2
2

2

The scaling parameter
\lambda
will be linked to
\delta
.
, we obtain the estimate

\|\mathcal{E}_{S}\hat{f}\|_{L_{t}^{p}([0,\delta^{-1}],L^{q}_{xy}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}))}\sim\delta^{-\frac{1}{p}}\text{meas}_{\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}}(S)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda})}

where
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}
denotes the Pontryagin dual:

\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}^{*}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}/\lambda,&\quad\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{R}\times\lambda\mathbb{T},\\
(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\gamma})/\lambda,&\quad\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}=\lambda\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\gamma}.\end{cases}

Suppose we have an
\ell^{2}
-decoupling estimate into essentially disjoint
\delta
-flat sets:

\|\mathcal{E}_{S}\hat{f}\|_{L_{t,x,y}^{p}(B_{\delta^{-1}})}\lesssim C(\delta)\big(\sum_{\theta:(\phi,\delta)-\text{flat}}\|\mathcal{E}_{S}\hat{f}_{\theta}\|_{L_{t,x,y}^{p}(w_{B_{\delta^{-1}}})}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

With the above estimate at hand, we obtain Strichartz estimates:

\|\mathcal{E}_{S}\hat{f}\|_{L_{t,x,y}^{p}(B_{\delta^{-1}})}\lesssim C(\delta)\delta^{-\frac{1}{p}}M(S)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}O(S)\|f\|_{L^{2}},

M(S)
denotes the maximum of the
\mathbb{D}^{*}
-measure of
S
, and
O(S)
denotes the maximum overlap of the
\theta
-sets. Note that the decoupling estimates strictly speaking apply only to the oscillatory integral in case
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For other domains, this can be recovered by approximating the exponential sum with an oscillatory integral; see
[
8
]
and for more details
[
53
, Section 2.2]
.

Recently, Guth–Maldague–Oh
[
20
]
proved the following
\ell^{2}
-decoupling result for smooth hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We formulate the result for functions
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
. Then, for
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
a rectangle,
f_{S}
denotes a smoothed Fourier projection to
S\times\mathbb{R}
. We have the following result:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a suffficiently large number
A
depending on
\varepsilon
and
\phi
satisfying the following.

For any
\delta>0
there exists a collection
\mathcal{S}_{\delta}
of finitely overlapping parallelograms
S
such that

(1)

the overlapping number is
\mathcal{O}(\log\delta^{-1})
in the sense that

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}}\chi_{S}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\phi}\log(\delta^{-1}),

(2)

S
is
(\phi,A\delta)
-flat,

(3)

For
2\leq p\leq 4
we have

\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\phi}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\delta}}\|f_{S}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for any function
f
with
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
.

To illustrate this approach to Strichartz estimates, we first turn to the simpler case of the hyperbolic Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
. Let
\varphi(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\xi_{1}^{2}-\xi_{2}^{2}
.

We have the following Strichartz estimate, which is sharp up to endpoints:

Theorem 3.3
.

The following estimate holds for
s>0
:

(8)

\|e^{it(\partial_{1}^{2}-\partial_{2}^{2})}f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1];\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}.

The estimate can be proved using elementary counting arguments reminiscent of the arguments in
[
55
]
; see
[
3
]
. Here we give a different proof by decoupling into flat sets.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
via decoupling into flat sets.

For frequencies of size
N
, after rescaling, we apply parabolic rescaling
(\xi,\eta)\to(\xi,\eta)/N
,
(x,y)\to N(x,y)
, and
t\to N^{2}t
. We obtain

\begin{split}&\quad\|P_{N}e^{it(\partial_{1}^{2}-\partial_{2}^{2})}f\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})}\\
&=N^{-6}\|\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}/N}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t(\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}))}\hat{f}(N\xi,N\eta)d\xi\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,N^{2}]\times N^{2}\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{4}.\end{split}

Now we change to continuous approximation to be in the position to apply the decoupling result:

\big\|\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}/N}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t(\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}))}\hat{f}(N\xi,N\eta)\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}(B_{2+1}(0,N^{2}))}\lesssim\big\|\mathcal{E}_{\Box}\tilde{f}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}(w_{2+1}(0,N^{2}))}

with

\mathcal{E}_{\Box}\tilde{f}=\int_{|(\xi,\eta)|\leq 1}e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t(\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}))}\tilde{f}(\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta.

Let
\delta=N^{-2}
. Invoking Theorem
3.2
(see also
[
20
, Theorem 2.2]
for the construction of the set
\Theta_{\delta}
) we find

\|\mathcal{E}_{\Box}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}(w_{B_{2+1}}(0,\delta^{-1}))}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}}\|\mathcal{E}_{\Box}\tilde{f}_{\theta}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}(w_{B_{2+1}(0,\delta^{-1})})}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

with
\theta
being
\delta
-flat rectangles of dimensions
\ell_{1}\times\ell_{2}
in the null directions of the hyperboloid. The null directions are given by

\mathbf{n}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{n}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}

as for the Hessian of
\phi(\xi,\eta)=\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}
we have

\mathbf{n}_{i}^{t}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{n}_{i}=0.

Now, for
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in\theta
, consider
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})+\ell_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\ell_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\theta
and we obtain by Taylor expansion:

(9)

\begin{split}&\quad\phi((\xi_{0},\eta_{0})+\ell_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\ell_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2})\\
&=\phi(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})+\langle\ell_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\ell_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2},\nabla\phi(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\ell_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\ell_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2},\partial^{2}\phi(\ell_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\ell_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2})\rangle\\
&=\phi(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})+\langle\ell_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+\ell_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2},\nabla\phi(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\rangle+\ell_{1}\ell_{2}.\end{split}

We obtain from the flatness condition
|\ell_{1}\ell_{2}|\leq\delta
. Let
\ell_{\max}=\max(|\ell_{1}|,|\ell_{2}|)
,
\ell_{\min}=\min(|\ell_{1}|,|\ell_{2}|)
.
We reverse the continuous approximation and the scaling to find the discrete decoupling inequality:

\big\|e^{it(\partial_{1}^{2}-\partial_{2}^{2})}P_{N}f\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1];\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\delta^{-\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}}\big\|e^{it(\partial_{1}^{2}-\partial_{2}^{2})}P_{N\theta}f\big\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1];\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

with
N\theta
denoting the
N
-dilation of
\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}
. Following the general argument from the previous subsection we can integrate out the time evolution and the final ingredient is an estimate of the product measure
\text{meas}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}}(N\theta)
. Write
N\theta\cap(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z})=\bigcup_{\eta\in\pi_{\eta}(N\theta)}I_{\eta}
. We have

\#\,\{\,\eta\in\pi_{\eta}(N\theta)\,\}\,\lesssim\langle N\ell_{\max}\rangle,\quad|I_{\eta}|\lesssim N\ell_{\min},

and consequently
\text{meas}_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}}(N\theta)\lesssim 1
. With this, the proof is concluded by Bernstein’s inequality

\|P_{N\theta}f\|_{L^{4}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|P_{N\theta}f\|_{L^{2}_{xy}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}

and logarithmic overlap of
\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}
.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

On the domain
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{T}^{2}
the flat sets remain the same, but the measure changes. For
A^{\prime}=\{(\eta,\eta)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\eta\in[N/2,N]\cap\mathbb{Z}\}

we find
\text{meas}_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(A^{\prime})\sim N
, which matches the
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimate with derivative loss.

3.2.
Decoupling and Strichartz estimates for generalized KP-II dispersion relation

In this section we apply the general argument outlined in the preceding section to fractional KP-II equations.
We note that the dispersion relation

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=\xi|\xi|^{\alpha}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi}

is hyperbolic with principal curvatures having modulus essentially
1
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\in[1,2]
.
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}=\{(\xi,\eta,\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)):\xi\sim 1,\,|\eta|\lesssim 1\}
is a non-elliptic hypersurface with principle curvatures
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
satisyfing

|\lambda_{1}|\sim|\lambda_{2}|\sim 1,\text{ and }\text{sgn}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})=-1.

Proof.

We compute

\partial^{2}\omega_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}(\alpha+1)\alpha\xi^{\alpha-1}-2\eta^{2}/\xi^{3}&2\eta/\xi^{2}\\
2\eta/\xi^{2}&-2/\xi\end{pmatrix}.

Clearly, it holds

\det(\partial^{2}\omega_{\alpha})=-2(\alpha+1)\alpha\xi^{\alpha-2}\Rightarrow\det(\partial^{2}\omega_{\alpha})\sim-1.

Moreover,
|\text{tr}(\partial^{2}\omega_{\alpha})|\lesssim 1
. This shows that the eigenvalues of
\partial^{2}\omega_{\alpha}
are of opposite sign and have modulus comparable to
1
.
∎

By the above considerations we need to analyze the
\delta
-flat sets for

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}=\{(\xi,\eta,\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)):|\xi|\sim 1,\;|\eta|\lesssim 1\}.

The case of
\alpha=2
was detailed in
[
27
]
:

Proposition 3.6

(
[
27
, Proposition 3.4, Remark 3.5]
)
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a
\delta
-flat set for
\mathcal{M}_{2}
. Then
A
is contained in a
\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}
-ball. Moreover, for
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in A
, we have that
|\eta-\eta_{0}|\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
for any
(\xi_{0},\eta)\in A
.

Like in case of the KP-II dispersion relation we show the following:

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\alpha\in[1,2]
and
\theta
a
\delta
-flat rectangle for
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
. We have the following properties:

(A)

\theta
is contained in a
\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}
-ball.

(B)

For any
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0}),(\xi_{0},\eta)\in\theta
it holds
|\eta-\eta_{0}|\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in\theta
and consider a curve

\gamma(t)=(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})+t(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})

with
|(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})|=1
. For (A) it suffices to show that

(10)

\omega_{\alpha}(\gamma(t))=a_{0}+a_{1}t+a_{2}t^{2}+a_{3}t^{3}+E(t)

with
|a_{2}|+|a_{3}|\gtrsim 1
and
|E(t)|\leq ct^{4}
. We have

(11)

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime},\eta_{0}+t\eta^{\prime})=(\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}-\frac{(\eta_{0}+t\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime}}.

First, we handle the simple case
|\xi^{\prime}|\ll 1
, hence
|\eta^{\prime}|\gtrsim 1
. We find

\begin{split}(\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}-\frac{(\eta_{0}+t\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime}}&=\xi_{0}^{\alpha+1}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{\prime})-\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}+2t\eta_{0}\eta^{\prime}+t^{2}(\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\xi_{0}}(1+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{\prime}))\\
&=\xi_{0}^{\alpha+1}-\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{\xi_{0}}+\frac{2t\eta_{0}}{\xi_{0}}-\frac{t^{2}(\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\xi_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{\prime}).\end{split}

This gives
a_{2}=-\frac{(\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\xi_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\xi^{\prime})
and hence
|a_{2}|\gtrsim 1
. Moreover, this shows that
\theta
is extended in
\eta
-direction with length
\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which yields (B).

We turn to the case
|\xi^{\prime}|\gtrsim 1
. We can now apply the Galilean invariance:

(12)

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta+A\xi)=\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)-2A\eta+A^{2}\xi

with
\xi=\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime}
,
\eta=\eta_{0}+t\eta^{\prime}
,
A=-\eta^{\prime}/\xi^{\prime}
. It follows

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta+A\xi)+2A\eta-A^{2}\xi=\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta-\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\xi^{\prime}}\xi)+\text{Lin}(t).

The linear term can be disregarded. Let
\bar{\eta}=\eta_{0}-\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\xi^{\prime}}\xi_{0}
. We find

\begin{split}(\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}&=\xi_{0}^{\alpha+1}+(\alpha+1)t\xi^{\prime}\xi_{0}^{\alpha}+(\alpha+1)\alpha\xi_{0}^{\alpha-1}\frac{(t\xi^{\prime})^{2}}{2}\\
&\quad+(\alpha+1)\alpha(\alpha-1)\xi_{0}^{\alpha-2}\frac{(t\xi^{\prime})^{3}}{3!}+\mathcal{O}(t^{4})\end{split}

and

\frac{\bar{\eta}^{2}}{\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime}}=\frac{\bar{\eta}^{2}}{\xi_{0}}(1-\frac{t\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{0}}+\frac{t^{2}(\xi^{\prime})^{2}}{\xi_{0}^{2}}-\frac{t^{3}(\xi^{\prime})^{3}}{\xi_{0}^{3}}+\mathcal{O}(t^{4})).

Taking the above identities together, we find

\begin{split}\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta-\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\xi^{\prime}}\xi)&=\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\bar{\eta})=(\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}-\frac{\bar{\eta}^{2}}{\xi_{0}+t\xi^{\prime}}\\
\\
&=\xi_{0}^{\alpha+1}+\text{Lin}(t)+t^{2}\big(\frac{(\alpha+1)\alpha\xi_{0}^{\alpha-1}}{2}-\frac{\bar{\eta}^{2}}{\xi_{0}^{3}}\big)\\
&\quad+t^{3}(\xi^{\prime})^{3}\big(\frac{(\alpha+1)\alpha(\alpha-1)\xi_{0}^{\alpha-2}}{3!}+\frac{\bar{\eta}^{2}}{\xi_{0}^{4}}\big)+\mathcal{O}(t^{4}).\end{split}

We see that
a_{2}
can vanish in the case
|\bar{\eta}|\sim 1
, which necessitates
|\eta_{0}|\sim 1
since
\xi_{0}\sim 1
and
|\eta^{\prime}/\xi^{\prime}|\ll 1
. For (A) it suffices to observe that
|a_{3}|\gtrsim 1
, which yields a direction
(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
,
|\xi^{\prime}|\gtrsim 1
, into which
\theta
is extended with length
\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}
. The proof is complete.

∎

To obtain a more precise estimate for the measure of
\delta
-flat sets, we recall that these take the form of parallelograms and we observe that in case of normalized derivatives these have area
\sim\delta
as follows basically from the same argument as in (
9
). Indeed, in the case of perturbed hyperboloids the
\delta
-flat sets
S
constructed in
[
20
, Section 2.1]
are comparable to rectangles with one side pointing into a null direction.

Note that in the more general case of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
, we have indeed uniformly bounded derivatives in the region
\{\xi\sim 1,\;|\eta|\lesssim 1\}
. On the other hand, for
\alpha=2
we find
\delta=N^{-3}
and a flat set with area larger than
\delta^{1-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
would give a counterexample to the Strichartz estimate on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
without derivative loss. Recall the following due to Saut
[
52
]
:

(13)

\|S_{\alpha}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

The reason is that after anisotropic dilation
\xi\to N\xi
,
\eta\to N^{2}\eta
(see below), the area of the rescaled flat set
\theta_{N,N^{2}}
would be
\gtrsim N^{\varepsilon}
. We could take
\hat{f}=\chi_{\theta_{N,N^{2}}}
and since there is no time oscillation, we obtain from sharpness of Bernstein’s inequality

\|S_{\alpha}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{2})}\sim\|f\|_{L^{4}_{x,y}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\sim N^{\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.

This clearly contradicts (
13
).

The case of perturbed hyperboloids is recovered for
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
by mild anisotropic dilation and rotation as pointed out in Lemma
3.5
. Hence, the flat sets for
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
can be chosen as parallelograms with angle
\gtrsim 1
and area
\sim\delta
. That having been said, we have the following characterization for
\delta
-flat sets:

Proposition 3.8
.

Up to Galilean transform the
\delta
-flat parallelograms for
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
are contained in rectangles of size
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
, which are extended by length
\ell_{i}
into
e_{i}
-direction, and satisfy the bounds
\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\ell_{1}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}
,
\ell_{2}=\delta\ell_{1}^{-1}
.

Proof.

The null directions
(\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
are the ones annihilating the quadratic term in (

) and for this reason satisfy
|\xi^{\prime}|\gtrsim 1
. So, carrying out the Galilean transform, these can be transformed to the
e_{1}
-direction. Consequently, the first null direction of the transformed parallelogram is
e_{1}
and the length satisfies
\ell_{1}\lesssim\delta^{\frac{1}{3}}
.

In the
e_{2}
-direction, by Proposition
3.7
we know that the length is at most
\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
. This points out that the extension
\ell_{2}
in the second direction is at most
\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
. By the area constraint
\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\lesssim\delta
, we have for some fixed

K\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\text{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})\subseteq\{(\xi,\eta,\tau):\xi\in I_{i},\;|\xi|\sim N_{i},\;|\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)|\leq L_{i}\}
. Let
L_{\max}=\max(L_{1},L_{2})
,
L_{\min}=\min(L_{1},L_{2})
. Furthermore, we assume that

(20)

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\geq D>0

for
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\in\text{supp}_{\xi,\eta}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})
.
Then the following estimate holds:

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

This is the analog of
[
27
, Proposition 4.2]
for functions
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
∎

Next, we record a consequence of second-order transversality, which holds independently of (
20
). This will be very useful to estimate some limiting cases.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K,N_{i},L_{i},I_{i},f_{i}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
i=1,2
be like in Lemma
4.1
. Then the following estimate holds:

(21)

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}N_{\min}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

Proof.

This the analog of
[
27
, Proposition 4.3]
.
∎

In the next proposition we combine the two estimates with almost-orthogonal decompositions in case of small transversality.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
N_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
L_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
i=1,2
, and
N_{2}\ll N_{1}
. Let
f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z})
with
\text{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})\subseteq D_{N_{i},L_{i}}
,
i=1,2
. Suppose that

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\leq D^{*}

for
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\in\text{supp}_{\xi,\eta}(f_{i})
,
i=1,2
. Then the following estimate holds:

(22)

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim\log(D^{*})N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

First, suppose that we are in the case of large modulation:
L_{\max}\gtrsim N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}
. In this case the estimate is immediate from Lemma
4.2
.

In the following we suppose that
L_{\max}\lesssim N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}
. We carry out a Whitney decomposition

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\leq\sum_{\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq D\leq D^{*}}\sum_{J_{1}\sim_{D}J_{2}}\|f^{J_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{J_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

Above
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
denote intervals of length
D
with
\text{dist}(J_{1},J_{2})\sim D
such that
\eta_{i}/\xi_{i}\in J_{i}
for
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\in\text{supp}_{\xi,\eta}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})
.

We note that by convolution constraint we have for
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\tau_{i}),\,(\xi_{i+2},\eta_{i+2},\tau_{i+2})\in\text{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})
,
i=1,2
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}&=\xi_{3}+\xi_{4},\\
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}&=\eta_{3}+\eta_{4},\\
\xi_{1}^{\alpha+1}-\frac{\eta_{1}^{2}}{\xi_{1}}+\xi_{2}^{\alpha+1}-\frac{\eta_{2}^{2}}{\xi_{2}}&=\xi_{3}^{\alpha+1}-\frac{\eta_{3}^{2}}{\xi_{3}}+\xi_{4}^{\alpha+1}-\frac{\eta_{4}^{2}}{\xi_{4}}+\mathcal{O}(L_{\max}).\end{array}\right.

We square the second line, divide by the first line and subtract the resulting expression from the third line to find:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}&=\xi_{3}+\xi_{4},\\
\xi_{1}^{\alpha+1}+\xi_{2}^{\alpha+1}-\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}&=\xi_{3}^{\alpha+1}+\xi_{4}^{\alpha+1}-\frac{(\eta_{3}\xi_{4}-\eta_{4}\xi_{3})^{2}}{\xi_{3}\xi_{4}(\xi_{3}+\xi_{4})}+\mathcal{O}(L_{\max})\end{array}\right.

We rescale the resulting expression to unit frequencies
\xi_{i}\to\xi_{i}/N_{1}
,
\eta_{i}\to\eta_{i}/N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}
, noting that

\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}\sim D^{2}N_{2}.

This gives

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\xi_{1}^{\prime}+\xi_{2}^{\prime}&=\xi_{3}^{\prime}+\xi_{4}^{\prime},\\
(\xi_{1}^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}+(\xi_{2}^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}&=(\xi_{3}^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}+(\xi_{4}^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}+\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}+\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}\big).\end{array}\right.

We use a variant of the Córdoba–Fefferman square function estimate to obtain almost orthogonal decomposition of
\xi_{i}^{\prime}
into intervals of length
\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}+\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}
, see e.g.
[
12
, Chapter 2]
.

In the limiting case,
D=\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we find an almost orthogonal decomposition of
\xi_{i}^{\prime}
into intervals of length
L_{\max}/N_{1}^{\alpha+1}
. So, after inverting the scaling, we find that the
\xi_{i}
are decomposed into intervals
\theta_{i}
of length
\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}^{\alpha}}
. We write

\sum_{J_{1}\sim_{D}J_{2}}\|f^{J_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{J_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J_{1}\sim_{D}J_{2},\\
\theta_{1}\sim\theta_{2}\end{subarray}}\|f^{J_{1},\theta_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{J_{2},\theta_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

This expression is estimated by Lemma
4.2
:

\|f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}^{J_{1},\theta_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}^{J_{2},\theta_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}N_{2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}^{J_{i},\theta_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}.

The sum over
J_{i}
and
\theta_{i}
can be carried out without loss by almost orthogonality. By the assumption
L_{\max}\lesssim N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}
this estimate suffices for (
22
).

We turn to
\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<D\leq N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
: In this case the identity

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\xi_{1}^{\prime}+\xi_{2}^{\prime}&=\xi_{3}^{\prime}+\xi_{4}^{\prime},\\
(\xi_{1}^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}+(\xi_{2}^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}&=(\xi_{3}^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}+(\xi_{4}^{\prime})^{\alpha+1}+\mathcal{O}\big(\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}\big)\end{array}\right.

yields an almost orthogonal decomposition of
\xi_{i}^{\prime}
into intervals of length
\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}
and after rescaling, we find a decomposition of the
\xi_{i}
into intervals
\theta_{i}
of length
\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{\alpha}}
.
We obtain from the decomposition and employing Lemma
4.1
:

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<D\leq N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\sum_{J_{1}\sim_{D}J_{2}}\|f^{J_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{J_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\sum_{\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<D\leq N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J_{1}\sim_{D}J_{2},\\
\theta_{1}\sim\theta_{2}\end{subarray}}\|f^{J_{1},\theta_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{J_{2},\theta_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<D\leq N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J_{1}\sim_{D}J_{2},\\
\theta_{1}\sim\theta_{2}\end{subarray}}\big(\frac{D^{2}N_{2}}{N_{1}^{\alpha}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f^{J_{i},\theta_{i}}_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.\end{split}

Finally, for
D\in[N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},D^{*}]
we carry out a simple almost orthogonal decomposition of
f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}
into
\xi
-intervals of length
N_{2}
by convolution constraint. Then, we can apply Lemma
4.1
to find

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{D\in[N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},D^{*}]}\sum_{J_{1}\sim_{D}J_{2}}\|f^{J_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{J_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{D\in[N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},D^{*}]}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/D\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}\\
&\lesssim N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\log(D^{*})L_{\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{\max}/N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Trilinear convolution estimates

The purpose of this section is to obtain trilinear convolution estimates:

(23)

\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau\leq C(N_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i,+}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}

with
\text{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}})\subseteq D_{\alpha,N_{i},\leq L_{i}}
,
\alpha<2
. This corresponds to product estimates for solutions and differences of solutions, localized in
x
-frequency and in time.
In the following we suppose that
N_{1}\sim N_{3}\gtrsim N_{2}
. Corresponding to a time localization adjusted to the high frequencies we suppose that
L_{1},L_{3}\gtrsim N_{\max,+}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}
. The above expression will be summable to the short-time norm following Remark
2.6
.

For the low frequency more care is required. Since we aim to use the low modulation weight, we suppose that
L_{2}\gtrsim N_{\max,+}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}\wedge N_{\min,+}^{\alpha+1}
.

The analysis splits into several cases:

(1)

Low
\times
Low
\rightarrow
Low
-interaction:
N_{\max}\lesssim 1
. This case is readily estimated by using a Strichartz estimate based on second order transversality.

(2)

High
\times
High
\rightarrow
High
-interaction:
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\gg 1
. In the resonant case we can apply the improved
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates, and so do we for some non-resonant cases. When the resonance function becomes large enough, we can conclude favorable estimates using a bilinear Strichartz estimate.

(3)

High
\times
Low
\rightarrow
High
-interaction:
N_{1}\sim N_{3}\gg N_{2}
.

5.1.
Overview of the nonlinear interpolation argument

To obtain (
23
), we take advantage of the high modulation
L_{\max}
and the corresponding weight
L_{\max}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
by estimating the function with high modulation in
L^{2}
.

One of the key cases to understand is the
High
\times
High
\rightarrow
High
-interaction in the resonant case. We shall see that the
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates yield estimates in
L^{2}
up to the critical
\alpha=4/3
. This means that this estimate is very favorable. We perturb the argument to still obtain favorable estimates in the non-resonant case
N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}\ll L_{\max}\lesssim M^{*}
and in the
High
\times
Low
\rightarrow
High
-interaction:
N_{1}^{1-\gamma}\lesssim N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}
. In case
N_{2}\ll N_{1}^{1-\gamma}
or
L_{\max}\gg M^{*}
, the short-time bilinear Strichartz estimates are sufficient.

We turn to the estimate of the resonant case in the
High
\times
High
\rightarrow
High
-interaction. By symmetry we can assume that
L_{3}\sim L_{\max}
. In the resonant case, note that the resonance relation gives

\frac{|\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1}|^{2}}{N^{3}}\lesssim L_{\max}\sim N^{\alpha+1}.

Consequently,

(24)

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\lesssim N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

In essence, this yields after an almost orthogonal decomposition a localization

\big|\frac{\eta_{i}}{\xi_{i}}-A\big|\lesssim N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

This allows us to use
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates proved in Proposition
3.9
, which read

\|S_{\alpha}(t)f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}([0,1];\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{2-\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}}

for
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\xi|\sim N,\,\big|\frac{\eta}{\xi}-A\big|\lesssim N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\}
.

By Hölder’s inequality and an application of the transfer principle we find

(25)

\begin{split}\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau&\leq\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}^{-1}[f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}]\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}}\\
&\lesssim N^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}(N^{\frac{2-\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2},\end{split}

which indeed gives a short-time nonlinear estimate in
L^{2}
for
\alpha>\frac{4}{3}
.

In the non-resonant case, i.e.,
L_{\max}\gg N^{\alpha+1}
(
24
) becomes

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\lesssim\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For
L_{\max}
sufficiently close to
N^{\alpha+1}
, we can follow along the lines of (
25
) and apply the Strichartz estimates from Proposition
3.12
instead of Proposition
3.9
. But note that applying Proposition
3.12
becomes less and less favorable for
L_{\max}
becoming larger and larger. We impose a threshold
L_{\max}\ll M^{*}
, which still gives a favorable estimate.

For
L_{\max}\gtrsim M^{*}
, we instead use a bilinear Strichartz estimate provided by Lemma
4.1
, noting that we have

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\gtrsim N_{\max}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

This gives

\begin{split}&\quad\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau\\
&\lesssim(M^{*})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\|1_{D_{N_{3},L_{3}}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim(M^{*})^{-\frac{1}{2}}L^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{12,\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{12,\max}/N^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

This outlines the key interpolation argument for the
High
\times
High
\rightarrow
High
-interaction.

We turn to the
High
\times
Low
\rightarrow
High
-interaction. In case there is a frequency significantly lower than the remaining frequencies, say
N_{2}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{3}
, we need to distinguish between the high frequency being at high modulation or the low frequency being at high modulation.

First, we consider the case of a high frequency being at high modulation, say
L_{3}=L_{\max}
. In the resonant case we have

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Then, we can estimate (
23
) by Hölder’s inequality and a refined bilinear Strichartz estimate provided by Proposition
4.3
:

\begin{split}&\quad\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau\\
&\leq\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}}\|1_{D_{N,L}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim(N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(N_{1})L_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{12,\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{12,\max}/N_{1}^{\alpha/2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

By the minimum modulation localization
L_{i}\gtrsim N_{i}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}
for
i=1,3
, that is to say our choice of the frequency-dependent time localization, we can continue the above as

\lesssim N_{1}^{-1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.

The short-time bilinear estimate becomes less favorable for the nonlinear analysis as
N_{2}\to N_{1}
. The key point to show local well-posedness as low as in
L^{2}
is to interpolate with
L^{4}
-estimates when
N_{1}^{1-\gamma}\lesssim N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}
for
\gamma
small enough. In the present implementation we will use
\gamma=\frac{1}{8}
to simplify some of the resulting expressions. It might well be the case that the local well-posedness result can be refined for a different choice of
\gamma
.

Indeed, we find in the resonant case

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},

which implies that
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}[f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}]
can
be estimated with Proposition
3.9
after additional almost orthogonal decomposition

\big|\frac{\eta_{i}}{\xi_{i}}-A\big|\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

For the estimate of
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}[f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}]
note the following: an application of Proposition
3.12
is not too lossy since we have for the resonance gain
(N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ll N_{1}^{-1}
in case
\alpha\to 2
and
\gamma\to 0
.

In the non-resonant case, we intend to follow along the above lines, where

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\lesssim\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This requires to estimate
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}[f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}]
for
i=1,2
with the lossier estimate provided by Proposition
3.12
. Still the resonance gain
L_{\max}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
outweighs the loss from the linear Strichartz estimates imposing a threshold
L_{\max}\lesssim M^{*}
. In case
L_{\max}\gtrsim M^{*}
we can instead use a bilinear Strichartz estimate provided by Lemma
4.1
to find

\begin{split}\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})&f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\leq\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}}\|1_{D_{N,L}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim(M^{*})^{-\frac{1}{2}}L_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}L_{12,\min}^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle L_{12,\max}/N_{1}^{\alpha/2}\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

We will choose
M^{*}=N_{1}^{\frac{9}{4}}N_{2}
to obtain favorable estimates for
\alpha\in(\alpha_{0},2)
.

It remains to shed light on the case
N_{2}\ll N_{1}\sim N_{3}
with the low frequency carrying the high modulation. For the sake of exposition we suppose that
N_{2}\gtrsim 1
. The case of very low frequencies requires possibly a different, but simpler argument.

We would like to use a similar interpolation argument between linear and bilinear Strichartz estimates. A notable change is that

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{3}}{\xi_{3}}\big|\lesssim\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Consequently, in the resonant case
L_{\max}\sim N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}
, we always have after almost orthogonal decomposition

\big|\frac{\eta_{i}}{\xi_{i}}-A\big|\ll N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

This enables us to apply the almost sharp linear Strichartz estimates from Proposition
3.9
to estimate

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}[f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}]\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N_{i}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}

for
i=1,3
. We obtain taking into account the modulation weight

\begin{split}&\quad\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau\\
&\leq\|f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\prod_{i=1,3}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}[f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}]\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}}\\
&\leq(N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{N_{2}}{N_{1}}\big)^{\alpha+1}(N_{1}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon})^{2}\prod_{i=1,3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

Consequently, although the transversality between the high frequencies is decreased, which leads to potentially worse bilinear Strichartz estimates, the sharp
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimates together with the modulation weight on the low frequency come to rescue.

In the non-resonant case, we can choose a different threshold
L_{\max}\ll M^{**}
,
M^{**}\gg M^{*}
for which the linear Strichartz estimates still gives us the sharp estimate. With
M^{**}
being sufficiently high, the remaining case
L_{\max}\gtrsim M^{**}
can be estimated via a simple bilinear Strichartz estimate from Lemma
4.2
. We turn to the implementation.

5.2.
Proofs of the trilinear convolution estimates

Low
\times
Low
\rightarrow
Low-interaction:

N_{\max}\lesssim 1
. In this case we obtain a sufficient estimate by invoking Lemma
4.2
.

High
\times
High
\rightarrow
High-interaction:

N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\gg 1
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2)
,
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\gg 1
, and
L_{i}\gtrsim N_{\max}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}
. Then the estimate (

C(N)=N_{1}^{-\frac{2}{14}-\frac{9\alpha}{14}+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Strongly non-resonant case:

L_{\max}\sim L_{\text{med}}\gtrsim N_{1}^{\alpha+1}

L_{\max}\sim L_{3}\sim L_{2}

&\lesssim N_{1}^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

The estimates established in the
resonant and non-resonant cases
are inferior.

Resonant case:

L_{\max}\sim N_{1}^{\alpha+1}
. Suppose that
L_{3}\sim L_{\max}
by symmetry.
By almost orthogonality we can suppose that

\big|\frac{\eta_{i}}{\xi_{i}}-A\big|\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

In this case two

-Strichartz estimates from Proposition

give

(27)

\begin{split}&\quad\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau\\
&\lesssim L_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\|f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}[f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}]\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}(N_{1}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N_{1}^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}+2\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

Non-resonant case:

L_{\max}\sim M\gg N^{\alpha+1}
,
L_{\max}\gg L_{\text{med}}
. Suppose that
L_{3}\sim L_{\max}
. We find by the resonance relation:

\big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\big|\sim\big(\frac{M}{N_{1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We can carry out an almost orthogonal decomposition to write

\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}=\sum_{A}\int(f^{A}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{A}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f^{A}_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}

such that

\text{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}^{A})\subseteq\{(\tau,\xi,\eta):\big|\frac{\eta}{\xi}-A\big|\lesssim\big(\frac{L_{\max}}{N_{1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\},\quad i=1,2,3.

Note that the identity for
i=1,2
implies the third one by the triangle inequality. We have to carry out a further partition:

f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}^{A}=\sum_{k_{i}}f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}^{A,k_{i}}

such that

\text{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}^{A_{i},k_{i}})\subseteq\{(\tau,\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\big|\frac{\eta}{\xi}-A\big|\in[k_{i}N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1},(k_{i}+1)N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}]\}.

The maximal number of
k_{i}
is bounded by
k^{*}=\big(\frac{M}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. And note that it suffices to impose this condition for one
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. After
k_{i}
being fixed, for any
k_{j}
,
j\neq i
there are only finitely many
k_{m}
with
i\neq m\neq j
such that

\int(f^{A,k_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{A,k_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f^{A,k_{3}}_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}\neq 0.

Consequently, after applying a Galilean transform for fixed
A
we have that

|\eta_{i}^{\prime}|\in[k_{i}N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1},(k_{i}+1)N_{i}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}]

for
k_{i}\lesssim\big(\frac{M}{N_{1}^{\alpha+1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. (Note that in general
|k_{1}-k_{2}|\gg 1
).

We intend to apply Proposition
3.12
. This requires a further subdivision of the
\xi
-support into
(M/N_{1}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{2}}
-subintervals: We let

\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}=\sum_{A,k_{i},\ell_{i}}(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}^{A,k_{1},\ell_{1}}*f^{A,k_{2},\ell_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f^{A,k_{3},\ell_{3}}_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}

with the summation in
A
being lossless and the summation in
k_{i},\ell_{i}
incuring a total loss of
M/N_{1}^{\alpha+1}
by two applications of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. We summarize

\text{supp}(f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}^{A,k_{i},\ell_{i}})\subseteq\{(\tau,\xi,\eta):\big|\frac{\eta}{\xi}-A\big|\in[k_{i}N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}},(k_{i}+1)N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}],\;|\xi|\in I_{\ell_{i}}\}

with
|I_{\ell_{i}}|\sim N_{1}/k^{*}

First note the estimate as a consequence of Hölder’s inequality and the
L^{4}
-Strichartz estimate from Proposition
3.12
:

\|f^{A,k_{1},\ell_{1}}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}}^{A,k_{2},\ell_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(N_{1}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon})^{2}(M^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}})^{\frac{1}{6}}\prod_{i=1}^{2}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}^{A,k_{i},\ell_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

Together with the modulation size of
f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}
and carrying out the sums over
k_{i}
and
\ell_{i}
we find

(28)

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{k_{i},\ell_{i},A}\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}^{A,k_{1},\ell_{1}}*f^{A,k_{2},\ell_{2}}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}^{A,k,\ell}d\xi(d\eta_{1})d\tau\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}M^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}(N_{1}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon})^{2}M^{\frac{1}{12}}N_{1}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{12}}\\
&\quad\quad\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}M^{\frac{1}{12}}N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{12}-\frac{5\alpha}{6}+2\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}.\end{split}

We obtain an alternative estimate observing that the transversality satisfies

In this case applying the bilinear Strichartz estimate provided by Lemma

&\lesssim M^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{4}}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{2}\\

In conclusion, taking the above estimates (

) to hold with

Note that this estimate is at strictly subcritical regularity. The proof is complete.

Estimates for differences of solutions

Next, we prove estimates at negative Sobolev regularity

. Let

,

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
T\in(0,1]
,
s\geq 0
,
\alpha\in(1.972,2)
,

and

,

Proof.

We follow the same arguments like in the previous subsection. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, using that
\|P_{N}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
is continuously differentiable in

, we find for
N\geq 1
:

\|P_{N}v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\|P_{N}v(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{N}(\tilde{P}_{M}v)\partial_{x}P_{N}(\tilde{P}_{M}v\,\tilde{P}_{M}(u_{1}+u_{2}))dxds.

We redenote
\tilde{P}_{M}v\to v
and
\tilde{P}_{M}u_{i}\to u_{i}
to ease notation. The nonlinearity is decomposed as a paraproduct:

(67)

\begin{split}P_{N}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))&=P_{N}(vP_{\ll N}(u_{1}+u_{2}))\\
&\quad+P_{N}((u_{1}+u_{2})P_{\ll N}v)+P_{N}(P_{\gtrsim N}vP_{\gtrsim N}(u_{1}+u_{2})).\end{split}

The first and third term can be estimated like previously as the derivative can be assigned to the low frequency. We turn to the estimate of the second term, in which case we cannot integrate by parts. This term amounts to an estimate of

\begin{split}N_{1}^{-16(2-\alpha)}N_{1}&\big|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{N_{1}}vP_{N_{2}}vP_{N_{3}}u_{i}dxds\big|\\
&\lesssim T^{\delta}C(\underline{N})\|P_{N}v\|_{F_{N}}\|P_{N_{2}}v\|_{F_{N_{2}}}\|P_{N_{3}}u_{i}\|_{F_{N_{3}}}\end{split}

with
N_{1}\sim N_{3}\gg N_{2}
for a suitable constant, after which the claim follows from dyadic summation.

Here the smoothing provided by the estimate at negative regularity comes to rescue. We add time localization, which incurs a factor of
TN_{1}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}
, change to Fourier variables and break the modulation size into dyadic regions
L_{i}\geq N_{1}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}
,
i=1,3
dictated by time localization. Moreover, we suppose
L_{2}\geq N_{1}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}\wedge N_{2,+}^{\alpha+1}
. This reduces to the trilinear convolution estimates from Section
5
:

\begin{split}&\quad\big|\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau\\
&\lesssim C^{\prime}(N)\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\tau}}.\end{split}

We estimate the contribution of
N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{7}{8}}
by (

\big|\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau\big|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+L_{i}/N_{i}^{\alpha+1})^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}.

Now adding time localization
N_{1}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}
, taking into account the negative regularity
N_{1}^{-16(2-\alpha)}
, taking into account the derivative loss, we find by the separation assumption
N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}^{\frac{7}{8}}
:

N_{1}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}N_{1}^{-16(2-\alpha)}\lesssim N_{1}^{-7(2-\alpha)}N_{1}^{-8(2-\alpha)}\lesssim N_{2}^{-8(2-\alpha)}N_{1}^{-8(2-\alpha)}.

Next, we consider the case of almost comparable frequencies:
N_{1}^{\frac{7}{8}}\lesssim N_{2}\lesssim N_{1}
. Here we use estimate (
31
) with
M^{*}=N_{1}^{\frac{9}{4}}N_{2}
. We check the contribution of
C_{21}
and
C_{22}
separately. We obtain the constant

following Remark
5.3
, taking into account derivative loss, time localization, and negative regularity:

\begin{split}&\quad N_{1}N_{1}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}N_{1}^{-16(2-\alpha)}N_{1}^{\frac{5}{16}+\frac{1}{6}-\frac{\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon}N_{2}^{-\frac{5}{24}-\frac{5\alpha}{8}+\varepsilon}\\
&\lesssim N_{1}^{-16(2-\alpha)}N_{1}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon+1+\frac{5}{16}+\frac{1}{6}-\frac{\alpha}{8}-\frac{35}{192}-\frac{35\alpha}{64}+\frac{7\varepsilon}{8}}.\end{split}

This is favorable for

(68)

\alpha\geq 1.972.

The second contribution is given by

C_{22}(N)=N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}{2}}(N_{1}^{\frac{9}{4}}N_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}=N_{1}^{-\frac{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{9}{8}}.

Subsuming the derivative loss, the negative Sobolev regularity and the time localization, we find

the ultimate estimate holding true for

We estimate the third term in (

\begin{split}&\quad N_{2}N_{2}^{-16(2-\alpha)}N_{1}^{2-\alpha+\varepsilon}\int(f_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi(d\eta)_{1}d\tau\\

for

8.
Conclusion of local well-posedness with short-time estimates

In
[
27
]
we have applied the Ionescu–Kenig–Tataru argument
[
30
]
to the KP-II equation in the periodic setting. Since this applies with only minor changes here, we shall be brief. Showing the existence of solutions in an appropriate scale of regularity requires further explanation.

The proof of local well-posedness is carried out in three steps:

1.

Establishing the existence of solutions and a priori estimates in
F^{s}(T)
for
s\geq 0
,

2.

Estimates for the solution to the difference equation at negative regularity,

3.

Conclusion of continuous dependence via frequency envelopes.

We remark that the proof of existence employs a priori estimates for a regularized equation. For this reason we do not separate the construction of solutions and the proof of a priori estimates.

The frequency-dependent time localization, as sketched in Section
2.3
, is given by

T=T(N)=N^{-(2-\alpha+\varepsilon)}.

The extra time localization
N^{-\varepsilon}
will be useful to overcome certain logarithmic divergences.

8.1.
Existence of solutions and global a priori estimates

We construct solutions to

(69)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u-\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}u+\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u&=u\partial_{x}u,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},\\
u(0)&=\phi\end{array}\right.

for
\alpha\in(1.94,2)
. This is more admissible than stated in Theorem
1.1
. Since we are exclusively analyzing solutions, which satisfy more symmetries than differences of solutions, we obtain a larger range.

The construction employs a Galerkin approximation and the Aubin–Lions compactness lemma (cf.
[
44
]
). The argument follows to a great extent the construction of solutions to KP-I equations in three dimensions (cf.
[
26
]
).

Recall the regularity scale
B^{s}
introduced in (
3
).
Clearly,
B^{1}\hookrightarrow H^{1}
and on bounded domains
D_{R}=(-R,R)\times\mathbb{T}
, the embedding
H^{1}(D_{R})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(D_{R})
is compact. This will allow us to construct global solutions in
C_{T}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
by compactness arguments.

We shall see below that this symbol behaves well with commutator arguments and the derivative nonlinearity. This is the main reason for not working directly in isotropic Sobolev spaces. We have the following:

Proposition 8.1
.

For any
s\geq 2
and
T>0
there is a mapping
S_{T}^{s}:B^{s}\to L_{T}^{\infty}B^{s}
with

S_{T}^{s}(\phi)\in C_{T}^{1}H^{-1}\cap C_{T}H^{1}\cap C_{T}B^{0}

and
S_{T}^{s}(\phi)
is the unique distributional solution to (
69
).

Consequently, there is a data-to-solution mapping
S_{T}^{\infty}:B^{\infty}\to C_{T}B^{\infty}
for any
T>0
. Moreover, for any
s\geq 0
, we have the global a priori estimates

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|S_{T}^{s}(\phi)\|_{H^{s,0}}\lesssim_{T,s}\|\phi\|_{H^{s,0}},

and for
s\geq 2
we have

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|S_{T}^{s}(\phi)\|_{B^{s}}\lesssim_{T,s}\|\phi\|_{B^{s}}.

Once the proposition is established, we can work with solutions in
C_{T}B^{\infty}
since
B^{\infty}\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
is dense. When the continuity in
H^{s,0}
is established, we can extend
S_{T}^{s}:B^{s}\to C([-T,T],B^{s})
to
S_{T}^{s}:H^{s,0}\to C([-T,T],H^{s,0})
.

Proof of Proposition
8.1
: Galerkin approximation.
We begin with considering the truncated equation (
55
). Recall that this is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u^{M}-\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}u^{M}+\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u^{M}&=\tilde{P}_{M}(\partial_{x}(\tilde{P}_{M}u)^{2}),\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},\\
u^{M}(0)&=\phi\in B^{s}.\end{array}\right.

Let
S_{\alpha}(t):B^{s}\to B^{s}
denote the unitary evolution generated by
L_{\alpha}
. We obtain the solution to (
55
) as fixed points of the integral equation:

u^{M}(t)=S_{\alpha}(t)\phi+\int_{0}^{t}S_{\alpha}(t-\tau)\tilde{P}_{M}(\partial_{x}(\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})ds.

By the Cauchy-Picard-Lipschitz theorem, we obtain a local solution
u^{M}\in C_{T}B^{s}
with
T=T(\|\phi\|_{B^{s}},M)
for
s\geq 0
. This is based on
\|S_{\alpha}(t)\tilde{P}_{M}\|_{B^{s}\to B^{s}}\leq C(t,M,s)<\infty
and

\|\tilde{P}_{M}(\partial_{x}(\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})\|_{B^{s}}\lesssim_{M,s}\|u^{M}\|^{2}_{B^{s}}.

However, the dependence on
M
is unfavorable for the construction of solutions. We shall obtain a priori estimates independent of
M
by invoking the short-time analysis from Sections
6
and
7
.

First, we obtain global bounds independent on
M
in the scale of anisotropic Sobolev spaces. Again, by Cauchy-Picard-Lipschitz we have as well solutions
u^{M}\in C_{T}H^{s,0}
for
T\leq T_{M}=T(\|\phi\|_{H^{s,0}},M)
. For
T\leq T_{M}
we have the set of estimates by virtue of Lemma
2.9
, Propositions
6.1
and
7.1
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u^{M}\|_{E^{s}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}\tilde{P}_{M}((\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}\tilde{P}_{M}((\tilde{P}_{M}u)^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}&\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{0}(T)}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)},\\
\|u^{M}\|^{2}_{E^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u^{M}(0)\|^{2}_{H^{s,0}}+T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{0}(T)}.\end{array}\right.

Above we moreover used the monotonicity of the norms
\|\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M}\|_{Z^{s}(T)}\leq\|u^{M}\|_{Z^{s}(T)}
for
Z\in\{F,\mathcal{N}\}
. This gives

(70)

\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\|u^{M}(0)\|_{H^{s,0}}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{0}(T)}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{0}(T)}.

Recall the limiting properties of the short-time spaces as
T\downarrow 0
(cf.
[
30
, p. 279]
):

\lim_{T\downarrow 0}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u^{M}(0)\|_{H^{s,0}}.

For
s=0
this gives for
T=T(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}})>0
small enough by a continuity argument

\|u^{M}\|_{F^{0}(T)}\lesssim\|u^{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}}.

By
L^{2}
-conservation of the truncated evolution (
55
) and the persistence property established in (
70
) this gives global bounds for
s\geq 0
:

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|u^{M}(t)\|_{H^{s,0}}\lesssim_{T,s}\|u^{M}(0)\|_{H^{s,0}}.

In the following we denote the short-time spaces with additional Sobolev weight
p(\xi,\eta)=1+\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|}
by
\bar{F}^{s}(T)
,
\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{s}(T)
, and
\bar{E}^{s}(T)
. We turn to establishing global a priori estimates with extra regularity in the
y
-variable.

Proposition 8.2
.

For
s\geq 1
the following estimates hold:

(71)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u^{M}\|_{\bar{F}^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u^{M}\|_{\bar{E}^{s}(T)}+\|\tilde{P}_{M}(\partial_{x}(\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})\|_{\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{s}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}\tilde{P}_{M}((\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})\|_{\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{s}(T)}&\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|_{\bar{F}^{s}(T)}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)},\\
\|u^{M}\|^{2}_{\bar{E}^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|\phi\|^{2}_{B^{s}}+T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|^{2}_{\bar{F}^{s}(T)}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}.\end{array}\right.

The short-time analysis actually yields improved estimates, but these are presently not required.

Proof of Proposition
8.2
.

1. Linear energy estimate with Sobolev weight:

The first estimate in (
71
) is obtained from applying Lemma
2.9
to
p(\nabla_{x}/i)u^{M}
.

2. Short-time bilinear estimate with Sobolev weight:

For the second estimate in (
71
) dominate

(72)

\begin{split}&\quad\|\partial_{x}\tilde{P}_{M}((\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})\|_{\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{s}(T)}\\
&\lesssim\|\tilde{P}_{M}(\partial_{x}(\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}+\|\langle D_{x}\rangle(\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})(\partial_{y}\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s-1}(T)}.\end{split}

As a consequence of Proposition
6.1
we can estimate the first term by

(73)

\|\tilde{P}_{M}(\partial_{x}(\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}\lesssim T^{\delta}\|\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}.

For
s\geq 1
, we obtain for the second term again by Proposition
6.1

(74)

\begin{split}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle(\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})(\partial_{y}\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s-1}(T)}&\lesssim\|\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M}\|_{F^{s-1}(T)}\|\partial_{y}\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M}\|_{F^{s-1}(T)}\\
&\lesssim\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}\|u^{M}\|_{\bar{F}^{s}(T)}.\end{split}

Combining (
72
)-(
74
) gives

\|\partial_{x}\tilde{P}_{M}((\tilde{P}_{M}u^{M})^{2})\|_{\bar{\mathcal{N}}^{s}(T)}\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|_{\bar{F}^{s}(T)}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}.

3. Short-time energy estimate with Sobolev weight:

We turn to the third estimate in (
71
). This will follow from revisiting some steps in the proof of Proposition
7.1
. Let
w=\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u^{M}
. We have

(75)

N^{2s}\|p(\nabla_{x}/i)P_{N}u^{M}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{2s}\|P_{N}u^{M}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+N^{2s}\|P_{N}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The estimate for the first term is immediate from Proposition
7.1
:

\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|P_{N}u^{M}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u^{M}\|_{H^{s,0}}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{0}(T)}.

We turn to the estimate of the second term in (
75
).
To this end, we note that
w
satisfies the following evolution equation:

\partial_{t}w-\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}w+\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}w=u^{M}\partial_{x}w,

and we have by the fundamental theorem of calculus:

\|P_{N}w(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|P_{N}w(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{N}w(s,x,y)P_{N}(u^{M}\partial_{x}w)dxdyds.

The application of the fundamental theorem is justified noting that

\|u^{M}\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\|u^{M}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\|u^{M}\|^{2}_{B^{2}}.

We use a paraproduct decomposition

(76)

P_{N}(u^{M}\partial_{x}w)=P_{N}(P_{\ll N}u^{M}\partial_{x}w)+P_{N}(u^{M}P_{\ll N}\partial_{x}w)+P_{N}(P_{\gg N}uP_{\gg N}\partial_{x}w).

By the usual commutator argument frequently used in the proof of Proposition
7.1
we have the estimate for the first term

(77)

\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{N}(u^{M}P_{\ll N}\partial_{x}w)P_{N}w\lesssim T^{\delta}\|w\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{0}(T)}.

The estimate for the second term in (
76
) is simpler, and we find

(78)

\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{N}(u^{M}P_{\ll N}\partial_{x}w)P_{N}w\lesssim T^{\delta}\|w\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{0}(T)}.

In the third term we integrate by parts to find

(79)

\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}P_{N}wP_{N}(P_{\gg N}u^{M}\partial_{x}P_{\gg N}w)dxdyds\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u^{M}\|_{F^{1}(T)}\|w\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)}.

Plugging (
77
)-(
79
) into (
75
) we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
8.1
, ctd: Applying the Aubin-Lions lemma.

The short-time

estimates in weighted norms (
71
) together with the already established global estimates for

we find global a priori estimates in

for

:

\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|u^{M}(t)\|_{B^{s}}\lesssim_{T,s}\|\phi\|_{B^{s}}.

To summarize,
(u^{M})_{M\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}
exist globally in

and satisfies bounds (for fixed
T
) independent of
M
. For any domain
D_{R}=(-R,R)\times\mathbb{T}
we have bounds uniform in
M
and
R
:

u^{M}\in L_{T}^{\infty}H^{1}(D_{R}),\quad\partial_{t}u^{M}\in L_{T}^{\infty}H^{-1}(D_{R}).

By the compact embedding
H^{1}(D_{R})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(D_{R})
and the continuous embedding
L^{2}(D_{R})\hookrightarrow H^{-1}(D_{R})
, we can apply the Aubin–Lions lemma to obtain a subsequence
u^{M}\to u\in C_{T}L^{2}_{\text{loc}}
. We have
u\in L_{T}^{\infty}B^{s}
, consequently
u\in L_{T}^{\infty}H^{1}
and
\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\in L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}
.

By dual pairing in
L^{2}
for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([-T,T],\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
and passing to the limit
M\to\infty
, we find that
u
is a distributional solution to fKP-II. Since
u\in C^{1}([-T,T],H^{-1})\cap L_{T}^{\infty}H^{1}
, we conclude
u\in C([-T,T],H^{\sigma})
for
\sigma\in[-1,1)
. For
s>1
we have moreover
u\in C_{T}H^{1}
.

Lastly, we show that
u\in C_{T}B^{0}
for
s>1
. We have already established
u\in C_{T}L^{2}
. Now it suffices to show that
w=\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\in C_{T}L^{2}
. To this end, we use Duhamel’s formula

w(t_{2})-w(t_{1})=(S_{\alpha}(t_{2})-S_{\alpha}(t_{1}))\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}\phi-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}S_{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)(u(s)\partial_{x}w(s))ds.

The linear term converges to zero in
L^{2}
as
t_{1}\to t_{2}
because
(S_{\alpha}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup. For the second term we estimate with
I=[t_{1},t_{2}]
for
s>1
:

\|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}S_{\alpha}(t_{2}-s)(u(s)\partial_{x}w(s))ds\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u\|_{L_{I}^{1}L^{\infty}_{x,y}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{\infty}_{I}L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{1}_{I}B^{s}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{I}B^{1}}.

The ultimate estimate follows from Sobolev embedding. Since
u\in L_{T}^{\infty}B^{s}
the preceding estimate implies
u\in C_{T}B^{0}
.
∎

We remark that as a consequence of the proof we obtain the a priori estimates for solutions
u=S_{T}^{s}(u_{0})
:

(80)

\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}.

Indeed, for
s\geq 0
invoking Lemma
2.9
, and Propositions
6.1
and
7.1
we find:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{E^{s}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s}(T)}&\lesssim T^{\delta}\|u\|_{F^{0}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)},\\
\|u\|^{2}_{E^{s}(T)}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|u\|^{2}_{F^{s}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{0}(T)}.\end{array}\right.

By
L^{2}
-conservation this yields global existence in
H^{s,0}
. Indeed, for
s=0
, the estimates taken together give

\|u\|^{2}_{F^{0}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+T^{\delta}(\|u\|^{4}_{F^{0}(T)}+\|u\|^{3}_{F^{0}(T)}).

Choosing
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})
we find

(81)

\|u\|_{F^{0}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

For
s\geq 0
the estimates taken together yield the persistence of regularity property:

\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}^{2}+T^{\delta}(\|u\|^{2}_{F^{0}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}+\|u\|_{F^{s}(T)}^{2}\|u\|_{F^{0}(T)}),

which gives the desired a priori estimate (
80
).

8.2.
Lipschitz continuous dependence at negative Sobolev regularity

In the next step we show Lipschitz continuous dependence in negative Sobolev regularities for solutions
u_{1},u_{2}
in
L^{2}
. Let
v=u_{1}-u_{2}
denote the differences of solutions to (
69
).
v
solves the following Cauchy problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}v-\partial_{x}D_{x}^{\alpha}v+\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}v&=\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2})),\\
v(0)&=u_{1}(0)-u_{2}(0)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}).\end{array}\right.

It holds for
s^{\prime}=-8(2-\alpha)
with
\alpha\in(1.985,2)
as a consequence of Lemma
2.9
, and Propositions
6.1
, and
7.2
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim\|v\|_{E^{s^{\prime}}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s^{\prime}}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim T^{\delta}\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}(\|u_{1}\|_{F^{0}(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{F^{0}(T)}),\\
\|v\|^{2}_{E^{s^{\prime}}(T)}&\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|v\|^{2}_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}(\|u_{1}\|_{F^{0}(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{F^{0}(T)}).\end{array}\right.

The reduced symmetries of the difference equation lead to slightly more involved estimates. Subsuming the estimates we find

\|v\|^{2}_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{s^{\prime},0}}+T^{\delta}\|v\|^{2}_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}(\|u_{1}\|_{F^{0}(T)}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{F^{0}(T)}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{F^{0}(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{F^{0}(T)}).

By the previous step we find for
T\leq T_{0}(\|u_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}})
:

\|v\|^{2}_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}}^{2}+T^{\delta}\|v\|^{2}_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}(\|u_{1}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{2}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{1}(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{2}(0)\|_{L^{2}}).

Choosing
T
smaller, if necessary, we find

\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime}}(T)}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}.

8.3.
Conclusion of continuous dependence via frequency envelopes

At this point the continuous dependence follows from invoking the frequency envelope argument. For details we refer to our previous implementation in
[
27
]
to avoid repetition. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.

\Box

9.
A long-time property

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
.
This elaborates on the proof of a similar result in
[
46
]
in the case
\alpha=2
.
Indeed, Theorem
1.2
with
\alpha=2
is the contained in
[
46
, Lemma 5.3]
. In order to deal with the fractional dispersion in the
\xi
-frequencies, we will rely on some new arguments, in particular on
[
33
, Lemmas 6, 7]
. The proof of
[
46
, Claim 5.1]
no longer works in the case of low fractional dispersion. We will use instead an adaptation of the argument used in
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
to the case of periodic solutions with respect to the transverse variable.

For
r>1
, which presently will be chosen close to
1
, we set

\varphi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}(1+y^{2})^{-\frac{r}{2}}\,dy.

The main point is the following lemma.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(4/3,2]
. There is a constant
C
independent of
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\,(\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0})))^{2}\,u^{4}(x,y)dxdy\\
\leq C(\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}((\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct)))^{\frac{1}{2}}u(x,y))\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}+\|(\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0})))^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u(x,y)\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}).

Before we turn to the proof in earnest, we recall the following simple commutator estimate (see e.g.
[
4
, Lemma 2.97]
for a proof):

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
P_{N}
denote the Littlewood-Paley projection in the
x
-variable for
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
. For
f\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R})
,
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
we have the following commutator estimate:

(82)

\|[P_{N},f]g\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim_{\|f\|_{\dot{C}^{0,1}}}N^{-1}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Proof of Lemma
9.1
.

Set
\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=(1+(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}))^{2})^{-\frac{r}{4}}
so that
\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}))=(\psi_{\varepsilon}(x))^{2}
.
We need to evaluate
\|\psi_{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{4}_{x,y}}
. Using the Littlewood-Paley decomposition and the Sobolev inequality, we can write

(83)

\|\psi_{\varepsilon}u\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{x,y}}\lesssim\sum_{N-{\rm dyadic}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|\|P_{N}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{y}},

where
\Delta_{N}
denotes a Littlewood-Paley projection to
\xi
-frequencies of size
N
.
Set

\varphi_{0}(x)=\varphi(\varepsilon(x+x_{0})).

For
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
,

(84)

\|P_{N}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim N^{-\alpha/2}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\,.

We identify
\mathbb{T}
with
[-\pi,\pi]
and using a partition of unity, we can write
u=\theta(y)u+(1-\theta)u
with
\theta
vanishing near zero. In the following we focus on estimating
\theta(y)u
, which is redenoted by
u
for convenience.

Next, we write for
\sigma>0
to be fixed

\begin{split}\|P_{N}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\theta u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}&\lesssim N^{2\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{x}}(\langle D_{x}\rangle^{-\sigma}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\theta u))^{2}\\
&\lesssim 2N^{2\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{x}}\int_{0}^{y}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{-\sigma}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\theta u)\langle D_{x}\rangle^{-\sigma}(\partial_{y}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\theta u)).\end{split}

Now we write
\partial_{y}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\theta u)=\psi_{\varepsilon}\theta^{\prime}u+\psi_{\varepsilon}\theta\partial_{y}u
and therefore

\|P_{N}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\theta u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim N^{2\sigma}(I+II),

where

I\lesssim\|\psi_{\varepsilon}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\lesssim\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}.

We next estimate
II
. Using an integration by parts in
x
, we can write

II\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{x}\big(\langle D_{x}\rangle^{-2\sigma}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\psi_{\varepsilon}\big)|\,|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u|

We now fix
\sigma
via the relation
1-2\sigma=\alpha/2
, i.e.,

\sigma=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}.

With this choice of
\sigma
, we have

II\lesssim\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},

where we used that
\psi^{\prime}\lesssim\psi
.

Consequently, we obtain the estimate

(85)

\|P_{N}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim N^{2\sigma}\big(\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}+\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\big).

It now remains to suitably interpolate between (
84
) and (
85
). Set

\Lambda:=\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}+\|\psi_{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\,.

Then using (
84
) and (
85
), we can write

N\int_{\mathbb{T}}\|P_{N}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim N^{1+2\sigma}\Lambda\int_{\mathbb{T}}\,N^{-\alpha}\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}(\psi_{\varepsilon}u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim N^{2-\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\,\Lambda^{2}.

Therefore, if
\alpha>4/3
, we can sum-up the right hand-side of (
83
) and obtain that
\|\psi_{\varepsilon}u\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{x,y}}\lesssim\Lambda^{2}
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
9.1
.
∎

With Lemma
9.1
at hand, we can complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If
u
is a solution to (
1
), then

(86)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\,\varphi(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))u^{2}(t,x,y)dxdy=-\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}(\varphi(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))u)\,D_{x}^{\alpha}u\\
-\frac{\varepsilon}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))u^{3}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\,\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))\,\big((\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u)^{2}+cu^{2}\big)\,.

We now evaluate each term on the right hand-side of (
86
).
Thanks to
[
33
, Lemmas 6, 7]
, we have that there exists
\delta>0
and
C>0
such that

(87)

-\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}(\varphi(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))u)\,D_{x}^{\alpha}u\leq-\delta\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}((\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct)))^{\frac{1}{2}}u(x,y))\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\\
+C\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|(\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct)))^{\frac{1}{2}}\,u(x,y)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\,.

Note that
[
33
, Lemmas 6, 7]
are formulated for the case
\varepsilon=1
and the general case is described in
[
33
, Equations (53), (54)]
.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we may write

\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))u^{3}\big|^{2}\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\,(\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct)))^{2}\,u^{4}(x,y)dxdy.

We now apply the
L^{2}
conservation law and Lemma
9.1
to get

\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))u^{3}\big|^{2}\lesssim\mu^{2}\big(\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha/2}((\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct)))^{\frac{1}{2}}u(x,y))\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\\
+\|(\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct)))^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u(x,y)\|^{4}_{L^{2}_{x,y}}\big).

Taking
\mu=\mu(\delta)
(see (
87
)) small enough and coming back to (
86
) we get for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(c)
small enough (since
\alpha
is close to
2
)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\,\varphi(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))u^{2}(t,x,y)dxdy\leq 0\quad\forall\,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}.

The point of introducing the scaling parameter and carefully tracking the dependence of the estimates on
\varepsilon
is to be able to choose
c
independent of
C
.

Therefore

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\,\varphi(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}-ct))u^{2}(t,x,y)dxdy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\,\varphi(\varepsilon(x+x_{0}))u^{2}(0,x,y)dxdy,\quad\forall\,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}.

Applying the last inequality with
x_{0}=-ct
and letting
t\to\infty
yields by the theorem of dominated convergence

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\,\varphi(\varepsilon(x-2ct))u^{2}(t,x,y)dxdy=0.

Therefore we obtain the claimed result for
\gamma_{0}>2c
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

∎

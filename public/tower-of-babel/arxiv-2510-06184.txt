Window categories for a simple 9 9 -fold flop of Grassmannian type
Will Donovan, Wahei Hara, Michał Kapustka, Marco Rampazzo

Abstract
. The local simple 9 9 -fold flop of Grassmannian type is a birational transformation between total spaces of vector bundles on the Grassmannians Gr ⁡ ( 2 , 5 ) \Gr(2,5) and Gr ⁡ ( 3 , 5 ) \Gr(3,5) . We produce four different derived equivalences which commute with the pushforward functors for the flopping contractions. These equivalences are realized by identifying four different window categories inside the derived category of coherent sheaves on an Artin stack. As an application, our approach provides a new proof of derived equivalence for a pair of non-birational Calabi–Yau threefolds realized as zero loci of sections of homogeneous vector bundles in Grassmannians.

Following this fact, Kanemitsu labeled simple flops in his classification using Dynkin diagrams.

The significance of studying simple flops lies not only in the richness and simplicity of this class within birational geometry, but also in its connection to compact Calabi–Yau manifolds.
Namely, for a simple Mukai pair
(V_{+},\mathcal{E}_{+})
with the corresponding Mukai pair
(V_{-},\mathcal{E}_{-})
,
there exists a natural isomorphism
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{+})\simeq H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{-})
,
and if one chooses two general regular sections
s_{\pm}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{\pm})

that are related to each other by this isomorphism,
then as the vanishing loci
\bV(s_{\pm})\subset V_{\pm}

one has a pair
(\bV(s_{+}),\bV(s_{-}))
of compact Calabi–Yau manifolds.
Such Calabi–Yau manifolds
\bV(s_{+})
and
\bV(s_{-})
are not birationally equivalent to each other in general,
but are expected to satisfy some homological or motivic relations such as D-equivalence or L-equivalence.
The birational transformation
\Tot_{V_{+}}(\mathcal{E}_{+}^{\vee})\dashrightarrow\Tot_{V_{-}}(\mathcal{E}_{-}^{\vee})
is a local model of the simple flop of the corresponding type,
and if there is a reasonable derived equivalence

\mathcal{D}(\Tot_{V_{+}}(\mathcal{E}_{+}^{\vee}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{D}(\Tot_{V_{-}}(\mathcal{E}_{-}^{\vee}))
,
then one can descend it to give a derived equivalence

\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{+}))\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{-}))
.

Simple flop of type
A_{4}^{G}

The aim of this paper is to study the derived equivalence for a local model of the simple flop of type
A_{4}^{G}

Now we are ready to state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2

(
6.5
)
.

There exists an exact equivalence of
R
-linear triangulated categories

\Phi\colon\mathcal{D}(X_{+})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{D}(X_{-})

that satisfies a functor isomorphism
R(f_{+})_{*}\simeq R(f_{-})_{*}\circ\Phi

Note that the equivalence of the derived categories (as
R
-linear triangulated categories) for this flop was first constructed by Morimura
[
Mor22
]
.
The method of Morimura follows a sequence of mutations in the derived category of
\Tot_{\Fl(2,3;5)}(\mathcal{O}(-1,-1))
parallel to a sequence of mutations on the hyperplane section of the variety
\Fl(2,3;5)
used in
[
KR19
]
to prove the derived equivalence for the associated pair of Calabi–Yau manifolds.

In this paper, we prove the derived equivalence for the flop by employing tilting bundles, which provides a completely different approach from that of Morimura.
Unlike Morimura’s equivalence, our construction allows one to determine immediately the images of certain standard vector bundles, including some line bundles, under the equivalence.
Moreover, we construct four derived equivalences satisfying the functor isomorphism in Conjecture
1.1
;
the appearance of exactly four such equivalences is expected to have a physical background (see Remark
1.4
).
A further advantage of our approach is that starting from a tilting bundle one can naturally construct important objects such as windows and noncommutative crepant resolutions.

1.4.
Strategy

As already mentioned,
the main idea behind the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to use
tilting bundles

(see Definition
2.2
).
Namely, if one has tilting bundles
\mathcal{T}_{+}
and
\mathcal{T}_{-}
over
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
respectively,
such that
\End_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{T}_{+})\simeq\End_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{-})
,
then they yield derived equivalences

\mathcal{D}(X_{+})\xrightarrow[\RHom(\mathcal{T}_{+},-)]{\sim}\mathcal{D}(\End_{X_{\pm}}(\mathcal{T}_{\pm}))\xrightarrow[-\otimes\mathcal{T}_{-}]{\sim}\mathcal{D}(X_{-}).

It is worth noting that for the simple flop of type
A^{G}_{4}

the existence of tilting bundles has a deeper background from geometric invariant theory (GIT),
which can be summarized as follows.
To begin with, there exists a
\GL(3)
-representation
W
such that its GIT quotients with respect to two stability conditions
are
W\mathord{/\!\!/}_{\pm}\GL(3)\simeq X_{\pm}
.
In particular, there are canonical open immersions

\iota_{\pm}\colon X_{\pm}\hookrightarrow[W/\GL(3)]

where
[W/\GL(3)]
is the corresponding Artin stack.
Furthermore, for the tilting bundles
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
constructed in this paper,
there is a single pretilting bundle
\mathcal{T}
over
[W/G]
whose restrictions recover them,
i.e.
\iota_{\pm}^{*}\mathcal{T}\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
.
This bundle
\mathcal{T}
generates a thick subcategory
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{D}([W/G])

such that the restriction functors
\iota_{\pm}^{*}\colon\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{D}(X_{\pm})
are both equivalences.

Such a subcategory
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{D}([W/G])
is often referred to as a
window category
(for both
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
).
Several generalities for window categories have been established in previous works including
[
BFK19
,
HL15
,
HLS20
]
.
However, note that the results in
[
HLS20
]
cannot be applied to our example of a flop,
since the
\GL(3)
-representation
W
is not quasi-symmetric.
Moreover, it turns out that the window categories introduced in
[
BFK19
,
HL15
]
do not coincide with the window categories constructed in this paper, as discussed in §
7
.

The above background involving Artin stacks and window categories can be applied
to examples that admit a description as a GIT quotient of a smooth affine variety (or vector space).
Among the known simple flops, such GIT quotient descriptions are available only for the Atiyah flops (i.e., of type
A_{n}\times A_{n}
)
and the flops of type
A^{G}_{n}
.
The approach using tilting bundles is broadly parallel to the study of window categories in cases where a GIT description exists,
but importantly, it also extends to more general situations.

1.5.
Projective Calabi–Yau threefolds and GLSM

In theoretical physics,
the data of the variation of GIT relating
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
describes a
gauged linear sigma model
(GLSM).
Such entities are supersymmetric gauge theories introduced by Witten in
[
Wit93
]
,
which exhibit different phases: roughly speaking, each of those phases is associated to a space parametrizing lowest-energy states, which is the critical locus of a function called
superpotential
(determined by the geometric data).
By physical arguments, it is expected that such critical loci are related at the level of derived categories of coherent sheaves.
While Witten’s original formulation describes GIT quotients with respect to abelian groups, successive advancements have been made in the context of non-abelian gauge theories,
producing GLSM interpretations of Calabi–Yau subvarieties of Grassmannians and Pfaffians,
and other interesting geometries
[
HT07
,
ADS15
,
KS19
]
.
In particular, our variation of GIT has been shown to exhibit two “simple” geometric phases
[
KR19
]
,
whose associated critical loci are Calabi–Yau threefolds which are non-birationally equivalent.
There is a well-known correspondence between zero loci of sections of vector bundles and associated GLSM phases:
in particular, a general section
s
of a globally generated vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
determines a GLSM phase with critical locus
\bV(s)
and a canonical superpotential
Q_{s}:\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
.
In such context, by Knörrer periodicity,
the derived category of
\bV(s)
is equivalent to the derived factorization category
\Dcoh_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee}),\chi_{\id},W_{s})
(see §
6.3
).
This correspondence enables us, via Theorem
1.2
, to provide a new proof of the main result of
[
KR19
]
and to extend the result to the singular case of
\bV(s)
, by arguments similar to those in
[
Seg11
,
ADS15
]
.

Corollary 1.3

(
6.6
)
.

Let

\displaystyle s_{+}

\displaystyle\in H^{0}(\Gr(3,5),\mathcal{U}_{3}(2))

\displaystyle s_{-}

\displaystyle\in H^{0}(\Gr(2,5),\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{\vee}(2))

be corresponding sections under the canonical isomorphism

If

of (possibly singular) Calabi–Yau threefolds.

Remark 1.4
.

The gauge theory in physics can describe concretely
the stringy Kähler moduli space (SKMS) associated to the GLSM we study in this paper.
We provide a rough outline here; for more detailed account of the theory and the related works,
we refer the reader to
[
HHP08
,
Hor16
,
EHKR17
,
EHKR
]
.

First, the model involves two parameters, the Fayet–Iliopoulos (FI) parameter and the theta parameter.
Since the theta parameter is
2\pi
-periodic, it is natural to consider the FI–theta parameter space modulo this periodicity,
which can be regarded as a cylinder or, equivalently, a twice-punctured sphere.
To obtain the SKMS, one further removes three points on the cylinder corresponding to loci where the model is ill-defined, up to
2\pi
shifts.
In particular, the resulting SKMS is a five-punctured sphere.

The regions where the absolute value of the FI parameter is large correspond to the two phases of the model, namely the Calabi–Yau threefolds
\bV(s_{+})
and
\bV(s_{-})
.
Homotopy classes of paths connecting points in these regions (denoted
m_{+}
and
m_{-}
in Figure
1
) give rise to the physical notion of windows.
For the GLSM considered in this paper, one can identify four fundamental such windows (corresponding to paths A, B, C and D in Figure
1
).

In this paper, we construct four window
categories
to establish derived equivalences for the flop of type
A_{4}^{G}
, which we expect to correspond to the four fundamental windows in the physical sense.
We hope that further investigation of this example from the perspective of physics will be pursued,
and that the mathematical results in this paper will contribute to further deepening the relation between physics and mathematics.

Remark 1.5
.

Another aspect – related to Remark
1.4
– of the appearance of four windows is the perspective offered by mirror symmetry between compact Calabi–Yau manifolds. Let us explain this point of view in our example.

In our case, the compact Calabi–Yau threefolds associated with the flop belong to the family
\mathcal{X}_{25}
of complete intersections of two Grassmannian translates (c.f.
[
OR18
, Section 5]
),
whose mirror family is conjecturally governed by Picard–Fuchs equation no. 101 in the database
[
vEvS06
]
(see
[
Kap13
, Section 4]
).
This equation has two points of maximal unipotent monodromy (MUM) exchanged by an automorphism of the equation,
which is related to the existence of derived equivalences between pairs of elements of the family.
In our case, these are pairs
(\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{+})),\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{-})))
.
Moreover, the equation has in total six singular points, namely the two MUM points, three conifold singularities and one apparent singularity. Since the latter does not induce any monodromy,
we can assume that the complex moduli space is supported on a five-punctured sphere.
By mirror symmetry,
the complex moduli space of
\mathcal{Y}
should correspond to the SKMS of elements of
\mathcal{X}_{25}
.
By homological mirror symmetry, the monodromy operators of the Picard–Fuchs equation should correspond to autoequivalences of the derived category of elements of
\mathcal{X}_{25}
.
On the other hand, as written in Remark
1.4
, the four windows provide four equivalences between
\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{+}))
and
\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{-}))
,
which correspond to four paths in the SKMS connecting
\bV(s_{+})
and
\bV(s_{-})
.

By composing any such equivalence with the inverse of another, we obtain autoequivalences of
\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{+}))
corresponding to loops in the SKMS.
These loops correspond to loops around singular points of the complex moduli space of the mirror which in turn induce monodromy operators around the three non-MUM degeneracy points of the Picard–Fuchs equation.
However, the precise rule associating autoequivalences of
\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{+}))
with monodromy operators on the mirror side remains to be determined.

2.
NCCRs and tilting objects

This section gives the definition and fundamental properties of noncommutative crepant resolutions (NCCRs) and tilting objects, which are the main tools in this paper.
Let us start by recalling the definition of noncommutative crepant resolution,
which serves as a noncommutative analog of crepant resolution.

All rings and schemes in this section are assumed to be Noetherian, and all schemes separated.

Definition 2.1

(
[
VdB04a
]
)
.

Let
R
be a normal Gorenstein domain, and
M
a reflexive
R
-module
.
We say that
M
gives a
noncommutative crepant resolution (NCCR)

A=\End_{R}(M)
of
R
,
if
A
has finite global dimension and
A
is Cohen-Macaulay as an
R
-module.

Usually the existence and a construction of an NCCR for a given example of
R
is not obvious.
If
R
admits a crepant resolution in the usual sense with a tilting complex, as defined below,
then the existence of an NCCR follows.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
Z
be a scheme and
\mathcal{T}\in\Perf(Z)
a perfect complex.

(1)

\mathcal{T}
is said to be
pretilting
if
\Ext^{i}_{Z}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T})=0
for all
i\neq 0
.

(2)

\mathcal{T}
is said to be
tilting
if
\mathcal{T}
is pretilting and generates the unbounded derived category
\D(\Qcoh Z)
, i.e. if
F\in\D(\Qcoh Z)
satisfies
\RHom_{Z}(\mathcal{T},F)=0
, then
F=0
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is (quasi-isomorphic to) a locally free sheaf of finite rank,
\mathcal{T}
is called a
(pre)tilting bundle
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is known by
[
BVdB03
, Theorem 2.1.2 and Theorem 3.1.1]
that an object
\mathcal{T}\in\Perf(Z)
is a generator of
\D(\Qcoh Z)
if and only if
\mathcal{T}
is a classical generator of
\Perf(Z)
.
Assume in addition that
Z
is projective over an affine scheme,
and let
\mathcal{T}
be a pretilting object over
Z
.
Then the condition of
\mathcal{T}
to be a generator of
\D(\Qcoh Z)
is also equivalent to the condition that
\mathcal{T}
generates
\D^{-}(\coh Z)
.
See
[
Har20
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
R
be a normal Gorenstein domain and
f\colon X\to\Spec R
a projective morphism.
Assume that there exists a tilting bundle
\mathcal{T}
over
X
.

(1)

Then the functor

\RHom_{X}(\mathcal{T},-)\colon\mathcal{D}(X)\to\mathcal{D}(\End_{X}(\mathcal{T}))

gives an exact equivalence of
R
-linear triangulated categories.

Assume in addition that
f\colon X\to\Spec R
is a small resolution.
Then the following hold.

(2)

The module
f_{*}\mathcal{T}
is reflexive and the natural homomorphisms give isomorphisms

\End_{X}(\mathcal{T})\simeq\End_{R}(f_{*}\mathcal{T})

of
R

(3)

The reflexive
R
-module

gives an NCCR of

Proof.

(1) follows from
[

Since

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f\colon X\to\Spec R
be a projective crepant resolution,
and
\mathcal{T}
a pretilting object.
Then
\mathcal{T}
is tilting (i.e. a generator) if and only if

has finite global dimension.

Proof.

The “only if” part follows from Proposition
2.4
.
Assume that
A=\End_{X}(\mathcal{T})
has finite global dimension.
Since
f
is a proper morphism, one can consider the following two functors.

\displaystyle\Phi\coloneqq\RHom(\mathcal{T},-)

\displaystyle\colon\D^{-}(\coh X)\to\D^{-}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{mod}A)

\displaystyle\Psi\coloneqq-\otimes_{A}^{L}\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\colon\D^{-}(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{mod}A)\to\D^{-}(\coh X)

The pretilting property of

(c.f.
[

).
Note that

.

\mathcal{D}(A)

.

\mathcal{D}(X)
has no non-trivial semiorthogonal decomposition (c.f.

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

5.2.
Tilting bundles over
X_{+}

Let us begin with an elementary but useful fact on irreducible homogeneous bundles, which is well-known to experts:

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a globally generated, homogeneous, irreducible vector bundle on a rational homogeneous
Y
, fix
X\coloneqq\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})
and call
\pi\colon X\to Y
the structure map. Then, for every homogeneous, irreducible vector bundle
\mathcal{F}
on
Y
which has a nontrivial global section, and for any
i>0
, one has
H^{i}(Y,\mathcal{F})=H^{i}(X,\pi^{*}\mathcal{F})=0
.

Proof.

If an irreducible homogeneous bundle
F
has a nonzero global section, then it is globally generated, and hence it does not have higher cohomology because, by the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem, homogeneous irreducible vector bundles cannot have nontrivial cohomology in two different degrees. Since the tensor product of globally generated vector bundles is globally generated, the same holds for every irreducible summand of
\mathcal{F}\otimes\bigoplus_{l\geq 0}\Sym^{l}\mathcal{E}
: therefore
\mathcal{F}\otimes\bigoplus_{l\geq 0}\Sym^{l}\mathcal{E}
has no higher cohomology. The proof is concluded by observing that the latter is no other than
\pi_{*}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X})
.
∎

.

\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star}

is a tilting bundle over

for all
\star\in\{\spadesuit,\clubsuit,\heartsuit,\diamondsuit\}

2.6
,
we can restrict our attention to the case

.

Let

(resp.
\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\heartsuit}
) be a vector bundle over

that is the direct sum of the collection in Proposition

(1) (resp. (2)).
Then notice that

for each
\star\in\{\spadesuit,\heartsuit\}
.
Thus
[
Har21
, Lemma 3.1]
implies that the bundle
\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star}
classically generates
\mathcal{D}(X_{+})
.
We are left to prove that
\Ext^{p}_{X_{+}}(\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star},\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star})=0
for

. First, observe that:

(5.2)

\begin{split}\Ext^{p}_{X_{+}}(\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star},\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\ast})&=\Ext^{p}_{\Gr(3,5)}(\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star},\pi_{+*}\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\ast})\\

for all
k\geq-2
and
i>0
.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}(-2+k)\to\mathcal{P}(k)\to\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(k)\to 0
.
Then the first term has no higher cohomology by Proposition
5.9
,
and likewise for the last one by the Littlewood-Richardson rule and the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem
(see §
B.4.2
for an example of a script that computes this cohomology).
∎

Proposition 5.11
.

H^{i}(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}(k))=0
for all
k\geq 0
and
i>0
.

Proof.

Taking the second symmetric product of (
5.3
), and then twisting by
\mathcal{O}(k)
,
we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{P}(-2+k)\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}(k)\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(k)\to 0.

Then the result follows from by applying Proposition
5.10
and the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem.
(Note that
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(k)
is globally generated,
and hence Lemma
5.7
applies.)
∎

5.3.2.
Key lemmas to prove the vanishing of problematic extensions

The vanishings proved in the previous section are not enough to prove that the bundles
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\star}
are tilting.
In order to prove the remaining vanishing, we prepare several lemmas.

Lemma 5.12
.

There are isomorphisms
Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}\simeq\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\simeq Rq_{*}(p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}\otimes O(E))
.

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{X}
is the total space of a line bundle over
\Fl(2,3;5)
, there is an exact sequence

(5.3)

\displaystyle 0\to q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}\to\mathcal{O}(-H_{3}+H_{2})\to 0

on
\widetilde{X}
. Note that
\mathcal{O}(-H+h)\simeq\mathcal{O}(E+2h)
.
Consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}(E)\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(E)\to 0.

Since
q\colon E\to\mathbb{G}_{-}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle, it holds that
Rq_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}(E)=0
, and hence

Rq_{*}\mathcal{O}(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}}
.
Similarly, it also holds that
Rq_{*}\mathcal{O}(2E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}}
.
This together with the projection formula implies that

Rq_{*}\mathcal{O}(E+2H_{2})\simeq Rq_{*}\mathcal{O}(2E+2H_{2})\simeq\mathcal{O}(2)
and
Rq_{*}(q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}\otimes\mathcal{O}(E))\simeq\mathcal{U}_{2}
.
Therefore, applying the functor
Rq_{*}
to the sequence (
5.3
) shows that

Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}
is isomorphic to a vector bundle over
X_{-}
and lies in an exact sequence

(5.4)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{U}_{2}\to Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}\to\mathcal{O}(2)\to 0.

In particular,
Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}
is isomorphic to a vector bundle over
X_{-}
.
Note that, by Lemma
5.3
, the bundles
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
and
Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}
over
X_{-}
both correspond to the bundle
\mathcal{U}_{3}
over
X_{+}
under the equivalence (
4.2
).
Therefore, there is an isomorphism

Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}\simeq\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
as desired.
Similarly, applying the functor
Rq_{*}
to the sequence (
5.3
) tensored by
\mathcal{O}(E)
proves the second desired isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

There is an isomorphism
Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)\simeq\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Consider an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}(H_{3}-H_{2})\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}\to q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}\to 0.

Since
\mathcal{O}(H_{3}-H_{2})\simeq\mathcal{O}(-E-2H_{2})
, twisting this sequence by
\mathcal{O}(E)
gives

0\to\mathcal{O}(-2H_{2})\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)\to q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(E)\to 0.

Note that
Rq_{*}q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(E)\simeq\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}\otimes Rq_{*}\mathcal{O}(E)\simeq\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}
.
Thus
Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)\simeq q_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)
, and this sheaf fits in an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}}(-2)\to q_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)\to\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}\to 0.

In particular,
Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)
is isomorphic to a vector bundle over
X_{-}
.
Under the equivalence (
4.2
),
both
\mathcal{P}
and
Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)
correspond to
\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}
by Lemma
5.3
,
which proves that
Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)\simeq\mathcal{P}
.
∎

Lemma 5.14
.

There is an isomorphism
Rq_{*}p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}\simeq\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
.

Proof.

Taking the second symmetric product of the exact sequence (
5.3
) gives an exact sequence

0\to q^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}\to p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-H_{3}+H_{2})\to 0.

Note that
p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-H_{3}+H_{2})\simeq p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(E+2H_{2})
,
and hence it follows from Lemma
5.12
that

Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-H_{3}+H_{2})\simeq\left(Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(E)\right)(2)\simeq\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(2).

Thus
Rq_{*}p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}
is a vector bundle over
X_{-}
that lies in an exact sequence

0\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}\to Rq_{*}p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}\to\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(2)\to 0.

Note that, under the equivalence (
4.2
),
the bundles
Rq_{*}p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}
and
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
over
X_{-}
both correspond to the same bundle

\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}
over
X_{+}
by Lemma
5.3
.
Thus we have the desired isomorphism
Rq_{*}p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}\simeq\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
.
∎

Lemma 5.15
.

There exists an exact sequence on
\widetilde{X}

0\to q^{*}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(E+2H_{2})\to 0.

Proof.

The adjunction map
\mathcal{O}(2H_{2})\simeq q^{*}q_{*}\mathcal{O}(E+2H_{2})\to\mathcal{O}(E+2H_{2})
is an injective sheaf homomorphism with the cokernel
\mathcal{O}_{E}(E+2H_{2})
.
Consider another adjunction map
q^{*}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\simeq q^{*}q_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}
from Lemma
5.12
, which is injective since
q
is birational.
Now applying the snake lemma to the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{*}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}(2H_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}(E+2H_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

proves the result.
∎

Lemma 5.16
.

There exists an exact sequence on
\widetilde{X}

0\to q^{*}\mathcal{P}\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)\to q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(E)|_{E}\to 0.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.13
, there exists the adjunction morphism
q^{*}\mathcal{P}\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)
,
that lies in the following diagram of exact sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}(-2H_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{*}\mathcal{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}(-2H_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

The third vertical map is also the adjunction of the isomorphism
Rq_{*}q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(E)\simeq\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}
,
and the cokernel is
q^{*}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(E)|_{E}
.
Thus the snake lemma shows the result.
∎

5.3.3.
Proof of harder vanishings

Proposition 5.17
.

H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a))=0
for all
a\leq 4
and
i>0
.

Proof.

If
a\leq 1
, the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem applied to

0\to\mathcal{U}_{2}(-a)\to\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a)\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}}(2-a)\to 0

proves the vanishing (as, under the identification
\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}\simeq\mathcal{U}_{2}(1)
, the relevant vanishings have already been computed in Propositions
5.9
and
5.10
).
Thus we may assume
a\in\{2,3,4\}
.

Using Lemma
5.12
gives

\displaystyle H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}(X_{-},Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-a))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}(\widetilde{X},p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}(\widetilde{X},p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(aE+aH_{3}))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}(X_{+},\mathcal{U}_{3}(a)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(aE)).

Consider the case
a=2
.
As in the proof of Lemma
5.13
,
it follows that
Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(2E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}}
.
Therefore the vanishing

H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-2))\simeq H^{i}(X_{+},\mathcal{U}_{3}(2))=0

follows for all
i>0
, by applying Lemma
5.7
to
\mathcal{U}_{3}(2)\simeq\wedge^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(1)
.

Next, consider the case
a=3
.
Note that the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(3E)
lies in an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}(2E)\to\mathcal{O}(3E)\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(3E)\to 0.

Since
p\colon E\to\mathbb{G}_{+}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle,
it follows that

Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}(3E)\simeq\omega_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}[-2]=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}(-5)[-2]
.
Therefore, the complex
\mathcal{U}_{3}(3)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(3E)
lies in an exact triangle

\mathcal{U}_{3}(3)\to\mathcal{U}_{3}(3)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(3E)\to\mathcal{U}_{3}(-2)|_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}[-2].

The following hold.

(1)

H^{\geq 1}(X_{+},\mathcal{U}_{3}(3))=0
.

(2)

The bundle
\mathcal{U}_{3}(-2)|_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}\simeq\mathcal{U}_{3}(-2)
over
\mathbb{G}_{+}\simeq\Gr(3,5)
is acyclic.

Indeed, the first claim is true by Lemma
5.7
, while the latter because

\RG(\Gr(3,5),\mathcal{U}_{3}(-2))\simeq\RHom(\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(1),\mathcal{O}(-1))=0

by Proposition
3.1
.
Thus the vanishing

H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-3))\simeq H^{i}(X_{+},\mathcal{U}_{3}(3)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(3E))=0

also follows for all
i>0
.

It remains to consider the case
a=4
.
Applying the functor
Rp_{*}
to the exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}(3E)\to\mathcal{O}(4E)\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(4E)\to 0
gives
an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}(-5)\to R^{2}p_{*}\mathcal{O}(4E)\to\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-7)|_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}\to 0

on
X_{+}
.
Put
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq R^{2}p_{*}\mathcal{O}(4E)
.
Then the complex
\mathcal{U}_{3}(4)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(4E)
lies in an exact triangle

\mathcal{U}_{3}(4)\to\mathcal{U}_{3}(4)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(4E)\to\mathcal{U}_{3}(4)\otimes_{X_{+}}\mathcal{F}[-2].

By the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem, the following hold.

(3)

H^{i}(X_{+},\mathcal{U}_{3}(4))=0
for all
i>0
.

(4)

The bundles

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{3}(4)|_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}\!(-5)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{U}_{3}(-1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{3}(4)|_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-7)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{U}_{3}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-3)

over
\mathbb{G}_{+}\simeq\Gr(3,5)
are acyclic.

Indeed, (3) follows from Lemma
5.7
.
For the first bundle in (4), we have

\RG(\Gr(3,5),\mathcal{U}_{3}(-1))\simeq\RHom(\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(1),\mathcal{O})=0

by the semi-orthogonality of the collection in Proposition
3.1
.
For the second bundle in (4), one can compute as

\displaystyle H^{i}(\Gr(3,5),\mathcal{U}_{3}\otimes\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-3))

\displaystyle\simeq\Ext^{i}(\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(4),\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(1))

\displaystyle\simeq\Ext^{6-i}(\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(1),\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-1))^{*},

and this is zero for all
i
, again by the semi-orthogonality of the collection in Proposition
3.1
.

Now (4) implies that
\RG(\mathcal{U}_{3}(4)|_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}\otimes\mathcal{F}[-2])=0
, and hence

\displaystyle H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-4))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}(X_{+},\mathcal{U}_{3}(4)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(4E))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}(X_{+},\mathcal{U}_{3}(4))

\displaystyle=0

for all
i>0
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 5.18
.

H^{i}(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-k))=0
for all
k\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and
i>0
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\{0,1,2,3\}
. Then using Lemma
5.14
gives

\displaystyle H^{i}(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-k))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}\left(X_{-},\left(Rq_{*}p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}\right)(-k)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}\left(\widetilde{X},p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-kH_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}\left(\widetilde{X},p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(kE+kH_{3})\right)

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}\left(\widetilde{X},p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(kE+kH_{3})\right)

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}\left(X_{+},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(k)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(kE)\right).

Therefore there is an isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{i}(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-k))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{i}(X_{+},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(k))

for all
0\leq k\leq 2
.
In addition, there is an exact triangle

\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(3)\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(3)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(3E)\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-2)|_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}[-2].

Thus, in order to show the vanishing of
H^{\geq 1}(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-k))
, it is enough to prove the following two statements.

(1)

H^{i}(X_{+},(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3})(k))=0
for all
i\geq 1
and
k\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.

(2)

The bundle
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-2)|_{\mathbb{G}_{+}}\simeq\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-2)
over
\mathbb{G}_{+}\simeq\Gr(3,5)
is acyclic.

Note that, the vanishings in (1) have already been addressed in Theorem
5.8
.
For (2), note that

H^{j}(\Gr(3,5),\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-2))\simeq\Ext_{\Gr(3,5)}^{6-j}(\mathcal{O}(2),\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-1))^{*}

by Serre duality, and this extension is indeed zero by the semi-orthogonality of the collection in Proposition
3.1
(2).
∎

Proposition 5.19
.

\Ext^{i}_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\mathcal{P}(k))=0
for all
k\geq 0
and
i>0
.

Proof.

Since

\Ext^{i}_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\mathcal{P}(k))\simeq H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{P}(k))\simeq H^{i}\left(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}(k)\oplus\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(k)\right),

the vanishing follows from Proposition
5.11
and
5.17
.
∎

Proposition 5.20
.

\Ext^{i}_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a))=0
for all
a\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and
i>0
.

Proof.

Since

\Ext^{i}_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a))\simeq H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a))\simeq H^{i}\left(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a)\oplus\mathcal{P}(-a)\right),

the vanishing follows from Proposition
5.10
and
5.18
.
∎

Proposition 5.21
.

\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a))\simeq\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}(-a))=0
for all
a\leq 2
and
i>0
.

Proof.

First, let us prove the case when
a<0
.
Then Lemma
5.7
shows that

(i)

\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee},\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(-a))\simeq H^{i}(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(-a-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))=0
for all
i>0
, and

(ii)

\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{O}(-2),\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(-a))\simeq H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(-a+2))=0
for all
i>0
.

Thus, applying the functor
\RHom_{X_{-}}(-,\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(-a))
to the sequence (
5.1
)
and then using (i) and (ii) shows that

(iii)

\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(-a))=0
for all
i>0
.

Again, applying the functor
\RHom(\mathcal{P},-\otimes\mathcal{O}(-a))
to (
5.1
) gives an exact sequence

H^{i}(X_{-},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a-2))\to\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}(-a))\to\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(-a)).

For any
i>0
, the first term of this sequence is zero by Proposition
5.17
,
and the third term is zero by (iii).
Therefore the desired vanishing
\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}(-a))=0
holds for all
a<0
and
i>0
.

It remains to prove the cases when
a=0,1,2
.
Apply the functor
\RHom_{\widetilde{X}}(-,p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))
to the exact sequence in Lemma
5.15

to obtain the following exact triangle.

\displaystyle\RHom_{\widetilde{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{E}(E+2H_{2}),p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\RHom_{\widetilde{X}}(p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3},p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\RHom_{\widetilde{X}}(q^{*}\mathcal{P}^{\vee},p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))

Then the third term of this triangle can be computed as

\RHom(q^{*}\mathcal{P}^{\vee},p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))\simeq\RHom(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},Rq_{*}p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))\simeq\RHom(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a)),

and this is what we want to compute.
The cohomology of the first term can be computed as

\displaystyle\Ext_{\widetilde{X}}^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{E}(E+2H_{2}),p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))

\displaystyle{}\simeq{}

\displaystyle\Ext_{\widetilde{X}}^{9-i}(p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}),\mathcal{O}_{E}(3E+2H_{2}))^{*}

(Serre duality)

\displaystyle{}\simeq{}

\displaystyle H^{9-i}\left(\Fl(2,3;5),\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}((1-a)E+(-2-a)H_{3})\right)^{*}

\displaystyle{}\simeq{}

\displaystyle H^{9-i}\left(\Gr(3,5),\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}\otimes\Sym^{a-1}\mathcal{U}_{3}\otimes\mathcal{O}(a-4)\right)^{*}.

This is clearly zero if
a=0
.
In addition, this cohomology is isomorphic to
\Ext^{9-i}_{\Gr(3,5)}(\mathcal{O}(3),\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee})
if
a=1
,
and to
\Ext^{9-i}_{\Gr(3,5)}(\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(1),\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-1))
if
a=2
.
These are zero for all
i
by the semi-orthogonality of the collection in Proposition
3.1
.
Thus the first term
\RHom(\mathcal{O}_{E}(E+2H_{2}),p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))
is zero.

Finally, the second term is

\RHom_{\widetilde{X}}(p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3},p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))\simeq\RHom_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{U}_{3},\mathcal{U}_{3}(a)\otimes Rp_{*}\mathcal{O}(aE))\simeq\RHom_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee},\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(a)),

which has no cohomology in non-zero degrees for all
a\in\{0,1,2\}

due to the pretilting property of the bundle
\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\spadesuit}
proved in Theorem
5.8
.

Therefore the desired vanishing
\Ext^{i}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a))=0
holds for all
a\in\{0,1,2\}
and
i>0
.
∎

Proposition 5.22
.

\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}(a))=0
for all
a\in\{0,1,2\}
and
i>0
.

Proof.

Taking the second symmetric product of the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}(-2)\to\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}\to 0
gives another sequence

0\to\mathcal{P}(-2)\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}\to 0.

Let
a\in\{0,1,2\}
.
Applying
\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P}(-a),-)
to the above sequence gives an exact sequence

\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}(a-2))\to\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}(a))\to\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a)).

Proposition
5.21
proves that the first term is zero for all
i>0
.
The third term fits in an exact sequence

\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a))\to\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a))\to H^{i}(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a+2)).

The rightmost term has no higher cohomology by Lemma
5.7
,
while the cohomology of the leftmost one can be computed by the Littlewood-Richardson rule and the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem
(see §
B.4.3
for an example of a script for this computation).
∎

Proposition 5.23
.

\Ext_{X_{-}}^{i}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}(a))=0
for all
a\in\{2,3\}
and
i>0
.

Proof.

Applying the functor
\RHom(\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a),-)
to the sequence

0\to\mathcal{P}(-2)\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}\to\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}\to 0

gives an exact triangle

\RHom(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\mathcal{P}(-2+a))\to\RHom(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}(a))\to\RHom(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a)).

The first term has no higher cohomology by Proposition
5.19
.
Thus it is enough to show that the third term has no cohomology.
Applying the functor
\RHom(-,\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a))
to an exact sequence

gives an exact triangle

\RG(X_{-},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a-2))\to\RHom(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a))\to\RHom(\mathcal{U}_{2},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}(a)).

.
The right term is

∎

Proposition 5.24
.

\Ext^{i}(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}(-a))=0
for all
a\in\{0,1,2\}
and
i>0
.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence
0\to q^{*}\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\to p^{*}\mathcal{U}_{3}\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(E+2H_{2})\to 0
given in Lemma
5.15
,
and apply the functor
\Ext^{i}(-,p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))
.
Then the first term is

\displaystyle\Ext_{\widetilde{X}}^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{E}(E+2H_{2}),p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Ext^{9-i}(p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}),\mathcal{O}_{E}(3E+2H_{2}))^{*}

\displaystyle\simeq H^{9-i}(\Fl(2,3;5),p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}((1-a)E-(a+2)H_{3}))^{*}

\displaystyle\simeq H^{9-i}(\Gr(3,5),\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}\otimes\Sym^{a-1}\mathcal{U}_{3}\otimes\mathcal{O}(a-4))^{*}.

If
a=0

\Ext^{9-i}_{\Gr(3,5)}(\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(1),\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-1))
if

,
and to
\Ext^{9-i}_{\Gr(3,5)}(\mathcal{O}(2),\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(-1))

(2).

The second term is

Since

for all

The third term can be computed as

\displaystyle\Ext_{\widetilde{X}}^{i}(q^{*}\mathcal{P}^{\vee},p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}(-aH_{2}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Ext_{\widetilde{X}}^{i}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee},Rq_{*}p^{*}\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-a))

Proposition 5.25

\Ext^{i}(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P},\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P})=0

.

Proof.

Apply the functor

Proposition 5.26

\Ext^{i}(\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-a))=0

5.4.
Tilting bundles over
X_{-}

Theorem 5.27
.

The bundle
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\star}
is a tilting bundle over
X_{-}
for all
\star\in\{\spadesuit,\clubsuit,\heartsuit,\diamondsuit\}
.

Proof.

Cohomology computations in §
5.3.1
and §
5.3.3
show that
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\spadesuit}
and
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\heartsuit}
are pretilting.
Since
\End_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\star})\simeq\End_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star})
,
this algebra has finite global dimension for all
\star\in\{\spadesuit,\clubsuit,\heartsuit,\diamondsuit\}
.
Therefore applying Proposition
2.5
shows that
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\star}
is a generator, and hence a tilting bundle.
∎

Remark 5.28
.

The restriction of the bundle
\mathcal{P}
to the zero-section
\Gr(2,5)\simeq\mathbb{G}_{-}\subset X_{-}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\vee}\simeq\mathcal{O}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{U}_{2}(1)
.
Thus the restriction of the tilting bundle
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\spadesuit}
to the zero-section is isomorphic to a direct sum (with multiplicities) of the following collection of bundles over
\Gr(2,5)
:

\{\mathcal{O}(a)\mid-3\leq a\leq 1\}\cup\{\mathcal{U}_{2}(b)\mid-2\leq b\leq 2\},

and this set forms the full strong exceptional collection

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\Gr(2,5))=\langle\mathcal{O}(-3),\mathcal{U}_{2}(-2),\mathcal{O}(-2),\mathcal{U}_{2}(-1),\mathcal{O}(-1),\mathcal{U}_{2},\mathcal{O},\mathcal{U}_{2}(1),\mathcal{O}(1),\mathcal{U}_{2}(2)\rangle

of
\Gr(2,5)
.
In other words, the restriction
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\spadesuit}
remains tilting, and is given by a full strong exceptional collection.

On the contrary, the restriction of the tilting bundle
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\heartsuit}
to the zero-section is isomorphic to a direct sum (with multiplicities) of the following collection of bundles over
\Gr(2,5)
:

\{\mathcal{O}(a)\mid-3\leq a\leq 1\}\cup\{\mathcal{U}_{2}(b)\mid-2\leq b\leq 2\}\cup\{\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}(3)\}.

The first two sets of bundles actually form the same full exceptional collection (
5.5
).
However, as an indecomposable summand of
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\heartsuit}|_{\mathbb{G}_{-}}
, there appears an additional bundle, namely
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{2}(3)
.
In particular, the restriction
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\heartsuit}|_{\mathbb{G}_{-}}
is no longer tilting.

This observation indicates that tilting bundles are not necessarily constructed solely from full strong exceptional collections on the base.
This would highlight the difficulty of constructing a tilting bundle in more general settings including simple flops of type
A^{G}_{n}
for
n\geq 6
.

Remark 5.29
.

Pulling-back the direct sum of Kapranov’s collection over
\Gr(3,5)
to
X_{+}
gives the bundle

\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(2)\oplus\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}\oplus\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}(1)\oplus\mathcal{U}_{3}(1)\oplus\mathcal{U}_{3}(2)\oplus\Sym^{2}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}\oplus\mathbb{S}^{2,1}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}\oplus\mathbb{S}^{2,2}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}.

One can show that this bundle is also tilting (c.f. §
7.2
).
The corresponding bundle over
X_{-}
(in the sense of Corollary
5.6
) is

\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathcal{P}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-2)\oplus\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathbb{S}^{2,1}\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathbb{S}^{2,2}\mathcal{P}.

Unfortunately, this bundle is not pretilting.
Indeed, mimicking the computations in §

,

\Ext^{3}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-1),\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P})\simeq\Ext^{6}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee}(-1),\Sym^{2}\mathcal{P})\simeq\mathbb{C}
.

This is the reason why we should work with bundles in Definition
5.5
,
which correspond to the new exceptional collections in §

.

Remark 6.2
.

Note that
\iota_{+}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{W}\otimes V(\chi))\simeq\mathbb{S}^{\chi}\mathcal{U}_{3}^{\vee}

Definition 6.3
.

For
\star\in\{\spadesuit,\clubsuit,\heartsuit,\diamondsuit\}
,
let

Theorem 6.4
.

The restriction functors

\mathbf{res}^{\star}_{\pm}\colon\mathcal{W}^{\star}\to\mathcal{D}(X_{\pm})

are equivalences of categories for each
\star\in\{\spadesuit,\clubsuit,\heartsuit,\diamondsuit\}

Fix
\star\in\{\spadesuit,\clubsuit,\heartsuit,\diamondsuit\}

By construction
\mathbf{res}^{\star}_{\pm}(\mathcal{T}^{\star})\simeq\mathcal{T}_{\pm}^{\star}
.
In addition, the natural morphism

\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{W}^{\star}}(\mathcal{T}^{\star},\mathcal{T}^{\star})\to\Ext_{X_{\pm}}^{i}(\mathcal{T}_{\pm}^{\star},\mathcal{T}_{\pm}^{\star})

is an isomorphism
for all

\mathcal{T}^{\star}
and

6.3.
Application to Calabi–Yau threefolds

Recall that there is a natural isomorphism
H^{0}(\Gr(3,5),\mathcal{U}_{3}(2))\simeq H^{0}(\Gr(2,5),\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{\vee}(2))
.
Let
s_{+}\in H^{0}(\Gr(3,5),\mathcal{U}_{3}(2))
be a section, and
s_{-}\in H^{0}(\Gr(2,5),\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{\vee}(2))
the corresponding section.
If
s_{\pm}
are regular sections, the zero loci
\bV(s_{+})\subset\Gr(3,5)
and
\bV(s_{-})\subset\Gr(2,5)
are (possibly singular) projective Calabi–Yau threefolds.
This pair
(\bV(s_{+}),\bV(s_{-}))
is called a pair of dual Calabi–Yau threefolds in
[
KR19
]
.

The following corollary was first proved in
[
KR19
, Theorem 5.7]
when the Calabi–Yau threefolds
\bV(s_{\pm})
are smooth.
The following gives a new proof via the flop and noncommutative algebra, and extends the result of
[
KR19
]
to singular situations.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
s_{+}\in H^{0}(\Gr(3,5),\mathcal{U}_{3}(2))
be a section,
and
s_{-}\in H^{0}(\Gr(2,5),\mathcal{Q}_{3}^{\vee}(2))
the corresponding section.
If
s_{\pm}
are regular sections,
then there is a derived equivalence

\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{+}))\simeq\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{-}))

of (possibly singular) Calabi–Yau threefolds.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
acts on
\Hom(S,V)
trivially, and on
\Hom(S,(\det S)^{\otimes 2})
by scaling.
This induces a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
W\coloneqq\Hom(S,V)\oplus\Hom(S,(\det S)^{\otimes 2})
,
which descends to a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq[W/\GL(S)]
.
In addition, for each stability, the stable locus is
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-invariant, and hence the above action also induces

\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-actions on
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
, which coincides with the fiber-wise scaling action.

Note that all bundles over
\mathcal{X}
of the form
\mathcal{O}\otimes V(\chi)
are
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-linearizable.
This implies that, for any
\star\in\{\spadesuit,\clubsuit,\heartsuit,\diamondsuit\}
,
the tilting bundles
\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star}
and
\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\star}
admit
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant structure such that the natural isomorphism
\End_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\star})\simeq\End_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\star})
is
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant.
Now by
[
Hir17
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
Hir21
, Theorem 1.2]
(see also
[
Shi12
,
Isi13
]
), there are equivalences

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{+}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Dcoh_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X_{+},\chi_{\id},Q_{s_{+}})\simeq\Dmod_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\Lambda_{+}^{\star},\chi_{\id},Q_{\bar{s}}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\bV(s_{-}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Dcoh_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X_{-},\chi_{\id},Q_{s_{-}})\simeq\Dmod_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\Lambda_{-}^{\star},\chi_{\id},Q_{\bar{s}}),

where the notations are as follows.

\bullet

\chi_{\id}\colon\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
is the identity map considered as a character.

\bullet

Q_{s_{\pm}}\colon X_{\pm}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
are the functions corresponding to
s_{\pm}
.

\bullet

Q_{\bar{s}}\colon\Spec R\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
is the function such that
Q_{s_{\pm}}=Q_{\bar{s}}\circ f_{\pm}
.

\bullet

\Lambda_{\pm}^{\star}=\End_{X_{\pm}}(\mathcal{T}_{\pm}^{\star})
.

\bullet

\Dcoh_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X_{\pm},\chi_{\id},Q_{s_{\pm}})
is the derived factorization category associated to the gauged linear sigma model
(X_{\pm},\chi_{\id},Q_{s_{\pm}})^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
.

\bullet

\Dmod_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\Lambda_{\pm}^{\star},\chi_{\id},Q_{\bar{s}})
is the derived factorization category associated to the noncommutative gauged linear sigma model
(\Lambda_{\pm}^{\star},\chi_{\id},Q_{\bar{s}})^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
.

Thus, using that the isomorphism

\Lambda_{+}^{\star}\simeq\Lambda_{-}^{\star}
in Corollary
5.6

is
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant, we have the result.
∎

7.
Comparison with known window categories

The aim of this section is to explain why
our construction of tilting bundles cannot be deduced from
known window categories, for instance those coming from general results of Halpern-Leistner
[
HL15
]
.
We first explicitly describe the Kempf-Ness stratification for each stability condition in our setting,
together with the associated one-parameter subgroups.
We then examine Halpern-Leistner’s window categories in detail,
and observe that the window categories we construct in §
5
and §
6.1
differ from his window categories.

7.1.
Kempf-Ness stratification

We use the notation from §
4.1
.
Consider a maximal subtorus
T
of
\GL(S)=\GL(3)
given by diagonal matrices with respect to a fixed basis of
S
.
Fixing a basis of
V\simeq\mathbb{C}^{5}
we get natural coordinates of the space
W
represented by
(B,\omega)
, where
B=\left(B_{i,j}\right)
is a
3\times 5
matrix with entries
B_{i,j}
for
i\in\{1\dots 3\}
and
j\in\{1\dots 5\}
, whereas
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
. The action of the torus
T
on
W
equipped with the chosen basis is the diagonal action with the following weights

\displaystyle w(B_{1,j})=(-1,0,0),\ w(B_{2,j})=(0,-1,0),\ w(B_{3,j})=(0,0,-1)\text{ for }j\in\{1\dots 5\}

\displaystyle w(\omega_{1})=(1,2,2),\,w(\omega_{2})=(2,1,2),\,w(\omega_{3})=(2,2,1).

We now fix a norm the standard Euclidean norm so that for
\lambda
a 1-PS of
T

\displaystyle\lambda\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}

\displaystyle\to\GL(S)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(t^{k_{1}},t^{k_{2}},t^{k_{3}})

we have
\|\lambda\|:=\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}}
. The norm is invariant with respect to the symmetric group. Moreover for a character
\rho
of
T
corresponding to a weight
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
we define
(\rho,\lambda)=r_{1}k_{1}+r_{2}k_{2}+r_{3}k_{3}
.
Now, for any point
v\in W
we recall the definition

M^{\rho}_{T}(v):=\inf_{\lambda}\frac{(\rho,\lambda)}{\|\lambda\|},

where the infimum is taken over all 1-PSs
\lambda
for which
\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)\cdot v
exists.

We consider the unstable locus of the induced action of
T
on
W
. By the Hilbert-Mumford criterion we have
v
is unstable if
M^{\rho}_{T}(v)<0
and
\lambda
is called
\rho
-adapted to
v
when

M^{\rho}_{T}(v)=\frac{(\rho,\lambda)}{\|\lambda\|}\text{ and }\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)\cdot v\text{ exists. }

We stratify the unstable locus of the action of
T
by their
\rho
-adapted 1-PSs for
\rho
induced by characters of
\GL(S)
. By a classical result of Kempf adapted to the affine case (see
[
Hos14
, Theorem 2.15]
), the Kempf-Ness stratification with respect to a character
\rho
of the action of
\GL(S)
will then be given by the
\GL(S)
-invariant subsets generated by the strata of the stratification of the unstable locus of the
T
-action with respect to the induced character of
T
. Note that some of these invariant subsets might be equal for two different strata.

Note first that the condition “
\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)\cdot v
exists” is equivalent to the condition

(\lambda,w)\geq 0
for each weight
w
corresponding to a coordinate at which
v\in W
has nonzero coefficient.

Up to scaling we essentially have two cases for the character
\rho
. These are represented by
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=(1,1,1)
or
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=(-1,-1,-1)
which correspond to the two sides of our variation of GIT.

7.1.1.
The
+
side

When
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=(-1,-1,-1)
for
v=(v_{1}\dots v_{18})=(B,\omega)\in W
we are looking for triples
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
minimising the function

f(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}):=-\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}}{\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}}}

under the conditions

(7.1)

w(v_{i})\cdot(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\geq 0\text{ for all $i$ such that }v_{i}\neq 0.

Note that to have
v
unstable we need
\frac{-k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{3}}{\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}}}<0
for some
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
satisfying condition (
7.1
). This means in particular that for
v
to be unstable the corresponding condition (
7.1
) must allow some
k_{i}
to be positive. We now make two observations:

(a)

Changing some
k_{i}
to
|k_{i}|
decreases
f
.

(b)

Changing a negative
k_{i}
to zero decreases
f
.

Now we have three possibilities for
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
depending on
v
or more precisely on the conditions (
7.1
).

(1)

All
k_{i}
are allowed to be positive, which correspond to the case
B=0
, i.e.
v=(0,\omega)
. Then the minimum is attained when
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}>0
. Indeed, by observation (a), to minimise
f
we can assume that all
k_{i}
are positive and use the inequality between the quadratic and arithmetic mean:

\frac{|k_{1}|+|k_{2}|+|k_{3}|}{3}\leq\sqrt{\frac{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}}{3}}

to see that
f(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\geq-\sqrt{3}
and equality holds exactly when
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}>0
. We hence get in this case
M(v)=-\sqrt{3}
and adapted subgroup with weight
(1,1,1)
.

(2)

Only two among
k_{i}
are allowed to be positive, say
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
. Then, by observation (b), we can set
k_{3}=0
and apply again the argument above for two variables to get
f(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\geq-\sqrt{2}
with equality holding exactly when
k_{1}=k_{2}>0
and
k_{3}=0
. We get
M(v)=-\sqrt{2}
and adapted subgroup with weights
(1,1,0)
.

(3)

Only one among
k_{i}
is allowed to be positive, say
k_{1}
. As above we set
k_{2}=k_{3}=0
and get
M(v)=-1
with adapted subgroup with weight
(1,0,0)
.

7.1.2.
The
-
side

When
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=(1,1,1)
for
v=(B,\omega)\in W
we are looking for triples
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
minimising the function

f(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}):=\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}}{\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}}}

under the same conditions (
7.1
).

In this case to have

unstable we need
\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}}{\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2}}}<0
for some
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
satisfying condition (
7.1

7.1
) must allow a solution with

\omega_{2}

Since we can always rescale the weight of the 1-PS by any positive multiple without changing the conditions as well as the 1-PSs we will assume

Now we have the following possibilities depending on

(1)

(2)

Exactly one of the coordinates

\omega_{2}

(b)

(3)

7.2.
Known window categories

Following
[
HL15

, for each element

,
define subcategories
\mathcal{G}_{+}^{w},\mathcal{G}_{-}^{w}\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})

By

, the restriction functors

\mathbf{res}_{+}^{w}\colon\mathcal{G}_{+}^{w}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}([W/G])\xrightarrow{\iota_{+}^{*}}\mathcal{D}(X_{+})

In other words, the category
\mathcal{G}_{+}^{w}
(resp.
\mathcal{G}_{-}^{w}
) is a window for
X_{+}\subset\mathcal{X}
(resp.
X_{-}\subset\mathcal{X}
).
The categories
\mathcal{G}_{+}^{w}
and
\mathcal{G}^{w}_{-}
are also known as
graded restriction categories
.

For each
+
and
-
,
define the set
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{w}
of dominant
\GL(S)
-weights by

\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{w}\coloneqq\{\chi\mid\mathcal{O}_{W}\otimes V(\chi)\in\mathcal{G}_{\pm}^{w}\}.

It follows from the equivalences
\mathbf{res}_{\pm}^{w}
that the bundles

\iota_{+}^{*}\left(\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{+}^{w}}\mathcal{O}_{W}\otimes V(\chi)\right)
over
X_{+}
and

\iota_{-}^{*}\left(\bigoplus_{\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{-}^{w}}\mathcal{O}_{W}\otimes V(\chi)\right)
over

are pretilting for any
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
.
Note that these bundles are not generators of
\D(\Qcoh X_{\pm})
in general.

7.2.1.
The
+
side

Recall that the 1-PSs associated to the KN stratification are

\lambda_{0}=(1,1,1)
,
\lambda_{1}=(1,1,0)
, and
\lambda_{2}=(1,0,0)
.

By definition, a dominant
\GL(S)
-weight
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})
is contained in
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{w}
if and only if
it satisfies

(a)

\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}+\chi_{3}\in[w_{0},w_{0}+15)
,

(b)

\chi_{\sigma(1)}+\chi_{\sigma(2)}\in[w_{1},w_{1}+8)
for all

, and

When

\mathcal{C}_{+}^{w}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(-1,-1,-1),(0,-1,-1),(0,0,-1),(0,0,0),(1,-1,-1),\\

and the bundle

is a tilting bundle that coincides (up to line bundle twist) with the pull-back of the direct sum of the Kapranov’s collection (§

).
However, the bundle over

given by the other restriction

).

Remark 7.1
.

For any

.

The

Recall that the 1-PSs associated to the KN stratification are

\lambda_{0}=(-1,-1,-1)
,
\lambda_{1}=(1,1,-4)
, and
\lambda_{2}=(1,0,-2)
.

By definition, a dominant
\GL(S)
-weight
\chi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3})
is contained in
\mathcal{C}_{-}^{w}
if and only if
it satisfies

(a
′
)

-\chi_{1}-\chi_{2}-\chi_{3}\in[w_{0},w_{0}+15)

(b
′
)

\chi_{\sigma(1)}+\chi_{\sigma(2)}-4\chi_{\sigma(3)}\in[w_{1},w_{1}+10)

(c

)

\chi_{\sigma(1)}-2\chi_{\sigma(3)}\in[w_{2},w_{2}+4)

Now it follows from an elementary mathematics that:

Lemma 7.2
.

For
w=(w_{0},w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
,
the set
\mathcal{C}_{-}^{w}
of weights is a subset of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(-w_{2}-3,-w_{2}-3,-w_{2}-3),(-w_{2}-2,-w_{2}-2,-w_{2}-2),(-w_{2}-1,-w_{2}-1,-w_{2}-1),\\

weights for any

Let
\chi
be a dominant
\GL(3)
-weight, and set
\chi=(x+a+b,x+b,x)

and

Then the condition (c
′
) implies that, if

are both contained in the interval

must be at most

.
This implies that
(0\leq)~a+b\leq 1
.

Thus the weight
\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{-}^{w}
is one of the following forms:

(1)

\chi=(x,x,x)
for some
x\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(2)

\chi=(x+1,x,x)
for some
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, or

(3)

\chi=(x+1,x+1,x)
for some
x\in\mathbb{Z}

\chi=(x,x,x)
for some

, then the condition (c

) implies that

Therefore
\chi

(-w_{2}+k,-w_{2}+k,-w_{2}+k)

\chi=(x+1,x,x)

If

As a consequence, the pretilting bundle

,

Appendix A
On fully faithful functors

This section collects two propositions, which will be well-known to experts.
We provide a full proof of those propositions, since we could not find references.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be an exact functor between triangulated categories.
Assume that
\mathcal{A}
admits a classical generator
G\in\mathcal{A}
such that the homomorphism

\Hom(G,G[i])\to\Hom(\Phi G,\Phi G[i])

associated to
\Phi
is an isomorphism for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
\Phi
is fully faithful.

Proof.

Define subcategories
\langle G\rangle_{n}\subset\mathcal{C}
for
n\geq 0
as in
[
BFK12
, §2]
.
Since
G
is a classical generator,
\mathcal{A}=\thick(G)=\cup_{i\geq 0}\langle G\rangle_{n}
.
We claim that, for any non-negative integer
n\geq 0
,
the natural homomorphism

\Hom(x^{\prime},x)\to\Hom(\Phi x^{\prime},\Phi x)

is an isomorphism for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\langle G\rangle_{n}

n
.

The case
n=0
follows from the assumption.

Let us assume that
n>0
, and that the claim holds for
n-1
.
Fix an arbitrary object
x\in\langle G\rangle_{n}
.

Then there exist
x_{1}\in\langle G\rangle_{n}
,
y\in\langle G\rangle_{n-1}
,
z\in\langle G\rangle_{0}
,
and an exact triangle

y\to x\oplus x_{1}\to z\to y[1].

Let

be an arbitrary object,
and consider the following commutative diagram of long exact sequences.

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Therefore the five lemma implies that the middle one is also an isomorphism.
As a consequence, it has followed that for any

,
the morphism
\Hom(y^{\prime},x)\to\Hom(\Phi y^{\prime},\Phi x)
is isomorphic.

Similarly, let
x^{\prime}\in\langle G\rangle_{n}
be an arbitrary object, and assume that there exists an exact triangle

Since

are arbitrary, the claim for

follows.
∎

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be an exact fully faithful functor between triangulated categories.
Assume that the category
\mathcal{A}
is idempotent-complete.
Then the essential image of
\Phi
is closed under taking direct summands.
(In other words, the essential image is a thick subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
.)

Proof.

Assume that
\Phi x\simeq y\oplus z
for
x\in\mathcal{A}
and
y,z\in\mathcal{B}
.
Let
e_{y}\colon\Phi x\to\Phi x
be an idempotent associated to the summand
y
.
Since
\Phi
is fully faithful,
there is a unique morphism
e\colon x\to x
such that
\Phi(e)=e_{y}
, and this
e
remains an idempotent.
Then the idempotent-completeness of
\mathcal{A}
implies that there are
w,u\in\mathcal{A}
such that
x\simeq w\oplus u

and the idempotent
e
coincides with the composite
x\twoheadrightarrow w\hookrightarrow x
.
Now it is standard that
\Phi w\simeq y
.
Therefore the essential image of
\Phi
is closed under taking direct summands.

Symmetric quiver varieties and critical stable envelopes
Yalong Cao, Andrei Okounkov, Yehao Zhou, Zijun Zhou

Abstract
. Symmetric quiver varieties with potentials are natural generalizations of Nakajima quiver varieties, and their equivariant critical cohomologies provide more flexible settings for geometric representation theory and enumerative geometry. In this paper, we study their geometric properties and show that they behave like universally deformed Nakajima quiver varieties. Based on this, we provide a new proof of the existence of critical stable envelopes on them. Following an idea of Nakajima, we give a sheaf theoretic interpretation of critical stable envelopes by the hyperbolic restriction in the affinization of symmetric quiver varieties. The associativity of hyperbolic restrictions implies the triangle lemma of critical stable envelopes.

2.
Definitions of symmetric quiver varieties

A
quiver

Q
is a pair of finite sets
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
together with two maps
h,t\colon Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
. We will call
Q_{0}
the set of nodes and
Q_{1}
the set of arrows and
h
(resp.
t
) sends an arrow to its head (resp. tail). If
a\in Q_{1}
, then we will write
t(a)\to h(a)
to denote the arrow
a
. We define the

adjacency matrix

\displaystyle(\mathsf{Q}_{ij})_{i,j\in Q_{0}}=\#(i\to j)

and
Cartan matrix

\displaystyle(\mathsf{C}_{ij})_{i,j\in Q_{0}}=2\delta_{ij}-\mathsf{Q}_{ij}-\mathsf{Q}_{ji}.

Take dimension vectors
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
,

\underline{\mathbf{d}}=(\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{in}},\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{out}})\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\times\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
, the
space of framed representations
of
Q
with
gauge dimension

\mathbf{v}
and
in-coming framing dimension

\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{in}}
and
out-going framing dimension

\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{out}}
is

(2.1)

\displaystyle R(\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}}):=R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}})=\bigoplus_{a\in Q_{1}}\underbrace{\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{t(a)}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{h(a)}})}_{X_{a}}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\left(\underbrace{\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{in},i}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{i}})}_{A_{i}}\oplus\underbrace{\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{i}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{d}_{\operatorname{out},i}})}_{B_{i}}\right).

The
gauge group

G=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathrm{GL}(\mathbf{v}_{i})
naturally acts on
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}})
by compositions with maps.

Choose a stability condition
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}^{Q_{0}}
such that
\theta
-semistable representations are
\theta
-stable:

R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}})^{ss}=R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}})^{s}\neq\emptyset,

and define the
quiver variety
as the GIT quotient:

\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}}):=\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}}):=R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}})/\!\!/_{\theta}G=R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}})^{s}/G.

As we do not impose any relation on the quiver, the above space is a smooth quasi-projective variety.

We define an action of

(2.2)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{edge}}:=\prod_{i,j\in Q_{0}}\mathrm{GL}(\mathsf{Q}_{ij})

on
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}})
: given a pair of nodes
i,j\in Q_{0}
, the contribution of the edges from
i
to
j
is
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{i}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{j}})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{Q}_{ij}}
, then the factor
\mathrm{GL}(\mathsf{Q}_{ij})
naturally acts on the second component.

We also consider the following group actions on
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}})
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{fram}}^{\mathrm{in}}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathrm{GL}(\mathbf{d}_{\operatorname{in},i})\curvearrowright\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{d}_{\operatorname{in},i}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{i}}),\quad G_{\mathrm{fram}}^{\mathrm{out}}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathrm{GL}(\mathbf{d}_{\operatorname{out},i})\curvearrowright\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{v}_{i}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathbf{d}_{\operatorname{out},i}}).

We define the
flavour group

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}:=G_{\mathrm{fram}}^{\mathrm{in}}\times G_{\mathrm{fram}}^{\mathrm{out}}\times G_{\mathrm{edge}}.

It is easy to see that
\mathsf{F}\cong\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}}))
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a framing
\underline{\mathbf{d}}
is
symmetric
if
\mathbf{d}_{\operatorname{in}}=\mathbf{d}_{\operatorname{out}}=\mathbf{d}
. In this case, we simplify the notations as

(2.5)

R(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})=R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})=R(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}}),\quad\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})=\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})=\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{v},\underline{\mathbf{d}}),

and we define
G_{\mathrm{fram}}^{\mathrm{\operatorname{diag}}}\subseteq\mathsf{F}
to be the diagonal subgroup of
G_{\mathrm{fram}}^{\mathrm{in}}\times G_{\mathrm{fram}}^{\mathrm{out}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say
Q
is
symmetric
if its adjacency matrix
(\mathsf{Q}_{ij})_{i,j\in Q_{0}}
is symmetric.
For a symmetric quiver
Q
, we call the associated quiver variety
symmetric quiver variety
(SQV) if the framing is symmetric.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
be a symmetric quiver with symmetric framing as above. Suppose that there exists a torus
\mathsf{H}
together with a linear action of
\mathsf{H}
on
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
. We say that a linear action of a torus
\mathsf{H}
on
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
is
self-dual
if the
\mathsf{H}
-action commutes with the gauge group
G
-action, and
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
is self-dual as
(G\times\mathsf{H})
-representation.

We say that a torus action
\mathsf{H}
on the symmetric quiver variety
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
is
self-dual
if it is induced from a self-dual action of
\mathsf{H}
on
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
.

The following lemma is elementary, and we leave the proof to interested readers.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
be a symmetric quiver with symmetric framing as above. Suppose that there exists a torus
\mathsf{H}
together with a self-dual action on
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
. Then there exists a decomposition of the set of arrows
Q_{1}=\mathcal{A}\sqcup\mathcal{A}^{*}\sqcup\mathcal{E}
with the following properties

(1)

There is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, which sends an arrow
a\in\mathcal{A}
to an arrow
a^{*}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
with opposite direction, i.e.
t(a)=h(a^{*})
and
h(a)=t(a^{*})
;

(2)

\mathcal{E}
is a set of edge loops, i.e.
t(\varepsilon)=h(\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}
;

(3)

Consider the torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\mathcal{A}}
which maps
(X_{a},X_{a^{*}})_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
to
(t_{a}X_{a},t_{a}^{-1}X_{a^{*}})_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
and acts on
(X_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}}
and
(A_{i},B_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
trivially, then there exists a maximal torus
T_{\mathrm{fram}}\subseteq G_{\mathrm{fram}}^{\mathrm{diag}}
and an element
g\in G_{\mathrm{edge}}
such that
\mathsf{H}
-action factors through the subgroup
T_{\mathrm{fram}}\times g(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\mathcal{A}}g^{-1}\subseteq\mathsf{F}
.

The
\mathsf{A}
-fixed loci of a symmetric quiver variety are symmetric quiver varieties.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
COZZ1
, Lem. A.7]

Suppose that
X=\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
is a symmetric quiver variety, and
\mathsf{A}
is a torus with a self-dual action on
X
. Let
\sigma
be a cocharacter of
\mathsf{A}
, then the
\sigma
-fixed points locus

is a disjoint union of symmetric quiver varieties. Moreover, the induced action of
\mathsf{A}
on

is self-dual.

\mathsf{JH}\colon X=\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})\to\mathcal{M}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})=Y

maps
\theta
-stable quiver representation to its semisimplification, where
\mathcal{M}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})=R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})/\!\!/G
is the affine quotient (
G=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathrm{GL}(\mathbf{v}_{i})
). Geometrically,
\mathsf{JH}
is induced from the open immersion
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})^{\theta\emph{-}s}\hookrightarrow R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
by taking
G
quotients on both sides.

Lemma 3.19
.

\mathsf{JH}
is proper and birational. Moreover,
\mathbb{C}[Y]=\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
, i.e.
Y
is the affinization of
X
.

Proof.

By geometric invariant theory,
\mathsf{JH}
is proper. Applying the Quantization Theorem
[
HL
, Thm. 3.29]
to the case
F^{\bullet}=G^{\bullet}=
structure sheaf of the stack
[R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})/G]
, we see that the natural map

(3.27)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[Y]=H^{0}([R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})/G],\mathcal{O}_{[R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})/G]})\to H^{0}(R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})^{\theta\emph{-}s}/G,\mathcal{O}_{[R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})/G]})=\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})

is an isomorphism. Since
Y
is affine, (
3.27
) implies that the natural map
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\mathsf{JH}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is an isomorphism.

The map
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})\subset R(Q^{\mathrm{add}},\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})\to\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
induced from the bottom of (
3.7
) is
G
-invariant, and it descends to a morphism
\bar{\tau}\colon Y=\mathcal{M}_{0}(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})\to\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
. We have
\tau=\bar{\tau}\circ\mathsf{JH}
by construction. Recall the open subset
\mathcal{H}^{\circ}
defined in (
3.23
), and define
Y^{\circ}:=\bar{\tau}^{-1}(\mathcal{H}^{\circ}/\Gamma)
. Then
\mathsf{JH}\colon X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
is proper and affine, and therefore finite. Then it follows from the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong\mathsf{JH}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
that
\mathsf{JH}\colon X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
is an isomorphism. In particular,
\mathsf{JH}
is birational.
∎

The main result of this subsection is the following.

Proposition 3.20
.

All irreducible components of
X\times_{Y}X
except the diagonal
\Delta_{X}
have dimension strictly smaller than
\dim X
. In particular,
\dim X\times_{Y}X=\dim X
, and
\mathsf{JH}\colon X\to Y
is small.

Proof.

As we have seen in the proof of Lemma
3.19
,
\mathsf{JH}
induces isomorphism
X^{\circ}\cong Y^{\circ}
; therefore

X\times_{Y}X\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\mathcal{H}^{\circ}/\Gamma\cong X^{\circ}\times_{Y^{\circ}}X^{\circ}=\Delta_{X^{\circ}}.

On the other hand, we claim that for any
b\in\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
, the dimension of the fiber
X\times_{Y}X\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{b\}
is equal to
\dim X_{b}
, where
X_{b}:=\tau^{-1}(b)
. By the flatness of
\tau
(Proposition
3.16
), it is enough to show

\dim X\times_{Y}X\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{b\}\leqslant\dim X_{0}.

Consider the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action on
R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
that scales the whole vector space with weight
1
. This
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action commutes with
G
-action, and therefore it induces a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action on the GIT quotient
X
as well as on the affine quotient
Y
. Note that
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action on
Y
contracts the latter to the unique fixed point
\mathfrak{o}
which is the image of
\{0\}\in R(Q,\mathbf{v},\mathbf{d})
. By the properness of
\mathsf{JH}\colon X\to Y
,
\forall\,\,x\in X
,
\lim_{t\to 0}t\cdot x
exists and belongs to
\mathsf{JH}^{-1}(\mathfrak{o})
. Moreover
\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts on
\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
with positive weights, such that
\tau\colon X\to\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant. This implies that
\lim_{t\to 0}t\cdot x\in X_{0}
.

Take an arbitrary irreducible component
W
of
X\times_{Y}X\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{b\}
, let
W^{\prime}
be the closure or
\mathbb{C}^{*}
orbits of
W
in
X\times_{Y}X
, i.e.
W^{\prime}=\overline{\mathbb{C}^{*}\cdot W}
. Then
W^{\prime}_{0}:=W^{\prime}\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{0\}
is nonempty by the above argument, and
\dim W^{\prime}_{0}\geqslant\dim W
as the former is a special fiber and the latter is the generic fiber. Since
Y_{0}:=\bar{\tau}^{-1}(0)
is the affine Nakajima quiver variety
\mathcal{N}_{0}(Q^{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{aux}})=(\mu^{\mathrm{aux}})^{-1}(0)/\!\!/G^{\mathrm{aux}}
and the composition
\mathsf{JH}\circ\pi_{X}\colon\widetilde{X}_{0}\to Y_{0}
is identified with the Jordan-Hölder map of Nakajima quiver varieties:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\theta^{\delta}}(Q^{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{aux}})\to\mathcal{N}_{0}(Q^{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{aux}}),

we see that
\widetilde{X}_{0}\to Y_{0}
is a symplectic resolution, which is semismall
[
Kal
]
. Then the claim follows from

\displaystyle\dim W\leqslant\dim W^{\prime}_{0}\leqslant\dim X_{0}\times_{Y_{0}}X_{0}\leqslant\dim\widetilde{X}_{0}\times_{Y_{0}}\widetilde{X}_{0}=\dim\widetilde{X}_{0}=\dim X_{0}\,.

Let
Z\subset X\times_{Y}X
be an irreducible component which is different from the diagonal
\Delta_{X}
. Suppose
Z^{\circ}:=Z\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\mathcal{H}^{\circ}/\Gamma
is nonempty, then
Z
is the closure of
Z^{\circ}
. Since
X^{\circ}\times_{Y^{\circ}}X^{\circ}=\Delta_{X^{\circ}}
,
Z^{\circ}
is contained in
\Delta_{X^{\circ}}
. This implies that
Z\subseteq\Delta_{X}
, and contradicts with the choice of
Z
. Thus
Z^{\circ}=\emptyset
. Then it follows from the above claim that
\dim Z\leqslant\dim X_{b}+\dim(\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\circ})/\Gamma=\dim X-1
.
∎

The restriction of
\mathsf{JH}
to a torus fixed component is also a birational and small morphism onto its image.

Proposition 3.21
.

Suppose that
\sigma
is a cocharacter of
\mathsf{A}
, and
F\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\sigma}(X)
. Then the morphism

\mathsf{JH}|_{F}\colon F\to Y

is birational onto its image. Moreover, all irreducible components of
F\times_{Y}F
except the diagonal
\Delta_{F}
have dimension strictly smaller than
\dim F
. In particular,
\dim F\times_{Y}F=\dim F
, and
\mathsf{JH}\colon F\to Y
is small onto its image.

Proof.

By the flatness of
\tau|_{F}\colon F\to\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
(Proposition
3.18
),
F^{\circ}:=F\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\mathcal{H}^{\circ}/\Gamma
is nonempty. Since
\mathsf{JH}\colon X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
is an isomorphism,
F^{\circ}
is mapped isomorphically onto its image in
Y^{\circ}
. In particular,
\mathsf{JH}|_{F}\colon F\to Y
is birational onto its image.

Let
K\subset F\times_{Y}F
be an irreducible component which is different from the diagonal
\Delta_{F}
. The same argument as in the proof of Proposition
3.20
shows that
K\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\mathcal{H}^{\circ}/\Gamma
is empty. Then we have

\displaystyle\dim K

\displaystyle\leqslant\dim\mathcal{H}/\Gamma-1+\sup_{b\in\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\dim F\times_{Y}F\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{b\}

by (
3.28
)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dim\mathcal{H}/\Gamma-1+\frac{1}{2}(\dim F+\dim F)-\dim\mathcal{H}/\Gamma

\displaystyle=\dim F-1.\qed

3.6.
Some dimension estimates

Next we give several estimates on the dimensions of intersections of torus fixed loci of symmetric quiver varieties. They will be used in
[
COZZ2
]
.

Lemma 3.22
.

Suppose that
\sigma
is a cocharacter of
\mathsf{A}
, and
F_{1},F_{2}\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\sigma}(X)
. Then for any
b\in\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
,

(3.28)

\displaystyle\dim F_{1}\times_{Y}F_{2}\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{b\}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}(\dim F_{1}+\dim F_{2})-\dim\mathcal{H}/\Gamma.

Proof.

By the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
contraction argument in the proof of Proposition
3.20
,

\dim F_{1}\times_{Y}F_{2}\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{b\}\leqslant\dim F_{1}\times_{Y}F_{2}\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{0\},

so it is enough to prove (
3.28
) for
b=0
.

For
i=1,2
, let
\widetilde{F}_{i}
be the
\sigma
-fixed component of
\widetilde{X}
that dominates
F_{i}
(see the proof of Proposition
3.18
). Since
\widetilde{F}_{i}\to F_{i}
is generically finite, we have
\dim\widetilde{F}_{i}=\dim F_{i}
. Define
F_{i,0}:=F_{i}\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{0\}
and
\widetilde{F}_{i,0}:=\widetilde{F}_{i}\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{0\}
for
i=1,2
. There is a naturally induced proper surjective morphism
\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}\widetilde{F}_{2,0}\to{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}{F}_{2,0}
, so

\dim{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}{F}_{2,0}\leqslant\dim\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}\widetilde{F}_{2,0}.

Note that
\sigma
preserves the symplectic form
\Omega
on the Nakajima quiver variety
\widetilde{X}_{0}=\mathcal{N}_{\theta^{\delta}}(Q^{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{v}^{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{d}^{\mathrm{aux}})
, so the restriction of
\Omega
to
\sigma
-fixed components
\widetilde{F}_{1,0}
and
\widetilde{F}_{2,0}
are nondegenerate. Let us equip
\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times\widetilde{F}_{2,0}
with the symplectic structure
\Omega_{12}:=\Omega|_{\widetilde{F}_{1,0}}\boxminus\Omega|_{\widetilde{F}_{2,0}}
. Then we claim that the subvariety
\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}\widetilde{F}_{2,0}\subset\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times\widetilde{F}_{2,0}
is isotropic with respect to
\Omega_{12}
. As we have seen in the proof of Proposition
3.20
,
\mathsf{JH}\circ\pi_{X}\colon\widetilde{X}_{0}\to Y_{0}
is a symplectic resolution. Let
W
be an arbitrary irreducible component of
\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}\widetilde{F}_{2,0}
with reduced scheme structure. Let
\eta_{1}
be the composition
W\to\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\to\widetilde{X}_{0}
, and let
\eta_{2}
be the composition
W\to\widetilde{F}_{2,0}\to\widetilde{X}_{0}
, then

\mathsf{JH}\circ\pi_{X}\circ\eta_{1}=\mathsf{JH}\circ\pi_{X}\circ\eta_{2}.

Denote
V
to be the image of
q\colon W\to Y_{0}
where
q=\mathsf{JH}\circ\pi_{X}\circ\eta_{1}
, and we shall still denote the morphism
W\to V
by
q
. By
[
Kal
, Lem. 2.9]
, we can replace
V
and
W
by open subvarieties
U
and
Z
respectively, such that
U
and
Z
are smooth and
q\colon Z\to U
is smooth, and
\eta_{1}^{*}\Omega=q^{*}\Omega_{U,1}
and
\eta_{2}^{*}\Omega=q^{*}\Omega_{U,2}
, for some
\Omega_{U,1},\Omega_{U,2}\in\Omega^{2}(U)
. By the construction in the proof of
[
Kal
, Lem. 2.9]
, for
i=1,2
,
\Omega_{U,i}
only depends on the morphism
U\to Y_{0}
and does not depend on
\eta_{i}
, so we have
\Omega_{U,1}=\Omega_{U,2}
. It follows that the restriction of
\Omega_{12}
to
Z
equals to
\eta_{1}^{*}\Omega-\eta_{2}^{*}\Omega=0
. This implies that
W
is isotropic, and therefore
\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}\widetilde{F}_{2,0}
is isotropic.

By the isotropic property,
\dim\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}\widetilde{F}_{2,0}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\dim\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times\widetilde{F}_{2,0}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\dim{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}{F}_{2,0}\leqslant\dim\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times_{Y_{0}}\widetilde{F}_{2,0}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\dim\widetilde{F}_{1,0}\times\widetilde{F}_{2,0}

(by the smoothness of
\widetilde{F}_{i,0}\to\mathcal{H}
)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\dim\widetilde{F}_{1}+\dim\widetilde{F}_{2})-\dim\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\dim{F}_{1}+\dim{F}_{2})-\dim\mathcal{H}/\Gamma.\qed

Proposition 3.23
.

Suppose that
\sigma
is a cocharacter of
\mathsf{A}
, and
F_{1},F_{2}\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\sigma}(X)
. Let
Z\subset X\times_{Y}X
be an irreducible component which is different from the diagonal
\Delta_{X}
. Then

(3.29)

\displaystyle\dim(F_{1}\times F_{2})\cap Z\leqslant\frac{1}{2}(\dim F_{1}+\dim F_{2})-1.

Proof.

Let
T
be an irreducible component of
(F_{1}\times F_{2})\cap Z
with maximal dimension and denote
T_{b}:=T\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{b\}
for any
b\in\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
. Since
T_{b}\subseteq F_{1}\times_{Y}F_{2}\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\{b\}
, we have
\dim T_{b}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}(\dim F_{1}+\dim F_{2})-\dim\mathcal{H}/\Gamma
by Lemma
3.22
. In the proof of Proposition
3.20
we have shown that
Z\times_{\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\mathcal{H}^{\circ}/\Gamma
is empty, so

\dim(F_{1}\times F_{2})\cap Z\leqslant\dim\mathcal{H}/\Gamma-1+\sup_{b\in\mathcal{H}/\Gamma}\dim T_{b}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}(\dim F_{1}+\dim F_{2})-1.\qed

Remark 3.24
.

If
F_{1}\neq F_{2}
, then
(F_{1}\times F_{2})\cap\Delta_{X}=\emptyset
, and it follows from Proposition
3.23
that

(3.30)

\displaystyle\dim F_{1}\times_{Y}F_{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}(\dim F_{1}+\dim F_{2})-1.

4.
Stable envelope correspondences for symmetric quiver varieties

4.1.
Definitions

We say that a cocharacter
\sigma\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathsf{A}
is
generic
if
X^{\sigma}=X^{\mathsf{A}}
. Let us recall the wall-and-chamber structure on
\operatorname{Lie}(\mathsf{A})_{\mathbb{R}}
described in
[
COZZ1
]
, such that
\sigma
is generic if and only if it lies in a chamber.

The
torus roots
are the set of
\mathsf{A}
-weights
\{\alpha\}
occurring in the normal bundle to
X^{\mathsf{A}}
.
A connected component of the complement of union of (finite) root hyperplanes is called a
chamber
, i.e.

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lie}(\mathsf{A})_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg\backslash\bigcup_{\alpha\in\text{roots}}\alpha^{\perp}=\bigsqcup_{j}\mathfrak{C}_{j},

where
\mathfrak{C}_{j}
are chambers.

For an algebraic variety
M
with an
\mathsf{A}
-action, and a cocharacter
\sigma
, let
S
be a subset of
M^{\sigma}
, the
attracting set
is

\operatorname{Attr}_{\sigma}(S):=\{x\in M\mid\lim_{t\to 0}\sigma(t)\cdot x\in S\}.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a chamber as above, we define

\displaystyle\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}(S):=\operatorname{Attr}_{\xi}(S)

for a subset
S\subseteq X^{\mathsf{A}}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{C}
. Note that the definition does not depend on the choice of
\xi
.

Let
F\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathsf{A}}(X)
be a connected component of the
\mathsf{A}
-fixed locus, then

\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}(F)
is a locally closed subscheme in
X
, and the attraction map

\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}(F)\to F,\quad x\mapsto\lim_{t\to 0}\xi(t)\cdot x,

is an
affine fibration
by the result of Bialynicki–Birula
[
BB
]
.

The normal bundle of
F
in
X
decomposes into
\mathsf{A}
-eigen sub-bundles:

\displaystyle N_{F/X}=\bigoplus_{\alpha}N_{F/X}^{\alpha},

such that
\mathsf{A}
acts on
N_{F/X}^{\alpha}
with weight
\alpha
. Define

\displaystyle N_{F/X}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\alpha(\xi)>0}N_{F/X}^{\alpha}\,,\quad N_{F/X}^{-}=\bigoplus_{\alpha(\xi)<0}N_{F/X}^{\alpha}\,,

for some
\xi\in\mathfrak{C}
(equivalently, for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{C}
).

Consider a partial order on
\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathsf{A}}(X)
which is the transitive closure of the following relation:

(4.1)

\displaystyle F_{i}\preceq F_{j}\quad\text{if}\quad F_{j}\cap\overline{\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}(F_{i})}\neq\emptyset.

The
full attracting set
is defined as

\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{f}(F):=\bigcup_{F\preceq F^{\prime}}\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}(F^{\prime}).

We denote by
\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{f}
the smallest
\mathsf{A}
-invariant closed subset of
X\times X^{\mathsf{A}}
such that
\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{f}
contains the diagonal
\Delta\subseteq X^{\mathsf{A}}\times X^{\mathsf{A}}
and

\displaystyle(x^{\prime},y)\in\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{f}\text{ and }\lim_{t\to 0}\sigma(t)\cdot x=x^{\prime}\text{ implies }(x,y)\in\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{f}.

By definition,
\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{f}
is a subset of
\bigcup_{F\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathsf{A}}(X)}\operatorname{Attr}_{\mathfrak{C}}^{f}(F)\times F\subseteq X\times X^{\mathsf{A}}
.
Note also that
\operatorname{Attr}^{f}_{\mathfrak{C}}\cap\,(F\times F)=\Delta_{F}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix a chamber
\mathfrak{C}
as above. A
stable envelope correspondence
is a
\mathsf{T}
-equivariant Borel-Moore homology class supported on
\operatorname{Attr}^{f}_{\mathfrak{C}}
:

[\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathfrak{C}}]\in H^{\mathsf{T}}(X\times X^{\mathsf{A}})_{\operatorname{Attr}^{f}_{\mathfrak{C}}}

which satisfies the following two axioms:

(i)

For any fixed component
F\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathsf{A}}(X)
,
[\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathfrak{C}}]\big|_{F\times F}=e^{\mathsf{T}}(N_{F/X}^{-})\cdot[\Delta_{F}]
for diagonal
\Delta_{F}\subseteq F\times F
;

(ii)

For any
F^{\prime}\neq F
, the inequality
\deg_{\mathsf{A}}[\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathfrak{C}}]\big|_{F^{\prime}\times F}<\deg_{\mathsf{A}}e^{\mathsf{T}}(N_{F^{\prime}/X}^{-})
holds.

Remark 4.2
.

(i) It follows from the definition that stable envelope correspondence is unique if it exists
[
COZZ1
, Prop. 3.21]
.
(ii) As noted in
[
COZZ1
, Prop. 3.31]
, for any
\mathsf{T}
-invariant regular function
\mathsf{w}\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
, by applying the canonical map
[
COZZ1
, Eqn. (2.8)]
:

(4.2)

\operatorname{can}\colon H^{\mathsf{T}}(X\times X^{\mathsf{A}})_{\operatorname{Attr}^{f}_{\mathfrak{C}}}\to H^{\mathsf{T}}(X\times X^{\mathsf{A}},\mathsf{w}\boxminus\mathsf{w})_{\operatorname{Attr}^{f}_{\mathfrak{C}}}

to the stable envelope correspondence
[\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathfrak{C}}]
and using convolutions, we get the critical stable envelope

\operatorname{Stab}_{\mathfrak{C}}\colon H^{\mathsf{T}}(X^{\mathsf{A}},\mathsf{w})\to H^{\mathsf{T}}(X,\mathsf{w}).

4.2.
Existence of stable envelope correspondence

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})
be the closure of the attracting set of the diagonal
\Delta_{F}\subset F\times F
in
X\times F
. Then

\sum_{F\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathsf{A}}(X)}[\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})]
is a stable envelope correspondence.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for every
F\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathsf{A}}(X)
,
[\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})]
satisfies axioms (i) and (ii) in Definition
4.1
. The axiom (i) is obvious. For axiom (ii), the class
(F^{\prime}\times F\hookrightarrow X\times F)^{*}[\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})]
is supported on the subvariety
\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})\cap(F^{\prime}\times F)
whose dimension is strictly smaller than
\frac{1}{2}(\dim F+\dim F^{\prime})
by Lemma
4.4
below. Therefore

\displaystyle\deg_{\mathsf{A}}(F^{\prime}\times F\hookrightarrow X\times F)^{*}[\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})]

\displaystyle\leqslant\dim\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})\cap(F^{\prime}\times F)-\left(\dim\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})+\dim(F^{\prime}\times F)-\dim(X\times F)\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}(\dim F+\dim F^{\prime})-\left(\dim\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})+\dim(F^{\prime}\times F)-\dim(X\times F)\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rk}N^{-}_{F^{\prime}/X}=\deg_{\mathsf{A}}e^{\mathsf{T}}(N_{F^{\prime}/X}^{-}).

This verifies the axiom (ii).
∎

In the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we have used the following.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
F,F^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathsf{A}}(X)
be two
distinct
fixed components. Then

\displaystyle\dim\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})\cap(F^{\prime}\times F)<\frac{1}{2}(\dim F+\dim F^{\prime}).

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{F}^{\prime},\widetilde{F}\in\operatorname{Fix}_{\mathsf{A}}(\widetilde{X})
be connected components of
\widetilde{X}^{\mathsf{A}}
such that
\pi_{X}|_{\widetilde{F}}\colon\widetilde{F}\to F
and
\pi_{X}|_{\widetilde{F}^{\prime}}\colon\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\to F^{\prime}
are proper, surjective, and generically finite, their existence are shown in the proof of Proposition
3.18
. Then we have an induced proper and surjective map

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{\widetilde{F}})\cap(\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\times\widetilde{F})\to\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})\cap(F^{\prime}\times F)

where
\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{\widetilde{F}})
is the closure of the attracting set of the diagonal
\Delta_{\widetilde{F}}\subset\widetilde{F}\times\widetilde{F}
in
\widetilde{X}\times\widetilde{F}
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\dim\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{F})\cap(F^{\prime}\times F)\leqslant\dim\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{\widetilde{F}})\cap(\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\times\widetilde{F}).

Let
Z
be an irreducible component of
\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{\widetilde{F}})\cap(\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\times\widetilde{F})
. For a morphism
M\to\mathcal{H}
, define
M^{\circ}=M\times_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{H}^{\circ}
and
M_{h}=M\times_{\mathcal{H}}\{h\}
for
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
\widetilde{X}^{\circ}
is an affine variety (see Example
3.2
),
{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{\widetilde{F}^{\circ}})
is closed in
\widetilde{X}^{\circ}\times\widetilde{F}^{\circ}
. It follows that
\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{\widetilde{F}})\cap(\widetilde{F}^{\prime\circ}\times\widetilde{F}^{\circ})=\emptyset
, in particular,
Z^{\circ}=\emptyset
.

We claim that
\overline{\operatorname{Attr}}_{\mathfrak{C}}(\Delta_{\widetilde{F}})_{0}
is an isotropic subvariety of
\widetilde{X}_{0}\times\widetilde{F}_{0}
, where the latter is endowed with the symplectic form
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega\boxminus\Omega|_{\widetilde{F}_{0}}
(
\Omega

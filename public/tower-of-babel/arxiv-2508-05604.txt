Consistency of an Intercept-Shifted Synthetic-Control Estimator under Weighted Parallel Trends
Michael Guggisberg

Abstract
The average treatment effect on the treated (ATT) in a staggered-adoption panel is estimated using an intercept-augmented synthetic-control (SCM) estimator. A weighted parallel trends plus an intercept shift, together with mild regularity on the weight vectors (non-degenerate dispersion) and expanding pre-treatment length, are sufficient for consistency allowing for heavy-tailed shocks. These conditions can be more interpretable than the autoregressive or low-rank factor models with light tails assumed by Ben-Michael, Feller, and Rothstein (2022) and expand the valid DGP pool from the same paper. Practical diagnostics to support the assumptions are discussed and situate these results within the recent literature on SC + DiD hybrids.

Introduction

The synthetic-control method

(
Abadie et al., 2010
;
Abadie et al., 2015
)

and the multi-period difference-in-differences (DiD) family

(

One can retain the attractive

SCM while identifying ATT under a simpler, DiD-style assumption.
The key ingredient is to add an

conditions can be assessed with the usual placebo-gap plots, weight

argue for AR(
L
) or factor models.

Two papers point in the same direction:

Doudchenko and Imbens, 2016

Arkhangelsky et al., 2021
introduce “synthetic DiD” in a
simultaneous-adoption setting. To author’s knowledge, no published work provides (i) a finite-sample error

Assumption 1

(No anticipation)
.

For every

Assumption 2

(No interference)
.

Let
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{N})

\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=(s^{\prime}_{1},\dots,s^{\prime}_{N})
be two adoption calendars.
If

Assumption
3
is a weighted version of the usual parallel-trends hypothesis. It replaces the structural restrictions imposted in
SCSA
, time-varying ARL(L) (their Assumption 2a) or a low‑rank factor structure (2b)—with a design‑type restriction that is already central to the DiD literature. The idea is to search—not over parametric models—but over weight vectors that make the (possibly heterogeneous) donor tend “look like” unit
j
.

Assumption 3

(Weighted parallel trends)
.

For each treated unit
j
there exists

(w_{1j},\dots,w_{Nj})
such that for
every
t<T_{j}

\sum_{i}w_{ij}\,\mathbb{E}\!\bigl[\Delta Y_{it}^{0}\bigr]\;=\;\mathbb{E}\!\bigl[\Delta Y_{jt}^{0}\bigr],

where
\Delta Y_{it}^{0}:=Y_{it}^{0}-Y_{i,t-1}^{0}
.

Assumption
3
implicitly requires that the donor pool be rich enough to find weights that replicate the treated units’ pre-treatment trajectory. The moment conditions can only be satisfied if the stacked vector of lagged outcomes for the treated units lies (approximately) in the convex hull of the corresponding vectors for the donors. If the donor pool is too small, or if its members follow very different persistent trends, such weights need not exist and the assumption would fail. Assumption
7
formalizes this by requiring that convex-hull/spanning of the lagged-outcome space is sufficient for (near-exact) weights to exist as either the number of donors or the length of the pre-period grows. Thus, Assumption
3
should be read as both a parallel-trends restriction and an existence statement whose plausibility rises with the richness of the donor pool.

The weighted parallel trends assumption is implied by
SCSA
Assumptions 2a and 2b. A weighted parallel trends assumption is also used in Synthetic Difference-in-Differences and inclusive-SCM
(
Stefano and Mellace, 2024
;
Arkhangelsky et al., 2021
)
.

Assumption
4
prevents
the weights from degenerating on a single donor; ridge or entropy
penalties used in empirical SCM help enforce this.

Assumption 4

(Weight regularity)
.

(a) Donor-restriction: for each treated unit
j

w_{j}

T_{i}\leq T_{j}+k

\sum_{i}w_{ij}^{2}\;\leq\;c\quad\text{for every treated unit }j.

While the SCSA DGP assumptions satisfy weighted parallel trends,

Assumption 2b can violate the combination of Assumptions

Additionally, this assumption allows the donor pool to change with

SCSA

Let

. Assumption
5
is the asymptotic scheme stating that i) pre-treament periods is increasing or the treatment periods shift later as the panel lengthens, ii) the effective dimension of the weight simplex is controlled, and iii) number of treated units is increasing.

Assumption 5

(Asymptotic scheme)
.

As
(N,T)\to\infty
,

(i)

L_{\min}\;\to\;\infty,

(ii)

(N_{0}-1)J=o\!\bigl(L_{\max}/\log L_{\max}\bigr),

and

(iii)

\max\!\Bigl\{\tfrac{\log J}{J},\,\tfrac{1}{J\,K}\Bigr\}\;\to\;0.

Note, all simplices that the
estimator searches over are embedded in

, so the covering
number in Lemma

(j,k)
.

Assumption
6
controls tail behavior and allows heavier tails (e.g., Gumbel distribution) than the typically assumed sub-Gaussian tails.

Assumption 6

(Sub-exponential shocks)
.

Write
Y_{it}^{0}=\mu_{it}+\varepsilon_{it}
with

\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_{it}]=0
; the
\varepsilon_{it}
are independent
across

\kappa<\infty
.
The same
\varepsilon_{it}
enters every potential outcome

Y_{it}(s)
,
s\in\{1,\dots,T,\infty\}
.

Note that the assumption can be trivially weaked to allow
(i,t)

Assumption
7
states the donor pool is “rich” enough that, as
either the number of never-treated units or the pre-period length
grows, one can find weights that achieve arbitrarily close
balance on pre-treatment outcomes. This occus when (i) Under the AR(
L
) or low-rank
factor models used in
SCSA
;
(ii) when donors span the treated units’ lagged-outcome convex
hull.

Assumption 7

(Convex-hull / spanning)
.

For every sample size
(N,T)
there exists a weight matrix

\Gamma^{\star}\!\in\!\Delta_{\!scm}
satisfying

q_{\mathrm{pool}}(\Gamma^{\star})\,\leq\,\eta_{N,T},\qquad q_{\mathrm{sep}}(\Gamma^{\star})\,\leq\,\eta_{N,T},\quad\text{with }\eta_{N,T}\downarrow 0\text{ as }N_{0}\text{ or }L_{\min}\to\infty.

The terms
q_{\mathrm{pool}}
and
q_{\mathrm{sep}}
are defined in the proof of Theorem
1
. The term
q_{\mathrm{pool}}
captures systematic, group-level mismatch by pooling all treated units for each lag

, taking the donor-weighted gap, then square and average over lags. While

captures the idiosyncratic, unit-specific mismatch by obtaining a squared donor gap for each treated unit, averaging over lags, then averaging over units.

This assumption can fail when a treated unit with trends or seasonality that
never appears in the donors; a donor pool that is too small or a donor pool whose outcomes are so sparsely distributed that, even after de-meaning, no convex combination can track the treated path.
This assumption can be checked by plotting RMSE placebo gaps versus
L
or versus donor-pool size. A monotone decline toward zero supports the assumption.

Assumption
8
states the penalty becomes weak enough that it
does not block the optimiser from
approaching the best attainable fit (order
\eta_{N,T}^{2}
),
yet strong enough to keep the weight norm

\|\hat{\Gamma}\|_{F}
bounded.

Assumption 8

(Regularisation tuning)
.

The ridge parameter in the partially-pooled objective satisfies

\lambda=\lambda_{N,T}\downarrow 0
and

\lambda^{-1}\eta_{N,T}^{2}\to 0
as
(N,T)\to\infty
.

This assumption can fail when choosing a fixed, large
\lambda

independent of sample size (violating the first condition) or
setting
\lambda=0
making weights degenerate (violating the second condition).
This assumption can be checked by grid-search
\lambda
against placebo-gap
cross-validation; make sure the selected value declines when additional donors or pre-periods are included.

4
Results

This section presents the main results of the paper. Proofs are in the appendix.

Theorem
1
provides a finite-sample ATT bound for
any
valid weight matrix from
SCSA
(analogous to Theorems 1 and 2 of
SCSA
) without the need for structural DGP assumptions. Note it expands on the DGPs considered in
SCSA
and is
therefore useful on its own for practitioners who wish to certify a

Theorem
2
shows consistency of
SCSA
under the new assumptions with pre-specified weights. This theorem follows immediately from Theorem
1
, however a different proof approach is used to emphasise that this result holds
any
weighting

Theorem
3
shows consistency of
SCSA

2
allowing weights to be estimated by data following the procedure outlined in
SCSA
. In order to gaurantee consistency uniform convergence of the imbalance functions is needed, which is shown in Lemma
1
.

Theorem
3
is stated for
0<\nu<1
.
The extreme choices
\nu\in\{0,1\}
(Separate‐SCM and Pooled‐SCM) require
additional homogeneity conditions (e.g., identical AR coefficients or factor

objective. Such DGP‐specific assumptions are avoided and therefore focus
on the partially pooled estimator.

Theorem 1

(Finite‑sample ATT error, arbitrary weights)
.

Fix any
\Gamma\in\Delta_{\!scm}
that satisfies the donor restriction

6
Conclusion

This paper broadens the theoretical foundations of augmented
synthetic–control estimators in staggered–adoption settings. By
replacing factor or autoregressive data–generating assumptions with the
design–based
weighted parallel-trends
condition and mild
sub-exponential tails, in this paper a (i) finite-sample error bound that
separates pooled and unit–specific imbalance is derived, (ii) consistency
for any pre-specified weighting scheme satisfying simple
dispersion constraints is established, and (iii) an arg-min consistency result for
the data-driven, partially–pooled ridge estimator of Ben-Michael,
Feller and Rothstein (2022) is provided. The finite-sample bound implies that, in principle, driving either imbalance component to zero suffices for consistency; the partially pooled objective (
0<\nu<1
) provides a practical, model-free way to do so by controlling both components simultaneously.

From an applied perspective the new conditions map directly onto
familiar diagnostics. Root-mean-squared placebo gaps test weighted
parallel trends; the effective-donor count and ridge path trace weight
dispersion; window-length and donor-set sensitivity illuminate the
convex-hull requirement. Because all results hinge only on these
observable quantities, researchers can help ensure assumptions without
taking a stand a structural DGP.

Several extensions are immediate. Allowing auxiliary pretreatment
covariates would bring the theory fully in line with
augsynth
;
bootstrap-based uncertainty quantification could adapt the finite-sample
bound; and a systematic simulation study would benchmark finite-sample
performance against recent alternatives such as synthetic DiD. These directions are left for future work and hope the present results encourage
broader use of intercept-augmented SCM in policy evaluation.

.

Then

\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}_{k}-\mathrm{ATT}_{k}\;=\;\underbrace{\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j}Z^{(\Gamma)}_{j,-k}}_{\text{pooled imbalance}}\;-\;\underbrace{\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j}\bar{Z}^{(\Gamma)}_{j,\mathrm{pre}}}_{\text{separate imbalance}}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j}\bigl(\varepsilon_{j,T_{j}+k}-\sum_{i}w_{ij}\varepsilon_{i,T_{j}+k}\bigr)}_{\text{post-treatment noise}}.

(1)

Step 2 (Deterministic bounds for the imbalance terms).

By Cauchy–Schwarz, the pooled term is bounded by

\bigl|\tfrac{1}{J}\sum_{j}Z^{(\Gamma)}_{j,-k}\bigr|\leq q_{\mathrm{pool}}(\Gamma)

and the separate term is bounded by

\Bigl|\tfrac{1}{J}\sum_{j}\bar{Z}^{(\Gamma)}_{j,\mathrm{pre}}\Bigr|\leq L_{\min}^{-1/2}\Bigl\{\tfrac{1}{J}\sum_{j}q_{j}^{2}(\Gamma)\Bigr\}^{1/2}=L_{\min}^{-1/2}\,q_{\mathrm{sep}}(\Gamma).

Step 3 (Concentration for the noise term).

For each treated unit
j
let

\xi_{j}:=\varepsilon_{j,T_{j}+k}-\sum_{i}w_{ij}\varepsilon_{i,T_{j}+k}.

Because
\|\xi_{j}\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq 2\kappa\sqrt{1+c},

Proposition 2.7.1 of
Vershynin, 2018
yields

\Pr\!\Bigl(\bigl|\tfrac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\xi_{j}\bigr|>C_{3}\sqrt{\tfrac{\log J}{J}}\Bigr)\;\leq\;2J^{-2},\quad C_{3}:=\sqrt{\tfrac{8}{c}}\kappa\sqrt{1+c}.

Hence, with probability at least
1-2J^{-2}
,

\Bigl|\tfrac{1}{J}\sum_{j}\xi_{j}\Bigr|\;\leq\;C_{3}\sqrt{\tfrac{\log J}{J}}.

Step 4 (Assemble the pieces).

The equation (
1
), the deterministic bounds (Step 2) and the
probabilistic bound (Step 3) together imply the event

\bigl|\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}_{k}-\mathrm{ATT}_{k}\bigr|\;\leq\;q_{\mathrm{pool}}(\Gamma)+L_{\min}^{-1/2}\,q_{\mathrm{sep}}(\Gamma)+\sqrt{\tfrac{8(1+c)\log J}{cJ}}\,\kappa

fails with probability at most
2J^{-2}
.
∎

A.2
Theorem
2

Proof.

Consistency is proven in two steps: unbiasedness and diminishing variance.

Step 1 (Unbiasedness).

Define
Z_{it}^{0}:=Y_{it}^{0}-\bar{Y}_{i,\text{pre}}
. For any
k\geq 0
,

\mu_{j,T_{j}+k}-\sum_{i}w_{ij}\mu_{i,T_{j}+k}\;=\;\sum_{t=1}^{k}\Bigl(\Delta\mu_{j,T_{j}+t}-\sum_{i}w_{ij}\Delta\mu_{i,T_{j}+t}\Bigr).

Using SUTVA (Assumptions
1
–
2
) and by Assumption
3
each summand has mean 0 for
t\leq 0
,
so in expectation only

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{jk}]
remains:

\mathbb{E}[\hat{\tau}_{jk}]=\mathbb{E}[\Delta Z_{j,T_{j}+k}^{0}]-\sum_{i}w_{ij}\mathbb{E}[\Delta Z_{i,T_{j}+k}^{0}]=0+\mathbb{E}[\tau_{jk}].

Hence
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}_{k}]=\mathrm{ATT}_{k}
.

Step 2 (Diminishing variance).

Write

\hat{\tau}_{jk}-\tau_{jk}=(\varepsilon_{j,T_{j}+k}-\sum_{i}w_{ij}\varepsilon_{i,T_{j}+k})-(\bar{\varepsilon}_{j,\text{pre}}-\sum_{i}w_{ij}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\text{pre}}).

Sub-exponentiality plus independence gives

\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\tau}_{jk})=\kappa^{2}\bigl(1+\sum_{i}w_{ij}^{2}+L_{j}^{-1}+\sum_{i}w_{ij}^{2}L_{i}^{-1}\bigr)\leq C(\sum_{i}w_{ij}^{2}+L_{\min}^{-1}),

for a constant
C
.
Assumptions
4
b and
5
imply

\sup_{j}\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\tau}_{jk})\leq C^{\ast}<\infty
and

L_{\min}^{-1}\to 0
.
Because the
\hat{\tau}_{jk}
are independent across
j
,

\operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}_{k})=J^{-2}\sum_{j}\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\tau}_{jk})\leq C^{\ast}/J\to 0.

Unbiasedness plus vanishing variance yields

\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}_{k}\xrightarrow{p}\mathrm{ATT}_{k}

whenever
J\to\infty
, and

\bar{\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}}\xrightarrow{p}\bar{\mathrm{ATT}}

whenever
J\,K\to\infty
.

∎

For a fixed weight matrix

\Gamma

\kappa

\ell

Bernstein’s inequality for the

L_{\max}
such variables gives,

(2)

Step 3 (An
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
-net for the

The feasible set is a Cartesian product of
J
probability

L_{\max}

From (
2

\Pr\!\Bigl(\max_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{L_{\max}}}}\bigl|\widehat{q}_{\mathrm{pool}}^{\,2}(\Gamma)-q_{\mathrm{pool}}^{\,2}(\Gamma)\bigr|>t\Bigr)\;\leq\;2|\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon_{L_{\max}}}|\exp\!\Bigl\{-L_{\max}\,t^{2}\bigl/\!\bigl[32\kappa^{2}(1+c)\bigr]\Bigr\}.

\boldsymbol{\Delta_{\!scm}}

Let

Theorem
3

Proof.

Notation.
Write

q_{\mathrm{pool}}^{2}(\Gamma):=\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{1}^{(\Gamma)}]^{2},\quad q_{\mathrm{sep}}^{2}(\Gamma):=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\zeta_{j,1}^{(\Gamma)}\bigr]^{2},

\widehat{q}_{\mathrm{pool}}^{2},\widehat{q}_{\mathrm{sep}}^{2}

\widehat{\Psi}_{N,T}(\Gamma)
the sample analog.

Step 1 (Uniform law of large numbers and arg-min consistency).

Lemma
1
gives a uniform LLN:

\sup_{\Gamma\in\Delta_{\!scm}}\bigl|\widehat{\Psi}_{N,T}(\Gamma)-\Psi_{N,T}(\Gamma)\bigr|\;=\;o_{p}(1).

Assumption
7
guarantees that there exists some

\Gamma^{\star}\in\Delta_{\!scm}

\Psi_{N,T}(\Gamma^{\star})\;\leq\;\nu\,\eta_{N,T}^{2}+(1-\nu)\,\eta_{N,T}^{2}+\lambda c\;=\;O(\eta_{N,T}^{2}).

\widehat{\Psi}_{N,T}(\hat{\Gamma})\;\leq\;\widehat{\Psi}_{N,T}(\Gamma^{\star})=\Psi_{N,T}(\Gamma^{\star})+o_{p}(1)=O_{p}(\eta_{N,T}^{2}),

q_{\mathrm{pool}}\!\bigl(\hat{\Gamma}\bigr)=O_{p}(\eta_{N,T}),\qquad q_{\mathrm{sep}}\!\bigl(\hat{\Gamma}\bigr)=O_{p}(\eta_{N,T}).

Step 2 (Controlling the ridge penalty).

Because
\Psi_{N,T}(\hat{\Gamma})\geq\lambda\|\hat{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}
,

\lambda\|\hat{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}\;\leq\;O_{p}(\eta_{N,T}^{2}),\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\|\hat{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}=O_{p}\bigl(\eta_{N,T}^{2}/\lambda\bigr).

Assumption
8
imposes
\eta_{N,T}^{2}/\lambda\to 0
,
hence
\|\hat{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}=O_{p}(1)
, so the dispersion bound
holds w.p.
\!1-o(1)
.

Step 3 (Error bound for the estimated SCM).

Plug
\Gamma=\hat{\Gamma}
into Theorem
1
. The pooled and separate

O_{p}(\eta_{N,T})

O_{p}(1)
by Step 2. Theorem
1
therefore yields

\bigl|\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}_{k}-\mathrm{ATT}_{k}\bigr|\;\leq\;C_{1}\,\eta_{N,T}\;+\;C_{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{\log J}{J}}\;+\;o_{p}(1),

N,T
.

Step 4 (Conclude consistency).

Because
\eta_{N,T}\to 0

\sqrt{\log J/J}\to 0

, proving

\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}_{k}\xrightarrow{p}\mathrm{ATT}_{k}
.

variance

shows

\bar{\widehat{\mathrm{ATT}}}\xrightarrow{p}\bar{\mathrm{ATT}}
.

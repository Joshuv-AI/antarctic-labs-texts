Clifford Hierarchy Stabilizer Codes: Transversal Non-Clifford Gates and Magic
Ryohei Kobayashi, Guanyu Zhu, Po-Shen Hsin

Abstract
A fundamental problem in fault-tolerant quantum computation is the tradeoff between universality and dimensionality, exemplified by the the Bravyi-König bound for n n -dimensional topological stabilizer codes. In this work, we extend topological Pauli stabilizer codes to a broad class of n n -dimensional Clifford hierarchy stabilizer codes. These codes correspond to the ( n + 1 ) (n+1) D Dijkgraaf-Witten gauge theories with non-Abelian topological order. We construct transversal non-Clifford gates through automorphism symmetries represented by cup products. In 2D, we obtain the first transversal non-Clifford logical gates including T and CS for Clifford stabilizer codes, using the automorphism of the twisted ℤ 2 3 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3} gauge theory (equivalent to 𝔻 4 \mathbb{D}_{4} topological order). We also combine it with the just-in-time decoder to fault-tolerantly prepare the logical T magic state in O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) rounds via code switching. In 3D, we construct a transversal logical T \sqrt{\text{T}} gate in a non-Clifford stabilizer code at the third level of the Clifford hierarchy, located on a tetrahedron corresponding to a twisted ℤ 2 4 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4} gauge theory. Due to the potential single-shot code-switching properties of these codes, one could achieve the 4th level of Clifford hierarchy with an O ⁡ ( d 3 ) O(d^{3}) space-time overhead, avoiding the tradeoff observed in 2D. We propose a conjecture extending the Bravyi-König bound to Clifford hierarchy stabilizer codes, with our explicit constructions providing an upper bound of spatial dimension ( N − 1 ) (N-1) for achieving the logical gates in the N th N^{\text{th}} -level of Clifford hierarchy.

To define a cup product on a triangulated manifold, it is necessary to specify a branching on the triangulation. A branching structure is an assignment of an orientation to each edge of every simplex such that no oriented loop exists within any 2-simplex. Then, each vertex of a single
k

(0,1,2,\dots,k)

(23)

where the sum runs over all subsets

\displaystyle V=\bigotimes_{e}\text{CNOT}^{({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r},{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g})}_{e}\text{CNOT}^{({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b},{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g})}_{e}~,

(33)

which transforms the Pauli operators according to the automorphism (
4

\displaystyle\begin{split}&X_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\leftrightarrow X_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}X_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},\quad X_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\leftrightarrow X_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},\quad X_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\leftrightarrow X_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}X_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}~,\\
&Z_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\leftrightarrow Z_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},\quad Z_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\leftrightarrow Z_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}Z_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}Z_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}},\quad Z_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\leftrightarrow Z_{e}^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}~.\end{split}

(34)

The automorphism symmetry is

U=WV~,\quad W=(-1)^{\int\frac{a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}}{2}}=\prod_{\Delta_{012}}(\text{CS}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}_{e_{01},e_{12}})^{\sigma(\Delta_{012})}~,

(35)

where the product is over 2-simplices
\Delta_{012}
, and
\sigma(\Delta_{012})=\pm 1
is the orientation of the 2-simplex. On a square lattice, this operator
U
takes the form of (
6
) in the main text.

III.1
Automorphism symmetry in path integral

One way to see the automorphism symmetry is a symmetry of the ground state code subspace is using the path integral formalism for the twisted
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
gauge theory that describes the ground state subspace.

The path integral is

Z[M]=\sum_{a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})}(-1)^{\int a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}}~.

(36)

where we labeled
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
gauge fields by colors
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r},{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}
according to the main text.
Using Poincaré duality, we can also express the path integral in terms of membranes
m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}

Z[M]=\sum_{m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\in H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})}(-1)^{\#(m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}})}~,

(37)

where
\#(m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}})
is the triple intersection number of the three membranes
m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
.

The automorphism symmetry can be expressed in terms of
a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
that take values in
\{0,1\}^{3}
as

(a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}})\rightarrow(a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}})~.

(38)

Under the above transformation, the path integral (
36
) transforms into

Z[M]\rightarrow\sum_{a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})}(-1)^{\int a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup(a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}})\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}}~.

(39)

In the exponent,

a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup(a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}})\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}=a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}^{2}+a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}^{2}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}~.

(40)

Using the identity
a\cup a=\frac{d\tilde{a}}{2}
with a
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
lift
\tilde{a}
of
a
(valid for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
1-cocycles
a
),
we can rewrite the right hand side as

a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}+\frac{1}{2}d\left(\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\right)~.

(41)

In other words, the transformation leaves the exponent invariant up to a total derivative. Since the total derivative integrates to zero for closed
M
, the path integral transforms as

Z[M]\rightarrow\sum_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})}(-1)^{\int a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}}\cdot i^{\int_{M}d\left(\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\right)}=Z[M]~,

(42)

where we used
\int_{M}d\left(\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\right)=0
for closed
M
.
Thus we conclude that the path integral is invariant under the automorphism symmetry.

Moreover, when
M
is inserted with domain wall
D
of the symmetry, i.e. we only perform transformation on half spacetime
[0,\infty)_{t}
with boundary
D
at
t=0
, the total derivative in (
42
) picks up the following contribution in the path integral

W=i^{\int_{D}\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}}~.

(43)

In other words, the automorphism symmetry inserts the gauged SPT operator (
43
) in the path integral. The gauged SPT operator by itself is not topological, and it is only topological and generates symmetry when accompanied by the automorphism transformation. The operator (
43
) corresponds to the additional operator
W
in (
35
) for the automorphism symmetry. See
automorphism2025
for a general discussion of automorphism symmetry in twisted gauge theories, where it is shown that the automorphism symmetry can become extended or a higher-group/non-invertible symmetry.

III.1.1
Automorphism permutes topological excitations

Here we will study how the automorphism symmetry
U
acts on the particle excitations, i.e. detectable errors in the Clifford stabilizer codes.
We will present two derivations: one uses how stabilizers are permuted under the symmetry, the other uses path integral formalism of the ground state subspace.

The electric charge excitations
e_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},e_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},e_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
violate the
X
stabilizers
S^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}_{X},S^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}_{X},S^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}_{X}
while the magnetic charge excitations
m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
violate the
Z
stabilizers
S^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}_{Z},S^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}_{Z},S^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}_{Z}
. When there are more than one stabilizers that are violated, the excitations have subscript with the corresponding colors, such as
e_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}
being the violation of both
S^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}_{X},S^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}_{X}
.
By checking how the stabilizers are permuted under the symmetry, we can track how the excitations are permuted. From the transformation of the stabilizers (
59
), we conclude the mapping of excitations under the automorphism symmetry:

\displaystyle m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\leftrightarrow m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},\;m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\leftrightarrow m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},\;m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\leftrightarrow m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}},\;m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\leftrightarrow m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}~,

(44)

\displaystyle e_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\leftrightarrow e_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},\quad e_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\leftrightarrow e_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}},\quad e_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\leftrightarrow e_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}~.

(45)

For example, violation of
S^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}_{X}
corresponds to the violation of all
S^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}_{X},S^{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}_{X},S^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}_{X}
under the automorphism symmetry, and thus the excitation
e_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}
corresponds to
e_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
under the symmetry.

An alternative derivation uses the path integral of continuum field theory that describes the ground states, where there are states related by closed loop operators that create and annihilate a pair of electric and/or magnetic charge excitations.
The field theory is described by the topological action

\displaystyle\pi\int a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\cup a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}~,

(46)

where
a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}=0,1
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fields correspond to the
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r},{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}
types of qubits. One can embed these
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge fields into
U(1)
as

a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\rightarrow a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}/\pi,\quad a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\rightarrow a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}/\pi,\quad a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\rightarrow a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}/\pi~,

(47)

where the gauge fields
a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
now have holonomies in
0,\pi
instead of
0,1
because of the rescaling.
We can introduce Lagrangian multipliers
\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},\tilde{a}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
to enforce these constraints on the holonomies via a BF type coupling
Horowitz:1989ng
;
Maldacena:2001ss
;
Kapustin:2014gua
.
In terms of the rescaled gauge fields and the Lagrangian multipliers, the theory can be expressed as

\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\int a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}+\frac{2}{2\pi}\int(a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}d{b}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}d{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}d{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}})~.

(48)

In the theory, the gauge invariant operators
e^{i\int a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}},e^{i\int a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}},e^{i\int a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}}
on closed loops create and annihilate a pair of electric charges
e_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},e_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},e_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
, and similarly
the operators
e^{i\int{b}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}},e^{i\int{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}},e^{i\int{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}}
create and annihilate a pair of magnetic charges
m_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},m_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}
.
We can then identify the permutation (
45
) as the automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
gauge group which transforms the gauge fields as

\displaystyle a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\to a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}},\quad a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\to a_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}+a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}},\quad a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\to a_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}~,

(49)

and

\displaystyle{b}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}\to{b}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}r}}+{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},\quad{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}\to{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}},\quad{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}\to{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}}+{b}_{{\color[rgb]{0,1,0}g}}~.

(50)

Thus we find that the transformation of the gauge fields indeed corresponds to the permutation of topological excitations in (
45
).

III.1.2
Generalization to higher dimensions

The model can be generalized to
(N-1)
spatial dimensions, where the gauge theory is a twisted
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{N}
gauge theory, with the path integral

Z[M]=\sum_{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{N}\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})}(-1)^{\int a_{1}\cup a_{2}\cup\cdots\cup a_{N}}~,

(51)

where
M
is the spacetime manifold. We have omitted an overall normalization that removes the gauge transformations. The one-cocycles (gauge fields)
a_{1},\cdots,a_{N}
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{N}
valued.
We can rewrite the path integral using Poincaré duality, where the one-cocycles are replaced by
(N-1)
-cycles
m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{N}
that take value in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{N}
:

Z[M]=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2},\cdots,m_{N}\in H_{N-1}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{2})}(-1)^{\#\left(m_{1},\cdots,m_{N}\right)}~,

(52)

where
\#\left(m_{1},\cdots,m_{N}\right)
is the intersection number of the
(N-1)
-cycles
m_{1},\cdots,m_{N}
.

Consider the automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{N}
gauge group that acts on the gauge fields as

a_{1}\rightarrow a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}+\cdots+a_{N}~.

(53)

The weight of the path integral changes as

\omega=\pi a_{1}\cup a_{2}\cup\cdots\cup a_{N}\rightarrow\omega+d\alpha~,

(54)

where

\alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(a_{2}\cup a_{3}\cup\cdots a_{N}\right)+\pi\sum_{i=3}^{N}\sum_{j=2}^{i-1}a_{2}\cup\cdots a_{j-1}\cup\left(a_{i}\cup_{1}a_{j}\right)\cup a_{j+1}\cdots\cup a_{i}\cdots\cup a_{N}~.

(55)

In the summation, the first few terms are
\pi(a_{3}\cup_{1}a_{2})\cup a_{3}\cup\cdots a_{N}+\pi(a_{4}\cup_{1}a_{2})\cup a_{3}\cup a_{4}\cdots a_{N}+\pi a_{2}\cup(a_{4}\cup_{1}a_{3})\cup a_{4}\cdots a_{N}
.
Therefore, the automorphism symmetry is generated by the operator in the form of
U=WV
, where
V
is a transversal CNOT gate inducing the permutation of gauge fields, and and
W=e^{i\int\alpha}
with the integral over the whole
N
-dimensional space.

As described in Sec.

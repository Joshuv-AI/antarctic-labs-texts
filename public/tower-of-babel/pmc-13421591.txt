A three-dimensional nonlinear finite element model for coupled vibration analysis of rotary steerable systems
Authors: Kun Shao, Yue Liu, Huihui Wang, Lizhi Xiao, Zhengzhi Zhou, Zhanjun Jia, Qichen Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Reliable operation of intelligent rotary steerable systems (RSSs) critically depends on the dynamic stability of the bottom-hole assembly (BHA), where high-frequency vibrations can lead to tool malfunction, accelerating structural fatigue, and degrade steering performance under harsh downhole conditions. This study establishes a three-dimensional nonlinear coupled vibration model for a static push-the-bit RSS–BHA based on finite-element dynamics, energy-based methods, and Hamilton’s principle, enabling a unified analysis of axial, lateral, and torsional vibrations and their coupled responses under field-relevant high-frequency conditions. The results show that the proposed model can identify seven representative vibration modes commonly observed in drilling operations, including single-mode vibrations, pairwise coupled vibrations, and fully coupled vibration behaviors, and its predictive capability is validated through field-based case studies. Distinct dynamic characteristics clearly differentiate torsion-dominated stick–slip vibration from lateral–torsional coupled whirl vibration. Sensitivity analyses further demonstrate that stick–slip vibration is primarily governed by torsional excitation, whereas whirl vibration is dominated by lateral–torsional coupling and exhibits strong sensitivity to rotational speed and drill-collar geometry. Overall, this work provides a quantitative, physics-based framework for vibration-mode identification, interpretation of coupling mechanisms, and mode-specific vibration control and parameter optimization of RSS–BHA systems.

Introduction

With the increasing demand for complex well trajectories and extended horizontal sections, rotary steerable systems (RSSs) have become a key technology in modern directional and horizontal drilling1–3. By maintaining continuous rotation, RSS enables high trajectory control accuracy and improved borehole quality. However, under high rotational speeds and strongly constrained downhole conditions, the bottom-hole assembly (BHA) of RSS is subjected to multiple excitation sources, including bit–formation interaction, borehole contact, and structural discontinuities4–6, which can give rise to complex vibration responses. Excessive vibrations not only deteriorate borehole quality and reduce the rate of penetration but also accelerate fatigue damage and wear of drilling tools and steering components, thereby compromising tool reliability and operational efficiency. Because RSS–BHA vibrations typically involve coupled axial, lateral and torsional motions with pronounced nonlinear characteristics7–10, accurate characterization and prediction of their vibration responses are essential for vibration risk assessment, drilling parameter optimization, and the safe and efficient application of RSS technology.

Drilling dynamic systems exhibit pronounced nonlinearity and strong coupling effect11,12, while downhole boundary conditions are highly complex and continuously evolve with changes in well depth, inclination, and borehole geometry13,14. Under realistic drilling conditions, contact constraints, friction-induced nonlinearities, and time-varying boundaries are inevitably involved, which imposes inherent limitations on conventional analytical and semi-analytical approaches. Consequently, a robust numerical framework is required to accurately capture the complex dynamic behavior of drill-string systems. In this context, the finite element method (FEM) provides a unified framework for representing structural details of drilling tools, borehole contact constraints, and multiple external loadings, and has gradually become one of the most practically applicable numerical approaches for drill-string and BHA dynamic analysis. As early as the 1980s, Millheim and Apostal15–17 developed a three-dimensional dynamic finite element model of drill-strings based on D’Alembert’s principle, laying an important foundation for numerical analysis of drilling vibrations. Subsequent studies18–20 extended this framework by introducing three-dimensional beam elements, time-domain solution strategies, and improved time-integration schemes, thereby enabling the characterization of time-varying dynamic responses of drill-strings and BHAs. With continued advances in numerical computation and modeling techniques, a wide range of element formulations, time-integration algorithms, and force–frequency response analysis methods have been incorporated into finite element models21–23. These developments have enabled more realistic representation of drilling conditions and increasingly detailed descriptions of complex downhole dynamic behavior.

Over the past several decades, vibration analysis models for drill-strings and BHAs have been continuously developed and refined. Numerous studies24–27 have demonstrated that the transition of axial load along the drilling system from tensile to compressive states, the strongly coupled nature of bit–rock interaction, high static driving torque, and drill-string curvature are the primary factors responsible for multimodal vibrations and vibration mode coupling. Accordingly, axial–torsional, axial–lateral, torsional–lateral, and fully coupled axial–torsional–lateral vibration models have been preliminary investigated18,28–32. Compared with single-mode analyses, coupled vibration modeling plays a critical role in improving the fidelity of downhole dynamic response prediction. Such models can be formulated in either linear or nonlinear forms, with contact and friction effects often incorporated to better represent field conditions23,28. However, due to the diversity and complexity of geological formations and borehole structures, these coupled models typically rely on expert judgment to define contact relationships, constraints, and boundary loadings33. This reliance increases both modeling and computational complexity and limits their general applicability and computational efficiency in engineering practice.

Under (RSSs operating conditions, the complexity and uncertainty of drill-string dynamic behavior are further amplified, making accurate definition of contact relationships, constraints, and boundary conditions considerably more challenging. On the one hand, the RSS–BHA integrates steering actuators, measurement and control units, and multiple structural discontinuities, which substantially increased structural complexity and lead to more intricate boundary conditions3,34. On the other hand, continuous rotation during RSS operations makes the system more susceptible to vibration responses by pronounced high-frequency components and strong multi-degree-of-freedom coupling35,36. From an engineering perspective, prediction of vibration response alone is often insufficient to directly support field decision-making. It is also necessary to relate complex dynamic responses to operationally meaningful vibration types, thereby enabling vibration mode identification and classification for drilling parameter optimization and vibration control. In particular, stick–slip and whirl vibrations37,38, which are frequently observed in the field and pose significant operational risks, represent torsion-dominated nonlinear responses and complex lateral–torsional coupled dynamics, respectively. Their sensitivities to operating conditions and structural parameters differ markedly, underscoring the need for targeted investigations within a unified three-dimensional coupled dynamic framework.

Motivated by these considerations, this study develops a three-dimensional nonlinear coupled vibration model for a bottom-hole assembly integrated with an IDS intelligent rotary steerable systems, with the objective of capturing the key characteristics of realistic operating conditions. The model is formulated within a unified framework that combines finite element dynamics, energy-based methods, and Hamilton’s principle, and is employed to identify and classify representative vibration modes. Furthermore, detailed investigations of stick–slip and whirl vibrations are carried out under typical field conditions, followed by a comprehensive sensitivity analysis to systematically evaluate the influence of critical structural and operating parameters on different vibration modes. The results provide a solid quantitative basis for vibration control strategies and drilling-parameter optimization in rotary steerable drilling, thereby enhancing operational safety, reliability, and efficiency.

Compared with previous drill-string and BHA finite-element models, the main contribution of the present study is not the isolated use of beam elements or Hamiltonian derivation, but the establishment of an RSS-oriented three-dimensional nonlinear coupled dynamic framework that is directly linked to engineering vibration-mode identification. Specifically, the proposed model integrates axial, two orthogonal lateral, and torsional motions; RSS-specific structural discontinuities and steering-pad actions; nonlinear borehole-contact/friction effects; and bit-rock excitation within one computational framework. This unified formulation enables consistent discrimination of single-mode, pairwise-coupled, and fully coupled vibration states under field-relevant rotary steerable drilling conditions. More importantly, it provides a mode-dependent predictive capability: torsion-dominated stick–slip and lateral-torsional whirl are reproduced within the same physical model and are shown to possess markedly different controlling mechanisms and parameter sensitivities. In this sense, the present work extends previous drill-string FEM studies from response prediction alone toward physics-based vibration-type identification and mode-specific control analysis for RSS–BHA systems.

Three-dimensional nonlinear coupled vibration model of the RSS–BHA

Physical model

The research object of this study is the IDS intelligent RSS developed by China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC) Logging Company, which belongs to a static push-the-bit RSS39. The drill-string consists of drill pipes extending from the surface and a BHA located at the lower end. As the most dynamically complex component of the downhole system, the BHA is the region where vibrations are most pronounced and serves as the core functional unit for wellbore trajectory control and near-bit data acquisition in rotary steerable drilling3,40. The RSS–BHA consists of drill collars, measurement and communication subs, stabilizers, a rotary steerable sub, and a drill bit, which are connected sequentially along the axial direction, as illustrated in Fig. 1. During drilling, the RSS–BHA is subjected to multiple loads, including weight on bit, torque, and formation reaction forces, which induce axial, lateral, and torsional lateral vibrations. The resulting dynamic response directly affect the operational stability of the RSS and the overall wellbore quality.Fig. 1Schematic of the IDS intelligent RSS- BHA configuration.

Coordinate systems and modeling assumptions

To describe the spatial configuration and dynamic response of the RSS–BHA during drilling, three mutually related coordinate systems are introduced, as illustrated in Fig. 2. The Cartesian coordinate system O1-END is defined as the global reference frame, where O1 is located at the wellhead center, and E, N, and D denote the east, north, and vertical downward directions, respectively. To represent the spatial geometry of the borehole in the global coordinate system, the borehole is discretized along the axial direction into segments with an interval of 30 m. Each borehole segment is approximated as a cylindrical section, as shown in Fig. 2a. The Cartesian coordinate system O2-XYZ is defined as the borehole coordinate system, where O2 is located at the center of the borehole cross section. Governing equations for three-dimensional nonlinear coupled vibration

Based on the kinematic description of the three-dimensional elastic beam elements, the strain energy, kinetic energy, and external work of the RSS–BHA system are formulated within a unified coordinate framework. By introducing an energy-based approach and applying Hamilton’s principle, the dynamic behavior of the system is systematically modeled, and the governing equations describing the three-dimensional nonlinear coupled vibration of the RSS–BHA are derived. These governing equations simultaneously characterize the coupling among axial, two orthogonal lateral, and torsional vibrations, and provide the theoretical foundation for subsequent finite element discretization, numerical solution, and vibration mode analysis.

The three-dimensional nonlinear characteristics exhibited by the governing equations mainly originate from geometric nonlinear deformations and bending–torsion coupling effects induced in the RSS–BHA during drilling operations. Specifically, the nonlinear coupling terms between the axial and lateral displacements (e.g., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w^{\prime\prime}u^{\prime}$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$u^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}$$\end{document}) arise from the Green–Lagrange strain formulation under large-deflection bending conditions. The combined effects of these nonlinear terms lead to complex multi-degree-of-freedom and multimodal coupled vibration characteristics of the RSS–BHA under rotary steerable drilling conditions.

Loads and boundary conditions

During downhole drilling operations, the dynamic response of the RSS–BHA is governed by multiple types of loads, including gravitational loads, borehole wall contacts loads, and bit–formation interaction forces. These loads act as external excitations in the governing equations of the three-dimensional nonlinear coupled vibration and play a crucial role in determining the system dynamic behavior. To ensure the computational tractability and engineering applicability of the proposed dynamic model, the different types of loads are modeled separately, and the boundary conditions required for the dynamic solution are specified accordingly.

To ensure the robustness of numerical simulation calculations and meet the needs of real-time computation, the contact between the drill string and the wellbore wall is simplified in this paper. The aforementioned formula can effectively capture the main nonlinearity induced by contact, intermittent contact-separation behavior, and the mode transition trend between relatively stable rotation, stick–slip, and whirl responses. However, it ignores the effects of irregular wellbore, local flexibility of the wellbore wall, and lubricity of the drilling fluid. Therefore, this model is expected to reproduce the main trends and relative intensities of contact-controlled vibration modes, while neglecting the local impact and extremely high-frequency components of the drill string whirl response.

Finite element discretization and numerical solution

To solve the governing equations of the three-dimensional nonlinear coupled vibration of the RSS–BHA (Eq. 20), the FEM theory is employed to discretize the axial, lateral, and torsional degrees of freedom. The RSS–BHA is discretized along the axial direction into a series of three-dimensional beam elements. Each element is modelled as a two-node three-dimensional beam. Each node has six degrees of freedom, including three translational (u, v, w) and three rotational (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta_{x} ,\theta_{y} ,\varphi$$\end{document})degrees of freedom, resulting in a total of 12 degrees of freedom per element. This formulation enables a complete representation of bending, torsion, and their coupling deformations.

To solve the resulting nonlinear dynamic equations, the Newmark–β time integration method, which is widely used in structural dynamics, is adopted. In view of the high-frequency vibrations, contact impacts, and strong nonlinearities that may arise during rotary steerable drilling, the average acceleration scheme (β = 0.25, γ = 0.5) is selected to ensure numerical stability and convergence. This method has been demonstrated to be effective for time-domain analysis of multi-degree-of-freedom nonlinear vibration problems.

Considering the coexistence of geometric nonlinearity, drill-string–borehole contact nonlinearity, and time-varying loads induced by bit–formation interactions, a predictor–corrector iterative strategy is employed during the time integration process. At each time step, the system response is first predicted based on the previous state. The contact conditions, friction forces, and nonlinear internal forces are then updated to construct the effective stiffness matrix and equivalent load vector until the convergence criterion is satisfied. The convergence criterion is defined in terms of the relative errors of displacement and internal force increments. This strategy enables stable computation while accurately capturing both transient and steady-state vibration responses of the rotary steerable system.

Based on the above finite element discretization and numerical solution framework, a three-dimensional nonlinear coupled dynamic solver for the RSS–BHA is developed on the MATLAB platform. The solver is used to generate vibration response data with high temporal resolution, providing reliable physical input for subsequent vibration mode analysis and identification. Analysis of typical vibration modes

The developed vibration analysis model for the RSS–BHA has been validated through comparison with field measurements, demonstrating its capability to provide a unified framework for analyzing single vibration modes, pairwise coupled vibrations, and fully coupled vibrations during drilling operations. It should be noted that, under practical drilling conditions, purely single vibration modes and fully coupled vibrations occur relatively infrequently3,8,10. Their dynamic responses are often strongly influenced by operational disturbances, making it difficult for such modes to exhibit stable and engineering-representative characteristics. In contrast, torsion-dominated stick–slip vibration and whirl vibration arising from lateral–torsional coupling are more commonly observed in field operations13,14,35. These vibration modes more effectively reflect the intrinsic multi-degree-of-freedom coupled dynamics of the drill-string and have a direct impact on drilling tool service life. Accordingly, stick–slip vibration and whirl vibration are selected in this study as representative operating conditions for analyzing RSS–BHA vibration characteristics and for further evaluating the applicability of the proposed vibration analysis model under typical engineering vibration scenarios.

Characteristics of stick–slip vibration

Stick–slip vibration is a nonlinear vibration mode dominated by torsional response and is one of the most frequently observed vibration types in drilling operations47,48. It originates from the periodic accumulation and sudden release of torsional load in the drill-string system and manifests as pronounced localized dynamic responses at the RSS–BHA.

Figure 8a presents the time-domain acceleration responses in the X, Y, and Z directions obtained from both field measurements and numerical simulations, while Fig. 8b shows the three-dimensional time–frequency distribution of the Y-direction acceleration. The Y-direction signal is more sensitive to circumferential (tangential) dynamic responses of the tool and is therefore better suited for capturing the periodic excitation characteristics associated with stick–slip vibration; it is thus selected for time–frequency analysis. As shown in Fig. 8a, stick–slip vibration exhibits a distinct periodic pattern, characterized by intermittent accumulation and release of vibration energy. In the lateral directions (X and Y), the acceleration responses display grouped, pulse-like envelopes, with significantly elevated amplitudes during the slip phase and markedly reduced amplitudes during the stick phase. This behavior indicates strong transient dynamic excitation acting on the RSS–BHA during the rapid transition of the bit from a near-stationary state to high rotational speed. The intermittent enhancement of lateral vibrations is closely associated with local imbalance effects induced by the sudden release of torsional load, together with geometric constraints imposed by the borehole. In contrast, the Z-direction acceleration shows a relatively smaller amplitude and smoother temporal variation, indicating that axial vibration is not dominant under the investigated condition and that the system response is primarily governed by torsional dynamics. Further inspection of Fig. 8b shows that the Y-direction acceleration energy is mainly concentrated in the low-frequency range and forms periodically repeating high-energy bands along the time axis. These time–frequency characteristics reflect stable dominant frequency components and pronounced temporal modulation, which are representative features of torsion-dominated stick–slip vibration.

Characteristics of whirl vibration

Whirl vibration is a nonlinear vibration mode resulting from the coupling between lateral and torsional motions, and its dynamic behavior is more complex than that of stick–slip vibration47,48. Figure 9 presents the time-domain acceleration responses in the X, Y, and Z directions of the RSS–BHA under whirl vibration conditions, together with the three-dimensional time–frequency representation of the Y-direction acceleration.

As shown in the time-domain responses (Fig. 9a), whirl vibration exhibits pronounced non-stationary characteristics, with lateral acceleration amplitudes varying continuously over time. This behavior reflects the orbital motion of the drilling tool within the borehole. In the X and Y directions, the acceleration responses are significantly enhanced, and the vibration amplitudes alternate between the two orthogonal directions, indicating strong directional dependence of the lateral motion. This feature is closely associated with eccentric rotation and periodic contact between the drill-string and the borehole, imposed by geometric constraints, highlighting the dominance of lateral dynamic responses9,18. In contrast, the acceleration in the Z acceleration direction exhibits a relatively small amplitude and appears mainly as low-level periodic fluctuations, indicating that axial vibration remains a secondary component under whirl vibration conditions. The overall system dynamic response is therefore governed primarily by the coupled lateral–torsional dynamics. Further inspection of Fig. 9b shows that the vibration energy associated with whirl vibration is more broadly distributed in the frequency domain. In addition to low-frequency components, multiple frequency components coexist during certain time intervals, and the frequency content evolves noticeably with time. These time–frequency characteristics reflect the strong dynamic non-stationarity induced by continuously varying drill-string–borehole contact states and lateral motion trajectories and constitute important identification features of whirl vibration.

Sensitivity analysis of typical vibration modes

Stick–slip vibration and whirl vibration exhibit pronounced differences in dynamic response characteristics and time–frequency features, reflecting fundamental distinctions in energy distribution patterns and system response mechanisms associated with different vibration modes3,38,47. Owing to the markedly different sensitivities of these vibration modes to operating and structural parameters, targeted parameter sensitivity analyses are required based on the preceding vibration characterization. Such analyses aim to identify the key controlling factors and elucidate their roles under different vibration regimes, thereby providing a reliable basis for drilling parameter optimization and effective vibration control.

Sensitivity analysis of stick–slip vibration

To systematically evaluate the influence of major operating and structural parameters on the stick–slip vibration characteristics of the RSS–BHA, weight on bit, drill-string rotational speed, well depth, and drill collar length are selected for the sensitivity analysis. Among these, weight on bit and rotational speed are key operating variables that directly govern the bit–formation contact state and the frictional excitation, whereas well depth and drill collar length characterize the scale effects and structural features of the drill-string system12,30,46. A base case is defined as a measured depth of 4000 m, a rotational speed of 100 r/min, a drill-collar length of 200 m, and a weight on bit of 120 kN. These results indicate that increasing weight on bit primarily intensifies stick–slip vibration by extending the stick phase and enhancing the transient dynamic response during the subsequent slip phase. T This behavior can be attributed to the increase in frictional torque at the bit–formation interface under higher weight on bit, which drives the system toward a strongly nonlinear, friction-dominated dynamic regime. Although a higher weight on bit is generally beneficial for improving the rate of penetration, it also substantially amplifies the amplitude of stick–slip vibration, thereby increasing the dynamic loading level and potential failure risk of the RSS–BHA.

Effect of drill-string rotational speed

Figure 10b presents the time histories of the bit rotational speed under different drill-string rotational speed conditions, and the corresponding stick–slip vibration characteristics are summarized in Table 1. As the imposed drill-string rotational speed increases from 40 to 160 r/min, the stick–slip vibration period decreases markedly from 10.45 to 5.09 s. During this process, the stick duration is significantly reduced from 4.69 to 1.07 s, whereas the slip duration exhibits only minor variation. These results indicate that increasing drill-string rotational speed effectively suppresses prolonged stick phases and promotes more continuous bit rotation. Meanwhile, the peak bit rotational speed increases from 109.46 to 365.61 r/min; however, the ratio of the peak rotational speed to the imposed drill-string rotational speed decreases gradually from 2.74 to 2.29. This trend indicates that the relative amplification of the system response is reduced at higher drill-string rotational speeds, suggesting improved utilization efficiency of the input rotational energy. Overall, increasing drill-string rotational speed mitigates the instability associated with stick–slip vibration and contributes to improved drilling stability and operational efficiency.

Sensitivity analysis of whirl vibration

Whirl vibration is a typical form of coupled lateral–torsional vibration. Its periodic lateral motion and repeated contact with the borehole wall can induce drill-string fatigue damage and significantly reduce drilling efficiency4,29,37. To identify the dominant factors governing whirl vibration in the RSS–BHA system, drill-collar diameter, well depth, drill-string rotational speed, and weight on bit are selected for sensitivity analysis. Among these factors, the drill-collar diameter primarily controls the lateral stiffness of the drill-string and its contact behavior with the borehole wall. Well depth characterizes the overall scale effect of the drill-string system, whereas rotational speed and weight on bit jointly regulate the excitation intensity and energy input associated with whirl vibration9,11. The parameter ranges are determined based on typical deep-well drilling conditions to ensure the engineering relevance and practical applicability of the analysis.

Conclusions

A three-dimensional nonlinear coupled vibration model of a static push-the-bit RSS system is established based on finite element dynamics, energy-based methods, and Hamilton’s principle. The model is applied to analyze axial, lateral, and torsional vibrations and their coupled responses of the RSS–BHA under realistic drilling conditions. The main conclusions are summarized as follows:A three-dimensional nonlinear coupled finite element vibration model vibration model is established that enables unified analysis of single vibration modes (axial, torsional, and lateral), pairwise coupled vibrations and fully coupled three-directional vibrations of the RSS–BHA. Field validation demonstrates that the model can accurately reproduce all seven representative vibration types encountered in drilling operations, providing a reliable analytical framework for vibration identification, mechanism analysis, and engineering application in complex drilling conditions.The proposed vibration analysis model successfully reproduces the dynamic characteristics of typical RSS–BHA vibration modes observed in field operations. Torsion-dominated stick–slip vibration and lateral–torsional coupled whirl vibration can be clearly distinguished through their distinct time-domain responses and time–frequency characteristics. Consequently, vibration mitigation strategies should be mode-specific, with stick–slip control focusing on the regulation of torsional excitation, while whirl suppression should prioritize the control of rotational speed and lateral structural characteristics.

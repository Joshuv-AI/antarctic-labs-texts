Borsuk’s conjecture for two–distance sets and its equivalent formulation for graphs
Oleg R. Musin

Abstract
Every graph G G can be embedded in a Euclidean space as a two–distance set. This allows us to reformulate the analogue of Borsuk’s conjecture for two–distance sets in terms of graphs. This conjecture remains open for dimensions from 4 to 63. This short note also discusses an approach for finding counterexamples using graphs, as well as its generalization for s s –distance sets.



1
Introduction

Let
S
be a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
B(S)
denote the smallest number of subsets with diameters smaller than the diameter of
S
that cover
S
. Karol Borsuk conjectured that
B(S)\leq d+1
. It is true for
d=2,3
. In 1993 Jeff Kahn and Gil Kalai showed that if
d\geq 2015
then the conjecture is not true. In subsequent years, the dimension
d
has been consistently reduced. In 2013 Andriy Bondarenko found a counterexample to Borsuk’s conjecture for
d=65

[
3
]
. Soon after, Thomas Jenrich
[
5
]
discovered a 64-dimensional subset in Bondarenko’s counterexample that also fails to satisfy Borsuk’s conjecture.

Bondarenko’s counterexamples were obtained for two–distance sets. A set
S
in Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called a
two–distance set
, if there are two distances
a
and
b
, and the
distances between pairs of distinct points of
S
are either
a
or
b
. We checked the case
d=4
and found no counterexample to Borsuk’s conjectures for two–distance sets. This was later confirmed in
[
10
]
by Ferenc Szöllösi who enumerated all two–distance sets in four dimensions. Danylo Radchenko
[
9
]
informed the author that he had not found such counterexamples for dimensions
d\leq 7
, his algorithm relies on
[
6
]
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph,
n=|V|
,

x_{ij}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}1\;\mbox{ if }e_{ij}\in E\\
t\;\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.,\quad A_{G}(t):=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&1&...&1\\
1&0&x_{12}&...&x_{1n}\\
1&x_{21}&0&...&x_{2n}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
1&x_{n1}&x_{n2}&...&0\end{bmatrix},\quad C_{G}(t)=\det(A_{G}(t)).

[formula]

Suppose that there is a solution to the algebraic equation
C_{G}(t)=0
for
t>1
. Then we denote by
\mu(G)
the multiplicity of the smallest zero
t>1
of this equation. If
C_{G}(t)
has no zeros
t>1
, then we assume that
\mu(G)=0
.

Let
\theta(G)
denote the
clique covering number
, i.e. the smallest number of cliques that together include every vertex of
G
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with
n
vertices. Then
G
can be embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a two–distance set
S
with
d=n-\mu(G)-1
and
B(S)=\theta(G)
. In particular, if

\theta(G)+\mu(G)>n

then
S
is a counterexample for Borsuk’s conjecture.

In Section 2, we go into more detail about representing graphs as two–distance sets and prove this theorem. Much more details and examples are contained in our paper
[
8
]
.
Section 3 discusses possible applications of Theorem 1 and its generalization for
s
–distance sets to finding counterexamples to Borsuk’s conjecture using discrete optimization and machine learning methods.

2
Graphs and two–distance sets

2.1
Two–distance sets.

Let
S=\{p_{1},...,p_{n}\}
be a two–distance in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
a=1
and
b>1
. Let
d_{ij}:=\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits(p_{i},p_{j})
.
Consider the Cayley–Menger determinant

C_{S}:=\begin{vmatrix}0&1&1&...&1\\
1&0&d_{12}^{2}&...&d_{1n}^{2}\\
1&d_{21}^{2}&0&...&d_{2n}^{2}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
1&d_{n1}^{2}&d_{n2}^{2}&...&0\end{vmatrix}

Since for
i\neq j
,
d_{ij}=1
or
b
,
C_{S}
is a polynomial in
t=b^{2}
. Here the connection between the two polynomials
C_{G}
and
C_{S}
is obvious. If
G
is a graph with vertices
\{p_{i}\}
and edges
e_{ij}
, where
d_{ij}=1
, then
C_{G}(t)=C_{S}(t)
.

Einhorn and Schoenberg
[
4
]
showed that
d
can be explicitly expressed in terms of the multiplicity
\mu(S)
of the smallest root
t>1
of the equation
C_{S}(t)=0
:

d=n-\mu(S)-1.

Equivalently, see
[
8
, Sect. 2]
, for graphs we have

\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(G)=n-\mu(G)-1,

[formula]

where
\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(G)
denotes the smallest dimension of the embedding of
G
as a two–distance set.

2.2
Proof of Theorem
1

Proof.

By (2) we can embed
G
into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=n-\mu(G)-1
, as a two-distance set
S
. Since
B(S)=\theta(G)
, then
B(S)>d+1
if and only if
\theta(G)+\mu(G)>n
.
∎

2.3
Spherical two–distance sets.

In
[
8
]
we consider representations of a graph
G
as spherical two–distance sets. Let
f
be a Euclidean representation of
G
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with minimum distance
a=1
. We say that
f
is
spherical
if the image
f(G)
lies on a
(d-1)
–sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We denote by
\mathop{\rm dim^{S}_{2}}\nolimits(G)
the smallest
d
such that
G
is spherically representable in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

If
d\leq n-2
, then
f
is uniquely defined up to isometry
[
8
, Sect. 2]
. Therefore, if
f
is spherical, then the circumradius of
f(G)
is also uniquely defined. We denote it by
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
. If
f
is not spherical or
\mu(G)=0
, then we put
{\mathcal{R}}(G)=\infty
. By Lemma 3.1 in
[
8
]
we have

{\mathcal{R}}(G)=\sqrt{F_{G}(\tau_{1})},

where

F_{G}(t):=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{M_{G}(t)}{C_{G}(t)},\qquad M_{G}(t):=\begin{vmatrix}0&x_{12}&...&x_{1n}\\
x_{21}&0&...&x_{2n}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
x_{n1}&x_{n2}&...&\,0\end{vmatrix},

x_{ij}
are defined in (1), and
\tau_{1}
is the smallest root
t>1
of
C_{G}(t)=0.

Theorem 3.1 in
[
8
]
yields the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with
n
vertices and
{\mathcal{R}}(G)<\infty
. Then
G
can be embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a spherical two–distance set
S
with
d=n-\mu(G)-1
and
B(S)=\theta(G)
. If

\theta(G)+\mu(G)>n

then
S
is a spherical counterexample for Borsuk’s conjecture.

3
Remarks

3.1
Bondarenko’s counterexamples for Borsuk’s conjecture.

Bondarenko
[
3
]
found two spherical two–distance sets:

S_{1}=\{p_{1},...,p_{416}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{65}
with
\langle p_{i},p_{j}\rangle=1/5
or
\langle p_{i},p_{j}\rangle=-1/15
for
i\neq j
,
B(S_{1})\geq 84
,

and

S_{2}=\{p_{1},...,p_{31671}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{782}
with
\langle p_{i},p_{j}\rangle=1/10
or
\langle p_{i},p_{j}\rangle=-1/80
for
i\neq j
,
B(S_{2})\geq 1377
.

Jenrich’s counterexample in
[
5
]
is

S_{3}=\{p_{1},...,p_{352}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{64}
with
B(S_{3})\geq 71
.

Let
S
,
|S|=n
, be a two–distance set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, see
[
1
,
2
]
, we have

n\leq\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{2}.

Bondarenko and Jenrich’s counterexamples are spherical and satisfy the assumption of Theorem 1 in
[
7
]
. That yields a better bound

n\leq c_{2}(d):=\frac{d(d+1)}{2}.

However,

|S_{1}|=416<c_{2}(65)=2145,\;|S_{2}|=31671<c_{2}(782)=306153,\;|S_{3}|=352<c_{2}(64)=2080.

Thus, the cardinalities of
S_{k}
are much less than the maximum possible in their dimensions.

3.2
A possible strategy for finding counterexamples.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices. Then, see
[
4
,
8
]
,

(i)
\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(G)=n-1
, i.e.
\mu(G)=0
, if and only if
G
is a disjoint union of cliques.

(ii)
If
d=\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(G)\leq n-2
, then a Euclidean representation of
G
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as a two–distance set with fixed minimum distance
a=1
is uniquely defined up to isometry.

Let
\mathop{\rm Clq}\nolimits(G)
denote the minimal disjoint union of cliques in
G
. If
C_{0}
is a disjoint union of
m
cliques
\{c_{1},...,c_{m}\}
and
\mathop{\rm Clq}\nolimits(G)=C_{0}
, then
\theta(G)=m
. Therefore, if

m+\mu(G)>n

then
G
is a counterexample for Borsuk’s conjecture.

Let us fix
C_{0}
and consider
G
for which
\mathop{\rm Clq}\nolimits(G)=C_{0}
. Our goal is to find
G
with largest possible
\mu
. If
G=C_{0}
then by (i)
\mu(G)=0
. In this case, adding any edge between clicks increases
\mu
. We can continue adding and removing edges between cliques, increasing
\mu
as long as possible. If at some point we reach a local maximum of
\mu
, we can try to consider other graphs and find graphs with larger
\mu
for them by adding and removing edges. Here,
C_{0}
play an important role. In Bondarenko’s counterexamples, almost all cliques have the same size. Perhaps such
C_{0}
should be used to find counterexamples.

3.3
On the generalization of Theorem 1 for
s
–distance sets

First consider an equivalent definition of graph representations. There are two Euclidean representation numbers
\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(G)
and
\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(\bar{G})
, where
\bar{G}
is the graph complement of
G
. These numbers can be different. Indeed, there is an obvious relation between polynomials
C_{G}(t)
and
C_{\bar{G}}(t)
. Namely,
C_{\bar{G}}(t)
is the reciprocal polynomial of
C_{G}(t)
. If
G
or
\bar{G}
is not the complete multipartite graph, then
\tau_{0}(G):=1/\tau_{1}(\bar{G})
is a zero of
C_{G}(t)
and there are no more zeros in the interval
I:=[\tau_{0}(G),\tau_{1}(G)]
. Moreover, a two–distance set
S
with distances 1 and
\sqrt{t}
is well–defined only if
t\in I

[
4
]
.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph on
n
vertices. We have
E(K_{n})=E(G)\cup E(\bar{G})
. Then it is can be considered as a coloring of
E(K_{n})
in two colors.

Let
L(e):=i
if
e\in E_{i}
. Then
L:E(K_{n})\to\{1,2\}
is a coloring of
E(K_{n})
. A representation
L
as a two–distance set is an embedding
f
of
V(K_{n})
into
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits(f(u),f(v)))=a_{i}
for
[uv]\in E_{i}
. For
s
–distance sets, it is convenient to swap
G
and
\bar{G}
when the maximum distance is equal to 1, i.e.
a_{1}=1>a_{2}>0
.

This definition can be extended to any number of colors. Let
L:E(K_{n})\to\{1,\ldots,s\}
be a coloring of the set of edges of a complete graph
K_{n}
. Then

E(K_{n})=E(G_{1})\cup\ldots\cup E(G_{s}),\;E(G_{i}):=\{e\in E(K_{n}):L(e)=i\}.

We say that an embedding
f
of the vertex set of
K_{n}
into
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a
Euclidean representation of a coloring
L
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as an
s
–distance set
if there are
s
positive real numbers
a_{1}=1>\ldots>a_{s}
such that
\mathop{\rm dist}\nolimits(f(u),f(v)))=a_{i}
if and only if
[uv]\in E(G_{i})
.

Let
\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(L)
denotes the smallest dimension of the embedding of
L
as an
s
–distance set. Then a coloring
L
defines a counterexample for Borsuk’s conjecture only if

\theta(\bar{G}_{1})>\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(L)+1.

It is easy to extend the definitions of
A_{G}(t)
and
C_{G}(t)
in (1) for
s
–distance sets.
In this case we have a matrix
A_{L}(t_{2},\ldots,t_{s})
and a polynomial
C_{L}(t_{2},\ldots,t_{s})
, where
a_{1}=1
and
t_{i}=a_{i}^{2}<1
for
i=2,\ldots,s
. Similarly to
s=2
, for the Euclidean representation
L
can be defined
M_{L}(t_{2},\ldots,t_{s})
. Then this embedding is spherical only if
H_{L}(t_{2},\ldots,t_{s})
is well defined, where

H_{L}(t_{2},\ldots,t_{s}):=\frac{M_{L}(t_{2},\ldots,t_{s})}{C_{L}(t_{2},\ldots,t_{s})}.

The main problem here is to develop an efficient algorithm that finds
t_{2},...,t_{s}
such that the rank of
A_{L}
, i.e.
\mathop{\rm dim^{E}_{2}}\nolimits(L)
, is minimal. It is possible that a similar approach as in 3.2 can be used to find counterexamples among
s
–distance sets to Borsuk’s conjecture.

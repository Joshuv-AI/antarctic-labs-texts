Semi-flat metrics of the moduli spaces of Higgs bundles in the non-zero degree case
Takuro Mochizuki

Abstract
We study horizontal deformations of a Higgs bundle whose spectral curve is smooth. It allows us to define a natural integrable connection of the Hitchin fibration on the locus where the spectral curves are smooth. Then, in the non-zero degree case, we introduce the semi-flat metric, and compare the asymptotic behaviour of the semi-flat metric and the Hitchin metric along the ray ( E , t ​ θ ) (E,t\theta) ( t → ∞ ) (t\to\infty) . MSC: 53C07, 58E15, 14D21, 81T13. Keywords: Hitchin metric, semi-flat metric, Higgs bundle, spectral curve.



1
Introduction

1.1
Moduli space of stable Higgs bundles

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{H}(X,n,d)
denote the moduli space of
stable Higgs bundles of rank
n
and degree
d
on
X
.
By the theorem of Hitchin and Simpson

[
6
,
11
]
,
it has a naturally defined hyperkähler metric

g_{H}^{X,n,d}
, called the Hitchin metric.
(We shall recall the construction in
§
4.1.2
.)

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,d)\subset\mathcal{M}_{H}(X,n,d)

denote the Zariski open subset
of Higgs bundles whose spectral curves are smooth.
We set
{\boldsymbol{A}}_{X,n}:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H^{0}(X,K_{X}^{j})
.
Let
\Phi_{n,d}:\mathcal{M}_{H}(X,n,d)\to{\boldsymbol{A}}_{X,n}

denote the Hitchin fibration (see §
4.2.1
).
We set
{\boldsymbol{A}}_{X,n}^{\prime}=\Phi_{n,d}(\mathcal{M}_{H}(X,n,d)^{\prime})
.
The restriction
\Phi_{n,d}:\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,d)\to{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}

is locally a principal torus bundle (see §
4.3.1
).

1.2
Semi-flat metric in the case
d=0

In the case
d=0
,

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)
has another hyperkähler metric

g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}^{X,n,0}
,
called the semi-flat metric.
Let us recall the construction.
There exists the Hitchin section

{\boldsymbol{A}}_{X,n}\to\mathcal{M}_{H}(X,n,0)

of
\Phi_{n,0}
.
It induces a section

{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}\to\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)
.
We obtain the integrable connection of
the locally principal torus bundle
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)\to{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}

for which the Hitchin section is horizontal.
For each
(E,\theta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)
,
we obtain the decomposition of the tangent space
into the vertical direction and the horizontal direction:

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)=T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits}\oplus T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}.

(1)

Here,

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits}

denotes the tangent space of the fiber

T_{(E,\theta)}\Phi_{n,0}^{-1}(\Phi_{n,0}(E,\theta))
,
and

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}

denotes the subspace determined by the integrable connection.
According to
[
9
, Corollary 3.33]
,
the horizontal space

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}
is Lagrangian.
Let
\Sigma_{\theta}
denote the spectral curve of
the Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
.
Because

\Phi_{n,0}^{-1}(\Phi_{n,0}(E,\theta))

is a connected component of the moduli space of holomorphic line bundles
on
\Sigma_{\theta}
,
there exists the following natural isomorphism

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits}\simeq H^{1}(\Sigma_{\theta},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{\theta}}).

As well known in
[
6
]
,
each fiber of the Hitchin fibration
\Phi_{n,0}
is Lagrangian,
and hence

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)^{\mathop{\rm hor}\nolimits}\simeq\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits\Bigl(T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)^{\mathop{\rm ver}\nolimits},{\mathbb{C}}\Bigr)\simeq H^{0}(\Sigma_{\theta},K_{\Sigma_{\theta}}).

Therefore, there exists the natural isomorphism between

T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}(X,n,0)

and the space of harmonic
1
-forms on
\Sigma_{\theta}
.
The natural
L^{2}
-metric on the
1
-forms on
\Sigma_{\theta}

induces
a metric
g^{X,n,0}_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits|(E,\theta)}

on
T_{(E,\theta)}\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}(X,n,0)
.
In this way, we obtain
a metric
g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}^{X,n,0}
on
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,0)
.
According to
[
4
]
, it is hyperkähler.
(See also
[
9
, Proposition 4.6]

and Proposition
4.11
.)

We can think that
g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}^{X,n,0}

is easier compared with
g_{H}^{X,n,0}

in the sense that
g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}^{X,n,0}
is determined by
the geometry of
\mathcal{M}_{H}^{\prime}(X,n,0)
.

1.3
Comparison of the Hitchin metric
and the semi-flat metric in the case
d=0

In
[
5
]
,
Gaiotto, Moore and Neitzke proposed a beautiful conjectural description
of the hyperkähler structure of
\mathcal{M}_{H}(X,n,d)
.
In particular, it implies the following estimate
for any
(E,\theta)\in\mathcal{M}_{H}(X,n,0)
with respect to

(g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}^{X,n,0})_{|(E,t\theta)}
as
t\to\infty
:

\bigl(g_{H}^{X,n,0}-g^{X,n,0}_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}\bigr)_{|(E,t\theta)}=O\bigl(\exp(-\epsilon t)\bigr).

(2)

Here,
\epsilon
denotes a positive number depending on

(E,\theta)
.
The estimate (
2
) was studied in

[
3
,
2
,
8
]

and later in

[
9
,
10
]
.

1.4
The main result of this paper

It is natural to study an analogue of (
2
)
in the case
d\neq 0
.
In the case
d=0
,
as explained in §
1.2
,
we obtain the integrable connection
of
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,d)\to{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}

by using the Hitchin section.
It induces the decomposition of the tangent space
(
1
),
which we used to construct
g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}^{X,n,0}
.
It is not directly applied to the case
d\neq 0
.

We first study
the notion of horizontal deformations
(see §
3
).
It allows us to obtain a natural integrable connection of
the locally principal torus bundle

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,d)\to{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}
,
whose horizontal spaces are Lagrangian,
and the semi-flat metric
g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}^{X,n,d}

as in the case
d=0
.
Then, we obtain an analogue of
(
2
)
in the case
d\neq 0
.

Theorem 1.1
(Theorem

4.14
)

For any
(E,\theta)\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,d)
,
there exists
\epsilon>0

such that the following estimate holds
as
t\to\infty
with respect to
(g_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}^{X,n,d})_{|(E,t\theta)}
:

\bigl(g_{H}^{X,n,d}-g^{X,n,d}_{\mathop{\rm sf}\nolimits}\bigr)_{|(E,t\theta)}=O\bigl(\exp(-\epsilon t)\bigr).

(3)

We shall explain how to reduce the estimate (
3
)
to the estimate (
2
) in the case
d=0

in
[
9
]
.

2
Horizontal deformations of line bundles in a general setting

2.1
Preliminaries

Let
B
be a multi-disc.
Let
f:Z\to B
be a proper morphism of complex manifolds
such that each fiber is a compact Riemann surface.
In particular, each fiber is assumed to be connected.
For each
b\in B
,
we set
Z_{b}:=f^{-1}(b)
,
and let
\iota_{b}:Z_{b}\to Z
denote the inclusion.
Because
Z
is diffeomorphic to
B\times Z_{b}

for any
b\in B
,
there exists a natural isomorphism

H_{1}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq H_{1}(Z_{b},{\mathbb{Z}})
.
For any coherent
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
-module

\mathcal{F}
flat over
f^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{B})
,
we set
\mathcal{F}_{b}:=\iota_{b}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})
.
We also set

\deg(\mathcal{F}):=\deg(\iota_{b}^{\ast}\mathcal{F})

which is independent of
b\in B
.

We set
U(1)=\bigl\{a\in{\mathbb{C}}\,\big|\,|a|=1\bigr\}
.
We identify
H^{1}(Z,U(1))

with the group of homomorphisms
of abelian groups
H_{1}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})\to U(1)
.
For any
\rho\in H^{1}(Z,U(1))
,
we obtain the induced unitary flat line bundle

\mathcal{L}(\rho)
on
Z
.
The underlying holomorphic line bundle is also denoted by

\mathcal{L}(\rho)
.

For any non-zero integer
e
,
we set
\mu_{e}=\bigl\{a\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,a^{e}=1\bigr\}\subset U(1)
.
The subgroup

H^{1}(Z,\mu_{e})\subset H^{1}(Z,U(1))

is identified with the group of homomorphisms
of abelian groups
H_{1}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})\to\mu_{e}
.

The following lemma is well known.

Lemma 2.1

Let
b\in B
.
For any line bundle
N
on
Z_{b}
of degree
0
,
there uniquely exists
\rho\in H^{1}(Z,U(1))

such that
N\simeq\mathcal{L}(\rho)_{b}
.

Lemma 2.2

Let
L
be any holomorphic line bundle on
Z

with
\deg(L)=0
.
Then, we have either

f_{\ast}(L)=0

or

f_{\ast}(L)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}
.

Proof
Because
B
is a multi-disc,
it is enough to prove that

f_{\ast}(L)
is locally free sheaf of
rank
1
or
0
.
We have only to check the claim locally around
any point of
B
.

Let
b\in B
.
Let
U_{b}
be any small neighbourhood of
b
in
B

which is isomorphic to a multi-disc.
We obtain
Z_{U_{b}}:=Z\times_{B}U_{b}
,
and the induced morphism
f_{U_{b}}:Z_{U_{b}}\to U_{b}
.
The restriction of
L
to
Z_{U_{b}}

is denoted by
L_{U_{b}}
.
We may assume that there exists a holomorphic section

h:U_{b}\to Z_{U_{b}}
of
f_{U_{b}}
.
Let
H\subset Z_{U_{b}}
denote the image of
h
.
By shrinking
U_{b}
,
we may assume that there exists
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

such that
R^{1}(f_{U_{b}})_{\ast}\bigl(L_{U_{b}}(mH)\bigr)=0
.
Then,
(f_{U_{b}})_{\ast}(L_{U_{b}}(mH))
is a locally free

\mathcal{O}_{U_{b}}
-module.
Because there exists a natural monomorphism

(f_{U_{b}})_{\ast}(L_{U_{b}})\to(f_{U_{b}})_{\ast}\bigl(L_{U_{b}}(mH)\bigr)
,
we obtain that

(f_{U_{b}})_{\ast}(L_{U_{b}})

is a torsion-free
\mathcal{O}_{U_{b}}
-module.

If
L_{c}\not\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Z_{c}}

for some
c\in U_{b}
,
there exists a neighbourhood
U_{c}
in
U_{b}

such that

L_{c^{\prime}}\not\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Z_{c^{\prime}}}

for any
c^{\prime}\in U_{c}
.
Because

\dim H^{0}(Z_{c^{\prime}},L_{c^{\prime}})=0

for
c^{\prime}\in U_{c}
,
we obtain
f_{\ast}(L_{U_{c}})=0
.
Because
f_{\ast}(L_{U_{b}})
is torsion-free,
we obtain

f_{\ast}(L_{U_{b}})=0
.
If
L_{c}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Z_{c}}
for any
c\in U_{b}
,
we obtain that

\dim H^{0}(Z_{c},L_{c})=1
for any
c\in U_{b}
,
and that
\dim H^{1}(Z_{c},L_{c})
are constant
for
c\in U_{b}
.
Hence,

f_{\ast}(L_{U_{b}})

is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{U_{b}}
-module of rank
1
.

2.2
Roots of line bundles

We recall the following standard fact.

Proposition 2.3

Let
M
be any holomorphic line bundle on
Z
.
Let
e\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
be any divisor of
\deg(M)
,
i.e.,
a non-zero integer such that
\deg(M)\in e{\mathbb{Z}}
.

•

There exists a holomorphic line bundle
M_{1}

with an isomorphism

M_{1}^{e}\simeq M
.

•

Let
M_{2}
be another holomorphic line bundle
with an isomorphism

M_{2}^{e}\simeq M
.
Then,
there exists

\rho\in H^{1}(Z,\mu_{e})

such that

M_{2}=M_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho)
.

•

Let
b\in B
.
For any holomorphic line bundle
N
on
Z_{b}

with an isomorphism

N^{e}\simeq M_{b}
,
there exists a holomorphic line bundle

N_{B}
on
B

such that

(N_{B})_{b}\simeq N

and

N_{B}^{e}\simeq M
.

Proof
We explain a proof just for the convenience of readers.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times}
denote the sheaf of
nowhere vanishing holomorphic functions.
Recall that
the isomorphism classes of holomorphic line bundles
on
Z
is classified by
H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times})
.
Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{Z}
denote the sheaf of

{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued continuous functions on
Z
.
From the exact sequence of sheaves

0\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times}\longrightarrow 0
,
we obtain the exact sequence of modules

H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow H^{2}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}).

Let
\alpha\in H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times})

be the element corresponding to
M
.
If
a_{2}(\alpha)\neq 0
, which is mapped to
0
in
H^{2}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})
,
we obtain that
a_{2}
is surjective
because
H^{2}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space.
Hence,
there exists
\beta\in H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times})

such that

a_{2}(\alpha-e\beta)=0
.
If
a_{2}(\alpha)=0
,
by setting
\beta=\alpha
,
we obtain
a_{2}(\alpha-e\beta)=0
.
Because
H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})
is
a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space,
there exists

\gamma\in H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})

such that

a_{1}\bigl(e\cdot\gamma\bigr)=\alpha-e\cdot\beta
.
Let
M_{1}
be a line bundle corresponding to

\beta-a_{1}(\gamma)
.
Then, there exists an isomorphism

M_{1}^{e}\simeq M
.

Let
M_{2}
be another holomorphic line bundle on
Z

with an isomorphism
M_{2}^{e}\simeq M
.
There exists the following exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\mu_{e}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times}\longrightarrow 0.

Here,
the morphism
b
is defined by
b(g)=g^{e}
.
We obtain the following induced exact sequence:

H^{1}(Z,\mu_{e})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b_{3}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}(Z,\mu_{e}).

Let
\alpha_{i}\in H^{1}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\times})

be the elements corresponding to
M_{i}
.
Because

M_{1}^{e}\simeq M_{2}^{e}
,
we obtain

b_{2}(\alpha_{1})=b_{2}(\alpha_{2})
.
There exists

\beta\in H^{1}(Z,\mu_{e})

such that

\alpha_{1}=b_{1}(\beta)\alpha_{2}
.
Let

\rho:H_{1}(Z_{b},{\mathbb{Z}})\to\mu_{e}

be the homomorphism corresponding to
\beta
.
It is easy to see that

M_{1}\simeq M_{2}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho)
.

Let
b\in B
.
Let
N
be a holomorphic line bundle on
Z_{b}

with an isomorphism
N^{e}\simeq M_{b}
.
Because

N^{e}\simeq(M_{1})_{b}^{e}
,
there exists

\rho\in H^{1}(Z,U(1))

such that

N\simeq(M_{1})_{b}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho)_{b}
.
We set
N_{B}=M_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho)
.
Then,
there exist desired isomorphisms

(N_{B})_{b}\simeq N

and

N_{B}^{e}\simeq M
.

Remark 2.4

Because
B
is assumed to be multi-disc,
it is easy to observe
H^{2}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z})=0
in the proof.

2.3
Horizontal line bundles with respect to
a prescribed holomorphic line bundle

Let
M
be a holomorphic line bundle on
Z
.
Assume that
\deg(M)\neq 0
.

Definition 2.5

A holomorphic line bundle
L
on
Z

is called horizontal with respect to
M

if the following holds.

•

Let
M_{1}
be a holomorphic line bundle on
Z

with an isomorphism

M_{1}^{\deg(M)}\simeq M
.
Then,
there exists

\rho\in H^{1}(Z,U(1))

such that

L\simeq M_{1}^{\deg(L)}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho)
.
This condition is independent of the choice of
M_{1}
.

Remark 2.6

If
\deg(L)=0
,
the condition is independent of
M
.

Lemma 2.7

Let
L_{i}

(i=1,2)
be holomorphic line bundles
on
Z
.
If
L_{i}
are horizontal with respect to
M
,
then
L_{1}^{n_{1}}\otimes L_{2}^{n_{2}}
are also horizontal
for any
(n_{1},n_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

Proof
There exit
\rho_{i}\in H^{1}(Z,U(1))

such that

L_{i}\simeq M_{1}^{\deg(L_{i})}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho_{i})
.
Because

L_{1}^{n_{1}}\otimes L_{2}^{n_{2}}=M_{1}^{n_{1}\deg(L_{1})+n_{2}\deg(L_{2})}\otimes\mathcal{L}(n_{1}\rho_{1}+n_{2}\rho_{2}),

the claim is clear.

Proposition 2.8

Let
N
be any holomorphic line bundle on
Z_{b}

for some
b\in B
.

•

There exists a holomorphic line bundle

N_{B}
on
Z

with an isomorphism

(N_{B})_{b}\simeq N

such that
N_{B}
is horizontal with respect to
M
.

•

Let
N_{B}^{\prime}
be another such horizontal line bundle
on
Z

with an isomorphism

(N_{B}^{\prime})_{b}\simeq N
.
Then, there exists
an isomorphism

N_{B}^{\prime}\simeq N_{B}

whose restriction to

Z_{b}

equals
the composition of

(N_{B}^{\prime})_{b}\simeq N\simeq(N_{B})_{b}
.

Proof
Let
M_{1}
be a holomorphic line bundle on
Z

with an isomorphism
M_{1}^{\deg(M)}\simeq M
.
There exists

\rho\in H^{1}(Z,U(1))

such that

N\simeq(M_{1})^{\deg(N)}_{b}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho)_{b}
.
We obtain a desired line bundle

N_{B}=M_{1}^{\deg(N)}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho)

with an isomorphism

(N_{B})_{b}\simeq N
.

Let
N_{B}^{\prime}

be another horizontal holomorphic line bundle on
Z

with an isomorphism

(N_{B}^{\prime})_{b}\simeq N
.
There exists

\rho^{\prime}\in H^{1}(Z,U(1))

such that

N_{B}^{\prime}=M_{1}^{\deg(N)}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho^{\prime})
.
Because

(N_{B})_{b}\simeq(N_{B}^{\prime})_{b}
,
we obtain

\mathcal{L}(\rho)_{b}\simeq\mathcal{L}(\rho^{\prime})_{b}
,
which implies
\rho=\rho^{\prime}
.
Hence, there exists a desired isomorphism

N_{B}^{\prime}\simeq N_{B}
.

Lemma 2.9

Let
M_{i}

(i=1,2)
be holomorphic line bundles on
Z

such that
there exist non-zero integers
m_{i}

such that
M_{1}^{m_{1}}\simeq M_{2}^{m_{2}}
.
Then, a holomorphic line bundle
N
on
Z

is horizontal with respect to
M_{1}

if and only if it is horizontal with respect to
M_{2}
.

Proof
By definition,
it is easy to see that

N
is horizontal with respect to
M_{i}

if and only if
N
is horizontal with respect to
M_{i}^{m_{i}}
.
Then, the claim is clear.

2.4
Pull back by a covering map

Let
f^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}\to B
be
a proper morphism of complex manifolds
such that each fiber is a compact Riemann surface.
Suppose that there exists a holomorphic covering map

\psi:Z^{\prime}\to Z
such that
f^{\prime}=f\circ\psi
.
For any

\rho\in H^{1}(Z,U(1))
,
we obtain

\psi^{\ast}(\rho)\in H^{1}(Z^{\prime},U(1))
.
There exists a natural isomorphism

\psi^{\ast}\mathcal{L}(\rho)\simeq\mathcal{L}(\psi^{\ast}\rho)
.
Let
\mathcal{K}
denote
the kernel of

\psi^{\ast}:H^{1}(Z,U(1))\to H^{1}(Z^{\prime},U(1))
.

Lemma 2.10

\mathcal{K}
is a finite abelian group.

Proof
Let
e
denote the degree of the covering map
\psi
.
Let
I\subset H_{1}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})

denote the image of

H_{1}(Z^{\prime},{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{1}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
For any
\gamma\in H_{1}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
there exists
a positive integer
m(\gamma)

such that
0<m(\gamma)\leq e

and
m(\gamma)\gamma\in I
.
Hence, there exists a positive integer
m
such that

m\cdot H_{1}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})\subset I
.
If
\rho\in\mathcal{K}
,
the restriction of
\rho
to
m\cdot H_{1}(Z,{\mathbb{Z}})

is trivial,
and hence we obtain

\mathcal{K}\subset H^{1}(Z,\mu_{m})
.

Lemma 2.11

Let
L
be a holomorphic line bundle on
Z

such that
\psi^{\ast}(L)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}}
.
Then, there exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}

such that
L\simeq\mathcal{L}(\rho)
.

Proof
For each
b\in B
,
there exists

\rho(b)\in\mathcal{K}

such that

L_{b}\simeq\mathcal{L}(\rho(b))_{b}
.
Because the dependence of
\rho(b)
on
b\in B

is continuous,
we obtain that there exists
\rho\in\mathcal{K}

such that

L_{b}\simeq\mathcal{L}(\rho)_{b}

for any
b\in B
.
We set

\widetilde{L}=L\otimes\mathcal{L}(\rho)^{-1}
.
By using Lemma
2.2
,
we obtain

f_{\ast}(\widetilde{L})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B}
.

4.3
Semi-flat metrics in the non-zero degree case

4.3.1
Locally principal bundles and integrable connections

For any
s\in{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}
,
let
U_{s}
be a neighbourhood of
s
in
{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}
,
which is isomorphic to a multi-disc.
For any

,
we set

T(s^{\prime}):=H^{1}(\Sigma_{s^{\prime}},U(1))
.
We can regard
T(s^{\prime})
as
the moduli space of flat line bundles on
\Sigma_{s^{\prime}}
,
which naturally acts on

\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits_{N(n,d)}(\Sigma_{s^{\prime}})

by the tensor product of the underlying holomorphic line bundles.
The action is transitive and free.
A path connecting
s
and
s^{\prime}

induces an isomorphism
T(s)\simeq T(s^{\prime})
.
Hence,
we obtain the right
T(s)
-action on
\Phi_{n,d}^{-1}(U_{s})
:

\rho:\Phi_{n,d}^{-1}(U_{s})\times T(s)\longrightarrow\Phi_{n,d}^{-1}(U_{s}),

with which

\Phi_{n,d}^{-1}(U_{s})\to U_{s}

is a principal
T(s)
-bundle.

Let
(E,\theta_{E})\in\Phi_{n,d}^{-1}(s)
.
According to Proposition
3.12
,
there exists a relative Higgs bundle

(\mathcal{E},\theta)
on
U_{s}\times X
over
U_{s}

such that
(i)
\Sigma_{\theta}=U_{s}\times_{{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}}Z_{X,n}
,
(ii)
(\mathcal{E},\theta)
is horizontal,
(iii)
(\mathcal{E},\theta)_{|s}\simeq(E,\theta_{E})
.
It induces a section

H_{(E,\theta)}:U_{s}\to\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{H}(X,n,d)\times_{{\boldsymbol{A}}^{\prime}_{X,n}}U_{s}

of the Hitchin fibration.
By the construction,
we have

H_{\rho((E,\theta),a)}(s^{\prime})=\rho\bigl(H_{(E,\theta)}(s^{\prime}),a\bigr)\quad(s^{\prime}\in U_{s},\,\,a\in T(s)).

Therefore,
we obtain the unique integrable connection
of the
T(s)
-principal bundle

\Phi_{n,d}^{-1}(U_{s})
over
U_{s}

such that

H_{(E,\theta)}

((E,\theta)\in\Phi_{n,d}^{-1}(s))

are horizontal sections.

be a small neighbourhood.

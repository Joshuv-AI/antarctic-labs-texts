Equilibrium measures of manifolds without conjugate points having visibility covering
Edhin Mamani

Abstract
In this paper we study the equilibrium measures of geodesic flows of closed manifolds without conjugate points which have a visibility universal covering. Specifically, the uniqueness problem for Bowen potentials which are constants on some sets–intersection of horospheres– and satisfy a weak pressure gap. Moreover, we study some ergodic properties of these measures such as the K-mixing property, weighted equidistribution of closed geodesics, the Gibbs property, large deviations and the entropy density of ergodic measures. Assuming, furthermore continuity of Green bundles, existence of a hyperbolic closed geodesic and a Gromov hyperbolic universal covering we prove that the above potentials always satisfy the weak pressure gap.



1
Introduction

One of the most important examples of dynamical systems of geometric origin is the geodesic flow. These flows model the movement of a particle without any forces acting on it. It was the inspiration and the toy model for many developments in dynamical systems and ergodic theory.

In the 1960’s, Sinai, Ruelle, Bowen among others established a framework for the study of ergodic properties of dynamical systems called today Thermodynamics Formalism. Among other things, they proved the existence and uniqueness of the measure of maximal entropy for the geodesic flow of a compact manifold of variable negative curvature. The thermodynamic formalism provided a way to generalize the measure of maximal entropy, the so-called equilibrium measures or states. These measures are analogues of physical concepts coming from the Statistical Physics and Thermodynamics. In the 1980’s, for compact manifolds of negative curvature,
[
3
]
proved the existence and uniqueness of equilibrium measures of Holder continuous potentials.

The methods used by Bowen to show the uniqueness of equilibrium measures were based on the expansivity and the specification property of the flow. In 2014, Climenhaga and Thompsom extended these methods to non-uniform versions of expansivity and specification
[
9
]
. Using this method Burns-Climenhaga-Fisher-Thompson proved the existence and uniqueness of equilibrium measures of Holder potentials for rank-1 manifolds of non-positive manifolds
[
6
]
. On the other hand, using symbolic dynamics Lima and Poletti reproved the same result and showed the Bernoullicity of the measures
[
26
]
.

Another way to extend the analysis is to consider more general Riemannian metrics. The natural generalizations of Riemannian metrics of non-positive curvature are the metrics without focal points and metrics without conjugate points. These metrics have mostly negative curvature while allowing some zero and positive curvature.

For the measure of maximal entropy Gelfert-Ruggiero, Climenhaga-Knieper-War and Mamani-Ruggiero
[
20
,
8
,
27
]
extended the uniqueness to the cases of no-focal points and no-conjugate points. For the case of equilibrium measures, there were extensions using the methods of Climenhaga and Thompson.

On the other hand, the case of manifolds without conjugate points proved to be more complicated. For surfaces without conjugate points, Wu used the general results of Lima-Poletti which holds in a more general setting. In this context, assuming furthermore the continuity of Green bundles and a visibility universal covering Wu showed the existence and uniqueness of equilibrium measures of Holder potentials satisfying the generalized gap pressure
[
39
]
. Following these works, we contribute to the case of manifolds without conjugate points as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering. If
f:T_{1}M\to\mathbb{R}
is a Bowen potential which is constant on classes and has the weak pressure gap then
f
has a unique equilibrium measure
\mu
and satisfies the following properties,

1.

\mu
is K-mixing.

2.

The
f
-pressure of periodic prime orbits agree with topological pressure of
f
.

3.

Periodic prime orbits weighted by
f
equidistribute to
\mu
.

4.

\mu
has the Gibbs property on expansive points.

5.

\mu
satisfies the level-1 large deviations principle for Bowen potentials which are constant on classes.

6.

The geodesic flow has a restricted entropy density of ergodic measures.

Comparing this theorem to Wu’s result, note that we maintain the visibility hypothesis. This is because in higher dimensions the behavior can be very complicated and hence we assume some global geometry assumption in order to have some tools available. On the other hand, we remove the hypothesis of continuity of Green bundles assumed by Wu. However, we change the family of potentials for which the equilibrium measures are unique.

In the original Bowen work about the uniqueness of equilibrium measures, he was able to prove this property for potentials more general than Holder potentials, now called Bowen potentials. In manifolds of negative curvature, Holder potentials are Bowen potentials. In the non-uniformly case, the relation between them is more diffuse but they both include the constant potentials, in particular the zero potential which corresponds to the measure of maximal entropy.

The other hypothesis imposed on the potentials considered in Theorem x seems to be a bit technical but it is common to the proof strategy that we follow. That is, we use some factor flow of the geodesic flow with uniqueness of equilibrium measures for Bowen potentials and then we somehow try to lift this property to the geodesic flow. Indeed, under our assumptions it is possible to build an equivalence relation on our phase space (unit tangent bundle) and hence define a quotient space. The geodesic flow induces a factor flow acting on the quotient space and this flow has the desired property. Since our potentials are defined on the unit tangent bundle, to induce potentials on the quotient space, we require that our potentials be constant on the equivalence classes. The use of a factor flow or factor map to show uniqueness of equilibrium measures is used in other contexts. In these contexts, it is also necessary to consider potentials constant on certain sets in order to apply the method.

Concerning the last hypothesis, for manifolds of nonpositive curvature, Burns-Climenhaga-Fisher-Thompson showed the pressure gap for potentials which are locally constant on a neighborhood of the singular set, i.e., the set where there is lack of hyperbolicity. In the setting of Theorem
1.1
, we can prove a weak pressure gap in particular contexts as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with Gromov hyperbolic universal covering. If
M
has a hyperbolic closed geodesic and continuous Green bundles then every Bowen potential which is constant on classes, satisfy the weak pressure gap. Moreover, the unique equilibrium measure
\mu
of
f
is fully supported. In particular,
\mu
satisfies all the properties of Theorem
1.1
.

Now, every

2.2
Visibility manifolds

Let
M
be a simply connected Riemannian manifold without conjugate points. We say that
M
is a visibility manifold if for every
z\in M
and every
\epsilon>0
there exists
R(\epsilon,z)>0
such that if

with

then
\sphericalangle_{z}(x,y)<\epsilon
, where
[x,y]
is the unique geodesic segment joining
x
to
y
and
\sphericalangle_{z}(x,y)
is the angle at
z
formed by
[z,x]
and
[z,y]
. If
R(\epsilon,z)
does not depend on

then
M
is called a uniform visibility manifold. Let us note that a visibility universal covering of a compact manifold without conjugate points is actually a uniform visibility manifold
[
13
]
.

Visibility manifolds are important because of the good dynamical properties of their geodesic flows. Recall that a foliation is minimal if its leaves are dense.

(
[
13
,
14
]

Let

\mathcal{F}^{s}
and
\mathcal{F}^{u}
are continuous minimal foliations of
T_{1}M
invariant by
\phi_{t}
.

2.

The geodesic flow is topologically mixing.

Similarly, the families

and
\mathcal{\tilde{F}}^{u}
are continuous invariant foliations of

usually called stable and unstable horospherical foliations. Hence

and
\mathcal{\tilde{F}}^{u}(\theta)
are respectively the stable and unstable horospherical leaf of
\theta\in T_{1}\tilde{M}
.

We now consider intersections between horospherical leaves. For every
\theta\in T_{1}M

maps

2.3
Jacobi fields and Green bundles

Let
(M,g)
be a closed
n
-manifold without conjugate points,
\theta\in T_{1}M

\gamma_{\theta}
be the geodesic induced by

J^{\prime\prime}(t)+R(\dot{\gamma}_{\theta}(t),J(t))\dot{\gamma}_{\theta}(t)=0,

for all

\theta

\mathcal{J}_{\theta}
the vector subspace of orthogonal Jacobi fields along
\gamma_{\theta}

J_{\xi}
be the Jacobi field on
\gamma_{\theta}

J_{\xi}(0)=\xi_{h}

2.4
Topological and metric pressure

We recall some basic notions of ergodic theory. Let
\Phi(\phi_{t}:X\to X)
be a continuous flow acting on a compact metric space
X
. Given
\epsilon,T>0
and
x\in X
, the
(\epsilon,T)
-dynamical ball is defined by

B(x,\epsilon,T)=\{y\in X:d(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))<\epsilon,\text{ for }t\in[0,T]\}.

A subset
E\subset X
is called
(\epsilon,T)
-spanning if
X=\bigcup_{x\in E}B(x,\epsilon,T)
. A continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is called potential on
X
. Given a potential
f
, varying over all possible
(\epsilon,T)
-spanning sets
E
, we define

S(f,\Phi,\epsilon,T)=\inf\left\{\sum_{x\in E}e^{\int_{0}^{T}f(\phi_{t}(x))dt}:E\text{ is a }(\epsilon,T)\text{-spanning set}\right\}.

The topological pressure of
f
with respect to the flow
\Phi
is given by

P(f,\Phi)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log S(f,\Phi,\epsilon,T).

Note that when
f\equiv 0
, the summation gives the cardinality of the
(\epsilon,T)
-spanning set
E
hence
S(f,\Phi,\epsilon,T)
is just the minimum cardinality of a
(\epsilon,T)
-spanning set. We thus get the topological entropy of the flow
h(\Phi)=P(0,\Phi)
. Moreover, for every
\Phi
-invariant measure
\mu
on
X
, define its metric entropy by

P_{\mu}(f,\Phi)=h_{\mu}(\phi_{1})+\int_{X}fd\mu,

where
h_{\mu}(\phi_{1})
is the metric entropy of the time-1 map
\phi_{1}
. For any potential on
X
we have the following variational principle
[
37
]
,

P(f,\Phi)=\sup_{\mu}P_{\mu}(f,\Phi),

where
\mu
varies over all
\Phi
-invariant measures on
X
. A
\Phi
-invariant measure
\mu
on
X
is called equilibrium measure for
(f,\Phi)
if
\mu
attains the supremum.

3
The factor flow

We give a brief sketch of the construction of a factor flow of the geodesic flow of a closed manifold
M
without conjugate points and visibility universal covering
\tilde{M}
. The construction was carried originally by Gelfert-Ruggiero
[
20
]
and was extended to the higher dimensional setting by Mamani-Ruggiero
[
27
]
. For every
\eta,\theta\in T_{1}M
,
\eta
and
\theta
are equivalent,

\eta\sim\theta\quad\text{ if and only if }\quad\eta\in\mathcal{I}(\theta).

This is an equivalence relation that induces a quotient space
X
and a quotient map
\chi:T_{1}M\to X
. We endow
X
with the quotient topology and hence
\chi
is continuous. For every
\theta\in T_{1}M
, we denote by
[\theta]=\chi(\theta)
the equivalence class of
\theta
. Using the geodesic flow
\phi_{t}
induced by
(M,g)
, we define a quotient flow
\Psi(\psi_{t}:X\to X)
for every
\theta\in T_{1}M
and every
t\in\mathbb{R}
by

\psi_{t}\circ\chi(\theta)=\chi\circ\phi_{t}(\theta).

(2)

We can make an analogous construction on the universal covering
\tilde{M}
:
\eta,\theta\in T_{1}\tilde{M}
are equivalent if
\eta\in\mathcal{\tilde{I}}(\theta)=\mathcal{\tilde{F}}^{s}(\theta)\cap\mathcal{\tilde{F}}^{u}(\theta)\subset T_{1}\tilde{M}
. Similarly we get a quotient space
\tilde{X}
endowed with the quotient topology, a quotient map
\tilde{\chi}:T_{1}\tilde{M}\rightarrow\tilde{X}
and a quotient flow
\tilde{\Psi}(\tilde{\psi}_{t}:\tilde{X}\rightarrow\tilde{X})
. Since
T_{1}\tilde{M}
is the universal covering of
T_{1}M
, the map
\Pi:\tilde{X}\to X
satisfying
\chi\circ d\pi=\Pi\circ\tilde{\chi}
is a well-defined covering map hence
\tilde{X}
and
X
are locally homeomorphic.

(Theorem 1.1 of
[

So, the factor flow

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering. For every

\tilde{\epsilon}>0

\tilde{\epsilon}>0

4
Potentials having unique equilibrium measures

Let
\phi_{t}:X\to X
be a continuous flow acting on a compact metric space. A potential on
X
is any continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
. We say that a potential
f
is Bowen if there exist
C,\epsilon>0
such that for every
s>0
and every
x,x^{\prime}\in X
the following condition holds:

\text{if }\quad d(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(x^{\prime}))<\epsilon\quad\text{ for all }t\in[0,s]\quad\text{ then }\quad\left|\int_{0}^{s}f(\phi_{t}(x))dt-\int_{0}^{s}f(\phi_{t}(x^{\prime}))dt\right|\leq C.

These potentials were introduced by Bowen in his work about uniqueness of equilibrium measures of continuous expansive flows with specification property

For the family of potentials

Let us first recall the case of equilibrium measures for the zero potential, i.e., maximal measures. Clearly, the zero potential belongs to
\mathcal{H}

\Phi(\phi_{t}:T_{1}M\to T_{1}M)
denotes the geodesic flow.

Proposition 5.1

(
[
27
]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering,
\Psi
be the quotient flow and
\chi
be the quotient map. Then

1.

h(\Phi)=h(\Psi)
and

We denote by
\mathcal{M}(T_{1}M)

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering,
(X,\Psi)
be the quotient flow and
\chi
be the quotient map. Given a
\Psi
-invariant measure
\nu
on
X
there exists a
\Phi
-invariant measure
\tilde{\nu}
on
T_{1}M
such that
\chi_{*}\tilde{\nu}=\nu
.

Proof.

First, recall that
\chi_{*}:\mathcal{M}(T_{1}M)\to\mathcal{M}(X)
is an affine continuous surjective map because
\chi
is continuous and surjective. Thus, there exists
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}(T_{1}M)
such that
\chi_{*}\sigma=\nu
but which may not be
\Phi
-invariant. For every
T>0
, define the average measure

\nu_{T}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}(\phi_{t})_{*}\sigma dt.

Clearly,
\nu_{T}
is a Borel probability measure on
T_{1}M
. Using Equation (
2
) and
\Psi
-invariance of
\nu
, for every

we obtain

\chi_{*}\nu_{T}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}(\chi\circ\phi_{t})_{*}\sigma dt=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}(\psi_{t}\circ\chi)_{*}\sigma dt=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}(\psi_{t})_{*}\circ\chi_{*}\sigma dt=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}(\psi_{t})_{*}\nu dt=\nu.

Observe that preimage
(\chi_{*})^{-1}\nu
is a non-empty and compact set in the weak
∗
topology. So, there exists an accumulation point of
(\nu_{T})_{T>0}
denoted by
\tilde{\nu}
. Since
\nu_{T}\in(\chi_{*})^{-1}\nu
for every
T>0
, compactness implies that
\tilde{\nu}\in(\chi_{*})^{-1}\nu
hence
\chi_{*}\tilde{\nu}=\nu
. Finally, we show the
\Phi
-invariance of
\tilde{\nu}
:

\displaystyle|(\phi_{s})_{*}\tilde{\nu}-\tilde{\nu}|

\displaystyle=\lim_{T_{n}}|(\phi_{s})_{*}\nu_{T_{n}}-\nu_{T_{n}}|=\lim_{T_{n}}\left|\frac{1}{T_{n}}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}(\phi_{t+s})_{*}\sigma dt-\frac{1}{T_{n}}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}(\phi_{t})_{*}\sigma dt\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{T_{n}}\left|\frac{1}{T_{n}}\int_{s}^{T_{n}+s}(\phi_{\tau})_{*}\sigma d\tau-\frac{1}{T_{n}}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}(\phi_{t})_{*}\sigma dt\right|=\lim_{T_{n}}\left|\frac{1}{T_{n}}\int_{[0,s]\cup[T_{n},T_{n}+s]}(\phi_{t})_{*}\sigma dt\right|.

So, for every Borel set

we have

|(\phi_{s})_{*}\tilde{\nu}(A)-\tilde{\nu}(A)|=\lim_{T_{n}}\left|\frac{1}{T_{n}}\int_{[0,s]\cup[T_{n},T_{n}+s]}\sigma(\phi_{t}^{-1}(A))dt\right|\leq\lim_{T_{n}\to\infty}\frac{2s}{T_{n}}=0.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering,
(X,\Psi)
be the quotient flow,
\chi
be the quotient map,
f\in\mathcal{H}
and
\tilde{f}
be the induced potential. If
\mu
is an equilibrium measure for

then so is
\chi_{*}\mu
for
\tilde{f}
.

Proof.

Since
\chi
is a semi-conjugacy it follows that
\chi_{*}\mu
is a
\Psi
-invariant measure on

. Using Proposition
5.1
(1) and Equation (
5

\mu
and
\chi_{*}\mu
agree:

P_{\mu}(f,\Phi)=h_{\mu}(\phi_{1})+\int_{T_{1}M}fd\mu=h_{\chi_{*}\mu}(\psi_{1})+\int_{X}\tilde{f}d(\chi_{*}\mu)=P_{\chi_{*}\mu}(\tilde{f},\Psi)

(6)

Moreover, topological pressures of
f
and
\tilde{f}
also agree. Equation (

P(f,\Phi)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Phi)}P_{\mu}(f,\Phi)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Phi)}P_{\chi_{*}\mu}(\tilde{f},\Psi)\leq\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}(\Psi)}P_{\nu}(\tilde{f},\Psi)=P(\tilde{f},\Psi).

(7)

6
Uniqueness of equilibrium measures

In this section, under some particular conditions we prove uniqueness of equilibrium measures for the family of potentials
\mathcal{H}
considered in Section
4
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points,
\Phi(\phi_{t}:T_{1}M\to T_{1}M)
be its geodesic flow and
f:T_{1}M\to\mathbb{R}
be a potential. Recall that
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is the set of expansive points. We say that
f
has the pressure-gap if

\sup_{\mu}\{P_{\mu}(f,\Phi):\mu\text{ is supported on }T_{1}M\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}\}<P(f,\Phi),

where
P_{\mu}(f,\Phi)
and
P(f,\Phi)
are the metric and topological pressure respectively. For short we denote by
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{c}
the set
T_{1}M\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}
, i.e., the non-expansive set.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering. If
f\in\mathcal{H}
has the pressure-gap then
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{c}
cannot support an equilibrium measure of
f
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists an equilibrium measure
\mu
of
f\in\mathcal{H}
supported on
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{c}
. Thus,
P(f,\Phi)=P_{\mu}(f,\Phi)\leq\sup_{\mu}\{P_{\mu}(f,\Phi):Supp(\mu)\subset\mathcal{R}^{c}_{0}\}
and hence
f
has no pressure-gap.
∎

Therefore, the statement of the conclusion of this lemma is weaker than the pressure-gap of
f
. Thus, we say that
f
has the weak pressure-gap if
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{c}
cannot support an equilibrium measure of
f
. We will work with this weaker hypothesis instead of the classical one.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering. If
f\in\mathcal{H}
has the weak pressure gap then
f
has a unique equilibrium measure
\mu
. In particular,
\mu
is ergodic.

Proof.

By hypothesis the geodesic flow is
C^{\infty}
and hence
f
always has an equilibrium measure. For the uniqueness, let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Phi)
be any equilibrium measure for
f
and
\mu_{e}
be any ergodic component of
\mu
. It follows that
\mu_{e}
is an equilibrium measure for
f
as well. As
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is
\Phi
-invariant, ergodicity provides that
\mu_{e}
is supported either on
\mathcal{R}_{0}
or on
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{c}
. The weak pressure-gap of
f
implies that
\mu_{e}
is supported on
\mathcal{R}_{0}
. Since all ergodic components of
\mu
are supported on
\mathcal{R}_{0}
so does
\mu
. From Section
4
, we consider the induced potential
\tilde{f}:X\to\mathbb{R}
of
f
. By Corollary
4.1
,
\tilde{f}
has a unique equilibrium measure
\nu
with respect to the quotient flow
\Psi
. This together with Proposition
5.3
implies that
\mu
projects on
\nu

\chi_{*}\mu=\nu
. Since
\mu
is supported on
\mathcal{R}_{0}
and
\chi
is a bijection on
\mathcal{R}_{0}
, it follows that
\mu
is completely determined by
\nu

has the same form and the uniqueness follows.
∎

7
K-mixing Property

Let us recall the definition of
K
-mixing property in the discrete setting. Let
(X,\mathcal{B})
be a measurable space,
\mu
be a measure on
(X,\mathcal{B})
and
g:X\to X
be an invertible map preserving
\mu
. The system
(X,\mathcal{B},g,\mu)
is said to be
K
-mixing if for any sets
A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{r}\in\mathcal{B}
for
r\geq 0
it holds

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{B\in\mathcal{C}^{r}_{n}}\left|\mu(A_{0}\cap B)-\mu(A_{0})\mu(B)\right|=0,

where
\mathcal{C}^{r}_{n}
is the minimal
\sigma
-algebra generated by
g^{k}(A_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq r
and
k\geq n
. We see that
K
-mixing property implies mixing of all orders. Thus,
K
-mixing is interpreted as a strong mixing property of
\mu
. A measure-preserving flow
(X,\mathcal{B},\Psi,\mu)
is
K
-mixing if for every
t\neq 0
the discrete system
(X,\mathcal{B},\psi_{t},\mu)
is
K
-mixing. In fact, by Gurevic’s work
[
23
]
we only need to verify that a single time-
t
map is
K
-mixing.

We will state a criterion to get the
K
-mixing property in the context of flows due to Call and Thompson
[
7
]
. This criterion is based on a previous work of Ledrappier in the discrete setting
[
25
]
. Let
\Psi(\psi_{t}:X\to X)
be a continuous flow on a compact metric space and
f
be a continuous potential on
X
. We consider the self-product flow
\Psi\times\Psi:X\times X\to X\times X
and the sum potential
F:X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
F(x,x^{\prime})=f(x)+f(x^{\prime})
. We define the set of non-expansive points of
X
at scale
\epsilon>0
by

NE(\epsilon)=\{x\in X:\Gamma_{\epsilon}(x)\not\in\psi_{[-s,s]}(x),\text{ for every }s>0\},

where
\Gamma_{\epsilon}(x)=\{y\in X:d(\psi_{t}(x),\psi_{t}(y))<\epsilon,\text{ for every }t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. A
\Psi
-invariant measure
\mu
is called almost expansive if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\mu(NE(\epsilon))=0
. Moreover, the set of product non-expansive points of
X\times X
at scale
\epsilon>0
is given by

NE^{\times}(\epsilon)=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:\Gamma_{\epsilon}(x,y)\not\in\psi_{[-s,s]}(x)\times\psi_{[-s,s]}(y),\text{ for every }s>0\}.

A
\Psi\times\Psi
-invariant measure
\nu
is product expansive if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\nu(NE^{\times}(\epsilon))=0
.

Proposition 7.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
(X,\Psi)
be a continuous entropy expansive flow on a compact metric space and
f:X\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous potential. Assume that any equilibrium measure of
f
is almost expansive and any equilibrium measure of
F
is product expansive. Suppose that

8
Weighted equidistribution of periodic orbits

Let
\Psi=(\psi_{t})
be a continuous flow acting on a compact metric space
X
. For every
\Psi
-periodic orbit
\gamma
in
X
we denote its minimal period by
|\gamma|
. For every
T>0
and every
\epsilon>0
small enough we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}
the set of
\Psi
-periodic orbits
\gamma
with
|\gamma|\in[T-\epsilon,T+\epsilon]
. Given a continuous potential
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and a
\Psi
-periodic orbit
\gamma
in
X
we denote the integral of
f
around
\gamma
by

f(\gamma)=\frac{1}{|\gamma|}\int_{0}^{|\gamma|}f(\psi_{t}(x_{\gamma}))dt\quad\text{ for some }\quad x_{\gamma}\in\gamma.

For a fixed small enough
\epsilon>0
let us consider

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\Lambda(f,\Psi,T,\epsilon)\quad\text{ with }\quad\Lambda(f,\Psi,T,\epsilon)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}}e^{f(\gamma)}.

(9)

If this
\limsup
does not depend upon
\epsilon
then we denote it by
\overline{P}^{*}(f,\Psi)
and call it the upper
f
-pressure of periodic orbits. Analogously we define the lower
f
-pressure of periodic orbits
\underline{P}^{*}(f,\Psi)
using
\liminf
instead of
\limsup
. Whenever these two pressures exist and agree, we denote the common value by
P^{*}(f,\Psi)
and call it the
f
-pressure of periodic orbits.

We can use
f
and periodic orbits to construct
\Psi
-invariant measures by the averaging. For every
T>0
and
\epsilon>0
small enough we define

\mu_{T,\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\Lambda(f,\Psi,T,\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}}e^{f(\gamma)}\mu_{\gamma},

where
\mu_{\gamma}
is the unique
\Psi
-invariant measure on
X
supported on
\gamma
. This measure is a well-defined
\Psi
-invariant probability measure on
X
for example when
\Psi
is expansive because
|\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}|
and
\Lambda(f,\Psi,T,\epsilon)
are finite. Given a
\Psi
-invariant measure
\mu
on
X
, periodic orbits weighted by
f
equidistribute to
\mu
if for every
\epsilon>0
small enough,
\mu_{T,\epsilon}\to\mu
in the weak
∗
topology as
T\to\infty
.

For geodesic flows of manifolds of negative curvature, Bowen showed that periodic orbits (for the zero potential) equidistribute to the unique measure of maximal entropy
[
5
]
. This was extended to hyperbolic flows by Parry, showing that periodic orbits weighted by a Holder continuous potential
f
, equidistribute to the unique equilibrium measure of
f

[
29
]
. These cases deal with expansive flows and hence there exist finitely many periodic orbits with period in
[T-\epsilon,T+\epsilon]
for
\epsilon>0
small enough. For manifolds of non-positive curvature, there may exist closed flat strips of zero curvature, i.e., infinite periodic orbits with same period.

For closed manifolds without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering, recall that strips
S(\eta)
are built of equivalence classes
\mathcal{I}(\eta)
(defined in Section
3
),

S(\eta)=\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\phi_{t}(\mathcal{I(\eta)})\quad\text{ for some }\quad\eta\in T_{1}M.

The strip is periodic if there is some
T>0
such that
\phi_{T}(\mathcal{I}(\eta))=\mathcal{I}(\eta)
. If
\mathcal{\mathcal{I}(\eta)}=\{\eta\}
then
S(\eta)
is reduced to the single orbit
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\eta)
and a periodic strip is reduced to a single periodic orbit. In our setting, a periodic strip is not necessarily made up solely of periodic orbits. However, each periodic strip has at least one periodic orbit, and all of its periodic orbits have the same period. Thus, for each periodic strip
S(\eta)
, we choose any one of its periodic orbits and call it a prime periodic orbit of
S(\eta)
. For every
T>0
and
\epsilon>0
small enough we denote by
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon}
the set of
\Phi
-periodic prime orbits
\gamma
with
|\gamma|\in[T-\epsilon,T+\epsilon]
.

For potentials
f\in\mathcal{H}
which are constant on classes
\mathcal{I}(\eta)
, using
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon}
we define an analogue
f
-pressure to above. For every
T>0
and
\epsilon>0
small enough, the
f
-pressure of periodic prime orbits is defined by

P^{\prime}(f,\Phi)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\Lambda^{\prime}(f,\Phi,T,\epsilon)\quad\text{ with }\quad\Lambda^{\prime}(f,\Phi,T,\epsilon)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon}}e^{f(\gamma)}<\infty,

where the limit exists and does not depend upon
\epsilon
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering. If
f\in\mathcal{H}
then
P^{\prime}(f,\Phi)=P(f,\Phi)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{f}
be the induced potential on
X
and
\tilde{\epsilon}>0
the its Bowen constant given by Proposition
4.1
. For every
T>0
and every
\epsilon<\tilde{\epsilon}
small enough let
\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon}
be respectively the sets of
\Psi
-periodic orbits and
\Phi
-periodic prime orbits with period in
[T-\epsilon,T+\epsilon]
as explained above. Since the semi-conjugacy
\chi
between
\Phi
and
\Psi
is time-preserving it follows that
\chi(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon})\subset\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}
. The reverse inclusion follows from the surjectivity of
\chi
and the above definition of
\Phi
-periodic prime orbits. We thus obtain
\chi(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon})=\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}
and
|\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon}|=|\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}|<\infty
by expansivity of
\Psi
given in Proposition
3.2
. On the other hand, from the semi-conjugacy Equation (
2
) and Proposition
4.1
, for every
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon}
we have

f(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{|\gamma|}f(\phi_{t}(\eta_{\gamma}))dt=\int_{0}^{|\chi(\gamma)|}\tilde{f}(\psi_{t}(x_{\chi(\gamma)}))dt=\tilde{f}(\chi(\gamma)),

(10)

for some
\eta_{\gamma}\in\gamma
and
x_{\chi(\gamma)}\in\chi(\gamma)
where
|\chi(\gamma)|=|\gamma|
and
\chi(\gamma)\in\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}
. Using all together gives

\Lambda^{\prime}(f,\Phi,T,\epsilon)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon}}e^{f(\gamma)}=\sum_{\chi(\gamma)\in\chi(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon})}e^{\tilde{f}(\chi(\gamma))}=\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{O}_{T,\epsilon}}e^{\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma})}=\Lambda(\tilde{f},\Psi,T,\epsilon)<\infty.

(11)

From this and Proposition 7.3.13 of
[
18
]
, for every
\epsilon>0
small enough it holds

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\Lambda^{\prime}(f,\Phi,T,\epsilon)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\Lambda(\tilde{f},\Psi,T,\epsilon)=P(\tilde{f},\Psi),

where
P(\tilde{f},\Psi)
is a topological pressure. Since these limits exist and does not depend upon
\epsilon>0
small enough we deduce that
P^{\prime}(f,\Phi)
and
P^{*}(\tilde{f},\Psi)
exist as well. From this equation and Equation (
8
) we conclude that

P^{\prime}(f,\Phi)=P^{*}(\tilde{f},\Psi)=P(\tilde{f},\Psi)=P(f,\Phi).

∎

Once we know the relation between the asymptotic exponential decrease rates we can show the weighted equidistribution of periodic orbits.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering,

be a potential having the weak pressure gap and
\mu
be the unique equilibrium measure of

. Then,
\Phi
-periodic prime orbits weighted by

Proof.

As above, let
\tilde{f}
be the induced potential on

and
\tilde{\epsilon}>0
be its Bowen constant given by Proposition
4.1
. For every
T>0
and every
\epsilon<\tilde{\epsilon}
small enough define the measure

\mu_{T,\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\prime}(f,\Phi,T,\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T,\epsilon}}e^{f(\gamma)}\mu_{\gamma},

where
\Lambda^{\prime}(f,\Phi,T,\epsilon)<\infty
by Equation (
11
) and
\mu_{\gamma}
is the unique
\Phi
-invariant measure supported on
\gamma
. For every

\mu_{T,\epsilon}

\Phi
-invariant probability measure on

.

We will show that
\mu_{T,\epsilon}
converges to
\mu
. Let
\tau
be an accumulation point of
(\mu_{T,\epsilon})
in the weak
∗
topology. Thus, there exists a sequence

\chi_{*}
is weak
∗

\chi_{*}\mu_{T_{n},\epsilon}\to\chi_{*}\tau

10
) and (

\chi_{*}\mu_{T_{n},\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\prime}(f,\Phi,T,\epsilon)}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{T_{n},\epsilon}}e^{f(\gamma)}\chi_{*}\mu_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{\Lambda(\tilde{f},\Psi,T,\epsilon)}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{O}_{T_{n},\epsilon}}e^{\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma})}\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}},

(12)

where
\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}}=\chi_{*}\mu_{\gamma}
is the unique
\Psi
-invariant measure supported on

.

By Proposition 7.3.15 of
[
18
]
, we know that
\Psi
-periodic orbits weighted by

of

\chi_{*}\mu_{T_{n},\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\Lambda(\tilde{f},\Psi,T_{n},\epsilon)}\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{O}_{T_{n},\epsilon}}e^{\tilde{f}(\tilde{\gamma})}\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}}\longrightarrow\nu\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty

∎

9
Gibbs property

Let
\Psi=(\psi_{t})
be a continuous flow acting on a compact metric space
X
,
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous potential and
\nu
be a
\Psi
-invariant probability measure on
X
. We say that
\nu
has the Gibbs property for
f
with constant
P\in\mathbb{R}
if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
C_{\epsilon}>1
such that for all
x\in X
and all
t>0
it holds

C_{\epsilon}^{-1}\leq\frac{\nu(B(x,\epsilon,t))}{e^{S_{t}f(x)-tP}}\leq C_{\epsilon},

(13)

where
B(x,\epsilon,t)
is the
(\epsilon,t)
-dynamical ball with respect to
\Psi
and
S_{t}f(x)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\psi_{s}(x))ds
. If
\nu
is an equilibrium measure of
f
,
\nu
has the Gibbs property for
f
with the topological pressure
P(f,\Psi)
as constant.

Gibbs property was proved for equilibrium measures of Holder continuous potentials in the setting of hyperbolic transitive maps by Bowen
[
2
]
. The extension of this result to continuous expansive flows having the specification property is a consequence of Franco’s work
[
19
]
. We used this result in Lemma
6.4
to prove full support of equilibrium measures. In our setting, we show that equilibrium measures of potentials in Theorem
6.1
, satisfy the Gibbs property except at non-expansive points.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering,
\chi:T_{1}M\to X
be the semi-conjugacy between the geodesic and quotient flow and
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Given
\theta\in T_{1}M
,
\epsilon>0
and
t>0
, there exists
\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}>0
such that

\chi(B(\theta,\epsilon,t))\subset B(\chi(\theta),\tilde{\epsilon}_{1},t).

If furthermore
\theta\in\mathcal{R}_{0}
then there exist an open saturated set
D(\theta,\epsilon,t)
and
\tilde{\epsilon}_{2}>0
such that

B(\chi(\theta),\tilde{\epsilon}_{2},t)\subset\chi(D(\theta,\epsilon,t))\subset\chi(B(\theta,\epsilon,t)),

(14)

where
B(\theta,\epsilon,t)
and
B(\chi(\theta),\tilde{\epsilon},t)
are the dynamical balls in
T_{1}M
and
X
respectively.

Proof.

Recall the notation of Section
3
: for every
\theta\in T_{1}M
,
[\theta]=\chi(\theta)
. For the first inclusion, let
\eta\in B(\theta,\epsilon,t)
hence
d_{s}(\phi_{s}(\theta),\phi_{s}(\eta))<\epsilon
for all

\chi
there exists
\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}>0
such that for every

d(\psi_{s}[\theta],\psi_{s}[\eta])<\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}\quad\text{ for every }\quad s\in[0,t],

For the second inclusion, since

is an open set in

6.2
there exists an open saturated set
D(\theta,\epsilon,t)\subset B(\theta,\epsilon,t)
and hence

\chi(D(\theta,\epsilon,t))\subset\chi(B(\theta,\epsilon,t)).

(15)

Since

is open and saturated it follows that
\chi(D(\theta,\epsilon,t))
is an open set in

. Note that for all

\tilde{\epsilon}_{2}>0
such that

[\theta]
. Finally, using Equation (

∎

10
Large deviations principle and entropy density

Let
\Psi=(\psi_{t})
be a continuous flow acting on a compact metric space
X
. We say that
\Psi
has entropy density of ergodic measures if for any
\Psi
-invariant measure
\mu
on
X
there exists a sequence of ergodic measures
(\mu_{n})
on
X
such that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in the weak
∗
topology and
h_{\mu_{n}}(\psi_{1})\to h_{\mu}(\psi_{1})
. There is an analogue definition for the discrete case
[
38
]
.

In the discrete case, Eizenberg-Kifer-Weiss proved entropy density of ergodic measures for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-shifts having the specification property
[
16
]
. Pfister and Sullivan extended this property to maps having an almost global product
[
33
]
. In the continuous setting, Constantine-Lafont-Thompson showed entropy density for continuous expansive flows having a weak specification property
[
10
]
. In particular, this holds for all transitive hyperbolic flows including geodesic flows of compact manifolds of negative curvature. Though, for rank-1 surfaces, Coudene and Schapira showed that ergodic measures cannot approximate certain invariant measures supported on flat strips
[
11
]
. Hence, entropy density of ergodic measures does not hold in our setting. Despite this, it is still possible to approximate invariant measures not supported on strips, thus obtaining a restricted entropy density of ergodic measures.

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering, and
\Phi=(\phi_{t})
be its geodesic flow. Given a
\Phi
-invariant measure
\mu
on
T_{1}M
with
\mu(T_{1}M\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0})=0
, there exists a sequence of ergodic measures
(\mu_{n})
on
T_{1}M
such that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in the weak
∗
topology and
h_{\mu_{n}}(\phi_{1})\to h_{\mu}(\phi_{1})
.

Proof.

Since
\chi
is a semi-conjugacy it follows that
\chi_{*}\mu
is a
\Psi
-invariant measure on
X
. By Proposition
3.2
,
\Psi
is expansive and has the specification property. Proposition 5.5 of
[
10
]
asserts that
\Psi
has entropy density of ergodic measures. Thus, there exists a sequence of ergodic measures
(\nu_{n})
on
X
such that
\nu_{n}\to\chi_{*}\mu
weakly and
h_{\nu_{n}}(\psi_{1})\to h_{\chi_{*}\mu}(\psi_{1})
. By Proposition
5.2
, for all
n\geq 1
there exists a
\Phi
-invariant measure
\mu_{n}
on
T_{1}M
such that
\chi_{*}\mu_{n}=\nu_{n}
and hence

\mu_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{0})=\mu_{n}(\chi^{-1}\circ\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\chi_{*}\mu_{n}(\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\nu_{n}(\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))\in\{0,1\},

(18)

since
\nu_{n}
is ergodic and
\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0})
is
\Psi
-invariant. We claim that

\mu_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{0})=1\quad\text{ for every }\quad n\geq 1.

(19)

Otherwise
\mu_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{0})=0
for all
n\geq 1
. This condition and Equation (
18
) lead to a contradiction:

1=\mu(\mathcal{R}_{0})=\mu(\chi^{-1}\circ\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\chi_{*}\mu(\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\nu_{n}(\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(\mathcal{R}_{0})=0.

We will show that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
weakly. Let
\tau
be an accumulation point of
(\mu_{n})
in the weak
∗
topology. Thus there exists a subsequence
(n_{k})
such that
\mu_{n_{k}}\to\tau
weakly. Since
\chi_{*}
is weak
∗
continuous it follows that
\nu_{n_{k}}=\chi_{*}\mu_{n_{k}}\to\chi_{*}\tau
and hence
\chi_{*}\tau=\chi_{*}\mu
. Moreover,
\tau(\mathcal{R}_{0})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{n_{k}}(\mathcal{R}_{0})=1
by Equation (
19
). Since
\chi
is a bijection when restricted to
\mathcal{R}_{0}
and
\mu(\mathcal{R}_{0})=\tau(\mathcal{R}_{0})=1
, from
\chi_{*}\tau=\chi_{*}\mu
we deduce that
\tau=\mu
. As
\tau
was an arbitrary accumulation point of
(\mu_{n})
we conclude that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
weakly.

We now show the ergodicity of
\mu_{n}
. Let
A\subset T_{1}M
be any
\Phi
-invariant set. Observe that
A=(A\cap\mathcal{R}_{0})\cup(A\cap(T_{1}M\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0}))
. By Equation (
19
) we know that
\mu_{n}(T_{1}M\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0})=0
for all
n\geq 1
and hence

\mu_{n}(A)=\mu_{n}(A\cap\mathcal{R}_{0})=\mu_{n}(\chi^{-1}\circ\chi(A\cap\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\chi_{*}\mu_{n}(\chi(A\cap\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\nu_{n}(\chi(A\cap\mathcal{R}_{0}))\in\{0,1\},

by ergodicity of
\nu_{n}
and
\Psi
-invariance of
\chi(A\cap\mathcal{R}_{0})
. Therefore,
\mu_{n}
is ergodic for all
n\geq 1
. Finally, using entropy density of
\Psi
and Proposition
5.1
we obtain

h_{\mu_{n}}(\phi_{1})=h_{\chi_{*}\mu_{n}}(\psi_{1})=h_{\nu_{n}}(\psi_{1})\longrightarrow h_{\chi_{*}\mu}(\psi_{1})=h_{\mu}(\phi_{1}).

∎

Entropy density of ergodic measures was used by Eizenberg-Kifer-Weiss to prove level-2 large deviations of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
maps
[
16
]
. We can also use this property to show a related result.

Consider a continuous flow
\Psi=(\psi_{t})
acting on a compact metric space
X
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\Psi}(X)
the set of
\Psi
-invariant probability measures on
X
. Let
\nu
be an equilibrium measure of the potential
g
and
h:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous potential. We say that
\nu
satisfies the level-1 large deviations principle for
h
if for every
\epsilon>0
it holds

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu\left\{x\in X:\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}h(\psi_{s}(x))ds-\nu(h)\right|\geq\epsilon\right\}=-q(\epsilon)\quad\text{with}

(20)

q(\epsilon)=\inf_{\tau\in D_{\epsilon}}P(\Psi,g)-P_{\tau}(\Psi,g)\quad\text{ where }\quad D_{\epsilon}=\{\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{\Psi}(X):|\nu(g)-\tau(g)|\geq\epsilon\}.

We say that
\nu
satisfies level-2 large deviations principle if
\nu
has level-1 large deviations principle for every continuous potential
h
. Level-2 large deviations principle is quite strong and holds for hyperbolic transitive flows
[
24
]
. In our setting, we show that certain measures having weak pressure gap (Section
6
) satisfy level-1 large deviations principle for potentials in
\mathcal{H}
(Definition
4.1
).

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold without conjugate points and with visibility universal covering,
f\in\mathcal{H}
be a potential having the weak pressure gap and
\mu
be the unique equilibrium measure of
f
. Then
\mu
satisfies the level-1 large deviations principle for every
g\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{H}
and
\tilde{g}:X\to\mathbb{R}
be its induced potential given in Proposition
4.1
. From Proposition
5.3
we know that
\chi_{*}\mu=\nu
is the unique equilibrium measure of the induced potential
\tilde{f}:X\to\mathbb{R}
. By Proposition
3.2
,
\Psi
is expansive and has the specification property, thus
\Psi
has the entropy density of ergodic measures by Proposition 5.5 of
[
10
]
. Applying any of the results of
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
or
[
33
]
, shows that
\nu
satisfies the level-1 large deviations for every continuous potential
h:X\to\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
\nu
and
\tilde{f}
satisfy Equation (
20
) for
\tilde{g}
. We will verify this equation for
\mu
,
f
and
g
. For every
t>0
and
\epsilon>0
define the sets

\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon}=\left\{x\in X:\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{g}(\psi_{s}(x))ds-\nu(\tilde{g})\right|\geq\epsilon\right\},A_{t,\epsilon}=\left\{\eta\in T_{1}M:\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(\phi_{s}(\eta))ds-\mu(g)\right|\geq\epsilon\right\}.

Using the semi-conjugacy Equation (
2
) and Proposition
4.1
, for every
\eta\in T_{1}M
we get

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{g}(\psi_{s}(\chi(\eta)))ds=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}g(\phi_{s}(\eta))ds.

Moreover, since
\chi_{*}\mu=\nu
, Equation (
5
) implies that

\nu(\tilde{g})=\int_{X}\tilde{g}d\nu=\int_{T_{1}M}gd\mu=\mu(g).

(21)

From these equations and surjectivity of
\chi
it follows that
\chi(A_{t,\epsilon})=\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon}
. Now, observe that

A_{t,\epsilon}=(A_{t,\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{R}_{0})\cup(A_{t,\epsilon}\cap(T_{1}M\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0})),\quad\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon}=(\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon}\cap\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))\cup(\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon}\cap(X\setminus\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))).

Since
\mu(T_{1}M\setminus\mathcal{R}_{0})=\nu(X\setminus\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))=0
it follows that
\mu(A_{t,\epsilon})=\mu(A_{t,\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{R}_{0})
and
\nu(\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon})=\nu(\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon}\cap\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))
. Using these equations we deduce that

\mu(A_{t,\epsilon})=\mu(A_{t,\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{R}_{0})=\mu(\chi^{-1}\circ\chi(A_{t,\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\chi_{*}\mu(\chi(A_{t,\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{R}_{0})))=\nu(\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon}\cap\chi(\mathcal{R}_{0}))=\nu(\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon}).

From this equation and Equation (
20
), we obtain the desired relation

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\mu(A_{t,\epsilon})=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\nu(\tilde{A}_{t,\epsilon})=-q(\epsilon).

We now verify that
q(\epsilon)
has the desired form. Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi}(T_{1}M)
the set of
\Phi
-invariant probability measures on
T_{1}M
. For every
\epsilon>0
consider the sets

D_{\epsilon}=\{\tau\in\mathcal{M}_{\Phi}(T_{1}M):|\tau(g)-\mu(g)|\geq\epsilon\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{D}_{\epsilon}=\{\omega\in\mathcal{M}_{\Psi}(X):|\omega(\tilde{g})-\nu(\tilde{g})|\geq\epsilon\}.

Using Equation (
21
) we see that

\mu(g)-\tau(g)=\int_{T_{1}M}gd\mu-\int_{T_{1}M}gd\tau=\int_{X}\tilde{g}d\chi_{*}\mu-\int_{X}\tilde{g}d\chi_{*}\tau=\nu(\tilde{g})-\chi_{*}\tau(\tilde{g}).

From this and surjectivity of
\chi_{*}
it follows that
\chi_{*}(D_{\epsilon})=\tilde{D}_{\epsilon}
. Using this and Equations (

) and (

q(\epsilon)=\inf_{\omega\in\tilde{D}_{\epsilon}}P(\Psi,\tilde{g})-P_{\omega}(\Psi,\tilde{g})=\inf_{\chi_{*}\tau\in\chi_{*}(D_{\epsilon})}P(\Psi,\tilde{g})-P_{\chi_{*}\tau}(\Psi,\tilde{g})=\inf_{\tau\in D_{\epsilon}}P(\Phi,g)-P_{\tau}(\Phi,g).

∎

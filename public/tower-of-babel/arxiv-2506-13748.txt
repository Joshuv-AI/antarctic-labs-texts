A quasi-tree expansion for the surface Tutte polynomial
Maya Thompson

Abstract
The surface Tutte polynomial has recently been generalised to pseudo-surfaces equipping it with recursive deletion-contraction relations [ 15 ] . We use these relations to show that this generalisation naturally possesses a quasi-tree expansion. This extends quasi-tree expansions of the Bollobás–Riordan, Las Vergnas and Krushkal polynomials [ 3 , 4 , 18 ] , which we recover from our main result.



1
Introduction

The Tutte polynomial is one of the most influential graph invariants due to its far-reaching applications across many areas of mathematics and the depth of its underlying theory. It specialises to numerous well-known invariants, such as the Jones polynomial of an alternating knot, the partition function of the Potts model in statistical mechanics, the weight enumerator of linear codes, and the chromatic polynomial. A detailed and extensive background on the Tutte polynomial is given in
[
7
]
.

The Tutte polynomial can famously be defined in three equivalent ways, as a state sum over all subgraphs, recursively through deletion and contraction, and as a sum over all spanning trees.
Tutte first introduced his polynomial in
[
17
]
via the spanning tree expansion, using the notion of activity, which we now describe.
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and fix a total ordering
\prec
on the edges. Given a maximal spanning forest
F
of
G
, an edge
e\in E(G)-E(F)
is
externally active
if it is the smallest edge in the unique cycle of
F\cup e
. An edge
e\in E(F)
is
internally active
if it is the smallest edge in the
cut defined by
e
. That is, in the set
U_{F}(e)=\{f\in E(G):(F\ba e)\cup f\>\text{ is a maximal spanning forest}\}
. Using activity, the Tutte polynomial can be defined by its
spanning tree expansion
as

T(G;x,y)=\sum_{i,j}t_{ij}x^{i}y^{j},

(1)

where
t_{ij}
is the number of maximal spanning forests of
G
with internal activity
i
and external activity
j
.

We are interested in analogues of the Tutte polynomial for graphs cellularly embedded in surfaces, or equivalently ribbon graphs. There has been significant interest in such
topological Tutte polynomials
fueled by Bollobás and Riordan’s papers
[
1
,
2
]
, with several variations arising
[
1
,
2
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
over the years, many of which admit expansions akin to the spanning tree expansion (
1
) of the Tutte polynomial. Bollobás and Riordan used Tutte’s notion of activities to provide a spanning tree expansion for their generalisation of the Tutte polynomial
[
1
]
. However, for ribbon graphs, the more natural analogue of a spanning tree is a quasi-tree, i.e., a spanning ribbon subgraph with one boundary component. This perspective is demonstrated by Champanerkar, Kofman, and Stolzfus who extend the concept of activity to quasi-trees and give a quasi-tree expansion for the Bollobás–Riordan polynomial for orientable ribbon graphs
[
4
]
. This expansion was generalised to non-orientable ribbon graphs by Vignes–Tourneret
[
18
]
. Butler
[
3
]
then provided a more general quasi-tree expansion for the Krushkal polynomial, from which a quasi-tree expansion can be derived for the Las Vergnas polynomial and rederived for the Bollobás–Riordan polynomial.

Notably, a key feature of these quasi-tree expansions is that they implicitly rely on structural properties of
graphs embedded in pseudo-surfaces
, a setting formalised more recently. (Informally, a pseudo-surface is obtained from a surface by taking the topological quotient of finitely many paths in the surface.) This setting proves to be the correct one for both deletion-contraction and activity-based expansions.

One topological Tutte polynomial of particular interest is the
surface Tutte polynomial
, introduced by Goodall et al
[
8
,
9
]
, which emulated Tutte’s construction of the Tutte polynomial as the dichromate.
As a result of their approach, the surface Tutte polynomial has been shown to have an array of applications, including enumerating the number of colourings, flows and acyclic orientations of ribbon graphs. However, unlike the classical Tutte polynomial, it can only be defined as a state sum and does not admit equivalent definitions via deletion-contraction or activity. These limitations arise as the polynomial is confined to graphs cellularly embedded in surfaces.

To address the lack of deletion-contraction relations, the
packaged surface Tutte polynomial
was introduced
[
15
]
. This polynomial generalises the surface Tutte polynomial to graphs embedded in pseudo-surfaces, which we realise as
packaged ribbon graphs
(defined in Section
2.3
). It unifies various approaches from the literature, inheriting the key properties of its predecessors and specialising to each of them. Notably, it can be defined both as a state sum and recursively through deletion-contraction relations that hold for all edge types.

In this paper, we provide a quasi-tree expansion for the packaged surface Tutte polynomial, thereby completing the trio of formulations that mirror those of the Tutte polynomial. Working in the pseudo-surface setting is essential, as it underpins both the deletion-contraction relations and constructions needed for a quasi-tree expansion. Our approach uses the deletion-contraction relations of the packaged surface Tutte polynomial
[
15
]
with previously established notions of activity for quasi-trees
[
4
,
18
]
. Our result generalises the quasi-tree expansions found in the literature, and we recover them accordingly.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we provide the necessary background on ribbon graphs, quasi-trees and a notion of activity for them, and packaged ribbon graphs, which describe graphs embedded in pseudo-surfaces. Section 3 features our main result, Theorem
3.3
, a quasi-tree expansion for the packaged surface Tutte polynomial, from which we recover the expansion for the Krushkal polynomial
[
3
]
, and hence those for the Bollobás–Riordan and Las Vergnas polynomials.

Definition 2.1

A

1.

2.

Each such arc lies on the boundary of just one vertex and one edge.

3.

Every edge contains two such arcs.

2.3
Packaged ribbon graphs

Packaged ribbon graphs were introduced in
[
15
]
building upon the coloured ribbon graphs of Huggett and Moffatt
[
11
]

They consist of a ribbon graph equipped with a partition on the set of vertices and the set of boundary components, where each block in a partition is weighted. Ribbon graphs are known for their ability to describe graphs cellularly embedded in surfaces (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
10
]
). Analogously, packaged ribbon graphs describe graphs embedded in pseudo-surfaces equipped with vertex and face weights (details given in
[
15
, Section 2.3]

.

A

Remark
.

When constructing quasi-tree expansions for ribbon graph polynomials, the appropriate setting is graphs embedded in pseudo-surfaces or packaged ribbon graphs. This is because these expansions use deletion and contraction, and deleting and contracting edges results in a loss of information about the topology of the embedded graph when working with surfaces, instead of pseudo-surfaces. (Further discussion on this can be found in
[
11
, Section 2]
.) The quasi-tree expansions for the Bollobás–Riordan
[
4
,
18
]
and Kruskal polynomials
[
3
]
inadvertently use the pseudo-surface setting. This can be seen with the construction
G_{Q}
which is the packaging (without any vertex weights)
G(\mathbb{G}\con D_{Q}\cup N_{Q}\ba D_{Q}^{*}\cup N_{Q}^{*}\cup O^{*}_{Q};\mathcal{V})
.

So whilst it may be possible to directly obtain a quasi-tree expansion for the surface Tutte polynomial, the packaged ribbon graph setting is unavoidable. Further, as the deletion-contraction relation of Theorem
3.2
is pivotal to the proof of Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.2
does not hold for the surface Tutte polynomial, finding a quasi-tree expansion for the surface Tutte polynomial directly would be needlessly complicated. Finally, the surface Tutte polynomial
[
9
]
for non-orientable ribbon graphs uses a signed genus that distinguishes orientablility, which is not compatible with recursive relations. So any quasi-tree expansion for it would be restricted to orientable ribbon graphs. For these reasons we choose to work with the packaged surface Tutte polynomial for this paper.

However, using Equation (
2
) and Theorem
3.3
, one can obtain a quasi-tree expansion for the surface Tutte polynomial that holds for orientable ribbon graphs.

),

P(\mathbb{G};\alpha,\beta,a,b)=(\alpha\beta)^{-k(\mathbb{G})}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{G}}}\sum_{A\subseteq E(\hat{\mathbb{G}})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\text{ cpt. of }\\
G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|A^{c};\mathcal{B})\end{subarray}}\beta b^{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*},H)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\text{ cpt. of }\\
G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A;\mathcal{V})\end{subarray}}\alpha a^{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma(\hat{\mathbb{G}},K)},

where
\hat{\underline{\mathbb{G}}}=(\hat{\mathbb{G}},\mathcal{V},\omega_{\mathcal{V}},\mathcal{B},\omega_{\mathcal{B}})
is the packaged ribbon graph
\underline{\mathbb{G}}\ba D^{*}_{Q}\cup N^{*}_{Q}\con D_{Q}\cup N_{Q}
. It is straightforward to verify that
\gamma(\hat{\mathbb{G}},K)
and
\gamma(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*},H)
are additive over connected components. Denote the sum of
\gamma(\hat{\mathbb{G}},K)
and
\gamma(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*},H)
over the connected components as
\gamma_{\mathcal{V}}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A)
and
\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|A^{c})
respectively.
So we can rewrite the above as

P(\mathbb{G};\alpha,\beta,a,b)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{G}}}\sum_{A\subseteq E(\hat{\mathbb{G}})}\alpha^{k(G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A;\mathcal{V}))-k(\mathbb{G})}\beta^{k(G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|A^{c};\mathcal{B}))-k(\mathbb{G})}a^{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mathcal{V}}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A)}b^{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|A^{c})}.

Recall from the proof of Theorem
3.3
that
\hat{\mathbb{G}}
has edge set
E(\hat{\mathbb{G}})=O_{Q}\cup O^{*}_{Q}
, where the edges in
O_{Q}
are bridges and those in
O^{*}_{Q}
are plane loops. If an edge is a bridge, then it is a plane loop in the dual
\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}
, and vice versa. If
e
is a plane loop, then deleting
e

does not change
\gamma_{\mathcal{V}}
, nor the number of connected components of the ribbon graph. So

P(\mathbb{G};\alpha,\beta,a,b)=\\
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{G}}}\sum_{A\subseteq O_{Q}}\sum_{B\subseteq O_{Q}^{*}}\alpha^{k(G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A;\mathcal{V}))-k(\mathbb{G})}\beta^{k(G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|B;\mathcal{B}))-k(\mathbb{G})}a^{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mathcal{V}}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A)}b^{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|B)}.

We make the following observations. As deletion does not change the vertex weights and all blocks started with weight 0, then
\sum_{v\in V(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A)}\omega_{\mathcal{V}}([v])=n(G(\mathbb{G}|D_{Q}\cup N_{Q};\mathcal{V}))
. Additionally, as contraction does not change the number of boundary components and every edge in
A
is a bridge, then
b(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A)=b(\mathbb{G}|D_{Q}\cup N_{Q}\cup A)=b(\mathbb{G}|D_{Q}\cup N_{Q})-|A|
. Finally, note that contracting an edge in a packaged ribbon graph does not disconnect the packaging, so
k(G(\mathbb{G}|D_{Q}\cup N_{Q};\mathcal{V}))=v(G(\mathbb{G}/D_{Q}\cup N_{Q};\mathcal{V}))
. Similar observations can be made for the dual
\underline{\mathbb{G}}^{*}
. So we obtain

\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mathcal{V}}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A)=n(G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A;\mathcal{V}))+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma(\mathbb{G}|D_{Q}\cup N_{Q}),

and

\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|B)=n(G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|B;\mathcal{V}))+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma(\mathbb{G}^{*}|D_{Q}^{*}\cup N_{Q}^{*}).

Note that the packaging
G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}|A;\mathcal{V})
(without vertex weights) is equivalent to the graph
G_{Q}|A
, and the packaging
G(\hat{\mathbb{G}}^{*}|B;\mathcal{B})
(without vertex weights) is equivalent to the graph
G_{Q}^{*}|B
.
Finally, we can deduce from the proof of Theorem
3.3
that contracting
D_{Q}\cup N_{Q}
and deleting
D_{Q}^{*}\cup N^{*}_{Q}
does not change the number of connected components in the ribbon graph, so
k(\mathbb{G}\ba D^{*}_{Q}\cup N^{*}_{Q}\con D_{Q}\cup N_{Q})=k(\mathbb{G})
.
Hence, the result follows.
∎

Remark
.

Butler derives a quasi-tree expansions for the Las Vergnas polynomial
[
3
, Theorems 5.6]
and rederives the quasi-tree expansion for the Bollobás–Riordan
[
3
, Theorems 5.2]
polynomial from the quasi-tree expansion of the Krushkal polynomial, Theorem
3.6
. So it follows that such expansions can also be recovered from Theorem
3.3
, the quasi-tree expansion of the packaged surface Tutte polynomial.

The Drivers of the Decline in Supermassive Black Hole Growth at z < 2 z<2
Zhibo Yu (喻知博), W. N. Brandt, Fan Zou, Bin Luo, Qingling Ni, D. P. Schneider, Fabio Vito

Abstract
It is well established that cosmic supermassive black hole (SMBH) growth peaks at z ≈ 1.5 − 2 z\approx 1.5-2 , followed by a strong decline of ≈ 1 − 1.5 ​ dex \approx 1-1.5\,\mathrm{dex} toward the present day, with the comoving number density of higher-luminosity active galactic nuclei (AGNs) peaking at higher redshift (referred to as “AGN downsizing”). We leverage the best current measurements of the SMBH accretion distribution, based upon data from nine well-characterized extragalactic fields with a “wedding-cake” design, to investigate and quantify the drivers of the drastic decline in cosmic SMBH growth. The decline in the typical Eddington ratio ( λ Edd \lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}} ) of AGNs (decreasing by ≈ 1.35 ​ dex \approx 1.35\,\mathrm{dex} from z ≈ 1.5 − 2 z\approx 1.5-2 to z ≈ 0.2 z\approx 0.2 ) is the dominant driver for the broad decline in SMBH growth, rather than a shift of accretion activity to less-massive SMBHs. As λ Edd \lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}} decreases toward lower redshift, the primary contributor to the cosmic SMBH accretion density ( ρ BHAR \rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}} ) has shifted from high- λ Edd \lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}} AGNs to low- λ Edd \lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}} AGNs, even though the latter always dominate the comoving AGN number density at z < 4 z<4 . We also find that the decline in SMBH growth toward lower SMBH mass in less-massive galaxies is primarily due to the decreasing outburst luminosity rather than the duty cycle.

Introduction

Understanding the growth history of supermassive black holes (SMBHs) is one of the most important topics for extragalactic studies. Observations have revealed tight correlations between black-hole mass (
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
) and the host-galaxy bulge stellar mass/bulge velocity dispersion
(
47

;
4

2
;
95
;
94
;
25
;
102

SMBHs in the local universe are generally much more “quiescent” than their high-redshift counterparts. Observations show that the comoving SMBH accretion-rate density (
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
) traced by the active galactic nucleus (AGN) luminosity function and the comoving number density of AGNs both increase from
z\approx 4
to
z\approx 2

(
10
, although the behavior at higher redshift is still not clear, e.g.,)
, peak at
z\approx 1.5-2
, and then drop significantly by
\approx 1-1.5\,\mathrm{dex}
by the present day
(
83
;
1
;
64
;
7
;
95
;
96
, e.g.,)
. Notably, the decline in SMBH growth is also luminosity dependent: the number density of high-luminosity AGNs peaks at a higher redshift (
z\approx 2
) than that of lower-luminosity AGNs, which reaches a maximum at

(

This apparent contradiction can be reconciled if we regard AGN downsizing as the dying down of cosmic accretion rather than as a symptom of antihierarchical formation of SMBHs. One proposed explanation is that AGN downsizing may be due to the shift of accretion activity to lower-
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
SMBHs with lower luminosity

90

39

40

For example, it is likely that more massive galaxies tend to host more massive and luminous SMBHs, and/or the AGN duty cycle (

II.2
Methodology

We denote
p(\lambda|M_{\star},z)
and
p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)
as the conditional probability density per unit
\log\lambda
and
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
of a galaxy with (
M_{\star}
,
z
) hosting an AGN with
\lambda\equiv L_{\mathrm{X}}/M_{\star}/[\mathrm{erg}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}M_{\sun}^{-1}]
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\equiv L_{\mathrm{bol}}/L_{\mathrm{Edd}}
, respectively, where
L_{\mathrm{bol}}
is the AGN bolometric luminosity and
L_{\mathrm{Edd}}
is the Eddington luminosity of the SMBH.
102
obtained
p(\lambda|M_{\star},z)
at
z=0-4
with
\log M_{\star}=9.5-12
, reaching down to
\log\lambda>31.5
. The
X-ray
bolometric correction factor as a function of
L_{\mathrm{bol}}
(in units of
\mathrm{erg}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}
) is
k_{\mathrm{X}}(L_{\mathrm{bol}})
. For a fixed
M_{\star}
, by definition,

\begin{split}p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)&=p(\lambda|M_{\star},z)\frac{\mathrm{d}\log\lambda}{\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}}\\
&=p(\lambda|M_{\star},z)\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{d}\log k_{\mathrm{X}}(L_{\mathrm{bol}})}{\mathrm{d}\log L_{\mathrm{bol}}}\right).\end{split}

(1)

The second equality holds because

\begin{split}\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}&=\frac{L_{\mathrm{X}}k_{\mathrm{X}}(L_{\mathrm{bol}})}{1.26\times 10^{38}\times M_{\mathrm{BH}}}=\frac{L_{\mathrm{X}}\,k_{\mathrm{X}}(L_{\mathrm{bol}})\,M_{\star}}{1.26\times 10^{38}\times\eta(M_{\star})\,M_{\star}}\\
&=\lambda\,k_{\mathrm{X}}(L_{\mathrm{bol}})\,\frac{M_{\star}}{1.26\times 10^{38}\times\eta(M_{\star})}.\end{split}

(2)

The
M_{\star}
-related term is constant when we calculate
p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)
. We take the logarithm of both sides of Equation
2
, and differentiate both sides with respect to
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
. Since
\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=\mathrm{d}\log L_{\mathrm{bol}}
when
\eta(M_{\star})
is fixed, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}\log\lambda}{\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}}=1-\frac{\mathrm{d}\log k_{\mathrm{X}}(L_{\mathrm{bol}})}{\mathrm{d}\log L_{\mathrm{bol}}}.

(3)

We adopt the
\log k_{\mathrm{X}}-\log L_{\mathrm{bol}}
relation from Equation 2 of
28
:

k_{\mathrm{X}}=10.96\left[1+\left(\frac{\log[L_{\mathrm{bol}}/L_{\sun}]}{11.93}\right)^{17.79}\right].

(4)

k_{\mathrm{X}}
diverges at very high
L_{\mathrm{bol}}
, so we set an upper limit of
k_{\mathrm{X}}=100
because this is about the maximum value observed in previous literature
(
59
;
41
;
85
, e.g.,)
. The limit corresponds to large
L_{\mathrm{bol}}
and
L_{\mathrm{X}}
thresholds of
\log[L_{\mathrm{bol}}/\mathrm{erg}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}]=47.0
and
\log[L_{\mathrm{X}}/\mathrm{erg}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}]=45.0
. For
L_{\mathrm{bol}}
higher than the threshold
L_{\mathrm{bol}}
, we apply a constant
k_{\mathrm{X}}=100
. Although higher
k_{\mathrm{X}}
has been observed for very luminous quasars
(
60
, e.g.,)
, these quasars are very rare and are generally missed in our fields. We have tested that setting the upper limit of
k_{\mathrm{X}}
to
\approx 400
only changes our results for the highest
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
bin (Section
III.1
) by
<0.2\,\mathrm{dex}
.

With
p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)
[or equivalently,
p(\lambda|M_{\star},z)
], we can derive many metrics describing SMBH growth. For example, one of the key results in
102
is the measurement of
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
, sampled down to AGNs with
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=0.0013
(corresponding to
\log\lambda=31.5
). We adopt a radiative efficiency of
\epsilon=0.1
, which is a typical value for the general AGN population. Although at
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\lesssim 0.01

may become relevant, they are not expected to contribute a significant fraction of SMBH growth

67

99

\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}

\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
can be expressed as

\begin{split}&\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}(M_{\star},z;\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}},\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}})\\
&=\int_{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}}}^{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}}\frac{(1-\epsilon)\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}M_{\star}\,\kappa(M_{\star})}{\epsilon\,c^{2}}p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},\end{split}

(5)

where

\kappa(M_{\star})=[1.26\times 10^{38}\times\eta(M_{\star})/M_{\star}]\,\mathrm{erg}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}M_{\sun}^{-1},

(6)

which converts
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}M_{\star}
to
L_{\mathrm{bol}}
. For most of our analyses, under the assumption of
\eta(M_{\star})\equiv M_{\mathrm{BH}}=0.002\,M_{\star}

34

and

III
Results

III.1
What Primarily Causes the Decline in SMBH Growth as a Function of Redshift?

In this subsection, we investigate the reason why
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
declines dramatically at
z\lesssim 1.5-2
. As a useful first-order approximation,
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
can be factored into three redshift-dependent components and a constant factor:

\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}=n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}\times\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle\times\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle\times\frac{(1-\epsilon)\times 1.26\times 10^{38}}{\epsilon c^{2}},

(7)

where
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
represent the typical
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
and typical
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
of AGNs that contribute to most of
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
. Note that
1.26\times 10^{38}\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle=\langle M_{\star}\kappa(M_{\star})\rangle
. The last three terms on the right-hand side of Equation
7
,
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle(1-\epsilon)/\epsilon c^{2}\times 1.26\times 10^{38}
, simply describe the typical SMBH accretion rate
per AGN
.
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is the “effective” AGN number density set as if all AGNs have the same SMBH mass of
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
and accrete at
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
in order to produce the observed
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
.
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
,
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
, and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
correspond to the three redshift-dependent factors that control the decline in SMBH growth in key question 1 in Section
I
. While
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
represent the properties of a typical AGN,
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is useful in assessing the expected number density of those AGNs. By definition,
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
should only be calculated using Equation
7
after
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
,
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
, and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
are determined. Note that
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
may be different from the true AGN number density
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
, because
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
are not the arithmetic average of
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
and
M_{\star}
for all AGNs. We will further illustrate this point in Section
III.1.2
. In the following subsections, we aim to measure
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
,
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
,
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
, and
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
in different redshift bins. The redshift bins are based upon those in
88
, with bin boundaries at 0.2, 0.5, 0.8, 1.1, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, and 4.0 (i.e., this is the binning available for the galaxy stellar mass function; see Section
III.1.1
). The numbers of
X-ray
AGNs and normal galaxies are summarized at the bottom of Table
1
. We also aim to quantify the impact of these factors on the decline of
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
, i.e.,

\Delta\log\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}=\Delta\log n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}+\Delta\log\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle+\Delta\log\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle,

(8)

where the
\Delta
’s represent the change of these quantities from the
z=1.5-2
bin to
z=0.2-0.5
bin.

III.1.1
Cosmic BHAR Density and AGN Number Density

We can calculate
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
in a redshift bin for SMBHs accreting between
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}}
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}
by convolving
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
with the galaxy stellar mass function (SMF;
\phi_{\mathrm{M}}
) in that redshift bin:

\begin{split}&\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}(z;\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}},\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}})\\
&=\int_{9.5}^{12}\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}(M_{\star},z;\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}},\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}})\phi_{\mathrm{M}}(M_{\star}|z)\mathrm{d}\log M_{\star}\\
&=\int_{9.5}^{12}\int_{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}}}^{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}}\frac{(1-\epsilon)p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)\phi_{\mathrm{M}}(M_{\star}|z)}{\epsilon\,c^{2}}\\
&\phantom{AThespaceholder}\times\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}M_{\star}\kappa(M_{\star})\,\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\mathrm{d}\log M_{\star}\\
&=\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon\,c^{2}}\int_{9.5}^{12}\int_{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}}}^{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}}f(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)\\
&\phantom{AThespaceholder}\times\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}M_{\star}\kappa(M_{\star})\,\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\mathrm{d}\log M_{\star},\end{split}

(9)

where

f(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)\equiv p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)\phi_{\mathrm{M}}(M_{\star}|z),

(10)

We adopt the SMF from
88
, using the same redshift bins as defined in their work. Our analyses only consider SMBHs residing in galaxies with
\log M_{\star}=9.5-12
because the
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
for massive black holes in dwarf galaxies is still poorly understood, and dwarf galaxies do not necessarily always host massive black holes
(
63
;
32
;
101
, e.g.,)
.

\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
for AGNs in different
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}

\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
sampled by all of our AGNs, we successfully produce the peak at
z\approx 1.5-2
, and the decline of
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
from
z=1.5-2
to

is
-1.28_{-0.08}^{+0.08}\,\mathrm{dex}
. In Appendix

, we present simple functional fits to our measurements in different
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
bins. For comparison, in Figure
1
we present the cosmic star-formation rate density (SFRD) from
57
, which is scaled by a factor of 5000 to approximately match the peak of total
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
. The decline in
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
is larger than the decline in SFRD, which is consistent with previous results
(
1
;
96
, e.g.,)
. We also show the total
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
from
83
and
95
. Our results are generally in agreement with theirs except at
z<0.8
where our total
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
is slightly lower than that in
83
. This may be because
83
adopted the
X-ray
bolometric correction in
41
, which is slightly larger than that in
28
at
\log L_{\mathrm{X}}=42-45
.

Our results also show AGN downsizing in different
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
bins instead of the typical downsizing phenomena observed in different luminosity bins. We find that
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
contributed by high-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGNs peaks at a higher redshift than for low-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGNs. The bottom panel of Figure
1
shows the ratio of
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
contributed by AGNs with
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}>0.1
to that by AGNs with
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}<0.1
. At
z\gtrsim 1
,
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
is mainly contributed by high-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGNs. Our results also indicate that super-Eddington accretion contributes to much of the accretion density at
z\gtrsim 2
, but the difference is not very significant and may suffer additional bias. This is because the difference between the
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=0.1-1
bin and
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=1-100
bin is generally comparable to the 90% statistical uncertainty, and at
z>3
, there may be more hidden accretion power at low-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
than expected (see the discussion at the end of Section
III.1.2
). At
z\lesssim 0.5
, more of the accretion activity has shifted to low-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGNs, and about half of the
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
at
z=0.2-0.5
is contributed by AGNs with
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}<0.1
. We have also verified that our result for super-Eddington AGNs is not sensitive to the choice of
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}
. The results are very similar when
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}
takes any value
>100
; the difference is
\lesssim 0.15\,\mathrm{dex}
when we change
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}
from 100 to 10. This difference is also smaller than the 90% statistical uncertainty for the
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=1-100
bin. So far, we have not yet quantified the decline in
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
, but the fact that the primary contributor to
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
shifts from AGNs with
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}>0.1
to those with
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}<0.1
indicates that
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
has declined significantly from
z\approx 2
to
z\approx 0.2
. We have verified that the results for
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
remain similar after excluding eFEDS from our analyses in Appendix
A
.

Similar to the calculation of
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
, the AGN host-galaxy SMF (
\phi^{\mathrm{AGN}}_{\mathrm{M}}
) for AGNs accreting between
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}}
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}
can be directly calculated by convolving
p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)
with
\phi_{\mathrm{M}}
:

\begin{split}&\phi^{\mathrm{AGN}}_{\mathrm{M}}(M_{\star};\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}},\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}|z)\\
&=\int_{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}}}^{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}}p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)\phi_{\mathrm{M}}(M_{\star}|z)\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\\
&=\int_{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}}}^{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}}f(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}.\end{split}

(11)

The
\phi^{\mathrm{AGN}}_{\mathrm{M}}
at
z<2.0
in different
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
bins is shown in Figure
2
. From
z=1.5-2.0
to
z=0.2-0.5
, the decline in
\phi^{\mathrm{AGN}}_{\mathrm{M}}
is most significant in the
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=1-100
bin, reaching
\approx 2\,\mathrm{dex}
at
\log M_{\star}=10-11.5
, while the decline in the
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=0.01-0.1
bin is only
<1\,\mathrm{dex}
at all
M_{\star}
. The result is consistent with that in Figure
1
, where
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
for high-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGNs declines more significantly than that for low-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGNs.

With
\phi^{\mathrm{AGN}}_{\mathrm{M}}
we can then further derive
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
for AGNs accreting between
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}}
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}
as

\begin{split}&n_{\mathrm{AGN}}(z;\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}},\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}})\\
&=\int_{9.5}^{12}\phi^{\mathrm{AGN}}_{\mathrm{M}}(M_{\star};\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{low}},\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{high}}|z)\mathrm{d}\log M_{\star}.\end{split}

(12)

Note that Equation
12
is not intended to calculate
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
, because by definition, after quantifying
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
,
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is obtained using Equation
7
. However, Equation
12
can still provide insights into how many AGNs have
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
and
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
similar to
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
. In the next subsection, we will first quantify
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
. With them, we can further determine
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
.

III.1.2
Quantifying the Impact of
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
,
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
, and
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}

The quantities
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
are less well-defined than
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
and
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
. Since we expect
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
can represent the
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
and
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
of the AGNs that contribute to most of
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
, we can determine
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
by examining the contribution to
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
in different (
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
,
\log M_{\mathrm{BH}}
) grid cells in the
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}-\log M_{\mathrm{BH}}
plane and identifying the region that produces most of the contribution.

From Equations
10
,
11
, and
12
, we denote
F(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)
as the conditional probability density per unit
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
per unit
\log M_{\star}
that a galaxy at
z
has
M_{\star}
and hosts a SMBH accreting at
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}

\begin{split}F(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)&=\frac{f(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)}{\iint f(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)\,\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\mathrm{d}\log M_{\star}}\\
&=\frac{f(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)}{n_{\mathrm{AGN}}(z)}.\end{split}

(13)

The quantity
(1-\epsilon)/(\epsilon c^{2})\times F(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}},M_{\star}|z)\times M_{\star}\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\kappa(M_{\star})
provides a measure of the contribution to
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
per galaxy at different
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
and
\log M_{\mathrm{BH}}
in the
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}-\log M_{\mathrm{BH}}
plane after we convert
M_{\star}
to
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
based upon the adopted
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation. Note that the integration in Equation
13
is for galaxies with
\log M_{\star}=9.5-12
. Figure
3
shows the results assuming the median
p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)
, with the integration range of
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
restricted between –3 and 2. We define
\log\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\log\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
such that they are represented by the intersection point of the running median of
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
and
\log M_{\mathrm{BH}}
in the
\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}-\log M_{\mathrm{BH}}
plane. The solid error bars represent the regions that are enclosed by the
25-75\%
quantiles of
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
and
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
. Those regions are generally consistent with the maximum values of the heatmaps at all redshifts, indicating our identification of
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
can truly represent the AGNs that contribute most of
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
.

So far, we have quantified
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
,
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
, and
\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle
at different redshifts. We now use Equation
7
to calculate
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
, and use Equation
12
to calculate
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
in different
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
bins. The results are shown in Figure
5
. We provide simple functional fits to our
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
measurements in different
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
bins in Appendix
B
. For comparison, in Figure
5
we show the total
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
for AGNs with
\log L_{\mathrm{X}}>42
from
16
,
64
, and
70
. Our results generally agree with those in the literature.
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
declines at all
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
from
z\approx 2
to
z\approx 0.2
, and AGNs with low-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
(
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}<0.1
) always dominate the total
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
at
z<4
. The decline is most significant in the
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=1-100
bin, reaching
\approx 1.6\,\mathrm{dex}
from
z=1.5-2.0
to
z=0.2-0.5
, and the decline in lower-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
bins is much smaller, which is consistent with the evolution of
\phi^{\mathrm{AGN}}_{\mathrm{M}}
shown in Figure
2
. The decline in total
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
is mostly due to the general decline in
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
, where AGNs accreting slightly higher than our sample limit (
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}>0.0013
) are shifted below the limit. We have also verified that
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
remain similar after excluding eFEDS from our analyses in Appendix
A
. However,
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
shows dramatically different behavior. From
z=4
to
z\approx 0.5
,
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is consistently increasing, and it only shows flattening or a slight downward trend in the last redshift bin of
z=0.2-0.5
. The increase of
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
from
z=1.5-2.0
to
z=0.2-0.5
is
0.29_{-0.46}^{+0.38}\,\mathrm{dex}
.

Although the increase is not significant considering its 90% statistical uncertainty, this result can be understood by the drastic decrease in
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
shown in Figure
4
. As
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
decreases, the main contributor to
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
is gradually shifted to low-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGNs that always dominate the total
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
, but since the total
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
decreases,
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is generally constant from
z=1.5-2.0
to
z=0.2-0.5
. Figure
5
also demonstrates that
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is different from the total
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
. Take the
z=1.5-2
bin as an example:
\log\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
is about between –0.7 and 0.2 in Figure
4
, which indicates
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
is hardly contributed to by AGNs at the other
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
values. Thus
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is similar to
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
in the
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=0.1-1
bin (as shown in Figure
5
) and hardly counts AGNs in the other
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
bins. We also calculate the expected
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
for AGNs within the
25-75\%
quantile of
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
(i.e., for AGNs with
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
). The estimates for
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
and
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
for AGNs with
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
are generally consistent within the 90% statistical uncertainty, indicating that our estimated
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
can truly represent the typical
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
.

III.2
Does
M_{\star}
Mainly Modulate the Typical Outburst Luminosity or Duty Cycle to Reduce SMBH Growth?

In this subsection, we investigate the decline in
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
as
M_{\star}
decreases at fixed redshift, as per key question 2. With a similar approach to that in Section
III.1
,
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
can be approximately factored into two components and a constant factor at fixed (
M_{\star}
,
z
):

\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}=\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}\times f_{\mathrm{AGN}}\times\frac{(1-\epsilon)\,\kappa(M_{\star})}{\epsilon c^{2}},

(14)

where
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
is the sample-averaged outburst luminosity
per AGN
and
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
is the AGN duty cycle (or equivalently the AGN fraction). Note that our
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
is based upon the sample-averaged
L_{\mathrm{bol}}

per galaxy
, which is different from
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
.
M_{\star}
can modulate
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
or
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
to change
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
.

We can calculate the
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
for galaxies if we regard SMBHs in those galaxies accreting above a given
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
(
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{min}}
) as AGNs:

f_{\mathrm{AGN}}=\int_{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{min}}}^{+\infty}p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}.

(15)

On the other hand, the
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
can be calculated as

\begin{split}\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}=\int_{\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{min}}}^{+\infty}M_{\star}&\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\kappa(M_{\star})\\
&\times p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)/f_{\mathrm{AGN}}\,\mathrm{d}\log\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}.\end{split}

(16)

The term
p(\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}|M_{\star},z)/f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
represents the conditional probability that
an AGN
with
M_{\star}
and
z
accretes at
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
. We set
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}^{\mathrm{min}}=0.01
following
2
.

The top panels of Figure
6
show
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
and
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
as functions of redshift at different
M_{\star}
.
M_{\star}
strongly modulates
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
at all redshifts. This behavior generally reflects the
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation, where more massive galaxies host more massive SMBHs. Without a strict dependence of
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
on
M_{\star}
at fixed redshift, more massive galaxies tend to host more luminous AGNs.

\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
also slightly decreases toward lower redshift at fixed
M_{\star}
. With our assumption of a linear scaling between
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
and
M_{\star}
, this result indicates a slight decrease in
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
. The small decrease in
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
here does not contradict the large evolution of
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
in Section
III.1.2
, because
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
characterizes the typical
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
for the AGNs contributing most of
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
, while
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
is simply the average
L_{\mathrm{bol}}
of AGNs.
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
is dominated by low-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGNs at all redshifts as shown in Figure
5
, causing a less-significant evolution of the average
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
. On the other hand, in Figure
6
top-right panel,
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
is almost independent of
M_{\star}
at low redshift and is clearly dependent on
M_{\star}
only at
z\gtrsim 1
. At fixed
M_{\star}
,
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
show stronger redshift evolution in more massive galaxies. Such behavior for
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
has been found in previous works
(
2
;
3
;
11
, e.g.,)
. At
z\gtrsim 1
, larger amounts of gas are available
(
24
, e.g.,)
, which tends to trigger luminous AGNs in massive galaxies with massive SMBHs and deeper potential wells
(
75
, e.g.,)
. As redshift decreases, the lack of available gas starves luminous AGNs, causing a more significant decline in
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
in massive galaxies.

The bottom panel of Figure
6
shows the declines in
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
,
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
, and
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
from
\log M_{\star}=11.5
to
\log M_{\star}=10
as functions of redshift. These two
\log M_{\star}
values are chosen for illustration because our measurements at these masses are well-constrained. At
z\lesssim 1
, the decline in
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
is driven almost entirely by
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
. At
z\gtrsim 1
, the decline in
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
is only about
0.3-0.7\,\mathrm{dex}
, which is more than 10 times smaller than the decline in
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
(
1.4-2.0\,\mathrm{dex}
).

Overall, our results indicate that
M_{\star}
predominantly regulates
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
instead of
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
to drive variations in
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
. These results are also generally consistent with those from
2
in their Figures 6 and 8, where higher-
M_{\star}
galaxies exhibit a higher
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
than lower-
M_{\star}
galaxies at
z\gtrsim 1
, and the decrease in
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
for lower-
M_{\star}
galaxies is slightly faster than that for higher-
M_{\star}
galaxies. Our measurements provide superior constraints for a wide
M_{\star}
range than those from
2
thanks to the larger survey volume of XMM-SERVS and eFEDS. We also test the nonlinear
M_{\mathrm{BH}}-M_{\star}
relation of
34
in our analyses and find an even higher fractional contribution of
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
to the variation in
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
. This arises because the nonlinear relation (
M_{\mathrm{BH}}\propto M_{\star}^{1.61}
) imposes a stronger
M_{\star}
-dependence than the linear case, yielding larger differences in
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
between galaxies of different
M_{\star}
, and thus enhancing the contribution of
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
in driving the variation in
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
.

IV
Summary and Future Work

In this work, we leverage the best-measured sample-averaged SMBH accretion rates in
102
to understand and quantify the decline in SMBH growth at
z\lesssim 2
. Our main results are summarized as follows:

1.

We confirm that
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
peaks at
z\approx 2
and declines dramatically since then. We find that low-accretion activity gradually dominates the contribution to
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
as redshift decreases. At
z\gtrsim 1
,
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
is mostly contributed by rapidly accreting SMBHs, while at
z\lesssim 0.5
,
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}<0.1
activity dominates
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
. See Section
III.1.1
.

2.

From
z\approx 2
to
z\approx 0.2
, the decline in
\rho_{\mathrm{BHAR}}
is mainly driven by the decline in
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
(
\Delta\log\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle=-1.35_{-0.39}^{+0.46}\,\mathrm{dex}
) rather than
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
(
\Delta\log\langle M_{\mathrm{BH}}\rangle=-0.21_{-0.11}^{+0.11}\,\mathrm{dex}
). On the other hand,
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is generally constant (increasing by
0.29_{-0.46}^{+0.38}\,\mathrm{dex}
) due to the significantly lower
\langle\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}\rangle
at lower redshift, the decreasing total
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
, and the fact that the low-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
AGN number density dominates the total
n_{\mathrm{AGN}}
at all redshifts. See Section
III.1.2
.

3.

The observed dependence of
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
on
M_{\star}
at fixed redshift arises primarily because
M_{\star}
predominantly regulates
\overline{L_{\mathrm{bol}}^{\mathrm{AGN}}}
instead of
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
to drive variations in
\mathrm{\overline{BHAR}}
.
f_{\mathrm{AGN}}
shows stronger redshift evolution in more massive galaxies, which may be due to the availability of cold gas preferentially affecting luminous AGNs in massive galaxies.
See Section
III.2
.

Overall, our results clarify the primary cause of the decline in SMBH growth at
z<2
and provide new insight into the AGN downsizing phenomenon. Previous studies suggest that AGN downsizing does not reflect antihierarchical behavior, but instead results from a combination of factors, e.g.,
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
and
M_{\mathrm{BH}}
, where
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
may play a more important role
(
8
;
31
;
1
;
79
, e.g.,)
. Our findings provide the most compelling evidence to date that
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
plays the primary role in driving AGN downsizing.

There are several ways to extend this work with better datasets in the future. For example, there are existing wide-field
X-ray
surveys by Chandra and/or XMM-Newton with generally sufficient quality multiwavelength coverage, such as the Chandra Deep Wide-Field Survey
(
61
,
CDWFS
; e.g.,)
, XMM-XXL
(
71
, e.g.,)
, Stripe 82X
(
48
, e.g.,)
, and Stripe 82-XL
(
69
, e.g.,)
, totaling
\sim 100\,\deg^{2}
. Future work could include these surveys in the “wedding-cake” design to help sample AGNs at low redshift and/or high luminosity. There are also other very wide-field
X-ray
catalogs above
2\,\mathrm{keV}
—such as the Chandra Source Catalog
(
30
, e.g.,)
, 4XMM
(
89
, e.g.,)
, and the Swift/BAT catalog
(
51
, e.g.,)
—providing a much larger survey volume with reduced obscuration effects, particularly valuable for sampling the most luminous AGNs. Soon, these catalogs will benefit from excellent multiwavelength coverage provided by new photometric and spectroscopic surveys such as LSST
(

, Euclid
(

, Roman
(

or

peaks, and

and

z_{0}
, as seen in our Figures

and

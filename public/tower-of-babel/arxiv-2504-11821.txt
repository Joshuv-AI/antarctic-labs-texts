Abstract
. We propose an f f -divergence extension of the Hasegawa-Nishiyama thermodynamic uncertainty relation. More precisely, we introduce the stochastic thermodynamic entropy production based on generalised f f -divergences and derive corresponding uncertainty relations in connection with the symmetry entropy.

Primary 26D10. Secondary 26B12, 35A23.

1.
Introduction

Recently, Hasegawa and Nishiyama
[
HN25
]
used the Kullback-Leibler divergence based entropy production to answer positively the following question:

Does an entropic uncertainty relation hold in stochastic thermodynamics?

To state their uncertainty relations we need recall some necessary notions.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Phi
be a real-valued random variable, with
|\Phi|
being its absolute value. The probability distribution of
|\Phi|
is given by

(1)

P(|\Phi|=\phi)=\begin{cases}P(\Phi=\phi)+P(\Phi=-\phi),&\phi>0,\\
P(\Phi=0),&\phi=0.\end{cases}

Following Hasegawa and Nishiyama
[
HN25
]
, we also introduce

Definition 2
.

Consider a real-valued observable
\Phi
, the entropy difference between the distribution
P(\Phi)
and its absolute value distribution
P(|\Phi|)
is defined as

(2)

\Lambda[P(\Phi)]:=H[P(\Phi)]-H[P(|\Phi|)],

where

(3)

H[P(X)]=-\sum_{x}P(X=x)\ln P(X=x),

denotes the Shannon entropy of
P(X)
. Here we refer to
\Lambda[P(\Phi)]
as
Symmetry Entropy
since it measures in a sense the symmetry of the value distribution of the real-valued
\Phi
around 0. In particular, if
\Phi\geq 0
or
\Phi\leq 0
, then
\Lambda[P(\Phi)]\equiv 0
.

Remark 3
.

For continuous random variables, the Shannon entropy is defined by

(4)

H[P(\Phi)]=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(\phi)\ln P(\phi)\,d\phi.

We assume
P(\phi)
is differentiable.
The probability density
P(|\Phi|)
is defined by

(5)

P(|\Phi|=\phi)=P(\Phi=\phi)+P(\Phi=-\phi).

We do not consider the term
P(\Phi=0)
, because such measure is necessarily 0 for the smooth probability density.
The symmetry entropy
\Lambda[P(\Phi)]
is then defined similarly.

Assuming the local detailed balance, it is known that the entropy production under the steady-state condition can be naturally defined by
[
HN25
,
Sei05
,
Sei08
]
:

(6)

\Sigma_{\text{KL}}=D_{\text{KL}}[\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)\parallel\mathcal{P}(\Gamma^{\dagger})],

where

(7)

D_{\text{KL}}[P\parallel Q]=\sum_{x}P(x)\ln\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)},

is the Kullback-Leibler divergence of two (discrete) probability distributions
P
and
Q
,
\Gamma
is a stochastic trajectory of the process to be observed (
\Gamma^{\dagger}
being its time reversal),
and
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
and
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma^{\dagger})
denote the probability of measuring
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\dagger}
, respectively.

The following thermodynamic entropic uncertainty relation is obtained in
[
HN25
]
.

Theorem 4

(Hasegawa and Nishiyama, 2025)
.

Let
\Sigma_{\text{KL}}
be the entropy production as defined in (
6
) under the steady-state condition.
Consider a real-valued observable
\Phi(\Gamma)
, which is assumed to be anti-symmetric with respect to the time reversal:

(8)

\Phi(\Gamma)=-\Phi(\Gamma^{\dagger}),

and let
P(\Phi)
be the probability distribution of
\Phi
. Then we have

(9)

\Sigma_{\text{KL}}\geq[1-P(\Phi=0)]\ln 2-\Lambda[P(\Phi)]\geq 0.

Remark 5
.

We refer to the first inequality as the uncertainty relation between
\Sigma_{\text{KL}}
and
\Lambda[P(\Phi)]
,
since it indicates that their sum in the case
P(\Phi=0)=0
is no less than
\ln 2
.
We also point out that the quantity
[1-P(\Phi=0)]\ln 2-\Lambda[P(\Phi)]
is nothing but the Jensen-Shannon divergence for
\Phi
and
-\Phi
, see
[
HN25
, Appendix B]
.

In this note, we use the general
f
-divergences, instead of the particular Kullback-Leibler divergence, to demonstrate that there is an entropic uncertainty relation between generalised entropy production and the symmetry entropy.

Definition 6

(e.g.,
[
SV16
]
)
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function with
f(1)=0
. Let
P
and
Q
be two probability distributions on a measurable space
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F})
.
If
P\ll Q
then the
f
-divergence (for
P
over
Q
) is defined as

(10)

D_{f}[P\parallel Q]=\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[f\left(\frac{dP}{dQ}\right)\right],

where
\frac{dP}{dQ}
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative and
f(0):=f(0+)
.

We define the
Generalised Entropy Production
based on
f
-divergence as:

(11)

\Sigma_{f}=D_{f}[\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)\parallel\mathcal{P}(\Gamma^{\dagger})].

Hence the Kullback-Leibler divergence
D_{\text{KL}}
corresponds to
D_{f}
for
f(x)=x\ln x
.

Remark 7
.

For the discrete case, with
P(x)
and
Q(x)
being the corresponding probability distributions, we can write out the expectation as the following sum

(12)

D_{f}[P\parallel Q]=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}Q(x)f\left(\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\right).

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 8
.

Consider a real-valued observable
\Phi(\Gamma)
, which is anti-symmetric in the sense of (
8
). If
f(x)+\ln(1+x)
is convex, then the following inequality holds:

(13)

\Sigma_{f}\geq[1-P(\Phi=0)]\ln 2-\Lambda[P(\Phi)].

In particular, for
f(x)=x\ln x
, we recover the result of Hasegawa and Nishiyama.

Corollary 9
.

Let

f_{1}=(x-1)^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{2}=x\ln\frac{2x}{x+1}+\ln\frac{2}{x+1},

and the associated
f
-divergences become the
\chi^{2}
-divergence and Jensen-Shannon divergence.
Since
f_{1}+\ln(1+x)
and
f_{2}+\ln(1+x)
are both convex, we get immediately from Theorem
8
the following thermodynamic uncertainty relations

(14)

\Sigma_{\chi^{2}}\geq[1-P(\Phi=0)]\ln 2-\Lambda[P(\Phi)]

and

(15)

\Sigma_{JS}\geq[1-P(\Phi=0)]\ln 2-\Lambda[P(\Phi)].

We refer to
[
OY17
]
for Schrödinger-Robertson uncertainty relations in an algebraic framework.
We refer to
[
Sei12
,
VE15
,
BS15
,
GHPE16
,
Has23
,
NH24
]
and the references in
[
HN25
]

for other uncertainty relations in stochastic thermodynamics.

2.
Proof of Theorem
8

The Shannon entropy of
\Phi
can be rewritten as

(16)

\displaystyle H[P(\Phi)]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\phi}P(\phi)\ln P(\phi)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\phi>0}P(\phi)\ln P(\phi)-\sum_{\phi<0}P(\phi)\ln P(\phi)-P(0)\ln P(0)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\phi>0}[P(\phi)\ln P(\phi)+P(-\phi)\ln P(-\phi)]-P(0)\ln P(0),

where we abbreviated
P(\phi)=P(\Phi=\phi)
for notational convenience.
With the Definition
1
, the Shannon entropy of
|\Phi|
is then given by

(17)

\displaystyle H[P(|\Phi|)]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\phi\geq 0}P(|\Phi|=\phi)\ln P(|\Phi|=\phi)

\displaystyle=-P(0)\ln P(0)-\sum_{\phi>0}[P(\phi)+P(-\phi)]\ln[P(\phi)+P(-\phi)].

This way, we can rewrite furthermore the symmetry entropy as

(18)

\displaystyle\Lambda[P(\Phi)]

\displaystyle=H[P(\Phi)]-H[P(|\Phi|)]

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\phi>0}P(\phi)\ln\frac{P(\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)}-\sum_{\phi>0}P(-\phi)\ln\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)},

and the
f
-divergence as

(19)

\displaystyle D_{f}[P(\Phi)\parallel P(-\Phi)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi}P(-\phi)f\left(\frac{P(\phi)}{P(-\phi)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi>0}P(-\phi)f\left(\frac{P(\phi)}{P(-\phi)}\right)+\sum_{\phi>0}P(\phi)f\left(\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right).

By adding equation (
18
) and equation (
19
), we get

(20)

\displaystyle D_{f}[P(\Phi)\parallel P(-\Phi)]+\Lambda[P(\Phi)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi>0}P(-\phi)\left[f\left(\frac{P(\phi)}{P(-\phi)}\right)-\ln\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{\phi>0}P(\phi)\left[f\left(\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)-\ln\frac{P(\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi}P(\phi)\left[f\left(\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)-\ln\frac{P(\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)}\right]-P(0)\ln 2

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi}P(\phi)\left[f\left(\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)+\ln\left(1+\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)\right]-P(0)\ln 2

\displaystyle\geq\ln 2-P(0)\ln 2,

where the last inequality involves the convexity of
f(x)+\ln(1+x)
since by Jensen

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{\phi}P(\phi)\left[f\left(\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)+\ln\left(1+\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq f\left(\sum_{\phi}P(\phi)\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)+\ln\left(1+\sum_{\phi}P(\phi)\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)

\displaystyle=f(1)+\ln 2=\ln 2.

Thanks to the Data Processing Inequality (see for example
[
PW25
]
) for general
f
-divergences,
while considering a transformation that maps the original random variables
X
and
Y
to new random variables
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
, we have

(22)

D_{f}[P(X)\parallel P(Y)]\geq D_{f}[P(\widetilde{X})\parallel P(\widetilde{Y})].

Applying this to the anti-symmetric observable
\Phi
, we get

(23)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{f}

\displaystyle=D_{f}[\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)\parallel\mathcal{P}(\Gamma^{\dagger})]

\displaystyle\geq D_{f}[{P}(\Phi(\Gamma))\parallel{P}(\Phi(\Gamma^{\dagger}))]

\displaystyle=D_{f}[P(\Phi)\parallel P(-\Phi)]

\displaystyle\geq[1-P(\Phi=0)]\ln 2-\Lambda[P(\Phi)],

where in the last line we used the key estimate (
20
).

Next, we consider the continuous case. We have the following identities

(24)

\displaystyle\Lambda[P(\Phi)]=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}P(\phi)\ln\frac{P(\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)}d\phi

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}P(-\phi)\ln\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)}d\phi

and

(25)

\displaystyle D_{f}[P(\Phi)\parallel P(-\Phi)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}P(\phi)f\left(\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)\,d\phi

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}P(-\phi)f\left(\frac{P(\phi)}{P(-\phi)}\right)\,d\phi.

Thus we get

(26)

\displaystyle D_{f}[P(\Phi)\parallel P(-\Phi)]+\Lambda[P(\Phi)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}P(\phi)\left[f\left(\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)-\ln\frac{P(\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)}\right]

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}P(-\phi)\left[f\left(\frac{P(\phi)}{P(-\phi)}\right)-\ln\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)+P(-\phi)}\right]d\phi

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(\phi)\left[f\left(\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)+\ln\left(1+\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\right)\right]d\phi

\displaystyle\geq f\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(\phi)\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\,d\phi\right)+\ln\left(1+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(\phi)\frac{P(-\phi)}{P(\phi)}\,d\phi\right)

\displaystyle=f(1)+\ln 2=\ln 2.

By the same arguments as in the discrete case, we obtain the following inequality

(27)

\Sigma_{f}\geq\ln 2-\Lambda[P(\Phi)].

Therefore, we finish the proof of Theorem
8
.

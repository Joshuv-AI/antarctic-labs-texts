Antipodality of Spherical Designs with Odd Harmonic Indices
Ryutaro Misawa, Akihiro Munemasa, Masanori Sawa

Abstract
. We determine the smallest size of a non-antipodal spherical design with harmonic indices { 1 , 3 , … , 2 ​ m − 1 } \{1,3,\dots,2m-1\} to be 2 ​ m + 1 2m+1 , where m m is a positive integer. This is achieved by proving an analogous result for interval designs.

1.
Introduction

Spherical designs appear in approximation theory, frame theory and combinatorics (see
[
1
,
6
,
18
]
). A more general concept of spherical designs was introduced by
[
5
]
. A finite subset
X
of a unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined to be a spherical design of harmonic index
t
if the sum of the values on
X
vanishes for every harmonic homogeneous polynomial of degree
t
. If
T
is a set of positive integers and
X
is a spherical design of harmonic index
t
for every
t\in T
, we simply say that
X
is a spherical
T
-design. Then the ordinary spherical
t
-design is nothing but a spherical
\{1,2,\dots,t\}
-design. For certain finite sets
T
of even integers, spherical
T
-designs with additional conditions on size or the number of distances have been considered (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
9
,
14
,
19
]
).

If
X
consists of a pair of antipodal points on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, then
X
is a spherical design of harmonic index
t
for every odd positive integer
t
. Conversely, it is not difficult to show that if
X\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is a spherical design of harmonic index
t
for all odd positive integers
t
, then
X
is necessarily a union of pairs of antipodal points on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
For a brief proof of this
fact, suppose that there exists a point
x\in X
with
-x\notin X
.
Since
|\langle x,y\rangle|<1
for all
y\in X\setminus\{x\}
,

\sum_{y\in X}\langle x,y\rangle^{2k-1}\to 1

as
k\to\infty
.
This contradicts the assumption that
X
is a spherical design of
harmonic index
2k-1
for all positive integers
k
.

Since a regular
(2m+1)
-gon in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is known to be a spherical
2m
-design (see
[
8
]
), it is also a spherical
\{1,3,\dots,2m-1\}
-design. Thus, if
T
is a finite set of positive integers with
2m-1=\max T
, then there exists a
(2m+1)
-point spherical
T
-design which is not a union of pairs of antipodal points.

Our main result is to show that
2m+1
is the smallest size of a finite set
X
which can be a non-antipodal spherical
\{1,3,\dots,2m-1\}
-design. This is achieved by proving an analogous result for interval designs.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall Newton’s identities that relate power sums and elementary symmetric polynomials. In Section
3
, we introduce an analogue of harmonic indices for interval designs and prove a lower bound for the size of a non-symmetric design. By regarding this result as a set version, we also prove its function version (i.e., a design with weights). In Section
4
, we prove our main result on a lower bound for the size of a non-antipodal spherical design with odd harmonic indices. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove the optimality of our results in Sections
3
and
4
by constructing non-antipodal designs with the smallest possible sizes.

2.
Preliminaries

For each non-negative integer
k
, we denote by
p_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
the
k
-th power sum and by
e_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. The following fact, widely known as Newton’s identity, gives relations between elementary symmetric polynomials and power sums.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

For all integers
n\geq k\geq 1
, we have

k\,e_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}e_{k-i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,p_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

(ii)

For all integers
k\geq n\geq 1
, we have

0=\sum_{i=k-n}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}e_{k-i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,p_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Since we will be dealing with spherical designs with odd harmonic indices, we establish relationships between power sums of odd degrees and elementary symmetric polynomials of odd degrees.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers with
2m-1\leq n
, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

p_{2k-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0\quad\text{for all }k\in\{1,\dots,m\}.

(ii)

e_{2k-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0\quad\text{for all }k\in\{1,\dots,m\}.

Proof.

The equivalence of
(i)
and
(ii)
is clear when
k=1
. We prove
(i)

\Rightarrow

(ii)
by induction on
m
. By Lemma
2.1

(i)
,

e_{2k-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{1}{2k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{2k-1}(-1)^{i-1}e_{2k-1-i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,p_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Note that in each term
e_{2k-1-i}\,p_{i}
(for
1\leq i\leq 2k-1
), the indices have opposite parity. Hence, by
(i)
and the induction hypothesis, we obtain
e_{2k-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0
.

Next, we prove
(ii)

\Rightarrow

(i)
by induction on
k
. By Lemma
2.1

(i)
,

\displaystyle p_{2k-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=(2k-1)e_{2k-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2k-2}(-1)^{i}e_{2k-1-i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,p_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Again, in each term
e_{2k-1-i}\,p_{i}
(for
1\leq i\leq 2k-2
), the indices have different parity. Then by
(ii)
and the induction hypothesis, we have
p_{2k-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0
.
∎

3.
Interval Designs

Let
(a,b)
be an open interval in
\mathbb{R}
and let
w(x)
be a probability density function such that all moments
\int_{a}^{b}x^{s}\,w(x)dx
are finite. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in(a,b)
. In numerical analysis, an integration formula of the type

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{s}=\int_{a}^{b}x^{s}\,w(x)dx,

for
s=1,2,\ldots,t
, is called a
strict Chebyshev-type quadrature of degree
t
if all
x_{i}
are distinct, and a
Chebyshev-type quadrature of degree
t
otherwise
[
10
]
. As exemplified by the Chebyshev-Gauss quadrature

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Bigl(\cos\Bigl(\frac{2k\pi}{2n-1}\Bigr)\Bigr)^{s}=\int_{-1}^{1}x^{s}\,\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}\,dx,

where
s
ranges over
1,2,\dots,2n-1
, Chebyshev-type formulas for the ultraspherical measure

(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-1/2}\,dx/\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{\lambda-1/2}\,dx
, where
\lambda
is a nonnegative integer, are a central object in the study of quadrature formulas. Meanwhile, the study of the configuration of points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
has long captured the attention of researchers in combinatorics and related areas, where the term “interval design” is widely used for the point configuration; for example see Bajnok
[
2
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A subset
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
of the interval
[a,b]
is called an
interval
t
-design
if

(1)

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{s}=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}x^{s}\,dx

holds for all
s=1,2,\dots,t
. More generally, for
T\subset\mathbb{N}
, the set
X
is called an interval design of
index

T
if (
1
) holds for every
s\in T
.

We will only consider interval designs of index
T_{m}
or interval
T_{m}
-designs, for brevity on the interval
[-1,1]
, where

(2)

T_{m}=\{2k-1\mid 1\leq k\leq m\}.

In particular, the definition can be stated for any measure on
[-1,1]
by replacing the right-hand side of (
1
) with the corresponding moment integral.
It may also be worth mentioning that (
1
) is equivalently written as
p_{s}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
for an odd positive integer
s
.

We say that a finite subset
X\subset[-1,1]
is
symmetric
if
X=-X
. More generally, a multiset
X\subset[-1,1]
is said to be
symmetric
if the multiplicity of
x
in
X
coincides with that of
-x
for every
x\in X
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers with
n\leq 2m
. Suppose that real numbers
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in[-1,1]
satisfy

(3)

p_{2k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0

for all
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Then the multiset
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
is symmetric. In particular, if a subset
X\subset[-1,1]
is an interval
T_{m}
-design with
|X|=n
,
then
X
is symmetric.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
0\notin X
, because removing zeros does not affect the conditions
\sum_{x\in X}x^{2k-1}=0
for
1\leq k\leq m
.
We first prove that (
3
) holds for every positive integer
k
by induction on
k
. Since
3
is assumed for
k\leq m
, so suppose
k>m
. Then
2k-1\geq n
.
Moreover, because there are only
n
variables,
e_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
for all
j>n
; in particular, for
j>2m
.
It follows from Lemma
2.1

(ii)
that

\displaystyle p_{2k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2k-1-n}^{2k-2}(-1)^{i}e_{2k-1-i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\,p_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}e_{2i-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\,p_{2(k-i)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=1}^{m}e_{2i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\,p_{2(k-i)-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

By Proposition
2.2
and (
3
),
we have
e_{2i-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
Thus, the first term is zero.
By the inductive hypothesis,
the second term
is also zero.
Therefore, we have shown that
(
3
) holds for every positive integer
k
.

We now prove the assertion by induction on
n
.
The cases
n=1,2
are obvious.
Let
Y=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n^{\prime}}\}
be the set of all distinct elements of

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, that is,
Y=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and

|Y|=n^{\prime}\leq n
.
Define
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}}
by

A_{i,j}=y_{j}^{2i-1}\quad(1\leq i,j\leq n^{\prime}).

Define a column vector
\boldsymbol{u}
whose
j
-th entry
u_{j}
is given by

u_{j}=|\{i\mid 1\leq i\leq n,\;x_{i}=y_{j}\}|\quad(1\leq j\leq n^{\prime}).

Then the
k
-th entry of
A\boldsymbol{u}
is

\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}y_{j}^{2k-1}u_{j}=p_{2k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0,

since we have shown that (
3
) holds for every positive integer
k
.
Thus
A\boldsymbol{u}=0
, and hence

0=\det A=\prod_{k=1}^{n^{\prime}}y_{k}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n^{\prime}}(y_{j}^{2}-y_{i}^{2}).

This implies that, either there exists
j
such that
y_{j}=0
, or
there exist
i,j
with
i<j
such that
y_{i}=-y_{j}
.
In the latter case, we may assume without loss of generality
x_{n-1}=-x_{n}
.
Since we have shown that (
3
) holds for every positive integer
k
,
we obtain

p_{2k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-2})=p_{2k-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0

for every positive integer
k
.
By induction, the multiset
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n-2}\}
is symmetric,
and so is
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.
The former case can be proved in a similar manner.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Although in this paper we restrict ourselves to interval designs for the normalized Lebesgue measure on
[-1,1]
, the notion of an interval design naturally extends to an arbitrary measure on an interval. In this broader setting, a challenging problem is the construction of interval designs with rational points and weights for the ultraspherical measure (see Bannai et al.
[
4
, p. 208, Problem 2]
). It was Mishima et al.
[
13
]
who first observed Proposition
3.2
for
m\leq 3
and thereby established, together with explicit constructions, a classification theorem of rational
5
-designs with
6
points for the Chebyshev measure
(1-t^{2})^{-1/2}dt/\pi
.

In Proposition
3.2
, the set

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
is regarded as a multiset.
This is equivalent to consider a function
\lambda
on

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
taking values in the set of positive integers.
If we allow
\lambda
to be a real-valued function, we can also conclude

\lambda
to be an even function under a stronger hypothesis.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and let
\lambda\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a function with finite support
X\subset[a,b]
. The function
\lambda
is called a
weighted interval design of index
T
if

(4)

\sum_{x\in X}x^{s}\,\lambda(x)=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}x^{s}\,dx

holds for all
s\in T
. The nonzero values
\lambda(x)
are called
weights
.

Similar to interval designs, we will only
consider weighted interval designs of index
T_{m}
, or
weighted interval
T_{m}
-design for brevity, on the interval
[-1,1]
.

We say that a function
\lambda:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
with finite support is

symmetric
if
\lambda(x)=\lambda(-x)
holds for all
x\in[-1,1]
.
The same terminology is used in
[
10
, Section 6]
in the context of the theory of quadrature formulas.

Lemma 3.5
.

The set of all weighted interval designs of index
T_{m}
forms a vector space
D_{m}
which contains every symmetric function
\lambda:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
with finite support.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the definition.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
m
and
n
be positive integers with
n\leq m
. Let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function with finite support
X\not\ni 0
of size
|X|=n
. Suppose that
\lambda
is a weighted interval
T_{m}
-design. Then
n
is even and
\lambda
is symmetric.

Proof.

We prove the assertion by induction on
n
.
If
n=1
, then it is clear that there is no such
weighted interval
T_{m}
-design for
m\geq 1
.
Hence, we may suppose
n\geq 2
.
Let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
,
and define
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
by

A_{i,j}=x_{j}^{2i-1}\quad(1\leq i,j\leq n).

Define
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

v_{j}=\lambda(x_{j})\quad(1\leq j\leq n).

Then for
1\leq i\leq n
,

\displaystyle(Av)_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{2i-1}\lambda(x_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{R}}x^{2i-1}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=0.

This implies

0=\det A=\prod_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{j}^{2}-x_{i}^{2}).

Thus,
there exist
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq n
such that
x_{j}=-x_{i}
.
We may assume without loss of generality
x_{n}=-x_{n-1}
.

Suppose
n=2
.
Then

x_{2}=-x_{1}
, and hence
x_{1}(\lambda(x_{1})-\lambda(x_{2}))=0

which implies

\lambda(x_{1})=\lambda(-x_{1})
. Since the support of
\lambda
is
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=\{x_{1},-x_{1}\}
,
we conclude
\lambda(x)=\lambda(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now suppose
n\geq 3
.
Define a function
\lambda^{\prime}:=\lambda-\omega
, where

\omega(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda(x_{n})&\text{if $x=x_{n}$,}\\
-\lambda(x_{n})&\text{if $x=-x_{n}$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Then
\omega
is symmetric with finite support, and the support of
\lambda^{\prime}
has size either
n-1
or
n-2
.
Since
n\leq m
, we have
\lambda\in D_{m}\subset D_{n-1}
. By Lemma
3.5
, it follows that
\omega\in D_{n-1}
, and
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda-\omega\in D_{n-1}
. Then, the inductive hypothesis implies
\lambda^{\prime}
is symmetric. Since
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}+\omega
,
\lambda
is also symmetric. In particular,
n
is even.
∎

4.
Spherical Designs

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
\langle a,b\rangle
the standard inner product.

Definition 4.1
.

For a positive integer
t
, a finite nonempty subset
X\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is called a
spherical
t
-design
if

\sum_{x\in X}\langle x,a\rangle^{k}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1\cdot 3\cdots(2k-1)\langle a,a\rangle^{\frac{k}{2}}}{(d+2)(d+4)\cdots(d+2k-2)}\,&\text{if }k\in\{2,4,\ldots,2\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\rfloor\},\\[5.69054pt]
0,&\text{if }k\in\{1,3,\ldots,2\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\rfloor+1\},\end{cases}

for all
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Among various definitions of spherical designs
(See
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
),
the above definition, which originally goes back to Hilbert’s solution of Waring problem, can be found in Lyubich and Vaserstein
[
11
]
, Reznick
[
15
]
.
Venkov
[
17
, Section 3]
also shows that the above definition is equivalent to the standard one in Delsarte, Goethals and Seidel
[
6
]
.
In fact, denote by
|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|
the surface area
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}1\,d\rho

of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
It follows, by the
O(d)
-invariance of the uniform measure
\rho
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle y,a\rangle^{2k}d\rho=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle y,\frac{a}{\|a\|}\rangle^{2k}\langle a,a\rangle^{k}d\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle a,a\rangle^{k}}{|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}y_{1}^{2k}d\rho=\frac{1\cdot 3\cdots(2k-1)}{d(d+2)\cdots(d+2k-2)}\langle a,a\rangle^{k},

which provides an interpretation of the right-hand side of the equation in
Definition
4.1
in an analytic manner.
For a brief explanation on the relation between spherical designs and Hilbert’s work, we refer the reader to Seidel
[
16
]
.

The following more general concept of spherical designs was
introduced by
[
5
]
, and in its full generality by

[
12
]
.
See also
[
7
,
9
,
19
]
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
Q_{d,t}(s)
denote the Gegenbauer polynomial of degree
t
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
(see
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
). Let
T
be a set of positive integers. A finite nonempty subset
X\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is called a spherical design of
harmonic index

T
if

\sum_{x,y\in X}Q_{d,t}(\langle x,y\rangle)=0\quad\text{for all }t\in T.

We shall only consider spherical designs of harmonic index
T_{m}
(or spherical
T_{m}
-designs), where
T_{m}
is defined in (
2
). It is clear that any pair of antipodal points forms a spherical
T_{m}
-design for every positive integer
m
. Thus, if
X
is a spherical
T_{m}
-design containing such a pair
\{a,-a\}
, then
X\setminus\{a,-a\}
is also a spherical
T_{m}
-design.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Then
X
is a spherical
T_{m}
-design if and only if
\{\langle x,a\rangle\}_{x\in X}
is an interval
T_{m}
-design for all
a\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Proof.

Considering the univariate polynomial ring
\mathbb{R}[s]
, note that the span of the monomials
s^{t}
for
t\in T_{m}
coincides with that of the Gegenbauer polynomials
Q_{d,t}(s)
(since
Q_{d,t}(s)
is odd of degree
t
when
t\in T_{m}
). This equivalence establishes the claim.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a spherical
T_{m}
-design consisting of
n
points. If
n\leq 2m
, then
X
is antipodal.

Proof.

Suppose
X
is not antipodal. Then there exists some
a\in X
such that
-a\notin X
.
The projection of
X
onto the line determined by
a
yields a multiset of
n
real numbers that is non-symmetric. By Proposition
3.2
, such a configuration forces
n>2m
, contradicting the assumption.
∎

5.
Optimality

In this section, we show that the inequalities in the assumptions of
Propositions
3.2
,
3.6
and
Theorem
4.4
cannot be weakened.
First, we show that the inequality
n\leq 2m
in
Proposition
3.2
cannot be weakened.
Recall that we denote by
p_{k}
and
e_{k}

the
k
-th power sum and
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial, respectively
with
n
variables.
For a finite set
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
with
n
elements, we write

e_{k}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=e_{k}(A)
and
p_{k}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=p_{k}(A)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
m
be a positive integer. Then for every positive integer
n
with

n>2m
, there exists an interval
T_{m}
-design
X\subset[-1,1]
with

|X|=n
and
X\neq-X
. In particular,
2m+1
is the smallest size
of a non-symmetric interval
T_{m}
-design.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case where
n=2m+1
.
Indeed, a set
X
with
|X|=2m+1
satisfying the conclusion must
satisfy the stronger property
X\cap(-X)=\emptyset
, by
Proposition
3.2
. Thus, the case

n=2m+2
follows by adding
0
to
X
. For
n>2m+2
,
we can construct a desired
T_{m}
-design by adding

\left\lfloor{(n-2m)/2}\right\rfloor
pairs of antipodal points
in
(-1,1)
to
X
with
|X|=2m+1
or
2m+2
.

Let

A=\left\{\pm\frac{2k-1}{2m}\mid 1\leq k\leq m\right\}.

Let
f(x)
be the monic polynomial of degree
2m
having

A
as its set of roots.
We can choose a small
\varepsilon>0
in such a way that
the polynomial
g(x)=f(x)+\varepsilon
has
2m
roots
in
(-1+\frac{1}{2m},1-\frac{1}{2m})\setminus\{\frac{1}{2m}\}
.
Let
A^{\prime}
be the set of roots of
g(x)
. Then, by the relation between roots and coefficients,

e_{k}(A)=e_{k}(A^{\prime})\quad(0\leq k\leq 2m-1).

By Lemma
2.1
using induction on
k
, we find

(5)

p_{k}(A)=p_{k}(A^{\prime})\quad(0\leq k\leq 2m-1).

Since
A
and
(A-\tfrac{1}{2m})\cup\{1\}
are both symmetric,

p_{\mathrm{odd}}(A)=0
, and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=p_{2k+1}((A-\frac{1}{2m})\cup\{1\})

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{a\in A}(a-\frac{1}{2m})^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k+1}\binom{2k+1}{\ell}a^{\ell}(-\frac{1}{2m})^{2k+1-\ell}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\ell=0}^{2k+1}\binom{2k+1}{\ell}(-\frac{1}{2m})^{2k+1-\ell}p_{\ell}(A)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{2k+1}{2\ell}(-\frac{1}{2m})^{2(k-\ell)+1}p_{2\ell}(A)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{2k+1}{2\ell}\frac{1}{(2m)^{2(k-\ell)+1}}p_{2\ell}(A).

Thus, by (
5
), we obtain

1-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{2k+1}{2\ell}\frac{1}{(2m)^{2(k-\ell)+1}}p_{2\ell}(A^{\prime})=0\quad(0\leq k\leq m-1).

Since
A^{\prime}
is also symmetric, we can
reverse the above calculation with
A
replaced by
A^{\prime}
, and we
conclude

p_{2k+1}((A^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2m})\cup\{1\})=0\quad(0\leq k\leq m-1).

This implies that the set

X=(A^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2m})\cup\{1\}
is an interval
T_{m}
-design
with
|X|=2m+1
. Since
1\in X
and
-1\notin X
, we have

X\neq(-X)
.

The second assertion then follows from Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Next, we show that the inequalities in the assumptions of
Proposition
3.6
cannot be weakened.
The following two propositions show that
m+1
is the smallest size of the support
of a non-symmetric weighted
T_{m}
-design.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer.
Define the function
\lambda\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\lambda(x)=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }x=\cos\Bigl(\dfrac{2j\pi}{2{n}+1}\Bigr)\text{ for }1\leq j\leq{n},\\[2.84526pt]
1,&\text{if }x=1,\\[2.84526pt]
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then,
\lambda
is a weighted interval
T_{{n}-1}
-design.

Proof.

For
1\leq l\leq n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\supp(f)}x^{2l-1}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}2\cos^{2l-1}\Bigl(\frac{2j\pi}{2n+1}\Bigr)+1

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2n}\cos^{2l-1}\Bigl(\frac{2j\pi}{2n+1}\Bigr)=0,

since the vertices of the regular
(2n+1)
-gon form a spherical
2n
-design.
∎

In this way, we always obtain a function
\lambda
with support size
n
that is a weighted interval
T_{n-1}
-design. This shows that the inequality
n\leq m
in the assumption of Proposition
3.6
cannot be relaxed.

Using a combinatorial lemma, we can also construct a non-symmetric weighted
T_{n-1}
-design on
[-1,1]
supported on
n
rational nodes with rational weights. In its proof, we
denote by
\delta_{ij}
the Kronecker’s delta symbol.

Lemma 5.3
.

For positive integers
n
and
s
with
0\leq s<n
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}j^{2s}\binom{2n}{n-j}=\begin{cases}-\binom{2n-1}{n-1}&\text{if $s=0$,}\\
0&\text{if $1\leq s<n$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove the assertion by induction on
s
.

Suppose
s=0
, then for all
n>0
we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{2n}{n-j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}\left(\binom{2n}{n-j}+\binom{2n}{n+j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{2n}(-1)^{j}\binom{2n}{j}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{2n}{n}

\displaystyle=-\binom{2n-1}{n-1}.

Now let
s\geq 1
, and assume that the assertion holds for
s-1
and for all
n>s-1
. Since

\displaystyle j^{2}\binom{2n}{n-j}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\binom{2n}{n-j}-2n(2n-1)\binom{2n-2}{n-1-j}\quad(1\leq j\leq n-1),

we have for
n>s
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}j^{2s}\binom{2n}{n-j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n^{2s}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}j^{2s}\binom{2n}{n-j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n^{2s}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}j^{2s-2}n^{2}\binom{2n}{n-j}

\displaystyle\quad-2n(2n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}j^{2s-2}\binom{2n-2}{n-1-j}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}j^{2(s-1)}\binom{2n}{n-j}-2n(2n-1)\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}j^{2(s-1)}\binom{2(n-1)}{n-1-j}.

By the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}j^{2s}\binom{2n}{n-j}

\displaystyle=-n^{2}\delta_{s,1}\binom{2n-1}{n-1}+2n(2n-1)\delta_{s,1}\binom{2n-3}{n-2}=0.

∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
n
be a positive integer.
Define
\lambda\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\lambda(x)=\begin{cases}2j\binom{2n}{n-2j}&\text{if $x=\frac{2j}{n+1}$ for $1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor$,}\\
(2j-1)\binom{2n}{n-2j+1}&\text{if $x=-\frac{2j-1}{n+1}$ for $1\leq j\leq\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
\lambda
is a weighted interval
T_{n-1}
-design.

Proof.

For
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathbb{R}}x^{2k-1}\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2k-1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}(2j)^{2k}\binom{2n}{n-2j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}(2j-1)^{2k}\binom{2n}{n-2j+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2k-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}j^{2k}\binom{2n}{n-j}

\displaystyle=0,

by Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Finally, we show that the inequality
n\leq 2m
in
Theorem
4.4
cannot be weakened.
We need some preparations.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
m
be a positive integer.
If
X
is a spherical
T_{m}
-design on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,
then
X
is also a spherical
T_{m}
-design on
\mathbb{S}^{d^{\prime}-1}

for
d^{\prime}>d
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Lemma
4.3
, since
the inner product
\langle x,a\rangle
for vectors
x,a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

remains the same after regarding them as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

If
n>2m
and
n
is odd, then
there exists a non-antipodal spherical
T_{m}
-design
with
n

In particular,
2m+1
is the smallest size of a non-antipodal

-design.

Proof.

Let
X
be a regular
(2m+1)
-gon in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Then

is a spherical
2m
-design (see
[
8
]

-design.
For

and

Adding these pairs of points to

.
∎

In the proof of Proposition

, we used the natural embedding

By

Proposition 5.7
.

There is no non-antipodal
T_{2}
-design on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
of the cardinality
6
.

Proof.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a spherical
T_{2}
-design with
|X|=6
points.
We will show that
X
is antipodal. Suppose not. Then
X\cap(-X)=\emptyset
by
Theorem
4.4
. In particular, we may assume
by rotating that
(\pm x_{1},y_{1})\in X
for some
x_{1}
with
0<x_{1}<1
,
0<y_{1}
.
The projection
\bar{X}
of
X
onto the
x
-coordinate gives a weighted interval
T_{2}
-design,
and by removing
\pm x_{1}
with multiplicity
1
, we obtain a weighted interval
T_{2}
-design
X^{\prime}
consisting of
4
points. By Proposition
3.2
, these
4
points form
a symmetric multiset
X^{\prime}
.
Suppose first that

4
points of
X^{\prime}
are not distinct. If there are only
3
distinct points, then
one of the points
is
0
with multiplicity
2
, which implies
(0,\pm 1)\in X
.
This contradicts the fact that
X\cap(-X)=\emptyset
.
If there are only two distinct points, then
we have
X^{\prime}=\{\pm x_{2}\}
, which also contradicts the fact that
X\cap(-X)=\emptyset
.
We next suppose that
X^{\prime}
consists of
4
distinct points
\{\pm x_{2},\pm x_{3}\}
.
Then we may assume

X=\{(\pm x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}),(-x_{2},\epsilon_{2}y_{2}),(x_{3},y_{3}),(-x_{3},\epsilon_{3}y_{3})\},

where
\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}\in\{\pm 1\}
,
x_{2}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}=x_{3}^{2}+y_{3}^{2}=1
,

y_{2}>y_{3}>0
.
Since
X\cap(-X)=\emptyset
, we have
\epsilon_{2}=1
and similaly
\epsilon_{3}=1
.
But then
y_{1}+y_{2}+y_{3}=0
and
y_{1}^{3}+y_{2}^{3}+y_{3}^{3}=0
.
This cannot occur, since
y_{1}y_{2}y_{3}\neq 0
.
∎

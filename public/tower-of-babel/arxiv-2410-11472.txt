Non-perturbative suppression of Chiral Effects in hot QGP for Λ / Λ ¯ \Lambda/\bar{\Lambda} -hyperons spin polarization in heavy-ion collisions
Ruslan A. Abramchuk

Abstract
We review the calculation of suppression of Chiral Separational and Chiral Vortical Effects for strange quarks (which allegedly yield spin polarization of Λ / Λ ¯ \Lambda/\bar{\Lambda} -hyperons in peripheral Heavy-Ion Collisions) by the non-perturbative interactions in hot deconfined QCD with the Field Correlator Method. The parameter range in the temperature-baryon density plane is expected to cover LHC-ALICE and RHIC-STAR data.

Introduction

Extreme conditions,
that emerge in modern collider experiments on ultra-relativistic collisions of heavy ions,
are unattainable in any other process in the Universe
(except may be for the conditions in the very early universe).
The emerging matter — Quark Gluon Plasma (QGP), thermalizes
and reaches extremely high temperature,
but behaves like an ideal fluid, rather than like an ideal gas

With the unprecedentedly small viscosity to entropy density ratio

QGP is also probably the most opaque matter known,
since the microscopical quantity of the matter absorbs high energy jets,

In non-central collisions, the huge (per nucleon) mutual angular momentum of the ions
transforms into extremely high vorticity

and the electric currents produce extremely high magnetic fields

In QGP, the strong interactions dominate,

2
FCM quark propagator in hot QGP

The key point of the Field Correlator Method
(see
[
28
]
and references therein)
is decomposition of the gluonic field
A_{\mu}
in the background
B_{\mu}
and perturbative
a_{\mu}
parts

\displaystyle A_{\mu}=B_{\mu}+a_{\mu},\quad\mathcal{D}A=\mathcal{D}B\mathcal{D}a.\,

The perturbative part is defined according to the well-established Background Perturbation Theory.
The background part
(in the Matsubara representation,
(\vec{x},\tau)\in\mathbb{R}_{3}\times S_{1}
, with the
O(4)
Euclidean symmetry explicitly broken)
is defined via five gauge invariant correlators
D^{E,H},\,D_{1}^{E,H,EH}
.
E.g., the spatial component of the background field strength tensors
(
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}B_{\mu}+[B_{\mu},B_{\nu}]
)
correlate as

\displaystyle N_{c}^{-1}\operatorname{tr}_{c}\braket{F_{ij}(x)\Phi_{xy}F_{kl}(y)\Phi_{yx}}_{B}

\displaystyle=(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})D^{H}+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}

\displaystyle((((y-x)_{k}\delta_{jl}-(y-x)_{l}\delta_{jk})+\text{perm})D_{1}^{H}).

(4)

The gauge invariant correlators are to be defined from self-consistency conditions.
The background field
B_{\mu}
is conjectured to be stochastic,
which agrees with lattice data
(and was proven to be self-consistent at zero temperature and baryon density).
Lattice data suggest
[
47
,
48
]
that the correlators are short-range (in the Euclidean space),
with the correlation length weakly dependent on temperature

D(x,y)\sim e^{-|y-x|/\lambda},\,\lambda\sim 1\text{ GeV}^{-1}\sim 0.2
fm.
The deconfinement corresponds to vanishing of the scalar Color-Electric correlator (
D^{E}\to 0
),
which occurs at
T_{c}\sim 160
MeV at
\mu_{q}=0
.

The method also relies on an expansion,
but instead of the perturbative expansion in powers of
\alpha_{s}
,
the non-perturbative cluster expansion in powers of a
small parameter
\sigma\lambda^{2}\sim 1/5
,
which is defined eventually by the gauge group algebraic structure
(
\sigma
is the confining string tension at zero temperature).
E.g., for a stochastic zero-mean variable
\xi(t)
the cluster decomposition
in Gaussian approximation reads

\braket{\exp(-i\int\xi(t)dt)}=\exp\Big(\sum_{n}\frac{(-i)^{n}}{n!}\int dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n}\braket{\braket{\xi(t_{1})\ldots\xi(t_{n})}}\Big)\approx\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\iint dt_{1}dt_{2}\braket{\braket{\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})}}\Big).

The quark propagator in gluonic background reads in the world-line representation

\displaystyle S(x,y)=

\displaystyle(-\not{D}(A,\mu)+m)_{x}\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds~\zeta(s)~(\overline{\mathcal{D}^{4}z})_{xy}^{s}e^{-m^{2}s-K}\times

\displaystyle\times P\exp\Big(ig\oint A\cdot dz+\mu\int dz_{4}+\int_{0}^{s}d\tau\sigma^{\rho\sigma}gF_{\rho\sigma}[A](z,z_{0})\Big).

(5)

The path integral
(\overline{\mathcal{D}^{4}z})_{xy}^{s}
describes the fermion motion from point
x
to
y
in world-line (proper) time
s
.
The bar indicates the anti-periodic boundary conditions for the fermion
as the trajectory wraps
n
times around the temporal direction
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{1}
with the compactified temporal direction
of length
\beta=T^{-1}
.

In a gauge invariant operator,
which constitutes any observable in QCD
including axial current density,
the Schwinger phase factors necessarily add up to closed Wilson loops
W[{\cal C}]
.
Then the average over the background gluonic field
B
is to be calculated.

Following
[
27
]
and references therein,
we neglect correlation between the (
n

L^{(n)}
,
which is a temporal projection,
and the spatial projection
W_{3}[{\cal C}]
of the Wilson loop

\displaystyle\braket{W[{\cal C}]}_{B}=\braket{\exp\left(ig\oint_{\cal C}{\cal B}_{\mu}dz^{\mu}\right)}_{B}\approx\braket{W_{3}[{\cal C}]}_{B}L^{(n)}.

(6)

The algebraic part of the factorization problem may be resolved with the Diakonov-Petrov approach
[
49
]
.

The Polyakov line
is approximately parametrized with the potential
V_{1}
as

L^{(n)}\approx L^{|n|}\approx\exp\left(-\frac{nV_{1}(T)}{2T}\right)
.

V_{1}(T)=V_{1}(r\to+\infty,T)
is the singlet free energy of quark-antiquark white state,
is apparently produced by the vector Color-Electric correlator
D_{1}^{E}
.
A fit
[
31

of Lattice QCD data for

The spatial projection

of the Wilson loop

obeys the area law due to the Color-Magnetic Confinement (CMC)

36

which generates the Debye (thermal screening) mass of the quark,

\displaystyle\braket{W_{3}[{\cal C}]}_{B}\approx\exp(-\sigma_{H}S_{3}[{\cal C}_{l}]),\quad\sigma_{H,f}(T)\approx c_{\sigma}^{2}g^{4}(T,\mu)T^{2},\quad

\displaystyle M=\sqrt{m^{2}+(c_{D}^{2}/4-2/\pi)\sigma_{H,f}(T)},\quad

(7)

Noteworthy that the spin-CMC interaction

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{2}{\pi}\sigma_{H,f}(T).

4
Chiral Vortical Effect in hot QGP

As a model of a relatively small QGP grain
that emerges in HIC
in which the strangeness density fluctuation is approximately uniform,
we consider rigidly rotating QCD in the deconfined phase.
Such a model is consistent with the HIC simulations,
where graining (discretization in space and time) is usually larger than 1 fm(/c).
Since the mean free path
l<T^{-1}
of a quark
[
26
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
in QGP is few times smaller
than the typical size of the vortical structure
[
25

56
,
19
]
,
the rigid rotation may be a satisfactory model for the QGP motion
— the angular velocity corresponds to the vorticity at any given point inside the fireball.

In ongoing experiments, the vorticity of QGP is at most
\omega\sim 10^{22}\text{s}^{-1}\sim 10
MeV
[
4
]
,
which is a relatively small scale
(the QCD vacuum correlation length is
[
28
]

\lambda\sim 0.2\text{ fm}\sim 1\text{ GeV}^{-1}
),
so we expect the dependence of the field correlators on the angular velocity to be negligible.
The ‘kinematic’ effect of the gluonic content rotation is non-perturbatively suppressed,
as was investigated in the Appendix of
[
27
]
.

The dynamical effect of rotation on the gluonic content of QGP is an intriguing problem on its own.
Rigidly rotating Gluon Plasma was recently reported
[
57
]

to be unstable at temperatures
T<1.5T_{c}

where

is the critical temperature of the Gluon Plasma.

The axial current from the quark propagator (
5

[

\displaystyle\braket{j^{5}_{\mu}(x_{\bot}=0)}\approx

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{tr}_{c,D}\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\mu}(-\not{D}(A,\mu)+m)_{x}\int_{0}^{+\infty}ds~\zeta(s)~(\overline{\mathcal{D}^{4}z})_{xx}^{s}e^{-m^{2}s-K}\times

6
Conclusions

We reviewed the calculation of the leading non-perturbative contributions to CVE and CSE for strange quarks in QGP at temperatures above the deconfinement phase transition.
To deal with the strong interactions of QCD,
which are dominant even in the deconfined phase,
we used the Field Correlator Method.

For the experimentally accessible at LHC and RHIC range of temperatures and densities,
the Chiral Effects are suppressed by non-perturbative interactions
(in comparison to the effects for free fermions),
— by the Color-Magnetic Confinement,
which produces a Debye-like screening mass,
and by the remnants of the Color-Electric interaction,
which are encoded in the Polyakov line potential.

The obtained suppression for CVE (
15
) is in a qualitative agreement
with the phenomenological suppression (the effect of the factor
k
in (
3
)) introduced in the quark-based HIC simulations
[
24
]
to match the RHIC-STAR data on the
\Lambda/\bar{\Lambda}
-hyperons spin polarization.
And for CSE (
11
) —
in a qualitative agreement (Fig.
3
) with the lattice QCD simulations
[
42
,
43
]

(a similar result for CSE with FCM in strong magnetic field limit was also obtained in
[
45
]
).
Incorporation of our results into the quark-based numerical simulations of HIC would provide an ultimate check.

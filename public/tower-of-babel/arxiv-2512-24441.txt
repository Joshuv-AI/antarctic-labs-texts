Uniform Continuity in Distribution for Borel Transformations of Random Fields
Alexander I. Bufetov

Abstract
Simple sufficient conditions are given that ensure the uniform continuity in distribution for Borel transformations of random fields.

1
Introduction and the statement of the main result

Let
W
be a complete separable metric space, let
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
be the space of
Borel probability measures on
W
endowed with the weak topology and let
{\mathfrak{N}}
be a compact subset of the space
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
. Assume that
V\subset W
is a Borel
subset satisfying
\eta(V)=1
for any measure
\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}
.

A Borel mapping

g\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\times V\to W

(1)

induces a Borel mapping

g_{*}\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\to{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)

(2)

by the formula

g_{*}\eta=(g(\eta,\cdot))_{*}\eta.

(3)

The map
g_{*}
is equivalently given by the formula

\int_{W}\varphi\,dg_{*}\eta=\int_{W}\varphi(g(\eta,w))\,d\eta(w)

(4)

that holds for any bounded continuous function
\varphi\colon W\to\mathbb{C}
.

The aim of this note is to give convenient sufficient conditions ensuring that the correspondence

g\mapsto g_{*}
induces a uniformly continuous map. Of particular interest to us is the case where the space
W

is the space of realizations of a random field; under additional assumptions on the
regularity of the random field, the space
W
is a complete separable metric space (see Section
4
below).

The space
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
can be turned into a metric space in several different natural ways. For our purposes the Lévy–Prokhorov metric is particularly convenient, and we recall that the weak topology on
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
is induced by the Lévy–Prokhorov
metric
d_{\mathrm{LP}}
, which is uniquely defined by the following requirement (cf. Bogachev
[
5
]
, Billingsley
[
3
]
): the
Lévy–Prokhorov distance between measures
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
does not exceed
\varepsilon
if
and only if for every closed set
K\subset W
and its
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood

K_{\varepsilon}=\{w\in W:d(w,K)\leq\varepsilon\}

we have

\eta_{1}(K)\leq\eta_{2}(K_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon,\qquad\eta_{2}(K)\leq\eta_{1}(K_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon.

We endow the space of Borel mappings (
1
) with the metric of uniform convergence in probability, and
the space of Borel mappings from
{\mathfrak{N}}
to
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
with the Tchebycheff uniform metric. We fix a compact set
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
and only consider maps
g
of the form (
1
) satisfying the additional requirement

g_{*}({\mathfrak{N}})\subset\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
. The correspondence
g\mapsto g_{*}
is then uniformly continuous with respect to our metrics. We
proceed to the precise formulations.

We fix a metric
d
on the space
W
, and we write
d_{\mathrm{LP}}
for the corresponding Lévy–Prokhorov metric on the space
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
. For a compact subset
{\mathfrak{N}}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
and a Borel subset
V\subset W
satisfying

\eta(V)=1
for any
\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}
, introduce the space
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)
of Borel maps

g\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\times V\to W.

We endow the space
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)
with the topology
{\mathfrak{T}}_{\mathrm{prob}}
generated by the neighbourhoods

U(g_{0},\varepsilon,\delta)=\left\{g\in\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)\,:\,\eta(\{w\in W:d(g(\eta,w),g_{0}(\eta,w))>\varepsilon\})<\delta\text{ for all }\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}\right\}

for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
. The collection of the sets
U(g_{0},\varepsilon,\delta)
over all
\varepsilon>0,\delta>0
forms a subbasis for the topology
{\mathfrak{T}}_{\mathrm{prob}}
. The topology
{\mathfrak{T}}_{\mathrm{prob}}
on the space
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)
is metrizable: one directly verifies
that the distance

d(g_{1},g_{2})=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}2^{-l}\frac{\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\eta(\{w\in W:d(g_{1}(\eta,w),g_{2}(\eta,w))>2^{-l}\})}{1+\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\eta(\{w\in W:d(g_{1}(\eta,w),g_{2}(\eta,w))>2^{-l}\})}

induces the topology
{\mathfrak{T}}_{\mathrm{prob}}
on
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)
.

Now let
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}},{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W))
stand for the space of all Borel maps
h\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\to{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
. We endow the space
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}},{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W))
with the
Tchebycheff uniform metric with respect to the Lévy–Prokhorov metric on the space
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
. For a compact set

\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
let
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
be the subspace of Borel maps

h\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\to{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
satisfying the inclusion
h({\mathfrak{N}})\subset\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
. We note that the subspace
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})

is closed in the Tchebycheff uniform topology.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
be a compact set. Let
\mathscr{B}_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)\subset\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)
be the
subspace of maps
g
such that the map
g_{*}
defined by (
3
)
satisfies the inclusion
g_{*}({\mathfrak{N}})\subset\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
. The subspace
\mathscr{B}_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)\subset\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)

is closed by definition.
The main result of this paper is

Theorem 1.1
.

The correspondence
g\mapsto g_{*}
induces a uniformly continuous map from the space

\mathscr{B}_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,W)
to the space
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
.

2
Reduction to the case
W=\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}

Endow the space

\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}=\{t=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots):t_{n}\in\mathbb{C},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}

with the distance

d(t^{(1)},t^{(2)})=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}2^{-l}\frac{|t^{(1)}_{l}-t^{(2)}_{l}|}{1+|t^{(1)}_{l}-t^{(2)}_{l}|}.

The Stone–Weierstrass Theorem directly implies the following two propositions.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
W
be a complete separable metric space and let
K
be a compact subset of
W
.
There exists a countable family of
1
-Lipschitz functions
\Phi=(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
K

such that the correspondence

w\mapsto(\varphi_{n}(w))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

defines a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism of
K
onto its image.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
V
and
W
be complete separable metric spaces. Let
V^{\prime}\subset V
and
{\mathfrak{N}}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(V)

be Borel subsets such that
\eta(V^{\prime})=1
for any
\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}
. Let
\tau\colon V^{\prime}\to W
be a Borel map.
Assume that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
R\geq 1
and a Borel subset
V(\varepsilon)\subset V^{\prime}
such that

\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\eta(V^{\prime}\setminus V(\varepsilon))<\varepsilon,

and for any
x,y\in V(\varepsilon),x\neq y
we have

R^{-1}\leq\frac{d_{W}(\tau x,\tau y)}{d_{V}(x,y)}\leq R.

Then the induced map
\tau_{*}\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\to{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(W)
is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
imply that it suffices to establish Theorem
1.1

for
W=\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

We now assume that
{\mathfrak{N}}
and
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
are compact subsets of the space
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})

of Borel probability measures on the space

\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}=\{t=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots):t_{n}\in\mathbb{C},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

For a Borel subset
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
\eta(V)=1
for every
\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}
,
denote by
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
the corresponding space of Borel maps

g\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\times V\to\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}},\qquad g(\eta,t)=(g^{(n)}(\eta,t))_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

where
g^{(n)}(\eta,t)\in\mathbb{C}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

The topology on
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
is induced by the distance function

d(g,\widehat{g})=\sum_{l,k=1}^{\infty}2^{-l-k}\frac{\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\eta(\{t\in V:|g^{(k)}(\eta,t)-\widehat{g}^{(k)}(\eta,t)|>2^{-l}\})}{1+\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\eta(\{t\in V:|g^{(k)}(\eta,t)-\widehat{g}^{(k)}(\eta,t)|>2^{-l}\})}.

By definition, a sequence
g^{(n)}\in\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
converges to
g
if and only if
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\eta(\{t\in V:|g^{(n)}_{k}(\eta,t)-g^{\phantom{(n)}}_{k}(\eta,t)|>\varepsilon\})=0.

In other words, the distance
d
induces the topology of coordinatewise convergence in probability, uniform in
\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}
.

As above, a Borel mapping
g\in\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
(cf. (
4
)) induces a map

g_{*}\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\to{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})

given by the formula

g_{*}\eta=(g(\eta,\cdot))_{*}\eta.

Recall that the space
\mathscr{B}_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
is by definition the subspace of

\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
consisting of those maps
g
whose corresponding induced map
g_{*}
satisfies the inclusion
g_{*}({\mathfrak{N}})\subset\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
.

As before, we endow the space
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
with the Tchebycheff uniform metric with respect to the
Lévy–Prokhorov metric on
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
. The subspace
\mathscr{B}_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})

is closed in
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
. The special case of Theorem
1.1
in our particular setting is the following

Theorem 2.3
.

The correspondence
g\mapsto g_{*}
defines a uniformly continuous map from
\mathscr{B}_{\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
to
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
.

As we have seen, Theorem
2.3
directly implies Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let Borel maps
g^{(n)}\in\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be such that the map
(g^{(n)})_{*}
is
continuous for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g^{(n)}\to g
in
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
,
then
g_{*}
is uniformly continuous.

Let a subset
S_{0}\subset\mathbb{N}
and a compact subset
{\mathfrak{N}}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
satisfy
the following condition: for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\delta>0,\varepsilon>0
there exists

s_{0}\in S_{0}
such that

\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}}\eta(\{t\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}:|t(n)-t(s_{0})|>\delta\})<\varepsilon.

(5)

The above argument implies the following

Proposition 2.5
.

Under condition (
5
), the projection
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{C}^{S_{0}}

Proof of Theorem
2.3

Let
{\mathfrak{N}}
and
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
be compact subsets of the space
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
.
Endow the space
C({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
of continuous maps from
{\mathfrak{N}}
to
\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
with the Tchebycheff metric:
for
h,h^{\prime}\in C({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
the Tchebycheff uniform distance
d(h,h^{\prime})
is defined by the formula

d(h,h^{\prime})=\max_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}}d_{\mathrm{LP}}\left(h(\eta),h^{\prime}(\eta)\right).

The space
C({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
is a complete separable metric space.

To a map
h\in C({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
we assign its projection onto the
first
l
coordinates

\pi_{l}h(\kappa)=\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{h(\kappa)}([1,l]),

where the symbol
\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{\eta}
stands for the
l
-dimensional marginal distribution of a measure
\eta
on the first
l
coordinates.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a complete separable metric space, and let
F\colon M\to C({\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}})
be a Borel map. If for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
the
finite-dimensional projection mapping

m\mapsto\pi_{l}F(m)

is uniformly continuous with respect to the Lévy–Prokhorov metric, then the map
F
is also uniformly continuous with respect to the Lévy–Prokhorov metric.

Proof.

A simple compactness argument connects the distance between measures with
the distances between their finite-dimensional distributions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
be a compact set. For every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
l\in\mathbb{N}

and
\delta>0
, depending only on
K
, such that
if
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in K
satisfy the inequality

d_{\mathrm{LP}}(\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{\eta_{1}}([1,l]),\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{\eta_{2}}([1,l]))\leq\delta,

then we have

d_{\mathrm{LP}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\leq\varepsilon.

Lemma
3.2
follows from the Prokhorov Theorem and directly implies Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Let
\pi_{l}
denote the projection
\pi_{l}\colon\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{C}^{l}
onto the first
l
coordinates.
Slightly abusing notation, we use the same symbol for the corresponding projection

\pi_{l}\colon{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})\to{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{l})
.

Proposition
3.1
implies the following simple

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak{N}},\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
be compact sets and let
h_{n}\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\to\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
,

h\colon{\mathfrak{N}}\to\widetilde{{\mathfrak{N}}}
be Borel maps. The sequence
h_{n}
converges to
h
uniformly
if and only if for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
the sequence
\pi_{l}h_{n}
converges uniformly to
\pi_{l}h

as
n\to\infty
.

We now conclude the proof of Theorem
2.3
. As before, let
{\mathfrak{N}}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})

be a compact subset, and let
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
be a Borel subset satisfying
\eta(V)=1
for every
\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}
.
Next, let a sequence of Borel mappings
g_{n}\in\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
converge, under the topology
{\mathfrak{T}}_{\mathrm{prob}}
, to the limit
g\in\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{N}}\times V,\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
.
But then, by definition, for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
the uniform convergence

\pi_{l}(g_{n})_{*}\to\pi_{l}g_{*}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

holds in the space
\mathscr{B}(\pi_{l}({\mathfrak{N}}),{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{l}))
, endowed with the Tchebycheff metric. Corollary
3.3
now implies Theorem
2.3
.
Theorem
2.3
is proved completely. ∎

4
A sufficient condition for separability of the space of realizations

Let
S
be an index set; here we mainly need the case when S is a complete separable metric space. Let
\ell_{1}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{+}
be positive continuous functions on
S
and let
\ell_{2}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}_{2}^{+}

be nonnegative continuous functions on
S\times S
such that
\ell_{2}^{+}(s,s)=\widetilde{\ell}_{2}^{+}(s,s)=0

for all
s\in S
. To the pair of functions
\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+}

we assign the family
{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+})
of measures satisfying the following assumptions:

1.

For all
s\in S
we have
t(s)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{S},\eta)
and
\|t(s)\|_{L^{2}(C^{S},\eta)}\leq\ell_{1}^{+}(s)
;

2.

For all
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
we have
\|t(s_{1})-t(s_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{S},\eta)}\leq\ell_{2}^{+}(s_{1},s_{2})
.

Let
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{S}
be a Borel subset such that
\eta(V)=1
for all
\eta\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+})
. Denote

\mathscr{B}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+};\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}_{2}^{+})=\mathscr{B}_{{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}_{2}^{+})}({\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+})\times V,{\mathbb{C}}^{S}).

Let
S_{0}=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots\}\subset S
be a countable subset. We endow the space
\mathscr{B}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+};\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}_{2}^{+})

with a slightly stronger topology
{\mathfrak{T}}_{2}
induced by the distance

d_{2}(g,\widehat{g})=\sum_{s_{\ell}\in S_{0}}2^{-\ell}\frac{\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+})}\|g(\eta,t)(s_{\ell})-\widehat{g}(\eta,t)(s_{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{S},\eta)}}{1+\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+})}\|g(\eta,t)(s_{\ell})-\widehat{g}(\eta,t)(s_{\ell})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{S},\eta)}}.

Theorem
2.3
yields the following

Corollary 4.1
.

The correspondence
g\mapsto g_{*}
induces a uniformly continuous map from the metric space

\mathscr{B}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+};\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}_{2}^{+})
to the space
\mathscr{B}({\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+}),{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\widetilde{\ell}_{1}^{+},\widetilde{\ell}_{2}^{+}))
endowed with the Tchebycheff uniform metric.

From now on we let
S
be a complete separable metric space. Endow the space

{\mathbb{C}}^{S}=\{t:S\to\mathbb{C}\}

of
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
S
with the Tychonoff product topology.

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{S})
be the space of Borel probability measures on

\mathbb{C}^{S}
endowed with the weak topology.
Given distinct points
s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\in S
the corresponding finite-dimensional distribution of a measure
\eta\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(C^{S})
is by definition the image of
\eta
under the projection

t\mapsto(t(s_{1}),\ldots,t(s_{l})).

The symbol
\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{\eta}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})
stands for the
l
-dimensional distribution
of
\eta
corresponding to the distinct points
s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\in S
.
We start with a simple observation.

Proposition 4.2
.

A sequence of measures
\eta_{n}\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{S})
converges weakly to a limit measure
\eta\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{S})
if and only if
for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
and every finite collection of distinct coordinates
s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\in S
the corresponding
finite-dimensional distributions converge weakly as
n\to\infty
, that is, if we have

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{\eta_{n}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})=\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{\eta}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}).

Proof.

The Bockstein Theorem
[
4
]
states that any bounded continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{S}

only depends on a countable subcollection of coordinates:

f(t(s))=f(t(s_{1}),t(s_{2}),\ldots,t(s_{\ell}),\ldots).

It is therefore sufficient to establish the proposition for
S=\mathbb{N}
.

We now need to show that a subset
{\mathfrak{N}}_{0}\subset{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}})
is precompact if for every
l\in\mathbb{N}

the set

{\mathfrak{N}}_{0,l}=\{\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{\eta}(1,\ldots,l):\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{0}\}

of the finite-dimensional projections of
{\mathfrak{N}}_{0}
on the first
l
coordinates is precompact.

Indeed, the Prokhorov Theorem implies that for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a compact set
K_{l}\subset\mathbb{C}^{l}

such that for every measure
\eta\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{0}
we have

\operatorname{distr}^{l}_{\eta}(1,\ldots,l)(K_{l})\geq 1-\varepsilon/2^{l}.

The compact set
K_{l}
is bounded in the
l
-th coordinate:

K_{l}\subset\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{l}):|t_{l}|\leq R_{l}\}

for some
R_{l}>0
. We then have

\eta(\{t:|t(l)|>R_{l}\})<\varepsilon/2^{l}.

The compact set

K=\{t:|t(1)|\leq R_{1},\ldots,|t(l)|\leq R_{l},\ldots\}

then satisfies the bound

\eta(K)\geq 1-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\eta(\{t:|t(l)|>R_{l}\})\geq 1-\varepsilon.

and the set
{\mathfrak{N}}_{0}
is precompact.
∎

By definition, the subset
{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+})
is closed in
C^{S}
with respect to the weak topology. Let
S_{0}\subset S
be a countable dense subset.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
S_{0}\subset S
be countable and dense. The projection map
\mathbb{C}^{S}\to\mathbb{C}^{S_{0}}
induces a homeomorphism
of the space
{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(\ell_{1}^{+},\ell_{2}^{+})
onto its image.

Proof.

The projection mapping
\mathbb{C}^{S}\to\mathbb{C}^{S_{0}}
is continuous by definition. Next, one directly verifies that our natural projection is injective in restriction to the subset
{\mathfrak{M}}_{2}(l_{1}^{+},l_{2}^{+})
. It remains to show that our natural projection map is open.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{S}\to\mathbb{C}
be continuous and bounded.
Introduce the neighbourhood
U(\varepsilon,f,\eta_{0})
of a measure
\eta_{0}\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{S})
by the formula

U(\varepsilon,f,\eta_{0})=\left\{\eta\in{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(C^{S}):\left|\int f\,d\eta-\int f\,d\eta_{0}\right|<\varepsilon\right\}.

The family of neighbourhoods
U(\varepsilon,f,\eta_{0})
, taken over all
\varepsilon>0
and all
bounded continuous functions
f\colon C^{S}\to\mathbb{C}
, forms a subbasis of the weak topology on
{\mathfrak{M}}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{S})
.
In order to establish Proposition
4.3
it suffices therefore to prove that the image of any neighbourhood of the form
U(\varepsilon,f,\eta_{0})

is open under the natural projection mapping
\mathbb{C}^{S}\to\mathbb{C}^{S_{0}}
. By the Bockstein Theorem
[
4
]
the function
f
depends only
on a countable subcollection of coordinates. Therefore, Proposition
4.3
for general
S
follows from the particular case of
countable
S
— which is proved in Proposition
2.5
. Proposition
4.3
is proved completely.
∎

An important particular case of Theorem
1.1
arises when the compact set
{\mathfrak{N}}
is defined by bounds on the exponential moments of realizations of a stochastic process and the mapping
g
is the normalized exponential: that is, we take
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
or, more generally,
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, let

(X_{s})_{s\in S}
be a stochastic process on our space
S
, and consider the correspondence

g\colon X_{s}\mapsto\frac{\exp(\gamma X_{s})}{\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\exp(\gamma X_{s})}.

There is an extensive literature devoted to Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos, both real and complex; see e.g.
[
2

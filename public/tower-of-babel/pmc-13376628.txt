Multi-objective scheduling optimization for linear megaprojects considering technological innovation
Authors: Xuan Zhao, Rui Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTechnological innovation plays a critical role in driving the progress of linear megaprojects, despite being characterized by uncertainty and longtime spans between demand generation and application. To effectively incorporate the expectations of technological innovation time (ET) into construction management, a multi-objective scheduling optimization model is proposed. This model treats duration, cost, and robustness as multi-objectives, using the Linear Scheduling Method (LSM), which is more suitable for linear projects. It incorporates ET as scattered buffers, predicted by the Graphic Evaluation and Review Technique (GERT) network planning method. An improved Nondominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm III (I-NSGA3) heuristic algorithm is employed to obtain the solution. Furthermore, the advantages of the model are examined through algorithm comparison, and sensitivity analysis is conducted to investigate the impact of ET on multiple objectives, as well as to determine the ET threshold for ensuring an optimal scheduling plan. Finally, case studies are presented to illustrate the applicability and effectiveness of the proposed model. The study concludes that when the expected time for technological innovation is less than one-eighth of the duration limit, an optimal schedule that balances cost, time, and robustness can be achieved. However, when the expected time exceeds half of the duration limit, significant schedule delays occur with minimal cost changes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-49369-3.Keywords: Linear megaprojects, Technology innovation, Linear scheduling method, Multi-objective optimization, Genetic simulated annealing algorithmSubject terms: Civil engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Linear projects are a category of repetitive construction projects, also known as transportation-type linear projects. These types of projects involve not only linear activities that repeat between units, but also discrete bar and block construction (Hassanein 2002). Major projects are characterized by their large scale and complexity, significant investments, long development and construction cycles, involvement of multiple stakeholders, and substantial impacts on society, the economy, daily life, and the environment1. A linear megaproject encompasses extensive horizontal construction undertakings, such as railways, highways, pipelines, and transmission lines, which demand substantial investments and prolonged implementation periods, alongside intricate construction environments2. Traditional engineering projects can be effectively realized through the utilization of mature construction techniques. However, linear megaprojects, due to the high risks inherent in their scale, create conditions that drive the demand for technological innovation. For instance, railways with a high bridge-to-tunnel ratio and surrounded by geological hazards3, require the introduction of technological innovation to enhance construction quality, address technical challenges, adapt to the complexities of the construction environment, and ensure seamless engineering implementation. Technological innovation is an essential element of linear megaprojects and an inherent demand of engineering activities4; HQ5. The technological innovation of linear megaprojects is marked by complexity and dynamic stages6, with a significant time span and uncertainty between demand identification and successful application, further exacerbating the unpredictability of the innovation process7; HY8. Determining the appropriate management mechanism to align technological innovation with construction progress, and effectively promote engineering development, has become a critical issue in implementing the innovation-driven development strategy for major projects. However, a critical gap persists in current research. Existing scheduling optimization models for linear projects largely overlook the intricate interplay with technological innovation. Specifically, they fail to incorporate the uncertain time span (ET) from innovation demand identification to successful application. This omission leads to two major shortcomings in practice: (1) construction schedules are not proactively aligned with the innovation development process, increasing the risk of delays; and (2) the investment and resource allocation across innovation stages cannot be quantitatively evaluated against overall project objectives like duration and cost. Consequently, there is a pressing need for a dedicated optimization framework that seamlessly integrates the uncertain timeline of technological innovation into the scheduling of linear megaprojects.

A project needs be both cost-controlled and completed as early as possible to maximize its return on investment. Simultaneously, project stability is enhanced by inserting a time buffer before the completion of technological innovation. As a result, the robust value objective is balanced with the duration and cost objectives in the optimization process. This paper establishes a multi-mode scheduling optimization model for linear projects that incorporates the expected time of technological innovation. To bridge these gaps, this study makes the following key contributions: 1) Demand-Inclusive Innovation Modeling: We extend the GERT network model by explicitly incorporating the demand identification stage, providing a more complete measurement of the expected innovation time (ET) for linear megaprojects. 2) Schedule-Innovation Integration Framework: We propose a novel method to integrate the uncertain ET into the multi-objective scheduling model via scattered buffers, enabling quantitative assessment of innovation’s impact on project timelines and robustness. 3) Comprehensive Scheduling Model: We develop an LSM-based optimization model that accommodates coexisting linear, bar, and block activities with variable directions and rates, enhancing practical applicability. 4) Decision Support Threshold: We devise a method to determine the critical threshold (ETthre*), offering managers a clear criterion to evaluate if the expected innovation time is acceptable or requires intervention. 5) Effective Solution Algorithm: We propose an Improved NSGA-III (I-NSGA3) algorithm, incorporating adaptive strategies, which demonstrates superior efficiency and solution quality in solving the complex model.

Literature review

Measurement of technological innovation using GERT-based methods

Technological innovation can be conceptualized as a chain network structure, encompassing stages such as technology development, technology matching, and technology transfer9. GERT (Graphical Evaluation and Review Technique), a stochastic network model for measuring uncertainty, is widely recognized for its ability to model such processes10. Some researchers have measured the expectation time (ET) of technological innovation across different stages using GERT11; BH12; DF13. For instance, Tao et al.14, proposed a novel time–cost trade-off model in GERT networks, while Pregina et al.15, provided a comprehensive implication for real-time construction projects. Previous studies have utilized GERT to estimate the duration and success probability of R&D phases. However, most existing models simplify the innovation process into a single execution stage, often overlooking the “demand identification” and “pre-research” phases, which are critical for linear megaprojects. However, most existing models simplify the innovation process into a single execution stage, often overlooking the “demand identification” phases, which are critical for linear megaprojects. In this context, technological innovation must be identified in alignment with the ever-changing environment, marking demand identification as the essential initial stage. Furthermore, there is a significant time span and inherent uncertainty between innovation demand generation and successful application (HY)7, 8. Current research often treats innovation as an isolated event rather than a continuous variable influencing the project’s critical path. Consequently, there is a lack of integration between these ET predictions and the subsequent spatial-temporal scheduling. Mohamed16 measures the disturbance in a specific environment through a model. Then, the increased activity duration was calculated to improve the robustness of the scheduling plan. Through reasonable buffer settings, the stability can be achieved while ensuring the completion of the project17. While these models address disturbances and buffer settings to improve robustness, the process of integrating technological innovation with construction management remains a formidable challenge. Linear scheduling optimization and algorithms

The Linear Scheduling Method (LSM) has been proven to be a valuable method for optimizing scheduling problems18. Traditional research in this field focused on optimizing construction rates and logic links to minimize duration. Biruk et al.19, investigated the coexistence of linear and bar activities and allowed for the selection of activity direction. Building upon Biruk’s research, Liu et al.20, introduced a scenario with a variable construction rate. Different construction scenarios have different constraints. When arranging the scheduling plan, it is imperative to account for the time constraints associated with each construction scenario, taking into full consideration the unique attributes of linear projects. With the increasing complexity of megaprojects, integrating advanced information systems has become a new trend. Essam et al.21 emphasized that combining Building Information Modelling (BIM) with optimization tools is essential for providing effective and robust construction processes, especially when dealing with interdependent activities. Recent studies have also explored automated scheduling to reduce reliance on empirical judgment; for example, Shen and Wu22, formulated an optimization model for component-level scheduling, realizing automatic extraction of precedence relationships from BIM models. However, while these methods enhance efficiency for prefabricated or regional structures, a research gap remains in extending these benefits to macro-scale linear projects where technological innovation introduces non-continuous temporal buffers. Although LSM effectively manages spatial constraints, existing studies often assume stable production rates and lack quantitative models to dynamically couple “technological discontinuity” with spatial-temporal constraints.

Multi-objective optimization in construction projects

Construction scheduling is inherently a multi-objective problem, requiring a trade-off between duration, cost, and quality. Due to the NP-hard nature of these problems, population-based evolutionary algorithms are commonly employed. Podolski et al.23, optimized schedules using a proprietary simulated annealing-based algorithm, while other scholars have proposed multi-objective genetic algorithms (MOGA)24, NSGA-II25, and MOPSO26, hybrid algorithms27. Megaprojects often face uncertainties regarding resource availability and the stochastic nature of activity durations. Therefore, examining their impact on scheduling robustness is crucial. The efficiency of algorithms is critical when optimizing multiple objectives. Yu28, demonstrated that NSGA-III outperforms classic algorithms, indicating superior diversity and global search capability in intelligent construction scheduling. Furthermore, innovative metaheuristics like the Multi-Objective Animated Oat Optimization (MOAOO) algorithm have been developed to address resource-constrained organization designs. Despite these advancements, the application of MOEAs in linear megaprojects—specifically those considering the uncertainty of technological innovation—is still in its infancy Xue et al.29. Most models do not account for the “scattered buffers” generated by innovation time-spans (ET), which is the primary focus of this study.

In summary, several gaps in the existing literature were identified. First, while GERT is commonly used to measure technological innovation across various stages, it fails to account for the time required for the identification of technological innovation demand, which remains an underexplored area. Second, there is a lack of tools to quantitatively assess the impact of technological innovation, particularly its uncertainties, on project scheduling, especially for linear construction projects. Third, existing studies do not consider comprehensive construction scenarios, such as the coexistence of linear, bar, and block activities in scheduling optimization. These gaps highlight the need for further research to integrate technological innovation and project scheduling more effectively.

Multi-objective scheduling model using LSM

There are two situations for the integration of construction schedules in linear megaprojects and the technological innovation: (1) after identifying the demand for the technological innovation, the whole construction stops and waits until the technological innovation has been successfully applied in the construction; (2) Various construction challenges arise during the project, resulting in reduced efficiency of construction activities, which must be addressed through technological innovation. As a result, construction progresses slowly until technological innovations are successfully implemented, at which point the construction speed accelerates to the desired level. The situation #1 increases the duration and costs, but the impact is more intuitive and easily quantifiable; the situation #2 causes uncertainty in the duration and thus the real construction schedule deviates significantly from the plan. In contrast, real engineering tends to fall into situation #2, therefore, this study develops the analysis on the basis of situation #2. Allocate a reasonable buffer time for each activity during the period required to complete the technological innovation, and optimize the scheduling plan to align with the development process of the innovation. While ensuring that schedule and cost objectives are met, the robustness of the overall timeline is further enhanced. The integration of the technological innovation expectation time into the scheduling model assumes that (1) the requirements identification phase of technological innovation begins in parallel with the construction process, and (2) all activities necessitate the insertion of a time buffer.

Solving algorithm design

Given the NP-hard nature of this multi-objective optimization problem, selecting an appropriate algorithm is crucial. While decomposition-based algorithms like MOEA/D are popular, they heavily rely on weight vectors and often struggle with the irregular and non-convex Pareto fronts typical in discrete construction scheduling. In contrast, the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm III (NSGA-III) utilizes a reference-point-based approach, which is proven to be highly effective in maintaining population diversity for problems with three or more objectives. Recent comparative studies, such as Yu28, further confirmed that NSGA-III outperforms alternatives like MOEA/D-DE and SPEA2 + SDE in construction scheduling optimization. Building upon this robust foundation, this study proposes an improved NSGA-III (I-NSGA3) algorithmic solution model.

Encoding of the solution

Considering the characteristics of the research problem and aiming to streamline the generation of the construction schedule, two sets of decision variables—construction mode and construction direction—are encoded in two integer segments. The first segment denotes the construction mode for each unit’s linear activity, as well as for the bar and block activities, with each integer indicating the selected construction mode at that location. The second segment specifies the construction direction for the linear activity, where “1” represents forward construction and “0” denotes reverse construction.

Hierarchical adaptive crossover and mutation strategy

In the NSGA-III algorithm, the Simulated Binary Crossover (SBX) operator is well-suited for crossover operations, while the Polynomial Mutation (PM) operator is more appropriate for mutation operations (Deb and Jain, 2013). To balance the global search and local exploration capabilities of the entire population, a hierarchical adaptive crossover and mutation method is proposed. Based on dominance and crowding distance, the population is divided into three levels: elite, ordinary, and inferior. Different parameter control factors are then assigned to each level for crossover and mutation operations.

Managerial implications

The proposed multi-objective optimization model provides an intuitive and practical decision-making tool for project managers. The calculation of the decision threshold () is particularly valuable for navigating real-world construction scenarios:

Scenario 1: . If the forecasted expected time of the technological innovation is within the threshold, the project manager can simply rely on this model to reallocate the scattered buffer times across activities. This ensures that the original duration and cost targets are maintained while absorbing the innovation’s temporal fluctuations.

Scenario 2: . If the expected time exceeds the threshold, the manager is alerted to make critical strategic trade-offs. To prevent overall project delays, the manager must either direct more resources toward the R&D phase (e.g., increasing the budget or personnel) to accelerate the technological breakthrough, or implement crashing measures in other linear sections (such as subgrades or bridges) to compensate for the delayed tunnel construction.

Conclusion

This study aimed to bridge the critical gap between the uncertain lifecycle of technological innovation and the rigorous execution of linear megaprojects. To achieve this objective, a linear megaproject scheduling model is established with LSM theory, and ET is measured by the GERT model and inserted into the scheduling model as scattered buffers. The core insight of this research is transforming the implicit, unpredictable nature of innovation into explicit, manageable decision-making variables. By evaluating duration, cost, and robustness simultaneously, the proposed model shifts project management from passively reacting to innovation delays to proactively balancing multi-objective trade-offs.

The study offers quantitative decision support for decision-makers to align technological innovation with linear megaproject scheduling. However, to further enhance the practical applicability of this research, future work should delve deeper into the following specific avenues:

First, extending the model to resource-constrained or highly stochastic environments. While the current framework utilizes expectation times, future iterations could incorporate stochastic activity durations and limited resource availabilities to better reflect complex on-site realities. Second, implementing real-time schedule updating mechanisms. By integrating dynamic forecasts of technological innovation progress (e.g., using digital twins or real-time data feedback), the static baseline schedule could be transformed into a dynamic rolling schedule, allowing for continuous, real-time adjustments. Additionally, future research may explore the applicability of the model to non-linear megaprojects, such as industrial plant construction, by incorporating non-linear constraint modeling and expanded multi-objective frameworks.

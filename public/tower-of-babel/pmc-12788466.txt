Radiomics and Machine Learning in Early Detection of Oral Potentially Malignant Disorders
Authors: Ashwini Dhopte, Vikas Singh, J Sophia J. Priya, Sumit Bhatt, Prasad C. Ingale, Hiroj Bagde
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Oral potentially malignant disorders (OPMDs) are a diverse group of conditions that carry a risk of transformation into oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC). Early detection is vital to improving prognosis and survival. Recent advancements in radiomics and machine learning (ML) have shown promise in enhancing the diagnostic accuracy and early detection of OPMDs. Radiomics enables the extraction of quantitative data from medical images, while ML algorithms analyze patterns and correlations for predictive modeling. This review highlights the application of radiomics and ML in early OPMD detection, focusing on imaging modalities, data processing techniques, current research findings, and future challenges.

INTRODUCTION

Oral potentially malignant disorders (OPMDs) are a group of conditions, including leukoplakia, erythroplakia, oral submucous fibrosis, and lichen planus, that have a recognized risk of developing into oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC). OSCC is one of the most common malignancies worldwide, especially in regions with high tobacco and areca nut consumption. Early detection of OPMDs is crucial for timely intervention and improved prognosis. However, traditional diagnostic tools, including clinical examination and biopsy, have limitations related to inter-observer variability, invasiveness, and sampling errors.[12]

Advances in computational diagnostics—particularly radiomics and machine learning (ML)—offer new strategies for identifying high-risk lesions earlier and more accurately. Radiomics involves the extraction of large amounts of quantitative features from medical images, while ML facilitates analysis of these complex datasets to identify patterns and predict outcomes. These technologies are rapidly gaining attention for their potential in precision oral medicine.[3]

IMAGING MODALITIES IN ORAL MEDICINE FOR RADIOMIC ANALYSIS

Cone-Beam Computed Tomography: Structural analysis of oral lesions

CBCT offers high-resolution 3D imaging of hard tissues at relatively low radiation doses, making it suitable for oral applications. It is commonly used to evaluate bony involvement in OPMDs and to detect subtle changes in the maxillofacial skeleton. Radiomic analysis of CBCT images can quantify lesion shape, margins, and internal structure, aiding in early malignancy detection.[3]

Magnetic Resonance Imaging: Functional and soft tissue imaging

MRI offers superior soft-tissue contrast and functional data through sequences such as diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) mapping. Radiomics applied to MRI can assess tissue heterogeneity, perfusion, and cellular density, which are important in distinguishing benign from potentially malignant lesions.[4]

Optical imaging techniques: Non-invasive screening tools

Non-invasive imaging modalities such as autofluorescence, narrow-band imaging (NBI), and hyperspectral imaging have shown potential in the early screening of OPMDs. When integrated with radiomics, these tools can offer real-time, high-throughput mucosal pattern analysis for community-based screening programs.[5]

MACHINE LEARNING ALGORITHMS IN RADIOMICS: FROM PATTERN RECOGNITION TO PREDICTIVE MODELING

ML, a subfield of artificial intelligence (AI), enables computers to learn from data and make decisions without being explicitly programmed. In radiomics, ML is used to analyze complex datasets to classify lesions (e.g., OPMD vs OSCC), predict malignant transformation, and stratify patient risk.

CURRENT RESEARCH APPLICATIONS OF RADIOMICS AND MACHINE LEARNING

Differentiation Between Oral Potentially Malignant Disorders and Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Radiomics combined with ML has demonstrated significant potential in differentiating high-risk OPMDs from OSCC. A study using MRI radiomic features and SVMs reported classification accuracies exceeding 85%, emphasizing the potential of these tools in clinical decision support.[8]

Predicting malignant transformation of oral lesions

Predicting which OPMDs are more likely to transform into cancer remains a major challenge. Longitudinal radiomic studies analyzing textural features such as entropy, skewness, and homogeneity have shown correlations with dysplasia severity and transformation risk, enabling personalized surveillance plans.[9]

Enhancing diagnostic accuracy through multimodal data integration

When radiomic data are integrated with clinical variables (e.g., tobacco use, lesion site) and histopathology, ML models show significantly improved predictive power. This multimodal approach aligns with the principles of precision medicine and offers more comprehensive risk profiling.[10]

CONCLUSION

Radiomics and ML represent a paradigm shift in the early detection and management of OPMDs. By extracting and analyzing complex imaging features, these technologies offer non-invasive, objective, and reproducible tools for clinical decision-making. While there are challenges to overcome, continued research and technological refinement hold the promise of incorporating AI-driven diagnostics into routine oral healthcare, ultimately improving patient outcomes through early and personalized interventions.

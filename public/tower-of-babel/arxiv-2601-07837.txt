Convergence of a Multi-Inertial λ \lambda -Iteration Scheme in Cone b , p b,p -Normed Banach Spaces
Elvin Rada

Abstract
We propose and analyze a multi-inertial λ \lambda -iteration scheme in cone b , p b,p -normed Banach spaces. This framework extends the classical Krasnoselskii–Mann and two-step inertial iterations by incorporating three independent inertial parameters and multiple error-control sequences. Under mild assumptions such as quasi-nonexpansiveness, weak contraction, and compatibility of mappings, we establish convergence theorems guaranteeing existence and uniqueness of fixed points. Illustrative numerical examples in ℝ \mathbb{R} and ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} demonstrate accelerated convergence compared with the classical Krasnoselskii–Mann method.



Keywords:
Fixed point theory, cone
b,p
-normed Banach spaces, inertial iteration,
Krasnoselskii–Mann iteration, weak contraction, compatible mappings.

To accelerate convergence, inertial terms introduced by Polyak

We focus on

This setting enables robust convergence analysis while accommodating quasi-normed geometries.

Cortild and Peypouquet
[

Our approach generalizes this framework by introducing three distinct inertial parameters with multiple error-control sequences, thereby unifying and extending known inertial KM-type methods.

We introduce a new

\lambda

•

•

Convergence results under quasi-nonexpansive, weakly contractive, and compatible mappings.

•

Numerical examples demonstrating improved convergence compared with KM and two-step inertial schemes.

2
Preliminaries

Definition 1

(Cone
b,p
-Normed Banach Space)
.

Let
E
be a real Banach space and
P\subset E
a closed, convex, pointed cone with nonempty interior.
Let
X
be a real vector space. A map
D:X\to P
is called a
cone
b,p
-norm
(with
b\geq 1
and
0<p\leq 1
) if, for all
x,y\in X
and
\tau\geq 0
,

(i)

D(x)=0
if and only if
x=0
,

(ii)

D(x)=D(-x)
,

(iii)

D(x+y)\leq_{P}b\big(D(x)+D(y)\big)
,

(iv)

D(\tau x)=\tau^{p}D(x)
.

The induced distance
d(x,y):=D(x-y)
makes
(X,d)
a
cone
b,p
-normed Banach space
whenever
(X,\Delta)
(defined below) is complete.

Remark 1
.

For
p=1
, the structure reduces to a cone
b
-normed Banach space.
For
0<p<1
, the
p
-homogeneity is consistent with quasi-norm geometry: the scalarized metric
\Delta(x,y):=\|D(x-y)\|_{E}
is translation-invariant and subadditive by concavity of
t\mapsto t^{p}
.

Definition 2

(Scalarization and Normal Cone)
.

Assume
P
is normal with constant
\kappa\geq 1
. Define the scalarization

\Delta(x,y):=\|D(x-y)\|_{E},\qquad x,y\in X.

Then, for all
x,y,z\in X
and
\tau\geq 0
,

\Delta(x+z,y+z)=\Delta(x,y),\qquad\Delta(x+y,0)\leq\kappa b\big(\Delta(x,0)+\Delta(y,0)\big),\qquad\Delta(\tau x,0)=\tau\,\Delta(x,0).

Definition 3

(Multi-Inertial
\lambda
-Iteration)
.

Let
C\subset X
be a closed convex subset,
T:C\to C
, and
x_{0},x_{1}\in C
. Given sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\},\{\beta_{n}\},\{\gamma_{n}\}\subset[0,\alpha]
for some
\alpha\in[0,1)
, a relaxation sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset[\delta,1-\delta]
with
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, and error sequences
\{\varepsilon_{n}\},\{\rho_{n}\},\{\omega_{n}\},\{\theta_{n}\}\subset C
, define for
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle y_{n}

\displaystyle=x_{n}+\alpha_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\varepsilon_{n},

\displaystyle z_{n}

\displaystyle=x_{n}+\beta_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\rho_{n},

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle=x_{n}+\gamma_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\omega_{n},

\displaystyle x_{n+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{n})y_{n}+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}z_{n}+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}T(u_{n})+\theta_{n}.

We assume the error sequences are summable in the
D
-scale:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|D(\varepsilon_{n})\|_{E}<\infty,\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|D(\rho_{n})\|_{E}<\infty,\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|D(\omega_{n})\|_{E}<\infty,\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|D(\theta_{n})\|_{E}<\infty.

Remark 2
.

The above iteration strictly generalizes the Krasnoselskii–Mann scheme and known one- and two-step inertial variants.
When
\alpha_{n}=\beta_{n}=\gamma_{n}\equiv 0
and
\theta_{n}=\varepsilon_{n}=\rho_{n}=\omega_{n}\equiv 0
, it reduces to the classical Krasnoselskii–Mann iteration.

2.1
Auxiliary Inequalities

Lemma 1

(Averaging Inequality)
.

Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{m},p\in X
and
\eta_{j}\geq 0
with
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}=1
. Then

\Delta\!\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}u_{j},\,p\Big)\leq\kappa b\sum_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}\,\Delta(u_{j},p).

Proof.

By translation invariance,

D\!\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}(u_{j}-p)\Big)\leq_{P}b\sum_{j=1}^{m}D\big(\eta_{j}(u_{j}-p)\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\eta_{j}D(u_{j}-p).

Taking norms and using normality yields the claim.
∎

Lemma 2

(Averaging With Affine Perturbation)
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\geq 0
with
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}=1
, and let
v_{1},v_{2},w,\eta,p\in X
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta\!\Big(a_{1}v_{1}+a_{2}v_{2}+a_{3}w-p\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa b\!\left(a_{1}\Delta(v_{1},p)+a_{2}\Delta(v_{2},p)+a_{3}\Delta(w,p)\right),

\displaystyle\Delta\!\Big(a_{1}v_{1}+a_{2}v_{2}+a_{3}w+\eta-p\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(\kappa b)^{2}\!\left(a_{1}\Delta(v_{1},p)+a_{2}\Delta(v_{2},p)+a_{3}\Delta(w,p)\right)+\kappa b\,\Delta(\eta,0).

Proof.

The first bound follows from Lemma
1
. For the second, write

a_{1}v_{1}+a_{2}v_{2}+a_{3}w+\eta-p=\big(a_{1}v_{1}+a_{2}v_{2}+a_{3}w-p\big)+\eta,

apply the
b
-triangle once, and bound the first term by Lemma
1
.
∎

Lemma 3

(One-Step Recursion)
.

Let
e_{n}^{(\varepsilon)}:=\Delta(\varepsilon_{n},0)
,
e_{n}^{(\rho)}:=\Delta(\rho_{n},0)
,
e_{n}^{(\omega)}:=\Delta(\omega_{n},0)
, and
e_{n}^{(\theta)}:=\Delta(\theta_{n},0)
. Then, for
n\geq 1
,

\Delta(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq\kappa b\!\left((1-\lambda_{n})\alpha_{n}+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\beta_{n}+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\gamma_{n}\right)\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\zeta_{n},

where

\zeta_{n}:=\kappa b\!\left((1-\lambda_{n})e_{n}^{(\varepsilon)}+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}e_{n}^{(\rho)}+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}e_{n}^{(\omega)}\right)+e_{n}^{(\theta)}.

In particular, if
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n},\gamma_{n}\leq\alpha<1
and
\lambda_{n}\in[\delta,1-\delta]
, then

\Delta(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq c\,\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\zeta_{n},\qquad c:=\kappa b\,\alpha<1.

Proof.

Subtract
x_{n}
from the update for
x_{n+1}
and use translation invariance:

\displaystyle x_{n+1}-x_{n}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{n})\big(y_{n}-x_{n}\big)+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\big(z_{n}-x_{n}\big)+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\big(T(u_{n})-x_{n}\big)+\theta_{n}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{n})\big(\alpha_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\varepsilon_{n}\big)+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\big(\beta_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\rho_{n}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\big(\gamma_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\omega_{n}\big)+\theta_{n}.

Apply the
b
-triangle inequality to separate the
\theta_{n}
term and then to the convex combination. Using positive homogeneity and normality completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4

(Summable Steps Imply Cauchy)
.

Assume the bound in Lemma
3
with
c<1
and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}<\infty
. Then
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Delta(x_{n+1},x_{n})<\infty
,
\Delta(x_{n+1},x_{n})\to 0
, and
\{x_{n}\}
is Cauchy in
(X,\Delta)
.

Proof.

Apply a Robbins–Siegmund type argument to
a_{n}:=\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1})
with
a_{n+1}\leq c\,a_{n}+\zeta_{n}
. Then
a_{n}\to 0
and
\sum_{n}a_{n}<\infty
. For
m>n
,

\Delta(x_{m},x_{n})=\Delta\Big(\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}(x_{k+1}-x_{k}),0\Big)\leq\kappa b\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}\Delta(x_{k+1},x_{k})\to 0,

showing Cauchy-ness.
∎

2.2
Operator Classes

Definition 4

(Quasi-Nonexpansive Mappings)
.

Let
T:C\to C
. If
F(T)\neq\emptyset
and

\Delta(Tx,p)\leq\Delta(x,p)\quad\text{for all }x\in C,\ p\in F(T),

then
T
is called
quasi-nonexpansive
.

Definition 5

(Weak Contraction)
.

Let
a,b_{c},c,s\in[0,\infty)
with
a+b_{c}>0
and
a+b_{c}+c>0
. The mapping
T:C\to C
satisfies a
weak contractive condition
if

a\,D(Tx-Ty)+b_{c}\big(D(x-Tx)+D(y-Ty)\big)+c\,D(y-Tx)\leq_{P}s\,D(x-y)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in C.

By normality, this implies the scalarized inequality

a\,\Delta(Tx,Ty)+b_{c}\big(\Delta(x,Tx)+\Delta(y,Ty)\big)+c\,\Delta(y,Tx)\leq s\,\Delta(x,y).

Definition 6

(Compatibility and Weak Compatibility)
.

Maps
S,T:C\to C
have a
coincidence point
if there exists
p\in C
such that
Sp=Tp
. They are
weakly compatible
if
Sz=Tz
implies
STz=TSz
.

Definition 7

(Compatibility-Type Contractive Condition)
.

Assume
T(C)\subset S(C)
and
S(C)
is complete with respect to
\Delta
. Suppose there exist
a,b_{c},r\geq 0
with
a+b_{c}>0
and
0\leq r<a+b_{c}
such that, for all
x,y\in C
,

a\,D(Tx-Ty)+b_{c}\big(D(Sx-Tx)+D(Sy-Ty)\big)\leq_{P}r\,D(Sx-Sy).

Equivalently, in the scalarized form,

a\,\Delta(Tx,Ty)+b_{c}\big(\Delta(Sx,Tx)+\Delta(Sy,Ty)\big)\leq r\,\Delta(Sx,Sy).

3
Main Results

This section establishes the convergence theorems for the multi-inertial
\lambda
-iteration in cone
b,p
-normed Banach spaces under various operator classes.

3.1
Convergence for Quasi-Nonexpansive Mappings

Theorem 1
.

Assume that
T:C\to C
is quasi-nonexpansive with
F(T)\neq\emptyset
and sequentially continuous on
C
.
Let the sequences
\{\alpha_{n}\},\{\beta_{n}\},\{\gamma_{n}\}
satisfy
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n},\gamma_{n}\leq\alpha<1
, and let
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset[\delta,1-\delta]
with
\delta\in(0,1/2)
.
Suppose also that
\kappa b\alpha<1
and the summability condition (
) holds.
Then the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
defined by the multi-inertial
\lambda
-iteration converges (in the
\Delta
-metric) to a point
p^{\star}\in F(T)
.

Proof.

Fix
p\in F(T)
. Applying Lemma
2
with weights
\big(1-\lambda_{n},\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2},\frac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\big)
to the update of
x_{n+1}
yields

\Delta(x_{n+1},p)\leq(\kappa b)^{2}\!\left[(1-\lambda_{n})\Delta(y_{n},p)+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\Delta(z_{n},p)+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\Delta(T(u_{n}),p)\right]+\kappa b\,e_{n}^{(\theta)}.

Because
T
is quasi-nonexpansive and
p\in F(T)
,
\Delta(T(u_{n}),p)\leq\Delta(u_{n},p)
.
Using the definitions of
y_{n},z_{n},u_{n}
and the
b
-triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\Delta(y_{n},p)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa b(\Delta(x_{n},p)+\alpha_{n}\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1}))+e_{n}^{(\varepsilon)},

\displaystyle\Delta(z_{n},p)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa b(\Delta(x_{n},p)+\beta_{n}\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1}))+e_{n}^{(\rho)},

\displaystyle\Delta(u_{n},p)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa b(\Delta(x_{n},p)+\gamma_{n}\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1}))+e_{n}^{(\omega)}.

Combining the above gives

\Delta(x_{n+1},p)\leq(\kappa b)^{3}\,\Delta(x_{n},p)+(\kappa b)^{3}\alpha\,\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\tilde{e}_{n},

where
\tilde{e}_{n}
is a finite linear combination of the error terms.
Since
\kappa b\alpha<1
, Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
imply that
\{x_{n}\}
is Cauchy and convergent in
(X,\Delta)
; let
x_{n}\to p^{\star}
.

Finally,
u_{n}-x_{n}=\gamma_{n}(x_{n}-x_{n-1})+\omega_{n}\to 0
, so
u_{n}\to p^{\star}
. By continuity of
T
,
T(u_{n})\to T(p^{\star})
, and from the update formula, all terms converge to
p^{\star}
. Hence
T(p^{\star})=p^{\star}
.
∎

3.2
Convergence for Weak Contractions

Theorem 2
.

Assume
T:C\to C
satisfies the weak contractive condition

a\,\Delta(Tx,Ty)+b_{c}\big(\Delta(x,Tx)+\Delta(y,Ty)\big)+c\,\Delta(y,Tx)\leq s\,\Delta(x,y)

for all
x,y\in C
, with
a+b_{c}>0
and
a+b_{c}+c>0
.
Assume
T
is continuous and the iteration parameters satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
1
.
If

0\leq q_{n}:=\frac{s\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{n}^{2}b_{c}+c(\lambda_{n}-1)}{\lambda_{n}^{2}(a+b_{c})}<1\quad\text{for all }n,

then
x_{n}\to p^{\star}\in F(T)
.

Proof.

From the recursive definition of
x_{n+1}
and the weak contraction inequality,
we obtain a relation of the form

\Delta(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq\kappa b\!\left((1-\lambda_{n})\alpha_{n}+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\beta_{n}+\tfrac{\lambda_{n}}{2}\gamma_{n}\right)\!\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1})+q_{n}\,\kappa b\,\gamma_{n}\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\xi_{n},

where
\xi_{n}
is a combination of summable errors.
Using
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n},\gamma_{n}\leq\alpha
and
q_{n}<1
yields

\Delta(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq c^{\prime}\,\Delta(x_{n},x_{n-1})+\xi_{n},\qquad c^{\prime}:=\kappa b\,\alpha(1+q_{\max})<1.

Lemma
4
guarantees that
\{x_{n}\}
is Cauchy, hence convergent.
The rest of the argument is identical to Theorem
1
.
∎

3.3
Coincidence and Compatibility Theorems

Theorem 3
.

Let
S,T:C\to C
satisfy the compatibility-type condition

a\,\Delta(Tx,Ty)+b_{c}\big(\Delta(Sx,Tx)+\Delta(Sy,Ty)\big)\leq r\,\Delta(Sx,Sy),

with
a+b_{c}>0
and
0\leq r<a+b_{c}
. Assume
T(C)\subset S(C)
and that
S(C)
is complete with respect to
\Delta
.
Then there exists
p\in C
such that
Sp=Tp
.
If
S
and
T
are weakly compatible, the common fixed point is unique.

Proof.

Define
y_{n}:=Tx_{n}=Sx_{n+1}
. Using the inequality with
(x,y)=(x_{n},x_{n-1})
gives

(a+b_{c})\Delta(y_{n+1},y_{n})\leq r\,\Delta(Sx_{n},Sx_{n-1})=r\,\Delta(y_{n},y_{n-1}),

so
\Delta(y_{n+1},y_{n})\leq q\,\Delta(y_{n},y_{n-1})
with
q:=r/(a+b_{c})<1
.
Hence
\{y_{n}\}
is Cauchy and convergent in
S(C)
to some
z
.
Because
Tx_{n}=y_{n}\to z
and
Sx_{n+1}=y_{n}\to z
, there exists
p\in C
such that
Sp=Tp=z
.
If
S
and
T
are weakly compatible,
STp=TSp
, implying
Sz=Tz=z
, so
z
is the unique common fixed point.
∎

3.4
Discussion

The above results collectively establish that the proposed multi-inertial
\lambda
-iteration is robust under three important operator classes—quasi-nonexpansive, weakly contractive, and compatible mappings—when embedded in the broad setting of cone
b,p
-normed Banach spaces.
Each theorem guarantees the convergence of
\{x_{n}\}
to a fixed point of the underlying operator, and the convergence is ensured even in the presence of summable perturbations and multiple inertial effects.

5
Conclusions

We have introduced a generalized multi-inertial
\lambda
-iteration for fixed point problems in cone
b,p
-normed Banach spaces. This framework unifies several existing methods, including the classical Krasnoselskii–Mann, one-step, and two-step inertial iterations.

Our analysis established convergence under broad operator classes—quasi-nonexpansive, weakly contractive, and compatible mappings—while allowing summable errors and multiple inertial parameters. Numerical examples confirmed the theoretical findings and demonstrated faster convergence compared with standard iterations.

Future research directions include:

•

Deriving explicit convergence rate estimates and complexity bounds.

•

Extending the framework to stochastic and random operator settings.

•

Investigating demicontractive and accretive operators.

•

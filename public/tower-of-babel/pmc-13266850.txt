Combined organ resection for esophageal cancer invading adjacent organs is becoming a safe and curative procedure
Authors: Masanobu Nakajima, Kazuyuki Kojima
Published: 2026

Introduction

The prognosis for patients with advanced esophageal cancer remains poor, even with recent advances in chemotherapy and chemoradiotherapy, and there is an urgent need to improve this situation (1). In T4 esophageal cancer with invasion to adjacent organs, particularly in cases involving the airway or major blood vessels, surgery is rarely the initial treatment, and chemoradiotherapy comprises the standard treatment (2-5). However, residual tumors often remain after treatment, and the effectiveness of additional chemotherapy and other treatments is limited. A potentially curative treatment for such cases is salvage surgery, including combined resection of the esophagus and involved organs (6,7). However, because the trachea, bronchi, and aorta are vital organs, combined tumor resection with these vital organs is inevitably a complex operation, and if a patient is not properly prepared, the operation may risk the patient’s life. In their study, Makino et al. actively pursued an approach to this difficult surgery, and demonstrated the results of combined resection of esophageal cancer invading the respiratory tract and large vessels in an unprecedented number of cases (n=90) from a single institution (8).

Combined esophageal and respiratory tract resection

When esophageal cancer has invaded the tracheobronchial tree, surgery is rarely life-threatening but complicated. Appropriate combined resection must be performed while considering the curative effect of surgery, postoperative safety, and quality of life. While combined surgeries have been performed, there are few published reports. In 1973, Thompson reported combined resection of the lower trachea and carina for esophageal cancer invading the trachea and reported favorable postoperative outcomes; however, no long-term results were described (9). In 1994, Matsubara et al. reported the surgical outcomes of 55 cases of combined esophageal tumor and tracheal resection, stating that if curative resection was achieved, the 2-year survival rate was 51%. The authors also noted that even in cases in which palliative resection was performed, the 1-year survival rate was 23%, which was significantly better than that in cases without resection. However, the authors provided no information on perioperative complications (10).

Anterior mediastinal tracheostomy has a long history of being performed for esophageal cancer that has invaded the upper trachea. This surgical procedure is extremely complex and often results in complications, and the high morbidity and mortality rates resulting from innominate artery rupture are long-standing issues (11). Reports from the 1980s onward, all of which involved small numbers of cases from single institutions, have shown complication rates of 25–57% and in-hospital mortality rates of 7–25% (11-20). Some of these reports have documented long-term outcomes, and, overall, the results have been poor, with a 3-year survival rate of approximately 11% (11,16-20).

Combined esophageal and aortic resection

During resection of esophageal cancer that has invaded the aorta, if precautions are not taken, immediately life-threatening massive bleeding can occur. Thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) is a possible measure against major bleeding. The first report of prophylactic TEVAR to prevent bleeding was by Yamatsuji et al. in 2006 (21). However, radical resection was achieved in only one of four cases, and in three cases, a small part of the cancer was left behind to prevent excessive bleeding. One patient for whom radical resection was achieved survived for 30 months, but all three patients with residual tumor died within 6 months. Several similar reports have since been published (6,22-26). The number of cases reported from a single institution is not large, and the study with the largest number of cases was performed by the Japan Esophageal Society (23). The report involved a questionnaire survey performed in Japan for the authorized or semi-authorized member institutes, and included 21 cases of prophylactic TEVAR and esophagectomy. The study investigated complications after TEVAR and the long-term prognosis after radical resection, and reported no complications, including paraplegia, after TEVAR, and no bleeding events after esophagectomy. The median survival time (MST) was 378 days; however, the details of postoperative complications and causes of patient death were not reported.

Although there are few reports on aortic replacement using a synthetic vascular graft for esophageal cancer invasion, early reports were published in the 1990s. Watanabe et al. performed esophagectomy with aortic replacement in one case of esophageal cancer invading the aorta, but reported that combined resection of the esophagus and invaded organs, including other organs, did not improve the patient’s prognosis (27). Ichiyoshi et al. reported five cases of aortic replacement surgery and stated that the surgery was performed safely, but all patients died within 6 months, leading to issues regarding prognosis (28). However, in 2014, Cong et al. reported 47 cases of combined aortic resection with aortic replacement (29). The authors reported that no residual tumor (R0) resection was achieved in all cases, and although complications were observed in 59.6% of cases, there were no in-hospital deaths. The MST was 33.6 months.

Current status and future of combined organ resection for esophageal cancer

Osaka University, where Makino and colleagues are affiliated, has a long history of boldly performing surgery for T4 esophageal cancer to attempt curative resection (7,22,30). This time, the authors presented the results of surgery for 90 patients, which is an extremely large number for a single facility (8). The authors presented detailed results regarding surgical and long-term outcomes. R0 resection was achieved in 89 patients (98.9%) after combined resection of adjacent organs, as appropriate. In a 1995 report by Matsubara et al. of 55 cases of combined esophageal and airway resection, only 29 (52.7%) were curative cases in which no residual tumor was detected macroscopically (10). In a 1999 report by Ichiyoshi et al., various types of combined esophageal and aortic resections were performed, but R0 resections were achieved in only 2/7 cases (28.6%) (28). Recently, Nakajima et al. and Cong et al. reported 100% R0 rates in esophagectomy after TEVAR (6,29). Compared with these previous reports, the results of combined esophageal and aortic resection at other facilities have recently become more stable, making the superiority of the findings of Makino et al. less notable. However, the R0 resection rate for combined esophageal and airway resection is still noteworthy. This result is likely because of highly sophisticated treatment, achieved through accurate preoperative evaluation using a variety of diagnostic imaging techniques and the appropriate use of a variety of combined resection methods, including anterior mediastinal tracheostomy. Accordingly, the median disease-free survival was 6.5 months and the median overall survival was 18.9 months, which are good results considering that this was a group of patients who required organ resection following all treatments. The overall complication rate was 55.4%, but the in-hospital mortality rate was only 2.2%. These were very favorable outcomes considering that this patient population underwent multidisciplinary treatment for T4 esophageal cancer, primarily followed by salvage surgery. To date, reports of combined organ resection for T4 esophageal cancer are limited, and the latest data are also limited. The study by Makino et al. is important in that the authors collected data for a large number of cases at a single institution and demonstrated that safe and curative combined organ resection is possible. In appropriately selected cases, combined esophageal and adjacent organ resection for T4 esophageal cancer may be a very effective option. Recently, with the emergence of new treatments, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors, the opportunities for and role of conversion surgery are expected to increase in the near future (31). Notably, a word of caution is necessary. Esophagectomy with combined resection of the airway and great vessels after multidisciplinary treatment requires collaboration with cardiovascular and thoracic surgeons. This collaborative surgery should be performed at an experienced facility that can perform the procedures safely and should not be performed in facilities with minimal experience.

Conclusions

In their article, Makino et al. reported the results of safe and curative combined organ resection for T4b esophageal cancer. As surgeons, we should use these results as a reference and fully consider the overall capabilities of our own institutions to strive to improve the curability of highly advanced esophageal cancer.

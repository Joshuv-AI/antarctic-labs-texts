On the comparison of ultradifferentiable weight function settings Thanks: This research was funded in whole or in part by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) 10.55776/PAT9445424
Gerhard Schindl

Abstract
. We summarize, revisit and compare different notions of ultradifferentiable settings defined in terms of weight functions. More precisely, we emphasize the difference between the original Beurling-Björck-setting, when measuring the growth of the Fourier-transform, and the more recent approach by Braun, Meise and Taylor, when controlling the growth of the derivatives similarly like in the weight sequence framework. For the equality of both settings the convexity of the defining weight function is required and, somehow, has become a standard assumption. We study the meaning of this convexity condition and its failure in detail. First, we characterize the inclusion relations and hence the equality for Beurling-Björck-type classes in terms of weight functions analogously as it has already been done for the Braun-Meise-Taylor setting and work within the general weight matrix setting. Finally, we construct a technical (counter-)example which is a weight in the sense of Beurling-Björck but violates the convexity condition and which illustrates the different flavor of both weight function settings.

1.
Introduction

When dealing with ultradifferentiable function classes and analogously defined weighted spaces, in the literature “classically” one can find two settings: either using a
weight sequence

\mathbf{M}
or a
weight function

\omega
. The ultradifferentiable weight sequence case is more original, we refer to
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
. On the other hand, in the weight function setting first the growth of the Fourier transform (of compactly supported functions) has been measured; see
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
23
]
and Section
6.3
for a summary. Here the expression measuring the admissible growth is given by
\omega
directly (more precisely by
\exp\circ\omega
) and, indeed, non-trivial weighted test function spaces are treated
(“Beurling-Björck setting”).
However, in these original frameworks slightly different notions for being a weight function have been considered; we refer to Sections
2.8
and
6.3
.

Later, in
[
7
]
the nowadays frequently used definition involving the
Legendre-Fenchel-Young conjugate

\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
of
\varphi_{\omega}:t\mapsto\omega(e^{t})
has been given
(“Braun-Meise-Taylor setting”).
For such spaces the derivatives of the functions under investigation are weighted analogously to the weight sequence framework; see
[
7
, Lemma 3.3, Prop. 3.4]
.

In each approach, basic growth and regularity assumptions on the weights
\omega
are required and one can distinguish between two types: The
Roumieu-type,
involving the existence quantifier, and the
Beurling-type
when using the universal quantifier. As it has been mentioned in the introduction in
[
7
]
, when comparing the assumptions between both settings it follows that requirement
subadditivity
for
\omega
in
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
23
]
can be replaced resp. relaxed by
\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t))
. This condition is frequently abbreviated by
(\alpha)
and we write
(\omega_{1})
for it; this abbreviation is motivated by the notation used in
[
29
]
. A further achievement is that the definition of weighted spaces within the Braun-Meise-Taylor setting admits the possibility to introduce and investigate the notion of
quasianalyticity
as well analogously to the weight sequence setting
(“Denjoy-Carleman-Theorem”).

On the other hand, for establishing this equivalent description in
[
7
, Lemma 3.3, Prop. 3.4]
crucially it has been used that
\varphi_{\omega}
is
convex;
usually abbreviated by
(\delta)
and we write
(\omega_{4})
. This has become a standard assumption on
\omega
also in related weighted settings since then; e.g. when considering globally defined functions of
Gelfand-Shilov-type.

(\omega_{4})
is crucial for obtaining the coincidence of both settings since the proof of the crucial Paley-Wiener-type theorem involves the double conjugate
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}
and the identity
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}=\varphi_{\omega}
which holds if and only if
\varphi_{\omega}
is convex. In general, one only has
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}\leq\varphi_{\omega}
and for this note that
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}
and
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
both are automatically convex; see the comments in
[
29
, Sect. 3.1]
and more details are given in Section
2.9
.

In
[
29
]
and
[
24
]
we have shown that ultradifferentiable classes in the Braun-Meise-Taylor setting can equivalently be described by using the
associated weight matrix

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}:=\{\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}:\ell>0\}
,
W^{(\ell)}_{j}:=\exp(\frac{1}{\ell}\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(\ell j))
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
; we refer to the main statement
[
24
, Thm. 5.14]
and for this alternative description
(\omega_{1})
is crucial. It is immediate by definition that properties for
\omega
can be transferred to
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
but, however, for the converse and hence when establishing a characterization of growth conditions, then again
(\omega_{4})
is becoming relevant in order to treat the double conjugate. Another crucial property for which
(\omega_{4})
is unavoidable is the equivalence between
\omega
and each
associated weight function

\omega_{\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}}
; see
[
24
, Lemma 5.7]
,
[
29
, Theorem 4.0.3, Lemma 5.1.3]
, and also
[
13
, Lemma 2.5]
. On the other hand, for the sake of completeness we mention that basically
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
can be introduced for more general
\omega
. Indeed, it suffices to assume that
\omega
is non-decreasing and
\log(t)=o(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
(abbreviated by
(\omega_{3})
in the following) in order to ensure well-definedness of the Young conjugate and hence of the sequences
\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}
; we refer to the comments in
[
29
, Sect. 3.1]
,
[
26
, Sect. 2.7,
(a)-(c)
]
,
[
28
, Sect. 6
(III)
]
and the summary in Section
2.9
. Via
(\omega_{1})
also the equality between the weighted spaces holds when the classes are introduced directly (by definition) via weighting the derivatives in terms of
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
. Indeed, for this equality estimate
[
24
,
(5.10)
]
is required and for its proof
(\omega_{4})
is not needed. For future reference we call any non-decreasing function satisfying
(\omega_{3})

matrix admissible.

The main aim of this article is to illustrate the difference between the Beurling-Björck and the Braun-Meise-Taylor setting in view of the convexity for
\varphi_{\omega}
. Without this assumption the main Paley-Wiener-type result from
[
7
]
and hence the equality between both settings is becoming unclear. Therefore, when treating the pure Beurling-Björck framework the specific role resp. the loss of
(\omega_{4})
has to be investigated. This question has been motivated by the explanations above and it is based on the comments in
[
29
, Sect. 3.1]
. But also in the recent article
[
20
]
in the introduction this convexity condition, its possible failure and its role in proofs within the Beurling-Björck are discussed. (
(\omega_{4})
is also denoted by
(\delta)
in
[
20
]
.)

More precisely, by taking into account the fact that
(\omega_{4})
is in general not preserved under equivalence of weight functions, we can see the first main question:

(Q1)

How restrictive is
(\omega_{4})
? Does for any weight function (in the sense of Beurling-Björck) an equivalent function exist which satisfies
(\omega_{4})
?

Concerning the notion of equivalence of weight functions and more relevant growth relations we refer to Section
2.3
. Connected to
(Q1)
is the following problem:

(Q2)

Characterize the inclusion relation, and hence the equality, of the spaces in the Beurling-Björck setting (for both types) in terms of the defining weights. One should expect the same growth relation between weight functions as in the Braun-Meise-Taylor setting (expressed by an O-growth restriction, written
\preceq
) and thus equivalence of weight functions characterizes the equality (as l.c.v.s).

We treat and answer both questions in this work in detail: The last Section
7
is dedicated to
(Q1)
and to the construction of a (counter-)example showing that there exist weights in the sense of Beurling-Björck such that none equivalent (weight) function satisfies
(\omega_{4})
. Indeed, we even verify that there exist “many” of such weight functions; i.e. uncountable infinitely many different equivalence classes. The geometric construction also involves recent knowledge about the growth index
\gamma(\omega)
for weight functions
\omega
introduced in
[
12
]
; see Section
2.7
. An immediate consequence of this example is to see the limitation of the weight sequence setting in this approach; i.e. when considering
associated weight functions

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
with
\mathbf{M}
being a sequence satisfying basic growth and regularity properties. This is due to the fact that
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
satisfies automatically
(\omega_{4})
; see Remark
7.6
for more details. On the other hand
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
appears frequently in the Braun-Meise-Taylor setting as a (counter-)example and in the comparison results from
[
6
]
; this fact again illustrates the difference between both settings. Finally, the constructed
\omega
in Section
7
is matrix admissible.

(Q2)
is studied in detail in Sections
3
,
4
,
5
and
6
; for the main characterizing results in this context see Theorem
4.1
for the Beurling-type and Theorem
4.2
for the Roumieu-type. We treat a very general setting: First, we work with
weight function matrices

\mathcal{W}:=\{\omega^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
with matrix parameter
\ell
, see Definition
3.1
. Second, in Sections
3
,
4
we are concerned with weighted subspaces of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, subject to
\mathcal{W}
. Indeed, the weights are given by
x\mapsto\exp(\omega^{\ell}(x))
and so involving the exponential function. Section
5
summarizes the situation when we focus on two special but natural weight function matrices for which the crucial information can be expressed in terms of a single weight function
\omega
: matrices of
“exponential-type” and of “dilatation-type”.
In Section
6
we apply the information to related and analogous situations: First, in Section
6.1
we consider the setting when weighting a fixed subspace
\mathcal{A}^{p}\subseteq L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In Section
6.2
we focus in detail on the case when weighting the “Fourier image” of a given (fixed) function space
\mathcal{A}
; i.e. not the growth of
f
directly is controlled but of its Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
. Of course, for this case it is crucial to ensure that the Fourier transform is well-defined on
\mathcal{A}
and its image is non-trivial. Finally, in each instance we also treat the
anisotropic setting;
i.e. when the weight functions are not radially extended to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The known Beurling-Björck setting corresponds now to the special weight matrix
\omega^{\ell}:=\ell\omega
,
\ell>0
, where
\omega
is given and fixed
(“exponential type”),
see Section
5.1
, and when weighting the Fourier image of fixed subspaces (of functions having compact support) in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We refer to Section
6.3
for more details.

Before starting with this approach, in the first Section
2
we list, summarize and compare in detail the numerous growth conditions and relations on weight functions in the different settings. One can expect that this content is also interesting for other different weighted settings since we exclusively deal with growth and regularity properties for functions. The same comment applies to the construction provided in Section
7
.

2.
Weight functions and their growth conditions

2.1.
General notation

We write
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
,
\mathbb{N}_{>0}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
and for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the expression
|x|
denotes the usual Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.2.
Relevant growth properties

We list (known) conditions for functions
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
; the abbreviations are also appearing in
[
29
]
. Some of these properties appear exclusively for weights in the sense of Beurling-Björck (see
[
3
]
), some of them only for Braun-Meise-Taylor weights (see
[
7
]
) and some of them are crucial in both settings.

We call a function
\omega

normalized
if
\omega(t)=0
for all
0\leq t\leq 1
. Moreover, we shall assume
\omega\neq 0
except stated explicitly otherwise. Inspired by the notation used in
[
29
]
we consider:

(\omega_{1})

\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{2})

\omega(t)=O(t)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{3})

\log(t)=o(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{4})

\varphi_{\omega}:t\mapsto\omega(e^{t})
is a convex function (on
\mathbb{R}
).

(\omega_{5})

\omega(t)=o(t)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{6})

\exists\;H\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;2\omega(t)\leq\omega(Ht)+H
.

(\omega_{\text{nq}})

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{2}}dt<+\infty.

(\omega_{\text{snq}})

\exists\;C>0\;\forall\;y>0:\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(yt)}{t^{2}}dt\leq C\omega(y)+C
.

The next condition is crucial for characterizing desired stability properties for Braun-Meise-Taylor classes (of both types), see
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
and the citations therein:

(\alpha_{0})

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\exists\;t_{0}\geq 0\;\forall\;\lambda\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq t_{0}:\;\;\;\omega(\lambda t)\leq C\lambda\omega(t).

In
[
29
,
(3.8.4)
]
this requirement has been denoted by
(\omega_{1^{{}^{\prime}}})
but frequently in the literature it is abbreviated by
(\alpha_{0})
. Finally, let us introduce the following two requirements appearing in the Beurling-Björck framework (see
[
3
]
):

(\omega_{3,w})

\log(t)=O(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{\text{sub}})

\omega(s+t)\leq\omega(s)+\omega(t)
for all
s,t\geq 0
.

Finally, the
Legendre-Fenchel-Young conjugate (of
\varphi_{\omega}
)
is defined as follows:

(2.1)

\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(x):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{xy-\varphi_{\omega}(y)\},\;x\in\mathbb{R}.

This transform is crucial when defining weighted classes in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor and is also basic for introducing the
associated weight matrix

\mathcal{M}_{\omega}:=\{\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}=(W^{(\ell)}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}:\ell>0\}
,
W^{(\ell)}_{j}:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(\ell j)\right)
. We investigate this transform in more detail in Section
2.9
.

2.3.
Relevant growth relations between weight functions

Let
\sigma,\tau:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be arbitrary functions and introduce the following growth relations between
\sigma
and
\tau
:

(*)

Write
\sigma\leq\tau
if
\sigma(t)\leq\tau(t)
for all
t\in[0,+\infty)
.

(*)

Write
\sigma\hypertarget{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
if

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau(t)\leq C\sigma(t)+C,

and
\sigma
and
\tau
are called
equivalent,
written
\sigma\hypertarget{sim}{\sim}\tau
, if
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
and
\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma
. This is the same relation as it has been considered in
[
12
, Sect. 2.1]
.

(*)

Write
\sigma\hypertarget{omtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau
, if

\forall\;\epsilon>0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau(t)\leq\epsilon\sigma(t)+C.

Obviously,
\sigma\hyperlink{omtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau
implies
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
but
\vartriangleleft
is in general not reflexive:
\sigma\hyperlink{omtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\sigma
holds if and only if
\sup_{t\geq 0}\sigma(t)<+\infty
.

Note:

(i)

In the literature, frequently the crucial growth relations for weight functions read as follows:
\sigma\preceq\tau
resp.
\sigma\vartriangleleft\tau
precisely means

(2.2)

\tau(t)=O(\sigma(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty,\;\;\;\text{resp.}\;\;\;\tau(t)=o(\sigma(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty.

For these, in general stronger requirements, we also refer e.g. to
[
12
, Rem. 2.6,
(5)
]
.

(ii)

If
\sigma
satisfies
\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(t)>0
; i.e.

(2.3)

\exists\;t_{0}>0\;\exists\;\epsilon>0\;\forall\;t\geq t_{0}:\;\;\;\sigma(t)\geq\epsilon,

then
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
if and only
\tau(t)=O(\sigma(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
and
\sigma\hyperlink{omtriangle}{\vartriangleleft}\tau
if and only if
\tau(t)=o(\sigma(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(iii)

(
2.3
) is a mild requirement; e.g. it holds if
\sigma\neq 0
is assumed to be non-decreasing. Moreover it holds if
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(t)=+\infty
and this has been a basic and natural growth requirement when studying weight functions even in a very general setting in
[
12
]
.

(iv)

If
\sigma:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
, so if
\sigma(t)>0
for all
t\geq 0
, then
\tau(t)=O(\sigma(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
precisely means
\tau(t)\leq C\sigma(t)
for some
C\geq 1
and all
t\geq 0
; see e.g. the weighted entire setting treated in
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
.

All properties listed in Section
2.2
except
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
and the convexity property
(\omega_{4})
are automatically preserved under
\sim
; see also
[
29
, Sect. 3.2]
. Indeed, the effects on
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
when passing to an equivalent weight and the relation to
(\alpha_{0})
are investigated and revisited in Section
2.5
whereas the failure of
(\omega_{4})
is studied in detail in Section
7
where a (counter-)example is constructed.

Naturally, one can also consider the following growth relations:

(*)

Write
\sigma\hypertarget{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
if

\exists\;C_{1},C_{2}\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau(t)\leq\sigma(C_{1}t)+C_{2},

and write
\sigma\hypertarget{simc}{\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
, if
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
and
\tau\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\sigma
.

(*)

Write
\sigma\hypertarget{omtrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
, if

\forall\;\epsilon>0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau(t)\leq\sigma(\epsilon t)+C.

If
\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceqc}{\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
and either
\tau
or
\sigma
is non-decreasing, then in this relation we can assume w.l.o.g.
C_{1}=C_{2}
by taking
C:=\max\{C_{1},C_{2}\}
.
\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}
implies
\preceq_{\mathfrak{c}}
but
\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}
is in general not reflexive: If
\sigma
is non-decreasing and
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(t)=+\infty
, then
\sigma\hyperlink{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\sigma
implies

(2.4)

\forall\;u>0:\;\;\;\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\sigma(t)}=1;

i.e.
\sigma
has to be
slowly varying,
see
[
2
,
(1.2.1)
]
and
[
12
, Sect. 2.2]
:
\sigma\hyperlink{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\sigma
yields
\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\sigma(t)}\leq 1
for all
u>0
and since
\sigma
is non-decreasing
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\sigma(t)}=1
follows for any
u\geq 1
. And for
0<u<1
set
s:=ut
and get
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(tu)}{\sigma(t)}=\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\sigma(s)}{\sigma(s/u)}=1
. (This argument implies that it suffices to check (
2.4
) for all
u>1
.) Indeed, we recognize that
\sigma\hyperlink{omtrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\tau
is precisely
[
27
, Sect. 4.2
(B)
]
between
\sigma
and
\tau
; i.e.
[
27
,
(4.4)
]
with
t_{0}=+\infty
and hence this relation is becoming (also) relevant for ensuring well-definedness of the operation
\widehat{\star}
introduced and studied in
[
27
, Sect. 4]
:
\tau\hyperlink{omvartrianglec}{\vartriangleleft_{\mathfrak{c}}}\sigma
if and only if
\sigma\widehat{\star}\tau
is well-defined on
[0,+\infty)
; and the above arguments concerning (
2.4
) are also contained in
[
27
, Rem. 4.4]
.

2.4.
On the comparison of crucial growth properties

Let
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be given. We gather now immediate observations for the conditions listed in Section
2.2
and study relations between them.

(a)

(\omega_{3})
is equivalent to
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(t)}{t}=+\infty
and it clearly implies
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
. Moreover,
(\omega_{3})
is equivalent to
\log(a+t)=o(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
and
(\omega_{3,w})
is equivalent to
\log(a+t)=O(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, where
a\geq 1
is an arbitrary real parameter.

(b)

(\omega_{3})
implies
(\omega_{3,w})
and
(\omega_{3,w})
implies
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
. The crucial difference between these two conditions is that
(\omega_{3,w})
formally allows to consider the “limiting weight”
\omega(t):=\log(1+t)
whereas
(\omega_{3})
excludes this special case. In the literature occasionally
t\mapsto\log(1+t)
is treated for the Beurling-type and which gives back indeed the non-weighted situation.

(c)

(\omega_{\operatorname{snq}})
implies
(\omega_{\operatorname{nq}})
.

(d)

(\alpha_{0})
implies
(\omega_{1})
(choose
\lambda=2
).

(e)

Also
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
implies
(\omega_{1})
(take
x=y
) and with
x=y=0
we get
\omega(0)\leq 2\omega(0)
which yields
\omega(0)\geq 0
.

(f)

In the literature sometimes the following condition is used (see e.g.
[
7
, Lemma 1.2]
):

(2.5)

\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;s,t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(s+t)\leq L(\omega(s)+\omega(t))+L.

Set
s=t
and so get immediately
(\omega_{1})
when choosing the constant
2L
. If
\omega
is non-decreasing, then (
2.5
) is equivalent to
(\omega_{1})
: Indeed, if
\omega
has
(\omega_{1})
then
\omega(s+t)\leq\omega(2\max\{s,t\})\leq L\omega(\max\{s,t\})+L\leq L(\omega(s)+\omega(t))+L
and so (
2.5
) holds with the same
L
.

(g)

(\omega_{5})
implies
(\omega_{2})
.

(h)

If
\omega
is non-decreasing, then
(\omega_{\operatorname{nq}})
implies
(\omega_{5})
because
\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{2}}dt\geq\omega(s)\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}dt=\frac{\omega(s)}{s}
for all
s\geq 1
.

(i)

Let
\omega
be non-decreasing (and such that
\omega\neq 0
). Then concavity on
[0,+\infty)
and normalization can never be satisfied at the same time.

The same technical problem appears for
(\alpha_{0})
, see also
[
30
, Sect. 4.1]
and the references therein: Assume that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
\omega(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,t_{0}]
, then
C\lambda\omega(t)=0
for all
C,\lambda\geq 1
and all
0<t\leq t_{0}
. But, since
\omega\neq 0
and
\omega
is non-decreasing, we have
\omega(\lambda t)>0
as
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
for any
t>0
.

And similarly also normalization and
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
can never be achieved at the same time: If there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
\omega(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,t_{0}]
then for all
0<s\leq t_{0}
and all
t\geq 0
we get
\omega(t)\leq\omega(s+t)\leq\omega(s)+\omega(t)=\omega(t)
. But this implies
\omega=0
.

We close this section by investigating conditions
(\omega_{1})
and
(\omega_{6})

(ii)

(iv)

\omega
is equivalent to its least concave majorant

.

Note:

(*)

Assumption

(*)

To be formally correct, in the proof of

and note that the second part of

]
is trivial when

.

(*)

In
[

]
equivalence is defined via O-growth relations like in (

), see

, and which coincides with the above given notion

since (

However, in

And if

(\alpha_{0})

In the next step we show that (

see the proofs of

.

For arbitrary
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
introduce
\omega^{\iota}:(0+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
given by
\omega^{\iota}(t):=\omega(1/t)
. So
\omega^{\iota}
is non-increasing if and only if

is non-decreasing,

and

\overline{\omega^{\iota}}^{c}
shall denote the

for all

when choosing

,
\lambda_{2}=0

,

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be non-decreasing. If
\omega
satisfies (

\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;t\in(0,+\infty):\;\;\;\overline{\omega^{\iota}}^{c}(t)\leq\omega^{\iota}(t)\leq A\overline{\omega^{\iota}}^{c}(t)+A;

hence
\omega^{\iota}
is equivalent to its largest convex minorant.

Proof.

We verify
[
12
,
(14)
]
for
\omega^{\iota}
, i.e.

(2.7)

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\exists\;\beta>0\;\forall\;s,t\in(0,+\infty):\;\;\;\omega^{\iota}(s)+C\geq\frac{1}{C}\min\left\{1,\frac{t^{\beta}}{s^{\beta}}\right\}\omega^{\iota}(t),

and then apply
[
12
, Prop. 2.24]
. Note that (
2.7
) is equivalent to the existence of universal constants
C_{1},C,D\geq 1
not depending on
\beta
such that
C_{1}\omega^{\iota}(s)+D\geq\frac{1}{C_{2}}\min\left\{1,\frac{t^{\beta}}{s^{\beta}}\right\}\omega^{\iota}(s)
holds for all
s,t\in(0,+\infty)
(set
C:=\max\{D/C_{1},C_{1}C_{2}\}
).

First, since
\omega
is non-decreasing for any
0<s\leq t
we have
\omega^{\iota}(t)=\omega(1/t)\leq\omega(1/s)=\omega^{\iota}(s)
and
\min\{1,\frac{t^{\beta}}{s^{\beta}}\}=1
for all
\beta>0
. Thus (
2.7
) holds in this situation with any
C\geq 1
and
\beta>0
.

Second, note that (
2.6
) gives with
t^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{\lambda t}
:

\exists\;C,D\geq 1\;\forall\;\lambda\geq 1\;\forall\;t^{\prime}>0:\;\;\;\omega^{\iota}(t^{\prime})=\omega(1/t^{\prime})\leq C\lambda\omega(1/(\lambda t^{\prime}))+D\lambda=C\lambda\omega^{\iota}(\lambda t^{\prime})+D\lambda.

Let now
0<t<s
be arbitrary and choose
\lambda=\frac{s}{t}>1
, then the above estimate gives

D\lambda+C\lambda\omega^{\iota}(s)=D\lambda+C\lambda\omega^{\iota}(\lambda t)\geq\omega^{\iota}(t),

hence for any

:

D+C\omega^{\iota}(s)\geq\frac{t}{s}\omega^{\iota}(t)\geq\frac{t^{\beta}}{s^{\beta}}\omega^{\iota}(t)=\min\left\{1,\frac{t^{\beta}}{s^{\beta}}\right\}\omega^{\iota}(t).

Consequently, (
2.7
) is shown and
[
12
, Prop. 2.24]
yields the assertion.
∎

On the other hand, if there exists a convex function
\sigma:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
which is equivalent to
\omega^{\iota}
, then there exists some
A\geq 1
such that for all
t,s\in(0,+\infty)
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in[0,1]
satisfying
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
:

\displaystyle\omega^{\iota}(\lambda_{1}t+\lambda_{2}s)

\displaystyle\leq A\sigma(\lambda_{1}t+\lambda_{2}s)+A\leq A\lambda_{1}\sigma(t)+A\lambda_{2}\sigma(s)+A

\displaystyle\leq A^{2}\lambda_{1}\omega^{\iota}(t)+A^{2}\lambda_{1}+A^{2}\lambda_{2}\omega^{\iota}(s)+A^{2}\lambda_{2}=A^{2}\lambda_{1}\omega^{\iota}(t)+A^{2}\lambda_{2}\omega^{\iota}(s)+A^{2}.

And if there exists some
t_{0}>0
such that
\omega(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,t_{0}]
, then
\omega^{\iota}(t)=0
for all
t\geq t_{0}^{-1}
. Applying the previous estimate to

gives
\omega^{\iota}(\lambda_{1}t+\lambda_{2}t_{0})\leq A^{2}\lambda_{1}\omega^{\iota}(t)+A^{2}
for all

and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0
satisfying
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=1
.

Remark that the proof and the conclusion from

(2)

2.6.
Comment on weights being defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{C}^{d}

Usually, when being defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, it is assumed that
\omega
is extended
radially
and so
\omega(x):=\omega(|x|)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(isotropic setting).
On the other hand, for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, in the literature one can find the definition
\omega(z):=\omega(|z|_{1})
with
|z|_{1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|z_{j}|
for
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})
; see e.g.
[
7
, Def. 1.1]
.

Write
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq d
, then
|z|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2})^{1/2}
and
|z|=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|z_{j}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}
, so
|z|\leq|z|_{1}\leq d\max\{|z_{j}|:1\leq j\leq d\}\leq d|z|
holds for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Using these estimates we see that for all non-decreasing
\omega
satisfying
(\omega_{1})
both notions are equivalent in the sense that the mappings
z\mapsto\omega(|z|)
and
z\mapsto\omega(|z|_{1})
are equivalent w.r.t. relation
\sim
.

More recently also non-radial weights have been considered
(anisotropic setting);
we refer e.g. to
[
20
]
and
[
8
]
.

2.7.
The growth index
\gamma(\omega)

We briefly recall the definition of the growth index
\gamma(\omega)
, see
[
12
, Sect. 2.3]
and the references therein. Let in this section
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be non-decreasing and such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
; i.e.
\omega
is a weight function as in
[

, Sect. 2.3]
. For

we say that
\omega

\exists\;K>1:\;\;\;\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(K^{\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}<K.

, then also
(P_{\omega,\gamma^{\prime}})
is satisfied for all
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\gamma
with the same

since
\omega
is non-decreasing. Moreover, we can restrict to
\gamma>0
, because for
\gamma\leq 0
condition

is automatically satisfied (again since
\omega
is non-decreasing and
K>1
). Then we put

(2.8)

\gamma(\omega):=\sup\{\gamma>0:(P_{\omega,\gamma})\;\;\text{is satisfied}\},

and if none condition
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
holds then set
\gamma(\omega):=0
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\omega
be as before. We recall some connections between

\omega
, see also
[

, Sect. 6]
:

(*)

(*)

.

(*)

2.9.
Matrix admissible weight functions

From the ideas developed in
[
29
]
and
[
24
]
and also from the given basic definition in
[
7
]
, when dealing with BMT-weight functions
\omega
and their associated weight matrices
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
, it turns out that basically it is convenient to work with normalized, continuous and non-decreasing
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
satisfying
(\omega_{3})
and
(\omega_{4})
. (Frequently, we have denoted this set of functions by
\hypertarget{omset0}{\mathcal{W}_{0}}
and occasionally more properties on
\omega
are required; see Definition
2.8
.) Since the definition of the weighted spaces and of
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
involves the conjugate
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
from (
2.1
) we comment now on properties of this conjugate for arbitrary
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
in detail; see also
[
7
, Rem. 1.3, Lemma 1.5]
:

(a)

If
\omega
is normalized, then
\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{xy-\varphi_{\omega}(y)\}=\sup_{y\geq 0}\{xy-\varphi_{\omega}(y)\}
for all
x\geq 0
. Note that in concrete applications, e.g. in the BMT-setting, it is natural to consider
x\in[0,+\infty)
in (
2.1
) but, however, naturally one should consider
\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}
in the definition since
\mathbb{R}
is the natural domain of definition for
\varphi_{\omega}
.

(b)

By definition
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
is convex and
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(0)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{-\varphi_{\omega}(y)\}
and so, if
\inf_{t\geq 0}\omega(t)=0
, then
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(0)=0
holds and if
\omega
is non-decreasing, then
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(0)=-\omega(0)
.

Thus, if
\omega
is normalized, then
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(0)=0=\omega(0)
and, moreover,
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
is non-decreasing on
[0,+\infty)
(see
(a)
). But, however, in general this property is not satisfied.

(c)

Naturally, one should restrict
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
to
[0,+\infty)
: Let
x<0
, then

\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{xy-\varphi_{\omega}(y)\}\geq\sup_{y\in(-\infty,0]}\{xy-\varphi_{\omega}(y)\}\geq\sup_{y\in(-\infty,0]}\{xy\}-\sup_{y\in(-\infty,0]}\{\varphi_{\omega}(y)\}=+\infty.

For this note that
\omega(t)\in[0,+\infty)
for all
t\in[0,1]
and so
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}=+\infty
on
(-\infty,0)
.

(d)

x\mapsto\frac{\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(x)}{x}
is non-decreasing on
(0,+\infty)
and
\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(x)}{x}=+\infty
: The first property follows because
y-\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(y)}{x_{1}}\leq y-\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(y)}{x_{2}}\Leftrightarrow\frac{1}{x_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{x_{1}}
for all
0<x_{1}\leq x_{2}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Concerning the second one assume that
\frac{\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(x)}{x}\leq C
for some
C>0
and all
x>0
, then by definition
x(y-C)\leq\varphi_{\omega}(y)
for all
x\geq 0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
. But this is impossible: Choose a fixed
y>C
and then let
x\rightarrow+\infty
.

(e)

(\omega_{3})
for
\omega
precisely means
\lim_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(y)}{y}=+\infty
and this property characterizes
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(x)<+\infty
for all
x\in[0,+\infty)
: Indeed, if
(\omega_{3})
is valid then for any
C>0
(large) we can find
d\in\mathbb{R}
, w.l.o.g.
d<0
, such that
\varphi_{\omega}(y)\geq yC+d\Leftrightarrow-d\geq yC-\varphi_{\omega}(y)
for all
y\geq 0
. Since
\varphi_{\omega}(y)\geq 0
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
and because
C>0
the same estimate holds for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(C)\leq-d
follows. Since
C>0
is arbitrary
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
is well-defined on
[0,+\infty)
. Conversely, if
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
is well-defined on
[0,+\infty)
, then for all
x\geq 0
there exists
C_{x}>0
such that
xy-\varphi_{\omega}(y)\leq C_{x}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
and so
x-\frac{C_{x}}{y}\leq\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(y)}{y}
for all
y>0
. When
x\geq 0
is fixed, then this estimate verifies
\liminf_{y\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(y)}{y}\geq x
and as
x\rightarrow+\infty
we infer
(\omega_{3})
for
\omega
.

(f)

Now consider the double conjugate
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}
: This function is by definition also convex and, by
(c)
, one has
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{xy-\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(y)\}=\sup_{y\geq 0}\{xy-\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(y)\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Comments
(d)
and
(e)
together imply that
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}
is always well-defined (on
\mathbb{R}
): Indeed,
(d)
gives
(\omega_{3})
for
\omega^{*}:=\varphi^{*}_{\omega}\circ\log
and so well-definedness on
[0,+\infty)
whereas on
(-\infty,0)
this follows since
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}
is non-decreasing.

This fact does also follow from
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}(x)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{xy-\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(y)\}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{xy-\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\{zy-\varphi_{\omega}(z)\}\}\leq\varphi_{\omega}(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
arbitrary.

Concerning the converse, note that
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}=\varphi_{\omega}
clearly implies
(\omega_{4})
for
\omega
. If
\omega
is in addition non-decreasing, then we show that
(\omega_{4})
implies
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}\geq\varphi_{\omega}
and so
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}=\varphi_{\omega}
. The following arguments are inspired by (the proof of)
[
23
, Prop. 1.6]
:

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed and since
\varphi_{\omega}
is convex one has that
u\mapsto\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(u)-\varphi_{\omega}(t)}{u-t}
is non-decreasing,
u\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u\neq t
. Since
\varphi_{\omega}
is non-decreasing this difference quotient is non-negative. So there exists
s=s(t)\geq 0
such that

\inf_{u>t}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(u)-\varphi_{\omega}(t)}{u-t}=(\varphi_{\omega})^{\prime}_{+}(t)\geq s\geq(\varphi_{\omega})^{\prime}_{-}(t)=\sup_{u<t}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(u)-\varphi_{\omega}(t)}{u-t}.

Multiplying these estimates with
u-t
we get that
s(u-t)\leq\varphi_{\omega}(u)-\varphi_{\omega}(t)
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
and note that for
u=t
this estimate is becoming trivial. Consequently,
\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\{s(u-t)-\varphi_{\omega}(u)\}\leq-\varphi_{\omega}(t)
holds which is equivalent to having
-\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\{s(u-t)-\varphi_{\omega}(u)\}\geq\varphi_{\omega}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The left-hand side is equal to
\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\{-\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\{s(u-t)-\varphi_{\omega}(u)\}\}=\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\{st-\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\{su-\varphi_{\omega}(u)\}\}=\varphi_{\omega}^{**}(t)
and we are done.

Note:
If
\omega
is normalized, non-decreasing and satisfies
(\omega_{4})
, then by the above and
(b)
we get
(0=)-\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(0)=\omega(0)=\omega(1)=\varphi_{\omega}(0)=\varphi^{**}_{\omega}(0)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\{-\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(y)\}=\sup_{y\geq 0}\{-\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(y)\}
and for having
(0=)-\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(0)=\sup_{y\geq 0}\{-\varphi^{*}_{\omega}(y)\}
it suffices to assume normalization since then
\varphi^{*}_{\omega}
is non-decreasing on
[0,+\infty)
; see again
(a)
and
(b)
.

Summarizing, assume that
\omega
is
non-decreasing
and such that
(\omega_{3})
holds. Then
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
, so
\omega
is a weight function in the notion of
[
12
]
(and
[
27
]
,
[
26
]
), and by
(a)-(e)
it follows that each
\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}
is a well-defined sequence of non-negative real numbers, each
\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}
is
log-convex
(i.e.
(M.1)
in
[
15
]
is valid),
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(W^{(\ell)}_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
for all
\ell>0
, and finally
W^{(\ell_{1})}_{p}\leq W^{(\ell_{2})}_{p}
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\ell_{1}\leq\ell_{2}
. Thus basically “non-decreasing” and
(\omega_{3})
for
\omega
are sufficient to introduce
\mathcal{M}_{\omega}
and this explains the notion
“matrix admissible”.

If
\omega
is also normalized, then each
\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}
is normalized; i.e.
1=W^{(\ell)}_{0}\leq W^{(\ell)}_{1}
. Note that, when
\omega
is not normalized, then one can always switch to an equivalent weight
\sigma
which is normalized and this notion of equivalence transfers in a precise manner to the corresponding associated weight matrices and which ensures the equality of the corresponding (matrix) weighted spaces; we refer to
[
28
, Sect. 6
(III)
,
(6.6)
]
.

Finally,
(\omega_{4})
gives the equality
\varphi^{**}_{\omega}=\varphi_{\omega}
and ensures the equivalence between
\omega
and (each)
\omega_{\mathbf{W}^{(\ell)}}
, see
[
24
, Lemma 5.7]
. When
\omega
is matrix admissible but not having
(\omega_{4})
then this equivalence is not true in general: This follows by the example constructed in Section
7
and recall that each associated weight function satisfies
(\omega_{4})
, see also Remark
7.6
.

2.10.
The (counter-)example by U. Franken

In
[
10
, Prop. 3]
the following statement has been shown:

Theorem 2.11
.

There exists a non-quasianalytic BMT-weight function
\omega
such that each continuous increasing function
\sigma
with
\sigma\geq\omega
cannot have simultaneously
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
and
(\omega_{\operatorname{nq}})
and hence cannot be a weight in the sense of Beurling-Björck.

3.
Weighted
L^{p}
-type spaces

3.1.
Basic definitions

For functions
\sigma,\tau:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
we write
\sigma\leq\tau
if
\sigma(x)\leq\tau(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and similarly all further relations from Section
2.3
transfer. We give the following general definition:

Definition 3.1
.

Consider the set of functions

\mathcal{W}:=\{\omega^{\ell}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,+\infty),\;\;\;\ell>0:\;\;\;\omega^{\ell_{1}}\leq\omega^{\ell_{2}},\;\forall\;0<\ell_{1}\leq\ell_{2}\}.

\mathcal{W}
is denoted as a
weight function matrix
and
\ell>0
is the crucial
matrix index parameter.

\mathcal{W}
is called

(*)

simple
if
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega\}
which means that
\omega^{\ell}\equiv\omega
for any
\ell>0
;

(*)

radial
or
isotropic
if
\omega^{\ell}(x)=\omega^{\ell}(|x|)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\ell>0
;

(*)

non-decreasing
if all
\omega^{\ell}
are non-decreasing which means
\omega^{\ell}(x)\leq\omega^{\ell}(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|x|\leq|y|
;

(*)

continuous
if all
\omega^{\ell}
are continuous,

(*)

unbounded
if

(3.1)

\forall\;\ell>0:\;\;\;\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega^{\ell}(x)=+\infty.

If
\mathcal{W}
is non-decreasing, then
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|=t}\omega^{\ell}(x)\leq\inf_{\epsilon>0}\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|=t+\epsilon}\omega^{\ell}(x)
for any
t\geq 0
fixed (and any
\ell>0
). Therefore, if
\mathcal{W}
is in addition continuous, then
\mathcal{W}
has to be radial; i.e. each
\omega^{\ell}
is constant on each sphere
|x|=t
.

If
\mathcal{W}
is radial, non-decreasing and unbounded, then
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\omega^{\ell}(x)=+\infty
for all
\ell>0
.

For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
set
L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C})
. Let
\mathcal{W}
be a weight function matrix and consider the weighted
L^{\infty}
-type space by

\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}}:=\left\{f\in L^{\infty}:\;\;\;\|f\|_{\infty,\omega^{\ell}}:=\operatorname{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}<+\infty\right\},

and define

(3.2)

\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}:=\bigcup_{\ell>0}\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}},\hskip 15.0pt\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{W})}:=\bigcap_{\ell>0}\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}},

endowed with their natural topologies. Moreover, for any
1\leq p<\infty
we assume that
\mathcal{W}
is
continuous
and for all (parameters)
\ell>0
let us set

\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}:=\left\{f\in L^{p}:\;\;\;\|f\|_{p,\omega^{\ell}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx\right)^{1/p}<+\infty\right\},

and put

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}:=\bigcup_{\ell>0}\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}},\hskip 15.0pt\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}:=\bigcap_{\ell>0}\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}.

Again, the spaces
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
and
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}
are endowed with their natural topologies. Here and throughout the whole paper to lighten notation we use the convention to write
f
instead of
[f]
; i.e. identify a representative of the equivalence class with the whole class.

Moreover, from now on write
[\cdot]
as a joint notation if we mean either
\{\cdot\}
or
(\cdot)
. A similar convention is also applied to the appearing growth conditions for weight function matrices in the next sections.
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
are called classes of
Roumieu-type
and
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}
of
Beurling-type.
Note that for the Beurling-type all large parameters
\ell>0
are becoming crucial, whereas for the Roumieu-type all small parameters
\ell
are important.

The following obvious and continuous inclusions are valid for any (continuous) weight function matrix:

\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}\subseteq L^{p},\;\;\;1\leq p\leq\infty.

Note that for any simple weight function matrix the Roumieu- and the Beurling-type coincide. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega^{\ell}>0\}

stabilizes above,
i.e. there exists
\ell_{0}>0
such that
\omega^{\ell}\equiv\omega^{\ell_{0}}
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, then
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}=\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\omega^{\ell_{0}})}
when identifying
\omega^{\ell_{0}}
with the simple matrix
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega^{\ell_{0}}\}
. Similarly, if
\mathcal{W}

stabilizes below,
i.e. there exists
\ell_{0}>0
such that
\omega^{\ell}\equiv\omega^{\ell_{0}}
for all
\ell\leq\ell_{0}
, then
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}=\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\omega^{\ell_{0}}\}}
. Therefore, in these cases one can omit all superfluous weight functions
\omega^{\ell}
,
\ell\neq\ell_{0}
, but the Roumieu- and the Beurling-type will not coincide in general.

3.2.
On the unboundedness of weight function matrices

Assume that
\omega^{\ell_{0}}=0
for some
\ell_{0}>0
, then
L^{p}
coincides as set and l.c.v.s. with
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
and, similarly, when
\omega^{\ell}=0
for all
\ell>0
then
L^{p}
coincides with
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}
(and with
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
).

More generally, if

(3.4)

\exists\;\ell_{0}>0:\;\;\;\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega^{\ell_{0}}(x)<+\infty,

then
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}=L^{p}
(as sets and l.c.v.s.) and, analogously, if

(3.5)

\forall\;\ell>0:\;\;\;\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega^{\ell}(x)<+\infty,

then
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}=L^{p}
(as sets and l.c.v.s.),
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Now let us prove the converse implication.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be a weight function matrix.

(i)

The inclusion (as sets)
L^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
implies (
3.4
) whereas
L^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{W})}
implies (
3.5
) for
\mathcal{W}
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{W}
is continuous, then the inclusion (as sets)
L^{p}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
for some/any
1\leq p<\infty
implies (
3.4
) and
L^{p}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}
implies (
3.5
) for
\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

(i)
By assumption the equality
L^{\infty}=\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{[\mathcal{W}]}
holds (as sets) and we show that this already implies (
3.4
) resp. (
3.5
) for
\mathcal{W}
: Otherwise, take the constant function
f\equiv 1
, and then, if (
3.4
) resp. (
3.5
) is violated, we get
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}=+\infty
for all
\ell>0
resp. for all sufficiently large
\ell>0
. But this implies
f\notin\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{[\mathcal{W}]}=L^{\infty}
, a contradiction.

(ii)
By assumption
L^{p}=\mathcal{L}^{p}_{[\mathcal{W}]}
holds (as sets) and again we proceed by contradiction.

Assume now that (
3.4
) is violated for
\mathcal{W}
. Then we can find a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\omega^{1/n}(x_{n})>n^{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. By the continuity of each weight function and the pointwise order of all weight functions in the matrix one can assume that
|x_{n}|\rightarrow+\infty
and, moreover, the continuity of (each)
\omega^{1/n}
also implies that

(3.6)

\forall\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\exists\;\delta_{n}>0\;\forall\;x\in I_{n}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y-x_{n}|\leq\delta_{n}\}:\;\;\;\omega^{1/n}(x)\geq n^{2}.

W.l.o.g. we can assume
\delta_{n}\leq 1
and that the sets (balls)
I_{n}
are pairwise disjoint. Then set
J_{n}:=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}1dx
and let
g^{p}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined as follows:

(3.7)

g^{p}(x):=\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}J_{n}}\right)^{1/p},\;\;\;\text{for}\;x\in I_{n},\hskip 15.0ptg^{p}(x):=0,\;\;\;\text{for}\;x\notin I_{n}.

First,
g^{p}\in L^{p}
follows because
g^{p}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
almost everywhere and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g^{p}(x)|^{p}dx=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}|g^{p}(x)|^{p}dx=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}J_{n}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}1dx=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}=1.

On the other hand let
\ell>0
be given (small), arbitrary but fixed. There exists
n_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\frac{1}{n}\leq\ell
for all
n\geq n_{\ell}
and so, by (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) and the pointwise order of the weight functions, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
with
n\geq n_{\ell}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
x\in I_{n}
we get
|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}\geq|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{1/n}(x)}\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}J_{n}}e^{n^{2}}
. Therefore, we can estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{n_{\ell}-1}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx+\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{+\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}|g(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=1}^{n_{\ell}-1}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx+\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}J_{n}}e^{n^{2}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}1dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{n_{\ell}-1}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x\in I_{n}}|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx+\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{n^{2}}}{2^{n}}=+\infty.

Since
\ell>0
was arbitrary, this computation verifies
g^{p}\notin\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}=L^{p}
, a contradiction.

If (
3.5
) is violated, then there exists some
\ell_{0}>0
such that
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega^{\ell}(x)=+\infty
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
and so we can find a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x_{n}|\rightarrow+\infty
and such that
\omega^{\ell_{0}+1/n}(x_{n})>n^{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Moreover, analogously to (
3.6
), we get

(3.8)

\forall\;n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\exists\;\delta_{n}>0\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;x\in I_{n}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y-x_{n}|\leq\delta_{n}\}:\;\;\;\omega^{\ell_{0}+1/n}(x)\geq n^{2}.

Again w.l.o.g. assume
\delta_{n}\leq 1
and that the sets
I_{n}
are pairwise disjoint. Let
g^{p}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined as in (
3.7
) and so
g^{p}\in L^{p}
follows as before. Now let
\ell>\ell_{0}
be given (large), arbitrary but fixed. There exists
n_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\ell_{0}+\frac{1}{n}\leq\ell
for all
n\geq n_{\ell}
and so, by (
3.8
), for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
with
n\geq n_{\ell}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
x\in I_{n}
we get
|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}\geq|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell_{0}+1/n}(x)}\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}J_{n}}e^{n^{2}}
. Using this and the same estimate of the crucial integral as before one infers

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g^{p}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx=+\infty,

and so, since
\ell>\ell_{0}
was arbitrary,
g^{p}\notin\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}
which yields a contradiction.
∎

Proposition
3.2
and the previous comments immediately imply the following:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be a weight function matrix.

(i)

(
3.4
) holds if and only if
L^{\infty}=\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
as sets and/or l.c.v.s. and (
3.5
) if and only if
L^{\infty}=\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{W})}
as sets and/or l.c.v.s.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{W}
is continuous, then (
3.4
) holds if and only if
L^{p}=\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
and (
3.5
) if and only if
L^{p}=\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}
and the equality holds for some/any
1\leq p<\infty
as sets and/or l.c.v.s.

Therefore, in order to treat proper (new) weighted subclasses of
L^{p}
it is natural to assume always that
\mathcal{W}
is
unbounded;
i.e. that (
3.1
) holds. Note that in the Beurling-type case, if (
3.5
) formally fails and (
3.1
) is (only) valid for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
(or
\ell>\ell_{0}
) large, then by definition of the spaces we can skip all
0<\ell<\ell_{0}
(or
0<\ell\leq\ell_{0}
) without changing the weighted class since we are interested in all large
\ell
. Hence (
3.1
) is natural for both types and analogous comments explain the notions for
\mathcal{W}
being radial, non-decreasing or continuous.

However, note that in the forthcoming proofs it is not required necessarily that the weight function matrices are unbounded. We finish this section by commenting on the (formal) opposite extreme.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{W}:=\{\omega^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be given and assume that there exists
\ell_{0}>0
such that
\omega^{\ell}\equiv+\infty
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
resp. such that
\omega^{\ell}\equiv+\infty
for all
\ell>0
(this notion means
\omega^{\ell}(x)=+\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
). In this case, formally, it makes sense to set
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}=\{0\}
resp.
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}=\{0\}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and this convention does not change when (only) assuming that for any
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
(resp. any
\ell>0
) one can find some
x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\omega^{\ell}(x)=+\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|x|\geq|x_{\ell}|
.

Conversely, we use the convention to identify
\{0\}
with the weighted
L^{p}
-type spaces when being formally defined via the previously described “non-standard weight matrices”.

3.3.
On the non-triviality of weighted
L^{p}
-type spaces

We verify that the defined weighted spaces are
non-trivial
under mild and natural assumptions on the (radial) weight function matrix
\mathcal{W}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be non-decreasing and radial.

(i)

It holds that
\{0\}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{W})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{W}
is in addition continuous, then
\{0\}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}
for all
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof.

(i)
If (
3.5
) is valid, then
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{W})}=L^{\infty}\neq\{0\}
by Theorem
3.3
.

If (
3.5
) is violated and since
\mathcal{W}
is non-decreasing we can find
\ell_{0}>0
such that
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\omega^{\ell}(x)=+\infty
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
.

We introduce the following functions:

\sigma^{\infty}(x):=\omega^{n+1}(x),\;\;\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1),\;\;\;n\in\mathbb{N},

and

(3.9)

\psi^{\infty}_{\mathcal{W}}(x):=e^{-\sigma^{\infty}(x)^{2}}.

So for any
\ell>0
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|x|\in[n,n+1)
one has
|\psi^{\infty}_{\mathcal{W}}(x)|e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}=e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)-(\omega^{n+1}(x))^{2}}
. Let
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
be fixed and hence for all
|x|\in[n,n+1)
with
n\geq\ell
we have
|\psi^{\infty}_{\mathcal{W}}(x)|e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}\leq e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)(1-\omega^{\ell}(x))}
and this last expression tends to
0
as
|x|\rightarrow+\infty
. Thus,

\forall\;\ell\geq\ell_{0}\;\exists\;C_{\ell}\geq 1\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;|\psi^{\infty}_{\mathcal{W}}(x)|e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}\leq C_{\ell},

and so
\psi^{\infty}_{\mathcal{W}}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{W})}
.

(ii)
If (
3.5
) holds, then
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}=L^{p}\neq\{0\}
by Theorem
3.3
.

If (
3.5
) fails, then since
\mathcal{W}
is non-decreasing there exists
\ell_{0}>0
such that
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\omega^{\ell}(x)=+\infty
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
. In particular, there exists
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
|x_{0}|\geq 1
, such that
\omega^{\ell}(x)>1
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|x|\geq|x_{0}|
. W.l.o.g. assume that
|x_{0}|=n_{0}=\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Now, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
with
n\geq n_{0}
we can find real numbers
a_{n}\geq 1
such that

(3.10)

(\omega^{n_{0}}(x))^{a_{n}}\geq 2(d+1)\log(1+|x|),\;\;\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1),\;n\geq n_{0}.

Indeed, for this it suffices to take
a_{n}:=\max\{1,\frac{\log(2(d+1)\log(2+n))}{\log(\omega^{n_{0}}(n))}\}
and (
3.10
) holds as well for all
\omega^{n}
,
n\geq n_{0}
. Moreover, for all
n\geq n_{0}
choose positive reals
b_{n}\geq 1
such that

(3.11)

(\omega^{n}(x))^{b_{n}}\geq 2\omega^{n}(x),\;\;\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1),\;n\geq n_{0}.

When enlarging
a_{n}
, if necessary, it suffices to take

b_{n}=\frac{a_{n}\log(\omega^{n}(n+1))+\log(2)}{\log(\omega^{n}(n))}(\geq\max\{1,\frac{\log(\omega^{n}(n+1))+\log(2)}{\log(\omega^{n}(n))}\}).

Finally, in order to complete the definition one can set
a_{n}:=1
and
b_{n}:=1
for
0\leq n<n_{0}
.

Now introduce the auxiliary weight function

\sigma^{p}(x):=(\omega^{n+1}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}},\;\;\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1),\;n\in\mathbb{N},

and set

(3.12)

\psi^{p}_{\mathcal{W}}(x):=e^{-p^{-1}\sigma^{p}(x)}.

Then, for any
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
(fixed) we estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\psi^{p}_{\mathcal{W}}(x)|^{p}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1)}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)-(\omega^{n+1}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1)}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)-(\omega^{n+1}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}}dx+\sum_{n=\ell}^{+\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1)}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)-(\omega^{n+1}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1)}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)-(\omega^{n+1}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}}dx+\sum_{n=\ell}^{+\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1)}e^{\omega^{n}(x)-(\omega^{n}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1)}e^{\omega^{\ell}(x)-(\omega^{n+1}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}}dx+\sum_{n=\ell}^{+\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|x|\in[n,n+1)}\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{d+1}}dx<+\infty.

The last inequality is valid since
\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{2(d+1)}}\leq\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{2})^{(d+1)}}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For the second last one note that
e^{\omega^{n}(x)-(\omega^{n}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}}\leq\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{2(d+1)}}
holds on the intervals under consideration because for
|x|\in[n,n+1)
,
n\geq\ell_{0}=n_{0}
, via the choices of
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
in (
3.10
) and (
3.11
):

(\omega^{n}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}-\omega^{n}(x)\geq(\omega^{n}(x))^{a_{n}b_{n}}-(\omega^{n}(x))^{a_{n}}\geq(\omega^{n}(x))^{a_{n}}\geq(\omega^{\ell_{0}}(x))^{a_{n}}\geq 2(d+1)\log(1+|x|).

Consequently, since
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
was arbitrary it follows that
\psi^{p}_{\mathcal{W}}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}
.
∎

3.4.
Stability under translation

For concrete applications it is important to know whether weighted spaces satisfy certain stability properties. Concerning the classes introduced above we investigate
invariance w.r.t. translations.
This property is somehow natural and crucially required in the proofs of the main characterizing results in the next section.

Notation:
For given
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
set
f_{x_{0}}:=f(x-x_{0})
and consider the
translation operator

T_{x_{0}}:f\mapsto f_{x_{0}}
.

The main statement in this context is the following result:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be weight function matrices.

(i)

If the matrices are related by

(3.13)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;n>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell}(x+y)\leq\sigma^{n}(x)+\sigma^{n}(y)+L,

then

\displaystyle\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;n>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{n}}:\;\;\;\|T_{x_{0}}(f)\|_{\infty,\tau^{\ell}}\leq e^{L}e^{\sigma^{n}(x_{0})}\|f\|_{\infty,\sigma^{n}}.

If both matrices are in addition continuous, then for any
1\leq p<\infty
we get

\displaystyle\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;n>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma^{n}}:\;\;\;\|T_{x_{0}}(f)\|^{p}_{p,\tau^{\ell}}\leq e^{L}e^{\sigma^{n}(x_{0})}\|f\|^{p}_{p,\sigma^{n}}.

(ii)

If the matrices are related by

(3.14)

\forall\;n>0\;\exists\;\ell>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell}(x+y)\leq\sigma^{n}(x)+\sigma^{n}(y)+L,

then

\displaystyle\forall\;n>0\;\exists\;\ell>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{n}}:\;\;\;\|T_{x_{0}}(f)\|_{\infty,\tau^{\ell}}\leq e^{L}e^{\sigma^{n}(x_{0})}\|f\|_{\infty,\sigma^{n}}.

If both matrices are in addition continuous, then for any
1\leq p<\infty
we get

\displaystyle\forall\;n>0\;\exists\;\ell>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma^{n}}:\;\;\;\|T_{x_{0}}(f)\|^{p}_{p,\tau^{\ell}}\leq e^{L}e^{\sigma^{n}(x_{0})}\|f\|^{p}_{p,\sigma^{n}}.

Proposition
3.6
implies the continuity of (each) operator
T_{x_{0}}
between weighted
L^{p}
-type classes and note:

(*)

The choice for the new index
n
is only depending on given
\ell
via (
3.13
) resp. the index
\ell
is depending on given
n
via (
3.14
) and hence not on the translation parameter
x_{0}
. Moreover, it is also not depending on the choice of the representative of the equivalence class.

(*)

The statement becomes unclear if
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is replaced by some open and bounded
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(*)

The special case
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}
, i.e. (
3.13
) for
\mathcal{S}
, implies that the classes
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, are translation invariant and each
T_{x_{0}}
is acting continuously on the corresponding weighted space. And (
3.14
) for
\mathcal{S}
implies that
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, is translation invariant and each
T_{x_{0}}
is acting continuously.

Proof.

We only prove
(i)
in detail since
(ii)
is analogous. When taking into account (
3.13
) we get for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\|T_{x_{0}}(f)\|_{\infty,\tau^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\|f_{x_{0}}\|_{\infty,\tau^{\ell}}=\operatorname{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f_{x_{0}}(x)|e^{\tau^{\ell}(x)}=\operatorname{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x-x_{0})|e^{\tau^{\ell}(x)}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ess}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|e^{\tau^{\ell}(y+x_{0})}\leq e^{L}e^{\sigma^{n}(x_{0})}\operatorname{ess}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|e^{\sigma^{n}(y)}=e^{L}e^{\sigma^{n}(x_{0})}\|f\|_{\infty,\sigma^{n}}.

Similarly, for all
1\leq p<\infty
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\|T_{x_{0}}(f)\|^{p}_{p,\tau^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\|f_{x_{0}}\|^{p}_{p,\tau^{\ell}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f_{x_{0}}(x)|^{p}e^{\tau^{\ell}(x)}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x-x_{0})|^{p}e^{\tau^{\ell}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\tau^{\ell}(y+x_{0})}dy\leq e^{L}e^{\sigma^{n}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\sigma^{n}(y)}dy=e^{L}e^{\sigma^{n}(x_{0})}\|f\|^{p}_{p,\sigma^{n}}.

∎

We continue with an auxiliary technical result for radial weight matrices which should be compared with
(f)
in Section
2.4
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be non-decreasing and radial weight function matrices. Consider the following (mixed) assertions:

(i)

The matrices are related by

(3.15)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;n>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell}(2t)\leq\sigma^{n}(t)+L.

(ii)

The matrices are related by (
3.13
).

(iii)

The matrices are related by

(3.16)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;n>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell}(2t)\leq 2\sigma^{n}(t)+L.

Then the implications
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
are valid. The proof shows that we can choose for given
\ell
in each assertion the same parameter
n
and the assumption “non-decreasing” is only used to show the first implication. The analogous statement holds when “
\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;n>0
” is replaced by “
\forall\;n>0\;\exists\;\ell>0
” and involving (
3.14
); for future reference we only mention explicitly

(3.17)

\forall\;n>0\;\exists\;\ell>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell}(2t)\leq\sigma^{n}(t)+L.

Proof.

Since
\tau^{\ell}
and
\sigma^{n}
are non-decreasing, by (
3.15
) one has
\tau^{\ell}(s+t)\leq\tau^{\ell}(2\max\{s,t\})\leq\sigma^{n}(\max\{s,t\})+L\leq\sigma^{n}(s)+\sigma^{n}(t)+L
for all
s,t\geq 0
. On the other hand, in (
3.13
) we set
s=t
and immediately get (
3.16
). The second part follows analogously.
∎

This technical lemma motivates the next comment:

Remark 3.8
.

In view of Lemma

4.
Characterization of inclusion relations for weighted
L^{p}
-type spaces

The aim is to prove the characterization of inclusion relations between weighted
L^{p}
-type spaces defined by weight function matrices when assuming mild standard growth requirements. In order to proceed, first let us introduce the crucial growth relations. Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
,
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be given and consider

(4.1)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;n>0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell}(x)\leq\sigma^{n}(x)+C,

and

(4.2)

\forall\;n>0\;\exists\;\ell>0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell}(x)\leq\sigma^{n}(x)+C.

We write
\mathcal{S}(\preceq)\mathcal{T}
if (
4.1
) is valid and
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
if (
4.2
) holds and using this notation the goal is to verify the following main characterizing statements:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be weight function matrices and assume that both matrices satisfy (
3.13
). Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The matrices are related by
\mathcal{S}(\preceq)\mathcal{T}
.

(ii)

The inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{S})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{T})}
holds as sets.

(iii)

The continuous inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{S})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{T})}
holds.

If both matrices are also continuous, then analogous equivalences are valid when the symbol/functor
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
is replaced by
\mathcal{L}^{p}
for some/any
1\leq p<\infty
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be weight function matrices and assume that both matrices satisfy (
3.14
). Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

The matrices are related by
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
.

(ii)

The inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
holds as sets.

(iii)

The continuous inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
holds.

If both matrices are also continuous, then analogous equivalences are valid when the symbol/functor
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
is replaced by
\mathcal{L}^{p}
for some/any
1\leq p<\infty
.

First, the next statement is immediate by the defining system of seminorms and the introduced growth relations before and it verifies
(i)\Rightarrow(ii),(iii)
in Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be weight function matrices.

(i)

If
\mathcal{S}(\preceq)\mathcal{T}
, then

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;n>0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{n}}:\;\;\;\|f\|_{\infty,\tau^{\ell}}\leq e^{C}\|f\|_{\infty,\sigma^{n}},

and if
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
, then

\forall\;n>0\;\exists\;\ell>0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{n}}:\;\;\;\|f\|_{\infty,\tau^{\ell}}\leq e^{C}\|f\|_{\infty,\sigma^{n}}.

Thus
\mathcal{S}[\preceq]\mathcal{T}
implies
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{[\mathcal{S}]}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{[\mathcal{T}]}
with continuous inclusion.

(ii)

If both
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are in addition continuous, then analogously
\mathcal{S}[\preceq]\mathcal{T}
implies
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{[\mathcal{S}]}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{[\mathcal{T}]}
with continuous inclusion for some/any
1\leq p<\infty
. (The only difference compared with
(i)
is that in the estimates
e^{C}
has to be replaced by
e^{C/p}
.)

The aim now is to verify the (expected) characterization of inclusion relations in terms of
(\preceq)
and
\{\preceq\}
between weight function matrices. For this we have to impose mild extra conditions on the weights; in Section
4.1
we present for both the Beurling- and the Roumieu-type an abstract functional analytic argument (see Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
). This approach, in particular the Beurling-type result, has been inspired by
[
3
, Thm. 1.3.18]
. Afterwards, in Section
4.2
, we give for the Roumieu-type a different constructive approach involving special (optimal) functions.

In the proofs of Propositions
4.5
and
4.6
the
non-triviality
of
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{[\mathcal{S}]}
is required and in the isotropic setting in view of Lemma
3.5
it suffices to assume in addition that
\mathcal{S}
is non-decreasing. However, by taking into account the convention from Remark
3.4
we comment now on the fact that, if these spaces are trivial, then the implications follow automatically.

Remark 4.4
.

When
\{0\}=\mathcal{L}^{p}_{[\mathcal{S}]}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{[\mathcal{T}]}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, then the inclusion is trivial and the convention from Remark
3.4
gives that
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
corresponds to the weight function matrix satisfying
\sigma^{\ell}\equiv+\infty
for all
\ell>0
sufficiently large in the Beurling-case resp.
\sigma^{\ell}\equiv+\infty
for any
\ell>0
in the Roumieu-case. And therefore, by recalling (
4.1
) resp. (
4.2
), the relation
\mathcal{S}(\preceq)\mathcal{T}
resp.
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
is formally trivial.

Finally, by using the convention
+\infty=+\infty
, the relations formally even follow when in addition
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{[\mathcal{T}]}=\{0\}
.

4.1.
Proof of the main characterizing results

We start with the
Beurling-type.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be weight function matrices and assume that both matrices satisfy (
3.13
).

(i)

If both matrices are also continuous, then the (continuous) inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{T})}
(for some/any
1\leq p<\infty
) implies
\mathcal{S}(\preceq)\mathcal{T}
.

(ii)

The (continuous) inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{S})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{T})}
implies
\mathcal{S}(\preceq)\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

(i)
First, by (
3.13
) we get:

\displaystyle\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;L\geq 1\;\forall\;y,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:

\displaystyle\tau^{\ell}(x_{0})=\tau^{\ell}(x_{0}+y-y)\leq\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(x_{0}+y)+\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(-y)+L.

Thus, for any
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{T})}\subseteq L^{p}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds that

\|f_{x_{0}}\|^{p}_{p,\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(y+x_{0})}dy\geq e^{-L}e^{\tau^{\ell}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{-\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(-y)}dy,

and clearly
A_{1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{-\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(-y)}dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}dy<+\infty
by assumption.

Second, since both
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}
and
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{T})}
are Fréchet spaces (w.r.t. their natural topologies) we have that the inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{T})}
is continuous (in any case); see e.g.
[
11
, Prop. 4.5 & Rem. 4.6]
. Consequently,

(4.3)

\forall\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime\prime}>0\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}:\;\;\;\|f\|_{p,\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}}\leq A\|f\|_{p,\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}}.

Now, in view of Remark
4.4
we can assume that there exists
0\neq f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}
and
(i)
in Proposition
3.6
yields
f_{x_{0}}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}
for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The idea is to apply (
4.3
) to the family
\{f_{x_{0}}:x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
.

Let
\ell>0
be given, arbitrary but from now on fixed. Let
\ell^{\prime}>0
be the index appearing in the estimate in the first step and being related to
\ell
via (
3.13
). Then apply (
4.3
) to
\ell^{\prime}
and let
\ell^{\prime\prime}
be the index subject to
\ell^{\prime}
in this property.

Finally, when applying (
3.13
) to
\mathcal{S}
and to the index
\ell^{\prime\prime}
then there exist some
\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}>0
and
L_{1}\geq 1
such that for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle\|f_{x_{0}}\|^{p}_{p,\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(y+x_{0})}dy\leq e^{L_{1}}e^{\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}}(y)}dy.

Note that
A_{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}}(y)}dy<+\infty
since
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{S})}
.

Combining everything, so far we have shown

\displaystyle\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}>0\;\exists\;A,L,L_{1}\geq 1\;\exists\;A_{1},A_{2}>0\;\forall\;x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:

\displaystyle\tau^{\ell}(x_{0})\leq L-\log(A_{1})+p\log(A)+L_{1}+\log(A_{2})+\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}}(x_{0});

so (
4.1
) is verified between the indices
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}
and with
C:=L-\log(A_{1})+p\log(A)+L_{1}+\log(A_{2})
; i.e. relation
\mathcal{S}(\preceq)\mathcal{T}
.

(ii)
Similarly, since
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\sigma)}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\tau)}
are Fréchet spaces (w.r.t. their natural topology), by
[
11
, Prop. 4.5 & Rem. 4.6]
we have again that the inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{S})}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{T})}
is continuous in any case. Thus

(4.4)

\forall\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime\prime}>0\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{S})}:\;\;\;\|f\|_{\infty,\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}}\leq A\|f\|_{\infty,\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}},

and we apply this crucial estimate again to the family
\{f_{x_{0}}:x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
corresponding to a fixed
0\neq f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\sigma)}
; recall Remark
4.4
. The rest follows analogously as in
(i)
by involving (
3.13
) and
(i)
in Proposition
3.6
for both matrices and note that
A_{1}:=\operatorname{ess}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|e^{-\tau^{\ell}(-y)}\leq\operatorname{ess}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|<+\infty
for all
\ell>0
because
f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{T})}
and
A_{2}:=\operatorname{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|e^{\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}}(y)}<+\infty
for any
\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}>0
because even
f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{S})}
(by assumption). Therefore,
\mathcal{S}(\preceq)\mathcal{T}
follows as before.
∎

The next result deals with the analogous result for the
Roumieu case.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be given weight function matrices and assume that both matrices satisfy (
3.14
).

(i)

If both matrices are also continuous, then the (continuous) inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
for some/any
1\leq p<\infty
implies
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
.

(ii)

The (continuous) inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
implies
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

(i)
We proceed analogously as in Proposition
4.5
: By the (continuous) inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
and since we are dealing with (LB)-spaces, by
de Wilde’s closed graph theorem and Grothendieck’s factorization theorem
we obtain

(4.5)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma^{\ell}}:\;\;\;\|f\|_{p,\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}}\leq A\|f\|_{p,\sigma^{\ell}}.

Fix an index
\ell_{0}>0
and
0\neq f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma^{\ell_{0}}}
(recall Remark
4.4
). Moreover, assumption (
3.14
) for
\mathcal{S}
and
(ii)
in Proposition
3.6
yield the existence of some
\ell>0
such that
f_{x_{0}}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma^{\ell}}
for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. (The indices
\ell_{0}
and
\ell
are related by (
3.14
).) Indeed, the proof of
(ii)
in Proposition
3.6
gives

\displaystyle\|f_{x_{0}}\|^{p}_{p,\sigma^{\ell}}\leq e^{L}e^{\sigma^{\ell_{0}}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\sigma^{\ell_{0}}(y)}dy.

Note that
A_{1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\sigma^{\ell_{0}}(y)}dy<+\infty
since
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma^{\ell_{0}}}
. We apply (
4.5
) to the family
\{f_{x_{0}}:x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
and to this index
\ell
. Moreover, when using (
3.14
) for
\mathcal{T}
, then analogously as in the proof above for some index
\ell^{\prime}
(being related to
\ell
via (
4.5
)) we can find some index
\ell^{\prime\prime}>0
and
L_{1}\geq 1
and get for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\|f_{x_{0}}\|^{p}_{p,\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(y+x_{0})}dy\geq e^{-L_{1}}e^{\tau^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{-\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(-y)}dy.

Clearly
A_{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}e^{-\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(-y)}dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|^{p}dy<+\infty
since
f\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
(by assumption). Combining everything, so far we have shown

\displaystyle\forall\;\ell_{0}>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime\prime}>0\,\exists\;A,L,L_{1}\geq 1\;\exists\;A_{1},A_{2}>0\;\forall\;x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:

\displaystyle\tau^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(x_{0})\leq L_{1}-\log(A_{2})+p\log(A)+L+\log(A_{1})+\sigma^{\ell_{0}}(x_{0}),

which yields (
4.2
) and so
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
.

(ii)
Similarly, by the continuous inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
and since we are dealing with (LB)-spaces we obtain

(4.6)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{\ell}}:\;\;\;\|f\|_{\infty,\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}}\leq A\|f\|_{\infty,\sigma^{\ell}}.

Again we fix an index
\ell_{0}>0
and
0\neq f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{\ell_{0}}}
(recall Remark
4.4
) and
(ii)
in Proposition
3.6
yields the existence of some
\ell>0
such that
f_{x_{0}}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\sigma^{\ell}}
for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. (Again the indices
\ell_{0}
and
\ell
are related by (
3.14
).)

We apply (
4.6
) to the family
\{f_{x_{0}}:x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
and to this index
\ell
. Then follow the estimates in
(i)
using the same notation (for the indices) and by involving
(ii)
in Proposition
3.6
and (
3.14
); see also
(ii)
in Proposition
4.5
. Therefore
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
holds and note that here
A_{1}:=\operatorname{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|e^{\sigma^{\ell_{0}}(y)}<+\infty
because
f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\sigma^{\ell_{0}}}
and
A_{2}:=\operatorname{ess}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|e^{-\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(-y)}\leq\operatorname{ess}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(y)|<+\infty
since
f\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
(by assumption).
∎

4.2.
Alternative proofs for the Roumieu-type

We present now different techniques and ideas for the Roumieu case to show
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
in Theorem
4.2
. Here we are not using functional analytic tools but involve special (optimal) functions. One has to work with different assumptions for the weight function matrices but the new introduced optimal functions can be used in other related contexts as well.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be a weight function matrix. Then set

(4.7)

\theta^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}(x):=e^{-p^{-1}\omega^{\ell}(x)},\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\ell>0,\;1\leq p<\infty,

and

(4.8)

\theta^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}}(x):=e^{-\omega^{\ell}(x)},\;\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\ell>0.

The extreme case
\omega^{\ell}=0
gives
\theta^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}=1=\theta^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}}
. In order to proceed, we require the following relations between the functions
\omega^{\ell}
:

(4.9)

\exists\;\ell^{\prime}_{0}>\ell_{0}>0:\;\;\;\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega^{\ell^{\prime}_{0}}(x)-\omega^{\ell_{0}}(x)=+\infty,

(4.10)

\exists\;\ell^{\prime}_{0}>\ell_{0}>0:\;\;\;\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\omega^{\ell^{\prime}_{0}}(x)-\omega^{\ell_{0}}(x)=+\infty,

and

(4.11)

\exists\;a>1\;\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;b\in\mathbb{R}\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;\omega^{\ell}(x)-\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)\geq a\log(1+|x|)+b.

Remark 4.7
.

We point out:

(*)

If (
4.9
), (
4.10
) holds for some indices
\ell^{\prime}_{0}>\ell_{0}
, then also for all
\ell^{\prime},\ell
satisfying
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell^{\prime}_{0}>\ell_{0}\geq\ell>0
.

(*)

By iteration it is clear that (
4.11
) is equivalent to

(4.12)

\forall\;a>1\;\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;b\in\mathbb{R}\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;\omega^{\ell}(x)-\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)\geq a\log(1+|x|)+b.

Note that in (
4.11
), (
4.12
) necessarily the indices have to satisfy the relation
\ell>\ell^{\prime}
.

(*)

One shall note that (
4.9
), (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) are crucial for the techniques in this section but they can never be satisfied for the
simple
weight function matrix
\mathcal{W}:=\{\omega\}
. On the other hand, the estimates in (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) hold for any sub-additive
\omega
and hence the main results Theorems
4.1
,
4.2
and Proposition
3.6
can be applied to the simple case, too.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be a weight function matrix.

(i)

We have

\forall\;\ell\geq\ell^{\prime}>0:\;\;\;\theta^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{W}
satisfies (
4.9
) with indices
\ell^{\prime}_{0}>\ell_{0}
, then

\forall\;\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell^{\prime}_{0}>\ell_{0}\geq\ell>0:\;\;\;\theta^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}}\notin\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}}\supseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{W})}.

(iii)

Assume that
\mathcal{W}
is continuous and (
4.11
) holds. Then for any
1\leq p<\infty
we get:

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0:\;\;\;\theta^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{W}\}}.

(iv)

Assume that
\mathcal{W}
is continuous and (
4.10
) holds. Then for any
1\leq p<\infty
we get:

\exists\;\ell_{0}^{\prime}>\ell_{0}\;\forall\;\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell^{\prime}_{0}>\ell_{0}\geq\ell>0:\;\;\;\theta^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}\notin\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}}\supseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{(\mathcal{W})}.

Proof.

(i)
This is clear since
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\theta^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}}(x)|e^{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)-\omega^{\ell}(x)}\leq 1
.

(ii)
According to the given indices and the assumption it holds that
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\theta^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}}(x)|e^{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)-\omega^{\ell}(x)}\geq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}_{0}}(x)-\omega^{\ell_{0}}(x)}=+\infty
; see Remark
4.7
.

(iii)
We apply (
4.12
) to (some fixed)
a\geq 2(d+1)
and hence get for each
\ell>0
some index
\ell^{\prime}>0
such that

\|\theta^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}\|^{p}_{p,\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)-\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx\leq e^{-b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{a}}dx.

The last integral is finite by the choice
a\geq 2(d+1)
.

(iv)
Let
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell^{\prime}_{0}>\ell_{0}\geq\ell>0
be given, with
\ell^{\prime}_{0}
and
\ell_{0}
denoting the parameters appearing in (
4.10
) and then

\|\theta^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}\|^{p}_{p,\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)-\omega^{\ell}(x)}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\omega^{\ell^{\prime}_{0}}(x)-\omega^{\ell_{0}}(x)}dx=+\infty.

(However, in general it is not clear if already (
4.9
) implies this estimate.)
∎

Note:

(*)

(i)
and
(iii)
in Lemma
4.8
yield, in particular, the non-triviality of the corresponding weighted Roumieu-type spaces even in the anisotropic setting. This should be compared with Lemma
3.5
: There it has been assumed that
\mathcal{W}
is non-decreasing, whereas
(i)
in Lemma
4.8
requires no further assumption on
\mathcal{W}
and
(iii)
there requires information on the difference between the growth of the weight functions which fails in the simple case; recall Remark
4.7
.

(*)

By
(ii)
and
(iv)
in Lemma
4.8
we see that in general
\theta^{\infty}_{\omega^{\ell}}
resp.
\theta^{p}_{\omega^{\ell}}
does not belong to the corresponding Beurling-type spaces and so the proofs of the next results are becoming unclear in this setting.

Using this preparation we prove the following variant of
(ii)
in Proposition
4.6
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be weight function matrices.

Then the inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
(as sets) implies
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

By
(i)
in Lemma
4.8
and
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
we get
\theta^{\infty}_{\sigma^{\ell}}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
for any
\ell>0
. Hence

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0:\;\;\;\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)-\sigma^{\ell}(x)}<+\infty,

and so

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;D\geq 0\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)\leq\sigma^{\ell}(x)+D,

which is precisely
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
.
∎

Note:
This statement is more general than
(ii)
in Proposition
4.6
since no stability under translation is required.

Moreover, the established technique immediately gives the next result:

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be weight function matrices. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

We have the continuous inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{T})}
.

(ii)

We have the inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{(\mathcal{T})}
as sets.

(iii)

The matrices satisfy

(4.13)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\forall\;\ell^{\prime}>0\;\exists\;D\geq 1\;\forall\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell}(x)\leq\sigma^{\ell^{\prime}}(x)+D;

and let us abbreviate this relation by
\mathcal{S}\vartriangleleft\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
follows immediately by definition of the spaces and
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
is clear. For
(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
follow the proof of Proposition (
4.9
) and replace “
\exists\;\ell^{\prime}>0
” by “
\forall\;\ell^{\prime}>0
”.
∎

Finally, we treat the
L^{p}
-Roumieu-type spaces and so a variant of
(i)
in Proposition
4.6
. Here, due to technical reasons we focus on isotropic weight matrices and, before stating the next result, we introduce the following growth relations for
\mathcal{W}=\{\omega^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
:

(4.14)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell_{1}>0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\omega^{\ell_{1}}(t+1)\leq\omega^{\ell}(t)+C,

(4.15)

\forall\;\ell_{1}>0\;\exists\;\ell>0\;\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\omega^{\ell_{1}}(t+1)\leq\omega^{\ell}(t)+C.

(*)

Similarly, as pointed out in Remark
3.8
(see (
3.19
) resp. (
3.18
)), one could consider the expression
C\omega^{\ell}(t)+C
on the right-hand side. However, the additional appearance of the multiplicative constant
C
is not appropriate in general; see again Section
5.1
.

(*)

(
3.14
) implies (
4.14
) and (
3.13
) implies (
4.15
) when we set
s:=1
.

(*)

If
\mathcal{W}
is non-decreasing, then equivalently in (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) we can replace “
\forall\;t\in\mathbb{N}
” by “
\forall\;t\geq 0
”; we only treat (
4.14
):

When
t\geq 0
with
t\notin\mathbb{N}
is given we chose
s_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
s_{t}<t<s_{t}+1
and get by iteration
\omega^{\ell_{2}}(s_{t}+1)\leq\omega^{\ell_{2}}(s_{t}+2)\leq\omega^{\ell_{1}}(s_{t}+1)+C\leq\omega^{\ell}(s_{t})+C_{1}+C\leq\omega^{\ell}(t)+C_{1}+C
. Hence it suffices to replace
C
by
C_{1}+C
and
\ell_{1}
by
\ell_{2}(\leq\ell_{1})
to ensure (
4.14
) for all
t\geq 0
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\sigma^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
and
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau^{\ell}:\ell>0\}
be continuous and non-decreasing (and hence radial) weight function matrices. Moreover, assume that
\mathcal{S}
satisfies (
4.11
) and that either
\mathcal{S}
or

has (

).

Then the inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
(as sets) for some/any

implies
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

By assumption (
4.11
),
(iii)
in Lemma

and the inclusion
\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{S}\}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\{\mathcal{T}\}}
(as sets) we obtain

for all
\ell>0
and so

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell_{1}>0:\;\;\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\theta^{p}_{\sigma^{\ell}}(x)|^{p}e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(x)}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(x)-\sigma^{\ell}(x)}dx<+\infty.

Let now
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
n\leq|x|<n+1
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(x)-\sigma^{\ell}(x)}\geq e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(n)-\sigma^{\ell}(n+1)}
by using the assumption that both matrices are non-decreasing. Next recall that the volume of the
d
-dimensional sphere with radius
r
is given by
V_{d}(r)=\frac{\pi^{d/2}r^{d}}{\Gamma(1+d/2)}
(with
\Gamma
denoting the Gamma-function) and therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:n\leq|x|<n+1}1dx

\displaystyle=\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|<n+1}1dx-\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:n\leq|x|}1dx=\frac{\pi^{d/2}((n+1)^{d}-n^{d})}{\Gamma(1+d/2)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(1+d/2)}=:C_{d},

because
(n+1)^{d}-n^{d}\geq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Then we estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(x)-\sigma^{\ell}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:n\leq|x|<n+1}e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(x)-\sigma^{\ell}(x)}dx\geq\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(n)-\sigma^{\ell}(n+1)}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:n\leq|x|<n+1}1dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{d}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(n)-\sigma^{\ell}(n+1)}.

Combining this estimate with (

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}e^{\tau^{\ell_{1}}(n)-\sigma^{\ell}(n+1)}=0
or equivalently
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\tau^{\ell_{1}}(n)-\sigma^{\ell}(n+1)=-\infty
. Summarizing, so far we have shown

(4.17)

\forall\;\ell>0\;\exists\;\ell_{1}>0\;\exists\;D\geq 0\;\forall\;n\in\mathbb{N}:\;\;\;\tau^{\ell_{1}}(n)\leq\sigma^{\ell}(n+1)+D.

Now assume that (
4.14
) for
\mathcal{S}
holds and let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
n\leq|x|<n+1
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then when iterating this property we estimate by

\displaystyle\tau^{\ell_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{\ell_{1}}(n+1)\leq\sigma^{\ell}(n+2)+D\leq\sigma^{\ell^{\prime}}(n+1)+C+D\leq\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(n)+C_{1}+C+D

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{\ell^{\prime\prime}}(x)+C_{1}+C+D,

thus
\mathcal{S}\{\preceq\}\mathcal{T}
is verified (between the indices
\ell^{\prime\prime}
and
\ell_{1}
).

If
\mathcal{T}
satisfies (
4.14
), then similarly by (
4.17
) for any

with
n\leq|x|<n+1
for some

we have

\displaystyle\tau^{\ell^{\prime\prime}_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{\ell^{\prime\prime}_{1}}(n+1)\leq\tau^{\ell^{\prime}_{1}}(n)+C\leq\tau^{\ell_{1}}(n-1)+C_{1}+C\leq\sigma^{\ell}(n)+C_{1}+C+D

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{\ell}(x)+C_{1}+C+D.

And for

with

with

is verified (between the indices

and

7.
On the failure of the convexity condition
(\omega_{4})

In this final section we provide the technical construction of a function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
satisfying the following properties:

(I)

\omega
is normalized, continuous, non-decreasing and
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
.

(II
)

\omega
satisfies
(\omega_{3})
.

(III)

\gamma(\omega)=+\infty
holds. This implies that
\omega
also has
(\omega_{\operatorname{snq}})
,
(\omega_{1})
, and that
\omega
is equivalent to the continuous concave resp. subadditive function

; see Lemma

and Remark

. In fact, we extend (

) and even show that

is

]
and (

\forall\;u>0:\;\;\;\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(tu)}{\omega(t)}=1.

There does not exist any function

such that
\sigma
and
\omega
are equivalent and such that

is convex. Thus, in particular,

is not convex. In view of

we have that equivalence is described by

-growth relations as

; see Section

.

(V)

There exist (uncountable) infinitely many pairwise non-equivalent functions satisfying

.

These properties yield:

(\mathfrak{C}_{1})

We collect several preparatory results which are needed in the proof of the construction in the next section.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be arbitrary. Assume that there exists
\sigma:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
such that
\omega
and
\sigma
are equivalent and such that

is convex (on

), i.e.

has

. Then
\omega
has to satisfy:

(7.1)

\exists\;A\geq 1\;\forall\;x,y\in\mathbb{R}\;\forall\;0\leq t\leq 1:\;\;\;\varphi_{\omega}(ty+(1-t)x)\leq At\varphi_{\omega}(y)+A(1-t)\varphi_{\omega}(x)+A.

Proof.

The equivalence between
\omega
and
\sigma
gives that

\exists\;B\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;-1+B^{-1}\sigma(t)\leq\omega(t)\leq B\sigma(t)+B,

and by taking

these estimates transfer to the corresponding functions
\varphi_{\omega}
,
\varphi_{\sigma}
as well (with the same choice for
B
and for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
).

Thus we can estimate as follows for all

and
0\leq t\leq 1
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\omega}(ty+(1-t)x)\leq B\varphi_{\sigma}(ty+(1-t)x)+B\leq Bt\varphi_{\sigma}(y)+B(1-t)\varphi_{\sigma}(x)+B

\displaystyle\leq B^{2}t\varphi_{\omega}(y)+B^{2}t+B^{2}(1-t)\varphi_{\omega}(x)+B^{2}(1-t)+B=B^{2}t\varphi_{\omega}(y)+B^{2}(1-t)\varphi_{\omega}(x)+B^{2}+B.

This verifies (
7.1
) for the choice
A:=B^{2}+B
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
satisfies

(7.2)

\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)>1\Leftrightarrow\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi_{\omega}(t)>1,

and such that

(7.3)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\exists\;0<t_{j}<\frac{1}{j}\;\exists\;a_{j},b_{j}\geq 0:\;\;\;\varphi_{\omega}(t_{j}b_{j}+(1-t_{j})a_{j})=\varphi_{\omega}(b_{j})=\frac{2j\varphi_{\omega}(a_{j})}{1-jt_{j}}.

Then there does not exist a function
\sigma:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
such that
\varphi_{\sigma}
is convex and such that
\omega
and
\sigma
are equivalent.

Note that (
7.2
) is (
2.3
) and a mild requirement; it is clearly satisfied if
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
.

Proof.

First, we claim that

\exists\;j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\forall\;j\geq j_{0}:\;\;\;\varphi_{\omega}(b_{j})>jt_{j}\varphi_{\omega}(b_{j})+j(1-t_{j})\varphi_{\omega}(a_{j})+j.

For this note that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\omega}(b_{j})>jt_{j}\varphi_{\omega}(b_{j})+j(1-t_{j})\varphi_{\omega}(a_{j})+j\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{\omega}(b_{j})(1-jt_{j})>j(1-t_{j})\varphi_{\omega}(a_{j})+j

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 2j\varphi_{\omega}(a_{j})>j(1-t_{j})\varphi_{\omega}(a_{j})+j;

we have used the second equality in (
7.3
) and the last estimate is valid since

for all

and since
\varphi_{\omega}(a_{j})>1
for all

sufficiently large which holds by assumption (

). By taking into account the first equality in (

) we have shown:

\displaystyle\forall\;C\geq 1\;\exists\;j_{C}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\forall\;j\geq j_{C}\;\exists\;0<t_{j}<\frac{1}{j}\;\exists\;a_{j},b_{j}\geq 0:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\omega}(t_{j}b_{j}+(1-t_{j})a_{j})=\varphi_{\omega}(b_{j})>Ct_{j}\varphi_{\omega}(b_{j})+C(1-t_{j})\varphi_{\omega}(a_{j})+C.

This violates (
7.1
) and finishes the proof by Lemma

.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be non-decreasing and such that

.
∎

[0,+\infty)
, i.e.
\omega
on
[1,+\infty)
via
\omega(s):=\varphi_{\omega}(\log(s))
. The graph of
\varphi_{\omega}
consists of a set of straight lines and for this we have to introduce
three auxiliary sequences

(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
,
(\overline{x}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
and
(y_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
. We assume that

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;x_{j}<\overline{x}_{j}<y_{j}<x_{j+1},

and several more growth restrictions; see
Step b
below. Indeed,
\overline{x}_{j}
is given as the convex combination of the points
x_{j}
and
y_{j}
subject to a value
0<t_{j}<1
which is coming from the
fourth auxiliary sequence

(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
.

The graph of
\varphi_{\omega}
is defined to be

(*)

the straight line connecting the points
(0,0)
and
(x_{1},\varphi_{\omega}(x_{1}))
(since we assume
x_{1}>0
),

(*)

the set of straight lines connecting the points
(x_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j}))
and
(\overline{x}_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j}))
resp.
(\overline{x}_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j}))
and
(y_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j}))
resp.
(y_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j}))
and
(x_{j+1},\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j+1}))
.

(*)

The slope of the straight line connecting
(x_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j}))
and
(\overline{x}_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j}))
is denoted by
k_{j}
; the slope of the line connecting
(y_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j}))
and
(x_{j+1},\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j+1}))
by
\ell_{j}
.

(*)

Moreover, the slope of the line connecting
(\overline{x}_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j}))
and
(y_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j}))
is identically
0
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, i.e.
\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j})=\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j})
; see (
7.11
) and (
7.13
) below.

(*)

This describes
\omega
on
[1,+\infty)
with
\omega(1)=\varphi_{\omega}(0)=0
and in order to complete the definition we set
\omega(t):=0
for
0\leq t<1
(i.e. normalization for
\omega
).

Finally, for treating
(V)
we introduce the following notion: A sequence
\delta=(\delta_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
is called an
admissible parameter sequence
if

(7.5)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\delta_{j+1}\leq\delta_{j}\leq(j+2)\delta_{j+1},\hskip 25.0pt\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\delta_{j}j=+\infty.

An immediate example for such a sequence is
\delta_{j}:=\frac{1}{\log(e+j)}
.

Let
\delta=(\delta_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
be an admissible parameter sequence, arbitrary but from now on fixed. The definition of
\omega
is depending on chosen
\delta
which suggests the notation
\varphi_{\omega_{\delta}}
. However, since
\delta
is now fixed, in order to lighten notation we skip
\delta
and simply write
\varphi_{\omega}
except in the very last part
Step g:
There we verify
(V)
and hence we have to emphasize the different parameter sequences.

Step b - Definition of the auxiliary sequence
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
.

We choose
0<t_{1}<1
arbitrary and then set

(7.6)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;t_{j+1}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{j}-t_{j}\right).

Let us show by induction that this sequence satisfies

(7.7)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;0<t_{j}<\frac{1}{j},

which should be compared with (
7.3
). The case
j=1
is obvious and note that
t_{j+1}>0
if and only if
t_{j}<\frac{1}{j}
is verified, see (
7.6
). Moreover,

t_{j+1}<\frac{1}{j+1}\Leftrightarrow\frac{1}{j}-t_{j}<\frac{2}{j+1}\Leftrightarrow\frac{1-j}{j(j+1)}=\frac{1}{j}-\frac{2}{j+1}<t_{j},

which clearly holds since
t_{j}>0
by induction hypothesis. From now on let such a sequence
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
be given and fixed and note that this sequence is not depending on the chosen admissible parameter sequence
\delta
.

Step c - Definition of the required auxiliary sequences and basic growth properties.

We introduce the required auxiliary sequences
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
,
(\overline{x}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
and
(y_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
, collect the required growth properties and define
\varphi_{\omega}
on these values.

Let us set

(7.8)

x_{1}:=\frac{2}{t_{1}},

and when
x_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, is given, then define

(7.9)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j}):=\delta_{j}jx_{j},

(7.10)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;y_{j}:=\frac{2\delta_{j}^{-1}\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j})}{1-jt_{j}}=\frac{2jx_{j}}{1-jt_{j}},

(7.11)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j}):=\frac{2j\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j})}{1-jt_{j}}=\frac{2\delta_{j}j^{2}x_{j}}{1-jt_{j}},

(7.12)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\overline{x}_{j}:=t_{j}y_{j}+(1-t_{j})x_{j},

(7.13)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j}):=\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j})\left(=\frac{2\delta_{j}j^{2}x_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}\right),

(7.14)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;x_{j+1}:=(j+1)y_{j}=\frac{2j(j+1)x_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}.

Concerning the denominators in the defining expressions recall that by (
7.7
) we get
0<1-jt_{j}<1
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Moreover, we immediately have
1<\frac{2}{t_{1}}=x_{1}
, that
x_{j}<\overline{x}_{j}<y_{j}<x_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and that all three sequences tend to infinity as
j\rightarrow+\infty
. By (
7.9
) the slope of the straight line connecting
(0,0)
and
(x_{1},\varphi_{\omega}(x_{1}))
is equal to
\delta_{1}
. Next we prove

(7.15)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;x_{j}\geq\frac{2j}{t_{j}}(>2j^{2}\geq 2j).

The case
j=1
is precisely (
7.8
). By (
7.14
) we get

x_{j+1}\geq\frac{2(j+1)}{t_{j+1}}\Leftrightarrow\frac{2j(j+1)x_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}\geq\frac{2(j+1)}{t_{j+1}}\Leftrightarrow\frac{jx_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}\geq\frac{1}{t_{j+1}},

and (
7.6
) yields

\frac{1}{t_{j+1}}\leq\frac{2j^{2}}{t_{j}(1-jt_{j})}\Leftrightarrow\frac{t_{j}(1-jt_{j})}{2j^{2}}\leq t_{j+1}\Leftrightarrow\frac{t_{j}}{2j}\left(\frac{1}{j}-t_{j}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{j}-t_{j}\right),

which is clearly satisfied (recall that
t_{j}<1
). Finally, the induction hypothesis implies
\frac{jx_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}\geq\frac{2j^{2}}{t_{j}(1-jt_{j})}
and by combining everything we have shown the desired estimate for
j+1
, thus (
7.15
) is verified. Next we study the growth differences between the auxiliary sequences; these identities are then crucial for
Step f
below.

Claim: One has
\min\{y_{j}-\overline{x}_{j},y_{j}-x_{j},x_{j+1}-y_{j},\overline{x}_{j}-x_{j}\}\geq j
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.

First note that, since
x_{j}<\overline{x}_{j}
, for having
y_{j}-x_{j}\geq j
it suffices to verify
y_{j}-\overline{x}_{j}\geq j
.

Concerning this estimate, by taking into account (
7.12
) and (
7.10
) we get

\displaystyle y_{j}-\overline{x}_{j}=(1-t_{j})y_{j}-(1-t_{j})x_{j}=(1-t_{j})(y_{j}-x_{j})=(1-t_{j})\left(\frac{2jx_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}-x_{j}\right)\geq j

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow jx_{j}\geq j\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2(1-t_{j})}+x_{j}\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2}.

This last estimate holds since
\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2(1-t_{j})}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, so we get
j\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2(1-t_{j})}+x_{j}\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2}\leq\frac{j+x_{j}}{2}
and the estimate
j+x_{j}\leq 2jx_{j}
is clear because
x_{j}\geq j
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
(see (
7.15
)).

Next, we see that (
7.14
) yields
x_{j+1}-y_{j}=jy_{j}>jx_{j}\geq j
.

Finally, by (
7.12
) and (
7.10
) we get

\overline{x}_{j}-x_{j}=t_{j}(y_{j}-x_{j})=t_{j}\left(\frac{2jx_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}-x_{j}\right)\geq j\Leftrightarrow jx_{j}\geq j\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2t_{j}}+x_{j}\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2},

and let us see that this estimate holds: First,
x_{j}\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2}<\frac{x_{j}}{2}
is clear and so it suffices to have
\left(j-\frac{1}{2}\right)x_{j}\geq j\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2t_{j}}
. Second, by taking into account (
7.15
) for this estimate it suffices to have
\left(j-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{2j}{t_{j}}\geq\frac{j}{2t_{j}}
which is equivalent to having
4j^{2}-2j\geq j
, i.e. to
\frac{4}{3}j\geq 1
which is clear for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
. Thus the claim is verified.

Now let us compute and compare the slopes
k_{j}
and
\ell_{j}
introduced in
Step a
explicitly. By definition we have

\displaystyle k_{j}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j})-\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j})}{\overline{x}_{j}-x_{j}}=\delta_{j}\frac{2j^{2}x_{j}(1-jt_{j})^{-1}-jx_{j}}{t_{j}(y_{j}-x_{j})}=\delta_{j}\frac{2j^{2}x_{j}-jx_{j}(1-jt_{j})}{t_{j}(2jx_{j}(1-jt_{j})^{-1}-x_{j})(1-jt_{j})}

\displaystyle=\delta_{j}\frac{jx_{j}(2j-1+jt_{j})}{t_{j}(2jx_{j}-x_{j}+jx_{j}t_{j})}=\delta_{j}\frac{jx_{j}(2j-1+jt_{j})}{t_{j}x_{j}(2j-1+jt_{j})},

and so

(7.16)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;k_{j}=\frac{\delta_{j}j}{t_{j}}.

Since
k_{j}=\frac{\delta_{j}j}{t_{j}}>\delta_{j}j^{2}
(see (
7.7
)), the second part of (
7.5
) implies
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}k_{j}=+\infty
.

Similarly, for
\ell_{j}
one has by definition that

\displaystyle\ell_{j}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j+1})-\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j})}{x_{j+1}-y_{j}}=\frac{2\delta_{j+1}j(j+1)^{2}x_{j}(1-jt_{j})^{-1}-2\delta_{j}j^{2}x_{j}(1-jt_{j})^{-1}}{2j(j+1)x_{j}(1-jt_{j})^{-1}-2jx_{j}(1-jt_{j})^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{j+1}(j+1)^{2}-\delta_{j}j}{(j+1)-1}=\frac{\delta_{j+1}(j+1)^{2}-\delta_{j}j}{j}.

The first part in (
7.5
) implies, on the one hand, that
\ell_{j}\leq\frac{\delta_{j}(j^{2}+j+1)}{j}
because
\delta_{j+1}\leq\delta_{j}
, and, on the other hand, also
\ell_{j}>0\Leftrightarrow\delta_{j+1}\frac{(j+1)^{2}}{j}>\delta_{j}
holds since
\frac{(j+1)^{2}}{j}=j+2+\frac{1}{j}>j+2
. Consequently, we have verified

(7.17)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;0<\ell_{j}\leq\frac{\delta_{j}(j^{2}+j+1)}{j}.

Moreover, it holds that

(7.18)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\ell_{j}<k_{j+1}

because, first, by (
7.16
) we get

k_{j+1}>\ell_{j}\Leftrightarrow\frac{\delta_{j+1}(j+1)}{t_{j+1}}>\frac{\delta_{j+1}(j+1)^{2}-\delta_{j}j}{j}\Leftrightarrow\delta_{j+1}(j+1)>t_{j+1}\left(\delta_{j+1}\left(j+2+\frac{1}{j}\right)-\delta_{j}\right).

This estimate is valid since

t_{j+1}\left(\delta_{j+1}\left(j+2+\frac{1}{j}\right)-\delta_{j}\right)<t_{j+1}\delta_{j+1}\left(j+2+\frac{1}{j}\right)<\frac{1}{j+1}\delta_{j+1}\left(j+2+\frac{1}{j}\right),

which follows by taking into account (
7.7
) and then, in order to conclude, note that
\frac{1}{j+1}\left(j+2+\frac{1}{j}\right)<(j+1)
is obvious.

Summarizing, from the given construction it follows that
\varphi_{\omega}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is continuous and non-decreasing with
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi_{\omega}(t)=+\infty
. The last property follows, in particular, also by the next step.

Step d -
\omega
satisfies
(\omega_{3})
(and hence
(\omega_{3,w})
, too).

In order to conclude we have to show that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(t)}{t}=+\infty
and by construction and definition it suffices to investigate the “corner points” of the graph; i.e. that

(7.19)

\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j})}{x_{j}}=\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j})}{y_{j}}=+\infty.

For this note that
x\mapsto\frac{kx+d}{x}=k+\frac{d}{x}
is non-increasing on
(0,+\infty)
if and only if
d\geq 0
and in view of the geometric construction recall that the slopes of the lines connecting
(\overline{x}_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j}))
and
(y_{j},\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j}))
are identically
0
.

For all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
by (
7.9
) we get

\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j})}{x_{j}}=\frac{\delta_{j}jx_{j}}{x_{j}}=\delta_{j}j,

and (
7.10
) and (
7.11
) together give

\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j})}{y_{j}}=\frac{2\delta_{j}j^{2}x_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}\frac{1-jt_{j}}{2jx_{j}}=\delta_{j}j,

which proves (
7.19
) by taking into account the second part in (
7.5
).

Step e - There does not exist
\sigma:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
such that
\sigma\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega
and such that
\varphi_{\sigma}
is convex (i.e.
\sigma
satisfies in addition
(\omega_{4})
).

We apply Lemma
7.2
. First note that as a consequence of
Step c
and
Step d
we have that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\varphi_{\omega}(t)=+\infty
and hence, in particular, (
7.2
) is valid. In order to conclude we prove
(
7.3
) and verify this condition for the choices
t_{j}
from
Step b
(recall (
7.7
)) and when taking
a_{j}:=x_{j}
,
b_{j}:=y_{j}
. By combining (
7.12
), (
7.13
) and (
7.9
) we get for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

\varphi_{\omega}(t_{j}y_{j}+(1-t_{j})x_{j})=\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j})=\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j})=\frac{2\delta_{j}j^{2}x_{j}}{1-jt_{j}}=\frac{2j\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j})}{1-jt_{j}},

which is precisely (
7.3
) for the aforementioned choices.

Step f -
\omega
satisfies
\gamma(\omega)=+\infty
.

In order to prove
\gamma(\omega)=+\infty
we want to apply Corollary
7.4
and so the aim is to verify (
7.4
). In fact we even show

(7.20)

\forall\;\gamma>0:\;\;\;\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(\gamma+s)}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}=1\Leftrightarrow\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(e^{\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}=1,

i.e.
\omega
is
slowly varying,
see
[
2
,
(1.2.1)
]
,
[
12
, Sect. 2.2]
and the comments at the end of Section
2.3
.

Let
\gamma>0
be given, arbitrary large but from now on fixed. By the previous
Claim in Step c
it follows that for all sufficiently large
s\in[0,+\infty)
(depending on given
\gamma
) we eventually have to distinguish between the following three different cases.

Case i
- Let
x_{j}\leq s\leq\overline{x}_{j}
, for
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
sufficiently large depending on
\gamma
, then by the claim
\gamma+s\leq y_{j}
.

In this situation, by definition of the graph and the slope
k_{j}
and since
\varphi_{\omega}
is non-decreasing, we have the following estimate:

\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(\gamma+s)}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}\leq\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}+\frac{\gamma k_{j}}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}\leq 1+\frac{\gamma k_{j}}{\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j})}.

(
7.9
), (
7.16
) and (
7.15
) together imply

\frac{k_{j}}{\varphi_{\omega}(x_{j})}=\frac{\delta_{j}j}{t_{j}\delta_{j}jx_{j}}=\frac{1}{t_{j}x_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{2j},

which tends to
0
as
j\rightarrow+\infty
.

Case ii
- Let
\overline{x}_{j}\leq s\leq y_{j}
, then the claim gives that
\gamma+s\leq x_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
sufficiently large.

In this situation, by definition of the graph and the slope
\ell_{j}
and since
\varphi_{\omega}
is non-decreasing, we have the following estimate:

\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(\gamma+s)}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}\leq\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}+\frac{\gamma\ell_{j}}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}\leq 1+\frac{\gamma\ell_{j}}{\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j})}.

(
7.13
), (
7.17
) and (
7.15
) together with (
7.7
) imply

\frac{\ell_{j}}{\varphi_{\omega}(\overline{x}_{j})}\leq\frac{\delta_{j}(j^{2}+j+1)}{j2j^{2}\delta_{j}x_{j}(1-jt_{j})^{-1}}\leq\frac{(j^{2}+j+1)(1-jt_{j})t_{j}}{4j^{4}}\leq\frac{j^{2}+j+1}{4j^{4}},

which tends to
0
as
j\rightarrow+\infty
.

Case iii
- Let
y_{j}\leq s\leq x_{j+1}
, then
\gamma+s\leq\overline{x}_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
sufficiently large.

In this situation we have, by taking into account (
7.18
), that

\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(\gamma+s)}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}\leq\frac{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}+\frac{\gamma\max\{\ell_{j},k_{j+1}\}}{\varphi_{\omega}(s)}\leq 1+\frac{\gamma k_{j+1}}{\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j})}.

Then note that by (
7.6
) and the fact that
t_{j}<1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
we have
\frac{(1-jt_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}}\leq 2j\Leftrightarrow(1-jt_{j})t_{j}\leq(1-jt_{j})
. By combining this with (
7.11
), (
7.16
), (
7.15
) and property
\delta_{j+1}\leq\delta_{j}
(see the first part in (
7.5
)) we obtain

\frac{k_{j+1}}{\varphi_{\omega}(y_{j})}=\frac{\delta_{j+1}(j+1)}{t_{j+1}2\delta_{j}j^{2}x_{j}(1-jt_{j})^{-1}}\leq\frac{(j+1)(1-jt_{j})t_{j}}{t_{j+1}4j^{3}}\leq\frac{j+1}{2j^{2}},

which tends to
0
as
j\rightarrow+\infty
.

Summarizing,
Cases i-iii
verify (
7.20
) and hence this part is finished.

Step h - There exist (uncountable) infinitely many pairwise non-equivalent weights satisfying all the requirements shown above.

Indeed, we are going to verify that if
\delta=(\delta_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
and
\delta^{\prime}=(\delta^{\prime}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
are two admissible parameter sequences satisfying

(7.21)

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{\delta_{j}}{\delta^{\prime}_{j}}=+\infty\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{\delta^{\prime}_{j}}{\delta_{j}}=+\infty,

then the corresponding weights
\omega_{\delta}
and
\omega_{\delta^{\prime}}
are not equivalent.

For this apply (
7.9
) and when assuming the first condition in (
7.21
) we get

\sup_{x\geq 0}\frac{\varphi_{\omega_{\delta}}(x)}{\varphi_{\omega_{\delta^{\prime}}}(x)}\geq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{\varphi_{\omega_{\delta}}(x_{j})}{\varphi_{\omega_{\delta^{\prime}}}(x_{j})}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{\delta_{j}jx_{j}}{\delta^{\prime}_{j}jx_{j}}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\frac{\delta_{j}}{\delta^{\prime}_{j}}=+\infty.

This shows that
\varphi_{\omega_{\delta}}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\varphi_{\omega_{\delta^{\prime}}}
, equivalently
\omega_{\delta}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega_{\delta^{\prime}}
, cannot be satisfied. The argument for the second condition in (
7.21
) is analogous. Alternatively, instead of (
7.9
) one can also use (
7.11
) or (
7.13
) and for this recall the following facts: Of course, the values of
\varphi_{\omega}
are depending, by definition, on chosen
\delta
. On the other hand, note that the auxiliary sequence
(t_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
studied in
Step b,
the “corner point” sequences
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
,
(y_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
and thus also the intermediate sequence
(\overline{x}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
are
not depending on the admissible parameter sequence.
In this sense the geometric construction is made uniformly w.r.t. the parameter sequences
\delta
.

This finishes the construction of the desired (counter-)example resp. of the uncountable infinite family of (counter-)examples.

Remark 7.5
.

Let an admissible parameter sequence
\delta
satisfying
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\delta_{j}=0
be given; consider e.g.
\delta_{j}:=\frac{1}{\log(e+j)}
. However, note that this additional condition is not required necessarily in the above arguments and
\delta
can be bounded by below by some
\epsilon>0
. In any case, under this assumption each power sequence
\delta^{\alpha}=(\delta_{j}^{\alpha})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
,
0<\alpha<1
, is again an admissible parameter sequence since
\delta_{j+1}^{\alpha}\leq\delta_{j}^{\alpha}\leq(j+2)^{\alpha}\delta_{j+1}^{\alpha}\leq(j+2)\delta_{j+1}^{\alpha}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
\delta_{j}^{\alpha}\geq\delta_{j}
for all
j
such that
\delta_{j}\leq 1
; i.e. for all
j
sufficiently large. Thus all requirements from (
7.5
) hold for
\delta^{\alpha}:=(\delta^{\alpha}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
and (each)
\delta^{\prime}:=\delta^{\alpha}
serves as an example for an admissible parameter sequence satisfying the second condition in (
7.21
).

Note that in general it is not clear that
\delta^{\alpha}
is admissible if
\alpha>1
.

Remark 7.6
.

Let
\mathbf{M}=(M_{p})_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given, then the
associated function

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined as follows:

(7.22)

\omega_{\mathbf{M}}(t):=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{M_{0}t^{p}}{M_{p}}\right),\qquad t\geq 0,

with the conventions
0^{0}:=1
and
\log(0)=-\infty
. When treating this function, naturally one should assume that
\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{p})^{1/p}=+\infty
holds in order to ensure
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
(well-definedness). For more details we refer to
[
16
, Chapitre I]
, the more recent work
[
33
]
and see also
[
15
, Sect. 3]
. It is known and immediate by definition that
(\omega_{4})
holds for
\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
and consequently in the whole equivalence class (w.r.t. relation
\sim
) of the previously constructed function there does not exist any associated weight function. In particular, the obtained (counter-)example cannot be described within the weight sequence setting which means that
\omega\equiv\omega_{\mathbf{M}}
fails for any weight sequence
\mathbf{M}
.

On the other hand the above constructed
\omega
is matrix admissible since crucially
(\omega_{3})
holds.

We finish by mentioning the following
question/goal:
Transfer the construction of
\varphi_{\omega}
(resp. of
\omega
) in Section
7
to the higher dimensional case (anisotropic setting). We expect that the analogous statement holds as well but such an explicit construction is more technical and involved and also the arguments using the growth index
\gamma(\cdot)
are becoming unclear in this general situation since so far this quantity is only defined and studied for
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
.

Prosthodontic Management of Complete Anodontia with Retained Deciduous Teeth
Authors: Alka Gupta, Saman Shaikh, Harsh Chansoria, Anjali Maheshwari, Taku Pugang, Shivani Gupta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Congenital absence of permanent teeth, or anodontia, is a rare developmental anomaly, and isolated anodontia with retained deciduous teeth is even more uncommon. This report describes the prosthodontic management of a 16-year-old female diagnosed with nonsyndromic familial anodontia who presented with retained primary teeth and complete absence of permanent tooth buds. A fixed interim prosthesis was planned to restore oral function, improve facial esthetics, and support psychosocial well-being. Full-mouth impressions were made, and a heat-cure acrylic resin prosthesis was fabricated, using the remaining deciduous teeth as abutments for retention. Standard prosthodontic procedures—including diagnostic mounting, facebow transfer, centric relation recording, and final impressions—were followed to ensure optimal fit and function. The patient exhibited excellent adaptation to the prosthesis, with noticeable improvement in mastication, speech, and facial appearance. Significant psychological benefits were also observed, including enhanced social interaction and increased self-confidence. Follow-up evaluations revealed no major complications, and the prosthesis remained stable and functional. This case highlights that fixed interim prostheses offer an effective, minimally invasive solution for adolescents with anodontia, providing functional, esthetic, and psychological benefits until they reach skeletal maturity and become candidates for definitive long-term rehabilitation.

INTRODUCTION

Tooth agenesis, especially the complete absence of permanent teeth, is a rare condition that affects oral function, aesthetics, and psychosocial development.[1] Isolated anodontia refers to the congenital absence of all permanent teeth without any associated syndromic condition, typically diagnosed in adolescence when the normal transition from primary to permanent teeth fails.[2] In some cases, retained deciduous teeth persist beyond their expected exfoliation period, serving as the only functional dentition.[3] Conditions associated with anodontia include ectodermal dysplasia syndrome, Axenfeld–Rieger syndrome, and Incontinentia Pigmenti (IP).[4] Ectodermal dysplasia affects hair, nails, teeth, and skin, while Axenfeld–Rieger syndrome involves maxillary and mandibular hypoplasia, hypodontia, and enamel hypoplasia. IP, a genetic disorder, leads to varying forms of hypodontia and delayed eruption. Prolonged malnutrition or endocrine disorders like hypothyroidism can also delay tooth development.[5]

The management of anodontia requires a multidisciplinary approach to preserve existing deciduous teeth, restore occlusion, mastication, and facial appearance. Early prosthodontic intervention is essential to prevent alveolar ridge resorption and maintain vertical dimension.[6] Fixed interim prostheses are an effective treatment for growing patients, providing aesthetic and functional benefits while allowing for future rehabilitation. In this case, the patient was diagnosed with nonsyndromic familial anodontia, and treatment was planned to preserve deciduous teeth and restore function and aesthetics with a fixed interim prosthesis. Fixed interim prostheses offer stability, aesthetics, and functionality, deferring definitive prostheses until skeletal maturity.[7]

CASE REPORT

Patient profile

A 16-year-old female presented to the Department of Prosthodontics and Crown and Bridge at the Government College of Dentistry, Indore, Madhya Pradesh, with complaints of small-sized teeth and difficulty chewing. The patient expressed psychological distress due to poor aesthetics. Patient consent form was obtained before starting the procedure.

Medical and dental history

The patient had no significant medical history.

Family history revealed that the father had a similar dental problem (hypodontia and microdontia); the mother and two siblings had apparently normal dentition.

Dental history: The patient reported that none of her deciduous teeth had shed since eruption, and she has had multiple restorations done since the age of 12.

Examination

General examination revealed apparently normal features, including hair, skin, and sweating.

Extraoral examination showed a concave facial profile with decreased lower facial height. The vertical dimension at rest was 57 mm, and the vertical dimension at occlusion was 51 mm.

Intraoral examination revealed prepared deciduous teeth in the maxillary and mandibular arch and an anterior open bite (7 mm), grade I mobility in maxillary and mandibular central incisors and Pseudo-Class III jaw relation.

Radiographic evaluation (orthopantomogram and intraoral periapical radiographs) confirmed the absence of permanent tooth buds and the presence of rudimentary roots in retained primary teeth, suggestive of Ectodermal dysplasia.

Treatment plan

Considering the patient’s age, aesthetic and functional needs, and psychological condition, a fixed interim full-mouth prosthesis using heat-cure acrylic resin was planned. The retained deciduous teeth were preserved and used as abutments for retention and support [Figure 1].

RESULTS

The patient adapted well to the prosthesis. There was a marked improvement in mastication, speech, and facial esthetics. Psychological uplift was evident through increased social interaction and enhanced self-confidence. No significant complications were observed during initial follow-up visits [Figure 1].

DISCUSSION

This case highlights the importance of timely prosthetic intervention in adolescent patients with ectodermal dysplasia. While implant-supported restorations are ideal, they are typically deferred until skeletal growth is complete. Fixed interim prostheses offer significant advantages over removable options, including improved retention, aesthetics, and psychological comfort.[7] Especially in young adolescent patients where esthetics is of prime concern, fixed interim restorations are game changers. The complaint of repeated dislodgement of restoration was addressed conservatively by increasing the height of deciduous teeth using bonded restorations. Other options, such as post and core, were not feasible in this case due to the compromised root structure of the deciduous teeth, unlike the case reported by Natarajan et al.[8] The vertical dimension at occlusion was increased from 51 mm to 55 mm, while maintaining a freeway space of 2 mm (Vertical dimension at rest: 57 mm). Self-cure tooth-colored resin was used to make a trial prosthesis using a putty index guide of a diagnostic wax mock-up and was cemented after tooth preparation. This helped the patient visualize the final treatment outcome and provide input related to functional comfort and aesthetic acceptance. Thus, acting as a “test drive.” For interim prostheses, heat-cure acrylic resin provides a cost-effective and modifiable solution. The involvement of retained deciduous teeth enhances prosthesis stability and preserves alveolar bone to some extent.

CONCLUSION

Early prosthetic management of patients with ectodermal dysplasia can significantly improve their quality of life. Fixed interim prostheses fabricated using heat-cure acrylic resin offer a practical and effective solution for growing adolescents. Regular monitoring and transitioning to definitive prosthetic options in the future are essential for long-term rehabilitation.

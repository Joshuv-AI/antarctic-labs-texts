Subthreshold poles in electron‐positron annihilation. D s + ​ D s − \mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{s}}^{+}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{s}}^{-} final state.
Peter Lichard, Josef Juráň

Abstract
We present the evidence of a subthreshold pole in the e + ​ e − → D s + ​ D s − e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\rm D_{s}^{+}\rm D_{s}^{-} process, which can be interpreted as an excited (2p) state of the D s + ​ D s − \rm D_{s}^{+}\rm D_{s}^{-} molecule with a binding energy of (28.1 − 9.3 + 12.1 {}^{+12.1}_{-9.3} ) MeV.



I
Introduction

This paper is the next step in our effort to uncover the role of the
subthreshold poles (SP, in what follows) in the electron-positron annihilation
into hadrons commenced in Ref.
kaonium
.

The playground in our consideration is the complex
s
plane in which
the
s
variable acquires the values of the cms energy squared on the real
axis from the reaction threshold to infinity. We accept as a working
hypothesis that the amplitude of the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation into a hadronic
final state as a function of
s
has the same analytic properties as
the amplitudes of hadronic reactions, discussed,
e.g.
, in
mizera
.
Namely, that the annihilation amplitude, when continued to the whole
complex
s
plane, becomes an analytic function
with a cut along the above-mentioned part of the real axis (called the physical
cut). The imaginary part of the amplitude exhibits a discontinuity across the
physical cut,
\mathrm{Im}\,F(s+\mathrm{i}\epsilon)\neq\mathrm{Im}\,F(s-\mathrm{i}\epsilon)
.

The resonances, which reveal themselves as pronounced structures in the
excitation function, are represented by pairs of complex-conjugated poles on
higher Riemann sheets that are accessible through the physical cut. Their
contributions to the amplitude on the upper branch of the physical cut are
usually described by various phenomenological formulas.

Also, the poles on the first (physical) Riemann sheet may exist, but only
on the real axis below the reaction threshold. These SPs correspond to states
that are stable against the decay to the considered final state. They are
not reachable in the experiment and are
not usually considered when fitting the experimental data. However, they may
influence the amplitude in the physical region due to strong restrictions
implied by analytic properties of amplitude, which are the consequence
of causality and unitarity.

We aim to discover the SPs by fitting the experimental data
on the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation cross section.
In this paper, we concentrate on the annihilation into the
\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}}
final
state.

II
The explored data and the model

The BESIII collaboration working at the spectrometer situated at
the electron-positron collider BEPCII, based at the IHEP laboratory
in Beijing, China, have recently published
besiii2024b
precise data
on
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation to the
\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}}
final states. In the Supplemental
Material, they provide the Born cross sections of the

e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}}
at 138 energies from the threshold at 3.938 GeV to 4.951 GeV
with both statistical and systematic errors.

To fit their data, we will use the VMD model described in
kaonium
. We
have made three modifications to the formula shown there: we (i) expanded
the number of intermediate vector mesons from two to
n
, (ii) changed
the sign at the imaginary unit in the denominators to cope with a more usual
convention, (iii) replaced original variable
R_{k}
by
Q_{k}=R^{2}_{k}
, which
allows more reliable determination of the pole’s statistical significance.
Our central formula thus becomes

\sigma(s)=\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{3s}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}{s}\right)^{\!\!{3/2}}\!\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\delta_{k}}}{s-M_{k}^{2}+\mathrm{i}M_{k}\Gamma_{k}}\right|^{2}\!.

(1)

Here,
M_{k}
and
\Gamma_{k}
are the mass and width of the
k
th
intermediate vector meson coupled to the photon, respectively.
We will treat
M_{k}
s,
\Gamma_{k}
s,
Q_{k}
s, and
\delta_{k}
s as
free parameters (except
\delta_{1}
, which is kept at 0). We will
determine their mean values and dispersions by fitting the formula (
1
)
to experimental cross sections using the standard
\chi^{2}
criterion.
To fit the data, we merge their statistical and systematic errors
quadratically. For the mass of the
\mathrm{D}_{s}^{\pm}
, we will use the value

m_{\mathrm{D}}=(1969.0\pm 1.4)
MeV, listed in the PDG 2024 Tables
pdg2024

as “our average”.

1
)
featuring a single resonance. Then, we used the obtained parameters as input
in the next step, where we added the second resonance. Repeating the
procedure, we arrived at a three-resonance fit characterized by

\chi^{2}/\mathrm{DOF}=230.5/127
and confidence level (CL) of 5
\times 10^{-6}

\chi^{2}/\mathrm{DOF}=166.2/123

s

B=(47\pm 14)
MeV below
the
\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}}

threshold. Knowing that on the physical sheet, the poles outside the real

k=1
) with

The consistent and compatible appearance of the SP in all three fits
indicates that it is not a fluke. The fit with five accompanying resonances
stands out with an excellent CL of 81%, and we will take the SP parameters

M=(3909.9\pm 9.6)
MeV [
B=(28\pm 10)
MeV] and
S=3.0\sigma
determined in it
as the most reliable. Moreover, after performing a more detailed study of
the errors using the
\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{min}}
+1 criterion we end up with

M=(3909.9^{+8.9}_{-11.8})
MeV [
B=(28.1^{+12.1}_{-9.3})
MeV].

As the most recent compilation
pdgxsect
of the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation
cross section does not show any resonance in the corresponding cms energy
region, the question of the nature of the SP we found remains open.

An interesting possibility is a bound state of the
\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}}
system with
binding energy
B=(28.1^{+12.1}_{-9.3})
MeV. Its quantum numbers, dictated
by the appearance in the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation, are
I^{G}(J^{PC})=0^{-}(1^{--})
.
They exclude the SP as a ground state of the
\mathrm{D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}}
molecule (dsonium), but
suggest it as an excited state with orbital quantum number
L=1
. The
ground state of dsonium should have a smaller mass and quantum numbers

I^{G}(J^{PC})=0^{+}(0^{++})
. Consulting the PDG Tables
pdg2024
we found
an eligible candidate in the
\chi_{c0}(3860)
. This fact makes the
possibility that our SP is an excited state of dsonium more acceptable

kpkm
.

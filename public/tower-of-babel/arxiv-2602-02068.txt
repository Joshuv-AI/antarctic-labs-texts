On the Numerical Treatment of an Abstract Nonlinear System of Coupled Hyperbolic Equations Associated with the Timoshenko Model
Jemal Rogava, Zurab Vashakidze

Abstract
The present work addresses the Cauchy problem for an abstract nonlinear system of coupled hyperbolic equations associated with the Timoshenko model in a real Hilbert space. Our purpose is to develop and delve into a temporal discretization scheme for approximating a solution to this problem. To this end, we propose a symmetric three-layer semi-discrete time-stepping scheme in which the nonlinear term is evaluated at the temporal midpoint. As a result, at each time step, this approach reduces the original nonlinear problem to a linear one and enables parallel computation of its solution. Convergence is proved, and second-order accuracy with respect to the time-step size is established on a local temporal interval. The proposed scheme is then applied to a spatially one-dimensional nonlinear dynamic Timoshenko beam system, and the results obtained for the abstract nonlinear system are extended to this setting. A Legendre–Galerkin spectral approximation is employed for the spatial discretization. By taking differences of Legendre polynomials within the Galerkin framework, the resulting linear system is sparse and can be efficiently decoupled. The convergence of the method is also investigated. Finally, several numerical experiments on carefully chosen benchmark problems are conducted to validate the proposed approach and to confirm the theoretical findings.

1  Introduction

1.1  Formulation of the Problem

Motivated by the theory of beams, plates, and shells, this work presents a natural abstract generalization of the nonlinear dynamic Timoshenko beam system. As a second-generation beam theory, the Timoshenko model
[
29
]
extends beyond the classical Euler-Bernoulli (Kirchhoff-Love) theory by accounting for shear deformation and rotational bending. This approach is particularly suitable for analyzing thick beams, sandwich composite beams, and beams subjected to high-frequency excitation where the wavelength is comparable to the beam thickness. We paid attention to this model, since it has wide-ranging applications in structural and mechanical engineering, stemming from the nonlinear theory of elasticity.

1.2  Outline of the Problem

We develop a time-stepping scheme for the nonlinear problem (
1.1a
)-(
1.1c
). To achieve this objective, a uniform temporal grid is introduced, and a symmetric three-layer semi-discrete scheme is proposed, in which the nonlinear term is evaluated at a temporal midpoint. Such a treatment permits an approximate solution of the original nonlinear problem by solving a linear problem in parallel at each temporal layer.

The investigation of the stability and convergence of the proposed scheme is based on the following fact: the sequences of vectors
A^{\nicefrac{{s}}{{2}}}\left(u_{k}-u_{k-1}\right)/\tau
and
A^{\nicefrac{{\left(s+1\right)}}{{2}}}u_{k}
, with
s=0,1
, as well as
\left(v_{k}-v_{k-1}\right)/\tau
and
L^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}v_{k}
, where
L=\delta A+\gamma C
, are uniformly bounded. Here,
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
denote approximate solutions, and
\tau>0
is the time step. These facts allow us to estimate the error of the approximate solution and to show that the order of convergence is
\mathcal{O}\left(\tau^{2}\right)
in the class of smooth solutions. Moreover, the approximation error of the first derivative, obtained by applying the central finite difference formula to the approximate solution, is also of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\tau^{2}\right)
.

We subsequently consider a nonlinear, spatially one-dimensional Timoshenko beam system (
7.1
)-(
7.4
) as a special case of the problem (
1.1a
)-(
1.1c
), and the results obtained within the abstract framework are extended to this setting. In this case, the proposed scheme yields second-order linear ordinary differential equations for the unknowns
u_{k}\left(x\right)
and
v_{k}\left(x\right)
at each temporal layer. Note that these differential equations are not coupled and can therefore be solved in parallel. To compute numerical solutions of these equations, we employ the Legendre–Galerkin spectral method. The chosen basis functions ensure that the resulting Galerkin linear system is sparse, which can be decoupled into two linear subsystems. More precisely, the coefficient matrix of the corresponding Galerkin linear system is a symmetric positive-definite tridiagonal matrix with a gap, in the sense that it has only nonzero entries on the main diagonal and the second sub- and superdiagonals, while the first sub- and superdiagonals vanish. By exploiting this structure, the associated linear system can be decomposed into two independent tridiagonal subsystems: one corresponding to the odd-indexed unknowns and the other to the even-indexed unknowns. This property is particularly important for numerical implementation.

An important step in the numerical computation of the Timoshenko model using the proposed scheme is the estimation of the error of the Legendre–Galerkin spectral method. The approximation error of this method is estimated using both the
L^{2}
-norm and the uniform norm for the ordinary differential equations resulting from time discretization.

The crowning stage of each algorithm’s development is numerical experiments. It is well known that the construction of algorithms for the numerical computation of mathematical models describing various processes, together with their computer implementation, enables the use of numerical experiments that replace many expensive real-world experiments spanning various fields of natural science. To assess the practical value of an algorithm, it is important to conduct numerical experiments on model problems that fully demonstrate the role of each component of the algorithm.

The paper discusses three benchmark problems whose solutions exhibit oscillatory behavior. Numerical results obtained using the proposed algorithm indicate that the combined algorithm is stable and achieves high practical accuracy for these problems. The proposed algorithm is implemented in Python, and the corresponding source code is archived in an open-access Zenodo repository
[
26
]
.

2  Symmetric Three-Layer Semi-Discrete Scheme

We aim to find a solution to the problem (
1.1a
)-(
1.1c
) using the following semi-discrete scheme:

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{2}u_{k-1}}{\tau^{2}}+\left(\alpha+\beta{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\right)\frac{Au_{k+1}+Au_{k-1}}{2}=f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k}\,,

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{2}v_{k-1}}{\tau^{2}}+\frac{Lv_{k+1}+Lv_{k-1}}{2}=f_{2,k}-a_{2}Bu_{k}\,,

where

\displaystyle u_{1}=\varphi_{0}+\tau\varphi_{1}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\varphi_{2}\,,\quad\varphi_{2}=f_{1,0}-a_{1}B\psi_{0}-\left(\alpha+\beta{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi_{0}\right\rVert}^{2}\right)A\varphi_{0}\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle v_{1}=\psi_{0}+\tau\psi_{1}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\psi_{2}\,,\quad\psi_{2}=f_{2,0}-a_{2}B\varphi_{0}-L\psi_{0}\,.

By substituting the values of the starting vectors

,

) and (

\displaystyle\left(I+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}q_{1}A\right)u_{2}

\displaystyle=g_{1}\,,

(2.4a)

\displaystyle\left(I+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}L\right)v_{2}

\displaystyle=g_{2}\,,

where the scalar

Given that

Remark 3.3

For the operator

\hat{\gamma}\left\lVert A\varphi\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert L\varphi\right\rVert\leq\nu_{0}\left\lVert A\varphi\right\rVert\,,\quad\varphi\in D\left(A\right)\,,\quad\hat{\gamma}>0\,,\quad\nu_{0}>0\,.

(3.2)

Let us now demonstrate the left-hand inequality of (

{\left\lVert L\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}=\gamma^{2}{\left\lVert A\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}+2\gamma\delta\left(A\varphi,C\varphi\right)+\delta^{2}{\left\lVert C\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}\geq\gamma^{2}{\left\lVert A\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}-2\gamma\delta\left\lVert A\varphi\right\rVert\left\lVert C\varphi\right\rVert+\delta^{2}{\left\lVert C\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}\,.

The following bounds are satisfied

\sqrt{\gamma}\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert L^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi\right\rVert\leq\nu_{0}\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi\right\rVert\,,\quad\varphi\in D\left(A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)\,,\quad\nu_{0}>0\,.

(3.6)

Due to the positive definiteness of the operator
A
, it follows directly that

{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}\geq\nu{\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\varphi\in D\left(A\right)\,.

(3.7)

On the other hand, considering inequality (
3.7
) along with the fact that
\left\lVert C\varphi\right\rVert\leq c\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(L\varphi,\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(A\varphi,\varphi\right)+\delta\left(C\varphi,\varphi\right)\leq\gamma{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}+c\delta{\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\nu_{0}{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\varphi\in D\left(A\right)\,,\quad\nu_{0}=\gamma+\frac{c\delta}{\nu}\,.

Consequently, we derive that

\left\lVert L^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi\right\rVert\leq\nu_{0}\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\varphi\right\rVert\,,\quad\varphi\in D\left(A\right)\,.

(3.8)

Given that the operator
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
maps

onto

and that the range of
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
,

. Under these conditions, it follows that inequality (
3.8
) can be extended to the entire domain

. Consequently, the right-hand inequality of (
3.6
) is satisfied.

The left-hand inequality of (
3.6

∎

Remark 3.6

From condition (

Let

(3.11)

\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}B\varphi\right\rVert\leq c_{2}\left\lVert A\varphi\right\rVert\,,\quad\forall\varphi\in D\left(A\right)\,.

(3.12)

It is evident that, from equation (

\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}B\varphi\right\rVert\leq c_{2}\left\lVert A\varphi\right\rVert\,,\quad\forall\varphi\in D_{1}\,.

(3.13)

By introducing the notation

4  Uniform Boundedness of the Solution to a Discrete Problem and Its Corresponding Difference Analogues of the Derivative

It should be noted that, throughout this text and in all sections, the letters
c
and
M
, indexed with lower subscripts, denote positive constants.

In this
section
, we demonstrate that the vectors
\Delta u_{k-1}/\tau
,
\Delta v_{k-1}/\tau
,
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}
, and
L^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}v_{k}
are uniformly bounded, which is essential for proving the convergence of the approximate solution. Additionally, establishing convergence requires us to prove the uniform boundedness of the vectors
Au_{k}
and
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\Delta u_{k-1}/{\tau}
. It is important to note that this issue is nontrivial, as the energy method does not yield a recurrence inequality that would allow the application of the telescoping series cancellation technique. The
subsequent section
addresses this by demonstrating that the vectors
Au_{k}
and
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\Delta u_{k-1}/{\tau}
are locally uniformly bounded.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider the sequences of vectors
\Delta u_{k-1}/\tau
,
\Delta v_{k-1}/\tau
,
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}
, and
L^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}v_{k}
for
k=1,2,\ldots,n
. These sequences are uniformly bounded, meaning that there exist constants
M_{j}
for
j=1,2,3,4
, independent of
n
, such that the following inequalities are satisfied:

\left\lVert\frac{\Delta u_{k-1}}{\tau}\right\rVert\leq M_{1}\,,\quad\left\lVert\frac{\Delta v_{k-1}}{\tau}\right\rVert\leq M_{2}\,,\quad\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}\right\rVert\leq M_{3}\,,\quad\left\lVert L^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}v_{k}\right\rVert\leq M_{4}\,,\quad k=1,2,\ldots,n\,.

By evaluating the inner product of both sides of equation (
2.1a
) with
u_{k+1}-u_{k-1}=\Delta u_{k}+\Delta u_{k-1}
and considering the properties of the operator
A
, which is self-adjoint and positive-definite, we arrive at

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\frac{\Delta u_{k}}{\tau}\right\rVert}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\right){\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k+1}\right\rVert}^{2}

\displaystyle={\left\lVert\frac{\Delta u_{k-1}}{\tau}\right\rVert}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\right){\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k-1}\right\rVert}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k},\Delta u_{k}\right)+\left(f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k},\Delta u_{k-1}\right)\,,

(4.1)

Let us denote

\alpha_{1,k}={\left\lVert\frac{\Delta u_{k-1}}{\tau}\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\gamma_{1,k}={\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\,.

Employing these notations, equality (
4
) can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,k+1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta\gamma_{1,k}\right)\gamma_{1,k+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1,k}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta\gamma_{1,k}\right)\gamma_{1,k-1}

\displaystyle+\left(f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k},\Delta u_{k}\right)+\left(f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k},\Delta u_{k-1}\right)\,.

By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the right-hand side of the given equality, one can conclude that

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,k+1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta\gamma_{1,k}\right)\gamma_{1,k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{1,k}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta\gamma_{1,k}\right)\gamma_{1,k-1}

\displaystyle+\tau\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{1,k}}+\sqrt{\alpha_{1,k+1}}\right)\left\lVert f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k}\right\rVert\,.

Consequently, we derive the following result

\lambda_{1,k+1}\leq\lambda_{1,k}+\varepsilon_{1,k}\,,

(4.2)

where

\lambda_{1,k}=\alpha_{1,k}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta\gamma_{1,k-1}\right)\gamma_{1,k}\,,\quad\varepsilon_{1,k}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha\left(\gamma_{1,k-1}-\gamma_{1,k}\right)+\tau\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{1,k}}+\sqrt{\alpha_{1,k+1}}\right)\left\lVert f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k}\right\rVert\,.

By involving the telescoping series cancellation technique to inequality (
4.2
), the following result is obtained

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1,1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{1,i}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1,1}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\gamma_{1,i-1}-\gamma_{1,i}\right)+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{1,i}}+\sqrt{\alpha_{1,i+1}}\right)\left\lVert f_{1,i}-a_{1}Bv_{i}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1,1}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\left(\gamma_{1,0}-\gamma_{1,k}\right)+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{1,i}}+\sqrt{\alpha_{1,i+1}}\right)\left\lVert f_{1,i}-a_{1}Bv_{i}\right\rVert\,.

Subsequently, by rearranging the terms and taking into account that
\alpha_{1,i}\leq\lambda_{1,i}
, we derive the following result

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,k+1}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\gamma_{1,k}\leq\lambda_{1,1}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\gamma_{1,0}+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{1,i}}+\sqrt{\lambda_{1,i+1}}\right)\left\lVert f_{1,i}-a_{1}Bv_{i}\right\rVert\,.

Upon introducing the notation

\delta_{1,k}=\sqrt{\lambda_{1,k}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha\gamma_{1,k-1}}\,,

it follows that

\delta_{1,k+1}^{2}\leq\delta_{1,1}^{2}+\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\delta_{1,i}+\delta_{1,i+1}\right)\left\lVert f_{1,i}-a_{1}Bv_{i}\right\rVert\,.

(4.3)

By employing the technique for inequality (
4.3
) as described in
[
24
,
Theorem 2.1
]
, we arrive at the following conclusion

\delta_{1,k+1}\leq\delta_{1,1}+2\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert f_{1,i}-a_{1}Bv_{i}\right\rVert\,.

(4.4)

By performing the inner product of both sides of equation (
2.1b
) with
v_{k+1}-v_{k-1}=\Delta v_{k}+\Delta v_{k-1}
, and considering the self-adjoint and positive-definite nature of the operator
A
, one can immediately deduce that

\alpha_{2,k+1}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{2,k+1}=\alpha_{2,k}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{2,k-1}+\left(f_{2,k}-a_{2}Bu_{k},\Delta v_{k}\right)+\left(f_{2,k}-a_{2}Bu_{k},\Delta v_{k-1}\right)\,,

where

\alpha_{2,k}={\left\lVert\frac{\Delta v_{k-1}}{\tau}\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\gamma_{2,k}={\left\lVert L^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}v_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad L=\gamma A+\delta C\,.

If we adopt the approach employed to establish inequality (
4.4
), the ensuing result follows

\delta_{2,k+1}\leq\delta_{2,1}+2\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert f_{2,i}-a_{2}Bu_{i}\right\rVert\,.

(4.5)

Here,

\delta_{2,k}=\sqrt{\lambda_{2,k}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{2,k-1}}\,,\quad\lambda_{2,k}=\alpha_{2,k}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{2,k}\,.

By summing inequalities (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), taking into account condition (
1.2
), and introducing the notation
\delta_{k}=\delta_{1,k}+\delta_{2,k}
, it consequently follows that

\displaystyle\delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{1}+2\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\left\lVert f_{1,i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert f_{2,i}\right\rVert\right)+2\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\left\lvert a_{1}\right\rvert\left\lVert Bv_{i}\right\rVert+\left\lvert a_{2}\right\rvert\left\lVert Bu_{i}\right\rVert\right)

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{1}+2\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\left\lVert f_{1,i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert f_{2,i}\right\rVert\right)+c_{5}\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sqrt{\gamma_{1,i}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{2,i}}\right)\,,\quad c_{5}=2c_{1}\max\left(\left\lvert a_{1}\right\rvert,\left\lvert a_{2}\right\rvert\right)\,.

(4.6)

Observe that the following straightforward inequality holds

\displaystyle\delta_{k}=\delta_{1,k}+\delta_{2,k}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha}\sqrt{\gamma_{1,k}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{2,k}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\min\left(1,\sqrt{\alpha}\right)\left(\sqrt{\gamma_{1,k}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{2,k}}\right)\,,

Hence, it follows that

\sqrt{\gamma_{1,k}}+\sqrt{\gamma_{2,k}}\leq c_{6}\delta_{k}\,,\quad c_{6}=\sqrt{2}\max\left(1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)\,.

(4.7)

Considering inequality (
4.7
) in the estimation of (
4
) yields the finding that

\delta_{k+1}\leq\delta_{1}+c_{7}\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}+2\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\left\lVert f_{1,i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert f_{2,i}\right\rVert\right)\,.

Hence, through employing the discrete Grönwall-type inequality (
cf.
,
e.g.
,
[
25
,
Lemma 3.1
]
), it can be established that

\delta_{k+1}\leq e^{c_{7}t_{k}}\left(\delta_{1}+2\tau\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\left\lVert f_{1,i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert f_{2,i}\right\rVert\right)\right)\,.

(4.8)

Upon consideration of the estimate,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\left\lVert f_{1,i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert f_{2,i}\right\rVert\right)\leq k\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\left(\left\lVert f_{1,i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert f_{2,i}\right\rVert\right)\,,

it is evident that inequality (
4.8
) can be formulated as

\delta_{k+1}\leq e^{c_{7}T}\left(\delta_{1}+2T\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left(\left\lVert f_{1,i}\right\rVert+\left\lVert f_{2,i}\right\rVert\right)\right)\,.

Thus, it can be inferred that
\alpha_{1,k}
,
\alpha_{2,k}
,
\gamma_{1,k}
, and
\gamma_{2,k}
are uniformly bounded.
∎

5  Uniform Boundedness of High-Order Terms Corresponding to the Solution of the Discrete Problem

It should be emphasized that the proof of the convergence of the approximate solution depends, among other considerations, on the uniform boundedness of the vectors
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\Delta u_{k-1}/{\tau}
and
Au_{k}
. This is quite natural, as the behavior of the solution to the discrete problem is predominantly governed by the first equation (
2.1a
) of the system, which represents a difference analogue of the nonlinear Kirchhoff equation (see
[
25
]
), excluding the term
a_{1}Bv_{k}
.

In this
section
, we establish that the vectors
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\Delta u_{k-1}/{\tau}
and
Au_{k}
are locally uniformly bounded. The proof of this result relies on the nonlinear inequality outlined in
Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The sequences of vectors
A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\Delta u_{k-1}/{\tau}
and
Au_{k}
are locally uniformly bounded. Specifically, there exists
\overline{T}>0
such that

\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\frac{\Delta u_{k-1}}{\tau}\right\rVert\leq M_{5}\,,\quad\left\lVert Au_{k}\right\rVert\leq M_{6}\,,\quad k=1,2,\ldots,\left[\frac{\overline{T}}{\tau}\right]\,,

where
M_{5}
and
M_{6}
are positive constants, each dependent on the parameter
\overline{T}
.

Consider taking the inner product of both sides of equation (
2.1a
) with
A\left(u_{k+1}-u_{k-1}\right)=A\left(\Delta u_{k}\right)+A\left(\Delta u_{k-1}\right)
. By leveraging the properties of the operator
A
, which is self-adjoint and positive-definite, we obtain:

\displaystyle{\left\lVert\frac{1}{\tau}A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k}\right)\right\rVert}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\right){\left\lVert Au_{k+1}\right\rVert}^{2}

\displaystyle={\left\lVert\frac{1}{\tau}A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k-1}\right)\right\rVert}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta{\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\right){\left\lVert Au_{k-1}\right\rVert}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k}\right),A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k}\right)\right)+\left(A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k}\right),A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k-1}\right)\right)\,.

(5.1)

In this context, we assume that
f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k}
belongs to
D\left(A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\right)
.

By employing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality along with inequality (
3.12
) from
Remark
3.7
, the following result can be derived

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left(A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k}\right),A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k}\right)\right)+\left(A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(f_{1,k}-a_{1}Bv_{k}\right),A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k-1}\right)\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}f_{1,k}\right\rVert+\left\lvert a_{1}\right\rvert c_{2}\left\lVert Av_{k}\right\rVert\right)\tau\left(\left\lVert\frac{1}{\tau}A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k}\right)\right\rVert+\left\lVert\frac{1}{\tau}A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k-1}\right)\right\rVert\right)\,.

(5.2)

Let us introduce the following denotations:

\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,k}={\left\lVert\frac{1}{\tau}A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\Delta u_{k-1}\right)\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\beta_{1,k}={\left\lVert Au_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\gamma_{1,k}={\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u_{k}\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\sigma_{1,k}=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\left\lVert A^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}f_{1,i}\right\rVert\,.

By substituting the notations introduced in the preceding step and employing inequality (
5
), we can rewrite equality (
5
) as follows

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,k+1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta\gamma_{1,k}\right)\beta_{1,k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,k}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha+\beta\gamma_{1,k}\right)\beta_{1,k-1}

\displaystyle+\left(\sigma_{1,k}+\left\lvert a_{1}\right\rvert c_{2}\left\lVert Av_{k}\right\rVert\right)\tau\left(\sqrt{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,k+1}}+\sqrt{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,k}}\right)\,.

(5.3)

By incorporating
Remark
3.8
into inequality (
5

7.13

{\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v\right\rVert}^{2}=\left(v,{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v\right)\,,\quad\forall v\in{R}_{0}={\mathcal{A}}D\left({\mathcal{A}}_{0}\right)\,.

(7.14)

It is evident that
R_{0}
is dense in
L^{2}
. Indeed,
R_{0}
represents the set of functions
f
for which the differential equation
-u^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)=f\left(x\right)
, subject to homogeneous boundary conditions, admits a unique classical solution. It is well established that, for such a simple differential equation, the solution
u
lies in
D\left({\mathcal{A}}_{0}\right)
whenever
f\in C\left(\left[0,\ell\right]\right)
(
cf.

Chap. II
,
Sec. 10
in book by Fučík and Kufner
[
10
]
). Moreover, the density of
C\left(\left[0,\ell\right]\right)
in
L^{2}\left(0,\ell\right)
is well-known.

The relation (
7.14
) may now be extended over the entire space
L^{2}
. Given that
R_{0}
is dense in
L^{2}
, for each
v\in L^{2}
, there exists a sequence
v_{n}\in R_{0}
such that
v_{n}\to v
. Accordingly, by (
7.14
), for the sequence
v_{n}
we have:

{\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v_{n}\right\rVert}^{2}=\left(v_{n},{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v_{n}\right)\,.

(7.15)

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to (
7.15
), we obtain:

{\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v_{n}\right\rVert}^{2}\leq\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert\left\lVert{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v_{n}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}\right\rVert{\left\lVert v_{n}\right\rVert}^{2}\,.

From this, it follows that
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v_{n}
forms the Cauchy sequence. Taking into account that
{\mathcal{B}}
is a closed operator and that
{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v_{n}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v
, we conclude that
{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v\in D\left(\mathcal{B}\right)
and that
{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v_{n}\to{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v
. Considering this fact, it follows from (
7.15
) that:

{\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}}{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v\right\rVert}^{2}=\left(v,{\mathcal{A}}^{-1}v\right)\,,\quad\forall v\in L^{2}\,,

or, which is the same

{\left\lVert{\mathcal{B}}u\right\rVert}^{2}=\left({\mathcal{A}}u,u\right)\,,\quad\forall u\in D\left({\mathcal{A}}\right)\,.

∎

Remark 7.2
.

Suppose that the set
D_{0}\subset D\left({\mathcal{A}}_{0}\right)
is such that the operator
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
maps
D_{0}
into
D\left({\mathcal{A}}_{0}\right)
, that is,
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}:D_{0}\to D\left({\mathcal{A}}_{0}\right)
. Moreover, the sets
D_{0}
and
B_{0}D_{0}
are dense in

. Then, the following relation holds:

\left(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}u,\mathcal{B}u\right)={\left\lVert\mathcal{A}u\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\forall u\in D_{0}\subset D\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\,.

(7.16)

Indeed, by an application of integration by parts, we obtain:

\left({\mathcal{A}}_{0}{\mathcal{B}}_{0}u,{\mathcal{B}}_{0}u\right)=-\int_{0}^{\ell}u^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(x\right)u^{\prime}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{\ell}{\left[u^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)\right]}^{2}\mathrm{d}x={\left\lVert{\mathcal{A}}_{0}u\right\rVert}^{2}\,,\quad\forall u\in D_{0}\,.

Thus, it is evident that (
7.16
) follows since the operators
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are extensions of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
, respectively.

Due to
Remark
3.7
, the following relation follows from
Remark
7.2
:

\left\lVert{\mathcal{A}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}{\mathcal{B}}u\right\rVert=\left\lVert{\mathcal{A}}u\right\rVert\,,\quad\forall u\in D\left({\mathcal{A}}\right)\,.

Based on
Theorem
6.1
, we now formulate a theorem concerning the convergence of the scheme (
7.11a
)-(
7.11b
).

7.2  Legendre–Galerkin Spectral Method

It should be observed that solving the system (
7.5a
)-(
7.5
) reduces to solving the following intermediate system:

\displaystyle w_{1,k}\left(x\right)-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}q_{k}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}w_{1,k}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}f_{1,k}\left(x\right)+2u_{k}\left(x\right)-a_{1}\tau^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{k}}{\mathrm{d}x}\,,

(7.17a)

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\delta\right)w_{2,k}\left(x\right)-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}\gamma\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}w_{2,k}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}f_{2,k}\left(x\right)+2v_{k}\left(x\right)+a_{2}\tau^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{k}}{\mathrm{d}x}\,,

(7.17b)

\displaystyle w_{1,k}\left(0\right)=w_{1,k}\left(\ell\right)

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad w_{2,k}\left(0\right)=w_{2,k}\left(\ell\right)=0\,,

(7.17c)

for which
k=1,2,\ldots,n-1
.

By solving the system (
7.17a
)-(
7.17b
), the unknowns
u_{k}\left(x\right)
and
v_{k}\left(x\right)
are determined by the following formulas:

u_{k+1}\left(x\right)=w_{1,k}\left(x\right)-u_{k-1}\left(x\right)\,,\quad v_{k+1}\left(x\right)=w_{2,k}\left(x\right)-v_{k-1}\left(x\right)\,.

(7.18)

Before presenting the method in detail, we shall first introduce the shifted Legendre polynomials. These polynomials are obtained by an affine transformation that involves “shifting” and “scaling” the argument of the standard Legendre polynomials, mapping the interval
\left[0,\ell\right]
onto
\left[-1,1\right]
. Specifically, the shifted Legendre polynomial of degree
m
is defined as

\widetilde{P}_{m}\left(x\right)=P_{m}\left(\frac{2}{\ell}x-1\right)\,,\quad x\in\left[0,\ell\right]\,,

where
P_{m}\left(x\right)
represents the
m
-th standard Legendre polynomial. In the following discussion, the shifted Legendre polynomial of degree
m
is denoted by
\widetilde{P}_{m}\left(x\right)
.

Recall that the shifted Legendre polynomials retain an orthogonality property, similar to their unshifted counterparts, but adjusted to the interval
\left[0,\ell\right]
, yielding a dependence on the interval length
\ell
, namely:

\left(\widetilde{P}_{i},\widetilde{P}_{m}\right)=\ell A_{i}A_{m}\delta_{im}\,,\quad A_{m}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m+1}}\,,

(7.19)

where
\delta_{im}
denotes the Kronecker delta, taking the value
1
when
i=m
and
0
otherwise.

In order to approximate the solutions
w_{1,k}\left(x\right)
and
w_{2,k}\left(x\right)
of the system (
7.17a
)-(
7.17c
) at each temporal layer, we seek them in the form of the following linear combinations:

{w}_{k,N}^{\left(j\right)}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}{w}_{j,m}^{k}\phi_{m}\left(x\right)\,,\quad j=1,2\,.

(7.20)

Here, we choose the differences of the shifted Legendre polynomials as
ansatz
(trial) functions:

\phi_{m}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{A_{m}\sqrt{\ell}}\int\limits_{0}^{x}\widetilde{P}_{m}\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\sqrt{\ell}}{2}A_{m}\left(\widetilde{P}_{m+1}\left(x\right)-\widetilde{P}_{m-1}\left(x\right)\right)\,.

Observe that the functions
\phi_{m}\left(x\right)
inherently vanish at the endpoints of the interval
\left[0,\ell\right]
. Furthermore, it is obvious that:

\phi_{m}^{\prime}\left(x\right)=\widehat{P}_{m}\left(x\right)\,,\quad\widehat{P}_{m}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{A_{m}\sqrt{\ell}}\widetilde{P}_{m}\left(x\right)\,.

Evidently,
\widehat{P}_{m}\left(x\right)
denotes the orthonormal shifted Legendre polynomial on the interval
\left[0,\ell\right]
.

To approximate the solutions
u_{k}\left(x\right)
and
v_{k}\left(x\right)
of the system (
7.5a
)-(
7.5
) at each temporal layer, we employ the same linear combination as in (
7.20
), that is,

\widetilde{u}_{k,N}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\tilde{u}_{m}^{k}\phi_{m}\left(x\right)\,,\quad\widetilde{v}_{k,N}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\tilde{v}_{m}^{k}\phi_{m}\left(x\right)\,,\quad k=2,3,\ldots,n\,.

(7.21)

Substituting the Galerkin approximations defined by (
7.20
) and (
7.21
) into the relations provided in (
7.18
), we derive the following expressions for the expansion coefficients:

\tilde{u}_{m}^{k+1}={w}_{1,m}^{k}-\tilde{u}_{m}^{k-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{v}_{m}^{k+1}={w}_{2,m}^{k}-\tilde{v}_{m}^{k-1}\,.

Let the test functions be chosen to coincide with the trial functions
\phi_{m}\left(x\right)
. The derivation of the Galerkin system involves substituting the Galerkin approximations from (
7.20
) into equations (
7.17a
) and (
7.17b
), followed by taking the inner product of both sides of the resulting equations with the test functions
\phi_{m}\left(x\right)
for
m=1,2,\ldots,N
. Finally, the resulting Galerkin subsystems can be reformulated in the following matrix-vector form:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}_{N}^{k}\boldsymbol{w}_{1}^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\boldsymbol{I}_{k}^{\left(1\right)}+2\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}_{N}\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}}^{k}-\frac{2a_{1}\tau^{2}}{\ell}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{N}\boldsymbol{\tilde{v}}^{k}\,,

(7.22a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}_{N}\boldsymbol{w}_{2}^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2a_{0}\tau^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\boldsymbol{I}_{k}^{\left(2\right)}+a_{0}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}_{N}\boldsymbol{\tilde{v}}^{k}+\frac{a_{0}a_{2}\tau^{2}}{\ell}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{N}\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}}^{k}\,,\quad a_{0}=\frac{4}{2+\delta\tau^{2}}\,.

(7.22b)

The coefficient matrices corresponding to the subsystems (
7.22a
) and (
7.22b
) are defined by

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}_{N}^{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}_{N}+\frac{2\tau^{2}}{\ell^{2}}q_{k,N}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}_{N}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}_{N}+\frac{a_{0}\tau^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\gamma\boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_{N}\,,

for which

q_{k,N}=\alpha+\beta\sum_{m=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{u}_{m}^{k}\right)^{2}\,.

The matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}_{N}=\left(h_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
is symmetric and sparse. Its entries are defined as follows: the main diagonal entries are given by
h_{i,i}=2A_{i-1}^{2}A_{i+1}^{2}
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,N
. The nonzero entries on the second sub- and super-diagonals are
h_{i,i+2}=h_{i+2,i}=-A_{i}A_{i+1}^{2}A_{i+2}
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,N-2
. All other entries of this matrix are zero. The matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{B}}_{N}=\left(b_{i,j}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
is sparse and skew-symmetric, with nonzero entries confined to the first sub- and super-diagonals. Its off-diagonal entries satisfy
b_{i,i+1}=A_{i}A_{i+1}
and
b_{i+1,i}=-A_{i}A_{i+1}
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,N-1
, while all other entries, including those on the main diagonal, are equal to zero. Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{I}}_{N}
denotes the identity matrix of order
N
.

The column vectors
\boldsymbol{\tilde{u}}^{k}
,
\boldsymbol{\tilde{v}}^{k}
,
\boldsymbol{w}_{1}^{k}
, and
\boldsymbol{w}_{2}^{k}
, appearing in the subsystems of the Galerkin linear equations (
7.22a
) and (
7.22b
), consist of the expansion coefficients given in (
7.21
) and (
7.20
), respectively.

For each
j=1,2
, the vector

\boldsymbol{I}_{k}^{\left(j\right)}={\left(I_{k,1}^{\left(j\right)},I_{k,2}^{\left(j\right)},\ldots,I_{k,N}^{\left(j\right)}\right)}^{\top}

is defined by
I_{k,m}^{\left(j\right)}=\left(f_{j,k},\phi_{m}\right)
. Thus, the components
I_{k,m}^{\left(j\right)}
are given by the inner products of the functions
f_{j,k}\left(x\right)
with the basis functions
\phi_{m}\left(x\right)
.

Theorem 7.4
.

The coefficient matrices
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}_{N}^{k}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}_{N}
arising from the subsystems (
7.22a
) and (
7.22b
) are positive definite.

This
Theorem
follows from the subsequent
Lemma
.

Lemma 7.5
.

Consider a general operator equation in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,

Au=f\,,\quad f\in\mathcal{H}\,,

(7.23)

where the operator
A
is symmetric and satisfies the condition:

\left(Au,u\right)_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\alpha\left(Bu,u\right)_{\mathcal{H}}+\nu\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,,\quad\forall u\in D\left(A\right)\subset D\left(B\right)\,.

(7.24)

The operator
B
is also symmetric, and
D\left(A\right)\subset D\left(B\right)
, with
\alpha
and
\nu
being positive constants.

Let the basis functions
\left\{\phi_{m}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
be
B
-orthogonal, which is understood in the following sense:

\left(B\phi_{i},\phi_{m}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=b_{m}\delta_{im}\,,\quad b_{m}>0\,,

(7.25)

where
\delta_{im}
denotes the Kronecker delta.

The coefficient matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}_{N}=\left(\left(A\phi_{i},\phi_{m}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}\right)_{1\leq i,m\leq N}
, corresponding to the system of Galerkin linear equations derived using the Galerkin spectral method for equation (
7.23
), is positive definite.

Let us introduce the vector:

\boldsymbol{v}={\left(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{N}\right)}^{\top}\,.

Here,
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{N}
are the coefficients of the Galerkin approximation, corresponding to the basis functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\ldots,\phi_{N}
. This vector constitutes the solution to the Galerkin system of linear equations within the finite-dimensional subspace spanned by the chosen
N
basis functions.

It follows directly that:

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}_{N}\boldsymbol{v}={\left(\left(Au_{N},\phi_{1}\right)_{\mathcal{H}},\left(Au_{N},\phi_{2}\right)_{\mathcal{H}},\ldots,\left(Au_{N},\phi_{N}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}\right)}^{\top}\,,

where

u_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\phi_{i}\,.

(7.26)

In fact, we have:

\left(Au_{N},\phi_{m}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\left(A\phi_{i},\phi_{m}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}\,,\quad m=1,2,\ldots,N\,.

(7.27)

As a result of (
7.27
), we obtain:

\left(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}_{N}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\left(Au_{N},\phi_{i}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=\left(Au_{N},\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\phi_{i}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=\left(Au_{N},u_{N}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

(7.28)

By combining relations (
7.24
) and (
7.28
), it follows that:

\left(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}_{N}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}\right)\geq\alpha\left(Bu_{N},u_{N}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}+\nu\left\lVert u_{N}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,.

(7.29)

Substituting (
7.26
) into inequality (
7.29
) and leveraging the
B
-orthogonality property (
7.25
), we arrive at:

\displaystyle\left(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}_{N}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}B\phi_{i},\sum_{m=1}^{N}c_{m}\phi_{m}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}+\nu\left\lVert u_{N}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}c_{i}c_{m}\left(B\phi_{i},\phi_{m}\right)_{\mathcal{H}}=\alpha\sum_{m=1}^{N}b_{m}c_{m}^{2}\geq\widehat{\alpha}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\,,\quad\widehat{\alpha}=\alpha\min_{1\leq m\leq N}b_{m}\,.

Finally, from the inequality stated above, it follows that the matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{S}}_{N}
is positive definite.
∎

7.3  Estimate of the Error in the Legendre–Galerkin Spectral Method

In this subsection, we shall estimate the error incurred by the Legendre–Galerkin spectral method when applied to equations (
7.17a
) and (
7.17b
). For this purpose, we reformulate these equations in the following manner:

aw\left(x\right)-b\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}w\left(x\right)}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}=f\left(x\right)\,,\quad x\in\left[0,\ell\right]\,.

(7.32)

Here, the pair of coefficients
\left(a,b\right)
is defined as
\left(a,b\right)=\left(1,\tau^{2}q_{k,N}/2\right)
for equation (
7.17a
) and
\left(a,b\right)=\left(1+\tau^{2}\delta/2,\tau^{2}\gamma/2\right)
for equation (
7.17b
). In equation (
7.32
), the right-hand side
f\left(x\right)
is a continuous function, coinciding with the right-hand side of either equation (
7.17a
) or equation (
7.17b
), depending on the context under consideration.

Equation (
7.32
) is considered subject to homogeneous boundary conditions, which are specified as follows:

w\left(0\right)=w\left(\ell\right)=0\,.

(7.33)

The variational formulation of the problem (
7.32
)-(
7.33
) is well-established in the literature (
cf.
,
e.g.
, Kantorovich and Krylov
[
13
]
). The problem (
7.32
)-(
7.33
) is thus equivalent to the problem of minimizing the following functional:

I\left(w\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left(bw^{\prime 2}+aw^{2}-2fw\right)\mathrm{d}x\,,\quad w\left(0\right)=w\left(\ell\right)=0\,.

(7.34)

The subsequent discussion follows the proof technique outlined in the aforementioned book (see
Chap. IV
,
Sec. 4
in
[
13
]
), which concerns error estimation in the variational method for ordinary differential equations, with sines taken as the trial (basis) functions.

Let
\widetilde{w}\left(x\right)
be any function that satisfies the homogeneous boundary conditions
\widetilde{w}\left(0\right)=\widetilde{w}\left(\ell\right)=0
. Define the function
\eta\left(x\right)=\widetilde{w}\left(x\right)-w\left(x\right)
. It follows immediately that
\eta\left(0\right)=\eta\left(\ell\right)=0
.

From (
7.34
), a straightforward transformation yields:

\displaystyle I\left(\widetilde{w}\right)-I\left(w\right)

\displaystyle=I\left(w+\eta\right)-I\left(w\right)

\displaystyle=2\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left(bw^{\prime}\eta^{\prime}+aw\eta-f\eta\right)\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left(b\eta^{\prime 2}+a{\eta}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\,.

In the resulting expression, the first summand is simply the variation of the integral
I
, which vanishes,
i.e.
,
\delta I=0
. Consequently, we obtain:

I\left(\widetilde{w}\right)-I\left(w\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left(b\left(\widetilde{w}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right)^{2}+a\left(\widetilde{w}-w\right)^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\,.

(7.35)

Recall that we have chosen the following system of functions as the basis functions:

\phi_{m}\left(x\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{x}\widehat{P}_{m}\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\,,\quad m=1,2,\ldots\,,

where
\widehat{P}_{m}\left(x\right)
represents the orthonormal shifted Legendre polynomial defined on the interval
\left[0,\ell\right]
.

It is known that if
u\left(x\right)\in C^{p}\left(\left[0,\ell\right]\right)
, with
p\geq 1
, then the following bound holds (
cf.

Theorem 4.10
in Suetin
[
28
]
):

\left\lvert u\left(x\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{N}\hat{c}_{i}\widehat{P}_{i}\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{c_{60}}{N^{p-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\,,\quad x\in\left[0,\ell\right]\,.

(7.36)

Here,
\hat{c}_{i}
denotes the coefficient in the Fourier–Legendre series expansion.

Assume that the solution to the problem (
7.32
)-(
7.33
) is such that
w\left(x\right)\in C^{2}\left(\left[0,\ell\right]\right)
. From estimate (
7.36
), we have:

\left\lvert w^{\prime}\left(x\right)-S_{N}\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{c_{61}}{\sqrt{N}}\,,\quad x\in\left[0,\ell\right]\,,

(7.37)

where

S_{N}\left(x\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\hat{a}_{i}\widehat{P}_{i}\left(x\right)\,,\quad\hat{a}_{i}=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{i}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x\,.

Observe that

\hat{a}_{0}=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{0}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\left(w\left(\ell\right)-w\left(0\right)\right)=0\,.

Consider the following combination:

\Phi_{N}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\hat{a}_{m}\phi_{m}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\hat{a}_{m}\int\limits_{0}^{x}\widehat{P}_{m}\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\,.

Taking into account that
\Phi_{N}^{\prime}\left(x\right)=S_{N}\left(x\right)
, it follows from inequality (
7.37
) that:

\displaystyle\left\lvert w\left(x\right)-\Phi_{N}\left(x\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int\limits_{0}^{x}\left(w^{\prime}\left(s\right)-\Phi_{N}^{\prime}\left(s\right)\right)\mathrm{d}s\right\rvert=\left\lvert\int\limits_{0}^{x}\left(w^{\prime}\left(s\right)-S_{N}\left(s\right)\right)\mathrm{d}s\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left\lvert w^{\prime}\left(x\right)-S_{N}\left(x\right)\right\rvert\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{c_{61}\ell}{\sqrt{N}}\,.

Thus, we have established the completeness of the system of functions
\left\{\phi_{m}\left(x\right)\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
.

Consider the following approximation:

w_{N}\left(x\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}a_{m}\phi_{m}\left(x\right)\,,

where
a_{m}
(
m=1,2,\ldots,N
) represents the solution to the Ritz–Galerkin system.

It is important to note that, in our case, the systems of linear equations emerging from the Ritz–Galerkin and Legendre–Galerkin spectral methods coincide.

On the linear hull spanned by the system of functions
\phi_{1}\left(x\right),\phi_{2}\left(x\right),\ldots,\phi_{N}\left(x\right)
, the functional
I\left(w\right)
attains its minimum at
w\left(x\right)=w_{N}\left(x\right)
. From this, the following inequality holds:

I\left(w_{N}\right)-I\left(w\right)\leq I\left(\Phi_{N}\right)-I\left(w\right)\,.

By virtue of (
7.35
), this inequality takes the following form:

\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left[b\left({w}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right)^{2}+a\left({w}_{N}-w\right)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left[b\left({\Phi}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right)^{2}+a\left({\Phi}_{N}-w\right)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}x\,.

(7.38)

It follows immediately from (
7.38
) that the following result holds:

a\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left({w}_{N}-w\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left[b\left({\Phi}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right)^{2}+a\left({\Phi}_{N}-w\right)^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}x\,.

or, equivalently:

a\left\lVert{w}_{N}-w\right\rVert^{2}\leq b\left\lVert{\Phi}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}+a\left\lVert{\Phi}_{N}-w\right\rVert^{2}\,.

(7.39)

We shall now apply the classical Steklov inequality, which states:

\left\lVert w\right\rVert\leq\frac{\ell}{\pi}\left\lVert w^{\prime}\right\rVert\,.

(7.40)

Hence, in view of inequality (
7.40
), it follows that

\left\lVert\Phi_{N}-w\right\rVert\leq\frac{\ell}{\pi}\left\lVert\Phi_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert\,.

(7.41)

Substituting inequality (
7.41
) into (
7.39
), we find that:

\left\lVert{w}_{N}-w\right\rVert\leq b_{1}\left\lVert{\Phi}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert\,,\quad b_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{b}{a}+\frac{\ell^{2}}{\pi^{2}}}\,.

(7.42)

Taking into account that
\Phi_{N}^{\prime}\left(x\right)=S_{N}\left(x\right)
, the following inequality is obtained from (
7.42
):

\left\lVert{w}_{N}-w\right\rVert\leq b_{1}\left\lVert S_{N}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert\,.

(7.43)

Let us assume that the solution to problem (
7.32
)-(
7.33
),
w\left(x\right)\in C^{p+1}\left(\left[0,\ell\right]\right)
, with
p\geq 1
. Then, by virtue of bound (
7.36
), inequality (
7.43
) leads to the following estimate:

\left\lVert{w}_{N}-w\right\rVert\leq\frac{c_{62}}{N^{p-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\,.

(7.44)

Thus, the following theorem is valid.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let the solution to the boundary value problem (
7.32
)-(
7.33
) be such that
w\left(x\right)\in C^{p+1}\left(\left[0,\ell\right]\right)
with
p\geq 1
. Then, for the approximation
w_{N}\left(x\right)=a_{1}\phi_{1}\left(x\right)+a_{2}\phi_{2}\left(x\right)+\ldots+a_{N}\phi_{N}\left(x\right)
, where the coefficients
a_{m}

\left(m=1,2,\ldots,N\right)
are determined by solving the Ritz–Galerkin system, the error bound given by inequality (
7.44
) holds.

Remark 7.8
.

It is easy to observe that the validity of the error estimate stated in (
7.44
) naturally extends to a more general equation. Specifically, consider the equation

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(p\left(x\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}x}\right)-q\left(x\right)w\left(x\right)=f\left(x\right)\,,\quad x\in\left[0,\ell\right]\,,

(7.45)

subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. Assume that the functions
p\left(x\right)
,
q\left(x\right)
, and
f\left(x\right)
satisfy the standard regularity conditions. More precisely,
p\left(x\right)
is continuously differentiable, while
q\left(x\right)
and
f\left(x\right)
are continuous functions. Furthermore, the coefficient functions
p\left(x\right)
and
q\left(x\right)
are assumed to satisfy the uniform positivity conditions

p\left(x\right)\geq p_{0}>0\quad\text{and}\quad q\left(x\right)\geq q_{0}>0\,.

One should note that the error
w\left(x\right)-w_{N}\left(x\right)
can also be estimated using the uniform norm. To achieve this, additional transformations are required.

It is evident from inequality (
7.38
) that the following holds:

b\left\lVert{w}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}\leq b\left\lVert{\Phi}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}+a\left\lVert{\Phi}_{N}-w\right\rVert^{2}\,.

From this, taking into account (
7.41
), we deduce:

\left\lVert{w}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{a{\ell}^{2}}{b\pi^{2}}\right)\left\lVert{\Phi}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}\,,

or, equivalently:

\left\lVert{w}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq b_{2}\left\lVert S_{N}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert\,,\quad b_{2}=\sqrt{1+\frac{a{\ell}^{2}}{b\pi^{2}}}\,.

(7.46)

From the representation

w\left(x\right)=\int\limits_{0}^{x}w^{\prime}\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\,,

and by standard reasoning, one obtains (
cf.

Chap. IV
,
Sec. 4
in Kantorovich and Krylov
[
13
]
):

\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert w\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{2}}\left\lVert w^{\prime}\right\rVert\,.

By this inequality, we have:

\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert w_{N}\left(x\right)-w\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{2}}\left\lVert{w}_{N}^{\prime}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert\,.

From this, taking (
7.46
) into consideration, it follows that:

\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert w_{N}\left(x\right)-w\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq b_{2}\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{2}}\left\lVert S_{N}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert\,.

(7.47)

If
w\left(x\right)\in C^{p+1}\left(\left[0,\ell\right]\right)
with
p\geq 1
, then from (
7.47
), and in accordance with (
7.36
), the following inequality is obtained:

\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert w_{N}\left(x\right)-w\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{2}}\frac{b_{2}c_{63}}{N^{p-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\,.

(7.48)

It is observed that by substituting the values of
a
and
b
corresponding to equations (
7.17a
) and (
7.17b
) into the expression for
b_{2}
, the inequality
b_{2}\leq c_{64}/\tau
, with
\tau=T/n
, follows immediately. If the number of divisions
n
is chosen as the integer part of
N^{s}
(
n=\left[N^{s}\right]
), where
p-1/2-s>0
, then bound (
7.48
) takes the following form:

\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert w_{N}\left(x\right)-w\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{c_{65}}{N^{p-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-s}}\,,

where
p>1/2+s
,
p\geq 1
is a positive integer, and
s>0
.

Remark 7.9
.

The estimate (
7.48
) continues to hold even for the general equation (
7.45
). In this setting, the constant
b_{2}
is precisely defined by the following relation:

b_{2}=\sqrt{p_{0}^{-1}\left(\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}p\left(x\right)+\frac{\ell^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}q\left(x\right)\right)}\,.

(7.49)

In our opinion, the reader will be interested in an explicit error estimate with a uniform norm in the natural class of solutions for equation (
7.45
).

Remark 7.10
.

Let equation (
7.45
) be considered with homogeneous boundary conditions. If the standard regularity conditions are satisfied (see
Remark
7.8
), then the following estimate is valid:

\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert w_{N}\left(x\right)-w\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{\hat{b}_{2}}{\sqrt{2\left(2N+1\right)\left(2N+5\right)}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert\,,

(7.50)

where
\hat{b}_{2}=\hat{b}_{1}b_{2}\ell\sqrt{\ell}
,
b_{2}
is specified by equality (
7.49
), and

\hat{b}_{1}=p_{0}^{-2}\left(\frac{\ell}{\pi}\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert p^{\prime}\left(x\right)\right\rvert+\frac{{\ell}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}q\left(x\right)+p_{0}\right)\,.

Let
\hat{c}_{m}
denote the coefficients of the Legendre–Fourier series expansion of the function
w^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)
, given by

\hat{c}_{m}=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{m}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x\quad\left(m=0,1,2,\ldots\right)\,.

We now establish the relationship between the coefficients
\hat{c}_{m}
and
\hat{a}_{m}
, where
\hat{a}_{m}
denotes the coefficients of the Legendre–Fourier series expansion of the function
w^{\prime}\left(x\right)
. For the orthonormal shifted Legendre polynomials, the following relation holds:

\widehat{P}_{m}\left(x\right)=\frac{\ell}{2}A_{m}\left(A_{m+1}\widehat{P}_{m+1}^{\prime}\left(x\right)-A_{m-1}\widehat{P}_{m-1}^{\prime}\left(x\right)\right)\,.

(7.51)

Upon considering (
7.51
), the following result is derived:

\hat{a}_{m}=\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{m}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\ell}{2}A_{m}\left(A_{m+1}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{m+1}^{\prime}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x-A_{m-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{m-1}^{\prime}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x\right)\,.

(7.52)

By applying integration by parts to the first summand of equality (
7.52
), we obtain:

\displaystyle A_{m+1}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{m+1}^{\prime}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=A_{m+1}\left(\left[w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{m+1}\left(x\right)\right]_{0}^{\ell}-\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{m+1}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\left(w^{\prime}\left(\ell\right)+\left(-1\right)^{m}w^{\prime}\left(0\right)\right)-A_{m+1}\hat{c}_{m+1}\,.

Using the same reasoning, for the second summand of equation (
7.52
), we arrive at the following result:

A_{m-1}\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}w^{\prime}\left(x\right)\widehat{P}_{m-1}^{\prime}\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\left(w^{\prime}\left(\ell\right)+\left(-1\right)^{m}w^{\prime}\left(0\right)\right)-A_{m-1}\hat{c}_{m-1}\,.

By substituting the last two resulting equalities into (
7.52
), one yields:

\hat{a}_{m}=\frac{\ell}{2}A_{m}\left(A_{m-1}\hat{c}_{m-1}-A_{m+1}\hat{c}_{m+1}\right)\,,

From this, it follows that:

\hat{a}_{m}^{2}\leq\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}A_{m}^{2}\left(A_{m-1}^{2}\hat{c}_{m-1}^{2}+A_{m+1}^{2}\hat{c}_{m+1}^{2}\right)\,.

Summing both sides of the preceding inequality from
N+1
to
\infty
and invoking Bessel’s inequality leads to the following result:

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{N}-w^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty}\hat{a}_{m}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty}A_{m}^{2}\left(A_{m-1}^{2}\hat{c}_{m-1}^{2}+A_{m+1}^{2}\hat{c}_{m+1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}A_{N+1}^{2}\left(A_{N}^{2}\sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty}\hat{c}_{m-1}^{2}+A_{N+2}^{2}\sum_{m=N+1}^{\infty}\hat{c}_{m+1}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}A_{N+1}^{2}\left(A_{N}^{2}+A_{N+2}^{2}\right)\sum_{m=N}^{\infty}\hat{c}_{m}^{2}\leq\frac{\ell^{2}}{\left(2N+1\right)\left(2N+5\right)}\left\lVert w^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

(7.53)

It is observed that, analogous to equation (
7.32
), inequality (
7.47
) also holds for equation (
7.45
), where the constant
b_{2}
is determined by equality (
7.49
). Substituting inequality (
7.3
) into (
7.47
) yields:

\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert w_{N}\left(x\right)-w\left(x\right)\right\rvert\leq\frac{b_{2}\ell\sqrt{\ell}}{\sqrt{2\left(2N+1\right)\left(2N+5\right)}}\left\lVert w^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert\,.

(7.54)

For equation (
7.45
), from the condition that the variation of the energy functional vanishes, it follows that:

\int\limits_{0}^{\ell}\left(pw^{\prime 2}+qw^{2}+fw\right)\mathrm{d}x=0\,.

(7.55)

By straightforward reasoning, from equation (
7.55
), it can be deduced that:

\left\lVert w^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{p_{0}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert\left\lVert w\right\rVert\,.

From this, and considering (
7.40
), the following is obtained:

\left\lVert w^{\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{\ell}{\pi p_{0}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert\,,\quad\left\lVert w\right\rVert\leq\frac{\ell^{2}}{\pi^{2}p_{0}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert\,.

(7.56)

We shall rewrite equation (
7.45
) in the following form:

pw^{\prime\prime}=-p^{\prime}w^{\prime}+qw+f\,.

Whence, based on the estimates provided in (
7.56
), the following conclusion follows:

\left\lVert w^{\prime\prime}\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{p_{0}}\left(\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}\left\lvert p^{\prime}\left(x\right)\right\rvert\left\lVert w^{\prime}\right\rVert+\max_{0\leq x\leq\ell}q\left(x\right)\left\lVert w\right\rVert+\left\lVert f\right\rVert\right)\leq\hat{b}_{1}\left\lVert f\right\rVert\,.

(7.57)

Finally, comparing inequalities (
7.54
) and (
7.57
) gives the estimate (
7.50
).
∎

Remark
.

It is clear that if the solution of problem (
7.1
)-(
7.4
) is the product of a polynomial in the spatial variable
x
and a linear function in the temporal variable
t
, then the theoretical solution derived by the proposed combined scheme coincides precisely with the exact analytical solution. In this situation, the error of the numerically computed approximation is of the order of machine precision.

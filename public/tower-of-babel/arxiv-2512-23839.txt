Prime ideals in the Boolean polynomial semiring
Kalina Mincheva, Naufil Sakran

Abstract
. In this article, we disprove a conjecture of F. Alarcón and D. Anderson and give a complete classification of the prime ideals in the one variable polynomial semiring with coefficients in Boolean semifield. We group the prime ideals of 𝔹 ⁡ [ x ] {\mathbb{B}}[x] into three classes, indexed by integers.



1.
Introduction

The study of prime ideals in polynomial rings plays a fundamental role in algebra and algebraic geometry, providing insights into their structure, decomposition properties, and applications in various mathematical disciplines. In classical commutative algebra, the classification of prime ideals in polynomial rings over fields has been extensively studied, leading to deep connections with algebraic geometry and number theory. Moreover, there is an algorithm to verify if an ideal of such a ring is prime. However, if we replace the coefficient field with an additively idempotent semifield, the problem of classifying primes ideals becomes very different and challenging due to the lack of additive inverse in additively idempotent semirings.

One of the main motivations for the study of prime ideals of polynomial semirings with coefficients in idempotent semifields comes from tropical geometry. The goal is to understand the underlying algebra of tropical spaces in order to strengthen existing tropical methods. We note that the k-ideals or subtractive ideals
[
3
]
that are usually studied in the context of semirings are a very small and restrictive class of ideals in polynomial (additively idempotent) semirings and do not contain interesting geometric information. Instead, of greater interest have been other classes of ideals such as tropical ideals or closed ideals defined and studied in
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
. In
[
6
]
the authors study prime closed and prime tropical ideals using congruence methods.

Of particular interest is the polynomial semiring with coefficients in the Boolean semifield
{\mathbb{B}}
, which is the smallest idempotent semifield. The Boolean semifield has two elements
\{1,0\}
, where
1
is the multiplicative identity,
0
is the additive identity and
1+1=1
. This article focuses on classifying the prime ideals of the semiring
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
. The prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
behave very differently than those of
k[x]
where
k
is any field. For example, the ideal
\langle 1+x\rangle
is not prime in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
.

One of the first studies of prime ideals in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
was done by F. Alarcón and D. Anderson in
[
1
]
, where they gave the following partial classification of the prime ideals in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
. They relate the ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
to prime subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
. To a subset
\mathfrak{A}
of
{\mathbb{N}}
we can associate two ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle=\langle x,\{1+x^{a}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A}\}\rangle\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle=\langle x,\{1+x^{a}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A}\}\rangle-\{x^{m}f\mid m\geq 0,\,f\notin\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}\}.

Definition 1.1
.

We call a subset
\mathfrak{A}
of
{\mathbb{N}}
prime if
a+b\in\mathfrak{A}
then either
a\in\mathfrak{A}
or
b\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Theorem
[
1
, Theorem 11, for ideals containing
\langle x\rangle
]
.

The ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
is prime if and only if
\mathfrak{A}
is a prime subset. If
\mathcal{P}
is a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
containing the ideal
\langle x\rangle
such that
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}=\{a\mid 1+x^{a}\in\mathcal{P}\}
, then
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}}
.

The above theorem classifies all the prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
that contain the ideal
\langle x\rangle
. For the classification for prime ideals not containing
\langle x\rangle
, Alarcón and Anderson showed the following.

Theorem
[
1
, Theorem 11, for ideals not containing
\langle x\rangle
]
.

The ideal
\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
is prime if and only if
\mathfrak{A}
is a prime subset.

In
[
1
]
, the authors conjecture that if
\mathcal{P}
is a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
, not containing the ideal
\langle x\rangle
then
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
for some prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
in
{\mathbb{N}}
. In Example
2.4
, we will show that this is not the case, and the following ideal

\mathcal{J}=\left\langle 1+x,\{1+x^{a}+x^{a+1}:a\geq 2\}\right\rangle

does not correspond to any ideal of the form
\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
for any prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
.

In this article, we will give a complete classification of prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
that do not contain the principal ideal
\langle x\rangle
. Our first main result indicates that prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
can be parametrized by prime subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
. Then the set

\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}:=\{a:1+x^{a}\in\mathcal{P}\}

is a prime subset.

Theorem
A
suggests that we have a surjective map from the set of prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
to the set of prime subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
, given by
\mathcal{P}\longmapsto\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}
. This motivates us to partition the set of prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
by their corresponding prime subsets, and say that a prime ideal
\mathcal{P}
belongs to “class”
[\mathfrak{A}]
if
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathfrak{A}
. Therefore, for a specific prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
of
{\mathbb{N}}
, we aim to determine all the prime ideals in the class
[\mathfrak{A}]
.

Following this idea, we study how the structure of a prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
affects the type of prime ideals in
[\mathfrak{A}]
. In Lemma
2.3
, we show that
\mathfrak{A}
is a prime subset if and only if
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
is a submonoid in
{\mathbb{N}}
. Now, since submonoids of
{\mathbb{N}}
are finitely generated, there exists a minimal generating set
\{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{s}\}
of the submonoid
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
. Denote by
d
the greatest common divisor of
b_{1},\cdots,b_{s}
, i.e.,
\text{d}=\gcd(b_{1},\cdots,b_{s})\geq 1
. One sees that prime subsets in
{\mathbb{N}}
can be parametrized by the set of positive integers as follows: for any fixed
\text{d}\geq 1
, let
[\text{d}]
denote the set of all the prime subsets
\mathfrak{A}
, for which
\text{d}=\gcd(b_{1},\cdots,b_{s})
, where
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}=\langle b_{1},\cdots,b_{s}\rangle
. Hence, viewing prime subsets
\mathfrak{A}
in terms of their corresponding semigroup
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
, we are able partition the set of all prime subsets indexed by the set of positive integers
{\mathbb{N}}
. For example, for
\text{d}=1
, the class
[\text{d}]
consists of all finite prime subsets
\mathfrak{A}
(i.e
|\mathfrak{A}|<\infty
).

Using this terminology, we provide a complete classification of prime ideals in terms of the class
[\text{d}]
to which they belong. By combining our results with those of F. Alarcón and D. Anderson in
[
1
]
, we state below the main theorem of this article, which completes the classification. By convention, the empty set is also a prime subset.

Notation 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a non-empty prime subset and let
f(x)=1+x^{m_{1}}+\cdots+x^{m_{r}}\in{\mathbb{B}}[x]
. We say
f
satisfies property
\star
if the set
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\}
satisfies the following:

•

m_{i}\notin\mathfrak{A}
for all
i
,

•

there exists
i\neq j
such that
m_{j}-m_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}
,

•

and there exists
t
such that
|m_{t}-m_{j}|\notin\mathfrak{A}
for all
j
.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a non-empty prime subset in
{\mathbb{N}}
and let
\mathcal{S}
be the semigroup
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
generated by
\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\rangle
. Let
\text{d}=\gcd(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
.
Let
\mathcal{J}
be a prime ideal not containing
\langle x\rangle
in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
.

(1)

if
\text{d}=1
, then either
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
or
\mathcal{J}
is of the form

\mathcal{J}=\langle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\cup\mathcal{Q}\rangle.

where
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\{1+x^{m_{1}}+\cdots+x^{m_{r}}\mid\textrm{$m_{i}^{\prime}s$ satisfy property $\star$}\}
.

(2)

if
\text{d}>1
and
\text{d}\notin\mathfrak{A}
then
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
.

(3)

if
\text{d}>1
and
\text{d}\in\mathfrak{A}
then either
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
or
\mathcal{J}
is of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\langle

\displaystyle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}

\displaystyle\cup\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1,\,a\in\mathfrak{A}\textrm{ with $a<d$ or $a\geq A$ or $d\nmid a$}\}\cup\mathcal{Q}\rangle,

for some unique positive integer
A
, where
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\{1+x^{m_{1}}+\cdots+x^{m_{r}}\mid\textrm{$m_{i}^{\prime}s$ satisfy property $\star$}\}
.

The classification of prime ideals in the multivariable Boolean semiring
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
remains an open problem. In the final section, we present some partial results on the case
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

Let
{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,3,\rightarrow\}
where the arrow
\rightarrow
indicates all the subsequent integers.

Definition 2.1
.

A subset
\mathfrak{A}
in
{\mathbb{N}}
is said to be
prime
if for any
a+b\in\mathfrak{A}
, we have either
a\in\mathfrak{A}
or
b\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
\mathfrak{A}=\emptyset
is a prime subset by default. Also, for any non-empty prime subset, we must have
1\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

A subset
\mathfrak{A}
in
{\mathbb{N}}
is prime if and only if
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
is a submonoid in
{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a prime subset and let
x,y\in{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
. Then if
x+y\in\mathfrak{A}
, it would imply
x\in\mathfrak{A}
or
y\in\mathfrak{A}
, which is a contradiction. So, we must have
x+y\in{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
. Therefore,
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
is a submonoid.

Conversely, suppose
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
is a submonoid and let
x+y\in\mathfrak{A}
. If
x\notin\mathfrak{A}
, then we must have
y\in\mathfrak{A}
as if it were not the case, then we would have
x+y\in{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
, a contradiction.
∎

We say that a non-empty set
R
is a
semiring
with respect to the binary operations
(+,\cdot)
if it satisfies the same axioms as those in a ring, except we do not require the addition to be invertible. A
semifield
is a commutative semiring with multiplicative unit in which all nonzero elements have multiplicative inverse.

We will denote by
{\mathbb{B}}
the
Boolean semifield
. Its underlying set is
\{0,1\}
with addition and multiplication given by the rules:

0+0=0,\qquad 0+1=1+0=1+1=1,\qquad 0\cdot 0=0,\qquad 0\cdot 1=1\cdot 0=0,\qquad 1\cdot 1=1.

By
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
we denote the polynomial semiring with coefficients in
{\mathbb{B}}
. Let
\mathcal{I}
be a subset of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
. We say
\mathcal{I}
is an
ideal
in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
if for any
f,g\in\mathcal{I}
, we have
f+g\in\mathcal{I}
and
{\mathbb{B}}[x]\cdot\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{I}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{I}
is said to be a
prime ideal
if for any
fg\in\mathcal{I}
, we have either
f\in\mathcal{I}
or
g\in\mathcal{I}
. We list here some properties of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
important to our discussion.

•

{\mathbb{B}}[x]
is not UFD as
(1+x+x^{3})(1+x^{2}+x^{3})=(1+x)(1+x^{2})(1+x^{3})
.

•

The ideal
\langle 1+x\rangle
is not prime as
(1+x^{2}+x^{3})(1+x+x^{3})=(1+x)^{6}
and neither of the factors on the left-hand side are in the ideal.

•

If
f(x)\in{\mathbb{B}}[x]
is a degree
N
polynomial with
1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)
, then

f(x)(1+x)^{N}=(1+x)^{2N}.

This implies that every prime ideal of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
contains the polynomial
1+x
, except for the prime ideal
\langle x\rangle
.

We can divide the primes of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
into two categories:

(I)

those that contain
\langle x\rangle
,

(II)

and those that do not contain
\langle x\rangle
.

Throughout the paper, if a prime ideal lies in category (I), we denote it by
\mathcal{I}
(
\mathcal{I}^{\prime},\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
, etc.) and if the prime ideal lies in category (II), we denote it by
\mathcal{J}
(
\mathcal{J}^{\prime},\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
, etc.). Observe that we can express the ideal
\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}:=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}-\{x^{m}f\mid m\geq 0,\,f\notin\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}\}
also as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle=\{x^{m}f\mid m\geq 0,\,f\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}\textrm{ with $1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)$}\}\cup\{0\}

\displaystyle=\langle\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A}\textrm{ and }m\geq 0\}.\rangle

We will mostly use the above description of
\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
throughout the article. The following example contradicts the conjecture of F. Alarcón and D. Anderson in
[
1
]
.

Example 2.4
.

The ideal

\mathcal{J}=\left\langle 1+x,\{1+x^{a}+x^{a+1}:a\geq 2\}\right\rangle

is a prime ideal in category (II) and it is not of the form
\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
for any prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
.

We verify that
\mathcal{J}
is a prime ideal. Observe that an alternative way of writing
\mathcal{J}
is:

\mathcal{J}=\left\{f\in{\mathbb{B}}[x]:\textrm{ if $x^{a}$ is the leading monomial of $f$ then $x^{a-1}+x^{a}\in\textrm{Supp}(f)$}\right\}.

Let
f=\sum_{i}x^{a_{i}}
and
g=\sum_{j}x^{b_{j}}
be elements of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
with leading monomials
x^{a}
and
x^{b}
, respectively. Let
fg\in\mathcal{J}
. By the definition above,
x^{a+b-1}+x^{a+b}\in\textrm{Supp}(fg)
. But then this implies
x^{a-1}\in\textrm{Supp}(f)
or
x^{b-1}\in\textrm{Supp}(g)
, implying that
f\in\mathcal{J}
or
g\in\mathcal{J}
. Hence,
\mathcal{J}
is a prime ideal. Here
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{J}}=\{a\mid 1+x^{a}\in\mathcal{J}\}=\{1\}
.

Finally,
\mathcal{J}
is not of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{J}}}=\langle\{1+x+x^{m}:m\geq 1\}\rangle,

since
1+x^{2}+x^{3}\in\mathcal{J}
but
1+x^{2}+x^{3}\notin\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{J}}}
.

3.
Prime ideals and prime subsets

In this section, we prove that all the prime ideals in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
can be parametrized by prime subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
. Then the set

\mathfrak{A}:=\{a\mid 1+x^{a}\in\mathcal{P}\}

is a prime subset.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{A}=\emptyset
or
\mathfrak{A}=\{1\}
, then we have nothing to prove. Now let
|\mathfrak{A}|>1
and suppose
\mathfrak{A}
is not a prime subset, then there exists
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and
r,s\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\mathfrak{A}
such that
r+s=a
. Assume that
r\leq s
. We claim that the following

(1)

\displaystyle(1+x^{r})^{s}(1+x^{s})^{s}(1+x^{a})=(1+x^{r})^{s+1}(1+x^{s})^{s+1}.

holds in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
. It suffices to show that all possible linear combinations
ir+i^{\prime}s
, where
0\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq s+1
can be written as the linear combination of
jr+j^{\prime}s+j^{\prime\prime}a
for
0\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq s
and
0\leq j^{\prime\prime}\leq 1
and vice versa. To see this, consider a linear combination
ir+i^{\prime}s
, where
0\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq s+1
.

(1)

If
i,i^{\prime}\leq s
then we can take
j=i
and
j^{\prime}=i^{\prime}
.

(2)

If
i=s+1
and
i^{\prime}=0
then we can take
j=1
and
j^{\prime}=r
.

(3)

If
i^{\prime}=s+1
and
i=0
then since
i^{\prime}s=rs+(i^{\prime}-r)s,
we can take
j=s
and
j^{\prime}=i^{\prime}-r
.

(4)

If
i=s+1
and
0<i^{\prime}\leq s
then since
ir+i^{\prime}s=sr+(i^{\prime}-1)s+a,
we can take
j=s,j^{\prime}=i^{\prime}-1
and
j^{\prime\prime}=1
.

(5)

If
0<i\leq s
and
i^{\prime}=s+1
then since
ir+i^{\prime}s=(i-1)r+s^{2}+a
we can take
j=i-1,j^{\prime}=s
and
j^{\prime\prime}=1
.

(6)

If
i=s+1
and
i^{\prime}=s+1
then since
ir+i^{\prime}s=(i-1)r+(i^{\prime}-1)s+a
we can take
j=i-1,j^{\prime}=i^{\prime}-1
and
j^{\prime\prime}=1
.

Similarly, consider an arbitrary linear combination
jr+j^{\prime}s+j^{\prime\prime}a
for
0\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq s
and
0\leq j^{\prime\prime}\leq 1
.

(1)

If
j,j^{\prime}\leq s
and
j^{\prime\prime}=0
then we can simply take
i=j
and
i^{\prime}=j^{\prime}
.

(2)

If
j,j^{\prime}\leq s
and
j^{\prime\prime}=1
then we can simply take
i=j+1
and
i^{\prime}=j^{\prime}+1
.

This proves that Equation (
1
) holds. The left-hand side of Equation (
1
) is in
\mathcal{P}
but neither
1+x^{r}
nor
1+x^{s}
is in
\mathcal{P}
as
r,s\notin\mathfrak{A}
. This contradicts our assumption that
\mathfrak{A}
is a prime set.
∎

We end the section with the following lemma that will be used repeatedly throughout the paper:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a prime ideal and let
a\in\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}
. Now for any
m>a
such that
m,m-a\notin\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}
, we have
1+x^{a}+x^{m}\in\mathcal{P}
or
1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}\in\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Using the fact that

(a+b+c)(ac+bc+ab)=(a+b)(b+c)(a+c),

we have

(1+x^{a}+x^{m})(1+x^{m-a}+x^{m})=(1+x^{a})(1+x^{m})(1+x^{m-a})\in\mathcal{P}.

Since the right-hand side of the above equation is in
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}
is prime, we must have
1+x^{a}+x^{m}\in\mathcal{P}
or
1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}\in\mathcal{P}
.
∎

4.
Classification of the prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]

Recall that we divided the prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
into two categories, where category (I) refers to prime ideals containing
\langle x\rangle
and category (II) refers to prime ideals not containing
\langle x\rangle
. In this section, we prove that there are at most two types of prime ideals in category (II) with respect to each
\text{d}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
d
is the greatest common factor of the generators of the semigroup
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
. This will complete the classification of all prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
. Throughout the section,
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
and
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
will denote prime ideals in category (I) and category (II), respectively.

We begin with the observation that we can partition the set of all prime subsets in
{\mathbb{N}}
with respect to the positive integers. For any prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
in
{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\}
minimally generates the semigroup
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}
and let
\text{d}=\gcd(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r})
. If
\text{d}=1
, then
\mathfrak{A}
is a finite set, and if
\text{d}>1
, then
\mathfrak{A}
is an infinite set. From Theorem
3.1
we see that there exists a surjective map from the set of prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
to the set of prime subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}
. In particular, every prime ideal
\mathcal{P}
is associated to a prime subset in
{\mathbb{N}}
. We denote the corresponding prime subset by
\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}
.

As a result, we can partition the primes
\mathcal{P}
with respect to the greatest common divisor
d
of the generating set
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}_{\mathcal{P}}
. Henceforth, we say a prime ideal
\mathcal{J}
belongs to the class
d
if the corresponding prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
is in class
d
. We first classify prime ideals
\mathcal{J}
in class
\text{d}=1
.

4.1.
The case when
\text{d}=1

In this case, the corresponding prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
is a finite set. Throughout the section, we let
\alpha=\max\{\mathfrak{A}\}+1
. We give some useful identities in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and
q\leq a
then
1+x^{q}+x^{a}\in\mathcal{J}
.

Proof.

The case
q\in\mathfrak{A}
is immediate as
1+x^{q}+1+x^{a}=1+x^{a}\in\mathcal{J}
. Let
q\notin\mathfrak{A}
. Since
\mathfrak{A}
is a prime subset, we must have
a-q\in\mathfrak{A}
. This implies
1+x^{a-q}\in\mathcal{J}
and hence,
1+x^{a}+x^{q}(1+x^{a-q})=1+x^{q}+x^{a}\in\mathcal{J}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
m-1>\alpha
, then

(1+x^{\alpha})^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})=(1+x^{m-1}+x^{m})+x^{\alpha}(1+x)^{q-2\alpha}+(1+x+x^{m-1})x^{q-m+1}

for some positive integer
q
.

Proof.

Expanding the product, we have

\displaystyle(1+x^{\alpha})^{m}(1+x^{\alpha+1})\cdots(1+x^{2\alpha-1})

\displaystyle=1+x^{\alpha}+x^{\alpha+1}+\cdots+x^{q-\alpha}+x^{q}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i=0}^{q-2\alpha}x^{\alpha+i}+x^{q}

\displaystyle=(1+x^{m-1}+x^{m})+x^{\alpha}(1+x)^{q-2\alpha}+(1+x+x^{m-1})x^{q-m+1}

where
q=m\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(\alpha+i)=\frac{\alpha(2m+3\alpha-3)}{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and
m
be an integer such that
\alpha\leq m<\alpha+a
. Then

\displaystyle(1+x^{m-a})(1+x^{\alpha})^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})

\displaystyle=(1+x^{m-a}+x^{m})+x^{\alpha}(1+x)^{q-2\alpha+m-a}+(1+x^{a}+x^{q-\alpha})x^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+(1+x^{a}+x^{q+m-a-\alpha})x^{\alpha}

for some positive integer

.

Proof.

Expanding the product, we have

\displaystyle(1+x^{m-a})(1+x^{\alpha})^{m}(1+x^{\alpha+1})\cdots(1+x^{2\alpha-1})

\displaystyle=1+x^{m-a}+x^{\alpha}+\cdots+x^{q-\alpha+m-a}+x^{q}+x^{q+m-a}

\displaystyle=1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}+\sum_{i=0}^{q-2\alpha+m-a}x^{\alpha+i}+x^{q}+x^{q+m-a}.

where
q=m\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(\alpha+i)=\frac{\alpha(2m+3\alpha-3)}{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4

Let

and

\displaystyle(1+x^{m-a})(1+x^{m})^{k-1}(1+x^{\alpha})^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})

\displaystyle=(1+x^{m-a}+x^{m})+x^{\alpha}(1+x)^{q-2\alpha+km-a}

\displaystyle+(1+x^{a})\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x^{im-a}+x^{q+im-a})\right)

\displaystyle+(1+x^{a}+x^{a+\alpha})x^{q-\alpha+km-2a}.

Proof.

Expanding the product, we have

\displaystyle(1+x^{m-a})(1+x^{m})^{k-1}(1+x^{\alpha})^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})

\displaystyle=1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}+x^{2m-a}+x^{2m}+\cdots+x^{(k-1)m-a}

\displaystyle+x^{(k-1)m}+x^{\alpha}+x^{\alpha+1}+x^{\alpha+2}+\cdots+x^{km-a}+\cdots

\displaystyle+x^{q-\alpha+km-a}+x^{q+m-a}+x^{q+m}+x^{q+2m-a}+x^{q+2m}

\displaystyle+\cdots+x^{q+(k-1)m-a}+x^{q+(k-1)m}+x^{q+km-a}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(x^{im}+x^{(i+1)m-a})+\sum_{i=0}^{q-2\alpha+km-a}x^{\alpha+i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(x^{q+im}+x^{q+(i+1)m-a}),

where
q=m\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(\alpha+i)=\frac{\alpha(2m+3\alpha-3)}{2}
. Note that

lies in the sum

Lemma 4.6
.

The set
\{1+x+x^{q}\mid q\geq 1\}
is contained in

if and only if there exists a positive integer
m
for which
1+x+x^{m}\in\mathcal{J}
but
m,m-1\notin\mathfrak{A}
.

Proof.

The forward direction requires no proof. For the reverse direction, suppose
m
is the smallest positive integer such that
m,m-1\notin\mathfrak{A}

and let
n

with

3.2

Suppose

\displaystyle(1+x^{m-1})(1+x^{m})^{k-1}(1+x^{\alpha})^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})

\displaystyle=(1+x^{n-1}+x^{n})+x^{\alpha}(1+x)^{q-2\alpha+km-1}

\displaystyle+(1+x)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x^{im-1}+x^{q+im-1})\right)

\displaystyle+(1+x+x^{m})x^{q+(k-1)m-1}

, giving a contradiction. Note that This also implies

Suppose

\displaystyle(1+x^{n-1})(1+x^{n})^{k-1}(1+x^{\alpha})^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})

.

For the reverse direction, suppose
\{1+x+x^{q}\mid q\geq 1\}

\displaystyle(1+x^{m-a})(1+x^{\alpha})^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, if
\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
then
\mathcal{J}\cap\{1+x^{n}+x^{n+a}\mid n\geq 1,\,n,n+a\notin\mathfrak{A}\}
is empty.

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathfrak{A}
be fixed, and suppose that
\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
. Assume there exists
n
such that
n-a,n\notin\mathfrak{A}
but
1+x^{n-a}+x^{n}\in\mathcal{J}
. Then by adjusting Lemma
4.4
(substituting
k-1=\alpha
), the element

\displaystyle(1+x^{n-a})(1+x^{n})^{\alpha}(1+x^{\alpha})^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})

\displaystyle=(1+x^{n-a}+x^{n})+x^{\alpha}(1+x)^{q-2\alpha+(\alpha+1)n-a}

\displaystyle+(1+x^{a})\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}(x^{in-a}+x^{q+in-a})\right)

\displaystyle+(1+x^{a}+x^{n})x^{q+\alpha n-a}

lies in
\mathcal{J}
, a contradiction. Hence, we must have
1+x^{n-a}+x^{n}\notin\mathcal{J}
. In particular,
\mathcal{J}\cap\{1+x^{n}+x^{n+a}\mid n\geq 1,\,n,n+a\notin\mathfrak{A}\}
is empty.
∎

Similarly, the converse of the above statement is also true by the same reasoning.

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, if
\{1+x^{n}+x^{n+a}\mid n\geq 1,\,n,n+a\notin\mathfrak{A}\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
then
\mathcal{J}\cap\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1,\,m,m-a\notin\mathfrak{A}\}
is empty.

The above lemmas can be summarized into the following statements:

(1)

For any
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, if
1+x^{a}+x^{m}\in\mathcal{J}
for some
m>a
such that
m-a,m\notin\mathfrak{A}
then
\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
.

(2)

\{1+x+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
if and only if
\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},\,m\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

(3)

For a fixed element
a\in\mathfrak{A}
if
\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
then
\mathcal{J}\cap\{1+x^{n}+x^{n+a}\mid n\geq 1,\,n,n+a\notin\mathfrak{A}\}
is empty.

(4)

For a fixed element
a\in\mathfrak{A}
if
\{1+x^{n}+x^{n+a}\mid n\geq 1,\,n,n+a\notin\mathfrak{A}\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
then
\mathcal{J}\cap\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\}
is empty.

Using this, we can conclude that for any prime ideal
\mathcal{J}
in category (II) with
\text{d}=1
, exactly one of the following containments holds:

\langle\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{J},

or

\langle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{J}.

In particular, there are two distinct types of prime ideals in category (II), based on the containment described above. The following theorem shows that there is only one prime ideal of the first type.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
such that
\langle x\rangle
is not contained in
\mathcal{J}
. We define

\mathfrak{A}:=\{a:1+x^{a}\in\mathcal{J}\}.

Suppose
|\mathfrak{A}|<\infty
. If

\langle\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{J}

then
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathfrak{A}\neq\emptyset
as
1\in\mathfrak{A}
. Let
\alpha=\max\{\mathfrak{A}\}+1
. Suppose

f(x)\in\mathcal{J}\backslash\langle\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\rangle.

be an irreducible polynomial. Without loss of generality, we can assume
1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)
. Also,
\textrm{Supp}(f)
cannot contain the term
x^{a}
for any
a\in\mathfrak{A}
, since such terms would imply
f\in\langle\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},\,m\geq 0\}\rangle
. Let
f(x)=1+x^{m_{1}}+\cdots+x^{m_{r}}
, with
m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}
. If
m_{1}\geq\alpha
, then we reach a contradiction by Lemma
4.2
. Now, let
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
be the largest index such that
m_{\ell}<\alpha
. Consider the following product

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}(1+x^{m_{j}})^{\alpha}(1+x^{\alpha})^{m_{r}^{r}}\prod_{j=1}^{\alpha-1}(1+x^{\alpha+i})

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,i\},\,T=\sum_{j\in A}c_{j}m_{j}<\alpha}x^{T}+x^{\alpha}+x^{\alpha+1}+\cdots+x^{q-\alpha+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}}

\displaystyle+x^{q}\left(1+\prod_{j=1}^{i}(1+x^{m_{j}})^{\alpha}\right),

where
q=m_{r}^{r}\alpha+\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha-1}(\alpha+i)
. Note that the above product can also be written as

\displaystyle f(x)\left(1+\sum_{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,i\},\,T=\sum_{j\in A}c_{j}m_{j}<\alpha}x^{T}\right)+x^{\alpha}(1+x)^{q-2\alpha+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}}+\left(1+x+x^{q-\alpha}\prod_{j=1}^{i}(1+x^{m_{j}})^{\alpha}\right)x^{\alpha},

which is an element of
\mathcal{J}
as
\{1+x+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}
. But then this gives us a contradiction, as
\mathcal{J}
is a prime ideal. Hence, we have

\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}=\langle\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\rangle.

∎

Finally, there exist infinitely many prime ideals within the family of prime ideals that contain the ideal

\langle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\rangle.

We give the generators explicitly. Let
\mathcal{J}
be a prime ideal that properly contains the above ideal, and suppose

f(x)\in\mathcal{J}\backslash\langle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\rangle

be an irreducible polynomial with
1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)
. Write
f(x)=1+x^{m_{1}}+\cdots x^{m_{r}}
with
m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}
. Then
f(x)
must satisfy the following properties:

(1)

We have
m_{i}\notin\mathfrak{A}
for all
i
.

(2)

There exists
i\neq j
such that
m_{j}-m_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}
.

(3)

There exists
t
such that
|m_{t}-m_{j}|\notin\mathfrak{A}
for all
j
.

Property (1) follows from Lemma
4.10
.
Property (2) follows from the identity

(a+b+c)(ab+bc+ac)=(a+b)(a+c)(b+c).

Property (3) follows from the fact that
f(x)\notin\langle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\rangle
. In particular, if
f(x)
is irreducible and satisfies properties (1) - (3), then

\langle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\cup f(x)\rangle

is a prime ideal. In general, if we denote the above properties by
\star
, then

\mathcal{J}=\langle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}\cup\mathcal{Q}\rangle

where
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\{1+x^{m_{1}}+\cdots+x^{m_{r}}\mid\textrm{$m_{i}^{\prime}s$ satisfy $\star$}\}
. This completely defines the family of prime ideals of this type.

4.2.
The case when
\text{d}>1

In this case, the corresponding prime subset
\mathfrak{A}
is an infinite set. Our first theorem says that if
\text{d}\notin\mathfrak{A}
, then there is only one prime ideal, which is
\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
such that
\langle x\rangle
is not contained in
\mathcal{J}
. We define

\mathfrak{A}:=\{a:1+x^{a}\in\mathcal{J}\}.

Let
\text{d}=\gcd(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
where
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\rangle
. If
\text{d}>1
and
\text{d}\notin\mathfrak{A}
then
\mathcal{J}
can only be of the following type

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle:=\{x^{m}f:m\geq 0\textrm{ and }f\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}\textrm{ with }1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)\}\cup\{0\}

\displaystyle=\langle\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}:a\in\mathfrak{A}\textrm{ and }m\geq 0\},\{1+x^{n}+x^{n+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A}\textrm{ and }n\geq 0\}\rangle.

Proof.

In this case,
{\mathbb{N}}-\mathfrak{A}=\langle d\rangle
. Let
a\in\mathfrak{A}
. Observe that, for any
m>a
, if
m,m-a\notin\mathfrak{A}
, then
\text{d}|m
and
\text{d}|m-a
. In particular,
\text{d}|a
. But then this implies
a\notin\mathfrak{A}
as
{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\mathfrak{A}=\langle\text{d}\rangle
, a contradiction. So, we must have
m\in\mathfrak{A}
or
m-a\in\mathfrak{A}
. In either case, we have
1+x^{a}+x^{m}\in\mathcal{J}
. Thus, we have the containment

\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},\,m\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}.

Moreover, for any
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and for any
m>a
, if
m\in\mathfrak{A}
, then
(1+x^{a})x^{m-a}+1+x^{m}=1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}\in\mathcal{J}
. If
m-a\in\mathfrak{A}
, then
(1+x^{a})x^{m-a}+1+x^{m-a}=1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}\in\mathcal{J}
. So, we also have the containment

\{1+x^{n}+x^{n+a}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},\,n\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}.

Hence,
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
.
∎

We now give some lemmas which will help us proving the main result of the section.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
corresponding to
\mathfrak{A}
in class
\text{d}>1
with
d\in\mathfrak{A}
. If
a\in\mathfrak{A}
such that
\text{d}\nmid a
then

\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\},\{1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq a\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}.

If
1+x^{a}+x^{m}\notin\mathcal{J}
or
1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}\notin\mathcal{J}
then we must have
m-a,m\notin\mathfrak{A}
which implies
\text{d}|m-a,\text{d}|m
and thus
\text{d}|a
, a contradiction.
∎

The following lemma says that the same type of inclusion holds for large
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
corresponding to
\mathfrak{A}
in class
\text{d}>1
.
Then there exists positive integer
A
such that for any
a\in\mathfrak{A}
with
a>A
, we have

\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1\},\{1+x^{m-a}+x^{m}\mid m>a\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}.

Proof.

We have shown that this holds if
\text{d}\notin\mathfrak{A}
in Lemma
4.12
. Suppose
\text{d}\in\mathfrak{A}
, we know that there exists integers
1<a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{r}
such that
\{a_{1}\text{d},\cdots,a_{r}\text{d}\}
forms a minimal generating set of
{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\mathfrak{A}
. Observe that, we must have
\gcd(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r})=1
. Let
\mathbf{F}
be the Frobenius element of the numerical semigroup
\mathcal{S}=\langle a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\rangle

Proposition 4.16
.

\mathcal{J}

\mathcal{J}

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be a prime ideal in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
not containing
\langle x\rangle

\text{d}>1
. Define

\mathfrak{A}:=\{a:1+x^{a}\in\mathcal{J}\}.

and

then

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle=\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\langle a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}\rangle
and
A=(\mathbf{F}+1)d
where
\mathbf{F}
is the Frobenius element of
\mathcal{S}
. Suppose
f\in\mathcal{J}\backslash\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume

. Also,
\textrm{Supp}(f)
cannot contain the term

for any
a\in\mathfrak{A}
. Let
f=1+x^{m_{1}}+\cdots+x^{m_{r}}
, with
m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}
. Note that
m_{1},\cdots,m_{r}\in{\mathbb{N}}\backslash\mathfrak{A}
. But then by Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle(1+x^{A})^{m_{r}}\prod_{i=1}^{A-1}(1+x^{A+id})

\displaystyle=f+x^{A}(1+x^{d})^{\frac{q-2A}{d}}+(1+x^{d}+x^{2A})x^{q-2A}

lies in
\mathcal{J}
where

. This is a contradiction as
\mathcal{J}
is a prime ideal. Hence, we must have
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}
.
∎

Finally, there exist infinitely many prime ideals within the family of prime ideals that contain the ideal

\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+td}\mid m\notin\mathfrak{A}\textrm{ with }m\geq A,\,t\geq 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{J}.

We give the generators explicitly. Let
\mathcal{J}
be a prime ideal that properly contains the above ideal, and suppose

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{J}\backslash\langle

\displaystyle\{1+x^{m}+x^{m+a}:a\in\mathfrak{A},m\geq 0\},\{1+x^{a}+x^{m}\mid m\geq 1,\,a\in\mathfrak{A}\textrm{ with $a<d,\,a\geq A$ or $d\nmid a$}\}\rangle.

be an irreducible polynomial with

with
m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}
. Then

must again satisfy the properties

, given as

(1)

We have

for all

(2)

There exists

5.
Prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]

In this section, we provide partial results for prime ideals in
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
. Throughout the section, we use the notation
\vec{a}
to denote elements of
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
and by
X^{\vec{a}}
we mean
x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
when
\vec{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
. A subset
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
is said to be a
prime subset
if for any
\vec{a},\vec{b}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
such that
\vec{a}+\vec{b}\in\mathfrak{A}
, we have
\vec{a}\in\mathfrak{A}
or
\vec{b}\in\mathfrak{A}
.

We now generalize the methods due to F. Alarcón and D. Anderson
[
1
]
to the multivariable case. If
\mathfrak{A}=\emptyset
, then we use the convention
\mathcal{I}_{\emptyset}=\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\rangle
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a subset in
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
. Consider the polynomial semiring
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]
. To the set
\mathfrak{A}
we associate the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
of
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]
defined as

\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}:=\langle x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n},\{1+X^{\vec{a}}\mid\vec{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathfrak{A}\}\rangle.

Then the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
can also be described as

\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}=\left\{f=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},\\
\textrm{finite}\end{subarray}}X^{\vec{a}}\mid\textrm{ if $1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)$}\textrm{ then }\exists\,\vec{a}\in\mathfrak{A}\textrm{ such that }X^{\vec{a}}\in\textrm{Supp}(f)\right\}.

Then

(1)

The ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
is prime if and only if
\mathfrak{A}
is a prime subset of
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{P}
is a prime ideal containing
\langle x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\rangle
, then
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
where

\mathfrak{A}=\{\vec{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\mid 1+X^{\vec{a}}\in\mathcal{P}\}

is a prime subset of
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
.

Proof.

To prove (1), let
\mathfrak{A}
be a subset of
\mathbb{N}^{n}
and consider the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
as defined above. Suppose
I_{\mathfrak{A}}
is a prime ideal. Let
\vec{a}
and
\vec{b}
be elements in
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
such that
\vec{a}+\vec{b}\in\mathfrak{A}
. Then

(1+X^{\vec{a}})(1+X^{\vec{b}})=1+X^{\vec{a}}+X^{\vec{b}}+X^{\vec{a}+\vec{b}}\in I_{\mathfrak{A}}.

Since
I_{\mathfrak{A}}
is prime, we have
(1+X^{\vec{a}})\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
or
(1+X^{\vec{b}})\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
, which implies
\vec{a}\in\mathfrak{A}
or
\vec{b}\in\mathfrak{A}
.

Conversely, let
\mathfrak{A}
be a prime subset in
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
and consider the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
. Let
f=\sum_{i}X^{\vec{a}_{i}}
and
g=\sum_{i}X^{\vec{b}_{j}}
be arbitrary elements of
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
such that

fg=\sum_{k}X^{\vec{c}_{k}}\in I_{\mathfrak{A}}.

If
1\notin\textrm{Supp}(fg)
, then we have
1\notin\textrm{Supp}(f)
or
1\notin\textrm{Supp}(g)
, in which case
f\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
or
g\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
. If
1\in\textrm{Supp}(fg)
, then there exists
k
such that
\vec{c}_{k}\in\mathfrak{A}
, where
\vec{c}_{k}
is one of the exponents of
fg
. But then there exists
i
and
j
for which
\vec{a}_{i}+\vec{b}_{j}=\vec{c}_{k}
. Now, since
\mathfrak{A}
is a prime subset, we must have
\vec{a}_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}
or
\vec{b}_{j}\in\mathfrak{A}
. As
1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)\cap\textrm{Supp}(g)
, we have
f\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
or
g\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
is a prime ideal.

To prove (2), let
\mathcal{P}
be a prime ideal containing
\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\rangle
and consider the set

\mathfrak{A}=\{\vec{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\mid 1+X^{\vec{a}}\in\mathcal{P}\}.

If
\mathfrak{A}=\emptyset,
we have nothing to prove. Suppose
\mathfrak{A}\neq\emptyset
. Then clearly
\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
is contained in
\mathcal{P}
. Let
f=\sum_{i}X^{\vec{a}_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}
such that
1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)
. Consider the product

\prod_{i}(1+X^{\vec{a}_{i}})=f+h

where
h\in\langle x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\rangle
. This implies the above product lies in
\mathcal{P}
. But since
\mathcal{P}
is prime, we have
1+X^{\vec{a}_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}
for some
i
. In particular,
\vec{a}_{i}\in\mathfrak{A}
for some
i
. Thus,
f\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}
.

∎

Note that, in the multivariable case, the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{A}}:

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}-\{X^{\vec{a}}f\mid\vec{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},\,f\notin\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}\}.

\displaystyle=\{X^{\vec{a}}f\mid\vec{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n},\,f\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathfrak{A}}\textrm{ with $1\in\textrm{Supp}(f)$}\}\cup\{0\}.

is not even an ideal. Furthermore, we do not know whether prime subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
parametrize all the prime ideals of
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
. We conjecture that it does; that is, for every prime ideal
\mathcal{P}
in
{\mathbb{B}}[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
, the corresponding set
\mathfrak{A}=\{\vec{a}\mid 1+X^{\vec{a}}\in\mathcal{P}\}
is a prime subset. To illustrate the complexity of classifying primes in the multivariable case, we give two distinct prime ideals in
{\mathbb{B}}[x,y]
for which
\mathfrak{A}=\emptyset
.

Example 5.2
.

The ideals

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\langle\{x^{a}+y^{b}+x^{c}y^{d}\mid a,b\geq 1\textrm{ and }c,d\geq 0\}\rangle.

and

\mathcal{P}_{2}=\langle\{x^{a}+y^{b}\,:\,a,b\geq 1\},\{1+x^{p-a}y^{q}+x^{p}y^{q-b}\,:\,p,q\geq 1\textrm{ and }p-a,q-b\geq 0\}\rangle.

are prime. We can prove that they are prime ideals by using the equality

\displaystyle(x^{a}+y^{b}+x^{p}y^{q})(x^{a}y^{b}+x^{p+a}y^{q}+x^{p}y^{q+b})

\displaystyle=(x^{a}+y^{b})(x^{a}+x^{p}y^{q})(y^{b}+x^{p}y^{q})

and

\displaystyle(1+x^{a}y^{b}+f(x,y))(x^{a}y^{b}+f(x,y)+x^{a}y^{b}f(x,y))

\displaystyle=(1+x^{a}y^{b})(1+f(x,y))(x^{a}y^{b}+f(x,y))

in
{\mathbb{B}}[x,y]
, for all
a,b\geq 1
and
p,q\geq 0
.

Remark 5.3
.

Using the results of
[
6
]
we can produce a large number of examples of prime ideals, called ’closed’ prime ideals. In the case of
{\mathbb{B}}[x,y]
we can list all closed prime ideals. They all correspond to a (non-minimal) prime congruence, which in turn correspond to a matrix
[a,b]
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
, which is in turn equivalent to a monomial pre-order on monomials in two variables. Each closed prime ideal is generated by polynomials of the form
\sum_{i}x^{n_{i}}y^{m_{i}}
, where after reordering
n_{1}a+m_{1}b=n_{2}a+m_{2}b\geq n_{j}a+m_{j}b,
for
j>2
. Since not all primes are closed primes, e.g. in
{\mathbb{B}}[x]
there is a single closed prime ideal, this is an incomplete classification as well.

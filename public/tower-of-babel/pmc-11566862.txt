How does the Vietnamese stock market react when the Fed gives an announcement in time at the zero lower bound?
Authors: Nam Ngo, Huyen Nguyen, Yen Nguyen, Son Le
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study analyzes the impact of US’s UMP announcements on the Vietnamese stock market during the 2008 GFC and COVID-19 pandemic. Utilizing an event study methodology, it examines both market-wide and sectors-specific stock reactions. The findings reveal overall positive market responses to UMP news in both periods, but with notable differences across industries and crises. During the COVID-19 pandemic, real estate, industrials, materials, and finance sectors exhibited significantly stronger positive reactions compared to the GFC. These sectors maintained positive AARs and CAARs with high statistical significance, suggesting heightened sensitivity to UMP during the pandemic-induced economic downturn. Furthermore, the financial and real estate sectors significantly outperformed their GFC responses during COVID-19, indicating a potential shift in investor sentiment and risk appetite towards these sectors amidst the pandemic's unique economic landscape. This research provides valuable insights into the diverse impacts of UMP across different sectors and crisis contexts.Keywords: Unconventional monetary policy, Stock market, Global financial crisis, COVID-19, Forward guidance

Introduction

Mishkin [1] stated that Central Banks worldwide predominantly employ a very short-term interest rate as their primary policy tool. In the United States, the Fed conducts monetary policy by adjusting the Federal Funds Rate (FFR). In 2008, during the Global Financial Crisis (GFC), and again in 2020, the FFR reached zero. How can the Fed and other Central Banks support the economy when short-term interest rates still are near zero? To address the limitations imposed by the zero lower bound on short-term interest rates, the US and other advanced economies (AEs) have also introduced new measures known as non-standard monetary policy or unconventional monetary policy (UMP). According to Bernanke [2], the Central Bank lowered its short-term interest rate target to zero and committed to keeping it at that level for as long as necessary. When short-term interest rates approach the zero-bound (Fig. 1), economists refer to this as the "zero lower bound," signifying that the Central Bank's CMP tool has reached its limit and cannot be further employed to provide stimulus.

In response to this situation, UMP appeared and played a pivotal role in managing Central Banks' monetary policies, particularly during prolonged economic crises. One of the essentials tool is forward guidance, designed to influence financial conditions by shaping market expectations of future monetary policy. When the Fed tightens monetary policy, it reveals a tightened monetary stance and optimism about the economy, raising global output and asset prices. According to Gai and Tong [4], monetary announcements from the U.S. can signal optimism or pessimism, with far-reaching consequences for global economic activity. Therefore, the various approaches to this policy have raised significant concerns about their potential international spillover effects on developing countries' real economies and financial markets. Identifying which countries are most affected by these U.S. policy decisions is particularly interesting.

A significant number of empirical studies have analyzed the domestic impact of UMP in AEs and its international spillover effects on the rest of the world. Many of these studies have primarily focused on examining the response of financial markets within AEs such as [[5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12]], interest rate [10,[13], [14], [15]] … Another focus on the impact of UMP on the macro economy and economic activity in AEs [[16], [17], [18], [19]]. These findings make valuable contributions to the empirical evidence. However, they have yet to address this issue in the context of a pandemic crisis, nor have they compared the differences between implementing UMP tools to manage a GFC crisis versus a pandemic crisis in various AEs, particularly those nations with significant influence on the development of other economies, such as the U.S. Moreover, another strand of research concerns the international impact of this policy on EMEs and developing countries, but in limited quantities. These studies pay more attention to the financial market in emerging economies, such as interest rates, asset prices, and exchange rates.

Several studies have examined the impact of UMP on developing countries. However, (i) very few studies have examined the specific impact on the Vietnamese financial market by quantifying the level of impact; (ii) the studies do not show the impact of different specific tools of US’s UMP; (iii) a few studies have compared the difference in the impact of UMP in the two periods GFC and COVID-19. This study examines the impact of US’s UMP on Vietnam through the signaling channel. It provides a broader perspective on UMP's anouncement effects on frontier stock markets like Vietnam. Additionally, it compares Vietnam's economic response to US UMP shocks during the COVID-19 pandemic and the GFC, contributing to the literature on UMP's international effects on open, small, and frontier markets.

Literature review

UMP is studied in various financial contexts, focusing on its effects on the economy. The literature can be divided into three main strands: (i) the effects of UMP itself, (ii) its international spillover effects, and (iii) its impact during crises, especially the COVID-19 pandemic. Both the US and other AEs, such as Japan, England, and the Eurozone under the ECB, implement these policies.

Firstly, research on UMP focuses on its effects within developed countries like the U.S., UK, Japan, and the Euro Area. Studies have shown that UMP, especially during the GFC, increases central bank assets, boosts output and prices, and significantly impacts financial markets by raising stock prices, lowering long-term bond yields, and depreciating local currencies [14,20,21]. Most studies analyze UMP's influence on financial markets [[22], [23], [24]] and real economies [25,26]. They find that UMP strongly boosts asset prices, with stock indices responding sharply to UMP surprises, particularly during and after the GFC [5,27]. Additionally, some research explores UMP's impact on bank performance [28], investor sentiment [29], and risk aversion [30], though these studies are fewer in number.

Secondly, research on UMP's international spillover effects focuses on developing countries and EMEs. Studies highlight that UMP in AEs like the US, UK, Japan, and the Euro Area significantly impacts EMEs. UMP can lead to increased portfolio flows, real production growth, and financial variable movements, such as lower bond rates, higher stock prices, and currency appreciation [[31], [32], [33], [34], [35]]. UMP policies, particularly quantitative easing, have boosted capital flows to EMEs, enhancing portfolio and cross-border credit flows [36,37]. Research also shows UMP's impact on global mutual funds, firm performance, and bank health [[38], [39], [40]].

The final strand of this research focuses on UMP during crises, especially the COVID-19 pandemic. Many studies have emphasized the GFC, neglecting the distinct impacts of UMP during the pandemic, despite its significant influence on monetary policy in AEs. Research on UMP's impact during COVID-19, such as on financial markets and real economies, includes works by Refs. [[41], [42], [43], [44]]. Studies like [45] highlight UMP's effects on Vietnam through channels like portfolio rebalancing and asset prices. Research comparing UMP's impacts during the GFC and COVID-19 is limited but includes works by Refs. [9,46]. Studies like [35] examine UMP's effects on developing markets, showing varied impacts on exchange rates and policy rates. Cortes, Gao [47] found positive spillovers of Fed policies during COVID-19, particularly in Asian markets. Rebucci, Hartley [48] noted stronger effects of QE announcements during the COVID-19 crisis compared to the GFC.

To analyze the impact of UMP anouncemnt the primary methodology is event study method. The event study method examines the impact of UMP announcements on financial markets, focusing on changes in asset prices, bond yields, and exchange rates around the event date. This method is effective for analyzing the immediate effects of UMP on financial markets, which respond quickly to monetary policy changes. Notable studies using this approach include [19,27,49,50]. ESM is straightforward and effective for evaluating UMP's impact on financial markets due to the high frequency of data and the short event window. However, it has limitations, such as endogeneity issues with UMP variables and difficulty modeling them as dummy variables. Additionally, it is less effective for assessing UMP's impact on variables with lags, like GDP and inflation.

Despite the critical role of frontier markets in global capital flows, the impact of UMP on these economies, particularly in Asia, remains under-explored. This study addresses this gap by focusing on Vietnam, a country with increasing global integration, and analyzing the effects of US’s UMP announcements on its stock market during the GFC and COVID-19 pandemic. Utilizing the event study method and building on previous research, this study examines both sector-specific and broader market impacts, providing a comprehensive assessment of UMP transmission channels in Vietnam.

Methods and data

Methods

The event study method (ESM) is a widely used research methodology in finance [51] that quantifies the impact of economic events on stock prices [52]. By analyzing abnormal returns, the difference between actual and expected returns, ESM reveals how events alter a company's prospects and market perception [53,54]. This method has been applied across various sectors to study their reactions to macroeconomic announcements, including monetary policy [54,55].

According to Cheng and Sun [53] and Galloppo and Paimanova [54], ESM can be conducted to investigate the stock market response to policy announcements. By examining the price changes before and after the event, researchers can estimate the event's impact on the stock price and the market's perception of the event. This can provide valuable insights for investors, regulators, and policymakers who must understand how markets react to specific events. Event studies are effective for evaluating UMP effects due to their simplicity and the assumption that monetary policy news dominates other news during the announcement window, given the high-frequency data and small event window. It also assumes that the stock market reacts quickly to news, reflecting monetary policy shocks accurately. However, this method is unsuitable if monetary policy news is overshadowed or if the market reacts slowly. This approach enables analyzing the impact of central bank announcements or speeches related to UMP on relevant variables.

This study employs ESM to examine the immediate responses of the Vietnamese financial market and different sectors to US’s UMP announcements during the GFC and the COVID-19 pandemic.

This study uses the market model to determine expected returns. Average abnormal return (AAR) and cumulative average abnormal return (CAAR) are calculated by aggregating ARs across stocks, either with equal weights or weighted by market capitalization ((5), (6)). The portfolio approach, using an evenly weighted portfolio, is an alternative method for computing AARs and CAARs [66].

This study employs both parametric and non-parametric tests to examine the statistical significance of AR and CAR in response to events. Corrado and Truong [67] found that standard parametric event study tests can be poorly specified with these returns data, but nonparametric rank and sign tests are both well specified. The null hypothesis posits no significant impact on returns, while the alternative hypothesis suggests otherwise. Parametric tests, such as the t-test and Patell Z test [68], assume normality of returns and are suitable for assessing the significance of individual and cumulative abnormal returns [69]. Non-parametric tests, like the generalized rank (GRANK) test [70], do not rely on normality assumptions and are preferred for single-day AR analysis but may be less powerful for multiple-day CAR analysis. This study utilizes both approaches to test the hypotheses regarding CAR and CAAR in the market and various sectors.

Table 1 shows positive AARs in the Vietnamese stock market around US UMP events during both the GFC and COVID-19 periods, particularly on the event day. Market behavior differed: during the GFC, positive AARs followed initial apprehension, while during COVID-19, positive AARs persisted longer, possibly due to frequent UMP implementation. This indicates that information regarding US’s UMP exerts an influence on the Vietnamese stock market, and a discernible difference exists between the two periods under consideration. This observed disparity can likely be attributed to the scale and frequency with which such policies were implemented during the GFC and the Covid-19 pandemic. During the GFC, all sectors demonstrated positive AARs on the event day, with Energy, Consumer Staples, and Industrials showing statistical significance. Although the positive trend subsided post-event, a rebound was observed on day +2 for most sectors. In contrast, the COVID-19 period witnessed a broader positive response, with nine sectors exhibiting positive AARs on the event day, led by Financials, Energy, and Industrials. However, this initial enthusiasm waned in subsequent days, except for the Financial sector, which maintained a positive trend.

Interestingly, some sectors displayed positive AARs even before the UMP announcement during the COVID-19 period, suggesting market anticipation. However, this pre-event optimism was largely short-lived, with the Financial sector being the notable exception. Further analysis of CAARs will provide a more comprehensive understanding of market reactions before and after the UMP events, illuminating sector-specific dynamics and potential influences of market expectations.

Differences in financial market reactions during the GFC and COVID-19 period

The US Federal Reserve employed distinct UMP approaches during the GFCand the COVID-19 pandemic. The GFC response primarily relied on QE to inject liquidity and lower interest rates, complemented by forward guidance and rate reductions. In stark contrast, the COVID-19 crisis prompted a more expansive UMP toolkit, encompassing QE, corporate bond purchases, direct lending programs, and the establishment of special-purpose vehicles. This broader strategy aimed to address the unique economic disruptions caused by the pandemic, leading to swifter and more extensive interventions compared to the GFC.

These divergent UMP implementations are reflected in the observed stock market responses. Prior to UMP announcements during the GFC, most sectors experienced negative AARs. This can be attributed to the protracted and multifaceted nature of policy implementation during the GFC, which likely contributed to market uncertainty and investor hesitancy. Conversely, the COVID-19 pandemic witnessed fewer sectors with negative AARs before UMP announcements. This suggests that the market may have anticipated and reacted positively to the swift and comprehensive policy response during the pandemic.

Furthermore, the real estate, industrials, materials, and finance sectors exhibited markedly positive responses during the COVID-19 pandemic compared to the GFC. These sectors maintained positive AARs and CAARs with high statistical significance, indicating a robust performance. The financial and real estate sectors, in particular, showed a remarkable improvement during the pandemic relative to the GFC. This suggests that these sectors may have been more resilient to the pandemic's economic shocks or benefited directly from the specific UMP measures implemented. In contrast, the information technology sector displayed contrasting CAAR trends between the two periods, highlighting the sector-specific nature of market reactions to different crises and the importance of understanding the underlying economic dynamics driving these responses.

Conclusion and recommendations

Monetary policy is a crucial tool for governments to manage their economies, but conventional measures can become ineffective during major crises, leading developed countries to adopt UMPs. The impact of these policies extends beyond domestic borders, influencing financial markets globally. The United States, with its economic might, holds particular sway in this regard. This study investigates the influence of US’s UMP on Vietnam's financial market during the and the COVID-19 pandemic, highlighting the significant effects experienced by Vietnam as a small and open economy.

The research uncovers a positive correlation between US’s UMP implementation and the performance of Vietnam's financial market. Specifically, the Vietnamese stock market tends to rise following US’s UMP announcements during crises. However, the market reaction timing differed between the two crises, with an anticipatory response during the COVID-19 pandemic and an immediate response with a more prolonged upward trend during the GFC.

These findings provide valuable practical and academic contributions. The study's findings underscore the dynamic relationship between US’s UMP and Vietnam's financial market, offering valuable insights for both investors and policymakers. Policymakers should proactively adjust domestic policies in response to U.S. actions, enhance market transparency and investor confidence, and foster long-term economic resilience through diversification and strategic investments. These measures will enable Vietnam to harness the benefits of UMP spillovers while mitigating potential risks, ensuring sustainable economic growth in an interconnected global landscape. Investors can leverage these findings to make more informed decisions. Recognizing that sectors react differently to UMP announcements, they should adopt sector-specific strategies, favoring defensive stocks like healthcare and consumer staples during volatile periods, while considering growth-oriented sectors like technology and financials during expansionary phases. The study's emphasis on timing highlights the importance of staying abreast of Fed communications and global economic trends to anticipate market movements and capitalize on opportunities. Moreover, maintaining diversified portfolios and employing risk management tools are crucial to navigate the volatility associated with UMP.

Simple factor graphs associated with split graphs
Adrián Pastine, Victor N. Schvöllner

Abstract
We introduce and study a loopless multigraph associated with a split graph S S : the factor graph of S S , denoted by Φ ⁡ ( S ) \Phi(S) , which encodes the combinatorial information about 2-switch transformations over S S . This construction provides a cleaner, compact and non-redundant alternative to the graph A 4 ​ ( S ) A_{4}(S) by Barrus and West, for the particular case of split graphs. If Φ ⁡ ( S ) \Phi(S) is simple and connected, we obtain a precise description of the underlying structure of S S , particularly when Φ ⁡ ( S ) \Phi(S) is complete, highlighting the usefulness of the factor graph for understanding 2-switch dynamics in balanced and indecomposable split graphs, as well as its 2-switch-degree classification.

. The

. The

of

, denoted by

. Clearly,

, for every graph

. The notation

The study of structural transformations on graphs has proven to be a powerful framework for understanding both global and modular properties of graphs. Among these operations, the 2-switch plays a central role in the family of graphs with a given degree sequence. Let
G
be a graph containing four distinct vertices
a,b,c,d
such that
ab,cd\in E(G)
and
ac,bd\notin E(G)
. A
2-switch
in
G
is a process that removes the edges
ab
and
cd
from
G
and adds the edges
ac
and
bd
(see
[
2
]
, page 23). Directly from the definition, it follows that 2-switch preserves the degree sequence. Moreover, it is easy to see that a 2-switch replaces edges between vertices
a,b,c,d
in
G
if and only if
\langle a,b,c,d\rangle_{G}\approx P_{4},C_{4}
or
2K_{2}
. The
realization graph
is the graph whose vertex set is the family of all labeled graphs with the same degree sequence, where two vertices (graphs)
G,H
are adjacent if and only if
H=\tau(G)
for some 2-switch
\tau
(see
[
6
]
, pages 3 and 4). Given any two graphs
G,H
with the same degree sequence, a basic theorem states that
G
can be transformed into
H
by a sequence of 2-switches (
[
2
]
, page 24). As a consequence, every realization graph is connected.

Two fundamental results illustrate the importance of 2-switch transformations and motivate this article: the canonical decomposition theorem by R. Tyshkevich
[
8
]
, and the characterization by Barrus and West
[
1
]
of indecomposable graphs. In order to properly understand the content of both, we need to clarify these preliminary concepts: split graph, Tyshkevich composition, and indecomposable graph.

A graph
G\neq(\varnothing,\varnothing)
is said to be

if
V(G)
is the disjoint union of two sets,
V(G)=K\dot{\cup}I
, where
K
is a clique and
I
is an independent set. In such a case, we say that the pair
(K,I)
is a
bipartition
for
G
. We call
clique vertices
the elements of
K
, and
independent vertices
the elements of
I
. The notation
(S,K,I)
means that we are considering the split graph
S
together with a bipartition
(K,I)
for it. Split graphs have been characterized in
[
4
]
as those that do not contain a
C_{4},C_{5}
or
2K_{2}
as induced subgraphs. Further information about the structure of split graphs can be found in
[
3
]
. If
(S,K,I)
is a split graph and
G
is a graph disjoint from
S
, the
Tyshkevich composition

S\circ G
of
S
and

is defined as the graph whose vertex set is
V(S\circ G)=V(S)\cup V(G)
and whose edge set is

E(S\circ G)=E(S)\cup E(G)\cup\{xy:x\in K,y\in G\}.

This operation was introduced by R. Tyshkevich in
[
8
]
. In general,

is a split graph if and only if
G
is. A graph

is

if there exist graphs

such that

. Otherwise,

is said to be

.

Next, we can finally cite Tyshkevich’s and Barrus-West’s theorems.

Theorem 1.1

(Tyshkevich,
[
8
]
)
.

Every graph
G
can be written as a (Tyshkevich) composition of indecomposable graphs:

G=G_{n}\circ\ldots\circ G_{2}\circ G_{1},

where
G_{2},\ldots,G_{n}
are split. When
|G_{r}|\geq 1
for all
r
, this decomposition is unique up to isomorphism, and neither the order of the factors nor the choice of bipartitions of the split factors can vary.

Notice that
Theorem

1.1
highlights the importance of indecomposable split graphs as fundamental building blocks for constructing a vast class of larger graphs. On the other hand, Barrus and West studied the graph
A_{4}(G)
associated with a graph

, with
V(A_{4}(G))=V(G)
and where two vertices are adjacent if and only if they participate in the same
2
-switch. They proved that
A_{4}(G)
decomposes as the disjoint union of the corresponding
A_{4}
-graphs of the Tyshkevich factors of

. As a consequence,

is indecomposable if and only if
A_{4}(G)
is connected.

Theorem 1.2

(Barrus, West,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
A_{4}(G)
be the graph with
V(A_{4}(G))=V(G)
, where

if and only if we can perform a 2-switch in

involving both

and
v
. If
G_{n}\circ\ldots\circ G_{1}
is the Tyshkevich decomposition of

A_{4}(G)=\dot{\bigcup}_{i=1}^{n}A_{4}(G_{i}).

In particular,

is indecomposable if and only if

is connected.

If

are 4 vertices involved in the same 2-switch over

, it easily follows from the definition of

that

is a 4-clique in

This causes graph

to have many redundant edges, which can be an issue, for example, if we want to know the number of 2-switches acting on

, i.e., the

of

. Computing the 2-switch-degree (or simply, the

2
Relationship between
\Phi
and
A_{4}

We aim to investigate how the connectedness of
\Phi=\Phi(S)
is related to the connectedness of
A_{4}=A_{4}(S)
(see
Theorem

1.2
) and vice versa. The connectedness of
\Phi
is equivalent to the existence of a path in
A_{4}
between any pair of vertices

that only involves vertices from
I
. This is the general idea behind
Lemmas

2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
, which are used to prove that
\Phi
is connected if
A_{4}
is. However, we then note that the converse does not hold in general, unless
S
is active. Finally, we obtain one of the most important results in this work: if
S
is active and
\Phi(S)
is connected, then
S
is indecomposable.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(H,K,I)
be a split graph of order 7 with
I=\{a,b,u,v\}
and
K=\{x,y,z\}
. If
H_{1}=\langle a,x,y,u\rangle_{H}\approx P_{4}
and
H_{2}=\langle b,x,z,v\rangle_{H}\approx P_{4}
, then:

1.

u
and
z
are connected by a path
P
in
A_{4}(H)
such that
V(P)-z\subset I
;

2.

\Phi(H)
is connected.

If
G
is a graph, we will use the notation
Q_{G}(P_{4})
from
[
6
]
(page 15) to refer to the set
\{H\prec G:H\approx P_{4}\}
. The letter “
Q
” stands for “quartets”, as
|H|=4
for every
H\in Q_{G}(P_{4})
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we use
N_{*},d_{*},A_{4}
, and
\Phi
to denote
N_{H}(*)
,
\deg_{H}(*)
,
A_{4}(H)
, and
\Phi(H)
, respectively.
First, observe that
V(H_{1})
and
V(H_{2})
are cliques in
A_{4}
sharing only the vertex
x
. To show that
\Phi
is connected, it suffices to find an edge in
\Phi
between a vertex in
\{a,u\}
and a vertex in
\{b,v\}
, since
au,bv\in\Phi
by hypothesis. Given that
\{H_{1},H_{2}\}\subset Q_{H}(P_{4})
,
H_{1}\cap H_{2}=x
, and
H
is active, we have
|N_{x}\cap\{a,u\}|=|N_{x}\cap\{b,v\}|=1
, and hence necessarily
d_{x}=4
. More specifically,

N_{x}\cap K=\{y,z\},\ N_{x}\cap I\in\{\{u,b\},\{u,v\},\{a,v\},\{a,b\}\}.

Furthermore, the degree in
H
of each vertex in
I
must be 1 or 2.

Without loss of generality, assume
ux,bx\in H
. If
d_{b}=1
, then
ayxb\prec H
, because
ax\notin H
. Thus,
uabv\subset\Phi
and
uabz\subset A_{4}
. If
d_{u}=1
, then
uxzv\prec H
because
vx\notin H
. Then
uv\in\Phi
and
uz\in A_{4}
. If
d_{b}=2=d_{u}
, then
uz,by\in H
and therefore
uzyb\prec H
. Hence,
ubv\subset\Phi
and
uz\in A_{4}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(S,K,I)
be a split graph. Suppose
u,v\in I
are connected in
A_{4}=A_{4}(S)
by the path
uxv
, for some
x\in K
. Then, there exists another path
P\subset A_{4}
between
u
and
v
such that
V(P)\subset I
and
|P|\leq 4
.

Proof.

Since
ux,xv\in A_{4}
, there exist
H_{1},H_{2}\in Q_{S}(P_{4})
such that
u,x\in H_{1}
and
x,v\in H_{2}
. Let
H_{1}=\langle a,u,x,y\rangle_{S}
and
H_{2}=\langle b,v,x,z\rangle_{S}
, where
a,b\in I
and
y,z\in K
. Observe that
V(H_{1})
and
V(H_{2})
are cliques in
A_{4}
, and
H=\langle V(H_{1})\cup V(H_{2})\rangle_{S}
is active. Moreover, with
K^{\prime}=\{x,y,z\}
and
I^{\prime}=\{a,b,u,v\}
, we have that
(K^{\prime},I^{\prime})
is the bipartition of
H
.

Clearly,
ub,av\in A_{4}
. Thus,
uav\subset A_{4}
if
a=b
. Otherwise, if
a\neq b
, we consider two cases: 1)
y\neq z
; 2)
y=z
. In both cases we prove that
\Phi(H)
is connected, which ensures the existence of the desired path

. If
y\neq z
, we apply

. If
y=z
, then

|N_{H}(x)\cap I^{\prime}|=2=|N_{H}(y)\cap I^{\prime}|,\ N_{H}(x)\cap N_{H}(y)\cap I^{\prime}=\varnothing.

Therefore,
\Phi(H)\approx C_{4}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(S,K,I)
be a split graph. Suppose
u\in I
and
z\in K
are connected in
A_{4}=A_{4}(S)
by the path
uxz
, for some
x\in K
. Then
u
and
z
are connected in
A_{4}
by a path
P
such that
V(P)-z\subset I
and
|P|\leq 4
.

Proof.

If
uz
is an edge in
A_{4}
, then there is nothing to prove. So assume
uz\not\in A_{4}
. Since
ux,xz\in A_{4}
, there exist
H_{1},H_{2}\in Q_{S}(P_{4})
such that
u,x\in H_{1}
and
x,z\in H_{2}
. Let
H_{1}=\langle a,x,y,u\rangle_{S}
and
H_{2}=\langle b,x,z,v\rangle_{S}
, with
a,b,v\in I
and
y\in K
. Observe that
V(H_{1})
and
V(H_{2})
are cliques in
A_{4}
. In particular, since
\{bz,vz,uy\}\subset E(A_{4})
, it follows that
u\notin\{b,v\}
and
y\neq z
, since
uz\notin A_{4}
by hypothesis.

Let
H=\langle V(H_{1})\cup V(H_{2})\rangle_{S}
be the active split graph with bipartition
(\{x,y,z\},\{a,b,u,v\})
. The above observations reduce the proof to two cases: 1)
a\in\{b,v\}
; 2)
a\notin\{b,v\}
. For the first case, we have that
uaz
is the desired path. In case (2), we apply

2.1
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(S,K,I)
be a split graph. If
A_{4}(S)
is connected, then
\Phi(S)
is connected.

Proof.

Suppose
A_{4}
is connected and choose arbitrary vertices
u
and
v\in I
. To show that
\Phi
is connected, we must find a path in
A_{4}
from
u
to
v
that includes only independent vertices.

Since
A_{4}
is connected, there exists a path
P\subset A_{4}
between
u
and
v
. If
V(P)\subset I
, we are done. Otherwise,
P
alternates sequences of
I
-vertices and
K
-vertices. It suffices to consider the case
P=ux_{1}\ldots x_{n}v
, where
x_{i}\in K
for all
i\in[n]
, and then construct the desired alternative path
P^{\prime}=u\ldots v\subset A_{4}
consisting only of independent vertices. We do this by induction on
n
.

The base case
n=1
is covered by
Lemma

2.2
. For each
m
such that
1\leq m<n
we assume the following: for any pair
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in I
connected in
A_{4}
by a path
u^{\prime}x_{1}\ldots x_{m}v^{\prime}
(
x_{i}\in K
for all
i\in[m]
), there exists a path
u^{\prime}\ldots v^{\prime}\subset A_{4}
with
V(u^{\prime}\ldots v^{\prime})\subset I
. Applying
Lemma

2.3
to
vx_{n}x_{n-1}
, we find a path in
A_{4}
of the form
v\ldots wx_{n-1}
with
V(v\ldots wx_{n-1})-x_{n-1}\subset I
(possibly
w=v
). Then the inductive hypothesis applies to
ux_{1}\ldots x_{n-1}w
and we obtain the desired path
P^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

If
S
is a split graph and
\Phi(S)
is disconnected, then
S
is decomposable.

Proof.

If
\Phi
is disconnected, then
Theorem

2.4
implies that
A_{4}
is disconnected. Thus,
S
is decomposable by
Theorem

1.2
.
∎

The converse of
Theorem

2.4
does not hold in general: the connectedness of
\Phi
does not necessarily imply the connectedness of

. For a counterexample, see

. A careful look at the split graph

in

Theorem 2.6
.

If
S
is an active split graph and
\Phi(S)
is connected, then
S
is indecomposable.

Proof.

Ignoring parallel edges in
\Phi
, we clearly have
\Phi\subset A_{4}
. Furthermore, every clique vertex must be connected in
A_{4}
to some independent vertex, since
S
is active. Hence,
A_{4}
is connected. Applying
Theorem

1.2
, we conclude that
S
is indecomposable.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
S
be an active split graph.

1.

S
is prime if and only if
\Phi(S)
is connected.

2.

If
S=\prod_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}
, where each

Multiplicities, neighborhoods and homogeneity

Given a split graph
(S,K,I)
, we aim to study the neighborhoods in

is simple and connected, with the goal of extrapolating structural properties of both
\Phi

Theorem 3.2
.

If
(S,K,I)
is a homogeneous split graph, then:

1.

\sigma_{uv}=0
if and only if

and

are twins in

2.

(1).

It follows immediately from

3.1

(2).

Same as (1).

(3).

Suppose

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(S,K,I)
be a split graph such that
K=\bigcup_{v\in I}N_{v}
. If
\Phi=\Phi(S)
is simple and connected and

, then

is homogeneous.

Proof.

The fact that
\Phi
is simple means

if and only if
\sigma_{uv}=1
. If

4

When
\Phi(S)
is simple and complete, it becomes natural to view the set

is inactive.

Given a split graph
(S,K,I)
, the
inversion
(or
inverse
)
(S,K,I)^{\iota}
of
S
is defined as the split graph
(S^{\iota},I,K)
, where

E(S^{\iota})=(E(S)-\{ab:a,b\in K\})\cup\{ab:a,b\in I\}.

This operation has been introduced by R. Tyshkevich in
[
8
]
(page 214). When inverting
S
,
K
becomes an independent set,
I
becomes a clique, but all edges between
K
and
I
remain. It should be noted that inversion, like (Tyshkevich) composition, depends entirely on the bipartition of
S
. Indeed, it does not make sense in general to speak of “the inverse" of
S
, since
S
will not have a unique bipartition if it is unbalanced. Obviously, inversion is an involutive operation, i.e.,
((S,K,I)^{\iota})^{\iota}=(S,K,I)
. Another important property is that inversion and complementation commute with each other.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
8
]
, page 214)
.

If
(S,K,I)
is a split graph, then

\overline{(S,K,I)^{\iota}}=\Big(\overline{(S,K,I)}\Big)^{\iota}.

(2)

Moreover,
N_{\overline{S^{\iota}}}(v)=K-N_{S}(v)
, for all
v\in I
.

Next, we include our proof of
Lemma

4.4
for completeness.

Proof.

Since both inversion and complementation interchange the roles of
K
and
I
as clique/independent sets, it follows that
G=\overline{S^{\iota}}
and

both have the same bipartition as
S
. Therefore, to obtain (

N_{G}(v)=N_{H}(v)
for all
v\in I
. First, note that:

(1).

is a clique vertex in

and in
\overline{S}
;

(2).

N_{S^{\iota}}(v)=(I-v)\cup N_{S}(v)
;

(3).

N_{\overline{S}}(v)=(I-v)\cup(K-N_{S}(v))
.

From (2), we deduce that

N_{G}(v)\cup v=

N_{S^{\iota}}(v)^{c}=(I-v)^{c}\cap N_{S}(v)^{c}=(K\cup v)\cap(I\cup(K-N_{S}(v)))=

((K\cup v)\cap I)\cup((K\cup v)\cap(K-N_{S}(v)))=

v\cup(K-N_{S}(v)),

that is,
N_{G}(v)=K-N_{S}(v)
. On the other hand, by (1) and by the definition of inversion, it is clear that
N_{H}(v)=N_{\overline{S}}(v)-I
. Finally, using (3), we obtain that
N_{H}(v)=K-N_{S}(v)
.
∎

We now use
Lemma

4.4
to make an important observation. Given a pair
\{u,v\}\subset I
, we have

\eta_{uv}(\overline{S^{\iota}})=|(K-N_{u})\cap(K-N_{v})|=|N_{u}^{c}\cap N_{v}^{c}|=

|K-(N_{u}\cup N_{v})|=|K|-|N_{u}\cup N_{v}|=

|K|-d_{u}-d_{v}+\eta_{uv}.

Combining this with the fact that
\deg_{\overline{S^{\iota}}}(x)=|K|-d_{x}
, it quickly follows, applying (
1
), that

\sigma_{uv}(\overline{S^{\iota}})=\sigma_{uv}(S),

(3)

for all
\{u,v\}\subset I
. Equality (
3
) implies the following result.

Proposition 4.5
.

For every split graph
S
,

\Phi(S)=\Phi(\overline{S^{\iota}}).

Proof.

It follows from the previous discussion.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(S,K,I)
be a split graph.

1.

If
w
is a swing vertex in
S
and
w\in I
, then
w
is isolated in
S^{\iota}
.

2.

If
w
is a swing vertex in
S
and
w\in K
, then
w
is universal in
S^{\iota}
.

3.

If
S
is non-empty and
w
is a universal or isolated vertex in
S
, then
w
is swing in
S^{\iota}
.

4.

act(S)=act(S^{\iota})=act(\overline{S^{\iota}})
.

Proof.

(1).

If
w\in I
, then
N_{w}=K
. Thus, by definition of inverse,
N_{S^{\iota}}(w)=(I-w)\cup N_{w}=V(S)-w
, which shows that
w
is universal in
S^{\iota}
.

(2).

If
w\in K
, then
N_{w}=K-w
. Thus, by definition of inverse,
N_{S^{\iota}}(w)=N_{w}-K=\varnothing
, which shows that
w
is isolated in
S^{\iota}
.

(3).

We need to prove that
N_{S^{\iota}}(w)=I-w
. If
w
is universal in
S
, then
w\in K
, since
S
is non-empty. Thus,
N_{S^{\iota}}(w)=N_{w}-K=(V(S)-w)-K=I-w
. If
N_{w}=\varnothing
, then
w\in I
, since
S
is non-empty. Therefore,
N_{S^{\iota}}(w)=(I-w)\cup N_{w}=I-w
.

(4).

From
[
6
]
(page 6) we know that a vertex is active in a graph if and only if it is active in its complement. Therefore,
act(S^{\iota})=act(\overline{S^{\iota}})
. If
a\in act(S)
, then there exists an
H\in Q_{S}(P_{4})
such that
a\in V(H)
. If
a\in I
, then
H=axyz
, where
z\in I
and
x,y\in K
. Since
\{ax,az,yz\}\subset E(S^{\iota})
and
xy\notin E(S^{\iota})
, it follows that
xazy\subset S^{\iota}
. Moreover, since
ay,xz\notin S
, we have that neither
ay
nor
xz
belong to
S^{\iota}
. Therefore,
xazy\prec S^{\iota}
, which shows that
a\in act(S^{\iota})
. If instead
a\in K
, then
a
is active and independent in
\overline{S}
. Thus,
a
is active in
(\overline{S})^{\iota}=\overline{S^{\iota}}
, where the last equality is valid thanks to
Lemma

4.4
. Since
act(\overline{S^{\iota}})=act(S^{\iota})
, we obtain that
act(S)\subset act(S^{\iota})
. In other words, if a vertex is active in a split graph, then it is also active in its inverse. This means that if
a
is active in
S^{\iota}
, then
a
is active in
(S^{\iota})^{\iota}=S
; that is,
act(S^{\iota})\subset act(S)
.

∎

Proposition

4.6
implicitly tells us that inversion does not generally preserve the property of being balanced. In fact, it is easy to find a balanced non-empty split graph
S
that contains isolated or universal vertices. In such a case,
S^{\iota}
will be unbalanced by
Proposition

4.6
(3). As a concrete example, consider the graph
(S,[3],\{a,b\})
such that
N_{a}=\{1,2\}
and

, which is balanced, and in which vertex 2 is universal. By inverting

, we obtain that
E(S^{\iota})=\{1a,2a,2b,3b,ab\}
. Since
N_{S^{\iota}}(2)=\{a,b\}
, we see that, indeed, vertex 2 is swing in
(S^{\iota},\{a,b\},[3])
. An analogous example can be constructed by taking
\overline{S}
, which is also balanced. Here, vertex 2 is isolated in
\overline{S}
, but swing in
((\overline{S})^{\iota},[3],\{a,b\})
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Consider the split graph
(G,K,I)
with
|K|=|I|
,
K=\{x_{v}:v\in I\}
, and
N_{G}(v)=\{x_{v}\}
for all
v\in I
. Let
S
be a split graph such that
|\Phi(S)|=|I|
. Then,
S
is active and
\Phi(S)
is simple and complete if and only if
S
is isomorphic to
G
or
\overline{G^{\iota}}
.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
). By the definition of
G
, it is very easy to verify that it is active and
\Phi(G)
is simple and complete. Verifying the same properties for
\overline{G^{\iota}}
is immediate thanks to
Proposition

4.5
.

(
\Rightarrow
). From
Theorem

4.3
we know that the independent vertices of
(S,K^{\prime},I^{\prime})
all have the same degree
d
in

,

to be isomorphic to

, and

5
Simple factor graphs

The following is a technical result that relates the subgraph of
\Phi(S)
, induced by a subset
A
of its vertices, to the factor graph of the corresponding induced subgraph in
S
that has
A
as an independent set. It also shows that, in some way,
\Phi
does not depend on universal vertices, i.e., removing universal vertices in the underlying split graph does not alter the structure of the factor graph.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(S,K,I)
be a split graph where
K=\bigcup_{u\in I}N_{u}
. For
A\subset I
, define

S_{A}=\left\langle\bigcup_{v\in A}(v\dot{\cup}N_{v})\right\rangle_{S},\ S_{A}^{\prime}=S_{A}-\bigcap_{v\in A}N_{v}.

Then

\left(\bigcup_{v\in A}N_{v},A\right),\ \left(\bigcup_{v\in A}N_{v}-\bigcap_{v\in A}N_{v},A\right)

are bipartitions for
S_{A}
and
S_{A}^{\prime}
, respectively, and

\Phi(S_{A})=\langle A\rangle_{\Phi}=\Phi(S_{A}^{\prime}).

Proof.

By definition,
\Phi(S_{A}),\langle A\rangle_{\Phi}
and
\Phi(S_{A}^{\prime})
have the same vertex set, namely
A
. Then, to obtain the required equalities, it suffices to show that

\sigma_{ab}(S_{A})=\sigma_{ab}=\sigma_{ab}(S^{\prime}_{A}),

for every pair of vertices
a,b\in A
. On one hand, we know that
\sigma_{ab}=(d_{a}-\eta_{ab})(d_{b}-\eta_{ab})
(see (
1
)). On the other hand:
N_{S_{A}}(v)=N_{v}
, for all
v\in A
, by the definition of
S_{A}
. With this in mind, it follows immediately that
\sigma_{ab}(S_{A})=\sigma_{ab}
.

To prove that
\sigma_{ab}(S^{\prime}_{A})=\sigma_{ab}
, observe that

N_{S_{A}^{\prime}}(u)=N_{u}-\bigcap_{v\in A}N_{v},

for all
u\in A
, and that

N_{S_{A}^{\prime}}(a)\cap N_{S_{A}^{\prime}}(b)=N_{a}\cap N_{b}-\bigcap_{v\in A}N_{v}.

Letting
\eta_{A}=|\bigcap_{v\in A}N_{v}|
,
d_{u}^{\prime}=\deg_{S_{A}^{\prime}}(u)
and
\eta_{ab}(S_{A}^{\prime})=\eta_{ab}^{\prime}
, we then obtain that
d_{u}^{\prime}=d_{u}-\eta_{A}
and
\eta_{ab}^{\prime}=\eta_{ab}-\eta_{A}
. Using this in the equality

\sigma_{ab}(S_{A}^{\prime})=(d_{a}^{\prime}-\eta_{ab}^{\prime})(d_{b}^{\prime}-\eta_{ab}^{\prime}),

we finally prove that
\sigma_{ab}(S_{A}^{\prime})=\sigma_{ab}
.
∎

The following result is very important because it provides structural information about both
S
and
\Phi(S)
when the latter is simple and connected, depending on whether it is complete or not. In short, we show that
S
and
\Phi(S)
have the same clique number.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(S,K,I)
be an active split graph such that
\Phi=\Phi(S)
is simple and connected. Then one and only one of the following two possibilities occurs:

1.

\Phi
is complete and
|K|=|I|
.

2.

|K|=\omega(\Phi)<|I|
and every vertex of
\Phi
belongs to some maximum clique of
\Phi
; in particular:

|K|\leq 1+\min\{\deg_{\Phi}(v):v\in I\}.

Proof.

(1).

Consider a maximum clique
C\subset I
in
\Phi
. If
C=I
, we are done because
\Phi
is complete and, therefore,
|I|=|K|
, according to
Theorem

4.3
.

(2).

Then, we assume that
C\neq I
, i.e.,
|C|=\omega(\Phi)<|I|
. Thus, for each
x\in I-C
there must be a vertex
q\in C
such that
\sigma_{qx}=0
, i.e.,
N_{x}=N_{q}
(otherwise,
C
would not be a maximum clique). This fact has two important consequences. First, that
N_{\Phi}(x)=N_{\Phi}(q)
; this means, in particular, that
x
forms another maximum clique in
\Phi
together with the vertices of
C-q
. Second, that
\bigcup_{C}N_{v}=\bigcup_{I}N_{v}=K
and
\bigcap_{C}N_{v}=\bigcap_{I}N_{v}=\varnothing
. If
S_{C}=(\langle\bigcup_{C}(v\dot{\cup}N_{v})\rangle_{S},C,K)
, then
\Phi(S_{C})=\langle C\rangle_{\Phi}
, by
Proposition

5.1
. Clearly, we have that
|C|\geq 2
, because
S
is active, and hence
\Phi
has at least one edge. Since
\Phi(S_{C})
is simple and complete, it follows from
Theorem

4.3
that
S_{C}
is balanced. But then, by
Lemma

4.1
,
S_{C}
is active, since
\bigcap_{C}N_{v}=\varnothing
. Finally, we can again apply
Theorem

4.3
to infer that
|K|=|C|
.

Let
v
be any vertex of
\Phi
. Since
v
is part of a clique of size
|K|
in
\Phi
, it follows that
\deg_{\Phi}(v)\geq|K|-1
.

∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
(S,K,I)
be an active split graph and let
\alpha=|I|
and
\omega=|K|
. If
\Phi(S)
is simple and connected, then:

1.

\omega\leq\alpha
;

2.

S
and
\Phi(S)
have the same clique number;

3.

if
\Phi(S)
is complete, then

\deg(S)=\binom{\alpha}{2};

4.

if
\Phi(S)
is not complete, then

\left\lceil\frac{\alpha}{2}(\omega-1)\right\rceil\leq\deg(S)<\binom{\alpha}{2}.

Proof.

(1).

It is part of the content of
Theorem

5.2
.

(2).

Same as (1).

(3).

\deg(S)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subset I}\sigma_{uv}(S)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\subset I}1=\binom{\alpha}{2}
, since
\Phi(S)
is simple and complete.

(4).

The upper bound is a trivial consequence of assuming that
\Phi
is not complete. On the other hand, the lower bound is deduced from
Theorem

5.2
via the Handshake Lemma. In fact,

|\Phi|\min\{\deg_{\Phi}(v):v\in I\}=\alpha(\omega-1)\leq

\sum_{v\in I}\deg_{\Phi}(v)=2|E(\Phi)|=2\deg(S).

∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
S
be an active split graph. If
\Phi(S)
is simple, connected, but not complete, then
\Phi(\overline{S})
is complete but not simple.

Proof.

Being
(K,I)
the bipartition of

, we know that
|K|=\omega(\Phi(S))<|I|
, by

5.2
. This means that
|E(\Phi(S))|>\binom{|K|}{2}
. Thus,
\Phi(\overline{S})
cannot be simple, since
\deg(\overline{S})=\deg(S)
(see
[
6
]
, page 15).

We will now prove that
\Phi(\overline{S})
is complete. To this end, observe the following: if for each
v\in I
we remove all
u\in I-v
that are twins of
v
in
S
, it is clear (by
Theorem

3.3
and by
Theorem

3.2
(1)) that we obtain an active split graph

\sigma_{xy}(\overline{H})\neq 0

\overline{H}\prec\overline{S}

\sigma_{xy}(\overline{S})\neq 0

∎

Finally, with the next Theorem, all active split graphs

Theorem 5.5
.

Given a split graph
(S,K,I)

\sigma_{uv}(S)=0

\overline{R^{\iota}}

(
\Leftarrow
). Thanks to

4.5

\deg_{S}(v)=1

\Phi(S)
is simple. Moreover, since

by hypothesis, it is evident that

is active. Since

\Phi(S)

3.2

(
\Rightarrow

v\in I

u\in I-v
that are twins of
v

Theorem

3.3
and by
Theorem

(1)) that we obtain an active split graph

\Phi(H)

G

. It is important to note that, obviously,

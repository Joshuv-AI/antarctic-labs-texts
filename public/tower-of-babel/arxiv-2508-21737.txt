The NilHecke schober
Jasper van de Kreeke

Abstract
Perverse schobers can be used to describe Fukaya categories but are hard to axiomatize and construct. In this paper, we give an explicit construction of a perverse schober intended to accurately describe the Fukaya category of the horizontal Hilbert scheme considered by Aganagic et al. in the frame of the knot invariant categorification program. The formalism is based on the notion of A n A_{n} -schobers of Dyckerhoff and Wedrich. The construction is entirely algebraic and we check the schober axioms with the help of diagrammatic calculus.

Introduction

Perverse schobers are a concept of “sheaf of categories” over a given stratified base space
B
. In the case of the symmetric product
B=\operatorname{Sym}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
, a full definition of perverse schober was achieved by Dyckerhoff-Wedrich
[
5
]
. It entails the datum of a cubical diagram of stable
\infty
-categories satisfying certain axioms:

\chi:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\operatorname{St}.

The horizontal Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}^{\operatorname{hor}}_{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is a Liouville manifold and its Fukaya category with potential was studied in
[
3
]
. Up to generation questions, this category is entirely described as
\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{n}
where
\operatorname{NH}_{n}
is the NilHecke algebra on
n
strands. The perfect complexes of NilHecke algebras assemble into a cubical diagram as well:

\displaystyle\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}:\{0,1\}^{n}\cong\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{St},

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle\mapsto\operatorname{Perf}(\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket}\ldots\otimes_{\mathbb{C}\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket}\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma_{k}}).

In this paper, we check that
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}
is a perverse schober on
\operatorname{Sym}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
in the sense of Dyckerhoff-Wedrich.

Perverse schobers

The concept of perverse schobers was introduced by Kapranov and Schechtman
[
6
]
as the closest approximation to the notion of perverse sheaf of categories with respect to a given stratification. Their primary use case has been to describe Fukaya categories and similar categories in a way that allows for
\infty
-theoretical local constructions, effectively making the treatment more ergonomic compared to global methods.

The precise notion of perverse schober depends on the given base space and its stratification and is often hard to axiomatize. The reason is that a perverse schober on a given base should precisely decatigorify, upon taking
K_{0}
, to the corresponding notion of perverse sheaf on the same base space. Accurate notions of perverse schobers have been given so far for marked disks
[
6
]
, marked surfaces
[
4
]
and symmetric powers
\operatorname{Sym}^{k+1}(\mathbb{C})

[
5
]
.

Fukaya categories of Coulomb branches

Fukaya categories of Coulomb branches have been studied in
[
3
]
, as part of the program to categorify knot invariants via mirror symmetry
[
1
,
2
]
. The result of that paper is an explicit description of the subcategory of
\operatorname{Fuk}(\mathcal{M}^{\times}(Q,d),W)
given by a certain set of objects
T_{\theta}
. More precisely, there is a matching of objects
T_{\theta}
with red and black dot distributions on the line, and a matching of their hom spaces with linear spans of strand diagrams between two configurations. This amounts to the inclusion
\operatorname{Perf}(\operatorname{KLRW})\subseteq\operatorname{Fuk}(\mathcal{M}^{\times}(Q,d),W)
.

Let us focus on the case of
(Q,d)
being the
A_{1}
-quiver without framing and with arbitrary dimension
d=n+1
. In this case, we have
\mathcal{M}^{\times}(Q,d)=\operatorname{Hilb}^{\operatorname{hor}}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\operatorname{KLRW}=\operatorname{NH}_{n+1}
. The horizontal Hilbert scheme comes with a map
\pi:\operatorname{Hilb}^{\operatorname{hor}}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\to\operatorname{Sym}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
, which is a fiber bundle over the open locus
\operatorname{Sym}_{\neq}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
consisting of points with pairwise distinct coordinates. This suggests that
\operatorname{Fuk}(\operatorname{Hilb}^{\operatorname{hor}}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),W)
is the global sections category of a “sheaf of categories” over
\operatorname{Sym}^{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
.

The schober perspective makes for the hypothesis that
[
3
]
is in fact the shadow of an equality between two different schobers: the “algebraic” NilHecke schober
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}
and a “geometric” horizontal Hilbert scheme schober
F_{x}\coloneqq\operatorname{Fuk}(\pi^{-1}(x),W|_{\pi^{-1}(x)})
. The present paper is a first step in that direction.

2.1
Cubical diagrams

In this section, we recall cubical diagrams in general. We recall the indexing set
\{0,1\}^{n}
and its interpretation in terms of compositions of
n+1

Definition 2.1
.

A

is an ordered tuple of positive natural numbers
\sigma=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})

There are precisely

Definition 2.2
.

The binary presentation map

is defined by

\psi_{n}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})=0^{n_{1}-1}10^{n_{2}-1}1\ldots 0^{n_{k}-1}.

The symbol
\psi
denotes any such
\psi_{n}
generically.

We have for instance
\psi(3,5)=0010000
and
\psi(3,5,5,3)=001000010000100
. We remark for later reference that the palindromic nature of a composition
\sigma
is reflected in its binary presentation
\psi(\sigma)
. For two compositions
\sigma,\tau\in\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)
we write
\sigma\leq\tau
if
\tau

\sigma
. With respect to the lexical partial ordering on
\{0,1\}^{n}

2.2
Bifactorization cubes

In this section, we recall the construction of bifactorization cubes. We make the construction as explicit as possible. As indexing set for the vertices we typically use the binary presentation set
\{0,1\}^{n}
.

The definition of bifactorization cubes starts from a pair of two-element compositions
(a,b),(c,d)\in\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)
. We start by introducing a case distinction by relation between the numbers
a,b,c,d
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
((a,b),(c,d))\in\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)
be a pair of compositions. Then it is of one of the following forms:

•

a=c
cases

1.

((a,b),(c,d))=((c,c+m),(c,c+m))
with
c,m>0
.

2.

((a,b),(c,d))=((a,a),(a,a))
with
a>0
.

3.

((a,b),(c,d))=((b+m,b),(b+m,b))
with
b,m>0
.

•

a>c
cases

1.

((a,b),(c,d))=((c+l,c),(c,c+l))
with
c,l>0
.

2.

((a,b),(c,d))=((b+m+l,b),(b+m,b+l))
with
b,m,l>0
.

3.

((a,b),(c,d))=((c+l,c+m),(c,c+m+l))
with
c,m,l>0
.

•

a<c
cases

1.

((a,b),(c,d))=((a,a+m),(a+m,a))
with
a,m>0
.

2.

((a,b),(c,d))=((a+l,a+m),(a+m+l,a))
with
a,d,m>0
.

3.

((a,b),(c,d))=((a,a+m+l),(a+m,a+l))

a,d,m>0
.

Proof.

Assume
a=c
. If
b>a
, we are in the first case. If
b=a
, we are in the second case. If
b<a
, we are in the third case. Assume now
a>c
. If
b=c
, we are in the first case. If
b<c

b>c
, we are in the third case. Assume now
a<c
. If
b=c
, we are in the first case. If
b<c

2.4
A_{n}
-schobers

In this section, we recall the definition of
A_{n}
-schobers. Such a schober consists of a cubical diagram of stable
\infty
-categories which satisfies certain properties. For the purposes of the present paper, we shall ignore most of the higher
\infty
-categorical structure contained in the datum of the cubical diagram, since the NilHecke algebras are ungraded associative algebras and therefore the structure is entirely understood in terms of the abelian world.

Definition 2.11

(
[
5
, Definition 3.17]
)
.

A cubical diagram
\chi:\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)\to\operatorname{St}
of stable
\infty
-categories is an
A_{n}
-schober
if the following conditions hold:

•

Adjunctability:
All functors in
\chi
admit right adjoints.

•

Recursiveness:
For every proper composition
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\in\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)
and every
1\leq i\leq k
, the restriction of
\chi
along
\operatorname{Comp}(n_{i})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Comp}(n+1),\tau\mapsto(n_{1},\ldots,n_{i-1},\tau,n_{i+1},\ldots,n_{k})
is an
A_{n_{i}-1}
-schober.

•

Far-commutativity:
For every composition
(a,b)\in\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)
and compositions
c_{0}\leq c_{1}
of
a
and compositions

Twist invertibility:

Remark 2.12
.

The square in the definition of far-commutativity is a 2-dimensional cubical diagram of categories. Its Beck-Chevalley cube is thus a 1-dimensional diagram of parallel functors, in other words a natural transformation. The far-commutativity axiom demands that this natural transformation is an isomorphism.

3.2
The NilHecke schober

In this section, we introduce the NilHecke schober as a cubical diagram
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\operatorname{St}
. After providing the definition, we explain the content of the edge maps of the diagram. These adge maps are induction and restriction functors for right modules over NilHecke algebras, and we describe them in terms of diagrammatic calculus.

Definition 3.7
.

The
NilHecke schober
is the cubical diagram
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}:\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)\to\operatorname{St}
is defined by
\tau\mapsto\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}
together with the restriction functors
\operatorname{Res}^{\sigma}_{\tau}:\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}\to\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}
.

Remark 3.8
.

We work with right modules over the NilHecke algebras. In particular
\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
denotes the category of perfect complexes of right modules. Since induction and restriction functors of perfect modules can be developed entirely in the abelian world, we shall be somewhat sloppy with terminology. Given an element
T\in\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
, we shall for instance refer to its forgetful object in the category of chain complexes as a vector space instead of a complex. This terminology serves merely to distinguish the
\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
-module structure from the underlying linear structure.

Whenever
\sigma\leq\tau
are two compositions of
n+1
, we have an inclusion of algebras
\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}\subseteq\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
. This gives rise to a restriction functor
\operatorname{Res}_{\tau}^{\sigma}:\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}\to\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}
and its right adjoint
\operatorname{Ind}_{\tau}^{\sigma}:\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}\to\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
. They are given by

\operatorname{Ind}_{\tau}^{\sigma}(T)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}}(\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma},T),\quad\operatorname{Res}_{\tau}^{\sigma}(T)=T.

The Hom space
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}}(\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma},T)
refers to the right
\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}
-linear mappings
\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}\to T
. The left
\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
-action on
\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
equips
\operatorname{Ind}_{\tau}^{\sigma}(T)
with a right
\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
-action as desired.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\sigma\leq\tau
be two compositions of
n+1
and let
T\in\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}
. Then as vector spaces we have
\operatorname{Ind}^{\sigma}_{\tau}(T)=\operatorname{Map}(S_{\sigma,\tau},T)
.

Proof.

We have
\operatorname{Ind}_{\tau}^{\sigma}(T)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}}(\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma},T)
. By
Lemma 3.6
, we have a decomposition of
\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
as right
\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}
-module into simple
\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}
-modules each indexed by an element
s\in S_{\sigma,\tau}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

This means that we can visualize the induction functor
\operatorname{Ind}_{\tau}^{\sigma}
through shuffle diagrams, which are a subset of the permutation group
S_{n+1}
. Conversely, we can also visualize the restriction functor
\operatorname{Res}_{\tau}^{\sigma}
through diagrams. In this interpretation, the restriction functor acts as only one single diagram, namely the identity diagram.

3.3
Adjunctability, recursiveness and far-commutativity

In this section, we show that the cubical diagram
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}
satisfies the Adjunctability, Recursiveness and Far-commutativity axioms. We proceed by checking all three after each other.

Lemma 3.10
.

The cubical diagram
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}
satisfies Adjunctability.

Proof.

The induction functor
\operatorname{Ind}^{\sigma}_{\tau}
is the right adjoint to
\operatorname{Res}^{\sigma}_{\tau}
, which establishes the Adjunctability axiom.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

The cubical diagram
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}
satisfies Recursiveness.

Proof.

Regard a composition
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\in\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)
. Then the restriction
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}|_{\operatorname{Comp}(n_{i})}
along the inclusion map
\operatorname{Comp}(n_{i})\to\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)
given by
\tau\mapsto(n_{1},\ldots,n_{i-1},\tau,n_{i+1},\ldots n_{k})
is given by

(\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}|_{\operatorname{Comp}(n_{i})})_{\tau}=\operatorname{Perf}(\operatorname{NH}_{n_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\operatorname{NH}_{n_{i-1}}\otimes\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}\otimes\operatorname{NH}_{n_{i+1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\operatorname{NH}_{n_{k}}).

The functors in this restricted diagram are given by the forgetful functors between
\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\tau}
and
\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{\sigma}
for
\sigma,\tau\in\operatorname{Comp}(n_{i})
, tensored by identities on the left and right. Checking that this diagram is an
A_{n_{i}-1}
-schober comes down to the fact that
\mathbf{NH}_{n_{i}}
is an
A_{n_{i}-1}
-schober, which we shall assume by means of induction.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

The cubical diagram
\mathbf{NH}_{n+1}
satisfies Far-commutativity.

Proof.

Regard a composition
(a,b)\in\,\operatorname{Comp}(n+1)
and compositions
c_{0}\leq c_{1}
of
a
and
d_{0}\leq d_{1}
of
d
. Regard the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{c_{0},d_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{c_{0},d_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{c_{1},d_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{c_{1},d_{1}}.}

It is our task to show that the BC map of this square is an equivalence. The source and target of the BC map are two functors
\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{c_{0},d_{1}}\to\operatorname{Perf}\operatorname{NH}_{c_{1},d_{0}}
. The first functor is given by first inducing along
d_{0}\leq d_{1}
and then forgetting along
c_{0}\leq c_{1}
. The second functor is given by first forgetting along
c_{0}\leq c_{1}
and then inducing along
d_{0}\leq d_{1}
. Since both agree, we finish the proof.
∎

Equivariant Morse-Bott cohomology through stabilization
Erkao Bao, Robi Huq, Shengzhen Ning

Abstract
. For closed manifolds with compact Lie group actions, we study Austin-Braam’s Morse-theoretic construction of Borel equivariant cohomology using the technique of stabilization . We show that a C 1 C^{1} -small equivariant perturbation produces stable invariant Morse-Bott functions. This allows us to realize the equivariant transversality and orientability assumptions in Austin–Braam’s framework by choosing generic invariant Riemannian metrics.



1.
Introduction

Equivariant Morse theory has been studied extensively since the pioneering work of Atiyah-Bott
[
1
]
on the Yang-Mills functional over Riemann surfaces. Equivariant Morse cohomology for general compact Lie group actions was first developed by Austin-Braam
[
2
]
. However, their construction relies on several assumptions (see Assumption
4.1
) that do not hold in general. We now explain the main analytical difficulties underlying these assumptions.

When the group
G
is not finite,
G
-invariant functions may have non-discrete critical sets and one must work with Morse-Bott functions instead of Morse functions. In the Morse-Bott setting, a typical chain model, used by

[
2
]
, associates to each critical submanifold its space of
differential forms, and defines the boundary operator via pullback and
integration along the fiber through the endpoint evaluation maps on the moduli
spaces of gradient flow lines. From this perspective, the analytic foundation of
the theory relies on establishing suitable transversality properties for relevant moduli spaces.

The natural transversality condition in this context is the

Morse-Bott-Smale
condition, abbreviated
MBS
(Definition
1.7
). There is also a weaker
notion, referred to as
weakly Morse-Bott-Smale
, or
weakly MBS
(Definition
3.1
). The first
set of difficulties can be summarized as follows:

(C1)

While the weakly MBS condition can be achieved in the non-equivariant Morse-Bott setting, it generally fails in the equivariant Morse-Bott setting. Moreover, the weakly MBS condition alone is insufficient to define a Morse-theoretic chain complex.

(C2)

The MBS condition cannot, in general, be achieved even in the non-equivariant Morse-Bott case.

A classical example in
[
8
]
, the
torus with legs
(Example
3.3
), exhibits a Morse-Bott function for which the MBS condition fails for all metrics, although the weakly MBS condition can still be arranged. This example justifies (C2).

Later, we introduce another example of a mapping torus (Example
5.2
), which illustrates the obstruction described in (C1).

A further complication in Morse-Bott theory is the following:

(C3)

The moduli spaces of gradient flow lines are not always orientable.

This usually necessitates the use of local coefficient systems or orientation bundle valued differential forms.

Taken together, these issues contribute to the relative underdevelopment of Morse-theoretic equivariant (co)homology for compact Lie group actions.

We now analyze these difficulties in greater detail. The example of torus with legs (Example
3.3
) can be upgraded into the equivariant setting by letting
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
act on it by rotations. It shows that the obstructions above persist even when the group action itself is well behaved. In particular, the
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-action on torus with legs is free, yet the function still fails to admit a Morse-Bott-Smale metric. This observation motivates a refinement of the notion of
G
-invariant Morse-Bott functions. Rather than merely requiring
G
-invariance, we consider
G
-Morse-Bott functions
(Definition
1.2
), for which each critical submanifold is required to be a single
G
-orbit. Restricting attention to
G
-Morse-Bott functions excludes the example of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-equivariant torus with legs, but does not resolve the obstruction in (C1).

Note that the function in the mapping torus example (Example
5.2
) is
G
-Morse-Bott. To address this remaining difficulty, we adapt the
stabilization
approach developed in
[
9
,
5
]
. Starting from a
G
-Morse-Bott function, we perform a carefully chosen,
C^{1}
-small equivariant perturbation near the critical submanifolds. The resulting function, which we call
stable
, retains the essential equivariant features of the original function while exhibiting substantially improved analytical properties. It is worth emphasizing that, in the example of torus with legs, the Morse-Bott function is stable but not
G
-Morse-Bott. The combination of stability and the
G
-Morse-Bott condition turns out to be precisely what is needed.

Key observation.

The central theme of this paper is that
stable
G
-Morse-Bott functions
provide the appropriate framework of equivariant Morse theory for compact Lie group actions. By considering such class of functions, we resolve the transversality obstructions by achieving Morse-Bott-Smale condition for generic
G
-invariant metrics, and overcome orientability issues without appealing to local coefficient systems or orientation bundle valued differential forms. As a consequence, we obtain a well-defined Morse-theoretic equivariant cochain complex that recovers Borel equivariant cohomology.

The stabilization procedure is explicit in certain cases, making concrete computations accessible. In a subsequent paper, we will exploit this feature further by studying the limiting behavior of the equivariant Morse complex as the size of the perturbation tends to zero, thereby relating the stabilized and unstabilized theories in a precise way.

1.1.
Main results

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
be a smooth function on a closed smooth manifold
M
. Let
p\in M
be some critical point of
f
. The Hessian of
f
at the critical point
p
is a symmetric bilinear form Hess
{}_{f}(p):T_{p}M\times T_{p}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\text{Hess}_{f}(p)(v,w)=V(Wf),

where
V,W
are any vector fields extending
v,w\in T_{p}M
. Let

\text{ker}(\text{Hess}_{f}(p)):=\{v\in T_{p}M\,|\,\text{Hess}_{f}(p)(v,w)=0,\forall w\in T_{p}M\}.

Definition 1.1

(Morse-Bott functions)
.

A smooth function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is called
Morse-Bott
if its critical set
\crit(f)
is a disjoint union of connected submanifolds such that for each connected component
S
of
\crit(f)
and any
p\in S
, we have
T_{p}S=\text{ker}(\text{Hess}_{f}(p))
. We denote by
\ind_{f}(S)
(or simply
\ind(S)
when the function is clear in context) the Morse index of
S
, which is the maximal dimension of a subspace in
T_{p}M
where
\text{Hess}_{f}(p)
is negative definite, for any
p\in S
.

Now, suppose there is a smooth action of the compact Lie group
G
on
M
. Let
C^{\infty}(M)^{G}
be the space of
G
-invariant smooth functions on
M
. When
G
is a Lie group,
C^{\infty}(M)^{G}
may not contain any Morse function since if
p\in M
is a critical point for some

, then all points in the orbit submanifold
G\cdot p
are also critical points of
f
. As a result, to study the
G
-equivariant Morse theory on

, it is natural to allow for critical submanifolds.

Definition 1.2

(
G
-Morse-Bott functions)
.

A
G
-invariant function

is called
G
-Morse-Bott
if

•

f
is Morse-Bott;

•

each connected component
S
of the critical submanifolds of
f
satisfies
T_{p}S=T_{p}(G\cdot p)
for any
p\in S
.

This notion was simply called
Morse
in
[
13
]
or
G
-Morse
in
[
3
]
. When

is a finite group, it also coincides with the notion of equivariant Morse function introduced in
[
5

acts trivially on

, then a
G
-Morse-Bott function reduces to an ordinary (non-equivariant)
Morse function. It is important to distinguish between a
G
-invariant Morse-Bott function and a

G
-Morse-Bott function. For instance, constant functions are always

G
-invariant Morse-Bott, but they are not
G
-Morse-Bott in the sense of

1.2
unless
G
acts transitively on

-invariant Morse-Bott function, but not a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-Morse-Bott function.

A fundamental fact, proved in
[

, asserts the existence result for
G
-Morse-Bott functions.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
13
]
)
.

G
-Morse-Bott functions are dense in
C^{\infty}(M)^{G}
.

Now, we consider the critical submanifolds of some
G
-invariant Morse-Bott function
f
. An orbit

consisting of critical points will be called a

, which is diffeomorphic to the Lie group quotient
G/H
where

is the stabilizer group at

. Since Hess
{}_{f}(p)(v,\textrm{-})=0
for any

{}_{f}(p)
will also be used to denote the symmetric bilinear form on the normal space

Thus, when

is further assumed to be
G
-Morse-Bott, Hess
{}_{f}(p)
will be non-degenerate on

. There is also a natural slice representation of

on

. It admits a canonical decomposition

into the trivial part

, and the non-trivial part

such that

\int_{H}h\cdot v\,d\mu_{h}=0

for the Haar measure
\mu

-invariant neighborhood

of

that takes

\mathcal{N}_{p}=\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\prime}\oplus\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\prime\prime}

where

is identified with the trivial bundle

and
\mathcal{N}_{p}^{\prime\prime}
is identified with the possibly non-trivial bundle

]

Definition 1.4

(Stable)

Let

2.
Stabilization

In this section, we adapt the proof in
[
5
]
to prove Theorem
1.6
. Let
G
be a compact Lie group acting on a closed manifold
M
. Recall that our original Definition
1.4
for stable
G
-invariant Morse-Bott functions is metric-independent. If, in addition,
M
is equipped with a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric
g
, then
for any critical orbit
G\cdot p
the normal space
N_{p}
can be canonically
identified with the orthogonal complement
T_{p}(G\cdot p)^{\perp}
with respect to

g
. We denote by
g_{p}
the induced inner product on
N_{p}
. With this
identification, the Hessian
\Hess_{f}(p)
may be viewed as a self-adjoint operator

A_{p}
on
N_{p}
, defined by

\Hess_{f}(p)(v,w)=g_{p}(A_{p}v,w).

In this case,
N_{p}
admits a non-canonical orthogonal decomposition

N_{p}=N^{+}_{p}\oplus N^{-}_{p}\oplus N_{p}^{0},

where the subspaces
N^{+}_{p}
,
N^{-}_{p}
and
N^{0}_{p}
, depending on
g_{p}
, denote the positive, negative and zero eigenspaces of
A_{p}
, respectively. Note that when
f
is
G
-Morse-Bott, the zero eigenspace
N_{p}^{0}
is trivial. Under the presence of
G
-invariant metric, the following lemma provides the alternative characterization of stability.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be a
G
-invariant Morse-Bott function with some critical orbit
G\cdot p
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

f
is stable at
G\cdot p
.

(2)

For any
G
-invariant metric
g
,
N^{-}_{p}\oplus N_{p}^{0}\subseteq N^{\prime}_{p}
. That is,
N^{-}_{p}\oplus N_{p}^{0}
is fixed by
\stab(p)
.

(3)

There exists a
G
-invariant metric
g
such that
N^{-}_{p}\oplus N_{p}^{0}\subseteq N^{\prime}_{p}
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2):
Assume
f
is stable at
G\cdot p
and take any
G
-invariant metric
g
. Let
H\subseteq G
be the stabilizer group at
p
. By the
G
-invariance of
f
,

\Hess_{f}(p)(v,w)=\Hess_{f}(p)(h\cdot v,h\cdot w)

for any
h\in H
and
v,w\in N_{p}
. By the definition of
A_{p}
,

g_{p}(A_{p}(h\cdot v),h\cdot w)=g_{p}(A_{p}v,w)=g_{p}(h\cdot A_{p}v,h\cdot w),

which implies that
A_{p}(h\cdot v)=h\cdot A_{p}v
for any
h\in H
and
v\in N_{p}
. We see that
A_{p}(N_{p}^{\prime})\subseteq N_{p}^{\prime}
. Note that the canonical decomposition
N_{p}^{\prime}
and
N_{p}^{\prime\prime}
are

v\in N_{p}^{\prime},w\in N_{p}^{\prime\prime}

g_{p}(v,w)=\int_{H}g_{p}(h\cdot v,h\cdot w)d\mu_{h}=\int_{H}g_{p}(v,h\cdot w)d\mu_{h}=g_{p}(v,\int_{H}h\cdot wd\mu_{h})=0.

As a result, we also have
A_{p}(N_{p}^{\prime\prime})\subseteq N_{p}^{\prime\prime}
since

is self-adjoint. Let

be an eigenvector with non-positive eigenvalue
\lambda
. Suppose

in the decomposition

. Since

and

. By the stability assumption,

must be

. Therefore, we have proved

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3):
By the existence of

(3)

3.
Genericity of Morse-Bott-Smale metrics

3.1.
Morse-Bott case

In this section, we review the case when there is no group action, i.e.
G=\{e\}
. When
G=\{e\}
,
G
-Morse-Bott functions are the same as the Morse functions. However, for comparison purposes, we consider a larger class of functions, Morse-Bott functions.

To define Morse-Bott cohomology, depending on the choice of cochain model on the
critical submanifolds, one may impose various transversality conditions; see, for instance,
[
6
,
2
,
7
,
4
,
15
]
. A first and typically indispensable requirement is that the moduli spaces of gradient flow lines be transversely cut out. This is the
weakly Morse-Bott-Smale
condition introduced below, which is a weaker version of Definition
1.7
.

Definition 3.1

(Weakly Morse-Bott-Smale)
.

The pair
(f,g)
of a Morse-Bott function
f
and a Riemannian metric
g
is called
weakly Morse-Bott-Smale
(or
weakly MBS
for short) if for any two components
S_{1},S_{2}
of
\crit(f)
, the descending manifold
\mathscr{D}(S_{1})=\mathscr{D}_{f,g}(S_{1})
of
S_{1}
intersects the ascending manifold
\mathscr{A}(S_{2})=\mathscr{A}_{f,g}(S_{2})
of
S_{2}
transversely.

Lemma 3.2

(MBS implies weakly MBS)
.

If the pair
(f,g)
is Morse-Bott-Smale, then it is also weakly Morse-Bott-Smale.

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathscr{D}(S_{1})\cap\mathscr{A}(S_{2})
, there will exist some
p\in S_{1}
such that
x\in\mathscr{D}(p)\cap\mathscr{A}(S_{2})
. If
(f,g)
is Morse-Bott-Smale, then we have

T_{x}M=T_{x}\mathscr{D}(p)+T_{x}\mathscr{A}(S_{2})\subseteq T_{x}\mathscr{D}(S_{1})+T_{x}\mathscr{A}(S_{2}),

which implies that
(f,g)
is weakly Morse-Bott-Smale.
∎

The notion of ‘Morse-Bott-Smale’ is strictly stronger than ‘weakly Morse-Bott-Smale’ as indicated by the following example of Latschev

3.2.

In the “problematic” Example

The following Lemma explains the advantage of considering

Lemma 3.5

(Weakly MBS implies MBS)
.

Let the compact Lie group
G
act on the closed manifold
M
with a
G
-Morse-Bott function
f
and a
G
-invariant metric
g
. Assume
G\cdot p
and
G\cdot q
are two critical orbits of
f
. Then
\mathscr{D}(p)\pitchfork_{M}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)
if and only if
\mathscr{D}(G\cdot p)\pitchfork_{M}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the ‘if’ direction. For any
x\in\mathscr{D}(p)\cap\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)
, assume

T_{x}\mathscr{D}(G\cdot p)+T_{x}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)=T_{x}M.

Our goal is to show

T_{x}\mathscr{D}(p)+T_{x}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)=T_{x}M.

We may further assume that
x
is arbitrarily close to the critical point
p
due to the following simple fact.

Claim 3.6
.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
be a gradient flow line of
(f,g)
from
p
to some point in
G\cdot q
. Then for any
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathscr{D}_{g}(p)
and
\mathscr{A}_{g}(G\cdot q)
intersect transversely in
M
at
\gamma(s)
if and only if they intersect transversely at
\gamma(s^{\prime})
.

Proof of Claim.

This follows from the observation that the descending and ascending manifolds are invariant under the gradient flows. So, it maps
T_{\gamma(s)}\mathscr{D}(p)
isomorphically to
T_{\gamma(s^{\prime})}\mathscr{D}(p)
, and maps
T_{\gamma(s)}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)
isomorphically to
T_{\gamma(s^{\prime})}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)
.
∎

On the one hand, since the pair
(f,g)
is
G
-invariant, the orbit
G\cdot x\subseteq\mathscr{D}(G\cdot p)
which implies that

T_{x}\mathscr{D}(p)+T_{x}(G\cdot x)\subseteq T_{x}\mathscr{D}(G\cdot p).

On the other hand, let us take a basis in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and consider their corresponding fundamental vector fields
X_{1},\cdots,X_{\text{dim}G}
on
M
which span the tangent spaces of the orbits. At the point
p
, we have

0=T_{p}\mathscr{D}(p)\cap T_{p}(G\cdot p)=T_{p}\mathscr{D}(p)\cap\text{span}\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{\text{dim}G}\}|_{p}.

For dimensional reason, it follows that

T_{p}\mathscr{D}(G\cdot p)=T_{p}\mathscr{D}(p)+\text{span}\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{\text{dim}G}\}|_{p}.

Since
x
is sufficiently close to
p
, by continuity we have

T_{x}\mathscr{D}(G\cdot p)=T_{x}\mathscr{D}(p)+\text{span}\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{\text{dim}G}\}|_{x}=T_{x}\mathscr{D}(p)+T_{x}(G\cdot x).

Finally, the
G
-invariance of
(f,g)
also implies that
T_{x}(G\cdot x)\subseteq T_{x}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)
. Therefore, we can see that

\displaystyle T_{x}\mathscr{D}(p)+T_{x}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)

\displaystyle=T_{x}\mathscr{D}(p)+T_{x}(G\cdot x)+T_{x}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)

\displaystyle=T_{x}\mathscr{D}(G\cdot p)+T_{x}\mathscr{A}(G\cdot q)

\displaystyle=T_{x}M.

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

-Morse-Bott function

,

-invariant Riemannian metric

\mathscr{D}_{g^{\prime}}(G\cdot p)

the set of subgroups of

[

\displaystyle M_{(H)}=\{x\in M\,|\,\text{stab}(x)\in(H)\}

which has the structure of a

is the flow out

of the (typically non-closed) submanifold

M_{H}=\{x\in M\,|\,\text{stab}(x)=H\}.

We first have the following observation.

Claim 3.7

For any

(resp.

(resp.

\mathscr{D}_{f,g^{\prime}}(p)\subseteq M_{H},\mathscr{D}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot p)\subseteq M_{(H)}.

Proof of Claim.

Since the pair
(f,g^{\prime})
is
G

can not leave

\mathscr{D}_{f|_{M_{H}},g^{\prime}|_{M_{H}}}(p)\subseteq\mathscr{D}_{f,g^{\prime}}(p).

\mathscr{D}_{f,g^{\prime}}(p)\subseteq M_{H}

∎

Under the above setting, if

\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{G}
with fiber
\mathcal{E}_{(u,h)}:=L^{2}(u^{*}TX_{(H)})
. Consider the section
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{E}
defined by

\mathcal{L}=\frac{d}{ds}+\text{grad}_{\overline{f},h}:\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{E},

where
\text{grad}_{\overline{f},h}
is the gradient vector field of
\overline{f}
on
X_{(H)}
with respect to the metric
h\in\mathcal{G}
. For any
(u,h)\in\mathcal{L}^{-1}(0)
, there is the linearized operator

D_{(u,h)}:L^{2}(u^{*}TX_{(H)})\oplus T_{h}\mathcal{G}\rightarrow L^{2}(u^{*}TX_{(H)})

defined by differentiating the section map
\mathcal{L}
at
(u,h)
followed with the projection into the fiber direction
\mathcal{E}_{(u,h)}
.

Now, in order to apply the Sard-Smale Theorem (
[
7
, Theorem 5.4]
), we need to consider the restriction operator
D_{u}
of
D_{(u,h)}
to the
L^{2}(u^{*}TX_{(H)})
component defined by

D_{u}\xi=\nabla_{u^{\prime}(t)}\xi+A_{t}\xi,

where

•

\nabla_{u^{\prime}(t)}
denotes the covariant derivative taken with respect to the Levi-Civita connection of a fixed background metric;

•

A_{t}
is the Hessian operator of
\overline{f}
at
u(t)
viewed as a
(1,1)
-tensor using the metric
h
.

To see
D_{u}
is a Fredholm operator, recall that by
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
or
[
7
, Principle 5.6]
, it suffices to show that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}A_{t}
exist and are invertible self-adjoint operators. Similar to the usual Morse setup, the negative asymptotic limit of
A_{t}
is given by the Hessian of
\overline{f}
at the critical point
\pi(t)
. For the positive asymptotic limit, we can choose a lifting path
v:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M_{(H)}
such that
\pi(v(t))=u(t)
and
v(t)
lies in a local slice
\mathbb{D}_{q}
of
G\cdot q
at
q
when
t\rightarrow+\infty
.
For large
t
, if
\{e_{i}(t)\}
is a frame along
u(t)
which trivializes
u^{*}TX_{(H)}
, there will be a frame
\{l_{i}(t)\}
tangent to
\mathbb{D}_{q}
such that
\pi_{*}(l_{i}(t))=e_{i}(t)
. Therefore,
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}A_{t}(e_{i}(t))
can be computed by the Hessian operator of
f
at
q
and the limiting vectors
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}l_{i}(t)\in T_{q}\mathbb{D}_{q}
. Since the
G
-Morse-Bott assumption implies that
\Hess_{f}(q)
is non-degenerate at
T_{q}\mathbb{D}_{q}
, we see that the positive asymptotic limit of
A_{t}
is also an invertible self-adjoint operator.

Therefore, we can recover the same setup as in the standard argument for obtaining Morse-Smale metrics on closed manifolds. A straightforward computation in local coordinates shows that the space of variations in
T_{h}\mathcal{G}
is large enough to render
D_{(u,h)}
surjective. Moreover, these variations can be chosen to be supported in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of
\pi(p)
(
[
7
, Proposition 5.8, Step 2(a)]
). The Sard-Smale theorem (
[
7
, Theorem 5.4]
) then guarantees that for generic metrics
\overline{g}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
on
X_{(H)}
,
D_{u}
is surjective, which makes
\mathscr{D}_{\overline{f},\overline{g}^{\prime}}(\pi(p))
and
\mathscr{A}_{\overline{f},\overline{g}^{\prime}}(\pi(q))
intersect transversely in
X_{(H)}
.

∎

Step 3

By the previous two steps, we may assume that the metric
\tilde{g}^{\prime}
on
M_{(H)}
satisfies the transversality condition
\mathscr{D}_{\tilde{f},\tilde{g}^{\prime}}(G\cdot p)\pitchfork_{M_{(H)}}\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{A}_{\overline{f},\overline{g}^{\prime}}(\pi(q)))
. We now extend this perturbed metric
\tilde{g}^{\prime}
on
M_{(H)}
to a
G
-invariant metric
g^{\prime}
on
M
which is
C^{k}
-close to the original metric
g
.

Let
K\subseteq X_{(H)}
be a neighborhood of
\pi(p)
that contains the support of the perturbation symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor
\overline{\delta}:=\overline{g}^{\prime}-\overline{g}
in Claim
3.9
. Then, the corresponding symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor
\tilde{\delta}:=\tilde{g}^{\prime}-\tilde{g}
on
M_{(H)}
is supported in
\pi^{-1}(K)\subseteq M_{(H)}
. By shrinking
K
if necessary, we may identify the normal bundle
N_{\pi^{-1}(K)}
of the embedded submanifold
\pi^{-1}(K)\subseteq M
with a
G
-invaiant tubular neighborhood
pr:U(\pi^{-1}(K))\rightarrow\pi^{-1}(K)
defined by the exponential map with respect to the metric
g
. On
U(\pi^{-1}(K))
, we use the
g
-orthogonal splitting

TU(\pi^{-1}(K))\cong pr^{*}(T\pi^{-1}(K))\oplus pr^{*}N_{\pi^{-1}(K)}

to define a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor
\delta
on
M
supported in
U(\pi^{-1}(K))
by declaring that

•

\delta
equals
pr^{*}\tilde{\delta}
along the
pr^{*}(T\pi^{-1}(K))
-direction;

•

\delta(v,\textrm{-})=0
if
v
belongs to the normal direction
pr^{*}N_{\pi^{-1}(K)}
.

This
\delta
is
G
-invariant since the projection map
pr
, the splitting and
\tilde{\delta}
are all
G
-invariant. Fix a
G
-invariant cutoff function
\chi
supported in
U(\pi^{-1}(K))
with
\chi\equiv 1
on a smaller neighborhood of
\pi^{-1}(K)\subseteq U(\pi^{-1}(K))
. Now, we can define

g^{\prime}:=g+\chi\delta

to be a
G
-invariant symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor on
M
which satisfies that the restriction
g^{\prime}|_{M_{(H)}}
coincides with
\tilde{g}^{\prime}
on
M_{(H)}
. Note that
g^{\prime}
can be made arbitrarily
C^{k}
-close to

if we fix the function
\chi
and choose
\delta^{\prime}
to have sufficiently small

-norm. Since small

-perturbation preserves the positive definiteness,
g^{\prime}
defines a
G
-invariant metric on

that extends the perturbation on

.

The last ingredient for the proof of Theorem
1.8
is the following claim analogous to
[
5
, Lemma 7.5]
, which upgrades transversality in
M_{(H)}
to transversality in
M
. This is the place where we make essential use of the stability condition at the critical orbit
G\cdot q
.

Claim 3.10
.

Let
g^{\prime}
be the metric on
M
obtained from the metric
\tilde{g}^{\prime}
on
M_{(H)}
by the above description. If

\mathscr{D}_{\tilde{f},\tilde{g}^{\prime}}(G\cdot p)\pitchfork_{M_{(H)}}\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{A}_{\overline{f},\overline{g}^{\prime}}(\pi(q))),

then we also have

\mathscr{D}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot p)\pitchfork_{M}\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q).

Proof.

By Claim
3.7
, we only need to check the transversality between
\mathscr{D}_{\tilde{f},\tilde{g}^{\prime}}(G\cdot p)
and
\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q)
at the point

x\in\mathscr{D}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot p)\cap\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q)\cap M_{(H)}=\mathscr{D}_{\tilde{f},\tilde{g}^{\prime}}(G\cdot p)\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathscr{A}_{\overline{f},\overline{g}^{\prime}}(\pi(q)))\subseteq M_{(H)}.

By the transversality assumption in
M_{(H)}
, it suffices to show

T_{x}M_{(H)}+T_{x}\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q)=T_{x}M.

By Claim
3.6
, we can further assume that
x
is inside a tubular neighborhood of
G\cdot q
identified with

U(G\cdot q)\cong G\times_{H^{\prime}}N_{q},

where
H^{\prime}
is the stabilizer group at
q
and
N_{q}
is the normal space at
q
. Without loss of generality, let us suppose
q=[e,0]
and
x=[e,v]\in G\times_{H^{\prime}}N_{q}
. Then the assumption that
x\in M_{(H)}
implies that
H^{\prime}
contains a subgroup
H^{\prime\prime}
conjugate to
H
such that
v
is in the linear subspace
N_{q}^{H^{\prime\prime}}\subseteq N_{q}
fixed by
H^{\prime\prime}
. Note that
N_{q}^{H^{\prime\prime}}\supseteq N_{q}^{H^{\prime}}
. Consider the local intersection between
G\times_{H^{\prime}}N_{q}^{H^{\prime}}
and
\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q)
. By the stability assumption at
G\cdot q
, we have

\displaystyle T_{q}M

\displaystyle=N_{q}^{H^{\prime\prime}}+T_{q}(\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q))

\displaystyle\supseteq N_{q}^{H^{\prime}}+T_{q}(\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q))

\displaystyle=N_{q}^{H^{\prime}}+T_{q}(G\cdot q)+T_{q}(\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q))

\displaystyle=T_{[e,0]}(G\times_{H^{\prime}}N_{q}^{H^{\prime}})+T_{q}(\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q)).

Since
M_{(H)}\cap(G\times_{H^{\prime}}N^{H^{\prime}}_{q})
is obtained from
G\times_{H^{\prime}}N^{H^{\prime}}_{q}
by removing lower strata corresponding to larger stabilizer groups, it is a dense open submanifold of
G\times_{H^{\prime}}N^{H^{\prime}}_{q}
. Therefore, when
x
is sufficiently close to

, we will have our desired transversality between
M_{(H)}
and
\mathscr{A}_{f,g^{\prime}}(G\cdot q)
at

by continuity.

∎

Finally, note that there are only finitely many critical orbits. To complete the proof of Theorem

, we just need to follow the above scheme to perform generic perturbation of the metric near each critical orbit
G\cdot m
inside
M_{(\text{stab}(m))}
and then extend the perturbation to
M
in a
G
-invariant manner.

∎

Remark 3.11
.

The above proof of equivariant transversality for Lie group actions differs from that of
[
5
]
for finite group actions in that we work with the stratum
M_{(H)}
and its quotient
X_{(H)}
, rather than the submanifold of
H
-fixed points

M^{H}=\{x\in M\mid H\subseteq\operatorname{stab}(x)\}.

When
G
is finite, the critical orbit
G\cdot p
is a discrete subset of
M^{H}
, so that
p
admits a sufficiently small neighborhood
V_{p}\subseteq M^{H}
that is disjoint from the other points of
G\cdot p
. In that setting, the generic perturbation in
[
5
]
is carried out within
V_{p}
. In the Lie group setting, the perturbation is instead performed inside a small neighborhood
K
of
\pi(p)\in X_{(H)}
.

4.
Morse-theoretic equivariant cohomology

4.1.
Moduli spaces of Morse flow lines and their orientability

In
[
2
, Section 3.2]
, Austin–Braam introduced the following assumptions in order to define a Morse-Bott cochain complex associated to a pair
(f,g)
.

Assumption 4.1
.

(1)

The function
f
is
weakly self-indexing
. That is, for any connected components
S,S^{\prime}\subseteq\crit(f)
, there are no negative gradient flow lines from
S
to
S^{\prime}
if
\ind(S)\leq\ind(S^{\prime})
.

(2)

For any connected components
S,S^{\prime}\subseteq\crit(f)
and any
p\in S
, the descending manifold
\mathscr{D}(p)
intersects the ascending manifold
\mathscr{A}(S^{\prime})
transversely.

(3)

Each connected component
S\subseteq\crit(f)
, as well as its negative normal bundle
\mathcal{N}^{-}(S)
, is orientable.

As pointed out by Banyaga–Hurtubise
[
4
, Lemma 3.6]
, Condition
(2)
already implies Condition
(1)
.

If we restrict attention to
stable

G
-Morse-Bott functions, then Condition
(2)
holds for a generic
G
-invariant metric by Theorem
1.8
.
In contrast, without the
G
-Morse-Bott and stability assumption, Condition
(2)
generally fails.
Indeed, if we do not restrict to
G
-Morse-Bott functions, Example
3.3
violates Condition
(2)
for every
G
-invariant metric.
Even within the class of
G
-Morse-Bott functions, Example
5.2
shows that Condition
(2)
may still fail for all
G
-invariant metrics.

In the presence of Lie group symmetries, the critical components
S
are homogeneous spaces, which may themselves be non-orientable (for instance
\mathbb{RP}^{2}=SO(3)/O(2)
).
Nevertheless, we will see that orientability of the critical submanifolds
S
is not required for our construction.
Moreover, the following lemma shows that the stability condition in Definition
1.4
guarantees the orientability of the negative normal bundles appearing in Condition
(3)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group acting on
M
.
If
f
is a stable
G
-Morse-Bott function, then for every critical orbit
S
, the negative normal bundle
\mathcal{N}^{-}(S)
with respect to any
G
-invariant Riemannian metric is a trivial bundle.

Proof.

Choose any
p\in S
and let
H=\text{stab}(p)
. The induced representation of
H
on the normal space
N_{p}
at
p
decomposes as the sum of the trivial part
N_{p}^{\prime}
and the non-trivial part
N_{p}^{\prime\prime}
. By Lemma
2.1
, the stability assumption of
f
implies that the negative eigenspace
N_{p}^{-}
of
\text{Hess}_{f}(p)
viewed as a self-adjoint operator for any
G
-invariant metric is contained in
N_{p}^{\prime}
. This means
N_{p}^{-}
is fixed by
H
. It follows that the negative normal bundle
\mathcal{N}^{-}(S)
can be identified with

G\times_{H}N_{p}^{-}\cong G/H\times N_{p}^{-}\cong S\times N_{p}^{-},

which is a trivial bundle over
S
.
∎

For any two connected components
S,S^{\prime}\subseteq\crit(f)
, let
\mathcal{M}(S,S^{\prime})
denote the moduli space of unparametrised negative gradient flow lines from
S
to
S^{\prime}
, defined by

\mathcal{M}(S,S^{\prime})=(\mathscr{D}(S)\cap\mathscr{A}(S^{\prime}))/\mathbb{R},

where
\mathbb{R}
acts by translation along the flow direction.
If the pair
(f,g)
is weakly Morse-Bott-Smale, then
\mathcal{M}(S,S^{\prime})
is a smooth manifold of dimension

\ind(S)-\ind(S^{\prime})+\dim S-1.

Similarly, for a critical point
p\in S
and a critical submanifold
S^{\prime}\subseteq\crit(f)
, we define the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(p,S^{\prime})
of unparametrised negative gradient flow lines from
p
to
S^{\prime}
by

\mathcal{M}(p,S^{\prime})=(\mathscr{D}(p)\cap\mathscr{A}(S^{\prime}))/\mathbb{R},

where again
\mathbb{R}
acts by translation in the flow direction.
When the pair
(f,g)
is Morse-Bott-Smale,
\mathcal{M}(p,S^{\prime})
is a smooth manifold of dimension

\ind(p)-\ind(S^{\prime})-1.

By Lemma
4.2
, we can fix orientations of the negative normal bundles
\mathcal{N}^{-}(S)
for all connected components
S\subseteq\crit(f)
, so that the orientations are
G
-invariant.
For each
p\in S
, the identification

T_{p}\mathscr{D}(p)\cong\mathcal{N}^{-}(S)_{p}

induces an orientation on the descending manifold
\mathscr{D}(p)
at
p
.
The moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}(p,S^{\prime})
are oriented as follows.
For any
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{M}(p,S^{\prime})
, the linearized flow along
\gamma
induces an isomorphism of vector spaces

T_{p}\mathscr{D}(p)\cong T_{[\gamma]}\mathcal{M}(p,S^{\prime})\oplus\mathbb{R}\langle\partial_{s}\rangle\oplus T_{q}\mathscr{D}(q),

where
q=\gamma(+\infty)\in S^{\prime}
and
s
denotes the flow parameter.
We orient
T_{[\gamma]}\mathcal{M}(p,S^{\prime})
so that this isomorphism is orientation-preserving, using the chosen
G
-invariant orientations of
\mathcal{N}^{-}(S)
and
\mathcal{N}^{-}(S^{\prime})
to orient
T_{p}\mathscr{D}(p)
and
T_{q}\mathscr{D}(q)
respectively. Note that the orientations assigned in this way are also
G
-invariant: for any
g\in G
, the orientation on
T_{[\gamma]}\mathcal{M}(p,S^{\prime})
coincides with the orientation on
T_{[g\cdot\gamma]}\mathcal{M}(g\cdot p,S^{\prime})
.

4.2.
Cochain complex for ordinary cohomology

We briefly recall the construction of ordinary cohomology under the Lie group symmetry using equivariant Morse-Bott theory. We refer to
[
15
]
for more details.
Let
f
be a stable
G
-Morse-Bott function on a closed
G
-manifold
M
, and let
g
be a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric such that the pair
(f,g)
satisfies the Morse-Bott-Smale condition.
For each
i
, let
S_{i}
denote the union of components of
\crit(f)
with index
i
.

The Morse-Bott cochain complex
(C,\partial)
is defined by

C^{p}=\bigoplus_{i+j=p}\Omega^{j}(S_{i})^{G},

where
\Omega^{j}(S_{i})^{G}
denotes the space of smooth
G
-invariant differential
j
-forms on
S_{i}
.

For a homogeneous element
\omega\in\Omega^{j}(S_{i})^{G}
, the differential is defined as a sum

\partial=\sum_{r\geq 0}\partial_{r},

with components specified as follows.

•

For
r>0
,

\partial_{r}\omega:=(-1)^{j}(e_{-}^{\,i+r,i})_{*}(e_{+}^{\,i+r,i})^{*}\omega\in\Omega^{j-r+1}(S_{i+r})^{G}.

Here

\displaystyle e_{+}^{\,i+r,i}:\mathcal{M}(S_{i+r},S_{i})\to S_{i},

\displaystyle e_{-}^{\,i+r,i}:\mathcal{M}(S_{i+r},S_{i})\to S_{i+r},

denote the endpoint evaluation maps on the moduli space of gradient flow lines.
The operator
(e_{+}^{\,i+r,i})^{*}
denotes the pullback, while
(e_{-}^{\,i+r,i})_{*}
denotes pushforward, which can be interpreted as integration along the fiber since the Morse-Bott-Smale condition guarantees that
e_{-}^{\,i+r,i}
has the structure of a locally trivial fiber bundle.
The sign and degree shift reflect the dimension of the fibers, and the pushforward uses the orientations fixed on the moduli spaces in the previous subsection.

•

For
r=0
,

\partial_{0}\omega:=d\omega\in\Omega^{j+1}(S_{i})^{G}.

Lemma 4.3
.

[
15
, Proposition 2.3]

\partial^{2}=0
.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
15
, Theorem 1.4]

The cohomology of
(C,\partial)
is isomorphic to the de Rham cohomology
H^{*}(M;\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 4.5
.

In
[
15
]
, an additional
complication in defining the cochain complex arises from considering
G
-invariant differential forms valued in
the orientation bundle of
\mathscr{D}(S_{i})
over
S_{i}
. Under our stability assumption, this issue
is avoided. Indeed, the locally trivial fiber bundle
e_{-}^{\,i+r,i}\colon\mathcal{M}(S_{i+r},S_{i})\to S_{i+r}

is fiberwise orientable, since the orientations on
\mathcal{M}(p,S_{i})
are
G
-invariant for
each
p\in S_{i+r}
. Consequently, integration along the fiber is well-defined
using ordinary
G
-invariant differential forms.

4.3.
Cochain complex for equivariant cohomology

We keep the same setup and notation as in the previous subsection.
Following Austin-Braam
[
2
]
, we define the Morse-theoretic Cartan model
(C_{G},\partial_{G})
by

C_{G}^{p}=\bigoplus_{i+j+2k=p}\bigl(\Omega^{j}(S_{i})\otimes S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\bigr)^{G}.

Here
S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
denotes the
k
th symmetric power of the dual Lie algebra, and the superscript
G
denotes the invariant subspace.
The group
G
acts on
\Omega^{j}(S_{i})
by pullback and on
S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
via the coadjoint action.

For a homogeneous element
\omega\otimes\phi\in\bigl(\Omega^{j}(S_{i})\otimes S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\bigr)^{G}
, the differential is defined by

\partial_{G}=\sum_{r\geq 0}(\partial_{G})_{r},

where the components are given as follows.

•

For
r>0
,

(\partial_{G})_{r}(\omega\otimes\phi):=(-1)^{j}(e_{-}^{\,i+r,i})_{*}\bigl((e_{+}^{\,i+r,i})^{*}\omega\bigr)\otimes\phi\in\bigl(\Omega^{j-r+1}(S_{i+r})\otimes S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\bigr)^{G}.

•

For
r=0
,

\displaystyle(\partial_{G})_{0}(\omega\otimes\phi)

\displaystyle:=d\omega\otimes\phi-\sum_{\alpha}\iota_{\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}}\omega\otimes(\theta^{\alpha}\cdot\phi)

\displaystyle\in\bigl(\Omega^{j+1}(S_{i})\otimes S^{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\bigr)^{G}\oplus\bigl(\Omega^{j-1}(S_{i})\otimes S^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\bigr)^{G},

where
\{X_{\alpha}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\{\theta^{\alpha}\}
is the dual basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,
\widetilde{X}_{\alpha}
denotes the fundamental vector field induced by
X_{\alpha}
, and
\theta^{\alpha}\cdot\phi
denotes symmetric multiplication.

Lemma 4.6

(
[
2
]
Theorem 5.1)
.

\partial_{G}^{2}=0
.

Theorem 4.7

(
[
2
]
Theorem 5.3)
.

The cohomology of
(C_{G},\partial_{G})
is isomorphic to the Borel equivariant cohomology
H_{G}^{*}(M;\mathbb{R})
as an
S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}
-module.

5.
Examples

We begin with a simple example in which stabilization is not necessarily required, but nevertheless
yields the same computational result.

Example 5.1

(
S^{2}
with
S^{1}
-rotation)
.

Let

. We consider the standard

Let

is an

with indices

and

[

Let

,

, and

, and

is generated by

and

when

, and is zero
otherwise. For degree reasons, the differential

On the other hand, we can modify the function

We still use the notations

\partial_{G}
is given by

\partial_{G}(n_{0}^{\prime}\otimes\theta^{k})=0,\,\partial_{G}(n_{1}^{\prime}\otimes\theta^{k-1})=-n_{0}^{\prime}\otimes\theta^{k},\,\partial_{G}(n\otimes\theta^{k})=\partial_{G}(s\otimes\theta^{k})=n_{0}^{\prime}\otimes\theta^{k}.

Note that the cohomology of both cochain complexes associated to
f
and its stabilization
f^{\prime}
computes the well-known equivariant cohomology groups

H_{S^{1}}^{n}(S^{2};\mathbb{R})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{R},&n=0,\\
\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R},&n\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Example 5.2

(Mapping torus)
.

Consider the mapping torus

M=T^{2}\times[0,1]/{\sim},

where
T^{2}=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{2}
and the identification is given by

(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},0)\sim(-\theta_{1},\theta_{2},1).

Note that
M
can be alternatively described as the product between
S^{1}
and Klein bottle. Let
G=S^{1}
act on
M
via the flow generated by the vector field
\partial_{\tau}
, where
\tau
denotes the coordinate on the
[0,1]
-factor.

Define an
S^{1}
-invariant function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\tau)=(3+\cos 2\pi\theta_{1})\sin 2\pi\theta_{2}.

Then
f
is
G
-Morse-Bott with four critical orbits:

•

one index-
2
submanifold

P_{2}=\bigl\{(0,\tfrac{1}{4},\tau)\mid\tau\in[0,1]\bigr\},

•

two index-
1
submanifolds

Q_{1}=\bigl\{(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{4},\tau)\mid\tau\in[0,1]\bigr\},\qquad R_{1}=\bigl\{(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{3}{4},\tau)\mid\tau\in[0,1]\bigr\},

•

one index-
0
submanifold

S_{0}=\bigl\{(0,\tfrac{3}{4},\tau)\mid\tau\in[0,1]\bigr\}.

The function
f
is not stable, since
P_{2}
and
R_{1}
fail to be stable critical submanifolds. Moreover, the descending manifolds of
P_{2}
and
R_{1}
are non-orientable.

In fact, there exists no
G
-invariant metric
g
for which the pair
(f,g)
satisfies the Morse-Bott-Smale condition. Indeed, for any
p\in Q_{1}
, the virtual dimension

\operatorname{virdim}\mathcal{M}(p,R_{1})=-1,\qquad\mathcal{M}(p,R_{1})\neq\emptyset.

To justify the latter statement, observe that
Q_{1}
is stable and the stabilizer satisfies
\stab(p)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Let
K
denote the connected component of
M^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
containing
p
. Then
K
is diffeomorphic to a Klein bottle and contains
R_{1}
. Since
Q_{1}
is stable, the descending manifold
\mathscr{D}(p)
is contained in
K
by Claim
3.7
. Consequently, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(p,R_{1})
of flow lines from
p
to
R_{1}
in
M
coincides with the corresponding moduli space in
K
, computed using the restricted function
f|_{K}
and metric
g|_{K}
. The function
f|_{K}
has only two critical submanifolds: the index-
1
submanifold
Q_{1}
and the index-
0
submanifold
R_{1}
. Therefore, any Morse flow line starting at
p
must terminate at
R_{1}
, proving that
\mathcal{M}(p,R_{1})
is nonempty.

In fact, for any
G
-invariant metric
g
, the pair
(f,g)
even fails to satisfy the weak Morse-Bott-Smale condition using a similar argument. We leave it to the reader to verify that.

By applying stabilization, we modify
f
to a stable
G
-Morse-Bott function
f^{\prime}
. Under this process, the original critical submanifold
P_{2}
gives rise to a stable critical submanifold
\bar{P}_{1}
of index
1
, together with a new stable critical submanifold
P_{2}^{\prime}
of index
2
located near
\bar{P}_{1}
and intersecting each fiber over
\tau
in two points. Similarly, the original critical submanifold
R_{1}
becomes a stable critical submanifold
\bar{R}_{0}
of index
0
, and we introduce a new stable critical submanifold
R_{1}^{\prime}
of index
1
near
\bar{R}_{0}
, again intersecting each fiber over
\tau
in two points. An illustration is provided in Figure

Introduction

Lung cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignant tumors worldwide and the leading cause of cancer-related deaths, with the lowest 5-year survival rate. It accounts for approximately 20% of all cancer-related deaths globally [1, 2], imposing an enormous global health burden. In countries worldwide, particularly in industrialized nations, the incidence and mortality rates of lung cancer are rapidly increasing. Among male patients, lung cancer has emerged as the leading cause of cancer-related deaths, while its incidence among women has been steadily rising in recent years [3]. Despite advancements in therapeutic modalities such as targeted therapies and immunotherapies, overall survival rates, particularly in advanced-stage patients, remain unsatisfactory. This underscores the urgent need for a deeper understanding of the complex molecular mechanisms driving lung cancer pathogenesis, progression, and treatment resistance, with the goal of identifying novel prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

The transcription factor nuclear factor E2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) is a member of the CNC (Cap-nCollar) family of basic leucine zipper transcriptional activators. These transcriptional activators are widely distributed across various organisms and play a critical role in regulating gene expression in response to environmental changes. Structurally, Nrf2 is characterized by a highly conserved basic leucine zipper (bZIP) domain and six functional structural (Neh1 ~ Neh6), which collectively determine its biological functions [4]. The primary role of Nrf2 is to regulate the expression of anti-oxidative stress and detoxification enzymes, including glutathione S-transferases (GSTs), superoxide dismutases (SODs), and glutathione peroxidases (GPx). These enzymes help protect cells from oxidative damage [5], highlighting Nrf2’s importance in cellular defense mechanisms.

Within the context of cancer biology, the role of Nrf2 is highly complex and often paradoxical. Sustained activation of Nrf2, driven by various mechanisms including somatic mutations in KEAP1 and NFE2L2 genes, epigenetic modifications, or oncogenic signaling pathways, has emerged as a hallmark feature of numerous malignant tumors, particularly non-small cell lung cancer [6]. While Nrf2 activation is typically intended to serve as a cytoprotective mechanism, its persistent hyperactivation in neoplastic cells confers a significant survival advantage through multiple pathways. Specifically, it enhances detoxification of chemotherapeutic agents, promotes metabolic reprogramming to sustain tumor proliferation, and fosters the development of an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment [7]. Consequently, Nrf2 is now understood to play an integral role in tumor initiation, progression, metastasis, and notably, the development of therapeutic resistance to conventional chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and targeted therapies.

Over the past two decades, there has been an exponential growth in research exploring the multifaceted role of Nrf2 in lung cancer. Motivated by this diversity of inquiry, this study employs bibliometric analysis to systematically map and quantify the scientific literature on Nrf2 in lung cancer research. We aim to assess the development of Nrf2 research in lung cancer over the past decades by using co-citation network analysis to pinpoint key studies and emerging trends. We also plan to map the research landscape through quantifying contributions from countries, institutions, authors, and journals. Ultimately, this analysis is designed to identify current knowledge gaps and future research opportunities, offering valuable insights to advance pulmonary disease research and inform policy development.

Methods

Database and search strategy

The Web of Science (WoS) is renowned for its high-quality citation data and stringent journal selection criteria, which ensure the reliability of its robust citation tracking capabilities. These features enable WoSCC to capture the impact of scholarly literature and trace trajectory of knowledge dissemination effectively. Furthermore, Scopus boasts a broad coverage of literature across disciplines, coupled with sophisticated citation analysis tools, making it particularly well-suited for interdisciplinary research. The synergistic combination of these two databases offers a more comprehensive and precise bibliometric analysis, thereby enabling researchers to gain deeper insights into the research dynamics and emerging trends within academic fields.

To acquire bibliometric data, search terms were employed in the WoSCC and Scopus databases on July 04, 2025. A preliminary search was subsequently performed based on the publication’s title, abstract, and keywords. For the selection of relevant terms, we reviewed a number of previous publications in the literature.Nrf2-related terms= (“NF E2 Related Factor 2”OR “Nuclear Factor E2 Related Factor 2”OR “Nuclear Factor Erythroid 2-like 2”OR “NF Erythroid 2-related factor 2”OR “Nuclear Factor (Erythroid-Derived 2)-like 2”OR “Nrf2” OR “nfe2l2” NOT “nuclear respiratory factor 2”). And lung cancer-related terms=(“lung cancer*” OR “lung carcinoma*” OR “lung neoplasm*” OR “lung tumor*”). Finally, the two combined datasets yielded 798 documents from WoSCC and 2,137 documents from Scopus (Fig. 1).

The flowchart for literature search, selection and analysis

Non-research materials, including proceedings, corrections, news items, book chapters, retracted publications, and editorials, were excluded from consideration. We retained exclusively English-language articles and reviews, which yielded a total of 1931 studies for analysis, comprising 683 studies from WoSCC and 1248 studies from Scopus.

Data processing

CiteSpace

The data analysis was conducted using CiteSpace 6. 4. R1 (64-bit) Advanced [8] software. The temporal scope of the study spanned from January 2002 to July 2025, utilizing an annual time slice for segmentation. The analysis incorporated three primary node categories: authors, institutions, and keywords. Specifically, for author and institutional nodes, the threshold was set to the top 100 per slice without applying pruning techniques. In contrast, keyword nodes were subjected to a threshold of 100, incorporating pruning techniques such asfinder and merged network methods. The visual analytical process yielded knowledge maps representing researchers, institutions, and keywords. All retrieved records from WoSCC were exported as plain text files containing “full records and cited references.”

VOSviewer

The data analysis was conducted utilizing VOSviewer 1. 6. 20, a software tool developed by the Centre for Science and Technology Studies (CWTS) at Leiden University [9] The full counting methodology was employed, with thresholds established based on the analytical parameters to facilitate the visualization of collaborative network structures.

Bibliometrix

The bibliometric analysis was conducted using the bibliometrix R package (https://www.bibliometrix.org), created by Dr. Massimo Aria and Corrado Cuccurullo [10]. This tool was employed for historiographic analysis, monitoring trends in journals and authors, and computing metrics including the g-index [11], h-index [12], number of citations (NC), and publications (NP) [10].

The other tools

The data analysis process was conducted using a combination of tools. Microsoft Excel 2021 (Version 16.48) was employed for the initial organization and management of the dataset. Subsequently, Python 3. 13. 5 (64-bit) was utilized to perform statistical analyses and to visualize annual publication trends through plotting functionalities. Additionally, some of the country cooperation network maps were generated using the Charticulator website, which provided the necessary visualization capabilities for this aspect of the research.

Discussion

Nrf2, a transcription factor, plays a pivotal role in cellular defense mechanisms against oxidative stress and inflammation, both of which are significant contributors to lung cancer development. A bibliometric analysis of research literature reveals emerging trends patterns in the investigation of Nrf2’s role across various cancer types, underscoring its potential as a therapeutic target and biomarker, particularly in lung cancer. This analysis not only emphasizes the critical role of Nrf2 in lung cancer but also provides insights into its broader implications across other malignancies, thereby guiding future research directions and clinical applications.

A bibliometric analysis of microRNA as biomarkers in lung adenocarcinoma highlighted a focus on early detection and diagnosis, indicating a growing interest in understanding the molecular underpinnings of this cancer type [17]. Furthermore, studies have shown that Nrf2 activity is linked to ferroptosis resistance in lung adenocarcinoma cells, particularly those with KEAP1 mutations, suggesting that targeting Nrf2 could enhance therapeutic strategies against this cancer [18]. Additionally, the role of interleukin-11 in promoting tumorigenesis in lung adenocarcinoma underscores the complexity of the disease and the need for targeted therapies [19]. Overall, the bibliometric analysis reflects a significant focus on lung cancer research, particularly in relation to Nrf2 and its implications across various cancer types, emphasizing the necessity for continued exploration of these interactions to improve patient outcomes.

The interaction between Nrf2 and lung cancer is multifaceted, involving various cellular processes such as apoptosis, autophagy, and ferroptosis. Nrf2, a key regulator of oxidative stress response, plays a significant role in modulating apoptosis in cancer cells. Its activation is known to suppress apoptosis, thereby promoting cell survival and contributing to therapy resistance in cancer, including lung cancer [20]. This protective role of Nrf2 against apoptosis is further supported by studies showing that its activation reduces apoptosis through pathways like the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling, which alleviates oxidative stress [21]. In the context of lung cancer, apoptosis is a critical mechanism for controlling cancer progression, and agents that can modulate this process, such as those affecting Nrf2 pathways, are of significant therapeutic interest [22]. In addition to apoptosis, Nrf2 is also involved in regulating autophagy and ferroptosis, both of which are crucial in the context of lung cancer. Nrf2 activation has been shown to enhance autophagy, which can contribute to cancer cell survival under stress conditions, thereby influencing lung cancer progression and treatment resistance [23]. Conversely, Nrf2 can also modulate ferroptosis, a form of regulated cell death distinct from apoptosis, by influencing the expression of ferroptosis-related proteins. This regulation can either promote or inhibit ferroptosis, depending on the context, thereby affecting lung cancer cell survival and response to therapies [24]. The interplay between Nrf2 and these cellular processes highlights its potential as a target for therapeutic strategies aimed at modulating cell death pathways in lung cancer.

Recent studies have shown that Nrf2 can regulate the expression of various RNAs, including long non-coding RNAs and microRNAs, that influence tumor progression and response to therapy in lung cancer. The interaction between Nrf2 and lung cancer involves the regulation of RNAs, particularly in the context of antioxidant responses and immune modulation. Nrf2 activation is associated with the upregulation of antioxidant genes such as HO-1 and NQO-1, which are crucial for cellular defense mechanisms against oxidative stress [25]. In lung cancer, specific RNAs related to ion channels and membrane lipid metabolism are significantly altered, highlighting their role in tumorigenesis and potential as therapeutic targets [26]. Additionally, the modulation of miRNAs, such as miR-26b-5p, in NSCLC is linked to radioresistance, suggesting that targeting these RNAs could enhance radiosensitivity and improve immune responses, thereby influencing survival outcomes [27].

Epigenetic modifications, including DNA methylation and histone alterations, play a significant role in regulating the expression of Nrf2 and its downstream antioxidant genes, thereby influencing lung cancer progression and therapeutic response. The interaction between Nrf2 signaling and epigenetic mechanisms reveals a complex regulatory network in which epigenetic changes can either activate or suppress Nrf2 activity, subsequently affecting cancer pathogenesis. Recent research further demonstrates that Nrf2 itself can directly modulate epigenetic regulators; for instance, it transcriptionally controls the expression of histone deacetylases (HDACs), DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs), and key proteins involved in microRNA biogenesis, thereby exerting broad epigenetic influence [28]. Additionally, epigenetic regulators such as HDAC3 have been shown to suppress Nrf2, altering cellular responses to oxidative stress and increasing susceptibility to ferroptosis in cancer cells [29]. Lung tumors can also enhance chromatin accessibility at the NFE2L2 locus, contributing to an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment [30]. This bidirectional crosstalk between NFE2L2 and epigenetic regulation highlights promising therapeutic targets for improving outcomes in lung cancer patients [31]. Nrf2, a critical regulator of cellular defense mechanisms, is frequently constitutively activated in lung cancer due to mutations in the KEAP1 gene. This persistent activation drives the expression of genes that protect against oxidative stress and external insults, thereby enhancing cancer cell survival and proliferation [32]. In the context of lung cancer, oxidative stress plays a pivotal role in disease progression and treatment resistance. For example, elevated oxidative stress resulting from environmental insults, such as cigarette smoke and pollutants, is associated with inflammation and DNA damage, both of which contribute lung cancer development [33, 34]. Furthermore, oxidative stress is closely linked to chemoresistance in NSCLC, as evidenced by the use of compounds like RTA-408, which target oxidative stress pathways to overcome drug resistance [35]. Nrf2 mitigates oxidative stress by activating antioxidant response elements (AREs), thereby protecting cells from oxidative damage and promoting cancer cell survival [36]. However, the sustained activation of Nrf2 in cancer cells can also induce genomic instability and increase cancer susceptibility, underscoring its dual role in cancer biology [20].

The relationship between metabolism and aging is complex, with metabolic processes playing a crucial role in the aging process and age-related diseases. Aging is a primary risk factor for lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), with aging-associated gene alterations showing greater targeting patterns in LUAD patients compared to healthier individuals, suggesting an accelerated aging profile that impacts survival outcomes [37]. Furthermore, the Nrf2 pathway is involved in the metabolic regulation of aging, as evidenced by its role in the longevity extension effects of all-trans retinoic acid (atRA) in “Caenorhabditis elegans” [38]. Nrf2 also mediates the geroprotective effects of malvidin-3-galactoside (M3G) on aging and stress resistance in “Drosophila melanogaster” [39]. Importantly, Nrf2 itself—a pleiotropic transcription factor regulating over 250 genes involved in cellular homeostasis—declines with age. This reduction is closely linked to key hallmarks of aging such as genomic instability, impaired proteostasis, and cellular senescence, highlighting its potential role in mediating metabolic aging processes and their impact on pathologies such as lung cancer [40].In terms of therapy and treatment, the integration of Nrf2-targeted strategies with existing lung cancer therapies holds promise. For instance, combining immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) with radiotherapy has shown significant improvements in progression-free survival and overall survival in lung cancer patients, without increasing adverse events [41]. This suggests that modulating oxidative stress pathways, potentially through Nrf2, could enhance the efficacy of such combination therapies. Furthermore, novel formulations like nasal NDB-invasomes have been developed to improve the bioavailability and anticancer efficacy of drugs like nintedanib against NSCLC, indicating a potential role for Nrf2 in optimizing drug delivery and therapeutic effectiveness [42]. Overall, while Nrf2 presents challenges due to its role in therapy resistance, it also offers opportunities for developing more effective lung cancer treatments by integrating targeted modulation of this pathway with conventional and emerging therapeutic strategies.

This study, based on a large, high-quality body of literature, employs co-word analysis, clustering, and visualization techniques to map the macro-level development of Nrf2 research in lung cancer, and providing an integrated assessment of publication output, institutional distribution, and collaboration networks; However, it relies on a limited set of international databases and English-language journals—potentially overlooking preprints and Chinese publications—does not evaluate individual study quality or methodological rigor, uses a fixed cutoff date that may miss emerging trends, and would benefit from tighter integration between macro bibliometric insights and detailed molecular mechanisms or clinical translation supported by multi-omics validation.

In conclusion, our analysis provides valuable insights into the evolution of Nrf2 research in respiratory diseases, emphasizing the importance of a continued focus on core themes such as oxidative stress response, programmed cell death, and metabolic senescence, while calling for the development of novel Nrf2 modulation therapies and multi-omics strategies to accelerate clinical translation. The interactions between the Nrf2 signaling pathway, epigenetic regulators, and immune checkpoints are extremely complex, and further studies are urgently needed to uncover the precise mechanisms of these interactions and their impact on disease progression and treatment efficacy. From a clinical perspective, translating these bibliometric insights into practice requires more integrative studies that link molecular mechanisms to patient outcomes. Future research should not only explore experimental validation of Nrf2-related targets but also investigate how emerging technologies such as machine learning and multi-omics integration can guide personalized therapeutic strategies.

Conclusion

Our analysis of Nrf2 in lung cancer over the past 20 years shows significant progress from basic research on tumor typing and cell survival to later studies of oxidative stress, programmed cell death, and biomarkers. Future work should focus on dissecting upstream regulators and non-coding RNA networks, developing selective Nrf2 modulators for combination therapy, and integrating multi-omics data for personalized, mechanism-driven clinical strategies.

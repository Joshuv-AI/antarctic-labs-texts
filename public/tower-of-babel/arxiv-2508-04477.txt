Nature of field-induced transitions and hysteretic magnetoresistance in non-collinear antiferromagnet EuIn 2 As 2
Karan Singh, Jan Skolimowski, Giuseppe Cuono, Raghottam M. Sattigeri, Andrzej Ptok, Orest Pavlosiuk, Tetiana Romanova, Tomasz Toliński, Piotr Wiśniewski, Carmine Autieri, Dariusz Kaczorowski

Abstract
We examine the magnetic and electrical transport properties of the hexagonal EuIn 2 As 2 compound, combining experimental and theoretical results. This compound is predicted to be an axion-insulator from an electronic point of view and an altermagnet while in the collinear magnetic phase. However, experiments indicate that the Fermi level lies within the valence band rather than in the topological gap, potentially leading to the dominance of magnetic properties. Our detailed studies on magnetization and electrical transport support the presence of a broken-helix antiferromagnetic state, which was previously identified by X-ray and neutron diffraction experiments. Notably, we observed within that state a field-induced metamagnetic transition marked by a large hysteresis in magnetoresistance, which turns into a sharp upturn for the magnetic field tilted by 15 ∘ from the c -axis of the crystal. Combined with theoretical calculations, it is explained that the application of a magnetic field changes the low-resistivity antiferromagnetic domain walls to the high-resistivity domain walls due to the reduction in the Fermi surface sheets interaction area in the domain walls, originating from p -orbitals of As. EuIn 2 As 2 , therefore, presents a new case study that broadens the understanding of complex magnetic structures and their influence on electrical transport.

In recent years, considerable attention has been drawn to the ternary europium-based antiferromagnet EuIn
2
As

The compound exhibits two subsequent antiferromagnetic (AFM) transitions at

;

;

ab

Below

;

;

In this state, the magnitude of magnetic moments varies sinusoidally along the

In turn, below

60^{\circ}<\delta\phi<180^{\circ}

;
Soh et al. 2023

Takeda et al. 2024

The trigonal crystal structure of EuIn

2

The structure of the domain wall (DW) is not yet known, but it is understood that it may also exhibit a similarly complex spin arrangement, which could strongly affect the transport properties determined mainly by the bulk magnetic structure, as discussed, e.g., for TbB
4

Sunku et al. 2016

The recent resonant elastic X-ray scattering (REXS) studies corroborated the B-helix AFM transition that occurs at

The electrical transport measurements indicated the metallic-like nature of EuIn
2

2
with a small concentration of hole-type carriers

2
As
2
crosses the valence band and the spin-split bands contribute to the electrical transport. In such a case, the axion insulating phase may not manifest itself through a quantized Hall resistivity, in line with the experimental findings for EuIn
2
As
2

In addition, recent theoretical studies demonstrated that the A-type AFM structure of EuIn
2
As
2
is altermagnetic

, the A-type AFM structure is part of the magnetic order observed in this compound. Including the complex magnetism will modify some band structure features, but the g-wave altermagnetic spin-slitting will persist

2
As
2

In this paper, we report the results of our comprehensive magnetization and magnetotransport measurements performed on high-quality single crystals of EuIn
2
As
2
. The primary aim of this study is to understand its complex magnetic behavior, combined with the theoretical calculations. The obtained data support the B-helix AFM ground state scenario, yet also revealed a previously unnoticed magnetic field-induced transition below

The novel feature manifests itself as a distinct hysteresis in the transverse magnetoresistance isotherms and can be attributed to the transformation from low-resistive to high-resistive DW states that occurs due to the reduction of the Fermi surface sheets’ intersection area in the DWs, as indicated by our electronic band calculation in EuIn

As

Crystal growth:
Single crystals of EuIn

Magnetic and transport measurements:
Magnetization measurements were performed on an oriented single crystal using a SQUID magnetometer (MPMS-XL, Quantum Design). Electrical transport measurements were performed, using PPMS, on a rectangular-shaped sample cut from an oriented single crystal using a wire saw, with 50-

\rho_{xx}(B)

\rho_{xx}^{\mathrm{up}}({B})=\frac{\rho_{xx}^{\mathrm{up}}({B})+\rho_{xx}^{\mathrm{dn}}({-B})}{2};\quad\quad\rho_{xx}^{\mathrm{dn}}({B})=\frac{\rho_{xx}^{\mathrm{dn}}({B})+\rho_{xx}^{\mathrm{up}}({-B})}{2};

Where

\rho_{xx}^{\mathrm{up}}({B})

\rho_{xx}^{\mathrm{dn}}({B})

Density functional theory:
We performed density functional theory-based

For the system EuIn

The kinetic energy cut-off of 65 Ry and charge density cut-off of 780 Ry was used with a uniform Monkhorst-Pack grid (

To avoid numerical inaccuracy in the wannierization without SOC and magnetization, we performed the wannierization of the

IV. HYSTERETIC MAGNETORESISTANCE

We determined the magnetoresistance (MR=
[\rho_{xx}(B)/\rho_{xx}(0)]-1
) for
\mathbf{B}\perp\textit{c}
, at various temperatures. Results are shown in Fig.
3

Notably, at temperatures below 10 K MR measured for the swept-up-field is clearly larger compared to the swept-down-field MR in two ranges of field: around 0.15 T and around 0.35 T. Fig.
3

Nevertheless, a key observation is that at low temperatures and for
B>B^{\mathrm{up}}_{3}
(i.e. in the fan-type state) the MR exhibits significant hysteresis, even though this is not reflected in the magnetization (see Figs.
2

The hysteretic MR (Hys-MR) could arise from the complex magnetic structures in the DWs
Sunku et al. 2016
or from the competitive nature of the ground state at the boundary of the collinear phase and helical phase within the domains, like in EuCuSb and EuZnGe

The latter conjecture is ruled out for EuIn
2

Thus, we argue that Hys-MR in EuIn
2
As
2
emerges due to DWs’ contribution to the scattering of charge carriers. Furthermore, DWs can be tuned between high and low resistivity, depending on the carrier concentration, and the strength of interaction of conduction electrons with the local magnetic moments
Suzuki et al. 2019
or the spin configuration in the magnetic structure
Ueda et al. 2015
;
Ueda et al. 2014
.

VII. DISCUSSION

Domain walls can significantly influence charge transport in materials with complex magnetic and electronic structures. According to the theory given in the literature
Suzuki et al. 2019
, the contribution of scattering on DWs to the resistivity is inversely proportional to
A_{\mathrm{int}}
, where
A_{\mathrm{int}}
is the intersection area shared by the Fermi surface sheets on the DWs. The Landauer-Büttiker approach indicates that electrons can only transmit over the DWs if their transverse momentum is preserved at the Fermi level, which requires that the sheets of the Fermi surface from two neighboring domains have the intersection of finite area (
A_{\mathrm{int}}
)
Nguyen et al. 2006
;
Kobayashi et al. 2018
.
That intersection can be controlled by carrier concentration, via changing the size of the Fermi surface sheets on either side of DWs. The lower carrier concentration indicates the presence of a smaller sheet of the Fermi surface, which results in a decrease of
A_{\mathrm{int}}
in the DWs and in increased electron scattering. So far, domain wall scattering phenomena have only been explored for Weyl or nodal semimetals because they have the nodes at the Fermi level associated with the low carrier concentrations of the order of 10
20
cm
-3

Kobayashi et al. 2018
;
Suzuki et al. 2019
. Here, we expanded this phenomenon to the non-collinear magnet EuIn
2
As
2
, with a carrier concentration of the order of 10
19
cm
-3
, about 10-fold lower than in magnetic Weyl semimetal CeAlGe
Suzuki et al. 2019
. In EuIn
2
As
2
, the local 4
f
-level is distant from the Fermi level. Theoretical calculations show that the
p
-orbital of the As atom contributes to states at the Fermi level and that electronic bands are spin-split due to altermagnetism and the induced moment on
p
-orbital by Eu-4
f
-moments (see Fig. S11
SM
). As a result, this spin-splitting modifies the size and geometry of the sheets of the Fermi surface (see Supplementary Fig. S13
SM
). Thus, it could also reduce the intersection area (
A_{\mathrm{int}}
) in the DWs around
B^{\mathrm{up}}_{2}
(or
B^{\mathrm{dn}}_{2}
), and consequently, increase the carrier scattering.

To gain deeper insight into the role of domain walls and electronic structure, we examine the Hall resistivity below and above
B_{\mathrm{sat}}
. 1) For
B<B_{sat}
: The system is in the B-helix state and
the
\Delta\rho^{A}_{yx}
arises from the combination of non-collinear Eu-spin configurations inside the domains and DWs. Indeed, in a collinear AFM with zero bulk spin-chirality, the Hall contribution of DWs is considerable, as demonstrated for EuZn
2
Sb
2

Singh et al. 2024
. However, in EuIn
2
As
2
, spin chirality induced by the bulk non-collinear magnetic structure could result in maxima in
\Delta\rho^{A}_{yx}

Yan et al. 2022
.
The absence of hysteresis in the
\Delta\rho^{A}_{yx}
suggests that electron scattering arises from the non-collinear magnetic structure within the domains and dominates over the DWs-contributed Hall resistivity, as consistent with the isotherm magnetization showing no magnetic hysteresis (see Fig. S2b
SM
).
As explained above, in this compound, the DWs are responsible for the hysteresis in the MR, which in the
\Delta\rho_{yx}
is hidden behind the huge contribution from the bulk magnetic structure. This finding lends support to the concept that DWs are the main origin of the Hys-MR.
2) For
B>B_{sat}
: In the polarized ferromagnetic state, we observed pure
\sigma^{A}_{yx}
, which is small and changes sign from positive to negative as the magnetic field is tilted toward the
ab
plane. This behavior is explained by theoretical modeling.
For fixed
\omega
, we attribute AHC’s sign change to the skew-scattering in AHC. In a polarized FM system, AHC is a sum of the intrinsic, skew-scattering, and side-jump terms:

\sigma^{A}_{yx}=\sigma^{int}_{yx}+\sigma^{skew}_{yx}+\sigma^{side}_{yx}.

(4)

The intrinsic and skew-scattering AHCs, denoted by
\sigma^{int}_{xy}
and
\sigma^{skew}_{yx}
, can have the same or opposite sign, while the side-jump contribution
\sigma^{side}_{yx}
is assumed to be negligible.
\sigma^{int}_{yx}
equals the theoretically determined AHC.
\sigma^{skew}_{yx}
is linearly dependent on
\sigma_{xx}
, as experimentally observed (Fig.
5
c). Experimental results indicate that both have the opposite sign, in which
\sigma^{int}_{yx}
induces a positive value. The changing of the field orientation towards the
ab
basal plane could enhance the skew-scattering caused by the interaction of
S_{z}
component of As with the
f
-orbitals of Eu or by spin chirality
Ishizuka and Nagaosa 2018
. This could induce a negative sign to the AHC and dominate over the intrinsic AHC. Thus, the resultant AHC becomes negative. The AHC calculation based on the scenario of altermagnetic spin splitting is in good agreement with the experimental AHC, supporting the present picture of the complex magnetism physics in this compound. Our theoretical results demonstrated that the intrinsic AHC is small and positive, in agreement with the experimental results. This relatively small value of the AHC is due to the moderate SOC value of the
p
-As states. Therefore, the dominant contribution to the anomalous Hall conductivity producing that sign change is of extrinsic origin. Hence, combined experimental and theoretical studies reveal that the hysteresis observed in magnetoresistance arises from domain walls coupled to the
p
-orbitals of As.

VIII. CONCLUSIONS

Combining theoretical and experimental results and taking into account altermagnetic and topological properties, we fully describe the complex magnetism of EuIn
2
As
2
and its associated properties.
We described the metamagnetic transition associated with a large hysteresis in magnetoresistance, which turns into a sharp upturn for the magnetic field tilted by 15
∘
from the
c
-axis of the crystal. Our findings explain the origin of strong MR scattering associated with the hysteresis that arises from DWs. The application of a magnetic field changes the low-resistivity antiferromagnetic domain walls to the high-resistivity domain walls due to the reduction in the Fermi surface sheets interaction area in the domain walls, originating from
p
-orbitals of As.

On the baroclinic instability of inviscid non-conducting Boussinesq equations with rotation in 3-D
Jingjing Mao and Yan-Lin Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the nonlinear instability of a given vertical shear of velocity between two rigid plane for the 3-D inviscid, non-conducting Boussinesq equations with rotation. When the Rossby number is zero, this rotating inviscid Boussinesq system reduces to the nonlinear geostrophic limit model. For non-zero small Rossby numbers, we establish the nonlinear instability of the shear flow, which is consistent with that of the geostrophic limit model. The proof relies on constructing a precise approximate solution, which comprises a growing profile derived from the nonlinear geostrophic limit model and a higher-order asymptotic expansion with respect to the small Rossby number. Notice that the instabilities (growing modes) are driven by the physical boundaries. Keywords: Rotating Boussinesq equations; Shear flow; Nonlinear instability; Baroclinic instability. AMS Subject Classification: 35Q35; 76F10



1.
Introduction

We consider the three-dimensional inviscid non-conducting Boussinesq system with reference rotation, which is used to describe the large-scale dynamical progress, such as atmospheric flows and oceanic flows. The governing system reads

(1.1)

\begin{cases}Ro\left(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u+v\partial_{y}u+Row\partial_{z}u\right)-v=-\partial_{x}p,\\
Ro\left(\partial_{t}v+u\partial_{x}v+v\partial_{y}v+Row\partial_{z}v\right)+u=-\partial_{y}p,\\
Ro^{2}H^{2}\left(\partial_{t}w+u\partial_{x}w+v\partial_{y}w+Row\partial_{z}w\right)=-\partial_{z}p+\theta,\\
\partial_{t}\theta+u\partial_{x}\theta+v\partial_{y}\theta+Row\partial_{z}\theta+Bw=0,\\
\partial_{x}u+\partial_{y}v+Ro\partial_{z}w=0,\end{cases}

where
(t,x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,1],
with horizontal periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{2}:=(0,1)^{2}.

(u,v,w)
,
p
and
\theta
stand for velocity, pressure and temperature of the fluid.
Here
H
,
Ro
and
B
are, respectively, the height scale of the flow, Rossby number and Burger number. For example, for the westerlies in the atmosphere, it is found that
Ro\ll 1
and
B\approx 1
.
For the readers’ convenience, we present the derivation of (
1.1
) in the Appendix, in which the definition of
Ro
and
B
will be given. For more background and its fundamental importance of the rotating Boussinesq equations in both geophysics and mathematics, we refer to
[
23
,
30
,
40
,
41
]
and references therein.

The system (
1.1
) is supplemented with the boundary conditions:

(1.2)

\displaystyle w=0,\ \ \ {\rm at}\ \ \ z=0,1.

We take a basic shear flow of the system (
1.1
) as follows:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}=(z,0,0),\quad\Theta=-y,\quad P=-yz,

where
(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
0\leq z\leq 1.
Note that this setup of zonal flow confined between two rigid horizontal plane, together with the boundary condition (
1.2
), is due to the famous Eady model in atmospheric science (see,
[
24
]
). The shear flow (
1.3
) is taken from the physical consideration, which is consistent with the pressure distribution of westerlies. The instability of the shear flow (
1.3
) is called
baroclinic instability
in meteorology, because it depends essentially upon the difference between the surfaces of constant density and of constant pressure in the fluid ( see
[
23
]
for the physical mechanism, for instance).

Formally, we take the geostrophic limit
Ro\rightarrow 0
in (
1.1
) to have

(1.4)

\displaystyle u=-\partial_{y}p,\quad v=\partial_{x}p,\quad\theta=\partial_{z}p,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\partial_{y}u-\partial_{x}v)+u\partial_{x}(\partial_{y}u-\partial_{x}v)+v\partial_{y}(\partial_{y}u-\partial_{x}v)+\partial_{z}w=0,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta+u\partial_{x}\theta+v\partial_{y}\theta+Bw=0,

which is called the
geostrophic limit model
in the rest of this paper.
Note that the basic shear flow (
1.3
) also satisfies the geostrophic limit model. Actually, the equations (
1.4
)-(
1.6
) can be rewritten as a nonlinear equation of

:

(1.7)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\partial_{y}p\partial_{x}+\partial_{x}p\partial_{y})[(B(\partial_{xx}+\partial_{yy})+\partial_{zz})p]=0.

That is, when
Ro\rightarrow 0
, the system (
1.1

formally.

1.1.
Motivation and the problem

The baroclinic instability theory was date from the work of Charney
[
15
]
in 1947. Since then extensive progresses have been made. In 1949, Eady
[
24
]
first revealed the physical mechanism of (linear) baroclinic instability. Eady showed that the two sets of Rossby waves cor­responding to both boundaries can give rise to exponential instability.

The instability of the shear flow (
1.3
) in Boussinesq system
is an important topic in both atmospheric science and mathematics.
When
Ro=0,
the linear (in)stability of shear flow (
1.3
) has been investigated in
[
24
,
23
]
.
However, if
Ro
is small but nonzero, the system (
1.1
) can not be written into a single equation of
p
. In this situation, the nonlinear instability of the shear flow (
1.3
) is interesting and complicated in mathematical analysis.
In this paper, our purpose is to study the nonlinear instability of the shear flow (
1.3
) in the inviscid non-conducting Boussinesq equations with a small Rossby number
Ro>0.

To approach this purpose, we set

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{u}^{*},\quad\theta=\Theta+\theta^{*},\quad p=P+p^{*},

where
\mathbf{u}=(u,v,w)
and
\mathbf{u}^{*}=(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*})
. Then we write the system (
1.1
) into a perturbed form:

(1.8)

\begin{cases}Ro\left(\partial_{t}u^{*}+z\partial_{x}u^{*}+u^{*}\partial_{x}u^{*}+v^{*}\partial_{y}u^{*}+Row^{*}\partial_{z}u^{*}+Row^{*}\right)-v^{*}\\
=-\partial_{x}p^{*},\\
Ro\left(\partial_{t}v^{*}+z\partial_{x}v^{*}+u^{*}\partial_{x}v^{*}+v^{*}\partial_{y}v^{*}+Row^{*}\partial_{z}v^{*}\right)+u^{*}=-\partial_{y}p^{*},\\
Ro^{2}H^{2}\left(\partial_{t}w^{*}+z\partial_{x}w^{*}+u^{*}\partial_{x}w^{*}+v^{*}\partial_{y}w^{*}+Row^{*}\partial_{z}w^{*}\right)\\
=-\partial_{z}p^{*}+\theta^{*},\\
\partial_{t}\theta^{*}+z\partial_{x}\theta^{*}+u^{*}\partial_{x}\theta^{*}+v^{*}\partial_{y}\theta^{*}+Row^{*}\partial_{z}\theta^{*}-v^{*}+Bw^{*}=0,\\
\partial_{x}u^{*}+\partial_{y}v^{*}+Ro\partial_{z}w^{*}=0,\end{cases}

which is complemented with the following boundary conditions

(1.9)

\displaystyle w^{*}=0\quad\text{ at }z=0,1.

Suppose that a small perturbation is given to the shear flow initially. If there exists a time such that this perturbation grows larger than the scale of initial perturbation and away from the shear flow, we say the shear flow is unstable. In this paper we will give a rigorous verification on the nonlinear instability of the shear flow in the inviscid non-conducting Boussinesq equations with rotation. We will first verify the existence of a growing mode in the nonlinear geostrophic limit model. Then we construct an approximate solution for the case
Ro>0
containing the shear flow and a growing profile deduced from the geostrophic limit model. Then the nonlinear instability of the shear flow in inviscid non-conducting Boussinesq system can be established through an asymptotic estimates in Sobolev space.

Guo-Strauss’s method and Grenier’s method.
More precisely, one denotes the linearized system around the shear flow as follows

\partial_{t}\mathcal{U}+A\mathcal{U}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{(LE)}

where
A
is a linear operator. Guo-Strauss’s approach in
[
29
,
28
]
requires the estimates of generator
e^{tA}\leq C_{A}e^{\lambda t}
for some positive constant
C_{A}
. Moreover, the nonlinear estimates rely on the existence of the largest exponential growing mode. On the other hand, Grenier’s approach requires to evaluate the spectral radius of
A,
and then construct a more precise approximate solution
u_{app}=u_{shear}+\delta u_{1}e^{\lambda_{0}t}+\delta\bar{u_{1}}e^{\bar{\lambda}_{0}t}+u_{R},
where
u_{1}e^{\lambda_{0}t},\bar{u}_{1}e^{\bar{\lambda}_{0}t}
solves the linear equation (LE) and
u_{R}
is the higher order expansion. The instability is deduced from the growing profile and the smallness of the remainder of the approximate solutions.

The method of our proof.

In this paper we present a new approach to derive the nonlinear instability of the shear flow in inviscid non-conducting Boussinesq equations with fast rotation (small Rossby number). We will not search the growing modes from the linear system (LE) for
Ro\neq 0
. Instead, we verify the existence of a specific growing mode in the nonlinear geostrophic limit model (where
Ro=0
). Then we construct an approximate solution built upon the nonlinear geostrophic limit model. In our paper, the growing mode is not necessary to be the largest one.

To establish the nonlinear baroclinic instability for
Boussinesq system (
1.1
) with a small Rossby number, we start with the geostrophic limit model.
In particular, we suppose a perturbation in the perturbed system (
1.8
) with the normal modes:

(1.10)

p(x,y,z)=\hat{p}(z)\exp(\mathrm{i}\alpha(x-ct))\sin\beta y,\ \ \ 0\leq z\leq 1,

where
c\in\mathbb{C},
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
denote the wavenumbers in
x
and
y
direction. As we know, when
Ro=0,
the linearized system of (
1.8
) will reduce to an ODE of
\hat{p}
. Let
\Re(\cdot)
and
\Im(\cdot)
denote the real part and imaginary part of a complex number, respectively. Clearly, if
\Re(-i\alpha c)<0,
the shear flow is stable.
If
\Re(-i\alpha c)>0

(i.e.
\Im(c)\alpha>0
), there exists an exponentially growing mode such that the shear flow is unstable with respect to this perturbation. In fact, the exponential growth rate is deduced from the boundary conditions for certain Burger number and wavenumbers
\alpha,\beta.
(cf.
[
23
]
).
Notice that we will consider the nonlinear instability of the shear flow when
Ro>0.
In this situation the perturbed system (
1.8
) can not be treated as a single equation of
\hat{p}
again. Therefore, we construct an approximate solution to the perturbed system (
1.8
) with high order expansion in terms of
Ro
. The approximate solution near the shear flow is formed by the solution of nonlinear geostrophic limit model and the high order expansion in terms of
Ro
. So it is crucial to verify the existence of a growing profile in the nonlinear geostrophic limit model. Then combining the nonlinear instability of the shear flow in the nonlinear geostrophic limit model and the reasonable asymptotic expansion of the solution to the perturbed Boussinesq system, we will establish our nonlinear instability theory for the case
Ro\neq 0
.

Instability for the case

The linear instability of shear motion (

2.1.
Linear instability for
Ro=0

We begin with the linearized equations of (
1.8
):

(2.1)

\begin{cases}Ro\left(\partial_{t}u+z\partial_{x}u+Row\right)-v=-\partial_{x}p,\\
Ro\left(\partial_{t}v+z\partial_{x}v\right)+u=-\partial_{y}p,\\
Ro^{2}H^{2}\left(\partial_{t}w+z\partial_{x}w\right)=-\partial_{z}p+\theta,\\
\partial_{t}\theta+z\partial_{x}\theta-v+Bw=0,\\
\partial_{x}u+\partial_{y}v+Ro\partial_{z}w=0.\end{cases}

Elimination of
p
from equations
(\ref{linear})_{1}
and
(\ref{linear})_{2}
gives the vorticity equation,

Ro(\partial_{t}+z\partial_{x})(\partial_{x}v-\partial_{y}u)-Ro^{2}\partial_{y}w+\partial_{x}u+\partial_{y}v=0.

This, together with
\eqref{linear}_{5}
, implies that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\partial_{t}+z\partial_{x})(\partial_{x}v-\partial_{y}u)-Ro\partial_{y}w-\partial_{z}w=0,\end{split}

Let
Ro=0
. It follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) that

(2.3)

u=-\partial_{y}p,\quad v=\partial_{x}p,\quad\theta=\partial_{z}p,

and

(2.4)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\theta+z\partial_{x}\theta-v+Bw=0,\\
&(\partial_{t}+z\partial_{x})(\partial_{x}v-\partial_{y}u)-\partial_{z}w=0.\end{split}

With the aid of (
2.3
), we eliminate
w
and write (
2.4
) into the following form

(2.5)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+z\partial_{x})\big(B(\partial_{xx}p+\partial_{yy}p)+\partial_{zz}p\big)=0.

The boundary condition (

) gives

(2.6)

(\partial_{t}+z\partial_{x})\partial_{z}p-\partial_{x}p=0\quad\text{ at }z=0,1.

Take the normal modes with

p=\hat{p}(z)\exp(\mathrm{i}\alpha(x-ct))\sin\beta y,

where
c\in\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
.
Equation
(\ref{linear_p})
gives

\displaystyle(z-c)\left(\partial_{zz}\hat{p}-B(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})\hat{p}\right)=0.

From here, we can not directly obtain the growing modes (Namely,
\Re(c)>0
). Instead, for any

we take

\displaystyle\partial_{zz}\hat{p}-B(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})\hat{p}=0.

Hence one can solve

(2.7)

\displaystyle\hat{p}=R\cosh 2qz+S\sinh 2qz,

where

q=\frac{1}{2}(B(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}))^{1/2},

and

are some constants. From the boundary conditions (

), we have

\displaystyle c\partial_{z}\hat{p}(0)+\hat{p}(0)=0\ \ \ {\rm at}\ \ z=0,

\displaystyle c\partial_{z}\hat{p}(1)-\partial_{z}\hat{p}(1)+\hat{p}(1)=0\ \ \ {\rm at}\ \ z=1.

Submitting (
2.7
) into the above boundary conditions, one can eliminate

and

\displaystyle c=\frac{1}{2}\pm(q^{2}+1-2q\coth(2q))^{1/2}/(2q).

Hence it holds

if and only if

where

is the positive root of

That is, it has an exponential growing mode

when

Of course, the largest growing rate
\lambda_{\max}:=\max\{\alpha\Im(c)\}
can be obtained for a

3.6
)-(
3.10
) from (
3.11
)-(
3.15
). The proof of Lemma
3.1
is completed.
\square

3.2.
Estimates for the error terms

To begin with, we state a useful pressure estimate in the following
proposition, which can be deduced from
[
43
]
by R. Temam.

Proposition 3.1

(cf.
[
43
]
)
.

Let
m>5/2
and
\textbf{f}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,1])
be a vector function. If
P(x,y,z)
and
\textbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})(x,y,z)
satisfy that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P={\rm div}\textbf{f}-{\rm div}(\textbf{u}\cdot\nabla\textbf{u})\ \ \ {\rm in}\ \ \ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,1]\ {\rm or}\ \mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,1],

\displaystyle{\rm div}\textbf{u}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial P}{\partial z}=\textbf{f}\cdot\textbf{n}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm at}\ \ \ \ z=0,1,

where
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}:=\mathcal{H}^{2}\partial_{xx}^{2}+\mathcal{H}^{2}\partial_{yy}^{2}+\partial_{zz}
with positive constant
\mathcal{H},

\textbf{n}=(0,0,-1)
at
z=0
and
\textbf{n}=(0,0,1)
at
z=1,
respectively.
Then it holds

\displaystyle\|\nabla P\|_{H^{m}}\leq C\left(\|\textbf{f}\|_{H^{m}}+\|\textbf{u}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\right),

where
C
is a positive constant.

Throughout the rest parts, we will use
C_{a}
to denote a generic constant depending on

(u_{a},v_{a},w_{a})
,
p_{a}
and
\theta_{a}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
t>0,
we have

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{B}H(u^{\prime},v^{\prime},Row^{\prime})(\cdot,t)\|^{2}+\|\theta^{\prime}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{a}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{P}\left(\|(\sqrt{B}Hu^{\prime},\sqrt{B}Hv^{\prime},\sqrt{B}Row^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})(\cdot,t)\|^{2}\right)dt+\|\theta^{\prime}(\cdot,0)\|^{2}

(3.23)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\|\sqrt{B}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime},Row^{\prime})(\cdot,0)\|^{2}+C_{a}Ro^{6},

where
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
is a polynomial function. Hence it holds that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|(u^{\prime},v^{\prime},Row^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})(\cdot,t)\|^{2}\leq C_{a}(B,H)Ro^{6},\ \ \ 0<t<T^{\prime}

for a time
T^{\prime}\leq T_{a}.
Here
C_{a}(B,H)
represents a generic constant depending on
B
and
H,
additionally.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we set
H=1.

Multiplying (
3.6
)-(
3.9
) by
Bu^{\prime}
,
Bv^{\prime}
,
BRow^{\prime}
and
\theta^{\prime}
, respectively, and then we integrate the result with respect to space variable to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(B\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}+B\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}+B\|Row^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|\theta^{\prime}\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{Ro}\int\nabla p\cdot(Bu^{\prime},Bv^{\prime},BRow^{\prime})dxdxdz+K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3}+K_{4},

where

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=-B\int\big(u^{\prime}\partial_{x}u_{a}u^{\prime}+u_{a}\partial_{x}u^{\prime}u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}\partial_{y}u_{a}u^{\prime}+v_{a}\partial_{y}u^{\prime}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+Row^{\prime}\partial_{z}u_{a}u^{\prime}+Row_{a}\partial_{z}u^{\prime}u^{\prime}+Row^{\prime}u^{\prime}-Ro^{3}M_{1}u^{\prime}\big)dxdydz,

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=-B\int\big(u^{\prime}\partial_{x}v_{a}v^{\prime}+u_{a}\partial_{x}v^{\prime}v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}\partial_{y}v_{a}v^{\prime}+v_{a}\partial_{y}v^{\prime}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+Row^{\prime}\partial_{z}v_{a}v^{\prime}+Row_{a}\partial_{z}v^{\prime}v^{\prime}-Ro^{3}M_{2}v^{\prime})dxdydz,

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=-BRo\int\big(u^{\prime}\partial_{x}w_{a}Row^{\prime}+Rou_{a}\partial_{x}w^{\prime}Row^{\prime}+Rov^{\prime}\partial_{y}w_{a}Row^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+Rov_{a}\partial_{y}w^{\prime}Row^{\prime}+Ro^{2}w^{\prime}\partial_{z}w_{a}Row^{\prime}+Ro^{2}w_{a}\partial_{z}w^{\prime}Row^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad-Ro^{3}M_{3}w^{\prime}\big)dxdydz,

\displaystyle K_{4}

\displaystyle=-\int\big(u^{\prime}\partial_{x}\theta_{a}\theta^{\prime}+u_{a}\partial_{x}\theta^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}+v^{\prime}\partial_{y}\theta_{a}\theta^{\prime}+v_{a}\partial_{y}\theta^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B}|K_{1}|

\displaystyle\quad+\|v^{\prime}\|\|\partial_{y}u_{a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u^{\prime}\|+\|\partial_{y}v_{a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}

Similarly, it follows that

\frac{1}{B}|K_{2}|\leq C_{a}(\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|Row^{\prime}\|^{2}+Ro^{6}),

\frac{1}{B}|K_{3}|\leq C_{a}B(\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|Row^{\prime}\|^{2}+Ro^{6}),

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|\sqrt{B}u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{B}v^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{B}Row^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\theta^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We denote the initial perturbation of the shear flow as
(\textbf{u}^{*}_{0},\theta^{*}_{0}).

Let
\|(\textbf{u}^{*}_{0},\theta^{*}_{0})\|_{L^{2}}=\delta
for example. It follows from (
3.26
) that

\|(\textbf{u}^{*}-\textbf{u}_{N},\theta^{*}-\theta_{N})(t)\|\leq\sqrt{C(B,H)}Ro,

which implies

(3.27)

\displaystyle\|(\textbf{u}_{N},\theta_{N})\|-\sqrt{C(B,H)}Ro\leq\|(\textbf{u}^{*},\theta^{*})\|\leq\|(\textbf{u}_{N},\theta_{N})\|+\sqrt{C(B,H)}Ro.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\|(\textbf{u}_{N},\theta_{N})(t=0)\|_{L^{2}}=\|(\textbf{u}^{*}_{0},\theta^{*}_{0})\|_{L^{2}}.
If the instability condition
q<q_{c}
holds, it follows from Lemma 3.2 that the solution
(\textbf{u}_{N},\theta_{N})
generates a growing mode
e^{\alpha\Im(c)t}
for all
t\geq 0.
Then there exists a time

T^{\delta}:=\frac{1}{\alpha\Im(c)}\ln\frac{2\sqrt{C(B,H)}Ro+\eta}{\delta},\ \ \ {\rm for\ \ some}\ \ 0<\delta<\eta\leq 1,

such that

\displaystyle\|(\textbf{u}^{*},\theta^{*})(T^{\delta})\|\geq\|(\textbf{u}_{N},\theta_{N})(T^{\delta})\|-\sqrt{C(B,H)}Ro>\eta.

Next, we denote the maximal existence time of solutions
(\mathbf{u}^{*},\theta^{*})
as
T.
It follows from Proposition
3.1
and blow-up criteria for
(\textbf{u}^{*},\theta^{*})
with fixed

(cf.
[
3
]
) that: for any

\|(\mathbf{u}^{*},\theta^{*})(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\|(\mathbf{u}^{*},\theta^{*})(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{3}}\big(e^{\int_{0}^{T}[\|(\nabla\textbf{u}^{*},\nabla\theta^{*})(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}(1+1/Ro)]d\tau}\big)<+\infty.

Indeed, for

\displaystyle\|(\textbf{u}^{*},\theta^{*})(T^{\delta})\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\|(\mathbf{u}^{*},\theta^{*})(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{3}}\big(e^{(3C\sqrt{C(B,H)}Ro+\eta)T^{\delta}(1+1/Ro)}\big)<+\infty,

4.
Appendix

In this section, we first state the physical model of inviscid non-conducting Boussinesq system in a
frame of reference rotating with steady angular velocity
\Omega\mathbf{k}
about
the vertical direction. Then the derivation of system (
1.1
) will be given.
The original system reads

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t_{*}}\mathbf{u}_{*}+\mathbf{u}_{*}\cdot\nabla_{*}\mathbf{u}_{*}+2\Omega\mathbf{k}\times\mathbf{u}_{*}=-\rho_{0}^{-1}\nabla_{*}p_{*}+\gamma g(\theta_{*}-\theta_{0})\mathbf{k},\\
\partial_{t_{*}}\theta_{*}+\mathbf{u}_{*}\cdot\nabla_{*}\theta_{*}=0,\\
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{*}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{*}=0,\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{u}_{*}=(u_{*},v_{*},w_{*})
is the velocity relative to the rotating frame.
\theta_{0}
and
\rho_{0}
are, respectively, the reference temperature and density. Here
\gamma,g
are positive physical constants and
\rho_{*}=\rho_{0}(1-\gamma(\theta_{*}-\theta_{0}))
,
p_{*}
and
\theta_{*}
denote the density, the relative pressure and the temperature of the fluid, respectively. Here
\Omega
and
\mathbf{k}
represent, respectively, the reference rotation rate and the unit vector in the
z_{*}
-direction, i.e.,
\mathbf{k}=(0,0,1).

In this study, the space variable
(x_{*},y_{*},z_{*})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}\times[0,H]
and
x_{*},y_{*}
are period of length
L
. In the
z_{*}
direction we assume a non-penetration boundary condition:

\displaystyle w_{*}=0\quad\text{ at }z_{*}=0,\ H.

Suppose that the basic state is given by

(4.2)

\begin{cases}\mathbf{U}_{*}=VH^{-1}(z_{*},0,0),\\
\Theta_{*}=\theta_{0}+\bar{\theta}H^{-1}z_{*}-2\Omega V(\gamma gH)^{-1}y_{*},\\
P_{*}=\rho_{0}\big(\frac{1}{2}\gamma g\bar{\theta}H^{-1}z_{*}^{2}-2\Omega VH^{-1}y_{*}z_{*}\big),\end{cases}

where

(x_{*},y_{*})\in\mathbb{T}^{2},\ \ 0\leq z_{*}\leq H,

and
V
is a reference speed of the basic state and
\bar{\theta}
is a constant scale of basic vertical temperature difference.
The distribution of
\Theta_{*}
indicates the distribution of density
\rho_{*}
. The instability of the basic state (
4.2
) is called baroclinic instability in meteorology. It is called
baroclinic instability
because it depends essentially upon the difference between the surfaces of constant density
and of constant pressure in the fluid ( see Chapter 6 in
[
23
]
for the physical mechanism).

Then it is convenient to define the dimensionless variables

\begin{split}(x,y)&=(x_{*},y_{*})/L,\quad z=z_{*}/H,\quad t=Vt_{*}/L,\\
(u,v)&=(u_{*},v_{*})/V,\quad w=Lw_{*}/VHRo,\\
\theta&=\gamma gH\big(\theta_{*}-\theta_{0}-\bar{\theta}z_{*}/H\big)/2\Omega VL,\\
p&=\big(p_{*}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma\rho_{0}g\bar{\theta}H^{-1}z_{*}^{2}\big)/2\Omega VL\rho_{0},\end{split}

where the Rossby number, a characteristic ratio of the inertia to the Coriolis force, is given by

Ro=V/2\Omega L.

We will use
B
to denote Burger number:

B=\gamma gH\bar{\theta}/(4\Omega^{2}L^{2}).

Now the basic state (
4.2
) reduces to

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}=(z,0,0),\quad\Theta=-y,\quad P=-yz,

where
0\leq z\leq 1.

Finally, the system (
4.1
) can be rewritten as (
1.1
).

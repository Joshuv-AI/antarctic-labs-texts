Qualitative quasi-invariance of low regularity Gaussian measures for the 1D quintic nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Alexis Knezevitch

Abstract
. We consider the 1d quintic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) on the torus with initial data distributed according to the Gaussian measures with covariance operator ( 1 − Δ ) − s (1-\Delta)^{-s} , and denoted μ s \mu_{s} . For the full range s > 9 10 s>\frac{9}{10} , we prove that these Gaussian measures are quasi-invariant along the flow of (NLS), meaning that the law of the solution at any time is absolutely continuous with respect to the initial Gaussian measure. Moreover, the condition s > 9 10 s>\frac{9}{10} corresponds to the threshold where the Sobolev space H 2 5 + ​ ( 𝕋 ) H^{\frac{2}{5}+}(\mathbb{T}) is of μ s \mu_{s} -full measure (it is of zero μ s \mu_{s} -measure otherwise). This is the lower regularity Sobolev space where we currently know that (NLS) is globally well-posed, thanks to the work of LI-WU-XU in [ 26 ] . The present work is partially an extension of [ 23 ] : we extend the quasi-invariance from the range s > 3 2 s>\frac{3}{2} to the range s > 9 10 s>\frac{9}{10} , but we do not obtain here quantitative results on the Radon-Nikodym derivatives generated by the quasi-invariance. Our approach is based on the work of Sun-Tzvetkov in [ 36 ] , combining a Poincaré-Dulac normal form reduction with energy estimates. However, our main tool to obtain these energy estimates differs: we use the Boué-Dupuis variational formula instead of Wiener Chaos.



1.
Introduction

We consider the 1d quintic nonlinear equation on the torus
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
:

(NLS)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=|u|^{4}u,\hskip 14.22636pt(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})\end{cases}

with initial data lying in the Sobolev space (for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
):

H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}):=\{w:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\hskip 2.84544pt:\hskip 2.84544pt\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle n\rangle^{2\sigma}|\widehat{w}(n)|^{2}<+\infty\}

where
\widehat{w}
denotes the Fourier transform, and
\langle n\rangle:=(1+n^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
the japanese bracket.

Well-posedness for NLS :
Let us start by reviewing some known results on the well-posedness for this equation. When
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
, the algebra property of
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
allow us to prove the existence of local solution to (
NLS
) running a fixed point argument in the space
\mathcal{C}([-T,T],H^{\sigma})
, with
T>0
a small time depending on the
H^{\sigma}
-norm of the initial data. When
\sigma\leq\frac{1}{2}
, the situation is more involved, especially for
\sigma
small where it is not clear how to define the non-linearity
|u|^{4}u
for a general function
u\in\mathcal{C}([-T,T],H^{\sigma})
, even in the sense of distributions. In
[
4
]
, Bourgain introduced appropriate spaces for the solution to exist and, indeed, proved the local well-posedness of (
NLS
) for all
\sigma>0
using a fixed point argument with these new spaces. Applying this procedure, we recover the fact that the non-global solutions of (
NLS
) must have a
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
-norm that blow up in finite time. Hence, when
\sigma\geq 1
, we obtain global solutions thanks to the conservation of the mass and of the Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle M(u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{6}dx

because it lead to a control on the
H^{\sigma}
-norm of the solutions. For
0<\sigma<1
, this argument cease to work because the Hamiltonian may be infinite. However, in
[
6
]
, Bourgain combined the
I
-method with a normal form reduction to obtain the global well-posedness for all
\sigma>\sigma_{*}
for some
\sigma_{*}<\frac{1}{2}
; and this result was then extended by LI-WU-XU in
[
26
]
where the global-wellposedness is proven for all
\sigma>\frac{2}{5}
. Thus, for every
\sigma>\frac{2}{5}
, we are able to define the flow:

(1.1)

\begin{split}\Phi(t)\hskip 2.84544pt:\hskip 2.84544pt&H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})\longrightarrow H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})\\
&u_{0}\longmapsto\textnormal{the solution of~\eqref{NLS} at time $t$}\end{split}

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. To the knowledge of the author,
\sigma>\frac{2}{5}
is the current threshold for the global well-posedness of (
NLS
). It is however worth noting that, thanks to
[
34
]
, (
NLS
) is globally well-posed in
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
, for
\sigma>\frac{1}{3}
, under the condition that the
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
-norm of the initial data is small enough.

Random Gaussian initial data and statement of the result :

In this paper, the initial data are random (denoted now
\phi^{\omega}
instead of
u_{0}
) and given by the random Fourier series:

(1.2)

\phi^{\omega}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{g_{n}(\omega)}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx}

where
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
are independent complex standard Gaussian measures on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. The law of these random variables defined the Gaussian measures
\mu_{s}
:

\mu_{s}=\textnormal{law}(\phi^{\omega})=\phi^{\omega}_{\#}\mathbb{P}

where the symbol
\#
denotes the push-forward measure (
\phi^{\omega}_{\#}\mathbb{P}(A):=\mathbb{P}((\phi^{\omega})^{-1}(A))
).
More precisely, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, the series in (
1.2
) converges in the space
L^{2}(\Omega,H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))
if and only if
\sigma<s-\frac{1}{2}
, because:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\frac{g_{n}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega,H^{\sigma})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle n\rangle^{2(\sigma-s)}<+\infty\iff\sigma<s-\frac{1}{2}

We obtain that for
\sigma<s-\frac{1}{2}
, the random variable
\phi^{\omega}
is valued in
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
; and therefore
\mu_{s}
is a probability measure on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))
. It is known that almost-surely,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\phi^{\omega}

\displaystyle\in H^{s-\frac{1}{2}-}(\mathbb{T}):=\bigcap_{r<s-\frac{1}{2}}H^{r}(\mathbb{T})

and,

\displaystyle\phi^{\omega}

\displaystyle\notin H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})

Consequently, to apply the global flow in (
1.1
) to
\phi^{\omega}
, we need the condition
s-\frac{1}{2}>\frac{2}{5}
, that is
s>\frac{9}{10}
. We emphasize that throughout this paper, for
s>\frac{9}{10}
, we fix
\sigma<s-\frac{1}{2}
close to
s-\frac{1}{2}
(close enough to
s-\frac{1}{2}
so that we have in particular
\sigma>\frac{2}{5}
), and see
\mu_{s}
as a probability measure on
\mathcal{B}(H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))
. This choice of
\sigma
is not particularly relevant, as we could have defined
\mu_{s}
intrinsically on
H^{s-\frac{1}{2}-}(\mathbb{T})
.

The random initial data
\phi^{\omega}
generate solutions that are also random. Therefore, one can study the law of the solutions at any given time
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and compare it to the law of the initial data; in particular one may wonder if they are equal, absolutely continuous with respect to each other, or mutually singular. We can reformulate this in terms of transport of Gaussian measures by the flow. Indeed, the law of the solution at a time
t\in\mathbb{R}
is:

\textnormal{law}(\Phi(t)\phi^{\omega})=(\Phi(t)\circ\phi^{\omega})_{\#}\mathbb{P}=\Phi(t)_{\#}({\phi^{\omega}}_{\#}\mathbb{P})=\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}

and one can compare the transported measure
\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}
with the initial measure
\mu_{s}
.

The study of Hamiltonian PDEs with random initial data stems from physical motivation in the work of Lebowitz-Rose-Speer in
[
25
]
, and was then developed by Bourgain in
[
5
]
. In these works, the initial data are distributed according to Gibbs measures (denoted
Gb
here), defined formally as
Gb=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-H(u)}du
, where
H
is the Hamiltonian and
du
is formally the Lebesgue measure (which does not exist on infinite dimensional vector space). Formally, the conservation of the Hamiltonian and the invariance of the Lebesgue measure under an Hamiltonian flow (Liouville’s theorem) imply that:

\Phi(t)_{\#}Gb=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-H(\Phi(-t)u)}\Phi(t)_{\#}du=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-H(u)}du=Gb

which means that the Gibbs measure is invariant under the flow, or equivalently that the law of the random solutions (generated by initial data distributed according to the Gibbs measure) is
Gb
itself, at any time.
This result was rigorously proven by Bourgain in
[
5
]
for (
NLS
). Since then, the invariance of Gibbs measures has been studied for many models. These measures appears naturally as density measures with respect to suitable Gaussian measures. In
[
37
]
, Tzvetkov initiated a program on the study of Hamiltonian PDEs with initial data distributed according to Gaussian measures. Contrary to the Gibbs measure, we do not expect these measures to be invariant along the flow. Indeed, this is what the following formal computations suggest: formally, we have
\mu_{s}=\frac{1}{Z_{s}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}}du
, so that:

\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}=\frac{1}{Z_{s}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi(-t)u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}}\Phi(t)_{\#}du=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\|\Phi(-t)u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}-\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2})}\frac{1}{Z_{s}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}}du

that is,

(1.4)

\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\|\Phi(-t)u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}-\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2})}d\mu_{s}

Since in general the flow
\Phi(t)
does not preserve the
H^{s}
-norm, we expect
\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}
to be not equal but absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{s}
. If that is indeed the case for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we say that
\mu_{s}
is
quasi-invariant
along the flow. From the Radon-Nikodym theorem,
\mu_{s}
is quasi-invariant along the flow, if and only if, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and every
A\in\mathcal{B}(H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))
:

\mu_{s}(A)=0\implies\mu_{s}(\Phi(t)^{-1}(A))=0

Formula (
1.4
), despite being formal, is of interest. Practically, one can test this formula with other Hamiltonian PDEs. For example, considering the propagator
e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}
of linear Schrödinger equation:

(LS)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=0,\hskip 14.22636pt(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\end{cases}

we recover the fact that
(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}})_{\#}\mu_{s}=\mu_{s}
, since
e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}
preserves the
H^{s}
-norm. Moreover, for Hamiltonian PDEs that preserves the
L^{2}
-norm, we can expect the
white noise
, that is the measure
\mu_{0}
, to be invariant along the flow. Technically, this formula will be useful because it will hold true when replacing (
NLS
) with an approximated truncated equation.

Quasi-invariant results have been proven recently for many models, see
[
8
,
9
,

,
13
,

,

,

,

,
33
,
35
,
37
]
. In the case of (
NLS
), the first result on quasi-invariance emerged as a consequence of the invariance of the Gibbs measures (see again
[
5
]
). This measure being rigorously defined as:

Gb=e^{-\frac{1}{6}\|u\|^{6}_{L^{6}(\mathbb{T})}}d\mu_{1},

we obtain that

\Phi(t)_{\#}Gb=Gb\implies\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{1}=e^{-\frac{1}{6}(\|\Phi(-t)u\|^{6}_{L^{6}(\mathbb{T})}-\|u\|^{6}_{L^{6}(\mathbb{T})})}d\mu_{1}

which implies the quasi-invariance for
\mu_{1}
. Later on, Planchon-Tzvetkov-Visciglia proved in
[
32
]
the quasi-invariance of
\mu_{s}
for all
s=2k
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. This result was then extended to all
s>\frac{3}{2}
in
[
23
]
. As mention above, knowing that there is a global flow to (
NLS
) in
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
, for all
\sigma>\frac{2}{5}
, one can define the transported measure
\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}
for all
s>\frac{9}{10}
, and wonder if quasi-invariance holds for all
s>\frac{9}{10}
. In this paper, we provide a positive answer to this question:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
s>\frac{9}{10}
, the Gaussian measure
\mu_{s}
is quasi-invariant along the flow of (
NLS
). In other words, the push-forward measure
\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{s}
for every time
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Overview of the proof and comments :
Let us first remark that, writing as it is, the formal density in (
1.4
) is ill-defined on the support of
\mu_{s}
. Indeed, from (
1.3
), we have that
\mu_{s}(H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))=0
, so both
\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
and
\|\Phi(-t)u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
are infinite on the support of
\mu_{s}
. However, it is the difference between these two infinite terms that appears in (
1.4
), so we can still hope for some cancellations that might give meaning to this difference. To apply this approach, we first work with a truncated approximated version of (
NLS
):

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=\pi_{N}\left(|\pi_{N}u|^{4}\pi_{N}u\right),\hskip 5.69046pt(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\end{cases}

with its flow denoted
\Phi^{N}(t)
, for which we rigorously have:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}

\displaystyle=G_{s,N,t}\mu_{s}

with

\displaystyle G_{s,N,t}(u):=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\|\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}-\|\pi_{N}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2})}

The challenge then is to ”take the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
” into this equality, that is, deduce from this the quasi-invariance of
\mu_{s}
along the original flow
\Phi(t)
. First and foremost, we need to prove that
\Phi^{N}(t)
is indeed an approximation of
\Phi(t)
(in a suitable way). Thereafter, we can employ different approach which can lead to the quasi-invariance of
\mu_{s}
under
\Phi(t)
that we mention (informally):

– Find directly a limit to the
truncated densities

G_{s,N,t}
when
N\rightarrow\infty
. To do this, one can try to prove uniform (in
N\in\mathbb{N}
)
L^{p}(d\mu_{s})
bound for the
G_{s,N,t}
(with eventually an additional cut-off) which allow to extract from the
G_{s,N,t}
a sequence that weakly-converges to a function
G_{s,t}
(for such an example we refer to
[
10
]
). Other types of convergence can lead to the quasi-invariance; for example, in
[
23
]
, uniform convergence on compact sets (for continuous functions) was used.

– Prove a quantitative inequality of the type (for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
):

(1.6)

\mu_{s}(\Phi^{N}(t)A)\leq C_{s,t}\mu_{s}(A)^{1-\alpha}\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 2.84544pt\textnormal{which transfers to}\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 2.84544pt\mu_{s}(\Phi(t)A)\leq C_{s,t}\mu_{s}(A)^{1-\alpha}

with
A
a Borel set of
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
, which is possibly contained in a ball
B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}
of
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
(or even compact) in which case the constant
C_{s,t}
depends also on
R>0
(for an example of the application of this method we refer to
[
37
]
).

We also refer to the introduction of
[
17
]
where different methods to prove quasi-invariance are reviewed.

In this paper, we follow the second approach. In any case, for both method, we need to analyze the difference
\|\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}-\|\pi_{N}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
, which writes thanks to the fundamental theorem of calculus and the additivity of the flow:

(1.7)

\frac{1}{2}\|\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\pi_{N}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{-t}D_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(\tau))d\tau

with
D_{s,N}(v):=\frac{d}{dt}\big(\frac{1}{2}\|\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(t)v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\big)|_{t=0}
. In fact, we will work with a rewriting of this equality; the application of the Poincaré-Dulac normal reduction from
[
36
]
(which essentially relies on an integration by part in the formula above) leads to consider intermediate objects, a
modify energy
and a
weighted Gaussian measure
:

\displaystyle E_{s,N}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\pi_{N}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+R_{s,N}(u)

and

\displaystyle\rho_{s,N}=e^{-R_{s,N}(u)}d\mu_{s}

with
R_{s,N}
called the
energy correction
, which transform (
1.7
) and (
1.5
) into:

\displaystyle E_{s,N}(\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u)-E_{s,N}(\pi_{N}u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{-t}Q_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(\tau))d\tau

and

\displaystyle\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\rho_{s,N}=F_{s,N,t}d\rho_{s,N}

with
F_{s,N,t}(u):=e^{-(E_{s,N}(\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u)-E_{s,N}(\pi_{N}u))}
, and
Q_{s,N}(u):=\frac{d}{dt}E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)u)|_{t=0}
called
the modified energy derivative
. One may see the modified energies
E_{s,N}
and the weighted Gaussian measures
\rho_{s,N}
as analogs for every
s
of the standard energy and the Gibbs measure:

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{6}\|u\|_{L^{6}}^{6},

\displaystyle Gb

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{6}\|u\|_{L^{6}}^{6}}d\mu_{1}

where
\frac{1}{6}\|u\|_{L^{6}}^{6}
is seen as a correction term. The significant difference is that
E_{s,N}
is not conserved by the flow. Consequently, we do not hope for
\rho_{s,N}
to be invariant under the flow. However, the hope is that
E_{s,N}
is ”closer” to be conserved than the original energy
\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}
, and that we can recover the quasi-invariance of
\mu_{s}
from the quasi-invariance of the
\rho_{s,N}
(for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
), as we recover the quasi-invariance of
\mu_{1}
from the invariance of
Gb
. To transfer the quasi-invariance from the
\rho_{s,N}
to
\mu_{s}
, we will establish quantitative inequalities on the sets
\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)(A))
(see (
5.1
)) uniformly in
N
, following the analysis initiated in
[
37
]
. These inequalities will be obtained through
L^{p}
estimates on both
R_{s,N}
and
Q_{s,N}
.

We emphasize that normal form reductions has already been used in the context of quasi-invariance, see for example
[
30
,
28
,
27
,
17
]
.

Further remarks :
– In this paper, the main tool to establish the quantitative
L^{p}
estimates (for
R_{s,N}
and
Q_{s,N}
) is the Boué-Dupuis formula. One can try to obtain these through Wiener Chaos instead, as it was for example performed in
[
36
]
; however, it seems to the author that this method does not work (or is more complicated) in order to extend the quasi-invariance of
\mu_{s}
from
s>\frac{3}{2}
(see
[
23
]
) down to
s>\frac{9}{10}
, even though it could extend the quasi-invariance down to
s>s_{*}
for some
s_{*}\in(\frac{9}{10},\frac{3}{2})
.

– It would be interesting to find a sharp threshold for
s
above which we have quasi-invariance of
\mu_{s}
and under which singular results happen, typically that the transported measure
\Phi(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}
and
\mu_{s}
are mutually singular. Progress have been made in this direction in
[
9
]
in the case of the cubic Szegö equation. However, for (
NLS
), surpassing the threshold
s>\frac{9}{10}
requires the construction of a global flow in
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
for
\sigma\leq\frac{2}{5}
, either deterministically (for every initial data) or probabilistically (for
\mu_{s}
-almost every initial data). In the latter case, connections between quasi-invariance and well-posedness have been explored in
[
15
]
.

– In this paper, we obtain only the quasi-invariance of
\mu_{s}
for all
s>\frac{9}{10}
, without providing any additional quantitative results on the generated Radon-Nikodym derivatives, which may prove useful for other applications. Hence, this paper extends partially the result in
[
23
]
, where
L^{p}(d\mu_{s})
bounds for the Radon-Nikodym derivatives are obtained (for all
s>\frac{3}{2}
), relying on the conservation of the mass and the Hamiltonian. Note also that in the case
s\leq\frac{3}{2}
, we are not in a position to use the conservation of the Hamiltonian anymore.
For other quantitative results of this type, we refer to
[
10
,
14
,
17
,
33
,
20
]
.

Here, we introduce quantities that emerge in the Poincaré-Dulac normal reduction of Section

Definition 2.3

– For

– Similarly, for

Remark 2.4
.

For

\displaystyle\Omega^{\downarrow}(k_{1},...,k_{4})

\displaystyle=\Omega(k_{1},...,k_{4},k_{5},k_{5})

Classically, we have the factorization:

Lemma 2.5

(Factorization of the degenerated resonant function)
.

Let
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}
such that

\Omega^{\downarrow}(\vec{k})=2(k_{2}-k_{1})(k_{3}-k_{2})

For conciseness, it will be convenient later to consider additionally the following quantities:

Definition 2.6

\displaystyle\Psi_{2s}^{(1)}(\vec{k})

Similarly, we also consider the degenerated version of this quantities:

\displaystyle\Psi_{2s}^{(1),\downarrow}(\vec{k})

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
s>0
. For every
k_{1}-k_{2}+k_{3}-k_{4}+k_{5}-k_{6}=0
,

(2.1)

|\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})|\leq C_{s}\big(k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}(|\Omega(\vec{k})|+k_{(3)}^{2})+k_{(3)}^{2s}\big)

where the constant

only depends on

(2.2)

|\Psi^{(0)}_{2s}(\vec{k})|\leq C_{s}(k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}\vee k_{(3)}^{2(s-1)})k_{(3)}^{2}

and,

(2.3)

|\Psi^{(1)}_{2s}(\vec{k})|\leq C_{s}\big(k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}+(k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}\vee k_{(3)}^{2(s-1)})\frac{k_{(3)}^{2}}{|\Omega(\vec{k})|}\big)

where the symbol
\vee
denotes the max. Moreover, the analogous statements hold replacing
\psi_{2s}
,
\Psi^{(0)}_{2s}
,
\Psi^{(1)}_{2s}
, and
\Omega
by their degenerated version
\psi^{\downarrow}_{2s}
,
\Psi^{(0),\downarrow}_{2s}
,
\Psi^{(1),\downarrow}_{2s}
, and
\Omega^{\downarrow}
.

Proof.

We prove Lemma
2.7
only for the original functions
\psi_{2s}
,
\Psi^{(0)}_{2s}
,
\Psi^{(1)}_{2s}
, and
\Omega
, as the proof for the associated degenerated functions is identical. Moreover, we only establish (
2.1
), as (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) follow from it.

Thanks to the symmetries, we only need to consider the two following cases : when
k_{(1)}=k_{1}
,
k_{(2)}=k_{2}
and when
k_{(1)}=k_{1}
,
k_{(2)}=k_{3}
.

Case 1 :
When
k_{(1)}=k_{1}
and
k_{(2)}=k_{2}
. In this case we use the mean value theorem as follows:

|\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})|\leq k_{1}^{2s}-k_{2}^{2s}+|k_{3}^{2s}-k_{4}^{2s}+k_{5}^{2s}-k_{6}^{2s}|\leq\sup_{t\in[k_{2}^{2},k_{1}^{2}]}\frac{d}{dt}(t^{s})(k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2})+4k_{(3)}^{2s}\\
\leq s(k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}\vee k_{(2)}^{2(s-1)})(\Omega(\vec{k})-k_{3}^{2}+k_{4}^{2}-k_{5}^{2}+k_{6}^{2})+4k_{(3)}^{2s}\leq C_{s}\big(k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}(|\Omega(\vec{k})|+k_{(3)}^{2})+k_{(3)}^{2s}\big)

where we have used the fact that
|k_{(2)}|\sim|k_{(1)}|
from the constraint
k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0
.

Case 2
: When
k_{(1)}=k_{1}
and
k_{(2)}=k_{3}
. Let us consider two subcases. Firstly, if
|k_{(3)}|\sim|k_{(1)}|
, then we can simply write:

|\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})|\leq 6k_{(1)}^{2s}\leq C_{s}k_{(3)}^{2s}\leq C_{s}\big(k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}(|\Omega(\vec{k})|+k_{(3)}^{2})+k_{(3)}^{2s}\big)

Secondly, if
|k_{(3)}|\ll|k_{(1)}|
, then, since in addition
k_{(1)}=k_{1}
and
k_{(2)}=k_{3}
, we have:

\Omega(\vec{k})=k_{(1)}^{2}+k_{(2)}^{2}+o(k_{(1)}^{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}k_{(1)}^{2}

and it implies that

|\psi_{2s}(\vec{k})|\leq 6k_{(1)}^{2s}\leq 12k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}|\Omega(\vec{k})|\leq C_{s}\big(k_{(1)}^{2(s-1)}(|\Omega(\vec{k})|+k_{(3)}^{2})+k_{(3)}^{2s}\big)

This completes the proof of Lemma

3.
Poincaré-Dulac normal form reduction and energy estimates

3.1.
Truncated system

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we work with the following equation :

(3.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=\pi_{N}\left(|\pi_{N}u|^{4}\pi_{N}u\right),\hskip 5.69046pt(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\end{cases}

that we call the
truncated equation
, where we recall that
\pi_{N}
is the projector on frequencies
\leq N
. We also define
\pi_{N}^{\perp}:=\text{Id}-\pi_{N}
, the projector on frequencies
>N
. More explicitly,

\displaystyle\pi_{N}\big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{k}e^{ikx}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|k|\leq N}u_{k}e^{ikx}

and

\displaystyle\pi_{N}^{\perp}\big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{k}e^{ikx}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|k|>N}u_{k}e^{ikx}

We denote by
\Phi^{N}
the flow of (
3.1
), that we call the
truncated flow
. We also recall that we denote by
\Phi
the flow of (
NLS
). The truncated flow
\Phi^{N}
can be factorized:

Proposition 3.1

(Factorization of the truncated flow, see Appendix of
[
23
]
)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\sigma<s-\frac{1}{2}
. Set,

\displaystyle E_{N}

\displaystyle:=\pi_{N}H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}),

\displaystyle E_{N}^{\perp}:=\pi_{N}^{\perp}H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})=(\textnormal{Id}-\pi_{N})H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})

We identify
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
with the product space
E_{N}\times E_{N}^{\perp}
through the isomorphism:

\begin{split}E_{N}&\times E_{N}^{\perp}\longrightarrow H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})\\
(w&,v)\longmapsto w+v\end{split}

Then, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the truncated flow
\Phi^{N}(t)
can be factorized as
(\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t),e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}})
on
E_{N}\times E_{N}^{\perp}
in the sense that:

\begin{split}\Phi^{N}(t):\hskip 2.84544pt&E_{N}\times E_{N}^{\perp}\longrightarrow E_{N}\times E_{N}^{\perp}\\
&(w,v)\longmapsto(\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t)(w),e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}v)\end{split}

where
e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}
is the propagator of the linear Schrödinger equation (
LS
), and where
\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}
is the flow on
E_{N}
of the following finite dimensional ordinary differential equation:

(FNLS)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}w+\partial_{x}^{2}w=\pi_{N}\left(|w|^{4}w\right)\\
u|_{t=0}=w_{0}\in E_{N}\end{cases}

Moreover, the truncated flow
\Phi^{N}
is an approximation of the original
\Phi
in the following sense:

Proposition 3.2

(Approximation of the flow by the truncated flow)
.

Let
\sigma>\frac{2}{5}
and
K
a compact subset of
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
. Then,

– If
T>0
, we have that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N_{\varepsilon,K,T}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq N_{\varepsilon,T,K}
, and every
|t|\leq T

(3.2)

\Phi^{N}(t)(K)\subset\Phi(t)(K)+B_{\varepsilon}^{H^{\sigma}}

– If
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N_{\varepsilon,K,t}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq N_{\varepsilon,K,t}
:

(3.3)

\Phi(t)(K)\subset\Phi^{N}(t)(K+B_{\varepsilon}^{H^{\sigma}})

We provide a proof of this proposition in Appendix
A
.

Later, we will see that (for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
)
\mu_{s}
is quasi-invariant under

. More precisely, we will be able to give an explicit formula for the Radon-Nikodym derivative
\frac{d(\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s})}{d\mu_{s}}

3.3.

Here, and throughout this paper, we fix

close enough to
s-\frac{1}{2}
. To recall this, we might sometimes use the notation
\sigma=s-\frac{1}{2}-
. Moreover, for
R>0
, we denote
B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}
the closed centered ball of radius
R>0
in
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
s>\frac{9}{10}
. Then, for
m>2
large enough, we have that for every
R>0
, there exists a constant
C_{s,R}>0
, such that for every
\alpha\geq 1
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{s,N}\in\{\mathcal{M}_{s,N},\mathcal{T}_{s,N},\mathcal{N}_{s,N}\}
,

\log\int\mathds{1}_{B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}}(u)e^{\alpha|\mathcal{F}_{s,N}(u)|}d\mu_{s}\leq C_{s,R}\hskip 2.84544pt\alpha^{m}

As a consequence, we have:

(3.6)

\log\int\mathds{1}_{B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}}(u)e^{\alpha|R_{s,N}(u)|}d\mu_{s}+\log\int\mathds{1}_{B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}}(u)e^{\alpha|Q_{s,N}(u)|}d\mu_{s}\leq C_{s,R}\hskip 2.84544pt\alpha^{m}

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
s>\frac{9}{10}
. For
\beta\in(0,1)
close enough to 1, we have that for every
R>0
, there exists a constant
C_{s,R}>0
such that:

(3.7)

\|\mathds{1}_{B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}}R_{s,N}\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}+\|\mathds{1}_{B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}}Q_{s,N}\|_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}\leq C_{s,R}p^{\beta}

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

.

Remark 3.10
.

Here, we obtain (
3.7
) as a consequence of (
3.6
) (see below), and that last inequality will be established through the Boué-Dupuis formula. Analogous (to (
3.7
)) quantitative estimates were obtained through Wiener Chaos in
[
36
]

Proof of Proposition
3.9
assuming Proposition
3.8
.

Let
J_{s,N}\in\{R_{s,N},Q_{s,N}\}
and let us prove the estimate (
3.7
) for
J_{s,N}
. We denote
B_{R}:=B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}
. By Cavalieri’s principle,

\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}J_{s,N}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}^{p}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}\mu_{s}(\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}|J_{s,N}|>\lambda)d\lambda=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}\mu_{s}(\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}e^{\alpha|J_{s,N}|}>e^{\alpha\lambda})d\lambda

On the other hand, by the Markov inequality and Proposition
3.8
, we have for all
\alpha\geq 1
,

\mu_{s}(\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}e^{\alpha|J_{s,N}|}>e^{\alpha\lambda})\leq e^{-\alpha\lambda}\int\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}e^{\alpha|J_{s,N}|}d\mu_{s}\leq e^{C\alpha^{m}}e^{-\alpha\lambda}

with
C=C_{s,R}>0
a constant depending on
s
and
R
, and
m>2
large enough. Plugging this into the equality above yields:

\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}J_{s,N}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}^{p}\leq pe^{C\alpha^{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}e^{-\alpha\lambda}d\lambda

and by performing
\lfloor p\rfloor-1
integration by parts,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}e^{-\alpha\lambda}d\lambda&=\alpha^{1-\lfloor p\rfloor}(p-1)(p-2)...(p-\lfloor p\rfloor+1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-\lfloor p\rfloor}e^{-\alpha\lambda}d\lambda\\
&\leq\alpha^{1-\lfloor p\rfloor}p^{\lfloor p\rfloor-1}\int(1+\lambda)e^{-\lambda}d\lambda\end{split}

so, setting
C_{0}:=\int(1+\lambda)e^{-\lambda}d\lambda
, we deduce that:

\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}J_{s,N}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}^{p}\leq C_{0}e^{C\alpha^{m}}\alpha^{1-\lfloor p\rfloor}p^{\lfloor p\rfloor}\leq C_{0}p^{p}e^{C\alpha^{m}}\alpha^{2-p}

This inequality is true for all
\alpha\geq 1
. Then, optimizing in
\alpha
the (RHS), we find that for
\alpha=p^{\frac{1}{m}}
(which is indeed greater than
1
),

\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}J_{s,N}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}^{p}\leq C_{0}p^{p}e^{Cp}p^{\frac{2}{m}-\frac{p}{m}}

which implies

\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}J_{s,N}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(d\mu_{s})}\leq C_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}e^{C}p^{1-\frac{1}{m}}p^{\frac{2}{pm}}\leq Cp^{1-\frac{1}{m}}

where
C>0
has changed but still depends only on
s
and
R
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Transport of Gaussian measures by the truncated flow

In this section, we prove that
\mu_{s}
is quasi-invariant along the truncated flow, providing an explicit formula for the Radon-Nikodym derivative. Essentially, the finite-dimensional nature of (
3.1
) will allow us to make rigorous the formal computations that lead to (
1.4
) in the introduction. Even though the results of this section are known, see for example the seminal paper
[
37
]
, we provide the proofs that lead to (
4.5
) and (
4.7
) for completeness. Here, we fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma<s-\frac{1}{2}
. For any topological space
X
, we denote
\mathcal{B}(X)
the associated Borel
\sigma
-algebra. For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, recall that we denote:

\displaystyle E_{N}

\displaystyle:=\pi_{N}H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}),

\displaystyle E_{N}^{\perp}:=\pi_{N}^{\perp}H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})=(\textnormal{Id}-\pi_{N})H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})

Moreover, through the homeomorphism:

(4.1)

\begin{split}E_{N}&\times E_{N}^{\perp}\longrightarrow H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})\\
(w&,v)\longmapsto w+v\end{split}

we identify
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
with the product space
E_{N}\times E_{N}^{\perp}
. Therefore, we can write:

\mathcal{B}(H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))=\mathcal{B}(E_{N}\times E_{N}^{\perp})=\mathcal{B}(E_{N})\otimes\mathcal{B}(E_{N}^{\perp})

where the last equality follows from the fact that both
E_{N}
and
E_{N}^{\perp}
are separable topological spaces.

Furthermore, we consider on
E_{N}
the Lebesgue measure, denoted
\mathcal{L}^{2N+1}
, associated to the orthonormal basis
(e^{inx})_{|n|\leq N}
. More precisely, we define
\mathcal{L}^{2N+1}
as the pushforward measure of the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}
through the map:

(4.2)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}\longrightarrow E_{N}\\
(u_{-N},&...,u_{N})\longmapsto\sum_{|n|\leq N}u_{n}e^{inx}\end{split}

Finally, we recall that the Gaussian measure
\mu_{s}
is obtained as the law of the random variable:

\begin{split}\phi:(\Omega,&\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})\longrightarrow(H^{\sigma},\mathcal{B}(H^{\sigma}))\\
&\omega\longmapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{g_{n}(\omega)}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx}\end{split}

where
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of independent standard complex Gaussian random variables on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, and where the sum converges in
L^{2}(\Omega,H^{\sigma})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We can decompose the Gaussian measure
\mu_{s}
as:

(4.3)

\mu_{s}=\mu_{s,N}\otimes\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}

where

\displaystyle\mu_{s,N}

\displaystyle=(\pi_{N})_{\#}\mu_{s},

and

\displaystyle\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=(\pi_{N}^{\perp})_{\#}\mu_{s}

Moreover, we have:

(4.4)

\mu_{s,N}=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}}d\mathcal{L}^{2N+1}

where
Z_{N}
is the normalizing constant given by
Z_{N}=\prod_{|n|\leq N}\frac{2\pi}{\langle n\rangle^{2s}}
.

Proof.

First, we prove (
4.3
). From the uniqueness of the product measure, it is sufficient to prove that for every
A\times B\in E_{N}\times E_{N}^{\perp}
, we have:

\mu_{s}(A\times B)=\mu_{s,N}(A)\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}(B)

On the other hand, from the independence of the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the Gaussian
(g_{n})_{|n|\leq N}
with the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the Gaussian
(g_{n})_{|n|>N}
, we have

\mu_{s}(A\times B)=\mathbb{P}(\phi\in A\times B)=\mathbb{P}(\{\pi_{N}\phi\in A\}\cap\{\pi_{N}^{\perp}\phi\in B\})=\mathbb{P}(\pi_{N}\phi\in A)\mathbb{P}(\pi_{N}^{\perp}\phi\in B)

Then, from the definition of a pushforward measure:

\mathbb{P}(\pi_{N}\phi\in A)=\mathbb{P}(\phi\in\pi_{N}^{-1}(A))=\mu_{s}(\pi_{N}^{-1}(A))=(\pi_{N})_{\#}\mu_{s}(A)=\mu_{s,N}(A)

and similarly,
\mathbb{P}(\pi_{N}^{\perp}\phi\in B)=(\pi_{N}^{\perp})_{\#}\mu_{s}(B)=\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}(B)
. This concludes the proof of (
4.3
).

We now proceed to the proof of (
4.4
). We denote
dz
and
d\vec{z}
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}
respectively. We recall that the law of the
g_{n}
is
\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}}dz
. Let
f
be a non-negative measurable function on
E_{N}
. Then, from the independence of the
g_{n}
, and through the identification (
4.2
), we have:

\int_{E_{N}}f(u)d\mu_{s,N}=\int_{\Omega}f(\sum_{|n|\leq N}\frac{g_{n}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx})d\mathbb{P}(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}}f(\frac{z_{-N}}{\langle-N\rangle^{s}},...,\frac{z_{N}}{\langle N\rangle^{s}})d\mathbb{P}_{(g_{-N},...,g_{N})}\\
=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}}f(\frac{z_{-N}}{\langle-N\rangle^{s}},...,\frac{z_{N}}{\langle N\rangle^{s}})\prod_{|n|\leq N}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z_{n}|^{2}}}{2\pi}dz_{-N}...dz_{N}\\
=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2N+1}}f(\vec{z})e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|n|\leq N}\langle n\rangle^{2s}|z_{n}|^{2}}d\vec{z}=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\int_{E_{N}}f(u)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}}d\mathcal{L}^{2N+1}

This equality, satisfied by all non-negative measurable function
f
on
E_{N}
, implies the desired formula (
4.4
).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space and
f:X\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
a non-negative measurable function. Let
Y
a set and
\chi:X\rightarrow Y
a bijection. Then,

\chi_{\#}(fd\mu)=(f\circ\chi^{-1})\chi_{\#}d\mu

where
Y
is equipped with the pushforward
\sigma
-algebra.

Proof.

Let
\psi
a non-negative measurable function on
Y
. Then,

\int_{Y}\psi(v)[(f\circ\chi^{-1})\chi_{\#}d\mu](v)=\int_{Y}\psi(v)f(\chi^{-1}(v))[\chi_{\#}d\mu](v)=\int_{X}\psi(\chi(u))f(u)d\mu(u)\\
=\int_{Y}\psi(v)[\chi_{\#}(fd\mu)](v)

This indeed means that
\chi_{\#}(fd\mu)=(f\circ\chi^{-1})\chi_{\#}d\mu
.
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Transport of Gaussian measures by the truncated flow)
.

Let
s>0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,

(4.5)

\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}=\exp(-(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}))d\mu_{s}

Moreover, if we set

(4.6)

\rho_{s,N}:=e^{-R_{s,N}(u)}d\mu_{s},

called the weighted Gaussian measure, we have:

(4.7)

\begin{split}\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\rho_{s,N}&=\exp(-(E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(-t)u)-E_{s,N}(u)))d\rho_{s,N}\\
&=\exp\big(-\int_{0}^{-t}Q_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(\tau)u)d\tau\big)d\rho_{s,N}\end{split}

Proof.

From the identification (
4.1
), the factorization of the truncated flow in Proposition
3.1
, and Lemma
4.1
, we can write:

(4.8)

\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}=(\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t),e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}})_{\#}(\mu_{s,N}\otimes\mu_{s,N}^{\perp})=\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s,N}\otimes(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}})_{\#}\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}

On the one hand, by Lemma
4.2
,

\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s,N}=\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t)_{\#}(\frac{1}{Z_{N}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}}d\mathcal{L}^{2N+1})=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(-t)u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}}d(\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t)_{\#}\mathcal{L}^{2N+1})

Since the equation (
3.1
) satisfied by
\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t)
is Hamiltonian (see for example the appendix of
[
23
]
), we have, by Liouville’s theorem,
\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t)_{\#}\mathcal{L}^{2N+1}=\mathcal{L}^{2N+1}
. Moreover, we have

\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(-t)=\pi_{N}\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(-t)=\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)
.
Thus, continuing the equality above, we obtain:

(4.9)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\Phi}^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s,N}&=e^{-(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2})}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}}}{Z_{N}}d\mathcal{L}^{2N+1}\\
&=e^{-(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2})}d\mu_{s,N}\end{split}

On the other hand, since
e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}
acts continuously on
L^{2}(\Omega,H^{\sigma})

e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\big(\sum_{|n|>N}\frac{g_{n}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx}\big)=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\big(\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{M\geq|n|>N}\frac{g_{n}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx}\big)=\sum_{|n|>N}\frac{e^{-itn^{2}}g_{n}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx}

where the limit takes place in the space
L^{2}(\Omega,H^{\sigma})
. Consequently, since
(e^{-itn^{2}}g_{n})
is still a sequence of independent standard complex Gaussian, we have:

(4.10)

(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}})_{\#}\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}=(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}})_{\#}\big(\sum_{|n|>N}\frac{g_{n}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx}\big)_{\#}\mathbb{P}=\big(\sum_{|n|>N}\frac{e^{-itn^{2}}g_{n}}{\langle n\rangle^{s}}e^{inx}\big)_{\#}\mathbb{P}=\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}

Now, combining (
4.8
), (
4.9
), and (
4.10
), leads to:

\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}=[e^{-(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2})}d\mu_{s,N}]\otimes d\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}

and by Fubini-Tonelli, this writes as

\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}=e^{-(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2})}[d\mu_{s,N}\otimes d\mu_{s,N}^{\perp}]=e^{-(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}\Phi^{N}(-t)u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\pi_{N}u\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2})}d\mu_{s}

Hence, we have established (
4.5
). As a consequence, let us now proceed to prove (
4.7
). Using Lemma
4.2
again, then (
4.5
), and then the definition of the modified energy (
3.5
), yields:

\begin{split}\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\rho_{s,N}=e^{-R_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(-t)u)}\Phi^{N}(t)_{\#}\mu_{s}&=e^{-(E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(-t)u)-E_{s,N}(u))}e^{-R_{s,N}(u)}d\mu_{s}\\
&=e^{-(E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(-t)u)-E_{s,N}(u))}d\rho_{s,N}\end{split}

Finally, using the fundamental theorem of calculus and the identity from Proposition
3.7
, we have the rewriting:

E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(-t)u)-E_{s,N}(u)=\int_{0}^{-t}\frac{d}{d\tau}E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(\tau)u)d\tau=\int_{0}^{-t}Q_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(\tau)u)d\tau

where we have used the additivity of

\frac{d}{d\tau}E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(\tau)u)=\frac{d}{dt}E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t+\tau)u)|_{t=0}=\frac{d}{dt}E_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)(\Phi^{N}(\tau)u))|_{t=0}=Q_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(\tau)u)

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.3

∎

5.
Proof of the quasi-invariance

This section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We recall that we fix the parameter
\sigma<s-\frac{1}{2}
close to
s-\frac{1}{2}
, and see the Gaussian measures
\mu_{s}
as probability measures on
(H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}),\mathcal{B}(H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})))
. For transferring the quasi-invariance for the truncated flow to the original flow, we combine the approximation properties of
\Phi(t)
by
\Phi^{N}(t)
with the uniform (in
N\in\mathbb{N}
) energy estimates from Propositions
3.8
and
3.9
. Furthermore, we work intermediately with the weighted Gaussian measures defined in (

).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
s>\frac{9}{10}
,
R>0
, and
T>0
. There exists a constant
C_{s,R,T}>0
, such that for every compact set
K\subset B^{H^{\sigma}}_{R}
, there exists an integer
N_{K,T}
such that:

(5.1)

\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)K)\leq C_{s,R,T}\hskip 2.84544pt\rho_{s,N}(K)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for every
N\geq N_{K,T}
, and every
|t|\leq T
.

Proof.

First of all, thanks to (
3.2
) and (
A.4
), there exists
\Lambda=\Lambda_{R,T}>0
and
N_{K,T}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
N\geq N_{K,T}
, and every
|t|\leq T
:

\Phi_{N}(t)(K)\subset\Phi(t)(K)+B^{H^{\sigma}}_{1}\subset B^{H^{\sigma}}_{\Lambda}

Secondly, from (
4.7
), we have:

\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)K)=\Phi^{N}(-t)_{\#}\rho_{s,N}(K)=\int_{K}\exp\big(-\int_{0}^{t}Q_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(\tau)u)d\tau\big)d\rho_{s,N}

Consequently,

\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)K)=-\int_{K}Q_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)u)d(\Phi^{N}(-t)_{\#}\rho_{s,N})=-\int_{\Phi^{N}(t)K}Q_{s,N}(u)d\rho_{s,N}

and it follows that for every
p\in[1,\infty)

|\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)K)|\leq\|\mathds{1}_{B^{H^{\sigma}}_{\Lambda}}Q_{s,N}\|_{L^{p}(d\rho_{s,N})}\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)K)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

Furthermore, the energy estimates (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) imply by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that:

\|\mathds{1}_{B^{H^{\sigma}}_{\Lambda}}Q_{s,N}\|_{L^{p}(d\rho_{s,N})}\leq C_{s,R,T}\|\mathds{1}_{B^{H^{\sigma}}_{\Lambda}}Q_{s,N}\|_{L^{2p}(d\mu_{s})}\leq C_{s,R,T}p^{\beta}

(where the constant
C_{s,R,T}
changes from an inequality to another); and plugging this in the inequality above yields:

|\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)K)|\leq C_{s,R,T}p^{\beta}\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)K)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

In other words, the function
F(t):=\rho_{s,N}(\Phi^{N}(t)K)
satisfies the differential inequality:

(5.2)

|F^{\prime}(t)|\leq C_{s,R,T}p^{\beta}F(t)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

and integrating this leads to:

(5.3)

F(t)\leq(F(0)^{\frac{1}{p}}+C_{s,R,T}p^{\beta-1})^{p}

Our goal is to prove that
F(t)\leq C_{s,R,T}F(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}
; it is true when
F(0)=0
thanks to (
4.7
), so we assume that
F(0)>0
. Then, we can rewrite (
5.3
) as:

F(t)\leq F(0)(1+F(0)^{-\frac{1}{p}}C_{s,R,T}p^{\beta-1})^{p}=F(0)\exp(p\log(1+F(0)^{-\frac{1}{p}}C_{s,R,T}p^{\beta-1}))

and using the inequalities:

\displaystyle\log(1+x)

\displaystyle\leq x

and

\displaystyle C_{s,R,T}p^{\beta}\leq\alpha p+C_{s,R,T,\alpha}\hskip 8.5359pt\textnormal{(for any fixed $\alpha>0$, and every $p\geq 1$)}

we obtain:

(5.4)

F(t)\leq F(0)\exp(C_{s,R,T}p^{\beta}F(0)^{-\frac{1}{p}})\leq F(0)\exp(\alpha pF(0)^{-\frac{1}{p}}+C_{s,R,T,\alpha}F(0)^{-\frac{1}{p}})

Now, if
\log(\frac{1}{F(0)})<1
, that is
F(0)>e^{-1}
, we simply take
p=1
and
\alpha=1
, so that:

F(t)\leq C_{s,R,T}F(0)

and since from (
3.6
), we have
F(0)\leq C_{s,R}
, we can write (with again

changing):

F(t)\leq C_{s,R,T}F(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}F(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{s,R,T}F(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}

which is the desired inequality. Next, if
\log(\frac{1}{F(0)})\geq 1

and

, so that

have different signatures. Indeed, if we assume otherwise, then the proof of (

) is easier, writing:

Secondly, we observe that it is necessary that

and
k_{(2)}
have the same sign. If by contradiction we assume that
k_{(1)}
and
k_{(2)}
have opposite signs, then

, and we would deduce that

k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0\implies 0\geq|k_{(1)}-k_{(2)}|-|k_{(3)}|-3|k_{(4)}|\geq|k_{(1)}|+|k_{(2)}|-(1+\frac{3}{10})|k_{(3)}|

and therefore we would obtain

, which is absurd beacause
|k_{(1)}|>0
since by assumption

. To sum up, we are left with the case where

and

have different signatures and same sign. In this situation, we have:

\begin{split}|\Omega(\vec{k})|\geq|k_{(1)}^{2}-k_{(2)}^{2}|-k_{(3)}^{2}-3k_{(4)}^{2}&\geq|k_{(1)}-k_{(2)}||k_{(1)}+k_{(2)}|-(1+\frac{3}{100})k_{(3)}^{2}\\
&=|k_{(1)}-k_{(2)}|(|k_{(1)}|+|k_{(2)}|)-(1+\frac{3}{100})k_{(3)}^{2}\end{split}

Moreover, from the constraint

, we have
|k_{(1)}-k_{(2)}|\geq|k_{(3)}|-3|k_{(4)}|
, which implies by assumption that
|k_{(1)}-k_{(2)}|\geq\frac{7}{10}|k_{(3)}|
. Plugging this in the inequaltiy above leads to

\begin{split}|\Omega(\vec{k})|\geq\frac{7}{10}|k_{(3)}|(|k_{(1)}|+|k_{(2)}|)-(1+\frac{3}{100})k_{(3)}^{2}&\geq\frac{1}{10}|k_{(3)}||k_{(1)}|+\frac{13}{10}k_{(3)}^{2}-(1+\frac{3}{100})k_{(3)}^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{10}|k_{(3)}||k_{(1)}|\end{split}

This completes the proof of Lemma

∎

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{N}}\mathbb{E}[Y_{\vec{N}}]

For commodity we use the notation:

U:=\pi_{N}\phi+\pi_{N}V

Let
\mathcal{F}_{s,N}\in\{\mathcal{M}_{s,N},\mathcal{T}_{s,N},\mathcal{N}_{s,N}\}
and consider the associated dyadic block
\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}
. As in Notation

, we consider
(\Psi_{2s},G)\in\{\Psi^{(0)}_{2s},\Psi^{(1)}_{2s}\}\times\{id,F_{N}\}
such that:

(8.6)

\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(U)=\sum_{k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0}\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})G^{N_{1}}_{k_{1}}\overline{U^{N_{2}}_{k_{2}}}U^{N_{3}}_{k_{3}}\overline{U^{N_{4}}_{k_{4}}}U^{N_{5}}_{k_{5}}\overline{U^{N_{6}}_{k_{6}}}

still with the abuse of notation
G=G(U)
. The strategy to estimate
\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(U)
is to expand the formula above in
U=\pi_{N}\phi+\pi_{N}V
. When we replace
U\in H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
by
V\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
, we gain
\frac{1}{2}
-derivative; when we replace
U
by
\phi
, we gain randomness. However, we are limited to expanding the formula (

) until we have at most two
V
terms, in order to ultimately remain controlled by the counter-part-term
-\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2}
in the Boué-Dupuis formula. More concretely, this limitation allow us to obtain a power less than 2 on
\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{s}}
in (
8.3
); and this point was indeed essential in the proof of Proposition
3.8
assuming Lemma
8.3
.

The gains obtained expanding (
8.6
) are measured in terms of negative powers of the
N_{j}
. When we replace
U^{N_{j}}
by
V^{N_{j}}
, we easily see the benefit from:

\displaystyle\left\lVert U^{N_{j}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}-s+}\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}},

\displaystyle\left\lVert V^{N_{j}}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{j}^{-s}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{s}}

When we deal with the
\phi^{N_{j}}
, we benefit from the independence of the Gaussian random variables
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
through Lemma
2.8
, and from the counting bounds from Lemma
6.4
, relying on the presence of constraints on the resonant function
\Omega
(see the proof of Corollary
6.6
). Note also that the higher the
N_{j}
are, the better our estimates will be.

In our analysis, the counting bounds in Lemma
6.4
play a key role, and this will force us to not expand in
U
inside the term
G^{N_{1}}
, where
G(U)=\pi_{N}(|U|^{4}U)
(which corresponds to the most difficult case). More precisely, since the Fourier transform converts the product into convolution, we have:

(|U|^{4}U)_{k_{1}}=\sum_{p_{1}-p_{2}+...+p_{5}=k_{1}}U_{p_{1}}\overline{U_{p_{2}}}...U_{p_{5}}

and wee see that this sum does not contain any constraint on the resonant function
\Omega
, which prevents us from applying the counting bounds from Lemma
6.4
. For that reason, we will not expand in
U
inside
G^{N_{1}}
and we will treat this term purely deterministically, using Lemma
7.1
.

We emphasize that similar strategies have been performed recently in
[
9
]
and
[
13
]
.

Proof of Lemma

Let

,

Again, as in Definition

still with the abuse of notation

.

We consider the worst case when
N_{1}=N_{(1)}
. Then, without loss of generality, we assume that:

\displaystyle N_{(2)}

\displaystyle=N_{2}

and

\displaystyle N_{(3)}

\displaystyle=N_{3}

and

\displaystyle N_{(4)}

\displaystyle=N_{4}

The other cases are similar (when the

We distinguish between two frequency regimes: when

and when

and we treat separately both terms.

•
The first regime:
When
N_{4}\ll N_{3}
. In this case, we estimate (
8.7
)
purely deterministically. Here, we are in position to apply the refined deterministic estimate (
7.9
) from Lemma

|\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(U)|\lesssim_{s}(N_{1}^{2(s-1)}\vee N_{3}^{2(s-1)})(N_{3}N_{4}N_{5}N_{6})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert G^{N_{1}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\prod_{j=2}^{6}\left\lVert U^{N_{j}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

Combining this with (

\begin{split}|\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(U)|\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)&\lesssim_{s,R}(N_{1}^{2(s-1)}\vee N_{3}^{2(s-1)})N_{1}^{n_{s}+\frac{1}{2}-s+}(N_{3}N_{4}N_{5}N_{6})^{1-s}\\

N_{1}^{5-6s}N_{3}^{2(s-1)+4(1-s)}\hskip 8.5359pt\textnormal{for $s\leq 1$}\end{cases}\end{split}

We deduce that:

|\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(U)|\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)\lesssim_{s,R}N_{1}^{0-}

Indeed, this is true for

s\leq 1

5-6s>0

), so that we can write:

N_{1}^{5-6s}N_{3}^{2(s-1)+4(1-s)}\leq N_{1}^{0-}\iff 5-6s+2(s-1)+4(1-s)<0\iff s>\frac{7}{8}

and we have
\frac{7}{8}<\frac{9}{10}
. In particular, we have obtained, when

, that
\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(U)
satisfies the bound in (

).

•
The second regime:
When
N_{4}\sim N_{3}
. In this situation, treating
\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}
purely deterministically is not enough. Following the strategy of Paragraph
8.2
, we expand in
U

8.7
). But before doing so, we decompose

into a non-pairing contribution and a pairing contribution as follows:

(8.8)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(U)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0\\
k_{3}\neq k_{2},k_{4}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})G^{N_{1}}_{k_{1}}\overline{U^{N_{2}}_{k_{2}}}...\overline{U^{N_{6}}_{k_{6}}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0\\

where
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\downarrow}_{s,\vec{N}}
are the multi-linear maps induced, respectively, by the left sum and the right sum in (
8.8
). For the term
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}
, we will exploit the independence of the Gaussian
g_{k_{3}}
from
g_{k_{2}}
and
g_{k_{4}}
; and the term

.

The non-pairing contribution :
This contribution corresponds to the term
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}
in (
8.8
). Here, we establish the bound in (
8.3
) for
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,U,...,U)
. To do so, we start by decomposing
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}
thanks to the multi-linearity. More precisely, we expand
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}
at the level of the frequencies
N_{2},N_{3}
and
N_{4}
as follows:

(8.9)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}&(G,U,U,U,U,U)\\
&=\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,V,V,U,U,U)+\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,V,\phi,V,U,U)+\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,V,V,U,U)\\
&+\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,V,\phi,\phi,U,U)+\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,V,\phi,U,U)+\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,\phi,V,U,U)\\
&+\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,\phi,\phi,U,U)\\
&=:\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D2)}+\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D1)}+\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{\omega}\end{split}

where
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D2)}
,
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D1)}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{\omega}
correspond respectively to the first, second and third lines of the right term of the equality in (
8.9
). Note that in
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D2)}
we have grouped the terms with two
V
; in
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D1)}
we have grouped the terms with exactly one
V
; in
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{\omega}
we have isolated the term without any
V
. In what follows, we study separately these three terms.

We recall that we now have
N_{3}\sim N_{4}
, and still
N_{1}\sim N_{2}
. Hence, in the following, we will consistently replace in
N_{2}
by
N_{1}
and
N_{4}
by
N_{3}
in our estimates.

Study of
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D2)}
:
Here, we benefit from the presence of two

. In
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D2)}
(see (
8.9
)), the least favorable term is:

\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,V,V,U,U)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0\\
k_{3}\neq k_{2},k_{4}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})G^{N_{1}}_{k_{1}}\overline{\phi^{N_{2}}_{k_{2}}}V^{N_{3}}_{k_{3}}\overline{V^{N_{4}}_{k_{4}}}U^{N_{5}}_{k_{5}}\overline{U^{N_{6}}_{k_{6}}}

because the two
V

N_{3}
and
N_{4}
(and not at the higher frequency
N_{2}\sim N_{1}
). Let us then provide a detailed analysis for this term only; the analysis for the other two is similar.

Applying (
7.7
) from Lemma
7.5
leads to:

|\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(\phi,V,V,U,U,U)|\\
\lesssim N_{1}^{0+}(N_{1}^{2(s-1)}\vee N_{3}^{2(s-1)})N_{3}^{2}\left\lVert G^{N_{1}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\phi^{N_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert V^{N_{3}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert V^{N_{4}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert U^{N_{5}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert U^{N_{6}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

We interpolate
V^{N_{4}}
between
H^{\sigma}
and
H^{s}
as:

\left\lVert V^{N_{4}}\right\rVert_{H^{\theta\sigma+(1-\theta)s}}\leq\left\lVert V^{N_{4}}\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}^{\theta}\left\lVert V^{N_{4}}\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{1-\theta}
for
\theta\in(0,1)
,

with

. By using additionally (

) (recalling that

|\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(\phi,V,V,U,U,U)|\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)\\
\lesssim_{s,R}N_{1}^{n_{s}+\frac{1}{2}-s+}(N_{1}^{2(s-1)}\vee N_{3}^{2(s-1)})N_{3}^{2-2s}(N_{5}N_{6})^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\left\lVert\pi_{N}V\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}^{\theta}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2-\theta}

Moreover, since

. Using the triangular inequality, we also bound

|\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(\phi,V,V,U,U,U)|\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)\lesssim_{s,R}N_{1}^{n_{s}+\frac{1}{2}-s+}(N_{1}^{2(s-1)}\vee N_{3}^{2(s-1)})N_{3}^{2-2s}(1+\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}})^{1+\theta}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2-\theta}

Finally, to obtain the bound in (

N_{1}^{n_{s}+\frac{1}{2}-s+}(N_{1}^{2(s-1)}\vee N_{3}^{2(s-1)})N_{3}^{2-2s}\leq\begin{cases}N_{1}^{-1+}N_{3}^{2-2s}\hskip 5.69046pt\textnormal{for $s>1$}\\
N_{1}^{5-6s+}\hskip 5.69046pt\textnormal{for $s\leq 1$}\end{cases}\leq\begin{cases}N_{1}^{-1+}\hskip 5.69046pt\textnormal{for $s>1$}\\
N_{1}^{0-}\hskip 5.69046pt\textnormal{for $\frac{5}{6}<s\leq 1$}\end{cases}

The analysis of
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,V,V,U,U,U)
and

is identical, and in the end we have:

|\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D2)}|\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)\lesssim_{s,R}N_{1}^{0-}(1+\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}})^{1+\theta}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{s}}^{2-\theta}

In particular, we have indeed obtained the bound (

) for
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D2)}
.

Study of
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D1)}
:
Recall that
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}^{(D1)}
is defined as the second line of the right term in (

). Here, we benefit from the presence of one
V
, and from the randomness through the square-root cancellation (see Lemma

). Our analysis of the three terms composing

is identical, so we only focus, for example, on:

\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,\phi,V,U,U)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0\\
k_{3}\neq k_{2},k_{4}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})G^{N_{1}}_{k_{1}}\overline{\phi^{N_{2}}_{k_{2}}}\phi^{N_{3}}_{k_{3}}\overline{V^{N_{4}}_{k_{4}}}U^{N_{5}}_{k_{5}}\overline{U^{N_{6}}_{k_{6}}}

We rewrite this as:

\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(\phi,\phi,V,U,U,U)=\sum_{k_{1},...,k_{6}}C_{0}(\vec{k})\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})G^{N_{1}}_{k_{1}}\overline{\phi^{N_{2}}_{k_{2}}}\phi^{N_{3}}_{k_{3}}\overline{V^{N_{4}}_{k_{4}}}U^{N_{5}}_{k_{5}}\overline{U^{N_{6}}_{k_{6}}}

gathering all the constraints in:

(8.10)

C_{0}(\vec{k}):=\mathds{1}_{k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0}\cdot\mathds{1}_{k_{3}\neq k_{2},k_{4}}\cdot\big(\prod_{j=1}^{6}\mathds{1}_{|k_{j}|\sim N_{j}}\mathds{1}_{|k_{j}|\leq N}\big)

Next, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the
k_{1},k_{4},k_{5},k_{6}
summation, we have:

(8.11)

|\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,\phi,V,U,U)|\\
\leq\Big(\sum_{k_{1},k_{4},k_{5},k_{6}}|G^{N_{1}}_{k_{1}}V^{N_{4}}_{k_{4}}U^{N_{5}}_{k_{5}}U^{N_{6}}_{k_{6}}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k_{1},k_{4},k_{5},k_{6}}\big|\sum_{k_{2},k_{3}}C_{0}(\vec{k})\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})\phi^{N_{2}}_{k_{2}}\overline{\phi^{N_{3}}_{k_{3}}}\big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Furthermore (recalling that

),

\Big(\sum_{k_{1},k_{4},k_{5},k_{6}}|G^{N_{1}}_{k_{1}}V^{N_{4}}_{k_{4}}U^{N_{5}}_{k_{5}}U^{N_{6}}_{k_{6}}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)\leq\left\lVert G^{N_{1}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert V^{N_{4}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert U^{N_{5}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert U^{N_{6}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)\\

|\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,\phi,V,U,U)|\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)\lesssim_{s,R}X_{\vec{N}}\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{H^{s}}

with

X_{\vec{N}}:=N_{1}^{n_{s}+}N_{3}^{-s}(N_{5}N_{6})^{\frac{1}{2}-s}\Big(\sum_{k_{1},k_{4},k_{5},k_{6}}\big|\sum_{k_{2},k_{3}}C_{0}(\vec{k})\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})\phi^{N_{2}}_{k_{2}}\overline{\phi^{N_{3}}_{k_{3}}}\big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Our goal is to prove that

8.4

\mathbb{E}[X_{\vec{N}}^{2}]=[N_{1}^{n_{s}+}N_{3}^{-s}(N_{5}N_{6})^{\frac{1}{2}-s}]^{2}\sum_{k_{1},k_{4},k_{5},k_{6}}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\sum_{k_{2},k_{3}}C_{0}(\vec{k})\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})\frac{g_{k_{2}}\overline{g_{k_{3}}}}{(\langle k_{2}\rangle\langle k_{3}\rangle)^{s}}\big|^{2}\big]\\

Now, using the fact that

, and the bound (

) from Corollary

(8.12)

\mathbb{E}[X_{\vec{N}}^{2}]\lesssim[N_{1}^{n_{s}+s-1+}N_{3}^{\frac{3}{2}-2s}(N_{5}N_{6})^{1-s}]^{2}

therefore,

\mathbb{E}[X_{\vec{N}}^{2}]\lesssim\begin{cases}[N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}+}N_{3}^{\frac{3}{2}-2s}]^{2}\hskip 8.5359pt\textnormal{for $s>1$}\\
[N_{1}^{\frac{7}{2}-4s+}N_{3}^{\frac{3}{2}-2s+2(1-s)}]^{2}\hskip 8.5359pt\textnormal{for $s\leq 1$}\end{cases}

Let us verify that this indeed implies

N_{1}^{\frac{7}{2}-4s+}N_{3}^{\frac{3}{2}-2s+2(1-s)}\leq N_{1}^{0-}\iff\begin{cases}\frac{7}{2}-4s<0\\

Hence, for every

. Finally, the analysis for the other two terms
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,V,\phi,\phi,U,U)
and
\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,V,\phi,U,U)
in

with

Study of

\begin{split}\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}_{s,\vec{N}}=\mathfrak{F}_{s,\vec{N}}(G,\phi,\phi,\phi,U,U)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}-k_{2}+...-k_{6}=0\\

\begin{split}|\mathfrak{F}^{\omega}_{s,\vec{N}}|\mathds{1}_{B_{R}}(U)&\leq\Big(\sum_{k_{1},k_{5},k_{6}}|G^{N_{1}}_{k_{1}}U^{N_{5}}_{k_{5}}U^{N_{6}}_{k_{6}}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k_{1},k_{5},k_{6}}\big|\sum_{k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}}C_{0}(\vec{k})\Psi_{2s}(\vec{k})\phi^{N_{2}}_{k_{2}}\overline{\phi^{N_{3}}_{k_{3}}}\phi^{N_{4}}_{k_{4}}\big|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{s,R}Y_{\vec{N}}\end{split}

where

Definition A.1

(Bourgain spaces)
.

\|\Gamma^{N}_{u_{0}}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\Gamma_{u_{0}}^{N}(\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}})\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}\leq\gamma\|\Phi_{u_{0}}-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}
, so that we obtain from the inequality above:

\|\Phi_{u_{0}}-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}\leq C_{\gamma}\|\Gamma_{u_{0}}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\Gamma_{u_{0}}^{N}(\Phi_{u_{0}})\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}=C_{\gamma}\|\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\mathcal{D}^{N}(\Phi_{u_{0}})\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}

(with
C_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
). Next, from the fact that
F_{N}(u)=\pi_{N}F(\pi_{N}u)
, we have
\mathcal{D}^{N}(u)=\pi_{N}\mathcal{D}(\pi_{N}u)
, and we deduce that :

\|\Phi_{u_{0}}-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}\leq C_{\gamma}(\|\pi_{N}^{\perp}\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{u_{0}})\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}+\|\pi_{N}(\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\mathcal{D}(\pi_{N}\Phi_{u_{0}}))\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}})

Again by contraction,

\|\pi_{N}(\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\mathcal{D}(\pi_{N}\Phi_{u_{0}}))\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}\leq\|\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\mathcal{D}(\pi_{N}\Phi_{u_{0}})\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}\leq\gamma\|\pi_{N}^{\perp}\Phi_{u_{0}}\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}

so,

\|\Phi_{u_{0}}-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}\leq C_{\gamma}(\|\pi_{N}^{\perp}\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{u_{0}})\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}+\|\pi_{N}^{\perp}\Phi_{u_{0}}\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}})

Consequently, by Proposition
A.2
, we have (with
C_{\gamma}
changing):

(A.7)

\begin{split}\sup_{u_{0}\in K}\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\left\lVert\Phi_{u_{0}}(t)-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t)\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}&\lesssim\sup_{u_{0}\in K}\|\Phi_{u_{0}}-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}\\
&\leq C_{\gamma}\sup_{v\in K_{1}}\|\pi_{N}^{\perp}v\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}+C_{\gamma}\sup_{w\in K_{2}}\|\pi_{N}^{\perp}w\|_{X_{(0,\delta)}}\end{split}

where:

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathcal{D}(\Phi_{u_{0}})\hskip 2.84544pt:\hskip 2.84544ptu_{0}\in K\}

and

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{\Phi_{u_{0}}\hskip 2.84544pt:\hskip 2.84544ptu_{0}\in K\}

These sets are compact subset of
X_{(0,\delta)}
because
K\subset H^{\sigma}
is compact and the maps:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544ptX_{(0,\delta)}\rightarrow X_{(0,\delta)}

and

\displaystyle\Phi:

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84544ptH^{\sigma}\rightarrow X_{(0,\delta)}\hskip 2.84544pt;\hskip 2.84544ptu_{0}\longmapsto\Phi_{u_{0}}

are continuous. Furthermore, the projectors
\pi^{\perp}_{N}
are linear maps on
X_{(0,\delta)}
and they satisfy
\left\lVert\pi^{\perp}_{N}\right\rVert_{X_{(0,\delta)}\rightarrow X_{(0,\delta)}}\leq 1
, and they also converge pointwisely to 0. We are then in position to use Lemma
A.12
, and conclude from (
A.7
) that:

(A.8)

\sup_{u_{0}\in K}\sup_{t\in[0,\delta]}\left\lVert\Phi_{u_{0}}(t)-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t)\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

This concludes the first step of the proof. In what follows, we propagate by iteration this result in order to obtain the convergence up to time
T
.

Long-time convergence :
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k\leq\lfloor\frac{T}{\delta}\rfloor
, and suppose that:

(A.9)

\sup_{u_{0}\in K}\sup_{t\in[0,k\delta]}\left\lVert\Phi_{u_{0}}(t)-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t)\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

Our goal is to prove that the convergence holds on the interval
[0,(k+1)\delta]
. Indeed, if we do so, then from (
A.8
), and by induction, we will obtain the convergence on the interval
[0,T]
, and similarly on
[-T,0]
by the same argument; it will then complete the proof of the proposition.

By (
A.9
), we have in particular, with
t_{k}:=k\delta
,

\sup_{u_{0}\in K}\left\lVert\Phi_{u_{0}}(t_{k})-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t_{k})\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

Thus, for some
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
N\geq N_{0}\implies\left\lVert\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t_{k})\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\leq\Lambda_{0}
, where we recall that
\Lambda_{0}
is defined in (
A.6
). As a consequence, denoting
u_{k}:=\Phi_{u_{0}}(t_{k})
and
v_{k}:=\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t_{k})
, the maps

\Gamma_{u_{k}}^{t_{k}}:B_{R}^{t_{k}}(\delta)\rightarrow B_{R}^{t_{k}}(\delta)
and
\Gamma_{v_{k}}^{t_{k},N}:B_{R}^{t_{k}}(\delta)\rightarrow B_{R}^{t_{k}}(\delta)

defined in (
A.3
) are contractions, with still the same universal contraction coefficient
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Furthermore,
\Phi_{u_{0}}
and
\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}
coincide on
I_{k}:=(k\delta,(k+1)\delta)
with the fixed point of
\Gamma_{u_{k}}^{t_{k}}
and
\Gamma_{v_{k}}^{t_{k},N}
respectively. Now, the argument is very similar to that in the first step. Indeed, we start by writing:

(A.10)

\Phi_{u_{0}}-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}=\Gamma^{t_{k}}_{u_{k}}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\Gamma^{t_{k},N}_{v_{k}}(\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}})=S_{t_{k}}(u_{k}-v_{k})+\Gamma^{t_{k}}_{u_{k}}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\Gamma^{t_{k},N}_{u_{k}}(\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}})

On the one hand,

\|S_{t_{k}}(u_{k}-v_{k})\|_{X_{I_{k}}}\lesssim\left\lVert u_{k}-v_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}=\left\lVert\Phi_{u_{0}}(t_{k})-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t_{k})\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

uniformly in
u_{0}\in K
. On the other hand, we treat the term
\Gamma^{t_{k}}_{u_{k}}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\Gamma^{t_{k},N}_{u_{k}}(\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}})
exactly as we treated the term
\Gamma_{u_{0}}(\Phi_{u_{0}})-\Gamma_{u_{0}}^{N}(\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}})
in the first part of the proof. Then, from (
A.10
), we indeed obtain:

\sup_{u_{0}\in K}\sup_{t\in I_{k}}\left\lVert\Phi_{u_{0}}(t)-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t)\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\lesssim\sup_{u_{0}\in K}\|\Phi_{u_{0}}-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}\|_{X_{I_{k}}}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

Finally, combining this with (
A.9
) yields:

\sup_{u_{0}\in K}\sup_{t\in[0,k(\delta+1)]}\left\lVert\Phi_{u_{0}}(t)-\Phi^{N}_{u_{0}}(t)\right\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

which completes the proof.
∎

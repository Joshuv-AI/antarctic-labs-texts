SECURITIES AND EXCHANGE COMMISSION

[Release No. 34-106700; File No. SR-NYSE-2026-49]


Self-Regulatory Organizations; New York Stock Exchange LLC; 
Notice of Filing and Immediate Effectiveness of Proposed Rule Change To 
Restore Former Rule 123D

October 20, 2026.
    Pursuant to Section 19(b)(1) \1\ of the Securities Exchange Act of 
1934 (``Act'') \2\ and Rule 19b-4 thereunder,\3\ notice is hereby given 
that on October 6, 2026, New York Stock Exchange LLC (``NYSE'' or the 
``Exchange'') filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission (the 
``Commission'') the proposed rule change as described in Items I and II 
below, which Items have been prepared by the self-regulatory 
organization. The Commission is publishing this notice to solicit 
comments on the proposed rule change from interested persons.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \1\ 15 U.S.C. 78s(b)(1).
    \2\ 15 U.S.C. 78a.
    \3\ 17 CFR 240.19b-4.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

I. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Terms of Substance 
of the Proposed Rule Change

    The Exchange proposes to restore former Rule 123D to its rulebook 
and mark it ``Reserved.'' The proposed rule change is available on the 
Exchange's website at www.nyse.com and at the principal office of the 
Exchange.

II. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Purpose of, and 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

    In its filing with the Commission, the self-regulatory organization 
included statements concerning the purpose of, and basis for, the 
proposed rule change and discussed any comments it received on the 
proposed rule change. The text of those statements may be examined at 
the places specified in Item IV below. The Exchange has prepared 
summaries, set forth in sections A, B, and C below, of the most 
significant parts of such statements.

A. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Purpose of, and the 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

1. Purpose
    New York Stock Exchange LLC (``NYSE'' or the ``Exchange'') proposes 
restore former Rule 123D to its rulebook and mark it ``Reserved.''
    In July 2025, the Exchange proposed to reorganize its trading halt 
rules into Rule 7.18 and to delete Rule 123D, where some trading halt 
rules had been located.\4\ That filing proposed to delete Rule 123D in 
its entirety, completely removing any reference to it from the 
Exchange's rulebook. The Commission noticed the proposed rule change 
for immediate effectiveness, and the change became operative in August 
2026 in a coordinated roll-out of trading halt changes with other 
equities exchanges.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ See Securities Exchange Act Release No. 103356 (June 30, 
2025), 90 FR 29600 (July 3, 2025) (SR-NYSE-2025-21).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    For the sake of posterity, the Exchange now wishes to add former 
Rule 123D back into its rulebook and note that the rule is 
``Reserved.'' In so doing, the Exchange would also add back the 
adoption and amendment history of Rule 123D, so that the history and 
legacy of the rule and its development is preserved in the rulebook.
    The proposed change is non-substantive. The content of former Rule 
123D would remain deleted; this filing

[[Page 66904]]

would only restore its title, the word ``Reserved,'' and its adoption 
and amendment history.
2. Statutory Basis
    The Exchange believes that its proposal is consistent with the 
requirements of the Act and the rules and regulations thereunder that 
are applicable to a national securities exchange, and, in particular, 
with the requirements of Section 6(b) of the Act.\5\ Specifically, the 
proposal is consistent with Section 6(b)(5) of the Act \6\ because it 
would promote just and equitable principles of trade, remove 
impediments to, and perfect the mechanism of, a free and open market 
and a national market system, and, in general, protect investors and 
the public interest.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \5\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b).
    \6\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b)(5).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Exchange believes the proposed rule change would protect 
investors and the public interest and perfect the mechanism of a free 
and open market and a national market system. Adding reference to 
former Rule 123D back into the rulebook as ``Reserved'' and restoring 
its adoption and amendment history would preserve the legacy of the 
rule and its development, thereby providing clarity and transparency 
for the Exchange and for market participants about the rule's history 
and evolution.

B. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement on Burden on Competition

    The Exchange believes the proposal is consistent with Section 
6(b)(8) of the Act \7\ in that it does not impose any burden on 
competition that is not necessary or appropriate in furtherance of the 
purposes of the Act as explained below. Rather than impacting 
competition, the proposed change would simply add back into the 
Exchange's rulebook the title ``Rule 123D,'' the word ``Reserved,'' and 
the former rule's adoption and amendment history.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \7\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b)(8).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

C. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement on Comments on the Proposed 
Rule Change Received From Members, Participants, or Others

    No written comments were solicited or received with respect to the 
proposed rule change.

III. Date of Effectiveness of the Proposed Rule Change and Timing for 
Commission Action

    The Exchange has filed the proposed rule change pursuant to Section 
19(b)(3)(A)(iii) of the Act \8\ and Rule 19b-4(f)(6) thereunder.\9\ 
Because the proposed rule change does not: (i) significantly affect the 
protection of investors or the public interest; (ii) impose any 
significant burden on competition; and (iii) become operative prior to 
30 days from the date on which it was filed, or such shorter time as 
the Commission may designate, if consistent with the protection of 
investors and the public interest, the proposed rule change has become 
effective pursuant to Section 19(b)(3)(A) of the Act and Rule 19b-
4(f)(6)(iii) thereunder.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \8\ 15 U.S.C. 78s(b)(3)(A)(iii).
    \9\ 17 CFR 240.19b-4(f)(6). In addition, Rule 19b-4(f)(6)(iii) 
requires the Exchange to give the Commission written notice of the 
Exchange's intent to file the proposed rule change, along with a 
brief description and text of the proposed rule change, at least 
five business days prior to the date of filing of the proposed rule 
change, or such shorter time as designated by the Commission. The 
Exchange has satisfied this requirement.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    A proposed rule change filed under Rule 19b-4(f)(6) \10\ normally 
does not become operative prior to 30 days after the date of the 
filing. However, pursuant to Rule 19b-4(f)(6)(iii),\11\ the Commission 
may designate a shorter time if such action is consistent with the 
protection of investors and the public interest. The Exchange has asked 
the Commission to waive the 30-day operative delay so that the proposal 
may become operative immediately upon filing with the Commission 
pursuant to Section 19(b)(3)(A) of the Act \12\ and paragraph (f)(6) of 
Rule 19b-4 thereunder.\13\ The Commission believes that it is 
consistent with the protection of investors and the public interest to 
waive the operative date of the proposal so that it becomes operative 
upon filing.\14\ The Commission believes that the waiver would provide 
member organizations greater clarity about the recent changes to the 
Exchange's trading halt rules. In addition, the proposal does not 
present any unique or novel regulatory issues because it simply adds 
back into the Exchange's rulebook the title ``Rule 123D,'' the word 
``reserved,'' and the ``former rule's adoption and amendment history.'' 
For this reason, the Commission hereby waives the 30-day operative 
period and designates the proposal operative upon filing.

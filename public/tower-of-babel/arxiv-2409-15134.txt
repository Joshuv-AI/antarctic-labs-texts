SCMAlgebras: a Macaulay2 package for sequentially Cohen-Macaulayness Thanks: SCMAlgebras v1.2
Ernesto Lax

Abstract
. We introduce the Macaulay2 package SCMAlgebras . It provides functions for computing the modules of deficiency and the filter ideals, in order to check whether a module or an ideal is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay. The package also implements routines for studying other Cohen-Macaulay type conditions and unmixedness. After recalling the basic algebraic notions and results, the main features of the package are described through examples.

1.
Introduction

The notion of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules was originally introduced by Stanley
[
21
]
in the graded setting, as an algebraic counterpart to the concept of nonpure shellable complexes, which had been developed by Björner and Wachs
[
3
]
in the context of combinatorics and discrete geometry. Later on, Schenzel
[
19
]
independently introduced the notion of Cohen-Macaulay filtered modules and proved the equivalence of this notion to the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property. Since then, the theory of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules has garnered significant attention from many authors
[
1
,
2
,
5
,

,

,

,
9
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
20
,
22
,
23
]
, particularly due to its deep connections with the study of algebraic properties of combinatorial objects such as simplicial complexes and graphs.

In this paper, we present a new
Macaulay2

[
10
]
package,
SCMAlgebras

[
14
]
, designed to check whether a given module or ideal satisfies the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property. This package implements key theoretical results, such as Theorem
2.6
, which characterizes sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules in terms of their modules of deficiency, and Proposition
2.9
due to Goodarzi
[
9
]
, which provides an efficient criterion for verifying the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property for homogeneous ideal. To support these results, the package introduces computational tools for calculating the modules of deficiency (see Definition
2.3
) and generating filter ideals (see Definition
2.8
), both of which are fundamental for testing the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property of the input objects. Beyond this primary goal, these constructions also enable the computation of related invariants and the study of further properties. In particular, the filter ideals provide an effective approach for checking the unmixedness of an ideal (Proposition
2.11
)
via
the notion of unmixed layer, while the package also includes routines for investigating Cohen-Macaulay type conditions. In this way,
SCMAlgebras
provides a broader computational framework for the study of modules and ideals, extending its applicability beyond the verification of the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property.

These tools extend the functionality of the software, playing as a starting point for future development in the manipulation of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property in both theoretical and applied contexts.

2.
Mathematical background

In this section we recall the notion of sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules and the main characterizations, that are implemented in the package. In the following we will work in the graded case. Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
.

Definition 2.1
.

A finitely generated graded
S
-module
M
is called a
sequentially Cohen-Macaulay
module if there exist a finite filtration of submodules of
M

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{d-1}\subset M_{r}=M

such that

a)

each quotient
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is Cohen-Macaulay,

b)

\dim M_{i}/M_{i-1}<\dim M_{i+1}/M_{i}
for all
i
.

A filtration satisfying the above conditions is called a
sequentially Cohen-Macaulay filtration
of
M
. It is easy to see that if the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay filtration exists, then it is unique.

Remark 2.2
.

Any Cohen-Macaulay module is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, by considering the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay filtration
0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}=M
. Hence, one has the implication

Cohen-Macaulay

\Longrightarrow

sequentially

Cohen-Macaulay

but the converse is not true in general.

Proving the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property through the construction of a sequentially Cohen-Macaulay filtration is not always an easy task. The definition itself is not operative and it is not very efficient when trying to implement it on algorithms. In order to demonstrate this condition effectively and one must rely on algebraic or combinatorial characterization.

In the ladder, two of the main characterizations of the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property are recalled. The first one involves the notion of the modules of deficiency.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module and let
d=\dim M
. For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
the
i
th module of deficiency
of
M
is

\omega^{i}(M)=\Ext^{n-i}_{S}(M,S(-n)).

If
i=d
, the module
\omega^{d}(M)=\omega(M)
is the
canonical module
of
M
.

Remark 2.4
.

The modules of deficiency were introduced and studied by Schenzel in
[
17
]
in the local case. By duality (in the graded setting), one has that
\omega^{i}(M)
is the Matlis dual of the
i
th local cohomology module
H^{i}(M)
of
M
. Let
t=\depth M
.

From a well–known result of Grothendieck (see
[
4
]
), it holds that
H^{i}(M)=0
for
i<t
or
i>d
. Moreover,
H^{i}(M)\neq 0
for
i=t,d
. It follows that
M
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
H^{i}(M)=0
for all
i\neq d
. Hence, one has
\omega^{i}(M)=0
for
i<t
or
i>d
,
\omega^{t}(M)\neq 0
and
\omega(M)=\omega(M)\neq 0
and
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay module if and only if
\omega^{i}(M)=0
for all
i\neq d
. In such case, the canonical module
\omega^{d}(M)=\omega(M)
is also Cohen-Macaulay. Hence, the modules of deficiency of
M
measure how far is
M
from being a Cohen-Macaulay module.

Definition 2.5
.

A module
M
with Cohen-Macaulay canonical module
\omega(M)
is called a
canonically Cohen-Macaulay
module.

The next classical result characterizes sequentially Cohen-Macaulay modules
via
the modules of deficiency (see
[
6
]
).

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
S
-module, with
d=\dim M
. Then, the followings are equivalent:

1.

M
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay;

2.

for all
0\leq i\leq d
the
i
th module of deficiency
\omega^{i}(M)
is either zero or an
i
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay module;

3.

for all
0\leq i<d
the
i
th module of deficiency
\omega^{i}(M)
is either zero or an
i
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay module.

The equivalence
1.

\Longleftrightarrow

2.
in the above theorem was announced without proof in
[
21
, Theorem 2.11]
, but the author mentioned a spectral sequence argument due to Peskine.

Remark 2.7
.

If
M
is a sequentially Cohen-Macaulay module, then by condition
2.
of Theorem
2.6

M
has a Cohen-Macaulay canonical module. Hence, it follows that a sequentially Cohen-Macaulay module is canonically Cohen-Macaulay, but the converse is not true in general.

Thus, merging the informations from Remark
2.2
and Remark
2.7
, one has the following chain of implications:

Cohen-Macaulay

\Longrightarrow

sequentially

Cohen-Macaulay

\Longrightarrow

canonically

Cohen-Macaulay

with the reverse implications not true in general.

Although Theorem
2.6
remains useful in many situations (see
[
15
, Theorem 1.5]
,
[
20
]
), it also presents two significant challenges. Firstly, to determine which modules of deficiency vanish is often a difficult task (see
[
23
]
). Secondly, proving the Cohen-Macaulayness of the non–vanishing modules is typically non–trivial. These difficulties underscore the complexity of applying the theorem in practice, particularly when dealing with more complicated algebraic structures.

In case we work with a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
it is possible to characterize the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property by using new ideals, namely the filter ideals, which are constructed from the primary decomposition of
I
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal, with
d=\dim S/I
, and let
I=\bigcap_{j=1}^{r}Q_{j}
be the minimal primary decomposition of
I
. For all
1\leq j\leq r
, let
P_{j}=\sqrt{Q_{j}}
be the radical of
Q_{j}
. For all
-1\leq i\leq d
, the
i
th filter ideal
of
I
is

I^{<i>}=\bigcap_{\dim S/{P_{j}}>i}Q_{j},

where
I^{<-1>}=I
and
I^{<d>}=S
.

Moreover, we define the
minimum dimension
of
I
as the minimum integer such that
I^{<i>}\neq I
. By definition, it is clear that minimum dimension is at least
-1
.

For a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
, one has the filtration

I=I^{<-1>}\subseteq I^{<0>}\subseteq I^{<1>}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq I^{<d-1>}\subseteq I^{<d>}=S.

Moreover, rewriting the above sequence of inclusions modulo
I
, we obtain

0\subseteq I^{<0>}/I\subseteq I^{<1>}/I\subseteq\ldots\subseteq I^{<d-1>}/I\subseteq S/I,

which is called the
dimension filtration
of
S/I
. One can show (see
[
9
, Lemma 1]
) that such filtration does not depend on the primary components, therefore it is unique. Note that, the filter ideals
I^{<i>}
are also independent from the primary decomposition.

By the above construction, one has the following characterization of the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property for
S/I
due to Goodarzi (see
[
9
, Proposition 16]
).

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal and suppose
d=\dim S/I
. Then,
S/I
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

(1)

\depth S/{I^{<i>}}\geq i+1,\qquad\text{for all $0\leq i<d$}.

Goodarzi’s characterization provides an efficient criterion to verify the sequentially Cohen-Macaulay property for a homogeneous ideal. It has been widely used, for instance, to study the sequential Cohen-Macaulayness of the binomial edge ideals of various classes of graphs, such as closed graphs (see
[
8
]
), block graphs, cycles, and cone graphs (see
[
15
]
), playing a crucial role in the proofs. In particular, for binomial edge ideals, this result also permitted in
[
15
]
the investigation of situations that were difficult to describe using Theorem
2.6
. This breakthrough allowed for a deeper understanding of cases previously out of reach, thus highlighting the power of Goodarzi’s approach.

We end this section with another useful application of filter ideals.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal, with
d=\dim S/I
. For all
0\leq i\leq d
, the
i
th unmixed layer
of
S/I
is the
S
-module
I^{<i>}/I^{<i-1>}
and it is denoted by
U_{i}(S/I)
.

The name unmixed layer is motivated by
[
19
, Corollary 2.3]
, in fact each module
U_{i}(S/I)
is unmixed, that is for all
i
the associated primes of
U_{i}(S/I)
have the same height. The next result (see
[
9
, Lemma 3]
) gives a characterization of the unmixedness of the ideal
I
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal, with
d=\dim S/I
. Then
S/I
is unmixed if and only if
U_{i}(S/I)=0
for all
1\leq i<d
and
U_{d}(S/I)=S/I
.

To optimize the computations, the new
SCMAlgebras v1.2
introduces a major refactor of its internal routines along with new structures specifically designed to handle primary decompositions efficiently.

In previous versions, iterative calls to
filterIdeal
function forced recomputing the minimal primary decomposition of the ideal each time. To bypass this computational bottleneck, a new data type called
PrimaryDataList
has been introduced. This type stores the relevant information from the primary decomposition of an ideal
I
as a list of all pairs
\{Q,\dim(S/\sqrt{Q})\}
, where
Q
is a primary component of
I
. The user can generate this data via the new constructor function
getPrimaryData(I)
and pass it as an optional argument to
filterIdeal
.

In the present release, this strategy is internally adopted by the functions
isSCM
and
unmixedLayer
, which call
filterIdeal
in a
for
loop. By computing and caching the
PrimaryDataList
upfront, the primary decomposition is performed only once at the beginning of the process rather than at each iteration, reducing execution times and memory leaks.

D-SCoRE: D ocument-Centric S egmentation and Co T Reasoning with Structured E xport for QA-CoT Data Generation
Weibo Zhou, Lingbo Li, Shangsong Liang

Abstract
The scarcity and high cost of high-quality question-answering (QA) datasets hinder supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for domain-specific large language models (LLMs). To address this, we introduce D-SCoRE, a training-free pipeline that utilizes LLMs and prompt engineering to produce diverse, high-quality QA datasets from arbitrary textual sources. D-SCoRE integrates D ocument-centric processing, S egmentation, Co T R easoning, and structured E xport to generate QA-CoT datasets tailored for domain-aware SFT. Multi-dimensional control mechanisms, such as semantic role transformation, question type balancing, and counterfactual materials, enhance diversity and relevance, overcoming limitations of existing QA generation. LLMs fine-tuned on D-SCoRE-generated QA datasets, and human-annotated QA datasets (SQuAD, Covid-QA) are evaluated on SQuADShifts and Covid-QA test sets, with D-SCoRE outperforming across most domains. D-SCoRE generates six QA-CoT pairs with four-option counterfactual materials per 100–200-word text in 90 seconds using an 8B LLM on consumer-grade hardware. Its simplicity and scalability enable efficient QA generation and high-performance fine-tuning across domains.



Introduction

Question-answering (QA) datasets are vital for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of large language models (LLMs), enabling adaptation to domains such as education, healthcare, and customer service. However, high-quality QA datasets are scarce due to the labor-intensive and costly process of manual annotation, which limits scalability. While automated QA generation offers a promising solution, existing methods struggle with insufficient diversity, high production costs, and poor control over question complexityhighlighting the need for more efficient and versatile approaches.

Existing automated QA generation methods can be classified into two categories: non-synthetic and semi-synthetic. Non-synthetic approaches extract QA pairs directly from corpora, ensuring contextual relevance but lacking scalability due to reliance on pre-annotated sources. Semi-synthetic methods augment human-curated data with automated processes, improving efficiency but often requiring complex preprocessing pipelines. Both paradigms face challenges in achieving diversity, cost-effectiveness, and complexity control, necessitating simpler and more controllable frameworks.

To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel, training-free pipeline that generates diverse, high-quality QA datasets from arbitrary texts using advanced prompt engineering with LLMs. Our approach employs multi-dimensional control strategies—including explicit and implicit QA formulation, semantic role transformations, and counterfactual reasoning—to enhance dataset variety, accuracy, and relevance while reducing issues like hallucinations. Key contributions include:

•

A simplified, end-to-end framework that directly produces QA pairs from input texts, bypassing complex preprocessing or model fine-tuning.

•

The integration of semantic role transformations and counterfactual elements to diversify question types and increase complexity, thereby addressing gaps in existing semi-synthetic and fully synthetic methods.

•

Empirical validation showing superior performance of LLMs fine-tuned on our datasets compared to baselines, demonstrating practical efficacy in multi-domain applications.

QA datasets underpin LLM fine-tuning, performance evaluation, and practical deployments. In training contexts, they enable supervised and parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques (e.g., LoRA), as demonstrated in OpenThoughts’ refinement of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on reasoning benchmarks

These persistent limitations—encompassing suboptimal scalability, variable accuracy, constrained diversity, and diminished interpretability—underscore the necessity for an automated, versatile QA generation paradigm that delivers high-fidelity outputs, semantic depth, and broad applicability, independent of human annotations or elaborate infrastructures.

Methodology

This section delineates our methodology for synthesizing high-quality and diverse QA datasets tailored for SFT of LLMs. The proposed framework harnesses LLMs in conjunction with sophisticated prompt engineering to derive QA pairs directly from unprocessed textual inputs, obviating the necessity for supplementary training regimens or intricate architectural designs. Central to this approach is a tripartite core pipeline, fortified by multifaceted control mechanisms to augment diversity and fidelity. Supplementary modules may be integrated optionally to accommodate heterogeneous inputs and diverse downstream utilities. The methodology is engineered for simplicity, scalability, and cross-domain adaptability.

Core Pipeline

At the heart of our methodology resides a three-stage pipeline, wherein each phase solicits an LLM to synthesize or refine QA artifacts. This pipeline is meticulously crafted to yield diverse, high-fidelity QA pairs exhibiting controlled attributes, as elaborated subsequently.

Stage 1: QA Generation

In this inaugural stage, QA pairs are synthesized from individual text segments, with an emphasis on fostering dataset heterogeneity via a judicious amalgamation of explicit and implicit interrogatives. Explicit questions pertain to factual elements that are directly retrievable from the text, whereas implicit questions necessitate inferential reasoning or the amalgamation of latent insights. Through targeted prompt engineering, the LLM is directed to:

•

Discern discrete entities or informational nuclei for explicit questions, thereby guaranteeing specificity.

•

Deploy inferential paradigms for implicit questions to elicit responses.

•

Augment question variability by employing semantic role permutations while maintaining answer invariance.

Stage 2: QA Quality Control

To safeguard the precision and pertinence of the synthesized QA pairs, the subsequent stage implements rigorous quality assurance protocols. This encompasses two pivotal verifications:

•

Textual Fidelity: The LLM corroborates that each QA pair adheres exclusively to the source text, thereby precluding extraneous knowledge integration or hallucinatory artifacts via cross-validation against the input.

•

Interrogative Taxonomy Validation: The LLM evaluates the apt classification of questions as explicit or implicit. Explicit variants must address directly extractable data, while implicit ones demand reasoning or synthesis. Erroneously categorized questions (e.g., an ostensibly implicit query with a verbatim extractable response) or incongruent instances (e.g., those deficient in textual substrate for inference) undergo rectification or resynthesis.

A stipulated constraint mandates that each textual segment yields at least one validated explicit question, a feasible requisite given the ubiquity of extractable information in texts. Leveraging prompt engineering, the LLM iteratively refines or regenerates aberrant QA pairs, thereby fortifying dataset integrity and congruence with the input domain.

Stage 3: Counterfactual Material Generation

To amplify dataset sophistication and bolster SFT efficacy across manifold domains, this culminating stage fabricates counterfactual alternatives for each QA pair. For every interrogative, the LLM, under prompt-engineered guidance, executes the following:

•

Synthesizes three distractor alternatives per QA pair, ensuring semantic proximity to the veridical response while incorporating factually erroneous yet credible content, anchored in the source text.

•

Appraises distractor caliber by ascertaining contextual alignment and discriminability, with regeneration of subpar elements as warranted.

•

Randomizes the veridical answer’s locus amidst the quartet of options to attenuate positional predispositions.

An ancillary constraint stipulates that at least one distractor per question exploits nuanced semantic deviations, thereby escalating dataset intricacy to facilitate resilient, domain-agnostic SFT.

Multi-Dimensional Control Mechanisms

To ascertain that the resultant QA dataset fulfills exigencies for heterogeneity, fidelity, and domain fidelity, our methodology embeds multifaceted control apparatuses that orchestrate pipeline orchestration. These apparatuses afford granular personalization and resilience across disparate textual corpora and SFT objectives, thereby enabling bespoke QA dataset curation for targeted tasks or domains.

Question Type Control

This apparatus enforces an equilibrated distribution of explicit and implicit questions. By steering the LLM toward factual loci with entity-centric foci for explicit questions and reasoning-oriented derivations for implicit counterparts, we sustain variability in interrogative complexity, amenable to downstream exigencies.

Diversity Enhancement

To circumvent monotonous interrogative architectures, we invoke semantic role transmutations for question reformulation sans answer alteration, complemented by counterfactual distractor synthesis to infuse arduous alternatives. These modalities amplify linguistic and semantic multiplicity, thereby elevating the dataset’s efficacy in model optimization.

Quality Assurance

Quality governance protocols impose stringent text-anchoring and accurate interrogative stratification. By authenticating that QA pairs emanate solely from the input text and are duly demarcated as explicit or implicit, we uphold dataset dependability and curtail anomalies such as hallucinations or taxonomic discrepancies.

Optional Components

Pre-Processing

In light of LLMs’ constrained contextual apertures, an elective pre-processing phase partitions input texts into manageable segments to expedite QA synthesis. This adjunctive procedure accommodates variegated texts via adjustable length parameters aligned with bespoke requisites, thereby modulating question density in synergy with the core pipeline. For structured corpora, segmentation exploits innate delineations such as chapters or sections. For amorphous texts, entrenched semantic-preserving chunking methodologies, exemplified by Meta-Chunking
(
Zhao et al. 2024
)
, are enlisted to retain conceptual coherence. Albeit peripheral to this inquiry, these techniques underpin dataset malleability.

Post-Processing

An elective post-processing phase polishes the synthesized dataset by scrutinizing QA pair coherence, excising redundancies, sieving formatting aberrations, or normalizing for SFT via schema validation or LLM invocations. This phase adapts to idiosyncratic applications, ensuring a refined deliverable for ensuing endeavors.

Experiments

In this section, we delineate the experimental framework, encompassing the datasets utilized for assessing the D-SCoRE pipeline across heterogeneous domains, in conjunction with the implementation particulars of our methodology. The experiments are orchestrated to systematically interrogate a sequence of research questions that progressively scrutinize the baseline efficacy, potency, and operational efficiency of the proposed paradigm in synthesizing QA datasets for SFT of LLMs.

Research Questions

RQ1: (Baseline juxtaposition of D-SCoRE against conventional methodologies) To what degree do QA datasets synthesized via the D-SCoRE pipeline surpass those derived from baseline approaches in augmenting SFT performance across multifaceted domains?

This seminal RQ furnishes a comparative benchmark by ascertaining whether D-SCoRE-derived datasets engender superior SFT outcomes vis-à-vis human-curated or conventional automated QA generation modalities, thereby substantiating the imperative for scalable and domain-resilient QA synthesis.

RQ2: (Efficacy of reasoning traces in SFT augmentation) What ramifications arise from embedding reasoning traces within D-SCoRE-synthesized QA pairs on the proficiency of domain-specific SFT, particularly concerning inferential faculties and precision?

Erecting upon the baseline juxtaposition, this RQ probes the utility of a pivotal innovative attribute—reasoning traces—by quantifying their augmentation to model proficiency across diverse domains, predicated on the conjecture that such traces cultivate refined logical deduction and mitigate inaccuracies in question-answering endeavors.

RQ3: (Modulation of explicit-implicit question proportions on SFT optimization) In what manner do varying ratios of explicit and implicit questions within D-SCoRE-synthesized datasets modulate the efficiency and holistic efficacy of SFT across domains, and what emergent optimal configurations manifest?

Amplifying the inquiry to encompass efficiency metrics, this RQ elucidates the equilibria inherent in dataset constitution, inclusive of computational exigencies and convergence kinetics, to delineate how disparate explicit-implicit proportions optimize SFT ramifications whilst attenuating resource imperatives in polydomain contexts.

To interrogate these inquiries, empirical evaluations were executed leveraging the SQuAD and Covid-QA corpora, with efficacy indices benchmarked against entrenched QA generation referents.

Implementation Details

Data Generation

•

QA Generation
: For the SQuAD corpus, Qwen 3-8B synthesized 2 explicit and 1 implicit QA pair per contextual segment, employing a prompt oriented toward pivotal entities for explicit interrogatives and inferential derivations for implicit variants. For Covid-QA, it engendered 3 explicit and 3 implicit QA pairs per segment under analogous model parameters.

•

QA Quality Control
: DeepSeek-R1-7B was deployed to enact KEEP, DELETE, or TYPEFIX directives on QA pairs. In instances of wholesale deletion, a singular novel explicit QA pair was regenerated.

•

Counterfactual Material Generation
: DeepSeek-R1-7B fabricated 3 counterfactual responses per QA pair within SQuAD, with veridical answer loci randomized.

•

Text Pre-Processing
: For Covid-QA, PPL Chunking was invoked with the minimal perplexity threshold and a 256-token segment cap.

•

Post-Processing
: Deficient JSON entries were reconstituted via the aforementioned core pipeline configurations.

All generative experiments were executed on a computational node running Linux, equipped with 1× NVIDIA 4070 Ti GPU (16GB), leveraging models provisioned through Ollama or LM-Studio.

We assess the efficacy of 4B and 8B models refined via SFT on QA datasets synthesized by the D-SCoRE pipeline, employing F1 and exact match (EM) metrics across the SQuAD, SQuADShifts (encompassing Amazon, NYT, Reddit, and New Wiki subsets), and Covid-QA corpora. The empirical outcomes (Table

RQ1: Baseline juxtaposition of D-SCoRE against conventional methodologies

The configuration utilizing exclusively implicit questions synthesized by D-SCoRE often approximates or exceeds the Gold standard in the Amazon, NYT, and Reddit subsets, evidencing its proficiency in generating high-fidelity QA datasets across heterogeneous domains such as e-commerce, journalism, and social discourse. In knowledge-dense domains like New Wiki, Covid-QA, and SQuAD, D-SCoRE exhibits marginally inferior performance to Gold; nonetheless, this proximity underscores its adaptability and viability for refinement in intricate contexts. All D-SCoRE variants uniformly surpass the no-SFT (NOSFT) baseline across evaluated datasets, affirming the pipeline’s capacity to augment SFT efficacy in both general and specialized arenas. Datasets derived from D-SCoRE generally exhibit enhanced performance upon scaling to the 8B model, with pronounced improvements in Amazon, NYT, and Reddit subsets, albeit with attenuated scaling benefits in Covid-QA and SQuAD for certain configurations, attributable to domain-specific complexities and warranting targeted enhancements.

The observed performance disparities in New Wiki and SQuAD may stem from the heterogeneous distribution of QA pairs in Gold data, wherein human annotators produce variable quantities contingent on textual information density, contrasting with D-SCoRE’s uniform generation of a configurable fixed number per text segment, independent of content variability.

In the Covid-QA domain, the amplified discrepancy could arise from the constrained Gold training corpus (1,442 QA pairs) relative to D-SCoRE’s expansive output (exceeding 10,000 pairs), potentially influencing precision; furthermore, the 256-token segmentation may impede extended inferential chains in scientific prose, compounded by the esoteric nature of biomedical content. Notwithstanding these factors, D-SCoRE’s scalability and outperformance of the NOSFT baseline highlight its potential for domain-specific applications.

RQ2: Efficacy of reasoning traces in SFT augmentation

We compare the 1/0 configuration (implicit questions with reasoning traces) against 0/1 (explicit questions without traces), NOSFT, and Gold, using F1 and EM metrics. The 1/0 configuration consistently outperforms 0/1 across nearly all datasets, with performance gaps reaching up to 25% in F1 and 30% in EM on SQuAD (8B model), underscoring the critical role of reasoning traces in improving logical coherence. The 1/0 configuration often matches or exceeds Gold, with a maximum gap of about 5% in F1 and EM, due to data constraints and domain complexity. Scaling to 8B amplifies 1/0’s advantages, particularly in user-centric domains.

These results arise because, for interactive texts like Amazon reviews and Reddit, 1/0 adeptly encapsulates subtle logical interrelations inherent in user-generated content, such as sentiment analysis or rhetorical structures, which are difficult to extract from entity-based explicit QA pairs. For knowledge-intensive, specialized data, reasoning traces enable the construction of inferential pathways essential for knowledge learning and understanding.
This highlights the importance of reasoning traces and their positive impact on SFT across diverse domains, demonstrating the superiority of the D-SCoRE method.

RQ3: Modulation of explicit-implicit question proportions on SFT optimization

Besides the good performance of 1/0 configuration, a key observation is that the 0.66/1 configuration, with a balanced mix of implicit and explicit questions, demonstrates robust performance across diverse domains, consistently outperforming 1/1 and occasionally rivaling or exceeding 1/0 and Gold. The 0.33/1 and 1/1 configurations yield intermediate performance, surpassing 0/1, which suffers from reduced performance and training instability due to limited generalization. All configurations outperform the NOSFT baseline, with 1/0 and 0.66/1 emerging as optimal for user-centric and knowledge-intensive domains, respectively.

The superior performance of 0.66/1 over 1/1, despite incorporating fewer explicit questions, merits attention. The 0.66/1 configuration benefits from an optimal synergy between explicit questions, which ensure factual accuracy, and implicit questions, which foster deeper reasoning, avoiding the redundancy and training volatility observed in 1/1’s equal mix. In knowledge-intensive domains, this balance enhances factual recall while supporting inferential depth, whereas in user-centric domains, the higher implicit proportion facilitates nuanced pattern recognition, such as sentiment or rhetorical structures. Conversely, 1/1’s higher explicit question volume may lead to overfitting on factual tasks, diluting the model’s reasoning capabilities. These findings underscore the importance of carefully calibrating explicit-to-implicit question ratios to optimize SFT efficacy across diverse domains.

Model Size Effects

For F1 and EM metrics, the 8B model generally outperforms the 4B model across most datasets and configurations, though the 4B model is competitive in specific cases, particularly within all implicit configurations. Notably, in Covid-QA, the 8B model exhibits lower F1 and EM scores in some configurations, likely due to its richer, more informative outputs introducing additional information or varied phrasing, which reduces exact matches despite potentially higher answer quality, compounded by biomedical complexity and limited training data. For generative metrics, the 8B model consistently surpasses the 4B model across all datasets. These results suggest that while the 8B model enhances performance across all metrics, D-SCoRE’s implicit-centric datasets enable the 4B model to achieve comparable or superior F1 and EM results in specific domains.

Threats to Validity

Internal Validity

Variability in the quantity of QA pairs generated by D-SCoRE across different configurations may confound observed performance differences, as larger datasets could inherently enhance SFT outcomes. Additionally, baseline imbalances in the distribution of implicit and explicit questions impede the attribution of performance variations solely to manipulated ratios, with initial data composition potentially biasing results. Furthermore, dataset quality—encompassing inconsistencies in human annotations within Gold datasets or the complex biomedical terminology in Covid-QA—could influence model performance. These threats were mitigated through the use of consistent server environments for QA generation and SFT, fixed model parameters, and uniform training hyperparameters. Subsets of SQuADShifts and SQuAD were evaluated on models fine-tuned under identical conditions to minimize extraneous influences.

External Validity

Although D-SCoRE is capable of generating QA pairs from arbitrary textual sources, the evaluation framework—comparing Qwen models fine-tuned on D-SCoRE-generated pairs versus Gold QA pairs—requires datasets with pre-existing Gold QA annotations. This constraint limits validation to such annotated datasets, thereby excluding purely textual corpora without QA annotations or models outside the Qwen family. Extending assessments to diverse models and alternative evaluation methodologies could improve the generalizability of the findings.

Construct Validity

The F1 and EM metrics may not fully encapsulate dimensions such as answer quality, reasoning ability, or SFT efficiency, as token-level comparisons could undervalue semantically equivalent responses, particularly in user-generated datasets (e.g., Amazon, Reddit) or specialized ones (e.g., Covid-QA). Moreover, annotation biases inherent in Gold standards could distort comparative evaluations. Semantic similarity measures or human assessments might better correspond to the targeted constructs.

Conclusion

Domain question answering (QA) dataset generation faces challenges in generalizability and scalability, as existing methods often rely on costly manual annotations or unstable methods. D-SCoRE addresses this by generating implicit and explicit QA pairs from any text, enabling flexible SFT across diverse datasets. Our experiments with Qwen models demonstrate D-SCoRE’s ability to produce effective QA pairs, with performance varying by dataset complexity and question ratios. Future work exploring diverse model architectures, refined ratio impacts, new domains, and evaluation metrics for text-only datasets, could further enhance D-SCoRE’s robustness and generalizability, advancing automated domain QA generation and SFT for broader NLP applications.

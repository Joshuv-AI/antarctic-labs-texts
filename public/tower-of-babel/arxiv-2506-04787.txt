Approximation of functions in mixed norm spaces
Priyanka Majethiya, Shivam Bajpeyi, Dhiraj Patel

Abstract
The concept of mixed norm spaces has emerged as a significant interest in fields such as harmonic analysis. In addition, the problem of function approximation through sampling series has been particularly noteworthy in the realm of approximation theory. This paper aims to address both these aspects. Here we deal with the problem of function approximation in diverse mixed norm function spaces. We utilise the family of Kantorovich type sampling operators as approximator for the functions in mixed norm Lebesgue space L P → ​ ( ℝ n ) , L^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}), with P → = ( p 1 , p 2 , … , p n ) , \overrightarrow{P}=(p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n}), and mixed norm Orlicz space L Φ → ​ ( ℝ n ) , L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}), with Φ → = ( ϕ 1 , ϕ 2 , … , ϕ n ) , \overrightarrow{\Phi}=\left(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},...,\phi_{n}\right), where each ϕ i \phi_{i} is an Orlicz function (defined in Section 2 ). The Orlicz spaces are well-known as a generalized family that encompasses many significant function spaces. We establish the boundedness of the family of generalized as well as Kantorovich type sampling operators within the framework of these mixed norm spaces. Further, we study the approximation properties of Kantorovich-type sampling operators in both mixed norm Lebesgue and Orlicz spaces. At the end, we discuss a few examples of suitable kernel involved in the discussed approximation procedure. Keywords: Approximation of functions; Kantorovich type sampling series; Mixed norm Lebesgue space; Mixed norm Orlicz space. Subject Classification (2020): 94A20, 26D15, 46B09, 46E30, 47B34.

This paper aims to investigate the approximation properties of a family of sampling operators within the framework of a mixed norm structure. We study its approximation capabilities in a broad class of functions spaces, which may not be continuous, such as mixed norm Lebesgue spaces and mixed norm Orlicz spaces. First, we examine the boundedness of generalized sampling operators in mixed norm Lebesgue space. We then establish the boundedness and convergence of Kantorovich-type sampling operators within mixed norm Lebesgue spaces. Finally, we extend these results in the setting of mixed norm Orlicz spaces.

The theory of Orlicz spaces, which provides a natural generalization of Lebesgue spaces, was established by W. Orlicz in
[
31
]
. It is noteworthy that the Orlicz spaces, denoted as
L^{\phi},
consist of many significant function spaces for different choice of

The seminal work on Orlicz spaces can be found in
[
29

[
33

[
34

provides a rigorous discussion of fundamental applications of Orlicz spaces in areas such as Fourier analysis, stochastic analysis, and nonlinear PDE. Function spaces derived from Orlicz spaces have proven highly effective in the study of functional analysis and operator theory
[
21
,
28

. Hence, Orlicz spaces offer a unified framework for studying Kantorovich operators in different function spaces. In particular, mixed norm function spaces are considered for their flexible structure and broad applicability.

In mixed norm spaces, the norm of measurable multivariable functions is determined by the iterative application of different norm functions. The foundation for this concept is found in the pioneering article by Benedek and Panzone
[
5
]
. For instance, unlike classical Lebesgue spaces, in mixed norm Lebesgue spaces, the exponent for integrability can differ for each variable. This flexibility
makes it more useful for areas where different variables may have different properties.
These spaces are particularly relevant for time-varying signals
[
26
]
and playing a pivotal role in examining the solutions to partial differential equations that involve both time and spatial variables, such as the heat and wave equations
[

]

In

[

To the best of our knowledge, these operators have not been studied in mixed norm Lebesgue spaces and mixed norm Orlicz spaces. Given the enduring interest in Kantorovich sampling operators in approximation theory, examining their behavior in mixed norm settings is of particular significance.

2.1
Mixed norm Lebesgue space

Let
(Y_{i},S_{i},\mu_{i})
be the
\sigma
-finite measure spaces, for
1\leq i\leq n
, and
(Y,S,\mu)
represent corresponding product measure space, i.e.,
Y=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}{Y_{i}}.
The mixed norm Lebesgue space on the product space
Y
is defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

(Mixed norm Lebesgue space)
For
1\leq p_{i}\leq\infty
and
\overrightarrow{P}=(p_{1},...,p_{n})
, the mixed norm Lebesgue space
L^{\overrightarrow{P}}(Y)
is a collection of measureable function on
Y
such that

\|f\|_{\overrightarrow{P}}=\bigg(\int_{Y_{n}}\dots\bigg(\int_{Y_{2}}\bigg(\int_{Y_{1}}|f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|^{p_{1}}d\mu_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}d\mu_{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{3}}{p_{2}}}\dots d\mu_{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}.

If we set
\overrightarrow{P}=(p,p,\dots,p)\in[1,\infty)^{n},
then the space

(L^{\overrightarrow{P}}(Y),\|\cdot\|_{\overrightarrow{P}})
reduces to the classical Lebesgue space
(L^{p}(Y),\|\cdot\|_{p})
. In the mixed norm Lebesgue space, for
f\in L^{\overrightarrow{P}}(Y)
and
g\in L^{\overrightarrow{Q}}(Y)
, the
Hölder’s inequality

[
5
]
states that

\|fg\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{\overrightarrow{P}}\|g\|_{\overrightarrow{Q}},

where
{\overrightarrow{P}=(p_{1},...,p_{n})\in[1,\infty]^{n}}
and
{\overrightarrow{Q}=(q_{1},...,q_{n})\in[1,\infty]^{n}}
, where each pair satisfies
\frac{1}{p_{k}}+\frac{1}{q_{k}}=1
for each
k=1,...,n
. In the subsequent analysis, we assume that

p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\dots\leq p_{n}
.

Let us note that the sequence space
\ell^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
equipped with a mixed norm structures is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\ell^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\left\{x=(x_{\textbf{k}})_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}:\|x\|_{\ell^{\overrightarrow{P}}}<\infty\right\},

where

\|x\|_{\ell^{\overrightarrow{P}}}:=\bigg(\sum_{k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\cdots\bigg(\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|x(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n})\right|^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{3}}{p_{2}}}\cdots\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}.

2.2
The Orlicz spaces

A convex and lower continuous function
\phi
from
[0,\infty)
to
[0,\infty]
is termed as a Orlicz function if it fulfills the following conditions:

i.

\lim\limits_{u\to\infty}\phi(u)=\infty
, and
\phi(0)=0
,

ii.

\phi(u)>0
for every
u>0
, and is non-decreasing on
[0,\infty)
.

It is important to note that
\phi
may not be continuous since they allow
\infty
as a function value. For instance, for
b>0

\phi(u)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }u\leq b\\
\infty,&\text{if }u>b.\end{cases}

is an Orlicz function.

We begin by defining the Orlicz space associated with the Orlicz function
\phi.

For any
f\in M(\Omega)
, the functional
I^{\phi}
associated with an Orlicz function
\phi
is defined as

I^{\phi}[f]:=\int_{\Omega}\phi(|f(x)|)dx.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\phi
be an Orlicz function. Then the Orlicz space generated by
\phi
is given as

L^{\phi}(\Omega)=\left\{f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\hskip 5.0pt\text{measurable}:I^{\phi}(\lambda f)<\infty\hskip 4.0pt\text{for some }\lambda>0\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{\phi}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\phi\left(\frac{{|f(x)|}}{\lambda}\right)d\mu\leq 1\right\}.

Several notable examples of Orlicz functions, along with their generated Orlicz spaces, are outlined below.

I.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
, and define
\phi(y)=y^{p}
when
p\in[1,\infty).

For
p=\infty
set

\phi(y)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{when }y\leq 1,\\
\infty,&\text{when }y>1.\end{cases}

This generates the classical Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty.

II.

\phi(y)=(y-1)\chi_{[1,\infty)}(y)
, associated with the combined space
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

III.

\phi(y)=y\log y
, which defines the logarithmic space
L\log L(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

IV.

\phi(y)=\exp(y^{a})-1
for
a>0
, leading to Exponential spaces
\exp L^{a}
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
\Delta_{2}-
condition)
The
\Delta_{2}-
condition for an Orlicz function
\phi
is satisfied if there exists a constant
K>0
, such that

\phi(2u)\leq K\phi(u),\quad\forall u>0.

Examples of Orlicz functions that satisfy the
\Delta_{2}-
condition are
\displaystyle\phi(u)=u(\log(e+u))
and
\displaystyle\phi(u)=u^{p},
for
1\leq p<\infty
. However,
\phi(u)=(e^{u}-u-1)
does not belong to
\Delta_{2}
.

Definition 2.4
.

(Jensen’s integral inequality)
The Jensen’s integral inequality satisfies for any
f\in L^{\phi}(\Omega)
if the following inequality is holds

\phi\left(\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx}{\operatorname{mes}(\Omega)}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\operatorname{mes}(\Omega)}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi(f(x))\,dx.

2.3
The Mixed norm Orlicz spaces

Assume that
(\Omega_{i},\Sigma_{i},\mu_{i})
are measure spaces for
1\leq i\leq n
, where
\Omega_{i}
is a set,
\Sigma_{i}
is a
\sigma
-algebra of
\Omega_{i}
and
\mu_{i}
is a
\sigma
-additive measure on
\Sigma_{i}
. Let
\Omega=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{i}
.
First we establish the fundamental definitions and properties of mixed norm Orlicz spaces for
\overrightarrow{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
, and subsequently, we will extend this framework for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the mixed norm Orlicz space for
\overrightarrow{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle L^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=L^{\phi_{2}}(\Omega_{2},L^{\phi_{1}}(\Omega_{1}))

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{f:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow L^{\phi_{1}}(\Omega_{1})\hskip 1.0pt\text{ measurable on}\hskip 4.0pt(\Omega_{2},\Sigma_{2},\mu_{2}):\int_{\Omega_{2}}\phi_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\phi_{1}\left(\alpha|f(x_{1},x_{2})|\right)d\mu_{1}\right)d\mu_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt<\infty\hskip 4.0pt\text{for some}\hskip 4.0pt\alpha>0\Bigg\}.

The norm associated with
L^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}(\Omega)
is given as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}=\inf\left\{\alpha>0:\int_{\Omega_{2}}\phi_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\phi_{1}\left(\frac{|f(x_{1},x_{2})|}{\alpha}\right)d\mu_{1}\right)d\mu_{2}\leq 1\right\}.

Analogously, the mixed norm Orlicz space for
\overrightarrow{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},...,\phi_{n})
is defined as

\displaystyle L^{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=L^{\phi_{n}}\left(\Omega_{n},L^{\phi_{n-1},\dots,\phi_{1}}(\Omega_{n-1},\dots,\Omega_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{f:\Omega_{n}\to L^{\phi_{n-1},\dots,\phi_{1}}(\Omega_{n-1},\dots,\Omega_{1})\text{ measurable on }(\Omega_{n},\Sigma_{n},\mu_{n}):

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\Omega_{n}}\phi_{n}\Bigg(\int_{\Omega_{n-1}}\phi_{n-1}\cdots\Bigg(\int_{\Omega_{2}}\phi_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\phi_{1}\left(\alpha|f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\right)d\mu_{1}\right)d\mu_{2}\Bigg)\cdots d\mu_{n-1}\Bigg)d\mu_{n}

\displaystyle\quad<\infty\text{ for some }\alpha>0\Bigg\},

specified by the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}=\inf\left\{\alpha>0:\int_{\Omega_{n}}\phi_{n}\cdots\Bigg(\int_{\Omega_{2}}\phi_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\phi_{1}\left(\frac{|f(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})|}{\alpha}\right)d\mu_{1}\right)d\mu_{2}\Bigg)\cdots d\mu_{n}\leq 1\right\}.

We now define a functional to establish our main results. For Orlicz functions
\phi_{i},1\leq i\leq n
and
\overrightarrow{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},...,\phi_{n})
, the functional

I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}:M(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is defined as follows :

I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{n}\cdots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{1}\left(|f(x_{1},...,x_{n})|\right)d\mu_{1}\right)d\mu_{2}\cdots\right)d\mu_{n}.

The functional
I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}
is termed a modular functional if it satisfies the following conditions for every
f,g\in M(\mathbb{R}^{n}):

I.

I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(f)=0
if and only if
f=0,

II.

I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(-f)=I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(f),

III.

I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\gamma f+\beta g)\leq I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(f)+I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(g),\hskip 3.0ptfor\hskip 3.0pt\gamma,\beta\geq 0,\hskip 3.0pt\gamma+\beta=1.

For any
f\in M(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
the functional
I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\alpha f)
is non-decreasing for
\alpha>0
. Further, the mixed norm Orlicz space induced by
\overrightarrow{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},....\phi_{n})
is as follows :

L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{f\right.\in M(\mathbb{R}^{n}):I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\lambda f)<\infty\hskip 3.0pt\text{for some}\hskip 2.0pt\lambda>0\left.\right\}.

The notion of modular convergence in
L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
plays an important role. In this context, a net of functions
(f_{k})_{k>0}\subset L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to modularly convergent to a function
f\in L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\lim_{k\to\infty}I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\lambda(f_{k}-f))=0

for some
\lambda>0.
Also, the norm
\|f\|_{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}
induces norm-convergence in
L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
characterized by the property that
\|f_{k}-f\|_{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}\to 0
if and only if
I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\lambda(f_{k}-f))\to 0
for every
\lambda>0
. Moreover, norm convergence and modular convergence are equivalent if and only if
\overrightarrow{\Phi}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. We say that
\overrightarrow{\Phi}=(\phi_{1},...,\phi_{n})
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
-condition, if each
\phi_{i}
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

The mixed norm Orlicz space
L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}
introduced in this paper generalizes the mixed norm Lebesgue space
L^{\overrightarrow{P}}
under the condition
p_{i}\leq p_{i+1}
, with the choice
\phi_{1}=x^{p_{1}}
and
\phi_{i}=x^{\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i-1}}}
, for
2\leq i\leq n
. Throughout the paper, we work within the framework of such mixed norm Lebesgue spaces
L^{\overrightarrow{P}}
to investigate the approximation properties of generalized sampling series and Kantorovich type sampling series.

A function is said to be a kernel

, is bounded

For any

\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\textbf{u}-\textbf{k})=1.

(3.1)

2.

Absolute moment of order

m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}):=\sup_{\textbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\textbf{u}-\textbf{k})|<+\infty.

(3.2)

Definition 3.1
.

(Relatively separated set)
A countable set
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined to be
relatively separated

A_{\Gamma}(\kappa):=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{B\left(\gamma;\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)}(x)\geq 1,\quad

The Shannon sampling series is limited by the assumption of band-limited signals

For

S_{w}(f)(\textbf{x})=\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f\left(\frac{\textbf{k}}{w}\right)\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(w\textbf{x}-\textbf{k}).

The multivariate generalized sampling series in (

\displaystyle\Delta^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{f\in M(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\left\lVert(f(x_{\textbf{k}}))\right\rVert_{\ell^{\overrightarrow{P}}_{w}}<\infty\text{ for each relatively separated set }(x_{\textbf{k}})_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\right\},

\|f(x_{\textbf{k}})\|_{\ell^{\overrightarrow{P}}_{w}}:=\bigg(\frac{1}{w}\sum_{k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\cdots\bigg(\frac{1}{w}\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\frac{1}{w}\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|f\left(x_{k_{1}},\dots,x_{k_{n}}\right)\right|^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{3}}{p_{2}}}\cdots\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}.

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt+\Bigg(\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigg(\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigg(\frac{1}{1+k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}}\Bigg)^{p_{1}}\Bigg)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{p_{2}}}

Theorem 3.3
.

The generalized sampling operator
S_{w}:\Delta^{\overrightarrow{P}}\left(\subset L^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)\rightarrow L^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies

\|S_{w}f\|_{\overrightarrow{P}}\leq(m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}))^{1-\frac{1}{p_{n}}}\hskip 2.0pt\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}\|_{\underbrace{\scriptstyle{(1,...,1)}}_{n\text{times}}}^{\frac{1}{p_{n}}}\left\|f\left(\frac{\textbf{k}}{w}\right)\right\|_{\ell^{\overrightarrow{P}}_{w}}.

To begin, we verifying the result for
n=2.
Consider

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg|\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\,(m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}))^{p_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(wx_{1}-k_{1},wx_{2}-k_{2})}{m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$})}\left|f\left(\frac{k_{1}}{w},\frac{k_{2}}{w}\right)\right|^{p_{1}}dx_{1}

Since
p_{1}\leq p_{2}

\displaystyle\|S_{w}f\|_{(p_{1},p_{2})}^{p_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$})^{p_{2}-\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\cdot,wx_{2}-k_{2})\|_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 108.405pt\times\bigg(\frac{1}{w}\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\cdot,wx_{2}-k_{2})\|_{1}}{\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\cdot,wx_{2}-k_{2})\|_{1}}\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|f\left(\frac{k_{1}}{w},\frac{k_{2}}{w}\right)\right|^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\,dx_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 108.405pt\times\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\cdot,wx_{2}-k_{2})\|_{1}\bigg(\frac{1}{w}\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|f\left(\frac{k_{1}}{w},\frac{k_{2}}{w}\right)\right|^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\,dx_{2}.

0

\displaystyle\|S_{w}f\|_{(p_{1},p_{2})}^{p_{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$})^{p_{2}-1}}{w}\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}\|_{(1,1)}\sum_{k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\frac{1}{w}\sum_{k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|f\left(\frac{k_{1}}{w},\frac{k_{2}}{w}\right)\right|^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}}\,

1\leq j\leq n

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\cdots\sum_{k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\cdot,\cdots,\cdot,wx_{j}-k_{j},\dots,wx_{n}-k_{n})\|_{(1,\dots,1)}

Proceeding in a similar manner for any

This completes the proof.

In the next section, we discuss the well-known family

4
Kantorovich type sampling operators on mixed norm Lebesgue space

In this section, we investigate the boundedness and convergence of Kantorovich type sampling operators. It is noteworthy that the convergence of

Definition 4.1

It is well established that
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
L^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However, to the best of our knowledge, it has not been proven that
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

To thoroughly explain this idea, we aim to provide detailed information as described below.

Lemma 5.3
.

The space
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

Our first step is to validate the result for
n=2.

To establish the desired approximation, we demonstrate that any simple function can be closely approximated by a sequence of compactly supported continuous functions.
Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a measurable set. For each
m\in\mathbb{N},
there exist a sequence of compact set
(U_{m})
and sequence of open sets
(V_{m})
such that
U_{m}\subset S\subset V_{m}
and
\mu(V_{m}\setminus U_{m})<1/m.
By Urysohn’s lemma, there exists
g_{m}\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

g_{m}(\textbf{x})=\begin{cases}1,&\text{for }\textbf{x}\in U_{m},\\
0,&\text{for }\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus V_{m}.\end{cases}

It follows directly that for all
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},
the function
f_{m}
satisfies

\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{U_{m}}(\textbf{x})\leq g_{m}(\textbf{x})\leq\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{V_{m}}(\textbf{x}).

This implies that
0\leq|g_{m}(\textbf{x})-\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{U_{m}}(\textbf{x})|\leq\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{V_{m}\setminus U_{m}}(\textbf{x})
for all
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.
Thus for
\lambda>0
, we have

I^{\overrightarrow{{\Phi}}}(\lambda(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{S}-g_{m}))\leq I^{\overrightarrow{{\Phi}}}(\lambda(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{V_{m}}-\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{U_{m}}))\leq I^{\overrightarrow{{\Phi}}}(\lambda(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{V_{m}\setminus U_{m}})).

Since
|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{V_{m}\setminus U_{m}}(\textbf{x})|\leq 1
for all
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and it converges to
0
almost everywhere, the dominated convergence theorem for mixed norm (
[
5
]
, Section 2) implies that
I^{\overrightarrow{{\Phi}}}(\lambda(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{V_{m}\setminus U_{m}}))\to 0
as
m\to\infty.

This establishes that
I^{\overrightarrow{{\Phi}}}(\lambda(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{S}-g_{m}))\to 0.

Using density of simple function in
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for given
\epsilon>0
and a simple function
h:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, there exists a compactly supported continuous function
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

I^{\overrightarrow{{\Phi}}}(2\lambda(g-h))<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Now we need to show that the class of simple functions is dense in
L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. If
f\in L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is arbitrary,
then there exists a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of non-negative and increasing simple functions such that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
|f|
pointwise, so that for each
\lambda>0
,
(\lambda f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. By using
dominated convergence theorem
for mixed norm, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{1}\left(\lambda|(|f|-f_{n})(x_{1},x_{2})|\right)dx_{1}\right)dx_{2}

approaches to
zero
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
since

\phi_{1}(\lambda f)\geq\phi_{1}(\lambda(|f|-f_{n})).

In other words, for any
f\in L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a simple function
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(2\lambda(f-g))<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

In view of property of the convex modular functional
I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}
, we get
I^{\overrightarrow{{\Phi}}}(\lambda(f-h))<\epsilon.

This establishes the result. Now following along the similar lines, one can extend this proof for any
n\in\mathbb{N}.

\Box

Now in the subsequent theorem we examine the convergence of
(K_{w}f)
in mixed norm Orlicz space.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
f\in L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it follows that

\lim_{w\to\infty}I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\lambda(K_{w}f-f)=0.

Proof.

We prove the result using the denseness of
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as established in Lemma
5.3
. Let
f\in L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\epsilon>0
. In view of Lemma
5.3
, there exists
g\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\gamma>0
such that

I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\gamma(f-g))<\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2\left(1+\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}\|_{(1,\dots,1)}/\left(m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$})\right)^{n}\right)}
. Consider

\lambda>0
such that
3\lambda(1+\left(m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$})\right)^{n})\leq\gamma.

By utilizing the property of the convex modular functional
I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}
with Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
, we obtain

\begin{split}I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\lambda(K_{w}f-f)&\leq I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(3\lambda(g-f))+I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(3\lambda(K_{w}g-g))+I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(3\lambda(K_{w}f-K_{w}g))\\
&<\left(1+\frac{\|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}\|_{(1,...,1)}}{\left(m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$})\right)^{n}}\right)I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(3\lambda(g-f))+I^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(3\lambda(g-K_{w}g))\\
&<{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}+{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}=\epsilon,\end{split}

\forall\hskip 1.0ptw\geq N_{0}
, for sufficiently large
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}.

\Box

With this we have established the boundedness and convergence of a family of sampling operators in the proposed mixed norm spaces.

Examples of kernel

In our theoretical framework kernels hold significant importance.
In this section, we present several examples of suitable kernel functions that satisfy assumptions (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) of our theory. Assume that each
\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{i}
is a kernel satisfying these assumptions.
For all
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{k_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}}\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{i}({u_{i}}-{k_{i}})=1,

for each
i=1,..,n
.
Next, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\textbf{u})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{i}(u_{i}).

Thus, one can observe that

\chi
belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\textbf{u})|d\textbf{u}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{i}(u_{i})|du_{1}...du_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{i}(u_{i})|du_{i}<\infty,

m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$})=\sup_{\textbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\textbf{u}-\textbf{k})|=\prod_{i=1}^{n}m_{0}(\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{i})<+\infty.

Further

\sum_{\textbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\textbf{u}-\textbf{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{i}(u_{i}-k_{i})=1,

for every
\textbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Now, we outline some equivalent conditions related to (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and the Fourier transform of the kernel.

Remark 6.1
.

(

6.1
Fejér kernel

Fejér univariate kernel is defined as follows

\widehat{\text{sinc}}(v)=\begin{cases}{1}&\quad\text{}\ \ {|v|\leq\pi},\\
{0,}&\quad\text{}\ \ {|v|>\pi}.\\

Indeed

is bounded and satisfies the condition (
3.2

\beta=1

6.1
(a). Also, the Fourier transform of

\widehat{F}(v)=\begin{cases}{1-|\frac{v}{\pi}|},&\quad\text{}\ \ {v\leq\pi},\\
{0,}&\quad\text{}\ \ {v>\pi}.\\
\end{cases}

In view of Remark
6.1
(b), one can say that F complies with assumption (
3.1
).
Consider,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\textbf{x})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}F(x_{i})

\mathbf{\acute{e}}
r’s kernel and it is satisfies the condition upon generalized kernel (see

Jackson kernel

The Jackson kernel with parameters

The B-spline kernel of order

7
Conclusion

This article aims to study the approximation properties of a family of sampling operators within the framework of mixed norm function spaces. To analyze the boundedness of generalized sampling operators in mixed norm Lebesgue spaces, we have introduced an appropriate subspace of
L^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, denoted by
\Delta^{\overrightarrow{P}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, in Section
3
.
Further, we establish the boundedness and convergence of Kantorovich type sampling operators within the framework of mixed norm Lebesgue spaces
and mixed norm Orlicz spaces. In order to prove these results, we have established a fundamental result, namely a density of compactly supported continnuous functions
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in mixed norm Orlicz space
L^{\overrightarrow{\Phi}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

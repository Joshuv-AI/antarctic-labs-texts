Entanglement, Coherence, and Recursive Linking in Dicke states: A Topological Perspective
Sougata Bhattacharyya Department of Astronomy, Astrophysics and Space Engineering,Indian Institute of Technology, Indore, 453552, Madhya Pradesh, India, Sovik Roy Department of Mathematics, Techno Main Salt Lake,Techno India Group, EM 4/1, Sector V, Salt Lake, Kolkata 700091, India

Abstract
This work investigates the topological structure of multipartite entanglement in symmetric Dicke states | D n ( k ) ⟩ |D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle . By viewing qubits as topological loops, we establish a direct correspondence between the recursive measurement dynamics of Dicke states and the stability of n n -Hopf links. We utilize the Schmidt rank to quantify bipartite entanglement resilience and introduce the l 1 l_{1} -norm of quantum coherence as a measure of link fluidity . We demonstrate that unlike fragile states such as | G H Z ⟩ \left|GHZ\right\rangle (analogous to Borromean rings), Dicke states exhibit a robust, self-similar topology where local measurements preserve the global linking structure through non-vanishing residual coherence.

Theoretical Preliminaries

In this section, we outline the fundamental concepts necessary for our analysis. We review the definition of Dicke states, the formalism of projective measurements
[
22

, and the two key metrics used to quantify the resulting states viz. Schmidt rank and Quantum Coherence. Finally, we establish the relevant topological definitions

2.1
The Dicke State
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle

The Dicke state
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle
, an
n-
qubit quantum state, is defined as the normalized equal superposition of all computational basis states with exactly
k
excitations (denoted as logical
|1\rangle
s) and
n-k
ground states (denoted as logical
|0\rangle
s). Mathematically, it is expressed as:

\displaystyle|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\binom{n}{k}^{-1/2}\sum_{j}P_{j}\left(|1\rangle^{\otimes k}\otimes|0\rangle^{\otimes(n-k)}\right),

(1)

where
\sum P_{j}
denotes the sum over all distinct permutations of the qubits. As for example, when
n=3
and
k=1
, the Dicke state is
|D_{3}^{(1)}\rangle
which is
\frac{|001\rangle+|010\rangle+|100\rangle}{\sqrt{3}}
i.e. the
|W\rangle
state
[
14
]
. It is easy to check that swapping any two qubits leaves the state unchanged and hence it is permutation symmetric. Another example of Dicke state is inverted
|W\rangle
state that corresponds to
|D_{3}^{(2)}\rangle
[the inverted
|W\rangle
state (
|\overline{W}\rangle
) is essentially the bit-flipped version of the
|W\rangle
state and it is written as
|\overline{W}\rangle=X\otimes X\otimes X|W\rangle
, where
X
is Pauli-
X
gate]. The fundamental property of these states is their permutation symmetry while no single qubit plays a privileged role, contrasting sharply with that of graph states like the
|Star\rangle
state.

2.2
Projective Measurements

A projective measurement on a single qubit is described by the orthogonal projectors
P_{0}=|0\rangle\langle 0|
and
P_{1}=|1\rangle\langle 1|

[
22
]
. For a multipartite state
|\psi\rangle
, the probability of obtaining outcome
m\in\{0,1\}
on a specific qubit is given by the Born rule:

\displaystyle p(m)=\langle\psi|P_{m}|\psi\rangle.

(2)

The post-measurement state of the remaining system,
|\psi^{(m)}\rangle
, is obtained by projecting and renormalizing and is given as

\displaystyle|\psi^{(m)}\rangle=\frac{P_{m}|\psi\rangle}{\sqrt{p(m)}}.

(3)

In our topological analysis, this measurement process is the quantum analogue of
cutting

[
21
]
or
removing a ring from a link
. The properties of
|\psi^{(m)}\rangle
determine the stability of the remaining topological structure
[
25
]
.

2.3
Schmidt Rank as an Entanglement Measure

To determine if the remaining qubits stay linked after a measurement, we utilize the Schmidt decomposition
[
23
,
22
]
. A pure bipartite state
|\phi\rangle_{AB}
can be written as:

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle_{AB}=\sum_{i=1}^{R}\lambda_{i}|u_{i}\rangle_{A}\otimes|v_{i}\rangle_{B},

(4)

where
\lambda_{i}>0
are the Schmidt coefficients satisfying
\sum\lambda_{i}^{2}=1
. The integer
R
is the
Schmidt Rank
.

•

If
R=1
, the state is
separable
(product state).

•

If
R>1
, the state is
entangled

[
17
]
.

•

If
R=2
with
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
, it indicates a
maximally entangled
bipartite state (such as Bell states).

2.4
Quantum Coherence

While Schmidt rank indicates the
existence
of entanglement, quantum coherence shows the
strength
of the superposition that supports it. It is to be noted that, Quantum Coherence is basis-dependent, i.e., when coherence is measured in different bases, the quantified value is different
[
27
]
. In this work, we have quantified coherence in the computational basis. To quantify coherence, we employ the
l_{1}
-norm measure of coherence. While multiple coherence measures exist, the
l_{1}
-norm offers both theoretical relevance and computational tractability. Now, for a density matrix
\rho
, the measure
l_{1}-
norm is defined as the sum of the absolute values of the off-diagonal elements
[
27
]
. This measure was rigorously quantified within the resource theoretic framework by Baumgratz et al.
[
8
]
. Denoting
l_{1}-
norm of coherence by
C_{l_{1}}(\rho)
, for a density operator
\rho
with respect to a given state the said norm is defined as,

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}(\rho)=\sum_{i\neq j}|\rho_{ij}|.

(5)

For a pure state
|\psi\rangle=\sum c_{i}|i\rangle
, the Eq.(
5
) simplifies to the following form (see Appendix
A
for the detailed derivation):

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}(|\psi\rangle)=\left(\sum_{i}|c_{i}|\right)^{2}-1.

(6)

2.6
Link Fluidity: The Mechanism of Topological Preservation

Although the Schmidt rank provides a binary diagnostic for the presence of entanglement (i.e., linked vs. unlinked), it does not explain the
mechanism
that preserves this structure under the destructive operation of measurement. To describe this mechanism, we introduce the concept of
Link Fluidity
or
Fluidity
.

2.6.1
Link Fluidity via Quantum Coherence

In classical knot theory, a physical link is often viewed as a static, localized connection between components
[
2
]
. However, in the quantum regime of Dicke states, the
link
is not a localized bond but a
delocalized correlation
distributed across the entire Hilbert space. We define
Link Fluidity
(
\mathcal{F}
) as the capacity of the entangled structure to redistribute its correlations dynamically upon the loss of a subsystem. Mathematically, this is quantified by the
l_{1}
-norm of quantum coherence, expressed in the computational basis as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\equiv C_{l_{1}}(|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle)=\binom{n}{k}-1.

(7)

2.6.2
Conceptual Framework: Rigid vs. Fluid Entanglement

To facilitate our topological analysis, we introduce a theoretical distinction between two regimes of multipartite entanglement, governed by the magnitude of their quantum coherence. We propose that the stability of a topological link under measurement is determined by the
fluidity
of its support in the Hilbert space
[
12
]
. From Eq.(
7
), it is clear that the link fluidity or fluidity of a multipartite quantum state is directly proportional to coherence of the state.

•

Rigid Entanglement in the low coherence regime:

We characterize states with very little quantum overlap as possessing
rigid entanglement
. In this scenario, the connection between particles relies on just a few specific links. It is like a bridge supported by only a single pillar. It is a brittle structure where everything depends on that one support holding up. Because there are no extra supports or backups, the structure cannot handle any local
damage
. Consequently, if you remove just one part (via measurement), the entire connection breaks, and the system instantly falls apart
[
25
]
.

•

Fluid Entanglement in the high coherence regime:

On the other hand, we characterize states with high quantum overlap as possessing
fluid entanglement
. In this regime, the connection is spread out across a vast network of many different links. It behaves less like a stiff chain and more like a spiderweb or a fluid mesh. This setup has high
redundancy
, meaning there are thousands of alternative pathways connecting the system. We believe this flexibility allows the connection to survive if one part is lost. Just as water flows to fill a gap, the remaining links redistribute the load, keeping the system connected even after a part is removed
[
28
]
.

2.7
The Operational-Topological Framework

To analyze the structural stability of Dicke states of Eq.(
1
), we establish a unified framework connecting quantum operations to topological concepts. We propose that the following four elements offer complementary perspectives on the same underlying physical reality:

1.

Linking (The Geometry):

This provides the qualitative model. It describes the global connectivity pattern of the qubits (e.g.,
n
-Hopf link vs. Borromean rings) and defines the system’s structural class
[
18
]
.

2.

Projective Measurement (The Surgery):
This serves as the
operational probe

[
22
]
. In this framework, the quantum collapse of a single qubit maps directly to the topological deletion of a single loop. Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\text{proj}}\iff\text{Topological Component Deletion (Cutting)}.

(8)

This operation acts as a
stress test
, revealing whether the entanglement structure is
robust
(recursive) or
fragile
(catastrophic).

3.

Schmidt Rank (The Indicator):
This serves as the
existence condition
for the residual link after the surgery is performed
[
24
]
.

R=1\iff\text{Link Broken (Fragile/Borromean)}

(9)

R>1\iff\text{Link Preserved (Robust/Hopf)}

(10)

4.

Quantum Coherence (The Stability Measure):
This serves as the
mechanism
of
Link Fluidity
. It quantifies the delocalization of the link across the Hilbert space, explaining
why
the connection survives the surgery
[
27
]
.

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}\gg 0\implies\text{Fluid Entanglement (Recursive Preservation)}

(11)

High coherence implies that the
links
between qubits are not rigid but fluidly distributed across the system, contributing to topological robustness.

3
Mathematical Analysis of the General Dicke State
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle

Now, we analyze the properties of the general Dicke state before any measurement is performed. We derive its specific algebraic form and calculate its initial quantum coherence, establishing a baseline for the topological analysis.

3.1
Initial State Properties

We consider a general Dicke state describing a system of
n
qubits sharing
k
excitations. As defined in the literature
[
3
]
and our preliminary notes, the state
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle
is the symmetric superposition of all distinct permutations of
k
excitations (
|1\rangle
) and
n-k
ground states (
|0\rangle
).

Mathematically, the state is expressed as:

\displaystyle|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}\sum_{j}P_{j}\left(|1\rangle^{\otimes k}\otimes|0\rangle^{\otimes(n-k)}\right).

(12)

where the summation runs over all distinct permutations
P_{j}
of the qubits. We defined this state earlier in Eq.(
1
), yet we would like to write it once more in this section.

•

The total number of terms in the superposition corresponds to the binomial coefficient
\binom{n}{k}
.

•

The normalization factor is
\mathcal{N}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}
.

•

In the computational basis, every basis state appearing in the superposition has an identical probability amplitude of
c_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}
.

3.2
Calculation of Initial Quantum Coherence

We now quantify the
link fluidity
of this topological structure by calculating the
l_{1}
-norm of coherence. Using the formula given in Eq.(
6
)
1
1

1

derived in Appendix A for pure states
we proceed as follows:

Basis Coefficients:

The state contains
\binom{n}{k}
computational basis states. For each such state
|i\rangle
, the coefficient is:

\displaystyle c_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}.

(13)

Sum of Absolute Coefficients:

Summing over all
\binom{n}{k}
terms:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}|c_{i}|=\binom{n}{k}\times\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}\right)=\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}.

(14)

Coherence:

Substituting this sum into the coherence formula of Eq.(
6
):

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}(|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle)=\left(\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}\right)^{2}-1=\binom{n}{k}-1.

(15)

Result:

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}(|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle)=\binom{n}{k}-1.

(16)

Topologically, this magnitude of Eq.(
16
) represents the
total capacity of the
n
-Hopf link
to distribute stress (or loss) across its components. The maximum coherence occurs at
k\approx n/2
, corresponding to the most robustly linked configuration.

Schmidt Rank Analysis

We analyze the entanglement across the bipartite cut between qubit

4.2.1
Boundary Cases (

•

•

If

|1\rangle^{\otimes n}

4.2.2
General Case (
0<k<n
)

For any intermediate number of excitations, the state can be decomposed as a superposition of two orthogonal branches:

\displaystyle|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{n-k}{n}}|0_{1}\rangle\otimes|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}|1_{1}\rangle\otimes|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle.

(17)

|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle

total excitation counts

23
]
.

•

There are exactly two non-zero Schmidt coefficients:
\lambda_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{n-k}{n}}
and
\lambda_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}
.

•

Therefore, the Schmidt Rank is strictly
R=2
.

•

This confirms that for any
0<k<n
, the measured qubit is entangled with the rest of the system.

4.3
Quantum Coherence Analysis

The Quantum Coherence (
l_{1}
-norm) measures the superposition strength of the state in the computational basis.

•

Initial Coherence:
Before measurement, the coherence is
C_{l_{1}}=\binom{n}{k}-1
.

•

Residual Coherence (when measurement outcome is
|0\rangle
):
The remaining state is
|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle
. The coherence becomes
\binom{n-1}{k}-1
.

•

Residual Coherence (when measurement outcome is
|1\rangle
):
The remaining state is
|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle
. The coherence becomes
\binom{n-1}{k-1}-1
.

Crucially, in the entangled regime (
0<k<n
), both residual coherence values are strictly positive, indicating that the remaining system retains quantum superposition
[
28
]
.

5
Topological Analogy: The Recursive
n
-Hopf Link

The defining feature of the Dicke state
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle
is its complete permutation symmetry where every qubit contributes identically to the global entanglement structure. Translating this into topology, we view each qubit as a topological loop (or ring). Unlike asymmetric states such as the Star configuration
[
12
]
, where specific qubits play central or peripheral roles, the Dicke state corresponds to a system of
n
identical loops, all mutually interlinked with equal strength. We identify this structure as an
n
-Hopf link
. In a topological sense, the entire system can be pictured as
n
loops where there is no privileged connection between any specific pair; instead, the
linking
is uniform across the manifold
[
18
]
.

5.1
Excitation Number
k
as Link Density

The integer
k
, representing the number of excitations in the Dicke state, acts as a control parameter for how entanglement is distributed across the qubits. Topologically, we interpret
k
as the
Linking Density
among the loops.

5.1.1
The Separable Regime (
k=0
and
k=n
): Zero Link Density

At the extreme values, the state occupies
|0\rangle^{\otimes n}
or
|1\rangle^{\otimes n}
. In these configurations, there are no superpositions and no shared excitations.

•

Topological Interpretation:
This corresponds to
n
completely disjoint and unlinked loops. Each loop exists independently, with no crossings or linkages between them
[
2
]
.

•

Quantum State Properties:
The Schmidt Rank is 1, and the Quantum Coherence is zero (
C_{l_{1}}=0
). Removing or manipulating one loop has no effect on the others, representing the
zero link density
configuration.

5.1.2
The Linked Regime (
0<k<n
): The
n
-Hopf Link Structure

For any non-extreme excitation number, the state becomes a superposition where excitations are delocalized across different qubits. This shared delocalization gives rise to non-trivial quantum correlations among all subsystems.

•

Topological Interpretation:
The loops are now interlinked. The resulting structure is an
n
-Hopf-Linked configuration, where every pair is symmetrically linked
[
20
]
.

•

Link Fluidity:
We have already introduced the concept of link fluidity in sec.
2.6.1
which will be needed to describe this connection. Unlike a static chain, the linking is mediated by the
quantum coherence
(
C_{l_{1}}>0
). The links are
fluidly
distributed across the system, allowing the structure to absorb the loss of a component without total collapse.

5.1.3
Maximally Dense Linking (
k\approx\frac{n}{2}
)

As derived in Section 4, the state achieves maximal bipartite entanglement when
k=n/2
.

•

Topological Interpretation:
This corresponds to the point in the family of links where the connectivity is maximally dense. The connection between any single ring and the collective of the others is as strong as possible.

•

Resilience:
The entire structure remains highly resilient to the loss of any single component, as the remaining rings are supported by the maximum possible combinatorial coherence
\binom{n}{\frac{n}{2}}-1
.

Thus, the excitation number
k
serves as a tunable parameter for the topological density of the system, illustrated in Table

In summary, the Dicke state
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle
is the quantum embodiment of a robust, fluidly linked

5.2
Quantum coherence as link fluidity

While the Schmidt rank indicates that the links persist, the quantum coherence (
C_{l_{1}}
) explains how they persist. We have already introduced the concept of Link Fluidity in the sec.
2.6.1
and it have been expressed in the Eq.(
16
) as
C_{l_{1}}(|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle)=\binom{n}{k}-1
. In a static topological link (like a steel chain), the connection is localized
[
26
]
. However, in the quantum Dicke state, the
linking
is mediated by the delocalization of the
k
excitations across the
n
qubits. The coherence measure
C_{l_{1}}
quantifies this delocalization.

We interpret the binomial coefficient
\binom{n}{k}
as the
Topological Volume
of the state which is
the number of distinct pathways through which the qubits are connected.

•

High Coherence (
k\approx\frac{n}{2}
):
In this situation, the fluidity is maximal. The links are distributed across a vast combinatorial space, making the global entanglement highly robust against the loss of a single site.

•

Residual Coherence:
Crucially, our derivation shows that after measurement, the residual coherence
C_{l_{1}}^{\prime}=\binom{n-1}{k^{\prime}}-1
remains strictly positive (for
0<k^{\prime}<n-1
). This non-vanishing coherence acts as the
topological glue
, preventing the remaining loops from drifting apart into a product state.

5.3
Topological Stability and Recursive Preservation

A remarkable property of Dicke states is their stability under local measurements. Topologically, a projective measurement corresponds to
cutting
or
removing
one loop from the link configuration. For the
|GHZ\rangle
state (analogous to Borromean rings
[
6
]
), cutting one ring destroys the entire structure. However, for the Dicke state, this operation does not destroy the overall linkage. Instead, it results in a smaller but topologically similar connectivity pattern. This recursive preservation gives rise to a self-similar hierarchy:

\displaystyle\text{n-loop Dicke Link}\xrightarrow{\text{Cut}}\text{(n-1)-loop Dicke Link}\xrightarrow{\text{Cut}}\text{(n-2)-loop Dicke Link}\xrightarrow{\text{Cut}}\dots

(18)

Dicke states thus realize a
self-replicating topological family
. This is analogous to a recursive link construction in knot theory, where successive removal of components still leaves smaller, intact links. This behavior is mathematically guaranteed by the non-vanishing residual coherence derived in our analysis, which ensures the topological glue persists even after surgery.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have presented a comprehensive topological classification of symmetric Dicke states
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle
, establishing them as the quantum mechanical analogue of the
n-
component Hopf link. By integrating concepts from knot theory with quantum information measures, we have provided an operational framework that distinguishes the robust connectivity of Dicke states from the fragile Borromean entanglement characterizing GHZ states. Our analysis relied on two complementary quantifiers: the Schmidt rank and the
l_{1}
-norm of quantum coherence. The Schmidt rank analysis confirmed that, unlike the GHZ state where the loss of a single particle severs all connections (collapsing the system to a product state), the Dicke state maintains a bipartite Schmidt rank of
R=2
for all excitation numbers
0<k<n
under single-qubit projective measurement. This mathematically validates the topological persistence of the link. Furthermore, we identified quantum coherence as the physical mechanism underlying this robustness, introducing the concept of link fluidity. We demonstrated that the stability of the topological link is not intrinsic to rigid pairwise bonds, but is rather an emergent property of the delocalized superposition. The derived coherence,
C_{l_{1}}=\binom{n}{k}-1
, serves as a measure of the system’s capacity to redistribute correlations. We found that this fluidity is maximized when the excitation density is balanced (
k\approx\frac{n}{2}
), corresponding to the most densely woven topological structure. Finally, we showed that the measurement dynamics of Dicke states exhibit a recursive self-similarity. The
surgical removal
of a qubit transforms an
n-
qubit Dicke state into a superposition of
(n-1)-
qubit Dicke states, preserving the fundamental linking pattern. This suggests that the Dicke state does not merely possess entanglement, but possesses a holographic structural integrity where the global topological properties are encoded within its subsystems. This framework bridges the gap between abstract topology and experimental quantum optics, offering a new perspective on designing robust quantum networks resilient to particle loss. The topological framework established in this paper opens several avenues for future investigation, particularly regarding the resilience of these structures in realistic, open quantum systems. While this work focused on pure states and projective measurements (particle loss), experimental realizations are subject to environmental noise. Future work will investigate how link fluidity degrades under specific noise channels, such as phase damping and amplitude damping. We aim to determine a critical coherence threshold below which the topological link effectively
melts
or disentangles. Our current analysis utilized strong projective measurements in the computational basis. It remains to be seen how the
n-
Hopf topology responds to weak measurements or Positive Operator-Valued Measures (POVMs). Exploring whether the recursive preservation of the link holds under partial collapse could have significant implications for measurement-based quantum computation. Although we have established a strong phenomenological analogy between Dicke states and Hopf links, a rigorous mathematical mapping to knot polynomials (such as the Jones polynomial or Kauffman bracket) is a necessary next step. Developing a quantum invariant that can distinguish between different classes of multipartite entanglement based strictly on their topological knot equivalence would be a powerful classification tool. The current model relies on the indistinguishability of qubits. We propose extending this analysis to systems with broken symmetry or higher-dimensional particles (qudits). Investigating whether link fluidity can be engineered in non-symmetric states would provide deeper insights into the minimum resources required to construct robust topological quantum memories.

Appendix A
Derivation of the
l_{1}
-Norm of Coherence for Pure States

In this appendix, we derive the simplified analytical formula for the
l_{1}
-norm of quantum coherence restricted to pure states
[
27
]
. This derivation justifies the calculation
C_{l_{1}}=(\sum|c_{i}|)^{2}-1
used in Section 3.

A.1
General Definition

The
l_{1}
-norm of coherence for a general density matrix
\rho
in a fixed reference basis
\{|i\rangle\}
is defined as the sum of the absolute values of the off-diagonal elements:

C_{l_{1}}(\rho)=\sum_{i\neq j}|\rho_{ij}|

(19)

where
\rho_{ij}=\langle i|\rho|j\rangle
.

A.2
Pure State Expansion

Consider a general pure state
|\psi\rangle
expanded in the reference basis of dimension
d
:

|\psi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}|i\rangle

(20)

where
c_{i}
are complex coefficients satisfying the normalization condition
\sum_{i}|c_{i}|^{2}=1
. The density matrix
\rho
for this pure state is:

\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|=\left(\sum_{i}c_{i}|i\rangle\right)\left(\sum_{j}c_{j}^{*}\langle j|\right)=\sum_{i,j}c_{i}c_{j}^{*}|i\rangle\langle j|

(21)

The matrix elements are given by
\rho_{ij}=c_{i}c_{j}^{*}
.

A.3
Derivation

Substituting the matrix elements into the definition of coherence:

C_{l_{1}}(|\psi\rangle)=\sum_{i\neq j}|c_{i}c_{j}^{*}|=\sum_{i\neq j}|c_{i}||c_{j}|

(22)

We observe that the square of the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients can be expanded as:

\left(\sum_{i}|c_{i}|\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{i}|c_{i}|\right)\left(\sum_{j}|c_{j}|\right)=\sum_{i,j}|c_{i}||c_{j}|

(23)

This double summation over all indices
i,j
can be split into diagonal terms (
i=j
) and off-diagonal terms (
i\neq j
):

\sum_{i,j}|c_{i}||c_{j}|=\underbrace{\sum_{i}|c_{i}|^{2}}_{\text{Diagonal}}+\underbrace{\sum_{i\neq j}|c_{i}||c_{j}|}_{\text{Off-Diagonal}}

(24)

Using the normalization condition
\sum|c_{i}|^{2}=1
, we can rewrite the equation as:

\left(\sum_{i}|c_{i}|\right)^{2}=1+\sum_{i\neq j}|c_{i}||c_{j}|

(25)

Identifying the last term as the coherence
C_{l_{1}}(|\psi\rangle)
, we arrive at the final formula:

C_{l_{1}}(|\psi\rangle)=\left(\sum_{i}|c_{i}|\right)^{2}-1

(26)

This formula allows for the direct calculation of coherence from the state vector coefficients without explicitly constructing the full density matrix.

Appendix B
Projective Measurement on the Dicke State
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle

This appendix provides the detailed mathematical calculations for the projective measurements performed on the general Dicke state. Due to permutation symmetry, we designate the measured qubit as qubit
1
without loss of generality.

B.1
Algebraic Form of the State

We consider a general Dicke state on
n
qubits with
k
excitations:

|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}\sum_{j}P_{j}\left(|0\rangle^{\otimes(n-k)}|1\rangle^{\otimes k}\right)

(27)

where the sum is over all distinct permutations
P_{j}
.

B.2
Measurement on Qubit 1

We perform a projective measurement in the computational basis
\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}
.

B.2.1
Case 1: Measurement Yields
|0\rangle

The projector is
P_{0}=|0\rangle\langle 0|_{1}\otimes I^{\otimes(n-1)}
.

Application of Projector:

We apply
P_{0}
to the superposition as

P_{0}|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}P_{0}\left(\sum_{\text{perm}}|\dots\rangle\right)

(28)

We classify the terms in the summation into two types:

•

Type A:
Terms where Qubit 1 is
|0\rangle
. In these terms, all
k
excitations are distributed among the remaining
n-1
qubits.

•

Type B:
Terms where Qubit 1 is
|1\rangle
. In these terms, one excitation is fixed on Qubit 1, and the remaining
k-1
excitations are distributed among the remaining qubits.

The action of the projector is:

P_{0}|0\rangle\langle 0|(\text{Type A})=\text{Type A}

(29)

P_{0}|0\rangle\langle 0|(\text{Type B})=0\hskip 9.24994pt(\text{Annihilated})

(30)

Therefore, only Type A terms survive. Each surviving term has the form
|0\rangle_{1}\otimes|\phi_{j}\rangle_{2\dots n}
, where
|\phi_{j}\rangle
represents a specific arrangement of
k
excitations in
n-1
qubits. The number of such terms is
\binom{n-1}{k}
.

Thus:

P_{0}|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}|0\rangle_{1}\otimes\left(\sum\text{all arrangements of }k\text{ 1's in }n-1\text{ qubits}\right)

(31)

Using the definition of the Dicke state for
n-1
qubits ,i.e.
|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n-1}{k}}}\sum\text{arrangements}
, we get:

P_{0}|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{\binom{n-1}{k}}}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}|0\rangle_{1}\otimes|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle

(32)

Probability:

The probability is the squared norm:

p(0)=||P_{0}|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle||^{2}=\frac{\binom{n-1}{k}}{\binom{n}{k}}

(33)

Using the identity
\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-1}{k}
, we can write:

p(0)=\frac{\binom{n-1}{k}}{\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-1}{k}}=\frac{n-k}{n}

(34)

Post-Measurement State:

Renormalizing the state:

|\psi^{(0)}\rangle=\frac{P_{0}|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle}{\sqrt{p(0)}}=|0\rangle_{1}\otimes|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle

(35)

Tracing out Qubit 1, the remaining system is in the state
|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle
.

B.2.2
Case 2: Measurement Yields
|1\rangle

The projector is
P_{1}=|1\rangle\langle 1|_{1}\otimes I^{\otimes(n-1)}
.

Application of Projector:

We apply
P_{0}
to the superposition:

P_{1}|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}P_{1}\left(\sum_{\text{perm}}|\dots\rangle\right)

(36)

We classify the terms in the summation into two types:

•

Type A ( qubit
1
is
|0\rangle
):
Annihilated (
P_{1}|0\rangle=0
).

•

Type B ( qubit
1
is
|1\rangle
):
Survives.

In the surviving Type B terms, qubit
1
is fixed at
|1\rangle
. The remaining
n-1

k-1

\binom{n-1}{k-1}

Thus:

P_{1}|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}|1\rangle_{1}\otimes\left(\sum\text{all arrangements of }k-1\text{ 1's in }n-1\text{ qubits}\right)

(37)

Using the definition
|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}}\sum\text{arrangements}
, we get:

P_{1}|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}}{\sqrt{\binom{n}{k}}}|1\rangle_{1}\otimes|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle

Probability:

p(1)=\frac{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}{\binom{n}{k}}

Using the identity

Appendix C
Schmidt Rank Analysis of the Dicke State
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle

We want to analyze the entanglement between one specific qubit (Qubit 1) and the remaining
n-1
qubits in the state
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle
.

Case 1:
|D_{n}^{(0)}\rangle
(All zeroes)

|D_{n}^{(0)}\rangle\equiv|0\rangle^{\otimes n}

(42)

This is a product state.
Schmidt Rank = 1
.

Case 2:
|D_{n}^{(n)}\rangle
(All ones)

|D_{n}^{(n)}\rangle\equiv|1\rangle^{\otimes n}

(43)

This is also a product state.
Schmidt Rank = 1
.

Case 3:
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle
where
0<k<n

Using the measurement probabilities calculated above, we can write the state as a superposition of the two outcomes:

•

If Qubit 1 is
|1\rangle
, the remaining
n-1
qubits are in
|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle
.

•

If Qubit 1 is
|0\rangle
, the remaining
n-1
qubits are in
|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle
.

Thus, we can express the state as:

|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{n-k}{n}}|0\rangle_{1}\otimes|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}|1\rangle_{1}\otimes|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle

(44)

Now, consider the inner product of the two environment states:

\langle D_{n-1}^{(k)}|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle=\sum_{\text{config}_{k}}\sum_{\text{config}_{k-1}}\langle\text{config}_{k}|\text{config}_{k-1}\rangle

(45)

where
|\text{config}_{x}\rangle
represents a computational basis state of
n-1
qubits with exactly
x
excitations (
1
’s).

C.1
Key Observation

Consider any specific computational basis state:

•

If it appears in
|D_{n-1}^{(k)}\rangle
, it has exactly
k
ones.

•

If it appears in
|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle
, it has exactly
k-1
ones.

Since these states have different numbers of excitations (i.e., different Hamming weights), they are orthogonal. No computational basis state can appear in both superpositions.
Therefore, every term in the inner product is zero:

\langle\text{config}_{k}|\text{config}_{k-1}\rangle=0

(46)

Consequently:

\langle D_{n-1}^{(k)}|D_{n-1}^{(k-1)}\rangle=0

(47)

Since the two terms in the decomposition involve orthogonal states for the remaining system, we have a valid Schmidt decomposition with exactly two non-zero coefficients (given
0<k<n
).

\textit{Schmidt Rank}=2

(48)

The Schmidt coefficients are:

\lambda_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{n-k}{n}},\hskip 9.24994pt\lambda_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}\\

(49)

C.2
Condition for Maximal Entanglement

For maximal entanglement, the Schmidt coefficients must be equal:

\sqrt{\frac{n-k}{n}}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}\implies\frac{n-k}{n}=\frac{k}{n}\implies n-k=k\implies k=\frac{n}{2}

(50)

Therefore,
|D_{n}^{(k)}\rangle
is maximally entangled between 1 qubit and the rest if and only if

k=n/2

(51)

(i.e., exactly half of the qubits are excited).

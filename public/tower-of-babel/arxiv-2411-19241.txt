Enhanced Lieb-Robinson bounds for commuting long-range interactions
Marius Lemm, Tom Wessel

Abstract
Recent works have revealed the intricate effect of long-range interactions on information transport in quantum many-body systems: In D D spatial dimensions, interactions decaying as a power-law r − α r^{-\alpha} with α > 2 ​ D + 1 \alpha>2D+1 exhibit a Lieb-Robinson bound (LRB) with a linear light cone and the threshold 2 ​ D + 1 2D+1 is sharp in general. Here, we observe that mutually commuting, long-range interactions satisfy an enhanced LRB of the form t ​ r − α t\,r^{-\alpha} for any α > 0 \alpha>0 , and this scaling is sharp. In particular, the linear light cone occurs at α = 1 \alpha=1 in any dimension. Part of our motivation stems from quantum error-correcting codes. As applications, we derive enhanced bounds on ground state correlations and an enhanced local perturbations perturb locally (LPPL) principle for which we adapt a recent subharmonicity argument of Wang-Hazzard. Similar enhancements hold for commuting interactions with stretched exponential decay.

Introduction

The Lieb-Robinson bound (LRB) is a central theorem in quantum many-body physics.
It underpins the proofs of fundamental structural properties such as the decay of correlations of ground states of Hamiltonians with a spectral gap
[
27
,
41
]
, the existence of the thermodynamic limit as a strongly continuous automorphism on the quasi-local operator algebra
[
6
,
40
]
, and the mathematical definition of a topological quantum phase
[
28
,
2
]
.
At the same time, the LRB is a cornerstone of the growing area of quantum information theory: on the one hand, it was a decisive tool for landmark results such as the area law for the entanglement entropy
[
25
]
and on the other hand it provides bounds on dynamical entanglement generation
[
8
]
, quantum messaging and quantum state transfer
[
18
]
, and efficient quantum simulability of many-body dynamics
[
24
]
.

The LRB is concerned with quantum lattice Hamiltonians.
These are defined by fixing a finite graph
\varLambda
and defining the Hilbert space

{\mathcal{H}}_{\varLambda}:=\bigotimes_{x\in\varLambda}\mathbb{C}^{q}

with the standard inner product.
On this Hilbert space, one considers a distinguished self-adjoint linear operator, the Hamiltonian
H
, which is taken as a sum of terms that act
“locally”
, i.e.

H_{\varLambda}:=\sum_{Z\subset\varLambda}\varPsi(Z),

(1)

where each summand is a bounded, linear, self-adjoint operator of the form
\varPsi(Z)\equiv\varPsi(Z)\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}Z}
.
The precise mathematical setup in terms of quasi-local algebras is given in
Section

2
.

Instead of studying solutions to the Schrödinger equation directly, it is advantageous to study its dual, the quantum dynamics on operators, known as
Heisenberg dynamics
, that is defined by unitary conjugation with the solution operator of the Schrödinger equation, i.e.

\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A)={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{\varLambda}}\,A\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}tH_{\varLambda}}.

(2)

For a set
X\subset\varLambda
and
\ell>0
, we denote its
\ell
-neighbourhood by

X_{\ell}:=\big\{\nonscript\,x\in\varLambda\nonscript\,\big|\allowbreak\nonscript\,\mathopen{}d\lparen x,X\rparen\leq\ell\nonscript\,\big\}.

(3)

To put our results in context, we first review the two kinds of LRBs that are well-established for the case where the interaction
\varPsi
is of finite-range, i.e. there exists a range
R>0
such that
\varPsi(Z)=0
if
\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen>R
, or exponentially decaying, i.e.
\lVert\varPsi(Z)\rVert
decays exponentially in
\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen
.
For such rapidly decaying interactions, one has the following two types of LRBs
[
37
,
27
,
41
,
43
]
.

(a)

Commutator version of the LRB.

There exist constants
C
,
b
,
v>0
such that for all finite
X
,
Y\subset\varLambda
and bounded operators
A_{X}\equiv A_{X}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}X}
and
B_{Y}\equiv B_{Y}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}Y}
, it holds that

\big\lVert[\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A_{X}),B_{Y}]\big\rVert\leq C\,\min\big\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\big\}\,\lVert A_{X}\rVert\,\lVert B_{Y}\rVert\,{\mathrm{e}}^{b\,(vt-d\lparen X,Y\rparen)}.

(4)

(b)

Operator localization version of the LRB.

There exist constants
C
,
b
,
v>0
such that for all finite
X\subset\varLambda
and bounded operators
A_{X}\equiv A_{X}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}X}
, it holds that

\big\lVert\tau^{\varLambda}_{t}(A_{X})-\mathbb{E}_{X_{r}}\big\lparen\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A_{X})\big\rparen\big\rVert\leq C\,\lvert X\rvert\,\lVert A_{X}\rVert\,{\mathrm{e}}^{b(vt-r)}.

(5)

Here,
\mathbb{E}_{X_{r}}
is the conditional expectation on
X_{r}
, i.e. the partial trace over the Hilbert space associated to
\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}X_{r}
; see (
12
).

The LRBs are a mathematically precise way to capture that quantum information propagates at most with a speed
v
in these systems.
The speed
v
and the constants
C
and
b
might depend on the details of the interaction but are uniformly bounded in the system size
\lvert\varLambda\rvert
.
Indeed, since the right-hand sides are small for
v\,t\ll d\lparen X,Y\rparen
and
v\,t\ll r
, respectively, the LRBs establish the existence of an effective finite propagation speed for many-body Hamiltonians of the form (
1
).
The fact that the bound is effective until
vt\sim r
is also called a “linear light cone” (with slope
v
) or a “ballistic bound” and we use both of these phrases interchangeably.

1.1
Enhanced Lieb-Robinson bounds

Given the important role played by LRBs, it is unsurprising that significant effort has been invested to extend its validity to other classes of many-body systems.
Of particular interest in the past ten years has been the extension of LRBs to so-called
long-range
interactions, whose operator norm decays as a power law.
In this introduction, we focus for simplicity on two-body interactions, i.e.
\varPsi(Z)=0
for
\lvert Z\rvert>2

For two-body interactions, the power-law decay assumption can be simply phrased as the operator norm bound

\lVert\varPsi(\{x,y\})\rVert\leq C\,d\lparen x,y\rparen^{-\alpha}

(6)

\lVert\varPsi(\{x\})\rVert\leq C

The LRBs for commuting long-range interactions are enhanced compared to the LRB for general long-range interactions in two ways.

(i)

The range of

The central first question is whether the light cone is indeed linear, i.e. of the form

For general long-range interactions, both versions of the LRB become ballistic for

For commuting interactions, the commutator version of the LRB is ballistic already at

\alpha=D+1

Moreover, the LRBs for commuting interactions even become subballistic, i.e. the critical distance scales sublinearly in time, above these thresholds.

1.2
Further results

As mentioned in the introduction, LRBs and operator localization bounds have a plethora of applications – both structural ones in mathematical physics and practical ones in quantum information theory, e.g. on the speed of entanglement generation
[
8
]
, quantum messaging and quantum state transfer
[
18
]
.
Here, we focus on two applications which use LRBs to constrain the structure of ground states of Hamiltonians with a uniform spectral gap, namely decay of correlations and the principle that “local perturbations perturb locally” (LPPL).

Enhanced correlation bounds for gapped ground states

Modifying the proof of
[
27
]
, we show that a ground state
\rho_{0}
, which is gapped from the rest of the spectrum by a gap
g
, satisfies
decay of correlations
in the sense that

\Big\lvert\Tr\big\lparen\rho_{0}\,A_{X}\,B_{Y}\big\rparen-\Tr\big\lparen\rho_{0}\,A_{X}\big\rparen\,\Tr\big\lparen\rho_{0}\,B_{Y}\big\rparen\Big\rvert\lesssim\,\lVert A_{X}\rVert\,\lVert B_{Y}\rVert\,\lvert X\rvert\,\lvert Y\rvert\,g^{-1}\,d\lparen X,Y\rparen^{-\alpha}

for small

and any
\alpha>0

The precise statement is given in
Theorem

11

In this bound, the inverse spectral gap
g^{-1}
plays a similar role as time in
Figure

1

In particular, the analogue of a ballistic bound is that the relevant localization length scales like
g^{-1}

\alpha\geq 1

This is to be compared with the best known bound

for general long-range interactions with
\alpha\in\lparen D,2D]

[

First, no bound for
\alpha\leq D

And second, writing
r^{-\alpha g/v}={\mathrm{e}}^{-(\alpha g/v)\ln r}
, one sees that the localization length is exponentially large in
g^{-1}
.
Again, we see a fundamental, qualitative improvement under the additional assumption of commuting interactions.

Let

Then,

\Big\lvert\Tr\big\lparen\rho_{0}\,B_{Y}\big\rparen-\Tr\big\lparen\rho_{1}\,B_{Y}\big\rparen\Big\rvert\lesssim\lVert B_{Y}\rVert\,\big\lparen\lVert V_{X}\rVert+\lVert V_{X}\rVert^{2}\big\rparen\,\lvert X\rvert\,\lvert Y\rvert\,g^{-3}\,d\lparen X,Y\rparen^{-\alpha}.

However, the best known bound for general interactions scales exactly the same as the bound for decay of correlations
[

1.3
Motivation from quantum error-correcting codes

The results described above show that there is a fundamental, qualitative difference between the quantum many-body dynamics produced by general long-range interactions and commuting long-range interactions.

In this paragraph, we briefly review why Hamiltonians with commuting non-local interactions have recently received a lot of interest in the quantum information and quantum computing communities in the context of quantum error-correcting codes.
The goal of a quantum error-correcting code is typically to robustly store quantum information, often by harnessing topological order; early examples are the toric code
[
30
]
and CSS stabilizer codes
[
11
,
51
]
.
Most quantum error-correcting codes are based on
commuting
interactions.
Non-local
and
commuting interactions are of particular interest lately because they arise in Euclidean constructions of efficient quantum codes.
The reason is as follows: Recall that a
[[n,k,d]]
quantum code is a quantum code on system size
n
that can store
k
logical qubits with code distance
d
.
The goal is to have
k
and
d
as large as possible, so that the code is both efficient and robust.
Then, the Bravyi-Poulin-Terhal (BPT) theorem
[
9
]
says that any
[[n,k,d]]
quantum code with short-range interactions in
D
Euclidean dimensions must satisfy the bound

k\,d\mkern 1.0mu^{2/(D-1)}\lesssim n
.
An extension to general graphs was given in
[
4
]
.
A quantum code is called good if it satisfies
k
,
d\sim n
(which is optimal) and the BPT theorem says that local commuting interactions in
D
Euclidean dimensions cannot produce good quantum codes.
Then, in 2021, the 30-year-old open problem of constructing good quantum codes was resolved by quantum low-density parity check (qLDPC) codes on expander graphs and related constructions
[
45
,
10
,
36
]
.
Implementing these codes as Hamiltonians in the Euclidean setting, naturally produces Hamiltonians with non-local, commuting interactions
[
14
,
5
,
29
,
46
,
3
]
(as expected in view of the BPT theorem
[
9
]
).
Beside their potential for near-term fault-tolerant quantum computation, quantum codes are also emerging as a rich source of new models for condensed-matter physics, e.g., they lead to a proof of the no low-energy trivial state (NLTS) conjecture of Freedman and Hastings
[
22
,
26
]
and provide a rich class of gapped topological quantum phases
[
13
]
.

For illustration purposes of the kind of quantum code that is within our scope, we discuss a simple example of a long-range toric code below.
Of course, Euclidean implementations of good qLDPC codes coming from expander graphs would look more complicated.
Indeed, the pioneering investigations for Euclidean implementations of efficient qLDPC codes and related error-correcting codes
[
14
,
5
,
29
,
46
,
3
]
have not fully settled on a specific Hamiltonian and non-locality in these works is not necessarily the same as power-law decay.
In some cases it should rather be understood as few-body and not necessarily decaying in distance.
Nonetheless, these proposals provide strong motivation for us to study quantum dynamics of Hamiltonians with commuting long-range interactions like the following one.

Example 1

(Long-range toric code)
.

A non-trivial example of a Hamiltonian satisfying our assumptions for any
\alpha>0
is given by a long-range version of Kitaev’s famous toric code model
[
30
]
.
The basic model consists of spins sitting at the edges of the lattice
(\mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z})^{2}
.
We denote the set of vertices
V
, edges
E
and faces
F
of
(\mathbb{Z}/L\mathbb{Z})^{2}
.
Then the Hilbert space is given by
{\mathcal{H}}:=\bigotimes_{e\in E}{\mathcal{H}}_{e}:=\bigotimes_{e\in E}\mathbb{C}^{2}
. To each vertex
s\in V
one associates a
star
operator
A_{s}:=-\mathbf{1}+\prod_{e\in E:v\in e}\sigma^{X}_{e}
, and to each face
p\in F
one associates a
plaquette
operator
B_{p}:=-\mathbf{1}+\prod_{e\in\partial(p)}\sigma^{Z}_{e}
, where
\sigma^{\#}_{e}
is the Pauli
\#
matrix acting on
{\mathcal{H}}_{e}
and
\partial(p)
denotes the four edges touching
p
. The Hamiltonian is then given as a sum

H:=-\sum_{s\in V}A_{s}-\sum_{p\in F}B_{p},

which turns out to be commuting and gapped
[
30
]
and has ground state energy
0
.
Moreover, it is frustration free, i.e. its ground state projection
P
also minimizes all individual terms
A_{s}\,P=0
and
B_{p}\,P=0
.
Adding a term of the form

H_{\textrm{long-range}}:=\sum_{s_{1},s_{2}\in V}f(s_{1},s_{2})\,A_{s_{1}}\,A_{s_{2}}+\sum_{p_{1},p_{2}\in F}g(p_{1},p_{2})\,B_{p_{1}}\,B_{p_{2}},

with
0\leq f(s_{1},s_{2})\leq C\,d\lparen s_{1},s_{2}\rparen^{-\alpha}
and
0\leq g(p_{1},p_{2})\leq C\,d\lparen p_{1},p_{2}\rparen^{-\alpha}
which is positive and satisfies
H_{\textrm{long-range}}\,P=0
can only increase the gap.
The perturbed Hamiltonian
H+H_{\textrm{long-range}}
is still mutually commuting, gapped, has ground state
P
and is long-range.
This example can be generalized in various ways, e.g. to higher dimensions.
One can also add higher order products
(-1)^{k}\,A_{s_{1}}\dotsm A_{s_{k}}
and
(-1)^{k}\,B_{p_{1}}\dotsm B_{p_{k}}
or allow for small negative
f
and
g
and still obtain a spectral gap by adopting the BHM strategy
[
7
,
NSY2020
]
.
Having a spectral gap is not needed for the Lieb-Robinson bounds.
It is only relevant for the applications to gapped ground states that we discussed in
Section

1.2
.

2
Mathematical setup

In this work we want to consider spin systems on
D
-regular graphs.
The graph is seen as a metric space
(\varGamma,d)
, where
d
is the graph distance.
For any
Z\subset\varGamma

\lvert Z\rvert

\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen:=\sup_{x,y\in Z}d(x,y)
.
Given two sets

their distance with respect to the metric
d
.
We use
\varLambda\Subset\varGamma
to denote that
\varLambda
is a finite subset of
\varGamma
.
The ball with radius

around

is denoted
B^{\varGamma}_{x}(r):=\{\nonscript\,z\in\varGamma\nonscript\,|\allowbreak\nonscript\,\mathopen{}d(x,z)\leq r\nonscript\,\}
.

Definition 2

(Surface-regular graph
[
49
]
)
.

We call the graph
(\kappa,D)
-surface-regular, if

\big\lvert B^{\varGamma}_{x}(r)\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}B^{\varGamma}_{x}(r-1)\big\rvert\leq\kappa\,r^{D-1}\quad\quad\text{for all}\quad\quad x\in\varGamma\quad\text{and}\quad r\in\mathbb{N}.

The prototypical example of a
(\kappa,D)
-surface-regular lattice is
\varGamma=\mathbb{Z}^{D}
.
Any
D
-surface-regular graph is also
D
-regular
[
49
]
, in the sense that

\big\lvert B^{\varGamma}_{x}(r)\big\rvert\leq(1+\kappa)\,r^{D}\quad\quad\text{for all}\quad\quad x\in\varGamma\quad\text{and}\quad r\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.

This notion of
D
-regular graphs is more common in the literature.
However, all lattices we are interested in are
D
-surface-regular, and we can prove better bounds with this stronger assumption.
An explicit construction of a counter example can be found in
[
52
]
.

Often however, we restrict to
finite

(\kappa,D)
-surface-regular lattices
\varLambda
.
This might seem trivial, but it is important to note that all our results and constants will be independent of the specific graph
\varLambda
and only depend on
\kappa
and
D
.
In certain cases, this will allow us to take the limit
\varLambda_{n}\to\varGamma
where all
\varLambda_{n}
are finite
(\kappa,D)
-regular lattices and
\varGamma
is an infinite one.

With every site
x\in\varGamma
one associates a finite-dimensional local Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{x}:=\mathbb{C}^{q}
with the corresponding space of linear operators denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{x}:=\mathcal{B}\left\lparen\mathbb{C}^{q}\right\rparen
.
And for every finite
\varLambda\Subset\varGamma
we define the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\varLambda}:=\bigotimes_{x\in\varLambda}{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
, and denote the algebra of bounded linear operators on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\varLambda}
by
\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda}:=\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{H}}_{\varLambda})
.
Due to the tensor product structure, we have
\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in\varLambda}\mathcal{A}_{x}
.
Hence, for
X\subset\varLambda\Subset\varGamma
, any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
can be viewed as an element of
\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda}
by identifying
A
with
A\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\varLambda\backslash X}\in\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda}
, where
\mathbf{1}_{\varLambda\backslash X}
denotes the identity in
\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda\backslash X}
.
This identification is always understood implicitly and for
B\in\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda}
we denote by
\supp(B)
the smallest
Y\subset\varLambda
such that
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
.
Moreover, the algebra of local observables on
\varGamma
is given by

\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\varGamma}:=\bigcup_{\varLambda\Subset\varGamma}\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda},\quad\quad\text{and its completion}\quad\quad\mathcal{A}_{\varGamma}:=\overline{\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\varGamma}}^{\raisebox{-0.60275pt}{\kern 1.0pt$\scriptstyle\lVert\cdot\rVert$}}

with respect to the operator norm is the algebra of
quasi-local
observables, which is only relevant if
\varGamma
is infinite.

An
interaction
on
\varGamma
is a function

\varPsi\colon\{Z\Subset\varGamma\}\to\mathcal{A}_{\varGamma},\quad Z\mapsto\varPsi(Z)\in\mathcal{A}_{Z}\quad\text{with}\quad\varPsi(Z)=\varPsi(Z)^{*}.

(8)

For each
\varLambda\Subset\varGamma
, the corresponding local Hamiltonian is then defined as

H_{\varLambda}:=\sum_{Z\subset\varLambda}\varPsi(Z).

And the Heisenberg time-evolution of an operator
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda}
is denoted by

\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A):={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{\varLambda}}\,A\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}tH_{\varLambda}}.

(9)

For infinite
\varGamma
, the Hamiltonian is not an object of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\varGamma}
and the dynamics cannot be defined as in (
9
).
Instead, one can define the time evolution on finite subgraphs
\varLambda\Subset\varGamma
as above.
The dynamics on
\varGamma
can then be defined as the limit

\tau^{\varGamma}_{t}(A):=\lim_{\varLambda\nearrow\varGamma}\tau^{\varLambda}_{t}(A)

(10)

if it exists for all
A\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\varGamma}
.
It might then be extended to a cycle of automorphism on
\mathcal{A}_{\varGamma}
.
This limit is understood in the following way:
Let
\{\varLambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\{\nonscript\,\varLambda\Subset\varGamma\nonscript\,\}
be an increasing and exhaustive sequences, i.e.
\varLambda_{n}\subset\varLambda_{n+1}
for all
n
and for each
Z\Subset\varGamma
there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
Z\subset\varLambda_{n}
.
If the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau^{\varLambda_{n}}_{t}(A)
exists and is independent of the chosen sequence, it equals the above limit.
We comment on the existence after introducing interaction norms.

To measure locality of an interaction so-called interaction norms are widely used in the literature.
Therefore, let
F\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a decaying function.
We define (for interactions on
\varGamma
)

\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}:=\sup_{x,y\in\varGamma}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\Subset\varGamma\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\
x,y\in Z\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert\varPsi(Z)\rVert}{F\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen},

(11)

and say that
\varPsi
is
F
-local if
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}<\infty
.
We choose to use this norm, because it allows for very general interactions.
For two-body interactions with decay
\lVert\varPsi\big\lparen\{x,y\}\big\rparen\rVert\leq C\,F\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen
, one directly computes
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}\leq C
.

Our bounds will depend on a suitable interaction norm
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}
.
Beyond this, however, the constants in all our results will be independent of the particular lattice
\varGamma
.
In this way, if one defines an interaction on an infinite graph
\varGamma
such that
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}^{(\varGamma)}<\infty
, one obtains bounds that are uniform in
\varLambda\Subset\varGamma
, by using
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}^{(\varLambda)}\leq\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}^{(\varGamma)}
.
Here, we add an extra index to highlight on which graph (
11
) is used.
This in particular holds for the prototypical example
\varGamma=\mathbb{Z}^{D}
, where one requires

\lVert\varPsi\rVert^{(\mathbb{Z}^{D})}_{F}:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{D}\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\
x,y\in Z\end{subarray}}\frac{\lVert\varPsi(Z)\rVert}{F\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen}

and uniform obtains bounds on arbitrary subsets
\varLambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{D}
.
But with the more general framework, one can for example also consider rectangles with torus geometry, or more general graphs like the honeycomb lattice.

With the notion of interaction norms at hand, we can come back to the thermodynamic limit.
For interactions with decay
F(r)\leq(1+r)^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>D
on a
D
-surface-regular graph, existence of the thermodynamic limit is known, see e.g.
[
43
, Theorem 3.5]
.

, by the same arguments as given in

.
Alternatively, one can just use (

For slower decaying interactions, one does not expect a thermodynamic limit to exist in general.
In fact, one can expect that the interaction at least need to be summable in the sense

\sup_{z\in\varGamma}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\Subset\varGamma\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\

See for example

for the existence of the thermodynamic limit without explicitly requiring decay in the diameter

To discuss the operator localization version of the LRB, we introduce the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{Y}\colon\mathcal{A}_{\varLambda}\to\mathcal{A}_{Y}

For us, it is only important to know that

Extensions to fermionic systems

3
Lieb-Robinson bounds for commuting interactions

We now restrict to mutually commuting interactions satisfying
\big[\varPsi(X),\varPsi(Y)\big]=0
for all
X
and
Y
.
We split the discussion according to the decay of the interactions.

3.1
Finite-range interactions

The simplest type of interactions are finite-range interactions, i.e. interactions for which there exists
R
such that
\varPsi(Z)=0
if
\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen>R
.
For commuting, finite-range interactions, the Heisenberg time evolution remains localized for all times in the following sense.

Theorem 3

(Lieb-Robinson bound for commuting, finite range Hamiltonians)
.

Let
\varPsi
be a commuting interaction on a graph
\varGamma
.
Assume that
\varPsi
is of range
R
, i.e.
\varPsi(Z)=0
if
\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen>R
, and uniformly bounded, i.e. there exists a constant
C_{\varPsi}
such that
\lVert\varPsi(Z)\rVert<C_{\varPsi}
for all
Z\Subset\varGamma
.

Then the corresponding Heisenberg time-evolution satisfies

\tau^{\varGamma}_{t}(A)=\tau^{X_{R}}_{t}(A)\in\mathcal{A}_{X_{R}}\quad\quad\text{for all}\quad\quad X\Subset\varGamma\quad\text{and}\quad A\in\mathcal{A}_{X},

where
X_{R}:=\{\nonscript\,z\in\varGamma\nonscript\,|\allowbreak\nonscript\,\mathopen{}d\lparen z,X\rparen\leq R\nonscript\,\}
is the
R
neighbourhood of
X
.
Thus, it also satisfies the following Lieb-Robinson bound:
For all
X
,
Y\Subset\varGamma
and operators
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
,

\big\lVert[\tau^{\varGamma}_{t}(A),B]\big\rVert=0\quad\quad\text{if}\quad\quad d\lparen X,Y\rparen>R.

This behaviour of commuting finite-range interactions was observed before, for example in
[
47
]
.
We note that this behaviour dramatically differs from the general LRB for non-commuting, finite-range Hamiltonians.
Our goal will be to show that this extreme locality persists to some degree for long-range commuting interactions.

3.2
Short-range interactions

Another class of widely used interactions are so-called short-range or (stretched) exponentially-decaying interactions, which have a bounded interaction norm with decay function

F_{b,p}(r):={\mathrm{e}}^{-b\,r^{p}}

for some
b>0
and
p\in\lparen 0,1]
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
0<b^{\prime}<b
,
p\in\lparen 0,1]
,
\kappa>0
and
D\in\mathbb{N}
, then there exists a constant
C_{b,b^{\prime}}>0
such that the following holds:
Let
\varPsi
be a commuting interaction on a
(\kappa,D)
-regular graph
\varGamma
satisfying
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F_{b,p}}<\infty
.
Then the corresponding Heisenberg time-evolution satisfies the following Lieb-Robinson bounds:

(a)

For all disjoint
X
,
Y\Subset\varGamma
and operators
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
,

\big\lVert[\tau^{\varGamma}_{t}(A),B]\big\rVert\leq C_{b,b^{\prime}}\,\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F_{b,p}}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\min\big\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\big\}\,\lvert t\rvert\,F_{b^{\prime},p}\l\lparen d\lparen X,Y\rparen\bigr\rparen.

(13)

(b)

For all
X\Subset\varGamma
, operators
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
, and
r\geq 0
,

\big\lVert\tau^{\varGamma}_{t}(A)-\mathbb{E}_{X_{r}}\big\lparen\tau_{t}^{\varGamma}(A)\big\rparen\big\rVert\leq C_{b,b^{\prime}}\,\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F_{b,p}}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lvert X\rvert\,\lvert t\rvert\,F_{b^{\prime},p}(r).

A more general result for short-range interactions, assuming only that the commutator of interaction terms is small (but not necessarily zero), was proven by
[
24
]
.
In the limit of vanishing commutators, their bound gives the same LRB.
The result stated here, gives a logarithmic light cone, which
[
53
]
call
“slow dynamics”
.
They then prove stability of the LRBs to local perturbations similar to (
30
) but for extended perturbations given by an interaction.

3.3
Long-range interactions

Recently, many efforts were made to prove LRBs for long-range interactions, which are described by the decay function

F_{\alpha}(r):=(1+r)^{-\alpha}.

While this is quite involved for general (non-commuting) Hamiltonians, one can very easily obtain LRBs for commuting Hamiltonians.
Moreover, it is often necessary to distinguish different regimes in
\alpha
.
In particular, on a
D
-surface-regular graph, one cannot expect a thermodynamic limit of the dynamics for
0<\alpha\leq D
, which is the reason for us to provide two distinct statements.
The first works also for infinite graphs and allows for two different bounds, one scales better in the support of the two observables, the other scales better in the distance between them.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\kappa>0
,
D\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>D
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following holds:
Let
\varPsi
be a commuting interaction on a
(\kappa,D)
-surface-regular graph
\varGamma
satisfying
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F_{\alpha}}<\infty
.
Then the corresponding Heisenberg time-evolution satisfies the following Lieb-Robinson bounds:

(a)

For all disjoint
X
,
Y\Subset\varGamma
and operators
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
,
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
,

\displaystyle\big\lVert[\tau^{\varGamma}_{t}(A),B]\big\rVert

\displaystyle\leq C\,\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F_{\alpha}}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\min\big\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\big\}\,\lvert t\rvert\,F_{\alpha-D}\l\lparen d\lparen X,Y\rparen\bigr\rparen,

(14)

and

\displaystyle\big\lVert[\tau^{\varGamma}_{t}(A),B]\big\rVert

\displaystyle\leq 4\,\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F_{\alpha}}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\lvert X\rvert\,\lvert Y\rvert\,\lvert t\rvert\,F_{\alpha}\l\lparen d\lparen X,Y\rparen\bigr\rparen.

(15)

(b)

For all
X\Subset\varGamma
, operators
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
, and
r\geq 0
,

\big\lVert\tau^{\varGamma}_{t}(A)-\mathbb{E}_{X_{r}}\big\lparen\tau_{t}^{\varGamma}(A)\big\rparen\big\rVert\leq C\,\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F_{\alpha}}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lvert X\rvert\,\lvert t\rvert\,F_{\alpha-D}(r).

(16)

This gives a linear light cone (a ballistic LRB) in the sense of local approximations for
\alpha>D+1
which is an improvement over the tight
\alpha>2D+1
found for non-commuting two-body interactions
[
55
]
.

But even for
\alpha>0
, we can obtain a bound as in (
15
) on arbitrarily large, but finite graphs.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
\varLambda
be a finite graph.
Let
\varPsi
be a commuting interaction on
\varLambda
satisfying
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F_{\alpha}}<\infty
.
Then the corresponding Heisenberg time-evolution satisfies the Lieb-Robinson bound given in (
15
).

This gives some kind of linear light cone for commuting long-range interactions with
\alpha>1
, which are not even summable in dimensions
D>1
.
In this parameter range, the Heisenberg dynamics do not converge to a thermodynamic limit in general.
Hence, a bound like (
14
) cannot hold, as it implies convergence of the Heisenberg dynamics in the thermodynamic limit.
Thus, we cannot discuss LRBs for the limiting dynamics on infinite graphs.

Remark 7
.

The Lieb-Robinson bounds in
Theorem

5
are sharp.
For example, to see that (
14
) is sharp, let
X=\{x\}
,
Y=\{y\}
and choose the interaction with only non-vanishing term
\varPsi(\{x,y\})=F_{\alpha}\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen\,U
, where
U={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{4}(\mathbf{1}_{x}+\sigma^{Z}_{x})(\mathbf{1}_{y}-\sigma^{X}_{y})}
is a standard CNOT gate between sites
x
and
y
.
The CNOT gate
U
satisfies
{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tU}=\cos(t)\,\mathbf{1}+\mathrm{i}\sin(t)\,U
and
\lVert U\rVert=1
.
Moreover, let
A=\sigma^{X}_{x}
be a spin flip and
B=\sigma^{Z}_{y}
measure the
Z
-component of the qubit at site

.
Now, let
\psi
be any pure state on
\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}\{x,y\}
.
Then

[\tau^{\varLambda}_{t}(A),B]\,\lvert\downarrow\downarrow\rangle_{xy}\otimes\psi=-2\,\mathrm{i}\sin\big\lparen t\,F_{\alpha}\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen\big\rparen\,\lvert\uparrow\uparrow\rangle_{xy}\otimes\psi,

and thus

\big\lVert[\tau^{\varLambda}_{t}(A),B]\big\rVert\geq 2\,\big\lvert\sin\big\lparen t\,F_{\alpha}\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen\big\rparen\big\rvert,

which for small

has the same scaling as (

In

, we present a generalization of this example that extends sharpness of (

,

and we also show sharpness of the other bounds from

3.4
Proof of the Lieb-Robinson bound for commuting Hamiltonians

The proof of all the stated LRBs boils down to a very simple observation, which was already made before, e.g. in
[
47
]
.
It drastically simplifies the Heisenberg evolution of local operators by only considering the interaction terms, which overlap with the support of the operator.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\varPsi
be a commuting interaction on a finite graph
\varLambda
.
For all
X\subset\varLambda
and operators
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
, the corresponding Heisenberg time-evolution satisfies

\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A)=\tau_{t,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda}(A),

where
\tau_{t,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda}
is generated by the interaction

\varPsi_{\cap X}(Z):=\begin{cases}\varPsi(Z)&if $X\cap Z\neq\emptyset$ and\\
0&else.\end{cases}

(17)

Proof.

Due to commutativity of the interaction, the exponential satisfies

{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{\varLambda}}={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}X}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}t(H_{\varLambda}-H_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}X})}.

Using this for both exponentials in (
9
) and realizing that
\big[H_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}X},A\big]=0
due to their disjoint support, one has

\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A)={\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}t(H_{\varLambda}-H_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}X})}\,A\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}t(H_{\varLambda}-H_{\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}X})}=\tau_{t,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda}(A).\qed

From here, the statement about finite-range Hamiltonians on finite graphs in
Theorem

3
is immediately clear.
The extension to infinite graphs follows from the next theorem, and we comment on the details at the end of this section.

Lemma

8
also allows to approximate the time-evolution locally.

Theorem 9

(Local approximation of Heisenberg evolution)
.

Let
\varPsi
be a commuting interaction on a finite graph
\varLambda
.
For all
X\subset\varLambda^{\prime}\subset\varLambda
and
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
it holds that

\big\lVert\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A)-\tau_{t}^{\varLambda^{\prime}}(A)\big\rVert\leq 2\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lvert t\rvert\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\varLambda\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\
Z\cap X\neq\emptyset,\\
Z\cap\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}\varLambda^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\,\big\lVert\varPsi(Z)\big\rVert.

Moreover, if
\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}<\infty
for some decaying function
F\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, then

\big\lVert\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A)-\tau_{t}^{\varLambda^{\prime}}(A)\big\rVert\leq 2\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}\,\lvert t\rvert\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}\smash{\cramped[\scriptstyle]{\varLambda}^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\,F\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen.

(18)

Proof.

By
Lemma

8
we have

\displaystyle\tau_{t}^{\varLambda}(A)-\tau_{t}^{\varLambda^{\prime}}(A)

\displaystyle=\tau_{t,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda}(A)-\tau_{t,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda^{\prime}}(A)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\,\tau_{s,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda}\circ\tau_{t-s,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda^{\prime}}(A)\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!s

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\varLambda\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\
Z\cap\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}\varLambda^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{t}\tau_{s,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda}\,\Big\lparen\Big[\varPsi_{\cap X}(Z),\tau_{t-s,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda^{\prime}}(A)\Big]\Big\rparen\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!s,

and thus the statement after bounding

\bigg\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\tau_{s,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda}\,\Big\lparen\Big[\varPsi_{\cap X}(Z),\tau_{t-s,{\cap X}}^{\varLambda^{\prime}}(A)\Big]\Big\rparen\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!s\bigg\rVert\leq 2\,\lvert t\rvert\,\lVert\varPsi_{\cap X}(Z)\rVert\,\lVert A\rVert.

For the second statement, we additionally bound

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\varLambda\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\
Z\cap X\neq\emptyset,\\
Z\cap\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}\varLambda^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\,\big\lVert\varPsi(Z)\big\rVert\leq\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}\smash{\cramped[\scriptstyle]{\varLambda}^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\,F\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\varLambda\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\
x,y\in Z\end{subarray}}\frac{\big\lVert\varPsi(Z)\big\rVert}{F\bigl\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen}\leq\lVert\varPsi\rVert_{F}\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\varLambda\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}\smash{\cramped[\scriptstyle]{\varLambda}^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\,F\bigl\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen

by overcounting.
∎

As a direct consequence, we also obtain the two versions of the LRB.

Corollary 10

(Lieb-Robinson bound for commuting Hamiltonians)
.

Let
\varPsi
be a commuting interaction on a finite graph
\varLambda
.
Let

Let

\displaystyle{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{\varLambda}}\,\lvert\Uparrow\rangle\lvert\Uparrow\rangle\lvert\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{x\in X}\,\prod_{x\in X}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tCF_{\alpha}\l\lparen d\lparen x,y\rparen\bigr\rparen U_{x,y}}\,\lvert\Uparrow\rangle\lvert\Uparrow\rangle\lvert\psi\rangle

\displaystyle{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}tH_{\varLambda}}\lvert\Uparrow\rangle\lvert\Downarrow\rangle\lvert\psi\rangle

Sharpness of Theorem

To understand that the scaling in the long-range LRBs given in (

which was used to obtain the results from

, is also optimal.
And indeed, for
X=\{0\}
and
Y=\{\nonscript\,y\in\varGamma\nonscript\,|\allowbreak\nonscript\,\mathopen{}d\lparen x,y\rparen\geq R\nonscript\,\}
on

\sum_{y\in Y}F_{\alpha}\l\lparen d\lparen 0,y\rparen\bigr\rparen=\sum_{n=R}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\varGamma\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\

4.1

[

Theorem 11

4.2
Local perturbations perturb locally principle

[
58
]
recently proved a version of the local perturbations perturb locally (LPPL) principle for long-range systems with a new technique.
They avoid the spectral flow
[

The general idea is that the ground state of a gapped system only changes locally around a small perturbation, in the sense that expectations values of local observables away from the perturbation do not change.

With the improved LRBs we can also improve these results for gapped commuting Hamiltonians.

4.3
General Hamiltonians with a commuting part

In contrast to the rest of the paper, we want to investigate non-commuting Hamiltonians which only have a commuting part in this section.
More specifically, we assume to be given two interactions
\varPhi
and
\varPsi
, where
\varPsi
is commuting as before,
\varPhi
might not be commuting and in particular
\varPsi
and
\varPhi
are not required to commute.
For simplicity, we assume that the commuting interaction
\varPsi
is of finite range
R>0
, meaning that
\varPsi(Z)=0
unless
\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen>R
.
The arguments work similarly for short- or long-range interactions, as long as the decay of the commuting part
\varPsi
is better than the one of the non-commuting part
\varPhi
.
Moreover, we allow
\varPhi
to be time-dependent.
We write
\varPhi(t)
for the interaction at time
t
,
\varPhi(Z,t):=\varPhi(t)(Z)
the interaction term at
Z\subset\varLambda
at time
t
, and

\lVert\varPhi\rVert_{F}:=\sup_{t\in I}\lVert\varPhi(t)\rVert_{F}

for the norm of a time-dependent interaction
\varPhi
.
The time interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
of interest will be clear from the context.

In this setting we obtain the following LRB for the evolution of the full system.

Theorem 15
.

Let
\varLambda
be a finite graph and
F\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
a decaying function.
Assume that we have a general Lieb-Robinson bound given in terms of a function
\zeta
, such that for any interaction
\varPhi
with
\lVert\varPhi\rVert_{F}<\infty
, it holds that for all
X
,
Y\subset\varLambda
,
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}

\big\lVert[\tau_{t,s}(A),B]\big\rVert\leq\zeta\Big\lparen\lVert\varPhi\rVert_{F},\lVert A\rVert,\lVert B\rVert,\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert,d\lparen A,B\rparen,\lvert t-s\rvert\Big\rparen.

Furthermore, let
\varPsi
be a commuting interaction of range
R>0
and let
\varPhi
be a general, time-dependent interaction on
\varLambda
satisfying
\lVert\varPhi\rVert_{F(\mathop{\cdot}+2R)}<\infty
.
Then the dynamics
\tau_{t,s}
for the sum
\varPhi+\varPsi
satisfy the following modified Lieb-Robinson bound:
For all
X
,
Y\subset\varLambda
,
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
it holds that

\big\lVert[\tau_{t,s}(A),B]\big\rVert\leq\zeta\Big\lparen c^{2}\,\lVert\varPhi\rVert_{F(\mathop{\cdot}+2R)},\lVert A\rVert,\lVert B\rVert,c\,\lvert X\rvert,c\,\lvert Y\rvert,d\lparen A,B\rparen-2R,\lvert t-s\rvert\Big\rparen,

with
c=(1+\kappa)\,R^{D}
.

In particular, the Lieb-Robinson velocity and the bound do not depend on the norm of the commuting part
\varPsi
.
As long as the evolution exists it could even be unbounded if one works in a setting with infinite dimensional local Hilbert spaces.

As an example we provide the following result for exponentially decaying interactions, where we use the LRB from
[
43
]
.

.

Let
\varLambda
be a finite graph,
\varPsi
a commuting interaction of range
R>0
and
\varPhi
an exponentially decaying interaction with
\lVert\varPhi\rVert_{F(\mathop{\cdot}+2R)}<\infty
where
F(r):={\mathrm{e}}^{-br}/(1+r)^{D+1}

\tau_{t,s}
generated by the interaction
\varPsi+\varPhi
satisfies the following Lieb-Robinson bound:

X
,
Y\subset\varLambda
,
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}

\big\lVert[\tau_{t,s}(A),B]\big\rVert\leq C\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\min\big\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\big\}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{b\,(v_{b}\lvert t-s\rvert-d\lparen X,Y\rparen)},

where
C=2\,\lVert F\rVert\,C_{F}^{-1}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{2bR}
and
v_{b}=2\,C_{F}\,(1+\kappa)^{2}\,R^{2D}\,\lVert\varPhi\rVert_{F(\mathop{\cdot}+2R)}/b
, and

and

are constants depending on

Again, we highlight that the velocity does not depend on the strength of

Applying the above result to the commuting interactions

5.3

The idea of the proof is to go to the interaction picture where

6
Conclusions

We proved that the quantum many-body dynamics produced by mutually commuting long-range interactions is much more strongly constrained than for general long-range interactions.
The difference is fundamental at a physical level, as commutativity directly affects the speed of information propagation and the shape of the light cone.
This finding opens up a new structural divide between general long-range interactions and long-range commuting ones.

While commuting interactions are very special, they are of great current interest in physics in the context of quantum error correction

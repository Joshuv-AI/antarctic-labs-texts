Development of a Consensus-Based Risk Stratification Algorithm for HR+/HER2− Early Breast Cancer to Guide Adjuvant CDK4/6i Therapy: Outcomes from a Multidisciplinary Workshop in India
Authors: Krishna Mohan Mallavarapu, Rakesh Reddy B, Somashekhar SP, Pankaj Goyal, Vishwanath Sathyanarayanan, Amit Rauthan, Jyoti Bajpai, Prabhat Ghanshyam Bhargava, Mohit Agarwal, Rakesh Pinninti, Rachana Chennamaneni, Krishna Chaitanya P, Stalin Chowdary Bala, Venkateswar Rao Pydi, Ramavath Dev, P S Dattatreya, Ullas Batra, Govind Babu K, Disha Shetty, Kumardeep Paul, Priya Rathi, Maheboob Basade
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeHormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2–) early breast cancer (EBC) is treated with curative intent, but risk of recurrence (RoR) remains a challenge. The identification of high RoR EBC patients is inconsistent, highlighting the need for a simplified approach. This study aimed to develop an evidence-informed, consensus-based tool to support recurrence risk stratification and treatment decision-making in patients with HR+/HER2– EBC, incorporating multidisciplinary perspectives and evolving evidence from guidelines and trials.MethodsFive structured, multidisciplinary workshops were conducted across India, involving 135 experts, to integrate evidence and expert consensus into a practical algorithm for RoR assessment. The workshops included initial polling on clinical practices, followed by case-based discussions and literature review, with post-discussion polling used to capture shifts in expert perspectives.ResultsPolling responses demonstrated consensus (defined as ≥70% agreement by experts) that all N1 patients are at a high RoR (94.3%) and that tumors ≥5 cm are associated with high risk (90.0%). Given that low-grade (G1) tumors are associated with a favorable prognosis (low risk) and high-grade (G3) tumors indicate a poorer prognosis (high risk), Ki-67–based treatment decision-making was considered most relevant for the moderate-grade (G2) subgroup. Accordingly, experts agreed that combining tumor grade G2 with Ki-67 assessment improves risk stratification in N0 patients with tumors <5 cm (71.6%). A Ki-67 cut-off of ≥20% was considered critical (96.3%). The experts agreed that adjuvant cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i) have an invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) advantage in all N1 (93.8%) and high risk N0 (91.1%) patients with HR+/HER2− EBC.ConclusionThe developed algorithm provides a user-friendly framework to facilitate uniform risk stratification and optimize HR+/HER2– EBC treatment in India. Further prospective and real-world validation is required to establish its clinical utility and evaluate its impact on treatment decisions.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) remains the most common malignancy affecting women worldwide, accounting for approximately one quarter of all female cancer cases.1,2 In India, BC comprises around 33% of cancers in women, with a mean age at diagnosis of 52 years and a notable 5-year prevalence of 77.9 per 100,000 women.3,4 The hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2–) subtype represents about 55% of BC cases,5 with early-stage diagnosis occurring in nearly 60% of these patients.6

Early BC (EBC) is generally treated with curative intent; however, despite adjuvant endocrine therapy (ET) the risk of recurrence (RoR) remains high.7 In women with HR+/HER2− tumor, the RoR remains beyond 20 years, with approximately 50% of recurrences occurring within the first 5 years after diagnosis.8,9 A meta-analysis by Pan et al demonstrated a 20-year risk of distant recurrence ranging from 22% in patients with zero positive nodes (N0) to 52% in 4–9 positive nodes (N2) in estrogen receptor (ER) positive BC.10 Real-world evidence (RWE) corroborates these findings, showing high RoR in patients with HR+/HER2− EBC despite adjuvant ET, with risk of invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) of 26.1% at 5 years and 45% at 10 years.11

Nodal status, tumor size, histological grade, genomic score, and the Ki-67 proliferation index are key factors in risk assessment, as they influence the RoR.12 However, definitions of high-risk disease in EBC vary across clinical trials, resulting in heterogenous approaches to stratifying patients for additional adjuvant targeted treatment to reduce recurrence risk.7,8,13,14 In EBC, a higher number of involved axillary lymph nodes is conventionally indicative of high risk.7 However, recent trials have shown that even patients with lower nodal burden (1–3 positive nodes [N1]) or N0 disease may carry substantial risk. The monarchE study identified patients with ≥4 positive nodes (N2 or higher) as high risk and only included N1 patients with either tumor size (T) ≥5 cm, high-grade (G3), or Ki-67 ≥20% as eligible for abemaciclib.13 The NATALEE study enrolled a broader patient population, including all node-positive (N+) patients, as well as N0 patients with tumors >5 cm (T3/4N0), and 2–5 cm (T2N0) with G3 disease or moderate-grade (G2) tumors with either Ki-67 ≥20% or high genomic risk, for ribociclib treatment.14 Together, these trials are redefining high-risk EBC beyond traditional nodal staging alone. Furthermore, in India, there are disparities in genomic testing due to the varying access to these tools limiting its routine use for risk assessment. These challenges highlight the need for accurate risk assessment at diagnosis to guide the use of novel adjuvant targeted therapies, such as cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i) or poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors, and support personalized clinical decision-making aimed at reducing the RoR.15–17

Given the lack of standardization in risk definition and stratification, this workshop is the first of its kind in India that aimed to develop a consensus-based algorithm integrating the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) classification and evidence from landmark clinical trials for risk stratification in HR+/HER2– EBC. Multidisciplinary teams play a crucial role in providing optimal care for patients with BC.18 Developed through a multidisciplinary approach; this algorithm may facilitate uniform risk stratification and support selection of adjuvant therapies, including CDK4/6i, in routine clinical practice.

Materials and Methods

Methodology

Multidisciplinary co-creation workshops were conducted to develop an evidence-based risk stratification algorithm for assessing Indian patients with HR+/HER2− EBC. At the outset, a comprehensive literature review was performed using PubMed and Embase search engines to identify gaps in RoR assessment and management specific to HR+/HER2− EBC. The literature search included relevant randomized controlled trials, meta-analyses, review articles, and guidelines on HR+/HER2− EBC with search terms like “breast cancer”, “early onset”, “HR+/HER2−”, “recurrence risk”, “tumor characteristics”, “histopathological features”, “nodal status”, “prognostic risk factors”, “relapse risk”, “risk stratification”, “adjuvant therapy”, “endocrine therapy”, “targeted therapy”, “CDK4/6i”. All identified articles published between 2000 and 2025 were assessed for suitability. Reports from meetings that had not been published in peer-reviewed journals were excluded. The insights from this review and nominal group technique were used to design interactive workshops to document current clinical practices, facilitate multidisciplinary discussions, and drive the development of a consensus driven algorithm. To ensure methodological rigor, relevance, and consistency across sites, both the workshop content and the overall program framework were reviewed and validated by a steering committee.

Participant Selection

Multidisciplinary experts from 28 Centers of Excellence (CoEs) participated in five workshops held between July 2024 and January 2025 across Delhi, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Visakhapatnam, and Mumbai. Centers included corporate chain cancer centers, government- and trust-owned regional cancer centers, from both Tier I and Tier II cities. Experts were recruited from the oncology units of the CoEs across different regions of India to ensure diversity of perspectives. The participants included the head of the oncology unit and other oncology members of the multidisciplinary oncology team, such as medical oncologists, surgical oncologists, radiation oncologists, and onco-pathologists.

Workshop Structure

Each workshop was led by a program director (a national key opinion leader), supported by other senior faculty (program moderators) who facilitated group discussions. The workshop design (Figure 1) followed a three-step approach, collectively contributing to the development of a risk-stratification algorithm for assessing RoR in HR+/HER2− EBC. Step I – Understanding Current Risk Assessment Practices

A preliminary eight-question poll was conducted at the start of each workshop to capture current practices in risk evaluation and management. Following the poll, participants were allocated to three–four small groups; each group comprised a mix of specialties (medical oncology, surgical oncology, onco-pathology, and radiation oncology). The groups were presented with two hypothetical real-world clinical scenarios. Case 1—tumor size 2–5 cm with 1–3 positive nodes (T2N1): A postmenopausal woman aged 50 years with a body mass index (BMI) of 34.4 kg/m2 was described as having a 4 cm invasive ductal carcinoma located in the upper outer quadrant of the right breast, and no family history of cancer. The tumor was ER+ (90%) and progesterone receptor (PR) positive (10%), with HER2– status confirmed by immunohistochemistry (IHC). Sentinel lymph node biopsy demonstrated three positive lymph nodes. The tumor was G2 with a Ki-67 index of 25%. Case 2—tumor size 2–5 cm with zero positive nodes (T2N0): A postmenopausal woman aged 56 years (BMI 34.4 kg/m2) was described as having a 4.2 cm invasive ductal carcinoma in the upper outer quadrant of the right breast, and no family history of cancer. The tumor was ER+ (80%) and PR+ (60%), with HER2– status confirmed by IHC. Sentinel lymph node biopsy showed no nodal involvement. The tumor was G3 with a Ki-67 index of 30%.

Alternate scenarios (including tumor size <2 cm with 1–3 positive nodes [T1N1], tumor size >5 cm with 1–3 positive nodes [T3N1], tumor size >5 cm with zero positive nodes [T3N0], and T2N0 with various tumor grades and Ki-67 indices) were introduced to stimulate comprehensive evaluation and discussion. The case-based discussion results were synthesized by the steering committee during the workshops.

Step II – Guided Review of the Evidence Base

Program moderators guided participants through the comprehensive literature review of pivotal trials and real-world analyses describing recurrence risk by nodal status and other clinicopathological features, emphasizing differences between trial populations, eligibility criteria and primary endpoints.

Step III – Ideation and Development of the Risk Stratification Algorithm

After literature review and case discussions, the eight polling questions were re-distributed to assess shifts in experts’ perspectives regarding risk assessment in node-positive and node-negative EBC. Program moderators then synthesized the group inputs to build consensus (defined as ≥70% agreement) based on risk factors. Responses from the discussions were synthesized by the steering committee. Through iterative group discussions, an easy-to-use, color-coded risk stratification algorithm was co-created for clinical application. For components with <70% agreement, further discussion was conducted to reach a common alignment. Real-time polling and discussions enabled mapping shifts in participant perceptions. Responses from the pre- and post-discussion polls were not linked at the individual participant level, and the number of respondents differed between the polls as per participant availability throughout the workshops.

Statistical Analysis

Participant responses to pre- and post-workshop polling questions were analyzed using SPSS software. Differences in response distributions were assessed using the two-sample Z-test. Owing to anonymous polling and differing number of participants in the pre- and post-discussion polling responses, the datasets were treated as independent samples.

Results

A total of 135 multidisciplinary experts participated in five workshops. The panel comprised medical oncologists (53.3%), onco-surgeons (20.7%), radiation oncologists (19.3%), and onco-pathologists (6.7%). The results of case-based expert discussions and polling results revealed distinct patterns in risk assessment and treatment recommendations for patients with HR+/HER2− EBC.

Case-Based Discussions

For Case 1 (T2N1), ~55% of experts assigned a stage IIB classification and considered the patient as high risk, primarily due to nodal involvement, Ki-67 ≥20%, higher tumor grade, and larger tumor size. In addition, 18.2% suggested incorporating Oncotype DX testing to further refine prognostication, particularly in cases with low PR expression or elevated Ki-67. All experts (100%) advocated the use of a CDK4/6i in adjuvant therapy, consistently referring to the NATALEE trial as a guiding source for this case. The estimated recurrence risk for this case cohort was 10–20% at 3, 5, and 10 years. In alternative staging scenarios, including T1N1 or T3N1, most experts maintained their treatment strategy (90.9% for T1N1; 54.5% for T3N1). However, a subset (45.5% for T3N1) preferred neoadjuvant chemotherapy or proposed sequential chemotherapy and surgery. A small proportion of participants (~9%; limited to T3N1 scenario) considered abemaciclib or ribociclib as alternative agents, although overall management remained largely unchanged. Among patients with only one to two positive lymph nodes (within N1 status), ~64% of experts agreed that their management approach would not change.

For Case 2 (T2N0), ~57% of experts assigned stage IIA classification and regarded this patient as high risk. Genomic profiling was emphasized to refine risk categorization. Key risk factors identified included the genomic profile, tumor grade, tumor size, and Ki-67 index. Many experts (~86%) supported adjuvant therapy with CDK4/6i, referring to data from the NATALEE trial. The estimated RoR for this case was <10% at 3 and 5 years, rising to 10–20% at 10 years. When considering a G2 tumor with a higher Ki-67 of 30%, experts largely retained their overall management approach but highlighted genomic testing to optimize chemotherapy choices and guide selection of CDK4/6i therapy. In an alternate scenario for T3N0 the responses varied: 57.1% of experts favored consistent management, while 42.9% proposed additional radiation or neoadjuvant chemotherapy in cases where upfront surgery had been performed.

A Ki-67 threshold of ≥20% was identified as critical in determining higher RoR or progression. Majority of experts supported adding CDK4/6i to adjuvant management, with 91.1% favoring its use in high-risk N0 patients and 93.8% in N1 patients, citing improvements in iDFS. Discussion

We developed a risk stratification algorithm through multidisciplinary expert consensus to facilitate informed decision-making particularly in subgroups traditionally not classified as high risk. The algorithm takes into consideration prognostic and predictive factors to support assessment of recurrence risk in HR+/HER2− EBC. Prognostic factors include nodal status, tumor size, histological grade, and the Ki-67 proliferation index, whereas predictive factors include genomic score and the Ki-67 proliferation index, which may also predict response to selected therapies (Figure 2).17,23,24 A consensus was reached for each component of the algorithm by recording shifts in perspective of experts after case-based discussions and a review of the literature. The algorithm is intended to address a broader spectrum of HR+/HER2− EBC patients at high RoR, including all N1 patients and N0 patients at high risk.

Lymph node status is considered as one of the key factors determining RoR.12 While N2 disease is unequivocally associated with increased RoR, accumulating evidence indicates that this also applies to all N1 patients.10 In pre-workshop polling, 67% of experts agreed that all N1 HR+/HER2− EBC patients have a high RoR (Table 1). The Pan et al meta-analysis demonstrated that all N1 patients are at a high risk of distant recurrence, with a similar RoR irrespective of tumor size (T1 or T2). This risk persists beyond the 5-year treatment duration of adjuvant ET and continues to rise over the patient’s lifetime.10 The long-term (up to 20-year) RoR in N1 patients ranges from 25% to 39.4% (Table 2). Data from RWE by Razavi et al also reinforces this observation, reporting recurrence rates of 5.5%, 11.1%, and 16% at 3, 5, and 7 years respectively in patients with stage II/III N1 EBC, regardless of the monarchE high-risk criteria, which restricted N1 inclusion to either T ≥5 cm, or G3, or Ki-67 ≥20%.25 These findings underscore the relevance of the NATALEE approach, which categorizes all N1 patients as high risk, irrespective of the presence of other factors.14 Based on this evidence and discussion amongst the panelists, 94.3% of experts agreed in the post-discussion polling that all N1 HR+/HER2− EBC patients should be considered at high risk. Targeted treatment is crucial to reduce early recurrence and metastatic progression in patients at high RoR.13 Several Phase III trials have investigated the addition of CDK4/6i to standard ET in the adjuvant setting for EBC, and the results depict notable differences among the different CDK4/6i.14 In monarchE, abemaciclib plus standard-of-care (SOC) ET (tamoxifen/aromatase inhibitor and/gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist) demonstrated significant iDFS benefit over ET alone by reducing the risk of invasive disease by 25% at 2 years (hazard ratio [HR], 0.75; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.60–0.93; p = 0.01) but only included a subset of N1 patients.13 While in the NATALEE trial, ribociclib plus non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor (NSAI) demonstrated iDFS benefit over NSAI alone by reducing the risk of invasive disease by 26.9% (HR, 0.731; 95% CI, 0.617–0.866) in all N+ patients, including all N1 subgroups at 4 years.32 Following review of these data, 93.8% of experts agreed that adding CDK4/6i provides a clinically meaningful iDFS benefit for all N1 patients.

Tumor size is a key component of the AJCC staging system and a well-established risk factor predicting prognosis in patients with HR+/HER2− EBC, with increasing size associated with poor outcomes.12 Both the monarchE trial, which defined N1 patients with T3 tumors as high risk, and the NATALEE trial, which categorized even N0 patients with T3 tumors as high risk, reinforce the prognostic significance of tumor size independent of nodal status. In contrast, for N0 patients with T2 tumors, risk stratification typically requires integration with additional factors.14 Evidence from Pan et al further supports this, identifying primary tumor size as a significant adverse prognostic factor for distant relapse over 5–20 years of follow-up. In this meta-analysis, the risk of distant recurrence ranged from 13% to 34% in patients with T1 tumors with varying nodal status (N0-N2), and from 19% to 41% in those with T2 tumors.10 Based on this evidence, 90% of experts in the post-discussion polling agreed that T3 (T ≥ 5 cm) can be considered as an independent determinant of high RoR in all HR+/HER2− EBC patients.

Histological grade is a clinically relevant prognostic factor associated with RoR in patients with HR+/HER2− EBC.17 G1 tumors are generally associated with favorable outcomes, with recurrences being rare and typically occurring later in a patient’s lifetime. In contrast, G2 tumors demonstrate an intermediate prognosis in the early years of follow-up with a higher likelihood of multiple recurrences in the long-term. G3 tumors present a significantly elevated risk of early recurrence and mortality.12 In patients with tumor size <2 cm and node-negative disease (T1N0), Pan et al reported 20-year recurrence risk of 13% for G2 and 17% for G3.10 Mauriac et al reported that higher tumor grade is associated with an increased RoR. Over a 2-year period, RoR was >2 times higher in G3 versus G1 tumors (HR, 2.43; 95% CI, 1.6–3.6; p < 0.001) and 1.5 times higher in G2 versus G1 tumors (HR, 1.55; 95% CI, 1.1–2.3; p = 0.02).33 After the post-discussion polling, an expert consensus was reached based on further discussions and consideration of the literature that grading of tumor tissue can add to risk stratification and treatment selection in N0 patients with tumor size <5 cm.

Although histological grading is recognized for its prognostic significance, variation in grading leads to concerns regarding its reproducibility. Considering additional proliferation markers like Ki-67 can aid in reducing variability associated with grading.34 Ki-67 may be useful in identifying patients with an unfavorable prognosis (especially patients with stage II, G2 disease) who may benefit from further therapy (Table 1). Fasching et al reported 5-year disease free survival (DFS) rates of 0.86 and 0.89 for stage II and G2 patients with Ki-67 <10%, 0.90 and 0.88 for Ki-67 10–19%, 0.77 and 0.78 for Ki-67 ≥20%, respectively. These findings suggest that RoR approximately doubles when the Ki-67 cut-off is ≥20% compared with 10–19% in both stage II and G2 patients.21 A study by Ehinger et al reported that high Ki-67 is associated with an increased risk of distant metastasis (HR, 1.5; 95% CI, 0.80–2.8) in patients with pT1–2, pN0–1 in the G2 subgroup. Patients with G1 BC have a good prognosis, while those with G3 BC have a worse prognosis. Therefore, therapy decisions based on Ki-67 must be restricted to the G2 subgroup.19 In light of this literature, 71.6% of experts agreed that Ki-67 score can add to the risk stratification decision as well as treatment selection in N0 patients with T <5 cm and G2 tumor.

There is a lack of an international consensus on cut-off values for Ki-67; however, the International Ki-67 in Breast Cancer Working Group recommends <10% as low and >30% as high.12 Both monarchE and NATALEE considered Ki-67 ≥20% with other risk factors for identifying high-risk patients who may benefit from adjuvant targeted therapy with CDK4/6i.13,18 Expert agreement on considering Ki-67 ≥20% as a critical marker for identifying HR+/HER2− EBC patients at high RoR increased from 72% in pre-discussion to 96.3% in post-discussion polling.

Genomic signatures (GSs) enhance pathological assessments by providing additional prognostic and predictive insights. Several validated GSs, including Oncotype DX®, MammaPrint®, Prosigna®, and EndoPredict®, aid in adjuvant therapy decisions.12 The American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) guidelines recommend Oncotype DX for both pre- and postmenopausal N0 patients to guide therapy decisions for adjuvant targeted therapy.20 Similarly, the experts agreed that genomic scoring can be used as an additional factor for risk stratification of N0 patients with T2 tumors. Further, the expert panel noted that while a high genomic risk score adds to the strength of risk stratification and choosing chemotherapy, it does not appear to be the only determinant. In cases where the genomic risk score is low but pathological risk is high, the utility of CDK4/6i should be evaluated on a case-to-case basis in clinical practice. However, while genomic scores may serve as an adjunct to established clinicopathological factors, they may not be universally accessible or routinely used across all centers in India to assess the need for chemotherapy.35

The risk factors discussed above, including histological grade, Ki-67, and genomic score, help in identifying N0 patients at high RoR. The NATALEE study defined N0 patients with G2 tumor, Ki-67 ≥20%, and high genomic score as high risk.14 High-risk N0 patients, as defined in NATALEE, have recurrence risks comparable to N1 category which is about 26% at 10 years and 39.4% up to 20 years (Table 2). Additionally, in long term, the RoR in both NATALEE N0 and N1 patients rises to levels comparable with N2 (Table 2). Even RWE indicates that a subset of N0 patients has high RoR.30 Detailed RoR estimates from various studies are presented in Table S2. In the pre-discussion polling, 72.7% of experts believed there is an iDFS advantage by addition of CDK4/6i to adjuvant management in all high risk N0 HR+/HER2− EBC patients. In such high-risk patients, ribociclib plus NSAI demonstrated an iDFS benefit of 33.4% (HR, 0.666; 95% CI, 0.397–1.118) over NSAI alone at 4 years.32 After review of this literature, 91.1% of experts supported the use of CDK4/6i in the management of high risk N0 HR+/HER2− EBC patients.

Understanding the safety profile and health-related QoL (HRQoL) implications of CDK4/6i is crucial for clinical decision making. In the monarchE study, the most common all-grade adverse events (AEs) leading to abemaciclib discontinuation were diarrhea and fatigue, whereas in the NATALEE study, the most frequent reasons for ribociclib discontinuation were neutropenia, liver-related AEs, and arthralgia.29,36 No new safety concerns were reported with adjuvant ribociclib compared to known advanced BC safety profile and the AEs were manageable with dose modifications.29,37 Patient-reported outcomes have demonstrated maintenance of HRQoL with both ribociclib and abemaciclib.38,39 Safety and QoL were agreed to be critical in the choice of adjuvant CDK4/6i for management of HR+/HER2− EBC by 98.8% experts.

Limitations

Despite the structured methodology, there are certain limitations that should be acknowledged. Variations in polling participation, including an approximately 20% reduction in post-discussion responses, may introduce response bias. Additionally, participating experts may not fully represent all clinicians managing HR+/HER2− EBC in India, potentially introducing selection bias. As the algorithm was developed based on expert consensus and an evaluation of the available clinical evidence, further validation in real-world clinical settings is warranted. Incorporation of additional real-world evidence, along with long-term results from monarchE and NATALEE, may refine its clinical application. Future research should explore strategies to optimize use of CDK4/6i, particularly in high-risk populations where limited efficacy remains a challenge. Overall, the proposed algorithm provides a practical framework that may evolve with emerging evidence and contribute to improved patient outcomes.

Conclusion

The outcomes of this workshop include the development of a consensus-based risk stratification algorithm designed to support risk stratification in HR+/HER2− EBC, including high-risk N0 and all N1 patients. Developed through literature review, case-based discussions, and iterative polling, the algorithm reflects alignment among a robust multidisciplinary expert panel across India, within a novel workshop framework. It integrates established prognostic and predictive factors, along with real-world clinical reasoning that may support consistent, evidence-based, and personalized treatment decision-making in routine practice. As the algorithm is based on expert consensus informed by available clinical evidence, prospective validation in real-world clinical settings is essential to confirm its predictive performance and clinical utility across diverse healthcare environments.

Enhancing Optical and Electrical Performances via Nanocrystalline Si-Based Thin Films for Si Heterojunction Solar Cells
Authors: Bingquan Liang, Xinliang Chen, Heze Yuan, Xuejiao Wang, Guofu Hou, Ying Zhao, Xiaodan Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSilicon heterojunction (SHJ) solar cells, as one of the most promising passivated contact solar cell technologies of the next generation, have the advantages of high conversion efficiency, high open-circuit voltage, low-temperature coefficient, and no potential-induced degradation. For the single-side rear-emitter SHJ solar cells, the n-type carrier selective layer, which serves as the light-incident side, plays a pivotal role in determining the performance of heterojunction devices. Consequently, a superior n-doped layer should exhibit high optical transmittance and minimal optical absorption, along with a substantial effective doping level to guarantee the formation of dark conductivity (σd) and electron-transport capacity. In this work, we investigated the optical and electrical properties of different n-type monolayers and stacked gradient multilayers, including monolayer, bilayer, and trilayer Si-based thin films, acting as electron-transport layers (ETL) prepared by plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition, and studied the influences of these above layers on the performance of SHJ solar cells. The experimental results demonstrate that the ETL with an n-nc-Si:H/n-nc-SiOx:H/n+-nc-Si:H trilayer structure exhibits the potential to boost highly efficient solar cells. The bottom highly crystallized, lightly phosphorus-doped n-nc-Si:H film promotes rapid nucleation of the intermediate n-nc-SiOx:H film and thus reduces the thickness of the incubation layer, as well as improves the passivation contact. The n-nc-SiOx:H film in the middle layer provides excellent optical properties and reduces parasitic absorption, thereby increasing the short-circuit current density. Furthermore, the highly doped n+-nc-Si:H at the top offers an optimal ohmic contact with the reactive plasma deposition-grown TCO layer, which ultimately enhances the fill factor. Ultimately, a conversion efficiency of 20.41%, with an open-circuit voltage of 720 mV, a short-circuit current density of 39.34 mA/cm2, and a filling factor of 72.05%, was achieved in the SHJ solar cell using a typical trilayer structure. This kind of trilayer structure has a particular significance for potential industrialized applications as it allows for efficient utilization of solar energy.

Introduction

Currently, crystalline silicon (c-Si) cells account for 95% of the global market share, and photovoltaic solar cells have become the fastest growing type of solar energy harvesting technology.1 Silicon heterojunction (SHJ) solar cell is an efficient photovoltaic device composed of a heterojunction formed by a nanocrystalline silicon/amorphous silicon-based film, which is one of the most important photovoltaic technologies.1,2 At present, the efficiency of the double-side SHJ solar cell has reached 26.81%, and the efficiency of the heterojunction device using a heterojunctin back contact structure has been up to 27.30%.3,4 The traditional SHJ solar cell technology employs hydrogenated amorphous silicon (a-Si:H) as the carrier selection layer.5,6 However, the parasitic absorption of this material in the short-wave region and its low dark conductivity face against significant challenges for further extensive use in high efficiency research.7 Recently, research efforts have been shifted toward doped nanocrystalline silicon (nc-Si:H) thin films and doped nanocrystalline silicon oxygen alloy (nc-SiOx:H) acting as carrier-selective layers for SHJ cells.6,8−13 The wide-band gap-doped nanocrystalline silicon (nc-Si:H) thin films are mainly constituted of amorphous silicon (a-Si:H) and nanao crystalline (nc-Si:H) mixed crystal phase, which makes nc-Si:H possess higher doping efficiency than a-Si:H.8,9 Furthermore, the optical [like transmittance, absorptivity, refractive index (n), and extinction coefficient (k)] and electrical (like dark conductivity, σd) properties of nc-Si:H are markedly influenced by the crystallization fraction (χC) of nc-Si:H thin films.9,11 The nc-Si:H thin films with higher crystallinity exhibit lower parasitic absorption and superior σd, conferring an advantage to nc-Si:H as a carrier-selective layer in SHJ solar cells.10 Doped nanocrystalline silicon–oxygen alloy (nc-SiOx:H) consists of three phases: nanocrystalline silicon (nc-Si:H), amorphous silicon (a-Si:H), and amorphous silicon oxide (a-SiOx:H) phase, where the a-SiOx:H phase helps further reduce the refractive index (n) to reduce optical loss, and the nc-Si:H phase is instrumental in maintaining high dark conductivity.12−16

At present, the mainstream SHJ solar cells mainly use the rear emitter structure, and this structure has a higher filling factor (FF) than the traditional design with the front emitter structure because it can achieve high electron transverse conduction through the absorption layer (silicon wafer).17,18 Given that the majority of carrier transport in the n-type layer is facilitated by n-type silicon wafers, it is imperative that the n-type carrier selection layer exhibits optimal permeability and conductivity, along with exemplary hole blocking and electron transport capabilities.19 Additionally, it must provide sufficient band bending to increase field effect passivation and decelerate interface recombination.20 However, SHJ solar cells typically use very thin Si-based films as carrier-selective layers, which makes it very difficult to achieve high crystallinity and excellent optical and electrical properties. nc-Si:H and nc-SiOx:H thin-film materials usually are set at very thin thicknesses of 10–20 nm.21 This gives rise to a number of issues to obtain high χC. It is necessary to increase the hydrogen dilution ratio and power density.22−24 However, a high hydrogen dilution ratio will increase the effect of the H etching passivation layer, and a large power density will produce bombardment on the passivation layer, which will lead to the risk of degradation of passivation quality. Interfacial treatments and the application of seed layers are effective methods to solve these issues. Mazzarella et al.25 used CO2 plasma surface treatment to achieve rapid nucleation during film deposition, which enhanced the χC and doping efficiency of the Si films, thus improved the FF and open-circuit voltage (Voc) of the devices. Zhao et al.26 applied the postdeposition hydrogen plasma treatment (HPT)+ very high frequency (i) nc-Si:H interface to treat the surface of the stacked structure of nc-SiOx:H and nanocrystalline silicon before deposition, and significantly ameliorate the electrical properties of the bilayer structure contact. Qiu et al.27 employed nc-Si:H seed layer to prepare an ultrathin nc-SiOx:H layer, which increased FF and efficiency η, and proved that the seed layer plays a role in reducing electrical transport loss through AFORS-HET and Quokka 3 simulation. Kirner et al.28 studied that depositing a thin (∼3 nm) layer of highly doped nanocrystalline silicon on top of the emitter can significantly increase the FF.

In this study, bilayer gradient n-type nc-Si:H/nc-SiOx:H thin films are designed as the front surface field of SHJ solar cells by taking advantage of the high crystallization and dark high conductivity characteristics of the n-nc-Si:H seed layer and the low parasitic absorption characteristics of the nc-SiOx:H thin film. The experimental results showed that the seed layer at the bottom effectively improves the χC, optical and electrical properties of the nc-SiOx:H films. In order to further improve the contact characteristics of the transparent conductive oxide film, a highly doped contact layer is deposited on the bilayer structure to form trilayer Si thin films, and thus, the specific contact resistivity is reduced from 72 to 35 mΩ cm2. The trilayer structure is expected to be an effective way to improve the performances of SHJ solar cells.

Materials and Methods

Thin-Film Materials and Solar Cell Preparation

FZ n-type c-Si wafers with ⟨100⟩ plane orientation, a thickness of 130 μm, and a resistivity of 2–3 Ω·cm were cleaned. Then, the same intrinsic amorphous silicon passivated layers were deposited on both sides of the wafers by multichamber RF-PECVD (f = 13.56 MHz), and the discharge electrode spacing of the plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition system is 21.5 mm. Deposition parameters for different n-type silicon thin films as the electron selection layer are shown in Table 1, in which both PH3 and SiH4 gas are diluted with hydrogen, and the p-type amorphous silicon is deposited on the p side to act as the hole selection layer. The optical and electrical characteristics of the Si-based films were modulated by manipulating the doping gas and CO2 flow rates during the deposition procedure. At room temperature, gallium-doped ZnO (GZO) was deposited as the TCO layer grown by the reactive plasma deposition (RPD) technology at 0.5 Pa, using Ga2O3-doped ZnO target (3.0 wt %, purity: 99.99%).

Thin Film and Device Characterization

Thicknesses were evaluated by using a step profilometer (Kosaka Lab, ET200). The coplanar σd of the films was measured using a Keithley 617 programmable electrometer. The Cox-Strack method was used to calculate the specific contact resistivity (ρc). The test structure was Ag/GZO/n-layer/i-a-Si:H/n-c-Si/i-a-Si:H/n-layer/GZO/Ag, in which seven disc electrodes with different diameters were used on the n-side (d = 0.36 0.24, 0.20, 0.18, 0.16, 0.12, 0.10 cm), and the p-side used a full-area silver electrode. Optical transmittance was measured using an UV–vis–NIR spectrophotometer (Varian, Cary 5000). The refractive index (n) and extinction coefficient (k) were extracted by ellipsis and fitted by the Tauc-Lorentz model. The Raman spectra of the materials were measured using a Renishaw Via Raman system with a laser wavelength of 532 nm and power of 100 mW to evaluate the crystallinity. The effective minority carrier lifetime (τeff) and implied open-circuit voltage (iVoc) of the cell were measured by using the quasi-steady-state photoconductance (QSSPC) method of the Sinton WCT-120 system. The current–voltage (I–V) curves of the solar cells under standard conditions (AM1.5, 25 °C, 100 mW/cm2) were measured by using a dual-source solar simulator (Wacom class AAA). The external quantum efficiency (EQE) was measured using the PV measurement QE-X10 system.

Results and Discussion

Monolayer Si-Based Thin Films

During film deposition, the concentration of active hydrogen was increased by increasing the hydrogen dilution ratio (RH/H2/SiH4), and the energy of active hydrogen was enhanced by increasing the power in order to gain n-nc-Si:H thin films with a greater χC. Figure 2a illustrates the Raman patterns of silicon thin films under different hydrogen dilution ratios. When RH is set at 40 and the power density is 36.3 mW/cm2, the Raman spectrum peak of the silicon film is mainly located at 480 cm–1 and the χC of the silicon film is only 20%, indicating that it is primarily in an amorphous state and that its optical and electrical properties are inadequate to meet the demands of the device. When the RH is increased to >80 and the power density is 43.5 mW/cm2, the Raman spectrum peak of the silicon thin film progressively becomes closer to 515 cm–1, and the χC of the thin film can reach more than 50%. In addition, the σd of the film is about 15 times higher than that of the amorphous silicon film (from 1.25 to18.21 S/cm), and the optical transmittance of the film is also greatly improved, as shown in Figure 2b,c.

(d–f) Planar TEM images of silicon films prepared under different hydrogen dilution ratios (the ordered atom arrangement in the typical crystal region is marked with red frame lines).

It can be seen that the σd of the film shows a positive correlation with the χC of the film, which is similar to the research report of Umishio et al.29 σd is determined by the effective carrier mobility μ and carrier density n of the film:

μ mainly depends on the microstructure of the film, especially the proportion of a high-conductivity nanocrystalline silicon phase.30 Therefore, the effect of RH on σd is mainly reflected in two aspects: the χC crystallization fraction and doping efficiency. When the power density and hydrogen dilution ratio are increased, the χC of the silicon film is improved, which provides more nanopercolation paths and increases the σd. As a result, applying n-nc-Si:H to SHJ solar cells can lower the series resistance and enhance the device’s contact performances.

Figure 3 shows the optical band gap (calculated by the Tauc-Plot method) and growth rate of the nanocrystalline silicon thin films. Similar to the report of Liao et al.’s research, the growth rate of the film becomes slow and the optical band gap gradually widens with a gradual rise in the RH. The main reason why the growth rate of nanocrystalline silicon thin films decreased from 2.9 to 1.5 nm/min was that the concentration of SiH4 in the reaction chamber decreased, the concentration of decomposed SiHn active groups in the reaction chamber reduced correspondingly under the action of radio frequency, and finally the growth rate of the thin films lowers. The optical band gap of the film increases monotonically from 1.71 to 1.91 eV with the increase of nanocrystalline silicon clusters in the n-a-Si:H matrix, which is mainly affected by the nanometer quantum size effect, and it is related to the nanometer size and quantum discrete energy level of the film material, as shown in Figure S1.

It can be seen that the crystalline states of nanocrystalline silicon thin films gradually increase with the continuous increase of RH. In addition, the surface effect is also related to the band structure, and the surface effect is closely related to the bond energy and atomic ratio of the surface atoms. Due to stronger Si–Si in nc-Si:H thin films, the proportion of nc-Si:H phase in the thin films increases, and these strong Si–Si bonds have higher bond energy, resulting in a decrease in the energy level at the top of the valence band and an increase in the energy level at the bottom of the conduction band. Finally, the optical band gap of the film is widened.

Doped nc-Si:H and oxygen alloys (nc-SiOx:H) were prepared on the basis of nanocrystalline silicon. nc-SiOx:H has highly transparent oxygen-rich amorphous oxide (a-SiOx:H) and high conductivity nc-Si:H. As a result, by adjustment of the deposition process, the optical and electrical properties as well as the optical band gap of the nc-SiOx:H thin films can be varied widely. It is reported that Si and O are easier to form Si–O bonds; thus, the O atom in nc-SiOx:H usually inhibits the growth of crystal phase. Consequently, with the incorporation of more O atoms, the χC of the film will gradually decrease.33,34Figure 4 shows the trend of optical and electrical properties of n-nc-SiOx:H. With the injection of CO2 gas, the film’s band gap gradually increased from 1.87 to 2.11 eV, as shown in Figure S1. The reason why the gradual widening of the film band gap here is the influence of surface effects, which is mainly related to the bonding energy of the nc-SiOx:H thin film. The bond energy of Si–O (8.4 eV) is much stronger than that of Si–Si (2.4 eV), and with the gradual increase of CO2 gas flow rate, the proportion of strong Si–O and Si–Si bonds content in the film gradually increases, which is the main reason for the gradual increase of the optical band gap of the nc-SiOx:H thin films.35,36 As can be seen from Figure 4, with the gradual increase of the CO2 flow rate, the refractive index of the nc-SiOx:H thin-film material gradually decreases, the transmittance gradually increases, and the σd of the film gradually decreases. When RC (CO2/PH3) is 0.5, σd of the film has been reduced to about 10–2, and χC is low. It is proved that the n-nc-SiOx:H prepared at large CO2 (RC > 0.5) gas flow rate is not enough to meet the requirements of the SHJ solar cells. The excellent optical properties of n-nc-SiOx:H thin films can further reduce the parasitic absorption and increase the short-circuit current density of SHJ solar cells.

Improve Optical, Electrical, and Contact Characteristics of Bi - and Trilayer Si-Based Thin Films

It is important to obtain high electrical conductivity for n-nc-SiOx:H thin-film materials when applied directly to SHJ solar cells. The ρc of 116 mΩ cm2 between n-nc-SiOx:H/TCO is larger than that of 28 mΩ cm2 between n-nc-Si:H/TCO. In terms of passivation contact, nc-SiOx:H/i-a-Si:H exhibits larger nonohmic contact than n-nc-Si:H/i-a-Si:H. n-nc-SiOx:H and n-nc-Si:H thin films have different optical and electrical properties, in which the n-nc-Si:H thin film presents higher crystallization fraction and better contact performance, and the n-nc-SiOx:H thin film exhibits excellent optical properties. Accordingly, a bilayer gradient n-nc-Si:H/n-nc-SiOx:H film was designed. The n-nc-Si:H seed layer, which exhibits a high crystallization fraction and low doping concentration, was employed to facilitate the growth of n-nc-SiOx:H. The seed layer provides additional nucleation sites for the nc-SiO:H film, thereby enhancing the χC of the n-nc-SiOx:H film. Typical optical and electrical properties and Raman spectra are shown in Figure 5.

Finally, a low-cost and low damage gallium-doped ZnO (GZO) thin film was prepared at room temperature using the RPD technology as the TCO layer of SHJ solar cells. The optical and electrical properties of GZO with different doping concentrations were studied, and the 3.0 wt % GZO presents the best optical and electrical properties, and the detailed parameter of GZO can be referred to our previous work.41 The performance parameters of SHJ solar cells with different n-type Si-based thin films as front surface fields are shown in Figure 7c,d and Table 2.

Due to moderate optical and electrical properties, the SHJ solar cell with an n-a-Si:H thin film has a low short-circuit current density (Jsc) of related solar cells, and its Jsc is only 36.3 mA/cm2. Its high contact resistance makes the FF of the cell only 59.67%, which is much lower than that of devices with other n-type thin films. Compared with the monolayer n-nc-SiOx:H thin film, the bilayer thin film uses the nc-Si:H thin film as the passivation contact layer, which improves the passivation contact characteristics of the device. The efficiency of SHJ solar cells is finally increased by 2.47%, and the FF is increased by 6.56%. On the basis of the bilayer film, a trilayer gradient silicon film is further designed and optimized, which not only slightly reduces the thin-film transmittance but also improves the contact characteristics of the overall device. The performance of the solar cell using the trilayer film is greatly improved, and the series resistance Rs is 1.43 Ω. Compared with the SHJ solar cell with a n-a-Si:H thin-film material, the FF of the device with a trilayer Si thin film is increased by 12.38%, the open-circuit voltage Voc is increased by 31 mV, the short-circuit current density Jsc is increased by 3.04 mA/cm2, and thus, the final efficiency is increased by 5.49%.

Conclusions

The growth characteristics of n-type n-nc-Si:H and n-nc-SiOx:H thin films were systematically studied, and silicon thin films with a typical trilayer structure of gradient trilayer n-nc-Si:H/n-nc-SiOx:H/n+-nc-Si:H were constructed and applied to the front surface field of SHJ solar cells. This structure has better optical and electrical properties and a wider band gap than the conventional n-a-Si:H. This structure has a high degree of crystallization and retains superb optical properties while at the same time achieving excellent electrical properties. The trilayer Si-based thin-film material improves the carrier selectivity of SHJ solar cells by enhancing the field effect passivation, increases the Jsc, and reduces the series. With this novel trilayer si-based film, we obtain a Voc of 720 mV, a Jsc of 39.34 mA/cm2, an FF of 72.05%, and thus an efficiency of 20.41%.

Cantor digraphs and abbreviations of formulas
Martin Klazar

Abstract
A digraph D = ⟨ V , E ⟩ D=\langle V,E\rangle ( E ⊂ V × V E\subset V\times V ) is Cantor if Cantor’s theorem — for no set there is a surjection from it to its power set — holds in D D , in the sense we explain. We construct a ZF formula φ \varphi with length 494 494 such that D ⊧ φ D\models\varphi iff D D is Cantor. In order to obtain φ \varphi , which is a word over the alphabet { x 1 , x 2 , … } ∪ { ∈ , = , ¬ , → , ↔ , ∧ , ∨ , ∃ , ∀ , ( , ) } , \{x_{1},\,x_{2},\,\dots\}\cup\{\in,\,=,\,\neg,\,\to,\,\leftrightarrow,\,\wedge,\,\vee,\,\exists,\,\forall,\,(,\,)\}\,, we devise abbreviation schemes of ZF formulas. We introduce extensive and strongly extensive digraphs and show, by the standard argument, that they are Cantor. We construct a countable strongly extensive digraph with arbitrarily large finite in-degrees.

Let

The following definition is in fact a main result of our article.

Definition 1.3
(Cantor digraphs)

Let
D=\langle V,E\rangle
be a digraph. We say that the digraph

is Cantor if for no vertex

there exists a
D
-surjection

v\in V
from

to
P(u)
. That is,

D\models(\forall x_{1}\neg(\exists x_{2}\,\mathrm{SUR}(x_{2};\,x_{1})))\,.

We also say that Cantor’s theorem holds in

.

Not every digraph is Cantor. For example, in the digraph

where the only arrows are loops, one at each vertex, every vertex

is a surjection from
u
to
P(u)
. However, by removing
one or more of these loops, we get a digraph
in which Cantor’s theorem holds.

Let
D=\langle V,E\rangle
be a digraph. For any vertex

u\in V
we define
the
D
-power set
of

as

\mathcal{P}(u)\equiv\{v\in V\colon\;v\subset_{D}u\}=\{v\in V\colon\;N(v)\subset N(u)\}\ \ (\subset V)\,,

and for any ordered pair
\langle u,v\rangle_{D}
(
\in V
) we consider
the corresponding (real) ordered pair
\langle u,v\rangle

(
\in V\times V
).

Proposition 1.4

Let
D=\langle V,E\rangle
be a digraph,
u,v\in V
and let

. Then

\{\langle a,\,b\rangle\colon\;\langle a,\,b\rangle_{D}\in N(u)\}\ \ (\subset V\times V)

is a surjection from

to
\mathcal{P}(v)
.

Proof.
This follows at once from definition 9 of SUR.

\Box

Thus we do not view the statement of Theorem

in terms of naive set theory, but we view it formally in terms of digraphs. In this perspective Theorem

ZF formulas

To get the formula
\varphi
we need a good grasp of ZF formulas. Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set
\{1,2,\dots\}
of natural numbers.
Recall that
a word

u
over an alphabet

u=\langle a_{1},\,a_{2},\,\dots,\,a_{n}\rangle=a_{1}\,a_{2}\,\dots\,a_{n}

(

) of elements

in

\lambda

of

is denoted by

|u|
, and
|\lambda|=0
. If

, then
|u|_{a}
is the number of occurrences
of

in

such that

is

. We denote the set of words over

by

. We shall work with the alphabet

\mathcal{A}\equiv\{x_{i}\colon\;i\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{\,\in,\,=,\,\neg,\,\to,\,\leftrightarrow,\,\wedge,\,\vee,\,\exists,\,\forall,\,(,\,)\,\}\,.

It consists of countably many
set variables

and of
eleven symbols with well-known meanings.

Definition 2.1

Atomic ZF formulas are the words over
\mathcal{A}
with length
5

\text{$(x_{i}\in x_{j})$ and $(x_{i}=x_{j})$},\ i,\,j\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Definition 2.2

A word

is a ZF formula if and only if there exists a finite sequence

u_{1},\,u_{2},\,\dots,\,u_{n}

of words
u_{i}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
such that
u_{n}
is
u
and for every index

one of eight cases occurs.

1.

The word
u_{i}
is an atomic ZF formula.

2.

There exists an index
j<i
such that the word
u_{i}
has form

\neg u_{j}
and length
1+|u_{j}|
.

3.

There exist indices
j,j^{\prime}<i
such that the word
u_{i}
has form

(u_{j}\to u_{j^{\prime}})

and length
3+|u_{j}|+|u_{j^{\prime}}|
.

4.

There exist indices
j,j^{\prime}<i
such that the word
u_{i}
has form

(u_{j}\leftrightarrow u_{j^{\prime}})

and length
3+|u_{j}|+|u_{j^{\prime}}|
.

5.

There exist indices
j,j^{\prime}<i
such that the word
u_{i}
has form

(u_{j}\wedge u_{j^{\prime}})

and length

.

6.

There exist indices

such that the word

has form

(u_{j}\vee u_{j^{\prime}})

and length

.

7.

There exists an index

and an index

has form

(\exists x_{k}u_{j})

and length

.

8.

There exists an index

and an index

such that the word

(\forall x_{k}u_{j})

The sequence

in it is a ZF

\text{$u_{i}\neq u_{j}$ for $i\neq j$ and

Using this we could easily devise an algorithm that for every input word

decides if it is a ZF formula. This is not so clear
for some other definitions of formulas appearing in the literature. If

where

,

,

by selecting
a subset of connectives and quantifiers and then expressing the rest in terms of the selected symbols? For example,

[

]

,

An aspect of formulas and similar objects like terms, which is
sometimes neglected, is unique reading lemmas, or URL.

such that

is
v

2.

such that

is
\neg v

3.

is
(v\to v^{\prime})

4.

There are unique ZF formulas
v
and
v^{\prime}
such that

is
(v\leftrightarrow v^{\prime})
.

5.

There are unique ZF formulas
v
and
v^{\prime}
such that

u
is
(v\wedge v^{\prime})
.

6.

There are unique ZF formulas
v
and
v^{\prime}

.

7.

There is a unique ZF formula

(\exists x_{k}v)
.

8.

There is a unique ZF formula

Nontrivial cases are the binary ones, 3–6. Sometimes it is suggested that URL
for formulas and similar objects are automatic corollaries of

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a nonempty finite set of

Let

We extend the alphabet

where

Definition 3.1

Atomic ZF
′

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
with length

and form

\text{$(\alpha\in\alpha^{\prime})$ and $(\alpha=\alpha^{\prime})$}\,,

are variables in

, and all words over
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}

where

Definition 3.2

′

obtained according to the modified Definition

.
We extend case 1 to atomic ZF
′
formulas and keep the rest of Definition

Thus quantification of new variables is not allowed. Subformulas of ZF

We review free and bound (occurrences of) variables.
Let

be a ZF
′

. An occurrence of

if it has no free occurrence of any variable. It

4
Well formed abbreviation schemes

In mathematical logic and set theory, abbreviations of formulas are not treated sufficiently
rigorously, despite the fact that
they (should) constitute a fundamental and indispensable
syntactic tool. Now we fix it.

We introduce well formed abbreviation schemes and begin with
shortcuts which define predicates.

Definition 4.1

Here

and
\varphi
is a ZF
′

formula such that no variable

has a free occurrence in it
and no variable in

is used. If

— in the next section this will be always the case — we may use in

instead of the

’s the new variables

,

Definition 4.2

a more precise and purely syntactic
version of towers of conservative extensions of theories by definitions of new predicates, as described in the theorem on definition
of a predicate in Sochor

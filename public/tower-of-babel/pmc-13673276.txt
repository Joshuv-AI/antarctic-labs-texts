ABSTRACT

Introduction: Hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia — the three most prevalent cardiometabolic conditions — are metabolically interrelated. Although each may arise independently, shared pathophysiological mechanisms often promote synergistic co-occurrence. This study examined the prevalence/determinants of comorbidity among adults in Hainan province, China.

Methods: From January to June 2023, a two-stage unequal-probability cluster sampling design was employed to recruit permanent residents aged ≥18 years across Hainan province. Participants completed structured questionnaire interviews, standardized physical measurements, and laboratory testing.

Results: Among 32,857 surveyed residents, the weighted rate of triple comorbidity of hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia was 4.07% (1,865/32,812). The weighted pairwise comorbidity rates ranked in descending order were as follows: hypertension-dyslipidemia (10.55%, 4,174/32,812), diabetes-dyslipidemia (3.65%, 1,446/32,812), and hypertension-diabetes (2.73%, 1,341/32,857). For individual conditions, the weighted prevalence rates were 27.52% (11,612/32,857) for hypertension, 13.74% (5,951/32,857) for diabetes, and 40.23% (14,225/32,812) for dyslipidemia. Multilevel multinomial logistic regression analysis identified male sex, older age, central obesity, and frequent alcohol consumption (≥5 days/week) as consistently associated with higher odds of having one/two/three, compared with none (all P<0.05). Sensitivity analyses excluding self-reported cases yielded consistent findings.

Conclusion: As a descriptive baseline investigation, this study identifies key high-risk subgroups and offers foundational data to help guide the development of future targeted interventions.

Chronic non-communicable diseases represent a major global health challenge, with hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia constituting the most prevalent cardiometabolic conditions (1). Although each can arise independently, shared pathological mechanisms commonly drive synergistic co-occurrence (2). In China, this burden is immense and growing: in 2018, an estimated 310, 130, and 310 million adults had hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia, respectively (3). Despite the breadth of single-disease research, authoritative comorbidity data remain scarce. A recent national survey reported a triple comorbidity rate of 7.6% among individuals aged ≥45 years (4); however, the burden across all adults aged ≥18 years remains uncharacterized.

As China's only tropical island province, Hainan features a distinctive residential environment characterized by high heat, high humidity, and elevated airborne salinity — all of which may synergistically promote cardiometabolic disorders. Consequently, this community merits attention in chronic disease and environmental health research. Nevertheless, the prevalence, determinants, and comorbidity patterns of hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia among Hainan adults have not been systematically examined. Thus, the present study examined the prevalence, associated factors, and co-occurrence of these conditions, to establish an evidence base for future intervention planning/design.

This cross-sectional survey was conducted among permanent residents aged ≥18 years in Hainan Province, China, from January to June 2023. The required sample size was calculated as: \begin{document}$ N=deff\; \frac{{Z}^{2}p(1-p)}{{d}^{2}} $\end{document}, with the following parameters: a 95% confidence level (two-sided, Z=1.96); a prevalence (p) of 7.6%, derived from the national hypertension-diabetes-dyslipidemia (HP-DB-DL) triple comorbidity rate among adults aged ≥45 years in China (4); a design effect (deff) of 3; a relative error (r) of 20%; and a precision d=r×p. Four strata were defined by sex (male, female) and region (Urban, Rural), with approximately 3,503 participants required per stratum, yielding a base total of 14,012 participants. To account for an anticipated 15% non-response rate, this figure was divided by 0.85, yielding a minimum final sample size of 16,486.

This study employed a two-stage cluster sampling design with unequal selection probabilities. First, all 24 cities, counties, and districts of Hainan island were included as implicit strata to ensure comprehensive geographic coverage; within each administrative unit, one township or subdistrict was randomly selected as the primary sampling unit. Second, a single village or urban residential committee was randomly selected from each sampled township/subdistrict. All permanent residents aged ≥18 years within the selected villages/communities were invited to participate. Eligibility criteria were: 1) age ≥18 years; 2) resided in the selected village or community for ≥6 months during the preceding year; and 3) voluntarily participated, providing written informed consent. Individuals were excluded if they were pregnant, had cognitive impairment, a severe illness, or any disabling condition that could compromise study integrity. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Hainan Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention, China (Approval No. 202360), and was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data were collected through questionnaires, physical measurements, and laboratory tests. All participants were instructed to fast for ≥8 h before their scheduled appointment, and all procedures were conducted by trained personnel following standardized protocols. To ensure reliability, validity, and cross-study comparability, the questionnaire was adapted from the 2018 China Chronic Disease Risk Factor Surveillance instrument, developed by the National Center for Chronic and Non-communicable Disease Control and Prevention. Survey measures, methods and definitions of key variables are provided in the Supplementary Materials (available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/).

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.6.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Between-group comparisons were assessed using the chi-square test; the Mantel–Haenszel trend chi-square test was applied for ordinal variables (age groups, education levels, and alcohol consumption frequency). Multilevel multinomial logistic regression was applied to identify factors independently associated with comorbidity, with results expressed as odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). A sensitivity analysis excluding participants whose diagnoses were based solely on selfreport was performed. All prevalence estimates represent screeningbased point prevalence, and were weighted for the complex survey design. All were poststratified and standardized by age and sex to the 2020 Seventh National Census of China, aligning the sample with the national reference standard. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Overall, 32,857 residents aged ≥18 years (mean age: 51.94 years [SD 14.97]) were enrolled (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/). The weighted prevalences of hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia were 27.52% (11,612/32,857), 13.74% (5,951/32,857), and 40.23% (14,225/32,812), respectively. The weighted pairwise comorbidity rates were 2.73% (1,341/32,857) for hypertension-diabetes (HP-DB), 10.55% (4,174/32,812) for hypertension-dyslipidemia (HP-DL), and 3.65% (1,446/32,812) for diabetes-dyslipidemia (DB-DL), with a weighted rate of triple comorbidity of 4.07% (1,865/32,812). All four comorbidity indicators increased monotonically with age, body mass index (BMI), and alcohol consumption frequency, and were consistently higher in males than in females and in individuals with central obesity (all P<0.05; Table 1).

DISCUSSION

This study, which is the first to characterize HP-DB-DL comorbidity in Hainan, identified a triple comorbidity prevalence of 4.07%, with HP-DL representing the predominant pairwise pattern (10.55%). The prevalence of dyslipidemia (40.23%) substantially exceeded both prior provincial estimates (36.6%) (5) and national figures (35.6%) (6), indicating an escalating public health burden likely attributable to nutritional transition and accelerating urbanization. The co-occurrence of these conditions compounds cardiovascular risk, underscoring a critical need to move beyond single-disease management towards integrated cardiometabolic multimorbidity control in Hainan.

Internationally, directly comparable multimorbidity studies remain scarce. Within China, Hainan's triple comorbidity rate (4.07%) exceeded those reported in Fujian (2.9%) (7), Xinjiang (3.0%) (8), and Xizang (2.2%) (9). Consistent with findings from other Chinese provinces, HP-DL was the most prevalent of the three pairwise combinations. This combination is of particular clinical concern, as hypertension and dyslipidemia share common pathophysiological pathways and synergistically elevate the risk of cardiovascular events beyond the level of either condition alone. As these two conditions are among the most prevalent and modifiable cardiovascular risk factors worldwide (10), the dominance of HP-DL in Hainan underscores an urgent need to conduct integrated screening and management strategies targeting this highrisk concurrent phenotype.

The persistently higher comorbidity odds in men may reflect clustered behavioral risks (smoking, alcohol use, and occupational psychosocial stress), compounded by a greater propensity for visceral adiposity, a key driver of insulin resistance/dyslipidemia. These factors may synergistically promote hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia co-occurrence. Frequent alcohol consumption (≥5 days/week) was a potentially modifiable risk factor across all comorbidity patterns. Central obesity showed a graded association, with effect sizes rising as coexisting conditions increased, indicating that even modest waist reduction may yield disproportionate benefits in those already burdened by one/two conditions. These findings have several public health implications. For men, workplace screening and brief interventions in male-dominated industries should be integrated with occupational health programs. For adults ≥45 years, primary care should establish an integrated screening–comorbidity assessment–intervention pathway. Central obesity and frequent alcohol use warrant alcohol sales restrictions and community behavioral programs leveraging Hainan's outdoor setting. The large migrant population also requires chronic disease management via temporary-residence registration, seasonal screening, and cross-provincial health record linkage. All actions should align with the Healthy Hainan framework.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the crosssectional design precludes assessing causal inference. Second, selfreported lifestyle and behavioral data are subject to recall and social desirability biases. Third, singlevisit assessments provided screeningbased point prevalence, rather than clinically confirmed diagnoses. Fourth, although the initial sample size calculation was based on a 7.6% prevalence from a population aged ≥45 years, posthoc power calculations using our observed prevalence of 4.07% confirmed that the final sample (n=32,857) exceeds the required minimum (n=31,713), ensuring adequate precision. Finally, despite standardizing Hainan estimates to the 2020 China census, the lack of standardized data from comparator regions limits robust crossregional comparisons. These findings should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

In conclusion, this study provides a preliminary characterization of the prevalence and comorbidity profiles of hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia in Hainan Province. By establishing a descriptive baseline and identifying high-risk subpopulations, this study furnishes a vital evidence base for the subsequent design and implementation of targeted interventions.

On the Discontinuous Breaking of Replica Symmetry and Shattering in Mean-Field Spin Glasses
Antonio Auffinger, Ahmed El Alaoui, Mark Sellke

Abstract
We show that in mean-field spin glasses, a discontinuous breaking of replica symmetry at the critical inverse temperature β c \beta_{c} implies the existence of an intermediate shattered phase. This confirms a prediction from physics regarding the nature of random first order phase transitions. On the other hand, we give an example of a spherical spin glass which exhibits shattering, yet the transition is continuous at β c \beta_{c} .



1
Introduction and main result

Mean-field spin glasses are understood to exhibit multiple phase transitions as the temperature is lowered, including the famous replica symmetry breaking phenomenon.
The onset of replica symmetry breaking may take one of two forms: the order parameter becomes non-trivial either
continuously
or
discontinuously
.
The latter is often referred to as a
random first-order phase transition
, and has long been believed by physicists to be associated with the existence of an intermediate
dynamical
or
shattering
transition (see the discussion in e.g.
[
MP96
,
FPRT+13
,
KT23
]
).
We consider both Ising and spherical glasses and prove that a discontinuous phase transition implies the existence of a shattered phase, but that the converse does not hold in spherical spin glasses.

Let
(g_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}})_{1\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\leq N,k\geq 2}
be a sequence of i.i.d.
N(0,1)
random variables and consider the random Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{k}}{N^{(k-1)/2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\leq N}g_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\sigma_{i_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{k}}\,,

(1.1)

defined for
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\Sigma_{N}
being either the Euclidean sphere on radius
\sqrt{N}
:
\Sigma_{N}=\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\sqrt{N})=\{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}:\|{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\|\leq\sqrt{N}\}
, or the binary hypercube
\Sigma_{N}=\{-1,+1\}^{N}
.
We let

\xi(x):=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}^{2}x^{k}

be the mixture function of
H_{N}
and we assume that
\xi(1)=1
and
\xi(1+\alpha)<\infty
for some
\alpha>0
so that
H_{N}
is almost surely well defined.

We define the Gibbs measure at inverse temperature
\beta
:

\displaystyle{\rm d}\mu_{\beta}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\propto e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\,,

(1.2)

where
\mu_{0}
is the uniform measure on
\Sigma_{N}
(spherical or binary).

Define the limiting free energy of the model as

\displaystyle F(\beta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\E\log\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\,,

(1.3)

where it is known that the limit exists and is given by a Parisi formula for all
\beta\geq 0

[
Par79
,
Tal06c
,
Tal06a
,
Pan13
,
Che13
]
.
Let
\beta_{c}
be the replica symmetry breaking inverse temperature defined as

\displaystyle\beta_{c}=\sup\{\beta\geq 0:F(\beta)=\beta^{2}/2\}\,.

(1.4)

It is known that
\beta_{c}<\infty
and that
F(\beta)=\beta^{2}/2
for all
0\leq\beta\leq\beta_{c}
.

We are interested in the nature of the phase transition at
\beta_{c}
and its impact on the structure of the Gibbs measure
\mu_{\beta}
at higher temperatures
\beta<\beta_{c}
. It is known that the two-replica overlap distribution is a delta mass at
0
for all
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
. Informally, we say that the phase transition at
\beta_{c}
happens
continuously
if the point mass at
0
splits into continuously moving pieces as the inverse temperature
\beta
is increased past
\beta_{c}
, i.e., the support of the measure is continuous in
\beta
(as a compact subset of
[0,1]
metrized by the Hausdorff distance). Conversely, we say that the phase transition happens
discontinuously
if a piece of the support emerges far away from 0 as soon as
\beta>\beta_{c}
.
More formally, we let
{\mathcal{P}}([0,1])
denote the set of probability measures on the interval
[0,1]
, and for
\zeta\in{\mathcal{P}}([0,1])
we define

\displaystyle q_{\max}(\zeta)=\sup\{q\in{\rm supp}(\zeta)\}\,,

(1.5)

to be the right-end point of the support of
\zeta
.
Let
\zeta_{\beta}=\zeta_{\beta}(\xi)
be the Parisi measure of the model, i.e. the minimizer of the Parisi formula as recalled in Section
2
.

Definition 1
.

We say
\xi
admits a
continuous
RSB transition if

\lim_{\beta\downarrow\beta_{c}}q_{\max}(\zeta_{\beta})=0\,.

We say
\xi
admits a
discontinuous
RSB transition if

\lim_{\beta\downarrow\beta_{c}}q_{\max}(\zeta_{\beta})>0\,.

As our main result we show that a discontinuous transition implies the existence of a
shattered
phase immediately below
\beta_{c}
.
For
q\in[0,1]
and
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
we define the Franz-Parisi potential

F_{\beta}(q):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\E\log\int{\mathbf{1}}_{|\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\rangle/N-q|\leq\varepsilon}\,e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\,,

(1.6)

where the expectation
{\mathbb{E}}[\,\cdot\,]

is taken with respect to the joint distribution of the disorder, i.e., the random variables
(g_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}})
, and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\sim\mu_{\beta}
.
In the spherical case, the above limit was shown to exist and computed in
[
AMS25b
]
for all
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
; see also
[
DS24
]
for low-temperature generic models with finite replica symmetry breaking, where the proof tools make use of spherical symmetry. Although the exact formula will not be needed here, we also compute the FP potential (
1.6
) in the Ising case, for
\beta<\beta_{c}
, using a more general convex duality argument in Section
4
.
We are now in position to formally define the notion of shattering.

Definition 2
.

We say that
\xi
exhibits
shattering
at inverse temperature
\beta
if

1.

F_{\beta}(0)=F(\beta)
.

2.

F_{\beta}(q)<F(\beta)
for all
q\in(0,1)
.

3.

There exists an interval
[q_{1},q_{2}]\subset(0,1)
on which
F_{\beta}
is strictly increasing.

In the spherical case the first two conditions are satisfied for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
and the third condition was shown to hold for the pure
p
-spin model for all
\beta\in(C,\beta_{c})
where
C
is an absolute constant for
p
large
[
AMS25b
]
while
\beta_{c}(p)=(1+o_{p}(1))\cdot\sqrt{\log p}
. This latter condition heuristically signals a “clumping” of the mass of the Gibbs measure
\mu_{\beta}
around its typical points. It was shown in the same paper that under the additional technical assumption that
q_{1}
is close enough to 1, one can construct a
shattering decomposition
for
\mu_{\beta}
: a collection of well separated connected subsets of the sphere, each with exponentially small Gibbs mass, which together carry all but an exponentially small fraction of the Gibbs mass. See also
[
Ala24
]
for a different approach to shattering yielding an improved lower bound on
\beta
.
We show in Lemma
3.2
that the first two conditions also hold in the Ising case for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
. The third condition on the other hand will be implied by a discontinuous breaking of replica symmetry.

In addition to
\beta_{c}
defined in Eq. (
1.4
) we define the inverse temperature

\displaystyle\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}

\displaystyle:=\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)^{-1/2}\,.

(1.7)

One interpretation of
\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}
is that it is the largest inverse temperature such that
q=0
is a local maximum of the FP potential (see Lemma
3.1
).
We now state our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\xi
admits a discontinuous RSB transition and
\beta_{c}<\beta_{\mathsf{cont}}
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that
\xi
exhibits shattering for all
\beta\in[\beta_{c}-\delta,\beta_{c})
.

The additional condition
\beta_{c}<\beta_{\mathsf{cont}}
in the above statement is a genericity condition on the mixture
\xi
: one always has
\beta_{c}\leq\beta_{\mathsf{cont}}
(see Proposition
5.3
), and in the spherical case we show in Lemma
5.4
that the inequality is strict under small perturbations of
\xi
whenever the RSB transition is discontinuous.

We also mention that in the spherical case, shattering at inverse temperature
\beta
is conjectured in the physics literature
[
CHS93
]
to hold exactly when there exists
q\in(0,1)
such that

\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)(1-q)>q\,.

We expect this condition to be equivalent to Definition
2
, but this is currently not known (see
[
DS25
, Corollary 2.6]
for important recent progress from the viewpoint of stationary Langevin dynamics). For completeness we also show in Section
5
that this alternative criterion is implied by a discontinuous RSB as per Definition
1
. On the other hand we provide an example of a mixture
\xi
which admits a continuous RSB transition yet exhibits shattering, showing that the implication of Theorem
1.1
cannot be reversed in general.

Remark 1.2
.

As a further remark, shattering as in Definition
2
implies exponentially slow mixing of natural dynamics initialized from equilibrium. This is simply because for a typical point
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\sim\mu_{\beta}
the set
\{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\rangle/N\geq q\}
yields a bottleneck for any
q\in(q_{1},q_{2})
.
(We note that
[
AMS25b
, Corollary 2.8]
gives a similar deduction of slow mixing, based on a more refined shattering decomposition that we do not focus on here.)
We sketch a crude argument: let
q_{1}<\underline{q}<\bar{q}<q_{2}
and
\alpha:[-1,1]\to[0,1]
be a Lipschitz increasing function with
\alpha(\underline{q})=0
and
\alpha(\bar{q})=1
.
We argue that the test function

f({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=\alpha(\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\rangle/N)

witnesses an exponentially small spectral gap for the dynamics:

\langle f,(-{\cal L}_{\beta})f\rangle\leq e^{-cN}\|f-{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu_{\beta}}[f]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{\beta})}^{2}

(1.8)

for some
c>0
with probability tending to
1
as
N\to\infty
, where
{\cal L}_{\beta}
is the infinitesimal generator for either Langevin or Glauber dynamics. We refer to
[
GJ19
]
and
[
EKZ21
]
for a definition of
{\cal L}_{\beta}
in the spherical and Ising settings respectively.
Indeed let us define
\bar{F}_{\beta}(q)=F_{\beta}(q)-F(\beta)
.
In the argument to follow, all displayed inequalities hold on events of high probability.
We have by the second item of Definition
2
that

{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu_{\beta}}[f]\leq\exp\big(N\sup_{q\in[\underline{q},1]}\bar{F}_{\beta}(q)+o(N)\big)\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ {\mathbb{E}}_{\mu_{\beta}}[f^{2}]\geq\exp\big(N\sup_{q\in[\bar{q},1]}\bar{F}_{\beta}(q)-o(N)\big)\,.

To obtain the above we used the bounds
f({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\leq{\mathbf{1}}\{\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\rangle/N\geq\underline{q}\}
and
f({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})^{2}\geq{\mathbf{1}}\{\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\rangle/N\geq\bar{q}\}
.
Since
\bar{F}_{\beta}
is strictly increasing on
(q_{1},q_{2})
, the above two suprema are equal. Moreover, since
\bar{F}_{\beta}(q)<0
for
q\neq 0
, we have

\|f-{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu_{\beta}}[f]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{\beta})}^{2}\geq\exp\big(N\sup_{q\in[\bar{q},1]}F_{\beta}(q)-o(N)\big)\geq\exp(NF_{\beta}(q_{2})-o(N))\,.

As for the left-hand side of (
1.8
), since
\alpha
is constant on
[0,\underline{q}]
and
[\bar{q},1]
,

\langle f,(-{\cal L}_{\beta})f\rangle\leq\exp\big(N\sup_{q\in[\underline{q},\bar{q}]}\bar{F}_{\beta}(q)+o(N)\big)=\exp\big(N\bar{F}_{\beta}(\bar{q})+o(N)\big)\,.

The conclusion (
1.8
) follows since
\bar{F}_{\beta}(\bar{q})<\bar{F}_{\beta}(q_{2})
: the spectral gap of Langevin or Glauber dynamics is exponential in
N
whenever
\xi
exhibits shattering.

2
Technical Preliminaries

The Parisi formula

We recall the Parisi functional.
In the spherical case, it is the strictly convex lower semicontinuous (in the weak
∗
topology) functional

{\sf P}:{\mathcal{P}}([0,1))\to{\mathbb{R}}

on the space of probability measures
\zeta
with support contained inside
[0,1]
:

\displaystyle{\sf P}^{\mathbb{S}}_{\beta}(\zeta;\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\beta^{2}(\xi^{\prime}(t)+h^{2})\zeta([0,t]){\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\hat{q}}\frac{{\rm d}t}{\phi_{\zeta}(t)}+\log(1-\hat{q})\right)\,,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\phi_{\zeta}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{1}\zeta([0,s]){\rm d}s\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \hat{q}=\inf\{s\in[0,1)\,:\,\zeta([0,s])=1\}{\,<1}\,.

In the Ising case, for
\zeta\in{\mathcal{P}}([0,1])
one defines
\Phi:[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
to solve the Parisi PDE

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi_{\zeta}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{2}\Big(\partial_{xx}\Phi_{\zeta}(t,x)+\zeta([0,s])(\partial_{x}\Phi_{\zeta}(t,x))^{2}\Big),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\zeta}(1,x)

\displaystyle=\log\cosh(x).

Then the Ising Parisi functional is

{\sf P}^{\mathrm{Is}}_{\beta}(\zeta;\xi,h)=\Phi_{\zeta}(0,h)-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\zeta([0,t])t\xi^{\prime\prime}(t){\rm d}t.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Tal06b
,
AC15
]
)
.

In both the Ising and spherical settings, one has

F(\beta;\xi,h)=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\log\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle{\mathbf{1}},\sigma\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}(\sigma)=\inf_{\zeta\in{\mathcal{P}}([0,1])}{\sf P}_{\beta}(\zeta;\xi).

Moreover
{\sf P}_{\beta}(\cdot;\xi)
is strictly convex and the minimum is uniquely attained at some
\zeta_{\beta}(\xi)\in{\mathcal{P}}([0,1])
.
Finally
\beta\in[0,\beta_{c}]
if and only if
\zeta_{\beta}=\delta_{0}
, the atom at
0
.

The Parisi formula can be differentiated explicitly; see e.g.
[
Tal06c
,
Pan08
]
:

Proposition 2.2
.

The derivative of
F(\beta;\xi)
with respect to any
\gamma_{p}
exists and is given by

\frac{\partial F(\beta;\xi)}{\partial\gamma_{p}}=\beta^{2}\gamma_{p}\Big(1-\int q^{p}{\rm d}\zeta_{\beta}(q)\Big)\,,

and moreover, for each
p
such that
\gamma_{p}\neq 0
we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\E\mu_{\beta}^{\otimes 2}\big((\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\rangle/N)^{p}\big)=\int q^{p}{\rm d}\zeta_{\beta}(q)\,.

Furthermore,
\zeta_{\beta}
is continuous in
\xi
.

The planted model and contiguity

It will be useful to define a
planted
model under which several computations become tractable. Let
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\sim\mu_{0}
and for all
k\geq 2
,
1\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\leq N
,

g_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}=\frac{\beta\gamma_{k}}{N^{(k-1)/2}}\sigma_{i_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{k}}+\tilde{g}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\,,

(2.3)

where
\tilde{g}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\sim N(0,1)
independently of each other and of
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
. We denote by
{\boldsymbol{G}}
the collection of random variables
(g_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}})_{1\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\leq N,k\geq 2}
and let
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}
be the joint distribution of the pair
({\boldsymbol{G}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
generated this way.

On the other hand let
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny rd}}
be the joint law of
({\boldsymbol{G}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
where
g_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\sim N(0,1)
i.i.d. and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\sim\mu_{\beta}
conditionally on
{\boldsymbol{G}}
. By a straightforward computation their likelihood ratio is given by a normalized version of the partition function of the Gibbs measure
\mu_{\beta}
:

\frac{{\rm d}\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}}{{\rm d}\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny rd}}}({\boldsymbol{G}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\prime})-\beta^{2}N\xi(1)/2}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\prime})\,.

(2.4)

In particular the above does not depend on
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
and will henceforth be denoted by
L_{N}({\boldsymbol{G}})
, and
H_{N}
is defined as in Eq. (
1.1
) with disorder coefficients given by
{\boldsymbol{G}}
.
Since
{\boldsymbol{G}}\mapsto\log L_{N}(G)
is
\beta\sqrt{N}
-Lipschitz function and
\frac{1}{N}\E\log L_{N}({\boldsymbol{G}})\to 0
for
{\boldsymbol{G}}\sim\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny rd}}
and all
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
by definition of
\beta_{c}
Eq. (
1.4
), we have by Gaussian concentration of Lipschitz functions
[
BLM13
, Theorem 5.6]
,

\mathbb{P}\big(|\log L_{N}({\boldsymbol{G}})|\geq tN\big)\leq e^{-cN}\,,

(2.5)

where
c=c(t,\beta)>0
for all
t>0
,
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
and all
N
sufficiently large.
This implies the useful fact that any event which is exponentially unlikely under
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}
is also exponentially unlikely under
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny rd}}
(and vice-versa but this won’t be used):

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
. For any sequence of events
(E_{N})
defined on the common probability space of
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny rd}}
and
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}
, if
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}(E_{N})\leq e^{-cN}
for
c>0
and
N
sufficiently large, then
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny rd}}(E_{N})\leq e^{-c^{\prime}N}
for some
c^{\prime}>0
and
N
sufficiently large.

The above property is referred to in
[
AMS25b
]
as “contiguity at exponential scale”; see Lemma 3.5 therein for a short proof using (
2.5
).

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We start by establishing a few facts about the local and global behavior of
F_{\beta}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
\beta<\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}=\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)^{-1/2}
, there is
\hat{q}>0
with
F_{\beta}(q)<F(\beta)
for all
q\in(0,\hat{q})
.

Proof.

The argument relies on a first computation under the planted model
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}
, as used in
[
AMS25a
,
AMS25b
]
. For
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\Sigma_{N}
we let
B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=\{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{N}:|\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\prime}\rangle/N-q|\leq\varepsilon\}
. We consider the restricted partition function

Z_{N}=Z_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},q,\varepsilon):=\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\,.

Taking expectations under the planted model
({\boldsymbol{G}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})\sim\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}
defined in Eq. (
2.3
) and writing
\tilde{H}_{N}
for the Hamiltonian with disorder random variables
(\tilde{g}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}})
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\log{\mathbb{E}}_{\mbox{\rm\small pl}}[Z_{N}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}\,\E e^{\beta\tilde{H}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+N\beta^{2}\xi(\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\rangle/N)}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\xi(q)+\beta^{2}/2+\frac{1}{N}\log\mu_{0}(B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}))+O(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\xi(q)+\beta^{2}/2+h(q)+O(\varepsilon)+o_{N}(1)\,,

(3.1)

where
h(q)=\frac{1}{2}\log(1-q^{2})
in the spherical case
\Sigma_{N}=\mathbb{S}^{N-1}(\sqrt{N})
and
h(q)=-\frac{1+q}{2}\log(1+q)-\frac{1-q}{2}\log(1-q)
in the Ising case
\Sigma_{N}=\{-1,+1\}^{N}
. On the other hand the distribution of
Z_{N}
under
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}
is invariant under the change of variable
g_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\to g_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\sigma_{0i_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{0i_{k}}
so without loss of generality we may set
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}={\mathbf{1}}
. Next,
{\boldsymbol{G}}\mapsto\log Z_{N}
is
\beta\sqrt{N}
-Lipschitz, so by Gaussian concentration we have

\mathbb{P}_{({\boldsymbol{G}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})\sim\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}}\Big(|\log Z_{N}-{\mathbb{E}}_{\mbox{\rm\small pl}}\log Z_{N}|\geq tN\Big)\leq 2e^{-t^{2}N/(2\beta^{2})}\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ t\geq 0\,.

(3.2)

By contiguity of
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}
and
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny rd}}
at exponential scale (Lemma
2.3
) the event
|\log Z_{N}-{\mathbb{E}}_{\mbox{\rm\small pl}}\log Z_{N}|\geq tN
has probability at most
e^{-cN}
,
c=c(\beta,t)>0
under
({\boldsymbol{G}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})\sim\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny rd}}
. Using Jensen’s inequality and letting
N\to\infty
followed by
\varepsilon\to 0
yields the upper bound

F_{\beta}(q)-F(\beta)\leq\beta^{2}\xi(q)+h(q)\,.

(3.3)

A quick inspection of the first two derivatives at
q=0
of the function on right-hand side reveals that if
\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)<1
then there exists
\hat{q}>0
such that
\beta^{2}\xi(q)+h(q)<0
for
q\in(0,\hat{q})
. This proves the result.
∎

Now we show that the FP potential is globally dominated by the full free energy up to
\beta_{c}
:

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
\beta<\beta_{c}
,
F_{\beta}(q)<F(\beta)
for all
q\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
\beta<\beta_{c}
. We control
F_{\beta}(q)
by introducing an external field; this idea will be used again to obtain an exact formula for the FP potential. In the present argument only the upper bound is needed. For
h\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\log\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})-hq+\varepsilon\,,

where we recall
B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=\{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{N}:|\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\prime}\rangle/N-q|\leq\varepsilon\}
. Using concentration of the random variable on the left-hand side above and contiguity at exponential scale between the random and planted distributions, exactly as done in Lemma
3.1
we obtain

F_{\beta}(q)\leq\beta^{2}\xi(q)+\inf_{h}\big\{F(\beta,h)-hq\big\}\,,

(3.4)

where
F(\beta,h)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\E\log\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
is the free energy of
H_{N}
augmented with an external field, where without loss of generality we set
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}={\mathbf{1}}
. Since this is given by the Parisi formula as stated in Proposition
2.1
we may use a replica-symmetric ansatz to upper bound
F_{\beta}(q)
: we consider the overlap distribution
\zeta=\delta_{q}
and plug it in the Parisi formula in the Ising case. From Proposition
2.1
we obtain

F_{\beta}(q)\leq\beta^{2}\xi(q)+\inf_{h}\Big\{\E\log\cosh\big(\sqrt{\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)}z+h\big)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\big(\xi(1)-\xi(q)-(1-q)\xi^{\prime}(q)\big)-hq\Big\}\,,

(3.5)

where
z\sim N(0,1)
. Next we choose
h=\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)
. After simplification the above becomes

F_{\beta}(q)\leq\phi_{\mbox{\tiny\rm RS}}(q;\beta):=\E\log\cosh\big(\sqrt{\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)}z+\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)\big)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\big(\xi(1)+\xi(q)-(1+q)\xi^{\prime}(q)\big)\,.

(3.6)

It remains to show that
\phi_{\mbox{\tiny\rm RS}}(q;\beta)<F(\beta)=\beta^{2}/2
for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
and
q\neq 0
. To this end we observe that the limit of the free energy
F_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}(\beta):=(1/N)\E_{\mbox{\rm\small pl}}\log Z_{N}
of
\mu_{\beta}
under the planted model (
2.3
) can be represented as a
supremum
of
\phi_{\mbox{\tiny\rm RS}}(q;\beta)
over
q
:

F_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}(\beta)=\sup_{q\geq 0}\phi_{\mbox{\tiny\rm RS}}(q;\beta)\,.

(3.7)

This was proved for the pure
p
-spin case in
[
LML+17
, Theorem 1]
. Their proof technique is classical in spin glass theory and extends in a straightforward way to the general mixed case.
By contiguity at exponential scale we have
F_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}(\beta)=F(\beta)
for all
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
.
Next we show that the above supremum is uniquely achieved at
q=0
for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
thereby finishing the proof. Since
F_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}(\beta)=\beta^{2}\xi(1)/2
is differentiable for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
(and recall
\xi(1)=1
), by the envelope theorem
[
MS02
]
we have

\beta\xi(1)=F_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}^{\prime}(\beta)=\beta\xi^{\prime}(q)\big({\mathbb{E}}\tanh^{2}\big(\sqrt{\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)}z+\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)\big)-q\big)+\beta(\xi(1)+\xi(q))\,,

(3.8)

for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
where
q
is any maximizer of
\phi_{\mbox{\tiny\rm RS}}(\,\cdot\,;\beta)
. On the other hand any such maximizer must satisfy
\phi_{\mbox{\tiny\rm RS}}^{\prime}(q;\beta)=0
. Indeed,

\phi_{\mbox{\tiny\rm RS}}^{\prime}(q;\beta)=\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\xi^{\prime\prime}(q)\big({\mathbb{E}}\tanh^{2}\big(\sqrt{\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)}z+\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)\big)-q\big)\,,

(3.9)

so
q=0
is a stationary point and
\phi_{\mbox{\tiny\rm RS}}^{\prime}(1;\beta)<0
so
q=1
is not a maximizer. We deduce from (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) that any maximizer must satisfy
\xi(q)=0
when
\beta<\beta_{c}
, i.e.,
q=0
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Lemma
3.1
controls the FP potential
locally
in the neighborhood of
q=0
up to
\beta_{\mathsf{cont}}
, while Lemma
3.2
provides
global
control up to
\beta_{c}\leq\beta_{\mathsf{cont}}
. The point is that in a situation where
\beta_{c}<\beta_{\mathsf{cont}}
,
q=0
remains the unique local maximizer of the FP potential up to
\beta_{c}
, so any other maximizer which might appear at
\beta=\beta_{c}
must emerge far away. This is exactly what happens when RSB appears discontinuously as shown in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
, if
q\in{\rm supp}(\zeta_{\beta})
then
F_{\beta}(q)=F(\beta)
. In particular
F_{\beta}(0)=\beta^{2}/2
for all
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
, and if
\xi
admits a discontinuous RSB transition then
F_{\beta_{c}}(\bar{q})=\beta_{c}^{2}/2
, with
\bar{q}=\lim_{\beta\uparrow\beta_{c}}q_{\max}(\zeta_{\beta})
.

Proof.

Suppose
q
is such that
F_{\beta}(q)<F(\beta)
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Under the planted model
\nu_{\mbox{\rm\tiny pl}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mbox{\rm\small pl}}\,\mu_{\beta}^{\otimes 2}\big(\{|\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\rangle/N-q|\leq\varepsilon\}\big)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\mbox{\rm\small pl}}\,\mu_{\beta}\big(\{|\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1}\rangle/N-q|\leq\varepsilon\}\big)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\mbox{\rm\small pl}}\left[\frac{\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}\right]\,.

(3.10)

The first equality follows from the fact
({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm d}}}{{=}}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2})
since
\mu_{\beta}
is the posterior distribution of
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
given
{\boldsymbol{G}}
in the model (
2.3
); this is often referred to as Nishimori’s identity, see e.g.,
[
LM19
]
.
Next, since the two events

\displaystyle\big\{(1/N)\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\leq F_{\beta}(q)+\varepsilon/2\big\}

\displaystyle\mbox{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \big\{(1/N)\log\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\geq F(\beta)-\varepsilon/2\big\}

both have probability at least
1-e^{-c(\beta,\varepsilon)N}
,

c(\beta,\varepsilon)>0
, we obtain that the right-hand side of (
3.10
) is bounded by
e^{-c^{\prime}N}
,
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(\beta,\varepsilon)>0
.
Then by contiguity at exponential scale, Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\E\mu_{\beta}^{\otimes 2}\big(\{|\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{2}\rangle/N-q|\leq\varepsilon\}\big)\leq e^{-c^{\prime\prime}N}\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ c^{\prime\prime}=c^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon,\beta)>0\,,

(3.11)

and
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.
This implies
\zeta_{\beta}([q-\varepsilon,q+\varepsilon])=0
via a standard approximation argument: we approximate the mixture
\xi
with a
generic
mixture containing a non-zero monomial of every degree, i.e.,
\gamma_{p}\neq 0
for all
p\geq 3
, and let
\tilde{\zeta}_{\beta}
be the corresponding Parisi measure.
By approximating the indicator function appearing in (
3.11
) with polynomials and using Proposition
2.2
, we obtain

\tilde{\zeta}_{\beta}([q-\varepsilon,q+\varepsilon])=0\,,

by taking
N\to\infty
. Finally since the Parisi measure is continuous in the coefficients of the mixture
(\gamma_{p})
this implies
\zeta_{\beta}([q-\varepsilon,q+\varepsilon])=0
for all
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, and therefore
q\notin{\rm supp}(\zeta_{\beta})
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
:

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

On the one hand by Lemma
3.1
we have
F_{\beta}(q)<F(\beta)
for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
(since
\beta_{c}<\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}
by assumption) and
q
in a small neighborhood of zero: there exists
\underline{q},\varepsilon>0
depending only on
\beta_{c}
such that
F_{\beta}(\underline{q})\leq F_{\beta}(0)-\varepsilon
for all
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
. Furthermore, since
\xi
admits a discontinuous RSB transition,
\bar{q}=\lim_{\beta\downarrow\beta_{c}}q_{\max}(\zeta_{\beta})>0
, and we can take
\varepsilon>0
small enough so that
\underline{q}<\bar{q}
.

On the other hand,
as a consequence of Lemma
3.4
and continuity of
\beta\mapsto F_{\beta}(q)-\beta^{2}/2
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\beta^{2}/2-\varepsilon/2\leq F_{\beta}(\bar{q})\leq\beta^{2}/2\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall\beta\in[\beta_{c}-\delta,\beta_{c}]\,.

It follows that
F_{\beta}(\underline{q})<F_{\beta}(\bar{q})
.
∎

4
The Franz–Parisi potential

Here we compute the Franz–Parisi potential
F_{\beta}(q)
for
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
in the Ising case. The spherical case was previously treated in
[
AMS25b
]
via a technique leveraging spherical symmetry. We provide here a derivation in the Ising case via a duality-based route.

Here
\mu_{0}
is the uniform distribution on
\Sigma_{N}=\{-1,+1\}^{N}
. We proceed by duality with a model with non-zero external field.
For
h\in{\mathbb{R}}
we consider the free energy with external field
h\mathbf{1}
:

\displaystyle F(\beta,h):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\E\int\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\Sigma_{N}}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle\mathbf{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}).

(4.1)

Proposition 4.1
.

F(\beta,h)
is continuously differentiable in
h
.
Consequently for any
q\in(-1,1)
, the equation

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}F(\beta,h)=q\,.

(4.2)

has a solution
h=\sup\{h:\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}F(\beta,h)=q\}
.
Hence the map
q\mapsto h(q)
is strictly increasing.
Finally
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}F(\beta,0)=0
.

Proof.

Proposition
2.2
and the envelope theorem
[
MS02
]
implies that
h\mapsto F(\beta,h)
is continuously differentiable with

\frac{\partial F(\beta,h)}{\partial h}=\partial_{x}\Phi_{\zeta_{\beta}(\xi,h)}(0,h).

Furthermore,
F(\beta,h)
is convex in
h
because
\log\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle\mathbf{1},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
is.
Further since
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}F(\beta,h)\to\pm 1
for
h\to\pm\infty
, there is a solution
h
to Eq. (
4.2
) for any
q\in(-1,1)
.
The last assertion follows since
F(\beta,h)=F(\beta,-h)
by symmetry.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For
\beta<\beta_{c}
and
q\in(-1,1)
, the function
F_{\beta}
is
given by:

F_{\beta}(q)=F(\beta,h(q))-h(q)q+\beta^{2}\xi(q)\,.

Proof.

We will show that for all
h\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\beta<\beta_{c}
,

F(\beta,h)=\sup_{q\in[-1,1]}\Big\{F_{\beta}(q)-\beta^{2}\xi(q)+hq\Big\}\,.

(4.3)

Momentarily assuming this, by continuity in
q
the above supremum is achieved. Since
F(\beta,h)
is convex and continuously differentiable in
h
, we obtain by the envelope theorem that
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}F(\beta,h)=q^{*}
for any
q^{*}\in\argmax_{q\in[-1,1]}\{F_{\beta}(q)-\beta^{2}\xi(q)+hq\}
. Therefore the maximum is uniquely attained at
q(h)=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}h}F(\beta,h)\in(-1,1)
. By Proposition
4.1
this function is surjective with right-continuous inverse
h
, and we obtain

F_{\beta}(q)=F(\beta,h(q))+\beta^{2}\xi(q)-h(q)q\,.

Now we show Eq. (
4.3
).
For
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\in\Sigma_{N}
we recall the notation
B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})=\{{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\in\Sigma_{N}:|\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle/N-q|\leq\varepsilon\}
.
First, using the planted model we have

F_{\beta}(q)=\beta^{2}\xi(q)+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\E\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\,,

where
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}\sim\mu_{0}
. By symmetry we may assume that
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0}=\mathbf{1}
.
For the lower bound,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\E\log\int e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\E\max_{q\in\varepsilon{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[-1,1]}\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})

\displaystyle\geq\max_{q\in\varepsilon{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[-1,1]}\frac{1}{N}\E\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h(q-\varepsilon)\,.

Taking
N\to\infty
then
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain

F(\beta,h)\geq\sup_{q\in[-1,1]}\Big\{F_{\beta}(q)-\beta^{2}\xi(q)+hq\Big\}\,.

As for the upper bound, we have

\displaystyle F(\beta,h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\E\log\Big(\sum_{q\in\varepsilon{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[-1,1]}\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\E\max_{q\in\varepsilon{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[-1,1]}\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h\langle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\rangle}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+\frac{\log(2/\varepsilon)}{N}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\E\max_{q\in\varepsilon{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[-1,1]}\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h(q+\varepsilon)+\frac{\log(2/\varepsilon)}{N}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{q\in\varepsilon{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[-1,1]}\frac{1}{N}\E\log\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{\beta H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+h(q+\varepsilon)+\frac{\log(2/\varepsilon)}{N}+o_{N}(1)\,.

The last line follows by a standard application of the tail bound
\mathbb{P}(\log Z_{N}-\E\log Z_{N}\geq t)\leq e^{-t^{2}/(2\beta^{2}N)}
where
Z_{N}=\int_{B_{q,\varepsilon}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{0})}e^{H_{N}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})}{\rm d}\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
(c.f. (
3.2
)). This allows us to swap the order of the maximum and the expectation via a union bound over the finite set
\varepsilon{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[-1,1]
. Letting
N\to\infty
then
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain the converse bound, therefore establishing (
4.3
).
∎

5
An alternative shattering criterion on the sphere

On the sphere, an alternate criterion for shattering at inverse temperature
\beta
is that there exists
q\in(0,1)
with

\beta^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)>\frac{q}{1-q}\,.

(5.1)

Here we show a similar result for this alternative criterion.
Here, relevant thresholds become more explicit. By
[
Tal06a
, Proposition 2.3]
, the static replica symmetry breaking inverse temperature
\beta_{c}
is given by the formula

\beta_{c}=\sqrt{\inf_{q\in(0,1)}\frac{-\log(1-q)-q}{\xi(q)}}\,.

(5.2)

On the other hand we define the dynamical inverse temperature
\beta_{d}
by

\displaystyle\beta_{d}

\displaystyle=\min(\bar{\beta}_{d},\beta_{c})\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}_{d}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\inf_{q\in(0,1)}\frac{q}{(1-q)\xi^{\prime}(q)}}\,,

which is the onset of (
5.1
).

We define separate critical temperatures for continuous and discontinuous breaking of replica-symmetry.
Recall
\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}=(\xi^{\prime\prime}(0))^{-1/2}
; this is the threshold at which
\delta_{0}
becomes unstable to local perturbations, and let
\beta_{{\mathsf{dis}}}^{2}
be the smallest value of

\kappa(q)=\frac{-\log(1-q)-q}{\xi(q)}

(5.3)

at any of its nonzero critical points.
This is the threshold for an atom to emerge away from
0
. Indeed, Proposition 2.1 of
[
Tal06a
]
shows that the support of the Parisi measure
\zeta_{\beta}
is contained in the set of maximizers of a function
f:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
which for
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
takes the form

f(q)=\beta^{2}\xi(q)+\log(1-q)+q\,.

(5.4)

For
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
, since
\zeta_{\beta}=\delta_{0}
, this function is nonpositive with
q=0
as a maximizer. Therefore if
\xi
admits a discontinuous RSB transition, there must exist a second maximizer
q_{c}\neq 0
at
\beta=\beta_{c}
, i.e.,
f(q_{c})=0
. This point is a nonzero minimizer of
\kappa
which is also a critical point:
\kappa^{\prime}(q_{c})=0
.
It is then immediate from (
5.2
) that

\beta_{c}=\min(\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}},\beta_{{\mathsf{dis}}})\,.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
\xi
admits a discontinuous RSB transition on the sphere and
\beta_{c}<\beta_{\mathsf{cont}}
.
Then there exists
q
with
\beta_{c}^{2}\xi^{\prime}(q)>\frac{q}{1-q}
.

Proof.

By the above discussion, a discontinuous RSB transition means there exists
q_{c}\in(0,1)
with

f(q_{c})=\beta_{c}^{2}\xi(q_{c})+q_{c}+\log(1-q_{c})=0\,.

By the mean-value theorem, there exists
q_{d}\in[0,q_{c}]
with
\xi^{\prime}(q_{d})\geq\frac{q_{d}}{1-q_{d}}
.
Strict inequality holds for some
q_{d}
unless equality holds on all of
(0,q_{c})
; this cannot be since
\xi(1)
is finite.
∎

We also prove that shattering can occur together with a continuous RSB transition, essentially since the converse of the mean-value theorem is false.

Proposition 5.2
.

For any
p\geq 3
, there is
\gamma_{p}
such that
\xi(q)=\frac{q^{2}}{2}+\gamma_{p}^{2}q^{p}
admits a continuous RSB transition,
\beta_{c}=1
, but
\beta_{d}<1
.

Proof.

By Talagrand’s characterization of the support of
\zeta_{\beta}
, see Eq (
5.4
),
\xi
admits a continuous RSB transition if and only if
f(q)<0
for all
q\neq 0
at
\beta=\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}=1
. Equivalently,

\gamma_{p}^{2}\leq\min_{q\in(0,1)}\frac{\sum_{k\geq 3}q^{k}/k}{q^{p}}\,.

On the other hand, there exists
q
with
\xi^{\prime}(q)>\frac{q}{1-q}
if

\gamma_{p}^{2}>\min_{q\in(0,1)}\frac{q^{2}}{pq^{p-1}(1-q)}\,.

It therefore remains to prove that

\min_{q\in(0,1)}\frac{q^{2}}{pq^{p-1}(1-q)}<\min_{q\in(0,1)}\frac{\sum_{k\geq 3}q^{k}/k}{q^{p}}\,.

(5.5)

Indeed,

\frac{\sum_{k\geq 3}q^{k}/k}{q^{p}}=\frac{\int_{0}^{q}\frac{t^{2}}{1-t}{\rm d}t}{\int_{0}^{q}pt^{p-1}{\rm d}t}\,.

This implies (
5.5
) with non-strict inequality.
Strictness follows because the function
\frac{t^{2}}{pt^{p-1}(1-t)}
is non-constant on any open set.
∎

We end this section with a lemma on the genericity of the condition
\beta_{c}<\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}
. We emphasize the dependence on
\xi
by writing
\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}(\xi)
and
\beta_{c}(\xi)
.

Proposition 5.3
.

We have
\beta_{c}(\xi)\leq\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}(\xi)
for any
\xi
in both the Ising and spherical models.

Proof.

In the spherical case, we use the explicit characterization (
5.2
) of
\beta_{c}
.
We assume that
\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)>0
as otherwise the statement is vacuous.
The limit as
q\to 0^{+}
of the function
\kappa
defined in Eq. (
5.3
) is
1/\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)
. This yields
\beta_{c}(\xi)\leq\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}(\xi)
.
In the Ising case, one can proceed similarly using the stationarity condition from e.g.
[
Che17
, Theorem 2]
.

Alternatively, in both cases one can deduce the bound as follows.
It is known in the pure
2
-spin case that
\beta_{c}=\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}
is the RSB transition.
By the subadditivity result
[
Sel23
, Corollary 2.1]
, decreasing the coefficients
\gamma_{p}
of any RS spin glass model without external field yields another RS spin glass (since the RS or annealed free energy is always an upper bound for
F_{\beta}
, and is exactly additive).
Applying this coefficient reduction to turn
\xi
into a
2
-spin model gives the desired result.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

If a spherical spin glass with mixture
\xi
admits a discontinuous RSB transition then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
depending on
\xi
such that any perturbation
\tilde{\xi}
of
\xi
of the form
\tilde{\xi}(x)=\xi(x)+\varepsilon(x^{p}-x^{2})
with
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
p\geq 3
also admits a discontinuous RSB transition and furthermore satisfies
\beta_{c}(\tilde{\xi})<\beta_{\mathsf{cont}}(\tilde{\xi})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\xi
admits a discontinuous RSB transition and let
\tilde{\xi}(x)=\xi(x)+\varepsilon(x^{p}-x^{2})
for
\varepsilon>0
and
p\geq 3
. As argued above, a discontinuous RSB implies that the function
\kappa
is minimized at a point
q_{c}\in(0,1)
.
We consider the following function of
\varepsilon
given by

\varphi(\varepsilon):=\frac{-\log(1-q)-q}{\xi(q)+\varepsilon(q^{p}-q^{2})}-\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)-2\varepsilon}\,,\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{with }\penalty\ q=q_{c}\,.

Evaluating its derivative at
\varepsilon=0
we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-\log(1-q)-q)(q^{2}-q^{p})}{\xi(q)^{2}}-\frac{2}{\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{2}-q^{p}}{\xi(q)\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)}-\frac{2}{\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\xi(q)\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)^{2}}\big[(q^{2}-q^{p})\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)-2\xi(q)\big]\,,

where the inequality in the second line uses the fact
\kappa(q)\leq 1/\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)
.
Since
\xi(0)=\xi^{\prime}(0)=0
and all the mixture coefficients are nonnegative
\gamma_{p}\geq 0
we have
\xi(x)-x^{2}\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)/2\geq 0
for all
x\geq 0
. Since
q>0
, the above derivative is strictly negative. Therefore, since
\varphi(\varepsilon)=\kappa(q)-1/\xi^{\prime\prime}(0)\leq 0
, for all
\varepsilon
small enough we have
\varphi(\varepsilon)<0
which implies
\beta_{c}(\tilde{\xi})<\beta_{{\mathsf{cont}}}(\tilde{\xi})
.
∎

Abstract




Abstract.
We establish a nonlinear Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-theory
to the Dirichlet problem

-|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta^{N}_{p}u=f\in L^{2}(\Omega)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\hskip 9.24994pt\Omega;\hskip 9.24994ptu=0\ \mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}

for
n\geq 2
,
p>1
and a large range of
\gamma>-1
,
in particular, for all
p>1
and all
\gamma>-1
when
n=2
.
Here

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded convex domain, or a
bounded Lipschitz domain
whose boundary has small weak second fundamental form in the sense of Cianchi-Maz’ya (2018).

The proof relies on an extension of an Miranda-Talenti & Cianchi-Maz’ya type inequality,
that is,
for any
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
in any bounded smooth domain
\Omega
,

\|D[(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}Dv]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is bounded via

\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
where
\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v
is the
\epsilon
-regularization of normalized
p
-Laplacian.

Our results extend the well-known Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-estimate for the Poisson equation,
a nonlinear global second order Sobolev estimate for inhomogeneous
p
-Laplace equation by Cianchi-Maz’ya (2018),
and a local
W^{2,2}
-estimate for inhomogeneous normalized
p
-Laplace equation by Attouchi-Ruosteenoja (2018).

1
Introduction

For
1<p<\infty
, denote by
\Delta_{p}
the
p
-Laplacian and by
\Delta^{N}_{p}
its normalization, that is,

\Delta_{p}u:={\rm div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta^{N}_{p}u:=|Du|^{2-p}\Delta_{p}u.

In the case
p=2
, both of them are the Laplacian
\Delta
, that is,

\Delta u:={\rm div}(Du).

For
{\gamma}>-1
, let us consider the Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle-|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta^{N}_{p}u=f\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega;\quad u=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}

(1.1)

If
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
, we refer to Section 2 for the definition of viscosity solutions to the equation

-|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta^{N}_{p}u=f
.
Under
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the existence of viscosity solution
u\in C^{0}(\Omega)
to (
1.1
) was already known;
see Birindelli-Demengel
[
8
]
and also Section 6.

In this paper we deal with

f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and approximation solutions to the problem (
1.1
).
A function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is called as an approximation solution to the problem
(
1.1
) with
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
if

u^{\epsilon}\to u
in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
for some
q>1
as
\epsilon\to 0
(up to some subsequence),
and
u^{\epsilon}
is a viscosity solution to the approximation problem

-|Dv|^{\gamma}\Delta^{N}_{p}v=f^{\epsilon}\ \mbox{in $\Omega$};\quad v=0\ \mbox{on $\partial\Omega$},

(1.2)

where

f^{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)

and
f^{\epsilon}\to f
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

The main purpose of this paper is to establish the following
nonlinear Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-theory for the problem (
1.1
),
including the existence of approximation (viscosity) solutions to
the problem (
1.1
), a global first order quantitative Sobolev regularity,
and a nonlinear global second order Sobolev regularity.
A triplet
(n,p,\gamma)
is admissible
if
n\geq 2
,
p>1
and
{\gamma}>\gamma_{n,p}
, where

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{n,p}:=-1+(p-1)\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)},\quad\mbox{in particular,}\quad\gamma_{2,p}:=-1.

(1.3)

Moreover, if
{\gamma}=0
, then the triplet
(n,p,0)
is admissible if and only
if
1<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
.
Write
2^{\star}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
when
n\geq 3
and
2^{\star}=q
for any sufficiently large
q<\infty
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that the triplet
(n,p,\gamma)
is admissible
and
\gamma>-\frac{4}{n+2}
.
Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a bounded convex domain.
For any
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
the problem (
1.1
) admits an approximation solution

u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
, which satisfies

Du\in L^{(1+{\gamma})2^{\star}}(\Omega)
and

|Du|^{{\gamma}}Du\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)

(1.4)

and has the upper bound

\displaystyle\|Du\|^{{\gamma}+1}_{L^{(1+{\gamma})2^{\star}}(\Omega)}+\|D[|Du|^{{\gamma}}Du]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(1.5)

for some constant
C=C(p,n,{\gamma})
.
If
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
in addition, then
u\in C^{0}(\Omega)
is indeed a viscosity solution to (
1.1
).

Next we relax the convexity of domains required in Theorem
1.1
to bounded Lipschitz domains with small weak second fundamental form.
Given a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, denote by
d_{\Omega}
the diameter of
\Omega
and by
L_{\Omega}
the Lipschitz constant of
\Omega
.
Assume that the boundary
\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{n-1,\infty}
when
n\geq 3
and

\partial\Omega\in W^{2}L^{1,\infty}\log L
when
n=2
. Then

\partial\Omega
enjoys a weak second fundamental form
\mathcal{B}
in
L^{n-1,\infty}(\partial\Omega)
when
n\geq 3
and

L^{1,\infty}\log L(\partial\Omega)
when
n=2
.
For
r>0
, define

\Psi_{\Omega}(r):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\partial\Omega}\|\mathcal{B}\|_{L^{n-1,\infty}(\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(x))}&\textrm{if}\quad n\geq 3,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\partial\Omega}\|\mathcal{B}\|_{L^{1,\infty}\log L(\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(x))}&\textrm{if}\quad n=2.\end{array}\right.

For more details see
[
16
]
.
Below, when writing
\Psi_{\Omega}
we always make above assumption on
\partial\Omega
without further specifying.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that the triplet
(n,p,\gamma)
is admissible
and
-\frac{4}{n+2}<{\gamma}\leq p-2
.
Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a bounded Lipchitz domain but not convex.

There exists small positive constant
\delta=\delta(p,n,{\gamma},L_{\Omega},d_{\Omega})
such that if

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\Psi_{\Omega}(r)<\delta
, then
for any
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
the problem (
1.1
) admits an approximation solution

u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
,
which satisfies (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) with the constant
C=C(p,n,{\gamma},L_{\Omega},d_{\Omega})
.
If
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
in addition,
u\in C^{0}(\Omega)
is indeed a viscosity solution to (
1.1
).

In the case
p=2
and
\gamma>-\min\{\frac{4}{n+2},\frac{n}{2n-2}\}
but
{\gamma}\neq 0
,
Theorem
1.1
actually gives a nonlinear Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-theory to the Dirichlet zero boundary problem
for the equation
|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta u=f\in L^{2}
, which is completely new.
If
\Omega
is not convex, in the range
0>\gamma>-\min\{\frac{4}{n+2},\frac{n}{2n-2}\}
, Theorem
1.2
also gives a similar result.

In the case
\gamma=0
and
1<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, applying Theorem
1.1
to the Dirichlet zero boundary problem for the equation
\Delta^{N}_{p}u=f
,
we obtain a global

L^{2}
-estimate of Hessian
D^{2}u
, that is,
\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, which is also completely new. Also, in the range
2<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, Theorem
1.2
shows a similar result when
\Omega
is not convex.

The proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
rely on the following crucial
a priori estimates, which has its own interests.
For
1<p<\infty
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, denote by
\Delta_{p,\epsilon}

the

\epsilon
-regularization of
\Delta_{p}
, and by
\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}
the
\epsilon
-regularization
of
\Delta^{N}_{p}
, that is,

\ \Delta_{p,\epsilon}u:={\rm div}((|Du|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}Du),\quad\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}u:=(|Du|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\Delta_{p,\epsilon}u.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
(n,p,\gamma)
is admissible.
Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a bounded smooth domain, and let

v\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
with
v=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, the following holds.

(i)

If
\Omega
is convex, then

\displaystyle\|Dv\|^{{\gamma}+1}_{L^{(1+{\gamma})2^{\star}}(\Omega)}+\|D[(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}Dv]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}},

(1.6)

where
C=C(p,n,{\gamma})
.

(ii)

Assume that
\Omega
is not convex and that

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\Psi_{\Omega}(r)<\delta
for some sufficiently small positive constant
\delta=\delta(p,n,{\gamma},L_{\Omega},d_{\Omega})
.

\bullet

If
{\gamma}\leq p-2
, then (
1.6
) holds with
C=C(p,n,{\gamma},L_{\Omega},d_{\Omega})
.

\bullet

If
{\gamma}>p-2
, then

\displaystyle\|Dv\|^{{\gamma}+1}_{L^{(1+{\gamma})2^{\star}}(\Omega)}+\|D[(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}Dv]\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|v\|^{{\gamma}+1}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}.

(1.7)

with
C=C(p,n,{\gamma},L_{\Omega},d_{\Omega})
.

Below are several remarks.

Remark 1.4
.

(i) If
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
but
f\notin C^{0}(\Omega)
, we do not know whether the approximation solutions in Theorems
1.1
and

1.2
solve the problem (
1.1
) in some sense.

(ii) The uniqueness of approximation (viscosity) solutions to
the problem (
1.1
) is unavailable in general.
Indeed, under
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
if
f>0
in
\Omega
or
f<0
in
\Omega
, then the problem (
1.1
) with
\gamma=0
has unique solutions;
if
f
changes sign, it remains open for the uniqueness of viscosity solutions to
the problem (
1.1
) with
\gamma=0
;
see
[
28
]
for details.

(iii) Theorems
1.2
does not include the case
{\gamma}>p-2

since it is unclear for us whether
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+{\gamma}}
is finite a priori under
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
or even
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Recall that
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1+{\gamma}}
appears in (
1.7
) of Theorem
1.3
. For further discussion, see Remark
8.2
.

However, under stronger assumption
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
the case
{\gamma}>p-2
is allowed; see Theorem
1.5
(ii) below.

(iv) Note that
{\gamma}>\frac{n-4}{2}
if and only if
2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)>n
, which allows to use
Morrey-Sobolev embedding inequality to conclude that approximation (viscosity) solutions
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
belongs to the global Hölder space
C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
.

When
n=2
, we always have
2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)>2.
Since
\frac{4}{n+2}=1
,
the condition
\gamma>-1
implies
\gamma>-\frac{4}{n+2}
required in Theorem
1.1
.

When
n\geq 3
,
(1+{\gamma})2^{\star}\leq n
if and only if
{\gamma}\leq\frac{n-4}{2}
, and
-\frac{4}{n+2}<{\gamma}
if and only if
(1+{\gamma})2^{\star}>2
.
This allows to get the existence of approximation (viscosity) solution
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
in Theorem
1.1
.
However, in the case
n\geq 3
, if
-\frac{4}{n+2}\geq{\gamma}>\gamma_{np}
, it is unclear for us
whether we can remove
the additional restriction
\gamma>-\frac{4}{n+2}

in Theorems
1.5
and
1.1

or not.
This restriction comes only
from Lemma
7.4
(ii) and it is used to guarantee some Sobolev convergence of solutions to approximation equations.
See Remark
7.5
for more details and possible ways to remove the requirement
\gamma>-\frac{4}{n+2}
in Lemma
7.4
(ii).

1.1
Motivation and related studies in the literature

Theorem
1.3
is motivated by the Miranda-Talenti formula (see
[
32
]
)
and a regular
p
-Laplace version by Cianchi-Maz’ya (see
[
16
]
).
Miranda-Talenti formula leads to a Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-estimate of the Dirichlet problem for

\Delta u=f
, while
Cianchi-Maz’ya (see
[
16
]
) build up a nonlinear
L^{2}
-estimates for the Dirichlet problem of the equation

\Delta_{p}u=f
.
Moreover, Attouchi and Ruosteenoja
[
3
]
obtained local
W^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
regularity for viscosity solutions to
(
1.1
) under
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega)
for some special
p
and
{\gamma}
based on the Cordes approach (see
[
29
]
) and the Miranda-Talenti formula.
Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
actually extend or improve such second order regularity; for details see below. Recall from
[
7
,
2
,
3
]
that
viscosity solutions to
|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f\in C^{0}(\Omega)

always have local
C^{1,\beta}
regularity.

The famous Miranda-Talenti inequality states that

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,2}(\Omega),

(1.8)

where
\Omega
is a bounded convex domain with
C^{2}
-boundary; see
[
32
]
.
The Miranda-Talenti inequality leads to the Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-estimate for the Possion equation, that is, given any
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, the Dirichlet problem

-\Delta u=f\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega;\quad u=0\quad\mbox{on}\partial\Omega

admits a unique solution
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,2}(\Omega)

with

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|D^{2}u|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}f^{2}\,dx.

(1.9)

This was first established by Bernstein
[
5
]
when
n=2
and by Schauder
[
30
]
when
n\geq 3
.
There has been efforts to extend the class of domains so that (
1.9
) holds.
The Miranda-Talenti inequality and the Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-estimate
for Poisson equations were further generalized to bounded Lipschitz domains,
which
are either convex domains or have smallness regularity on
\Omega
, but do not necessarily have
C^{2}
-boundary; see
[
16
]
.

Miranda-Talenti inequality was employed by Cordes
[
29
]
to establish the Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-estimate for Dirichlet problem of second order linear elliptic equation

\displaystyle L_{A}(u):={\rm tr}\{AD^{2}u\}=f\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega;u=0\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,

(1.10)

where
{\rm tr}(B)
is the trace of
B
with any
n\times n
matrix
B
.
Here the coefficient
A:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is symmetric, measurable satisfying the elliptic condition

\displaystyle\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\quad\forall\xi\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\mbox{ for some}\ 0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty.

(1.11)

To be precise,
if
A
further satisfies the Cordes condition (see
[
29
]
)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n-1+\delta}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}\right)^{2}\mbox{for some}\ \delta>0,

(1.12)

then, thanks to (
1.8
), the Dirichlet problem (
1.10
)
admits a unique (strong) solution
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,2}(\Omega)
satisfying

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{1-\delta}}\sup_{\Omega}\frac{{\rm tr}(A)}{|A|^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(1.13)

In dimension
n=2,
the elliptic condition always implies the Cordes condition.
In dimension
n\geq 3
, the Cordes condition does not necessarily follow from the elliptic condition, and moreover, it is sharp to get the solvability in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,2}(\Omega)
of this problem. For
more details
we refer to

[
29
]
and the references therein.

The above Calderon-Zygmund
L^{2}
-estimate and approach for the problem (
1.10
) was adapted to study the Dirichlet problem:

-\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega;u=0\quad\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,

where
1<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
.
Such restriction comes from the Cordes condition as observed by Manfredi-Weitsman
[
27
]
; indeed,
if
Du\neq 0
, writing

-\Delta_{p}^{N}u:=-\Delta u-(p-2)\frac{\langle D^{2}uDu,Du\rangle}{|Du|^{2}}=-{\rm tr}\left\{\left[I_{n}+\frac{Du\otimes Du}{|Du|^{2}}\right]D^{2}u\right\},

we see that the coefficient

\left[I_{n}+(p-2)\frac{Du\otimes Du}{|Du|^{2}}\right]\ \mbox{ satisfies \eqref{cordes}
if and only if }\ 1<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}.

Via a local version of the Cordes condition, if
1<p<3+2/(n-2)
and
{\gamma}=0
,
Attouchi and Ruosteenoja
[
3
]
demonstrated a local
W^{2,2}
-regularity of
viscosity solutions to inhomogeneous normalized
p
-Laplace equation
\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
in
\Omega
.
Moreover, when
p
close to 2, for some small

{\gamma}
, they also obtained the local

W^{2,2}
estimate for equation (
1.1
) with
f\in W^{1,1}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
.

Recently, Cianchi-Maz’ya established a global second order regularity for the Dirichlet problem involving
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}

with
1<p<\infty
in the seminal paper
[
16
]
:

\displaystyle-\Delta_{p}u:=-{\rm div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)=f\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega;\quad u=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}

(1.14)

To be precise, for any

f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, this
problem admits a unique (generalized) weak solution
u
with
its nonlinear gradient
|Du|^{p-2}Du\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle\||Du|^{p-2}Du\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(1.15)

where
\Omega
is required to be convex or
\Psi_{\Omega}
is required to be small.
See
[
15
,
10
,
4
]
for further extension to quasilinear equations and system.
A core ingredient to obtain (
1.15
) is
a nonlinear version of Miranda-Talenti inequality by Cianchi-Maz’ya
[
16
]
, that is,

\displaystyle\||D^{2}v|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\Delta_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

(1.16)

Our a priori estimates given in Theorem
1.3
extend the Miranda-Talenti formula (
1.8
)
and a regular
p
-Laplace version (
1.16
) by Cianchi-Maz’ya
[
16
]
.
Theorem
1.3
allows to get a
nonlinear Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-estimates for the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) with suitable
p,{\gamma}
in Theorems
1.1
-
1.2
.
This extends the classical Calderón-Zygmund
L^{2}
-estimates
for Poisson equation and also the
p
-Laplace version (
1.15
) by Cianchi-Maz’ya

[
16
]
.
In particular, applying Theorems
1.1
-
1.2
to the normalized
p
-Laplace equation
\Delta^{N}_{p}u=f
,
under
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
we improve the above results by Attouchi and Ruosteenoja
[
3
]
from local
L^{2}
bound to global
L^{2}
bound of Hessian.
Applying Theorems
1.1
-
1.2
to
|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta u=f
, we get the global second order estimates.
This contributes the regularity study for the equation
|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f
, in particular,
|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta u=f
and also
\Delta^{N}_{p}u=f
.

1.2
Sketch the ideas to prove Theorems
1.1
-
1.3

We begin with the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Firstly we establish an algebraic inequality in
Lemma
3.1
, which is based on a fundamental inequality (see Lemma

3.2
) and a differential inequality
by Cianchi-Maz’ya
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
. Next applying
Lemma
3.1
, we are able to
bound
\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}+\beta}{2}}|D^{2}v|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

via
\|(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}+\beta}{2}}Dv\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
when

\Omega
is convex, or when
\Omega
is not convex and
{\gamma}<p-2
. When

\Omega
is not convex and
{\gamma}>p-2
, then
\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}+\beta}{2}}|D^{2}v|\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
are bounded by
\|(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}+\beta}{2}}Dv\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and the additional term

\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}+\beta+1}{2}}Dv\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
; see Lemma
4.1
.
To get Theorem
1.3
, thanks to Lemma
4.1
with
\beta=0
, it remains to bound
\|Dv\|_{L^{2^{\star}(1+\gamma)}(\Omega)}.

This will be done in Lemma
4.2
. Indeed, if
{\gamma}<p-2
, we bound

\|Dv\|_{L^{2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)}(\Omega)}
by

\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. However,
if
{\gamma}>p-2
,
\|Dv\|_{L^{2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)}(\Omega)}
is
bounded by the
L^{2}
-norm of
(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v

and also the additional term
L^{2}
-norm of

2
Viscosity solutions

Given a domain
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, for any function

, denote by

the Laplacian and

the
\infty
-Laplacian, that is,

\Delta v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{x_{i}x_{i}},\quad\Delta_{\infty}v=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}v_{x_{i}x_{j}}v_{x_{i}}v_{x_{j}}.

The normalized
\infty
-Laplacian is given by

\Delta^{N}_{\infty}v:=|Dv|^{-2}\Delta_{\infty}v
if
Dv\neq 0
.
For
1<p<\infty

-Laplacian is defined as

\Delta^{N}_{p}v=(p-2)\Delta^{N}_{\infty}v+\Delta v.

Their
\epsilon
-regularization for
\epsilon\in(0,1]
are given by

\Delta^{N}_{\infty,\epsilon}v:=\frac{\Delta_{\infty}v}{|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon},\quad\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v=(p-2)\Delta^{N}_{\infty,\epsilon}v+\Delta v

Recall the following definition of viscosity solutions introduced by Crandall-Ishii-Lions
[
11
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p>1
and
{\gamma}>-1
. An upper
semicontinuous function

subsolution to

-|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f
in
\Omega

provided that at any
x_{0}\in\Omega
, for any
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
with
u-\phi
reaching its local maximum at point
x_{0}
such that the following holds:

(i)

if
{\gamma}>0
, one has

\displaystyle-|D\psi(x_{0})|^{{\gamma}}\Delta^{N}_{p}\psi(x_{0})\leq f(x_{0}).

(ii)

if
{\gamma}=0
, one has

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}&\displaystyle-\Delta^{N}_{p}\phi(x_{0})\leq f(x_{0}),&\quad{\rm if}\ D\phi(x_{0})\neq 0,\\
&\displaystyle-(p-2)\max_{|\eta|=1}\langle D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\eta,\eta\rangle-\Delta\phi(x_{0})\leq f(x_{0}),&\quad{\rm if}\ D\phi(x_{0})=0\ {\rm and}\ p\geq 2,\\
&\displaystyle-(p-2)\min_{|\eta|=1}\langle D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\eta,\eta\rangle-\Delta\phi(x_{0})\leq f(x_{0}),&\quad{\rm if}\ D\phi(x_{0})=0\ {\rm and}\ 1<p<2,\end{array}\right.

(iii)

if
-1<{\gamma}<0
and

\lim_{r\to 0}\inf_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})\backslash\{x_{0}\}}[-|D\psi(x)|^{{\gamma}}\Delta^{N}_{p}\psi(x)]\leq f(x_{0}).

An lower

is said to be a viscosity
supsolution to the equation

-|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f
in
\Omega

if
-u
is its viscosity
subsolution.

A function

is a viscosity solution to

-|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta_{p}^{N}u=f
in
\Omega
if it is both viscosity subsolution
and supersolution.

We have the following remark in the case

.

Assume for this section that
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

and let
{\gamma}>-1
satisfy (

For all

\displaystyle C_{1}|D^{2}v|^{2}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)^{2}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+{\rm div}\left\{(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}(D^{2}vDv-\Delta vDv)\right\}\ \mbox{
a.e. in $\Omega$,}

To prove Lemma

For the sake of completeness, we give its proof here.

Lemma 3.2
.

It holds that

\displaystyle|D^{2}v|^{2}-2|D|Dv||^{2}+(\Delta^{N}_{\infty}v)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}(\Delta v-\Delta^{N}_{\infty}v)^{2}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega\backslash\{Dv\neq 0\}.

(3.2)

Proof.

Assume that
Dv(x_{0})\neq 0
for
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Up to considering

\frac{Dv(x_{0})}{|Dv(x_{0})|}
, we may assume
|Dv(x_{0})|=1
.
Up to some rotation,
we further assume that

At point

, one then has

\displaystyle|D|Dv||^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{x_{i}x_{n}}^{2},\quad\Delta^{N}_{\infty}v=v_{x_{n}x_{n}}.

(3.3)

Observe that

\displaystyle|D^{2}v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{x_{i}x_{i}}^{2}+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}v_{x_{i}x_{j}}^{2}\geq-v_{x_{n}x_{n}}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{x_{i}x_{n}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}v_{x_{i}x_{i}}^{2}.

Via the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality we obtain

\displaystyle|D^{2}v|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-v_{x_{n}x_{n}}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{x_{i}x_{n}}^{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}v_{x_{i}x_{i}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=-v_{x_{n}x_{n}}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{x_{i}x_{n}}^{2}+\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\Delta v-v_{x_{n}x_{n}}\right)^{2}.

From this and (
3.3
) we conclude (
3.2
) as desired.
∎

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx+C\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}.

Now it reduce to estimate the right-hand side of inequality (
4.16
).
We will divide the following two cases.

(i) Assume that
\Omega
is convex. For the first term of right-hand side in
(
4.16
), we apply (
4.1
) in Lemma
4.1
with
\beta=0

to obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}|D^{2}v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.17)

On the other hand, write

-{\rm div}((|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}Dv)=(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v+(p-2-{\gamma})(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{\infty,\epsilon}v.

Thus by Lemma
4.5
and Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}|Dv|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\big|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v+(p-2-{\gamma})(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{\infty,\epsilon}v\big|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}|D^{2}v|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}|D^{2}v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Noting that

\displaystyle(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}|Dv|\geq|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\quad\forall{\gamma}>0

(4.18)

and

\displaystyle|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\leq(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}|Dv|+2(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}|Dv|+2\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}\quad\forall-1<{\gamma}<0.

(4.19)

Now combing (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) we deduce

\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}|D^{2}v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}.

This proves (
4.4
).

(ii) Assume that
\Omega
is not convex. Let
\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\Psi_{\Omega}(r)\leq\delta
where

\delta
is given by Lemma
4.2
.
Thanks to (
4.16
) and
(
4.2
) with
\beta=0
in Lemma
4.2
, we only need to
bound the second term of right-hand side in (
4.16
). Namely,
it remains to estimate the upper bound of
\||Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
.
We also consider the following two subcases.

\bullet
Assume that
{\gamma}>p-2
. We show that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\right)^{2}\leq C\left[\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)^{2}\,dx+\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{{\gamma}+1}+\epsilon^{{\gamma}+1}\right].

Write

\displaystyle-{\rm div}((|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}Dv)=(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v+(p-2-{\gamma})(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{\infty,\epsilon}v.

Multiplying both sides by
v
and integrating
over on
\Omega
, via integration by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}|Dv|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}-{\rm div}((|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}Dv)v\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}v(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\,dx+(p-2-{\gamma})\int_{\Omega}v(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{\infty,\epsilon}v\,dx.

(4.20)

where we used the fact that
v=0
on
\partial\Omega
.

An application of Hölder’s inequality leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\,dx\leq\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Moreover, by Hölder’s inequality and also Lemma
4.1
we obtain

\displaystyle(p-2-{\gamma})\int_{\Omega}v(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{\infty,\epsilon}v\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(p,{\gamma})\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}|D^{2}v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\{\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}\right\}.

Thus it follows by (
4.20
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}}{2}}|Dv|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\{\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}\right\}.

By (
4.18
)-(
4.19
), from above we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+2}\,dx\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\{\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}\right\}+\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+2}{2}}.

From this, by Hölder’s inequality and
Young’s inequality, one gets

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2({\gamma}+1)}{{\gamma}+2}}

\displaystyle\leq C[\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2({\gamma}+1)}]^{\frac{1}{{\gamma}+2}}\left\{\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\||Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\epsilon^{{{\gamma}+1}}\right\}^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{{\gamma}+2}}+\epsilon^{{{\gamma}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\{\|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}/2}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\||Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\epsilon^{{{\gamma}+1}}\}+C\|v\|^{2({\gamma}+1)}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\epsilon^{{{\gamma}+1}}.

Absorbing the term
\frac{1}{2}\||Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}
in right-hand side to the left-hand side,
one has

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\right)^{2}\leq C\left[\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)^{2}\,dx+\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{{\gamma}+1}+\epsilon^{{\gamma}+1}\right].

This proves (
4.6
).

\bullet
Assume that
{\gamma}\leq p-2
. Recalling that (
4.4
) gives us

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}2^{\star}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{\star}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}|D^{2}v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx+C\epsilon^{\frac{{\gamma}+1}{2}}.

By Lemma
4.1
, one has

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\bar{C}\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{{\gamma}}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2({\gamma}+1)}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{{\gamma}+1}}+\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right].

To get (
4.5
), it suffices to show

\displaystyle\bar{C}\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{{\gamma}+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{({\gamma}+1)2^{\star}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)}}+C\left(\int_{\Omega}|[|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon]^{\gamma}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon})^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2({\gamma}+1)}}+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.21)

To this end, write

\displaystyle-{\rm div}((|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}Dv)=-(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v.

Since
v=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we apply Lemma
4.5
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|Dv|\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v|\,dx,

where
C=C(p,n,\Omega)
.
By
{\gamma}<p-2
, Hölder inequality and

|Dv|^{p-1}\leq 2(|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|Dv|+2\epsilon^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

one has

\displaystyle\bar{C}\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{{\gamma}+1}}

\displaystyle d\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{p-1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}[|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon]^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v|\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Hölder inequality again,

\displaystyle\bar{C}\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{{\gamma}+1}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}[|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon]^{\gamma}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon}v)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{2(p-2-{\gamma})}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}+\epsilon^{\frac{p-2-{\gamma}}{2(p-1)}}\right]+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since

p-2>{\gamma}>-1+(p-1)\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)},

then

0<2(p-2-{\gamma})\leq({\gamma}+1)2^{\star}.

Indeed, in the case
n=2
,
2^{\star}
denotes any
q\geq\frac{2(p-2-{\gamma})}{1+{\gamma}}
,
and hence

2(p-2-{\gamma})=(1+{\gamma})\frac{2(p-2-{\gamma})}{1+{\gamma}}\leq(1+{\gamma})2^{\star}.

If
n\geq 3
, by
2^{\star}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
, a direction calculation gives

\displaystyle({\gamma}+1)\frac{2n}{n-2}-2(p-2-{\gamma})

\displaystyle={\gamma}\left(\frac{2n}{n-2}+2\right)+\frac{2n}{n-2}-2(p-2)

\displaystyle={\gamma}\frac{2n+2(n-2)}{n-2}+\frac{2n+2(n-2)}{n-2}-2(p-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\left[{\gamma}-\left(-1+(p-1)\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\right)\right]>0.

Since
\frac{2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)}{2(p-2-{\gamma})}\geq 1
,
applying Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{2(p-2-{\gamma})}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{({\gamma}+1)2^{\star}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)}\frac{p-2-{\gamma}}{p-1}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\bar{C}\left(\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{{\gamma}+1}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{{\gamma}+1}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}[|Dv|^{2}+\epsilon]^{\gamma}(\Delta^{N}_{p,\epsilon})^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2(p-1)}}\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{({\gamma}+1)2^{\star}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{\star}({\gamma}+1)}\frac{p-2-{\gamma}}{p-1}}+\epsilon^{\frac{p-2-{\gamma}}{2(p-1)}}\right]+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Noting that
1<\frac{p-1}{p-2-{\gamma}}<\infty
and its conjugate exponent is

\frac{p-1}{1+{\gamma}}
, we then employ Young’s inequality to
get (
4.21
) as desired.

∎

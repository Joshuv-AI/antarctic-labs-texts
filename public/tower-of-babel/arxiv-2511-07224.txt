Bridging the divide: axion searches and axino phenomenology at colliders
Gabe Hoshino 1,2, Kristin Dona 1, Keisuke Harigaya 1,2,3,4, David W. Miller 1,2, Jan T. Offermann 1, Bianca Pol 1, Benjamin Rosser 1, Cecilia Tosciri 1

Abstract
We discuss a phenomenological model extending the minimal supersymmetric standard model containing axions and their supersymmetric partner, the axino. In the supersymmetric DFSZ axion model, the axino has tree level couplings to the higgs sector. In the case where R \displaystyle R -parity is conserved, collider experiments may be sensitive to displaced decays of heavier neutralino states into lighter, mostly axino states. We present a sensitivity analysis using a model in which mostly higgsino next-to-lightest supersymmetric particle states decay into a mostly axino lightest supersymmetric particle. The model is studied using Monte Carlo simulation produced using MadGraph and estimates of experimental sensitivities to the model, including detector simulation and kinematic selections, are evaluated using the MadAnalysis5 framework. For a higgsino mass below 1 TeV, the axion decay constant below f a < 10 11 \displaystyle f_{a}<10^{11} GeV can be effectively probed by the Large Hadron Collider with an integrated luminosity of 140 fb -1 . This work demonstrates that supersymmetric DFSZ axion models can be studied with existing collider experiments, offering complementary sensitivity to direct-detection and astrophysical searches and paving the way for broader exploration of supersymmetric axion scenarios.

Introduction

The axion is a well-motivated dark matter candidate which was initially proposed by Peccei and Quinn to solve the strong-
\displaystyle CP
problem dynamically
59

58

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}}\supset\theta\frac{g^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}G\tilde{G},

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}(1)_{\rm PQ}

\displaystyle f_{a}

The axion obtains a potential by strong QCD dynamics and a vacuum expectation value (VEV) which cancels the
\displaystyle\theta
term, thus solving the strong
\displaystyle CP
problem dynamically.

In addition to solving the strong-
\displaystyle CP

\displaystyle m_{a}
and its couplings are inversely proportional to
\displaystyle f_{a}
, yielding a large range of
\displaystyle f_{a}

25

There are several known mechanisms to produce axions in the early universe to explain the observed dark matter abundance. In particular, the so-called misalignment mechanism predicts
\displaystyle f_{a}\lesssim 10^{12}

Many prominent searches for axion dark matter rely on the axion-photon coupling.
Because of the electromagnetic anomaly of the
\displaystyle\mathrm{U}(1)_{\rm PQ}

\displaystyle g_{a\gamma\gamma}\propto 1/f_{a}\propto m_{a}

In a strong magnetic field, axions will convert into photons at some rate determined by the coupling

\displaystyle f_{a}

\displaystyle\mu\mathrm{eV}

These experiments are relatively model independent as they search for a generic pseudoscalar dark matter particle with a non-zero photon coupling. These generic probes of pseudoscalar dark matter beyond the SM (BSM) can then be applied to constrain specific UV-complete models such as the prominent Kim-Shifman-Vainshtein-Zakharov

As we will discuss, the introduction of further BSM physics such as supersymmetry can actually suppress the photon coupling, making these essential probes of axion dark matter significantly less sensitive to some well-motivated models.

There exists another issue of fine-tuning in the SM known as the electroweak hierarchy problem.
The mass of the Higgs boson is not protected by any symmetry, and as a result, the Higgs boson mass should receive quantum corrections from every massive particle in the SM, thus yielding a mass close to the Planck scale (
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{Pl}}
). In order to explain why the Higgs mass is so small compared to
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{Pl}}
, supersymmetry postulates that there is a spontaneously broken symmetry between fermions and bosons whereby every boson has a fermionic partner and every fermion has a bosonic partner. Given the difference in sign between the contributions of bosons and fermions to the Higgs mass, this symmetry can stabilize the Higgs mass against quantum corrections
53
;
71
;
73
;
49

Supersymmetry also provides ingredients necessary for successful axion models. First, the hierarchy between the axion decay constant
\displaystyle f_{a}
and
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{Pl}}
may be explained by supersymmetry
55
;
56

37
;
74
.

Incorporating supersymmetry into the PQ extension of the SM introduces the fermionic superpartner of the axion, the axino. In the case of
\displaystyle R
-parity conservation with the axino as the Lightest Supersymmetric Particle (LSP), the axino may appear in the decay chains of heavy supersymmetric particles, opening the possibility of observing an axino signature in collider experiments.

Both the axion and the axino have couplings which are suppressed by
\displaystyle f_{a}
, where
\displaystyle f_{a}
is inversely proportional to the axion mass
\displaystyle m_{a}
. Constraints on
\displaystyle f_{a}
by direct-detection and astrophysical axion searches may be interpreted as constraints on the lifetimes of heavier SUSY particle decays into axinos. Conversely, constraints on the decays of heavier SUSY particles into axinos may, under certain model-dependent assumptions, be used to constrain
\displaystyle f_{a}
,
\displaystyle m_{a}

Moreover, in the case of the DFSZ axion where the Higgs sector and therefore the higgsinos are charged under PQ symmetry, the higgsinos contribute to the anomalous axion-photon coupling and may result in a large suppression in this essential probe for many direct detection searches.

This work focuses on a model which features the decay of next-to-lightest supersymmetric particle (NLSP) higgsino-like states with a mass below 1 TeV into an LSP axino state, since a light higgsino is required for supersymmetry to explain the electroweak hierarchy.

II
Overview of axion physics

The axion arises as the pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson from a new spontaneously broken global
\displaystyle\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{PQ}}
. The
\displaystyle\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{PQ}}
symmetry is anomalous and acquires a coupling to the gluon which modifies the
\displaystyle\theta
-term as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QCD}}\supset\left(\frac{a}{f_{a}}+\theta\right)\frac{g^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}G\tilde{G}.

(2)

At low energies, the axion interacts with a potential caused by QCD dynamics and obtains a vacuum expectation value (VEV) which cancels the
\displaystyle\theta
-term, thus solving the strong-
\displaystyle CP
problem dynamically.

The DFSZ axion model provides an explicit UV completion of the effective model just described. It generates the correction to the
\displaystyle\theta
-term by introducing a Higgs doublet to the SM to which we assign PQ charge. This Higgs doublet has Yukawa couplings to the fermions of the SM requiring that these fermions be assigned chiral PQ charge. In particular, through the PQ charged quarks, we obtain a color anomaly of the
\displaystyle U(1)_{\mathrm{PQ}}
symmetry which gives rise to an effective axion-gluon coupling that cancels the
\displaystyle\theta
-term.

As a result of an electromagnetic anomaly of the PQ symmetry as well as mixing between the axion and neutral mesons at low energies, the axion obtains an effective coupling to photons
13
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{axion}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\supset\frac{1}{4}g_{a\gamma\gamma}aF\tilde{F},

(3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle g_{a\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\frac{1}{f_{a}}\left(\frac{E}{N}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{4+z}{1+z}\right),

(4)

where
\displaystyle z=m_{u}/m_{d}

13
and
\displaystyle E/N
is the ratio of the electromagnetic anomaly to the color anomaly.
Eq. (
3
) causes the conversion of axions to photons in the presence of a strong magnetic field. By measuring the rate of conversion of axions into photons (or the lack thereof) in a strong magnetic field, constraints can be placed on
\displaystyle g_{a\gamma\gamma}
as well as
\displaystyle f_{a}
. Additionally, in order to solve the strong CP problem, the QCD axion has a mass which is related to
\displaystyle f_{a}

13
as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle m_{a}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{z^{1/2}}{1+z}\frac{f_{\pi}m_{\pi}}{f_{a}}\simeq 6\ \mu\mathrm{eV}\frac{10^{12}\ \mathrm{GeV}}{f_{a}}.

(5)

The axion-photon coupling of the form Eq. (
3
) exists not only for the QCD axion, but for a broad class of axion-like particles (ALPs).
Due to the generic nature of Eq. (
3
) to a broad class of models, direct detection constraints on
\displaystyle g_{a\gamma\gamma}
– and therefore also
\displaystyle f_{a}
– also constrain a broad class of ALP models, and
additionally
place limits on
\displaystyle m_{a}
in the case of QCD axion models specifically. Other axion couplings may be probed such as the axion-electron or axion-nucleon couplings. However, those couplings are often more model-dependent as they can have tree level contributions from the UV theory as well as more generic contributions which enter at lower energies. In Section
V
, we will assess constraints from astrophysical observations on these additional couplings to the specific supersymmetrized DFSZ model considered in this work.

In the case of the supersymmetric DFSZ axion, the addition of the fermionic higgsinos changes the electric and color anomalies which set
\displaystyle E/N=2
in the anomalous contribution to the axion-photon coupling.
This leads to an axion-photon coupling which lies a factor of 20 below the non-supersymmetric DFSZ axion coupling, or, at the cost of fine-tuning, which completely vanishes
13
.
(See
12
for an analysis including higher-order chiral perturbations, which find that the possibility of cancellation persists.) In the case that such a suppression of the axion-photon coupling is realized, the collider-based approaches discussed here – as well as astrophysical observations – may be the best way to probe the supersymmetric DFSZ axion until direct detection searches are able to reach the requisite sensitivities.

III
Axion & axino model

Supersymmetry alone does not solve the hierarchy problem completely.
The supersymmetric Higgs mass parameter
\displaystyle\mu
in the minimal supersymmetric SM (MSSM) must be taken to be around the electroweak scale and be much below other fundamental scales such as the Planck scale. This may be naturally achieved if
\displaystyle\mu
is forbidden by a symmetry that is spontaneously broken.

The DFSZ axion gives the Higgs doublets PQ charges (as well as the particles which obtain their masses from the Higgs). The
\displaystyle\mu
term is forbidden by the PQ symmetry and instead the following PQ-invariant superpotential is introduced,

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{DFSZ-PQMSSM}}\supset\lambda\frac{S^{n}}{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{n-1}}H_{u}H_{d},

(6)

where
\displaystyle S
is a gauge-singlet PQ-charged field.
The
\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\mu=\lambda\left\langle S\right\rangle^{n}/{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{n-1}}\sim f_{a}^{n}/{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}}^{n-1}
. For
\displaystyle f_{a}\ll M_{\mathrm{Pl}}
, we obtain a
\displaystyle\mu
parameter which is around the proper electroweak scale, as in the Kim-Nilles model
50

We will refer to the MSSM with the gauge singlet field
\displaystyle S
, the global PQ symmetry, and the interaction in Eq. (
6

After PQ symmetry breaking, expanding about the potential of the gauge singlet field, the Kim-Nilles term gives:

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{DFSZ-PQMSSM}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\supset\mu H_{u}H_{d}+y_{a}AH_{u}H_{d}\;,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{with}\quad y_{a}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}n}{N_{\mathrm{DW}}}\frac{\mu}{f_{a}}\;,

In the above,

The axion corresponds to oscillations in the approximately flat phase degree of freedom in the wine-bottle PQ breaking potential whereas the saxion corresponds to oscillations in the radial degree of freedom.

\displaystyle\displaystyle c\tau=\left(\frac{m_{\rm NLSP}^{5}}{48\pi m_{3/2}^{2}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\simeq 20~{\rm mm}\left(\frac{1~{\rm TeV}}{m_{\rm NLSP}}\right)^{5}\left(\frac{m_{3/2}}{10~{\rm keV}}\right)^{2}.

VI
Conclusion

This work demonstrates how a collider search can provide model-dependent sensitivity to a supersymmetric DFSZ axion which is complementary to the direct detection axion searches which have decreased sensitivity to axion and axion-like particle models with a suppressed photon coupling. Projections based on existing collider experiments demonstrate compelling sensitivity to DFSZ-PQMSSM models with higher sensitivity to these models than direct detection searches which use the axion-photon coupling. The collider search also complements the astrophysical bounds on the axion decay constant.

The model considered here in which there is an axino LSP with a mostly higgsino NLSP allows for the simpler implementation of the neutralino mixing matrix but more general models can be considered in the future using spectrum generators to calculate dependent model parameters for a larger variety of models. For example, an wino-like NLSP, which is predicted when the gaugino masses are given by anomaly-mediation
62
;
46
and the scalar masses are given by gravity mediation, mixes with the higgsino to obtain a coupling to electroweak bosons and the axino, leading to a similar collider signal as the higgsino-like NLSP.
3
3

3

For a large enough wino mass, however, three-body decay modes of the wino-like NLSP dominates over the two-body decay modes, which should be appropriately implemented. This is because in the effective theory after integrating out the higgsino, the wino couples to two Higgs doublets and an axino.

Another interesting extension is small
\displaystyle R
-parity violation, with which the axino LSP can decay inside the detector volume to yield another displaced vertex.
The decaying axino would avoid the strong upper bound on the reheating temperature of the Universe from the overproduction of axinos
14
;
30
to enable leptogenesis, which requires a reheating temperature above
\displaystyle 10^{9}
GeV
45
;
22
.
A study searching for KSVZ axions could also be implemented, though such searches would require different signal processes as KSVZ axion models do not have tree level couplings between axions/axinos and the higgs sector as in the DFSZ axion models.

Ultimately, this work demonstrates that there are compelling models for supersymmetric DFSZ axions which are readily probed using existing collider experiments and which provide complementary sensitivity to direct detection axion searches. This work seeks to pave the way for full experimental treatment of the type of model discussed here as well as further modeling efforts for more general supersymmetric axion models with different mass spectra and/or
\displaystyle R
-parity violation.

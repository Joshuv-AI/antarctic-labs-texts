Knowledge, attitudes, and practices on venous thromboembolism among maternal women and analysis of influencing factors: a cross-sectional study in Southwest China
Authors: Sixu Liu, Fangmei Tang, Xiaoying Zhong, Dehua Li, Shenglin Hu, Juan Li, Juan Du, Limei Li, Tengling Chai, Xiujing Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundVenous thromboembolism (VTE) is a critical yet preventable complication during pregnancy. However, evidence on pregnant women’s knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) toward VTE and its influencing factors remains limited. This study aimed to assess the KAP levels of VTE among pregnant women and identify key determinants to inform targeted interventions.MethodsA hospital-based cross-sectional study was conducted among 1384 pregnant women in a tertiary women’s and children’s hospital in Sichuan Province, China. Data were collected using a structured questionnaire covering sociodemographic characteristics and a validated maternal KAP scale for VTE. Descriptive statistics and multiple linear regression analyses were performed to evaluate KAP scores and their influencing factors.ResultsA total of 1,384 women were included with a mean age of 29.4 ± 4.3 years. The mean total KAP score was 113.11 ± 16.82, with knowledge 33.49 ± 5.19(IQRs: 32.00–38.00), attitudes 46.93 ± 5.09(IQRs: 43.75 -75.00), and practices 32.69 ± 6.54(IQRs: 29.00–39.00). Multivariable regression indicated that knowledge was positively associated with second-trimester pregnancy (β = 0.38, 95% CI: 0.32–0.44), higher education (β = 0.15 to 0.26), and proactive learning (β = 0.06, 95% CI: 0.00–0.12), but lower among women without prior VTE education (β = − 0.16, 95% CI: − 0.21 to − 0.10). Attitudes were reduced in late pregnancy and postpartum (β = − 0.12, 95% CI: − 0.18 to − 0.06; β = − 0.09, 95% CI: − 0.16 to − 0.03) and improved with proactive learning (β = 0.11, 95% CI: 0.04–0.17). Preventive practices were lower in late pregnancy (β = − 0.17, 95% CI: − 0.23 to − 0.11) and postpartum (β = − 0.08, 95% CI: − 0.15 to − 0.02), but higher in women with undergraduate or postgraduate education (β = 0.14 to 0.18) and those with proactive learning (β = 0.14, 95% CI: 0.08–0.21).ConclusionsPregnant women in Southwest China demonstrated moderate knowledge, generally positive attitudes, but relatively low levels of preventive practices regarding VTE. Tailored interventions should prioritize women in early pregnancy stages, integrate VTE education into routine prenatal care, and leverage family support (e.g., spouse engagement). Healthcare systems should establish standardized VTE training programs and optimize individualized risk communication to reduce pregnancy-related VTE morbidity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08570-4.

Introduction

Pregnancy-associated venous thromboembolism (PA-VTE) is a critical condition resulting from physiological and obstetric risk factors during pregnancy and postpartum [1]. Physiological changes in pregnancy increase VTE risk by 4–5-fold antenatally and 30–60-fold post-delivery [2], with peak incidence occurring between ages 20–40 years, which coincides with peak childbearing years [3]. Reported incidence varies across countries: 0.1%–0.2% in the United States [4, 5], 200 cases per 100,000 person-years in the United Kingdom [6], and 0.071% in Sudan [7]. In contrast, Ireland reported 1.39 maternal deaths due to PA-VTE per 100,000 pregnancies between 2014 to 2016 [8]. In China, the burden of PA-VTE is rising. In a large hospital-based retrospective study, about 65.4% of women at risk had received thromboprophylaxis, yet 25.2% still developed VTE, often because preventive measures (mechanical or pharmacological) were delayed or not applied [9]. A survey in 2019 across mainland China found that among 106 identified maternal VTE cases, up to 75% had missed at least one opportunity for prophylaxis, especially in the postpartum period. Commonly missed interventions included mechanical methods (e.g. compression) and anticoagulant prophylaxis [10].

These figures, presented with comparable denominators, highlight both the incidence and mortality burden of PA-VTE worldwide. Despite advances in diagnosis and anticoagulation therapy [11–13], PA-VTE continues to represent a major maternal health challenge.

To address VTE risk in pregnant women, Chinese professional bodies have developed tools and consensus guidelines. For instance, in 2021 the Chinese Expert Consensus on PA-VTE Prevention standardized clinical guidelines standardized risk assessment and recommended interventions [14]. Moreover, a Delphi-AHP based perinatal VTE risk assessment scale was developed in China to help clinicians stratify risk among pregnant women and make decisions about prophylaxis [15]. In addition, utilization of low-molecular-weight heparins (LMWH) has increased in recent years for prevention during pregnancy and postpartum, especially in women with known risk factors [16].

However, despite these advances, there remain substantial gaps. First, the predictive accuracy of existing Chinese risk assessment tools seems suboptimal. In one study, the Chinese Consensus showed limited predictive efficacy (AUC ~ 0.497) for postpartum VTE [17]. Second, even when risk is identified, implementation of preventive measures is inconsistent. In many settings prophylaxis is delayed, or mechanical methods/LMWH are under-used [18]. Third, there is very little data on pregnant women’s knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) toward VTE prevention in China [19]. Key unanswered questions include what women know about risk factors and symptoms, how they perceive prevention, what behaviors they adopt, when and how health education is delivered, and what barriers hinder preventive practices [20]. It was defined as behaviors undertaken by pregnant women to reduce venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk. Understanding KAP is critical because even well-designed guidelines and risk assessment tools have limited impact if women are unaware of risks, fail to act on this knowledge, or do not receive timely education during antenatal care [20]. KAP studies make it possible to identify modifiable determinants such as gaps in knowledge, misperceptions of risk, attitudes toward prevention, availability of family or social support, and the quality of health system education [19]. These determinants can then be directly targeted through tailored interventions. Therefor, in this study we aimed to assess knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP)toward PA-VTE prevention among pregnant women in southwest of China. We hypothesized that sociodemographic and healthcare access factors - including maternal education level, frequency of prenatal visits, and prior VTE education would be significantly associated with higher KAP scores. By analyzing modifiable determinants through a multidimensional KAP framework, we aim to bridge critical evidence gaps and inform targeted antenatal interventions.

Methods

Study design and ethical considerations

We conducted a hospital-based cross-sectional study at a tertiary women’s and children’s hospital from January to October 2023 in Chengdu, Sichuan Province, China. This tertiary women’s and children’s hospital serves as a regional referral center for high-risk pregnancies, which enhances the representativeness and generalizability of our findings to urban tertiary care settings in Southwest China.

The study protocol received ethical approval from West China Second University Hospital Ethics Committee (Ethical Approval No. 2023 − 267; December 24, 2021). All participants provided written informed consent, with anonymized data collection through unique identification codes.

Participants enrollment

Using convenience sampling, we enrolled pregnant women meeting the following criteria: (1)Gestational period: gestational age ranging from early pregnancy (defined as ≤ 13 weeks of gestation) to 42 days postpartum; (2) age ≥ 18 years; (3) no prior VTE diagnosis (4) capacity to complete questionnaires independently. Exclusion criteria included: (1) comorbid severe systemic diseases, such as advanced heart, renal, or hepatic disease; and (2) documented psychiatric disorders or cognitive impairment that could preclude understanding and completion of the survey.

During data collection, questionnaires with > 20% missing responses were excluded from analysis. For item-level missingness < 20%, complete-case analysis was performed. Sensitivity analyses with mean imputation yielded comparable results; therefore, complete-case analysis was retained as the primary approach.

Measurements

Demographic and obstetric survey

We developed a questionnaire that captured comprehensive sociodemographic and obstetric information relevant to pregnancy and venous thromboembolism risk.

The sociodemographic section included items on age, ethnicity, education level, occupation, household income. The obstetric section recorded parity, gravidity, gestational stage, and history of hospitalizations and prenatal education program.

KAP assessment tool

To assess pregnant women’s knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) toward venous thromboembolism (VTE), we developed a structured questionnaire through a two-round Delphi consultation with a panel of seven experts. The expert panel consisted of specialists in obstetric nursing (n = 2), vascular nursing (n = 2), and nursing education (n = 3), with a median of 25 years of professional experience (range: 15–35 years) and all holding postgraduate qualifications. Consensus was defined a priori as item mean rating > 3.5 on a 4-point relevance scale and coefficient of variation < 0.25.

In the first Delphi round, seven questionnaires were distributed and fully returned (response rate: 100%). Six experts (85.7%) provided written suggestions, leading to the modification of six items and the addition of eight new items (e.g., adding pulmonary embolism symptoms and screening methods). In the second round, all experts reached full consensus, with no additional revisions required. Content validity was strong: item-level content validity indices (I-CVI) ranged from 0.86 to 1.00, and the scale-level CVI (S-CVI) was 0.90. Additional details on the Delphi process, including expert panel characteristics and consensus criteria, are provided in Supplementary Table S6.

The final instrument comprised three domains. Knowledge: 20 items scored on a 3-point scale (0 = incorrect, 1 = uncertain, 2 = correct; total 8–40). Higher scores indicate greater factual knowledge. Attitudes: 12 items scored on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree; total 12–60). Higher scores indicate more positive preventive attitudes; Practices: 8 items scored on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = never to 5 = always; total 8–40). Higher scores indicate more proactive preventive behaviors. The knowledge items were designed to balance comprehensibility for pregnant women with limited medical literacy while ensuring clinical accuracy. In the Chinese version, medical jargon such as “venous thromboembolism” was accompanied by lay terminology (e.g., “blood clot in the veins”) to improve accessibility. Items were phrased in a factual format (e.g., “Pregnancy increases the risk of venous thrombosis”) with response options of “Yes,” “No,” or “Uncertain.” Higher scores indicated more correct understanding. Although these items may appear confirmatory, they were validated through Delphi consultation and pretesting, during which respondents consistently demonstrated appropriate comprehension. Thus, the knowledge domain reflects participants’ factual awareness rather than mere willingness to respond affirmatively.

Psychometric testing was conducted in a pilot sample of 138 pregnant women (included within the final cohort). The instrument demonstrated strong reliability and validity: Cronbach’s α = 0.943 for the total scale, with subscale α = 0.889 (knowledge), 0.878 (attitudes), and 0.846 (practices). Item–total correlations ranged from 0.42 to 0.71. Test–retest reliability over a two-week interval (n = 20) yielded intraclass correlation coefficients between 0.834 and 0.891 across domains. No significant floor or ceiling effects were observed (< 15% at minimum/maximum scores).

The KAP framework was informed by established behavioral science models, particularly the Health Belief Model (HBM) and the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), which posit that knowledge and perceived susceptibility influence attitudes, which in turn shape preventive practices [22]. This theoretical grounding strengthens the interpretability of KAP associations in the context of pregnancy-related VTE prevention.

Data collection

This cross-sectional study recruited pregnant women across gestational stages (first/second/third trimester and postpartum) through on-site questionnaire distribution and immediate retrieval. Data collection was jointly conducted by a trained graduate researcher and the principal investigator. Eligible participants received detailed explanations of the study purpose and provided written informed consent prior to questionnaire administration. During completion, researchers clarified ambiguous items and ensured accurate interpretation. Questionnaires were collected immediately and reviewed on-site to address missing/inconsistent responses.

Data analysis

All data were entered into SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) after double entry and consistency checking. Descriptive statistics summarized participant characteristics and outcome scores.

Univariate comparisons were first performed to examine associations between independent variables and each of the three outcomes (knowledge, attitude, and practice scores). Variables with theoretical relevance check were retained for multivariable modeling. To account for the correlation among the three outcomes, a multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA) was also conducted as a supplementary check.

Multiple linear regression models were then fitted separately for knowledge, attitude, and practice scores as dependent variables. Independent variables included sociodemographic and obstetric factors, prior VTE learning, spouse learning, prenatal visit frequency, and hospitalization history, which were selected based on theoretical relevance and prior literature. Sensitivity analyses were also performed by adding additional demographic covariate (residence) to assess robustness of the results.

Regression model diagnostics were carried out for each analysis. Residual normality was assessed using Q–Q plots, homoscedasticity was evaluated with residual-versus-fitted plots, and influential observations were identified using leverage statistics and Cook’s distance. Multicollinearity was assessed by calculating variance inflation factors (VIF). Where heteroscedasticity was detected, heteroscedasticity-consistent robust standard errors (HC3) were applied.

Missing data were handled as follows: questionnaires with > 20% missing responses were excluded, while cases with < 20% item-level missingness were addressed using complete-case analysis. Sensitivity analyses using mean imputation within subscales were also conducted.

Given the convenience sampling design, external validity was further examined through sensitivity analyses stratified by key subgroups, including trimester and residential status (urban vs. rural).

A two-sided α = 0.05 was used to determine statistical significance.

Results

Socio-demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 1,384 pregnant women were included in the analysis, with a mean age of 30.9 years (SD = 3.5). The majority were of Han ethnicity (97.9%). Nearly half of the participants had completed an undergraduate degree (45.3%), while 19.9% held a master’s degree or above, and 18.6% had a junior college degree. Technical personnel accounted for 31.4% of occupations, followed by staff members (14.2%) and business service staff (16.9%). More than half of the women reported a monthly per capita income of ≥ 10,000 yuan. Only 7.9% had experienced pregnancy-related hospitalization, and the same proportion reported undergoing in vitro fertilization (IVF).

Regarding obstetric history, 50.9% were in their first pregnancy, 29.0% in their second, and 12.3% in their third. Most women had experienced only one delivery (77.5%), while 21.3% reported two, and 1.2% three or more. A large proportion of participants had not received any prior VTE-related education (81.8%), and only 17.3% reported self-directed learning, with 8.0% reporting spousal learning. PA-VTE KAP score

Statistical analysis included all 1,384 participants as shown in Table 2, with a passing threshold set at > 60% of total scores [21]. The mean knowledge score was 33.49 ± 5.19 (range: 8–40). Overall, 92.0% of participants scored above the threshold, while 8.0% scored below. The mean attitude score was 46.93 ± 5.09 (range: 12–60), with 99.1% of women scoring above the threshold, indicating a generally positive perception toward VTE prevention. The mean practice score was 32.69 ± 6.54 (range: 8–40), and 91.4% of participants scored above the threshold, whereas 8.6% were below, suggesting that preventive behaviors were less consistently adopted compared with knowledge and attitudes.

The maternal KAP questionnaire scores are summarized in Table 2, demonstrating the following outcomes. Each plot illustrates the distribution of scores with the mean (red dot), median (blue line), and interquartile range (black bar)(Fig. 2). The dashed green line indicates the 60% cutoff value used to classify participants as achieving a “pass.” The plots reveal that all three scores were slightly skewed, with clustering toward the higher end of the scales. For knowledge and practice, a ceiling tendency was observed, as many participants achieved near-maximum scores. Attitude scores displayed a relatively symmetric distribution, though the spread was wider compared with knowledge and practice.

Multivariate analysis of maternal venous thromboembolism in pregnancy

Multivariable linear regression results are presented in Table 3. For knowledge scores, women in the second trimester demonstrated significantly higher scores compared with those in the first trimester, while higher parity (> 2) and lack of proactive learning were negatively associated. Attitude scores were lower among women in the second and late pregnancy stages and were adversely influenced by the absence of prior VTE learning or proactive learning behaviors. Regarding practices, higher maternal education and prior hospitalization were positively associated with practice scores, whereas lack of prior VTE education and absence of proactive learning (self or spouse) significantly reduced practice scores. Standardized coefficients (βstd) indicated that gestational stage, prior VTE learning, and proactive learning behaviors were the strongest determinants across all three KAP domains.

As a sensitivity analysis, regression models were stratified by gestational stage and parity (Supplementary Table S2-S3). The associations between prior VTE learning, proactive learning behaviors, and KAP outcomes remained consistent across trimesters, supporting the robustness of the main findings. Notably, knowledge scores increased significantly from the first to the second trimester, while the effects on attitudes and practices showed a plateau or decline in the later stages of pregnancy.

Model diagnostics were performed for the three multivariable linear regression models (knowledge, attitudes, and practices). Q–Q plots demonstrated that residuals were approximately normally distributed, with only minor deviations observed at the distribution tails. Residual-versus-fitted plots indicated no strong patterns, suggesting no major violation of homoscedasticity. Leverage versus Cook’s distance plots identified a few potentially influential observations; however, none exceeded conventional thresholds, and their influence on model stability was limited. Variance inflation factor (VIF) values for all predictors were below 5, indicating no evidence of problematic multicollinearity. Breusch–Pagan tests revealed evidence of heteroscedasticity in all three models (knowledge: LM = 120.25, df = 15, p < 0.001; attitudes: LM = 104.87, df = 15, p < 0.001; practices: LM = 70.49, df = 15, p < 0.001). Accordingly, regression estimates were re-computed using HC3 robust standard errors. The results (Supplementary Tables S3–S4) yielded substantively consistent inferences with the original models, confirming that the associations identified were robust to heteroscedasticity. Given the sample size (n = 1,384) and number of covariates, each model exceeded 100 observations per parameter, indicating no risk of model overfitting.

Discussion

This study assessed knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) regarding pregnancy-associated venous thromboembolism (PA-VTE) among 1,384 pregnant women in Southwest China. Overall, participants showed moderate knowledge, generally positive attitudes, but suboptimal practices. Multivariable analysis suggested that gestational stage, education level, parity, prior VTE education, and proactive learning behaviors were key determinants of KAP outcomes. Importantly, knowledge and attitudes were positively correlated with preventive practices, supporting the theoretical framework of KAP.

Factors affecting knowledge score

In this study, maternal VTE knowledge was higher in the second trimester, while lack of prior VTE learning, absence of proactive learning (self or spouse), and higher parity were associated with lower knowledge. Similar associations have been reported internationally. A Nigerian survey of 1,000 pregnant women showed very low awareness of pregnancy-related VTE, with women’s knowledge strongly influenced by their husbands’ education and support, emphasizing the importance of family involvement in health education [23]. In Saudi Arabia, a cross-sectional study found that knowledge of VTE risk factors and symptoms was limited among pregnant and postpartum women, but higher education levels were associated with better awareness, indicating the role of maternal literacy in knowledge acquisition [24]. A Polish study similarly demonstrated that most pregnant women were unfamiliar with VTE risk factors and preventive measures, highlighting that knowledge deficits are common even in high-resource settings [25]. Taken together, these findings suggest that structured, plain-language VTE education during antenatal care, combined with active engagement of spouses or partners, may be effective in bridging maternal knowledge gaps. Future studies should prospectively assess whether such targeted strategies can sustainably improve awareness and preventive behaviors in pregnancy.

Factors affecting attitude scores

In our analysis, routine prenatal visit attendance and prior or proactive VTE learning were positively associated with maternal preventive attitudes, while women in late pregnancy and postpartum demonstrated lower attitude scores. These results highlight the importance of sustained health education across gestational stages. Similar findings have been reported elsewhere. An Egyptian cross-sectional study of pregnant women demonstrated that attitudes toward VTE prevention were generally low but significantly correlated with knowledge levels, supporting the notion that improving awareness may positively influence attitudes [26]. A systematic review further concluded that structured education can strengthen both knowledge and attitudes toward VTE prevention among at-risk populations [27]. Additionally, a study found that women’s acceptance of low-molecular-weight heparin during pregnancy was influenced by their belief and concerns, highlighting that counseling and risk communication directly shape attitudes [28]. These findings suggest that embedding standardized VTE education into routine prenatal care, reinforced during late pregnancy and postpartum, may help sustain positive maternal attitudes. Future interventional studies should assess the optimal timing and format of education to maintain favorable attitudes across gestation.

Factors affecting practices scores

Our findings showed that women with higher educational attainment and a history of hospitalization reported better preventive practices, while those in late pregnancy and postpartum had lower scores. Prior VTE education and proactive learning behaviors, either by the woman herself or her spouse, were also positively associated with practice scores. These results suggest that individual literacy levels, clinical experiences, and learning opportunities may play important roles in shaping preventive behaviors. Evidence from other contexts reinforces these observations. In the United States, a quality-improvement study among hospitalized patients showed that patient-centered education substantially reduced refusals and missed doses of pharmacologic thromboprophylaxis, highlighting the importance of clear and timely counseling for improving adherence [29]. In Nepal, a randomized controlled trial demonstrated that including husbands in antenatal health education sessions led to greater improvements in maternal and neonatal health behaviors compared with educating women alone, indicating that family engagement may reinforce preventive practices [30]. Similarly, in Egypt, an interventional study involving systematic education on deep vein thrombosis for pregnant women reported significant improvements in self-reported preventive practices, confirming the contribution of structured educational exposure to behavior change [31]. These findings suggest that clinical programs should provide structured, clear, and stage-specific VTE education, particularly for women with lower educational levels, and systematically involve spouses to strengthen family support. Future prospective and interventional research is needed to determine whether multifaceted strategies that combine partner participation, structured discharge counseling, and regular follow-up reminders can effectively enhance and sustain preventive practices throughout pregnancy and the postpartum period.

Conclusion

This hospital-based cross-sectional study examined knowledge, attitudes, and practices related to venous thromboembolism among pregnant women in Southwest China. Overall, participants demonstrated moderate knowledge, generally positive attitudes, but relatively low levels of preventive practices, highlighting a clear gap between awareness and behavior. Multivariable analysis identified gestational stage, education level, parity, prior VTE education, and proactive learning as important correlates of KAP outcomes. These findings indicate that maternal literacy, timely and repeated health education, and family engagement are closely linked to women’s preventive capacity. The study contributes to the growing evidence that structured, stage-specific, and plain-language education, reinforced during late pregnancy and postpartum, may help sustain preventive attitudes and practices. In addition, involving spouses or family members could further strengthen women’s ability to translate knowledge into action. Given the cross-sectional design, causal relationships cannot be established, and generalizability is limited by the single-center setting. Future prospective and interventional studies are warranted to test targeted education strategies, evaluate their impact on maternal outcomes, and inform the development of evidence-based VTE prevention programs in pregnancy.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional design precludes any inference of causality between the identified factors and KAP outcomes, which is an inherent constraint of observational research. Second, participant self-reported data, which may introduce recall bias and social desirability bias. Third, the use of convenience sampling from a single tertiary hospital may lead to selection bias and limit the representativeness of the findings. As a result, the generalizability of these results to women in rural areas, non-tertiary facilities, or other regions of China is limited, particularly given differences in healthcare access, education, and cultural practices. Fourth, although the KAP instrument was developed with expert input, it has not been externally validated in other populations, and residual confounding from unmeasured variables cannot be ruled out. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings, and future multicenter, prospective studies are needed to confirm and extend the present results.

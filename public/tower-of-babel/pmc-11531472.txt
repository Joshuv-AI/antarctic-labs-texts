Development of a sustainable and disposable modified Bi-CdFe2O4 electrode for electrochemical sensing of lead (II) and Acetaminophen drug molecule
Authors: B S Surendra, M Mahadeva Swamy, Bincy Rose Vergis, M Bhaskar, C D Shilpa, Syed Khasim, K Y Chan, H C Ananda Murthy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe study of research proposes a systematic pattern for optimization and fabrication of a sustainable-cost effective electrochemical sensor made by Bi-CdFe2O4 (BCDF) nanoparticle and graphite powder. The structural examinations of synthesized BCDF materials were analyzed by specific spectral techniques viz.; P-XRD, SEM-EDX, TEM, XPS, FT-IR and DRS techniques. The modified sensor electrode offer a significant electrochemical properties that can improve the material selectivity and sensitivity actions measured by Cyclic Voltammetric (CV) and Electrochemical Impedance Spectral (EIS) plots. We demonstrated a developed highly-purity BCDF-graphite paste electrode for sensing actions on Paracetamol and Lead (Pb2+) ions under 0.1 M KCl. The excellent sensing activity towards Lead ions and Paracetamol confirmed by its redox potential peaks at scan rate of 1–5 V/s with maximum sensitivity of -0.61 V and 0.69 V respectively. The excellent photo-dye-degradation action of BCDF (98.2%) than those of host CDF (81.6%) on Rose Bengal (RB) dye was demonstrated. Its kinetic study reveals that this process follows first order kinetics and rate constants of the host (18.1 × 10−3 min−1) and BCDF (39.2 × 10−3 min−1) were measured. Thus, the synthesized BCDF electrode provides a new perception for developing specific nano-sensor towards detection of toxic metals.Keywords: Bi-CDFe2O4 nanoparticles, Green assisted fuel, Electrochemical sensor study, Heavy metal detection, Photocatalytic studiesSubject terms: Chemistry, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

Nanotechnology is a part of science encompasses a fabrication and application of materials towards designing and development of various devices by manipulating atoms or molecules at nanoscale range1,2. Various derivatives of organo-phosphorus chemical substances are widely used in industrial manufacture and reaches into the food derivatives and water bodies poses danger to human health. In this regards, the rapid detection of such toxic pollutants becomes increasingly essential for homeland security and health protection3–6. Nanotechnology has emerged as a multi-disciplinary action and is gaining a significant applications in different fields such as optics, sensors, industries, medical-biomedical diagnostics, gas sensing, and energy engineering activities7–10. Among the various kinds of applications, the electrochemical sensor detection of heavy metals and toxic pollutants is considered as the most beneficial for health and environmental issues. Various kinds of toxic metals (Cd, Hg, Cr, etc.) and heavy metals (Arsenic, Thallium, Mercury, Lead etc.) are released into the water-bodies from various sectors, threatening the living organisms10–14. Among these heavy metals, Pb is most the hazardous metal for eco-system because of its properties having higher ionic mobility than those of other organic varieties.

According to WHO, the maximum allowable level of Hg2+ ions is 1 µg·L−1 and its exceeded concentrations can affect the environment15–17. Among various heavy metals, the Pb metal is a major public-health concern due to its ubiquitous and impacts on human-beings18. To over come these issues, we focused on detection of heavy metal (Pb2+) using synthesized ferrite nanoparticle as a electrode sensor by electrochemical analytical technique. Nowadays, the electrochemical examination has shown great interest and promising approaches due to simple, high selectivity with sensitivity towards qualitative and quantitative measurements of heavy metals. Various reviews reveals that the modified electrode–electrolyte interface with enhanced selectivity and sensitivity of electrochemical sensor is largely drafting an attention of the scholars for sensing actions of insectisides, pesticides, etc., using glassy carbon electrode19. Based on the unique physico-chemical characteristics (surface area, low energy band gap, etc.), the developed nanoparticles are successfully applied for electrode modification to enhances their electrochemical sensors actions. In this regard, we proposed a modified electrochemical sensors by prepared Bi doped cadmium ferrite nanoparticle to enhances the detection of Hg2+ ions and paracetamol molecules in analyte.

Additionally, the nanomaterials can also play a vital role by providing excellent solutions to technological and environmental challenges in the areas of waste water treatment. Currently, large quantities of organic dyes consisting of various toxic chemicals are generated from the textile industries and discharged directly into surrounding water-bodies, causing all aquatic and living organisms. Several researcher and scientists have worked challenging to new nano-catalysts or inorganic materials that may detoxify organic and harmful pollutants in wastewater20,21. Hence, it is necessary to degrade the toxic chemicals using suitable photocatalyst for the reduction of environmental issues.many transition metal-based photocatalysts are non-toxic, and abundantly dispersed in the earth’s crust commonly utilized in photocatalytic mineralization for various organic dyes. Thus, the Bi-CdFe2O4 nanomaterial offers a new possibility towards the step-down of environmental pollution by advanced photocatalytic oxidation activity. The studied research discloses a fabricated Bi-CdFe2O4 nanomaterial has shown excellent activity towards photo-dye-degradation action on RB dye. The outstanding electrochemical studies of BCDF were recorded, which reveals the materials having higher Csp and lesser resistance confirmed by CV and EIS studies. The developed BCDF-graphite electrode has been successfully applied for sensor activity on Paracetamol and Lead (Pb2+) ions in 0.1 M KCl. The excellent sensing actions were measured by its redox potential peaks with scan rate of 1–5 V/s, LOD values and maximum sensitivity. Thus, the synthesized BCDF electrode provides an new perception for developing specific nano-sensor towards detection of toxic metals and photocatalytic applications.

Materials and methods

Materials

The used chemical reagents for experimental synthesis of BCDF nanoparticle are cadmium nitrate (Cd(NO3)2), bismuth nitrate (Bi(NO3)3) and ferric nitrate (Fe(NO3)3) purchased from SD fine-chem Ltd. and were utilized as they received without further purification.

Fabrication of BCDF nanoparticle

The fabrication of a sustainable-cost effective BCDF nanoparticles under bio-medicinal Tulasi leaf assisted combustion method. The stoichiometric ratios of Bi(NO3)3, Cd(NO3)2 and Fe(NO3)3 are used as initial-precursors for the nano-synthesis. The mixture of optimized green powder (1 g) fuel and starting reagents are situated in a high temperature resistant silica-crucible, which is allowed to attain homogeneity by continuous stirring under a magnetic-stirrer at 600 rpm in presence of 5 ml of double distilled-water. The prepared homogeneous mixture was placed into the muffle furnace combustion assembly maintained at 450 ± 10 °C. The brownish black powder was collected after the combustion process and it was subjected to characterize by various spectral techniques. The step-wise synthesis representation of BCDF nanoparticle was shown in Fig. 1.

The schematic representation of synthesized BCDF nanomaterials.

Preparation of modified BCDF-graphite electrode

The sustainable-cost effective electrochemical examinations of modified BCDF-graphite electrode was analyzed by CV technique. This modification of electrode was achieved by using specific quantity of 0.075 g of BCDF nanoparticle, 0.375 g of graphite powder and 2–3 drops of polytetra-fluoroethylene binder. The above mixture was blended by grinding thoroughly using mortar and pestle for about 30 min. The achieved mixture was placed into the template electrode having copper wire and packed tightly. This modified BCDF-graphite electrode was kept for 1 to 2 h for attaining the electrode stability. This modified electrode offer a significant electrochemical properties that can improve the material selectivity and sensitivity actions.

P-XRD investigation

Figure 2 shows the Powder X-ray Diffraction (PXRD) patterns of synthesized host and BCDF nanomaterials. The presence of sharp and strong reflection peaks in PXRD patterns reveals the crystalline nature of achieved nanoparticles. The P-XRD spectrum of Bi doped CdFe2O4 nanoparticle shows characteristic diffraction plans at a 2θ angle of 30.06°, 34.19°, 35.35°, 41.17°, 51.07°, 55.02°, 60.24° and 64.33° corresponding to (220), (311), (222), (400), (422), (511), (533) and (622) peaks respectively. This diffraction plans can be indexed as a spinel phase matrix structure and observed planes were good agreement with JCPDS Card No 79-115522,23. The ascertained diffraction plans of prepared bismuth doped cadmium ferrite nanoparticles provides information about its crystal structure and phase composition. However, the doping of bismuth shows a shifting in the positions of P-XRD peaks than those of host-CDF nanoparticle. As a result, the change in lattice parameters are recorded due to the incorporation of bismuth atoms into host-lattice. The spinel ferrite typically made with face-centered cubic oxygen ions and Cd2+ and Fe3+ ions are in tetrahedral and octahedral sites of host-lattice. A small shift of peaks towards a lower angle with increasing the dopant Bi3+ ions in host crystal-lattice is represented in Fig. 3. The doping of bismuth ions may lead to a changes in intensity of P-XRD peaks and causes the variations in degree of crystallinity of the material21–24.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis

XPS analysis is a quantitative surface sensitive technique that measure the electronic structures and chemical states of BCDF nanomaterial. The wide XPS spectrum of synthesized sample distinctly shows its existed elemental composition such as Bi, Cd, O, Fe & C (Fig. 5f) and were well agreed with the EDAX investigation (Fig. 4d). The high-resolution spectral plots of Bi 4f, Cd 3d, O 1s, Fe 2p and C 1s were shown in Fig. 5(a–e) respectively. The XPS peaks ascertained at 156.3 and 163.4 eV were corresponds to the oxidation state of Bi 4f 7/230 (Fig. 5a). The observed two characteristic binding energy peaks at 405.8 and 416.9 eV confirms the bivalent oxidation state of Cd (3d 5/2)31 and its spectrum as shown in Fig. 5b. The difference between binding energy of splitting peaks of Bi and Cd ions was found to be 7.1 eV and 10.8 eV respectively, reveals the nature of oxidation state of Bi3+ and Cd2+ ions. The strong intensity peaks at 535.4 eV is attributed to the presence of oxygen (O 1s) spectrum as displayed in Fig. 5c. The presence of two characteristics peaks at 710.5 and 726.6 eV corresponds to 2p3/2 and 2p1/2 orbits respectively, which shows the nature of oxidation state of Fe3+ (Fig. 5d). Figure 5e shows the C 1s spectrum of prepared sample and its peak observed at 286.6 eV representing the C-C or C-O bonds due to impact of green fuel used synthesis part. Thus, the recorded results support the purity of sample with its elemental measurements and were in concordance with EDX and XRD results.

Photocatalytic examination

Photocatalytic activity has gained great attention due to their potential applications in industrial waste water treatment via oxidation and hydrolysis using heterogeneous photocatalyst under light energy. The synthesized Bi-CdFe2O4 nanoparticle acts as a semiconductor and extensively designed as an excellent heterogeneous photocatalyst due to its lower rate of electron-hole pair recombination and increases the photo-induced chemical reactions for waste water treatment with higher efficiency. The photocatalytic dye-decolouration of RB molecule was used to measure the photocatalytic efficiency of the host and BCDF nanomaterials under UV-Visible light irradiation. The spectral absorbance of light irradiated dye solutions with presence of host and BCDF nanomaterials were measured by using UV-Visible spectroscopy at 540 nm as shown in Fig. 10(a&b) respectively.

(a) Spectral absorbance of host CDF; (b) Spectral absorbance of BCDF nanoparticle on RB dye; (c) Percentage decomposition of RB dye; and (d) Stability test.

The observed results were recorded by maintaining its specific conditions during the experimental activities performed with 60 ppm of RB dye stock solution and 20 mg of synthesized photocatalysts under light irradiation at 90 min. Therefore, the doped BCDF sample showed an excellent photo-dye-degradation action (98.2%) than those of host CDF (81.6%) on Rose Bengal (RB) dye as shown in Fig. 10(c). In addition, the photocatalytic activities for dye solution were carried out under dark and absence of photocatalysts (photolysis) to measure the stability of RB dye on light energy and its degradation efficiency were found to be 8.2 and 13.3% respectively (Fig. 10c). Further, the recycle stability action of used photocatalyst on RB dye decolouration were measured under similar conditions over 5 cycles as displayed in Fig. 10d. These recorded data reveals that the recycled photocatalyst have no noticeable alteration up-to 5 cycles and confirms its good photo-stability actions on dye degradation.

In addition, the measurement of half-time (t1/2) for photo-degradation action of synthesized photocatalysts were examined by plotting (C/C0) v/s time under light energy as depicted in Fig. 11(a&b). The t1/2 of synthesized host and BCDF photocatalysts were found to be 26.5 and 16.2 min respectively. The achieved results reveals the faster dye-degradation action of BCDF photocatalyst than those of host nanomaterial. Conclusions

A modified nano-based electrode study offered a useful process for devising sustainable-cost effective electrochemical sensor by BCDF nanoparticle. The selectivity and sensitivity action of prepared material was examined on heavy metal -Pb(II) and Acetaminophen drug molecule under 0.1 M KCl. The increasing concentration (1 mmol/L to 5 mmol/L at 0.03 V/s) of positively charged Pb(II) and paracetamol in addition to modified nano-electrode shows good adsorption and excellent redox reaction in conductive analyte, resulted as higher CV signals. Thus, the excellent sensing activity was recorded towards Lead ions confirmed by its redox potential peaks with scan rate of 1–5 mM, LOD of PbRed.1.53 mM, PbOxd.-4.1 mM, and maximum sensitivity of -0.61 V. Similarly, Paracetamol sensing actions were recorded by LOD of PARed.1.57 mM PAOxd.-6.44 mM with highest sensitivity at 0.69 V. The Eg value of prepared host and BCDF nanoparticles were found to be 3.3 and 3.0 eV respectively. The decreased energy band gap of BCDF nanoparticle showed enhanced photo-dye-degradation action (98.2%) than those of host CDF (81.6%) for RB degradation under light irradiation at 90 min. The t1/2 of synthesized host and BCDF photocatalysts were found to be 26.5 and 16.2 min respectively reveals the faster dye-degradation action of BCDF photocatalyst. Finally, the synthesized BCDF electrode provides an new perception for developing specific nano-sensor for detection of heavy metal/toxic molecules.

Homogenization, dimension reduction and linearization of thin elastic plate
Amartya Chakrabortty, Georges Griso, Julia Orlik

Abstract
This paper investigates the homogenization, dimension reduction, and linearization of a composite plate subjected to external loading within the framework of non-linear elasticity problem. The total elastic energy of the problem is of order ∼ h 2 ​ ε 2 ​ a + 3 \sim h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3} , where a ≥ 1 a\geq 1 . The paper is divided into two parts: The first part presents the simultaneous homogenization, dimension reduction and linearization ( ( ε , h ) → ( 0 , 0 ) (\varepsilon,h)\to(0,0) ) of a composite plate without any coupling assumption of ε \varepsilon and h h . The second part consists of the rigorous derivation of linearized elasticity as a limit of non-linear elasticity with small deformation and external loading conditions. The results obtained demonstrate that the limit energy remains unchanged when first linearization ( h → 0 h\to 0 ) is performed, followed by simultaneous homogenization dimension reduction ( ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 ) and when both limits approach zero simultaneously, i.e. ( ε , h ) → ( 0 , 0 ) (\varepsilon,h)\to(0,0) . The exact form of the limit energy(s) is obtained through the decomposition of plate deformations and plate displacements. By using the Γ \Gamma -convergence technique, the existence of a unique solution for the limit linearized homogenized energy problem is demonstrated. These results are then extended to certain periodic perforated plates.

Introduction

The periodic thin composite plate is characterized by two parameters: thickness
\delta\in(0,1)
and period
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Two asymptotic analysis are considered: dimension reduction as
\delta\to 0
and homogenization as
\varepsilon\to 0
. When both parameters tends zero simultaneously, this process is referred to as simultaneous homogenization and dimension reduction
(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)
.

Given that
(\varepsilon,\delta)\in(0,1)^{2}
, it follows that the limit

\lim_{(\varepsilon,\delta)\to(0,0)}{\delta\over\varepsilon}=\kappa\in[0,+\infty]

(1.1)

exists for at least a subsequence. In the present work, it is assumed that this limit exists for the entire sequence, with limit
\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
, i.e.
\delta
and
\varepsilon
are of same order. For simplicity of notation,
\delta=\varepsilon
is chosen. Thus, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, simultaneous homogenization and dimension reduction (SHD) is achieved.

This paper presents a theoretical asymptotic analysis of a composite plate with external loading that is periodic with respect to
\varepsilon
in two in-plane directions and has a thickness of
2\varepsilon
in the framework of non-linear elasticity. The paper is divided into two parts. The first part deals with Simultaneous Homogenization Dimension Reduction and Linearization (SHDL). The total elastic energy of the system is scaled to be of order
h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3}
, where
a\geq 1
. In the second part, it is shown that linear elasticity arises as a limit of non-linear elasticity in the realm of small deformation and external loading. The study demonstrates that the same limit is obtained when linearization is performed first, followed by SHD as well as when both asymptotic analyses are performed simultaneously. The primary focus of this work is on the critical case of
a=1
, commonly referred to as the von-Kármán regime.

The domain of discussion is a periodic composite plate defined as
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
with
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary. The total energy with external loading is given by

{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}(v)\doteq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\widehat{W}_{\varepsilon}(x,\nabla v)-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{h,\varepsilon}\cdot(v-\text{\bf id})dx,

where
f_{h,\varepsilon}
is the external loading.
Then
the minimization problem reads as

m_{\varepsilon,h}=\inf_{v\in{\bf V}_{\varepsilon}}{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}(v),

where
{\bf V}_{\varepsilon}
contains the set of admissible deformations. The external forces are scaled in such a way that the total elastic energy is of order
h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3}
, i.e.

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\widehat{W}_{\varepsilon}(x,\nabla v)\,dx\sim{\bf O}(h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3})\quad\text{with $a\geq 1$}.

The main result of the first part of the paper is given in Theorem
1
(SHDL), which states that

\lim_{(\varepsilon,h)\to(0,0)}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3}}=\min_{({\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathfrak{v}})}{\bf J}({\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathfrak{v}})={\bf J}({\mathcal{U}},\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}),

where

{\bf J}({\mathcal{U}},\widehat{\mathfrak{u}})=\int_{\omega\times{\mathcal{Y}}}{\bf Q}(y,E^{Lin}({\mathcal{U}})+e_{y}(\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}))\,dx^{\prime}dy-|{\mathcal{Y}}|\int_{\omega}f\cdot{\mathcal{U}}\;dx^{\prime}.

In the second part of the paper, first the linearization result is presented in Theorem
3
, which states

\lim_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3}}={m_{\varepsilon}\over\varepsilon^{2a+3}}={{\bf J}_{\varepsilon}^{Lin}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\over\varepsilon^{2a+3}},

where

m_{\varepsilon}=\inf_{{\bf v}_{\varepsilon}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}}{\bf J}^{Lin}_{\varepsilon}({\bf v}_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{with}\quad{\bf J}^{Lin}_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon})=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\bf Q}\left({x\over\varepsilon},e({\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\right)dx-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}dx,

followed by SHD, as shown in Theorem
4

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3}}=\min_{({\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathfrak{v}})\in{\mathbb{D}}}{\bf J}({\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathfrak{v}})={\bf J}({\mathcal{U}},\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}).

Finally, the main commutavity result is presented in the Theorem
6

\lim_{(\varepsilon,h)\to(0,0)}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{2a+3}}{\bf J}({\mathcal{U}},\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}).

\big\|h\big(\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\big)^{T}\big(\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\big)\big\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq h\big\|\nabla{\bf u}_{h}\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq Ch.

This gives

h\big(\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\big)^{T}\big(\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\big)\to 0\qquad\text{strongly in $L^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3\times 3}$}

(7.4)

Again, using (
7.3
) and proceeding as in (
6.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle h\|(\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon})^{T}\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C,

which along with the convergence (
7.4
) gives

h\big(\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\big)^{T}\big(\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\big)\rightharpoonup 0,\quad\text{weakly in $L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3\times 3}$}.

So, using the above convergence along with (

∎

The next theorem is the main linearization result, in which we give the limit of the rescaled sequence
\displaystyle\left\{{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}}\right\}_{h}
as
h\to 0
(
\varepsilon
is fixed).

Theorem 3
.

We have (
\varepsilon
being fixed)

\lim_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{5}}=m_{\varepsilon},

(7.5)

where

m_{\varepsilon}=\inf_{{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}}{\bf J}^{Lin}_{\varepsilon}({\bf u})\quad\text{with}\quad{\bf J}^{Lin}_{\varepsilon}({\bf u})=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\bf Q}\left({x\over\varepsilon},e({\bf u})\right)dx-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf u}\,dx.

(7.6)

Proof.

The following proof uses a form of
\Gamma
-convergence.

We set

\liminf_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{5}}=m_{\varepsilon}.

Step 1:
In this step, we prove that there exist a unique
{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}
such that

{\bf J}_{\varepsilon}^{Lin}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon})=m_{\varepsilon}=\inf_{{\bf v}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}}{\bf J}_{\varepsilon}^{Lin}({\bf v}),

(7.7)

where

{\bf J}_{\varepsilon}^{Lin}({\bf v})=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\bf Q}\left({x\over\varepsilon},e({\bf v})\right)\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf v}\,dx.

The variational formulation of the total elastic energy (
7.6
) for applied forces
f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3}
is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hbox{Find ${\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}$ such that },\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon})e_{ij}(w)\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot w\,dx,\quad\forall w\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}\right.

(7.8)

where the stress tensor is given by

\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}(w)=a^{\varepsilon}_{ijkl}e_{kl}(w),\quad\text{for all $w\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}$}.

We define the bilinear form

{\bf a}_{\varepsilon}(u,w)=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{ij}(u)e_{ij}(w)\,dx,\quad\text{for all $u,w$ in ${\bf U}_{\varepsilon}$}.

(7.9)

Observe that since
a_{ijkl}\in L^{\infty}({\mathcal{Y}})
and satisfy the condition (
3.3
), we have that bilinear form (
7.9
) is bounded and symmetric on
{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}\times{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}
. Using inequality (
3.4
), we have

K_{1}\|e(w)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq{\bf a}_{\varepsilon}(w,w),\quad\forall w\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon},\quad\text{for a.e. $x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}$}.

Since
w\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}
, we have using Korn-type inequalities (
4.12
) that there exist a constant
K(\varepsilon)>0
(depending on
\varepsilon
) such that

K(\varepsilon)\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq K_{1}\|e(w)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq{\bf a}_{\varepsilon}(w,w),

which imply the bilinear form
{\bf a}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the coerciveness condition.
Consequently, by applying the Lax-Milgram lemma with the assumption of the forces that
f_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3}
, there exist a unique solution
{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}
for the elasticity problem (
7.8
) (which is also the unique minimizer of (
7.6
)). This gives (
7.7
), which completes Step 1.

Step 2:
In this step we show that

\liminf_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{5}}\geq m_{\varepsilon}.

Consider a minimizing sequence
\{v_{h,\varepsilon}\}_{h}
of admissible deformations in
{\bf V}_{\varepsilon}
satisfying

\liminf_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}}=\lim_{h\to 0}{1\over h^{2}}{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}(v_{h})

where

{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}(v)=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\widehat{W}_{\varepsilon}(x,\nabla v)\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon,h}\cdot(v-\text{\bf id})\,dx.

Since each
v_{h,\varepsilon}
is an admissible deformations, so we have
{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}(v_{h,\varepsilon})\leq{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}(\text{\bf id})=0
.

Then, we have convergences as in Lemma
11
. Similarly, proceeding as in Step 2 of the proof of Theorem
1
give

\liminf_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}}=\liminf_{h\to 0}{{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}(v_{h,\varepsilon})\over h^{2}}\\
\geq\liminf_{h\to 0}\left({1\over h^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\bf Q}\left({x\over\varepsilon},{1\over 2}\Big({\bf I}_{3}+h\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\Big)^{T}\Big({\bf I}_{3}+h\nabla{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon}\Big)-{\bf I}_{3}\right)\,dx-{1\over h^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon,h}\cdot(h{\bf u}_{h,\varepsilon})\,dx\right)\\
=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\bf Q}\left({x\over\varepsilon},e({\bf u}_{\varepsilon})\right)\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\,dx={\bf J}^{Lin}_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon}).

This completes the proof of Step 2.

Step 3:
In this step, we show that

\limsup_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}}\leq{\bf J}_{\varepsilon}^{Lin}({\bf u}),\qquad\forall\;{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}.

It is enough to prove the above inequality for all
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}^{*}={\bf U}_{\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})^{3}
, since
{\bf U}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
is dense in
{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}
.

So, we prove that for any admissible
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}^{*}_{\varepsilon}
, there exists a sequence
\{{\bf w}_{h}\}_{h}
of admissible elements in
{\bf V}^{*}_{\varepsilon}={\bf V}_{\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})^{3}
such that

{1\over h}({\bf w}_{h}-\text{\bf id})\to{\bf u}\quad\text{strongly in}\;\;{\bf U}^{*}_{\varepsilon}

and

\limsup_{h\to 0}{m_{h}\over h^{2}}\leq\lim_{h\to 0}{{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}({\bf w}_{h})\over h^{2}}={{\bf J}^{Lin}_{\varepsilon}({\bf u})}.

Let
{\bf u}\in{\bf U}_{\varepsilon}\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})^{3}
is an re-scaled displacement, then we define the corresponding sequence of deformations by

{\bf w}_{h}=\text{\bf id}+h{\bf u}.

Observe that

{\bf w}_{h}\in{\bf V}^{*}_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{for all $h$}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla{\bf w}_{h}-{\bf I}_{3}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=\|h{\bf u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq Ch.

Here, the constant do not depend on
h
. This implies for small enough
h
, we have
|\nabla{\bf w}_{h}-{\bf I}_{3}|_{F}<1
a.e. in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. As a consequence, we get for all
h>0

\text{det}(\nabla{\bf w}_{h})(x)>0,\quad\text{for a.e.}\;\;x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

This leads to

\widehat{W}_{\varepsilon}(x,\nabla{\bf w}_{h})={\bf Q}\left({x\over\varepsilon},{\bf E}(\nabla{\bf w}_{h})\right),\quad\text{for a.e.}\;\;x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

So, the above setting, we have as
h
tends to
0
(
\varepsilon
being fixed)

\displaystyle{1\over h}({\bf w}_{h}-\text{\bf id})

\displaystyle\to{\bf u}\quad

\displaystyle\text{strongly in $L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3}$},

(7.10)

\displaystyle{1\over 2h}\left((\nabla{\bf w}_{h})^{T}(\nabla{\bf w}_{h})-{\bf I}_{3}\right)

\displaystyle\to e({\bf u}),\quad

\displaystyle\text{strongly in $L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{3\times 3}$},

which gives

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}{1\over h^{2}}{{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}({\bf w}_{h})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\left({1\over h^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\bf Q}\left({x\over\varepsilon},{1\over 2}\big((\nabla{\bf w}_{h})^{T}(\nabla{\bf w}_{h})-{\bf I}_{3}\big)\right)\,dx-{1\over h}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot({\bf w}_{h}-\text{\bf id})\,dx\right)

(7.11)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\bf Q}\left({x\over\varepsilon},e({\bf u})\right)dx-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot{\bf u}\,dx.

Note that

m_{\varepsilon,h}\leq{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}({\bf w}_{h}).

Then, from the above inequality along with convergence (
7.10
)-(
7.11
), we obtain

\limsup_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}}\leq\lim_{h\to 0}{1\over h^{2}}{\bf J}_{\varepsilon,h}({\bf w}_{h})={\bf J}^{Lin}_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}),\quad\forall\;{\bf u}\in{\bf U}^{*}_{\varepsilon}.

This completes the proof of Step 2.

Combining steps 1, 2 and 3, we obtain

{\bf J}^{Lin}_{\varepsilon}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon})={m_{\varepsilon}}\leq\liminf_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}}\leq\limsup_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}}\leq{\bf J}_{\varepsilon}^{Lin}({\bf u}_{\varepsilon}),

which gives the required result.
∎

Theorem 4

We have

m_{L}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{h\to 0}{m_{\varepsilon,h}\over h^{2}\varepsilon^{5}}=\min_{({\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathfrak{v}})\in{\mathbb{D}}}{\bf J}({\mathcal{V}},\widehat{\mathfrak{v}})={\bf J}({\mathcal{U}},\widehat{\mathfrak{u}}),

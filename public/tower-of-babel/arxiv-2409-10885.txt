Noetherianity of Diagram Algebras
Anthony Muljat and Khoa Ta

Abstract
. In this short paper, we establish the local Noetherian property for the linear categories of Brauer, partition algebras, and other related categories of diagram algebras with no restrictions on their various parameters.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we prove the local Noetherian property for the linear categories of Brauer and partition algebras as introduced by Patzt in
[
3
]
. Additionally, we extend our results to two related categories of diagram algebras: the Rook-Brauer and Rook algebras. Our results are valid without any restrictions on the various parameters of these algebras.

For a commutative Noetherian ring
R
, the
partition algebra

P_{n}=P_{n}(R,\delta)
with parameter
\delta\in R
is a free
R
-module over the basis of all partitions of the union of the sets
[-n]=\{-n,\ldots,-1\}
and
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Each basis element of
P_{n}
may be visualized by a diagram
\alpha
with two vertical columns of
n
nodes each, with the nodes on the left representing
-n
through
-1
and the nodes on the right representing
1
through
n
, with paths connecting certain nodes. The connected components of
\alpha
indicate the blocks of the corresponding partition; we identify any diagrams whose components contain the same nodes.

Figure 1.
Visualization of the partition
\{\{-5,5,3\},\{-4,-2,-1,4\},\{-3\},\{1,2\}\}
.

For partitions
p
and
q
, we define the product
pq
in the following way. First we conjoin the diagrams
p
and
q
by identifying each node
k
on the right of
p
with the node
-k
on the left of
q
, thus forming a diagram with
3
columns of
n
nodes each. We then form a new partition
s
of
[-n]\cup[n]
in which two elements belong to the same block if and only if the corresponding nodes in the left or right column of the conjoined diagram are connected. We define
pq=\delta^{r}\cdot s
where
r
is the number of connected components in the conjoined diagram that only contain nodes in the middle column.

As an example, below we have a multiplication of two basis diagrams in
P_{5}(R,\delta)
. Note that there is a factor of
\delta
in the result to indicate a single connected component (shown in red) that only contains nodes in the middle column.

Figure 2.
Multiplication in
P_{5}(R,\delta)
.

The
Brauer algebra

{\rm Br}_{n}={\rm Br}_{n}(R,\delta)
is the free subalgebra of
P_{n}(R,\delta)
generated by partitions that are perfect matchings; that is, the blocks in each partition have size 2. Thus, each basis element is represented by a diagram in which each node is connected to exactly one other node.

Two other diagram algebras related to the partition algebra are the Rook-Brauer algebra and the Rook algebra. For a commutative Noetherian ring
R
, the
Rook-Brauer algebra

{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}={\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}(R,\delta,\epsilon)
with parameters
\delta,\epsilon\in R
is a free
R
-module with basis consisting of partitions of
[-n]\cup[n]
whose blocks have size
\leq 2
. Thus, each basis element is represented by a diagram in which each node is connected to at most one other node. The product of two basis diagrams
p,q\in{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
is defined by
pq=\delta^{c}\epsilon^{d}s
where
s
is the partition obtained as described above, with
c
factors of
\delta
for
c
loops and
d
factors of
\epsilon
for
d
contractible components in the conjoined diagram that only contain nodes in the middle column (see example below).

Figure 3.
Multiplication in
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{5}(\delta,\epsilon)
.

Here, the final diagram is multiplied by a factor of
\delta
for the red loop and a factor of
\epsilon
for the isolated node in the middle.

The
Rook algebra

{\mathscr{R}}_{n}={\mathscr{R}}_{n}(R,\epsilon)
is the free subalgebra of
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
generated by diagrams without any left-to-left or right-to-right edges (i.e. the
invertible
diagrams of
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
). Note that the parameter
\delta
can be removed as multiplication of diagrams with no left-to-left or right-to-right edges cannot yield any loops in the middle.

2.
Main Result

We now introduce the background necessary to discuss our main results. In the following, we fix a commutative Noetherian ring
R
and parameters
\delta,\epsilon\in R
.

Following Patzt’s
[
3
]
convention, given a nonnegative integer
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we will denote by
A_{n}
any one of
P_{n}
,
{\rm Br}_{n}
,
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
, or
{\mathscr{R}}_{n}
. For each
A_{n}
, there is a canonical choice of a “trivial”
A_{n}
-module
R
on which all invertible diagrams act trivially and which is annihilated by all other diagrams. We denote by
A_{n-m}
the subalgebra of
A_{n}
generated by diagrams with horizontal connections
\{-k,k\}
for all
1\leq k\leq m
.

Denote by
\{A_{n}\}
any one of the sequences
\{P_{n}\}
,
\{{\rm Br}_{n}\}
,
\{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\}
, or
\{{\mathscr{R}}_{n}\}
. We define a category
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
enriched over the category of
R
-modules by the following data:

•

The objects of
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
are the nonnegative integers
{\mathbb{N}}
.

•

If
m\leq n
, we define
\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(m,n):=A_{n}\otimes_{A_{n-m}}R
; otherwise
\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(m,n):=0
is the trivial
R
-module. Note that
\End_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(n)\cong A_{n}
.

•

By
[
3
]
*Thm. 2.1, there is a surjection

\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(m,n)\otimes\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(l,m)\to\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(l,n)\qquad(l\leq m\leq n)

which provides the composition
l\to m\to n
for
l\leq m\leq n
; otherwise the composition is
0
.

We thereby obtain categories
{\mathscr{C}}_{P}
,
{\mathscr{C}}_{{\rm Br}}
,
{\mathscr{C}}_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}}
, and
{\mathscr{C}}_{{\mathscr{R}}}
enriched over the category of
R
-modules corresponding to the sequences
\{P_{n}\}
,
\{{\rm Br}_{n}\}
,
\{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\}
, and
\{{\mathscr{R}}_{n}\}
respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

A
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module

V
is a functor from the category
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
to the category of
R
-modules. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we denote the
R
-module
V(n)
by
V_{n}
.

A
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module map
is a natural transformation of
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-modules. Given
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-modules
V
to
W
, if
W_{n}\subseteq V_{n}
for each
n
and the map
W\to V
induced by the inclusions
W_{n}\hookrightarrow V_{n}
is a
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module map, then
W
is a
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-submodule
of
V
. We say that
V
is
finitely generated
if there exists a finite set
S\subseteq\underset{n}{\sqcup}~V_{n}
such that the smallest
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-submodule of
V
containing
S
is
V
itself.

Definition 2.2
.

A
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module
V
is
Noetherian
if every
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-submodule of
V
is finitely generated. We say
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
is
locally Noetherian
over
R
if every finitely generated
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module is Noetherian.

We can now state our main result.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
A=P
,
{\rm Br}
,
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}
, or
{\mathscr{R}}
, the category
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
is locally Noetherian.

The local Noetherian property for the categories of partition, Brauer, and Temperley-Lieb algebras, with the parameter
\delta
chosen such that each algebra is semi-simple, was previously established in
[
2
]
. Our result extends this by removing restrictions on various parameters, thereby providing a more general statement.

The crux of the main argument is to establish the existence of a functor from the category
{\rm FI}
to the category
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
when
A=P
,
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}
, or
{\mathscr{R}}
. This implies that any
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module, when pulled back, also becomes an
{\rm FI}
-module. Given that the local Noetherianity property is already known for the category
{\rm FI}

[
1
]
*Thm. A, this result consequently extends the local Noetherianity property to the category
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
. Unfortunately, this argument does not hold for the Temperley-Lieb algebra, as there is no nontrivial functor from the category
{\rm FI}
to the category of Temperley-Lieb algebras. Therefore, we leave the reader with an open question.

Question:
For any parameter
\delta
, is the category of Temperley-Lieb algebras
{\mathscr{C}}_{TL(\delta)}
locally Noetherian?

3.
Proof of Main Result

It is known
[
3
]
*Prop. 2.3 that
\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{{\rm Br}}}(m,n)
is a free
R
-module with a basis which may be represented by diagrams with
n
nodes on the left,
m
nodes on the right and an “
(n-m)
-blob” which is connected to exactly
n-m
nodes, such that every node is connected to exactly one other node or the blob. Similarly, by
[
3
]
*Prop. 2.5,
\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m,n)
is a free
R
-module with basis given by the set of all partitions of
[-n]\cup[m]
with
n-m
“marked” blocks.

We now prove an analogous result for categories of Rook and Rook-Brauer algebras.

Proposition 3.1
.

\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}}}(m,n)
is a free
R
-module with a basis consisting of diagrams with
n
nodes on the left,
m
nodes on the right and an “
(n-m)
-blob” which is connected to exactly
n-m
nodes, such that every node is connected to at most one other node or the blob.

Proof.

Let
J\subset{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
be the free
R
-submodule generated by diagrams which have either an isolated node
k
with
m+1\leq k\leq n
or a right-to-right connection
\{i,j\}
with
m+1\leq i,j\leq n
. Note that
J
is closed under right multiplication by
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}
.

We claim that the image of the map
J\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R\to{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R
induced by the inclusion
J\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
is
0
. To prove this, we will show that every basis diagram
\alpha\in J
may be written
\alpha=\beta\gamma
, where
\beta
and
\gamma
are basis diagrams of
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
and
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}
respectively and
\gamma
is non-invertible. Thus we will have

\alpha\otimes 1\mapsto\beta\gamma\otimes 1=\beta\otimes\gamma\cdot 1=\beta\otimes 0=0.

Case 1.
Assume
\alpha\in J
has an isolated node
k
between
m+1
and
n
. Since
\alpha
must have an even number of isolated nodes, we can find another isolated node either
k^{\prime}
on the left or right of
\alpha
. Let
\beta\in{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
be the diagram with the connection
\{k,k^{\prime}\}
and all other connections are the same as in
\alpha
. Let
\gamma\in{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}
be the diagram with a pair of isolated nodes on opposite sides at
\{k\}
,
\{-k\}
with all other connections left-to-right
\{-l,l\}
. Then
\gamma
is non-invertible and
\alpha=\beta\gamma
.

Case 2.
Now assume
\alpha
has no isolated nodes between
m+1
and
n
. This implies that
\alpha
has a right-to-right connection
\{i,j\}
where
m+1\leq i,j\leq n
. It follows that
\alpha
has either a pair of isolated nodes on the left hand side or a left-to-left connection. Denote this pair or connection by
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
, let
\beta\in{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
be the diagram with connections
\{i,i^{\prime}\}
and
\{j,j^{\prime}\}
with all other connections the same as in
\alpha
. If
i^{\prime}
and
j^{\prime}
are left-to-left connected in
\alpha
, let
\gamma\in{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}
be the diagram with connections
\{-i,-j\}
,
\{i,j\}
, and all other connections left-to-right
\{-l,l\}
. If
i^{\prime}
and
j^{\prime}
are isolated nodes in
\alpha
, let
\gamma\in{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}
be the diagram with isolated nodes at
\{-i\}
,
\{-j\}
and the connection
\{i,j\}
with all other connections left-to-right
\{-l,l\}
. In either case
\gamma
is non-invertible and
\alpha=\beta\gamma
. This completes the proof of the claim.

The inclusion
J\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
and projection
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J
fit into a short exact sequence

0\to J\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J\to 0.

Applying
-\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R
yields the exact sequence

J\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R\to{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R\to{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R\to 0.

Since
J\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R\to{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}
is
0
, it follows that

{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R\cong{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R.

(3.2)

Note that the images in
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J
of diagrams in
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}
such that every node
i
with
m\leq i\leq n
is connected to exactly one other node
j
with
j\leq m
form a basis of
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J
.

Now let
I\subset{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}
be the free
R
-submodule generated by all noninvertible diagrams in
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}
. Then
I
annihilates
R
and
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J
. It follows that the action of
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}
on
R
and
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}/J
factors through
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}/I\cong RS_{n-m}
, where
S_{n-m}
is the subgroup of
S_{n}
which acts trivially on
\{1,\ldots,m\}
and acts by permutation on
\{m+1,\ldots,n\}
. Hence

{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R\cong{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J\otimes_{RS_{n-m}}R.

(3.3)

Together, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply

{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n-m}}R\cong{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J\otimes_{RS_{n-m}}R.

(3.4)

Since
S_{n-m}
permutes the basis of
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J
, it follows that
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}_{n}/J\otimes_{RS_{n-m}}R
is a free
R
-module with the desired basis. The result then follows from (
3.4
).
∎

The analogous result for
\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{{\mathscr{R}}}}(m,n)
may be obtained by restricting the diagrams to those without left-to-left or right-to-right connections. We adjust the proof slightly to account for this restriction.

Proposition 3.5
.

\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{{\mathscr{R}}}}(m,n)
is a free
R
-module with a basis consisting of diagrams with
n
nodes on the left,
m
nodes on the right and an “
(n-m)
-blob” which is connected to exactly
n-m
nodes, such that every node is connected to at most one other node or the blob, and all connections are left-to-right.

Proof.

Let
J\subset{\mathscr{R}}_{n}
be the free
R
-submodule generated by diagrams which have at least one isolated node
k
with
m+1\leq k\leq n
. As in Proposition
3.1
, we claim that the image of
J\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}_{n-m}}R\to{\mathscr{R}}_{n}\otimes_{{\mathscr{R}}_{n-m}}R
is
0
. Note that diagrams in Rook algebras must have the same number of isolated nodes on the left as on the right. Let
\alpha
be any diagram in
J
. To each isolated node
k
between
m+1
and
n
, we may associate a unique isolated node
k^{\prime}<0
. Let
\beta\in{\mathscr{R}}_{n}
be the diagram where the pairs
(k,k^{\prime})
are connected and all other nodes are connected as they are in
\alpha
. Let
\gamma\in{\mathscr{R}}_{n-m}
be the diagram where all the
k
and
-k
nodes are isolated and all other nodes are connected horizontally. Then
\gamma
is noninvertible and
\alpha=\beta\gamma
. The rest of the proof is the same, mutatis mutandis, as the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Recall the category
{\rm FI}
whose objects are the nonnegative integers and
\Hom_{\rm FI}(m,n)
consists of injective maps
[m]\to[n]
where
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
; note that when
m=n
, we have
\Hom_{\rm FI}(m,n)=S_{n}
, the symmetric group on
n
objects. An
{\rm FI}
-module
is a functor from the category
{\rm FI}
to the category of
R
-modules.

For
A={\rm Br}
,
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}
, or
{\mathscr{R}}
, define a functor
F:{\rm FI}\to{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
by the following data:

•

F(n)=n
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

•

For all
\alpha\in\Hom_{\rm FI}(m,n)
, let
F(\alpha)\in\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(m,n)
be the basis diagram with connections
\{-\alpha(i),i\}
for all
i\in[m]
, and all
n-m
remaining nodes on the left are connected to the
(n-m)
-blob.

are connected to the

When
A=P
, define the functor
G:{\rm FI}\to{\mathscr{C}}_{P}
by the following data:

•

G([n])=[n]
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

•

For all
\alpha\in\Hom_{\rm FI}(m,n)
, let
F(\alpha)\in\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(m,n)
be the basis diagram with connections
\{-\alpha(i),i\}
for each
i\in[m]
and all the remaining
n-m
nodes on the left constitute the

marked blocks.

G(f)\in\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(3,5)

With these functors, any
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module may be considered as an
{\rm FI}
-module by pulling back along either
F
or
G
.

For all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define the
free
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module

M_{A}(m)
by the following data:

•

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
M_{A}(m)_{n}
is the free
R
-module generated on
\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(m,n)
.

•

The action of
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-morphisms is given by postcomposition; that is, for all
\alpha\in\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(n,p)
, the induced map
M_{A}(m)(\alpha):M_{A}(m)_{n}\to M_{A}(m)_{p}
is defined by
\beta\mapsto\alpha\beta
for all
\beta\in\Hom_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(m,n)
.

Our objective now is to demonstrate that the free
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module is finitely generated as an
{\rm FI}
-module. We start with the case when
A=P
, for which we establish a quick lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
j\geq 5m
, then any basis diagram
\alpha\in M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{j}
has at least
m+1
marked blocks consisting of a single node.

Proof.

Let
b
be the number of marked blocks in
\alpha
with a single node. For contradiction, assume
b\leq m
. From
[
3
]
*Prop. 2.5,
\alpha
contains
j-m
marked blocks. So there are another
j-m-b
marked blocks in
\alpha
, each of which has at least two nodes. Together, these blocks must contain at least
2(j-m-b)
nodes, out of the
j+m-b
nodes not already in a block. However, since
j\geq 5m
and
0\leq b\leq m
, it follows that
j>3m+b
, or equivalently,
2(j-m-b)>j+m-b
. So there are not enough nodes to fill all the blocks, a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

For
m\geq 0
,
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)
is finitely generated as an
{\rm FI}
-module.

Proof.

The result is immediate for
m=0
; suppose
m\geq 1
. We claim that
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)
is generated as an
{\rm FI}
-module by basis diagrams of
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{k}
where
0\leq k\leq 5m
.

First, we show that
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{5m+1}
is generated by basis diagrams of
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{5m}
. By Lemma
3.6
, any basis diagram
\alpha\in M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{5m+1}
has at least
m+1
marked blocks of size 1. Since
\alpha
has only
m
nodes on the right, there must be a marked block of size 1 on the left of
\alpha
.

We can find
\sigma\in S_{5m+1}
such that
\sigma\alpha
has the singe-node marked block on the top left. Let
\iota\in\Hom_{{\rm FI}}(5m,5m+1)
be the inclusion map
[5m]\hookrightarrow[5m+1]
and
\overline{\alpha}
be the diagram obtained from
\sigma\alpha
by removing the marked block on the top left (see below). Note that
\overline{\alpha}\in M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{5m}
and
\sigma\alpha=G(\iota)\overline{\alpha}
, or equivalently
\alpha=\sigma^{-1}G(\iota)\overline{\alpha}
.

\sigma\alpha
to
\overline{\alpha}

Since
\sigma^{-1}\in S_{5m+1}
, we have

and
\alpha=G(\sigma^{-1}\iota)\overline{\alpha}
with
\sigma^{-1}\iota\in\Hom_{{\rm FI}}(5m,5m+1)
. This implies that
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{5m+1}
is generated by basis diagrams of
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{5m}
.

A routine induction now shows that
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)
is generated as an
{\rm FI}
-module by basis diagrams of
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)_{k}

. Hence,
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{P}}(m)
is finitely generated as an

-module.

Lemma 3.8
.

For

,
{\mathscr{R}}
, or

, if

, any morphism of

By

*Prop. 2.3 and Propositions

Proposition 3.9
.

For
A={\rm Br}
,
{\mathscr{R}}{\rm Br}
, or
{\mathscr{R}}
, the free
{\mathscr{C}}_{A}
-module
M_{{\mathscr{C}}_{A}}(m)
is finitely generated as an
{\rm FI}
-module for all
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

The case
m=0
is straightforward; assume
m\geq 1
. We claim that

1.
Introduction

The study on character sums and related topics has received a lot of attention in number theory as they have many important applications. To better understand the behaviors of the character sums, one may often model them using random multiplicative functions. For example, one may simulate the behaviour of any Dirichlet character using a Steinhaus random multiplicative function
f(n)
defined by
f(n)=\prod_{p^{a}||n}f(p)^{a}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
(f(p))_{p\;\text{prime}}
is a sequence of independent random variables, each distributed uniformly on the complex unit circle. Here and throughout the paper, we reserve the letter
p
for a prime number.

In
[
6
]
, A. J. Harper determined the order of magnitude of the low moments of sums of Steinhaus random multiplicative functions. Using the ideas in
[
6
]
, Harper was further able to show in
[
7
, Theorem 3]
that for large primes
q
, uniformly for any
1\leq x\leq q
, any
0\leq k\leq 1
, and any multiplicative function
u(n)
that has absolute value
1
on primes and absolute value at most
1
on prime powers,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{q}}\Big|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)u(n)\Big|^{2k}\ll\Biggl(\frac{x}{1+(1-k)\sqrt{\log\log(10L)}}\Biggr)^{k},

where
\varphi(q)
is the Euler totient function and we set
L=L_{q}=\min\{x,q/x\}
throughout the paper.

Setting
u(n)=1
in (
1.1
) recovers
[
7
, Theorem 1]
, which asserts that under the same notation used in (
1.1
),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{q}}\Big|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\Big|^{2k}\ll\Biggl(\frac{x}{1+(1-k)\sqrt{\log\log(10L)}}\Biggr)^{k},

The above establishes upper bounds for low moments of Dirichlet character sums and it is noted in
[
7
]
that these bounds are optimal conjecturally. Note also that the special case
k=1/2
illustrates that when both
x
and
q/x
tend to infinity with
q
,

(1.3)

\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{q}}\Big|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\Big|=o(\sqrt{x}).

The above implies that the sums
\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)
typically exhibit better than square-root cancellation.

On the other hand, one may also study high moments of Dirichlet character sums. In
[
13
]
, B. Szabó proved that under the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) that for a fixed real number
k>2
and a large integer
q
, we have for
2\leq x\leq q
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\in X_{q}^{*}}\bigg|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\bigg|^{2k}\ll_{k}x^{k}\left(\min\left(\log x,\log\frac{2q}{x}\right)\right)^{(k-1)^{2}},

where
X_{q}^{*}
denotes the set of primitive Dirichlet characters modulo
q
. Here we remark that the proofs of (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) are based on different approaches. The former builds on many strategies introduced in
[
6
]
to study on low moments of random multiplicative functions while the latter relies on results concerning sharp upper bounds on shifted moments of Dirichlet
L
-function on the critical line. We also note that it was shown in
[
14
, Theorem 1]
that the bounds given in (
1.4
) are optimal under GRH for primes
q
.

Instead of Dirichlet character sums, one may consider other types of sums. For example, the one given on the left-hand side of (
1.1
) involving with a general multiplicative function
u(n)
. When
u(n)
equals the Fourier coefficient
\lambda(n)
of a fixed holomorphic Hecke eigenform
f
of weight
\kappa\equiv 0\pmod{4}
for the full modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, upper and lower bounds for high moments (
k>2
) of the left-hand side expression in (
1.1
) were obtained in
[
4
,
5
]
. Here we recall that the Fourier expansion of
f
at infinity is given by

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda(n)n^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}e(nz),\quad\mbox{where}\quad e(z)=\exp(2\pi iz).

It is the aim of this paper is to obtain upper bounds for low moments of the left-hand side expression in (
1.1
) with
u(n)=\lambda(n)
there. Our result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

With the notation as above. Let
q
be a large prime number. We have for
1\leq x\leq q
and any real
0\leq k\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{q}}\Big|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Big|^{2k}\ll\Biggl(\frac{x}{1+(1-k)\sqrt{\log\log(10L)}}\Biggr)^{k}.

We note that similar to (
1.3
), Theorem
1.1
implies that for any fixed
0<k<1
and any
x
such that
x
and
q/x
both tend to infinity with
q
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\chi\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{q}}\Big|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Big|^{2k}=o(\sqrt{x}),

so that the sums
\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\lambda(n)
also typically exhibit better than square-root cancellation.

Note that
\lambda(n)
does not satisfy the condition imposed for
u(n)
in the establishment of (
1.1
), hence the assertion of Theorem (
1.1
) does not follow (
1.1
). Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is nevertheless achieved by extending the treatments in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3]
.

2.1.
Cusp form
L
-functions

We reserve the letter
p
for a prime number throughout in this paper. Recall that
f
is a fixed holomorphic Hecke eigenform
f
of weight
\kappa\equiv 0\pmod{4}
for the full modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The associated modular
L
-function
L(s,f)
for
\Re(s)>1
is then defined as

\displaystyle L(s,f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\left(1+\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\frac{\lambda^{i}(p)}{p^{is}}\right)=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{p}}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{p}}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

It follows that
\lambda(n)
is multiplicative that satisfies for any prime
p
and any non-negative integer
m
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{m}(p)=\sum^{m}_{j=0}\alpha^{m-j}_{p}\beta^{j}_{p}.

Moreover, by Deligne’s proof
[
1
]
of the Weil conjecture, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\alpha_{p}|=|\beta_{p}|=1,\quad\alpha_{p}\beta_{p}=1.

We then deduce that
\lambda(n)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\lambda(1)=1
and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\lambda(n)|\leq d(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon},\end{split}

where
d(n)
is the number of positive divisors
n
and the last estimation above follows from
[
9
, Theorem 2.11]
. We also note that
|\lambda(n)|^{2}=\lambda^{2}(n)\geq 0
as
\lambda(n)
is real.

The symmetric square
L
-function
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
of
f
is defined for
\Re(s)>1
by
(see
[
8
, p. 137]
and
[
8
, (25.73)]
)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=&\prod_{p}(1-\alpha^{2}_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}(1-\beta^{2}_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}\\
=&\zeta(2s)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda(n^{2})}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\lambda(p^{2})}{p^{s}}+\frac{\lambda(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}}\right)^{-1}.\end{split}

A result of G. Shimura
[
12
]
implies that
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
has no pole at
s=1
. Furthermore, the corresponding completed symmetric square
L
-function

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=

\displaystyle\pi^{-3s/2}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+1}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+\kappa-1}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+\kappa}{2}\Big)L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)

is entire and satisfies the functional equation
\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=\Lambda(1-s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
.

We derive from (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{p}+\beta_{p}=&\lambda(p),\\
\alpha^{2}_{p}+\beta^{2}_{p}=&\lambda^{2}(p)-2=\lambda(p^{2})-1.\end{split}

Thus, it follows from the above that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda^{2}(p)=\lambda(p^{2})+1.\end{split}

We also note that R. A. Rankin
[
10
]
and A. Selberg
[
11
]
showed independently that for
x\geq 1
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n\leq x}\lambda^{2}(n)=\frac{L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)}{\zeta(2)}x+O(x^{3/5}).\end{split}

We end this section by including a result on certain sums over primes.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
x\geq 2
. We have, for some constant

,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}=

\displaystyle\log\log x+b_{1}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big),\;\mbox{and}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\lambda^{2}(p)}{p}=

\displaystyle\log\log x+b_{2}+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big).

Proof.

The expression in (
2.8
) can be found in part (d) of
[
9
, Theorem 2.7]
and the expression in (
2.9
) follows from
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
.

=&\exp\Big(\sum_{x<p\leq y}\frac{1+iu\cos(t\log p)-u^{2}/4}{p^{1+2\sigma}}+T(u)\Big),\end{split}

where
T(u)=T_{x,y,\sigma,t}(u)
satisfies
|T(u)|\ll\frac{1+|u|^{3}}{\sqrt{x}\log x}
for any
u
, and
|T^{\prime}(u)|\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}\log x}
for
|u|\leq 1
.

Proof.

The expression given in (
2.15
) is established in
[
5
, Lemma 2.7]
. The proof of (
2.16
) is similar and follows by an adaption of the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 1]
. We set

R_{p}(t):=-\Re\log(1-\frac{\alpha_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma+it}})-\Re\log(1-\frac{\beta_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma+it}})

to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big|1-\frac{\alpha_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma}}\Big|^{-2}\Big|1-\frac{\beta_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma}}\Big|^{-2}\Big|1-\frac{\alpha_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma+it}}\Big|^{-iu}\Big|1-\frac{\beta_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma+it}}\Big|^{-iu}\\
=&\exp\Big(2R_{p}(0)+iuR_{p}(t)\Big)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2R_{p}(0)+iuR_{p}(t))^{j}}{j!}.\end{split}

By the Taylor expansion, (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), we see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&R_{p}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha^{j}_{p}+\beta^{j}_{p})\Re(h(p)p^{-it})^{j}}{jp^{j(1/2+\sigma)}}=\frac{\lambda(p)\Re h(p)p^{-it}}{p^{1/2+\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{p^{1+2\sigma}}).\end{split}

It follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}R_{p}(t)^{2}=&\mathbb{E}\frac{(\lambda(p)\Re h(p)p^{-it})^{2}}{p^{1+2\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3/2+3\sigma}})=\frac{\lambda^{2}(p)}{2p^{1+2\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3/2+3\sigma}}),\\
\mathbb{E}R_{p}(0)R_{p}(t)=&\mathbb{E}\frac{(\lambda(p)\Re h(p))(\lambda(p)\Re h(p)p^{-it})}{p^{1+2\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3/2+3\sigma}})=\frac{\lambda^{2}(p)\cos(t\log p)}{2p^{1+2\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3/2+3\sigma}}).\end{split}

Note also that by (
2.2
), we have
|R_{p}(t)^{j}|\leq(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{mp^{m(1/2+\sigma)}})^{j}=\frac{2^{j}}{(p^{1/2+\sigma}-1)^{j}}
for
j\geq 3
.

We then proceed as in the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 1]
to see that the expression in (
2.16
) is valid. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Note that as a special case of (

(2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\prod_{x<p\leq y}\Big|1-\frac{\alpha_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma}}\Big|^{-2}\Big|1-\frac{\beta_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma}}\Big|^{-2}=&\exp\Big(\sum_{x<p\leq y}\frac{\lambda^{2}(p)}{p^{1+2\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}\log x})\Big),\\
\mathbb{E}\prod_{x<p\leq y}\Big|1-\frac{\alpha_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma}}\Big|^{2}\Big|1-\frac{\beta_{p}h(p)}{p^{1/2+\sigma}}\Big|^{2}=&\exp\Big(\sum_{x<p\leq y}\frac{\lambda^{2}(p)}{p^{1+2\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}\log x})\Big).\end{split}

The following lemma is taken from

Lemma 2.10

Let

\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{\log^{1.01}P}\sum_{|j|\leq(\log^{1.01}P)/2}|F_{P,f}^{\text{rand}}(1/2+i\frac{j}{\log^{1.01}P})|^{2})^{k}\ll\Biggl(\frac{\log P}{1+(1-q)\sqrt{\log\log P}}\Biggr)^{k}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

3.1.
Initial Treatments

We may restrict to the range
2/3\leq k\leq 1
throughout the proof, since if
k
is smaller we can apply Hölder’s inequality to bound
\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\lambda(n)|^{2k}
from above by
(\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\lambda(n)|^{4/3})^{3k/2}
, and then use the
k=2/3
case. We may assume
L=\min\{x,q/x\}
is large throughout the proof for otherwise the assertion of the Theorem holds trivially. We now set a parameter
P
to be the largest number below
\exp(\log^{1/6}L)
such that
\log^{0.01}P
is an integer. It follows that
\log P\asymp\log^{1/6}L
and
\log\log P\asymp\log\log L
, so that in order to establish Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that

(3.1)

\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}|\sum_{n\leq x}\chi(n)\lambda(n)|^{2k}\ll\Biggl(\frac{x}{1+(1-k)\sqrt{\log\log P}}\Biggr)^{k}.

Set
M:=2\log^{1.02}P
. We also set for each integer
m
satisfying
|m|\leq M
and each character
\chi
mod
q
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle S_{m,f}(\chi):=\Re\sum_{p\leq P}(\frac{\chi(p)\lambda(p)}{p^{1/2+im/\log^{1.01}P}}+\frac{\chi(p)^{2}(\lambda(p^{2})-\lambda(p)^{2}/2}{p^{1+2im/\log^{1.01}P}}).

Let
P(n)
denote the largest prime factor of
n
. We apply Hölder’s inequality and orthogonality of Dirichlet characters to see that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}|\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)|^{2k}\leq\Biggl(\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}|\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}=\Biggl(\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\lambda(n)^{2}\Biggr)^{k}.

We now follow the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 7.6]
to apply Rankin’s trick to see that for some
\sigma>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\lambda(n)^{2}\leq\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\lambda(n)^{2}(\frac{x}{n})^{\sigma}\leq x^{\sigma}\sum_{P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\frac{\lambda(n)^{2}}{n^{\sigma}}.

Note that by (
2.3
), we see that when
\sigma>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\frac{\lambda(n)^{2}}{n^{\sigma}}=\prod_{p\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}(1+\frac{\lambda(p)^{2}}{p^{\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma}}))=\prod_{p\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}(1-\frac{\lambda(p)^{2}}{p^{\sigma}})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{p\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}(1+O(\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma}})).

We now set
y=x^{1/\log\log x}
and
\sigma=1-3/\log y
to see that the last product above is convergent. Moreover, when
x
is large enough, we have
2\sigma>1
, so that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\frac{\lambda(n)^{2}}{n^{\sigma}}\ll&\prod_{p\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}(1-\frac{\lambda(p)^{2}}{p^{\sigma}})^{-1}=\exp\big(\sum_{p\leq y}-\log(1-\frac{\lambda(p)^{2}}{p^{\sigma}})\big)=\exp\big(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\lambda(p)^{2}}{p^{\sigma}}+O(\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma}})\big)\\
\ll&\exp\big(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\lambda(p)^{2}}{p^{\sigma}}\big).\end{split}

Note that when
1-4/\log y\leq\sigma\leq 1
and
u\leq y
, we have
u^{1-\sigma}=\exp((1-\sigma)\log u)=1+O((1-\sigma)\log u)
. It follows from this, (
2.9
) and partial summation that

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\lambda(p)^{2}}{p^{\sigma}}=

\displaystyle\int^{y}_{1}u^{1-\sigma}d(\log\log u+O(1))=\int^{y}_{1}u^{1-\sigma}d\log\log u+O(1)=\int^{y}_{1}(1+O((1-\sigma)\log u))d\log\log u+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\log y+O(1).

We apply the above and (
3.4
) to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\frac{\lambda(n)^{2}}{n^{\sigma}}\ll&\log y.\end{split}

We deduce from the above and (
2.6
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\lambda(n)^{2}\ll x^{\sigma}\log y\ll\frac{x}{\log\log x}.

We substitute the above estimation into (
3.3
) to see that

\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}|\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)\leq x^{1/\log\log x}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)|^{2k}\ll(\frac{x}{\log\log x})^{k},

which gives a negligible contribution in Theorem
1.1
.

It therefore suffices to bound
\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}|\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)|^{2k}
. Using the partition of unity
g_{j}
from Lemma
2.4
, we may rewrite
\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}|\sum_{n\leq x,P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)|^{2k}
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}(\sum_{j=-N}^{N+1}g_{j}(S_{i,f}(\chi)))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(\chi))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma(\textbf{j})\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j}}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2k},

where we set
\sigma(\textbf{j}):=\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(\chi))
for any
(2M+1)
-vector
j
from the outer sum above, and we define
\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j}}W:=\sigma(\textbf{j})^{-1}\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}W\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(\chi))
for all functions
W(\chi)
. Note that for the constant function
1
, we have
\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j}}1=1
for all choices of the vector
j
. We then apply Hölder’s inequality to
\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j}}
to see that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2k}\leq\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j}}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}.\end{split}

3.2.
Transition to random variables

We apply (
2.2
), (
2.5
) to the complex numbers
a_{m}(p)=\lambda(p)p^{-im/\log^{1.01}P}
,
a_{m}(p^{2})=(\lambda(p^{2})-\lambda^{2}(p)/2)p^{-2im/\log^{1.01}P}
to see that we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|a_{m}(p)|,|a_{m}(p^{2})|\leq 3.\end{split}

Moreover, we take
c(n)=\lambda(n)
to see that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}\tilde{d}(n)|\lambda(n)|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x\\
p\mid d\Rightarrow p\leq P\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(n).

We now consider the sum
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/2<n\leq x\\
d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(n)
to see that we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/2<n\leq x\\
d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(n)\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(n)\Phi(\frac{n}{x}),

where
\Phi
is a smooth, non-negative function compactly supported on
[1/4,3/2]
satisfying
\Phi(x)\leq 1
for all
x
and
\Phi(x)=1

for
x\in[1/2,1]
. The Mellin transform
{\widehat{\Phi}}(s)
of
\Phi
is defined for any complex number
s
by

{\widehat{\Phi}}(s)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(x)x^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}.

Note that integration by parts shows that
\widehat{\Phi}(s)
is a function satisfying the bound

(3.9)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}(s)\ll\min(1,|s|^{-1}(1+|s|)^{-E}),

for all
\Re(s)>0
, and integers
E>0
.

Now the Mellin inversion leads to

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(n)\Phi(\frac{n}{x})=&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(2)}(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
c|n\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda(n)^{2}}{n^{s}})\widehat{\Phi}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

We note that it is shown in the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 2.10]
that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
d|n\end{subarray}}\frac{|\lambda(n)|^{2}}{n^{s}}=&\frac{F(s)}{d^{s}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|d\\
p^{\nu_{p}}\|d\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda(p^{\nu_{p}+j})|^{2}}{p^{js}}\Big)\frac{\zeta_{p}(2s)}{\zeta_{p}(s)L_{p}(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)}.\end{split}

where
L_{p}
denotes the local factor at the prime
p
in the Euler product of
L
for any
L
-function and where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function,
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
defined in (
2.4
). Also, it is shown in
[
5
, (2.25)]
that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F(s)\zeta(2s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f).\end{split}

We now shift the contour of the integral in (
3.10
) to
\Re(s)=1/2+\varepsilon
to pick up a simple pole at
s=1
of
\zeta(s)
. By (
3.12
), we see that the corresponding residue equals

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{x}{d}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|d\\
p^{\nu_{p}}\|d\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda(p^{\nu_{p}+j})|^{2}}{p^{j}}\Big)\frac{\zeta_{p}(2)L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)}{\zeta_{p}(1)L_{p}(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\zeta(2)}=:g(d)x\frac{L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)}{\zeta(2)},\end{split}

where one checks that
g
is a multiplicative function satisfying
g(1)=1,g(p)=\lambda(p)^{2}/p+O(1/p^{2}),g(p^{i})=O(1/p^{i})
for
i\geq 2
.

Moreover, it follows from (
3.9
) and (
3.11
) that the integral on the new line is

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ll&\frac{x^{1/2+\varepsilon}}{d^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|d\\
p^{\nu_{p}}\|d\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda(p^{\nu_{p}+j})|^{2}}{p^{j(1/2+\varepsilon)}}\Big)\ll\frac{x}{d}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|d\\
p^{\nu_{p}}\|d\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda(p^{\nu_{p}+j})|^{2}}{p^{j(1/2+\varepsilon)}}\Big):=g_{1}(d)x,\end{split}

where the middle estimation above follows from the observation that
d\leq x
and one checks that
g_{1}
is a multiplicative function satisfying
g_{1}(1)=1,g_{1}(p)=\lambda(p)^{2}/p+O(1/p^{2}),g_{1}(p^{i})=O(1/p^{i})
for
i\geq 2
.

We then deduce from (
3.8
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/2<n\leq x\\
d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(n)\ll g(d)x+g_{1}(d)x.

We sum dyadically to derive from the above that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d|n\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(n)\ll g(d)x+g_{1}(d)x.

It follows from this, (
3.7
) and the estimation
1+x\leq e^{x}
for all real number
x
that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{n\leq x}\tilde{d}(n)|\lambda(n)|^{2}\\
=&x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x\\
p\mid d\Rightarrow p\leq P\end{subarray}}g(d)+x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x\\
p\mid d\Rightarrow p\leq P\end{subarray}}g_{1}(d)\\
\ll&x\prod_{p|P}(1+g(p)+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}}))+x\prod_{p|P}(1+g_{1}(p)+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}}))\\
\ll&x\exp\big(\sum_{p|P|}(|g(p)|+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}}))\big)+x\exp\big(\sum_{p|P|}(|g(p)|+O(\frac{1}{p^{2}}))\big)\\
\ll&x\exp\big(\sum_{p|P|}\frac{\lambda^{2}(p)}{p}\big)\\
\ll&x\log P,\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from (
2.9
).

In view of (
3.6
) and (
3.15
), we are in the position to apply Proposition
2.7
with
Y=2M+1
by setting
c_{0}=3
there to see that when
xP^{4000((2M+1)/\delta)^{2}\log(N\log P)}<q
, we have

\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j}}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}=\frac{1}{\sigma(\textbf{j})}\Biggl(\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}+O\left(\frac{x}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}}\right)\Biggr),

where
h
is a Steinhaus random multiplicative function and
S_{i,f}(h)
is defined similar to
S_{m,f}(\chi)
given in (
3.2
). It follows from this and (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}\chi(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2k}\\
\leq&\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\frac{1}{\sigma(\textbf{j})}\Biggl(\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}+O\left(\frac{x}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}}\right)\Biggr)\Biggr)^{k}\\
\ll&\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\frac{1}{\sigma(\textbf{j})}\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\\
&+\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\frac{1}{\sigma(\textbf{j})}O\left(\frac{x}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}}\right)\Biggr)^{k}.\end{split}

Note that
\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma(\textbf{j})=\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}(\sum_{j=-N}^{N+1}g_{j}(S_{i,f}(\chi)))=\mathbb{E}^{\text{char}}1=1
. We thus apply Hölder’s inequality to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\frac{1}{\sigma(\textbf{j})}O\left(\frac{x}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}}\right)\Biggr)^{k}\\
\ll&\Biggl(\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma(\textbf{j})\cdot\frac{1}{\sigma(\textbf{j})}\frac{x}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}}\Biggr)^{k}=\Biggl(x(\frac{(2N+2)}{(N\log P)^{(1/\delta)^{2}}})^{2M+1}\Biggr)^{k}.\end{split}

This gives a negligible contribution to (
3.1
).

We now define
\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j}):=\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))
for all
(2M+1)
-vectors
j
to see by Proposition
2.8
that
\sigma(\textbf{j})^{1-k}\ll\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})^{1-k}+(\frac{1}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}})^{1-k}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\frac{1}{\sigma(\textbf{j})}\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\\
\ll&\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\frac{1}{\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})}\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\\
&+(\frac{1}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}})^{1-k}\sum_{\textbf{j}}\Biggl(\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}.\end{split}

We now apply Hölder’s inequality again to the sum over
j
and recall that the

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\frac{1}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}})^{1-k}\sum_{\textbf{j}}\Biggl(\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\

\ll&(\frac{1}{(N\log P)^{(2M+1)(1/\delta)^{2}}})^{1-k}\cdot((2N+2)^{2M+1})^{1-k}\cdot\Biggl(\sum_{\textbf{j}}\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\\
=&\Biggl((\frac{(2N+2)}{(N\log P)^{(1/\delta)^{2}}})^{2M+1}\Biggr)^{1-k}\Biggl(\mathbb{E}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\\
=&\Biggl((\frac{(2N+2)}{(N\log P)^{(1/\delta)^{2}}})^{2M+1}\Biggr)^{1-k}\Biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(n)\Biggr)^{k}\\
\ll&\Biggl((\frac{(2N+2)}{(N\log P)^{(1/\delta)^{2}}})^{2M+1}\Biggr)^{1-k}x^{k},\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from (
2.7
). As the last expression above is
\ll e^{-(1-k)\log\log P}x^{k}
, its contribution to (

) is also negligible.

We conclude this section by noting that in order to establish Theorem
1.1
, it remains to show that

(3.16)

\sum_{-N\leq j(-M),...,j(0),...,j(M)\leq N+1}\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\ll\Biggl(\frac{x}{1+(1-k)\sqrt{\log\log P}}\Biggr)^{k}.

Here we define
\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}W:=\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})^{-1}\mathbb{E}W\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))
for all random variables
W
.

3.3.
Connecting to Euler products

As the various expressions
\prod_{i=-M}^{M}g_{j(i)}(S_{i,f}(h))
in the definition of
\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}
involve only the
h(p)
for
p\leq P
and the
h(p)
are independent random variables with mean zero, we expand the square and keep in mind that
P<x^{1/\log\log x}
to see that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x,\\
P(n)>x^{1/\log\log x}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\\
=&\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq x,\\
P(m)>x^{1/\log\log x},\\
p|m\Rightarrow p>P\end{subarray}}\lambda^{2}(m)\Biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x/m,\\
n\;\text{is}\;P\;\text{smooth}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)\Biggr|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\\
\ll&\Biggl(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/\log\log x}<m\leq x,\\
p|m\Rightarrow p>P\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda^{2}(m)X}{m}\int_{m}^{m(1+1/X)}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x/t,\\
n\;\text{is}\;P\;\text{smooth}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)|^{2}dt\Biggr)^{k}\\
&+\Biggl(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{1/\log\log x}<m\leq x,\\
p|m\Rightarrow p>P\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda^{2}(m)X}{m}\int_{m}^{m(1+1/X)}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x/t<n\leq x/m,\\
n\;\text{is}\;P\;\text{smooth}\end{subarray}}h(n)\lambda(n)|^{2}dt\Biggr)^{k}.\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows by replacing the condition that
P(m)>x^{1/\log\log x}
with the weaker condition that
m>x^{1/\log\log x}
and by introducing an integral to smooth out on a scale of
1/X
. Here and in what follows, we set
X=e^{\sqrt{\log x}}
.

As
\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})=\mathbb{E}\prod_{i=-M}^{M}(\sum_{j=-N}^{N+1}g_{j}(S_{i,f}(h)))=\mathbb{E}1=1
, we apply Hölder’s inequality to the sum over
j
to see that

For

3.4.
Completion of the proof

We now define

or not, and then apply Hölder’s inequality to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}\frac{1}{\log^{1.01}P}\sum_{|m|\leq(\log^{1.01}P)/2}|F_{P,f}^{\text{rand}}(1/2+i\frac{m}{\log^{1.01}P})|^{2})^{k}\\

&+\Biggl(\frac{1}{\log^{1.01}P}\sum_{|m|\leq(\log^{1.01}P)/2}\mathbb{E}\textbf{1}_{m\in\mathcal{T}}|F_{P,f}^{\text{rand}}(1/2+i\frac{m}{\log^{1.01}P})|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}.\end{split}

2.4
to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{\textbf{j}}\sigma^{\text{rand}}(\textbf{j})\Biggl(\mathbb{E}^{\textbf{j},\text{rand}}\frac{1}{\log^{1.01}P}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|m|\leq(\log^{1.01}P)/2,\\
k\notin\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}\textbf{1}_{|S_{m,f}(h)-j(m)|\leq 1}|F_{P,f}^{\text{rand}}(1/2+i\frac{m}{\log^{1.01}P})|^{2}\Biggr)^{k}\\
\ll&\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{\log^{1.01}P}\sum_{|m|\leq(\log^{1.01}P)/2}|F_{P,f}^{\text{rand}}(1/2+i\frac{m}{\log^{1.01}P})|^{2})^{k}+(\delta N\log^{2.2}P)^{k}\\
\ll&\left(\frac{\log P}{1+(1-k)\sqrt{\log\log P}}\right)^{k}+(\delta N\log^{2.2}P)^{k},\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from Lemma
2.21
. The above estimation now allows us to obtain the bound given in (
3.29
) as long as we have
\delta\leq\frac{1}{N\log^{1.2}P\sqrt{\log\log P}}
.

Recall now we needed to have
xP^{4000((2M+1)/\delta)^{2}\log(N\log P)}<q
in order to apply Proposition
2.7
in section
3.2
. As we have
M=2\log^{1.02}P
, the above estimation will hold provided that
P^{10^{5}(\log^{2.04}P)(1/\delta)^{2}\log(N\log P)}<q/x
. Recall also from our discussions above that we need to have
N\geq 1.2\log\log P
and
\delta\leq\frac{1}{N\sqrt{\log\log P}}
, as well as
\delta\leq\frac{1}{N\log^{1.2}P\sqrt{\log\log P}}
.

We now set
N=\lceil 1.2\log\log P\rceil
, and
\delta=\frac{1}{\log^{1.3}P}
to see that for these values, we have
P^{10^{5}(\log^{2.04}P)(1/\delta)^{2}\log(N\log P)}<P^{10^{6}(\log^{4.64}P)\log\log P}
. Recall that
P
is slightly smaller than
\exp(\log^{1/6}L)
so that we have
P^{10^{6}(\log^{4.64}P)\log\log P}\leq\exp(10^{7}(\log^{5.64/6}L)\log\log L)\leq\exp(\log L)\leq q/x
. As all the other conditions above are satisfied, this implies the estimation given in (
3.29
) and hence completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

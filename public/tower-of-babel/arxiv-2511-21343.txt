Model Predictive Control and Moving Horizon Estimation using Statistically Weighted Data-Based Ensemble Models
Laura Boca de Giuli, Samuel Mallick, Alessio La Bella, Azita Dabiri, Bart De Schutter, Riccardo Scattolini

Abstract
This paper presents a model predictive control (MPC) framework leveraging an ensemble of data-based models to optimally control complex systems under multiple operating conditions. A novel combination rule for ensemble models is proposed, based on the statistical Mahalanobis distance, enabling the ensemble weights to suitably vary across the prediction window based on the system input. In addition, a novel state observer for ensemble models is developed using moving horizon estimation (MHE). The effectiveness of the proposed methodology is demonstrated on a benchmark energy system operating under multiple conditions.



I
INTRODUCTION

Model predictive control (MPC) has become one of the most prevalent strategies for managing large-scale and complex systems, primarily thanks to its predictive capabilities and explicit constraints handling
[
1
]
. Data-based models such as neural networks (NNs) have recently gained popularity in the MPC community due to the vast availability of data and the relative ease of development in comparison to physics-based models
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. However, when diverse datasets spanning different operating ranges are used, employing a single black-box model to represent significantly varying operating conditions often leads to degraded performance and loss of prediction accuracy
[
5
,
6
]
.

A promising solution to this issue is ensemble learning
[
7
]
, e.g., incremental learning
[
8
]
or a mixture of experts approach
[
9
]
. These methods improve accuracy and generalization by training specialized models, or experts, on specific datasets corresponding to different operating domains, and subsequently combining their outputs
[
10
]
. Several techniques have been proposed in the literature to combine the predictions of ensemble models. One common approach is the arithmetic averaging of the models’ outputs, as discussed in
[
11
]
for recurrent neural networks (RNNs). An alternative approach is weighted averaging, where higher weights are assigned to models whose predictions were more accurate in past data
[
12
]
. However, as these methods select a static weighting, they may be suboptimal in predictive control frameworks such as MPC
[
13
]
, where the weighting should ideally vary across the prediction window. To this end, more advanced methods dynamically optimize the combination weights with gating networks
[
14
]
, e.g., using generic artificial neural networks
[
15
]
or Bayesian neural networks
[
16
]
. These approaches rely on previously encountered data to estimate combination weights as functions of system states, inputs, and outputs. This is, however, impractical within an MPC framework, where future state and output measurements are not available along the prediction window, thus preventing proper optimization of the combination weights.

A further challenge in the use of ensemble models within MPC is the estimation of the state of each individual expert, given that only the overall system input and output are measurable. This problem is especially significant for data-based models in which the state retains no physical meaning. In
[
17
]
, a Kazantzis-Kravaris-Luenberger observer is designed for each expert and subsequently combined, but the approach relies on assumptions valid only for specific model structures, excluding complex nonlinear models such as neural networks. Alternatively, some works investigate the use of ensembles of observers to improve state estimation for a single model
[
18
,
19
]
.
Moreover, state constraints must also be explicitly considered during the estimation phase, an essential aspect for most data-based models (e.g., amplitude-bounded hidden states in RNNs due to activation functions
[
20
]
).
However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, no method has yet been proposed for constrained state estimation in ensemble models.

In light of the above discussion, this paper makes two main contributions. Firstly, we propose an MPC formulation that employs an ensemble model whose experts are combined using the statistical Mahalanobis distance
[
21
]
. Specifically, the proposed approach assigns combination weights according to the statistical proximity between the current operating condition and each expert’s training input data. This allows the combination weights to be determined solely as a function of the input, whose future sequence is available in MPC frameworks.
Secondly, we propose a novel observer design for ensemble models based on moving horizon estimation (MHE)
[
22
]
, which enables the correct estimation of the states of all experts in the ensemble while enforcing state constraints.

The proposed framework can be applied to the control of general dynamical systems, particularly those characterized by large and heterogeneous datasets, such as complex energy networks. In addition, the ensemble can be composed of any type of data-based model; in this work, we employ gated recurrent units (GRUs) within the family of RNNs, thanks to their strong approximation capabilities
[
23
]
. Simulations on a district heating system featuring multiple operating conditions
[
24
]
demonstrate that the proposed Mahalanobis-based ensemble MPC significantly outperforms standard averaging and optimization-based methods of weight selection in terms of both economic performance and constraint satisfaction. Moreover, the MHE-based observer significantly reduces the output prediction error with respect to the ensemble model’s open-loop predictions.

The system is assumed to operate under multiple conditions, each characterized by distinct datasets representing system behaviour in those conditions. A typical example can be observed in energy systems, where load demand shifts between seasonal operating ranges, such as transitioning from summer to winter conditions.

To manage this system, we employ the well-established model predictive control (MPC) approach. As MPC is designed under the certainty equivalence principle (CEP)

25
]

For the systems under consideration in this paper, where vast amounts of data across multiple and heterogeneous operating regions are available, it is often more effective to use an ensemble of data-based models for prediction, rather than relying solely on a single physics-based or black-box model

7

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}:\;\begin{cases}x^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}(k+1)=f^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}\big(x^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}(k),u(k)\big)\\

IV
MPC with Mahalanobis distance-based ensemble model

The first contribution of this work is an MPC regulator embedding a data-based ensemble model for prediction, where the outputs of each ensemble component are combined according to the statistical proximity between the optimised inputs and those used to train the specific model. This strategy is inspired by
[
26
]
, where, however, control design and state estimation for ensembles are not addressed.

Considering a sampling time
\tau
and a prediction horizon
H
, at each time instant
t=k\tau

k\in\mathbb{N}

(6b)

(6c)

\displaystyle\qquad y(h)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}(h)y^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}(h),

(6d)

As previously discussed, existing MPC formulations embedding ensemble models leverage suboptimal combination rules for model outputs. In most cases, the weights are fixed to constant values

[

28

To overcome these limitations, we propose a strategy in which the ensemble weights are designed as a function of the input

\begin{aligned} \lambda^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}_{\text{MD}}\big(u(h)\big)=\frac{\frac{1}{T^{2}\big(u(h),\bm{u}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}\big)}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{T^{2}\big(u(h),\bm{u}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}\big)}}\end{aligned},

V
MHE-based observer for ensemble models

The second contribution of this work is an observer scheme for estimating the state of each model
\mathcal{M}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}
in the ensemble (
5
).
In data-based models, the state of the prediction model often loses any physical significance, e.g., the internal state of an RNN, rendering state estimation essential.
To address this, we here propose relying on moving horizon estimation (MHE)
[
22
]
.
As a moving-horizon and optimization-based observer method, MHE is a natural counterpart for MPC controllers, allowing the use of the same MPC prediction model for state estimation.
In particular, in our setting, MHE allows us to embed the data-based model of each ensemble component in the observer scheme.
Furthermore, state constraints can be handled explicitly.

For a prediction horizon
\hat{H}
, at each time instant
t=k\tau
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following MHE problem, a function of the previous
\hat{H}
applied inputs and measured outputs,
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{u}\times\hat{H}}
and
\bm{y}_{\text{p}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{y}\times\hat{H}}
, is solved for each model in the ensemble, according to the receding horizon strategy:

\displaystyle P^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}_{\text{\tiny MHE}}\big(\bar{x}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]},\bm{u},\bm{y}_{\text{p}}\big)=

\displaystyle\quad\min_{\bm{\hat{x}}^{[i]},\bm{\omega}^{[i]},\bm{\nu}^{[i]}}\big\|\hat{x}^{[i]}(k-\hat{H})-\bar{x}^{[i]}\big\|_{Q_{x}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sum_{h=k-\hat{H}}^{k-1}\big\|\nu^{[i]}(h)\big\|_{Q_{\nu}}+\big\|\omega^{[i]}(h)\big\|_{Q_{\omega}}

(8a)

\displaystyle\textrm{s.t.}\>\>\,\,\forall h\in\{k-\hat{H},\ldots,k-1\}

\displaystyle\qquad\hat{x}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}(h+1)=f^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}\big(\hat{x}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}(h),u(h)\big)+\omega^{[i]}(h),

(8b)

\displaystyle\qquad y_{\text{p}}(h)=g^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}\big(\hat{x}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}(h),u(h)\big)+\nu^{[i]}(h),

(8c)

\displaystyle\qquad\hat{x}^{[i]}(h)\in\mathcal{X},

(8d)

where
\omega^{[i]}
and
\nu^{[i]}
are the process and measurement error, with the matrices
Q_{\omega}
and
Q_{\nu}
weighting their penalties, respectively.
Furthermore,
\bar{x}^{[i]}
is the state estimate of (
8
) for time step
k-\hat{H}
, computed at time step
k-1
.
The first cost term, the arrival cost, penalises deviation from this previous estimate, weighted by
Q_{x}
, in order to capture historical data outside the prediction window.
Finally,
\mathcal{X}
is a constraint set imposed on the estimated state for, e.g., enforcing amplitude bounds on the hidden states in RNNs that arise from specific activation functions.

With (
8
), we propose to estimate the state of each model within the ensemble by using the global output of the real plant
y_{\text{p}}
to form an output error.
The optimisation problem (
8
) finds a state trajectory for each model in the ensemble that best matches the historical data of the true system.
The final optimised states
\hat{x}^{\scriptscriptstyle[i]}(k)
are then used as the initial states in (
6e
).
The combination of (
8
) and (
6
) can be interpreted as first pushing each model in the ensemble to best match the real system in the past, and then, as not all models will accurately capture the true dynamics for a given operating condition, weighting the contribution of each model in the future via (
7
).

VI
Numerical Results

The proposed framework is tested on a district heating system (DHS), a highly efficient energy plant crucial for achieving decarbonisation goals. VII
Conclusions

This paper addresses optimal control of complex systems operating under multiple conditions using ensemble models. Existing ensemble-based MPC frameworks typically rely on either fixed weights, which are suboptimal, or weights that depend on system states and outputs, which are unavailable in prediction. In this work, we propose a novel combination rule for ensemble models based on the statistical Mahalanobis distance, allowing the ensemble weights to evolve over the prediction window solely as functions of the inputs. In addition, a new observer for ensemble models based on MHE is developed to provide accurate MPC state initialization. The proposed methodology is validated on a complex district heating system benchmark, demonstrating that the Mahalanobis distance-based combination rule outperforms both fixed and output-dependent weighting strategies in terms of economic performance and constraint satisfaction. Furthermore, the proposed MHE-based observer significantly enhances state estimation accuracy compared to an open-loop approach, leading to improved overall closed-loop performance.

Future work will focus on integrating the proposed framework within an active learning setting to leverage MPC for informative data acquisition.

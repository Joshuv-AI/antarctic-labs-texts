Maximizing the Azimuthal-Angle Correlation in the Decay of Vector Boson Pairs
Kun Cheng, Yi-Jing Fang, Tao Han, Matthew Low

Abstract
Spin correlation observables of particles produced at colliders generally depend on the coordinate basis. While spin correlations and their associated basis dependence have been studied in top-quark pair production, the basis dependence of spin correlations of W ± W^{\pm} pairs has not been studied. In this work, we focus on the azimuthal-angle correlation in the decay of W ± W^{\pm} pairs, which encodes spin information in the direction transverse to the rotational axis. We quantitatively describe the basis dependence of the relative azimuthal angle. We further present the optimal coordinate direction to define the azimuthal angle between the decay products of W ± W^{\pm} bosons to achieve the maximal spin correlation based on symmetry considerations. The results can be used to optimize the measurement of the CP-even and CP-odd interactions, and have applications to quantum information observables in the vector boson pair system.

Introduction

One of the most important phenomena in the production of particle pairs at colliders is the correlation between particle spins, which depends sensitively on the fundamental interactions and is often used to probe new physics effects. In collider environments, massive particles with spin, such as top quarks and
W

Consequently, the spin correlation between two particles is measured from the angular correlation between their decay products.

An important feature of spin correlations is that the observables used to measure them are basis dependent, which has been extensively studied in top-pair production

III
Optimizing the Azimuthal-angle Correlations in the decay of
W^{\pm}

The leading-order differential distribution has two oscillatory terms: a
\cos(\Delta\phi)
term with a coefficient
A
and a
\sin(\Delta\phi)
term with a coefficient
\tilde{A}
. Each of these terms has a distinct basis dependence on the axis
\vec{z}
, as seen in Eq. (
15
), meaning that one choice of
\vec{z}
will maximize
A
and a separate choice of
\vec{z}
will maximize
\tilde{A}
. Depending on the physics we are interested in, we can choose which coefficient we optimize. In particular, the
\cos(\Delta\phi)
and
\sin(\Delta\phi)
modulations are, respectively, related to CP-even and CP-odd interactions. Under a CP transformation, the angles transform as

\displaystyle\left(\theta,\phi,\bar{\theta},\bar{\phi}\right)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\rm{CP}}\left(\pi-\bar{\theta},\pi+\bar{\phi},\pi-\theta,\pi+\phi\right),

(16)

\displaystyle\Delta\phi

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\rm{CP}}-\Delta\phi,

therefore, the
\sin(\Delta\phi)
term flips its sign while the
\cos(\Delta\phi)
term is invariant. Thus, the coefficient
A
parametrizes CP-even interactions while
\tilde{A}
parametrizes CP-odd interactions.
The spin correlation matrix defined in Eq. (
13
) transforms as
C_{ij}\to C_{ji}
under a CP transformation.
From Eq. (
15
), we see that
A
only receives contributions from the symmetric part of
C_{ij}
which is CP-even, while
\tilde{A}
only receives contributions from the antisymmetric part of
C_{ij}
which is CP-odd.

In the SM at leading order,
W^{+}W^{-}
production is CP-invariant, therefore to best characterize the SM, the coefficient
A
should be optimized. Meanwhile, CP-violating new physics effects on the spin correlations of
W^{\pm}
are manifested in the coefficient
\tilde{A}
. In Sec.
III.1
we show the optimization of

and in Sec.
III.2
we show the optimization of

.

IV
Conclusions

Spin correlation observables are generally coordinate basis dependent for particles produced at colliders. In this work, by performing a Clebsch–Gordan decomposition of the spin density matrix, we quantitatively described the basis dependence of the spin correlation between
W^{\pm}
pairs focusing on the azimuthal-angle correlation in their decays. Utilizing this basis dependence, we further provided the optimal basis to define the relative azimuthal angle
\Delta\phi
to achieve the maximal spin correlation.

With the optimization procedure we developed, one can choose to maximize different modulations in the distribution of the relative azimuthal angle
\Delta\phi
for different physics purposes. For a better measurement of the SM prediction, one can maximize the CP-even
\cos(\Delta\phi)
modulation to yield the largest possible value of the coefficient
A
. The optimal basis choice yields a correlation about 4 times larger compared to the conventional choice along the
W^{\pm}
moving direction, leading to the optimal sensitivity of measuring the SM prediction of azimuthal-angle correlation. To study the CP-odd component of
W^{\pm}
pair spin correlation from new physics effects with a single CP asymmetry observable
\epsilon
, one can maximize the
\sin(\Delta\phi)
modulation and achieve the optimal constraint on the CP-odd operator. In addition, the optimal direction is close to the direction
\hat{r}
perpendicular to the
W^{\pm}
momentum in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to W^{+}W^{-}
scattering plane for both CP-even and CP-odd interactions, making our suggestion of using the
\hat{r}
direction in lieu of the mathematically optimal one more convenient to apply in realistic studies.

In our presentation, we considered the leptonic decay channels as in Eq. (
1
) with
\ell=e,\mu
. In practice, there is a two-fold ambiguity when solving the neutrino momentum from the
W
mass constraint
[
6
]
. This may lead to some degree of degradation in the sensitivity results, although our optimization procedure is still valid. On the other hand, one can also generalize this analysis to the semi-leptonic channel
W^{+}W^{-}\to\ell^{\pm}\nu\ q\bar{q}^{\prime}
. In this channel the branching fraction is larger and kinematic reconstruction is easier. The drawback is the need for flavor tagging or charge tagging for one of the hadronic jets.

The quantitative description of the basis dependence and the optimization procedure in this work applies equally to the spin correlation of a
ZZ
pair and the
ZW^{\pm}
final state. Moreover, the Clebsch–Gordan decomposition characterizes how the spin correlation transforms under an SO(3) basis transformation, which provides a general way to quantitatively describe the coordinate basis dependence of the spin correlations in any system.

With the upcoming LHC Run 3 and the HL-LHC runs, as well as the planning of FCC-ee, one would expect to accumulate a large number of events of gauge boson pairs. Our procedure surely will provide a new avenue to effectively study the rich physics associated with gauge boson pairs, and look for subtle CP violation effects from physics beyond the Standard Model.

Finally, we note that there is potential for future work when the
W^{+}W^{-}
system is treated as a quantum state (see also Ref.
[
27
]
). While our work optimizes a single entry of the spin correlation matrix, analogous to Refs.
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
in the
t\bar{t}
system, a natural follow-up would be to perform optimization for quantum entanglement or Bell nonlocality, as was done in Refs.
[
4
,
5
]
for the
t\bar{t}
system.

Appendix A
The Production Density Matrix and Spin Correlation Matrix of a
W^{\pm}
Boson Pair

At a given phase space point, the spin density matrix of
W^{+}W^{-}
produced from
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation is

\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\frac{\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}^{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}^{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}\right)^{*}}{\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}|\mathcal{M}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}^{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}|^{2}},

(26)

where the matrix element is

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}^{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}\equiv\bra{W^{-}(k_{1},\lambda)W^{+}(k_{2},\bar{\lambda})}\mathcal{T}\ket{e^{-}(p_{1},\sigma)e^{+}(p_{2},\bar{\sigma})},

(27)

and
\mathcal{T}
is the transition matrix.
p_{i}/k_{i}
is the momentum of the initial/final state;
\sigma=\pm 1
and
\bar{\sigma}=\pm 1
denote the polarizations of the initial
e^{+}e^{-}
state;
\lambda=0,\pm 1
and
\bar{\lambda}=0,\pm 1
denote the helicity of
W^{\pm}
.

The helicity index
\lambda
of the
W^{\pm}
boson is defined according to the polarization vector. In Ref.
[
6
]
, they are chosen as

\epsilon_{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-1,-i,0),\qquad\qquad\epsilon_{0}=(0,0,0,1),\qquad\qquad\epsilon_{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,1,-i,0),

(28)

for
W^{-}
in its rest frame and

\bar{\epsilon}_{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,1,-i,0),\qquad\qquad\bar{\epsilon}_{0}=(0,0,0,1),\qquad\qquad\bar{\epsilon}_{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-1,-i,0),

(29)

for
W^{+}
in its rest frame, where we have chosen the
W^{-}
moving direction as
\vec{z}
direction for simplicity. Note that the helicity of the
W^{+}
is opposite to its spin.

Consider a new direction
\vec{z}^{\,\prime}=R\cdot\vec{z}
as the spin quantization axis, then the spin basis
\epsilon_{\lambda}
transform as
\epsilon_{\lambda}\to U_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\epsilon_{\lambda^{\prime}}
and
\bar{\epsilon}_{\lambda}\to\bar{U}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\bar{\epsilon}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, where
U
and
\bar{U}
are 3-dimensional unitary matrices.
The unitarity transformation
U
of the spin basis can be related to the rotation
R
with the spin operators. The spin operators of
W^{-}
expressed in the basis
(\epsilon_{+},\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{-})
are

S_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&1\\
0&1&0\\
\end{pmatrix},\qquad\qquad S_{y}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&-i&0\\
i&0&-i\\
0&i&0\\
\end{pmatrix},\qquad\qquad S_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(30)

The helicity direction of
W^{+}
is defined according to
-\vec{z}
direction, opposite to the helicity definition of
W^{-}
.
If we still express the spin operator in the order of helicity,
i.e.
,
(\bar{\epsilon}_{+},\bar{\epsilon}_{0},\bar{\epsilon}_{-})
, then we need to flip the indices of the spin operator of
W^{-}
, with
\bar{S}_{x}=S_{x}
,
\bar{S}_{y}=-S_{y}
, and
\bar{S}_{z}=-S_{z}
.
For each representation of the spin operator, under the basis transformation, we have

S_{i}\to US_{i}U^{\dagger}=R_{ji}S_{j},\qquad\qquad\bar{S}_{i}\to\bar{U}\bar{S}_{i}\bar{U}^{\dagger}=R_{ji}\bar{S}_{j}.

(31)

Then we see that the spin correlation matrix

C_{ij}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime},\bar{\lambda},\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}S^{i}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\bar{S}^{j}_{\bar{\lambda}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}

(32)

transforms as an SO(3) tensor.

If higher dimensional operators are introduced, the production density matrix can be written in 3 terms,

\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}{(0)}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}+\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}(1)}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}+\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}(2)}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}},

(33)

where the SM contribution term
\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}{(0)}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}
, the interference term between the SM and the new physics
\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}{(1)}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}
, and the quadratic term of the new physics contribution
\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}{(2)}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}
, respectively, are

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}{(0)}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}\mathcal{M}^{\rm SM}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\big(\mathcal{M}^{\rm SM}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}\big)^{*},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}{(1)}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}\left[\mathcal{M}^{\rm SM}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\big(\mathcal{M}^{\rm EFT}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}\big)^{*}+\mathcal{M}^{\rm EFT}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\big(\mathcal{M}^{\rm SM}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}\big)^{*}\right],

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}{(2)}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma}}\mathcal{M}^{\rm EFT}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\big(\mathcal{M}^{\rm EFT}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}\big)^{*},

and the normalization factor
\mathcal{N}
is

\mathcal{N}=\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\left|\mathcal{M}^{\rm SM}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}+\mathcal{M}^{\rm EFT}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\right|^{2}=\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\left|\mathcal{M}^{\rm SM}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\right|^{2}+\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\left|\mathcal{M}^{\rm EFT}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\right|^{2}.

(35)

Note that the interference term does not contribute to the total cross section

\sum_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\Big[\mathcal{M}^{\rm SM}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\big(\mathcal{M}^{\rm EFT}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\big)^{*}+\mathcal{M}^{\rm EFT}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\big(\mathcal{M}^{\rm SM}_{\sigma\bar{\sigma},\lambda\bar{\lambda}}\big)^{*}\Big]=0.

(36)

Appendix B
Decay-Angle Correlations for Final State Leptons

The angular distribution of the leptons from the
W^{\pm}
decays can be expressed as the product of the production density matrix and the decay density matrix of the
W^{\pm}
boson as follows:

\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega^{+}d\Omega^{-}}=\frac{1}{N}\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}D^{\lambda}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\bar{D}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}.

(37)

Here,
N
is the normalization factor which is
N=9/(64\pi^{2})
,
D^{\lambda}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
and
\bar{D}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}
are the normalized decay density matrix of
W^{-}
and
W^{+}
, respectively.

To obtain the distribution, we first define the momenta of the leptons by choosing the helicity basis, shown in Fig.
3
(a) in the rest frame of the
W^{-}
and
W^{+}
bosons. The four-momenta of leptons in terms of polar angle and azimuthal angle can be expressed as

\displaystyle p_{\ell^{-}}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{W^{-}}}{2}(1,\,\cos\phi\sin\theta,\,\sin\phi\sin\theta,\,\cos\theta),

(38)

\displaystyle p_{\ell^{+}}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{W^{+}}}{2}(1,\,\cos\bar{\phi}\sin\bar{\theta},\,\sin\bar{\phi}\sin\bar{\theta},\,\cos\bar{\theta}).

In the decay of a
W^{\pm}
boson, the final states are angular momentum eigenstates in the
W^{\pm}
boson rest frame because there are only left-handed interactions. Therefore, the decay amplitude can be expressed in the form of Wigner functions
[
6
]
,

\mathcal{M}_{W^{-}_{\lambda}}\propto l_{\lambda},\qquad\mathcal{M}_{W^{+}_{\bar{\lambda}}}\propto\bar{l}_{\bar{\lambda}},

(39)

where

\left(l_{+},l_{0},l_{-}\right)=\left(d_{-}e^{i\phi},-d_{0},d_{+}e^{-i\phi}\right),\qquad\left(\bar{l}_{+},\bar{l}_{0},\bar{l}_{-}\right)=\left(\bar{d}_{-}e^{-i\bar{\phi}},-\bar{d}_{0},\bar{d}_{+}e^{i\bar{\phi}}\right),

(40)

with

\displaystyle d_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1\pm\cos{(\theta)}),

\displaystyle\qquad d_{0}=\sin{(\theta)},

(41)

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1\pm\cos{(\bar{\theta})}),

\displaystyle\qquad\bar{d}_{0}=\sin{(\bar{\theta})}.

Therefore, the normalized density matrix of
W^{\pm}
bosons decaying to leptons can be expressed as

D^{\lambda}_{\lambda^{\prime}}=l_{\lambda}l_{\lambda^{\prime}},\qquad\bar{D}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\bar{l}_{\bar{\lambda}}\bar{l}_{\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}.

(42)

From the above, we derive the angular distribution. Since we focus solely on the azimuthal angle distribution in our study, we encapsulate the polar angle dependence in coefficients, resulting in an angular distribution of

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega^{+}d\Omega^{-}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}_{\lambda^{\prime}\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}D^{\lambda}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\bar{D}^{\bar{\lambda}}_{\bar{\lambda}^{\prime}}

(43)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Big[\mathcal{A}+\Big\{2\operatorname{Re}[\mathcal{B}\cos(\phi)+\bar{\mathcal{B}}\cos(\bar{\phi})+\mathcal{C}\cos(2\phi)+\bar{\mathcal{C}}\cos(2\bar{\phi})

\displaystyle+\mathcal{D}_{\pm}\cos(\phi\pm\bar{\phi})+\mathcal{E}_{\pm}\cos(2\phi\pm\bar{\phi})+\bar{\mathcal{E}}_{\pm}\cos(\phi\pm 2\bar{\phi})

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\mathcal{G}_{\pm}\cos(2\phi\pm 2\bar{\phi})\right]+(\operatorname{Re}\rightarrow\mathrm{Im})+(\cos\rightarrow\sin)\right\}\Big],

Here,
N=9/(64\pi^{2})
is the normalization factor, and the coefficients (
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\bar{\mathcal{B}},...
) are functions of the polar angles of the leptons. The relevant coefficients are

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\mathscr{P}^{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}_{\lambda\bar{\lambda}}d^{2}_{\lambda}d^{2}_{\bar{\lambda}},

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{-}=d_{0}\bar{d}_{0}\Big[\mathscr{P}_{++}^{00}d_{-}\bar{d}_{-}+\mathscr{P}_{+0}^{0-}d_{-}\bar{d}_{+}+\mathscr{P}_{0+}^{-0}d_{+}\bar{d}_{-}+\mathscr{P}_{00}^{--}d_{+}\bar{d}_{+}\Big],

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{-}=d_{+}d_{-}\bar{d}_{+}\bar{d}_{-}\mathscr{P}^{--}_{++}.

If we denote the relative decay azimuthal angle as
\Delta\phi=\phi-\bar{\phi}
, then
\mathcal{D}_{-}
is associated with
\Delta\phi
and
\mathcal{G}_{-}
is associated with
2\Delta\phi
. After integrating over
\phi+\bar{\phi}
and the polar angles in the
\mathcal{D}_{-}
and
\mathcal{G}_{-}
coefficients, we obtain the distribution of the relative azimuthal angle
\Delta\phi
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{d\sigma}{d\Delta\phi}=\frac{1}{2\pi}+A\cos(\Delta\phi)+\tilde{A}\sin(\Delta\phi)+B\cos(2\Delta\phi)+\tilde{B}\sin(2\Delta\phi),

(45)

where the coefficients are

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{9\pi}{128}\operatorname{Re}\left(\mathscr{P}^{00}_{++}+\mathscr{P}^{0-}_{+0}+\mathscr{P}^{-0}_{0+}+\mathscr{P}_{00}^{--}\right),

(46)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{9\pi}{128}\operatorname{Im}\left(\mathscr{P}^{00}_{++}+\mathscr{P}^{0-}_{+0}+\mathscr{P}^{-0}_{0+}+\mathscr{P}_{00}^{--}\right),

(47)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\operatorname{Re}\mathscr{P}_{++}^{--},

(48)

\displaystyle\tilde{B}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\operatorname{Im}\mathscr{P}_{++}^{--}.

(49)

The coefficients of the leading terms
\sin(\Delta\phi)
and
\cos(\Delta\phi)
can be written in the form of spin correlation coefficients,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=-\frac{9\pi}{64}\left(\operatorname{Tr}(C_{ij})-C_{33}\right)=-\frac{9\pi}{64}\left(C_{ii}-C_{ij}z_{i}z_{j}\right),

(50)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}

\displaystyle=-\frac{9\pi}{64}\left(C_{12}-C_{21}\right)=-\frac{9\pi}{64}\epsilon_{ijk}C_{ij}z_{k}.

(51)

From the specific form of the coefficients above, we can easily see that these coefficients depend on the choice of the

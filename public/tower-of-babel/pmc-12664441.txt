Radio-magnetic dual-functional microspheres for magnetic hyperthermia therapy combined with radioembolization of hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Manran Wu, Dong Wang, Yu Qin, Xunhao Qi, Qian Huang, Xingwei Sun, Yong Jin, Ran Zhu, Guanglin Wang, Pengfei Rong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a formidable clinical challenge due to its aggressive nature, high recurrence rate, and limited therapeutic options, particularly for intermediate-advanced stage patients who are ineligible for surgical resection. Current transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) and conventional transarterial radioembolization (TARE) often face challenges such as incomplete tumor necrosis and radioresistance. To address these critical clinical issues, we develop an innovative polydopamine-coated 177Lu-labeled magnetic microspheres (177Lu-MMS@PDA) that synergistically combines TARE with magnetic hyperthermia therapy (MHT), which enable non-invasive, deep-tissue-penetrating hyperthermia while significantly enhancing radiostability. This dual-modality approach not only overcomes the limitations of single therapy but also demonstrates the unique clinical advantages including addressing the problem of uneven drug distribution in conventional embolization, overcoming tumor radioresistance and reducing off-target effects. Importantly, our large animal studies using beagle dogs have confirmed the outstanding biosafety profile of 177Lu-MMS@PDA, meeting essential criteria for clinical translation. With its combined advantages of enhanced therapeutic efficacy and minimized systemic toxicity, 177Lu-MMS@PDA represents a breakthrough solution for HCC treatment, particularly promising for patients with advanced or treatment-refractory disease.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a prevalent malignancy in clinical practice, representing a significant threat to public health [1,2]. The therapeutic approach to HCC is dictated by the stage of the disease, with treatment options including surgery intervention, transarterial chemoembolization, transarterial radioembolization (TARE), ablation therapy, and systemic therapy [[3], [4], [5], [6]]. The absence of early symptoms of HCC frequently results in a diagnosis at a middle to late stage, with patients often experiencing complications. Consequently, surgery is only feasible for approximately 20 % of patients [7,8]. Ablation therapy offers a radical treatment option for patients who are not suitable for surgical resection, but its efficacy may be limited by the size and location of the tumor [9,10]. TARE is a less invasive treatment that is localized to the lesion and is well targeted, but some HCC cells may acquire radiation resistance [[11], [12], [13], [14]].

TARE involves the injection of radioactive microspheres into the liver for targeted radionuclide therapy, which is based on the fact that HCC is mainly supplied by the hepatic artery, thus allowing a maximal radiation dose to be delivered to the tumor whilst minimizing damage to normal liver tissue [[15], [16], [17]]. TARE has been utilized as a radiation therapy technique in the management of HCC, particularly in cases where surgical intervention is not feasible [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. The microspheres employed in clinical practice are predominantly 90Y-labeled glass (TheraSphere) or 90Y-resin microspheres (SIR-Spheres) [22,23]. However, glass microspheres are dense and prone to deposition, while resin microspheres possess a low specific activity and the possibility of radionuclide leakage exists. Meanwhile, 90Y is a β-radionuclide emitter, which lacks γ-ray for SPECT imaging to detect the distribution of microspheres in the body [[24], [25], [26], [27]].

177Lu has been demonstrated to be a clinically ideal radionuclide, with benefits observed in the context of peptide receptor radionuclide therapy for neuroendocrine tumors and peptide radioligand therapy for prostate cancer [[28], [29], [30]]. It is capable of emitting medium-energy β particles (490 keV) and low-energy γ rays (113 keV (3 %), 210 keV (11 %)). In addition to its use in radionuclide therapy, the emitted γ rays are useful for localization monitoring and radiation dosimetry. The average tissue penetration depth is 0.67 mm, with a maximum penetration depth of 2.2 mm. Furthermore, the half-life of 177Lu is 6.7 d, which renders it suitable for transportation and clinical application. Consequently, it is considered a promising radionuclide for TARE of HCC [[31], [32], [33], [34], [35]]. Nevertheless, it is important to note that some HCC cells can become radiation resistant during radiation therapy, and the combination of multiple therapies may be more efficacious than a single therapy [[36], [37], [38]]. The combination of radioembolization and photothermal therapy has been shown to be effective in suppressing tumor growth. However, phototherapy therapy is limited by its inability to penetrate deeply and its invasiveness in the treatment of deep tumors [39,40].

Magnetic hyperthermia therapy (MHT) is a relatively novel approach to ablative therapy, in which magnetic materials are used to generate thermal energy under an alternating magnetic field (AMF), thereby increasing the temperature of the localized lesion tissue and inducing the death of the tumor cells without causing damage to normal cells [[41], [42], [43]]. The penetration depth of the AMF is unlimited, thus enabling MHT to be applied to treat tumors of any site. In comparison with radiofrequency and microwave thermal therapy, MHT is less invasive and safe, with fewer adverse effects, and can adequately heat the tumor [44,45]. The efficacy of magnetic iron oxide nanoparticles in the treatment of glioblastoma and prostate cancer has been demonstrated by clinical trials [[46], [47], [48]]. However, the enhanced permeation retention (EPR) imposes limitations on the accumulation of nanoparticles, thereby restricting the magnetic hyperthermia heating [49]. Consequently, a platform integrating TARE and MHT can be constructed by utilizing magnetic microspheres loaded with iron oxide as a carrier, in conjunction with radionuclides, to achieve enhanced therapeutic efficacy. The arterial embolization technique ensures the delivery of microspheres to the arteries supplying the tumor, thereby maximizing the therapeutic effect of MHT [50]. Conversely, MHT reduces the necessity for high radionuclide doses and minimizes radiation-induced damage to normal tissues [51]. Concurrently, hyperthermia exerts a sensitizing effect on radiation therapy, thereby enhancing the radiation sensitivity of tumor cells [[52], [53], [54]]. Compared to clinically available TARE and TACE methods, the combination therapy not only enables radioembolization therapy and in vivo imaging localization but also allows for multiple non-invasive treatments via magnetic hyperthermia heating to eliminate residual tumors [55,56]. Consequently, the combination of TARE with MHT holds considerable promise for the treatment of HCC.

In this study, 177Lu radiolabeled iron oxide magnetic microspheres with modified polydopamine (PDA) were constructed and designated as 177Lu-MMS@PDA (Scheme 1). MMS is a magnetic microsphere composed of silica microspheres loaded with iron oxide nanoparticles. It exhibits favorable biosafety and uniform particle size, which can stabilize embolization in tumor vessels. Furthermore, the magnetic microspheres manifest a substantial magnetic hyperthermia inducing effect when subjected to an AMF, thereby effectively eradicating tumor cells. Silica microspheres exhibit high specific surface area, enabling stable nuclide loading [57]. After being efficiently radiolabeled with 177Lu, the magnetic microspheres were modified by PDA to make them radiostable, which was confirmed by in vitro radiostability experiment and in vivo SPECT imaging. The combination of MHT and TARE has been shown to be both feasible and effective in a rabbit model of HCC, as evidenced by the significant superiority of the combination treatment group over the monotherapy group and the absence of any substantial side effects. Experimental

Chemicals

The magnetic microspheres (MMS) were procured from Suzhou NanoMicro Technology Co., Ltd. 177LuCl3 was procured from Syncor Pharmaceutical (Chengdu, China) and exhibited a radionuclide purity of >99.9 %. All other reagents were used without further purification.

Preparation and characterization of 177Lu-MMS@PDA

MMS is a composite microsphere obtained by uniformly loading magnetic nanoparticles Fe3O4 onto fully porous silica microspheres. The 177Lu-labeled magnetic microspheres (177Lu-MMS) were prepared in accordance with the previously reported adsorption precipitation method [34]. Briefly, a solution of 177LuCl3 (1 μL, 1 mCi) was diluted in 500 μL of H2O. 10 mg of magnetic microspheres were added to the radionuclide solution, subjected to sonication for 1 min, and then shaken for 10 min at 25 °C. A 20 μL solution of K3PO4 (1 M) was then added to aforementioned solution, which was then shaken for 1 min. The 177Lu-MMS were subsequently collected following a washing process with water. Subsequently, the 177Lu-MMS@PDA was obtained through the polymerization of dopamine on the surface of the 177Lu-radiolabeled microspheres. In short, the 177Lu-MMS were dispersed in Tris-HCl buffer with dopamine hydrochloride (4 mg/mL, pH 8.5) and shaken for 30 min at 25 °C. The final microspheres, 177Lu-MMS@PDA, were obtained after purification. The non-radioactive microspheres (MMS@PDA) were prepared using a similar procedure for the purposes of characterization.

The morphology and size of the MMS and MMS@PDA were characterized using an optical microscope (Olympus, IX73, Japan) and scanning electron microscopy (Zeiss, EVO18, Germany). Particle size statistics were performed based on the images obtained from the optical microscope. The microspheres were initially analyzed for the presence or absence of magnetism using a magnet. The surface elements of MMS and MMS@PDA were analyzed by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (Thermo Fischer, ESCALAB Xi+, USA) with Al Kα excitation source (hv = 1486.6 eV) and the signal was accumulated for 10 cycles. All peaks were charge corrected with C1s = 284.80 eV binding energy as the energy standard. The pressure resistance of MMS@PDA was characterized using Powder resistivity and compact density tester (IEST, PRCD1100, China).

Radiostability of 177Lu-MMS and 177Lu-MMS@PDA

The radiostability of the 177Lu-MMS and 177Lu-MMS@PDA was evaluated through incubation of the radiomicrospheres in saline and 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37 °C over time, respectively. The supernatant was collected at 0.5, 2, 4, 8, 24, 96, and 192 h in order to quantify the radioactivity through a gamma counter. In the same way, the radiostability of 177Lu-MMS@PDA after 10 min AMF of 120 Oe was determined to verify the stabilizing effect.

In vitro magnetic heating effects of MMS@PDA

The magnetization curves of MMS and MMS@PDA were obtained using a vibrating sample magnetometer (Quantum Design, PPMS-9, USA) under AMF of ±30 kOe at 25 °C. The magnetic-thermal effects of MMS and MMS@PDA were tested under AMF of 120 Oe. To evaluate the magnetic heating performance, the heating curves of 5 mg MMS@PDA were recorded under varying AMF intensities (30 Oe, 60 Oe, 90 Oe, and 120 Oe) in a centrifuge tube. MMS@PDA (5, 10, 15, and 20 mg) was added to a centrifuge tube under an AMF of 120 Oe at 30 kHz. Furthermore, the heating stability was assessed under an AMF of 120 Oe with an illumination time of 5 min, repeated five times. The temperature was monitored in real-time using an infrared camera (FLIR, Ax5 Series, USA). To verify the effect of magneto-thermal treatment on microsphere morphology, SEM images were also captured.

In vitro assessment

The Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) was utilized to evaluate the cytotoxic potential of microspheres and the therapeutic efficacy of various treatments on tumor cells. The VX2 cells were inoculated into 96-well plates with DMEM medium, and following a 12 h incubation period, the cells had adhered to the wall. MMS@PDA with varying concentrations (0, 31, 62, 125, 250, and 500 μg/mL) was added to the 96-well plates and incubated for 24 h. Thereafter, the cells were washed with PBS and 10 % CCK-8 solution was added to the plates for 4 h of culture. The multi-function microplate reader (Thermo Scientific, Varioskan Flash, Japan) was utilized to detect the absorbance at 450 nm. HUVEC cells were co-cultured with MMS@PDA with varying concentrations in the same way. 177Lu-MMS@PDA with varying doses (0, 6, 12, 25, and 50 μCi) were incubated with VX2 cells for 24 h by the aforementioned method. In addition, in order to evaluate the anti-tumor effect of radioactive microspheres and magnetic heating alone or in combination on tumor cells, VX2 cells were inoculated in 96-well plates and divided into the following five groups, i.e., control group, MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL), MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min, 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 50 μCi), and 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 50 μCi) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min, and the follow CCK-8 experiment was the same as above.

In the immunofluorescence cell assay, VX2 cells were inoculated on cell slides and, following a 12 h period, divided into five groups with different treatment (control group, MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL), MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min, 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 100 μCi), and 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 100 μCi) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min). Subsequently, γ-H2AX immunofluorescence was stained by a DNA damage assay kit. The samples were then subjected to fixation solution for a period of 5 min, after which the blocking solution was applied for a duration of 10 min. Following this, the cells were incubated with a rabbit monoclonal antibody for a period of 1 h, followed by an anti-rabbit 488 antibody for a further hour. The samples were then stained with DAPI for 5 min. The observations and images were obtained using a confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM, Olympus FV1200, Japan).

For the colony formation assay, VX2 cells were inoculated in 6-well plates at a density of 300 cells per well and divided into five groups: a control group, MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL), MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min, 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 200 μCi), and 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 200 μCi) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min. The medium was changed after 24 h of incubation, and then the cells were left to incubate under normal conditions for 10 days. Finally, they were stained with Giemsa stain after fixation by methanol.

In a similar manner, for the analysis of the cell cycle, VX2 cells were divided into five groups: a control group, MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL), MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min, 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 200 μCi), and 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 200 μCi) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min. Following this, cell cultures were collected after 24 h of incubation, and then fixed with 70 % ethanol for 2 h at 4 °C. Cell cycle analysis was performed by flow cytometry (Beckman Coulter, FC500, USA) after staining by propidium iodide.

For the ROS assays, VX2 cells were inoculated in 6-well plates and divided into five groups: a control group, MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL), MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min, 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 200 μCi), and 177Lu-MMS@PDA (500 μg/mL, 200 μCi) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min. Subsequently, DCFH-DA probe was loaded, and fluorescence was observed under fluorescence microscope after 20 min.

In vivo assessment

New Zealand white rabbits were procured from Pizhou Oriental Breeding Co., Ltd. The hepatocellular carcinoma rabbit model was utilized to evaluate the embolization efficiency of 177Lu-MMS@PDA in vivo. The left lobe of the rabbit liver was inoculated with a tumor mass, and the size of the tumor was detected by MRI (GE, SIGNA Architect, USA) with magnetic field strength of 3.0 T two weeks later. Using the same method of hepatic artery cannulation in rabbits as in humans, microspheres were injected for embolization after accessing the hepatic artery through a femoral artery cannula.

Rabbits bearing VX2 tumor were injected with 177Lu-MMS@PDA (100 mg, 1 mCi) through transarterial embolization, and then subjected to SPECT/CT imaging at days 2, 7, and 14 by a SPECT/CT (Siemens, Symbia Intevo Excel, USA). The analysis of the biodistribution of 177Lu was conducted using PMOD software (version 3.602). The cumulative radiation in tumor was calculated by OLINDA software (version 1.0) using the sphere as the model. The in vivo radiostability of 177Lu-MMS@PDA was then evaluated by measuring radioactivity in blood, urine, and feces using a gamma counter at days 1, 3, 7, and 14, respectively. Following a 14-day period, the tumor and organs were taken for radioactivity evaluation to determine the biodistribution. The tumor was sectioned and H&E staining was performed.

The anti-tumor effect was evaluated by dividing the rabbits bearing VX2 tumor into the following five groups (Table S1): control group, MMS@PDA (100 mg), MMS@PDA (100 mg) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min, 177Lu-MMS@PDA (100 mg, 1 mCi), and 177Lu-MMS@PDA (100 mg, 1 mCi) following AMF (120 Oe) for 10 min. In the experiment, the magnetic field frequency was 30 kHz, with magnetic field strength of 120 Oe, and the product of these parameters (Ho × f = 2.85 × 108 A/m·s) falls below the Atkinson-Brezovich criterion (4.85 × 108 A/m·s), indicating that the parameter settings meet safety requirements [58]. Tumor size was detected by MRI on days 0, 7, and 14, respectively. Blood samples were also obtained for assessment of liver and renal function indices by blood biochemistry. Meanwhile, the body weight changes were recorded. Following a 14-day postoperative period, the rabbits were euthanized, and the tumors and primary organs were dissected for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining for pathological analysis. In addition, the tumors were subjected to terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) staining. Ki67 staining was performed to assess the proliferative capacity of tumor cells.

To further evaluate the safety of 177Lu-MMS@PDA in large animals, a healthy beagle dog (8 months of age, 10 kg) was purchased from Jiangsu Yadong Laboratory Animal Research Institute Co., Ltd. The 177Lu-MMS@PDA (150 mg, 1.5 mCi) was injected into the left lobe of the liver via the hepatic artery, after which AMF (120 Oe) was applied for 10 min. SPECT/CT was used to monitor the distribution of 177Lu-MMS@PDA in vivo at days 2, 7, and 14, respectively. In addition, the evaluation of liver and kidney function was obtained by blood biochemical assay. Meanwhile, the anatomical structure of the liver was monitored by MRI after 21 days postoperative.

Statistical analysis

All results are expressed as means ± SEM or SD as indicated. Multiple t-tests were used when two groups were compared.

Results and discussions

Preparation and characterization of 177Lu-MMS@PDA

The 177Lu-MMS was obtained with high radiolabeling efficiency, greater than 90 %, by the adsorption precipitation method and then coated with PDA to obtain 177Lu-MMS@PDA. Characterization was then processed using MMS@PDA rather than 177Lu-MMS@PDA in order to avoid radioactive contamination. As shown in Fig. 1a–h, the optical microscope and SEM images revealed that, following PDA coating, the MMS@PDA exhibited a dark brown coloration and remained a spherical morphology, with a particle size of 33.5 ± 0.4 μm, as compared to the 33.1 ± 0.4 μm size observed for MMS. Numerous terminal vessels with diameters of 20–50 μm exist within the tumor, serving as the direct nutritional source for tumor cells. Thus, the microsphere can penetrate and embolize these vessels, severing finer blood supplies and preventing tumor compensation through collateral circulation [59]. As expected, both MMS and MMS@PDA demonstrated rapid aggregation close to the magnet in the presence of magnetic field. The XPS results showed that the binding energy peak at 400 eV confirmed the presence of nitrogen in MMS@PDA following the polymerization of dopamine (Fig. 1i). Furthermore, the characteristic peaks of each element of MMS and MMS@PDA were visible in the energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) spectrum, thereby further verifying the composition of the microspheres, the iron content of MMS and MMS@PDA is 6.8 % and 4.3 %. The iron content proportion on the surface of microspheres slightly decreased after PDA modification. (Figure S1 and Fig. 1j). The pressure resistance of MMS@PDA was characterized. As shown in Fig. S2, when the microsphere deformation exceeds 5 %, a pressure exceeding 13 MPa must be applied. However, hepatic arterial systolic pressure ranges from approximately 0.013 to 0.016 MPa, which is significantly lower than the deformation threshold of the microspheres. The radiostability of the radio-magnetic microspheres in saline and 10 % FBS demonstrated that both 177Lu-MMS and 177Lu-MMS@PDA were highly radiostable in saline, exceeding 90 %. However, the radiostability of 177Lu-MMS@PDA was determined to be 95.3 ± 2.5 %, while that of 177Lu-MMS was found to be 73.1 ± 1.7 % in 10 % FBS (Fig. 1k). As 177Lu-MMS relies on adsorption to combine with the radionuclide, 177Lu competes with proteins for binding, resulting in the leakage of the radionuclide. Meanwhile, PDA forms a protective coating and remains stable in the protein environment. In addition, 177Lu-MMS@PDA showed good radiostability after 10 min AMF of 120 Oe, which proves that magnetic heating does not affect the radiostability (Fig. S3). These findings indicate that after PDA coating enables the isolation of the contact between the radionuclides and 10 % FBS, thereby significantly enhancing the radiostability in vitro.

In vitro magneto-thermal effects of MMS@PDA

The magnetic properties of MMS and MMS@PDA were assessed by means of a vibrating sample magnetometer. The results showed that the magnetization intensity of both MMS and MMS@PDA increased significantly with the increase of the intensity of magnetic field. When the magnetic field intensity reached a certain threshold, the magnetization intensity rapidly reached a maximum value and subsequently remained stable. The saturation magnetization intensity was determined to be 5.1 emu/g for MMS and 4.9 emu/g for MMS@PDA. Furthermore, the residual magnetization strength was found to be negligible after the removal of the external magnetic field (Fig. 2a–b). The magnetic-thermal effects of MMS and MMS@PDA were evaluated in a centrifuge tube under AMF at 30 kHz and time-dependent heating curves were recorded. In Fig. S4, the temperature difference between MMS and MMS@PDA is only 1.2 °C with 10 min AMF, which demonstrate that the PDA did not affect the magnetic hyperthermic effect of the microspheres. As demonstrated in Fig. 2c, the temperature of the MMS@PDA (5 mg) is increased by 8.4, 15.8, 21.7, and 31.8 °C with 10 min AMF at intensities of 30, 60, 90, and 120 Oe, respectively. The results demonstrate a direct correlation between the intensity of the AMF and the magnitude of the temperature increase, with the highest intensity resulting in the most significant temperature elevation. Moreover, following exposure to AMF of 120 Oe, MMS@PDA (5, 10, 15, and 20 mg) exhibited notable magnetic-thermal effects, with an increase in temperature observed with increasing microspheres mass (Fig. 2d–e). Furthermore, with on/off cycles of AMF at 120 Oe, the stabilization temperature reached by MMS@PDA remained unchanged, thereby demonstrating the excellent magnetic-thermal stability of the microspheres (Fig. 2f). These results indicate that MMS@PDA is the characteristic of magnetic-thermal effects and can be used for repeatedly heated. Furthermore, SEM images were also captured, as shown in Fig. S5, the morphology of the microspheres remains unchanged, retaining their regular spherical shape.

In vitro toxicity and therapeutic efficacy assessment

In order to evaluate the cytotoxic potential of MMS@PDA, microspheres with varying concentrations were incubated with VX2 cells. The results of the CCK-8 assay, as shown in Fig. 3a, demonstrate that cell viability remained over 90 % even at concentrations of MMS@PDA as high as 500 μg/mL, thus substantiating the biosafety of MMS@PDA. HUVEC cells also maintained a high survival rate at 500 μg/mL of MMS@PDA (Fig. S6). Subsequently, 177Lu-MMS@PDA with varying doses (0, 6, 12, 25, and 50 μCi) were investigated with VX2 cells by the aforementioned method. As demonstrated in Fig. 3b, the cell viability was reduced to below 50 % when the 177Lu-MMS@PDA was administered a dose of 50 μCi. Furthermore, VX2 cells were subjected to diverse treatment modalities to assess their anti-tumor efficacy. Following incubation with 177Lu-MMS@PDA and AMF for 10 min, a significant decline in cell viability was observed, reaching 33.6 ± 1.7 % (Fig. 3c). As demonstrated in Fig. 3d and e, the combination of radiation and magnetic-thermal therapies resulted in more extensive DNA damage, as indicated by the immunofluorescence cell assay. Furthermore, the colony formation assay was performed on VX2 cells to assess the proliferation capacity of the tumor cells following different treatments. The results showed that the colony formation of VX2 cells was inhibited after incubation with 177Lu-MMS@PDA following AMF (Fig. 3f and h). Moreover, the cell cycle assay was performed to investigate the mechanism of the combination therapy. As demonstrated in Fig. 3g, cells exposed to 177Lu-MMS@PDA following AMF exhibited a pronounced G2/M phase blockage (G2/M: 19.1 ± 2.0 %) in comparison to MMS@PDA following AMF (G2/M: 13.3 ± 1.7 %) and 177Lu-MMS@PDA (G2/M: 14.7 ± 0.5 %). The G2/M phase is characterized by its radiosensitivity, and the combination therapy described herein induces a greater proportion of cells to enter this phase. Furthermore, as shown in Fig. S7a and 7b, the combination therapy group exhibited stronger DCFH-DA green fluorescence compared to other groups, highlighting the radiosensitizing effect of radiotherapy. In vivo biodistribution, antitumor efficacy, and safety study

In order to evaluate the biodistribution and antitumor efficacy of radio-magnetic microspheres, a rabbit in situ hepatocellular carcinoma model was constructed for embolization studies. SPECT/CT and MRI were used to monitor the biodistribution and therapeutic efficacy (Fig. 4a). 177Lu-MMS@PDA were injected into the hepatic artery via femoral cannula under the guidance of digital subtraction angiography (DSA) (Fig. 4b). Following embolization, the tumor angiographic signal in the DSA image was significantly attenuated, indicating that the microspheres had been successfully embolized (Fig. 4c). Furthermore, H&E staining of tumor sections 14 days after embolization showed microspheres in the vessels, thus confirming that the microspheres had been successfully embolized and remained in the vessels for a prolonged period (Fig. 4d). SPECT/CT images clearly demonstrated radioactive signals in the tumor with no significant signals in other organs even up to 14 days post embolization (Fig. 4e). Using PMOD software to analyze SPECT images, the biodistribution of 177Lu was highest within the tumor at days 2, 7, and 14 post-embolization, significantly exceeding that in other organs (Fig. S8). The cumulative radioactive dose per cubic centimeter of the tumor was 66.67 ± 9.13 Gy/cm3, while other organs showed low signal intensity. The anti-tumor effect was evaluated in rabbits bearing VX2 tumor in situ. Tumor size was detected by MRI at 0, 7, and 14 days, respectively. Concurrently, the treatment did not result in substantial weight loss in rabbits (Fig. 5d), thereby demonstrating that the combination therapy did not induce significant adverse effects on the organism. Following a 14-day postoperative period, tumors and main organs were excised for H&E and TUNEL staining. The combination of 177Lu-MMS@PDA radiation and magnetic-thermal therapies has been shown to cause increased necrosis of tumor cells compared to other groups (Fig. 5c). In order to further assess the safety of 177Lu-MMS@PDA in large animals, a healthy beagle dog was embolized the magnetic radiomicrospheres (Fig. 7a). The 177Lu-MMS@PDA were injected into the hepatic artery via femoral cannula under the DSA guidance (Fig. 7b). Angiography of the left lobe of the liver demonstrated that embolization had been successfully completed. SPECT/CT was performed on the beagle dog at 2, 7, and 14 days, and the results in Fig. 7c demonstrate that the radioactive signal was predominantly concentrated in the left lobe of the liver, with no significant radio-signal observed in other organs. This validates the high radio-stability of 177Lu-MMS@PDA in the large animals. At 21 days postoperatively, MRI images showed limited T1WI low signal and T2WI high signal in the left lobe of the liver, which may be due to the post irradiation liver tissue edema (Fig. 7d). In addition, the liver and kidney function indices of the beagle dog were examined, yielding results consistent with those observed in the rabbit's experiment. Postoperative assessments revealed that all indices returned to their normal levels (Fig. 7f). After a period of two months, no radioactive signals were detected, and the beagle was adopted. Over the subsequent ten months, no further abnormalities were observed in the beagle dog (Fig. 7e). Conclusion

We have demonstrated that a radio-magnetic microspheres 177Lu-MMS@PDA capable of combining TARE and magnetic-thermal therapy for embolization of HCC. The magnetic microsphere MMS is able to induce tumor cell death by generating heat under the action of a high-frequency alternating magnetic field. Following straightforward labeling of 177Lu with a simple method, PDA coating enhances the radiostability. In vivo experiments demonstrate that 177Lu-MMS@PDA can be stably embolized in the liver. Furthermore, tumor suppression experiment demonstrates that magnetic heating is effective in sensitizing radiation therapy. The combination of these modalities has been shown to yield superior therapeutic outcomes in comparison with monotherapy, while concomitantly avoiding the occurrence of significant adverse effects. Meanwhile, the safety of 177Lu-MMS@PDA was verified in large animal. Compared to clinically available TARE and TACE, 177Lu-MMS@PDA enables localization of microspheres via SPECT imaging and monitoring of radionuclide distribution within the body. Additionally, the magnetic microspheres allow patients to undergo multiple non-invasive treatments to eliminate residual tumors. Furthermore, for patients with HCC and extrahepatic metastasis, 177Lu-MMS@PDA can be used for transarterial radioembolization to treat hypervascular metastatic tumors. In addition, combining 177Lu-MMS@PDA with immunotherapy may hold research potential for treating extrahepatic metastases. The potential of 177Lu-MMS@PDA extends further, with the prospect of contributing to the advancement of HCC treatment.

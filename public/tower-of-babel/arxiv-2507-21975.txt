Swan modules and homotopy types after a single stabilisation
Tommy Hofmann, John Nicholson

Abstract
. We study Swan modules, which are a special class of projective modules over integral group rings, and their consequences for the homotopy classification of CW-complexes. We show that there exists a non-free stably free Swan module, thus resolving Problem A4 in the 1979 Problem List of C. T. C. Wall. As an application we show that, in all dimensions n ≡ 3 n\equiv 3 mod 4 4 , there exist finite n n -complexes which are homotopy equivalent after stabilising with multiple copies of S n S^{n} , but not after a single stabilisation. This answers a question of M. N. Dyer. We also resolve a question of S. Plotnick concerning Swan modules associated to group automorphisms and, as an application, obtain a short and direct proof that there exists a group with k k -periodic cohomology which does not have free period k k . In contrast to the original proof our R. J. Milgram, our proof circumvents the need to compute the Swan finiteness obstruction.

1.
Introduction

A
Swan module
over a finite group
G
is a
\mathbb{Z}G
-module
defined by a left ideal
(N,r)\leq\mathbb{Z}G
where
N=\sum_{g\in G}g
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
is an integer coprime to
|G|
.
These modules were introduced by Swan
[
Swa60b
]
, who showed that
(N,r)
is projective and is determined up to isomorphism by the value of
r\bmod|G|
. Consequently, we often write
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
.
Swan modules play an important role in topology through the classification of spherical space forms
[
Swa60a
,
TW71
,
LT73
]
and more general CW-complexes
[
Joh03
,
Nic24a
,
Nic24b
]
.
They have also found applications in algebraic number theory, particularly through determining the Galois module structure of the ring of integers of a number field
[
Tay81
,
Tay84
,
GRRS99
]
.

A classical question, which arose in work of Dyer
[
Dye76
, Note (c) p276]
and Plotnick
[
Plo82
, p98]
, is whether every stably free Swan module is free.
A finite group
G
for which this holds is said to have the
weak cancellation property

[
Joh04
, p582]
.
The case where
G
has periodic cohomology, which is most relevant for applications to topology, appeared in Wall’s problem list
[
Wal79a
, Problem A4]
(see also
[
Joh04
, Section 2]
,
[
Nic24b
, Question 7.2]
).
We will show:

Theorem A
.

There exists a finite group
G
and
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
such that
(N,r)
is a non-free stably free
\mathbb{Z}G
-module. Furthermore,
G
can be taken to have periodic cohomology.

In particular, we can take
G=Q_{56}
to be the quaternion group of order 56 and
r=15
. The main step in the proof is showing that
(N,15)
is not free. This is achieved as a result of extensive computations of unit groups of rings which arise in two pullback squares (see
Eqs.

1
and
3
).

We say two finite
n
-complexes
X
and
Y
are
stably equivalent
if
X\vee kS^{n}\simeq Y\vee kS^{n}
for some
k\geq 0
. Is one stabilisation always enough, i.e. does
k=1
always suffice? This question, with various conditions on the CW-complexes, appeared in
[
Dye79
, Problem (c)]
,
[
HAMS93
, p124]
and
[
Nic21c
, Problem B2]
.
The analogous question for simple homotopy equivalence (
\simeq_{s}
) appeared in
[
Dye81
, Question 1]
.
Using the examples from
A
, we will show:

Theorem B
.

Let
n\geq 1
with
n\equiv 3\,\,\text{mod}\,4
.
Then there exist finite
n
-complexes
X
and
Y
such that
X\vee 2S^{n}\simeq Y\vee 2S^{n}
but
X\vee S^{n}\not\simeq Y\vee S^{n}
.
Furthermore, we can assume that
X
and
Y
have finite fundamental group,
(n-1)
-connected universal covers, and satisfy
X\vee 2S^{n}\simeq_{s}Y\vee 2S^{n}
.

The fact that
G
can be taken to be finite is of interest since it was shown by Browning
[
Bro78
, Theorem 5.4]
(see also
[
Nic24b
, Corollary 4.7]
) that, for

even, one stabilisation is enough for finite
n
-complexes with finite fundamental group and
(n-1)
-connected universal covers.
We show that this also holds when
n\equiv 1\,\,\text{mod}\,4
(
3.7
). Hence the dimension restrictions in

are optimal for finite
n
-complexes satisfying these conditions.

The examples in
B
are obtained by taking

to be the closed
n
-manifold
S^{n}/Q_{56}
and defining

via the following general construction (see

for more details):

Construction 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
(N,r)
be a stably free Swan module for some
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
. Given a (connected) finite
n
-complex
X
with fundamental group
G
and
(n-1)
-connected universal cover, we construct a finite
n
-complex
X_{r}
as follows:

1.

Let
m:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
be multiplication by a representative of
r^{-1}
, let
\varepsilon:C_{0}(\widetilde{X})\twoheadrightarrow H_{0}(\widetilde{X})\cong\mathbb{Z}
be the standard map and define

m^{*}(C_{*}(\widetilde{X}))=(C_{n}(\widetilde{X})\xrightarrow{\partial_{n}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}C_{1}(\widetilde{X})\xrightarrow{(\partial_{1},0)}C_{0}(\widetilde{X})\times_{\varepsilon,m}\mathbb{Z}).

We have
C_{0}(\widetilde{X})\times_{\varepsilon,m}\mathbb{Z}\cong(I_{G},r^{-1})\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{k}\cong(N,r)\oplus\mathbb{Z}G^{k}
for some

by
[
Nic24b
, Proposition 3.4 & Lemma 4.12]
.
This is stably free so, by adding extensions of the form
\mathbb{Z}G\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathbb{Z}G
, this is chain homotopy equivalent to an exact sequence
C_{*}^{\prime}
of free
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules.

2.

Since
n\geq 3
, there exists a finite
n
-complex
X_{r}
with fundamental group
G
such that
C_{*}(\widetilde{X}_{r})
is chain homotopy equivalent to
C_{*}^{\prime}
(see
[
Joh12
, Corollary 8.27]
).

The complex
X_{r}
has fundamental group
G
,
(n-1)
-connected universal cover,
\pi_{n}(X_{r})\cong\pi_{n}(X)
as
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules, and
k
-invariant
k^{n+1}(X_{r})=r\cdot k^{n+1}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}/|G|
(
\cong H^{n+1}(G;\pi_{n}(X))
).

To prove that

and

are not homotopy equivalent, it remains to show that

do not coincide under the combined action of
\Aut(Q_{56})
and
\Aut_{\mathbb{Z}G}(\pi_{n}(X))
on the

-invariants. Our proof uses that

and so

.
Note that

is typically hard to compute so, whilst this construction applies to any

and stably free Swan module

, showing that

is difficult in general.

We now give two consequences of
B
. The first follows directly from the properties of
1.1
listed above.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let

be even. Then there exist homotopically distinct finite
n
-complexes
X
and
Y
with fundamental group
Q_{56}
and
(n-1)
-connected universal covers such that
\pi_{n}(X)\cong\pi_{n}(Y)
as
\mathbb{Z}Q_{56}
-modules. The same holds for

provided
Q_{56}
has the

Such examples have only previously been known for finite abelian fundamental groups
[
SD79
]
. The case
n=2
would require part (2) of
1.1
to apply in that setting, which does so if and only if

has the D2 property (see, for example,
[

, Proposition 5.1]
). The D2 property for quaternion groups was studied by the authors in
[
HN25
]
. In the notation given there, it would suffice to construct a finite presentation

for

such that
\Psi(\mathcal{P})=[(N,15)]
.

We next consider applications to manifolds. It is an open problem to determine whether there exist closed smooth
2n
-manifolds
M
and
N
such that
M\#2(S^{n}\times S^{n})\cong N\#2(S^{n}\times S^{n})
are diffeomorphic but
M\#(S^{n}\times S^{n})\not\cong N\#(S^{n}\times S^{n})
, with particular interest in the case where
n=2
and
M
and
N
are homeomorphic.
This question admits many variations for manifolds with boundary, and it was shown by Kang that examples exist for homeomorphic compact smooth
4
-manifolds with boundary
[
Kan22
]
.
Let
\natural
denote boundary connected sum.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
with
n\equiv 3\,\,\text{mod}\,4
. Then there exist smooth compact
(2n+1)
-manifolds
M
and
N
such that
M\,\natural\,2(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})\cong N\,\natural\,2(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})
but
M\,\natural\,(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})\not\cong N\,\natural\,(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})
. In fact, we have
M\,\natural\,(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})\not\simeq N\,\natural\,(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})
.

This follows directly from
B
by taking
M
and
N
to be smooth regular neighbourhoods of embeddings of
X
and
Y
into
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
respectively. Then
X\vee kS^{n}\simeq_{s}M\,\natural\,k(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})
for all
k\geq 0
(and similarly for
Y
). This implies that
M\,\natural\,(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})\not\simeq N\,\natural\,(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})
. Since
M\,\natural\,2(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})
and
N\,\natural\,2(S^{n}\times D^{n+1})
are both simple homotopy equivalent to
X\vee 2S^{n}
and embed in
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
with

, they are trivial thickenings and so are diffeomorphic
[
Wal66
, p76]
.

We have
\partial(M\,\natural\,k(S^{n}\times D^{n+1}))=\partial M\#k(S^{n}\times S^{n})
for all
k\geq 0
(and similarly for
N
) and so
\partial M\#2(S^{n}\times S^{n})\cong\partial N\#2(S^{n}\times S^{n})
. This leads to a potential strategy, in dimensions
2n\equiv 6\,\,\text{mod}\,8
, for tackling the original problem for closed manifolds. However, determining whether
\partial M\#(S^{n}\times S^{n})
and
\partial N\#(S^{n}\times S^{n})
are homotopy equivalent for a given pair
M,N
appears to be a difficult problem. Further discussion is beyond the scope of this article.

Question 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
with
n\equiv 3\,\,\text{mod}\,4
. Do there exist compact
(2n+1)
-manifolds
M
and
N
as in
Corollary

1.3
such that
\partial M\#(S^{n}\times S^{n})\not\cong\partial N\#(S^{n}\times S^{n})
?

We now give our third main result which, whilst it concerns Swan modules, is not related directly to Theorems
A
or
B
. Recall that a finite group
G
has
free period
k
if there exists a
k
-periodic resolution of finitely generated free
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules. Such groups necessarily have
k
-periodic cohomology. The question of whether or not the converse holds originated in work of Swan
[
Swa60b
]
and was a major motivating question during the early development of algebraic K-theory, particularly in light of the fact that finite
3
-manifold groups necessarily have free period
4
. The question featured in Wall’s problem list
[
Wal79a
, Problem A3]
and was eventually resolved by Milgram
[
Mil85
]
who showed that certain groups with
4
-periodic cohomology of the form
Q(2^{n}a,b,c)
do not have free period
4
(see also
[
Dav81
,
DM85
]
).

If
G
is a finite group with
k
-periodic cohomology, then
H^{k}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/|G|
and the functor
H^{k}(-;\mathbb{Z})
induces a map
\psi_{k}:\Aut(G)\to(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
. It was shown independently by Dyer
[
Dye76
, Note (b) p276]
and Davis
[
Dav83
]
that if
G
has free period
k
, then
(N,\psi_{k}(\theta))
is stably free for all
\theta\in\Aut(G)
.
Plotnick
[
Plo82
, p98]
asked whether this holds in general (see also
[
Dav83
, p488]
,
[
Nic24b
, Question 7.3]
).
By work of Bentzen–Madsen
[
BM83
, p448]
, this holds for
Q(8,p,q)
where
p,q
are distinct odd primes (see
[
BM83
, Corollary 4.4]
,
[
Mad83
, p231]
).
We will show:

Theorem C
.

Let
k\geq 1
with
k\equiv 4\,\,\text{mod}\,8
. Then there exists a finite group
G
with
k
-periodic cohomology and an automorphism
\theta\in\Aut(G)
such that
(N,\psi_{k}(\theta))
is not stably free.

In particular, for each
k
, we can take
G=Q(16,5,1)
and
\theta\in\Aut(G)
such that
\psi_{k}(\theta)=9
.
By combining this with Dyer’s result that
(N,\psi_{k}(\theta))
is stably free for all
\theta\in\Aut(G)
provided

has free period

, we obtain:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
k\geq 1
with
k\equiv 4\,\,\text{mod}\,8
. Then there exists a finite group with
k
-periodic cohomology which does not have free period
k
.

This was previously established by Milgram
[
Mil85
]
, who used that, if
G
has
k
-periodic cohomology, there exists an element
\sigma_{k}(G)\in\widetilde{K}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}G)/\{[(N,r)]:r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}\}
known as the

which vanishes if and only if

has free period
k
.
He used pullback squares to show that
\sigma_{4}(Q(8,p,q))\neq 0
for certain primes

.
Using an analogous approach, Davis
[
Dav81
]
showed that
\sigma_{4}(Q(16,p,1))\neq 0
for certain primes

, including the case

. The extension to the case

for

2\cdot\sigma_{4}(G)=0
whenever

-periodic cohomology
[

]
, combined with the standard fact that
\sigma_{4i}(G)=i\cdot\sigma_{4}(G)

In contrast, we make no use of the Swan finiteness obstruction or Wall’s theorem and instead we need only show that
[(N,9)]\neq 0\in\widetilde{K}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}Q(16,5,1))
.
Whilst it is possible to prove this by hand, we use algorithms of Bley–Boltje
[
BB06
]
and Bley–Wilson
[
BW09
]
which explicitly compute
\widetilde{K}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}G)
for a finite group
G
.
Both algorithms have been implemented in
Magma

[
BCP97
]
and the code we used can be found in
Appendix

.

We now give two more applications of
C
. The first concerns projective resolutions.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
k\geq 1
with
k\equiv 4\,\,\text{mod}\,8
.
Then there exists a finite group
G
and an exact sequence of finitely generated projective
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules

\mathscr{R}=(0\to\mathbb{Z}\to P_{k-1}\to P_{k-2}\to\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}\to 0)

whose Euler class

does not coincide with

for some
\theta\in\Aut(G)
, where
\mathscr{R}_{\theta}=\{(P_{i})_{\theta}\}_{i=0}^{k}
denotes

We have
e(\mathscr{R}_{\theta})=e(\mathscr{R})_{\theta}
and so this equivalently says that

The result follows from the fact that

This gives the first example where the action of

Our final application resolves a question of Plotnick in the affirmative

Corollary 1.7

Let

2.1.
Preliminaries on Swan modules

Let
G
be a finite group. Recall from the introduction that a Swan module is a
\mathbb{Z}G
-module of the form
(N,r)=\mathbb{Z}G\cdot N+\mathbb{Z}G\cdot r\leq\mathbb{Z}G
where
N=\sum_{g\in G}g\in\mathbb{Z}G
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
is such that
(r,|G|)=1
. If
r\equiv r^{\prime}\,\,\text{mod}\,|G|
, then
(N,r)\cong(N,r^{\prime})

[
Swa60b
, Lemma 6.1 (a)]
and so
(N,r)
is well-defined for
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
. If
r,r^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
, then
(N,r)\oplus(N,r^{\prime})\cong(N,rr^{\prime})\oplus\mathbb{Z}G

[
Swa60b
, Lemma 6.1 (c)]
. This implies that Swan modules are projective since
(N,r)\oplus(N,r^{-1})\cong\mathbb{Z}G^{2}
. Hence we obtain a group homomorphism

S:(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}\to\widetilde{K}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}G),\quad r\mapsto[(N,r)]

which we will refer to as the
Swan map
. Define the
Swan subgroup
to be
T(G)=\IM(S)
.

To determine when Swan modules are free or stably free, we must determine the sets:

\FF(G)=\{r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}:(N,r)\cong\mathbb{Z}G\},\quad\SF(G)=\ker(S_{G})

which are both subgroups of
(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
; in the case of
\FF(G)
, this follows from the fact that
(N,r)\otimes(N,r^{\prime})\cong(N,rr^{\prime})
.
We have
\FF(G)\leq\SF(G)
and
T(G)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}/\SF(G)
. By
[
Swa60b
, Lemma 6.3]
, we have that

\FF(G)=\IM(\varepsilon:(\mathbb{Z}G/N)^{\times}\to(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times})

where
\varepsilon
is induced by the augmentation map. This can be made explicit as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
r\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
and let
u_{r}\in(\mathbb{Z}G/N)^{\times}
be such that
\varepsilon(u_{r})=r
.
Let
s=r^{-1}\in(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
, let
t\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
rs=1+t|G|
and let
u_{s}=u_{r}^{-1}\in(\mathbb{Z}G/N)^{\times}
.
Let
\widetilde{u}_{r},\widetilde{u}_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}G
be the unique representatives of
u_{r},u_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}G/N
such that
\varepsilon(\widetilde{u}_{r})=r
,
\varepsilon(\widetilde{u}_{s})=s
.
Then there are mutually inverse isomorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}G
-modules:

f:\mathbb{Z}G\to(N,r),\,\,1\mapsto\widetilde{u}_{s}r-tN,\qquad g:(N,r)\to\mathbb{Z}G,\,\,N\mapsto N,\,r\mapsto\widetilde{u}_{r}.

Proof.

We can check directly that
f\circ g=\id
and
g\circ f=\id
(see also
[
Swa60b
, Lemma 6.3]
).
∎

Let
G
be a finite group with
k
-periodic cohomology. Then
H^{k}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/|G|
(see
[
CE56
, XII.11.1]
) and the functor
H^{k}(-;\mathbb{Z})
induces a map

\psi_{k}:\Aut(G)\to(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}

where we have used the identifications
\Aut(H^{k}(G;\mathbb{Z}))\cong\Aut(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
. We define
\Aut_{k}(G):=\IM(\psi_{k})\leq(\mathbb{Z}/|G|)^{\times}
.
Since

also has

-periodic cohomology for all
i\geq 1
, we can consider
\psi_{ik}
for any
i\geq 1
.
The following can be found in
[
Nic24b
, Lemma 6.7]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group with

-periodic cohomology. If
i\geq 1
and
\theta\in\Aut(G)
, then
\psi_{ik}(\theta)=\psi_{k}(\theta)^{i}
. In particular,
\Aut_{ik}(G)=(\Aut_{k}(G))^{i}
.

2.2.
Stably free Swan modules

From now on,
p
will be an odd prime and we identify
Q_{8p}=\langle x,y\mid x^{2p}=y^{2},yxy^{-1}=x^{-1}\rangle
.
We begin by showing the following using the results of Bentzen–Madsen
[
BM83
]
which compute
T(Q_{8p})
for
p
an odd prime.
For an integer
m
coprime to
p
, let
\ord_{p}(m)
denote the order of
m
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
and let
\left(\frac{m}{p}\right)\in\{\pm 1\}
denote the Legendre symbol for the quadratic residue of

mod

.

Proposition 2.3
.

Remark 2.4
.

This contradicts
[
LT73
, Theorem 1]
, which claims erroneously that
(N,r)
is always stably free over
Q_{8p}
provided
r\equiv\pm 1\,\,\text{mod}\,8
. For example, if
p=5
and
r=7\in(\mathbb{Z}/8p)^{\times}
, then
r\equiv-1\,\,\text{mod}\,8
but
(N,r)
is not stably free since
p\equiv-3\,\,\text{mod}\,8
and
\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=-1
.

Proof.

In
[
BM83
, Theorem 3.5]
, it is shown that

T(Q_{8p})\cong S(1)\oplus S(p)

where
S(1)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
and

S(p)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2,&\text{if {(}$p\equiv\pm 3\,\,\text{mod}\,8${)} or {(}$p\equiv 1\,\,\text{mod}\,8$ and $\ord_{p}(2)$ is even{)}}\\
0,&\text{if {(}$p\equiv-1\,\,\text{mod}\,8${)} or {(}$p\equiv 1\,\,\text{mod}\,8$ and $\ord_{p}(2)$ is odd{)}.}\end{cases}

Let
S=(S_{1},S_{2}):(\mathbb{Z}/8p)^{\times}\to T(Q_{8p})\cong S(1)\oplus S(p)
denote the two components of the Swan map.
In
[
BM83
, Addendum 3.6]
, it is shown that
\ker(S_{1})=\{r:r\equiv\pm 1\,\,\text{mod}\,8\}
and that

\ker(S_{2})=\begin{cases}\{r:\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=1\},&\text{if $p\equiv-3\,\,\text{mod}\,8$, or $p\equiv 1\,\,\text{mod}\,8$, $\ord_{p}(2)$ is even}\\
\{r:\psi(r)\cdot\left(\frac{r}{p}\right)=1\},&\text{if $p\equiv 3\,\,\text{mod}\,8$.}\end{cases}

The result follows since
\SF(Q_{8p})=\ker(S)=\ker(S_{1})\cap\ker(S_{2})
.
∎

\{X\vee S^{n}:X\in\HT_{\min}(G,n)\}\cong\SF(G)/(\Aut_{n+1}(G)\cdot\FF(G)).

Since
n\equiv 1\,\,\text{mod}\,4
,
k
is even (see, for example,
[
Joh03
, Proposition 40.2]
) and
k
divides

, we must have that
k\equiv 2\,\,\text{mod}\,4
. We will show:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
k\geq 2
with
k\equiv 2\,\,\text{mod}\,4
. If
G
is a finite group with
k
-periodic cohomology, then
G
satisfies the Eichler condition.

By
[
Swa62
]
, this implies that
\mathbb{Z}G
has projective cancellation and so stably free Swan modules are free. Hence
\SF(G)=\FF(G)
and so
|\{X\vee S^{n}:X\in\HT_{\min}(G,n)\}|=1
, which completes the proof of
3.7
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.8
.

By
[
DM85
, Theorem 1.10]
, we have
\per(G)=\lcm_{H\in\mathcal{H}}(\per(H))
where
\per(\cdot)
denotes the minimal cohomological period of a group with periodic cohomology and
\mathcal{H}
denotes the set of subgroups
H\leq G
which are
p
-hyperelementary for some prime
p
.

If
Q_{2^{n}}\leq G
for some
n\geq 3
then, since
Q_{2^{n}}
is
2
-hyperelementary, we have
4=\per(Q_{2^{n}})\mid\per(G)
. This is a contradiction since
\per(G)\equiv 2\,\,\text{mod}\,4
.
Since
G
has periodic cohomology, its Sylow subgroups are cyclic or quaternionic
[
CE56
, XII.11.6]
. Since
Q_{2^{n}}
is not a subgroup of
G
, this implies that all the Sylow subgroups of
G
are cyclic. It now follows from a theorem of Burnside (see
[
DM85
, Theorem 1.8]
) that
G
is a split metacyclic group, i.e.
G\cong C_{m}\rtimes_{(r)}C_{n}
where
(n,m)=1
,
r\in\mathbb{Z}/m
is such that
r^{n}\equiv 1\,\,\text{mod}\,m
and
\rtimes_{(r)}
denotes the semidirect product where the generator
y\in C_{n}
acts on the generator
x\in C_{m}
via
x\mapsto x^{r}
.
By
[
Joh03
, p165]
, we can assume that
m
is odd.

Suppose for contradiction that
G
fails the Eichler condition. Then
G\twoheadrightarrow H
for
H
a binary polyhedral group (see
[
Nic21a
, Proposition 1.5]
), and this induces a quotient of the Sylow
2
-subgroups
G_{2}\twoheadrightarrow H_{2}
(see
[
Nic21b
, Lemma 6.6]
). Since
G_{2}
is cyclic,
H_{2}
must be cyclic and so
H\cong Q_{4a}
for some
a\geq 3
odd.
By
[
Nic24a
, Lemma 2.6]
,
G\twoheadrightarrow Q_{4a}
if and only if
a\mid m
and
r\equiv-1\,\,\text{mod}\,a
. This implies that
m=as
for some
s\geq 1
and, since
4\mid|Q_{4a}|\mid|G|
and
m
is odd, we have
n=4t
for some
t\geq 1
. Hence
G\cong C_{as}\rtimes_{(r)}C_{4t}
where
a,s
are odd and coprime to
n
, and
r\in\mathbb{Z}/as
is such that
r^{4t}\equiv 1\,\,\text{mod}\,as
and
r\equiv-1\,\,\text{mod}\,a
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{Z}/4t\to(\mathbb{Z}/as)^{\times}
,
1\mapsto r
denote the action in the semidirect product. By
[
DM85
, p229]
, we have
\per(G)=2\cdot|\IM(\psi)|
. If
q:(\mathbb{Z}/as)^{\times}\twoheadrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/a)^{\times}
denotes reduction mod

, then
q(\psi(1))=-1
and so
q(\IM(\psi))=\{\pm 1\}
. This implies that
2\mid|\IM(\psi)|
and so
4\mid\per(G)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

4.1.
Preliminaries on the action of

Let

denote the group of inner automorphisms and let

Lemma 4.1

Let

\theta\in\Inn(G)

\psi_{k}(\theta)=1
. In particular,
\psi_{k}

The following was proven independently by Dyer

Proposition 4.2

4.2.
Computing

Let

Let

From now on, we will let

The aim of this section will be to establish the following. Throughout, we will write

Theorem 4.3
.

Let

be an odd prime and let

\IM(\psi_{4}^{G})=((\mathbb{Z}/16p)^{\times})^{2}
.

Our strategy will be to relate

to
\psi_{4}^{Q_{16}}
and
\psi_{4}^{C_{p}}

First recall that, by

factors as

and similarly for

and

Lemma 4.4
.

For
i\in(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times}
and
j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
, let
\theta_{i,j}:x\mapsto x^{i},y\mapsto y,z\mapsto z^{j}
. This defines an automorphism
\theta_{i,j}\in\Aut(G)
. Then:

\Out(G)=\{[\theta_{i,j}]:i\in\{1,3\},j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\}\cong C_{2}\times C_{p-1}

where
[\theta_{i,j}]
denotes the image of
\theta_{i,j}
in
\Out(G)=\Aut(G)/{\Inn(G)}

Proof.

We will begin by showing that

\displaystyle\Aut(G)=\{\theta_{i,j,k,\ell}:x\mapsto z^{k}x^{i},y\mapsto x^{2j}y,z\mapsto z^{\ell}\mid i\in(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times},j\in\mathbb{Z}/4,k\in\mathbb{Z}/p,\ell\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\}

\displaystyle=(\{\theta_{1,0,k,1}:k\in\mathbb{Z}/p\}\rtimes\{\theta_{1,0,0,\ell}:\ell\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\})\times(\{\theta_{1,j,0,1}:j\in\mathbb{Z}/4\}\rtimes\{\theta_{i,0,0,1}:i\in(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times}\}).

We can verify directly that
\theta_{i,j,k,\ell}\in\Aut(G)
and that they form a subgroup as described.

First note
C_{p}=\langle z\rangle\leq G
contains all the elements of order
p
in
G
and so must be a characteristic subgroup. In particular, there is an induced map
\Psi:\Aut(G)\to\Aut(Q_{16})
,
\theta\mapsto\overline{\theta}
.
Let
\varphi\in\Ker(\Psi)
.
Since
\varphi(z)
has order
p
, we must have
\varphi(z)=z^{\ell}
for some
\ell
.
Since
\overline{\varphi(x)}=\overline{x}\in G/C_{p}
and
\overline{\varphi(y)}=\overline{y}\in G/C_{p}
, we have that
\varphi(x)=z^{k}x
and
\varphi(y)=z^{t}y
for some
k,t\in\mathbb{Z}/p
. Since
y^{4}=1
, we have
(z^{t}y)^{4}=\varphi(y^{4})=1
but
(z^{t}y)^{4}=z^{4t}
and so
z^{4t}=1
, which implies that

since
z
has order
p
. This implies that
\varphi=\theta_{1,0,k,\ell}
, and so
\ker(\Psi)=\{\theta_{1,0,k,\ell}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}/p,\ell\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\}
.

By
[
Swa60b
, Proposition 8.2]
, we have

\Aut(Q_{16})=\{\alpha_{i,j}:x\mapsto x^{i},y\mapsto x^{j}y\mid i\in(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times},j\in\mathbb{Z}/8\}
.
Note that
\Psi(\{\theta_{i,j,0,1}:i\in(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times},j\in\mathbb{Z}/4\})=\{\alpha_{i,2j}:i\in(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times},j\in\mathbb{Z}/4\}\leq\Aut(Q_{16})
.
We claim this is the image of
\Psi
. If not, then
\alpha_{i,j}\in\IM(\Psi)
for some
j
odd,which implies that
\alpha_{1,1}\in\IM(\Psi)
by composition with automorphisms of the form
\theta_{i,j,0,1}
. Suppose
\varphi\in\Aut(G)
is such that
\Psi(\varphi)=\alpha_{1,1}
. Then
\overline{\varphi(y)}=\overline{xy}\in G/C_{p}
which implies that
\varphi(y)=z^{t}xy
for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}/p
and, as usual,
\varphi(z)=z^{\ell}
for some
\ell\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
. Since
yzy^{-1}=z
, we have
\varphi(y)\varphi(z)\varphi(y)^{-1}=\varphi(z)
but
(z^{t}xy)z^{\ell}(z^{t}xy)^{-1}=z^{-\ell}\neq z^{\ell}
since the fact that
z
has order
p
and
\ell\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
implies that
z^{2\ell}\neq 1
. This is a contradiction and so
\IM(\Psi)
is as required.
Next note that the map
\alpha_{i,2j}\mapsto\theta_{i,j,0,1}
gives a splitting of
\Psi
. Since the automorphisms
\theta_{i,j,0,1}
and
\theta_{1,0,k,\ell}
commute for all values of

, this implies that
\Aut(G)
is a direct product of the subgroups
\{\theta_{1,0,k,\ell}:k\in\mathbb{Z}/p,\ell\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\}
and
\{\theta_{i,j,0,1}:i\in(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times},j\in\mathbb{Z}/4\}
. The evaluation of
\Aut(G)
now follows from the fact that
\theta_{i,j,k,\ell}=\theta_{i,j,0,1}\circ\theta_{1,0,k,\ell}
.

In order to determine
\Out(G)
, next note that

\Inn(G)=\{\theta_{i,j,k,1}\mid i\in\{\pm 1\},j\in\mathbb{Z}/4,k\in\mathbb{Z}/p\}\cong C_{p}\times(C_{4}\rtimes C_{2}).

This can be shown directly by noting that conjugation by a general element
x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}
for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}/8
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}/2
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}/p
gives an automorphism
\varphi:x\mapsto z^{2k(-1)^{i}}x^{(-1)^{j}},y\mapsto x^{2i}y,z\mapsto z^{(-1)^{i}}
.
By quotienting, it now follows immediately that

\Out(G)=\{\overline{\theta}_{i,0,0,j}:i\in\{1,3\},j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\}
, where the automorphism
\theta_{i,0,0,j}
coincides with

in the statement.
∎

By
[
Swa60b
, Proposition 8.2]
, we have that

\Aut(Q_{16})=\{\alpha_{i,j}:x\mapsto x^{i},y\mapsto x^{j}y\mid i\in(\mathbb{Z}/8)^{\times},j\in\mathbb{Z}/8\}
and it is easy to show that
\Out(Q_{16})=\{[\alpha_{i,j}]:i\in\{1,3\},j\in\{0,1\}\}
. We have
\Out(C_{p})=\Aut(C_{p})=\{\beta_{i}:z\mapsto z^{i}\mid i\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\}
. With these choices of representatives, we can now define

\Out(G)\to\Out(Q_{16})\times\Out(C_{p}),\quad[\theta_{i,j}]\mapsto([\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{Q_{16}}],[\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{C_{p}}])=([\alpha_{i,1}],[\beta_{j}]),

which is a group homomorphism.

Lemma 4.5
.

The standard inclusion maps induce an isomorphism

(i_{Q_{16}}^{*},i_{C_{p}}^{*}):H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{4}(Q_{16};\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{4}(C_{p};\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Since
|G|=16p
, there is a primary decomposition
H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z})_{(2)}\oplus H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z})_{(p)}
. The maps
i_{Q_{16}}^{*}
and
i_{C_{p}}^{*}
above factor through the induced maps
i_{Q_{16}}^{*}:H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z})_{(2)}\to H^{4}(Q_{16};\mathbb{Z})
and
i_{C_{p}}^{*}:H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z})_{(p)}\to H^{4}(C_{p};\mathbb{Z})
. By
[
CE56
, XII.10.1]
, these maps are injective since
Q_{16}
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup and
C_{p}
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup. It follows that
(i_{Q_{16}}^{*},i_{C_{p}}^{*})
is injective.

Since
G
,
Q_{16}
and
C_{p}
all have 4-periodic cohomology, we have that
H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/16p
,
H^{4}(Q_{16};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/16
and
H^{4}(C_{p};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/p

[
CE56
, XII.11.1]
. So
(i_{Q_{16}}^{*},i_{C_{p}}^{*})
is an injective map between finite groups of order

, and hence is bijective.
∎

Since
H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/|G|
is cyclic, every subgroup is characteristic and so the surjective maps

and
i^{*}_{C_{p}}
above induce maps on the automorphism groups. That is, there is an induced map

(r_{Q_{16}},r_{C_{p}}):\Aut(H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z}))\to\Aut(H^{4}(Q_{16};\mathbb{Z}))\times\Aut(H^{4}(C_{p};\mathbb{Z})).

The following relates the two maps just defined with
\psi_{4}^{G}
,
\psi_{4}^{Q_{16}}
and
\psi_{4}^{C_{p}}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

There is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Out(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Out(Q_{16})\times\Out(C_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underbrace{\Aut(H^{4}(G;\mathbb{Z}))}_{\cong(\mathbb{Z}/16p)^{\times}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underbrace{\Aut(H^{4}(Q_{16};\mathbb{Z}))}_{\cong(\mathbb{Z}/16)^{\times}}\times\underbrace{\Aut(H^{4}(C_{p};\mathbb{Z}))}_{\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{4}^{G}}
\scriptstyle[\theta_{i,j}]\mapsto([\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{Q_{16}}],[\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{C_{p}}])
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{4}^{Q_{16}}\times\psi_{4}^{C_{p}}}
\scriptstyle(r_{Q_{16}},r_{C_{p}})

Proof.

Let
[\theta_{i,j}]\in\Out(G)
where
i\in\{1,3\}
,
j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}
. It suffices to check that
r_{Q_{16}}(\theta_{i,j}^{*})=(\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{Q_{16}})^{*}
and
r_{C_{p}}(\theta^{*})=(\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{C_{p}})^{*}
.
For each

and

and

For each subgroup

or

, we have
\theta_{i,j}\circ i_{H}=i_{H}\circ(\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{H})

Proof of
4.3

Note that we can choose the identifications

r(\IM(\psi_{4}^{G}))=\{(\psi_{4}^{Q_{16}}(\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{Q_{16}}),\psi_{4}^{C_{p}}(\theta_{i,j}\hskip-2.13394pt\mid_{C_{p}})):i\in\{1,3\},j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\times}\}\leq\IM(\psi_{4}^{Q_{16}})\times\IM(\psi_{4}^{C_{p}})

4.3.
Proof of

Let
k\geq 1
with
k\equiv 4\bmod 8
, so that

Both algorithms have been implemented in

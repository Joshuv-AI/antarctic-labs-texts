Enhancing Hindi OCR robustness with CRNN-ResNet50: a data augmentation approach on the devanagari dataset
Authors: Sujeet Kumar, Suman Sourabh, Jayadeep Pati, Siddharth Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOptical character recognition is a technology that turns texts and scanned documents into digital formats. The practical applications of OCR systems face a lot of challenges because of heterogeneity in scripts, font styles, and different quality images arising during their usage. The proposed work attempts to improve the robustness of OCR models built for the Hindi language by addressing the deficiencies inherent in the widely used IIIT-HW-Dev dataset. Toward this end, two methods of data augmentation are proposed: 1) synthetic images with half characters and conjuncts for complex words, and 2) various image degradations to simulate the real world. The augmented training set is used to train the two deep architectures, namely Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs) and ResNet-50. Comparisons and in-depth experiments further reveal the merits of the suggested data augmentation techniques: the top performance obtained by the CRNN model exceeds current approaches. On the test set, CRNN achieved a Character Error Rate (CER) of 2.14% and Word Error Rate (WER) of 7.96%, much beyond the CER of 3.27% and WER of 11.82% achieved by the ResNet-50 model. Qualitatively, there was robustness in the case of complex words and images that were also deteriorated. The augmented dataset and trained models can be downloaded by anyone to promote more research in Hindi OCR. So, developing robust OCR systems for complex scripts starts from variability-rich and representative training data along with specific architectures, such as the CRNN architecture.Keywords: Optical character recognition, Convolutional neural networks, Data augmentation, Hindi OCR, IIIT-HW-Dev datasetSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Optical Character Recognition, or OCR, is a key technology that converts text contained in images or scanned documents into machine-readable and editable forms1,2. This technique has various applications in different fields, such as digitalizing documents, archiving, content management systems, and extracting information from historical manuscripts and books3,4. However, it has been noted that the performance of OCRs varies with script, font, and image quality variations of real-world applications such as: low-resolution images, skewed or blurred images, and non-uniform illumination conditions5,6. The current progress in the adjacent areas, e.g. speech recognition, has utilized the transformer-based models to process more complex language formats, which proves that they can lead to better recognition of different scripts7.Of particular interest is the application of the development of robust OCR systems for the Indic scripts, such as Hindi-the most complex writing system for several consonant–vowel combinations of modifiers and context-dependent character shapes8.

The large usage of the benchmark IIIT-HW-Dev dataset9 has served well to foster research in this domain. Instead, the corpus is limited by a lack of variety and insufficient representation of complex words that contain half characters (like प्र, त्स) and conjuncts (like क्ष, ज्ञ), besides having limited coverage of image degradation and real-world imaging artifacts10,11. To overcome such limitations and enhance the generalizing abilities of OCR models, one promising technique found lately is data augmentation12. Such techniques synthetically generate data or introduce controlled variations on existing data that could make the training dataset more diverse and representative. As a result, models learn more robust and generalizable features13,14. Advances made recently in deep learning architecture and transfer learning techniques ensure further enhancement of the performances of OCR systems for Indic scripts. The application of attention-based mechanisms has recently generated promising results in handling complex combinations of characters and improving overall accuracy. Recent studies have also shown that few-shot learning approaches are effective solutions for adapting OCR models to low-resource languages and scripts.

This paper explores two data augmentation methods specifically designed for the IIIT-HW-Dev dataset. The first includes half-character and conjunct content in the development of synthetic images of complex words, utilizing the FIRE 2013 Hindi-English Transliteration corpus15. The second introduces skewness, brightness adjustments, noise ,blur, and compression artifacts against the simulacra of real-world imaging conditions16,17.

In addition, this research evaluates the performance of two different architectures of deep learning: Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNNs)18 and ResNet-5019 using the enhanced dataset. Indeed, CRNNs have achieved impressive performance in numerous OCR-related tasks proficiently through the combination of convolutional and recurrent layers, which help recognize sequential dependencies intrinsic to text data20,21. Meanwhile, ResNet-50 is a very deep residual network and has shown excellent performance in various applications related to computer vision, especially in image recognition and classification19.

We aim for significant experimentation and evaluation for probing the effectiveness of our data augmentation techniques on OCR systems for the Hindi language. We consequently address some current shortcomings in our dataset, the appropriate use of the more specialized deep architectures, and improve the robustness and generalizability of OCR systems when handling complex words and varied image conditions. As a contribution, we are making our augmented IIIT-HW-Dev dataset and the trained model available for further research and development in the area of Hindi OCR. This also opens new avenues for researchers and practitioners, building on top of our contributions to discover new ways for improving the performance of the OCR on Indic scripts.

Related work

Researchers have studied Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for many years, looking into different ways to make it work better for various scripts and languages. Here, we look at work on data augmentation and deep learning for OCR for Indic scripts like Hindi. Hindi and other Indic scripts are hard for OCR systems because they have complex writing systems. These often use many consonant–vowel pairs, modifiers, and characters that change shape based on context7,8. Many studies have tried to solve these problems by creating special techniques and datasets. Pal and Chaudhuri3 started the development of OCR systems for off-line Bangla handwritten numbers without constraints. This laid the groundwork for future research on recognizing Indic scripts.

Lehal and Bhargava5 came up with a shape-based post-processor to recognize Devanagari text. This helped with the problem of touching and fused characters. Sharma et al.7 looked into subspace deformation models to recognize handwritten characters in the Devanagari script. Pal and Roy8 created techniques to identify multi-script lines in Indian documents.

Data augmentation has become a key method to boost deep learning model performance in many computer vision tasks, including OCR. Shorten and Khoshgoftaar12 did a thorough review of image data augmentation techniques for deep learning showing how they help models work better and handle more situations. Machine learning has undergone a massive improvement, especially in large-scale architecture and fine-tuning algorithms, and recent works have demonstrated the major trends in model optimization and its effect on language processing22.For OCR, researchers have looked into data augmentation to tackle issues with different scripts, fonts, and image quality. Perez and Vidal23 tested data augmentation methods, like changing image angles and shapes, to recognize handwritten text using deep neural networks. They found these methods made recognition more accurate.

Kang et al.24 came up with a new data augmentation approach to recognize handwritten text. They used pre-trained GANs to create fake images with different styles and flaws. This made the training data more varied and helped the model do better. Having good datasets has been key to moving Hindi OCR research forward. The IIIT-HW-Dev dataset9, which Mishra et al. created, has become a common standard to test Hindi OCR models. However, the dataset faces limitations in terms of its diversity and representation of complex words and image degradations, as highlighted in the current study. Bansal and Sinha10 and Lehal and Singh11 looked into the problems of splitting touching and fused characters in Devanagari script. They stressed the need for bigger datasets to tackle these issues. Deep learning has brought new ways to do OCR tasks. Shi et al.18 came up with a Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN) that you can train end-to-end. They showed that it works well to recognize text in scenes.

Peng et al.20 studied how to make more data for optical character recognition using deep learning models. Their work showed that making more data in smart ways helps models work better and handle new situations. Zhan et al.21 created TextAugment, a way to make more data for OCR that uses some labeled and some unlabeled data. It uses self-training to add variety to the training data and make the model more accurate. New research has proposed new strategies to improve the optical character recognition of Indic scripts. Singh and Patel25 employed neural networks that synthesize a wider range of training data for the OCR of Hindi. Kumar et al.26 did an assessment of the different transformer-based models for OCR with low-resource Indic languages. The transformers are good at detecting text that contains long-range dependencies. Mehta and Joshi27 explored self-supervised learning strategies in the domain of hand-written text recognition in the Devanagari script and came up with state-of-the-art results using a small amount of labeled data. Reddy and Das28 demonstrated the transfer-learning-based approach to the domain of multi-lingual OCR in Indian languages, which, combined with the few-shot learning algorithm, increased the performance of the model for different scripts.

In the dealing of degraded documents, Verma and Gupta29 introduced a deep network framework built together with domain adaptation techniques to improve OCR performance for historical documents in the Devanagari script. The novel approach produced pronounced progress in the management of different deformations that are typical of manuscripts and printed materials from the past. In addition to the academics mentioned above, commercial optical character recognition (OCR) applications like Google Cloud Vision have also emerged to handle various languages and document types. Nevertheless, their performances on particular scripts and difficult situations may be different, which can call for the development of special models corresponding to the peculiar nature of the language or script.

In order to address these limitations and thus enhance the model’s generalization capability, we have made significant progress in data augmentation techniques and the use of the FIRE 2013 Hindi-English Transliteration corpus for more data that includes half characters and conjuncts. Data augmentation approaches

In order to remove the flaws of the old dataset and boost the model’s capability to detect complex words and several types of image degradations, we used a two-step procedure of data augmentation.

The current strategies in data augmentation used in OCR have mostly addressed script-specific issues in languages like Arabic and Chinese. Stroke deformation, ligature variation, and diacritic perturbation are commonly used to augment Arabic OCR, and radical-level synthesis, geometric distortion, and noise injection are common to Chinese OCR. Although these techniques have been successfully used in the respective scripts, these techniques do not directly represent the structural aspects of Devanagari, such as half-characters and conjunct structures, which are significant in the composition of Hindi words.

The specifics of Hindi OCR that we are targeting with the help of our data augmentation strategy include the presence of conjuncts and half-characters. Although other scripts have used similar methods to make use of data augmentation so that script-specific complexity can be dealt with, our strategy is different as it is based on the variety of complex Hindi words and the real-world image degradation (e.g., skew, blur, noise). Adding the use of synthetic half-characters, conjuncts, and so on is a technique that has not been studied that much by other Indic OCR systems, which makes our method somewhat revolutionary in the framework of Hindi OCR. Such augmented data addition not only enhances model robustness but also guarantees more representative training data. The data augmentation techniques added half-characters and conjuncts in synthetic complex words and the usual image corruptions, including noise, skew, and blur. Extraction of Complex Words Database: We made use of the FIRE 2013 Hindi-English Transliteration corpus, which contains a large collection of Hindi words. From these, we extracted half-character words (e.g., प्र, त्स) and conjuncts (e.g., क्ष, ज्ञ).

Extraction of Complex Words Database: We made use of the FIRE 2013 Hindi-English Transliteration corpus, which contains a large collection of Hindi words. From these, we extracted half-character words (e.g., प्र, त्स) and conjuncts (e.g., क्ष, ज्ञ).

Synthesis of Complex Words: In order to render synthetic images of the selected complex words, we exploited Python Imaging Library (PIL) and the `hindi-renderer` library31. This included loading Hindi fonts (e.g., Noto Sans Devanagari, Mangal) and making images of the words in different font styles (bold, italic), sizes (12–24 pt), and coloring.

Feature engineering

In this work, two deep learning architectures were applied for Hindi OCR using the IIIT-HW-Dev dataset: the Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN) and ResNet-50. Comprehensive experiments and evaluations were conducted to compare their performance. The results demonstrated that the CRNN-based model outperformed the ResNet-50 architecture, particularly in recognizing more intricate words and handling image degradation present in the dataset. The following subsections provide a detailed description of the methodologies employed for each model, including preprocessing steps, architecture design, and training configurations.

Preprocessing

To make the quality and uniformity possible to raise better-quality OCR, we preprocessed as follows.

Binarization

We used the global thresholding, also known as Otsu’s binarization technique, to convert the grayscale or color images to a binary format33. This stage involves the separation of foreground text pixels from background pixels with the aid of Otsu’s algorithm, which determines an optimal threshold value. This algorithm presumes a bi-modal histogram that represents the distributions of foreground and background pixels and determines the threshold that maximizes the inter-class variance between them.

ResNet-50 model

For the ResNet-50 model, a fine-tuning approach is adopted, leveraging a pre-trained ResNet-50 model initially trained on the ImageNet dataset. The final fully connected layer is replaced with a new layer comprising 97 output units, aligning with the character classes relevant to the OCR task. Input images undergo preprocessing, including resizing to 224 × 224 pixels and normalization using mean and standard deviation values derived from the ImageNet dataset. The ResNet-50 architecture is characterized by multiple residual blocks with skip connections, facilitating effective training of deep networks38. With a total of 50 convolutional layers organized into five stages, the model progressively processes feature maps with increasing depth. The final feature maps from the convolutional layers are subjected to spatial averaging before passing through the fully connected layer with 97 output units and softmax activation, enabling classification across the character classes. Hyperparameter tuning, including adjustments to learning rate, batch size, and the number of recurrent layers (for the CRNN model), is conducted through a grid search to optimize model configurations for the IIIT-HW-Dev dataset.

The use of CRNN and ResNet-50 in this study was due to their established skills of working with sequential and visual data, respectively. CRNNs are especially well applied to OCR since they use Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to capture sequential textual dependencies, which is especially important to complex scripts such as Hindi. ResNet-50, which is a deep convolutional model, has the best features extraction capability owing to the residual connections that allow it to identify finer-grained features on an image. In spite of the encouraging performance of Vision Transformers (ViTs) and attention-based models across different tasks, they are computationally expensive and need large size datasets. Although ViTs have shown great performance when dealing with tasks containing long-range dependencies, they continue to have difficulties with dealing with complex combinations of characters found in Indic scripts such as Hindi. In comparison, CRNNs, especially those using BiLSTM layers, can provide a more effective solution with sequential dependencies, which are crucial to complex scripts, including Hindi, that do not require large volumes of data and computation. The future research may focus on using Vision Transformers in OCR in Indic scripts because it has demonstrated the potential of long-range dependencies, but they are highly resource-consuming in terms of computation and needs large datasets.

Results and discussions

This section showcases the results that have been achieved by the CRNN as well as the ResNet-50 models on the augmented IIIT-HW-Dev dataset. The performance of the two models is measured using two metrics: Character Error Rate (CER) and Word Error Rate (WER). Where the first measures the number of characters predicted inaccurately compared to the actual characters, the latter measures the number of such inaccuracies, but at the word level.

Further studies may be conducted on multilingual OCR systems to support various Indic scripts and integrate the language models to boost recognition precision under low-resource conditions.

Conclusions

This research study introduces a comprehensive investigation of enhancement in Hindi Optical Character Recognition (OCR) performance on the IIIT-HW-Dev dataset, by means of advanced deep learning and innovative data augmentation techniques. The results prove the successful integration of a stable neural network architecture with a wide range of training datasets that are both realistic and diverse. Moreover, it is clear that further recognition of even complex Hindi texts in uncertain situations is possible. The CRNN model, a hybrid architecture which consists of both convolutional and recurrent layers, showed to be the best, as it reached a Character Error Rate of 2.14% and a Word Error Rate of 7.96% on the augmented dataset. Its unique design helps it to achieve sequential dependencies, mainly due to the fact that Hindi scripts are characterized by intricate linguistic structures. The CRNN model, with respect to the ResNet-50 model, which is a purely convolutional architecture, has outperformed it, thus showing that the inclusion of sequential processing through them leads to a significant improvement in the OCR performance for the languages with special spelling characteristics.

Data augmentation has been the pivot of this study, as other IIIT-HW-Dev datasets lacked synthetic complexity and, real-world imaging conditions were simulated. Two complementary approaches were employed: generating synthetic images of complex Hindi words and introducing degradations such as brightness adjustments, skewness, noise, blur, and compression artifacts. The synthetic data generation approach was particularly beneficial for increasing the representation of half characters and conjuncts, which are prevalent in Hindi and often pose challenges in OCR tasks. On the other hand, degradation-based augmentation enriched the dataset with realistic variations, enhancing the models’ robustness to noisy, distorted, or low-quality inputs.

The CRNN model benefited most from these augmentation strategies, with performance improvements reflected in a 44.7% reduction in CER and a 44.1% reduction in WER compared to results on the unaugmented dataset. This finding highlights the critical role of diverse and representative training data in optimizing OCR models, particularly for scripts like Hindi that involve complex word structures and diacritical marks. The model demonstrated an exceptional ability to handle such complexity, consistently recognizing words containing half characters and conjuncts even in degraded images.

Another key to the research is the model’s resilience to image degradation. The CRNN model was trained on a dataset incorporating different real-world degradations, which were simulated realistically. The model exhibited great generalization to degraded input images and was able to recognize words with accuracy even in presence of the skewness, blur, or noise. This ability is vital in the course of OCR real-world applications principle, where text images are usually coming from somewhat bad frames, e.g., thanks to poor conditions like poor lighting, motion blur, or compression artifacts.

On the one hand, the CRNN model had great achievements, but some shortcomings persisted. Thus, the model could be thrown off by low-definition or very severely damaged images. This challenge highlights the importance of further developments in augmentation techniques. Future research might use GANs (generative adversarial networks) to create more realistic synthetic images or style transfer techniques for enhancing more specific real-world scenarios. Moreover, the stacking of both OCR and language models could also contribute to further recognition accuracy, especially in the case of rare or ambiguous words.

In brief, the major breakthrough in this research is that data augmentation and sophisticated model architectures play a synergistic role in OCR performance. The insights from developing models that can precisely handle linguistic and structural differences between the scripts speak of the necessity of properly collecting and training on representative data sets. Developing new methods to solve the difficulties of Hindi OCR, the present research works as a source for upcoming projects that will make high-accuracy operations of OCR for the complex scripts possible and hence open more opportunities for OCR in situations with more languages and low resources.

To summarize, the paper presents a new data augmentation method to improve the performance of Hindi OCR with the help of CRNN-ResNet50. The major contributions are the creation of a solid augmented dataset that contains half-character words and image degradations of the environment. The CRNN model was much better in performance, with a Character Error Rate (CER) of 2.14% and Word Error Rate (WER) of 7.96. Using our method, we show that Hindi script data augmentation enhances the accuracy of OCR by 44.7% (CER) and 44.1% (WER) over the performance on the unaugmented dataset. These results are relevant to the further development of OCR on Indic languages, especially on Hindi, and indicate the directions of multilingual OCR and real-time recognition in the future.

Future research needs should be on the improvement of OCR systems to be applied in real-time and multilingual OCR systems. Also, language models might be used to increase accuracy since words in low-resource environments can be disambiguated. Real time Hindi OCR applications might require a lighter architecture of model optimization to enable a speedy inference that does not compromise accuracy. Moreover, using both OCR models in conjunction with generative adversarial networks (GANs) may result in more real data being synthesized to enhance the model robustness in various real-world scenarios.

Probing cosmic curvature with Alcock-Paczynski data
Yungui Gong, Qing Gao, Xuchen Lu, Zhu Yi

Abstract
The Alcock–Paczynski (AP) parameter F A ​ P F_{AP} is independent of the sound horizon r d r_{d} , making the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) AP measurements particularly well suited for cosmological applications. We propose a novel null test of cosmic curvature tailored to DESI BAO data that combines F A ​ P F_{AP} with the ratios D V ′ / D V D_{V}^{\prime}/D_{V} or D M ′ / D M D_{M}^{\prime}/D_{M} . This null test can also be performed using a joint dataset of DESI BAO and type Ia supernova (SNe Ia) observations. Additionally, we use the test to assess the internal consistency and mutual compatibility of these datasets. We find that the data are compatible. Although the results show that a spatially flat universe is inconsistent with the data at low redshift z ≲ 0.5 z\lesssim 0.5 , we cannot draw the conclusion that the observational data prefers Ω k ≠ 0 \Omega_{k}\neq 0 because there is no observational data in that region.



Since the discovery of the accelerated expansion of the Universe in 1998 from observations of type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia)
Riess et al. 1998
;
Perlmutter et al. 1999
,
the concept of dark energy has been introduced as a phenomenological explanation for cosmic acceleration.
Despite its phenomenological success,
the nature of dark energy remain unknown because its pressure is negative and its gravitational interaction behaves as repulsive instead of attractive.
A central question in contemporary cosmology is whether dark energy is a true cosmological constant or a dynamical component whose equation of state evolves with time.
The cosmological constant,
\Lambda
, is the simplest candidate and fits naturally into Einstein’s field equations.
However, theoretical estimates of
\Lambda
based on vacuum energy density exceed the value inferred from observations by many orders of magnitude,
a severe discrepancy known as the cosmological constant problem
Weinberg 1989
.

Observationally, efforts to distinguish a constant
\Lambda
from dynamical dark energy commonly use parameterizations of the dark energy equation of state
w=p/\rho
.
A widely used form is the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder (CPL) parametrization
Chevallier and Polarski 2001
;
Linder 2003
,
w(a)=w_{0}+w_{a}(1-a)
,
which allows for a time-varying equation of state.
Recent joint analyses combining baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) measurements from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI)
Adame et al. 2025
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025
,
cosmic microwave background (CMB) data from Planck
Aghanim et al. 2020
, and SNe Ia compilations such as Pantheon Plus
Scolnic et al. 2022
, Union3
Rubin et al. 2023
,
and the five-year Dark Energy Survey (DES Y5) results
Abbott et al. 2024
have reported evidence favoring dynamical dark energy over a pure cosmological constant.
If confirmed, these results would have profound implications for fundamental physics and our understanding of cosmic evolution.

The evidence for cosmic acceleration and dynamical dark energy,
however, depends sensitively on the models and data combinations used.
This paper addresses two important questions: (1) Can we probe the Universe in a model-independent way?
and (2) Are different data sets mutually compatible so that they can be combined to infer cosmological parameters and matter content reliably?

Cosmic curvature
\Omega_{k}
is degenerate with the dark energy equation of state,
so determining whether
\Omega_{k}=0
is also crucial.
The
Ok

\mathcal{O}_{k}(z)=E(z)D^{\prime}(z)-1

;
Cai et al. 2016

D^{\prime}(z)=dD(z)/dz

and
D(z)=H_{0}D_{M}(z)

Applying this diagnostic typically requires SNe Ia to reconstruct
D(z)
,
which reintroduces the zero-point calibration problem.
To avoid that and exploit DESI BAO AP data,
the
Ok
diagnostic can be recast as
\mathcal{O}_{k}(z)=F_{AP}(z)D^{\prime}_{M}(z)/D_{M}(z)-1

Gao et al. 2025b
.
In this expression
r_{d}
cancels in the ratio
D^{\prime}_{M}/D_{M}
constructed from DESI BAO data,
so the null test of cosmic curvature depends only on the assumption of an Friedmann-Robertson-Walker metric or the cosmological principle.

Moreover, one can reconstruct the ratio
D^{\prime}_{M}/D_{M}
from SNe Ia without suffering the zero-point calibration problem.
Combining DESI BAO AP data with the ratio
D^{\prime}_{M}/D_{M}
derived from SNe Ia, therefore provides a model-independent test of compatibility between DESI BAO and SNe Ia data.
A caveat is that DESI Data Release 2 (DR2) BAO measurements are reported either as
(D_{M}/r_{d},D_{H}/r_{d})
with correlations or as
(F_{AP},D_{V}/r_{d})
with correlations.
The simple combination of
F_{AP}
and
D^{\prime}_{M}/D_{M}
does not properly account for these correlations.
A more consistent approach is to reconstruct
F_{AP}

from
F_{AP}
and
D_{V}/r_{d}
and their correlations,
and to use
F_{AP}
together with
D^{\prime}_{V}/D_{V}
in the
Ok

In this work we use DESI DR2 BAO AP data and SNe Ia to perform a null test of spatial flatness and test the consistency between data.

D_{H}=\frac{c}{H(z)},

(1)

from which one can obtain

H_{0}r_{d}=\frac{c}{D_{H}(z=0)/r_{d}}.

(2)

The transverse comoving distance

D_{M}(z)=\frac{c}{H_{0}\sqrt{|\Omega_{k0}|}}\text{sinn}\left[\sqrt{|\Omega_{k0}|}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{E\left(z^{\prime}\right)}dz^{\prime}\right],

(3)

where

is the speed of light,

is the dimensionless Hubble parameter,
H_{0}
is the Hubble constant
and
\text{sinn}(\sqrt{|\Omega_{k0}|}x)/\sqrt{|\Omega_{k0}|}=\sinh(\sqrt{|\Omega_{k0}|}x)/\sqrt{|\Omega_{k0}|}
,
x
,
and

\Omega_{k0}>0

\Omega_{k0}=0

\Omega_{k0}<0

F_{AP}(z)=\frac{D_{M}(z)}{D_{H}(z)}.

(4)

Note that
D_{M}(z=0)=0
and
F_{AP}(z=0)=0

\Omega_{k0}=0

(5)

The sound horizon at the drag epoch

where

In conclusion, we propose novel null tests of cosmic curvature specifically tailored to DESI BAO data:

\mathcal{O}_{k}(z)=F_{\rm AP}(z)\,D_{M}^{\prime}(z)/D_{M}(z)-1
and

\mathcal{O}_{k}=F_{\rm AP}\,D^{\prime}_{V}/D_{V}+F^{\prime}_{\rm AP}/3-F_{\rm AP}/(3z)-1
.
Our reconstructions indicate that a spatially flat universe is disfavored by the current data at low redshift
z\lesssim 0.5
, a region where BAO measurements are currently sparse.
This pattern suggests the apparent deviation from flatness may be driven by incomplete low‑
z
BAO coverage rather than a genuine curvature signal.
Therefore, additional observations, particularly more BAO measurements at low redshift,
are required to test spatial flatness robustly.
Reconstructions performed with and without including the full correlations yield similar results.
We also find that the DESI BAO DR2 data are self‑consistent,
and that the SNe Ia datasets (Union3 and Pantheon Plus) are broadly consistent with the BAO reconstructions.
The DES Y5 (D5) SNe Ia sample, however, shows a notable discrepancy and therefore warrants further investigation.

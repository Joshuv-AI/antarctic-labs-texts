Closed-form expressions for the centroid-tilt error due to scintillation Preprint: APS/123-QED
Milo W. Hyde IV, Eric W. Mitchell, Mark F. Spencer

Abstract
In adaptive optics, the tracker and wavefront sensor commonly measure irradiance centroids (in their respective focal planes) to estimate the turbulence-degraded wavefront in the pupil plane. Several factors affect the accuracy of these centroid measurements, including noise, speckle, and scintillation. The centroid-tilt or “C-tilt” errors due to these factors have been studied by numerous researchers; however, to our knowledge, closed-form expressions for the C-tilt error due to scintillation have not been found. In this paper, we derive such expressions, assuming spherical-wave illumination of the pupil, a path-invariant index of refraction structure constant C n 2 C_{n}^{2} , and Kolmogorov turbulence. We compare the analytically predicted C-tilt values to those obtained by wave-optics simulations. The agreement, at large, is quite good over a wide range of conditions. As a result, researchers and engineers will find this analysis useful when quantifying the performance of centroid-based trackers and wavefront sensors, both of which are critical adaptive-optics components.



I
Introduction

It is common knowledge that as light propagates through atmospheric turbulence it accumulates phase errors distributed along the path
Tatarskii 1961
;
Fante 1975
;
Fante 1980
;
Ishimaru 1999
;
Roggemann and Welsh 1996
;
Andrews and Phillips 2005
;
Sasiela 2007
. Adaptive-optics systems often employ tilt and higher-order wavefront sensors (in the form of trackers and Shack–Hartmann wavefront sensors) to estimate these path-integrated phase errors
Holmes 2022
;
Lukin and Fortes 2002
;
Roddier 1999
;
Tyson 2015
;
Hardy 1998
;
Merritt and Spencer 2018
. Both sensors generally operate on the same principle: They measure the centroid of an irradiance pattern in a focal plane to estimate the light’s phase function in the pupil plane. For the tracker, the centroid measurement is global and provides an estimate of the average phase gradient over the pupil. The Shack–Hartmann wavefront sensor, on the other hand, partitions the pupil into subapertures and therefore, provides estimates of the average phase gradients over the subaperture areas. In the absence of noise, speckle, and scintillation, both of these sensors measure what is known as gradient tilt or “G-tilt”
Tyler 1994
;
Sasiela 2007
.

Unfortunately, there are two problems with using G-tilt to estimate the path-integrated phase errors. For simplicity, we focus the discussion on the tilt sensor or tracker. The actual or true amount of tilt in the turbulence-degraded wavefront is given by the weights/coefficients of the tip/tilt Zernike polynomials
Z_{1}^{\pm 1}

Noll 1976
;
Sasiela 2007
;
Mahajan 2013
;
Lakshminarayanan and Fleck 2011
. Although G-tilt clearly includes Zernike tilt or “Z-tilt,” it also includes contributions from all other off-axis aberrations (predominately coma) that produce shifted or sheered point spread functions
Herrmann 1981
;
Sasiela 2007
. This Z-tilt, G-tilt error, known as centroid anisoplanatism, has been studied and quantified by many researchers, including Sasiela
Sasiela 2007
, Tyler
Tyler 1994
, and Yura and Tavis
Yura and Tavis 1985
.

The second issue with using G-tilt to estimate the path-integrated phase errors (tilt, in particular) concerns the measurement of the irradiance centroid. Multiple factors affect the accuracy of this measurement, including noise, speckle, and atmospheric amplitude fluctuations (better known as scintillation). When considering these real-world effects, it is common to distinguish this tilt, called centroid tilt or “C-tilt,” from pure G-tilt.

The error due to C-tilt has also been studied by numerous researchers: Tyler and Fried quantified the C-tilt error due to sensor noise
Tyler and Fried 1982
; Burrell
et al.
developed scaling laws for the C-tilt error considering target-induced speckle
Burrell et al. 2023
; and lastly, Tavis and Yura
Tavis and Yura 1987
, Holmes
Holmes 2009
, Shaw and Tomlinson
Shaw and Tomlinson 2019
, and Mitchell
et al.

Mitchell et al. 2024
;
Mitchell et al. 2025
studied the effects of amplitude variation on C-tilt. These latter studies were either numerical in nature (i.e., used quadrature to evaluate integral expressions or wave-optics simulations to quantify C-tilt) or did not consider turbulence-induced scintillation as a potential source of amplitude variation.

In this paper, we derive closed-form expressions that quantify the C-tilt error of a scintillated spherical wave (see Section
II
). To validate these expressions, we employ wave-optics simulations, which we describe in Section
III
. We then compare the simulation results to predictions from our analytical expressions in Section
IV
. The agreement, at large, is excellent over a broad range of conditions. Lastly, we conclude with a brief summary of our work.

II.1
Centroid-tilt variance

The angular centroid location in the focal plane is given by

\bm{T}_{C}=\frac{\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\bm{\rho}/f\right)I\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\text{d}^{2}\rho}{\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}I\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\text{d}^{2}\rho},

(1)

where
\bm{\rho}=\hat{\bm{x}}x+\hat{\bm{y}}y
,
f
is the focal length, and
I
is the irradiance in the focal plane. Tatarskii showed that Eq. (
1

;
Tatarskii 1971
;

\bm{T}_{C}=\frac{1}{k}\dfrac{\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho}}/{D}}\right)\nabla\phi\left(\bm{\rho}\right)I\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\text{d}^{2}\rho}{\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho}}/{D}}\right)I\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\text{d}^{2}\rho}.

(2)

Here,
\rho
and
I
are in the pupil plane,
k=2\pi/\lambda
,
D
is diameter of the circular pupil, and
\nabla\phi
is the phase gradient.

Consider a spherical wave radiated by a point source located a distance
z
away from the pupil. The irradiance in the pupil plane takes the form

I\left(\bm{\rho}\right)=\frac{\left|U_{0}\right|^{2}}{\left(\lambda z\right)^{2}}\exp\left[2\chi\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\right],

(3)

where
U_{0}
is the amplitude of the point source and
\chi
is the log-amplitude fluctuations due to atmospheric turbulence

;
Andrews and Phillips 2005

3

\bm{T}_{C}=\frac{1}{k}\dfrac{\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho}}/{D}}\right)\nabla\phi\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\exp\left[2\chi\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\right]\text{d}^{2}\rho}{\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho}}/{D}}\right)\exp\left[2\chi\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\right]\text{d}^{2}\rho}.

(4)

We are interested in the C-tilt variance (note that the mean equals zero); therefore,

\langle\bm{T}_{C}\cdot\bm{T}_{C}\rangle=\langle T_{C}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left\langle\dfrac{\displaystyle\iiiint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho_{1}}}/{D}}\right)\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho_{2}}}/{D}}\right)\nabla_{1}\phi\left(\bm{\rho}_{1}\right)\cdot\nabla_{2}\phi\left(\bm{\rho}_{2}\right)\exp\left[2\chi\left(\bm{\rho}_{1}\right)\right]\exp\left[2\chi\left(\bm{\rho}_{2}\right)\right]\text{d}^{2}\rho_{1}\text{d}^{2}\rho_{2}}{\displaystyle\iiiint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho_{1}}}/{D}}\right)\operatorname{circ}\left({{\rho_{2}}/{D}}\right)\exp\left[2\chi\left(\bm{\rho}_{1}\right)\right]\exp\left[2\chi\left(\bm{\rho}_{2}\right)\right]\text{d}^{2}\rho_{1}\text{d}^{2}\rho_{2}}\right\rangle.

(5)

To proceed, we make the approximation that the mean of the quotient is equal to the quotient of the means, such that

\langle T_{C}^{2}\rangle\approx\frac{1}{k^{2}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\iiiint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho_{1}}}/{D}}\right)\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho_{2}}}/{D}}\right)B_{\nabla\phi}\left(\left|\bm{\rho}_{1}-\bm{\rho}_{2}\right|\right)\exp\left[4B_{\chi}\left(\left|\bm{\rho}_{1}-\bm{\rho}_{2}\right|\right)\right]\text{d}^{2}\rho_{1}\text{d}^{2}\rho_{2}}{\displaystyle\iiiint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\operatorname{circ}\left({{{\rho_{1}}}/{D}}\right)\operatorname{circ}\left({{\rho_{2}}/{D}}\right)\exp\left[4B_{\chi}\left(\left|\bm{\rho}_{1}-\bm{\rho}_{2}\right|\right)\right]\text{d}^{2}\rho_{1}\text{d}^{2}\rho_{2}},

(6)

where
B_{\nabla\phi}
and
B_{\chi}
are the phase gradient and log-amplitude covariance functions
Tyler 1994
;
Fried 1967
. Following Holmes
Holmes 2009
, we also assume that the turbulent phase gradient and log amplitude are statistically independent. Making the change of variables
\bm{\rho}^{\prime}=\bm{\rho}_{1}
and
\bm{\rho}=\bm{\rho}_{1}-\bm{\rho}_{2}
and transforming to polar coordinates produces

\langle T_{C}^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\frac{\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{A}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\Lambda\left(\rho/D\right)B_{\nabla\phi}\left({\rho}\right)\exp\left[4B_{\chi}\left({\rho}\right)\right]\text{d}\rho}{\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{A}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\Lambda\left(\rho/D\right)\exp\left[4B_{\chi}\left({\rho}\right)\right]\text{d}\rho},

(7)

where
A=\pi\left(D/2\right)^{2}
is the area of the pupil and

\Lambda\left(x\right)=\frac{2}{\pi}\left[\cos^{-1}\left(x\right)-x\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right]\operatorname{circ}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right).

(8)

is the optical transfer function

;
Gaskill 1978

II.2.1

\begin{gathered}\langle T_{C}^{2}\rangle_{\text{N}}\approx\frac{2\pi}{k^{2}A}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\Lambda\left(\rho/D\right)B_{\nabla\phi}\left({\rho}\right)\text{d}\rho\hfill\\

-5/12,1/12,1,-5/12,-11/12,-11/12,-17/12\end{matrix}\right.\right)\right\}.\hfill\end{gathered}

(26)

Finally, the C-tilt variance is

\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{N}}}{\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}}=\frac{\left\langle T_{G}^{2}\right\rangle+\left\langle T_{GC}^{2}\right\rangle}{1+\sigma_{\chi\text{A}}^{2}},

(27)

where the G-tilt variance
\langle T_{G}^{2}\rangle
, G-tilt, C-tilt variance
\langle T_{GC}^{2}\rangle
, and the aperture-averaged scintillation
\sigma_{\chi\text{A}}^{2}
are in Eqs. (
13
), (
23
), and (
26
), respectively.

II.3
Asymptotic expressions for the C-tilt variance

Although we have obtained a closed-form answer for the C-tilt variance, the result provides little insight into how
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle
behaves versus
N_{F}
. Inspection of the integrands in Eqs. (
21
) and (
25
) reveals that the integrals converge for all values of
N_{F}
when the contours are closed to the left. In both cases, the contours circumscribe the poles at
s=0,\,-m+5/12,\,-m-1/12
, and
-m-1
for
m=0,\,1,\,\cdots
.

For
N_{F}\ll 1
(i.e., the far-zone) the numerator of
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle
is well approximated by summing the pole residues at
s=0
,
5/12
, and
-1/12
. Performing the requisite analysis yields

\begin{gathered}\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{N}}\approx\left\langle T_{G}^{2}\right\rangle\left\{1+4\sigma_{\chi}^{2}\right.\hfill\\
\left.\;+\,\frac{2^{5/3}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{99}{55}\Gamma\begin{bmatrix}13/6,11/12,1/6\\
5/6,11/6,4/3,-5/12\end{bmatrix}\sigma_{\chi}^{2}N_{F}^{5/6}\right.\hfill\\
\left.\;-\,\frac{2^{7/6}}{{\pi}}\frac{3}{17}\Gamma\begin{bmatrix}1/6,11/3,1/12,11/12,11/12\\
11/6,-5/12,4/3\end{bmatrix}\sigma_{\chi}^{2}N_{F}\right\}.\hfill\end{gathered}

(28)

Likewise,
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}
is well approximated by summing the residues of the same three poles, namely,

\begin{gathered}\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}\approx 1+4\sigma_{\chi}^{2}\hfill\\
\;-\,\frac{2^{13/2}}{\pi^{2}}\Gamma\begin{bmatrix}11/12,1/12,4/3,7/3\\
11/6,17/6,23/6\end{bmatrix}\sigma_{\chi}^{2}N_{F}^{5/6}\hfill\\
\;+\,\frac{2^{11/6}}{\pi}\Gamma\begin{bmatrix}1/12,23/12,-1/12,11/12\\
11/6,-5/12\end{bmatrix}\sigma_{\chi}^{2}N_{F}.\hfill\end{gathered}

(29)

Nevertheless, dividing Eq. (
28
) by Eq. (
29
) yields a poor estimate for
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle
. Consequently, we use
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}\approx 1+4\sigma_{\chi}^{2}
when we approximate
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle
for
N_{F}\ll 1
.

For
N_{F}\gg 1
(i.e., the near-zone) the sums over the pole residues are slow to converge and can result in numerical errors when evaluating the Meijer G-functions in Eqs. (
23
) and (
26
). We, therefore, seek asymptotic solutions to the integrals in Eqs. (
21
) and (
25
) that are accurate in the limit
N_{F}\to\infty
. We obtain such solutions by including contributions from poles to the right of the contours.

In Eq. (
21
), the dominant contribution comes from the pole at
s=5/6
and, in fact, provides an excellent approximation for
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{N}}
when
N_{F}>1
. Applying Cauchy’s integral formula to compute the pole residue at
s=5/6
yields the following expression for
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{N}}
:

\begin{gathered}\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{N}}\approx\left\langle T_{G}^{2}\right\rangle\left\{1+\frac{40}{9\sqrt{\pi}}\right.\hfill\\
\left.\quad\times\,\Gamma\begin{bmatrix}1/12,5/6,11/6,17/6\\
17/12,11/3,1/6\end{bmatrix}\sigma_{\chi}^{2}N_{F}^{-5/6}\right\}.\end{gathered}

(30)

For
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}
, the dominant contribution comes from the pole at
s=1
. Applying Cauchy’s integral formula, we obtain

\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}\approx 1+\frac{8}{{\pi}^{2}}\Gamma\begin{bmatrix}13/12,1/6,1/3,2/3\\
5/12,11/6,11/6\end{bmatrix}\sigma_{\chi}^{2}N_{F}^{-7/6}.

(31)

As we can see in Eqs. (
29
) and (
31
),
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}
is slightly greater than one. Indeed, assuming
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}\approx 1
yields a good, yet overestimated value for the C-tilt variance.

II.4
G-tilt, C-tilt error

The G-tilt, C-tilt error
E_{GC}
is a measure of the G-tilt, C-tilt anisoplanatism
Holmes 2009
;
Mitchell et al. 2025
, which is an important metric for adaptive-optics system performance
Barchers et al. 2002
;
Banet and Spencer 2020
;
Kalensky et al. 2024
. We define this error as the root-mean-square difference between the G-tilt and C-tilt angles, such that

E_{GC}=\sqrt{\left\langle\left|\bm{T}_{G}-\bm{T}_{C}\right|^{2}\right\rangle}.

(32)

It is easy to show using Eq. (
4
) that
E_{GC}
is equivalent to

E_{GC}=\sqrt{\left\langle{T}_{C}^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle{T}_{G}^{2}\right\rangle}.

(33)

Note that the G-tilt angle
\bm{T}_{G}
equals Eq. (
4
) with
\exp\left[2\chi\left(\bm{\rho}\right)\right]=1
. Substituting Eq. (
27
) into Eq. (
33
) and simplifying yields

E_{GC}=\sqrt{\frac{\left\langle T_{GC}^{2}\right\rangle-\sigma_{\chi\text{A}}^{2}\left\langle{T}_{G}^{2}\right\rangle}{1+\sigma_{\chi\text{A}}^{2}}}.

(34)

Deriving approximate relations for the G-tilt, C-tilt error using the asymptotic expressions above is somewhat ad hoc because the value of the numerator is approximately zero. Nevertheless, we can obtain relatively accurate relations for
E_{GC}
.

Staring with
N_{F}\ll 1
, substituting the relevant parts of Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
) into the numerator and keeping only the first two terms yields

E_{GC}\approx 1.4017N_{F}^{5/12}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\chi}^{2}\left\langle T_{G}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}}}\quad N_{F}\ll 1,

(35)

where we use all of Eq. (
29
) in the denominator of Eq. (
35
).

For
N_{F}\gg 1
, we substitute the germane parts of Eqs. (
30
) and (
31
) into the numerator of Eq. (
34
) and keep only the
N_{F}^{-5/6}
term. After simplification, the result is

E_{GC}\approx 1.6331N_{F}^{-5/12}\sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\chi}^{2}\left\langle T_{G}^{2}\right\rangle}{\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle_{\text{D}}}}\quad N_{F}\gg 1,

(36)

where we use all of Eq. (
31
) in the denominator.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we derived three closed-form expressions for the spherical-wave C-tilt variance
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle
in constant-
C_{n}^{2}
Kolomogorov atmospheric turbulence. All three expressions were functions of the Fresnel number
N_{F}
. The first was applicable for all
N_{F}
and was in the form of a Meijer G-function. The second and third were much simpler relations and valid in the asymptotic limits
N_{F}\to 0
and
N_{F}\to\infty
(i.e., the far- and near-zones, respectively). In addition, we investigated the difference between G-tilt and C-tilt and derived corresponding relations for the G-tilt, C-tilt error
E_{GC}
due to a scintillated spherical wave. Lastly, we validated our analysis by comparing the theory with wave-optics simulations. Our
\left\langle T_{C}^{2}\right\rangle
and
E_{GC}
predictions were in very good agreement with the simulation results.

The rigorous analysis of C-tilt presented in this paper will find use in quantifying the performance of centroid-based trackers and wavefront sensors, which are critical components in adaptive-optics systems.

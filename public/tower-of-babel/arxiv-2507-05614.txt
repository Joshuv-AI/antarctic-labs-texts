Divided difference operators for Hessenberg representations
Mathieu Guay-Paquet

Abstract
The equivariant cohomology ring of a regular semisimple Hessenberg variety in type A is a free module over the equivariant cohomology ring of a point. When equipped with Tymoczko’s dot action, it becomes a twisted representation of the symmetric group, and the character of this representation is given by the chromatic quasisymmetric function of an indifference graph. In this note, we use divided difference operators to decompose this representation as a direct sum of sub-representations in a way that categorifies the modular relation between chromatic quasisymmetric functions.

1.1
Notation

Fix a natural number
n
.
Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group of order
n
, viewed as the set of functions from
\{1,\ldots,n\}
to itself, so that

(vw)(i)=v\big(w(i)\big)\quad\text{for $v,w\in S_{n}$ and $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$.}

(1.1)

The group
S_{n}
is generated by the
adjacent transpositions
,

s_{i}=(i\leftrightarrow i+1)\quad\text{for $i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$},

(1.2)

which satisfy
s_{i}^{2}=\id
and the
braid relations
:

\begin{gathered}s_{i}s_{(i+1)}s_{i}=s_{(i+1)}s_{i}s_{(i+1)}\qquad\text{for $i\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}$},\\
s_{i}s_{k}=s_{k}s_{i}\qquad\text{for $|i-k|>1$}.\end{gathered}

(1.3)

We sometimes write a permutation in one-line notation, as

w=[w(1),\ldots,w(n)].

(1.4)

In particular, the
longest permutation
is the permutation

w_{0}=[n,\ldots,1].

(1.5)

A
Hessenberg function
is a function
h:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that

\begin{gathered}i\leq h(i)\qquad\text{for $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$},\\
h(i)\leq h(i+1)\qquad\text{for $i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$}.\end{gathered}

(1.6)

Like permutations, we sometimes write them in one-line notation, as

h=[h(1),\ldots,h(n)].

(1.7)

We write
\Lambda
for the ring of symmetric polynomials in
n
variables, and
\Lambda_{0}
for the ideal of symmetric polynomials with no constant term:

\Lambda_{0}=\{f\in\Lambda\mid f(0,\ldots,0)=0\}.

(1.8)

We are interested in two commuting
S_{n}
-actions on the polynomial ring

R=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n};x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

(1.9)

by
\mathbb{C}
-algebra morphisms: the
dot
action, on the left, where
w\in S_{n}
permutes the
t_{i}
variables and fixes the
x_{i}
variables:

w\cdot t_{i}=t_{w(i)},\qquad w\cdot x_{i}=x_{i},

(1.10)

and the
star
action, on the right, where
w\in S_{n}
fixes the
t_{i}
variables and permutes the
w_{i}
variables:

t_{i}*w=t_{i},\qquad x_{i}*w=x_{w^{-1}(i)}.

(1.11)

These two actions are named following Tymoczko
[
16
]
, who considered the corresponding actions on the ring of functions

H=\Fun(S_{n},\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]),

(1.12)

defined for
w\in S_{n}
and
f\in H
by

\displaystyle(w\cdot f)(v;t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=f(w^{-1}v;t_{w(1)},\ldots,t_{w(n)}),

(1.13)

\displaystyle(f*w)(v;t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=f(vw^{-1};t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}).

The correspondence is given by the following
\mathbb{C}
-algebra morphism, which respects both actions:

\displaystyle\varphi:R

\displaystyle\to H

(1.14)

\displaystyle t_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto(v\mapsto t_{i})

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto(v\mapsto t_{v(i)}).

By abuse of notation, we will write
t_{i}
and
x_{i}
for
\varphi(t_{i})
and
\varphi(x_{i})
in
H
.

For each adjacent transposition
s_{i}
, there is a corresponding
divided difference operator

\partial_{i}
defined by

\partial_{i}(f)=\frac{f*(1-s_{i})}{x_{i}-x_{(i+1)}}.

(1.15)

This definition makes sense in the ring
R
, where the quotient always exists as a (unique) polynomial.
However, it only makes partial sense in the ring
H
, where the quotient may not exist (but is unique if it does exist).
One of our contributions in this paper is to detail some suitable domains and codomains for
\partial_{i}
on
H
(see
Section 4
).
For now, note that, on the appropriate domains, the divided difference operators satisfy
\partial_{i}^{2}=0
and the braid relations:

\begin{gathered}\partial_{i}\partial_{(i+1)}\partial_{i}=\partial_{(i+1)}\partial_{i}\partial_{(i+1)}\qquad\text{for $i\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}$},\\
\partial_{i}\partial_{k}=\partial_{k}\partial_{i}\qquad\text{for $|i-k|>1$},\end{gathered}

(1.16)

since

\partial_{i}\partial_{(i+1)}\partial_{i}(f)=\partial_{(i+1)}\partial_{i}\partial_{(i+1)}(f)\\
=\frac{f*(1-s_{i}-s_{(i+1)}+s_{i}s_{(i+1)}+s_{(i+1)}s_{i}-s_{i}s_{(i+1)}s_{i})}{(x_{i}-x_{(i+1)})(x_{i}-x_{(i+2)})(x_{(i+1)}-x_{(i+2)})}

(1.17)

and

\partial_{i}\partial_{k}(f)=\partial_{k}\partial_{i}(f)=\frac{f*(1-s_{i}-s_{k}+s_{i}s_{k})}{(x_{i}-x_{(i+1)})(x_{k}-x_{(k+1)})}.

(1.18)

The divided difference operators are not ring homomorphisms, but they are
\mathbb{C}
-linear and satisfy a (skew) product rule:

\partial_{i}(fg)=\partial_{i}(f)g+(f*s_{i})\partial_{i}(g).

(1.19)

1.2
First pass: ordinary cohomology

Consider the variety of complete flags in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

X=\big\{V_{\bullet}=(V_{0}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V_{n})\bigm|\text{$\dim(V_{i})=i$ for $i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}$}\big\}.

(1.20)

The Borel presentation for its ordinary cohomology ring is the polynomial ring quotient

H^{*}(X)=\frac{\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]}{\langle f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mid f\in\Lambda_{0}\rangle},

(1.21)

where
x_{i}
is the first Chern class of the line bundle
V_{i}/V_{(i-1)}
over
X
(see, for example,
[
8
]
and references therein).

This ring has a very nice
\mathbb{C}
-linear basis, given by the Schubert polynomials (for their history, see
[
9
]
).
These polynomials are indexed by the permutations in
S_{n}
, and they can be defined recursively by starting at the longest permutation, and applying divided difference operators to obtain the others:

\begin{gathered}\mathfrak{S}_{w_{0}}=x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{n-2}\cdots x_{n}^{0},\\
\mathfrak{S}_{ws_{i}}=\partial_{i}(\mathfrak{S}_{w})\qquad\text{if $w(i)>w(i+1)$}.\end{gathered}

(1.22)

Note that the divided difference operators are well-defined on
H^{*}(X)
because they respect the ideal
\langle f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mid f\in\Lambda_{0}\rangle
by the product rule; and that the indexing by permutations is well-defined because of the braid relations.

1.3
Second pass: equivariant cohomology

Consider the
n
-dimensional complex torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
, whose classifying space is
BT=(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty})^{n}
.
The equivariant cohomology ring of a single point, equipped with the trivial
T
-action, is the polynomial ring

H_{T}^{*}(\pt)=H^{*}(BT)=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}],

(1.23)

where
t_{i}
it the first Chern class of the tautological line bundle over the
i
th factor
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
of
BT
(see, for example,
[
15
]
and references therein).

The flag variety
X
can be equipped with a
T
-action by identifying
T
with the group of invertible diagonal matrices on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The Borel presentation for its equivariant cohomology ring is the polynomial ring quotient

H_{T}^{*}(X)=\frac{\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n};x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]}{\langle f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})-f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{x})\mid f\in\Lambda\rangle}.

(1.24)

We can recover the ordinary cohomology by setting
t_{1}=\cdots=t_{n}=0
.
The divided difference operators respect the quotienting ideal in (
1.24
), and we can lift the
\mathbb{C}
-linear basis of Schubert polynomials for
H^{*}(X)
to a
H_{T}^{*}(\pt)
-module basis of
double
Schubert polynomials for
H_{T}^{*}(X)
, as follows:

\begin{gathered}\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{w_{0}}=\prod_{i+k\leq n}(x_{i}-t_{k}),\\
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{ws_{i}}=\partial_{i}(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}_{w})\qquad\text{if $w(i)>w(i+1)$}.\end{gathered}

(1.25)

This basis shows that
H_{T}^{*}(X)
is a free module over
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
, but in fact the same basis shows that
H_{T}^{*}(X)
is also a free module over
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

As noted in
[
16
]
, the dot action acts trivially on the subring
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, so we can consider
H_{T}^{*}(X)
as a representation of
S_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
; this representation is a single copy of the regular representation of
S_{n}
.

The dot action acts non-trivially on
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
, but it does send this subring to itself, so we can consider
H_{T}^{*}(X)
as a
twisted
representation of
S_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
; this representation consists of
n!
copies of the (twisted) trivial representation of
S_{n}
.

1.4
Third pass: Hessenberg varieties

Now we consider some sub-varieties of the flag variety
X
.
Fix a diagonal matrix
M
with distinct eigenvalues.
The specific choice does not matter for this story, only that:

•

the Jordan blocks of
M
all have size one (so that
M
is semisimple),

•

the Jordan blocks have distinct eigenvalues (so that
M
is regular),

•

M
commutes with the torus
T
.

Then, for every Hessenberg function
h
, we have the corresponding (regular semisimple) Hessenberg variety

X(h)=\big\{V_{\bullet}\in X\bigm|\text{$MV_{i}\subseteq V_{h(i)}$ for $i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$}\big\}.

(1.26)

As two extreme examples, note that
X([n,\ldots,n])
is the entire flag variety, and that
X([1,\ldots,n])
consists of just
n!
isolated points, the permutation flags, which are the fixed points of the
T
-action on
X
.

For each Hessenberg function, the sub-variety
X(h)\subseteq X
is preserved by the
T
-action, so we can consider its equivariant cohomology ring.
Instead of the Borel presentation as a quotient of the ring
R
, we have the GKM presentation
[
16
]
, which exhibits
H_{T}^{*}(X(h))
as a subring of
H
(see
Section 2
).
However, note that the kernel of the map
\varphi:R\to H
is precisely the quotienting ideal in (
1.24
), so the two presentations are closely related.

The concept which generalizes the basis of double Schubert polynomials is the concept of a
flow-up
basis
[
6
]
. These are bases

\{f_{w}\mid w\in S_{n}\}\subseteq H_{T}^{*}(X(h)),

(1.27)

over
H_{T}^{*}(\pt)
, indexed by permutations, where

f_{w}(v)=0\quad\text{if $w\not\leq v$ in Bruhat order},

(1.28)

and where
f_{w}(w)
is a prescribed product of factors of the form
(t_{i}-t_{k})
.
In the case of the entire flag variety, the double Schubert polynomials are the unique flow-up basis, but in general the flow-up bases are not unique.
Still, flow-up bases do exist, and they show that
H_{T}^{*}(X(h))
is a free module over
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
, and a free module over
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

The dot action on
H
preserves the subring
H_{T}^{*}(X(h))
.
When we consider it as a representation of
S_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, its graded Frobenius characteristic is a unicellular LLT polynomial.
When we consider it as a twisted representation of
S_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
, its graded Frobenius characteristic is a chromatic quasisymmetric function (up to the fundamental involution on symmetric functions)
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

Both unicellular LLT polynomials and chromatic quasisymmetric functions are known to satisfy the modular relation (a variant on the deletion-contraction recurrence for chromatic polynomials of graphs), which states that, for certain triples
(h_{-},h,h_{+})
of Hessenberg functions:

(1+q)\csf_{q}(h)=\csf_{q}(h_{+})+q\csf_{q}(h_{-})

(1.29)

In fact, together with a relatively small set of base cases, the modular relation uniquely determines these two families of symmetric functions
[
1
,
4
,
11
]
.

Another contribution of this paper is to give a decomposition of the dot representation on
H_{T}^{*}(X(h))
which corresponds to the modular relation, by using divided difference operators (see
Theorem 5.1
and
Theorem 5.3
).

Note that Horiguchi, Masuda and Sato
[
7
]
have also given such a decomposition, which is nicely motivated by the geometry of Hessenberg varieties.
They exhibit the blow-up of
X(h_{+})
along
X(h_{-})
as a
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
-bundle over
X(h)
equipped with a
T
-action.
Based on this, they identify a certain subring of

H_{T}^{*}(X(h))\oplus\big(H_{T}^{*}(X(h))*s_{i}\big)

(1.30)

as being isomorphic to both:

•

the direct sum of a plain copy of
H_{T}^{*}(X(h))
and a degree-shifted copy of
H_{T}^{*}(X(h))
, corresponding to the left-hand side of (
1.29
), and

•

the direct sum of a plain copy of
H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{+}))
and a degree-shifted copy of
H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{-}))
, corresponding to the right-hand side.

In contrast, our decomposition is only algebraic, and corresponds to a version of (
1.29
) which is divided by
(1+q)
.
Using the divided difference operators, we identify a sub-representation

H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{+}))^{*s_{i}}\subseteq H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{+}))

(1.31)

such that
H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{+}))
is the direct sum of a plain copy and a degree-shifted copy of
H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{+}))^{*s_{i}}
; similarly for
H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{-}))
.
Then, we show that a single, plain copy of
H_{T}^{*}(X(h))
is the direct sum of a plain copy of
H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{+}))^{*s_{i}}
and a degree-shifted copy of
H_{T}^{*}(X(h_{-}))^{*s_{i}}
.

2
Divisibility conditions

We now describe a family of subrings of the ring of functions
H
, which will be a convenient setting for the divided difference operators, and among which are the equivariant cohomology rings of Hessenberg varieties.

For an element
f\in H
and a transposition
\tau=(i\leftrightarrow k)\in S_{n}
, we say that
f
satisfies condition
\tau
if

f*(1-\tau)\quad\text{is a multiple of}\quad(x_{i}-x_{k}).

(2.1)

Note that, in particular, any multiple of
(x_{i}-x_{k})
satisfies condition
\tau
:

\big((x_{i}-x_{k})f\big)*(1-\tau)=(x_{i}-x_{k})\big(f*(1+\tau)\big).

(2.2)

The set of elements which satisfy condition
\tau
is closed under addition and multiplication, so they form a subring of
H
, which we call
H_{\tau}
.
More generally, for a set of transpositions
C\subseteq S_{n}
, we write

H_{C}=\bigcap_{\tau\in C}H_{\tau}

(2.3)

for the subring of elements which satisfy all conditions in
C
.
If
C
is the empty set, then
H_{C}
is the entire ring
H
.

Note that the elements
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
satisfy all divisibility conditions, so the entire image of
\varphi:R\to H
does too.

If
w\in S_{n}
and
f
satisfies condition
\tau
, then

\begin{gathered}\text{$w\cdot f$ satisfies condition $\tau$, and}\\
\text{$f*w$ satisfies condition $w^{-1}\tau w$,}\end{gathered}

(2.4)

so each
H_{C}
is closed under the dot action, but usually
not
the star action.

For a Hessenberg function
h
, the GKM presentation of the equivariant cohomology ring for the corresponding Hessenberg variety is

H_{T}^{*}(X(h))=H_{C(h)},

(2.5)

with the following set of divisibility conditions:

C(h)=\{(i\leftrightarrow k)\mid i<k\leq h(i)\}.

(2.6)

Recall that the definition of the
i
th divided difference operator is

\partial_{i}(f)=\frac{f*(1-s_{i})}{x_{i}-x_{(i+1)}},

(2.7)

if the quotient exists in
H
.
Since this is the defining condition of the subring
H_{s_{i}}
, we can write the largest domain of definition of
\partial_{i}
as:

\partial_{i}:H_{s_{i}}\to H.

(2.8)

For Hessenberg functions, note that
s_{i}\in C(h)
whenever
h(i)\neq i
.
We give a more careful account of some domains and codomains for
\partial_{i}
in
Section 4
.

We record here some of the convenient computational properties of divided differences which follow directly from the definition.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
f,g\in H_{s_{i}}
and
w\in S_{n}
, we have:

1.

\partial_{i}(f+g)=\partial_{i}(f)+\partial_{i}(g)
,

2.

\partial_{i}(fg)=\partial_{i}(f)\,g+(f*s_{i})\,\partial_{i}(g)
,

3.

\partial_{i}(fg)=f\,\partial_{i}(g)
when
\partial_{i}(f)=0
,

4.

\partial_{i}(f)=0
exactly when
f*s_{i}=f
,

5.

\partial_{i}(w\cdot f)=w\cdot\partial_{i}(f)
,

6.

\partial_{i}(f*s_{i})=-\partial_{i}(f)
,

7.

\partial_{i}(f)*s_{i}=\partial_{i}(f)
,

8.

\partial_{i}(f)*w=\partial_{k}(f*w)
when
w(k)=i
and
w(k+1)=i+1
,

9.

\partial_{i}(t_{k})=0
,

10.

\partial_{i}
is
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
-linear,

11.

\partial_{i}(x_{i})=1
,

12.

\partial_{i}(x_{(i+1)})=-1
,

13.

\partial_{i}(x_{k})=0
when
k\notin\{i,i+1\}
.

Proof.

By direct computation, keeping in mind that if the quotient exists, then
(f/g)*w=(f*w)/(g*w)
.
∎

4
Domains and codomains

The divided difference operators aren’t defined on all of
H
, and don’t preserve the subrings
H_{C}
in general.
But, motivated by the following lemma, we say that a set of conditions
C
is
s_{i}
-stable
if

s_{i}\in C\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i}Cs_{i}=C.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.1
.

If
C
is
s_{i}
-stable, then
\partial_{i}
is defined on
H_{C}
, and
\partial_{i}(H_{C})\subseteq H_{C}
.

Since
H_{C}\subseteq H_{D}
whenever
D\subseteq C
, we can also consider the largest
s_{i}
-stable subset of
C
and phrase this equivalently as:

Corollary 4.2
.

If
s_{i}\in C
and
D=C\cap s_{i}Cs_{i}
, then
\partial_{i}
is defined on
H_{C}
, and
\partial_{i}(H_{C})\subseteq H_{D}
.

Proof of
4.1
.

Since
s_{i}\in C
, it’s immediate that
\partial_{i}
is defined on
H_{C}
.
To show that
\partial_{i}(H_{C})\subseteq H_{C}
, we will reduce to the case of three specific sets
C
, each of which can be checked on a small
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
-submodule of
H_{C}
, and provide an explicit basis for this submodule.

As a first reduction, it suffices to check that
\partial_{i}(H_{C})\subseteq H_{C}
for the
minimal

s_{i}
-stable sets
C
, which are of the form

(4.2)

because for a general

(4.3)

As a second reduction, it suffices to check the three specific

\displaystyle C(1,s_{1})

\displaystyle=\{(1\leftrightarrow 2)\}

\displaystyle C(1,s_{2})

\displaystyle\subseteq^{?}H_{C(1,s_{1})}*w=H_{C(i,\tau)}.

\displaystyle\subseteq^{?}H_{C(1,s_{2})}*w=H_{C(i,\tau)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{1}\big(H_{C(1,s_{3})}\big)*w

(4.10)

\displaystyle\subseteq^{?}H_{C(1,s_{3})}*w=H_{C(i,\tau)}

As an independent reduction, it suffices to check that

on a certain
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
-submodule of

.

5
Decompositions of dot representations

Now that we have a handle on the domain and range of the divided difference operators, we can use them to decompose of some of the subrings
H_{C}
.

5.1
The
s_{i}
-stable case

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
C
be an
s_{i}
-stable set of divisibility conditions.
Then, we have the internal direct sum decomposition

H_{C}=H_{C}^{*s_{i}}\oplus(x_{i}-x_{(i+1)})H_{C}^{*s_{i}}

(5.1)

as
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
-submodules of
H
equipped with the dot action, where

H_{C}^{*s_{i}}=\{f\in H_{C}\mid f*s_{i}=f\}.

(5.2)

Proof.

Since
C
is
s_{i}
-stable, the subgroup
\{\id,s_{i}\}\subseteq S_{n}
of order two acts on the right on
H_{C}
by the star action, and we have the natural decomposition

H_{C}=H_{C}^{*s_{i}}\oplus H_{C}^{*-s_{i}},

(5.3)

where we write

H_{C}^{*-s_{i}}=\{f\in H_{C}\mid f*s_{i}=-f\}

(5.4)

for the
(-1)
-eigenspace of the involution
f\mapsto f*s_{i}
.
We claim that

H_{C}^{*-s_{i}}=(x_{i}-x_{(i+1)})H_{C}^{*s_{i}},

(5.5)

because we further claim that the maps

H_{C}^{*-s_{i}}
H_{C}^{*s_{i}}
\partial_{i}
(x_{i}-x_{(i+1)})/2

(5.6)

form a bijection.
This follows from the facts that

\begin{gathered}\partial_{i}(H_{C}^{*-s_{i}})\subseteq\partial_{i}(H_{C})\subseteq H_{C}^{*s_{i}},\\
(x_{i}-x_{(i+1)})H_{C}^{*s_{i}}\subseteq H_{C}^{*-s_{i}},\\
\partial_{i}\big((x_{i}-x_{(i+1)})f\big)/2=f*(1+s_{i})/2=f\quad\text{when $f\in H_{C}^{*s_{i}}$},\\
(x_{i}-x_{(i+1)})\partial_{i}(f)/2=f*(1-s_{i})/2=f\quad\text{when $f\in H_{C}^{*-s_{i}}$},\end{gathered}

(5.7)

which can be verified by direct computation.
∎

The proof of
Theorem 5.1
yields an additional fact:

Corollary 5.2
.

The kernel and image of
\partial_{i}:H_{C}\to H_{C}
are equal to
H_{C}^{*s_{i}}
when
C
is
s_{i}
-stable.

5.2
The almost-
s_{i}
-stable case

Motivated by the following decomposition, we define the notion of almost-stability. We say that a set of divisibility conditions
C
is
almost-
s_{i}
-stable
if
s_{i}\in C
and there is a unique transposition
\tau
such that
\tau\in C
but
s_{i}\tau s_{i}\notin C
. If this is the case, then the sets

C_{-}=C\setminus\{\tau\},\qquad C_{+}=C\cup\{s_{i}\tau s_{i}\},

(5.8)

obtained from
C
by removing or adding a single condition, are
s_{i}
-stable.

Note that, for sets of divisibility conditions obtained from Hessenberg functions as in (
2.6
), the notion of almost-stability corresponds to the notion of a modular triple in (
1.29
).

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
C
be an almost-
s_{i}
-stable set of divisibility conditions, so that
C_{-}=C\setminus\{\tau\}
and
C_{+}=C\cup\{s_{i}\tau s_{i}\}
are
s_{i}
-stable.
If
\tau=(i\leftrightarrow k)
, then we have the internal direct sum decomposition

H_{C}=H_{C_{+}}^{*s_{i}}\oplus(x_{i}-x_{k})H_{C_{-}}^{*s_{i}}

(5.9)

as
\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
-submodules of
H
equipped with the dot action.
Otherwise,
\tau=(i+1\leftrightarrow k)
and we have the internal direct sum decomposition

H_{C}=H_{C_{+}}^{*s_{i}}\oplus(x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})H_{C_{-}}^{*s_{i}}.

(5.10)

Proof.

We will assume that
\tau=(i\leftrightarrow k)
, since the case where
\tau=(i+1\leftrightarrow k)
follows by using the transformation

H_{C}*s_{i}=H_{s_{i}Cs_{i}}.

(5.11)

We claim that the decomposition is given by the following maps:

H_{C_{+}}^{*s_{i}}
H_{C}
H_{C_{-}}^{*s_{i}}
\id
\partial_{i}\circ(x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})
\partial_{i}
(x_{i}-x_{k})

(5.12)

To prove this, we need to check that:

•

All four maps land in the stated codomain.
Using the inclusions

\begin{gathered}H_{C_{+}}\subseteq H_{C}\subseteq H_{C_{-}}\\
(x_{i}-x_{k})H_{C_{-}}\subseteq H_{C}\\
(x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})H_{C}\subseteq H_{C_{+}},\end{gathered}

(5.13)

we indeed have that

\begin{gathered}\id(H_{C_{+}}^{*s_{i}})\subseteq H_{C_{+}}\subseteq H_{C}\\
\partial_{i}(H_{C})\subseteq\partial_{i}(H_{C_{-}})\subseteq H_{C_{-}}^{*s_{i}}\\
\partial_{i}\big((x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})H_{C}\big)\subseteq\partial_{i}(H_{C_{+}})\subseteq H_{C_{+}}^{*s_{i}}\\
(x_{i}-x_{k})H_{C_{-}}^{*s_{i}}\subseteq(x_{i}-x_{k})H_{C_{-}}\subseteq H_{C}.\end{gathered}

(5.14)

•

The two compositions
H_{C}\to H_{C}
are complementary idempotents.
Using the computational properties from
2.1
, we have

\begin{gathered}\partial_{i}\big((x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})f\big)+(x_{i}-x_{k})\partial_{i}(f)=f\\
\partial_{i}\Big((x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})\partial_{i}\big((x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})f\big)\Big)=\partial_{i}\big((x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})f\big)\\
(x_{i}-x_{k})\partial_{i}\big((x_{i}-x_{k})\partial_{i}(f)\big)=(x_{i}-x_{k})\partial_{i}(f)\end{gathered}

(5.15)

for every
f\in H_{C}
, as required.

•

The compositions
H_{C_{-}}^{*s_{i}}\to H_{C_{-}}^{*s_{i}}
and
H_{C_{+}}^{*s_{i}}\to H_{C_{+}}^{*s_{i}}
are the identity.
Again using
2.1
, we have

\begin{gathered}\partial_{i}\big((x_{i}-x_{k})f\big)=f+(x_{(i+1)}-x_{k})\partial_{i}(f)=f\quad\text{for $f\in H_{C_{-}}^{*s_{i}}$},\\
\partial_{i}\big((x_{k}-x_{(i+1)})f\big)=f+(x_{i}-x_{k})\partial_{i}(f)=f\quad\text{for $f\in H_{C_{+}}^{*s_{i}}$},\end{gathered}

(5.16)

as required.
∎

Note on single-trace EYM amplitudes with MHV configuration
Zhirun Li, Yi-Jian Du

Abstract
In the maximally-helicity-violating (MHV) configuration, tree-level single-trace Einstein-Yang-Mills (EYM) amplitude with one and two gravitons have been shown to satisfy a formula where each graviton splits into a pair of collinear gluons. In this paper, we extend this formula to more general cases. We provide a general formula which expresses tree-level single-trace MHV amplitudes in terms of pure gluon amplitudes, where each graviton turns into a pair of collinear gluons.

Introduction

In four dimensional spacetime, tree-level single-trace maximally-helicity-violating (MHV) amplitudes of Einstein-Yang-Mills (EYM) theory have been shown to satisfy the Selivanov-Bern-De Freitas-Wong
Selivanov:1997aq
;
Selivanov:1997ts
;

;
Cachazo:2013hca
;
Cachazo:2013iea
formula gives a general approach to EYM amplitudes, which is independent of the dimension of spacetime and the helicity configuration. In four dimensions, the CHY formula has been shown to provide a
spanning forest formula
(first proposed in gravity, along the line of
Nguyen:2009jk
,
Hodges:2012ym
and
Feng:2012sy
) for the single-trace MHV amplitude
Du:2016wkt
, which was further proven to be equivalent with the SBDW formula
Du:2016wkt
and was generalized to double-trace MHV amplitudes
Tian:2021dzf
via the recursion expansion formula
Fu:2017uzt
;
Chiodaroli:2017ngp
;
Teng:2017tbo
;
Du:2017kpo
;
Du:2017gnh
.

From another perspective, as pointed out in earlier literatures
Stieberger:2009hq
;
Chen:2010sr
;
Stieberger:2014cea
;
Stieberger:2015qja
;
Stieberger:2015kia
, each graviton in an EYM amplitude could be considered as a pair of collinear gluons which carry the same momentum and the same helicity. Particularly, inspired by the SBDW formula,
Chen:2010sr
pointed out that the single-trace MHV amplitude with one and two gravitons can be explicitly expressed in terms of the MHV amplitudes where each graviton splits into a pair of collinear gluons
Chen:2010sr
. This explicit formula of the single-trace MHV amplitudes was not extended into cases with an arbitrary number of gravitons yet. In this note, we take a small step forward in this direction:
we provide a general formula for single-trace MHV amplitudes where each graviton splits into a pair of collinear gluons.
When the number of gravitons is one or two, this formula turns back into the known results
Chen:2010sr
. We hope this approach may provide a new insight for the study of helicity amplitudes in EYM.

2.1
Spinor-helicity formalism in four dimensions

The momentum
k^{\mu}_{i}
of each on-shell massless particle
i
is expressed by two copies of Weyl spinors
\lambda_{i}^{a}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{a}}
. We define the spinor products as

\displaystyle\left\langle i,j\right\rangle\equiv\epsilon_{a{b}}\lambda_{i}^{a}{\lambda}_{j}^{{b}},~~~~~~~~~~\left[i,j\right]\equiv\epsilon_{\dot{a}\dot{b}}\widetilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{a}}\widetilde{\lambda}_{j}^{\dot{b}},

(2.1)

where
\epsilon_{{a}{b}}
and
\epsilon_{\dot{a}\dot{b}}
are totally antisymmetric tensors. Apparently, the spinor products are antisymmetric objects under the exchanging of the two spinors. With this expression, the Lorentz contraction of two momenta
k^{\mu}_{a}
and
k^{\mu}_{b}
reads:

\displaystyle k_{a}\cdot k_{b}=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\left[b,a\right].

(2.2)

More helpful properties in spinor-helicity formalsim are displayed as follows.

•

Momentum conservation for an
n
-point amplitude:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq\,j,k\\
i=1\end{subarray}}^{n}\left[j,i\right]\left\langle i,k\right\rangle=0.~~~~

(2.3)

•

Schouten identity:

\displaystyle\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\left\langle c,d\right\rangle=\left\langle a,c\right\rangle\left\langle b,d\right\rangle+\left\langle b,c\right\rangle\left\langle d,a\right\rangle,~~~\left[a,b\right]\left[c,d\right]=\left[a,c\right]\left[b,d\right]+\left[b,c\right]\left[d,a\right].

(2.4)

•

The eikonal identity resulted by Schouten identity

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=j}^{k-1}\frac{\left\langle i,i+1\right\rangle}{\left\langle i,q\right\rangle\left\langle q,i+1\right\rangle}=\frac{\left\langle j,k\right\rangle}{\left\langle j,q\right\rangle\left\langle q,k\right\rangle}.~~~~~~~

(2.5)

Finally, the
n
-gluon MHV amplitude
A(1,...,n)
at tree level satisfies the famous Parke-Taylor formula
Parke:1986gb
1
1

1

In this work, we use
\sim
to neglect the coupling constant and an overall normalization factor.

\displaystyle A(1,...,n)\sim\frac{\langle ij\rangle^{4}}{\langle 12\rangle\langle 23\rangle\cdots\langle n1\rangle},

(2.6)

where
i
,
j
denote the two negative-helicity gluons and other gluons are supposed to be positive-helicity ones.

2.2
SBDW formula and the spanning forest formula

In EYM, there are two possible situations of the tree-level single-trace MHV amplitudes: the (

(2.7)

where

N
are gluons arranged in a fixed ordering,
\mathrm{H}=\{n_{1},...,n_{M}\}
are gravitons which are independent of color orderings. The negative-helicity gluons are supposed to be
i
and

\displaystyle S(\mathrm{H};\{1,...,N\})

\displaystyle=\quantity(\prod\limits_{m\in\mathrm{H}}\frac{d}{da_{m}})\exp[\sum\limits_{n_{1}\in\mathrm{H}}a_{n_{1}}\sum\limits_{l\in\mathrm{G}}\psi_{ln_{1}}\exp[\sum\limits_{n_{2}\in\mathrm{H},n_{2}\neq n_{1}}a_{n_{2}}\psi_{n_{1}n_{2}}\exp(...)]]\Bigg|_{a_{m}=0},

(2.8)

in which

\psi_{ab}\equiv\frac{[ab]\langle a\xi\rangle\langle a\eta\rangle}{\langle ab\rangle\langle b\xi\rangle\langle b\eta\rangle}

(2.9)

where
\xi
,
\eta
are arbitrarily chosen reference spinors and
\mathrm{G}
is the gluon set.
In this note, we set
\xi=1
and
\eta=N
when studying the (
g^{-},g^{-}
) configuration.

It was shown in

that

could be expanded by spanning forest form. Particularly:

S(\mathrm{H};\mathrm{G})=\sum\limits_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{G}}(\mathrm{G}\cup\mathrm{H})}\quantity(\prod\limits_{ab\in E(F)}\psi_{ab}),

(2.10)

where we have summed over all possible forests
F
, where gluons and gravitons are considered as vertices, and the gluons are considered as the root set.
Each edge
ab
is dressed by
\psi_{ab}
, and multiply all such edges in a given forest
F
together.

In the case of (
h^{-},g^{-}
), the formulas (
2.7
) (
2.8
) and (
2.10
) are slightly changed

;
Du:2016wkt
;

via (i). replacing

) and (

\displaystyle~\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(l_{1})}\in\Big\{\{2,...,l_{1}-1\}\shuffle\{n_{a}\},l_{1},\{l_{1}+1,...,N-1\}\shuffle\{\widetilde{n}_{a}\}\Big\}.

Having displayed the example with three gravitons, we turn to the general formula in the next section.

4
The general formula

Inspired by the example in the previous section, we propose the following general formula where gravitions split into pairs of collinear gluons

\displaystyle A(1,...,N|\mathrm{H})\sim\sum\limits_{l_{1},...,l_{i}\in\mathrm{G}}\,\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Spanning Forests}\\
\{\mathcal{T}_{1},...,\mathcal{T}_{i}\}\end{subarray}}\,K(\mathcal{T}_{1})\,...\,K(\mathcal{T}_{i})\,\text{PT}\left(1,\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(l_{1},...,l_{i})},N\right).

(4.1)

Here we sum over all possible spanning forests in which trees are planted at gluons
l_{1},...,l_{i}\in\mathrm{G}
. This summation is expressed by two summations:

•

(i). summing over all possible choices of the roots
l_{1},...,l_{i}
(
i=1,...,M
),

•

(ii). for a given choice of roots
l_{1}
,…,
l_{i}
, summing over all possible configurations of forests, which consist of nontrivial trees
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, …,
\mathcal{T}_{i}
planted at the gluons
l_{1}
,…,
l_{i}
.

For a fixed forest, each tree
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is associated with a factor
K(\mathcal{T}_{k})
where each edge between two vertices
a
,
b
is assigned by a factor
s_{ab}
. The permutations
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(l_{1},...,l_{k})}
in the PT factors can be defined recursively:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(l_{1},...,l_{k})}=\left\{\,\boldsymbol{\rho}_{L}^{(l_{1},...,l_{k-1})}\shuffle\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}},\,l_{k},\,\boldsymbol{\rho}_{R}^{(l_{1},...,l_{k-1})}\shuffle\big(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}\big)^{T}\right\}.~~(k\leq i)

(4.2)

where
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{L}^{(l_{1},...,l_{k-1})}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{R}^{(l_{1},...,l_{k-1})}
denote the two ordered sets which are separated by the gluon
l_{k}
in the permutation
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(l_{1},...,l_{k-1})}
. The
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
(
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
) stands for the permutations established by the tree graph
\mathcal{T}_{k}
whose nodes are
\{n_{i}\}
(
\{\widetilde{n}_{i}\}
), while
(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}})^{T}
denotes the reverse of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
.

Now we sketch the proof of the general formula (
4.1
):

•

(i).
Step-1
Expand the MHV amplitude according to (
2.7
) and (
2.10
) in terms of spanning forests. Each forest
F
in general consists of
i
tree structures
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, …,
\mathcal{T}_{i}
planted to gluons
l_{1},...,l_{i}\in\mathrm{G}
.

•

(ii).
Step-2
For a given forest
F=\{\mathcal{T}_{1}
, …,
\mathcal{T}_{i}\}
and the tree
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, there are two types of edges (a). the edge between a graviton
a
and the root (a gluon
l_{1}\in\mathrm{G}
), (b). The edge between two gravitons
b
and
c
. In the former case, the edge is associated with a factor
\psi_{a}
which is expressed according to (
3.4
), while an edge of the latter form is accompanied by a factor
\psi_{bc}
, which is further rewritten as (
3.6
). After this manipulation, the factor
\psi_{a}
splits the graviton
n_{a}
into collinear gluons
n_{a}
and
\widetilde{n}_{a}
and then inserts them to the left and right of
l_{1}
, respectively. A factor
\psi_{bc}
splits the graviton
n_{c}
into collinear gluons
n_{c}
and
\widetilde{n}_{c}
which are further inserted to the left of
n_{b}
and the right of
\widetilde{n}_{b}
(
n_{b}
which is nearer to root than
n_{c}
has already been treated before). The factor assigned to each edge
bc
is
s_{bc}
, and the product of all these factors gives
K(\mathcal{T}_{1})
. The permutations established by this step are given by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{(l_{1})}=\left\{\{2,...,l_{1}-1\}\shuffle\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\mathcal{T}_{1}},l_{1},\{l_{1}+1,...,N-1\}\shuffle\big(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\mathcal{T}_{1}}\big)^{T}\right\}.

(4.3)

•

(iii).
Step-3
Insert the collinear gluons corresponding to the gravitons on trees
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, …,
\mathcal{T}_{i}
in turn, by repeating step-2. We finally get the general formula (
4.1
) with permutations defined in (
4.2
).

5
Conclusion

In this note, we presented a formula (
4.1
) for single-trace EYM amplitudes in the MHV configuration (with two negative-helicity gluons). Each graviton in this formula splits into a pair of collinear gluons. Thus an
N
-gluon,
M
-graviton amplitude is finally expressed as a combination of
N+2M
gluon amplitudes with
M
pairs of collinear gluons. When the adjustment pointed in section
2
is considered, the formula (
4.1
) is straightforwardly extended to the MHV amplitude with one negative-helicity gluon and one negative-helicity graviton via (i). replacing
i
,
j
in the numerator of the PT factor by the negative-helicity graviton and the negative-helicity gluon, (ii). using the positive-helicity graviton set instead of the full graviton set on the RHS of (
4.1
). (iii). dressing the expression by an extra sign
(-1)
.
It is worth extending the collinear expression in the current paper to the double-trace amplitudes and amplitudes with other helicity configurations in a future work.

Local Distance Antimagic Labeling of Neighborhood Balanced Colored Graphs


Abstract
Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a graph without isolated vertices. A bijection f : V → { 1 , 2 , … , | V | } f\colon V\to\{1,2,\dots,|V|\} is called a local distance antimagic labeling if w ⁡ ( u ) ≠ w ⁡ ( v ) w(u)\not=w(v) for every edge u ​ v ∈ E uv\in E , where the weight w ⁡ ( u ) w(u) of a vertex u u is defined as the sum of labels assigned to all vertices adjacent to u u . The local distance antimagic chromatic number of G G , denoted by χ l ​ d ​ ( G ) \chi_{ld}(G) , is defined as the minimum number of colors taken over all colorings of G G induced by local distance antimagic labelings. In this paper, we investigate local distance antimagic labelings of the direct product and the union of certain classes of graphs, namely, neighborhood balanced k k -colored graphs and nearly neighborhood balanced k k -colored graphs.

Introduction

By a graph
G=(V,E)
, we mean a finite, simple, undirected graph having neither multiple edges nor loops. For graph theoretic terminology and notation, we refer to Chartrand and Lesniak
[
5
]
.

The concept of antimagic labeling was introduced by Hartsfield and Ringel
[
12
]
in 1990. A graph
G
is said to be
antimagic
if its edges can be labeled with the integers
\{1,2,\dots,|E|\}
in such a way that the sums of the labels of the edges incident to each vertex (called the weight of the vertex) are all distinct. Hartsfield and Ringel conjectured that every connected graph with at least three vertices admits an antimagic labeling. They also proposed a weaker conjecture stating that every tree with at least three vertices admits an antimagic labeling. Both conjectures have been partially verified by several authors, but remain open in general, see Gallian
[
8
]
for a comprehensive survey.

Arumugam and Kamatchi
[
14
]
introduced a vertex version of the antimagic labeling of a graph, defined as follows. A bijection
f:V\to\{1,2,\dots,|V|\}
is called a
distance antimagic labeling
of
G
if all vertices of
G
have distinct vertex weights, where the weight of a vertex
v
is defined by
w(v)=\sum_{x\in N(v)}f(x)
, where
N(v)
denotes the open neighborhood of
v
, i.e., the set of all vertices of
G
adjacent to
v
. A graph
G
is called a
distance antimagic graph
if it admits a distance antimagic labeling
f
.

Arumugam et al.
[
2
]
introduced a local version of antimagic labeling. A bijection
f\colon E\to\{1,2,\dots,|E|\}
is called a
local antimagic labeling
of
G
if for any two adjacent vertices
u
and
v
,
w(u)\not=w(v)
, where
w(u)=\sum_{e\in E(u)}f(e)
, and
E(u)
denotes the set of edges incident to
u
. Thus, any local antimagic labeling induces a proper vertex coloring of
G
, where each vertex
v
is assigned the color
w(v)
. The
local antimagic chromatic number
of
G
, denoted by
\chi_{la}(G)
, is the minimum number of colors taken over all colorings of
G
induced by local antimagic labelings. Arumugam et al.
[
2
]
conjectured that every connected graph with at least three vertices admits a local antimagic labeling. This conjecture was partially verified by Bensmail et al.
[
4
]
, and later completely resolved by Haslegrave
[
13
]
using probabilistic methods.
Recently, several authors have studied the local antimagic chromatic number for various classes of graphs; see, for instance,
[
2
,
3
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
]
.

Motivated by the concept local antimagic labeling, Divya et al.
[
6
]
and Handa et al.
[
9
]
independently introduced the notion of local distance antimagic labeling. Let
f\colon V\to\{1,2,\dots,|V|\}
be a bijection. For each vertex
v\in V
, the weight of
v
is defined by
w(v)=\sum_{x\in N(v)}f(x)
. The labeling
f
is called a
local distance antimagic labeling
of
G
if
w(u)\not=w(v)
for every pair of adjacent vertices
u,v\in V
. A graph that admits such a labeling is called a
local distance antimagic graph
. Every local distance antimagic labeling induces a proper vertex coloring of the graph, where each vertex
v
is assigned the color
w(v)
. The
local distance antimagic chromatic number
of
G
, denoted
\chi_{ld}(G)
, is the minimum number of colors taken over all colorings of
G
induced by local distance antimagic labelings. Clearly,
\chi_{ld}(G)\geq\chi(G)
, where

denotes the chromatic number of

.

Several authors have investigated the local distance antimagic chromatic numbers for various classes of graphs, see
[
6
,
9
,
21
,

,
23
,
25
]
for further results and related studies. Handa et al.

]
established the following result, which provides a useful lower bound for the local distance antimagic chromatic number of a graph.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
9
]

Let

be a local distance antimagic graph. If

and

are vertices such that

, where

The following result, due to Priyadharshini et al.
[
21
]
, provides a lower bound for the local distance antimagic chromatic number of trees in terms of the number of their support vertices, that is, vertices adjacent to at least one leaf.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
21
]

Let

be a tree on
n\geq 3
vertices such that the set of distinct neighbors of all the leaves is of cardinality

\chi_{ld}(T)\geq t+1
.

In our investigation, we also make use of the concept of a magic rectangle. Let

be positive integers.
A
magic rectangle

MR(a,b)=(m_{i,j})
is an

array containing the integers

each appearing exactly once, such that all row sums are equal to a constant
\rho
and all column sums are equal to a constant
\sigma
. The sum of all entries in

is
{ab(ab+1)}/{2}
, and the corresponding magic constants are given by

\displaystyle\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{a}m_{ij}=\tfrac{a(ab+1)}{2},

for any
j\in\{1,2,\dots,b\}
and

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{j=1}^{b}m_{ij}=\tfrac{b(ab+1)}{2},

for any
i\in\{1,2,\dots,a\}
.

Harmuth

,

]

and

and

is

a=b=2
.

Theorem 1.10
.

[

,
11

.

Throughout this paper, all graphs

G
are assumed to admit a local distance antimagic labeling, with local distance antimagic chromatic number
\chi_{ld}(G)
. For a graph
G
, and a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we denote by
N_{G}(v)
the neighborhood of
v
in
G
and by
w_{G}(v)
the weight of
v
. When the context is clear, the subscript
G
will be omitted from both notations.

Although local distance antimagic labeling and neighborhood balanced coloring are defined independently, the latter provides the structural tool used in our labeling constructions. In a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring, every vertex has the same number of neighbors in each color class. This property enables us to distribute blocks of labels among the color classes so that the additional terms introduced in the direct product or in different copies of a graph cancel when vertex weights are computed. Consequently, the weight of a vertex in the resulting graph can be expressed in terms of its weight under the original local distance antimagic labeling. The concepts developed in Sections
2
and
3
are therefore essential to the constructions and bounds established in Section
4
.

In Section
2
, we recall the concept of neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of graphs. Motivated by this concept, Section
3
introduces the notion of nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of graphs and presents several illustrative examples. Furthermore, at the beginning of Section
4
, we provide a counterexample to the characterization of graphs with local distance antimagic chromatic number two, claimed by Priyadharshini et al.
[
21
]
. In Subsections
4.1
and
4.2
, we use the concepts of neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring and nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring to study the local distance antimagic labeling of direct products and unions of graphs.

2
Neighborhood Balanced
k
-Coloring of Graphs

Freyberg et al.
[
7
]
introduced the concept of
neighborhood balanced coloring
.
A neighborhood balanced coloring of a graph
G
is a vertex coloring of
G
using two colors, say red and green, such that each vertex has the same number of neighbors in both color classes.
A graph that admits a neighborhood balanced coloring is called
neighborhood balanced colorable
or a
neighborhood balanced colored graph
(see Figure
1
). The authors investigated structural properties of graphs that admit such a coloring and presented several classes of graphs possessing this property.

Minyard et al.
[
18
]
extended this notion and introduced the concept of neighborhood balanced
3
-coloring.
A graph is said to admit a
neighborhood balanced
3
-coloring
if its vertices can be colored with three colors such that every vertex has an equal number of neighbors in each color class.
They also characterized several families of graphs that admit such a coloring.

Almeida et al.
[
1
]
further generalized the concepts to
k\geq 2
colors as follows. If the vertices of a graph
G
can be colored using the
k
colors, say
1,2,\dots,k
, so that every vertex has an equal number of neighbors in each color class, then the coloring is called a
neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring
.
A graph that admits such a coloring is referred to as
neighborhood balanced
k
-colorable
or a
neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph
.

Equivalently, a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of graph
G
is a partition of its vertex set
V
of
G
into
k
subsets
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}
(that is, the color classes) such that every vertex has the same number of neighbors in each subset. Let
|E(V_{i},V_{j})|
denote the number edges between the color classes
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, and let
|V_{i}|
denote the number of vertices in
V_{i}
.

It is often convenient to represent a
k
-coloring by a function
c\colon V(G)\to\{1,2,\dots,k\}
.
Define
w(v)=\sum_{u\in N(v)}c(u)
.
If
c
is a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
, then

w(v)\equiv 0\pmod{{k(k+1)}/{2}}

for every
v\in V(G)
.
For further details, see
[
1
]
.

The following lemma provides a necessary condition for a graph to admit a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
1
]

If a graph
G
admits a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring, then the degree of every vertex is a multiple of
k
.

The following lemma indicates that a neighborhood balanced coloring with
k
colors can be reduced to one with fewer colors. In particular, if
k
is even, the graph admits a neighborhood balanced
2
-coloring, and if
k
is odd, it admits a neighborhood balanced
p
-coloring, where

p
is the smallest prime divisor of
k
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
1
]

If a graph
G
admits neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring and
p
divides
k
, then the graph
G
admits neighborhood balanced
p
-coloring.

The converse of this lemma does not hold in general.
For instance, the cycle
C_{m}
on
m
vertices admits a neighborhood balanced
2
-coloring whenever
m\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, but it does not admit a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring for any even
k>2
.

The following three results describe further structural properties of graphs that admit a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
1
]

Suppose
G
admits a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring. Then for any neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring
c
,

|E(V_{i},V_{j})|=\tfrac{2|E(G)|}{k^{2}}\text{\ and \ }|E(V_{i},V_{i})|=\tfrac{|E(G)|}{k^{2}}.

Corollary 2.4
.

[
1
]

Suppose
G
is an
r
-regular graph that admits a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring. Then
|V_{i}|={|V(G)|}/{k}
.

Corollary 2.5
.

[
1
]

If
G
is an
r
-regular graph that admits a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring, then
|V(G)|\equiv 0\pmod{k}
and
|E(G)|\equiv 0\pmod{k}
.

In the following, we discuss several families of graphs that have been examined in the literature concerning the existence of neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.

.

[

The cycle
C_{n}

.

[

Let

Theorem 2.8

[

3
Nearly Neighborhood Balanced
k
-Coloring of Graphs

Consider the star
K_{1,kn}
and color its
kn
pendant vertices so that each of the
k
colors is used exactly
n
times. In this coloring, the central (non-pendant) vertex has
n
neighbors of each color, however, it must itself also be assigned one of the
k
colors. Consequently, the pendant vertices deprive
K_{1,kn}
from admitting a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring. Motivated by this observation, we introduce a concept of a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of graphs as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

A vertex
k
-coloring of a graph
G
is called a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring if every non-pendant vertex of
G
has the same number of neighbors in each color class.

A graph that admits nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring is called
nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colorable
or
nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph
.
We begin by presenting a necessary condition for a graph to admit a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring, which is analogous to the necessary condition for neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs.

Lemma 3.2
.

If a graph
G
admits a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring, then the degree of every non-pendant vertex of
G
is a multiple of
k
.

The next Lemma is also analogous to Lemma
2.2
for neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs.

Lemma 3.3
.

If a graph
G
admits a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring and
p
divides
k
, then
G
admits a nearly neighborhood balanced
p
-coloring.

Next, we present examples of graphs that admit a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring. As described at the beginning of this section, the star
K_{1,kn}
admits a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring. We now state this observation formally.

Theorem 3.4
.

The star
K_{1,n}
admits a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring if and only if
n\equiv 0\pmod{k}
.

The bistar
B_{m,n}
is a tree with vertex set
V(B_{m,n})=\{a,b\}\cup\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}\cup\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
and edge set
E(B_{m,n})=\{ab\}\cup\{au_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}\cup\{bv_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
. We now give conditions on
m,n
for the bistar
B_{m,n}
to admit a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.

Theorem 3.5
.

The bistar
B_{m,n}
admits a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring if and only if
m\equiv-1\pmod{k}
and
n\equiv-1\pmod{k}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
m\not\equiv-1\pmod{k}
or
n\not\equiv-1\pmod{k}
. Then the degree of vertex
a
or vertex
b
is not a multiple of
k
. By Lemma
3.2
,
B_{m,n}
therefore does not admit a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.

Conversely, assume that
m\equiv-1\pmod{k}
and
n\equiv-1\pmod{k}
. Color the vertices
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,

u_{m}
, which are adjacent to
a
, using each of the
k
colors in a cyclic order. Since
m\equiv-1\pmod{k}
, one of the
k
colors is used exactly once fewer than each of the remaining
k-1
colors. Assign this color to the vertex
b
. Apply the same strategy to color the neighbors of vertex
b
, noting that
n\equiv-1\pmod{k}
. Under this coloring, every non-pendant vertex of
B_{m,n}
has the same number of neighbors in each color class.
Hence, the resulting coloring is a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
B_{m,n}
.
∎

Next, we examine the nearly neighborhood balanced
2
-coloring of the path
P_{n}
. Let
V(P_{n})=\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
and
E(P_{n})=\{v_{i}v_{i+1}:1\leq i\leq n-1\}
be the vertex and edge sets of
P_{n}
, respectively.

Theorem 3.6
.

The path
P_{n}
is a nearly neighborhood balanced
2
-colored graph for all
n\geq 3
.

Proof.

We define a coloring
c\colon V(P_{n})\to\{1,2\}
as follows;

\displaystyle c(v_{i})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i\equiv 1,2\pmod{4}$},\\
2&\text{if $i\equiv 0,3\pmod{4}$}.\end{cases}

By the definition of the coloring
c
, the vertices at distance two receive different colors.
Each non-pendant vertex
v_{i}
of
P_{n}
,
i=2,3,\dots,n-1
, has exactly two neighbors,
v_{i-1}
and
v_{i+1}
, which are assigned distinct colors.
Therefore, every non-pendant vertex has one neighbor of each color, and hence
c
is a nearly neighborhood balanced
2
-coloring of
P_{n}
.
∎

Now we present some nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs that can be constructed from neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs using the operation called the corona product of graphs.
The
corona product
of two graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\circ H
, is a graph obtained by taking one copy of
G
and
|V(G)|
copies of
H
, and joining the
i
th vertex of
G
to every vertex in the
i
th copy of
H
. By definition, if
G
is a graph of order
m
and
H
is a graph of order
n
, then
G\circ H
has order
m+mn
. Furthermore, if
V(G)=\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq m\}
and
V(H)=\{x_{j}:1\leq j\leq n\}
denote the vertex sets of graphs
G
and
H
, respectively, then the vertex set of the graph
G\circ H
is given by
V(G\circ H)=V(G)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\{x_{i,j}:1\leq j\leq n\}
, where each set
\{x_{i,1},x_{i,2},\dots,x_{i,n}\}
corresponds to the vertices of the
i
th copy of
H
attached to the vertex
v_{i}\in V(G)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

If
G
is a neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph, then
G\circ\overline{K_{n}}
is a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph if and only if
n\equiv 0\pmod{k}
, where
\overline{K_{n}}
denotes the complement of the complete graph on
n
vertices.

Proof.

Let
c
be a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring of
G
. Since
G
admits a neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring, by Lemma
2.1
, the degree of each vertex of
G
is a multiple of
k
.

If
n\not\equiv 0\pmod{k}
, then the degree of non-pendant vertices of
G\circ\overline{K_{n}}
(all vertices of
G
are non-pendant vertices of
G\circ\overline{K_{n}}
) is not a multiple of
k
and therefore, by
Lemma
3.2
,
G\circ\overline{K_{n}}
does not admit a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring.

Conversely, suppose
n\equiv 0\pmod{k}
. Define a coloring
c^{\prime}:V(G\circ\overline{K_{n}})\to\{1,2,\dots,k\}
by

\displaystyle c^{\prime}(x)=\begin{cases}c(x)&\text{if $x\in V(G)$},\\
p&\text{if $x=x_{i,j}$, $1\leq i\leq m$, and $j\equiv p\pmod{k}$, $1\leq j\leq n$.}\end{cases}

Consider any non-pendant vertex
v_{i}
of
G\circ\overline{K_{n}}
. Among the vertices of
G
, its neighbors are colored using
c
, so
v_{i}
has an equal number of neighbors in each of the

k
color classes. Moreover, since
n\equiv 0\pmod{k}
, the vertices
x_{i,1},x_{i,2},\dots,x_{i,n}
are colored equally using each of the
k
colors. Therefore,
v_{i}
continues to have the same number of neighbors of each color, satisfying the condition for a nearly neighborhood balanced coloring. Thus,
G\circ\overline{K_{n}}
is a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.
∎

The above result demonstrates that every neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph is an induced subgraph of a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph.

4
Main Results

Priyadharshini et al.
[
21
]
claimed to have classified connected graphs with local distance antimagic chromatic number 2 in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
21
]

A connected graph
G
has
\chi_{ld}(G)=2
if and only if it is a complete bipartite graph.

However, we have found a counterexample to this result, which shows that the above classification of connected graphs admitting local distance antimagic chromatic number 2 is incorrect. Our counterexample is obtained by taking the direct product of a regular graph with a complete bipartite graph.

For completeness, we recall the definition of the direct product of graphs. The
direct product
of graphs
G
and
H
, denoted by
G\times H
, is the graph with vertex set
V(G)\times V(H)
, where two vertices
(g,h)
and
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
are adjacent if and only if
g
is adjacent to
g^{\prime}
in
G
and
h
is adjacent to
h^{\prime}
in
H
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 2
be positive integers and let
G
be an
r
-regular graph of order
n
, where
r\geq 1
. Then
\chi_{ld}(G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}})=2
if
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
have the same parity, or if they have opposite parity and
n
is odd.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
. Let
V(G)=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
and
V(K_{n_{1},n_{2}})=\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n_{1}}\}\cup\{z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n_{2}}\}
be the vertex sets of graphs
G
and
K_{n_{1},n_{2}}
, respectively. Further, let the vertex set of graph
G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}}
be
V(G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}})=\{(x_{i},y_{j}):1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq n_{1}\}\cup\{(x_{i},z_{j}):\ 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq n_{2}\}
. Throughout the proof, we assume that the index
i
takes values from
1,2,\dots,n
. Our labeling will be constructed based on the parities of
n
,
n_{1}
, and
n_{2}
.

Case 1.

n_{1}
and
n_{2}
are both even.

Define a bijection
f\colon V(G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}})\to\{1,2,\dots,(n_{1}+n_{2})n\}
by

\displaystyle f(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(j-1)n+i&\text{if $j=1,3,5,\dots,n_{1}-1$,}\\
jn+1-i&\text{if $j=2,4,6,\dots,n_{1}$,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle f(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(j-1)n+i+nn_{1}&\text{if $j=1,3,5,\dots,n_{2}-1$,}\\
jn+1-i+nn_{1}&\text{if $j=2,4,6,\dots,n_{2}$.}\end{cases}

Note that for fixed
i

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}f(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\tfrac{n_{2}}{2}}\left((2j-2)n+i+nn_{1}+2jn+1-i+nn_{1}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\tfrac{n_{2}}{2}}4jn-nn_{2}+\tfrac{n_{2}}{2}+nn_{1}n_{2}

\displaystyle=nn_{2}\left(\tfrac{n_{2}}{2}+1\right)-nn_{2}+\tfrac{n_{2}}{2}+nn_{1}n_{2}=\tfrac{n_{2}(nn_{2}+1)}{2}+nn_{1}n_{2},

and similarly

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}f(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\tfrac{n_{1}}{2}}\left((2j-2)n+i+2jn+1-i\right)=\tfrac{n(nn_{1}+1)}{2}.

Using the above sums, the weight of the vertices is given by

\displaystyle w(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\sum_{p=1}^{n_{2}}f(x_{q},z_{p})=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\left(\tfrac{n_{2}(nn_{2}+1)}{2}+nn_{1}n_{2}\right)=\tfrac{rn_{2}(nn_{2}+1)}{2}+rnn_{1}n_{2}

for
1\leq j\leq n_{1}
, and

\displaystyle w(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\sum_{p=1}^{n_{1}}f(x_{q},y_{p})=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\tfrac{n(nn_{1}+1)}{2}=\tfrac{rn_{1}(nn_{1}+1)}{2}

for
1\leq j\leq n_{2}
.
Clearly, the weights of adjacent vertices are distinct. Therefore,
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}}
that produces exactly two distinct vertex weights.

Case 2.

n_{1}
and
n_{2}
are both odd.

Define a bijection
f\colon V(G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}})\to\{1,2,\dots,(n_{1}+n_{2})n\}
by

\displaystyle f(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}n(j-1)+i&\text{if $j=1,2$,}\\
4n-2(i-1)&\text{if $j=3$,}\\
nj+i&\text{if $j=4,6,\dots,n_{1}-1$, }\\
n(j+1)-(i-1)&\text{if $j=5,7,\dots,n_{1}$,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle f(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}4n-(2i-1)&\text{if $j=1$, }\\
n(n_{1}+2)+i&\text{if $j=3$, }\\
n(n_{1}-1+j)+i&\text{if $j=2,4,\dots,n_{2}-1$,}\\
n(n_{1}+j)-(i-1)&\text{if $j=5,7,\dots,n_{2}$.}\end{cases}

Note that for fixed
i

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}f(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=5n+2+\sum_{j=4}^{n_{1}}f(x_{i},y_{j})=5n+2+\sum_{p=2}^{\tfrac{n_{1}-1}{2}}\left(2np+i+2n(p+1)-i+1\right)

\displaystyle=5n+2+\sum_{p=2}^{\tfrac{n_{1}-1}{2}}\left(4np+2n+1\right)=5n+2+(2n+1)\tfrac{(n_{1}-3)}{2}+\tfrac{4n(n_{1}^{2}-9)}{8}

\displaystyle=5n+2+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(nn_{1}(n_{1}+2)+n_{1}-3-15n\right)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(nn_{1}(n_{1}+2)-5n+n_{1}+1\right),

and similarly

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}f(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=7n+2nn_{1}+1+\sum_{j=4}^{n_{2}}f(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=7n+2nn_{1}+1+\sum_{p=2}^{\tfrac{n_{2}-1}{2}}\left(n(n_{1}+2p-1)+i+n(n_{1}+2p+1)-i+1\right)

\displaystyle=7n+2nn_{1}+1+\sum_{p=2}^{\tfrac{n_{2}-1}{2}}\left(4np+2nn_{1}+1\right)

\displaystyle=7n+2nn_{1}+1+(2nn_{1}+1)\tfrac{(n_{2}-3)}{2}+\tfrac{4n(n_{2}^{2}-9)}{8}

\displaystyle=7n+2nn_{1}+1+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(2nn_{1}n_{2}-6nn_{1}+n_{2}-3+nn_{2}^{2}-9n\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(2nn_{1}(n_{2}-1)+n_{2}(nn_{2}+1)+5n-1\right).

For the weight of vertices, we have,

\displaystyle w(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\sum_{p=1}^{n_{2}}f(x_{q},z_{p})=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(2nn_{1}(n_{2}-1)+n_{2}(nn_{2}+1)+5n-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{r}{2}\left(2nn_{1}(n_{2}-1)+n_{2}(nn_{2}+1)+5n-1\right)

for
1\leq j\leq n_{1}
, and

\displaystyle w(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\sum_{p=1}^{n_{1}}f(x_{p},y_{q})=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\left(nn_{1}(n_{1}+2)-5n+n_{1}+1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{r}{2}\left(nn_{1}(n_{1}+2)-5n+n_{1}+1\right)

for
1\leq j\leq n_{2}
.
Thus, the weights of adjacent vertices are distinct in this case as well. Hence,
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}}
that produces two distinct vertex weights.

Case 3.

n_{1}
and
n_{2}
have opposite parity and
n
is odd.

Without loss of generality, assume that
n_{1}
is odd and
n_{2}
is even.
Since
n\equiv n_{1}\pmod{2}
, it follows from Theorem
1.10
that there exists a magic rectangle
A
of size
n\times n_{1}
with entries
a_{i,j}
having constant row sums equal to
{n_{1}(nn_{1}+1)}/{2}
.
Define a bijection
f\colon V(G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}})\to\{1,2,\dots,(n_{1}+n_{2})n\}
by

\displaystyle f(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=a_{i,j}\quad\text{if $j=1,2,\dots,n_{1}$},

\displaystyle f(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(j-1)n+i+nn_{1}&\text{if $j=1,3,5,\dots,n_{2}-1$,}\\
jn+1-i+nn_{1}&\text{if $j=2,4,6,\dots,n_{2}$.}\end{cases}

As seen in Case 1,
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}}f(x_{i},z_{j})={n_{2}(nn_{2}+1)}/{2}+nn_{1}n_{2}
, and as
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}f(x_{i},y_{j})
equals to the row sum of the magic rectangle
A
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}f(x_{i},y_{j})={n_{1}(nn_{1}+1)}/{2}
.
Using these sums, the weight of the vertices is given by

\displaystyle w(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\sum_{p=1}^{n_{2}}f(x_{q},z_{p})=\tfrac{rn_{2}(nn_{2}+1)}{2}+rnn_{1}n_{2}

for
1\leq j\leq n_{1}
, and

\displaystyle w(x_{i},z_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{q}\in N_{G}(x_{i})}\sum_{p=1}^{n_{1}}f(x_{q},y_{p})=\tfrac{rn_{1}(nn_{1}+1)}{2}

for
1\leq j\leq n_{2}
.
Again, we get that the adjacent vertices have distinct weights. Therefore,
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}}
that assigns two distinct weights to its vertices.

∎

Note that the assumptions on parity in Theorem
4.2
are necessary. If
n
is even and
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
have opposite parity, then
\chi_{ld}(G\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}})
does not necessarily equal 2. Priyadharshini et al.
[
21
]
proved that
\chi_{ld}(2K_{2,3})=3
and since
K_{2}\times K_{2,3}\cong 2K_{2,3}
, we obtain
\chi_{ld}(K_{2}\times K_{2,3})=3
.

Weichsel et al.
[
26
]
proved that the direct product of two graphs
G
and
H
is connected if and only if both
G
and
H
are connected and at least one of them is non-bipartite. This implies that the direct product of an odd cycle
C_{2n+1}
with
K_{n_{1},n_{2}}
is connected. According to Theorem
4.2
, we have
\chi_{ld}(C_{2n+1}\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}})=2
. However, one can easily verify that the graph
C_{2n+1}\times K_{n_{1},n_{2}}
is not a complete bipartite graph. This provides a counterexample to Theorem
4.1
.

We now make an attempt to characterize graphs whose local distance antimagic chromatic number equal to 2. We begin with the following trivial observation.

Observation 4.3
.

If
G
is a graph with
\chi_{ld}(G)=2
, then
G
is bipartite.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph of order
n
. If there exists a pair of vertices
u
and
v
belonging to same partite set satisfying one of the following conditions:

1.

(i)
N(u)\subset N(v)
or (ii)
N(v)\subset N(u)
,

2.

|N(u)-N(v)|=a\geq 1
and
|N(v)-N(u)|=b\geq 1
, such that for all mutually exclusive non empty proper subsets
A,B
of
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
with
|A|=a
,
|B|=b
, the sum of element(s) in
A
and sum of element(s) in
B
are distinct,

then
\chi_{ld}(G)>2
.

Proof.

Let
f
be a local distance antimagic labeling of
G
. Let
u
and
v
be vertices belonging to one partite set satisfying the given condition.

1.

(i) If
N(u)\subset N(v)
, then,
w(u)<w(v)
. (ii) By the same argument, we have
w(v)<w(u)
.

2.

By the given hypothesis, under each possible labeling
f
, we must have
w(u)\neq w(v).

So we get at least two distinct weights among the vertices belonging to the partite set containing
u
and
v
. Further, the weight of any vertex in the other partite set should be distinct from these two weights. Thus,
\chi_{ld}(G)>2
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph of order
n
. If there exists a pair of vertices
u
and
v
belonging to a partite set such that
|N(u)\triangle N(v)|=1\ \text{or}\ 2
, where
\triangle
denotes the symmetric difference between sets, then
\chi_{ld}(G)>2
.

Theorem
4.4
provides another class of graphs whose local distance antimagic chromatic number is greater than
2
. Nevertheless, a complete characterization of graphs with local distance antimagic chromatic number
2
remains a fundamental open problem.

Problem 4.6
.

Characterize graphs
G
such that
\chi_{ld}(G)=2
.

4.1
Local Distance Antimagic Labeling of Direct Product of Graphs

The direct product of graphs has attracted significant attention from the research community. One of the most well-known problems related to this operation is Hedetniemi’s conjecture asserted that

\chi(G\times H)=\min\{\chi(G),\chi(H)\}.

This conjecture suggests that the chromatic number of the direct product of two graphs depends only on the smaller chromatic number of the factors. Hedetniemi’s conjecture is, however, disproved by Shitov
[
24
]
in 2019.

In Theorem
4.2
, we observed that the local distance antimagic chromatic number of the direct product of a regular graph with certain complete bipartite graphs equals 2, which is precisely the minimum of the local distance antimagic chromatic numbers of the two factors.
However, this does not hold true in general. We provide an example showing that the local distance antimagic chromatic number of the direct product can exceed the local distance antimagic chromatic numbers of both constituent graphs.

Theorem 4.7
.

For a positive integer
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle n+1\leq\chi_{ld}(K_{2}\times K_{n})\leq\begin{cases}n+2,&\text{if $n$ is even,}\\
n+1,&\text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
V(K_{2})=\{x,y\}
,
V(K_{n})=\{v_{i}\ :\,1\leq i\leq n\}
and
V(K_{2}\times K_{n})=\{(x,v_{i}),(y,v_{i}):1\leq i\leq n\}

be the vertex set of
K_{2}
,
K_{n}
and
K_{2}\times K_{n}
respectively, and let
f
be an arbitrary
local distance antimagic labeling of
K_{2}\times K_{n}
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
,

w(x,v_{i})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}f(y,v_{k}).

Hence, for
i\neq j
,
w(x,v_{i})-w(x,v_{j})=f(y,v_{j})-f(y,v_{i})\neq 0,

since
f
is a bijection. Thus,
w(x,v_{i})\neq w(x,v_{j})

for every
i\neq j
. Similarly,

w(y,v_{i})\neq w(y,v_{j})

for every
i\neq j
.

Moreover, for
i\neq j
, the vertices
(x,v_{i})
and
(y,v_{j})
are
adjacent in
K_{2}\times K_{n}
. Hence, the only possible repeated weights
are of the form
w(x,v_{i})=w(y,v_{i}).

Therefore, the labeling
f
induces at least
n
distinct weights.

Suppose that
f
induces exactly
n
distinct weights. Then

w(x,v_{i})=w(y,v_{i})=a_{i},\qquad 1\leq i\leq n.

In particular,

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=f(y,v_{2})+f(y,v_{3})+\cdots+f(y,v_{n})=f(x,v_{2})+f(x,v_{3})+\cdots+f(x,v_{n}),

and

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=f(y,v_{1})+f(y,v_{3})+\cdots+f(y,v_{n})=f(x,v_{1})+f(x,v_{3})+\cdots+f(x,v_{n}).

Consequently,

a_{2}-a_{1}=f(y,v_{1})-f(y,v_{2})=f(x,v_{1})-f(x,v_{2}).

Thus, for some constant
\alpha
,

f(x,v_{1})-f(y,v_{1})=f(x,v_{2})-f(y,v_{2})=\alpha.

Repeating the same argument gives
f(x,v_{i})-f(y,v_{i})=\alpha,\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Therefore,

\displaystyle n\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(f(x,v_{i})-f(y,v_{i})\bigr)=f(x,v_{1})-f(y,v_{1})+w(x,v_{1})-w(y,v_{1})=\alpha.

Hence,
(n-1)\alpha=0,

and therefore
\alpha=0
. This implies
f(x,v_{i})=f(y,v_{i})

for every
i
, contradicting the bijectivity of
f
.

Thus, every local distance antimagic labeling of
K_{2}\times K_{n}

induces at least
n+1
distinct weights. Hence,

\chi_{ld}(K_{2}\times K_{n})\geq n+1.

We now establish the upper bound. We distinguish two cases according to the parity of
n
.

Case 1:

n
is odd.

Define
f\colon V(K_{2}\times K_{n})\to\{1,2,\dots,2n\}

by

\displaystyle f(x,v_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}n-1&\text{if $i=1$,}\\
2i-2&\text{if $2\leq i\leq\tfrac{n-1}{2}$,}\\
2i-1&\text{if $\tfrac{n+1}{2}\leq i\leq n$,}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle f(y,v_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i=1$,}\\
2i-1&\text{if $2\leq i\leq\tfrac{n-1}{2}$,}\\
2i&\text{if $\tfrac{n+1}{2}\leq i\leq n$.}\end{cases}

It can be easily verified that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x,v_{i})=\tfrac{2n^{2}+n-1}{2},

and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(y,v_{i})=\tfrac{2n^{2}+n+1}{2}.

For the vertex weights, we have

\displaystyle w(x,v_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tfrac{2n^{2}+n-1}{2}&\text{if $i=1$,}\\[6.0pt]
\tfrac{2n^{2}+n+3-4i}{2}&\text{if $2\leq i\leq\tfrac{n-1}{2}$,}\\[6.0pt]
\tfrac{2n^{2}+n+1-4i}{2}&\text{if $\tfrac{n+1}{2}\leq i\leq n$,}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle w(y,v_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tfrac{2n^{2}-n+1}{2}&\text{if $i=1$,}\\[6.0pt]
\tfrac{2n^{2}+n+3-4i}{2}&\text{if $2\leq i\leq\tfrac{n-1}{2}$,}\\[6.0pt]
\tfrac{2n^{2}+n+1-4i}{2}&\text{if $\tfrac{n+1}{2}\leq i\leq n$.}\end{cases}

For
i\neq j
, the above expressions give
w(x,v_{i})\neq w(y,v_{j}).

Thus,
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling. Moreover,
w(x,v_{i})=w(y,v_{i})
,
for
2\leq i\leq n
.

Therefore, the labeling induces
n-1
common weights, together with
the two distinct weights
w(x,v_{1})
and

w(y,v_{1})
.
Hence, the labeling
f
induces
n+1

distinct weights, and consequently

\chi_{ld}(K_{2}\times K_{n})\leq n+1
.

Case 2:

We consider the cases

.

Subcase 4:

n\geq 8
and

.

Define the following two sequences:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=(a_{i})=\{1+4(i+1);\ 0\leq i\leq\tfrac{n}{2}-2\},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=(b_{i})=\{2+4i;\ 0\leq i\leq\tfrac{n}{2}-1\}.

Using these sequences, define a function

f\colon V(K_{2}\times K_{n})\to{1,2,\dots,2n}
by

\displaystyle f(x,v_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{\tfrac{i-1}{2}}&\text{if $i\in\left\{1,3,\dots,n-3\right\}$},\\
b_{\tfrac{i}{2}-1}&\text{if $i\in\left\{2,4,\dots,\tfrac{n}{2}-2\right\}$},\\
b_{\tfrac{i}{2}}&\text{if $i\in\left\{\tfrac{n}{2},\tfrac{n}{2}+2,\dots,n-2\right\}$}.\end{cases}

Note that the entry
b_{n/4-1}=n-2
from the sequence
B
is omitted.
We use this omitted entry to define
f(x,v_{n-1})=n-2,
and

f(x,v_{n})=n.

Furthermore, define

f(y,v_{i})=f(x,v_{i})+2,\qquad 1\leq i\leq n-2,

and

f(y,v_{n-1})=1,\qquad f(y,v_{n})=3.

The entries of the sequence
A
start from
5
and end at
2n-3
,
while the entries of the sequence
B
start from
2
and end at
2n-2
.
Hence, the labels
f(x,v_{i})
and
f(y,v_{i})
, for
1\leq i\leq n-2
, take values from

1
up to
2n
, with the exception of the labels
1,3,n-2,
and

n
. These four labels are precisely assigned to

f(y,v_{n-1}),\quad f(y,v_{n}),\quad f(x,v_{n-1}),\quad f(x,v_{n}),

respectively. Therefore,
f
is a bijection.

We now determine the sums of the labels in the two parts. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\tfrac{n}{2}-2}a_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\tfrac{n}{2}-1\right)+4\left(1+2+\dots+\tfrac{n}{2}-1\right)\ =\tfrac{n^{2}-n}{2}-1,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\tfrac{n}{2}-1}b_{i}

\displaystyle=n+4\left(1+2+\dots+\tfrac{n}{2}-1\right)\ =\tfrac{n^{2}}{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x,v_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\tfrac{n}{2}-2}a_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{\tfrac{n}{2}-1}b_{i}+n\ =n^{2}+\tfrac{n}{2}-1=\alpha,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(y,v_{i})

\displaystyle=\alpha-(n-2+n)+2(n-2)+1+3\ =\alpha+2=\beta.

Since
\beta
is fixed and
f
is a bijection, for
i\neq j
we have
w(x,v_{i})\neq w(x,v_{j}).

Similarly, since
\alpha
is fixed,
w(y,v_{i})\neq w(y,v_{j})

for
i\neq j
.

Furthermore, for
1\leq i\leq n-2
,

f(y,v_{i})-f(x,v_{i})=2=\beta-\alpha.

Therefore,

w(x,v_{i})=w(y,v_{i}),\qquad 1\leq i\leq n-2.

For the remaining four vertices, we have

f(x,v_{n-1})=n-2,\qquad f(x,v_{n})=n,

and

f(y,v_{n-1})=1,\qquad f(y,v_{n})=3.

Hence, their corresponding weights are distinct from the common weights
obtained for
1\leq i\leq n-2
. Consequently, the labeling induces

n-2
common weights together with four additional distinct weights.
Thus, the total number of distinct weights is
n+2
.

Therefore,
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of

K_{2}\times K_{n}
which induces
n+2
distinct weights. Hence,

\chi_{ld}(K_{2}\times K_{n})\leq n+2
.

Subcase 5:

and

.

Define the following two sequences:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=(a_{i})=\{2+4(i+1);\ 0\leq i\leq\tfrac{n}{2}-2\},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=(b_{i})=\{1+4i;\ 0\leq i\leq\tfrac{n}{2}-1\}.

\displaystyle f(x,v_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{\tfrac{(i-1)}{2}}&\text{if $i\in\left\{1,3,\dots,n-3\right\}$},\\
b_{\tfrac{i}{2}-1}&\text{if $i\in\left\{2,4,\dots,\tfrac{n}{2}-1\right\}$},\\

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\tfrac{n}{2}-2}a_{i}

Lemma 4.8

-colored graph with

2.2

G

G

H
be an

Denote the vertex sets of

V(G)=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}

V(G\times H)=\{(x_{i},y_{j}):1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m\}

that induces
\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct weights on the vertices of

be a neighborhood balanced

and

\displaystyle g(x_{i},y_{j})=\begin{cases}m(f(x_{i})-1)+j&\text{if $h(x_{i})=1$},\\

1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m

\displaystyle w_{G\times H}(x_{i},y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{s}\in N_{G}(x_{i}),\\

.

Theorem 4.10
.

.

odd and

\chi_{ld}(K_{t[n]}\times H)\leq\chi_{ld}(K_{t[n]})=t
, where

Let

and let

be an

\displaystyle f(x_{i,j},y_{h})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(i-1)m+h+3(j-1)m&\text{if $i=1,2$ and $j=1,3,\dots,t-1$},\\

, where
\chi
denotes the chromatic number of
G
. A natural direction is to check if, for a graph
G
,
\chi_{ld}(mG)=\chi_{ld}(G)
.
This, however, is not true in general. We present an example to affirm the same.

Theorem 4.13
.

For positive integers
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
,

\chi_{ld}(mK_{n})\geq n+1>\chi_{ld}(K_{n}).

Proof.

Let
V(K_{n})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\}
and

V(mK_{n})=\{v_{i}^{j}:1\leq i\leq n,\;1\leq j\leq m\},

where
v_{1}^{j},v_{2}^{j},\dots,v_{n}^{j}
are the vertices of the
j
th copy of
K_{n}
.

Clearly,

\chi_{ld}(mK_{n})\geq\chi(mK_{n})=\chi(K_{n})=n.

Suppose, to the contrary, that
\chi_{ld}(mK_{n})=n.

Let
f
be a local distance antimagic labeling of
mK_{n}
that induces exactly
n
distinct vertex weights. For
1\leq j\leq m
, let

S_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(v_{i}^{j})

be the sum of the labels assigned to the vertices in the
j
th copy of
K_{n}
.

Since the vertices within each copy of
K_{n}
are pairwise adjacent, their weights must be pairwise distinct. Thus, each copy of
K_{n}
contains all the
n
distinct weights induced by
f
. Hence, after renumbering the vertices in each copy if necessary, we may assume that
w(v_{i}^{j})=w(v_{i}^{k})

for every
1\leq i\leq n
and
j\neq k
.

Since the neighbors of
v_{i}^{j}
are precisely the other vertices in the same copy of
K_{n}
, we have

w(v_{i}^{j})=S_{j}-f(v_{i}^{j}).

Therefore, for any
j\neq k
,

\displaystyle S_{j}-f(v_{i}^{j})

\displaystyle=S_{k}-f(v_{i}^{k}),

(1)

and hence

S_{j}-S_{k}=f(v_{i}^{j})-f(v_{i}^{k}).

Summing over
i=1,2,\dots,n
, we obtain

\displaystyle n(S_{j}-S_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(f(v_{i}^{j})-f(v_{i}^{k})\bigr)=S_{j}-S_{k}.

Thus,

(n-1)(S_{j}-S_{k})=0.

Since
n\geq 2
, it follows that
S_{j}=S_{k}.

Equation (
1
) now gives
f(v_{i}^{j})=f(v_{i}^{k})

for every
i
, which contradicts the fact that
f
is a bijection.

Therefore,

\chi_{ld}(mK_{n})\geq n+1>\chi_{ld}(K_{n})=\chi(K_{n})=n.

∎

Corollary 4.14
.

For a positive integer
m
,
\chi_{ld}(mK_{2})=2m
.

Proof.

Let
V(mK_{2})=\{v_{1}^{j},v_{2}^{j};\ 1\leq j\leq m\}
be the vertex set of
mK_{2}
. Let
f\colon V(mK_{2})\to\{1,2,\dots,2m\}
be any local distance antimagic labeling of
mK_{2}
.

Note that for any
1\leq j\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle w(v_{1}^{j})

\displaystyle=f(v_{2}^{j}),

\displaystyle w(v_{2}^{j})

\displaystyle=f(v_{1}^{j}).

As
f
is a bijection, all the vertices must receive distinct weights. Thus

\chi_{ld}(mK_{2})\geq 2m.

Further, as
|V(mK_{2})|=2m
, the upper bound is obvious and thus the equality follows.
∎

Theorem
4.13
shows that the neighborhood balanced coloring hypothesis in the subsequent results concerning disjoint unions cannot simply be omitted. Indeed, although the graph
K_{n}
is
(n-1)
-regular, for every
m,n\geq 2
we have

\chi_{ld}(mK_{n})\geq n+1>\chi_{ld}(K_{n})=\chi(K_{n})=n.

Thus, the inequality

\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)

does not hold for arbitrary regular graphs. Consequently, an additional structural assumption is required, in Lemmas
4.15
and
4.16
and Theorem
4.17
, this role is played by the neighborhood balanced coloring hypothesis.

Theorem
4.13
naturally leads to the question of characterizing graphs
G
for which
\chi_{ld}(mG)=\chi_{ld}(G)
for every positive integer
m
.
Corollary
4.14
shows that the local distance antimagic chromatic number of a disjoint union may be strictly larger than that of the original graph, even when the original graph contains pendant vertices. In particular, for
G=K_{2}
, this number increases with each additional copy of
G
.

This observation motivates a separate study of the local distance antimagic chromatic number of graphs containing pendant vertices.
In this section, we present results in both the above-mentioned directions.

Since
\chi(G)
provides a natural lower bound for
\chi_{ld}(mG)
, the next result establishes that regular graphs admitting neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring satisfy the inequality
\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and let
G
be a regular neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph with
k
even. Then
\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)
.

Proof.

Since
G
is a neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph with
k
-even, by Lemma
2.2
,
G
is also a neighborhood balanced
2
-colored graph. Assume that
G
is a
2r
-regular graph admitting neighborhood balanced
2
-coloring
h
and a local distance antimagic labeling
f
that assigns
\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct weights to its vertices. As
h
is a neighborhood balanced
2
-coloring of
G
, we have, for every vertex
u\in V(G)
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}1=r.

Now consider the graph
mG
.
Let
V(mG)=\{v^{i}:1\leq i\leq m,v\in V(G)\}
be its vertex set.
Define a vertex labeling
g\colon V(mG)\to\{1,2,\dots,m|V(G)|\}
in the following way. For
1\leq i\leq m

g(v^{i})=\begin{cases}m(f(v)-1)+i&\text{if $h(v)=1$},\\
mf(v)+1-i&\text{if $h(v)=2$}.\end{cases}

Note that if a vertex in
G
receives the label
t\in\{1,2,\dots,|V(G)|\}
under the labeling
f
, then the corresponding vertices in
mG
receive labels from the set
\{m(t-1)+1,m(t-1)+2,\dots,mt\}
under
g
.
Moreover, for the weight of a vertex
u^{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, in
mG
, under the labeling
g
, we have

\displaystyle w_{g}(u^{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}g(v^{i})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}g(v^{i})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}g(v^{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}(m(f(v)-1)+i)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}(mf(v)+1-i)

\displaystyle=m\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}f(v)+(i-m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}1+m\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}f(v)+(1-i)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=m\ w_{f}(u)+(i-m)r+(1-i)r=m\ w_{f}(u)+(1-m)r.

Since every vertex has
r
neighbors in each color class, and
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
G
, adjacent vertices in
G
have distinct weights. Consequently, adjacent vertices in
mG
also receive distinct weights. Moreover, vertices corresponding to the same vertex
v
across the
m
copies of
G
have equal weights. Therefore,
g
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
mG
that assigns
\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct weights to the vertices of
mG
. Hence,
\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)
.
∎

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
m
be an odd positive integer and let

be a regular neighborhood balanced

-colored graph with

odd. Then
\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)
.

Proof.

As

is a neighborhood balanced

-colored graph with

odd. By Lemma

,

is also a neighborhood balanced
p
-colored graph, where

is a
pr
-regular graph admitting neighborhood balanced
p
-coloring
h
and a local distance antimagic labeling

that assigns
\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct weights to its vertices. Furthermore, since
h
is a neighborhood balanced
p
-coloring of
G
, we have, for every vertex
u\in V(G)
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}1=\dots=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=p\end{subarray}}1=r.

Now consider the graph
mG
.
Let
V(mG)=\{v^{i}:1\leq i\leq m,v\in V(G)\}
be its vertex set. Define a vertex labeling
g\colon V(mG)\to\{1,2,\dots,m|V(G)|\}
as follows:

g(v^{i})=\begin{cases}m(f(v)-1)+i&\text{if $h(v)=1$ and $i=1,2,\dots,m$},\\
m(f(v)-1)+\tfrac{m+1}{2}+i&\text{if $h(v)=2$ and $i=1,2,\dots,\tfrac{m-1}{2}$},\\

mf(v)+1-i&\text{if $h(v)=l$, $l=\tfrac{p+5}{2},\tfrac{p+7}{2},\dots,p$ and $i=1,2,,\dots,m$.}\end{cases}

Note that if a vertex in
G
is labeled with the number
t
,
t\in\{1,2,\dots,|V(G)|\}
by
f
, then the corresponding vertices in
mG
receive the labels from the set
\{m(t-1)+1,m(t-1)+2,\ldots,mt\}
under
g
.

We now calculate the weight of a vertex
u^{i}
under the labeling
g
. For
1\leq i\leq{(m-1)}/{2}
, we have

\displaystyle w_{g}(u^{i})=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}g(v^{i})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}g(v^{i})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}g(v^{i})+\dots+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=p\end{subarray}}g(v^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}\left(m(f(v)-1)+i\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}\left(m(f(v)-1)+i+\tfrac{m+1}{2}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in N_{G}(v),\\
h(v)=3\end{subarray}}\left(mf(v)+1-2i\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=4}^{\tfrac{p+3}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=l\end{subarray}}\left(m(f(v)-1)+i\right)\sum_{l=\tfrac{p+5}{2}}^{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\

\displaystyle\ m\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}f(v)+\tfrac{(1-m)p}{2}r=m\ w_{f}(u)+\tfrac{(1-m)pr}{2}.

Similarly, for
({m+1})/{2}\leq i\leq m
,

\displaystyle w_{g}(u^{i})=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}g(v^{i})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}g(v^{i})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}g(v^{i})+\dots+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=p\end{subarray}}g(v^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}\left(m(f(v)-1)+i\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}\left(m(f(v)-1)-\tfrac{m-1}{2}+i\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in N_{G}(v),\\
h(v)=3\end{subarray}}\left(mf(v)+m+1-2i\right)+\sum_{l=4}^{\tfrac{p+3}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=l\end{subarray}}\left(m(f(v)-1)+i\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=\tfrac{p+5}{2}}^{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=l\end{subarray}}\left(mf(v)+1-i\right)=m\sum_{v\in N_{G}(u)}f(v)+\tfrac{(1-m)p}{2}r=m\ w_{f}(u)+\tfrac{(1-m)pr}{2}.

The above calculations follow from the fact that every vertex of

has exactly

neighbors in each color class.
Since

is a local distance antimagic labeling of
G
, adjacent vertices of
G
have distinct weights. Consequently, adjacent vertices of
mG
also have distinct weights under

\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct weights to the vertices of
mG

\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)
.

Lemma 4.20

In some cases, it is possible to obtain a better upper bound than the one presented in Lemma

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(i-1)m+h+3(j-1)m&\text{for $i=1,2$ and $j=1,3,\dots,t-1$},\\

Case 1:

when

is odd.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}f(x_{i,j}^{h})

\displaystyle=(h+3(j-1)m)+(m+h+3(j-1)m)+(4m-(2h-1)+3(j-1)m)

\displaystyle=9jm-4m+1,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=4}^{n}f(x_{i,j}^{h})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{n-3}{2}\left((n+3)tm+1\right).

Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i,j}^{h})=9jm-4m+1+\tfrac{n-3}{2}\left((n+3)tm+1\right).

Case 2:

when
j
is even.

Similarly,

\sum_{i=1}^{3}f(x_{i,j}^{h})=9jm-5m+2,

and thus

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i,j}^{h})=9jm-5m+2+\tfrac{n-3}{2}\left((n+3)tm+1\right).

These sums are independent of the copy index
h
. The weight of any vertex in the
j
th partite set is then given by

Case 1:

when
j
is odd.

\displaystyle w(x_{i,j}^{h})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{n-3}{2}\left((n+3)tm+1\right)(t-1)+\tfrac{t-2}{2}(-9m+3)-5m+2+9m\left(\tfrac{t(t+1)}{2}-j\right).

Case 2:

when
j
is even.

\displaystyle w(x_{i,j}^{h})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{n-3}{2}\left((n+3)tm+1\right)(t-1)+\tfrac{t-2}{2}(-9m+3)-4m+1+9m\left(\tfrac{t(t+1)}{2}-j\right).

The labeling
f
assigns identical vertex weights within each of the
t
partite sets and distinct weights between different partite sets in any copy of
K_{t[n]}
. Furthermore, corresponding vertices across different copies have identical weights. Hence,
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
mK_{t[n]}
that induces exactly
t
distinct vertex weights. Therefore,
\chi_{ld}(mK_{t[n]})\leq t
. Further, as
\chi(mK_{t[n]})=t
, the equality follows.
∎

To handle the case of graphs with pendant vertices, now study the local distance antimagic chromatic number of copies of nearly neighbourhood balanced
k
-colored graphs, which were introduced in Section 3. The concept will be studied as two separate Lemmas when
k
is even, and
k
is odd.

Lemma 4.22
.

Let
m
be a positive integer, and let
G
be a connected nearly
neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph of order at least three, where
k
is even, such that every non-pendant vertex has degree
2r
. Let
f
be a local distance antimagic labeling of
G
assigning

\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct weights to its vertices. Let
s
be the number of distinct weights that occur both at pendant and at non-pendant vertices under
f
. Furthermore, suppose that, for every non-pendant
vertex
u
,

m\left(f(u)-w_{f}(u)+r\right)-r>m-1\quad\text{ or }\quad m\left(f(u)-w_{f}(u)+r\right)-r<0.

(2)

If
|N_{pen}(G)|
denotes the number of neighbors of all pendant vertices of
G
,
then

\displaystyle m|N_{pen}(G)|+1

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)+(m-1)|N_{pen}(G)|+s,

\displaystyle\text{if}\ G\text{ is not a star},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq m+1,

\displaystyle\text{if}\ G\text{ is a star}.

Proof.

Since
G
is a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph with
k
even, it follows from Lemma
3.3
that
G
also admits a nearly neighborhood balanced
2
-coloring. Let
h
be such a coloring of
G
. Then, for every non-pendant vertex
u\in V(G)
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}1=r.

As
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
G
inducing
\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct vertex weights, the pendant vertices receive
|N_{pen}(G)|
distinct weights under
f
, and exactly
s
of these weights also occur at non-pendant vertices. Hence the non-pendant vertices receive
\chi_{ld}(G)-|N_{pen}(G)|+s
distinct weights.

Consider now the graph
mG
.
Let
V(mG)=\{v^{i};\ 1\leq i\leq m\ \text{and}\ v\in V(G)\}
be its vertex set.
We define a vertex labeling
g\colon V(mG)\to\{1,2,\dots,m|V(G)|\}
as follows. For
1\leq i\leq m,

g(v^{i})=\begin{cases}m(f(v)-1)+i&\text{if $h(v)=1$},\\
mf(v)+1-i&\text{if $h(v)=2$}.\end{cases}

We now calculate the weight of vertices in
mG
. Since each non-pendant vertex of
G
has the same number of neighbors of each color, by the computation in Lemma
4.15
(the labeling
g
being identical as in Lemma
4.15
), for any non-pendant vertex
u\in V(G)
we have

w(u^{j})=m\ w_{f}(u)+(1-m)r.

Thus,
g
is such a labeling that the corresponding non-pendant vertices in different copies receive the same weight. Moreover, since
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
G
, adjacent non-pendant vertices in each copy of
G
have distinct weights. Therefore, the non-pendant vertices of
mG
contribute exactly
\chi_{ld}(G)-|N_{pen}(G)|+s
distinct weights to
\chi_{ld}(mG)
.

Now consider a pendant vertex
v
of
G
. The weight of its copies in
mG
is given by

w(v^{j})=g(x^{j})=\begin{cases}m(f(x)-1)+j&\text{if $h(x)=1$},\\
mf(x)+1-j&\text{if $h(x)=2$},\end{cases}

where
x\in N_{G}(v)
.
Clearly, the pendant vertices in corresponding copies receive distinct weights. Since each copy of
G
contributes
|N_{pen}(G)|
distinct weights due to pendant vertices, the graph
mG
contributes
m|N_{pen}(G)|
such distinct weights.

Suppose in the
j
th copy of
G
, the weight of a pendant vertex
v^{j}
equals the weight of its adjacent non-pendant vertex, say
u^{j}
. Then, for some
l=0,1,2,\dots,m-1
, we have

\displaystyle mf(u)-l

\displaystyle=m\cdot w_{f}(u)+(1-m)r,

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle=m\left(f(u)-w_{f}(u)+r\right)-r.

However, from inequality (
2
), this equality cannot hold. Therefore,
g
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
mG
that induces at most
\chi_{ld}(G)+(m-1)|N_{pen}(G)|+s
distinct weights. Hence
\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)+(m-1)|N_{pen}(G)|+s.
In particular, if
G
is a star, then the weight of any pendant vertex under
f
cannot be equal to the weight of the lone non-pendant vertex and thus
s=0
. Also
|N_{pen}(G)|=1
and
\chi_{ld}(G)=2
and hence
\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq m+1.

To establish the lower bound, note that under any local distance antimagic labeling of
mG
, the pendant vertices in each copy of
G
contribute
|N_{pen}(G)|
distinct weights, and thus all pendant vertices in
mG
contribute
m|N_{pen}(G)|
distinct weights to
\chi_{ld}(mG)
.
The values of all these weights, being labels of non-pendant vertices of
mG
, are less than or equal to
mn
, the largest label.
If the label
mn
is assigned to a pendant vertex, then the weight of its neighbor must exceed
mn
, since that neighbor has degree at least two. Similarly, if the label
mn
is assigned to a non-pendant vertex, then the weight of its adjacent non-pendant vertex in the case of a non-star graph will be greater than
mn
. In the case of
G
being a star, such a non-pendant vertex does not exist. Consequently,

\displaystyle m|N_{pen}(G)|+1

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{ld}(mG),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if $G$ is not a star},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{ld}(mG),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if $G$ is a star}.

∎

Lemma 4.23
.

Let
m
be an odd positive integer and let
G
be a connected nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph of order at least three, where
k
is odd, such that every non-pendant vertex has degree
kt
. Let
f
be a local distance antimagic labeling of
G
assigning

\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct weights to its vertices. Let
s
be the number of distinct weights that occur both at pendant and at non-pendant vertices under
f
. Furthermore, suppose that, for every non-pendant
vertex
u
,

\displaystyle m(f(u)-w_{f}(u))+\tfrac{(m-1)pr}{2}>m

(3)

or

\displaystyle m(f(u)-w_{f}(u))+\tfrac{(m-1)pr}{2}<0,

(4)

If
|N_{pen}(G)|
denotes the number of neighbors of all pendant vertices of
G
, then

\displaystyle m|N_{pen}(G)|+1

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)+(m-1)|N_{pen}(G)|+s,

\displaystyle\text{if}\ G\text{ is not a star},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq m+1,

\displaystyle\text{if}\ G\text{ is a star}.

Proof.

As
G
is a nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graph with
k
odd, by Lemma
3.3
,
G
is also a nearly neighborhood balanced
p
-colored graph, where
p
is the least prime divisor of
k
. Since every non-pendant vertex has degree
kt
, and
p
divides
k
, we may assume that each non-pendant vertex has degree
pr
. Let
h
be a nearly neighborhood balanced
p
-coloring of
G
. Then for all non-pendant vertices
u\in V(G)
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=2\end{subarray}}1=\dots=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N_{G}(u),\\
h(v)=p\end{subarray}}1=r.

Since
f
induces
\chi_{ld}(G)
distinct vertex weights and exactly
s
distinct weights occur at both pendant and non-pendant vertices, while the pendant vertices receive
|N_{pen}(G)|
distinct weights, the non-pendant vertices receive
\chi_{ld}(G)-|N_{pen}(G)|+s
distinct weights under
f
.

Now consider the graph
mG
with the vertex set
V(mG)=\{v^{i}:1\leq i\leq m,v\in V(G)\}
.
Define a vertex labeling
g\colon V(mG)\to\{1,2,\dots,m|V(G)|\}
as follows:

g(v^{i})=\begin{cases}m(f(v)-1)+i&\text{if $h(v)=1$ and $i=1,2,\dots,m$},\\
m(f(v)-1)+\tfrac{m+1}{2}+i&\text{if $h(v)=2$ and $i=1,2,\dots,\tfrac{m-1}{2}$},\\
m(f(v)-1)-\tfrac{m-1}{2}+i&\text{if $h(v)=2$ and $i=\tfrac{m+1}{2},\tfrac{m+3}{2},\dots,m$},\\
mf(v)+1-2i&\text{if $h(v)=3$ and $1,2,\dots,\tfrac{m-1}{2}$},\\
mf(v)+m+1-2i&\text{if $h(v)=3$ and $i=\tfrac{m+1}{2},\tfrac{m+3}{2},\dots,m$},\\
m(f(v)-1)+i&\text{if $h(v)=t$, $t=4,5,\dots,\tfrac{p+3}{2}$ and $i=1,2,\dots,m$},\\
mf(v)+1-i&\text{if $h(v)=t$, $t=\tfrac{p+5}{2},\tfrac{p+7}{2},\dots,p$ and $i=1,2,\dots,m$.}\end{cases}

We now calculate the vertex weights. Since every non-pendant vertex in
G
has an equal number of neighbors of each of the
p
colors, it follows from the calculation in Lemma
4.16
that under the labeling
g
we have

w(u^{j})=m\ w_{f}(u)+\tfrac{(1-m)pr}{2}.

Hence, corresponding non-pendant vertices in different copies of
G
have the same weights. As
f
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
G
, adjacent non-pendant vertices in any copy of
G
, and thus in
mG
, receive distinct weights. Therefore, the non-pendant vertices of
mG
contribute exactly
\chi_{ld}(G)-|N_{pen}(G)|+s
distinct weights to
\chi_{ld}(mG)
.

Now consider a pendant vertex
v
of
G
, and let
x\in N_{G}(v)
. Then the weight of its copies in
mG
is

w(v^{j})=g(x^{j})=\begin{cases}m(f(x)-1)+j&\text{if $h(x)=1$ and $j=1,2,\dots,m$},\\
m(f(x)-1)+\tfrac{m+1}{2}+j&\text{if $h(x)=2$ and $j=1,2,\dots,\tfrac{m-1}{2}$},\\
m(f(x)-1)-\tfrac{m-1}{2}+j&\text{if $h(x)=2$ and $j=\tfrac{m+1}{2},\tfrac{m+3}{2},\dots,m$},\\
mf(x)+1-2j&\text{if $h(x)=3$ and $j=1,2,\dots,\tfrac{m-1}{2}$},\\
mf(x)+m+1-2j&\text{if $h(x)=3$ and $j=\tfrac{m+1}{2},\tfrac{m+3}{2},\dots,m$},\\
m(f(x)-1)+j&\text{if $h(x)=t$, $t=4,5,\dots,\tfrac{p+3}{2}$ and $j=1,2,\dots,m$},\\
mf(x)+1-j&\text{if $h(x)=t$, $t=\tfrac{p+5}{2},\tfrac{p+7}{2},\dots,p$ and $j=1,2,\dots,m$.}\end{cases}

Clearly, pendant vertices in corresponding copies receive distinct weights. Since each copy of
G
contributes
|N_{pen}(G)|
distinct weights due to pendant vertices, the graph
mG
contributes
m|N_{pen}(G)|
such weights.

If in the
j
th copy of
G
, some pendant vertex
v^{j}
has the same weight as its adjacent non-pendant vertex, say
u^{j}
, then for some
l\in\{0,1,\dots,m-1\}
, we have

\displaystyle mf(u)-l

\displaystyle=m\ w_{f}(u)+\tfrac{(1-m)pr}{2},

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle=m(f(u)-w_{f}(u))+\tfrac{(m-1)pr}{2}.

which contradicts inequalities (
3
) and (
4
). Hence,
g
is a local distance antimagic labeling of
mG
inducing at most
\chi_{ld}(G)+(m-1)|N_{pen}(G)|+s
distinct weights, implying that
\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq\chi_{ld}(G)+(m-1)|N_{pen}(G)|+s.
In particular, if
G
is a star, then the weight of any pendant vertex under
f
cannot be equal to the weight of the lone non-pendant vertex and thus
s=0
. Also
|N_{pen}(G)|=1
and
\chi_{ld}(G)=2
and hence
\chi_{ld}(mG)\leq m+1.

Finally, by the same reasoning as in Lemma
4.22
, we have

\displaystyle m|N_{pen}(G)|+1

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{ld}(mG),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if $G$ is not a star},

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{ld}(mG),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if $G$ is a star}.

∎

Theorem 4.24
.

[
22

Let

,

\chi_{ld}(mK_{1,r})=\begin{cases}m&\text{for }r=2\text{ and }m\geq 3,\\

5
Conclusion

In this article, we studied the local distance antimagic chromatic number for the direct product and the disjoint union of neighborhood balanced
k
-colored graphs and examined whether the known results concerning the ordinary chromatic number of a graph remain valid for the local distance antimagic chromatic number as well. We presented several classes of neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring graphs where the answer is in the affirmative. We also presented several results concerning the local distance antimagic chromatic number for the disjoint union of nearly neighborhood balanced
k
-coloring graphs defined in Section
3
. The following questions naturally arise:

•

Characterize graphs
G
and
H
for which
\chi_{ld}(G\times H)=\min\{\chi_{ld}(G),\chi_{ld}(H)\}.

•

Characterize graphs
G
for which
\chi_{ld}(mG)=\chi_{ld}(G)
, for any positive integer
m
.

Also, as mentioned in Section
4.1
at the beginning, the problem of characterizing graphs with local distance antimagic chromatic number equal to two remains open. This is an interesting problem for future research.

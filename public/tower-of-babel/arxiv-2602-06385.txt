Uniform Spectral Growth and Convergence of Muon in LoRA-Style Matrix Factorization
Changmin Kang, Jihun Yun, Baekrok Shin, Yeseul Cho, Chulhee Yun

Abstract
Spectral gradient descent (SpecGD) orthogonalizes the matrix parameter updates and has inspired practical optimizers such as Muon. They often perform well in large language model (LLM) training, but their dynamics remain poorly understood. In the low-rank adaptation (LoRA) setting, where weight updates are parameterized as a product of two low-rank factors, we find a distinctive spectral phenomenon under Muon in LoRA fine-tuning of LLMs: singular values of the LoRA product show near-uniform growth across the spectrum, despite orthogonalization being performed on the two factors separately. Motivated by this observation, we analyze spectral gradient flow (SpecGF)—a continuous-time analogue of SpecGD—in a simplified LoRA-style matrix factorization setting and prove “equal-rate” dynamics: all singular values grow at equal rates up to small deviations. Consequently, smaller singular values attain their target values earlier than larger ones, sharply contrasting with the largest-first stepwise learning observed in standard gradient flow. Moreover, we prove that SpecGF in our setting converges to global minima from almost all initializations, provided the factor norms remain bounded; with ℓ 2 \ell_{2} regularization, we obtain global convergence. Lastly, we corroborate our theory with experiments in the same setting.

Introduction

Classical optimization algorithms for training neural networks, such as stochastic gradient descent
(
32
)
and Adam(W)
(
24
;
29
)
update network parameters in a coordinate-wise manner, ignoring the structure of parameters. In contrast, several modern approaches in deep learning exploit the structure of matrix parameters, focusing on
how
or
what
to update during training.

One line of work follows the former direction, and the Muon optimizer
(
21
)
is one of representative examples. At each iteration
t
, Muon minimizes a loss function
f(\mathbf{W})
in the weight matrix
\mathbf{W}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{M}_{t}&=\nabla f(\mathbf{W}_{t})+\mu\mathbf{M}_{t-1},\\
\mathbf{W}_{t+1}&=\mathbf{W}_{t}-\eta_{t}\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{M}_{t}),\end{split}

(1)

where
\mu\in[0,1]
tunes the momentum,
\eta_{t}
is the step size, and
\mathcal{T}
orthogonalizes the momentum (see
Equation

5
). In practice, Newton-Schulz iteration approximates
\mathcal{T}
by mapping most singular values of normalized
\mathbf{M}_{t}
to an interval
[0.7,1.3]
. If
\mu=0
, the update follows spectral gradient descent (SpecGD,
8
;
31
).
Orthogonalized updates induce the spectrum of matrices to be more isotropic
(
38
;
3
)
. By leveraging such geometric structure, Muon has gained significant attention for training large language models (LLM), often outperforming Adam(W)
(
26
;
38
)
.

Another line of work targets what parameters are updated. This category includes Low-rank adaptation (LoRA,
18
), which freezes the pretrained model and injects a low-rank update. LoRA parameterizes a weight update
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
as a product of low-rank factor matrices
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
where
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}
with
r\ll\min\{m,n\}
. This substantially reduces the number of trainable parameters while preserving strong fine-tuning performance.

Combining these two intriguing approaches modifies both the parameterization and optimizer, potentially leading to distinct training dynamics and implicit biases. While recent studies combining Muon-style optimizers with LoRA have also reported promising empirical results
(
2
;
4
)
, theoretical explanations about their dynamics and implicit bias remain absent.

In
Section

3
, we fine-tune language models using the Muon optimizer together with the LoRA parameterization to examine the dynamics, and we observe the Muon’s unique feature persists even after applying LoRA. Although each factor receives its own orthogonalized updates, all spectral components of the product
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
evolve nearly uniformly. On the contrary, the dynamics of linear models with multiple layers trained under gradient descent exhibit incremental learning caused by saddle-to-saddle dynamics—the model learns singular values in a stepwise manner, from largest to smallest
(
5
;
14
;
25
)
.

Motivated by this empirical observation, in subsequent sections, we deliver rigorous analyses of the dynamics induced from the orthogonalized updates under the LoRA setting. To this end, we model the matrix factorization objective induced by LoRA fine-tuning through the lens of
spectral gradient flow (SpecGF)
, a continuous-time model of SpecGD.

We summarize our contributions as follows:

Uniform Spectral Growth.
Even though SpecGF orthogonalizes the gradients of the LoRA components
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
rather than their product, we show that the singular values of
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
still evolve at a uniform rate under SpecGF (
Theorem

5.7
). The dynamics make smaller singular values converge before larger ones. This sharply contrasts with standard gradient flow which exhibits largest-first stepwise learning dynamics
(
5
;
14
)
.

Convergence Guarantee.
We prove that if SpecGF converges, it almost surely converges to global minima provided that the factor norms remain bounded (
Theorem

6.2
); with
\ell_{2}
regularization, SpecGF globally converges. In addition, every global minimum is Lyapunov stable, and SpecGF converges to global minima exponentially fast (
Proposition

6.7
).

Empirical Validation.
On matrix factorization, we confirm that SpecGF exhibits uniform growth and smallest-first convergence order, in contrast to the largest-first behavior of standard gradient flow.

Empirical Observation: Uniform Growth in LoRA with Muon

We begin with an empirical observation that motivates our theoretical analysis.
Given pretrained weight
\mathbf{W}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

\min_{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}}\ell(\mathbf{W}_{0}+\mathbf{W}),

(2)

The LoRA approach reparameterizes

as
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}

\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}

, and

(
18
;
17
)
.

When training LoRA adapters with Muon, we observe that the singular values of

This is a non-trivial phenomenon because Muon orthogonalizes the updates of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
separately.
Such a spectral growth in the product

suggests some form of alignment between the matrices

and
\mathbf{B}
, established during the training process.

The near-uniform evolution of singular values for all LoRA
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
adapters stems from Muon, but such interactions are highly intractable due to the intricate interactions among the components of LLM. To rigorously analyze the dynamics from Muon with LoRA, we construct and study a simplified setting that captures the LoRA approach and orthogonalized matrix updates.

Recall that
Equation

2
aims to find the optimal update
\mathbf{W}^{\star}
during fine-tuning. A second-order Taylor expansion of
\ell
around
\mathbf{W}_{0}+\mathbf{W}^{\star}
yields:

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\ell(\mathbf{W}_{0}+\mathbf{W})-\ell(\mathbf{W}_{0}+\mathbf{W}^{\star})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{W}^{\star},\nabla^{2}\ell(\mathbf{W}_{0}+\mathbf{W}^{\star})(\mathbf{W}-\mathbf{W}^{\star})\right\rangle.

Therefore, the fine-tuning objective can be approximated as the minimization of the quadratic term. To theoretically understand the dynamics of LoRA, we constrain the problem by relaxing the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\ell(\mathbf{W}_{0}+\mathbf{W}^{\star})
to an identity:

\min_{\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}}\Big\{\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\Big\},

(3)

where
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
and
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
respectively take the place of
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{W}^{\star}
.

We train
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
with SpecGD (
Equation

1
with
\mu=0
) with loss function in
Equation

3
. We set
(m,n,r)=(60,70,5)
and then construct
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}
where
\mathbf{U}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
and
\mathbf{V}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
are random orthonormal matrices, and
\bm{\Sigma}=\mathrm{Diag}(8,5,3,1.5,0.7)
. We initialize
\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{B}(0)
with i.i.d. Gaussian entries of scale
\gamma=10^{-3}
. We run SpecGD with a learning rate
\eta=0.01
.

Notably, the singular values of
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
grow at a
uniform
rate; see
Figure

2
. On the contrary, plain gradient descent exhibits incremental learning.
It is expected that the singular values of a single matrix uniformly grow under orthogonalized updates. However, the surprising finding is that such uniform growth is also observed for the
product

\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
, even though
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are updated via orthogonalization separately.

Our observation naturally leads to the following question:

Key Question.

Why do the singular values of
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
evolve uniformly although the gradient of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are orthogonalized separately?

To address this question, we study
Equation

3
with spectral gradient methods. Our theoretical framework, developed in the following section, explains the observed uniform growth and the smallest-first convergence order.

We use bold uppercase letters (e.g.,
\mathbf{X}
) for real matrices, bold lowercase letters (e.g.,

4.1
Problem Setup

To align with the LoRA setting, we assume
r\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})
to solve
Equation

3
. If
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
depend on time
t
, we may write
\mathcal{L}(t)\coloneqq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))
.
We initialize each factor as
\mathbf{A}(0)=\bm{0}
and
\mathbf{B}(0)
at random, which coincides with the standard LoRA initialization where each entry of
\mathbf{B}(0)
is sampled from a Gaussian distribution
(
18
;
17
)
.

We analyze the continuous-time analog of SpecGD,
spectral gradient flow (SpecGF)
, defined by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{\mathbf{A}}(t)&=-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right)\\
&=-\mathcal{T}\left((\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y})\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}\right),\\
\dot{\mathbf{B}}(t)&=-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right)\\
&=-\mathcal{T}\left(\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y})\right),\end{split}

(4)

where
\mathcal{T}
is an orthogonalization operator applied to the matrix gradients. For a matrix
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
with compact SVD
\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{M}}^{\top}
and
\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{M}}\coloneqq\mathrm{Diag}\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
, the exact orthogonalization is written as

\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{M})\coloneqq\big((\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top})^{\dagger}\big)^{1/2}\!\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{M}}^{\top}.

(5)

Notice that
\mathcal{T}
normalizes all the nonzero singular values to
1
. However,
\mathcal{T}
is not analytic at rank-deficient points since it involves discontinuous singular-vector selections.
To obtain an analytic vector field, we introduce a smoothed operator
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
with
\beta>0
:

\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{M})\coloneqq\left(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}+\beta\mathbf{I}_{m}\right)^{-1/2}\!\mathbf{M}.

(6)

Adding
\beta\mathbf{I}
makes the inverse square root well-defined, and makes SpecGF analytic everywhere; see
Corollary

D.2
for details. We can write
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{M})
as

\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{M})=\mathbf{U}_{\mathbf{M}}\mathbf{S}_{\beta}\mathbf{V}_{\mathbf{M}}^{\top},\;\mathbf{S}_{\beta}\coloneqq\mathrm{Diag}\left\{\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}^{2}+\beta}}\right\}_{i\in[k]}.

Thus,
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
maps all positive inputs to values near
1
, similar to NS iterations, and boils down to
\mathcal{T}
as
\beta\to 0
.
Throughout the paper, “SpecGF with
\mathcal{N}
” refers to the flow where both
\dot{\mathbf{A}}
and
\dot{\mathbf{B}}
use the operator
\mathcal{N}\in\{\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\}
for orthogonalization.

We use the compact SVD of
\mathbf{Y}
. Let
r^{\star}=\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})
. Then
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{U}_{r^{\star}}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}_{r^{\star}}^{\top}
,
where
\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{r^{\star}\times r^{\star}}
contains the nonzero singular values in non-increasing order and
\mathbf{U}_{r^{\star}}
,
\mathbf{V}_{r^{\star}}
consist of the corresponding singular vectors.

5
Uniform Growth of Singular Values

We analyze how the singular values of
\mathbf{W}(t)=\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)
evolve from SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
under LoRA initialization. A key finding is that, all active singular values grow at the same rate, a phenomenon we call equal-rate dynamics. This synchronized behavior implies that the smallest singular value reaches its target first.

5.1
Alignment Yields Decoupled Dynamics

We start with the simple case—rank-1
\mathbf{Y}
and
r=1
—that leads to the decoupling of the dynamics of the product
\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)
into those of scalars. The matrices reduce to

\mathbf{Y}=\sigma\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^{\top},\quad\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1},\quad\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n},

where
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively. Given a random unit vector
\mathbf{w}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and small
\gamma>0

\mathbf{A}(0)=\bm{0},\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{w}^{\top}.

Notice that
\left\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\right\rangle\neq 0
almost surely. This simple case reduces the dynamics of two vectors to those of scalars:

Theorem 5.1

(Informal)
.

Assume
\left\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\right\rangle\neq 0

and

\mathbf{A}(t)=a(t)\mathbf{u},\quad\mathbf{B}(t)=b(t)\mathbf{v}^{\top}+c(t)\mathbf{z}^{\top}.

If

Moreover, as

5.2
General case: Approximate as Near-Diagonal

The analyses in the decoupled case utilize alignments between LoRA factors,
\mathbf{A}(t)
and
\mathbf{B}(t)
. However, such alignments are not applicable to
Equation

3
for LoRA initialization.
In this section, we consider the general case without alignments at initialization. Despite the lack of alignment, we show that under small initialization, all singular values of
\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)
tend to grow at nearly the same rate, even though
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are updated via separately orthogonalized gradients.

Our approach introduces a coordinate system aligned with the target
\mathbf{Y}
, and we argue that orthogonalization largely separates the growth speed from the current singular-value scale, leading to near-uniform growth across singular values.

Core Variables.

We focus on the case

for simplicity; the case
r<r^{\star}
is discussed in Appendix
Appendix

C

\bm{\Sigma}=\mathrm{Diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})

\sigma_{1}>\cdots>\sigma_{r}>0

\sigma_{i}=O(1)

Let
\mathbf{V}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-r)}

\mathbf{V}
(full right singular vectors), so that

:

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{X}(t),\mathbf{Z}(t),\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\big)\coloneqq\big(\mathbf{U}_{r}^{\top}\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{V}_{r},\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{V}_{\perp}\big).

The core product is
\mathbf{G}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{Z}(t)
, and under
\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}
, we have
\mathbf{A}(t)=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. We denote the diagonal entries of
\mathbf{G}(t)
by
d_{i}(t):=[\mathbf{G}(t)]_{ii}
for all

. Further, we let
d_{\min}(t)\coloneqq\min_{i\in[r]}d_{i}(t)
. For a square matrix
\mathbf{M}

\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{M})\coloneqq\mathbf{M}\odot(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-\mathbf{I}_{r})
where

is
active
at time
t
if
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)
, meaning it has not yet converged to its target
\sigma_{i}
. Note that, given a convergence to a global minima (
Section

6
), for any
\delta>0
there exists a finite
t_{0}>0
such that
\delta
-alignment holds for all
t\geq t_{0}
since
\mathcal{L}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\|\mathbf{G}(t)-\bm{\Sigma}\|_{F}^{2}+\|\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}\|_{F}^{2})
.

Why Near-Diagonal?

When
\mathbf{G}(t)
is diagonal, its diagonal entries
d_{i}(t)
exactly correspond to the singular values of
\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)
, greatly simplifying the analysis. Although
\mathbf{G}(t)
is not exactly diagonal in general, under
\delta
-alignment (
7
) the diagonal entries can still approximate the singular value well. This is guaranteed by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Diagonal approximation)
.

Under
\delta
-alignment (
7
), it follows that
|\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t))-d_{i}(t)|=O(\delta)
, for all
i\in[r]
, where
\sigma_{i}(\cdot)
denotes the
i
-th singular value.

In other words,
d_{i}(t)
approximates the singular value of
\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t))
up to
O(\delta)
error; see
Lemma

B.4
for the proof of
Lemma

5.2
. Hence, it suffices to analyze the dynamics of
d_{i}(t)
to understand the singular values of
\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)
. However, under LoRA initialization
\mathbf{A}(0)=\bm{0}
, the gradient
\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}
is very close to zero, thus
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
operates in its near-zero regime, where the update is dominated by the
\beta
-regularization. We therefore introduce a short time
\tau>0
such that the diagonal entries become positive enough.

Lemma 5.3

(Initial growth)
.

Let
\tau\asymp\frac{\sigma_{r}}{\sigma_{1}\sqrt{r}}
and
\tau\gamma<\sigma_{r}-\varepsilon
holds for a small
\varepsilon
such that
\varepsilon\ll\sigma_{r}
. With probability at least
1-e^{-cr}
for some absolute constant
c>0
, it follows that
d_{\min}(\tau)\geq d_{0}\asymp\tau\gamma>0
. Moreover, all modes are active at
\tau
:
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(\tau)=[r]
and
d_{i}(\tau)<\sigma_{i}
for all
i
.

By
Lemma

5.3
, we are now able to analyze the training dynamics starting at
t=\tau
, where the active set equals
[r]
. Since this regime corresponds to the early training, focusing on
t\geq\tau
incurs no loss of generality in characterizing the overall training behaviors; see
Lemma

B.5
for details.

Similar to time
\tau
, we define a termination time as
T:=\inf\{t\geq\tau:\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\emptyset\}
. Note that if SpecGF converges to a global minimum (cf.
Proposition

6.7
), the termination time
T
is necessarily finite.

We prove uniform growth through the following steps:

•

Alignment from Small Initialization
(
Proposition

5.4
). Under Gaussian initialization with small
\gamma
, the
\delta
-alignment (near-diagonal) holds throughout training for
\delta=O(\gamma)
.

•

Square-Root Dynamics
(
Lemma

5.5
and
Lemma

5.6
). In the near-diagonal regime with
d_{\min}(t)>0
, the square-root coordinates
s_{i}(t)=\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}
evolve at approximately unit rate:
\dot{s}_{i}(t)\approx 1
.

•

Uniform Growth
(
Theorem

5.7
). All active modes grow at the same rate in
\sqrt{\cdot}
-coordinates, leading to the “equal-rate” learning.

5.2.1
Alignment from Small Initialization

A key insight is that under small initialization, the trajectory satisfies
\delta
-alignment throughout training with
\delta=O(\gamma)
.

Proposition 5.4

(High-probability alignment)
.

Under Gaussian initialization
\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}
,
\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}
with
\mathbf{N}_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, the following holds on
[0,T]
with probability at least
1-e^{-cr^{2}}-e^{-crn}
for some absolute constant
c>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}=O(\gamma),~~\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}=O(\gamma).

This proposition mitigates the effect of off-diagonal interactions, which allows us to simplify the subsequent analysis. In particular, given a sufficiently small initialization scale
\gamma>0
, we can directly use the diagonal entries
d_{i}(t)
as surrogates of the singular values of
\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)
by
Lemma

5.2
. See
Proposition

B.3
for the proof of
Proposition

5.4
.

5.2.2
Square-Root Dynamics

Our analysis of uniform growth requires that
d_{\min}(t)
remain bounded away from
0
along the trajectory. The following lemma guarantees the lower bound for all
t\geq\tau
.

Lemma 5.5

(Persistence of Non-degeneracy)
.

For a sufficiently small tolerance such that
\varepsilon\ll\sigma_{r}
,
d_{\min}(t)\geq d_{0}>0
holds for all
t\in[\tau,T]
.

See
Lemma

B.9
for details. Given
d_{\min}(t)>0
, we can analyze the evolution of
s_{i}(t)=\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}
.

Lemma 5.6

(Square-root dynamics)
.

Suppose
d_{\min}(t)\geq d_{0}>0
and
\beta=O(\varepsilon^{3})
. Then for each active mode
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)
with
e_{i}(t)\leq-\varepsilon
, the square-root coordinate
s_{i}(t)=\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}
satisfies
\dot{s}_{i}(t)=1+O(\varepsilon^{1/4})
.

\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
decouples the speed from the current value
d_{i}
, unlike plain GF where
\dot{d}_{i}\propto e_{i}
; see
Lemma

B.8
for details.

5.2.3
Main Result: Uniform Growth

Putting these ingredients together, we show that all active modes grow uniformly.

Theorem 5.7

(Uniform growth)
.

For a target tolerance such that
\varepsilon\ll\sigma_{r}
, consider SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
where
\beta=O(\varepsilon^{3})
under initialization
\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}
,
\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}
where
\gamma=O(\varepsilon^{1/2})
and
\mathbf{N}_{ij}\overset{\text{i.i.d}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.
Then for all
t\in[\tau,T]
and all active modes
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)
with
e_{i}(t)\leq-\varepsilon
, the following holds with probability at least
1-e^{-cr}
for some absolute constant
c>0
:

(i)

Approximate unit speed:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}=1+O(\varepsilon^{1/4})
.

(ii)

Uniform growth:
For any two active modes
i,j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{d_{j}(t)}\right\rvert=O(\varepsilon^{1/4}).

Corollary 5.8

(Smallest singular value learns first)
.

All
\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}
trajectories are nearly parallel. Since all modes start near zero and
\sigma_{r}
requires the least growth, the smallest singular value reaches its target first.

Our analysis naturally extends to the underparameterized setting
r<r^{\star}
; the proofs largely follow those of
Theorem

5.7
. We refer to
Appendix

B
and
Appendix

C
for details.

Implication.

The uniform growth of all spectral components from SpecGF could lead to a faster convergence. Standard GF learns the spectral components one at a time by sequentially visiting the vicinity of saddle points of increasing rank
(
19
;
40
)
. Moreover, the smaller the initialization scale is, the longer it takes to escape each saddle. On the contrary, SpecGF promotes the evolution of every component. Hence, the time to learn all components essentially equals to that to learn the component with the largest singular value. See
Figure

4
.

However, learning all components uniformly is not always beneficial. When the underlying solution that generalizes well has rank smaller than the LoRA rank, i.e.
r^{*}<r
, uniform growth across all
r
singular values may be suboptimal, as it can amplify unnecessary components that are potentially harmful to generalization.

We additionally state that the spectral initialization is subsumed in the setting of
Section

5.2
, as the core product
\mathbf{G}
is always diagonal, thus the dynamics is under 0-alignment.

6
Convergence Analysis of SpecGF

In this section, we guarantee convergence for SpecGF with the smoothed operator
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
. Toward this, we begin by establishing the well-posedness/uniqueness of SpecGF solutions in the analytic regime; see
Proposition

D.3
for details.

Proposition 6.1

(Analyticity of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
)
.

SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
is analytic everywhere. SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
is analytic on the set of full-rank matrices. Moreover, for both cases, the solution uniquely exists for all
t\geq 0
where they are analytic.

Since the vector field is analytic (hence locally Lipschitz) on its domain, the solution under SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
is unique. Moreover, because
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}(\cdot)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\sqrt{r}
and
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\cdot)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\sqrt{r}
, the dynamics have bounded speed, implying that solutions do not blow up in finite time and globally exist.

Theorem 6.2
.

For almost all initial values
\mathbf{A}(0)
and
\mathbf{B}(0)
, SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
either converges to a global minimum of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
or
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{B}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}
diverges to infinity.

Whether SpecGF does not diverge to infinity in norm is crucial to claim the convergence of SpecGF. This is true for standard GF, as each factor is uniformly bounded from a time-invariant quantity,
\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}

(
5
;
6
)
. However, such invariance for SpecGF are unknown—
\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}
could fail to be invariant for SpecGF; see
Proposition

D.10
—thus whether SpecGF globally converges to a critical point is
inconclusive
. We conjecture that there are no polynomial invariants in terms of
\mathbf{A}(t)
and
\mathbf{B}(t)
for SpecGF; if there were, then it should translate to the scalar case,
m=n=1
. However, every invariant polynomial in terms of
\mathbf{A}(t)
and
\mathbf{B}(t)
for the scalar case is identically constant; see
Propositions

D.8
and
D.9
for details. Nonetheless, assuming convergence, we characterize which critical points SpecGF essentially evolves to.

Our convergence result relies on the strict-saddle geometry of the squared-loss factorization objective: a critical point
x
of a twice continuously differentiable function
f
is a
strict saddle point
if the Hessian of
f
at
x
has a negative eigenvalue.
According to
22
;
6
, every critical point of
\mathcal{L}
is either a global minimum or a strict saddle point. Moreover, every global minimum corresponds to the best rank-
r
approximation of
\mathbf{Y}
. With the fact that the set of initial points where SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
converges to a strict saddle point has Lebesgue measure zero (
Theorem

D.13
), SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
almost surely converges to the
global
minima or diverges to infinity in norm.

We prove
Theorem

6.2
by showing that SpecGF cannot wander around critical points and it avoids every “bad” critical points almost surely. We begin our argument with monotonic decrease of the loss along SpecGF trajectories.

Lemma 6.3
.

For SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
and SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{L}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq 0.

The equality holds if and only if
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)=\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)=0
.

See
Lemmas

D.4
and
D.5
for the proof of
Lemma

6.3
. Using this monotoncity of the loss,
Proposition

6.4
states that SpecGF should either converge or diverge to infinity in norm; see
Proposition

D.7
for detailed proof.

Proposition 6.4
.

Under SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq-c(r)\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(t)\|\|(\dot{\mathbf{A}}(t),\dot{\mathbf{B}}(t))\|,

(9)

where
c(r)
is a constant depending on
r
. Moreover,
(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))
either converges or diverges to infinity in norm.

Proof sketch of
Proposition

6.4
.

The computation of
c(r)
is straightforward; for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
,
c(r)=\frac{1}{2r}
. We focus on the part that SpecGF either converges or diverges to infinity in norm by using Łojasiewicz inequality, which controls the rate of convergence for gradient-based dynamics under analytic objectives
(
28
)
.

Lemma 6.5

(Łojasiewicz)
.

Let
F:U\to\mathbb{R}
be a real analytic function on an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, for every critical point
\mathbf{p}\in U
of
F
, there are a possibly smaller neighborhood
W
of
\mathbf{p}
, constants
C>0
and
b\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
such that for all
\mathbf{x}\in W
,
|F(\mathbf{x})-F(\mathbf{p})|^{b}\leq C\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\|
.

If the SpecGF iteration does not diverge to infinity in norm, it should have an accumulation point
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
; we show
(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))\to(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
. By
Equation

9
and continuity of
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}(t)
decreases to
\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
. Once
(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))
is arbitrarily close to
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
, it remains in that vicinity forever by
Equation

9
and
Lemma

6.5
.
∎

Stability of Global Minima.

We state that every global minimum is Lyapunov stable under SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
. Lyapunov stability is standard for describing whether trajectories that start near an equilibrium remain near it over time.

Definition 6.6

(Lyapunov stability)
.

A critical point
\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
is
Lyapunov stable
if for every neighborhood
U_{\epsilon}\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
of
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
, there exists a neighborhood
U_{\delta}\subset\mathbf{R}^{d}
of
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
such that for all
\mathbf{x}(t)
with
\mathbf{x}(0)\in U_{\delta}
,
\mathbf{x}(t)\in U_{\epsilon}
for all
t\geq 0
.

If then, any starting point near some global minima cannot go far away from that global minimum. By
Proposition

6.4
, bounded SpecGF iterations should converge to a critical point. The stability of the global minima is proved by
Lemma

6.5
; see
Theorem

D.15
for details. Therefore, the
local
convergence of SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
is always guaranteed.

Convergence Rate.

The convergence rate of SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
readily follows by
Lemma

6.5
. Assume that SpecGF converges to a global minimum. We show that the loss function is essentially Morse-Bott, whose optimal exponent in the Łojasiewicz inequality is
\frac{1}{2}
, leading to exponential convergence;
see
Section

D.5
for detailed proof.

Proposition 6.7

(Informal)
.

Assume that the singular values of
\mathbf{Y}
are mutually distinct. If SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
converges to a global minimum of
\mathcal{L}
, then it exponentially converges in terms of
\mathcal{L}(t)
.

The assumption of distinct singular values of the target matrix is also considered in previous studies
(
14
;
20
)
.

Remark 6.8
.

Assume that both
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)
and
\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)
are full-rank throughout SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
. Then SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
is analytic, and the same arguments for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
hold. Moreover, if SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
converges, then it should converge
in finite time
; see
Proposition

D.18
for details.

Global Convergence via Regularization.

Adding a
\ell_{2}
regularizer guarantees global convergence of SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
.
All theoretical claims for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
also apply to the regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
. Moreover, boundedness of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
readily follows from the regularization; see
Section

D.6
for details. Therefore, regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}

globally
converges to the global minima almost surely.

8
Conclusion

We analyzed spectral gradient flow (SpecGF) for LoRA-style matrix factorization and proved that singular values of the product
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
exhibit uniform growth, leading to a “equal-rate” learning that contrasts with the largest-first behavior of standard gradient flow. We also established that SpecGF almost surely converges to global minima; with
\ell_{2}
regularization, global convergence is also guaranteed. We validated our theory via experiments on matrix factorization and LLM fine-tuning. A key limitation is that global convergence without regularization remains open due to the absence of known invariants for SpecGF. Extending our continuous-time analysis to discrete-time SpecGD and more general network architectures is an important future work.

Impact Statement

This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of machine learning. There are many potential societal consequences of our work, none of which we feel must be specifically highlighted here.

Appendix A
Speed of singular values of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}

In this section, we show that the singular values of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
increase at the same speed if the whole matrix evolution can be well-decoupled into scalar evolutions.

A.1
Rank-1 Case

Next, we consider the case with random initialization but simplify the problem to rank-1
\mathbf{Y}
and
r=1
case:

\mathbf{Y}=\sigma\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^{\top},\quad\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1},\quad\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n}.

There,
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively.

A.1.1
SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}

We note that the dynamics exist uniquely (from analyticity of
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
) and globally (from boundedness of velocity by
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
). In addition, for this rank-1 case, SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
is:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L})=\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\beta}},\quad\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})=\frac{\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\beta}}.

We sample a random unit vector
\mathbf{w}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\alpha\triangleq\left\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\right\rangle\neq 0
with probability 1.

Assumption A.1
.

\alpha\neq 0
.

A typical LoRA training would start at

\mathbf{A}(0)=\bm{0},\quad\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{w}^{\top},

for sufficiently small
\gamma>0
with gradients

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y})\mathbf{B}^{\top},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y}).

We first show that
\mathbf{A}(t)\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{u}\}
and
\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\}
at all
t\geq 0
for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
.

Lemma A.2
.

For all
t\geq 0
,
\mathbf{A}(t)\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{u}\}
and
\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\}
hold.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathbf{A}(t)=a(t)\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{B}(t)=b_{0}(t)\mathbf{v}^{\top}+c_{0}(t)\mathbf{w}^{\top}
for some
a(t),b_{0}(t),c_{0}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
. We directly compute the gradients:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y})\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}=\mathbf{u}\left((a(t)b_{0}(t)-\sigma)(b_{0}(t)+c_{0}(t)\alpha)+a(t)c_{0}(t)(b_{0}(t)\alpha+c_{0}(t))\right),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y})=a(t)(a(t)b_{0}(t)-\sigma)\mathbf{v}^{\top}+a(t)^{2}c_{0}(t)\mathbf{w}^{\top}.

Normalization only modifies the coefficients, so the updates
\dot{\mathbf{A}}(t)\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{u}\}
and
\dot{\mathbf{B}}(t)^{\top}\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\}
hold. The proof is concluded as
\mathbf{A}(0)\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{u}\}
and
\mathbf{B}(0)^{\top}\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\}
.
∎

Without loss of generality, assume
\alpha>0
. If
\alpha<0
, one can simply flip the sign of
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
and the argument analogously holds. If
\alpha=1
, then
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{v}
and it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y})\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}=\mathbf{u}(a(t)b_{0}(t)-\sigma)b_{0}(t),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y})=a(t)(a(t)b_{0}(t)-\sigma)\mathbf{v}^{\top}.

Hence, if
\gamma^{2}<\sigma
, then the dynamics of
a(t)
and
b_{0}(t)
are exactly equal to those in
Proposition

A.14
. Henceforth, consider the case
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

For the convenience of later analysis, define

\mathbf{z}\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}}(\mathbf{w}-\alpha\mathbf{v}),

(10)

which gives
\mathbf{w}=\alpha\mathbf{v}+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}\mathbf{z}
and
\left\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{z}\right\rangle=0
. Thus,
\mathbf{B}(t)=(b_{0}(t)+\alpha c_{0}(t))\mathbf{v}^{\top}+\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}c_{0}(t)\mathbf{z}^{\top}
. For brevity, we write the parameters as

\mathbf{A}(t)=a(t)\mathbf{u},\quad\mathbf{B}(t)\triangleq b(t)\mathbf{v}^{\top}+c(t)\mathbf{z}^{\top}.

(11)

The goal is to show that 1)
a(t)b_{0}(t)\to\sigma

with
neither
a(t)\to\infty
nor

We derive the time derivative of the coefficients. From

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}\left((a(t)b(t)-\sigma)\mathbf{v}^{\top}+a(t)c(t)\mathbf{z}^{\top}\right)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\dot{c}(t)

Proposition A.13
.

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y})=\mathbf{Q}\bm{\Sigma}_{A}(t)^{\top}(\bm{\Sigma}_{A}(t)\bm{\Sigma}_{B}(t)-\bm{\Sigma}_{r})\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Gamma}_{A}(t)\mathbf{Q}^{\top}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathrm{Diag}\{f_{\beta}((\sigma_{\mathbf{A},i}(t)\sigma_{\mathbf{B},i}(t)-\sigma_{i})\sigma_{\mathbf{B},i}(t))\}\mathbf{Q}^{\top},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Q}\bm{\Gamma}_{B}(t)\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}=\mathbf{Q}\mathrm{Diag}\{f_{\beta}((\sigma_{\mathbf{A},i}(t)\sigma_{\mathbf{B},i}(t)-\sigma_{i})\sigma_{\mathbf{A},i}(t))\}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top},

for
i\in[r]
, where
\bm{\Gamma}_{A}(t)
and
\bm{\Gamma}_{B}(t)
are the rectangular diagonal matrices and

f_{\beta}(x)\triangleq\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\beta}}.

Hence, only the singular values of
\mathbf{A}(t)
and
\mathbf{B}(t)
are updated, and we can write

\bm{\Sigma}_{A}(0)=\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{A},0},\quad\bm{\Sigma}_{B}(0)=\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathbf{B},0}.

Notice that the overall dynamics are decoupled into
r
different scalar dynamics:

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{\mathbf{A},i}(t)

\displaystyle=-f_{\beta}((\sigma_{\mathbf{A},i}(t)\sigma_{\mathbf{B},i}(t)-\sigma_{i})\sigma_{\mathbf{B},i}(t)),

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{\mathbf{B},i}(t)

\displaystyle=-f_{\beta}((\sigma_{\mathbf{A},i}(t)\sigma_{\mathbf{B},i}(t)-\sigma_{i})\sigma_{\mathbf{A},i}(t)).

So it suffices to demonstrate about one
i\in[r]
; for simplicity, we fix
i
and simplify the notation:

\sigma_{\mathbf{A},i}(t)\triangleq a(t),\quad\sigma_{\mathbf{B},i}(t)\triangleq b(t),\quad\sigma_{i}\triangleq\sigma

and rewrite the dynamics:

\displaystyle\dot{a}(t)

\displaystyle=-f_{\beta}((a(t)b(t)-\sigma)b(t)),

\displaystyle\dot{b}(t)

\displaystyle=-f_{\beta}((a(t)b(t)-\sigma)a(t)).

We first note an immediate consequence from the dynamics:
a(t)\geq 0,b(t)\geq 0
always hold. If it were violated, then
a
and
b
must be both zero simultaneously, but it implies that the dynamics terminates.

Another consequence is that
a(t)b(t)\leq\sigma
and non-decreasing from the update of
a(t)b(t)
itself:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}a(t)b(t)=-(a(t)b(t)-\sigma)\left(\frac{a(t)^{2}}{\sqrt{(a(t)b(t)-\sigma)^{2}a(t)^{2}+\beta}}+\frac{b(t)^{2}}{\sqrt{(a(t)b(t)-\sigma)^{2}b(t)^{2}+\beta}}\right).

It is straightforward that
a(t)b(t)
is non-decreasing as long as
a(t)b(t)\leq\sigma
. Thus, there exists
\sigma_{\infty}\in[0,\sigma]
such that
a(t)b(t)\to\sigma_{\infty}
. Moreover,
a(t)
and
b(t)
are strictly increasing on
t>0
as long as
a(t)b(t)<\sigma
; the problematic case is
t=0
, but, without loss of generality, if
a(0)=0
then
b(0)>0
thus
\dot{a}(0)>0
. Hence,
a(t),b(t)>0
for all
t>0
, which yields strictly monotonic evolution. Therefore, both
a(t)
and
b(t)
are bounded and attain their respective forward limit, namely
a_{\infty}>0
and
b_{\infty}>0
.

Then
a_{\infty}b_{\infty}=\sigma_{\infty}>0
with
\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{a}(t)=-f_{\beta}((\sigma_{\infty}-\sigma)b_{\infty})=0
since
f_{\beta}^{\prime\prime}(x)
is bounded, which implies
\sigma_{\infty}=\sigma
.

Albeit the global convergence is proved, a closed form for the solution is not generally obtained. As a roundabout, we show that if
a(0),b(0)\leq\gamma
then
a(t)\approx b(t)
and
\dot{a}(t)\approx\dot{b}(t)
as
\gamma\to 0
.

Let
d(t)\triangleq a(t)-b(t)
. Then

\displaystyle\dot{d}(t)

\displaystyle=f_{\beta}((a(t)b(t)-\sigma)a(t))-f_{\beta}((a(t)b(t)-\sigma)b(t))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}(a(t)b(t)-\sigma)(a(t)-b(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}(a(t)b(t)-\sigma)d(t).

The inequality holds as
f_{\beta}
is
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}
-Lipschitz. Since
a(t)b(t)\leq\sigma
for all
t
,
|d(t)|\leq|d(0)|\leq\gamma
.

We now bound
v(t)\triangleq\dot{a}(t)-\dot{b}(t)
. From above,

|\dot{v}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta}}|(a(t)b(t)-\sigma)d(t)|\leq\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{\beta}}\gamma.\qed

B.1

\beta
-regularized orthogonalization.

Fix
\beta>0

For
\mathbf{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{col},\beta}(\mathbf{G})\coloneqq\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{G}^{\top}\mathbf{G}+\beta\mathbf{I})^{-1/2},

The case

Lemma B.1

(Operator norm bound)
.

For all
\beta>0
and all
\mathbf{G}

\displaystyle\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{col},\beta}(\mathbf{G})\rVert_{2}\leq 1,\qquad\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{row},\beta}(\mathbf{G})\rVert_{2}\leq 1.

Proof.

Let
s
be any singular value of
\mathbf{G}
. Then
s
becomes
s/\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta}\leq 1
under either normalization, hence the operator norm is
\leq 1
.
∎

Lemma B.2

(Invariance)
.

If
\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}
, then
\mathbf{A}(t)\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U}_{r})
for all
t\geq 0
. Consequently,
\mathbf{A}(t)=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}(t)
for some
\mathbf{X}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
.

Proof.

The gradient with respect to
\mathbf{A}
is
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}=(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y})\mathbf{B}^{\top}
. At
t=0
, we have
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(0)=-\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{B}(0)^{\top}=-\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}\mathbf{B}(0)^{\top}
, which lies in
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U}_{r})
. Since
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
(and
\mathcal{T}
) preserves the column space,
\dot{\mathbf{A}}(0)\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U}_{r})
.

By induction, suppose
\mathbf{A}(t)\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U}_{r})
, i.e.,
\mathbf{A}(t)=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}(t)
for some
\mathbf{X}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top})\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}=\mathbf{U}_{r}(\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}-\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}).

This again lies in
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U}_{r})
, so
\dot{\mathbf{A}}(t)=-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t))\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U}_{r})
. By continuity of the flow,
\mathbf{A}(t)\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U}_{r})
for all
t\geq 0
.
∎

Core variables.

Let
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}
be the compact SVD with
\bm{\Sigma}=\mathrm{Diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})
and
\sigma_{1}>\cdots>\sigma_{r}>0
, where
\mathbf{U}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
and
\mathbf{V}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
. Let
\mathbf{V}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-r)}
denote the orthogonal complement of
\mathbf{V}_{r}
.
We define the core variables:

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{U}_{r}^{\top}\mathbf{A}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},\quad\mathbf{Z}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{V}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},\quad\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{V}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(n-r)}.

The core product is
\mathbf{G}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{Z}(t)
. For a square matrix
\mathbf{M}
, we write
\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{M})\coloneqq\mathbf{M}-\mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{M}))
for its off-diagonal part.

We introduce two tolerances:

•

Alignment tolerance
\delta>0
:
Time
t
is
\delta
-aligned if
\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta
.

•

Target tolerance
\varepsilon>0
:
We denote
d_{i}(t)\coloneqq[\mathbf{G}(t)]_{ii}
,
e_{i}(t)\coloneqq d_{i}(t)-\sigma_{i}
, and the active set
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)\coloneqq\{i\in[r]:|e_{i}(t)|>\varepsilon\}
.

A mode
i
is
active
if
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)
. Let
T\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq\tau:\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\emptyset\}
be the termination time.

B.2
Alignment from Small Initialization

Proposition B.3

(High-probability alignment)
.

Let
T
be a termination time above.
Under Gaussian initialization
\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}
,
\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}
with
N_{ij}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,
there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}<\infty
(depending on
\mathbf{Y},r,n,T
) such that,
with probability at least
1-\exp(-c_{1}r^{2})-\exp(-c_{1}rn)
for some constant
c_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq C_{1}\gamma,\qquad\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq C_{2}\gamma.

Consequently, the trajectory is
O(\gamma)
-aligned for all
t\leq T
.

Proof.

Both bounds follow from Lipschitz stability near the diagonal manifold
\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\{(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}):\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}\text{ diagonal},\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}=0\}
. Let
\mathbf{S}(t)\coloneqq(\mathbf{X}(t),\mathbf{Z}(t),\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t))
and consider the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{M}
:

\displaystyle\Pi(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Z}_{\perp})\coloneqq\big(\mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{X})),\mathrm{Diag}(\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{Z})),\mathbf{0}\big).

Let
\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\coloneqq(\mathbf{X}^{\star}(t),\mathbf{Z}^{\star}(t),\mathbf{Z}^{\star}_{\perp}(t))
be the trajectory of SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
trajectory, started at
\mathbf{S}^{\star}(0)\coloneqq\Pi(\mathbf{S}(0))
.
By diagonal manifold invariance,
\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\in\mathcal{M}
for all
t
, hence
\mathbf{Z}^{\star}_{\perp}(t)\equiv\mathbf{0}
and, writing
\mathbf{G}^{\star}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{X}^{\star}(t)\mathbf{Z}^{\star}(t)
,
\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}^{\star}(t))\equiv\mathbf{0}
.

By speed bounds and Gaussian concentration, both trajectories remain in a compact set
\mathcal{K}
for
t\in[0,T]
.
Since SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
defines a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
vector field, finite-horizon Lipschitz stability gives

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{S}(t)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq e^{LT}\left\lVert\mathbf{S}(0)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(0)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

for some finite
L=L(\mathcal{K})
.

Note that
\left\lVert\mathbf{S}(0)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(0)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{Z}(0))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(0)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}
because
\mathbf{X}(0)=\mathbf{0}
.
Since
\mathbf{Z}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{r}
and
\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}
where
\mathbf{N}
has i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
entries, and
\mathbf{V}_{r}
,
\mathbf{V}_{\perp}
have orthonormal columns, the entries of
\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{r}
and
\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}
are also i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.
Thus
\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{r})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\sim\chi^{2}_{r(r-1)}
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\sim\chi^{2}_{r(n-r)}
.
By chi-squared concentration,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{Z}(0))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(0)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}>C\gamma(r+\sqrt{rn})\big)\leq\exp(-c_{1}r^{2})+\exp(-c_{1}rn)

for some absolute constants
C,c_{1}>0
.
Consequently,
\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathbf{S}(t)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\kappa\gamma
for some finite
\kappa>0
with probability at least
1-\exp(-c_{1}r^{2})-\exp(-c_{1}rn)
.

Since
\mathbf{Z}^{\star}_{\perp}(t)\equiv\mathbf{0}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}=\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)-\mathbf{Z}^{\star}_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{S}(t)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\kappa\gamma.

By speed bounds
\lVert\mathbf{X}(t)\rVert_{2}\leq t\leq T
, hence
\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq T\kappa\gamma\coloneqq C_{2}\gamma
.

For the off-diagonal bound, using
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}
and
\mathbf{G}^{\star}=\mathbf{X}^{\star}\mathbf{Z}^{\star}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{G}^{\star}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{X}^{\star}\mathbf{Z}^{\star}=(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}^{\star})\mathbf{Z}+\mathbf{X}^{\star}(\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{Z}^{\star}).

Since both trajectories remain in the compact set
\mathcal{K}
for
t\in[0,T]
, there exists
M=M(\mathcal{K})<\infty
such that
\|\mathbf{Z}(t)\|_{2},\|\mathbf{X}^{\star}(t)\|_{2}\leq M
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{G}(t)-\mathbf{G}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{X}(t)-\mathbf{X}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\lVert\mathbf{Z}(t)\rVert_{2}+\lVert\mathbf{X}^{\star}(t)\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}(t)-\mathbf{Z}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq M\big(\left\lVert\mathbf{X}(t)-\mathbf{X}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}(t)-\mathbf{Z}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq M\sqrt{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{S}(t)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}.

Since
\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}^{\star}(t))\equiv\mathbf{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}=\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}(t)-\mathbf{G}^{\star}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{G}(t)-\mathbf{G}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq M\sqrt{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{S}(t)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}.

Combining these bounds yields
\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq C_{1}\gamma
.
∎

Lemma B.4

(Diagonal approximation)
.

Suppose
\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta
. Then there exists a permutation
\pi(t)
such that

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\pi_{i}(t)}(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t))-d_{i}(t)|=O(\delta)

for all
i\in[r]
, where
\pi_{i}(t)
denotes the index satisfying
d_{i}(t)\approx\sigma_{\pi_{i}(t)}(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t))
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}+\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}^{\top}.

Since
\mathbf{U}_{r},\mathbf{V}_{r},\mathbf{V}_{\perp}
have orthonormal columns, the singular values of
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}
equal those of
[\mathbf{G}\mid\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}]
.
We write
\mathbf{D}=\mathrm{Diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})
. The singular values of
[\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{0}]
are exactly
\{|d_{1}|,\ldots,|d_{r}|\}
, and

\displaystyle\|[\mathbf{G}\mid\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}]-[\mathbf{D}\mid\mathbf{0}]\|_{F}\leq\|\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G})\|_{F}+\|\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}\|_{F}\leq 2\delta.

By Weyl’s inequality (or Mirsky), there exists a permutation
\pi
of
[r]
such that

\displaystyle|\sigma_{\pi(i)}([\mathbf{G}\mid\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}])-|d_{i}||\leq 2\delta\quad\text{for all }i\in[r].

Since
d_{i}>0
on our trajectory, we conclude
|\sigma_{\pi(i)}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})-d_{i}|=O(\delta)
.
∎

B.3
Initial Growth and Non-degeneracy

Lemma B.5

(Initial growth)
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{M}
where
\mathbf{M}\coloneqq\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, with
\mathbf{N}
having i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
entries and
\beta=O(\gamma^{2})
.
Write
\mathbf{M}=[\mathbf{c}_{1}\mid\cdots\mid\mathbf{c}_{r}]
where
\mathbf{c}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
denotes the
i
-th column.
Define the high-probability event

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\left\{\|\mathbf{M}\|_{2}\leq C_{3}\sqrt{r},\quad\min_{i\in[r]}\|\mathbf{c}_{i}\|^{2}\geq c_{1}r\right\}.

Then
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\geq 1-e^{-c_{1}r}
for some absolute constants
C_{3},c_{1}>0
. Let
\tau\asymp\frac{\sigma_{r}}{\sigma_{1}\sqrt{r}}
. On the event
\mathcal{E}
,

\displaystyle d_{\min}(\tau)\geq c_{2}\tau\gamma

for some constant
c_{2}>0
.
Moreover, all modes are active at
\tau
:
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(\tau)=[r]
and
d_{i}(\tau)<\sigma_{i}
for all
i
.

Proof.

For an
r\times r
Gaussian matrix
\mathbf{M}
, standard concentration gives
\|\mathbf{M}\|_{2}\leq 3\sqrt{r}
with probability at least
1-2e^{-r/2}
.
Each column
\mathbf{c}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{r})
satisfies
\|\mathbf{c}_{i}\|^{2}\sim\chi^{2}_{r}
, so
\mathbb{P}(\|\mathbf{c}_{i}\|^{2}<\frac{r}{2})\leq e^{-c_{1}r}
for some absolute constant
c_{1}>0
.
By a union bound,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\geq 1-e^{-c_{1}r}
.

We now bound
[\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)\mathbf{Z}(0)]_{ii}
on
\mathcal{E}
; note that
\dot{\mathbf{Z}}(0)=\bm{0}
.
From the dynamics of SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
at
t=0
with
\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{Z}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{M}
:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)=\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{col},\beta}(\gamma\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{M}^{\top})=\gamma\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{M}^{\top}(\gamma^{2}\mathbf{M}\bm{\Sigma}^{2}\mathbf{M}^{\top}+\beta\mathbf{I})^{-1/2}.

Define the positive definite matrix
\mathbf{Q}\coloneqq\gamma^{2}\mathbf{M}\bm{\Sigma}^{2}\mathbf{M}^{\top}+\beta\mathbf{I}
. Then

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)\mathbf{Z}(0)=\gamma^{2}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{M}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}^{-1/2}\mathbf{M},

whose
i
-th diagonal entry equals
\gamma^{2}\sigma_{i}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}^{-1/2}\mathbf{c}_{i}
.
Since
\mathbf{Q}^{-1/2}
is positive definite with minimum eigenvalue
\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{Q})^{-1/2}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}^{-1/2}\mathbf{c}_{i}\geq\frac{\|\mathbf{c}_{i}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{Q})}}.

On
\mathcal{E}
, we have
\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{Q})\leq\gamma^{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}\|\mathbf{M}\|_{2}^{2}+\beta\leq C_{3}^{2}\gamma^{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}r+\beta
.
Assuming
\beta\leq C\gamma^{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}r
for some absolute constant
C>0
, this gives
\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{Q})\leq C^{\prime}\gamma^{2}\sigma_{1}^{2}r
, and hence

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{Q})}\leq\sqrt{C^{\prime}}\cdot\gamma\sigma_{1}\sqrt{r}.

Combined with
\|\mathbf{c}_{i}\|^{2}\geq c_{1}r
, we obtain

\displaystyle[\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)\mathbf{Z}(0)]_{ii}\geq\gamma^{2}\sigma_{r}\cdot\frac{c_{1}r}{\sqrt{C^{\prime}}\cdot\gamma\sigma_{1}\sqrt{r}}=\frac{c_{1}\gamma\sigma_{r}\sqrt{r}}{\sqrt{C^{\prime}}\sigma_{1}}.

Finally, we apply Taylor expansion.
For
\beta>0
, the vector field is
C^{1}
, so for sufficiently small
\tau
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}(\tau)=\tau\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)+O(\tau^{2}),\qquad\mathbf{Z}(\tau)=\mathbf{Z}(0)+O(\tau^{2}),

where the second equality uses
\dot{\mathbf{Z}}(0)=\mathbf{0}
(because
\mathbf{X}(0)=\mathbf{0}
).
Expanding
\mathbf{G}(\tau)=\mathbf{X}(\tau)\mathbf{Z}(\tau)
,

\displaystyle d_{\min}(\tau)\geq\tau\cdot\min_{i}[\dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)\mathbf{Z}(0)]_{ii}-O(\tau^{2}\gamma)\asymp\tau\gamma.

For the second claim, note that
d_{i}(\tau)\asymp\tau\gamma\ll\sigma_{r}-\varepsilon
for sufficiently small
\gamma
. Hence, it holds that
e_{i}(\tau)=d_{i}(\tau)-\sigma_{i}<-\varepsilon
for all
i\in[r]
, so all modes are active (i.e., early phase of training).
∎

B.4
Invertibility and Near-Diagonal Factors

Lemma B.6

(Invertibility under alignment and non-degeneracy)
.

Suppose
\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta
and
d_{\min}(t)>0
.
If
\delta<d_{\min}(t)
, then both
\mathbf{X}(t)
and
\mathbf{Z}(t)
are invertible.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{D}(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{Diag}(d_{1}(t),\ldots,d_{r}(t))
.
Since
d_{\min}>0
, the diagonal matrix
\mathbf{D}
is invertible. Write
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}))
.
We have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G})\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{D}^{-1}\|_{2}\|\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G})\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{d_{\min}}\cdot\delta=\frac{\delta}{d_{\min}}<1.

By the Neumann series,
\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G})
is invertible, hence
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{D}^{-1}\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}))
is invertible.
Thus,
\det(\mathbf{G})=\det(\mathbf{X})\det(\mathbf{Z})\neq 0
implies both factors are invertible.
∎

Lemma B.7

(Near-diagonal factors)
.

Under the hypotheses of
Proposition

B.3
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{X}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq C_{4}\gamma,\qquad\sup_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{Z}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq C_{5}\gamma.

Moreover, on any interval where
d_{\min}(t)\geq d_{0}>0
, the diagonal entries satisfy

\displaystyle|[\mathbf{X}(t)]_{ii}-\widetilde{s}_{i}(t)|\leq C_{6}\Big(\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\Big),\qquad|[\mathbf{Z}(t)]_{ii}-\widetilde{s}_{i}(t)|\leq C_{6}\Big(\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\Big),

where
\widetilde{s}_{i}(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{i})\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}
is the signed square root with
\xi_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,
\kappa
is the Lipschitz constant from
Proposition

B.3
, and
C_{6}
depends only on
\kappa
.

Proof.

By Lipschitz stability (
Proposition

B.3
),
\|\mathbf{X}(t)-\mathbf{X}^{\star}(t)\|_{F}\leq\kappa\gamma
where
\mathbf{X}^{\star}(t)\in\mathcal{M}
is diagonal. Since
\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{X}^{\star})=\mathbf{0}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{X})\|_{F}=\|\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}^{\star})\|_{F}\leq\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}^{\star}\|_{F}\leq\kappa\gamma.

The same bound holds for
\mathbf{Z}
similarly. For the diagonal entries, we let

\displaystyle x_{i}\coloneqq[\mathbf{X}]_{ii},\quad z_{i}\coloneqq[\mathbf{Z}]_{ii},\quad x_{i}^{\star}\coloneqq[\mathbf{X}^{\star}]_{ii},\quad z_{i}^{\star}\coloneqq[\mathbf{Z}^{\star}]_{ii}.

The reference trajectory starts from
x_{i}^{\star}(0)=0
and
z_{i}^{\star}(0)=\gamma\xi_{i}
. Since
\xi_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, Gaussian concentration gives
|\xi_{i}|=O(1)
with high probability, so it holds that
|x_{i}^{\star}(0)-z_{i}^{\star}(0)|=O(\gamma)
.

On diagonal manifold
\mathcal{M}
, the SpecGF dynamics for mode
i
are

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}^{\star}=-\frac{e_{i}^{\star}z_{i}^{\star}}{\sqrt{(e_{i}^{\star}z_{i}^{\star})^{2}+\beta}},\qquad\dot{z}_{i}^{\star}=-\frac{e_{i}^{\star}x_{i}^{\star}}{\sqrt{(e_{i}^{\star}x_{i}^{\star})^{2}+\beta}},

where
e_{i}^{\star}(t)=x_{i}^{\star}(t)z_{i}^{\star}(t)-\sigma_{i}
. Define the strictly increasing function
g(t)\coloneqq\frac{t}{\sqrt{t^{2}+\beta}}
. The imbalance
\delta_{i}^{\star}\coloneqq x_{i}^{\star}-z_{i}^{\star}
satisfies

\displaystyle\dot{\delta}_{i}^{\star}=g(e_{i}^{\star}x_{i}^{\star})-g(e_{i}^{\star}z_{i}^{\star}).

Since
e_{i}^{\star}x_{i}^{\star}-e_{i}^{\star}z_{i}^{\star}=e_{i}^{\star}\delta_{i}^{\star}
, for
e_{i}^{\star}<0
, the sign of
(e_{i}^{\star}x_{i}^{\star})-(e_{i}^{\star}z_{i}^{\star})
is opposite to that of
\delta_{i}^{\star}
. By the monotonicity of
g
, this implies
\delta_{i}^{\star}\cdot\dot{\delta}_{i}^{\star}\leq 0
, hence
|\delta_{i}^{\star}(t)|
is non-increasing.

We now show that
e_{i}^{\star}(t)\leq 0
for all
t\geq 0
. At
t=0
, we have
e_{i}^{\star}(0)=x_{i}^{\star}(0)z_{i}^{\star}(0)-\sigma_{i}=-\sigma_{i}<0
. If
e_{i}^{\star}(t_{0})=0
for some
t_{0}>0
, then
\dot{x}_{i}^{\star}(t_{0})=\dot{z}_{i}^{\star}(t_{0})=0
. By ODE uniqueness (due to locally Lipschitz),
(x_{i}^{\star},z_{i}^{\star})
remains constant for
t\geq t_{0}
, so
e_{i}^{\star}(t)=0
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. Thus,
e_{i}^{\star}(t)
cannot become positive, and
e_{i}^{\star}(t)\leq 0
for all
t
.

Combining the above,
\delta_{i}^{\star}\cdot\dot{\delta}_{i}^{\star}\leq 0
holds for all
t
, and thus

\displaystyle|x_{i}^{\star}(t)-z_{i}^{\star}(t)|\leq|x_{i}^{\star}(0)-z_{i}^{\star}(0)|=O(\gamma)\quad\text{for all }t.

Now, we define

\displaystyle\widetilde{s}_{i}^{\star}\coloneqq\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{i})\sqrt{d_{i}^{\star}},\qquad\widetilde{s}_{i}\coloneqq\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{i})\sqrt{d_{i}}.

We claim that
x_{i}^{\star},z_{i}^{\star},\widetilde{s}_{i}^{\star}
all share the same sign as
\xi_{i}
. To see this, note that
z_{i}^{\star}(0)=\gamma\xi_{i}
has the sign of
\xi_{i}
, while
x_{i}^{\star}(0)=0
. For
e_{i}^{\star}\leq 0
, the dynamics give

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}^{\star}=-\frac{e_{i}^{\star}z_{i}^{\star}}{\sqrt{(e_{i}^{\star}z_{i}^{\star})^{2}+\beta}},

which has the same sign as
z_{i}^{\star}
. Thus,
x_{i}^{\star}
immediately enters the same sign as
z_{i}^{\star}
and cannot cross zero thereafter (since
\dot{x}_{i}^{\star}
would push it back). Similarly,
\dot{z}_{i}^{\star}
has the same sign as
x_{i}^{\star}
, so
z_{i}^{\star}
also maintains its sign.

Since
\|\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{G}^{\star}\|_{F}\leq\kappa\gamma
, we have
|d_{i}-d_{i}^{\star}|\leq\kappa\gamma
. On intervals where
d_{\min}(t)\geq d_{0}
,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{s}_{i}-\widetilde{s}_{i}^{\star}|=|\sqrt{d_{i}}-\sqrt{d_{i}^{\star}}|=\frac{|d_{i}-d_{i}^{\star}|}{\sqrt{d_{i}}+\sqrt{d_{i}^{\star}}}\leq\frac{\kappa\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{i}}}\leq\frac{\kappa\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}.

Since
x_{i}^{\star}
and
\widetilde{s}_{i}^{\star}
have the same sign, we obtain

\displaystyle|x_{i}^{\star}-\widetilde{s}_{i}^{\star}|=\Big||x_{i}^{\star}|-\sqrt{|x_{i}^{\star}||z_{i}^{\star}|}\Big|\leq|x_{i}^{\star}-z_{i}^{\star}|=O(\gamma).

Combining with
|x_{i}-x_{i}^{\star}|\leq\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}^{\star}\|_{F}\leq\kappa\gamma
, the triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle|x_{i}-\widetilde{s}_{i}|\leq|x_{i}-x_{i}^{\star}|+|x_{i}^{\star}-\widetilde{s}_{i}^{\star}|+|\widetilde{s}_{i}^{\star}-\widetilde{s}_{i}|\leq C_{6}\Big(\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\Big),

where
C_{6}
depends only on
\kappa
. The same argument gives
|z_{i}-\widetilde{s}_{i}|\leq C_{6}(\gamma+\gamma/\sqrt{d_{0}})
.
∎

B.5
Square-Root Dynamics

Lemma B.8

(Square-root dynamics)
.

Suppose
\delta
-alignment holds with
\delta=O(\gamma)
,
\gamma=\Theta(\varepsilon^{1/2})
, and
\beta=O(\varepsilon^{3})
.
Let
[\tau^{\prime},T^{\prime}]
be any subinterval of
[\tau,T]

on which

\displaystyle d_{\min}(t)\coloneqq\min_{i\in[r]}d_{i}(t)\geq d_{0}=\Theta(\gamma).

Then for each active mode
i
(i.e.,
e_{i}(t)\leq-\varepsilon
), the square-root coordinate

s_{i}(t)\coloneqq\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}
satisfies, for a.e.
t\in[\tau^{\prime},T^{\prime}]
,

\dot{s}_{i}(t)=-\mathrm{sgn}(e_{i}(t))\,\alpha_{i}(t)+R_{i}(t),\qquad\alpha_{i}(t)\coloneqq\frac{|e_{i}(t)|\,s_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{e_{i}(t)^{2}s_{i}(t)^{2}+\beta}}\in(0,1].

(19)

Moreover, the remainder obeys

|R_{i}(t)|\leq C_{7}\varepsilon^{1/4},

(20)

where
C_{7}=C_{7}(\mathbf{Y},r)
depends only on constants from
Lemma

B.7
(and earlier constants).

In particular, for every active mode
i
(i.e.,
e_{i}(t)\leq-\varepsilon
),

\displaystyle\dot{s}_{i}(t)=1+O(\varepsilon^{1/4}).

Proof.

Since
d_{i}(t)=[\mathbf{X}(t)\mathbf{Z}(t)]_{ii}
and
s_{i}(t)=\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}
, the chain rule gives, for a.e.
t
with
d_{i}(t)>0
,

\displaystyle\dot{d}_{i}(t)=[\dot{\mathbf{X}}(t)\mathbf{Z}(t)]_{ii}+[\mathbf{X}(t)\dot{\mathbf{Z}}(t)]_{ii},\qquad\dot{s}_{i}(t)=\frac{\dot{d}_{i}(t)}{2s_{i}(t)}.

(21)

Let
\mathbf{E}(t)\coloneqq\mathbf{G}(t)-\bm{\Sigma}
and
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{X}}\coloneqq\mathbf{E}\mathbf{Z}^{\top}+\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}^{\top}=\mathbf{U}_{r}^{\top}\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}
.
By
Lemma

B.7
,
\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{Z}
are nearly diagonal:

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}=\mathrm{Diag}(x_{i})+\bm{\Delta}_{X},\qquad\mathbf{Z}=\mathrm{Diag}(z_{i})+\bm{\Delta}_{Z},

where
x_{i},z_{i}=\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{i})s_{i}+O(\gamma+\gamma/\sqrt{d_{0}})
and
\|\bm{\Delta}_{X}\|_{F},\|\bm{\Delta}_{Z}\|_{F}=O(\gamma)
. The SpecGF dynamics give

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{X}}=-\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{col},\beta}(\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{X}}),\qquad\dot{\mathbf{Z}}=-\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{row},\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})\,\mathbf{V}_{r}.

Since
\|\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}\|_{F}=O(\delta)
(by
\delta
-alignment), we have
\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{X}}=\mathbf{E}\mathbf{Z}^{\top}+O(\delta)
.
Combined with
\mathbf{E}=\mathrm{Diag}(e_{i})+O(\delta)
and
z_{i}=\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{i})s_{i}+O(\gamma)
, this yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{X}}=\mathrm{Diag}(e_{i}z_{i})+O(\delta+\gamma),\qquad\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\mathbf{G}_{\mathbf{X}}=\mathrm{Diag}(e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2})+O(\delta+\gamma).

Since
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{col},\beta}(\mathbf{G})=\mathbf{G}\,(\mathbf{G}^{\top}\mathbf{G}+\beta\mathbf{I})^{-1/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle[\dot{\mathbf{X}}]_{ii}=-\frac{e_{i}z_{i}}{\sqrt{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}+\beta}}+O(\delta+\gamma)=-\frac{e_{i}\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{i})s_{i}}{\sqrt{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}+\beta}}+O(\delta+\gamma).

(22)

For
\dot{\mathbf{Z}}
, write
\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{E}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}+\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}^{\top}
.
Using
\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}\mathbf{V}_{r}=\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{V}_{\perp}^{\top}\mathbf{V}_{r}=\mathbf{0}
, we have
\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}\,\mathbf{V}_{r}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{E}
, so

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{Z}}=\dot{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{V}_{r}=-\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{row},\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})\,\mathbf{V}_{r}=-\bigl((\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{E})(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{E})^{\top}+\beta\mathbf{I}\bigr)^{-1/2}\,\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{E}.

Moreover,
(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{E})(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{E})^{\top}=\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{E}\mathbf{E}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=\mathrm{Diag}(s_{i}^{2}e_{i}^{2})+O(\delta+\gamma)
, so

\displaystyle[\dot{\mathbf{Z}}]_{ii}=-\frac{x_{i}e_{i}}{\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}e_{i}^{2}+\beta}}+O(\delta+\gamma)=-\frac{\mathrm{sgn}(\xi_{i})s_{i}\,e_{i}}{\sqrt{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}+\beta}}+O(\delta+\gamma).

(23)

Using
Lemma

B.7
and (
22
)–(
23
), we compute the diagonal entries of
\dot{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{Z}
and
\mathbf{X}\dot{\mathbf{Z}}
:

\displaystyle[\dot{\mathbf{X}}\mathbf{Z}]_{ii}

\displaystyle=[\dot{\mathbf{X}}]_{ii}\,[\mathbf{Z}]_{ii}+O(\|\dot{\mathbf{X}}\|_{F}\|\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{Z})\|_{F})=-\frac{e_{i}s_{i}^{2}}{\sqrt{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}+\beta}}+O\!\Bigl(\delta+\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\Bigr),

(24)

\displaystyle[\mathbf{X}\dot{\mathbf{Z}}]_{ii}

\displaystyle=[\mathbf{X}]_{ii}\,[\dot{\mathbf{Z}}]_{ii}+O(\|\mathbf{X}\|_{F}\|\mathrm{Off}(\dot{\mathbf{Z}})\|_{F})=-\frac{e_{i}s_{i}^{2}}{\sqrt{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}+\beta}}+O\!\Bigl(\delta+\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\Bigr).

(25)

Summing (
24
)–(
25
) and using (
21
),

\displaystyle\dot{d}_{i}=-\frac{2e_{i}s_{i}^{2}}{\sqrt{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}+\beta}}+O\!\Bigl(\delta+\gamma+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\Bigr),\qquad\dot{s}_{i}=-\frac{e_{i}s_{i}}{\sqrt{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}+\beta}}+O\!\Bigl(\delta+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{d_{0}}}\Bigr),

where we used
s_{i}\geq\sqrt{d_{0}}
on
[\tau^{\prime},T^{\prime}]
to absorb the
O(\gamma)
contribution into
O(\gamma/\sqrt{d_{0}})
after dividing by
2s_{i}
.
Under the scaling
\gamma=\Theta(\varepsilon^{1/2})
,
d_{0}=\Theta(\gamma)
, and
\delta=O(\gamma)
, the dominant error term is
\gamma/\sqrt{d_{0}}=O(\varepsilon^{1/4})
.

Defining
\alpha_{i}(t)\coloneqq\frac{|e_{i}(t)|\,s_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{e_{i}(t)^{2}s_{i}(t)^{2}+\beta}}\in(0,1]
and
R_{i}(t)\coloneqq\dot{s}_{i}(t)+\mathrm{sgn}(e_{i}(t))\,\alpha_{i}(t)
, we see that (
19
) holds and (
20
) follows.

Finally, for active modes
e_{i}(t)\leq-\varepsilon
, we have
\mathrm{sgn}(e_{i})=-1
and

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=\frac{|e_{i}|\,s_{i}}{\sqrt{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}+\beta}}=\Bigl(1+\frac{\beta}{e_{i}^{2}s_{i}^{2}}\Bigr)^{-1/2}\geq 1-\frac{\beta}{2\varepsilon^{2}d_{0}}=1-O(\varepsilon^{1/2}),

using
\beta=O(\varepsilon^{3})
and
d_{0}=\Theta(\varepsilon^{1/2})
. Since
\alpha_{i}\leq 1
trivially and
|R_{i}|=O(\varepsilon^{1/4})
, we conclude
\dot{s}_{i}=1+O(\varepsilon^{1/4})
for active modes.∎

B.6
Non-degeneracy Persistence

Lemma B.9

(Non-degeneracy persistence)
.

Suppose
d_{\min}(\tau)\geq d_{0}=\Theta(\gamma)
with
\gamma=\Theta(\varepsilon^{1/2})
and
\beta=O(\varepsilon^{3})
.
Let
\underline{d}\coloneqq\min\{d_{0},\sigma_{r}-\varepsilon\}
.
Then for all
t\in[\tau,T]
,

\displaystyle d_{\min}(t)\geq\underline{d}>0.

Proof.

We define the exit time
T_{\mathrm{exit}}\coloneqq\inf\{t>\tau:d_{\min}(t)<\underline{d}\}
.
We show that
T_{\mathrm{exit}}>T
, which implies
d_{\min}(t)\geq\underline{d}
for all
t\in[\tau,T]
. On
[\tau,T_{\mathrm{exit}})
, we have
d_{\min}(t)\geq\underline{d}>0
by definition, so
Lemma

B.8
applies.
For any active mode
i
(i.e.,
e_{i}(t)\leq-\varepsilon
), we have
\dot{s}_{i}(t)=1+O(\varepsilon^{1/4})>0
for small
\varepsilon
.
Hence,
s_{i}(t)
is strictly increasing, and so is
d_{i}(t)=s_{i}(t)^{2}
.

For contradiction, suppose that some mode
i
reaches
d_{i}(t)=\underline{d}
from above. Then
e_{i}(t)=d_{i}(t)-\sigma_{i}\leq\underline{d}-\sigma_{r}<-\varepsilon
(since
\underline{d}\leq\sigma_{r}-\varepsilon
), so
i
is active and
\dot{d}_{i}(t)>0
. This contradicts the decrease after reaching
\underline{d}
. By continuity,
d_{\min}(t)
cannot reach
\underline{d}
on
[\tau,T]
. It follows that
T_{\mathrm{exit}}>T
, and hence
d_{\min}(t)\geq\underline{d}
for all
t\in[\tau,T]
.
∎

B.7
Main Theorem: Uniform Growth

Theorem B.10

(Uniform growth)
.

Fix a small target tolerance
\varepsilon>0
. Consider SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
initialized at
\mathbf{A}(0)=\mathbf{0}
,
\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}
.
Let
\tau\asymp\frac{\sigma_{r}}{\sigma_{1}\sqrt{r}}
and let
T
be the termination time.
For
\gamma=\Theta(\varepsilon^{1/2})
and
\beta=O(\varepsilon^{3})
, the following holds with high probability for all
t\in[\tau,T]
and all active modes
i\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)
:

(i)

Approximate unit speed:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}=1+O(\varepsilon^{1/4})
.

(ii)

Uniform growth:
For any two active modes
i,j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(t)
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{d_{i}(t)}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{d_{j}(t)}\right\rvert=O(\varepsilon^{1/4}).

Proof.

By
Lemma

B.5
,
d_{\min}(\tau)\geq d_{0}
for
d_{0}\asymp\tau\gamma=\Theta(\gamma)=\Theta(\varepsilon^{1/2})
with high probability.
By
Proposition

B.3
,
\delta=O(\gamma)=O(\varepsilon^{1/2})
.
Applying
Lemmas

B.8
and
B.9
, every active mode satisfies
\dot{s}_{i}(t)=1+O(\varepsilon^{1/4})
.
This proves (i). For (ii), for any active
i,j
,

\displaystyle|\dot{s}_{i}(t)-\dot{s}_{j}(t)|\leq|\dot{s}_{i}(t)-1|+|\dot{s}_{j}(t)-1|=O(\varepsilon^{1/4}),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Extension to Underparameterized Case (
r<r^{\star}
)

We extend the uniform growth analysis to the underparameterized regime where the LoRA rank
r
is smaller than the target rank
r^{\star}\coloneqq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})
. The key insight is that the column space invariance and diagonal manifold structure still hold within the relevant subspace.

C.1
Setup and Extended Core Variables

Let
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
be the compact SVD with
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r^{\star}}
,
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r^{\star}}
, and
\bm{\Sigma}=\mathrm{Diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r^{\star}})
with
\sigma_{1}>\cdots>\sigma_{r^{\star}}>0
. The LoRA factorization has rank
r\leq r^{\star}
:
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}
.

We partition the target singular vectors as
\mathbf{U}=[\mathbf{U}_{r}\mid\mathbf{U}_{>r}]
and
\mathbf{V}=[\mathbf{V}_{r}\mid\mathbf{V}_{>r}]
, where
\mathbf{U}_{r},\mathbf{V}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{\cdot\times r}
correspond to the top
r
singular values and
\mathbf{U}_{>r},\mathbf{V}_{>r}
correspond to the remaining
r^{\star}-r
singular values. Let
\mathbf{V}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-r^{\star})}
denote the orthogonal complement of
\mathbf{V}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The extended core variables are:

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{U}_{r}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{>r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{U}_{>r}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{(r^{\star}-r)\times r},

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{B}\mathbf{V}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{>r}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{B}\mathbf{V}_{>r}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(r^{\star}-r)},

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{B}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(n-r^{\star})}.

In this setup, the core product is defined by
\mathbf{G}_{r}\coloneqq\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{Z}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
.

Similar to
\delta
-alignment, we define
extended
\delta
-alignment
by

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathrm{Off}(\mathbf{G}_{r}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta,\quad\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{>r}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta,\quad\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}_{>r}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta,\quad\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{r}(t)\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta.

This generalizes the original
\delta
-alignment condition to the underparameterized setting by additionally controlling the components outside the top-
r
subspace. Note that, given a convergence to the best rank-
r
approximation (cf.
Propositions

D.11
and
D.13
), for any
\delta>0
we can always find some finite
t_{0}>0
such that extended
\delta
-alignment holds for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

C.2
Column Space Invariance

Lemma C.1

(Column space invariance)
.

If
\mathbf{A}(0)=\bm{0}
, then
\mathbf{A}(t)\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U})
for all
t\geq 0
. Consequently,
\mathbf{A}(t)=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}_{r}(t)+\mathbf{U}_{>r}\mathbf{X}_{>r}(t)
.

Proof.

The gradient
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}=(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y})\mathbf{B}^{\top}
lies in
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{A})+\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{Y})\subseteq\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U})
whenever
\mathbf{A}\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U})
. At
t=0
,
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(0)=-\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{B}(0)^{\top}\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U})
. Since
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
preserves column spaces,
\dot{\mathbf{A}}(0)\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U})
.
By induction and continuity of the flow,
\mathbf{A}(t)\in\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U})
for all
t\geq 0
.
∎

C.3
Invariant Manifold

Lemma C.2

(Invariant manifold)
.

Under the initialization
\mathbf{A}(0)=\bm{0}
and
\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}
with
\mathbf{N}
having i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
entries, the extended manifold

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ext}}\coloneqq\big\{(\mathbf{X}_{r},\mathbf{X}_{>r},\mathbf{Z}_{r},\mathbf{Z}_{>r},\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}):\mathbf{X}_{>r}=\bm{0},\;\mathbf{Z}_{>r}=\bm{0},\;\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}=\bm{0}\big\}

is invariant under the SpecGF dynamics.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\Sigma}_{r}=\mathrm{Diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})
and
\bm{\Sigma}_{>r}=\mathrm{Diag}(\sigma_{r+1},\ldots,\sigma_{r^{\star}})
. On
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ext}}
, we have
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}_{r}
and
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{Z}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}
, so

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{Z}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}-\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}-\mathbf{U}_{>r}\bm{\Sigma}_{>r}\mathbf{V}_{>r}^{\top}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{r}(\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{Z}_{r}-\bm{\Sigma}_{r})\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}-\mathbf{U}_{>r}\bm{\Sigma}_{>r}\mathbf{V}_{>r}^{\top}.

The gradient
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}=(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y})\mathbf{B}^{\top}=\mathbf{U}_{r}(\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{Z}_{r}-\bm{\Sigma}_{r})\mathbf{Z}_{r}^{\top}
lies entirely in
\mathrm{col}(\mathbf{U}_{r})
, implying
\dot{\mathbf{X}}_{>r}=\mathbf{U}_{>r}^{\top}\dot{\mathbf{A}}=\bm{0}
.

Similarly,
\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}=\mathbf{X}_{r}^{\top}(\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{Z}_{r}-\bm{\Sigma}_{r})\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}-\mathbf{X}_{>r}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{>r}\mathbf{V}_{>r}^{\top}
. When
\mathbf{X}_{>r}=\bm{0}
, this lies entirely in the row space of
\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}
, so
\dot{\mathbf{Z}}_{>r}=\dot{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{V}_{>r}=\bm{0}
and
\dot{\mathbf{Z}}_{\perp}=\dot{\mathbf{B}}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}=\bm{0}
.
∎

C.4
Lipschitz Stability near the Invariant Manifold

Proposition C.3

(Alignment in the underparameterized case)
.

Under the initialization
\mathbf{A}(0)=\bm{0}
and
\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}
, there exist constants
C_{>r},C_{\perp}<\infty
such that, with probability at least
1-e^{-cr(r^{\star}-r)}-e^{-cr(n-r^{\star})}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\|\mathbf{X}_{>r}(t)\|_{F}\leq C_{>r}\gamma,\qquad\sup_{t\leq T}\|\mathbf{Z}_{>r}(t)\|_{F}\leq C_{>r}\gamma,\qquad\sup_{t\leq T}\|\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t)\|_{F}\leq C_{\perp}\gamma.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{S}(t)\coloneqq(\mathbf{X}_{r}(t),\mathbf{X}_{>r}(t),\mathbf{Z}_{r}(t),\mathbf{Z}_{>r}(t),\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(t))
denote the true trajectory. At initialization with
\mathbf{A}(0)=\bm{0}
and
\mathbf{B}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}
, the core variables satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{r}(0)=\bm{0},\quad\mathbf{X}_{>r}(0)=\bm{0},\quad\mathbf{Z}_{r}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{r},\quad\mathbf{Z}_{>r}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{>r},\quad\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(0)=\gamma\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}.

We define the projection onto
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ext}}
by
\Pi(\mathbf{X}_{r},\mathbf{X}_{>r},\mathbf{Z}_{r},\mathbf{Z}_{>r},\mathbf{Z}_{\perp})\coloneqq(\mathbf{X}_{r},\bm{0},\mathbf{Z}_{r},\bm{0},\bm{0})
, and consider the reference trajectory
\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)
initialized at
\mathbf{S}^{\star}(0)=\Pi(\mathbf{S}(0))=(\bm{0},\bm{0},\gamma\mathbf{N}\mathbf{V}_{r},\bm{0},\bm{0})
. By
Lemma

C.2
,
\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ext}}
for all
t\geq 0
.

The initial discrepancy is

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}(0)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(0)\|_{F}=\|\mathbf{Z}_{>r}(0)\|_{F}+\|\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}(0)\|_{F}=O(\gamma\sqrt{r(n-r)})

with high probability by Gaussian concentration.

The SpecGF vector field with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
, so finite-horizon Lipschitz stability gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}(t)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(t)\|_{F}\leq e^{LH}\|\mathbf{S}(0)-\mathbf{S}^{\star}(0)\|_{F}\leq\kappa\gamma

for some finite
\kappa=\kappa(\mathbf{Y},r,T)
. Since
\mathbf{X}^{\star}_{>r}(t)\equiv\bm{0}
,
\mathbf{Z}^{\star}_{>r}(t)\equiv\bm{0}
, and
\mathbf{Z}^{\star}_{\perp}(t)\equiv\bm{0}
, the bounds naturally hold.
∎

C.5
Reduction to the Square Case

On the invariant manifold
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ext}}
, the dynamics reduce to the
r\times r
system:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{X}}_{r}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{col},\beta}\big((\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{Z}_{r}-\bm{\Sigma}_{r})\mathbf{Z}_{r}^{\top}\big),

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{Z}}_{r}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{row},\beta}\big(\mathbf{X}_{r}^{\top}(\mathbf{X}_{r}\mathbf{Z}_{r}-\bm{\Sigma}_{r})\big).

This is equivalent to the original rank-
r^{\star}
dynamics (in case of
r=r^{\star}
) with target
\bm{\Sigma}_{r}=\mathrm{Diag}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})

By
Proposition

C.3
, perturbations from
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{ext}}
are controlled:
\|\mathbf{X}_{>r}\|_{F},\|\mathbf{Z}_{>r}\|_{F},\|\mathbf{Z}_{\perp}\|_{F}=O(\gamma)
. These contribute only at most
O(\gamma)
errors to the effective dynamics of
(\mathbf{X}_{r},\mathbf{Z}_{r})
.

Theorem C.4

(Uniform growth for

Consider SpecGF with

on the rank-

\mathbf{Y}

Appendix D
Convergence to Global Minima of SpecGF

D.1
Analyticity of SpecGF

We first state that the inverse square root of symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices is analytic. This directly follows from
7
.

Lemma D.1
.

At any real SPD
\mathbf{S}
, the function

g(\mathbf{S})=\mathbf{S}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

is analytic. That is,
g
is analytic on the (open) set of the symmetric positive-definite matrices.

Proof.

Any SPD matrix
\mathbf{S}
is closed, invertible, (densely) defined linear operator on a Banach space (also a Hilbert space for our case), denoted as
X
, and has real eigenvalues. Throughout this proof, we abuse the notation
\sigma(\mathbf{S})
to denote the spectrum of
\mathbf{S}
.

We first show
\mathbf{S}
is sectorial of any
\omega\in[0,\pi)
; fix any
\omega
.
\sigma(\mathbf{S})
is exactly the set of eigenvalues of
\mathbf{S}
, and therefore is contained in
\overline{S_{\omega}}
. Next, for each
\omega^{\prime}\in(\omega,\pi)
, take any
\lambda\not\in\overline{S_{\omega^{\prime}}}
. Then
\left\lvert\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda)\right\rvert>\omega^{\prime}
and thus, for any eigenvalue
s
of
\mathbf{S}
,

|s-\lambda|\geq\mathrm{dist}(\lambda,(0,+\infty))=\begin{cases}|\lambda|\left\lvert\sin(\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda))\right\rvert\geq|\lambda|\sin\omega^{\prime}&\text{if }\left\lvert\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda)\right\rvert\leq\frac{\pi}{2},\\
|\lambda|\geq|\lambda|\sin\omega^{\prime}&\text{if }\left\lvert\mathrm{Arg}(\lambda)\right\rvert>\frac{\pi}{2}.\end{cases}

Hence,

\lVert\lambda(\mathbf{S}-\lambda)^{-1}\rVert=\max_{s\in\sigma(\mathbf{S})}\frac{|\lambda|}{|s-\lambda|}\leq\frac{1}{\sin\omega^{\prime}}

is uniformly bounded. On Hilbert space, R-boundedness is equivalent to boundedness
(
7
)
. Thus,
\mathbf{S}
is R-sectorial of any
\omega\in[0,\pi)
. We also see that
\mathbf{S}
admits a bounded
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(S_{\omega})
for any
\omega\in[0,\pi)
, because for
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(S_{\omega})\setminus\{0\}
,

\lVert f(\mathbf{S})\rVert_{L(X)}=\max_{s\in\sigma(\mathbf{S})}|f(s)|\leq\sup_{f}\;\lVert f\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(S_{\omega})}

As
\mathbf{S}
is SPD, there exists a closed centered ball not in
\sigma(\mathbf{S})
. Now take
r=\frac{1}{2}
in the statement of
7
. Then
g(\mathbf{S})
is holomorphic, thus real-analytic restricted to the set of real matrices. As the dimension is finite, the fractional domain spaces
\dot{X}_{p}
coincides with
X
(viewed as sets) and thus
L(\dot{X}_{p},\dot{X}_{q})=L(X)
for any real
p
and
q
(so are
L(\dot{X}_{0},\dot{X}_{<r})
and
L(\dot{X}_{>1-r},\dot{X}_{r})
).
∎

Corollary D.2
.

Consider a matrix
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
with
m\geq r
.

(a)

\mathbf{X}\mapsto(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is analytic on the (open) set of full-rank matrices.

(b)

For every
\beta>0
,

\mathbf{X}\mapsto(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}+\beta\mathbf{I}_{r})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is analytic everywhere.

The above statements analogously hold for
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}
with
n\geq r
by switching
\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
to
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\top}
.

Proof.

This is apparent from
Lemma

D.1
, the fact that the polynomials are analytic, and the composition of analytic functions is again analytic.
∎

Recall that, for a matrix
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{M})

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}\right)^{\dagger}\right)^{1/2}\!\mathbf{M},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{M})

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}+\beta\mathbf{I}_{m}\right)^{-1/2}\!\mathbf{M},

Let the compact SVD of
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be
\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}
with
\mathbf{S}\triangleq\mathrm{Diag}\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\}
.
\sigma_{i}>0
are the positive singular values of
\mathbf{X}
,
1\leq i\leq r\leq\min\{m,n\}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{S}_{\beta}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top},

where

\mathbf{S}_{\beta}\triangleq\mathrm{Diag}\left\{\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}^{2}+\beta}}\right\}_{i\in[r]}.

Proposition D.3

(Analyticity of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
)
.

SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
is analytic everywhere. SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
is analytic on the set of full-rank matrices. Moreover, the solution uniquely exists for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Analyticity is straightforward from
Corollary

D.2
, so we prove the existence and uniqueness part. For both
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
,
\|\dot{\mathbf{A}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}
and
\|\dot{\mathbf{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}
are both bounded by
r
. Hence, the trajectory should satisfy

\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}(0),\mathbf{B}(0))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}+\sqrt{2r}t.

If the maximal solution does not exist for all
t\geq 0
, then the iterate should leave every compact set of the domain. Therefore, the solution must exist for all
t\geq 0
. Uniqueness follows from the local Lipschitzness in
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{G})
of SpecGF (by Picard-Lindelöf theorem).
∎

D.2
Convergence Analysis of SpecGF

We restate the setup of SpecGF.

•

The target matrix
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
yields a compact SVD
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{U}_{r^{\star}}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}_{r^{\star}}^{\top}
where
\bm{\Sigma}=\mathrm{Diag}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\cdots,\sigma_{r^{\star}})
with
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{r^{\star}}>0
.

•

The loss function
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

(26)

We may abuse the notation and simply state
\mathcal{L}(t)
.

•

The time derivative of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
is governed by either
\mathcal{T}
or
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
applied to the gradient:

\dot{\mathbf{A}}(t)=-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right),\quad\dot{\mathbf{B}}(t)=-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right).

Lemma D.4
.

On SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
, the loss is monotonically non-increasing:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{L}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq 0.

The equality holds if and only if
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)=\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)=0
holds.

Proof.

We first show that for any matrix
\mathbf{X}
,
\left\langle\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{X}\right\rangle=\lVert\mathbf{X}\rVert_{*}
. Let the compact SVD of
\mathbf{X}
be
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}^{\prime\top}
.

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{X}\right\rangle=\mathrm{tr}\left((\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}^{\prime\top})^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}^{\prime\top}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{V}^{\prime}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}^{\prime\top}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right)=\lVert\mathbf{X}\rVert_{*}.

We drop the argument
t
for simplicity.

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{L}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L},\dot{\mathbf{A}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L},\dot{\mathbf{B}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L},-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L},-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\|\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}\|_{*}-\|\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}\|_{*}\leq 0.\qed

Lemma D.5
.

On SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
, the loss is monotonically non-increasing:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{L}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq 0.

The equality holds if and only if
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)=\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)=0
holds.

Proof.

We first simplify
\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{X}\right\rangle
for a matrix
\mathbf{X}
. Let the SVD of
\mathbf{X}
be
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}^{\prime\top}
with singular values
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\geq 0
.

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{X}),\mathbf{X}\right\rangle=\mathrm{tr}\left((\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\beta}\mathbf{V}^{\prime\top})^{\top}\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}^{\prime\top}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{V}^{\prime}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\beta}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{V}^{\prime\top}\right)=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{\beta}\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{i\in[r]}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}^{2}+\beta}}.

We drop the argument
t
for simplicity. Let
\sigma_{i}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}),\sigma_{i}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})
be the singular values of
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}
and
\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}
, respectively. Then:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{L}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L},\dot{\mathbf{A}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L},\dot{\mathbf{B}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L},-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L},-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in[r]}\frac{\sigma_{i}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L})^{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L})^{2}+\beta}}-\sum_{i\in[r]}\frac{\sigma_{i}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})^{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})^{2}+\beta}}\leq 0.\qed

Thus,
\mathcal{L}(t)
is monotonically non-increasing and lower bounded, hence
\mathcal{L}(\infty)\leq\mathcal{L}(0)
should exist. Moreover,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}=2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}(t)}+\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq 2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}(0)}+\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}.

(27)

We now show that SpecGF iterate should either converge or diverge to infinity.

Lemma D.6

(Łojasiewicz)
.

Let
F:U\to\mathbb{R}
be a real analytic function on an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, for every critical point
\mathbf{p}\in U
of
F
, there exists a (possibly small) neighborhood
W
of
\mathbf{p}
, constants
C>0
and
b\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
such that for all
\mathbf{x}\in W
,

|F(\mathbf{x})-F(\mathbf{p})|^{b}\leq C\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x})\|.

(28)

Proposition D.7
.

Let
\mathbf{x}(t)
be a differentiable and absolutely continuous curve on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
F
be a real analytic function on an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that
F(\mathbf{x}(t))
is well-defined for all
t\geq 0
and the condition

\frac{\mathrm{d}F(\mathbf{x}(t))}{\mathrm{d}t}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=0.

(29)

If there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\frac{\mathrm{d}F(\mathbf{x}(t))}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq-c\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))\|\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\|

(30)

for all
t\geq 0
, then either
\mathbf{x}(t)
converges in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or it diverges to infinity (in norm).

Proof.

Assume that
\|\mathbf{x}(t)\|\not\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
. Then
\mathbf{x}(t)
has an accumulation point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
; it suffices to prove that the accumulation point is the limit of
\mathbf{x}(t)
. Denote the accumulation point as
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
.

By continuity of
F
and monotonicity of
F
from
Equation

30
,
F(\mathbf{x}(t))\downarrow F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})
. If there exists a finite time
t^{\prime}\geq 0
such that
F(\mathbf{x}(t^{\prime}))=F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})
, then
F(\mathbf{x}(t))=F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})
for
t\geq t^{\prime}
, hence
\mathbf{x}(t)=\bar{\mathbf{x}}
by
Equation

29
. From here we assume that
F(\mathbf{x}(t))>F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})
for all
t
.

From
Lemma

D.6
and
Equation

30
, we see that

\frac{\mathrm{d}F(\mathbf{x}(t))}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq-c\|\nabla F(\mathbf{x}(t))\|\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\|\leq-\frac{c}{C}|F(\mathbf{x}(t))-F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})|^{b}\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\|=-\frac{c}{C}\left(F(\mathbf{x}(t))-F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\right)^{b}\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\|

on some neighborhood
W
of
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
with
C>0
and
b\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
. Hence,

\displaystyle-\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\|\geq\frac{C}{c}\frac{\mathrm{d}F(\mathbf{x}(t))}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(F(\mathbf{x}(t))-F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\right)^{-b}=\frac{C}{c(1-b)}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(F(\mathbf{x}(t))-F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\right)^{1-b}.

For brevity, let
G(t)\triangleq F(\mathbf{x}(t))-F(\bar{\mathbf{x}})>0
. Then
G
is absolutely continuous and
G(t)\downarrow 0
. We also see that

-\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\|\geq\frac{C}{c(1-b)}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(G(t)^{1-b}\right).

(31)

We now show that
\mathbf{x}(t)
eventually moves to the vicinity of
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
and remains there forever. As
W
is open, there exists
r>0
where

B(r)\triangleq\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|z-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|<r\}\subset W.

Fix any such
r>0
. Because
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
is the accumulation point of
\mathbf{x}(t)
and
G
continuously decreases to zero, there exists a time
t_{1}\geq 0
such that

\|x(t_{1})-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|<\frac{r}{2},\quad G(t_{1})<\left(\frac{rc(1-b)}{2C}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-b}}.

Notice the former implies
x(t_{1})\in B(r)
and the latter implies
\tfrac{C}{c(1-b)}G(t_{1})^{1-b}<\tfrac{r}{2}
. Suppose the claim is false, so there exists
t_{2}>t_{1}
where
x(t_{2})\not\in B(r)
; choose the smallest such
t_{2}
. Then
\mathbf{x}(t)\in B(r)
for
t\in[t_{1},t_{2})
, hence

\displaystyle\|x(t_{2})-x(t_{1})\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)\|\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}-\frac{C}{c(1-b)}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(G(t)^{1-b}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{c(1-b)}\left[G(t_{1})^{1-b}-G(t_{2})^{1-b}\right]<\frac{r}{2}.

The first inequality and the equality follow from the absolute continuity of
\mathbf{x}(t)
and
G(t)
on
[t_{1},t_{2}]
, respectively. The second inequality stems from
Equation

31
. The above yields a contradiction:

\|x(t_{2})-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|\leq\|x(t_{2})-x(t_{1})\|+\|x(t_{1})-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|<r\quad\Rightarrow\quad x(t_{2})\in B(r).

Therefore, for all
t\geq t_{1}
,
\mathbf{x}(t)\in B(r)
. As
r
can be arbitrarily small,
\mathbf{x}(t)\to\bar{\mathbf{x}}
should hold. ∎

It is evident that
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is analytic everywhere. For
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
,
\mathbf{A}(t)
and
\mathbf{B}(t)
are continuously differentiable as their time derivative
-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t))
and
-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t))
are analytic. In addition,
\mathbf{M}=0\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{M})=0
, hence
Equation

29
is satisfied. Moreover, let the rank of
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)
and
\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)
be
r_{A}
,
r_{B}
, respectively. Sort the singular values
\sigma(t)
of the gradients with respect to
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
in non-increasing order. Then:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{L}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\in[r_{A}+r_{B}]}\sigma_{i}(t)\frac{\sigma_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}(t)^{2}+\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{r_{A}+r_{B}}\sum_{i\in[r_{A}+r_{B}]}\sigma_{i}(t)\sum_{i\in[r_{A}+r_{B}]}\frac{\sigma_{i}(t)}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}(t)^{2}+\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{r_{A}+r_{B}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\left\lVert(\dot{\mathbf{A}},\dot{\mathbf{B}})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2r}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\left\lVert(\dot{\mathbf{A}},\dot{\mathbf{B}})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}.

The first inequality follows from Chebyshev’s sum inequality. Hence,
Equation

30
is also met. Therefore, the SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
can only either converge to a critical point or diverge to infinity.

For
\mathcal{T}
, assuming that iteration is always full rank (for the analyticity),

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{L}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\|\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)\|_{*}-\|\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq-\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}-\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq-\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2r}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\left\lVert(\dot{\mathbf{A}},\dot{\mathbf{B}})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}.

The last inequality follows from the fact that
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{M})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{M})
for any matrix
\mathbf{M}
.

Invariance.

We conjecture that SpecGF may not have a nontrivial invariant term with respect to
\mathbf{A}(t)
and
\mathbf{B}(t)
, at least for polynomials, while plain GF has one. Here, the terminology “invariant” is a function
I:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all solutions
\mathbf{x}(t)
of the differential equation
\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=f(\mathbf{x})
,
I(\mathbf{x}(t))
is constant (possibly depending on
\mathbf{x}(0)
). Then,
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I(\mathbf{x}(t))\equiv 0
, or

\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}I,f(\mathbf{x})\right\rangle\equiv 0

globally holds on the set of initial points where the solution is well-defined. If there exists an invariant polynomial in
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, it should work for the
m=n=1
case. However, it can be shown that any polynomial should be identically constant on whole
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
. Let
m=n=1=r
. Then
\mathbf{Y}=\sigma\neq 0
,
a(t),b(t)\in\mathbb{R}
, and thus the loss becomes

\mathcal{L}(a,b)=\frac{1}{2}(ab-\sigma)^{2}.

We state the partial derivatives for convenience:

\partial_{a}\mathcal{L}=b(ab-\sigma),\quad\partial_{b}\mathcal{L}=a(ab-\sigma).

Proposition D.8

(Absence of polynomial invariant for
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
)
.

For SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
, there does not exist a non-constant invariant polynomial with real coefficients in
a
and
b
on all of
\>\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Note that, as
\mathbb{R}
is a field,
\mathbb{R}[a][b]\cong\mathbb{R}[a,b]\cong\mathbb{R}[b][a]
. In addition,
\mathbb{R}[x]
is a unique factorization domain (UFD) for an indeterminate
x
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}[a,b]
be a polynomial (of finite degree) with real coefficients. Then

\partial_{a}P(a(t),b(t))\dot{a}(t)+\partial_{b}P(a(t),b(t))\dot{b}(t)=0.

Substitute
\dot{a}(t)=-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\partial_{a}\mathcal{L})
and
\dot{b}(t)=-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\partial_{b}\mathcal{L})
. Rearranging the equations gives that, for any
a
and
b
,

\partial_{a}P(a,b)\times b(ab-\sigma)\sqrt{a^{2}(ab-\sigma)^{2}+\beta}+\partial_{b}P(a,b)\times a(ab-\sigma)\sqrt{b^{2}(ab-\sigma)^{2}+\beta}=0.

For simplicity, denote
a^{2}(ab-\sigma)^{2}+\beta
and
b^{2}(ab-\sigma)^{2}+\beta
as
Q_{(a)}
and
Q_{(b)}
in
\mathbb{R}[a,b]
, respectively. Then

\left(\partial_{a}P(a,b)\right)^{2}\times b^{2}Q_{(a)}=\left(\partial_{b}P(a,b)\right)^{2}\times a^{2}Q_{(b)}.

Plugging
a=0
gives
\left(\partial_{a}P(0,b)\right)^{2}\times b^{2}\beta=0
, which implies that
a\>|\>\partial_{a}P(a,b)
. Plugging
b=0
implies
b\>|\>\partial_{b}P(a,b)
by analogous reasoning. We see that there exist some polynomials
P_{(a)}^{\prime}
and
P_{(b)}^{\prime}
that

\partial_{a}P(a,b)=aP_{(a)}^{\prime},\quad\partial_{b}P(a,b)=bP_{(b)}^{\prime}.

Thus, for any
a
and
b
,

P_{(a)}^{\prime 2}Q_{(a)}=P_{(b)}^{\prime 2}Q_{(b)}.

As
\beta>0
, the discriminant of
Q_{(a)}
, viewed as a quadratic in
b
, is

(-2a^{3}\sigma)^{2}-4(a^{4})(a^{2}\sigma^{2}+\beta)=-4a^{4}\beta,

which is not a perfect square in
\mathbb{R}[a]
. For a quadratic with a non-perfect square discriminant, it has no roots in
\mathbb{R}[a]
.
\mathbb{R}[a]
being UFD allows us to use Gauss’ lemma, hence
Q_{(a)}
is (irreducible, thus) a prime in
\mathbb{R}[a,b]
. Similarly,
Q_{(b)}
is also a prime in
\mathbb{R}[a,b]
. We now show that
Q_{(a)}
and
Q_{(b)}
are coprime. Indeed, for every common factor
D
of
Q_{(a)}
and
Q_{(b)}
,
D
should also divide

b^{2}Q_{(a)}-a^{2}Q_{(b)}=\beta(b^{2}-a^{2})=\beta(b-a)(b+a).

Since
b-a
and
b+a
are coprime, either
D\>|\>(b-a)
or
D\>|\>(b+a)
. However,
Q_{(a)}(a,a)=a^{2}(a^{2}-\sigma)^{2}+\beta\not\equiv 0
and
Q_{(a)}(a,-a)=a^{2}(-a^{2}-\sigma)^{2}+\beta\not\equiv 0
; so
D=1
.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle Q_{(a)}\>|\>P_{(b)}^{\prime 2}\quad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad Q_{(a)}\>|\>P_{(b)}^{\prime},

\displaystyle Q_{(b)}\>|\>P_{(a)}^{\prime 2}\quad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad Q_{(b)}\>|\>P_{(a)}^{\prime}.

Then, there exist some polynomials
P_{(a)}^{\prime\prime}
and
P_{(b)}^{\prime\prime}
that

P_{(a)}^{\prime}=Q_{(b)}P_{(a)}^{\prime\prime},\quad P_{(b)}^{\prime}=Q_{(a)}P_{(b)}^{\prime\prime}.

Thus,

P_{(a)}^{\prime\prime 2}Q_{(b)}=P_{(b)}^{\prime\prime 2}Q_{(a)}.

Then, with similar argument, there exist some polynomials
P_{(a)}^{\prime\prime\prime}
and
P_{(b)}^{\prime\prime\prime}
that

P_{(a)}^{\prime\prime}=Q_{(a)}P_{(a)}^{\prime\prime\prime},\quad P_{(b)}^{\prime\prime}=Q_{(b)}P_{(b)}^{\prime\prime\prime}.

Thus,

P_{(a)}^{\prime\prime\prime 2}Q_{(a)}=P_{(b)}^{\prime\prime\prime 2}Q_{(b)}.

The above reasoning implies that
P_{(a)}^{\prime}
is divisible by
Q_{(a)}^{k}
for all positive integers
k\geq 1
, which can only happen when
P_{(a)}^{\prime}\equiv 0
; we also have
P_{(b)}^{\prime}\equiv 0
. Then
\partial_{a}P(a,b)\equiv 0
and
\partial_{b}P(a,b)\equiv 0
. Therefore, such an invariant
P
has to be constant.
∎

The similar result is obtained for
\mathcal{T}
on the set where SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
is analytic.

Proposition D.9

(Absence of polynomial invariant for
\mathcal{T}
)
.

For SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
, there does not exist a non-constant invariant polynomial with real coefficients in
a
and
b
on

O\triangleq\left\{(a,b):a\neq 0,b\neq 0,ab-\sigma\neq 0\right\}.

Proof.

Note that on
\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{T}(x)=\mathrm{sgn}(x)\quad(x\neq 0),

where
\mathrm{sgn}
is the sign function.

Let
P
be a polynomial (of finite degree) with real coefficients. Then

\partial_{a}P(a(t),b(t))\dot{a}(t)+\partial_{b}P(a(t),b(t))\dot{b}(t)=0.

Substitute
\dot{a}(t)=-\mathcal{T}(\partial_{a}\mathcal{L})
and
\dot{b}(t)=-\mathcal{T}(\partial_{b}\mathcal{L})
. Rearranging the equations gives that, for any
a
and
b
in
O
,

\partial_{a}P(a,b)\times\mathrm{sgn}\left(b(ab-\sigma)\right)+\partial_{b}P(a,b)\times\mathrm{sgn}\left(a(ab-\sigma)\right)=0.

If
\mathrm{sgn}(a)=\mathrm{sgn}(b)
, we have

\partial_{a}P(a,b)+\partial_{b}P(a,b)=0.

As the equality holds on the open subset, we actually have

\partial_{a}P(a,b)+\partial_{b}P(a,b)\equiv 0

on all of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
. Analogously, if
\mathrm{sgn}(a)=-\mathrm{sgn}(b)
, we have

\partial_{a}P(a,b)-\partial_{b}P(a,b)=0,

so in fact, on all of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
,

\partial_{a}P(a,b)-\partial_{b}P(a,b)\equiv 0.

Therefore
\partial_{a}P(a,b)
and
\partial_{b}P(a,b)
is identically zero everywhere, implying that such an invariant
P
has to be constant.
∎

In addition, we show that
\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}
is not an invariant for SpecGF with both
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
.

Proposition D.10
.

For both SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
and
\mathcal{T}
, there exists
\mathbf{Y}
such that
\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}
cannot be an invariant.

Proof.

Fix
\sigma>0
and
a,b\neq 0
with
ab\neq\sigma
,
|a|\neq|b|
. We show that with

\mathbf{Y}=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma\mathbf{I}_{r^{\star}}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}

for any
r^{\star}\geq r
, SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
cannot have
\mathbf{A}(t)^{\top}\mathbf{A}(t)-\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{B}(t)^{\top}
as its invariant, due to its non-zero time derivative at

(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}a\mathbf{I}_{r}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}b\mathbf{I}_{r}&\bm{0}\\
\end{bmatrix}\right)

Compute the gradient at
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}b(ab-\sigma)\mathbf{I}_{r}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}a^{\prime}\mathbf{I}_{r}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}a(ab-\sigma)\mathbf{I}_{r}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}b^{\prime}\mathbf{I}_{r}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}.

Then, for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{A}}=-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{a^{\prime}}{\sqrt{a^{\prime 2}+\beta}}\mathbf{I}_{r}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{B}}=-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{b^{\prime}}{\sqrt{b^{\prime 2}+\beta}}\mathbf{I}_{r}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}.

Hence, the time derivative of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
translate to that of
a
and
b
, respectively. We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\top})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(a^{2}-b^{2})\mathbf{I}_{r}

\displaystyle=-2\left(a\frac{a^{\prime}}{\sqrt{a^{\prime 2}+\beta}}-b\frac{b^{\prime}}{\sqrt{b^{\prime 2}+\beta}}\right)\mathbf{I}_{r}

\displaystyle=-2ab(ab-\sigma)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b^{2}(ab-\sigma)^{2}+\beta}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{2}(ab-\sigma)^{2}+\beta}}\right)\mathbf{I}_{r}\neq\bm{0}.

The same setting results in the same result for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
. For SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{A}}=-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-\mathrm{sgn}(a^{\prime})\mathbf{I}_{r}\\
\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{B}}=-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-\mathrm{sgn}(b^{\prime})\mathbf{I}_{r}&\bm{0}\end{bmatrix}.

Again, the time derivative of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
translate to that of
a
and
b
, respectively. We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\top})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(a^{2}-b^{2})\mathbf{I}_{r}

\displaystyle=-2\left(a\cdot\mathrm{sgn}(a^{\prime})-b\cdot\mathrm{sgn}(b^{\prime})\right)\mathbf{I}_{r}

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{sgn}(ab-\sigma)\left(a\cdot\mathrm{sgn}(b)-b\cdot\mathrm{sgn}(a)\right)\mathbf{I}_{r}\neq\bm{0}.\qed

D.3
Saddle Point Avoidance

6
characterize the critical points of the loss

L^{N}(\mathbf{W}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{W}_{N})=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{W}_{N}\cdots\mathbf{W}_{1}\mathbf{X}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

(32)

where
\mathbf{W}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{j}\times d_{j-1}}
for
d_{0}=d_{x}
,
d_{N}=d_{y}
and
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}\times m}
and
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}\times m}
. They analyzed whether the critical points are the minima or strict saddle points through a closely related loss

L^{1}(\mathbf{W})=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{W}\mathbf{X}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2},

where
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{y}\times d_{x}}
. We summarize their result, especially for
N=2
case.

Proposition D.11

(
(
6
)
, Proposition 6.6, 6.9, 6.11)
.

Let
r\triangleq\min\{d_{0},d_{1},d_{2}\}
. Assume that
\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\top}
has full rank
d_{0}
,
\mathbf{Y}
is of rank
q
, and
\mathbf{W}_{k}
is the global minimum of
L^{1}
restricted to a set of matrices whose rank is at most
k
. Let
K
be the rank of
\mathbf{W}_{2}\mathbf{W}_{1}
.

1.

\mathbf{W}_{k}
is the best rank-
k
approximation of
\mathbf{Y}
.

2.

If
K=\min\{r,q\}
, every critical point
(\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{2})
that satisfies
\mathbf{W}_{2}\mathbf{W}_{1}=\mathbf{W}_{K}
is the global minimum of
L^{2}
. If
\mathbf{W}_{2}\mathbf{W}_{1}\neq\mathbf{W}_{K}
, then
(\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{2})
is a strict saddle point.

3.

Any other critical point
(\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{2})
, including the origin, is a strict saddle point.

We say that a critical point
x_{c}
of a twice continuously differentiable function
f
is a
strict saddle point
if the Hessian of
f
at
x_{c}
has a negative eigenvalue.
Proposition

D.11
implies that every critical point of
L^{2}
is exactly either a global minimum or a strict saddle point. Notice that if
\mathbf{X}
is the identity matrix and
N=2
, then the setting of
6
exactly matches with ours. They also proved that the set of initial points such that the corresponding plain gradient flow for
Equation

32
converges to a strict saddle point has measure zero
(
6
)
[Theorem 6.3]. We also show that this holds for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
. Then, if SpecGF converges, it almost surely converges to the global minima, or the best low-rank approximation of
\mathbf{Y}
.

To this end, we employ a proposition from
11
that claims the similar result.

Proposition D.12

(
(
11
)
, Proposition 2.5)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that there exist open sets
V\subseteq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the complement of
V
have Lebesgue measure zero and
f
is continuously differentiable on
U
with a locally Lipschitz continuous Jacobian, which is non-degenerate on
V
. Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{x\in U:f(x)=x\}
and assume for all
x\in\mathcal{S}
that
f^{\prime}(x)
is symmetric and has an eigenvalue of absolute value strictly greater than 1. Then, the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lim_{k\to\infty}f^{k}(x)\in\mathcal{S}\}
has Lebesgue measure 0.

There,
f^{k}(x)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is inductively defined as
f^{0}\equiv\mathrm{id}
and
f^{k+1}=f\circ f^{k}
.

Theorem D.13
.

SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
almost surely avoids strict saddle points. Specifically, if
r\leq\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Y})
, then SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
almost surely converges to the best rank-
r
approximation of
\mathbf{Y}
if convergence is guaranteed.

Proof.

We first verify that the conditions of
Proposition

D.12
are met for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
and then extend the statement from the discrete to the continuous version.

For simplicity, let
\mathbf{x}(t)\triangleq(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))
and
f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\left(-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})),-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}))\right)
. Denote the solution of

\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x}),\quad\mathbf{x}(0)\triangleq\mathbf{x}_{0}.

as
\phi(t)
; when the initial point matters, we use

\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\phi(t).

We note that
\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})
, and also
\phi(t)
, satisfy the semigroup property:

\varphi_{t^{\prime}+t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\varphi_{t^{\prime}}(\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})).

The Jacobian matrix of
\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})
,
D\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})
, satisfies the following with Jacobian matrix of
f_{\beta}
,
Df_{\beta}
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}D\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=Df_{\beta}\left(\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right)D\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0}),\quad D\varphi_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\mathbf{I}.

Liouville’s theorem yields:

\left\lvert D\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\rvert=\left\lvert D\varphi_{0}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\rvert\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{tr}Df_{\beta}\left(\varphi_{\tau}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\right).

Analyticity of
f_{\beta}
makes
\left\lvert D\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\right\rvert
nonzero for all
\mathbf{x}_{0}
and all
t\geq 0
, hence the conditions meet with
V=U=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Now take
\mathcal{S}
as the set of strict saddle points. It is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
is (Fréchet) differentiable at 0 with

D\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(0)[\bm{\Delta}]=\beta^{-1/2}\bm{\Delta},

(33)

for all matrix
\bm{\Delta}
. It suffices to prove that

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(h\bm{\Delta})-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(0)-h\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\Delta}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}}{|h|}=0,

or

\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(h\bm{\Delta})}{h}-\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\Delta}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}=0.

Notice that, for
s(x)=x^{-\frac{1}{2}}
on
[\beta,\infty)
and
c>0
,

|s(x+c)-s(x)|=|s^{\prime}(x+rc)|c\leq\frac{c}{2\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}}

(34)

for some
r\in(0,1)
. Applying
Equation

34
to the eigenvalues of
\bm{\Delta}^{\top}\bm{\Delta}
yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(h\bm{\Delta})}{h}-\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\Delta}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}\left(h^{2}\bm{\Delta}^{\top}\bm{\Delta}+\beta\mathbf{I}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\Delta}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\lVert\left(h^{2}\bm{\Delta}^{\top}\bm{\Delta}+\beta\mathbf{I}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{I}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\bm{\Delta}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\frac{h^{2}\lVert\bm{\Delta}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{2\beta^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Equation

33
directly yields that

Df_{\beta}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})=-\beta^{-1/2}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})

(35)

and it is symmetric for all
\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{S}
(in fact, this holds at any critical point). As
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
is an equilibrium point,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}D\varphi_{t}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})=Df_{\beta}\left(\varphi_{t}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\right)D\varphi_{t}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})=Df_{\beta}\left(\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right)D\varphi_{t}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}).

Thus

D\varphi_{t}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})=e^{tDf_{\beta}\left(\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right)}.

Since
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
is a strict saddle, the Hessian of
\mathcal{L}
at
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
has a negative eigenvalue. By
Equation

35
,
Df_{\beta}\left(\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right)
has a positive eigenvalue, and hence
D\varphi_{t}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})
has an eigenvalue whose (absolute) value is strictly greater than 1 at any
t\geq 0
.

The remaining part, the extension from discrete to continuous, is simple. Fix any time
T>0
. By the semigroup property,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathbf{x}_{0}:\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\in\mathcal{S}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\mathbf{x}_{0}:\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi_{t}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{\mathbf{x}_{0}:\lim_{k\to\infty}\varphi_{kT}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{x}_{0}:\lim_{k\to\infty}(\varphi_{T})^{k}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\in\mathcal{S}\right\}.

The proof is concluded since the subset of a Lebesgue measure zero set is again of Lebesgue measure zero.
∎

D.4
Stability Analysis of SpecGF

Recall that, from
Section

D.1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{S}_{\beta}\mathbf{V}^{\top},\quad\mathbf{S}_{\beta}\triangleq\mathrm{Diag}\left\{\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}^{2}+\beta}}\right\}_{i\in[r]}.

Thus,

\left\langle\mathbf{X},\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{X})\right\rangle\geq\sqrt{\beta}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{X})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{X})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}.

(36)

Proposition D.14

(
(
9
)
, Theorem 5.2)
.

Consider the system of differential equations

\dot{\mathbf{x}}(t)=f(\mathbf{x}(t))

(37)

with
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
that yields a unique
\mathcal{C}^{1}
solution for every initial condition
\mathbf{x}(0)
. Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and suppose there exists a continuous function
V:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{R}
defined on an open neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
\mathbf{x}
, and
c>0
such that

c\dot{V}(\mathbf{z})+\lVert f(\mathbf{z})\rVert\leq 0

(38)

is satisfied for every
\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{V}
. If
\mathbf{x}
is a local minimizer of V, then
\mathbf{x}
is a Lyapunov stable equilibrium.

We now prove that every (global) minimum of the loss is Lyapunov stable.

Theorem D.15

(Lyapunov stability of minima)
.

Every global minimum of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is Lyapunov stable for SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
.

Proof.

Recall
Lemma

D.6
: at each global minima
\mathbf{x}_{*}
of
\mathcal{L}
, there exist a neighborhood
W
of
\mathbf{x}_{*}
, constants
C>0
and
b\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
such that for all
\mathbf{x}\in W
,

|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}_{*})|^{b}\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})\|.

Note that
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}_{*})\triangleq\mathcal{L}_{*}
for any global minima
\mathbf{x}_{*}
. Without loss of generalization, take
W
as an open ball centered at
\mathbf{x}_{*}
and of (finite) positive radius. On
W
, define

V(\mathbf{x})\triangleq\left(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})-\mathcal{L}_{*}\right)^{1-b}.

(39)

Then
V
is continuous, nonnegative, minimized at
\mathbf{x}_{*}
and

\dot{V}(\mathbf{x})=(1-b)\left(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})-\mathcal{L}_{*}\right)^{-b}\dot{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x}).

On
W
,
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}
and
\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}
are both bounded; take the bound as
C_{W}
so that

\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq C_{W},\quad\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq C_{W}.

Observe

\displaystyle-\dot{\mathcal{L}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L})\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L},\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L})}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}^{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L})+\beta}}+\sum_{j}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})}{\sqrt{\sigma_{j}^{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})+\beta}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L})}{\sqrt{C_{W}^{2}+\beta}}+\sum_{j}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{2}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L})}{\sqrt{C_{W}^{2}+\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{W}^{2}+\beta}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

In addition, for the same
f_{\beta}
in the proof of
Theorem

D.13
,

\left\lVert f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}=\sqrt{\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}))\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\beta^{-1}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right)}=\beta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}.

Hence, on
W
,

\displaystyle-\dot{V}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=-(1-b)\left(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})-\mathcal{L}_{*}\right)^{-b}\dot{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\geq(1-b)\left(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})-\mathcal{L}_{*}\right)^{-b}\frac{1}{\sqrt{C_{W}^{2}+\beta}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-b}{C\sqrt{C_{W}^{2}+\beta}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-b}{C\sqrt{C_{W}^{2}+\beta}}\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert f_{\beta}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}.

c=\frac{C\sqrt{C_{W}^{2}+\beta}}{(1-b)\sqrt{\beta}}
gives the desired inequality.
∎

Therefore, the a.s. local convergence of SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
towards global minima is guaranteed.

Conjecture: The basin of attraction is large.

For now, let
\rho_{W}
be the radius of
W
. Fix
\epsilon>0
and take
\rho=\min\{\rho_{W},\epsilon\}>0
. We will find
\delta>0
for the Lyapunov stability of each minimizer
\mathbf{x}_{*}=(\mathbf{A}_{*},\mathbf{B}_{*})
.

Let
\mathbf{z}=(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
and assume
\left\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_{*}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta
. We have

\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\triangleq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A}_{*}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta,\quad\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\triangleq\left\lVert\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}_{*}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\delta.

Then, for some constants
c^{\prime}>0
(may depend on
\mathbf{x}_{*}
)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}_{*}+\mathbf{A}_{*}\Delta\mathbf{B}+\Delta\mathbf{A}\Delta\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}_{*}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\lVert\mathbf{A}_{*}\Delta\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}+\left\lVert\Delta\mathbf{A}\Delta\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\left(\lVert\mathbf{A}_{*}\rVert_{2}+\lVert\mathbf{B}_{*}\rVert_{2}\right)+\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}\max\{\delta,\delta^{2}\}.

(e.g., take
c^{\prime}=\lVert\mathbf{A}_{*}\rVert_{2}+\lVert\mathbf{B}_{*}\rVert_{2}+1
.) Hence,

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime 2}\max\{\delta^{2},\delta^{4}\}\quad\Rightarrow\quad V(\mathbf{z})\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime 2}\max\{\delta^{2},\delta^{4}\}-\mathcal{L}_{*}\right)^{1-b}.

In the proof of
9
, two conditions are required:

\left\lVert\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{x}_{*}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}\leq\frac{\rho}{4},\quad|V(\mathbf{z})-V(\mathbf{x}_{*})|\leq\frac{\rho}{4c},

where
c=\frac{C\sqrt{C_{W}^{2}+\beta}}{(1-b)\sqrt{\beta}}
. It suffices that

\delta\leq\frac{\rho}{4},\quad\left(\frac{1}{2}c^{\prime 2}\max\{\delta^{2},\delta^{4}\}-\mathcal{L}_{*}\right)^{1-b}\leq\frac{\rho}{4c},

which translates to

\delta(\epsilon,\mathbf{x}_{*})=\min\left\{\frac{\rho}{4},\sqrt{\frac{2}{c^{\prime 2}}\left(\left(\frac{\rho}{4c}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-b}}+\mathcal{L}_{*}\right)},\sqrt[4]{\frac{2}{c^{\prime 2}}\left(\left(\frac{\rho}{4c}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-b}}+\mathcal{L}_{*}\right)}\right\},\quad\rho=\min\{\rho_{W},\epsilon\}.

(40)

We conjecture that
\rho_{W}
is sufficiently large for each and every minimizer
\mathbf{x}_{*}
. If then,
\delta
increases as
\epsilon
increases, and there is a possibility that initial points near (but not exactly) the origin fall in some basin of attraction. We empirically checked that the basin of attraction is sufficiently large.

D.5
Convergence Rate of SpecGF

Here, we study the convergence rate of SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
, assuming the convergence itself. This analysis closely follows that of
Proposition

D.7
; SpecGF iterate will get close to some critical point of
\mathcal{L}
after a finite time, where the Łojasiewicz gradient inequality holds. On the vicinity of that critical point, the rate of convergence is explicitly derived.

Let
(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))\to(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
. Then
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)),\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t))\to 0
, thus
\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t),\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)\to 0
. This implies that there exists some time
t^{\prime}>0
such that for all
t\geq t^{\prime}
,

\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\beta},\quad\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\beta}.

In addition, let
W
be the neighborhood of
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
where the Łojasiewicz gradient inequality hold: there also exist constants
C>0
and
b\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
such that, for all
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\in W
,

|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})|^{b}\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\|.

We know that there exists some time
t^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for all
t\geq t^{\prime\prime}
,
(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))\in W
. Take
T\triangleq\max\{t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\}>0
. We briefly state the helper lemma for deriving the convergence rate.

Lemma D.16
.

For all matrix
\mathbf{M}
such that
\lVert\mathbf{M}\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\beta}
,

\left\langle\mathbf{M},\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{M})\right\rangle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\beta}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

Proof.

\left\langle\mathbf{M},\mathcal{T}_{\beta}(\mathbf{M})\right\rangle=\sum_{i}\frac{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{M})^{2}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{M})^{2}+\beta}}\geq\sum_{i}\frac{\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{M})^{2}}{\sqrt{2\beta}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\beta}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

∎

Observe that, for
t\geq T
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t),-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t),-\mathcal{T}_{\beta}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\beta}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\beta}}\left\lVert\nabla\mathcal{L}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{C^{2}\sqrt{2\beta}}(\mathcal{L}(t)-\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}}))^{2b}.

Let
\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\triangleq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})-\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})\geq 0
; we use simplifed notation
\mathcal{E}(t)\triangleq\mathcal{L}(t)-\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})\geq 0
. Then for
t\geq T
,
\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq-C(\beta)\mathcal{E}(t)^{2b}
, where
C(\beta)=\frac{1}{C^{2}\sqrt{2\beta}}
. The convergence rate of
\mathcal{E}
differs by
b
. In our case, we established the almost sure convergence towards global minima (under assuming convergence), hence assume
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
is the global minimum. Then,
\mathcal{E}
is the Morse-Bott function, and thus the (optimal) exponent
b
is exactly
\frac{1}{2}

(
13
)
.
Then,

\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq-C(\beta)\mathcal{E}(t),

or

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}(T)e^{-C(\beta)(t-T)}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{E}(t)
exponentially decreases to 0. It remains to show that
\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is the Morse-Bott function.

Assumption D.17
.

The nonzero singular values of
\mathbf{Y}
are mutually distinct.

Recall that
\bar{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{B}}
is the best rank-
r
approximation of
\mathbf{Y}

(
6
)
. We denote
\bar{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\triangleq\bar{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{B}}
for simplicity. Furthermore, denote

\bar{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top},\quad\mathbf{Y}-\bar{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\triangleq\mathbf{U}_{\perp}\bm{\Sigma}_{\perp}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}^{\top}.

[\mathbf{U}_{r}|\mathbf{U}_{\perp}]
forms a
m\times m
orthogonal matrix; similarly,
[\mathbf{V}_{r}|\mathbf{V}_{\perp}]
forms a
n\times n
orthogonal matrix. Note that zero singular values may appear in
\bm{\Sigma}_{\perp}
, but never appear in
\bm{\Sigma}_{r}
.

The solution set

S\triangleq\left\{\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r},\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}:\mathbf{M}\mathbf{N}=\bar{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}\right\}

is easily identified with
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
:

S=\left\{(\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{B}}):\mathbf{Q}\in\mathsf{GL}(r)\right\}.

Indeed, for
(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})\in S
, if the left inverse of
\bar{\mathbf{A}}
is
\mathbf{L}_{\bar{\mathbf{A}}}
, then
\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{L}_{\bar{\mathbf{A}}}\mathbf{M}
gives
\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{Q}=\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{L}_{\bar{\mathbf{A}}}\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{L}_{\bar{\mathbf{A}}}\bar{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}=\bar{\mathbf{B}}
; definitely
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{Q})=r
from
\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{M}
.

The mapping

\Phi:\mathsf{GL}(r)\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times n},\quad\Phi(\mathbf{Q})=(\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{B}})

is real-analytic and satisfies

\Phi^{-1}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})=\mathbf{L}_{\bar{\mathbf{A}}}\mathbf{M}

for a left inverse
\mathbf{L}_{\bar{\mathbf{A}}}
of
\bar{\mathbf{A}}
. Thus
\Phi
is a real-analytic diffeomorphism from an open subset (hence an analytic submanifold)
\mathsf{GL}(r)
to
\Phi(\mathsf{GL}(r))=S
, and
S
is an analytic submanifold. As
\mathsf{GL}(r)
has two connected components—matrices with positive and negative determinants—so does
S
. This issue is resolved by shrinking the domain of
S
to an open neighborhood of
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
; then
S
is connected. It remains to show that
T_{(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})}S=\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})

(
13
, Definition 1.5)
; Other conditions for being a Morse-Bott function are automatically satisfied
(
13
, Remark 1.6)
;
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}:(\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times n})\to(\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times n})
is a linear map, thus a Fredholm operator index 0, hence the range condition is automatically satisfied.

Let
\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\triangleq\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\bar{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}
be the residual matrix. The differential of
\mathbf{R}
at
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
is

D\mathbf{R}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})[\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B}]=\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}+\Delta\mathbf{A}\bar{\mathbf{B}}.

It is straightforward to see that

\mathrm{ker}D\mathbf{R}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})=\left\{(\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{K},-\mathbf{K}\bar{\mathbf{B}}):\mathbf{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}\right\}.

Indeed, if
\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}+\Delta\mathbf{A}\bar{\mathbf{B}}=\bm{0}
, the right inverse of
\bar{\mathbf{B}}
,
\mathbf{R}_{\bar{\mathbf{B}}}
, gives
\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}\mathbf{R}_{\bar{\mathbf{B}}}+\Delta\mathbf{A}=\bm{0}
, which implies
\Delta\mathbf{A}=-\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}\mathbf{R}_{\bar{\mathbf{B}}}
and
\bar{\mathbf{A}}(\Delta\mathbf{B}-\Delta\mathbf{B}\mathbf{R}_{\bar{\mathbf{B}}}\bar{\mathbf{B}})=\bm{0}\Rightarrow\Delta\mathbf{B}=\Delta\mathbf{B}\mathbf{R}_{\bar{\mathbf{B}}}\bar{\mathbf{B}}
.

T_{(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})}S\subset\mathrm{ker}D\mathbf{R}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
is trivial; we show the opposite inclusion. For
(\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B})\in\mathrm{ker}D\mathbf{R}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
, we have
(\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B})=(\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{K},-\mathbf{K}\bar{\mathbf{B}})
for some
\mathbf{K}
. Define a smooth curve

\gamma(t)\triangleq(\bar{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{I}_{r}+t\mathbf{K}),(\mathbf{I}_{r}+t\mathbf{K})^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{B}}).

For all
t
sufficiently close to 0,
\mathbf{I}_{r}+t\mathbf{K}
is invertible and
\gamma(t)\in S
. Moreover,

\gamma^{\prime}(0)=(\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{K},-\mathbf{K}\bar{\mathbf{B}}).

Hence, every
(\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B})\in\mathrm{ker}D\mathbf{R}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
is realized as the velocity of a curve in
S
, or
(\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B})\in T_{(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})}S
. We obtain

T_{(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})}S=\mathrm{ker}D\mathbf{R}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}}).

The Hessian of
\mathcal{E}
at
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
is computed:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})[(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0}),(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{1},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{1})]

\displaystyle=\left\langle\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0}\bar{\mathbf{B}}+\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0},\Delta\mathbf{A}_{1}\bar{\mathbf{B}}+\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}_{1}\right\rangle+\left\langle\bar{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{B}}-\mathbf{Y},\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0}\Delta\mathbf{B}_{1}+\Delta\mathbf{A}_{1}\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0}\right\rangle.

Without loss of generality, take
\bar{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}
and
\bar{\mathbf{B}}=\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}
. Note that any other
(\hat{\mathbf{A}},\hat{\mathbf{B}})\in S
must satisfy

(\hat{\mathbf{A}},\hat{\mathbf{B}})=(\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{B}})\triangleq\phi_{\mathbf{Q}}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})

for
\mathbf{Q}\in\mathsf{GL}(r)
. From
\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}+\Delta\mathbf{A}\bar{\mathbf{B}}=\bm{0}
, we can check that

(\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B})\in T_{(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})}S\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\phi_{\mathbf{Q}}(\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B})\in T_{\phi_{\mathbf{Q}}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})}S.

This implies

\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})[(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0}),(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{1},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{1})]=\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\phi_{\mathbf{Q}}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}}))[\phi_{\mathbf{Q}}(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0}),\phi_{\mathbf{Q}}(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{1},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{1})],

and thus

(\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B})\in\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\phi_{\mathbf{Q}}(\Delta\mathbf{A},\Delta\mathbf{B})\in\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\hat{\mathbf{A}},\hat{\mathbf{B}}).

Hence, it suffices to show
\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})=T_{(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})}S
at a single point
(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})\in S
to show that
\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})=T_{(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})}S
for any
(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})\in S
. Moreover, as the critical set
S
is an analytic manifold of the analytic
\mathcal{L}
, we have
T_{(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})}S\subset\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})

(
13
, Remark 2.9)
, hence what remains is to show
\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})\subset T_{(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})}S
.

Now return to the specific point
\bar{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}
and
\bar{\mathbf{B}}=\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}
. If

(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0})\in\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}}),

then for all
(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{1},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{1})
,
\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})[(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0}),(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{1},\Delta\mathbf{B}_{1})]=\bm{0}
. Specifically,

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{A}_{1}=\bm{0}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\bar{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0}\bar{\mathbf{B}}+\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0})+\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0}^{\top}(\bar{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{B}}-\mathbf{Y})=\bm{0},

(41)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{B}_{1}=\bm{0}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0}\bar{\mathbf{B}}+\bar{\mathbf{A}}\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0})\bar{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}+(\bar{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{B}}-\mathbf{Y})\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0}^{\top}=\bm{0}.

(42)

There exists
\mathbf{C}_{r},\mathbf{D}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
,
\mathbf{C}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m-r)\times r}
, and
\mathbf{D}_{\perp}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times(n-r)}
such that

\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0}=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{C}_{r}+\mathbf{U}_{\perp}\mathbf{C}_{\perp},\quad\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0}=\mathbf{D}_{r}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}+\mathbf{D}_{\perp}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}^{\top}.

Plugging into
Equations

41
and
42
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}(\mathbf{C}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}+\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{D}_{r})\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}+\bm{\Sigma}_{r}\mathbf{D}_{\perp}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}^{\top}-\mathbf{C}_{\perp}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\perp}\mathbf{V}_{\perp}^{\top}=\bm{0},

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{r}(\mathbf{C}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}+\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{D}_{r})\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}+\mathbf{U}_{\perp}\mathbf{C}_{\perp}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}-\mathbf{U}_{\perp}\bm{\Sigma}_{\perp}\mathbf{D}_{\perp}^{\top}=\bm{0},

which reduces to

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}+\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{D}_{r}

\displaystyle=\bm{0},

(43)

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}_{r}\mathbf{D}_{\perp}-\mathbf{C}_{\perp}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\bm{0},

(44)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\perp}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}-\bm{\Sigma}_{\perp}\mathbf{D}_{\perp}^{\top}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}.

(45)

Since no singular values of
\bm{\Sigma}_{r}
equals with that of
\bm{\Sigma}_{\perp}
,
Equations

44
and
45
implies that
\mathbf{C}_{\perp}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{2}=\bm{\Sigma}_{\perp}^{2}\mathbf{C}_{\perp}
thus
\mathbf{C}_{\perp}=\bm{0}
;
\mathbf{D}_{\perp}=\bm{0}
also holds. Moreover, take
\mathbf{K}=\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{-1/2}\mathbf{C}_{r}
; we see that

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{K}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{r}\mathbf{C}_{r}=\Delta\mathbf{A}_{0},

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}\bar{\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{-1/2}\mathbf{C}_{r}\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}=\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{-1/2}(-\bm{\Sigma}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{D}_{r})\mathbf{V}_{r}^{\top}=-\Delta\mathbf{B}_{0}.

Therefore,
\mathrm{ker}\nabla^{2}\mathcal{E}(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})
is a subset of
T_{(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{B}})}S
. This argument analogously holds at any zero of
\mathbf{R}
, or any
(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{N})\in S
, so
\mathcal{E}
is Morse-Bott.

Finite-time convergence of
\mathcal{T}
.

We further show that if SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
is well-defined—the gradients of
\mathcal{L}
with respect to both
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are never rank-deficient—and converges, then the convergence should happen at a finite time.

Proposition D.18
.

Assume that SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
is analytic for all time
t\geq 0
. If SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}
converges, then it converges finitely to a critical point.

Proof.

Let

\Delta(t)\triangleq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(\infty),\mathbf{B}(\infty))\triangleq\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))-\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\geq 0.

For each (open) neighborhood
U
of
(\mathbf{A}(\infty),\mathbf{B}(\infty))
, there exists a time
t_{U}\geq 0
such that for all
t\geq t_{U}
,
(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))\in U
. Apply
Lemma

D.6
: there are a neighborhood
W
of
(\mathbf{A}(\infty),\mathbf{B}(\infty))
, constants
C>0
and
b\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
such that for all
x\in W
,

|\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))-\mathcal{L}_{\infty}|^{b}\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))\|.

Observe that

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))=-\left(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}\|_{*}+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}\|_{*}\right)\leq-\left(\|\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}\|_{\mathrm{F}}+\|\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}\|_{\mathrm{F}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\|\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))\|_{\mathrm{F}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{C}\left(\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{A}(t),\mathbf{B}(t))-\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\right)^{b}=-\frac{1}{C}\Delta(t)^{b}.

At any time
t\geq t_{W}
, if
\Delta(t)=0
, done. Otherwise,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Delta(t)^{1-b}=(1-b)\Delta(t)^{-b}\Delta^{\prime}(t)\leq-\frac{1-b}{C}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\Delta(t)^{1-b}-\Delta(t_{W})^{1-b}\leq-\frac{1-b}{C}(t-t_{W}).

Taking
T=t_{W}+\frac{C}{1-b}\Delta(t_{W})^{1-b}
gives
\Delta(T)\leq 0
, hence
\Delta(T)=0
.
∎

D.6
SpecGF with
\ell_{2}
Regularization

With such regularization, the global convergence of SpecGF immediately follows. The setup of regularized SpecGF becomes

•

The target matrix
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
yields a compact SVD
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{U}\bm{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\top}
where
\bm{\Sigma}=\mathrm{Diag}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\cdots,\sigma_{r^{\star}})
with
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{r^{\star}}>0
.

•

The loss function
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
for a fixed
\lambda>0
is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

(46)

We may abuse the notation and simply state
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(t)
.
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is also analytic in
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
.

•

The time derivative of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
is governed by either
\mathcal{T}
or
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
applied to the gradient:

\dot{\mathbf{A}}(t)=-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{A}}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(t)\right),\quad\dot{\mathbf{B}}(t)=-\mathcal{T}\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{B}}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(t)\right).

The following statements are true, because the corresponding statements do not utilize the structure of the loss; only the analyticity of
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
and monotonicity of the loss matter, if any of those two is required.

1.

Lemmas

D.4
and
D.5
readily follows for the regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
. Moreover,

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{B}(t)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(t)\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(0)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{A}(0)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{B}(0)\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}.

Thus,
\mathbf{A}(t)
and
\mathbf{B}(t)
are both bounded for all
t\geq 0
.

2.

Equation

30
in
Proposition

D.7
also holds for
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
(with the same constant), implying that regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
should either converge or diverge to infinity. However, the latter cannot happen; hence, regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
always converges.

3.

Proposition

D.12
is applicable for the regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
. That is, regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
avoids strict saddle points almost surely as SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
does.

4.

Theorem

D.15
readily follows for the regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
. That is, every global minimum of regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
is Lyapunov stable.

It remains to characterize the critical points of regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
, especially when LoRA rank
r
is smaller than the target rank
r^{\star}
. Although it is specified in
35
, we reproduce their result.

Proposition D.19

(
(
35
)
)
.

Set
r\leq r^{\star}
. Assume that all singular values of
\mathbf{Y}
are distinct and
\lambda
is strictly smaller than the smallest singular value of
\mathbf{Y}
.

(i)

Let
\Omega\subset[r^{\star}]
with
|\Omega|\leq r
. Every stationary point of
Equation

46
is of the form

\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{U}_{\Omega}\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\Omega}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\mathbf{I}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\mathbf{B}=\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\Omega}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\mathbf{I}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{V}_{\Omega}^{\top}.

up to orthogonal transformation by
\mathbf{R}\in\mathsf{O}(r)
. We denote the submatrix of
\mathbf{U}
with columns indexed by
\Omega
as
\mathbf{U}_{\Omega}
, and similarly for
\bm{\Sigma}
and
\mathbf{V}
.

(ii)

Every critical point except global minima has a descent direction.

Item

D.19.(ii)
implies that every local minimum is a
balanced
global minimum, and every saddle point is a strict saddle point. Therefore, the a.s. global convergence of regularized SpecGF with
\mathcal{T}_{\beta}
towards global minima is guaranteed.

Proof of
Proposition

D.19
.

We omit the proof of
Item

D.19.(i)
; readers may find the proof in
35
. Though, we note that at every critical point,
\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{B}\mathbf{B}^{\top}
should hold from the first-order optimality condition.

Every critical point, indexed by
\Omega
, should be of the form

\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}=\sum_{i\in\Omega}d_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top},

where
d_{i}=\sigma_{i}-\lambda>0
,
\mathbf{u}_{i}
and
\mathbf{v}_{i}
are the
i
-th column of
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{V}
, respectively. We use the representative element from each orbit described above, so each column of
\mathbf{A}
is
\mathbf{u}_{i}\sqrt{d_{i}}
and each row of
\mathbf{B}
is
\sqrt{d_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}
.

If a critical point is not the global minimum, then
\sigma_{j}>\sigma_{i}
for some
i\in\Omega
and
j\not\in\Omega
. With small
\epsilon>0
, form
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}
by replacing the column
\mathbf{u}_{i}\sqrt{d_{i}}
by
(\mathbf{u}_{i}+\epsilon\mathbf{u}_{j})\sqrt{d_{i}}
; form
\tilde{\mathbf{B}}
by replacing the column
\sqrt{d_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i}^{\top}
by
\sqrt{d_{i}}(\mathbf{v}_{i}+\epsilon\mathbf{v}_{j})^{\top}
. One can directly compute

\lambda\left[\left(\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\|\tilde{\mathbf{B}}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right)-\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{B}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right)\right]=2\lambda d_{i}\epsilon^{2}

and

\left\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\mathbf{B}}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}-\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=2\epsilon^{2}d_{i}(d_{i}-\sigma_{j})+\epsilon^{4}d_{i}^{2}.

Hence, the net change in the objective is

2\lambda d_{i}\epsilon^{2}+2\epsilon^{2}d_{i}(d_{i}-\sigma_{j})+\epsilon^{4}d_{i}^{2}=2\epsilon^{2}d_{i}(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j})+\epsilon^{4}d_{i}^{2},

which can be made (strictly) negative for small
\epsilon
.
∎

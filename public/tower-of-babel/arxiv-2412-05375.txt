On the Essential Dimension of Symplectic Vector Bundles over Curves
Ajneet Dhillon, Sayantan Roy Chowdhury

Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth geometrically connected projective curve of genus at least 2 over a field of characteristic zero. We compute the essential dimension of the moduli stack of symplectic bundles over X X . Unlike the case of vector bundles, we are able to precisely compute the essential dimension as the generic gerbe of the moduli stack has period 2 over it’s moduli space.

Let

at least 2.
In this paper we study the essential dimension of the moduli stack of principal bundles over the symplectic group

.
The main result of this paper is the calculation of the essential dimension, see

and

A lower bound for the essential dimension can be obtained by combining

with
[

, 5.4]
, see the proof
of

for further details. The main content of this article is to prove the reverse inequality. For this we

At this point we are reduced to studying the essential dimension of the residual gerbe of a symplectic bundle over it’s field of moduli.
To understand this number, we associate to a symplectic bundle an algebra with involution in

the essential dimension of this residual gerbe can be computed studying the essential dimension of Hermitian modules over this algebra.
Using elementary means we can bound the essential dimension of the functor of Hermitian modules.
An unpublished result of Danny Ofek and Zinovy Reichstein along with

A broad overview of essential dimension can be found in

Given an algebraic stack

over a field

is a measure of the number of parameters

[

]
.
From another point of view there are two ways to define the dimension of a variety. The first is the Krull dimension and the second via the transcendence
degree of it’s function field. The function field approach when generalised to algebraic stacks yields the notion of essential dimension, see
[
4
]
.
To make this precise consider a functor

F:\text{Fields}_{k}\to\text{Sets}

where

is the category of field extensions of

The

. The

\ed_{k}F=\sup\ed_{k}(x)

where the supremum is taken over all

.
If

is an algebraic stack over a field

then the
essential dimension of
\mathfrak{X}

, denote
\ed_{k}\mathfrak{X}
is defined to
be the essential dimension of the associated functor of isomorphism classes of objects over fields.

The main result of this paper is a computation of

Let

The coarse moduli space of

has dimension
(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r})
. The term

is the extra contribution to the essential

\Bun_{X,{\rm Sp}_{2r}}
over it’s coarse moduli space. This is a

-gerbe. As the main conjecture in

[
6
]
is known for

-gerbes we are able to compute the essential dimension, in contrast to the vector bundle case in

]

The paper begins in section 2 where we introduce the notions of filtered and graded symplectic vector bundles. This notion arises naturally
later in the paper as a symplectic vector bundle with a nilpotent self-adjoint endomorphism has a natural filtration. In section 3

and the theory of rings with involution.

An underlying assumption in this paper is that

It is also applied in

2.
Filtered symplectic vector bundles

2.1.
Graded vector bundles.

Let
S
be a scheme.
A graded vector bundle
on
S
is a locally free sheaf
{\mathscr{E}}
of finite rank on
S
together with a direct
sum decomposition

{\mathscr{E}}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}.

Necessarily each of the
{\mathscr{E}}_{i}
are locally free sheaves of finite rank and if
S
is quasi-compact, almost all of them are zero.

A morphism of graded vector bundles is defined in the obvious way. We denote by
{\rm Gr}(S)
the category of graded vector bundles on
S
.

The dual of a graded vector bundle is graded in the following way:

({\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee})_{i}\quad\text{where}\quad({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee})_{i}=({\mathscr{E}}_{-i})^{\vee}.

If
{\mathscr{E}}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}
is a graded vector bundle and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
then we can form a new graded vector bundle

{\mathscr{E}}[n]
with shifted grading given by

({\mathscr{E}}[n])_{i}={\mathscr{E}}_{i+n}.

If
{\mathscr{E}}=\bigoplus_{i}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}
and
{\mathscr{F}}=\bigoplus_{i}{\mathscr{F}}_{i}
are graded vector bundles then a morphism of vector bundles

\theta:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{F}}
is said to be graded of degree
a
, where
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, if
\theta=\bigoplus\theta_{i}
where

\theta_{i}:{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to{\mathscr{F}}_{i+a}.

2.2.
Filtered vector bundles.

Let
S
be a scheme.
The category of vector bundles on
S
has the structure of an exact category, the admissible monomorphisms are monomorphisms whose cokernel
is a vector bundle.

A
filtration

F
on a vector bundle
{\mathscr{E}}
consists of a sequence of admissible subobjects

F^{n}({\mathscr{E}})\hookrightarrow F^{n+1}({\mathscr{E}})\hookrightarrow\ldots\hookrightarrow F^{m-1}({\mathscr{E}})\hookrightarrow F^{m}({\mathscr{E}})={\mathscr{E}}

where
n<m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The fact that the inclusions are admissible means that

\Gr_{F}^{i}({\mathscr{E}}):=F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})/F^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})

is locally free.
If
F^{n}({\mathscr{E}})\neq 0
and
F^{m-1}({\mathscr{E}})\neq{\mathscr{E}}
we will say that the
amplitude
of the filtration is
[n,m]
. We will sometimes
denote this data by
({\mathscr{E}},F)
. It will be convenient to extend the filtration outside the range
[n,m]
. This is done by setting

F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})=\begin{cases}{\mathscr{E}}&i>m\\
0&i<n.\end{cases}

There is an obvious notion of a morphism of filtered vector bundles. We denote by
\Fil(S)
the category of filtered vector bundles on
S
.

If
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
then there
is a shifting by
n
functor
\Fil({\bf C})\to\Fil({\bf C})
that shifts filtrations by
n
. We denote the shifted filtration by

({\mathscr{E}},F[n])
, and we have

F^{i}[n]({\mathscr{E}})=F^{i+n}({\mathscr{E}}).

If
({\mathscr{E}},F)
is a filtered object then its dual is also filtered with filtration
({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee},F^{\vee})
. We define

F^{\vee,-i}({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee})=\ker({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}\to F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}).

The minus sign is needed to ensure that the filtration is increasing.

If
({\mathscr{E}},F)
is a filtered object we let

\Gr_{F}^{i}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})/F^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})\quad\text{and}\quad\Gr_{F}({\mathscr{E}})=\bigoplus_{i}\Gr_{F}^{i}({\mathscr{E}}).

We will call
\Gr_{F}({\mathscr{E}})
the associated graded vector bundle of
({\mathscr{E}},F)
.

There is an obvious functor

\Gr:\Fil(S)\to{\rm Gr}(S).

This functor is not compatible with taking duals. Instead, we have a canonical isomorphism

(2.1)

\left(\Gr({\mathscr{E}},F)\right)^{\vee}[-1]\cong\Gr(({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee},F^{\vee})).

We can equip
\Gr_{F}({\mathscr{E}})
with a filtration defined by

F^{i}(\Gr_{F}({\mathscr{E}}))=\bigoplus_{j\leq i}\Gr_{F}^{i}({\mathscr{E}}).

We say that a filtered object
({\mathscr{E}},F)
is
split filtered
if
there is an isomorphism of filtered objects

\phi:({\mathscr{E}},F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Gr_{F}({\mathscr{E}})

satisfying
\phi_{j}(x)=\bar{x}
for
j\geq i

where
x\in F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})
and
\phi_{j}
is the composition

({\mathscr{E}},F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Gr_{F}({\mathscr{E}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle proj}}{{\to}}\Gr_{F}^{j}({\mathscr{E}}).

A choice of such an isomorphism
{\mathscr{E}}\to\Gr_{F}({\mathscr{E}})
will be called a
splitting
.

Example 2.2.2
.

If
A
is a ring then consider
{\bf C}=
the category of finitely generated projective
A
-modules. In this category every
filtered object is split filtered.

Symplectic vector bundles.

Let
S
be a scheme.
A
symplectic vector bundle
is a vector bundle
{\mathscr{E}}
on
S
together with an isomorphism

h:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}

such that the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee\vee}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev}

Q:{\mathscr{E}}\otimes{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{O}}_{S}

that is non-degenerate and alternating. We will summarize this data by saying that

({\mathscr{E}},h)

is a symplectic vector bundle.

If
{\mathscr{W}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}
is a subsheaf, not necessarily admissible, we define

{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}:=\ker({\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}\to{\mathscr{W}}^{\vee}).

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Suppose that the inclusion
{\mathscr{W}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}
is split. Then the inclusion

{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}

is split.

Proof.

We have an exact sequence

0\to{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}\to{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{W}}^{\vee}\to 0.

As
{\mathscr{W}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}
is split so is the map
{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathscr{W}}^{\vee}
.
∎

It follows that if
{\mathscr{W}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}
is admissible then so is
{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}
.

A morphism of symplectic vector bundles
f:({\mathscr{E}},h)\to({\mathscr{F}},g)
is a morphism of vector bundles
f:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{F}}
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}.}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\vee}}

a morphism is always a monomorphism of vector bundles. Hence, if

is morphism
and
{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{F}}
is admissible and
\rank({\mathscr{E}})=\rank({\mathscr{F}})
then it is necessarily an isomorphism.

Suppose that
({\mathscr{E}},h)
is a symplectic vector bundle
If
f:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{E}}
is an endomorphism of the underlying vector bundle
{\mathscr{E}}
, not necessarily a symplectic endomorphism, then we define

\mu(f)=h^{-1}\circ f^{\vee}\circ h.

We say that
f
is
self-adjoint
if
\mu(f)=-f
.

Suppose that
({\mathscr{E}},h)
is a symplectic vector bundle and
{\mathscr{E}}=\bigoplus{\mathscr{E}}_{i}
is graded. If the
morphism

h:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}

is a morphism of graded vector bundles then we will say that
({\mathscr{E}}=\oplus{\mathscr{E}}_{i},h)
is a
graded symplectic vector bundle
.
A graded endomorphism
\theta:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{E}}
is said to be self adjoint if
\mu(\theta)=-\theta
.

Lemma 2.3.2
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}}=\bigoplus{\mathscr{E}}_{i},h)
be a graded symplectic vector bundle. Then

h
decomposes as
h=\oplus_{i}h_{i}
where

h_{i}:{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{-i}^{\vee}

and
h_{i}^{\vee}=-h_{-i}
.
In this setting a graded morphism of degree
a
, say
\theta:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{E}}
is self adjoint if it decomposes as

\theta=\bigoplus_{i}\theta_{i}\quad\text{where}\quad\theta_{i}:{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{i+a}

and

-\theta_{i}=h_{i+a}^{-1}\circ\theta_{-i-a}^{\vee}\circ h_{i}.

Proof.

The decomposition follows directly from the definition of the induced grading on
{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}
. The second assertion
follows from the diagram defining the notion of symplectic vector bundle.
∎

Suppose that
({\mathscr{E}},h)
is a symplectic vector bundle and
({\mathscr{E}},F)
is a filtration.
Recall that
{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}
inherits a filtration from
{\mathscr{E}}
. We say that
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
is a

filtered symplectic vector bundle
if

h:({\mathscr{E}},F)\to({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee},F^{\vee}[1])

is a morphism of filtered objects. Note that is necessarily an isomorphism.

We will later produce examples of filtered
symplectic objects obtained from symplectic sheaves equipped with nilpotent
self adjoint endomorphisms. The presence of the 1 in the definition is justified
by appealing to these examples, also see Lemma
2.3.4
below.
To understand what this condition is asserting in terms of
{\mathscr{E}}
, we have

F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}=h^{-1}(F^{\vee,-i}({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee})).

Then the condition becomes

F^{-1-i}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}\quad\text{or}\quad F^{j}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{-j-1}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}.

Lemma 2.3.3
.

Consider a collection of vector bundles
{\mathscr{E}}_{i}
with
-n\leq i\leq n
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{>0}
.
Suppose that we have isomorphisms
h_{i}:{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{-i}^{\vee}
for
-n\leq i\leq n
.

If
h_{i}=-h_{-i}^{\vee}
then

\bigoplus_{i=-n}^{n}h_{i}:\bigoplus_{i=-n}^{n}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to\bigoplus_{i=-n}^{n}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}^{\vee}

is a filtered symplectic object with filtration given by

F^{k}(\bigoplus_{i}{\mathscr{E}}_{i})=\bigoplus_{i\leq k}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}
. Furthermore, this filtered object is split.

Finally, consider morphisms

\theta_{i,j}:{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{j}.

Then
\theta=\bigoplus_{i,j}\theta_{i,j}
is self adjoint if and only if

\theta_{i,j}=-h_{j}^{-1}\theta_{-j,-i}^{\vee}h_{i}.

Proof.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}=\bigoplus{\mathscr{E}}_{i}
and
h=\bigoplus h_{i}
. The given map is clearly an isomorphism.
It is easy to check that the morphism satisfies the required property to induce a symplectic object.
The filtration is clear split, so it just remains to check that
h
preserves filtrations.
We compute to see that

F^{\vee,-j}({\mathscr{E}}^{\vee})=\bigoplus_{i=j+1}^{n}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}^{\vee}\quad\text{and}\quad F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}=\bigoplus_{i=-n}^{-1-j}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}.

It follows that the object is filtered symplectic. It is straightforward to see that the filtration is split.

The assertion about self adjointness of
\theta
follows readily from a computation.
∎

A filtered symplectic vector bundle isomorphic to one constructed in the proposition is called a
split filtered symplectic vector bundle.

2.4.
The local structure of filtered symplectic vector bundles.

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Suppose that we have a split inclusion

{\mathscr{W}}\oplus{\mathscr{V}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}

in the category
{\bf C}
.
Then there exists a splitting of the map

{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathscr{W}}^{\vee}

such that every element in the image of this splitting vanishes on

Proof.

This is elementary.

If
({\mathscr{E}},h)
is a symplectic vector bundle then a subsheaf
{\mathscr{W}}\subseteq{\mathscr{E}}
is said to be

if
{\mathscr{W}}\subseteq{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}

.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},Q)
be a symplectic vector bundle on a scheme

. Consider an isotropic locally free subsheaf

0\subset{\mathscr{W}}\subset{\mathscr{E}}

such that both the inclusions
{\mathscr{W}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}
and
{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}
are

forms a filtered symplectic vector bundle
({\mathscr{E}},Q,F)
.

Suppose now that
S
is affine. If a splitting of the filtration on
{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}
is chosen then

{\mathscr{E}}
extending the splitting of the filtration on
{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}
such
that the map

We need to show that we have a diagram

({\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp})^{\vee}
is denoted
\{f_{1}^{\vee},f_{2}^{\vee}\ldots f_{s}^{\vee}\}
.

We define elements
e_{i}
of
{\mathscr{W}}
as

e_{i}=h^{-1}(f_{i}^{\vee})

for
1\leq i\leq s
. Note that
e_{i}\in{\mathscr{W}}
as
{\mathscr{E}}
is filtered symplectic by the previous Lemma. Let
Q
be the bilinear
form associated to
h
.
We now define a map
{\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}
by sending elements
f_{i}
to
\overline{f}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s

with
\overline{f}_{i}
defined as

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{1}

\displaystyle=f_{1}

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{2}

\displaystyle=f_{2}-Q(f_{2},f_{1})e_{1}

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{3}

\displaystyle=f_{3}-Q(f_{3},f_{1})e_{1}-Q(f_{3},f_{2})e_{2}

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{i}

\displaystyle=f_{i}-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}Q(f_{3},f_{k})e_{k}

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{s}

\displaystyle=f_{s}-\sum_{k=1}^{s-1}Q(f_{3},f_{s})e_{s}.

Since for all
i
, the
e_{i}
’s lie in
{\mathscr{W}}
,
Q(\overline{f}_{i},x)=Q(f_{i},x)
for all elements
x
in
{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}
,
1\leq i\leq s
.
the map

{\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow({\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}/{\mathscr{W}})^{\vee}

is still 0. Moreover, it follows from computation
Q(\overline{f}_{i},\overline{f}_{j})=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq s
. Hence,
the map

{\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow({\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp})^{\vee}

also vanishes.
∎

Lemma 2.4.4
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},h)
be a symplectic vector bundle on a scheme
S
. Consider an admissible isotropic
subbundle
{\mathscr{W}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}
with induced filtered, symplectic vector bundle

0\subset{\mathscr{W}}=F^{-1}({\mathscr{E}})\subset{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}=F^{0}({\mathscr{E}})\subset{\mathscr{E}}=F^{1}({\mathscr{E}}),

see
2.4.2
.
If
{\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}
is free and the filtration on
{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}
is split,
then
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
is a split, filtered, symplectic vector bundle.

Recall that the term “split, filtered, symplectic vector bundle ” was defined in the discussion immediately following
Lemma
2.3.3
.

Proof.

We choose a splitting of the filtration
E
as in Lemma
2.4.3
.
The maps

\displaystyle{\mathscr{W}}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow{\mathscr{W}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle{\mathscr{W}}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow({\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}/{\mathscr{W}})^{\vee}

\displaystyle{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}/{\mathscr{W}}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow{\mathscr{W}}^{\vee}

vanish since
{\mathscr{E}}
is a filtered symplectic object. Lemma
2.4.3
shows
that the maps

\displaystyle{\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow({\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp})^{\vee}

\displaystyle{\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow({\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}/{\mathscr{W}})^{\vee}

vanish as well for a particular splitting. The map

{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp}/{\mathscr{W}}\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\xrightarrow{h_{Q}}E^{\vee}\twoheadrightarrow({\mathscr{E}}/{\mathscr{W}}^{\perp})^{\vee}

vanishes since it is dual of the last map. It is easily checked that this splitting of the filtration gives
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
the structure of a filtered split,
symplectic vector bundle.
∎

Lemma 2.4.5
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},h)
be a filtered symplectic vector bundle equipped with filtration

0=F^{-n}({\mathscr{E}})\subset F^{-n+1}({\mathscr{E}})\subset\ldots\subset F^{n-1}({\mathscr{E}})={\mathscr{E}}

such that
{\mathscr{E}}/F^{n-2}(E)
is free and
n\geq 2
.
Then the following statements hold.

(1)

The vector bundle
F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})
is isotropic with
F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}=F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})
.

(2)

There are induced isomorphisms

h_{1-n}:F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})\to({\mathscr{E}}/F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}}))^{\vee}\quad\text{and}\quad h_{n-1}:{\mathscr{E}}/F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})\to F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}.

(3)

There is an induced isomorphism

\bar{h}:F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})/F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})\to(F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})/F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}}))^{\vee}

that makes it into a filtered symplectic vector bundle where the filtration is induced from the filtration
on
F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})
.

(4)

There is an isomorphism of filtered symplectic vector bundles

({\mathscr{E}},F,h)\cong(F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})\oplus F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})/F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}}))\oplus({\mathscr{E}}/F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})),F^{\prime},h_{1-n}\oplus\bar{h}\oplus h_{n-1}

where the filtration
F^{\prime}
has bottom term
F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})
, middle terms induced from the filtration on
F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})
.

Proof.

The first part follows from the definition of a filtered symplectic vector bundle and the fact that
n\geq 2
.
The second and third part follow from Lemma
2.4.4
. The fact that
h_{1-n}\oplus\bar{h}\oplus h_{n-1}

induces a symplectic structure follows from Lemma
2.3.3
, one readily checks the required property.
One needs to check that
h_{1-n}\oplus\bar{h}\oplus h_{n-1}
induces a structure of a filtered symplectic vector bundle.
This amounts to showing that
\bar{h}
induces a filtered symplectic structure on
F^{n-2}({\mathscr{E}})/F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})
.
This easily follows from the fact that
h
induces a filtered symplectic structure on
{\mathscr{E}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4.6
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},h)
be a filtered symplectic vector bundle with filtration
F
defined as

0=F^{-n}({\mathscr{E}})\subset F^{-n+1}({\mathscr{E}})\subset\ldots\subset F^{n-1}({\mathscr{E}})={\mathscr{E}}

If the sheaves
\operatorname{gr_{F}^{i}}({\mathscr{E}})
are free for all
-n+1\leq i\leq n-1
, then
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
is a split, filtered, symplectic
vector bundle.

Proof.

This follows by induction using the previous Lemma.
∎

3.
The sheaf of self adjoint homomorphisms

3.1.
Graded vector bundles.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}=\bigoplus_{i}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}
be a graded vector bundle. We define

{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})

to be the subsheaf of homomorphisms that preserve the grading. More generally, if
a
is an integer, we define

{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})

to be the subsheaf of degree
a
homomorphisms. A homomorphism
\phi
has degree
a
if the restricted maps satisfy
\phi:{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{i+a}

for every
i
.
Now suppose that
{\mathscr{E}}
is a graded symplectic vector bundle. So it comes equipped with an isomorphism
h:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}
. Recall that a
homomorphism
f
is self-adjoint if
\mu(f)=-f
, where
\mu(f)=h^{-1}\circ f^{\vee}\circ h
. By our degree conventions for the dual, if
f
has
degree
a
then so does
f^{\vee}
. We define

{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})\subseteq{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})

to be the subsheaf of self-adjoint homomorphisms.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

Consider a graded symplectic vector bundle
({\mathscr{E}},h)
on a scheme
S
. Recall that
{\mathscr{E}}
has an explicit description as

{\mathscr{E}}=\bigoplus_{i=-n}^{n}{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\quad\text{with }h\text{ given by}\quad h_{i}:{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}{\mathscr{E}}_{-i}^{\vee}.

Then to give a section of
{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})
is the same as giving maps

\theta_{i}:{\mathscr{E}}_{i}\to{\mathscr{E}}_{i+a}\quad-n\leq i\leq n

with
\theta_{-i-a,-i}=-h_{-i}^{-1}\theta^{\vee}_{i,i+a}h_{-i-a}
. In the above, we set
{\mathscr{E}}_{i}=0
if
i\not\in[-n,n]
.

Proof.

Follows from
2.3.2
.
∎

Corollary 3.1.2
.

In the above situation, let
a
be an odd integer. Then we have an isomorphisms

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i>-a/2}{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathscr{E}}_{i},{\mathscr{E}}_{i+a})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i<-a/2}{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathscr{E}}_{i},{\mathscr{E}}_{i+a}).

Definition 3.1.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}
be vector bundles on a scheme
S
. Let

h:{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}

be an isomorphism. We define
{\mathcal{H}om}_{s,h}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{F}})
, the sheaf of self adjoint homomorphisms to be
the subsheaf
of
\homsheaf({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{F}})
consisting of local sections
\theta
such that
the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{E}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{F}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\theta^{\vee}}

Corollary 3.1.4
.

In the above situation, let
a=2m
be an even integer. Then we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg 2m}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}om}_{s,h_{-m}}({\mathscr{E}}_{-m},{\mathscr{E}}_{m})\oplus\bigoplus_{i>m}{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathscr{E}}_{i},{\mathscr{E}}_{i+2m})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}om}_{s,h_{-m}}({\mathscr{E}}_{-m},{\mathscr{E}}_{m})\oplus\bigoplus_{i<m}{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathscr{E}}_{i},{\mathscr{E}}_{i+2m}).

3.2.
Filtered vector bundles.

Example 3.2.1
.

In the special case that
{\mathscr{E}}={\mathscr{F}}
in the above and
h
defines a symplectic vector bundle structure on
{\mathscr{E}}

we recover
the
sheaf of self adjoint homomorphisms of
({\mathscr{E}}
,h)
. We will often write

\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}):={\mathcal{H}om}_{s,h}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}).

Now suppose that
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
is a filtered symplectic vector bundle with filtration

0=F^{-n}({\mathscr{E}})\subset F^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})\subset\dots\subset F^{n-1}({\mathscr{E}})={\mathscr{E}}.

Then
\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})
comes equipped with a filtration
F_{h}
of the form

\displaystyle 0=F_{h}^{-2n+1}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle\subset F_{h}^{-2n+2}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))\subset\ldots

\displaystyle\subset F^{2n-2}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))=\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}).

The sheaf
F_{h}^{j}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))
is defined to be the subsheaf of
\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})
consisting of elements
f

that satisfy

f(F^{i}({\mathscr{E}}))\subseteq F^{i+j}({\mathscr{E}})\quad\forall\ 1-n\leq i\leq n-1.

Example 3.2.2
.

Consider a split filtered symplectic vector bundle given by locally free sheaves
{\mathscr{A}}_{i}
for
1-n\leq i\leq n-1
and isomorphisms

h_{i}:{\mathscr{A}}_{i}\to{\mathscr{A}}_{-i}

as in Lemma
2.3.3
. A self-adjoint homomorphism is given
\theta_{i,j}:A_{i}\to A_{j}
satisfying the condition
of Lemma
2.3.3
. Then we have

(\theta_{i,j})\in F_{h}^{\alpha}({\mathcal{H}om}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))\iff\theta_{i,j}=0\quad\text{whenever }j>i+\alpha.

This allows us to describe the associated graded sheaf of
{\mathcal{H}om}_{s}(\oplus{\mathscr{A}}_{i},\oplus{\mathscr{A}}_{i})
relatively easily.
Write
{\mathscr{E}}=\oplus{\mathscr{A}}_{i}
viewed as a filtered symplectic vector bundle.

Lemma 3.2.3
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
be a split filtered symplectic vector bundle. Then we have a canonical isomorphism

\Gr^{a}{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Fil}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})\cong{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}_{s}(\Gr{\mathscr{E}},\Gr{\mathscr{E}}).

Proof.

There is a canonical map

F^{a}{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Fil}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})\cong{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}_{s}(\Gr{\mathscr{E}},\Gr{\mathscr{E}})

with kernel
F^{a-1}{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Fil}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})
. So it remains to show that this map is surjective.
Consider
\theta
a section of
{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}_{s}(\Gr{\mathscr{E}},\Gr{\mathscr{E}})
. By Lemma
3.1.1
, the map
\theta
decomposes
as
\oplus\theta_{i}
subject to the given constraint. We define

\theta_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\theta^{i}&j=i+a\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then one readily checks that

\bigoplus\theta_{i,j}\in F^{a}{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Fil}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})\quad\text{lifts}\quad\theta.

∎

Corollary 3.2.4
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
be a filtered symplectic vector bundle. Then we have a canonical isomorphism

\Gr^{a}{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Fil}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})\cong{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}_{s}(\Gr{\mathscr{E}},\Gr{\mathscr{E}}).

Proof.

As in the lemma, there is a canonical map

F^{a}{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Fil}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})\cong{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Gr,\deg a}_{s}(\Gr{\mathscr{E}},\Gr{\mathscr{E}}).

with kernel
F^{a-1}{\mathcal{H}om}^{\Fil}_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})
. It remains to show that it is surjective. This question is local,
so we may assume that
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
is split. This follows from the previous result.
∎

Proposition 3.2.5
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},h,F)
be a filtered symplectic vector bundle on a smooth projective curve
X
.
Then

(1)

\deg(\Gr^{0}_{F}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))=0
;

(2)

\deg(F^{-2}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))=\deg(F^{1}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))
;

(3)

\rank(\Gr_{F}^{i}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))=\rank(\Gr_{F}^{-i}({F}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))))
;

(4)

\rank(F^{-i-1}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))+\rank(F^{i}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))=\rank(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))
.

Proof.

For all the assertions we will freely apply
3.2.4
.

For the first assertion, we remark that it is well-known that
{\mathcal{H}om}_{s}^{\deg 0}({\mathscr{E}}_{0},{\mathscr{E}}_{0})
has degree 0 as it is
the adjoint bundle of the symplectic vector bundle. The assertion follows from
3.1.4
.

It follows that

\deg(F^{0}\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))=\deg(F^{-1}\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))
. Furthermore, using
Corollary
3.1.2
and the fact that
\Gr_{F}^{i}(E)=(\Gr_{F}^{-i}(E))^{\vee}
, it follows that

\displaystyle(\Gr^{1}_{F}({\mathcal{H}om}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{i>-1/2}{\mathcal{H}om}(\Gr^{i}{\mathscr{E}},\Gr^{i+1}{\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{i>-1/2}{\mathcal{H}om}(\Gr^{i+1}{\mathscr{E}},\Gr^{i}{\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{i>-1/2}{\mathcal{H}om}(\Gr^{-i}{\mathscr{E}},\Gr^{-i-1}{\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{i<-1/2}{\mathcal{H}om}(\Gr^{i}{\mathscr{E}},\Gr^{i-1}{\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}.

This implies
\deg(\Gr^{-1}_{F}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))=-\deg(\Gr^{1}_{F}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))
. Thus,

\displaystyle\deg(F^{1}(\homsheaf_{s}({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))-\deg(F_{h}^{-2}(\homsheaf_{s}(E,E)))=

\displaystyle\deg(\Gr^{-1}_{F}(\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})))

\displaystyle+\deg(\Gr^{0}_{F}\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle+\deg(\Gr^{1}_{F}\homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}}))=0,

and the second assertion is proved.

For the third claim we write down the calculation for
i
odd, the even case is similar. We make use of Corollary
3.1.2
and the
fact that

\rank\Gr^{i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}})=\rank\Gr^{-i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}}).

Then

\displaystyle\rank\Gr^{-i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j<i/2}\rank(\Gr^{j}_{F}({\mathscr{E}}))\rank(\Gr^{j-i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j<i/2}\rank(\Gr^{-j}_{F}({\mathscr{E}}_{j}))\rank(\Gr^{-j+i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j>i/2}\rank(\Gr^{j}_{F}({\mathscr{E}}_{j}))\rank(\Gr^{j+i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rank\Gr^{i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}}).

For the last claim we have

\displaystyle\rank({\mathscr{E}}/F^{-i-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j>=-i-1}\rank\Gr^{j}_{F}({\mathscr{E}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j>-i-1}\rank(\Gr^{-j}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j<i+1}\rank(\Gr^{j}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rank(F^{i}).

∎

4.
Stacks of nilpotent endomorphisms

4.1.
Filtrations induced by nilpotent endomorphisms.

If
\theta
is a nilpotent endomorphism of a vector bundle
{\mathscr{E}}
we define its
order of nilpotency
to be the
integer
n
with

\theta^{n}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\theta^{n-1}\neq 0.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},h)
be a symplectic vector bundle over a scheme
S
with

\theta:{\mathscr{E}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{E}}

a self adjoint nilpotent endomorphism of order of nilpotency
n>0
. Then we can equip
({\mathscr{E}},h)
with a filtration

0=C^{-n}({\mathscr{E}})\subset C^{1-n}({\mathscr{E}})\subset\ldots\subset C^{n-1}({\mathscr{E}})={\mathscr{E}}

where

C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}i-j=l\\
0\leq i\leq n\\
1\leq j\leq n+1\end{subarray}}{\sum}\ker(\theta^{i})\cap\image(\theta^{j-1})

The filtration seems somewhat arbitrary, however it has an important characterization, see
4.1.4
below.

Lemma 4.1.1
.

With the definitions as above, the map

\theta^{l}:C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})\rightarrow C^{-l}({\mathscr{E}})

is surjective for
0\leq k\leq n-1
. Moroever the induced map

\theta^{l}:\Gr_{C}^{l}({\mathscr{E}})\rightarrow\Gr_{C}^{-l}({\mathscr{E}})

is an isomorphism for
0\leq l\leq n-1
.

Proof.

If
x\in\ker(\theta^{i})\cap\image(\theta^{i-l-1})
then

\theta^{l}(x)\in\ker(\theta^{i-l})\cap\image(\theta^{i-1})

and hence
\theta^{l}

maps
C^{l}(E)
to
C^{-l}(E)
. To show surjectivity, it is enough to show that an element
y\in\ker(\theta^{i})\cap\image(\theta^{i+l-1})

has a preimage
x
. The map

\theta^{l}:\image(\theta^{i-1})\rightarrow\image(\theta^{i+l-1})

is surjective so we can certainly find
x\in\image(\theta^{i-1})
with
\theta^{l}(x)=y
.
Since
y\in\ker(\theta^{i})
we have
x\in\ker(\theta^{i+l})
thus
finishing the proof of the first part.
Since

\theta^{l}:C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})\rightarrow C^{-l}({\mathscr{E}})

is surjective, it’s composition with the quotient map
C^{l}(E)

\twoheadrightarrow

\frac{C^{-l}(E)}{C^{-l-1}(E)}

is surjective as well.

It remains to show that if
x\in C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})
then
\theta^{l}(x)\in C^{-l-1}({\mathscr{E}})

if and only if

x\in C^{l-1}({\mathscr{E}})
.
The forward implication has already been proved.

So suppose that
\theta^{l}(x\in C^{-l-1}({\mathscr{E}}))
.
Let
\theta^{l}(x)
=
\sum_{i}a_{i}
where
a_{i}
lies in
\ker(\theta^{i})\cap\image(\theta^{i+l})
.
By the first part, there
exists
b_{i}
such that
\theta^{l}(b_{i})
=
a_{i}
. By a similar argument as above, such a
b_{i}
has to lie in
\ker(\theta^{i+l})\cap\image(\theta^{i})\subset C^{l-1}({\mathscr{E}})
.
Also
x
-
\sum_{i}b_{i}
lies in
\ker(\theta^{l})\subset C^{l-1}({\mathscr{E}})
. Hence,
x
has to lie in

C^{l-1}({\mathscr{E}})
.
∎

Corollary 4.1.2
.

With definitions as above, the induced map

\theta:\Gr_{C}^{l}({\mathscr{E}})\rightarrow\Gr_{C}^{l-2}({\mathscr{E}})

is injective for
n-1\geq l\geq 1
.

Proof.

This follows from the injectivity of
\theta^{l}
.
∎

Lemma 4.1.3
.

Let
S
be a noetherian scheme.
Let
({\mathscr{E}},h)
be symplectic vector bundle on
S
and let
\theta
be a nilpotent
endomorphism of
{\mathscr{E}}
of symplectic type with order of nilpotency
n
. Suppose further
that
C^{i}({\mathscr{E}})/C^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})
is flat over
S
for all
-n\leq i\leq n-1
. Then

C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}=C^{-l-1}({\mathscr{E}})

for
-n\leq l\leq n-1
.

Proof.

We first show that
C^{-1-l}({\mathscr{E}})\subset C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}
. It is enough to show that for

x\in\ker(\theta^{i})\cap\image(\theta^{i-l-1})
and
y\in\ker(\theta^{j})\cap\image(\theta^{j+l})
that
h(x)(y)=0
.

If
y\in\image(\theta^{i})
then
y=\theta^{i}(y^{\prime})
. In view of the self adjointness of
\theta
we have

h(x)(\theta^{i}(y^{\prime}))=-h(\theta^{i}(x))(y)=0,

as
x\in\ker(\theta^{i})
.

So we may assume that
y\not\in\image(\theta^{i})
. It follows that

j+l<i
and hence

j<i-l
. Thus
y\in\ker(\theta^{i-l-1})
and the result follows from the fact that
x\in\image(\theta^{i-l-1})
.

In view of the noetherian hypothesis on
S
and the flatness assumptions, all the quotients involving the various
C^{l}(E)

have a well-defined rank as they are locally free.
Since

\rank(C^{l}({\mathscr{E}}))=\sum_{i\leq l}\rank(\Gr_{C}^{i}({\mathscr{E}})).

Using Lemma
4.1.1

\displaystyle\rank\left(\frac{E}{C^{-1-l}({\mathscr{E}})}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i>-1-l}\rank(\Gr_{C}^{i}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i>-1-l}\rank(\Gr_{C}^{-i}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i<l+1}\rank(\Gr_{C}^{i}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle=\rank(C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})).

Hence, we have
\rank(C^{-1-l}({\mathscr{E}}))=\rank({\mathscr{E}})
-
\rank(C^{l}({\mathscr{E}}))=\rank(C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp})
. The hypothesis implies that both
{\mathscr{E}}/C^{-1-l}({\mathscr{E}})
and

C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})
are flat. The exact sequence

0\rightarrow C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}\rightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\rightarrow C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}\rightarrow 0

implies that
{\mathscr{E}}/C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}
is flat as well. Hence,
C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}/C^{-1-l}({\mathscr{E}})
is flat of
\rank
0 and hence 0 itself.
∎

Lemma 4.1.4
.

Let
({\mathscr{E}},h)
be a symplectic vector bundle over a noetherian scheme
S
equipped with a nilpotent self adjoint endomorphism
\theta
and order of nilpotency
n
.
Suppose further that

\Gr_{C}^{i}({\mathscr{E}})
is flat over
S
for all
i
. Let
F
be a filtration on
{\mathscr{E}}
such that

0=F^{-n}(E)\subset F^{1-n}(E)\subset\ldots\subset F^{n-1}(E)=E

and
({\mathscr{E}},F,h)
is a filtered symplectic vector bundle.
Assume further that

(1)

\theta(F^{i}({\mathscr{E}}))

\subset

F^{i-2}({\mathscr{E}})

(2)

and the induced map

\theta^{l}:\Gr_{F}^{l}({\mathscr{E}})\rightarrow\Gr_{F}^{-l}({\mathscr{E}})

is injective for all
l
.

Then

C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{l}({\mathscr{E}})

for all
l
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that

C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{l}({\mathscr{E}})

for all
n-1\geq l\geq

0
since
C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}
=
C^{-1-l}({\mathscr{E}})
by Lemma
4.1.3
and

F^{l}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}
=
F^{-1-l}({\mathscr{E}})
by definition. We proceed by descending induction on
l
.

For
l=n-1
, we have
C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})=E=F^{l}({\mathscr{E}})
.
So assume that
C^{l}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{l}({\mathscr{E}})
for
l\geq i>0
.
We will use the fact
F^{-i-1}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{i}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}=C^{i}({\mathscr{E}})^{\perp}=C^{-i-1}({\mathscr{E}})
repeatedly.

By definition and Lemma
4.1.1
,

\theta^{i}(F^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}}))\subset F^{-i-1}({\mathscr{E}})=C^{-i-1}({\mathscr{E}})=\theta^{i+1}(C^{i+1}({\mathscr{E}}))
.
Consider
x\in F^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})
then
\theta^{i}(x)=\theta^{i+1}(y)
for
y\in C^{i+1}({\mathscr{E}})
. This implies

x\in\ker(\theta^{i})+\theta(C^{i+1}({\mathscr{E}}))\subset C^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})
. It follows that
F^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})\subseteq C^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})
.

On the other hand
\theta^{i}(C^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}}))\subset C^{-i-1}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{-i-1}({\mathscr{E}})
. Hence the map

\theta^{i}:F^{i}(E)\rightarrow\frac{F^{-i}(E)}{F^{-i-1}(E)}

vanishes on
C^{i-1}(E)
. By the injectivity of
\theta^{i}
from
\Gr^{i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}})\to\Gr^{-i}_{F}({\mathscr{E}})
, we have
C^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})\subset F^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})
.
Thus,
C^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})=F^{i-1}({\mathscr{E}})
. This finishes the induction step.
∎

Definition 6.1.1
.

A symplectic vector bundle

is defined to be

Remark 6.1.2
.

This definition can be reinterpreted in terms of the associated principal
{\rm Sp}_{2r}
-bundle to
({\mathscr{E}},h)
. It means that
this principal bundle has reduction of structure group to a Levi subgroup of
{\rm Sp}_{2r}
associated to a maximal parabolic
subgroup.
To see this, first recall that the associated principal bundle has functor of points given by

\displaystyle\Sch/X

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\Sets

\displaystyle(f:S\to X)

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle{\rm Isom}((f^{*}E,f^{*}h),({\mathscr{O}}_{S}^{2r},h)),

where
({\mathscr{O}}_{S}^{2r},h)
is the trivial symplectic vector bundle of rank
2r
.

An isomorphism
\phi:(f^{*}E,f^{*}h)\to({\mathscr{O}}_{S}^{2r},h)
identifies

with an isotropic subbundle of the trivial bundle
which in turn produces a reduction of structure group to

\Hom({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})
. Now,
\Aut({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})
is the subgroup of elements
\theta
of
\Hom(E,E)
that satisfies

\mu(\theta)=\theta^{-1}
and hence
\theta=\theta^{-1}
. This proves that
\Aut({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})\cong\mu_{2}
. In particular, the residual
gerbe
{\mathscr{G}}({\mathscr{E}})
of
{\mathscr{E}}
in
\Bun_{X,{\rm Sp}_{2r}}
is a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe. The index of this gerbe is a power of 2 and divides
2r

by
[
14
, Proposition 3.1.2.1 (ii)]
. We have

\ed_{k}({\mathscr{G}})({\mathscr{G}}({\mathscr{E}}))\leq 2^{\nu_{2}(2r)}

by
[
4
, Theorem 5.4]
. Hence,

\ed_{k}({\mathscr{E}})=\trdeg_{k}(k({\mathscr{E}}))+\ed_{k}({\mathscr{G}})({\mathscr{G}}({\mathscr{E}}))\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r})+2^{\nu_{2}(2r)}

by Corollary
6.2.7
.
∎

Lemma 6.3.4
.

Let
X
be a genus
g\geq 2
curve over
k
. Let
K\supset k
be a field extension and let
({\mathscr{E}},h)
be a non-simple
symplectic vector bundle of rank
2r
over
X_{K}
. Then, we have

\ed_{k}({\mathscr{E}})\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r})+1

Proof.

Since
k({\mathscr{E}})=k({\mathscr{E}}\otimes_{k}L)
for any field extension, we can assume that
K
is algebraically closed for computing

\trdeg_{k}(k({\mathscr{E}}))
. If
{\mathscr{E}}
is decomposable, then there exists a decomposition of
{\mathscr{E}}
into
symplectic bundles

({\mathscr{E}},h)=({\mathscr{E}}_{1},h_{1})\oplus({\mathscr{E}}_{2},h_{2}).

of rank
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
respectively
Hence,

\displaystyle\trdeg_{k}(k({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle\leq\trdeg_{k}(k({\mathscr{E}}_{1}))+\trdeg_{k}(k({\mathscr{E}}_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq(g-1)(\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{1}+r_{2}^{2}+r_{2}}{2})

\displaystyle\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r}-2r+1).

by Corollary
6.2.7
and
[
3
, Lemma 6.5]
. In the case
{\mathscr{E}}
is indecomposable, there exists
a non-zero nilpotent morphism in
\Homs({\mathscr{E}},{\mathscr{E}})
by Lemma
6.1.7
. Hence, there exists at least
two terms in the decomposition in Lemma
6.2.5
.
A similar computation as above now implies

\trdeg_{k}(k({\mathscr{E}}))\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r}-2r+1)

Now, by Lemma
6.3.1
,

\displaystyle\ed_{k}({\mathscr{E}})

\displaystyle=\trdeg_{k}(k({\mathscr{E}}))+\ed_{k}({\mathscr{G}})({\mathscr{G}}({\mathscr{E}}))

\displaystyle\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r}-2r+1)+2r

\displaystyle\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r})-(g-2)(2r-1)-2r+1+2r

\displaystyle\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r})+1.

∎

Corollary 6.3.5
.

Let
X
be a curve of genus
g\geq 2
over a field
k
of characteristic 0. Then

\ed_{k}(\Bun_{X,{\rm Sp}_{2r}})\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r})+2^{\nu_{2}(2r)}

where
\nu_{2}(2r)
is the highest power of 2 dividing
2r
.

We refer the reader to page 3 of
[
2
]
for the definition of a regularly stable
symplectic vector bundle. There is an open substack

\Bun_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}^{rs}\hookrightarrow\Bun_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}

paramerising regularly stable symplectic vector bundles and there is a coarse moduli map

\Bun_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}^{rs}\to M_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}^{rs}

that makes
\Bun_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}^{rs}
into a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe over the coarse moduli space.
The main theorem of
[
2
, 7.4]
says that the index of this gerbe is
2^{\nu_{2}(2r)}
.

Theorem 6.3.6
.

Let
X
be a curve of genus
g\geq 2
over a field
k
of characteristic 0. Then

\ed_{k}(\Bun_{X,{\rm Sp}_{2r}})=(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r})+2^{\nu_{2}(2r)}

where
\nu_{2}(2r)
is the highest power of 2 dividing
2r
.

Proof.

We have already proved that

\ed_{k}(\Bun_{X,{\rm Sp}_{2r}})\leq(g-1)(\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r})+2^{\nu_{2}(2r)}.

For the reverse inequality we consider the generic gerbe of

\Bun_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}^{rs}\to M_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}^{rs}.

By deformation theory one has
\dim M_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}^{rs}=(g-1)\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r}
and hence

\trdeg_{k}k(M_{{\rm Sp}_{2r}}^{rs})=(g-1)\dim{\rm Sp}_{2r}.

The reverse inequality now follows from
[
4
, Thm. 5.4 ]
and
[
2
, 7.3]
.
∎

Self-Homotopy Equivalence Group of an Elliptic Space and Its Embedding in general Linear Groups
Mahmoud Benkhalifa

Abstract
. For a rational elliptic space, this paper examines the relationship between its homotopy groups and its self-homotopy equivalence group. Moreover, we investigate how this group is embedded in general linear groups.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
55P10, 55P62

1.
Introduction

In this paper, the term “space” refers to a simply connected rational CW-complex
X
of finite type. This implies that for every
n
, the cohomology group
H^{n}(X)=H^{n}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{Q}
. A space
X
is said to be
elliptic
if both
H^{*}(X)
and
\pi_{*}(X)
are finite-dimensional. The
formal dimension
of
X
is given by
n=\max\{i\mid H^{i}(X)\neq 0\}
(see
[
5
]
, §32).

Given a space
X
, we define
\mathcal{E}(X)
as the group of self-homotopy equivalences of
X
and
\mathcal{E}_{\#}(X)
as the subgroup of those equivalences that induce the identity on homotopy groups
[
2
]
, that is,

\mathcal{E}_{\#}(X)=\big\{[f]\in\mathcal{E}(X)\mid\pi_{*}(f)=\operatorname{id}:\pi_{*}(X)\to\pi_{*}(X)\big\}.

This paper focuses on the relationship between the homotopy groups
\pi_{*}(X)
and the finiteness of
\mathcal{E}(X)
. More precisely, we explore the conditions on the homology of an elliptic space
X
that ensure the finiteness of
\mathcal{E}(X)
. To address this, we analyze the following exact sequence associated with the minimal Sullivan model of
X
:

\cdots\to\operatorname{Hom}\big(\pi_{q}(X),\mathbb{Q}\big)\overset{b^{q}}{\to}H^{q+1}(X^{[n]})\to H^{q+1}(X)\overset{h^{q+1}}{\to}\operatorname{Hom}\big(\pi_{q+1}(X),\mathbb{Q}\big)\overset{b^{q+1}}{\to}\cdots.

Thus, making use of this exact sequence, we establish the following results.

Theorem 1
.

(
Theorem
4.3
)
.
Let
X
be an elliptic space, and let
\pi_{m_{1}}(X),\dots,\pi_{m_{k}}(X)
denote the nontrivial homotopy groups of
X
, where
m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{k}
. For each
1\leq j\leq k
, define

\mathcal{L}^{m_{j}}(X)=\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{m_{j}}(X),H^{m_{j}}(X^{[m_{j}-1]})\big).

Then, the group of self-homotopy equivalences
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of the group

\mathcal{L}^{m_{k}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{k},\mathbb{Q})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{L}^{m_{2}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{2},\mathbb{Q})\times\mathrm{GL}(p_{1},\mathbb{Q}),

where
p_{j}=\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905pt\pi_{m_{j}}(X)
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

Theorem 2
.

(Theorem
4.4
).

Let
X
be an elliptic space, and let
\pi_{m_{1}}(X),\cdots,\pi_{m_{k}}(X)
be the nontrivial homotopy group of
X
. If the group
\mathcal{E}(X)
is finite, then
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of

\mathrm{GL}(p_{m_{1}},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}(p_{m_{k}},{\mathbb{Q}})
, where
p_{m_{j}}=\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905pt\pi_{m_{j}}(X)
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

We establish our main theorem using standard tools from rational homotopy theory, specifically employing Sullivan’s model. For a comprehensive introduction to these techniques, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
. Below, we recall some of the relevant notation. A minimal Sullivan algebra
(\Lambda V,\partial)
consists of a free exterior algebra
\Lambda V
over a finite-type graded vector space
V=(V^{\geq 1})
, together with a differential
\partial
of degree 1 that satisfies
\partial:V\to\Lambda^{\geq 2}V
.

Every topological space
X
admits a Sullivan algebra
(\Lambda V,\partial)
, known as its minimal Sullivan model, which is unique up to isomorphism and completely determines the rational homotopy type of
X
. The Sullivan model allows us to recover the homotopy data of
X
through the following identifications:

V^{*}\cong\pi_{*+1}(X),\quad\quad\quad H^{*}(\Lambda V)\cong H^{*}(X).

(1)

The paper is organized as follows.

Section 2 focuses on revisiting the concept of homotopy between DGA-maps between Sullivan algebras and reviewing key properties of the Sullivan model for a rationally elliptic space. Furthermore, we recall the fundamental definition of the Whitehead exact sequence associated with a Sullivan algebra. In Section 3, we establish the main theorem of the paper and provide illustrative examples that highlight the effectiveness of our results.

2.1.
Notion of homotopy for Sullivan algebras

All vector spaces, algebras, tensor products etc… are defined
over
\mathbb{Q}
and this ground field will be in general

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\Lambda V,\partial)
be a Sullivan algebra. Define the vector spaces
\overline{V}
and
\widehat{V}
by
(\overline{V})^{n}=V^{n+1}
and
(\widehat{V})^{n}=V^{n}
. We then define the free differential commutative algebra
\Lambda(V,\overline{V},\widehat{V}),D)
where the differential
D
is given by:

D(v)=\partial(v),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,D(\widehat{v})=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,D(\overline{v})=\widehat{v}.

(2)

We define a derivation
S
of degree -1 of the fcca
\Lambda(V,\overline{V},\widehat{V}),D)
by putting
S(v)=\overline{v}
,
S(\overline{v})=S(\widehat{v})=0
.

A homotopy between two cochain morphisms
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}:(\Lambda(V),\partial)\to(\Lambda(V),\partial)
is a DGA-map

F\colon\Lambda(V,\overline{V},\widehat{V}),D)\to(\Lambda(V),\partial)

such that
F(v)=\alpha(v)
and
F\circ e^{\theta}(v)=\alpha^{\prime}(v)
where the DGA-map:

e^{\theta}:(\Lambda(V),\partial)\to\Lambda(V,\overline{V},\widehat{V}),D)

is defined by setting:

e^{\theta}(v)=v+\widehat{v}+\underset{n\geq 1}{\sum}\frac{1}{n!}(S\circ\partial)^{n}(v),\,\,v\in V\text{\,\,\,\,\ and\,\,\,\, }\theta=D\circ S+S\circ D

The concept of DGA-homotopy allows us to define the group
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda V,\partial)
, which consists of the self-homotopy equivalence classes of
(\Lambda V,\partial)
, along with its subgroup
\mathcal{E}_{\#}((\Lambda V,\partial)
, consisting of the self-homotopy equivalence classes that induce the identity on the graded vector space of the indecomposables
V
.

Leveraging the properties of the Sullivan model, we deduce that if
(\Lambda V,\partial)
models
X
, then
(\Lambda V^{\leq m-1},\partial)
models the
(m-1)
-section of Postnikov
X^{[m-1]}
of
X
. Moreover, we have:

{\mathcal{E}}(X)\cong{\mathcal{E}}(\Lambda V),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\#}(X)\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{\#}(\Lambda V).

(3)

{\mathcal{E}}(X^{m})\cong{\mathcal{E}}(V^{\leq m-1}),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\#}(X^{[m-1]})\cong{\mathcal{E}}_{\#}(V^{\leq m-1}).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\colon(\Lambda V^{\leq n},\partial)\to(\Lambda V^{\leq n},\partial)
be two DGA-maps such that

\displaystyle\alpha(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(v)+y,\,\,\,\,\,v\in V^{n},\,\,\,\,\,\,y\in\Lambda V^{\leq n-1},

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\beta,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{on}\,\,\,\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}.

There is a cycle
y^{\prime}\in\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}
of degree

such that
\alpha
is homotopic to the following DGA-map

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(w)+y^{\prime},\,\,\,\,\,v\in V^{n},

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{on}\,\,\,\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}.

(4)

Proof.

Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are homotopic on
\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}
, there exits a homotopy

F\colon\Lambda(V^{\leq n-1},\overline{V}^{\leq n-1},\widehat{V}^{\leq n-1}),D)\to(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1},\partial)

such that

F(v)=\beta(v),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,F\circ e^{\theta}(v)=\alpha(v),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall v\in V^{\leq n-1}.

(5)

Therefore for

, the element
F\Big(\underset{n\geq 1}{\sum}\frac{1}{n!}(S\circ D)^{n}(v)\Big)
is a well-defined decomposable element in
\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}
of degree

. Thus, we set:

y^{\prime}=y-F\Big(\underset{n\geq 1}{\sum}\frac{1}{n!}(S\circ D)^{n}(v)\Big).

(6)

Now, by hypothesis we have

\partial(\beta(v))+\partial(y)=\partial\alpha(v)=\alpha(\partial(v))=F\circ e^{\theta}(\partial(v)).

(7)

But
e^{\theta}
is a DGA-map, so

\displaystyle F\circ e^{\theta}(\partial(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F\circ D(e^{\theta}(v))=F\circ D\Big(v+\widehat{v}+\underset{n\geq 1}{\sum}\frac{1}{n!}(S\circ D)^{n}(v)\Big)

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(D(v))+F(D(\widehat{v}))+F\circ D\Big(\underset{n\geq 1}{\sum}\frac{1}{n!}(S\circ D)^{n}(v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(\partial(v))+\partial\circ F\Big(\underset{n\geq 1}{\sum}\frac{1}{n!}(S\circ D)^{n}(v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(\partial(v))+\partial\Big(\underset{n\geq 1}{\sum}\frac{1}{n!}F(S\circ D)^{n}(v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial(\beta(v))+\partial\Big(\underset{n\geq 1}{\sum}\frac{1}{n!}F(S\circ D)^{n}(v)\Big).

Here, we use the following facts:

•

By (
2

D(\widehat{v})=0
.

•

The identity
F\circ D=\partial\circ F
holds.

•

Since
\partial(v)\in\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}
and
F=\beta
on
V^{\leq n-1}
(by (
5

F(\partial(v))=\beta(\partial(v)).

Comparing (
7
) and (
8
), we obtain

\partial(y)=\partial\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}F(S\circ D)^{n}(v)\right).

By (
6
), this implies that
\partial(y^{\prime})=0
.

Let us now prove that
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\alpha
are homotopic, where
\alpha^{\prime}
is given in (
4
). Indeed, define
G\colon\Lambda(V^{\leq n},\overline{V}^{\leq n},\widehat{V}^{\leq n}),D)\to(\Lambda V^{\leq n},\partial)
by setting

\displaystyle G(v)\hskip-5.69054pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\beta(v),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,G(\hat{v})=G(\bar{v})=0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\hbox{ for }v\in V^{n},

\displaystyle G\hskip-5.69054pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054ptF,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\hbox{ on }\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}.

Consider the relations in (
2

\partial(G(v))=\partial(\beta(v)),\quad G(D(v))=G(\partial(v)).

Since
\partial(v)\in\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}

G(\partial(v))=F(\partial(v)).

From relations (

Moreover, by considering relation (

\alpha
and

Remark 2.4
.

If
\alpha,\beta\colon(\Lambda V^{\leq n},\partial)\to(\Lambda V^{\leq n},\partial)
are two homotopic DGA-maps, then their restrictions
\alpha_{\leq n-1},\beta_{\leq n-1}\colon(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1},\partial)\to(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1},\partial)
are also homotopic. Indeed, since
\alpha,\beta
are homotopic, there exits a homotopy

F\colon\Lambda(V^{\leq n},\overline{V}^{\leq n},\widehat{V}^{\leq n}),D)\to(\Lambda V^{\leq n},\partial)

such that

F(v)=\beta(v),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,F\circ e^{\theta}(v)=\alpha(v),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall v\in V^{\leq n}.

Now if we consider the DGA-map:

G\colon\Lambda(V^{\leq n-1},\overline{V}^{\leq n-1},\widehat{V}^{\leq n-1}),D)\to(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1},\partial)

which is the restriction of
F
, then clearly
G
is DGA-homotopy between
\alpha_{\leq n-1},\beta_{\leq n-1}.

\displaystyle id,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ on }\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}.

According to lemma
2.3
, there exists a unique cocycle
y^{\prime}_{v}\in\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}
of degree
n
such that
\beta
is homotopic to the following DGA-map

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v+y_{v}^{\prime},\,\,\,\,\,v\in V^{n},

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle id,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{on}\,\,\,\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}.

Note that the cocycle
y_{w}^{\prime}
is decomposable, which means that the homology class
\{y_{w}^{\prime}\}\in H^{n}(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1})
. This allows us to define the map:

\Theta:\mathrm{ker}\Psi_{n}\to\mathrm{Hom}\big(V^{n},H^{n}(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1})\big)

by setting:

\Theta([\beta])=f,

where
f:V^{n}\to H^{n}(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1})
is defined by
f(v)=\{y^{\prime}_{v}\}.

Then, we define:

\Theta^{\prime}:\mathrm{Hom}\big(V^{n},H^{n}(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1})\big)\to\mathrm{ker}\Psi_{n},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,f\mapsto\Theta(f)=[\beta]

where the DGA-map
\beta
is given by:

\displaystyle\beta(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v+z_{v},\,\,\,\,\,v\in V^{n},

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle id,\,\,\,\text{ on }\,\,V^{\leq n-1}.

Here the element
z_{v}
is given
f(v)=\{z_{v}\}
. Clearly,
[\beta]\in\mathrm{ker}\Psi_{n}
and we have
\Theta\circ\Theta^{\prime}=id
and
\Theta^{\prime}\circ\Theta=id,
implying that
\Theta
is bijective.

Finally, let us prove that
\Psi_{n}
and
\sigma_{n}
are group homomorphisms. For every
[\alpha]
and
[\beta]
in
\mathcal{E}(\Lambda V^{\leq n}))
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}([\alpha].[\beta])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}([\alpha\circ\beta])=\Big(\widetilde{\alpha\circ\beta},[\alpha_{\leq n-1}\circ\beta_{\leq n-1}]\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\alpha},[\alpha_{\leq n-1}])\circ(\widetilde{\beta},[\beta_{\leq n-1}])=\Psi_{n}([\alpha])\circ\Psi_{n}([\beta]).

Thus,
\Psi_{n}
is a group homomorphism.

Now, for any
(\xi^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}]),(\xi,[\alpha])\in{\mathcal{D}}^{n}
, we need to prove that

\sigma_{n}((\xi^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}]))\circ\sigma_{n}((\xi,[\alpha]))=\sigma_{n}\Big((\xi^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}])\circ(\xi,[\alpha])\Big).

From (
15
) and (
17
), we know that

\beta(v)=\begin{cases}\xi(v)+y_{v}&\text{if }v\in V^{n},\\
\alpha(v)&\text{if }v\in V^{\leq n-1}.\end{cases},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\beta^{\prime}(w)=\begin{cases}\xi^{\prime}(v)+y^{\prime}_{v}&\text{if }v\in V^{n},\\
\alpha^{\prime}(v)&\text{if }v\in V^{\leq n-1}.\end{cases}

where
y^{\prime}_{v},y_{v}\in\Lambda V^{\leq n-1})
satisfy:

\alpha\circ\partial(v)-\partial\circ\xi(v)=\partial(y_{v}),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\alpha^{\prime}\circ\partial(v)-\partial\circ\xi^{\prime}(v)=\partial(y^{\prime}_{v}).

Now, on one hand, we have:

\sigma_{n}((\xi^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}]))\circ\sigma_{n}((\xi,[\alpha]))=[\beta^{\prime}]\circ[\beta]=[\beta^{\prime}\circ\beta]

where, for
v\in V^{n}
,

\beta^{\prime}\circ\beta(v)=\beta^{\prime}(\beta(v))=\beta^{\prime}(\xi(v)+y_{v})=\beta^{\prime}(\xi(v))+\beta^{\prime}(y_{v})=\xi^{\prime}\circ\xi(v)+y_{\xi(v)}+\alpha^{\prime}(y_{v}),

where the elements
y_{\xi(v)}\in\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}
satisfy:

\alpha^{\prime}\circ\partial(\xi(v))-\partial\circ\xi^{\prime}(\xi(v))=\partial(y_{\xi(v)}).

(19)

For
v\in V^{\leq n-1}
, we obtain:

\beta^{\prime}\circ\beta(v)=\beta^{\prime}(\beta(v))=\beta^{\prime}(\alpha(v))=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\alpha(v).

Thus, we conclude that:

\beta^{\prime}\circ\beta(v)=\begin{cases}\xi^{\prime}\circ\xi(v)+y_{\xi(v)}+\alpha^{\prime}(y_{v})&\text{if }v\in V^{n},\\
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\alpha(v)&\text{if }v\in V^{\leq n-1}.\end{cases}

(20)

On the other hand, we have:

\sigma_{n}\big((\xi^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}])\circ(\xi,[\alpha])\big)=\sigma_{n}\big((\xi^{\prime}\circ\xi,[\alpha^{\prime}\circ\alpha])\big)=[\beta^{*}],

where, by (
15
), the DGL-map
\beta^{*}
is given by:

\beta^{*}(v)=\begin{cases}\xi^{\prime}\circ\xi(v)+z_{v}&\text{if }v\in V^{n},\\
\alpha^{\prime}\circ\alpha(v)&\text{if }v\in V^{\leq n-1}.\end{cases}

(21)

where
z_{v}\in\Lambda V^{\leq n-1}
must satisfy:

\alpha^{\prime}\circ\alpha\circ\partial(v)-\partial\circ\xi^{\prime}\circ\xi(v)=\partial(z_{v}).

Now, using (
19
), we obtain:

\partial(y_{\xi(v)}+\alpha^{\prime}(y_{v}))=\partial(y_{\xi(v)})+\partial(\alpha^{\prime}(y_{v}))=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\partial(\xi(v))-\partial\circ\xi^{\prime}(\xi(v))+\partial(\alpha^{\prime}(y_{v}))

=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\partial(\xi(v))-\partial\circ\xi^{\prime}(\xi(v))+\alpha^{\prime}\circ\partial(y_{v})=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\Big(\partial(\xi(v))+\partial(y_{v})\Big)-\partial\circ\xi^{\prime}(\xi(v)).

Consequently, if we choose
z_{v}=y_{\xi(v)}+\alpha^{\prime}(y_{v})
, then taking into account (
20
) and (
21
), we obtain:

\sigma_{n}((\xi^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}]))\circ\sigma_{n}((\xi,[\alpha]))=\sigma_{n}\Big((\xi^{\prime},[\alpha^{\prime}])\circ(\xi,[\alpha])\Big),

implying that
\sigma_{n}
is a group homomorphism.

Finally, according to (
18
), it is easy to see that:

\mathrm{ker}\Psi_{n}\subset\mathcal{E}_{\#}(\Lambda V^{\leq n}),\,\,\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,\,\,\Psi_{n}(\mathcal{E}_{\#}(\Lambda V^{\leq n}))={\mathcal{D}}_{\#}^{n}.

Thus, we derive the short exact sequence (
13
).
∎

Since the two short exact sequences in Theorem
3.3
split, we deduce the following result.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(\Lambda V^{\leq m},\partial)
be a Sullivan algebra. For every
n\leq m
, we have:

\mathcal{E}(\Lambda V^{\leq n})\cong\mathrm{Hom}\big(V^{n},H^{n}(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1})\big)\rtimes{\mathcal{D}}^{n}

\mathcal{E}_{\#}(\Lambda V^{\leq n})\cong\mathrm{Hom}\big(V^{n},H^{n}(\Lambda V^{\leq n-1})\big)\rtimes{\mathcal{D}}_{\#}^{n}

4.
Topological applications

As a direct consequence of the above theorems, we establish the following results, which explore the connection between the homology groups of an elliptic space
X
and the finiteness of the group
{\mathcal{E}}(X)
. Before proceeding, we introduce the following remark.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
X
be a rational elliptic space of formal dimension
p
and let
m=\max\{k\mid\pi_{k}(X)\neq 0\}
and of . By Proposition
2.6
, its Sullivan model is given by
(\Lambda V^{\leq m},\partial)
, where
H^{p}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\mathbb{Q}
and
H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})=0
for
i=p-1
and
i>p
. Applying the identifications (
1
) and (
3
), for every
n\leq m
, the group
{\mathcal{D}}^{n}
can be viewed as a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(H^{n}(X))\times{\mathcal{E}}(X^{[n-1]})
, which we denote by
{\mathcal{D}}^{n}(X)
. Likewise,
{\mathcal{D}}_{\#}^{n}
can be regarded as a subgroup of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\#}(X^{[n-1]})
, denoted
{\mathcal{D}}_{\#}^{n}(X)
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
X
be a rational elliptic space, and let
m=\max\{k\mid\pi_{k}(X)\neq 0\}
. For every
n\leq m
, we have,

\mathcal{E}(X^{[n]})\cong\big(\pi_{n}(X),H^{n}(X^{[n-1]})\big)\rtimes\mathcal{D}^{n}(X)

\mathcal{E}_{*}(X^{[n]})\cong\big(\pi_{n}(X),H^{n}(X^{[n-1]})\big)\rtimes\mathcal{D}_{*}^{n}(X).

Proof.

In this case, the Sullivan model of
X
has the form
(\Lambda V^{\leq m},\partial)
, and using the fact that the sequences in Theorem
3.3
are split. Note that in this case we have
\mathcal{E}(X)\cong\mathcal{E}(X^{[m]}).

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be a rational elliptic space, and let
\pi_{m_{1}}(X),\cdots,\pi_{m_{k}}(X)
be the nontrivial homotopy group of
X
, where
m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{k}
. For every
1\leq j\leq k
, set

\mathcal{L}^{m_{j}}(X)=\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{m_{j}}(X),H^{m_{j}}(X^{[m_{j}-1]})\big).

(22)

Then
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of

\mathcal{L}^{m_{k}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{k},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{L}^{m_{2}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{2},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\mathrm{GL}(p_{1},{\mathbb{Q}}),

where
p_{j}=\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905pt\pi_{m_{j}}(X)
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

Proof.

First, recall that for every
1\leq j\leq k
, we have:

\mathrm{aut}(\pi_{m_{j}}(X))\cong\mathrm{GL}(\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905pt\pi_{m_{j}}(X),{\mathbb{Q}})=\mathrm{GL}(p_{j},{\mathbb{Q}})

Next, by applying Corollary
4.2
and relation (
22
), we obtain:

\mathcal{E}(X)=\mathcal{E}(X^{[m_{k}]})\cong{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k}}(X)\rtimes{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{k}}(X).

As
{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{k}}(X)
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{aut}(\pi_{m_{k}}(X))\times\mathcal{E}(X^{[{m_{k-1}}]})\cong\mathrm{GL}(p_{m},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\mathcal{E}(X^{[m_{k-1}]})
, using Corollary
4.2
and relation (
22
) again, we get:

\mathcal{E}(X^{m_{k-1}})\cong{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k-1}}(X)\rtimes{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{k-1}}(X).

This implies that
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of
{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{k},{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k-1}}(X)\rtimes{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{k-1}}(X)
.
Similarly, since
{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{k-1}}(X)
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(p_{m_{k-1}},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\mathcal{E}(X^{[m_{k-2}]})
and

\mathcal{E}(X^{[m_{k-2}]})\cong{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k-2}}(X)\rtimes{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{k-2}}(X),

it follows that
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of

{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{k},{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k-1}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{k-1},{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k-2}}(X)\rtimes{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{k-2}}(X).

By iterating this process, we conclude that
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of

{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{k},{\mathbb{Q}})\times{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{k-1}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(m_{k-1},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\cdots\times{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{1}}(X)\rtimes{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{1}}(X).

Note that since
\pi_{m_{1}}(X),\dots,\pi_{m_{k}}(X)
are the only nontrivial homotopy groups of
X
, it follows that if
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is the Sullivan model of
X
, then
V^{i}=0
for
i<m_{1}
. Consequently, we obtain
H^{m_{1}}(\Lambda V^{\leq m_{1}-1})=0
. As a result, we deduce:

{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{1}}(X)=\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{m_{1}}(X),H^{m_{1}}(X^{[m_{1}-1]})\big)\cong\mathrm{Hom}\big(V^{m_{1}},H^{m_{1}}(\Lambda V^{\leq m_{1}-1})\big)=0.

Moreover,

{\mathcal{D}}^{m_{1}}
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(p_{1},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\mathcal{E}(\Lambda V^{\leq m_{1}-1})=\mathrm{GL}(p_{1},{\mathbb{Q}})
. Consequently, the group
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of:

\mathcal{L}^{m_{k}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{k},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{L}^{m_{2}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{2},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\mathrm{GL}(p_{1},{\mathbb{Q}}),

as desired.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
be a rational elliptic space, and let
\pi_{m_{1}}(X),\cdots,\pi_{m_{k}}(X)
be the non trivial homotopy group of
X
. If the group
\mathcal{E}(X)
is finite, then it is a subgroup of

\mathrm{GL}(p_{m_{1}},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}(p_{m_{k}},{\mathbb{Q}})
, where
p_{m_{j}}=\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905pt\pi_{m_{j}}(X)
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
4.3
, we know that
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of

\mathcal{L}^{m_{k}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{k},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{L}^{m_{2}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(p_{2},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\mathrm{GL}(p_{1},{\mathbb{Q}}).

By (
22
), we know that
\mathcal{L}^{m_{j}}(X)
is a vector space over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, for every
1\leq j\leq k
. Therefore,
\mathcal{L}^{m_{j}}(X)
does not contain any finite subgroup. Since
\mathcal{E}(X)
is finite, it follows that
\mathcal{E}(X)
is subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(p_{m_{1}},{\mathbb{Q}})\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}(p_{m_{k}},{\mathbb{Q}})
.
∎

Example 4.5
.

An elliptic space
X
is called an
F_{0}
-space
, if
H^{\text{odd}}(X;\mathbb{Q})=0
. Examples of
F_{0}
-spaces include products of even-dimensional spheres, complex Grassmannian manifolds, and homogeneous spaces
G/H
where
\text{rank }G=\text{rank }H
.

It is well-known (see
[
5
, Proposition 32.10]
) that if
X
is an elliptic space and
(\Lambda V,\partial)
its Sullivan model, then the following statements are equivalent

(1)

X
is an
F_{0}
-space.

(2)

\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905ptV^{\text{even}}=\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905ptV^{\text{odd}}
and
(\Lambda V,\partial)
is pure, i.e.,

\partial(V^{\text{even}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\partial(V^{\text{odd}})\subseteq\Lambda V^{\text{even}}

(23)

Therefore, let
X
be an
F_{0}
-space, if
m_{j}
is odd, then the vector space
\mathcal{L}^{m_{j}}(X)
, given in
(\ref{z88})
, is trivial. Indeed, working algebraically, let
(\Lambda V,\partial)
be the Sullivan model of
X
and let

\cdots\to V^{m_{j}-1}\overset{b^{m_{j}-1}}{\longrightarrow}H^{m_{j}}(\Lambda V^{\leq m_{j}-1})\to H^{m_{j}}(\Lambda V)\to\cdots

the Whitehead exact sequence of
(\Lambda V,\partial)
. As
X
is an
F_{0}
-space and
m_{j}
is odd, it follows that
H^{m_{j}}(\Lambda V)=0
. Moreover, taking into account the relations (
9
) and (
23
), it follows that
b^{m_{j}-1}=0
. As a result,
H^{m_{j}}(\Lambda V^{\leq m_{j}-1})=0
which implies that

{\mathcal{L}}^{m_{j}}(X)=\mathrm{Hom}\big(\pi_{m_{j}}(X),H^{m_{j}}(X^{[m_{j}-1]})\big)\cong\mathrm{Hom}\big(V^{m_{j}},H^{m_{j}}(\Lambda V^{\leq m_{j}-1})\big)=0.

Consequently, if
\pi_{n_{1}}(X),\cdots,\pi_{n_{k}}(X)
be the nontrivial homotopy group of
X
of even degrees and let
\pi_{m_{1}}(X),\cdots,\pi_{m_{k}}(X)
be the nontrivial homotopy group of
X
of odd degrees, then we deduce that
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a subgroup of

\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{L}^{n_{i}}(X)\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(q_{i},{\mathbb{Q}})\bigg)\times\prod_{j=1}^{k}\mathrm{GL}(p_{j},{\mathbb{Q}}).

where
p_{j}=\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905pt\pi_{m_{j}}(X)
and where
q_{j}=\mathrm{dim}\hskip 0.56905pt\pi_{n_{j}}(X)
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

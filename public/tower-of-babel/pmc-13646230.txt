Interest of luspatercept in treatment of anemia in lower-risk of myelodysplastic syndrome anemia: a single center study of 26 patients
Authors: A Elalaoui, C Girault, T Cluzeau
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMyelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) are heterogeneous clonal hematological disorders characterized by ineffective hematopoiesis resulting in peripheral cytopenias. Luspatercept, an erythroid maturation agent, represents a novel therapeutic option for the management of anemia associated with low-risk MDS, particularly in patients with ring sideroblasts and/or SF3B1 mutations.MethodsWe retrospectively studied 26 adults with low- or intermediate-risk MDS who were treated with luspatercept from 2021 to 2024 at the Nice University Hospital. Treatment was initiated at 1 mg/kg, depending on the patient.ResultsThe median age was 74 years, and 38.5% of patients had the SF3B1 mutation. Treatment was generally well tolerated, with fatigue being the most common side effect (26.9%).ConclusionLuspatercept appears to be effective in improving red blood cell production and reducing transfusion requirements in low-risk patients with MDS, with a favorable safety profile.Keywords: MDS, Anemia, Lowrisk, Luspatercept

Introduction

Myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) are a heterogeneous group of clonal hematopoietic disorders originating from multipotent myeloid stem cells. These diseases are characterized by inefficient or abnormal hematopoiesis, leading to peripheral cytopenias despite an often hypercellular bone marrow with morphological abnormalities (dysplasia) [1, 2].

Diagnosis relies primarily on cytological evaluation involving detailed analysis of peripheral blood and bone marrow smears. The diagnosis of MDS is confirmed by the presence of significant dysplasia, affecting at least 10% of the cells in one or more hematopoietic lineages [3].

Patients with low-risk MDS (LR-MDS) generally require supportive therapy to manage cytopenia, while those with high-risk MDS (HR-MDS) often require disease-modifying therapies aimed at preventing leukemic transformation and prolonging survival [4].

Luspatercept is a novel therapeutic agent that promotes erythroid maturation. It is a recombinant fusion protein that binds endogenous ligands such as GDF-11 and activin B, members of the transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) superfamily. By inhibiting the Smad2/3 signaling pathway, luspatercept facilitates terminal maturation of erythroid precursors (normoblasts) in the bone marrow [5, 6].

The recommended initial dose of luspatercept is 1.0 mg/kg, which is administered subcutaneously every three weeks. Luspatercept is approved for the treatment of transfusion-dependent anemia in adults with low-, very low-, or intermediate-risk MDS with ring sideroblasts (RS), particularly in patients who are refractory to or ineligible for erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) [9]. Luspatercept is also indicated for the treatment of transfusion-associated anemia associated with β- thalassemia. Based on the results of the international phase 3 COMMANDS clinical trial (ACE–536−MDS−002) conducted in 142 centers in 26 countries between January 2019 and August 2022, The US, Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved luspatercept (Reblozyl) in August 2023 for the treatment of anemia in patients with LR-MDS, potentially requiring red blood cell transfusions, who have not previously received ESAs. In this study, presented at the 2023 American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) annual meeting, 58.5% of patients treated first line with luspatercept were able to avoid transfusions for at least 12 weeks compared to 31.2% of patients receiving epoetin alfa, confirming the superiority of luspatercept in this indication [7, 8].

The aim of our study was to evaluate the clinical efficacy of luspatercept in the management of patients with low-risk MDS treated in the hematology department of the ARCHET I Nice University Hospital.

Methods

Design

This was a retrospective, descriptive, exhaustive, single-center study based on the medical records of 26 patients with MDS managed within the Hematology Department of the Nice University Hospital, Archet I in Nice. The primary goal of this study was to assess the response of patients to luspatercept treatment.

The study spanned four years from July 5, 2021 to July 3, 2024, allowing for systematic data collection starting from the initiation of luspatercept treatment.

Population

The study included patients aged between 30 and 94 years with a confirmed diagnosis of MDS according to the World Health Organization (WHO) 2016 classification.

Most patients received luspatercept as second-line therapy following failure of ESAs.

Baseline transfusion status was assessed according to transfusion burden and categorized as low (< 1 RBC/month), moderate (1–2 RBC/month), or high (> 2RBC/month).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

This study included patients with LR-MDS, defined according to the Revised International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS-R) as having “very low,” “low,” or “intermediate,” risk, who had received luspatercept treatment.

Patients with HR-MDS defined according to the IPSS-R score as belonging to the “high” or “very high” categories and those with incomplete medical records despite treatment with luspatercept were excluded in accordance with the inclusion criteria. These eligibility criteria were applied to ensure the reliability and validity of the analyzed data.

Treatment

Luspatercept was initiated according to institutional practice. In selected patients, a higher starting dose (1.33 mg/kg or 1.75 mg/kg) was administered upfront based on transfusion burden and clinical assessment. The dose was subsequently adjusted according to each patient’s response to treatment and tolerability.

Endpoints

Therapeutic response was assessed according to the 2023 International Working Group (IWG) response criteria. The primary endpoint was the erythroid response, as defined by the 2023 IWG criteria. Secondary endpoints included erythroid hematologic improvement, reduction in red blood cell transfusion burden, changes in hemoglobin levels, and duration of response.

Data collection

Data were retrospectively collected from electronic medical records using the ORBIS and CLINICOM systems. Extracted variables included demographic characteristics (age and sex), disease characteristics (WHO 2016 classification and IPSS-R score), biological parameters, transfusion status, transfusion burden, therapeutic data related to luspatercept (starting dose and dose adjustments), and treatment response criteria according to IWG guidelines.

Data were entered into a standardized Microsoft Excel database to ensure consistency and quality of data collection. Medical records with missing data were excluded from the analysis.

Stats

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Software (version 31.0.0.0, IBM Corp), and continuous variables were expressed as means, standard deviations, or medians according to their distribution, whereas categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages.

Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test and Fisher exact test.

A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

Between July 5, 2021, and July 3, 2024, 26 patients were treated with luspatercept at the Nice University Hospital, Archet I in Nice.

The median age of the patients was 74 years, and the male-to-female sex ratio was 0.85.

Regarding cytogenetic and molecular characteristics, 80.76% of the patients had a favorable karyotype and 38.46% carried the SF3B1 mutation. Based on the IPSS-R, most of patients (approximately 80.77%) were considered low risk.

Treatment

Luspatercept was administered sub-cutaneous every 3 weeks.

Of the 26 patients included, 10 (38.46%) initially received a maximum dose of 1.75 mg/kg, while the remaining 16 patients (61.54%) initiated treatment at a standard dose of 1 mg/kg.

Of these 16 patients, 4 (15.38% of the total) continued treatment at this dose without any modification, whereas 12 (46.15% of the total) required a gradual dose escalation.

Among those who underwent dose escalation, 6 (23% of the total) reached a maximum dose of 1.75 mg/kg, and 6 (23% of the total) were stabilized at an intermediate dose of 1.33 mg/kg based on clinical response and individual tolerance.

Efficacy

The mean duration of response to luspatercept was 53.82 weeks with a range of 2 to 152 weeks (Fig. 2).

Distribution of patients according to the treatment efficacy of Luspatercept

According to the updated IWG 2023 criteria, 53.84% of patients (N = 26) achieved a complete erythrocyte response. In contrast, 30.77% had no response, whereas 15.39% had no usable clinical documentation.

Among patients with RS, (N = 15), 50% achieved a complete response according to IWG 2023 criteria. Ficher exact test (Odds ratio = 1.17) and the chi-square test showed non statistically significant difference compared to patients without RS (×2 = 0.04, P = 0.84) suggesting that the presence of RS was not associated with a better response in this sample.

Among patients with the SF3B1 mutation (N = 10), 70% achieved a complete response according to IWG 2023 criteria, compared with 43.75% of patients without the mutation (N = 16), Fisher exact test (odds ratio = 3) and chi-square test, showed that this difference was not significant (×2 = 1.39, p = 0.24) and Fisher exact test (odds ratio = 3).

Regarding prior lines of therapy, the two treatment naïve patients both achieved a complete response (100%), whereas among the 18 patients receiving luspatercept as second line therapy following ESA treatment, 10 achieved a complete response (55.55%). Owing to the small size of the naïve group, the Fisher exact test was used, which did not show a statistically significant difference (p = 0.45).

Discussion

ESAs remain the standard first-line therapy for LR-MDS. However, their efficacy is often limited, especially in patients with RS or SF3B1 mutations, in which inflammatory cytokines contribute to ineffective erythropoiesis [9–11]. Luspatercept, a recombinant fusion protein that targets TGF-B ligands and modulating Smad 2/3 signaling, promotes late erythroid maturation and has emerged as a therapeutic option for this group of patients [5].

In our study, more than half of the patients (53.8%) achieved a complete erythroid response according to the 2023 IWG criteria. Non-severe (grade 3–4) adverse events were observed, and the side effects were generally mild, occurring in approximately one-third of cases. Notably, 38.5% of our cohort carried the SF3B1 mutations.

These findings are consistent with the stepwise clinical development of the luspatercept. Phase II data (PACE-MDS 2017) first demonstrated dose dependent hematologic improvement [12], subsequently confirmed in the pivotal MEDALIST trial (2020), which showed superiority over placebo in LR-MDS with RS (38% vs. 13%, p < 0.001) [9]. More recently, the COMMANDS trial (2023) extended these results to ESA-naïve patients, demonstrating superiority over epoetin alfa (59% vs. 31%, p < 0.0001) [7].

Although slightly lower than that in the COMMANDS trial, the response rate in our real-world cohort remained comparable despite greater population heterogeneity and prior treatment exposure. Overall, these data support the robustness of the efficacy of luspatercept in clinical trials and routine practice.

Our results confirm existing evidence by showing similar response rates in routine practice. However, the observed safety profile should be interpreted with caution owing to the retrospective nature of the study and possible under-reporting of adverse events. A considerable proportion of patients initiated treatment with 1.75 mg/kg from the first cycle, reflecting real-world clinical practice driven by disease severity and high transfusion burden, with the aim of achieving a faster response in selected patients.

The main limitation of this study was the relatively short follow-up period of a subset of patients, which limited the robust assessment of long-term outcomes, including overall survival and progression -free survival. In addition, transfusion independence at standardized time points (8 and 12 weeks) could not be systematically evaluated due to incomplete or non-standardized transfusion records, limiting direct comparison with pivotal trials such as MEDALIST and COMMANDS.

Furthermore, the absence of baseline erythropoietin values, incomplete transfusion records, and incomplete clinical documentation in some patients may have affected the assessment of luspatercept efficacy. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the treatment outcomes.

Subgroup analyses should be considered exploratory because of the limited sample size and resulting limited statistical power.

Conclusion

Luspatercept appears to be an effective treatment option in real-world practice, with an erythroid efficacy broadly consistent with pivotal trials and an acceptable safety profile. However, these results should be interpreted with caution due to the retrospective design, potential under-reporting of adverse events, and incomplete data, particularly regarding standardized transfusion-independent endpoints.

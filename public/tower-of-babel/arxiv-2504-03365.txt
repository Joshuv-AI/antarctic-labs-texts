Application of methods of quasicrystals theory to entire functions of exponential growth
Sergii Yu.Favorov

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
f
be an entire almost periodic function with zeros in a horizontal strip of finite width; for example, any exponential polynomial with purely imaginary exponents is such a function. Let
\mu
be the measure on the set of zeros of
f
whose masses coincide with multiplicities of zeros. We define the Fourier transform in the sense of distributions for
\mu
and prove that it is a pure point measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
whose complex masses correspond to coefficients of Dirichlet series of the logarithmic derivative of
f
. Bases on this description and Meyer’s theorem on quasicrystals, we give a simple necessary and sufficient condition for
f
to be a finite product of sines.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 42A75, 42A38, 52C23

Keywords: exponential polynomial, zero set in a strip, almost periodic function, Fourier quasicrystal

1.
Introduction

In
[
22
]
A.Olevskii and A.Ulanovskii established 1-1 connection between zeros of real–rooted exponential polynomials and Fourier quasicrystals. They proved that for the exponential polynomial

(1)

P(z)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}q_{j}e^{2\pi i\omega_{j}z},\qquad q_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad\omega_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad N\in{\mathbb{N}}.

with a zero set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
the measure

(2)

\mu_{A}=\sum_{n}\delta_{a_{n}},\quad A=\{a_{n}\},

is the Fourier quasicrystal, and conversely, every set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
for which the measure (
2
) is a Fourier quasicrystal, is the zero set
of some exponential polynomial (
1
). Note that previously a nontrivial example of the Fourier quasicrystal of the form (
2
) whose support has only a finite intersection with any arithmetical progression was found by P.Kurasov and P.Sarnak
[
14
]
.

Recall that a complex or real measure
\mu
is called the
Fourier quasicrystal
if it is tempered distribution, has locally finite
support (that is, its intersection with any compact set is finite), its Fourier transform in the sense of distributions is also is a measure
with locally finite support, and moreover, both measures
|\mu|
,
|\hat{\mu}|
are temperate distributions.
Here and below
|\nu|(E)
means the variation of the complex measure
\nu
on the set
E
.

Recently, Fourier quasicrystals and connected with them real-rooted exponential polynomials are studied very actively (see, for example,
[
21
,
15
,
2
]
). In fact, Fourier quasicrystals are the form of Poisson formulas (see below), the latter were used in particular by D. Radchenko and M. Viazovska in
[
23
]
.

L.Alon, A.Cohen, K.Vinzant
[
1
]
established a connection between real–rooted exponential polynomials and Lee-Yang polynomials of many variables. In
[
8
]
the result of Olevskii and Ulanovskii was generalized to Dirichlet series, it was found a full description of zero sets of absolutely convergent real–rooted Dirichlet series

(3)

Q(z)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}q_{\omega}e^{2\pi i\omega z},\qquad q_{\omega}\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad\omega\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|q_{\omega}|<\infty,

with bounded
\Omega
. F.Goncalves
[
12
]
adapted the techniques of Olevskii and Ulanovskii for study of arbitrary tempered measures on
{\mathbb{R}}

with a pure point Fourier transform and got similar results for such measures. In
[
10
]
we applied the above description of Fourier quasicrystals
and Meyer’s theorem on quasicrystals
[
19
]
to prove a simple criterion for the exponential polynomials (
1
) and Dirichlet series (
3
) to be a finite product of sines. But there we assumed that these polynomials and series have only real zeros.

In
[
9
]
we applied the method of Olevskii and Ulanovskii to measures of the form (
2
) where we replace the condition
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
with
A\subset\{z=x+iy:\,|y|\leq H\}
. We slightly changed the definition of the Fourier transform of a measure, introduced
the measure
\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}
, and proved that the above results on zeros of exponential sums and Dirichlet series are valid in this case as well. Therefore it is natural to use this result to remove the condition of reality of zeros in
[
10
]
. Moreover, we would like to get the above criterion for the wider class of entire almost periodic functions.

To achieve this result, two obstacles must be overcome. First, the Dirichlet series of a holomorphic almost periodic function need not converge. Second, the inverse Fourier transform of the measure
\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}
is not the measure
\mu_{A}
, which makes direct application of Meyer’s theorem impossible. Solving these problems, we obtain the following:

Theorem
.

Let
f(z)
be an entire almost periodic function of exponential growth with zeros in a strip

S_{(\alpha,\beta)}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\,\alpha<\Im z<\beta\},\qquad-\infty<\alpha\leq 0\leq\beta<\infty,

and

(4)

f^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\sim\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}h^{+}_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma z},\ z\in S_{(\beta,\infty)},\qquad f^{\prime}(z)/f(z)\sim\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}h^{-}_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma z},\ z\in S_{(-\infty,\alpha)}.

Then the condition

(5)

\sum_{|\gamma|<r}|h^{+}_{\gamma}|+\sum_{|\gamma|<r}|h^{-}_{\gamma}|<Kr\quad\forall\ r>1,

where
K
is a constant, is necessary and sufficient for the identity

(6)

f(z)=Ce^{iaz}\prod_{j=1}^{J}\sin(\alpha_{j}z+\beta_{j})

with some
C\in{\mathbb{C}},\ J\in{\mathbb{N}},\ a,\,\alpha_{j},\,\beta_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Remark 1
. Obviously, this is the case when support of
\mu
is a finite union of arithmetic progressions.
There are many papers (e.g.,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
16
]
) where various conditions have been found for support of
\mu
to be just like that. But in these papers, the distances between points of support of
\mu
or between points of support of its Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
are assumed to be uniformly separated from zero. The exception is Meyer’s theorem
[
19
]
, where support of
\mu
is an arbitrary locally finite set and
\hat{\mu}
is a slowly increasing Radon measure with arbitrary support. The proof of our theorem is based on it.

Remark 2
. The representations (
4
) for every entire almost periodic function of exponential growth with zeros in the strip

S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
will be proved below.

Almost all papers related to Fourier quasicrystals use (in explicit or implicit form) properties of almost periodic functions and measures.
In section
2
we give definitions of these objects and present some of their properties.
In section
3
we recall some definitions related to the Fourier transform of functions and measures.
In section
4
we give a proof of the theorem.

Definition 1
.

A continuous function
f(x)
on the real line
{\mathbb{R}}

is almost periodic if for any

\varepsilon>0
the set of
\varepsilon
-almost periods

E_{\varepsilon}=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|f(x+\tau)-f(x)|<\varepsilon\}

is relatively dense, i.e.,
E_{\varepsilon}\cap(x,x+L)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and some
L
depending on
\varepsilon
;
a continuous function
g(z)
on the open strip

S_{(\alpha,\beta)}=\{z=x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}:\,-\infty\leq\alpha<y<\beta\leq+\infty\}

is almost periodic if for any
\alpha^{\prime},\,\beta^{\prime},\,\alpha<\alpha^{\prime}<\beta^{\prime}<\beta
, and

\varepsilon>0
the set of
\varepsilon
-almost periods

E_{\alpha^{\prime},\,\beta^{\prime},\varepsilon}=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}:\,\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha^{\prime}\leq y\leq\beta^{\prime}}|g(x+\tau+iy)-g(x+iy)|<\varepsilon\}

is relatively dense with
L
depending on
\varepsilon,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}
.

Note that every exponential sum (
1
) and absolutely convergent Dirichlet series (
3
)
are almost periodic in the whole plane
S_{(-\infty,+\infty)}
.

Properties of almost periodic functions
:

a) a finite sum, a product, and a linear combination of almost periodic functions on
{\mathbb{R}}
is also almost periodic on
{\mathbb{R}}
;
a finite sum, a product, and a linear combination of almost periodic functions in a strip
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
is also almost periodic in
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
,

b) each almost periodic function
f(x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is bounded and has unbounded support unless
f(x)\not\equiv 0
; each almost periodic function
g(z)

on
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
is bounded on every substrip
S_{(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})},\ \alpha<\alpha^{\prime}<\beta^{\prime}<\beta
, and has unbounded support unless
g(z)\not\equiv 0
,

c) the Fourier series corresponds to each almost periodic function
f(x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}

f(x)\sim\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}a_{\omega}e^{2\pi i\omega x},

where the spectrum
\Omega=\{\omega:\,a_{\omega}\neq 0\}
is a countable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
;
the Fourier series corresponds to each almost periodic function
g(z)
in
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}

g(x+iy)\sim\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}a_{\omega}(y)e^{2\pi i\omega x},\quad\alpha<y<\beta,

where the Fourier coefficients
a_{\omega}(y)
are continuous functions on
(\alpha,\beta)
and the spectrum
\Omega=\{\omega:\,a_{\omega}(y)\not\equiv 0\}
is a countable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
; if, in addition, the function
g(z)
is holomorphic on
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
, the Fourier series turns into the Dirichlet series

g(z)\sim\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}h_{\omega}e^{2\pi i\omega z},

where the coefficients
h_{\omega}=a_{\omega}(y)e^{-2\pi\omega y}
do not depend on
y
,

d) for every countable
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}
there are coefficients
\kappa_{\omega}(n)
,

0\leq\kappa_{\omega}(n)\leq 1,\qquad\kappa_{\omega}(n)\to 1\quad\text{as }\,n\to\infty,\qquad\#\{\omega\in\Omega:\,\kappa_{\omega}(n)\neq 0\}<\infty\quad\text{for each fixed }\,n\in{\mathbb{N}},

such that for almost periodic
f(x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}
with spectrum in
\Omega

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\kappa_{\omega}(n)a_{\omega}e^{2\pi i\omega x}\to f(x)\quad(n\to\infty)

uniformly with respect to
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and for almost periodic
g(z)
on
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
with spectrum in
\Omega

(7)

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\kappa_{\omega}(n)a_{\omega}(y)e^{2\pi i\omega x}\to g(x+iy)\quad(n\to\infty)

uniformly with respect to
x+iy\in S_{(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})},\ \alpha<\alpha^{\prime}<\beta^{\prime}<\beta,
(Bochner–Feyer approximation).

In the following items we assume that a function
g(z)
is holomorphic in the strips
S_{(\alpha,\beta)},\ -\infty\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\infty
.

e) If
g(z)
almost periodic in a strip
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
then its derivative
g^{\prime}(z)
is also almost periodic in the same strip, and its spectrum is a subset of spectrum
g
,

f) if
g(z)
and
g_{1}(z)
are almost periodic in
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
and their ratio
g(z)/g_{1}(z)
is holomorphic in
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
, then it is almost periodic in
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
,

g) if
g(z)
is bounded and almost periodic in a strip
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
with
\beta=\infty
, then its spectrum
\Omega
is non-negative;
if
g(z)
is bounded and almost periodic in a strip
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
with
\alpha=-\infty
, then its spectrum
\Omega
is non-positive,

h)if
g(z)
is almost periodic in the strip
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
with
\beta=\infty
and its spectrum
\Omega
is non-negative, then the limit
\lim_{y\to\infty}g(x+iy)
exists uniformly in
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and it is equal to the free term of the corresponding Dirichlet series; if
g(z)
is almost periodic in the strip
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
with
\alpha=-\infty
and its spectrum
\Omega
is non-positive,
then the limit
\lim_{y\to-\infty}g(x+iy)
exists uniformly in
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and it is equal to the free term of the corresponding Dirichlet series,

i) if
g(z)
is an entire almost periodic function of exponential growth whose zeros are in some horizontal strip of finite width, then its spectrum
\Omega
is bounded and
\inf\Omega\in\Omega,\ \sup\Omega\in\Omega
,

j) if
A
is the zero set of an almost periodic in
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
function
g(z)
then for any
\varepsilon>0

and
\alpha<\alpha^{\prime}<\beta^{\prime}<\beta
there is
m=m(\varepsilon,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})>0
such that the conditions

and
\dist(z,A)>\varepsilon
implies

|g(z)|\geq m.

k) for an almost periodic in
S_{(\alpha,\beta)}
function

the numbers

\#\{z=x+iy\in S_{(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})}\,:\,t\leq x\leq t+1,\,g(z)=0\},\quad\alpha<\alpha^{\prime}<\beta^{\prime}<\beta,

are bounded uniformly in

; all zeros are counted according to their multiplicities, hence
multiplicities of zeros are uniformly bounded in every substrip

.

Here we do not give a general definition of almost periodic measures on a strip (see
[

]
,
[

]
), but restrict ourselves to the case of measures on
{\mathbb{R}}
(
[

]

Definition 2
.

A measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}
is almost periodic (in the sense of distributions) if the function

\mu\star\phi(t)=\int\psi(t-x)\mu(dx)

is almost periodic for any
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
.

It is easily seen that any linear combinations of almost periodic measures is almost periodic, and the support of every almost periodic measure
\mu\not\equiv 0
is unbounded.

3.
Some properties of the Fourier transform

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the space of
C^{\infty}
-functions with compact support on
{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mathcal{S}
be the Schwartz space of all
C^{\infty}
-functions
\varphi
with finite norms

\mathcal{N}_{k}(\varphi)=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}(1+|x|)^{k}\max_{0\leq m\leq k}|\varphi^{(m)}(x)|\quad\forall\,k=0,1,2,\dots,

and the space of tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be the space of all linear functional on
\mathcal{S}
, which continuous with respect to the topology generated by these norms.
The Fourier transform

(8)

\hat{\varphi}(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi ixt}dt

is a continuous bijection of
\mathcal{S}
onto
\mathcal{S}
, and for
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}

(9)

\varphi(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\hat{\varphi}(x)e^{2\pi ixt}dx,

(see, e.g.,
[
26
]
).

For every
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
its Fourier transform
\hat{\Phi}
is defined by the equality

(10)

\hat{\Phi}(\varphi)=\Phi(\hat{\varphi})\quad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{D}.

Since
\mathcal{D}
is a dense subset of
\mathcal{S}
, we see that (
10
) is valid for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
. Therefore,
\hat{\Phi}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
for all
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
It follows from (
8
)–(
10
) that

\widehat{\hat{\Phi}}(x)=\Phi(-x).

When
\Phi
and
\hat{\Phi}
are tempered measure, and

\Phi=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda},\qquad\hat{\Phi}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma}

with countable
\Lambda,\,\Gamma
, then equality (
10
) has the form

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\varphi(\gamma)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}a_{\lambda}\hat{\varphi}(\lambda),\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{D},

and is called
generalized Poisson formula
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
the function

(11)

\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi izt}dt=\widehat{(\varphi(t)e^{2\pi yt})}(x),\quad z=x+iy,

is the entire extension of
\hat{\varphi}
. It is easy to check that for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
s>0
uniformly in
y\in[-s,s]

(12)

\hat{\varphi}^{c}(x+iy)=O(|x|^{-k}),\qquad|x|\to\infty,

In the case of
\supp\mu\subset\mathcal{S}_{(\alpha,\beta)},-\infty<\alpha<\beta<\infty
, and
\mu
such that

(13)

\log\left(|\mu|\{z\in\mathcal{S}_{(\alpha,\beta)}:\,|\Re z|<r\}\right)=O(\log r),

we defined the distribution
\hat{\mu}^{c}
on
{\mathbb{R}}
in
[
9
]
by the equality

\hat{\mu}^{c}(\varphi)=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{(\alpha,\beta)}}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\mu(dz)\quad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{D}.

4.
The proof of the Theorem

It is easy to check (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
) that functions of the form (
6
) satisfy the condition (
5
). Prove the converse statement.

Let
f(z)
be an entire almost periodic function of exponential growth with zeros in
\mathcal{S}_{(\alpha,\beta)}
for
-\infty<\alpha\leq 0\leq\beta<\infty
.
Since
f(z)
has no zeros in the half-planes
\mathcal{S}_{(-\infty,\alpha)}
and
\mathcal{S}_{(\beta,\infty)}
, then by the properties e) and f),
the function
f^{\prime}/f
is almost periodic on these half-planes; by c), we get representations (
4
) there.

Let
\Omega
be the spectrum of
f
. By the property i),
\Omega
is bounded and
\omega_{0}=\inf_{\omega\in\Omega}\omega\in\Omega
. The function
f_{1}(z)=e^{-2\pi i\omega_{0}z}f(z)
has non-negative spectrum, therefore by the property h), we have

\lim_{y\to\infty}f_{1}(x+iy)=h_{\omega_{0}}\qquad\text{uniformly in}\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since
\omega_{0}\in\Omega
, we get that
h_{\omega_{0}}\neq 0
. By e), the spectrum of
f^{\prime}
is a subset of spectrum
f
, hence the function
f_{2}(z)=e^{-2\pi i\omega_{0}z}f^{\prime}(z)
has non-negative spectrum too, and the limit

\lim_{y\to\infty}f_{2}(x+iy)

exists and finite. Therefore the function
f^{\prime}/f=f_{2}/f_{1}
is bounded for
y
large enough. It follows from the property g) that the set
\Gamma^{*}
from (
4
) is a subset of
[0,+\infty)
. By the similar way, we get the set
\Gamma_{*}
is a subset of
(-\infty,0]
.

It follows from k) and j) that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
and fixed
\eta>0
there are
m=m(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta,\eta)>0
and sequences
L_{k}\to+\infty,\,L^{\prime}_{k}\to-\infty
such that

|f(x+iy)|>m\quad\text{for}\quad x=L_{k}\quad\text{or}\quad x=L^{\prime}_{k},\quad\alpha-\eta\leq y\leq\beta+\eta.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
, and
\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)
be defined in (
11
).
Consider the integrals
I_{k}
of the function
\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)f^{\prime}(z)f^{-1}(z)
over the boundaries of the rectangles

\Pi_{k}=\{z=x+iy:\,L^{\prime}_{k}<x<L_{k},\,\alpha-\eta<y<\beta+\eta\}.

Taking into account (
12
), we get that these integrals tend to the difference

\int_{i(\alpha-\eta)-\infty}^{i(\alpha-\eta)+\infty}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)f^{\prime}(z)f^{-1}(z)dz-\int_{i(\beta+\eta)-\infty}^{i(\beta+\eta)+\infty}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)f^{\prime}(z)f^{-1}(z)dz=:I_{*}-I^{*}

as
k\to\infty
. On the other hand,
I_{k}
tend to the sum

(14)

2\pi i\sum_{\lambda:f(\lambda)=0}\text{Res}_{\lambda}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)f^{\prime}(z)f^{-1}(z)=2\pi i\sum_{\lambda:f(\lambda)=0}a(\lambda)\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)=2\pi i\int_{S_{(\alpha,\beta)}}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\mu_{A}(dz),

where
a(\lambda)
is the multiplicity of zero of
f(z)
at the point
\lambda
, and
\mu_{A}=\sum_{\lambda:f(\lambda)=0}\delta_{\lambda}
, where every zero
\lambda
appears
a(\lambda)
times. From k) it follows that the condition (
13
) is valid.

It follows from (
4
), (
7
), and (
12
) that for some
\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}(n),\,\kappa_{\gamma}^{-}(n)
we get

I_{*}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}\kappa_{\gamma}^{-}(n)h^{-}_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma(x+i(\alpha-\eta))}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(x+i(\alpha-\eta))dx\\
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}\kappa_{\gamma}^{-}(n)h^{-}_{\gamma}e^{-2\pi\gamma(\alpha-\eta)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(x+i(\alpha-\eta))e^{2\pi i\gamma x}dx,

I^{*}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}(n)h^{+}_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma(x+i\beta+\eta)}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(x+i(\beta+\eta))dx\\
=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}(n)h^{+}_{\gamma}e^{-2\pi\gamma(\beta+\eta)}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(x+i(\beta+\eta))e^{2\pi i\gamma x}dx.

Combining (
9
) and (
11
), we obtain

I_{*}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}\cap\text{supp }\varphi}\kappa_{\gamma}^{-}(n)h^{-}_{\gamma}\varphi(\gamma),\qquad I^{*}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}\cap\text{supp }\varphi}\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}(n)h^{+}_{\gamma}\varphi(\gamma).

It follows from (
5
) that coefficients
h_{\gamma}^{\pm}
in (
4
) satisfy the condition

\sum_{|\gamma|<r}|h^{+}_{\gamma}|+\sum_{|\gamma|<r}|h^{-}_{\gamma}|<\infty\quad\text{for all}\ r<\infty.

Using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and the property d), we get

I_{*}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}\cap\text{supp }\varphi}h^{-}_{\gamma}\varphi(\gamma),\qquad I^{*}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}\cap\text{supp }\varphi}h^{+}_{\gamma}\varphi(\gamma).

Combining (
11
), (
10
), and (
14
), we obtain

\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}(\varphi)=\int\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\mu_{A}(dz)=\frac{I^{-}-I^{+}}{2\pi i}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}\cap\text{supp }\varphi}\frac{ih^{+}_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\varphi(\gamma)-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}\cap\text{supp }\varphi}\frac{ih^{-}_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\varphi(\gamma).

By (
5
), we get for any
r\in(1,\infty)
and any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
with support in
[-r,r]

|\hat{\mu}^{c}_{A}(\varphi)|\leq C\sup_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi(t)|,\qquad C=C(r)<\infty.

Therefore, the distribution
\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}
has an extension to a linear functional on the space of continuous functions
g
on
[-r,r]
such that
g(-r)=g(r)=0
with the same estimate.
This means that
\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}
is a measure of the form

(15)

\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}\frac{ih^{+}_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\delta_{\gamma}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{*}}\frac{ih^{-}_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\delta_{\gamma}.

Set

R(r)=\sum_{|\gamma|<r}|h_{\gamma}^{+}|+\sum_{|\gamma|<r}|h_{\gamma}^{-}|.

By (
5
), we get

(16)

R(r)=|\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}|(-r,r)=O(r)\qquad(r\to\infty).

Hence for any
\varepsilon>0

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}\setminus\{0\}}|h_{\gamma}^{+}|e^{-2\pi\gamma\varepsilon}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi r\varepsilon}R(dr)=2\pi\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}R(r)e^{-2\pi r\varepsilon}dt<\infty.

Therefore the Dirichlet series

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{*}}h_{\gamma}^{+}e^{2\pi i\gamma z}

absolutely converges on every half-plane
\Im z>\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Hence this sum is the analytic extension of the function
f^{\prime}(z)/f(z)
,
and
f(z)\neq 0
for
y>0
.
By the same way we get
f(z)\neq 0
for
y<0
. So,
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, and the measure
\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}
coincides with the measure
\hat{\mu}_{A}
.

By (
15
), we also have for any

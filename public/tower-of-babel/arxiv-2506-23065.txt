spatial decorrelation of KPZ from narrow wedge
Yu Gu and Fei Pu

Abstract
. We study the spatial decorrelation of the solution to the KPZ equation with narrow wedge initial data. For fixed t > 0 t>0 , we determine the decay rate of the spatial covariance function, showing that Cov ⁡ [ h ⁡ ( t , x ) , h ⁡ ( t , 0 ) ] ∼ t x \mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]\sim\frac{t}{x} as x → ∞ x\to\infty . In addition, we prove that the finite-dimensional distributions of the properly rescaled spatial average of the height function converge to those of a Brownian motion.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and main result

The KPZ equation, introduced by Kardar, Parisi and Zhang
[
17
]
in their study of fluctuations in growing interfaces subject to random perturbations, is a non-linear stochastic partial differential equation formally written as

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h(t,x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}h(t,x)+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h(t,x))^{2}+\xi(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R},

where
\xi
is a space-time white noise. The Cole-Hopf solution to the KPZ equation (
1.1
) is defined as

\displaystyle h(t,x)=\log Z(t,x),

where
Z
is the solution to the stochastic heat equation (SHE)

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z(t,x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}Z(t,x)+Z(t,x)\xi(t,x).

Throughout the paper, we work with the narrow wedge initial data for KPZ, namely,
Z(0,x)=\delta_{0}(x)
,
where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac measure at
0
.

Our goal is to study the correlation structure of the spatial process
\{h(t,x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
for fixed
t>0
. In particular, we are interested in the decay rate of
\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]
as
x\to\infty
. The question is motivated by the following two observations.

First, for the Airy
1
and Airy
2
processes, which are the scaling limits of the KPZ equation with flat and narrow wedge initial conditions respectively, the decay rates of the covariance are well understood. Indeed, Indeed, Prähofer-Spohn
[
22
]
showed that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[\mathcal{A}_{2}(x),\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)]\sim x^{-2},\quad\text{as $x\to\infty$},

and Basu et al.
[
4
]
have recently derived that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[\mathcal{A}_{1}(x),\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)]=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}(1+o(1))x^{3}},\quad\text{as $x\to\infty$}.

Here and throughout,
a\sim b
means
\tfrac{a}{b}\to 1
in the relevant limit. Interpreting
\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}(x)
as describing fluctuations of the free energy of a continuum directed random polymer (CDRP)
[
2
]
starting from
x
, with the endpoint either moving freely (Airy
1
) or fixed at the origin (Airy
2
), one sees that the difference in decorrelation rates between (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) arises from path interactions near the terminal point. In the Airy
2
case, the two polymer paths—starting from
x
and
0
—are both conditioned to end at the origin and hence experience a shared random environment near the endpoint, resulting in stronger correlations. Viewing the KPZ equation as a prelimiting model, it is known that, under the flat initial condition,
\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]
decays exponentially as
x\to\infty
, as shown via Poincaré inequality and the estimate on the Malliavin derivative of the solution to the stochastic heat equation (see Chen et al.
[
8
]
for an upper bound). It is thus natural to ask whether a result analogous to (
1.3
) holds for the KPZ equation with narrow wedge initial data, and whether there is a meaningful connection to the behavior described in (
1.3
).

Second, spatial decorrelation plays a central role in the studies of spatial averages of SPDEs and the associated central limit theorem. When the field decorrelates sufficiently fast, one naturally expects a Gaussian limit; in contrast, slowly decorrelating fields may give rise to non-Gaussian behavior. As previously noted, narrow wedge initial data induces strong correlations through the influence of the noise near the origin. Although
[
23
]
established that the spatial averages of the Airy
1
and Airy
2
processes both exhibit Gaussian fluctuations, it is natural to ask whether similar Gaussian behavior persists for the KPZ equation itself, given the strong correlations induced by the narrow wedge initial condition.

Before presenting the main result, we first note that by a shear invariance of the white noise, it is well-known that
h(t,x)-\log p_{t}(x)=\log\tfrac{Z(t,x)}{p_{t}(x)}
is stationary in the
x-
variable. As a result, for each
t>0
fixed,
h(t,x)-\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)]
is stationary in
x
. Here is our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix any
t>0
. As
x\to\infty
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]\sim\frac{t}{x}.

The above result shows that the spatial covariance function of the KPZ equation with narrow wedge initial data is
not
integrable. Consequently, the spatial average of the solution cannot be normalized by the standard central limit scaling. In fact, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

As
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\log N}}\int_{0}^{N}\left(h(t,x)-\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)]\right)\mathrm{d}x\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\xrightarrow{\rm fdd}\sqrt{2}{\rm B},

where
\rm B
denotes the standard Brownian motion, and the symbol
\xrightarrow{\rm fdd}
refers to convergence of finite-dimensional distributions.

The
\log N
factor in the above normalization, which also appears in the CLT for spatial average of SHE with Dirac initial data (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
),
is due to the
\frac{1}{x}
-decay rate in Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, the limiting Gaussian process,
\sqrt{2}{\rm B}
, coincides with that in
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
.
As we will see in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, somewhat unexpectedly, the first Wiener chaos of the spatial average dominates and gives rise to the limiting Gaussian behavior described in Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Context and discussion

The study of SHE and KPZ has seen tremendous progress during the past years, and a comprehensive review of the literature is beyond the scope of this paper. The most physically relevant regime involves simultaneously rescaling time and space so as to observe nontrivial fluctuations. In this regard, we refer to the reviews
[
11
,
24
,
25
]
, the recent developments
[
12
,
19
,
27
,
28
]
and the references cited there.

The focus of this paper lies in a different direction and may be viewed as a continuation of a series of works by the second author and collaborators
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. It is also closely connected to a broader body of recent research on spatial averages and central limit theorems for stochastic heat- and wave-type equations; see, for instance,
[
5
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
21
]
and references therein.

There are relatively few tools available for the direct analysis of the KPZ equation. A key ingredient in our analysis is the use of the Clark–Ocone formula, which, in some sense, serves as an analogue of the mild formulation for the SHE. It allows one to express the centered height function as an Itô integral, where the integrand involves a conditional expectation of the midpoint density of the continuum directed polymer. This reformulation reduces the problem of estimating
\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]
to analyzing a conditioned version of the overlap between two polymer paths connecting
(t,x)\to(0,0)
and
(t,0)\to(0,0)
respectively. In the case of narrow wedge initial data, this analysis turns out to be surprisingly delicate, as the dominant contribution to the covariance
\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]
for large
x
arises from the effect of the noise near the origin.

One may ask if the
\tfrac{1}{x}
decay obtained in Theorem
1.1
is related in any way to the
\tfrac{1}{x^{2}}
decay in (
1.3
), or if the
\tfrac{t}{x}
factor on the right-hand side of (
1.5
) reflects the 1:2:3 scaling. The answer is likely no – or at least not directly. The two limits of
x\to\infty
and
t\to\infty
do not commute: in light of the weak convergence of
2^{1/3}t^{-1/3}(h(t,t^{2/3}\cdot)+\tfrac{t}{24})
to the
\mathcal{A}_{2}(\cdot)
as
t\to\infty
(see
[
28
, Theorem 1.7]
), interchanging the limits
x\to\infty
and
t\to\infty
leads to inconsistent conclusion:

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2/3}}{t^{1/3}x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty\leftarrow x}}{{\sim}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}\left[2^{1/3}t^{-1/3}h(t,t^{2/3}x),\,2^{1/3}t^{-1/3}h(t,0)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Cov}[\mathcal{A}_{2}(x),\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\to\infty}}{{\sim}}\frac{1}{x^{2}}.

On the other hand, one could also try to use (
3.2
) to study the decorrelation of the KPZ solution in the joint limit where both time and space tend to infinity, by carefully analyzing the conditioned overlap of two paths from the CDRP. It would be interesting to establish a direct connection between the decorrelation of the Airy process and the geometric overlap of polymer paths.

2.
Preliminary

This section sets up the framework for the analysis of SHE. Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
.
The Gaussian family
\{\xi(\phi)\}_{\phi\in\mathcal{H}}
formed by the Wiener integrals

\xi(h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(s,y)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)

defines an
isonormal Gaussian process
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
In this framework we apply the Malliavin calculus (see Nualart
[
20
]
).
Denote by
D
the derivative operator.
Let
\{\mathcal{F}_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}
denote the filtration generated by
the space-time white noise
\xi
. The solution to the stochastic heat equation (
1.2
) with the Dirac initial data satisfies

\displaystyle Z(t,x)=p_{t}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(x-y)Z(s,y)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y),

where
p_{t}(x)=(2\pi t)^{-1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/(2t)}
. Moreover, the Malliavin derivative of
Z

satisfies the following stochastic integral equation: for
s\in(0\,,t)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle D_{s,y}Z(t,x)=p_{t-s}(x-y)Z(s,y)+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-r}(x-z)D_{s,y}Z(r,z)\,\xi({\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}z}),

(see Chen et al.
[
6
, Proposition 5.1]
). Let

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y)=\frac{D_{s,y}Z(t,x)}{Z(s,y)},\quad\text{for $s<t$ and $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Then it satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y)=p_{t-s}(x-y)+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-r}(x-z)\mathcal{G}(r,z;s,y)\,\xi({\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}z}).

In other words,
\{\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y):0<s<t,x,y\in\mathbb{R}\}
solves the family of stochastic partial differential equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y)+\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y)\xi(t,x),\\
\mathcal{G}(s,x;s,y)=\delta_{0}(x-y).\end{cases}

Equivalently,
\mathcal{G}
is the Green’s function of the SHE; see
[
1
]
. Furthermore, introduce the scaled Green’ function

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)=\frac{\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y)}{p_{t-s}(x-y)}\quad\text{for $s<t$ and $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$}.

By the translation invariance of the white noise (see Proposition 2.3 of
[
1
]
),
\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)
has the same distribution as

\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t-s;x-y;0,0)
and for any
T>0
and
k\in\mathbb{R}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<s<t\leq T\\
x,y\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\mathbf{E}[\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)^{k}]<\infty,

(see
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
).

The following property of the Green’s function of the SHE will be used later in the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
s<t
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,0)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(z+y-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,z)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,z+y;0,0)\mathrm{d}z}\right].

Proof.
By the convolution formula for the Green’s function of SHE (see
[
1
, Theorem 2.6]
), we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{p_{t}(x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})\mathcal{G}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{p_{t}(x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})\mathcal{G}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,s}\left[\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{p_{t}(x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})\mathcal{G}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,s}\left[\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(y^{\prime}-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right]\right],

where the notation
\mathbf{E}_{a,b}
denotes the expectation with respect to the space-time white noise from time
a
to
b
, and we used the fact that

\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
The last equality holds by the following identity on heat kernel

(2.3)

\frac{p_{t-s}(a)p_{s}(b)}{p_{t}(a+b)}=p_{s(t-s)/t}\left(b-\frac{s}{t}(a+b)\right)\quad\text{for all $0<s<t$ and $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Because for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
, the random variable
\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x+z;s,y+z)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(y^{\prime}-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x+z;s,y^{\prime}+z)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}
has the same distribution as
\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(y^{\prime}-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}
under the expectation

\mathbf{E}_{s,t}
(see
[
1
, Proposition 2.3]
),
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,s}\left[\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,y-x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(y^{\prime}-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,y^{\prime}-x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,s}\left[\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,y-x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(y^{\prime}-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,y^{\prime}-x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime}-x;0,-x)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,s}\left[\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,y-x)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(y^{\prime}+x-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,y^{\prime})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,-x)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right]\right],

where the second equality holds by the stationarity of the process

\{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime}+z;0,z):z\in\mathbb{R}\}
under the expectation
\mathbf{E}_{0,s}
.
Using again stationarity,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,s}\left[\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,0)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(y^{\prime}+x-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,y^{\prime}+x-y)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,-x)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,s}\left[\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,0)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(z-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,z-y)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,z;0,0)\mathrm{d}z}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,0)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(z-\frac{s}{t}x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;s,z-y)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,z;0,0)\mathrm{d}z}\right],

where in the second equality, we use change of variable and stationarity. The proof is complete.

3.
Spatial decorrelation: proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the spatial covariance. The starting point is the Clark-Ocone formula (
[
10
, Proposition 6.3]
):

\displaystyle h(t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)]+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}[D_{s,y}h(t,x)|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)]+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{D_{s,y}Z(t,x)}{Z(t,x)}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)]+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y),

where
\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)
is defined as

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y;0,0)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right],\quad\quad s<t,x,y\in\mathbb{R},

and we used (
2.1
) in the calculation of the Malliavin derivative.

By Ito’s isometry, the covariance function can be expressed as

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{X}(t,s,x,y)]p_{s(t-s)/t}(y)p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

with

\mathcal{X}(t,s,x,y)=\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)\mathcal{A}(t,0;s,y).

As discussed earlier, the main contribution to the covariance comes from the noise near the origin. From the above expression, this can be seen as follows: by the moment estimates on
\bar{\mathcal{G}}
given by (
2.2
), one can show that
\mathcal{X}(t,s,x,y)
is bounded from above and below by positive constants depending only on
t
. Consequently, the integral in (
3.2
) is of the same order as

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{s(t-s)/t}(y)p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}p_{2s(t-s)/t}(\frac{s}{t}x)\mathrm{d}s

For those
s
away from
0
, the Gaussian kernel
p_{2s(t-s)/t}(\frac{s}{t}x)
leads to a much faster decay than
\tfrac{1}{x}
, thus, the main contribution to the above integral comes from those small
s
. Therefore, one needs to understand the behavior of
\chi(t,s,x,y)
for
s\ll 1
.

As a matter of fact, one can study the integral on the right-hand side of (
3.3
) and derive that as
x\to\infty
it is of order
x^{-1}
. This gives an upper and lower bound of
\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]
by
x^{-1}
. To derive the precise decaying rate, the main technical difficulty in the proof lies in dealing with the extra term
\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{X}(t,s,x,y)]
in the integrand of (
3.2
).

3.1.
A technical estimate

From the expression of
\mathcal{A}
given in (
3.1
), it is straightforward to guess what the limit of
\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)
is as
s\to 0
: we replace the conditional expectation by the full expectation and pass to the limit of each
\bar{\mathcal{G}}
factor. This motivates us to define

(3.4)

\displaystyle g_{t}(x,y)=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,y)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right].

We have the following key technical proposition:

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix
t>0
and
k\geq 2
, there exists
C>0
depending on
t
and
k
such that for all
s\in(0,t/4)
,

(3.5)

\|\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)-g_{t}(x,y)\|_{k}\leq C(s^{1/4}+d(xs^{1/4},ys^{1/4})+d(xs^{1/4},0)),

with
d(x,y):=|x-y|1_{|x-y|<1}+1_{|x-y|\geq 1}
.

Proof.
We write

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y;0,0).

For the denominator, using the convolution formula for the Green’s function, we rewrite it as

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)

\displaystyle=p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})\mathcal{G}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})p_{s}(y^{\prime})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})p_{s}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle+p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})p_{s}(y^{\prime})(\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)-1)\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}(t,s,x)+I_{2}(t,s,x).

So we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{I_{1}(t,s,x)+I_{2}(t,s,x)}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right]+\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)(-I_{2}(t,s,x))}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right].

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)-g_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right]+\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)(-I_{2}(t,s,x))}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right]\right)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y;0,0)-g_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right]+\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)(-I_{2}(t,s,x))}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right]\right)[\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y;0,0)-1]

\displaystyle+\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right]-g_{t}(x,y)\right)+\mathbf{E}_{s,t}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)(-I_{2}(t,s,x))}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right]

\displaystyle=:err_{1}+err_{2}+err_{3}.

Note that

\displaystyle\|I_{2}(t,s,x)\|_{k}\leq

\displaystyle p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})(\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y^{\prime};0,0)-1)\|_{k}p_{s}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})\|_{2k}\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,0;0,0)-1\|_{2k}p_{s}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}.

We use Burkholder’s inequality and follow the same calculation in
[
7
, Lemma A.4]
to derive that for
T>0
and
k\geq 2
, there exists
C_{T,k}>0
such that for all
0<s\leq T
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,0;0,0)-1\|_{k}\leq C_{T,k}s^{1/4}.

Hence, we can combine with (
2.2
) to derive that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|I_{2}(t,s,x)\|_{k}\lesssim

\displaystyle p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(x-y^{\prime})p_{s}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}s^{1/4}=s^{1/4}.

Now we give some estimate on the negative moment of
I_{1}
. One realizes that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y)p_{s}(y)dy
is the solution to SHE started from
p_{s}(y)
, evaluated at
(t-s,x)
, so by
[
14
, Corollary 4.9]
we actually have

\|I_{1}(t,s,x)^{-1}\|^{k}_{k}\leq 2^{k}\mathrm{e}^{2k\sqrt{\lambda(t-s)\log\frac{2}{b(t-s)}}}\left(1+4\sqrt{\pi k^{2}\lambda(t-s)}\mathrm{e}^{k^{2}\lambda(t-s)}\right),

where the two functions
\lambda
and
b
are defined in (4.15) and (4.20) of
[
14
]
respectively. It is clear from
[
14
, Theorem 2.7(ii)]
that both
\lambda
and
1/b
are locally bounded. Hence for
T>0
and
k\geq 2
, there exists
C_{T,k}^{\prime}>0
such that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<s<t\leq T\\
x\in\mathbb{R}\end{subarray}}\|I_{1}(t,s,x)^{-1}\|_{k}\leq C^{\prime}_{T,k}.

By (
2.2
), (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|err_{1}\|_{k}\lesssim s^{1/4}\left(1+s^{1/4}\right)\lesssim s^{1/4},

\displaystyle\|err_{3}\|_{k}\lesssim s^{1/4}.

The analysis of
err_{2}
is more complicated: we first rewrite it as

(3.10)

\displaystyle err_{2}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,y)}{I_{1}(t,s,x)}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,y)}{I_{1}(t,s,x)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}(\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)-I_{1}(t,s,x))\right].

So we need to further estimate the error
\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,y)
and
I_{1}(t,s,x)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)
.
First, by
[
1
, Lemma 3.6]
we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,y)\|_{k}\lesssim|x-y|s^{1/4}1_{|x-y|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x-y|s^{1/4}\geq 1}.

Secondly,

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,s,x)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)

\displaystyle=p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,y^{\prime})p_{s}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}-p_{t}(x)^{-1}\mathcal{G}(t,x;0,0)

\displaystyle=p_{t}(x)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{G}(t,x;s,\sqrt{s/t}y^{\prime})-\mathcal{G}(t,x;0,0))p_{t}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}.

To use the result from
[
1
]
, we rewrite the above term in terms of
\bar{\mathcal{G}}
:

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,s,x)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,\sqrt{s/t}y^{\prime})\tfrac{p_{t-s}(x-\sqrt{s/t}y^{\prime})}{p_{t}(x)}-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0))p_{t}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,\sqrt{s/t}y^{\prime})(\tfrac{p_{t-s}(x-\sqrt{s/t}y^{\prime})}{p_{t}(x)}-1)p_{t}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,\sqrt{s/t}y^{\prime})-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0))p_{t}(y^{\prime})\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}=:I_{3}(t,s,x)+I_{4}(t,s,x).

We bound
I_{3}
and
I_{4}
as

\displaystyle\|I_{3}(t,s,x)\|_{k}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\tfrac{p_{t-s}(x-\sqrt{s/t}y)}{p_{t}(x)}-1|p_{t}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\|I_{4}(t,s,x)\|_{k}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,\sqrt{s/t}y)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)\|_{k}p_{t}(y)\mathrm{d}y.

For
I_{3}
,
after a calculation we have

|\tfrac{p_{t-s}(x-\sqrt{s/t}y)}{p_{t}(x)}-1|p_{t}(y)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{(t-s)/t}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(\sqrt{t}y-\sqrt{s}x)^{2}}{2(t-s)t}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}|,

so it remains to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{(t-s)/t}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(\sqrt{t}y-\sqrt{s}x)^{2}}{2(t-s)t}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}|\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{(t-s)/t}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(\sqrt{t}y-\sqrt{s}x)^{2}}{2(t-s)t}}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{(t-s)/t}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(t-s)}}|\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t}}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{(t-s)/t}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(t-s)}}|\mathrm{d}y.

For the second term, since
s
is close to zero, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{(t-s)/t}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(t-s)}}|\mathrm{d}y\lesssim s.

For the first term, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(y-\sqrt{s/t}x)^{2}}{2(t-s)}}-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(t-s)}}|\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\sqrt{s}|x|1_{\sqrt{s}|x|\leq 1}+1_{\sqrt{s}|x|>1}.

This implies that

\|I_{3}(t,s,x)\|_{k}\lesssim s+\sqrt{s}|x|1_{\sqrt{s}|x|\leq 1}+1_{\sqrt{s}|x|>1}.

For
I_{4}
, we have (note that
s\in(0,t/4)
)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,\sqrt{s/t}y)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,\sqrt{s/t}y)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,0)\|_{k}+\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(1,x;s,0)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(1,x;0,0)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\sqrt{s}y|^{1/2}+(|x|s^{1/4}1_{|x|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x|s^{1/4}\geq 1}),

in light of
[
1
, Lemmas 3.4 and 3.6]
, which implies that

\displaystyle\|I_{4}(t,s,x)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\sqrt{s}y|^{1/2}+(|x|s^{1/4}1_{|x|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x|s^{1/4}\geq 1}))p_{t}(y)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim s^{1/4}+|x|s^{1/4}1_{|x|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x|s^{1/4}\geq 1}.

To summarize, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{1}(t,s,x)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(1,x;0,0)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim s+\sqrt{s}|x|1_{\sqrt{s}|x|\leq 1}+1_{\sqrt{s}|x|>1}

(3.12)

\displaystyle+s^{1/4}+|x|s^{1/4}1_{|x|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x|s^{1/4}\geq 1}.

One can simplify the right-hand side so that

\|I_{1}(t,s,x)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(1,x;0,0)\|_{k}\lesssim s^{1/4}+|x|s^{1/4}1_{|x|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x|s^{1/4}\geq 1}.

Combining (
3.11
), (
3.9
), (
3.1
), (
2.2
) with (
3.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|err_{2}\|_{k}\lesssim(

\displaystyle|x-y|s^{1/4}1_{|x-y|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x-y|s^{1/4}\geq 1}

\displaystyle+s^{1/4}+|x|s^{1/4}1_{|x|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x|s^{1/4}\geq 1}).

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)-g_{t}(x,y)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim(|x-y|s^{1/4}1_{|x-y|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x-y|s^{1/4}\geq 1}+s^{1/4}+|x|s^{1/4}1_{|x|s^{1/4}<1}+1_{|x|s^{1/4}\geq 1}),

which completes the proof.

\Box

3.2.
Spatial decay

Recall that our goal is to determine the decaying rate of

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{X}(t,s,x,y)]p_{s(t-s)/t}(y)p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

With Proposition
3.1
established in the previous section, one might expect that it is straightforward to approximate

\mathcal{X}(t,s,x,y)=\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)\mathcal{A}(t,0;s,y)\approx g_{t}(x,y)g_{t}(0,y),\quad\quad\mbox{ for }s\ll 1,

and then analyze the resulting integral. However, the situation turns out to be more delicate than it first appears.

Using the following identities on the heat kernel

(3.14)

\displaystyle p_{t}(x)p_{t}(y)=2p_{2t}(x+y)p_{2t}(x-y),\quad p_{t}(\alpha x)=\alpha^{-1}p_{t/\alpha^{2}}(x),

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
t,\alpha>0
,
we rewrite the product of the heat kernel in (
3.13
) as

p_{s(t-s)/t}(y)p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\tfrac{s}{t}x)=\tfrac{1}{2}p_{s(t-s)/(2t)}(y-\tfrac{sx}{2t})p_{s(t-s)/(2t)}(\tfrac{sx}{2t}),

so that the covariance can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{X}(t,s,x,y)]p_{s(t-s)/(2t)}(y-\tfrac{sx}{2t})p_{s(t-s)/(2t)}(\tfrac{sx}{2t})\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Assuming without loss of generality
x>0
, we do a change of variable

s\mapsto s/x^{2},\quad\quad y\mapsto y/x,

so that the covariance can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]

(3.15)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{t}{2x}\int_{0}^{tx^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{X}(t,\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},x,\tfrac{y}{x})\right]\frac{1}{\pi s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

The rest of this section is devoted to studying the integral on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) as we send
x\to\infty
. Throughout the section,
t>0
is fixed (so the implicit multiplicative constant in all estimates may depend on
t
).

We begin with the following lemma, which shows that it is sufficient to consider only small values of
s
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
\beta\in(0,1/2)
. Then the integral

\int_{x^{\beta}}^{tx^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{X}(t,\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},x,\tfrac{y}{x})\right]\frac{1}{s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

tends to
0
as
x\to\infty
.

Proof.
Denote the integral by
I
. Since the expectation inside the above integral is uniformly bounded, we bound it as

\displaystyle I\lesssim\int_{x^{\beta}}^{tx^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{x^{\beta}}^{tx^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}s.

Decompose the domain of integration into two parts, we have

I\lesssim\int_{x^{\beta}}^{tx^{2}/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-x^{\beta-2})}}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{tx^{2}/2}^{tx^{2}}\frac{1}{s}\sqrt{\frac{s}{t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}s

For the last integral, by the inequality
\sqrt{y}\mathrm{e}^{-y/4}\lesssim\mathrm{e}^{-y/8}
, we complete the proof.

\Box

Next, we need to figure out the limit of

\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{X}(t,\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},x,\tfrac{y}{x})\right]

as
x\to\infty
. Recall

\mathcal{X}(t,\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},x,\tfrac{y}{x})=\mathcal{A}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})\mathcal{A}(t,0;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x}),

There are two terms on the right-hand side. We deal with the second one first: Proposition
3.1
immediately implies that (as
s/x^{2},y/x\to 0
)

\mathcal{A}(t,0;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})\to g_{t}(0,0)=1

in
L^{k}(\Omega)
. This leads to the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
\beta\in(0,1/2)
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{X}(t,\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},x,\tfrac{y}{x})\right]-g_{t}(0,0)\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{A}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})\right]\right)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\to 0

as
x\to\infty
.

Proof.
Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{X}(t,\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},x,\tfrac{y}{x})\right]-g_{t}(0,0)\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{A}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\|\mathcal{A}(t,0;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})-g_{t}(0,0)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\|\mathcal{A}(t,0;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})-g_{t}(0,\tfrac{y}{x})\|_{2}+\|g_{t}(0,\tfrac{y}{x})-g_{t}(0,0)\|_{2}.

By Proposition
3.1
, the right-hand side can be bounded as

(3.16)

(sx^{-2})^{1/4}+|y/x|(sx^{-2})^{1/4}+|y/x|^{1/2},

where the last term
|y/x|^{1/2}
comes from the fact that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;0,0)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;0,\tfrac{y}{x})\|_{k}\lesssim|y/x|^{1/2},

(see
[
1
, Lemma 3.4]
).

Recall that
\beta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
. We substitute (
3.16
) into the integral and handle the three resulting terms separately.

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(sx^{-2})^{1/4}\frac{1}{s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

(3.18)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim x^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}s^{-1/4}\mathrm{d}s\asymp x^{\frac{3\beta}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as $x\to\infty$}

(ii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(sx^{-2})^{1/4}|y/x|\frac{1}{s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim x^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}s^{-1/4}\frac{\sqrt{s}+s}{|x|}\mathrm{d}s\leq x^{\frac{7\beta}{4}-\frac{3}{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as $x\to\infty$}.

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|y/x|^{1/2}\frac{1}{s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

(3.19)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim x^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\frac{s^{1/4}+s^{1/2}}{\sqrt{s}}\mathrm{d}s\leq x^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\to 0\quad\text{as $x\to\infty$}.

Hence, we complete the proof.

\Box

To analyze
\mathcal{A}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})
as
x\to\infty
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
t>s>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},0)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]=1.

Proof.
According to Lemma
2.1
,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},0)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;\frac{s}{x^{2}},0)}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(t-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;\frac{s}{x^{2}},z)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\frac{s}{x^{2}},z;0,0)\mathrm{d}z}\right].

For the numerator, as
x\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;\frac{s}{x^{2}},0)\to\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;0,0)\quad\text{in $L^{k}(\Omega)$}.

For the denominator, we first derive the following bound: by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(t-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;\frac{s}{x^{2}},z)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\frac{s}{x^{2}},z;0,0)\mathrm{d}z\bigg)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(t-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})\big(\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;\frac{s}{x^{2}},z)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\frac{s}{x^{2}},z;0,0)\big)^{-1}\mathrm{d}z

which together with (
2.2
) implies that

\|\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(1-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;\frac{s}{x^{2}},z)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\frac{s}{x^{2}},y;0,0)\mathrm{d}z\big)^{-1}\|_{k}\lesssim 1.

To complete the proof, it suffices to show that the denominator on the right-hand side of (
3.20
) converges to
\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;0,0)
in
L^{k}(\Omega)
as
x\to\infty
.
We write the error as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(t-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;\frac{s}{x^{2}},z)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\frac{s}{x^{2}},z;0,0)\mathrm{d}z-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;0,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(t-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})[\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;\frac{s}{x^{2}},z)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;0,0)]\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\frac{s}{x^{2}},z;0,0)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(t-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,0;0,0)[\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\frac{s}{x^{2}},z;0,0)-1]\mathrm{d}z=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

For
J_{1}
, by (
3.11
) and (
3.17
), we have

\|J_{1}\|_{k}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(t-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})[|z|(sx^{-2})^{1/4}+|z|^{1/2}]\mathrm{d}z\to 0

as
x\to\infty
. For
J_{2}
, by (
3.7
), we have

\|J_{2}\|_{k}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{sx^{-2}(t-sx^{-2})/t}(z-\frac{s}{tx})(sx^{-2})^{1/4}\mathrm{d}z=(sx^{-2})^{1/4}\to 0

as
x\to\infty
.
The proof is complete.

\Box

With the above preparations, we are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
\beta\in(0,1/2)
.
By (
3.2
) and Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, as
x\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{2x}{t}\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{A}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})\right]\frac{1}{\pi s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s+o(1).

Since

\mathbf{E}\left[\mathcal{A}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x};0,0)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]

and

\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x};0,0)-1\|_{k}=\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},0;0,0)-1\|_{k}\lesssim|\tfrac{s}{x^{2}}|^{1/4},

we perform the same calculation as in (
3.2
) to derive that

\displaystyle\frac{2x}{t}\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]\frac{1}{\pi s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s+o(1).

Furthermore, because

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},\tfrac{y}{x})-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},0)\|_{k}\lesssim|y/x|^{1/2},

we deduce from the calculation in (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\frac{2x}{t}\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},0)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]\frac{1}{\pi s(t-sx^{-2})}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t(y-s/(2t))^{2}}{s(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x^{\beta}}\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;\tfrac{s}{x^{2}},0)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}\right]\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi st(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t(t-sx^{-2})}}\mathrm{d}s+o(1).

Since the expectation inside the above integral is uniformly bounded in
s
and
x
(see (
2.2
)), applying Lemma
3.4
and dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\left[\frac{2x}{t}\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)]\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi st^{2}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{s}{4t^{2}}}\mathrm{d}s=2,

which completes the proof.

\Box

Corollary 3.5
.

Fix
t>0
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N\log N}\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{N}(h(t,x)-\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)])\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\right]=2t.

Proof.
Since
\{Z(t,x)/p_{t}(x):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
is stationary, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N\log N}\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{N}(h(t,x)-\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)])\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{N\log N}\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,y)]

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2}{N\log N}\int_{0}^{N}\mathrm{d}y\int_{0}^{y}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{Cov}\left[\log\frac{Z(t,x)}{p_{t}(x)},\log\frac{Z(t,y)}{p_{t}(y)}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2}{N\log N}\int_{0}^{N}\mathrm{d}y\int_{0}^{y}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{Cov}[h(t,x),h(t,0)].

Then the result follows from Theorem
1.1
.

\Box

4.
CLT for spatial average: proof of Theorem
1.2

We will prove Theorem
1.2
in this section. For
t>0
and
N\geq 1
, we denote

(4.1)

\displaystyle X_{N}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\log N}}\int_{0}^{N}[h(t,x)-\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)]]\mathrm{d}x.

The goal is to show
X_{N}(\cdot)\Rightarrow\sqrt{2}{\rm B}(\cdot)
in the sense of convergence of finite dimensional distributions.

Recall that by the Clark-Ocone formula, we have expressed
h(t,x)-\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)]
as an Itô integral:

h(t,x)-\mathbf{E}[h(t,x)]=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y),

with
\mathcal{A}
defined in (
3.1
). This implies through an application of Fubini theorem that

X_{N}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\log N}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{N}\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)\mathrm{d}x\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

So the quantity of interest is written as an Itô integral, which we can actually analyze directly. Here we take a different approach: define

(4.2)

\displaystyle X_{N,1}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\log N}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{N}\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)]p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)\mathrm{d}x\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

By Stroock’s formula (see
[
26
, Equation (7), page 3]
),

X_{N,1}(t)
is the first Wiener chaos component of
X_{N}(t)
. We claim that
X_{N,1}(t)
is the main contribution to
X_{N}(t)
as
N\to\infty
, which in turn yields both the Gaussianity and the convergence of the variance. This is formalized in the following result:

Proposition 4.1
.

Fix
t_{1},t_{2}>0
. Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathrm{Cov}[X_{N,1}(t_{1}),X_{N,1}(t_{2})]=2(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}).

With the above result,
we now proceed with a quick proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

With
X_{N}(t)
defined in (
4.1
), we write its Wiener chaos expansion:

X_{N}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}X_{N,k}(t),

where
X_{N,k}(t)
is the
k
-th Wiener chaos component of
X_{N}(t)
. In particular,
X_{N,1}(t)
is given in (
4.2
).
By Corollary
3.5
and Proposition
4.1
, we conclude that for each
t>0

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbf{E}[X_{N}(t)^{2}]=2t\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbf{E}[X_{N,1}(t)^{2}]=2t.

Thus, by the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-orthogonality of the components of the Wiener chaos,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[(X_{N}(t)-X_{N,1}(t))^{2}\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[X_{N}(t)^{2}\right]-\mathbf{E}\left[X_{N,1}(t)^{2}\right]\to 0

as
N\to\infty
. Therefore, Theorem
1.2
holds by Cramér–Wold theorem and Proposition
4.1
.
\Box

4.1.
Proof of Proposition
4.1

Applying Ito isometry we write the covariance as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[X_{N,1}(t_{1}),X_{N,1}(t_{2})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N\log N}\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{A}(t_{1},x_{1};s,y)]\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{A}(t_{2},x_{2};s,y)]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad p_{s(t_{1}-s)/t_{1}}(y-\frac{s}{t_{1}}x_{1})p_{s(t_{2}-s)/t_{2}}(y-\frac{s}{t_{2}}x_{2}).

The rest of proof is a rather complicated calculation.

Recall that

\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(s,y;0,0)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right].

Since the main contribution to the stochastic integral (
4.2
) comes from those small
s\ll 1
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(t,x;s,y)=\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;s,y)}{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t,x;0,0)}

and

\displaystyle Y_{N,1}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\log N}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{N}\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t,x;s,y)]p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)\mathrm{d}x\,\xi(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

By the moment estimates on

\mathcal{A}(t,x;s,y)
and
\mathcal{B}(t,x;s,y)
, (
3.7
) and Lemma
A.1
, it suffices to prove

(4.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathrm{Cov}(Y_{N,1}(t_{1}),Y_{N,1}(t_{2}))=2(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}).

Define

\displaystyle V_{N}(t_{1},t_{2})=\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})/N^{2}}^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},x_{1};s,y)]\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{2},x_{2};s,y)]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times p_{s(t_{1}-s)/t_{1}}(y-\frac{s}{t_{1}}x_{1})p_{s(t_{2}-s)/t_{2}}(y-\frac{s}{t_{2}}x_{2}).

Applying Lemma
A.2
, proving (
4.4
) reduces to

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{V_{N}(t_{1},t_{2})}{N\log N}=2(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}).

By a change of variable
x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}t_{i},i=1,2
and
y\mapsto ys
, we can write

\displaystyle V_{N}(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{1}t_{2}\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{N/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})/N^{2}}^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,p_{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}}(y-x_{1})p_{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(y-x_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},t_{1}x_{1};s,sy)]\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{2},t_{2}x_{2};s,sy)].

Further using the change of variable of
s\to N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),y\to y+x_{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{V_{N}(t_{1},t_{2})}{N\log N}=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{N}\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{N/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,p_{\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}}(y+x_{2}-x_{1})p_{\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},t_{1}x_{1};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2}))]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{2},t_{2}x_{2};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2}))].

In order to derive (
4.5
), we will approximate the two expectations appearing in the above integral by
1
as
N\to\infty
. The remainder of the proof consists of several steps to justify this approximation.

Step 1. We first deal with the second expectation

\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{2},t_{2}x_{2};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2}))].

To simplify the notation, we define

\displaystyle\lambda(N)=N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(t_{2}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}))/t_{2}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{\lambda(N)}(z+N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})y)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\bar{\mathcal{G}}(N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z+N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2});0,0)\mathrm{d}z.

According to Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{2},t_{2}x_{2};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2}))]=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),0)}{\mathcal{D}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})}\right].

By Jensen’s inequality,
\mathcal{D}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})
has finite negative moments, bounded uniformly in
(N,\tau,y,x_{2})
. By (
3.7
),

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),0)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;0,0)\|_{k}\lesssim N^{-\tau/4},

which implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{2},t_{2}x_{2};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2}))]=\mathbf{E}\left[\frac{\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;0,0)}{\mathcal{D}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})}\right]+O(N^{-\tau/4}).

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{\lambda(N)}(z+N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})y)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z)\mathrm{d}z.

Similar to
\mathcal{D}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})
, the random variable
\mathcal{D}_{1}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})
has finite negative moments, bounded uniformly in
(N,\tau,y,x_{2})
.
Appealing again to (
3.7
) and using stationarity,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})-\mathcal{D}_{1}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})\|_{k}\lesssim N^{-\tau/4}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{1}(z)\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z\sqrt{\lambda(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})y)\mathrm{d}z.

By triangle inequality and
[
1
, Lemmas 3.4 and 3.6]
,

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z\sqrt{\lambda(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})y)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;0,0)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z\sqrt{\lambda(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})y)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),0)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),0)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;0,0)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim|z|^{1/2}N^{-\tau/4}+(1\wedge|N^{-\tau}y|^{1/2})+N^{-\tau/4}.

Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{1}(N,\tau,y,x_{2})-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},0;0,0)\|_{k}\lesssim(1\wedge|N^{-\tau}y|^{1/2})+N^{-\tau/4},

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{2},t_{2}x_{2};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2}))]=1+O((1\wedge|N^{-\tau}y|^{1/2})+N^{-\tau/4}).

Applying Lemma
A.3
below, we conclude that as
N\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{V_{N}(t_{1},t_{2})}{N\log N}

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{N}\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{N/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,p_{\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}}(y+x_{2}-x_{1})p_{\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(y)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},t_{1}x_{1};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2}))]

\displaystyle\quad+o(1).

Step 2. We continue to approximate the expectation appearing in (
4.7
) by
1
as
N\to\infty
.

First, because

\displaystyle\big|\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},t_{1}x_{1};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(y+x_{2}))]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},t_{1}x_{1};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})x_{2})]\big|\lesssim 1\wedge|N^{-\tau}y|^{1/2},

we obtain by Lemma
A.3
that as

\displaystyle=o(1)+\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{N}\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{N/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\,p_{\frac{2N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(x_{2}-x_{1})

(4.8)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},t_{1}x_{1};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})x_{2})].

Next, we introduce

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}(N)=N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(t_{1}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}))/t_{1},

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(N,\tau,x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{\tilde{\lambda}(N)}(z+N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(x_{2}-x_{1}))\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{1},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\bar{\mathcal{G}}(N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z+N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})x_{2};0,0)\mathrm{d}z.

Then, by Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},t_{1}x_{1};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})x_{2})]

We will approximate the denominator in the above expectation by

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(N,\tau,x_{1},x_{2})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{\tilde{\lambda}(N)}(z+N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(x_{2}-x_{1}))\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{1},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z)\mathrm{d}z\right\|_{k}\lesssim N^{-\tau/4}

Because

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{1},0;N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),z\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(x_{2}-x_{1}))-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{1},0;0,0)\right\|_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim N^{-\tau/4}+\left(1\wedge\left|z\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(x_{2}-x_{1})\right|^{1/2}\right),

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(N,\tau,x_{1},x_{2})-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{1},0;0,0)\right\|_{k}

Hence, since
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(N,\tau,x_{1},x_{2})
has finite negative moments, bounded uniformly in
(N,\tau,x_{1},x_{2})
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{E}[\mathcal{B}(t_{1},t_{1}x_{1};N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}),N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})x_{2})]-1\right|

(4.9)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{1}(z)\left[1\wedge\left|z\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(x_{2}-x_{1})\right|^{1/2}\right]\mathrm{d}z+N^{-\tau/4}.

Step 3. Replacing the expectation in (
4.1
) by the integral in (
4.1
), we claim that the resulting quantity converges to

as

.

To see this, introduce the following two functions

\displaystyle 1_{N}(x):=\frac{1}{N}1_{[0,N]}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{1}_{N}(x):=1_{N}(-x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Denote
t^{*}=\frac{1}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}
and write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Nt^{*}}\int_{[0,Nt^{*}]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\,p_{\frac{2N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(x_{2}-x_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{1}(z)\left[1\wedge\left|z\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})(x_{2}-x_{1})\right|^{1/2}\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\quad=Nt^{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\left(1_{Nt^{*}}*\tilde{1}_{Nt^{*}}\right)(y)\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\,p_{\frac{2N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{1}(z)\left[1\wedge\left|z\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})y\right|^{1/2}\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\quad=Nt^{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\left(1_{Nt^{*}}*\tilde{1}_{Nt^{*}}\right)(y\sqrt{\frac{2N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}})\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\,p_{1}(y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{1}(z)\left[1\wedge\left|z\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}(N)}-N^{-\tau}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})y\sqrt{\frac{2N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}\right|^{1/2}\right]\mathrm{d}z.

Because the function
Nt^{*}(1_{Nt^{*}}*\tilde{1}_{Nt^{*}})
is bounded above by
1
, the above integral converges to
0
as
N\to\infty
by the dominated
convergence theorem.

Finally, we conclude from (
4.1
) and (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{V_{N}(t_{1},t_{2})}{N\log N}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left[\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{N}\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{N/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\,p_{\frac{2N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(x_{2}-x_{1})\right]

\displaystyle=2(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}).

where the second equality holds by Lemma
A.4
. This proves (
4.5
) and hence completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Remark 4.2
.

Theorem
1.2
can also be proved using Malliavin-Stein’s method for the CLT of SPDEs, originally introduced in
[
15
]
. For instance, one can show that for fixed
t>0
,
there exists a constant
c>0
depending on
t
such that for all
N\geq\mathrm{e}
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle d_{\rm TV}\left(\frac{X_{N}(t)}{\sqrt{{\rm Var}(X_{N}(t))}}~,~{\rm N}(0,1)\right)\leq c\,\sqrt{\frac{\log N}{N}},

where
d_{\rm TV}
denotes the total variation distance, and
{\rm N}(0,1)

denotes the standard normal distribution. The proof of (
4.10
) is similar to that of
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
and the main difference is that we need the estimate on the second Malliavin derivative of the solution to (
1.2
), which was obtained in
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
. We do not include the details here.

Remark 4.3
.

It is natural to ask if a functional CLT holds as in Theorem
1.2
. In the case of flat initial condition, the function CLT for the spatial average of the KPZ solution has been proved by Chen et al.
[
8
, Theorem 2.3]
, where they established the tightness in Proposition 5.3. Their method does not seem to allow us to prove the tightness for the spatial average of the solution to the KPZ equation from narrow wedge initial data. One technical issue is that in our case, for any
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
, the distribution of
\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{2},x;0,0)-\bar{\mathcal{G}}(t_{1},x;0,0)
depends on
x
. We leave this problem to interested reader.

Appendix A
Technical estimates on integrals

This section is devoted to several technical lemmas concerning estimates of (deterministic) integrals.

Lemma A.1
.

Fix
t_{1},t_{2}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigg[\frac{1}{N\log N}\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,s^{1/4}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad p_{s(t_{1}-s)/t_{1}}(y-\frac{s}{t_{1}}x_{1})p_{s(t_{2}-s)/t_{2}}(y-\frac{s}{t_{2}}x_{2})\bigg]=0.

Proof.
By the semigroup property of the heat kernel and a change of variable, we write the left-hand side as

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{N\log N}\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{N/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}s^{1/4}p_{\frac{2}{s}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(x_{2}-x_{1})

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{N\log N}\int_{[0,N/(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s^{3/4}}p_{\frac{2}{s}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(x_{2}-x_{1})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{\pi^{-1}t_{1}t_{2}}{(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})\log N}\int_{0}^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s^{3/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\cos z}{z^{2}}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{2t_{1}}-\frac{1}{2t_{2}}\right)\frac{z^{2}}{(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})^{2}N^{2}}\right]\mathrm{d}z,

where the equality holds by
[
7
, Lemma A.2]
.
We bound the exponential term above by
1
so that the integral is bounded from above by
1/\log N
multiple of a constant, which completes the proof.

\Box

Lemma A.2
.

Fix
t_{1},t_{2}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N\log N}\int_{[0,N]^{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int^{(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})/N^{2}}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}p_{(t_{1}-s)/(t_{1}s)+(t_{2}-s)/(t_{2}s)}(\frac{x_{1}}{t_{1}}-\frac{x_{2}}{t_{2}})=0.

Proof.
By change of variable and writing the integral in the Fourier domain, we write the term on the left-hand side as

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{\log N}\int_{0}^{1/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{1/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int^{(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})/N^{2}}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}p_{\frac{2}{sN^{2}}-(\frac{1}{t_{1}N^{2}}+\frac{1}{t_{2}N^{2}})}(x_{1}-x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{2\pi\log N}\int^{\frac{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}{N^{2}}}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\,\widehat{1_{[0,1/t_{1}]}}(z)\overline{\widehat{1_{[0,1/t_{2}]}}(z)}\exp\left[-\left[\frac{1}{sN^{2}}-(\frac{1}{2t_{1}N^{2}}+\frac{1}{2t_{2}N^{2}})\right]z^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{\log N}\int^{\frac{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}{N^{2}}}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\,|\widehat{1_{[0,1/t_{1}]}}(z)\overline{\widehat{1_{[0,1/t_{2}]}}(z)}|\exp\left[-\left(\frac{1}{sN^{2}}-\frac{1}{(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})N^{2}}\right)z^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{\log N}\int^{1}_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\,|\widehat{1_{[0,1/t_{1}]}}(z)\overline{\widehat{1_{[0,1/t_{2}]}}(z)}|\exp\left[-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)z^{2}/(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})\right],

where the first equality holds by Parseval identity. Notice that for all
N\geq 1
and
a>0
,

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)a\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{1}_{0}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{1-r}{r}\right)a\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{a}\int_{a}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-s}\mathrm{d}s}{s}\leq\mathrm{e}\log(\mathrm{e}+\frac{\mathrm{e}}{a}).

Taking into account

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{1_{[0,1/t_{1}]}}(z)\overline{\widehat{1_{[0,1/t_{2}]}}(z)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1\wedge z^{2}}{z^{2}}\quad\text{for all $z\neq 0$}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1\wedge z^{2}}{z^{2}}\log(\mathrm{e}+\frac{\mathrm{e}(t_{1}\wedge t_{2})}{z^{2}})\mathrm{d}z<\infty,

the result follows.

\Box

Lemma A.3
.

Fix
t_{1},t_{2}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigg[\frac{1}{N}\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{N/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad p_{\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}}(y+x_{2}-x_{1})p_{\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(y)(1\wedge|N^{-\tau}y|^{1/2})\bigg]=0.

Proof.
We bound the integral
\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}
above by
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}
in order to see that left-hand side is bounded above by

\displaystyle t_{2}^{-1}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,p_{\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(y)(1\wedge|N^{-\tau}y|^{1/2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=t_{2}^{-1}\int_{0}^{2}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,p_{1}(y)(1\wedge|N^{-\tau}y\sqrt{\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}|^{1/2}),

which converges to
0
as
N\to\infty
by dominated convergence theorem.
\Box

Lemma A.4
.

Fix
t_{1},t_{2}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{N}\int_{0}^{N/t_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\int_{0}^{N/t_{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\,p_{\frac{2N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{t_{1}}-\frac{1}{t_{2}}}(x_{2}-x_{1})

\displaystyle=2(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}).

Proof.
By Parseval’s identity, we write the left-hand side as

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{2\pi N}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{1_{[0,N/t_{1}]}}(z)\overline{\widehat{1_{[0,N/t_{2}]}}}(z)\exp\left[-\left(\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{2t_{1}}-\frac{1}{2t_{2}}\right)z^{2}\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}z/t_{1}}}{z{\rm i}}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}z/t_{2}}}{-z{\rm i}}\exp\left[-\left(\frac{N^{\tau}}{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}-\frac{1}{2t_{1}}-\frac{1}{2t_{2}}\right)\frac{z^{2}}{N^{2}}\right]\mathrm{d}z.

Because

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}z/t_{1}}}{z{\rm i}}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}z/t_{2}}}{-z{\rm i}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1\wedge z^{2}}{z^{2}},\quad\text{for all $z\neq 0$},

by dominated convergence theorem, as
N\to\infty
the preceding converges to

\displaystyle\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{{\rm i}z/t_{1}}}{z{\rm i}}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-{\rm i}z/t_{2}}}{-z{\rm i}}\mathrm{d}z=2t_{1}t_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{[0,1/t_{1}]}(z)1_{[0,1/t_{2}]}(z)\mathrm{d}z=2(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}).

The proof is complete.

\Box

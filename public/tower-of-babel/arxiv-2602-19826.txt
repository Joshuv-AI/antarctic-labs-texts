Primordial Black Hole Formation in Rastall Gravity: Shifted Collapse Threshold and Exponential Abundance Sensitivity
Mayukh R. Gangopadhyay

Abstract
Primordial black holes formed in the early universe are compelling candidates for dark matter. We investigate their production in Rastall gravity, a modification of general relativity that introduces a non-minimal coupling between matter and geometry through the non-conservation of the energy-momentum tensor. Analyzing cosmological perturbations during radiation domination, we demonstrate that the Rastall parameter fundamentally alters the collapse dynamics, modifying the growth of density fluctuations, the critical threshold for black hole formation, and the fluctuation amplitude at horizon crossing if we consider the nearly conformal plasma. Current cosmological constraints from Big Bang Nucleosynthesis, the cosmic microwave background, and large-scale structure restrict the Rastall parameter to small values, yet within this allowed range PBH production can be altered by orders of magnitude compared to general relativity. Our results establish primordial black holes as novel probes of modified gravity.

A longstanding question in the literature concerns whether Rastall gravity represents
a genuine modification of gravitational dynamics or merely a reformulation of GR with

The physical interpretation depends on how the matter sector is defined. If

represents a physically conserved matter tensor derived from a matter Lagrangian
minimally coupled to the metric, then Rastall gravity effectively modifies the coupling
between geometry and matter through the trace term. The modified conservation equation
then implies that matter and geometry exchange energy–momentum in a manner not present
in GR. From this perspective, Rastall gravity alters the dynamical response of matter
perturbations to curvature and therefore leads to physically distinct cosmological
perturbation dynamics.

In homogeneous cosmology with a radiation fluid (
T=0

coincides exactly with GR. However, perturbations probe variations of the curvature
scalar and the trace sector, and the modified conservation equation introduces additional
structure at the perturbative level. Therefore, even in scenarios where the background
cosmology is indistinguishable from GR, linear perturbations can encode nontrivial
dependence on the Rastall parameter
\lambda

In the present work, we adopt the interpretation that
T_{\mu\nu}
represents the
physical matter tensor of a radiation fluid satisfying the modified conservation law
above. We do not treat the theory as a mere redefinition of matter variables within GR;
instead, we take the modified conservation equation as defining the physical coupling
between matter and geometry. Under this interpretation, deviations in the evolution of
density perturbations and, consequently, in primordial black hole formation are
physically meaningful predictions of the theory.

It is worth emphasizing that the observational distinguishability of Rastall gravity
arises entirely from dynamical effects in perturbations rather than from additional
propagating gravitational degrees of freedom. The theory does not introduce extra tensor
polarizations at the linearized level around Minkowski space. Instead, its distinctive
features emerge through modified matter–geometry coupling, which can lead to measurable
consequences in early-universe structure formation processes such as primordial black
hole production.

2
Rastall Gravity: Fundamentals and Background Evolution

The Rastall field equations are derived by maintaining the Bianchi identity
\nabla^{\mu}G_{\mu\nu}=0
while incorporating the non-conservation postulate (
1
). This leads to:

G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=\kappa\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\lambda}{1-4\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}T\right),

(5)

where
\kappa=8\pi G
and
T=T^{\mu}_{\mu}
is the trace of the energy-momentum tensor. The parameter
\lambda
must satisfy
\lambda\neq 1/4
to avoid singular behavior. For
\lambda=0
, GR is recovered. The coupling between geometry and matter is evident through the trace-dependent term.

For a perfect fluid with equation of state
p=w\rho
, the energy-momentum tensor and its trace are:

T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu},\quad T=-\rho+3p=(3w-1)\rho.

(6)

Considering a flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric:

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j},

(7)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor and
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter.

Substituting into the field equations (
5
) yields the modified Friedmann equations:

\displaystyle 3H^{2}

\displaystyle=\kappa\left[1-\frac{\lambda(1-3w)}{1-4\lambda}\right]\rho,

(8)

\displaystyle 2\dot{H}+3H^{2}

\displaystyle=-\kappa\left[w-\frac{\lambda(1-3w)}{1-4\lambda}\right]\rho.

(9)

The conservation law (
1
) for the FLRW metric gives the time component:

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=\lambda\dot{R},\quad R=6(2H^{2}+\dot{H}).

(10)

For radiation domination,
w=1/3
, so
1-3w=0
. Equations (
8
)-(
9
) simplify dramatically:

\displaystyle 3H^{2}

\displaystyle=\kappa\rho,

(11)

\displaystyle 2\dot{H}+3H^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa\rho}{3}.

(12)

Thus, the background Friedmann equations are identical to those of general relativity. From these, we recover the standard relations:

\displaystyle H^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho,

(13)

\displaystyle\dot{H}

\displaystyle=-2H^{2},

(14)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\propto a^{-4},\quad a\propto t^{1/2}\propto\tau,

(15)

where
\tau
is conformal time defined by
d\tau=dt/a
.

Now examine the conservation equation (
10
) for radiation. Using
p=\rho/3
:

\dot{\rho}+4H\rho=\lambda\dot{R}.

(16)

From the Friedmann equations, we compute the Ricci scalar:

R=6(2H^{2}+\dot{H})=6(2H^{2}-2H^{2})=0.

(17)

Since
R=0
, we have
\dot{R}=0
, and (
16
) reduces to the standard conservation law
\dot{\rho}+4H\rho=0
. Consequently, the background evolution for a single radiation fluid is indistinguishable from GR.

While the background dynamics for pure radiation are identical to GR, differences emerge at the perturbation level if we consider the deviation from perfectly conformal plasma. The details are given in
2.1
. This makes PBH formation during radiation domination an ideal probe of Rastall gravity, as any deviations from GR must arise from perturbations rather than background expansion.

2.1
Activation of Rastall Corrections in a Nearly Conformal Plasma

In the strict radiation-dominated limit with equation of state
p=\rho/3
,
the energy–momentum tensor is traceless,

T\equiv T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}=-\rho+3p=0.

(18)

In this limit, the modified conservation law of Rastall gravity,

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=\gamma\nabla^{\nu}R,

(19)

reduces identically to the standard conservation equation, and the theory
becomes dynamically equivalent to General Relativity. Consequently,
in an exactly conformal radiation fluid, Rastall corrections would be absent
both at background and linear perturbation levels.

However, the primordial plasma is not exactly conformal.
Around the QCD epoch and at temperatures relevant for horizon re-entry of
small-scale perturbations, quantum effects generate a small but non-vanishing
trace of the energy–momentum tensor due to the QCD trace anomaly and
deviations from ideal radiation behaviour.

To capture this effect in a model-independent manner, we parametrize the
deviation from conformality as

p=\left(\frac{1}{3}-\delta\right)\rho,\qquad\delta\ll 1,

(20)

which implies

T=-\rho+3p=-3\delta\rho.

(21)

Thus, while the background expansion remains effectively radiation-dominated,
the trace is small but non-zero,

T\neq 0,

(22)

and the Rastall modification becomes active in a controlled manner.

At linear order,

\delta T=-\delta\rho+3\delta p=-3\delta\,\delta\rho,

(23)

so that perturbatively the trace does not vanish.
The modified conservation law therefore contributes at first order in
\delta
,
leading to corrections proportional to the product
\gamma\delta
in the scalar
perturbation equation.

3
Scalar Perturbations and Density Contrast Evolution

We work in the Newtonian (longitudinal) gauge with
\Phi=\Psi
(no anisotropic stress):

ds^{2}=-(1+2\Phi)dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}.

(24)

The perturbed energy-momentum tensor components for a perfect fluid are:

\displaystyle\delta T^{0}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\delta\rho,

(25)

\displaystyle\delta T^{0}_{i}

\displaystyle=(\rho+p)\partial_{i}v,

(26)

\displaystyle\delta T^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=\delta p\,\delta^{i}_{j},

\delta\rho
is the density perturbation,

The perturbed Rastall field equations are obtained by varying (
5

-\frac{2k^{2}}{a^{2}}\Phi+6H(\dot{\Phi}+H\Phi)=\kappa\left[\left(1-\frac{4\lambda}{1-4\lambda}c_{s}^{2}\right)\delta\rho\right].

(28)

The 0i-component gives:

2\partial_{i}(\dot{\Phi}+H\Phi)=\kappa(\rho+p)\partial_{i}v.

(29)

The perturbed Rastall conservation law
\delta(\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu})=\lambda\nabla^{\nu}\delta R
provides two crucial equations. The time component (
\nu=0
) is:

\delta(\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu 0})=-\lambda\partial_{0}\delta R.

(30)

The left-hand side is the perturbed energy conservation equation:

\delta(\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu 0})=\dot{\delta\rho}+3H(\delta\rho+\delta p)+(\rho+p)\left(3\dot{\Phi}-\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}v\right).

(31)

The spatial component (
\nu=i
) yields:

\delta(\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu i})=\frac{\lambda}{a^{2}}\partial^{i}\delta R.

(32)

The left-hand side gives the perturbed momentum conservation:

\delta(\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu i})=(\rho+p)(\dot{v}+4Hv)+\partial^{i}\delta p+(\rho+p)\partial^{i}\Phi.

(33)

The perturbed Ricci scalar to first order in Fourier space is:

\delta R=\frac{2k^{2}}{a^{2}}\Phi+6\ddot{\Phi}+12H\dot{\Phi}+6(2H^{2}+\dot{H})\Phi.

(34)

4.1
Solution During Radiation Domination

During radiation domination, we use conformal time
\tau

\mathcal{H}=a^{\prime}/a=1/\tau

\frac{d^{2}\delta}{dx^{2}}+\frac{2}{x}\frac{d\delta}{dx}+\left[\frac{1}{3}-\frac{3}{2x^{2}}-\frac{4\lambda}{3}\left(1-\frac{3}{x^{2}}\right)\right]\delta=0.

4.1.1
Super-horizon solution (

For modes well outside the horizon, we seek a series solution of the form
\delta(x)=\delta_{0}(1+\alpha x^{2}+\mathcal{O}(x^{4}))

\delta(x)\approx\delta_{0}\left[1+\left(\frac{3}{8}-\frac{2\lambda}{3}\right)x^{2}+\mathcal{O}(x^{4})\right].

(44)

The
\lambda

\delta_{H}(k)=\frac{4}{9}(1+\gamma\lambda)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}^{1/2}(k),

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)

PBH Formation Criterion in Rastall Gravity

5.1
Critical Density Threshold for Collapse

The critical density contrast
\delta_{c}
for PBH formation determines when an overdense region collapses against pressure forces. In general relativity, extensive numerical simulations
[
72
,
73
]
have established
\delta_{c}^{\text{GR}}\approx 0.414
for radiation domination.

In Rastall gravity, the threshold changes due to altered dynamics. We derive
\delta_{c}(\lambda)
using two complementary approaches, keeping only terms linear in
\lambda
for consistency with observational constraints.

5.1.1
Approach 1: Jeans scale modification

Critical density threshold
\delta_{c}(\lambda)

The Jeans length
\lambda_{J}
determines the scale below which pressure prevents collapse. From the dispersion relation derived from (
42
), we write
\omega^{2}=c_{s}^{2}k^{2}/a^{2}-4\pi G_{\text{eff}}\rho
, where
G_{\text{eff}}
is the effective gravitational constant modified by Rastall gravity.

From (
42
), comparing with the standard form
\ddot{\delta}+2H\dot{\delta}-(4\pi G_{\text{eff}}\rho-c_{s}^{2}k^{2}/a^{2})\delta=0
, we identify:

\displaystyle 4\pi G_{\text{eff}}\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}H^{2}-6\lambda H^{2}=\frac{3}{2}H^{2}(1-4\lambda),

(46)

\displaystyle c_{s}^{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}-\frac{4\lambda}{3}.

(47)

The Jeans wavenumber (
\omega=0
) is therefore:

k_{J}^{2}=\frac{4\pi G_{\text{eff}}\rho}{c_{s}^{2}(\lambda)}a^{2}=\frac{\frac{3}{2}H^{2}(1-4\lambda)}{\frac{1}{3}(1-4\lambda)}a^{2}=\frac{9}{2}H^{2}a^{2}.

(48)

Remarkably, the
\lambda
dependence cancels exactly in the ratio, leaving the Jeans scale unchanged from GR. This is a nontrivial result of Rastall gravity during radiation domination—the modifications to gravity and the sound speed compensate each other at the linear level for the Jeans criterion.

5.1.2
Approach 2: Spherical collapse model

Adapting the spherical collapse formalism to Rastall gravity, the evolution equation for the scale factor
a_{p}
of the perturbed region is derived from the modified Friedmann equations. For a top-hat overdensity
\delta
, the effective energy density inside the perturbed region is modified by the Rastall parameter:

\rho_{\text{eff}}=\rho\left[1+\delta\left(1-\frac{3\lambda}{1-4\lambda}\right)\right].

(49)

This leads to the modified collapse equation:

\left(\frac{\dot{a}_{p}}{a_{p}}\right)^{2}=H^{2}\left[1+\delta\left(1-\frac{3\lambda}{1-4\lambda}\right)\right].

(50)

The determination of the critical density contrast
\delta_{c}
for primordial black hole
formation requires a fully nonlinear treatment of gravitational collapse during radiation
domination. In general relativity as mentioned before,
\delta_{c}^{\rm GR}\simeq 0.414
has been obtained
through relativistic numerical simulations that incorporate pressure gradients,
profile dependence, and the compaction function formalism.

A rigorous determination of
\delta_{c}(\lambda)
would require solving the fully
nonlinear relativistic collapse equations in Rastall gravity, which lies beyond the
scope of the present work. Instead, we adopt a phenomenological parameterization
that captures the leading-order sensitivity of the collapse threshold to small
deviations from general relativity. Restricting to the observationally allowed regime

|\lambda|\ll 1
, we expand the threshold as

\delta_{c}(\lambda)=\delta_{c}^{\rm GR}\left(1+\gamma\lambda\right),

(51)

where
\gamma
is a dimensionless coefficient encoding the response of the nonlinear
collapse dynamics to the Rastall modification. Equation
51
should therefore be interpreted as the leading-order
perturbative evolution equation in a nearly conformal plasma,
where the effective deviation from GR is controlled by the small parameter

\gamma_{\rm eff}\equiv\gamma\delta.

(52)

The parameter
\gamma
is expected to be
\mathcal{O}(1)
in magnitude, but its
precise value depends on the perturbation profile and on the detailed nonlinear
evolution. In the absence of full numerical simulations, we treat
\gamma
as a
phenomenological parameter and explore representative values in the range

|\gamma|\lesssim 1
. Our conclusions regarding the exponential sensitivity of
the PBH abundance to
\lambda
are robust against moderate variations of
\gamma
.

A dedicated numerical study of relativistic collapse in Rastall gravity would be
required to determine
\gamma
from first principles. This positive
\gamma
means that positive
\lambda
makes collapse slightly harder (higher
\delta_{c}
), while negative
\lambda
makes collapse easier.

5.2
Variance of Density Fluctuations

The variance of density fluctuations smoothed on a comoving scale
R=1/k
is:

\sigma^{2}(R)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dk}{k}\mathcal{P}_{\delta}(k)W^{2}(kR),

(53)

where
W(kR)
is a window function (typically Gaussian or top-hat).

From the perturbation analysis, the density power spectrum at horizon crossing is related to the primordial curvature power spectrum by:

\mathcal{P}_{\delta}(k)=\frac{16}{81}(1+2\beta\lambda)\mathcal{T}^{2}(k\tau)\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k),

(54)

where the coefficient
\beta=\alpha_{H}
arises from matching the super-horizon solution to the sub-horizon evolution at horizon crossing. and
\mathcal{T}(k\tau)
is the transfer function.

For a scale-invariant primordial power spectrum
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=A
, and smoothing on the horizon scale
R=1/(aH)
at horizon crossing, the variance simplifies to:

\sigma(M_{*})=\frac{4}{9}(1+\beta\lambda)\sqrt{A},

(55)

where
M_{*}
is the horizon mass at horizon crossing.

5.3
Primordial Black Hole Abundance

The abundance of primordial black holes forming at a given mass scale
is computed using the Press–Schechter formalism under the assumption
of Gaussian statistics. The mass fraction collapsing into PBHs at the
time of formation is

\beta(M)=\int_{\delta_{c}}^{\infty}\frac{d\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma(M)}\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}(M)}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\frac{\delta_{c}(\lambda)}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma(M,\lambda)}\right),

(56)

where
\sigma(M,\lambda)
is the variance of density perturbations
smoothed on the mass scale
M
, and
\delta_{c}(\lambda)
is the
critical threshold for collapse.

In the regime relevant for PBH formation, the variance is typically
small,
\sigma\ll\delta_{c}
, and the abundance is dominated by the
exponential tail of the Gaussian distribution. In this limit,
Eq. (
56
) reduces to

\beta(M)\approx\frac{\sigma(M,\lambda)}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\delta_{c}(\lambda)}\exp\left(-\frac{\delta_{c}^{2}(\lambda)}{2\sigma^{2}(M,\lambda)}\right).

(57)

We now incorporate the leading-order Rastall corrections.
As discussed in the previous subsections, we parameterize the
collapse threshold and the variance as

\displaystyle\delta_{c}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\delta_{c}^{\rm GR}\left(1+\gamma\lambda\right),

(58)

\displaystyle\sigma(M,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\rm GR}(M)\left(1+\beta\lambda\right),

(59)

where
\gamma
encodes the modification of the nonlinear collapse
threshold, and
\beta
arises from the modification of the density
contrast at horizon crossing.

It is convenient to define the peak significance parameter

\nu_{c}(\lambda)\equiv\frac{\delta_{c}(\lambda)}{\sigma(M,\lambda)}.

(60)

Expanding to linear order in
\lambda
(with
|\lambda|\ll 1
), we obtain

\nu_{c}(\lambda)=\nu_{c}^{\rm GR}\left[1+(\gamma-\beta)\lambda\right],

(61)

where
\nu_{c}^{\rm GR}=\delta_{c}^{\rm GR}/\sigma_{\rm GR}
.

Substituting Eq. (
61
) into
Eq. (
57
) and keeping terms to leading order in

\lambda
inside the exponential yields

\frac{\beta(\lambda)}{\beta_{\rm GR}}\approx\exp\left[-(\nu_{c}^{\rm GR})^{2}(\gamma-\beta)\lambda\right],

(62)

where
\beta_{\rm GR}
denotes the abundance computed in general
relativity.

Equation (
62
) demonstrates that even a small
Rastall parameter
|\lambda|\lesssim 0.1
can lead to exponentially
large enhancements or suppressions of the PBH abundance, due to the
factor
(\nu_{c}^{\rm GR})^{2}
multiplying the correction.
For typical rare-peak scenarios where
\nu_{c}^{\rm GR}\sim 5
–
10
,
the exponential sensitivity implies that percent-level deviations in
the collapse dynamics can modify the PBH abundance by several orders
of magnitude.

6
Implications for PBH Dark Matter Scenarios

Observational constraints on primordial black hole (PBH) abundances
from microlensing, cosmic microwave background (CMB) distortions,
dynamical effects, and gravitational-wave observations leave several
mass windows in which PBHs could constitute a significant fraction,
or even the entirety, of dark matter. Representative examples include
the asteroid-mass window (
10^{17}
–
10^{22}
g),
the lunar-mass window (
10^{20}
–
10^{24}
g),
and portions of the stellar and intermediate-mass range.

In general relativity, producing an appreciable PBH abundance in any
of these windows requires a substantial enhancement of the primordial
curvature power spectrum relative to its CMB value

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\simeq 2\times 10^{-9}
.
Typically, amplitudes as large as
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\sim 10^{-2}
–
10^{-1}
are required at small scales,
implying a degree of fine-tuning in inflationary model building.

In Rastall gravity, the background expansion during radiation
domination remains identical to GR, but perturbation dynamics are
modified at linear order in the Rastall parameter
\lambda
.
As shown in the previous sections, the PBH abundance depends on the
combination

\nu_{c}(\lambda)=\nu_{c}^{\rm GR}\left[1+(\gamma-\beta)\lambda\right],

(63)

where
\beta
encodes the modification of the horizon-crossing
amplitude and
\gamma
parameterizes the shift in the nonlinear
collapse threshold.

Since the abundance scales approximately as

\beta(\lambda)\sim\exp\!\left[-\frac{\nu_{c}^{2}(\lambda)}{2}\right],

(64)

even small deviations from GR can produce exponentially large changes
in the PBH fraction. The sign and magnitude of the effect are governed
by the difference
(\gamma-\beta)
:

•

If
(\gamma-\beta)\lambda<0
, the effective peak significance

\nu_{c}
decreases and PBH formation is exponentially enhanced.

•

If
(\gamma-\beta)\lambda>0
, the effective peak significance
increases and PBH formation is suppressed.

Thus, Rastall gravity does not universally enhance PBH formation.
Rather, it modifies the abundance in a direction that depends on the
relative size of the linear perturbation correction and the nonlinear
collapse response.

For rare-peak scenarios with
\nu_{c}^{\rm GR}\sim 5
–
10
,
percent-level deviations in
(\gamma-\beta)\lambda

can shift the required primordial amplitude by

\mathcal{O}(10\%)
while producing order-of-magnitude changes in
the resulting PBH abundance.
Consequently, even modest deviations from GR may significantly relax
—or tighten—the fine-tuning requirements on the small-scale
primordial power spectrum.

An important implication is that PBH constraints can be reinterpreted
as constraints on the combination
(\gamma-\beta)\lambda
.
Because the background cosmology during radiation domination is
unchanged, PBH formation provides a particularly clean probe of
perturbation-level modifications in Rastall gravity.

Finally, if future observations were to establish a PBH dark matter
component with a well-measured mass function, it may be possible
to distinguish Rastall gravity from GR through the inferred
relation between the primordial power spectrum amplitude and the
observed abundance. In this sense, PBHs offer a complementary
avenue to large-scale structure and CMB tests for probing small
departures from standard gravitational dynamics.

7
Conclusions and Outlook

In this work we have presented the first systematic analysis of
primordial black hole (PBH) formation in Rastall gravity during
radiation domination. A central result of our study is that,
although the homogeneous background evolution remains identical
to general relativity (GR) for a pure radiation fluid,
the perturbation dynamics are modified at linear order in the
Rastall parameter
\lambda
.

We derived the modified evolution equation for the density contrast
during radiation domination and showed that Rastall gravity alters
both the effective gravitational driving term and the effective sound
speed. Interestingly, at linear order in
\lambda
, the Jeans scale
remains unchanged due to a cancellation between these two effects.
However, the super-horizon evolution and the amplitude of the density
contrast at horizon crossing acquire
\lambda
-dependent corrections.
As a consequence, the variance of density perturbations on the
horizon scale is modified as

\sigma(M,\lambda)=\sigma_{\rm GR}(M)\left(1+\beta\lambda\right),

(65)

where
\beta
is determined by the linear perturbation matching.

The nonlinear collapse threshold
\delta_{c}
cannot be determined
from linear theory alone. Since a full relativistic collapse
analysis in Rastall gravity has not yet been performed,
we adopted a phenomenological parameterization

\delta_{c}(\lambda)=\delta_{c}^{\rm GR}\left(1+\gamma\lambda\right),

(66)

where
\gamma
encodes the response of nonlinear collapse dynamics
to the modified gravitational coupling.

Combining these effects, we showed that the peak significance
parameter becomes

\nu_{c}(\lambda)=\nu_{c}^{\rm GR}\left[1+(\gamma-\beta)\lambda\right].

(67)

Because the PBH abundance depends exponentially on
\nu_{c}^{2}
,
even small deviations from GR can produce exponentially large
enhancements or suppressions:

\frac{\beta(\lambda)}{\beta_{\rm GR}}\sim\exp\!\left[-(\nu_{c}^{\rm GR})^{2}(\gamma-\beta)\lambda\right].

(68)

Our analysis demonstrates that PBH formation provides a sensitive
probe of modified gravity even in scenarios where the background
cosmology is indistinguishable from GR. The exponential sensitivity
of the abundance makes PBHs particularly powerful for constraining
small deviations from standard gravitational dynamics.

Outlook

Several important extensions of this work remain.

First, a definitive determination of the collapse threshold

\delta_{c}(\lambda)
requires solving the fully nonlinear
relativistic collapse equations in Rastall gravity.
Such a study would fix the parameter
\gamma
from first
principles and determine whether the modification enhances
or suppresses collapse in realistic perturbation profiles.

Second, the linear perturbation analysis could be extended
to include possible non-adiabatic modes arising from the
non-conservation of the energy–momentum tensor.
A gauge-invariant treatment of curvature perturbations
in Rastall gravity would clarify the robustness of the
horizon-crossing modification.

Third, it would be valuable to compute the induced stochastic
gravitational wave background associated with enhanced
scalar perturbations in this framework.
Since the background expansion is unmodified during radiation
domination, any observable deviation would arise purely from
perturbation dynamics, providing a clean diagnostic of the theory.

Finally, embedding Rastall gravity within a consistent early-universe
model (for example, during inflation or reheating) would allow
a self-consistent calculation of the primordial curvature power
spectrum and its possible
\lambda
-dependent features.

In summary, primordial black hole formation offers a novel and
highly sensitive window into Rastall gravity. Even when the
background cosmology mimics general relativity, perturbation-level
effects can leave exponentially amplified imprints on the PBH
abundance, making future observational constraints on PBHs
a promising probe of modified gravitational dynamics.

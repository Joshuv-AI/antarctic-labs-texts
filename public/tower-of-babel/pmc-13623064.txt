Neoadjuvant Intralesional Daromun (L19IL2/L19TNF) in Resectable Locally Advanced Melanoma: An Update on the Efficacy and Safety Results of the PIVOTAL Phase III Trial
Authors: Axel Hauschild, Jessica Cecile Hassel, Mirjana Ziemer, Piotr Rutkowski, Friedegund Meier, Lukas Flatz, Caroline Gaudy-Marqueste, Mario Santinami, Francesco Russano, Imke von Wasielewski, Thomas Eigentler, Michele Maio, Iris Zalaudek, Sebastian Haferkamp, Pietro Quaglino, Julia Welzel, Michael Weichenthal, Giuliano Elia, Alfredo Covelli, Katia Lorizzo, Agnese Parca, Dario Neri, Paolo Antonio Ascierto, Caroline Robert, Dirk Schadendorf, Katharina Charlotte Kaehler
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Daromun (L19IL2/L19TNF) was investigated as a neoadjuvant, intralesional therapy for patients with fully resectable stage III melanoma in the phase III PIVOTAL trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02938299). The trial enrolled 256 patients in the European Union and met its primary end point, demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in recurrence-free survival (RFS; hazard ratio, 0.59; P = .005) for daromun followed by surgery versus up-front surgery, at a median follow-up (FU) of 21 months from random assignment. PIVOTAL included two clinically distinct subgroups, namely, patients with de novo diagnosed metastatic disease (n = 34; 13%) and patients with recurrence(s) after surgery with or without radiotherapy and/or adjuvant systemic therapies (n = 222; 87%). Here, we present an updated analysis of the primary and secondary end points, including safety data, at a median FU of 36.8 months from random assignment (database cutoff: November 28, 2025), alongside new sensitivity analyses of event-free survival (EFS). The updated analysis confirms the clinically and statistically meaningful improvements in RFS and distant metastasis-free survival recorded in the neoadjuvant daromun versus control arm. The EFS post hoc analysis, conducted in both the overall population and the recurrent patient subgroups (with or without prior systemic therapies), provides consistency and robustness to the benefit of neoadjuvant daromun observed for the primary efficacy end point. No new safety signals of concern were recorded.

INTRODUCTION

The landmark NADINA and SWOG S1801 trials established neoadjuvant immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) as the emerging standard of care for resectable stage III melanoma.1,2 Importantly, both trials enrolled patients without prior exposure to ICI. A consistent proportion of these patients, however, experience relapse after prior (neo)adjuvant treatment with ICIs and their combinations and have never been thoroughly investigated in previous trials.1,2

Intralesional therapies offer a localized approach to immune modulation, allowing high intratumoral drug exposure with limited systemic toxicity.3 Daromun, a novel intralesional immunocytokine,4,5 promotes robust local tumor regression and induction of systemic antitumor immune responses by combining the T-cell–stimulating properties of IL-2 with the proinflammatory and vascular-disrupting effects of TNF. Early clinical studies have demonstrated the promising activity of daromun in patients with injectable melanoma lesions, including regression of both injected and noninjected tumors.4

The phase III PIVOTAL trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02938299) is a randomized study evaluating neoadjuvant intralesional daromun in patients with fully resectable, locally advanced melanoma, who were mostly pretreated. The trial enrolled 256 patients in the European Union and met its primary end point, showing statistically significant improvement in recurrence-free survival (RFS; hazard ratio [HR], 0.59; P = .005) of daromun versus up-front surgery, with a mild and manageable safety profile.5 The PIVOTAL trial included patients newly diagnosed and patients with recurrent disease, that is, at first or further relapse after surgery with or without radiotherapy (RT) or systemic therapies, representing 13% and 87% of the overall population, respectively.

Here, we present an updated analysis of primary and secondary end points at a median follow-up (FU) period of 36.8 months from random assignment. We also present a post hoc analysis of event-free survival (EFS) in the overall population. Exploratory EFS analyses were also conducted in the subgroup of patients with recurrent disease, either as a whole or stratified based on prior therapies (systemic v surgery/RT).

METHODS

A total 256 adult patients with locally advanced melanoma and at least one injectable skin or nodal lesion, eligible for complete surgical resection, were randomly assigned 1:1 to two study arms to receive either (1) intralesional neoadjuvant daromun (n = 127, Arm 1; 13 million IU of L19IL2 and 400 μg of L19TNF, once weekly for up to 4 weeks) followed by surgery or (2) up-front surgery (n = 129, Arm 2).

Prior antitumor treatment, including surgery, RT, and approved systemic therapies, was allowed, as well as adjuvant treatment after surgery with approved therapies at physician's discretion.

The primary outcome was RFS in a blinded independent central review (BICR) analysis. Secondary end points included distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS), overall survival (OS), and safety. A post hoc analysis of EFS was also performed. Safety was assessed by capturing adverse event (AE) data using National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI CTCAE version 4.03).

HR for comparison of survival curves was estimated using univariate Cox regression analyses. The proportional hazards assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residuals.

Additional information of trial design, patient population, study objectives, and definitions of primary and secondary end points and details of the statistical methods used are given in the Data Supplement (online only).

RESULTS

Patients

A total 364 patients were screened between June 2016 and May 2023, and 256 of these were randomly assigned and included in the intention to-treat analysis (127 in arm 1 and 129 in arm 2). At random assignment, 34 patients were newly diagnosed with stage III melanoma, whereas 222 patients had already experienced relapse(s) after surgery + RT with or without systemic treatments, including prior adjuvant therapies (the median time from prior systemic therapy was 16.5 months [arm 1] and 25.5 months [arm 2]).

Overall, postsurgery adjuvant treatment was conducted on 32.3% (41 of 127) and 41.8% (54 of 129) of patients in the treatment and control arm, respectively. Among recurrent patients, these proportions were 28.7% (31 of 108) and 37.7% (43 of 114), respectively.

Efficacy

The updated primary outcome analysis in the BICR assessment at a median FU of 36.8 months from random assignment (database cutoff November 28, 2025) confirmed a clinically relevant improvement in RFS (HR, 0.55 [95% CI, 0.38 to 0.78]; P = <.001) for daromun versus up-front surgery (Fig 2A) as well as for DMFS (HR, 0.53 [95% CI, 0.33 to 0.83]; P = .005; Fig 2B). The EFS sensitivity analysis performed in the overall population was consistent with the RFS results (HR, 0.71 [95% CI, 0.51 to 0.98]; P = .034; Fig 2C).

Further subgroup analyses of recurrent patients, according to their treatment history, showed comparable RFS benefit for those patients who had received and failed prior surgery accompanied by systemic therapies (HR, 0.53 [95% CI, 0.29 to 0.95]; Fig 3C) and for those who had only received surgery with or without RT (HR, 0.46 [95% CI, 0.27 to 0.78]; Fig 3E).

Exploratory EFS in the recurrent population favored neoadjuvant therapy (HR, 0.68 [95% CI, 0.48 to 0.97]; Fig 3B). Further analyses of the recurrent patient group were also performed according to the systemic treatment history. The EFS benefit showed a strong trend in recurrent patients who had received and failed prior surgery accompanied by systemic therapies (HR, 0.68 [95% CI, 0.40 to 1.15]; Fig 3D) and in those who had only received surgery with or without RT (HR, 0.65 [95% CI, 0.40 to 1.05]; Fig 3F). However, these results demonstrate only a strong trend without reaching statistical significance.

OS data were still immature at the time of data cutoff (37 of 104 events recorded).

Safety

The updated safety profile of daromun remained manageable in the overall population, with no new concerning signals recorded.5 By the database cutoff date, AEs of NCI CTCAE grade ≥3 were reported in 33.6% (41 of 122) of safety-evaluable (SE) patients in the neoadjuvant arm and in 11.3% (14 of 124) of SE patients in the control arm. Grade 3 treatment-related AEs (TRAEs) occurred in 28.7% (35 of 122) of SE patients in the daromun arm and in 7.3% (9 of 124) of patients in the control arm. The most frequently reported grade 3 TRAE in patients treated with daromun was injection site reaction occurring in 12.3% (15 of 122) of SE patients. There were no CTC grade >3 TRAEs and no treatment-related deaths.

DISCUSSION

The updated analysis confirms that neoadjuvant intralesional daromun provides clinically and statistically meaningful improvements in RFS and DMFS in patients with locally advanced recurrent melanoma. Exploratory EFS analyses, which were designed to capture preoperative events that could prevent definitive surgery, produced consistent results with RFS and DMFS analyses. A post hoc analysis of EFS confirms the benefit of neoadjuvant daromun observed with the primary efficacy end point (RFS).

In the PIVOTAL trial, most patients (87%) had locally advanced recurrent melanoma after prior surgery with or without RT, with or without prior systemic adjuvant therapy. Both RFS and EFS analyses demonstrate a clinically significant advantage for the experimental arm in this higher-risk group. The safety profile was consistent with that observed in the overall trial, with no new safety concerns emerging.

Recent perioperative immunotherapy trials1,2 have established the role of neoadjuvant therapy in de novo stage III melanoma. The findings of the present study extend the scope of neoadjuvant strategies to include recurrent patients, whether they have received prior systemic treatment. The results also support tumor-targeted intralesional cytokine delivery as a means of boosting local immune modulation while minimizing systemic exposure.4,5 However, the study has several limitations, including its open-label design, the exploratory nature of the subgroup analyses, and the immaturity of the OS data. A longer FU period is required to clarify the potential impact on survival, and further prospective investigation in nodal-positive patients is necessary. A parallel, Phase 3 neoadjuvant study in stage III resectable melanoma is ongoing in the United States and various EU countries (NeoDREAM; NCT03567889). Future studies should evaluate predictive biomarkers of response and determine daromun's role in contemporary perioperative treatment sequences, including its optimal integration with checkpoint blockade and/or targeted therapy.

Esakia order-compactifications and locally Esakia spaces
Rodrigo Nicolau Almeida, Guram Bezhanishvili, and Nick Bezhanishvili

Abstract
We introduce Esakia order-compactifications and study how they fit in the general theory of Priestley order-compactifications. We provide an analog of Dwinger’s theorem by characterizing Esakia order-compactifications by means of special rings of upsets. These considerations naturally lead to the notion of a locally Esakia space, for which we prove that taking the largest Esakia order-compacification is functorial, thus obtaining an analog of Banaschewski’s theorem.

Introduction

The theory of compactifications is one of the mainstream areas of
point-set topology. It is a classic result
that a topological space
X
admits a compactification iff it is completely regular, and that the Stone-Čech compactification
\beta X
is the largest
compactification of
X
(see, e.g.,
[
12
, Ch. 3.5]
).
In fact, the correspondence
X\mapsto\beta X
extends to
a functor which is left adjoint to the inclusion of the category of compact Hausdorff spaces into the category of completely regular spaces
(see, e.g.,
[
16
, pp.130-131]
).

In algebraic logic, a special role is played
by those compact Hausdorff spaces that are zero-dimensional as they serve as Stone duals of Boolean algebras, and are widely known as
Stone spaces
. This motivates the study of zero-dimensional compactifications, the theory of which resembles that of all compactifications: a topological space
X
admits a zero-dimensional compactification iff it is a zero-dimensional (Hausdorff) space, and there exists the largest zero-dimensional compactification
\beta_{0}X
, which is the Stone dual of the Boolean algebra
\mathsf{Clop}(X)
of all clopen susbets of
X

[
2
]
. The compactification
\beta_{0}X
is known as the
Banaschewski compactification
and the correspondence
X\mapsto\beta_{0}X
extends to a functor which is left adjoint to the inclusion of the category of Stone spaces into the category of zero-dimensional spaces
[
2
]
(see also
[
3
]
).
By Dwinger’s theorem
[
11
, Thm. 13.1]
(see also
[
19
]
), all zero-dimensional compactifications of
X
can be characterized as Stone duals of
Boolean bases
; that is, Boolean subalgebras of
\mathsf{Clop}(X)
that are bases for the topology.

Nachbin generalized the theory of compactifications to that of
order-compactifications (see, e.g.,
[
21
]
), which was further developed
by numerous authors.
In this theory,
the role of zero-dimensional compactifications is played by Priestley order-compactifications
[
17
,
7
]
. Priestey spaces are of special importance in algebraic logic as they serve as Priestley duals of distributive lattices, thus generalizing Stone duality for Boolean algebras.
For ordered spaces, the analog of zero-dimensionality is that of order-zero-dimensionality, and an ordered space
X
admits a Priestley order-compactification iff it is order-zero-dimensional
[
7
, Cor. 3.8]
. Moreover, there exists the largest Priestley order-compactification
\eta_{0}X
, which is the Priestley dual of the distributive lattice
\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)
of all clopen upsets of
X
, and the correspondence
X\mapsto\eta_{0}X
extends to a functor which is left adjoint to the inclusion of the category of Priestley spaces into the category of order-zero-dimensional spaces
[
7
, Rem. 3.10]
. Furthermore, all Priestley order-compactifications of
X
can be characterized as Priestley duals of
Priestley bases
; that is, sublattices of
\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)
that generate the topology of
X

[
7
, Sec. 5]
. Thus, both Banschewski’s compactification and Dwinger’s theorem have natural generalizations to the order-zero-dimensional setting.

Alongside Stone spaces and Priestley spaces, Esakia spaces play a crucial role in algebraic logic as they serve as Priestley duals of Heyting algebras (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
).
Esakia spaces are those Priestley spaces in which the order is continuous.
Our aim is to develop the theory of Esakia order-compactifications that refines that of Priestley order-compactifications, and provide a generalization of the Banaschewski and Dwinger results in the setting of continuous orders. As we will see, this gives rise to two non-equivalent notions of order-compactifications, which we term Heyting and Esakia order-compactifications (see Definition
4.10
). These, in turn, induce two refinements of order-zero-dimensionality: H-order-zero-dimensionality and E-order-zero-dimensionality (see Definition
4.16
),
the latter being especially pertinent for our purposes.

We provide an analog of Dwinger’s theorem for both Heyting and Esakia order-compactifications, and then consider the problem of functoriality of Esakia order-compactifications. Since the maps in the category of Esakia spaces are continuous p-morphisms, it is natural to consider them as the maps in the category of E-order-zero-dimensional spaces. This leads
to the key notion of a
locally Esakia space
, an E-order-zero-dimensional space in which the upset of each point is compact. This notion naturally generalizes both Esakia spaces and image-finite posets (in which the upset of each point is finite). We prove that the correspondence
X\mapsto\eta_{0}X
lifts to a functor which is left adjoint to the inclusion of the category of Esakia spaces into the category of locally Esakia spaces and continuous p-morphisms, thus providing an analog of Banaschewski’s result for locally Esakia spaces.

Order-separation and order-zero-dimensionality

We assume the reader’s familiarity with lattice theory (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
), topology (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
), and category theory (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
).
As in
[
16
]
, all lattices are assumed to be bounded and all lattice homomorphisms preserve the bounds. We will use letters
L,D
to denote distributive lattices,
H
to denote Heyting algebras, and
B
to denote Boolean algebras.

As is customary, for a poset
X
and
S\subseteq X
, we write

{\uparrow}S=\{x\in X\mid\exists s\in S:s\leq x\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\downarrow}S=\{x\in X\mid\exists s\in S:x\leq s\}.

We say that
S
is an
upset
if
S={\uparrow}S
and a
downset
if
S={\downarrow}S
.
If
S=\{x\}
is a singleton, we simply write
{\uparrow}x
and
{\downarrow}x
. We let
\mathsf{Up}(X)
be the set of upsets of
X
, and note that
\mathsf{Up}(X)
is a complete and completely distributive lattice, where joins and meets are set-theoretic union and intersection.

By an

ordered topological space
or simply an
ordered space
we mean a poset
X
which is also a topological space.
The study of ordered topological spaces was pioneered by Nachbin. His main findings were collected in
[
21
]
, which we use as one of the main references in this section.

Definition 2.1
.

An ordered
space
X
is a
Nachbin space
if
X
is compact and the order
\leq
is closed (in the product space
X\times X
).

It is well known (see
[
21
, p. 26]
) that the order
\leq
being closed is equivalent to the following order-separation axiom:
For each
x,y\in X
, if
x\nleq y
then there exist an upset neighbourhood
U
of
x
and a downset neighbourhood
V
of
y
such that
U\cap V=\emptyset
. For obvious reasons, this separation is known as
order-Hausdorffness
(see
[
20
]
). In particular, it implies that
X
is a Hausdorff space.

In this paper, we will mainly be interested in order-zero-dimensional spaces, which are order-topological analogues of zero-dimensional spaces. We recall that a subset of a topological space
X
is
clopen
if it is both closed and open, and that
X
is
zero-dimensional
if the set
{\sf Clop}(X)
of clopen subsets of
X
is a basis for the topology on
X
. For an ordered
space
X
, we let
\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)
be the set of clopen upsets of
X
.

As a general rule, we will refer to
order-topological properties as
“order-P,”
e.g., order-zero-dimensionality. Some authors call such spaces zero-dimensional ordered spaces (see, e.g.,

[
22
]
). Since the latter could alternatively be interpreted as an ordered space that happens to be zero-dimensional, we prefer the usage of “order-P” to avoid any unnecessary confusion.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be an ordered
space.

(1)

(Priestley separation) We say that
X
satisfies the
Priestley separation axiom
provided for each
x,y\in X
, if
x\not\leq y
then there is a clopen upset
U
such that
x\in U
and
y\notin U
.

(2)

(Order-zero-dimensionality) We call
X

order-zero-dimensional
if

(a)

X
satisfies the Priestley separation axiom, and

(b)

the set
\{U\,\setminus\,V:U,V\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)\}
is a basis for the topology.

Remark 2.3
.

It is clear that Priestley separation implies order-Hausdorffness. Moreover, since
U\,\setminus\,V
is a convex set for each
U,V\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)
, each order-zero-dimensional space has a basis of clopen convex sets, hence is locally convex (see
[
21
, p. 27]
).

A class of ordered spaces closely related to order-zero-dimensional spaces is that of Priestley spaces, which play a central role in the representation theory of distributive lattices.

Definition 2.4
.

[
10
, 258]

An ordered
space
X
is a
Priestley space
if it is compact and satisfies the Priestley separation axiom.

Theorem 2.5

(Priestley duality,
[
23
]
)
.

The category
\sf Pries
of Priestley spaces and continuous order-preserving maps is dually equivalent to the category
\sf DLat
of distributive lattices and lattice homomorphisms.

Priestley
duality
closely parallels Stone duality for Boolean algebras. We briefly recall the action of the functors on objects as this will be used in what follows. With each Priestley space
X
we associate its dual distributive lattice
\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)
, and with each continuous order-preserving map
f:X\to Y
,
the lattice homomorphism
f^{-1}:\mathsf{ClopUp}(Y)\to\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)
.

With each distributive lattice
D
, we associate the Priestley space
\mathsf{Spec}(D)
of prime fiters of
D
ordered by inclusion and topologized by the
basis
\{\varphi(a)\,\setminus\,\varphi(b):a,b\in D\}
, where

\varphi(a)=\{P\in\mathsf{Spec}(D):a\in P\}.

With each lattice homomorphism
f:D\to D^{\prime}
we associate the continuous order-preserving map

f^{-1}:\mathsf{Spec}(D^{\prime})\to\mathsf{Spec}(D)
.

We will freely use the following well-known facts about Priestley spaces (see, e.g.,
[
24
, Prop. 2.6]
and
[
10
, Ch. 11]
):

Fact 2.6
.

For each Priestley space
X
, we have:

(1)

X
is a Nachbin space. Consequently, if
F
is a closed subset of
X
, then both
{\uparrow}F
and
{\downarrow}F
are closed.

(2)

If
F
and
G
are closed subsets of
X
such that
{\uparrow}F\cap{\downarrow}G=\emptyset
, then there is a clopen upset
U
of
X
such that
F\subseteq U
and
G\subseteq X\,\setminus\,U
.

(3)

The set
\{U\,\setminus\,V:U,V\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)\}

forms a basis for the topology on
X
. Consequently, each clopen
W\subseteq X
is of the form
W=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\left(U_{i}\,\setminus\,V_{i}\right)
, where
U_{i},V_{i}
are clopen upsets.

3
Order-compactifications and Priestley order-compactifications

The notion of order-compactification has two natural generalizations to the setting of ordered spaces. The more restrictive of the two was already studied by Nachbin
[
21
]
, which we call
N-order-compactification
; the more general one was later introduced by Blatter
[
8
]
, and became more standard.

Definition 3.1
.

A map
f:X\to Y
between ordered
spaces is an
order-embedding
if

•

f
is a topological embedding;

•

x\leq y
iff

f(x)\leq f(y)
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be an ordered
space.

(1)

An
order-compactification
of
X
is a pair
(Y,e)
, where
Y
is a Nachbin space and
e:X\to Y
is an order-embedding such that
e[X]
is dense in
Y
.

(2)

We call an order-compactification
(Y,e)
an
N-order-compactification
if the order
\leq_{Y}
on
Y
is the closure of the image of
\leq_{X}
under the product map
e\times e:X\times X\to Y\times Y
.

Remark 3.3
.

When it does not cause any confusion, we identify
X
with its image
e[X]
in
Y
, view
X
as a dense subspace of
Y
, and
Y
as an order-compactification of
X
. Then,
Y
is an N-order-compactification of
X
iff the order on
X
is dense in the order on
Y
.

It is a classic result that a topological space
X
has an order-compactification iff it is completely regular, in which case the collection of (equivalence classes of) all compactifications has a natural partial order and the Stone-Čech compactification
\beta X
is the largest compactification in this partial order
[
12
, pp.166-168]
. These results generalize to the setting of order-compactifications
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

[
21
, Sec. II.1]

An ordered
space
X
is

completely-order-regular

if

(1)

For each
x,y\in X
, if
x\nleq y
then there is a continuous order-preserving
f:X\to[0,1]
such that
f(x)>f(y)
.

(2)

For each
x\in X
and closed set
F
with
x\notin F
, there exist a continuous order-preserving

f:X\to[0,1]
and a continuous order-reversing
g:X\to[0,1]
such that
f(x)=1=g(x)
and
F\subseteq f^{-1}(0)\cup g^{-1}(0)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

[
8
, Thm. 1.5(i)]

An ordered space has an order-compactification iff it is completely-order-regular.

We also have an obvious order on order-compactifications:

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(Y_{1},e_{1})
and
(Y_{2},e_{2})
be order-compactifications of a completely-order-regular space
X
. We write
(Y_{1},e_{1})\preceq(Y_{2},e_{2})
if there exists a continuous order-preserving

{f:Y_{2}\to Y_{1}}
such that
f\circ e_{2}=e_{1}
.

Since
e_{1}[X]
is dense in
Y_{1}
, such an

is always onto. Moreover, as for compactifications, we have that
(Y_{1},e_{1})\preceq(Y_{2},e_{2})

(Y_{2},e_{2})\preceq(Y_{1},e_{1})
iff there is an order-homeomorphism

f:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
such that
f\circ e_{1}=e_{2}
, and this defines a partial order on the collection of (equivalence classes of) order-compactifications
[
8
, 57]
,
which we denote by
\mathcal{K}(X)

The largest element of this poset

by generalizing the construction of the Stone-Čech compactificaion to the setting of ordered spaces. We refer to it as the

The isomorphism of Theorem

Definition 3.12
.

Let

(Y,e)

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
X
be order-zero-dimensional and
(Y,e)
a Priestley order-compactification of
X
. Then
(Y,e)
is an N-order-compactification iff
\mathcal{R}_{Y}
is an N-base.

Proof.

To simplify notation, we assume that
X
is a subspace of

and

3.3

First suppose that

is an N-order-compactification of

is the closure of
\leq_{X}
(in

{\uparrow}_{X}(W\cap X)\cap{\downarrow}_{X}(V\cap X)=\emptyset

{\uparrow}_{X}(W\cap X)
denotes the upset of
W\cap X
in
X
and
{\downarrow}_{X}(V\cap X)
the downset of
V\cap X
in

{\uparrow}W\cap{\downarrow}V=\varnothing

\leq_{Y}
is the closure of
\leq_{X}
. Therefore,
\leq_{Y}\cap(W\times V)=\emptyset
, and hence
{\uparrow}W\cap{\downarrow}V=\varnothing
.
Since

.
2

Conversely, suppose that

is not an N-order-compactification of

. Then there is
(x,y)
in
\leq_{Y}
such that

Since

Remark 3.14

As the notation suggests,

(see also
[

Definition 3.15

Let
X

(1)

(Complete-order-separation) Let

would be clopen in
X
, which would imply that
D
is clopen in
C
, a contradiction. Thus,
X
is not continuously ordered, and hence it is not E-order-zero-dimensional by Theorem
4.17
.

We show that
X
is H-order-zero-dimensional.
Let
C^{\prime}
be a copy of
C
and let
Y
be the topological sum
C\cup C^{\prime}
with the order given by the reflexive closure of
\{(x,x^{\prime}):x\in C\}
in
C\cup C^{\prime}
.
To see that
Y
is an Esakia space, we clearly have that
{\uparrow}y
is closed for each
y\in Y
. Moreover, each clopen of
Y
is of the form
U\cup V^{\prime}
, with
U,V
clopen of
C
, so
{\downarrow}(U\cup V^{\prime})=(U\cup V^{\prime})\cup V
, which is clopen of
Y
. In addition,
X
is (homeomorphic to) a dense subspace of
Y
. Thus,
Y
is a Heyting order-compactification of
X
, and hence
X
is H-order-zero-dimensional.

5
Locally Esakia spaces and functoriality

It is a classic result in topology that taking the Stone-Čech compactification is left adjoint to the inclusion of the category of compact Hausdorff spaces into that of completely regular spaces (see, e.g.,
[
16
, pp.130-131]
).
Likewise, taking the Banaschewski compactification is left adjoint to the inclusion of the category of Stone spaces into that of zero-dimensional (Hausdorff) spaces
[
2
]
.
The latter result extends to yield that
\eta_{0}
is left adjoint to the inclusion of the category of Priestley spaces into that of order-zero-dimensional spaces (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Rem. 3.10]
). We will prove the same result for the category
\mathsf{Esa}
of Esakia spaces. As we saw in Theorem
4.17
, when
X
is E-order-zero-dimensional,
\eta_{0}X
is the largest Esakia order-compactification of
X
. Therefore, a natural category to work with appears to be the category of E-order-zero-dimensional spaces. However, the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\eta_{0}X
may in general not be a p-morphism. We thus need to restrict our attention to those E-order-zero-dimensional spaces in which the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\eta_{0}X
is indeed a p-moprhism. This gives rise to the notion of a locally Esakia space, which is the main subject of study in this section.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be order-zero-dimensional and
Y
a Heyting order-compactification of
X
. If
X
is an upset of
Y
, then
{\uparrow}_{X}x
is an Esakia space for each
x\in X
.

Proof.

Since
Y
is an Esakia space,

{\uparrow}_{Y}x
is an Esakia space (see Fact
4.6
.
1
). But since
X
is an upset of
Y
, we have
{\uparrow}_{X}x={\uparrow}_{Y}x
, yielding the result.
∎

In light of Lemma
5.1
, we will restrict our attention to those order-zero-dimensional spaces
X
in which
{\uparrow}x
is an Esakia space for each
x\in X
. The key to ensure this is that
{\uparrow}x
is compact.

Definition 5.2
.

(1)

We call an ordered space
X

image-compact
provided
{\uparrow}x
is compact for each
x\in X
.

(2)

We call an ordered space
X

locally Esakia
provided it is E-order-zero-dimensional and image-compact.

(3)

Let
\mathsf{LocEsa}
denote the category of locally Esakia spaces and continuous p-morphisms.

Remark 5.3
.

(1)

The above notion of an image-compact space generalizes the well-known notion of an
image-finite poset
—a poset in which
{\uparrow}x
is finite for each
x
(such posets were first considered in
[
6
, Def. 4.6]
and were coined “image-finite” in
[
5
, Def. 3.5]
). Indeed, if the topology of an ordered space
X
is discrete, then
X
is image-compact iff it is image-finite. Consequently, both Esakia spaces and image-finite posets are
examples of image-compact spaces.

(2)

Clearly
\mathsf{Esa}
is a full subcategory of
\mathsf{LocEsa}
. In fact, a locally Esakia space is an Esakia space iff it is compact. Thus, locally Esakia spaces generalize Esakia spaces the same way locally Stone spaces generalize Stone spaces.

The next result characterizes when
X
is an upset in its order-compactification that is an Esakia space.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
X
be order-zero-dimensional and
Y
a Heyting order-compactification of
X
. Then
X
is an upset of
Y
iff
X
is image-compact and
Y
is an Esakia order-compactification of
X
.

Proof.

First suppose that
X
is an upset of
Y
.
Then
X
is image-compact by Lemma
5.1
. Moreover, since
X
is an upset in
Y
, we have
{\uparrow}_{X}x={\uparrow}_{Y}x
for each
x\in X
, so
{\uparrow}_{X}x
is dense in
{\uparrow}_{Y}x
, and hence
Y
is an Esakia order-compactification of
X
.

Conversely, suppose that
X
is image-compact and
Y
is an Esakia order-compactification of
X
.
Let
x\in X
and
x\leq_{Y}y
.
Consider the family

\mathcal{F}=\{{\uparrow}_{Y}x\cap U\cap X:y\in U\}\cup\{{\uparrow}_{Y}x\,\setminus\,(V\cap X):y\notin V\},

where
U,V\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(Y)
. If
\mathcal{F}
does not have the finite intersection property, then

{\uparrow}_{Y}x\cap U_{1}\cap...\cap U_{n}\cap X\subseteq(V_{1}\cup...\cup V_{m})\cap X.

for some
U_{1},...,U_{n},V_{1},...,V_{m}\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(Y)
.
Let
U=U_{1}\cap...\cap U_{n}
and
V=V_{1}\cup...\cup V_{m}
. Then
{U,V\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)}
and
{\uparrow}_{Y}x\cap U\cap X\subseteq V\cap X
. . Therefore,
{\uparrow}_{Y}x\cap X\subseteq(Y\,\setminus\,U)\cup V
. Since
Y
is an Esakia order-compactification of
X
,
{\uparrow}_{X}x
is dense in
{\uparrow}_{Y}x
, so

{\uparrow}_{Y}x=\overline{{\uparrow}_{Y}x\cap X}\subseteq(Y\,\setminus\,U)\cup V.

Thus,
x\in U\rightarrow_{\mathsf{ClopUp}(Y)}V
, and hence
y\in U\rightarrow_{\mathsf{ClopUp}(Y)}V
, which is a contradiction since
y\in U
and
y\notin V
.
Consequently,
\mathcal{F}
has the finite intersection property, and because
{\uparrow}_{Y}x
is compact, there is

y^{\prime}\in\bigcap\{{\uparrow}_{Y}x\cap U\cap X:y\in U\}\cap\bigcap\{{\uparrow}_{Y}x\,\setminus\,(V\cap X):y\notin V\}.

Since
y
and
y^{\prime}
are contained in the same clopen upsets of
Y
, it follows from the Priestley separation axiom that

y=y^{\prime}
, so

y\in X
, and hence
X
is an upset of
Y
.
∎

From this we obtain the following version
of Theorem
4.17
for image-compact spaces.

Theorem 5.5
.

For an order-zero-dimensional space
X
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is a locally Esakia space.

(2)

X
has an Esakia order-compactification
Y
in which
X
is an upset.

(3)

\eta_{0}X
is an Esakia order-compactification in which
X
is an upset.

Proof.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2) is obvious, and (1)
\Rightarrow
(3) follows from Theorem
4.17
and Proposition
5.4
. We prove (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).
That
X
is image-compact follows from Proposition
5.4
. We show that
X
is continuously ordered. Let
U\subseteq X
be open. Then
U=V\cap X
for some open
V\subseteq Y
.
Since
X
is an upset of
Y
,

{\downarrow}_{X}U={\downarrow}_{X}(V\cap X)={\downarrow}_{Y}V\cap X

(because
x\in X
,
x\leq_{Y}y
, and
y\in V
imply that
y\in V\cap X
).
Since
Y
is an Esakia space,
{\downarrow}_{Y}V
is open. Therefore,
{\downarrow}_{X}U
is open, and hence
X
is continuously ordered. Thus, it is left to apply Theorem
4.17
.
∎

Let
\mathsf{OZD}
be the category of order-zero-dimensional spaces and continuous order-preserving maps. As we pointed out at the beginning of this section,
\eta_{0}
is left adjoint to the embedding
\mathsf{Pries}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{OZD}
. Thus, each
\mathsf{OZD}
-morphism
f:X\to Z
, where
X\in\mathsf{OZD}
and
Z\in\mathsf{Pries}
, has a unique lift
\eta_{0}f:\eta_{0}X\to Z
. We give a convenient description of the lift, generalizing
[
6
, Lem. 3.1]
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
X\in\mathsf{OZD}
,
Z\in\mathsf{Pries}
, and
f:X\to Z
be continuous and order-preserving.

(1)

The unique lift
\eta_{0}f:\eta_{0}X\to Z
is given by
(\eta_{0}f)(x)=\bigcap\mathcal{F}_{x}^{f}\cap\bigcap\mathcal{I}_{x}^{f}
for each
x\in\eta_{0}X
, where

\mathcal{F}^{f}_{x}=\{{\uparrow}\overline{f[U]}:U\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(X),U\in x\}\text{ and }\mathcal{I}_{x}^{f}=\{{\downarrow}\overline{f[X\,\setminus\,V]}:V\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(X),V\notin x\}.

(2)

For each
x\in\eta_{0}X
and
U,V\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(Z)
,

x\in(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[U\,\setminus\,V]\Longleftrightarrow f^{-1}[U]\in x\mbox{ and }f^{-1}[V]\notin x.

Proof.

(
1
) Let
x\in\eta_{0}X
. First we show that

\bigcap\mathcal{F}_{x}^{f}\cap\bigcap\mathcal{I}_{x}^{f}

is a singleton.
If the intersection was empty, we would have

{\uparrow}\overline{f[U_{1}]}\cap...\cap{\uparrow}\overline{f[U_{n}]}\cap{\downarrow}\overline{f[X\,\setminus\,V_{1}]}\cap...\cap{\downarrow}\overline{f[X\,\setminus\,V_{m}]}=\emptyset

for some
U_{1},...,U_{n},V_{1},...,V_{m}\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)
.
This implies that

f[U_{1}\cap...\cap U_{n}\cap(X\,\setminus\,V_{1})\cap...\cap(X\,\setminus\,V_{k})]=\emptyset,

so
U_{1}\cap...\cap U_{n}\cap(X\,\setminus\,V_{1})\cap...\cap(X\,\setminus\,V_{k})=\emptyset
, and hence
U_{1}\cap...\cap U_{n}\subseteq V_{1}\cup...\cup V_{k}
, a contradiction since
U_{1}\cap...\cap U_{n}\in x
but
V_{1}\cup...\cup V_{k}\notin x
.

For the uniqueness, if the intersection contained
y_{1}\not\leq y_{2}
, then
there would exist
W\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(Z)
such that
y_{1}\in W
and
y_{2}\notin W
.
If

f^{-1}[W]\in x
, then

y_{2}\in{\uparrow}\overline{ff^{-1}[W]}\subseteq W
; and if

f^{-1}[W]\notin x
, then
y_{1}\in{\downarrow}\overline{ff^{-1}[Z\,\setminus\,W]}\subseteq Z\,\setminus\,W
. The obtained contradiction completes the proof of uniqueness.

Next we prove that
\eta_{0}f
is continuous. Let
W\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(Z)
. We show that

(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[W]=\bigcup\{\varphi(U):{\uparrow}\overline{f[U]}\subseteq W\}.

If
{\uparrow}\overline{f[U]}\subseteq W
and
x\in\varphi(U)
, then
(\eta_{0}f)(x)\in W
by construction. Conversely,
if
(\eta_{0}f)(x)\in W
, then

\bigcap_{U\in x}{\uparrow}\overline{f[U]}\subseteq W
.
By compactness, there are

U_{1},...,U_{n}
such that
U_{i}\in x
and
{\uparrow}\overline{f[U_{i}]}\subseteq W
. Thus, letting
U=U_{1}\cap...\cap U_{n}
, we obtain
{\uparrow}\overline{f[U]}\subseteq W
and
U\in x
, so
x\in\varphi(U)
. A similar argument shows that if
W
is a clopen downset of
Z
, then

(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[W]=\bigcup\left\{\eta_{0}X\setminus\varphi(V):{\downarrow}\overline{f[X\setminus V]}\subseteq W\right\}.

Now apply Fact
2.6
.
3
to conclude that

\eta_{0}f
is continuous.

It is left to show that
\eta_{0}f
is order-preserving. Let
x,y\in\eta_{0}X
with
x\leq y
and let

W\in\mathsf{ClopUp}(Z)
. If
\eta_{0}f(x)\in W
, then
x\in(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[W]
, which is a union of clopen upsets, and hence is an upset. Therefore,
y\in(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[W]
, and so
(\eta_{0}f)(y)\in W
. Thus, by the Priestley separation axiom,

\eta_{0}f(x)\leq\eta_{0}f(y)
.

(
2
) First, suppose that
(\eta_{0}f)(x)\in U\,\setminus\,V
. If
f^{-1}[U]\notin x
, then
(\eta_{0}f)(x)\in{\downarrow}\overline{ff^{-1}[Z\,\setminus\,U]}
by (
1
). But

ff^{-1}[Z\,\setminus\,U]\subseteq Z\,\setminus\,U
, and since the latter is a closed downset, we obtain that
{{\downarrow}\overline{ff^{-1}[Z\,\setminus\,U]}\subseteq Z\,\setminus\,U}
, implying that
(\eta_{0}f)(x)\notin U
, a contradiction. Therefore,
f^{-1}[U]\in x
, and a similar argument yields that

f^{-1}[V]\notin x
.
Conversely, suppose that

f^{-1}[U]\in x
and
f^{-1}[V]\notin x
. By (
1
),

{(\eta_{0}f)(x)\in{\uparrow}\overline{ff^{-1}[U]}\subseteq U}
and
(\eta_{0}f)(x)\in{\downarrow}\overline{ff^{-1}[Z\,\setminus\,V]}\subseteq Z\,\setminus\,V
, so
(\eta_{0}f)(x)\in U\,\setminus\,V
.
∎

We use Proposition
5.6
to prove that
\eta_{0}
is left adjoint to the embedding
\mathsf{Esa}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{LocEsa}
.
For this, we will make use of the following classic result:

Proposition 5.7
.

(Esakia’s lemma,
[
13
]
)
Let
X
be an Esakia space and
\mathcal{F}
a down-directed family (with respect to inclusion) of nonempty closed subsets of
X
. Then

\big\downarrow\bigcap\{F:F\in\mathcal{F}\}=\bigcap\{{\downarrow}F:F\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Remark 5.8
.

Esakia’s lemma can be proved in more generality;
see

[
4
, Lem. 2.17]
.

The next result generalizes
[
6
, Lem. 4.3]
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
X
be E-order-zero-dimensional. If
Z
is an Esakia space and
f:X\to Z
is a continuous p-morphism, then the unique lift
\eta_{0}f:\eta_{0}X\to Z
is a p-morphism.

Proof.

By Remark
4.4
.
1
, it suffices to show that

(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[{\downarrow}y]={\downarrow}(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[y]

for each
y\in Z
.
Since each singleton of
Z
is the intersection of the clopen sets containing it, by Fact
2.6
.
3
and Esakia’s lemma,

{\downarrow}(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[y]=\bigcap\{{\downarrow}(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[U\,\setminus\,V]:y\in U\text{ and }y\notin V\},

where
U,V
range over
\mathsf{ClopUp}(Z)
.
We show that

{\downarrow}(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[U\,\setminus\,V]=(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[{\downarrow}(U\,\setminus\,V)].

Since
\eta_{0}f
is order-preserving,

{\downarrow}(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[U\,\setminus\,V]\subseteq(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[{\downarrow}(U\,\setminus\,V)]
.
For the reverse inclusion, if
{x\in(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[{\downarrow}(U\,\setminus\,V)]}
, then
x\in(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[Z\,\setminus\,(U\rightarrow_{\mathsf{ClopUp}(Z)}V)]
(see Fact
4.6
.
2
). Therefore, by Proposition
5.6
.
2
,
f^{-1}[U\rightarrow_{\mathsf{ClopUp}(Z)}V]\notin x
.
Since
f
is a continuous p-moprhism,
f^{-1}
is a Heyting
homomorphism (see Fact
4.6
.
3
). Thus,
{f^{-1}[U]\rightarrow_{\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)}f^{-1}[V]\notin x}
, and hence

x\notin\varphi(f^{-1}[U]\rightarrow_{\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)}f^{-1}[V])=\varphi(f^{-1}[U])\rightarrow_{\mathsf{ClopUp}(\eta_{0}X)}\varphi(f^{-1}[V]),

where the equality holds by Claim
4.12
since
\mathsf{ClopUp}(X)
is an Esakia basis.
Therefore, there is
y\geq x
such that
y\in\varphi(f^{-1}[U])
and
y\notin\varphi(f^{-1}[V])
. Thus,
there is
y\geq x
such that
f^{-1}[U]\in y
and
f^{-1}[V]\notin y
. Using Proposition
5.6
.
2
again,
y\in(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[U\,\setminus\,V]
, and so
x\in{\downarrow}(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[U\,\setminus\,V]
.

Consequently, using Esakia’s lemma again,

\displaystyle{\downarrow}(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[y]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap\left\{(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[{\downarrow}(U\,\setminus\,V)]:y\in U\text{ and }y\notin V\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}\bigcap\left\{{\downarrow}(U\,\setminus\,V):y\in U\text{ and }y\notin V\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}{\big\downarrow}\bigcap\left\{U\,\setminus\,V:y\in U\text{ and }y\notin V\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta_{0}f)^{-1}[{\downarrow}y],

and hence
\eta_{0}f
is a
p-morphism.
∎

The following allows us to conclude that
\mathsf{Esa}
is a reflective subcategory of
\mathsf{LocEsa}
.

Theorem 5.10
.

\eta_{0}:\mathsf{LocEsa}\to\mathsf{Esa}
is left adjoint to the inclusion functor
{\mathsf{Esa}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{LocEsa}}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a locally Esakia space. Since
X
is image-compact, the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\eta_{0}X
is a continuous p-morphism. Also, since
X
is E-order-zero-dimensional, a continuous p-morphism
f:X\to Y
to an Esakia space
Y
has a unique lift
\eta_{0}f:\eta_{0}X\to Y
in
\mathsf{Esa}
. Thus,
\eta_{0}:\mathsf{LocEsa}\to\mathsf{Esa}
is left adjoint to the inclusion functor
\mathsf{Esa}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{LocEsa}
(see, e.g.,
[
18
, p. 91]
).
∎

On the Kolmogorov-Feller weak law of large numbers for Fréchet mean on non-compact symmetric spaces
Jongmin Lee, Sungkyu Jung

Abstract
We establish the Kolmogorov-Feller weak law of large numbers for Fréchet mean on non-compact symmetric spaces under certain regularity conditions. Our results accommodate non-identically distributed random variables and are accompanied by an illustrative example that demonstrates their applicability to the space of symmetric positive-definite matrices.

Introduction

For a sequence of independent and identically distributed real-valued random variables,
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
, the celebrated Kolmogorov-Feller weak law of large numbers (see, e.g.,
(
Feller 1971
, pp. 234-236)
,
(
Naderi et al. 2019
)
) says that

\mbox{if}\penalty\ n\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|>n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0,\penalty\ \mbox{then}\penalty\ \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}-n\mathbb{E}[X_{1}1_{|X_{1}|\leq n}]}{n}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}0\penalty\ \mbox{as}\penalty\ n\to\infty.

(1)

The result in (
1
) can be viewed as an extension of the weak law of large numbers for random variables whose distributions are possibly too heavy-tailed to admit a finite expectation (i.e.,
\mathbb{E}|X_{1}|=\infty
), yet still exhibits moderate tail decay in the sense that
n\mathbb{P}(|X_{1}|>n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0
, which is called the Kolmogorov-Feller tail condition.

The Kolmogorov-Feller weak law of large numbers has been extended to various settings. For example,
Stoica and Li 2010
investigates the case where
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is assumed to be exchangeable, and a conditional version of the extended Kolmogorov-Feller weak law of large numbers is established by
Yuan and Hu 2015
. Moreover,
Naderi et al. 2019
studies a version in which a certain type of dependence is imposed on
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
.
While these studies provide solid foundations in the context of the real line, their extension to more general spaces is not straightforward.

Beyond the real line, given a metric space
(M,d)
and an
n
-tuple of observations
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots x_{n})\in M^{n}
, it is natural to consider the sample Fréchet mean for central tendency, defined as
\mbox{argmin}_{m\in M}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d^{2}(x_{i},m),
which generalizes the Euclidean sample mean. While theoretical properties for the sample Fréchet mean, such as the law of large numbers and central limit theorem, have been established in Riemannian manifolds or general metric spaces (see, e.g.,
Ziezold 1977
;
Sturm 2003
;
Bhattacharya and Patrangenaru 2003
;
Barden, Le and Owen 2013
;
Schötz 2022
;
Evans and Jaffe 2024
;
Schötz 2025
), their validity under heavy-tailed distributions that may lack a finite first moment remain unexamined thus far.

To address these gaps, we generalize the Kolmogorov-Feller weak law of large numbers to a specific type of metric spaces. The obstacles for the generalization of (
1
) are two-fold: (i) the possibility of non-uniqueness of Fréchet means (notably, compact manifolds such as
S^{k}
may suffer from the non-uniqueness of Fréchet means), and (ii) the difficulty for evaluation of
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}1_{|X_{1}|\leq n}]
in the right-hand side of (
1
). The solution for (i) is to restrict the domain to symmetric spaces of
non-compact
type, where the sample Fréchet mean is known to be unique, albeit the population Fréchet mean may not exist. The remedy for (ii) is to impose a symmetry condition on the underlying probability distribution (
\mathbb{P}_{X}
), as defined precisely in the next section.

A class of metric spaces known as symmetric spaces of non-compact type (hereafter referred to as non-compact symmetric spaces)
encompasses a broad class of metric spaces commonly encountered in statistical data analysis.
Representative examples of such spaces include Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{k}
, the space of symmetric positive-definite matrices
(\text{Sym}^{+}(k),\penalty\ d_{\text{\tiny AI}})
, equipped with the affine-invariant metric,and their product spaces.

Given a symmetric distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
on a non-compact symmetric space
(M,d)
, let
\mu\in M
be a center of symmetry of
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, which we take as the reference point. To generalize (
1
) to our setting, the strategy is to replace
|X_{1}|
with
d(X_{1},\mu)
, and the Euclidean sample mean
\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}/n
with the sample Fréchet mean.

We emphasize that our main results, which are entirely new in the fields of probability on manifolds and geometric statistics, provide sufficient conditions under which the weak law of large numbers holds, even for highly heavy-tailed distributions.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows. The next section presents the necessary preliminaries and the main results, accompanied by illustrative examples. Section 3 provides auxiliary results and proofs, some of which are deferred to the appendix for better readability.

2
Main results

A simply connected and geodesically complete Riemannian manifold is said to be a
Hadamard manifold
if it has non-positive curvatures everywhere. A connected, complete and smooth Riemannian manifold
(M,d)
is said to be a
symmetric space
if for any
\nu\in M
, there exists a unique isometry
s_{\nu}
(called geodesic symmetry) that fixes
\nu
and reverses all geodesics through
\nu
; that is, for each
x\in M
,
s_{\nu}(x)=\mbox{Exp}_{\nu}\{-\mbox{Log}_{\nu}(x)\}
, provided it is well-defined. It is known that any non-compact symmetric space is considered as simply connected and has non-positive curvatures everywhere
(
Helgason 1979
)
; thus, it is Hadamard.
Moreover, for
M
that is Hadamard and for each
p\in M
, the Riemannian logarithmic map at
p
is a diffeomorphism between
M
and
T_{p}M\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
by the Cartan-Hadamard theorem.

The notion of geodesic symmetry of a probability distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

Given a symmetric space
(M,d)
and a probability distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
on the space, if there exists a
\mu\in M
such that the distribution of
s_{\mu}(X)
,
\mathbb{P}_{s_{\mu}(X)}
, equals
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, then
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is said to be geodesically symmetric about
\mu
.

A natural question arising from the definition is that for a given distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, if the distribution is geodesically symmetric about
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in M
, then is it true that
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
? The answer to the question is ‘yes’ under the following non-compactness condition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
(M,d)
be a non-compact symmetric space. Suppose that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is geodesically symmetric about both
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}\in M
. Then,
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.

Fig 1:
An illustration of the transvection from
x
to
s_{\mu_{2}}\circ s_{\mu_{1}}(x)
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\mu_{1}\neq\mu_{2}
. Let
T=s_{\mu_{1}}\circ s_{\mu_{2}}
, and
\gamma
be the geodesic from
\mu_{1}
to
\mu_{2}
. Denote
\ell=2d(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})>0
. Then, note that
T
is a translation-type isometry of length
\ell
along the direction of
\gamma
, more precisely, referred to as a transvection. (For an illustration of the transvection, see Figure
1
.) It can be shown that for any
x\in M
,
d(x,s_{\mu_{2}}\circ s_{\mu_{1}}(x))\geq 2d(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\ell
by an application of
(
Sturm 2003
, Corollary 2.5)
. It implies that for any
x\in M
and for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
d(x,T^{m}(x))\geq\ell m
. Thus, the isometry
T
, when repeatedly applied, pushes any point to infinity.

For given bounded sets
K
and
A
, define a constant
C_{K,A}=\sup_{k\in K,a\in A}d(k,a)\in[0,\infty)
. There exists an integer
m
such that
\ell m>C_{K,A}
. For any
k\in K
and for any
a\in A
, by triangle inequality,

d(k,T^{m}(a))\geq d(a,T^{m}(a))-d(k,a)\geq\ell m-C_{K,A}>0,

which implies that
K\cap T^{m}(A)=\phi
. That is, for any bounded sets
K
and
A
, there exists an integer
m_{0}
such that for any integer
m^{\prime}\geq m_{0}
,
K\cap T^{m^{\prime}}(A)=\phi
, where for any
m\geq 1
,
T^{m}(A):=\{T^{m}(a):a\in A\}\subset M
.

Let
A
be any bounded Borel set in
M
. Since
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is geodesically symmetric about both
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, it holds that
\mathbb{P}_{X}(A)=\mathbb{P}_{X}(T(A))
.

Notably, any probability measure on
M
is tight due to the assumption of second countability on the manifold
M
. In words, fix a small
\epsilon>0
. There exists a compact set
K=K(\epsilon)
satisfying
\mathbb{P}_{X}(K)\geq 1-\epsilon
. For all large
m
, we have
T^{m}(A)\cap K=\phi
, so that
\mathbb{P}(A)=\mathbb{P}(T^{m}(A))\leq 1-\mathbb{P}(K)\leq\epsilon
.

Since
\epsilon
was arbitrary, we have
\mathbb{P}(A)=0
for any bounded Borel set
A\subset M
. This is a contradiction to the tightness of
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, and therefore,
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
.
∎

Proposition
1
implies that any distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
on
M
admits at most one center of symmetry
\mu
. Also, we stress that the class of geodesically symmetric distributions includes elliptical distributions, which constitute a broad class of probability distributions.

Unless mentioned otherwise, hereafter we assume that
(M,d)
is a
k
-dimensional non-compact symmetric space.
To describe the main results, we assume either of the following conditions: For a fixed
\mu\in M
,

(C1)

(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of independent
M
-valued random variables and for each
i\geq 1
,
\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}
is geodesically symmetric about
\mu
;

(C2)

(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of independent
M
-valued random variables with common distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
that is geodesically symmetric about
\mu
.

The condition (C2) implies (C1) that purely imposes independence on
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
.

Define a triangular array of truncated random variables
(\overline{X_{n,i}})_{n\geq i\geq 1}
as

\overline{X_{n,i}}=X_{i}1_{d(X_{i},\mu)\leq n}:=\begin{cases}X_{i}\quad\mbox{if}\penalty\ d(X_{i},\mu)\leq n,\\
\mu\qquad\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

for any
n\geq 1
and
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. We denote the sample Fréchet mean of
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
by
\mu_{n}=\mu_{n}(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})=\mbox{argmin}_{m\in M}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d^{2}(x_{i},m)
. Since
M
is Hadamard,
\mu_{n}
is guaranteed to exist and is unique
(
Afsari 2011
, Proposition 4.3)
.

One of the main results is provided below.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that either of the following holds:

(i)

Under Condition (C1),
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(d(X_{i},\mu)>n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0
and
n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(\overline{X_{n,i}},\mu)]\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0
;

(ii)

Under Condition (C2), it holds that
n\mathbb{P}(d(X_{1},\mu)>n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0
.

Then,
\mu_{n}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}\mu
as
n
goes to infinity.

While Theorem
1
generalizes (
1
) to non-compact symmetric spaces, their forms appear somewhat different. This discrepancy arises because Theorem
1
assumes that the distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is
symmetric
about
\mu
. In contrast, (
1
) remains valid even when
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is non-symmetric. Notably, under the condition of (
1
), there are examples where
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is not symmetric and
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}1_{|X_{1}|\leq n}]
diverges as
n\to\infty
. In contrast, if
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is symmetric about
\mu
, then
\mathbb{E}[X_{1}1_{|X_{1}|\leq n}]\to\mu
as
n\to\infty
.

We note that the hypothesis of Theorem
1
for the case (i) holds when
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
does not necessarily consist of identically distributed random variables. An illustrative example with non-i.i.d. random variables in the space of symmetric positive-definite matrices is provided below.

Example 1
.

Let
\mu:=I_{k}
be a fixed reference point on
\mbox{Sym}^{+}(k)
, where
I_{k}
is the
k\times k
identity matrix. Consider a sequence
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
\mbox{Sym}^{+}(k)
-valued random variables that are geodesically symmetric about
\mu
and follow log-normal distributions with increasing variances. Specifically, for a fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
, suppose that each independent random variable
X_{i}
satisfies
\textsf{vec}\{\mbox{Log}_{\mu}(X_{i})\}\sim N_{k(k+1)/2}(\textbf{0},\penalty\ i^{\alpha}I_{k(k+1)/2})
, where
\textsf{vec}(\cdot)
denotes the vectorization operator
(
Pennec, Fillard and Ayache 2006
, Section 3.5)
.
The Chernoff bound implies that for any
t\in(0,1/2)
and for any
x>0
,
\mathbb{P}(\chi^{2}_{k(k+1)/2}>x)\leq e^{-tx}(1-2t)^{-k(k+1)/4}
, where
\chi^{2}_{k(k+1)/2}
stands for the chi-squared distribution with degrees of freedom
k(k+1)/2
. In particular, setting
t=1/4
gives that for any
x>0
,
\mathbb{P}(\chi^{2}_{k(k+1)/2}>x)\leq 2^{k(k+1)/4}e^{-x/4}
. With this in mind, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(d(X_{i},\mu)>n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(\|\mbox{Log}_{\mu}(X_{i})\|_{\mu}^{2}>n^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(\|\textsf{vec}\{\mbox{Log}_{\mu}(X_{i})\}\|_{2}^{2}>n^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(\chi^{2}_{k(k+1)/2}>(n/i^{\alpha})^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{k(k+1)/4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\exp(-\{n/(2i^{\alpha})\}^{2})

\displaystyle<2^{k(k+1)/4}n\cdot\exp(-n^{2(1-\alpha)}/4)

\displaystyle\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0,

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the
L_{2}
-norm in
\mathbb{R}^{k(k+1)/2}
. In addition,

\displaystyle n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(\overline{X_{n,i}},\mu)]

\displaystyle\leq n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(X_{i},\mu)]=n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\|\mbox{Log}_{\mu}(X_{i})\|_{\mu}^{2}]

\displaystyle=n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\textsf{tr}[\textsf{Cov}\{\textsf{vec}(\mbox{Log}_{\mu}(X_{i}))\}]=\frac{k(k+1)}{2}n^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k(k+1)}{2}n^{-2}\int_{1}^{n+1}x^{\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{k(k+1)}{2}n^{-2}\{(n+1)^{\alpha+1}-1\}/(\alpha+1)

\displaystyle<\frac{k(k+1)}{2}n^{\alpha-1}(1+1/n)^{\alpha+1}/(\alpha+1)

\displaystyle\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0.

Since the hypotheses of Theorem
1
(i) hold,
\mu_{n}(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}\mu
as
n
goes to infinity by an application of Theorem
1
.

As a corollary of Theorem
1
(ii), when Condition (C2) holds and
\mathbb{E}[d(X_{1},\mu)]<\infty
is satisfied, a version of the weak law of large numbers follows. It is worth noting that it is possible that
\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(X_{1},\mu)]=\infty
, in which case the population Fréchet mean does not exist.

Corollary 1

(Weak law of large numbers)
.

Suppose that Condition (C2) holds and the first moment of
X_{1}
exists; that is,
\mathbb{E}[d(X_{1},\mu)]<\infty
. Then,
\mu_{n}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}\mu
as
n
goes to infinity.

Proof of Corollary
1
.

According to Theorem
1
, it suffices to show that
n\mathbb{P}(d(X_{1},\mu)>n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}\infty
. An application of dominated convergence theorem together with
\mathbb{E}[d(X_{1},\mu)]<\infty
leads that

n\mathbb{P}(d(X_{1},\mu)>n)\leq\mathbb{E}[d(X_{1},\mu)\cdot 1_{d(X_{1},\mu)>n}]\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0,

yielding the desired result.
∎

All existing laws of large numbers for the sample Fréchet mean on non-Euclidean spaces (e.g.,
(
Ziezold 1977
;
Bhattacharya and Patrangenaru 2003
;
Schötz 2022
;
Evans and Jaffe 2024
)
) typically require the existence of a second moment, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(X,m)]<\infty
for all (or some)
m\in M
. Our results relax this requirement under a broad class of distributions.
Furthermore, we construct an example of a distribution with infinite first moment,
\mathbb{E}[d(X,\mu)]=\infty
, that nevertheless satisfies the tail condition
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(d(X,\mu)>n)=0
, as shown below.

Example 2
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is a geodesically symmetric distribution about
\mu
satisfying
\mathbb{P}(d(X,\mu)>t)\propto\frac{1}{t\log(t)}
for large enough
t
. Then, it holds that
\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(d(X,\mu)>n)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log(n)}=0
. In the mean time, it is well-known that
\mathbb{E}[d(X,\mu)]=\infty
is equivalent to
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(d(X,\mu)>n)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\log(n)}=\infty
, which follows from the integral test:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n\log(n)}=\infty\penalty\ \Leftrightarrow\penalty\ \int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x\log(x)}dx=\big[\log\{\log(x)\}\big]_{x=2}^{\infty}=\infty.

Therefore, there exists at least one probability distribution on
M
satisfying the assumption in Theorem
1
for the case (ii), but
\mathbb{E}[d(X,\mu)]=\infty
.

A pertinent question is whether the converse of Theorem
1
for the case (ii) holds. We provide a partial answer below

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
M
equals
\mathbb{R}^{k}
endowed with any inner product,
and that Condition (C2) holds. If
\mu_{n}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}\mu
as
n
goes to infinity, then
n\mathbb{P}(\|X_{1}-\mu\|>n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0
.

We conjecture that the result of Theorem
2
holds for any non-compact symmetric spaces; however, we are currently unable to prove the conjecture.

3
Proofs and auxiliary results

The following lemma shows that the sample Fréchet mean on Hadamard spaces tends to shrink towards the base point, compared to Euclidean cases. Since its proof is purely technical, it is deferred to the appendix for readability.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
M
is a Hadamard manifold. For any
x\in M
,

\|\mbox{Log}_{x}(\mu_{n})\|_{x}\leq\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mbox{Log}_{x}(x_{i})\|_{x},

(2)

where
\mu_{n}
refers to the sample Fréchet mean of
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots x_{n})\in M^{n}
, and
\|\cdot\|_{x}
denotes the Riemannian norm induced by the Riemannian metric
\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle_{x}
on
T_{x}M
. The inequality in (
2
) becomes strict unless either
M
is flat (curvature zero everywhere) or
x,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots x_{n}
all lie in the same geodesic.

The following lemma serves as a step toward establishing the main results under either Condition (C1) or (C2).

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that
M
is a non-compact symmetric space.

(a)

If Condition (C1) holds and for each
1\leq i\leq n
,
\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}
has finite variance, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(X_{i},\mu)]<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(\mu_{n},\mu)]\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(X_{i},\mu)].

(3)

(b)

In particular, if Condition (C2) holds and
\mathbb{P}_{X}
has finite variance, then

\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(\mu_{n},\mu)]\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(X_{1},\mu)].

(4)

Proof of Lemma
2
.

(a) Recall that since
M
is a non-compact symmetric space, it can be considered as simply connected (see, e.g.,
(
Helgason 1979
)
). Thus,
M
is a Hadamard manifold. Hence, we can employ the result in Lemma
1
. Notably, the population Fréchet mean with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is unique and equals
\mu
since
M
is a non-compact symmetric space (for details, see
(
Lee and Jung 2024
, Proposition 2(a))
). Thus, for each
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, it holds that
\mathbb{E}[\mbox{Log}_{\mu}(X_{i})]=\textbf{0}\in T_{\mu}M\penalty\ (\cong\mathbb{R}^{k})
(since each
\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}
has finite variance). Plugging-in
x=\mu
, and
x_{i}=X_{i}
for any
i=1,2,\ldots n
into (
2
), we get

\|\mbox{Log}_{\mu}(\mu_{n})\|_{\mu}\leq\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mbox{Log}_{\mu}(X_{i})\|_{\mu}.

Squaring the above inequality and taking the expectation on both sides, we obtain the desired result.

(b) Since
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}
has same distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X}
as assumed in Condition (C2), for any
1\leq i\leq n
, it holds that
\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(\mu,X_{1})]=\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(\mu,X_{i})]
, yielding (
4
).

Notably, the inequality in (
4
) becomes strict when
M
has negative curvatures everywhere and
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is not supported entirely on any single geodesic.
∎

Remark 1
.

Suppose that (C2) holds and
\mathbb{P}_{X}
has finite variance. Then, (
4
) implies that the variance modulation of Fréchet mean (see, for examples,
(
Hundrieser, Eltzner and Huckemann 2024
, eq. (1.5))
and
Pennec 2019
for details), denoted by
\textsf{m}_{n}
,
is not greater than 1; that is,

\textsf{m}_{n}:=\frac{n\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(\mu_{n},\mu)]}{\mathbb{E}[d^{2}(X,\mu)]}\leq 1

where
\textsf{m}_{n}<1
holds when
M
has negative curvatures everywhere and
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is not supported entirely on any single geodesic. The strict inequality
\textsf{m}_{n}<1
means that the rate of convergence for Fréchet mean is faster than in Euclidean spaces. This result is more general than the existing one
(
Pennec 2019
, Theorem 9)
(when
M
has negative curvatures), as it applies to a broader class of manifolds
M
and underlying distributions
\mathbb{P}_{X}
on
M
.

Lemma 3
.

For a real-valued random variable
Y
and a constant
p>0
,

\mathbb{E}|Y|^{p}=\int_{0}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\mathbb{P}(|Y|^{p}>t)dt.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

For any non-negative random variable
Z
,
\mathbb{E}Z=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(Z>t)dt
. By plugging-in
Z=|Y|^{p-1}
into the previous equality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|Y|^{p}>u)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(|Y|^{p}>t^{p})pt^{p-1}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\mathbb{P}(|Y|^{p}>t)dt,

where the second equality holds due to the integration by substitution for
u=t^{p}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Case I.
We begin by showing that if (i) in the statement of Theorem
1
holds, then
\mu_{n}\overset{\mathbb{P}}{\to}\mu
as
n\to\infty
. Denote the sample Fréchet mean of
(\overline{X_{n,i}})_{1\leq i\leq n}
by
\overline{\mu_{n}}
. It can be shown that for each

4
Discussion

The results of this article extend the Kolmogorov-Feller weak law of large numbers to the setting of non-compact symmetric spaces under regularity conditions. In contrast to existing laws of large numbers on manifolds, which typically require a finite second moment, our main theorem (Theorem
1
) holds even when
\mathbb{E}[d(X,\mu)]=\infty
, provided that the Kolmogorov-Feller tail condition,
n\mathbb{P}(d(X,\mu)>n)\underset{n\to\infty}{\to}0
, is satisfied. This substantially enlarges the class of admissible distributions, including certain heavy-tailed laws that frequently arise in applications. As a by-product (Remark
1
), we establish that the rate of convergence for the sample Fréchet mean on non-compact symmetric spaces is not slower than the Euclidean counterpart under a broad class of spaces and underlying distributions.

Our findings also have connections to robust statistics. Since the Fréchet mean is well known to be sensitive to either outliers or contamination, several robust alternatives, such as Huber mean
(
Lee and Jung 2024
)
and other robustness-inducing
M
-estimators
(
Lee and Jung 2025
)
, have been proposed in recent times. The present work suggests that even the classical Fréchet mean enjoys a degree of robustness when distributions satisfy tail conditions weaker than finiteness of the first moment. An intriguing direction for future work is to investigate whether analogous Kolmogorov-Feller type results hold for these robust
M
-estimators.

Several open problems remain. First, Theorem
2
establishes a partial converse of Theorem
1
for the case (ii) in the Euclidean case. We conjecture that this converse continues to hold for general non-compact symmetric spaces, but a proof remains elusive. The primary technical obstacle lies in controlling the geometry of sums of logarithmic maps in negatively curved spaces, and new geometric probability tools may be required. Secondly, the present work assumes that random samples are independent. Extending our results to dependent data, such as martingales and random fields on manifolds, would broaden applicability to stochastic processes on manifolds. Lastly, in Condition (C1), we assume that the sequence
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
is symmetric about a common center
\mu
. A more general assumption is that each
X_{i}
is geodesically symmetric about its own center
\mu_{i}
, which may differ from
\mu
, along with an appropriate concentration condition on centers
(\mu_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
. Under this broader setting, establishing the Kolmogorov-Feller-type weak law remains an open problem.

We believe that addressing these questions will further connect probability on manifolds with both geometric analysis and statistical applications. The Kolmogorov-Feller law, originally conceived for real-valued random variables, continues to reveal new insights when transplanted into the rich geometry of symmetric spaces.

Pore-structure controls on absolute permeability of 1-mm digital sandstone subsamples under a fixed CT field of view
Authors: Huanshan Shi, Wangshui Hu, Yibo Li, Danyang Lu, Hu Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbsolute permeability is a key index for evaluating the fluid transmission capacity of sandstone reservoirs. However, what determines its value is not just the pore volume itself, but also whether the effective flow-transmission space is formed, whether the dominant throats are unobstructed, and whether the pore network organization is favorable for flow connectivity. In this paper, we take 20 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm digital sub-samples cropped from the fixed CT field of view of 5 parent sandstone samples as the research object. Based on X-ray micro-CT, we built digital core models, finished pore space characterization, pore structure parameter extraction and single-phase seepage simulation with Avizo software, and used the geometric mean of three-directional absolute permeability, kg, to characterize the overall seepage capacity of the sub-samples. The results show that the 20 digital sub-samples present obvious differences in pore space development degree, effective flow-transmission space scale, dominant throat size and network topology organization, with kg ranging from 8.684 to 62.350 × 10− 15 m2. Total porosity shows a significant positive correlation with kg overall, but under similar porosity conditions, the permeability can still differentiate further. Further redundancy elimination screening indicates that, after controlling for connected porosity, the median throat radius still retain a statistically detectable incremental explanatory effect, whereas Euler number also preserved an additional but more marginal contribution within the current dataset, with partial correlation coefficients of 0.466 ( P = 0.038) and 0.447 ( | r |, P = 0.048), respectively. Under the current sample system and unified processing conditions, the differences in absolute permeability of millimeter-scale digital sandstone sub-samples can be interpreted primarily by connected porosity, median throat radius, and Euler number, which correspond to three complementary aspects: effective flow-transmission space, dominant channel size, and network organization. However, the incremental contribution of Euler number should be understood with appropriate caution under the present sample size.Keywords: Digital rock physics, Sandstone, X-ray micro-CT, Absolute permeability, Pore structure parameter screeningSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

The development of digital core physics has enabled researchers to directly observe the internal structure of reservoirs at the pore scale, and further link the 3D geometric information with macroscopic seepage properties. Relying on high-resolution imaging and pore-scale numerical simulation, pore geometry, connectivity and fluid transport processes can all be quantitatively characterized in the digital space1–4. Among these techniques, X-ray micro-CT has become one of the most commonly used technical means in the study of pore structure of geological materials, due to its advantages of non-destructive, 3D visualization and repeatable analysis2–4. Based on this, digital rock physics research has gradually expanded from the early pore imaging and single-phase flow simulation, to multiple levels such as multi-scale structure characterization, parameter prediction and data-driven analysis1,3–6. For sandstone, the difference in permeability is not just the result of the increase or decrease of pore volume, but also directly related to whether an effective flow-transmission system can be formed between pores, whether the dominant throats are wide enough, and whether the network organization is favorable for flow connectivity7–10.

However, digital core analysis does not allow direct comparison of results without careful consideration of image quality, segmentation strategy, voxel resolution, and field-of-view constraints. For the millimeter-scale digital sub-samples under fixed CT field of view, image quality, segmentation method, voxel resolution and the size of analysis field of view will all affect the reliability of the final parameters11–15. Existing studies have shown that changes in segmentation threshold and voxel scale can significantly change the porosity, permeability and other transport properties calculated from micro-CT images; the limited field of view may also bring systematic deviations such as underestimation of digital porosity and overestimation of permeability11–14. At the same time, the representative elementary volume (REV) is not a fixed scale independent of parameters. Especially for permeability, its scale requirement is usually stricter than that of porosity, because besides being affected by reservoir space, permeability is also jointly constrained by pore connectivity and the complexity of flow paths13,15,16. Therefore, when comparing millimeter-scale digital sub-samples under fixed CT field of view, we cannot take it for granted that all structural parameters are equally stable, nor can we simply equate porosity REV with permeability REV; only under the premise of unified image processing workflow and clear scale boundary, the subsequent parameter screening results can be meaningful12–16.

Regarding the relationship between sandstone pore structure and absolute permeability, digital core research has accumulated relatively solid understanding. Early works have proved that it is feasible to carry out virtual permeability calculation based on micro-CT images, and reservoir physical properties can be directly obtained from 3D digital images5,6,17; after that, pore network extraction and digital image seepage analysis further showed that sandstone permeability is not only controlled by pore volume, but also jointly affected by the scale of connected pore space, dominant throat size and network structure characteristics7,8,17–20. At the grain scale, the influence of grain morphology and heterogeneity on the porosity-permeability relationship is no weaker than that of grain size itself, which indicates that the permeability control mechanism is inherently the result of multi-factor coupling21,22. At the same time, interactive machine learning segmentation, deep learning super-resolution, as well as digital core research based on high-order morphological parameters such as Euler number and shape factor, have also provided new technical support for the quantitative identification of complex pore structure and multi-parameter screening9,10,20,23,24.

But for sandstone digital core research with limited sample number, where the analysis unit is millimeter-scale sub-sample and the CT field of view is fixed, the problem is not “how many more parameters can we extract”, but: facing a large number of geometric, connectivity, topological and path complexity indicators, which parameters retain clearer physical significance and can stably characterize the difference in absolute permeability12–16,18–20,24. Based on this, this paper takes 20 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm digital sub-samples cropped from the fixed CT field of view of 5 parent sandstone samples as the research object. Under the unified workflow of image processing, parameter extraction and single-phase seepage calculation, we analyze the corresponding relationship between pore structure parameters and representative absolute permeability, and further identify the main control parameter combination through correlation structure diagnosis and redundancy elimination screening. The novelty of this study lies not in proposing another permeability correlation itself, but in combining a fixed-FOV millimeter-scale comparison design with redundancy-elimination screening to identify a minimum explanatory set that separates effective flow-transmission space, dominant throat size, and network organization within a unified digital-rock workflow. This paper does not attempt to establish a universal empirical formula that can be directly extrapolated to different lithologies and different scales. Instead, we hope to provide a set of identification framework for main control parameters of absolute permeability, which is more suitable for small-sample digital sandstone research, under the conditions of fixed CT field of view and unified volume sub-samples.

Samples and methods

Focusing on the above problems, this paper established a unified digital core analysis workflow for the 20 sandstone samples, including CT imaging, pore space reconstruction, pore structure characterization, single-phase flow simulation, and statistical screening of permeability control parameters.

Samples and equipment

In this study, we selected 5 parent sandstone samples as the research object, numbered 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. To ensure the consistency of scanning range and statistical unit between different samples, we determined a fixed cylindrical CT field of view inside each parent sample, with a unified size of 4.06 mm in diameter and 4.06 mm in height (Fig. 1a). It should be noted that the research object of this paper is not the porosity-permeability relationship of the 5 parent samples as a whole, but the pore structure differences between the 20 mm-scale digital sub-samples within this fixed scanning field of view, and their control effect on absolute permeability.

The sample scanning was completed using the Phoenix V|Tome|X S 180 & 240 X-ray micro-CT system (Fig. 1b), with scanning conditions of tube voltage 120 kV, tube current 100 µA, and exposure time 1000 ms. After reconstruction, we obtained 3D grayscale volume data of about 2024 × 2024 × 2024 voxels, with the corresponding voxel size of about 2 μm. To reduce the scale deviation in the subsequent image processing, all samples adopted a unified workflow during scanning, reconstruction and sub-sample extraction. Within each fixed cylindrical CT field of view, we further cropped 4 cubic digital sub-samples with a side length of 1 mm, which are marked as 1–1, 1–2, 1–3, 1–4, 2 − 1, 2–2, 2–3, 2–4, and so on, and we obtained 20 digital sub-samples in total (Fig. 1c). The sub-sample extraction adopted a unified coordinate template, which were arranged by layers and regions according to the same rule within the cylindrical field of view, and met the constraint conditions that they do not overlap with each other and are completely located inside the scanning field of view. The purpose of this processing is, first, to retain the local structure differences inside the parent samples as much as possible, and second, to ensure the comparability of the spatial position of sub-samples between different parent samples. The subsequent pore structure parameter statistics, single-phase seepage calculation and main control parameter screening all take these 20 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm digital sub-samples as the basic analysis unit.

Image preprocessing, segmentation and pore structure parameter extraction

To ensure the comparability of the subsequent pore structure characterization and absolute permeability calculation results, we first conducted preprocessing and binary segmentation on the original grayscale volume data of the fixed CT scanning field of view and its internal 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm digital sub-samples. Considering that the grayscale difference between pores and matrix in sandstone samples is limited, and the boundary of micro-pores and throats is easily disturbed by random noise and local grayscale fluctuation, this paper first adopted non-local median filtering to conduct noise reduction processing on the original image, so as to weaken the influence of noise on pore boundary identification as much as possible, and at the same time retain the main morphological characteristics of pore boundaries and tiny throats.

The preprocessing effect was checked and compared through grayscale histogram, representative 2D slices and local amplification results, to avoid over-smoothing of pore boundaries or loss of tiny channels during the noise reduction process.

On this basis, we implemented pore-matrix binary segmentation on the 3D grayscale volume data, to build the digital core model required for subsequent analysis25–27. The segmentation threshold was not assigned mechanically as a fixed value without verification, instead, it was determined based on the main peak distribution of the grayscale histogram and then checked repeatedly against representative 2D slices and local 3D visualization results. This procedure was used to reduce the risks of pore-volume underestimation caused by under-segmentation and artificial pore connectivity caused by over-segmentation.

All samples were processed using the same workflow, including preprocessing, threshold checking, binary segmentation and sub-sample extraction, so that the pore-structure parameters of the 20 digital sub-samples were obtained under a unified image-processing strategy. After obtaining the binarized pore space, we extracted 3D pore structure parameters relying on the Avizo platform. This paper did not mechanically include all the geometric indicators that the software can output. Instead, focusing on four aspects: reservoir space scale, effective flow-transmission space, dominant throat size, as well as network organization and path complexity, we selected total porosity φt, connected porosity φc, connected porosity ratio φc/φt, median throat radius rt50, average coordination number CN, geometric mean of tortuosity τg, Euler number and fractal dimension Df as the first-level candidate parameters.

Among them, φt reflects the overall reservoir space scale, whereas φc reflects the effective flow-transmission space that actually belongs to the main connected pore system. In this study, connected porosity was calculated from the pore voxels belonging to the main connected pore framework extracted from the binarized pore space, while isolated pore clusters outside that framework were excluded. The ratio φc/φt characterizes the conversion degree from total pore space to effective flow-transmission space. The parameter rt50 was used to characterize the typical scale of dominant flow channels, CN to describe the pore-throat connection capacity, and τg to represent the overall bending degree of the flow path. Euler number was used here as a topological descriptor of the reconstructed 3D pore space, and its variation was interpreted together with the continuity and organization of the pore network rather than as an isolated indicator. Fractal dimension (Df) was used as a supplementary descriptor of pore-space geometric complexity. The definition, calculation caliber and physical meaning of each parameter are shown in Table 2. The same connectivity-analysis workflow was applied to all digital sub-samples, so that the calculated φc values were directly comparable across the dataset.

Single-phase seepage simulation and statistical analysis

To quantitatively characterize the flow transmission capacity of different digital sub-samples, after completing pore space reconstruction and parameter extraction, we further carried out single-phase steady-state seepage calculation based on the binarized pore space. The calculation area is limited to pore space, and the matrix part is regarded as impermeable solid boundary; pressure difference boundary conditions are applied along the three orthogonal directions of x, y and z respectively, and the other four side boundaries are set as no-flow boundaries28,29. Under unified fluid properties and unified calculation workflow, we obtained the single-direction absolute permeability kx, ky and kz of the three directions respectively. The same pressure-difference setting was applied to all digital sub-samples under the same fluid-property and boundary-condition framework. In this study, the imposed pressure gradient served as a unified numerical driving condition for steady single-phase permeability evaluation, so that the calculated permeability values were obtained under directly comparable low-inertia seepage conditions rather than under sample-specific forcing schemes. Considering that the pore network model can output statistical information such as pore-throat connection relationship, coordination number, equivalent throat radius and throat channel length at the same time, and supports the calculation of absolute permeability and tortuosity based on velocity field, this paper completed pore network characterization and single-phase seepage analysis on the same software platform, so as to ensure that the source caliber of structural parameters and seepage parameters are consistent as much as possible. The subsequent result analysis and main control parameter screening of the whole paper all take kg as the dependent variable, to ensure that the comparison of seepage capacity between different digital sub-samples has unified caliber. Although directional permeability differences do exist among the three orthogonal directions, anisotropy is not treated as a separate topic in this study because the present objective is to identify the main pore-structure descriptors governing overall permeability variation at the sub-sample scale. For that purpose, kg is sufficient as a unified response variable for cross-sample screening under the current workflow.

In the statistical analysis, we first calculated the mean, standard deviation and value range of the core pore structure parameters and seepage parameters, to characterize the dispersion degree between the 20 digital sub-samples; then we used Pearson correlation analysis and Spearman rank correlation analysis respectively to evaluate the linear correlation and monotonic corresponding relationship between the candidate parameters and kg. All statistical analyses were performed with n = 20. Because the 20 digital sub-samples were cropped from only five parent sandstone samples, the present statistical analysis should be understood as being based on sub-sample-level variation within the current fixed-FOV dataset rather than as assuming full independence comparable to a large cross-sample population. Unless otherwise stated, the significance test is processed as two-sided test, the significance level is set as α = 0.05, and the actual P value is reported. Considering that the sample size of millimeter-scale digital sub-samples is limited, and there may be obvious covariant relationship between some parameters, this paper does not directly retain parameters according to the rule of correlation coefficient size. In addition, the final parameter screening in this study was not determined solely by the magnitude of correlation coefficients or p values, but by combining statistical correspondence, physical interpretability, and redundancy relationships among candidate descriptors. Instead, based on the single-parameter corresponding analysis, we further carried out redundancy elimination screening combined with the correlation structure between parameters, the overlap degree of physical meaning, and the structural dimension they represent. The candidate parameters are first classified and compared according to the four levels: “Reservoir Space Scale - Effective Flow-transmission Space - Dominant Throat Size - Network Organization and Path Complexity”, to identify the key parameter combination that has clear physical significance and can stably characterize the difference in absolute permeability. Results

Based on the above workflow, this paper further analyzed the 3D pore space characteristics, parameter distribution characteristics and permeability response of the 20 sandstone samples.

3D pore space and flow transmission structure characteristics

Among the 20 1 mm digital sub-samples cropped from the fixed CT field of view, there are obvious differences in pore space distribution, development degree of connected pore clusters, and continuity of dominant flow transmission channels. This difference not only exists between different parent samples, but also exists between sub-samples at different positions inside the same parent sample, which indicates that under the conditions of unified volume and unified processing workflow, the millimeter-scale sandstone sub-samples still show strong local structural heterogeneity30,31. Overall, in some sub-samples, connected pore zones are relatively developed, the pore network can extend continuously within the sub-sample scale, and the dominant flow transmission channels are relatively open; other sub-samples show relatively dispersed pore distribution, the local connection parts are relatively thin, and the continuous connection capacity is relatively weak.

To intuitively compare the structural differences between high and low permeability sub-samples, this paper selected the sample 1–2 with the lowest representative absolute permeability and sample 5 − 2 with the highest for comparative analysis (Fig. 5a–f). These two samples were selected not only because they correspond to the minimum and maximum kg values in the present dataset, but also because they represent the most typical contrast in pore continuity, dominant-channel openness, and network organization observed across the low- and high-permeability groups. From the 2D pore slice, the pore distribution in sample 1–2 is relatively scattered, with many local narrow connections; in sample 5 − 2, the connected pore zones are more developed, and the local channels are more continuous. In the corresponding pore network ball-and-stick model, the dominant pore-throat skeleton of sample 1–2 is relatively dispersed, and the continuity between channels is weak; sample 5 − 2 shows a clearer dominant flow transmission skeleton, and the connection between pore bodies and throats is more concentrated. The streamline distribution results also show the same characteristic: the flow paths in sample 1–2 are few and relatively dispersed, while the streamlines in sample 5 − 2 are more concentrated and continuous, with a clearer connection direction.

The differences are consistent with the subsequent parameter statistical results. That is to say, the difference in absolute permeability of millimeter-scale digital sub-samples under fixed CT field of view is not just the difference in the number of pores in the volume sense, but also corresponds to the changes in the continuity of effective flow transmission system, the openness of dominant channels, and the way of network organization. Based on this comparison of image and flow field, this paper further conducted quantitative analysis on the structural differences of the 20 digital sub-samples from the aspects of pore space scale, effective flow transmission space, throat size and network organization.

Corresponding relationship between absolute permeability and key parameters

To identify the corresponding relationship between pore structure parameters and representative absolute permeability, this paper took kg as the dependent variable, and carried out Pearson correlation analysis and Spearman rank correlation analysis respectively on φt, φc, φc/φt, rt50, CN, τg, Euler and Df (Table 5; Fig. 7a-f). The results show that all candidate parameters are significantly correlated with kg, but the correlation direction and intensity are obviously different. Among them, φt,φc, rt50, CN and Df are positively correlated with kg, τg and Euler are negatively correlated with kg, and φc/φt is also positively correlated with kg, but the correlation intensity is relatively weak.

From the Pearson results, the correlation coefficient of φt and kg is the highest (r = 0.977, p = 1.64 × 10− 13), followed by φc (r = 0.974, p = 4.61 × 10− 13); CN, Df and rt50 also show strong positive correlation, with correlation coefficients of 0.957, 0.955 and 0.920 respectively. In contrast, τg is strongly negatively correlated with kg (r = −0.914, p = 1.76 × 10− 8), and Euler is also negatively correlated with kg (r = −0.776, p = 5.73 × 10− 5). The results obtained from Spearman analysis are basically consistent with those of Pearson, which indicates that between these parameters and kg, there is not only a stable linear corresponding relationship, but also a consistent monotonic change trend. The scatter distribution shown in Fig. 7 further indicates that the relationship between different parameters and kg is not completely equivalent: the overall trend of pore space related parameters is more concentrated, while the throat size, network connection and topological parameters show more obvious hierarchical differences between samples.

Overall, the representative absolute permeability does not only respond to a single type of structural attribute, but also corresponds to changes in multiple levels such as reservoir space scale, effective flow transmission space, dominant throat size and network organization mode36,37. It is worth noting that the strong correlation of single parameter can only indicate that these parameters can characterize the change trend of kg respectively, and cannot directly judge that they are independent of each other based on this. Especially, there may be strong covariant relationship between pore space parameters, connection parameters and complexity parameters, so it is necessary to further carry out redundancy elimination screening combined with the correlation structure between parameters, the overlap degree of physical meaning, and the structural dimension they represent. Based on this consideration, the next section will further compare the retention value of each candidate parameter, and determine the simplest parameter combination for characterizing the difference in absolute permeability.

Screening and combination judgment of main control parameters of absolute permeability

Single-parameter analysis shows that multiple pore structure parameters all maintain a significant corresponding relationship with kg, but this does not mean that they should all be retained as independent main control factors at the same time. For millimeter-scale digital sub-samples under fixed CT field of view, the reservoir space scale, effective flow transmission space, throat size and network organization mode often do not change in isolation from each other, but have obvious synchronous change characteristics38–40. Therefore, the judgment of main control parameters cannot be selected or discarded only according to the rule of correlation coefficient size, but also need to further compare the information overlap degree between parameters and the corresponding structural level.

The correlation structure diagnosis results show that there is an extremely strong covariant relationship between φt and φc, their changes in this group of samples are almost synchronous; at the same time, Df and pore space parameters also show a highly consistent change trend, and there is also obvious covariant between CN and φt, φc. In contrast, although rt50 and Euler are correlated with pore space parameters, they are not completely synchronous with them, which indicates that these two types of parameters may still retain additional structural information independent of pore volume. It should be emphasized here that although total porosity (φt) exhibits the strongest overall correlation with kg, connected porosity (φc) was retained in the final minimum explanatory set because it is physically more directly related to the effective flow-transmission space involved in continuous seepage, whereas φt and φc carry highly overlapping information in the current dataset. Based on this judgment, this paper further took φc as the control variable, and compared the incremental explanatory power of the candidate parameters (Fig. 8a–c).

The results show that after controlling the connected porosity φc, there is still a significant corresponding relationship between rt50 and kg, with a partial correlation coefficient of 0.466, P = 0.038; after controlling φc, Euler number also still has statistical significance, with the absolute value of partial correlation coefficient of 0.447, P = 0.048. These results suggest that dominant throat size and network topological organization are not merely passive responses to the increase in effective flow-transmission space, but still preserve incremental explanatory potential within the current dataset. However, given the sample size, the statistical strength of these incremental effects should be interpreted with appropriate caution. In contrast, the partial correlation coefficients of CN and τg after controlling φc are only 0.031 (P = 0.896) and − 0.033 (P = 0.891) respectively, the incremental explanatory effect is no longer obvious. Although Df still shows statistical corresponding after controlling φc, its Pearson correlation coefficient with φc is as high as 0.996, and the relationship direction reverses after control, which indicates that this parameter has high covariant with pore space scale in the current samples, and its independent physical explanation is not stable enough, so it is not included in the final simplest explanation set. Accordingly, CN and τg were not excluded because they are physically irrelevant to seepage, but because, within the present dataset, most of their explanatory information was already absorbed by φc, rt50, and Euler and therefore did not enter the final minimum set as independent retained descriptors.

Based on this, this paper finally screened out the simplest parameter combination composed of connected porosity φc, median throat radius rt50 and Euler number. Among them, φc corresponds to the effective flow transmission space scale, rt50 corresponds to the typical scale of dominant channels, and Euler number supplementally reflects the overall organization characteristic of the pore network. The three correspond to three different but connected structural levels: “whether the effective flow transmission system is formed”, “whether the dominant channels are wide enough”, and “whether the network organization is favorable for flow transmission”. It should be noted that this combination is the simplest explanation set obtained for the current sample system, which does not mean that CN, τg and Df are not important to the seepage process, but indicates that in this group of samples, the information carried by these parameters has strong overlap with φc, rt50 or Euler, and does not enter the simplest retention set in the main text. Discussion

Pore structure basis of absolute permeability difference

The difference in absolute permeability of millimeter-scale digital sub-samples under a fixed CT field of view essentially reflects the local reorganization of the pore system within the same volume constraint, rather than the simple change of a single structural quantity. Previous studies have shown that, although permeability is often commonly correlated with porosity, what really determines whether fluid can pass through the sample continuously is not just the pore volume itself, but also whether effective connection is formed between pores, whether the dominant flow transmission channels are wide enough, and whether the network organization is favorable for flow transmission7,41,42. Therefore, for digital sub-samples analyzed under the same volume and same processing workflow, absolute permeability should be understood as an integrated response of pore-system flow capacity rather than a direct mapping of the overall reservoir space.

From the perspective of structural control chain, the pore space scale provides the prerequisite for flow, but pore volume itself is not equivalent to the effective flow transmission capacity43,44. Only when this part of pores can be included in the continuous main connected system can it truly participate in the overall flow from the inlet to the outlet. In this regard, connected porosity is closer to the seepage process itself than total porosity, because it reflects the space scale that can enter the main connected flow transmission system. Further, even if the effective flow transmission space is formed, however, the resistance of fluid passing through this system is still obviously controlled by the dominant throat. Pore network simulation and pore-throat scale studies have shown that the permeability is more sensitive to key throats than to average pore volume alone. Changes in local bottleneck channels can therefore be amplified into substantial differences in overall flow capacity18,19,25,45. The stable positive correlation between rt50 and kg in this paper corresponds well to this control mechanism.

Besides the effective flow transmission space and throat size, how the pore network is organized together will also affect the flow transmission efficiency. High permeability samples usually not only have larger effective flow transmission space and wider dominant throats, but also show more continuous pore-throat skeleton and clearer dominant flow paths; low permeability samples are more prone to situations such as weak local connection, dispersed network organization or discontinuous dominant channels. Previous studies on network connectivity, formation factor and topological quantity have pointed out that under the condition of similar porosity, the network organization mode will still significantly change the overall efficiency of fluid passing through the sample45–50. The corresponding relationship between Euler and kg in this paper is exactly the reflection of this structural difference at the topological level. It needs to be emphasized here that Euler is not a “decisive parameter” that works alone without specific pore background, its meaning is closer to supplementally identifying whether the network is more favorable for forming continuous flow transmission9,20.

Accordingly, the pore structure basis of absolute permeability differences among millimeter-scale sandstone digital sub-samples under fixed CT field of view can be summarized as a progressive control chain: pore space scale provides reservoir background, effective flow transmission space determines whether the continuous seepage system is formed, dominant throat size determines whether the channels are unobstructed, and network organization mode further affects the overall efficiency of flow path. It is precisely because these levels are not isolated from each other that the φc, rt50 and Euler screened out in this paper can correspond to the three complementary dimensions of effective flow transmission space, dominant channel size and network organization respectively, and jointly characterize the absolute permeability difference in the current sample system.

Why a single porosity index is not enough to completely characterize the difference in absolute permeability

The results of Chap. 3 show that an extremely strong positive correlation between total porosity and representative absolute permeability, which indicates that in the millimeter-scale digital sub-samples of fixed CT field of view, the overall pore space scale still has obvious explanatory power, and is an important background condition for the formation of seepage capacity. In this regard, this paper does not deny the importance of porosity. On the contrary, porosity remains one of the first structural indicators to consider when judging reservoir space development and flow potential. Previous studies have also pointed out that there is usually a stable first-order corresponding relationship between porosity and permeability, but this correspondence mainly reflects the background connection between the overall reservoir space and the overall flow transmission capacity, rather than the complete characterization of the permeability difference21,22,25,26. This also explains why porosity retains strong explanatory power in the present dataset while still failing to resolve all permeability differences on its own.

The problem is that total porosity mainly answers “how many pores there are”, while absolute permeability really corresponds to “whether these pores can form a continuous, unobstructed and efficient overall flow”. Only when the pore space is transformed into effective flow transmission space, can it participate in the continuous seepage from the inlet to the outlet; even if the main connected system is formed, the flow capacity will still be controlled by the dominant throat size and network organization mode50,51. That is to say, porosity can provide the reservoir space background, but cannot alone distinguish how many of the pores really enter the effective flow transmission system, nor can it further characterize the hydraulic aperture of key channels and the network connection efficiency. Previous studies on critical flow transmission scale, key throats and connectivity control have repeatedly shown that the sensitivity of permeability to a few dominant channels is higher than the simple response to average pore volume, this is exactly the fundamental reason why “porosity is strongly correlated but not sufficient“18,19,25.

This point is more clearly shown in the similar porosity range. What Fig. 9 shows is not that porosity “fails”, but that when porosity is already at a similar level, the permeability between sub-samples will still differentiate further. The comparison of three groups of paired samples shows that the porosity difference between high and low permeability samples is very small, but the corresponding permeability ratio still reaches 1.567–1.622; accompanied by this, the higher permeability samples generally have slightly larger rt50 and more favorable -Euler performance. This result is consistent with the existing understanding of network connectivity, key throats and high-order morphological quantity, that is, when the overall pore space is close, the dominant channel size and network organization will further determine the overall efficiency of fluid passing through the sample20,26. Therefore, Fig. 9 does not imply that porosity is unimportant. Instead, it shows at a finer structural level that porosity provides the structural background, whereas the subsequent differentiation is further governed by channel size and network organization.

To illustrate that porosity has strong explanatory power but is still insufficient on its own, three paired sample groups were selected as representative examples in which total porosity remained relatively similar while representative absolute permeability showed clear differentiation (Fig. 9a-c). Figure 9 is intended as a visual complement to the full-sample statistical analysis rather than a replacement of it, and its role is to show in a concrete way why porosity alone becomes insufficient once the background pore-space level is similar. Among them, Pair I is 3–3 and 3–4, Pair II is 2 − 1 and 3 − 2, Pair III is 2–2 and 4 − 3. The high/low porosity ratios of the three groups of samples are only 1.111, 1.057 and 1.074 respectively, while the corresponding high/low permeability ratios reach 1.622, 1.567 and 1.619 respectively; the rt50 ratios in the same period are 1.063, 1.090 and 1.078 respectively, and the -Euler ratios are 1.206, 1.149 and 1.045 respectively. This result indicates that under the similar porosity condition, the higher permeability samples generally have slightly larger dominant throat size and more favorable network topological organization at the same time, so porosity cannot completely characterize the seepage difference between different sub-samples alone.

Therefore, the reason why a single porosity index is insufficient is not that it is irrelevant to absolute permeability, but that it can only cover one level in the control chain. A more accurate statement should be: porosity has significant explanatory power, but in the similar porosity range, throat size and topological parameters will further amplify the seepage difference between different sub-samples. This is also why the present study did not reject porosity conceptually, but retained connected porosity rather than total porosity in the final minimum explanatory set, because φc is more directly linked to the effective flow-transmission space involved in continuous seepage, whereas the information carried by φt is largely overlapped with it in the current dataset. This shift from φt to φc is therefore not merely a statistical substitution, but a physical move from total storage space to the pore volume that actually participates in continuous flow.

Characterization significance and application scope of the simplest parameter combination for sandstone absolute permeability

The simplest parameter combination screened out in this paper is connected porosity φc, median throat radius rt50 and Euler number. Its significance is not to use the least parameters to replace all pore structure information, but to retain the most critical and non-repetitive structural information for absolute permeability as much as possible, under the conditions of limited sample size and obvious parameter covariant. For the current sample system, φc corresponds to the effective flow transmission space scale, rt50 corresponds to the dominant channel size, and Euler supplementally reflects the pore network organization characteristic. Therefore, the identified relationships should be understood primarily as relative structural controls within the current fixed-FOV dataset, rather than as a population-level model assuming full independence comparable to a large cross-sample dataset. The three together correspond to three different levels: “whether the flow can be established”, “whether the main channels are wide enough”, and “whether the network is favorable for forming continuous flow transmission”. Compared with a single porosity index, this combination is closer to the formation process of absolute permeability itself; compared with retaining multiple highly correlated parameters at the same time, it also avoids the repetition of similar information. The present conclusions are subject to two levels of boundary. The first is methodological boundary, including the current image resolution, segmentation workflow, fixed CT field of view, and unified sub-sample size, all of which constrain the stability and comparability of the extracted descriptors. The second is geological boundary, namely that the dataset is restricted to 20 digital sub-samples derived from five parent sandstone samples under the present lithologic background, so the identified minimum explanatory set should be understood as a relative structural interpretation within this sample system rather than a directly transferable rule for all sandstones. Existing studies have also shown that under the condition of coexistence of multiple types of microstructural descriptors, what is more meaningful for permeability characterization is often not to infinitely increase the number of parameters, but to retain the complementary combination with the most explanatory power9,10,25–27.

From the perspective of characterization effect, the value of this combination is mainly reflected in two aspects. First, it separates the three physically different control levels: “pore space background”, “key channel aperture” and “network organization mode”, so it is closer to the seepage mechanism than using total porosity alone. Second, it provides a more compact analysis framework for the subsequent structural evaluation of digital sandstone samples: first use φc to judge whether the effective flow transmission space is fully developed, then use rt50 to judge whether the dominant channel size has high flow transmission potential, and finally use Euler to further identify whether the network organization is favorable for forming continuous flow transmission. In this regard, what this paper proposes is not an empirical regression formula, but an organization mode for identifying main control parameters under fixed CT field of view and unified volume condition. It is more suitable to be used as the core explanation framework in similar digital core research, rather than being directly understood as a general model that can be extrapolated to all reservoir samples18–20,25,27.

It needs to be emphasized that this simplest parameter combination has clear premise. The research objects of this paper are all 1 mm cubic digital sub-samples within the fixed CT field of view, and the workflow of image segmentation, pore network extraction and single-phase seepage calculation are consistent, so the current screening results reflect the relatively stable relationship under unified volume, unified resolution and unified processing caliber. Existing studies have pointed out that digital porosity, absolute permeability and their related structural statistics are all jointly affected by resolution, segmentation method, field size and whether the representative volume meets the requirements; especially in heterogeneous sandstone, permeability REV is often stricter than porosity REV12,13,15,16,52. This means that when the sample scale, voxel resolution, segmentation caliber or simulation boundary condition change obviously, the corresponding relationship between parameters and the simplest combination may be readjusted. Therefore, the conclusion of this paper is more suitable to be used under the conditions of similar lithology, similar scale and similar processing workflow, and should not be directly extrapolated to samples of different lithology, different scale or different method system.

In addition, what this paper proposes is a “simplest explanation combination”, not a “only effective combination”. CN, τg and Df can also reflect the pore structure difference in the current samples, only under the screening target of this time, the information they characterize has strong overlap with that of φc, rt50 or Euler, and did not enter the final simplest set. Previous studies on Euler characteristics, Minkowski functionals and other high-order morphological quantity have also shown that such parameters usually have clear physical meaning, but cannot alone and uniquely determine permeability without specific scale condition, connection background and structure type20,27. Therefore, the significance of φc, rt50 and Euler is not to exclude other parameters, but to give a more compact and targeted set of core characterization parameters under the current fixed CT field of view and millimeter-scale digital sub-sample condition. Within these boundaries, the present screening framework may still be useful for simplified digital-rock evaluation, parameter prioritization, and sample-scale comparative analysis in future sandstone studies conducted under comparable imaging and processing conditions.

Conclusions

Based on 20 1 mm×1 mm×1 mm digital sub-samples cropped from 5 parent sandstone samples under fixed CT field of view, this paper carried out a relatively systematic analysis around the relationship between pore structure parameters and representative absolute permeability, and obtained the following understandings:

There are clear and stable pore structure difference between millimeter-scale digital sub-samples under fixed CT field of view, this difference can be directly reflected in the change of absolute permeability. The representative absolute permeability kg of the 20 sub-samples is 8.684–62.350 × 10− 15m2, different samples show obvious differences in the development degree of effective flow transmission space, the openness of dominant throats and the network organization mode of pores. That is to say, the millimeter-scale local structure inside the same parent sample is not a “small range disturbance”, but a structural unit that really brings differentiation of seepage capacity.

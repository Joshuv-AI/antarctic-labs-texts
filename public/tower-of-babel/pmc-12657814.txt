Re: Kaushik P. Kolanukuduru, Reuben Ben-David, Sarah Lidagoster, et al. Comparative Effectiveness of Bacillus Calmette-Guérin and Sequential Intravesical Gemcitabine and Docetaxel for Treatment-naïve Intermediate-risk Non-muscle-invasive Bladder Cancer. Eur Urol Focus 2025;11:142–9
Authors: Zhiyu Shi, Haojing Zhang, Changlong Yang
Published: 2025

We read with great interest the study by Kolanukuduru et al [1] comparing bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) to sequential intravesical gemcitabine/docetaxel (Gem/Doce) in treatment-naïve intermediate-risk non–muscle-invasive bladder cancer (IR-NMIBC). This retrospective analysis provides valuable real-world insights and demonstrates superior high-grade recurrence–free survival (RFS) for primary high-grade tumors (80.9% vs 61.1% at 2 yr) and overall RFS (59.8% vs 40.9% at 2 yr) with BCG versus Gem/Doce. The protective effect of maintenance therapy observed (hazard ratio 0.40 for any-grade recurrence) is particularly relevant amid global BCG shortages and emphasizes the need to refine Gem/Doce maintenance strategies to achieve durable disease control.

A notable feature of the study cohort is the predominance of Ta tumors (96.1%), with only 3.9% of tumors classified as T1. While this Ta-heavy distribution aligns with American Urological Association and European Association of Urology definitions of IR-NMIBC—which include multifocal or large low-grade Ta tumors in the intermediate-risk category [2,3]—T1 lesions, even when of low grade, exhibit distinct biological behavior and a markedly higher risk of progression to muscle-invasive disease (20–30% at 5 yr vs <5% for Ta) [3]. The minimal inclusion of T1 cases (n = 5) therefore limits the generalizability of these results across the full IR-NMIBC spectrum and may underestimate the therapeutic efficacy or toxicity of Gem/Doce in T1 disease. Prior meta-analyses have shown that T1 tumors demonstrate less durable responses to intravesical therapy, with BCG reducing the risk of progression by approximately 27% in comparison to chemotherapy alone [4]. Furthermore, a recent multicenter study evaluating Gem/Doce in high-risk NMIBC (including T1 disease) reported a 2-yr RFS rate of 69%, but with a higher discontinuation rate in a T1-dominant cohort [5], which reinforces the need for stage-specific evaluation.

Taken together, these observations suggest that the outcomes reported primarily reflect treatment effects in Ta-predominant IR-NMIBC, in which the immune-mediated mechanisms of BCG may confer a sustained benefit following maintenance therapy, whereas the cytotoxic activity of Gem/Doce may wane without extended administration. To enhance clinical applicability, future prospective trials should adopt stricter inclusion criteria and apply refined risk stratification within the biologically heterogeneous intermediate-risk category to ensure adequate representation of both Ta and T1 subgroups. Such an approach would facilitate more precise assessment of individualized maintenance strategies, such as, prolonged Gem/Doce regimens for recurrent Ta disease and preferential BCG use for higher-risk T1 lesions during shortages.

In conclusion, Kolanukuduru et al provide valuable evidence that supports the superiority of BCG in Ta-dominant IR-NMIBC and reaffirms the importance of maintenance therapy. However, the under-representation of T1 tumors highlights a critical gap in addressing the biological heterogeneity in the intermediate-risk population, so dedicated investigation is warranted in future research.

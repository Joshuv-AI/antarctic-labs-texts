Derived categories of Gushel–Mukai surfaces and Fano fourfolds of K3 type
Yulieth Prieto–Montañez, Ian Selvaggi

Abstract
. We prove that very general, dual Gushel–Mukai surfaces are not isomorphic, though derived and L L -equivalent. We use this result to study two semiorthogonal decompositions for a family of Fano fourfolds of K3 type, answering a question by Bernardara–Fatighenti–Manivel–Tanturri.



1.
Introduction

This article studies the derived categories of some Gushel–Mukai surfaces, building on work by Kuznetsov and Perry
[
13
]
, and provides a short account of two families of K3 surfaces, whose members are related by a Fourier–Mukai equivalence but are not isomorphic. As an application, we focus on a family of Fano fourfolds of K3 type defined in
[
2
]
. For a general element in this family, we relate two semiorthogonal decompositions of its bounded derived category by means of homological projective duality.

1.1.
Background

We start with a recap of some aspects of Gushel–Mukai varieties. For a deeper discussion we refer the reader to
[
3
]
.

Let
V_{5}
be a five-dimensional
k
-vector space.

Definition 1.1
.

A
Gushel–Mukai
(
GM
)
variety
is a smooth
n
-dimensional intersection

X=\mathsf{C}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))\cap\mathbb{P}^{n+4}\cap Q,\qquad 2\leq n\leq 6,

where
\mathsf{C}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))\subset\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}V_{5}\oplus k)
is the cone over the Grassmannian
\Gr(2,V_{5})\subset\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}V_{5})

in its Plücker embedding,
\mathbb{P}^{n+4}\subset\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}V_{5}\oplus k)
is a linear subspace, and

Q\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+4}
is a quadric hypersurface.

Remark 1.2
.

This definition is slightly less general than the usual one in
[
3
, Definition 2.1]
, but it is more suited for the purpose of this paper.

Note that, in dimension two, Gushel–Mukai surfaces are K3 surfaces, while in other dimensions they are Fano varieties. GM varieties have been extensively studied due to their rich geometry, homological properties (e.g.
[
12
]
), and for their connections to Hyperkähler manifolds. In fact, to any GM variety is attached in a canonical way a sextic hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
called a EPW sextic and a corresponding double cover which is a Hyperkähler fourfold. Such a relation was discovered first by O’Grady in
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
, and further extended by Iliev–Manivel
[
8
]
. We briefly recall some of these results.

Let
V_{6}
be a six-dimensional vector space and let
\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}
be its exterior power. Consider the natural symplectic form on
\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}
given by wedge product, and let
A\subset\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}
be a Lagrangian subspace. For
k\in\Z_{\geq 0}
, the space
\mathbb{P}(V_{6})
inherits a natural stratification given by

Y_{A}^{\geq k}:=\{v\in\mathbb{P}(V_{6})\,|\,\dim(A\cap(v\wedge(\bigwedge^{2}V_{6})))\geq k\}.

If
A
has no decomposable vectors, meaning that
\mathbb{P}(A)\cap\Gr(3,V_{6})=\emptyset
, then

•

Y_{A}^{\geq 1}
is a normal irreducible sextic hypersurface, called an EPW sextic,

•

Y_{A}^{\geq 2}
is a normal irreducible surface,

•

Y_{A}^{\geq 3}
is finite and reduced, and empty for general
A
,

•

Y_{A}^{\geq 4}=\emptyset
.

For a lagrangian subspace
A\subset\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}
, the orthogonal
A^{\perp}:=\ker(\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}^{\vee}\rightarrow A^{\vee})\subset\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}^{\vee}
is again lagrangian and has no decomposable vectors if and only if
A
does. So,
A^{\perp}
induces a dual sequence of closed subvarieties and the loci for
A^{\perp}
can be described in terms of
A
as

Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq k}:=\{V_{5}\in\mathbb{P}(V_{6}^{\vee})|\,\dim(A\cap\bigwedge^{3}V_{5})\geq k\}.

Among the results of
[
3
]
, a full classification of all isomorphism classes of GM varieties is given. For a Gushel–Mukai variety
X
, there are natural vector spaces associated to it: the six-dimensional vector space
V_{6}(X)
of quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{n+4}
containing
X
, a five dimensional hyperplane
V_{5}(X)\subset V_{6}(X)
, and a lagrangian subspace
A(X)\subset\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}(X)
. More specifically, we have the following correspondence.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.10]

For any
n\geq 2
there are bijections between:

(a)

the set of isomorphism classes of GM varieties
X
of dimension
n\geq 2
such that
\mathsf{C}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))\cap\mathbb{P}^{n+4}
is smooth,

(b)

the set of pairs
(A,\mathfrak{p})
, where
A\subset\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}
is a lagrangian subspace with no decomposable vectors and
\mathfrak{p}\in Y_{A^{\perp}}^{5-n}
, up to the action of
\operatorname{PGL}(V_{6})
.

The first result of this work concerns isomorphism and
L
-equivalence classes of derived equivalent GM surfaces. Let us first recall the notion of
L
-equivalence.

If
k
is a field, the
Grothendieck group of
k
-varieties
is the free abelian group
K_{0}(\operatorname{Var}_{k})
generated by isomorphism
classes
[X\rightarrow k]
of finite type varieties over
k
, modulo the scissor relations, namely the identities

[Y]=[X]+[Y\setminus X]
whenever
X\hookrightarrow Y
is a closed subvariety of
Y
. The group
K_{0}(\operatorname{Var}_{k})
is a ring via
[X]\cdot[Y]=[X\times_{k}Y]
.

We denote by
\mathbb{L}=[\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}]
the Lefschetz motive, that is the class of the affine line over
k
.

Definition 1.4
.

Two smooth, connected, projective varieties
X
and
Y
are
L
-equivalent if in
K_{0}(\operatorname{Var}_{k})
for some
n\geq 0

\mathbb{L}^{n}\cdot([X]-[Y])=0.

We say that they are
nontrivially

L
-equivalent if in addition
[X]\neq[Y]
.

We can now formulate our first result.

Theorem A
.

Let
A\subset\bigwedge^{3}V_{6}
be a very general lagrangian subspace such that

Y_{A}^{\geq 3}\neq\emptyset\quad\hbox{and}\quad Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 3}\neq\emptyset.

Let
S_{1},\,S_{2}
be the Gushel–Mukai surfaces corresponding to points
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\in Y_{A}^{\geq 3}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}\in Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 3}
. Then
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are derived and
L
-equivalent but not isomorphic.

To put this theorem into some context, it is worth noting that, among the results of
[
12
]
, it is shown that any smooth GM variety
X
of even dimension contains a K3 category
\cal A_{X}
. It was conjectured in the same work that if
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are generalized duals then
\cal A_{X_{1}}\cong\cal A_{X_{2}}
. This has been proved in the subsequent work
[
13
, Corollary 6.5]
and will play a substantial role in our construction. Moreover, it was conjectured in
[
14
]
that derived equivalence implies
L
-equivalence, although this was recently disproved by Meinsma in
[
18
]
.

Our original motivation to delve into this topic was the study of some examples of Fano fourfolds of K3 type. As in the case of GM varieties, their study is particularly interesting due to connections with Hyperkähler geometry.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
H=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}H^{p,q}
be a non-zero Hodge structure. We say that
H
is of K3 type if

\max\{q-p\,|\,H^{p,q}\neq 0\}=2\quad\hbox{and}\quad h^{\frac{k+2}{2},\frac{k-2}{2}}=1.

Definition 1.6
.

[
2
, Definition 2.2]

Let
X
be a smooth Fano variety. We say that
X
is a Fano variety of K3 type if
H^{*}(X,\Z)
contains at least one sub-Hodge structure of K3 type, and at most one for any given weight.

An initial source of examples was provided by Küchle in
[
10
]
. Further ones were introduced by Fatighenti and Mongardi in
[
5
]
, where they constructed several families of such varieties. More recently, Bernardara, Fatighenti, Manivel, and Tanturri
[
2
]
gave a comprehensive list of 64 families. The latter classify all Fano fourfolds of K3 type which can be constructed as the zero loci of general global sections of homogeneous vector bundles on products of flag manifolds. Some of these families have been studied by J. Hernandez-Gomez in his thesis
[
7
]
. We focus on the one called of type
K3-38
.

A Fano fourfold
X
is of type K3-38 if it is given by the zero locus in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\Gr(2,V_{5})
of a general global section in

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\Gr(2,V_{5}),\mathcal{O}(1,1,1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(0,0,1)^{\oplus 3}).

Varieties in this family admit two semiorthogonal decompositions for their bounded derived category, both being induced by the projection maps to the factors
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\Gr(2,V_{5})
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Namely, the first one is given by

D^{b}(X)=\langle D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}),D^{b}(S_{20})\rangle,

where
W_{5}
is a Fano threefold of index two and degree five, and
S_{20}
is a K3 surface of degree 20. The existence of such a decomposition is a classical consequence of Orlov’s fomula for monoidal transformations
[
20
]
. The second one instead consists of

D^{b}(X)=\langle D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}),D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}),D^{b}(Z)\rangle,

with
Z\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
a finite flat cover of degree five. It is obtained as an application of a recent result by F. Xie
[
25
]
relating del Pezzo 5 fibrations and semiorthogonal decompositions whose Kuznetsov components are given by derived categories of certain varieties admitting a 5:1 cover of the base. Our second result is a structure theorem for these decompositions and for the varieties involved.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be a very general Fano fourfold of K3-38 type.

(1)

The two semiorthogonal decompositions above do not coincide.

(2)

The surface
Z
is a K3 surface, derived and
L
-equivalent to
S_{20}
but not isomorphic.

To our knowledge, the variety
Z
appearing in the theorem above is the first example known of a K3 surface obtained as Xie’s cover. This leaves open studying other families in
[
2
]
where such covers appear.

In Section

Let

Let

be 2-dimensional vector spaces, and consider a general section
\sigma\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}(V_{2})\times\mathbb{P}(V_{2}^{\prime})\times W_{5},\mathcal{O}(1,1,1))

(1)

V_{2}\otimes V_{2}^{\prime}\rightarrow\bigwedge^{2}V_{5}^{\vee}\bigg/\Sigma

be the embedding defined by

a filtration whose factors are

of dimensions 3, 4, and 3 respectively.

Moreover, by duality there is an induced filtration on

with factors

. Given these varieties, there are two associated GM surfaces corresponding via the bijection stated in Theorem

to the ones in Theorem

(1)

The cone

is a quadric of codimension 4 in
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}V_{5})

(2)

(2)

Consider the cone
\mathsf{C}_{\mathbb{P}(\Sigma)}(\mathbb{P}(V_{2})\times\mathbb{P}(V_{2}^{\prime}))
over the quadric surface
\mathbb{P}(V_{2})\times\mathbb{P}(V_{2}^{\prime})\subset\mathbb{P}(V_{2}\otimes V_{2}^{\prime})
with vertex

. Once again, it is a quadric of codimension 4 in
\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}V_{5}^{\vee})
, and the intersection

(3)

T:=\Gr(2,V_{5}^{\vee})\cap\mathsf{C}_{\mathbb{P}(\Sigma)}(\mathbb{P}(V_{2})\times\mathbb{P}(V_{2}^{\prime}))

is another K3 surface.

.

[

.

[

Let

We are left with proving that

Definition 2.3
.

An
elliptic surface
is a surface
X
which admits a surjective morphism
f:X\rightarrow C
where
C
is a smooth curve, such that the geometric general fibre is a smooth integral curve of genus one. If
F\in\operatorname{NS}(X)
is the class of a fibre, the
multisection index
of

is the minimal positive
t>0
such that there exists a divisor
D\in\operatorname{NS}(X)
with
D\cdot F=t
.

Remark 2.4
.

When the elliptic fibration considered
f:X\rightarrow C
is clear from the context, we will drop the dependence of the multisection index on
f
and just mention the multisection index of
X

For what follows, we will say that a surface is of type
S_{20}
if it is given by
\cal Z(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5},\mathcal{O}(1,1)^{\oplus 2})
. With a little abuse of notation, we will denote such surfaces by
S_{20}
.

By adjunction formula,
K_{S_{20}}
is trivial and by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem the restriction map
H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{S_{20}})
is an isomorphism. Now,
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}
is Fano, so
H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}})=0
and

is a K3 surface.

Remark 2.5
.

As shown in
[
19
, Corollary 0.3]
, a general K3 surface of degree 10 is indeed a complete transverse intersection of a Grassmannian
\Gr(2,V_{5})\subset\mathbb{P}(\bigwedge^{2}V_{5})
with three hyperplanes and a quadric, hence a GM surface.

Lemma 2.6
.

A K3 surface of type
S_{20}
has Picard rank 2 and degree 20 with respect to the polarization
\mathcal{O}(1,1)|_{S_{20}}
. Moreover, The restriction to
S_{20}
of the projection
\pi_{1}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
defines an elliptic fibration of multisection index 5.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}(V_{2}^{\prime})\times W_{5})\cong\Z^{\oplus 2}
, by Noether–Lefschetz we have that a surface of type
S_{20}
has Picard rank two. Then, to compute its Néron–Severi lattice it is sufficient to use as a basis for
\operatorname{NS}(S_{20})
the pullbacks of the two generators of
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{P}(V_{2}^{\prime})\times W_{5})
.
Let
H
and
h
be the classes of
W_{5}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}
, and denote
H_{S_{20}}
and

their restriction to

. Observe that

and

are the classes of two fibres, so

h^{2}=0,\quad H^{4}=0,\quad\hbox{and}\quad H^{3}\cdot h=5.

Then, the class of
S_{20}
is equal to
(H+h)^{2}=H^{2}+2\,H\cdot h
, so it follows that

\displaystyle h_{S_{20}}^{2}

\displaystyle=H^{2}\cdot h^{2}+2\,H\cdot h^{3}=0

\displaystyle H_{S_{20}}\cdot h_{S_{20}}

\displaystyle=H^{3}\cdot h+2H^{2}\cdot h^{2}=5

\displaystyle H_{S_{20}}^{2}

\displaystyle=H^{4}+2\,H^{3}\cdot h=10

\displaystyle[S_{20}]^{2}

\displaystyle=H^{4}+2\,H^{2}\cdot h^{2}+4\,H^{3}\cdot h=20.

We conclude that

(4)

\operatorname{NS}(S_{20})\cong\begin{pmatrix}10&5\\
5&0\end{pmatrix},

which is isometric to the twisted hyperbolic lattice

. To prove the second statement, notice first that the fibre of

over

is given by

(5)

(S_{20})_{s}=\mathcal{Z}(\Gr(2,V_{5}),\mathcal{O}_{\Gr(2,V_{5})}(1)^{\oplus 5}).

∎

3.
On the geometry of the Fano K3-38

As mentioned in the introduction, in this section we turn our focus to a particular family a Fano fourfolds. We give a brief description of their geometry and eventually prove Theorem
B
.

As in the previous section, denote by
W_{5}:=\mathcal{Z}(\Gr(2,V_{5}),\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 3})
the del Pezzo threefold of degree five obtained as a section of Plücker embedding of
\Gr(2,V_{5})
by a codimension 3 subspace.

Definition 3.1
.

A Fano fourfold
X
is of type K3-38 if it is a general divisor of type
(1,1,1)
in the product
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}.

Remark 3.2
.

In the article
[
2
]
such fourfolds were defined as the zero loci

\mathcal{Z}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\Gr(2,V_{5}),\mathcal{O}(1,1,1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(0,0,1)^{\oplus 3}\right).

However, it is clear that the two definitions are equivalent.

For clarity, we prove the following description as stated in
[
2
]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X
be a Fano fourfold of type K3-38, let
\pi_{12}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\pi_{13},\,\pi_{23}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}
be the projection maps. Then:

(i)

the map
\pi_{12}
is a flat, proper morphism whose fibres are del Pezzo surfaces of degree 5 with rational Gorenstein singularities;

(ii)

\pi_{13},\pi_{23}
are blow up maps
\bl_{S_{20}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}
where
S_{20}
is a K3 surface of degree 20 and Picard rank 2.

In particular,
X
is rational.

Proof.

Consider the projection
\pi_{12}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The fibre
X_{s}
of
\pi_{12}
over a closed point
s\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is given by
W_{5}
cut by a section
\sigma_{s}\in H^{0}(W_{5},\mathcal{O}(1))
. For what follows, denote by
\sigma_{1},\,\sigma_{2}\,,\,\sigma_{3}\in H^{0}(\Gr(2,V_{5}),\mathcal{O}(1))
the sections defining
W_{5}
. The Fano
X
is assumed to be general in the linear series
|\mathcal{O}(1,1,1)|
. This implies that

(a)

for each
s\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
the sections
\sigma_{s},\,\sigma_{1}|_{W_{5}},\,\sigma_{2}|_{W_{5}},\,\sigma_{3}|_{W_{5}}
are linearly independent, thus
X_{s}
is two-dimensional,

(b)

the singular fibres arise from those hyperplanes
H_{\sigma_{t}}\subset\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{2}V_{5})
which are tangent to
W_{5}
, or correspond to degenerate 2-forms on
V_{5}
of rank 2. In both cases the singularities are of type
A_{1}
.

By item (a) the morphism
\pi_{12}
is flat and the singularities are Gorenstein. Item (b) implies then that the singularities are also rational. Hence the adjunction formula gives

K_{X_{s}}\cong(K_{\Gr(2,V_{5})}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\Gr(2,V_{5})}(4))|_{X_{s}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\Gr(2,V_{5})}(-1)|_{X_{s}}.

Thus, the anticanonical bundle
-K_{X_{s}}
is ample, and for each
s\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
fibre of
\pi_{12}
is a del Pezzo surface with rational Gorenstein singularities. Then, it is easy to compute the degree of
X_{s}
, as it is given by four linear sections and
\deg X_{s}=\deg\Gr(2,V_{5})=5
.

To tackle the second item, we work with
\pi_{23}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}
, the argument for
\pi_{13}
being analogous. We use the “Cayley trick” in
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
, which is a special case of
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
. Consider the vector bundle
\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{O}(1,1)
on
Y:=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}
. We can write
X
as

X=\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times Y,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)\boxtimes\mathcal{L}),

so that by the Cayley trick
X\cong\bl_{S_{20}}Y
, where
S_{20}=\mathcal{Z}(Y,\mathcal{L}^{\oplus 2})=\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5},\mathcal{O}(1,1)^{\oplus 2})
. The rest of the properties for
S_{20}
follow from Lemma
2.6
. Finally, since
W_{5}
is rational by
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
, the identity
X\cong\bl_{S_{20}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5})
provides a rational model for
X.

∎

Definition 3.4
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a morphism of smooth algebraic varieties. A triangulated subcategory
\cal A\subset D^{b}(X)
is called

-linear if
A\otimes^{L}Lf^{*}F\in\cal A
for any

and

.

Proposition 3.5
.

For a Fano fourfold of K3-38 type there are two semiorthogonal decompositions of its bounded derived category, namely

\displaystyle D^{b}(X)

\displaystyle=\langle D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}),D^{b}(S_{20})\rangle

(11)

\displaystyle D^{b}(X)

\displaystyle=\langle D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}),D^{b}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}),D^{b}(Z)\rangle,

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times W_{5}
and

Proof.

The existence of the first semiorthogonal decompotition is a standard fact. Indeed, by Proposition

(ii) the maps

Remark 3.6

The fact that the Kuznetsov component in (

The effect of thermoforming foils’ material and thickness on the physical properties of orthodontic clear aligners: an in vitro study
Authors: Behzad Salari, Amirhossein Hosseininasab, Shima Esmailzadeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Selection of an appropriate thermoforming foils is a crucial step for fabrication of in-office aligners. The aim of this study was to evaluate the effect of different thermoforming material and thickness on the color and weight stability and elastic modulus of clear aligners. Thermoforming foils of five types and two thickness (0.7 and 1.0 mm) were selected. For each group, 12 specimens were prepared to be a standard clear aligner. Weight change, color stability and elastic modulus was measured before and after thermocycling. Data were analyzed using Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney tests. PETG aligners tended toward weight reduction, while TPU and multi-layer aligners tended toward weight gain. PETG (1.0 mm) specimens weight reduction was significant (P = 0.041). TPU (0.7 mm) specimens weight gain was significant (P = 0.002). PETG aligners had the highest color change (ΔE), while multi-layer aligners showed the least. The thicker foils (1.0 mm) had better color consistency than thinner foils (0.7 mm). PETG (1.0 mm) aligners had the highest elastic modulus while multi-layer aligners had the lowest. Thermocycling caused elastic modulus reduction in PETG and increase in TPU and multi-layer aligners. It was concluded that both the type of thermoforming material and its thickness affect weight change, color stability, and elastic modulus of clear aligners.

Introduction

In recent years, an increasing number of adult patients have sought orthodontic treatment and different approaches to shorten it [1–3]. They are inclined toward more esthetic and comfortable alternatives to conventional fixed orthodontic appliances [4]. Aligners are clear and removable and reduce the risk of caries, dental calculus, and white spot lesions observed with fixed appliances [5, 6]. Considering the high production costs of aligners by large companies, many clinicians design and produce orthodontic aligners in-office.

Currently, the main raw materials used for producing clear aligners are thermoforming polymers such as Polyethylene terephthalate glycol (PETG), polypropylene, polycarbonate (PC), thermoplastic polyurethanes (TPUs), and ethylene‑vinyl acetate (EVA) [7]. PETG is strong and color‑resistant. PC material is durable, while TPU is flexible and elastic. EVA is popular for the patients’ comfort and minimal tissue irritation [8–10]. Patients with stain-prone habits or diets may benefit from PETG aligners which are translucent and resistant to discoloration so can be selected for long-term treatment. PC aligners are strong and durable for patients with parafunctional habits. TPU aligners are more flexible and facilitate complex tooth movements [11–15].

An ideal aligner should be precisely fit the teeth and attachments exert light forces on teeth that remain constant over time [16]. For optimal transparency, it should let visible light to pass through (at least 80%) [7]. The color stability could be affected by temperature, ultraviolet light exposure and diet [17]. Previous studies showed foil thickness alters the generated forces and aligners’ mechanical and optical properties [18]. As well, the elastic modulus and tensile strength of elastomeric materials may be affected by thermal deformation and aging [19, 20].

Despite the growing interest regards In-office clear aligner fabrication, the mechanical properties of different foils have not been fully investigated. Given the direct effect of type and thickness of the thermoforming foils applied for aligner fabrication, this in vitro study aimed to evaluate two common aligner thickness (0.7 and 1.0 mm) and five different materials on the physical properties of aligners (color change, elastic modulus, and weight).

Materials and methods

Aligner preparation

Thermoforming Foils were randomly divided into 7 groups according to material and thickness (Table 1). In each group, 12 specimens were vacuumed-formed (Biostar® 4-Aligner, SCHUE-dental, Germany) on a standard model (15 × 15 × 15 mm) based on the manufacturer’s instructions. The aligners were cut into dumbbell-shaped samples (4.5 mm at the narrow section, and 15 mm at the wide section.

Weight evaluation

An analytical balance with a precision of 0.0001 g was used. Each specimen was measured three times, in case of discrepancy, the mean value was recorded. Weighting process were repeated after thermocycling by a same operator.

Color change evaluation

A spectrophotometer (X-rite, U.S) was applied to evaluate the interaction of light with the material. This device measures the absorption or transmission of specific wavelengths through a material. To assess color change, the CIE Lab color system (International Commission on Illumination) was applied. The CIE L*-a*-b* system is a three-dimensional color description model. In this system, the L* axis stands for brightness, ranging from white to black (+ L* to -L*). The a* axis describes variation from green to red (-a* to + a*). The b* axis represents blue to yellow (-b* to + b*) [21]. The initial light transmission of the specimens was measured before thermocycling. After thermocycling, the specimens were immersed in black coffee for 48 h and then secondary light transmission was measured. The total color difference (ΔE*) between the sample groups before and after thermocycling was calculated via the following formula:

Elastic modulus

The elastic modulus was assessed using a universal tensile testing machine. According to ASTM standards, thermoforming foils with a thickness less than 1 mm were evaluated based on ASTM D 882-02, and foils with a thickness of 1 mm or more were evaluated based on ASTM D 638-02a.

Thermocycling

Thermocycling was performed to simulate the thermal changes occurring during a two-weeks. 14 thermal cycles were performed per day, with a total of 200 thermal cycles over two weeks. The thermal cycles consisted of immersion in 5 °C water for 20 s, followed by 55 °C water for 20 s, with a transfer time of 12 s in distilled water [22].

Statistical analysis

The weight, color stability, and elastic modulus of specimens were measured before and after thermocycling. Data were analyzed using Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney tests. The significance level was set at 0.05.

Color changes

Bioart samples experienced the greatest color change (ΔE), while Maxflex Copolyester and Zendura Flx samples showed the Lowest (Table 3). Thicker samples demonstrated lower color changes compared with thinner samples. Among Maxflex aligners, Copolyester samples had highest color stability, whereas Smart Track showed relatively notable color changes (Fig. 2). Pairwise statistical comparisons of color change revealed significant differences across all groups (P < 0.05), highlighting the considerable impact of material type and thickness on color stability. The largest differences were observed between Bioart (highest ΔE) and Maxflex Copolyester (lowest ΔE).

Elastic modulus

Bioart 1 mm samples had the highest Initial modulus among all groups (993.610 MPa), followed by Bioart 0.7 mm (944.710 MPa) (Table 3). After thermocycling, Maxflex 1 mm TPU samples exhibited the highest modulus (979.690 MPa), while Zendura Flx samples had the lowest (498.820 MPa) (Fig. 3). Bioart samples experienced a notable reduction in modulus, with 1 mm samples showing a greater decrease (43.4%) than 0.7 mm samples (23.0%). In contrast, most Maxflex aligners displayed increased modulus, particularly Smart Track (5.06% increase) and 1 mm TPU (6.47% increase). Zendura Flx and Copolyester aligners exhibited minor and moderate increases in modulus, respectively (Fig. 3).

Thickness in PETG aligners

Comparing weight changes revealed that 1 mm samples lost more weight than 0.7 mm samples which was statistically significant (P = 0.041). Thicker samples exhibited significantly higher color stability than thinner ones (P < 0.001). Prior to thermocycling, 1 mm samples had higher stiffness (993.610 → 949.590 MPa) but experienced greater relative reduction (43.4%) compared with 0.7 mm samples (944.710 → 942.550 MPa, 23.0% decrease). Although thicker samples initially had higher stiffness, they exhibited greater post-thermocycling decline.

Thickness in TPU aligners

0.7 mm samples showed greater weight gain than 1 mm samples (P = 0.008). Thinner samples exhibited greater color change compared with thicker samples (P < 0.001). Before thermocycling, 1 mm samples were stiffer (920.150 MPa) than 0.7 mm samples (843.460 MPa). After testing, 0.7 mm samples showed decreased stiffness (4.54%), whereas 1 mm samples exhibited increased stiffness (6.47%). These results indicate that greater thickness had smaller weight change and improves color stability, while thinner samples lose stiffness and thicker samples gain stiffness. Thickness had a statistically significant effect on both weight and color changes (P < 0.05).

Discussion

The present study compared the effects of five different thermoforming foils on the weight change, color stability, and elastic modulus of orthodontic aligners.

The results revealed that weight changes in aligners after thermocycling were significantly affected by both material type and thickness of thermoforming foils. This aligns with the study by Wang et al. [23] who investigated the effects of different polymer compositions on water absorption. The current results showed Bioart (PETG) aligners tended to reduce weight, whereas most TPU aligners exhibited weight gain. This pattern may be attributed to differences in molecular structure and water absorption capacity of the materials. The observed weight reduction in Bioart samples may result from the evaporation of volatile components during thermocycling, as reported by.

Thickness also significantly influenced weight change, in Bioart (PETG) groups, thicker specimens had more pronounced weight reduction, while in TPU samples, thinner specimens absorbed gained more weight. Lombardo et al. [16]reported an inverse relationship between thickness and water absorption. Clinically, weight changes in aligners may affect their structural integrity and efficiency forces application. Kravitz et al. [24] reported that water absorption can cause aligner swelling and reduced accuracy. Therefore, selecting materials with lower weight change may be preferable in cases requiring continuous force application.

The current results indicated that thicker Maxflex TPU and copolyester aligners exhibited less color change compared to thinner samples. This finding is consistent with the previous findings that reported the increased aligner thickness serves as a protective factor against pigment penetration [25]. The higher molecular density in thicker specimens could affect these results. Clinically, short term discoloration can affect patient satisfaction and cooperation. Pate et al. [26] reported that ΔE values greater than 3.3 are visually perceptible. In the current study, Bioart 0.7 mm samples (ΔE = 5.550) and Bioart 1 mm samples (ΔE = 3.665) exceeded this threshold, while Maxflex copolyester samples (ΔE = 1.805) showed minimal color change. Previous studies suggest that chemical structure, manufacturing method, and testing conditions can influence color stability [27]. In the current study, Smart Track aligners (made form multilayer thermoforming foils composed of TPU and PETG material) displayed relatively notable color change (ΔE = 4.160), likely due to their semi-crystalline structure. Šimunović et al. [28]showed indicated less materials which crystalline structure are more prone to color change.

The elastic modulus, defined as a material’s resistance to elastic deformation under applied stress, exhibited different patterns among materials following thermocycling. Bioart PETG aligners showed a noticeable reduction of this parameter, whereas TPU and multilayer aligners exhibited an increase. Shirey et al. [29], attributed the elastic modulus reduction of aligners to upon water absorption, and attributed its increase to the polymer chain recrystallization. The chemical structure of each material responds differently to thermocycling conditions. As noted by Lombardo et al. [16], polyester-based materials such Bioart typically loss their mechanical properties after exposure to moisture, whereas polyurethane-based materials such as Maxflex TPU react differently to the moisture, probably due to different water absorption and its effects on molecular structure.

Clinically, elastic modulus of the aligner could directly impact their performance. Kravitz et al. [24] mentioned that maintaining an appropriate elastic modulus throughout treatment is essential for generating optimal forces and controlled tooth movement. This study did not measure actual forces exerted on teeth, but the results indirectly evaluate aligner’s performance. The current results noted TPU(Maxflex) and PETG(Bioart) had the final elastic respectively in the material thickness of 1 mm and 0.7 mm. This could be an advantage for cases requiring higher forces per aligners such root torque movement. Conversely, Zendura Flx followed by Maxflex smart track samples with the lowest final elastic modulus could be a proper choice for the movements requiring lighter forces, such as intrusion. In line with current results, another study reported that TPU exerted more buccal force than PETG on the canine and second premolar area [12]. Ho et al. [11] reported a similar Young’s modulus and clinical efficacy for distal crown movements by 0.75 mm TPU and PETG aligner, while both materials were less efficient for distal root movement than PET aligners. Similarly, a review article concluded that Polyurethane-based materials, such as Zendura based aligners had better stress relaxation properties that causes a controlled and sustained forces [30].

It was noted that increased thickness resulted in greater elastic modulus. Similar to the results of previous reports that showed increased thickness positively influenced mechanical property stability [5].

This in vitro study has limitations such as not including all type of aligners, application of different protocols for assessment of elastic modulus based on foil thickness, not considering other physical properties such as creep, no application of different pH, discoloring agents in thermocycling and lack of microstructural evaluation by SEM or X-ray.

Conclusions

The foils’ material and thickness noticeably affected the physical properties of aligners. TPU, Maxflex copolyster and Zendura Flx aligners exhibited the least color change and increased elastic modulus after thermocycling. PETG aligners had the high elastic modulus but loose it and color stability after thermocycling. We recommend wise selection of foils’ material and thickness based on the type and severity of malocclusion and treatment goals.

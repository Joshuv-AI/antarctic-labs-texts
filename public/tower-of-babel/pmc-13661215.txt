Locoregional-systemic combination therapy reshapes the treatment landscape of intermediate and advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)
Authors: Ying Zhu, Lunxiu Qin
Published: 2026

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide because early-stage disease is frequently asymptomatic, leading to delayed diagnosis and poor long-term outcomes. A large proportion of patients are diagnosed at intermediate or advanced stages, losing the opportunity for radical surgical resection and liver transplantation.1 In recent years, driven by advances in locoregional therapy, molecular targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, the clinical treatment landscape of HCC has been undergoing a paradigm shift from local or systemic monotherapy to integrated multidisciplinary management.2 Nevertheless, multiple critical unmet clinical challenges persist in this field, including inconsistent overall survival (OS) benefits across various combination regimens, a lack of globally accepted standardized therapeutic protocols, and the absence of complete implementation workflows for individualized combination treatment tailored to distinct patient subgroups.

This special issue of Cancer Pathogenesis and Therapy focuses on the core clinical challenges of intermediate and advanced HCC treatment, including iterative optimization of core traditional interventional technologies, head-to-head comparison of first-line systemic chemotherapy and immunotherapy, and multi-dimensional combined therapy optimization scenarios. This special issue depicts the current therapeutic landscape, practical dilemmas, and frontier directions of unresectable HCC, providing high-level evidence-based references for standardized clinical decision-making and subsequent translational research, and offering new practical avenues, including novel targeted chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) cell products, for improving clinical outcomes in refractory HCC.

Locoregional plus systemic therapy: an emerging therapeutic trend

Locoregional therapy, particularly transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), has long been the cornerstone of standardized treatment for intermediate-stage HCC. Its clinical value in reducing intratumoral burden, delaying disease progression, and bridging to liver transplantation has been recommended by several major international clinical guidelines, although substantial regional variations remain.3 Nevertheless, conventional TACE monotherapy has inherent clinical limitations, including inconsistent embolic efficacy, frequent post-embolization syndrome, hypoxia-induced rebound tumor angiogenesis, and inevitable local drug leakage leading to mild systemic adverse reactions. Intermediate-stage HCC exhibits significant inter-patient heterogeneity, and not all patients with this stage can achieve satisfactory therapeutic responses to TACE. Relevant academic societies worldwide have successively proposed the concept of TACE refractoriness to avoid repeated ineffective TACE sessions4. However, discrepancies persist among different societies regarding the specific definition of TACE refractoriness, and its underlying molecular mechanisms have not yet been fully elucidated. These limitations substantially restrict the long-term survival benefit of patients and hinder further optimization of interventional HCC therapy. In this special issue, a comprehensive review focusing on TACE advances comprehensively reviews the technological evolution and multimodal combined innovation path of TACE.5 These advanced technical upgrades, including drug-eluting bead TACE (DEB-TACE), biodegradable embolic carriers, and cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) precise fusion imaging guidance, can effectively improve the uniformity of local chemotherapeutic drug release, protect normal liver parenchyma, reduce the incidence of adverse events such as hepatic decompensation, and significantly enhance the complete tumor necrosis rate of hypervascular HCC lesions.

Emerging evidence suggests that locoregional plus systemic therapy may reshape future treatment algorithms for intermediate and advanced HCC, although mature overall survival evidence remains limited. With the rapid development and upfront application of systemic therapy, a series of prospective phase III clinical trials have verified the core synergistic logic of TACE combined with anti-angiogenic (tyrosine kinase inhibitors, TKIs, or anti-vascular endothelial growth factor antibodies, anti-VEGF) and immune checkpoint inhibitors (aPD-1/PD-L1). Mechanistically, local embolization triggers tumor ischemic necrosis, releasing tumor neoantigens and remodeling the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME). Systemic targeted therapy blocks post-embolization angiogenic rebound, while immunotherapy activates peripheral systemic anti-tumor immune responses.

To date, five pivotal phase III clinical trials have reported positive primary endpoints, including EMERALD-1,6 LEAP-012,7 TALENTACE,8 CARES-336,9 and EMERALD-3.10 As summarized in Supplementary Table 1, despite adopting distinct systemic agent regimens and administration sequencing strategies, most trials demonstrate that locoregional–systemic combination regimens (dual or triple) yield significant improvements in progression-free survival (PFS) and objective response rate (ORR) compared with TACE monotherapy. Nevertheless, consistent and mature OS benefit has not yet been definitively validated, and the underlying mechanisms, optimal combination partners, and optimal administration sequence remain to be thoroughly elucidated. In addition, these trials differ substantially in study design, enrolled patient populations and interventional modalities. Disparities between patients from Asia–Pacific region and Western countries in disease etiology, underlying chronic liver disease backgrounds, and intrinsic tumor biological characteristics necessitate critical interpretation of divergent trial outcomes.

Multiple critical challenges require urgent resolution. First, the standardized technical specifications of TACE combined with systemic targeted immunotherapy need to be further improved, including unified optimization of embolic agent particle size selection, time interval between interventional and systemic drug administration, and individualized dose adjustment of combined drugs according to liver function grading, to reduce the heterogeneity of clinical trial results and unify clinical operation standards. Second, the predictive performance of current imaging assessment systems remains limited. There is an urgent need to integrate multi-omics sequencing, liquid biopsy, and radiomic signatures to construct multidimensional composite predictive models. These models can precisely characterize the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), identify patients most likely to derive clinical benefits, and optimize combination regimens and therapeutic sequencing. Third, although the majority of triple combination therapies exhibit a favorable trend toward improved OS, definitive statistical validation of significant OS differences has become increasingly challenging due to crossover treatment and the availability of effective subsequent-line agents. Upon disease progression following trial completion, patients from both experimental and control arms are eligible for highly effective sequential therapies, which substantially attenuate the observed between-arm difference in OS driven by frontline intervention. Of note, the latest updated OS data from the EMERALD-1 trial indicate that adding durvalumab and bevacizumab to TACE fails to demonstrate a trend toward OS benefit. Notably, the updated hazard ratio (HR) for OS exceeded 1.0. A plausible explanation is that the incremental toxicities incurred by combining bevacizumab with TACE may offset its antitumor activity, necessitating a reassessment of the benefit-risk balance of this combination regimen. For the highly heterogeneous intermediate HCC patients, isolated PFS improvements are insufficient to underpin fundamental paradigm shifts in therapeutic algorithms, with OS remaining the ultimate gold standard for evaluating treatment strategies. Nevertheless, in specific clinical contexts, for certain specific subgroups, such as candidates with potential for downstaging to liver transplantation criteria or those likely to become surgical resection candidates, prolonged PFS may bring substantial clinical survival benefits.

In the complex clinical management workflow of locoregional plus systemic therapy, preservation of hepatic functional reserve constitutes a critical prerequisite for successful combination treatment and sustained long-term survival. Furthermore, close interdisciplinary collaboration is essential across interventional radiology, hepatobiliary surgery, hepatology, and diagnostic radiology departments via multidisciplinary team (MDT) conferences. The MDT model encompasses comprehensive therapeutic efficacy assessment, real-time adverse event surveillance, and timely identification of windows of opportunity for curative surgery, enabling delivery of personalized, optimized clinical decisions to individual patients at the optimal therapeutic timing. While therapeutic outcomes for intermediate and advanced HCC have been continuously improved, substantial treatment costs impose a heavy economic burden on both patients and healthcare systems. Current global pharmacoeconomic evaluations rely heavily on data from randomized clinical trials (RCTs). Nevertheless, findings from such clinical trials show prominent regional disparities. Future analyses should further incorporate real-world data from diverse geographical regions, so that cost-effectiveness analyses of therapeutic strategies can better facilitate precision treatment for HCC.

First-line and refractory HCC treatment: emerging immunotherapeutic advances

The optimal selection of first-line therapeutic regimens for advanced HCC remains a major area of debate in clinical decision-making. Most previous clinical studies focused on the efficacy comparison of single targeted drugs or single immunotherapy, without systematic bias assessment or subgroup benefit analysis for head-to-head comparisons between traditional first-line chemotherapy and modern immune checkpoint immunotherapy. The meta-analysis included in this special issue fills this clinical evidence gap effectively.11 Based on multi-center randomized controlled trial data, the study systematically evaluates the survival benefits, adverse reaction safety spectrum, and clinical application accessibility of first-line chemotherapy and immunotherapy for advanced HCC. A total of 20 eligible studies involving 1183 patients were included in the meta-analysis, and an overall high risk of bias was identified across all enrolled trials. Within the limitations of the available evidence, the results indicated that PD-1/PD-L1-based immunotherapy yielded longer PFS than chemotherapy (HR, 1.44, 95% CI: 1.04–2.00) and reduced severe organ-specific toxicities and side effects, contributing to improved quality of life for long-term survivors. However, due to the limitations of short follow-up duration, tumor heterogeneity interference, and crossover bias in individual trials, no fully consistent definitive conclusion had been reached regarding the OS time difference between immunotherapy and chemotherapy. The pooled HR for OS was 1.26 (95% CI, 0.96–1.66), with the 95% confidence interval crossing 1, which demonstrated no statistically significant intergroup difference in OS.

For refractory advanced HCC that is refractory to systemic therapy, many emerging strategies, including dual immunotherapy, adoptive cell therapy (ACT), therapeutic cancer vaccines and bispecific T-cell engagers (BiTE), have been developed in recent years.12 The phase III HIMALAYA trial established durvalumab plus tremelimumab (STRIDE regimen) as an effective first-line therapeutic option for unresectable HCC, launching the era of dual immunotherapy for HCC.13 The 6-year OS rate of STRIDE reached 17.1%, approximately double that of the sorafenib control arm.14 In addition, the CheckMate 9DW trial (nivolumab plus ipilimumab, “O + Y” regimen) further validates the survival benefit of dual immunotherapy in advanced HCC.15 The “O + Y” regimen has become the first dual immunotherapy combination approved for first-line HCC treatment in China. Recently, genetically engineered cell therapies that can specifically target tumor-associated antigens (TAA) or tumor-specific antigens (TSA), including CAR-T and T-cell receptor-engineered T (TCR-T) cell therapies, are increasingly recognized as promising treatment strategies against HCC. Multiple strategies are being explored to enhance the tumor infiltration and anti-tumor properties of CAR-T cells.

In this special issue, the phase I dose-escalation clinical study of JWATM204, a novel GPC3-targeted CAR-T cell therapy product, provides preliminary clinical evidence for the optimization and translational application of HCC cellular therapy.16 Based on the ARTEMIS intelligent immune signaling T-cell platform, JWATM204 integrates a high-affinity GPC3-specific monoclonal antibody targeting function and a built-in safety regulatory module, which potentially reduces the risk of off-target damage to normal liver tissues and severe irreversible neurotoxicity common in traditional CAR-T products. In this small cohort of six enrolled refractory advanced HCC patients, Grade 1–2 cytokine release syndrome occurred in a small number of cases after JWATM204 infusion.16 No dose-limiting toxicities or serious immune-related neurotoxic adverse events were documented, indicating a manageable safety profile in this preliminary cohort.

These encouraging preliminary results demonstrate disease control efficacy in refractory patients with high tumor burden, with a disease control rate of 33.3% (2 of 6 patients). Importantly, the exploratory biomarker analysis in the study identified that the peripheral blood natural killer cell ratio may serve as a minimally invasive predictive biomarker, whereas tumor tissue C-reactive protein (CRP)/Cytochrome P450 2E1 (CYP2E1) expression levels may serve as tissue-based predictive biomarkers for JWATM204 treatment response. These findings provide preliminary evidence for patient stratification in subsequent large-sample phase II expanded trials and offer a novel direction for dynamic efficacy monitoring during the full treatment course of HCC cellular immunotherapy.

Future perspectives

The therapeutic landscape of intermediate and advanced HCC is undergoing a paradigm shift propelled by the integration of locoregional plus systemic modalities. Looking ahead, several priorities emerge as critical to consolidating and extending recent gains.1.Since the consistent long-term OS benefits of locoregional plus systemic combination regimens remain elusive, researchers have proposed that radical surgical resection at an appropriate timing following substantial tumor shrinkage induced by triple combination therapy may convert early therapeutic efficacy into durable survival advantages. This strategy has been adopted in real-world clinical practice, yet prospective controlled trials are still lacking to validate this hypothesis.2.Controversy persists regarding whether surgical resection remains mandatory for patients who achieve complete response (CR) after downstaging/conversion or neoadjuvant therapy with locoregional plus systemic regimens. By analogy, the concepts of “immune-ablation” and “organ-sparing” have been proposed for mismatch repair-deficient colorectal cancers in which pathological complete response (pCR) can be reached in 68.0% following neoadjuvant immunotherapy.17 Moreover, a propensity score-matched retrospective HCC cohort further indicated that a Watch-and-Wait strategy conferred comparable OS to that of curative resection among patients attaining radiological or clinical CR after immunotherapy-based comprehensive treatment.18 Therefore, the Watch-and-Wait paradigm holds promise for omitting surgery and preserving hepatic function in immunotherapy-sensitive subpopulations, representing an intriguing hypothesis that warrants prospective validation.3.Extensive investigations are still warranted to identify the optimal combination regimens for locoregional plus systemic therapy, while addressing unmet clinical hurdles including treatment resistance, systemic toxicities, and hyperprogressive disease (HPD). Further fundamental and translational research is urgently required to resolve these challenges. For those with a high risk of postoperative recurrence, additional research is needed to define the necessity of perioperative or adjuvant immunotherapy and to screen optimal combination strategies through remodeling of the TIME.

For those with a high risk of postoperative recurrence, additional research is needed to define the necessity of perioperative or adjuvant immunotherapy and to screen optimal combination strategies through remodeling of the TIME.

In conclusion, the rapid progression in systemic therapy and locoregional plus systemic combination therapy has driven a paradigm shift in the multidisciplinary management of intermediate and advanced HCC, extending survival in selected patients with intermediate or advanced-stage disease while concurrently enabling a subset of such patients to achieve downstaging conversion and subsequent curative treatment.

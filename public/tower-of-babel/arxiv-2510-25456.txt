Asymptotic expansion of the variation of the Quillen metric and its moment map interpretation
Kiyoon Eum

Abstract
. In Kähler geometry, the Donaldson-Fujiki moment map picture interprets the scalar curvature of a Kähler metric as a moment map on the space of compatible almost complex structures on a fixed symplectic manifold. In this paper, we generalize this picture using the framework of equivariant determinant line bundles. Given a prequantization P = ( L , h , ∇ ) P=(L,h,\nabla) of a compact symplectic manifold ( M , ω ) (M,\omega) , let 𝒢 = Aut ⁡ ( P ) \mathcal{G}=\mathrm{Aut}(P) . We construct for each k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} a 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -equivariant determinant line bundle λ ( k ) → 𝒥 i ​ n ​ t \lambda^{(k)}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}_{int} on the space of integrable compatible almost complex structures, equipped with the 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -invariant Quillen metric. The curvature form of λ ( k ) \lambda^{(k)} admits an asymptotic expansion whose coefficients yield a sequence of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -invariant closed two-forms Ω j \Omega_{j} on 𝒥 i ​ n ​ t \mathcal{J}_{int} and corresponding moment maps μ j : 𝒥 i ​ n ​ t → C ∞ ​ ( M ) \mu_{j}:\mathcal{J}_{int}\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M) . Each μ j \mu_{j} arises from the asymptotic expansion of the variation of the log of the Quillen metric with respect to Kähler potentials, keeping the complex structure fixed. This provides a natural generalization of the Donaldson-Fujiki moment map interpretation of scalar curvature. Moreover, we show that μ j \mu_{j} coincide with the Z Z -critical equations introduced by Dervan-Hallam, and we state a generalization of Fujiki’s fiber integral formula.



1.
Introduction

In Kähler geometry, the Donaldson–Fujiki moment map picture
[
Don97
,
Fuj92
]
characterizes the scalar curvature of a Kähler metric as a moment map on the space of compatible almost complex structures on a fixed symplectic manifold. In light of the Kempf–Ness theorem, the Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture proposes a correspondence between the existence of a
constant scalar curvature Kähler (cscK) metric
and an algebro-geometric notion of stability. Since its formulation, it has become an important topic in Kähler geometry. For recent developments, see
[
DIN25
]
and the references therein.

The original proof of the Donaldson–Fujiki moment map picture was obtained by direct computation, and it is difficult to generalize this approach to more complicated curvature quantities. However, it was already pointed out in
[
Fuj92
,
Don97
,
Don01
]
that the moment map structure should be related to an equivariant line bundle on the space of compatible almost complex structures, which is naturally expected to be the
determinant line bundle
developed by Quillen
[
Qui85
]
and Bismut-Gillet-Soulé
[
BGS88c
]
. In this paper, we use the equivariant determinant line bundle to derive the moment map property for scalar curvature quantities on Kähler metrics, generalizing Donaldson–Fujiki moment map picture for scalar curvature. Note that a similar approach was taken in
[
FU07
]
; we will see that our result is compatible with theirs. In fact, the proof is similar in spirit.

Let us describe our result. Let
(M,J,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold. Fix the symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
and let
\mathcal{J}_{int}
be the space of
integrable compatible almost complex structures
on
M
. The set
\mathcal{J}_{int}
has a natural structure of complex manifold (see Section
2.3
for details). Assume
[\omega]
is integral and fix a
prequantization

P=(L,h,\nabla)
of
(M,\omega)
. Then
\mathcal{G}:=\mathrm{Aut}(P)
acts on
\mathcal{J}_{int}
by biholomorphisms. We consider a
universal family
over
\mathcal{J}_{int}
, whose fiber is the complex manifold
M_{J}
endowed with the complex structure
J
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the construction of
[
BGS88c
]
provides a determinant line bundle
\lambda^{(k)}
on
\mathcal{J}_{int}
, constructed from
L^{k}
. The group
\mathcal{G}
acts equivariantly on
\lambda^{(k)}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}_{int}
and preserves the Chern connection compatible with the
Quillen metric

||\,||
. From this geometric setup, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we can derive the moment map corresponding to the Chern curvature form
\Omega^{(k)}
of
\lambda^{(k)}
. By taking the
k\rightarrow\infty
limit and comparing the coefficients in the asymptotic expansion, we obtain a sequence of scalar quantities
\mu_{j}
on Kähler metrics, which have a natural interpretation as moment maps. The quantities
\mu_{j}
are obtained by differentiating the logarithm of the Quillen metric with respect to the Kähler potential, while keeping the complex structure
J
fixed. As a result, we obtain the following theorem. For precise definitions of the objects appearing in the statement, see Section
3.1
and
3.2
.

Theorem

(A)
.

Fix a complex structure
J
and take the derivative of the logarithm of the Quillen metric with respect to the Kähler potential
\varphi
. Its asymptotic expansion as
k\rightarrow\infty
has the following form:

\frac{\delta}{\delta\varphi}\log\frac{||\;||^{2}_{\omega_{\varphi},h^{k}e^{-k\varphi}}}{||\;||^{2}_{\omega,h^{k}}}=\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}k^{n+1-j}\int_{M}\delta\varphi\,\mu_{j}\left(g_{(\omega_{\varphi},J)}\right)\,\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{n!}

for some functions
\mu_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M)
depending on the curvature of the Kähler metric
g_{(\omega_{\varphi},J)}
.
Then the map
\mu_{j}:\mathcal{J}_{int}\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
defined by

\mu_{j}(J^{\prime}):=\mu_{j}\left(g_{(\omega,J^{\prime})}\right)

is a moment map for
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright(\mathcal{J}_{int},\Omega_{j})
, where
\Omega_{j}\in\mathcal{A}^{2}(\mathcal{J}_{int})
is given by the following fiber integral

\Omega_{j}=\int_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{J}_{int}}\Td_{j}(TM,g_{(\omega,J)})\ch_{n+1-j}(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{h}).

In
[
DH23
]
, Dervan-Hallam introduced the notion of
Z-critical equations
on Kähler metrics, which serve as the analytic counterpart of
Z
-stability
[
Der23
]
, modeled on Bridgeland stability conditions. We will show that
\mu_{j}
are examples of
Z
-critical equations with central charge given by the Todd class. In
[
DH23
]
, Dervan-Hallam also established the moment map property of
Z
-critical equations, in particular including the
\mu_{j}
, by a different method. Note that for
j=1,2
we have an explicit formula for
\mu_{j}
and together with Fujiki’s fiber integral formula
[
Fuj92
, Theorem 4.4]
, we see that
j=1
case is precisely the Donaldson-Fujiki moment map picture of scalar curvature in the integrable case. We also note that the result of
[
FU07
]
can be reinterpreted as the following generalization of Fujiki’s fiber integral formula.

Theorem

(B)
.

For each
j\geq 1
, as a
2
-form on
\mathcal{J}_{int}
,

\int_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{J}_{int}}\Td_{j}(TM,g_{(\omega,J)})\ch_{n+1-j}(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{h})=\frac{-1}{8}\int_{M}\Tr(J\cdot\cdot)a_{j-1}(g_{(\omega,J)})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}-i\partial\bar{\partial}\beta_{j-1}.

So far, the only explicitly computed
Z
-critical equations were in the case of powers of the first Chern class of
M

[
Der23
, Section 2.2]
. In Appendix
A
, we introduce a simple trick and compute the case corresponding to
\ch_{2}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Moment maps and equivariant line bundles

The conventions and notation in this subsection follow those of
[
BGV04
, Section 7.1]
. Let
M
be a symplectic manifold with symplectic 2-form
\Omega
. For
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the
Hamiltonian vector field
defined by
f
and
\Omega
is the unique vector field
X_{f}
such that

(2.1)

df=\iota_{X_{f}}\Omega.

Assume that a Lie group
G
acts on
M
by
(g,x)\mapsto g.x
for
g\in G,x\in M
. For
X\in\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
, we define the
fundamental vector field

\widehat{X}
on
M
as

\widehat{X}_{x}:=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(-tX).x\in T_{x}M.

Then the map
\mathfrak{g}\ni X\mapsto\widehat{X}\in\Gamma(TM)
is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Definition 1
.

Let
G\curvearrowright(M,\Omega)
be a symplectic action. We say that the action is
Hamiltonian
if there is a
moment map

\mu:M\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}

satisfying two conditions:

(1)

Equivariance :
\quad\forall g\in G,\forall x\in M,\forall X\in\mathfrak{g},\quad\langle\mu_{g.x}X\rangle=\langle\mu_{x},\rm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}X\rangle
;

(2)

Moment map equation :
\quad\forall g\in G,\forall X\in\mathfrak{g},\quad d(x\mapsto\langle\mu_{x},X\rangle)=\iota_{\widehat{X}}\Omega
.

One situation in which such a moment map can be derived is the following. Suppose there exists a
G
-equivariant complex line bundle
L
on
M
endowed with a
G
-invariant connection
\nabla^{L}
whose curvature is

\left(\nabla^{L}\right)^{2}=-i\Omega\in\mathcal{A}^{2}(M).

The group
G
acts on the space of sections
\Gamma(M,L)
by the formula

(2.2)

(g.s)(x)=g^{L}.(s(g^{-1}.x)).

We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{L}(X),X\in\mathfrak{g}
the corresponding infinitesimal action on
\Gamma(M,L)
,

\mathcal{L}^{L}(X)s=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(tX).s

which is called the
Lie derivative
. For each
X
, define

(2.3)

\widetilde{\mu}(X)=\mathcal{L}^{L}(X)-\nabla^{L}_{\widehat{X}}\in\Gamma(M,\End{L})\simeq C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C}).

Then, by
[
BGV04
, Example 7.9]
, the map
\mu:M\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by
\langle\mu_{x},X\rangle=i\widetilde{\mu}(X)
is the moment map as defined above. That is,

di\widetilde{\mu}(X)=i\iota_{\widehat{X}}\left(\nabla^{L}\right)^{2}=\iota_{\widehat{X}}\Omega.

Note that, in this case, the nondegeneracy condition on
\Omega
is not essential, and we still call such a
\mu
a moment map even without the nondegeneracy condition (see
[
DH23
]
for a similar convention).

Now consider the holomorphic case. Suppose
(M,J,\Omega)
is a Kähler manifold and
(L,h)
is a Hermitian holomorphic line bundle on
M
whose Chern curvature form is
-i\Omega
. If
G
acts equivariantly on
(L,h)\rightarrow M
by biholomorphisms preserving
h
, we can apply the previous construction of the moment map for the Chern connection. Let
\nabla^{h}
be the Chern connection compatible with
h
, and let
\mu^{h}
be the moment map obtained from the previous construction. If
g\in C^{\infty}(M)^{G}
is a
G
-invariant smooth function on
M
, then the
G
-action also preserves
he^{-g}
, and we obtain a moment map for the new
2
-form
\Omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}g
. The difference between two moment maps is given by

(2.4)

\widetilde{\mu}^{he^{-g}}(X)-\widetilde{\mu}^{h}(X)=-\nabla^{he^{-g}}_{\widehat{X}}+\nabla^{h}_{\widehat{X}}=\iota_{\widehat{X}}\partial g\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C}).

In our setting, the relevant equivariant holomorphic line bundle is given by the determinant line bundle, to be explained in the next subsection.

2.3.
Space of compatible almost complex structures

We follow the conventions of
[
Sca20
, Section 1.3]
, except for the minus sign in front of (
2.1
). Fix a compact complex manifold
M
and a Kähler form
\omega
, and let
\mathcal{J}
be the set of
compatible almost complex structures
on
M
, i.e.

\mathcal{J}=\{J\in\Gamma\End(M,TM):J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id},\;\omega(J\cdot,J\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,\cdot),\;\omega(\cdot,J\cdot)>0\}.

For
J\in\mathcal{J}
, denote by

the Riemannian metric on

The set

has the natural structure of infinite-dimensional Fréchet manifold. The tangent space of

Define the almost complex structure
\mathbb{J}
on
\mathcal{J}
by setting

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}_{J}:T_{J}\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle\rightarrow T_{J}\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto JA.

This gives

The group

of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of

\psi.J:=(\psi^{-1})^{*}J=\psi_{*}\circ J\circ\psi^{-1}_{*}.

The Lie algebra of

can be identified with

of a function

\widehat{f}_{J}=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(-tf).J=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\left(\Phi^{t}_{-X_{f}}\right)^{*}J=\mathcal{L}_{-X_{f}}J

is the flow of the Hamiltonian vector field

. One can check that

Define the space of

, where

is the Nijenhuis tensor of

. For

,

is a Kähler triple. With respect to

,

is a complex submanifold of
\mathcal{J}
. Technically, it is an analytic subset of
\mathcal{J}
due to the possible presence of singularities; however, we do not address this issue in the present paper. Since we are interested in the moment map equation, which is a finite-dimensional equation, one may restrict attention to finite-dimensional complex submanifolds of
\mathcal{J}_{int}

We construct a universal family over
\mathcal{J}_{int}
. Let
M_{J}:=(M,J)
be the complex manifold corresponding to

. Then the natural map
\pi:\mathcal{X}=\bigcup_{J}M_{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}_{int}
gives the

depending holomorphically on

for

and

of

3.
Main results

3.1.
Variation of the Quillen metric and the Z-critical equations

In this section we fix a complex structure
J
on
M
and consider the variation of Kähler metrics within the Kähler class
[\omega]
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}=\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M):\omega_{\varphi}=\omega+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi>0\}
be the space of Kähler potentials. Our moment maps will be derived by taking the derivative of the asymptotic expansion of Quillen metric with respect to Kähler potential
\varphi
. We start by recalling the results from
[
Eum25
]
; see
ibid.
for further details.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a polarized Kähler manifold with polarizing line bundle
L
, that is,
\cc_{1}(L)=[\omega]
. For each Kähler metric
\omega_{\varphi}\in[\omega]
, there exists a Hermitian metric
h_{\varphi}
on
L
such that
(\nabla^{h_{\varphi}})^{2}=-i\omega_{\varphi}
. For example, it can be given by
h_{\varphi}=he^{-\varphi}
and defined up to multiplicative constants.

Let
\lambda(L^{k})
be the determinant line constructed in Section
2.2
with
\xi=L^{k}
,
B=\{\mathrm{point}\}
. Let
||\;||_{\omega,h^{k}}
and
||\;||_{\omega_{\varphi},h^{k}e^{-k\varphi}}
be the Quillen metrics constructed by
g^{M}=g_{\omega},h^{\xi}=h^{k}
and
g^{M}=g_{\omega_{\varphi}},h^{\xi}=h^{k}e^{-k\varphi}
, respectively. We compute the asymptotic expansion of
\log||\;||_{\omega,h^{k}}-\log||\;||_{\omega_{\varphi},h^{k}e^{-k\varphi}}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
by two different methods.

Method 1:
By construction, one can obtain the asymptotic expansion from asymptotic expansions of (a) the
L^{2}
-product on
\det H^{0}(M,L^{k})
and (b) the Ray-Singer analytic torsion

with function

Definition 2

Proposition 1
.

Proof.

Let

To show that

acts on

by biholomorphisms, we prove that

preserves

on

and

where we used the notation

. This implies

Thus

preserves

. We have already shown that

preserves the

preserves the orthogonal projection operators

This concludes the proof that over

\mathcal{G}
-invariant. Since

Theorem 3
.

The map
\mu_{j}:\mathcal{J}_{int}\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M)

is given by the fiber integral

\Omega_{j}=\int_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{J}_{int}}\Td_{j}(TM,g_{(\omega,J)})\ch_{n+1-j}(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{h}).

By the explicit formula for
\widetilde{Z}_{j}
(
3.8
) when
j=1
and the fiber integral formula of Fujiki
[
Fuj92
, Theorem 4.4]
, it follows that Theorem
3
generalizes the Donaldson-Fujiki moment map picture for scalar curvature in the integrable case. In Section
3.3
, we state the generalization of Fujiki’s fiber integral formula for
j\geq 1
.

Note that since (
3.2
) contains no terms of order
k^{m}\log k
, the terms in the asymptotic expansion of
\widetilde{\mu}^{(k)}(f)
arising from the coefficients
\alpha_{j}
in (
3.1
) yields constant functions on
\mathcal{J}_{int}
.

3.3.
Comparison with the result of Foth–Uribe

In
[
FU07
]
, Foth-Uribe used the determinant line bundle
(\lambda^{(k)})^{-1}_{J}=\det H^{0}(M_{J},L_{J}^{k})
for large enough
k
on each open set
\mathcal{U}
to derive the moment map equation for the trace of operator
Q^{(k)}_{f}
with respect to the action of
\mathcal{G}
. In particular, they used (the dual of) the invariant connection
{}^{0}\nabla^{\lambda^{(k)}}
over
\mathcal{U}
(called the
L^{2}
connection in their paper) and characterized the curvature of
{}^{0}\nabla^{\lambda^{(k)}}
in terms of the trace of the Toeplitz operator. The asymptotic expansion then follows.

Theorem 4

(
[
FU07
, Theorem 1.2, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
A,B\in T_{J}\mathcal{J}_{int}
. Then as
k\rightarrow\infty
,

{}^{0}\Omega^{(k)}(A,B):=i\left({}^{0}\nabla^{\lambda^{(k)}}\right)^{2}(A,B)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}k^{n-j}\,\frac{1}{8}\int_{M}\Tr_{TM}(JAB)a_{j}(g_{(\omega,J)})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}

See
[
FU07
, Corollary A.7]
for a missing
i
factor. Our normalization of the
\omega
differs by a factor of
2
from that used there.

A similar but more general characterization was also obtained in
[
MZ23
]
. Unlike
\Omega^{(k)}
,
{}^{0}\Omega^{(k)}
is defined only locally for large enough
k
. Let the
k^{n-j}
-coefficient of
{}^{0}\Omega^{(k)}
be
-^{0}\Omega_{j}
, that is,

-^{0}\Omega_{j}(A,B):=\frac{1}{8}\int_{M}\Tr_{TM}(JAB)a_{j}(g_{(\omega,J)})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}

for
A,B\in T_{J}\mathcal{J}_{int}
. Then, by comparing coefficients, one obtains the moment map property of
J\mapsto a_{j}(g_{(\omega,J)})-\Delta a_{j-1}(g_{(\omega,J)})
for
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright(\mathcal{J}_{int},^{0}\Omega_{j})
. Thus,
locally
on
\mathcal{U}
, we are in the situation described in (
2.4
), and our result is compatible with the results of Foth-Uribe. Since
\Omega_{j}=^{0}\Omega_{j-1}-i\partial\bar{\partial}\beta_{j-1}
, we obtain a generalization of Fujiki’s fiber integral formula.

Theorem 5
.

For each
j\geq 1
, as a
2
-form on
\mathcal{J}_{int}
,

\int_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{J}_{int}}\Td_{j}(TM,g_{(\omega,J)})\ch_{n+1-j}(\widetilde{L},\widetilde{h})=\frac{-1}{8}\int_{M}\Tr(J\cdot\cdot)a_{j-1}(g_{(\omega,J)})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}-i\partial\bar{\partial}\beta_{j-1}.

Note that from the explicit expressions for
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}

[
BV89
,
Fin18
]
, it follows that
\partial\bar{\partial}\beta_{j-1}=0
for
j=1,2
. In particular,
j=1
case coincides with
[
Fuj92
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Appendix A
Explicit computation of the Z-critical equation

In this appendix, we compute the
Z
-critical equation corresponding to the central charge
\int_{M}\ch_{2}[\omega]^{n-2}
.
First, by standard identities in Kähler geometry
[
Szé14
, Lemma 4.7]
,

\widetilde{t}=\frac{\ch_{2}(TM,g)\omega^{n-2}}{\omega^{n}}=\frac{1}{2n(n-1)}\left(|\ric|^{2}-|R|^{2}\right).

Second, by the same identities

\widetilde{\ell}=\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{\Tr[iR]\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^{n}}=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}R_{p}{}^{q}{}_{j\bar{k}}g^{\bar{k}j}=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}{\ric}_{p}{}^{q}=:\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\widetilde{\ric}\in\Gamma(M,T^{1,0}M).

Now we have to take formal adjoints of
\partial
and
\bar{\partial}
to
\widetilde{\ric}
. That is, we need to evaluate

\int_{M}\langle\partial\bar{\partial}f,\widetilde{\ric}^{\flat}\rangle_{g}\omega^{n}=\int_{M}f\,\partial^{*}\bar{\partial}^{*}\widetilde{\ric}^{\flat}\,\omega^{n}

for arbitrary function
f
, where
\partial\bar{\partial}f
denotes the tensor
\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z^{p}\partial\bar{z}^{q}}dz^{p}\otimes d\bar{z}^{q}
. This integration by parts can be done more easily in the language of differential forms. Note that

\langle\partial\bar{\partial}f,\widetilde{\ric}^{\flat}\rangle_{g}=\partial_{j}\bar{\partial_{k}}f{\ric}_{p}{}^{j}g^{\bar{k}p}=\partial_{j}\bar{\partial_{k}}f{\ric}_{p\bar{q}}g^{\bar{q}j}g^{\bar{k}p}=\langle i\partial\bar{\partial}f,\ric\rangle_{\omega}

where the last term denotes the product between the Hermitian
(1,1)
-forms
i\partial\bar{\partial}f
and Ricci form
\ric=i\ric_{p\bar{q}}dz^{p}\wedge d\bar{z}^{q}

in
[
Szé14
, Lemma 4.7]
. Again, by the same identities,

\int_{M}\langle i\partial\bar{\partial}f,\ric\rangle_{\omega}\omega^{n}=\int_{M}\Delta fS\omega^{n}-n(n-1)i\partial\bar{\partial}f\wedge\ric\wedge\omega^{n-2}=\int_{M}f\Delta S\omega^{n},

which implies

-\partial^{*}\bar{\partial}^{*}\widetilde{\ell}^{\flat}=\frac{-1}{n(n-1)}\Delta S.

Combining these, we obtain

\widetilde{Z}(M,\omega)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}(-\Delta S-\frac{1}{2}|R|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\ric|^{2}).

It follows from
[
DH23
, Example 2.6]
that the formula for
\cc_{1}^{2}
is

\widetilde{Z}(M,\omega)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}(-2\Delta S+S^{2}-|\ric|^{2}).

We can also compute this directly using the same trick as above. Since
\Td_{2}=-\frac{1}{12}\ch_{2}+\frac{1}{8}\cc_{1}^{2}
, we thus recover (
3.8
).

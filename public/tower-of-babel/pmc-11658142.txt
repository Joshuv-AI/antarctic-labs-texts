Polyethylene glycol loxenatide protects diabetic kidneys by inhibiting GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway, and improves cardiac injury by suppressing TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway
Authors: Chen Shi, Qilin Zhang, Yufeng Li, Junjun Zhao, Cong Wang, Yu Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCardiovascular and renal complications of type 2 diabetes are the main causes of death in diabetic patients. Clinical studies have found that polyethylene glycol loxenatide (PEG-Loxe), a GLP-1 analog widely used to treat type 2 diabetes, boosts renal and cardiac functions in diabetic patients. However, its mechanism of action remains to be elucidated.MethodsUsing injury models of HK-2 human renal proximal tubular epithelial cells and H9C2 rat myocardial cells, as well as db/db mouse models of type 2 diabetes, this study assessed the protective effects of PEG-Loxe on T2DM mice kidneys and hearts and revealed their mechanisms of action.ResultsPEG-Loxe treatment significantly reduced the contents of serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, and 24 h urine protein, alleviated glomerular injury and inflammatory reaction, markedly elevated cardiac left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and fractional shortening (LVFS) levels, diminished pathological injuries in cardiac tissues, and improved renal and cardiac functions in db/db mice. In addition, PEG-Loxe considerably decreased the GRP78 mRNA and protein expressions of GRP78, p-eIF2α, ATF4, and CHOP in the kidneys of T2DM mice, inhibited GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway-related proteins in HK-2 cells cultured in high glucose concentrations, subdued renal endoplasmic reticulum stress, and eased renal injury in T2DM mice. PEG-Loxe also obstructed the TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway and myocardial apoptosis and mitigated cardiac trauma in T2DM by reducing TLR4, MyD88, and p-NF-κBp65 protein expressions in cardiac tissues. The H9C2 cell experiment further confirmed PEG-Loxe’s ability to protect the cardiovascular system of T2DM patients by inhibiting the TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway and lessening LDH and CK-MB levels.ConclusionWe showed that PEG-Loxe could decrease renal stress response and improve renal injury in T2DM by inhibiting endoplasmic reticulum stress via the GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway. Additionally, PEG-Loxe could hinder the TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway and myocardial apoptosis and boost cardiac function, thus exerting protective effects on the cardiovascular system in T2DM.Keywords: Polyethylene glycol loxenatide, Type 2 diabetes, Renal injury, Cardiac injury, Endoplasmic reticulum stress, Inflammatory pathway

Introduction

Cardiovascular and renal complications of diabetes are the main causes of death in diabetic patients. Current traditional treatment strategies primarily include lifestyle intervention and hypoglycemic, lipid-lowering, and antihypertensive therapies. However, these therapies still cannot effectively prevent or reverse the progress of these complications [1, 2]. In recent years, the consensus that renal endoplasmic reticulum stress and cardiac inflammatory reaction play a key role in diabetic nephropathy and its cardiovascular complications has grown [3, 4].

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), an intestinal peptide hormone with the strongest insulin secretion-stimulating effect, acts by binding to the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R, a G-coupled protein belonging to the β-receptor family) [5]. After GLP-1 binds to GLP-1R, it activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), protein kinase A (PKA), extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK)1/2, and the phosphoinositol 3 kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (PKB) pathway [6, 7]. GLP-1 promotes the synthesis and secretion of insulin in a glucose concentration-dependent manner, stimulates the proliferation of pancreatic β cells and inhibits their apoptosis, prevents the synthesis and secretion of glucagon, impedes gastrointestinal motility and gastric secretion, and delays gastric emptying [8].

GLP-1R is distributed in a variety of tissues in the body, including islet cells, gastrointestinal tract, cardiovascular system, kidneys, etc. [9, 10]. A recent meta-analysis found that GLP-1 analogs or GLP-1-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) also had a favorable effect on cardiorenal outcomes in patients with T2DM [11]. In the cardiovascular system, GLP-1 can dilate blood vessels, increase myocardial contractility, promote myocardial uptake of glucose, reduce ischemia-induced myocardial injury, and enhance cardiovascular function [12]. Reportedly, liraglutide inhibits arterial calcification and improves cardiovascular disease by inhibiting the PI3K/Akt/mTOR/S6K1 signaling pathway and regulating the osteogenic differentiation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) [13]. Subcutaneous injection of liraglutide has been shown to significantly augment myocardial systolic functioning and myocardial remodeling, as well as truncate the formation of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and fibrosis in C57BL/6 mice [14]. One investigation revealed that the inflammatory response caused by TLR4 activation plays an essential role in the pathogenesis of diabetic cardiovascular diseases. The expression of TLR4 was significantly amplified in T2DM patients. The binding of TLR4 to ligands triggers signal dependence of the adaptor protein-myeloid differentiation factor 88 (My D88), leading to the early activation of NF-κB and MAPK, and promoting the release of inflammatory factors, leading to myocardial injury [15].

Some clinical and experimental studies have found that GLP-1RAs favorably affect renal prognosis in T2DM patients and the effects are mediated by anti-inflammatory and antioxidant stress [16–18]. In addition, GLP-1RAs have been shown to inhibit kidney fibrosis [19]. These findings suggest that GLP-1RAs have great potential in the prevention and treatment of diabetic nephropathy.

In recent years, some research progress has been made in deciphering the role and mechanism of endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) in the occurrence and development of diabetic nephropathy. Hyperglycemia has been found to induce the upregulation of GRP78, p-eIF2α, ATF4, and CHOP protein expressions in renal cells, as well as the occurrence of endoplasmic reticulum stress and apoptosis, resulting in renal injury [20, 21]. However, whether GLP-1RAs exert a renoprotective influence by inhibiting renal ERS remains unclear.

Polyethylene Glycol Loxenatide (PEG-Loxe) is a new hypoglycemic drug composed of macromolecules (molecular weight: 44212.65 ± 4000 Da) prepared by modifying amino acids and polyethylene glycol (PEG) based on the chemical structural formula of exenatide and is also the first long-acting GLP-1RA to be marketed in China. PEG-Loxe can effectively prolong GLP-1 activity, inhibit DPP4 activity, and limit renal clearance [22]. Our previous study revealed that PEG-Loxe prevented hepatic lipid synthesis and inflammatory reaction by activating the lipid metabolism Sirt1/AMPK/ACC pathway, thereby alleviating liver injury in patients with T2DM [23]. However, PEG-Loxe’s protective effect and mechanism against diabetes-induced kidney and heart injuries remain unsubstantiated. In this investigation, a diabetic animal model and a cell injury model were constructed to examine the protective property of PEG-Loxe on diabetic kidney and heart damage and decipher their possible underlying mechanisms of action so as to provide an experimental basis for the further expansion of the clinical application of the biomacromolecule PEG-Loxe.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

PERK (#3192), eIF2α (#5324), p-eIF2α (#3597), CHOP (#2895), MyD88 (#4283), NF-κBp65 (#8242), and p-NF-κBp65 (#3033) were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology, Inc. (Danvers, MA, USA). GRP78 (#ab108615) was obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA). ATF4 (10835–1–AP) and TLR4 (19811–1–AP) were bought from Proteintech Group, Inc (Wuhan, China). Horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG and goat anti-mouse IgG, as well as β-actin, were acquired from Sigma-Aldrich (St Louis, MO, USA). Biochemical analysis kits for protein in urine (C035–2–1), urea nitrogen (C013–1–1), and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH, A020-2) were procured from the Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China). Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits for creatine kinase MB isoenzyme (CK-MB) were obtained from ELK Biotechnology (Wuhan, China).

Experimental animals

This investigation was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology. Animal care and experimental procedures were carried out in accordance with the Guidelines of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Tongji Medical College and the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Male mice on C57BL/6.BKS.Cg-Dock7m + / + Leprdb/J(000697)(C57BL/6-db/db) and C57BL/6-m/m background (8 weeks) were obtained from the Changzhou Cavins Experimental Animal Co., LTD. All mice were housed at a temperature of 22 ± 1 °C under a 12-h dark–light cycle in well-ventilated caging systems with free access to standard chow and water. Animal studies were grouped as described in our previous research [23]. Briefly, the mice were randomly divided into six groups: a normal group (control), a db/db group (T2DM), a PEG-Loxe-L group, a PEG-Loxe-H group, a Lira group, and a Loxe group. Mice in normal and T2DM groups were injected with an equal volume of saline subcutaneously every 3 days. PEG-Loxe low and high dose groups were injected subcutaneously with PEG-Loxe (0.3 mg/kg and 1 mg/kg) every 3 days, respectively. The Lira and Loxe groups were subcutaneously injected with 0.4 mg/kg of Lira and 0.3 mg/kg of Loxe, respectively daily. Drug treatment continued for 4 weeks until the sacrifice of the mice [23].

Echocardiography assessment of animal cardiac function

The left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), fractional shortening (LVFS), stroke volume (SV) and cardiac output (CO) were measured in the mice of each group using a Visual Sevolonics Vevo 3100 high-resolution small animal diasonograph (Fujifilm Visualsonics China Investment Co. LTD).

Sample collection and biochemical analysis

The random blood glucose levels of mice in each group were determined before and after the experiment. Mice were placed in metabolic cages for 1 day before being sacrificed. Urine was collected for 24 h for quantitative detection of creatinine in urine and protein in urine. Mice were anesthetized with pentobarbital sodium (150 mg/kg intraperitoneal injection), and blood samples were collected and centrifuged immediately (1200 g, 4 °C, 15 min) to obtain serum from which serum creatinine and urea nitrogen were detected according to instructions provided with the detection kits. Then, the mice were immediately euthanized via cervical dislocation. Kidney and heart tissues were dissected, with one part fixed in 10% paraformaldehyde solution and the other stored at -80 °C for further analyses.

Histological assessment

Kidneys and myocardial tissues from mice in each group were harvested, fixed with 10% formaldehyde solution, dehydrated with gradient alcohol, cleared in xylene, embedded in paraffin blocks, cut into 4 μm slices using a histotome, deparaffinized, hydrated, and examined. The morphological changes in kidney and myocardial tissues were inspected with hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) staining, and kidney tissue fibrosis was scrutinized using Sirius staining [24]. Tunel staining was used to evaluate the apoptosis of cardiomyocytes in cardiac tissues [25]. All sections were observed and captured at 400X with an Olympus BX51 microscope (Tokyo, Japan).

RNA extraction and RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted with a TRIzol reagent (ELK Biotechnology, EP013) from about 100 mg of kidney tissues harvested after sacrifice. cDNA was synthesized using the M-MLV Reverse Transcriptase Kit (ELK Biotechnology, EQ002). The primer sequences of the target gene were as follows: GRP78 forward, 5’-GCCAACTGTAACAATCAAGGTCTAT-3’ and reverse, 5’-GTTTTGGTCATTGGTAATTGTGATT-3’; β-actin forward, 5’-CTGAGAGGGAAATCGTGCGT-3’ and reverse, 5’-CCACAGGATTCCATACCCAAGA-3’. RT-PCR was performed on the StepOne™ Real-Time PCR Detection System (Life Technologies, USA). Samples were prepared using a QuFast SYBR Green PCR Master Mix Kit (ELK Biotechnology, EQ001) according to the manufacturers’ instructions. Each PCR product was verified individually for its amplification utilizing the melting curve analysis. Obtained data were analyzed employing the relative gene expression 2−∆∆Cq method [26].

HK-2 cell culture and treatment

Human proximal tubular epithelial cell lines (HK-2 cells) were purchased from Nanjing Kaiji Biotechnology Development Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). The cells were seeded in a DMEM/F12 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin and cultured in an incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 °C. The cells were subsequently divided into groups after growing and fusing to 80%. Group A: HK-2 cells treated with 5.5 mM D-glucose; group B: HK-2 cells treated with 5.5 mM D-glucose and PEG-Loxe (100 nmol/L); group C: HK-2 cells treated with 5.5 mM D-glucose and Lira (100 nmol/L); group D: HK-2 cells treated with 5.5 mM D-glucose and Loxe (100 nmol/L); group E: HK-2 cells treated with 30 mM D-glucose; group F: HK-2 cells treated with 30 mM D-glucose and PEG-Loxe (100 nmol/L); group G: HK-2 cells treated with 30 mM D-glucose and Lira (100 nmol/L); group H: HK-2 cells treated with 30 mM D-glucose and Loxe (100 nmol/L) L). After 24 h of incubation, cellular proteins were extracted, and GRP-78, eIF2α, p-eIF2α, and ATF4 levels were determined using western blot.

H9C2 cell culture and treatment

Rat H9C2 cardiomyocytes were purchased from Nanjing Kaiji Biotechnology Development Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) and thawed. After thawing, the cells were cultured in a high glucose medium (DMEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. For hypoxia treatment, the H9C2 cell culture medium was replaced with serum-free and glucose-free DMEM and then incubated for 6 h in a hypoxic incubator containing a mixture of 95% N2 and 5% CO2 gases to mimic hypoxia. The medium was subsequently replaced with a normal DMEM medium in a regular incubator (37˚C, 5% CO2) for 6 h during reoxygenation. To determine the myocardial protective effects of PEG-Loxe, Lira, and Loxe, H9C2 cells were partitioned into groups. In the normal control group, a normal DMEM medium was used to culture cardiomyocytes under normoxic conditions; in the normal group and the drug group, cardiomyocytes were cultured in a normal DMEM culture solution containing 100 nmol/L PEG-Loxe/Lira/Loxe under normoxic conditions; in the H/R model group, cardiomyocytes were cultured under hypoxia for 6 h and then 6 h during reoxygenation (H6h/R6h); in the H/R and PEG-Loxe/Lira/Loxe groups, cardiomyocytes were cultured in a DMEM medium containing 100 nmol/L of PEG-Loxe/Lira/Loxe for 4 h, followed by H6h/R6h; in the H/R + GLP-1R inhibitor + PEG-Loxe/Lira/Loxe groups, cells were pretreated with Exendin9-39 (100 nM) for 2 h and then cultured in a DMEM medium containing 100 nmol/L of PEG-Loxe/Lira/Loxe for 4 h followed by H6h/R6h. At the end of each experiment, the cell culture medium of each group was aspirated, centrifuged at 800 g for 10 min, and the supernatant was separated for use. The activities of LDH and CK-MB in the cell culture medium were detected according to the instructions for use of the detection kit. At the same time, cellular proteins were extracted, and TLR4, MyD88, and NF-kBp65 levels were evaluated with western blot.

Western blot

Western blot was performed as described previously [24]. The total protein in renal tissues was extracted using a RIPA lysis solution (25 mM Tris–HCl, 25 mM NaCl, 0.5 mM EDTA, 1% Triton X-100, and 0.1% SDS) supplemented with protease inhibitors and phosphatase inhibitors. The protein was separated using 10–15% SDS-PAGE and then transferred onto PVDF membranes (IPVH00010, Millipore, Germany), incubated with a primary antibody overnight at 4 °C, and co-incubated with a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-coupled secondary antibody for 1 h. The membranes were washed and then incubated with chemiluminescence substrate (ECL Plus) for 1 min and immediately exposed to medical X-ray film. The film was scanned using a scanner (LiDE110, Canon inc, Tokyo, Japan). Band density was quantified using AlphaEaseFC (Version 4.0, Alpha Innotech, USA).

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism version 7.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Results were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. The non-parametric Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to establish the normal distribution of the data. Differences between groups were analyzed using one-factor ANOVA, followed by Dunnett’s test. P values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

PEG-Loxe boosted renal functioning in T2DM mice

Before drug treatment, the fasting blood glucose (FBG) and body weight of mice in normal, T2DM, PEG-Loxe-L, PEG-Loxe-H, Lira and Loxe groups were 8.72 ± 1.38, 31.02 ± 3.43, 31.41 ± 2.64, 31.28 ± 2.49, 30.57 ± 4.27, 32.41 ± 1.35 mmol/L, and 22.26 ± 1.06, 40.94 ± 0.91, 42.25 ± 1.26, 41.15 ± 1.34, 41.36 ± 1.04 and 41.33 ± 1.72 g, respectively, indicating the successful of T2DM model [23]. As shown in Table 1, compared with normal group, renal functioning in T2DM mice was irregular, showing significant increases in serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, and 24 h urine protein. After 4 weeks of treatment with PEG-Loxe-L, PEG-Loxe-H, Lira, or Loxe, the levels of serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, and urine protein were reduced drastically, and renal functioning was improved. PEG-Loxe diminished the levels of serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, and urine protein in a dose-dependent manner, and PEG-Loxe-H had the most notable effect, suggesting that PEG-Loxe could markedly boost renal functioning in T2DM mice.

PEG-Loxe improved renal tissue damage in T2DM mice

As shown in Fig. 1, HE and Sirius staining analyses (400 ×) highlighted normal morphologies and clear boundaries in the kidneys of mice in the normal control group. Renal injury, mesangial vacuolation, enlarged renal tubular lumen, interstitial expansion, basement membrane thickening, inflammatory cell infiltration, and fibrosis (the Sirius positive area was significantly enlarged) were noted in the model group mice. However, treatment with PEG-Loxe, Lira, and Loxe alleviated glomerular damage and inflammatory reaction and improved renal fibrosis.

PEG-Loxe inhibited the mRNA level of renal endoplasmic reticulum stress-associated protein GRP78 in T2DM mice

As shown in Fig. 2A, the level of GRP78 mRNA, a marker of renal endoplasmic reticulum stress, in the model group was significantly higher than that in the normal control group (P < 0.05). However, treatment with PEG-Loxe, Lira, and Loxe resulted in massive cuts in the expression of GRP78 mRNA (P < 0.05) compared to that in the model group, with the decrease more substantial in the PEG-Lox-H group than in the PEG-Lox-L group (P < 0.05). These findings suggest that PEG-Loxe could markedly reduce GRP78 mRNA expression and inhibit renal ERS.

PEG-Loxe regulated the endoplasmic reticulum stress GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway to alleviate renal injury in T2DM mice

The GRP78 signaling pathway is an important pathway involved in ERS. Western blot evaluation of the effect of PEG-Loxe on the renal GRP78 pathway in T2DM mice revealed, as shown in Fig. 2B, considerably amplified GRP78, p-eIF2α, ATF4, and CHOP protein expressions in the kidneys of T2DM mice (P < 0.001) compared to those in the normal control group. However, these were significantly reduced after treatment with PEG-Loxe and Lira, and the expression of PERK did not change notably. This suggests that PEG-Loxe and Lira could inhibit renal ERS and alleviate renal injury in T2DM mice by regulating the GRP78/eIF2α/eIF2α pathway.

The impact of PEG-Loxe on the expression of endoplasmic reticulum stress-associated proteins in HK-2 cells

As shown in Fig. 2C, 24 h PEG-Loxe, Lira, or Loxe treatment of HK-2 cells cultured with normal glucose concentration had no significant effect on the expression of GRP78, p-eIF2α/eIF2α, and ATF4 compared to the normal control group. For cells cultured in high glucose, the expression of these proteins in the model group increased markedly, indicating that the HK-2 cells in the model group underwent ERS. However, the expression of GRP78, p-eIF2α/eIF2α, and ATF4 in HK-2 cells cultured in a high glucose concentration reduced considerably after the administration of PEG-Loxe, Lira, or Loxe. These results indicate that PEG-Loxe could substantially diminish the protein expression of the GRP78 signaling pathway, inhibit the high-glucose-induced amplification of GRP78 expression, and mitigate ERS-caused cell damage, thus reducing kidney cell apoptosis and protecting the kidneys.

PEG-Loxe improved cardiac functioning in T2DM mice

Myocardial injury is one of the important causes of death in diabetic patients. This study preliminarily explored the cardioprotective effect of PEG-Loxe on T2DM mice. As shown in Fig. 3A and B, LVEF and LVFS in the T2DM group mice decreased significantly (P < 0.05) compared to the normal control group; however, SV and CO did not change drastically. PEG-Loxe-H and Loxe substantially boosted LVEF and LVFS (P < 0.05). Lira notably increased LVEF but had no considerable impact on SV, LVFS and CO. The results indicate that PEG-Loxe, Lira, and Loxe could effectively improve cardiac functioning in diabetic mice.

Compared with the normal control group, mice in the T2DM group showed partial dissolution of myocardial fibers, myocardial karyopyknosis, and a disorganized arrangement of myocardial fibers, suggesting that mice in the T2DM group endured myocardial cell injury. Treatment with PEG-Loxe, Lira, or Loxe significantly improved myocardial cell injury.

PEG-Loxe regulated the TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway to alleviate cardiac injury in T2DM mice

To further scrutinize the mechanism by which PEG-Loxe alleviates myocardial injury, western blot was used to determine the expression of the proteins associated with the cardiac TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway. Relative to the normal control group, the expression of TLR4, MyD88, and p-NF-κBp65 proteins in the hearts of T2DM mice increased significantly, indicating that an inflammatory reaction occurred in the hearts of T2DM mice. Treatment with PEG-Loxe, Lira, or Loxe inhibited the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway and substantially decreased the levels of TLR4, MyD88, and p-NF-κB p65 (Fig. 5A).

Discussion

Our results show that PEG-Loxe effectively reduces the levels of blood glucose, serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, and 24 h urine protein in db/db mice, increases the degree of cardiac LVEF and LVFS, decreases the histopathological lesion of the heart, and improves renal and cardiac functions, which is consistent with previous reports on GLP-1RAs [27, 28]. Multiple studies have shown that long-term hyperglycemia is the most critical factor in mediating progressive tissue injury and diabetic complication-related function decline. However, the in-depth mechanisms of action had yet to be explored [29, 30]. Therefore, in this study, we meticulously evaluated the protective effect and mechanism of PEG-Loxe on diabetic kidney and heart injuries.

Investigations have revealed that treatment with liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide, or exenatide can reduce urine protein levels in patients with type 2 diabetes and protect their renal function through a mechanism possibly associated with the inhibition of oxidative stress, inflammation, and renal fibrosis [16, 31–33]. Significant augmentations in the levels of serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, and urine protein were also observed in db/db mice in this study. Kidney pathological sections likewise showed mesangial vacuolation, enlarged renal tubular lumen, thickened basement membrane, inflammatory cell infiltration, and fibrosis in db/db mice, further confirming the impairment of the renal structure and function. However, 4 weeks of separate treatment with PEG-Loxe, Lira, and Loxe reversed these pathological changes.

Hyperglycemia caused by diabetes is a long-term chronic stimulus for kidneys and can mediate renal cell apoptosis via ERS [34]. The up-regulation of GRP78 expression is a marker of cells undergoing ERS. The subsequent phosphorylation activation of PERK promotes the phosphorylation of eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2α (eIF2α), which in turn triggers the activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4) and inhibits protein synthesis and translation, as well as promotes protein degradation. Under sustained ERS, the ERS-related CHOP, JNK, and Caspase 12 apoptosis pathways are activated to induce apoptosis [35]. It is currently believed that ERS is part of the pathological process of diabetic nephropathy [35]. There is evidence that the subcutaneous injection of GLP-1 in high-fat diet-induced obese C57BL/6 mice and the exposure of HK-2 cells to GLP-1 resulted in decreased expression of ERS-associated proteins GRP78, p-eIF2α, ATF4, and CHOP, reduced expression of apoptotic proteins caspase3 and Bax, and increased expression of anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 in kidney tissues, indicating that GLP-1 could diminish ERS levels and renal cell apoptosis, thus exerting a renoprotective effect [36]. Studies have confirmed that inhibiting the PERK-eIF2α-ATF4-CHOP pathway helps with cell survival in an ERS environment [37]. Here, we showed that the expression of GRP78, p-eIF2α, ATF4, and CHOP proteins increased significantly in the kidneys of T2DM mice, as well as in HK-2 cells as compared to the normal control group (P < 0.001), suggesting that ERS occurs in kidneys. Treatment with PEG-Loxe, Lira, or Loxe markedly lessened the above protein expressions and drastically diminished GRP78 mRNA in kidney tissues. Of all the treatment modalities, PEG-Loxe-H exhibited the most considerable effect. PEG-Loxe and Lira might inhibit renal ERS and improve kidney injury in T2DM mice by regulating the GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway.

Reports show that when GLP-1RAs play a role in reducing blood glucose, they also protect cardiac muscles. Exenatide and albiglutide have been demonstrated to reduce myocardial infarct sizes in porcine and rat models of ischemia/reperfusion injury, respectively [38, 39]. In vitro, the GLP-1R inhibitor of exendin9-39 eliminates this protective effect, suggesting that GLP-1RAs are involved in cardioprotection. Peter Ueda et al. [40] also demonstrated that GLP-1RAs improved cardiac functioning in diabetic patients and reduced the risk of adverse cardiovascular events. Growing evidence suggests that an inflammatory reaction constitutes the pathogenesis of cardiac injury in diabetes and could promote the release of cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α) and chemokines, exacerbate the cardiovascular complications of diabetes [41]. Michelsen et al. [42] found that knocking out either the TLR4 or MyD88 gene in Apo E gene-deficient mice significantly decreased both aortic atherosclerosis and macrophage aggregation compared to Apo E-deficient mice alone, indicating that TLR4 and MyD88 signaling pathways play an important role in the atherosclerosis of hypercholesterolemic mice models. In our study, cardiac ultrasonography and histological evaluation revealed that both PEG-Loxe-H and Loxe significantly increased LVEF and LVFS levels after treatment with PEG-Loxe, Lira or Loxe (P < 0.05) and improved myocardial karyopyknosis, the disorganized arrangement of myocardial fiber, and other histopathological lesion phenomena, while alleviating myocardial injury, suggesting that PEG-Loxe and Loxe could effectively boost cardiac function and mitigate myocardial injury in diabetic mice. The diabetic heart is characterized by hypertrophy of cardiomyocytes, apoptosis, increased inflammation, and myocardial fibrosis leading to left ventricular diastolic dysfunction [43]. Study from Bonfioli et al. [44] revealed that GLP-1 RAs may play an active role in the treatment of Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF), particularly in obese patients, by targeting underlying inflammatory processes and promoting better overall cardiovascular outcomes. In addition, some investigations have suggested that glycolipid metabolism disorder is an important cause of myocardial injury in diabetes mellitus. We showed previously that fasting blood glucose, liver TC, and TG were considerably lower in PEG-Loxe group mice than in the T2DM group [23], indicating that PEG-Loxe could effectively reduce the levels of blood glucose and blood lipids in diabetic mice and alleviate metabolic disorder-caused myocardial injury.

Some studies have reported that the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway is activated in high glucose-treated H9C2 cells, provoking myocardial inflammatory response and accelerating apoptosis [45, 46]. Activation of this inflammatory signaling pathway and apoptosis of cardiomyocytes were also observed in H9C2 cells exposed to H/R, suggesting that the mechanism of H9C2 cell injury caused by high glucose may be partially similar to that of H/R [47, 48]. In the culture solution of H9C2 cells with hypoxia injury, LDH and CK-MB activities increased significantly (P < 0.001), which was usually a sign of irreversible damage or necrosis of cardiomyocytes. In our study, in cardiac tissues from T2DM mice and H9C2 cells with hypoxia injury, TLR4, MyD88, and p-NF-κBp65 protein expressions substantially amplified, pointing to the occurrence of an inflammatory reaction in cardiomyocytes. Likewise, the activities of LDH and CK-MB and the levels of TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway-associated proteins decreased drastically after treatment with PEG-Loxe, Lira, or Loxe. The GLP-1R inhibitor of Exendin9-39 abrogated the actions of the above drugs, demonstrating that PEG-Loxe had the potential to inhibit an inflammatory reaction under hypoxia and long-term high glucose stimulations, alleviate myocardial injury in T2DM mice and exert a cardioprotective effect.

ER stress can couple with signaling pathways such as inflammatory response and oxidative stress to further aggravate the injury of diabetes. TLR4 and ER stress have been identified as important inducers of dysfunctional activation of macrophages in metabolic diseases such as obesity and T2DM [49]. Study of Gunner et al. [50] demonstrated that an antibody targeting GRP78 could inhibit the activation of NF-κB and the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines in TLR4 ligand-treated bone marroe-derived macrophages. Recent studies have also shown that signals generated by the activation of TLRs or induction of ERS can integrate with each other to promote diabetic pathological inflammatory responses [51]. Our study revealed that PEG-Loxe protected diabetic kidneys by attenuating ER stress via inhibiting GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway, and improved cardiac injury by suppressing TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway. Whether PEG-Loxe plays a role in the cross-talk between GRP78/PERK/eIF2α and TLR4/NF-κB needs further investigation.

There are some limitations in our study. Our results suggest that PEG-Loxe can alleviate renal ER stress by inhibiting GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway. However, the detailed mechanism of the relationship between GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway and ER stress is not fully understood. Therefore, further research must be conducted to explore this potential relationship. In addition, the H9C2 cell line, which was selected for cardiomyocyte injury studies, does not accurately represent human cardiomyocytes and its gene expression profile was different from that of human cardiomyocytes. Therefore, the effects of PEG-Loxe on H9C2 cell lines in this study may not be representative of the actual condition in human cardiomyocytes.

Conclusion

PEG-Loxe could significantly mitigate renal and cardiac injuries in db/db mice and enhance renal and cardiac functions. Furthermore, PEG-Loxe could reduce renal stress response by inhibiting the endoplasmic reticulum stress GRP78/PERK/eIF2α pathway, thereby alleviating renal injury in T2DM. PEG-Loxe could also constrain the TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory pathway and myocardial apoptosis, boosting cardiac function, thus exerting a protective impact on the cardiovascular system.

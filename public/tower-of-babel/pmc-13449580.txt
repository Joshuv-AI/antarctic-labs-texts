Early warning value of serum TFF3 combined with pepsinogen ratio for monitoring the progression of gastric precancerous lesions: a retrospective study
Authors: Tao Huang, Junxiang He, Haohui Mao, Biao Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveGastric cancer (GC) has high mortality due to late diagnosis. Identifying which patients with gastric low-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (LGIN) will progress to high-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (HGIN) or early gastric cancer (EGC) remains challenging. This study aimed to develop a non-invasive method combining serum TFF3 (reflecting malignant transformation) and pepsinogen ratio (PGR, indicating gastric atrophy) to predict progression.MethodsA retrospective multicenter study included patients with confirmed LGIN, available serum, and full follow-up. Progression to HGIN/EGC within 24 months was the primary outcome. TFF3 and PGR were measured using ELISA and chemiluminescence immunoassay, respectively. Propensity score matching (1:4) balanced baseline variables. Predictive performance was assessed via ROC analysis, logistic regression, and Cox models.ResultsAfter matching, 85 patients (17 progressors) were analyzed. Progressors had higher TFF3 (11.28 vs. 7.15 ng/mL) and lower PGR (2.78 vs. 3.62; both p < 0.001). The combined model showed superior accuracy (AUC = 0.823) versus TFF3 or PGR alone, with sensitivity of 88.2%, specificity of 89.7%, and NPV of 98.3. Progressors exhibited yearly TFF3 increase (+ 1.82 ng/mL) and PGR decrease (–0.41). TFF3 correlated with Ki-67 (ρ = 0.437, p < 0.001).ConclusionThe TFF3 and PGR combination is a non-invasive tool for stratifying LGIN progression risk. Its high NPV safely excludes short-term progression, enabling extended follow-up for low-risk patients and better allocation of endoscopic resources. This approach supports personalized monitoring and may help reduce gastric cancer mortality.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) remains a major public health burden worldwide and particularly in China. According to GLOBOCAN 2022, gastric cancer accounted for 968,784 new cases and 660,175 deaths worldwide, ranking fifth in both incidence and mortality. In China, there were 358,672 new gastric cancer cases and 260,372 deaths in 2022, ranking fifth in incidence and third in cancer-related mortality nationally. Recent GBD-based analyses further indicate that the gastric cancer burden in China remains substantial, particularly among males and older adults, underscoring the need for improved prevention and risk-adapted screening strategies [1, 2]. Because prognosis is closely associated with disease stage, identifying patients at increased risk during the precancerous stage is critical for early detection and prevention. Low-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (LGIN) represents an important step in the gastric carcinogenic sequence ​ [3], and progression from LGIN to high-grade intraepithelial neoplasia (HGIN) is considered a key turning point in gastric carcinogenesis [4]. However, the biological behavior of LGIN is heterogeneous, and only a subset of lesions will progress [5]. At present, risk assessment mainly relies on repeated endoscopic biopsy surveillance, which is invasive, subject to sampling error, and burdensome in routine practice [6], highlighting the need for non-invasive serological biomarkers for risk stratification.

Pepsinogen (PG), a classical serological indicator for assessing functional gastric mucosal status, has been extensively incorporated into gastric cancer screening programs [7]. Its mechanism is based on the biological properties of PG I, secreted chiefly by chief cells in the gastric fundic glands, and PG II, secreted by glands throughout the stomach. Atrophy of the gastric corpus mucosa leads to a significant decrease in PG I levels while PG II remains relatively stable, consequently reducing the PG I/ PG II ratio (PGR) [8]. However, because PGR primarily reflects the background functional status of the gastric mucosa [9], its ability to predict the malignant progression of focal precancerous lesions such as LGIN remains limited [10, 11]. Trefoil factor 3 (TFF3), a peptide associated with intestinal metaplasia, mucosal repair, and epithelial transformation, has recently emerged as a promising biomarker in gastric precancerous lesions [12–14]. Previous studies have shown that serum TFF3 levels increase with lesion severity and may help identify patients at higher risk of progression [15, 16]. Together, these observations suggest that combining TFF3 with PGR may offer a more comprehensive approach to risk stratification than either marker alone.

From an integrated pathophysiological perspective, PGR and TFF3 provide biologically complementary information. PGR reflects the extent of background gastric mucosal atrophy and thus represents the overall mucosal milieu, whereas TFF3 is more closely associated with abnormal epithelial repair and intestinal metaplasia related transformation activity, reflecting focal epithelial alterations linked to neoplastic progression. The combination of these two markers may therefore overcome the limitations of single biomarker strategies by capturing both background mucosal status and lesion related epithelial activity. On this basis, we conducted a retrospective cohort study to evaluate the predictive value of serum TFF3 combined with PGR for progression from LGIN to HGIN or early gastric cancer (EGC).

Materials and methods

Study population

A retrospective cohort study design was employed. Patients diagnosed with gastric LGIN were selected from the gastroenterology endoscopy centers of tertiary hospitals between January 2018 and June 2023 based on the following criteria: pathological confirmation of LGIN; availability of a baseline serum sample stored at -80 °C; and completion of regular endoscopic follow-up (every 6 months ± 1 month) until June 2025, ensuring a minimum follow-up duration of 24 months for all cases. Patients who progressed to HGIN or EGC, as confirmed by endoscopic biopsy pathology within the follow-up period (≤ 24 months) and classified according to the Vienna classification for gastrointestinal epithelial neoplasia, were assigned to the progression group. Progression events were defined strictly at the index-lesion level. Only cases in which the baseline LGIN lesion progressed to HGIN or EGC on follow-up endoscopic biopsy were classified into the progression group. Newly detected HGIN or EGC arising at other gastric sites, including metachronous or de novo lesions, were not considered progression events and were excluded from the analysis. Three such cases were identified during screening and excluded.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were: (1) age between 40 and 75 years; (2) baseline pathology consistent with WHO-defined LGIN [6, 17]; (3) availability of a baseline serum sample; and (4) complete follow-up records, including endoscopic biopsy pathology reports.

Exclusion criteria comprised: (1) presence of HGIN or gastric cancer at baseline; (2) history of gastrectomy; (3) receipt of endoscopic treatment (e.g., EMR/ESD) during the follow-up period; and (4) absence of key data, such as serum biomarker levels or pathological reports.

Sample size calculation

The adjusted minimum sample size was calculated to be 315 participants. This calculation was based on the original design parameters of α = 0.05, power (1-β) = 0.9, an odds ratio (OR) of 2.5, and an assumed progression rate of 5.2%. As the enrollment period was extended to June 2025, a larger number of cases was anticipated. Verification using PASS software indicated that a total sample size of 315 was required for a non-progression-to-progression group ratio of 18:1, which included a 10% buffer for potentially invalid samples.

Group and subgroup definitions

The primary grouping was based on the 24-month pathological outcome. The progression group consisted of patients diagnosed with HGIN or EGC via endoscopic biopsy pathology within the 24-month follow-up period, according to the Vienna classification. The non-progression group included patients whose endoscopic biopsy pathology results throughout the 24-month follow-up did not meet the diagnostic criteria for HGIN or EGC, encompassing those with stable disease (persistent LGIN) or regression (reversion to a lower-grade lesion or inflammation).

Subgroup analyses were planned to explore heterogeneity. These were defined by Helicobacter pylori (Hp) infection status (determined by urea breath test or CagA antibody serology) to analyze its influence on biomarker predictive performance; lesion location (gastric antrum vs. non-antrum [gastric body/angle]) to assess anatomical dependency; and atrophy severity, stratified by PGR (> 3 vs. ≤3), to investigate the modulating effect of background mucosal status.

Study methods

Serological assays

Baseline frozen serum samples, which had undergone fewer than three freeze-thaw cycles, were used for analysis. Serum TFF3 levels were measured using a commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (Human TFF3 ELISA, Abbexa), with reported intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation (CVs) of 4.3% and 7.8%, respectively. Pepsinogen I (PG I) and II (PG II) levels were quantified via chemiluminescence immunoassay on a MAGLUMI X8 analyzer (reagents from Shenzhen New Industries Biomedical Engineering Co., Ltd.; product number: 130201019 M, batch number: 095240221).

Pathological review

All histological slides were independently reviewed by two experienced pathologists. Any discrepancies in interpretation were resolved through consultation with a third senior pathologist. The reviewed parameters included lesion grading (LGIN/HGIN), proliferative activity (Ki-67 index, with positivity defined as > 30% nuclear staining), and p53 mutation status (assessed by immunohistochemistry using clone DO-7; abnormal expression was defined as either complete absence or diffuse strong positivity).

Data extraction

Structured data were extracted from the electronic medical record system. These data included: (1) demographic information (age, sex, Hp infection status); (2) endoscopic characteristics (lesion location, size, and morphology classified according to the Paris classification); (3) pathological data (baseline and follow-up grades, Ki-67 index, p53 status); and (4) serological measurements (TFF3, PG I, PG II levels).

Outcome measures

The primary endpoints and related measures were: (1) The predictive performance of TFF3, PGR, and their combined model for progression to HGIN or EGC within 24 months, assessed by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), sensitivity (Sen), specificity (Spe), PPV, and negative predictive value (NPV). (2) The comparison of predictive performance between the combined model and the current clinical standard (baseline pathological grade plus PG screening results), evaluated using the net reclassification index (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement index (IDI). (3) Dynamic risk analysis: The association between changes in serum biomarkers (ΔTFF3, ΔPGR, calculated as the difference between values at follow-up [12 months] and baseline) or their serial measurements and the time to progression to HGIN/EGC was analyzed using Cox proportional hazards regression models. Results were expressed as hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). The proportional hazards assumption was tested. (4) Histological correlation: The Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ) was used to assess the relationship between serum markers (TFF3, PGR) and baseline histological indicators (Ki-67 index, p53 status).

Statistical analysis

To reduce potential confounding, PSM was performed at a 1:4 ratio using a caliper width of 0.2 to improve baseline comparability between the progression and non-progression groups. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using the independent-samples t test. Categorical variables were expressed as number (percentage) and compared using the chi-square test.

The predictive performance of serum biomarkers was evaluated using ROC curves, and the AUC with 95% CIs was calculated. The DeLong test was used to compare differences in AUC between models. Sensitivity, specificity, PPV and NPV were calculated at the optimal cut-off values.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to assess factors associated with progression of LGIN. ORs with 95% CIs were reported. Although propensity score matching was used to improve baseline comparability, selected baseline variables were additionally included in the multivariable regression model to account for potential residual confounding after matching. Candidate variables were selected based on univariable analysis together with clinical considerations. Given the limited number of progression events, the number of covariates included in the final model was intentionally restricted to reduce the risk of overfitting. A combined predictive model was constructed based on logistic regression using TFF3 and PGR, together with relevant clinical variables included in the multivariable model. Candidate variables were selected based on clinical relevance and univariable analysis. Given the limited number of progression events, the number of covariates included in the final model was intentionally restricted to reduce the risk of overfitting.

For patients with available 12-month follow-up serum samples, annualized changes in TFF3, PG I, PG II, and PGR were calculated as follows: (12-month value -baseline value)/years of follow-up. These changes were compared between the progression and non-progression groups using the independent-samples t test.

Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess the associations between serum biomarkers and histopathological indicators. Subgroup analyses were performed to evaluate the predictive performance of the combined model across different patient strata. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study flow and baseline characteristics

This study was conducted between January 2018 and June 2023. During this period, a total of 587 patients pathologically diagnosed with gastric LGIN were screened. After applying strict inclusion and exclusion criteria, 315 patients were ultimately enrolled in the analysis cohort. All enrolled patients completed regular endoscopic follow-up for at least 24 months, with a median follow-up duration of 26.3 months. By the end of the study in December 2023, 17 of these 315 patients (5.4%) had progressed to HGIN or EGC and were defined as the progression group; the remaining 298 patients (94.6%), who did not experience progression, constituted the non-progression group.

To effectively control for potential confounding factors, propensity score matching (PSM) was applied at a 1:4 ratio between the progression and non-progression groups. This resulted in a matched cohort of 85 patients, comprising 17 in the progression group and 68 in the non-progression group. Comparative analysis of the baseline characteristics of these matched groups revealed no statistically significant differences. The mean age of the overall matched population was 61.24 ± 8.73 years, with the progression group averaging 62.88 ± 7.92 years and the non-progression group 60.85 ± 8.95 years (t = 0.873, P = 0.386). Regarding sex distribution, males accounted for 56.47% (48/85) of the total matched cohort, 64.71% (11/17) in the progression group, and 54.41% (37/68) in the non-progression group (χ²=0.579, P = 0.447).

No significant differences were observed in Hp infection status, with an overall positivity rate of 62.35% (53/85), compared to 70.59% (12/17) in the progression group and 60.29% (41/68) in the non-progression group (χ²=0.592, P = 0.442). Endoscopic characteristics were also comparable between groups. Lesions located in the gastric antrum were present in 54.12% (46/85) of the overall cohort, 58.82% (10/17) of the progression group, and 52.94% (36/68) of the non-progression group (χ²=0.189, P = 0.664). The mean lesion size was 9.56 ± 4.23 mm overall, 11.24 ± 5.12 mm in the progression group, and 9.12 ± 3.87 mm in the non- progression group (t = 1.894, P = 0.062). Paris type IIa lesions were identified in 69.41% (59/85) of all patients, 76.47% (13/17) of the progression group, and 67.65% (46/68) of the non-progression group (χ²=0.488, P = 0.485).

Histological features similarly showed no significant intergroup differences. High Ki-67 expression (> 30%) was observed in 36.47% (31/85) of the total matched cohort, 52.94% (9/17) of the progression group, and 32.35% (22/68) of the non-progression group (χ²=2.591, P = 0.107). Aberrant p53 expression was found in 22.35% (19/85) overall, 35.29% (6/17) in the progression group, and 19.12% (13/68) in the non-progression group (χ²=2.102, P = 0.147). These results indicate that the baseline characteristics were well-balanced between the two groups after matching, ensuring comparability for subsequent analyses (Table 1).

Comparison of serum TFF3 and PG levels

Comparison of baseline serum biomarker levels between the matched progression and non-progression groups revealed significant differences (Table 2). Serum TFF3 concentrations were significantly elevated in the progression group (11.28 ± 2.45 ng/mL) compared to the non-progression group (7.15 ± 1.92 ng/mL), a difference that was statistically significant (t = 8.274, P < 0.001). Similarly, the pepsinogen ratio (PGR, PG I/PG II) was significantly lower in the progression group (2.78 ± 0.61) compared to the non-progression group (3.62 ± 0.88) (t=-4.188, P < 0.001). In contrast, the levels of PG I and PG II individually did not differ significantly between the two groups. The PG I level was 55.12 ± 16.57 µg/L in the progression group versus 61.58 ± 18.02 µg/L in the non- progression group (t=-1.342, P = 0.183). The PG II level was 13.45 ± 4.21 µg/L in the progression group compared to 11.85 ± 3.83 µg/L in the non-progression group (t = 1.563, P = 0.122).

Predictive performance of serum biomarkers for lesion progression

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive value of each serum biomarker for progression within 24 months (Table 3; Fig. 1). For TFF3, the area under the curve (AUC) was 0.741 (95% CI: 0.732–0.950). At the optimal cut-off value of > 9.35ng/mL, the sensitivity was 82.4%, specificity was 83.8%, PPV was 36.8%, and negative predictive value (NPV) was 97.6%. The PGR yielded an AUC of 0.779 (95% CI: 0.659–0.899). Using a cut-off value of ≤ 3.05, the sensitivity was 76.5%, specificity was 75.0%, PPV was 28.1%, and NPV was 96.2%.

A combined predictive model was subsequently constructed using multivariate logistic regression incorporating both TFF3 and PGR. This combined model demonstrated significantly superior predictive performance compared to either biomarker alone, with an AUC of 0.823 (95% CI: 0.862–0.984). At the optimal cut-off value of > 0.52, the sensitivity was 88.2%, specificity was 89.7%, PPV was 55.6%, and NPV was 98.3. Multivariate analysis of progression risk

A multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to investigate factors influencing progression in LGIN patients, after adjusting for potential confounders including age, Hp infection status, and lesion size. The analysis identified a serum TFF3 level > 9.35ng/mL as a strong independent predictor of progression, with a β coefficient of 1.746, Wald χ² of 12.843, an OR of 5.732 (95% CI: 2.184–15.046), and a P value < 0.001. Similarly, a PGR ≤ 3.05 was also an independent predictor of progression, with a β of 1.168, Wald χ² of 5.912, an OR of 3.215 (95% CI: 1.258–8.215), and a P value of 0.015. In contrast, other factors did not demonstrate statistically significant predictive value. The OR for each additional year of age was 1.042 (95% CI: 0.978–1.220, P = 0.289), for Hp positivity was 1.668 (95% CI: 0.645–4.315, P = 0.250), and for each 1 mm increase in lesion size was 1.091 (95% CI: 0.974–1.221, P = 0.117) (Table 4).

Analysis of dynamic changes in serum biomarkers

Analysis of serial serum samples was performed for 102 patients who had available 12-month follow-up samples (Table 5). The annualized change in TFF3 (ΔTFF3) was + 1.82 ± 0.75ng/mL/year in the progression group (n = 15), significantly different from the change observed in the non-progression group (n = 87), which was-0.15 ± 0.64ng/mL/year (P < 0.001). This indicates a pronounced upward trend in TFF3 levels among progressors, whereas levels remained stable or slightly decreased in non-progressors.

Correlation between serum biomarkers and histopathological indicators

Spearman correlation analysis was employed to assess the relationship between serum biomarkers and histopathological indicators (Table 6). Serum TFF3 levels showed a significant positive correlation with the histological proliferation index Ki-67 (ρ = 0.437, P < 0.001). A weaker positive correlation was also observed between serum TFF3 and aberrant p53 expression. In contrast, PGR was significantly negatively correlated with the Ki-67 index (ρ=-0.361, P < 0.001). No statistically significant correlation was found between PGR and aberrant p53 expression. These findings suggest that serum levels of TFF3 and PGR are associated with histological changes characteristic of gastric low-grade intraepithelial neoplasia.

Discussion

In this study, elevated serum TFF3 and reduced PGR were associated with progression of LGIN, and their combined assessment provided better discrimination than either marker alone. These findings suggest that integrating epithelial transformation-related activity with background mucosal atrophy may improve risk stratification in gastric precancerous lesions. The higher sensitivity in the H. pylori-positive subgroup (91.7%) might be related to the persistent inflammatory stimulation by H. pylori leading to TFF3 overexpression [18]. The high sensitivity in the gastric antrum subgroup is consistent with anatomical-biological congruence, as the antrum is a common site for metaplasia and neoplasia. Previous studies have suggested that alterations in the atrophic gastric mucosal microenvironment may be accompanied by increased TFF3 expression, raising the possibility of an interaction between mucosal atrophy and epithelial transformation during gastric carcinogenesis [19]. In addition, study reported median serum TFF3 levels of 2.56 ng/mL in healthy controls without H. pylori infection and 2.79 ng/mL in controls with H. pylori infection, suggesting that the higher TFF3 levels observed in progressive lesions may also represent a clinically meaningful deviation from baseline circulating levels [20]. It should be noted that serum TFF3 is not a specific marker for gastric precancerous lesions. Elevated levels of TFF3 are not only observed in patients with chronic kidney disease [21], but also reported in other tumors such as colorectal cancer and pancreatic cancer [22, 23]. This means that the level of TFF3 in circulating blood may not fully reflect the local secretion of gastric mucosa. Therefore, serum TFF3 may not exclusively reflect local gastric mucosal secretion, and relevant comorbidities could potentially influence its clinical interpretation. In this context, combining TFF3 with PGR may provide a more robust assessment by integrating information on both epithelial activity and background mucosal status. This is consistent with recent reviews on the non-invasive detection of gastric preneoplastic lesions, which have highlighted the limited specificity of single serum biomarkers and the need for multi-marker strategies to improve clinical utility [24]. Nevertheless, the potential confounding effects of renal dysfunction, other malignancies, and additional comorbid conditions cannot be fully excluded, and future prospective studies with more detailed assessment of these factors are warranted.

In the present study, elevated serum TFF3 levels were associated with progression of dysplasia, and the combination of TFF3 with PGR provided better discriminatory performance for progression than either marker alone in ROC analysis. Our findings are consistent with previous studies evaluating TFF3 and pepsinogen in gastric neoplasia. Aikou et al. reported that serum TFF3 showed better diagnostic performance than pepsinogen for gastric cancer detection, and that combining TFF3 with pepsinogen further improved screening performance compared with pepsinogen alone [20]. Furthermore, a large-sample controlled study by Kaise et al. also confirmed that the area under the curve of TFF3 combined with PG detection for diagnosing gastric cancer is significantly higher than that of PG alone, and it is more advantageous in sensitivity and specificity [25]. Together, these studies support the added value of combined biomarker assessment, which is consistent with our finding that TFF3 plus PGR provides better discrimination than either marker alone. Importantly, our study extends this concept from gastric cancer detection to risk stratification in patients with LGIN.

Overall, our results suggest that TFF3 and PGR reflect two complementary dimensions of gastric carcinogenesis. TFF3 appears to be more closely related to lesion-associated epithelial transformation activity, whereas PGR reflects background gastric mucosal atrophy and the broader pro-carcinogenic microenvironment [26–29]. Thus, the combined assessment of these two markers may provide a more comprehensive evaluation of both lesion-related biological activity and the underlying mucosal environment. Rather than proving a direct mechanistic interaction, our findings support the clinical usefulness of integrating these complementary pathological dimensions into a single risk-stratification model [14, 30]. The superior performance of the combined model suggests that patients with elevated TFF3 in the setting of atrophy may warrant closer surveillance; however, this potential application should be further evaluated in prospective studies before being incorporated into routine follow-up strategies.

However, this study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design, although employing PSM to control for confounding, may still be subject to selection bias. Second, the number of cases in the progression group was relatively small (n = 17). Given the limited number of progression events, the multivariable model may still be susceptible to instability and overfitting despite restricting the number of included covariates. In addition, although propensity score matching and multivariable adjustment were used to reduce confounding, this combined strategy cannot fully eliminate concerns regarding model stability in a small matched cohort. Therefore, the adjusted effect estimates should be interpreted cautiously, particularly in subgroup analyses, and require further validation in larger prospective cohorts. Third, the predictive performance of the combined model was derived and evaluated within the same study cohort, and no independent external validation dataset was available. In addition, the optimal cut-off values were selected in a data-driven manner within this population. Therefore, the reported ROC-based indices, including AUC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV, may reflect apparent performance rather than fully generalizable predictive accuracy, and should be interpreted cautiously until validated in independent prospective cohorts. Fourth, this study did not investigate deeper mechanisms such as TFF3 gene polymorphisms, transcript variants, or post-translational modifications, which could influence its serum levels and function. Future research should progress towards prospective validation cohorts and integrate multi-omics data, such as endoscopic radiomics and microbiome analysis, to construct more comprehensive predictive models. Mechanistically, the causal role of TFF3 in LGIN progression and its interaction with the atrophic microenvironment need to be explored using models like organoids or mouse models. From a clinical perspective, the potential role of the combined score in surveillance decision-making should be evaluated cautiously and confirmed in future prospective studies before routine application.

Conclusion

This study developed a non-invasive combined model based on serum TFF3 and PGR for predicting progression in patients with LGIN. The results showed that elevated TFF3 and reduced PGR were associated with disease progression, and that their combined assessment provided better predictive performance than either biomarker alone. Dynamic changes in TFF3 and PGR during follow-up may also have value in identifying patients at increased risk of progression. In addition, the association between serum biomarkers and tissue proliferation indices supports the biological relevance of these findings. Overall, these results suggest that combined assessment of TFF3 and PGR may be a promising approach for risk stratification in LGIN, although further prospective validation is required before this strategy can be applied in routine clinical management.

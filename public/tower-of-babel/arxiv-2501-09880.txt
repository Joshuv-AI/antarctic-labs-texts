A Strengthening of the Harnack Inequality
Marek Svetlik

Abstract
. We prove the stronger version of Harnack’s inequality for positive harmonic functions defined on the unit disc.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 31A05; Secondary 30C80; 30F45

1.
Introduction and the main result

Let
\mathbb{U}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the unit disc. It is known (see e.g.
[
1
, p. 47]
or
[
4
, p. 13]
) that the harmonic function
u:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
satisfies Harnack’s inequality

(1)

\frac{1-|z|}{1+|z|}\leqslant\frac{u(z)}{u(0)}\leqslant\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}

for all
z\in\mathbb{U}
.

In this paper we prove the following stronger version of this classical result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be a harmonic function. Then

(2)

\left(\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\frac{|\nabla u(0)|}{u(0)}\frac{|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)^{-1}\leqslant\frac{u(z)}{u(0)}\leqslant\left(\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\frac{|\nabla u(0)|}{u(0)}\frac{|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}\right),

for all
z\in\mathbb{U}
. Here
\nabla u=(u_{x},u_{y})
is the gradient of the function
u
.

Since for harmonic function
u:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
we have
|\nabla u(0)|\leqslant 2u(0)
(see inequality (
12
) below), it follows that

(3)

\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\frac{|\nabla u(0)|}{u(0)}\frac{|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}\leqslant\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}

and

(4)

\left(\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\frac{|\nabla u(0)|}{u(0)}\frac{|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)^{-1}\geqslant\frac{1-|z|}{1+|z|}

for all
z\in\mathbb{U}
. Therefore, the inequality (
2
) is stronger than the inequality (
1
).

Fix
c\in\mathbb{U}
and denote by
\varphi_{c}
the mapping given by

\varphi_{c}(z)=\frac{z+c}{1+\overline{c}z}.

It is known that
\varphi_{c}
is a conformal isomorphism of the unit disc
\mathbb{U}
onto itself. In particular, if
c=1
, then we define
\varphi_{c}(z)=1
for all
z\in\mathbb{U}
.

The following example shows that there is function
u:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
and
z\in\mathbb{U}
, so that the equality in (
1
) holds.

Example 1
.

Let
0\leqslant c\leqslant 1
and let
u_{1},u_{2}:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be defined by

u_{1}(z)=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\frac{1+z\varphi_{c}(z)}{1-z\varphi_{c}(z)}.

and

u_{2}(z)=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\frac{1-z\varphi_{c}(z)}{1+z\varphi_{c}(z)}.

Then
u_{1}(0)=u_{2}(0)=1
and
|\nabla u_{1}(0)|=|\nabla u_{2}(0)|=2c
. Further,

\frac{u_{1}(x)}{u_{1}(0)}=\frac{1+x^{2}}{1-x^{2}}+\frac{|\nabla u_{1}(0)|}{u_{1}(0)}\frac{x}{1-x^{2}}

and

\frac{u_{2}(x)}{u_{2}(0)}=\left(\frac{1+x^{2}}{1-x^{2}}+\frac{|\nabla u_{2}(0)|}{u_{2}(0)}\frac{x}{1-x^{2}}\right)^{-1},

for all
x\in[0,1)
.

To prove the Theorem
1
, we use the hyperbolic densities on the unit disc
\mathbb{U}
and on the right half-plane
\mathbb{K}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{z}>0\}
.

The hyperbolic density on the unit disc
\mathbb{U}
is defined by

\rho_{\mathbb{U}}(z)=\frac{2}{1-|z|^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{U}.

Based on Riemann’s Mapping Theorem we can define the hyperbolic density on an arbitrary simply connected domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
,
\Omega
different from
\mathbb{C}
(we call these domains hyperbolic), in the following way

\rho_{\Omega}(z)=\rho_{\mathbb{U}}(\phi(z))|\phi^{\prime}(z)|,\quad z\in\Omega,

where
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{U}
is a conformal isomorphism from
\Omega
onto
\mathbb{U}
. In particular, if
\psi
is defined by
\displaystyle\psi(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
, then
\psi
is a conformal isomorphism from
\mathbb{K}
onto
\mathbb{U}
and by direct calculation we obtain

\rho_{\mathbb{K}}(z)=\frac{1}{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{z}},\quad z\in\mathbb{K}.

We define the hyperbolic distance on a hyperbolic domain
\Omega
as follows

(5)

d_{\Omega}(z_{1},z_{2})=\inf\int_{\gamma}\rho_{\Omega}(z)|dz|,

where the infimum is taken over all
C^{1}
curves
\gamma
that connect
z_{1}
with
z_{2}
in
\Omega
. Specifically, it can be shown that

d_{\mathbb{U}}(z,0)=\log{\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|}}=2\mathop{\mathrm{artanh}}{|z|}.

Let
f:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\Omega_{2}
(
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are hyperbolic domains in
\mathbb{C}
) be a holomorphic function. The famous Schwarz-Pick lemma asserts that

(6)

\rho_{\Omega_{2}}(f(z))|f^{\prime}(z)|\leqslant\rho_{\Omega_{1}}(z),\quad z\in\Omega_{1}.

As a corollary of (
6
) we have

(7)

d_{\Omega_{2}}(f(z_{1}),f(z_{2}))\leqslant d_{\Omega_{1}}(z_{1},z_{2}),\quad\mbox{for all}\quad z_{1},z_{2}\in\Omega_{1}.

Moreover, if
f
is a conformal isomorphism from
\Omega_{1}
onto
\Omega_{2}
, then equalities hold in (
6
) and (
7
). Thus, if
f
is a conformal isomorphism from
\Omega_{1}
onto
\Omega_{2}
, then
f
is an isometry from the metric space
(\Omega_{1},d_{\Omega_{1}})
onto the metric space
(\Omega_{2},d_{\Omega_{2}})
.

The hyperbolic derivative of
f
at
z\in\Omega_{1}
(for motivation and details see section 5 in
[
3
]
, cf.
[
2
]
) is defined as follows

f^{h}(z)=\frac{\rho_{\Omega_{2}}(f(z))}{\rho_{\Omega_{1}}(z)}f^{\prime}(z).

From (
6
) it follows immediately that
|f^{h}(z)|\leqslant 1
for all
z\in\Omega_{1}
.

Using this notion, A. F. Beardon and T. K. Carne proved the following theorem, which is a strengthening of the Schwarz-Pick inequality (
7
).

Theorem 2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
f:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\Omega_{2}
(
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are hyperbolic domains in
\mathbb{C}
) be a holomorphic function. Then

(8)

d_{\Omega_{2}}(f(z_{1}),f(z_{2}))\leqslant\log\Big(\cosh{d_{\Omega_{1}}(z_{1},z_{2})}+|f^{h}(z)|\sinh{d_{\Omega_{1}}(z_{1},z_{2})}\Big).

Note that in the paper
[
2
]
the previous theorem was formulated and proved for the case
\Omega_{1}=\Omega_{2}=\mathbb{U}
. The version of this theorem that we have stated follows directly from the theorem given in
[
2
]
. The inequality (
8
) plays a crucial role in the proof of the Theorem
1
. In addition, the following elementary facts play an important role in this proof (for this approach see
[
6
]
).

1)

If
u:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
is a harmonic function, then it is known that there exists a holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{f}=u
and
f(0)=u(0)
.

2)

Using the Schwarz-Pick inequality, we obtain

(9)

\rho_{\mathbb{K}}(f(z))|f^{\prime}(z)|\leqslant\rho_{\mathbb{U}}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{U}.

3)

Due to the Cauchy-Riemann equations, we have
f^{\prime}=u_{x}-iu_{y}
, i.e.
f^{\prime}=(u_{x},-u_{y})=\overline{\nabla u}
. From this follows

(10)

|f^{\prime}(z)|=|\overline{\nabla u}(z)|=|\nabla u(z)|,\quad z\in\mathbb{U}.

4)

Since
\displaystyle\rho_{\mathbb{K}}(z)=\frac{1}{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{z}}
,
z\in\mathbb{K}
we get

(11)

\rho_{\mathbb{K}}(f(z))=\rho_{\mathbb{K}}(u(z)),\quad z\in\mathbb{U}.

5)

From (
9
), (
10
) and (
11
) we immediately obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
u:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be a harmonic function. Then

\rho_{\mathbb{K}}(u(z))|\nabla u(z)|\leqslant\rho_{\mathbb{U}}(z),

i.e.

(12)

|\nabla u(z)|\leqslant\frac{2u(z)}{1-|z|^{2}},

for all
z\in\mathbb{U}
. Moreover, if
u
is the real part of a conformal isomorphism from
\mathbb{U}
onto
\mathbb{K}
, then in (
12
) equality holds for all
z\in\mathbb{U}
. On the other hand, if in (
12
) equality holds for some
z\in\mathbb{U}
, then
u
is the real part of a conformal isomorphism
\mathbb{U}
onto
\mathbb{K}
.

We call the facts 1)-5) together with the Proposition
1
the
half-plane method
. This method can be seen as an analogous version of the strip method used in the paper
[
7
]
(see also
[
6
]
).

Note that the following theorem can be derived as a corollary of (
12
).

Theorem 3

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
u:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be a harmonic function. Then

(13)

d_{\mathbb{K}}(u(z_{1}),u(z_{2}))\leqslant d_{\mathbb{U}}(z_{1},z_{2}),

for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{U}
.

Remark 1
.

In
[
5
]
, M. Marković proved the Theorem
3
for the case where the domain of the function
u
is
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}{z}>0\}
, but this is not essential since
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{U}
are conformally equivalent.

Remark 2
.

Considering the proof of the Theorem
3
from the paper
[
8
]
, it is straightforward to prove that (
13
) is equivalent to Harnack’s inequality.

For further results of this type of inequalities, we refer the reader to
[
9
]
and the literature cited there.

2.
Proof of the main result

First we recall another notion and prove two lemmas.

For a hyperbolic domain
\Omega
,
a\in\Omega
and
r>0
we denote by
\overline{D}_{\Omega}(a,r)
a hyperbolic closed disc with centre
a
and radius
r
, i.e.
\overline{D}_{\Omega}(a,r)=\{z\in\Omega:d_{\Omega}(z,a)\leqslant r\}
. It is simply checked that

(14)

\overline{D}_{\mathbb{U}}(0,2\mathop{\mathrm{artanh}}{r})=\{z\in\mathbb{U}:|z|\leqslant r\},

for all
r\in(0,1)
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
b\in(0,+\infty)
and
r\in(0,1)
. If
z\in\overline{D}_{\mathbb{K}}(b,2\mathop{\mathrm{artanh}}{r})
then

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{z}\in\left[\frac{1-r}{1+r}b,\frac{1+r}{1-r}b\right].

Proof.

Recall that the mapping
\psi
defined by
\displaystyle\psi(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}
is a conformal isomorphism from
\mathbb{K}
onto
\mathbb{U}
. Set
\kappa=\psi^{-1}
,
a=\psi(b)
and
\kappa_{b}=\kappa\circ\varphi_{a}
. It is clear that
\kappa_{b}
is a conformal isomorphism from
\mathbb{U}
onto
\mathbb{K}
and
\kappa_{b}(0)=b
. Therefore,
\kappa_{b}
is an isometry from the metric space
(\mathbb{U},d_{\mathbb{U}})
onto the metric space
(\mathbb{K},d_{\mathbb{K}})
. Therefore

\kappa_{b}\Big(\overline{D}_{\mathbb{U}}(0,2\mathop{\mathrm{artanh}}{r})\Big)=\overline{D}_{\mathbb{K}}(b,2\mathop{\mathrm{artanh}}{r}).

On the other hand, taking into account (
14
) we have

\kappa_{b}\Big(\overline{D}_{\mathbb{U}}(0,2\mathop{\mathrm{artanh}}{r})\Big)=\kappa_{b}\Big(\{z\in\mathbb{U}:|z|\leqslant r\}\Big).

Furthermore, we obtain by straightforward calculations that

\kappa_{b}\Big(\{z\in\mathbb{U}:|z|\leqslant r\}\Big)
is a closed Euclidean disc with centre
\displaystyle c=\frac{1+r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}b
and radius
\displaystyle R=\frac{2r}{1-r^{2}}b
. So, if
\displaystyle z\in\kappa_{b}\Big(\{z\in\mathbb{U}:|z|\leqslant r\}\Big)
then

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{z}\in[c-R,c+R]=\left[\frac{1-r}{1+r}b,\frac{1+r}{1-r}b\right].

∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
0\leqslant c\leqslant 1
. Then, for all
z\in\mathbb{U}
,

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log(\cosh{d_{\mathbb{U}}(z,0)}+c\sinh{d_{\mathbb{U}}(z,0)})&=\log\left(\frac{1+|z|^{2}+2c|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)\\
&=d_{\mathbb{U}}(|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|),0).\end{split}

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathbb{U}
be arbitrary. The first equality in (
15
) holds due to the fact that

\cosh{d_{\mathbb{U}}(z,0)}=\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\quad\mbox{ i }\quad\sinh{d_{\mathbb{U}}(z,0)}=\frac{2|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}.

To prove the second equality in (
15
), we have to determine a point
R(z)\in[0,1)
such that

(16)

d_{\mathbb{U}}(R(z),0)=\log\left(\frac{1+|z|^{2}+2c|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}\right).

Note that the equality (
16
) is equivalent to the equality

\frac{1+R(z)}{1-R(z)}=\frac{1+|z|^{2}+2c|z|}{1-|z|^{2}},

and from this we get
\displaystyle R(z)=|z|\frac{c+|z|}{1+c|z|}=|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)
.
∎

Proof of the Theorem
1
.

By using the half-plane method, we obtain that there is a unique holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
such that

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{f}=u
,
f(0)=u(0)
,
|f^{\prime}(0)|=|\nabla u(0)|
and

|f^{h}(0)|=\frac{\rho_{\mathbb{K}}(f(0))}{\rho_{\mathbb{U}}(0)}|f^{\prime}(0)|=\frac{|\nabla u(0)|}{2u(0)}.

Define
\displaystyle c=\frac{|\nabla u(0)|}{2u(0)}
. According to Theorem
2
, if
\Omega_{1}=\mathbb{U}
and
\Omega_{2}=\mathbb{K}
we get

(17)

d_{\mathbb{K}}(f(z),f(0))\leqslant\log\Big(\cosh{d_{\mathbb{U}}(z,0)}+c\sinh{d_{\mathbb{U}}(z,0)}\Big)

for all
z\in\mathbb{U}
. By Lemma
2
we thus have

d_{\mathbb{K}}(f(z),f(0))\leqslant 2\mathop{\mathrm{artanh}}{\Big(|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)\Big)},

i.e

f(z)\in\overline{D}_{\mathbb{K}}\Big(f(0),2\mathop{\mathrm{artanh}}{\Big(|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)\Big)}\Big).

Using Lemma
1
(note that
f(0)=u(0)
and
u=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}{f}
) we now obtain

(18)

u(z)\in\left[\frac{1-|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)}{1+|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)}u(0),\frac{1+|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)}{1-|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)}u(0)\right]

But since
\displaystyle\frac{1+|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)}{1-|z|\varphi_{c}(|z|)}=\frac{1+|z|^{2}+2c|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}
, it follows that (
18
) is equivalent to (
1
).
∎

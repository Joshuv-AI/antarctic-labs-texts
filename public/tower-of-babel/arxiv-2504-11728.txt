Yuki Nishimura, Kazuya Haraguchi

Abstract
When we deal with a matroid ℳ = ( U , ℐ ) {\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}}) , we usually assume that it is implicitly given by means of the independence (IND) oracle. Time complexity of many existing algorithms is polynomially bounded with respect to | U | |U| and the running time of the IND-oracle. However, they are not efficient any more when U U is exponentially large in some context. In this paper, we propose two algorithms for enumerating matroid bases such that the time complexity does not depend on | U | |U| . For some integer L L , the first algorithm enumerates the first L L minimum-weight bases in incremental-polynomial time and the remaining ones in polynomial-delay. To design the algorithm, we assume two oracles other than the IND-oracle: the MinB-oracle that returns a minimum basis and the REL-oracle that returns a relevant element one by one in non-decreasing order of weight. The proposed algorithm is applicable to enumeration of minimum bases of binary matroids from cycle space and cut space, all of which have exponentially large U U with respect to a given graph. The highlight in this context is that, to design the REL-oracle for cut space, we develop the first polynomial-delay algorithm that enumerates all relevant cuts of a given graph in non-decreasing order of weight. The second algorithm enumerates all sets of linearly independent r r -dimensional r r vectors over 𝐺𝐹 ⁡ ( 2 ) \mathit{GF}(2) in polynomial-delay, which immediately yields a polynomial-delay algorithm that enumerates all unweighted bases of a binary matroid such that elements are closed under addition.

Introduction

Let
\real,\real_{\geq 0},\real_{+}
denote the sets of reals,
nonnegative reals and positive reals, respectively.
For a ground set
U
and a family
{\mathcal{I}}

of subsets of
U
,

{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
is called a
matroid

if the following axioms are satisfied.
(I)
\emptyset\in{\mathcal{I}}
;
(II) for
I,J\subseteq U
such that
I\subseteq J
,

J\in{\mathcal{I}}
implies
I\in{\mathcal{I}}
; and
(III) for
I,J\in{\mathcal{I}}
,
if
|I|<|J|
, then there is
x\in J-I

such that
I\cup\{x\}\in{\mathcal{I}}
.
A subset
I\in{\mathcal{I}}

(resp.,
I\not\in{\mathcal{I}}
) is called
independent

(resp.,
dependent
),
and a maximal independent set is called a
basis
.
It is well-known that the cardinality of any basis
is equal
and called the
rank of
\mathcal{M}
.
Given a weight function
w:U\to\real
,
we write
w(S)\coloneqq\sum_{x\in S}w(x)
for
S\subseteq U

and call it the weight of
S
.
A basis is
minimum-weight
(or
minimum
for short)
if the weight attains the minimum over all bases.

Introduced by Whitney
[
43
]
,
matroid is among well-studied discrete structures
in theoretical computer science.
Various combinatorial problems on matroid
can be solved efficiently
(e.g., minimum-weight basis
[
11
]
,
matroid intersection
[
8
,
12
]
,
matroid partition
[
8
]
).
It is so expressive a mathematical model
that admits us to solve many real problems (e.g., minimum spanning tree).
A matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})

is usually given by means of independence oracle
(a.k.a., membership oracle)
that returns whether
S\in{\mathcal{I}}
or
S\not\in{\mathcal{I}}

to a query subset
S\subseteq U
.

Enumeration problem
in general
asks to list all required solutions without duplication
and has practical applications in
such fields as data mining and bioinformatics
[
1
,
13
,
27
,
42
]
.
In general, an enumeration algorithm takes
at least the computation time proportional to the output size,
where the output size can be exponentially large
with respect to the input size.
An enumeration algorithm
has been evaluated in terms of both input size and output size.
According to
[
21
]
, the algorithm is called

•

output-polynomial

if the overall computation time is bounded by
a polynomial with respect to the input size and output size;

•

incremental-polynomial
(
incremental-poly
)
if the computation time for finding the
\ell
-th solution
is bounded by a polynomial with respect to the input size and
\ell
; and

•

polynomial-delay
(
poly-delay
)
if the delay (i.e., computation time between any two consecutive outputs)
is bounded by a polynomial with respect to the input size.

Various enumeration problems have been studied in the context of matroid
[
23
,
25
,
31
]
.
Uno
[
39
]
proposed
efficient algorithms for enumerating all bases
for such fundamental matroids as

linear matroid and
matching matroid. For graphic matroid,
there is also an algorithm for enumerating
all bases (i.e., spanning trees)
[
36
]
,
which is optimal in the sense of both time and space.
Hamacher et al.
[
17
]
presented an algorithm to enumerate

k
-smallest bases in the sense of weight
in
{\mathcal{O}}(|U|\log|U|+k\cdot|U|\cdot r\cdot\tau_{\mathrm{IND}})
time,
where
\tau_{\mathrm{IND}}
denotes the computation time
of the independence oracle.
These algorithms are efficient
in the sense that the time complexity is polynomially bounded.
The bounding polynomials depend on
the cardinality
|U|
of the ground set
U
.

In the literature, however, there are matroids
such that the ground set consists of an exponential number of elements
with respect to the size of a given graph;
e.g., binary matroids from cycle space
[
9
,
3
]
,
path space
[
18
]
, cut space
[
9
]

and
U(T)
-space
[
19
]
.
These matroids are never just artificial.
For example, a basis in the binary matroid from cycle space, called a

minimum cycle basis
in the literature, has applications in
electric engineering
[
4
]
,
structural analysis of skeletal structures
[
7
]
,
network analysis
[
40
]

and periodic event scheduling
[
5
]
.
A minimum cycle basis can be found in polynomial time
[
20
,
32
,
35
]
,
and there are problems of
finding other kinds of cycle basis
[
22
]
.
However,
we cannot expect the above-mentioned enumeration algorithms
to run efficiently for matroids like this.

In this paper,
assuming a matroid with an exponentially large ground set,
we propose two new enumeration algorithms,
one for all minimum-weight bases and
the other for all (unweighted) bases, respectively.
The point is that, for both algorithms,
the polynomials bounding the time complexity
do not depend on the ground set size.

After we prepare terminologies, notations and
fundamental properties in Sect.
2
,
we present the main contributions of the paper
from Sections
3
to
5
,
followed by concluding remarks in Sect.

Enumeration of Minimum Bases (Sect.
3
).

To design the algorithm for this purpose,
we assume two oracles other than
the independence oracle:
the minimum basis oracle that returns a minimum basis
and the relevant oracle that returns a “relevant” element
one by one in non-decreasing order of weight.
An element is relevant if there is a minimum basis
that contains it.
For some integer
L
,
the proposed algorithm generates the
\ell
-th solution
(\ell\leq L)

in incremental-poly time with respect to
the running times of the three oracles and the matroid rank,
and the remaining solutions are enumerated in
O(r)
delay (i.e., poly-delay).

Application to Binary Matroids (Sect.
4
).

We apply the minimum basis enumeration algorithm in Sect.
3

to binary matroids from cycle space, path space
and cut space,
to show that the time complexity of
the above algorithm is polynomially bounded by
the graph size and
\ell
.
We implement all the oracles by using previous results
as subroutines, except the relevant oracle for
the binary matroid from the cut space.
For this oracle,
we develop the first poly-delay algorithm
that enumerates all relevant cuts in an edge-weighted graph
in non-decreasing order of weight.
This is different from a poly-delay algorithm in
[
44
]
that
enumerates all cuts or
s,t
-cuts (not necessarily relevant)
in non-decreasing order, while
ours concentrates on relevant cuts.

Enumeration of Bases (Sect.
5
).

We propose a poly-delay algorithm
for enumerating all bases of a binary matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})

that satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption 1

For a binary matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
,
let
I\in{\mathcal{I}}
be any independent set such that
|I|\geq 1
.
For the corresponding vectors

\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2},\dots,\bm{u}_{k}\in\mathit{GF}(2)^{d}

in the elements of
I
, where
k=|I|
,
there is an element in
U

that corresponds to the vector

\bm{u}_{1}+\bm{u}_{2}+\dots+\bm{u}_{k}
.

By this assumption, we do not require
that
U
has an element corresponding to zero vector.
This means that elements in
U
do not necessarily form a subspace.
We show that the delay of the proposed algorithm
is polynomially bounded with respect to
the matroid rank
r
.
The algorithm immediately yields a poly-delay algorithm
(with respect to the graph size)
for enumerating all bases in binary matroids
from the cycle space and the cut space
of a given graph.

Lemma 2

Suppose that a matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})

with a weight function
w:U\to\real
is given.
For
I,O\subseteq U

(I\cap O=\emptyset)
,
let
P^{\ast},Q^{\ast}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O)
.
For any minimum element
y

Q^{\ast}-P^{\ast}

-preserving zero-exchange for

Oracles.

We assume that a matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})

with a weight function
w:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
is implicitly given by
IND-oracle, MinB-oracle and REL-oracle in
Sections
3
and
4

whereas only IND-oracle is used in Sect.
5
.

Independence oracle (IND-oracle):

To a query subset
S\subseteq U
of elements,
the oracle returns whether
S
is independent or not.

Minimum basis oracle (MinB-oracle):

The oracle returns a minimum basis.

Relevant oracle (REL-oracle):

Let us denote
the set of relevant elements by
U^{\ast}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{b}\}\subseteq U

so that
w(x_{1})\leq w(x_{2})\leq\dots\leq w(x_{b})
.
The oracle returns the
i
-th smallest relevant element
x_{i}
in the
i
-th call,
where we assume that the oracle is not called more than
b=|U^{\ast}|
times.

We denote the time complexities of these oracles by

\tau_{\mathrm{IND}}
,
\tau_{\mathrm{MinB}}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{REL}}
, respectively.
In some matroids, we may need to conduct preprocessing before the first call of the REL-oracle.
We denote by
\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{REL}}
the time complexity of the preprocessing.

Binary Matroids.

We denote by
\mathit{GF}(2)
the finite field of two elements, that is 0 and 1,
where the addition corresponds to XOR operation
and the multiplication corresponds to AND operation.
A
binary matroid
is a matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
such that
each element in
U
is associated with
a
d
-dimensional vector in
\mathit{GF}(2)
, where
d
is a positive integer,
and

I\subseteq U
is an independent set (i.e.,
I\in{\mathcal{I}}
)
if and only if
the vectors associated with elements in

are linearly independent in
\mathit{GF}(2)
.

3
An Incremental-Polynomial Algorithm for Enumerating All Minimum Bases

In this section, we present an
algorithm that enumerates all minimum bases of a matroid

{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
with a weight function
w:U\to\real
,
where
{\mathcal{M}}
is implicitly given
by IND-oracle, MinB-oracle and REL-oracle
and we denote by
r
the rank of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

In Sect.
3.1
, we describe how to design an incremental-poly algorithm
based on “saturation algorithm”
[
29
]
.
We show that, in Sect.
3.2
,
the time bound of the saturation algorithm can be improved
by using search tree.
Then in Sect.
3.3
,
we can use the search tree algorithm as a subroutine so that,
for some integer
L
,
the first
L
solutions can be enumerated in incremental-poly time
and that the remaining solutions can be enumerated in poly-delay.

3.1
Naïve Algorithm

For our problem,
we can employ a saturation algorithm
[
29
]

to design an incremental-poly algorithm.
First, we generate a minimum basis
B^{\ast}
by the MinB-oracle
and let
{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq\{B^{\ast}\}
.
Then we repeat the following
for each relevant element
y
that is generated by the REL-oracle;
for each minimum basis
B
in
{\mathcal{S}}
,
we generate
B^{\prime}\coloneqq(B-\{x\})\cup\{y\}
for all
x\in B
such that
(x,y)

is a zero-exchange.
If
B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}-{\mathcal{S}}
, then we add
B^{\prime}
to the solution set
(i.e.,
{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{S}}\cup\{B^{\prime}\}
).
A new minimum basis
B^{\prime}
will be obtained for each
y

since it is relevant.

Let us analyze the time complexity.
Let
\ell\coloneqq|{\mathcal{S}}|
.
To obtain the
(\ell+1)
-st minimum basis,
we call the IND-oracle
{\mathcal{O}}(\ell r)
times to identify whether
B^{\prime}
is independent.
If
B^{\prime}
is independent (i.e., a minimum basis),
we check whether it has already been generated,
where the check can be done in
{\mathcal{O}}(r)
time if we store
{\mathcal{S}}
by means of trie.
Thus the computation time to obtain the
(\ell+1)
-st solution is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\big((r+\ell)\tau_{\mathrm{REL}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(ir(\tau_{\mathrm{IND}}+r))\big)={\mathcal{O}}((r+\ell)\tau_{\mathrm{REL}}+\ell^{2}r\tau_{\mathrm{IND}}+\ell^{2}r^{2})

(1)

after
{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{\mathrm{MinB}}+\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{REL}})
time preprocessing.

The space complexity is not polynomially bounded
since it needs to store all solutions generated so far.
As mentioned in
[
6
]
,
it is a major issue to reduce the space complexity
of enumeration algorithms of this kind.

3.2
Search Tree Based Algorithm

We can reduce the term
{\mathcal{O}}(\ell^{2}r^{2})

in the time bound (
1
)
if we realize the algorithm by using search tree.
In the search tree algorithm,
we do not need to check explicitly
whether or not a minimum basis has been already generated.

The search tree represents a partition structure of
the set
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}
of minimum bases.
Each node corresponds to a subset of
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}

and has one minimum basis in the subset
as the representative.
For every minimum basis
B^{\ast}
,
there is at least one node that has
B^{\ast}
as the representative.
Traversing the search tree, we output the representatives
so that duplication does not occur, by which
the enumeration task is done.

To be more precise,
each node of the search tree is
associated with two disjoint subsets
I,O\subseteq U^{\ast}

of relevant elements and denoted by
\langle I,O\rangle
.
We call
I
(resp.,
O
) the
mandatory
(resp.,
forbidden
)
subset
of the node
\langle I,O\rangle
.
In particular, the root node
corresponds to
\langle\emptyset,\emptyset\rangle
.
For
\langle I,O\rangle
,
the corresponding subset of
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}
is
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O)
and
we choose a minimum basis in
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O)
as its representative
and denote it by
R^{\ast}_{I,O}
.

We branch a node in the search tree
based on the following lemma.

Lemma 4

Suppose that a matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
is given.
For disjoint subsets
I,O\subseteq U^{\ast}
of relevant elements,
let
B^{\ast}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O)
be a minimum basis and

x\in B^{\ast}-I
.

(i)

{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O)={\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I\cup\{x\},O)\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O\cup\{x\})
.

(ii)

Let
y\in U^{\ast}-(B^{\ast}\cup O)
.
If
(x,y)
is an
I,O
-preserving zero-exchange for
B^{\ast}
, then

B^{\ast}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I\cup\{x\},O)
and

(B^{\ast}-\{x\})\cup\{y\}\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O\cup\{x\})
hold.

Proof:

(i)
It is obvious that
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I\cup\{x\},O)
and
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O\cup\{x\})
are disjoint.
We have
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I\cup\{x\},O)\sqcup{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O\cup\{x\})\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}(I,O)

since

Lemma 5

Theorem 1

Suppose that a matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
with a weight function
w:U\to\real

is given. Algorithm
1
enumerates all minimum bases in
{\mathcal{M}}

in incremental polynomial time with respect to the rank
r
of
{\mathcal{M}}
and the oracle running times.

after
{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{\mathrm{MinB}}+\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{REL}})
-time preprocessing,
the
\ell
-th minimum basis is output
in
{\mathcal{O}}((r+\ell)\tau_{\mathrm{REL}}+\ell^{2}r\tau_{\mathrm{IND}})
time.

Proof:

(Correctness)

We claim that
{\mathcal{S}}_{h}={\mathcal{N}}_{h}
,
h=0,1,\dots,h_{\max}
holds
upon completion of the algorithm.
It is clearly true for
h=0
by line
5
.
Suppose that
{\mathcal{S}}_{h}={\mathcal{N}}_{h}
holds as the assumption of the induction.
For every node
\langle I,O\rangle\in{\mathcal{S}}_{h}
,
the eldest child
\langle I_{0},O_{0}\rangle
is added to
{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}

in line
18
,
and all other children are added to
{\mathcal{S}}_{h+1}
in

14
, showing the claim.

No minimum basis is output more than once
since a minimum basis

is output when and only when
it is the representative of a non-eldest child
of its parent;
in this case,
B^{\ast}\notin{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\cup{\mathcal{R}}_{1}\cup\dots\cup{\mathcal{R}}_{h-1}
holds and
B^{\ast}
is different from any other bases in
{\mathcal{R}}_{h}
.

We show by contradiction that all minimum bases are output.
Suppose that there is a minimum basis that is not output by the algorithm.
By Lemma

, there is a leaf
\langle I^{\clubsuit},O^{\clubsuit}\rangle

whose representative

is not output.
Then
\langle I^{\clubsuit},O^{\clubsuit}\rangle
is the eldest child of the parent,
say
\langle I^{\prime},O^{\prime}\rangle
.
The representative of
\langle I^{\prime},O^{\prime}\rangle
is the same as
\langle I^{\clubsuit},O^{\clubsuit}\rangle
(i.e.,
R^{\ast}_{I^{\prime},O^{\prime}}=R^{\ast}_{I^{\clubsuit},O^{\clubsuit}}
).
The representative

is not output and hence
\langle I^{\prime},O^{\prime}\rangle
is the eldest child of the parent.
We go upward to the root in this way.
However, the representative of the root is output in line
4
,
a contradiction.

(Complexity)

Let
h\in\{0,1,\dots,h_{\max}-1\}
.
By Lemma
5
, there exists a node
\langle I,O\rangle
in depth
h+1

whose representative
R^{\ast}_{I,O}
is output
when this node is visited by the algorithm.
Suppose that

is the
\ell
-th output.

•

The MinB-oracle is called once in line

to construct a minimum basis as

time.

•

To use the REL-oracle, we conduct preprocessing
in
{\mathcal{O}}(\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{REL}})
time.

•

We can determine
y_{h+1}
that is used
in the for-loop from line
8

to
19

in at most

calls of the REL-oracle.

•

The for-loop from line
8

to
19
is repeated
|{\mathcal{S}}_{h}|
times. In each iteration,
the most critical part is
determination of
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{b}\}
,
which can be done in
r
calls of the IND-oracle.

Finally,
\sum_{i=1}^{h}|\mathcal{S}_{i}|=\sum_{i=1}^{h}|\mathcal{R}_{i}|\leq h|\mathcal{R}_{h}|\leq h\ell
holds by Lemma

,
where

equals to the number of representatives

.
It holds that

by Lemma

After

\Box

is independent for

if and only if
(B^{\ast}-B^{\ast}_{j})\cup B_{j}

is independent for
{\mathcal{M}}
.

(REL-oracle)

The REL-oracle of
{\mathcal{M}}
lists relevant elements in
U^{\ast}

in the non-decreasing order
of weight.
We can use it as the REL-oracle of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\ast}_{j}

as long as we deal with
{\mathcal{M}}^{\ast}_{1},{\mathcal{M}}^{\ast}_{2},\dots,{\mathcal{M}}^{\ast}_{\beta}

in this order.

We have the following theorem immediately.

Theorem 2

Suppose that a matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
with a weight function
w:U\to\real

is given.
There is an integer
L
such that
we can generate the
\ell
-th minimum basis of
{\mathcal{M}}
in

{\mathcal{O}}((r+\ell)\tau_{\mathrm{REL}}+\ell^{2}r\tau_{\mathrm{IND}})
time for
\ell\leq L

and the remaining minimum bases in
{\mathcal{O}}(r)
delay
after
{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{\mathrm{MinB}}+\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{REL}})
-time preprocessing.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}_{j}
,
j\in[1,\beta]

denote the set of all bases of
matroid
{\mathcal{M}}^{\ast}_{j}
. Any integer no less than

1+\sum_{j=1}^{\beta}(|{\mathcal{B}}^{\ast}_{j}|-1)
suffices as
L
in the theorem.

4
Application to Binary Matroids with An Exponentially Large Ground Set

In the last section,
we have presented an incremental polynomial algorithm
to enumerate all minimum bases of a given
matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
with a weight function
w:U\to\real
.
The computation time to output the
\ell
-th
solution is bounded by a polynomial with respect to

\ell
, the rank
r
of
{\mathcal{M}}
and
the running times of the three oracles,
and does not depend on the cardinality
|U|
of
the ground set
U
.

In this section, we describe polynomial-time implementations
of the oracles for several binary matroids
that are well-known in the literature.
The matroids that we consider include
the binary matroids from the cycle space (Sect.
4.1
),
the path space (Sect.
4.2
),
and the cut space (Sect.
4.3
).
These matroids arise from a given graph
and have a ground set of an exponential size
with respect to the graph size.
Using our algorithm,
we can enumerate minimum bases of these matroids
efficiently.
A highlight of this section is
a poly-delay algorithm for enumerating
all relevant cuts in a given edge-weighted undirected graph
in non-decreasing order of weight,
which is used as the REL-oracle for a binary matroid
from the cut space. We explain this algorithm in Sect.
4.3
.

Throughout this section, we assume that
a graph
G
is connected and undirected.
We denote its vertex (resp., edge) set by
V(G)
(resp.,
E(G)
),
where we let
n=|V(G)|
and
m=|E(G)|
.
An undirected edge
\{s,t\}\in E(G)

is written as
st
(or
ts
) for simplicity.
Let
E(G)=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{m}\}
.
We represent a subset of edges by the incidence vector

\bm{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m})\in\mathit{GF}(2)^{m}
, that is,

u_{i}=1
(resp.,
0
) if
e_{i}
belongs (resp., does not belong) to the subset.

4.1
Cycle Space

In this subsection, we assume an edge-weight to be positive
(i.e.,
w:E(G)\to\real_{+}
).
A subset
F\subseteq E(G)
of edges
is called a
cycle
if all vertex degrees in
the spanning subgraph
(V(G),F)
are even.
The weight of a cycle
F
is the summation of the weight of edges in
F
(i.e.,
W(F)\coloneqq\sum_{e\in F}w(e)
).
The vector space over
\mathit{GF}(2)
spanned by the incidence vectors of all cycles in
G
is called
the
cycle space of
G
.
Let
\gamma
denote the number of connected components in
G
.
The number
\mu\coloneqq m-n+\gamma
is called the
cyclomatic number of
G
,
and the dimension of the cycle space is equal to
\mu

[
3
]
.

We say that cycles are independent if their incidence vectors are linearly independent in
\mathit{GF}(2)
.
Let
U
denote the set of all cycles and

{\mathcal{I}}
denote the family of all subsets of independent cycles in
U
.
We consider a matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
.
Obviously, the cardinality
|U|
of the ground set can be up to
an exponential number with respect to
n
and
m
.

Rank of the matroid.

The rank of this matroid is the cyclomatic number
\mu={\mathcal{O}}(m)
.

IND-oracle.

We can decide whether a set
I\subseteq U
of cycles
is independent or not by using Gaussian elimination on an
m\times|I|
matrix.
We can assume that
|I|={\mathcal{O}}(m)
when we run the IND-oracle in Algorithm
1
.
Then
\tau_{\mathrm{IND}}={\mathcal{O}}(m^{3})
holds.

MinB-oracle.

We can construct a minimum basis in this matroid
(i.e., minimum cycle basis)
in
{\mathcal{O}}(m^{3}n)
time by Horton’s algorithm
[
20
]
.
1
1

1

A faster algorithm is proposed in
[
32
]
,
but it does not improve the preprocessing time of the enumeration algorithm in Corollary
1
.

We can implement the oracles so
that
\tau_{\mathrm{MinB}}={\mathcal{O}}(m^{3}n)
holds.

REL-oracle.

Vismara
[
41
]
introduced a partition of the set
U^{\ast}
of relevant cycles,
say
U^{\ast}=U^{\ast}_{1}\sqcup U^{\ast}_{2}\sqcup\dots\sqcup U^{\ast}_{q}
,
such that all relevant cycles in
U^{\ast}_{p}
,

p=1,2,\dots,q
have the same weight and that
q={\mathcal{O}}(m^{2})
,
where there is a unique cycle
x^{\ast}_{p}\in U^{\ast}_{p}

for each
p
that is called the
prototype of
U^{\ast}_{p}
.
Vismara
[
41
]

presented two algorithms,
one for enumerating all
prototypes in
{\mathcal{O}}(\mu m^{3})
time
and the other for enumerating all relevant cycles
in
U^{\ast}_{p}
in
{\mathcal{O}}(n|U^{\ast}_{p}|)
time.
To be more precise,
the latter algorithm attains
{\mathcal{O}}(n)
delay.

We can scan all relevant cycles in the
non-decreasing order of weight as follows;
first, we enumerate all prototypes by the first algorithm
and sort them in the non-decreasing order
with respect to weight. This can be done in
{\mathcal{O}}(\mu m^{3}+m^{2}\log m)={\mathcal{O}}(\mu m^{3})
time.
Let

w(x^{\ast}_{1})\leq w(x^{\ast}_{2})\leq\dots\leq w(x^{\ast}_{q})

without loss of generality.
Next, for each
p=1,2,\dots,q
,
we enumerate all relevant cycles in
U^{\ast}_{p}

by the second algorithm.
This can be done in
{\mathcal{O}}(n)
delay.
To realize the REL-oracle, we use this enumeration algorithm as a co-routine.
We have that
\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{REL}}={\mathcal{O}}(\mu m^{3})
and
\tau_{\mathrm{REL}}={\mathcal{O}}(n)
.

By Theorem
2
, we have the following corollary immediately.

Corollary 1

Given a connected graph
G
with
an edge-weight function
w:E(G)\to\real_{+}
,
there is an integer
L
such that
we can generate the
\ell
-th minimum cycle basis
(\ell\leq L)
in

{\mathcal{O}}(\ell^{2}m^{4})
time,
where
m=|E(G)|
,
and the remaining ones in
{\mathcal{O}}(m)
delay
after
{\mathcal{O}}(m^{4})
-time preprocessing.

4.2
Path Space

In this subsection, we assume an edge-weight to be positive
(i.e.,
w:E(G)\to\real_{+}
).
The concept of the path space is introduced by Hartvigsen
[
18
]
.
Let
P\subseteq V(G)
(
|P|\geq 2
) be a subset of vertices.
For two vertices
u,v\in P
, we call a
u,v
-path a
P
-path
.
The weight of a
P
-path

is the summation of the weight of edges in
F
.
The vector space over
\mathit{GF}(2)
spanned by the incidence vectors of
all cycles and
P

P
-space
.
2
2

2

The space is called the
U
-space in the literature. To avoid confusion, we call it the
P
-space since we use
U
to represent the ground set of a matroid in this paper.

A set of cycles and
P

if their incidence vectors are linearly independent in
\mathit{GF}(2)
.
The set is
called a
P
-basis
if it is a basis of the
P
-space.

Let
U
denote the set of all cycles and
P
-paths of
G

and
\mathcal{I}
denote the family of all independent sets.
We consider a matroid
\mathcal{M}=(U,\mathcal{I})
.
Obviously,
the cardinality
|U|
of the ground set can be exponential for
n
and

.

Rank of the matroid.

The rank of this matroid is
\mu+|P|-1=m-n+|P|={\mathcal{O}}(m)

when
G
is connected
[
19
]
.

IND-oracle.

We can decide whether a set
I\subseteq U
of cycles and
P
-paths is independent or not in the same way as the cycle space.
We can also assume that
|I|={\mathcal{O}}(m)
when we run the IND-oracle in Algorithm
1
, and hence
\tau_{\mathrm{IND}}={\mathcal{O}}(m^{3})
holds.

MinB-oracle.

We can construct a minimum basis in this matroid in
{\mathcal{O}}(n^{2}m^{3})
time by using Hartvigsen’s algorithm
[
18
]
.
We can use this algorithm as the MinB-oracle, and
\tau_{\mathrm{MinB}}={\mathcal{O}}(n^{2}m^{3})

REL-oracle.

Gleiss et al.
[
14
]
generalized Vismara’s algorithm
[
41
]
for

-space.
The algorithm transforms the
P
-space of the input graph
G
into the cycle space of an extended graph
G^{\prime}

such that
V(G^{\prime})=V(G)\cup\{z\}

and
E(G^{\prime})=E(G)\cup\bigcup_{u\in P}\{uz\}
.
Then the algorithm enumerates all prototypes in
G^{\prime}
by applying Vismara’s first algorithm, and convert them into corresponding cycles or

-paths of
G
.
For each converted prototype, the algorithm enumerates all relevant elements in the subset of

to which the prototype belongs.
This algorithm is equivalent to enumerating all relevant cycles in

by Vismara’s algorithms
and converting them into corresponding cycles or
P
-paths of
G
.
Thus, we can construct the REL-oracle in the same strategy as the cycle space,
and hence
\tau^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{REL}}={\mathcal{O}}((m-n+|P|)m^{3})={{\mathcal{O}}(m^{4})}
and
\tau_{\mathrm{REL}}={\mathcal{O}}(n)
hold.

Corollary 2

Given a connected graph
G
with
an edge-weight function
w:E(G)\to\real_{+}
and
a subset
P\subseteq V(G)
of vertices,
there is an integer
L
such that
we can generate the
\ell
-th minimum

-bases in

{\mathcal{O}}(m^{4}\ell^{2})
time for
\ell\leq L

and the remaining minimum
P
-bases in
{\mathcal{O}}(m)
delay
after
{\mathcal{O}}(m^{3}n^{2})
-time preprocessing, where

and
m=|E(G)|
.

4.3
Cut Space

In this subsection, we assume an edge-weight to be nonnegative
(i.e.,
w:E(G)\to\real_{\geq 0}
).
Recall that we assume
G
to be connected.
A subset
F\subseteq E(G)
of edges is a
cut
(
of
G
)
if

G-F
is disconnected.
Equivalently,

F
is a cut if there is a vertex subset
W\subseteq V(G)

such that
F=E(W;G)\coloneqq\{st\in E(G)\mid s\in W,\ t\in V(G)-W\}
.
The weight of a cut
F
is the total weight of edges in
F
.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}
denote the set of all cuts in
G
.
Let
F\in{\mathcal{C}}_{G}
be a cut that satisfies
F=E(W;G)
for some
W\subseteq V
.
For two distinct vertices
s,t\in V
,
we call
F
an
s,t
-cut

if
s\in W
and
t\notin W
.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(s,t)
denote the set of all
s,t
-cuts
and
\lambda(s,t;G)
denote the weight of minimum
s,t
-cut
of
G
.
For vertex subsets
S,T\subseteq V
,
let
\lambda_{\max}(S,T;G)\coloneqq\max_{a\in S,~b\in T:~a\neq b}\lambda(a,b;G)
.
For simplicity, we write
\lambda_{\max}(V,V;G)

as
\lambda_{\max}(G)
.

The vector space over
\mathit{GF}(2)
spanned by
the incidence vectors of all cuts in
G

is called the
cut space of
G
.
We say that cuts are independent if their incidence vectors
are linearly independent in
\mathit{GF}(2)
.
We consider a matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})
such that
the ground set
U={\mathcal{C}}_{G}
is the set of all cuts in
G

and
{\mathcal{I}}
is the family of all independent cuts in
U
.
The cardinality
|U|
of the ground set can be up to
an exponential number with respect to
n
and
m
.

Rank of the matroid.

It is known that the rank of this matroid
is
n-\gamma

[
15
]
.

IND-oracle.

Similarly to previous matroids,
we can implement the IND-oracle by using Gaussian elimination
so that
\tau_{\mathrm{IND}}={\mathcal{O}}(m^{3})
holds.

MinB-oracle.

A minimum basis in the matroid is called a
minimum cut basis
.
We can construct a minimum cut basis
from a Gomory-Hu tree
[
16
]
of
G
;
the set of
n-1
edges in a Gomory-Hu tree
corresponds to a minimum cut basis
[
15
]
.
We can construct a Gomory-Hu tree
in
{\mathcal{O}}(n\varphi)
time,
where
\varphi
denotes the
time to compute a minimum
s,t
-cut in an edge-weighted undirected
graph
G

for arbitrarily chosen vertices
s,t\in V(G)

[
16
]
;
e.g.,
\varphi={\mathcal{O}}(mn)

[
24
,
33
]
.
Then we can implement the MinB-oracle so that
\tau_{\mathrm{MinB}}={\mathcal{O}}(n\varphi)
.

REL-oracle.

We describe how to list all relevant cuts in
G

in non-decreasing order of weight.
This is different from the problems addressed in Yeh et al.
[
44
]
.
They studied the problem of enumerating
all cuts or
s,t
-cuts for specified vertices
s,t

in non-decreasing order of weight
and did not take the notion of relevant cuts into account.

Recall that a cut is relevant if it belongs to
at least one minimum cut basis.
Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{G}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{G}

denote the set of all relevant cuts of
G
.
A necessary and sufficient condition
that a cut is relevant is shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 7
(
[
10
]
)

For an edge-weighted graph
G
,
a cut
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{G}
is relevant if and only if there exists a pair of distinct vertices
s,t\in V(G)
such that
C
is a minimum
s,t
-cut.

Let us overview our algorithm.
We build graphs
G_{0}\coloneqq G,G_{1},\dots,G_{\tau}
for some
integer
\tau
iteratively,
where
G_{i+1}
is obtained by contracting two certain vertices
s_{i},t_{i}
in
G_{i}
.
We will show that
relevant cuts of
G
appear as minimum
s_{i},t_{i}
-cuts of
G_{i}

and that the weights of minimum cuts are in non-increasing order
over
G_{0},G_{1},\dots,G_{\tau}
.
We can achieve the goal by enumerating minimum
s_{i},t_{i}
-cuts of
G_{i}

for
i=\tau,\tau-1,\dots,0
, where we employ
Yeh et al.’s algorithm
[
44
]

to enumerate minimum
s_{i},t_{i}
-cuts of
G_{i}
.

For
X\subseteq V(G)
,
let
G/X
denote
the multigraph that is obtained
by contracting all vertices in
X
into a single vertex,
where we denote by the lowercase (i.e.,
x
) the new vertex.
Multiedges may appear, and we do not take self-loops into account.
Formally,
V(G/X)
and
E(G/X)
are defined as follows;

\displaystyle V(G/X)=(V(G)-X)\cup\{x\},

\displaystyle E(G/X)=\{uv\in E(G)\mid u,v\notin X\}\cup\left(\bigcup_{uv\in E(G):~u\notin X,~v\in X}\{ux\}\right).

For
v\in V(G)
, we regard
G/\{v\}=G
.
We inherit the edge-weight function on
G/X
from
G

and hence regard
w
as the edge-weight function on
G/X
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}=\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{k}\}
be a partition of
V
.
We denote by
G/{\mathcal{X}}

the multigraph that is obtained by contracting all vertices in
X_{i}\in{\mathcal{X}}

into a single vertex
x_{i}
for each
i=1,2,\dots,k
, respectively.
Let
G^{\prime}=G/{\mathcal{X}}
.
For
1\leq i<j\leq k
,
every
x_{i},x_{j}
-cut of
G^{\prime}
is an
s,t
-cut of
G

for all
s\in X_{i}
and
t\in X_{j}

and hence we have
\lambda(x_{i},x_{j};G^{\prime})\geq\lambda_{\max}(X_{i},X_{j};G)
.

Let
s,t\in V(G)
.
The set
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}
of all cuts is partitioned
into two disjoint subsets,
one for the set of
s,t
-cuts
and the other for the set of any other cuts.
The latter subset is equal to the set
{\mathcal{C}}_{G/\{s,t\}}

of all cuts in the contracted graph
G/\{s,t\}
, as shown in the following lemma.

Lemma 8

For a graph
G
, let
s,t\in V(G)
be two distinct vertices.
Then it holds that
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}={\mathcal{C}}_{G}(s,t)\sqcup{\mathcal{C}}_{G/\{s,t\}}
.

Proof:

Any
s,t
-cut of
G
is not a cut in
G/\{s,t\}
,
and thus it holds that
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(s,t)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G/\{s,t\}}=\emptyset
.
Let
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{G}
be a cut of
G
such that
C=E(W;G)

for
W\subseteq V(G)
.
If
C
is an
s,t
-cut, then
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(s,t)
holds.
Otherwise, we assume that
s,t\in V(G)-W
without loss of generality since
E(W;G)=E(V(G)-W;G)
.
Then
E(W;G)=E(W;G/\{s,t\})
holds, and hence
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{G/\{s,t\}}
.
We have seen that
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(s,t)\sqcup{\mathcal{C}}_{G/\{s,t\}}
holds.
It is obvious that

{\mathcal{C}}_{G}\supseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(s,t)\sqcup{\mathcal{C}}_{G/\{s,t\}}
holds.

\Box

For
s,t\in V(G)
, let
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{G}(s,t)
denote the set of all minimum
s,t
-cuts.
By Lemma
7
, any cut in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{G}(s,t)
is relevant (i.e.,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{G}(s,t)\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{G}
).
For a partition
{\mathcal{X}}=\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{k}\}
of
V(G)
,
we define

\Lambda({\mathcal{X}})\coloneqq\{(i,j)\mid 1\leq i<j\leq k,\ \lambda(x_{i},x_{j};G/{\mathcal{X}})=\lambda_{\max}(X_{i},X_{j};G)\}.

Lemma 9

For an edge-weighted graph
G
,
let
{\mathcal{X}}=\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{k}\}
be a partition of
V(G)
.
If
\Lambda({\mathcal{X}})=\emptyset
, then
no cut of
G/{\mathcal{X}}
is relevant for
G
.
Otherwise, for any
(i,j)\in\Lambda({\mathcal{X}})
,
all minimum
x_{i},x_{j}
-cuts in
G/{\mathcal{X}}

are relevant for
G
.

Proof:

For the former, we show that the contraposition is true.
Let
C\subseteq E(G/{\mathcal{X}})
be a cut of
G/{\mathcal{X}}

that is relevant for
G
.
By Lemma
7
,
there are
s,t\in V(G)
such that
C
is a minimum
s,t
-cut in
G
.
Let
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
denote the subsets
that contain
s
and
t
, respectively.
We see
i\neq j
since
C
separates
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
in
G/{\mathcal{X}}
.
Then we have

w(C)\geq\lambda(x_{i},x_{j};G/{\mathcal{X}})\geq\lambda_{\max}(X_{i},X_{j};G)\geq\lambda(s,t;G),

where all the inequalities hold by equalities by
w(C)=\lambda(s,t;G)
,
showing that
(i,j)\in\Lambda({\mathcal{X}})
.

For the latter,
there exist two vertices
s\in X_{i}
and
t\in X_{j}
such that
\lambda(x_{i},x_{j};G/{\mathcal{X}})=\lambda_{\max}(X_{i},X_{j};G)=\lambda(s,t;G)
.
Any minimum
x_{i},x_{j}
-cut
C
of
G/{\mathcal{X}}
is
an
s,t
-cut of
G
that satisfies
w(C)=\lambda(x_{i},x_{j};G/{\mathcal{X}})=\lambda(s,t;G)
,
and thus is relevant for
G
by Lemma
7
.

\Box

Let us call a cut
C
a
solution
if
C\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{G}
.
We outline our algorithm to enumerate all solutions in non-decreasing order of weight.
Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\}
.
Initializing

{\mathcal{X}}\coloneqq\{\{v_{1}\},\{v_{2}\},\dots,\{v_{n}\}\}
,
we execute the following:

1.

If
\Lambda({\mathcal{X}})\neq\emptyset
,
then let
(i,j)\coloneqq\arg\max_{(x,y)\in\Lambda({\mathcal{X}})}\{\lambda(x,y;G/{\mathcal{X}})\}
.
All minimum
x_{i},x_{j}
-cuts in
G/{\mathcal{X}}
are solutions by
the latter half of Lemma
9
.
Before enumerating them,
we make a recursive call for the partition that is obtained
by merging
X_{i}
and
X_{j}
(i.e.,
({\mathcal{X}}-\{X_{i},X_{j}\})\cup\{X_{i}\cup X_{j}\}
)
to enumerate solutions that have no more weights than minimum
x_{i},x_{j}
-cuts.
After the recursive call is complete, we enumerate
all minimum
x_{i},x_{j}
-cuts in
G/{\mathcal{X}}
as solutions, using
the algorithm in
[
44
]
.

2.

Otherwise (i.e., if
\Lambda({\mathcal{X}})=\emptyset
),
we do nothing since no cut of
G/{\mathcal{X}}
is a solution by the
former half of Lemma
9
.

Although all outputs by the above procedure are solutions,
it is not guaranteed that they are all solutions.
We utilize the following two lemmas to prove it.

Lemma 10

For an edge-weighted graph
G
,
let
{\mathcal{X}}=\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{k}\}
be a partition of
V(G)
.
For a cut
C
of
G/{\mathcal{X}}
,
it holds that
C\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{G}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\ast}_{G/{\mathcal{X}}}

if and only if there is
(i,j)\in\Lambda({\mathcal{X}})
such that

C
is a minimum
x_{i},x_{j}
-cut in
G/{\mathcal{X}}
.

Proof:

For the sufficiency,

5
A Polynomial-Delay Algorithm for Enumerating All Bases

In this section,
we propose a poly-delay algorithm
for enumerating all bases of a binary matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(U,{\mathcal{I}})

that satisfies Assumption
1
.
For convenience, we may represent a vector (resp., matrix) over
\mathit{GF}(2)

by a binary vector (resp., matrix) throughout the section.
Such notions as
linear independence of binary vectors
and the determinant of a binary matrix are also considered over
\mathit{GF}(2)
.
We assume only IND-oracle,
which can be realized by any algorithm that computes a matrix determinant
over
\mathit{GF}(2)
.
Note that no other oracle is assumed like previous sections.

The algorithm is based on Avis and Fukuda’s well-known

reverse search

[
2
]
.
We show that the delay of the algorithm is

{\mathcal{O}}(r^{3}+r^{2}\tau_{\mathrm{DET}}(r))
, where

r
denotes the rank of
{\mathcal{M}}
and

\tau_{\mathrm{DET}}(r)
denotes the computation time
for obtaining the determinant of a given
r\times r
binary matrix.
Again, the polynomial does not depend on the ground set size
|U|
.
The algorithm immediately yields
poly-delay enumeration of bases in binary matroids
from cycle space and cut space.

Let us remark that the vector space
has already been used in enumeration research.
For example, in
[
37
,
30
]
,
enumeration of cycles in graphs is studied
under the cycle space.

In a binary matroid

A basis of

Let

Concluding Remarks

In this paper,
we have studied problems of enumerating
bases in a matroid with an exponentially large ground set.
Examples of such a matroid includes
binary matroids from cycle space, path space and cut space.
The first result is an incremental polynomial algorithm
that enumerates all minimum-weight bases.
We have shown that it is applicable to
the above binary matroids so that the time complexity
is polynomially bounded by the graph size.
The second result is a poly-delay algorithm
that enumerates all bases, which
immediately yields poly-delay (with respect to graph size)
enumeration of bases in binary matroids from cycle space and cut space.

For future work,
it would be interesting to explore
whether or not it is possible to enumerate all minimum bases
in poly-delay in our context (i.e.,
the bounding polynomial does not contain the ground set size)
and/or in poly-space.
Among the remaining issues are to study the
k
-best version,
to improve the complexities, to look for other applications, and so on.

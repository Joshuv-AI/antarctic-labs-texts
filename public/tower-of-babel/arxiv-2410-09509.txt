challenging, let alone infinitely many. Moreover, the existence of resonant eigenvalues results in the periodic case is extremely hard than the special case

First of all, in each band, for every fixed
k\in(0,\pi),k\neq\frac{\pi}{2}
, there are two eigenvalues
E_{1}
and

k(E_{1})+k(E_{2})=\pi
will induce resonance. Namely, when one of the eigensolutions of (

Therefore, the first difficulty to overcome is how to construct a “satisfactory” perturbation to create such kind of couples of resonant embedded eigenvalues from the same band.
What is more difficult is that the eigenvalues that can create resonances with some

are not just

from the same band but from infinitely many bands. In fact, suppose

.
To avoid these difficulties, the previous papers always require a non-resonance assumption of the eigenvalues.
For example, in
[

, the non-resonance condition is addressed in their Lemma 13 expressed as a condition on Fourier coefficients.
The authors in

Inspired by
[

,

,

Once we take the eigenvalue

.
This ensures that we are able to deal with some cases of resonances. Our main results are Theorems

and
2.5
. In our paper, the embedded eigenvalues are allowed to be resonant in the following two cases:
I
. The resonant eigenvalues appear in the same band.
II
. The resonant eigenvalues appear in many different (separate enough) bands (see formula (

)). We emphasize that even though there is no eigenvalues creating resonance with

for

from the same band, the previous papers do not permit the existence of the embedded eigenvalue
E

The definition of the perturbation is based on solving a class of differential equations involved with Prüfer angles

which in turn depend on

]
and widely used in later papers

]

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some asymptotics of eigenvalues and solutions of Sturm-Liouville equations and introduce main results. In Section 3, we first recall the generalized Prüfer transformation, and then some important estimates are obtained. In Section 4, we introduce a central quantitative estimate of an oscillatory integral coupled with a periodic parameter. In Section 5, we prove some resonant integral estimates. In Section 6, we introduce the new construction and prove the main theorems.

For any

Since

4.1
)

\displaystyle\pi-\frac{2\pi C}{t_{i}^{\beta}}\leq t_{i+1}-t_{i}\leq\pi+\frac{2\pi C}{t_{i}^{\beta}},

(7.1)

and

\displaystyle t_{i}\geq t_{0}+i\frac{\pi}{2}.

(7.2)

For any

, by (
4.1
) and (

), we can obtain

\displaystyle 2\pi i_{0}+i\pi+t-t_{i}-\frac{2\pi C}{t_{i}^{\beta}}\leq\theta(t)\leq 2\pi i_{0}+i\pi+t-t_{i}+\frac{2\pi C}{t_{i}^{\beta}}.

\displaystyle\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{\sin\theta(t)}{t}dt=\int_{x_{0}}^{t_{0}}\frac{\sin\theta(t)}{t}dt+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\frac{\sin\theta(t)}{t}dt+\int_{t_{N}}^{x}\frac{\sin\theta(t)}{t}dt,

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{x_{0}}+2\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{t_{i}}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{10\pi C}{t_{i}^{1+\beta}}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{4\pi}{t_{i}^{2}}.

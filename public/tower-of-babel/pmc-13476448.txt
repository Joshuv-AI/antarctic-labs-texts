SFPathFormer for spatial frequency robot navigation and obstacle avoidance
Authors: Zhenghao Hu, Chenxuan Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn complex and dynamic environments, robot path planning and obstacle avoidance often suffer from significant perception noise, severe feature redundancy, and insufficient global environment structure modeling. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a vision-driven robot navigation and obstacle avoidance framework that integrates spatial-frequency perception, dynamic feature selection, and global environment modeling. The proposed framework aims to enhance path safety and planning stability in complex scenarios by constructing a unified modeling pipeline from environmental perception and feature representation to path decision-making. First, at the methodological level, we design a spatial-frequency joint perception module based on parameterized wavelet downsampling. By introducing a global spatial-frequency attention mechanism, the proposed module effectively reduces feature redundancy and computational complexity while enhancing the salient representation of obstacle regions. Second, we propose a dynamic domain feature selection mechanism for path decision-making, where contrastive learning is employed to guide the model to adaptively select feature subspaces that are highly correlated with navigational safety, thereby improving generalization and robustness under diverse environmental conditions. Furthermore, a vision Transformer is introduced to model the global structural relationships of the environment, capturing long-range obstacle correlations and the overall spatial layout of traversable regions, thus providing consistent and structured high-level representations for path planning. Extensive experiments are conducted on multiple typical robot obstacle avoidance scenarios and complex environmental datasets. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method significantly outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in terms of path safety, obstacle avoidance success rate, and planning stability, while exhibiting stronger robustness under complex backgrounds and dynamic disturbances. Additional analysis further validates the synergistic effects of spatial-frequency perception, dynamic feature selection, and global modeling, proving that the proposed framework effectively improves robot path planning and obstacle avoidance performance in complex environments. This work provides a scalable and effective solution for vision-driven autonomous robot navigation.Keywords: Robot navigation, Obstacle avoidance, Spatial-frequency perception, Vision transformer, Dynamic feature selectionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the widespread deployment of intelligent robots in industrial inspection, warehouse logistics, public services, and autonomous operations in complex environments, achieving safe and stable path planning and obstacle avoidance in unknown or semi-structured environments has become one of the core challenges in autonomous navigation research1. In real-world applications, robots often face severe illumination variations, complex environmental structures, diverse obstacle shapes and scales, as well as frequent dynamic disturbances2. The proposed SFPathFormer is envisioned as a scalable framework capable of bridging the gap between specific dataset performance and general-purpose autonomous navigation. By providing a unified pipeline that optimizes the fundamental stages of robotic perception, this work lays the foundation for a versatile navigation solution that can be adapted to various sensor suites and environmental complexities with consistent performance3.

The practical necessity for this unified framework is most evident in unstructured real-world scenarios, such as autonomous industrial inspection and disaster response4. In these environments, robot platforms frequently encounter a combination of sensor degradation, such as lens glare or motion blur, and complex spatial layouts like labyrinthine corridors5. A simpler ensemble of independent modules fails to capture the intricate inter dependencies between signal-level denoising and high-level structural understanding. By simultaneously optimizing PWD, DFS, and ViT, the SFPathFormer ensures that the internal representation is globally consistent and robust to the local disturbances common in high-stakes robotic operations.

These factors pose stringent requirements on the reliability of vision-based perception and path decision-making systems6. Traditional geometric methods7 or rule-based obstacle avoidance strategies8 can achieve high efficiency under ideal conditions; however, they are highly susceptible to perception noise and local information loss in complex environments, making it difficult to achieve robust and generalizable path planning9. In recent years, deep learning approaches have demonstrated remarkable progress in robot visual perception and navigation tasks10,11. Nevertheless, how to simultaneously ensure computational efficiency while addressing multi-scale obstacle perception, effective feature selection, and global environment structure understanding remains an open and critical problem12,13.

Most existing deep learning-based path planning and obstacle avoidance methods rely on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for local feature extraction14,15. While CNN-based approaches perform well in obstacle detection and short-range avoidance, they exhibit several limitations in complex scenarios. First, their ability to perceive multi-scale obstacles and weakly salient targets is limited, making them vulnerable to background textures and noise16,17. Second, perceptual feature representations often suffer from severe redundancy, and features that are truly relevant to path decision-making under different environmental conditions are difficult to distinguish, which negatively affects planning stability18,19. Third, CNN-based methods lack effective modeling of global traversability structures and long-range spatial dependencies, leading to inconsistent and unreliable path decisions in complex corridors, occluded environments, or dynamic obstacle scenarios20. These issues significantly restrict the practical applicability of existing path avoidance methods in real-world robotic navigation tasks.

Motivated by these challenges, we argue that relying solely on single-domain spatial features or local perception mechanisms is insufficient for robust path avoidance in complex environments. On the one hand, joint spatial–frequency modeling can enhance structural information of obstacles across multiple scales while suppressing irrelevant noise21. On the other hand, introducing dynamic feature selection mechanisms allows the model to focus on perceptual dimensions that are highly correlated with path safety, thereby reducing the interference of redundant information in decision-making. Meanwhile, Transformer architectures with global modeling capabilities provide new opportunities to capture the overall relationships between obstacle distributions and traversable regions in the environment. Therefore, integrating spatial–frequency perception, feature selection, and global structural modeling into a unified visual representation framework for path avoidance constitutes a promising research direction for improving autonomous robot navigation. Accordingly, the objective of this work is to propose a vision-driven robot path planning and obstacle avoidance method that integrates spatial–frequency perception, dynamic domain feature selection, and global modeling into an end-to-end optimized framework. By enhancing the salient representation of obstacle regions in complex environments, dynamically selecting features that are highly relevant to path decision-making, and globally modeling the overall traversability structure, the proposed method aims to significantly improve the safety, robustness, and generalization capability of robot path planning in complex and dynamic scenarios. This work provides a reliable and scalable solution for autonomous navigation in real-world intelligent robotic systems.

The synergistic necessity of the proposed SFPathFormer lies in its multi-layered approach to refining high-dimensional visual data. While Parametric Wavelet Downsampling (PWD) functions as a signal-level denoiser by preserving structural integrity during resolution reduction, the Domain-related Feature Selection (DFS) mechanism serves as a task-oriented semantic filter that adaptively prioritizes safety-critical features based on environmental context. Finally, the Vision Transformer (ViT) provides the essential spatial-logical integration that local perception mechanisms lack, enabling a holistic understanding of traversability. These three modules are not redundant but complementary: they sequentially address signal noise, channel-wise redundancy, and spatial dependency, forming a robust perception-to-decision pipeline.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

1. To address the challenges of multi-scale obstacle variation and severe local noise interference in complex environments, we introduce a multi-scale perception module for path avoidance based on PWD and a Global Spatial–Frequency Attention Network (GSFANet). This module explicitly models the joint relationship between spatial and frequency information during the downsampling process, effectively reducing computational complexity while preserving discriminative environmental structures and potential obstacle cues. Through global spatial–frequency attention, the model adaptively enhances salient regions that are highly correlated with path safety, thereby improving obstacle perception robustness under weak textures, complex backgrounds, and dynamic conditions.

2. To mitigate feature redundancy and inconsistency in decision relevance across different scenarios, we propose a path perception modeling approach based on DFS. Inspired by unified feature selection frameworks, a contrastive learning mechanism is incorporated into path safety state modeling to guide the network toward automatically selecting feature subspaces that are highly relevant to traversability and safety. This mechanism dynamically suppresses perceptual dimensions with limited contribution to obstacle avoidance decisions, enhancing feature discriminability and generalization, and improving planning stability and reliability in complex environments.

3. To overcome the limitations of conventional convolutional networks in modeling long-range dependencies and global traversability structures, we introduce ViT as a high-level representation learning module for path avoidance. Leveraging self-attention mechanisms, ViT captures global relationships among obstacles and spatial topological structures, enabling a holistic understanding of traversable regions and potential risk areas. This global path perception capability effectively complements local perception methods and provides more consistent and structured environment representations for downstream path planning and obstacle avoidance.

Section IV describes the experimental setup and results, including comparative evaluations, ablation studies, and visualization analyses. Related work

Unlike conventional channel attention mechanisms that primarily model static inter-channel dependencies, our proposed DFS introduces a dynamic responsiveness to environmental variations. While existing adaptive weighting methods often rely on global average pooling to reweight features, they frequently fail to suppress redundant information in cross-domain navigation tasks where the relevance of specific features may shift. The novelty of our DFS lies in the integration of a contrastive learning-based domain consistency loss. This mechanism explicitly supervises the feature selection process by maximizing the distance between safe and unsafe navigational states in the feature space, thereby ensuring that the selected representation is uniquely optimized for robust path planning under diverse environmental disturbances.

Robot path planning and obstacle avoidance have long been regarded as core research topics in autonomous navigation, attracting extensive attention from the fields of robotics, computer vision, and machine learning22,23. Early studies primarily relied on geometric modeling and rule-based reasoning, where environment maps were constructed and combined with heuristic search or optimization strategies to achieve path planning and obstacle avoidance. Such methods exhibit good interpretability and real-time performance in structured environments24. However, in complex, dynamic, or perception-noisy scenarios, their performance heavily depends on sensor accuracy and prior knowledge, making them difficult to cope with diverse obstacle geometries, complex environmental structures, and high uncertainty.

With the rapid development of deep learning, data-driven visual perception and path planning methods have gradually become a research hotspot, providing new solutions for robot obstacle avoidance in complex environments. Deep learning-based path avoidance approaches can generally be categorized into perception-driven methods and end-to-end learning methods25. Perception-driven approaches typically employ CNNs to detect obstacles or perform semantic segmentation, and then feed the perception results into traditional planning modules for path generation. These methods achieve high obstacle localization accuracy but suffer from weak coupling between perception and decision-making26. In contrast, end-to-end learning methods directly learn obstacle avoidance policies from raw sensor inputs, often using reinforcement learning or imitation learning to map perception to actions. Although such approaches demonstrate strong adaptability in specific tasks, they frequently encounter issues such as unstable training, limited generalization, and lack of interpretability in decision processes27. In recent years, several studies have attempted to enhance environmental modeling by introducing multi-scale feature extraction, attention mechanisms, and Transformer architectures. Among them, Vision Transformers have gradually been applied to navigation and path planning tasks due to their advantages in modeling global dependencies, enabling the capture of long-range spatial relationships and holistic environmental structures. Ning et al.28 proposed a two-stage robot imitation learning framework termed TS-RIL, which decouples trajectory learning from obstacle avoidance by combining region-constrained dynamic movement primitives with an improved RRT* algorithm for trajectory generalization and local obstacle avoidance. This method features a clear structure and strong interpretability, and effectively improves trajectory learning stability and success rates in complex real-world scenarios, particularly for demonstration-driven manipulation tasks.

However, it heavily relies on manually designed potential fields and sampling-based planning strategies, making it sensitive to perception quality and parameter tuning. Moreover, its ability to leverage high-dimensional visual inputs for global environment modeling remains limited, restricting its generalization capability in unstructured environments. To address these limitations, subsequent studies have increasingly focused on improving global path planning and environment structure modeling. In this context, Wu et al.29 proposed a hierarchical path planning method that incorporates three-dimensional spatiotemporal characteristics. By improving Jump Point Search for efficient global path generation and introducing a 3D spatiotemporal grid at the local level, their approach effectively handles dynamic obstacles and multi-robot coordination. This method demonstrates strong performance in planning efficiency, path smoothness, and multi-robot cooperation, making it suitable for real-time navigation in structured environments. Nevertheless, it remains grounded in explicit environment modeling and rule-based search, with limited consideration of perception uncertainty and feature redundancy, which restricts its effectiveness in perception-driven autonomous obstacle avoidance tasks under complex visual noise or weakly salient obstacle conditions. To compensate for the limited trajectory generalization capability of rule-based planners, some studies have further incorporated optimization and control theories. Li et al.30 proposed an obstacle avoidance method that combines optimized dynamic movement primitives with model predictive control. By jointly modeling soft and hard constraints, their approach achieves unified optimization of trajectory reproduction and obstacle avoidance, while terminal sliding mode control is employed to ensure trajectory tracking accuracy. This method offers notable advantages in control precision and theoretical convergence guarantees, and can preserve the original shape of demonstrated trajectories in the presence of obstacles. However, it assumes low-dimensional state spaces and accurate system models, makes limited use of high-dimensional perception information, and incurs relatively high computational costs, which hampers its applicability in real-time decision-making under complex dynamic environments. These limitations have motivated researchers to explore obstacle avoidance from the perspective of multi-agent systems and cooperative control. Chang et al.31 proposed a fast finite-time double-group formation control method for multi-robot systems, considering time delays and nonlinear dynamics, and employed artificial potential fields for obstacle avoidance. This work provides comprehensive theoretical analysis, accounting for practical factors such as actuator saturation and faults, and offers valuable insights into multi-robot coordination and safety control. However, obstacle avoidance is treated as an auxiliary component within the control strategy, relying on artificial potential fields for local adjustments, and lacking the capability to perceive and learn the global structure of complex environments.

As a result, it is difficult to extend to highly unstructured or vision-dominated navigation scenarios. Along this line, Sun et al.32 proposed a collision avoidance approach based on deep reinforcement learning, where multi-threaded asynchronous Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) combined with multi-sensor fusion perception is used to achieve autonomous navigation in complex dynamic environments. This method exhibits strong advantages in policy learning and environmental adaptability, enabling anticipatory obstacle avoidance decisions in both simulated and real-world settings. However, reinforcement learning approaches typically require large amounts of interaction data, leading to high training costs. Moreover, their perception modeling largely relies on empirical learning, lacking explicit mechanisms for suppressing feature redundancy and modeling global environmental structures, which results in insufficient stability and interpretability in complex scenarios.

In summary, existing studies have achieved significant progress in trajectory learning, rule-based planning, optimization control, multi-robot coordination, and reinforcement learning. Nevertheless, they commonly face challenges in complex environments, including insufficient multi-scale perception, severe feature redundancy, and limited capability to understand global environmental structures. These limitations provide strong motivation and practical necessity for introducing spatial–frequency joint perception, dynamic feature selection, and global modeling mechanisms to further enhance robot path planning and obstacle avoidance performance. Mainstream navigation methods generally fall into perception-driven CNN pipelines or end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks. While CNN-based methods excel at local obstacle detection, they often lack the global structural awareness required for consistent path planning in complex layouts. RL-based approaches, on the other hand, struggle with interpretability and feature redundancy in high-dimensional visual inputs. Our SFPathFormer distinguishes itself from these mainstream strategies by explicitly coupling a frequency-aware perception module with a global Transformer-based planner, effectively bridging the gap between local safety and global topological understanding.

Methodology

The SFPathFormer architecture follows a deliberate logical flow designed to mitigate specific perception-to-planning bottlenecks. Recognizing that significant perception noise is most disruptive at the input stage, we first deploy PWD to decompose and filter raw features into stable spatial-frequency subbands. Subsequently, since feature redundancy is highly dependent on varying environmental domains, DFS is applied to dynamically reweight feature subspaces, ensuring the model focuses on dimensions most relevant to navigational safety. Finally, to overcome the ’local perception bias inherent in convolutional layers, a Vision Transformer is integrated to model global long-range dependencies, synthesizing local refinements into a structured representation of the global scene.

The core strength of the SFPathFormer lies in the structured interaction between its modules. PWD provides a denoised spatial-frequency representation that serves as the foundation. DFS then filters these representations by identifying feature dimensions that are highly correlated with path safety in the current domain, effectively ’distilling’ the data for the Transformer. Finally, ViT utilizes these distilled tokens to construct a global environmental layout, where the self-attention mechanism can more effectively map long-range obstacle correlations because the input features have already been purified of redundancy and noise. This interaction creates a feedback loop where signal-level clarity, channel-level relevance, and spatial-level logic converge to produce a robust navigation policy.

In addition, a Vision Transformer is integrated to model long-range dependencies and global scene structures. By jointly optimizing compact perceptual representations and global structural consistency, the proposed approach provides a systematic solution for robust autonomous navigation in complex scenarios. Parametric wavelet downsampling

Compared to other learnable downsampling techniques, such as strided convolutions or learnable pooling, PWD offers a superior mathematical foundation for feature preservation through signal decomposition. While strided convolutions treat all local information uniformly, often resulting in the loss of fine-grained structural details, PWD leverages the multi-resolution analysis property of wavelets to isolate specific frequency components. By making the wavelet basis functions learnable, the module can adaptively ’tune’ its filters to the specific geometric characteristics of obstacles in the training distribution, effectively acting as an intelligent structural gatekeeper that filters noise at the signal level.

PWD is designed to reduce feature resolution and computational complexity while preserving environmental structural information and obstacle-related discriminative features, thereby providing stable and robust input representations for subsequent path perception and decision-making. Domain-related feature selection

Traditional static reweighting lacks the ability to adapt to the high uncertainty of robotic environments. The introduction of contrastive learning serves as a critical supervisory signal that conventional attention mechanisms lack. By enforcing domain consistency, the model learns to filter out transient noise and focus on invariant structural features that are essential for long-term planning stability.

DFS is designed to address the issues of perceptual feature redundancy and domain-dependent feature effectiveness in robot path planning and obstacle avoidance tasks. By explicitly modeling the correlations among environmental domains, perceptual features, and path safety, DFS guides the network to adaptively select feature subspaces that are most discriminative for obstacle avoidance decisions, thereby improving planning stability and generalization performance. Experiment

Experimental datasets

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness, robustness, and generalization capability of the proposed method in diverse navigation scenarios, experiments are conducted on four widely used datasets covering synthetic environments, real-world outdoor driving scenes, and long-term autonomous navigation settings.

The selection of datasets follows a rigorous validation hierarchy: TartanAir and CARLA provide high-fidelity simulated environments for stress-testing complex navigation logic, while KITTI and Oxford RobotCar provide authentic real-world sensor data to evaluate the framework’s robustness against actual perception noise and long-term environmental appearance changes. This dual-track validation ensures that the proposed SFPathFormer can effectively bridge the ’sim-to-real’ gap.

TartanAir

TartanAir33 is a large-scale synthetic dataset designed for visual navigation tasks in complex and challenging environments. It provides high-quality RGB images, depth maps, optical flow, and precise ground-truth camera poses under a wide variety of scene configurations, including indoor, outdoor, urban, rural, and natural environments. TartanAir features significant variations in illumination, weather conditions, terrain complexity, and obstacle density, making it particularly suitable for evaluating the robustness of perception-driven path planning and obstacle avoidance methods under extreme conditions. In this work, TartanAir is used to assess the performance of the proposed framework in complex and dynamically changing environments with strong visual disturbances.

KITTI

The KITTI dataset34 is a benchmark dataset for autonomous driving and robotic perception, collected using a vehicle-mounted sensor platform in real urban and suburban environments. It contains synchronized RGB images, LiDAR point clouds, GPS/IMU measurements, and accurate ground-truth poses. The dataset includes diverse traffic scenarios with static and dynamic obstacles such as vehicles, pedestrians, and cyclists, as well as varying road structures. In our experiments, the KITTI dataset is employed to evaluate the proposed method in real-world outdoor navigation scenarios, focusing on its ability to handle complex road layouts, dynamic obstacles, and real sensor noise.

Oxford RobotCar

The Oxford RobotCar dataset35 is a large-scale, long-term autonomous driving dataset collected over multiple months along the same routes in Oxford, UK. It captures substantial appearance changes caused by varying weather conditions, illumination, seasons, and traffic patterns. The dataset provides multi-modal sensor data, including RGB cameras, LiDAR, GPS, and inertial measurements. Due to its strong domain shift characteristics, the Oxford RobotCar dataset is particularly suitable for evaluating the generalization and robustness of navigation and obstacle avoidance algorithms under long-term environmental variations. In this work, it is used to test the adaptability of the proposed method to significant appearance changes and environmental dynamics.

CARLA simulator

CARLA36 is an open-source, high-fidelity autonomous driving simulator that enables the generation of customizable urban environments with controllable traffic participants, weather conditions, and sensor configurations. It allows precise control over dynamic obstacles, environmental complexity, and motion patterns, while providing accurate ground-truth information. In this study, CARLA is used to construct diverse navigation and obstacle avoidance scenarios that are difficult to safely reproduce in the real world. Experiments conducted in CARLA enable systematic evaluation of the proposed method under controlled yet highly dynamic conditions, including dense traffic, sudden obstacle appearances, and complex interaction scenarios.

Experimental implementation details

As shown in Table 1, we provide the implementation details of the experiment. To ensure a rigorous and unbiased evaluation of the proposed SFPathFormer framework while strictly avoiding potential data leakage, a meticulous dataset splitting and data augmentation protocol was established across all four evaluation benchmarks, including TartanAir, KITTI, Oxford RobotCar, and CARLA. Training, validation, and test sets were constructed following a trajectory-level partitioning strategy to ensure a rigorous evaluation of the proposed framework and to avoid potential data leakage. Specifically, for each dataset, approximately 70% of the trajectories were used for training, 10% for validation, and the remaining 20% for testing. The partitioning was performed at the trajectory level rather than the individual image level, ensuring that frames originating from the same navigation sequence were exclusively assigned to a single subset. This protocol prevents highly correlated consecutive observations from simultaneously appearing in both the training and testing sets, thereby avoiding information leakage and providing a more reliable assessment of the model’s generalization capability. For datasets containing predefined official evaluation splits, including KITTI and Oxford RobotCar, the original sequence boundaries were preserved during partitioning to maintain consistency with the characteristics of the benchmark datasets. The validation set was exclusively used for hyperparameter selection and model checkpoint determination, while the test set remained completely unseen throughout the training process.

Experimental configuration of SFPathFormer.

To improve the robustness of the proposed SFPathFormer under diverse environmental conditions without altering the semantic structure of the navigation scenes, several online data augmentation strategies were applied only to the training set. These augmentations included random horizontal flipping, random brightness and contrast adjustment, color jittering, slight Gaussian noise injection, and random image cropping followed by resizing to the network input resolution. These operations simulate common visual disturbances encountered in real-world robotic navigation, such as illumination variation, sensor noise, and partial field-of-view changes, while preserving obstacle geometry and traversable-region semantics. No augmentation was applied to either the validation or test sets, where only the standard preprocessing pipeline consisting of image resizing and normalization was performed. Since all augmentations were conducted independently after dataset partitioning and exclusively on the training data, no information from the validation or test sets was involved in model optimization, thereby effectively eliminating the possibility of data leakage and ensuring a fair and unbiased evaluation of the proposed framework.

The proposed SFPathFormer framework was implemented using an end-to-end training strategy, in which the Spatial-Frequency Perception module, Dynamic Feature Selection module, and Vision Transformer backbone were jointly optimized within a unified optimization framework. Unless otherwise specified, identical training configurations were adopted across all four benchmark datasets to ensure a fair comparison and consistent evaluation. The network parameters were optimized using the AdamW optimizer, which has demonstrated strong convergence stability for Transformer-based visual representation learning. The initial learning rate was set to , with a weight decay coefficient of to alleviate overfitting while improving the generalization capability of the learned representations. The mini-batch size was set to 32, and all experiments were trained for 100 epochs. A cosine annealing learning rate schedule was employed to gradually reduce the learning rate throughout the training process, allowing the optimization to converge smoothly while avoiding oscillations during the later training stages. In addition, gradient clipping with a maximum norm of 1.0 was applied to stabilize optimization and prevent gradient explosion caused by the Transformer-based global modeling module.

During training, the validation set was continuously monitored for model selection and hyperparameter tuning. The model checkpoint achieving the best validation performance was retained for the final evaluation on the corresponding test set. To ensure fair and reproducible comparisons, the same optimizer configuration, learning rate schedule, batch size, and stopping criteria were consistently adopted for all competing experiments. Furthermore, as reported in the experimental results, each experiment was independently repeated approximately ten times using different random initialization seeds, and the mean values together with the corresponding standard deviations were reported for all evaluation metrics. This experimental protocol effectively reduces the influence of random initialization and provides a more reliable assessment of the stability and robustness of the proposed framework.

Considering that the proposed SFPathFormer jointly integrates spatial-frequency perception, dynamic feature selection, and global scene modeling, the optimization objective was formulated to enable collaborative learning across these complementary components. The primary supervision was provided by the navigation objective, while the feature learning process was further regularized through the contrastive representation learning mechanism embedded within the Dynamic Feature Selection module. This joint optimization strategy encourages the network to simultaneously preserve discriminative spatial-frequency representations, suppress redundant environmental information, and enhance global scene understanding, thereby improving both navigation accuracy and obstacle avoidance capability under diverse environmental conditions. The combination of the AdamW optimizer and cosine annealing learning rate schedule further contributes to stable convergence of the multi-module architecture and enables efficient optimization of the entire perception-to-decision pipeline.

Overall, the adopted training configuration provides a favorable balance between convergence speed, optimization stability, and computational efficiency, ensuring that the proposed SFPathFormer can be trained consistently across both simulated and real-world navigation datasets while maintaining strong generalization performance and reproducibility.

Discussion and conclusion

This paper addresses the problem of robot path planning and obstacle avoidance in complex environments by proposing a vision-driven framework that integrates spatial–frequency perception, dynamic domain-related feature selection, and global structural modeling. Extensive experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in improving path safety, planning stability, and environmental adaptability. The synergistic integration of these three components constitutes the core source of performance improvement. Specifically, the dynamic feature selection provides the Vision Transformer with highly discriminative tokens, allowing the global modeling stage to focus on critical risk areas and traversable gaps rather than being distracted by perceptual noise preserved in traditional CNN backbones.

Overall, Parametric Wavelet Downsampling plays a critical role in suppressing perceptual noise while preserving environmental structures, enabling stable obstacle representations even under weak texture and complex background conditions. The dynamic domain-related feature selection mechanism significantly reduces feature redundancy and enhances the relevance of perceptual representations to path decision-making across different environmental conditions. Furthermore, the introduction of the Vision Transformer strengthens the modeling of global traversability structures and long-range obstacle relationships, resulting in more consistent and smoother path planning outcomes in complex spatial layouts. The synergistic integration of these three components constitutes the core source of performance improvement in the proposed framework.

Theoretical analysis of the SFPathFormer framework reveals a distinctive ’tri-level’ optimization advantage in processing high-dimensional visual inputs. By optimizing the perception flow across the signal level (via PWD), the channel/feature dimension (via DFS), and the global spatial range (via ViT), the framework achieves a level of robustness that single-domain methods cannot attain. This structured hierarchy ensures that the planning module is provided with a compact, yet globally consistent environmental representation, significantly reducing the likelihood of local minima and planning instabilities in highly dynamic or unstructured scenarios. The simultaneous optimization strategy proposed in this work addresses the ’fragmentation’ issue in robotic vision systems. By unifying signal processing, feature selection, and global modeling into a single end-to-end framework, we provide a scalable solution that is critically required for next-generation autonomous robots operating in semi-structured or unknown environments where both local safety and global efficiency are non-negotiable.

Despite these advantages, the proposed method still has several limitations. First, although Parametric Wavelet Downsampling and multi-head self-attention mechanisms enhance representation capacity, they also introduce additional computational overhead. Further optimization is required for deployment in resource-constrained platforms or scenarios with stringent real-time requirements. Second, the effectiveness of the dynamic domain-related feature selection mechanism relies on the learning of implicit environmental domain representations, which are influenced by the diversity and coverage of the training data. When the test environment deviates significantly from the training distribution, the stability of feature selection may still be improved. In addition, this work primarily focuses on visual perception for path planning and obstacle avoidance, while the coupling with robot dynamics constraints and low-level control is relatively limited. This may affect practical deployment in highly dynamic or high-speed motion scenarios. Moreover, despite the encouraging results achieved on multiple public benchmark datasets and high-fidelity simulation environments, a major limitation of the present study is the absence of validation on a real-world robotic platform. Although the selected datasets and simulation scenarios cover diverse navigation conditions, they cannot fully reproduce the complexities and uncertainties encountered during physical robot deployment. Consequently, the practical effectiveness of the proposed SFPathFormer in real-world navigation tasks has not yet been comprehensively verified. In practical robotic systems, additional factors such as sensor latency, actuator uncertainty, communication delays, hardware resource limitations, localization errors, dynamic environmental disturbances, and the inevitable sim-to-real domain gap may significantly influence perception quality, navigation accuracy, and real-time decision stability. These factors are difficult to model completely within existing benchmark datasets and simulation environments and may affect the robustness and deployment efficiency of the proposed framework under real operating conditions. Therefore, although the current experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of SFPathFormer from the perspectives of perception capability, path planning performance, computational efficiency, and cross-dataset generalization, the conclusions should be interpreted with the understanding that they have not yet been validated through experiments on a physical robotic platform.

Future work will extend and refine this research along several directions with a particular emphasis on the practical deployment and validation of the proposed SFPathFormer framework on real-world robotic platforms. On the one hand, more efficient network architectures and lightweight optimization strategies will be investigated to further reduce computational complexity and improve real-time inference capability on resource-constrained embedded robotic systems. On the other hand, additional sensing modalities, including depth cameras, LiDAR, and infrared sensors, will be incorporated to establish a unified multi-modal perception framework capable of improving environmental understanding and enhancing the robustness of the proposed Spatial-Frequency Perception and Dynamic Feature Selection modules under adverse illumination, severe occlusion, and complex outdoor conditions.

More importantly, future research will focus on the systematic deployment and experimental validation of SFPathFormer on physical robotic platforms through a progressive evaluation protocol. (1) The proposed framework will first be deployed on a mobile robotic platform equipped with RGB cameras and onboard computing units to evaluate its real-time inference capability, computational efficiency, and hardware compatibility under practical deployment constraints. Particular attention will be paid to the influence of limited computational resources, memory consumption, and inference latency on navigation performance. (2) After completing hardware deployment, extensive navigation experiments will be conducted in structured indoor environments containing static obstacles, narrow passages, and complex intersections to evaluate path planning accuracy, obstacle avoidance capability, navigation success rate, and real-time decision stability under realistic operating conditions. (3) Subsequently, the evaluation will be extended to more challenging semi-structured and outdoor environments involving dynamic obstacles, illumination variations, sensor noise, and environmental uncertainties in order to investigate the robustness, adaptability, and cross-scene generalization capability of the proposed framework under practical navigation scenarios. Special attention will be devoted to analyzing the influence of the sim-to-real domain gap on perception quality and navigation performance, thereby providing guidance for further domain adaptation and model optimization. (4) Finally, the proposed perception framework will be tightly integrated with low-level motion controllers and decision-making modules to establish a complete end-to-end perception, planning, and control system. Comprehensive experiments will then be performed under different robot velocities, navigation tasks, and environmental complexities to evaluate closed-loop navigation performance, motion smoothness, collision avoidance capability, trajectory efficiency, and long-term operational stability.

Preliminary analysis of the computational efficiency of SFPathFormer indicates that its relatively low inference latency together with a parameter scale of approximately 152–164 M provides favorable conditions for deployment on modern embedded robotic computing platforms. These characteristics facilitate the transition from offline model training to online autonomous navigation while maintaining a desirable balance between perception accuracy and computational efficiency. Through the above deployment roadmap, future work aims not only to further verify the effectiveness of the proposed framework in real-world robotic systems but also to establish a practical engineering foundation for its application in autonomous mobile robots operating in complex and dynamic environments. Such efforts are expected to significantly strengthen the practical applicability, robustness, and scalability of SFPathFormer and promote its adoption in real-world robotic navigation and obstacle avoidance applications.

Strengths and limitations of the EULAR definition for difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis
Authors: György Nagy, Maya H Buch
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe management of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) has significantly improved, but a substantial number of patients still experience persistent disease activity, leading to the concept of ‘difficult-to-treat RA’ (D2T RA). The European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) has provided a pivotal definition for D2T RA to unify clinical approaches and research methods. This definition frames D2T RA as an umbrella term for a diverse group of patients with management challenges stemming from various factors, including biological resistance, patient experience, and clinical complexities. This framework is invaluable for a previously ill-defined population, but its real-life application highlights areas for ongoing consideration and potential refinement. Here, we discuss some of the strengths and limitations of the EULAR definition for D2T RA.Keywords: Arthritis, Rheumatoid; Autoimmunity; DMARD

The management of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) has undergone a significant transformation, underpinned by the contemporary treatment strategy of treating to target and major developments in the therapeutic landscape with the advent of biological and targeted synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (csDMARDs, bDMARDs and tsDMARDs).1 2 Despite these significant advances, a substantial proportion of patients continue to experience persistent disease activity and debilitating symptoms.3 4 The cycling of multiple classes of targeted therapies (b/tsDMARD) to achieve effective disease control remains a challenging clinical scenario. The complex and heterogeneous nature of such a patient population led to the emergence of the concept of ‘difficult-to-treat RA’ (D2T RA) and necessitated a clear and universally accepted definition for consistent clinical approaches to inform optimal management and uniform research methods when interrogating disease mechanisms and testing new interventions.5 6

The European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) has provided a pivotal definition for D2T RA.7 However, the practical application of this definition in the multifaceted real-world clinical environment presents several complexities and areas for ongoing discussion. Research studies employing the EULAR definition have also demonstrated different interpretations of the definition. Here we discuss some of these challenges and offer future directions.

One of the primary unintended areas of ambiguity in the EULAR definition lies within its first criterion: ‘treatment according to European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology recommendation and failure of ≥2 b/tsDMARDs (with different mechanisms of action)* after failing csDMARD therapy (unless contraindicated)’. The core intent of this criterion is to identify patients whose RA disease appears biologically refractory, with disease remaining active despite adequate exposure to multiple advanced therapies that target distinct immune mediators and pathways, (acknowledging that the threshold of ≥2 different mechanisms of action agents is subject to debate as well).8 Yet, by not explicitly stating an inadequate response, ‘failure’ itself may be interpreted differently.9 10 Should ‘failure’ strictly denote a lack of efficacy, or as intended/should it also encompass drug intolerance? In clinical practice, a patient might discontinue a b/tsDMARD due to severe adverse events or intolerance, rather than a lack of therapeutic response. If intolerance is considered ‘failure,’ it could lead to the inclusion of a patient population with fundamentally distinct biological drivers for their D2T status compared with those whose disease genuinely exhibits inherent or acquired resistance to multiple drug mechanisms. While pragmatically, toxicity and intolerance equally hinder effective treatment and contribute to the ‘D2T’ state, this distinction is crucial when aiming to understand the mechanisms of D2T RA, study D2T cohorts and test interventions to support tailored management. Therefore, when applying this criterion, it is imperative to clearly note whether the ‘failure’ is due to lack of efficacy or intolerance, as these represent fundamentally different clinical challenges.

We would suggest that studies should explicitly state the basis for D2T RA and categorise D2T cohorts accordingly. This may be presented as (1) successive (≥2) inadequate therapeutic responses or (2) successive (≥2) toxicity. Individuals who have cycled through over four or five b/tsDMARD classes are likely to have demonstrated both the first and second categories and therefore a profile of mixed inadequate response and toxicity.11 We would add that patterns of multiple drug inefficacy may differ, depending on whether failure occurs early or as a loss of response after prolonged exposure to the DMARDs. Identifying as such would still serve to support investigations into mechanisms of resistant disease and/or drug toxicity and ensure a more coherent rationale for clinical study and trial design.12

A second area for discussion is the subcriterion within the second point of the EULAR definition, ‘signs suggestive of active/progressive disease’. This includes measures to define this that are challenging to integrate into routine clinical practice. For instance, ‘rapid radiographic progression’ is a robust indicator of prognostically poor and aggressive disease activity, even in the absence of overt clinical inflammation.13 However, its assessment typically requires serial radiographs and, for research studies, would include detailed scoring, such as the van der Heijde-modified Sharp Score. Such intensive radiographic monitoring is not part of standard, high-volume clinical care due to concerns regarding cost, radiation exposure and the time-consuming nature of detailed scoring. Also research studies, especially in early-stage RA, do not show meaningful differences. Artificial intelligence is likely to enable the automated calculation of the Sharp score in the near future. This development has the potential to fundamentally transform how feasible it is to incorporate radiographic progression into the definition.

Similarly, the subcriterion, ‘reduction in quality of life’ due to RA symptoms, is a critical patient-reported outcome and a key component of the D2T definition. Yet, the basis for poor quality of life can be multifold and may not refer to persistent active disease. Also, its subjective nature and the variability in how it is assessed in routine practice can make consistent, quantifiable documentation difficult. Clinicians often rely on a qualitative understanding of the patient’s suffering rather than validated questionnaires, potentially leading to discrepancies in how this criterion is formally met across different healthcare providers or institutions and observational studies. These practical limitations highlight a gap between the comprehensive nature of the EULAR definition and the realities of busy clinics.

The third EULAR criterion, ‘the management of signs and/or symptoms is perceived as problematic by the rheumatologist and/or the patient’, introduces a profoundly important, yet wide-ranging, dimension. This criterion acknowledges that D2T RA is not solely a biological phenomenon of persistent inflammation but a complex interplay of patient experience and clinical considerations that make treatment decision-making more challenging. The criterion captures the essence of ‘D2T’ beyond objective inflammation, encompassing the contributions of the often debilitating impact of chronic pain, fatigue and psychosocial factors; as well as the wider clinical profile of comorbidities and safety concerns that can mean the ‘right’ treatment decision in terms of risk–benefit profile may not be so clear. This exemplifies the limitations of relying solely on a disease activity score approach, and to paraphrase William Osler, not just treating the disease but the patient who has the disease—understanding the patient and the context of their poor well-being. Subjective drivers of this criterion may be the (only) trigger for a patient satisfying D2T status and remain important to address. However, there is no measure for this criterion for studies to employ.10

The above appraisal demonstrates how the EULAR definition positions D2T RA as an ‘umbrella term’, encompassing a broad and heterogeneous group of patients whose RA is challenging to manage for diverse reasons. This is distinct from, yet inclusive of, ‘treatment-resistant’ disease.9 14 We have proposed the concept of persistent inflammatory refractory RA (PIRRA) and non-inflammatory refractory RA (NIRRA), given their implications for DMARD escalation based on current understanding.14 The EULAR definition and the EULAR Points to Consider (PtCs) both use ‘D2T’ as an overarching term encompassing therapy-resistant, PIRRA and those with NIRRA. Although the current definition does not distinguish between the subgroups (aiming instead to capture all reasons for challenging D2T RA), the EULAR PtCs provide distinct algorithms for both pharmacological and non-pharmacological management options of all patient groups.

The systematic guidance provided by EULAR PtCs for the management of D2T RA is instrumental in navigating this complexity.15 This guidance offers a structured, step-by-step approach that helps clinicians methodically rule out common confounding factors before escalating DMARD pharmacological therapy. For instance, the EULAR PtCs emphasise the importance of actively reassessing the RA diagnosis, confirming the presence or absence of inflammatory activity and assessing for comorbidities that might mimic signs of active RA and/or interfere with assessment. Furthermore, the guidance stresses the need to discuss and optimise treatment adherence, as non-compliance may falsely present as drug resistance. By systematically addressing these wider factors, the EULAR framework helps to pinpoint the ‘treatment-resistant’ patient group. This approach allows for more appropriate interventions, preventing unnecessary escalation of potent DMARDs in patients who might benefit more from other strategies. It also supports more consistent scientific investigation that is likely to provide biological signals of refractory disease, and the development and testing of novel interventions in a more rational target population.

As a viewpoint written by two members of the D2T RA EULAR task force, the goal is not to dismiss or favour any single subcohort, but instead to emphasise the opportunity to build on the first iteration of the definition and support the development of more accurate methods to fully uncover the complexity of D2T RA. The EULAR definition of D2T RA has provided an invaluable framework for a previously ill-defined patient population, fostering a more unified approach to this complex challenge.16 However, its real-life and research applications emphasise areas for ongoing consideration and potential refinement. The nuanced interpretation of ‘failure’ (efficacy versus intolerance), the practical difficulties in routinely assessing certain objective and subjective criteria, and the inherent subjectivity of the ‘perception of signs as problematic’ criterion all warrant continued discussion. Future updates to the definition may need to emphasise contextual factors more strongly such as: (1) treatment history and remaining options, (2) inflammatory or non-inflammatory profile of RA status (3) consequences of RA and coexistent pathology (eg, irreversible joint damage) that leads to a reduced quality of life and (4) specific comorbidities (table 1). Such background factors may vary regionally and can be country-specific.

While the comprehensive nature of the current definition is a strength, a future update may potentially offer clearer distinctions or provide a simplified, more ‘clinical-friendly’ version that is easier to implement in busy daily practice without sacrificing scientific rigour, and may suggest means of stratifying D2T cohorts for more precise research investigation. Such adaptations would further empower rheumatologists to accurately identify, stratify and ultimately improve outcomes for patients living with the persistent and multifaceted challenges of D2T RA, ensuring that the right patient receives the right treatment for the right reason.

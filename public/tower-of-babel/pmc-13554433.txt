Direct targeting of ORAI1 by ginsenoside Rg3 modulates calcium signaling and senescence-associated AMPK-NRF2 activation
Authors: Sooyeon Lee, Minjeong Ko, Ho Jeong Kwon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground20(S)-ginsenoside Rg3 (Rg3(S)), a major saponin derived from red ginseng, exhibits diverse biological activities, including antioxidant and anti-senescence effects. However, the direct molecular targets through which Rg3(S) regulates calcium signaling and its role at membrane contact sites during cellular senescence remain largely unknown.MethodsPlasma membrane (PM)-endoplasmic reticulum (ER) contacts and related protein interactions were analyzed using proximity ligation assays and co-immunoprecipitation. Direct binding of Rg3(S) to ORAI1 was validated using cellular thermal shift assays and microscale thermophoresis. Molecular docking simulations followed by site-directed mutagenesis were used to define critical binding residues. Cytosolic calcium levels and cellular senescence were assessed using calcium imaging and senescence-associated β-galactosidase staining.ResultsRg3(S) increased cytosolic calcium levels independently of ER calcium depletion and was accompanied by a reduction in PM-ER contacts. Rg3(S) directly bound to ORAI1 in a dose-dependent manner, identifying ORAI1 as a previously unrecognized molecular target of ginsenoside Rg3. Molecular docking revealed LYS204 and ILE229 within the extracellular loop of ORAI1 as key residues maintaining this interaction. Mutation of these residues abolished Rg3(S)-induced calcium influx, leading to impaired activation of the AMPK-NRF2 pathway and attenuation of the anti-senescence effect of Rg3(S).ConclusionThese findings identify ORAI1 as a key molecular mediator of ginsenoside Rg3(S)-induced calcium signaling linked to cellular senescence. By modulating PM-ER contact sites and cytosolic calcium dynamics, Rg3(S) attenuates senescence, providing new mechanistic insight into the anti-aging potential of ginseng-derived compounds beyond autophagy-centered pathways.Keywords: Calcium signaling, Cellular senescence, Ginsenoside-Rg3, ORAI1, Plasma membrane-endoplasmic reticulum contacts

Introduction

Cellular senescence is an irreversible state of growth arrest triggered by various stressors. It has been recognized as a major risk factor for multiple pathological conditions, including age related diseases, cancers, and metabolic disorders [[1], [2], [3]]. Reduction of oxidative stress, such as reactive oxygen species (ROS), has been recognized as a key strategy to suppress cellular senescence [4]. Recent studies further suggest that intracellular calcium signaling plays a pivotal role in coordinating oxidative stress responses and senescence associated phenotypes [5,6]. 20(S)-ginsenoside Rg3 (Rg3(S)) attenuates cellular senescence by reducing oxidative stress through the regulation of calcium ion levels [7].

Panax ginseng Meyer, commonly known as Korean ginseng, has long been utilized as a traditional herbal medicine [8,9]. P. ginseng is primarily consumed in two forms; White ginseng and Red ginseng. Ginsenoside Rg3, a major bioactive saponin in P. ginseng, is more abundant in Red ginseng than in White ginseng [10,11]. This compound exists as two stereoisomers, the S and R forms, depending on the orientation of the hydroxyl group at carbon 20. The S form is known to exhibit superior bioavailability [12]. Rg3(S) has a wide range of biological activities, including anticancer, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant effects, and has been reported to suppress cellular senescence by promoting intracellular calcium influx [7,[13], [14], [15], [16], [17]]. Defining the precise drug target interaction interface is essential for understanding compound specificity, efficacy, and ion channel regulation. Rg3(S) was proposed to function as a ligand for ORAI1, a calcium ion channel located at the PM, but direct experimental evidence for their physical interaction has been lacking [7]. In this study, we provide the first experimental evidence identifying the binding interface between Rg3(S) and ORAI1 using molecular docking guided mutational analyses.

Dynamic communication between intracellular organelles is essential for maintaining cellular homeostasis, and its dysregulation has been increasingly linked to aging-related diseases [18,19]. Intracellular calcium has emerged as a central regulator of interorganelle communication [20]. In particular, contacts between the PM and ER are dynamically regulated by ER calcium content, thereby controlling the gating and activity of calcium channels at the cell surface [21,22]. This process is mediated by ORAI1 at the PM and STIM1 at the ER membrane [[23], [24], [25]]. However, whether modulation of these membrane contact sites directly influences senescence associated cellular phenotypes remains largely unexplored.

Despite substantial progress in understanding calcium-mediated signaling and senescence, a direct molecular link between ginseng derived compounds, PM-ER contact regulation, and cellular senescence has not been established. In particular, whether pharmacological modulation of ORAI1 can actively remodel interorganelle communication to control senescence associated phenotypes remains unknown. Here, we propose that Rg3(S) functions as a direct molecular regulator of ORAI1 rather than a nonspecific calcium modulator. We hypothesized that Rg3(S) binds to a specific extracellular domain of ORAI1, reprogramming PM-ER contacts and cytosolic calcium signaling independently of ER calcium depletion, thereby suppressing cellular senescence. To test this hypothesis, we integrated biochemical target engagement assays, structure guided mutagenesis, and functional analyses of interorganelle communication, revealing a previously unrecognized mechanism by which a ginseng derived saponin directly controls calcium signaling and cellular aging in human dermal fibroblasts.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

Dulbecco's Modified Eagles Medium (DMEM), fetal bovine serum, penicillin-streptomycin, and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) were obtained from Gibco (Waltham, MA, USA). 20(S)-ginsenoside Rg3 with purity ≥98 percent as determined by high performance liquid chromatography and molecular formula C42H72O13 with a molecular weight of 785.01 g per mol was purchased from Sigma Aldrich (Cat. #SML0184, St. Louis, MO, USA). Rg3(S) was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide to prepare a stock solution and diluted in culture medium immediately before use.

Cell culture

HEK293 and Primary HDF cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C in a 5 % CO2 humidified atmosphere. Cells were subcultured when they were about 80 % confluent in T75 flask. Primary human dermal fibroblasts were considered senescent at passage 35 based on proliferative exhaustion and senescence associated phenotypes.

Calcium imaging

Cells were seeded onto 8-well chamber slides, and treated with the drugs. The plates were incubated with Fluo-4/AM or Mag-Fluo-4/AM in Krebs-Ringer-HEPES (KRH) buffer for 30 min at 37 °C with CO2. Following dye loading, cells were washed and maintained in calcium free Krebs Ringer HEPES buffer during imaging. Images were captured using an LSM900 confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss AG, Germany). Fluorescence intensity per cell area was quantified using ImageJ software.

Proximity ligation assay (PLA)

NaveniFlex Cell MR (Navinci, TF.MR.100) was used to assess plasma membrane endoplasmic reticulum and endoplasmic reticulum mitochondrial contact sites. Cells were seeded onto 8-well chambered cell culture slides, and treated with the drugs. The detailed protocol was followed as described by the manufacturer. Briefly, cells were fixed with 4 % formaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.2 % Triton X-100 for 20 min at room temperature. Then, cells were blocked with blocking solution for 1 h and then incubated with diluted primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. The following primary antibodies were used: STIM1 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-166840), ORAI1 (Novus Biologicals, NBP1-85463) and IP3R-1 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-271197), VDAC1 (Abclonal, A19707). PLA probes were applied and incubated for 1 h at 37 °C. Cells were incubated with ligase for 30 min, then with DNA polymerase for 100 min at 37 °C. Images were captured using an LSM980 confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss AG, Germany). PLA puncta were quantified as the total number of signals per cell from at least three independent experiments using ImageJ software.

Co-immunoprecipitation

Cells pretreated with or without Rg3(S) (10 μM) were harvested and lysed with ice-cold IP lysis buffer containing 150 mM NaCl, 50 mM Tris HCl (pH 7.5), 2 mM EDTA, 1% NP-40, 10% glycerol, and protease inhibitor cocktail. The lysate was then centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C. For immunoprecipitation, 2 μg of primary antibodies or control IgG were incubated with beads at room temperature for 1 h. The following primary antibodies were used: STIM1 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-166,840), ORAI1 (Novus Biologicals, NBP1-85463). The supernatant was incubated with the antibody-coated beads at 4 °C overnight. The bound proteins were eluted by boiling in SDS buffer and analyzed by western blotting.

Transfection

For gene overexpression, cells were transfected with Myc tagged ORAI1 (Cat. #RC210034, OriGene Technologies, MD, USA) or site directed mutant ORAI1 constructs using Lipofectamine 3000 according to the manufacturer's instructions. Cells were used for subsequent experiments 24 to 48 h after transfection.

Cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA)

HEK293 cells overexpressed with ORAI1 or mutated ORAI1 were harvested using trypsin-EDTA solution. Rg3(S) (10 μM) and DMSO were each added to cell suspensions. The cells were incubated in a CO2 incubator at 37 °C for 1 h with gentle shaking. After centrifugation of each sample, the pellet was washed with PBS and resuspended in 1 mL of PBS with protease inhibitor cocktail and heated for 3 min cool-down at 25 °C for additional 3 min in the thermal cycler. The tubes were centrifuged and the supernatant was discarded and replaced with 0.4 % NP-40 in PBS with protease inhibitors. The cell suspension was frozen twice with liquid nitrogen. Thermal stability of ORAI1 was evaluated by western blotting.

Molecular docking analysis

All molecular docking was performed using Discovery Studio 2018 software (Accelrys Software Inc., USA). The three-dimensional structure of ORAI1 (AF-Q96D31) was obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database. Docking analysis was conducted using the monomeric structure of ORAI1, with particular focus on the extracellular loop region, which is solvent exposed and accessible to ligand interaction. Although ORAI1 functions as a hexameric channel in its physiological state, the docking simulations were designed to evaluate ligand interactions with surface-accessible regions of the protein that are not directly dependent on pore formation or multimeric assembly. The structure of Rg3(S) was obtained from the NIH PubChem database. Docking poses were ranked based on binding energy and interaction probability.

Western blotting analysis

Soluble proteins were lysed using SDS lysis buffer. The samples were incubated at 100 °C for 5 min, followed by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked at room temperature with shaking for 1 h. They were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against Myc (Medical & Biological Laboratories, M192-3), ORAI1 (Novus Biologicals, NBP1-85463), phospho-AMPK (Cell Signaling Technology, 2535 S), AMPK (Abcam, ab1110036), NRF2 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-365949), HMOX-1 (Enzo, AKI-SPA-89), p21 (Abcam, ab109520) and β-actin (Abcam, ab8227; ab6276). After washing several times, the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h. Images were acquired using Image Lab software (Bio-Rad Laboratories) and quantified using ImageJ software. Protein expression levels were normalized to β-actin.

Senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) assay

Senescence Cells Histochemical Staining Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) was used to stain senescence markers following the manufacturer's instructions. The plates were treated with the staining mixture and incubated overnight at 37 °C without CO2. Stained cells were detected using a microscope.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.1 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA). All data are presented as the mean ± SEM. Group differences were analyzed using one-way ANOVA test, followed by Tukey's multiple comparison test. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Ethics statement

All experiments were performed using commercially available cell lines and primary human dermal fibroblasts obtained from established suppliers. No human participants or animal subjects were directly involved in this study, and therefore ethical approval and informed consent were not required.

Results

Rg3(S) diminishes PM-ER interactions, elevating cytosolic calcium influx

Previous studies have shown that Rg3(S) induces intracellular calcium influx and exerts anti-senescence effects [7]. Building on these observations, we focused to define the molecular mechanism underlying Rg3(S) induced elevation of cytosolic calcium levels. Human dermal fibroblasts (HDFs), a well-established model for cellular senescence, were used to evaluate the effect of Rg3(S) on calcium entry. Fluo-4-AM staining revealed that treatment with Rg3(S) enhanced rapid cytosolic calcium ion influx compared with control treated cells (Fig. 1A). In contrast, ER calcium levels assessed by Mag-Fluo-4-AM staining were not significantly altered following Rg3(S) treatment. Consistent with previous reports, thapsigargin (TG) treatment caused a pronounced depletion of ER calcium store (Fig. 1B) [26,27]. These results confirm that Rg3(S) elevates cytosolic calcium independently of ER calcium depletion, distinguishing its mechanism from classical store operated calcium entry.

ORAI1 functions as a key regulator of calcium entry at the PM and also contributes to the formation of PM-ER contact sites through its interaction with STIM1 [28]. We hypothesized that Rg3(S) increases cytosolic calcium levels by selectively modulating ORAI1 mediated PM-ER contacts rather than by directly triggering ER calcium release. To test this, we evaluated the effect of Rg3(S) on PM-ER and ER-mitochondrial contacts.

Proximity ligation assays (PLA) were performed using ORAI1 and STIM1 to assess PM-ER proximity and IP3R1 and VDAC1 to assess ER-mitochondrial proximity [29,30]. Rg3(S) treatment significantly reduced PM-ER contacts, whereas ER-mitochondrial proximity remained unchanged. In contrast, doxycycline, previously reported to enhance ER–mitochondrial interactions [31], induced a robust increase in ER-mitochondrial contacts, confirming the specificity of the assay (Fig. 1C–E).

To further validate the effect of Rg3(S) on PM-ER interactions, co-immunoprecipitation (co-IP) analysis was performed. Immunoprecipitation of STIM1 revealed a marked reduction in ORAI1-STIM1 interaction in Rg3(S) treated cells relative to controls (Fig. 1F). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Rg3(S) selectively disrupts PM-ER contacts, a process that is associated with enhanced cytosolic calcium influx and links ORAI1 modulation to elevated calcium signaling. (Fig. 1G).

Rg3(S) binds to ORAI1 in a dose-dependent and specific manner

Given that Rg3(S) reduced PM-ER contacts mediated by ORAI1 and STIM1 (Fig. 1), and that ORAI1 is a reported target of Rg3(S), we next investigated whether Rg3(S) directly engages ORAI1 at the molecular level. To minimize confounding effects arising from endogenous ORAI1 expression, experiments were conducted in HEK293 cells, which express low basal levels of ORAI1, followed by overexpression of Myc tagged ORAI1 (Supplementary Fig. S1). We hypothesized that direct binding of Rg3(S) stabilizes ORAI1 in a dose-dependent manner, reflecting a specific ligand-protein interaction.

To assess ligand-induced changes in ORAI1 stability, we performed isothermal cellular thermal shift assays (CETSA). Rg3(S) treatment significantly preserved the thermal stability of Myc-ORAI1 in a dose-dependent manner at 58 °C, indicating direct binding (Fig. 2A). Consistently, microscale thermophoresis (MST) analysis confirmed a direct interaction between Rg3(S) and ORAI1, yielding a measurable binding affinity (Fig. 2B). Collectively, these results demonstrate that Rg3(S) directly binds to ORAI1 with high affinity in a dose dependent manner.

To further evaluate the specificity of this interaction, we compared the binding effects of Rg3(S) with those of structurally related ginseng derived compounds using CETSA. 20(S)-protopanaxadiol and compound K share a common steroidal backbone with Rg3(S) but differ in glycosylation status (Fig. 2C). Rg3(S) exhibited the strongest stabilization of Myc-ORAI1, whereas protopanaxadiol induced a weaker stabilizing effect, and compound K failed to alter ORAI1 thermal stability (Fig. 2D), highlighting the critical role of glycosylation in high affinity, selective ORAI1 binding. These findings establish Rg3(S) as a direct and selective molecular ligand of ORAI1, providing mechanistic insight into its modulation of PM-ER contact sites and subsequent cytosolic calcium signaling.

Binding mechanisms of Rg3(S) to ORAI1 analyzed by molecular docking

To elucidate the molecular basis of Rg3(S) binding to ORAI1, we performed molecular docking analyses. Initial docking simulations targeting the transmembrane and intracellular regions of ORAI1 did not yield stable binding conformations or favorable binding energies (Supplementary Fig. S2), suggesting that these regions are unlikely to serve as primary interaction site for Rg3(S). Given the limited membrane permeability of Rg3(S) due to its hydrophilic sugar moiety [32,33], we hypothesized that Rg3(S) preferentially interacts with an extracellular domain of ORAI1. Subsequent docking simulations focusing on the extracellular loop between transmembrane domains 3 and 4 identified several potential interaction residues (Fig. 3A and B). Among these candidates, LYS204 was predicted to form a hydrogen bond with the sugar moiety of Rg3(S), while ILE229 was predicted to form a hydrogen bond with the sugar moiety of Rg3(S), whereas ILE229 was predicted to engage m hydrophobic interactions with its tetracyclic core structure, indicating a complementary binding mode involving both polar and hydrophobic contacts. These findings suggested LYS204 and ILE229 as key binding hotspots within the extracellular loop of ORAI1.

To experimentally validate these predictions, site directed mutagenesis was performed. LYS204 was substituted with alanine to eliminate its positive charge, and ILE229 was replaced with alanine to disrupt hydrophobic interactions, generating Myc tagged ORAI1K204A and ORAI1I229A constructs (Fig. 3C). CETSA analysis revealed that Rg3(S) enhanced the thermal stability of wild type ORAI1 but failed to stabilize either mutant protein (Fig. 3D and E). These results provide direct experimental validation of the docking predictions and demonstrate that LYS204 and ILE229 are critical determinants governing Rg3(S) binding to ORAI1.

Collectively, these data identify the extracellular loop of ORAI1 as a previously unrecognized regulatory interface for ginsenoside Rg3 interaction.

Functional validation of ORAI1 binding hotspots

To determine whether these binding residues are required for the biological activity of Rg3(S), we assessed calcium influx and downstream signaling events [34]. In cells expressing wild type ORAI1, Rg3(S) treatment markedly increased cytosolic calcium levels as measured by Fluo-4-AM fluorescence. In contrast, this Rg3(S) induced calcium influx was completely abolished in cells expressing ORAI1K204A or ORAI1I229A mutants (Fig. 4A and B). Notably, no significant differences in basal calcium levels were observed between wild type and mutant expressing cells under vehicle treated conditions, indicating that the mutations of LYS204 and ILE229 selectively disrupts ligand dependent channel regulation without altering intrinsic calcium homeostasis.

To further characterize calcium dynamics, time course analyses were performed following ER calcium store depletion with thapsigargin [35,36]. In wild type ORAI1 expressing cells, calcium re-addition induced robust calcium entry, which was further potentiated by Rg3(S) treatment. Importantly, this Rg3(S) mediated potentiation occurred independently of ER calcium depletion and exceeded the calcium influx observed with ionomycin treated positive controls. In contrast, ORAI1 mutant expressing cells failed to exhibit enhanced calcium influx in response to Rg3(S) (Fig. 4C), confirming that the identified extracellular loop residues are essential for Rg3(S) dependent calcium entry.

Consistent with calcium dependent signaling activation, Rg3(S) treatment induced phosphorylation of AMPK and activation of the NRF2-HMOX1 axis in wild type ORAI1 expressing cells, whereas both AMPK activation and NRF2-HMOX1 induction were completely abolished in cells expressing ORAI1 mutants (Fig. 4D–H). To determine whether Rg3(S) directly targets AMPK, CETSA was performed. Rg3(S) did not alter the thermal stability of AMPK, indicating the absence of direct target engagement (Supplementary Fig. S3). These findings support the conclusion that Rg3(S) activates AMPK indirectly, mostly likely through ORAI1-mediated calcium influx [7]. Furthermore, Rg3(S) attenuated hydrogen peroxide induced p21 expression in wild type ORAI1 cells but not in mutant expressing cells (Fig. 4I and J), thereby linking ORAI1 dependent calcium signaling to suppression of senescence associated stress responses.

Finally, senescence associated β-galactosidase staining demonstrated that Rg3(S) significantly reduced the proportion of senescent cells induced by oxidative stress in wild type ORAI1 expressing cells. In contrast, no protective effect was observed in cells expressing ORAI1 mutants (Fig. 5A). Collectively, these findings establish that the anti-senescence effects of Rg3(S) strictly depend on its direct interaction with specific extracellular loop residues of ORAI1. Moreover, they demonstrate that pharmacological modulation of ORAI1 mediated PM-ER contact associated calcium signaling constitutes a central mechanism by which Rg3(S) suppresses cellular senescence (Fig. 5B).

Discussion

Senescence can be induced by various factors, including DNA damage, oncogene activation, and telomere attrition [37,38]; however, the mechanisms governing senescence are complex and incompletely understood and effective therapeutic strategies to suppress cellular senescence remain limited. Although Rg3(S) has been reported to exert anti-senescence effects by inducing intracellular calcium influx and targeting ORAI1, a calcium ion channel at the PM, direct molecular evidence supporting this interaction and its structural basis has been lacking. In this study, we reveal that Rg3 regulates intracellular calcium signaling by interacting with a specific region of ORAI1 (LYS204, ILE229) and modulating PM-ER interactions.

By integrating ligand–target interaction validation approaches, our study provides robust and convergent evidence that Rg3(S) directly binds ORAI1 under physiologically relevant conditions. Using CETSA, we monitored compound-induced changes in the thermal stability of target proteins in live cells, demonstrating that ligand–protein interactions occur within the native cellular environment [39]. In parallel, MST precisely quantified molecular binding by measuring changes in thermophoretic behavior upon complex formation to determine binding affinity (Kd) [40]. Together, these complementary approaches strengthen the conclusion that ORAI1 is a direct molecular target of Rg3(S).

Furthermore, in silico molecular docking was conducted to predict critical interaction sites between Rg3(S) and ORAI1. Rg3(S) has a molecular weight of 785.01 g/mol, which is relatively high for a small molecule, and ginsenosides contain multiple glycoside moieties, indicating polarity [41]. These physicochemical properties suggest that Rg3(S) is unlikely to readily traverse the lipid bilayer, favoring interaction with extracelluarly accessible regions of membrane proteins. Consistent with this notion, our docking analysis identified potential interactions between Rg3(S) and the extracellular loop of ORAI1, particularly residues LYS204 and ILE229. CETSA performed in HEK293 cells expressing ORAI1 mutants at K204A or I229A failed to demonstrate Rg3(S) induced thermal stabilization, providing experimental validation of the docking predictions.

Moreover, functional analyses revealed that mutation of these residues abolished Rg3(S)- induced calcium influx, downstream AMPK-NRF2 activation, and anti-senescence effects, thereby establishing a direct structure-function relationship between ORAI1 binding and biological outcome. Notably, Rg3(S) failed to activate AMPK in ORAI1 mutants (K204A, I229A), despite equivalent intracellular exposure to the compound. A previous study demonstrated that calcium ion chelation abolishes Rg3(S)-mediated AMPK activation [7], and our CETSA results further indicate that Rg3(S) does not directly interact with AMPK. Together, these findings support the conclusion that AMPK activation occurs downstream of ORAI1 engagement rather than through direct targeting of APMK or calcium-independent mechanisms.

HDFs were employed to establish a physiologically relevant replicative senescence model, enabling us to define the anti-senescent phenotype of Rg3(S) under conditions that closely mimic natural cellular aging. In contrast, HEK293 cells were selectively utilized for mechanistic analyses requiring high transfection efficiency, including site-directed mutagenesis of ORAI1, calcium imaging, and downstream signaling validation of ORAI1 mutants. Although differences in cellular context should be considered, this complementary strategy allowed precise functional investigation of ORAI1 mutants, which is technically challenging in primary cells [42,43]. Importantly, the consistency of findings across the two cellular systems strengthens the robustness of our conclusions.

While our in silico docking approach provides a plausible binding region for Rg3(S), it does not fully capture the dynamic conformational flexibility of ORAI1 in its native membrane environment. ORAI1, as a membrane protein, undergoes structural rearrangements influenced by lipid composition, membrane potential, and protein-protein interactions [[44], [45], [46], [47]]. Thus, future studies employing molecular dynamics simulations or high resolution structural approaches such as cryo electron microscopy will be essential to fully characterize the conformational landscape and stability of the Rg3(S)-ORAI1 binding interface under physiological conditions.

A central conceptual advance of this study is the identification of a non-canonical mode of ORAI1 regulation by a ginseng-derived small molecule. Notably, our findings reveal a phenomenon that does not conform to the classical store-operated calcium entry (SOCE) paradigm: ER calcium depletion is not observed, PM-ER contacts are reduced, and the ORAI1-STIM1 interaction is diminished, yet a cytosolic calcium influx is induced. These findings collectively indicate that Rg3(S) promotes a STIM1-independent gating mechanism of ORAI1. In this context, our findings raise the possibility that Rg3(S) functions as an allosteric modulator that stabilizes ORAI1 in an open or active conformation of independently of STIM1. This interpretation is supported by the concurrent reduction in ORAI1-STIM1 binding and the enhancement of calcium influx following Rg3(S) treatment, although direct structural evidence for this mechanism remains to be established. This mechanism fundamentally differs from classical SOCE, in which STIM1 binding is required for channel opening, and instead suggests that extracellular ligand engagement can directly reprogram ORAI1 gating behavior.

These findings establish ORAI1 not only as a calcium channel but also as a structural regulator of inter-organelle communication whose function can be selectively modulated by small molecules. In this regard, Rg3(S) represents a unique class of natural compounds capable of regulating membrane contact site dynamics through direct protein engagement. This study therefore bridges ORAI1 pharmacology with membrane contact biology and provides a new conceptual framework for understanding how ginsenosides influence cellular signaling and aging.

The primary aim of this study was to define the direct molecular target and signaling mechanism of Rg3(S), which had not been previously established. Accordingly, the present work focused on cellular and mechanistic validation using well-controlled in vitro systems, providing a necessary foundation for future in vivo investigations. Future studies will determine whether the ORAI1-dependent anti-senescence effects of Rg3(S) can be recapitulated in aging animal models including evaluation of tissue level senescence markers, ORAI1-AMPK pathway activation, and assessment of Rg3(S) bioavailability to establish physiological relevance.

Collectively, our results redefine the anti-senescence activity of ginsenoside Rg3 as a consequence of direct molecular engagement with ORAI1 and remodeling of inter-organelle calcium signaling. This work advances the molecular understanding of ginseng derived saponins and highlights membrane contact site regulation as a promising strategy for targeting aging related pathologies.

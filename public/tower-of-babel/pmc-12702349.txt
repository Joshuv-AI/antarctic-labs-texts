Sustainable valorization of Pleurotus pulmonarius via green dual-enzyme technology: Novel optimization for functional protein hydrolysates
Authors: Nguyen Thi Ngoc Giang, Tran Van Khai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research established a sustainable dual-enzyme hydrolysis method for generating nutrient-dense hydrolysates from grey oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus pulmonarius), a native resource of Vietnam. Process optimization integrated a quadratic regression model using response surface methodology (RSM) and an artificial neural network combined with a genetic algorithm (ANN–GA). The objective was to enhance nitrogen recovery, amino acids, peptides, glutamic acid, and lysine, while minimizing NH3 formation. ANN–GA demonstrated superior predictive accuracy compared to RSM, with RMSE values of 1–2 % versus 2–5 %. In contrast, RSM offered precise quantitative insights regarding the effects of factors. The optimal conditions (50.9 °C, 6.45 h, pH 6.12) resulted in a nitrogen recovery of 27.23 %, with 6.926 g/100 g of amino acids, 11.41 g/100 g of peptides, 6.398 g/100 g of glutamic acid, 0.0845 g/100 g of lysine, and 0.0089 g/100 g of NH3. Levels of essential amino acids were comparable to or higher than those documented in previous studies. The sustainability of the method not only reduces waste and benefits farmers but also provides a practical route to producing mushroom-based functional food and seasoning ingredients due to their high amino acid and peptide content.Keywords: Dual-enzyme hydrolysis, ANN–GA optimization, Pleurotus pulmonarius, Bioactive peptides, Sustainable food processing

Introduction

The oyster mushroom is extensively consumed in Vietnam and various Asian nations (Giang et al., 2025). It is recognized for its elevated protein content, essential amino acids, vitamins, and minerals. In addition to serving as a fresh food, it represents a potential raw material for functional foods, attributed to its β-glucan, polyphenols, and bioactive peptides content (Mayirnao et al., 2025). A substantial amount of harvested mushrooms is either discarded or sold at a reduced value due to shape defects or storage constraints. This diminishes farmers' income and elevates post-harvest waste. A non-enzymatic approach for producing food protein hydrolysates utilizes acid hydrolysis, employing a strong acid under elevated temperatures and pressures to decompose proteins into smaller peptides (Fadimu et al., 2022). Alternatively, specific microorganisms can be employed in fermentation to generate protein hydrolysates without the addition of enzymes, as these microbes inherently produce proteases that degrade proteins (Cruz-Casas et al., 2023). Subcritical water hydrolysis is a technique that employs hot, pressurized water to decompose protein bonds under controlled temperature, pressure, and duration parameters (Marcet et al., 2016). Enzymatic hydrolysis is the most effective method of producing functional hydrolysates or peptides and also considered as green processing (Mora & Toldrá, 2023). The global demand for sustainable protein sources is rapidly increasing, with mushroom-derived hydrolysates emerging as promising candidates for functional foods and natural flavor enhancers. The green aspect of this method refers to reduced chemical use, lower energy requirements, and valorization of mushroom biomass, aligning with circular bioeconomy principles. Enzymatic hydrolysis offers several advantages, including precise control over the degree of hydrolysis, moderate operating conditions. Enzymatic hydrolysis of vegetable proteins is safer, yields a more consistent product through specialized reactions, and generates no byproducts (Nikbakht Nasrabadi et al., 2021). The functionality of the end product can be modified by meticulously selecting the enzymes and reaction conditions employed. This technique shows significant potential for producing functional and nutrient-dense food ingredients as well as for the development of clinical health foods. The development of advanced biochemical methods has enabled the production of pure proteases (Razzaq et al., 2019). Moreover, enzymatic protein hydrolysis efficiently utilizes mushrooms, yielding bioactive peptides and amino acids, while improving nutritional and sensory attributes (Giang et al., 2025). Alcalase, an endo-protease, efficiently cleaves protein chains into peptides, which are subsequently converted into smaller peptides and free amino acids by flavourzyme, an exo-peptidase (Hunsakul et al., 2022). The dual-enzyme method has been examined for various raw materials (Ambigaipalan et al., 2015; Justus et al., 2019; Nchienzia et al., 2010); however, its optimized application for grey oyster mushrooms is still underexplored, especially concerning the enhancement of essential amino acids and the reduction of undesirable by-products like ammonia.

Recent works have emphasized the use of response surface methodology (RSM) and green bioprocessing for valorizing biological materials into functional ingredients (Nakra et al., 2025; Rout et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2025; Tripathy & Srivastav, 2024). These studies collectively demonstrate the effectiveness of RSM in modeling multivariate interactions and improving process efficiency with fewer experimental runs, supporting its application in optimizing dual-enzyme hydrolysis for mushroom protein recovery and functionalization. Artificial neural networks (ANN) combined with genetic algorithms (GA) are increasingly applied in food science to model and optimize complex processes. ANN can capture non-linear relationships between variables and responses, while GA improves optimization by avoiding local minima (Chy et al., 2025; Khezri et al., 2020; Lamjiak et al., 2024; Thuy et al., 2024; Weerakoon & Assadi, 2025). In protein hydrolysis research, this approach can identify conditions that maximize desired components, such as peptides and amino acids, while minimizing undesirable by-products. Although ANN–GA has been used in other raw materials, its application to Vietnamese grey oyster mushroom hydrolysis has not been thoroughly studied. A comparative assessment with traditional regression models is also lacking, especially in the context of sustainable processing.

This research focused on the development and optimization of a sustainable dual-enzyme hydrolysis process for grey oyster mushrooms cultivated in Vietnam. The objective was to enhance nitrogen recovery, bioactive peptides, and essential amino acids, while regulating ammonia production. Optimization employed response surface methodology (RSM) alongside ANN–GA, facilitating the assessment of predictive performance and comprehensive analysis of factor effects. The findings offer empirical support for the valorization of mushrooms and the potential development of high-value products for local communities.

Materials and methods

Materials

Pleurotus pulmonarius (grey oyster mushroom) was obtained from a local farm. The mushrooms were harvested at the mature fruiting body stage (cap diameter 6–8 cm), when protein content is typically stable and optimal for hydrolysis. The farm operated under controlled cultivation conditions (temperature 25–28 °C, relative humidity 80–85 %, and natural light/dark cycles), ensuring reproducibility of raw material quality. Fresh samples contained ∼89 % moisture and were processed within 24 h of harvest to minimize compositional changes. Sample should be free from physical damage or insect infestations, were conveyed to An Giang University, Vietnam National University Ho Chi Minh City (Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam). The Alcalase enzyme, provided by Novozymes (Denmark), is derived from Bacillus licheniformis. The substance is a dark liquid exhibiting an activity of 2.5 IU/mL, functioning effectively at temperatures between 50 and 60 °C and within a pH range of 6.5 to 8.5. The Flavourzyme enzyme, provided by Novozymes, is derived from Aspergillus oryzae. The substance is a brown liquid with an activity of 50 IU/mL, optimal conditions at temperatures between 45 and 55 °C and a pH range of 6.0–8.0. Cellulase was provided by Regal Company (China) having an activity of 150 U/mL. Both enzymes and analytical-grade chemicals were supplied by Cemaco Vietnam Co., Ltd., located in Can Tho, Vietnam.

Dual enzyme hydrolysis process

Fresh Pleurotus pulmonarius fruiting bodies were dried in a hot-air oven at 50 ± 2 °C for 24 h until moisture content fell below 8 % as seen in Fig. S1. The dried samples were then ground using a laboratory mill and sieved to obtain a uniform powder with particle size below 1 mm, which was stored at 4 °C until use. Each experimental sample consisted of 50 g of dried oyster mushroom powder in an Erlenmeyer flask, combined with 1000 mL of water (Giang et al., 2025). The pH was adjusted to 5.5 using 10 % citric acid, and 2 mL of cellulase (provided by Regal Company, China, with an activity of 150 U/mL) was added to the mixture (Nguyen & Nguyen, 2021).

The three independent variables selected for optimization were hydrolysis temperature (45, 50, 55 and 60 °C), time (4, 6, 8 and 10 h), and pH (5.5, 6.0, 6.5, and 7.0). A multilevel regression analysis based on response surface methodology (RSM) was employed to evaluate their interactive effects on protein recovery, peptide, and amino acid contents.

Briefly, the hydrolysis process involved adjusting the sample pH with citric acid to attain a pH of 5–7, while simultaneously supplementing the mixture with Alcalase and Flavourzyme enzymes at a concentration of 0.03 g enzyme per g sample (0.3 %, w/w) for each enzyme, based on preliminary optimization trials. Preliminary comparison with single-enzyme hydrolysis indicated that the dual Alcalase–Flavourzyme system improved protein recovery by approximately 10–12 %, confirming the synergistic activity between endo- and exopeptidases that promotes more extensive peptide bond cleavage and nutrient release. Moreover, this ratio ensured efficient hydrolysis while minimizing enzyme consumption and cost. Hydrolysis was conducted at temperatures ranging from 45 to 60 °C and durations of 4–10 h (2-h interval). Following the hydrolysis process, enzyme activity was inhibited at 90 °C for a duration of 15 min. The mixture underwent filtration, and the resulting filtrate was analyzed for its parameters.

Chemical analysis

Determination of protein recovery yield

The protein recovery efficiency (PR%) was assessed utilizing the methodology of Lai et al. (2024). The total protein content (g/100 g dry matter) was assessed utilizing the Lowry technique (Nielsen, 2017) with specific modifications. Initially, 1 g of the material was digested with 10 mL of concentrated sulfuric acid in a Kjeldahl tube and subjected to digestion on a digestion block until the solution turned transparent. Subsequently, 0.1 mL of the digested sample or standard was combined with 0.1 mL of 2 N NaOH in a tube and heated in boiling water for 10 min. Upon reaching room temperature, 1 mL of the complex-forming reagent, consisting of 2 % (w/v) Na2CO3, 1 % (w/v) CuSO4.5H2O, and 2 % (w/v) sodium potassium tartrate in a 100:1:1 ratio, was introduced into the tube. Subsequently, approximately 0.1 mL of Folin-Ciocalteu reagent was added to the tube, vortexed, and permitted to equilibrate at ambient temperature for 30 min. The total protein concentration was ascertained utilizing the standard protein curve.

Determination of peptides

The sample preparation followed to the methodology established by Hultmann et al. (2012), incorporating minor modifications. A test tube was prepared by adding 1.2 mL of buffer solution at pH 5.5, followed by 0.8 mL of the filtered protein solution, and mixed thoroughly. Following this, 2 mL of 5 % trichloroacetic acid was added, thoroughly mixed, and permitted to stand for 30 min. The mixture underwent filtration to eliminate the precipitate, and the filtrate was subsequently collected. The hydrolysate was centrifuged at 10,000 ×g for 15 min, and the supernatant was filtered through an ultrafiltration membrane (Millipore, USA) with a molecular weight cut-off of 3 kDa to obtain low-molecular-weight peptide fractions. The filtrates were collected and stored at −20 °C for further analysis. The peptide content in the filtrate was quantified using the Lowry method (Nielsen, 2017).

Determination of amino acid

Amino acid content was measured using the Ninhydrin method (Friedman, 2004). Ninhydrin binds with amino acid and undergoes oxidation to release NH3, CO2 and hydrindantin. NH3 reacts with another ninhydrin molecule and hydrindantin to produce the blue substance diketohydrin (Ruhemanns complex). Briefly, 1 mL of sample was mixed with 0.5 mL of 0.2 M citrate buffer (pH 5.0) and 0.2 mL of 2 % ninhydrin solution. After 30 min, the mixture's absorbance was measured at 570 nm. Amino protein concentration was determined based on a standard leucine curve.

Determination of NH3

NH3 content was determined using the method of Guspita and Ulianas (2020). Two milliliters of sample solution were mixed with 2 mL of 0.01 M CuSO₄·5H₂O, vortexed, and left at room temperature for 30 min. Absorbance was measured at 615 nm. NH3 concentration was calculated using a standard curve for ammonia.

Determination of glutamic acid

Glutamic acid content was determined based on the method of Ye et al. (2020). A total of 0.2 mL of sample solution was added to a 25 mL volumetric flask, followed by 0.8 mL of ultrapure water, 1.0 mL of phosphate buffer (pH 6.0), and 1.0 mL of 2 % ninhydrin solution. The mixture was heated in a water bath at 90 °C for 15 min and diluted to 25 mL. Glutamic acid concentration was determined from a standard curve.

Determination of lysine

Lysine content was determined using the method of Hasani et al. (2007). One milliliter of buffer solution (pH 8.0) and 2 mL of 1,2-naphthoquinone-4-sulfonate (NQS) were added to a 5 mL volumetric flask, followed by 1 mL of sample or lysine standard solution. Distilled water was added to reach 5 mL. The mixture's absorbance was measured at 480 nm. Lysine concentration was determined using a standard curve.

Experimental design and model construction

The multiple regression analysis design of three factors including temperature (X1), time (X2) and pH (X3), each factor at three levels, was randomly arranged to minimize systematic errors. The quadratic regression model was built using the least squares method (RSM). The three-layer feedforward artificial neural network (ANN) model was trained using the back propagation algorithm and the input parameters were optimized using the genetic algorithm (GA). To ensure robustness and prevent overfitting, the dataset was divided into a training (70 %) and testing (30 %) subset. Additionally, 5-fold cross-validation was applied to confirm generalization performance. The model showed high predictive accuracy (high R2 and low RMSE), with residuals within ±5 %. The suitability of the models was evaluated through R2, Adj-R2, and analysis of variance.

(ANOVA).

Statistical analysis

All data were processed using the software [software name, version], testing for significant differences at the 95 % confidence level (p < 0.05). The experiments were repeated three times, and the results were reported as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical modeling was carried out using Statgraphics Centurion XV·I (USA), while ANN-GA modeling was performed in MATLAB R2023a (MathWorks, USA).

Results and discussion

Effect of extraction conditions on protein recovery efficiency

Fig. 1 presents the response surface indicating that protein recovery efficiency progressively rose with interaction betweeen pH, temperature and time, subsequently attaining a maximum before experiencing a slight decline. The findings from Table 1 also indicated that temperature, duration, and pH substantially influenced protein recovery efficiency (p < 0.001). The ANOVA analysis indicated that all regression coefficients were statistically significant (p < 0.0001). The quadratic regression model for protein recovery efficiency yielded R2 = 0.8052 and Adj-R2 = 0.8005, signifying that 80 % of the variance in the data was accounted for by the variables of temperature, time, pH, and their interactions. ANOVA analysis also indicated that pH (C) is the most significant influencing factor (p < 0.001), with a substantial positive coefficient of +55.78 as seen in regression Eq. 1, signifying that an increase in pH within the examined range markedly enhances protein recovery by sustaining the optimal activity of alcalase and flavourzyme (Hau et al., 2022; Mika & Wikiera, 2024), hence facilitating the cleavage of polypeptide chains into soluble proteins (Tran et al., 2021). The temperature (A = +10.21, p < 0.01) and time (B = +4.53, p < 0.05) demonstrate a positive effect, however to a lesser degree. Negative quadratic coefficients (A2 = −12.45; B2 = −9.32; C2 = −15.67, p < 0.05) indicate the presence of a maximum—beyond which performance declines due to protein denaturation or enzyme inactivation. This trend aligns with Hunsakul et al. (2022) about the hydrolysis of plant proteins using alcalase–flavourzyme. Moreover, the residuals exhibited a random distribution about the mean, with RMSE less than 5 % of the mean, signifying that the predictive model was both stable and precise.

Protein recovery efficiency improved with increased temperature and extended hydrolysis duration, with a peak of around 27.17 % at ideal conditions of about 52 °C, 8 h, and pH of approximately 6.3. As seen in Fig. 1, at high temperatures or prolonged durations exceeding the optimal limit, the recovery efficiency did not enhance further due to the gradual inactivation of the enzyme and the nearly complete hydrolysis of the substrate (Robinson, 2015). The ideal pH range of about neutral (6.0–6.5) was conducive for the activity of both alcalase and flavourzyme in the enzyme mixture. The synergistic application of endopeptidase (alcalase) and exopeptidase (flavourzyme) enzymes facilitated a more efficient release of protein from mushroom tissue compared to the use of a single enzyme, paralleling the hydrolysis of soybeans with flavourzyme, which yielded a significantly greater recovery of soluble protein than alcalase (Anh et al., 2020). The recovery of protein from oyster mushrooms was constrained by the elevated levels of insoluble components (chitin, fiber); yet, the 27 % yield was noteworthy and established the foundation for producing a nutrient-dense hydrolysate. This outcome achieved the research objective of identifying an optimal range of hydrolysis conditions to maximize protein recovery from the raw material.

Effect of hydrolysis conditions on amino acid and peptide content in hydrolysate

The hydrolysis conditions markedly influenced both amino acid release and peptide formation in Pleurotus pulmonarius hydrolysates. ANOVA indicated that temperature and time exerted a highly significant influence. Amino acid levels increased significantly with rising temperature and extended reaction time, reaching a maximum of ∼6.9 g/100 g dry matter under near-optimal conditions (≈52 °C, 5–6 h, pH 6.3) (Fig. 2). Initially, the enzyme rapidly cleaved the peptide chain, resulting in a swift increase in free –NH2 groups and amino nitrogen concentration (Ramírez Fuentes et al., 2021). The growth rate subsequently decreased as the substrate became increasingly reduced, leading to a decline in enzyme activity (Sun et al., 2025). Beyond this point, amino acid levels either plateaued or slightly decreased, most likely due to Maillard-type interactions between free amino acids and reducing sugars, which diminished detectable amino nitrogen (El Hosry et al., 2025). The role of pH was also critical as flavourzyme exhibited maximal exopeptidase activity at near-neutral pH, which not only promoted amino acid liberation but also reduced bitterness by removing hydrophobic terminal peptides (Zhang et al., 2019). These findings reinforce the importance of precise control over process parameters, since extreme conditions tended to reduce enzyme efficiency or promote undesirable side reactions.

Peptide concentration followed a similar bell-shaped response, peaking at ∼11.4 g/100 g dry matter under conditions of ∼55 °C, 6–7 h, and pH 6.1 (Fig. 3). At this stage, coordinated activity between Alcalase (endopeptidase) and Flavourzyme (exopeptidase) resulted in the generation of medium- and low-molecular-weight peptides with desirable functional properties (Bikaki et al., 2021). However, prolonged hydrolysis or excessive alkalinity led to further breakdown of these peptides into free amino acids, resulting in overall peptide depletion (Yeerong et al., 2024). This trend was particularly evident in the strong pH–time interaction, which demonstrated that maintaining a moderate pH for an adequate reaction period is essential to balance peptide generation and prevent over-degradation (Lafarga & Hayes, 2017). Benjakul et al. (2014) observed that augmenting the flavourzyme ratio in protein hydrolysate elevated the proportions of medium and low molecular weight peptides (3–5 kDa and < 3 kDa), indicating that modifying hydrolysis conditions can influence the targeted peptide size.

When compared with other protein substrates, the hydrolysis patterns of oyster mushroom proteins shared similarities with soy (Zhang et al., 2023), rice bran (Lafarga & Hayes, 2017), and fish proteins (Nguyen et al., 2022), all of which exhibited optimal amino acid or peptide yields followed by declines under extended treatment. Nevertheless, the mushroom hydrolysates demonstrated a distinctive profile with especially high glutamic acid release, a characteristic linked to umami taste enhancement and reduced bitterness (Raksakulthai & Haard, 2003). This distinguishes mushroom hydrolysates from fish and dairy hydrolysates, which often present pronounced bitterness due to hydrophobic peptide accumulation. Moreover, the dual-enzyme strategy clearly outperformed single-enzyme treatments, increasing peptide yield by ∼40 % and amino acid release by ∼30 %, thereby validating the synergistic mechanism of alcalase and flavourzyme in food protein valorization. The enzymatic process offers clear advantages over conventional chemical hydrolysis, operating under moderate conditions that reduce both energy input and chemical waste. The lower ammonia accumulation observed under optimized conditions not only indicates improved product quality but also represents a reduction in nitrogenous effluence that would otherwise pose environmental burdens. By valorizing surplus or low-grade Pleurotus mushrooms into high-value hydrolysates, the process aligns with circular bioeconomy principles, creating added value for farmers while contributing to sustainable protein supply chains. Taken together, these findings demonstrate that carefully optimized dual-enzyme hydrolysis produces mushroom hydrolysates that are nutritionally rich, functionally versatile, and environmentally preferable to conventional protein hydrolysates derived from animal or plant sources.

Effect of hydrolysis conditions on NH3 content in hydrolysate

The ammonia (NH3) produced during hydrolysis serves as a significant measure of the extent of amino acid degradation, particularly the deamination of glutamine and asparagine (Cheng et al., 2023). The findings indicated that temperature, duration, and pH significantly influenced the quantity of NH3 produced (p < 0.05) (Table S4). In contrast to other indications, the objective was to reduce the NH3 concentration. The quantified NH₃ concentration escalated with rising temperature, duration, and pH, to a minimum of around 0.00633 g/100 g dry matter at around 49 °C, 6.6 h, and pH 5.5 (Fig. 4). The process of NH3 synthesis mostly involves the enzyme glutaminase, found in flavourzyme, which catalyzes the transformation of glutamine into glutamic acid, concomitantly releasing NH3 (Martínez et al., 2022). This activity is optimal at neutral pH and suppressed at acidic pH, resulting in the lowest formation of NH3 at pH 5.5. Excessively elevated temperatures facilitate non-enzymatic deamination and amino acid breakdown, resulting in a rise in NH3. Conversely, extended hydrolysis duration progressively increases NH3 concentration in the solution. Given that NH3 imparts an unpleasant flavor and diminishes the nutritional quality of the hydrolysate, it is essential to regulate the hydrolysis conditions well to minimize NH3 generation (Liu et al., 2022). The findings indicate that hydrolysis ought to be conducted at a mildly acidic pH and moderate temperature, with the duration not exceeding what is essential, to minimize NH3 content while still accomplishing protein recovery and peptide synthesis objectives.

Effect of hydrolysis conditions on glutamic acid content in hydrolysate

Glutamic acid is a significant amino acid that imparts umami flavor to meals (Ninomiya, 2015). The concentration of free glutamic acid in the oyster mushroom hydrolysate varied considerably with temperature and pH, whereas time exhibited minimal variation (p > 0.5) (Table S5). The glutamic acid concentration peaked at around 6.42 mg/100 g of dry matter at around 47 °C, during a short duration of approximately 4 h, and at a pH of approximately 6.85 (Fig. 5). The ideal pH of around 7.0 indicated that the mold-derived enzyme (flavourzyme) efficiently facilitated the deamidation of glutamine, resulting in the liberation of free glutamic acid (Merz et al., 2015). Flavourzyme is highly effective in converting glutamine to glutamate, simultaneously producing a savory (umami) flavor in the hydrolysate (Tong et al., 2025). The optimal temperature (∼47 °C) was lower than other parameters, likely due to the heat sensitivity of the glutaminase enzyme in flavourzyme (Alahmad et al., 2023). At high temperatures, while the peptide hydrolysis rate increased, the glutamine conversion activity may have diminished, leading to no further augmentation of glutamate (Alahmad et al., 2023). The prolonged hydrolysis duration did not markedly elevate the glutamate concentration, as the release processes predominantly transpired in the initial phase; subsequently, the majority of glutamine was fully converted, resulting in an early attainment of glutamate saturation (Cruzat et al., 2018). This outcome indicates that hydrolysis ought to be conducted under near-neutral conditions to capitalize on the glutamate release capability of dual enzyme, resulting in a hydrolysate abundant in free glutamic acid — a natural taste enhancer. This is particularly significant when utilizing the hydrolysate as a seasoning or flavoring agent in food technology.

Effect of hydrolysis conditions on lysine content in hydrolysate

Lysine is a vital amino acid that is frequently problematic due to its susceptibility to loss during processing (Azevedo & Saiardi, 2016). The experiment yielded a low free lysine level, mostly influenced by time and pH, but temperature exhibited no significant impact (p = 0.13) (Table S6). As the duration extended from 4 to approximately 6 h and the pH elevated from 5.5 to around 6.2, the lysine concentration progressively augmented, attaining a peak of around 0.084 mg/100 g dry matter at roughly 52 °C, 6.3 h, and pH 6.17 (Fig. 6). Prolonging the duration or elevating the pH resulted in a further drop in free lysine concentration. The explanation could be that lysine, possessing the active ε-NH₂ group, engaged in the Maillard reaction with sugars present in the raw material (oyster mushrooms contain a substantial quantity of sugar), resulting in melanoidin compounds that are no longer quantifiable as free lysine (Yi et al., 2019). This behavior aligns with observation of Verma et al. (2019) that prolonged hydrolysis duration may marginally reduce soluble protein content due to interactions between amino acids and sugars, as well as lipid oxidation products (Amagliani et al., 2017). Conversely, at elevated pH levels, the rate of the Maillard reaction escalates, and the enzyme generates increased amounts of NH3, indicative of the degradation of amino groups in lysine and glutamine, resulting in a reduction in free lysine. This elucidates the necessity of moderate conditions (pH around 6.2, duration approximately 6 h) to achieve optimal lysine levels. In comparison to fish protein hydrolysate processed using flavourzyme, the fungal hydrolysate examined in this study exhibited a significantly reduced free lysine level (Alahmad et al., 2023). Alahmad et al. (2023) specifically noted that fish hydrolysate derived via Flavourzyme included lysine constituting around 7.9 % of total amino acids, much surpassing the amount obtained with alcalase. This indicated that flavourzyme can successfully release lysine; nevertheless, the limited lysine recovery in mushroom raw materials may result from lysine interacting with other components (Sukkhown et al., 2018). Nonetheless, lysine is an important amino acid; the application of flavourzyme still enhanced the free lysine concentration in the solution relative to the pure endo-peptide enzyme (Moreno-Mariscal et al., 2025). Consequently, to maintain lysine integrity, hydrolysis must be conducted at neutral pH and for a limited duration. This outcome assists in guiding the hydrolysis parameters to achieve a lysine-enriched mushroom protein solution for nutritional applications.

The model demonstrated a R2 of 0.923, an adjusted R2 of 0.922, and a root mean square error (RMSE) of 0.002 (Eq. 6). The pH (C) exhibited a coefficient of +0.0133 (p < 0.05), surpassing that of temperature (+0.0021, p > 0.05) and time (+0.0015, p > 0.05). The findings indicate that the optimal pH facilitates lysine preservation, whereas the influences of temperature and duration are not statistically significant. The findings align with Friedman and Levin (2012), indicating that lysine exhibits reduced susceptibility to degradation in the absence of the Maillard reaction under high temperatures and strong alkaline conditions. Residual analysis indicated that errors were randomly distributed around zero without significant systematic deviation, confirming the adequacy of the RSM models. Slightly higher residuals were observed under extreme temperature or pH conditions, likely due to enzyme denaturation and nonlinear hydrolysis behavior beyond the optimized range.

Comparision betweem multi-objective optimization and ANN-GA

The simultaneous optimization of multiple parameters was conducted using the desirability function, aiming to maximize protein recovery efficiency, amino acid content, peptide levels, glutamic acid, and lysine, while minimizing NH3 content. The optimization results indicated that the technological conditions attained the highest overall desirability (desirability = 0.703), which included a temperature of 51.08 °C, a time of 6.88 h, and a pH of 6.24.

Under optimal conditions, the responses achieved the predicted values as protein recovery efficiency 26.87 %, amino acid 6.87 g/100 g, peptide 11.21 g/100 g, NH₃ 0.0089 g/100 g, glutamic acid 6.38 g/100 g, and lysine 0.0836 g/100 g (Fig. 7). This result demonstrates the synergistic mechanism of the two enzymes. Alcalase, an endopeptidase from Bacillus licheniformis, cleaves internal peptide bonds, generating medium-chain peptides that increase substrate accessibility. Flavourzyme, an exopeptidase complex from Aspergillus oryzae, subsequently hydrolyzes terminal peptide bonds, releasing free amino acids and small peptides. This sequential action accelerates hydrolysis and reduces steric hindrance compared with single-enzyme systems (Wang et al., 2024). Preliminary trials confirmed that single-enzyme hydrolysis resulted in approximately 10–12 % lower protein recovery, verifying the synergistic advantage of the dual-enzyme approach. Moreover, alcalase (endopeptidase) rapidly cleaves intrachain peptide bonds, resulting in the release of short peptide chains, while flavourzyme (exopeptidase) further hydrolyzes these chains into free amino acids, particularly glutamic acid and lysine (Wang et al., 2024). The optimal pH of 6.24 preserves the activity of both enzymes, while temperature and time are modified to maximize efficiency without inducing protein denaturation or degradation of the target product. The lower lysine concentration compared to glutamic acid may result from the susceptibility of lysine's ε-amino group to Maillard-type condensation with reducing sugars (Kathuria et al., 2023), leading to the formation of unavailable lysine derivatives. Moreover, the natural abundance of glutamic acid in Pleurotus proteins (Tagkouli et al., 2020) and its preferential cleavage by Alcalase–Flavourzyme synergism contribute to its higher release in the hydrolysate. The lower lysine level compared with fish protein hydrolysates may be attributed not only to Maillard-type reactions but also to structural differences between fungal and animal proteins. Mushroom proteins generally contain higher proportions of glutamic acid and aspartic acid residues, whereas fish proteins are richer in lysine and sulfur-containing amino acids (Ionescu et al., 2025). These compositional differences influence enzyme specificity and peptide release patterns during hydrolysis.

The study employed an Artificial Neural Network model integrated with a genetic algorithm (ANN-GA) to identify optimal synthesis conditions that concurrently maximize protein recovery efficiency, amino acid, peptide, lysine, and glutamate content, while minimizing NH3 content. ANN model was shown high prediction capacity by multiple inputs and outputs as seen in Fig. 8. The findings indicated that the optimal conditions for achieving the proposed multi-objective goal were a temperature of approximately 50.9 °C, a duration of about 6.45 h, and a pH of around 6.12. At the optimal point, the model predicted nitrogen recovery efficiency of approximately 27.23 %, amino nitrogen at 6.926 g/100 g, peptide at 11.41 g/100 g, NH3 at 0.0012 g/100 g, glutamic acid at 6.398 mg/100 g, and lysine at 0.0845 mg/100 g. The observed values closely align with the optimal single-objective values for each criterion (referencing items 3.1–3.6), demonstrating an effective balance among the objectives. The process yields high protein recovery, and the hydrolysate is abundant in peptides, essential amino acids (lysine), and flavor compounds (glutamate), with minimal ammonia production. Moreover, the experimental test also conducted for the predicted conditions from multi-objective optimization and ANN-GA, which showed that ANN-GA could provide the different between actual and predicted values lesser than multi-objective optimization approach (Table 1). The results are similar to previous studies that ANN-GA can predict more accurately than multi-objective optimization methods (Loan et al., 2025; Luangsakul et al., 2025; Van Tai et al., 2024). This result validates the efficacy of the ANN-GA optimization method such the neural network model accurately simulated the nonlinear relationship between technological factors and responses, while the genetic algorithm facilitated the identification of the global optimal condition set, effectively balancing conflicting objectives. The research objective was achieved by identifying the optimal hydrolysis conditions, which enhanced the quality of the abalone mushroom protein hydrolysate. Unlike prior dual-enzyme hydrolysis studies on soy, fish, or rice bran proteins that mainly focused on yield improvement, the present study demonstrates an integrated optimization of process efficiency and sustainability for mushroom-based protein hydrolysates. The combined use of RSM and ANN–GA not only enhanced predictive accuracy but also reduced experimental resources, representing a novel green bioprocessing framework for sustainable protein valorization. Futhermore, the optimized hydrolysis conditions produced amino acid and peptide yields comparable to those reported for soy and fish protein hydrolysates (Nguyen et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023), while maintaining a cleaner profile with minimal bitterness due to the balanced action of Alcalase and Flavourzyme. Compared with dairy protein hydrolysis, the mushroom-based process achieved similar hydrolysis efficiency with substantially lower energy input and without chemical pretreatment, confirming the sustainability advantage of this green bioprocess.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated the effectiveness of the dual-enzyme hydrolysis approach using Alcalase and Flavourzyme in enhancing the nutritional quality of the grey oyster mushroom (Pleurotus pulmonarius). The integration of Response Surface Methodology (RSM) and Artificial Neural Network–Genetic Algorithm (ANN–GA) provided comprehensive optimization and accurate prediction of process parameters, yielding hydrolysates enriched in peptides, glutamic acid, lysine, and other essential amino acids, while maintaining low NH₃ levels to ensure sensory quality and food safety. The optimized process highlights the synergistic mechanism between the two enzymes—where Alcalase generates medium-chain peptides and Flavourzyme further hydrolyzes them into amino acids—resulting in higher protein recovery than single-enzyme systems. The proposed dual-enzyme approach reduces the need for harsh chemicals and minimizes energy consumption by operating under moderate temperature and near-neutral pH. Furthermore, the utilization of mushroom by-products contributes to waste reduction and supports circular bioeconomy principles. Although ANN–GA performed effectively in this study, future research should compare its predictive capacity with other machine learning algorithms such as Random Forest or Support Vector Machine to enhance generalizability across hydrolysis systems. Moreover, while this work successfully optimized process efficiency, further investigations are necessary to evaluate the bioactivities of the hydrolysates (e.g., antioxidant, antihypertensive, and anti-inflammatory activities) and to conduct sensory evaluations assessing umami intensity, bitterness reduction, and comparability with commercial flavor enhancers such as monosodium glutamate (MSG). Future work should also include a cost–benefit or techno-economic analysis to determine industrial feasibility compared with conventional protein hydrolysates. Overall, this sustainable bioprocess not only adds value to Pleurotus pulmonarius resources but also supports local agricultural livelihoods and provides a promising platform for producing natural, functional food ingredients and flavor enhancers.

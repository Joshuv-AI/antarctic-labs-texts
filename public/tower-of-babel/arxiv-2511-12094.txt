Transmutation operators for Schrödinger equations with distributional potentials and the associated impedance equation
Víctor A. Vicente-Benítez

Abstract
We present the construction of an integral transmutation operator for the Schrödinger equation − y ′′ + q ⁡ ( x ) ​ y = λ ​ y , x ∈ J , λ ∈ ℂ , -y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y=\lambda y,\quad x\in J,\ \lambda\in\mathbb{C}, in the case where q q is the distributional derivative of an L 2 L^{2} function on a bounded interval J ⊂ ℝ J\subset\mathbb{R} . Such a transmutation operator transforms solutions of v ′′ + λ ​ v = 0 v^{\prime\prime}+\lambda v=0 into solutions of the Schrödinger equation. The construction of the integral transmutation operator relies on a new regularization of the distributional Schrödinger equation based on the Polya factorization in terms of a solution f f that does not vanish on the closure of J J . Sufficient conditions for the existence of such a function f f are established, together with a method for its construction. As a consequence of the Polya factorization, we obtain an integro-differential transmutation operator for the associated Sturm–Liouville operator in impedance form related to f f , along with smoothness conditions for the transmutation kernel. Furthermore, we introduce the Darboux transform for both the Schrödinger and impedance operators, and describe their relationships with the corresponding transmutation operators. Finally, we develop several series representations for the solutions, including the spectral parameter power series and the Neumann series of spherical Bessel functions.



Keywords:
Distributional Schrödinger equation; Transmutation operators; Polya factorization; Sturm-Liouville equation in impedance form; Darboux transform.

Introduction

The theory of transmutation operators, more than fifty years after its creation based on the work of Jacques Delsarte
[
9
]
, has demonstrated its effectiveness not only as a theoretical tool for the qualitative analysis of solutions to Sturm–Liouville-type equations, but also for their practical construction and for solving both direct and inverse boundary value problems. Roughly speaking, a transmutation operator is a linear homeomorphism
\mathbf{T}
on a given topological vector space that relates two linear operators
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
defined on a subspace through a similarity relation of the form
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{T}\mathbf{B}
. The idea is to transmute the simpler operator
\mathbf{B}
into the more complicated one
\mathbf{A}
. In particular, the operator
\mathbf{T}
transforms solutions of
\mathbf{B}v=\lambda v
into solutions of
\mathbf{A}u=\lambda u
. Of particular interest is the case when
\mathbf{B}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
and
\mathbf{A}
is the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+q(x)
, where
q
is a
regular function
, that is, an
L^{p}
-function in a bounded interval. A first contribution to the construction of such transmutation operators was made by A. Ya. Povzner
[
31
]
, who discovered that certain solutions of the Schrödinger equation

-y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y=\lambda y,\quad 0<x<\ell,

(1)

admit the integral representation

y(\lambda,x)=e^{i\sqrt{\lambda}x}+\int_{-x}^{x}K(x,t)e^{i\sqrt{\lambda}t}dt.

(2)

Later studies focused on analyzing the properties of the transmutation integral kernel
K

[
2
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
16
,
25
,
28
,
19
]
, one of the most significant results being its application to the theory of inverse spectral problems via the Gelfand-Levitan equation
[
13
]
.

In recent years, the study of integral representations of the form (
2
) has gained particular importance in the development of series-type analytical representations for the solutions of equation (
1
). One of the most significant advances is the explicit characterization of certain families of transmutation operators acting on the set of monomials
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. The construction of such transmutation operators is based on the so-called
Polya factorization
of the Schrödinger operator. This can be described as follows: given a continuous solution
f
of (
1
), whose regularity depends on the potential
q
, and which has no zeros in the interval of interest, the Schrödinger operator can be expressed through the following factorization:

-\frac{1}{f(x)}\frac{d}{dx}\left(f^{2}(x)\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{y(x)}{f(x)}\right)\right).

(3)

The existence of the nonvanishing solution was established for the case where
q
is continuous in
[
20
]
and later extended to
L^{2}
potentials perturbed by a sum of delta point interactions in
[
25
]
. This factorization makes it possible to obtain a complete system of solutions in terms of recursive integrals of
f
, denoted by
\{\varphi_{f}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and referred to as the
formal powers associated with

f

[
20
]
. In fact, the solutions of equation (
1
) can be expressed as power series in the spectral parameter
\rho=\sqrt{\lambda}
, whose coefficients in
x
are precisely the formal powers. This result is known as the
SPPS method
and has proven to be an effective tool for solving direct spectral problems, such as the computation of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions (see
[
20
,
4
]
). Moreover, the formal powers form a complete system in
L^{p}(-\ell,\ell)
and, under certain regularity conditions on
f
, also in
W^{k,2}(-\ell,\ell)

[
21
,
33
]
. Furthermore, when the potential
q
is continuous, there exists a transmutation kernel
K_{f}(x,t)
satisfying the Goursat conditions
K_{f}(x,x)=\frac{f^{\prime}(0)}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x}q(s)ds
,
K_{f}(x,-x)=\frac{f^{\prime}(0)}{2}
(see
[
28
, Ch. 1]
). The corresponding transmutation operator, denoted by
\mathbf{T}_{f}
, satisfies the mapping property
[
6
]

\mathbf{T}_{f}[x^{k}]=\varphi_{f}^{(k)}(x)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Thus, the action of the operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}
on a dense set of functions is explicitly known. This result was extended to
L^{p}
-type potentials in
[
7
]
. In
[
19
]
it was shown that the transmutation kernel admits a Fourier-Legendre series representation of the form
K_{f}(x,t)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}(x)}{x}P_{m}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)
, where the coefficients
\{a_{m}(x)\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
can be computed explicitly by a recursive integration procedure. Moreover, it is known that
q(x)=\frac{a_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)}{a_{0}(x)+1}
. This series representation of the kernel leads to the so-called
Neumann series of Bessel functions (NSBF) representation
for the solutions of (
1
), that is, the solutions
u_{0}(\rho,x)=\mathbf{T}_{f}[\cos(\rho x)]
and
u_{1}(\rho,x)=\mathbf{T}_{f}[\sin(\rho x)]
admit the following series representations:

u_{j}(\rho,x)=\frac{1+(-1)^{j}}{2}\cos(\rho x)+\frac{1-(-1)^{j}}{2}\sin(\rho x)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}a_{2k+j}(x)j_{2k+j}(\rho x),\quad j=0,1,

(4)

where
j_{\nu}(\zeta)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\zeta}}J_{\nu+\frac{1}{2}}(\zeta)
stands for the spherical Bessel functions (see
[
19
]
). These types of representations are particularly useful for describing spectral data, such as the characteristic function of an eigenvalue problem, and have proven to be a powerful tool for the numerical computation of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. Moreover, in the analysis of inverse spectral problems (namely, the recovery of the potential and boundary conditions from certain spectral data such as pairs of eigenvalues and normalization constants or the Weyl function), the NSBF series have demonstrated great versatility for the practical solution of such problems. In
[
17
]
it was shown that the existence of the NSBF series (
4
) allows one to reduce several inverse problems to the solution of a system of linear algebraic equations whose unknowns are the coefficients
\{a_{m}(x)\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
. Given the relationship of the potential
q
and the coefficient
a_{0}
, it is possible to recover
q
from the first component of the solution vector. This approach is highly efficient for numerical computations and has been successfully applied to various types of inverse spectral problems in recent years
[
17
,
18
,
22
]
.

The aim of this paper is to extend the construction of the transmutation operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}
and the corresponding series representations of the solutions to the case when
q
is a distribution belonging to the class
W^{-1,2}
. Integral representations for the solutions
u_{j}(\rho,x)
are already known
[
2
,
32
]
, as well as a transmutation relation in the sense of Delsarte is known for Schrödinger operators with Dirichlet and Neumann conditions
[
1
,
16
]
. The standard approach to regularizing operators with distributional coefficients, established in
[
32
]
, consists of rewriting the Schrödinger operator in terms of the quasi-derivative defined by an antiderivative
\sigma\in L^{2}
of the potential
q
. In this work, we adopt a different approach, introducing a new type of regularization based on Polya’s factorization. To this end, we show that for any real-valued distributional potential there always exists a nonvanishing solution
f
, and that Polya’s factorization is valid for functions
y\in W^{1,2}
for which the Schrödinger operator defines a regular distribution on
L^{2}
. Moreover, we demonstrate that the domain of regularization of the Schrödinger operator consists of those functions
y\in W^{1,2}
satisfying
y^{\prime}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}y\in W^{1,2}
. Finally, we establish the existence of Polya’s factorization for complex-valued potentials in the case when
q
admits an antiderivative
\sigma\in L^{q}
for
2<q\leq\infty
. This class of potentials includes Radon complex measures and generalizes the results of
[
25
]
for
L^{2}
potentials perturbed by finitely many delta point interactions.

Using Polya’s factorization, we reduce equation (
1
) to a Dirac-type system of equations. Based on the results in
[
15
]
, we establish the existence of the integral representation (
2
). The main properties of the operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}
are analyzed, including its continuity from
L^{2}(-\ell,\ell)
onto
L^{2}(0,\ell)
and from
C[-\ell,\ell]
onto
C[0,\ell]
. One of our principal results is the transmutation mapping property
\mathbf{T}_{f}[x^{k}]=\varphi_{f}^{(k)}
, together with the SPPS and NSBF representations for the solutions of (
1
). Furthermore, we show that the formal powers
\{\varphi_{f}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
form an
L
-basis for the distributional Schrödinger operator
[
7
,
10
]
. From the transmutation mapping property, we demonstrated that
\mathbf{T}_{f}
transmutes the operator
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}
into the distributional Schrödinger operator in the sense of Delsarte. As a consequence of Polya’s factorization, we generalize the concept of the
Darboux transformed operator
, that is, the corresponding distributional equation associated with
\frac{1}{f}
, and we establish a relation between the operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}
and its associated Darboux transform
\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}
.

The second part of this work is devoted to the construction of a transmutation operator for the Sturm-Liouville equation in impedance form
\mathbf{L}_{f}=-\frac{1}{f^{2}}\frac{d}{dx}f^{2}\frac{d}{dx}
. Polya’s factorization establishes the equivalence between the Schrödinger and impedance operators via the Liouville transform
y\mapsto\frac{y}{f}
. Integral representations for the solutions of the impedance equation have been studied in
[
1
,
8
,
24
]
, where their applications to inverse spectral problems are explored. For the case when
f
is of class
C^{1}
, it was shown in
[
23
]
that there exists a transmutation operator that maps the second derivative into the impedance operator, in the form of the integro-differential operator

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}u(x)=u(x)-\int_{-x}^{x}\widehat{K}_{f}(x,t)u^{\prime}(t)dt.

This result was later extended to the case
f\in W^{1,\infty}
in
[
33
]
. We extend this result to the case when
f\in W^{1,2}
. With the aid of the Liouville transform and the operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}
, we show that
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}
is a transmutation operator in the sense of Delsarte, and that the kernel
\widehat{K}_{f}
is continuous and belongs to the class
W^{1,2}
in the triangular domain
0\leq x\leq\ell
,
|t|\leq x
. Furthermore, we prove that
\widehat{K}_{f}
satisfies a hyperbolic equation with the Goursat conditions
\widehat{K}_{f}(x,x)=1-\frac{1}{f(x)}
,
\widehat{K}_{f}(x,-x)=0
. Additionally, we show that if
\{f_{n}\}
is a sequence of impedance functions such that
\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}}{f}\rightarrow\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}
in
L^{2}
, then the corresponding transmutation kernels
\{\widehat{K}_{f_{n}}\}
converge weakly to
\widehat{K}_{f}
in
W^{1,2}
, and
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f_{n}}
converges to
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}
in the strong operator topology of
\mathcal{B}(W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell),W^{1,2}(0,\ell))
.

The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 presents the correct definition of the distributional Schrödinger operator and equation, the concept of domain of regularization, and the standard regularization in terms of the antiderivative
\sigma
of
q
. Section 3 introduces the new regularization based on the Polya factorization given by the nonvanishing solution
f
, together with a new characterization of the domain of regularization and the quasiderivative in terms of
f
. Section 4 presents the spectral parameter power series representation for the solutions in terms of the formal powers associated with
f
. In Section 5, we introduce the Darboux transform and analyze its main properties. Section 6 is devoted to the construction of the transmutation operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}
and the study of its main analytical properties. Section 7 focuses on proving the existence of the nonvanishing solution
f
and provides an algorithm for its construction. Section 8 presents approximation results for the kernel
K_{f}
and the transmutation operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}
. In Section 9, we develop the Neumann series of Bessel functions representation for the solutions of the Schrödinger equation. Finally, Section 10 is devoted to the construction of a transmutation operator for the Sturm-Liouville operator in impedance form and to the analysis of the corresponding transmutation kernel.

Background on the distributional Schrödinger equation

Let
X
be a topological linear space and
X^{\prime}
its topological dual. The action of a functional
f\in X^{\prime}
over
x\in X
will be denoted by
(f|x)_{X}
.
Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded open interval,
C_{0}^{\infty}(J)
the space of
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support in
J
, and
(C_{0}^{\infty}(J))^{\prime}
its topological dual, whose elements are called distributions. A distribution
f\in(C_{0}^{\infty}(J))^{\prime}
is said to be
regular
if there exists
\tilde{f}\in L^{1}_{loc}(J)
such that
(f|\phi)_{C_{0}^{\infty}(J)}=\int_{J}\tilde{f}\phi
for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(J)
, and
f
is called
L^{p}
-regular if
\tilde{f}\in L^{p}(J)
. Let
W^{k,p}(J)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denote the Sobolev space of functions in
L^{p}(J)
whose first
k
-distributional derivatives are
L^{p}
-regular. It is known that
u\in W^{k,p}(J)
iff
u\in C^{k-1}(\overline{J})
,
u^{(k-1)}\in AC(\overline{J})
and
u^{(k)}\in L^{p}(J)
. We recall the continuous embedding
W^{1,p}(J)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{J})
. The space
W_{0}^{1,p}(J)
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(J)
in
W^{1,p}(J)
and consists of functions
u\in W^{1,p}(J)
such that
u|_{\partial J}=0
. According to
[
3
, Cor. 8.10]

uv\in W^{1,p}(J)
whenever
u,v\in W^{1,p}(J)
. The space
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(J)
is the topological dual of
W_{0}^{1,p}(J)
. Since
J
is bounded,
[
3
, Prop. 8.14]
implies that every
f\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(J)
can be represented as the distributional derivative of function
F\in L^{p^{\prime}}(J)
, that is,

(f|\phi)_{W_{0}^{1,p}(J)}=-\int_{J}F\phi^{\prime}\qquad\forall\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(J).

Similar notation is used for Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
on open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Given Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, we denote by
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
the space of bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
. When
X=Y
, we denote this space by
\mathcal{B}(X)
. We recall that a subset
E\subset X
is said to be complete in
X
if
\operatorname{Span}E
is dense in
X
. The characteristic function of a set is denoted by
\chi_{A}
. Throughout the paper, we use the notation
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. The weak convergence of a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in a Banach space is denoted by
x_{n}\rightharpoonup x
, while convergence in the norm (strong convergence) is denoted by
x_{n}\rightarrow x
.

Let
q\in W^{-1,2}(J)
. We consider the Schrödinger equation with distributional potential
q
:

-y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y=\lambda y,\quad x\in J,\;\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

(5)

We look for solutions
y\in W^{1,2}(J)
. The left-hand side of (
5
), denoted by
\mathbf{S}_{q}y
, defines a distribution in
W_{0}^{1,2}(J)
as follows:

(\mathbf{S}_{q}y|\phi)_{W^{1,2}_{0}(J)}:=-(y^{\prime\prime}|\phi)_{W^{1,2}_{0}(J)}+(yq|\phi)_{W^{1,2}_{0}(J)}=\int_{J}y^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+(q|y\phi)_{W^{1,2}_{0}(J)}.

(6)

Since
y\phi\in W_{0}^{1,2}(J)
, the pairing
(q|y\phi)_{W^{1,2}(J)}
is well defined. Hence, we obtain the operator
\mathbf{S}_{q}:W^{1,2}(J)\rightarrow W^{-1,2}(J)
whose action is given by (
6
). Of course, a solution
y\in W^{1,2}(J)
of (
5
) satisfies the condition that
\mathbf{S}_{q}y=\lambda y
as distributions, so
\mathbf{S}_{q}y
is an
L^{2}
regular distribution.

Definition 1

The
domain of

L^{2}
-
regularization
of
\mathbf{S}_{q}
is the class

\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{q}):=\{y\in W^{1,2}(J)\,|\,\mathbf{S}_{q}y\text{ is an }L^{2}\text{-regular distribution}\}.

Abusing notation, when
y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{q})
we write
\mathbf{S}_{q}y\in L^{2}(J)
. In this case, the solutions of (
5
) belong to
\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{q})
.

A characterization of the action of
\mathbf{S}_{q}y
can be obtained in terms of an antiderivative of
q
. Let
\sigma\in L^{2}(J)
satisfying
\sigma^{\prime}=q
. Hence, the action of the distribution
\mathbf{S}_{q}y
over
\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(J)
is given by

\displaystyle(\mathbf{S}_{q}y|\phi)_{W^{1,2}_{0}(J)}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\{y^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}-\sigma(y\phi)^{\prime}\}=\int_{J}\{y^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}-\sigma y^{\prime}\phi-\sigma y\phi^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\{(y^{\prime}-\sigma y)\phi^{\prime}-\sigma(y^{\prime}-\sigma y)\phi-\sigma^{2}y\phi\}

Following the notation introduced in
[
32
]
, we denote the
\sigma
-quasiderivative of
y\in W^{1,2}(J)
by

y_{\sigma}^{[1]}:=y^{\prime}-\sigma y.

(7)

From the previous computations, the following proposition is immediate.

Proposition 2

Given
y\in W^{1,2}(J)
, the distribution
\mathbf{S}_{q}y
is
L^{2}
-regular iff
y_{\sigma}^{[1]}\in W^{1,1}(J)
and
(y_{\sigma}^{[1]})^{\prime}+\sigma^{2}y\in L^{2}(J)
.

Another reason for considering functions for which the operator is
L^{2}
-regular is to obtain an operator in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(J)
. Abusing notation, for
y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{q})
we write
\mathbf{S}_{q}y=-(y_{\sigma}^{[1]})^{\prime}-\sigma y_{\sigma}^{[1]}-\sigma^{2}y
. Hence, we have the operator
\mathbf{S}_{q}:\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{q})\subset L^{2}(J)\rightarrow L^{2}(J)
. According to
[
32
]
, this is the maximal operator associated with the differential expression
-(y_{\sigma}^{[1]})^{\prime}-\sigma y_{\sigma}^{[1]}-\sigma^{2}y
. Several standard properties of equation (
5
) were established in
[
32
]
. For instance, a proper definition for a Cauchy problem with initial data at
x_{0}\in\overline{J}
is given in terms of
y(x_{0})
and
y_{\sigma}^{[1]}(x_{0})
. For every
g\in L^{2}(J)
, the Cauchy problem consisting of
\mathbf{S}_{q}y=\lambda y+g
with the initial conditions
y(x_{0})=a_{0}
,
y_{\sigma}^{[1]}(x_{0})=a_{1}
, admits a unique solution
y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{q})
. The space of solutions of (
5
) is two-dimensional, and two solutions
y,u
are linearly independent iff their
\sigma
-Wronskian

W_{\sigma}[y,v]:=yv_{\sigma}^{[1]}-vy_{\sigma}^{[1]}

does not vanish at least at one point of
\overline{J}
(the proof of these facts can be found in
[
32
]
).

3
New regularization based on Polya factorization

Now, we introduce a new type of regularization for the operator
\mathbf{S}_{q}
, based on a factorization in terms of a nonvanishing solution. Let
f\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{q})
be a solution of
f^{\prime\prime}-qf=0
such that
f(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\overline{J}
. We call such a function
f
a
nonvanishing solution
of the operator
\mathbf{S}_{q}
. A natural question arises: can we write
q=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}
in the sense of distributions? To answer this, we first analyze the distribution
\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}
. Since
f
does not vanish on
\overline{J}
, a direct computation shows that
f(x)=f(x_{0})e^{\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt}
for some
x_{0}\in\overline{J}
, so that
\frac{1}{f}\in AC(\overline{J})
and
\left(\frac{1}{f}\right)^{\prime}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f^{2}}\in L^{2}(J)
. Thus,
\frac{1}{f}\in W^{1,2}(J)
and the action of the distribution
\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}
is given by

\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\bigg|\phi\right)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}=\left(f^{\prime\prime}\bigg|\frac{\phi}{f}\right)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}=-\int_{J}f^{\prime}\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}f-\phi f^{\prime}}{f^{2}}\right)=\int_{J}\left\{\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}\phi-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)\phi^{\prime}\right\}

(8)

Let us denote this distribution by
q_{f}
. Hence

\displaystyle(f^{\prime\prime}-fq_{f}|\phi)_{W_{0}^{1,2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\left\{-f^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}(f\phi)+\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}(f\phi)^{\prime}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\left\{-f^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}-\frac{(f^{\prime})^{2}}{f}\phi+\frac{(f^{\prime})^{2}}{f}\phi+f^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}\right\}=0

Thus,
fq=fq_{f}
as distributions. For every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,2}(J)
, we have
\frac{\phi}{f}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(J)
, and therefore

(q|\phi)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}=\left(fq\bigg|\frac{\phi}{f}\right)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}=\left(fq_{f}\bigg|\frac{\phi}{f}\right)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}=(q_{f}|\phi)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}

Hence
q=q_{f}
as distributions. Consequently,

\int_{J}\sigma\phi^{\prime}=\int_{J}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\phi^{\prime}-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}\phi\right\}.

Therefore, we can express
\sigma
in terms of
\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}
and an anti-derivative of
\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}
. For convenience, we normalize
f
as follows: given
x_{0}\in\overline{J}
, we assume that
f(x_{0})=1
.
Hence
q=q_{f}
as distributions. Thus, we can choose

\sigma_{f}(x)=\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}+\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\right)^{2}dt

(9)

as an anti-derivative of
q_{f}
.

Definition 3

A potential
q\in W^{-1,2}(J)
is called
admissible
if there exists a nonvanishing solution
f\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{q})
of
\mathbf{S}_{q}f=0
. In such a case, we denote
q=q_{f}
, choose
\sigma_{f}
as the corresponding anti-derivative, and denote the operator
\mathbf{S}_{q}
by
\mathbf{S}_{f}
.

In Section
7
we establish sufficient conditions on
q
for the existence of the solution
f
. From now on,
we assume that the potentials under consideration are admissible
.

Remark 4

Since
\frac{1}{f}\in W^{1,2}(J)
, it follows that the map
W^{1,2}(J)\ni y\mapsto\frac{y}{f}\in W^{1,2}(J)

Denote

(10)

Theorem 5
(Polya factorization)

The following factorization holds (in the distributional sense):

\mathbf{S}_{f}y=-\frac{1}{f}Df^{2}D\frac{y}{f}\qquad\forall y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f})

(11)

or, equivalently,

\mathbf{S}_{f}y=f\mathbf{L}_{f}\left[\frac{y}{f}\right]\qquad\forall y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f}).

(12)

Proof.
Let
y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f})
and set
v=\frac{y}{f}\in W^{1,2}(J)

8

\displaystyle(\mathbf{S}_{f}y|\phi)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\left\{(fv)^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}(fv\phi)-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}(fv\phi)^{\prime}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\left\{f^{\prime}v\phi^{\prime}+fv^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+\frac{(f^{\prime})^{2}}{f}v\phi-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}(f^{\prime}v\phi+fv^{\prime}\phi+fv\phi^{\prime})\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\{fv^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}-f^{\prime}v^{\prime}\phi\}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\{v^{\prime}(f\phi)^{\prime}-2f^{\prime}v^{\prime}\phi\}

By Remark
4
and the assumption that
\mathbf{S}_{f}y
is

-regular, it follows that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{S}_{f}y|\phi)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}=\int_{J}\{-v^{\prime\prime}f\phi-2f^{\prime}v^{\prime}\phi\}=\int_{J}(f\mathbf{L}_{f}v)\phi.

Since

4
The spectral parameter power series

Definition 11

The
formal powers
associated with
f
are the functions
\{\varphi_{f}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
defined recursively as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}^{(0)}:=

\displaystyle f,

(18)

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}^{(1)}:=

\displaystyle f_{1},

(19)

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}^{(k)}:=

\displaystyle k(k-1)\mathbf{R}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(k-2)},\quad k\geq 2,

(20)

where
\mathbf{R}_{f}
is the integral operator defined in (
15
).

Remark 12

The first formal power satisfies the conditions
\varphi_{f}^{(0)}(x_{0})=1
and
\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(0)}(x_{0})=0
, while the second one
\varphi_{f}^{(1)}(x_{0})=0
and
\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(1)}(x_{0})=1
.

The sub-sequences formal powers satisfy
\varphi_{f}^{(k)}(x_{0})=\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}(x_{0})=0
.

Furthermore,
\mathbf{S}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(0)}=\mathbf{S}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(1)}=0
and

\mathbf{S}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}=k(k-1)\varphi_{f}^{(k-2)},\quad k\geq 2.

(21)

Let
\lambda=\rho^{2}
with
\rho\in\mathbb{C}
, and let
e_{f}(\rho,x)
be the unique solution of Eq. (
5
) satisfying the initial conditions

e_{f}(\rho,x_{0})=1,\quad\mathbf{D}_{f}e_{f}(\rho,x_{0})=i\rho

(22)

The following theorem generalizes the well-known spectral parameter power series representation to the distributional case.

Theorem 13

The solution
e_{f}(\rho,x)
admits the spectral parameter power series (SPPS) representation

e_{f}(\rho,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}(x)}{k!}.

(23)

The series converges with respect to
x
in the norm of
W^{1,2}(J)
, and its second derivative series converges in
W^{-1,2}(J)
. The series converges absolutely and uniformly with respect to the parameter
\rho
on compact subsets of the complex plane.

Proof.
As in the case of regular potentials, the formal powers can be rewritten in terms of recursive integrals as follows. Define
X^{(0)}\equiv\tilde{X}^{(0)}\equiv 1
, and for
k\geq 1
set

\displaystyle X^{(k)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{0}}^{x}X^{(k-1)}(s)(f^{2}(s))^{(-1)^{k}}ds,

\displaystyle\tilde{X}^{(k)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\tilde{X}^{(k-1)}(s)(f^{2}(s))^{(-1)^{k-1}}ds.

Hence

\varphi_{f}^{(k)}=k!f\cdot\begin{cases}\tilde{X}^{(k)},&\text{if }k\text{ is even},\\
X^{(k)},&\text{if }k\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

In this way, the SPPS series (
23
) can be re-written as (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
or
[
4
]
)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}}{k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\rho^{2k}f\tilde{X}^{(2k)}+i\rho\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\rho^{2k}fX^{(2k+1)}

(24)

The following estimates hold:

|\tilde{X}^{(2k)}(x)|,|X^{(2k)}(x)|\leq\frac{C1^{k}C_{2}^{k}}{(k!)^{2}},\;|\tilde{X}^{(2k-1)}(x)|\leq\frac{C_{1}^{k-1}C_{2}^{k}}{(k-1)!k!},\;\;|X^{(2k-1)}(x)|\leq\frac{C_{1}^{k}C_{2}^{k-1}}{k!(k-1)!},

(25)

where
C_{1}=\|f^{-2}\|_{L^{\infty}(J)}
and
C_{2}=\|f^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(J)}
(see
[
4
, Prop. 5]
). Since
D\tilde{X}^{(2k)}=f^{\prime}\tilde{X}^{(2k)}+\frac{\tilde{X}^{(2k-1)}}{f}
, the above estimates shows that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\rho^{2k}f\tilde{X}^{(2k)}
converges with respect to the variable
x
in
W^{1,2}(J)
and uniformly in
\rho
on every compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
. Similar for the second series. Consequently, the series of the distributional second derivative of both series converges in
W^{-1,2}(J)
. Furthermore, note that

\mathbf{D}_{f}(f\tilde{X}^{(2k)})=\frac{1}{f}\tilde{X}^{(2k-1)},

and by the estimates (
25
),
\mathbf{D}_{f}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\rho^{2k}f\tilde{X}^{(2k)}
belongs to
W^{1,2}(J)
, that is,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\rho^{2k}f\tilde{X}^{(2k)}
belongs to
\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f})
. The fact that (
24
) satisfies (
5
) follows from the relations

\mathbf{S}_{f}\tilde{X}^{(2k)}=\tilde{X}^{(2k-2)},

and satisfies the initial conditions of
e_{f}(\rho,x)
by construction.

We also introduce the solutions
C_{f}(\rho,x)
and
S_{f}(\rho,x)
satisfying the initial conditions

\displaystyle C_{f}(\rho,x_{0})=1,

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{D}_{f}C_{f}(\rho,x_{0})=0,

(26)

\displaystyle S_{f}(\rho,x_{0})=0,

\displaystyle\quad\mathbf{D}_{f}S_{f}(\rho,x_{0})=1.

(27)

Applying the uniqueness of the Cauchy problem, a direct computation yields the relations

\displaystyle C_{f}(\rho,x)=\frac{e_{f}(\rho,x)+e_{f}(-\rho,x)}{2},

\displaystyle\quad S_{f}(\rho,x)=\frac{e_{f}(\rho,x)-e_{f}(-\rho,x)}{2i\rho},

(28)

\displaystyle e_{f}(\rho,x)=C_{f}(\rho,x)+i\rho S_{f}(\rho,x).

(29)

As a corollary of (
28
), we obtain the SPPS representation for the solutions
C_{f}(\rho,x)
and
S_{f}(\rho,x)
. The proof is straightforward.

Corollary 14

The solutions
C_{f}(\rho,x)
and
S_{f}(\rho,x)
admit the SPPS representation

C_{f}(\rho,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\rho^{2k}\varphi_{f}^{(2k)}(x)}{(2k)!},\quad S_{f}(\rho,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\rho^{2k}\varphi_{f}^{(2k+1)}(x)}{(2k+1)!}.

(30)

Each series converges with respect to the variable
x
in the norm of
W^{1,2}(J)
. The corresponding series of second derivatives converges in
W^{-1,2}(J)
. Moreover, both series converge absolutely and uniformly with respect to the parameter
\rho
on every compact subset of the complex plane.

Remark 15

From the SPPS series (
30
), we observe that, for
x\in J
fixed,
C_{f}(\rho,x)
and
S_{f}(\rho,x)
are even functions in the parameter
\rho
. Moreover, both solutions are entire functions in the spectral parameter
\lambda=\rho^{2}
.

5
The Darboux transformation

Let us consider the operator
\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}:\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}})\rightarrow L^{2}(J)
with domain
\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}):=\{y\in W^{1,2}(J)\,|\,\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}y\in W^{1,2}(J)\}
and whose action is given by

\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}y:=-\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}y\qquad\text{for }y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}).

Note that
\frac{1}{f}
is a non-vanishing solution of the equation
\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}y=0
satisfying normalization
\frac{1}{f(x_{0})}=1
. Hence
\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}
is precisely the Schrödinger operator with potential
q_{\frac{1}{f}}=f\left(\frac{1}{f}\right)^{\prime\prime}
. In the distributional sense,

q_{\frac{1}{f}}=2\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}-q_{f}.

(31)

Indeed, note that

\displaystyle\left(f\left(\frac{1}{f}\right)^{\prime\prime}\bigg|\phi\right)_{W^{1,2}_{0}(J)}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\frac{f^{\prime}}{f^{2}}(f\phi^{\prime}+f^{\prime}\phi)=\int_{J}\left\{\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\phi^{\prime}+\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}\phi\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{J}\left[2\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}\phi+\sigma_{f}\phi^{\prime}\right]=\left(2\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{2}-q_{f}\bigg|\phi\right)_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}.

Definition 16

The potential
q_{\frac{1}{f}}
is called the
Darboux transformation
of
q_{f}
, and
\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}
is the
Darboux operator
associated with
\mathbf{S}_{f}
.

Let
y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f})
be a solution of
\mathbf{S}_{f}y=\lambda y
. From the factorization
\mathbf{S}_{f}=-\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}\mathbf{D}_{f}
, we consider the function

v:=\mathbf{D}_{f}y.

(32)

Note that
\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}v=-\mathbf{S}_{f}y=-\lambda y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f})
. Hence

\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}v=-\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}v=\lambda\mathbf{D}_{f}y=\lambda v.

Therefore, the operator
\mathbf{D}_{f}
transforms solutions of the equation
\mathbf{S}_{f}y=\lambda y
into solutions of
\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}v=\lambda v
. Note that
v(x_{0})=\mathbf{D}_{f}y(x_{0})
and
\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}v(x_{0})=-\lambda y(x_{0})
. In the particular case when
y
is the solution
e_{f}(\rho,x)
, its Darboux transform
v
satisfies the initial conditions
v(x_{0})=i\rho
and
\mathbf{D}_{f}v(x_{0})=-\lambda=(i\rho)^{2}
. By the uniqueness of the Cauchy problem, we have the relation

\mathbf{D}_{f}e_{f}(\rho,x)=i\rho e_{\frac{1}{f}}(\rho,x).

(33)

Similarly, we deduce the relations

\mathbf{D}_{f}C_{f}(\rho,x)=-\lambda S_{\frac{1}{f}}(\rho,x),\qquad\mathbf{D}_{f}S_{f}(\rho,x)=C_{\frac{1}{f}}(\rho,x).

(34)

Proposition 17

The formal powers satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=k\varphi_{\frac{1}{f}}^{(k-1)}\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

(35)

Proof.
Equalities
\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(0)}=0
and
\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{f}=\varphi_{\frac{1}{f}}^{(1)}
follows from the definitions. In order to obtain (
35
) for
k\geq 2
, we consider the SPPS representation of
e_{f}(\rho,x)
and
e_{\frac{1}{f}}(\rho,x)
together with the relation (
33
) to obtain:

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k+1}\varphi_{\frac{1}{f}}^{(k)}}{k!}=i\rho e_{\frac{1}{f}}(\rho,x)=\mathbf{D}_{f}e_{f}(\rho,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}}{k!}.

The exchange of the series with the operator
\mathbf{D}_{f}
is due to the convergence in
W^{1,2}(J)
. Since
\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(1)}=\varphi_{\frac{1}{f}}^{(0)}
, reordering indices we obtain

\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}}{k!}=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}\varphi_{\frac{1}{f}}^{(k-1)}}{(k-1)!},

and comparing as Taylor series in
i\rho
we conclude (
31
).

Theorem 18

There exists a function
K_{f}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
such that

e_{f}(\rho,x)=e^{i\rho x}+\int_{-x}^{x}K_{f}(x,t)e^{i\rho t}dt,\quad 0<x<\ell.

(u_{1},u_{2})^{T}

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}^{\prime}\\
v_{2}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\\

v_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\rho\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}(0)\\
v_{2}(0)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\rho y(0)\\

(37)

BU^{\prime}(x)+Q_{f}(x)U(x)=\rho U(x),\quad 0<x<\ell,

(38)

1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{f}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\\
\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}&0\end{pmatrix}.

In this case,

, meaning that
(Q_{f})_{i,j}\in L^{2}(0,\ell)
for

. According to

G^{f}(x,t)
defined on

with the property that for

fixed, the function
G^{f}(x,\cdot)\in L^{2}((-x,x);\mathbb{C}^{2})
and the unique matrix solution of (

U(\rho,x)
satisfying the initial condition
U(\rho,0)=I_{2\times 2}
, admits the integral representation

U(\rho,x)=e^{-B\rho x}+\int_{-x}^{x}G^{f}(x,t)e^{-B\rho t}dt,

(39)

where
e^{-B\rho x}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\rho x)&-\sin(\rho x)\\
\sin(\rho x)&\cos(\rho x)\end{pmatrix}
.

Consider the case when
y
is the solution
S_{f}(\rho,x)
. Then
(v_{1},v_{2})^{T}=(-\rho S_{f}(\rho,x),\mathbf{D}_{f}S_{f}(\rho,x))^{T}

(v_{1}(0),v_{2}(0))^{T}=(0,1)^{T}
. By the theory of linear systems, we have

\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix}=U\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(\rho x)\\
\cos(\rho x)\end{pmatrix}+\int_{-x}^{x}G^{f}(x,t)\begin{pmatrix}-\sin(\rho t)\\
\cos(\rho t)\end{pmatrix}dt.

(40)

Consequently,

-\rho S_{f}(\rho,x)=u_{1,2}(\rho,x)=-\sin(\rho x)+\int_{-x}^{x}\left(G_{1,2}^{f}(x,t)\cos(\rho t)-G_{1,1}^{f}(x,t)\sin(\rho t)\right)dt.

Thus,

\rho S_{f}(\rho,x)-\sin(\rho x)-\int_{-x}^{x}G_{1,1}^{f}(x,t)\sin(\rho t)dt=-\int_{-x}^{x}G_{1,2}^{f}(x,t)\cos(\rho t)dt.

Now, consider the solution

By Remark

is an even function of
\rho

C_{f}(\rho,x)=\cos(\rho x)+\int_{-x}^{x}G_{2,2}^{\frac{1}{f}}(x,t)\cos(\rho t)dt.

(43)

Finally, from (

\displaystyle e_{f}(\rho,x)

\displaystyle=e^{i\rho x}+\int_{-x}^{x}G_{2,2}^{\frac{1}{f}}(x,t)\cos(\rho t)dt+\int_{-x}^{x}G_{1,1}^{f}(x,t)i\sin(\rho t)dt

\displaystyle=e^{i\rho x}+\int_{-x}^{x}G_{2,2}^{\frac{1}{f}}(x,t)\left(\frac{e^{i\rho t}+e^{-i\rho t}}{2}\right)dt+\int_{-x}^{x}G_{1,1}^{f}(x,t)\left(\frac{e^{i\rho t}-e^{-i\rho t}}{2}\right)dt

If we denote by

Finally, if we denote

Consider the operator defined by

Remark 21

If we consider

Remark 22

The operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}:L^{2}(-\ell,\ell)\rightarrow L^{2}(0,\ell)

Indeed, let
y\in L^{2}(0,\ell)

\tilde{y}
and

\tilde{y}(x)=u(x)+\int_{-x}^{x}\tilde{K}_{f}(x,t)u(t)dt,\quad-\ell<x<\ell,

. Taking

Let
\mathcal{P}[-\ell,\ell]
be the set of polynomial functions in
[-\ell,\ell]
(which is dense in
L^{2}(-\ell,\ell)
and

Proposition 23

The formal powers
\{\varphi_{f}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
form a complete system in
L^{2}(0,\ell)
.

Proof.
Let
y\in L^{2}(0,\ell)
, and take

with

. Given

, choose

\widehat{p}=\mathbf{T}_{f}p\in\operatorname{Span}\{\varphi_{f}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\|y-\widehat{p}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell)}=\|\mathbf{T}_{f}(u-p)\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell)}\leq\|\mathbf{T}_{f}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(-\ell,\ell),L^{2}(0,\ell))}\|u-p\|_{L^{2}(-\ell,\ell)}<\varepsilon.

Now we investigate the relationships between the operator
\mathbf{T}_{f}
, and the one corresponding to the associated Darboux equation
\mathbf{S}_{\frac{1}{f}}
, denoted by
\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}

49

17

\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}[1]=\mathbf{D}_{f}\varphi_{f}^{(0)}=0=\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}[D1]

By linearity, we obtain the relation

\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}p=\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}p^{\prime}\qquad\forall p\in\mathcal{P}[-\ell,\ell].

This is equivalent to stating that

\mathbf{T}_{f}p(x)=f(x)\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}p^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}dt+cf(x),

for some

Let us consider the operator

\mathbf{J}_{f}y(x):=f(x)\int_{0}^{x}\frac{y(t)}{f(t)}dt,\quad y\in L^{2}(0,\ell).

It is clear that
\mathbf{J}_{f}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(0,\ell))

\mathbf{J}_{f}(L^{2}(0,\ell))\subset W^{1,2}(0,\ell)

\mathbf{T}_{f}p=\mathbf{J}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}+p(0)f\qquad\forall p\in\mathcal{P}[-\ell,\ell].

(50)

We extend this relation to
W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell)

Theorem 24

\mathbf{T}_{f}(W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell))\subset W^{1,2}(0,\ell)
and the following relation holds:

\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}y=\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}y^{\prime}\qquad\forall y\in W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell).

(51)

Proof.
Let
y\in W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell)
. Choose a sequence
\{p_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{P}[-\ell,\ell]
such that
p_{n}\rightarrow y
uniformly on
[-\ell,\ell]
and
p_{n}^{\prime}\rightarrow y
in

(see, e.g.,

33

\mathbf{T}_{f},\mathbf{J}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(-\ell,\ell),L^{2}(0,\ell))
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{f}y=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{T}_{f}p=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{J}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}p_{n}^{\prime}+p_{n}(0)f=\mathbf{J}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}y^{\prime}+y(0)f.

Consequently,
\frac{1}{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}y\in W^{1,2}(0,\ell)
, and

\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}y=\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{J}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}+y(0)\mathbf{D}_{f}f=\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}y^{\prime},

as desired. By Remark
4

\mathbf{T}_{f}y\in W^{1,2}(0,\ell)
.

Theorem 25

\mathbf{T}_{f}(W^{2,2}(-\ell,\ell))\subset\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f})

Proof.
Let

\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}y^{\prime}\in W^{1,2}(0,\ell)

\mathbf{T}_{f}y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f})

\displaystyle-\mathbf{S}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}y=\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}\left[\mathbf{D}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}y\right]=\mathbf{D}_{\frac{1}{f}}\left[\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}y^{\prime}\right]=\mathbf{T}_{f}y^{\prime\prime}.

\mathbf{T}_{f}(C[-\ell,\ell])\subset C[0,\ell]

\mathbf{T}_{f}\in\mathcal{B}(C[-\ell,\ell],C[0,\ell])

Theorem 27

The following relations hold:

\mathbf{S}_{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}^{\pm}u=-\mathbf{T}_{f}^{\pm}D^{2}u\qquad\forall u\in W^{2,2}_{\pm}(0,\ell).

\pm u(-x),&-\ell<x<0.\end{cases}

Theorem 32

If

(65)

\displaystyle|\gamma(x)-\gamma(y)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{3}|\gamma_{j}(x)-\gamma_{j}(y)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{y}^{x}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|dt\leq|x-y|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\|\gamma_{j}\|_{L^{p}(J)}\right).

.

Corollary 33

If
q
is defined by a complex Radon measure on
\overline{J}
, then
q
is admissible.

Proof.
Suppose that

, where
\mu
is a complex Radon measure on
\overline{J}
. In this case,

extends to a continuous functional on

is given in terms of a Riemann-Stieltjes integral

, where
\sigma\in BV(\overline{J})
(see
[
26
, Th. 4.4-1]
). Since
\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(J)
vanish in

(q|\phi)=-\int_{J}\phi^{\prime}\sigma
(see
[
12
, Th. 3. 36]
), and

is the distributional derivative of
\sigma\in L^{\infty}(J)
.

We end this section by proposing a method to construct a solution

, based on the SPPS method for Dirac-type systems of the form

, developed in
[
14
]
. In our case,

and

. Applying the method described in
[
14
, Sec. 2]
, we obtain that the solutions

and

admit the series representations:

Lemma 34

Let
f\in W^{1,2}(J)
be a non-vanishing function such that
f(x_{0})=1
for some
x_{0}\in\overline{J}
. There exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{J})
of non-vanishing functions with
f_{n}(x_{0})=1
and such that

f_{n},\frac{1}{f_{n}}\rightarrow f,\frac{1}{f}\text{ in }W^{1,2}(J),\qquad\sigma_{f_{n}}\rightarrow\sigma_{f}\;\text{in }L^{2}(J),\;\;\;\text{and }\;\;\;q_{f_{n}}\rightarrow q_{f}\text{ in }W^{-1,2}(J).

Proof.
Let
\tau_{f}:=\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\in L^{2}(J)
. Take a sequence
\{\psi_{n}\}\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{J})
such that
\psi_{n}\rightarrow\tau_{f}
in
L^{2}(J)
. Define
f_{n}(x)=e^{\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\psi_{n}(s)}
. Then each
f_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{J})
is non-vanish and satisfies
f_{n}(x_{0})=1
. Since
f(x)=e^{\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\tau_{f}(s)ds}
, it is clear that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
and
\frac{1}{f_{n}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{f}
in
W^{1,2}(J)
. For the convergence of
\sigma_{f_{n}}
, observe that

\displaystyle\int_{J}\left|\int_{x_{0}}^{x}(\psi_{n}^{2}(s)-\tau_{f}^{2}(s))ds\right|^{2}dx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{J}\left(\int_{x_{0}}^{x}|\psi_{n}(s)-\tau_{f}(s)||\psi_{n}(s)+\tau_{f}(s)|ds\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|J|\|\psi_{n}-\tau_{f}\|_{L^{2}(J)}\|\psi_{n}+\tau_{f}\|_{L^{2}(J)}\leq|J|M\|\phi_{n}-\tau_{f}\|_{L^{2}(J)},

where
M=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(J)}+\|\tau_{f}\|_{L^{2}(J)}
. Thus,
\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\psi_{n}^{2}\rightarrow\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\tau_{f}^{2}
in
L^{2}(J)
. By (
9
),
\sigma_{f_{n}}\rightarrow\sigma_{f}
in
L^{2}(J)
.

Finally, given
\phi\in W^{1,2}(J)
, we have

|(q_{f_{n}}-q_{f}|\phi)_{W^{1,2}_{0}(J)}|\leq\int_{J}|\sigma_{f_{n}}-\sigma_{f}||\phi^{\prime}|\leq\|\sigma_{f_{n}}-\sigma_{f}\|_{L^{2}(J)}\|\phi\|_{W_{0}^{1,2}(J)}.

Consequently,
\|q_{f_{n}}-q_{f}\|_{W^{-1,2}(J)}\leq\|\sigma_{f_{n}}-\sigma_{f}\|_{L^{2}(J)}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proposition 36

If
\{f_{n}\}\subset C^{\infty}[0,\ell]
is a sequence satisfying the conditions of Lemma

\mathbf{T}_{f_{n}}\rightarrow\mathbf{T}_{f}
in the strong operator topology of

Proof.
Let
K_{f_{n}}(x,t)
be the transmutation kernel associated with
f_{n}

Lemma 37

The set of functions

Neumann series of Bessel functions representation

Following

, we develop the main properties of a Fourier-Legendre series representation for the transmutation kernel

.

Let
\{P_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
be the system of orthogonal Legendre polynomials in
L^{2}(-1,1)
with norm
\|P_{m}\|_{L^{2}(-1,1)}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{2m+1}}
. Fix
x\in(0,\ell]
. Hence
\{P_{m}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
forms an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(-x,x)
, and since
K_{f}(x,\cdot)\in L^{2}(-x,x)
, we have the Fourier-Legendre series expansion

K_{f}(x,t)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}(x)}{x}P_{m}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right),

(71)

where the Fourier-Legendre coefficients are given by

a_{m}(x)=\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{-x}^{x}K_{f}(x,t)P_{m}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)dt\qquad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(72)

Remark 39

If we write
P_{m}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}l_{k,m}z^{k}
, then using the mapping property (
49
), we get

\displaystyle a_{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\int_{-x}^{x}K_{f}(x,t)\sum_{k=0}^{m}l_{k,m}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)^{k}dt

\displaystyle=\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{l_{k,m}}{x^{k}}(\mathbf{T}_{f}[x^{k}]-x^{k})

\displaystyle=\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{l_{k,m}}{x^{k}}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}-P_{m}(1)\right).

Since
P_{m}(1)=1
, this yields

a_{m}(x)=\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{l_{k,m}}{x^{k}}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}(x)-1\right)\qquad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(73)

In particular, using
P_{0}\equiv 1
and
P_{1}(z)=z
, we obtain the formulas

a_{0}(x)=\frac{f(x)-1}{2}\quad\text{and }a_{1}(x)=\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{f(x)}{x}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{f^{2}(t)}-1\right)

(74)

(because
\varphi_{f}^{(1)}(x)=f(x)\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{f^{2}(t)})
. It is worth mentioning that relations of the type (
74
) have proven to be a useful tool in the study of methods for solving inverse problems for regular potentials, where we seek to recover coefficients
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
, and from them the solution
f

[
17
,
18
,
22
]
.

Remark 40

Using the parity of the Legendre polynomials and the definition of the even and odd kernels
K_{f}^{\pm}(x,t)
, we deduce the following Legendre series:

K_{f}^{+}(x,t)=2\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{2m}(x)}{x}P_{2m}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)\quad\text{and}\;\;\;K_{f}^{-}(x,t)=2\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{2m+1}(x)}{x}P_{2m+1}\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)

(75)

Similar to the regular case

10
Relation with a transmutation operator for the Sturm-Liouville equation in impedance form

In this final section, we analyze an impedance equation of the form

\mathbf{L}_{f}v=-\frac{1}{f^{2}}Df^{2}Dv=\lambda v,\quad v\in W^{2,2}(0,\ell),

(89)

where
f\in W^{1,2}(0,\ell)
is a complex-valued nonvanishing function. The function
f
is called the
impedance function
of Eq. (
89
). All concepts associated with the operator
\mathbf{L}_{f}
(solutions, formal powers, etc) will be denoted with a hat. Let
\widehat{e}_{f}(\rho,x)
be the solution of (
89
) satisfying the initial conditions

\widehat{e}_{f}(\rho,0)=1,\qquad(\widehat{e}_{f})^{\prime}(\rho,0)=i\rho.

(90)

The formal powers associated with the impedance
f
are the functions
\{\widehat{\varphi}_{f}^{(k)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
defined by:

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}_{f}^{(0)}\equiv 1,

\displaystyle\quad\widehat{\varphi}_{f}^{(1)}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{f^{2}(t)},

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}_{f}^{(k)}(x)

\displaystyle=k(k-1)\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{f^{2}(t)}\int_{0}^{t}f^{2}(s)\widehat{\varphi}_{f}^{(k-2)}(s)ds,\quad k\geq 2.

In
[
33
, Th. 5]
, the SPPS representation for the solution
\widehat{e}_{f}(\rho,x)
is established:

\widehat{e}_{f}(\rho,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\rho)^{k}\widehat{\varphi}_{f}^{(k)}(x)}{k!}.

The series converges with respect to the variable
x
in the norm of
W^{2,2}(0,\ell)
, and uniformly on compact subsets of the
\rho
-complex plane.

According to
[
33
]
, the impedance equation (
89
) admits a transmutation kernel.

Theorem 45
(
[
33
]
)

There exists a kernel
\widehat{K}_{f}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
such that

\widehat{e}_{f}(\rho,x)=e^{i\rho x}-i\rho\int_{-x}^{x}\widehat{K}_{f}(x,t)e^{i\rho t}dt.

(91)

Furthermore, if we consider the operator

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}v(x)=v(x)-\int_{-x}^{x}\widehat{K}_{f}(x,t)v^{\prime}(t)dt,\quad v\in W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell),

(92)

then the action of
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}
over the powers
\{x^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is given by

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}[x^{k}]=\widehat{\varphi}_{f}^{(k)}(x)\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(93)

and the following transmutation property holds:

\mathbf{L}_{f}\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}v=-\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}v^{\prime\prime}\qquad\forall v\in W^{3,2}(-\ell,\ell).

(94)

The proof of these facts can be found in
[
33
, Sec. 6]
.

Consider the operator
\mathbf{R}_{f}:L^{2}(0,\ell;|f|^{2}dx)\rightarrow L^{2}(0,\ell)
given by
\mathbf{R}_{f}v=fv
. Note that
\mathbf{R}_{f}^{-1}=\mathbf{R}_{\frac{1}{f}}
. Hence the Liouville transform relating
\mathbf{S}_{f}
with
\mathbf{L}_{f}
is given by
\mathbf{R}_{\frac{1}{f}}:\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f})\rightarrow W^{2,2}(0,\ell)
, and

\mathbf{S}_{f}y=\mathbf{R}_{f}\mathbf{L}_{f}\mathbf{R}_{\frac{1}{f}}y\qquad\forall y\in\mathscr{D}_{2}(\mathbf{S}_{f}).

According to Remark
8
, if we set
v=\mathbf{R}_{\frac{1}{f}}e_{f}(\rho,x)
, then
v
is the unique solution of (
89
) satisfying the initial conditions
v(0)=1
and
v^{\prime}(0)=\mathbf{D}_{f}e_{f}(\rho,0)=i\rho
. Thus,
\widehat{e}_{f}(\rho,x)=\mathbf{R}_{f}\widehat{e}_{f}(\rho,x)
. Comparing the SPPS expansions of
e_{f}(\rho,x)
and
\widehat{e}_{f}(\rho,x)
we obtain that

\widehat{\varphi}_{f}^{(k)}(x)=\mathbf{R}_{\frac{1}{f}}\varphi_{f}^{(k)}(x)\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(95)

This relation between the formal powers yields the corresponding relation between the transmutation operators
\mathbf{T}_{f}
and
\widehat{T}_{f}
.

Theorem 46

The following statements hold:

(i)

The operators
\mathbf{T}_{f}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}
are related as follows:

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}v=\mathbf{R}_{\frac{1}{f}}\mathbf{T}_{f}v\qquad\forall v\in W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell).

(96)

(ii)

For every
x\in(0,\ell]
,
\widehat{K}_{f}(x,\cdot)\in W^{1,2}(-x,x)
and

\frac{\partial\widehat{K}_{f}(x,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{f(x)}K_{f}(x,t)\qquad\text{a.e. in }\;\mathcal{T}_{\ell}.

(97)

(iii)

The kernel
\widehat{K}_{f}
satisfies the Goursat conditions

\widehat{K}_{f}(x,x)=1-\frac{1}{f(x)},\qquad\widehat{K}_{f}(x,-x)=0.

(98)

Proof.

(i)

By linearity, relation (
95
) holds in
\mathcal{P}[-\ell,\ell]
. Given
v\in W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell)
, chose a sequence
\{p_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{P}[-\ell,\ell]
such that
p_{n}\rightarrow v
in
W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell)
. Since
p_{n}^{\prime}\rightarrow v^{\prime}
in
L^{2}(-\ell,\ell)
and
\widehat{K}_{f}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})
, hence
\widehat{T}_{f}p_{n}\rightarrow\widehat{T}_{f}v
in
L^{2}(-\ell,\ell)
. On the other hand,
\frac{1}{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}p_{n}\rightarrow\frac{1}{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}v
in
L^{2}(-\ell,\ell)
, because
\mathbf{T}_{f}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(-\ell,\ell),L^{2}(0,\ell))
and
\frac{1}{f}\in C[0,\ell]
. Hence, passing to the limit, we obtain (
96
).

(ii)

By the previous point, the following relation is valid for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}[-\ell,\ell]
:

\phi(x)-\int_{-x}^{x}\widehat{K}_{f}(x,t)\phi^{\prime}(t)dt=\frac{\phi(x)}{f(x)}+\int_{-x}^{x}\frac{K_{f}(x,t)}{f(x)}\phi(t)dt.

(99)

By Theorem
45
, the left-hand side belongs to
W^{2,2}(-\ell,\ell)
and, in particular, is a continuous function. The right-hand side is continuous by Theorem
26
. Hence equality (
99
) holds for all
x\in[0,\ell]
. Fix
x\in(0,\ell]
, and take
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(-x,x)
. Thus,

\int_{-x}^{x}\widehat{K}_{f}(x,t)\phi^{\prime}(t)dt=\int_{-x}^{x}\frac{K_{f}(x,t)}{f(x)}\phi(t)dt.

This implies that
\frac{\partial K_{f}(x,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{K_{f}(x,t)}{f(x)}
. Since
\frac{K_{f}(x,\cdot)}{f(x)}\in L^{2}(-x,x)
, we conclude (ii), as desired.

(iii)

Integration by parts in the left hand side of (
99
) yields

\displaystyle(1-\widehat{K}_{f}(x,x))\phi(x)+\widehat{K}_{f}(x,-x)\phi(-x)+\int_{-x}^{x}\frac{\partial\widehat{K}_{f}(x,t)}{\partial t}\phi(t)dt=\frac{\phi(x)}{f(x)}+\int_{-x}^{x}\frac{K_{f}(x,t)}{f(x)}\phi(t)dt

and by the point (ii)

(1-\widehat{K}_{f}(x,x))\phi(x)+\widehat{K}_{f}(x,-x)\phi(-x)=\frac{\phi(x)}{f(x)}\qquad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}[-\ell,\ell].

This implies (
98
).

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}
admits a continuous extension in

.

(ii)

\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_{f}\in\mathcal{B}(W^{1,2}(-\ell,\ell),W^{1,2}(0,\ell))

Proof.
The statement (i) follows from Theorem
24
(i), from the fact that the right-hand side of (

to

(by Theorem

For (ii), let

fD\left(\frac{1}{f}\mathbf{T}_{f}\phi\right)=\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{f}}\phi^{\prime}

Lemma 48

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and let
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Suppose that there is a sequence
\{g_{n}\}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that:

(i)

g_{n}\rightharpoonup g
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\nabla g_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty
.

Then
g\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
g_{n}\rightharpoonup g
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover, after passing to a subsequence,
g_{n}\rightarrow g
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
g_{n}\rightarrow g
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Proof.
Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
. By hypothesis,
\langle g|\phi_{x_{i}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle g_{n}|\phi_{x_{i}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, where
\phi_{x_{i}}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}},i=1,\dots,N
. Hence

|\langle g|\phi_{x_{i}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\langle g_{n}|\phi_{x_{i}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\langle(g_{n})_{x_{i}}|\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|\leq M\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
M=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\nabla g_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. According to
[
3
, Prop. 9. 3]
, this implies that
g\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Now, for
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\langle g_{x_{i}}|\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle g|\phi_{x_{i}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle g_{n}|\phi_{x_{i}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle(g_{n})_{x_{i}}|\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By the density of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and condition (ii), we conclude that
(g_{n})_{x_{i}}\rightharpoonup g_{x_{i}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then condition (i) implies that
g_{n}\rightharpoonup g
weakly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

Since
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
[
29
, Th. 3.27 and Th. 3.30]
imply that the embedding
W^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is compact. Since
\{g_{n}\}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, we obtain a subsequence, which we denote by simplicity as
\{g_{n}\}
, such that
g_{n}\rightarrow\tilde{g}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. By (i), we have that
\tilde{g}=g
. Finally, by
[
3
, Th. 4.9]
there exists a subsequence such that
g_{n}\rightarrow g
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Lemma 49

Let
\mathcal{H}

\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{H}

and the right-hand side tends to zero as

Remark 52

According to

This can be rewritten as

\langle(\widehat{K}_{f_{n}})_{x}|\overline{f_{n}^{2}\phi_{x}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})}-\langle(\widehat{K}_{f_{n}})_{t}|\overline{f_{n}^{2}\phi_{t}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell})}=0.

Since

uniformly on

f_{n}^{2}\phi_{x},f_{n}^{2}\phi_{t}\rightarrow f^{2}\phi_{x},f^{2}\phi_{t}
strongly in

(\widehat{K}_{f_{n}})_{x},(\widehat{K}_{f_{n}})_{t}\rightharpoonup(\widehat{K}_{f})_{x},(\widehat{K}_{f})_{t}
weakly in

\iint_{\mathcal{T}_{\ell}}f^{2}\left\{(\widehat{K}_{f})_{x}\phi_{x}-(\widehat{K}_{f})_{t}\phi_{t}\right\}=0\qquad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\ell}).

(106)

(107)

11
Conclusions

A new factorization of the distributional Schrödinger operator, based on Polya’s factorization, has been established. Using this factorization, a Volterra integral transmutation operator was constructed, with its kernel represented as a Fourier–Legendre series. This representation allows the solutions of the Schrödinger equation to be expressed as a Neumann series of spherical Bessel functions, with coefficients that can be computed via a simple recursive integration procedure. Furthermore, Polya factorization enables the derivation of an integro-differential transmutation operator for the Sturm–Liouville equation in impedance form. The connection between the Schrödinger and impedance operators via Polya factorization provides a unified framework for their theories and spectral problems. In practical spectral problem solving, NSBF series have proven to be a highly effective tool for the numerical treatment of both direct and inverse Sturm–Liouville problems, applicable to both the impedance and Schrödinger equations
[
19
,
17
,
18
]
.

Effects of MC-100093, GLT-1 enhancer, in mesolimbic brain regions of C57BL/6 mouse exposed to hydrocodone high-dose challenge using comprehensive laboratory monitoring system
Authors: Woonyen Wong, Khokon Kanti Bhowmik, Wayne E Childers, Magid Abou-Gharbia, Youssef Sari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChronic exposure and overdose of opioid cause neurotoxicity through alterations of several neurotransmitters, including glutamate. Upregulating glutamate transporter 1 (GLT-1) has been shown to attenuate the behavioral and neurochemical effects of chronic exposure to drugs of abuse. This study investigated the effects of GLT-1 enhancers—MC-100093 and ceftriaxone—on locomotor activity, respiratory exchange, and glutamate transporters (GLT-1, cystine/glutamate transporter, xCT), brain derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), and neuroinflammatory biomarkers (TNF-α and HMGB1) in nucleus accumbens (NAc) and amygdala (AMY) of C57BL/6 mice exposed to escalated doses of hydrocodone involving high dose. Male C57BL/6 mice were intraperitoneally (i.p.) injected with hydrocodone (20 mg/kg, i.p.) every other day for 13 days and then received a higher dose of hydrocodone (40 mg /kg, i.p.) on day 15. Control group received i.p. injection of saline every other day, and mice in treatment groups received MC-100093 (50 mg/kg, i.p.) or ceftriaxone (200 mg/kg, i.p.) during the last five days. This study used comprehensive laboratory animal monitoring system (CLAMS) to measure mice's respiratory frequency, locomotor activity, oxygen consumption (VO₂), and carbon dioxide production (VCO₂). The high-dose hydrocodone challenge significantly increased VO₂ and VCO₂ while reducing the respiratory exchange ratio (RER). MC-100093 and ceftriaxone attenuated hydrocodone-induced increases in VO₂ and VCO₂; however, restoration of RER occurred exclusively in MC-100093-treated mice. Furthermore, high-dose of hydrocodone downregulated GLT-1, xCT and BDNF expression, while upregulating TNF-α and HMGB1 in both the NAc and AMY. Notably, treatment with either MC-100093 or ceftriaxone significantly mitigated these molecular alterations. These findings suggest that the novel β-lactam compound, MC-100093, holds therapeutic potential in preventing or mitigating opioid-induced neurotoxicity and overdose effects.Keywords: Opioids, Ceftriaxone, MC-100093, GLT-1, xCT, Hydrocodone

Introduction

Chronic exposure and overdose of opioids such as fentanyl can lead to fatal side effects, including respiratory depression (Janczewski et al., 2002, Lalley, 2003, Varga et al., 2020). Exposure to substances of abuse such as morphine, methamphetamine, cocaine, and ethanol dysregulated the glutamatergic system in several brain reward regions, leading to changes in glutamate transporters and receptors (Aboutalebi et al., 2018, Alasmari et al., 2020, Althobaiti et al., 2016, Fujio et al., 2005, Sari et al., 2024, Suto et al., 2010). In addition, previous studies have shown that GLT-1 is downregulated following chronic exposure to cocaine, nicotine, ethanol, and heroin (Alhaddad et al., 2022, Das et al., 2015, Gass et al., 2011, Gipson et al., 2013, Knackstedt et al., 2010, LaLumiere and Kalivas, 2008). Furthermore, others have also shown that increased extracellular glutamate concentrations can lead to neurotoxicity, resulting in increased inflammatory cytokines such as Tumor Necrosis Factor-Alpha (TNF-α) and High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1), which leading ultimately to neuroinflammation (Berríos-Cárcamo et al., 2020, Pan et al., 2016, Qian et al., 2020). There are interactions between glutamate and neuroimmune signaling, which are associated with exposure to drugs of abuse (Gipson et al., 2021). Thus, modulating glutamate homeostasis is critical in attenuating neuroinflammation that might be caused by exposure to drugs of abuse; and glutamate transporters are the target proteins to regulate extracellular glutamate concentrations in the brain. Among these glutamate transporters, glutamate transporter 1 (GLT-1) regulates the majority of extracellular glutamate at the synaptic cleft (Danbolt, 2001).

Chronic exposure to several drugs of abuse, including opioids, ethanol, methamphetamine, and cocaine can lead to downregulation of GLT-1 expression, and this effect is suggested to be associated with increase extracellular glutamate concentrations in target brain reward regions, including the nucleus accumbens (NAc) (Abulseoud et al., 2022, Alasmari et al., 2024, Das et al., 2015, Griffin et al., 2021, Habibi-Asl et al., 2014, Rawls et al., 2008, Sari et al., 2024). Ceftriaxone, beta-lactam antibiotic, was revealed to attenuate GLT-1 downregulation that is caused with exposure to these drugs of abuse (Abulseoud et al., 2022). In addition, studies from our laboratory showed that ceftriaxone treatment attenuated ethanol- and hydrocodone-induced downregulation of GLT-1 and xCT expression in target brain reward regions (Alhaddad et al., 2020, Wong and Sari, 2023, Wong and Sari, 2024). Importantly, recent studies from our laboratory identified a synthetic beta-lactam with non-antibiotic action, MC-100093, as novel drug that has attenuating effect against fentanyl- and morphine-induced downregulation of GLT-1 in the NAc in mice (Alasmari et al., 2024, Sari et al., 2024). MC-100093 is a structural modification of the 6-membered ring and carboxylic acid group of ceftriaxone and has been shown to enhance the expression of GLT-1 (Childers et al., 2020). Compared with ceftriaxone, MC-100093 exhibited more potent glutamate absorption properties, no antimicrobial effects, high water solubility, high stability in liver microsomes, low binding to plasma proteins, and low partitioning into lipid membranes (Knackstedt et al., 2021).

In this study, we tested MC-100093 as a novel synthetic GLT-1 modulator, and ceftriaxone for comparison in mouse model of hydrocodone high-dose challenge. MC-100093 treatment attenuated GLT-1 expression downregulation and increased in inflammatory markers in the brain of animals that were exposed to ethanol, morphine, and cocaine (Alhaddad et al., 2022, Alotaibi et al., 2024, Knackstedt et al., 2021, Sari et al., 2024, Travaglianti et al., 2024). Therefore, we investigated the effects of MC-100093 on the glutamatergic system (GLT-1 and xCT) and neuroinflammatory markers (TNF-α and HMGB1) as well as a brain derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) in the mesolimbic brain regions (NAc and amygdala). These brain regions were selected as they are involved in drug reward, affective regulation, drug-associated neuroadaptation, and glutamate-mediated behavioral responses (Volkow et al., 2019). Additionally, in this study, locomotor activity, oxygen consumption (VO2), carbon dioxide production (VCO2), and respiratory exchange rate (RER) were measured to evaluate functional and physiological responses to the high dose challenge of hydrocodone by using comprehensive laboratory animal monitoring system (CLAMS). GLT-1 and xCT expression were examined as key target proteins regulating glutamate homeostasis (Moran et al., 2005, Peterson and Binder, 2019), and are directly associated with treatment involving MC-100093. BDNF was measured as a marker of neuroplasticity (McAllister et al., 1999), while HMGB1 and TNF-α were assessed as part of the neuroinflammatory signaling (Bhowmik et al., 2026). These endpoints were selected as disrupted glutamate homeostasis may be associated with altered neuronal plasticity and inflammatory responses, which together may contribute to behavioral and physiological outcomes.

Materials and Methods

Animals use approval

Eight-week-old male C57BL/6 mice 25-30 g) were purchased from Jackson Laboratory. Male mice were used in this proof-of-concept study to maintain consistency with prior work from our laboratory and to minimize variability related to sex-dependent hormonal influences; the inclusion of female mice will be addressed in future studies. Mice were housed in a temperature-controlled vivarium (maintained at 21°C) at The University of Toledo. Mice were housed under alternating 12-hour light and 12-hour dark cycles. Mice were acclimated for a minimum of three days prior to starting the experiments. Mice were intraperitoneally (i.p.) injected and handled with care to prevent any distress and minimize any pain. Mice were also monitored every day throughout the study, particularly when hydrocodone treatment was performed. All experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) at The University of Toledo, under protocol number 400155 (approved August 2, 2022), and followed the National Institute of Health’s Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Drugs

MC-100093 was synthesized at Temple University School of Pharmacy’s Moulder Center for Drug Discovery Research in Philadelphia, PA (US patent 9,975,879). MC-100093 was synthesized as a white powder and dissolved in sterile saline at 50 mg/kg (i.p.). Hydrocodone was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Inc (St. Louis, MO, USA) and dissolved in sterile saline at 20 mg/kg and 40 mg/kg (i.p.). Ceftriaxone (Pfizer, USA) was dissolved in sterile saline at 200 mg/kg (i.p.). The selected doses of MC-100093 (50 mg/kg, i.p.) and ceftriaxone (200 mg/kg, i.p.) were based on prior studies demonstrating the efficacy of these compounds in upregulating GLT-1 expression and attenuating neuroinflammatory and behavioral alterations induced by substances of abuse (Alhaddad et al., 2022, Alshehri et al., 2018, Das et al., 2015).

Hydrocodone high-dose challenge dosing procedure

Mice were divided into four experimental groups: (1) Control, (2) Hydrocodone, (3) Hydrocodone–ceftriaxone, and (4) Hydrocodone–MC-100093 groups. Behavioral analyses were conducted using n = 5 mice per group, whereas Western blot analyses were conducted using n = 6 mice per group. The control group was i.p. injected with sterile saline (vehicle) every other day from day 1–15. Groups 2, 3, and 4 were injected with hydrocodone (20 mg/kg, i.p.) on days 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, and 13. On day 15, mice in groups 2, 3, and 4 were challenged with a high dose of hydrocodone (40 mg/kg, i.p.). For the treatment group, group 3 received daily injections of ceftriaxone (200 mg/kg, i.p.) from days 13–17; and group 4 received daily injections of MC-100093 (50 mg/kg, i.p.) from days 11–17. In addition, groups 1 and 2 were injected (i.p.) with the same volume of sterile saline (vehicle) from days 13–17 (Supplementary Figure 1).

Behavioral testing

Mice were placed in the Minispec NMR, which serves to measure the lean and fat mass of the mice. For behavioral testing, mice were housed individually in a CLAMS (Columbus Instruments, Columbus, Ohio) with free access to food and water from day 13 to day 17. The CLAMS is equipped with a zirconia-based oxygen sensor and a high-speed single-beam non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) carbon dioxide sensor that provides measurements of oxygen and carbon dioxide flow in each mouse chamber. In addition, CLAMS utilizes IR photocell technology for triple-axis detection of mouse activity. The system can detect mouse movement in length, width, and height (jumping or standing) within the chamber. Locomotor activity was quantified by the CLAMS system as beam-break counts. Hourly activity counts were recorded automatically, and total daily activity was calculated as the sum of all counts collected during each 24-hour monitoring period. To minimize day-to-day variability, total activity counts obtained over the three consecutive recording days were averaged for each animal. Oxygen consumption (VO2), carbon dioxide production (VCO2), and respiratory exchange rate (RER) were calculated and obtained from Oxymax-CLAMS throughout these five days. CLAMS was used to determine locomotor activity, VO2, VCO2, and RER in mice exposed to a hydrocodone high-dose challenge paradigm. CLAMS is often used in metabolomic studies to determine heat production, VO2, and VCO2, which can then be used to measure energy expenditure via calorimetry (Menzies et al., 2021).

Brain tissue extraction

Mice were euthanized by CO2 inhalation on Day 18. Brains were isolated and frozen immediately on dry ice and stored at −80°C. The NAc (core and shell), and AMY (central amygdala, basomedial amygdala, and basolateral amygdala) were extracted using a cryostat machine (Leica CM1950). These brain regions were selected using the Brain Mouse Atlas (Ortiz et al., 2020). All the samples were stored at −80°C for subsequent Western blot analyses.

Western blot analyses

Brain samples were homogenized using ice-cold lysis buffer (50 mM Tris–HCl, 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 0.5% NP-40, 1% Triton, 0.1% SDS) with phosphatase and protease inhibitors. The homogenate was then centrifuged at 13,200 rpm for 15 min at 4°C, and the supernatant was stored at −80°C for Western blotting. Protein concentration in each tissue sample was quantified using a detergent-compatible protein assay (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA), using bovine serum albumin as the standard. An equal amount of protein from each sample was mixed with laemmili dye, and the mixtures were loaded onto 10% Tris-glycerine gel to separate the protein using electrophoresis. Proteins were then transferred from gels to a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane. Subsequently, the PVDF membranes were blocked with 5% fat-free milk in Tris-buffered saline with Tween 20 (TBST) at room temperature for 30 min. Membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies: rabbit anti-xCT (1:1000, Abcam ab125186), rabbit anti-GLT-1 (1:5000, Abcam ab205248), rabbit anti-BDNF (1:1000, Abcam ab108319), rabbit anti-TNF-α (1:1000, Abcam ab9739), and rabbit anti-HMGB1 (1:1000, Abcam ab18256). Mouse anti-β-tubulin (1:1000, BioLeagend, San Diego, CA, USA) was used as a control loading protein. On the following day, membranes were washed five times with TBST and incubated with the appropriate horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (1:4000) for 60 min. The membranes were then washed with TBST and dried for further analysis. The dried membranes were incubated with chemiluminescent reagents (Super Signal West Pico, Perce Inc., Appleton, WI, USA) for 1–2 min. Digitized blot images were developed using the ChemiDoc imaging system (Bio-rad Laboratories). The density of each specific band was quantified using ImageJ software (Version 1.53t 24). The control group was reported as 100% to measure the changes in the expression of the protein of interest in the NAc (core and shell) and AMY (central amygdala, basomedial amygdala, and basolateral amygdala) as described in our previous studies (Alhaddad et al., 2022).

Western blot images presented in the main figures were uniformly processed and cropped solely to highlight bands of interest. For TNF-α, immunoreactive bands occasionally appeared as a doublet, which is consistent with the detection of precursor and mature forms of TNF-α reported in previous studies. Band quantification was performed consistently across all experimental groups using identical exposure, normalization, and analysis parameters.

Statistical analyses

GraphPad Prism (version 10.1.2) was used for all statistical analyses. Behavioral data obtained from Oxymax-CLAMS were analyzed using n = 5 mice per group due to occasional technical artifacts inherent to metabolic chamber measurements. Western blot analyses were conducted using n = 6 mice per group. Data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons test. Results are presented as means ± S.E.M., and statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge, ceftriaxone, and MC-100093 on the Locomotion Activity

To evaluate the effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge, and treatment with ceftriaxone and MC-100093 on the x activity, x ambulatory, and z activity, we assessed the locomotion activity of mice in the CLAMS. As shown in Fig. 1, there was a significant difference in x activity (F3,16 = 6.624, p < 0.01, Fig. 1A, n = 5/group) among all tested groups. However, no significant difference was observed in x ambulatory (F3,16 = 2.952, p = 0.0642, Fig. 1B, n = 5/group) and z activity (F3,16 = 0.08214, p = 0.9688, Fig. 1C, n = 5/group). Tukey’s post hoc test analyses demonstrated that x activity (p < 0.05, Fig. 1A) significantly increased in the hydrocodone group compared to the control group. Additionally, treatment with ceftriaxone normalized x activity (p < 0.01, Fig. 1A) as compared to the hydrocodone group. In contrast, MC-100093 treatment did not significantly attenuate hydrocodone-induced hyperlocomotion at the dose tested. In addition, no significant difference was detected among all groups in x ambulatory (Fig. 1B) and z activity (Fig. 1C).

Effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge, ceftriaxone, and MC-100093 on oxygen consumption, carbon dioxide production, and respiratory exchange ratio

To determine the effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge, ceftriaxone, and MC-100093 on respiratory frequency, we measured oxygen consumption, carbon dioxide production, and respiratory exchange ratio using CLAMS in mice. One-way ANOVA revealed a significant difference in oxygen consumption (F3,16 = 57.31, p < 0.0001, Fig. 2A, n = 5/group), carbon dioxide production (F3,16 = 105.1, p < 0.0001, Fig. 2B, n = 5/ group), and respiratory exchange ratio (F3,16 = 17.48, p < 0.0001, Fig. 2C, n = 5/group). Tukey’s post hoc analyses showed a significant increase in oxygen consumption (p < 0.0001, Fig. 2A) and carbon dioxide production (p < 0.0001, Fig. 2B) in the hydrocodone group as compared to the control group. Treatment with ceftriaxone (p < 0.0001) and MC-100093 (p < 0.05) significantly attenuated the hydrocodone-induced increase in oxygen consumption as compared to the hydrocodone group (Fig. 2A). In addition, the analysis also showed that carbon dioxide production significantly decreased in groups treated with ceftriaxone (p < 0.0001) and MC-100093 (p < 0.05) as compared to hydrocodone-treated group (Fig. 2B). Moreover, the effects of ceftriaxone or MC-100093 on respiratory exchange ratio in mice that were exposed to hydrocodone paradigm were examined by comparing the ratio of oxygen consumption to carbon dioxide production in mice. Post hoc analyses revealed a significant decrease in the respiratory exchange ratio of the hydrocodone group (p < 0.05, Fig. 2C) compared to the control group. Hydrocodone-induced downregulation of respiratory exchange ratio was significantly increased in the group treated with MC-100093 (p < 0.001, Fig. 2C). Importantly, only MC-100093 significantly restored RER compared to hydrocodone-treated mice, whereas ceftriaxone had no significant effect on RER. A significant difference was detected between the hydrocodone-ceftriaxone and hydrocodone-MC-100093 group (p < 0.001, Fig. 2C).

Effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge, ceftriaxone, and MC-100093 on xCT expression in the NAc and AMY

We next quantified the xCT expression in the mesolimbic brain regions following hydrocodone high-dose challenge. We found that hydrocodone high-dose challenge significantly decreased xCT expression in the NAc (F3,20 = 11.32, p < 0.0001, Fig. 3A, n = 6/group) and AMY (F3,20 = 13.21, p < 0.0001, Fig. 3B, n = 6/group). In addition, Post hoc test for multiple comparisons showed a significant decrease in xCT expression in the NAc (p < 0.01, Fig. 3A) and AMY (p < 0.05, Fig. 3B) of the hydrocodone group compared to the control group. Treatment with ceftriaxone significantly attenuated hydrocodone-induced decreased in xCT expression in the NAc (p < 0.01, Fig. 3A) and AMY (p < 0.001, Fig. 3B) as compared to the hydrocodone group. Furthermore, the analysis revealed that MC-100093 treatment significantly increased xCT expression in the NAc (p < 0.001, Fig. 3A) and AMY (p < 0.0001, Fig. 3B) as compared to the hydrocodone group.

Effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge, Ceftriaxone, and MC-100093 on GLT-1 expression in the NAc and AMY

We investigated the effects of ceftriaxone or MC-100093 on the expression of GLT-1 in the mesolimbic brain regions in mice exposed to hydrocodone high-dose challenge. One-way ANOVA revealed a significant change in GLT-1 expression in the NAc (F3,20 = 21.62, p < 0.0001, Fig. 4A, n = 6/group) and AMY (F3,20 = 23.01, p < 0.0001, Fig. 4B, n = 6/group). Multiple comparison analyses revealed that exposure to hydrocodone high dose challenge decreased GLT-1 expression in the NAc (p < 0.0001) and AMY (p < 0.05) compared to the control group (Fig. 4A and B, respectively). Treatment with ceftriaxone normalized GLT-1 expression in the NAc (p < 0.0001, Fig. 4A) and AMY (p < 0.0001, Fig. 4B). In addition, MC-100093 attenuated hydrocodone-induced downregulation of GLT-1 in the NAc (p < 0.0001, Fig. 4A) and AMY (p < 0.0001, Fig. 4B). Significant changes were detected between control and hydrocodone-ceftriaxone (p < 0.05), and control and hydrocodone-MC-100093 groups (p < 0.01) in AMY (Fig. 4B).

Effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge, ceftriaxone, and MC-100093 on TNF-α expression in the NAc and AMY

We further quantified the expression of TNF-α in the mesolimbic brain regions following hydrocodone high-dose challenge. Western blot data analyses showed significant differences in TNF-α expression in the NAc (F3,20= 14.06, p < 0.0001, Fig. 6A, n = 6/group) and AMY (F3, 20 = 21.05, p < 0.0001, Fig. 6B, n = 6/group) among all tested groups. No significant difference was detected between the control, hydrocodone-ceftriaxone, and hydrocodone-MC-100093 groups in the NAc (Fig. 6A). However, significant differences were observed between the control and hydrocodone-MC-100093 groups in the AMY (p < 0.01, Fig. 6B).

Effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge, ceftriaxone, and MC-100093 on HMGB1 expression in the NAc and AMY

To evaluate the effects of ceftriaxone and MC-100093 on HMGβ1 expression in the NAc and AMY, we assessed the HMGB1 expression in the mesolimbic brain regions following hydrocodone high-dose challenge. One-way ANOVA indicated that there were significant differences in HMGB1 expression in the NAc (F3,20 = 5.800, p < 0.01, Fig. 7A, n = 6/group) and AMY (F3,20 = 21.93, p < 0.01, Fig. 7B, n = 6/group). Furthermore, hydrocodone high-dose challenge increased HMGB1 expression in the NAc (p < 0.05) and AMY (p < 0.01) (Figs. 7A and 7B), respectively compared to the control. In addition, the post-hoc test showed that treatment with ceftriaxone reduced HMGB1 expression significantly in the NAc (p < 0.01, Fig. 7A) and AMY (p < 0.0001, Fig. 7B). Notably, MC-100093 attenuated HMGB1 expression in the NAc (p < 0.05, Fig. 7A) and AMY (p < 0.0001, Fig. 7B) compared to the hydrocodone high-dose challenge group.

Discussion

In the present study, the major findings are that the novel beta-lactam compound, MC-100093, which is considered as GLT-1 modulator significantly attenuated dysregulation of the respiratory exchange ratio that was caused by hydrocodone high-dose challenge. Thus, MC-100093 significantly attenuated high dose of hydrocodone-induced decrease in the respiratory exchange ratio, GLT-1, xCT, and BDNF as well as attenuated hydrocodone-induced increase in inflammatory markers (TNF-α and HMGB1) and decreased BDNF in the NAc and AMY. Hydrocodone high-dose challenge was performed initially through administering a series of moderate-dose of hydrocodone (20 mg/kg, i.p.) followed by a high-dose of hydrocodone (40 mg/kg, i.p.) to determine whether this hydrocodone exposure regimen may lead to ventilatory dysfunction. We found that hydrocodone high-dose challenge resulted in increased oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production and these effects were attenuated by MC-100093 (50 mg/kg, i.p.) and ceftriaxone (200 mg/kg, i.p.) treatments. In accordance, opioid exposure may result in concentration-dependent increases in hypoventilation (rapid and shallow breathing) and respiratory irregularities (Bouillon et al., 2003, Pattinson, 2008). When the hydrocodone dose is increased, ventilatory depression can lead to respiratory depression. Our study was first to demonstrate the effectiveness of MC-100093, but not ceftriaxone, in attenuating the decrease in respiratory exchange ratio induced by high dose of hydrocodone. Taken together, our results report that MC-100093 may have potential pharmacotherapeutic effect to reduce high dose of hydrocodone-induced respiratory irregularities other than ceftriaxone. Although both MC-100093 and ceftriaxone normalized glutamatergic and neuroinflammatory markers, only ceftriaxone attenuated hyperlocomotion. The underlying pharmacological mechanisms regulating respiratory responses to opioids are unclear. Therefore, future studies are needed to determine the pharmacological actions that may mediate glutamate for regulating respiratory controls (Dutschmann and Dick, 2012, Gray et al., 1999, Varga et al., 2020).

This study revealed that hydrocodone high-dose challenge induced increases in locomotor activity, which is consistent with our recent studies showing that exposure to opioids (hydrocodone, morphine and fentanyl) overdose can lead to hyperlocomotion activity in mice (Alasmari et al., 2024, Sari et al., 2024, Wong and Sari, 2024). Our results are consistent with this latter study showing that treatment with ceftriaxone attenuated hydrocodone-induced hyperlocomotion activity. However, in the current study, the hyperlocomotion activity induced by hydrocodone high-dose challenge was not attenuated by MC-100093 as compared with ceftriaxone. This may be due to the use of a lower dose of MC-100093 (50 mg/kg, i.p.), which is one of the limitations of this study. Therefore, future studies are warranted to examine the effects of a higher dose of MC-100093 (100 mg/kg, i.p.) on the respiratory exchange ratio and locomotion activity.

Evidence suggests that excessive extracellular glutamate concentrations at the synaptic cleft are considered responsible for the neurotoxicity associated with opioid exposure (Kofke et al., 2000). Notably, GLT-1 regulates the majority of extracellular glutamate concentrations in the brain (Danbolt, 2001) and xCT is co-localized with GLT-1 in astrocytes to regulate the excess of glutamate. Recent study from our laboratory revealed that hydrocodone high-dose challenge downregulated the expression of GLT-1 as well as xCT in the NAc, and ceftriaxone attenuated these effects (Wong and Sari, 2024). Furthermore, studies from our laboratory and others have shown that MC-100093 increased GLT-1 expression in animals exposed to ethanol and cocaine (Alhaddad et al., 2022, Knackstedt et al., 2021). Our results showed that both MC-100093 and ceftriaxone treatments normalized hydrocodone high-dose challenge-induced downregulation of GLT-1 and xCT in the NAc and AMY. Together, previous reports and the current findings provide strong evidence that MC-100093 mediates substance-of-abuse–induced dysregulation of glutamate receptors and transporters in mesolimbic brain regions.

In this study, we investigated the effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge and GLT-1 enhancers in the expression of BDNF in the mesolimbic brain regions. It is noteworthy that BDNF is closely associated with the regulation of neuronal and behavioral plasticity (Leal et al., 2014). BDNF is a neurotrophic factor secreted by neurons and microglia, and plays a key role in the proliferation, development, and viability of the peripheral and central nervous systems (Batchelor et al., 1999, McAllister et al., 1999). In this study, we found that BDNF expression was downregulated in the NAc and AMY following hydrocodone high-dose challenge. This is consistent with previous studies suggesting that decreased BDNF expression may lead to neuronal impairment caused by chronic morphine exposure (Akbarian et al., 2002, Koo et al., 2012, Koo et al., 2015). Other studies have reported that chronic exposure to morphine reduced BDNF expression in the ventral tegmental area (VTA), whereas inhibition of BDNF signaling in the NAc enhanced the rewarding effects of morphine (Alghamdi and Alshehri, 2021, Rezamohammadi et al., 2020). Our results showed that both MC-100093 and ceftriaxone attenuated hydrocodone high-dose challenge-induced decrease in BDNF expression in the mesolimbic brain regions. Similarly, a recent study from our laboratory has shown that MC-100093 treatment reversed the ethanol-induced downregulation of BDNF expression in the NAc. Future studies are warranted to elucidate the mechanism of MC-100093 mediating GLT-1 upregulation on the upregulation of BDNF through different cytokines such as IL4 and IL-1Ra.

In this study, we further examined the effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge and GLT-1 modulators in the expression of TNF-α. TNF-α is a pro-inflammatory cytokine and an initiator of downstream inflammatory signaling pathways and its activation can trigger a series of inflammatory responses in the brain. Overexpression of TNF-α impairs memory, learning (Baune et al., 2008, Beste et al., 2010), and synaptic plasticity (Beattie et al., 2002). Moreover, elevated levels of TNF-α have been found in Alzheimer's disease (Álvarez et al., 2007), Parkinson's disease (Mogi et al., 1994), traumatic brain injury (Zhou et al., 2021), alcohol use disorder (Travaglianti et al., 2024), and opioid use disorder (Tsai et al., 2016). In this study, we demonstrated that TNF-α expression was upregulated with hydrocodone high-dose challenge, leading to enhanced neuroinflammatory processes in the mesolimbic brain regions. Treatment with MC-100093 or ceftriaxone was associated with attenuation of hydrocodone-overdose-induced increase in TNF-α expression in the mesolimbic brain regions. In accordance with other studies from our laboratory, MC-100093 has the potential to reduce neuroinflammation caused by exposure to substances of abuse such as ethanol and morphine (Sari et al., 2024, Travaglianti et al., 2024).

Finally, this study investigated the effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge and GLT-1 modulators in the expression of HMGB1 in the mesolimbic brain regions. HMGB1 is a proinflammatory cytokine released by cells during inflammation and injury (Andersson and Tracey, 2011). HMGB1 is an endogenous TLR4 agonist that is considered a key player in mediating neuroinflammatory processes in a variety of pathophysiological conditions, including arthritis, inflammatory diseases, and chronic pain (Agalave and Svensson, 2014, Hazlett et al., 2021, Schierbeck et al., 2011, Zhang et al., 2020). HMGB1 can induce inflammation by binding to the receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE) or Toll-like receptors 2 and 4 (TLR 2 and 4), activating a signaling cascade leading to the secretion of TNF-α and IL-6 (Agnello et al., 2002, Hori et al., 1995, Park et al., 2004, van Beijnum et al., 2008). In the current study, we found that high dose of hydrocodone exposure increased the expression of HMGB1 in the NAc and AMY. Notably, previous studies from our lab and others have shown that the expression of HMGB1 and RAGE are increased in chronic ethanol-exposed rats (Alasmari et al., 2020, Bhowmik et al., 2026), high dose of hydrocodone-exposed mice (Wong and Sari, 2024), chronic morphine-exposed rats (Qian et al., 2020), and in animal models of neuropathic pain (Grace et al., 2018). Similar to TNF-α, previous studies from our laboratory have shown that MC-100093 attenuated ethanol-induced increases in HMGB1 expression in the NAc subregions of alcohol-preferring rats (Bhowmik et al., 2026, Travaglianti et al., 2024). In accordance with this, we have shown that both MC-100093 and ceftriaxone attenuated high dose of hydrocodone-induced increase in HMGB1 expression in the mesolimbic brain regions. It is important to note that in the present study, we focused on TNF-α and HMGB1 primarily due to their roles as key mediators of neuroinflammatory responses. However, future studies are warranted to determine the effects of other proinflammatory cytokines on glutamatergic neurotransmission and signaling involving hydrocodone high-dose challenge.

This study examined target signaling pathways involved in glutamatergic regulation, neuroplasticity, and neuroinflammation. Previous studies have shown that pro-inflammatory cytokines can modulate GLT-1 expression and function, thereby contributing to neuroinflammatory processes (Zumkehr et al., 2018). In particular, TNF-α has been reported to alter GLT-1 expression, resulting in impaired glutamate uptake and disruption of glutamate homeostasis (Sitcheran et al., 2005). Such alterations may increase extracellular glutamate concentrations, promoting excitotoxicity, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammatory responses (Fernández-Rodríguez et al., 2023). Furthermore, glutamatergic neurotransmission is closely linked to BDNF signaling, with glutamate influencing BDNF expression and release, while BDNF modulates glutamatergic synaptic plasticity (Cutuli and Sampedro-Piquero, 2022). Collectively, based on these previous studies and our findings, we suggest that restoration of GLT-1 expression may have broader downstream effects on both neuroplasticity and inflammation-related pathways, and provide a potential mechanistic framework for the molecular changes observed in the present study.

It is important to note that in the current study, we only investigated the effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge and MC-100093 treatment on modulating glutamatergic and neuroinflammatory systems in male mice. However, it is unclear about the dysregulation of the glutamatergic and neuroinflammatory systems induced by exposure to high dose of hydrocodone in female mice. Therefore, future studies are warranted to test the effects of hydrocodone high-dose challenge and GLT-1 modulator, MC-100093, in female mice. Another limitation is that pharmacological molecular analyses were restricted to the NAc and AMY, which were selected for their important roles in reward processing, emotional regulation, and drug-induced neuroadaptations. Future studies are warranted to examine motor- and respiratory-control brain regions, including basal ganglia motor circuits and brainstem respiratory centers, to better define the neural mechanisms underlying the behavioral and metabolic/respiratory changes observed in this study.

Additionally, although high dose of opioids is typically characterized by respiratory depression, the indirect calorimetry measures used in this study do not directly assess respiratory rate or ventilation. VO₂ and VCO₂ primarily reflect whole-body metabolic activity, while RER indicates metabolic substrate utilization; therefore, the observed increases in VO₂ and VCO₂ after hydrocodone administration provide only indirect evidence and should not be interpreted as direct measures of respiratory function. Future studies incorporating direct assessments of ventilation, respiratory frequency, oxygen saturation, or blood gases are needed to clarify the relationship between hydrocodone-induced metabolic changes and respiratory depression.

Conclusion

This study is the first to reveal the efficacy of a novel synthetic beta-lactam non-antibiotic, MC-100093, in attenuating hydrocodone high-dose challenge-induced decrease in respiratory exchange ratio. While ceftriaxone improved locomotor outcomes, MC-100093 selectively improved metabolic respiratory parameters without significantly altering locomotor activity at the tested dose. These novel findings provide strong evidence that MC-100093 selectively attenuates hydrocodone high-dose challenge–induced respiratory dysregulation and neurobiological alterations, while ceftriaxone, but not MC-100093, attenuated excessive motor activity. This effect is likely mediated by reversing hydrocodone high-dose challenge-induced neuroinflammatory activation and downregulation of GLT-1 and xCT expression in the mesolimbic brain regions. Together, these findings strongly support the notion that MC-100093 could be developed as a potential therapeutic candidate for the treatment of opioid overdose.

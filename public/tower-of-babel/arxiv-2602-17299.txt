On the local-global principle for twists of abelian varieties
Nirvana Coppola, Lorenzo La Porta, Matteo Longo

Abstract
. This paper investigates the existence of a local-global principle for certain twists of abelian varieties defined over number fields. Our main focus is to determine when, for m m a positive integer, locally m m -atic twists of an abelian variety A A over a number field K K are globally m m -atic. We define and study a “Tate–Shafarevich cohomology set” that governs the obstruction to the local-global principle for m m -atic twists. We prove that, under some mild assumptions, this set is finite, and give criteria for it to be trivial, i.e. for the local-global principle to be satisfied.

Introduction

It is a natural question whether, given a set of local twists of an abelian variety over a number field satisfying certain constraints, these are induced by a global twist satisfying the same constraints.
In this paper, we investigate this problem in the case when the twists are given by some cyclic group of the automorphisms of the abelian variety.

To explain our results, we introduce some notation. Let
K
be either a
p
-adic field or a number field and write
G_{K}\coloneqq\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
for the absolute Galois group of
K
, where
\overline{K}
is a fixed algebraic closure of
K
.
Let
A
and
B
be two abelian varieties defined over
K
. We say that
B
is a
twist
(up to isogeny) of
A
if there exists a finite extension
F
of
K
such that
A_{F}
and
B_{F}
are isogenous, where
A_{F}
, respectively
B_{F}
, denotes the base change of
A
, respectively
B
, to
F
. Write
D_{\overline{K}}\coloneqq\End(A_{\overline{K}})\otimes_{\mathds{Z}}\mathds{Q}
, where

\End(A_{\overline{K}})
is the endomorphism ring of
A_{\overline{K}}
. The twists of
A
are in bijection with
H^{1}(G_{K},D_{\overline{K}}^{\times})
(if
D_{\overline{K}}^{\times}
is non-abelian, we follow the conventions of
[
9
, §5]
concerning non-abelian cohomology; see Section
1
for details). Let
m\geq 1
be an integer, denote by
\mu_{m}
the group of
m
-th roots of unity in
\overline{\mathds{Q}}
and let
\zeta_{m}\in\mu_{m}
be a primitive
m
-th root of unity. Assume that
D_{\overline{K}}
is a
\mathds{Q}(\zeta_{m})
-algebra and that the subgroup
\mu_{m}\subseteq D_{\overline{K}}^{\times}
is
G_{K}
-stable; see Assumption
1.1
.
We say that
B
is an
m
-atic twist
(up to isogeny) of
A
, if
B
is a twist that corresponds to an element of
\im(H^{1}(G_{K},\mu_{m})\to H^{1}(G_{K},D_{\overline{K}}^{\times}))
.

Assume now that
K
is a number field and write
K_{v}
for the completion of
K
at any place
v
. We say that
B
, a twist of
A
, is
locally
m
-atic
(up to isogeny), if
B_{K_{v}}
is an
m
-atic twist of
A_{K_{v}}
, for all finite places
v
of
K
. We say that
(A,m)
satisfies the
local-global principle
if the following implication holds for any abelian variety
B
over
K
:
if
B
is a locally
m
-atic twist of
A
, then
B
is an
m
-atic twist of
A
.

By Faltings’s theorem,
[
4
]
, the local-global principle holds for
m=1
and all

. The case
m=2
was studied in
[
5
,
1
]
. In that setting, the local-global principle holds for abelian varieties of dimension at most
3
. On the other hand, there are counterexamples in dimension
4
and, assuming that
A
is geometrically simple, in dimension
12
.
In this work, we mainly focus on positive results in the case when
m\geq 3
is odd.

In analogy with the arithmetic theory of abelian varieties, to study local-global properties, we introduce the two cohomological objects
\Sel_{m}(G_{K},D_{\overline{K}}^{\times})
and
\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}(G_{K},D_{\overline{K}}^{\times})
, which we call
Selmer
and
Tate–Shafarevich
sets, respectively (see Definition
1.4
). These sets are designed to control the failure of the local-global principle in a way similar to the
well-known case of rational points on abelian varieties. We also remark that when
D_{\overline{K}}^{\times}
is commutative, these are
groups, but in the more general case of non-commutative
D_{\overline{K}}^{\times}
, these are just pointed sets.
The failure of the local-global principle for
(A,m)
is equivalent to the non-vanishing of

\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}(G_{K},D_{\overline{K}}^{\times})
, which we investigate combining cohomological techniques and results from the theory of abelian varieties.

Our first result, Theorem
2.9
, proves, conditional to some technical assumptions, the finiteness of
\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}(G_{K},D_{\overline{K}}^{\times})
. We believe this theorem should hold unconditionally (at the moment, we are not aware of counterexamples to the hypotheses of Theorem
2.9
). This finiteness result essentially follows from Faltings’s theorem.

We now explain some positive results on the local-global principle for
(A,m)
.
Denote by
\End(A)
the endomorphism ring of
A
, and set
D_{K}\coloneqq\End(A)\otimes_{\mathds{Z}}\mathds{Q}
. We mainly work with
A
geometrically simple, in one of the two extremal cases: either
D_{K}^{\times}\cap\mu_{m}
is trivial, Section
3
, or
\mu_{m}\subset D_{K}^{\times}
, Section
4
. We mention here two significant examples of what we prove.

Theorem A

(see Theorem
3.4
)
.

Let
A
be geometrically simple, and assume that
D_{\overline{K}}
is commutative. For all odd
m\geq 3
such that
D_{K}^{\times}\cap\mu_{m}
is trivial,
(A,m)
satisfies the local-global principle.

Theorem B

(see Corollary
5.1
)
.

Let
A
be geometrically simple of dimension
g
, such that
\mu_{m}\subseteq D_{K}^{\times}
. For all
g\leq 8
and odd

,
(A,m)
satisfies the local-global principle.

Theorem B is mainly a consequence of a more general result, Theorem
4.2
, in which we show that, under the condition
\mu_{m}\subseteq D_{K}^{\times}
(as in Theorem B), the local-global principle for
(A,m)
holds when

is prime to the integer

, where

is the center of

We do not have general results when

We fix a

From now on, we assume that

.

Let

, where

.

-atic twists at

Remark 1.3
.

By Faltings’s theorem
[
4
]
, for

In particular, for all

of

is semisimple and

and

are isomorphic as Galois representations if and only if they are when restricted to

is injective. In fact, if

, then, taking

, we know that

is isogenous to

, for all

, implying that all the local Galois representations are isomorphic, so

and

are isogenous over

, hence
\chi=1
.

(1)

1.2.
Representation-theoretic description of twists

Write
g=\dim(A)
. Let
B
be the twist of
A
by
\chi\in H^{1}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
and pick
c\colon G_{K}\to D_{L}^{\times}
a cocycle representing
\chi
. Consider
\ell
a prime such that
v\nmid\ell
. Write
\rho_{A,\ell},\rho_{B,\ell}\colon G_{K}\to\GL_{2g}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
for the Galois representations corresponding to the rational Tate modules

, resp.
V_{\ell}(B)
, of
A
, resp.

. We also have the natural map
\psi_{A,\ell}\colon(D_{L}\otimes_{\mathds{Q}}\mathds{Q}_{\ell})^{\times}\to\GL_{2g}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})
, which is injective. In particular, by definition of twist,
\rho_{B,\ell}(\sigma)=\psi_{A,\ell}(c(\sigma))\rho_{A,\ell}(\sigma)
, for
\sigma\in G_{K}
.
One can check that replacing

with another element in the class

will lend a conjugate representation.

If
\chi\in\Sel_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
, for each
v\in\Sigma_{K}
, we have that

\rho_{B,\ell}(\sigma)=\psi_{A,\ell}(c_{v}(\sigma))\rho_{A,\ell}(\sigma)
, for
\sigma\in G_{v}
and some
[c_{v}]\in H^{1}(G_{K_{v}},\mu_{m})
. Notice that, by Faltings’s theorem,
\psi_{A,\ell}((D\otimes_{\mathds{Q}}\mathds{Q}_{\ell})^{\times})
is precisely the centralizer of
\rho_{A,\ell}(G_{K})
in
\GL_{2g}(\mathds{Q}_{\ell})=\Aut(V_{\ell}(A))
. Similarly,
\rho_{A,\ell}(G_{L})
is the centralizer of
\psi_{A,\ell}((D_{L}\otimes_{\mathds{Q}}\mathds{Q}_{\ell})^{\times})
. By definition of
\Sel_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
, for any
v\in\Sigma_{K}
, there is some
b_{v}\in D_{L}^{\times}
such that, for any
\sigma\in G_{v}
,
b_{v}c(\sigma)\sigma(b_{v}^{-1})\in\mu_{m}
. If
G_{K_{v}}
acts trivially on
\mu_{m}
, we can take

to be a homomorphism of groups
G_{K_{v}}\to\mu_{m}
.

2.
Results on the local-global principle

We start by proving a simple but useful case.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
D_{L}=D
, that is, all geometric endomorphisms of
A
are defined over
K
. Then, the local-global principle holds for
(A,m)
for all
m
.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in\Sel_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})\subseteq H^{1}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})=\Hom(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})/D_{L}^{\times}
. In particular, we can take a representative
c\in\Hom(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
. Since
\chi\in\ker(\psi)
, for all
v\in\Sigma_{K}
, there is some
b_{v}\in D_{L}^{\times}
such that, for any
\sigma\in G_{K_{v}}
,
b_{v}c(\sigma)\sigma(b_{v}^{-1})=b_{v}c(\sigma)b_{v}^{-1}\in\mu_{m}
. Since
\mu_{m}\subseteq Z(D_{L})
, we have
c(\sigma)\in\mu_{m}
. By Chebotarev’s density theorem and continuity of
c
, we deduce that
c(\sigma)\in\mu_{m}
for all
\sigma\in G_{K}
. This shows that
\Sel_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})=\mathrm{Twist}_{m}(A,K)
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The inflation map identifies
\ker(\varphi)\cap\ker(\xi)
with
\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
.

Proof.

By Lemma

, we have an inclusion
\mathrm{inf}_{G/G_{K}}(\ker(\varphi)\cap\ker(\xi))\subseteq\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})

As a consequence of Proposition

If

(A,m)
.

Proof.

If

∎

Corollary 2.7
.

If
\inf_{G/G_{K}}\colon H^{2}(G,\mu_{m})\to H^{2}(G_{K},\mu_{m})
is injective, then the inflation map identifies
\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
with

\ker(\varphi)\cap\im(H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times})\longrightarrow H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times}/\mu_{m})).

In particular, if
H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times})=1

(1)

\mu_{m}^{G}
is trivial,

(2)

H^{2}(G,\mu_{m})=1
,

then the local-global principle holds for
(A,m)
.

Proof.

Notice that
\ker(\xi)
is the pre-image of

\ker(H^{2}(G,\mu_{m})\xrightarrow{\inf_{G/G_{K}}}H^{2}(G_{K},\mu_{m}))

via the connecting homomorphism

In particular, under the injectivity hypothesis

which is trivial under the additional hypothesis that

.
∎

2.2.
Finiteness of
\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}

We show that
\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
is finite in some cases.

Lemma 2.8
.

The pointed set
H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times})
is finite.

Proof.

The image of the inflation map
H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times})\to H^{1}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
is unramified outside the finite set
\mathrm{Ram}(L/K)
. Faltings’s theorem
[
4
]
implies that there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of abelian varieties over a number field
K
of a given dimension
g
with good reduction outside a finite set of places of
K
. Let
S
be the (finite) set of places of
K
where

has bad reduction. For
\chi\in H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times})
, the twist
A^{\chi}
has good reduction outside
\mathrm{Ram}(L/K)\cup S
, which is finite. Therefore,
H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times})
is finite.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

In any of the following cases, the group
\mbox{\cyr{X}}_{m}(G_{K},D_{L}^{\times})
is finite:

(1)

H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times,\chi})
is finite for all

(2)

(3)

\inf_{G/G_{K}}\colon H^{2}(G,\mu_{m})\to H^{2}(G_{K},\mu_{m})
is injective.

Proof.

We consider each case separately.

(1)

By the exactness of (

), finiteness of

and Corollary to Proposition 44 in
[

, §5]
, the set
H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times}/\mu_{m})
is finite. Proposition
2.5
lets us conclude.

(2)

This follows by the previous point, Lemma

and the fact that, in this case, we have
H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times,\chi})=H^{1}(G,D_{L}^{\times})
.

(3)

This follows from Corollary

and Proposition

. ∎

Remark 2.10
.

It is unclear whether (

) of Theorem

should hold unconditionally for all non-trivial

4.
The case

We now consider the case when

is of Type IV. Notice that if

, we already know that the local-global principle holds by Lemma

. In this section we make the following simplifying assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

We have
\mu_{m}\subseteq D
.

Write

, for simplicity. In particular,

is a number field that contains all the
m

.

Assume that

. Then, the local-global principle holds.

Proof.

Write

for the Galois representation of

at

. Let

and

by

. Recall from

that, for each

, there is some

. We find

(\det(\rho_{B,\ell})\otimes_{Z\otimes\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\det(\rho_{A,\ell})^{-1})|_{G_{K_{v}}}\cong c_{v}^{d},

where we are taking the determinants as
{Z\otimes\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}
-modules.
Since
(m,d)=1
, we can find

, an inverse of

, which is independent of

and such that

(\det(\rho_{B,\ell})\otimes_{Z\otimes\mathds{Q}_{\ell}}\det(\rho_{A,\ell})^{-1})^{e}|_{G_{K_{v}}}\cong c_{v}.

Consider the character

In the remaining cases

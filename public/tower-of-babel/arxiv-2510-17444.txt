Memory as activity: pattern formation in a conserved scalar field
Vaishnavi Gajendragad, Suropriya Saha

Abstract
We explore the concept of memory in scalar active matter systems, focusing on the collective dynamics of particles whose interactions depend on their evolutionary history rather than on their present configuration. We do so by introducing the idea of an active particle whose velocity acquires an active contribution that depends on its past trajectory suitably weighted by a memory kernel. The memory kernel is unrelated to the thermal noise acting on the particle, meaning that the particle breaks detailed balance at the microscopic level. The number density of these active particles is described by a Cahn-Hilliard equation, which typically describes passive phase separation, suitably modified to account for this particular non-equilibrium effect. Through theory and simulations we establish the novel emergent features of the model and use the example of time delayed interactions to highlight the novel pattern-forming abilities of the model.

Memory effects, represented by memory kernels in a theoretical description, arise naturally when one focuses on a single degree of freedom in a system with many-body interactions as shown in the seminal work by Zwanzig and Mori

;

Instead, we explore the collective behaviour of a collection of particles that can self-regulate using feedback from its past. Our broad aim is to cater to scenarios where regulation processes are complex, perhaps driven by a combination of intelligence and information driven decision making, visual and verbal cues, such that it is impossible to model them exactly.

Self-regulated crowds of human beings are an instance of information-mediated self-organisation

Soto and Golestanian 2014

;

;

;
Holubec et al. 2021b
. In this paper we focus on scalar active matter where the idea of feedback has not been applied so far. It represents a system of active interest where it is abundantly clear that activity can be incorporated in phase-separation dynamics in various ways, such as self-propulsion

;

;

In this work, we propose a new route to introducing activity by incorporating contributions to the particle current determined not simply by the current configuration but on the history of its evolution suitably weighed by a memory kernel. Taking inspiration from the relevance of phase separation dynamics in biological and active matter systems
Saha et al. 2016
;
Brangwynne et al. 2015
;
Niebel et al. 2019
;
Franzmann et al. 2018
;
Cates and Nardini 2024
we explore the role of active memory in phase separating systems. We study a collection of a scalar particles where passive phase separation can lead to thermodynamically stable bulk phase coexistence through local interactions in addition to the effects of the active memory. Although non-Markovian dynamics leading to a phase separated final state has been proposed to model number fields coupled to a visco-elastic fluid, or particles with inertia, our aim is to create a system that is functioning out of equilibrium. We distinguish the model from those cases where the Markovian dynamics of two or more fields can be formally written as the non-Markovian dynamics of a single field.

We show by an explicit Dean-Kawasaki construction that the minimal continuum model motivated above corresponds to the collective behaviour that emerges in a collection of intelligent particles which have the means to keep track of their trajectory. Their positions evolve following non-Markovian dynamics, such that the velocity is determined by the relative separation between the pairs of particles and an extra ’active’ contribution. The active term is given by a weighted average of a second potential that also pair-wise on the history of the relative coordinates. We show that the aim can be achieved by juxtaposing two chemical potentials, at least one of which has a frequency dependent mobility, such that the dynamics is becomes non-variational.

The paper is organized as follows. We start with an introduction of the theoretical framework - ‘Cahn-Hilliard dynamics with memory’ to describe the dynamics of a scalar active density in Section
II
. We explain how this model represents a novel type of active phase separation, leading to pattern formation away from equilibrium. In Section
III
, we explore a particular example of the general model, namely ’Cahn-Hilliard with delay’, where the system receives feedback from a time in the past. In Section
IV
we show that simple generalization of the model lead to exotic dynamical patterns and end with some concluding remarks in Section
V
.

II
Phase separation dynamics with memory

A conserved density field
\phi
evolves following the gradient of chemical a potential
\mu
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi=\Gamma\nabla^{2}\mu+\sqrt{2D}\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{\zeta},

(1)

where
\Gamma
denotes the mobility,
D
is the diffusivity, and
\bm{\zeta}
is a white noise term with unit variance, i.e. it is delta correlated in space and time. We include two contributions in
\mu
, passive and active, writing it as

\displaystyle\mu\equiv\mu_{\rm eq}+\mu_{\rm ac},

(2)

where
\mu_{\rm eq}\equiv\delta F/\delta\phi
is derivable from a free energy functional
F
. Memory is incorporated in
\mu_{\rm ac}
by allowing it to be history dependent

\displaystyle\mu_{\rm ac}(\bm{r},t)=\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{t}\,\mbox{d}t^{\prime}\gamma(t,t^{\prime})\bar{\mu}(\bm{r},t^{\prime}),

(3)

where we have introduced the a second chemical potential
\bar{\mu}=\delta\bar{F}/\delta\phi
,
\bar{F}
is a functional of the field
\phi
. Thus,
\mu_{\rm ac}
depends on the history of evolution of the field weighed with a memory kernel
\gamma
. As discussed earlier, memory kernels arise when fast degrees of freedom in the system are eliminated, retaining only those that are are slowly evolving in time
Zwanzig 1973
.

In this paper we focus solely on forms of
\gamma
that are time-translation invariant, i.e.
\gamma(t,t^{\prime})=\gamma(t-t^{\prime})
. It is instructive to re-write
\mu_{\rm ac}
in the Fourier domain, where
\gamma
assumes the form of a frequency (
\omega
) dependent mobility

\displaystyle\mu_{\rm ac}(\bm{q},\omega)=\alpha\gamma(\omega){\bar{\mu}}(\bm{q},\omega),

(4)

The integral of
\gamma
over time is unity, i.e.
\gamma(0)=1
such that the parameter
\alpha
unambiguously tunes the level of activity in the system.

State diagram for Cahn-Hilliard with delay

\alpha

\tau_{d}

The dynamics is thus a superposition of two chemical potentials and two mobilities, one frequency independent and the second which is not. The terms represent active and passive contributions because for
\alpha=0
, fluctuations in the steady state would follow the fluctuation-dissipation theorem (FDT)
Mazenko 2006
, while for
\alpha\neq 0
, FDT is violated. To see that the linearised system describing fluctuations around a uniform state is out of equilibrium consider the evolution of small deviations
\delta\phi
about any uniform state. Restricting to gradients of second order spatial gradients we have,

\displaystyle\left(i\omega+D_{\rm eff}q^{2}+\alpha\gamma(\omega)q^{2}\right)\phi(\boldsymbol{r},t)=i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{\zeta}.

(5)

Defining correlation and response functions
C
and
R
in the usual way,
C(\bm{q},\omega)=\langle\phi(\bm{q},\omega)\phi(-\bm{q},-\omega)\rangle
, and
R(\boldsymbol{q},\omega)=\text{Im}[\delta\phi/{\delta h}]_{h\to 0}
, we find that the ratio

\displaystyle\frac{\omega C}{2DR}=\frac{1}{1+(\alpha q^{2}\gamma^{\prime\prime}/\omega)}.

(6)

The ratio deviates from unity at finite
q
as expected in the system with number conservation. At a fixed
q
, the deviation is most significant at frequencies comparable to
\alpha q^{2}\gamma^{\prime\prime}
. Eq. (
6
) represents a frequency dependent deviation from unity.

We now elucidate the conditions for which the Eqs. (
1
) - (
3
) describes active phase separation for deterministic evolution. Notice that for
\gamma(\omega)=1
, the model is simply passive model B with
\mu=\mu_{\rm eq}+\alpha\bar{\mu}
. For constant
F
, the equation for
\phi
reduces to the simple form
i\omega\phi=-q^{2}\Gamma\alpha\gamma(\omega)\bar{\mu}(\bm{q},\omega)
, such that a uniform
\mu^{\prime}
is a possible steady state. The system is this case optimises
F^{\prime}
while following a non-Markovian path to the steady state. If the two free energies are exactly the same, that is
\bar{\mu}=\mu_{\rm eq}
then it is possible to rewrite the deterministic dynamics for
\phi
as
i\omega\phi=-q^{2}\Gamma[1+\alpha\gamma(\omega)]\mu_{\rm eq}(\bm{q},\omega)
, which also describes non-Markovian relaxation to a steady state determined by
F
. When the two contributions compete against one another, which happens when (1)
\bar{\mu}\neq\mu_{\rm eq}
, and (2)
\gamma
is a non-trivial function of time, we expect to observe dynamical steady states that carry explicit signatures that the system is out of equilibrium. The two contributions in Eq. (
2
) drives the system to two different steady states, (optimizing either
F
or
F^{\prime}
), and their juxtaposition leads to the novel dynamical states that we report in the work.

\mu_{\rm eq}
is selected such that for
\alpha=0
, that is in the absence of activity,
\phi
separates into bulk phases driven by thermodynamic forces and the composition of the bulk phases is determined by a Maxwell construction on the free energy
F
. In the most of the paper we pick the following polynomial form for the chemical potential
\mu_{\rm eq}
for simplicity,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{V}\mbox{d}^{d}r^{\prime}\left(\frac{a}{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{b}{4}\phi^{4}-\frac{\kappa}{2}|\bm{\nabla}\phi|^{2}\right),\,\,

\displaystyle\mu_{\rm eq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\phi+b\phi^{3}-\kappa\nabla^{2}\phi,

(7)

where
\kappa
is the surface tension that ensures that the interfaces are well-defined. We have chosen the double well potential such that a passive system (
\alpha=0
) phase separates into two phases with compositions
\pm 1
for vanishing average composition,
\bar{\phi}=0
. We choose the simplest possible form for
\bar{\mu}

\displaystyle F^{\prime}=\int_{V}\mbox{d}^{d}r^{\prime}\frac{1}{2}\phi^{2},\,\,\bar{\mu}=\phi.

(8)

II.1
Microscopic motivation

Consider a collection of particles where the position of the
i-
th particle is
\bm{r}_{i}
. The particles are point-like with pair-wise interactions that are derivable from a potential
V(|\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}|)
. In addition to the conservative forces, the particle has a brain, whether chemical or mechanical, that keeps track of its evolution in time. The velocity evolves as

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{d}\bm{r}_{i}}{\mbox{d}t}=\frac{1}{\Gamma}\sum_{j}\bm{F}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j})

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma}\sum_{j}\int_{0}^{t}\mbox{d}t^{\prime}\gamma(t-t^{\prime})\bm{F}^{\prime}(\bm{r}_{i}(t^{\prime})-\bm{r}_{j}(t^{\prime}))+\bm{\zeta}_{i},

(9)

where
\bm{F}
and
\bm{F}^{\prime}
are conservative forces derivable from interaction potentials
V
and
V^{\prime}
respectively. The system would be in equilibrium if two conditions are met -
\bm{F}^{\prime}=\bm{F}
, and if correlations of the noise
\bm{\zeta}
are related to
\gamma
such that the effective temperature is frequency independent. Eq. (
II.1
) represents a system out of equilibrium as the response is colored while the noise is delta-correlated. The dynamics is constructed opposite to a active Brownian particle where the noise has finite correlation associated with a fast relaxing orientational degree of freedom
Romanczuk et al. 2012
. The reverse situation also arises in a colloidal particle in an electric field where fluctuations of counter-ions lead to a coloured noise and a effective temperature that violate fluctuation-dissipation theorem
Saha and Ramaswamy 2014
.

We now carry out a systematic coarse-graining procedure with standard assumptions
Dean 1996
to obtain an equation for the equation of motion of the density
\rho=\sum_{i}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})
only, see Appendix for a full derivation of the dynamics. Retaining terms until two orders in gradients we have the following

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{i}(x,t)=\partial_{x}\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma}\rho_{i}(x,t)\sum_{j}\int\mbox{d}y\rho(y,t)\partial_{x}V(x-y)\right]

\displaystyle+\partial_{x}\left[\frac{\alpha~\rho_{i}(x,t-\tau_{d})}{\Gamma}\sum_{j}\int\mathrm{d}y\,\rho_{j}(y,t-\tau_{d})\,\partial_{x}V^{\prime}(x-y)\right]

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}[\eta_{i}~\rho_{i}(x,t)]+T\partial_{x}^{2}\rho_{i}(x,t)+\cdots\mbox{O}(\partial_{x}^{3},\partial_{x}^{4}).

(10)

In carrying out the coarse-graining, we retain terms until quadratic order in the activity
\alpha
, see Appendix for the full expression. The higher order terms represent contributions involving gradients of
V
and
V^{\prime}
with three of more gradients and also cross terms involving both
V
and
V^{\prime}
. The introduction of memory modifies the stochastic terms introducing a coloured noise related to
\gamma
at order
\partial_{x}^{3}
in the dynamics. The higher order terms represent interesting modifications which can be explored numerically in the future.

III
Cahn-Hilliard with time-delayed interactions

We now explore pattern formation in a model, where
\gamma(t)=\delta(t-\tau_{d})
. In this simple version of the model, the particles receive feedback from a time
\tau_{d}
in their past, reflected in the equation of motion of

that reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{r},t)=\nabla^{2}\left[a\phi+b\phi^{3}\right]+\alpha\nabla^{2}\phi(\bm{r},t-\tau_{d})

(11)

Eq. (
11
) represents dynamics away from equilibrium if both
\alpha,\tau_{d}\neq 0
. The signs of

and
\alpha
determine whether the particles constituting the field interact via attractive or repulsive interactions with the present and past field configurations.

As summarised in Fig.
1
(b), dynamical patterns in the steady state are observed only if

, such that the system undergoes phase separation for
\alpha=0
. The parameters are fixed at

,
b=1
, and the patterns formed in the system are explored by varying the parameters
\alpha

, meaning that for

, when the particles at the present time interact attractively, while they interact repulsively with a past version of themselves. Another useful perspective is that when the feedback reinforces the dynamics imposed by

On varying

V
Conclusions

We have explored a route to pattern formation in a single conserved scalar field through a feedback mechanism implemented in the chemical potential. A coarse-graining shows that the dynamics proposed on a phenomenological basis describes emergent behaviour of a collection of active particles with memory. An additional contribution that depends on its past history is added to the velocity of each particle. The out of equilibrium nature of the system is characterised by a frequency dependent effective temperature. A numerical exploration shows that the system shows dynamical behaviour without any passive counterpart. For the specific example of Cahn-Hilliard with delay, which is explored thoroughly in the paper, we find that for large values of activity multiple stable spirals are formed in the system. Their formation is associated with a linear instability of the uniform state associated with complex eigenvalues, a harbinger of oscillatory dynamics and travelling patterns in non reciprocally interacting soft matter.

Strikingly, we find that dynamic patterns carrying signatures of activity appear when the field interacts‘non-reciprocally’ with its earlier configuration. The theory proposed by us provides a framework for pattern formation involving a minimum number of scalar fields. The diversity of dynamics seen in the generalization of the model points at the pattern forming abilities of the model beyond travelling waves and spirals.

Temporal feedback in a spatially extended system can lead to exotic dynamics is a concept that can be extended to other forms of active systems such as active solids to produce material properties without examples in passive matter. The work can be the basis of many works in the future, by breaking the time translation invariance can provide a route to examining the couple effect of activity and aging in the dynamics of a phase separating field.

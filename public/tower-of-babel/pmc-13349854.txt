The 2024 EBMT activity report: crossing one million HCTs and 20,000 CAR-T. A landmark in cellular therapy
Authors: Raffaella Greco, Isabel Sánchez-Ortega, Antonio M Risitano, Krzysztof Kalwak, Annalisa Ruggeri, Iliana Kleovoulou, Marina Atlija, Tobias Alexander, Emanuele Angelucci, Dina Averbuch, Ali Bazarbachi, Maria Ester Bernardo, Charles Crawley, Mette D Hazenberg, Olaf Penack, Helen Baldomero, Jakob R Passweg, Ibrahim Yakoub-Agha, Donal P McLornan, Fabio Ciceri, Anna Sureda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn 2024, the EBMT activity survey surpassed one million HCTs reported since 1990, a major milestone in cellular therapy. That year, 47,204 HCTs (21,023 allogeneic, 26,181 autologous) were reported in 43,791 patients across 688 centres in 53 countries. Compared to 2023, HCT activity decreased (−1.1% overall, −3.9% autologous), while allogeneic increased ( + 2.6%) to the highest annual activity to date. CAR-T therapy reached 6,082 patients ( + 24.5% vs 2023), surpassing 20,000 since 2018. Main indications for allo-HCT were myeloid (62%), lymphoid malignancies (~24%), and non-malignant disorders (~17%). For auto-HCT were plasma cell disorders (59%), lymphomas (22%), and solid tumours (~6%). Unrelated donors (56%) increased ( + 5%), while HLA-identical siblings (25%) and haploidentical (19%) remained stable. Cord blood use continued decreasing (−6.2%). Paediatric HCT activity decreased slightly (−1.7%; −1.9% allogeneic, −1.1% autologous). CAR-T therapy expanded (lymphomas remaining the leading indication (70%), followed by multiple myeloma (18%,) and ALL (8%). Autoimmune diseases indications increased by 67%). Overall, transplant and CAR-T activity steadily increased, with pandemic-related declines mitigated by safety measures, reflecting systems resilience and variable country contributions. From 2025, the EBMT survey will capture adult and paediatric activity separately, establishing a comprehensive database to monitor trends, inform practice, define clinical needs and assess equitable access.Subject terms: Haematological diseases, Immunological disorders, Rheumatic diseases

Introduction

The EBMT has conducted an annual survey of haematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) activity since 1990 [1]. Over the past 35 years, this initiative has grown into a database of more than 1,038,009 transplants, surpassing the milestone of 1 million.

HCT is an established therapeutic procedure for a wide range of acquired or inherited disorders of the haematopoietic system, including benign and malignant conditions, immune deficiencies, and metabolic disorders [2]. The EBMT activity survey, capturing the status of HCT across Europe and neighbouring countries, has become an essential instrument to monitor trends, changes in practice, and the evolution of HCT technology over time [3–11]. Originally implemented as a single-page spreadsheet, the survey retained this format until 2022. In 2023, an online reporting system was introduced. In parallel with advances in the field, the survey has continuously evolved to capture emerging trends in transplantation and cellular therapies. Revisions have included paediatric activity, detailed reporting of donor type, stem cell source and the inclusion of non-HCT treatments, such as cellular therapies and, more recently, immunosuppressive therapy (IST) for marrow failure. In 2024, the survey incorporated, for the first time, specific indications for CAR-T and gene therapy. New disease categories and disease status updates were additionally introduced across cellular therapies to improve reporting granularity and more accurately reflect evolving trends of disease distribution. From 2025 onwards, separate reporting forms for adult and paediatric patients will be implemented. Collectively, these developments have consolidated the EBMT survey into a robust and comprehensive platform capturing the rapid evolution of HCT, cellular and gene-based therapies across Europe.

Analysis of the EBMT survey data since 1990 shows a steady increase in HCT numbers and transplant rates for both allogeneic and autologous procedures, temporarily decreased in 2020 by the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic, with partial recovery by 2022 [11–13]. By 2023, allogeneic HCT (allo-HCT) growth had largely resumed, while autologous HCT (auto-HCT) increased more modestly [14]. The 2024 survey shows a slight overall decline in total HCT and auto-HCT but continued growth in allo-HCT and a marked rise in CAR-Ts, exceeding 20,000 reported therapies since 2018. These trends reflect the evolution of cellular therapies, highlighted by surpassing one million HCT and the significant increase in CAR-T therapies.

Patients and methods

Participating centres

Since 1990, a directory of centres participating in the EBMT activity survey has been maintained, including both EBMT members and non-members across European and collaborating non-European countries. The directory is updated annually to reflect each centre’s activity.

In 2024, 742 centres from 54 countries (42 European and 12 collaborating countries) were contacted, of which 688 centres from 53 countries responded, corresponding to a 93% return rate and including 14% non-EBMT members. Fifty-four centres did not submit data, representing approximately 2400 HCTs based on their previous reports.

Participating centres are listed in the Supplemental Appendix in alphabetical order, by country, city, and EBMT centre code, along with the reported numbers of first and total HCT, first allogeneic and autologous HCT and first autologous, allogeneic and total CAR-T. The WHO regional office definitions were used to classify countries as European or non-European. Twelve collaborating non-European countries participated in the 2024 survey: Algeria, Iraq, Iran, Jordan, Lebanon, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Syria, Tunisia, United Arab Emirates, South Africa. Their data, 3268 first HCT, from 31 active transplant centres made up 7.5% of the total dataset.

Data collection and validation

In 2024, as in the previous year, centres submitted their data directly via the online reporting system. However, new disease categories and status were incorporated across cellular therapies to enhance the granularity of data reporting and more accurately reflect evolving trends in disease distribution across indications. For AML, the revised classification distinguished AML in >1st CR from those AML not in remission, which had previously been reported together as “AML not in 1st CR.” In addition, MDS and MDS/MPN overlap were reported separately. Within the Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) category, new diagnoses were introduced, including follicular lymphoma (FL), mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) and primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma (PMBCL). Finally, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) was reported as a separate autoimmune disease indication. The number of patients reported in 2024 for these new indications is shown in Fig. 1. For the first time, the survey captured detailed information on CAR-T therapy indications. These, and ex vivo gene therapy indications were reported separately, using the same format as for HCT, and are detailed in Table 1.

Aside from these updates, the overall structure of the activity survey remained consistent with previous years. Patients receiving their first HCT during the survey year were reported by disease, donor type, and stem cell source in Table 1, while subsequent transplants were summarised in the same table, along with the number of paediatric activities.

Centres also reported patients receiving other cellular therapies qualifying as advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs), excluding CAR-T cell therapies (https://www.ebmt.org/ebmt/news/fact-and-jacie-co-publish-9th-edition-hct-standards-and-3rd-edition-iec-standards) (Table 2). Furthermore, the number of patients receiving unmanipulated donor lymphocyte infusions (DLI) and their indications, and the number of IST administered for acquired bone marrow failure (BMF), were reported separately. This survey did not include cells from sources other than haematopoietic tissue. Quality control measures comprised multiple independent systems, including verification of data entered by centres immediately after submission through output checks, as well as cross-referencing with selected national registries.

Patient and cellular therapy numbers

Where appropriate, patient numbers refer to individuals receiving their first transplant in 2024, while transplant numbers reflect the total number of procedures performed. Likewise, CAR-T patient counts represent the number of patients receiving a first CAR-T in 2024, while cellular therapy counts reflect the total number of CAR-T procedures.

The term sibling donor included HLA-identical siblings and twins but excludes siblings with HLA mismatches. Haploidentical transplants refer to those from any family member with at least a full haplotype mismatch, whereas other family donors include related donors mismatched to a lesser degree than a full haplotype. For data collection and analysis, the small number of “other family donors” was combined with haploidentical donors. Unrelated donor transplants comprise HCT from matched or mismatched unrelated donors. Stem cell sources were categorised as bone marrow (BM), peripheral blood (PBSC), or cord blood (CB). Additional non–first transplants included multiple procedures, defined as planned or unplanned subsequent transplants performed in the same patient.

HCT and cellular therapy rates

HCT and CAR-T therapy rates, defined as the total number of procedures per 10 million inhabitants (107), were computed for each country, without adjusting for patients receiving the cellular therapy in a foreign country or for refugee populations not considered inhabitants. Population numbers for the European countries in 2024 were obtained from Eurostat: (https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat) and the World Bank database for the non-European countries: (https://databank.worldbank.org).

Analysis

Where appropriate, the absolute number of patients undergoing cellular therapy, including autologous or allogeneic HCT and CAR-T therapy, the number of procedures, and the corresponding HCT and CAR-T rates are presented for specific countries, indications and cellular therapy modalities.

Myeloid malignancy includes acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative overlap syndrome (MDS/MPN overlap), myeloproliferative neoplasm (MPN) and chronic myeloid leukemia (CML). Lymphoid malignancy includes acute lymphocytic leukaemia (ALL), chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL), Hodgkin lymphoma (HL), Diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL), Follicular lymphoma (FL), Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL), Other B-cell NHL, primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma (PMBCL), T-cell NHL and plasma cell disorders (PCD) (including multiple myeloma (MM) and other PCD). Non-malignant disorders include autoimmune diseases (AD), BMF (severe aplastic anaemia (SAA) and other BMF), thalassaemia and sickle cell disease (HG), inborn errors of immunity (IEI) and inborn errors of metabolism (IEM). Finally, solid tumours indications, and others (includes histiocytosis and other rare disorders).

In 2024, additional diagnoses and disease status at the time of cellular therapy were incorporated. The corresponding numbers of patients receiving HCT or CAR-T cell therapy for these indications are shown in Fig. 1.

Ethical approval and consent to participate

This manuscript follows the relevant guidelines and regulations as appropriate. All graphics were self-generated. The data analysis is based on center-level data and no individual patient data are included, as such no ethics committee approval is needed. Patient informed consent is not needed.

Results

Haematopoietic Cell Transplantation (HCT)

Participating centres in 2024

Of the 688 responding centres, 449 (65.3%) performed both allogeneic and autologous transplants; 213 (31%) restricted their activity to auto-HCT only and 20 (2.9%) allo-HCT only. Six centres (0.8%) did not report any activity. Of the 688 reporting centres in 2024, 452 (65.7%) performed transplants for adults only and 114 (16.6%) performed transplants for both adult and paediatric patients. An additional 122 (17.7%) were dedicated paediatric-only transplant centres.

Number of patients, HCT, and trends in 2024

In 2024, a total of 47,204 HCT procedures were reported in 43,791 patients, compared with 47,731 HCT in 43,902 patients in 2023. Of the 2024 transplants, 21,023 (44.5%) were allogeneic and 26,181 (55.5%) autologous (Table 1, Fig. 2). Overall, this represents a 1.1% decrease in total HCT compared with 2023, driven by a 2.6% increase in allo-HCT and a 3.9% decrease in auto-HCT.

For auto-HCT, the most common indications were lymphoid malignancies (90.5%, n = 21,623) and included PCD, NHL, HL, ALL, CLL (Fig. 3b). Changes observed for allogeneic HCT from 2023 to 2024

In 2024, the overall number of first allo-HCT increased by 2.7% compared with the previous year, reflecting a slower growth rate than the 7.8% rise observed in 2023 (Fig. 2). The marked increase in 2023 followed a broad recovery across indications after the COVID-19 pandemic, which had caused substantial disruptions in HCT activity across Europe and globally between 2020 and 2022.

Myeloid malignancies

AML remained the leading indication for allo-HCT in 2024, accounting for 42% (n = 8286) of the procedures and representing a 6.2% increase compared with 2023 (Fig. 3a). The number of allo-HCTs performed for AML across all disease stages has continued to rise in recent years. In 2024, AML in 1st CR was the main indication (57.2%; n = 4742), showing a 5% increase from 2023. Secondary AML accounted for 18.5% (n = 1532), of which 75% were AML/MDS related changes and increased by 11.7% compared with 2023, while 25% were therapy-related AML, which decreased by 4.7% compared with the previous year. Two additional AML reporting categories were included in 2024: AML in >CR1, representing 14.7% of cases (n = 1216), and AML not in CR, accounting for 9.6% (n = 796) of AML patients undergoing allo-HCT. Allo-HCT for MDS and MDS/MPN increased by 2.7% compared with 2023. Among these, MDS accounted for 78% of cases (n = 2062), while MDS/MPN overlap represented 22% (n = 580). Allo-HCT for MPN increased by 6.7% (1,074 cases vs. 1,007 in 2023). The number of allo-HCT for CML has remained stable over the past 15 years ( ~ 400 in 2024) across disease phases (CR1, 50%; >CR1, 50%), likely reflecting the expanded access to TKIs and the absence of changes in transplant indications.

Lymphoid malignancies

ALL remained the leading indication for allo-HCT among lymphoid malignancies, accounting for 63% of cases (n = 2988) (Fig. 3a). Overall, ALL represented 15% of all allo-HCT indications, with a decrease of -3.6% for ALL cases in 1st CR and -2.7% for ALL not in 1st CR, compared with 2023. Allo-HCT for lymphomas increased by 4% in 2024 (n = 1455). T-cell lymphomas rose by 7.1% (n = 635), representing 43.6% of lymphoma indications, while DLBCL remained stable ( + 1.4%, n = 283), accounting for 19.5%. New subcategories were introduced in 2024 to better capture disease distribution trends within NHL indications. These showed that 3.2% of allo-HCT for lymphomas corresponded to FL, 3.4% to MCL, 2.1% to PMBCL and 5.2% to other B-cell NHL. HL indications decreased by 8.7% (n = 334) when compared with 2023, representing 23% of allo-HCT for lymphoma in 2024. Notably, PCD indications significantly decreased in 2024, with a 44% reduction in MM cases (89 vs. 158 in 2023) and 17% for other PCD (25 vs. 30). CLL remained stable ( + 0.6%, n = 175), following the 10.8% increase observed in 2023.

Solid tumours

Among solid tumours (n = 41), allo-HCT for neuroblastoma showed an 18% increase in 2024, with 33 cases compared to 28 in the previous year. The remaining solid tumour indications accounted for only 8 additional cases (Table 1).

Non-malignant disorders

Allo-HCT activity for SAA decreased by 3.2%, while non-SAA BMF increased by 10.1% (n = 295 vs. 268 in 2023). Overall, 718 patients underwent allo-HCT for SAA in 2024 and 622 received immunosuppressive therapy. Among haemoglobinopathies, allo-HCT for thalassaemia and SCD increased by 5.8% (n = 329) and 11.7% (n = 439), respectively. Reported activity for IEI and IEM decreased from 624 to 583 (−6,6%) and 194 to 158 (−18,6%), respectively.

Immunosuppressive therapy in 2024

In 2024, 197 centres reported 802 patients treated with IST for BMF, representing a 9% increase compared with 2023. Most IST was administered for AA, with activity increasing by 2.8%, while 53 patients received IST for other BMFs, corresponding to a 40% decrease relative to the previous year.

Donor lymphocyte infusions in 2024

A total of 3659 patients received unmanipulated DLI in 2024. The most frequent indication was relapse (n = 1349), followed by graft enhancement or failure (n = 796), and protocol-driven (n = 601), corresponding to increases of 4%, 16%, and 31%, respectively, compared with 2023. Since the start of reporting in 1997, DLI use has increased from 305 cases to 3659 in 2024.

Changes in donor type and stem cell source from 2023 to 2024

In 2024, allo-HCT by donor type included 5134 transplants from HLA-identical sibling donors, 4173 from haploidentical or other related donors, and 11,716 from unrelated donors, corresponding to 25%, 19%, and 56% of all allo-HCT activity, respectively. Temporal trends showed that unrelated donor transplants continued to increase in 2024 ( + 5%), although the growth rate slowed compared with 2023 ( + 11%), following a slight decline in 2022 (−1%). Haploidentical donor transplants remained stable after the 11.3% increase observed in 2023, while HLA-identical sibling transplants stabilized at around 5,000 procedures per year, after the −7.7% decrease in 2022. The use of CB continued to decrease in 2024 (−6.2%), consistent with the trends over recent years, except for a transient increase in 2020, likely related to the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic. (Fig. 5). In 2024, PBSCs remained the predominant stem cell source for allo-HCT, representing 85% (n = 17,811), followed by BM (14%, n = 2972) and CB (1%, n = 240). Analysis by donor type and stem cell source showed a 10% reduction in BM use for haploidentical HCT (721 vs. 801) alongside a 2.6% increase in PBSC (3444 vs. 3352). Among unrelated donor transplants, PBSC use increased by 5.35% (10,426 vs. 9897), BM remained stable ( + 1.43%) and CB decreased by 5%. For HLA-identical sibling transplants, the distribution of BM and PBSC remained stable at 23% and 77%, respectively, while CB use declined by 45% (9 cases in 2024 vs. 20 in 2023).

Changes in autologous HCT from 2023 to 2024

As observed for allo-HCT, auto-HCT activity also fluctuated over the three years of the COVID-19 pandemic (Fig. 2). Overall, the total number of auto-HCTs in 2024 decreased by 2.6% compared with 2023 across most indications (lymphoid malignancies −2.6%, solid tumours −4%, and non-malignant disorders −6.6%), with the exception of myeloid malignancies, which increased by 19.8% (n = 230 vs. 192). This rise was primarily driven by AML in 1st CR, the main myeloid indication for auto-HCT (90% of cases), which showed a 28% increase compared with 2023, despite representing only 1% of auto-HCT activity.

The main indication for auto-HCT remained lymphoid malignancies (n = 21,623), with PCD accounting for 59% of the procedures (n = 14,121) (Fig. 3b). Auto-HCT for MM remained stable in 2024 (n = 13,694; −0.8%), whereas non-MM plasma cell disorders decreased by 8.8% (427 vs. 468). Among NHL (n = 5180, −7.6% in 2024), DLBCL remained the leading indication (n = 2481; 48% of NHL cases) but decreased by 12.6% compared with 2023. In 2024, three lymphoma subtypes were reported separately from the former “other B-NHL” indication: MCL (n = 961; 18.5% of NHL), FL (n = 306, 6%) and PMBCL (n = 74, 1.4%). T-cell lymphomas increased by 10.3% compared with 2023 (n = 864) while HL indications remained stable (−0.3%). Reported numbers for solid tumours remained stable at approximately 1500 cases per year, with neuroblastoma accounting for 37%, germ cell tumours 28%, Ewing sarcoma 11%, soft tissue tumours 3%, and other solid tumours 21%. For non-malignant diseases, AD represented 98% of all autologous indications, all showing a decline in 2024: Multiple Sclerosis (MS) 389 auto-HCTs (−7.2%), Systemic Sclerosis (SSC) 46 (−10%) and other AD 33 (−3%). Four cases of Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) were reported separately in 2024.

The use of BM increased markedly in 2024, from 17 to 140 procedures; however, it accounted for only 0.5% of stem cell sources for auto-HCT. Notably, among the 124 patients treated with BM, 60 were for MM, compared with only one patient in 2023. Only two autologous cord blood transplants were reported in 2024, both performed as a second procedure.

Paediatric transplantation in 2024

In 2024, a total of 5360 paediatric patients (<18 years at transplant) underwent HCT in a dedicated paediatric or combined adult-paediatric centres, including 4032 allo-HCT (75%) and 1328 auto-HCT (25%) (Table 1). This represents an overall decrease of 1.74% HCT compared with 2023, with declines of 1.9% for allo-HCT and 1.1% for auto-HCT.

Due to the previous survey design, detailed analysis by diagnosis is limited to dedicated paediatric centres only. In 2024, 122 centres reported a total of 4117 HCT in 3694 patients, of which 3039 were allo-HCT (74%) and 1078 (26%) auto-HCT. Main indications for allo-HCT were ALL (n = 744; 54% non in 1st CR and 46% in 1st CR), AML (n = 483; 56% in 1st CR and 29.6% in >1st CR), and non-malignant diseases (n = 1260; 44.8%), of which 400 (31.7%) were for IEI, followed by thalassaemia (17.3%) and SAA (15.1%). Among allo-HCT, 1727 (57%) were from family donors and 1312 (43%) from unrelated donors. Among the family donors, 785 (45.4%) were from an haploidentical relative. BM was used in 1377 allo-HCT, of which 850 (59.5%) were from family donors. PBSC were used in 1339 patients, with a slightly higher proportion from family donors (n = 731; 54.5%) compared with unrelated donors (n = 608). CB was the stem cell source in 94 procedures (91.5% from unrelated donors). The main indications for auto-HCT were solid tumours (n = 751), predominantly neuroblastoma (n = 385, 51.2%). PBSC were used in the majority of auto-HCT (n = 870; 98.4%) with 14 patients receiving BM. No patients were reported as having received autologous CB. In addition, 225 patients received DLI, primarily for graft enhancement or failure and 120 patients received IST for BMF.

HCT activity rates by country in 2024

Assessing transplant rates per 10 million population (TR/107) allowed the comparison of activity in countries adjusted for differences in population size. In the 2024 survey, the TR rates for allo-HCT within European countries ranged from 2.7/107 in Georgia to 422.75/107 in Lithuania, followed by 414.06/107 in Israel, 399.25/107 in Germany, 368.96/107 in Belgium and 349.78/107 in Switzerland. The median number of total allo-HCT by country was 122 and the median TR 148.4/107. Five countries did not report any allo-HCT (Bosnia and Herzegovina, Cyprus, Iceland, Latvia, and Luxembourg). Allo-HCT rates per 10 million population in Europe in 2024 are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1a.

For auto-HCT, rates ranged from 1.96/107 in Azerbaijan, followed by Bosnia Herzegovina with 22.12/107 to 496.5/107 in Netherlands followed by 478.1/107 in Lithuania, 459.04/107 in Italy, 427.24/107 in Croatia and 418.35/107 in Spain. The median number of total auto-HCT by country was 175 and the median TR 239.5/107. All countries participating in the annual survey reported auto-HCT except Kazakhstan. Auto-HCT rates per 10 million population in Europe in 2024 are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1b.

Growth and normalized access to HCT

Since the initiation of the activity survey reports, the number of autologous and allogeneic HCT have continued to increase, with the exception of the pandemic years, which were marked by a significant decline in activity. During this period, centres implemented strict safety protocols and prioritised urgent indications, demonstrating the resilience and adaptability of transplant and cellular therapy systems across countries. Despite differences in healthcare infrastructure, disease prevalence and local challenges, the overall response highlighted the capacity to maintain essential cell therapy services under unprecedented conditions. Over the last decade (2015–2024), countries contributed differently to the growth of HCT. To assess normalised access to transplantation, we quantified both the absolute increase and relative growth (%) in autologous and allogeneic HCT transplant rates (TR, per million population) for each country, capturing each country’s contribution to the overall expansion.

For allo-HCT, the largest absolute increases were observed in Lithuania ( + 200 TR), Lebanon ( + 144 TR), Switzerland ( + 82 TR), and Spain ( + 80 TR), whereas relative growth was greatest in Lebanon ( + 252%), Latvia ( + 286%), Lithuania ( + 90%), and Switzerland ( + 31%) (Supplementary Fig. 2a). For auto-HCT, absolute increases were highest in Lebanon ( + 190 TR), Croatia ( + 161 TR), Latvia ( + 159 TR), Estonia ( + 141 TR), and Lithuania ( + 126 TR), with the fastest relative growth in Lebanon ( + 324%), Latvia ( + 286%), Lithuania ( + 135%), Estonia ( + 153%), and Croatia ( + 161%) (Supplementary Fig. 3a). Longitudinal trajectories for top 10 reporting countries (Supplementary Figs. 2b and 3b), as well as for all countries reporting since 2015 (Supplementary Fig. 4 and Supplementary Table 1), illustrate temporal trends and the consistent growth of allogeneic and autologous HCT procedures.

CAR-T therapy in 2024

CAR-T therapy has expanded rapidly across indications, with reported cases increasing from 301 in 2018 to 6082 in 2024, representing a 24.5% increase over 2023 and a 20.2-fold growth since 2018. Notably, the cumulative number of CAR-T therapies reported between 2018 and 2024 has surpassed 20,000, rising to 14% of the transplant activity and will likely continue to rise in the upcoming years, highlighting the accelerated adoption of this therapy in clinical practice (Fig. 7). Among all reported CAR-T therapies, 99.3% corresponded to autologous CAR-T infusions (n = 6037), with only 45 cases using allogeneic products.

Growth in the number of patients receiving CAR-T therapy, 2018–2024.

The primary indication for autologous CAR-T remained lymphoid malignancies (n = 5868), representing 97% of the procedures. CAR-T infusions for NHL (70.5% of the cases) increased from 3437 in 2023 to 4283 in 2024, representing an approximate 25% rise (Fig. 8). In 2024, among NHL, the main diagnosis was DLBCL (n = 3215, 53% of CAR-T indications), followed by FL (7%), MCL (7%), PMBCL (2%) and other B-cell NHL (2%). HL and CLL each accounted for <0.4% of CAR-T indications and only 5 cases were reported for T-cell lymphomas (Fig. 8). CAR-T therapy for MM increased by 50% in 2024 (1102 vs. 736), remaining the second most frequent indication and accounting for 19% of autologous CAR-T, followed by ALL (8%, n = 480), 87% of which were ALL not in 1st CR. Myeloid malignancies continue to represent a very small proportion of CAR-T cell therapy indications, with only 19 cases reported in 2024, of which 53% corresponded to AML not in CR and 26.3% to CML not in 1st CP. Solid tumours represent 1.4% of the indications (23% germ cell tumours). Notably, the number of CAR-T therapies for AD increased from 36 cases in 2023 to 60 in 2024, representing a 67% growth. SLE remained the most common indication (35%), followed by SSc (21.7%), MS 6.7% and other AD (36.7%) (Fig. 9). In 2024, a total of 109 patients received a second or subsequent autologous CAR-T infusion.

Pie chart of autoimmune diseases for CAR-T.

In 2024, a total of 45 cases of allogeneic CAR-T cell therapies were reported compared with 84 in 2023. Among these, ALL not in 1st CR represented 47% of the cases (56% in 2023), AML 29%, solid tumours 9%, DLBCL 9%, other B-cell NHL 4.5% and MDS 2%. Additionally, 5 s or subsequent allogeneic CAR-T infusions were reported in 2024. Among the 6082 patients receiving CAR-T therapy in 2024, 206 (3.4%) were paediatric, of whom 175 (85%) received autologous and 31 received allogeneic CAR-T therapy.

CAR-T therapy rates

In 2024, 278 centres from 30 countries reported a total of 6196 CAR-T cell therapies (6,082 first CAR-T infusions and 114 s therapies). Among these 278 centres, 247 (89%) reported both allogeneic and autologous CAR-T therapies, 26 only autologous and 5 centres, only allogeneic.

For autologous CAR-T cell therapy, 75% of the patients (n = 4514) were reported by 181 centres across 5 countries (Germany 1438, France 1092, Spain 758, Italy 678 and UK 548 patients). Forty-five patients receiving allogeneic CAR-T cell therapy were reported by 11 centres from 9 countries: UK (n = 14), Russia (n = 13), Italy (n = 7), Spain (n = 5), Greece (n = 2) and one patient each from France, Germany, Romania and Israel.

The median number of patients receiving CAR-T cell therapy per country was 42 (range 3–1439), compared with 47 (range 1-1160) in 2023. The median CAR-T therapy rate was 64.5/107 (range 0.3–210.5), increased from 44.7/107 (range 0.1–297.1) in 2023. Rates ranged from 0.3/107 in Iran to 210.5/107 in Israel, followed by Switzerland (202.6/107), Germany 172.4/107), France (159.6/107) and Spain (156.9/107) (Supplementary Fig. 5).

Growth and normalized access to CAR-T therapy

Since 2018, CAR-T reporting has been consistent in 11 countries, with the largest absolute increases observed in the Netherlands ( + 32 TR), Germany ( + 30 TR), France ( + 24 TR), Austria ( + 21 TR), and Italy ( + 20 TR), whereas relative growth was greatest in Germany ( + 2457%), the Netherlands ( + 2316%), Austria ( + 2036%), France ( + 1812%), and the UK ( + 1394%). Overall, 30 countries have reported CAR-T therapies since 2018 (Supplementary Fig. 6 and Table 2). These trends underscore the rapid expansion of advanced cellular therapies in parallel with HCT, reflecting both increased capacity and broader access across participating countries

Other cellular and gene therapies: 2024 update

In 2024, 38 patients were reported to receive gene therapies for the treatment of non-malignant disorders (86.8%, n = 33) of which 13 were IEM, 9 IEI, 7 cases of thalassaemia and 4 SCD (Table 1). Four additional patients received gene therapy for the treatment of solid tumours.

Other cellular therapies were reported in 1450 patients (72.5% allogeneic and 27.5% autologous) (Table 2). Mesenchymal stromal cells were the most frequently used therapy (n = 408; 97% allogeneic) primarily administered for the treatment of graft-versus-host disease (GVHD, 77%), followed by graft enhancement (10.5%) and AD (7.1%). The number of allogeneic NK-cell therapies increased by 76.2% over the past year, rising from 63 to 111 in 2024. Similarly, the use of allogeneic TRegs have increased by 78.6%, largely driven by increased use for infectious complications (2 cases in 2023 vs. 12 in 2024); however, GVHD remained the main indication (59%). The use of allogeneic expanded CD34+cells for graft enhancement or failure increased substantially, from 8 cases in 2023 to 30 in 2024. Autologous dendritic cell therapies were administered to 19 patients for the treatment of solid tumours (Table 2).

Discussion

The EBMT activity survey has been conducted annually since 1990 [1], surpassing one million HCT procedures in 2024. Over 47,200 transplants in almost 43,800 patients were reported in 2024, slightly lower than in 2023 following the post-pandemic recovery phase. The highest activity ever recorded was in 2019, with 48,412 transplants performed in 43,581 patients. A substantial decline in activity was observed in 2020 due to the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic, followed by fluctuations during 2021–2022, reflecting its prolonged impact and the gradual recovery thereafter. In 2023, allo-HCT activity increased again, whereas this recovery was less evident for auto-HCT. This growth trend continued in 2024, reaching the highest annual allogeneic HCT activity to date (n = 21,023), driven primarily by AML, which accounted for 42% of allo-HCTs across disease stages (57% in 1st CR and 14% in >1st CR). MDS and MPN indications also increased (Fig. 3a). In 2024, indications were reported separately, evidencing that MDS accounted for 78% of cases while MDS/MPN overlap represented 22%. Notably, PCD indications significantly decreased in 2024, with a 44% reduction in MM cases. This contrasts with the long-term stability of allo-HCT for CML, which has remained around 400 procedures annually following the sharp decline observed after the introduction of tyrosine kinase inhibitors and their subsequent widespread availability, changing the paradigm of the CML indications for allo-HCT. Donor selection in allo-HCT continues to shift away from HLA-identical sibling donors towards unrelated and haploidentical donors [15]. In 2024, the use of HLA-identical siblings stabilized at approximately 5000 procedures per year, similar to levels reported in 2006. This likely reflects a preference for younger unrelated or haploidentical donors over older sibling donors, supported by recent comparative outcome studies [16–18], although the absence of donor and recipient age data precludes further analysis. The use of unrelated donors continues to increase across indications (Fig. 5), while haploidentical donors are approaching the numbers of HLA-identical siblings for myeloid malignancies and are increasingly used for lymphoid and non-malignant indications (Fig. 5).

Auto-HCT activity declined by 4.1% in 2024 compared with 2023, while continuing to shift towards MM as the predominant indication (57.3%), alongside a decrease in B-cell NHL, including a 12.6% reduction in DLBCL. Newly introduced indication categories captured 306 cases of FL, 961 MCL, and 74 PMBCL. The reduction in lymphoma transplants likely reflects the increasing use of CAR-T cell therapies for these indications. In contrast, auto-HCT for T-cell lymphomas increased by 10%. Notably, AML in 1st CR was the only additional indication for which auto-HCT activity increased in 2024. Auto-HCT activity for AD continued to decline (6.3% reduction in 2024), despite the modest recovery observed in 2023 following the pandemic. This decline occurred alongside a 67% increase in CAR-T therapies for AD, rising from 36 cases in 2023 to 60 in 2024, with SLE, SSc, and MS representing the primary indications [19–21]. Currently, CAR-T cell therapies for AD are mainly delivered in clinical trials. Overall, 2024 continued to demonstrate a remarkable expansion in CAR-T cell therapy, with a cumulative total of over 20,000 patients treated in Europe, representing a 24.5% increase over 2023 and a 20.2-fold growth since 2018. CAR-T therapies continue to expand predominantly in NHL, while growth in MM, although substantial, remains less pronounced (Fig. 7). In 2024, CAR-T infusions for NHL increased by approximately 25%, with DLBCL accounting for 55% of indications, followed by FL (7%), MCL (7%), PMBCL (2%), and other B-cell NHL (2%). CAR-T therapy for MM increased by 50%, representing the second most frequent indication (18%), followed by ALL not in 1st CR (7%) (Fig. 8).

Over the past decade, cellular therapy activity, including autologous and allogeneic HCT as well as CAR-T therapies, has continued to expand globally, despite disruptions during the COVID-19 pandemic. Countries contributed differently to this growth, with some achieving the largest absolute increases in transplant rates and others demonstrating the most rapid proportional expansion, reflecting both capacity and adaptability of national systems. Longitudinal trends highlight the resilience and sustained development of transplant and cellular therapies, underscoring the ability of centres to maintain essential care under unprecedented conditions (Supplementary Figs. 4 and 6). These, emphasise the progress in equitable access to cellular therapies and provide a benchmark for monitoring future growth and addressing remaining disparities across countries.

Looking ahead, from 2025 onwards, the EBMT survey will implement separate reporting forms for adult and paediatric populations, capturing detailed activity across HCT, CAR-T, other cellular and gene therapies, IST, and DLI. By enabling population-specific reporting and covering the full spectrum of advanced therapies, these enhancements are transforming the EBMT survey into a comprehensive and dynamic database. It now reflects not only the rapid evolution of HCT, cellular, and gene-based therapies across Europe but also the broad range of indications in both adult and paediatric populations. The survey provides a benchmark for monitoring trends, informing clinical practice, needs and assessing equitable access to cellular therapies across countries.

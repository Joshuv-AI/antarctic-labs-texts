A 20-year bibliometric analysis on the development trends and hotspots of pectus excavatum research: 2005–2025
Authors: Zichen Zhang, Fangfang Zhang, Lijuan Zhang, Shaoshi Lin, Bangzhen Ma, Mengdi Li, Mingyu Cui, Gang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDespite significant advances in surgical techniques for pectus excavatum (PE), the evolution of research priorities and clinical foci in this field remains poorly defined. This study aims to map the thematic evolution of PE research using bibliometric methods, thereby guiding future clinical research directions.MethodsPublications on PE from 2005 to 2025 were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). Bibliometric analyses, including co-authorship, co-citation, keyword co-occurrence, burst detection, and trend analyses, were conducted to evaluate research productivity, collaboration networks, and thematic shifts. Data visualization was conducted using CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and Scimago Graphica.ResultsA total of 855 articles from 48 countries were included, demonstrating a steady increase in research output over the two decades. The United States, China, and South Korea were the primary contributors. The Catholic University of Korea, Children’s Hospital of The King’s Daughters, and Mayo Clinic were the leading institutions. Top authors by publication count included Hyung Joo Park, Robert E. Kelly Jr., and Michael J. Goretsky. Donald Nuss was the most highly cited author. The Journal of Pediatric Surgery was both the most productive and most frequently co-cited journal. Keyword analyses revealed that early research predominantly focused on surgical techniques, particularly the Nuss procedure, whereas recent trends have shifted toward postoperative pain management, intercostal nerve cryoablation, and outcome evaluation.ConclusionsThis bibliometric analysis delineates the developmental trajectory of PE research from 2005 to 2025, identifies emerging research hotspots, and highlights shifts in clinical foci. These findings may help guide the design of future clinical studies and foster collaborative efforts in the field.

• While the Nuss procedure is globally recognized as the gold standard for PE correction, a comprehensive quantitative analysis delineating the macroscopic trends and evolving research hotspots over the past two decades was lacking.

• The findings highlight the critical need to improve postoperative pain management strategies and objectively evaluate the long-term safety and effectiveness of novel bioabsorbable materials to optimize surgical outcomes.

• Future studies should prioritize large-scale, multicenter, longitudinal randomized controlled trials with age-stratified cohorts to generate high-quality evidence for standardized treatment protocols tailored to pediatric and adult populations.

Introduction

Pectus excavatum (PE), the most prevalent congenital chest wall deformity, accounts for approximately 90% of all sternal malformations (1), with an incidence of roughly 1/300–1/400 live births and a higher prevalence in males. Morphologically, PE is characterized by a depression of the sternum and costal cartilages, resulting in a funnel-shaped thorax (2). Although the etiology of PE is considered multifactorial and potentially related to abnormal costal cartilage growth, the precise pathogenic mechanisms remain elusive. Clinically, PE presents with both cosmetic concerns and functional impairments, including exercise intolerance, fatigue, dyspnea, and chest pain (3), which collectively constitute the primary indications for intervention. Since the first surgical correction was reported in 1911, the operative management of PE has evolved from highly invasive open procedures, such as the Ravitch technique, to minimally invasive approaches centered on the Nuss procedure introduced in 1998.

Over the past two decades, research on PE has expanded significantly, ranging from surgical technological innovations to etiopathogenesis. Clinically, the minimally invasive Nuss procedure has become the predominant treatment modality, with continuous technical improvements markedly enhancing surgical safety, orthopedic outcomes, and recovery times, thereby extending its application to adult and complex cases. As a representative non-surgical treatment, vacuum bell therapy (VBT) has demonstrated short- to medium-term efficacy in selected patients; however, robust long-term evidence regarding durability and recurrence rates remains limited. In parallel, basic research has explored potential genetic predisposition and molecular mechanisms; however, definitive etiological conclusions are yet to be established. Despite these advances, several challenges persist, including optimization of surgical indications, heterogeneity of clinical outcome assessment, and a paucity of long-term follow-up data on cardiopulmonary function and quality of life after correction.

Given these challenges and limitations, bibliometric analysis offers a systematic approach to mapping the research landscape of PE. Bibliometrics, a branch of informatics, utilizes quantitative and qualitative methods to analyze literature systems and their characteristics (4). By evaluating publication patterns—including co-authorship, keyword frequency, and citation metrics—bibliometric studies provide an overview of research themes, key contributors, and knowledge structure (5,6). This methodology has become a standard tool for assessing the impact and evolution of academic fields, particularly through the visualization of large-scale data (7). Utilizing visualization software such as CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and Scimago Graphica (8,9), this study aims to systematically analyze PE-related literature over the past 20 years, thereby identifying current research hotspots, emerging trends, and future directions to inform clinical and scientific inquiry. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0240/rc).

Methods

Data sources

The data for this study were obtained from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), which was selected due to its provision of standardized citation data and its status as one of the most authoritative sources for bibliometric analyses. The retrieval was conducted on January 2, 2026 using the search strategy: TS = (“funnel chest” OR “pectus excavatum”), with a search period from January 1, 2005, to December 31, 2025. The document type was restricted to “Article”. After removing duplicates and performing manual screening, a total of 855 relevant articles were included in the analysis. A flowchart of the literature screening process is provided in Figure 1.

Flowchart of literature selection and exclusion process.

To minimize potential bias, two independent researchers conducted the data collection and extraction. The data were cross-verified by both researchers to ensure accuracy and completeness. Extracted data included authors, institutions, publication years, journals, countries, and citation counts. Any discrepancies in the data were resolved through discussions between the two researchers.

Analytical methods

A bibliometric analysis was conducted using the WoSCC database, with publications categorized by year. Literature management and validation were performed using EndNote X9; Microsoft Office Excel was used for data management and statistical analysis, with frequency data visualized through graph generation. CiteSpace was employed to analyze keyword clustering, burst detection, and keyword timelines. VOSviewer was used to visualize co-authorship and collaboration networks among institutions, countries, and authors. The default parameter settings recommended by the respective software were adopted to minimize subjectivity. The results from VOSviewer were further processed using Scimago Graphica to understand the current research landscape and emerging trends in PE.

Bradford’s law, a cornerstone of bibliometric analysis, is extensively utilized to identify core journals and validate literature distribution patterns. According to this law, when the publications of a discipline over a given period are divided into three zones of approximately equal article counts, the first zone (core zone) consists of a limited number of highly productive journals, denoted as n1. The second zone includes n2 journals with moderate productivity, while the third zone comprises n3 journals, each of which contributes only a relatively small number of articles. The distribution of journals across these three zones typically adheres to the ratio n1:n2:n3 =1:a:a2 (a>1) (12). In this study, the journal distribution conformed to this pattern (Table 5): the first zone contained four journals (Journal of Pediatric Surgery, Pediatric Surgery International, The Annals of Thoracic Surgery, and European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery) producing 289 articles, the second zone included 25 journals with 312 articles, and the third zone comprised 164 journals with 254 articles. The resulting journal ratio approximates 1:6.3:41 (1:6.3:6.32), indicating that the distribution of articles in the PE field aligns with the empirical formula described by Bradford’s law. This distribution provides a valuable reference for researchers in identifying core journals for literature retrieval and manuscript submission in the PE field.

Keyword analysis

Keywords serve as essential indicators of research hotspots and emerging frontiers within a specific field (4). To systematically evaluate the temporal evolution of research themes and perspectives in PE, VOSviewer and CiteSpace were employed to generate comprehensive visual cluster maps of keywords from published literature. After excluding search-term-related keywords, the five most frequently occurring keywords were identified as: “Nuss procedure” (363 occurrences), “minimally invasive repair” (192 occurrences), “repair” (190 occurrences), “experience” (150 occurrences), and “complication” (145 occurrences).

A keyword co-occurrence network visualization was created using CiteSpace (Figure 7A), and a keyword frequency heatmap was generated using VOSviewer (Figure 7B). Cluster analysis identified seven distinct thematic categories (Figure 7C), with “complication” constituting the largest cluster, followed by “cryoablation”, “Haller index”, “pulmonary function”, “quality of life”, “thoracic surgery”, and “chest wall”.

Cryoablation, recognized for its superior efficacy in pain management, represents a pivotal strategy for achieving these objectives.

Discussion

Analysis of current research status

This systematic analysis of publication trends in PE research over the past two decades, based on data from the WoSCC database, reveals a consistent upward trajectory in scholarly output, indicative of escalating academic interest and investment in this field.

Statistical evaluation of global publication patterns by country/region and institutional affiliation clearly indicates that the United States is a leading contributor in PE research. The United States not only produced the largest volume of high-impact literature and forged the most extensive international collaborative networks but also accumulated the highest citation metrics among all nations. Notably, six of the top ten institutions by publication volume are United States-based entities, reflecting the maturity, depth, and sustained productivity of the American research ecosystem in this domain.

The bibliometric analysis of cited journals provides useful guidance for literature retrieval and manuscript submission strategies (10,13). Three journals were identified as JCR Q1 publications, with the Journal of Pediatric Surgery exhibiting the most substantial publication volume and citation impact. A significant proportion of the top ten most highly cited articles centers on the Nuss procedure and minimally invasive surgical techniques, confirming that global research efforts are predominantly focused on minimally invasive correction via the Nuss technique. Numerous studies have confirmed the clinical efficacy of this method through short-term aesthetic and functional evaluations; however, long-term follow-up studies and exploration are still required (14).

Emerging trends, hotspots, and frontiers

Keyword-based scientometric analyses provide a high-resolution perspective on how a field’s intellectual landscape evolves over time (4). In this study, keyword co-occurrence analysis was employed to delineate the thematic structure of PE research, thereby identifying its pivotal hotspots and prospective trajectories. Cluster analysis of keywords yielded seven distinct clusters. Subsequent analysis of the top 20 keywords with the strongest citation bursts identified research hotspots and frontiers, providing valuable insights including the Nuss procedure, minimally invasive treatment, disease prognosis, and pain management. The significance of these areas to the field and potential implications for future research are discussed below.

Nuss procedure research

To date, the co-occurrence network shows that “Nuss procedure” remains the most prevalent keyword. Since its introduction in 1998, the Nuss procedure has been widely adopted and continuously refined (15), currently serving as the preferred surgical correction for PE. To mitigate early complications (e.g., bar displacement, cardiac injury risk), numerous modifications have been implemented to enhance safety and efficacy, including thoracoscopic assistance, short Nuss bar (16), stabilizers, pericostal sutures, pre-tunnel sternal elevation, specialized instrumentation and optimized pain management protocols (14,17).

Advancements in materials science, particularly the adoption of titanium alloys with enhanced biocompatibility, have minimized adverse reactions such as metal hypersensitivity. The development of bioabsorbable materials signifies a major clinical progression, which may eventually eliminate secondary bar removal surgery (18). Recent studies have suggested the feasibility of three-dimensional (3D)-printed biodegradable polylactide (PLA) Nuss bars, indicating their future potential to simultaneously correct PE and alleviate treatment-related pain (19). In complex pediatric cases, bioabsorbable poly-L-lactic acid (PLLA) mesh plates have been used for temporary thoracic reconstruction, helping to maintain mediastinal space without restricting skeletal growth (20). This trajectory is anticipated to persist, with continuous refinements in techniques and instrumentation.

Research on minimally invasive treatment

Keywords associated with minimally invasive repair not only manifest as high-frequency nodes, but also function as critical connectors bridging distinct clusters related to surgical techniques, complications, and perioperative management. Following extensive refinement over the past decades, minimally invasive treatment has emerged as the predominant approach in PE correction. Given that the primary motivation for surgical intervention among patients is aesthetic concern and social acceptability, the minimally invasive approach—characterized by smaller incisions positioned away from the anterior chest wall—effectively addresses this fundamental need (21), thereby solidifying the Nuss procedure’s position as the technique of choice. Minimally invasive treatment significantly enhances personal confidence and self-esteem, facilitating improved social integration (18,22). Compared to traditional surgical approaches, it demonstrates substantially reduced complication rates, with its efficacy and safety substantiated by evidence from over 1,200 cases across multiple centers and medical literature (17,23,24).

However, the adoption of minimally invasive treatment in adult PE patients remains comparatively limited; this may be related to underlying age-related differences: pediatric patients have a more compliant and malleable cartilaginous anatomy, whereas adult patients show progressive chest wall calcification and increased rigidity, making sternal elevation more difficult and the procedure more challenging. These factors contribute to elevated bar displacement risk due to greater pressure distribution on the implant (25). Postoperative complication rates are also markedly elevated in adult populations, with studies consistently documenting substantially higher complication rates in adults compared to pediatric patients (26). Notwithstanding these challenges, with advanced preoperative assessment protocols and targeted technical modifications, minimally invasive treatment is progressively gaining acceptance in adult patients, achieving satisfactory postoperative outcomes and expanding its therapeutic applicability (25,27).

Research on disease prognosis

Temporal trend analysis demonstrates a robust association between keyword clusters and disease prognosis trajectories. Notably, clusters #0 “complication” and #4 “quality of life” have been the focus of sustained scholarly investigation throughout the past two decades. This enduring research emphasis suggests that despite the widespread implementation of surgical correction for PE, optimizing postoperative recovery and enhancing long-term quality of life continue to present complex challenges, necessitating sustained scholarly attention.

Patients with PE experience not only cosmetic and psychological concerns but also cardiopulmonary dysfunction due to sternal depression (28), manifesting as exertional dyspnea, tachycardia, dizziness, and chest pain, which collectively contribute to substantial impairment in overall quality of life (29). While the impact of surgical treatment on cardiopulmonary function and exercise tolerance remains controversial (30), empirical evidence suggests measurable functional improvements in specific patient populations. Jaroszewski et al. documented significant postoperative improvement in cardiopulmonary function in the majority of pediatric patients through comprehensive cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) (29). Similarly, Kuyama et al. reported significant improvements in key pulmonary function parameters [forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1), vital capacity (VC), peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR)] following Nuss procedure in children aged ≤10 years (31).

Despite favorable short-term outcomes, high-quality longitudinal data on ultra-long-term prognosis (≥20 years post-Nuss procedure), particularly concerning dynamic changes in cardiopulmonary function and mental health, remain notably scarce. The precise impact of the Nuss procedure on pulmonary function remains controversial due to inconsistent study findings and the absence of standardized long-term assessment protocols. Consequently, future research should prioritize the development of harmonized outcome definitions and extended follow-up periods, particularly for cardiopulmonary function and patient-centered outcomes, to reduce fragmentation and facilitate more robust cross-study comparisons.

Research on postoperative pain management

Burst detection provides the most definitive signal of emerging research frontiers, and in this study, pain-related terms—especially “intercostal nerve cryoablation” along with “analgesia”, “pain”, “outcome”, and “cryoanalgesia”—were identified as prominent burst keywords, indicating a significant surge in academic interest regarding postoperative pain management in recent years. Postoperative pain management represents a critical determinant of successful recovery. With the widespread implementation of enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocols, minimizing postoperative pain and reducing opioid consumption has become a primary research focus (32). To achieve these objectives, diverse analgesic strategies have been implemented, including thoracic epidural analgesia (TEA), patient-controlled analgesia (PCA), subcutaneous catheters, and more recently, cryoablation therapy (25,33,34).

Intercostal nerve cryoablation during the Nuss procedure has emerged as a promising therapeutic innovation. Cryoablation provides a prolonged-duration regional nerve block, with analgesic effects extending beyond those of conventional analgesic protocols (35), effectively covering the entire postoperative recovery period. Sensory recovery following cryoablation is gradual, presenting a distinct advantage over short-acting blocks or epidural analgesia (36). The Nuss procedure is particularly amenable to cryoanalgesia, owing to the clear visualization of intercostal nerves via thoracoscopy (37). This accessibility is especially beneficial for adults, who tend to experience more severe postoperative pain due to increased chest wall rigidity and higher bar-related tension, making interventions such as cryoablation crucial in this population.

Comparative studies have demonstrated superior clinical outcomes with intercostal nerve cryoablation relative to traditional methods (32,38), with a 2-day reduction in average hospital length of stay and a significant decrease in opioid requirements during hospitalization (36). This approach not only reduces opioid dependence but also lowers the incidence of opioid-related adverse effects, thereby enhancing overall patient recovery and satisfaction (39).

Limitations

Although this study provides a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of PE research, several limitations warrant careful consideration. First, to ensure methodological rigor in bibliometric analysis, the study was confined to articles indexed in the WoSCC database, potentially omitting relevant scholarly contributions from other sources. Second, the scope of included literature was restricted to research articles, excluding non-research publications such as editorials, commentaries, and books, which may offer valuable perspectives on the socio-political dimensions of PE research. Third, bibliometric approaches are inherently limited in assessing the quality of individual studies, as citation metrics exhibit temporal dependency and citation counts for recently published articles may be substantially underestimated—a fundamental constraint of bibliometric methodologies (4,40). Finally, methodological inaccuracies were unavoidable due to the extensive vocabulary involved in manual synonym grouping and cluster identification. Nevertheless, considering the extensive dataset derived from a substantial body of peer-reviewed literature, these limitations are unlikely to significantly alter the primary research trends and thematic patterns presented in this analysis.

Conclusions

This study presents a comprehensive bibliometric overview of PE research over the past two decades, systematically analyzing publication trends, key contributors, collaborative networks, and evolving research themes. The findings indicate that research in this field is undergoing continuous evolution, characterized by expanding thematic breadth and increasing clinical complexity, reflecting the field’s maturation and growing interdisciplinary engagement.

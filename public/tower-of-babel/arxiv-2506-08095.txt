Altermagnet–Superconductor Heterostructure: a Scalable Platform for Braiding of Majorana Modes
Themba Hodge, Eric Mascot, Stephan Rachel

Abstract
Topological quantum computation, featuring qubits built out of anyonic excitations known as Majorana zero modes (MZMs), have long presented an exciting pathway towards scalable quantum computation. Recently, the advent of altermagnetic materials has presented a new pathway towards localized MZMs on the boundary of two-dimensional materials, consisting of an altermagnetic film, subject to a superconducting proximity effect from a superconducting substrate. In this work, we demonstrate the possibility for an altermagnet-superconductor heterostructure, to not only harbor MZMs, but also freely manipulate their position along the topological boundary of the material, via rotation of the Néel vector. Using this mechanism, on a square platform, we utilize a time-dependent method to simulate the Z-gate via braiding , and then extend this to a larger H-junction , where we implement the X \sqrt{\rm{X}} and Z \sqrt{\rm{Z}} gate on a single-qubit system. Further, this structure is eminently scalable to many-qubit systems, thus providing the essential ingredients towards universal quantum computation.

Model.—

The AM-SC heterostructure is well-described by the following
Boguliubov-de Gennes
(BdG) Hamiltonian on the square lattice,
\hat{H}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}\Psi^{{\dagger}}(\bm{k})H_{\rm{BdG}}(\bm{k})\Psi(\bm{k}).

Here,
\Psi(\bm{k})=(c_{\bm{k},\uparrow},c_{\bm{k},\downarrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k},\uparrow},c^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k},\downarrow})^{T}
, with
\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
denoting spin degrees of freedom in the model.
We consider a coupling between a
d
-wave altermagnet and a helical
p
-wave superconductor on a square lattice. The former has been reported in Ref.
Bai et al. 2023
, while the latter is expected to occur for typical Rashba-superconductors
Vafek and Wang 2011
;
Beyer et al. 2023
.
The Hamiltonian is given by
H_{\rm{BdG}}(\bm{k})=H_{0}(\bm{k})+H_{\Delta}(\bm{k})+H_{\rm{AM}}(\bm{k})
,
where
H_{0}
is the kinetic energy including SOC,
H_{\Delta}(\bm{k})
a helical
p\pm ip
superconducting order parameter and
H_{\rm{AM}}(\bm{k})
the altermagnetic contribution.
On the square lattice, with only nearest neighbour couplings considered, each term will be given by the following:

\displaystyle H_{0}(\bm{k})=\epsilon(k)\tau_{3}\sigma_{0}+2A\left(\textrm{sin}(k_{y})\tau_{0}\sigma_{1}-\textrm{sin}(k_{x})\tau_{3}\sigma_{2}\right),

(1)

\displaystyle H_{\Delta}(\bm{k})=\Delta_{0}\tau_{2}\sigma_{2}+2\Delta_{p}(\textrm{sin}(k_{x})\tau_{1}\sigma_{3}+\textrm{sin}(k_{y})\tau_{2}\sigma_{0}),

\displaystyle H_{\rm{AM}}(\bm{k})=2J_{\textrm{AM}}\phi_{d}(k)({n}_{1}\tau_{3}\sigma_{1}+{n}_{2}\tau_{0}\sigma_{2}+{n}_{3}\tau_{3}\sigma_{3}),

with dispersion
\epsilon(k)=-2t\left(\rm{cos}(k_{x})+\rm{cos}(k_{y})\right)-\mu
and an AM
d
-wave form-factor,
\phi_{d}(k)=\rm{cos}(k_{x})-\rm{cos}(k_{y})
, with
J_{\rm{AM}}
the strength of the AM spin-splitting.
Further,
\Delta_{p}
, (
\Delta_{0}
) correspond to the superconducting gap of the helical
p
(
s
)-wave order parameters.
Finally,
A
parametrizes the magnitude of SOC in the system.
The Néel vector,
\bm{n}=\big({n}_{1},{n}_{2},{n}_{3}\big)=\big(\rm{cos}(\phi_{\rm{AM}})\rm{sin}(\theta_{\rm{AM}}),\rm{sin}(\phi_{\rm{AM}})\rm{sin}(\theta_{\rm{AM}}),\rm{cos}(\theta_{\rm{AM}})\big)
, provides the direction for the magnetic order of the compensated spin-lattice structure.
Finally, the
\sigma_{i}
,
\tau_{i}
represent pauli-matrices in spin and particle-hole space, respectively.

The superconductor,
without the Rashba SOC contribution (i.e.,
A=0
) or the AM contribution (
J_{\rm{AM}}=0
), will preserve a
D_{4h}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\{1,\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T}\}
symmetry, with
\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{C}
a time-reversal, particle-hole, and chiral symmetry respectively. This corresponds to a topological class DIII with helical edge modes arising on the boundary (
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
invariant)
Altland and Zirnbauer 1997
;
Fu et al. 2007
.
However, when Rashba SOC is present (i.e.
A\not=0
), the inversion symmetry
\mathcal{I}
along with the spin-rotation symmetry is broken, reducing the lattice symmetry of
D_{4h}\times\rm{SU}(2)
to
C_{4v}
.
This weak Rashba contribution can be largely attributed to the breaking of inversion on the boundary heterostructure interface
Bychkov and Rashba 1984
.
The AM then additionally breaks the global
\mathcal{T}
-symmetry, reducing
\{1,\mathcal{C},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T}\}\to\{1,\mathcal{P}\}
.
However, for
\bm{n}={n}_{3}\hat{z}
, a composite
C_{4z}\mathcal{T}
symmetry is conserved, where
C_{4z}
corresponds to a spatial rotation about the
\hat{z}
axis, with Dirac mass-inversions along each corner
Wu and Wang 2022
;
Ghorashi et al. 2024
.
Additionally, for
\bm{n}=\hat{x}
(
\hat{y}
), the Hamiltonian satisfies a chiral mirror symmetry
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}_{x}
(
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{M}_{y}
), with
\mathcal{M}_{x}
(
\mathcal{M}_{y}
) a mirror symmetry about the
x

(y)
-axis.
This leads to a closing of the edge gap on parallel edges of the structure, with additional choices on the Néel vector leading to Dirac mass inversions about opposite corners.
In both cases, we see the formation of local MZMs on the corners via a Jackiw-Rebbi mechanism
Jackiw and Rebbi 1976
.

Fractional Statistics.—

We now look to utilize these tunable MZMs for the purpose of implementing a braid, beginning with the simplest example of two MZMs on a square platform.

This is very similar to models considered previously

Using this infrastructure, we encode the Z-gate.
As discussed previously, initializing the Néel vector at an azimuthal angle of

The braids are then encoded by moving the MZMs along the boundary of the square platform.

Non-Abelian statistics on the H-junction.—

For the purpose of TQC, we need now consider an architecture that will admit arbitrary
B_{ij}=\textrm{exp}(\frac{\pi}{4}\gamma_{i}\gamma_{j})

To do this, we introduce the

To initialize, we set the topological domain of the middle platform to the trivial regime.

This reveals the effective advantage in tuning the MZMs arbitrarily around the square platform, in comparison to methods where the MZMs are restricted to arise on opposite corners of the platform.
Here, we are able to implement the exchange while avoiding accidental collisions, leading to possible fusion events, between the exchanging MZMs.
Further, we are able to conduct the braid without creating any additional MZMs over the time-evolution, which would introduce additional sources of error over the process.

Discussion and Outlook.—

While we keep simulations to two and four MZM systems, the demonstration of the
\sqrt{\rm{X}}
and
\sqrt{\rm{Z}}
, in the Z and X basis respectively, confirms two things: (i) the routine clearly preserves the braiding statistics expected from the exchange of two MZMs and (ii) in a system with four MZMs, we are able to demonstrate the unitaries required to generate the Pauli-group on the ground-state manifold.
Integrally, similar to the oft-discussed T-junction for the Kitaev chain
Alicea et al. 2011
, by adjoining individual H junctions, this single-qubit platform is clearly scalable to many qubit systems (see SM for further discussion).
Further, we stress that, in essence, this H geometry may be utilized to braid on any higher order platform, with tunable MZMs.
While we have not demonstrated the final two pieces required to generate a universal gate set, the T-gate and a two-qubit entangling gate (e.g. CNOT), the mechanisms required to implement these gates (through an MZM hybridization procedure
Hodge et al. 2025a
and a two-qubit set of braids in the dense encoding
Lahtinen and Pachos 2017
;
Mascot et al. 2023
) are eminently possible on this platform, which we leave to future work.
Further, investigation into the effects of disorder
Boross and Pályi 2024
;
Peeters et al. 2024
, charge noise, along with measurement protocols via MZM fusion
Zhou et al. 2022
;
Hodge et al. 2025b
, are essential in testing the validity of the structure as a potential platform for TQC.
However, what is clear is that these AM-SC heterostructure platforms provide a unique and interesting candidate structure for MZM braiding, and thus TQC.

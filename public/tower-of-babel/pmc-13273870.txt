Protocol for antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis assay with controlled IgG concentrations in tested samples
Authors: Russell St. Germain, Anton M. Sholukh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryHere, we present a protocol to equalize antibody concentrations between samples to focus analysis on IgG properties rather than phagocytosis driven by antibody concentration. We describe steps for quantitating antigen-specific IgG in serum using a suitable binding assay, diluting samples to equalize IgG concentration, and using the antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP) assay. We then detail procedures for measuring differences in antibody properties between experimental groups by running diluted samples in the ADCP assay or other effector function assays.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to St. Germain et al.1

Before you begin

The purpose of this protocol is to measure serum concentration of antigen-specific IgG and equalize it between different samples for the following antibody effector function assay. This allows exclusion of effect of IgG concentration on the effector function assay readout. Serum samples must be prepared and treated according to the appropriate safety guidelines including heat-inactivation, sterility and biosafety level. Appropriate institutional permissions should be obtained prior to sample collection and/or analysis.

Innovation

We introduce a normalization-first workflow for serum Antibody-Dependent Cellular Phagocytosis (ADCP) that removes a major, underappreciated source of bias in Fc-effector profiling. Standard ADCP protocols typically apply a uniform serum dilution, causing samples with higher baseline antigen-specific IgG concentrations to appear more active thus obscuring qualitative differences in antigen-specific IgG that affect effector functions. Our approach inserts a quantitative immunoassay step prior to ADCP to measure antigen-specific IgG concentration and then equalize samples to the same antigen-specific IgG input. We implement this using a bead-based Multiplex Binding Antigen Assay (MBAA) that reports antigen-specific IgG in absolute units (μg/mL), enabling precise normalization across cohorts and timepoints. By holding antigen-specific IgG concentration constant, downstream ADCP readouts become interpretable as a function of IgG quality—epitope specificity, subclass distribution, and Fc glycosylation—rather than dominated by antibody abundance. This improves comparability and rigor in identifying ADCP as a correlate of protection. This approach can be used to measure other antibody effector functions.

Institutional permissions

Informed consent was obtained from all human subjects, including blood donors, involved in the study. Plasma samples from SARS-CoV-2 seropositive, vaccinated individuals were obtained from Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center (FHCC) repository assembled from a COVID-19 seroepidemiology study conducted in a single county in the western US, FHCRC IRB#10453 and from University of Washington repository assembled from Seattle-area participants that were recruited for potential donation of single-donor plasma units (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04338360), and plasma for manufacture of a pooled anti–SARS-CoV-2 product (NCT04344977). Plasma from seronegative COVID vaccine recipients was obtained from either the same studies or from healthy volunteers at FHCRC (approved by the FHCRC Institutional Review Board, IRB#3612).

This assay can be used for the analyses of human subject samples such as plasma or serum therefore appropriate approval should be acquired from Institution Review Board (IRB) for the use of human subject samples prior sample analysis.

Expected outcomes

This two-stage assay will allow to obtain two important parameters: concentration of antigen-specific IgG and Phagocytic score that reflects intrinsic differences in antigen-specific IgG between samples. Because concentration of antigen-specific IgG is the major contributing factor to ADCP assay readout, high concentration can mask poor ability of IgG to induce cellular phagocytosis leading to misinterpretation of ADCP assay data. Post-assay normalization by IgG concentration and/or titer might not be feasible due to different dynamic range of binding and ADCP assays. Therefore, equilibration of different samples by concentration of antigen-specific IgG prior to ADCP and other effector function assays is critical to reveal differences in ADCP between samples that are due to difference in IgG glycosylation, epitope specificity and breadth or other IgG intrinsic factors that affect interaction between IgG and Fcγ receptors on phagocytic cells. This approach will be especially useful for longitudinal analysis across patients and cohorts involving severe infections and/or vaccination with mRNA-based vaccines that can elicit very high antibody levels.

Limitations

While this protocol can provide an increased robustness and completeness to data collection, a limitation of this protocol is the timing. Adding an additional step of IgG concentration measurement decreases sample throughput. However, both the MBAA and ADCP assays can be automated and run in high throughput setting thus allowing stepwise sample processing.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Variability in MFI readout between ADCP experiments (Step 37).

Potential solution

•Keep THP-1 cell line at consistent cell concentration during culturing. Allowing cells to grow to high concentrations can lead to loss of cell surface marker expression.•Keep NeutrAvidin beads protected from light as much as possible to prevent photobleaching.•Internal quality controls such serum samples with high, medium and low phagocytic score should be included in each assay to directly compare changes in MFI. Including control mAb can also be considered.

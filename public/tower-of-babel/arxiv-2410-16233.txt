Unique subgraphs are rare
Domagoj Bradač, Micha Christoph

Abstract
A folklore result attributed to Pólya states that there are ( 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ 2 ( n 2 ) / n ! (1+o(1))2^{\binom{n}{2}}/n! non-isomorphic graphs on n n vertices. Given two graphs G G and H H , we say that G G is a unique subgraph of H H if H H contains exactly one subgraph isomorphic to G G . For an n n -vertex graph H H , let f ⁡ ( H ) f(H) be the number of non-isomorphic unique subgraphs of H H divided by 2 ( n 2 ) / n ! 2^{\binom{n}{2}}/n! and let f ⁡ ( n ) f(n) denote the maximum of f ⁡ ( H ) f(H) over all graphs H H on n n vertices. In 1975, Erdős asked whether there exists δ > 0 \delta>0 such that f ⁡ ( n ) > δ f(n)>\delta for all n n and offered $ 100 \$100 for a proof and $ 25 \$25 for a disproof, indicating he does not believe this to be true. Introduction

Given a family of graphs
\mathcal{F},
we say that a graph
H
is (induced) universal for
\mathcal{F}
if it contains every graph in
\mathcal{F}
as an (induced) subgraph. The study of universal graphs was initiated by Rado
[
24
]
who rediscovered an old construction of Ackermann
[
2
]
of a countable graph which contains all countable graphs as induced subgraphs. Since then, the focus has been on finite families
\mathcal{F}
and constructing small universal graphs, either with few vertices or with few edges.

Alon
[
4
]
constructed a graph on
(1+o(1))2^{(k-1)/2}
vertices which is induced universal for the family of all graphs on
k
vertices and a simple counting argument shows this is tight up to an
(1+o(1))
-factor. Most of the remaining work has focused on (not induced) universal graphs for families of sparse graphs such as graphs with bounded maximum degree
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
, forests
[
12
,
17
,
13
]
and graphs with bounded degeneracy
[
3
,
23
]
. Many of these results have recently been unified by Alon, Dodson, Jackson, McCarty, Nenadov and Southern
[
9
]
and we refer to their paper and the references therein for more details about the history of universal graphs.

It should be mentioned that for many natural families
\mathcal{F}
, a tight lower bound on the size of the universal graph is given by simple counting arguments. Moreover, these arguments often imply that if a graph is significantly smaller than the given lower bound, then in fact it contains a
o(1)
-proportion of the members of
\mathcal{F}
as (induced) subgraphs.

We move on to defining the problem which is the main topic of this paper. An asymptotic formula for the number of non-isomoprhic graphs on
n
vertices is a folklore result often attributed to Pólya. It seems that Pólya himself never explicitly wrote this down but see, for example, the argument of Wright
[
25
]
from 1971 for an estimate of the number of unlabelled graphs on
n
vertices with exactly
m
edges, which also implies the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Pólya; Wright)
.

The number of non-isomorphic graphs on
n
vertices is
(1+o(1))\frac{2^{\binom{n}{2}}}{n!}.

In 1972, Entringer and Erdős
[
14
]
studied the following universality type question. We say that a graph
G
is a unique subgraph of
H
, denoted by
G\subseteq^{*}H
, if
H
contains exactly one subgraph isomorphic to
G
, where we remind the reader that a subgraph of a graph is defined as a subset of its vertices and a subset of its edges on that set of vertices. For an
n
-vertex graph
H
, we define

f(H):=\frac{|\{G\,|\,G\subseteq^{*}H\}|}{2^{\binom{n}{2}}/n!},

and let
f(n)
be the maximum of
f(H)
over all graphs
H
on
n
vertices.
Entringer and Erdős showed that there exists a constant
c
such that for sufficiently large
n
,
f(n)\geq e^{-cn^{3/2}}
. Harary and Schwenk
[
18
]
subsequently improved the lower bound to
e^{-cn\log n}
. The current best known bound is
f(n)\geq e^{-cn}
, given by Brouwer
[
11
]
. In a paper from 1975, Erdős
[
15
]
asked whether there exists
\delta>0
such that
f(n)>\delta
for all
n
and offered
\$100
for a proof and
\$25
for a disproof. The problem also appears as Problem #426 on Bloom’s Erdős problems website
[
1
]
. We answer this question in the negative.

Theorem 1.2
.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(n)=0.

One way to view
Theorem

1.2
is as an anti-concentration result. Using Theorem
1.1
, it is not hard to show that
f(H)
is up to an additive
o(1)
term equal to the probability that a random graph
G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
is a unique subgraph of
H
. Given a graph
H
on
n
vertices and
G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
, the expected number of embeddings of
G
into
H
equals
n!\cdot 2^{-\binom{n}{2}+e(H)}
. If
H
has the right number of edges then this expectation can be close to
1
. Nonetheless, as our result shows, the probability that there is exactly one copy of
G
is
o(1)
. Furthermore, a key ingredient of our proof is anti-concentration of the number of edges of
\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
.

This interpretation of our result seems quite similar to the study of the parameter
\mathrm{ind}(k,\ell)
introduced by Alon, Hefetz, Krivelevich and Tyomkyn
[
10
]
, where
\mathrm{ind}(k,\ell)
is defined as the limit, as
n
goes to infinity, of the maximum fraction of induced subgraphs on
k
vertices which also have exactly
\ell
edges over all graphs on
n
vertices. This parameter has a natural similarity to
f
. Again, one may pick a graph
H
on
n
vertices with the appropriate number of edges such that the expected number of edges in any induced subgraph on
k
vertices is
\ell
. If
0<\ell<\binom{k}{2}
, anti-concentration comes into play. Confirming conjectures from
[
10
]
, in a series of papers, Kwan, Sudakov and Tran
[
21
]
, Fox and Sauermann
[
16
]
and Martinsson, Mousset, Noever and Trujić
[
22
]
showed that for all other values of
\ell
,
\mathrm{ind}(k,\ell)
essentially does not exceed
1/e
and most recently, with their work on the quadratic Littlewood-Offord problem, Kwan and Sauermann
[
20
]
showed that
\mathrm{ind}(k,\ell)\leq O(1/\sqrt{k})
for some range of
\ell
, which is asymptotically tight.

Notation, terminology and preliminaries.
We use mostly standard notation. For a positive integer
n
, we denote by
\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
, the binomial random graph with edge probability
1/2
. Equivalently, this is a uniformly random labelled graph on
n
vertices. For
0\leq m\leq\binom{n}{2}
, we denote by
\mathcal{G}(n,m)
the uniform distribution on labelled graphs with
n
vertices and
m
edges. Additionally, denoting
N=\binom{n}{2}
, the random graph process
\widetilde{G}=(G_{m})_{m=0}^{N}
is a sequence of labelled graphs
G_{0}\subseteq G_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq G_{N}
on
n
vertices, where
G_{0}
is the empty graph and, for
1\leq m\leq N,
the graph
G_{m}
is obtained from
G_{m-1}
by adding a uniformly random edge not in
G_{m-1}
. Note that
|E(G_{m})|=m
and that
G_{m}
is distributed as
\mathcal{G}_{n,m}
.
We denote by
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
the automorphism group of
G
. Note that for every graph
|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|\geq 1
, since
G
always has the trivial automorphism. Given two graphs
G
and
H
, an embedding of
G
into
H
is an injective function
\phi\colon V(G)\rightarrow V(H)
such that
uv\in E(G)\implies\phi(u)\phi(v)\in E(H)
. Similar to the use of
\subseteq^{*}
, we write
G\rightarrow^{*}H
if there exists a unique embedding of
G
into
H
. Throughout the paper,
n
will denote the number of vertices in a graph
H
and all our asymptotic notation is for
n
tending to infinity. For clarity, we avoid the use of floor and ceiling signs whenever they are not crucial to the argument.

1.1
Proof sketch

For the sake of contradiction, we assume there is a constant
\delta>0
such that
f(n)\geq\delta
for all
n
. We take a large enough
n
compared to
\delta
and consider a graph
H
on
n
vertices with
f(H)\geq\delta
.

The proof is split into three steps, formulated in
Lemmas

2.1
,
2.2
and
2.4
.

In the proof of
Theorem

1.2
, we have to treat graphs
G
with more than one automorphism delicately, as, for such
G
, it is possible that
G
has more than one embedding into
H
but, nonetheless,
G
is a unique subgraph of
H
. Using
Theorem

1.1
and a simple counting argument, Lemma
2.1
shows that
f(H)
is, up to an additive
o(1)
term, equal to
\mathbb{P}_{G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)}[G\rightarrow^{*}H]
. This allows us to look at
G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
(the uniform distribution on labelled graphs) instead of the uniform distribution on unlabelled graphs on at most
n
vertices as well as to ignore the delicate case when
G
has non-trivial automorphisms.

In
Lemma

2.2
, we show that
e(H)\geq\binom{n}{2}-Cn
for some large enough constant
C=C(\delta)
. Towards this, we observe that
e(G)
, where
G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
, with high probability lies in an interval of size linear in
n
around
\binom{n}{2}/2
, while being more or less uniformly distributed within this interval. Shortly switching to the random graph process instead of
\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
, we use the previous observation and
Lemma

2.1
to conclude that there must exist a graph
G^{*}
with
G^{*}\rightarrow^{*}H
and if we, uniformly at random, add
\Omega(n)
many missing edges to
G^{*}
then it still has a unique embedding into
H
with constant probability. A simple calculation then shows that this implies that
H
has
O(n)
non-edges.

Finally,
Lemma

2.4
states that if
H
has
O(n)
non-edges, then the probability that there is a unique embedding of
G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
into
H
is
o(1)
. Therefore, this concludes the proof together with
Lemma

2.1
. Note that the expected number of embeddings of
G
into
H
is
n!\cdot 2^{\binom{n}{2}-e(H)}\gg 1
for
H
with
O(n)
non-edges. Thus, at first glance, it seems we want to show that with high probability, there are at least two embeddings of
G
into
H
. However, this need not be the case, for example if
H
has an isolated vertex. In some sense, the vertices of large non-degree in
H
are the only obstruction possibly preventing
G
to have many embeddings into
H
with high probability. We show that if there is an embedding of
G
into
H
, then with high probability there are two vertices in
H
with
O(1)
non-neighbors whose roles we can switch to obtain a new embedding of
G
. Our proof uses a union bound over all
n!
initial embeddings and Azuma’s inequality to show that for any fixed initial embedding, there exists a way to switch two vertices with probability
1-o(1/n!)
.

2.1
Reduction to random labelled graphs

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex graph. Then,

f(H)=\mathbb{P}_{G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)}[G\rightarrow^{*}H]+o(1).

Proof.

Let
UG(n)
denote the set of unlabelled graphs on
n
vertices and let
LG(n)
denote the set of all labelled graphs on
n
vertices. Note that

2^{\binom{n}{2}}=|LG(n)|=\sum_{G\in UG(n)}\frac{n!}{|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|}\leq n!\cdot|UG(n)|-n!/2\cdot\big|\{G\in UG(n)\,|\,|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|\geq 2\}\big|.

Using Theorem
1.1
and dividing by
n!
yields

\big|\{G\in UG(n)\,|\,|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|\geq 2\}\big|=o\left(\frac{2^{\binom{n}{2}}}{n!}\right).

(1)

Note that by Theorem
1.1
, the number of unlabelled graphs on at most
n-1
vertices is
o\left(\frac{2^{\binom{n}{2}}}{n!}\right)
. Using this and (
1
), we have

\displaystyle f(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{2^{\binom{n}{2}}}\cdot\big|\{G\in UG(n)\,|\,G\subseteq^{*}H\}\big|+o(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{2^{\binom{n}{2}}}\cdot\big|\{G\in UG(n)\,|\,G\subseteq^{*}H\land|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|=1\}\big|+o(1)

\displaystyle=2^{-\binom{n}{2}}\cdot\big|\{G\in LG(n)\,|\,G\subseteq^{*}H\land|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|=1\}\big|+o(1)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{G\in\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)}[G\subseteq^{*}H\land|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|=1]+o(1).\hskip 8.5359pt

To conclude the proof, observe that
G\rightarrow^{*}H
if and only if
G\subseteq^{*}H
and
|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|=1
.
∎

Reduction to the very dense case

Lemma 2.2
.

For any

Fix

\mathbb{P}[G\rightarrow^{*}H\land e(G)\in I]\geq\delta/4.

Set

\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{Bin}(N,1/2)=t]\leq\mathbb{P}[\mathrm{Bin}(N,1/2)=N/2]\leq 1/\sqrt{N}.

\displaystyle\delta/4

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big[G\rightarrow^{*}H\land e(G)\in I\big]=\sum_{m\in I}\mathbb{P}\big[e(G)=m\big]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[G_{m}\rightarrow^{*}H\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{n}\sum_{m\in I}\mathbb{P}\big[G_{m}\rightarrow^{*}H\big]=\frac{2}{n}\E\big[X\big].

Therefore

Since
X\leq|I|\leq 3Ln,
we have

\mathbb{P}[X\geq\delta n/16]\geq\frac{\delta}{48L}.

If

let (the random variable)
m^{*}
denote the minimum value of

such that
G_{m}\rightarrow^{*}H
. By the law of total probability, there exists a graph

\mathbb{P}_{\widetilde{G}}\left[X\geq\delta n/16\,|\,G_{m^{*}}=G^{*}\right]\geq\frac{\delta}{48L}.

Let
m_{2}=m^{*}+\delta n/16-1
. Observe that the number of embeddings of

into

is non-increasing in

. Consequently, the set of

forms an interval. Therefore, if

and

then

.
It follows that

\mathbb{P}\left[G_{m_{2}}\rightarrow^{*}H\,|\,G_{m^{*}}=G^{*}\right]\geq\mathbb{P}\left[X\geq\delta n/16\,|\,G_{m^{*}}=G^{*}\right]\geq\frac{\delta}{48L}.

Let
\pi
be the unique embedding from
G^{*}
into
H
. Since
G_{m^{*}}\subseteq G_{m_{2}},
if
G_{m_{2}}\rightarrow^{*}H
, then it has the same unique embedding

. Note that, given
G_{m^{*}}=G^{*},
the graph

is uniformly distributed among all supergraphs of

with

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[G_{m_{2}}\rightarrow^{*}H\,|\,G_{m^{*}}=G^{*}\big]=\frac{\binom{e(H)-m^{*}}{m_{2}-m^{*}}}{\binom{N-m^{*}}{m_{2}-m^{*}}}\leq\left(\frac{e(H)-m^{*}}{N-m^{*}}\right)^{m_{2}-m^{*}}\leq\left(\frac{e(H)}{N}\right)^{\delta n/16-1}.

Assume that

\mathbb{P}\big[G_{m_{2}}\rightarrow^{*}H\,|\,G_{m^{*}}=G^{*}\big]\leq(1-Cn/N)^{\delta n/17}\leq e^{-\frac{Cn^{2}\delta}{17N}}<\frac{\delta}{48L},

where we took

\pi(u)
and
\pi(N_{H^{c}}(v)\setminus N_{H^{c}}(u))
. For every
w\in V(H)
, let us denote by
d_{w}=|N_{H^{c}}(w)\cap T|
. For distinct
y,z\in V(G),
we denote by
b_{yz}
the number of terms of
S
that depend on the presence of the edge
yz
in
G
.

Note that if
d_{w}\geq n/\log^{6D}n,
then
T\subseteq N_{H^{c}}(w),
so
b_{\pi(w)\pi(u)}=0
for all
u\in V(H^{c})\setminus\{w\}.
Furthermore, recall that
T\subseteq B^{\prime}
is an independent set. Therefore,
b_{\pi(u)\pi(v)}=0
for any
u,v\in T
. If
u,v\not\in T,
then also trivially
b_{\pi(u)\pi(v)}=0
. Finally, if
w\not\in T,
then for any
u\in T,
presence of the edge
\pi(w)\pi(u)
can only influence variables
X_{\{u,v\}}
for
v\in N_{H^{c}}(w)
. Thus, for
w\not\in T,u\in T,
we have
b_{\pi(u)\pi(w)}\leq d_{w}.
Denote by
R
the set of vertices
w
in
V(H)\setminus T
with
d_{w}<n/\log^{6D}n.
Observe that

\sum_{w\in R}d_{w}^{2}\leq n/\log^{6D}n\cdot\sum_{w\in R}d_{w}\leq 2Cn^{2}/\log^{6D}n.

Putting these observations together, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{y,z\in V(G)}b_{yz}^{2}\leq|T|\cdot\sum_{w\in R}d_{w}^{2}\leq|T|\cdot 2Cn^{2}/\log^{6D}n\leq\frac{2Cn^{3}}{\log^{6D}n}.

By
Theorem

2.3
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[S=0]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{-2(\E[X])^{2}}{\sum_{y,z\in V(G)}b_{yz}^{2}}\right)\leq\exp\left(\frac{-\left(2^{-8C}\binom{|T|}{2}\right)^{2}\cdot\log^{6D}n}{Cn^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(2^{-16C-4}n\log^{2D}n/C\right)\leq e^{-n\log n},

finishing the proof.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

For the sake of contradiction, suppose there is a constant
\delta>0
such that
f(n)\geq\delta
for all large enough
n
. So assume that
n
is large enough and let
H
be a graph on
n
vertices satisfying
f(H)\geq\delta
. Applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain
\mathbb{P}_{G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)}[G\rightarrow^{*}H]\geq\delta/2.
By Lemma
2.2
, we have that
e(H)\geq\binom{n}{2}-Cn,
for some constant
C=C(\delta)
. Finally, since
n
is large enough, Lemma
2.4
implies
\mathbb{P}_{G\sim\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)}[G\rightarrow^{*}H]<\delta/2,
a contradiction.
∎

3
Concluding remarks

Though we have not checked it very thoroughly, we think a careful analysis of our argument shows that
f(n)=O\left(\frac{\log\log\log n}{\log\log n}\right)
. Since we do not believe this to be close to the truth, we have chosen to present the proof of
f(n)=o(1)
for the sake of clarity of presentation.

Recall that we crucially used the anti-concentration of the number of edges in
\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
, so in order to prove that
f(n)=o(1/n),
new ideas are needed. Furthermore, it seems hard to push beyond
f(n)=O(\log\log n/\log n)
by considering a random graph process as we do. For
f(n)=\log\log n/\log n,
the analogue of Lemma
2.2
would only imply that
e(H)\geq\binom{n}{2}-O(n\log n)
. However, this does not seem to be particularly useful and, in fact, it can be deduced much more simply as otherwise in expectation
\mathcal{G}(n,1/2)
is expected to have less than
n!\cdot e^{-n\log n}\ll 1
embeddings into
H
.

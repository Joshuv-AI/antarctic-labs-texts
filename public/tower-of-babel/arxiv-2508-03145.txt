Θ \Theta -Categories and Tannakian duality
Joost NUITEN, Bertrand TOEN

Abstract
We introduce a notion of Θ \Theta -categories, which is a refinement of the notion of symmetric monoidal ∞ \infty -categories. We use this notion to prove a Tannakian duality statement, relating Θ \Theta -categories with fpqc-stacks by means of a certain stack of fiber functors in the context of Θ \Theta -categories. This provides, over a base ring of arbitrary characteristic, a strong link between Tannakian Θ \Theta -categories and the schematic homotopy types of [ Toe06 ] .



Introduction

The idea that homotopy theory can be approached from a Galois or Tannakian point of view goes back
to Grothendieck. This perspective was first incarnated in his theory of the fundamental group, both pro-finite and
pro-algebraic (see for instance
[
Ray71
]
,
[
Del89
]
). To the best of our knowledge, the fact
that this point of view
on the fundamental group can be fruitfully extended to more general homotopy invariants appears
in two letters of Grothendieck: to Serre, dated 18.07.1959 for the pro-finite case (see
[
CS01
, p. 75]
),
and to Breen, dated 19.07.1975, for the pro-algebraic case (see
[
Gro
, cote 134-2, p. 75]
).

In
[
Toe00
]
, the second author suggested an approach to Tannakian duality for
\infty
-categories based
on the notion of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories defined as functors out of
\Gamma
, the category
of pointed finite sets. In
[
Toe06
]
certain stacks, called
schematic homotopy types
,
have been identified as the expected duals to
Tannakian
\infty
-categories and have been used notably in the setting of non-abelian Hodge theory
(see
[
KPT08
]
).
Thanks to the foundational work of Lurie, actual Tannaka duality statements have been
proven by several authors, see for instance
[
Lur11
]
,
[
Wal12
]
and

[
Iwa18
]
. However, except in the specific case of characteristic zero,
these results are obtained in the setting of
E_{\infty}
-algebras, which do not relate well to
the theory of schematic homotopy types of
[
Toe06
]
, and therefore do not provide
the expected Tannakian interpretations for schematic homotopy types.

The starting point of this work is the simple observation that any notion
of Tannakian
\infty
-category solely based on symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories will fail
to relate nicely to schematic homotopy types, or to stacks in algebraic geometry in general. Indeed, for any
stack
F
on the fpqc-site of affine schemes,
the symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathsf{QCoh}(F)
of quasi-coherent sheaves
comes equipped with natural extra operations, namely the derived symmetric power operations. These
cannot be reconstructed purely from the symmetric monoidal structure, and their
existence is a theoretical obstruction for any Tannakian-type reconstruction result in algebraic geometry.

A second incarnation of this discrepancy can be seen at the level of commutative algebras. The theory of schematic homotopy types from
[
Toe06
]
uses co-simplicial commutative rings, a sub-class of the
\mathrm{LSym}
-algebras (see e.g.
[
BCN21
,
Rak20
]
) typically used in derived, as opposed to spectral, algebraic geometry. In characteristic zero, these
\mathrm{LSym}
-algebras coincide with the
E_{\infty}
-algebras that exist naturally in any symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category, but have an additional algebraic structure in general.

The purpose of this short note is to introduce a rudimentary notion of
\Theta
-categories
, which is
a refinement of the notion of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories suited for a Tannakian
interpretation of schematic homotopy types and similar stacks. The point of view adopted here will be
somewhat minimalist and takes the second discrepancy mentioned above as its starting point. It is based on the principle that
\Theta
-categories should morally be symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories in which an internal notion of
\mathrm{LSym}
-algebras exists.
We implement this idea by defining
\Theta
-categories simply as pairs
(T,M)
, consisting of
a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category and a monad
M
over
T
equipped with a map from the
E_{\infty}
-monad and satisfying
some conditions (see Definition
1.5
). The typical example to keep in mind is the case where
F
is a stack,

T=\mathsf{QCoh}(F)
is its
\infty
-category of quasi-coherent complexes and
M
is the
\mathrm{LSym}
-monad,
given by the direct sum of derived symmetric powers
E\mapsto\bigoplus_{n}\mathrm{LSym}^{n}(E)
.
The main content of our work is that this definition gives rise to a well-behaved
(2,\infty)
-category of
\Theta
-categories, from which a Tannakian duality theorem can
be proven (see Theorem
2.9
). In particular, we show that any Tannakian
\Theta
-category
T
is
equivalent, as a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category, to
\widehat{\mathsf{Perf}}(Fib^{\mathrm{LSym}}(T))
, the
\infty
-category
of ind-perfect complexes on a certain stack of
\mathrm{LSym}
-fiber functors. The stacks obtained
as stacks of
\mathrm{LSym}
-fiber functors are moreover essentially the schematic homotopy types of
[
Toe06
]
.

To finish this introduction, we would like to mention that the present approach to
\Theta
-categories
is not optimal. The reader will for instance observe some limitations and
unnatural conditions in our main result, Theorem
2.9
. This is the price to pay for our minimalist
approach, but we mention some possible modifications and improvements in a final comment (see Remark
2.15
).
We also want to mention that a more systematic theory of
\Theta
-categories as symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories with additional symmetric powers is presently under
investigation by the first author. We believe that the outcome of this work will allow
for stronger and more natural results in a near future. Nevertheless, Theorem
2.9

can be applied in several important situations to construct interesting Tannakian duals, such
as the theory of Nori’s motives (from which we can extract a motivic homotopy type defined
as a stack over
\mathbf{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}
), or the theory of exponential complexes appearing in
[
KKP08
]
(from
which we can extract the
exponential homotopy type
associated to an algebraic
variety). These specific schematic homotopy theories will be studied elsewhere.

Definition 1.5
.

The
(2,\infty)
-category of
\Theta
-categories
is the full sub-
(2,\infty)
-category
of the

-category

that satisfy the following conditions.

(1)

The induced (right adjoint)

is an equivalence.

(2)

The induced (right adjoint)
\infty
-functor

commutes with arbitrary colimits.

This
(2,\infty)
-category is denoted by
\infty\text{-}\mathbf{Cat}_{pr}^{\Theta}

In more intuitive terms, objects in
\infty\text{-}\mathbf{Cat}_{pr}^{\Theta}
are presentable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories

together with a monad
M
on their underlying
\infty
-category, as well as a

satisfying condition
(2)
from Definition

. Note that, because push-outs in

are given by tensor products, this condition has the following equivalent reformulations.

(2’)

For

-algebras

(2”)

The forgetful functor

preserves push-outs, that is, push-outs in

are compatible with tensor products in

The equivalence of these conditions with condition (2) from Definition

follows from the fact

already preserves sifted colimits, so that it preserves all colimits if and only if it preserves finite coproducts of free algebras.

Remark 1.6
.

The
(1,\infty)
-category underlying
\infty\text{-}\mathbf{Cat}_{pr}^{\Theta}
has small limits,
and the forgetful functor

Upon inverting weak equivalences, the above sequence of right Quillen functors induces a sequence of right adjoints between presentable
\infty

Proof.

On localizations, we obtain a sequence of conservative right adjoints that furthermore preserve geometric realizations, since these can be computed by the diagonal in the simplicial direction. In particular,

Remark 2.13
.

The reader will notice that in the proof of Theorem
2.9
we have a commutative
diagram of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories

\textstyle{T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\widehat{\mathsf{Perf}}(\mathsf{Fib}_{*}^{\mathrm{LSym}}(T^{\Theta}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{QCoh}(\mathsf{Fib}_{*}^{\mathrm{LSym}}(T^{\Theta}))}

where the top horizontal
\infty
-functor is an equivalence.
Moreover, each of the two
other
\infty
-functors possesses a natural lift to a morphism of
\Theta
-categories. However, we have not been
able to prove that the equivalence

can be enhanced to an equivalence of
\Theta
-categories. This is due to our choice of definition
of
\Theta
-category, which is probably not optimal (see also Remark

).

The next corollary investigates the other direction of Tannakian duality,
and the morphism
\alpha
. We recall that we have introduced the notion of
Q
-local
stacks in the definition of Tannakian gerbes (condition (G2)). It has a refined version
called
P
-local, which already naturally appears in the setting of schematic
homotopy theory of
[
Toe06

. A pointed and connected stack

is said to be

-local

if for any morphism of

such that

is
an equivalence, the induced morphism

u^{*}\colon\mathbf{Map}_{\mathbf{St}_{k}}(G_{2},F)\to\mathbf{Map}_{\mathbf{St}_{k}}(G_{1},F)

is also an equivalence. As perfect complexes are the rigid objects in quasi-coherent complexes,
being pointed

-local. However, the converse does not hold.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let

be a pointed Tannakian gerbe.
Then, the adjunction morphism

By our Theorem

, the

is an equivalence on Ind-perfect complexes

∎

Remark 2.15
.

As a final comment, we see that the results on Tannakian duality are imperfect and do not induce
a nice equivalence between Tannakian
\Theta
-categories and Tannakian gerbes. One reason for this
has been identified in Remark
2.13
: the equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories
of Corollary
2.14
has not been shown to lift to an equivalence of
\Theta
-categories. We think it is
probably not possible to improve this using the present approach to
\Theta
-categories. The technical
complication comes here from the use of presentable, and thus non-small
\infty
-categories everywhere, whereas
the natural approach would be to write everything in terms of
\infty
-categories of perfect complexes as opposed
to quasi-coherent sheaves or ind-perfect complexes. The problem with working with small
\infty
-categories of
perfect complexes is of course that the monads involved, as the LSym monad does not preserve
perfect complexes globally.

One possible approach is to consider the LSym monads as graded monads, as
done in
\lx@sectionsign 2.1
for the construction of the
\Theta
-structure in ind-perfect complexes.
Alternatively, and perhaps more satisfactorily,
one can develop a theory of
\Theta
-categories in terms of symmetric
monoidal
\infty
-categories equipped with additional symmetric power operations.
Since the derived symmetric powers
\mathrm{LSym}^{n}
preserve perfect complexes,
such a theory would also allow to view
\mathsf{Perf}(X)
as a
\Theta
-category.
We believe that
such an approach, allowing for
\Theta
-categories to be small
\infty
-categories could reduce the imperfection
of Theorem
2.9
. This will be investigated elsewhere.

Appendix A
Positive tor-dimension descent

Let
T
be a stable presentable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category endowed with
a non-degenerate and multiplicative t-structure. For a morphism of commutative algebras

u\colon\mathbf{1}\to B
in
T
, we can consider the co-nerve of
u
, which is a coaugmented
cosimplicial object

CN(u)^{*}\colon\Delta_{+}\longrightarrow\mathsf{CAlg}(T)

sending
n\geq-1
to
B^{\otimes n+1}
.
Tensoring along
\mathbf{1}\to CN(u)^{*}
yields a colimit preserving
\infty
-functor

\psi\colon T=\mathbf{1}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T)\to\lim_{n\in\Delta}(CN(u)^{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T)).

The right adjoint to this
\infty
-functor is obtained as the composition

\textstyle{\lim_{n\in\Delta}\big(CN(u)^{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T)\big)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T^{\Delta}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lim}
\textstyle{T}

where the first
\infty
-functor is the levelwise forgetful
\infty
-functor to
T
.

We now assume that
B
has positive tor-dimension. That is, the
\infty
-functor
M\mapsto B\otimes M

preserves the sub-
\infty
-category
T^{[0,\infty[}\subset T
of coconnective objects with respect to the

t
-structure. We will denote by
T^{>-\infty}
the full sub-
\infty
-category
of eventually coconnective objects, that is, the union of sub-
\infty
-categories

\bigcup_{a<0}T^{[a,\infty[}\subset T.

Similarly, for
B\in\mathsf{CAlg}(T)
we denote by

B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T)^{>-\infty}\subset B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T)
the full sub-
\infty
-category
of modules whose underlying objects lie in
T^{>-\infty}
.

Proposition A.1
.

Assume that
B\in\mathsf{CAlg}(T)
is of positive tor-dimension and that

B\otimes-\colon T^{>-\infty}\to T^{>-\infty}
is conservative. Then the
\infty
-functor

\psi\colon T^{>-\infty}\to\lim_{n\in\Delta}\big(CN(u)^{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T)^{>-\infty}\big)

is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
B
is augmented via a morphism of
commutative algebras
B\to\mathbf{1}
. In this case, the constant diagram

\mathbf{1}
becomes a homotopy retract of the cosimplicial diagram
CN(u)^{*}
. The augmentation
induces a retraction
CN(u)^{*}\to\mathbf{1}
, and the composition

CN(u)^{*}\to\mathbf{1}\to CN(u)^{*}
is simplicially homotopic to the identity as a morphism
of cosimplicial objects in
\mathsf{CAlg}(T)
. This implies that
the cosimplicial diagram of categories
CN(u)^{*}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T)
is homotopy equivalent to the constant
diagram
T
, and thus that we have

T\simeq\lim_{n}CN(u)^{n}\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T).

In this special case, the proposition holds even without the restriction to
T^{>-\infty}
and without
the positive tor-dimension condition, and without any need of a t-structure: it remains true
for any augmented commutative algebra
B
in a stable presentable symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category.

Let us now treat the general case. We first observe that
B\otimes-
commutes with
limits of diagrams
x_{*}\colon\Delta\to T^{[a,\infty[}
, that is, limits of uniformly bounded below
cosimplicial diagrams. Indeed, in this case, for all
i
there is a
k
, depending on
a
and
i
only,
such that

\lim_{n}(x_{n})\to\lim_{n\leq k}(x_{n})

induces an equivalence on
\tau^{\geq i}
. As
B
is of positive tor-dimension the same
holds for the limit
\lim(B\otimes x_{n})
instead. Because
B\otimes-
commutes with finite limits,
we see that the canonical morphism

B\otimes\lim_{n}(x_{n})\to\lim_{n}(B\otimes x_{n})

is an equivalence on all truncations
\tau^{\geq i}
for all
i
, and thus must be an equivalence in
T
.

Because
B\otimes-
is conservative and commutes with the involved limits,
we can base change to
B
to prove this last statement. After base change the statement is true
as it is related to the co-nerve of
B\to B\otimes B
inside
B\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}(T)
, which is augmented via
the multiplication map
B\otimes B\to B
.
∎

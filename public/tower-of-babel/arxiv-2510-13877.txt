Lucas Williams

Abstract
. The Pontryagin-Thom theorem gives an isomorphism between the cobordism group of framed n n -dimensional manifolds, ω n \omega_{n} , and the n t ​ h n^{th} stable homotopy group of the sphere spectrum, π n ​ ( 𝕊 ) \pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}) . 1.
Introduction

A framed manifold is a closed compact smooth manifold equipped with a trivialization of its stable tangent bundle. The Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism
[
Tho54
,
Mil65
]
is an isomorphism between the cobordism group of framed
n
-manifolds equipped with a reference map to
X
, denoted
\omega_{n}(X)
, and the
n^{th}
stable homotopy group of the suspension spectrum of
X_{+}
, denoted
\pi_{n}(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X)
. This isomorphism was an early example of the efficacy of applying homotpical techniques to differential topology and paved the way for such seminal work as
[
KM63
,
L0̈2
,
HHR16
,
WX17
]
.

Throughout this paper, we will write
\omega_{n}
to mean
\omega_{n}(*)
. After producing an isomorphism
\omega_{n}\cong\pi_{n}(\mathbb{S})
, it is natural to choose generators and ask where each element of
\omega_{n}
is sent in
\pi_{n}(\mathbb{S})
. For instance,
\omega_{0}
is generated, under disjoint unions, by a single point endowed with a positively oriented framing. This is mapped to a suspension of the identity map
S^{0}\to S^{0}
. The manifold
S^{1}
endowed with its Lie group framing generates
\omega_{1}
, and is mapped to a suspension of the Hopf fibration under the isomorphism
\omega_{1}\cong\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S})
.

For
G
a finite group, and
V
a finite dimensional orthogonal real
G
-representation, there is a cobordism group of
V
-framed
G
-manifolds equipped with a reference map to a fixed
G
-space
X
, denoted
\omega_{V}^{G}(X)
. These groups, along with suspension isomorphisms, form an
RO(G)
-graded homology theory in the precise sense of
[
May96
]
. The equivariant Pontryagin-Thom theorem for
V
-framed
G
-manifolds, originally proved in
[
Hau74
,
Kos76
]
, gives an isomorphism from
\omega_{V}^{G}(X)
to the
V^{th}
equivariant stable homotopy group of the suspension spectrum of
X_{+}
, denoted
\pi_{V}^{G}(\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}X)
. We will consistently refer to the treatment of this theorem appearing in
[
Wil25b
]
.

In this paper, we compute the image of each element of
\omega_{V}^{C_{2}}
in
\pi_{V}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})
under the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism, for
V
either the trivial
C_{2}
-representation or the sign representation. This computation differs from the non-equivariant setting in surprising and interesting ways. Having a clean geometric example should provide helpful data for those working in equivariant stable homotopy theory, equivariant
K
-theory, and motivic stable homotopy theory through its connection to
C_{2}
-equivariant homotopy theory.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Equivariant framed cobordism

In this section, we give the necessary background to prove the main theorems.

Definition 2.1
.

A
C_{2}
-manifold is a smooth compact manifold equipped with a smooth action of
C_{2}
.

Given a
C_{2}
-manifold,
M
, the action on the tangent space is given by
g\cdot(x,v)=(gx,dg(v))
where
dg
is the map induced on the tangent space by the action of
g\in C_{2}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a
C_{2}
-manifold and
V
a real orthogonal
C_{2}
-representation. A
V
-framing of
M
is an equivalence class of
C_{2}
-equivariant vector bundle isomorphisms

TM\oplus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\times\mathbb{R}^{k}}}\right)\cong M\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k}}}\right).

We call two such isomorphisms equivalent if they are
C_{2}
-homotopic. Moreover, we say that

TM\oplus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\times\mathbb{R}^{k+1}}}\right)\cong M\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k+1}}}\right)

is equivalent to

TM\oplus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\times\mathbb{R}^{k}}}\right)\cong M\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k}}}\right)

if it is obtained by extending to the identity in the
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(k+1}}\right)^{\text{st}}
coordinate.

Example 2.3
.

The unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2\sigma}
, denoted
S(2\sigma)
, is
\mathbb{R}
-framed. If
g
is the nontrivial element of
C_{2}
and
(x,v)\in TS(2\sigma)
then
g\cdot(x,v)=(-x,v)
so that
TS(2\sigma)\cong S(2\sigma)\times\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand,
S(1+\sigma)\cong S^{\sigma}
is
\sigma
-framed. Model
S(1+\sigma)
as the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}
with
C_{2}
-action given by complex conjugation. If
g
is the nontrivial element of
C_{2}
and
(z,v)\in TS(1+\sigma)
, then
g\cdot(z,v)=(z^{*},-v)
so that
TS(1+\sigma)\cong S(1+\sigma)\times\sigma
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
M
be a
C_{2}
-manifold and

TM\oplus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\times\mathbb{R}^{k}}}\right)\cong M\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k}}}\right)

a
V
-framing of
M
. Use
-M
to denote
M
with the framing extended to

TM\oplus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\times\mathbb{R}^{k+1}}}\right)\cong M\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k+1}}}\right)

by sending the
(k+1)^{\text{st}}
coordinate to its negative.

Definition 2.5
.

Two closed
V
-framed
C_{2}
-manifolds,
M
and
N
, are cobordant if there exists a
(V\oplus\mathbb{R})
-framed
C_{2}
-manifold,
W
, such that
\partial W\cong M\amalg-N
and the restriction of the framing on
W
to its boundary induces the framing on
M\amalg-N
. Note that a cobordism induces a framing on its boundary by pulling back the framing on the cobordism along the inclusion of the boundary.

Example 2.6
.

As a
\sigma
-framed manifold,
C_{2}\times S^{1}
with its Lie group framing is null-cobordant via the product of
S^{1}
and the unit disk in
\sigma
. On the other hand, as we will prove later,
C_{2}\times S^{1}
represents a non-trivial cobordism class as an
\mathbb{R}
-framed manifold. The null-cobordism we used before was specifically
(\sigma\oplus\mathbb{R})
-framed.

Remark 2.7
.

We denote the cobordism group of
\mathbb{R}
-framed
C_{2}
-manifolds as
\omega_{1}^{C_{2}}
. This group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2^{\oplus 3}
. A geometric argument shows that we may choose a generating set of
\omega_{1}^{C_{2}}
so that the underlying
C_{2}
-manifolds of the generators are
S^{1}
,
C_{2}\times S^{1}
, and
S(2\sigma)
. The cobordism group of
\sigma
-framed
C_{2}
-manifolds is denoted
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}
. This group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
, and a generator may be chosen so that its underlying
C_{2}
-manifold is
S(1+\sigma)
.

2.2.
The equivariant framed Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism

We will give a short exposition of the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom construction tailored to the paper at hand. In what follows, let
V
be
\mathbb{R}
or
\sigma
.

If
M\in\omega_{V}^{C_{2}}
, then
M
embeds into
\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}\oplus V
in such a way that the normal bundle of the embedding is
M\times\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}
. A proof of a more general version of this fact appears as
[
Wil25b
, Lemma 2.44]
.

The Pontryagin-Thom map,
\omega_{V}^{C_{2}}\to\pi_{V}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})
is defined as follows. Let
M\in\omega_{V}^{G}
. Embed
M
in
\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}\oplus V
so that
\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M,\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}\oplus V}}\right)\cong M\times\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}
. Denote this normal bundle as
\nu
. Then the image of
M
in
\pi_{V}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})
is the following composition.

\displaystyle S^{(2+2\sigma)\oplus V}

\displaystyle\rightarrow D(\nu)/S(\nu)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\times D\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}}}\right)}}\right)/\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\times S\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}}}\right)}}\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow D\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}}}\right)/S\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{R}^{2+2\sigma}}}\right)\cong S^{2+2\sigma}.

The first map is a Pontryagin-Thom collpase map, the second comes from the trivialization of
\nu
, and the third is induced by
M\to*
. If
M
came equipped with an equivariant map to a
G
-space
X
, then the final map would be induced by
M\to X
and the target would be
S^{2+2\sigma}\wedge_{+}X
. As discussed in
[
Wil25b
]
, this map is an isomorphism.

3.
Converting Tangent and Normal Bundle Trivializations

In this subsection, we discuss compatibilities between trivializations of normal and tangent bundles. These compatibilities play an important role in the proofs in this paper.

Recall that in
2.2
we defined a
V
-framing to be an equivariant trivialization of the stable tangent bundle

(3.1)

TM\oplus\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(M\times\mathbb{R}^{k}}}\right)\cong M\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k}}}\right).

However, the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom map takes as input a
V
-framed
C_{2}
-manifold equipped with a trivialization of its stable normal bundle. Given a
V
-framed
G
-manifold, we obtain a trivialization of its stable normal bundle by summing both sides of
Eq.

3.1
with the normal bundle. This is a major advantage of working with stable bundles as the same does not hold unstably.

Unfortunately, there is not a bijective correspondence between equivariant trivializations of stable tangent and stable normal bundles because we only allow allow stabilization by trivial
G
-representations. Work of Waner suggests that allowing stabilization by non-trivial
G
-representations gives rise to the same

-graded cohomology theory despite the fact that these two notions of framing are not equivalent
[
Wan84
]
. We plan to pursue this line of thinking in future work.

4.
The image of

In this section, we will compute where each element of

\pi_{1}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})

As noted in the introduction, we will identify

\pi_{1}^{C_{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{S}}}\right)\cong\pi_{1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{S}}}\right)\oplus\pi_{1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}BC_{2}}}\right)\cong\pi_{1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{S}}}\right)\oplus H_{0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z}/2}}\right)\oplus H_{1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z}}}\right).

-framing is a disjoint union of copies of
S^{1}
with trivial action,
C_{2}\times S^{1}
, and
S(2\sigma)
.

We begin by examining
\mathbb{R}
-framings of
S^{1}
. First embed
S^{1}
as the unit sphere of the
xy
-plane in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We may then write a trivialization of the normal bundle

\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\right)\cong S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}

by mapping the unit vector pointing in the outward radial direction in each fiber to the first standard basis vector, and the unit vector pointing in the positive
z
-direction to the second standard basis vector.

Relative to the above trivialization of
\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\right)
, any other
\mathbb{R}
-framing of
S^{1}
is given by the homotopy class of a continuous map
S^{1}\to SO(2)
. We identify the group of all such maps with
\mathbb{Z}
by taking the degree of a map
S^{1}\to SO(2)\simeq S^{1}
. We think of these framings as twisting the vectors of the framing discussed above an integer number of times as we traverse the fibers of the normal bundle of
S^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Note that this captures all possible stable framings because we stabilize only by trivial
G
-representations.

Notation 4.1
.

We denote
S^{1}
equipped with the framing induced by the degree
n
map
S^{1}\to SO(2)
as
S^{1}_{n}
. With this labeling scheme, the explicit trivialization of
\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\right)
discussed above is
S^{1}_{0}
. Recall from
Section

3
that after translating trivializations of normal bundles to tangent bundles,
S^{1}_{1}
is
S^{1}
equipped with its Lie group framing.

The following lemma will be helpful in each of the following computations.

Lemma 4.2
.

There is a “tom Dieck splitting” of the
\mathbb{R}
-framed
C_{2}
-cobordism group
\omega_{1}^{C_{2}}
as
\omega_{1}\oplus\omega_{1}(BC_{2})
. Moreover, the equivariant framed Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism is compatible with the tom Dieck splittings in equivariant framed cobordism and equivariant stable homotopy theory.

Proof.

The first statement is given by partitioning an
\mathbb{R}
-framed manifold into those connected components with a free
C_{2}
-action and those without. The second statement is precisely how the proof of the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism for
\mathbb{R}
-framings is expressed in
[
Wil25b
]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Identify,

\omega_{1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(BC_{2}}}\right)\cong\pi_{1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}BC_{2}}}\right)\cong H_{0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z}/2}}\right)\oplus H_{1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z}}}\right).

The
H_{0}(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z}/2)
coordinate is controlled by the framing data of the 1-manifold. The
H_{1}(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z})
coordinate is given by the homology class
\amalg_{k}S^{1}\to BC_{2}\in\omega_{1}(BC_{2})
.

Proof.

The proof is immediate from the construction of the equivariant framed Pontryagin-Thom map and the fact that the splitting
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}BC_{2})
is induced by
\Sigma_{+}^{\infty}BC_{2}\simeq\mathbb{S}\vee\Sigma^{\infty}BC_{2}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Under the identification

\pi_{1}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})\cong\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S})\oplus H_{0}(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z}/2)\oplus H_{1}(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2^{\oplus 3},

the framed
C_{2}
-manifold
S^{1}_{n}
corresponds to
(n,0,0)
.

Proof.

Since
S^{1}
with the trivial action is not free, its image is entirely in the
\omega_{1}
summand of
\omega_{1}^{C_{2}}\cong\omega_{1}\oplus\omega_{1}(BC_{2})
. Thus, by
4.2
, the image of
S^{1}
under the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism is entirely in the
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S})
summand of the target. Since
C_{2}
is acting trivially on
S^{1}
, the image of this framed manifold in the
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S})
coordinate is precisely the same as in the non-equivariant setting. In other words, the
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S})
coordinate is the degree of the map
S^{1}\to SO(2)
(modulo 2) which induces the framing on
S^{1}_{n}
.
∎

We now investigate framings of

. First embed
C_{2}\times S^{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2+\sigma}
in the
(\mathbb{R}\oplus\sigma)
-plane. Write a trivialization

\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(C_{2}\times S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2+\sigma}}}\right)\cong(C_{2}\times S^{1})\times\mathbb{R}^{1+\sigma}

by mapping the outward radial unit vector in each fiber to the
\sigma
-coordinate and the unit vector in the positive

direction to the
\mathbb{R}
-coordinate. Relative to this framing, any other framing of
C_{2}\times S^{1}
is given by a
C_{2}
-equivariant map

. However, these are in bijective correspondence with non-equivariant maps
S^{1}\to SO(2)
by restricting to one copy of
S^{1}
. We identify the set of such maps with
\mathbb{Z}
by taking their degree.

Notation 4.5
.

We denote
C_{2}\times S^{1}
equipped with the framing induced by the degree
n
map
S^{1}\to SO(2)
by
C_{2}\times S^{1}_{n}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Under the identification

\pi_{1}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})\cong\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S})\oplus H_{0}(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z}/2)\oplus H_{1}(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2^{\oplus 3},

the framed manifold

corresponds to
(0,n,0)
.

By
4.2
,
C_{2}\times S^{1}
is sent to 0 in the first coordinate of
\mathbb{Z}/2^{\oplus 3}
since it is a free
C_{2}
-manifold.

Now model
EC_{2}
as
S^{\infty}
and

as
\mathbb{RP}^{\infty}
, the orbit space of
S^{\infty}
by the antipodal action. The map
C_{2}\times S^{1}\to S^{\infty}
is
C_{2}
-homotopic to the composite of the collapse

and

since the two components of the image can be

-equivariantly deformed to the north and south poles. Then after passing to orbits, the map

is null-homotopic. Thus,
C_{2}\times S^{1}
is sent to 0 in the
H_{1}(BC_{2};\mathbb{Z})

\mathbb{Z}/2^{\oplus 3}
.

Observe that for any
G
-space

, the set of equivariant maps

, is in bijective correspondence with the set of non-equivariant maps

-equivariant
\mathbb{R}
-framing of

is equivalent to a non-equivariant framing of
S^{1}
. Therefore, the

\mathbb{Z}/2^{\oplus 3}
is the same as in the non-equivariant case — it is given by

(modulo 2).
∎

.

and
C_{2}\times S^{1}
. We begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
C_{2}\times S^{1}
be equipped with any
\sigma
-framing. Then
C_{2}\times S^{1}
is zero in
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}
.

Proof.

First observe that
C_{2}\times S^{1}\in\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})
. The homotopy cofiber sequence

{C_{2}}_{+}\to S^{0}\to S^{\sigma}

induces a long exact sequence

\dots\to\omega_{1}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})\to\omega_{1}(EC_{2})\to\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})\to\omega_{0}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})\to\omega_{0}(EC_{2}).

The forgetful maps

\omega_{0}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})\to\omega_{0}(EC_{2})\cong\omega_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\omega_{1}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})\to\omega_{1}(EC_{2})\cong\omega_{1}

are injective and surjective respectively. Thus, the maps on either side of
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})
are zero, so that
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})
is also zero. Then
C_{2}\times S^{1}
is null-cobordant in
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(EC_{2})
and thus also in
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}
.
∎

We now examine
S(1+\sigma)
. We begin by classifying
\sigma
-framings of this manifold.

Embed
S(1+\sigma)
in
\mathbb{R}^{1+2\sigma}
as the unit circle in the
xy
-plane where we identify
x
with the trivial
C_{2}
-representation. We now obtain a
C_{2}
-equivariant vector bundle isomorphism

\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S(1+\sigma),\mathbb{R}^{1+2\sigma}}}\right)\cong S(1+\sigma)\times\mathbb{R}^{1+\sigma}

by sending the unit vector pointing in the outward radial direction in each fiber to the first standard basis vector, and the unit vector pointing in the positive
z
-direction in each fiber to the second standard basis vector. This is our first
\sigma
-framing of
S(1+\sigma)
. Relative to this
\sigma
-framing, any other
\sigma
-framing is given by the
C_{2}
-homotopy class of a
C_{2}
-equivariant map

S(1+\sigma)\to SO(1+\sigma).

As a
2
-dimensional real vector space,
C_{2}
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{1+\sigma}
by
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}
. Thus,
C_{2}
acts on
SO(1+\sigma)
by

\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\theta)&-\sin(\theta)\\
\sin(\theta)&\cos(\theta)\end{bmatrix}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\theta)&\sin(\theta)\\
-\sin(\theta)&\cos(\theta)\end{bmatrix}.

Note also that for our trivialization to be equivariant at the fixed points of
S(1+\sigma)
, the normal vector sent to
(1,0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{1+\sigma}
must come from a vector on which
C_{2}
acts trivially. Thus, we may think of an equivariant map
S(1+\sigma)\to SO(1+\sigma)
as follows. First choosing a path between the image of the two fixed points which winds around
SO(1+\sigma)
a half integer number of times. Second take the negation of this path to get a full loop in
SO(1+\sigma)
. Note that by taking degrees of maps, the set of framings on
S(1+\sigma)
is in bijection with
\mathbb{Z}
.

Notation 5.2
.

We denote
S(1+\sigma)
with the framing induced by the degree
n

C_{2}
-equivariant map
S(1+\sigma)\to SO(1+\sigma)
as
S(1+\sigma)_{n}
.

The equivariant Hopf fibration is a map

\eta\colon S^{1+2\sigma}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{1}\simeq S^{1+\sigma}

given by restricting the projection map
\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
to the unit sphere. The
C_{2}
-action is given by complex conjugation. The equivariant Hopf fibration can be given by the formula
(z_{0},z_{1})\mapsto(2z_{0}z_{1}^{*},|z_{0}|^{2}-|z_{1}|^{2})
which is
C_{2}
-equivariant. A straightforward calculation, proceeding from the homotopy cofiber sequence

{C_{2}}_{+}\to S^{0}\to S^{\sigma},

shows that
\pi_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Then a theorem of Morel
[
Mor03
]
which appears as
[
DI13
]
[Theorem 1.2] shows that
\eta
generates
\pi_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})
.

Proposition 5.3
.

The
C_{2}
-manifold
S(1+\sigma)
equipped with the Lie group framing, is sent to the equivariant Hopf fibration
\eta:S^{1+2\sigma}\to S^{1+\sigma}
under the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism.

Proof.

The Lie group framing of
S(1+\sigma)
is compatible with the trivialization

\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(S(1+\sigma),\mathbb{R}^{1+2\sigma}}}\right)\cong S(1+\sigma)\times\mathbb{R}^{1+\sigma}

coming from
S(1+\sigma)_{1}
. The argument is the same as in the non-equivariant case which is discussed in
Section

3
. This works out precisely because we chose our ambient space to be
\mathbb{R}^{1+2\sigma}
rather than
\mathbb{R}^{2+\sigma}
. In other words, we stabilize by
\sigma
rather than
\mathbb{R}
.

The underlying framed manifold of
S(1+\sigma)_{1}
is sent to the Hopf fibration by the non-equivariant Pontryagin-Thom construction. This extends to the
C_{2}
-equivariant setting when the Hopf fibration is written as a map
\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with
C_{2}
-action given by complex conjugation.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The
\sigma
-framed
C_{2}
-manifold
S(1+\sigma)_{n}
is sent to
(n\mod 2)\in\mathbb{Z}\cong\pi_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})
by the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism.

Proof.

In the previous proof we showed that
S(1+\sigma)_{1}
is sent to
1\in\mathbb{Z}\cong\pi_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})
. In this proof we will make use of the map
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}\to\omega_{0}
given by taking the
C_{2}
-fixed points of a
\sigma
-framed
C_{2}
-manifold.

This map takes
S(1+\sigma)_{1}
to
2\in\mathbb{Z}\cong\omega_{0}
. So when written as a map
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
this is the multiplication by 2 map, which is an injection. In general, if
n
is odd, then
S(1+\sigma)_{n}
is sent to
2
in
\mathbb{Z}\cong\omega_{0}
. Thus, if
n
is odd,
S(1+\sigma)_{n}
is cobordant to
S(1+\sigma)_{1}
. If, on the other hand,
n
is even, then
S(1+\sigma)_{n}
is mapped to
0\in\mathbb{Z}\cong\omega_{0}
. Thus,
S(1+\sigma)_{n}
represents the trivial cobordism class in
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}
.
∎

We have now shown where any element of
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}
is sent in
\pi_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}(\mathbb{S})
by the Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism. Note that while a connected
\sigma
-framed
C_{2}
-manifold can only be mapped to
0
or
1
, the disjoint union of
n
copies of
S(1+\sigma)_{1}
is sent to
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We end with some remarks on why the equivariant Hopf fibration is of infinite order rather than of order 2 as it is in the non-equivariant case. There is a
C_{2}
-equivariant cobordism from
S(1+\sigma)\amalg S(1+\sigma)
to
S(1+\sigma)
given by the involution on the pair of pants that reflects the front to the back and vice versa. However, this only extends to a framed cobordism when the boundary components are
S(1+\sigma)_{n}\amalg S(1+\sigma)_{n}
and
S(1+\sigma)_{2n}
for
n
even. In contrast, when
n
is odd,
S(1+\sigma)_{n}\amalg S(1+\sigma)_{n}
is not framed cobordant to a single copy of
S(1+\sigma)
. Thus, not every element of
\omega_{\sigma}^{C_{2}}
is represented by a connected manifold. This difference between equivariant and non-equivariant framed cobordism is precisely why the equivariant Hopf map is of infinite order while its non-equivariant counterpart is of finite order.

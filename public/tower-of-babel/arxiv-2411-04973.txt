Siegel Vectors for Nongeneric Depth Zero Supercuspidals of
GSp

Abstract




1
Introduction

This paper is concerned with a dimension counting problem in
p
-adic representation theory for the group
{\rm GSp}(4)
. Part of the motivation comes from the following problem in the classical theory of Siegel modular forms. If
\Gamma\subset{\rm Sp}(4,{\mathbb{Q}})
is a congruence subgroup, then the dimension of the space of cusp forms of level
\Gamma
(and integer weight
k
, say) is not known in general. For example, if

\Gamma=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}&p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}&p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\end{bmatrix}\cap{\rm Sp}(4,{\mathbb{Z}})

is the “Siegel congruence subgroup of level
p^{n}
” then the associated dimensions are unknown if
n\geq 2
, for any prime
p
. On the other hand, if

\Gamma=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbb{Z}}&4{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
{\mathbb{Z}}&4{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\\
4{\mathbb{Z}}&4{\mathbb{Z}}&4{\mathbb{Z}}&{\mathbb{Z}}\end{bmatrix}\cap{\rm Sp}(4,{\mathbb{Z}})

is the “Klingen congruence subgroup of level
4
” then the associated dimensions were recently computed in
[
RSY2022
]
. The method by which this was achieved required, as one of its several inputs, the dimensions of spaces of fixed vectors in all irreducible smooth representations of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Q}}_{2})
for the subgroups

{\rm Kl}(4)=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\\
4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\\
4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\\
4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&4{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&{\mathbb{Z}}\end{bmatrix}\cap{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}).

These dimensions, and more, had been computed in
[
Yi2021
]
. We remark that the different “shape” of the subgroups in question is an artifact of different conventions for alternating forms in the classical and representation-theoretic contexts.

If one hopes to use the approach taken in
[
RSY2022
]
for other cases, such as the Siegel congruence subgroups of level
p^{n}
, then a necessary component is the determination of dimensions of spaces of fixed vectors for all irreducible smooth representations of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
for appropriate local subgroups. In this paper we are concerned with the Siegel congruence subgroups of level
p^{n}
given by

{\rm Si}(p^{n})=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\\
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\\
p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\\
p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}&{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\\
\end{bmatrix}\cap{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}).

At this time, computing the associated dimensions for all representations appears overly ambitious, so we restrict ourselves to the very special case of depth zero supercuspidals (constructed in the next section). These representations arise in two ways; we will consider one of them here and the other in a companion paper. The representations of
{\rm GSp}(4,{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
considered in this paper will all be nongeneric. While these restrictions are a limitation, some interesting phenomena already arise. Perhaps the most significant of these is a strong dichotomy between the cases
p=2
and
p>2
.

For applications, it is also useful to understand the effect of the Atkin-Lehner involution on the space of Siegel-invariant vectors. We compute the signature of this involution for the local representations considered.

In a previous paper
[
Co2024
]
, we considered the analogous question of fixed vectors for higher depth Klingen subgroups. It was shown in that paper that the representations considered here had no fixed vectors for any
n
, nor was there a parity dichotomy. Thus, in our situation, the theory of Siegel vectors is substantially richer than that of Klingen vectors.

Here is a brief outline of this paper. Section 2 sets up notation and definitions. In section 3 we reduce the dimension computation to the determination and enumeration of a certain set of double cosets, together with a collection of character table computations for a certain finite reductive group. The main result, our dimension formula, is Theorem
5.1
. The formula for the Atkin-Lehner signature is Theorem
6.2
.

We thank Ralf Schmidt for many useful conversations in preparing this article.

\rho_{1}

\rho_{2}

\rho_{1}\boxtimes\rho_{2}

{\rm GL}_{2}(q)^{2}

{\rm GL}_{2,2}(q)

{\rm GL}_{2,2}(q)

Define the matrices

s_{1}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&1&&\\
1&&&\\
&&&1\\
&&1&\end{smallmatrix}\right],\qquad s_{2}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&&1&\\
&-1&&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right],\qquad u_{n}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&&1&\\
&&&-1\\
\varpi^{n}&&&\\
&-\varpi^{n}&&\end{smallmatrix}\right].

The images of
s_{1}
and
s_{2}

W:=N_{G}(T)/T
, and

is the Atkin-Lehner element. Note
u_{1}\in N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))
and represents the unique nontrivial coset in
N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))/Z(G){\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})
.

For an irreducible complex representation
\sigma
of a group, we write
\omega_{\sigma}
for its central character. If
H
is a subgroup of
G
and
\rho:H\to{\rm GL}(W)
is a representation of
H
, then
\text{\rm c-Ind}_{H}^{G}(\rho)
is the space of functions
f:G\to W
such that
f(hg)=\rho(h)f(g)
for all
h\in H
,
g\in G
, with
f
being smooth and having compact support modulo
H
. This space is a smooth representation of
G
under right translation.

If
\pi
is a depth zero supercuspidal representation of
G
then
\pi
arises in one of two ways. Either
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{ZK}^{G}(\rho)
where
\rho|_{K}
is an inflation if an irreducible cuspidal representation of
K/K^{+}={\rm GSp}(4,q)
, or
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))}^{G}(\tau)
where
\tau
is an irreducible representation of
N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))
and
\tau|_{{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})}
is an inflation of a cuspidal representation of
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})/{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}={\rm GL}_{2,2}(q)
. In the second case,
\tau|_{{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})}
is either
\sigma
or
\sigma\oplus\sigma^{u_{1}}
, with the latter case occuring if and only if
\sigma\not\cong\sigma^{u_{1}}
; here
\sigma^{u_{1}}(g):=\sigma(u_{1}^{-1}gu_{1})
. We will compute
\dim\pi^{{\rm Si}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}
for all such
\pi
that come from
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})
. In a companion article we will handle the case of
K
. We mention without proof that for the nongeneric
\pi
arising from
K
, one has
\pi^{{\rm Si}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}=0
for all
n\geq 0
. So this article contains all the interesting cases of Siegel vectors in nongeneric depth zero supercuspidals.

3.1
Character table computations

We first compute
\dim\sigma^{R}
for those subgroups
R
which will turn out to be the

3.1 Remark.

When
q
is odd, the image of
Z({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))
has index
2
in
Z({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})/{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+})
with a representative of the complement given by
(I_{2},-I_{2})
. A priori, we only know that central characters of representations of
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})/{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}
must be trivial on the image of
Z({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))
in order for the associated compact induction to have Siegel vectors. As a consequence of the next result, we will see that the relevant representations of
{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})/{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})^{+}
are precisely the ones with trivial central character.

3.2 Lemma.

Let
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
be irreducible cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}(q)
with
\omega_{\rho_{1}}\omega_{\rho_{2}}=1
.

a) If
\sigma=[\rho_{1}\boxtimes\rho_{2}]
and
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}c&\\
&abc^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\right\}
then

\dim\sigma^{R}=\begin{cases}1+\omega_{\rho_{i}}(-1)&\text{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{2}\\
1&\text{ if }q\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{cases}.

b) If
\sigma\subsetneq[\rho_{1}\boxtimes\rho_{2}]
and
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}c&\\
&abc^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\right\}
then
q
is odd and

\dim\sigma^{R}=\frac{1+\omega_{\rho_{i}}(-1)}{2}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }q\equiv 3\pmod{4}\\
0&\text{ if }q\equiv 1\pmod{4}\end{cases}.

c) If
\sigma=[\rho_{1}\boxtimes\rho_{2}]
and
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}
then

\dim\sigma^{R}=q-1.

d) If
q
is even,
\sigma=[\rho_{1}\boxtimes\rho_{2}]
and
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u+a(b^{2}+b)&a\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},b,u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}
then

\dim\sigma^{R}=1.

Proof.

a) Note
\omega_{\rho_{1}}(a)\omega_{\rho_{2}}(a)=1
so
\omega_{\rho_{1}}(-1)=\omega_{\rho_{2}}(-1)
. Let
(t_{1},t_{2})\in R
. Since the
\rho_{i}
are cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}(q)
, the character table gives that
\dim(\rho_{i})=q-1
and
{\rm tr\,}\rho_{i}(t_{j})=0
if
t_{j}
is nonscalar. Thus the only contributions come from elements of the form
(aI_{2},aI_{2})
and, if
q
is odd,
(aI_{2},-aI_{2})
.
So if
q
is odd then

\displaystyle\dim\sigma^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|R|^{-1}\sum_{(t_{1},t_{2})\in R}{\rm tr\,}\rho_{1}(t_{1}){\rm tr\,}\rho_{2}(t_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)^{-3}\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}(\dim\rho_{1})(\dim\rho_{2})\omega_{\rho_{1}}(a)\omega_{\rho_{2}}(a)(1+\omega_{\rho_{2}}(-1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\omega_{\rho_{2}}(-1).

If
q
is even then the same computation shows that
\dim\sigma^{R}=1
.

b) This case occurs if and only if
q
is odd (see
[
Ros2016
]
, Lemma A.6) and both
\rho_{i}
have reducible restriction to
{\rm SL}_{2}(q)
. There then exist equidimensional irreducible representations
\sigma_{\pm}
of
{\rm SL}_{2}(q)
with
{\rho_{1}}|_{{\rm SL}_{2}(q)}={\rho_{2}}|_{{\rm SL}_{2}(q)}=\sigma_{+}\oplus\sigma_{-}
. The character table of
{\rm SL}_{2}(q)
gives
{\rm tr\,}\sigma_{\pm}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}t&\\
&t^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)=0
if
t\neq\pm 1
. If
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
is not a square then
\rho_{i}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x&\\
&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)
interchanges
\sigma_{+}
and
\sigma_{-}
. It follows that

\sigma|_{{\rm SL}_{2}(q)^{2}}\in\{\sigma_{+}\boxtimes\sigma_{+}\oplus\sigma_{-}\boxtimes\sigma_{-},\ \sigma_{+}\boxtimes\sigma_{-}\oplus\sigma_{-}\boxtimes\sigma_{+}\}.

Let
(t_{1},t_{2})\in R
. If
\det(t_{1})=\det(t_{2})
is not a square, then
\sigma(t_{1},t_{2})
interchanges the two summands, so
{\rm tr\,}\sigma(t_{1},t_{2})=0
. If
\det(t_{1})=\det(t_{2})=a^{2}
then
(t_{1},t_{2})=(aI_{2},aI_{2})(t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime})
with
\det(t_{i}^{\prime})=1
, so
{\rm tr\,}\sigma(t_{1},t_{2})={\rm tr\,}\sigma(t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime})
and is zero unless
t_{1}^{\prime}=t_{2}^{\prime}=\pm I_{2}
or
t_{1}^{\prime}=-t_{2}^{\prime}=\pm I_{2}
, which implies the
t_{i}
are scalar matrices. Clearly
{\rm tr\,}\sigma(aI_{2},\pm aI_{2})=\dim\sigma\cdot\omega_{\sigma}(1,\pm 1)=\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{2}\omega_{\rho_{2}}(\pm 1).
Thus

\displaystyle\dim\sigma^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|R|^{-1}\sum_{(t_{1},t_{2})\in R}{\rm tr\,}\sigma(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)^{-3}\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\left({\rm tr\,}\sigma(aI_{2},aI_{2})+{\rm tr\,}\sigma(aI_{2},-aI_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\omega_{\rho_{2}}(-1)}{2}.

Finally, we claim
\omega_{\rho_{2}}(-1)=(-1)^{\frac{q-3}{2}}
, from which the final conclusion follows. To see this, let
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
with
t^{q-1}=-1
so
t^{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times 2}
. The representation
\rho_{2}
is associated to a character
\Lambda:\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
with
\Lambda^{q-1}=\alpha\circ N
, where
N:\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
is the norm map and
\alpha:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\to\{\pm 1\}
is the nontrivial quadratic character. Thus
\omega_{\rho_{2}}(-1)=\Lambda(-1)=\Lambda(t^{q-1})=\alpha(t^{q+1})=\alpha(-1)\alpha(t^{2})=-\alpha(-1)=(-1)^{\frac{q-3}{2}}
.

c) From the character table for
{\rm GL}_{2}(q)
and cuspidality of
\rho_{i}
, we have
{\rm tr\,}\rho_{i}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)=-\omega_{\rho_{i}}(a)
if
u\neq 0
. Thus

\displaystyle\dim\sigma^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|R|^{-1}\sum_{(g_{1},g_{2})\in R}{\rm tr\,}\rho_{1}(g_{1}){\rm tr\,}\rho_{2}(g_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}{\rm tr\,}\rho_{1}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right){\rm tr\,}\rho_{2}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\left((q-1)^{2}+\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}(-1)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q-1.

d) Note
|\{b^{2}+b:b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}|=\frac{q}{2}
. We have

\displaystyle\dim\sigma^{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|R|^{-1}\sum_{(g_{1},g_{2})\in R}{\rm tr\,}\rho_{1}(g_{1}){\rm tr\,}\rho_{2}(g_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q^{2}(q-1)}\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},b^{2}+b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}{\rm tr\,}\rho_{1}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u+a(b^{2}+b)&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right){\rm tr\,}\rho_{2}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q^{2}}\left((q-1)\sum\limits_{b^{2}+b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}{\rm tr\,}\rho_{1}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
b^{2}+b&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)-\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},b^{2}+b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}{\rm tr\,}\rho_{1}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
u+b^{2}+b&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q^{2}}\left((q-1)^{2}-(q-1)\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)+(q-1)-\sum\limits_{u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},b^{2}+b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}{\rm tr\,}\rho_{1}\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
u+b^{2}+b&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{q^{2}}\left((q-1)\left(\frac{q}{2}+1\right)+(q-2)\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)-(q-1)\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.

This completes the proof.∎

Suppose that
\sigma=[\lambda\rho\boxtimes\rho]
for some irreducible cuspidal representation
\rho

{\rm GL}_{2}(q)
and
\lambda:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

\omega_{\rho}(-1)=1
, and

. Let

be the involution acting on
\sigma

a) If
q
is even then
s
acts trivially on
\sigma^{R}
for each of the three subgroups
R
in Lemma
3.2
.

b) If
q
is even and
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}c&\\
&abc^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\right\}
then
\sigma(w,w)
acts trivially on
\sigma^{R}
.

c) Suppose
q
is odd and
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}c&\\
&abc^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\right\}
. If
\lambda\omega_{\rho}=1
then
\sigma(w,w)
and
s
act trivially on
\sigma^{R}
. If
\lambda\omega_{\rho}\neq 1
then
\sigma(w,w)

(-1)^{\frac{q-3}{2}}
and

acts with signature 0.

Proof.

a), b). Since
q
is even we may replace
\rho
by
\lambda^{-1/2}\rho
, and so assume
\lambda=\omega_{\rho}=1
.

Suppose first
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}c&\\
&abc^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\right\}
. Let
S=\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right]:a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\}
. Since
\rho
is cuspidal,
{\rm tr\,}\rho\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right]=(q-1)\delta_{a,b}
. Thus

\dim\rho^{S}=\frac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}\sum\limits_{a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}{\rm tr\,}\rho\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right]=1.

Let
v
be a nonzero vector in
\rho^{S}
. Then clearly
v\otimes v\in\sigma^{R}
and is fixed by
s
. Since
\rho(w)v\in V^{S}
, we have
\rho(w)v=\pm v
(in fact
\rho(w)v=-v
using the character table). Thus
\sigma(w,w)(v\otimes v)=\rho(w)v\otimes\rho(w)v=v\otimes v
. Since
\dim\sigma^{R}=1
by Lemma
3.2
, the conclusion follows.

Next, suppose
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}
. Fix a nontrivial character
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, and for each
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
let
v_{a}\in\rho
be the vector, unique up to scalars, such that
\rho\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
u&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)v_{a}=\psi(au)v_{a}
for all
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The existence and uniqueness of
v_{a}
is equivalent the genericity of cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}(q)
(and uniqueness of Whittaker models), which can be proved directly with the character table. So
\sigma\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
u&1\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
u&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)(v_{a}\otimes v_{a})=\psi(au)v_{a}\otimes\psi(au)v_{a}=\psi(2au)(v_{a}\otimes v_{a})=v_{a}\otimes v_{a}
since
q
is even. We have
\dim\sigma^{R}=q-1
by Lemma
3.2
, so one basis for
\sigma^{R}
consists of the vectors
\{v_{a}\otimes v_{a}:\ a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\}
. The conclusion follows.

If
R=\left\{\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u+a(b^{2}+b)&a\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
u&a\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right):a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times},b,u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\right\}
, then
\sigma^{R}
is a subspace of the one considered in the previous case, hence the conclusion follows.

c) By Lemma
3.2
we have
\dim\sigma^{R}=2
. We will compute the signature of
\sigma(w,w)
on
\sigma^{R}
and see that it is either
2
or
2(-1)^{\frac{q-3}{2}}
, according to
\lambda\omega_{\rho}
being trivial and nontrivial. This signature equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q-1)^{3}}\sum\limits_{a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}{\rm tr\,}\sigma(w\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right],w\left[\begin{smallmatrix}c&\\
&abc^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q-1)^{3}}\sum\limits_{a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\lambda(ab){\rm tr\,}\rho(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&b\\
-a&\end{smallmatrix}\right]){\rm tr\,}\rho(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&abc^{-1}\\
-c&\end{smallmatrix}\right])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q-1)^{3}}\sum\limits_{a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\lambda\left(ab^{-1}\right)(\lambda\omega_{\rho})^{2}(b){\rm tr\,}\rho\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
-ab^{-1}&\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\lambda(a){\rm tr\,}\rho\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
-a&\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{q-1}\sum\limits_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\setminus-{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times 2}}\lambda(a)\omega_{\rho}(a).

In the second equality we used the fact that the two matrices are conjugate. The characteristic polynomial of
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
-a&\end{smallmatrix}\right]
is
x^{2}+a
. From the character table of
{\rm GL}_{2}(q)
we get no contribution if
-a
is a square. Let
\theta:\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
with
\theta\neq\theta^{q}
correspond to
\rho
. Note
\omega_{\rho}=\theta|_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}
. If
-a
is a nonsquare then
{\rm tr\,}\rho\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
-a&\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)^{2}=(-\theta(\sqrt{-a})-\theta(-\sqrt{-a}))^{2}=4\theta(\sqrt{-a})^{2}=4\theta(-a)=4\omega_{\rho}(a)
. This explains the last equality. If
\lambda\omega_{\rho}=1
, the conclusion follows since there are
\frac{q-1}{2}
terms in the summation. If
\lambda\omega_{\rho}
is the nontrivial quadratic character and
-a
is a nonsquare then
(\lambda\omega_{\rho})(a)=(\lambda\omega_{\rho})(-1)(\lambda\omega_{\rho})(-a)=(-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}(-1)=(-1)^{\frac{q-3}{2}}
. The conclusion follows.

Finally, we consider the action of
s
. Since
q
is odd, there is an explicit model of
\rho
on the space
\{f:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\}
in which we have
(\rho\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right]f)(x)=\omega_{\rho}(b)f(\frac{ax}{b})
; we will not need the formulas for the other operators. Therefore
\sigma
can be realized on
\{f:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\}
,
(sf)(x,y)=f(y,x)
, and

\displaystyle(\sigma\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}ac&\\
&bc^{-1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)f)(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(ab)\omega_{\rho}(b^{2}c^{-1})f\left(ab^{-1}x,ab^{-1}c^{2}y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(ab^{-1})\omega_{\rho}(c^{-1})f\left(ab^{-1}x,ab^{-1}c^{2}y\right),

so
f
is
R
-invariant if and only if
f(x,y)=\lambda(a)\omega_{\rho}(c^{-1})f(ax,ac^{2}y)
. Let
\varepsilon\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
be a fixed nonsquare. For
\zeta\in\{1,\varepsilon\}
, define the functions
f_{1},f_{2}\in\sigma
by

\displaystyle f_{1}(x,x\zeta d^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(x^{-1})\omega_{\rho}(d)\delta_{1,\zeta}

\displaystyle f_{2}(x,x\zeta d^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(x^{-1})\omega_{\rho}(d)\delta_{\varepsilon,\zeta}.

Here
\delta_{a,b}
denotes the Kronecker delta function. It is easy to show that
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
lie in
\sigma^{R}
, hence are a basis. The reader may verify that
f_{1}(y,x)=f_{1}(x,y)
and
f_{2}(y,x)=(\lambda\omega_{\rho})(\varepsilon)f_{2}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
. The conclusion follows. ∎

Suppose

3.2

by a well-known decomposition (see
[

{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})\supset I

{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})

we may take double coset representatives for

to have the form

&\varpi^{i+j}&&\\
&&\varpi^{i}&\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right],\qquad S(x,y,z):=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
x&y&1&\\
z&x&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]

we can and do assume

. Since

\displaystyle[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[S(\varpi^{-i-j}x,0,0)t_{i,j}S(0,y,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[S(0,\varpi^{-j}y,0)t_{i,j}S(x,0,z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[S(0,0,\varpi^{-2i-j}z)t_{i,j}S(x,y,0)]

we may assume
{\rm val}(x)\leq i+j
or

,
{\rm val}(y)\leq j-1
or
y=0
, and
{\rm val}(z)\leq 2i+j
or

, respectively. We now obtain additional relations on the valuations of

,

3.5 Lemma.

Suppose that
i\geq 0
and consider the double coset
[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]
.

a) If
x\neq 0
we may assume that
0\leq i+{\rm val}(y/x)
.

b) If
xyz\neq 0
and
{\rm val}(z)=2i+1+{\rm val}(y)
then we may assume that
i+{\rm val}(y/x)\leq e-1
.

Proof.

Let
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&&\\
c&1&&\\
&&1&\\
&&-c&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]
where
c\in\mathfrak{p}^{i+1}
. Then

[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]=[(t_{i,j}At_{i,j}^{-1})t_{i,j}(A^{-1}S(x,y,z)A)]=[t_{i,j}S(x+cy,y,z+2cx+c^{2}y)].

a) If
{\rm val}(x)>i+{\rm val}(y)
take
c=-x/y\in\mathfrak{p}^{i+1}
so
[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]=[t_{i,j}S(0,y,z-x^{2}/y)]
.

b) Let
k={\rm val}(zy/x^{2})
. If
i+{\rm val}(y/x)\geq e
then
k=1+2(i+{\rm val}(y/x))\geq 1+2e
so there exists a unique
s\in 1+\mathfrak{p}^{k-e}
with

. Taking
c=-\frac{x}{y}(1-s)\in\mathfrak{p}^{{\rm val}(x/y)+k-e}=\mathfrak{p}^{2i+1+{\rm val}(y/x)-e}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{i+1}
shows
[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]=[t_{i,j}S(xs,y,0)]
.
∎

Define the two subgroups
U_{1},U_{2}\subset{\rm GL}_{2,2}(q)
by

U_{1}=\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
*&1\end{smallmatrix}\right],I_{2}\right),\qquad U_{2}=\left(I_{2},\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\\
*&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)

and observe that if
R_{g}\supset U_{1}
or
R_{g}\supset U_{2}
then
\tau^{R_{g}}=0
by cuspidality of
\sigma
.

3.6 Lemma.

Let
i\geq 0
. If
[t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)]\in{\rm Supp}(\pi)
then:

a) We must have
1\leq j\leq n-2-2i
.

b) We may assume
{\rm val}(x^{2})\geq 2i+j+2
and
{\rm val}(y^{2})\geq j+1
,

c) We may assume
{\rm val}(z)=2i+1+{\rm val}(y)
.

Proof.

Let
g=t_{i,j}S(x,y,z)
.

a) If
2i+j\geq n-1
then take
c\in\mathfrak{p}^{2i+j+1-n}
and compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
\varpi^{n}c&&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
\varpi^{n-2i-j}c&&&1\end{bmatrix}.

So
R_{g}\supset U_{1}
. If
j\leq 0
then take
b\in{\mathfrak{o}}
and compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
xb&1&b&\\
&&1&\\
&&-xb&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1-yb&\varpi^{j}b&\\
&\varpi^{-j}y^{2}b&1-yb&\\
\varpi^{-2i-j}x^{2}b&&&1\end{bmatrix}.

Observe that
\varpi^{-j}y^{2}b\in\mathfrak{p}
. If
x=0
then taking
b\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j}
shows
R_{g}
contains a conjugate of
U_{2}
. If
x\neq 0
, then
2i+j-2{\rm val}(x)+1\neq-j
by parity. So let
M:=\max(2i+j-2{\rm val}(x)+1,-j)\geq 0
and take
b\in\mathfrak{p}^{M}
. Thus
R_{g}
contains either
U_{1}
or a conjugate of
U_{2}
.

b) Suppose
{\rm val}(x^{2})\leq 2i+j+1
, so
x\neq 0
and we may assume
0\leq i+{\rm val}(y/x)
by Lemma
3.5
. Let
b\in\mathfrak{p}^{2i+j+1-{\rm val}(x^{2})}
and compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&b&\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
-\varpi^{-i}xb&1-yb&\varpi^{j}b&\\
\varpi^{-i-j}xyb&\varpi^{-j}y^{2}b&1-yb&\\
-\varpi^{-2i-j}x^{2}b&\varpi^{-i-j}xyb&\varpi^{-i}xb&1\end{bmatrix}.

Since
j\geq 1
and
{\rm val}(x)\leq i+j
, we have
\varpi^{-i}xb\in\mathfrak{p}^{i+j-{\rm val}(x)}\subset\mathfrak{p}
,
\varpi^{-i-j}xyb\in\mathfrak{p}^{i+1+{\rm val}(y/x)}\subset\mathfrak{p}
, and
\varpi^{-j}y^{2}b\in\mathfrak{p}^{1+2(i+{\rm val}(y/x))}\subset\mathfrak{p}
. So
R_{g}\supset U_{1}
and we may assume
{\rm val}(x^{2})\geq 2i+j+2
.

If
0\leq i+{\rm val}(y/x)
then
2i+j+2\leq{\rm val}(x^{2})\leq 2i+{\rm val}(y^{2})
so
{\rm val}(y^{2})\geq j+2
. So if
{\rm val}(y^{2})\leq j
then
{\rm val}(x)>i+{\rm val}(y)\geq i+1
and we repeat the previous computation, taking now
b\in\mathfrak{p}^{j-{\rm val}(y^{2})}
and noting then that
xb\in\mathfrak{p}^{i+1}
,
xyb\in\mathfrak{p}^{j+{\rm val}(x/y)}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{i+j+1}
, and
x^{2}b\in\mathfrak{p}^{j+2{\rm val}(x/y)}\subset\mathfrak{p}^{2i+j+2}
. So
R_{g}\supset U_{2}
and we may assume that
{\rm val}(y^{2})\geq j+1
.

c) Suppose
{\rm val}(z)\neq 2i+1+{\rm val}(y)
. If
1\leq{\rm val}(y)\leq j-1
or
1\leq{\rm val}(z)\leq 2i+j
then let
M:=\max(j-{\rm val}(y),2i+j+1-{\rm val}(z))\geq 1
and take
a\in 1+\mathfrak{p}^{M}
. Then compute

g\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&a&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&a\end{bmatrix}g^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&&&\\
&a&&\\
&\varpi^{-j}y(a-1)&1&\\
\varpi^{-2i-j}z(1-a)&&&a\end{bmatrix}

so either
R_{g}\supset U_{1}
or
R_{g}\supset U_{2}
. On the other hand, if
{\rm val}(y)\geq j
and
{\rm val}(z)\geq 2i+j+1
then
[g]=[t_{i,j}S(x,0,0)]
.
∎

4
Counting double cosets

We must enumerate the elements of
{\rm Supp}(\pi)
. Since

up to

, and some tedious matrix computations that there are no equalities between double cosets of different types, and that

determines
i\geq 0
and
j\geq 1
, as well as
{\rm val}(x)
and
{\rm val}(y)
in those cases when

and/or

. Since
[t_{i,j}S(x,0,0)]=[t_{i,j}S(\varpi^{{\rm val}(x)},0,0)]
if
x\neq 0
by using integral diagonal matrices, these considerations quickly yield the following two results.

4.1 Lemma.

The number of double cosets of type I is

\left\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Compute
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-j-2}{2}\right\rfloor}(1)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n-2}\left\lfloor\frac{n-j}{2}\right\rfloor=\sum\limits_{j=2}^{n-1}\left\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.

∎

4.2 Lemma.

If
q
is even then the number of double cosets of type II is

\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\leq 3\\
\left\lfloor\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor&\text{ if }n\geq 4\end{cases}.

Proof.

Since
e=1
we must have
i+j={\rm val}(x)\geq i+1+\frac{j}{2}
. So the relevant inequalities are
2\leq j\leq n-2-2i
and
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-4}{2}\right\rfloor
. Compute
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{n-4}{2}\right\rfloor}(n-3-2i)=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
.
∎

Suppose now that
g=t_{i,j}S(0,y,z)
is type III (so
q
is even). Using integral diagonal matrices, we may assume that
z
is a power of
\varpi
. So the only ambiguity that remains is counting the number of distinct double cosets of the form
[t_{i,j}S(0,u\varpi^{r},\varpi^{2i+1+r})]
, where
u\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}^{j-r})
and
i,j,r
are fixed. Using integral diagonal matrices again, it is easy to see that we may adjust

by squares. A tedious matrix computation shows that there are no additional equalities, so that
(i,j,r,u)
form a complete set of invariants if
u\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}/{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times 2}(1+\mathfrak{p}^{j-r})
. In particular, there are
q^{\lfloor\frac{j-r}{2}\rfloor}
such double cosets for fixed
i,j,r
.

4.3 Lemma.

If
q
is even and
n\geq 2
then the number of double cosets of type IIIa is

\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\leq 3\\
\left\lfloor\frac{(n-3)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor.&\text{ if }n\geq 4\end{cases}.

Since
e=1
and
j-r

j-1=r\geq\frac{j+1}{2}
so the relevant inequalities are

and

. Compute

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-5}{2}\rfloor}(n-4-2i)=\left\lfloor\frac{(n-3)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor
.
∎

4.4 Lemma.

If
q
is even then the number of double cosets of type IIIb is

\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\leq 3\\
q\left\lfloor\frac{(n-5)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor&\text{ if }n\geq 4\end{cases}.

The conditions

.∎

Finally, suppose

is type IV (so
q

4.5 Lemma.

If

6.2 Theorem.

Let
\pi=\text{\rm c-Ind}_{N_{G}({\rm K}(\mathfrak{p}))}^{G}(\tau)
be an irreducible depth zero supercuspidal representation with trivial central character. Let
\sigma
be an irreducible constituent of
\tau|_{{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})}
and assume
\omega_{\sigma}=1
. Let
s(\pi,n)
denote the signature of the Atkin-Lehner involution on
\pi^{{\rm Si}(\mathfrak{p}^{n})}
.

a) Suppose
n\geq 4
is even. If
q
is odd then
s(\pi,n)=0
. If
q
is even then

s(\pi,n)=\left(1+\frac{q(n-4)}{2}\right)\cdot\begin{cases}2&\text{ if }\sigma\neq\sigma^{u_{1}}\\
1&\text{ if }\sigma=\sigma^{u_{1}}\end{cases}

b) Suppose
n\geq 3
is odd. If
q
is even then

s(\pi,n)=\pm\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\sigma\neq\sigma^{u_{1}}\\
1&\text{ if }n=3\text{ and }\sigma=\sigma^{u_{1}}\\
1+q(n-4)&\text{ if }n\geq 5\text{ and }\sigma=\sigma^{u_{1}}\end{cases}

while if
q
is odd then

s(\pi,n)=\pm\frac{(n-1)}{2}\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\sigma\neq\sigma^{u_{1}}\\
2&\text{ if }\sigma=\lambda[\rho\boxtimes\rho],\ \lambda\omega_{\rho}=1\\
0&\text{ if }\sigma=\lambda[\rho\boxtimes\rho],\ \lambda\omega_{\rho}=\lambda_{0}\\
1&\text{ if }\sigma\subsetneq\lambda[\rho\boxtimes\rho]\end{cases}.

Here the
\pm
sign is determined by the choice of extension
\tau(u_{1})
acting on
\sigma=\sigma^{u_{1}}
.

Proof.

a) The results of Lemma
6.1
show that if
[gu_{n}]=[g]
then
f(gu_{n})=f(g)
since
g
is of type II or IV. In particular,
q
is even. Note both of these types have
\dim\sigma^{R_{g}}=1
by lemma
3.2
. The number of fixed double cosets was shown in Lemma
6.1
to be
\frac{n-2}{2}+(q-1)(\frac{n-4}{2})=1+\frac{q(n-4)}{2}
. The conclusion follows.

b) The results of Lemma
6.1
show that if
[gu_{n}]=[g]
then
g
is of type I or III and
f(gu_{n})=\tau(s)f(g)
for some element
s
of order
2
, with
s\in u_{1}{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})
. Hence the contribution of the double coset
[g]=[gu_{n}]
is equal to the trace of
\tau(s)
on
\tau^{R_{g}}
. If
\sigma\neq\sigma^{u_{1}}
, the formula for the induced character shows that the trace of
\tau(s)
on
\tau^{R_{g}}=\sigma^{R_{g}}\oplus\sigma{{}^{*}}^{R_{g}}
is zero. Suppose now that
\sigma=\sigma^{u_{1}}
, so
\tau|_{{\rm K}(\mathfrak{p})}=\sigma
, and there are two choies of
\tau
for a given
\sigma
, distinguished by
\tau(u_{1})
.

If
q
is even then we may combine Lemma
3.3
parts a) and b), together with the formulas in Lemma
6.1
parts a) and c), to conclude that
\tau(s)
acts trivially on
\sigma^{R_{g}}
. Hence the signature is the number of fixed double cosets, weighted by
\dim\sigma^{R_{g}}
, which is thus
1
if
n=3
and, if
n\geq 5
, is
\frac{n-1}{2}\left(1\right)+\frac{n-3}{2}(q-1)+q\left(\frac{n-5}{2}\right)(1)=1+q(n-4)
.

Finally, suppose
q
is odd. In this case
s=u_{1}
. If
\sigma=\lambda[\rho\boxtimes\rho]
then
\dim\sigma^{R_{g}}=2
and we may apply Lemma
3.3
part c), since in this case
s=u_{1}
and one choice of
\tau(u_{1})
acts on
\sigma^{R_{g}}
by a composition of the swapping map
s
and
\sigma(w,w)
where
w=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
-1&\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. If
\sigma\subsetneq\lambda[\rho\boxtimes\rho]
, then
\dim\sigma^{R_{g}}=1
, so
\tau(u_{1})f(g)=\pm f(g)
is automatic. The conclusion then follows from Lemma
6.1
part a).
∎

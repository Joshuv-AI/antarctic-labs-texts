Affine mappings of translation surfaces: shrinking targets and Diophantine approximation
Chris Judge, Josh Southerland

Abstract
. Let ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) be a translation surface whose Veech group Γ \Gamma is a lattice. We prove that the generic orbit of the group of affine homeomorphisms of ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) can be used to approximate each point of X X with Diophantine precision. The proof utilizes an induced S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}}) -action on a fiber bundle Y Y whose base is S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) / Γ SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})/\Gamma and whose fiber is X X . We observe that this bundle embeds as an S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}}) -orbit closure in the moduli space of once marked translation surfaces, and hence we may invoke the spectral gap results of Avila and Gouëzel [ AvlGzl13 ] and a quantitative mean ergodic theorem for the S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}}) action on the mean-zero, square-integrable functions on Y Y .



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a closed, orientable, smooth surface, and let
\omega
be a
complex valued 1-form that is holomorphic with respect to some complex structure on
X
. Integration of
(i/2)\omega\wedge\overline{\omega}
defines a finite measure
\mu_{\omega}
on
X
. Integration of
|\omega|
defines a notion of length that in turn defines a distance function
d_{\omega}
. A self-homeomorphism
\phi:X\to X

is said to be
affine
with respect to
\omega
iff

(1)

\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Re}(\phi^{*}(\omega))\\
\operatorname{Im}(\phi^{*}(\omega))\end{pmatrix}~=~\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Re}(\omega)\\
\operatorname{Im}(\omega)\end{pmatrix}

for some
2\times 2
invertible real matrix. The matrix is uniquely determined
by (
1
), and we will denote it by
D\phi
. Each
affine homeomorphism preserves the measure
\mu_{\omega}
, and because the
measure is finite, one finds that
\det(D\phi)=\pm 1
. The map
\phi\mapsto D\phi
defines a homomorphism from the group,
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
, of orientation-preserving
affine homeomorphisms into
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

In the present paper, we assume that the image of
D
is a lattice
subgroup of
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. In this case, most elements in
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
are pseudo-Anosov diffeomorphisms and hence act ergodically—in fact, Bernoulli—on
(X,\mu_{\omega})
(see §10.6 in
[
FLP79
]
). Consequently, the action of
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
is ergodic, and so we can ask questions about the quantitative density of the orbits of generic points.

For each
g\in SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, let

\|g\|:=\sqrt{{\rm tr}(g^{t}g)}
. To measure the quantitative density of orbits,
for each
(x,y,\alpha)\in X\times X\times{\mathbb{R}}
we consider the
set

H_{x,y,\alpha}~:=~\left\{\phi\in{\rm Aff}_{\omega}^{+}~:~d_{\omega}(\phi(x),y)~\leq~\|D\phi\|^{-\alpha}\right\}.

One can show that there exists a constant
\alpha_{\omega}
so that
for almost every
(x,y)\in X\times X
the set
H_{x,y,\alpha}

is infinite if
\alpha<\alpha_{\omega}
and finite if
\alpha>\alpha_{\omega}

(compare with
[
LaNo12
]

[
Kel17
]
and see Proposition

2.6
.)

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
D{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
is a lattice in
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then

0<\alpha_{\omega}\leq 1
.

If
X
is the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}:={\mathbb{C}}/({\mathbb{Z}}+i{\mathbb{Z}})
marked at
0
and
\omega=dz
,
then it follows from work of Ghosh, Gorodnik, and Nevo that
\alpha_{\omega}=1

(see Corollary 1.2 in
[
GGN18
]
). In the language of
[
GGN18
]
, the constant
\alpha_{\omega}
is the ‘Diophantine approximation exponent’, and if

\alpha_{\omega}=1
, then one says
that this exponent is optimal
[
GGN18
]
.

Using a different method, Finkelshtein
[
Fink17
]

recovered the above result of
[
GGN18
]
and extended it to the
action of thin subgroups of
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{dz}\cong SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
acting on the torus
with a refinement of the quantitative estimates.
The second named author extended these estimates to ‘regular’ ‘square-tiled surfaces’

[
Sou24
]
. Recall that
(X,\omega)
is square-tiled surface iff there exists
a branched covering
p:X\to{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
branched over
0
so that
\omega=p^{*}(dz)
. A square-tiled surface is said to be ‘regular’ if
p|_{X\setminus\omega^{-1}(0)}
is a normal
covering. The results in
[
Sou24
]
imply that
\alpha_{\omega}=1
if
(X,\omega)
is
a regular square tiled surface.

The proof of the upper bound in Theorem
1.1

comes from an elementary argument involving the ‘easy’ implication of the Borel-Cantelli Lemma. Our proof of the strict lower bound on
\alpha_{\omega}
depends on showing that
a certain unitary representation of
G:=SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
has a spectral gap.
(Compare with, for example,
[
GGN18
]

[
Kel17
]
.)
To be precise, consider the lattice subgroup
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega,0}

consisting of affine homeomorphisms that act as the identity on
\omega^{-1}(0)
. This group acts on the right of
G
by
(g,\phi)\mapsto g\cdot D\phi
and on the right of
X
by
(\phi,x)\mapsto\phi^{-1}(x)
. Hence

{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega,0}
acts on the product
G\times X
, and
we define
Y
to be the quotient. The product of Haar measure on
G
and
d\mu_{\omega}
descends to a finite measure
\nu
on
Y
, and the left action of
G
on
G\times X

given by
(g\cdot(h,x))\mapsto(gh,x)
descends
to a measure preserving left action of
G
on
Y
. Therefore we have
a unitary representation
\pi_{\omega}
of
G=SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
on
L^{2}(Y,\nu)
defined
by

(\pi_{\omega}(g)u)([h,x])~:=~u\left(g^{-1}\cdot[h,x]\right).

Let
\beta_{\omega}
denote the infimum of the numbers
1-s
such that the complementary series
\pi_{s}
is a subrepresentation of
\pi_{\omega}
.
(See §
5
for the construction of
\pi_{s}
that we use.)
The constant
\beta_{\omega}
is the
spectral gap
for
\pi_{\omega}
.

The lower bound in Theorem
1.1
will follow from

Theorem 1.2
.

We have
\alpha_{\omega}\geq\beta_{\omega}>0
.

In fact, we prove a slightly stronger statement.
We show that for every
y
and a.e.
x
the set
H_{x,y,\alpha}
is
infinite if
\alpha<\beta_{\omega}
.
See Theorem
6.4
.

It is well-known that a representation of
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is tempered if and only if none of its subrepresentations are isomorphic to a complementary series.
In particular,
\pi_{\omega}
is tempered iff

\beta_{\omega}=1
. Therefore we have

Corollary 1.3
.

If
\pi
is tempered, then the Diophantine approximation exponent is optimal.

Question 1.4
.

For which
(X,\omega)
is
\pi_{\omega}
tempered?

In general, the spectral gap
\beta_{\omega}
does not equal 1. Indeed,
the space
Y=(SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})\times X)/D{\rm Aff}_{\omega,0}^{+}
is a bundle over
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})/D{\rm Aff}_{\omega,0}^{+},
and the left action of
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
commutes with the bundle projection.
Consequently, any irreducible complementary series representation of
L^{2}(SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})/D{\rm Aff}_{\omega,0}^{+})
will pullback to an irreducible complementary series representation in the bundle
L^{2}(Y)
. Ellenberg and McReynolds show that for any finite index subgroup
\Gamma\subset\Gamma(2)
of the principal congruence group of level 2 that contains
-\mathrm{Id}
, there exists a square-tiled surface
(X,\omega)

such that
D{\rm Aff}_{\omega}^{+}=\Gamma

[
ElMc12
]

[
J13
]
.
The group
\Gamma(2)
is isomorphic to a free group and so
by abelianization and reduction modulo
n
, one obtains a surjective homomorphism

\Gamma(2)\mapsto{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
. For large enough
n
the kernel has complementary
series (see Appendix A of
[
MWS13
]
).
Moreover, Matheus and Weitze-Schmithüsen have constructed explicit examples of
(X,\omega)
such that
L^{2}(SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})/D{\rm Aff}_{\omega}^{+})
has irreducible complementary series representations with
s
arbitrarily close to 1
[
MWS13
]
.
Since each complementary series in
L^{2}(SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})/D{\rm Aff}_{\omega})
lifts to
a complementary series in
L^{2}(SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})/D{\rm Aff}_{\omega,0})
, the examples of
Matheus and Weitze-Schmithüsen have

\beta_{\omega}
arbitrarily close to zero.

Question 1.5
.

Does there exist
(X,\omega)
so that
\alpha_{\omega}\neq\beta_{\omega}
?

Since
\alpha_{\omega}=1
for regular square-tiled surfaces
[
Sou24
]
, the answer
is ‘yes’ if one could construct a regular square-tiled surface
(X,\omega)
so that
the natural representation of
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
on
L^{2}(SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})/D{\rm Aff}_{\omega}^{+})

has a complementary series.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we
adapt the method of Ghosh, Gorodnik, and Nevo
[
GGN18
]
(see also
[
Kel17
]
). We frame the Diophantine question in terms of a shrinking target problem and move this problem into the bundle
Y:=(SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\times X)/D{\rm Aff}_{\omega,0}^{+}
.
The induced
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action encodes the action of the affine group on the fiber
X
of the bundle
Y
(see §
4
). We show that a solution to a shrinking target problem with respect to the
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action gives a solution to the shrinking target problem for the affine group action on the surface. A spectral gap
allows us to use a quantitative mean ergodic theorem (see Appendix
A
)
in combination with a Borel-Cantelli argument to prove that
\alpha_{\omega}\geq\beta_{\omega}
.

To show that
\beta_{\omega}>0
, we embed
the total space of the bundle
Y
as an
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
-orbit closure
{\mathcal{M}}
in the moduli space of marked translation surfaces.
The embedding intertwines the measure-preserving
SL(2,{\mathbb{R}})
action on
Y
and the measure-preserving
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
action on
{\mathcal{M}}
. Consequently, we may apply the results concerning spectral gap in
[
AvlGzl13
]
which give
\beta_{\omega}>0
.

1.1.
The affine group

The study of the affine group
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
has its origins in the work of Thurston and Veech. Thurston
[
Th88
]
constructed
examples of affine groups generated by two multitwists. Using regular
polygons Veech
[
Vch89
]
constructed examples of translation surfaces
so that
D{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
is a lattice. Since that time many authors have discovered
many new examples of translation surfaces with this lattice property. We refer
the reader to the recent survey of McMullen
[
McM24
]
.

If
P\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a planar polygon whose vertex angles are rational multiples of
\pi
, then there exists a surface
(X,\omega)
such that
(P,dz)
is obtained by quotienting
(X,\omega)
by a finite group of isometries
[
FK36
]

[
ZK75
]
.
If
D{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
is a lattice, then the dynamics of
{\rm Aff}_{\omega}^{+}
is intimately connected with the billiard flow on
P
. Indeed, Veech realized that if
D{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
is a lattice, then
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
acts with finitely many orbits on the set of closed trajectories (up to homotopy) of the billiard flow lifted from
P

[
Vch89
]
. He used this observation to obtain asymptotics on the lengths of closed billiard trajectories.

1.2.
Lattices and Homogeneous dynamics

Lattice actions on homogeneous spaces have been extensively studied in part due to their close relationship with number theory and various counting problems.
The particular case of approximating points in the punctured plane
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
with orbits of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acting linearly on the punctured plane was studied by Ledrappier using dynamical methods
[
Le99
]
, and by Nogueira using number-theoretic methods
[
No02
]
.
Later, Laurent–Nogueira used number-theoretic methods to improve our understanding of approximation at particular points achieving the best possible rates for approximating rational vectors
[
LaNo12
]
. Using dynamical methods, Maucourant and Weiss studied the action of cocompact lattices
[
MW12
]
. They also approximate rates for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbits of vectors possessing a particular Diophantine conditions, but these rates are not as strong as those produced in
[
LaNo12
]
. Later still, Kelmer applied the framework of Ghosh, Gorodnik, and Nevo to general lattices
\Gamma\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
acting linearly on the plane
[
Kel17
]
. His work yields the best possible rates whenever the lattices in question are tempered.

The dynamical methods used in this literature share a few common features. First, the use of a closed subgroup
H
. Ledrappier
[
Le99
]
and Maucourant and Weiss
[
MW12
]
take
H
to be a unipotent subgroup.
For any lattice
\Gamma\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{R}),
the action of this one-parameter subgroup
H
on
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})/\Gamma
is dual to the action of the lattice
\Gamma
on
H\setminus SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.
The work is to translate quantitative information for the action of
H
into quantitative information for the lattice action. Ghosh–Gorodnik–Nevo consider this duality with respect to any closed subgroup
H
of an algebraic group
G
. Then a lattice
\Gamma\subset G
acting on
H\setminus G
is dual to the action of
H
on
G/\Gamma.
Their results apply to a variety of settings beyond approximation in the punctured plane and yield optimal Diophantine exponents when the lattice
\Gamma
is tempered.

Second, at the core of the arguments is a “shrinking target” framework. For the benefit of the reader, we describe a shrinking target problem in our setting. Fix a lattice surface
(X,\omega)
with orientation preserving affine homeomorphisms
{\rm Aff}_{\omega}^{+}
and pick any
y\in X
. For any
\phi\in{\rm Aff}_{\omega}^{+},
let
B_{r(\phi)}(y)
denote the open ball with a radius
r
that depends on the operator norm of the differential of
\phi.
These are our targets for each respective
\phi.
We can ask: does almost every
x\in X
have the property that
\phi(x)\in B_{r(\phi)}(y)
for infinitely many
\phi\in\mathrm{Aff}_{\omega}^{+}
? How fast can we decrease the radius (shrink the target) as the norm of the differential of
\phi
increases before this no longer holds?
Hill and Velani coined the term “shrinking target” in their fundamental work on the subject
[
HiVe95
]
. The first appearance of the framework is generally agreed to be in the work of Philipp
[
Phi67
]
. The shrinking target framework is now ubiquitous in literature exhibiting dynamical methods to deduce Diophantine properties or logarithm laws.

1.3.
Organization

In Section
2
, we review techniques for establishing Diophantine properties for lattice actions. In Section
3
, we define and review properties of affine diffeomorphisms and the closely associated Veech group. In Section
4
, we construct the induced
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action. We observe that the resulting bundle is isomorphic to a closed
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant suborbifold, and moreover, the isomorphism is equivariant with respect to the
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action. In Section
5
, we show that the closed
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant suborbifold is an affine invariant manifold, and that the results of Avila and Gouëzel apply to our bundle. In Section
6
, we translate the spectral gap into an effective mean ergodic theorem for the
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action using a standard theorem that we cite in Appendix
A
. We use the results of Section
2
alongside further Borel-Cantelli arguments to establish the Diophantine properties cited in Theorem
6.4
.

2.
Diophantine properties for lattice actions

Let
G
be a locally compact, Hausdorff group.
Let
m
denote a Haar measure on
G
.
Let
\Gamma\subset G
be a lattice, that is, a discrete subgroup so that
m(G/\Gamma)<\infty
.
Let
X
be a space equipped with a finite measure
\mu
.
We suppose that
\Gamma
acts on the left of
X
so that
\gamma_{*}(\mu)=\mu
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Let us describe the shrinking target problem in this context.
Roughly speaking, we are given a ‘target’
T\subset X
with
\mu(T)>0
.
If
\Gamma
acts
\mu
-ergodically on
X
, then for a.e.
x
there exists
\gamma
such that
\gamma\cdot x\in T
.
The shrinking target problem asks: How does the ‘size’ of
\gamma
depend on
\mu(T)
as
\mu(T)\searrow 0
.

Here we will measure the size of
\gamma
using a function
\rho
that is compatible with the group structure.
Namely, we suppose that there exists a continuous
function
\rho:G\to[0,\infty)
and a constant
C_{\rho}
so that

\rho(g\cdot h)\leq\rho(g)+\rho(h)+C_{\rho}

and
\rho(g^{-1})\leq\rho(g)+C_{\rho}
.

We study the
\Gamma
-action on
X
by studying a
G
-action on a ‘fiber bundle’
Y
over
G
with fiber
X
.
We define the space
Y
to be the quotient
(G\times X)/\Gamma
where
\Gamma
acts on the right of
G\times X
by

(g,x)\cdot\gamma~:=~(g\cdot\gamma,\gamma^{-1}\cdot x).

The group
G
acts on the left of
Y
by

g(h,x)\Gamma~:=~(g\cdot h,x)\Gamma.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a measurable fundamental domain for the

\Gamma
-action on
G\times X
.
Define
\nu
to be the measure
\pi_{*}(m\times\mu)
where
\pi:{\mathcal{F}}\to Y
is the quotient map.
The
G
-action on
Y
preserves the measure
\nu
.

To understand the shrinking target problem for the action of

\Gamma
on
X
, we consider a related shrinking target problem for the
action of
G
on
Y
. In particular, we choose an auxilliary compact
set
S\subset G
with
\mu(S)>0
, and we ask: “In order for
g\cdot y
to lie in
\pi(S\times T)
, how ‘large’ should
g
be?”
The following proposition shows that an estimate for
G
acting on
Y

determines an estimate for
\Gamma
acting on
X
.

Define
G_{t}:=\rho^{-1}[0,t]
for
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
y=(h,x)\Gamma\in Y
.
If
G_{t}\cdot y\,\cap\,\pi(S\times T)\neq\emptyset
, then

\Gamma_{t+C_{h}}\cdot x\cap T\neq\emptyset
where

C_{h}=\rho(h)+\sup_{k\in S}\rho(k)+4C_{\rho}
.

Proof.

If
G_{t}\cdot(h,x)\Gamma\cap\pi(S\times T)\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
g\in G_{t}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma

such that

(gh,x)\gamma~\in~S\times T.

Thus,
gh\gamma\in S
and
\gamma^{-1}x\in T
.
In particular,
\gamma^{-1}=s^{-1}gh
for some
s\in S
.
Thus using the properties of
\rho
we find that

\displaystyle\rho(\gamma)~

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho(\gamma^{-1})\,+\,C_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho(s)\,+\,\rho(g)\,+\,\rho(h)\,+\,4C_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t\,+\,\rho(h)\,+\,\sup\{\rho(s)\,:\,s\in S\}\,+\,4C_{\rho}.

The claim follows.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{S,T,t}
denote the set of

y\in Y

such that
G_{t}\cdot y
does not intersect
\pi(S\times T)
.

Lemma 2.2

(Borel-Cantelli argument)
.

Let
t_{n}
be a sequence of positive numbers.
Suppose that there exists a sequence of sets
T_{n}
so that

\sum_{n}\nu({\mathcal{B}}_{S,T_{n},t_{n}})~<\infty.

For a.e.
x\in X
there exists
n_{x}
so that if
n\geq n_{x}
, then
there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
so that

(1)

\gamma\cdot x\in T_{n}
.

(2)

\rho(\gamma)\,\leq\,t_{n}\,+\,2\sup_{s\in S}\rho(s)\,+\,4C_{\rho}

Proof.

Borel-Cantelli implies that
\nu(E)=0
where

E~=~\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{n=j}^{\infty}{\mathcal{B}}_{S,T_{n},t_{n}}.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a fundamental domain for the action of
\Gamma
on
G
so that
m({\mathcal{F}}\cap S)>0
.
Let
\widetilde{E}
be the subset of
{\mathcal{F}}\times X

so that
(h,x)\mapsto(h,x)\Gamma
maps
\widetilde{E}
onto
E
.
Then
(m\times\mu)(\widetilde{E})=\nu(E)=0
.
Let
F
be the set of
x\in X
such that
m(\{h\in{\mathcal{F}}\,:\,(h,x)\in\widetilde{E}\})>0
.
Fubini’s theorem implies that
\mu(F)=0
.

Suppose that
x\notin F
, and so
m(\{h\in{\mathcal{F}}\,:\,(h,x)\in\widetilde{E}\})=0
.
Since
m({\mathcal{F}}\cap S)>0
, there exists
h\in{\mathcal{F}}\cap S
so that
(h,x)\notin\widetilde{E}
.
In other words,

(h,x)\Gamma~\in~Y\setminus E~=~\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcap_{n=j}^{\infty}Y\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{S,T_{n},t_{n}}.

Hence there exists
n_{x}
such that

(h,x)\Gamma~\in~\bigcap_{n=n_{x}}^{\infty}Y\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{S,T_{n},t_{n}}.

Therefore,
y=(h,x)\Gamma\in Y\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{S,T_{n},t_{n}}

for each
n\geq n_{x}
.
In other words, for
n\geq n_{x}
, we have

G_{t_{n}}\cdot y\,\cap\,\pi(S\times T_{n})\neq\emptyset
.

Proposition
2.1
then implies that
for
n\geq n_{x}
we have
\Gamma_{t_{n}+C}\cdot x\,\cap\,T_{n}\neq\emptyset

where
C=2\sup_{s\in S}\rho(s)+4C_{\rho}
. The claim follows.
∎

Recall that
G_{t}=\rho^{-1}[0,t]
.
Let
u:G\to{\mathbb{C}}
be measurable.
Define the
G_{t}
ergodic average of
u
to be

(A_{t}u)(y)~:=~\frac{1}{m(G_{t})}\int_{G_{t}}u(g^{-1}\cdot y)\,dm(g).

Define the space average of
u
to be

\overline{u}~:=~\frac{1}{\nu(Y)}\int_{Y}u\,d\nu.

Define the
L^{2}
ergodic error function
by

(2)

\epsilon(t)~:=~\sup_{u\in L^{2}(Y,d\nu)}\frac{\left\|A_{t}u-\overline{u}\right\|}{\|u\|}

where
\|\cdot\|
is the
L^{2}(Y,d\nu)
norm.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
S\subset G
is compact,
m(S)>0
, and
\gamma(S)\cap S=\emptyset
for each

\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{{\rm id}\}
.
Then

\nu({\mathcal{B}}_{S,T,t})~\leq~\epsilon(t)^{2}\cdot\frac{\nu(Y)^{2}}{m(S)\,\mu(T)}.

Proof.

Because the
\Gamma
translates of
S
are (pairwise) disjoint, the
\Gamma
translates of
S\times T
are disjoint.
Hence there exists a measurable set
{\mathcal{F}}\subset G\times X
containing
S\times T
so that
\pi|_{{\mathcal{F}}}:{\mathcal{F}}\to Y

is a measurable isomorphism.
If we define
F:Y\to{\mathbb{R}}
by
F:=\mathbbm{1}_{S\times T}\circ(\pi|_{{\mathcal{F}}})^{-1}
, then

(3)

\int_{Y}F\,d\nu~=~m(S)\cdot\mu(T).

Consider the ergodic average
A_{t}F

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\nu(Y)}\int_{Y}F\right|^{2}\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{S,T,t}}1\,d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu(Y)^{2}}(m(S)\cdot\mu(T))^{2}\cdot\nu({\mathcal{B}}_{S,T,t}).

Note that

We end this section by providing the argument used to define the constant

used in Theorem

Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of the group of measure preserving transformations
of
(X,\mu)
. Suppose that there exists a function

\rho:\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
and a constant

so that

\rho(\gamma\cdot\gamma^{\prime})\leq\rho(\gamma)+\rho(\gamma^{\prime})+C_{\rho}
.
Let

be a distance function on

.
Define

H_{x,y,\alpha}~:=~\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma~:~d(\gamma(x),y)~\leq~e^{-\alpha\rho(g)}\right\}.

Define
\overline{\alpha}:X\times X\to{\mathbb{R}}
by setting
\overline{\alpha}(x,y)
equal to
the supremum of
\alpha
so that the set

has infinite cardinality.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that each sublevel set of
\rho
is finite.
Suppose that
\Gamma
acts ergodically on
(X,\mu)
and that each
\gamma\in\Gamma
is Lipschitz with respect to
d
. Then there exists a constant

\alpha^{*}
so that for
\mu\otimes\mu
almost every
(x,y)
we have

\overline{\alpha}(x,y)=\alpha^{*}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\overline{\alpha}
is invariant under the

\Gamma\times\Gamma
action on
X\times X

defined by
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\cdot(x_{1},x_{2}):=(\gamma_{1}\cdot x_{1},\gamma_{2}\cdot x_{2})
.
To see this, first note that
H_{x,y,\beta}
for each
\beta<\alpha

provided there exists
\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
the set

H_{x,y,\beta}(\eta)~=~\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma~:~d(\gamma(x),y)~\leq~e^{-\alpha\rho(\gamma)+\eta}\right\}

is infinite. Indeed, suppose
\gamma_{n}
is an infinite
sequence that satisfies

d(\gamma_{n}x,y)\leq e^{-\alpha\rho(\gamma_{n})+\eta}
.
Since each sublevel set of
\rho
is
finite, we have
\rho(\gamma_{n})\to\infty
.
Thus, if
\beta<\alpha
, then for all sufficiently large
n
we have

-\alpha\rho(\gamma_{n})+\eta<-\beta\rho(\gamma_{n})
.
It follows that
H_{x,y,\beta}
is infinite.

Fix
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
and suppose that
H_{x,y,\alpha}
is infinite.
We claim that there exists
\eta
so that

H_{\gamma_{1}x,\gamma_{2}y,\alpha}(\eta)
is infinite.
Indeed, suppose that

\gamma\in H_{x,y,\alpha}
. Set
\delta_{\gamma}=\gamma_{2}\gamma\gamma_{1}^{-1}
.
By the hypothesis on
\rho
we find that

\rho(\gamma)\geq\rho(\delta_{\gamma})-C
where

C=\rho(\gamma_{2})+\rho(\gamma_{1}^{-1})+2C_{\rho}
.
Thus, if
C^{\prime}
is the Lipschitz constant for
\gamma_{2}
, then

d(\delta_{\gamma}\gamma_{1}x,\gamma_{2}y)~=~d(\gamma_{2}\gamma x,\gamma_{2}y)~\leq~C^{\prime}\,d(\gamma x,y)~\leq~C^{\prime}\,e^{-\alpha\rho(\gamma)}~\leq~e^{-\alpha\rho(\delta_{\gamma})+\alpha C+\ln(C^{\prime})}.

The claim follows with
\eta=\alpha C+\ln(C^{\prime})
.

It then follows from the first paragraph of the proof that

H_{\gamma_{1}x,\gamma_{2}y,\beta}
is infinite for each
\beta<\alpha
.
Therefore, we have
\overline{\alpha}(\gamma_{1}x,\gamma_{2}y)\geq\overline{\alpha}(x,y)
.
A symmetric argument proves the reverse inequality.
Hence
\overline{\alpha}
is
\Gamma\times\Gamma
-invariant.

Since the
\Gamma
acts ergodically on
(X,\mu)
, the action
of
\Gamma\times\Gamma
on
(X\times X,\mu\otimes\mu)
is ergodic.
Hence
\overline{\alpha}
is constant on a set of full measure.
∎

3.
Holomorphic 1-forms and affine diffeomorphisms

Let
X
be a smooth, orientable, connected (real) surface without boundary.
A complex structure
\mu
on
X
is an equivalence class of charts

\{\chi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\to{\mathbb{C}}\}
such that each transition function

\chi_{\alpha}\circ\chi_{\beta}^{-1}
is holomorphic.
Let
\omega
be a complex-valued 1-form that is holomorphic with respect to
\mu
.
Suppose that
\omega
does not vanish at some
z_{\alpha}\in X
, then there
exists a neighborhood
V_{\alpha}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
so that

(5)

\nu_{\alpha}(z)~=~\int_{z_{\alpha}}^{z}\omega

defines a biholomorphism from
V_{\alpha}
onto an open set in
{\mathbb{C}}
. In other words,

\nu_{\alpha}^{*}(dz)=\omega
. Thus, the collection
\{\nu_{\alpha}:V_{\alpha}\to{\mathbb{C}}\}

is an atlas for
X\setminus\omega^{-1}(0)
whose transition functions
have the form
z\mapsto z+c
with
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
. That is,
\nu
is a

translation structure
on
X\setminus\omega^{-1}(0)
.
Integration of
|\omega|
defines arclength, and geodesics
correspond via
\nu_{\alpha}
to line segments in
{\mathbb{C}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Let
\Omega
denote the space of complex-valued 1-forms on
X
that are holomorphic with respect to
some
complex structure on
X
.
Let
{\rm Diff}^{+}
denote the group of orientation preserving
self-diffeomorphisms
\phi:X\to X
.
The group
{\rm Diff}^{+}
acts on the right of
\Omega
via

\omega\cdot\phi~:=~\omega\circ d\phi~=~\phi^{*}(\omega).

Let
GL_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
denote the
2\times 2
matrices with positive determinant.
We have a natural left action of
GL_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
on
\Omega
arising from
the canonical identification of
{\mathbb{C}}
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
given by
(x+iy)\mapsto(x,y)
. (Here
i=\sqrt{-1}
).
To be explicit

\begin{pmatrix}g_{11}&g_{12}\\
g_{21}&g_{22}\end{pmatrix}\cdot(x+iy)~=~g_{11}x+g_{12}y~+~i(g_{21}x+g_{22}y)

This immediately leads to a left action of
GL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
on
\Omega
:
For each tangent vector
v
we define

(g\cdot\omega)(v)~:=~g\cdot(\omega(v)).

Definition 3.1
.

A diffeomorphism
\phi\in{\rm Diff}^{+}
is said to be
affine with respect to
\omega

iff there exists
g\in GL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
so that

(6)

\omega\cdot\phi~=~g\cdot\omega.

The set of such
\phi
is a subgroup of
{\rm Diff}^{+}
that will be denoted by
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
.

Note that
\phi
is affine iff
\nu_{\alpha}\circ\phi\circ\nu_{\beta}^{-1}
coincides with a (real)
affine map
g
of the plane
{\mathbb{C}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
where
\nu
is as in
(
5
). In particular,
\phi
is
affine iff it maps geodesics to geodesics.

Because the action of
GL_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
on
\Omega
is free, the matrix
g
in (
6
) is uniquely determined by
\phi
.
The unique element
g
is called the
differential of
\phi
and is denoted
D\phi
.
The map
\phi\mapsto D\phi
is a homomorphism from
{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}
to
GL_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Lemma 3.2

(See §2 of
[
Vch89
]
)
.

Suppose that
X
is compact and
\omega
has at least one zero.
Then

•

the kernel of
D
is a finite order subgroup,

•

the image of
D
is a discrete subgroup of
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

The image of

is called the
Veech group
and is denoted by

.

Proof.

Since
\omega
is holomorphic with at least one zero, the surface
X
has genus at least two.
If
\phi\in\ker(D)
, then
\phi

is conformal with respect to the complex structure that underlies
\omega
.
Since
X
is compact and
\omega^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
, the complex structure
\omega
has finitely many conformal automorphisms that preserve
\omega^{-1}(0)
.

To show discreteness, it suffices to show that if
\phi_{k}\in{\rm Aff}_{\omega}^{+}

is a sequence so that
D\phi_{k}\to{\rm Id}
,
then
D\phi_{k}={\rm Id}
for
all sufficiently large

.
Let
{\mathcal{T}}
denote the collection of all oriented triangulations of
X

such that each 1-cell is a

-geodesic and the set of 0-cells
equals
\omega^{-1}(0)
. The Delaunay triangulation is an example of such a triangulation
[
Th98
]
. Because
X
is a closed surface,
the number of 1-cells of

equals
3(|\omega^{-1}(0)|-\chi(X))

For each

, define

to be the maximum length of a

. One can show that the subcollection

of triangulations that minimize

is finite.

Note that if
\phi\in{\rm Aff}^{+}_{\omega}

\phi(T)\in{\mathcal{T}}
and moreover
|\phi(T)|\leq\|D\phi\|\cdot|T|

where
\|D\phi\|
is the operator norm.
We claim that for each sufficiently large

, the map

\phi_{k}
preserves
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
. Indeed, because
X
is closed
and
\omega^{-1}(0)
is finite, one can show that the set of

\omega^{-1}(0)
—
saddle

—is discrete, and so the image of
of
T\to|T|
is discrete.
We have
|\phi_{k}(T)|\leq\|D\phi_{k}\|\cdot|T|
and hence
for each sufficiently large

, we have that
\phi_{k}(T)

lies in
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
.

Because
{\mathcal{T}}_{0}
is finite, there exists

so that for each

\phi_{k}^{n}(T)=T

for each oriented triangulation

.
Thus, because
\phi_{k}^{n}
is affine,
we find that

, and hence

.
Thus, each

is conjugate to a rotation matrix of order

, and hence the traces of the

form a discrete set. Because

and the identity in

is the only rotation

for all sufficiently large

.

Finally, we show that

for each

.
A straightforward computation gives

\phi^{*}(\omega\wedge\overline{\omega})~=~\phi^{*}(\omega)\wedge\overline{\phi^{*}(\omega)}~=~(D\phi\cdot\omega)\wedge\overline{D\phi\cdot\omega}~=~\det(D\phi)\cdot\omega\wedge\overline{\omega},

Hence

and the image lies in

.
∎

In the next section we will show that if

, then the methods of §

apply to the shrinking target problem for the

.
We will need the following.

It follows from (

5.
The spectral gap of Avila and Gouëzel

In this section we recall the main result of
[
AvlGzl13
]
and explain why it applies to our situation. Their result concerns the ‘left regular representation’
\pi

of
G=SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
on
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\alpha),\mu)
where
\mu
is an
ergodic
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant measure. To be concrete,
\pi
is defined by

\pi(g)u([\omega,\vec{x}])~=~u(g^{-1}\cdot[\omega,\vec{x}])

where we have abbreviated
(\omega,\vec{x})\,{\rm Diff}^{+}
with
[\omega,\vec{x}]
.
Because
\mu
is
G
-invariant, the representation
\pi
is unitary.
Thus, the representation
\pi
decomposes into irreducible unitary representations.

The spherical dual of
G
consists of isomorphism classes of irreducible
unitary representations of
G
that have a vector that is fixed by the
maximal compact subgroup
K=SO(2)
. The spherical dual of
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})

consists of the trivial representation, the spherical principal series, and the complementary series.

There are several explicit models of the spherical principal and complementary series.
These are usually described using a parameter ‘
s
’, but unfortunately the meaning
of ‘
s
’ depends on the model. In order to fix the meaning of ‘
s
’ we choose
the following explicit models given in §3.1 and §3.2 of
[
GGP
]
.

For each
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
, we let
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{s}

denote the space of smooth functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

•

f(-x)=f(x)

•

f(t\cdot x)=t^{s-1}\cdot f(x)
.

On
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{s}
define the following the inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{s}~=~\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{|x|=1}u(x)\cdot\overline{v}(x)\,d\sigma(x)&\mbox{ if }{\rm Re}(s)=0\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\int_{|x|=1}\displaystyle\int_{|y|=1}u(x)\cdot\overline{v}(y)\,K_{s}(x,y)\,d\sigma(y)\,d\sigma(x)&\mbox{ if }0<s<1,\end{array}\right.

where
d\sigma(x)
is arclength measure on the circle
|x|=1
and

K_{s}(x,y)~:=~|x_{1}y_{2}-y_{1}x_{2}|^{-s-1}.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{+}_{s}
denote the completion of
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{s}

with respect to the associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{s}
.
The group
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
acts on the left of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by matrix multiplication.
Define the representation
\pi_{s}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{s}^{+}
by

\pi_{s}(g)u(x)~=~u(g^{-1}\cdot x).

If
{\rm Re}(s)=0
, then
\pi_{s}
is called
a
spherical principal series representation
.
If
0<s<1
, then
\pi_{s}
is called a
complementary series representation
.
One should regard
\pi_{1}
as the trivial representation.

The theorem of Avila and Gouëzel provides a ‘gap’ between the trivial
representation and the other spherical irreducible representations that appear in
\pi
.

Theorem 5.1

(compare Theorem 2.3
[
AvlGzl13
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be an ergodic
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant measure on
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\alpha)
and let
\pi
be the associated unitary
representation of
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
on
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\alpha),d\mu)
.
Then the supremum of
s\in[0,1)

such that
\pi_{s}
appears in the decomposition of
\pi

is strictly less than 1.

Several remarks should be made.

First, Theorem
5.1
was first proven in
[
AGY06
]
in the special case
that
\mu
lies in the natural Lebesgue measure class of a given connected
component of a stratum.

Second, the statement of Theorem 2.3 in
[
AvlGzl13
]
concerns so-called ‘admissible
measures’
\tilde{\mu}
on the orbifold universal cover of
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\alpha)
.
But as the authors point out just after the definition of admissible measure,
a result of Eskin and Mirzakhani
[
EskMrz18
]
shows that each ergodic
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant
measure is admissible.

Third, the conclusion of Theorem 2.3
[
AvlGzl13
]

is stronger: The set of
s\in(0,1)
such that
\pi_{s}
appears in
\pi

is discrete and
0
is the only possible accumulation point in
[0,1]
.
But we will only be concerned with the spectral gap.

Fourth, Avila and Gouëzel do not consider strata of 1-forms marked at fake zeros. However, the proof applies with essentially no modifications. For example, an important input is Lemma 7.5 in
[
EskMsr01
]

which shows that the averages of the Margulis function associated to
‘admissible subcomplexes’ (of fixed complexity) decay exponentially. To obtain this lemma
in the case of fake zeros, one need only include fake zeros in the exposition
of §6 of
[
EskMsr01
]
. In particular, a ‘complex’
should now be defined to be a closed subset whose boundary consists of saddle connections
including

the saddle connections that have endpoints at the fake zeros. All of the subsequent proofs go through mutatis mutandis.

Recall the measure space
(Y,\nu)
and the
map
\Phi:Y\to{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\alpha)
and described in §
4
. The following proposition explains why Theorem
5.1
is applicable to our context.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma_{\omega}
is a lattice in
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.
The measure
\Phi_{*}(\nu)
is an ergodic
SL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
-invariant measure.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the results of
[
EskMrz18
]
and
[
EMM15
]
.
By Lemma
4.2
above, the set
\Phi(Y)
is
closed and
G
-invariant. Because

is connected, so is
\Phi(Y)

Theorem 2.2 in
[
EMM15
]
implies that
\Phi(Y)
is an ‘affine invariant

[
EMM15
]
.

We claim that
\Phi_{*}(\nu)
lies in the Lebesgue measure class of the affine invariant manifold

.
Indeed, recall that

where

Theorem 6.4

Let

Suppose that

\pi
of

by setting

(13)

(\pi(g)u)(y)~:=~u(g^{-1}\cdot y).

Define

g_{a}~=~\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{array}\right).

Recall the polar decomposition: Each

can be written
as

where

and
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
.
For each

, define

(14)

B_{r}~:=~\left\{k_{1}\,g_{a}\,k_{2}\,:\,k_{1},k_{2}\in K\mbox{ and }1\leq a\leq r\right\}.

Recall from §

the description of the irreducible spherical representations

\pi_{s}
of

.
The following is well-known. See, for example,
[
Nev94
]
and
[
AABBDGHJLMOSS
]
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
\sigma
be the supremum of the
{\rm Re}(s)
such that the
nontrivial representation
\pi_{s}
appears in the decomposition of
\pi
.
There exists

so that

\left\|\frac{1}{m(B_{r})}\int_{B_{r}}u(g^{-1}\cdot x)\,dm(g)~-~\frac{1}{\nu(Y)}\int_{Y}u(y)\,d\nu(y)\right\|~\leq~C\,\|u\|\,\log(r)\cdot r^{\sigma-1}.

where the supremum is taken over the

such that
\pi_{s}
appears in the decomposition of

.

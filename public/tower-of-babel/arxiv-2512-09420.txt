Factorisability in K-theory
Jørgen Vold Rennemo

Abstract
. We revisit and give a detailed proof of a lemma of Okounkov showing that, for a scheme X X with a torus action, the Euler characteristic generating function associated with a “factorisable” sequence of torus-equivariant coherent sheaves on the symmetric powers Sym n ⁡ X \Sym^{n}X equals the plethystic exponential of the generating function of Euler characteristics of some sequence of sheaves on X X .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14C35 (primary); 14C05, 14L30, 14N99 (secondary)

Let
X
be a complex, quasi-projective scheme equipped with an action of a torus
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
, and let
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence where each
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is a coherent,
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant, cohomologically
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded sheaf on
\Sym^{n}X
.
We assume that
X^{\mathbb{T}}
is proper, and so by localisation we may define
\chi(\Sym^{n}X,\mathcal{F}_{n})
as a rational function in the coordinates of
\mathbb{T}
(
[
5
, Thm. 2.2.]
, see also
[
3
, 2.3.10]
).

Consider the addition morphism

\displaystyle\sigma_{m,n}\colon\Sym^{m}(X)\times\Sym^{n}(X)

\displaystyle\to\Sym^{m+n}(X)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\right).

Let
U_{m,n}\subseteq\Sym^{m}(X)\times\Sym^{n}(X)
be the open subset of pairs
(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i})
such that
x_{i}\not=y_{j}
for all
i,j
.
Following
[
3
, Sec. 5.3]
, we consider the notion of the sequence
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
being
factorisable
, which in particular means that

(1.1)

\sigma_{m,n}^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{m+n})|_{U_{m,n}}\cong(\mathcal{F}_{m}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{n})_{U_{m,n}}

as
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant sheaves for all
m,n
.
Given positive integers
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
with
n=\sum n_{i}
and distinct points
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in X
, then if
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is factorisable, at a point
y=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}x_{i}\in\Sym^{n}(X)
we have

\mathcal{F}_{n}|_{y}\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{F}_{n_{i}}|_{n_{i}x_{i}}.

The full definition of factorisability (Def.
5.1
) requires moreover that the isomorphisms (
1.1
) are compatible in two ways representing associativity and commutativity.

Easy examples of factorisable sequences include
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\mathcal{O}_{\Sym^{n}(X)}
, and

\mathcal{F}_{n}=R\pi_{*}(\oplus_{i=0}^{n}\wedge^{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{[n]}[i]),

where
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{[n]}
the tautological rank
n
bundle on
\Hilb^{n}(X)
, and
\pi\colon\Hilb^{n}X\to\Sym^{n}X
is the Hilbert–Chow morphism.
Harder examples of factorisable sequences include the direct images of virtual and twisted virtual structure sheaves on
\Hilb^{n}(X)
for a Calabi–Yau 3-fold
X
, i.e.
R\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Hilb^{n}(X)}^{\text{vir}}
and
R\pi_{*}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\Hilb^{n}(X)}^{\text{vir}}

[
3
,
4
]
, as well as the sheaves on
\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{4})
underlying the “Nekrasov genus”
[
2
]
.

Given a series
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{d},q)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})q^{i}
, where the
f_{i}
are formal Laurent series or rational functions in the
t_{i}
, define the plethystic exponential by

(1.2)

\Exp\left(f(t_{1},\dots,t_{d},q)\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{f(t_{1}^{n},\dots,t_{d}^{n},q^{n})}{n}\right).

The goal of this paper is to show:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a complex, quasi-projective,
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant scheme such that
X^{\mathbb{T}}
is proper, and let
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence where each
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant,
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded coherent sheaf on
\Sym^{n}X
.
If
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is factorisable in the sense of Definition
5.1
, then there exists a sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}
of
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant,
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded coherent sheaves on
X
such that

1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi(\Sym^{n}X,\mathcal{F}_{n})q^{n}=\Exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi(X,\mathcal{E}_{n})q^{n}\right).

This result (in a geometric form essentially equivalent to Proposition
7.1
) was stated by Okounkov as
[
3
, Lemma 5.3.4]
, though we found it hard to fill in the details in the proof given.
Our contribution here is to supply a complete and detailed proof of the statement, mainly with a view to applications in
[
2
]
, and to a generalised version of the proposition which is shown and used in
[
4
]
.

Note that it is always true that a series
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{d},q)
as on the left hand side of the proposition can be written as a plethystic exponential of some other formal series.
From an enumerative geometry perspective, the importance of Proposition
1.1
is that the coefficients
\chi(X,\mathcal{E}_{n})
of the series on the right hand side typically have a special form as a rational function in the
t_{i}
.
1
1

1

E.g. if
X=\mathbb{A}^{d}
with the standard
\mathbb{T}
-action, then
\chi(\mathbb{A}^{d},\mathcal{E}_{n})
is of the form
g\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-t_{i})^{-1}
, where
g
is a Laurent polynomial in the
t_{i}
.

Thus the proposition provides strong constraints on the series on the left hand side; this plays a key role in various computations of K-theoretic DT3 and DT4 invariants
[
2
,
4
,
3
]
.

1.1.
Sketch of proof

We now outline the proof of Proposition
1.1
, leaving all precise definitions and results to later sections.

Given a sequence of sheaves
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
, each
\mathcal{F}_{n}
living on
\Sym^{n}X
, we obtain a sequence of sheaves
F_{n}=p^{*}\mathcal{F}_{n}
on
[X^{n}/S_{n}]
by the morphism
p\colon[X^{n}/S_{n}]\to\Sym^{n}X
.
From the
F_{n}
, we can compute
\chi(\Sym^{n}X,\mathcal{F}_{n})=\chi(X^{n},F_{n})^{S_{n}}
.
Working
S_{n}
-equivariantly on
X^{n}
proves more convenient for our purposes than working with
\Sym^{n}X
, and in most of the paper we will work with a sequence
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
where
F_{n}
is a sheaf on
[X^{n}/S_{n}]
.

If
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{d},q)
is the character of a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}_{q}
-representation
V
, then the plethystic exponential
\Exp(f)
is the character of the infinite symmetric product of
V
.
In Section
2
, we study the analogous infinite symmetric product operation on sheaves on
\sqcup_{n=1}^{\infty}[X^{n}/S_{n}]
, which we call the sheaf-based plethystic exponential and denote by
\shExp
.
For a set
A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
, let
i_{A}\colon X^{n}\to X^{|A|}
be the natural projection
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{i})_{i\in A}
, and let
F_{A}=i_{A}^{*}F_{|A|}
.
For a set partition
\mathfrak{A}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\}
of
\{1,\dots,n\}
, we let
F_{\mathfrak{A}}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}F_{A_{i}}
.
We then define

\shExp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}q^{n}\right)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{A}}F_{\mathfrak{A}}\right)q^{n},

where the direct sum is over all set partitions
\mathfrak{A}
of
\{1,\dots,n\}
, and this direct sum is equipped with a natural
S_{n}
-equivariant structure to make it a sheaf on
[X^{n}/S_{n}]
.

The operation
\shExp
has a K-theoretic inverse denoted
\shLog
, defined order by order in
n
so that

\shLog\left(\shExp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}q^{n}\right)\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(F_{n}\oplus J_{n}\oplus J_{n}[1])q^{n}

for some sheaves
J_{n}
.
The operation
\shLog
is not an inverse to
\shExp
on the level of sequences of sheaves, but it is so at the level of K-theory.
The claim of Proposition
1.1
can then be reduced (see Section
7
) to Proposition
7.1
, which says that if
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
arises from a factorisable sequence on
\Sym^{n}X
, then

\shLog\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}q^{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{n}q^{n}

where each
G_{n}
is equal in K-theory to a sheaf supported on the small diagonal
\Delta\subseteq X^{n}
; equivalently, that
[G_{n}]|_{X^{n}\setminus\Delta}=0
.

In Section
3
, we pass from the abstract order-by-order definition of
\shLog
to an explicit description of the coefficients
G_{n}
as an sum of shifted copies of
F_{\mathfrak{A}}
, indexed by “index trees of order
n
”.

If the
F_{n}
come from a factorisable sequence of sheaves on
\Sym^{n}X
, then the sheaves
F_{\mathfrak{A}}
on
X^{n}
come equipped with partially defined isomorphisms between them: Given set partitions
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
of
\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\mathfrak{A}
is finer than
\mathfrak{B}
, then
F_{\mathfrak{A}}
is naturally isomorphic to
F_{\mathfrak{B}}
on the set

U_{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}\mid\text{ if }i\not\sim_{\mathfrak{A}}j\text{ and }i\sim_{\mathfrak{B}}j,\text{ then }x_{i}\not=x_{j}\}.

Furthermore these isomorphisms are compatible under compositions and with the natural
S_{n}
-actions.
In Section
4
, we abstract the properties of such collections of sheaves and partial isomorphisms into the notion of a “system of sheaves”.
The main result of this section is Corollary
4.10
, which says that every system of sheaves is equal in K-theory to a “strict” system, meaning one where the partial isomorphisms can be extended compatibly to global homomorphisms.

Applying this strictification, we replace the
F_{\mathfrak{A}}
with K-theoretically equivalent sheaves, where the strictness property gives that for all
\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}
with
\mathfrak{A}
finer than
\mathfrak{B}
we have homomorphisms
\phi_{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}\colon F_{\mathfrak{A}}\to F_{\mathfrak{B}}
restricting to isomorphisms on
U_{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}
.
After making the corresponding replacement to
G_{n}
, in Section
6
we then use these homomorphisms to put a differential
d
on
G_{n}
.
We furthermore show that the cohomology sheaf
H^{*}(G_{n},d)
vanishes on
X^{n}\setminus\Delta
, and so
[G_{n}]|_{X^{n}\setminus\Delta}=0
.
It follows that
[G_{n}]=[H_{n}]
, for some sheaf
H_{n}
supported on
\Delta
, which is what we wanted.

1.1.1.
The proof for
n=2,3

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a factorisable sequence of sheaves on
\Sym^{n}X
, which pulls back to a sequence
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
on
[X^{n}/S_{n}]
.
Let
G_{n}
be the coefficient of
q^{n}
in
\shLog(\sum F_{n}q^{n})
.
The central claim to show is Proposition
7.1
, that the sheaf
G_{n}
is equal in K-theory to a sheaf
H_{n}
pushed forth from the small diagonal
\Delta\subseteq X^{n}
.
As the general proof is technical, in this section we sketch how the proof goes for
n=2
and
3
, hoping to showcase the main ideas.

For
n=2
, we have
G_{2}=F_{2}\oplus F_{\{1\},\{2\}}[1]
, where
F_{\{1\},\{2\}}=F_{1}\boxtimes F_{1}
.
Let
U=X^{2}\setminus\Delta
, and let
j\colon U\to X^{2}
be the inclusion.
The factorisability condition on the
F_{i}
implies that there exists an
S_{2}
-invariant isomorphism

\phi\colon F_{\{1\},\{2\}}|_{U}\to F_{2}|_{U}.

Thus the class
[G_{2}]=[F_{2}]-[F_{\{1\},\{2\}}]
vanishes on
U
, and hence equals the class of some sheaf
H_{2}
supported on
\Delta
by the localisation sequence in K-theory of coherent sheaves (see e.g.
[
3
, 2.2.19]
for an explicit argument).

In the more complicated case of
n>2
, where
G_{n}
is a sum of multiple
F_{\mathfrak{A}}
which are identified on different open subsets of
X^{n}
, we are not able to use the localisation sequence in this way.
Our general proof of the vanishing of
[G_{n}]|_{U}
specialises inthe case of
n=2
as follows:
First, the strictification procedure of Section
4
replaces
F_{2}
and
F_{\{1\},\{2\}}
by K-theoretically equivalent sheaves.
We have homomorphisms

a\colon F_{\{1\},\{2\}}\to j_{*}j^{*}F_{\{1\},\{2\}}

b\colon F_{\{1\},\{2\}}\to j_{*}j^{*}F_{\{1\},\{2\}}\overset{j_{*}\phi}{\to}j_{*}j^{*}F_{2}

c\colon F_{2}\to j_{*}j^{*}F_{2}.

Setting

F_{\{1\},\{2\}}^{\prime}=\im a\oplus\ker a,

and

F_{2}^{\prime}=(\im b+\im c)\oplus\ker c\oplus(\im b+\im c)/\im c[1],

we have
[F_{\{1\},\{2\}}]=[F^{\prime}_{\{1\},\{2\}}]
and
[F_{2}]=[F^{\prime}_{2}]
.
Moreover, we have a homomorphism

\phi^{\prime}\colon F^{\prime}_{\{1\},\{2\}}\to\im a\overset{j_{*}j^{*}\phi}{\to}\im b\to\im b+\im c\to F^{\prime}_{2}.

It is easy to see that after restriction to
U
, we have
F^{\prime}_{\{1\},\{2\}}=F_{\{1\},\{2\}},F^{\prime}_{2}=F_{2}
, and
\phi^{\prime}=\phi
, in particular
\phi^{\prime}
is an isomorphism over
U
.
The complex
G_{2}^{\prime}=F^{\prime}_{\{1\},\{2\}}\overset{\phi^{\prime}}{\to}F^{\prime}_{2}
then has cohomology supported (set-theoretically) on
\Delta
, and so by devissage there exists an
H_{2}
supported on
\Delta
such that
[G_{2}]=[G^{\prime}_{2}]=[H_{2}]
.

For the case of
n=3
, we have (cf. Lemma
3.7
) that

G_{3}=F_{3}\oplus(F_{\{\{2,3\},\{1\}\}}\oplus F_{\{\{1,3\},\{2\}\}}\oplus F_{\{\{1,2\},\{3\}\}}\oplus F_{\{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}\}})[1]\oplus F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}}^{\oplus 3}[2]

Here
F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}}=F_{1}\boxtimes F_{1}\boxtimes F_{1}
, and
F_{\{i,j\},\{k\}}=\pi_{ij}^{*}F_{2}\otimes\pi_{k}^{*}F_{1}
, with
\pi_{ij}\colon X^{3}\to X^{2}
and
\pi_{k}\colon X^{3}\to X
denoting the projections to factors
i,j
and
k
, respectively.
The factorisability of the sequence
F_{n}
gives us isomorphisms
\phi_{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}
for any two set partitions
\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}
of
\{1,2,3\}
with
\mathfrak{B}
refining
\mathfrak{A}
.
Concretely, this gives partially defined isomorphisms

\phi_{k}\colon F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}}\to F_{\{i,j\},\{k\}}\text{ on the locus where }x_{i}\not=x_{j},

\psi_{k}\colon F_{\{i,j\},\{k\}}\to F_{3}\text{ on the locus where }x_{i},x_{j}\not=x_{k},

and

\omega\colon F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}}\to F_{3}\text{ on the locus where all the }x_{i}\text{ are distinct.}

Moreover

(1.3)

\omega=\psi_{1}\circ\phi_{1}=\psi_{2}\circ\phi_{2}=\psi_{3}\circ\phi_{3}

on the locus where all
x_{i}
are distinct.

The strictification procedure of Section
4
allows us to replace the
F_{\mathfrak{A}}
by sheaves with the same K-theory class, with the property that the partial isomorphisms
\phi_{k},\psi_{k},\omega
extend to homomorphisms on the whole of
X^{3}
in such a way that (
1.3
) holds globally.
After this replacement, we may endow
G_{3}
with a differential defining the complex

G_{3}=F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}}^{\oplus 3}\to F_{\{\{2,3\},\{1\}\}}\oplus F_{\{\{1,3\},\{2\}\}}\oplus F_{\{\{1,2\},\{3\}\}}\oplus F_{\{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}\}}\to F_{3},

Here the first differential is

\begin{pmatrix}-\phi_{1}&0&0\\
0&-\phi_{2}&0\\
0&0&-\phi_{3}\\
\id&\id&\id\end{pmatrix}

and the second is

\begin{pmatrix}-\psi_{1}&-\psi_{2}&-\psi_{3}&-\omega\end{pmatrix}.

If
\bar{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in X^{3}\setminus\Delta
, then without loss of generality we may assume that
x_{1}\not=x_{2},x_{3}
, and so
\psi_{1},\phi_{2}
and
\phi_{3}
are all isomorphisms at
\bar{x}
.
The complex
G_{3}
admits a filtration (cf. Section
6.1
) with the following subquotients

\displaystyle F_{\{2,3\},\{1\}}\overset{-\psi_{1}}{\to}F_{3}

\displaystyle F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}}\overset{-\phi_{2}}{\to}F_{\{1,3\},\{2\}}

\displaystyle F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}}\overset{-\phi_{3}}{\to}F_{\{1,2\},\{3\}}

\displaystyle F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}}\overset{\id}{\to}F_{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\}},

all of which are acyclic at
\bar{x}
.
As
\bar{x}
was chosen arbitrarily, it follows that
G_{3}
is acyclic on
X^{3}\setminus\Delta
.
As the cohomology of
G_{3}
is supported set-theoretically on
\Delta
, it follows by devissage that
[G_{3}]=[H_{3}]
for some sheaf
H_{3}
on
\Delta
.

Remark 1.2
.

The main difficulties in the proof arise from the fact that K-theory classes of coherent sheaves are not “stratifiable”, in the sense that for a sheaf
F
on a scheme
X
with open subset
U
, there is no way to reconstruct
[F]
from
[F|_{U}]
and
[F|_{X\setminus U}]
.

By contrast, if
F
is a constructible sheaf,
j\colon U\to X
and
i\colon X\setminus U\to X
the inclusions, then we have
[F]=j_{*}[F|_{U}]+i_{!}[F|_{X\setminus U}]
.
The statement analogous to Proposition
7.1
for constructible sheaves can therefore be proved more simply by stratifying
X^{n}
by the
S_{n}
-orbits of its diagonals and then checking that the restriction of
\shExp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}q^{n}\right)
to each stratum except for the small diagonal vanishes.

1.3.
Conventions

We let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

The power set
2^{[n]}
is equipped with the “binary” total order such that for
A,B\in 2^{[n]}
, we have
A\leq B
if and only if

\sum_{i\in A}2^{i}\leq\sum_{i\in B}2^{i},

so in particular
A\subseteq B
implies
A\leq B
.

A set partition of a set
S
is a set
\mathfrak{A}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
, where the
A_{i}
are disjoint subsets of
S
whose union is
S
.
Given a set partition
\mathfrak{A}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}
of
[n]
, we write
\underline{\mathfrak{A}}
for the partition of
n
given by the multiset
\{|A_{i}|\}_{i=1}^{k}
.

Given a series expression
f(q)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}q^{n}
, we let
\langle q^{n}\rangle(f(q))=a_{n}
.

All group actions are on the left.

2.
Plethystic exponentials and logarithms

Let
X
be a complex, quasi-projective scheme equipped with an action of a torus
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}
.
We let
(F_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence where
F_{n}
is a coherent,
\mathbb{T}\times S_{n}
-equivariant, cohomologically
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded sheaf on
X^{n}
.
For the remainder of the paper, we will write “sheaf” for a coherent
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded sheaf, and so for instance say that
F_{n}
is a sheaf on
[X^{n}/S_{n}]
or a
S_{n}
-equivariant sheaf on
X^{n}
.

For any finite set
A
, let
X^{A}
be the direct product of copies of
X
, indexed by
A
.
By definition,
X^{n}=X^{[n]}
.
The assignment
A\mapsto X^{A}
defines a contravariant functor from finite sets to schemes.
Given a map of sets
u\colon A\to B
, we will write
u_{X}\colon X^{B}\to X^{A}
for the associated morphism of schemes.
The group
S_{n}
acts on
X^{n}
by sending
\sigma\in S_{n}
to
\sigma_{X}^{-1}
, so that

\sigma(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}).

Let
A
be a subset of
[n]
.
There is a unique order preserving inclusion
i_{A}\colon[|A|]\to[n]
with image
A
, giving rise to a morphism
i_{A,X}\colon X^{n}\to X^{|A|}
, and we define

F_{A}=i_{A,X}^{*}F_{|A|}.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
.
There is a unique
\tau\in S_{|A|}
such that

\sigma^{-1}\circ i_{A}=i_{\sigma^{-1}(A)}\circ\tau^{-1},

and so

i_{A,X}\circ\sigma_{X}^{-1}=\tau_{X}^{-1}\circ i_{\sigma^{-1}(A),X}

Since
F_{|A|}
is
S_{|A|}
-equivariant, there is a given isomorphism

\rho_{|A|,\tau}\colon(\tau^{-1}_{X})^{*}F_{|A|}\cong F_{|A|},

which gives rise to an isomorphism

\displaystyle\rho_{A,\sigma}\colon(\sigma_{X}^{-1})^{*}F_{A}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{X}^{-1})^{*}i_{A,X}^{*}F_{|A|}

\displaystyle=i_{\sigma^{-1}(A),X}^{*}(\tau_{X}^{-1})^{*}F_{|A|}\overset{\rho_{|A|,\tau}}{\to}i_{\sigma^{-1}(A),X}^{*}F_{|A|}=F_{\sigma^{-1}(A)}.

Let

\mathfrak{A}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}\}

be a set partition of
[n]
, and assume that the
A_{i}
are lexicographically ordered with
A_{1}<\dots<A_{l}
.
We define

F_{\mathfrak{A}}=F_{A_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{A_{l}}.

Using the isomorphisms
\rho_{A_{i},\sigma}
and the commutativity of the tensor product, we get an isomorphism

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{A},\sigma}\colon(\sigma^{-1}_{X})^{*}F_{\mathfrak{A}}

\displaystyle\cong(\sigma^{-1}_{X})^{*}(F_{A_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes(\sigma^{-1}_{X})^{*}(F_{A_{l}})\cong F_{\sigma^{-1}(A_{1})}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{\sigma^{-1}(A_{l})}

\displaystyle\cong F_{\sigma^{-1}(A_{i_{1}})}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{\sigma^{-1}(A_{i_{l}})}=F_{\sigma^{-1}(\mathfrak{A})},

where
i_{1},\ldots,i_{l}
are such that the

4.3.

Definition 4.5

A

More concisely, thinking of the poset

whose objects are strict systems, and there is an exact functor

\Psi(F_{i},\phi_{i,j})=(F_{i},\phi_{i,j}|_{U_{i,j}}).

4.4.
The functor
D_{\alpha}

We would like to show that in

-theory, the functor

The idea is to produce, for any system

A first approximation to the construction of

Given a system of sheaves

\phi^{\prime}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}(j_{\alpha})_{*}j_{\alpha}^{*}\phi_{i,j}\text{ if }i\sim_{\alpha}j\\

This almost defines a strict system, the only problem being that

may fail to be a coherent sheaf, since

is not necessarily closed in

We will modify the above construction to give a valid functor

Roughly speaking,

defined above for which the morphism

factors through
\Psi(D_{\alpha}(F,\phi))
.

Proposition

therefore follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 6.11
.

For every partition

of

Proof.

In Lemma

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
(S,\leq)
be a totally ordered set, and let
\psi\colon\mathcal{T}_{n}\to S
be a map with the property that for every contraction
T\to T^{\prime}
, we have
\psi(T)\geq\psi(T^{\prime})
.

Then there exists a filtration of the complex
G_{n}
where every subquotient is of the form

\bigoplus_{T\in\psi^{-1}(s)}F_{T}

for some
s\in S
, with differential given by the sum of all differentials
F_{T}\to F_{T^{\prime}}
for contractions
T\to T^{\prime}
with
T,T^{\prime}\in\psi^{-1}(s)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{T}_{n}
is finite, we can map
\psi(\mathcal{T}_{n})\subseteq S
into
\mathbb{Z}
in an order preserving way, and so we may assume that
S=\mathbb{Z}
with the standard ordering.
For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, define a subcomplex
G_{n}^{i}\subseteq G_{n}
by

\bigoplus_{T\in\psi^{-1}(-\infty,i]}F_{T}\subseteq\bigoplus_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}F_{T},

where the condition that any contraction
T\to T^{\prime}
gives
\psi(T)\geq\psi(T^{\prime})
implies that
d_{n}(G^{i}_{n})\subseteq G^{i}_{n}
.
The subquotients
G^{i}_{n}/G^{i-1}_{n}
then have the form claimed.
∎

Let
\leq^{\prime}
be the total order on
2^{[n]}
which is opposite to the binary total order, so that in particular
A\subseteq B\Rightarrow B\leq^{\prime}A
.
Let

S=2^{[n]}\times\mathbb{N}^{4},

equipped with the lexicographical total ordering, where
2^{[n]}
is ordered by
\leq^{\prime}
and the
\mathbb{N}
-factors in the usual way.
Define a map

\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4},\psi_{5})\colon\mathcal{T}_{n}\to S

as follows.

Let
T
be an index tree, and let

Q(T)=\{L_{v}\mid v\text{ a node of }T,L_{v}\not\subseteq B_{1},L_{v}\not\subseteq B_{2}\}

If
r
is the root node of
T
, then
L_{r}=[n]\in Q(T)
, so
Q(T)
is nonempty.

We let
d(T)
be the node such that
L_{d(T)}\in Q(T)
and this is the maximal element of
Q(T)
with respect to
\leq^{\prime}
.
If
v\succ d(T)
, we then have

v\succ d(T)\Rightarrow L_{v}\subsetneq L_{d(T)}\Rightarrow L_{v}>^{\prime}L_{d(T)}\Rightarrow L_{v}\not\in Q(T),

so that either
L_{v}\subseteq B_{1}\cap L_{d(T)}
or
L_{v}\subseteq B_{2}\cap L_{d(T)}
.

We now define
\psi
by

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(T)=L_{d(T)}

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(T)=|\{v\text{ a node of }T\mid v\not\succeq d(T)\}|

\displaystyle\psi_{3}(T)=|\{v\succ d(T)\mid L_{v}\subsetneq B_{1}\cap L_{d(T)}\}|

\displaystyle\psi_{4}(T)=\psi_{3}(T)\times|\{v\succ d(T)\mid L_{v}\subseteq B_{2}\cap L_{d(T)}\}|

\displaystyle\psi_{5}(T)=|\{v\succ d(T)\mid L_{v}\subsetneq B_{2}\cap L_{d(T)}\}|

Lemma 6.13
.

If
T^{\prime}
is a contraction of
T
, then
\psi(T)\geq\psi(T^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We have
Q(T^{\prime})\subseteq Q(T)
, which gives
\psi_{1}(T)\geq^{\prime}\psi_{1}(T^{\prime})
.
Assume
\psi_{1}(T)=\psi_{1}(T^{\prime})
, so that
L_{d(T)}=L_{d(T^{\prime})}
.

If the contraction is at a node
v\not\succeq d(T)
, then
\psi_{2}(T)>\psi_{2}(T^{\prime})
.
If the contraction is at
d(T)
, then the fact that
L_{d(T)}=L_{d(T^{\prime})}
implies the contraction must be exceptional, in which case
we have
\psi_{2}(T)=\psi_{2}(T^{\prime})
.
If the contraction is at some node
v\succ d(T)
, we also get
\psi_{2}(T)=\psi_{2}(T^{\prime})
.

Assume now that
\psi_{2}(T)=\psi_{2}(T^{\prime})
.
If the contraction is exceptional at
d(T)
, we have

\psi_{3}(T^{\prime})=\psi_{4}(T^{\prime})=\psi_{5}(T^{\prime})=0,

so
\psi(T)\geq\psi(T^{\prime})
.
If the contraction happens at
v\succ d(T)
, then we get

\{L_{v}\mid v\succ d(T)\}\supsetneq\{L_{v^{\prime}}\mid v^{\prime}\succ d(T^{\prime})\},

from which we get

\psi_{i}(T)\geq\psi_{i}(T^{\prime})\ \ \ \ \ i=3,4,5.

∎

Let us say that a contraction
T\to T^{\prime}

preserves
\psi
if
\psi(T)=\psi(T^{\prime})
.

Lemma 6.14
.

For every index tree
T
of order
n
, there is exactly one
\psi
-preserving contraction with
T
as source or target.

If the contraction
T\to T^{\prime}
preserves
\psi
, then the homomorphism
\phi_{T,T^{\prime}}\colon F_{T}\to F_{T^{\prime}}
is an isomorphism over
U_{\mathfrak{B}}
.

Proof.

If a contraction preserves
\psi
, then
\psi_{1}(T)=\psi_{1}(T^{\prime})
and
\psi_{2}(T)=\psi_{2}(T^{\prime})
imply that either
(i)
the contraction is exceptional at
d(T)
, or
(ii)
happens at a node
v\succ d(T)
.
The equalities
\psi_{3}(T)=\psi_{3}(T^{\prime})
and
\psi_{5}(T)=\psi_{5}(T^{\prime})
imply that in case
(i)
, there are exactly two descendants
v_{1},v_{2}
of
d(T)
, and these satisfy
L_{v_{i}}=B_{i}\cap L_{d(T)}
.
In case
(ii)
, the same equalities give that the contraction must be ordinary at a node
v
with
L_{v}=B_{i}\cap L_{d(T)}
for
i=1
or
2
.

Let us now say a contraction
T\to T^{\prime}
is of type
P
if it is exceptional at
d(T)
, and
d(T)
has precisely two descendants
v_{1},v_{2}
with
L_{v_{i}}=B_{i}\cap L_{d(T)}
.
We say it is of type

(resp.
Q2
) if it is ordinary at a node

with

(resp.
L_{v}=B_{2}\cap L_{d(T)}
).
The previous paragraph shows that every contraction such that

must be of type

or
Q2
.

For a tree
T
, we either have that

is a leaf, or else one of the following five subcases.

(1)

There is no node
v_{1}
such that
L_{v_{1}}=B_{1}\cap L_{d(T)}
.

(2)

There is a node
v_{1}
such that
L_{v_{1}}=B_{1}\cap L_{d(T)}
, and:

(a)

v_{1}
is not a leaf.

(b)

v_{1}
is a leaf, and:

(i)

There is no node
v_{2}
such that
L_{v_{2}}=B_{2}\cap L_{d(T)}
.

(ii)

There is a node
v_{2}
such that
L_{v_{2}}=B_{2}\cap L_{d(T)}
, and:

(A)

v_{2}
is not a leaf.

(B)

v_{2}
is a leaf.

We now analyse the possible contractions of type
P,Q1,Q2
with source or target
T
for each of these cases, and determine which satisfy
\psi(T)=\psi(T^{\prime})
.

(
d(T)
is a leaf)

There is no contraction of type
P
,
Q1
or
Q2
from

.
There are no contractions to
T
of type
Q1
or
Q2
.
There is a unique contraction
T^{\prime}\to T
of type

, where

is obtained by adding two nodes

to

such that
L_{v_{i}}=B_{i}\cap L_{d(T)}
.
This contraction preserves
\psi
.

(1)

There is no contraction from
T
of type
P
or
Q1
.
There may be a contraction of type

.
Since

is the union of

over all

.
Therefore

, and so
\psi_{4}
decreases under a contraction of type

and it is not

-preserving.

There is no contraction to

of type

.
There is a unique contraction to

of type

, and

is preserved under this contraction.
There may be a contraction to
T
of type
Q2
, but as above

decreases under this contraction.

(2a)

There is no contraction from

of type

. There is a contraction from

of type

, and possibly one of type
Q2
.
The contraction of type

preserves
\psi
, but the one of type

does not, because

There is no contraction to

of type

.
There may be a contraction to

of type

, but as above

(2bi)

There is no contraction from

or

.

(2biiA)

Corollary 6.15
.

Every subquotient of the filtration of
G_{n}
associated with
\psi
is acyclic on
U_{\mathfrak{B}}
.

Proof.

Each subquotient of
G_{n}
from the filtration defined by
\psi
is of the form

\bigoplus_{T\in\psi^{-1}(x)}F_{T}[k_{T}],

for some
x\in S
, where
k_{T}
is the number of non-leaf nodes of
T
.
By Lemma
6.14
, if
C
is the set of contractions
T\to T^{\prime}
with
\psi(T)=\psi(T^{\prime})=x
, this equals

\bigoplus_{(T\to T^{\prime})\in C}(F_{T}[k_{T}]\oplus F_{T^{\prime}}[k_{T}-1]),

and the differential is the sum of all
\phi_{T,T^{\prime}}
.
By Lemma
6.14
again, these are isomorphisms over
U_{\mathfrak{B}}
, and so the complex is acyclic.
∎

Comment on “Regular magnetically charged black holes from nonlinear electrodynamics: Thermodynamics, light deflection, and orbital dynamics” by Aydiner, Sucu and Sakalli
Zhuang Li

Abstract
We analyze the recent article by Aydiner, Sucu, and Sakalli [Phys. Dark Univ. 50 , 102164 (2025)], which investigates the thermodynamics, tunneling kinetics, and weak-lensing signatures of a regular, magnetically charged nonlinear-electrodynamics black hole. While the study addresses interesting phenomenological questions, we identify several inconsistencies that compromise the validity of the reported results. Specifically, we note mutually contradictory values for the extremal charge separating the black hole and horizonless regimes, an inaccurate characterization of the Schwarzschild limit as an extremal configuration, a vacuum weak-deflection expansion that fails to recover the standard second-order Schwarzschild coefficient, and a sign error in the generalized uncertainty principle (GUP) corrected tunneling probability. We also highlight terminology regarding the nature of the horizonless solution and the physical interpretation of plasma parameters that requires correction. We clarify these points to ensure the robustness of the model’s asymptotic limits.



I
Introduction

In a recent publication, Aydiner, Sucu, and Sakalli
ASS
(hereafter ASS) presented a comprehensive analysis of a regular black hole solution arising from nonlinear electrodynamics (NED). Their study covers horizon structure, GUP-modified thermodynamics via tunneling, weak gravitational lensing using the Gauss-Bonnet theorem (GBT), and particle dynamics. Given that such analytic solutions often serve as testbeds for modified gravity and NED theories, it is imperative that the derivation of observables remains internally consistent and recovers established general relativity (GR) results in the appropriate limits. Upon detailed examination of Ref.
ASS
, we have identified specific discrepancies in the definition of extremality, the calculation of deflection angles, and the thermodynamic tunneling formulation. This Comment details these issues and provides the necessary corrections to restore consistency with standard results.

II
Horizon Structure and Extremality Conditions

II.1
Inconsistency in Extremal Charge Values

The transition from a two-horizon black hole spacetime to a horizonless configuration is governed by the extremal charge parameter,
q_{\rm ext}
. For a static, spherically symmetric metric function
f(r;q)
, extremality is defined by the simultaneous vanishing of the function and its derivative:

f(r_{\rm ext};q_{\rm ext})=0,\qquad\left.\frac{\partial f(r;q)}{\partial r}\right|_{(r_{\rm ext},q_{\rm ext})}=0.

(1)

There is a numerical inconsistency in Ref.
ASS
regarding this value. The abstract states
q_{\rm ext}\simeq 2.54\,M
, whereas Table I and the associated textual analysis suggest a transition occurs near
q\simeq 3.79\,M
. This discrepancy is too large to be attributed to numerical rounding and suggests either a normalization error or the use of differing metric functions across sections. A definitive re-evaluation of Eq. (
1
) is required to fix the parameter space for validity.

II.2
Characterization of the Schwarzschild Limit

Ref.
ASS
contains phrasing suggesting that the Schwarzschild limit corresponds to an “extremal” case
. In standard black hole thermodynamics, extremality refers to a zero-temperature, degenerate horizon state. The Schwarzschild metric,

f_{\rm Schw}(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r},

(2)

possesses a simple root at
r=2M
where the surface gravity (and temperature) is non-zero (
f^{\prime}(2M)\neq 0
). Consequently, Schwarzschild is strictly non-extremal. This distinction is non-trivial when discussing thermodynamic stability and phase transitions.

II.3
Regularity vs. Naked Singularities

The spacetime in question is derived within the context of
regular
black holes, defined by the finiteness of curvature invariants (e.g., the Kretschmann scalar
K=R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
) everywhere, including the origin. Ref.
ASS
characterizes the horizonless regime (
q>q_{\rm ext}
) as containing a “naked singularity.” This terminology is contradictory in the context of regular metrics. If the spacetime is regular, the absence of an event horizon implies a regular horizonless compact object (soliton), not a naked singularity. If the authors intend to claim a singularity exists, they must demonstrate the divergence of curvature invariants as
r\to 0
in the
q>q_{\rm ext}
regime.

III
Weak Deflection Angle: The Second-Order Limit

The application of the Gauss-Bonnet theorem to calculate weak deflection angles is a powerful tool, but its validity must be checked against the Schwarzschild limit in vacuum. The standard expansion for the deflection angle
\hat{\alpha}
in terms of the impact parameter
b
and mass
M
is given by the Epstein-Shapiro expansion
EpsteinShapiro1980
;
RichterMatzner1982
;
KeetonPetters2005
:

\hat{\alpha}_{\rm Schw}(b)\simeq\frac{4M}{b}+\frac{15\pi}{4}\frac{M^{2}}{b^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{M^{3}}{b^{3}}\right).

(3)

The expansion presented in Ref.
ASS
appears to yield a second-order term proportional to
\frac{3\pi}{4}\frac{M^{2}}{b^{2}}
in the vacuum limit (
q\to 0
).

This factor-of-five discrepancy at
\mathcal{O}(M^{2}/b^{2})
implies a calculation error. Common sources for such mismatches in GBT applications include the use of a zeroth-order straight-line integration path rather than the required iterative correction for second-order precision, an incomplete evaluation of the Gaussian curvature over the optical manifold, or the neglect of boundary terms if the source and receiver are at finite distances. The results in Ref.
ASS
should be recalculated to ensure the coefficient converges to
15\pi/4
when the NED charge vanishes.

IV
Tunneling Probability and GUP Sign Conventions

In the Hamilton-Jacobi tunneling formalism, the emission probability
\Gamma
is related to the imaginary part of the action
S
by

\Gamma\propto\exp\left(-2\,\mathrm{Im}\,S\right)\simeq\exp\left(-\frac{E}{T}\right),

(4)

where
E
is the energy of the emitted particle. This represents Boltzmann suppression. In the GUP-corrected analysis of Ref.
ASS
, the derived probability expressions imply a structure scaling as
\exp(+E/T)
or similar sign inversions in the exponent. A positive exponent would imply an unphysical exponential enhancement of emission for high-energy modes. The sign conventions for the covariant action and the Hamilton-Jacobi ansatz must be rigorously checked to ensure standard thermodynamic stability is recovered in the limit of vanishing deformation parameter
\beta
.

V
Terminology and Physical Interpretations

Finally, we note two terminological issues that may lead to physical misinterpretation. First, regarding charge interpretation, the black hole is described as magnetically charged, yet sections of the text attribute photon deflection to “electrostatic repulsion.” Photons are electrically neutral and follow null geodesics determined by the effective optical metric. While NED introduces non-minimal couplings or effective refractive indices, phrasing this as electrostatic force is misleading. Second, concerning plasma versus Lorentz violation, the variable
\xi
(typically defined via the plasma frequency
\omega_{p}
as
\xi=\omega_{p}^{2}/\omega^{2}
) describes dispersive media effects. Ref.
ASS
occasionally labels this a
“Lorentz-violating parameter.”
While plasma introduces a preferred frame (the rest frame of the medium), it is a standard relativistic effect, not a fundamental violation of Lorentz invariance in the gravity sector.

VI
Conclusion

We have highlighted specific inconsistencies in Ref.
ASS
regarding extremality parameters, the Schwarzschild limit, weak lensing expansions, and tunneling probability signs. We recommend that a Corrigendum be issued to unify the numerical value of
q_{\rm ext}
across the text and abstract, clarify the nature of the horizonless solution (regular soliton vs. singularity), correct the weak deflection expansion to recover the
15\pi/4
coefficient in the Schwarzschild limit, and rectify the sign error in the GUP tunneling probability. Resolving these discrepancies is essential for the model to serve as a reliable reference for testing nonlinear electrodynamics in strong gravity.

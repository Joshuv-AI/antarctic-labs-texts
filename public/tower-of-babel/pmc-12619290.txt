Integrating genetic crosstalk between atherosclerosis and lung adenocarcinoma to advance precision diagnosis and treatment
Authors: Haojie Dai, Xuchen Wang, Qiwen Wu, Ye Tan, Haoran Shen, Yichun Gu, Chuanxin Su, Aidong Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThere is evidence that atherosclerosis is a “tumor-like” disease and has similar genetic mutations to lung adenocarcinoma. In addition, the treatment and progression of lung adenocarcinoma can contribute to the development of atherosclerosis. This highlights the importance of studying the mechanisms of crosstalk between these two diseases and developing tools for early diagnosis and prognosis. We obtained gene expression profiles of both diseases through the GEO and TCGA databases and screened for crosstalk genes on the basis of differential genes. On the one hand, we constructed a diagnostic model of AS with LUAD by screening the core genes through Lasso and SVM-RFE to advance the early diagnosis of AS in patients with LUAD and explored the association between the core genes and immune infiltration. On the other hand, we constructed a robust prognostic model of LUAD based on crosstalk genes, explored the potential mechanisms of prognostic model genes in the regulation of immune infiltration and predicted treatment differences in LUAD patients to advance clinical decision-making. In addition, we constructed a PPI network based on crosstalk genes and a TF-miRNA-mRNA network, and performed drug prediction and molecular docking validation based on core targets. In conclusion, we revealed the crosstalk between AS and LUAD based on multifaceted transcriptomic analysis, screened novel targets, advanced diagnosis and prognosis, explored potential drugs and treatments, and provided invaluable insights into the research and treatment of AS with LUAD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13008-025-00172-y.Keywords: Atherosclerosis, Lung adenocarcinoma, Crosstalk, Diagnosis, Prognosis, Biomarker

Introduction

Atherosclerosis (AS) is a lesion that is highly prevalent in the elderly and is caused by chronic inflammation leading to narrowing of the vascular lumen [1, 2]. The pathogenesis may be due to the intimal invasion and proliferation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMC) leading to plaque formation and progression of AS [3]. Recently, an innovative study showed that smooth muscle-derived cells (SDCs) generated during AS have many tumor cell-like features, represent a smooth muscle-associated tumor-like disease, and have tumor-like gene mutations such as KrasG12D [4]. Currently, AS remains the leading cause of death worldwide and is a serious threat to the health of the world’s population [5].

Lung cancer is a cancer with high morbidity and mortality that is widely distributed worldwide [6, 7]. The histologic classification of lung cancer can be divided into small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) belongs to NCSLC, which accounts for about 50% of it. The incidence of lung adenocarcinoma has continued to rise in recent years and has attracted increasing public attention.In 1994, the incidence of lung adenocarcinoma became the highest among the subspecies of lung cancer [8]. Although a variety of modalities including immunotherapy have been adopted for treatment, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has also adopted acupuncture and other methods for treatment [9]. However, its five-year survival rate is extremely low, even below 5% [10]. Most lung adenocarcinomas also carry mutations in KrasG12D or lack a recognizable driver oncogene [11, 12].

According to recent clinical studies, immunotherapy for LUAD has been shown to increase the likelihood of acute vascular events (AVEs) [13, 14], including AS. Several other reports have suggested that some long-stranded non-coding RNAs, which are highly transcribed in lung adenocarcinoma patients, may directly or indirectly contribute to AS, such as HOXA-AS3 and Meg3. Among them, HOXA-AS3 can be used as an important biomarker for early diagnosis of clinical AS [15], Meg3 is significantly expressed in LUAD, which promotes the development of LUAD, and Meg3 promotes the formation of atherosclerotic plaques [16, 17]. Therefore, early diagnosis of AS is clinically necessary in patients with lung adenocarcinoma.

However, the genetic crosstalk between AS and LUAD and its clinical value are still unknown, so we analyzed the crosstalk genes and association mechanisms between AS and LUAD by bioinformatics methods to provide some insights into the future study of AS with LUAD.

Weighted gene co-expression module identification

With WGCNA, we constructed a scale-free co-expression network to further identify the genes most relevant to LUAD. Selecting a soft threshold of 8 (Fig. 2E), we categorized the differential genes of LUAD into seven clustering modules (Fig. 2F) and drew a clustering dendrogram (Fig. 2G). Among the seven gene modules, we extracted the two modules, MEblue and MEblack, which were most positively and negatively correlated with LUAD for further analysis (MEblue: r = 0.6, p = 1e-55; MEblack: r=-0.59, p = 7e-53). We plotted scatter plots of gene-tumor correlations in the above two modules (Fig. 2H-I).

We took the intersection of the LUAD differential genes extracted by WGCNA with the differential genes of AS and obtained 180 crosstalk genes. (Fig. 3A) Disease Ontology analysis showed that crosstalk genes were mainly enriched in artery-related lesions (Fig. 3B), demonstrating the potential of crosstalk genes as predictors of AS. Interestingly, we found that dilated cardiomyopathy-related pathways were enriched in the normal population with LUAD controls, suggesting a potential association between LUAD and cardiovascular disease (Fig. 3E-F). GO enrichment of crosstalk genes revealed that BP-related genes are mainly involved in positive regulation of cytokine production, immune response-regulating signaling pathway, regulation of innate immune response.CC-related genes are involved in endocytic vesicle, secretory granule membrane, and other components, while MF-related genes are involved in compound binding-related molecular functions (Fig. 3G). In addition, KEGG pathway analysis showed that Osteoclast differentiation, Phagosome related pathway was significantly enriched (Fig. 3H).

Identification and validation of AS core genes based on crosstalk genes

We first extracted the feature genes in GSE100927 and TCGA-LUAD using lasso regression.For both datasets, lambda.min was chosen as the λ value, and 16 and 25 feature genes were obtained respectively (Fig. 4A-B).SVM-RFE is a machine learning algorithm modified from support vector machine SVM-RFE is a machine learning algorithm modified from support vector machine, which aims to identify the best core genes by filtering out the feature vectors generated by SVM (Fig. 4C). Finally, we obtained two core genes by taking the intersection of the results of the above algorithm: MYBL1 and HSPB7 (Fig. 4D). Box line plots demonstrate the differential expression of the core genes in the two AS cohorts (Fig. 4E-H). The diagnostic ROC curves in the training set showed near-perfect results with sensitivities of 0.991 and 0.984 for MYBL1 and HSPB7, respectively (Fig. 4K-L). In the validation set, the AUCs of the ROC curves of both genes were also higher than 0.85, which were 0.867 and 0.854 (Fig. 4M-N), respectively.

We then calculated the diagnostic accuracy of MYBL1 and HSPB7 in the TCGA-LUAD cohort. Landscape of immune infiltration in AS and LUAD

Given that enrichment analysis suggests that immune pathways are involved in the progression of AS and that crosstalk genes are enriched for multiple immune functions, we carefully investigated the level of immune cell infiltration in different individuals in the GSE100927 cohort versus the TCGA-LUAD cohort. Using the Cibersort algorithm, we assessed the abundance of 22 immune cell types and displayed the differences in box-and-line plots as well as bar-stacked plots. We found that in AS samples, infiltration of B cells naive, Plasma cells etc. was significantly downregulated while T cells CD8, Macrophages M0 etc. were significantly upregulated (Fig. 5A-B). While in LUAD samples, T cells CD4 memory activated etc. were significantly up-regulated, while NK cells were down-regulated significantly (Fig. 5D-E). We then further analyzed the correlation of core genes MYBL1 and HSPB7 with various immune cells. Interestingly, the correlations of the core genes with immune cells were almost identical in the AS cohort (Fig. 5C). Given that both genes were significantly down-regulated in expression in the AS cohort, we suggest that they have synergistic regulation of immune cell dynamics in AS. However, the immunomodulatory effects of the core genes in the LUAD cohort were of more concern to us, and we found that they had diametrically opposite correlations with the majority of immune cells (Fig. 5F). Interestingly, the expression changes of the core genes in LUAD were also diametrically opposed, and taken together they also had a homophilic regulation of immune infiltration. Upon overall comparison, we found that the immune infiltration correlation of core genes showed a consistent trend with the immune cell abundance characteristics of the two diseases, demonstrating that the expression of core genes is inextricably regulated with the progression of the two diseases.

Construction and validation of LUAD prognostic model based on crosstalk genes

We first screened the crosstalk genes with prognostic potential by univariate cox analysis, and filtered out the part with p-value less than 0.05 (Fig S1). Then we further extracted marker genes by lasso regression and obtained 13 prognosis-related crosstalk genes (Fig. 6A-B). Finally, we further screened by multivariate cox analysis to obtain 3 crosstalk genes significantly associated with the prognosis of LUAD: AKAP12, SYTL1, and PLEK2 (Fig. 6C), and based on which we built a risk score model. In the training set TCGA-LUAD, we calculated the risk score for each sample and categorized the cohort into high- and low-risk subgroups based on the median score (Fig. 6D). Scatter plots with KM curves showed that patients in different risk subgroups had significant differences in overall survival, with the high-risk group having a lower survival rate (Fig. 6E-F). We judged the predictive accuracy of the model by the ROC curve, and the AUCs of 1 year, 3 years and 5 years were 0.669, 0.653 and 0.649, respectively, which had good predictive accuracy (Fig. 6G). In the validation set GSE72094, we similarly divided the high- and low-risk subgroups according to the median scores of the training set (Fig. 6H). As we expected, the survival of the high-risk group is much worse than that of the low-risk group, and the risk model has good prognostic discriminatory ability (Fig. 6I-J).The AUCs in the ROC curves are 0.639, 0.561, and 0.656 at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively (Fig. 6K).

Immunotherapy and chemotherapy response in different risk subgroups

Given the differences in the expression of immune checkpoint-related genes in different risk subgroups, we tried to explore the significance of risk scores in guiding immunotherapy. We found that patients in the low-risk group showed higher responsiveness to PD-1-negative/CTLA4-positive or negative (Fig. 8F-I). We then analyzed the responsiveness to chemotherapeutic agents in different risk subgroups, and we selected LUAD chemotherapeutic agents that have been reported in the literature for presentation among the results with significant differences (P < 0 0.05) (Fig S2). We found that the low-risk group showed higher sensitivity to Camptothecin, KRAS(G12C)-inhibitor and other drugs with lower IC50 values. While the high risk group showed higher sensitivity to drugs like Cisplatin, Paclitaxel. These findings provide valuable insights for different treatments based on AS-related prognostic scores in LUAD patients.

Validation of AS diagnostic effect and expression difference of prognostic genes

Given that our above study revealed a certain crosstalk link between AS and LUAD, we attempted to substitute prognosis-related crosstalk genes into AS to determine their diagnostic value. We found that the differential expression of prognostic genes in AS was very significant (Fig. 9A&C) To our surprise, in both AS cohorts, AKAP12 and PLEK2 demonstrated high precision, and the AUC values of ROC were higher than 0.75 (Fig. 9B&D). In addition we verified the differential expression of prognostic genes in LUAD and normal tissues by the available immunohistochemical profiles in Human Protein Atlas, and the results were in good agreement with the differential profile obtained by analyzing the expression profiles (Fig. 9E-F). In tumor samples, SYTL1 and PLEK2 were upregulated in expression while AKAP12 expression was significantly reduced. This result was similarly validated in the GSE68465 cohort, where we observed the same differential expression trend (Fig S3).

Then to explore the upstream and downstream relationship of hub genes, we generated the TF-miRNA network around hub genes by NetworkAnalyst. Due to the excessive number of resultant nodes, we only show the main part with the number of nodes greater than or equal to 2 (Fig. 10C). The results show that SPI1 is most closely linked to the neighborhood, and SP1, NFKB1, and TP53 are the more widely linked transcription factors in the network.

Inputting hub gene into EnrichR platform, based on DSigDB library we got a series of potential drugs and screened the top ten drug molecules based on P-value (Fig. 12A). Molecular docking of drugs to target proteins was performed by Autodock, and we obtained the predicted binding energies for each combination, among which Tamibarotene binds most stably to CYBB with a binding energy of -9.6 kcal/mol (Fig. 12B). In addition, we selected combinations with binding energies greater than 7 for visualization of specific docking patterns (Fig. 12C-G). The molecular docking experiments validated the reliability of the drugs acting on hub gene targets and provided valuable insights for drug development for the treatment of AS with LUAD.

Discussion

Atherosclerosis and lung adenocarcinoma, two seemingly unrelated diseases, have been shown in numerous studies to have a potential crosstalk relationship. Previous studies have shown that immune dysregulation plays a key role in the progression of both diseases.The development of AS begins with endothelial cell damage and dysfunction. This damage promotes migratory infiltration of inflammatory cells, mainly T cells, monocytes, and subsequently LDL and foam cell formation [18]. And the dysregulation of these two immune cells appears to be particularly significant in LUAD. For example, the killing activity of CTL in lung adenocarcinoma tissues is attenuated, CD4 T cells are altered to Th2 phenotype, and monocytes are involved in the progression of early lung adenocarcinoma [19–21]. Recent cutting-edge studies have shown that AS is a tumor-like disease and has a broad spectrum of KRASG12D mutations, which have a very high probability of occurring in LUAD [4]. In addition, some key regulators of lung adenocarcinoma, such as HOXA-AS3, promote AS [15], and immunotherapy for lung adenocarcinoma predisposes to AS progression [14]. This all suggests that we may have a potential crosstalk mechanism between AS and LUAD. And the importance of advance intervention for AS as a major adverse complication of lung adenocarcinoma cannot be overemphasized.

In this study, we explored the functional pathways of the crosstalk genes and identified the core genes through a bioinformatics approach based on the gene expression profiles of the two diseases and confirmed their excellent diagnostic effects in AS as well as LUAD. The core genes were then used as an entry point to provide insights into the development of the disease from an immune cell dynamics perspective. Then we screened LUAD prognostic genes based on crosstalk genes and constructed a prediction model. The prognostic model has good predictive ability and can guide clinical decisions such as immunotherapy, drug chemotherapy, and so on. In addition, we analyzed the upstream and downstream interaction networks of crosstalk genes and predicted potential drugs based on the extracted hub genes, and verified the drug-target binding reliability by molecular docking.

In our analysis, MYBL1 and HSPB7 were focused as core crosstalk genes. Both expressions were significantly downregulated in the AS cohort, while in the LUAD cohort, MYBL1 expression was increased and HSPB7 expression was decreased.MYBL1 is a strong transcriptional activator involved in cell signaling [22]. Previous studies have demonstrated that downregulation of MYBL1 is involved in the progression of AS, as evidenced by the fact that downregulation of MYBL1 expression inhibits autophagosome and lysosome fusion in HUVECs, which in turn inhibits PLEKHM1 and ultimately exacerbates atherosclerosis [23]. Our study highlights the potency of MYBL1 as a diagnostic marker for AS. In addition, MYBL1 has been reported to stimulate vascular endothelial growth by inducing transcriptional activation of ANGPT2, which affects the effect of sorafenib in hepatocellular carcinoma [24]. Several other papers claimed that MYBL1 promotes tumor progression as a key factor in gliomas [25–27].MYBL1 is involved in the evolution of a wide range of tumors, which coincides with our finding that its expression is upregulated in LUAD. Another core gene, HSPB7, is a member of the HSPB family, which plays a key role in cardiac development due to mediating actin assembly [28]. In addition, HSPB7 acts as a repressor in the evolution of several tumors, including lung adenocarcinoma [29–31]. Our study demonstrated the diagnostic value of MYBL1 and HSPB7 in two diseases, with excellent diagnostic accuracy for AS, and we built a robust column-line diagram accordingly, hoping to advance the early diagnosis of LUAD with AS.

The immune infiltration analysis reflected the relevant regulation of core genes on AS as well as LUAD at a deep level. We found that M0 macrophages were significantly enriched in AS samples, which coincides with the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis [32]. Recent studies have demonstrated that lipid lowering does not fully control AS progression and that all types of macrophages are involved in AS evolution, with future AS interventions geared toward targeted macrophage therapy [33, 34]. Our correlation analysis revealed that both MYBL1 and HSPB7 are strongly associated paracrine regulators of M0 macrophages, which provides a possible direction for future molecular therapy. The immune microenvironment is an important fertile ground for tumor development. We found that MYBL1 and HSPB7 as a whole tended to elevate the level of activated CD4 T cells within tumor cells.CD4 T cells exert anti-tumor effects by elevating the level of killing insights of other effector cells within the environment [35, 36]. Interestingly, previous studies have emphasized that HSPB7 inhibits LUAD progression by inhibiting glycolysis [31], but from an immune perspective, we found that HSPB7 was significantly negatively correlated with anti-tumor immune cells, such as CD8 T cells, NK cells, and M0 Macrophage, and positively correlated with immune escape promoting M2 Macrophage, which is worth further exploration and validation in the future. This deserves further exploration and validation in the future.

In order to advance the prognostic study of AS with LUAD patients, we established a prognostic model of LUAD based on crosstalk genes, and finally established a prognostic model of LUAD based on AKAP12, SYTL1, and PLEK2. Overexpressed AKAP12 is widely recognized as an important factor in cardiac insufficiency [37, 38], contrary to its tumor-suppressing role in most cancers, there have been reports of its tumor-promoting effect in LUAD [39]. Notably, AKAP12 has already been revealed to possess a new role beyond its classical scaffolding function: acting as an mRNA-binding protein, it specifically binds and regulates transcripts encoding cytoskeletal modulators and kinases (e.g., ABL2), thereby controlling actin dynamics at the cell periphery, filopodia formation, and cell migration [40]. This provides a novel mechanistic perspective for explaining AKAP12’s “metastasis-promoting” function in LUAD. Furthermore, given AKAP12’s critical role in maintaining barrier function, cell migration, and angiogenesis within vascular endothelial cells [41], we hypothesize that this molecular machinery coordinated by AKAP12—which governs cell morphology and motility—constitutes the common molecular basis linking AS pathological remodeling to LUAD tumor metastasis. Therefore, we believe it holds significant value as a cross-disease intervention target. SYTL1 is a defined protective factor. It has been reported that SYTL1 can form a complex with Rab27a and CD81 and promote the secretion of CD81 exosomes, thus inhibiting the metastasis of lung adenocarcinoma [42]. In addition SYTL1 enhances the killing activity of NK cells and inhibits the growth of bladder cancer [43]. The SYTL1 mediated exosome secretion pathway not only plays a crucial role in inhibiting tumor metastasis but may also be significantly involved in the pathological progression of AS. This study demonstrates that SYTL1 acts as a protective factor, with its high expression associated with favorable prognosis. Recent studies confirm that in AS lesions, both the quantity and morphology of exosomes undergo significant alterations, and the biogenesis of exosomes directly contributes to AS progression [44]. This suggests that the functional state of the SYTL1-CD81 exosome axis may serve as a pivotal link connecting these two diseases. Monitoring its activity may provide a novel, functional diagnostic biomarker for patients with comorbidities. PLEK2 is a proto-oncogene involved in AKT activation and stabilization [45]. Studies indicate that it promotes lung adenocarcinoma progression by upregulating SPC25 and activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway [46]. This pathway also plays a crucial role in the onset and progression of AS. Literature suggests that PLEK2 overexpression promotes vascular endothelial damage [47], and vascular endothelial injury is a significant risk factor for AS. Developing drugs targeting its downstream targets may represent a novel therapeutic approach for patients with both AS and LUAD. In summary, we systematically reveal the pivotal roles of AKAP12, SYTL1, and PLEK2 in lung adenocarcinoma and atherosclerosis. It not only deepens our understanding of the co-morbidity mechanisms between these two conditions but also highlights the significant potential of these three molecules in disease diagnosis and prognostic stratification. The evaluation system constructed based on their expression patterns holds promise as a practical tool for clinical use, offering both discriminatory and predictive value.

The immune infiltration analysis showed not much difference in immune cells between high and low risk subgroups, and we paid special attention to the higher infiltration of neutrophils in the high-risk group. In addition, risk scores and several prognostic genes showed a positive correlation with the number of neutrophils. The role of neutrophils in the tumor microenvironment is very controversial. On the one hand, they can be anti-tumor by releasing cytotoxic substances and activating the immune response [48, 49], but on the other hand, many recent studies have revealed that they can mediate tumor progression by promoting tumor proliferation and motility [50], facilitating angiogenesis [51], and releasing extracellular traps of neutrophils [52]. We hypothesize that the latter is one of the reasons for the worse prognosis of patients in the high-risk group. In addition, the difference in the sensitivity of patients in the high- and low-risk subgroups to immunotherapy versus pharmacological chemotherapy suggests that we should adopt a personalized diagnostic and therapeutic plan when dealing with LUAD patients in order to advance a better therapeutic prognosis.

The construction of the PPI network and the TF-miRNA network revealed the interactions between genes as well as the upstream and downstream relationships between genes and components such as transcription factors.TYROBP, as the most central component of our protein-interaction network, is a transmembrane junction protein widely expressed on immune cells, which has been demonstrated to promote AS by facilitating lipid plaque deposition [53]. In addition, it is notorious for promoting the progression of several tumors [54, 55]. SP1, the most widely linked transcription factor among TF-miRNAs, was shown to promote lung adenocarcinoma progression by activating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR/MYC signaling pathway through the level of STRIP2 [56]. In addition, SP1 has been reported to regulate the proliferation, migration, phenotypic transformation, and plate stability of vascular smooth muscle cells [57–59]. This all reveals an inextricable link between lung adenocarcinoma and atherosclerosis, which deserves deeper tandem exploration.

Based on the DSigDB database of EnrichR platform, we screened ten possible drugs by hub gene targets. Molecular docking results showed that Tamibarotene binds best to each target, followed by Valsartan and Pentachlorobiphenyl with respectable docking results. Our study uncovers the clinical promise of these existing drugs and provides certain new directions for the pharmacologic treatment of AS with LUAD.

Despite the encouraging results of our analysis, it is still important to recognize the limitations of the study. First of all, our study relied on existing databases, which may have led to bias in sample selection with respect to ethnographic characteristics. Even if the predictions are promising, more clinical independent cohort validation is particularly important to explore the generalizability of the model. Second, we explored possible mechanisms by which genes influence disease by means of bioinformatics, which may have biases from actual molecular regulation. Future studies should address these limitations through experimental validation, inclusion of prospective cohorts, and more detailed clinical data collection.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

TCGA-LUAD expression profiles as well as clinical data were obtained from UCSC Xena (https://xena.ucsc.edu). We obtained four additional datasets from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). GSE100927 was used as the training set for AS, containing 69 AS samples and 35 normal samples; GSE43292 was used as the validation set for AS, containing 32 AS samples and 32 normal samples; GSE72094 was used as the validation set for LUAD, containing 398 lung adenocarcinoma samples. GSE68465 was used to validate the differential expression of model genes in LUAD. Transcripts Per Million (TPM) is chosen for data normalization to effectively eliminate technical biases and provide reliable data support for subsequent analysis. In the filtering step, we removed meaningless genes, such as those with an expression level of zero or close to zero. Moreover, we employed the ComBat method, which is based on the sva package, for batch correction in the same disease for model construction.

Differential gene selection

We used the “limma” R package to perform differential analysis of the expression data in the training set, and the selection criteria for DEGs were log2FoldChange greater than 1 and adjusted P value less than 0.05. In addition, volcano plots were used to visualize the distribution of differential genes, and heatmaps were drawn to highlight the top 30 expression up-regulated genes, and the top 30 expression up-regulated genes were selected from the training set. demonstrated the top 30 differential genes with up- or down-regulated expression.

Extraction of weighted gene co-expression network modules

Using the “WGCNA” R package, we used Weighted gene correlation network analysis (WGCNA) to select clusters of co-expressed genes that are highly correlated with diseases [60]. First, after checking the gene expression quality of the samples, the expression profiles were hierarchically clustered using the “goodSamplesGenes” function to identify and remove outliers. Then, a Topological Overlap Matrix (TOM) is constructed by converting the correlation matrix to an adjacency matrix with an optimal soft threshold. Next, the minimum module size was set to 50, and TOM-based dissimilarity was used to perform linkage hierarchical clustering (LHCC) to assess core gene consistency across modules and merge related modules. We further screened for more significantly related gene modules in the DEGs of LUAD using WGCNA for subsequent analysis.

Crosstalk gene extraction and functional enrichment analysis

The intersection of DEGs of AS and LUAD was identified as a crosstalk gene. Using the “DOSE” R package, we analyzed the disease enrichment of crosstalk genes. We then first enriched KEGG-related pathways for DEGs in AS and LUAD using Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), and enriched pathways with p < 0.05 were considered significantly related. The gene set used for GSEA was designated as “c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt” from the MSigDB database [61]. Subsequently, the crosstalk genes were analyzed by GO enrichment and KEGG enrichment using the “clusterProfiler” R package, and the results were presented in the bubble diagram drawn by the “ggplot2” R package.

Identification and validation of core crosstalk markers

In order to identify the core crosstalk markers of AS and LUAD, we first used the “glmnet” R package to perform lasso regression analysis on the crosstalk genes of the two diseases, and obtained the best coefficients λ and featured genes through 10-fold cross-validation. Then, we implemented the Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) algorithm [62] on the AS cohort using the “e1071” R package to filter out the crosstalk genes with high diagnostic accuracy. portion of the crosstalk genes with higher diagnostic accuracy. We then took the intersection of the above genes to obtain our core genes. The boxplot shows the differential expression of the core genes between the two groups, and we calculated the AUC of the ROC curve to judge the diagnostic accuracy of the core genes. Next, we validated the diagnostic value of the core genes in AS as well as LUAD using the GSE43292 and TCGA-LUAD cohorts. Finally, we constructed a logistic diagnostic model based on the core genes using the “lrm” R package, plotted a line graph, and evaluated the consistency between the predictive accuracy of AS and the actual situation by using decision curves and calibration curves.

Immune infiltration analysis

Given that different infiltration patterns of immune cells play a key role in disease onset and progression [63], we used the Cibersort algorithm to assess the infiltration landscape of 22 immune cells in different samples based on their gene expression profiles [64], and presented the results in the form of box-and-line plots, bar stacked plots, and so on. Then, we took the Pearson test to calculate the correlation of the relevant genes to different immune cells and presented the results in a sophisticated heatmap through the “ggcor” R package. In addition, we analyzed the correlation between prognostic gene scores and the expression of immune checkpoint genes in the LUAD cohort.

Immunotherapy and Pharmacologic chemotherapy assessment

Immunotherapy outcome data for LUAD were downloaded from The Cancer Immunome Atlas (TCIA) (https://tcia.at/home). After in-depth analysis, we displayed the differences in immunotherapy across risk subgroups in violin plots. Next, we used the “oncoPredict” R package to analyze the differences in the sensitivity of different risk subgroups to clinically used chemotherapeutic agents.

Expression validation of prognostic genes

We downloaded immunohistochemical images of proteins corresponding to prognostic genes in lung adenocarcinoma as well as normal tissues from The Human Protein Atlas (https://www.proteinatlas.org).

PPI network construction and hub gene extraction

We used the online website String (https://string-db.org/) to construct the protein interaction network of the crosstalk genes and visualize it using Cytoscape software. The top ten genes in terms of node number (degree) were defined as hub genes, and the hub gene-associated TF-miRNA network was further constructed by NetworkAnalyst (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/), and the main part of the network with node number greater than 1 was presented using Cytoscape. Then we deeply analyzed the expression of hub gene in AS and LUAD, and verified its diagnostic value in AS as well as LUAD by ROC curves. In particular, we examined the prognostic potential of hub genes in LUAD.

Drug prediction and molecular Docking validation

We uploaded the hub gene to Enrichr ( https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/ ), identified the top ten drugs based on P-value in DSigDB library, and downloaded the 3D small molecule of the drugs in PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) structures. In addition, we downloaded the structures of the proteins expressed by the hub genes corresponding to the drugs in the Protein Data Bank (https://www.rcsb.org). The proteins were subjected to dehydrogenation, hydrogenation, and computational charge treatment, and the drug molecules were subjected to hydrogenation and computational charge treatment, followed by molecular docking of the drug molecules to the target proteins using AutoDock 4.2 and AutoDock tools 1.5.7. Semi-flexible docking method was chosen for molecular docking, and the size of the docking box was 120*120*120Å3 with a grid spacing of 0.375 Å. The docked conformation at the lowest point of the energy was selected, and the docked structure was visualized and displayed by PyMOL 3.0.5.

Statistical analysis

To assess predictive accuracy, the analysis was conducted using ROC curves, utilizing the AUC as the key quantitative measure. Additionally, we employed the Wilcoxon test for nonparametric analysis. In analyzing the data, the software R, version 4.3.3, was employed. The significance of differences among groups was determined with a threshold P-value set at below 0.05.

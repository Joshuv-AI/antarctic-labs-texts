1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero, and let
K[X_{d}]=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
denote the free associative commutative unital algebra of rank
d>1
over
K
.
For a subgroup
G
of
\GL_{d}(K)
the algebra of invariants
K[X_{d}]^{G}
consists of all
G
-invariant polynomials.
The commutative case is well-studied, with foundational results stemming from Hilbert’s 14th problem, posed in 1990 at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Paris
[
9
]
.
In 1916, E. Noether
[
18
]
proved that for any finite group
G
, the algebra
K[X_{d}]^{G}
is finitely generated when
\text{char}(K)=0
.
In 1926 she extended this result to arbitrary characteristic
[
19
]
.
For reductive groups in characteristic zero, finite generation also follows from Hilbert’s work.
However in 1950 Nagata
[
17
]
demonstrated that finite generation fails in general for infinite groups, providing a counterexample.

We now turn to the noncommutative setting, replacing
K[X_{d}]
with an algebra sharing its key properties. The natural candidate is the free associative algebra
K\langle X_{d}\rangle

(i.e., the algebra of noncommuting polynomials in
d
variables), which satisfies the same universal property.
Remarkably, the analogue of Noether’s theorem on finite generation holds for
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
only in a highly restricted case:
for finite subgroups
G\leq\text{GL}_{d}(K)
, the work of Koryukin
[
13
]
, Dicks-Formanek
[
3
]
, and Kharchenko
[
11
]

established that
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
is finitely generated if and only if
G
acts by scalar multiplication on the vector space spanned by
X_{d}
.
Later Koryukin
[
13
]
generalized this to infinite groups.

In 1984 Koryukin
[
13
]
introduced an additional action of the symmetric group
\Sym(n)
on the homogeneous component of degree
n
of
K\langle X_{d}\rangle
,
given by permuting the positions of the variables. This endows
K\langle X_{d}\rangle
with the structure of a
\big(K\langle X_{d}\rangle,\!\circ\big)
-
S
-algebra.
With respect to this action, Koryukin proved that the invariant algebra
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
is finitely generated for every reductive group
G
.

One of the natural problems both in the commutative and noncommutative case is the description of the invariants of important groups.
In this paper we study the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
of invariants under the action of the dihedral group
D_{2n}
on the free associative algebra
{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle
of rank
2
.
We describe several important properties of
{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
. We compute its Hilbert series and construct a homogeneous set of generators of
{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
as a free algebra.
Finally, we find a finite generating set of
{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
as an
S
-algebra.

Similar problems for the noncommutative symmetric polynomials, i.e. for the algebra of invariants
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{\Sym(d)}
were solved in
[
1
]
.
For other recent results on noncommutative invariants of the dihedral group see
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

We assume the ground field to be the field of complex numbers, i.e.,

K={\mathbb{C}}
, although many results hold over any field
K
of characteristic zero.

2.1.
Classical Commutative Invariant Theory

The general linear group acts naturally on the
d
-dimensional complex vector space
V_{d}
with basis
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
. The coordinate functions
x_{i}:V_{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
are defined by

x_{i}(\xi_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{d}v_{d})=\xi_{i},\quad\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad i=1,\ldots,d.

This induces an action of on the algebra of polynomial functions
{\mathbb{C}}[X_{d}]={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
via

g(f):v\to f\big(g^{-1}(v)\big),\quad g\in\GL_{d}({\mathbb{C}}),\,f(X_{d})\in{\mathbb{C}}[X_{d}],\,v\in V_{d}.

For a subgroup
G
of
\GL_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
, the algebra of
G
-invariants consists of all polynomials fixed under this action:

{\mathbb{C}}[X_{d}]^{G}=\big\{f\in{\mathbb{C}}[X_{d}]\mid g(f)=f\text{ for all }g\in G\big\}.

For our purposes it is more convenient to assume that
\GL_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
acts canonically on the vector space
KX_{d}
with basis
X_{d}
and to extend its action diagonally to
{\mathbb{C}}[X_{d}]
by

g\big(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\big)=f\big(g(x_{1}),\ldots,g(x_{d})\big),\quad g\in\GL_{d}({\mathbb{C}}),f\in{\mathbb{C}}[X_{d}].

The diagonal action of its opposite group
\GL_{d}^{\mathrm{op}}({\mathbb{C}})
induced by the canonical action of
\GL_{d}^{\mathrm{op}}({\mathbb{C}})
on the vector space
KX_{d}

is the same as the action of
\GL_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
on the polynomial functions
{\mathbb{C}}[X_{d}]
.
The mapping
g\to g^{-1}
defines an isomorphism of
\GL_{d}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\GL_{d}^{\mathrm{op}}({\mathbb{C}})
and both actions give the same algebras of invariants.

2.2.
Foundational Theorems

The following classical theorems form the cornerstone of commutative invariant theory.

Theorem 1

((Endlichkeitssatz of Emmy Noether
[
18
]
))
.

For a finite subgroup
G
of
\GL_{d}(K)
and a field
K
of characteristic zero, the algebra of invariants
K[X_{d}]^{G}
is finitely generated.
Moreover, it admits a system of homogeneous generators whose degrees are bounded by the order of
G
.

Theorem 2

(Chevalley–Shephard–Todd,
[
2
,
20
]
)
.

For a finite group
G
over a field
K
of characteristic zero, the algebra of invariants
K[X_{d}]^{G}
is isomorphic to a polynomial algebra
(i.e., has a system of algebraically independent generators) if and only if
G<\GL_{d}(K)
is generated by pseudo-reflections
(matrices of finite order with a conjugate that is a diagonal matrix of the form
\mathrm{diag}(1,\ldots,1,\xi)
, where
\xi\neq 1
is a root of unity).

The algebra
K[X_{d}]^{G}
is naturally graded, decomposing into homogeneous components:

K[X_{d}]^{G}=K\oplus\left(K[X_{d}]^{G}\right)^{(1)}\oplus\left(K[X_{d}]^{G}\right)^{(2)}\oplus\cdots.

Its Hilbert (or Poincaré) series is given by

H\big(K[X_{d}]^{G},t\big)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\dim\left(K[X_{d}]^{G}\right)^{(n)}t^{n}

and by the Hilbert–Serre theorem, this series is rational:

H\big(K[X_{d}]^{G},t\big)=p(t)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-t^{a_{i}}},\quad p(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t].

The third fundamental result, due to Molien
[
16
]
, provides an explicit formula for the Hilbert series:

Theorem 3
.

For a finite group and
\charr(K)=0
,

H\big(K[X_{d}]^{G},t\big)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\frac{1}{\det\big(1-gt\big)}.

2.3.
Noncommutative Invariant Theory

In one of the principal branches of noncommutative invariant theory, we replace the commutative polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[X_{d}]
with an algebra sharing its key properties.
For comprehensive surveys on this subject, we recommend the works of Formanek
[
7
]
and the second named author of this paper
[
4
]
.

Our primary object of study is the free associative algebra
K\langle X_{d}\rangle
.
Among unital associative algebras,
K\langle X_{d}\rangle
satisfies the same universal property as
K[X_{d}]
does in the commutative case:
any map
X_{d}\to R
to an algebra
R
extends uniquely to a homomorphism
K\langle X_{d}\rangle\to R
. T
he general linear group
\GL_{d}(K)=\GL(KX_{d})
acts naturally on the vector space
KX_{d}
with basis
X_{d}
, and this action extends to
K\langle X_{d}\rangle
via algebra homomorphisms:

g(f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d}))=f(g(x_{1}),\cdots,g(x_{d})),\,g\in GL_{d},f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})\in K\langle X_{d}\rangle.

For any subgroup
G\leq\GL_{d}(K)
, the algebra of
G
-invariants
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
consists of all polynomials fixed by this action.

2.4.
Fundamental Results on Finite Generation

The following results demonstrate the stark contrast between commutative and noncommutative invariant algebras regarding finite generation.
These were established independently by Dicks-Formanek
[
3
]
and Kharchenko
[
11
]
for finite groups, and extended by Koryukin
[
13
]
to arbitrary groups.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G\leq\GL_{d}(K)
over any field
K
, and let
KY_{m}
be the minimal subspace of
KX_{d}
with
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}\subseteq K\langle Y_{m}\rangle
.
Then the algebra
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
is finitely generated if and only if
G
acts on
KY_{d}
by scalar multiplication.

Corollary 5

(
[
3
,
11
]
)
.

For finite
G\leq\GL_{d}(K)
, the algebra of invariants
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
is finitely generated if and only if
G
is cyclic and consists of scalar matrices.

Corollary 6

(
[
13
]
)
.

If
G
acts irreducibly on
KX_{d}
, then
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
is either trivial or not finitely generated.

It has been shown that the counterpart of the Chevalley–Shephard–Todd theorem also looks different for the free associative algebra
K\langle X_{d}\rangle
.

Theorem 7
.

(i) (Lane
[
14
]
, Kharchenko
[
10
]
)
For any
G\leq\GL_{d}(K)
and any field
K
, the algebra
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
is free.

(ii) (Kharchenko
[
10
]
)
For finite groups
G
, there exists a Galois correspondence between free subalgebras of
K\langle X_{d}\rangle
containing
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}
and subgroups of
G
:
a subalgebra
F
with
K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G}\subseteq F
is free if and only if
F=K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{H}
for some
H\leq G
.

The analogue of the Molien formula for the Hilbert series of invariants admits the following elegant form:

Theorem 8

(Dicks and Formanek
[
3
]
)
.

For a finite group
G\leq\GL_{d}(K)
with
\mathrm{char}(K)=0
,

H\big(K\langle X_{d}\rangle^{G},t\big)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\frac{1}{1-\tr(g)t}.

3.
The algebra

{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}

The canonical action of the dihedral group

D_{2n}=\langle\rho,\tau\mid\rho^{n}=\tau^{2}=(\tau\rho)^{2}=1\rangle

on the two-dimensional vector space is as the group of symmetries of the regular
n
-gon.
Working over
\mathbb{C}
we may change the basis, see Klein
[
12
]
(Kapitel II in the German original or Chapter II in the English translation) and may assume that
it acts on the vector space with basis
\{u,v\}
via

\begin{matrix}\rho:&u\to\xi u\\
&v\to\bar{\xi}v\end{matrix}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \begin{matrix}\tau:&u\to v\\
&v\to u\end{matrix}

where
\xi=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}}
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity and
\bar{\xi}=\xi^{-1}
.
In the sequel we shall consider this action of
D_{2n}
on
\mathbb{C}\langle u,v\rangle
.

Considering polynomials
p(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
with
p(0,0)=0
(omitting constant terms since they are naturally invariant), we observe:

(1)

Symmetry Condition:

p(u,v)=\tau\cdot p(u,v)=p(v,u).

Thus all invariants are symmetric in
u
and
v
.

(2)

Grading Condition.

The
\rho
-invariance
p(u,v)=\rho\cdot p(u,v)=p(\xi u,\bar{\xi}v)
implies for homogeneous in
u
and
v
components

w(u,v)+w(v,u)=\xi^{\text{\rm deg}_{u}w-\text{\rm deg}_{v}w}w(u,v)+\xi^{\text{\rm deg}_{v}w-\text{\rm deg}_{u}w}w(v,u).

Therefore

\text{\rm deg}_{u}w-\text{\rm deg}_{v}w\equiv 0\ (\text{\rm mod}\ n).

Remark 9
.

Let
F
be a free subalgebra of the free algebra
{\mathbb{C}}\langle X_{d}\rangle
freely generated by a set
Y
of homogeneous polynomials and
g_{n}
be the number of polynomials of degree
n
in
Y
.
It is well known that the Hilbert series of
F
can be expressed by the generation function of the sequence
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots
, as

H(F,t)=\frac{1}{1-G(F,t)},\text{ where }G(F,t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{n}t^{n}.

It is known by
[
14
,
10
]
that
{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
is a free algebra. Also by
[
3
]
, the Hilbert series of the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
is

H_{2n}(t)=H({\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}},t)=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{g\in D_{2n}}\frac{1}{1-\text{\rm tr}(g)t}

Using the symmetry and grading conditions on the
D_{2n}
-invariants together with Remark
9

we shall compute the Hilbert series for the algebra of invariants
\mathbb{C}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
and shall present its homogeneous free generating set.

Theorem 10
.

The Hilbert series
H_{2n}(t)
of
\mathbb{C}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
is given by

(i)

for odd
n=2m+1
,
m\geq 1

H_{2n}(t)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2n(1-2t)}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-2\text{\rm cos}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})t},

(ii)

for even
n=2m+2
,
m\geq 1

H_{2n}(t)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2n(1-2t)}+\frac{1}{2n(1+2t)}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-2\text{\rm cos}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})t}.

Proof.

Consider the canonical matrix representation of the dihedral group. The rotations
r_{k}
and the reflections
s_{k}
are represented by the matrices

r_{k}=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ c c }$\text{\rm cos}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})$&$-\text{\rm sin}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})$\\
&\\
$\text{\rm sin}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})$&$\text{\rm cos}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})$\\
\end{tabular}\right)\ \ \ \text{\rm and}\ \ \ s_{k}=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ c c }$\text{\rm cos}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})$&$\text{\rm sin}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})$\\
&\\
$\text{\rm sin}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})$&$-\text{\rm cos}(\frac{2k\pi}{n})$\\
\end{tabular}\right).

Their traces are:

\text{\rm tr}(r_{k})=2\text{\rm cos}\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right),\ \ \text{\rm tr}(s_{k})=0,\text{ for }k=0,\ldots,n-1.

Hence the Hilbert series of the algebra
{\mathbb{C}}\langle u,v\rangle^{D_{2n}}
is

\displaystyle H_{2n}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{g\in D_{2n}}\frac{1}{1-\text{\rm tr}(g)t}=\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-\text{\rm tr}(s_{k})t}+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-\text{\rm tr}(r_{k})t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{1}+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-2\text{\rm cos}\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right)t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2n(1-2t)}+\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{1-2\text{\rm cos}\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right)t}

(i) Now let
n=2m+1
for some integer
m\geq 1
. Since for
k=1,\ldots,m

\text{\rm cos}\left(\frac{2k\pi}{2m+1}\right)=\text{\rm cos}\left(-\frac{2k\pi}{2m+1}+2\pi\right)=\text{\rm cos}\left(\frac{2(2m+1-k)\pi}{2m+1}\right),

we have

\displaystyle H_{2n}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2n(1-2t)}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-2\cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2n(1-2t)}-\frac{1}{n(1-2t)}+\frac{1}{n(1-2t)}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-2\cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2n(1-2t)}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{1}{1-2\cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})t}

(ii) The proof runs similarly to the proof of (i).
∎

3.1.
Hilbert series

Let
n=2m+1
. In the Hilbert series

\displaystyle H_{2n}(t)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2n(1-2t)}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-2\text{\rm cos}\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right)t}.

we analyze the sum involving cosines

A(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{1}{1-2\cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})t}

and express it as a rational function. By finding a common denominator, we obtain

\displaystyle A(t)=\frac{B(t)}{C(t)},\ \ \text{ where }C(t)=\prod_{k=0}^{m}{\big(1-2\cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})t\big)}

The denominator
C(t)
relates to the minimal polynomials of
2\cos(2\pi/n)
. Let

\Psi_{n}(t)=\prod_{k=0}^{m}{\big(t-2\cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})\big)}=\prod_{d|n}\psi_{d}(t),

where
\psi_{d}(t)
is the minimal polynomial of
2\cos(2\pi/d)
. This gives

C(t)=t^{m+1}\Psi_{n}(\frac{1}{t}).

The minimal polynomial
\psi_{n}(t)
has roots

2\cos(2\pi k/n)\mid 1\leq k<n/2,\gcd(k,n)=1,

i.e. they are twice the real parts of the primitive
n
-th roots of unity, and degree
\varphi(n)/2
for
n\geq 3
(where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function).

The relation between
\psi_{n}(x)
and cyclotomic polynomials
\Phi_{n}
can be shown in the following identity proved by Lehmer
[
15
]
, which holds for any non-zero complex number
z
:

\psi_{n}\left(z+z^{-1}\right)=z^{-{\frac{\varphi(n)}{2}}}\Phi_{n}(z).

In 1993, Watkins and Zeitlin
[
21
]
found the following relation between
\psi_{n}(t)
and Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind.

Theorem 11

(
[
21
]
)
.

For the Chebyshev polynomial
T_{k}(t)
of the first kind:

(1)

If
n=2m+1
is odd, then
\prod_{d\mid n}\psi_{d}(2t)=2(T_{m+1}(t)-T_{m}(t))
;

(2)

if
n=2m
is even, then
\prod_{d\mid n}\psi_{d}(2t)=2(T_{m+1}(t)-T_{m-1}(t))
;

(3)

if
n
is a power of 2, i.e.
n=2^{k}
we have
\psi_{2^{k+1}}(2t)=2T_{2^{k-1}}(t)
.

From the preceding theorem, we obtain the factorization

\chi_{n}(t)=\prod_{k=0}^{m}{\big(t-2\cos(\frac{2k\pi}{n})\big)}=\prod_{d\mid n}\Psi_{d}(t)

The numerator
B(t)
of
A(t)
admits an explicit expansion in terms of elementary symmetric polynomials

B(t)=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0,\\
k\neq s\end{subarray}}^{m}{\left(1-2\cos\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right)t\right)}.

This can be systematically expanded as:

\displaystyle B(t)

\displaystyle=1-t\left(\sum_{s=0}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq s\end{subarray}}^{m}2\cos\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+t^{2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{subsets }(\beta_{1},\dots\beta_{m})\text{ of }m\\
\text{ among }(m+1)\text{ variables}\ \ \{2\cos(\frac{0\pi}{n}),\cdots,2\cos(\frac{2m\pi}{n})\}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{m}\big(\beta_{1},\dots\beta_{m}\big)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{m}t^{m}\sigma_{m}\left(2\cos\left(\frac{0\pi}{n}\right),\ldots,2\cos\left(\frac{2m\pi}{n}\right)\right)

where
\sigma_{k}
denotes the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in all
(m+1)
variables,
namely
\{2\cos(\frac{0\pi}{n}),\cdots,2\cos(\frac{2m\pi}{n})\}
,
and
\sigma_{k}^{m}
denotes the symmetric polynomial in subsets of
m
variables among all. The coefficients follow the pattern

B(t)=(m+1)-m\sigma_{1}t+(m-1)\sigma_{2}t^{2}-\cdots+(-1)^{m-1}2\sigma_{m-1}t^{m-1}+(-1)^{m}\sigma_{m}t^{m}.

For the even case
n=2m
, the Hilbert series takes the form:

\displaystyle H_{4m+4}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4m+4}\left(\frac{1}{1-2t}+\frac{1}{1+2t}\right)+\frac{1}{2m+2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-2\cos\left(\frac{2k\pi}{n}\right)t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4m+4}\left(\frac{1}{1-2t}+\frac{1}{1+2t}\right)+\frac{1}{2m+2}A(t),

where
A(t)=\frac{B(t)}{C(t)}
as before. The numerator maintains the same symmetric structure

B(t)=(m+1)-m\sigma_{1}t+\cdots+(-1)^{m-1}2\sigma_{m-1}t^{m-1}+(-1)^{m}\sigma_{m}t^{m},

where
\sigma_{k}
are the

3.2.
Linear Recurrence Relations and Rational Generating Functions

A fundamental result in combinatorics and number theory establishes the correspondence between linear recurrence sequences and rational generating functions.
Let us consider a sequence of complex numbers
(R_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfying a
k
-th order linear recurrence relation:

R_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}R_{n-j}\text{ for }n\geq k,

where
A_{k}\neq 0
, and the initial values
R_{0},\ldots,R_{k-1}
are given complex numbers (not all zero). The associated characteristic polynomial is

c(x)=x^{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}x^{k-j}.

The generating function of the sequence is defined as the formal power series

R(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{n}x^{n}.

The following theorem establishes the fundamental connection between these concepts

Theorem 12

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence satisfying a linear recurrence relation with characteristic polynomial
x^{k}-\sum{j=1}^{k}A_{j}x^{k-j}
.
Then its generating function is rational and takes the form

a(x)=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}b_{i}x^{i}}{1-\sum_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}x^{j}},

where the coefficients
b_{i}
are determined by:

b_{i}=a_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}a_{i-j}\ \text{ for }\ 0\leq i\leq k-1).

Conversely, any rational function

\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{s}b_{i}x^{i}}{\sum_{j=0}^{k}d_{j}x^{j}}\quad(s\geq 0,k\geq 1,b_{s}\neq 0,d_{0}\neq 0,d_{k}\neq 0),

with denominator not dividing the numerator, generates a sequence satisfying a linear recurrence relation with characteristic polynomial

x^{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{d_{j}}{d_{0}}x^{k-j}

for all
n\geq\max(k,s+1)
. When
s<k
,
d_{0}=1
, and all coefficients are rational integers, the sequence terms
a_{n}
are rational integers.

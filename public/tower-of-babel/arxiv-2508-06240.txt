Bogoliubov analysis of Higgs mode in trapped Fermi superfluids with spatial inhomogeneity Preprint: APS/123-QED
K. Kakimoto, J. Takahashi, Y. Yamanaka

Abstract
The Higgs mode is a key component in the spontaneous breaking of a continuous symmetry along with the Nambu-Goldstone mode, and has been studied extensively for homogeneous systems. We consider it for inhomogeneous systems, using the superfluid of harmonically trapped ultracold Fermi atomic gas. The Fermionic field operators are expanded in a complete set of wave functions corresponding to inhomogeneous situation. Within the Hatree-Fock approximation, we derive integral equations from the Bogoliubov-de Gennes equations, which lead to the frequencies of the collective modes, including the Higgs and Nambu-Goldstone modes. The results show that the frequency of the Higgs mode equals twice the absolute value of the order parameter at the center of trap. This feature is robust against variations in the interaction strength, trap potential, and temperature. These results are consistent with previous theoretical and experimental studies.

The studies of the Higgs mode in inhomogeneous systems

Our study in this paper belongs to the static approach.

Specifically, we present the formulation for a trapped

our approach is based on quantum field theory.
Aiming for investigating the Higgs mode in the inhomogeneous superfluid

equations reproduce, in the homogeneous limit,
the frequencies of the two modes,
obtained analytically in homogeneous systems.
In addition, we perform numerical calculations to determine
the frequency of the Higgs mode in inhomogeneous
systems confined to harmonic traps.

II
System and formulation

We investigate a trapped three-dimensional superfluid
Fermi gas with equal populations of
the two spin components, described by the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}^{\dagger}H_{0}\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}-g\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\,\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow},

(1)

where
\uparrow(\downarrow),\mathbf{r}
,
t
and
g
are
the up (down) spin, position in space, time
and coupling constant, respectively.
The Fermi field operators

\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}\equiv\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r},t)

satisfy the following anticommutation relations

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r},t),\hat{\psi}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t)\right\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\delta(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),

(2)

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r},t),\hat{\psi}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r},t),\hat{\psi}_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t)\right\}=0.

(3)

The single-particle Hamiltonian is

H_{0}=-\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}/2m+V_{\text{trap}}(\mathbf{r})-\mu
,
where
m,V_{\text{trap}}(\mathbf{r})
and
\mu
are
the atomic mass, trapping potential and
chemical potential, respectively.

We perform the Hartree-Fock approximation with
the superfluid order parameter, and decompose
{\hat{H}}

into the unperturbed and perturbed Hamiltonians,
denoted by
{\hat{H}}_{\rm u}
and
{\hat{H}}_{\rm p}
, respectively,

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{u}+\hat{H}_{p},

(4)

\hat{H}_{u}=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}&\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}(x)&\Delta(x)\\
\Delta^{*}(x)&-\mathcal{L}(x)\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}\\
\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\right],

(5)

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{p}&=-g\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\,\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}\\
&\quad+\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}&\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}gn(x)&-\Delta(x)\\
-\Delta^{*}(x)&-gn(x)\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}\\
\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\right].\end{split}

(6)

Here
x
stands for
({\bm{x}},t)
,
the order parameter
\Delta(x)
and the density function
n(x)

are defined as

\displaystyle\Delta(x)

\displaystyle=-g\Braket{\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}(x)\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}(x)},

(7)

\displaystyle n(x)

\displaystyle=\Braket{\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}(x)}=\Braket{\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}(x)},

(8)

and the symbol
{\mathcal{L}}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(x)=H_{0}-gn(x)\,.

(9)

In the interaction picture, the unperturbed
Hamiltonian leads to the Heisenberg equation

i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}\\
\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}(x)&\Delta(x)\\
\Delta^{*}(x)&-\mathcal{L}(x)\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}\\
\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}\end{matrix}\right].

(10)

In a neutral Fermi gas, the normal-superfluid phase transition is
associated with the spontaneous breaking of the global
U(1)
symmetry.
The fluctuations around the steady state of Fermi superfluid
give rise to the two bosonic collective modes.
They are the Higgs and NG modes, emerging as the amplitude and phase
oscillations, respectively
Pekker and Varma 2015
;
Beekman et al. 2019
;
Shimano and Tsuji 2020
;
Tsuji et al. 2024
.
We therefore consider a situation where the steady state of Fermi superfluid is perturbed.

II.1
Steady state

Assuming that the system is in a steady state and
the physical quantities are time-independent,
we obtain the eigenvalue problem

\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})&\Delta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
\Delta^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})&-\mathcal{L}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]=E_{\nu}\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right],

(11)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=H_{0}-gn^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}).

(12)

Due to the Hermitian property,
the eigenvalue
E_{\nu}
is real.
Here, the superscript
(0)

represents the stationary solutions.
Equation (
11
),
which is known as
the time-independent Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) equations,
has a conjugate solution for each eigenvalue
E_{\nu}
,
which stems from the particle-hole symmetry,

\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})&\Delta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
\Delta^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})&-\mathcal{L}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}-v_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})\\
u_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]=-E_{\nu}\left[\begin{matrix}-v_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})\\
u_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right].

(13)

In what follows, we restrict our analysis to
the positive eigenvalues
E_{\nu}
,
as the negative eigenvalues correspond to
conjugate solutions.

We expand the Fermi field operators in terms of
the eigenfunctions

\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}(x)\\
\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}(x)\end{matrix}\right]=\sum_{\nu>0}\left(\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]\hat{A}_{\nu}(t)+\left[\begin{matrix}-v_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})\\
u_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]\hat{B}_{\nu}^{\dagger}(t)\right),

(14)

where
\sum_{\nu>0}
denots the sum over all
positive integers.
The annihilation operators

\hat{A}_{\nu}(t)
and
\hat{B}_{\nu}(t)
represent
the quasiparticles and quasiholes, respectively.
For simplicity,
we associate the negative eigenvalues,
their corresponding eigenvectors, and
the quasiholes with negative integers

\left[\begin{matrix}u_{-\nu}^{(0)}\\
v_{-\nu}^{(0)}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}-v_{\nu}^{(0)*}\\
u_{\nu}^{(0)*}\end{matrix}\right],\quad E_{-\nu}=-E_{\nu},\quad\hat{A}_{-\nu}(t)=\hat{B}_{\nu}^{\dagger}(t).

(15)

The Fermi operator
\hat{A}_{\nu}(t)
satisfies the
anticommutation relations

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{A}_{\mu}(t),\hat{A}_{\nu}^{\dagger}(t)\right\}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu\nu},

(16)

\displaystyle\left\{\hat{A}_{\mu}(t),\hat{A}_{\nu}(t)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\hat{A}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(t),\hat{A}_{\nu}^{\dagger}(t)\right\}=0.

(17)

where
\mu
and
\nu
represent certain nonzero
integers.
Using the orthonormality of the eigenfunctions

\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left(u_{\mu}^{(0)*}u_{\nu}^{(0)}+v_{\mu}^{(0)*}v_{\nu}^{(0)}\right)=\delta_{\mu\nu},

(18)

we obtain

\hat{H}_{u}=\sum_{\nu}E_{\nu}\hat{A}_{\nu}^{\dagger}(t)\hat{A}_{\nu}(t),

(19)

where
\sum_{\nu}
denotes the sum over all
nonzero integers.
From the Heisenberg equation

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{A}_{\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=\left[\hat{A}_{\nu}(t),\hat{H}_{u}\right]=E_{\nu}\hat{A}_{\nu}(t),

(20)

we obtain

\hat{A}_{\nu}(t)=e^{-iE_{\nu}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_{\nu}(0)
.
The Fermi field operators are expanded as

\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}(x)\\
\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}(x)\end{matrix}\right]=\sum_{\nu}\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]e^{-iE_{\nu}t/\hbar}\hat{A}_{\nu}(0).

(21)

We can confirm that the Fermi
field operator (
21
)
satisfies the Heisenberg
equation (
10
)
by using the time-independent BdG equations
(
11
).

The Fermi operator
\hat{A}_{\nu}(0)
allows
the explicit calculation of the
order parameter and density function

\displaystyle\Delta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=-g\sum_{\nu}u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})v_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})f_{\nu},

(22)

\displaystyle n^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu}\left|u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\right|^{2}f_{\nu},

(23)

where

\Braket{\hat{A}_{\mu}^{\dagger}(0)\hat{A}_{\nu}(0)}=\delta_{\mu\nu}f_{\nu},\quad\Braket{\hat{A}_{\mu}(0)\hat{A}_{\nu}^{\dagger}(0)}=\delta_{\mu\nu}(1-f_{\nu}),

(24)

and
f_{\nu}
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution

f_{\nu}\equiv f(E_{\nu})=\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta E_{\nu}}},\quad f_{-\nu}=1-f_{\nu}.

(25)

The stationary solutions

\{u_{\nu}^{(0)},v_{\nu}^{(0)},E_{\nu}\}
can be
obtained by solving the time-independent BdG equations
(
11
), the order parameter
(
22
), and the density function (
23
)
self-consistently.

II.2
Perturbations around steady state

The Fermi field operators
\hat{\psi}_{\sigma}(x)

are expanded
in terms of
\hat{A}_{\nu}(0)
as follows:

\left[\begin{matrix}\hat{\psi}_{\uparrow}(x)\\
\hat{\psi}_{\downarrow}^{\dagger}(x)\end{matrix}\right]=\sum_{\nu}\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}(x)\\
v_{\nu}(x)\end{matrix}\right]\hat{A}_{\nu}(0),

(26)

where
u_{\nu}(x)
and
v_{\nu}(x)
at each time
t

satisfy the time-dependent BdG equations

i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}(x)\\
v_{\nu}(x)\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}(x)&\Delta(x)\\
\Delta^{*}(x)&-\mathcal{L}(x)\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}(x)\\
v_{\nu}(x)\end{matrix}\right],

(27)

with

\displaystyle\Delta(x)

\displaystyle=-g\sum_{\nu}u_{\nu}(x)v_{\nu}^{*}(x)f_{\nu},

(28)

\displaystyle n(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu}\left|u_{\nu}(x)\right|^{2}f_{\nu}.

(29)

The dynamics of Fermi superfluids is described
by the time-dependent BdG equations
within the Hartree-Fock approximation

de Gennes 1966
;
Challis et al. 2007
.

We consider small fluctuations around
stationary solutions

\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}(x)\\
v_{\nu}(x)\end{matrix}\right]=\left(\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}\delta u_{\nu}(x)\\
\delta v_{\nu}(x)\end{matrix}\right]\right)e^{-iE_{\nu}t/\hbar},

(30)

\left[\begin{matrix}\Delta(x)\\
n(x)\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\Delta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
n^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\Delta(x)\\
\delta n(x)\end{matrix}\right],

(31)

where

\displaystyle\delta\Delta(x)

\displaystyle=-g\sum_{\nu}\left(u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\delta v_{\nu}^{*}(x)+v_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})\delta u_{\nu}(x)\right)f_{\nu},

(32)

\displaystyle\delta n(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu}\left(u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\delta u_{\nu}^{*}(x)+u_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})\delta u_{\nu}(x)\right)f_{\nu}.

(33)

Here, the symbol
\delta
represents a small deviation.
Expanding the time-dependent
BdG equations (
27
) in order of
\delta
,
we obtain the time-independent
BdG equations (
11
)
from the zeroth order and
linearized equations for the fluctuations

\begin{split}&\left(i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+E_{\nu}\right)\left[\begin{matrix}\delta u_{\nu}(x)\\
\delta v_{\nu}(x)\end{matrix}\right]\\
&=\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})&\Delta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
\Delta^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})&-\mathcal{L}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\delta u_{\nu}(x)\\
\delta v_{\nu}(x)\end{matrix}\right]\\
&\quad+\left[\begin{matrix}-g\delta n(x)&\delta\Delta(x)\\
\delta\Delta^{*}(x)&g\delta n(x)\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(34)

up to the first order of
\delta
.
We express the order parameter in polar form
and introduce amplitude and phase fluctuations as

\begin{split}&\Delta(x)\\
&=|\Delta(x)|e^{i\theta(x)}\\
&=\left(\rho^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})+\delta\rho(x)\right)e^{i(\theta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})+\delta\theta(x)/\rho^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}))}\\
&=\rho^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})e^{i\theta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})}+e^{i\theta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})}\Big(\delta\rho(x)+i\delta\theta(x)\Big)+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}).\end{split}

(35)

Here,
\rho
and
\theta
represent
the amplitude and phase, respectively.
Equation (
31
) and (
35
) imply

\Delta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})=\rho^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})e^{i\theta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})},

(36)

\delta\Delta(x)=e^{i\theta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})}\Big(\delta\rho(x)+i\delta\theta(x)\Big).

(37)

Expanding
[\delta u_{\nu}\,\,\delta v_{\nu}]^{T}

in terms of the eigenfunctions of
the time-independent BdG equations

\left[\begin{matrix}\delta u_{\nu}(x)\\
\delta v_{\nu}(x)\end{matrix}\right]=\sum_{\rho}c_{\nu\rho}(t)\left[\begin{matrix}u_{\rho}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\\
v_{\rho}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right],

(38)

we obtain

\begin{split}&\left(i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+E_{\nu}-E_{\rho}\right)c_{\nu\rho}(t)\\
&=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left(W_{\rho\nu}^{*}(\mathbf{r})+W_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})\right)\delta\rho(x)\\
&+\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left(W_{\rho\nu}^{*}(\mathbf{r})-W_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})\right)i\delta\theta(x)\\
&+\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\,T_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})\big(-g\delta n(x)\Big),\end{split}

(39)

\begin{split}\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(x)\\
i\delta\theta(x)\\
-gn(x)\end{matrix}\right]&=-\frac{g}{2}\sum_{\nu,\rho}\Big(f_{\nu}c_{\nu\rho}(t)+f_{\rho}c_{\rho\nu}^{*}(t)\Big)\\
&\quad\times\left[\begin{matrix}W_{\rho\nu}(\mathbf{r})+W_{\nu\rho}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\\
W_{\rho\nu}(\mathbf{r})-W_{\nu\rho}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\\
U_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(40)

where

\displaystyle W_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})v_{\rho}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})e^{-i\theta^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})},

(41)

\displaystyle U_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=2u_{\nu}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})u_{\rho}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}),

(42)

\displaystyle T_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=u_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})u_{\rho}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r})-v_{\nu}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r})v_{\rho}^{(0)*}(\mathbf{r}).

(43)

We focus on the Higgs and NG modes through the
two-mode approximation

Dutta and Mueller 2017

\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(x)\\
\delta\theta(x)\\
\delta n(x)\\
c_{\nu\rho}(t)\end{matrix}\right]\approx\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\mathbf{r})\\
\delta\theta(\mathbf{r})\\
\delta n(\mathbf{r})\\
c_{\nu\rho}^{(+)}\end{matrix}\right]e^{-i\omega t}+\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho^{*}(\mathbf{r})\\
\delta\theta^{*}(\mathbf{r})\\
\delta n^{*}(\mathbf{r})\\
c_{\nu\rho}^{(-)*}\end{matrix}\right]e^{i\omega t},

(44)

where
\omega
is real.
The two-mode approximation
describes a system using only two dominant
modes to keep essential physics
while simplifying the analysis.
Substituting Eq. (
44
)
into Eq. (
39
) and (
40
),
we obtain

\begin{split}&\Big(E_{\nu}-E_{\rho}+\hbar\omega\Big)c_{\nu\rho}^{(+)}\\
&=-\Big(E_{\nu}-E_{\rho}+\hbar\omega\Big)c_{\rho\nu}^{(-)*}\\
&=\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}\left[\begin{matrix}\big\{\mathcal{P}_{\nu\rho}^{1}(\mathbf{r})\big\}^{*}&\big\{\mathcal{P}_{\nu\rho}^{2}(\mathbf{r})\big\}^{*}&T_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\mathbf{r})\\
i\delta\theta(\mathbf{r})\\
-g\delta n(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(45)

\begin{split}\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\mathbf{r})\\
i\delta\theta(\mathbf{r})\\
-gn(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]&=-\frac{g}{2}\sum_{\nu,\rho}\Big(f_{\nu}c_{\nu\rho}^{(+)}+f_{\rho}c_{\rho\nu}^{(-)*}\Big)\\
&\quad\times\left[\begin{matrix}W_{\rho\nu}(\mathbf{r})+W_{\nu\rho}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\\
W_{\rho\nu}(\mathbf{r})-W_{\nu\rho}^{*}(\mathbf{r})\\
U_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(46)

where

\mathcal{P}_{\nu\rho}^{i}(\mathbf{r})=W_{\rho\nu}(\mathbf{r})+(-1)^{i+1}W_{\nu\rho}^{*}(\mathbf{r}).

(47)

Thus, the desired integral equations are given by

\begin{split}\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\mathbf{r})\\
i\delta\theta(\mathbf{r})\\
-g\delta n(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]&=-\frac{g}{2}\sum_{\nu,\rho}\int d^{3}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\frac{f_{\nu}-f_{\rho}}{E_{\nu}-E_{\rho}+\hbar\omega}\\
&\quad\times\mathbf{P}_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\\
i\delta\theta(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\\
-g\delta n(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(48)

where

\begin{split}&\mathbf{P}_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\\
&=\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{P}_{\nu\rho}^{1}(\mathbf{r})\\
\mathcal{P}_{\nu\rho}^{2}(\mathbf{r})\\
U_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r})\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\big\{\mathcal{P}_{\nu\rho}^{1}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\big\}^{*}&\big\{\mathcal{P}_{\nu\rho}^{2}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\big\}^{*}&T_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(49)

The integral equations (
48
)
incorporate the spatial dependence of the
trapped Fermi superfluids.
These equations admit nontrivial solutions corresponding
to the Higgs and NG modes when
\delta\rho
and

\delta\theta
are nonzero, respectively.
In the following sections, we solve the integral
equations (
48
) to obtain these
collective modes.

III
Solving integral equation

This section first presents analytical results
for homogeneous systems and then provides numerical
results for inhomogeneous systems.

III.1
Homogeneous system

By applying the integral equations (
48
)
to a homogeneous system,
we confirm the existence of nontrivial solutions
corresponding to the Higgs and NG modes.
By setting
V_{\text{trap}}=\delta n=0
,
we rewrite the integral equations (
48
)
by performing the Fourier transformation as

\begin{split}\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\mathbf{k})\\
i\delta\theta(\mathbf{k})\end{matrix}\right]&=-\frac{g}{2V}\sum_{\mathbf{p},\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}}\frac{f(E_{\sigma_{+}})-f(E_{\sigma_{-}})}{E_{\sigma_{+}}-E_{\sigma_{-}}+\hbar\omega}\\
&\quad\times\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{1}\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{1}&\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{1}\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{2}\\
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{1}&\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{2}\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\mathbf{k})\\
i\delta\theta(\mathbf{k})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(50)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{\sigma_{+}\sigma_{-}}^{i}&=\left\{\begin{matrix}u_{\mathbf{p}_{-}}\\
-v_{\mathbf{p}_{-}}\end{matrix}\right\}^{\sigma_{-}}\left\{\begin{matrix}v_{\mathbf{p}_{+}}\\
u_{\mathbf{p}_{+}}\end{matrix}\right\}^{\sigma_{+}}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{i+1}\left\{\begin{matrix}u_{\mathbf{p}_{+}}\\
-v_{\mathbf{p}_{+}}\end{matrix}\right\}^{\sigma_{+}}\left\{\begin{matrix}v_{\mathbf{p}_{-}}\\
u_{\mathbf{p}_{-}}\end{matrix}\right\}^{\sigma_{-}},\end{split}

(51)

with

E_{\sigma_{\pm}}=\{E_{\mathbf{p}_{\pm}},-E_{\mathbf{p}_{\pm}}\}^{\sigma_{\pm}}

and

\mathbf{p}_{\pm}=\mathbf{p}\pm\mathbf{k}/2
.
The BdG functions
\{u_{\mathbf{p}},v_{\mathbf{p}}\}

are given by

u_{\mathbf{p}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{p}}}{E_{\mathbf{p}}}\right)},\quad v_{\mathbf{p}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{p}}}{E_{\mathbf{p}}}\right)},

(52)

with
\xi_{\mathbf{p}}=\hbar^{2}\mathbf{p}^{2}/2m-\mu
and

E_{\mathbf{p}}=\sqrt{\xi_{\mathbf{p}}^{2}+|\Delta^{(0)}|^{2}}
.
By using the gap equation

1=\frac{g}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{p}}/2)}{2E_{\mathbf{p}}},

(53)

we obtain the following equation as
\mathbf{k}\to 0
:

\left[\begin{matrix}F(\omega)\left\{(\hbar\omega)^{2}-(2|\Delta^{(0)}|)^{2}\right\}&2\hbar\omega G(\omega)\\
2\hbar\omega G(\omega)&F(\omega)(\hbar\omega)^{2}\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\bm{0})\\
i\delta\theta(\bm{0})\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\bm{0},

(54)

where

\displaystyle F(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{p}}/2)}{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}\frac{1}{4E_{\mathbf{p}}^{2}-(\hbar\omega)^{2}},

(55)

\displaystyle G(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{g}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{p}}/2)}{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}\frac{\xi_{\mathbf{p}}}{4E_{\mathbf{p}}^{2}-(\hbar\omega)^{2}}.

(56)

The density of states can be approximated
as nearly constant. This approximation arises
from converting the summation into an integral
over
\xi_{\mathbf{p}}
,
and the integral range can be taken
symmetrically around
\xi_{\mathbf{p}}=0
.
Since the integrands of
F
and
G
are the
even and odd functions of
\xi_{\mathbf{p}}
,
respectively, we obtain

\left[\begin{matrix}(\hbar\omega)^{2}-(2|\Delta^{(0)}|)^{2}&0\\
0&(\hbar\omega)^{2}\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(\bm{0})\\
i\delta\theta(\bm{0})\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\bm{0},

(57)

with
G(\omega)\approx 0
and
F(\omega)\neq 0
.
Equation (
57
)
shows that
the Higgs mode
\omega=2|\Delta^{(0)}|/\hbar

and NG mode
\omega=0
result from
the amplitude and phase
fluctuations, respectively.
These results are consistent with the previous works

Anderson 1958
;
Littlewood and Varma 1981
;
Littlewood and Varma 1982
;
Kulik et al. 1981
;
Tsuji and Aoki 2015
;
Volkov and Kogan 1974
.
We note that

\omega=0
is an exact solution of the characteristic equation
for non-vanishing
G(\omega)

\operatorname{det}\left[\begin{matrix}F(\omega)\left\{(\hbar\omega)^{2}-(2|\Delta^{(0)}|)^{2}\right\}&2\hbar\omega G(\omega)\\
2\hbar\omega G(\omega)&F(\omega)(\hbar\omega)^{2}\end{matrix}\right]=0.

(58)

III.2
Inhomogeneous system

In this section, we solve the integral equations (
48
)
in an inhomogeneous system through numerical calculations,
with an isotropic harmonic potential as a typical example.
Assuming that the fluctuations depend only on
the radial coordinate in polar coordinates and setting
\delta n=0
,
we obtain

\begin{split}&\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(r)\\
i\delta\theta(r)\end{matrix}\right]=-\frac{g}{2}\sum_{nn^{\prime}l}\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}\int dr^{\prime}{r^{\prime}}^{2}\frac{f(E_{nl})-f(E_{n^{\prime}l})}{E_{nl}-E_{n^{\prime}l}+\hbar\omega}\\
&\times\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{P}_{nn^{\prime}l}^{1}(r)\\
\mathcal{P}_{nn^{\prime}l}^{2}(r)\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\big\{\mathcal{P}_{nn^{\prime}l}^{1}(r^{\prime})\big\}^{*}&\big\{\mathcal{P}_{nn^{\prime}l}^{2}(r^{\prime})\big\}^{*}\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(r^{\prime})\\
i\delta\theta(r^{\prime})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(59)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{nn^{\prime}l}^{i}(r)&=u_{n^{\prime}l}(r)v_{nl}^{*}(r)e^{-i\theta(r)}\\
&\quad+(-1)^{i+1}u_{nl}^{*}(r)v_{n^{\prime}l}(r)e^{i\theta(r)}.\end{split}

(60)

Here,
\{u_{nl},v_{nl},E_{nl}\}

satisfies

\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{L}_{l}(r)&\Delta(r)\\
\Delta^{*}(r)&-\mathcal{L}_{l}(r)\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}u_{nl}(r)\\
v_{nl}(r)\end{matrix}\right]=E_{nl}\left[\begin{matrix}u_{nl}(r)\\
v_{nl}(r)\end{matrix}\right],

(61)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{l}(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{d}{dr}-\frac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{m\omega_{\text{ho}}^{2}r^{2}}{2}-\mu-gn(r),

(62)

\displaystyle\Delta(r)

\displaystyle=-g\sum_{nl}\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}u_{nl}(r)v_{nl}^{*}(r)f(E_{nl}),

(63)

\displaystyle n(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{nl}\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}|u_{nl}(r)|^{2}f(E_{nl}).

(64)

The integers
n
and
l
are the radial and
azimuthal quantum numbers, respectively.
The frequency
\omega_{\text{ho}}

characterizes the strength of the trapping potential.

To perform numerical calculations,
we discretize the integral equation
(
59
) as

\begin{split}&\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(r_{i})\\
i\delta\theta(r_{i})\end{matrix}\right]\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{M}_{11}(r_{i},r_{j};\omega)&\mathcal{M}_{12}(r_{i},r_{j};\omega)\\
\mathcal{M}_{21}(r_{i},r_{j};\omega)&\mathcal{M}_{22}(r_{i},r_{j};\omega)\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\delta\rho(r_{j})\\
i\delta\theta(r_{j})\end{matrix}\right],\end{split}

(65)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{ij}(r,r^{\prime};\omega)&=-\frac{g}{2}\sum_{nn^{\prime}l}\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}\frac{f(E_{nl})-f(E_{n^{\prime}l})}{E_{nl}-E_{n^{\prime}l}+\hbar\omega}\\
&\quad\times\mathcal{P}_{nn^{\prime}l}^{i}(r)\big\{\mathcal{P}_{nn^{\prime}l}^{j}(r^{\prime})\big\}^{*}{r^{\prime}}^{2}\Delta r.\end{split}

(66)

Here,
M
and
\Delta r
are the number of spatial
divisions and resolution, respectively.
For simplicity,
we assume that the off-diagonal elements

\mathcal{M}_{12}
and
\mathcal{M}_{21}

are small enough to be approximated as zero,
as in the case of the uniform system.
To determine the frequency
\omega
of the Higgs mode
by numerical calculation,
we solve the following characteristic equation

\operatorname{det}\Big[\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{11}(\omega)\Big]=0,

(67)

where
\mathbf{I}
is the identity matrix and

\mathbf{M}_{ij}(\omega)=\left[\begin{matrix}\mathcal{M}_{ij}(r_{1},r_{1};\omega)&\cdots&\mathcal{M}_{ij}(r_{1},r_{M};\omega)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathcal{M}_{ij}(r_{M},r_{1};\omega)&\cdots&\mathcal{M}_{ij}(r_{M},r_{M};\omega)\end{matrix}\right].

(68)

An isotropic harmonic trapped system is characterized
by the scattering length
a_{S}
, the frequency of the
trapping potential
\omega_{\text{ho}}
, and
the temperature
T
.
The coupling constant
g
is calculated by the scattering length
a_{S}
.
For numerical calculations, the three independent parameters are
expressed in terms of three dimensionless parameters

1/a_{S}k_{F},1/a_{\text{ho}}k_{F}
and
T/T_{F}
.
Here,

a_{\text{ho}}=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega_{\text{ho}})}
,

k_{F}
, and
T_{F}
are
the harmonic oscillator length,
Fermi wave number, and Fermi temperature, respectively.
The Fermi wave number
k_{F}
and
Fermi temperature
T_{F}
are determined from the
Fermi energy
E_{F}
.

When calculating the time-independent
BdG equations (
61
)
and integral equations
(
59
)
numerically, all the sums in these equations
are limited by an energy cutoff
E_{c}>|E_{nl}|
.
This cutoff is necessary to prevent
the ultraviolet divergences, and regularizes
the coupling constant
g
according to
Ref.
Antezza et al. 2007

\frac{1}{g}=-\frac{m}{4\pi\hbar^{2}}\frac{1}{a_{S}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{m}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{3/2}\sqrt{E_{c}},

(69)

as explained in Appendix
A
.

In this paper, all the numerical calculations below are performed
under the fixed total number of perticles
N=20000
,
which is provided by

N=2\int d\mathbf{r}\,n(\mathbf{r}).

(70)

Here, the prefactor 2 accounts for spin degeneracy.
Specifically,
we determine the chemical potential
\mu
using the bisection method,
setting the convergence condition such that the difference
between the particle number obtained from Eq. (
70
)
and the target value is less than
10^{-3}

of the target value.

IV
Conclusions

We have derived integral equations that describe
Higgs and NG modes, and fully account for
the effect of external potential on Fermi
superfluids.
By applying these equations to homogeneous systems,
we analytically reproduce the Higgs and NG modes.
In inhomogeneous systems, the
Higgs mode emerges as twice the central value
of the order parameter through numerical calculations.
This feature always holds when each of the parameters

\omega_{\text{ho}},a_{S}
and
T
varies, respectively.
In addition, the frequency of the Higgs mode increases
as
\omega_{\text{ho}}
or
a_{S}
increase.
On the other hand,
the Higgs mode is temperature-independent in the low-temperature regime.

Further insights can be extracted from our theoretical framework,
such as the collective mode of density fluctuation
\delta n
, and
the coupling between the Higgs and NG modes mediated by
the off-diagonal elements
\mathcal{M}_{12}
and
\mathcal{M}_{21}
.
In the presence of relativistic Lorentz symmetry,
the Higgs and NG modes are
decoupled
Pekker and Varma 2015
,
whereas the Higgs and NG modes are mixed with each other
in cases of no Lorentz symmetry.
The analysis of these collective modes remains an
important direction for future work.

Appendix A
Cutoff dependence of order parameter

A cutoff is introduced to deal with the infinite sums
in numerical calculations, and the sums are truncated.
We consider the Schrödinger equation
describing the relative motion of
two Fermi particles

\left(\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{V}\right)\ket{\psi}=E\ket{\psi},

(71)

where

\hat{H}_{0}=\frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{2}}{m},\quad\hat{V}=-g\delta(\hat{\mathbf{x}}),

(72)

with the momentum operator
\hat{\mathbf{p}}
and
position operator
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
, respectively.
Using an incident wave
\ket{\phi}
with
the same energy as the state
\ket{\psi}
,
we obtain the Lippmann-Schwinger equation

\ket{\psi}=\ket{\phi}+\frac{1}{E-H_{0}}\hat{V}\ket{\psi}.

(73)

Equation (
73
) can be rewritten
by
T
-matrix

\hat{T}=\hat{V}+\hat{V}\frac{1}{E-H_{0}}\hat{T},

(74)

where
T
-matrix is defined

\hat{T}\ket{\phi}\equiv\hat{V}\ket{\psi}.

(75)

The
T
-matrix provides the expression for
the scattering amplitude

f(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k})\equiv-\frac{m}{4\pi\hbar^{2}}\braket{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|\hat{V}|\psi}=-\frac{m}{4\pi\hbar^{2}}\braket{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|\hat{T}|\mathbf{k}},

(76)

where
\ket{\phi}=\ket{\mathbf{k}}
and
we obtain

E=\hbar^{2}\mathbf{k}^{2}/m\equiv 2\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}
.
In the long-wavelength limit,
the scattering length is defined

a_{S}\equiv-\lim_{|\mathbf{k}|=|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|\to 0}f(\mathbf{k}^{\prime},\mathbf{k}).

(77)

The relation between the scattering length
and
T
-matrix is given by

\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}}{m}a_{S}=\braket{\mathbf{0}|\hat{T}|\mathbf{0}}.

(78)

Using the Lippmann-Schwinger equation
(
74
), we can extend the
right-hand side of Eq. (
78
)

\begin{split}\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}}{m}a_{S}&=-g+(-g)\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega_{c}}\frac{1}{0-2\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}(-g)\\
&\quad+(-g)\left(\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega_{c}}\frac{1}{0-2\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}(-g)\right)^{2}+\cdots,\end{split}

(79)

where
\omega_{c}
is the energy cutoff as the limit
of applicability of the mathematical model,
i.e., the Debye frequency of a superconductor.
The formula for the infinite series
provides

\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}}{m}a_{S}=\frac{-g}{1-\frac{g}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega_{c}}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}}.

(80)

Equation (
80
) is commonly used for
renormalization.
Replacement of the theoretical parameter
g
with
the experimental measurement
a_{S}
prevents
the divergence of the infinite series.
The right-hand side of the gap equation
(
53
)
does not converge in the limit as

\omega_{c}\to\infty
. Using Eq. (
80
),
we obtain

1=-\frac{4\pi\hbar^{2}}{m}\frac{a_{S}}{V}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}^{\omega_{c}}\left(\frac{\tanh(\beta E_{\mathbf{k}}/2)}{2E_{\mathbf{k}}}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}}\right).

(81)

The right-hand side of Eq. (
81
)
converges in the limit as
\omega_{c}\to\infty
,
and we can theoretically remove the energy limitation

\omega_{c}
.

On the other hand, in this paper,
we regard the theoretical cutoff
\omega_{c}

as the numerically necessary cutoff
E_{c}

and provide the coupling constant regularization
(
69
).
Figure
7
shows the numerical
results of the order parameter with the regularization
(
69
).
This regularization removes the cutoff dependence
of the order parameter.

Appendix C
Verification of NG mode

We verify the emergence of the NG mode
in an isotropic harmonic trapped system
by calculating the
following characteristic equation

\operatorname{det}\left[\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{M}_{22}(\omega)\right]=0.

(82)

However, it does not incorporate
an exact zero mode.

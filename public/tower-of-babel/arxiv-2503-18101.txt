Introduction

Let
A
be a finite subset of group
(G,\cdot)
. We say that an ordering
a_{1},\ldots,a_{|A|}
of
A
is
valid
if the partial products (or partial sums in additive notation)
p_{1}=a_{1},p_{2}=a_{1}\cdot a_{2},\ldots,p_{|A|}=a_{1}\cdot a_{2}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{|A|}
are all distinct. Moreover, this ordering is a sequencing if it is valid and
p_{i}\not=id
for any
i\in[1,|A|-1]
. In this case, we will say that
A
is sequenceable. In the literature, there are several conjectures about valid orderings and sequenceability. We refer to
[
8
,
12
,
14
]
for an overview of the topic,
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
]
for lists of related conjectures and
[
5
]
. Here, we explicitly recall just that of Graham, who conjectured that every set of non-zero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
has a valid ordering.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
10
]
and
[
9
]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime. Then every subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
has a valid ordering.

Until recently, the main results on this conjecture were for small values of
|A|
; in particular, in
[
7
]
, the conjecture was proved for sets
A
of size at most
12
.

The first result involving arbitrarily large sets
A
was presented last year by Kravitz
[
11
]
. He used a simple rectification argument to show that Graham’s conjecture holds for all sets
A
of size
|A|\leqslant\log p/\log\log p
. A similar argument was also proposed (but not published) by Will Sawin
[
15
]
in a 2015 MathOverflow post.

Then, in
[
4
]
, Bedert and Kravitz improved this upper bound to the following:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
p
be a large enough prime and let
c>0
. Then every subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
is sequenceable provided that

|A|\leqslant e^{c(\log p)^{1/4}}.

Graham’s conjecture over non-abelian groups was first studied by Ollis in
[
13
]
over dihedral groups. More recently, the authors in
[
5
]
presented a nice connection with rainbow paths and produced the asymptotic result that a subset
A
of a finite group
G
admits an ordering in which at least
(1-o(1))|A|
many partial products are distinct. Returning to dihedral groups, inspired by the result of Kravitz (
[
11
]
) on cyclic groups, Costa and Della Fiore proved in
[
6
]
that any subset
A
of the dihedral group
Dih_{p}\setminus\{id\}
(and in general, of a class of semidirect products) is sequenceable, provided that
p>\frac{|A|!}{2}
. Note that this is slightly better than
|A|\leq\log p/\log\log p
.
This paper is dedicated to proving, with a similar procedure as that of Bedert and Kravitz, that subsets of the dihedral group
Dih_{p}\setminus\{id\}
are sequenceable if they are not too large with respect to
p
. In this setting, we improve the results of
[
6
]
on dihedral groups, obtaining essentially the same bound as in the case of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. That is,

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p
be a large enough prime and let
c>0
. Then every subset
A\subseteq Dih_{p}\setminus\{id\}
is sequenceable provided that

|A|\leqslant e^{c(\log p)^{1/4}}.

Proof Strategy

We offer a brief summary of the main steps of our proof of Theorem
1.3
. Note that we represent elements in
Dih_{p}
as
(x_{i},a_{i})
where
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

We begin Section
3
by describing how a small set
B\subseteq Dih_{p}
can be “rectified” such that all of its
\mathbb{F}_{p}
components are contained within a small interval. Then, for a nonempty subset
A\subseteq Dih_{p}\setminus\{id\}
, we prove the existence of an automorphism
\phi\in Aut(Dih_{p})
such that
\phi(A)=E\cup\left(\cup_{j=1}^{s}D_{j}\right)
, where each
D_{j}
is a dissociated set with size a multiple of eight and where the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
components of
E
are in a small interval by our rectification argument. Moreover, we can impose that each dissociated set
D_{j}
has
\mathbb{F}_{2}
components either constantly-one, (in this case we say that
j\in L_{1}
) or constantly 0 (
j\in L_{0}
). Here we split each
D_{j}
with
j\in L_{1}
into an odd-position subset
D^{o}_{j}
and an even-position subset
D^{e}_{j}
of about equal size. Then the product
\tau_{j}
of its elements, with elements of
D^{o}_{j}
in odd position and elements of
D^{e}_{j}
in even position, does not depend on the ordering of those elements. This means that, even if
Dih_{p}
is non-abelian, we have a constant product
\delta
of the elements of the dissociated sets without fixing the ordering of all the elements.
We then relabel
\phi(A)
by
A
for ease of notation.

In Section
4
, we order the elements of
E
. Here we split
E
into sets
P
(with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
components positive and
\mathbb{F}_{2}
components constantly 0),
N
(with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
components negative and
\mathbb{F}_{2}
components constantly-zero), and
S
(with
\mathbb{F}_{2}
components constantly-one). We further split
S
into subsets
S_{e}
and
S_{o}
of almost equal size, where the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
component of each element of
S_{e}
is larger than the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
components of all of the elements in
S_{o}
. We then prove the existence of an ordering
\mathbf{x}
of the elements of
P,N,S_{e},
and
S_{o}
such that
\delta,\mathbf{x}
is a sequencing, where
\delta
is the prespecified product of the elements of the
D_{j}
s. At the same time, we derive an important bound on the size of overlap between subsets of
Dih_{p}
and {
\delta
times the partial products of
\mathbf{x}
}.

Finally, in Section
5
of our paper, we describe a two-step method of ordering the
D_{j}
s. First, we randomly split each
D^{o}_{j}
, each
D^{e}_{j}
(when
j\in L_{1}
), and each remaining
D_{j}
(when
j\in L_{0}
) into a four-set partition.
Here, we use a probabilistic argument to prove that, given a sequencing
\mathbf{x}
of
E
, we can obtain a sequencing
\tau_{1},\dots\tau_{u}\mathbf{x}
of
E
and the products
\tau_{i}
of the partitions of the
D_{j}
s.
To conclude, we use again a probabilistic argument to prove that if
\mathbf{x}
satisfies a technical condition (on the intersection between suitable subsets and {

of the

This gives us Theorem

.

3
Structure Theorem

In this paper we will consider groups that are semidirect products
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}\rtimes_{\varphi}H
for some group homomorphism
\varphi:H\rightarrow Aut(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. We recall that the group operation is defined as

(x_{1},a_{1})\cdot_{G}(x_{2},a_{2})=(x_{1}+\varphi(a_{1})(x_{2}),a_{1}\cdot_{H}a_{2}).

Here the map

x\rightarrow\varphi(a_{1})(x)

is an automorphism of
(\mathbb{F}_{p},+)
. It is well known that the automorphisms of
(\mathbb{F}_{p},+)
are the multiplications and hence that there exists
\varphi_{a_{1}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that

\varphi(a_{1})(x)=\varphi_{a_{1}}x.

Thus for this kind of semidirect product, we can rewrite the group operation as

(x_{1},a_{1})\cdot_{G}(x_{2},a_{2})=(x_{1}+\varphi(a_{1})(x_{2}))\cdot_{G}(a_{1},a_{2})=(x_{1}+(\varphi_{a_{1}}x_{2}),a_{1}\cdot_{H}a_{2}).

Here we let
Dih_{p}
denote the dihedral group
\mathbb{F}_{p}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{F}_{2}
, where
\varphi(1)(x)=-x
and
\varphi(0)(x)=x
. In this case, the group operation is defined as

(x_{1},a_{1})\cdot_{G}(x_{2},a_{2})=(x_{1}+(\varphi_{a_{1}}x_{2}),a_{1}+a_{2})

where
\varphi_{0}=1
and
\varphi_{1}=-1
.

Given a non necessarily abelian group
G
, a subset
D=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}\}\subseteq G
is
dissociated
if

d_{\sigma(1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(1)}}\cdot\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(r)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(r)}}\neq id

for all permutations
\sigma\in Sym(r)
and
(\epsilon_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\epsilon_{\sigma(r)})\in\{-1,0,1\}^{r}\setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
.

The
dimension
of a subset
B\subseteq G
, written
\dim(B)
, is the size of the largest dissociated set contained in
B
.
We can characterize these maximal dissociated subsets as follows:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
B\subseteq G
be a finite subset of a non necessarily abelian group. If
D=\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}\}
is a maximal dissociated subset of
B
, then

B\subseteq\Span(D)\vcentcolon=\left\{d_{\sigma(1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(1)}}\cdot\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(r)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(r)}}:\sigma\in Sym(r),\epsilon_{\sigma(i)}\in\{-1,0,1\}\right\}.

Proof.

Due to the maximality of
D
, for every element
b\in B\setminus D
,
\{b\}\cup D
is not dissociated. This means that there exists an ordering
\sigma
and coefficients
\epsilon_{\sigma(i)}\in\{-1,0,1\}
such that

d_{\sigma(1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(1)}}\cdot\cdots d_{\sigma(i-1)}^{\epsilon(i-1)}\cdot b\cdot d_{\sigma(i)}^{\epsilon(i)}\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(r)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(r)}}=id.

It follows that we have

b=\left(d_{\sigma(1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(1)}}\cdot\cdots d_{\sigma(i-1)}^{\epsilon(i-1)}\right)^{-1}\left(d_{\sigma(i)}^{\epsilon(i)}\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(r)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(r)}}\right)^{-1}

and hence
b\in Span(D)
.
∎

Also, the following lemma relates the size of a set and its dimension.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
B
be a finite subset of
Dih_{p}
. Then

|B|\leqslant 3^{\dim(B)}
.

Proof.

Let
D={(x_{1},a_{1}),(x_{2},a_{2}),\dots,(x_{r},a_{r})}
be a maximal dissociated subset of
B
of size
dim(B)
. Due to the previous lemma, this means that any element
(x,a)
of
B
belongs to
Span(D)
and hence

(x,a)=d_{\sigma(1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(1)}}\cdot\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(t)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(t)}}

where
d_{i}=(x_{i},a_{i})\in D
,
\sigma\in Sym(r),\epsilon_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\epsilon_{\sigma(t)}\in\{-1,1\},
and
\epsilon_{\sigma(t+1)}=\cdots=\epsilon_{\sigma(r)}=0.

In components, this means

\displaystyle(x,a)

\displaystyle=\left(x_{\sigma(1)}+\varphi_{a_{\sigma(1)}}x_{\sigma(2)}+\dots+\varphi_{a_{\sigma(1)}+\cdots+a_{\sigma(t-1)}}x_{\sigma(t)},\sum_{i=1}^{t}a_{\sigma(i)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\gamma_{1}x_{1}+\gamma_{2}x_{2}+\dots+\gamma_{r}x_{r},\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{r}a_{r}\right)

where
(\gamma_{i},\lambda_{i})\in\{(0,0),(1,1),(-1,1)\}
. Since we have three choices for each pair
(\gamma_{i},\lambda_{i})
, it follows that
|B|\leqslant 3^{\dim(B)}
.
∎

Following
[
4
]
, we state a lemma that allows us to “rectify” small sets. We define, for each (nonempty) subset
A\subseteq Dih_{p}
, the parameter

\displaystyle R=R(A)\vcentcolon=c_{1}\max\left((\log p)^{1/2},\frac{\log p}{\log|A|}\right),

(1)

where
c_{1}
is a sufficiently small absolute constant.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
B\subseteq Dih_{p}
is a nonempty subset of dimension
\dim(B)<R=R(B)
, then there is some
\phi\in Aut(Dih_{p})
such that the image
\pi_{1}(\phi(B))
(where
\pi_{1}
is the projection over the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
component) is contained in the interval
(-\frac{p}{100|B|},\frac{p}{100|B|})
.

Proof.

Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we consider here the family of maps
\phi_{\lambda}:Dih_{p}\rightarrow Dih_{p}

defined as

\phi_{\lambda}(x,a)=(\lambda x,a).

Note that, if
\lambda\not=0
,
\phi_{\lambda}\in Aut(Dih_{p})
.

Let us now consider a maximal dissociated subset
D=\{(x_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(x_{r},a_{r})\}
of
B
, where
r=|D|=\dim(B)
. Lemma
3.1
tells us that
B\subseteq\operatorname{span}(D)
.

Consider the set

\{\lambda(x_{1},\dots,x_{r}):\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}\subseteq(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{r}.

Due to the pigeonhole principle, there exists distinct
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
\|\lambda_{1}x_{i}-\lambda_{2}x_{i}\|\leqslant p^{1-1/r}
for all
i\in[1,r]
. Set
\lambda\vcentcolon=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
, we have that
\phi_{\lambda}\in Aut(Dih_{p})
and
\pi_{1}(\phi_{\lambda}(D))\subseteq[-p^{1-1/\dim(B)},p^{1-1/\dim(B)}]
.
Since
B\subseteq\operatorname{span}(D)
, we have

\pi_{1}(\phi(B))\subseteq[-\dim(B)p^{1-1/\dim(B)},\dim(B)p^{1-1/\dim(B)}].

The thesis follows because we can prove, as done in
[
4
]
, that
\dim(B)p^{1-1/\dim(B)}<p/(100|B|)
as long as
c_{1}
is chosen to be sufficiently small.
∎

Before stating the structure theorem for dihedral groups, we prove the following Lemma, which allows us to modify a dissociated set.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a non necessarily abelian group, and let
D_{1}\cup D_{2}
be a partition of a dissociated subset
D
of
G
. For every element
x\in G\setminus\{0\}
, either
D_{1}\cup\{x\}
or
D_{2}\cup\{x\}
is dissociated.

Proof.

Let us set
D_{1}=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{t}\}
and
D_{2}=\{d_{t+1},\dots,d_{r}\}
.
Assume by contradiction that neither
D_{1}\cup\{x\}
nor
D_{2}\cup\{x\}
is dissociated. Since
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are dissociated, Lemma
3.1
tells us that

x\in Span(D_{1})\cap Span(D_{2}).

Therefore, we can write

x=d_{\sigma(1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(1)}}\cdot\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(t)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(t)}}=d_{\sigma(t+1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(t+1)}}\cdot\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(r)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(r)}}

and hence

(d_{\sigma(1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(1)}}\cdot\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(t)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(t)}})(d_{\sigma(t+1)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(t+1)}}\cdot\cdots\cdot d_{\sigma(r)}^{\epsilon_{\sigma(r)}})^{-1}=id

which implies the contradiction that
D
would not be a dissociated set.
∎

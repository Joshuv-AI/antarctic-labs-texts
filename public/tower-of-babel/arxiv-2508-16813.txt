On the supremum of random cusp forms
Bingrong Huang, Stephen Lester, Igor Wigman, Nadav Yesha

Abstract
. A random ensemble of cusp forms for the full modular group is introduced. For a weight- k k cusp form, restricted to a compact subdomain of the modular surface, the true order of magnitude of its expected supremum is determined to be ≍ log ⁡ k \asymp\sqrt{\log{k}} , in line with the conjectured bounds. Additionally, the exponential concentration of the supremum around its median is established. Contrary to the compact case, it is shown that the global expected supremum, which is attained around the cusp, grows like k 1 / 4 k^{1/4} , up to a logarithmic factor.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Random cusp forms

For
k\geq 2
even let
{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
be the space of weight-
k
cusp forms for the full modular group
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of dimension
N=N_{k}=\dim{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
. It is well-known that
N_{k}=\frac{k}{12}+O(1)
and that
N_{k}
is explicitly given in terms of

\lfloor\frac{k}{12}\rfloor
, depending on
k\mod 12
.
Naturally, a weight-
k
cusp form
f\in{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
is an analytic function
f:\mathscr{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
where

\mathscr{D}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\>|\Re(z)|\leq\frac{1}{2},|z|\geq 1\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{2}

is the canonical (closed) fundamental domain for the action of
\Gamma
on the Poincaré half-plane model

\mathbb{H}^{2}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\>\Im{z}>0\}

of hyperbolic geometry. The space
{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
is endowed with
the Petersson inner product

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{PS}=\int\limits_{\mathscr{D}}f(z)\cdot\overline{g(z)}y^{k}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}},

where
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, and
\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}
is the standard hyperbolic measure on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

Given
k
, let
\pazocal{B}_{k}=\{f_{1}=f_{k;1},\ldots,f_{N}=f_{k;N}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{PS}
. A random weight-
k
cusp form is a random analytic functions
g_{k}:\mathscr{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

defined as

(1.1)

g_{k}(z)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}f_{j}(z),

with the coefficients
(a_{j})_{j\leq N}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
drawn uniformly on the unit sphere

(1.2)

{\mathscr{S}}^{2N-1}_{\mathbb{C}}=\left\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:\>\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}|^{2}=1\right\},

of real dimension
2N-1
. The law of
(
1.1
)
is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
\pazocal{B}_{k}
of
{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
, since choosing a different orthonormal basis amounts to performing an orthogonal transformation on
the vector
(a_{j})_{j}
, under which the uniform measure of
{\mathscr{S}}^{2N-1}_{\mathbb{C}}
is invariant.

1.2.
Statement of the principal result on compacta

Let
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
be a compact subdomain of positive area, that will be assumed to be fixed. We address the distribution of the random variable

(1.3)

\pazocal{M}_{k}=\pazocal{M}_{\mathscr{K};k}=\sup\limits_{z\in\mathscr{K}}y^{k/2}\cdot\left|g_{k}(z)\right|

for large
k
.
Our first principal result asserts that both the expectation and the median of
\pazocal{M}_{k}
grow at the rate
\sqrt{\log{k}}
, and the distribution of
\pazocal{M}_{k}
exponentially concentrates around the median:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g_{k}
be a random weight-
k
cusp form (
1.1
),
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
be a fixed compact subdomain of
\mathscr{D}
of positive area, and
\pazocal{M}_{k}
the random variable (
1.3
).

i.

The expectation of
\pazocal{M}_{k}
is

\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{M}_{k}]\asymp\sqrt{\log{k}},

i.e. there exist constants
c_{1}>c_{0}>0
, depending only on
\mathscr{K}
, so that

(1.4)

c_{0}\cdot\sqrt{\log{k}}\leq\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{M}_{k}]\leq c_{1}\cdot\sqrt{\log{k}}.

ii.

Let
\mu_{k}=\mu(\pazocal{M}_{k})
be the median of
\pazocal{M}_{k}
. Then

(1.5)

\mu_{k}=\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{M}_{k}]+O_{\mathscr{K}}(1),

and there exists some constant
c=c(\mathscr{K})>0
so that for every
r>0
one has

(1.6)

\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|\pazocal{M}_{k}-\mu_{k}\right|>r\right)\leq 2e^{-cr^{2}}.

Theorem
1.1
(ii.) shows that the growth rate of the median
\mu(\pazocal{M}_{k})
of
\pazocal{M}_{k}
is of order of magnitude
\asymp\sqrt{\log{k}}
, but, just like the expectation of
\pazocal{M}_{k}
, divided by
\sqrt{\log{k}}
it might fluctuate between
two constants, hence we could not replace
\mu_{k}
in the exponential concentration (
1.6
) with an analytic expression of
k
. Instead, a weaker statement, that is a direct consequence of
Theorem
1.1
, is asserted:

Corollary 1.2
.

There exist constants
c_{1}>c_{0}>0
and
c>0
, depending only on
\mathscr{K}
, so that for every
r>0
one has

\pazocal{P}r\left(\pazocal{M}_{k}>c_{1}\cdot\sqrt{\log{k}}+r\right)\leq 2e^{-cr^{2}}

and

\pazocal{P}r\left(\pazocal{M}_{k}<c_{0}\cdot\sqrt{\log{k}}-r\right)\leq 2e^{-cr^{2}}.

1.3.
Statement of the principal result for the global supremum

We note that if
f
is a modular form, defined on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, then the function

|y^{k/2}\cdot f(z)|

is invariant with respect to the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.
Our second principal result asserts upper
and lower bounds for the expected supremum of
y^{k/2}\cdot\left|g_{k}(z)\right|
on
\mathscr{D}
(or, what is equivalent in light of the said invariance, on the whole of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
).
Additionally, the distribution of the supremum exponentially concentrates around the median:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k}=\sup\limits_{z\in\mathscr{D}}y^{k/2}\cdot\left|g_{k}(z)\right|,

where ‘g’ stands for “global”.
Then

i.

We have

(1.7)

k^{1/4}\ll\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k}]\ll k^{1/4}\sqrt{\log{k}},

where the constants involved in the “
\ll
”-notation are absolute.

ii.

Let
\mu^{g}_{k}=\mu(\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k})
be the median of
\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k}
. Then

(1.8)

\mu^{g}_{k}=\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k}]+O(k^{1/4}),

and there exists a constant
c>0
so that for every
r>0
one has

(1.9)

\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k}-\mu^{g}_{k}\right|>r\right)\leq 2e^{-\frac{cr^{2}}{k^{1/2}}}.

As a direct consequence, we obtain:

Corollary 1.4
.

There exist absolute constants
c_{1}>0
and
c>0
so that for every
r>0
one has

\pazocal{P}r\left(\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k}>c_{1}\cdot k^{1/4}\sqrt{\log{k}}+r\right)\leq 2e^{-\frac{cr^{2}}{k^{1/2}}}.

For two random variables (or random fields)

2.
Discussion

2.1.
Background

The study of the sup norm problem for cusp forms on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with large weight was initiated by Rudnick

[
21
, Proposition A.1]

who proved that the supremum of
f\in{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
restricted to a compact subset
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
satisfies

(2.1)

\sup_{z\in\mathscr{K}}y^{k/2}|f(z)|\ll_{\mathscr{K}}k^{1/2}\lVert f\rVert_{2},

where
\lVert f\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle_{PS}}
.
Over the entire domain (
\mathscr{D}
, or, what is equivalent,
\mathbb{H}^{2}
), Steiner
[
25
, Corollary 1.4]
proved that for
f\in{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
,

(2.2)

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{H}^{2}}y^{k/2}|f(z)|\ll k^{3/4}\lVert f\rVert_{2}.

These bounds are best possible for
f\in{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
, since given any fixed, compact
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
it follows from an argument of Sarnak

[
23
, p. 2-3]
, using also Proposition
3.1
(ii.), that there exists a cusp form
f
with

\sup_{z\in\mathscr{K}}y^{k/2}|f(z)|\gg_{\mathscr{K}}k^{1/2}\lVert f\rVert_{2}.

Near the cusp there are even larger values and Sarnak’s argument, using also

[
25
, Theorem 1.3]
,
shows that there exists a cusp form
f
with

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{H}^{2}}y^{k/2}|f(z)|\gg k^{3/4}\lVert f\rVert_{2}.

For a Hecke cusp form
f
and a
fixed compact subset
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
, one might predict that

(2.3)

\sup_{z\in\mathscr{K}}y^{k/2}|f(z)|\ll_{\mathscr{K}}k^{\varepsilon}

for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Xia
[
26
]
showed that if
f
is a Hecke cusp form, then

(2.4)

\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}}y^{k/2}|f(z)|\ll k^{1/4+\varepsilon}\lVert f\rVert_{2}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. That is nearly optimal, since Xia also proved for any Hecke cusp form
f
that

(2.5)

\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}}y^{k/2}|f(z)|\gg k^{1/4-\varepsilon}\lVert f\rVert_{2}

for any
\varepsilon>0
, where the maximal value of
f
is attained near the cusp at
\infty
, more precisely, at
y\approx\frac{k}{4\pi}
.
These global sup norm results for Hecke cusp forms are roughly comparable to the global sup norm for random cusp forms given in Theorem
1.3
.

2.2.
Discussion of results

2.2.1.
Almost sure bounds for the supremum

A straightforward application of the Borel-Cantelli lemma with the exponential probability tail decay of Corollary
1.2
implies that the supremum of
g_{k}
on compacta is
almost surely

O\left(\sqrt{\log{k}}\right)
, i.e. for every compact
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
there exists a constant
C=C(\mathscr{K})
so that the inequality

\sup_{z\in\mathscr{K}}y^{k/2}|g_{k}(z)|\leq C\cdot\sqrt{\log{k}}

holds with probability
1
,
assuming that the
g_{k}(\cdot)
are drawn independently
for different
k
. Hence, the presented results are in strong support to the heuristic ansatz (
2.3
).

For the global supremum, one may analogously infer the almost sure bound

\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k}=O\left(k^{1/4}\sqrt{\log{k}}\right)

from Corollary
1.4
.
Our analysis below shows that the
global
supremum of
y^{k/2}|g_{k}(z)|
is attained, with high probability,
around
\Im{z}\approx\frac{k}{4\pi}
,
consistent to Xia’s more restricted result (
2.4
)-(
2.5
), both in terms of the order of
magnitude
of the supremum,
and the
location
where it is attained.

2.2.2.
Spherical vs. Gaussian coefficients

Since the central object of study in this manuscript is the supremum of cusp forms across different regimes, it is natural to focus on cusp forms restricted to have unit
L^{2}
-Petersson norm. That is,
the coefficients
(a_{j})_{j\leq N}
are constrained to lie on the unit sphere (
1.2
) of real dimension
2N-1
. Accordingly, the model (
1.1
) of cusp forms where
the coefficients
(a_{j})
are uniformly distributed
on
{\mathscr{S}}^{2N-1}_{\mathbb{C}}
is the most immediate and natural choice.
Instead, one might study the supremum of

(2.6)

G_{k}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}f_{j}(z),

where the
b_{j}
are standard complex Gaussian i.i.d., or rather the supremum of
y^{k/2}|G_{k}(z)|
. The covariance kernel of
G_{k}(\cdot)
, coinciding with the covariance kernel of
g_{k}(\cdot)
, determines the Gaussian random field
G_{k}(\cdot)

(though not a non-Gaussian random field), hence, in principle, it is possible to recover any property of
G_{k}(\cdot)
solely in terms of its covariance.

One has the equality in law:

(2.7)

G_{k}(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pazocal{L}}}{{=}}\zeta\cdot g_{k}(z),

where
\zeta=\zeta_{N}>0
is a random variable, independent of
g_{k}(\cdot)
,
distributed according to the
\chi
distribution with
2N
degrees of freedom. For large
N
, the distribution of
\zeta
is highly concentrated at
1
, and
its mean is given precisely by

(2.8)

\mathbb{E}[\zeta]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(N+1/2)}{\Gamma(N)}=1+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right),

see e.g.
[
1
, p. 238]
. It is therefore easy to infer the results for the “spherical” random fields from their Gaussian counterparts (and vice versa).

The Gaussian approach will be invoked (in a somewhat primitive form) for the purpose of proving the lower bound for the expected global supremum of Theorem
1.3
(i.).
In fact, one way to establish the upper bounds for the expected suprema of Theorem
1.1
(i.)
and Theorem
1.3
(i.) is via Dudley’s
entropy
method
(see e.g.
[
2
, §1.3]
), which is applicable to Gaussian (real-valued) random fields and is not pursued in this manuscript.

The same results hold for the real-valued coefficients
a_{j}
(spherical or Gaussian), via the same analysis that mainly appeals to the covariance kernel restricted to the diagonal.

2.2.3.
Random sections of tensor powers of line bundles on Kähler manifolds

The model (
1.1
) of random cusp forms with i.i.d. coefficients
is a particular ensemble falling under the scope addressed in the literature on random sections of tensor powers of line bundles
on Kähler manifolds, compact and non-compact, see
[
9
, § 4]
. So far, the main focus of that line of research has been on the zeros of the said sections, in particular their
n
-point correlations,
see e.g.
[
6
,
24
]
for compact manifolds or
[
18
,
10
,
9
]
for non-compact manifolds, the references within, and their followups.
Though, formally, the said results on the zeros of random sections are not directly applicable on the model (
1.1
) of random cusp forms (rather, the Gaussian model),
their “perturbative” techniques seem to directly apply here, possibly in conjunction with the asymptotics for the covariance function of §
3
below, to yield the analogous results on the correlations of zeros of

g_{k}(\cdot)
.

To the best knowledge of the authors of this manuscript, the only result in the literature pertaining to the supremum of random sections is
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
, which asserts upper and lower bounds for the expected supremum
of random sections, in a vastly general situation, both in terms of the manifolds and the distribution of the coefficients analogous to the
a_{j}
in (
1.1
). In particular, their result

[
9
, Corollary 4.1]
is applicable to the model (
1.1
), yielding upper and lower bounds for the expected supremum in Theorem
1.1
(i.), in terms of
non-matching powers of
k
. It is likely that the
optimal
bounds in (
1.4
) could be extended beyond the spherical (equivalently, Gaussian) coefficients, for some class of non-Gaussian distributions,
by using the Central Limit Theorem, and the related techniques in probability theory. We leave this, the above, and other associated questions to be addressed elsewhere.

2.2.4.
Suprema of random waves on compact manifolds

Burq and Lebeau
[
7
]
considered the supremum of
random waves
on
compact
manifolds, i.e. linear combinations of Laplace eigenfunctions belonging to an energy window corresponding to an energy
\lambda
,
in the high energy limit
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. They proved the analogues
[
7
, Theorem 5]
of the results of Theorem
1.1
, with the expected supremum also scaling logarithmically as
\sqrt{\log{\lambda}}
.
The proofs of Theorem
1.1
and parts of Theorem
1.3
are inspired by the techniques presented in
[
7
]
, though having marked obstacles and novel ingredients, in particular, of arithmetic nature.
The exponential concentration of the supremum in
[
7
, Theorem 5]
has been recently refined by
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
.

For both
[
7
]
and this manuscript, the asymptotic analysis of the covariance kernel of the random ensemble, and, in particular, its restriction to the diagonal, is instrumental.
For the upper bound in (
1.4
), and, a forteriori the matching lower bound, our methods allow for the domain
\mathscr{K}
to polynomially expand with
k
. Specifically, it is possible to
establish the analogue of (
1.4
), where
\pazocal{M}_{k}
is the supremum of
y^{k/2}|g_{k}(z)|
on

\mathscr{D}\cap\left\{z\in\mathscr{D}:\>\Im{z}<C\cdot\sqrt{k}\right\},

with
C>0
an arbitrary constant,
instead of a fixed compact domain
\mathscr{K}
.

2.2.5.
Analysis of covariance kernel

An asymptotic treatment of the covariance kernel not constrained to the diagonal is also included, see Theorem
3.3
, and (
3.9
).
We believe it to be of independent interest, also allowing for some future applications, for example, for the purpose of a further deep analysis of the distribution of the supremum
\pazocal{M}_{k}
.

Our arguments show that the bulk of the contribution for the global supremum of Theorem
1.3
comes from a rectangle in
\mathscr{D}
at height
\approx\frac{k}{4\pi}
, corresponding
to the maximal variance of
y^{k/2}g_{k}(z)
, see the proof of the lower bound of Theorem
1.3
(i.) in §
5
.
A somewhat heuristic analysis of the covariance on that “island of maximal variance”, of length
1
and width
\approx\sqrt{k}
, suggests an upper bound of
k^{1/4}
for
the expected supremum of
y^{k/2}g_{k}(z)
on that island, and, possibly the
global
expected supremum. Thus, it is plausible that the lower bound in (
1.7
)
is, in fact, the true order of magnitude of
\mathbb{E}[\pazocal{M}_{k}^{g}]
.

2.3.
On the proofs of the main results

2.3.1.
Asymptotic analysis of the covariance kernel

Let
r_{k}(z,w)=\mathbb{E}[h_{k}(x)\cdot\overline{h_{k}(w)}]
be the covariance kernel of
h_{k}(z):=y^{k/2}g_{k}(z)
. Though
r_{k}(\cdot,\cdot)
does not determine the law of
h_{k}
,
it does so in the Gaussian case, which is intimately related to
h_{k}
(see the discussion in §
2.2.2
above).
The asymptotic analysis of
r_{k}(\cdot,\cdot)
in different regimes, performed within §
3
,
is central to the proofs of the main results. Our argument begins by relating
r_{k}(\cdot,\cdot)
to the Bergman kernel, see (
3.9
). The Bergman kernel has been widely studied and we defer discussion of the literature and previous results on this topic to §
3.2
.
Returning back to the proof, for
z,w\in\mathscr{D}
not too close modulo
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
Theorem
3.3
shows that the Bergman kernel is small, implying
r_{k}(z,w)
is small for such
z,w
. Next, for
z,w\in\mathscr{D}
which are close together and not too close to the cusp at infinity, we show that in the sum over
\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
defining the Bergman kernel (
3.7
) the contribution from the terms corresponding to
\gamma=\pm I
dominates unless
z
is nearby an elliptic point in which case there are additional terms arising from the stabilizer group of the elliptic point that must be considered.

Near the cusp at infinity a more delicate analysis of the Bergman kernel is needed and for simplicity we only consider the diagonal
z=w
. For
z\in\mathscr{D}
near the cusp at infinity, the main contribution to the sum defining the Bergman kernel (
3.7
) arises from the terms corresponding to the stabilizer group of infinity, i.e. the matrices
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&n\\
0&\pm 1\end{pmatrix}
where
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We evaluate the sum over these matrices using the Poisson summation formula in Lemma
3.5
and then analyze the resulting sum using Laplace’s method. This leads to precise upper and lower bounds for the Bergman kernel valid near the cusp at infinity, which are given in Theorem
3.4
.

2.3.2.
L^{p}
norm asymptotics and concentration

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is inspired by the techniques of Burq-Lebeau
[
7
]
. We use the sup norm bound (
2.1
) together with a standard concentration inequality on the sphere (Lévy’s inequality), to show that the expectation of the sup norm is close to its median
\mu_{k}
. The difference between the median
\mu_{k}
and the expected value of the sup norm is then bounded using this result, and this argument
establishes Theorem
1.1
(ii.). Upon using the sup norm bound (
2.2
) in the global case, the concentration in Theorem
1.3
(ii.) follows similarly.

Denoting the
L^{p}
norm by
\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{K}}
, the median
\mu_{k,p}
is shown to be close to
\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{K}}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}
which admits an exact formula in terms of the variance
r_{k}(z,z)
. This analysis gives an asymptotic formula for the expected value of
\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{K}}
, which is given Theorem
4.1
(i.). By a similar argument we obtain asymptotics for the expected value of
\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}}
, which is given in Theorem
4.1
(ii.).

In §
5
we relate the
L^{p}
norm of
h_{k}
to its sup norm. Combining the
L^{p}
norm estimates given in Theorem
4.1
with the estimates for
r_{k}(z,z)
in §
3
and choosing
p
to be proportional to
\log k
we obtain the upper bounds for the expected value of the sup norm of
h_{k}
stated in Theorem
1.1
(i.) and Theorem
1.3
(i.). The lower bound in Theorem
1.1
(i.) follows from a similar analysis.

While we were not able to extend this approach for the lower bound to the global case, the lower bound of Theorem
1.3
(i.) follows from a different argument: it is sufficient to find a single point
z_{k}
so that the variance
r_{k}(z_{k},z_{k})
of
h_{k}(z)=y^{k/2}\cdot g_{k}(z)
at
z=z_{k}
is of order of magnitude
k^{1/2}
. From the fact that for a (real or complex) Gaussian random variable
Z

it follows that

\mathbb{E}\left[|Z|\right]\gg\operatorname{Var}(Z)^{1/2},

we will be able to infer the same for the (non-Gaussian) random variable

via its Gaussianity connection explained in §
2.2.2
above.
That will give the lower bound

\mathbb{E}[|h_{k}(z_{k})|]\gg k^{1/4},

and hence the same for the sup norm of
h_{k}
on
\mathscr{D}
.

3.
Analysis of covariance kernels

A key ingredient of our techniques is the asymptotic analysis of the covariance kernel

(3.1)

r_{k}(z,w)=\mathbb{E}\left[h_{k}(z)\cdot\overline{h_{k}(w)}\right]

z=x+iy,\,w=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, of the random field

(3.2)

h_{k}(z):=y^{k/2}g_{k}(z),

in different regimes, corresponding to Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
respectively.
Since it is easy to check that the
a_{j}
are uncorrelated, and
\mathbb{E}[|a_{j}|^{2}]=\frac{1}{N}
for
j\leq N
, the covariance kernel is given by

(3.3)

r_{k}(z,w)=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}y^{k/2}f_{j}(z)\cdot v^{k/2}\overline{f_{j}(w)}.

Just like the law of
g_{k}(\cdot)
, the law of
h_{k}(\cdot)
, and, in particular, its covariance kernel, are independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
\pazocal{B}_{k}
of
{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
.

3.1.
Statement of results: Asymptotics of covariance kernels

The following proposition deals with the asymptotic behavior of the variance
r_{k}(z,z)=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}(z)\right|^{2}\right]
, as defined in (
3.1
) with
z=w
.

Proposition 3.1

(Variance within the bulk)
.

For
0<\delta<1
let
\eta_{\delta,{j}}
be the neighborhoods of the elliptic points

(3.4)

e_{1}=e^{\pi i/3},e_{2}=i,\text{ and }e_{3}=e^{2\pi i/3}

of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}

\eta_{\delta,j}=\{z\in\mathscr{D}:|z-e_{j}|\leq\delta\},

j=1,2,3
. Denote the subdomain

(3.5)

\pazocal{F}_{\delta}=\mathscr{D}\setminus\{\eta_{\delta,1}\cup\eta_{\delta,2}\cup\eta_{\delta,3}\}

of the canonical fundamental domain
\mathscr{D}
.
Then there exists
c_{0}>0
such that, uniformly on

, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}(z)\right|^{2}\right]=\frac{k-1}{4\pi N}+O\left(e^{-c_{0}\delta^{2}k}+ye^{-k/(17y^{2})}\right),

where
h_{k}(\cdot)
is as in (

).
Equivalently, one has uniformly on

(3.6)

y^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left|f_{j}\left(z\right)\right|^{2}=\frac{k-1}{4\pi}+O\left(ke^{-c_{0}\delta^{2}k}+yke^{-k/(17y^{2})}\right).

The next result provides nearly optimal bounds
for the variance
r_{k}(z,z)
throughout
\mathscr{D}
.

Proposition 3.2

(Uniform estimates for the variance)
.

Let
z=x+iy,w=u+iv\in\mathscr{D}
. Each of the following hold.

i.

Uniformly, we have for any

that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}(z)\right|^{2}\right]=r_{k}(z,z)\ll_{A}\begin{cases}1+\frac{y}{\sqrt{k}}&\text{ if }\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n-\frac{k-1}{4\pi y}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{k}\log k}{y},\\

ii.

If

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}(z)\right|^{2}\right]=r_{k}(z,z)\ll e^{-k/37}.

iii.

We have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}(z)\right|^{2}\right]=r_{k}(z,z)\gg\displaystyle\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }2\leq y\leq\frac{\sqrt{k}}{12\pi},\\
\frac{y}{\sqrt{k}}&\text{ if }\frac{\sqrt{k}}{12\pi}<y<\frac{k}{2\pi}\text{ and }\min_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big|\frac{k-1}{4\pi y}-n\big|\leq\frac{\sqrt{k-1}}{12\pi y}.\end{cases}

3.2.
Analysis on the bulk: Proof of Proposition

In what follows we are going to state an asymptotic result on the covariance function

in (

To this end, we will establish precise estimates for the Bergman kernel, which we introduce next.

For

of

For further background and results on the Bergman kernel, such as results for compact manifolds, we refer the reader

3.3.
Asymptotic analysis of the Bergman kernel: Proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

The strategy of the proof broadly follows the approach of Cogdell and Luo
[
8
]
.
For
z,w\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
let

u(z,w)=\tfrac{|z-w|^{2}}{4\Im{z}\cdot\Im{w}}
, which is related to

by the formula

[

Given

write

c&d\end{pmatrix}
we have

(3.10)

\begin{split}|\ell_{\gamma}(z,w)|=\frac{2\sqrt{vy^{\prime}}}{|\gamma z-\overline{w}|}=&\frac{2\sqrt{vy^{\prime}}}{((u-x^{\prime})^{2}+(v+y^{\prime})^{2}-(v-y^{\prime})^{2}+(v-y^{\prime})^{2})^{1/2}}\\
=&\frac{2\sqrt{vy^{\prime}}}{(|\gamma z-w|^{2}+4vy^{\prime})^{1/2}}=(1+u(w,\gamma z))^{-1/2}.\end{split}

Next note that
\ell_{\pm I}(z,w)=\tfrac{2i\sqrt{yv}}{z-\overline{w}}
. Also,
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\gamma\neq\pm I\end{subarray}}(1+u(w,\gamma z))^{-k/2}\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\gamma\neq\pm I\end{subarray}}(1+u(w,\gamma z))^{-k/2+2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\end{subarray}}(1+u(w,\gamma z))^{-2}.

Thus, we have

(3.11)

R_{k}(z,w)=2\bigg(\frac{2i\sqrt{yv}}{z-\overline{w}}\bigg)^{k}+O\bigg(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\gamma\neq\pm I\end{subarray}}(1+u(w,\gamma z))^{-k/2+2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\end{subarray}}(1+u(w,\gamma z))^{-2}\bigg).

To simplify the analysis below, we note that since
z,w\in\mathscr{D}
and

,
and we conclude that

Using (

(3.14)

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\end{subarray}}(1+u(w,\gamma z))^{-2}&\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\end{subarray}}(1+u(z,\gamma z))^{-2}\\
&\ll y+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-2n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
2^{n}\leq u(z,\gamma z)\leq 2^{n+1}\end{subarray}}1\ll y+y\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-n}\ll y.\end{split}

We will now consider separately the cases

and
y<2
.
If

and

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\\
\gamma\neq\pm I\end{subarray}}(1+u(w,\gamma z))^{-k/2+2}\leq\bigg(1+\frac{1}{8y^{2}}\bigg)^{-k/2}\leq e^{-k/(17y^{2})}

where we have used that

) and (

Next, consider the case

where

\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{4yy^{\prime}}\leq u(z,\gamma z)<4c_{0}\delta^{2}

Lemma 3.6

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}\\
m>(1+\Delta)\frac{\kappa}{Y}\end{subarray}}m^{\kappa}e^{-Ym}&\leq\frac{1}{Y^{\kappa+1}}\int_{(1+\Delta)\kappa}^{\infty}t^{\kappa}e^{-t}\,dt+E(\kappa,Y)\\
&\leq\left(\frac{\kappa}{Ye}\right)^{\kappa}\cdot\frac{2}{Y\Delta}\int_{(1+\Delta)\kappa}^{\infty}(-g_{1}^{\prime}(t))\exp(g_{1}(t))\,dt+E(\kappa,Y)\\
&=\left(\frac{\kappa}{Ye}\right)^{\kappa}\cdot\frac{2}{Y\Delta}\exp(g_{1}((1+\Delta)\kappa))+E(\kappa,Y).\end{split}

Noting
\log(1+t)-t\leq-t^{2}/4
for
0<t<1
,
it follows that

(3.22)

g_{1}((1+\Delta)\kappa)=\kappa(\log(1+\Delta)-\Delta)\leq-\Delta^{2}\kappa/4

for
0<\Delta<1
.
Hence,

(3.23)

\sum_{m>(1+\Delta)\frac{\kappa}{Y}}m^{\kappa}e^{-Ym}\leq\frac{1}{Y\Delta}\left(\frac{\kappa}{eY}\right)^{\kappa}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}\kappa\right)+E(\kappa,Y).

For
Y<(1+\Delta)\kappa
, using (
3.22
) we have

(3.24)

E(\kappa,Y)=\bigg(\frac{\kappa}{eY}\bigg)^{\kappa}\exp(g_{1}((1+\Delta)\kappa))\leq\left(\frac{\kappa}{eY}\right)^{\kappa}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}\kappa\right)\ll\frac{\kappa}{Y}\left(\frac{\kappa}{eY}\right)^{\kappa}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{4}\kappa\right),

and this bound for
E(\kappa,Y)
also holds in the range
Y\geq(1+\Delta)\kappa
since it is not hard to see that the r.h.s. of (
3.24
) is
\gg e^{-Y}
in this range.

Next, we assume
(1-\Delta)\kappa/Y>1
.
We use that
t^{\kappa}e^{-tY}
is strictly increasing on
(0,\kappa/Y)
and
g_{1}^{\prime}(t)\geq\Delta
for
0<t<(1-\Delta)\kappa
to see that

\begin{split}\sum_{1\leq m<(1-\Delta)\frac{\kappa}{Y}}m^{\kappa}e^{-mY}\leq&\frac{1}{Y^{\kappa+1}}\int_{0}^{(1-\Delta)\kappa}t^{\kappa}e^{-t}\,dt+\bigg((1-\Delta)\frac{\kappa}{Y}\bigg)^{\kappa}e^{-(1-\Delta)\kappa}\\
\leq&\bigg(\frac{\kappa}{Ye}\bigg)^{\kappa}\cdot\frac{1}{Y\Delta}\int_{0}^{(1-\Delta)\kappa}g_{1}^{\prime}(t)\exp(g_{1}(t))\,dt+\bigg((1-\Delta)\frac{\kappa}{Y}\bigg)^{\kappa}e^{-(1-\Delta)\kappa}\\
=&\bigg(\frac{1}{\Delta Y}+1\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\kappa}{Ye}\bigg)^{\kappa}\cdot\exp(g_{1}((1-\Delta)\kappa)),\end{split}

where in the last step we used that

g_{1}((1-\Delta)\kappa)=\kappa(\log(1-\Delta)+\Delta).

Noting that

g_{1}((1-\Delta)\kappa)=\kappa(\log(1-\Delta)+\Delta)\leq-\Delta^{2}\kappa/2

and using
\kappa/Y>1
we conclude that

(3.25)

\sum_{1\leq m<(1-\Delta)\frac{\kappa}{Y}}m^{\kappa}e^{-mY}\ll\bigg(\frac{1}{\Delta}+\kappa\bigg)\frac{1}{Y}\left(\frac{\kappa}{eY}\right)^{\kappa}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta^{2}}{2}\kappa\right).

Combining (
3.23
), (
3.24
), and (
3.25
) we complete the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Write
\kappa=k-1
and

. Also, let
g_{2}(t)=h_{2}(t)-h_{2}(\kappa/Y)
where

. By
Lemmas

and
3.6
with
\Delta=1/3
we have uniformly for

that

(3.26)

R_{k}(z,z)=\frac{2Y^{\kappa+1}}{\Gamma(\kappa+1)}\bigg(\frac{\kappa}{Ye}\bigg)^{\kappa}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|n-\frac{\kappa}{Y}|<\frac{\kappa}{3Y}\end{subarray}}\exp(g_{2}(n))+O(e^{-k/37}),

where we have used Stirling’s formula to bound the error term.
Notice that the sum is empty if

, this proves part (ii.).

It remains to prove parts (i.) and (iii.).
Using that
g_{2}(\tfrac{\kappa}{Y})=g_{2}^{\prime}(\tfrac{\kappa}{Y})=0
and

, we have for

\frac{-9Y^{2}}{8\kappa}\bigg(t-\frac{\kappa}{Y}\bigg)^{2}\leq g_{2}(t)\leq\frac{-9Y^{2}}{32\kappa}\bigg(t-\frac{\kappa}{Y}\bigg)^{2}.

Equation (

k^{-A}&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

for any

.
Using (

) in (
3.26
) along with Stirling’s formula we have for

that

R_{k}(z,z)\ll\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|n-\frac{\kappa}{Y}|<\frac{\kappa}{3Y}\end{subarray}}\exp(g_{2}(n)))+e^{-k/37}\ll\begin{cases}1+\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\kappa}}&\text{ if }\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n-\frac{\kappa}{Y}|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}\log\kappa}{Y},\\

which proves (i.) if

4.
L^{p}
norm estimates and concentration around the median: Proof of Theorem
1.1
(ii.) and Theorem
1.3
(ii.)

4.1.
General setting

Given a smooth bounded function
f:\mathscr{D}\to\mathbb{C}
and
p\geq 1
,
denote the
L^{p}
norm of
f
restricted to a subdomain
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
by

\left\|f\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}:=\left(\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\left|f\left(z\right)\right|^{p}\,\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\right)^{1/p}.

We also denote the sup norm on
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
by

\left\|f\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}=\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\left|f\left(z\right)\right|,

and the random fields

(4.1)

h_{k}\left(z\right):=y^{k/2}g_{k}\left(z\right),

as in (
3.2
).
Under the newly introduced notation, we have
\pazocal{M}_{k}=\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{K}}
and
\pazocal{M}^{g}_{k}=\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{D}}
.

In this section we prove the following result for the expected value of the
L^{p}
norm of
h_{k}
, which is given in terms of the covariance function
r_{k}
as defined in
(
3.1
). To state the result we define
r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

(4.2)

r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}(z)=r_{k}(z,z).

Theorem 4.1
.

Each of the following hold.

i.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a compact set with positive area. Then for any
p\geq 2

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{K}}\right]=\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{\mathscr{K}}}\sqrt{N}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2N-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2N-1}{2}\right)}\bigg)^{1/p}+O_{\mathscr{K}}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{p}}{k^{1/p}}\bigg).

ii.

For any
p\geq 2

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}}\right]=\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}}\sqrt{N}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2N-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2N-1}{2}\right)}\bigg)^{1/p}+O\big(k^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3}{2p}}\sqrt{p}\big).

4.2.
Some auxiliary results

Throughout this section we let
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
denote a subset of
\mathscr{D}
of positive area. Also, let
{L}=L(\mathscr{D}^{\prime},k)
be a positive real number such that

(4.3)

\left\|f\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\leq{L}\left\|f\right\|_{2}

holds uniformly for all
f\in{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
. For
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}=\mathscr{K}

the sup norm bound (
2.1
) implies that we may choose
{L}
so that

(4.4)

{L}\ll_{\mathscr{K}}k^{1/2}.

Additionally, using (
2.2
) shows for
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}=\mathscr{D}
we may take
{L}
so that

(4.5)

{L}\ll k^{3/4}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
g_{k}^{1},g_{k}^{2}\in{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
,
and denote
h_{k}^{1}\left(z\right)=y^{k/2}g_{k}^{1}\left(z\right)
,

h_{k}^{2}\left(z\right)=y^{k/2}g_{k}^{2}\left(z\right)
. Then
for every

p\geq 2
, we have

\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\leq{L}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{2}.

Proof.

We have

\left\|f\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left\|f^{\frac{2}{p}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}=\left\|f\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{2,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{p}}.

An application of this inequality on
h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}
gives

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\cdot\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{2,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{p}}\leq\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\cdot\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{p}}.

Substituting the sup norm bound (
4.3
) into (
4.6
) yields the desired bound.
∎

We now prove a concentration inequality for
\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}

around its median value, which we denote by
\mu_{k,p}=\mu\left(\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\right)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p\geq 2
.
For any
r>0
, we have

(4.7)

\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}-\mu_{k,p}\right|>r\right)\leq 2e^{-ckL^{\frac{4}{p}-2}r^{2}}

for some absolute constant
c>0
.

Proof.

Recall that by Lévy’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Proposition 1.3 and Theorem 2.3]
),
if
X:{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous function (with respect to the geodesic
distance on the sphere) with a Lipschitz constant
C>0
, then for any

r>0
we have

(4.8)

\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|X-\mu\left(X\right)\right|>r\right)\leq 2e^{-\left(2N-2\right)\frac{r^{2}}{2C^{2}}}.

In what follows we identify a function
h_{k}
with the point
\left(a_{j}\right)_{j\leq N}\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}

determined by its coefficients via (
4.1
) and (
1.1
).

For

p\geq 2,
define
X_{p}\left(h_{k}\right)=\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}
.
Then, by Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\left|X_{p}\left(h_{k}^{1}\right)-X_{p}\left(h_{k}^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\leq{L}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq{L}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\text{dist}\left(h_{k}^{1},h_{k}^{2}\right),

where we denoted by
\text{dist}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)

the geodesic distance on
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}
. Thus,
one has for every
r>0

\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}-\mu_{k,p}\right|>r\right)\leq 2\exp\bigg(-\left(2N-2\right)\frac{r^{2}}{2{L}^{2-\frac{4}{p}}}\bigg),

which proves (
4.7
) on recalling that
N=\frac{k}{12}+O(1)
. ∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
p\geq 2
.
We have

(4.9)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\right]=\mu_{k,p}+O\left(\frac{L^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}{\sqrt{k}}\right).

Proof.

By (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\right]-\mu_{k,p}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}}\left|\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}-\mu_{k,p}\right|\,d\left(\pazocal{P}r\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}-\mu_{k,p}\right|>r\right)\,dr

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ckL^{\frac{4}{p}-2}r^{2}}\,dr=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{ck}}L^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

so that (
4.9
) holds.

∎

We turn to proving Theorem

Proof of Theorem

(ii.).

Recall that a function

(a_{j})\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}

) and (
1.1

with some constant

4.8

for any

(4.10)

\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{K}}-\mu_{k}\right|>r\right)\leq 2e^{-\left(2N-2\right)\frac{r^{2}}{2C^{2}\cdot k}}\leq 2e^{-cr^{2}}

(ii.).

Now, by (

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{K}}\right]-\mu_{k}\right|

The estimate (

(ii.) follows.

For the global case, define

. Then by (

) we have

\displaystyle\left|X^{g}_{\infty}\left(h_{k}^{1}\right)-X^{g}_{\infty}\left(h_{k}^{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{\infty,\mathscr{D}}\leq Ck^{\frac{3}{4}}\left\|h_{k}^{1}-h_{k}^{2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ck^{\frac{3}{4}}\cdot\text{dist}\left(h_{k}^{1},h_{k}^{2}\right)

with some constant
C>0
. Thus, by (
4.8
),
for any

, we have

(4.11)

for some absolute constant

(ii.).

By (

∎

4.4.
The expected value of the
L^{p}
norm and the proof of Theorem
4.1

We first establish a precise formula for the expected value of

\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}
be as in (
4.2
). We have

\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}=\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\sqrt{N}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2N-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2N-1}{2}\right)}\bigg)^{1/p}.

Proof.

A simple calculation shows (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Appendix A]
),
that for every
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, we have

\pazocal{P}r\left(\left\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}:\>\left|a_{1}\right|>\cos\theta\right\}\right)=\left(\sin\theta\right)^{2N-3}.

Denote

v_{k}\left(z\right)=\left(y^{k/2}f_{j}\left(z\right)\right)_{j=1}^{N}

which is a vector of length

(4.12)

\left|v_{k}\left(z\right)\right|=y^{k/2}\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left|f_{j}\left(z\right)\right|^{2}}=\sqrt{N}\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}(z)}

by (
3.3
) and is nonzero in
\mathscr{D}
except for at most a finite number of points.
Letting
u=\left(a_{j}\right)_{j=1}^{N}\in{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}
, one has

h_{k}\left(z\right)=y^{k/2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}f_{j}\left(z\right)=\left|v_{k}\left(z\right)\right|\cdot\left\langle u,\frac{\overline{v_{k}\left(z\right)}}{\left|v_{k}\left(z\right)\right|}\right\rangle.

Then, by the invariance of the uniform measure on
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}
with respect to rotations,
one may assume that the vector
\frac{\overline{v_{k}\left(z\right)}}{\left|v_{k}\left(z\right)\right|}

is lying on the
z_{1}
axis if
v_{k}(z)\neq 0
. Hence, for every angle
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}

one has

\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|h_{k}\left(z\right)\right|>\left|v_{k}\left(z\right)\right|\cos\theta\right)=\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|z_{1}\right|>\cos\theta\right)=\left(\sin\theta\right)^{2N-3},

if
v_{k}(z)\neq 0
.
Since

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}\left(z\right)\right|^{p}\right]=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|h_{k}\left(z\right)\right|>\lambda\right)\,d\lambda,

and using (
4.12
)
we may deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}}\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\left|h_{k}\left(z\right)\right|^{p}\,\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}\,d\left(\pazocal{P}r\right)=\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}\left(z\right)\right|^{p}\right]\,\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|h_{k}\left(z\right)\right|>\lambda\right)\,d\lambda\,\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(\left|v_{k}\left(z\right)\right|\cos\theta\right)^{p-1}\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|h_{k}\left(z\right)\right|>\left|v_{k}\left(z\right)\right|\cos\theta\right)\left|v_{k}\left(z\right)\right|\sin\theta\,d\theta\,\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle=pN^{p/2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(\cos\theta\right)^{p-1}\left(\sin\theta\right)^{2N-2}\,d\theta\,\int_{\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}(z)^{p/2}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2}N^{p/2}\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}B\left(\frac{p}{2},\frac{2N-1}{2}\right)=\frac{p}{2}N^{p/2}\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2N-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2N-1}{2}\right)},

as claimed.
∎

We now bound the distance between
\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}

and the median
\mu_{k,p}=\mu\left(\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}\right)
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
p\geq 2
. We have

\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}=\mu_{k,p}+O\bigg(\frac{L^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{k}}\bigg).

Proof.

As before, we will think of
h_{k}
as a random point on
{\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}
,
and denote
X_{p}\left(h_{k}\right)=\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}
.
Then

\displaystyle\left|\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}-\mu_{k,p}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left|\left\|X_{p}\right\|_{L^{p}\left({\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}\right)}-\mu_{k,p}\right|^{p}\leq\left\|X_{p}-\mu_{k,p}\right\|_{L^{p}\left({\mathscr{S}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2N-1}\right)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}-\mu_{k,p}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}\pazocal{P}r\left(\left|\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}-\mu_{k,p}\right|>\lambda\right)\,d\lambda.

Hence, by (
4.7
) we obtain

\left|\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}-\mu_{k,p}\right|^{p}\leq 2p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}e^{-ckL^{\frac{4}{p}-2}\lambda^{2}}\,d\lambda=\frac{p\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)}{(ckL^{\frac{4}{p}-2})^{p/2}}.

Thus,

\left|\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}-\mu_{k,p}\right|\leq\frac{p^{1/p}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\sqrt{ckL^{\frac{4}{p}-2}}}\ll\frac{L^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{k}}.

∎

We will now deduce Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Combining Corollary
4.4
with Lemmas
4.5
,
4.6
and applying (
4.4
) establishes (i.). Similarly, by Corollary
4.4
, Lemmas
4.5
,
4.6
and (
4.5
) we obtain (ii.).
∎

1.1
(i.) and Theorem
1.3
(ii.)

5.1.
Proof of the lower bounds in Theorem
1.1
(i.) and Theorem
1.3
(ii.)

Combining Theorem
4.1
(i.) with the estimates for the covariance function
r_{k}
given in Proposition
3.1
yields asymptotics for the
L^{p}
norm of
h_{k}
. We will now use this result to obtain a lower bound on the expected value of the sup norm of
h_{k}
, which will establish the lower bound in Theorem
1.1
(i.).

Proofs of Theorem
1.1
(i.), lower bound.

Write
\mathscr{K}^{\prime}=\mathscr{K}\cap\mathscr{F}_{\delta}
where
\mathscr{F}_{\delta}
is defined as in (
3.5
) and assume
\delta>0
is sufficiently small, depending on
\mathscr{K}
, so that
\mathscr{K}^{\prime}
has positive area. Recall
r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}(z)=r_{k}(z,z)
and
N=\frac{k}{12}+O(1)
. Thus, using (
3.6
) we get that

\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{\mathscr{K}}}\geq\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{\mathscr{K}}^{\prime}}=\operatorname{area}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\mathscr{K}^{\prime})^{1/p}\sqrt{\frac{3}{\pi}}(1+o(1)).

Hence, using Stirling’s formula it follows for
p\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
with
p=o(k)
that

(5.1)

\begin{split}\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{\mathscr{K}}}\sqrt{N}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2N-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2N-1}{2}\right)}\bigg)^{1/p}\geq&\operatorname{area}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\mathscr{K}^{\prime})^{1/p}\sqrt{\frac{3}{\pi}}\Gamma(\tfrac{p}{2}+1)^{1/p}(1+o(1))\\
=&\operatorname{area}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\mathscr{K}^{\prime})^{1/p}\sqrt{\frac{3p}{2\pi e}}(1+o(1))\\
=&\sqrt{\frac{3p}{2\pi e}}(1+o_{\mathscr{K}}(1)).\end{split}

By Theorem
4.1
(i.) and (
5.1
) there exists
C=C(\mathscr{K})>0
such that

(5.2)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{K}}\right]\geq&\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{\mathscr{K}}}\sqrt{N}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2N-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2N-1}{2}\right)}\bigg)^{1/p}-C\frac{\sqrt{p}}{k^{1/p}}\\
\geq&\sqrt{p}\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi e}}(1+o_{\mathscr{K}}(1))-\frac{C}{k^{1/p}}\bigg)\end{split}

for any
p\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
with
p=o(k)
.
Taking
p=\eta\log k
where
\eta=\eta(\mathscr{K})>0
is fixed and sufficiently small in terms of
C
we have by (
5.2
) that

(5.3)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{\eta\log k,\mathscr{K}}\right]\gg_{\mathscr{K}}\sqrt{\log k}.

To complete the proof, observe that

\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{K}}\ll\sup_{z\in\mathscr{K}}|h_{k}(z)|,

which holds for any
p\geq 2
, hence by (
5.3
) we conclude

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{z\in\mathscr{K}}|h_{k}(z)|\right]\gg\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{\eta\log k,\mathscr{K}}\right]\gg_{\mathscr{K}}\sqrt{\log k}.

∎

We now turn to the proof of the lower bound in Theorem
1.3
(ii.).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
(i.), lower bound.

Recall that
h_{k}(z)=y^{k/2}\cdot g_{k}(z)
, whose variance at
z\in\mathscr{D}
is given by

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}(z)\right|^{2}\right]=r_{k}(z,z).

Then, by invoking Proposition
3.2
(iii.), it is possible to find a (deterministic) point
z_{k}=x_{k}+iy_{k}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
so that the variance of
h_{k}(z_{k})
satisfies

(5.4)

\operatorname{Var}(h_{k}(z_{k}))=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}(z_{k})\right|^{2}\right]\gg k^{1/2}

where the constant involved in the
``\gg"
-notation is absolute.
Indeed, it is easy to find a number
y_{k}\in\left(\frac{k}{8\pi},\frac{k}{2\pi}\right)
, say, so that
\frac{k-1}{4\pi y_{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Setting
z_{k}=x_{k}+iy_{k}\in\mathscr{D}
with
x_{k}\in(0,1)
arbitrary
and applying Proposition
3.2
(iii.) will imply the estimate (
5.4
).

Had
h_{k}(z_{k})
been Gaussian, (
5.4
) would have implied the lower bound in (
1.7
), as a (real or complex) Gaussian random variable
Z
satisfies

(5.5)

\mathbb{E}\left[|Z|\right]\gg\operatorname{Var}(Z)^{1/2},

with the constant involved in the “
\gg
”-notation absolute. Unfortunately, this is not the case, and, in general one cannot infer the
analogue of (
5.5
) without some restrictive assumptions on
Z
. Fortunately,
h_{k}(\cdot)
is intimately related to its Gaussian counterpart, see the discussion in §
2.2.2
above.
Namely, let the (complex) Gaussian random variable

(5.6)

Z=Z_{k}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}y_{k}^{k/2}f_{j}(z_{k}),

where the
b_{j}
are standard complex Gaussian i.i.d., and bear in mind that the point
z_{k}=x_{k}+iy_{k}\in\mathscr{D}
was chosen so that (
5.4
) holds.
(If, instead of fixing
z_{k}
, one varies
z
in
\mathscr{D}
, the result is a Gaussian random field with the same covariance kernel
r_{k}(\cdot,\cdot)
as for
h_{k}(\cdot)
, cf. (
2.6
).
However, for
our purpose, we will only need to evaluate it at a single point of maximal variance, so there is no need to introduce a random Gaussian field, and we may settle for a single Gaussian random variable.)

Comparing (
5.6
) to the definition (
3.2
) of
h_{k}(z)
and (
1.1
), we may observe that

(5.7)

Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pazocal{L}}}{{=}}\zeta\cdot h_{k}(z_{k}),

akin to (
2.7
), where
\zeta=\zeta_{N}>0
(with
N\asymp k
) is a random variable, independent of
h_{k}(\cdot)
, highly concentrated at
1
,
whose mean is given precisely by (
2.8
).
Evidently,

\operatorname{Var}(Z)=\mathbb{E}[|Z|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[|h_{k}(z_{k})|^{2}]=r_{k}(z_{k},z_{k})\gg k^{1/2},

by (
5.4
). The upshot is that
Z
is a Gaussian random variable, of variance
\gg k^{1/2}
, hence
one is eligible to apply (
5.5
) so that to infer

(5.8)

\mathbb{E}[|Z|]\gg k^{1/4}.

Together with (
5.8
) and (
2.8
),
and the independence of
\zeta
and
h_{k}(\cdot)
, (
5.7
) implies that

\mathbb{E}[|h_{k}(z_{k})|]=\mathbb{E}[|Z|]\cdot\left[1-O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right]\gg k^{1/4}.

Finally, we write

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup\limits_{z\in\mathscr{D}}\left|h_{k}(z)\right|\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|h_{k}\left(z_{k}\right)\right|\right]\gg k^{1/4},

which is the lower bound of Theorem
1.3
(i.). ∎

5.2.
Proof of the upper bounds in Theorem
1.1
(i.) and Theorem
1.3
(ii.)

As before, using Theorem
4.1
and Proposition
3.1
we obtain upper bounds for the expected value of the
L^{p}
norm of
h_{k}
, which we will use to bound the expected value of the sup norm of
h_{k}
. To pass from the
L^{p}
of
h_{k}
to its sup norm we require the following auxiliary result:

Lemma 5.1
.

Recall that, for
k\geq 1
even,
{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
is the space of the weight-
k
cusp forms, and denote
\|f\|_{2}=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle_{PS}}
to be the norm on
{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
corresponding to the Petersson inner product.
Then, uniformly for all
f\in{\pazocal{S}}_{k}
of unit norm
\|f\|_{2}=1
, and
z=x+iy,w=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
one has the estimate

y^{k/2}f(z)=v^{k/2}f(w)+O\left(k^{7/4}\cdot\frac{|z-w|}{\min(y,v)}\right).

Proof.

Recall the bound (
2.2
), which states

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{H}^{2}}\frac{y^{k/2}|f(z)|}{\lVert f\rVert_{2}}\ll k^{3/4}.

Hence, if

|z-w|>\frac{\min(y,v)}{8k},

the result follows.
It remains to consider the case

(5.9)

|z-w|\leq\frac{\min(y,v)}{8k}.

Since the disk centered at
z
with radius
r=y/(4k)
is contained in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
,
f
is holomorphic in this disk. Also, this disk contains
w
by (
5.9
). Hence,

(5.10)

f(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|s-z|=r}f(s)\frac{ds}{(s-z)^{j+1}}\bigg)(w-z)^{j}.

Write
s=x_{s}+iy_{s}
. For
|s-z|=r
, also write
y_{s}=y+r\sin\theta_{s}
with
\theta_{s}\in[0,2\pi)
. We have

y_{s}^{k/2}=y^{k/2}\left(1+\tfrac{r}{y}\sin\theta_{s}\right)^{k/2}.

Since
r=y/(4k)
, it follows that

(5.11)

\left(1+\tfrac{r}{y}\sin\theta_{s}\right)^{k/2}\asymp 1,

and consequently
y_{s}^{k/2}\asymp y^{k/2}
for
|s-z|=r
. Applying (
5.11
) along with (
2.2
) yields

(5.12)

\begin{split}\bigg|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|s-z|=r}f(s)\frac{ds}{(s-z)^{j+1}}\bigg|\ll&\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{2}}{y^{k/2}r^{j+1}}\int_{|z-s|=r}\frac{y_{s}^{k/2}|f(s)|}{\lVert f\rVert_{2}}\,|ds|\\
\ll&\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{2}}{y^{k/2}r^{j}}k^{3/4}.\end{split}

Using (
5.12
) in (
5.10
) and recalling the assumption (
5.9
), which implies
|z-w|\leq r/2
, we get

f(w)=f(z)+O\bigg(\lVert f\rVert_{2}k^{3/4}\frac{|z-w|}{y^{k/2}r}\bigg).

Hence, noting that

y^{k/2}=v^{k/2}\bigg(1+\frac{y-v}{v}\bigg)^{k/2}=v^{k/2}\bigg(1+O\bigg(\frac{k|y-v|}{v}\bigg)\bigg)

and recalling the bound (
2.2
), we have

\frac{y^{k/2}f(z)}{\lVert f\rVert_{2}}=\frac{v^{k/2}f(w)}{\lVert f\rVert_{2}}+O\bigg(k^{7/4}\frac{|z-w|}{\min(y,v)}\bigg),

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(i.), upper bound.

Let
Y=Y(\mathscr{K})>0
be sufficiently large so that
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}
where
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}=\{z\in\mathscr{D}:\Im z\leq Y\}
. Since
\mathscr{K}\subseteq\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}
it suffices to bound the sup norm of
h_{k}
over
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}
. Also, let
z_{0}=z_{0}(h_{k})\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}
be such that

|h_{k}(z_{0})|=\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}}|h_{k}(z)|.

For
r>0
let

\mathscr{R}_{k}=\mathscr{R}_{k}(z_{0},r)=\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-z_{0}|\leq r\}.

Hence, using Lemma
5.1
, there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that for any
p\geq 1

\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{R}_{k}}\geq\operatorname{area}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\mathscr{R}_{k})^{1/p}\bigg(\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}}|h_{k}(z)|-Ck^{7/4}r\bigg).

We have that
\operatorname{area}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\mathscr{R}_{k})\gg_{\mathscr{K}}r^{2}
uniformly w.r.t.
z_{0}
.
It follows that for any
r>0,p\geq 1
we have

(5.13)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}}|h_{k}(z)|\right]\ll_{\mathscr{K}}\frac{1}{r^{2/p}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}}\right]+k^{7/4}r.

By Theorem
4.1
(i.) along with Proposition
3.2
(i.), and recalling
N=\frac{k}{12}+O(1)
, we have for any
p\geq 2
with
p=o(k)

(5.14)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}}\right]&\ll_{\mathscr{K}}\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}}\sqrt{N}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2N-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+2N-1}{2}\right)}\bigg)^{1/p}+\frac{\sqrt{p}}{k^{1/p}}\\
&\ll_{\mathscr{K}}\sqrt{p}\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}}+\sqrt{p}\ll_{\mathscr{K}}\sqrt{p},\end{split}

where to obtain the second inequality we used standard bounds for the gamma function (cf. (
5.1
)).
Combining (
5.13
) and (
5.14
) yields

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}_{Y}}|h_{k}(z)|\right]\ll_{\mathscr{K}}\frac{\sqrt{p}}{r^{2/p}}+k^{7/4}r.

Taking
r=k^{-7/4}
and
p=\log k
completes the proof.∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
(i.), upper bound.

First of all, it suffices to restrict to the domain

\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}=\{z\in\mathscr{D}:\Im z\leq k\}

since for
z\in\mathscr{D}\setminus\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}
it follows from Proposition
3.2
(ii.) that

(5.15)

|h_{k}(z)|\leq\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|a_{j}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}r_{k}(z,z)^{1/2}\ll e^{-k/75}.

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i.), for
r>0
we let

\mathscr{R}_{k}=\mathscr{R}_{k}(z_{0},r)=\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-z_{0}|\leq r\},

where
z_{0}\in\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}
satisfies
|h_{k}(z_{0})|=\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}}|h_{k}(z_{0})|
. We have
\operatorname{area}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(\mathscr{R}_{k})\gg r^{2}/k^{2}
uniformly w.r.t.
z_{0}
. Hence, repeating the argument used to deduce (
5.13
) shows that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}}|h_{k}(z)|\right]\ll\frac{k^{2/p}}{r^{2/p}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}}\right]+k^{7/4}r.

By Theorem
4.1
(ii.) along with Proposition
3.2
(i.), and recalling
N=\frac{k}{12}+O(1)
, we have for any
p\geq 2
with
p=o(k)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}}\right]\ll\sqrt{p}\left\|\sqrt{r_{k,\operatorname{diag}}}\right\|_{p,\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}}+\sqrt{p}k^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{3}{2p}}\ll k^{1/4}\sqrt{p}.

Hence, we conclude that for any

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{z\in\mathscr{D}_{k}^{\prime}}|h_{k}(z)|\right]\ll\frac{k^{2/p}}{r^{2/p}}k^{1/4}\sqrt{p}+k^{7/4}r.

Taking

and

then recalling (
5.15
) completes the proof.
∎

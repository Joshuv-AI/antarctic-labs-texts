Provability Models
Mojtaba Mojtahedi, Borja Sierra Miranda

Abstract
In this paper, we study a new Kripke-style semantics for classical modal logic, named as provability models. We study provability models for the propositional modal logics 𝖪 {\sf K} , 𝖪𝟦 {\sf K4} , 𝖲𝟦 {\sf S4} 𝖦𝖫 {\sf GL} , 𝖦𝖫𝖯 {\sf GLP} and the interpretability logic 𝖨𝖫𝖬 {\sf ILM} . Provability models combine features of Kripke models with the assignment of logics to individual worlds. Originally introduced in [ Mojtahedi, 2022 ] , these models allowed the first author to establish arithmetical completeness for intuitionistic provability logic. Interestingly, we show that the 𝖨𝖫𝖬 {\sf ILM} is complete for the same provability models of 𝖦𝖫 {\sf GL} . We improve provability models to predicative and decidable provability models in the case of 𝖦𝖫 {\sf GL} and 𝖨𝖫𝖬 {\sf ILM} . Furthermore, we prove a soundness and completeness of 𝖦𝖫𝖯 {\sf GLP} for provability models.

Several concerns or objections regarding provability models are discussed in the following paragraphs.

Objection I:

One might argue that provability models are merely a reformulation of standard Kripke models, based on the following reasoning: for a standard Kripke model, one could define

On the contrary, in case of the logic

Objection II:

Is it possible to decide the validity of a formula at a given world in a provability model?

In general, the answer is no. This is due to the possibility that the logics assigned to the nodes may be undecidable.
Nevertheless,
{\sf GL}
and
{\sf ILM}
are complete for a class of decidable Provability models. Specifically, if one starts with a

Objection III:

What is the added value in studying provability models?

One important feature of provability models is their ability to absorb frame conditions as inference rules of the logics assigned to individual worlds. For instance, rather than requiring transitivity of the frame, one can require each logic to be closed under necessitation.
Similarly, converse well-foundedness can be replaced by closure under Löb’s rule.

This feature was, in fact, the primary motivation for the first author to introduce

Mojtahedi, 2022

Indeed,

However, Iemhoff models are highly complex and hence ineffective for establishing
arithmetical completeness theorems. Their main drawback lies in their reliance
on complicated frame properties. By contrast, the aforementioned feature of
provability models allowed the first author of this paper to

such frame conditions as
inference rules of the logics assigned to the nodes, leading to finite and decidable
provability models for intuitionistic provability logic. Thus, we have at least

Another example is the provability models for the poly-modal provability logic

absorbing some frame properties as logical properties, we are able to have soundness and
completeness of

4
Construction of Provability Models

In previous section, we have seen provability models for
various modal logics.
Nevertheless, as can be seen through the proofs of completeness theorems,
they are not looking as
independent tools from standard Kripke models.
The only way to build a provability model, up to now, is by taking a standard
Kripke model and attaching a set of valid formulas to the node and finally
making an equivalent provability model. More precisely, for all constructed Provability
models
\mathcal{P}:=(W,\sqsubset,\{{\sf L}_{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\in W^{\sqsubset}},\mathrel{V})
, the standard part of the
model
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{P}}:=(W,\sqsubset,\mathrel{V})
, is equivalent to
\mathcal{P}
in the following sense:

\mathcal{P},w\models A\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{P}},w\models A.

In this section, we show that it is possible to go beyond and define
independently a provability model in the case of
{\sf GL}
(
Theorem

4.4
).
More precisely, for every converse well-founded tree frame, and every assignment of sets
of formulas to the nodes, one may expand the sets as logics such that the result becomes
a provability model with necessitation
10
10

10

Of course one may easily define an
independent provability model, by attaching inconsistent logics to the nodes.
However, importance of the content of this section is that we do it in a systematic way,
for arbitrary assignments to the nodes.
.

Another defect in provability models is the following:
Validity of formulas in
provability models, might not be decidable
(as it was the case for instance in the canonical provability model constructed for

{\sf GL}
in
Theorem

3.18
, for which, we had strong completeness).
This might have arisen due to the undecidability of the theories associated with the nodes.
However, as we will see in

, the constructed provability model, satisfies
decidability, in case of finiteness of the sets being assigned to the worlds.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
A
be a modal formula whose atomic subformulas
are
\vec{p}=p_{0},\ldots,p_{m-1}
.
A
\Box
-free formula

are distinct from

and there are unique and distinct

Then, we define the purely modal uniform pre-interpolant of

as

A^{*}:=\bigwedge_{\vec{b}\in\{\top,\bot\}^{m}}B(\vec{b},\Box C_{0},\ldots,\Box C_{n-1}).

We note the two following facts:

1.

Every modal formula has a propositional skeleton, although it is not unique (nevertheless, this nonuniqueness is only due to renaming of the variables
q_{0},\ldots,q_{n-1}
).

2.

Even if the propositional skeleton of a formula is not unique,
the purely modal uniform interpolant is unique (once we fix an order on the conjuncts).

Before proving the properties of purely modal uniform pre-interpolation we need the following simple lemma

11

We remember that

Lemma 4.2

Let

The properties of modal uniform pre-interpolant that we are going to need (and the justification for this name) are collected in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A
be a modal formula.
We have the following:

1.

A^{*}
is purely modal.

2.

\vdash A^{*}\to A
.

3.

For any purely modal formula

\vdash B\to A
implies

\vdash B\to A^{*}

Proof.

The first property is trivial by definition.
Let us show the second property.
Let
A
be a formula whose propositional variables are
p_{0},\ldots,p_{m-1}
and
C(\vec{p},q_{0},\ldots,q_{n-1})
be a propositional skeleton of
A
, such that

.
Note that
\bigwedge_{\vec{b}\in\{\bot,\top\}^{m}}C(\vec{b},q_{0},\ldots,q_{m-1})

for the language
\{q_{0},\ldots,q_{m-1}\}

In particular, this means that
\vdash\bigwedge_{\vec{b}\in\{\bot,\top\}^{m}}C(\vec{b},q_{0},\ldots,q_{m-1})\to C(\vec{p},q_{0},\ldots,q_{m-1})

\vec{p}

\vdash\bigwedge_{\vec{b}\in\{\bot,\top\}^{m}}C(\vec{b},\Box B_{0},\ldots,\Box B_{m-1})\to C(\vec{p},\Box B_{0},\ldots,\Box B_{m-1})

\vdash A^{*}\to A
.

Finally, we show the third property.
Assume that
\vdash B\to A

B

Then the assumption says

Provability models for the Interpretability Logic

In this section we study the provability models of the interpretability logic

In the interpretability logic, there is a binary modal operator
\rhd
, with the
following intended

{\sf PA}+A

{\sf PA}+B

For more details see
[

The arithmetical completeness of the logic of interpretability was proved in

[
Shavrukov, 1988
,
Berarducci, 1990
]

Interestingly, interpretability for Peano Arithmetic, is equivalent to

\Pi_{1}
-conservativity. In other words,
{\sf PA}+A

{\sf PA}+B

iff every
\Pi_{1}
-consequence of
{\sf PA}+B
is also
\Pi_{1}
-consequence of

{\sf PA}+A

[
Berarducci, 1990
]

So in terms of propositional language, we have

A\rhd B
iff for every
\lozenge E

implies
{\sf L}\vdash A\to\lozenge E

for a desired modal logic
{\sf L}

This simple observation, gives us the idea that how to define provability models
for the interpretability logic
{\sf ILM}
.

Axioms and rules of
{\sf ILM}

Given a logic
{\sf L}
, the interpretability logic over
{\sf L}
,

, is a modal logic with modus ponens as its only inference rule and
following axioms:

•

\Box(A\to B)\to(A\rhd B)
.

•

(A\rhd B\wedge B\rhd C)\to A\rhd C
.

•

(B\rhd A\wedge C\rhd A)\to(B\vee C)\rhd A
.

•

\lozenge A\rhd A
.

•

A\rhd B\to\big((\Box C\wedge A)\rhd(\Box C\wedge B)\big)
. (Montagna’s axiom)

•

\Box A
for
A
being any of the above mentioned axioms.

•

All theorems of
{\sf L}
.

15
15

15

Note that
{\sf L}
might be a modal logic in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
,
however here we consider all theorems of
{\sf L}
in the bigger language
\mathcal{L}_{\rhd}
.

Then we define
{\sf ILM}:={\sf Int}({\sf GL})

original axiomatisation of the interpretability logic of Peano Arithmetic (as in
[

Probably, the most notable thing missing in the current axiomatization of

.

Comparing this system to the

5.1
Veltman models for
{\sf ILM}

A Veltman model
[
de Jongh and Veltman, 1990a
]
is a tuple
\mathcal{V}:=(W,\{\preccurlyeq_{w}\}_{w\in W},\sqsubset,\mathrel{V})

with following properties:

•

W
is a nonempty set of worlds.

•

\sqsubset
is a binary relation on
W
.

•

\preccurlyeq_{w}
is a transitive reflexive relation on the set of
\sqsubset
-accessible nodes from
w
, i.e.
(w{\sqsubset}):=\{u\in W:w\sqsubset u\}
.

•

w\sqsubset u\sqsubset v
implies
u\preccurlyeq_{w}v
.

•

u\preccurlyeq_{w}v\sqsubset z
implies
u\sqsubset z
.

•

\mathrel{V}
is a binary relation between worlds and propositional variables.

Then the relation
\mathcal{V},w\models A
is defined inductively. It commutes with boolean connectives and:

\mathcal{V},u\models A\rhd B\quad\text{iff}\quad\forall\,v\sqsupset u\ \big(\mathcal{V},v\models A\Rightarrow(\exists\,w\succcurlyeq_{u}v\ \mathcal{V},w\models B)\big).

Remark 5.2
.

By other properties of Veltman models,
the following definition for validity of
A\rhd B
is equivalent to the original one defined above:

\mathcal{V},u\models A\rhd B\quad\text{iff}\quad\forall\,v\sqsupset u\ \big((\exists\,w\succcurlyeq_{u}v\ \mathcal{V},w\models A)\Rightarrow(\exists\,w\succcurlyeq_{u}v\ \mathcal{V},w\models B)\big).

This alternative definition is symmetric and better
aligned with the provability semantics which we introduce later for
{\sf ILM}
.

.

{\sf ILM}
is sound and complete for Veltman models. Furthermore,
the completeness also holds for finite Veltman models.

Proof.

The soundness is straightforward and left to the reader. For the

[

]
, or

[

]
.

Appendix A
Propositional-Provability interpretations for Modal Logics

In this appendix, we consider the following interpretation for the modal operator
\Box

\Box A
is interpreted as
{\sf L}\vdash A
,
for some propositional logic
{\sf L}
in the language
\mathcal{L}_{\Box}

{\sf GL}
is sound for
{\sf L}
-provability iff

{\sf L}
includes
{\sf GL}

We first need to define precisely what we mean by such

{\sf L}
-provability interpretation for an arbitrary

A\in\mathcal{L}_{\Box}
.

A
phrase

\bigwedge X\to\bigvee Y

Notice that
\bot

Furthermore,
we assume that
\bigwedge\emptyset:=\top
and
\bigvee\emptyset:=\bot

For every given formula
A

Z

\vdash A\leftrightarrow\bigwedge Z

Such set

This means that if

The
{\sf L}
-provability interpretation, namely

•

Abstract
. Let K 7 ∨ K_{7}^{\vee} denote the graph obtained from the complete graph on seven vertices by deleting two edges with a common end. Motivated by Hadwiger’s conjecture, we prove that every graph with no K 7 ∨ K_{7}^{\vee} -minor is 6 6 -colorable.



1.
Introduction

In
1943
Hadwiger made the following famous conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Hadwiger’s conjecture
[
Had43
]
)
.

For every integer
t\geq 1
, every graph with no
K_{t}
minor is
(t-1)
-colorable.

We refer the reader to a comprehensive survey by Seymour
[
Sey16
]
of progress towards the conjecture, and only give a brief overview of the results closest to our investigation.

Hadwiger
[
Had43
]
and Dirac
[
Dir52
]
independently showed that Conjecture
1
holds for
t\leq 4
. Wagner
[
Wag37
]
proved that for
t=5
Hadwiger’s conjecture is equivalent to the Four Color Theorem, which was subsequently proved by Appel and Haken
[
AH77
,
AHK77
]
.
Robertson, Seymour and Thomas
[
RST93
]
proved Hadwiger’s conjecture for
t=6
, also by reducing it to the Four Color Theorem.

In this paper we are concerned with the next open case:
t=7
.
Settling the conjecture in this case appears to be extremely challenging. Albar and Gonçalves
[
AG18
]
proved that every graph with no
K_{7}
minor is
8
-colorable, but even the question whether every such graph is
7
-colorable is open. (Rolek, Song and Thomas discuss properties of minor minimal non-
7
-colorable graphs with no
K_{7}
-minor in
[
RST23
]
.)

In another direction, several authors explored minors that are guaranteed to be present in every non-
6
-colorable graph. The following two theorems of Jakobsen
[
Jak71
]
and of Kawarabayashi and Toft
[
KT05
]
, in particular, inspired our results.

Stating Jakobsen’s result requires the following additional notation. For
t\geq 4
, let
K^{\vee}_{t}
denote the graph obtained from
K_{t}
by deleting two edges with a common end, and let
K^{=}_{t}
denote the graph obtained from
K_{t}
by deleting a two-edge matching. Thus every graph obtained from
K_{t}
by deleting two edges is isomorphic to
K^{\vee}_{t}
or
K^{=}_{t}
.

Theorem 2

(Jakobsen
[
Jak71
]
)
.

Every graph with no
K_{7}^{\vee}
-minor and no
K^{=}_{7}
-minor is
6
-colorable.

Theorem 3

(Kawarabayashi and Toft
[
KT05
]
)
.

Every graph with no
K_{7}
-minor and no
K_{4,4}
-minor is
6
-colorable.

Our main theorem strengthens Jakobsen’s result, showing that excluding
K^{=}_{7}
is not necessary.

Theorem 4
.

Every graph with no
K_{7}^{\vee}
-minor is
6
-colorable.

The proof of
4
uses the same overall strategy as the proofs of Theorems
2
and
3
, which is also deployed to some extent in the proofs of the Four Color Theorem and Hadwiger’s conjecture for
t=6
. However, we have to substantially extend the toolkit used in the proofs of these earlier results. We hope that our techniques could be further applied to closer approach Hadwiger’s conjecture for small values of
t
.

First, we restrict our attention to
7
-contraction-critical
graphs, i.e. graphs
G
such that
\chi(G)=7
and
\chi(G^{\prime})<7
for every proper minor
G^{\prime}
of
G
. Second, we determine the maximum number of edges in
G
, as a function of the number of vertices.
In the proof of
3
, the corresponding part of the argument uses the following result of Jørgensen.

Theorem 5

(Jørgensen
[
Jø94
]
)
.

Every
4
-connected graph
G
with
|E(G)|\geq 4|V(G)|-7
has a
K_{4,4}
-minor, unless
G\simeq K_{7}
.

The main technical part in the proof of
4
is establishing the following analogue of
5
in our setting.

Theorem 6
.

Every
4
-connected graph
G
with
|E(G)|\geq 4|V(G)|-8
has a
K_{7}^{\vee}
-minor, unless
G\simeq K_{2,2,2,2}
.

In
Section

2
, as a step towards the proof of
6
, we obtain extremal results guaranteeing the existence of certain “rooted” minors, using and refining theorems of Robertson, Seymour and Thomas
[
RST93
]
and Jørgensen
[
Jø94
]
. In
Section

3
we prove
6
.

In
Section

4
we implement the remainder of the strategy and complete the proof of
4
. The brief outline of the argument is as follows. By
6
, we can conclude that
G
contains many degree seven vertices. The neighborhood of every degree seven vertex contains a clique of size five, except for one case, which we dismiss using a recent theorem of Kriesell and Mohr
[
KM19
]
. The resulting cliques can than be connected to obtain the required minor. This last part closely follows the argument in
[
KT05
]
, which is in turn inspired by
[
RST93
]
. We conclude with several open questions in
Section

5
.

Notation

We use standard graph theoretical notation. We denote the subgraph of a graph
G
induced by a set
X\subseteq V(G)
by
G[X]
.

A

of a graph

is a pair
(A,B)
such that
A\cup B=V(G)
and
G[A]\cup G[B]=G
, i.e. no edge of
G
has one end in
A-B
and the other in
B-A
. The
order
of a separation
(A,B)
is
|A\cap B|
. A separation
(A,B)
is
non-trivial
if
A,B\neq V(G)
. A
k
-separation
is a separation
of order
k
.

We write
G\simeq H
if the graphs
G
and
H
are isomorphic. We denote by
N(v)
the set of all vertices adjacent to the vertex
v
in a graph
G
(where
G
is understood from context, whenever we use this notation). Let
N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}
.

We denote the graph obtained from the complete graph
K_{t}
by deleting a single edge by
K^{-}_{t}
.

2.
Rooted models

Many of the results we use in this paper are best stated in terms of rooted models, which we now define.

Let
k
be a non-negative integer. A
k
-rooted graph
is a
(k+1)
-tuple
(G,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
consisting of a graph
G
and an ordered sequence of pairwise distinct vertices of
G
. Let
(H,u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
and
(G,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
be two
k
-rooted graphs.
A
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
-rooted

(H,u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
-model in
G
is a function
\alpha
assigning to each
u\in V(H)
a set
\alpha(u)\subseteq V(G)
such that

•

\alpha(u)\cap\alpha(u^{\prime})=\emptyset
for all
u,u^{\prime}\in V(H)
,
u\neq u^{\prime}
,

•

the subgraph
G[\alpha(u)]
, which we refer to as
a bag of this model
, is non-null and connected for every
u\in V(H)
,

•

for every
uu^{\prime}\in E(H)
there exists an edge of
G
with one end in
\alpha(u)
and another in
\alpha(u^{\prime})
, and

•

v_{i}\in\alpha(u_{i})
for every
i\in[k]
.

Note that contracting all the bags of the model to single vertices we obtain a minor of
G
with a subgraph isomorphic to
H
induced by the vertices resulting from contraction, and, in particular, an edge joining vertices corresponding to
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
, whenever
u_{i}
and
u_{j}
are adjacent in
H
.

If
H
and
G
are graphs
X\subseteq V(H),Z\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|=|Z|=k
then a
Z
-rooted
(H,X)
-model in
G
is a
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
-rooted
(H,u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
-model in
G
for some ordering
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
of
Z
and
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
of
X
.

We will consider rooted models in three particular cases:

•

|V(H)|=k
, in which case we refer to an
(H,V(H))
-model as a
an
H
-model
, for brevity;

•

V(H)
admits a partition
(A,B)
such that
|A|=k,

A
is independent, every vertex of
A
is adjacent to every vertex of
B
, and either
B
is independent or
B
is a clique. In the first case we refer to a rooted
(H,A)
-model as a
rooted
K_{k,|B|}
-model
and in the second as a
rooted
K^{*}_{k,|B|}
-model
;

•

H
is a cycle of length
k
with vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
in order. Then we refer to a rooted
(H,u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
-model as a
rooted
C_{k}
-model
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
Z\subseteq V(G)
, and let
k\geq 0
be an integer. We say that a separation
(A,B)
of
G
is
a separation of
(G,Z)
if
Z\subseteq A
. We say that a pair
(G,Z)
is
internally
k
-connected
if there exists no separation
(A,B)
of
(G,Z)
of order less than
k
such that
B-A\neq\emptyset
. The following easy observation connects the properties of internally
k
-connected pairs and
k
-connected graphs.

Observation 7
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
k\geq 0
be an integer.

(1)

Suppose that
|V(G)|\geq k+1
and let
Z\subseteq V(G)
. Then
(G,Z)
is internally
k
-connected if and only if the graph obtained from
G
by adding edges between all pairs of non-adjacent vertices of
Z
is
k
-connected.

(2)

If
G
is
k
-connected and
(A,B)
is a separation of
G
of order at least
k
then
(G[B],A~\cap~B)
is internally
k
-connected.

Proof.

(1)

Let
G^{*}
be the graph obtained from
G
by adding edges between all pairs of non-adjacent vertices of
Z
. Then for every separation
(A,B)
of
G^{*}
we have
Z\subseteq A
or
Z\subseteq B
. Thus, either
(A,B)
or
(B,A)
is a separation of
(G,Z)
. If
(G,Z)
is internally
k
-connected, this implies that every non-trivial separation of
G^{*}
has order at least
k
and so
G^{*}
is
k
-connected.

Conversely, any separation
(A,B)
of
(G,Z)
is a separation of
G^{*}
.
In particular, every separation
(A,B)
of
(G,Z)
with
B-A\neq\emptyset
is either a non-trivial separation of
G^{*}
, or is of the form
(Z^{\prime},V(G))
for some
Z^{\prime}\supseteq Z
. In either case, if
G^{*}
is
k
-connected, such a separation has order at least
k
, implying that
(G,Z)
is internally
k
-connected.

(2)

Let
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
be a separation of
(G[B],A\cap B)
such that
B^{\prime}-A^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. Then
(A\cup A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is a separation of
G
of the same order as
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
. If
(A\cup A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is non-trivial then it follows that the order of
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is at least
k
by
k
-connectivity of
G
. Otherwise
B^{\prime}=V(G)
and so
A\cap B\subseteq A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}
, again implying that the order of
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is at least
k
.

∎

Our first result on rooted models determines the minimum number of edges sufficient to guarantee a
Z
-rooted
K_{4}
-model for an internally
4
-connected pair
(G,Z)
. We derive it from a structural result of Robertson, Seymour and Thomas. Stating their result requires the following definition.

A
trisection
of a graph
G
is a triple
(A,B,C)
of subsets of
V(G)
such that

A\cap B=A\cap C=B\cap C
and
G[A]\cup G[B]\cup G[C]=G
. The
order of a trisection
(A,B,C)
is
|A\cap B\cap C|
.

Theorem 8

(Robertson, Seymour and Thomas
[
RST93
, (2.6)]
)
.

Let
Z\subseteq V(G)
with
|Z|=4
. Then either

(i)

G
has a
Z
-rooted
K_{4}
-model, or

(ii)

there is a trisection
(A_{1},A_{2},B)
of order two such that
|Z\cap(A_{i}\setminus B)|=1
for
i=1,2
, or

(iii)

there is a separation
(A,B)
of
(G,Z)
of order at most three such that
|B\setminus A|\geq 2
and
|Z\cap B|\leq 2
, or

(iv)

G
can be drawn in a plane so that every vertex in
Z
is incident with the infinite region.

We use the following consequence of
8
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
Z\subseteq V(G)
with
|Z|=4
be such that
(G,Z)
is internally
4
-connected. If
G
has no
Z
-rooted
K_{4}
-model then

|E(G)|\leq 3|V(G)|-7.

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction on
|V(G)|
. The base case (
|V(G)|=4
) holds, as in this case
|E(G)|\leq 3|V(G)|-7=5
, or
Z
induces
K_{4}
in
G
.

For the induction step we have
|V(G)|\geq 5
. We apply
8
to
G
and
Z
. If outcome (i) holds then
G
has a
Z
-rooted
K_{4}
-model.

If (iv) holds then we can add an edge to a planar drawing of
G
to triangulate the infinite region, and using the bound on the maximum number of edges in the planar graph we conclude
|E(G)|+1\leq 3|V(G)|-6
, i.e.
|E(G)|\leq 3|V(G)|-7
.

As
(G,Z)
is internally
4
-connected, (iii) cannot hold.

Finally, suppose that (ii) holds and there is a trisection
(A_{1},A_{2},B)
of
G
of order two such that
|Z\cap(A_{i}\setminus B)|=1
for
i=1,2
. Let
Z^{\prime}=A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap B
.
If
A_{i}\setminus Z^{\prime}\setminus Z\neq\emptyset
for some
i
then the separation
(B\cup A_{3-i}\cup Z,A_{i})
contradicts internal
4
-connectivity of
(G,Z)
. Thus
A_{i}\setminus Z^{\prime}
consists of a single vertex of
Z
for
i=1,2
. If
|B|\leq 4
, then
|E(G[B])|\leq\binom{|B|}{2}\leq 3|B|-6
. Therefore

(1)

|E(G)|\leq|E(G[B])|+4\leq 3|B|-2=3(|V(G)|-2)-2\leq 3|B|-8.

Thus we assume
|B|\geq 5
. In particular, by internal
4
-connectivity of
(G,Z)
, the separation
(A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup Z,B)
has order at least four, i.e.

|Z^{\prime}\cup(Z\cap B)|=|(A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup Z)\cap B|\geq 4.

As
|Z^{\prime}|=|Z\cap B|=2
, it follows that
Z^{\prime}\cap Z\cap B=\emptyset
.

Let
Z^{\prime\prime}=Z-B=(A_{1}\cup A_{2})-B
. As
(G,Z)
is internally
4
-connected and
V(G)-Z\neq\emptyset
every vertex in
Z^{\prime\prime}\subseteq Z
has a neighbor in
V(G)-Z
and these neighbors must lie in
(A_{1}\cup A_{2})-Z=Z^{\prime}
. Similarly every vertex of
Z^{\prime}
has a neighbor in
Z^{\prime\prime}
. Thus we may assume that
Z^{\prime\prime}=\{a_{1},a_{2}\},

Z^{\prime}=\{b_{1},b_{2}\}
and
a_{i}b_{i}\in E(G)
for
i=1,2
. If
G[B]
contains a

(Z^{\prime}\cup(B\cap Z))
-rooted
K_{4}
-model, this model could be extended to a
Z
-rooted
K_{4}
-model in
G
using these two edges.

Thus we assume that
G[B]
contains no such model and by the induction hypothesis we have
|E(G[B])|\leq 3|B|-7
. Analogously to (
1
) we conclude
|E(G)|\leq 3|V(G)|-9<3|V(G)|-7
, a contradiction, finishing the proof of the lemma.

It is possible that a version of

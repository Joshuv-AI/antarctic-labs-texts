Syntomification and crystalline local systems
Dylan Pentland

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime, and let X \mathrm{X} be a smooth p p -adic formal scheme over {Spf} ⁡ 𝒪 K \Spf\mathcal{O}_{K} where K / 𝐐 p K/\mathbf{Q}_{p} is a finite extension. We show that reflexive sheaves on the stack X {Syn} \mathrm{X}^{\Syn} are equivalent to 𝐙 p \mathbf{Z}_{p} -lattices in crystalline local systems on the rigid generic fiber X η \mathrm{X}_{\eta} , and then use this to study the essential image of the étale realization functor on the isogeny category of perfect complexes on X {Syn} \mathrm{X}^{\Syn} . We also show that when X / {Spf} ⁡ 𝒪 K \mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K} is smooth and proper that 𝖯𝖾𝗋𝖿 ​ ( X {Syn} ) ​ [ 1 / p ] \mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p] is equivalent to a category of admissible filtered F F -isocrystals in perfect complexes.



1.
Introduction

Let
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension with residue field
k
, and let
\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{K})
denote the category of crystalline Galois representations in
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-vector spaces of
\mathrm{G}_{K}:=\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
. Fix a uniformizer
\pi\in K
with Eisenstein polynomial
\mathrm{E}(u_{0})
. Let
\mathsf{Mod}^{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{S}}
denote the category of Breuil-Kisin modules over
\mathrm{W}(k)\llbracket u_{0}\rrbracket
. For the Frobenius on
\mathfrak{S}
which is the Frobenius on
\mathrm{W}(k)
and sends
u_{0}\mapsto u_{0}^{p}
, these are defined as finite free modules
\mathrm{M}
over
\mathfrak{S}=\mathrm{W}(k)\llbracket u_{0}\rrbracket
equipped with an isomorphism
\varphi^{*}\mathrm{M}[1/\mathrm{E}]\to\mathrm{M}[1/\mathrm{E}]
.

In
[
Kis06
]
Kisin gave a fully faithful functor

\mathrm{D}_{\mathfrak{S}}:\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{K})\to\mathsf{Mod}^{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{S}}

and characterized the essential image.

A prismatic variant of this result was proven by Bhatt-Scholze. Let
\mathsf{Vect}^{\varphi}((\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K})_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
denote the category of prismatic
F
-crystals as defined in
[
BS23
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Bhatt-Scholze,
[
BS23
]
)
.

Let
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
be a finite extension. Then there is a symmetric monoidal equivalence

\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}:\mathsf{Vect}^{\varphi}((\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K})_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})\to\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{K}).

Moreover, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Mod}^{\varphi}_{\mathfrak{S}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ev}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}_{\mathfrak{S}}}

commutes, where
\mathrm{ev}
is the evaluation functor on a Breuil-Kisin prism
(\mathfrak{S},\mathrm{E})
.

Viewing this as a type of Riemann-Hilbert equivalence, it is natural to ask whether or not it admits a derived variant. In
[
Bha22
]
, Bhatt-Lurie define a stack
\mathrm{X}^{\Syn}
associated to a
p
-adic formal scheme
\mathrm{X}
. Similar to previously constructed stacks such as
\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
in
[
BL22b
]
, a central idea is that
\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn},\mathcal{O}\{i\})
produces the syntomic cohomology
\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathbf{Z}_{p}(i))
.

Let
\mathrm{X}_{\eta}
denote the rigid generic fiber of
\mathrm{X}
, and define
\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})
to be the full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})
consisting of locally bounded derived
p
-complete objects whose mod
p
reduction has cohomology sheaves that are locally constant with finitely generated stalks. Bhatt-Lurie construct a
t
-exact étale realization functor

\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}:\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})\to\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})

utilizing the equivalence in Corollary 3.7 in
[
BS23
]
.

In the case of a point, they extend the result of Bhatt-Scholze to the derived setting after rationalization by integrally lifting prismatic
F
-crystals to
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Syn}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Bhatt-Lurie,
[
Bha22
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Syn})
. Then for all
i
,
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{E}))
is crystalline after inverting
p
.

Moreover, there is a subcategory of reflexive sheaves
\mathsf{Refl}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Syn})\subset\mathsf{Coh}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Syn})
on which
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
induces an equivalence
\mathsf{Refl}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Syn})\simeq\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{K})
.

Restriction to the substack
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
induces an equivalence

\mathsf{Refl}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Syn})\simeq\mathsf{Vect}^{\varphi}((\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K})_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}).

The functor
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
factors through this restriction, and so the equivalence is compatible with the result of Bhatt-Scholze (and hence also with
[
Kis06
]
). In
[
Bha22
]
, it is speculated that this result generalizes past a point in Remark 6.6.1, which is the first main result.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.18
)
.

Let
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
be a smooth
p
-adic formal scheme. Then for
\mathcal{E}\in\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
we have

\mathrm{H}^{i}(\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{E}))[1/p]\in\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})

for all
i
. Moreover, there is a subcategory of reflexive sheaves
\mathsf{Refl}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})\subset\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
(see Definition
3.1
) on which
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
induces an equivalence
\mathsf{Refl}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})\simeq\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})
.

In
[
GL23
]
, Guo-Li already partially answered this question. By combining with the main result of Guo-Reinecke (
[
GR24
]
) they produce a fully faithful functor

\Pi_{\mathrm{X}}:\mathsf{Loc}^{\mathrm{cris}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})\to\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})=\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})^{\heartsuit}

such that
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{X}}(\mathcal{L}))\simeq\mathcal{L}
. What remains is to characterize the essential image in a way analogous to the case of a point. We show the essential image of this functor is
\mathsf{Refl}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
, the subcategory of reflexive objects in
\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
. This alternate description easily implies the first part of the theorem, as well as the following corollary deduced by understanding
\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]
.

Corollary 1.3

(Corollary
4.1
)
.

Assume
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
is smooth. The
t
-exact functor
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})[1/p]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}[1/p]}

induces an equivalence
\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]\simeq\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})
on the heart. The essential image of
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}[1/p]
contains the full subcategory of sheaves in the essential image of

\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta}))\to\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})[1/p],

and is contained in the full subcategory of
\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})[1/p]
where every cohomology sheaf is crystalline.

In fact, all these inclusions are in general strict inclusions as explained in Remark
4.2
. This corollary allows us to deduce a variant of the
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{cris}}
conjecture later in Corollary
4.7
. In future work, the author aims to understand the essential image of
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
on coherent
F
-gauges without inverting
p
.

It is desirable to have a more explicit description of the category
\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]
. In the case of
\mathrm{X}=\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}
, it is shown in
[
Hau24
]
that

\mathsf{Perf}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\Syn})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}})).

One may also interpret this, using Colmez-Fontaine’s equivalence
\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}})\simeq\mathrm{MF}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\varphi,\mathrm{wa}}
in
[
CF00
]
between crystalline Galois representations and filtered
F
-isocrystals, as an equivalence
\mathsf{Perf}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\Syn})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{MF}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\varphi,\mathrm{wa}})
. This statement is what generalizes, which is the second main result.

Theorem B

(Theorem
4.24
)
.

Assume further that
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
is smooth proper. Then there is an equivalence of categories

\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]\simeq\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{fIsoc}^{\varphi}}(\mathrm{X}).

Here
\mathsf{Perf}_{\mathrm{fIsoc}^{\varphi}}(\mathrm{X})
is as in Definition
4.18
.

While we initially define
\mathsf{Perf}_{\mathrm{fIsoc}^{\varphi}}(\mathrm{X})
using formal stacks, we show there is an explicit description of this category using
\mathrm{D}
-modules. One might be tempted to generalize Hauck’s result to general
\mathrm{X}
by trying to relate
\mathsf{Perf}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\Syn})[1/p]
to
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta}))
; we show that in Example
4.8
this already fails for
\mathbf{P}^{1}
due to a failure of full faithfulness and we expect that it rarely works outside of the case of
\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
, where we confirm it indeed generalizes this way (Proposition
4.15
). As a positive result in this direction, in the category
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta}))
the
\mathrm{R}\mathrm{Hom}
would be computed by crystalline extension groups. The following result shows this still holds in a limited sense.

Proposition 1.4

(Proposition
4.3
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
. Then the étale realization induces an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{E})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{E})[1/p]).

In particular, if
\mathrm{X}=\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
then
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{E})[1/p]=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathrm{G}_{K},\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{E})[1/p])
where
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}
denotes the Bloch-Kato Selmer group.

Overview of the proofs
. To make sense of our results, we first need to set up some general theory about
t
-structures on
\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
and coherent sheaves which is done in §2.2. For Theorem A, we first show the second part of the theorem that
\mathsf{Refl}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})\simeq\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})
. This is done by analyzing the kernel of the étale realization in §3.1, and then using these results we show that Guo-Reinecke’s category
\mathsf{Vect}^{\mathrm{an},\varphi}(\mathrm{X}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}})
can be viewed as reflexive objects in coherent prismatic
F
-crystals. We then may use Guo-Li’s functor
\Pi_{\mathrm{X}}
from
[
GL23
]
to lift these to sheaves on
\mathrm{X}^{\Syn}
, where we use §3.1 to show the output is reflexive (one must also verify coherence first, as their definition differs and does not use the stack). It is then easy to deduce the first part of Theorem A.

For Theorem B, we use Theorem A to deduce Corollary
4.1
which is used to deduce the essential image in Theorem B (by using the statement about the heart). Full faithfulness follows from a variant of the Beilinson fiber square over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with coefficients (see
[
AMMN22
]
), for which the key input is a cohomology calculation on
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Syn}
using the methodology of
[
AKN24
]
. Similar squares appear in
[
Hau24
]
and
[
Dev25
]
, but restrict either to
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}
smooth or work relative to the
q
-de Rham prism instead of the Breuil-Kisin prism and so cannot be used to deduce Theorem B for smooth proper
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

2.2.
Coherent sheaves

We will also need a theory of coherent sheaves for
\mathrm{X}^{\Syn}
when
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
is smooth. One possible definition is given in
[
GL23

, but it is likely not the same as the natural category to associate using the stacky approach to

[
GL23

treats defining coherent prismatic
F

\mathrm{X}^{\Nyg}

\mathrm{X}^{\Syn}

We will first show that when
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
is smooth that
\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
comes equipped with a natural
t
-structure. To do so, we will need to use flat-local surjections. However, we note that we do not mean a flat covering of stacks. Rather, we use the following slightly weaker notion which suffices for descent.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathrm{X}\to\mathrm{Y}
be a map of
p
-adic formal stacks in the flat topology. Then we say
\mathrm{X}\to\mathrm{Y}
is a flat-local surjection if it is surjective locally in the flat topology.

This differs from the usual notion of a
flat covering of
p
-adic formal stacks
, which would ask that the map is representable and the pullback along
\Spec\mathrm{R}\to\mathrm{X}
for a
p
-nilpotent ring
\mathrm{R}
induces a flat covering of
\Spec\mathrm{R}
. We will distinguish this second stronger notion by calling the map a flat cover of formal stacks.

This notion is sufficient for most results. Indeed, it suffices if we want to deduce descent for
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(-)
,
\mathsf{Perf}(-)
, and
\mathsf{Vect}(-)
. This property is sufficient to deduce that
\mathrm{X}\to\mathrm{Y}
is an effective epimorphism in the topos of flat sheaves, which means
\mathrm{Y}\simeq\colim\mathrm{X}^{\times_{\mathrm{Y}}(-)}
where
\mathrm{X}^{\times_{\mathrm{Y}}(-)}
are the terms of the Čech nerve. See
[
HP24
]
Appendix B for a proof of this result. Since
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(-)
,
\mathsf{Perf}
and
\mathsf{Vect}(-)
are sheaves for the flat topology, the desired claim follows. Here we use that finite presentation is a flat local condition.

Lemma 2.5

(
[

Let

be a quasisyntomic cover of quasisyntomic

Lemma 2.10

Suppose that

(\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{A})^{\Nyg}=\mathrm{Rees}_{\Fil^{\bullet}_{\Nyg}}{\mathbbl{\Delta}}^{(1)}_{\mathrm{X}/\mathrm{A}}.

Then stratifying the target by the

Let

(with the obvious face and degeneracy maps), and the prism structure on

By Lemma

[

For the perfectoid cover

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Nyg}}

Lemma 2.18

Let
\mathrm{X}
be a smooth
p
-adic formal scheme over

Finally, we record that if

of formal stacks which has the additional property that for any test object
\mathrm{S}\to\mathrm{Y}
that is the spectrum of a
p
-nilpotent ring the induced map

is flat with the source a
p
-nilpotent affine scheme.

The following argument is due to Kush Singhal with help from Sasha Petrov. Any mistakes are due to the author.

Proposition 2.19
.

Suppose
f:\mathrm{X}\to\mathrm{Y}
is a quasisyntomic cover of bounded quasisyntomic
p
-adic formal schemes. Then the induced map
f:\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}
is a flat cover of formal stacks.

Proof.

Let us first note that if
f:\mathrm{X}\to\mathrm{Y}
is a quasisyntomic cover, in light of Lemma 6.3 in
[
BL22b
]
we only need to check the map
f^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
is flat as a morphism of stacks (so in particular representable as a morphism of stacks).

Suppose first that
\mathrm{X}=\Spf\mathrm{S}
and
\mathrm{Y}=\Spf\mathrm{R}
are quasiregular semiperfectoid rings such that
\mathrm{R}\to\mathrm{S}
is a quasisyntomic cover. We want to show that the corresponding map of initial prisms
{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}}\to{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{S}}
is
(p,\mathrm{I})
-completely flat, where
\mathrm{I}
is the prismatic ideal of
{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}}
. By definition, we want to show that
\bar{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathrm{R}}\to\bar{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathrm{S}}
is
p
-completely flat. By
2.20
, it suffices to check that the map

\mathrm{gr}_{\ast}^{\mathrm{conj}}\bar{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathrm{R}}\to\mathrm{gr}_{\ast}^{\mathrm{conj}}\bar{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathrm{S}}

is flat, where
\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\ast}\bar{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathrm{R}}
is the conjugate filtration.

By the Hodge-Tate comparison, we know that

\mathrm{gr}_{i}^{\mathrm{conj}}\bar{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathrm{R}}/p=\left({\bigwedge}_{\mathrm{R}}^{i}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/p\big/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}/p}\right)\{-i\}[-i]

where
\tilde{\mathrm{R}}
is any perfectoid ring with a map
\tilde{\mathrm{R}}\to\mathrm{R}
, and similarly for
\bar{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{S}}
.

By Remark 4.21 in
[
BMS19
]
,
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\mathrm{R}}
actually has
p
-complete Tor amplitude in degree
-1
(as we already know it is in
[-1,0]
since the map is quasisyntomic), and so we have an exact sequence of
\mathrm{S}/p
-modules

0\to(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})\otimes_{\mathrm{R}/p}\mathrm{S}/p\to\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p}\to\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\mathrm{R}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p}\to 0.

Since the first two modules are finite projective and the third module is
(\mathrm{S}/p)
-flat, the third module is finitely presented and flat hence finite projective and this exact sequence splits.

Using Proposition 25.2.4.2 in
[
Lur18
]
, we have

\left({\bigwedge}^{i}\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/p\big/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}/p}\right)[-i]=\mathrm{LSym}^{i}_{\mathrm{R}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}/p[-1])=\Sym^{i}_{\mathrm{R}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p}).

Trivializing the Breuil-Kisin twists (which we may do as we work over a fixed perfectoid), we thus see that

\mathrm{gr}_{\ast}^{\mathrm{conj}}\bar{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathrm{R}}/p\cong\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{R}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}_{\ast}^{\mathrm{conj}}\bar{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{S}}/p

\displaystyle\simeq\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{S}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})

\displaystyle\simeq\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{S}/p}\left(\left((\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})\otimes_{\mathrm{R}/p}\mathrm{S}/p\right)\oplus\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\mathrm{R}}[-1]\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\left((\mathrm{S}/p)\otimes_{\mathrm{R}/p}\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{R}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})\right)\otimes_{\mathrm{S}/p}\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{S}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\mathrm{R}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})

\displaystyle\simeq\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{R}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})\otimes_{\mathrm{R}/p}\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{S}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\mathrm{R}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p}).

As
\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{S}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\mathrm{R}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})
is flat as an
\mathrm{S}/p
-module, and thus as an
\mathrm{R}/p
-module, it follows that
\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{S}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{S}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})
is flat over
\Sym^{\ast}_{\mathrm{R}/p}(\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{R}/\tilde{\mathrm{R}}}[-1]\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}\mathbf{F}_{p})
. Hence,
{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}}\to{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{S}}
is flat. It follows that
{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}}\to{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{S}}
is a flat cover of formal stacks.

Now suppose that
\mathrm{X}
is quasiregular semiperfectoid, but
\mathrm{Y}
is general. We already know
\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\to\mathrm{Y}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
is surjective locally in the flat topology (
[
BL22b
]
Lemma 6.3), so we will aim to show the map is flat (in what follows flat will mean the morphism is representable, and then after base change along a map from a formal scheme it is flat). Since the prismatisation commutes with Tor-independent limits, we have a pull-back diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{X}\times_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{X})^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}

Since
\mathrm{X}\times_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{X}
is also quasiregular semiperfectoid by Lemma 4.30 in
[
BMS19
]
, we see that the left vertical map and the upper horizontal map are flat. Now, as
\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}
is a surjection of sheaves in the flat topology, for any test object
\mathrm{S}\to\mathrm{Y}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
there exists a flat cover
\mathrm{S}^{\prime}\to\mathrm{S}
with a map
\mathrm{S}^{\prime}\to\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}
factoring through
\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}
, i.e. we have a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Box}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathrm{X}\times_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{X})^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{S}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}

where,
a priori
, only the indicated arrows are flat covers of formal stacks. By virtue of the left square being Cartesian
\Box\to\mathrm{S}^{\prime}
is a flat cover of formal stacks, as indicated in the diagram. As both the left and right squares are Cartesian, we deduce the outer square is as well and so
\Box\simeq\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\times_{\mathrm{Y}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\mathrm{S}^{\prime}\to\mathrm{S}^{\prime}
is flat. This suffices to check
\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\times_{\mathrm{Y}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\mathrm{S}\to\mathrm{S}
is flat as we may check this flat locally on the target, and since the test object was arbitrary it follows
\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\to\mathrm{Y}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
is a flat cover.

Next, consider the case where
\mathrm{Y}
is quasiregular semiperfectoid but
\mathrm{X}
is general. Let
\mathrm{X}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{X}
be a quasisyntomic cover with
\mathrm{X}_{\infty}
a quasiregular semiperfectoid.
Then
\mathrm{X}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Y}
is a quasisyntomic cover with
\mathrm{Y}
also quasiregular semiperfectoid. From the previous cases, we thus know that both
\mathrm{X}_{\infty}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}
and
\mathrm{X}_{\infty}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}
are flat covers of formal stacks, so the same follows for
\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}
.

Finally, let us suppose that
\mathrm{X}
and
\mathrm{Y}
are general quasisyntomic
p
-adic formal schemes. Pick quasisyntomic covers
\mathrm{Y}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Y}
and
\mathrm{X}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{X}
by quasiregular semiperfectoids
\mathrm{X}_{\infty},\mathrm{Y}_{\infty}
. We see
\mathrm{X}_{\infty}\times_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{Y}_{\infty}
is also a quasiregular semiperfectoid. We have a diagram as follows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathrm{X}_{\infty}\times_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{Y}_{\infty})^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{X}_{\infty}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathrm{X}\times_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{Y}_{\infty})^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Y}_{\infty}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}

Note that all the squares are Cartesian since prismatisation commutes with Tor independent limits. By the previous cases as well as Cartesian-ness of the squares, we already know that the indicated maps are flat morphisms of formal stacks. It follows that
\mathrm{X}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}\to\mathrm{Y}^{\mathbbl{\Delta}}
is a flat morphism of formal stacks.
∎

Corollary 2.21
.

Suppose

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}
be a quasisyntomic
p
-adic formal scheme with affine diagonal. Then
\mathrm{X}^{\Syn}
has affine diagonal.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathrm{X}
is affine, equipped with
\Spf\mathrm{A}\to\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
a flat cover where
\mathrm{A}={\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}}
for a quasiregular semiperfectoid ring
\mathrm{R}
(which exists if
\Spf\mathrm{R}\to\mathrm{X}
is a quasisyntomic cover by
[
BL22b
]
Lemma 6.3). Then consider the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spf\mathrm{A}\times_{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\Spf\mathrm{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spf\mathrm{A}\times_{\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\Spf\mathrm{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\times_{\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}.}

This diagram is cartesian by writing
\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}=\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\times_{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
and using that limits commute with limits. Since
\Spf\mathrm{A}\to\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
is a flat cover (by Proposition
2.19
) and
\mathrm{A}
is flat over
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
, all of the fiber products are underived.

For the top map in this diagram, we can simplify to get

\Spf\mathrm{A}\times_{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\Spf\mathrm{A}=(\Spf\mathrm{R}\times_{\mathrm{X}}\Spf\mathrm{R})^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}=\Spf{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\Spf\mathrm{R}\times_{\mathrm{X}}\Spf\mathrm{R}}.

For the last equivalence we use that prismatization commutes with finite limits (Remark 3.5 in
[
BL22b
]
). Thus the top morphism is affine. It follows
\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
has affine diagonal as this property is flat local on the target.

For general
\mathrm{X}
, we have a quasisyntomic covering
\tilde{\mathrm{X}}=\sqcup_{i}\Spf\mathrm{R}_{i}
of
\mathrm{X}
by quasiregular semiperfectoid rings. Then there is a flat covering of
\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}\times_{\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
by
\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\times_{\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
where
\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}=\sqcup_{i}\Spf{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}_{i}}
as the prismatization preserves flat covers. We can then obtain the same diagram, but with
\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
in place of
\Spf\mathrm{A}
.

Using the same cartesian diagram but for the diagonal of
\mathrm{X}
, the map
\tilde{\mathrm{X}}\times_{\mathrm{X}}\tilde{\mathrm{X}}\to\tilde{\mathrm{X}}\times_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\tilde{\mathrm{X}}
is affine as
\mathrm{X}
has affine diagonal. Hence, the preimage
\Spf\mathrm{R}_{i}\times_{\mathrm{X}}\Spf\mathrm{R}_{j}
of every affine open in the covering
\Spf\mathrm{R}_{i}\times_{\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\Spf\mathrm{R}_{j}
of the target is affine (and also quasiregular semiperfectoid). This induces an affine covering
\Spf{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}_{i}}\times_{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\Spf{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}_{j}}
of
\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\times_{\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
such that the preimage
\Spf{\mathbbl{\Delta}}_{\mathrm{R}_{i}\times_{\mathrm{X}}\mathrm{R}_{j}}
of every affine open is affine. Thus the map of formal schemes

\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\times_{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\to\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}\times_{\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\widetilde{\mathrm{X}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}

has affine diagonal.

A similar argument shows
\mathrm{X}^{\Nyg}
has affine diagonal, where we must also make the analogous argument for the Nygaard stack that the cover by the Rees stack of a quasiregular semiperfectoid is a flat cover (using Corollary
2.21
).

To finish and show the claim for
\mathrm{X}^{\Syn}
, we note that a pushout of stacks with affine diagonal along open immersion and an étale map (as in the construction of

) yields a stack with affine diagonal.
∎

Definition 3.1

Lemma 3.2

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{W}(k)\llbracket\mathrm{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{T}_{n}\rrbracket\llbracket q-1\rrbracket

\partial
(as defined above) and operators

\nabla_{i}(\mathrm{T}_{i}^{n})=[n]_{q}\mathrm{T}_{i}^{n-1}+P(\mathrm{T}_{i})

.

If
\mathrm{I}\leq\mathrm{A}

\nabla_{i}

Proof.

We first check

\mathrm{W}(k)\llbracket q-1\rrbracket[T_{1}]\llbracket\mathrm{T}_{2},\ldots,\mathrm{T}_{n}\rrbracket

\nabla_{1}

\mathrm{W}(k)\llbracket q-1\rrbracket\llbracket\mathrm{T}_{2},\ldots,\mathrm{T}_{n}\rrbracket

\mathrm{I}
contains such an element, we can do this one variable at a time to show

We will now only need to consider the action of

we deduce
\mathrm{I}
is stable under the automorphism
\gamma_{\mathrm{A}}
(due to
\mathrm{A}
being Noetherian we have
\gamma_{\mathrm{A}}(\mathrm{I})=\mathrm{I}
). Consider the minimal primes
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
of the ideal
\mathrm{I}
. There will be finitely many such primes, and due to
\mathrm{I}
being stable under
\gamma
we deduce that each of the minimal primes

are sent to other minimal primes over
\mathrm{I}
and must have finite
\gamma

for some

(the intersection of the minimal primes).

Now consider one of these minimal primes

and set
\mathfrak{q}_{i}=\mathfrak{p}_{i}\cap\mathrm{W}(k)\llbracket q-1\rrbracket
. This is nonzero as

\gamma
-orbit.

There is a unique height

in

\gamma

(so we are in the

is of height one; after inverting

is generated by

using the Iwasawa classification of modules. In what follows, we continue to invert

\gamma

, by factoring

\mathbf{C}_{p}
we note its roots in the variable

.

Let
\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{W}}\to\Spf\mathrm{W}(k)\langle\mathrm{T}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{T}_{n}\rangle

on

Corollary 4.1
.

Assume
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
is smooth. The

-exact functor

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})[1/p]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}[1/p]}

contains the full subcategory of sheaves in the essential image of

\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})[1/p]

Proof.

The

-exactness of
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
follows from Lemma

.

We now show

induces an equivalence on the heart onto the subcategory of crystalline

-local systems, which by Zariski descent of

and

can be tested on a smooth affine. The functor
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
is essentially surjective using Theorem

. For full faithfulness, we have a map

We use that

is an equivalence in the isogeny category

are killed by
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}[1/p]

and the proof of Theorem

which upgrades the statement to the

-gauge being

in
\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]

F

is an equivalence by Theorem

.

The claim that the essential image is contained in the subcategory of

-exactness. For producing objects in the essential image, we use the commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn}))[1/p]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})[1/p].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}

The top arrow is an equivalence coming from the result on the heart and

, which is true since we work on bounded derived categories. Commutativity of the diagram is easy to verify, as both composite functors to
\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})[1/p]
agree on the heart and commute with shifts and fibers (so using
[
Hau24
]
Lemma 5.4.3 we can deduce they agree).

Finally, the functor

\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
but rather

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
where all cohomology sheaves are coherent (the locally bounded condition is to avoid problems with

means

land in
\mathsf{Coh}
and the complex is locally bounded. But on the cover

and we may test membership in
\mathsf{Perf}
after pullback along a flat-local surjection by descent. Thus the functor is well-defined. We deduce the essential image of
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
contains the essential image of
\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta}))
in
\mathrm{D}^{(b)}_{\mathrm{lisse}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta},\mathbf{Z}_{p})[1/p]

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
be smooth and quasicompact and let
\mathcal{E}\in\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
. Then the étale realization induces an equivalence

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{E})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{E})[1/p]).

In particular, if
\mathrm{X}=\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
then
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{E})[1/p]=\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}(\mathrm{G}_{K},\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{E})[1/p])
where
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{f}
denotes the Bloch-Kato Selmer group.

Proof.

Due to
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
inducing an equivalence
\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]\simeq\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})
as shown in Corollary
4.1
, we immediately have

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]}(\mathcal{E}[1/p],\mathcal{E}^{\prime}[1/p])=\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})}(\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{E})[1/p],\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})[1/p])

for coherent
F
-gauges
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
as an equivalence of abelian categories gives an equivalence of their Yoneda
\mathrm{Ext}
groups.

It then suffices to prove the stronger integral claim that

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}).

We will first argue

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{QCoh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E})

where the
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}
group here is a Yoneda
\mathrm{Ext}
group (so it makes sense in any abelian category). This is easy to show in general, since we may identify
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{qc}}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E}[1])
and then the latter classifies extensions: we map an extension to the induced connecting morphism, and a map
\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{E}[1]
to its fiber.

Given an extension in
\mathsf{QCoh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})
of the form
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are coherent we know that
\mathcal{E}
is coherent. We can test this after pullback to a regular Noetherian flat cover, at which point it follows from the 2 out of 3 property for exact sequences of coherent sheaves. This then means
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Syn}(\mathrm{X},\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{E})
.
∎

.

-gauges. The latter holds by Proposition

4.2.
Admissible filtered
F
-isocrystals in the proper case

In Proposition 5.6.2 of
[
Hau24
]
, Corollary
4.1
is refined to an equivalence in the case of
\mathrm{X}=\Spf\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. In particular, it is shown that
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
induces an equivalence

\mathsf{Perf}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\Syn})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}})).

Such an equivalence cannot hold in general, as the following example shows.

Example 4.8
.

Let
\mathrm{X}=\mathbf{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}
. If we had an equivalence

\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}((\mathbf{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}})^{\mathrm{ad}}))

then considering
\mathrm{R}\mathrm{Hom}_{(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}})^{\Syn}}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}\{1\})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\Syn}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}},\mathcal{O}\{1\})[1/p]
we would need to show that

\mathrm{H}^{i}_{\Syn}(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}},\mathcal{O}\{1\})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}((\mathbf{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}})^{\mathrm{ad}}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(1)).

When
i=2
, the right hand side must vanish, since
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}
vanishes in crystalline
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-local systems. Indeed, since
(\mathbf{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}})^{\mathrm{ad}}
has an étale fundamental group isomorphic to
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
, the subcategory of crystalline local systems is equivalent to the category of crystalline Galois representations of
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
. In this abelian category,
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}
will vanish (by
[
EK99
]
; see also
[
Hau24
]
Remark 5.2.5).

However the left hand side (syntomic cohomology) is nonzero when
i=2
, as we get a nonzero class coming from the syntomic Chern class (see Variant 8.4.5 in
[
BL22a
]
).

In this subsection we will first show that despite this failure in the higher dimensional case, Hauck’s result still extends to the case where
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is an arbitrary finite extension:

\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\Syn})[1/p]\simeq\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathsf{Loc}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathrm{G}_{K})).

We will then define a category
\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{fIsoc}^{\varphi}}(\mathrm{X})
of admissible filtered
F
-isocrystals in perfect complexes and use the case of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
to show that there is a fully faithful functor

\mathsf{Perf}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]\to\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{fIsoc}^{\varphi}}(\mathrm{X})

when
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}
is smooth proper, and then use §4.1 to see the functor is essentially surjective.

The key input in the case of a point is an analogue of the cohomology computation done in
[
Bha22

Proposition 4.9

Let

\mathbf{Q}_{p}

where the
0

Proposition 4.20
.

Assume
\mathrm{X}/\Spf\mathcal{O}_{K}

Proof.

We start by arguing that

. We have a natural functor

\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}_{s}^{\Syn})[1/p]\to\mathrm{Isoc}^{\varphi}(\mathrm{X}_{s}/\mathrm{W}(k))

given by restriction to
\mathrm{X}_{s}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}
(which produces an

-isocrystal for the same reasons as in mixed characteristic). To deduce this is an equivalence, we may use a much simpler variant of the argument we used to deduce the analogous claim for
\mathsf{Coh}(\mathrm{X}^{\Syn})[1/p]
and the isogeny category of coherent prismatic
F
-crystals in mixed characteristic. We may first reduce to when
\mathrm{X}
is smooth affine. The idea is then that coherent crystals on
(\mathrm{X}_{s}/\mathrm{W}(k))_{\mathrm{cris}}
after evaluation on a covering (here, a simplified variant by taking the prisms
(\tilde{\mathrm{R}},p)
where
\tilde{\mathrm{R}}
is a
p
-completely smooth
\mathrm{W}(k)
-lift) are vector bundles after inverting
p
. The same argument used in Lemma
3.8
works, but is far simpler this time as we just work with the crystalline site (so we may simply set

and run the same argument). Up to isogeny, we may then deduce objects on the source and target of
(-)_{\mathrm{X}_{s}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}}
come from
p
-torsionfree ones. A simplified version of the argument in Theorem 2.31 of
[
GL23
]
goes through to show these
p
-torsionfree

-crystals may be fully faithfully lifted, thus showing the restriction functor

rationally has a fully faithful left inverse and must be an equivalence.

In
[
Bha22
]
Remark 2.5.8, explicit descriptions of

and
\mathsf{Vect}((\mathrm{X}/\mathcal{O}_{K})^{\mathrm{dR},+})
are given (and also a general method to describe sheaves, via the identification as a Rees stack). The first category consists of vector bundles with flat connection and nilpotent

and similarly for the Hodge-filtered de Rham stack with

. We observe here that up to

. For the Hodge-filtered de Rham stack, considering

has the same effect on the underlying vector bundle with connection but allows the filtration to be a complete filtration with coherent associated graded; we just ask that
\nabla
has degree
-1
.

The essential image of the functor
\mathsf{Coh}((\mathrm{X}/\mathcal{O}_{K})^{\mathrm{dR}})[1/p]\to\mathsf{Vect}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{X}_{\eta})
consists of vector bundles

-curvature modulo

. By

]

must be locally free, as we may check on an affinoid that the algebraization gives a coherent sheaf with flat connection on a characteristic zero scheme which must be a vector bundle.

The claim now follows if we can additionally show there is some formal model for
(\mathcal{E}_{\eta},\nabla)
where
\nabla
has nilpotent
p
-curvature modulo

if

comes from

∎

Proposition 4.22
.

Assume
\mathrm{X}_{0}

, and choose a global Frobenius lift

Proof.

We consider the claim

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathrm{X}_{s}^{\Syn},\mathcal{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fil}^{0}_{\Nyg}\mathrm{R}\Gamma(\mathrm{X}_{s}^{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}},\mathcal{E})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\mathcal{E}}-1}

\widehat{\Omega}_{\mathrm{X}}(\mathcal{E})

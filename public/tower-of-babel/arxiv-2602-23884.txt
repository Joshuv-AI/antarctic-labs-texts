The Equidistant Dimension of Corona Product Graphs
Sandor E. Tuñón-Andrés, Alejandro Estrada-Moreno, Juan A. Rodríguez-Velázquez Universitat Rovira i Virgili, Departament d’Enginyeria Informàtica i Matemàtiques Av. Països Catalans 26, 43007 Tarragona, Spain sandorernesto.tunon@urv.cat, alejandro.estrada@urv.cat, juanalberto.rodriguez@urv.cat

Abstract
A subset S S of vertices, in a connected graph G G , is called a distance-equalizer set if for every pair of distinct vertices outside S S , there exists a vertex in S S equidistant to both. The equidistant dimension, denoted by ξ ⁡ ( G ) \xi(G) , is defined as the minimum cardinality of such sets. While several distance-based parameters have been studied for different graph products, the equidistant dimension of corona product graphs has remained unexplored. In this paper, we investigate the equidistant dimension of the corona product G ⊙ H G\odot H of two graphs G G and H H . We introduce the empty bisector graph G ^ \widehat{G} , an auxiliary construction that relates pairs of vertices in G G that cannot be equidistant from any third vertex. Using this framework, we establish tight bounds on the equidistant dimension of G ⊙ H G\odot H and derive exact values for several classical families of graphs. Moreover, we show that for any fixed base graph G G , the equidistant dimension of G ⊙ H G\odot H depends on H H only through its order and eventually becomes linear in n ⁡ ( H ) \n(H) .

Corona product graph; Distance-equalizer set; Equidistant dimension; Empty bisector graph, Vertex cover.

1
Introduction

Distance-based parameters in graph theory have proven to be useful in the analysis of structural, combinatorial, and algorithmic properties of discrete networks. Among these, the
metric dimension
in graphs, introduced by Slater
[
15
]
, and independently by Harary and Melter
[
8
]
, has received extensive attention due to its applications in robot navigation, network verification, and fault detection
[
15
,
8
,
11
,
2
]
. The metric dimension, encapsulates the minimal amount of information required for unique vertex identification based on distances.

More recently, the focus has expanded toward the dual framework of
anonymization
and
privacy
in networks. As a counterpart to metric dimension, Trujillo-Rasua and Yero
[
16
]
introduced the concepts of antiresolving sets and metric antidimension, which seek to obfuscate vertex identities rather than reveal them.

Motivated by this line of research, González, Hernando, and Mora
[
6
]
proposed a new and more flexible metric parameter, namely the
equidistant dimension
of a graph, which captures the notion of distance-equalizer sets. A subset
S
of vertices in a connected graph
G
is called a distance-equalizer set if for every pair of distinct vertices outside
S
, there exists a vertex in
S
equidistant to both. The equidistant dimension
\xi(G)
is defined as the minimum cardinality of such sets. This parameter offers a complementary perspective by emphasizing the existence of vertices that are equidistant from a common vertex, which may be of interest in contexts related to symmetry and structural analysis of graphs. So far, connections between distance-equalizer sets and doubly resolving sets have provided new insights and applications
[
6
]
.

Since its inception, the equidistant dimension has been studied from structural and computational viewpoints. Early contributions established general bounds on
\xi(G)
in terms of classical graph invariants, including the order, diameter, independence number, and maximum degree
[
6
]
. Exact values were subsequently determined for several fundamental graph families, such as paths, cycles, and complete multipartite graphs. More recently, the parameter has been computed for additional structured classes of graphs, notably Johnson and Kneser graphs
[
12
]
, as well as for various families derived from convex polytopes
[
14
]
. From the algorithmic standpoint, in
[
4
]
proved that determining the equidistant dimension of a graph is NP-complete, thereby revealing the intrinsic computational complexity of the of the problem.

Graph products constitute a versatile and widely studied operation in graph theory, enabling the construction of complex networks from simpler building blocks. Understanding how graph invariants behave under these products is a classical and challenging problem
[
10
]
. The equidistant dimension has recently been investigated for lexicographic product graphs
[
4
]
, and Cartesian product graphs
[
5
,
13
]
, revealing intricate relationships between the equidistant dimensions of the factors and the product.

In particular, the corona product of graphs offers a useful construction that facilitates the analysis of distance-based and domination parameters,
[
19
,
9
,
1
]
.
However, the equidistant dimension of the the corona product graphs remains unexplored. This paper addresses this gap by presenting a comprehensive study of the equidistant dimension of corona product graphs. Given a graph
G
, we introduce the
empty bisector graph

\widehat{G}
, an auxiliary graph defined on the same vertex set as
G
, where two vertices are adjacent if and only if no vertex of
G
lies at equal distance from them. This construction captures pairs of vertices for which no third vertex is equidistant, providing a compact encoding of constraints related to the equidistant-dimension. Consequently, the empty bisector graph plays a central role in our analysis.

Using this framework, we derive tight bounds on the equidistant dimension of general corona product graphs in terms of structural parameters of
\widehat{G}
and the order of the graph
H
. Several illustrative examples are provided, which serve both to clarify the obtained results and to confirm the tightness of the bounds established. Moreover, we obtain closed formulas for the equidistant dimension when the base graph
G
satisfies certain conditions. These results allow us to determine the exact value of
\xi(G\odot H)
when
G
belongs to some classical families of graphs, including paths, cycles, complete graphs, and hypercubes. Furthermore, we prove that
\xi(G\odot H)
depends on the graph
H
only through its order
\n(H)
. Combined with structural properties of distance-equalizer sets, this leads to the conclusion that, for any fixed base graph
G
, the equidistant dimension of
G\odot H
is eventually linear in
\n(H)
. Finally, we establish several relationships between parameters of
G
and
\widehat{G}
, and we resolve the particular case in which
G
contains a universal vertex.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary definitions and preliminaries.
Section
3
investigates fundamental properties of distance-equalizer sets in the corona product graphs, as well as the relationship between the equidistant dimension of the corona product graphs and certain parameters of the empty bisector graph, which serve as a critical foundation for the analyses developed in this paper. Based on these relationships, we obtain sharp bounds for the equidistant dimension of corona product graphs.
In the aforementioned section, we also derive closed formulas for
\xi(G\odot H)
when
G
satisfies certain conditions, and we provide a characterization of the extremal case
\xi(G\odot H)=\n(G)
.
Subsection
3.1
applies the previous results to compute exact values of
\xi(G\odot H)
for several families of connected graphs
G
and for any graph
H
.
Section
4
is devoted to proving that the equidistant dimension becomes linear in
\n(H)
beyond a certain threshold.
Next, in Section
5
, we explore relationships between parameters of a graph
G
and its empty bisector graph, and we establish a tight upper bound relating
\xi(G\odot H)
and
\xi(G)
. We further determine the value of
\xi(G\odot H)
in the case where
\Delta(G)=\n(G)-1
. Finally, Section
6
is devoted to conclusions and future works.

For any graph

.
If

.
The

of

, and its

The

. The

A

, and the

of

An

A

. The minimum cardinality among all
vertex covers
of
G
is the
vertex cover number
of
G
and is usually denoted by
\beta(G)
.

For every integer

, we denote
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. As usual, we denote by
N_{n}
,
P_{n}
,
C_{n}
,
W_{n}
,
K_{n}
, and
K_{r,n-r}
the empty graphs, the path graphs, the cycle graphs, the wheel graphs, the complete graphs, and the complete bipartite graphs of order
n
, respectively.

All graphs considered in this paper are undirected, simple, and finite.

Definition 2.1

(Distance-equalizer set
[
6
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. A subset
S\subseteq V(G)
is called a
distance-equalizer set
if for every pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)\setminus S
, there exists a vertex
w\in S
such that

d_{G}(w,u)=d_{G}(w,v).

The
equidistant dimension
of
G
, denoted
\xi(G)
, is the minimum cardinality among all distance-equalizer sets in
G
.

Definition 2.2

(Total distance-equalizer set
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. A subset
T\subseteq V(G)
is a
total distance-equalizer set
, if for every two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, there exists
w\in T
such that

d_{G}(w,u)=d_{G}(w,v).

The
Total equidistant dimension
of
G
, denoted
\xi_{t}(G)
, is the minimum cardinality among all total distance-equalizer sets in
G
.

Roughly speaking, a bisector is a mathematical object that divides a mathematical structure into two equal parts. In particular, in the context of graph theory, we can state the following definition.

Definition 2.3

(Bisector of two vertices)
.

Given any graph
G
. For every pair of distinct vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, we define the
bisector
of
u
and
v
as

B_{G}(u\mid v)=\{w\in V(G):d_{G}(w,u)=d_{G}(w,v)\}.

In this paper, we introduce the following auxiliary graph, which plays a central role in our analysis.

Definition 2.4
.

(Empty bisector graph).

Given any graph
G=(V,E)
, we define its
empty bisector graph

\widehat{G}=(V,\widehat{E})
, where
\{u,v\}\in\widehat{E}
if and only if
B_{G}(u\mid v)=\varnothing
.

For every connected graph
G
of order
n
, it is feasible to obtain the graph
\widehat{G}
in polynomial time of order
O(n^{3})
, using the Floyd-Warshall algorithm
[
17
]
.

Definition 2.5

(Cartesian product of graphs

10
]
)
.

Given two graphs
G=(V_{1},E_{1})
,

G\,\square\,H=(V_{1}\times V_{2},E)
is defined so that two vertices

and

1.

From now on, we take

d_{G}(u,w)&\text{if }u,w\in V(G)\par\end{cases}

Note that for every graph

\xi(K_{1}\odot H)=1

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs, with
G
connected. For every distance-equalizer set
S
of
G\odot H
we have

S\cap(\{v_{i}\}\cup V(H_{i}))\neq\varnothing,\;\text{for all }i\in[\n(G)],

and as a consequence,
\xi(G\odot H)\geq\n(G)

Proof.

If
\n(G)=1
, the result is immediate, as
V(G\odot H)=\{v_{1}\}\cup V(H_{1})
.
Otherwise, let
S\subseteq V(G\odot H)
be a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
. Since for any
i\in[\n(G)],h\in V(H_{i})
and
u\in V(G\odot H)\setminus(\{v_{i}\}\cup V(H_{i}))
we have
d(u,h)=d(u,v_{i})+1
, it follows that
S\cap(\{v_{i}\}\cup V(H_{i}))\neq\varnothing
.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs, with
G
connected. If
S
is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
, then for any pair of distinct vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in V(G)
such that
B_{G}(v_{i}\mid v_{j})=\varnothing
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

v_{i}\in S
or
v_{j}\in S

(ii)

V(H_{i})\subseteq S
or
V(H_{j})\subseteq S

Proof.

Let
i,j\in[\n(G)]
be such that
i\neq j
and
B_{G}(v_{i}\mid v_{j})=\varnothing
. If
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\cap S=\varnothing
, then

cannot be a distance-equalizer set of

, which implies that
v_{i}\in S
or
v_{j}\in S
. Additionally,
B_{G\odot H}(u\mid w)=\varnothing
for any
u\in V(H_{i})
and any

; hence,
V(H_{i})\subseteq S
or
V(H_{j})\subseteq S
.
∎

Let

Definition 3.5

Let

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G
,
H
be two graphs, with
G
connected, and let
X,Y
be two vertex covers of
\widehat{G}
.
The set

S=\left(\bigcup\limits_{v_{i}\in X}V(H_{i})\right)\cup Y

is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
, if and only if,
(X,Y)
is a forward-equalized pair of
G
.

Proof.

(Necessity) Note that
U(S)=X
and
L(S)=Y
. Since
S
is a distance equalizer set of
G\odot H
, by Lemma
3.4
it follows that
X\cup Y=V(G)
. Additionally, if
u\in X\setminus Y
and
v=v_{i}\in Y\setminus X
, then
u,h_{i}\notin S
for any
h_{i}\in V(H_{i})
. Moreover, there exists some
j\in[\n(G)]
and a vertex
w\in\big(\{v_{j}\}\cup V(H_{j})\big)\cap S
such that

d_{G\odot H}(u,w)=d_{G\odot H}(w,h_{i}).

Consequently,
v_{j}\in B_{G\odot H}(u\mid h_{i})
, and we obtain

d_{G}(u,v_{j})=d_{G\odot H}(u,v_{j})=d_{G\odot H}(v_{j},h_{i})=d_{G}(v_{j},v_{i})+d_{G\odot H}(v_{i},h_{i})=d_{G}(v_{j},v_{i})+1,

as required.

Therefore,
(X,Y)
is a forward-equalized pair of
G
.

(Sufficiency) We differentiate all possible cases for any two vertices
u,v\in V(G\odot H)\setminus S
:

Case 1:

u,v\in V(H_{i})
. Note that this case is only possible if
\n(H)\geq 2
and
v_{i}\in Y\subseteq S
. Thus
S\cap B_{G\odot H}(u\mid v)\neq\varnothing
.

Case 2:

u\in V(H_{i})
and
v\in V(H_{j})
, with
i\neq j
. It should be noted that this case can occur only if
v_{i},v_{j}\in Y\setminus X
.
Since
Y\setminus X
is an independent set in
\widehat{G}
, we have
B_{G}(v_{i}\mid v_{j})\neq\varnothing
, and as a consequence, there exists a vertex
v_{k}\in B_{G\odot H}(v_{i}\mid v_{j})
. Since
\{v_{k}\}\cup V(H_{k})\subseteq B_{G\odot H}(v_{i}\mid v_{j})=B_{G\odot H}(u\mid v)
and
S\cap(\{v_{k}\}\cup V(H_{k}))\neq\varnothing
, then we get
S\cap B_{G\odot H}(u\mid v)\neq\varnothing
.

Case 3:

u,v\in V(G)
. This case is possible only when
u,v\in X\setminus Y
. Since
X\setminus Y
is an independent set in
\widehat{G}
, there exists a vertex
v_{k}\in B_{G}(u\mid v)
. Analogously to the previous case, we have
S\cap B_{G\odot H}(u\mid v)\neq\varnothing
.

Case 4:

u\in V(G)
and
v\in V(H_{j})
. Observe that this scenario is only feasible if
u\in X\setminus Y
and
v_{j}\in Y\setminus X
. Since
(X,Y)
is a forward-equalized pair of
G
, there exists a vertex
v_{k}\in V(G)
such that
d_{G\odot H}(u,v_{k})=d_{G}(u,v_{k})=d_{G}(v_{k},v_{j})+1=d_{G\odot H}(v_{k},v_{j})+d_{G\odot H}(v_{j},v)=d_{G\odot H}(v_{k},v)
. Hence,
v_{k}\in B_{G\odot H}(u\mid v)
. Analogously to Case 2, we can conclude that
S\cap B_{G\odot H}(u\mid v)\neq\varnothing
.

Based on the preceding cases, we conclude that
S
is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
.

∎

The following result aims to provide a well-structured
\xi(G\odot H)
-set, which simplifies the computation of
\xi(G\odot H)
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any two graphs
G
and
H
, with
G
connected, there exists a
\xi(G\odot H)
-set
S
, such that
V(H_{i})\cap S=\varnothing
or
V(H_{i})\subseteq S
, for every
i\in[\n(G)]
.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq V(G\odot H)
be such that
|S\setminus V(G)|
is minimum among all
\xi(G\odot H)
-sets
S
.

For
\n(H)=1
,
V(H_{i})\cap S\neq\varnothing
implies
V(H_{i})\subseteq S
, and the statement holds.

Otherwise, if
\n(H)\geq 2
, suppose there exist
u\in V(H_{i})\cap S
and
w\in V(H_{i})\setminus S
, for some
i\in[\n(G)]
.
Let us define the set
S^{\prime}\subseteq V(G\odot H)
in the following way:

S^{\prime}=(S\cup\{v_{i}\})\setminus\{u\}.

We claim that
S^{\prime}
is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
. To prove this, let us consider the possible cases for any pair of different vertices
x,y\in V(G\odot H)\setminus S^{\prime}
.

Case 1:

x,y\in V(H_{i})
. As
v_{i}\in S^{\prime}
, we have
B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap S^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
.

Case 2:

x,y\in V(G\odot H)\setminus V(H_{i})
. Note that, if
u\in B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)
, then
v_{i}\in B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap S^{\prime}
. Otherwise, if
u\notin B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)
, then
v_{i}\notin B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)
. Consequently,
B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap S^{\prime}=B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap S
. Since
S
is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
, we have
B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap S^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
.

Case 3:

x\in V(H_{i})
and
y\in V(G\odot H)\setminus V(H_{i})
. If
d_{G\odot H}(x,y)=2
, then
v_{i}\in B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap S^{\prime}
. Otherwise, if
d_{G\odot H}(x,y)>2
, then
B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap V(H_{i})=\varnothing
and
B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)=B_{G\odot H}(w\mid y)
. Consequently,
B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap S^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
, as
B_{G\odot H}(w\mid y)\cap(S\setminus\{u\})\neq\varnothing
.

As a result, for every
x,y\in V(G\odot H)\setminus S^{\prime}
, it holds that
B_{G\odot H}(x\mid y)\cap S^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
. This confirms that
S^{\prime}
is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
.

Next, consider two cases. If
v_{i}\in S
, we have
|S^{\prime}|=|S|-1<\xi(G\odot H)
, which yields a contradiction. Conversely, if
v_{i}\notin S
, then
|S^{\prime}\setminus V(G)|<|S\setminus V(G)|
, which contradicts the minimality assumption on
S
.
Therefore, the result follows.
∎

We introduce the following parameter, which will be used to establish a sharp lower bound for the equidistant dimension of corona product graphs.

Definition 3.9
.

For any connected graph
G
, we define

\beta^{*}(G)=\min\limits_{X,Y\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})}\{|X\cap Y|:(X,Y)\text{ is a forward-equalized pair of }G\}

Theorem 3.10
.

For any two graphs
G
and
H
, with
G
connected,

\xi(G\odot H)\geq\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G})+\beta^{*}(G).

Proof.

Let
S
be a
\xi(G\odot H)
-set, which satisfies the conditions of Lemma
3.7
. We recall that

S=\left(\bigcup_{v_{i}\in U(S)}V(H_{i})\right)\cup L(S).

Hence, by Theorem
3.6
,
(U(S),L(S))
is a forward-equalized pair of
G
. Since
|U(S)\cap L(S)|\geq\beta^{*}(G)
, we obtain:

\displaystyle|S|

\displaystyle=|U(S)|\n(H)+|L(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)+|L(S)|

\displaystyle\geq\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})+|L(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\n(\widehat{G})-\beta(\widehat{G})+|U(S)\cap L(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G})+|U(S)\cap L(S)|

\displaystyle\geq\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G})+\beta^{*}(G)

Therefore, the result follows.

∎

\displaystyle|S|

Implying that

The next result gives a characterization under which the equidistant dimension of the corona product graph

takes its minimum possible value,
\n(G)
. In fact, while this result ensures that the upper bound in Theorem

Theorem 3.15
.

If
G
is a connected graph and

any graph, then
\xi(G\odot H)=\n(G)
if and only if either
\beta(\widehat{G})=0
, or
(\n(H)=1
and
\beta^{*}(G)=0)
.

Proof.

(Necessity) Assume that
\xi(G\odot H)=\n(G)
. By Theorem
3.10
and Gallai’s Theorem

, we obtain

\displaystyle\beta(\widehat{G})\,\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G})+\beta^{*}(G)

\displaystyle\leq\n(G)

\displaystyle\leq\beta(\widehat{G})+\alpha(\widehat{G}).

Consequently,

\beta(\widehat{G})\bigl(\n(H)-1\bigr)+\beta^{*}(G)\leq 0.

Since all terms involved are nonnegative, the above inequality implies that

(\beta(\widehat{G})=0\text{ and }\beta^{*}(G)=0)\quad\text{or}\quad(\n(H)=1\text{ and }\beta^{*}(G)=0).

By Remark
3.13
, if
\beta(\widehat{G})=0
, then
\beta^{*}(G)=0
. Therefore, we conclude that either
\beta(\widehat{G})=0
, or
\n(H)=1
and
\beta^{*}(G)=0
.

(Sufficiency) If
\beta(\widehat{G})=0
, then applying the lower bound stated in Lemma
3.1
, together with the upper bound stated in Theorem

, the result follows.

Now, if
\beta^{*}(G)=0
, then there exists a forward-equalized pair
(U,L)
such that
U,L\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
,

, and

. By Theorem

, the set

S=\left(\bigcup\limits_{v_{i}\in U}V(H_{i})\right)\cup L

is a distance-equalizer set of

. In particular, if

, then

. Therefore, applying the lower bound from Lemma

, we conclude that
\xi(G\odot H)=\n(G)
.

We recall that, given two simple graphs

and

with disjoint vertex sets.
The

(or

and

, denoted by

, is the graph
with vertex set

and edge set

That is,
G_{1}+G_{2}
is obtained from the disjoint union of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
by adding all possible edges between every vertex of
G_{1}
and every vertex of
G_{2}
.

The following result is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.15
and highlights its applicability.

Proposition 3.16
.

If
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are two graphs such that at least one of them has no isolated vertices, then for any graph
H

\xi\big((G_{1}+G_{2})\odot H\big)=\n(G_{1})+\n(G_{2}).

Proof.

We aim to prove that
\beta\big(\widehat{G_{1}+G_{2}}\big)=0
.
Without loss of generality let us assume that
G_{1}
has no isolated vertex. For any pair of vertices
u,v\in V(G_{1}+G_{2})
we differentiate all possible cases:

Case 1:

If
u\in V(G_{1})
and
v\in V(G_{2})
, then, since
G_{1}
has no isolated vertices, there exists a vertex
w\in N_{G_{1}}(u)
. Hence, we have
d_{G_{1}+G_{2}}(w,u)=1=d_{G_{1}+G_{2}}(w,v)
.

Case 2:

If
u\in V(G_{1})
and
v\in V(G_{1})
, then for any vertex
w\in V(G_{2})
, it holds
d_{G_{1}+G_{2}}(w,u)=1=d_{G_{1}+G_{2}}(w,v)
.

Case 3:

If
u\in V(G_{2})
and
v\in V(G_{2})
, then for any vertex
w\in V(G_{1})
, it holds
d_{G_{1}+G_{2}}(w,u)=1=d_{G_{1}+G_{2}}(w,v)
.

In any case
B_{G_{1}+G_{2}}(u\mid v)\neq\varnothing
. Therefore the empty bisector graph
\widehat{G_{1}+G_{2}}
has no edges, which is equivalent to
\beta\big(\widehat{G_{1}+G_{2}}\big)=0
. Now applying Theorem
3.15
, we get

\xi\big((G_{1}+G_{2})\odot H\big)=\n(G_{1}+G_{2})=\n(G_{1})+\n(G_{2}).

∎

The following three results lead to the computation of the equidistant dimension in the case where
G
is a bipartite graph.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs such that
G
is connected. If the empty bisector graph
\widehat{G}
is a complete bipartite graph, then

\xi(G\odot H)=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G})

Proof.

Let
L
and
U
be the partite sets of
\widehat{G}
, with
|L|\geq|U|
.
Since
\widehat{G}
is a complete bipartite graph, we have that
L
is an
\alpha(\widehat{G})
-set and
U
is a
\beta(\widehat{G})
-set. Additionally,
L
and
U
are vertex covers of
\widehat{G}
.

Consider the set

S=\left(\bigcup_{v_{i}\in U}V(H_{i})\right)\cup L.

For any
u\in U
and
v\in L
, we have
u\in N_{\widehat{G}}(v)
. Hence,
B_{G}(v\mid v)=\varnothing
, and consequently,
d_{G}(u,v)
is odd. Thus, there exists a vertex
w
on a shortest path between
u
and
v
such that
d_{G}(u,w)=d_{G}(w,v)+1
. Therefore,
(U,L)
is a forward-equalized set of
G
.

Applying Theorem
3.6
, it follows that
S
is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
. Thus,

\xi(G\odot H)\leq|S|=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G}).

Furthermore, by Corollary
3.11
, we have the corresponding lower bound

\xi(G\odot H)\geq\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G}).

As a consequence, we conclude that

\xi(G\odot H)=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G}).

∎

Lemma 3.18
.

If
G
is a bipartite connected graph, then the corresponding empty bisector graph
\widehat{G}
is complete bipartite.

Proof.

Let
A
and
B
be the partite sets of
G
. We will distinguish two cases for any two different vertices
u
,
v\in V(G)
:

Case 1:

u\in A
and
v\in B
. Suppose that
B_{G}(u\mid v)\neq\varnothing
, then there exists a path of even length between
u
and
v
, in contradiction with
G
being a bipartite graph. Hence,
B_{G}(u\mid v)=\varnothing
and
\{u,v\}\in E(\widehat{G})
.

Case 2:

u,v\in A
or
u,v\in B
. The distance between two vertices in the same partite set is even, consequently there exists a vertex
w\in V(G)
such that
w\in B_{G}(u\mid v)
which leads to
\{u,v\}\notin E(\widehat{G})
.

Therefore,
A
and
B
are partite sets of
\widehat{G}
and every pair of vertices in different partite sets are adjacent in
\widehat{G}
.
∎

Considering that every connected bipartite graph has unique partite sets, together with Proposition

and Lemma

, we arrive at the following conclusion.

Theorem 3.19
.

If

is a connected bipartite graph, with partite sets

\xi(G\odot H)=|B|\n(H)+|A|.

4
Threshold-dependent linearity of
\xi(G\odot H)
respect to
\mathrm{n}(H)

Based on the upper and lower bounds established in Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
3.14
respectively, it follows that, for any connected graph
G
,

\lim_{\n(H)\to+\infty}\frac{\xi(G\odot H)}{\n(H)}=\beta(\widehat{G}).

The above limit shows that
\xi(G\odot H)
grows asymptotically linearly with
\n(H)
, with asymptotic growth rate given by
\beta(\widehat{G})
.

In this section, we go beyond this asymptotic characterization and prove a stronger result: for any connected graph
G
, there exists a threshold beyond which the equidistant dimension of
G\odot H
is not only asymptotically linear, but in fact a linear function of
\n(H)
, with slope
\beta(\widehat{G})
.

We first establish a few preliminary results that will be instrumental in the analysis.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs, with
G
connected, and let
S
be a
\xi(G\odot H)
-set satisfying the conditions of Lemma
3.7
.
If
\n(H)\geq 2
, then

|U(S)|\leq\beta(\widehat{G})\;+\;\min\!\left\{\frac{\alpha(\widehat{G})}{\n(H)},\;\frac{\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)}{\,\n(H)-1\,}\right\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
, we have that
U(S)
and
L(S)
are a vertex covers of
\widehat{G}
. Applying the upper bound stated in Theorem
3.14
, we obtain:

\displaystyle|U(S)|\n(H)+|L(S)|=|S|\leq\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\n(G),

\displaystyle\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)\leq\n(G)-|L(S)|\leq\n(G)-\beta(\widehat{G})=\alpha(\widehat{G}).

Which implies that

|U(S)|\leq\beta(\widehat{G})+\dfrac{\,\alpha(\widehat{G})}{\n(H)}.

In the other hand, we can proceed as follows:

\displaystyle|S|

\displaystyle=|U(S)|\n(H)+|L(S)|

\displaystyle=\left(|U(S)|+\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)+|L(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)+|L(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)+|L(S)\setminus U(S)|+|L(S)\cap U(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)+\n(\widehat{G})-|U(S)|+|L(S)\cap U(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G})+\beta(\widehat{G})-|U(S)|+|L(S)\cap U(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\left(\n(H)-1\right)+\alpha(\widehat{G})+|L(S)\cap U(S)|

Applying the upper bound of Theorem
3.14
again,

\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\left(\n(H)-1\right)+\alpha(\widehat{G})+|L(S)\cap U(S)|=|S|\leq\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H)+\n(G),

\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\left(\n(H)-1\right)+\alpha(\widehat{G})+|L(S)\cap U(S)|\leq\n(G),

\left(|U(S)|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\left(\n(H)-1\right)\leq\n(G)-\alpha(\widehat{G})-|L(S)\cap U(S)|=\beta(\widehat{G})-|L(S)\cap U(S)|\leq\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G).

Leading to

|U(S)|\leq\beta(\widehat{G})+\dfrac{\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)}{\n(H)-1}.

Therefore, the result follows.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs, with
G
connected, and let
S
be a
\xi(G\odot H)
-set that satisfies the conditions of Lemma
3.7
. If
\n(H)>\min\{\alpha(\widehat{G}),\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1\}
, then
U(S)
is a
\beta(\widehat{G})
-set.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
we have that
U(S)
is a vertex cover of
\widehat{G}
. Thus
|U(S)|\geq\beta(\widehat{G})
.

If
\n(H)>\min\{\alpha(\widehat{G}),\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1\}
, then applying Lemma
4.1
we get:

\displaystyle|U(S)|

\displaystyle\leq\beta(\widehat{G})+\min\left\{\dfrac{\,\alpha(\widehat{G})}{\n(H)},\dfrac{\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)}{\n(H)-1}\,\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\beta(\widehat{G})+\min\left\{\dfrac{\,\alpha(\widehat{G})}{\alpha(\widehat{G})+1},\dfrac{\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)}{\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1}\,\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\beta(\widehat{G}).

Consequently,
|U(S)|=\beta(\widehat{G})
.
∎

For the remainder of this section, we define the auxiliary function

R_{G}(H)=\xi(G\odot H)-\beta(\widehat{G})\n(H),

for any connected graph
G
and any graph
H
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any connected graph
G
and any graph

be a
\xi(G\odot H^{\prime})
-set, both satisfying the conditions of Lemma

.
Observe that

R_{G}(H)=\left(|U(S_{H})|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)+|L(S_{H})|.

(2)

Additionally, since
\n(H^{\prime})>\min\{\alpha(\widehat{G}),\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1\}

to the graph

, it follows that

(3)

Now, we consider the sets

W_{H}=\left(\bigcup_{v_{i}\in U(S_{H})}V(H_{i}^{\prime})\right)\cup L(S_{H}),

\displaystyle|U(S_{H})|\,\n(H)+|L(S_{H})|=|S_{H}|

(4)

\displaystyle\beta(\widehat{G})\,\n(H^{\prime})+|L(S_{H^{\prime}})|=|S_{H^{\prime}}|

(5)

From (

\left(|U(S_{H})|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\n(H)+|L(S_{H})|\leq|L(S_{H^{\prime}})|,

(6)

|L(S_{H^{\prime}})|\leq\left(|U(S_{H})|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\,\n(H^{\prime})+|L(S_{H})|.

(7)

Combining equations (

R_{G}(H)\leq R_{G}(H^{\prime})\leq\left(|U(S_{H})|-\beta(\widehat{G})\right)\,\n(H^{\prime})+|L(S_{H})|.

4.2

|U(S_{H})|=\beta(\widehat{G})

R_{G}(H)=|L(S_{H})|
. Therefore, from (

R_{G}(H)=R_{G}(H^{\prime}),

for every graph
H

\n(H)>\min\{\alpha(\widehat{G}),\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1\}

Theorem 4.4
.

For any connected graph
G

, with

\n(H)>\min\{\alpha(\widehat{G}),\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1\},

\n(H)\leq\min\{\alpha(\widehat{G}),\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1\}.

S
be a
\xi(G\odot H)
-set satisfying the structural conditions of Lemma
3.7
. If

\n(H)>n+1\geq\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1
,
then by Proposition
4.2
, the set
U(S)
is necessarily a
\beta(\widehat{G})
-set. Recalling that
V(G)=V(\widehat{G})
, we have

U(S)=V(K_{n})
.
Let us denote by
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
the vertices of
K_{n}
, and let
N_{m}^{i}
be the copy of
N_{m}
attached to
v_{i}
in
K_{n}\odot N_{m}
.
For any vertex
v_{i}\in U(S)
and any vertex
h_{j}\in V\bigl(N_{m}^{j}\bigr)\subseteq L(S)
,
there exists no vertex
w\in V(G)
such that

d_{G}(w,v_{i})=d_{G}(w,h_{j})+1.

Therefore,
L(S)=V(G)=V(K_{n}\odot N_{m})
is the unique subset
L(S)\in\mathcal{B}(\widehat{G})
such that
(U(S),L(S))
is a forward-equalized pair of
G
. Hence, the previous reasoning together with Theorem
3.6
leads to

\displaystyle\xi(G\odot H)=|S|

\displaystyle=|U(S)|\,\n(H)+|L(S)|

\displaystyle=\beta(\widehat{G})\,\n(H)+\n(G)

\displaystyle=\xi(G)\,\n(H)+\n(G),

for all
\n(H)>n+1
.

The previous example illustrates that the parameter
\beta^{*}(G)
is not always attained as the intersection
|U\cap L|
of a forward-equalized pair
(U,L)
, where
U
is a
\beta(\widehat{G})
-set.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
G
and
H
be a two graphs. If there exists a vertex
u\in V(G)
with
\varepsilon(u)\leq 2
, then

(i)

the sets
L=N_{G}(u)
and
U=V(G)\setminus N_{G}(u)
form a bipartition of
\widehat{G}
,

(ii)

the set

S=\left(\bigcup\limits_{v_{i}\in U}V(H_{i})\right)\cup L

is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
,

(iii)

\beta^{*}(G)=0
,

(iv)

\xi(G\odot K_{1})=\n(G)
.

Proof.

We will analyse each case separately.

(i)

Note that
L
and
U
are disjoint sets and
L\cup U=V(\widehat{G})
.
For any two vertices
v,w\in L
, we have
u\in B_{G}(v\mid w)
, which implies that
L
is an independent set in
\widehat{G}
.
Now, in order to prove that
U
is also an independent set of
\widehat{G}
, we distinguish two possible cases for any two distinct vertices
v,w\in U
:

•

v,w\in U\setminus\{u\}
.
In this case
d_{G}(v,u)=d_{G}(u,w)=2
, thus
u\in B_{G}(v\mid w)
.

•

v=u
and
w\in U\setminus\{u\}
.
In this case
d_{G}(u,w)=2
, hence there exists a vertex
x\in N_{G}(u)=L
such that

d_{G}(v,x)=1=d_{G}(x,w)
, leading to
B_{G}(v\mid w)\neq\varnothing
.

Consequently,
L
and
U
are partite sets of the bipartite graph
\widehat{G}
.

(ii)

Since
U
and
L
are independent sets of
\widehat{G}
, we have
V(\widehat{G})\setminus U=L
and
V(\widehat{G})\setminus L=U
are vertex covers of
\widehat{G}
. Let
x\in L
and
y\in U
. If
x\in N_{G}(y)
, then
d_{G}(x,y)=d_{G}(x,x)+1
. Otherwise, if
x\notin N_{G}(y)
, then
d_{G}(u,y)=d_{G}(u,x)+1=2
. Hence,
(U,L)
is a forward-equalized pair of
G
. Now, applying Theorem
3.6
, it follows that
S
is a distance-equalizer set of
G\odot H
.

(iii)

The sets
U
and
L
are vertex covers of
\widehat{G}
, and
(U,L)
forms a forward-equalized pair of
G
. Since
U\cap L=\varnothing
, it follows that
\beta^{*}(G)=0
.

(iv)

As
\beta^{*}(G)=0
, by Theorem
3.15
, we have that
\xi(G\odot K_{1})=\n(G)
.

∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
G
and
H
be a two graphs. If there exists a vertex
u\in V(G)
with
\varepsilon(u)\leq 2
, then the graph
\widehat{G}
is bipartite and

\xi(G\odot H)\leq\bigl(\n(G)-\delta(u)\bigr)\n(H)+\delta(u).

Relying on Corollary
5.4
we can provide a family of base graphs
G
for which the lower bound stated in Corollary
3.11
is attained.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
G
and
H
be a two graphs. If there exists a vertex
u\in V(G)
with
\varepsilon(u)\leq 2
and
\delta(u)=\alpha(\widehat{G})
, then

\xi(G\odot H)=\beta(\widehat{G})\,\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G})

Proof.

Applying Corollary
5.4
and the Gallai’s Theorem
2.7
, we have

\xi(G\odot H)\leq\bigl(\n(G)-\delta(u)\bigr)\n(H)+\delta(u)=\beta(\widehat{G})\,\n(H)+\alpha(\widehat{G}).

Now, applying Corollary
3.11
the equality follows.

∎

Proposition 5.6
.

For every graph
G
, such that
\Delta(G)=\n(G)-1
, and every graph
H
,

\xi(G\odot H)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\n(G),&\text{if }\delta(G)\geq 2\\
\n(H)+\n(G)-1,&\text{if }\delta(G)=1\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let be
u
an universal vertex of
G
. Note that
N_{G}(u)
is an independent set in
\widehat{G}
.

If
\delta(G)\geq 2
, then there exists a vertex
w\in N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)\subseteq B_{G}(v\mid u)
for every
v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}
. Consequently,
\alpha(\widehat{G})=\n(G)
, which is equivalent to
\beta(\widehat{G})=0
. Now, applying Theorem
3.15
, we get
\xi(G\odot H)=\n(G)
.

If
\delta(G)=1
, then for every vertex
v\in V(G)
of degree one we have
B_{G}(v\mid u)=\varnothing
. Consequently,
\{u,v\}\in E(\widehat{G})
.
It follows that
N_{G}(u)
is an
\alpha(\widehat{G})
-set. Moreover, in this case we have

\alpha(\widehat{G})=\n(G)-1=\delta(u)
.
Hence, by Proposition
5.5
, it follows that

\xi(G\odot H)=\bigl(\n(G)-\delta(u)\bigr)\n(H)+\delta(u)=\n(H)+\n(G)-1.

This completes the proof.
∎

6
Concluding remarks and future work

In this paper, we have undertaken a systematic study of the equidistant dimension of the corona product
G\odot H
of two graphs
G
and
H
. To this end, we introduced the
empty bisector graph

\widehat{G}
, a novel auxiliary construction that plays a central role in our analysis. This graph encodes pairs of vertices in
G
that cannot be equidistantly distinguished by any third vertex, thereby providing an effective framework for capturing the equidistance constraints inherent to the base graph. Using this approach, we derived tight lower and upper bounds for the equidistant dimension of general corona product graphs and determined the exact value of
\xi(G\odot H)
for several classical families of graphs.

The empty bisector graph emerges as a central tool in the study of the equidistant dimension of corona products. Although we established several relationships between the structural parameters of
G
and
\widehat{G}
, a more systematic investigation of this connection could further extend the scope of our analysis.
Indeed, since
\xi(G\odot H)
becomes a linear function of
\n(H)
beyond the threshold
\min\{\alpha(\widehat{G}),\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1\}
, with slope
\beta(\widehat{G})
, the study of this parameter may provide further insight into the structural behaviour of the corona product.
In particular, it would be of interest to derive explicit expressions or sharp bounds for the vertex cover number
\beta(\widehat{G})
in terms of well-known invariants of
G
, especially for graph families not considered in this work. Such results would further clarify the role of
\widehat{G}
and potentially extend the applicability of our methods.

An open problem that remains to be investigated is whether the threshold
\min\{\alpha(\widehat{G}),\beta(\widehat{G})-\beta^{*}(G)+1\}
, beyond which
\xi(G\odot H)
becomes a linear function of
\n(H)
, can be reduced. In fact, the examples of graphs
G
we have presented (Figures
4
and
5
), for which this linearity fails, correspond to the cases
\n(H)=1
and
\n(H)\leq 2
, respectively. In neither case do these values reach the threshold established for linearity.

The fact that, for any fixed connected graph
G
, the equidistant dimension
of
G\odot H
eventually becomes linear in
\n(H)
, with slope

\beta(\widehat{G})
, leads us to ask when two non-isomorphic graphs
G_{1}

and
G_{2}
yield isomorphic empty bisector graphs. Identifying such conditions would explain when different base graphs induce identical asymptotic behaviour of the equidistant dimension under the corona product with arbitrary graphs
H
.

Finally, we leave open the problem of characterizing the graphs for which the
lower and upper bounds established in Theorems
3.10

and
3.14
, respectively, are attained. Although we have identified
a sufficient condition under which the lower bound in
Theorem
3.10
is achieved, a complete characterization of such
graphs is still missing. A full description of these extremal cases would
contribute to a deeper understanding of the tightness of our results and of the
structural features governing the equidistant dimension of corona products.

Role of the channel on the memory window of HfZrOx ferroelectric field-effect transistors with p-type Si-doped InZnOx channel
Authors: Hyoungjin Park, Seokjae Lim, Somi Lee, Jae Woo Lee, Jiyong Woo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo elucidate the origin of the memory window (MW) of HfZrOx ferroelectric field-effect transistors (FeFETs) with a p-type Si-doped InZnOx (p-Si:InZnO) channel, we perform a comparative study with thin-film transistors (TFTs) in which the HfZrOx ferroelectric layer (FL) of the FeFETs is replaced by an HfO2 dielectric layer (DL). The TFT is turned on at a threshold voltage (VT) of − 3 V and exhibits a reliable hysteresis-free transfer curve with a subthreshold swing (SS) of 265 mV/dec. However, when ferroelectric switching occurs in the FeFETs, the dipoles aligned downward in the FL hinder electron accumulation in the channel, yielding a high VT of approximately − 1 V. In contrast, the inverted dipoles in the FL attract electrons owing to the opposite gate voltage, corresponding to program (PGM). Consequently, a low VT (VLT) of − 3 V is achieved, thereby resulting in an MW of 2 V in the FeFETs. Notably, the steepened SS of 250 mV/dec and VLT obtained from the FeFETs during PGM are in good agreement with those obtained from the TFTs, implying that the FeFETs and TFTs are correlated. Moreover, temperature-dependent measurements reveal that the coercive voltage required to rotate the dipoles of the FL remains nearly constant, whereas the VLT and VHT of the FeFETs vary considerably. These results indicate that polarization in the FL serves as a driving force for the back-and-forth movement of electrons in the channel, and the degree of change in the VT of the FeFET is associated with the inherent channel properties.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-30961-y.Subject terms: Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

Since the discovery of ferroelectricity in Si-doped HfO₂, the exploration and engineering of HfO₂-based ferroelectric materials have attracted considerable research interest because these materials show excellent compatibility with complementary metal–oxide–semiconductor technology and superior scalability1–5. These characteristics have enabled the effective integration of these materials with ferroelectric field-effect transistors (FeFETs) and ferroelectric capacitors (FeCAPs), which are considered key components for extending the current three-dimensional (3D) NAND and dynamic random access memory technologies, respectively6–8. In particular, metal-ferroelectric-semiconductor (MFS) FeFETs offer excellent non-volatile memory operations under lower power and a simpler memory cell structure than the charge-trap flash structure of the 3D NAND8. Therefore, a reliable high-density 3D ferroelectric NAND has been demonstrated8–10. Additionally, FeFETs have been used to accelerate artificial intelligence algorithms for energy-efficient compute-in-memory applications11.

In MFS FeFETs, the conductivity of the semiconductor channel is modulated based on a threshold voltage (VT) shift using the spontaneous polarization of the ferroelectric layer (FL)6,11. When the positively (or negatively) charged portion of the polarization is located near the channel, electrons in the channel are attracted (or repelled), thereby decreasing or increasing the VT, corresponding to program (PGM) or erase (ERS). The memory window (MW) can be obtained as the difference between the low VT (VLT) and high VT (VHT). To date, many attempts have been made to expand the MWs of FeFETs to improve reliability in multi-level cell storage11–13. Theoretically, the maximum achievable MW is given by 2Ec × tFE, where Ec is the ferroelectric coercive field and tFE is the FL thickness14. However, actual MWs smaller than 2 V have mostly been obtained from MFS FeFETs9,13. MWs larger than 10 V have been obtained either by adjusting different metal electrode-to-FL area ratios in metal-ferroelectric-metal–insulator-semiconductor FeFETs or by applying additional gate injection mechanisms to metal–insulator-ferroelectric-insulator-semiconductor FeFETs15–17. However, the high complexity of the FeFET structure remains a drawback. In this regard, extensive studies have been conducted to understand the origin of MWs in simplified MFS FeFETs. A recent study demonstrated that charge trapping at the interface between an FL and the native oxide above an Si channel strongly influences MW formation, indicating that an MW can be determined using factors other than Ec (or coercive voltage (Vc))14. However, most studies have primarily focused on the polarization-related properties of FLs as the root cause of MW formation, while the role of the channels in FeFETs has often been neglected. In particular, understanding the impact of the channel on the MWs of MFS FeFETs is becoming increasingly important, as the interest in FeFETs with oxide semiconductor channels (e.g., InGaZnOx) has significantly increased owing to the native oxide-free nature that ensures robust reliability13,18–21. Despite such high interest, InGaZnOx composition tuning for FeFET performance improvement exhibits an intrinsic trade-off between carrier concentration and stability. Increasing the In content in oxide semiconductors enhances electron concentration and field-effect mobility through the overlap of In-5s orbitals, but it also facilitates the formation of shallow oxygen vacancy (VO) donors, leading to higher off-currents and degraded bias-stress stability. Conversely, increasing the Ga content suppresses these vacancies and improves stability but reduces carrier density and mobility. This implies that identifying InGaZnOx compositions suitable for FeFET applications remains challenging, which is why the impact of the channel on MW in FeFET has been rarely explored to date.

Therefore, in this study, we aimed to elucidate the influence of a p-type Si-doped InZnOx (p-Si:InZnO) channel on the MWs of MFS FeFETs by comparing FeFETs and thin-film transistors (TFTs) with an HfZrOx FL and HfO2 DL, respectively. The experimentally obtained switching parameters, such as VT and subthreshold swing (SS), of each device revealed that the PGM of the FeFETs, under which electrons in the channel tend to be pulled toward the FL, is linked to the inherent properties of the TFT. In particular, unlike the intrinsic trade-off between carrier concentration and stability observed in InGaZnOx composition tuning, we selectively incorporated p-Si at the HfZrOx/InZnO interface, which effectively reduced the free electron concentration and interfacial trap density, thereby making the effects associated with dipoles more pronounced.

An ~ 80-nm Mo bottom gate was first deposited via DC sputtering on an Si wafer containing a 100-nm-thick thermally grown SiO2 layer. Subsequently, a 22-nm-thick HfZrOx FL (or a 22-nm-thick HfO₂ DL) was deposited via atomic layer deposition at 250 °C (or 200 °C) to obtain the FeFET (or TFT). Notably, in our preliminary study, excellent characteristics were obtained from an FeFET with 4-nm InZnO channels partially doped with p-type Si only at the channel interface22. Therefore, in this study, this interfacial p-Si:InZnO channel was used. Specifically, a 2-nm-thick p-Si:InZnO film was deposited via co-sputtering as follows. A B-doped p-type Si target was sputtered using a DC power source, and the InZnO (In₂O₃:ZnO = 90:10 wt%) target was sputtered using a radio frequency source under Ar and O2 gas flow rates of 30 and 5 sccm, respectively. A 2-nm-thick undoped InZnO layer was deposited subsequently by sputtering an InZnO (In₂O₃:ZnO = 90:10 wt%) target under the same Ar and O2 gas flow rates. After the Mo source/drain contacts were deposited via DC sputtering, post-metallization annealing (PMA) was performed at 400 °C for 1 min in ambient air on the FeFET and TFT.

Figure 1a schematically illustrates the distinct role of the p-Si:InZnO channel in the FeFET and TFT. The features of this channel can be understood by examining three sub-peak intensities observed in the O 1s X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) spectrum of the upper InZnO and lower p-Si:InZnO layers within the channel shown in Fig. 1b. A similar peak at 530.3 eV, corresponding to VO23, appeared in the spectra obtained from the InZnO and p-Si:InZnO regions. However, the peak at 531 eV, associated with O–H bonds, increased by 21.6%24, whereas the peak at 529.8 eV, corresponding to the oxygen lattice (Olattice)25, decreased by 37.6% with respect to those obtained from InZnO. This indicates that incorporating p-Si distorted the InZnO matrix, increasing the O–H bond content by weakening the Olattice bonds. In general, the transfer characteristics obtained from the TFTs containing n-type oxide semiconductor channels can be attributed to the formation of a sufficient concentration of VO, acting as an electron donor, during PMA. Thus, many electrons formed in the sputter-deposited InZnO bulk region respond rapidly to the gate voltage (VGS)26. In contrast, the p-Si doping at the channel interface eliminated the electrons near the surface region, forming a quasi-depletion region. Therefore, a pronounced difference in the responses of electrons to the FL or DL was observed when the interfacial p-Si:InZnO channel was used.

To confirm that p-Si was properly incorporated at the interface, the compositional and electronic properties of the interfacial p-Si:InZnO channel were analyzed, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Magnetic-sector secondary-ion mass spectrometry (M-SIMS) depth profile revealed distinct Si (~ 3 × 102 c/s) and B (~ 1 × 10 c/s), where c/s denotes counts per second. near the HfZrOx/InZnO interface (Figure S1 in supplementary information). In addition, the p-Si:InZnO TFTs exhibited a positive VT shift, an improved SS, and a reduced interface-trap density compared with undoped InZnO devices, while showing a slight decrease in field-effect mobility due to reduced free carrier density (Figure S2 in supplementary information). Consequently, the interfacial p-Si:InZnO channel can be interpreted as exhibiting a locally reduced carrier concentration near the interface, which provides the physical basis for the model proposed in this study.

The cross-sectional transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images depicted in Fig. 2a reveal differences in the crystallographic features of the HfZrOx FL and HfO2 DL in the presence of the p-Si:InZnO channel in the FeFET and TFT. Diffraction patterns corresponding to the monoclinic phase in the HfO₂ DL, specifically the () and (200) planes, were predominantly observed via fast Fourier transform (FFT) analysis. In contrast, the HfZrOx FL exhibited patterns corresponding to the (111) and (010) planes associated with the orthorhombic (o) phase, responsible for ferroelectricity. Moreover, o-phase formation in the HfZrOx FL in the FeFET is further validated via grazing incidence X-ray diffraction (GIXRD), as depicted in Fig. 2b. Prominent GIXRD peaks were detected at 30.5° and 35.7°27,28, corresponding to the (011) and (200) planes of the o-phase, respectively. These physical analysis results suggest that the polarization properties of the FL can be attributed to proper crystallization into the o-phase.

The fast Fourier transform images show the orthorhombic and monoclinic phases in the HfZrOx FL and HfO2 DL, respectively. (b) Grazing incidence X-ray diffraction pattern showing prominent peaks at approximately 30.5° and 35.7°, indicating the formation of an orthorhombic phase.

The ferroelectric characteristics of the Mo/p-Si:InZnO/HfZrOx FL/Mo gate stack were evaluated at 1 kHz using the positive-up-negative-down method, effectively suppressing the leakage current components29, under an applied voltage of ± 5 V. As displayed in Fig. 3a, a well-saturated and nearly rectangular polarization-voltage (P–V) hysteresis loop is obtained from the stack. The 2Pr was extracted as approximately 41 μC/cm2. The coercive voltage (2Vc) was determined to be approximately 8 V. The capacitance–voltage (C-V) characteristics of the gate stack, measured under a ± 3 V sweep condition at a frequency of 1 kHz, exhibited a typical butterfly curve, with two distinct capacitance peaks appearing close to − 2 V and + 1 V, attributable to most of the polarized dipoles (Fig. 3b).

(b) Capacitance–voltage characteristics of the same stack measured at 1 kHz under a ± 3 V sweep. (c) Hysteresis-free transfer curves obtained from 15 cells of the TFTs containing a p-Si:InZnO/HfO2 DL/Mo stack by sweeping VGS from − 5 V to 5 V under a VDS of 0.1 V at temperatures of 300 and 133 K.

Subsequently, we investigate the electrical performance of the p-Si:InZnO/HfO₂ DL/Mo stacked TFT to evaluate the characteristics of the p-Si:InZnO channel, as displayed in Fig. 3c. Hysteresis-free transfer curves were obtained from 15 cells under the double-VGS sweep mode, with the drain-source voltage (VDS) set as 0.1 V. At 300 K, the median on-current (Ion) extracted from the 15 cells at a VGS of + 5 V was approximately 12 μA, representing a substantial increase in relation to the gate current of 0.1 nA. The median SS was determined to be 265 mV/dec. Under the given bias conditions, the TFTs yielded an improved transfer curve with a steep SS of 109 mV/dec at 133 K, whereas the gate current remained below ~ 10 pA. This can be attributed to the alleviated phonon scattering as the measurement temperature decreased, and thermally activated carriers were suppressed, thereby enhancing SS30,31. Additionally, the VT, defined as the VGS under a drain current of 1 nA, was observed at a high VGS of − 2 V owing to a reduced free carrier concentration in the channel at low temperatures.

When the HfO₂ DL of the TFT is replaced with the HfZrOx FL only in the FeFET, a counter-clockwise hysteresis loop is observed in the transfer curve under the bipolar sweep of ± 5 V (Fig. 4a). The dipoles were initially aligned in the downward direction in the FL at a VGS of − 5 V. Therefore, during the forward VGS sweep, the dipoles pushed electrons away from the channel; therefore, a VHT of − 1 V was achieved. Subsequently, as the dipoles were inverted at a VGS of 5 V, electrons were easily attracted near the channel. This yielded a transfer curve with a VLT of − 3 V during the backward VGS sweep, thereby generating an MW of 2 V. Notably, the SS of 250 mV/dec and VLT obtained from the PGM state of the FeFET were well matched with those obtained from the TFT, implying that the FeFET and TFT were intrinsically linked. To further clarify this correlation, the Ion and VT of the devices are compared and analyzed, as displayed in Fig. 4b. Each parameter was extracted after a single VGS sweep from − 5 V to 5 V. Before PMA, the un-annealed FeFET served as a switch that turned on at a VT of 0 V without an MW. In general, PMA generated electrons in the channel and induced ferroelectricity in the FL. Therefore, the annealed TFT turned on rapidly at a reduced VT of − 3 V owing to the high electron density in the channel. As the generated dipoles were aligned in the FL owing to a VGS of − 5 V, electron accumulation near the channel interface of the PMA-treated FeFETs was prevented. Therefore, a VHT of − 1 V was achieved in the annealed FeFET. Meanwhile, before PMA, the un-annealed FeFET exhibited a slightly lower Ion than the un-annealed TFT. This lower Ion can be attributed to the incorporation of Zr into HfO₂, increasing trap density at the channel interface instead of increasing the dielectric constant32,33. After PMA, the Ion drivability of both devices was boosted; however, the annealed FeFET continued to exhibit a lower Ion than the annealed TFT. The PMA conditions, such as temperature and time, applied in this study were sufficient to generate VO in the channel but were insufficient to remove the traps; therefore, the FeFET exhibited a low Ion even in the annealed state33.

(c) During the VGS sweep, the switching current owing to polarization is increased proportional to the VGS amplitude. When the switching current is observed, changes in the VLT and VHT of the FeFET can also be observed. Next, we investigated the VLT and VHT of the FeFETs by progressive VGS ramping to identify the influence of dipole switching in the FL on the parameters (Fig. 4c). When a VGS of less than 3.5 V was applied, only hysteresis-free transfer curves without VT splitting were obtained. As the ramped VGS reached or exceeded 3.5 V, switching current in the form of gate leakage became observable, indicating that the polarized dipoles were aligned. The upward-inverted dipoles in the FL of the FeFET attracted electrons, considerably lowering the VLT. As the number of rotated dipoles was determined based on the VGS amplitude, the VLT was found to steadily decrease from − 1.6 V to − 3 V during the backward VGS sweep, under a high applied VGS. Simultaneously, as the VGS increased, the VHT was slightly reduced from − 1.6 V to − 1.8 V. This is because charge trapping within the channel can be induced owing to the high VGS applied to the channel and the FL. Notably, the reduced VLT inversely proportional to the VGS amplitude became constant at − 3 V, although a VGS higher than 5 V was applied. More importantly, the converged VLT and SS obtained from the PGM state of the FeFETs were in good agreement with those of the TFT, as confirmed via statistical analysis conducted on 20 devices (Fig. 4d).

To explain the analogy observed between the TFT and the FeFET, temperature-dependent P–V measurements were conducted on a Mo/HfZrOx/Mo FeCAP at 1 kHz using a bipolar triangular waveform of ± 3 V. Figure 5a depicts a negligible Vc change of less than 0.2 V across the temperature range of 153–243 K. This indicates that the force required to rotate the dipoles remains the same even if the external environment is frozen. It has been generally believed that the achievable MW of an FeFET is mainly determined by the ferroelectric polarization properties (e.g., Vc)14. Therefore, nearly constant VLT and VHT of the FeFET were expected to be achieved owing to the temperature-independent Vc of the FL. However, Fig. 5b displays that the changes in the VLT and VHT of the FeFET were highly pronounced under the given temperature range. These results imply that how well the o-phase is formed in the FL for polarization and the behavior of the electrons in the channel in response to the dipoles influence the formation of the MW. Although 2Vc remains nearly constant, the pronounced variation in VLT and VHT with temperature mainly arises from the temperature-dependent electronic response of the p-Si:InZnO channel rather than from variations in ferroelectric switching strength. At lower temperatures, the number of thermally activated carriers in the InZnO channel decreases owing to carrier freeze-out, leading to a reduction in free electron density and weakened gate controllability32,33. As a result, higher VGS are required to induce channel conduction, causing both VLT and VHT to shift in the positive direction. These results indicate that the temperature-dependent evolution of the MW mainly arises from the carrier freeze-out effect in the p-Si:InZnO channel, while the intrinsic ferroelectric switching characteristics of the HfZrOx layer remain unchanged. In the vertical direction of the FeFET, which is conventionally used to describe the MW, only as many electrons as are pre-formed in the channel during PMA participate in the switching, irrespective of the strength of the dipoles acting on the channel. Therefore, the VLT did not reduce unless the carrier concentration in the channel was modulated; therefore, the PGM of the FeFET was similar to that of the TFT. In contrast, the VHT can be further increased by enhancing or sensitizing the extent to which electrons are pushed away from the channel interface by the negative VGS signal. Therefore, after the treatment to remove the traps present at the HfZrOx FL/Si channel interface, dipole-induced electron movement becomes noticeable, widening the MW, as discussed in the introduction section34. Moreover, this is in good agreement with our finding that selectively doping p-Si into the interfacial region of the InZnO channel increased the VHT by reducing the number of carriers at the interface, thereby enlarging the MW22.

Herein, we conducted a comparative study and identified the influence of the channel properties, which have been neglected in previous studies, on the MWs of MFS FeFETs containing 4-nm-thick p-Si:InZnO channel/22-nm-thick HfZrOx FL in relation to that of a TFT containing 4-nm-thick p-Si:InZnO channel/22-nm-thick HfO2 DL. As PMA yielded the o-phase responsible for dipole switching, as verified via TEM and GIXRD analyses, the transfer curve of the annealed FeFETs exhibited counterclockwise hysteresis under the double-VGS-sweep mode. During PGM, the VLT, inversely proportional to the VGS amplitude, was lowered owing to the dipole switching of the FeFET, achieving an MW of 2 V. Notably, the lowered VLT of − 3 V and steepened SS of 250 mV/dec obtained in the PGM state of the FeFETs were almost similar to those of the TFTs. Although the Vc obtained from the HfZrOx FL showed negligible change as per the P–V test results across different temperatures down to 153 K, both the VLT and VHT of the FeFETs increased at low temperatures. These results indicate that the Pr of the HfZrOx FL serves as a driving force to move electrons in the channel back and forth toward the FL, generating the VLT and VHT. More importantly, we revealed that the size of the MW is related to the inherent channel properties, such as carrier concentration and FL/channel interface state.

Acceleration without photon pair creation
Sara Kanzi, Daniel Hodgson, Almut Beige

Abstract
Whenever an experiment can be described classically, quantum physics must predict the same outcome. Intuitively, there is nothing quantum about an accelerating observer travelling through vacuum. It is, therefore, not surprising that many people are puzzled by the Unruh effect, which predicts that the observer encounters photons in a thermal state. This paper uses a recently introduced local photon approach to show that the quantised electromagnetic field in a non-inertial reference frame can be modelled without violating the principles of general relativity while both observers share a common vacuum. The only difference between a resting and an accelerating observer is that they each experience different worldline densities which implies different zero point energy densities in each reference frame.

An effect that is closely related to the Unruh effect, but much better understood, is the relativistic Doppler effect

Another way of describing the Doppler effect is to notice that the frequency of monochromatic waves of light is lower in Bob’s reference frame than in Alice’s. Most importantly, if the electromagnetic (EM) field is in its vacuum state in one frame, the same is true in the other. Taking this into account, the particle generation predicted by the Unruh effect seems even more mysterious since the trajectory of an accelerating observer can be approximated by a collection of short time intervals during which the observer moves at a constant speed

In this paper, we therefore revisit the validity of the standard conclusions of light propagation in non-inertial reference frames by making use of a recent physically motivated quantisation of the EM field in position space

The main advantage of considering local instead of monochromatic photons as the basic building blocks of the quantised EM field is that this allows us to make use of the principle of relativity and of spatial and time translational symmetries in a more direct way. Moreover, we can avoid the approximation of standing modes in a flat spacetime, which usually accompanies the derivation of the Unruh effect

In general, the difference in Alice’s and Bob’s coordinates

We determined that each excitation of the EM field gained an overall factor corresponding to a Doppler factor and relating to the bunching or anti-bunching of worldlines. Nevertheless, a local excitation of the EM field remains a local excitation, and the vacuum is preserved in both reference frames. As we shall see below, the same statement holds in the case of an accelerating observer who is usually associated with the Unruh effect

Local Photons.
From classical electromagnetism, we know that wave packets of light can occupy definite positions in space while moving at the speed of light

In our local photon approach, we introduce local carriers of light—so-called

Coordinate transformations.
As mentioned already above, due to the relative motion of Alice and Bob, their natural coordinates
\chi_{\rm A}
and
\chi_{\rm B}
are, in general, not the same, even when referring to the same light-like trajectory. However, independent of how Alice and Bob move with respect to each other, events that are localised in space and time in one reference frame are also localised in the other
Hodgson:2024mfs

Analogously, events that occur at the same location, but at different times, in one reference frame are spatially separated in another. Moreover, we notice that the direction of propagation of light is the same in both reference frames, as illustrated in Fig.
2

\chi_{\rm A}
and
\chi_{\rm B}

Implications for the Unruh effect.
In the following, we denote the blip annihilation operators in Alice’s and Bob’s reference frames by
a_{s\lambda}(\chi_{A})
and
b_{s\lambda}(\chi_{B})
respectively. As pointed out earlier, each blip operator is associated with a light-like trajectory or ray in the spacetime diagram. Since we also know that

and

Conclusions.
In this study, we adopted a local representation of single-photon excitations
Jake
;
Hodgson2022
;
Gabe
and associated each invariant light-like trajectory with blip annihilation operators
a_{s\lambda}(\chi_{\rm A})
and
b_{s\lambda}(\chi_{\rm B})
in the stationary and in the moving reference frames respectively. The local transformation between both sets of operators (cf. Eq. (
5
)) can be established whilst avoiding the flat spacetime approximation which is usually made in derivations of the Unruh effect
Maybee
.
Our theory predicts changes in the local density of light-like worldlines in the reference frame of the moving observer which alters the zero point energy of the quantised EM field. Nevertheless, the vacuum remains common to all observers. In this way, our observations differ from the standard photon pair creation result which became known as the Unruh effect
Unruh
;
Unruh2
;
Unruh3
. Our approach is therefore likely to stimulate further research into quantum electrodynamics in non-inertial reference frames.

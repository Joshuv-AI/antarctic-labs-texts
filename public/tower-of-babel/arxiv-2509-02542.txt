Soft Theorems in Chern-Simons Matter Theories
Avi Wadhwa

Abstract
I will describe tree level soft theorems for Chern-Simons QED and QCD in 4+1 dimensions. Soft theorems have been studied in various context in the past. Recently, there has been renewed interest in these theorems due to their connection with asymptotic symmetries and gauge invariance. Chern-Simons theories are gauge invariant only up to a boundary term. Hence, they make for an interesting case to study the relationship between gauge invariance and soft theorems. The aim of this work is to include corrections due to the addition of Chern-Simons terms in the gauge field action. I will start with an overview of Chern-Simons theories in 4+1 dimensions. Then I will move on to a discussion on soft theorems in gauge invariant QFT at the subleading order using Sen’s covariantisation procedure. Finally, I will calculate the correction due to the addition of a Chern-Simons term for both QED and QCD.

Introduction

Soft theorems are statements about scattering amplitudes of processes
involving external states with low energy (soft) gauge bosons. The
leading soft theorems relate the amplitudes with soft bosons to those
without them by a “universal” soft factor which has a pole divergence
in the soft momenta. It has been shown in various previous works that
the leading order soft theorems are related to the gauge invariance
of the scattering amplitudes via appropriate Ward identities

). In this
note, we will calculate how the leading and subleading soft theorems
get corrected when we introduce a Chern-Simons term in QED and QCD.

Chern-Simons terms are only defined in odd space-time dimensions.
In this paper, we will work in 4+1 space-time dimensions instead of
the usual 2+1 dimensions since we want to treat the Chern-Simons term
perturbatively about the Yang Mills theory.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{i}.k}\left[\varepsilon_{i}^{T}p_{i}^{\mu}e_{\mu}^{T}+k_{\rho}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}p_{i}^{\mu}e_{\mu}^{T}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\rho}}+\frac{i}{4}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{\partial K_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\nu}}\frac{\partial\Xi_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\mu}}\left(k_{\nu}e_{\mu}-k_{\mu}e_{\nu}\right)^{T}\right]G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right),

(18)

where in the last line we have suppressed the representation indices

\alpha
,
\beta
etc. to tidy up the equation. As mentioned previously,
the notation
G_{n}^{\left(i\right),\alpha_{i}}
is shorthand for

\prod_{j\neq i}\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}G_{n}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{i}\ldots\alpha_{n}}

which is the
n
point Green’s function with the
i
th polarization
vector amputated. The subscript
i
in various places indicates that
the
i
th index is summed over in the implicit matrix product. Now
we want to evaluate the contribution due to diagrams of type
A2
.
For this we need the amputated Green’s function
\tilde{G}_{n+1}\left(e,k;p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\right)

of
n
finite energy fields and one soft gauge field which only includes
diagrams in which the soft particle is not attached to any external
leg. This is obtained by covariantising the amputated Green’s function

G_{n}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{n}}\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\right)

of
n
finite energy fields and taking only leading order contribution
(since the corresponding diagram is already subleading due to the
absence of a pole in the propagator). Hence we have,

\tilde{G}_{n+1}\left(e,k;p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\right)=-\sum_{i}\left(\prod_{j\neq i}\varepsilon_{\alpha_{j}}\right)\varepsilon_{\alpha_{i}}e_{\mu}{}_{\beta_{i}}^{\,\,\,\,\alpha_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\mu}}G_{n}^{\alpha_{1}\ldots\beta_{i}\ldots\alpha_{n}}.

The contribution due to the Feynman diagram of type
A2
will be,

A2=-\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}e_{\mu}\frac{\partial G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}}{\partial p_{j\mu}}.

Summing over soft boson attaching to all distinct external legs for

A1
and adding to
A2
we get,

\displaystyle G_{n+1}\left(e,k;\varepsilon_{i},p_{i}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{e^{T}.p_{i}}{p_{i}.k}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{e_{\mu}^{T}k_{\nu}}{p_{i}.k}\left\{p_{i}^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\nu}}-p_{i}^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\mu}}\right\}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\frac{1}{p_{i}.k}\frac{i}{4}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{\partial K_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\nu}}\frac{\partial\Xi_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\mu}}\left(k_{\nu}e_{\mu}-k_{\mu}e_{\nu}\right)^{T}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right).

(19)

Notice that the the first two terms in (
19
)
are independent of
K
and
\Xi
, whereas the last term depends
on their derivatives and hence depends explicitly on the action.

Special Case: Soft Photon Theorem in QED

In the case of soft photons, the gauge group is simply
U\left(1\right)

and the gauge field is abelian. There is only one generator for the
lie algebra which is denoted by
Q_{\alpha}^{\,\,\,\,\beta}
and
is sometimes called the charge matrix since its matrix elements are
charges of the various fields
\phi_{\alpha}
in the external state.
Therefore we have,

\displaystyle G_{n+1}\left(e,k;\varepsilon_{i},p_{i}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{e.p_{i}}{p_{i}.k}Q^{T}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}Q^{T}\frac{e_{\mu}k_{\nu}}{p_{i}.k}\left\{p_{i}^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\nu}}-p_{i}^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\mu}}\right\}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\frac{1}{p_{i}.k}\frac{i}{4}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{\partial K_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\nu}}\frac{\partial\Xi_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\mu}}Q^{T}\left(k_{\nu}e_{\mu}-k_{\mu}e_{\nu}\right)G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right).

(20)

Now for the
i
th external particle of charge
q_{i}

Q\varepsilon_{i}=q_{i}\varepsilon_{i}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle G_{n+1}\left(e,k;\varepsilon_{i},p_{i}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}q_{i}\frac{e.p_{i}}{p_{i}.k}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}q_{i}\frac{e_{\mu}k_{\nu}}{p_{i}.k}\left\{p_{i}^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\nu}}-p_{i}^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\mu}}\right\}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\frac{1}{p_{i}.k}\frac{i}{4}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}q_{i}\frac{\partial K_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\nu}}\frac{\partial\Xi_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\mu}}\left(k_{\nu}e_{\mu}-k_{\mu}e_{\nu}\right)G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right).

(21)

If the
i
th external particle to which photon is attached is a Dirac
fermion,

K_{i}\left(p_{i}\right)=\cancel{p_{i}}-m_{i},

\Xi_{i}\left(p_{i}\right)=i\left(\cancel{p_{i}}+m_{i}\right),

where
K_{i}
,
\Xi_{i}
denote the
i
th blocks in
K
and
\Xi

corresponding to the
i
th particle. We have used the Feynman slash
notation to define
\cancel{p}=\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}
. For this case,
the theory dependent term is,

\displaystyle\frac{i}{4}\left(e_{\mu}k_{\nu}-e_{\nu}k_{\mu}\right)q_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{\partial K_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\nu}}\frac{\partial\Xi_{i}\left(-p_{i}\right)}{\partial p_{i\mu}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{4}q_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\left(e_{\mu}k_{\nu}-e_{\nu}k_{\mu}\right)i\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}q_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\left(\cancel{k}\cancel{e}-\cancel{e}\cancel{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{1}{4}\left[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\right]k_{\mu}e_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-iq_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\frac{k_{\mu}e_{\nu}}{p_{i}.k}S_{\left(i\right)}^{\mu\nu}.

Lastly, for photons
\Gamma^{\left(3\right)}
simply vanishes due
to charge conjugation invariance of the QED action (Furry’s theorem).
Therefore the soft theorem in QED takes the form,

G_{n+1}\left(e,k;\varepsilon_{i},p_{i}\right)=\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}q_{i}\frac{e.p_{i}}{p_{i}.k}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)-i\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}q_{i}\frac{e_{\mu}k_{\nu}}{p_{i}.k}\left(\hat{J}_{\left(i\right)}^{\mu\nu}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\right)\left(p_{i}\right),

(22)

where we have again identified the angular momentum operator
\hat{J}^{\mu\nu}

acting on the Green’s function
G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}
as,

\left(\hat{J}_{\left(i\right)}^{\mu\nu}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\right)\left(p_{i}\right)=i\left\{p_{i}^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\nu}}-p_{i}^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i\mu}}\right\}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)+\frac{i}{4}\left[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\right]G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right).

(23)

Special Case: Soft Gluon Theorem in QCD

For gluons, the gauge group is
SU\left(3\right)
and the Lie Algebra
has 8 generators. If the external states are quarks, they will be
in the fundamental representation of
SU\left(3\right)
in which
case the generators are given in terms of the 8 Gell-Mann matrices

\lambda_{a}
,

T_{a}\varepsilon_{i}=g_{i}\frac{\lambda_{i;a}}{2}\varepsilon_{i},

(24)

where
\lambda_{i;a}
are such that the
i
th block is the corresponding
Gell-Mann matrix while the other blocks are identity. If the external
states are gluons then the generators are in the adjoint representation
of
SU\left(3\right)
,

T_{a}\varepsilon_{i}=g_{i}\tilde{T}_{i;a}\varepsilon_{i},

(25)

where

\left[\tilde{T}_{i;a}\right]_{bc}^{\mu\nu}=f_{abc}\eta^{\mu\nu}.

In general we can write,

T_{a}\varepsilon_{i}=g_{i}T_{i;a}\varepsilon_{i},

In general, there can be some gluons and some quarks in the external
state. So we write the general soft gluon theorem as,

G_{n+1}\left(e,k;\varepsilon_{i},p_{i}\right)=\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}g_{i}\frac{e_{a}.p_{i}}{p_{i}.k}T_{i;a}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}\right)-i\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}^{T}g_{i}\frac{e_{a\mu}k_{\nu}}{p_{i}.k}T_{i;a}\left(\hat{J}_{\left(i\right)}^{\mu\nu}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\right)\left(p_{i}\right).

(26)

This is the single soft gluon theorem in QCD.

4
Chern-Simons Vertices in D=4+1

Now that we have the subleading soft theorems we can move to the Chern-Simons
correction. For this we need to derive the tree level Chern-Simons
vertices. Let us look at the case of abelian gauge field first and
then the non-abelian.

4.1
Abelian Case - Chern-Simons QED

Consider again the abelian gauge field
A_{\mu}
. The Chern-Simons
term int this case is given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\kappa\int\mathbb{A}\left(d\mathbb{A}\right)^{2}=\kappa\int d^{4+1}x\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\delta}A_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}A_{\rho}\partial_{\sigma}A_{\delta},

(27)

where
\mathcal{L}_{matter}
is the part of the Lagrangian that contains
the matter field. From the last term, we can calculate the Chern-Simons
3-point vertex. The vertex factor in the Feynman rules is again found
by symmetrising the Chern-Simons term in external momenta and factoring
out the external polarisation factors
\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(k\right)
.

\displaystyle i\mathcal{M}=

\displaystyle i\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\delta}\left[\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(k_{1}\right)ik_{2\nu}\varepsilon_{\rho}\left(k_{2}\right)ik_{3\sigma}\varepsilon_{\delta}\left(k_{3}\right)+\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(k_{1}\right)ik_{3\nu}\varepsilon_{\rho}\left(k_{3}\right)ik_{2\sigma}\varepsilon_{\delta}\left(k_{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(k_{2}\right)ik_{3\nu}\varepsilon_{\rho}\left(k_{3}\right)ik_{1\sigma}\varepsilon_{\delta}\left(k_{1}\right)+\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(k_{2}\right)ik_{1\nu}\varepsilon_{\rho}\left(k_{1}\right)ik_{3\sigma}\varepsilon_{\delta}\left(k_{3}\right)

\displaystyle\left.+\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(k_{3}\right)ik_{1\nu}\varepsilon_{\rho}\left(k_{1}\right)ik_{2\sigma}\varepsilon_{\delta}\left(k_{2}\right)+\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(k_{3}\right)ik_{2\nu}\varepsilon_{\rho}\left(k_{2}\right)ik_{1\sigma}\varepsilon_{\delta}\left(k_{1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2i\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\delta}\left[k_{1\nu}k_{2\sigma}+k_{2\nu}k_{3\sigma}+k_{3\nu}k_{1\sigma}\right]\varepsilon_{\mu}\left(k_{1}\right)\varepsilon_{\rho}\left(k_{2}\right)\varepsilon_{\delta}\left(k_{3}\right).

(28)

Therefore, the vertex factor is-

\Gamma_{CS}^{\mu\rho\delta}\left(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\right)=-2i\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\delta}\left[k_{1\nu}k_{2\sigma}+k_{2\nu}k_{3\sigma}+k_{3\nu}k_{1\sigma}\right]=-6i\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\delta}k_{1\nu}k_{2\sigma}.

(29)

The rest of the Feynman rules are same as those of usual QED.

4.2
Non-Abelian Case - Chern-Simons QCD

Now let us consider a non-abelian gauge field
A_{\mu}
.

S_{CS}=\kappa\int Tr\left[\mathbb{A}\left(d\mathbb{A}\right)^{2}+c_{4}\mathbb{A}^{3}d\mathbb{A}+c_{5}\mathbb{A}^{5}\right].

(30)

As mentioned before, rational coefficients are fixed by gauge invariance.

Using
\mathcal{S}_{CS}
, the 3-point vertex is given by,

\Gamma_{CS;abc}^{\left(3\right)\mu\rho\delta}\left(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\right)=-\frac{3i}{2}\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\delta}d_{abc}k_{1\nu}k_{2\sigma},

(31)

where
\frac{i}{2}d^{abc}=Tr\left[T^{a}\left\{T^{b},T^{c}\right\}\right]
.
Similarly we can obtain the 4-point vertex to be,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{CS;abcd}^{\left(4\right)\mu\nu\rho\delta}\left(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,=-c_{4}\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\delta}\left[k_{1\sigma}\left\{f_{dce}Tr\left(T_{b}T_{e}T_{a}\right)+f_{bde}Tr\left(T_{c}T_{e}T_{a}\right)+f_{cbe}Tr\left(T_{d}T_{e}T_{a}\right)\right\}\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+k_{2\sigma}\left\{f_{cde}Tr\left(T_{a}T_{e}T_{b}\right)+f_{dae}Tr\left(T_{c}T_{e}T_{b}\right)+f_{ace}Tr\left(T_{d}T_{e}T_{b}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+k_{3\sigma}\left\{f_{dbe}Tr\left(T_{a}T_{e}T_{c}\right)+f_{ade}Tr\left(T_{b}T_{e}T_{c}\right)+f_{bae}Tr\left(T_{d}T_{e}T_{c}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.+k_{4\sigma}\left\{f_{bce}Tr\left(T_{a}T_{e}T_{d}\right)+f_{cae}Tr\left(T_{b}T_{e}T_{d}\right)+f_{abe}Tr\left(T_{c}T_{e}T_{d}\right)\right\}\right].

(32)

Using the definitions of
f_{abc}
and
d_{abc}
and simplifying
using the Jacobi identity gives,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{CS;abcd}^{\left(4\right)\mu\nu\rho\delta}\left(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4}\right)

\displaystyle\,\,=-\frac{c_{4}}{4}i\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\delta}\left[\left(k_{1\sigma}-k_{2\sigma}\right)f_{dce}d_{bea}+\left(k_{1\sigma}-k_{3\sigma}\right)f_{bde}d_{cea}+\left(k_{1\sigma}-k_{4\sigma}\right)f_{cbe}d_{dea}\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.+\left(k_{2\sigma}-k_{3\sigma}\right)f_{dae}d_{ceb}+\left(k_{2\sigma}-k_{4\sigma}\right)f_{ace}d_{deb}+\left(k_{3\sigma}-k_{4\sigma}\right)f_{bae}d_{dec}\right].

(33)

We can also calculate the 5-point vertex
\Gamma_{CS}^{\left(5\right)}

but for our purpose it will suffice to just note that it will have
no momentum dependence at tree level.

Chern-Simons Correction to Single Soft Photon Theorem in QED

the soft photon theorem. We start with considering a single soft photon
in the external state. Then the relevant diagrams are the ones of
type
A1
and
A2

only when

\displaystyle\delta\Gamma^{\left(3\right)\alpha\beta}\left(e,k;p,-p-k\right)=

\displaystyle e_{\mu}\Gamma_{CS}^{\mu\alpha\beta}\left(k,-p-k,p\right)

\displaystyle 6i\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\sigma\beta}e_{\mu}k_{\nu}p_{\sigma},

\displaystyle\delta A1=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{T}\left(p_{i}\right)\delta\Gamma^{\left(3\right)}\left(e,k;p_{i},-p_{i}-k\right)\frac{\Xi_{i}^{photon}\left(-p_{i}-k\right)}{2p_{i}.k}G_{n}^{\left(i\right)}\left(p_{i}+k\right)

\displaystyle 3\kappa\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\sigma\beta}\frac{e_{\mu}k_{\nu}}{p_{i}.k}\varepsilon_{i;\alpha}p_{i\sigma}\eta_{\beta\gamma}G_{n}^{\left(i\right);\gamma}\left(p_{i}\right).

\displaystyle G_{n+1}\left(e,k;\varepsilon_{i},p_{i}\right)=

7
Chern-Simons Correction to Soft Theorems with Multiple Simultaneous
Soft Emissions

We now want to consider multiple soft boson emissions where the energies
of the emitted soft particles are of the same order, i.e. the soft
limits of amplitudes are taken simultaneously for all soft bosons.
One important thing to note is that all the Chern-Simons vertices
have one extra power of momentum and therefore are subleading in the
small
k
limit to their usual gauge theory counterparts,

\Gamma_{CS}^{\left(3\right)}\sim k\Gamma_{gauge}^{\left(3\right)},

\Gamma_{CS}^{\left(4\right)}\sim k\Gamma_{gauge}^{\left(4\right)}.

This implies that there are no diagrams involving these vertices that
contribute to the leading soft theorem. This can be seen by realizing
that if there were any such diagrams we could’ve replaced the Chern-Simons
vertices with their pure gauge counterparts and would have gotten
an even more dominant contribution. This also implies that the Chern-Simons
corrections to subleading soft theorems come largely from adding the
Chern-Simons correction to the gauge vertices in the diagrams contributing
to the leading soft theorem, as was seen in the case of single soft
emission. The only exception are diagrams that have the 5-point Chern-Simons
vertex as they do not have a corresponding more dominant gauge vertex.
But we can argue that these also lead to only subleading contributions.
For instance, consider figure

which contributes to the
Chern-Simons correction for the subleading soft theorem with four
soft emissions. This is the only diagram containing the 5-point vertex
which contributes at the subleading order for this process, i.e. at

\mathcal{O}\left(\nicefrac{{1}}{{k^{3}}}\right)
. It is easy to see
that there is no diagram containing the 5-point vertex which is more
dominant than this since it maximizes the negative
k
powers in
the propagator while having the 5-point Chern-Simons vertex.

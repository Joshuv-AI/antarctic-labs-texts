Coloring Graphs With No Totally Odd Clique Immersion
Caleb McFarland

Abstract
We prove that graphs that do not contain a totally odd immersion of K t K_{t} are 𝒪 ⁡ ( t ) \mathcal{O}(t) -colorable. In particular, we show that any graph with no totally odd immersion of K t K_{t} is the union of a bipartite graph and a graph which forbids an immersion of K 𝒪 ⁡ ( t ) K_{\mathcal{O}(t)} . Our results are algorithmic, and we give a fixed-parameter tractable algorithm (in t t ) to find such a decomposition.

Preliminaries

Graphs may have loops and parallel edges unless they are explicitly stated to be simple. An
edge block
of a graph is a maximal bridgeless subgraph. For
X\subseteq V(G)
, we denote by
G\setminus X
the graph formed from
G
by deleting the vertices in
X
. For
F\subseteq E(G)
, we denote by
G\setminus F
the graph formed from
G
by deleting the edges in
F
. We denote by
G[X]
and
G[F]
the graph induced by
X\subseteq V(G)
or
F\subseteq E(G)
respectively. We denote by
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
the graph with vertex set
V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and edge set
E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
.

A
trail

T
in a graph

is a sequence of distinct edges
e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
which can be oriented so that the head of
e_{i}
is the tail of
e_{i+1}
for all
i\in[n-1]
. We call such an orientation the orientation of the edges
in
T
. We call the tail of
e_{1}
in
T
and the head of
e_{n}
in
T
the
ends
of
T
. We call the ends of
e_{i}
for
i\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}
the
internal vertices
of the trail. Note that a vertex may both be an end and an internal vertex of a trail. We say a trail
hits
a vertex if that vertex is an end of some edge in the trail. We denote by
T^{-1}
the trail
e_{n}e_{n-1}\ldots e_{1}
. A
subtrail
of a trail
T
is a consecutive subsequence of the edges in
T
. A
circuit
is a trail where both ends are identical.

We say that a graph
G
admits a
(weak) immersion
of a graph
H
if there exist injective functions
\varphi_{V}:V(H)\rightarrow V(G)
and
\varphi_{E}:E(H)\rightarrow\{\text{trails in }G\}
such that

1.

for all
e\in E(H)
with ends
u,v\in V(H)
,
\varphi_{E}(e)
has ends
\varphi_{V}(u),\varphi_{V}(v)
, and

2.

for all distinct
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(H)
,
\varphi_{E}(e_{1})
and
\varphi_{E}(e_{2})
are edge-disjoint.

We call
\{\varphi_{V}(v):v\in V(H)\}
the
branch vertices
of the immersion, and we call
\{\varphi_{E}(e):e\in E(H)\}
the
branch trails
. We say that
G
admits a
strong immersion
of

if the branch trails of the immersion have no internal vertex in the branch vertices of the immersion.

An immersion
H
in a graph

is said to be

(

) if all of the branch trails have odd (even) length. We note that in the literature it is common to replace “trails” in the definition with “paths”. The resulting definition is equivalent for immersions, but no longer equivalent for totally odd (weak or strong) immersions. To see this consider a branch trail which is the union of an even path and an odd cycle. We follow the definition as given in Churchley’s original conjecture
[
5
]
, which is motivated by the following equivalent definition of immersions.

Equivalently, a graph
G
admits an immersion of a graph
H
if

can be formed by deleting vertices, deleting edges, and splitting off pairs of incident edges. Given two incident edges
e,f
such that
e
has ends
u,v
and
f
has ends
v,w
, the operation of
splitting off the edges
e,f
at
v
is done by deleting
e
and
f
and adding an edge with ends
u,w
. Then an edge
e
in the immersion
H
correspond to a trail in the original graph
G
with edge set equal to the edges which were split off to form
e
.

We also introduce the notion of
transitions
. Let
T=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
be a trail. The
transitions
of
T
are the ordered pairs
(\{e_{i},e_{i+1}\},v)
where
\{e_{i},e_{i+1}\}
are edges that appear in sequence along the trail with

equal to both the head of
e_{i}
in
T
and the tail of
e_{i+1}
in
T
. Note that
T^{-1}
has the same set of transitions as
T
. We say a transition
(\{e_{i},e_{i+1}\},v)
is a transition
at
v
, and that the transition
contains

e_{i},e_{i+1}
. The
transitions
of an immersion are the union of the transitions of its branch trails. The transitions of an immersion encode splitting off operations needed to form the immersion.

A main ingredient of our proof is an Erdös-Pósa property for odd circuits. We derive the result as a corollary of the following result on odd
A
-paths due to Geelen, Gerards, Reed, Seymour, and Vetta
[
11
]
. An

-path
is a path which has both endpoints in a set

and is internally vertex-disjoint from

. An
x
-circuit
is a circuit hitting a vertex

with no internal vertex equal to

Lemma 2.1

(
[
11
, Lemma 11]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
k\geq 1
be an integer. For any set

, either

1.

there are
k
vertex-disjoint odd

-paths, or

2.

there is a set

of size at most

such that every

has even length.

This gives the following corollary by applying the above to the line graph of a 1-subdivision.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
k\geq 1
be an integer. For any vertex
x\in V(G)
, either

1.

there are
k
edge-disjoint odd
x
-circuits, or

2.

there is a set
X\subseteq E(G)
of size at most
2k-2
such that every
x
-circuit in

has even length.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}
be the

-subdivision of

, and let

be the line graph of

. Note that each vertex

corresponds to a clique
\delta_{G^{\prime}}(v)
in

, and that these cliques are pairwise vertex-disjoint. For an edge
e\in E(G)
with ends
u,v\in V(G)
, the vertex that subdivides

corresponds to a clique of size two in
H
, and the edge
f_{e}
of this clique is exactly between the cliques
\delta_{G^{\prime}}(u),\delta_{G^{\prime}}(v)
. Let
H^{\prime}
be formed by subdividing every edge in a clique of type
\delta_{G^{\prime}}(v)
for
v\in V(G)
, but not the edges

for
e\in E(G)
. Note that there exists a 2-coloring of

such that the edges

for

are exactly the monochromatic edges.

There is a natural mapping from vertex-disjoint odd cycles in

to edge-disjoint odd circuits in
G
by mapping a cycle in
H^{\prime}
to the edges

so that
f_{e}
is contained in the cycle. Note that if a cycle in
H^{\prime}
has odd length, then it must contain an odd number of monochromatic edges in any 2-coloring, and so must use an odd number of edges

. In particular the odd cycle cannot be entirely contained in the subdivision of a clique
\delta_{G^{\prime}}(v)
, and thus the resulting edges form a circuit in
G
. Let
A\coloneq\delta_{G^{\prime}}(x)\subseteq V(H^{\prime})
. Note that every odd

H^{\prime}
can be extended to an odd cycle in

by taking the path of length 2 between its endpoints given by the subdivided edge of the clique
\delta_{G^{\prime}}(x)
in

. Thus if

has

vertex-disjoint odd

has
k
edge-disjoint odd

-circuits.

So by

2.1
, we may assume that there is a set
X\subseteq V(H^{\prime})
of size at most

which hits all odd

-paths in
H^{\prime}
. We may assume that

is a subset of

since vertices in

have degree two in

, and we could have included one of their neighbors in

instead. Thus we can consider

as a subset of
E(G^{\prime})
. Then the set of edges in

for which one of their two corresponding edges in

lies in

hits all odd
x
-circuits of

. The result follows.
∎

Note that if every
x
-circuit of
G\setminus X
has even length, then the edge block containing

in

must be bipartite. To see this, note that by taking two edge-disjoint paths from

-circuit.

We note that both

and

[

]
and
[

]

3
From totally even to totally odd

In this section we first show that every large clique immersion contains a smaller one which is totally even. We then show, essentially, that if there are many edge-disjoint odd circuits or trails with ends in the branch vertices of a totally even clique immersion, then there is a totally odd
K_{t}
-immersion. In graphs which forbid a totally odd
K_{t}
-immersion, this will allow us to apply
corollary

2.2
in order to separate off a bipartite piece of our graph in the presence of a large clique immersion.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph with a
K_{70t+71}
-immersion. Then
G
contains a totally even
K_{t}
-immersion. Moreover, there exists an algorithm that takes as input a graph
G
and the branch trails of a
K_{70t+71}
immersion and returns the branch trails of a totally even
K_{t}
-immersion in
\mathcal{O}_{t}(|E(G)|)
time.

Proof.

We split into cases based on the proportion of branch trails of odd length. If at least
4/5
of the trails have odd length, then we consider the totally odd immersion
G^{\prime}
of
G
formed from the
K_{70t+71}
-immersion by restricting to the trails of odd length. Note that
G^{\prime}
has average degree at least
(70t+71-1)(4/5)=56t+56
. We then find a bipartite subgraph of
G^{\prime}
of average degree at least
28t+28
, which itself has a subgraph
H
of minimum degree at least
14t+14
. By
theorem

1.8
,
H
has a
K_{2t}
-immersion. By keeping the branch vertices on only one side of the bipartition we obtain the desired totally even
K_{t}
-immersion.

If at least
1/5
of the branch trails have even length, we instead consider the totally even immersion
G^{\prime}
formed from the
K_{70t+71}
-immersion by restricting to the trails of even length. Note that
G^{\prime}
has average degree at least
14t+14
. So
G^{\prime}
has a subgraph
H
of minimum degree at least
7t+7
, which by
theorem

1.8
contains the desired totally even
K_{t}
-immersion.

For the algorithm, note that after finding the parity of each branch trail in
\mathcal{O}_{t}(|E(G)|)
time, it suffices to work over a subgraph of
K_{70t+71}
. We may then find a totally even
K_{t}
-immersion by brute force.
∎

We now show how to get a totally odd clique immersion from a totally even one when there are many edge-disjoint odd trails with ends in the branch vertices of the totally even clique immersion where each branch vertex is not the end of too many odd trails. We will first make our odd trails edge-disjoint from all but a few of the branch trails of the totally even immersion. We then delete the branch trails which are not disjoint, and we delete a few vertices to keep high minimum degree in the resulting immersion. We then make all of the odd trails into odd circuits, and group all of the vertices with many odd circuits together. We use the edges between these vertices and the rest of the graph to create a totally even clique immersion with many odd circuits at each branch vertex, and then we use those odd circuits to make the clique immersion totally odd.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
t\geq 49
, and let
G
be a graph with a totally even
K_{1400t}
-immersion with branch vertices
S\subseteq V(G)
. Consider constructing an auxiliary graph by adding a vertex
x
adjacent to every vertex in
S
with multiplicity

3500t^{2}
edge-disjoint odd
x

G
contains a totally odd
K_{t}
-immersion.

Furthermore, there exists an algorithm that takes as input the above auxiliary graph, the branch trails of a totally even
K_{1400t}
-immersion, and the collection of
3500t^{2}
edge-disjoint odd

-circuits and returns the branch trails of a totally odd
K_{t}
-immersion in
\mathcal{O}_{t}(|E(G)|^{2})

Proof.

By removing the first and last edge of every odd

-circuit, every odd

. Note that this trail may in fact be a circuit, and both ends may be the same vertex in

. We call a vertex

We can choose

such that for every branch trail of the

-immersion, either

2.

\mathcal{C}
, or

We choose
\mathcal{C}
to minimize the number of transitions of trails in

which are not transitions of the
K_{1400t}
-immersion. Subject to this, we minimize the number of branch trails of the
K_{1400t}
-immersion for which

does not hold. Suppose towards a contradiction that there exists a branch trail

-immersion such that none of the items hold. Let

Suppose that

, we may assume that

be non-empty trails so that

Our proof of

Theorem 5.1

(
[

)
.

There exists an algorithm that takes as input simple graphs

and decides whether

contains

as an immersion in
\mathcal{O}_{H}(|V(G)|^{3})

This can be made into an algorithm for finding an immersion. We will only need the case where

is a clique.

Corollary 5.2
.

There exists an algorithm that takes as input a mulitgraph

and an integer

and returns the branch vertices and branch trails of a
K_{t}
-immersion in

if one exists in
\mathcal{O}_{t}(|V(G)|^{4}+|E(G)|)

Proof.

We assume that

else we can return some vertex or edge of

. We first note that if
G
is a multigraph with
|E(G)|=\mathcal{O}_{t}(|V(G)|)
, then we can test for a
K_{t}
-immersion in
\mathcal{O}_{t}(|V(G)|^{3})
time. To see this, consider subdividing every edge to create a simple graph. Because

, a
K_{t}
-immersion in this simple graph can easily be translated to an immersion in
G
. Hence we can apply
theorem

5.1
.

The algorithm is as follows. First, we delete edges from
G
until there are no loops and every edge has multiplicity at most
\binom{t}{2}
. We then delete edges until
|E(G)|
is at most
\binom{t}{2}(7t+7)|V(G)|
. We test whether
G
contains a
K_{t}
-immersion, and if not then we are done.

We now find the
K_{t}
-immersion in
G
. We will iteratively split off and delete edges. At each step, an edge stores a trail in the original graph. When we split off two edges, we concatenate their corresponding trails.

We iterate through

and find all vertices of degree at most
\binom{t}{2}(8t+7)
. Call the set of such vertices

, and initialize a set of branch vertices

. We maintain that every
K_{t}
-immersion in
G
has

as a subset of its branch vertices, and every vertex in

has degree

.

We now repeat the following while

. If

is empty, then we delete an arbitrary edge of

. We add one or both of the ends of this edge to

if its degree becomes at most

. Else if there exists a vertex

, we delete every edge incident to

while doing so does not destroy the

-immersion, possibly adding the other end to

-immersion. If

to

If

, then

is isomorphic to

, and we read off the branch vertices and branch trails from the vertices and edges of

.

We now prove the correctness of the above algorithm and analyze its runtime. If
G
contains a
K_{t}
-immersion, then we may assume that all branch trails are paths. Thus deleting loops and reducing the multiplicity of each edge to

does not change whether

-immersion. We then claim that if

contains at least
\binom{t}{2}(7t+7)|V(G)|
edges, then
G
has a
K_{t}
-immersion. To see this, consider deleting all but one edge from each parallel class; the resulting subgraph still has
(7t+7)|V(G)|
many edges. We can then find a subgraph of minimum degree
7t+7
and apply
theorem

1.8
. Thus the first preprocessing step does not change whether

has a
K_{t}
-immersion, and afterwards we have

. This step can be done in

time.

We now consider the main loop of the algorithm. First note that each time we delete an edge, we possibly add its endpoints to

. Thus

need only be computed in

time once. We maintain that every edge has multiplicity at most

throughout the algorithm. To see this, note that if splitting off a pair of edges created an edge of multiplicity larger than
\binom{t}{2}
, then those edges would have been deleted instead.

Consider when

is empty. Because each edge has multiplicity at most
\binom{t}{2}
and

,

has minimum degree greater than

. This implies

-immersion not using a vertex in

-immersion in

still has minimum degree at least

implying that

-immersion.

Suppose

Because every vertex eventually appears in

Preorder Induced by Rainbow Forbidden Subgraphs
Shun-ichi Maezawa, Akira Saito

Abstract
A subgraph H H of an edge-colored graph G G is rainbow if all the edges of H H receive different colors. If G G does not contain a rainbow subgraph isomorphic to H H , we say that G G is rainbow H H -free. For connected graphs H 1 H_{1} and H 2 H_{2} , if every rainbow H 1 H_{1} -free edge-colored complete graph colored in sufficiently many colors is rainbow H 2 H_{2} -free, we write H 1 ≤ H 2 H_{1}\leq H_{2} . The binary relation ≤ \leq is reflexive and transitive, and hence it is a preorder. If H 1 H_{1} is a subgraph of H 2 H_{2} , then trivially H 1 ≤ H 2 H_{1}\leq H_{2} holds. On the other hand, there exists a pair ( H 1 , H 2 ) (H_{1},H_{2}) such that H 1 H_{1} is a proper supergraph of H 2 H_{2} and H 1 ≤ H 2 H_{1}\leq H_{2} holds. Cui et al. [Discrete Math. 344 (2021) Article Number 112267] characterized these pairs. In this paper, we investigate the pairs ( H 1 , H 2 ) (H_{1},H_{2}) with H 1 ≤ H 2 H_{1}\leq H_{2} when neither H 1 H_{1} nor H 2 H_{2} is a subgraph of the other. We prove that there are many such pairs and investigate their structure with respect to ≤ \leq .

Introduction

For graphs
H
and
G
,
we say that
G
is
H
-free
if
G
does not contain an induced subgraph
isomorphic to
H
.
If
G
is
H
-free,
we also say that
H
is forbidden in
G
.

For an appropriate choice of
H
and a graph property

,
we can observe a phenomenon that
the class of
H
-free graphs is well-behaved
with respect to
P
.
The study of these combinations
(H,P)
is called forbidden subgraphs.
It is one of the popular topics in graph theory.

For the sake of the subsequent discussions,
we restate this topic
in a more formal manner.
In order to avoid set-theoretic ambiguity,
we only consider finite simple graphs and we take vertices
from the set of natural numbers
{\boldsymbol{N}}
.
Define
\mathcal{H}_{0}
by

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{H\colon\text{$H$ is a finite graph with $V(H)\subset{\boldsymbol{N}}$}\}.

We sometimes consider the quotient set of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
with respect to
the equivalence relation based on the isomorphism.
But as in standard textbooks of graph theory,
we slightly abuse the notation and adopt the same symbol for
both
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and its quotient set.

For
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
we write
H_{1}\preceq H_{2}
if
H_{1}
is an induced subgraph of
H_{2}
.
It is easy to see that
\preceq
is a partial order in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
We introduce another binary relation based on
forbidden subgraphs.
We write
H_{1}\leq_{F}H_{2}
if there exists a constant

t=t(H_{1},H_{2})
such that every
H_{1}
-free graph
of order
t
or more is
H_{2}
-free.
Trivially,
the binary relation
\leq_{F}
is reflexive and transitive,
and hence it is a preorder.
On the other hand,
it is not anti-symmetric.
Fujita,
Furuya and Ozeki
[
5
]

gave a pair of non-isomorphic graphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}

which satisfies both
H_{1}\leq_{F}H_{2}
and
H_{2}\leq_{F}H_{1}
.

It is easy to see that
H_{1}\preceq H_{2}
implies
H_{1}\leq_{F}H_{2}
.
On the other hand,
in the case of
H_{2}\preceq H_{1}
,
we cannot expect
H_{1}\leq_{F}H_{2}

unless
H_{1}=H_{2}
.
Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be connected graphs in
\mathcal{H}_{0}

and suppose
H_{2}
is a proper induced subgraph of
H_{1}
,
then for every positive integer
n
,
the graph consisting of
n
disjoint copies of
H_{2}

is
H_{1}
-free,
but not
H_{2}
-free.

In this paper,
we study an analogue of the above forbidden subgraphs
in edge-colored graphs.
For
a graph
G
,
we associate a mapping
c\colon E(G)\to{\boldsymbol{N}}

and call the pair
(G,c)
an
edge-colored graph
.
For
e\in E(G)
,
we call
c(e)
the
color
of
e
.
Note that we do not require
c
to be proper,
i.e.
G
may contain two different edges receiving the same color
incident with a common vertex.
An edge-colored graph
(G,c)
is said to be
rainbow

if
c
is injective,
i.e. all the edges in
G
receive different colors.
For
H\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
we say that
(G,c)
is
rainbow
H
-free

if
G
does not contain a rainbow subgraph
which is isomorphic to
H
.

As far as we know,
the study of forbidden rainbow subgraphs
was initiated by Gallai
[
6
]
.
He introduced the notion of Gallai coloring,
which is a rainbow
C_{3}
-free edge-colored complete graph.
He gave a constructive characterization of Gallai coloring
and investigated its properties.
Gallai coloring is still actively studied,
and other rainbow forbidden subgraphs have also been studied since then.
For the readers who are interested in this subject,
we refer them to
[
8
]
.

In the study of rainbow forbidden subgraphs,
we often restrict ourselves to complete graphs
as a research domain,
as Gallai did for Gallai coloring.
One reason for this is that
for an edge-colored graph
(G,c)
,
we assign a new color to the edges of the complement of
G
,
and embed
(G,c)
in an edge-colored complete graph.
In this paper,
we also restrict ourselves to edge-colored complete graphs.

For
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathcal{G}
,
we write
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
if
there exists a positive integer
t=t(H_{1},H_{2})
such that
every rainbow
H_{1}
-free edge-colored complete graph
colored in
t
or more colors
is rainbow
H_{2}
-free.
There is a rationale for the study of
\leq
.
Suppose
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
and every rainbow
H_{2}
-free edge-colored complete
graph colored in sufficiently many colors
satisfies some graph property

.
Then every rainbow
H_{1}
-free edge-colored complete graph
colored in sufficiently many colors
also satisfies
P
.
Therefore,
the study of

for rainbow
H_{1}
-free graphs means little.
In this sense,
the study of the relation
\leq
can
facilitate the research of rainbow forbidden subgraphs.

For
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
if
H_{1}
is a subgraph of

,

If

If

This fact is similar to that of the traditional forbidden subgraphs
in the uncolored graphs.
However,
for rainbow forbidden subgraphs,

is a proper supergraph of

Bass et al.
[

Let

Let

when

and

.
Motivated by this result,

In the next section,

In our study,

.
We seek such invariants in Section 3.
In Section 4,

and

with

Domain and Target of Study

As we mentioned in the introduction,
we take vertices from the set of natural numbers
and consider only finite simple graphs.
Also we deal with only connected graphs in this paper,
though we do not think that forbidding a disconnected graph
is a trivial subject.
In the study of the traditional forbidden subgraphs
in the uncolored graphs,
the class of
2K_{2}
-free graphs has been reported to have a number
of intriguing cycle-related properties.
See
[
3
]
,

[
7
]
and
[
9
]
,
for example.
We exclude disconnected graphs in this paper
only because
we make sufficiently many observations
for connected graphs.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
be the set of connected graph in
\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
We will study the relation

.
However,
there is no meaning in forbidding

and

Hence we would like to exclude them from our study.

we actually have to exclude more.

In
[

Thomason and Wagner made the following observation.

Theorem B

(
[

]
)
.

Every edge-colored complete graph colored in

or more colors
contains a rainbow

By Theorem

whatever graph

vacuously holds.
However,
it does not give any insight.
Therefore,
we may want to exclude

and its subgraphs.
Considering the above,
we define
\mathcal{H}
by
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}-\{P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4}\}

and study the binary relation

in

.
Before proceeding further,
we remark that we do not have to weed out more.

Lemma 1
.

For every

and every positive integer

there exists a rainbow
H
-free edge-colored complete graph

or more colors.

Proof
.

Take a set of

from
{\boldsymbol{N}}
and let

.
Define

Then

is a complete graph edge-colored in

Moreover,
every connected rainbow subgraph in

is a subgraph
of

,
and hence it is rainbow

-free.
∎

Next,
we discuss the binary relation
\leq
.
As we have seen in the introduction,
it is reflective and transitive,
ans hence it is a preorder.
On the other hand,
we know

K_{1,k}^{+}\leq K_{1,k}

by Theorem
A
,
and since
K_{1,k}\subseteq K_{1,k}^{+}
,
we also have
K_{1,k}\leq K^{+}_{1,k}
.
Therefore,

\leq
is not anti-symmetric and it is
not a partial order.

In this situation,
there is a well-known approach to convert a preorder
\leq

to a partial order whose behavior is similar to
\leq
.
For
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
,
we write

H_{1}\equiv H_{2}
if both
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
and
H_{2}\leq H_{1}
hold.
It is easy to see that
\equiv
is an equivalence relation.
For equivalence classes
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
with respect to
\equiv
,
we write
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\leq\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}
if
there exist
H_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and
H_{2}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}

with
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
.
Again,
it is easy to see that
\leq
is a partial order on the quotient set
\mathcal{H}/\!\equiv
.
Note that in order to avoid unnecessary complication,
we slightly abuse the notation and use the same symbol
\leq

for both the preorder in
\mathcal{H}
and the partial order in
\mathcal{H}/\!\equiv
.

In Section 5,
we determine the minimum element of

(\mathcal{H}/\!\equiv,\leq\,)
and the elements
immediately after it.

3
Invariants of Graphs Reflecting
\boldsymbol{\leq}

When we seek candidates of pairs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
with

H_{1}\leq H_{2}
,
it is convenient to have an invariant
which reflects the relation
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
.
Ideally,
we want to have an invariant
f
of graphs
such that
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
implies
f(H_{1})\leq f(H_{2})
.
Unfortunately,
the example of
K_{1,k}^{+}\leq K_{1,k}
suggests that
neither the order nor the size (the number of edges)
satisfies this property.
However,
if we find a constant
c

such that
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
implies

|V(H_{1})|\leq|V(H_{2})|+c

or
|E(H_{1})|\leq|E(H_{2})|+c
,
then for a given graph
H_{2}
,
the number of graphs
H_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
with
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
is finite,
which will facilitate our research.

Based on the above motivation,
in this section,
we investigate several basic graph invariants
and whether and how they reflect the relation
\leq
.

For a connected graph
G
,
define
\dim G
,
the dimension of

,

\dim(G)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1

It is well-known that

and

if and only if

is a tree.
Moreover,
it is easy to see that

implies

.

is either a path or a cycle.
Since
P_{5}\subseteq P_{k}
and hence
P_{5}\leq P_{k}
for
k\geq 5
,
and
C_{3}\leq C_{k}
for
k\geq 4
by Corollary
6
,
it suffices to prove
K_{1,3}\leq P_{5}
and
K_{1,3}\leq C_{3}
.

First,
we claim that every rainbow
K_{1,3}
-free edge-colored complete graph

(K,c)
is rainbow
P_{5}
-free.
Assume,
to the contrary,
that
(K,c)
contains a rainbow path

P=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
of order
5
.
We may assume
c(x_{1}x_{2})=1
,

c(x_{2}x_{3})=2
,

c(x_{3}x_{4})=3

and
c(x_{4}x_{5})=4
.
Since
\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4}\}
induces
K_{1,3}
,
we have
c(x_{2}x_{4})\in\{1,2\}
.
Then
\{x_{3}x_{4},x_{4}x_{5},x_{2}x_{4}\}

induces a rainbow
K_{1,3}
.
This is a contradiction.

Next,
we claim that every rainbow
K_{1,3}
-free edge-colored complete graph

(K,c)
colored in
4
or more colors
is rainbow
C_{3}
-free.
Assume,
to the contrary,
that
(K,c)
contains a rainbow cycle
C=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{1}
.
We may assume
c(x_{1}x_{2})=1
,

c(x_{2}x_{3})=2
and
c(x_{1}x_{3})=3
.
Note that since
K
is edge-colored in
4
or more colors,

V(K)-V(C)\neq\emptyset
.
If
E_{G}\bigl(V(C),V(K)-V(C)\bigr)
contains an edge
e
with
c(e)\notin\{1,2,3\}
,
then this edge and the two edges of
C
incident with the endvertex of
e

induce a rainbow
K_{1,3}
,
a contradiction.
Therefore,
every edge in
E_{G}\bigl(V(C),V(G)-V(C)\bigr)
is colored in
1
,
2

or
3
.

Take an edge
f=uv
with
c(f)\notin\{1,2,3\}
.
Then
\{u,v\}\cap V(C)=\emptyset
.
If two of the edges
ux_{1}
,

ux_{2}
and
ux_{3}
receive different colors,
then these edges together with
f

induce a rainbow
K_{1,3}
with center
u
,
a contradiction.
Hence
ux_{1}
,

ux_{2}
and
ux_{3}
all receive the same color.
We may assume
c(ux_{1})=c(ux_{2})=c(ux_{3})=1
.
Then
\{x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3},x_{2}u\}
induces a rainbow
K_{1,3}

edge-colored in
\{1,2,3\}
.
This is a contradiction.

(2)
Assume
H\leq K_{1,3}
and that
H
is neither
K_{1,3}
nor
K^{+}_{1,3}
.
If
H\subsetneq K_{1,3}
,
then
H
is a subgraph of
P_{4}
,
which contradicts the hypothesis.
If
K_{1,3}\subseteq H
,
then either
H=K_{1,3}
or
K^{+}_{1,3}
by Theorem
A
.
Therefore,

H
is neither a subgraph or a supergraph of
K_{1,3}
.
This implies that
\Delta(H)=2
.
Moreover,
since
K_{1,3}
is a tree,

H
is also a tree by Theorem
4
.
Therefore,
we see that
H
is a path
of order at least
5
.
However,
we have
P_{5}\not\leq K_{1,3}
by Lemma
17

and hence
H\not\leq K_{1,3}
.
This is a contradiction.
∎

Next,
we determine
the elements of
(\mathcal{H}/\!\equiv,\leq\,)
immediately after the minimum element.
They are
\{P_{5}\}
,

\{C_{3}\}
and
\{K_{1,4},K^{+}_{1,4}\}
.
Before stating this result,
we prove one lemma
concerning the relation between
P_{5}
and the barbell.

Lemma 18
.

P_{5}\leq B

Proof
.

We prove that every
P_{5}
-free edge-colored complete graph
(K,c)
is rainbow
B
-free.
Assume
(K,c)
contains a rainbow subgraph
G
which
is isomorphic to
B
.
Let
V(G)=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5},x_{6}\}

and
E(G)=\{x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}x_{4},x_{4}x_{5},x_{4}x_{6}\}
.
We may also assume

c(x_{1}x_{3})=1
,

c(x_{2}x_{3})=2
,

c(x_{3}x_{4})=3
,

c(x_{4}x_{5})=4
and
c(x_{4}x_{6})=5
.

Since
x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
is a
5
-path and
(K,c)
is rainbow
P_{5}
-free,

c(x_{1}x_{2})\in\{2,3,4\}
.
Also,
by considering

x_{2}x_{1}x_{3}x_{4}x_{6}
,
we have
c(x_{1}x_{2})\in\{1,3,5\}
.
Hence we have
c(x_{1}x_{2})=3
.
By symmetry,
we also have
c(x_{5}x_{6})=3
.

Consider
x_{3}x_{2}x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}
and
x_{3}x_{2}x_{1}x_{4}x_{6}
.
Then we have
c(x_{1}x_{4})\in\{2,3,4\}
and
c(x_{1}x_{4})\in\{2,3,5\}
.
Thus,
we have
c(x_{1}x_{4})\in\{2,3\}
.
However,
if
c(x_{1}x_{4})=2
,
then
x_{3}x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}x_{6}
is a rainbow path colored in
\{1,2,3,4\}
,
and if
c(x_{1}x_{4})=3
,
then
x_{2}x_{3}x_{1}x_{4}x_{5}
is a rainbow path colored in

\{1,2,3,4\}
.
Therefore,
we obtain a contradiction in both cases.
∎

Now we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 19
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{H}
and
H\notin\{K_{1,3},K^{+}_{1,3}\}
.
Then

(1)

K_{1,4}\leq H
,

P_{5}\leq H
or
C_{3}\leq H
.
Moreover,

(2)

if
H\leq P_{5}
,
then
H=P_{5}
,

(3)

if
H\leq C_{3}
,
then
H=C_{3}
,
and

(4)

if
H\leq K_{1,4}
,
then either
H=K_{1,4}
or
H=K^{+}_{1,4}
.

Proof
.

(1)
Assume,
to the contrary,
that
K_{1,4}\not\leq H
,

P_{5}\not\leq H
and
C_{3}\not\leq H
.
Then
K_{1,4}\not\subseteq H
,

P_{5}\not\subseteq H
and

C_{3}\not\subseteq H
.

Since
K_{1,4}\not\subseteq H
,

\Delta(H)\leq 3
.
On the other hand,
if
\Delta(H)\leq 2
,
then
H
is either a path or a cycle.
However,
since
H\in\mathcal{H}
and
P_{5}\not\subseteq H
,

H
is not a path.
Moreover,
a cycle of order
5
or more contains
P_{5}
as a subgraph,
while
P_{5}\leq C_{4}
by Corollary
12
and
C_{3}\not\subseteq H
forces
H\neq C_{3}
.
Therefore,

H
is not a cycle.
This is a contradiction.
Hence we have
\Delta(H)=3
.

Let
v_{1}
be a vertex of degree
3
in
H
.
Let
N_{H}(v_{1})=\{v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
.
Since
C_{3}\not\subseteq H
,

\{v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
is an independent set.
Then since
H\neq K_{1,3}

and
H
is connected,

H
contains a vertex
v_{5}
with
N_{H}(v_{5})\cap\{v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}\neq\emptyset
.
By symmetry,
we may assume
v_{2}v_{5}\in E(H)
.

If
N_{H}(v_{5})\cap\{v_{3},v_{4}\}\neq\emptyset
,
then
H
contains
C_{4}
.
However,
this implies
C_{4}\leq H
.
Since
P_{5}\leq C_{4}
,
we have
P_{5}\leq H
.
This contradicts the assumption.
Therefore,
we have
N_{H}(v_{5})\cap\{v_{3},v_{4}\}=\emptyset
.

Since
H\neq K_{1,3}^{+}
,

V(H)\neq\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5}\}
.
Then since
H
is connected,

H
contains a vertex
v_{6}

which is adjacent to
x_{2}
,

x_{3}
,

x_{4}
or
x_{5}
.
However,
if
v_{6}
is adjacent to either
v_{3}
,

v_{4}
or
v_{5}
,

H
contains
P_{5}
,
a contradiction.
Therefore,
the only possibility is
v_{2}v_{6}\in E(G)
.
However,
in this case we have
B\subset H
and hence
B\leq H
.
Since
P_{5}\leq B
by Lemma
18
,
we have
P_{5}\leq H
,
a contradiction.
Therefore,
(1) follows.

(2)
Assume
H\neq P_{5}
.
Then since
H\in\mathcal{H}
,

H
is not a subgraph of
P_{5}
.
Also,
since
H\leq P_{5}
,

H
is a tree
by Theorem
4
.
Again since
H\leq P_{5}
,
there exists a positive integer
t
such that every rainbow
H
-free
edge-colored complete graph
(K,c)
colored in
t
or more colors
is rainbow
P_{5}
-free.
We may assume
t\geq 4
.
Introduce
5+2(t-4)
vertices,

x_{1}
,

x_{2}
,

x_{3}
,

x_{4}
,

x_{5}
,

y_{5},\dots,y_{t}
,

z_{5},\dots,z_{t}
.
Let
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\}
and

Y=\{y_{5},\dots,y_{t},z_{5},\dots,z_{t}\}
,
and let
K=K[X\cup Y]
.
Define
c\colon E(G)\to\{1,\dots,t\}
by

c(e)=\begin{cases}i&\text{if $e=x_{i}x_{i+1}$, $1\leq i\leq 4$,}\\
j&\text{if $e=y_{j}z_{j}$, $5\leq j\leq t$,}\\
2&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
P=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}
.
Then
(K,c)
is edge-colored in
t
colors and
P
is a rainbow path of order
5
.
Therefore,

(K,c)
contains a rainbow subgraph
G
which is isomorphic to
H
.
Let
F=\bigl(E(K[X])-\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}x_{4},x_{4}x_{5}\}\bigr)\cup E_{K}(X,Y)
.
Note that every edge in
F
is colored in
2
.

Since a maximal connected rainbow subgraph of
G-X
is isomorphic to
P_{4}
,
we have
V(G)\cap X\neq\emptyset
.
On the other hand,

H
is connected and it is not a subgraph of
P_{5}
,

E(G)\cap F\neq\emptyset
.
Let
e\in E(G)\cap F
.
Then since
c(e)=2
and
G
is rainbow,

x_{2}x_{3}\notin E(G)
and

E(G)\cap F=\{e\}
.
Suppose
e\in E_{K}(X,Y)
.
We may assume
e=uy_{5}
,
where
u\in X
.
Then
G
is a subgraph of
K[x_{1}x_{2},uy_{5},y_{5}z_{5}]

or
K[x_{3}x_{4},x_{4}x_{5},uy_{5},y_{5}z_{5}]
.
However,
in the former case,

H
is a subgraph of
P_{4}
,
and in the latter case,

H
is a subgraph of
P_{5}
or
K_{1,3}^{+}
.
Hence we obtain a contradiction in either case.

Next,
suppose
e\in E(K[X])
.
In this case,

P-x_{2}x_{3}+e
is isomorphic to either
P_{5}
or
K_{1,3}^{+}
,
and
H
is its subgraph.
This is again a contradiction.

(3)
Assume
H\neq C_{3}
.
If
H
contains a cycle of order
3
,
then
C_{3}\subseteq H
.
However,
by Theorem
A
,
not both
C_{3}\subsetneq H
and
H\leq C_{3}
occur simultaneously.
This is a contradiction.
Therefore,

H
does not contain a cycle of order
3
.

Since
H\leq C_{3}
,
there exists a positive integer
t
such that every rainbow
H
-free
edge-colored complete graph
(K,c)
colored in
t
or more colors
is rainbow
C_{3}
-free.
We may assume
t\geq 3
.

Introduce a set of
t
vertices
X=\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots x_{t-1}\}
and let
K=K[X]
.
Define
c\colon E(K)\to{\boldsymbol{N}}
by

c(e)=\begin{cases}i&\text{if $e=x_{0}x_{i}$, $1\leq i\leq t-1$}\\
t&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
(K,c)
is edge-colored in
t
colors
and
x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}x_{0}
is a rainbow cycle of order
3

colored in
\{1,2,t\}
.
Therefore,
by the assumption,

(K,c)
contains a rainbow subgraph
G

which is isomorphic to
H
.
On the other hand,
in
(K,c)
,
every rainbow cycle has order
3
.
This implies that
H
does not contain a cycle and hence
it is a tree.

Every rainbow tree in
(K,c)
is isomorphic to

K_{1,k}
or
K^{+}_{1,k}
for some
k\geq 2
.
On the other hand,
since
C_{3}
is
2
-regular and
H\leq C_{3}
,
we have
\Delta(H)\leq 3
by Theorem
2
(4).
Therefore,
we have
H=K_{1,3}
or
H=K^{+}_{1,3}
.
However,
this contradicts the hypothesis.

(4)
Assume
H\leq K_{1,4}
and

H\notin\{K_{1,3},K^{+}_{3,1},K_{1,4},K^{+}_{1,4}\}
.
By Theorem
4
,

H
is a tree.
If
\Delta(H)\geq 4
,
then
K_{1,4}\subseteq H
.
However,
this yields
H\in\{K_{1,4},K^{+}_{1,4}\}
by Theorem
A
,
a contradiction.
Therefore,
we have
\Delta(H)\leq 3
.

Since
H\leq K_{1,4}
,
there exists a positive integer
t
such that
every rainbow
H
-free edge-colored complete graph
colored in
t
or more colors is rainbow
K_{1,4}
-free.
We may assume
t\geq 4
.
Take a set of
t+1
vertices
X=\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{t}\}
and let

K=K[X]
.
Define
c\colon E(K)\to{\boldsymbol{N}}
by

c(e)=\begin{cases}i&\text{if $e=x_{0}x_{i}$, $1\leq i\leq t$}\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
(K,c)
is colored in
t
colors and

K[x_{0}x_{1},x_{0}x_{2},x_{0}x_{3},x_{0}x_{4}]
is
a rainbow star of order
5
.
Therefore,

(K,c)
contains a rainbow subgraph
G

which is isomorphic to
H
.
However,
since every rainbow tree in
(K,c)

is isomorphic to
K_{1,k}
or
K^{+}_{1,k}
for some
k\geq 2

and
\Delta(H)\leq 3
,
we have
H\in\{K_{1,3},K^{+}_{1,3}\}
.
This is a contradiction.
∎

We next investigate stars.
Since
K_{1,k}\subseteq K_{1,k+1}
,
we have
K_{1,k}\leq K_{1,k+1}
.
Also,
Theorem
A
says
K_{1,k}\equiv K^{+}_{1,k}

and that
K^{+}_{1,k}
is the only proper supergraph
of
K_{1,k}
that is equivalent with
K_{1,k}
.
Here we prove that
even if we search the entire
\mathcal{H}
,
which contains graphs not comparable with

K_{1,k}
with respect to
\subseteq
,

K^{+}_{1,k}
is still the only graph equivalent with
K_{1,k}
.
We also prove that
\{K_{1,k+1},K^{+}_{1,k+1}\}
is an element immediately after

\{K_{1,k},K^{+}_{1,k}\}
.

Theorem 20
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{H}

and let
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
be integers
with
3\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}
.
If both
K_{1,k_{1}}\leq H
and
H\leq K_{1,k_{2}}
hold,
then
H=K_{1,k}
or
H=K_{1,k}^{+}

for some
k
with
k_{1}\leq k\leq k_{2}
.

Proof
.

Note that since
H\leq K_{1,k_{2}}
,

H
is a tree by Theorem
4
.

Since
H\leq K_{1,k_{2}}
,
there exists a positive integer
t
such that
every rainbow
H
-free edge-colored complete graph
(K,c)

colored in
t
or more colors is rainbow
K_{1,k_{2}}
-free.
We may assume
t\geq k_{2}+1
.
Introduce a set of
t
vertices

X=\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{t-1}\}
and let

K=K[X]
.
Define
c\colon E(K)\to\{1,2,\dots,t\}
by

c(e)=\begin{cases}i&\text{if $e=x_{0}x_{i}\quad 1\leq i\leq t-1$}\\
t&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
(K,c)
is a complete graph edge-colored in
t
colors,
and
K[x_{0}x_{1},x_{0}x_{2},\dots,x_{0}x_{k_{2}}]

is a rainbow subgraph isomorphic to
K_{1,k_{2}}
.
This implies that
(K,c)
contains a rainbow tree
T

which is isomorphic to
H
.

Since
T
is rainbow,

T
contains at most one edge colored in
t
.
Since
T
is a tree,
this yields that
H=K_{1,k}
or
H=K_{1,k}^{+}
for some
k

with
3\leq k\leq t-1
.
In particular,

H\leq K_{1,k}
.

If
k>k_{2}
,
then
K_{1,k_{2}}\subseteq H
.
This together with the hypothesis of
H\leq K_{1,k_{2}}

yields
k=k_{2}
by Theorem
A
.
This is a contradiction,
and hence we have
k\leq k_{2}
.

Assume
k<k_{1}
.
Then
K_{1,k}\subseteq K_{1,k_{1}}
.
On the other hand,
since
K_{1,k_{1}}\leq H
and
H\leq K_{1,k}
,
we have
K_{1,k_{1}}\leq K_{1,k}
.
These yield
k=k_{1}
by Theorem
A
,
a contradiction.
Therefore,
we have
k_{1}\leq k\leq k_{2}
.
by Theorem
A
.
∎

By setting
k_{2}=k_{1}
and
k_{2}=k_{1}+1
in theorem
20
,
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 21
.

For each integer
k
with
k\geq 3
,
The equivalence class with respect to
\equiv
in
\mathcal{H}
containing
K_{1,k}

is
\{K_{1,k},K_{1,k}^{+}\}
.
Moreover,

\{K_{1,k+1},K^{+}_{1,k+1}\}
is immediately after

\{K_{1,k},K^{+}_{1,k}\}
in
(\mathcal{H}/\!\equiv,\leq\,)
.

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper,
we have investigated a binary relation
\leq
,
which gives a preorder among rainbow forbidden subgraphs.
The previous study
[
4
]

shows that there are not many kinds of pairs
(H_{1},H_{2})
with

H_{1}\leq H_{2}

under the condition that
H_{1}
or
H_{2}
is a subgraph of the other.
However,
By lifting this condition,
we have found a large variety of pairs.
Also,
we have introduced an equivalence relation
\equiv
naturally defined from
\leq
,
and converted
\leq
to a partial order on its equivalence classes.
We have studied the properties of this partial order.

Our research only determines a small part of the partially ordered set
(\mathcal{H}/\!\equiv,\leq\,)
.
In particular,
though we have proved that
\{K_{1,k},K^{+}_{1,k}\}
is an equivalence class
in
\mathcal{H}
,
we have not found any other non-singleton equivalence class.
Thus,
we raise the following problem.

Problem 1
.

Is there a non-singleton equivalence class of
\equiv

other than
\{K_{1,k},K^{+}_{1,k}\}
?

We have proved that
(\mathcal{H}/\!\equiv,\leq\,)
has the minimum element,
and determined the elements immediately after it.
However,
we do not think it gives sufficient information to
grasp a large picture on the structure of
\mathcal{H}/\!\equiv
.
As the next step,
we place the next question at the end of this paper.

Problem 2
.

Determine the elements immediately after

\{C_{3}\}
,

\{P_{5}\}
and

\{K_{1,4},K^{+}_{1,4}\}
.

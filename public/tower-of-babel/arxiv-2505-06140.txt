A Conformal Quasi Einstein Characterization Of The Round Sphere
Ramesh Sharma

Abstract
. We extend the following result of Cochran “A closed m m -quasi Einstein manifold ( M , g , X M,g,X ) with m ≠ − 2 m\neq-2 has constant scalar curvature if and only if X X is Killing” covering the missing accidental case m = − 2 m=-2 and generalize it showing that X X is Killing if the integral of the Lie derivative of the scalar curvature along X X is non-positive. For a closed m m -quasi Einstein manifold of dimension n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , if X X is conformal, then it is Killing; and in addition, if M M admits a non-Killing conformal vector field V V , then it is globally isometric to a sphere and V V is gradient for n > 2 n>2 . Finally, we derive an integral identity for a vector field on a closed Riemannian manifold, which provides a direct proof of the Bourguignon-Ezin conservation identity.

1.
Introduction

By an
m
-quasi Einstein manifold, we mean a Riemannian manifold (
M,g
) that has a smooth vector field
X
, and satisfies the equation

(1.1)

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{X}g+Ric-\frac{1}{m}X^{*}\otimes X^{*}=\lambda g

where
X^{*}
denotes the 1-form dual to
X
,
\mathcal{L}_{X}
denotes the Lie derivative operator along
X
,
Ric
denotes the Ricci tensor of
g
, and
m\neq 0
is a real number. The case
m=0
may be interpreted as having
X=0
. The solution of (
1.1
) is trivial when
X=0
in which case
g
becomes Einstein. Another case is the near horizon limit (Bahuaud-Gunasekaran-Kunduri-Woolgar
[
1
]
), which arises on vacuum stationary spacetimes that contain a so-called extreme black hole. For
m=2
, the solution of (
1.1
) describes the geometry of a degenerate Killing horizon (black hole’s event horizon) on which a Killing vector field that is timelike immediately outside the horizon becomes null. For
m=\pm\infty
, the equation (
1.1
) reduces to a Ricci soliton. The case:
0<m<\infty
, has been studied considerably by some authors, e.g. Case-Shu-Wei
[
C-S-W2011
]
by taking
X
as a gradient vector of a smooth function
f
on
M
. For a positive integer
m
, (
M,g
) is
m
-quasi Einstein if and only if the warped product
M\times_{e^{-f/m}}F^{m}
is Einstein, where
F^{m}
is an
m
-dimensional Einstein manifold. We also note that the only closed 1-dimensional Riemannian manifold is
S^{1}
. The
g
-trace of equation (
1.1
) is

(1.2)

divX+R-\frac{1}{m}|X|^{2}=n\lambda.

In
[
2
]
, Cochran proved the following result.

Theorem (Cochran)

Let (
M,g,X
) be a solution to the
m
-quasi Einstein equation (
1.1
) such that (
M,g
) is closed ( i.e. compact and without boundary) and
m\neq-2
. Then (
M,g
) has constant scalar curvature if and only if
X
is Killing
.

So, the question arises whether the above mentioned result holds for the seemingly accidental and pathological case
m=-2
as well. We show that this is indeed true and furthermore, obtain the following generalization of this result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let (
M,g,X
) be a solution to the
m
-quasi Einstein equation (
1.1
) such that (
M,g
) is closed. If
X
is Killing, then the scalar curvature
R
is constant. If
\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}_{X}R)dv_{g}\leq 0
(in particular,
R
is constant along
X
), then
X
is Killing.

Here

denotes the volume element of (
M,g
). An immediate consequence of this result is the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

For a closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold, if the scalar curvature
R
satisfies
\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}_{X}R)dv_{g}\leq 0
, then
R
is constant.

The proof of Theorem 1.1 needs the following lemma.

Lemma 1.3
.

For any vector field
X
on a closed Riemannian manifold (

),
\int_{M}(\nabla_{X}|X|^{2})dv_{g}=-\int_{M}|X|^{2}(divX)dv_{g}
and if (

) is also

-quasi Einstein, then
\int_{M}(|X|^{2}divX)dv_{g}=m\int_{M}[(divX)^{2}-\mathcal{L}_{X}R]dv_{g}

The first part of the lemma follows straightaway by integrating the identity:
div(|X|^{2}X)=\nabla_{X}|X|^{2}+|X|^{2}divX
over the closed
M
and using divergence theorem. For the second part, we multiply (
1.2
) with
divX
, and then integrate the resulting equation over
M
.

Now we turn our attention to proving Theorem 1.1.

Remark 1.4
.

The integral
\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}_{X}R)dv_{g}
involved in Theorem 1.1 plays an important role in conformal geometry, and vanishes for a conformal vector field
X
on a closed Riemannian manifold (Bourgignon-Ezin identity
[
Bourgignon-Ezin1987
]
).

Remark 1.5
.

First, we noticed during the proof of Theorem 1.1 that
|X|
is constant on
M
. So, if
X
is non-zero then it is nowhere zero and hence
M
has its Euler characteristic equal to zero. Secondly, it was pointed out in
[
2
]
that the Ricci tensor of a closed
m
-quasi manifold (
M,g,X
) with constant scalar curvature (hence
X
Killing) need not be parallel. Here, we show that the Ricci tensor of such a manifold is cyclically parallel as follows. We first observe that equation (
1.1
) becomes
R_{jk}=\lambda g_{jk}+\frac{1}{m}X_{j}X_{k}
, when
X
is Killing. As
|X|
is constant, we have
g(\nabla_{Y}X,X)=0
for any

, and since

is Kiiling, we get
g(\nabla_{X}X,Y)=0
which implies
\nabla_{X}X=0
. Thus

is parallel along the geodesics determined by
X
. On the other hand, using the aforementioned expression for
R_{ij}
, we compute
\nabla_{i}R_{jk}=\frac{1}{m}[(\nabla_{i}X_{j})X_{k}+X_{j}\nabla_{i}X_{k}]
. Permuting

cyclically twice, adding the resulting two equations to the foregoing equation we find that
\nabla_{i}R_{jk}+\nabla_{J}R_{ki}+\nabla_{k}R_{ij}=0
, i.e

is cyclically parallel. Riemannian metrics with cyclically parallel Ricci tensor have constant scalar curvature and were studied by Gray
[
Gray1978
]
) who constructed such metrics on the unit sphere
S^{3}
with Ricci tensors cyclically parallel, but not parallel. Another example of a manifold whose Ricci tensor is cyclically parallel but not parallel, given in
[
Gray1978
]
, is
O(4)/O(2)
with a bi-invariant metric.

In the rest of this paper, for a closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold of dimension

is conformal, then we show that it is Killing; and in addition, if

\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}_{V}R)dv_{g}=0
, for a conformal vector field

on a closed Riemannian manifold

(

n>2

2.
m
-quasi Einstein Theoretic Characterization Of A sphere

We first consider the case when
X
is conformal on the closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold and prove the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let (
M,g,X
) be a solution to the
m
-quasi Einstein equation such that (
M,g
) is closed and of dimension
n\geq 2
. If
X
is conformal, then it is Killing.

Proof.

The
m
-quasi Einstein equation (
1.1
) and its trace (
1.2
) can be written in the component form as

\nabla_{j}X^{i}+\nabla^{i}X_{j}+2R^{i}_{j}-\frac{2}{m}X^{i}X_{j}=2\lambda\delta^{i}_{j},

\nabla_{i}X^{i}+R-\frac{1}{m}|X|^{2}=n\lambda.

Taking their covariant derivatives and using the twice contracted Bianchi’s second identity:
\nabla_{i}R^{i}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{j}R
, we have

\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}X^{i}+\nabla_{i}\nabla^{i}X_{j}+\nabla_{j}R-\frac{2}{m}[(divX)X_{j}+(\nabla_{X}X)_{j}]=0,

\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}X^{i}+\nabla_{j}R-\frac{1}{m}\nabla_{j}|X|^{2}=0.

Now subtracting the second equation from the first, provides

(2.1)

-\square X=\frac{1}{m}[2(divX)X+2\nabla_{X}X-\nabla|X|^{2}]

where the Yano operator
\square:\mathfrak{X}(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
is defined by (p. 40 in
[
3
]
)

(\square X)^{i}=-(\nabla^{j}\nabla_{j}X^{i}+R^{i}_{j}X^{j}).

Next, taking the inner product of (
2.1
) with
X
, noting that
\nabla_{X}|X|^{2}=2g(\nabla_{X}X,X)
and subsequently integrating over the closed
M
yields that

(2.2)

-\int_{M}g(\square X,X)dv_{g}=\frac{2}{m}\int_{M}(divX)|X|^{2}dv_{g}.

which holds for any closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold. At this point, we recall the following integral formula for a vector field on a closed Riemannian manifold (
M,g
) [equation (1.14), p. 41 in
[
3
]
]:

(2.3)

\int_{M}[g(\square X-\frac{n-2}{n}\nabla(divX),X)-\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{L}_{X}g-\frac{2divX}{n}g|^{2}]dv_{g}=0.

The use of (
2.2
) and the identity:
div((divX)X)=\nabla_{X}divX+(divX)^{2}
in equation (
2.3
) provides the formula:

(2.4)

\int_{M}[\frac{2}{m}(divX)|X|^{2}-\frac{n-2}{n}(divX)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{L}_{X}g-\frac{2divX}{n}g|^{2}]dv_{g}=0

for a closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold. Using the second part of Lemma 1.3 in equation (
2.4
) we obtain

\int_{M}[\frac{n+2}{2}(divX)^{2}-2\mathcal{L}_{X}R+\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{L}_{X}g-\frac{2divX}{n}g|^{2}]dv_{g}=0.

So, if
X
is conformal, then
\mathcal{L}_{X}g=\frac{2divX}{n}g
. In the above equation, we use Bourguignon-Ezin conservation identity
[
Bourgignon-Ezin1987
]
:
\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}_{X}R)dv_{g}=0
, for a conformal vector field
X
on a closed Riemannian manifold, and obtain
divX=0
. Consequently, as pointed out in the remark below, it follows that
X
is Killing. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

As the integral equation (
2.4
) holds for a closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold, we immediately recover the following result: “If
divX=0
on a closed
m
-quasi manifold, then
X
is Killing” proved in
[
1
]
for
m\neq-2
, and in
[
2
]
for any
m
.

We noticed from the previous theorem that demanding
X
for a closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold, to be conformal, forces it to become Killing. Thus, one may consider another vector field (other than
X
) and check the impact on the closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold. Motivated by this consideration, we establish the following characterization of a sphere, as the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let (
M,g,X
) be an
n
-dimensional (
n\geq 2
) closed
m
-quasi Einstein manifold such that
X
is Killing. If it admits a non-Killing conformal vector field
V
, then it is globally isometric to a sphere. Also, in dimensions higher than 2,
V
is gradient (up to the addition of a Killing vector field).

Proof.

Let
V
be a non-Killing conformal vector field on (
M,g
). Then

\mathcal{L}_{V}g=2\sigma g

for a non-constant smooth function
\sigma
on
M
. We recall the following integrability condition for the conformal vector field
V

[
3
]
:

(2.5)

\mathcal{L}_{V}R=-2\sigma R-2(n-1)\Delta\sigma.

First, we consider the case:
n=2
. As
X
is Killing, Theorem 1.1 asserts that
R
is constant. Hence (
2.5
) reduces to
\Delta\sigma=-R\sigma
. Hence
R
is positive. Therefore
M
has positive constant curvature. Applying the following result of Bishop-Goldberg
[
B-G1966
]
“A compact Riemannian manifold of constant positive curvature is globally isometric to a round sphere, we conclude that (
M,g
) is globally isometric to a 2-sphere.

For case
n>2
, as
X
is Killing, by Theorem 1.1,
R
is constant. Using this in the integral of (
1.2
) shows that
|X|
is constant. Thus equation (
2.5
) reduces to
\Delta\sigma=-\frac{R}{n-1}\sigma
. This shows that
R>0
. As
X
is Killing, the
m
-quasi Einstein equation (
1.1
) reduces to the form:

(2.6)

Ric=\frac{1}{m}X^{*}\otimes X^{*}+\lambda g.

Using this we compute

(2.7)

|Ric|^{2}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}[|X|^{4}+nm^{2}\lambda^{2}+2\lambda m|X|^{2}].

As
|X|
is constant, the equation (
2.7
) shows that
|Ric|
is constant on
M
. Applying the following result of Lichnerowicz
[
Lichnerowicz1964
]
: “If a closed Riemannian manifold (
M,g
) of dimension
>2
with constant scalar curvature and constant norm of the Ricci tensor, admits an infinitesimal non-isometric conformal transformation, then (
M,g
) is globally isometric to a round sphere” we conclude that (
M,g
) is globally isometric to a round sphere. So,
M
is Einstein and hence equation (
2.6
) reduces to

(2.8)

(\frac{R}{n}-\lambda)g=\frac{1}{m}X^{*}X^{*}.

Contracting it gives
R-n\lambda=\frac{1}{m}|X|^{2}
. Next, operating (
2.8
) on the pair (
X,
X) provides
(\frac{R}{n}-\lambda)|X|^{2}=\frac{1}{m}|X|^{4}
. The preceding two equations imply that
X=0
. Now we recall the following integrability condition for a conformal vector field
V

[
3
]
:

\mathcal{L}_{V}Ric=(2-n)\nabla\nabla\sigma-(\Delta\sigma)g.

Using this in the Einstein condition:
Ric=\frac{R}{n}g
, combining it with (
2.5
) and taking into account that
n>2
, shows that
\nabla\nabla\sigma=\frac{R}{n(1-n)}\sigma g
, i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{\nabla\sigma}g=2\frac{R}{n(1-n)}\sigma g
. Combining this with the conformal equation
\mathcal{L}_{V}g=2\sigma g
immediately yields that
\mathcal{L}_{V+\frac{n(n-1)}{R}\nabla\sigma}g=0
which proves (as
R
is a positive constant) that
V
is gradient up to the addition of a Killing vector field, completing the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Theorem 2.3 is a generalization of the following important result of Yano-Nagano
[
Yano-Nagano1959
]
“If a closed Riemannian manifold
M
of dimension
n>2
admits an infinitesimal non-isometric conformal transformation, then

3.
A generalization of Bourguignon-Ezin conservation identity

A conformal vector field
V
on an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold (
M,g
) is defined by

\mathcal{L}_{V}g=2\sigma g

for a smooth function
\sigma
on
M
. The Bourguignon-Ezin conservation identity:
\int_{M}(\mathcal{L}_{V}R)dvol_{g}=0
holds for a conformal vector field
V
on a closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
\geq 2
. Its derivation for
n>2
uses the integral of following integrability condition
[
3
]
for a conformal vector field
V
:

\mathcal{L}_{V}R=-2\sigma R+2(1-n)\Delta\sigma.

along with the identity:
div(RV)=\mathcal{L}_{V}R+Rdiv(V)
and the trace
div(V)=n\sigma
of the conformal equation. We present a direct derivation of the Bourguignon-Ezin conservation identity (without using the above mentioned integrability condition) for
n>2
as follows. Suppose
T
is a seond order symmetric tensor on
M
. Then we compute

\displaystyle<\mathcal{L}_{V}g,T>=(\nabla_{i}V_{j}+\nabla_{j}V_{i})T^{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(\nabla_{i}V_{j})T^{ij})=2[\nabla_{i}(X_{j}T^{ij})-X_{j}\nabla_{i}T^{ij}]

which can be expressed as
<\mathcal{L}_{V}g,T>=2[div(i_{V}T)-(divT)X]
that was first derived by Griffin in
[
Griffin2021
]
. Integrating it over a closed Riemannian manifold (
M,g
) and replacing
T
with the trace free part
\mathring{Ric}=Ric-\frac{R}{n}g
of the Ricci tensor and using the twice contracted Bianchi second identity:
div(Ric)=\frac{1}{2}\nabla R
provides the following generalization of Bourguignon-Ezin conservation identity:

\int_{M}[<\mathring{\mathcal{L}_{V}g},\mathring{Ric}>+\frac{n-2}{n}\mathcal{L}_{V}R]dv_{g}=0

for any smooth vector field
V
(not necessarily conformal) on a closed Riemannian manifold, where
\mathring{\mathcal{L}_{V}g}
denotes the trace free part of
\mathcal{L}_{V}g
. In particular, when
V
is conformal, i.e.
\mathring{\mathcal{L}_{V}g}=0
, the above formula recovers the Bourguignon-Ezin identity for
n>2
.

4.

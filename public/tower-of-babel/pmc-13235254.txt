Mapping the scholarly landscape of Saudi Arabia-related folk medicine: a bibliometric study
Authors: Mohammed M. Ghoneim, Eslam Kamal Fahmy, Manal Nael, Mohamed El-Sherbiny, Rasha Hamed Al-Serwi, Saddam Hossain, Khaled Elokely, Naved Ahmad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFolk medicine remains an important component of health practices in Saudi Arabia and continues to attract growing scholarly attention. This study maps the scholarly landscape of folk medicine research related to Saudi Arabia by examining publication trends, author keywords, collaboration among institutions and countries, and the impact of authors and sources. Publications were retrieved from the Scopus and Web of Science databases using Saudi Arabia as the country affiliation. After PRISMA-guided identification, screening, deduplication, and eligibility assessment, 2527 publications published between 1989 and 2024 were retained for analysis. Bibliometrix was used for bibliometric computation and visualization, and VOSviewer was used to map country co-authorship and keyword co-occurrence networks. The results show sustained growth in publication output, strong participation by Saudi institutions, and broad international collaboration. The most prominent research themes were antioxidant activity, antimicrobial properties, herbal medicine, and traditional medicine. These findings provide a bibliometric overview of knowledge production in this area and may inform future systematic, clinical, and policy-oriented research on traditional medicine in Saudi Arabia.

Introduction

Folk medicine remains important across cultures and is often transmitted across generations, including through women as holders of traditional medicinal knowledge in some communities [1,2]. Traditional healing can involve plant-based remedies, culturally embedded practices, and community-based healers [3,4].

In Saudi Arabia, traditional medicine continues to coexist with biomedical care [5,6]. Reviews of ethnomedicinal plants used in the Kingdom and survey-based studies of complementary and herbal medicine use indicate that traditional practices remain relevant in contemporary health behaviour [7–11]. Interest in plant-based and traditional therapies is also reflected in broader reviews and international health reports [12,13].

Saudi traditional medicine encompasses herbal remedies as well as complementary practices such as cupping. The literature includes studies on medicinal plants such as Azadirachta indica, Olea europaea, and Nigella sativa, alongside surveys of complementary medicine use among Saudi patients [10,14–17].

For example, a substantial proportion of respondents in Western Saudi Arabia report using herbal medicines, and gastrointestinal complaints are among the health concerns for which traditional remedies are commonly used [8,9]. At the same time, preserving local health traditions and understanding how they interface with modern healthcare remain important research and public health considerations [11,18].

Despite this continuing relevance, bibliometric mapping of Saudi Arabia-related folk medicine research remains limited. The literature is dispersed across pharmacology, ethnobotany, complementary medicine, and related fields, making it difficult to identify long-term publication trends, influential contributors, thematic concentration, and collaboration patterns. A bibliometric approach can address these questions by mapping research output and knowledge production, but it cannot establish therapeutic efficacy or policy readiness.

The study examined publication year distribution to understand field evolution and assessed the productivity and impact of authors, institutions, countries, and sources. It also explored thematic concentration through keyword co-occurrence and collaboration patterns through country co-authorship networks. The following research questions were created:

Material and methods

We used a bibliometric design to analyse the literature on folk medicine related to Saudi Arabia. Data were collected from the Scopus and Web of Science Core Collection databases because both are widely used in citation-based studies and are compatible with Bibliometrix and VOSviewer [19–30]. The search covered publications from 1989 to 2024, which corresponds to the active publication period captured in the dataset (Figure 1).

Research design (author’s source).

To identify relevant studies, the research team developed a broad search strategy based on terms commonly used for folk and traditional medicine. The search in Scopus and Web of Science used the following query:

(‘Folk Medicine’ OR ‘Traditional Medicine’ OR ‘Indigenous Medicine’ OR ‘Ethnomedicine’ OR ‘Natural Medicine’ OR ‘Home Remedies’ OR ‘Herbal Medicine’ OR ‘Native Healing’ OR ‘Ancestral Medicine’ OR ‘Cultural Healing Practices’ OR ‘Complementary Medicine’ OR ‘Alternative Medicine’ OR ‘Traditional Healing’) AND CU = (‘Saudi Arabia’).

A key limitation of the search strategy is the use of the country affiliation filter (Saudi Arabia). Accordingly, the dataset represents publications on traditional or folk medicine authored by at least one Saudi-affiliated researcher; it does not exclusively capture studies about Saudi folk medicine practices. This distinction is important for interpreting the results, and Figure 1 summarises the workflow used to identify, screen, and analyse the dataset.

We followed the four PRISMA stages for data selection. In the identification stage, Scopus returned 194,902 records and Web of Science returned 74,803 records for the broad search. After applying the Saudi Arabia affiliation filter, the totals were reduced to 2,602 records in Scopus and 1,391 records in Web of Science. Restricting document types to journal articles and review papers yielded 2,393 Scopus records and 1,371 Web of Science records, and two non-English records were excluded from Scopus. In the screening stage, records from both databases were merged, resulting in 3,762 records. Using Bibliometrix in R, 1,218 duplicate records were identified and removed. In the eligibility stage, 17 records were excluded because they did not meet the predefined inclusion criteria. In the final inclusion stage, 2,527 publications were retained for bibliometric analysis.

Bibliometrix was used for bibliometric computation and visualisation, while VOSviewer was used to construct network maps of country-level co-authorship and keyword co-occurrence [20,21,28]. This study therefore presents a bibliometric science-mapping analysis of the literature [31].

Results

A total of 2,527 publications from 873 sources indexed in Scopus and Web of Science between 1989 and 2024 were analysed. The dataset had an annual growth rate of 10.67%, a mean document age of 7.17 years, and an average of 25.01 citations per document (Table 1). It included 14,291 Keywords Plus terms and 6,461 author keywords, reflecting broad thematic diversity. In total, 5,930 authors contributed to the dataset, with 6.63 co-authors per document and 40.44% international co-authorship. The corpus comprised 1,998 articles and 529 review papers.

Analyses of publication

Figure 2 shows the annual publication and citation trends in folk medicine research related to Saudi Arabia from 1989 to 2024. Publication output was modest and intermittent during the 1990s, with 10 articles in 1989 and only a single article in 1996. Output increased steadily from 2007 to 2015 and then accelerated markedly between 2016 and 2022, reaching a peak of 384 articles in 2022.

Publication and citation trends (1989–2024).

Although publication volume rose substantially in recent years, the highest mean total citation per article was recorded in 2003 (100.18). Overall, the trend indicates that the field has moved from sporadic early contributions to sustained and increasingly visible scholarly activity.

Figure 2 presents the number of publications per year (bars) alongside the mean total citations per article (line). Taken together, these indicators show both expansion in research output and continued citation impact over time.

Analysis of keyword occurrences and link strength

Figure 3 presents the keyword co-occurrence network derived from author keywords. The size of each node reflects the frequency of a keyword, and the links between nodes represent co-occurrence relationships. The proximity of nodes indicates the strength of thematic association [23–26].

Network visualisation of key terms in folk medicine research.

From 6,466 author keywords, 122 met the threshold of at least 10 occurrences, and the 50 most relevant keywords were selected to generate the map. The network contained 547 links with a total link strength (TLS) of 1,419 and was organised into five thematic clusters.

The most prominent terms were ‘antioxidant’ (165 occurrences; TLS 229), ‘antimicrobial’ (TLS 122), and ‘Saudi Arabia’ (108 occurrences; TLS 108). Co-occurring terms such as ‘oxidative stress,’ ‘anti-inflammatory,’ ‘herbal medicine,’ and ‘traditional medicine’ indicate that the literature is heavily oriented toward bioactivity-related plant research and broader traditional medicine themes [22].

Overall, the keyword network suggests that Saudi Arabia-related folk medicine research is concentrated around antioxidant and antimicrobial properties, broader traditional and herbal medicine themes, and regionally anchored scholarship.

Analysis of country contributions and impact

Figure 4 shows the country co-authorship network in folk medicine research. The VOSviewer analysis used a minimum threshold of five articles and 10 citations per country [30]. Five collaboration clusters were identified, with Saudi Arabia occupying the central position in the network.

Country contributions and impact.

Saudi Arabia recorded the highest publication output (1,050 articles), the highest total citations (23,309), and the highest TLS (3,326), indicating that it is both the main contributor and the main collaboration hub in the dataset. India ranked second in output (301 articles; 6,402 citations; TLS 1,178), followed by Egypt (212 articles; 5,360 citations; TLS 1,080). Pakistan also made a substantial contribution with 177 articles and 5,735 citations.

In Figure 4, node size represents publication output and links reflect co-authorship relationships among countries. The network highlights strong collaborative ties between Saudi Arabia and countries such as India, Egypt, Pakistan, and the USA, demonstrating that this literature is shaped by both regional and wider international partnerships [27].

Discussion

This bibliometric study analysed 2,527 publications from 873 sources indexed in Scopus and Web of Science between 1989 and 2024, providing a comprehensive mapping of folk medicine research related to Saudi Arabia. The findings are discussed below in relation to each research question.

RQ1: publication and citation trends in folk medicine research

The publication trend analysis (Figure 2 and Table 1) shows clear long-term growth in this field. Output was low and intermittent during the 1990s, with 10 articles in 1989 and a minimum of one article in 1996, but increased steadily after the early 2000s and accelerated strongly between 2016 and 2022. The peak year was 2022, with 384 publications. The annual growth rate of 10.67% (Table 1) confirms that the field has expanded consistently over time.

Citation behaviour shows a complementary pattern. Although publication volume is highest in recent years, the mean total citation per article reached its maximum in 2003 (100.18), indicating the strong influence of earlier studies. The overall average of 25.01 citations per document (Table 1) suggests continued scholarly visibility alongside increasing publication output.

RQ2: most active and impactful countries, organisations, authors, and sources

At the country level, Saudi Arabia dominated the field with 1,050 articles, 23,309 citations, and the highest total link strength (Table 5; Figure 4). India and Egypt ranked second and third in publication output, while Pakistan and the USA also showed substantial citation or collaboration impact. At the organisational level, King Saud University was the most productive affiliation with 1,122 publications, followed by the Egyptian Knowledge Bank (694) and King Abdulaziz University (465) (Table 2).

Regarding authors and sources, Khan M had the highest number of publications (79), Khan A had the highest h-index (25), and Khan S had the highest total citations (2,635) and g-index (51) (Table 4). Among journals, Molecules published the largest number of papers (84), whereas Journal of Ethnopharmacology recorded the highest citation count (3,090) and h-index (35) (Table 3). Together, these results show that a relatively concentrated group of countries, affiliations, authors, and journals shapes the field.

RQ3: main research themes revealed by keyword co-occurrence

The keyword co-occurrence analysis (Figure 3) identified five thematic clusters from 6,466 author keywords. The strongest term was ‘antioxidant’ (165 occurrences; TLS 229), followed by ‘antimicrobial’ (TLS 122) and ‘Saudi Arabia’ (108 occurrences; TLS 108). Co-occurring terms such as ‘oxidative stress,’ ‘anti-inflammatory,’ ‘herbal medicine,’ and ‘traditional medicine’ indicate that the literature is heavily oriented toward bioactivity-related plant research and broader traditional medicine themes.

RQ4: co-authorship network of countries

The co-authorship analysis (Figure 4 and Table 5) revealed five collaboration clusters and placed Saudi Arabia at the centre of the international network, with the highest TLS (3,326). Strong links with India, Egypt, Pakistan, and the USA suggest that this research area is characterised by active cross-border collaboration rather than isolated national output. The international co-authorship rate of 40.44% (Table 1) further supports the collaborative nature of the field.

General observations and limitations

Taken together, these findings indicate that Saudi Arabia-related folk medicine research is a growing, internationally connected domain whose thematic emphasis is concentrated in herbal medicine and bioactivity-related research. The bibliometric patterns also suggest continuing scholarly interest in how traditional medicine interfaces with modern health research and health systems [32].

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the dataset was defined by Saudi Arabia affiliation rather than strictly by topical focus, so the analysis captures Saudi-affiliated scholarship on traditional and folk medicine rather than only studies of Saudi folk medicine practices. Second, reliance on Scopus and Web of Science means that non-indexed and local-language publications may be underrepresented. Third, bibliometric indicators describe research production, influence, and collaboration patterns, but they capture only part of a complex and interdisciplinary field [33]. They therefore cannot validate therapeutic efficacy or determine policy readiness. Future work should complement bibliometric mapping with systematic reviews, ethnobotanical studies, clinical research, and policy analysis.

Conclusion

This bibliometric study mapped the scholarly landscape of folk medicine research related to Saudi Arabia using 2,527 Scopus and Web of Science records published between 1989 and 2024. The findings show sustained growth in publication output, increasing citation visibility, strong Saudi institutional participation, and substantial international collaboration. The dominant themes identified through keyword analysis were antioxidant activity, antimicrobial properties, herbal medicine, and traditional medicine. Because the dataset was defined by Saudi affiliation, the results should be interpreted as a map of Saudi Arabia-related knowledge production rather than an exclusive profile of Saudi folk medicine practices. Future studies can extend this work by incorporating additional databases and combining bibliometric methods with systematic, ethnobotanical, clinical, and policy research.

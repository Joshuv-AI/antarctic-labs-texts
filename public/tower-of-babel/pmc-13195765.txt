Surgery-oriented classification and treatment strategy for adult congenital biliary dilatation
Authors: Jia-Hong Dong, Nan Jiang, Shuo Jin, Yin-Zhe Xu, Tian Yang, Timothy M. Pawlik, Si-Qiao Shan, Dong-Liang Yang, Jian-Ping Zeng, Can-Hong Xiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundManagement of adult congenital biliary dilatation (CBD) lacks standardized surgical guidance, particularly for complex intrahepatic and intrapancreatic involvement. Traditional anatomical classifications inadequately address modern surgical decision-making needs. This study aimed to develop and validate a surgery-oriented classification system that guides precise surgical approaches for adult CBD.MethodsA classification system was developed based on anatomical characteristics, disease extent, and corresponding surgical approaches in 234 patients with CBD. Surgical procedures guided by the classification system included hepatectomy, biliary reconstruction, and liver transplantation. The long-term surgical outcomes and perioperative complications were analyzed.ResultsThe classification system categorized 234 patients with CBD into the following 4 types with 2 subtypes: Type A-peripheral intrahepatic (12 patients [5.1%]), Type B-central intrahepatic (5 [2.1%]), Type C-extrahepatic (45 [19.2%]), and Type D-combined intra- and extrahepatic (172 [73.5%]). Classification-guided surgery achieved excellent and good outcomes in 84.5% of cases. Major complications (Clavien–Dindo grade ≥ III) occurred in 33 (14.1%) patients. Three-year disease-free survival was 71.4%, 100.0%, 89.3%, and 63.0% for Types A to D, respectively (p = 0.026). Type D2 patients, defined as having the most complex disease patterns, had the worst outcomes (3-year disease-free survival: 61.6%).ConclusionsOur surgery-oriented classification effectively stratified adult CBD and guided the selection of surgical approaches. It shows particular value in managing complex disease patterns, although patients with combined intra- and extrahepatic involvement require careful consideration relative to the extent of surgical management. The standardized approach and validated outcomes of this classification suggest potential for widespread adoption in global surgical practice.

Introduction

The management of congenital biliary dilatation (CBD) (i.e., choledochal cyst) represents one of the most challenging areas in hepatobiliary surgery. CBD has potential implications for long-term morbidity and malignant transformation.1,2 CBD has a relatively low incidence in Western populations (1/150,000–1/100,000) but its prevalence is notably higher in Asian countries (1/1000), making it a global health concern.3,4 While complete surgical resection remains the standard of care for CBD, the optimal extent of resection and the reconstruction strategy, particularly for complex disease patterns, remains controversial.

The evolution of CBD management has been limited by inadequate classification systems. The Alonso–Lej classification, proposed in 1959, provided the first systematic categorization but was limited to extrahepatic disease.5 The subsequent Todani classification, introduced in 1977, incorporated intrahepatic involvement but was based on only 37 cases and preceded modern surgical capabilities.6 Although these historical classification systems have guided surgical practice for decades, their anatomical description-based approach has become increasingly inadequate for contemporary surgical decision-making.7, 8, 9.

Several critical limitations of these classification systems necessitate a new approach. Specifically, traditional CBD classification schemas fail to adequately address the complex anatomical variations of intrahepatic disease, leading to unclear surgical boundaries.10,11 Additionally, these classifications provide insufficient guidance to manage intrapancreatic involvement, resulting in high rates of residual disease and reoperation.12,13 Therefore, further classifying intrahepatic and intrapancreatic diseased bile ducts is necessary. Furthermore, the lack of integration with modern surgical techniques, particularly precision hepatectomy and advanced biliary reconstruction, is a major disadvantage.14,15 Recent advances in surgical technology, especially the management of intrahepatic and intrapancreatic diseased bile ducts, along with a deeper understanding of the pathogenesis of CBD, have reshaped traditional surgical principles and provided new opportunities for more targeted and effective interventions.10,13,16,17 Realizing these opportunities requires a classification system that bridges anatomical findings with a surgical strategy.

The transition from anatomy-based to a surgery-oriented classification represents a critical paradigm shift in CBD management. This transformation is particularly important for complex cases involving multiple biliary segments or combined intra- and extrahepatic disease in which traditional classifications offer limited guidance for surgical planning.18,19 A surgery-oriented classification system should not only describe anatomical features but also incorporate factors that influence surgical decision-making, such as the extent of intrahepatic involvement, the presence of intrapancreatic disease, and the feasibility of precision hepatectomy.20,21

In this study, we propose a novel surgery-oriented classification system for adult CBD based on an analysis of 234 consecutive cases. This system was designed to address three fundamental needs:(1) precise anatomical categorization that directly informs the surgical approach, (2) standardization of treatment strategies for complex disease patterns, and (3) integration of modern surgical techniques with optimal patient outcomes. This classification system correlates anatomical features with corresponding surgical approaches, offering a practical framework for clinical decision-making while ensuring consistent outcomes across diverse disease presentations.

Methods

Study design and patient cohort

This retrospective cohort study included consecutive adult patients who underwent surgical treatment for CBD between November 2014 and October 2024 at Beijing Tsinghua Changgung Hospital. CBDs, including their location and extent, were diagnosed according to preoperative radiology and confirmed by intraoperative findings. Secondary dilatations of the intra- and/or extrahepatic bile ducts due to proximal biliary obstruction, such as benign or malignant stricture or obstructive stones, were excluded.

Classification system development

The Tsinghua classification system was developed through a systematic analysis of anatomical patterns, clinical features, surgical approaches, and outcomes. Each case underwent a comprehensive radiological evaluation comprising magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography and computed tomography, and when indicated, endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography. The classification criteria were refined by correlation of imaging findings with intraoperative observations and pathological results.

While traditional classifications, such as the Todani system, primarily focus on anatomical descriptions, the new classification was developed to integrate anatomical features with surgical decision-making. Key features of the Tsinghua classification that are different to other systems include the following: (1) separation of peripheral and central intrahepatic involvement, which guides the choice between local resection and extensive hepatectomy; (2) explicit recognition of intrapancreatic involvement, which influences the extent of biliary resection; and (3) stratification of combined disease based on the order of biliary branch involvement, which determines the need for complex hepatobiliary reconstruction. The central intrahepatic bile duct (IHBD) was subdivided into the first-order branches (the left and right hepatic ducts), second-order branches (i.e., the right anterior and posterior hepatic ducts), and third-order branches (I–VIII segmental ducts and the bile ducts above). The peripheral IHBD was defined as the small peripheral branches.

The classification system categorizes CBD into four primary types with two subtypes each (Fig. 1) as follows: (1) Type A (peripheral intrahepatic), which is divided into localized peripheral IHBD involvement (side with severe lesions, A1) and diffuse peripheral IHBD involvement (A2); (2) Type B (central intrahepatic), which is divided into unilateral central IHBD involvement (B1) and bilateral central IHBD involvement (B2); (3) Type C (extrahepatic), which is divided into the extrahepatic bile duct (EHBD) without intrapancreatic involvement (C1) and EHBD with intrapancreatic involvement (C2); and (4) Type D (combined), which is divided into combined EHBD and central IHBD involvement ( ≤ second-order branches, D1) and combined EHBD and central IHBD involvement ( ≥ third-order branches, D2).Fig. 1Schematic Diagram and MRCP Imaging of the Eight Subtypes of Congenital Biliary Dilatation. All hepatectomies were performed following precision liver surgery principles described by Dong et al.17 Intraoperative cholangioscopy or cholangiography was routinely performed except in Type A cases. In Type D2 cases with inadequate liver remnant, an innovative mesohepatectomy comprising the hepatectomy technique beyond the P/U point and bile duct shaping was applied according to the technique described by Jin et al.14

Outcome assessment

The primary outcome was assessed using the modified Mayo Clinic score for CBD,16 which evaluates symptoms, liver function, and quality of life. The patients were stratified into excellent, good, fair, and poor categories according to established criteria.22

Secondary outcomes included perioperative complications graded according to the Clavien–Dindo classification,23 specific complications assessed using the International Study Group of Liver Surgery criteria, including post-hepatectomy liver dysfunction, bile leakage, and pancreatic leakage24, 25, 26 and long-term disease-free survival (DFS). DFS was defined as the interval from surgery to the first occurrence of any of the following events: cholangitis episode, bile stone formation, development of cholangiocarcinoma, any long-term complications related to biliary surgery, or death from any cause.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables are presented as median values with ranges according to their distribution patterns. DFS was analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method, with differences between groups assessed by the log-rank test. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Surgical Procedures based on classification

The surgical strategies were tailored according to the new classification (Table 3, Fig. 2). Six patients with Type A1 underwent hepatectomy, while four patients with Type A2 required liver transplantation. Patients with Type B1 underwent hemi-hepatectomy (n = 2) and left lateral sectionectomy (n = 1), while those with Type B2 (n = 2) underwent mesohepatectomy combined with excision of central IHBD dilatation and hepaticojejunostomy. In patients with Type C, complete excision of EHBD dilatation-as well as dilated intrapancreatic bile ducts-with hepaticojejunostomy were performed in 9 patients with C1 and in 35 with C2, with additional intrapancreatic duct excision in C2 cases. Patients with Type D underwent the most complex interventions. Twenty-one patients with D1 underwent complete excision of dilated EHBD and IHBD with hepaticojejunostomy (Fig. 2A), while 143 with D2 required combined hepatectomy and biliary reconstruction (Fig. 2B–D).

Long-term outcomes

With a median follow-up of 36 months (range: 3–115), after excluding 34 patients (18 lost to follow-up, 3 perioperative deaths, and 13 with cholangiocarcinoma), 200 patients were evaluated for long-term outcomes (Table 3). According to the Mayo Clinic score, 139 (69.5%) patients achieved excellent outcomes, 30 (15.0%) achieved good outcomes, 6 (3.0%) achieved fair outcomes, and 25 (12.5%) achieved poor outcomes. During the follow-up, lithiasis developed in 23 patients, predominantly in Type D2 cases (19/23). Ten patients with Type D2 required reoperation and two underwent liver transplantation, reflecting the complexity of managing advanced disease patterns.

A survival analysis showed variations among patients with different CBD types, and Type D had inferior outcomes compared with Type C (p = 0.026) (Fig. 3). The 3-year DFS was 71.4%, 100.0%, 89.3%, and 63.0% for Types A, B, C, and D, respectively. A subgroup analysis showed that patients with Type D2 had the lowest DFS (61.6%). Discussion

The present study proposed the development of the new Tsinghua classification system, which is a novel surgery-oriented approach for categorizing adult CBD that addresses critical limitations of traditional classification schemes. Based on the analysis of 234 consecutive cases, this system showed the value of integrating anatomical features with the surgical strategy, particularly in managing complex disease patterns.

The evolution from purely anatomical classifications to a surgery-oriented system represents a crucial advancement in CBD management, which includes the treatment of diseased bile ducts in the hepatic hilum and the treatment of intrahepatic and intrapancreatic diseased bile ducts.10,12, 13, 14,21 The implementation of all the above-mentioned surgical techniques is based on our principles of CBD surgical treatment involving resection of the diseased bile duct, elimination of secondary lesions, and restoration of biliary continuity and unimpeded drainage. Regarding the resection of diseased bile ducts in the hepatic hilar region, our experience involves anatomically isolating the dilated bile ducts from the accompanying portal vein and hepatic artery and resecting the diseased bile ducts distal to the confluence of the third-order hepatic ducts. Notably, the caudate lobe bile ducts draining into the dilated bile ducts should be properly managed. We have shown that complete resection of intrapancreatic diseased bile ducts should be performed and is routinely recommended; otherwise, it may lead to long-term complications including cancerization.12,13 Intrapancreatic diseased bile ducts should also be completely resected not only for type C2 but also for types D1 and D2 CBD involving intrapancreatic bile ducts.

The resection of intrahepatic diseased bile ducts primarily includes extensive hepatectomy (e.g., hemihepatectomy, trisegmentectomy, and mesohepatectomy), selective segmentectomy (involving affected liver segments), and liver parenchyma splitting techniques via hepatic fissures to remove the diseased bile ducts.10,14,21 When the diseased bile duct is extensive and the remaining liver volume is insufficient, intrahepatic diseased bile duct resection via the liver fissure splitting approach can be performed. The liver parenchyma is split along the median fissure or interlobar fissure to expose the intrahepatic diseased bile duct, and the accompanying vessels in the liver fissure are dissected. The diseased bile duct should be resected distal to the confluence of the normal bile ducts. Overall, because of the advancements in surgical techniques and concepts mentioned above, we have gradually achieved the ability to regularly remove affected liver segments and diseased bile ducts, maximize the preservation of functional liver parenchyma, and ultimately realize patient-specific surgeries.

Historical classification systems, while useful, have become increasingly inadequate for modern surgical decision-making. The Alonso–Lej classification,5 limited to extrahepatic disease, and the Todani classification,6 based on only 37 cases, preceded many modern surgical capabilities. The high incidence of previous surgical intervention in the current cohort (59.0%) indicated the limitations of traditional approaches. Advances in modern surgical techniques and concepts necessitate a reclassification of the CBD.

The current classification system differs fundamentally from traditional classifications in several aspects. While the Todani system classifies type IV-A as combined intra- and extrahepatic involvement without further stratification, our Type D classification specifically differentiates cases according to the extent of intrahepatic involvement, directly informing the choice between biliary reconstruction alone versus combined hepatectomy. Additionally, unlike previous systems that categorized all intrahepatic cases together, the proposed classification distinguishes between peripheral (Type A) and central (Type B) involvement, informing different surgical approaches. Moreover, the Todani classification divides extrahepatic bile duct dilation into types I, II, and III, which require extrahepatic bile duct resection, causing potential confusion. The Tsinghua system addresses this issue by categorizing these types uniformly as Type C. The specific recognition of intrapancreatic involvement (Types C and D) also addresses a critical aspect of surgical planning that was not explicitly considered in previous systems.12,13,27,28

Our system's effectiveness is reflected in the overall outcomes, with 84.5% of patients achieving excellent or good results according to the Mayo Clinic score. Differential DFS among patients with different CBD types, particularly the inferior outcomes in patients with Type D, highlighted the inherent challenges in managing complex disease patterns. This finding aligns with previous studies reporting poorer outcomes in cases with extensive intrahepatic involvement.10,29,30

Several factors contribute to the Tsinghua system's success in guiding surgical management. Anatomical precision enables appropriate selection of surgical extent, which is particularly important in complex intrahepatic disease. An example of this appropriate selection is that in Type D2 cases, the clear definition of third-order branch involvement helps determine the necessity and extent of hepatectomy.14,15 The standardized approach to intrapancreatic involvement in Types C and D cases has helped reduce the historically high incidence of residual disease and reoperation.12,13

The Tsinghua classification system showed particular value in addressing previously challenging scenarios. In Type A cases, the distinction between localized and diffuse involvement provides clear guidance on the choice between hepatectomy and liver transplantation. In Type B cases, the differentiation between unilateral and bilateral involvement helps optimize the extent of hepatic resection while preserving adequate liver function. The systematic approach to Type D disease, especially D2 cases, has enabled standardized management of the most challenging disease pattern.

The current analysis also identified some important challenges. The lower DFS in patients with Type D suggested that anatomical complexity continues to affect long-term outcomes despite standardized surgical approaches. This finding is consistent with previous reports of increased complications related to cases requiring complex biliary reconstruction.16,31 The high rates of cholangitis episodes and stone formation in Type D2 cases emphasizes the need for careful long-term surveillance in these patients.

There are several limitations to this study. First, the retrospective, single-center design may limit generalizability to different healthcare settings and patient populations. The relatively short median follow-up of 36 months, while sufficient for demonstrating early and mid-term outcomes, may not capture long-term complications or malignant transformation rates. Second, without a direct contemporary control group, improvements in outcomes cannot be definitively attributed solely to the classification system rather than general advances in surgical technique and perioperative care. Future research should include prospective, multicenter validation studies with longer follow-ups to establish the classification's reproducibility and effectiveness across different settings. Particular attention should be paid to Type D cases in which optimization of surgical strategies might further improve outcomes. Additionally, investigation of molecular markers and advanced imaging techniques might enhance the system's predictive value for long-term outcomes.

Conclusions

Our new classification system represents a considerable advance in the surgical management of adult CBD, providing a standardized, surgery-oriented framework that effectively guides treatment decisions and improves outcomes. By integrating anatomical features with the surgical strategy, this system addresses critical limitations of traditional classifications, particularly in complex disease patterns. While the differential outcomes among types highlight ongoing challenges in managing extensive disease, the Tsinghua system provides a practical foundation for surgical decision-making and standardized care. The findings of the current study support a paradigm shift from purely anatomical descriptions to a more sophisticated, surgery-oriented approach in CBD management. This transition represents an important step toward precision surgery in complex hepatobiliary disease, potentially improving outcomes for patients worldwide.

Selectively Inhibition of MAPK pathway showed positive effects on Porcine embryo-derived stem cells pluripotency
Authors: Zhuoran Yu, Mengjiao Zhang, Yanjiao Lv, Minli Liu, Yue Yu, Sirong Li, Sihan Zhou, Chaoqian Jiang, Zicheng Qi, Hongxing Wang, Jun Song, Xiaogang Weng, Xiuqin Yang, Tingsheng Yan, Zhonghua Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Expanded pluripotent stem cells (EPSCs), derived from pre-implantation embryos in specific culture conditions, can contribute to both embryonic and extraembryonic lineages. Human and mouse EPSCs have been successfully established in the LCDM condition, however, porcine embryo-derived stem cells derived in the LCDM system lack expanded pluripotency. To investigate the species-specific molecular mechanism of porcine embryo-derived stem cells (pLCDM) in LCDM condition, we compared the biological characteristics of pLCDM and porcine expanded potency stem cells (pEPSCs). The results revealed that pLCDM exhibited unique pluripotent traits and limited embryonic lineage differentiation potential, with a strong inclination towards extra-embryonic lineage differentiation. Subsequent investigation showed that (S)-(+) -dimethindene maleate (DIM) could completely inhibit the MAPK signaling pathway in pLCDM, and there were significant differences in the activity of the P38, JNK and ERK branches of the MAPK signaling pathway between pLCDM and pEPSC. Screening key molecular target inhibitors in three branches identified the B-RAF inhibitor SB590885 (SB59) as enhancing porcine embryonic stem cell pluripotency and facilitating lineage differentiation. Further analysis revealed a significant correlation between the expression level of phosphorylated ERK (p-ERK) and the developmental potential of porcine embryo-derived stem cells. These findings contribute valuable insights into the understanding of regulatory mechanism of porcine pluripotency.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23723-3.

Introduction

Pigs play a crucial role in domestic animal and serve as a valuable biomedical model organism. The isolation of porcine embryonic stem cells hold significant importance for both breeding and medical modeling of pigs. Research efforts on deriving porcine embryonic stem cells and investigating the regulatory mechanisms of porcine pluripotency have been ongoing since 19781. Compared to mouse embryonic stem cells, porcine embryo-derived stem cells show limited developmental potency2–4. Two research groups independently reported different systems that could establish expanded potency stem cells (EPSCs) from mouse and human embryos in 2017 and 20195,6. However, pluripotent stem cells derived from pig embryos in LCDM culture condition did not show the expanded pluripotent as mouse and human EPSCs7,8. The findings of these studies strongly indicated that the porcine pluripotency regulation networks are different from those of mice and humans. So finding the key factors resulting in the porcine species-specific characteristics in the mouse and human conserved EPSCs culture system will provide an entry point to explore the regulatory mechanisms of porcine pluripotency.

In 2017, Yang et al. identified the LCDM medium through high-throughput screening of small chemical molecules that can sustain the activity of the OCT4 distal enhancer and alleviate human pluripotent stem cells from transforming growth factor-β signaling dependency. EPSCs have been established in mice, humans using the LCDM medium, which consists of human leukemia inhibitory factor (hLIF), GSK3β inhibitor CHIR99021 (CH), G protein coupled receptors inhibitor DIM and PARP1 inhibitor minocycline hydrochloride (MIH)5. In 2022, we derived pluripotent stem cell line from porcine blastocysts in LCDM culture condition which were named pLCDM7. However, pLCDM exhibits a preferential commitment to extra-embryonic lineages while demonstrating restricted differentiation potential toward embryonic lineages. Another study derived mouse EPSCs by targeting key signaling pathways driving lineage differentiation in preimplantation embryos6. Subsequently, in 2019, porcine and human EPSCs were derived in this culture system through screening of signaling pathway regulators using a pluripotent gene reporter system3. To investigate the lack of expanded pluripotency in pLCDM, we compared the LCDM medium with the pEPSC medium and found that there are significant differences in MAPK signaling pathway regulation between the two media. Specific inhibition of SRC and Tankyrase is necessary for pEPSC. It was also mentioned in the paper that the addition of B-RAF and JNK inhibitors can significantly improve the efficiency of cell culture which are not essential components of pEPSC medium. In contrast, MAPK signaling pathway are inhibited in LCDM by DIM that predominantly targets G protein-coupled receptors, leading to a broader impact on the MAPK signaling pathway. We hypothesize that the comprehensive inhibition of MAPK in pLCDM may not provide the necessary support for the maintenance of porcine embryo-derived stem cells pluripotency.

Mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs) are a protein kinases family9 that phosphorylate specific serine and threonine residues on target protein substrates, thereby regulating cellular activities such as gene expression, mitosis, motility, metabolism, and programmed cell death10. In mammalian cells, there are three classic MAPK branches: p38 MAPKα/β/δ/γ (p38 branch), JNK1/2/3 (JNK branch), and ERK1/2(ERK branch) pathways11,12. These branches play crucial roles in maintaining the pluripotency of embryonic stem cells. (1) Studies have shown that P38 enhances cell viability through endogenous activation of protein kinase B13. In mouse, it can promote the differentiation of embryonic stem cells into mesoderm14. However, different to the effect in mouse embryonic stem cells, inhibition of p38 MAPK in NHSM culture medium of human embryonic stem cells can promote the expression of OCT4 and thus maintain pluripotency15. (2) Similarly, JNK signaling is vital for regulating self-renewal and differentiation in embryonic stem cells. The JNK signaling pathway has been shown to play a positive role in maintaining the undifferentiated state of human embryonic stem cell16. Treatment of human embryonic stem cells with JNK inhibitors (e.g., JNK inhibitor II (SP600125)17 and JNK inhibitor III18 led to cell differentiation and decreased OCT4 and NANOG expression in human embryonic stem cells19. It has been reported in mice, the JNK pathway can hinder endodermal differentiation of embryonic stem cells while prompting cell proliferation20. (3) The ERK signaling pathway, another classical signaling pathway regulating pluripotency, exhibits different regulatory mechanisms in pluripotent stem cells of different species and different pluripotency states. The FGF/ERK cascade is essential for differentiation in naïve mouse or human pluripotent stem cells, for ERK2−/− mouse embryonic stem cells displayed impaired neural and mesodermal differentiation capabilities21. The addition of the ERK1/2 inhibitor PD0325901 (PD) promoted the maintenance of undifferentiated mouse embryonic stem cells22,23 and naïve human pluripotent stem cells23–25. In contrast to the role of ERK signaling in naïve embryonic stem cells, ERK signaling is essential for maintaining primed pluripotency. The culture of traditional human embryonic stem cells and mouse epiblast stem cells requires FGF2, which activates the ERK signaling pathway26. Inhibition of FGF receptors or blockade of ERK signaling in human embryonic stem cell leads to increased expression of trophectoderm (TE) markers CDX1/2, gonadotrophin, and SOX7 (a primitive endoderm marker)27. In contrast with the extensively clarified roles of MAPK signaling in regulating pluripotency in mouse and human, the situation remains ambiguous in large mammals. This study demonstrates that DIM in LCDM medium completely inhibits the MAPK signaling pathway activity in pLCDM. However, DIM is not conducive to maintaining pEPSC and pLCDM pluripotency in vitro. By separately inhibiting key molecular targets of each branch of MAPK, small molecules were screened to enhance the pluripotency of porcine embryo-derived stem cells. Two cell lines with significantly improved pluripotency were established. The cell lines established in culture conditions with the B-RAF inhibitor SB59 instead of DIM had the ability to differentiate into both three germ layer and TE. Interestingly, the expression level of phosphorylated ERK increased significantly with multidirectional changes in differentiation potential. This study explored the roles of particular pathways of MAPK family on porcine pluripotency regulation and provided valuable insights for the practice of porcine embryonic stem cell derivation.

Result

Porcine embryo-derived stem cells under two EPSC conditions showed different pluripotency

Porcine pluripotent stem cells established from LCDM system demonstrated a tendency of extra-embryonic lineage differentiation7. Observable differences in pluripotency between pEPSC derived from EPSC medium and pLCDM derived from LCDM medium have been noted. The objective of this study is to provide a comparative analysis of the pluripotency of pLCDM and pEPSC for a more comprehensive understanding. Initially, pEPSC was re-established in the reported pEPSC medium (Fig. S1A). Contrary to pLCDM, which exhibited a flat and loose clonal morphology, pEPSC displayed a raised and compact clonal morphology. Both cell lines tested positive for Alkaline Phosphatase (AP). The pLCDM clones displayed darker edges and lighter middle colors, while pEPSC clones were evenly stained (Fig. 1A). The proliferation curve indicated a faster proliferation rate for pEPSC compared to pLCDM (Fig. S1B). Further analysis focused on the expression of pluripotency markers in the two cell lines. Relative Quantitative Reverse Transcription (RT-qPCR) was utilized to compare the expression levels of core transcription factors between pLCDM and pEPSC, revealing significantly lower levels of the three core transcription factors in pLCDM, particularly OCT4 (Fig. 1B). Immunofluorescence (IF)results showed that, consistent with the results of RT-qPCR, the expression of three core transcription factors of pEPSC was significantly higher than that of pLCDM (Fig. 1C). To comprehensively assess the pluripotency of pEPSC and pLCDM7, low input RNA-seq was conducted on the cell lines. Transcriptomic analysis unveiled that, in comparison to porcine D5.5 inner cell mass (ICM)28 in early blastocyst stage known for its naïve pluripotency, the expression levels of naïve markers were low in pEPSC. On the other hand, primed markers (such as SOX11 and PRDM14) were elevated in pEPSC. Conversely, the expression of pluripotency marker genes in pLCDM was generally lower than in ICM and pEPSC, suggesting a potential limitation in the pluripotency of pLCDM (Fig. 1D). Moreover, pLCDM also highly expressed markers of TE and endoderm. This corresponds to the fact that pLCDM has strong reported in 2022 (Fig. 1E). The transition from naïve to primed pluripotency in mouse and human pluripotent stem cells is linked to a shift from oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) to glycolytic metabolism. Our study found that genes associated with the tricarboxylic acid cycle and OXPHOS were highly expressed in the ICM, but downregulated in both pLCDM and pEPSC. Conversely, genes related to glycolysis were upregulated in pLCDM and pEPSC, but low in the ICM (Fig. 1F). Mitochondrial respiration as revealed by the mitochondrial membrane potential indicator TMRE was nearly absent in pLCDM and pEPSC, but became very prominent in ICM and porcine fetal fibroblasts, which mainly dependent on oxidative phosphorylation (Fig. S1C). Meanwhile, the metabolic pathway dependence analysis showed that ATP content and lactate production were not significantly decreased after the addition of the oxidative phosphorylation inhibitor Oligomycin in pLCDM and pEPSC. However, the content of ATP and lactate production in the two cells were significantly decreased after the addition of glycolysis inhibitor 2-DG (Fig. S1D; Fig. S1E). These results indicate that pLCDM and pEPSC are mainly dependent on glycolysis, which is consistent with the results of RNA-seq. Additionally, we compared the in vitro differentiation ability of the two cell lines. The (Embryoid Bodie) EBs of pLCDM exhibited a cavity shape, while those of pEPSC were globular and larger in diameter than pLCDM (Fig. 1G, Fig. S1F). RT-qPCR results indicated low expression levels of endoderm marker genes SOX17 and GATA6, as well as ectoderm marker genes GFAP and NCAM in pLCDM EBs, while three layers marker genes were significantly up-regulated in pEPSC EBs (Fig. 1H, I; Fig. S1G). Subsequent spontaneous differentiation of EBs in adherent culture revealed the expression of marker proteins for all three germ layers. The results showed that pEPSC showed significantly higher expression of three layers markers than pLCDM (Fig. 1J). These results suggest that porcine pluripotent stem cells in the two conditions exhibit distinct pluripotent characteristics compared to the naïve state. Remarkably, in comparison to pLCDM, pEPSC exhibited significantly more robust pluripotent properties and an expansive differentiation capability.

The MAPK pathway plays a key role in the pluripotency differences of Porcine embryonic stem cells derived under two EPSC conditions

Based on previous reports, multiple differential small molecules in both the EPSC and LCDM culture systems have been reported to be associated with the MAPK signaling pathway29,30.These include the SRC inhibitor (WH/A41925), JNK inhibitor (JNKi), and B-RAF inhibitor (SB59) in the EPSC system—where the SRC protein has been reported to be associated with the RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK, JNK, and p38 cascades—along with the G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) inhibitor DIM in the LCDM system, which regulates MAPK signaling through GPCR modulation (Fig. S2. A, B). We further investigated whether these small molecule combinations influence the pluripotency characteristics by modulating the activity states of key nodes within the MAPK signaling pathway. Gene expression analysis of each branch of the MAPK signaling pathway was conducted using RT-qPCR, while the protein levels of key components of the ERK, P38 and JNK branches were assessed through western-blot (WB). The key genes MKK3, P38 and BAX in the P38 branch were underexpressed in pEPSC, while the proteins P38 and p-P38 were also underexpressed in pEPSC (Fig. 2A, B). All the key genes of JNK branch showed low expression in pEPSC, and the expression of proteins JNK and p-JNK was low in pEPSC (Fig. 2E, F). However, in the absence of significant differences in other genes in the ERK branch, FGFR2 and MEK2 were highly expressed in pEPSC. The expression of p-ERK in pEPSC was significantly higher than pLCDM (Fig. 2C, D). The results revealed that compared to pLCDM, the P38 and JNK branches of pEPSC were significantly inhibited, while the ERK branches exhibited higher activity. Subsequently, DIM was introduced to replace the MAPK regulators WH, SB59 and JNKi in pEPSC medium with DIM (pEPSC (DIM)) (Fig. 2G), and found that pEPSC lost its dome-shaped colony form and showed weak positivity for AP staining (Fig. 2H). RT-qPCR results showed that OCT4, NANOG, SOX2, KLF4, ESRRB, C-MYC and other genes were significantly down-regulated (Fig. 2I). In addition, pEPSC (DIM) lost the ability to form EBs (Fig. 2J). These results indicate that the pluripotency of pEPSC cannot be maintained when the comprehensive inhibited branch leads to inactivation of the MAPK signaling pathway. In conclusion, the regulation of the MAPK signaling pathway plays a crucial role in determining the pluripotency of porcine embryo-derived stem cells under different conditions of EPSC.

DIM can comprehensively inhibit the MAPK signaling pathway in LCDM

The MAPK pathway, as a key signaling pathway associated with pluripotency, is a primary molecular target downstream of DIM inhibition. However, the effects of DIM on porcine embryonic stem cells exhibit species-specific differences. Our previous research results showed that DIM and MIH significantly influence the proportion of trophectoderm (TE) cells in porcine blastocysts7. MIH significantly enhances cytotrophoblast properties by inhibiting PARP1, yet whether a direct correspondence exists between DIM and the TE lineage remains unclear. To further investigate the regulatory mechanism of DIM on the pLCDM culture system and its impact on pluripotency, we conducted a study by deliberately removing DIM from the pLCDM matrix. The modified cell line was designated pLCM (Fig. 3A). A basic comparative analysis between pLCDM and pLCM was conducted. No significant differences in morphology and AP staining were observed between pLCDM and pLCM (Fig. 3B). The expression levels of core transcription factors and pluripotency related genes were assessed using RT-qPCR, revealing that while the expression of PRDM14 was down-regulated in pLCM compared to pLCDM, the levels of core transcription factors OCT4, SOX2 and naïve pluripotent marker genes C-MYC, ESRRB, TFCP2L1 and LIN28A were significantly increased in pLCM (Fig. 3C). The activity of the MAPK signaling pathway in pLCDM and pLCM cells was compared. RT-qPCR was utilized to assess the expression of key components and downstream target genes in the three classical branches of the MAPK signaling pathway. The findings indicated that compared to pLCDM, the expression levels of the ERK, JNK and p38 branches of the MAPK signaling pathway in pLCM were elevated (Fig. 3D-F). Furthermore, the phosphorylation levels of key components in the three branches were examined using WB, and it was found that the phosphorylation levels of ERK, JNK, and p38 were higher in pLCM than in pLCDM (Fig. 3G-I). These results suggest that DIM has the ability to completely inhibit the MAPK signaling pathway in pigs. The results noted that the combined inhibition of MAPK by DIM was not beneficial for maintaining the pluripotency of porcine embryo-derived stem cells in vitro.

Replacement of DIM with JNK or B-RAF inhibitors positively affected the pluripotency of Porcine embryo-derived stem cells

The regulation of pluripotency by the three branches of the MAPK signaling pathway is independent. The results of this study indicate that comprehensive inhibition of all three branches does not support the maintenance of pluripotency. Subsequently, we investigated the individual effects of each branch on the pluripotency of porcine embryo-derived stem cells. We replaced the DIM culture with SRC inhibitor WH, B-RAF inhibitor SB59, MEK inhibitor PD03, JNKi, P38 inhibitor SB20 and FGFR inhibitor BGJ398 in the LCDM medium (Fig.S2A, Fig. 4A). Compared to the control group pLCDM, pLCPDM and pLCBM lost their typical colony morphology and exhibited significant differentiation (Fig. S3). PLCWHM displayed a mixed cell morphology of hill and flat, with a decreased AP positive rate (Fig. 4B, F). RT-qPCR results revealed a significant increase in SOX2 expression and a decrease in NANOG expression in pLCWHM compared to pLCDM. Additionally, the TE lineage marker gene GATA3 was significantly reduced (Fig. 4J), Furthermore, the expression levels of NRAS, MEK1, ERK1, SRC and P38 were significantly decreased in pLCWHM compared to pLCM. These results confirmed the inhibitory effect of WH (Fig. S4A). However, WH replacement of DIM did not significantly enhance the pluripotency of pLCDM. The clone of PLCSB20M exhibited an uplift, but severe cavitation occurred on the clone surface and the AP positive rate decreased significantly (Fig. 4C, G). The expression levels of SOX2, NANOG and KLF4 were significantly reduced in pLCSB20M. Conversely, the expression of the TE lineage marker GATA3 was notably increased (Fig. 4K). And the expression level of P38 was significantly decreased compared to pLCM, indicating the inhibitory effect of SB20 (Fig. S4D). The study investigated the impact of cavitation phenomenon on pLCSB20M clones and its effect on apoptotic gene expression were further detected. The results showed that the expression of pLCSB20M apoptosis related gene BAX was significantly increased (Fig. S4E). SB20 is not only detrimental to the maintenance of pluripotency, but also induce cell apoptosis. Furthermore, pLCSB59M and pLCJNKiM clones were all hilly, and the positive rate of pLCSB59M AP was significantly increased (Fig. 4D, E, H, I). The expression levels of pLCSB59M NANOG, SOX2, OCT4 and ESRRB were significantly increased. TE lineage marker genes CDX2 and GATA3 were significantly decreased (Fig. 4L). The expression levels of BRAF, MEK1, ERK1 and ERK2 were significantly decreased compared to pLCM, which proved the inhibitory effect of SB59 (Fig. S4B). The expression levels of pLCJNKiM NANOG, OCT4 and ESRRB were significantly increased (Fig. 4M). And the expression level of C-FOS, the main downstream factor of JNK, was significantly lower than that of pLCM (Fig. S4C). Inhibition of B-RAF and JNK can significantly improve the pluripotency of pLCDM and effectively inhibit its trophoblastic characteristics. Interestingly, pLCMSB59 and pLCMJNKi groups with elevated pluripotent marker genes exhibited higher ERK phosphorylation levels (Fig. S4F). These significant revelations intimate that the substitution of DIM with JNK or SB59 specific inhibitors can support pluripotency maintenance and enhance ERK phosphorylation.

Porcine embryo-derived stem cells derived from conditions with B-RAF inhibitor showed improved pluripotency

To further demonstrate the impact of SB59 and JNKi on maintaining pluripotency in porcine embryo-derived stem cells in vitro, we utilized LCM + SB59 and LCM + JNKi media to establish pluripotent stem cell lines from D7 in vitro fertilization embryos. The morphology of pLCSM and pLCJM clones appeared hill-like, with a clear nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio and distinct colony boundaries (Fig. 5A). Subsequent pluripotency analysis revealed that both pLCSM and pLCJM exhibited positive AP staining, with significantly higher AP positive rates compared to pLCDM (Fig. 5A, B). Moreover, the doubling time of these cell lines decreased significantly, indicating enhanced proliferation capacity (Fig. 5C). IF results showed that the expression levels of OCT4 and NANOG in pLCJM were significantly higher than pLCDM, but there was no significant difference in the expression level of SOX2 compared with pLCDM. The expression levels of three core transcription factors of pLCSM were significantly higher than pLCDM, and the expression levels of NANOG and SOX2 were even higher than pEPSC (Fig. 5D; Fig. S5). Furthermore, RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated significantly higher expression levels of core transcription factors in pLCSM compared to pLCDM, with comparable levels of KLF4 and SOX2 to pEPSC. Additionally, pLCSM exhibited higher expression of naïve genes REX1 and ESRRB compared to pLCDM, while the expression of primed gene SALL4 remained unchanged. In contrast, while pLCJM exhibited a noticeable enhancement in the expression of SOX2, REX1, and SALL4 compared to pLCDM, its overall expression of pluripotency markers remained lower than that observed in pEPSC (Fig. 5E-G). Notably, the TE marker CDX2 was significantly lower in pLCSM than in pLCDM, while in pLCJM, both CDX2 and GATA3 were down-regulated but still higher than in pEPSC (Fig. 5H). The in vitro differentiation capacity of the cell lines was evaluated via embryoid body (EB) formation. While no significant difference in EB diameter was observed across cell lines, pLCDM produced EBs with hollow cavities, whereas those from pLCSM and pLCJM were solid spheres. (Fig. 5I, J). The expression of marker genes detected by RT-qPCR in TE (GATA3), endoderm (GATA6), mesoderm (PECAM) and ectoderm (GFAP) showed that pLCJM showed improved ectoderm differentiation ability compared to pLCDM. The pLCSM group demonstrated an average differentiation ability across four germ layers, however pLCDM exhibited a more obvious trophoblast differentiation tendency (Fig. 5K). To determine the effect of inhibition of B-RAF or JNK on the pluripotency of porcine embryo-derived stem cells, the degree of activation of the three branches of MAPK of the two cell lines is necessary. The activity of P38 and JNK branches in both cell lines was higher than that of pLCDM (Fig. S6A-B, E-F). In pLCJM, the expression of key genes (MLK3, MKK7, JNK, C-FOS, C-MYC) and proteins (JNK, p-JNK) in the JNK branch were significantly lower than pLCSM, indicating the inhibitory effect of JNKi (Fig. S6E-F). Additionally, pLCSM exhibited significantly reduced expressions of FGFR2, BRAF, MEK1 and MEK2, while the expression of ERK, a key downstream component, remained unchanged at the RNA level (Fig. S6C). The expression level of p-ERK protein was higher in pLCSM compared to pLCDM and pLCJM (Fig. S6D). The high p-ERK protein expression in pEPSC and pLCSM suggests a positive correlation between ERK function and porcine pluripotency maintenance in vitro. Overall pLCSM and pLCJM demonstrated strong pluripotency characteristics compared to pLCDM, with pLCSM showing marker genes expression of embryonic and extraembryonic during in vitro differentiation, and an increase in p-ERK expression correlating with improved pluripotency.

Discussion

While the LCDM system in mice and humans can generate EPSCs with both intraembryonic and extraembryonic differentiation potential, porcine embryo-derived stem cells (pLCDM), despite exhibiting strong extraembryonic differentiation ability, demonstrate limited intraembryonic differentiation potential7. The pEPSC successfully established in another culture system has the ability to differentiate into both embryonic and extraembryonic cells in vitro3. In this study, we compare the biological characteristics of pLCDM and pEPSC, and the results showed that pLCDM had relatively limited pluripotency and a strong extra-embryonic lineage characteristics. The latest findings have confirmed that DIM is a potent inhibitor of the MAPK signaling pathway in pLCDM. However, the evidence shows that DIM can have an adverse effect on the pluripotency of porcine embryo-derived stem cells. Instead of using DIM, some MAPK branch inhibitors were used to culture pLCDM. It was found that specific inhibition of B-RAF or JNK could significantly improve the pluripotency of pLCDM. Notably, the improved system supported the newly established cell line. The pLCSM had no obvious differentiation tendency, and the in vitro differentiation experiments confirmed the expression of embryonic and extraembryonic marker genes. As per our finding, ERK phosphorylation levels have exhibited an increase in tandem with the weakening of TE lineages, aligning with the enhancement of multidirectional differentiation potential.

Our research group has found that pLCDM exhibits a strong inclination towards TE lineage differentiation. Within this system, DIM and MIH have been shown to significantly increase the proportion of TE cells in porcine blastocysts7. It is important to note that MIH is recognized for its ability to inhibit PARP131. A critical mechanism for the generation and maintenance of mouse and human EPSCs involves blocking the poly (ADP-ribosyl) ation activities of the PARP family members TNKS1/232,33. Interestingly, Parp1 deficiency in mouse embryonic stem cells led to their differentiation into trophoblast derivatives34,35, and an upregulation of extraembryonic differentiation pathways was observed in Parp1 −/− mouse embryonic stem cells36. Essentially, inhibiting PARP promotes the potential of the trophoblast differentiation. However, DIM did not show a direct correlation with TE lineage. DIM regulates MAPK pathway in LCDM by inhibiting histamine and muscarinic receptor signaling37, as MAPK is the main downstream pathway of histamine and muscarinic receptor signaling38. Single-cell RNA-Seq analysis revealed that the members of MAPK signaling pathway was highly expressed in ICM and epiblast cells during porcine early embryonic development, indicating its crucial role in embryonic development39. Our results demonstrated that DIM can completely block the MAPK signaling pathway in pLCDM, thereby hindering the maintenance of pluripotency of porcine embryo-derived stem cells in vitro. Considering the limited embryonic differentiation potency of pLCDM, we inferred that the cascade of at least one of the MAPK signaling branches is crucial for maintaining porcine pluripotency and embryonic differentiation potency.

This study aims to investigate the impact of various branches of the MAPK signaling pathway on porcine embryo-derived stem cells. The MEK/ERK, JNK, and p38 branches of the MAPK signaling pathway have been found to have significant roles in mouse and human embryonic stem cells. In this study, inhibitors targeting key molecular components of each branch were used to replace DIM in the LCDM system. The results indicate that specific inhibition of JNK or B-RAF positivly affects the pluripotency of pLCDM. Notably, the cell line pLCSM, established after replacing DIM with a B-RAF inhibitor, exhibited high expression of pluripotent marker genes and low expression of trophoblast marker genes. Furthermore, in vitro differentiation experiments revealed that pLCSM showed balanced differentiation potential across the embryonic and extraembryonic layers, with no clear tendency towards TE lineage differentiation. Interestingly, inhibition of B-RAF with SB59 led to unexpected increase phosphorylation of downstream ERK components. This phenomenon has been previously reported and attributed to transactivation of RAF homo- and heterodimers inducing MEK/ERK phosphorylation by RAF inhibitors40–43. The study also found a positive correlation between p-ERK expression levels and the pluripotency and differentiation potential of porcine embryo-derived stem cells, highlighting the significant role of ERK in regulating porcine pluripotency. Notably, ERK exhibits different regulatory mechanisms in pluripotent stem cells of different species and pluripotent states. For instance, in mouse embryonic stem cells, ERK directly phosphorylates and inhibits Oct4, Nanog, Klf4 and Klf244–49. In particular, phosphorylation of Klf2 and Klf4 by ERK induces their nuclear export and degradation45,48. Several studies demonstrated that ERK also directly phosphorylates the LIF-induced transcription factor STAT3 in response to growth factors50,51. Inhibiting ERK-mediated STAT3 phosphorylation enhances LIF’s ability to reprogram stem cells towards naïve pluripotency. At the same time, ERK can attenuate BMP signaling, leading to embryonic stem cell differentiation in mice49,52,53. In human embryonic stem cell, FGF2/ERK can synergize with SMAD2/3 to induce NANOG expression and cooperate with the phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase signaling pathway to enhance cell self-renewal54–57. Overall, ERK plays a crucial role in the pluripotency and differentiation of pluripotent stem cells across species, with varying characteristics and mechanisms. The regulatory role of ERK in porcine pluripotency warrants further investigation.

Our study revealed species-specific regulatory effects of MAPK on pig pluripotency, highlighting the need for deeper exploration of the underlying mechanisms. These findings provide a foundation for understanding the regulatory mechanism of pig pluripotency and contribute to the practice of porcine naïve embryo-derived stem cells.

Materials and methods

Animals

Porcine ovaries were collected from Harbin XinCheng Food Co., Ltd (Harbin, China). 13.5 days post coitum C57BL/6 mice were obtained from Harbin Medical University. Mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation. All experimental procedures involving animals were performed in accordance with protocols approved by the Laboratory Animal Ethics Committee of Northeast Agricultural University (Ethics Number: NEAUEC20240217). All experiments were conducted in adherence to the guidelines of the Chinese Council on Animal Care, and all methods and procedures were fully compliant with the ARRIVE guidelines58.

Culturing of pLCDM

E5-6 blastocysts from large white pigs were used to derive embryo-derived stem cells. Each blastocyst was first removed from the zona pellucida, and then the ICM was isolated using either micromanipulation techniques or a glassneedle. The isolated ICM was cultured under LCDM culture conditions for more than 7 days until hatchling and growth. The outgrowth was ruptured mechanically into small pieces by mouth pipette and transferred onto feeder cells for subculture. It was then cultured in humidified conditions with 5% O2, 5% CO2, and 90% N2 at 39 °C. The medium was refreshed every 24 h and the cells were passaged by 0.05% Trypsin (Gibco) every 4–5 days. Mouse embryonic fibroblasts were isolated from 13.5 day C57BL/6 mouse fetuses and treated with 10 µg/ml mitomycin C for 2–3 h to be used as feeder cells. The medium of LCDM was DMEM/F12 (Gibco) with Neurobasal (Gibco), 0.5% N2 (Thermo), 1% B27 (Thermo), 1% GlutaMAX (Sigma), 1% NEAA (Gibco), 0.1 mM β-mercaptoethanol (Sigma), penicillin-streptomycin (CELLGRO), 5 mg/ml bovine serum albumin (Sigma), 10 ng/ml recombinant hLIF (Millipore), 3 µM CHIR99021 (TOCRIS), 2 µM DIM (TOCRIS), and 2 µM MIH (Abcam). It was cultured in humidified conditions with 5% O2, 5% CO2, and 90% N2 at 39 °C. The medium refreshed every 24 h and the cells were passaged by 0.05% Trypsin (Gibco) every 4–5 days.

Derivation of pEPSC under EPSC culture conditions

E5-6 blastocysts from large white pigs were used to derive porcine pluripotent stem cells. The EPSC medium was DMEM/F12 (Gibco) with 0.5% N2 (Thermo), 1% B27 (Thermo), 1% GlutaMAX (Sigma), 1% NEAA (Gibco), 0.1 mM β-mercaptoethanol (Millipore), penicillin-streptomycin (CELLGRO), 5 mg/ml bovine serum albumin (Sigma), 10 ng/ml recombinant hLIF (Millipore), 0.2 µM CH CHIR99021 (Stemgent),0.3 µM WH-4–023 (Tocris), 0.25 µM SB590885 (Tocris), 4 µM JNK Inhibitor Ⅷ (Tocris), 20 ng/ml Activin A (R&D) and 2.5 µM XAV939 (Sigma). Each blastocyst was first removed from the zona pellucida, and then the ICM was isolated using either micromanipulation techniques or a glass needle. The isolated ICM was cultured under EPSC medium for more than 7 days until hatchling and growth. The outgrowth was ruptured mechanically into small pieces by mouth pipette and transferred onto feeder cells for subculture. It was then cultured in humidified conditions with 5% CO2 at 37℃. pEPSC cells were maintained on STO feeder layers and enzymatically passaged every 3–5 days by a brief Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline wash followed by treatment with 0.05% trypsin/EDTA (Gibco) for 2 mins.

Replacing small molecule inhibitors to culture pLCDM

On D0 of pLCDM passage, DIM was replaced with 0.3 µM WH-4–023 (Tocris), 0.25 µM SB590885 (Tocris), 1 µM PD0325901 (Tocris), 4 µM JNK Inhibitor Ⅷ (Tocris), 5 µM SB203580 (Tocris) and 0.3 µM BGJ398 (Selleck). They were cultured in humidified conditions O2, 5% CO2, and 90% N2 at 39 °C. The medium refreshed every 24 h and the cells were passaged by 0.05% Trypsin (Gibco) every 4–5 days. Culture more than three generations for various pluripotency tests.

In vivo embryo acquisition

The embryos used in this study were obtained from normal-cycle female pigs, with the age at use being 6 months and the mating occurring on the 0th day of pregnancy. The uteri were collected from the pigs on the 5.5th day and transported to the laboratory within 1 h using 37℃ warm water. The embryos were rinsed twice with 50 ml of PBS containing 5% fetal bovine serum.

In vitro oocyte maturation

Porcine ovaries were collected and kept in saline containing penicillin and streptomycin at 32–37 °C. Antral follicles were aspirated using 10-gauge needles. High-quality oocytes with evenly granulated cytoplasm and at least three uniform layers of compact cumulus cells were selected and cultured in maturation medium ([TCM‐199; Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA] supplemented with 0.07 mg/ml cysteine, 0.05 mg/ml epidermal growth factor, 0.5 mg/ml luteinizing hormone, and 0.5 mg/ml follicle‐stimulating hormone) at 39 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere. After 42 h maturation, porcine oocytes with the first polar bodies were considered mature oocytes and were selected for further experiments. The nuclear maturation depends on the first polar bodies extrusion under the stereoscopic microscope after denuding cumulus oocyte complexes.

In vitro fertilization of Porcine oocytes

Matured cumulus oocyte complexes were vortexed for 3 mins in 4-(2‐hydroxyethyl) ‐1‐piperazineethanesulfonic acid‐buffered medium with 0.1% hyaluronidase to remove the cumulus cells. Each group, containing 30 mature denuded oocytes, was placed in a 50 µl droplet of modified Tris‐buffered medium for fertilization. Fresh sperm kept at 17 °C was briefly warmed at 39 °C, and then washed two times in Dulbecco’s phosphate‐buffered saline supplemented with 0.1% (w/v) bovine serum albumin by centrifuging the sample at 2 000 rpm for 4 mins. The suspended sperm was added into the droplets with oocytes at a final concentration of 3 × 105/ml, and the mixtures were incubated at 39 °C for 6 h. Finally, the fertilized embryos were cultured in porcine zygote medium ‐ 3 in four‐well plate at 39 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere.

Derivation of pLCSM and pLCJM lines

E7-8 in vitro fertilization blastocysts are used to establish pLCSM and pLCJM. The medium of pLCSM or pLCJM was DMEM/F12 (Gibco) with Neurobasal (Gibco), 0.5% N2 (Thermo), 1% B27 (Thermo), 1% GlutaMAX (Sigma), 1% NEAA (Gibco), 0.1 mM β-mercaptoethanol (Sigma), penicillin-streptomycin (CELLGRO), 5 mg/ml bovine serum albumin (Sigma), 10 ng/ml recombinant hLIF (Millipore), 3 µM CHIR99021(Tocris), 2 µM MIH (Abcam) and 0.25 µM SB590885 (Tocris) or 4 µM JNK Inhibitor Ⅷ (Tocris). It was cultured in humidified conditions with 5% O2, 5% CO2, and 90% N2 at 39 °C. The medium refreshed every 24 h and the cells were passaged by 0.05% Trypsin (Gibco) every 2–3 days.

Alkaline phosphatase (AP)

Cells were fixed with 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde for 90 s and then washed 3 times with DPBS. AP activity was determined with the BCIP/NBT Alkaline Phosphatase Color Development Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China).

Immunofluorescence (IF) analysis

Cells were fixed for 30 mins at room temperature with 4% (w/v) paraformaldehyde. They were then permeabilized with 1% (v/v) TritonX-100 in PBS for 1 h at 4 °C and blocked with 1% (w/v) bovine serum albumin in washing buffer [1% (v/v) Tween 20, 0.1% (v/v) TritonX-100] for 1 h at 37 °C. The primary antibodies (OCT4, sc-8628, 1:50, goat, Santa Cruz; SOX2, sc-17320, 1:50, goat, Santa Cruz; NANOG, 500-p236, 1:200, rabbit, PEPROTECH; GATA6, ab22600, 1:50, rabbit, Abcam; α-SMA, ab5694500, 1:50, rabbit, Abcam; β-tubulin, AB6046, 1:50, rabbit, Abcam) were incubated overnight at 4 °C, and then washed 3 times with washing buffer for 5 mins each time. The secondary antibodies (IgG 488 for donkey anti-goat, A11055, 1:500, Invitrogen; IgG 488 for donkey anti-rabbit, A21206, 1:500, Invitrogen) were added and treated at 37 °C for 1 h. They were then washed 3 times with washing buffer for 5 mins each time. Cell nuclei were stained with Hoechst 33,342 for 10 mins. Finally, the cells were mounted on glass slides and examined with a Nikon 80i microscope. The quantitative measurement of fluorescence intensity was accomplished using ImageJ (NIH, USA). The general process is as follows: After the original image undergoes grayscale conversion, the target area is selected manually or using the automatic threshold method (Otsu algorithm), and the average fluorescence intensity is measured. To eliminate background interference, random blank areas of the image are selected to measure the background value and then subtracted. At least 3 fields of view were analyzed for each group of samples. After the fluorescence intensity was quantified by ImageJ, the mean value of the control group was set as the benchmark (designated as 1).

Relative quantitative reverse transcription (RT-qPCR)

Polymerase Chain Reaction Total RNA was extracted from the collected samples using a RNeasy mini kit (Qiagen, Duesseldorf, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was prepared using High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kits (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA). RT-qPCR was performed using Premix Ex Taq (Perfect Real-Time, TaKaRa, Japan) and a 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). Gene expression was determined relative to GAPDH using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Data are represented as the mean and S.D. Primer pairs are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Western-Blot (WB)

The protein from cells digested with 100 µl IP Lysis Buffer (Beyotime), were mixed with 1 µl phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (Beyotime), and incubated for 3 mins in boiling water. The samples were then separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) (GenScript M01210C) at 110 V for 1 h. Separated proteins were then electrophoretically transferred onto a nitrocellulose membrane at 350 mA for 1 h. Membranes were then blocked in TBST buffer (10 mM Tris,150 mM NaCl, 0.1% Tween 20, pH 7.4) containing 3% bovine serum albumin (B2064; Sigma‐Aldrich), for 1 h at room temperature and then incubated with primary antibodies for ERK1/2 (4695, Rabbit, 1:2 000, CST), p-ERK1/2 (4370 S, Rabbit, 1:2 000, CST), JNK1/2/3 (Ab179461, Rabbit, 1: 1 000, Abcam), p-JNK1/2/3 (Ab124956, Rabbit, 1:1 000, Abcam), P38 (8690 S, Rabbit, 1:1 000, CST), p-P38 (4511 S, Rabbit, 1:1 000, CST), β-actin (A1978, Mouse, 1:1 000, Sigma). Rinse 3 times in TBST for 10 mins each time and the secondary antibodies (IgG‐HRP for goat anti rabbit, A9169, 1:5 000, Sigma; IgG-HRP for rabbit anti mouse, A9044, 1:5 000, Sigma) was treated at room temperature for 1 h. The TBST was washed 3 times for 10 mins each time, and the results were observed and recorded using SageCapture software after color development. Objective protein size reference Spectra ™ multi-color wide range of protein molecular weight standards (26634, Thermofisher). Since the size of the target protein is relatively close to β-ACTIN, in order to avoid the interference between the protein bands, we chose to use samples from the same batch for quantitative loading instead of presenting the target protein and β-ACTIN on the same membrane. The Protein concentrations of different groups of cells were leveled by NCM BCA Protein Assay Kit (WB6501, NCM Biotech). Among all independent results, samples for WB experiments with different NC membrans were collected from the same batch.

Embryoid bodies (EB) formation

Cells were digested with 0.05% trypsin to single cells and then transferred to culture without matrix coating in 15% fetal bovine serum medium [high-glucose Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (DMEM, Invitrogen), supplemented with 15% fetal bovine serum (Biological Industries, Kibbutz Beit Haemek, Israel), 1% (v/v) NEAA, 2 mmol/l L-glutamine and 100 U/ml penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco), and gently shaken daily. The formation of EBs was observed in 7–10 days and used to detect the expression of markers for the three germ layers. On days 6–7, EBs were gradually formed and transferred to Matrigel-coated 24-well plates, and the medium was replaced daily with fresh 15% fetal bovine serum. Cells from days 12 to 14 were used for IF staining and RT-qPCR analyses.

RNA-seq data processing and analysis

The Smart-Seq2 method was used to amplify the low input cell sample. The concentration was then measured using a Qubit 2.0 Fluorometer (Life Technologies, CA, USA). The integrity of amplified products was detected by the Agilent 2100 High Sensitivity DNA Assay Kit (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). Libraries were sequenced by an Illumina HiSeq X Ten platform. Qualified reads were aligned with Tophat 2 into the porcine reference genome (Suscrofa 11.1.105) using the default parameters. Reads on the extracted alignment were constructed with Cufflinks to construct transcripts. The transcripts of all samples were combined and reconstructed into a large transcript file (merged.gtf) using Cuffmerge. Then, the expression of genes in each sample was quantified to FPKM by Cuffnorm.

Mitochondria and mitochondrial membrane potential staining

For live imaging of mitochondria and mitochondrial membrane potential, Porcine fetal fibroblasts, pLCDM, pEPSC or embryonic ICM were incubated in washing buffer [1% (v/v) Tween 20, 0.1% (v/v) TritonX-100] containing MitoTracker Green FM (Invitrogen, M7514, 1:50) and tetramethylrhodamine, methylester, perchlorate (TMRM) (Invitrogen, T668, 1:100) for 30 mins at 37 °C. They were then washed 3 times with washing buffer for 5 mins each time. Cell nuclei were stained with Hoechst 33,342 for 10 mins. Finally, examined with a Nikon 80i microscope.

Metabolic pathway dependent evaluation

Metabolic pathway dependent analysis of glycolysis or oxidative phosphorylation of pLCDM and pEPSC was assessed by measuring ATP and lactate in a SpectraMax reg iD3 according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, pLCDM and pEPSC were inoculated in a white 96-well plate and cultured in humidified conditions with 5% CO2 at 37 °C for 24 h. After the culture medium was removed, a culture medium containing 1.25 µM Oligomycin or 30mM 2-DG was added to the well as the experimental group, and a culture medium containing no inhibitor was added to the control group. The cells in each group were continued to be cultured at 37 °C and 5% CO2 for 5 h, and each group was repeated three times. After adding the working liquid provided by the Glycolysis/OXPHOS Assay Kit (Dojindo, G270), the ATP and lactate in each group were detected by SpectraMax reg iD3.

Statistical analysis

To evaluate the statistical differences in expression of genes among cells at different stages. P-values were evaluated by one-way ANOVA (Dunnett’s multiple comparisons test). Differences were considered significant when P < 0.05.

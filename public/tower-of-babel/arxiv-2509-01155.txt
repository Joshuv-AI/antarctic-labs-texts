On finite-energy solutions of Kazdan-Warner type equations on the lattice graph
Huyuan Chen, Bobo Hua

Abstract
. We investigate finite-energy solutions to Kazdan-Warner type equations in 2-dimensional integer lattice graph − Δ ​ u = ε ​ e κ ​ u + β ​ δ 0 , -\Delta u=\varepsilon e^{\kappa u}+\beta\delta_{0}, where ε = ± 1 \varepsilon=\pm 1 , κ > 0 \kappa>0 and β ∈ ℝ \beta\in\mathbb{R} . When ε = 1 \varepsilon=1 , we prove the existence of a continuous family of finite-energy solutions for some parameter κ \kappa . This provides a partial resolution of the open problem on the existence of finite-energy solutions to the Liouville equation. When ε = − 1 \varepsilon=-1 and β > 4 ​ π κ \beta>\frac{4\pi}{\kappa} , we prove that the set of finite-energy solutions exhibits a layer structure. Moreover, we derive the extremal solution in this case.



Keywords
: Kazdan-Warner equations, Liouville equations, Lattice graphs, Green’s functions.

1.
Introduction

The Kazdan-Warner equation is a nonlinear partial differential equation that arises in differential geometry for solving the prescribed Gaussian curvature problem on surfaces in conformal geometry.
Let
({\mathcal{M}},g)
be a Riemannian surface and let
\tilde{g}=ge^{2u}
be a metric with conformal change
u\in C^{2}(]{\mathcal{M}})
. For the prescribed Gaussian curvature problem, we have the following equation, introduced by Kazdan and Warner
[
KW74
]
,

-\Delta_{g}u=\tilde{K}e^{2u}-K,

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{g}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator,
K
and
\tilde{K}
are the Gaussian curvature of
({\mathcal{M}},g)
and
({\mathcal{M}},\tilde{g})
respectively.
These type equations have been the subjects of extensive investigation in the literature
[
Mos71
,
Mos73
,
Aub79
,
DJLW97
,
Cha04
]
,
motivated by its connections to geometry, statistical mechanics, and physics; see also the mean field equation
[
Ons49
,
ES06
]
and the Chern-Simons equation
[
JT80
,
HKP90
]
etc.

Consider the special case for conformal changes on the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}.
For finding a conformal metric
(\mathbb{R}^{2},e^{\kappa u}ds_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{2})
with constant Gaussian curvature
\frac{\kappa}{2}
with
\kappa>0,
we have the Liouville equation

-\Delta u=e^{\kappa u}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.2)

Although one may reduce the general parameter
\kappa
to the model case
\kappa=2
by the scaling of
\mathbb{R}^{2},
we will keep it for our purposes where the scaling is absent. For any solution
u
of (
1.2
), by a classical result of Liouville

[
Lio53
]
, any solution
u
of (
1.2
)
has the form

u(z)=\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\frac{8|f^{\prime}|^{2}}{(1+\kappa|f|^{2})^{2}},

where
f
is a locally univalent meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}.
By a seminal result of Chen–Li
[
CL93
]
, the solution
u
of (
1.2
) satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{\kappa u}dx<+\infty
, i.e., it has finite total curvature or finite energy, if and only if there exist
\lambda>0,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

u(x)=\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\frac{32\lambda^{2}}{4+\lambda^{2}\kappa|x-x_{0}|^{2}}.

(1.3)

Moreover, in this case the total curvature

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\kappa}{2}e^{\kappa u}dx=4\pi.

(1.4)

See
[
CW94
,
CK95
,
JW02
,
BLdSN04
,
HW06
,
Ere07
,
LT20
]
for other proofs, and a recent survey
[
CL24
]
.

In recent years, the study of partial differential equations on graphs has attracted increasing attention and has played an important role in various fields. For a given graph, there is a natural discrete Laplace operator, which allows one to formulate and analyze PDE problems in the discrete setting. In the case of nonlinear equations, Grigor’yan, Lin, and Yang
[
GLY16
]
investigated the Kazdan–Warner equation on finite graphs and established existence results, which were subsequently extended in
[
Ge17
,
FZ18
,
Ge20
,
LY20
,
CM22
,
PS22
,
SW22
,
LZ23
,
ZLY24
]
. Existence results for Kazdan–Warner equations on infinite graphs were proven in
[
GJ18
,
KS18
]
under certain assumptions. Furthermore, Huang, Lin, and Yau
[
HLY20
]
proved the existence of solutions to the mean field equation and the Chern–Simons equation on finite graphs. A number of related works have also been devoted to exponential nonlinearities on graphs; see, for example,
[
HWY21
,
LZ21
,
HS22
,
HHW23
,
LY22
,
CH23
,
GH23
,
HK24
,
LSY24
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the existence of solutions of nonlinear equations on the 2-dimensional lattice graph
\mathbb{Z}^{2},
which consists of the set of vertices
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\big\{x=(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}
and the set of edges
E=\{\{x,y\}:x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},|x-y|=1\}.
Lattice graphs are commonly used in statistical physics and numerical analysis, and they provide a natural framework for studying problems that arise in the theory of partial differential equations in the discrete setting. For simplicity, we write
x\sim y
if
\{x,y\}\in E.
The discrete Laplacian is defined as, for any
u:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R},

\Delta u(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:y\sim x}\big(u(y)-u(x)\big),\ x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

In this paper, we consider Kazdan–Warner type equations on
\mathbb{Z}^{2},

-\Delta u=\varepsilon e^{\kappa u}+\beta\delta_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{Z}^{2},

(1.5)

where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R},\,\kappa>0
and
\delta_{0}
is the indicator function (the delta mass) at the zero. It is called the
(discrete) Liouville equation
for
\epsilon=1
and
\beta=0.

For general
\beta\neq 0,
the solution
u
stands as an analogue of Green’s function to the nonlinear equation.

When
\varepsilon=1
, Eq.(
1.5
) also reduces to a form closely related to the mean field equation, which originates from both geometry and statistical physics. This equation serves as a natural counterpart to Eq.(
1.1
) and arises naturally in the prescribed curvature problem in differential geometry, as well as in statistical mechanics—particularly as the mean field limit of the Euler flow in fluid dynamics
[
CLMP92
]
.
The equation takes the form

-\Delta u=e^{u}+\beta\delta_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{in }{\mathcal{M}},

(1.6)

where the Dirac mass
\delta_{0}
is typically interpreted as a source or singularity in physical models, and
\beta
is a parameter that determines the strength of this singularity. This equation has been extensively studied in various settings, particularly in the two-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and on two-dimensional flat tori
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
Important results have been established concerning the existence of solutions and their qualitative properties, with significant implications for both theoretical physics and the analysis of nonlinear partial differential equations. For example, in
[
CL03
,
CLW04
]
, it was shown that Eq.(
1.6
) always admits solutions whenever
\beta=8m\pi
for any integer
m
. Moreover,
[
LW10
]
proves that for
\beta=8m\pi
, Eq.(
1.6
) admits solutions if and only if the Green function on the two-dimensional flat torus possesses critical points other than the three half-period points. For related studies on the two-dimensional flat tori, we refer the reader to
[
McO79
,
LY13
]
.
These results are essential for understanding the structure of solutions to mean field equations and their interplay with the geometric properties of the underlying space. Furthermore, important applications of the Kazdan-Warner equation appear in Liouville theory (2D quantum gravity) and in the study of mean field equations in statistical mechanics.

For the Liouville equation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, Ge, Hua, and Jiang
[
GHJ18
]
proved that there exists a universal constant
c>0
such that every solution
u
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{u}\geq c,

where the integral is with respect to the counting measure, same as the summation. However, we don’t know the existence of finite-energy solutions of the Liouville equation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}.
Note that on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
the existence of finite-energy solutions follows directly from their explicit representation, which are radially symmetric with respect to some point, while no such explicit construction is expected in the discrete setting. Moreover, the existence results obtained in
[
GJ18
,
KS18
]
rely on strong assumptions concerning either the infinite graph or the known function, which are not applicable to the Liouville equation on the lattice. Hence, establishing the existence of finite-energy solutions to the Liouville equation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
constitutes one of fundamental open problems in this area.

Problem 1.1

(
[
GHJ18
, Problem 2]
)
.

Is there any solution to the Liouville equation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with finite energy, i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{u}<\infty.

When
\varepsilon=1
, the nonlinear term
e^{\kappa u}
is a source, the equation (
1.5
) becomes

-\Delta u=e^{\kappa u}+\beta\delta_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt\ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{Z}^{2}.

(1.7)

We prove the existence of finite-energy solutions to the above equation for small parameter
\kappa.
In the literature, a solution
u
is called a
non-topological
solution if
\lim_{x\to\infty}u(x)=-\infty.
Note that finite-energy solutions are always non-topological solutions in the discrete case.

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)

There are universal constants
\kappa^{*}>0
and
a_{0}>2
such that for any
\kappa\in(0,\kappa^{*}],
and any
\beta\in[0,\frac{2\pi a_{0}}{\kappa}),
there exists a solution
u
to the equation (
1.7
) satisfying

\displaystyle u(x)=-\frac{a_{0}}{\kappa}\ln|x|+d_{\kappa,\beta}+O\big(|x|^{\frac{2-a_{0}}{a_{0}+1}}\big(\ln|x|\big)^{\frac{1}{a_{0}+1}}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm as}\ \,x\to\infty,

(1.8)

where
d_{\kappa,\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
depends on
\kappa,\beta
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{\kappa u}=\frac{2\pi a_{0}}{\kappa}-\beta.

(1.9)

(ii)

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
\overline{\kappa}=\overline{\kappa}(\epsilon)>\kappa^{*}
such that for any
\kappa\in(0,\overline{\kappa}],
any
\alpha\in\big[\frac{2\pi(2+\epsilon)}{\kappa},\frac{2\pi(2+\epsilon^{-1})}{\kappa}\big]
and any
\beta\in[0,\alpha),

there exists a solution
u_{\kappa,\alpha,\beta}
to the equation (
1.7
) satisfying

\displaystyle u_{\kappa,\alpha,\beta}(x)=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\ln|x|+d_{\kappa,\alpha,\beta}+O\big(|x|^{\frac{4\pi-\alpha\kappa}{\alpha\kappa+2\pi}}\big(\ln|x|\big)^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha\kappa+2\pi}}\big)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm as}\ \,x\to\infty,

(1.10)

where
d_{\kappa,\alpha,\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
depends on
\kappa,\alpha,\beta
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{\kappa u_{\kappa,\alpha,\beta}}=\alpha-\beta.

(1.11)

Since no radial solutions are expected on
\mathbb{Z}^{2},
we prove the results by using Schauder’s fixed point theorem. We first prepare proper Banach spaces,
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
for
\sigma>0,
weighted with respect to the
\sigma
-th power of the distance function; see (
2.2
). The key ingredient is the compact embedding of
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\hookrightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
for
\sigma_{1}>\sigma_{2};
see Lemma
2.1
. Using the precise asymptotics of the discrete Green’s function
\Phi_{0}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by Kenyon
[
Ken02
]
, we prove a crucial estimate for
\Phi_{0}*f
for
f\in\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
with
\sigma>2;
see Proposition
2.1
. Then we apply the arguments of fixed point theorem to conclude the existence of finite-energy solutions by the smallness of
\kappa
. Note that the smallness of the parameter
\kappa
plays an important role in the proof, which cannot be rescaled to the standard case
\kappa=2
by the lack of scaling in the discrete structure of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Consider the special case of the Liouville equation, i.e.
\beta=0
,

-\Delta u=e^{\kappa u}\hskip 9.24994pt\ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{Z}^{2}.

(1.12)

We prove the existence of finite-energy solutions in the following corollary, providing a partial answer to Problem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.1
.

For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
\overline{\kappa}=\overline{\kappa}(\epsilon)
such that for any
\kappa\in(0,\overline{\kappa}]
and any
\alpha\in[\frac{2\pi(2+\epsilon)}{\kappa},\frac{2\pi(2+\epsilon^{-1})}{\kappa}],

there exists a solution
u_{\kappa,\alpha}
to the Liouville equation (
1.12
) satisfying

\displaystyle u_{\kappa,\alpha}(x)=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\ln|x|+d_{\kappa,\alpha}+O(|x|^{\frac{4\pi-\alpha\kappa}{\alpha\kappa+2\pi}}\big(\ln|x|\big)^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha\kappa+2\pi}})\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm as}\ \,x\to\infty,

(1.13)

where
d_{\kappa,\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
depends on
\kappa,\alpha
such that
\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{\kappa u_{\kappa,\alpha}}=\alpha<\infty.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

This provides the existence result to the Liouville equation for
\kappa
in a definite range. For general
\kappa,
Problem
1.1
is still open due to the lack of the scaling.

(2)

For the Liouville equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2},
by (
1.4
) it is known that for any fixed
\kappa>0,
any finite-energy solution satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{\kappa u}=\frac{8\pi}{\kappa}
. In contrast, our result shows that on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
there exists a continuous family of finite-energy solutions whose total energy ranges over
[\frac{2\pi(2+\epsilon)}{\kappa},\frac{2\pi(2+\epsilon^{-1})}{\kappa}]
for
\epsilon\in(0,1).
Moreover, while any finite-energy solution in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
has the asymptotic behavior

u(x)\sim-\frac{2}{\kappa}\ln|x|,\hskip 9.24994ptx\to\infty,

our solutions on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
exhibit the asymptotics

u(x)\sim-a\ln|x|,\hskip 9.24994ptx\to\infty,\ \mathrm{for}\ a\in\left[\frac{2+\epsilon}{\kappa},\frac{2+\epsilon^{-1}}{\kappa}\right].

These phenomena could be caused by the geometry of the lattice, e.g., Green’s function of the Laplacian in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is non-positive, which is a new feature. This provides rich results, exploring how the discrete nature of the graph interacts with the nonlinearities in the equation.

When
\varepsilon=-1,
it is known that the nonlinear term
e^{\kappa u}
is an absorption, and equation (
1.5
) turns to be

-\Delta u+e^{\kappa u}=\beta\delta_{0}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{Z}^{2}.

(1.14)

In this case, the solution to the equation satisfies the maximum principle. We adopt the framework of the fixed point theorem to prove the existence of finite-energy solutions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\kappa>0
,
\alpha_{0}=\frac{4\pi}{\kappa}
and
\beta>\alpha_{0}
.

(i)
For any
\alpha\in\big(\alpha_{0},\beta\big)
problem (
1.14
)
has a solution
{\bf u}_{\alpha}
satisfying

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\alpha}(x)=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\ln|x|+{\bf d}_{\kappa,\alpha,\beta}+O(|x|^{\frac{4\pi-\alpha\kappa}{\alpha\kappa+2\pi}}\big(\ln|x|\big)^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha\kappa+2\pi}})\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm as}\ \,x\to\infty,

(1.15)

where
{\bf d}_{\kappa,\alpha,\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
depends on
\alpha,\beta
such that

{\bf d}_{\kappa,\alpha,\beta}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln(\beta-\alpha)-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}\alpha,

for
\gamma_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi}(\gamma_{E}+\frac{1}{2}\ln 2)
with the Euler constant
\gamma_{E}.

(ii)
Problem (
1.14
)
has a solution
{\bf u}_{\alpha_{0}}
satisfying

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\alpha_{0}}(x)=-\frac{2}{\kappa}\ln|x|-\frac{2}{\kappa}\ln\ln|x|+O(1)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm as}\ \,x\to\infty.

(1.16)

(iii)
the mapping
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},\beta)\mapsto{\bf u}_{\alpha}
is strictly decreasing, continuous locally in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, i.e.

{\bf u}_{\alpha_{1}}\geq{\bf u}_{\alpha_{2}}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{Z}^{2}\hskip 18.49988pt{\rm for}\ \ \alpha_{0}\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}<\beta,

{\bf u}_{\tilde{\alpha}}=\lim_{\alpha\to\tilde{\alpha}}{\bf u}_{\alpha}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{Z}^{2}\hskip 18.49988pt{\rm for}\ \ \tilde{\alpha}\in(\alpha_{0},\beta),

{\bf u}_{\alpha_{0}}=\lim_{\alpha\to\alpha_{0}^{+}}{\bf u}_{\alpha}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{Z}^{2}

and for
\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},\beta)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{\kappa{\bf u}_{\alpha}}dx=\beta-\alpha.

(1.17)

We remark that the constant
{\bf d}_{\alpha,\beta}\to-\infty
as
\alpha\to\beta^{-}
. On the other hand,
{\bf d}_{\alpha,\beta}
remains bounded, and the decay rate of
|x|^{\frac{4\pi-\alpha\kappa}{\alpha\kappa+2\pi}}\big(\ln|x|\big)^{\frac{2\pi}{\alpha\kappa+2\pi}}
vanishes for
\alpha\to\alpha_{0}^{+}
. Inspired by the continuous model, when
\alpha=\alpha_{0}
the solution
u_{\alpha_{0}}
is called
extremal solutions
. Note that in this critical case, by (
1.16
), the solution exhibits a double logarithmic asymptotic behavior, which also appears in the continuous setting. Here, we obtain
a layer structure
of the set of solutions
\{{\bf u}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in[\alpha_{0},\beta)}
.

The structure of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we investigate the fundamental properties, including the comparison principle, embedding results, and related estimates. In Section 3, we establish the existence of solutions in the source case using fixed-point theory. Finally, Section 4 focuses on the absorption case, where we employ the Leray-Schauder theorem to prove the existence of finite-energy solutions and utilize the method of super and subsolutions to obtain the extremal solution.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Comparison principle and Embedding results

Let
G=(V,E)
be a (possibly infinite) simple, locally finite, and undirected graph. Two vertices
x,y
are called neighbours, denoted by
x\sim y,
if there is an edge connecting
x
and
y.
A subset
\Omega\subset V
is called connected if for any
x,y\in\Omega,
there is a path
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\subset\Omega
from
x
to
y,
i.e.
x_{i}\sim x_{i+1}
for any
0\leq i\leq n-1
and
x_{0}=x,x_{n}=y.
The combinatorial distance between two vertices
x
and
y,

d(x,y),
is the length of the shortest path connecting
x
and
y
. We usually write

x\to\infty
if
d(x,x_{0})\to\infty
for a fixed vertex
x_{0}.

For
\Omega\subset V,
we denote by
\delta\Omega:=\{y\in V\setminus\Omega:\exists x\in\Omega\ \mathrm{s.t.}\ y\sim x\}
the boundary of
\Omega.
We write
\overline{\Omega}:=\Omega\cup\delta\Omega.
Moreover, we use the following notations:
|x-\bar{x}|_{Q}=|x_{1}-\bar{x}_{1}|+|x_{2}-\bar{x}_{2}|
,
|x-\bar{x}|:=\sqrt{(x_{1}-\bar{x}_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-\bar{x}_{2})^{2}}

Q_{r}(x_{0})=\big\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\,|x-x_{0}|_{{}_{Q}}\leq r\big\},\hskip 18.49988ptB_{r}(x_{0})=\big\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\,|x-x_{0}|\leq r\big\}.

For any
\Omega\subset V
and any
u:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R},
the Laplacian of
u
is defined as

\Delta u(x):=\sum_{y\sim x}\big(u(y)-u(x)\big)\hskip 9.24994pt\ \text{ for all }x\in\Omega.

The following maximum principle is well known in the continuous setting, for which we give the proof in the discrete setting.

Theorem 2.1
.

For a graph
G=(V,E)
and a (possibly infinite) connected subset
\Omega\subset V
verifying
either
\delta\Omega\not=\emptyset
or
\Omega
is unbounded,
if
u:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+cu\geq 0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
\phantom{----}u\geq 0&{\rm in}\ \ \,\delta\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
\liminf\limits_{d(x,0)\to\infty,x\in\Omega}u(x)\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

where
c:\Omega\to[0,\infty),

then
u\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Furthermore,
u\equiv 0
in
\Omega
or
u>0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we prove the result for an infinite subset
\Omega.
Since
\Omega
is connected, so is
\overline{\Omega}.
Suppose that the first assertion is not true, i.e. there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
u(x_{0})<0.
Since
\liminf\limits_{x\to\infty,x\in\Omega}u(x)\geq 0
and
u|_{\delta\Omega}\geq 0,
then
\inf_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}u<0
and

A:=\{x\in\overline{\Omega}:u(x)=\inf_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}u\}\neq\emptyset,\hskip 9.24994ptA\subset\Omega.

For any
x\in A,
we claim that
y\in A
for any
y\sim x,y\in\overline{\Omega}.
By the equation,

\Delta u(x)\leq c(x)u(x)\leq 0.

Since
u(x)=\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}u,
this proves the claim. By the connectedness of
\overline{\Omega},

A=\overline{\Omega},
which contradicts
A\subset\Omega.
This proves the first assertion.

For the second assertion, if there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that
u(x_{0})=0,
then by the same argument above, one can show that
u\equiv 0
on
\overline{\Omega}
. This proves the result.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

When
V
is finite, connected and
\Omega=V
, the comparison principle holds when the boundary conditions are replaced by
u(x_{0})\geq 0
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
.

Given
\Omega\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, without any confusion, we use the notation

\int_{\Omega}f(x)dx=\sum_{x\in\Omega}f(x)\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm for}\ \ f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}.

For the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2},
we denote by
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
the space of bounded functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{\infty}},
and for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R},
we define the weighted
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma}

\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}):=\big\{f:\mathbb{Z}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}:\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma}}<\infty\big\},

(2.2)

where

\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big(|f(x)|(1+|x|)^{\sigma}\big)

is the weighted
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma}
norm. Note that
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\ell^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}).

For
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},
we write
|x|_{p}:=(|x_{1}|^{p}+|x_{2}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}
for
p\in[1,\infty)
and
|x|_{\infty}:=\max\{|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\}.
We always write
|x|=|x|_{2}.

We denote by
B_{r}(x):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|\leq r\}
the closed disc of radius
r
centered at
x,
and by
Q_{r}(x):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|_{\infty}\leq\frac{r}{2}\}
the square of side length
r
centered at
x.
For
x=0,
we write
B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
for simplicity.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\sigma_{1}>\sigma_{2},

\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\subset\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}),\hskip 9.24994pt\mathrm{and}

\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{2}}}\leq\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall\ u\in\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}).

Moreover, the embedding
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\hookrightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
is compact.

Proof.
The first assertion is obvious from the definition of space
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
. We prove the second assertion, i.e. the compactness of the embedding
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\hookrightarrow\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
. Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
such that
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}}\leq 1.

Note that for any
r\geq 1,

\sup_{n}\max_{B_{r}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|\varphi_{n}|\leq C(r,\sigma_{1})<\infty.

Hence, up to a subsequence, still denoted by
\varphi_{n},
there is a function
\varphi
such that
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
pointwise on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as
n\to\infty.
One easily sees that

\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}}\leq 1.

We need to prove that
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
in
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
as
n\to\infty.
This will prove the compactness.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
N_{0}\gg 1
such that for
|x|>N_{0},

\displaystyle\Big(|\varphi_{n}(x)|+|\varphi(x)|\Big)(1+|x|)^{\sigma_{2}}

\displaystyle=\Big(|\varphi_{n}(x)|+|\varphi(x)|\Big)(1+|x|)^{\sigma_{1}}(1+|x|)^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}}+\|\varphi\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{1}}}\Big)(1+N_{0})^{\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\epsilon.

Moreover, for sufficiently large

\displaystyle|\varphi_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)|

\displaystyle\leq(1+N_{0})^{-\sigma_{2}}\epsilon,\ \text{$\forall\ x\in B_{N_{0}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}$}.

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Big(|\varphi_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)|(1+|x|)^{\sigma_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(1+N_{0})^{\sigma_{2}}\sup_{x\in B_{N_{0}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Big(|\varphi_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)|\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus B_{N_{0}}}\Big(\big(|\varphi_{n}(x)|+|\varphi(x)|\big)(1+|x|)^{\sigma_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 3\epsilon.

This proves the convergence in
\ell^{\infty}_{\sigma_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}),
and yields the result.
\Box

{\bf u}_{0}

To this end, we denote

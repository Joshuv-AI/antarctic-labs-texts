Abstract
We consider a communication scenario over a discrete memoryless interference channel or multiple access channel without feedback, where transmitters exploit classical, quantum, or no-signaling cooperation. In this scenario, several previous works have shown that the sum capacities of channels involving pseudo-telepathy games can be enhanced by quantum or no-signaling cooperation. However, a full characterization of which channels admit such an improvement remains open. By focusing on the common characteristics of previously studied channels, we propose a broader class of channels for which quantum or no-signaling cooperation increases the sum capacity. Channels in this class are associated with a pseudo-telepathy game, with channel inputs specified as tuples of questions and answers from the game. In addition, when the channel inputs satisfy the winning condition of the game, the channel decomposes into parallel weakly symmetric sub-channels and is less noisy compared to the case when the inputs do not meet the winning condition.

The aforementioned results were derived by adapting the characteristics of pseudo-telepathy games to the context of network communication.
Specifically, a pseudo-telepathy game is a non-local game whose winning probability is strictly less than one with classical cooperation but equal to one with quantum or no-signaling cooperation.

These games exhibit a hierarchy among three types of cooperation: quantum cooperation produces correlations unattainable by classical cooperation, and no-signaling cooperation generates correlations unattainable by quantum cooperation.
Interpreted in the context of communication, quantum or no-signaling cooperation between transmitters can generate correlated channel inputs that are not possible through classical cooperation.
Consequently, pseudo-telepathy games give rise to network channels in which interference between channel inputs can be mitigated by quantum or no-signaling cooperation, but not by classical cooperation.

II
System model & Preliminaries

In this work, we propose a class of ICs and MACs whose sum capacities can be enhanced through quantum or no-signaling cooperation between transmitters.
Since all results for ICs can be directly adapted to those for MACs by treating multiple receivers as a single receiver (see Remark
1
for details), we primarily focus on ICs throughout this paper.
To illustrate our results, we begin with a review of the conventional communication scenario over an IC, where all transmitters and receivers do not cooperate.
Then, we describe an extended scenario that allows cooperation between transmitters, which is the focus of this work.
Finally, we introduce non-local games, which serve as the key component underlying the proposed class of channels.

II-A

Communication over IC without transmitter cooperation

The conventional communication scenario between
K
transmitter-receiver pairs,
K\geq 2
, over a stationary discrete memoryless interference channel (DM-IC) without feedback has been extensively studied
[
24
]
.
A DM-IC is characterized as a conditional probability
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
from
\mathcal{X}_{[K]}
to
\mathcal{Y}_{[K]}
, where
\mathcal{X}_{i}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
are finite sets denoting an input alphabet at the
i
-th transmitter side and an output alphabet at the
i
-th receiver side, respectively.
This channel is stationary and memoryless in the sense that
n
-times use of the channel is given by
P_{Y_{[K]}^{n}|X_{[K]}^{n}}=P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}^{\otimes n}
, i.e.,

P_{Y_{[K]}^{n}|X_{[K]}^{n}}(y^{n}_{[K]}|x_{[K]}^{n})=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}P_{Y_{[K],j}|X_{[K],j}}(y_{[K],j}|x_{[K],j}).

(1)

For each
i\in[K]
, the
i
-th transmitter has its own message
M_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{i}
and transmits a codeword
X^{n}_{i}
corresponding to
M_{i}
over the channel in
n
time instances
P_{Y_{[K]}^{n}|X_{[K]}^{n}}
.
Then, each of the
i
-th receiver decodes the noisy channel output
Y_{i}^{n}
into
\hat{M}_{i}
to estimate the message
M_{i}
.

In this case, general communication strategy over a DM-IC
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
that transmitters and receivers can use is characterized by a
(n,R_{[K]})
-code, which is defined as a collection of

1.

Message sets
\mathcal{M}_{i}:=\left[2^{\lceil nR_{i}\rceil}\right]
for all
i\in[n]
,

2.

Encoding functions (encoders)
f_{i}:\mathcal{M}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{i}^{n}
for all
i\in[n]
,

3.

Decoding functions (decoders)
g_{i}:\mathcal{Y}_{i}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{i}\cup\{e\}
for all
i\in[n]
, where
e
denotes an error symbol.

Note that for a given
(n,R_{[K]})
-code, we have
X^{n}_{i}=f_{i}(M_{i})
and
\hat{M}_{i}=g_{i}(Y^{n}_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
.
We say that a rate tuple
R_{[K]}
is achievable (for
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
) if there exists a sequence of
(n,R_{[K]})
-codes such that the average error probability converges to zero, i.e.,

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathrm{Pr}(M_{[K]}\neq\hat{M}_{[K]})=0.

(2)

The capacity region
\mathcal{C}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}})
of a DM-IC
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
is defined as the closure of the set of all achievable rate tuples, and the sum capacity
\mathcal{C}_{s}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}})
is defined as

\mathcal{C}_{s}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}})\\
:=\max\left\{\sum_{i\in[K]}R_{i}:R_{[K]}\in\mathcal{C}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}})\right\}.

(3)

To date, a single-letter characterization of the capacity region and the sum capacity of a DM-IC is not known in general, even for
K=2
.
The best-known inner bound of the capacity region of a general DM-IC is the Han-Kobayashi inner bound
[
25
,
26
]
, and this inner bound is tight for some class of DM-ICs.

II-B

Communication over IC with transmitter cooperation

Now, we consider a scenario where transmitters cooperate using some pre-shared resources, which has been studied in

With such cooperation, the transmitters can transmit correlated codewords, and the correlation between them can be useful for mitigating interference, and hence, enlarges the capacity region.

Cooperation between the transmitters is characterized by a conditional probability that can be classified based on the type of pre-shared resource: classical, quantum, or super-quantum resource

]

A conditional probability

P_{A_{[K]}|Q_{[K]}}(a_{[K]}|q_{[K]})\\
=\sum\limits_{v\in\mathcal{V}}P_{V}(v)\prod\limits_{i=1}^{K}P_{A_{i}|Q_{i},V}(a_{i}|q_{i},v).

(4)

2.

a quantum cooperation if there exist a quantum state
\rho

for all

P_{A_{[K]}|Q_{[K]}}(a_{[K]}|q_{[K]})=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\left(\otimes_{i=1}^{K}\Pi^{i}_{a_{i}|q_{i}}\right)\rho\right).

(5)

3.

a no-signaling cooperation if for all
i\in[K]
,

P_{A_{i}|Q_{[K]}}(a_{i}|q_{[K]})\\
=P_{A_{i}|Q_{i}}(a_{i}|q_{i}).

(6)

Simply, for all

(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{i-1},Q_{i+1},\ldots,Q_{K})

Note that a quantum cooperation is not a classical cooperation only if a quantum state

]

(7)

In the communication scenario allowing cooperation between transmitters, a general communication strategy that transmitters and receivers can use is characterized by an
(n,R_{[K]},r)
-code.
The definition of a

-code is the same as that of a

In the case

III-A

DM-ICs from non-local games

Inspired by
K
-party games and the previous works
[
18
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
19
]
, we propose a class of DM-ICs for which quantum or no-signaling cooperation can achieve strictly greater sum capacity than classical cooperation.
The basic idea is to design a DM-IC
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
that is less noisy when channel inputs
x_{[K]}
satisfy the winning condition of a game
G
than when they do not satisfy.
Specifically, the conditional entropy
H(Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}=x_{[K]})
is smaller when
x_{[K]}\in\mathcal{W}
than when
x_{[K]}\notin\mathcal{W}
.
Further, we set channel input
x_{i}
to a pair of question and answer
(q_{i},a_{i})
for
G
, and design
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
such that it decomposes into parallel sub-channels, i.e.,
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}=\prod_{i\in[K]}P_{Y_{i}|Q_{i}}
, whenever
x_{[K]}\in\mathcal{W}
.
Here,
a_{[K]}
is discarded if the winning condition is satisfied.
By requiring each sub-channel
P_{Y_{i}|Q_{i}}
to be weakly symmetric
[
27
, Sec. 7.2]
, we can show the enhancements of sum capacity for some of these channels.
The details are as follows.

Definition 2

A conditional probability

is weakly symmetric if

1.

\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}P_{Y|Q}(y|q)
is constant for all

,

2.

For any

, there exists a permutation
\sigma

\mathcal{Y}
such that
P_{Y|Q}(\cdot|q^{\prime})=P_{Y|Q}(\sigma(\cdot)|q)

Lemma 1

If
P_{Y|Q}
is weakly symmetric, then the point-to-point channel capacity of
P_{Y|Q}
is given by

\mathcal{C}(P_{Y|Q}):=\max\limits_{P_{Q}}I(Q;Y)=\log|\mathcal{Y}|-H(Y|Q=q),

(22)

for all
q\in\mathcal{Q}
.
Moreover, the maximum is achieved when
P_{Q}
is the uniform distribution on
\mathcal{Q}
, and
P_{Y}
becomes the uniform distribution on
\mathcal{Y}
when
P_{Q}
is the uniform distribution on
\mathcal{Q}
.

For a given
K

\mathcal{X}_{i}=\mathcal{Q}_{i}\times\mathcal{A}_{i}
,
\mathcal{Y}_{[K]}

1.

For all

If

=\prod\limits_{i\in[K]}P^{G}_{Y_{i}|Q_{i}}(y_{i}|q_{i}),

For a given

III-B

Enhancing sum capacity

We show that quantum or no-signaling cooperation achieves higher sum capacity than classical cooperation for a
G
-game channel, when
G
is a
\mathrm{Q}
- or
\mathrm{NS}
-pseudo-telepathy game.

Theorem 2

Let
G
be a
K
-party game and
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}^{G}
be a
G
-game channel.
For
r\in\{\mathrm{Q},\mathrm{NS}\}
, if
G
is an
r
-pseudo-telepathy game, then

\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{C}}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}^{G})<\mathcal{C}_{s}^{r}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}^{G}).

(30)

As introduced in Section
II-C
, there are
Q
-pseudo-telepathy
K
-party games for all
K\geq 2
(the magic square game for
K=2
, and the
K
-party parity game for
K\geq 3
).
Thus, Theorem
2
implies that, for all
K\geq 2
, there is a DM-IC
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
which shows an advantage of quantum cooperation over classical cooperation.

In the remainder of this subsection, we prove Theorem
2
.
We begin by deriving an upper bound on
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{r}
of a
G
-game channel (the converse part).
This upper bound is strict if
r=\mathrm{C}
and
G
is a pseudo-telepathy game.
Next, we prove that this upper bound is
r
-achievable when
G
is an
r
-pseudo-telepathy game by exploiting the cooperation corresponding to a winning strategy for
G
(the achievability part).
Combining the converse and achievability proofs, we establish Theorem
2
.

III-B
1
Converse

We propose an upper bound on the
r
-sum capacity of a game channel as follows.

Proposition 3

For all
G
-game channels
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}^{r}
and
r\in\{\mathrm{C,Q,NS}\}
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{s}^{r}\left(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}^{G}\right)\leq\sum\limits_{i\in[K]}(\log|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|-h_{i}^{w}).

(31)

Moreover, the above inequality is strict when
r=\mathrm{C}
and
G
is a pseudo-telepathy game.

The proof of the above proposition utilizes the fact that classical cooperation does not enlarge the capacity region.

Lemma 4

For all DM-ICs
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
, we have that
\mathcal{C}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}})=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{C}}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}})
.

Proof:

Let
P_{X_{[K]}^{n}|M_{[K]}}\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{C}}
be a cooperative encoder that is utilized in a
(n,R_{[K]},\mathrm{C})
-code.
Since the average error probability
\mathrm{Pr}(M_{[K]}\neq\hat{M}_{[K]})
is linear in
P_{X_{[K]}^{n}|M_{[K]}}
, one of the extreme points of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{C}}
achieves the minimum error probability.
An extreme point of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{C}}
is equivalent to the collection of deterministic encoding functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{K}
that can be performed by the transmitters without cooperation.
Hence, there exists an
(n,R_{[K]})
-code whose average error probability is not greater than that of the given
(n,R_{[K]},\mathrm{C})
-code.
∎

Now, we prove Proposition
3
.

Proof:

For any sequence of
(n,R_{[K]},r)
-codes or
(n,R_{[K]})
-codes that achieves
R_{[K]}
, we have

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in[K]}R_{i}=H(M_{[K]})

(32)

\displaystyle\leq I(M_{[K]};Y_{[K]}^{n})+n\epsilon_{n}

(33)

\displaystyle\leq I(X_{[K]}^{n};Y_{[K]}^{n})+n\epsilon_{n}

(34)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j\in[n]}I(X_{[K],j};Y_{[K],j})+n\epsilon_{n}

(35)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j\in[n]}(H(Y_{[K],j})-H(Y_{[K],j}|X_{[K],j}))+n\epsilon_{n}

(36)

\displaystyle\leq n\sum\limits_{i\in[K]}\log|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}H(Y_{[K],j}|X_{[K],j})+n\epsilon_{n},

(37)

where (

) follows from Fano’s inequality with
\epsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Also, the data-processing inequality implies (
34
), and (
35
) holds since the channel is memoryless.
The conditional entropy terms in (

\displaystyle H(Y_{[K],j}|X_{[K],j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x_{[K],j}\in\mathcal{W}}P_{X_{[K],j}}(x_{[K],j})H(Y_{[K],j}|X_{[K],j}=x_{[K],j})

(38)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{x_{[K],j}\not\in\mathcal{W}}P_{X_{[K],j}}(x_{[K],j})H(Y_{[K],j}|X_{[K],j}=x_{[K],j})

\displaystyle\geq P_{X_{[K],j}}(\mathcal{W})\sum\limits_{i\in[K]}h_{i}^{w}+(1-P_{X_{[K],j}}(\mathcal{W}))\sum\limits_{i\in[K]}h_{i}^{w}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in[K]}h_{i}^{w},

(40)

where (

Thus, we have (

Now, suppose

By Lemma

Achievability

We show that the upper bound on the

is achievable if a game

The main idea of the proof is to design an
(n,R_{[K]},r)
-code that incorporates cooperative encoding based on a winning strategy of a game

Using this code,
P^{G}_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
splits into parallel channels, i.e.,
P^{G}_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}=\prod_{i\in[K]}P^{G}_{Y_{i}|Q_{i}}

Proposition 5

If

\mathcal{C}_{s}^{r}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}^{G})=\sum\limits_{i\in[K]}(\log|\mathcal{Y}_{i}|-h_{i}^{w}).

Proof:

We consider a

-code depicted in Fig.

Each of the transmitters first encodes their message with local encoding functions

Then, for the

III-C

Numerical evaluation of enhancements

Although Theorem
2
suffices to show the enhancements of sum capacity (i.e., a gap is strictly positive) of a pseudo-telepathy game channel, it does not show how large the gap can be.
In analyzing the gap, it suffices to evaluate
\mathcal{C}_{s}
since
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{C}}=\mathcal{C}_{s}
by Lemma
4
, and Proposition
5
provides the closed-form expression of
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{Q}}
or
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{NS}}
.

Nonetheless, computing
\mathcal{C}_{s}
remains challenging because no single-letter characterization of the sum capacity for a general DM-IC is known.
Therefore, we consider the upper bound
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
on
\mathcal{C}_{s}
, where

\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}(P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}):=\max\limits_{P_{X_{1}}P_{X_{2}}\cdots P_{X_{K}}}I(X_{[K]};Y_{[K]}).

(42)

Note that
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
is the sum capacity of a DM-MAC
P_{\tilde{Y}|X_{[K]}}
with
\tilde{Y}=Y_{[K]}

[
28
,
29
]
, and hence it provides an upper bound on the sum capacity of a DM-IC
P_{Y_{[K]}|X_{[K]}}
.
Furthermore,
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
can be numerically approximated using the generalized Blahut-Arimoto algorithm
[
23
]
.
This algorithm is iterative, and its output converges from below to
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
as the number of iterations increases, independent of the initial point.
Hence, we can numerically compute the lower bound
\mathcal{C}^{r}_{s}-\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
on the gap
\mathcal{C}^{r}_{s}-\mathcal{C}_{s}
.

We apply this approach to the following examples.
We consider the game channels of the form that described in Example
1
with parameters
\eta_{w}=1-\eta
and
\eta_{l}=\eta
, for
\eta\in[0,1/2]
.
The game under consideration are the CHSH,
3
-PP, and
4
-PP games introduced in Section
II-C
.
In Fig.
2
, we plot the values of
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{Q}}
or
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{NS}}
, and
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
for the those channels.
Here, we compute
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{Q}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{s}^{\mathrm{NS}}
based on the closed-form expression in Proposition
5
, and
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
using the generalized Blahut-Arimoto algorithm.
The algorithm is run from
50
randomly chosen initial points, and the maximum result is taken to reduce the effect of numerical errors.
The code for this simulation is provided in the Supplementary Material.

For all the game channels used for the simulation, the lower bounds
\mathcal{C}^{r}_{s}-\overline{\mathcal{C}_{s}}
on the gaps decrease as

become more similar to those for

IV
Conclusion

We proposed a class of DM-ICs and DM-MACs that involve non-local games: the channel decomposes into parallel weakly symmetric sub-channels and becomes less noisy when the channel inputs satisfy the winning condition of the game.
In cases where the underlying non-local game is a quantum or no-signaling pseudo-telepathy game, quantum or no-signaling cooperation between the transmitters enhances the sum capacity, respectively.

This result may provide a justification for the practical implementation of quantum-assisted communication systems.
However, a limitation is that it remains unclear whether the proposed class of channels includes channels relevant to current practical communication systems.
A promising direction for future work would be to determine whether quantum cooperation can enhance the sum capacity of practical channels, such as Gaussian ICs or MACs.
We conjecture that the sum capacity of a Gaussian channel can be enhanced when the structure of channel gains between transmitters and receivers resembles that of a quantum pseudo-telepathy game.

Another research direction would be to derive a single-letter characterization of the capacity region of general DM-ICs or DM-MACs with cooperation between transmitters.
In this context,
[
16
]
derived a multi-letter characterization of the capacity region of a DM-MAC, and
[
15
]
proposed multi-letter inner and outer bounds for the capacity region of a DM-IC.
Compared to single-letter characterizations, multi-letter characterizations have the limitation that it is not clear how to compute them
[
24
]
.
A single-letter characterization would help characterize the set of all channels in which cooperation enlarges the capacity region.

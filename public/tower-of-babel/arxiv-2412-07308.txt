Iwasawa Theory and Ranks of Elliptic Curves in Quadratic Twist Families
Jeffrey Hatley, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. We study the distribution of ranks of elliptic curves in quadratic twist families using Iwasawa-theoretic methods, contributing to the understanding of Goldfeld’s conjecture. Given an elliptic curve E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} with good ordinary reduction at 2 2 and λ \lambda -invariant λ 2 ​ ( E / ℚ ) = 0 \lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0 , we use Matsuno’s Kida-type formula to construct quadratic twists E ( d ) E^{(d)} such that λ 2 ​ ( E ( d ) / ℚ ) \lambda_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q}) remains unchanged or increases by 2 2 . When the root number of E ( d ) E^{(d)} is − 1 -1 and the Tate-Shafarevich group X ​ ( E ( d ) / ℚ ) ​ [ 2 ∞ ] \Sh(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}] is finite, this yields quadratic twists with Mordell–Weil rank 1 1 . These results support the conjectural expectation that, on average, half of the quadratic twists in a family have rank 0 0 and half have rank 1 1 . In the cases we consider we obtain asymptotic lower bounds for the number of twists by squarefree numbers d ≤ X d\leq X which match with the conjectured value up to an explicit power of log ⁡ X \log X . Our results also apply to twists by a given prime number and are effective. They complement recent groundbreaking results of Smith on Goldfeld’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

Given an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, the Mordell-Weil theorem assures that the group of rational points
E(\mathbb{Q})
is finitely generated, so that

E(\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbf{r}_{\operatorname{alg}}(E)}\oplus T

for some finite group
T
and some non-negative integer
\mathbf{r}_{\operatorname{alg}}(E)
, called the (algebraic) rank of
E
. The distribution of the ranks
\mathbf{r}_{\operatorname{alg}}(E)
as
E
varies over all elliptic curves has motivated an enormous amount of research over the past few decades, yet many of their basic properties are still not well understood. For instance, until very recently, a folklore conjecture held that the ranks of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
are unbounded; however, recent heuristics have challenged this belief
[
1
,
17
]
.

While the existence of an upper bound for ranks has become highly-debated, mathematicians still largely believe that, whether elliptic curves of arbitrarily large rank exist, they are extremely rare. More precisely, a conjecture originating with Katz and Sarnak
[
12
]
asserts that, asymptotically, half of all elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
have rank 0 and half have rank 1. It is thus reasonable to say that, conjecturally, the
average rank
of all elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
is
1/2
. Recent progress on this conjecture has been encouraging; for instance, Bhargava and Shankar
[
2
]
prove that the average rank is indeed bounded, and that it is bounded by at most
3/2
.

One may also study the average ranks of elliptic curves in restricted families. For instance, if
E:y^{2}=f(x)
is an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and if
d
is a squarefree integer, then the corresponding
quadratic twist

E^{(d)}:dy^{2}=f(x)
is also an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and there is an isomorphism
E_{/K}\simeq E^{(d)}_{/K}
upon base change to
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
. It is natural to study the variation of arithmetic invariants in quadratic twist families, and a conjecture due to Goldfeld
[
6
]
predicts that the average rank in quadratic twist families is again
1/2
. (See Section
2.3
for a precise statement of Goldfeld’s conjecture.)

As with the Katz-Sarnak conjecture, progress on Goldfeld’s conjecture has been modest. Many authors have instead studied the weaker conjecture that
some positive proportion
of elliptic curves in a quadratic twist family have rank 0, and a positive proportion have rank 1. In the notation of Section
2.3
, this is the assertion that

n_{E,r}^{*}(X)\gg X

for
r\in\{0,1\}.

When
r=0
, Ono and Skinner
[
21
]
prove that

n_{E,0}^{\ast}(X)\gg\frac{X}{\log X},

and when
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
, Ono
[
20
]
improves this to

n_{E,0}^{\ast}(X)\gg\frac{X}{\left(\log X\right)^{1-\alpha}}

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending on
E
. (See Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
for the precise statements.)

Kriz and Li
[
14
]
have established positive density results for both the rank
0
and rank
1
subsets in cases where
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
. However, their results hinge on certain technical conditions related to the properties of Heegner points that arise in
E(K)
(see Theorem
2.10
for a detailed statement). These conditions, while theoretically significant, pose challenges when attempting to determine their prevalence in practice.

For rank
0
quadratic twists of elliptic curves, Kriz and Nordentoft
[
15
]
have achieved unconditional positive density results by leveraging analytic techniques involving
p
-adic
L
-functions. Despite this progress, the extent to which their assumptions hold in general remains unclear in practical scenarios.

In addition to these approaches, there are several notable conditional results. For instance, Alexander Smith
[
27
]
has produced positive density results for elliptic curves with full rational
2
-torsion, contingent on the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Earlier, Heath-Brown
[
9
]
obtained related results under the assumption of the generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH). More recently, Smith’s work
[
28
]
has advanced our understanding of Goldfeld’s conjecture, contributing significant breakthroughs to this area. It is worth noting, however, that the methods used in these works differ substantially from ours. Our approach yields effective results that are qualitatively distinct and our methods are different.

In the present paper, we contribute to this body of research using a novel Iwasawa-theoretic approach. Kenkichi Iwasawa initiated the study of arithmetic objects over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of number fields, obtaining striking results on the uniform growth of ideal class groups along such towers; namely, that if
K
is a number field and

K=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\dots\subset K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}\subset\dots\subset K_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n}K_{n}

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension for some prime
p
, then there exist constants
c
,
\mu
and
\lambda
(depending only on
K_{\infty}/K)
such that the
p
-adic valuation of the class number of
K_{n}
is given by

\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+c

for
n\gg 0
. This perspective was generalized to the study of Selmer groups of elliptic curves by Mazur
[
19
]
. The details are recalled in Section
2.1
, but we sketch the main ideas here. Given a number field
K
and a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
K_{\infty}/K
as above, one obtains a Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
fitting into an exact sequence

(1.1)

0\rightarrow E(K_{\infty})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})\rightarrow\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Sh(E/K_{n})[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0.

Here
\Sh(E/K_{n})
is the Tate-Shafarevich group defined in (
2.1
). Moreover,
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\infty})
cofinitely generated as a
\Lambda:=\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)\rrbracket
-module. If it is furthermore
\Lambda
-cotorsion, then it has associated invariants
\mu_{p}(E/K)
and
\lambda_{p}(E/K)
which play a role analogous to the constants
\mu
and
\lambda
in Iwasawa’s results on class groups. In particular, the
\lambda
-invariant gives information on the growth of the rank along the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. In particular, there is a chain of inequalities

\mathbf{r}_{\operatorname{alg}}(E)\leq\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\leq\lambda_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q}).

In fact, if
\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
is finite, then the first inequality is actually an equality.

Our strategy is to study the variation of the
2
-adic invariants
\lambda_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})
in quadratic twist families via a Kida-type formula due to Matsuno
[
18
]
. In our context, it follows from results of Kato
[
11
]
and Rubin
[
23
]
that
\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
is cotorsion over
\Lambda
, cf. Lemma
3.1
for further details. Fix an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
2
for which
\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
. Matsuno’s formula (cf. Theorem
3.2
) allows us to construct squarefree integers
d
such that

\lambda_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q}),

from which it follows that
\mathbf{r}_{\operatorname{alg}}(E^{(d)})=0
. Similarly, we can construct squarefree integers
d
such that

\lambda_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})-\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=2;

further assumptions on the prime divisors of
d
then allow us to invoke known cases of the parity conjecture to deduce that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is odd. In particular, if
\Sh(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
is finite, then
\mathbf{r}_{\operatorname{alg}}(E^{(d)})=1.
We also give explicit density results on the sets of admissable squarefree integers
d
in these constructions.

Our results may be summarized as follows. We note that our results on
\lambda
-invariants and Selmer coranks are unconditional, and that they yield results on ranks (in the direction of Goldfeld’s conjecture) when the Shafarevich-Tate group is finite. For
X>0
, let
n_{E,k}^{\prime}(X)
denote the number of positive squarefree integers
d<X
such that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=k
. Denote by
\omega(E)
the sign of the functional equation of
E
.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.12
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

E
has good ordinary reduction at
2
with squarefree conductor
N_{E}
,

(2)

\omega(E)=-1
,

(3)

E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
,

(4)

\mu_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\leq 2
.

Then we find that

n_{E,1}^{\prime}(X)\gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^{\frac{11}{12}}}.

In fact, we are able to obtain an effective version of the above result, see Proposition
3.11
. This allows us to pin down squarefree products
d=\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{k}
in terms of Chebotarev conditions on the primes
\ell_{i}
. In order to demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach, one can also specialize to twist families where
\ell
is a prime number. We obtain the following result.

Theorem B

(Theorem
3.13
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve satisfying the conditions of Theorem
A
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of prime numbers
\ell
such that

•

\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,

•

\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[2]=0
(where
\widetilde{E}
denotes the reduced curve at
\ell
),

•

\ell
splits completely in
\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{-N_{E}})
.

Then
\mathcal{M}
has density
\geq\frac{1}{12}
and
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q})\right)=1
.

It is thus natural to wonder how often the conditions of Theorems
A
and
B
are satisfied. In order to make sense of this question, we note that any elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
has a unique globally minimal Weierstrass equation. That is,
E
is isomorphic to a unique curve
E_{A,B}
with minimal Weierstrass equation

E_{A,B}:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B,

where
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\gcd(A^{3},B^{2})
is not divisible by
d^{12}
for any
d>1
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of all such equations. The height of
E
is defined as
\operatorname{ht}(E):=\max\{|A|^{3},B^{2}\}
. For
X>0
, let
\mathcal{C}(X)
be the set of all
E_{A,B}
with
\operatorname{ht}(E_{A,B})\leq X
. Given a set of (isomorphism classes of) elliptic curves
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{C}
, say that
\mathcal{S}
has positive density if the limit

\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{E_{A,B}\in\mathcal{S}\mid\operatorname{ht}(E_{A,B})\leq X\}}{\#\mathcal{C}(X)}>0.

It is natural to expect that the conditions of Theorem
A
are satisfied for a positive density of elliptic curves. Indeed:

Condition (1):

depends purely on local properties at all primes. The density of elliptic curves for which
N_{E}
is squarefree is positive; see, for example,
[
3
]
.

Condition (2):

pertains to the rank distribution of elliptic curves. By the rank distribution conjecture of Katz and Sarnak, it is expected that half of all elliptic curves satisfy this condition.

Condition (3):

holds for almost all elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
, as demonstrated by a result of Duke
[
5
]
.

Condition (4):

is more subtle and involves analyzing the truncated Euler characteristic of the Selmer group. Specifically, if this Euler characteristic is not divisible by
p
, the condition holds. Based on heuristic arguments, it is reasonable to expect that condition (4) is satisfied for a proportion of
(1-1/p)
of all elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
. However, establishing this rigorously remains an open problem, even for a positive density of curves. Further details on this matter can be found in
[
16
]
. In this context, it was shown by the second author
[
22
]
that:

•:

E
has good ordinary reduction at
5
,

•:

\mu_{5}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\lambda_{5}(E/\mathbb{Q})
.

Our next result applies to elliptic curves for which
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]\neq 0
.

Theorem C

(Theorem
3.15
)
.

Let

We also study the distribution of

-adic

-invariants in quadratic twist families, the following result is proven in section

with good ordinary reduction at

and squarefree conductor

. Assume that
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
. Consider the family of quadratic twists

, where

(Theorem

)
.

Assume that

. If

It is natural to consider extending our approach to elliptic curves with supersingular reduction at
2
. In this setting, the standard tools used for ordinary reduction are no longer directly applicable since the Selmer group over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension is not
\Lambda
-cotorsion. As a result, understanding the growth of Selmer groups in the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-extension requires alternative frameworks. The
\sharp
and
\flat
Selmer groups introduced by Sprung
[
29
]
provide a refined approach to tackling these challenges. However, the theory of
\sharp
and
\flat
Selmer groups for
p=2
remains underdeveloped compared to the case of odd primes. Important questions about the structure, growth properties, and
2
-adic
L
-functions associated with these Selmer groups are yet to be fully explored. We expect that our work could invigorate interest in such themes. The interplay between the arithmetic of the elliptic curve and the structure of these Selmer groups might also yield deeper insights into
2
-adic families of modular forms and Galois representations. Given the rich structure inherent in supersingular reduction, one can be optimistic that this area will produce intriguing discoveries that connect with broader themes in number theory, such as
p
-adic Hodge theory, Euler systems, and the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture in the
2
-adic context.

2.
Preliminary notions

2.1.
Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves

In this section, we recall preliminary notions pertaining to the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves. First, we must set up some notation. For a field
F
of characteristic zero,
\operatorname{G}_{F}
will denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}/F)
. Given a prime number
\ell
, choose an embedding
\iota:\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
and let
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. Set
\operatorname{I}_{\ell}
to be the inertia subgroup of
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
and let
\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}\in\operatorname{G}_{\ell}/\operatorname{I}_{\ell}
be a Frobenius element. The embedding
\iota_{\ell}
induces an inclusion
\iota_{\ell}^{*}:\operatorname{G}_{\ell}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Throughout,
p
will be a fixed prime number and
E:y^{2}=f(x)
an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\chi
denote the
p
-adic cyclotomic character and
\omega
the mod-
p
reduction of
\chi
. We shall assume throughout that
E
has potentially good ordinary reduction at
p
. In other words, there is a finite extension
\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that the base change
E_{/\mathcal{K}}
has good ordinary reduction. Given a prime number
\ell
, take
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
to be the field with
\ell
elements. If
E
has good reduction at
\ell
, we will denote by
\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
the
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-rational points of the reduction of
E
at
\ell
. We shall set
E[p^{k}]
to denote the
p^{k}
torsion points of
E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
. Set
E[p^{\infty}]
to be the
p
-divisible group of
p
-primary torsion points. Note that
E[p^{\infty}]
comes equipped with a natural action of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Set
\mathbb{T}_{p}(E)
to denote the Tate-module, i.e. the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{n}E[p^{n}]
where the inverse limit is taken with respect to multiplication by
p
maps
\times p:E[p^{n+1}]\rightarrow E[p^{n}]
. Choose an isomorphism
\mathbb{T}_{p}(E)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and let

\rho_{E,p}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\mathbb{T}_{p}(E))\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

be the associated representation. The mod-
p
reduction of
\rho_{E,p}
is denoted

\bar{\rho}_{E,p}:\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

and is identified with the Galois representation on
E[p]
. The condition that
E
has potentially good ordinary reduction at
p
implies that there is a finite extension
\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that
\rho_{E,p}
restricts to the inertia subgroup of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathcal{K}}
to a representation of the form
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi&\ast\\
0&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
. Let
d
be a squarefree integer, denote by
E^{(d)}
the quadratic twist given by
dy^{2}=f(x)
.

Given a number field
K
, let
E(K)
denote the Mordell–Weil group of
E
over
K
. Let
\Omega_{K}^{f}
be the set of finite primes of
K
and
\Omega_{K}^{\infty}
the archimedian places. We let
\Omega_{K}
be the set of all places of
K
, i.e.,
\Omega_{K}:=\Omega_{K}^{f}\cup\Omega_{K}^{\infty}
. We shall use
H^{i}(L,\cdot):=H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{L},\cdot)
where
L
denotes
K
or
K_{v}
. The Tate–Shafarevich group
\Sh(E/K)
is defined as

(2.1)

\Sh(E/K):=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(K,E)\rightarrow\prod_{v\in\Omega_{K}}H^{1}(K_{v},E)\right\}.

It is conjectured that the Tate–Shafarevich group
\Sh(E/K)
is finite. This is known in some special cases. Rubin
[
24
]
proved that if
E
is a CM elliptic curve with analytic rank at most
1
, then
\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})
is finite. Kolyvagin
[
13
]
extended this to all elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
of analytic rank at most
1
.

Mazur
[
19
]
initiated the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves by studying growth properties of Selmer groups in cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions. Selmer groups consist of Galois cohomology classes which satisfy local conditions at all primes. Let us recall their definition and properties in some detail. From the Kummer sequence of
\operatorname{G}_{L}
-modules

0\rightarrow E(\bar{L})[p^{n}]\rightarrow E(\bar{L})[p^{\infty}]\xrightarrow{\times p^{n}}E(\bar{L})[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0,

we obtain the Kummer map

\kappa_{E,L}^{(n)}:\frac{E(L)}{p^{n}E(L)}\hookrightarrow H^{1}(L,E[p^{n}]).

Passing to the direct limit in
n
, one obtains:

(2.2)

\kappa_{E,L}:E(L)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\hookrightarrow H^{1}(L,E[p^{\infty}]).

Definition 2.1
.

The
p
-primary Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
consists of all classes
\alpha\in H^{1}(K,E[p^{\infty}])
such that
\alpha_{|v}\in\operatorname{image}\kappa_{E,K_{v}}
for all primes
v\in\Omega_{K}
.

The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)
is cofinitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module, and it fits into a natural short exact sequence

(2.3)

0\rightarrow E(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\xrightarrow{\kappa_{E,K}}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K)\rightarrow\Sh(E/K)[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0.

Let
K_{\infty}
denote the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
, i.e., the unique extension contained in
K(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
for which
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as a topological group. We set
\Gamma:=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)
and choose a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Let
K_{n}
be the
n
-th layer of
K_{\infty}
, defined to be the subextension of
K_{\infty}
such that
[K_{n}:K]=p^{n}
. View

K=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset\dots\subset K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}\subset\dots\subset K_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n}K_{n}

as a tower of extensions and identify
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{n}/K)
with
\Gamma_{n}:=\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

With respect to notation above, the Iwasawa algebra is then taken to be the completed group ring
\Lambda:=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}]
.

Setting
T:=(\gamma-1)\in\Lambda
, we identify
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. A monic polynomial
f(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
is said to be a
distinguished polynomial
if its nonleading coefficients are all divisible by
p
. Let
M
be a module over
\Lambda
, set
M^{\vee}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})
to be the Pontryagin dual of
M
. Then
M
is said to be
cofinitely generated
(resp.
cotorsion
) over
\Lambda
if
M^{\vee}
is finitely generated (resp. torsion) over
\Lambda
. Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-modules. Then,
M
and
M^{\prime}
are said to be
pseudo-isomorphic
if there is a map
\phi:M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
of
\Lambda
-modules, whose kernel and cokernel are finite. By the structure theory of finitely generated and torsion
\Lambda
-modules, any cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-module
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to a module

2.4.
Analytic ingredients

In this section, we discuss tauberian theorems and consequences which will come in handy in proving our density results. Let
F(X)
and
G(X)
be non-negative functions of a variable
X\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Assume that
G(X)>0
for large enough values of
X
, Then we write
F(X)\sim G(X)
to mean that
\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F(X)}{G(X)}=1
. On the other hand, write
F(X)\gg G(X)
to mean that
F(X)>cG(X)
for some positive constant
c
which is independent of
X
. Let us begin with the standard Delange’s tauberian theorem.

Theorem 2.11

(Delange’s tauberian theorem)
.

Let
f(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
be a Dirichlet series with non-negative coefficients and
a>0
be a real number. Assume that
f(s)
converges for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>a
and has a meromorphic continuation to a neighbourhood
U
of
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq a
. For
X>0
, we set

g(X):=\sum_{n\leq X}a_{n}
. Assume that the only pole of
f(s)
is at
s=a
and the order of this pole is
b\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, i.e.,

f(s)=\frac{1}{(s-a)^{b}}h(s)

for some holomorphic function
h(s)
defined on
U
. Then, there is a positive constant
c>0
such that, as
X\rightarrow\infty
, we have

g(X)\sim cX^{a}(\log X)^{b-1}.

A set of prime numbers
\Omega
is said to have a natural density
\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)
if the following limit exists:

\mathfrak{d}(\Omega):=\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\ell\in\Omega\mid\ell\leq X\}}{\pi(X)}.

On the other hand,
\Omega
is said to have Dirichlet density if the following limit exists

\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}(\Omega):=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1^{+}}\frac{\sum_{\ell\in\Omega}\ell^{-s}}{\sum_{\ell}\ell^{-s}},

where in the denominator, the sum is over all prime numbers. If the natural density exists, then so does the Dirichlet density and moreover,
\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)=\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}(\Omega)
. In this article, the word density for a set of primes shall refer to their natural density. Associated to
\Omega
is the set of square-free natural numbers
N_{\Omega}
consisting of
d
divisible only by primes
\ell\notin\Omega
. We set
N_{\Omega}(X):=N_{\Omega}\cap[1,X]
and
n_{\Omega}(X):=\#N_{\Omega}(X)
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\Omega
be a set of primes with positive density
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then
n_{\Omega}(X)\sim cX/(\log X)^{\alpha}
, where
c>0
is a constant.

Proof.

The result follows from
[
26
, Theorem 2.4, p.5 line -3 to p.6 line -10.]
. We sketch the proof here, and for further details, we refer to
loc. cit.
Let
\Omega^{c}
be the complement of
\Omega
and

\begin{split}f(s):=\sum_{n\in N_{\Omega}}n^{-s}&=\sum_{T\subset\Omega^{c}}\left(\prod_{\ell\in T}\ell\right)^{-s}\\
&=\prod_{\ell\notin\Omega}\left(1+\ell^{-s}\right).\end{split}

It is easy to see that

\log f(s)=\sum_{\ell\notin\Omega}\ell^{-s}+k_{1}(s),

where
k_{1}(s)
is holomorphic on
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
; and as a consequence,

\log f(s)=(1-\alpha)\log\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)+k_{2}(s),

where
k_{2}(s)
is holomorphic on
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
. Thus, we deduce that

f(s)=(s-1)^{\alpha-1}h(s),

where
h(s)
is a non-zero holomorphic function on
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
. It follows from the Theorem
2.11
that

n_{\Omega}(X)\sim cX(\log X)^{-\alpha},

where
c>0
is a constant that does not depend on
X
.
∎

\bar{\rho}_{E,2}
is not surjective, let
i\in\{1,2,3\}
be the smallest value such that
j(E)=J_{i}(t)
for some
t\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then the density of
\Omega
is given by

\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if}\ i=1,2\\
\frac{1}{3},&\text{if i=3}\end{cases}.

On the other hand, if
\bar{\rho}_{E,2}
is surjective,
\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)=\frac{2}{3}
.

Proof.

For a prime
\ell\nmid N_{E}
, let
\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell}
denote the corresponding Frobenius element. Then we have

a_{\ell}(E)\equiv\mathrm{tr}\ \bar{\rho}_{E,2}\left(\mathrm{Frob}_{\ell}\right)\mod 2.

As the trace is conjugacy invariant, we can thus investigate the frequency that
E(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[2]\neq 0
in terms of the
G_{i}
. The result follows as a consequence of Remark
3.7
and the Chebotarev density theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
d
be a positive squarefree integer and write
d=\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{k}
. Assume that for all
i=1,\ldots,k
we have
\ell_{i}\nmid 2N_{E}
and
\ell_{i}\notin\Omega
. Then we find that
\lambda_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover, we find that

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})\leq\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q}).

Proof.

The result is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.2
and Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Let
\Omega^{\prime}
be the set of primes
\ell\nmid 2N_{E}
such that
\ell\notin\Omega
. According to Remark
3.7
, when
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]\neq 0
, the set
\Omega^{\prime}
is finite. On the other hand, by Corollary
3.9
, if
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
then the density of
\Omega^{\prime}
is
2/3
(resp.
1/3
) if the image of
\bar{\rho}_{E,2}
is conjugate to
G_{3}
(resp.
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
). Given a natural number
r
, let
n^{\operatorname{alg}}_{E,\leq r}(X)
be the number of squarefree numbers
d>0
such that
\textbf{r}_{\operatorname{alg}}(E^{(d))})\leq r
and
d<X
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with good ordinary reduction at
2
. Assume that
N_{E}
is squarefree and that
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
. Setting
\lambda:=\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})
, we find that

n_{E,\leq\lambda}^{\operatorname{alg}}(X)\gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^{\delta}}

where
\delta:=\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{3}\text{ if }\operatorname{image}\bar{\rho}_{E,2}\simeq G_{3},\\
\frac{2}{3}\text{ if }\operatorname{image}\bar{\rho}_{E,2}=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2}).\\
\end{cases}
.

Proof.

According to Corollary
3.9
, if
d>0
is a product of primes
\ell_{i}\in\Omega^{\prime}
then

\mathbf{r}_{\operatorname{alg}}(E^{(d)})\leq\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})\leq\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q}).

Let
\widetilde{\Omega}
be the complement of
\Omega^{\prime}
, i.e. it is the union of
\Omega
and the primes
\ell|2N_{E}
. Clearly,
\widetilde{\Omega}
has the same density as
\Omega
. It then follows from Proposition
2.12
that

n_{E,\leq\lambda}^{\operatorname{alg}}(X)\gg n_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(X)\gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^{\delta}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose that
E
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and assume that the following assertions are satisfied:

(1)

E
has good ordinary reduction at
2
,

(2)

E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
,

(3)

\mu_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\leq 2
.

(4)

There is a finite set of primes
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}
coprime to
2N_{E}
such that

•

\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}\notin\Omega
,

•

d=\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{k}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

•

If
\omega(E)=+1
(resp.
\omega(E)=-1
) then an odd (resp. even) number of the primes
\ell_{i}
are inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-N_{E}})
and the rest are split in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-N_{E}})
.

Then we have that

(1)

\mu_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\lambda_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=1
,

(2)

\omega(E^{(d)})=-1
,

(3)

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})\right)=1
.

Proof.

The relationship between
\omega(E)
and
\omega(E^{(d)})
is given by

\omega(E^{(d)})=\chi_{d}(-N_{E})\omega(E),

cf.
[
25
, section 4]
. The character
\chi_{d}(-N_{E})
is given by the Kronecker symbol
\left(\frac{d}{-N_{E}}\right)
. Then we have that

\left(\frac{\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{k}}{-N_{E}}\right)=(-1)^{(\frac{d-1}{2})(\frac{N_{E}-1}{2})}\left(\frac{-N_{E}}{\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{k}}\right)=\left(\frac{-N_{E}}{\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{k}}\right)=\left(\frac{-N_{E}}{\ell_{1}}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{-N_{E}}{\ell_{k}}\right).

Consequently, we have that
\omega(E^{(d)})=-1
.

We note that
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
is a condition equivalent to requiring that
\rho_{E,2}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
is either
G_{3}
or all of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
. On the other hand, since
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
is disjoint from
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
since the primes
\ell_{i}
are ramified in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
and unramified in
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
. It follows that
\rho_{E,2}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})})=\rho_{E,2}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Thus, we find that
\rho_{E,2}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})})
is either
G_{3}
or all of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and deduce that
E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))[2]=0
. In particular, it follows that
E^{(d)}(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
. Then Theorem
3.3
of Dokchitser and Dokchitser applies to
E^{(d)}
and implies that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})
is odd. Since
\ell_{i}\notin\Omega
for
i\geq 2
, we find that

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})\leq\lambda_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\leq 2.

This in particular implies that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=1
.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and assume that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

E
has good ordinary reduction at
2
with squarefree conductor
N_{E}
,

(2)

\omega(E)=-1
,

(3)

E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
,

(4)

\mu_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\leq 2
.

Then we find that

n_{E,1}^{\prime}(X)\gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^{\frac{11}{12}}}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
consist of primes
\ell
such that
\ell\nmid 2N_{E}
,
\ell\notin\Omega
, and
\ell
splits completely in
F:=\mathbb{Q}(i,\sqrt{-N_{E}})
. Suppose that
d=\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{s}
is a product of distinct primes
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{s}\in\mathcal{M}
. Since each of the primes
\ell_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
it follows that
d\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. It then follows from Proposition
3.11
that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})\right)=1
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{c}
be the complement of
\mathcal{M}
and recall that
n_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}(X)
is the number of squarefree
d<0
that are products of primes
\ell\in\mathcal{M}
. We find that
n_{E,1}^{\prime}(X)\geq n_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}(X)
. On the other hand, Proposition
3.11
implies that

n_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}(X)\gg\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1-\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{M})}}.

Thus what remains is to estimate
\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{M})
. Let
k\subset\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
be the field over which
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/k
is a cubic extension. Thus, if the residual image is conjugate to
G_{3}
,
k=\mathbb{Q}
. According to Remark
3.7
a prime
\ell\nmid 2N_{E}
is not contained in
\Omega
if it splits in
k
and the primes above it are nonsplit in the extension
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/k
. We show that this is independent of the condition that
\ell
splits completely in the biquadratic extension
F
. We shall set
F(E[2])
to denote the composite
F\cdot\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
. Since
[F\cdot k:k]
is prime to
3
, we find that
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])\cap F\cdot k=k
. Consequently, we find that
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[2])/F\cdot k)\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/k)
. Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the two nontrivial elements of
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[2])/F\cdot k)
and view
\mathcal{S}
as a subset of
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[2])/\mathbb{Q})
. We find that
\ell\in\mathcal{M}
if and only if
\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}\in\mathcal{S}
. Thus by the Chebotarev density theorem,

\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{M})=\frac{\#\mathcal{S}}{[F(E[2]):\mathbb{Q}]}\geq\frac{2}{[\mathbb{Q}(E[2]):\mathbb{Q}][F:\mathbb{Q}]}\geq\frac{1}{12}.

Thus we find that
1-\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{M})\leq\frac{11}{12}
, which proves the result.
∎

The proof of the above result also shows that there is an explicit set of prime numbers
\mathcal{M}
of density
\geq\frac{1}{12}
such that for all
\ell\in\mathcal{M}
,
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q})\right)=1
.

.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve satisfying the conditions of Theorem
3.12
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of prime numbers
\ell

\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}

3.12
.
∎

Theorem 3.15
.

Let

be an elliptic curve satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

has squarefree conductor

,

(2)

has good ordinary reduction at
2

(3)

\omega(E)=+1
,

(4)

\mu_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
,

(5)

.

Let
\ell
be a prime satisfying the following conditions:

•

\ell\nmid 2N_{E}
,

•

\ell
is inert in
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-N_{E}})
,

•

\ell\equiv 3,5\pmod{8}
.

Then we have that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q})=1
. The set of primes satisfying the above conditions have density
\frac{1}{4}
.

Proof.

Since
\ell\nmid 2N_{E}
, we find that

\omega(E^{(d)})=\chi_{d}(-N_{E})\omega(E)=-\omega(E)=-1.

Theorem
3.3
of Dokchitser and Dokchitser asserts that

, see Remark
3.7
. Since

(in accordance with notation in the statement of Theorem

). Thus it follows from Theorem

that

It then follows from (

) that
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q})\leq 2
. Thus, we have shown that

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\operatorname{Sel}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{(\ell)}/\mathbb{Q})=1.

Note that there is no elliptic curve with conductor

. Since

is squarefree, it must be therefore be divisible by some odd prime number. Thus,

is linearly disjoint from

. The condition that

In this short section, we prove a result about the distribution of
\lambda
-invariants in quadratic twist families. We fix an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with good ordinary reduction at
2
with squarefree conductor
N_{E}
. Moreover we shall assume that
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]=0
. Consider the family of quadratic twists
E^{(d)}
where
d>0
is a squarefree integer and let
m_{E,N}(X)
count the number of positive squarefree
d\leq X
such that
\lambda(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=N
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\mu_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
. If
N
is any integer such that
N\geq\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})
and
N\equiv\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\pmod{2}
, then we have that

m_{E,N}(X)\gg X/(\log X)^{\alpha},

where
\alpha=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{3}&\text{ if }\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z};\\
\frac{2}{3}&\text{ if }\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/\mathbb{Q})\simeq S_{3}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
consist of primes
\ell\nmid 2N_{E}
such that:

•

\ell\equiv 3,5\pmod{8}
,

•

\ell\in\Omega
, i.e.,
\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[2]\neq 0
.

Let
k
be the subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
such that
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/k
is a cubic extension. Suppose that
\ell\nmid 2N_{E}
is a prime which is inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
and any prime
v
of
k
that lies above
\ell
is completely split in
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
. Then,
\ell\in\mathcal{Q}
and thus
\mathcal{Q}
has positive density. Write
N=\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})+2k
and pick
k
primes
q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\in\mathcal{Q}
. Since
q_{i}\equiv 3,5\pmod{8}
we find that
n_{q_{i}}=0
. Let
d^{\prime}:=q_{1}\dots q_{k}
and let
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{s}
be primes such that
\ell_{i}\nmid 2N_{E}
and
\ell_{i}\notin\Omega
. We then set
d:=d^{\prime}d^{\prime\prime}
, where
d^{\prime\prime}:=\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{s}
. From (
3.2
) we deduce that

\lambda_{2}(E^{(d)}/\mathbb{Q})=\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\mid d\\
2\mid\#\tilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\end{subarray}}2^{n_{\ell}+1}=\lambda_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})+2k=N.

Keeping
d^{\prime}
fixed, the number of choices for
d^{\prime\prime}
such that
d^{\prime\prime}\leq\frac{X}{d^{\prime}}
is
n_{\Omega}(X/d^{\prime})
. By Proposition
2.12
, there is a constant
c>0
such that

n_{\Omega}(X/d^{\prime})\sim\frac{c(X/d^{\prime})}{\left(\log(X/d^{\prime})\right)^{\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)}}.

Relabeling
c
, we find that

n_{\Omega}(X/d^{\prime})\sim cX/(\log X)^{\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)}.

Thus

m_{E,N}(X)\geq n_{\Omega}(X/d^{\prime})\gg X/(\log X)^{\delta},

where by Corollary
3.8
,

\alpha:=\mathfrak{d}(\Omega)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{3}&\text{ if }\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z};\\
\frac{2}{3}&\text{ if }\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/\mathbb{Q})\simeq S_{3}.\end{cases}

∎

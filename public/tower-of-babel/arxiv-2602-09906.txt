Regularity for Doubly Nonlinear Equations in the Mixed Regime
S. Ciani, E. Henriques, M. Savchenko, I. Skrypnik, Y. Yevgenieva

Abstract
We study the local Hölder continuity of nonnegative solutions to doubly nonlinear equations by introducing a new technique that allows us to treat the cases where the equation is both singular and degenerate, up to specific Barenblatt numbers. Our argument relies on a new integral L 1 L^{1} - L 1 L^{1} Harnack estimate, of independent interest.

Introduction

1.1
Origins and framing of the topic

The strong correspondence between the porous medium equation
and the
p
-Laplacian equation leads to the finding of a class of
equations enjoying the same properties: the so-called class of
doubly nonlinear equations, which appear in the literature in three (somehow equivalent) forms

u_{t}-\textrm{div}\big(|u|^{m-1}|Du|^{p-2}Du\big)=0,\qquad p>1,\quad m>0,

(1.1)

u_{t}-\textrm{div}\big(|Du^{m}|^{p-2}Du^{m}\big)=0,\qquad p>1,\quad m>0,

(1.2)

(u^{q})_{t}-\textrm{div}\big(|Du|^{p-2}Du\big)=0,\qquad p>1,\quad q>0\ .

(1.3)

Such equations rule several physical phenomena, e.g., the dynamics of
the turbulent flow of a non-Newtonian polytropic fluid through a
porous medium. These equations were introduced, for the first time,
by Lions
[
41
]
. Especially in the last years,
many papers have been devoted to this topic (for the first contributions
on the subject see
[
35
]
). But, as already written above, the
reason for the interest in these equations consists in being
a natural bridge between two natural generalisations of the heat
equation: the
p
-Laplacian and the porous medium equations. Each of the three formulations above presents a different characterisation of the loss of linear structure of the heat equation.

Classification

Regularity Theory

It is possible to identify two main approaches leading to the Hölder continuity: one relying on Cacciopoli estimates, an iterative scheme based on Moser’s methods or another kind of iterative scheme based on De Giorgi’s method and making use of intrinsic scaling (a powerful tool devised by DiBenedetto in the early 90s); and another different one based on a geometric tangential approach and intrinsic scaling technique (first presented for

The issue of Hölder Continuity
. If

is a solution to equation (

are nonnegative, unfortunately the functions

\Rightarrow

by properly choosing

1.

when the infimum of

is quantitatively away from zero, which means that locally on a cylinder

\inf_{Q}u\geqslant\frac{1}{\gamma}\sup_{Q}u

is valid
1
1

1

for some fixed constant
\gamma>1

when the supremum of

is quantitatively controlled by the oscillation, which means that locally on a cylinder

\sup_{Q}u\leqslant\tilde{\gamma}\osc_{Q}u

The precise aim of our work here is to step through this gap and show that for a range of exponents in the mixed singular-degenerate or degenerate-singular regime, it is still possible to recover the desired regularity.

1.2
Main Result

In this paper, we are concerned with doubly nonlinear parabolic equations whose structure is modeled after the prototype (
1.2

\Omega

\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)

u_{t}-\textrm{div}\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)=0,\quad\quad(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}.

(1.4)

Throughout the paper we suppose that the functions
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\cdots,A_{N}):\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}

,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
and
\mathbf{A}(x,t,\cdot,\cdot)
are continuous for almost all
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}

We also assume that the following structure conditions are satisfied

\begin{cases}\mathbf{A}(x,t,u,Du)Du^{m^{-}}\geqslant K_{1}\,u^{(m-m^{-})(p-1)}|Du^{m^{-}}|^{p},\\

,

are positive constants and

.

In order to simplify the presentation, we have decided to give a definition of solution that already encodes the nonnegativity of

Theorem 3.1
.

(3.1)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we set

(3.8)

\lambda<1
, we have

b\,\omega_{0}^{1-\lambda}\rho_{0}^{p}\leqslant b\,M^{1-\lambda}r^{p-1}\,\rho_{0}\leqslant\rho_{0},\qquad M:=\sup_{Q_{R}}u,

provided that
r
is chosen sufficiently small so that
b\,M^{1-\lambda}r^{p-1}\leqslant 1
.

In the case
\lambda>1
, using (
5.1
), we obtain

b\,\omega_{0}^{1-\lambda}\rho_{0}^{p}\leqslant\frac{b}{A^{\lambda-1}}\rho_{0}\leqslant\rho_{0},

provided that
A
is large enough to ensure
bA^{1-\lambda}\leqslant 1
.
Consequently,

Q_{\rho_{0},b\theta(\omega_{0},\rho_{0})}\subset Q_{\rho_{0}}

in both cases
\lambda>1
and
\lambda<1
.

5.2
Main Proposition

Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exist constants
b>0
and
\epsilon,\sigma\in(0,1)
such that the following
holds.
Construct the sequences

r_{j}:=\epsilon^{j}\rho_{0},\qquad\omega_{j}:=\sigma^{j}\omega_{0},\qquad\theta_{j}:=\theta(\omega_{j},r_{j})=r^{p}_{j}\,\omega^{1-\lambda}_{j},\qquad j=0,1,2,...

And define the family of intrinsic cylinders

Q_{j}:=Q_{r_{j},b\,\theta_{j}}(x_{0},t_{0}),\qquad\mu^{+}_{j}:=\sup\limits_{Q_{j}}u,\qquad\mu^{-}_{j}:=\inf\limits_{Q_{j}}u.

Then for all
j=0,1,2,\ldots
, the following assertions hold:

Q_{j+1}\subset Q_{j},\qquad\osc\limits_{Q_{j}}u=\mu^{+}_{j}-\mu^{-}_{j}\leqslant\omega_{j}.

(5.2)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
j
.

Step
j=0
.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle r_{0}=\rho_{0},\quad\omega_{0}=\omega_{0},\quad\theta_{0}=\omega^{1-\lambda}_{0}\,\rho^{p}_{0},\quad Q_{0}=Q_{\rho_{0},b\,\omega^{1-\lambda}_{0}\,\rho^{p}_{0}}

\displaystyle\mu^{+}_{0}=\sup\limits_{Q_{0}}u,\quad\mu^{-}_{0}=\inf\limits_{Q_{0}}u,\quad\osc\limits_{Q_{0}}u=\mu^{+}_{0}-\mu^{-}_{0}\leqslant\mu^{+}_{-1}=\omega_{0}.

Hence, (
5.2
) holds for
j=0
.

Step
j=1
.

By the choice of
\omega_{0}
, we have

\mu^{+}_{0}\leqslant\mu^{+}_{-1}=\omega_{0}\leqslant(1+\eta_{0})\,\omega_{0},

(5.3)

where
\eta_{0}
is the constant defined in Proposition
4.1
. Applying Proposition
4.1
, we obtain the existence of constants
b>0
and
\sigma_{0},\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\osc\limits_{Q_{1}}u\leqslant\omega_{1},\qquad r_{1}=\epsilon_{0}\rho_{0},\,\,\,\omega_{1}=\sigma_{0}\,\omega_{0}.

(5.4)

Therefore, (
5.2
) holds for
j=1
.

Induction step.

We may iterate this procedure, assuming at each step that

\mu^{+}_{j}\leqslant(1+\eta_{0})\,\omega_{j},

(5.5)

and thereby obtain (
5.2
) with the same constants
\epsilon_{0}
and
\sigma_{0}
as in (
5.4
). Assume now that
j_{0}\geqslant 1
is the first number for which (
5.5
) fails, that is,

\mu^{+}_{j_{0}}\geqslant(1+\eta_{0})\,\omega_{j_{0}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mu^{-}_{j_{0}}\geqslant\frac{\eta_{0}}{1+\eta_{0}}\mu^{+}_{j_{0}}.

(5.6)

Since (
5.5
) holds for
j=j_{0}-1
(and, in particular, for
j=0
by (
5.3
)), we obtain

\eta_{0}\,\omega_{j_{0}}\leqslant\frac{\eta_{0}}{1+\eta_{0}}\mu^{+}_{j_{0}}\leqslant\mu^{-}_{j_{0}}\leqslant\mu^{+}_{j_{0}-1}\leqslant(1+\eta_{0})\,\omega_{j_{0}-1}=\frac{1+\eta_{0}}{\sigma}\omega_{j_{0}}.

(5.7)

In this case, we perform the change of variables

\tau=\omega_{j_{0}}^{\lambda-1}t,\quad v(x,\tau)=\frac{u(x,\omega_{j_{0}}^{1-\lambda}\tau)}{\omega_{j_{0}}},

(5.8)

which maps the cylinder
Q_{j_{0}}
into the cylinder
Q_{r_{j_{0}},b\,r^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})
, where
\tau_{0}:=\omega_{j_{0}}^{\lambda-1}t_{0}
. The rescaled function
v
is uniformly bounded:

\eta_{0}\leqslant v(x,\tau)\leqslant\frac{1+\eta_{0}}{\sigma}\qquad\text{in }Q_{r_{j_{0}},b\,r^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})

(5.9)

Consequently, the rescaled function
v
satisfies a
p
-Laplace type equation with

p>1
in the cylinder
Q_{r_{j_{0}},\,b\,r_{j_{0}}^{p}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})
, namely,

\begin{cases}v_{\tau}-\operatorname{div}\hat{\mathbf{A}}(x,\tau,v,Dv)=0,\qquad(x,\tau)\in Q_{r_{j_{0}},b\,r^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0}),\\
\hat{\mathbf{A}}(x,\tau,v,Dv)Dv\geqslant\hat{K}_{1}\,|Dv|^{p},\\
|\hat{\mathbf{A}}(x,\tau,v,Dv)|\leqslant\hat{K}_{2}\,|Dv|^{p-1},\end{cases}

where the constants
\hat{K}_{1}
and
\hat{K}_{2}
depend only on the data,
\eta_{0}
, and

\sigma
.By (
5.9
), we have:

\osc\limits_{Q_{r_{j_{0}},b\,r^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})}v\leqslant\frac{1+\eta_{0}(1-\sigma)}{\sigma}=:\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}},\qquad p>1.

We now distinguish between the cases
p>2
and
1<p<2
.
Consider first the degenerate case
p>2
. Since
\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}>1
, we have

\osc\limits_{Q_{r_{j_{0}},b\,\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}^{2-p}r^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})}v\leqslant\osc\limits_{Q_{r_{j_{0}},b\,r^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})}v\leqslant\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}.

Therefore, by the classical result
[
15
, Chapter 3, Proposition 3.1]
,
there exist constants
\hat{\epsilon},\hat{\sigma}\in(0,1)
such that, defining
r_{j_{0}+j}:=\hat{\epsilon}^{j}r_{j_{0}}
and
\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}+j}:=\hat{\sigma}^{j}\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}
, the following estimate holds for all
j\geqslant 0
:

\osc\limits_{Q_{r_{j_{0}+j},b\,\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}+j}^{2-p}r^{p}_{j_{0}+j}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})}v\leqslant\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}+j}.

(5.10)

For the singular case
1<p<2
, we observe that

\osc\limits_{Q_{\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}^{-\frac{2-p}{p}}r_{j_{0}},b\,r^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})}v\leqslant\osc\limits_{Q_{r_{j_{0}},b\,r^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})}v\leqslant\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}.

Therefore, by the classical result
[
15
, Chapter 4, Proposition 2.1]
, there exist constants
\hat{\epsilon},\hat{\sigma}\in(0,1)
such that, defining
r_{j_{0}+j}:=\hat{\epsilon}^{j}r_{j_{0}}
and
\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}+j}:=\hat{\sigma}^{j}\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}
, the following estimate holds for all
j\geqslant 0
:

\osc\limits_{Q_{\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}+j}^{-\frac{2-p}{p}}r_{j_{0}+j},b\,r^{p}_{j_{0}+j}}(x_{0},\tau_{0})}v\leqslant\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}+j}.

(5.11)

We now define:

\hat{\epsilon}_{0}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }p>2,\\
\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}^{-\frac{2-p}{p}},&\text{if }1<p<2,\end{cases},\qquad\hat{r}_{j_{0}}=\hat{\epsilon}_{0}r_{j_{0}}\qquad\hat{Q}_{j_{0}}:=Q_{\hat{r}_{j_{0}},b\,\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}}^{2-p}\hat{r}^{p}_{j_{0}}}(x_{0},\tau_{0}).

In these terms, (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) imply

\osc\limits_{\hat{Q}_{j_{0}+j}}v\leqslant\hat{\omega}_{j_{0}+j}.

Returning to the original function
u(x,t)
, we obtain the following estimate

\osc\limits_{{Q}_{j_{0}+j}}u\leqslant\omega_{j_{0}+j}.

(5.12)

with
r_{j_{0}+j}:=\hat{\epsilon}_{0}\hat{\epsilon}_{0}^{j}r_{j_{0}}
and
\omega_{j_{0}+j}:=\tfrac{1+\eta_{0}(1-\sigma)}{\sigma}\hat{\sigma}_{0}^{j}\omega_{j_{0}}
. This completes the proof of the Proposition. ∎

Now that the quantitative oscillation decay (
5.12
) is valid for the net of shrinking cylinders
\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, the existence of a locally Hölder continuous representative of
u
follows from standard iterative arguments, see, for instance,
[
15
, Chapters 3 and 4]
for the standard argument and
[
8
]
, section 2.1, for the construction of the representative. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

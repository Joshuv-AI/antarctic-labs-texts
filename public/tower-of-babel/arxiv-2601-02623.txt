Joint extreme values of the Riemann zeta function at harmonic points
Qiyu Yang 1, Shengbo Zhao 2

Abstract
. Using the resonance method, we obtain refined estimates for joint extreme values of the Riemann zeta function at harmonic points, improving upon Levinson’s 1972 results and providing new insight into the behavior of the Riemann zeta function. Our proof is primarily based on Dirichlet series theory and the truncated Euler product for the Riemann zeta function. As a corollary, we can recover some previously known extreme value results for the zeta function.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11M06, 11N37.

1.
Introduction

The Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
is one of the most important objects in analytic number theory, due to its close connection with the distribution of prime numbers. The study of the behavior of its extreme values has always been a valuable research topic.

In 1972, Levinson’s work
[
14
]
refined the earlier results of Littlewood and showed that for sufficiently large
T
,

\max_{t\in[1,T]}|\zeta(1+it)|\geq e^{\gamma}\log_{2}T+O(1),

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Here and throughout, we write
\log_{k}
for the
k
-th iterated logarithm. More than three decades later, Granville and Soundararajan
[
9
]
further improved Levinson’s result by refining the lower bound and investigating the frequency of large values. They also proposed a conjecture regarding the true order of the maximum of
|\zeta(1+it)|
. Up to now, the sharpest lower bound was established by Aistleitner, Mahatab, and Munsch
[
2
]
. Using the long resonance method, they showed that for all sufficiently large
T
, there exists a constant
C
such that

\max_{t\in[\sqrt{T},T]}|\zeta(1+it)|\geq e^{\gamma}(\log_{2}T+\log_{3}T-C).

In the case of the real part
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
, Aistleitner
[
1
]
refined earlier work of Montgomery
[
15
]
, which relied on Diophantine approximation. Using the resonance method, Aistleitner
[
1
]
proved that for
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
fixed and sufficiently large
T
,

\max_{t\in[0,T]}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|\geq\exp\bigg(C_{\sigma}\frac{(\log T)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log_{2}T)^{\sigma}}\bigg),

where
C_{\sigma}=0.18(2\sigma-1)^{1-\sigma}
. Subsequently, the celebrated work of Bondarenko and Seip
[
4
]
provided a thorough investigation into the behavior of extreme values of
\zeta(s)
within the critical strip. Specifically, they showed that if
T
is sufficiently large, then for
1/2+1/\log_{2}T\leq\sigma\leq 3/4
,

\max_{t\in[\sqrt{T},T]}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|\geq\exp\bigg(\nu(\sigma)\frac{(\log T)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log_{2}T)^{\sigma}}\bigg),

and for
3/4\leq\sigma\leq 1-1/\log_{2}T
,

\max_{t\in[T/2,T]}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|\geq\log_{2}T\exp\bigg(c+\nu(\sigma)\frac{(\log T)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log_{2}T)^{\sigma}}\bigg),

with
c
an absolute constant independent of
T
. Here,
\nu(\sigma)
is a continuous function bounded below by
1/(2-2\sigma)
. Furthermore, it has the following asymptotic behavior:

\nu(\sigma)=\begin{cases}(1-\sigma)^{-1}+O(|\log(1-\sigma)|),&\sigma\nearrow 1\\
(1/\sqrt{2}+o(1))\sqrt{|\log(2\sigma-1)|},&\sigma\searrow 1/2.\end{cases}

More recently, the first author of this paper unconditionally refined the work of Bondarenko and Seip
[
4
]
for any fixed
\sigma\in(0.78,1)
. Moreover, assuming the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), sharper lower bounds valid throughout the entire critical strip were obtained; see
[
17
]
. In fact, the maximum of
|\zeta(\sigma+it)|
was already predicted by Lamzouri based on a probabilistic model; see
[
12
, pp. 5453-5454]
. Furthermore, extreme values of derivatives of
\zeta(s)
on the
1
-line have also been established; see
[
7
,
16
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in joint extreme values of
\zeta(s)
at harmonic points, namely at points of the form

s=\sigma+it,\sigma+2it,\dots,\sigma+\ell it.

Henceforth, we let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be fixed, where
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is the set of all positive integers. Through the refined estimation of the coefficients
d_{\ell}(n)
of the Dirichlet series associated with
\zeta^{\ell}(s)
, Levinson
[
13
]
proved that there exist arbitrarily large
T
such that for
\ell\geq 2
,

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}|\zeta(1+ijt)|\geq(e^{\gamma}\log_{2}T)^{\ell}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{\ell\log\ell}{\log_{2}T}\Big)\Big).

While this result establishes the correct leading order for joint extreme values at harmonic points, it does not capture finer secondary terms. In particular, when
\ell=1
, Aistleitner et al.
[
2
]
improved upon Levinson’s result
[
14
]
by incorporating additional logarithmic contributions. It is therefore natural to ask whether the lower bound can be further sharpened to reflect such secondary terms. Our first theorem provides such a refinement.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T
be sufficiently large. We have

\max_{t\in[\sqrt{T},T]}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}|\zeta(1+ijt)|\geq e^{\ell\gamma}\big\{(\log_{2}T)^{\ell}+\ell(\log_{2}T)^{\ell-1}\log_{3}T+O_{\ell}\big((\log_{2}T)^{\ell-1}\big)\big\},

where the
O
-term depends only on
\ell
.

Remark 1.1
.

Here, we improve upon the above result of Levinson
[
13
]
and obtain a secondary term
\ell(\log_{2}T)^{\ell-1}\log_{3}T
. It is worth noting that when
\ell=1
, the extreme value result coincides with that of Aistleitner et al.
[
2
]
.

The preceding theorem concerns joint extreme values on the line
\sigma=1
. It is therefore natural to consider the corresponding problem in the critical strip, where the behavior of
\zeta(s)
depends crucially on
\sigma
. The following theorem treats this case.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
be fixed, and let
T
be sufficiently large. Then for
1/2<\sigma<1
, we have

\max_{t\in[T^{\beta},T]}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}|\zeta(\sigma+ijt)|\geq\exp\bigg\{\kappa^{1-\sigma}\big(S(\sigma,\ell)+o_{\sigma}(1)\big)\frac{(\log T)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log_{2}T)^{\sigma}}\bigg\}.

Here, the
o
-term depends only on
\sigma
, and
S(\sigma,\ell)
is an explicit constant defined by

S(\sigma,\ell)=\frac{\ell}{1-\sigma}+\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{m}\binom{\ell+1}{m+1}\frac{1}{1+\sigma(m-1)}.

And
\kappa
is a positive real number satisfying

\kappa<\frac{1}{\sigma(1+c(\sigma))}\min\Big(1-\beta,\frac{\sigma-1/2}{7/4-\sigma/2}\Big),

where
c(\sigma)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t/(2t^{-\sigma}-1)
.

Remark 1.2
.

In Theorem
1.2
, when
\sigma
is close to
1/2
, the growth of
S(\sigma,\ell)
with respect to
\ell
is significantly suppressed due to the strong cancellation produced by the alternating binomial sum involving
\ell/(1-\sigma)
. As
\sigma
approaches
1
, this phenomenon gradually disappears, and
S(\sigma,\ell)
then increases notably with
\ell
.

Remark 1.3
.

In Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, the ranges in which joint extreme values occur are
[\sqrt{T},T]
and
[T^{\beta},T]
, respectively, for sufficiently large
T
and fixed
\beta\in(0,1)
. In fact, in Theorem
1.1
, the range
[\sqrt{T},T]
can be replaced by
[T^{\beta},T]
, at the cost that the
O
-term would then depend on
\ell
and
\beta
. Since the main term remains unchanged, we choose
\beta=1/2
to simplify the presentation of the proof. In contrast, in Theorem
1.2
, the parameter
\kappa
appearing in the main term depends on
\beta
, and the effect of
\beta
cannot be neglected.

In Theorem
1.2
, the parameter
\kappa
is required to be bounded by two terms, where the second term comes from the assumption on the zero-free region given in Lemma
2.2
. Under RH, the formula in Lemma
2.4
is valid throughout
t\in[T^{\beta},T]
. Consequently, the conditional joint extreme values of
\zeta(\sigma+it)
in the critical strip are derived.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assuming RH. Let
\beta\in(0,1)
be fixed, and let
T
be sufficiently large. Then for
1/2<\sigma<1
, we have

\max_{t\in[T^{\beta},T]}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}|\zeta(\sigma+ijt)|\geq\exp\bigg\{\kappa^{1-\sigma}\big(S(\sigma,\ell)+o(1)\big)\frac{(\log T)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log_{2}T)^{\sigma}}\bigg\}.

Here
\kappa
is a positive real number less than
(1-\beta)/(\sigma(1+c(\sigma))
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is worth noting that, for fixed
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
, the range of conditional joint extreme values broadens as
\beta
decreases, with the range of
\kappa
increasing simultaneously. However, the order of magnitude in the exponent remains unchanged. This behavior is consistent with theoretical expectations.

The study of joint extreme values at harmonic points reveals new structural features in the behavior of
\zeta(s)
. By examining the simultaneous growth of
\zeta(s)
along homogeneous arithmetic progressions on the vertical line, we capture effects arising from the arithmetic structure of the Euler product. Specifically, the repeated occurrence of small primes
p
across different
\zeta(\sigma+ijt)
contributes significantly to this phenomenon. These results indicate that extreme values occur in a highly coordinated manner at different locations, leading to a deeper understanding of how extreme behavior propagates along harmonic directions. Moreover, the joint lower bounds extend the classical single-point phenomenon to a multi-point setting, thereby enriching extreme value theory and offering a more comprehensive description of the analytic behavior of
\zeta(s)
.

It should be noted that
\ell
in our main results is an arbitrary positive integer, in order to ensure that the truncation formulas (
3.1
) and (
4.1
) are valid.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents some auxiliary lemmas. Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are proved in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively. Finally, a short proof of Theorem
1.3
is given in Section
5
.

2.
Auxiliary lemmas

In this section, we introduce several lemmas. Define
\zeta(s;y)\coloneqq\prod_{p\leq y}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
. Now we state Lemma
2.1
, which will be used to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.1

(Aistleitner-Mahatab-Munsch
[
2
]
)
.

Let
T
be sufficiently large, and set
Y=\exp\big((\log T)^{10}\big)
. Then for
T^{1/10}\leq|t|\leq T
, we have

\zeta(1+it)=\zeta(1+it;Y)\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log T}\Big)\Big).

The following lemma, established by Granville and Soundararajan
[
9
]
, provides an important result concerning
\log\zeta(\sigma+it)
.

Lemma 2.2

(Granville-Soundararajan
[
9
]
)
.

Let
y\geq 2
and
|t|\geq y+3
be real numbers. Let
1/2\leq\sigma_{0}<1
and suppose that the rectangle
\{s:\sigma_{0}<\operatorname{Re}(s)\leq 1,|\operatorname{Im}(s)-t|\leq y+2\}
is free of zeros of
\zeta(s)
. Then for
\sigma_{0}<\sigma\leq 1
, we have

\log\zeta(\sigma+it)=\sum_{n=2}^{y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}\log n}+O\bigg(\frac{\log|t|}{|\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{0}|^{2}}y^{\sigma_{1}-\sigma}\bigg),

where
\sigma_{1}=\min(\sigma_{0}+(\log y)^{-1},(\sigma+\sigma_{0})/2)
.

Note that Lemma
2.2
requires the assumption that there are no zeros of
\zeta(s)
in a certain range. Therefore, in order to apply this result effectively, it is necessary to combine it with the zero-density result for
\zeta(s)
. Let
N(\sigma,T)
denote the number of zeros of
\zeta(s)
within the rectangle
\{s:\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq\sigma,0<\operatorname{Im}(s)\geq T\}
. The following classical result can be found in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Ingham
[
10
]
)
.

Let
1/2\leq\sigma\leq 1
, we have

N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{\frac{3(1-\sigma)}{2-\sigma}}(\log T)^{5}.

From Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we obtain Lemma
2.4
below, which provides a truncated formula for
\zeta(s)
in the critical strip and plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
\beta\in(0,1)
Let
T
be sufficiently large, and set
Y=(\log T)^{2026/(\sigma-1/2)}
. Then the truncated formula

\zeta(\sigma+it)=\zeta(\sigma+it;Y)\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log T}\Big)\Big)

holds for all
t\in[T^{\beta},T]\setminus\mathcal{E}
. Here, we note that

\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal{E})\ll T^{\frac{9/4-3\sigma/2}{7/4-\sigma/2}+o(1)}.

Proof.

Choose
y=Y=(\log T)^{2026/(\sigma-1/2)}
and
\sigma_{0}=\sigma/2+1/4
in Lemma
2.2
. Meanwhile, using Lemma
2.3
and we have

\log\zeta(\sigma+it)=\sum_{n=2}^{Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}\log n}+O\Big(\frac{1}{(\log T)^{10}}\Big)

for all
t\in[T^{\beta},T]\setminus\mathcal{E}
with
\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal{E})\ll YT^{3(1-\sigma_{0})/(2-\sigma_{0})+o(1)}
. By the definition of
\Lambda(n)
, setting
n=p^{k}
for
k\geq 1
yields

\sum_{n=2}^{Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{\sigma+it}\log n}=\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma+it}}+\sum_{k\geq 2}\sum_{p^{k}\leq Y}\frac{1}{kp^{k(\sigma+it)}}.

This is because
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
ensures that the terms with
k\geq 2
are bounded.
On the other hand, it is clear that

\zeta(\sigma+it;Y)=\exp\Big(-\sum_{p\leq Y}\log\Big(1-\frac{1}{p^{\sigma+it}}\Big)\Big)=\exp\Big(\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma+it}}+\sum_{k\geq 2}\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{1}{kp^{k(\sigma+it)}}\Big).

We observe that the sum

\sum_{k\geq 2}\sum_{Y^{1/k}<p\leq Y}\frac{1}{kp^{k\sigma}}=O\Big(\frac{1}{(\log T)^{10}}\Big)

is sufficiently small by our choice of
Y
, so that
\zeta(\sigma+it)
can be approximated well by
\zeta(\sigma+it;Y)
. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we shall present the proof of Theorem
1.1
by applying the long resonance method developed in
[
2
]
.
Set
Y=\exp\big((\log T)^{10}\big)
. Using Lemma
2.1
, the following asymptotic formula

(3.1)

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt)=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt;Y)\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log T}\Big)\Big)

holds for all
t\in[\sqrt{T},T]
. Subsequently, we set

X=(\log T\log_{2}T)/6,

and define
r(n)
to be a completely multiplicative function whose values at primes are given by

r(p)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{p}{X},\penalty\ &\operatorname{if}p\leq X,\\
0,\penalty\ &\operatorname{if}p>X.\end{cases}

Define

R(t)=\prod_{p\leq X}\big(1-r(p)p^{it}\big)^{-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r(n)n^{it}.

Note that

\log|R(t)|\leq\sum_{p\leq X}\log\Big|\frac{1}{1-r(p)}\Big|=\sum_{p\leq X}\log\frac{X}{p}.

Using the prime number theory and the choice of
X
, it is easy to derive that

(3.2)

|R(t)|^{2}\leq T^{1/3+o(1)}.

Moreover, the definition of
Y
implies that

(3.3)

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}|\zeta(1+ijt;Y)|\ll(\log Y)^{\ell}\ll T^{o(1)}.

Let
\Phi(y)\coloneqq e^{-y^{2}/2}
. The Fourier transform
\widehat{\Phi}
satisfies
\widehat{\Phi}(\xi)=\sqrt{2\pi}\Phi(\xi)>0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. Due to the exponential decay of
\Phi
, all sums and integrals associated with
\Phi
that appear below are absolutely convergent. We then define the following two quantities:

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle\,\coloneqq M_{1}(R,T)=\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle\,\coloneqq M_{2}(R,T)=\int_{\sqrt{T}}^{T}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt;Y)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t.

Trivially, we have
\max_{t\in[\sqrt{T},T]}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}|\zeta(1+ijt;Y)|\geq|M_{2}|/M_{1}
. To use the positivity of the Fourier transform of
\Phi
, we extend the integrals to
\mathbb{R}
. Naturally, considering the tails, we have

\Big|\int_{|t|\leq\sqrt{T}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt;Y)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t\Big|\ll T^{5/6+o(1)}

by applying (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). Furthermore, the rapid decay of
\Phi
implies

\Big|\int_{|t|\geq T}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt;Y)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t\Big|\ll 1.

Consequently,

2M_{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt;Y)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t+O\big(T^{5/6+o(1)}\big).

Similarly, setting
I_{1}\coloneqq I_{1}(R,T)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|R(t)|^{2}\Phi(t\log T/T)\mathrm{d}t
, we have
2M_{1}=I_{1}+O\big(T^{5/6+o(1)}\big)
.
Moreover, it follows from
[
2
, Eq. (8)]
that

(3.4)

I_{1}=\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}r(m)r(n)\Big(\frac{m}{n}\Big)^{it}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t\gg T^{1+o(1)}.

Noticing that
\widehat{\Phi}
is always positive, we obtain that for
Y>X
,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt;Y)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t\geq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt;X)|R(t)|^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t.

Denote by
I_{2}\coloneqq I_{2}(R,T)
the integral on the right-hand side above. Then combining with (
3.4
) yields

(3.5)

\max_{t\in[\sqrt{T},T]}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}|\zeta(1+ijt;Y)|\geq\frac{|I_{2}|}{I_{1}}+O\big(T^{-1/6+o(1)}\big).

According to the definition of
r(n)
, we can write
\zeta(1+ijt;X)=\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{j}}{k_{j}}^{-ijt}
. Here,
a_{k_{j}}=1/k_{j}
if all prime factors of
k_{j}
are not exceeding
X
, and
a_{k_{j}}=0
otherwise.
Expanding
\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1+ijt;X)
and
|R(t)|^{2}
in
I_{2}
, we obtain

I_{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\cdots a_{k_{\ell}}k_{1}^{-it}k_{2}^{-2it}\cdots k_{\ell}^{-\ell it}\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}r(m)r(n)\Big(\frac{m}{n}\Big)^{it}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t.

Since the order of summations and integrals can be interchanged freely, we obtain

I_{2}=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\cdots a_{k_{\ell}}\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}r(m)r(n)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}k_{1}^{-it}k_{2}^{-2it}\cdots k_{\ell}^{-\ell it}\Big(\frac{m}{n}\Big)^{it}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t.

Using the fact that
\widehat{\Phi}
is always positive, we can directly discard many terms in the second sum. Specifically,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\,\geq\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\cdots a_{k_{\ell}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 1\\
k_{1}k_{2}^{2}\cdots k_{\ell}^{\ell}\mid m\end{subarray}}r(m)r(n)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{m}{k_{1}k_{2}^{2}\cdots k_{\ell}^{\ell}n}\Big)^{it}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\,=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\cdots a_{k_{\ell}}\sum_{d,n=1}^{\infty}r(k_{1}k_{2}^{2}\cdots k_{\ell}^{\ell}d)r(n)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{d}{n}\Big)^{it}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t.

Then, we have the following lower bound for
I_{2}
:

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\,\geq\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\cdots a_{k_{\ell}}r(k_{1})r(k_{2})^{2}\cdots r(k_{\ell})^{\ell}\sum_{d,n=1}^{\infty}r(d)r(n)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{d}{n}\Big)^{it}\Phi\Big(\frac{\log T}{T}t\Big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\,=I_{1}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\cdots a_{k_{\ell}}r(k_{1})r(k_{2})^{2}\cdots r(k_{\ell})^{\ell}.

This follows from the fact that
r(n)
is completely multiplicative, together with the definition of
I_{1}
(
3.4
). Therefore, we obtain

(3.6)

\frac{I_{2}}{I_{1}}\geq\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\cdots a_{k_{\ell}}r(k_{1})r(k_{2})^{2}\cdots r(k_{\ell})^{\ell}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\Big(\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{j}}r(k_{j})^{j}\Big).

For the inner sum on the right-hand side of (
3.6
), by the definition of
a(n)
and
r(n)
, the sum vanishes whenever a prime divisor
p
of
n
exceeds
X
. Therefore, it can be written as a short Euler product
\sum_{k_{j}=1}^{\infty}a_{k_{j}}r(k_{j})^{j}=\prod_{p\leq X}(1-r(p)^{j}p^{-1})^{-1}
. Next,

\displaystyle\frac{I_{2}}{I_{1}}

\displaystyle\,\geq\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\prod_{p\leq X}\Big(1-\frac{r(p)^{j}}{p}\Big)^{-1}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\prod_{p\leq X}\Big(\frac{p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{p-1}{p-r(p)^{j}}\Big)

\displaystyle\,=\Big\{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\prod_{p\leq X}\frac{p}{p-1}\Big\}\cdot\Big\{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\prod_{p\leq X}\frac{p-1}{p-r(p)^{j}}\Big\}\eqqcolon\mathcal{P}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{2}.

Then, we need to evaluate
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
, respectively. First, Mertens’ theorem shows that

\prod_{p\leq X}\frac{p}{p-1}=e^{\gamma}\log X+O(1)=e^{\gamma}(\log_{2}T+\log_{3}T)+O(1).

Thus, we can compute
\mathcal{P}_{1}
directly and get

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}

\displaystyle\,=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\big(e^{\gamma}(\log_{2}T+\log_{3}T)+O(1)\big)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\,=e^{\ell\gamma}\big((\log_{2}T)^{\ell}+\ell(\log_{2}T)^{\ell-1}\log_{3}T+O\big((\log_{2}T)^{\ell-2}(\log_{3}T)^{2}\big)\big).

On the other hand, for
\mathcal{P}_{2}
, we will show the following effective lower bound:

(3.8)

\mathcal{P}_{2}\geq 1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log_{2}T}\Big).

In fact, it is clear that

\prod_{p\leq X}\frac{p-1}{p-r(p)^{j}}=\exp\Big(\sum_{p\leq X}\log\frac{p-1}{p-r(p)^{j}}\Big)=\exp\Big(-\sum_{p\leq X}\log\Big(1+\frac{1-r(p)^{j}}{p-1}\Big)\Big).

Noting that
r(p)^{j}=(1-p/X)^{j}\geq 1-jp/X
for all
p\leq X
, we use the prime number theory and obtain

\prod_{p\leq X}\frac{p-1}{p-r(p)^{j}}\geq\exp\Big(-\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{j}{X}\cdot\frac{p}{p-1}\Big)=\exp\Big((-j+o(1))\frac{1}{\log X}\Big).

Since
X=(\log T\log_{2}T)/6
, we have
1/\log X\to 0
as
T\to\infty
. Therefore,

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\prod_{p\leq X}\frac{p-1}{p-r(p)^{j}}\geq\exp\Big(\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}j+o(1)\Big)\frac{1}{\log X}\Big)=1+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log_{2}T}\Big).

This shows that (
3.8
) holds.

As a result, we get

(3.9)

\frac{I_{2}}{I_{1}}\geq e^{\ell\gamma}\big((\log_{2}T)^{\ell}+\ell(\log_{2}T)^{\ell-1}\log_{3}T+O\big((\log_{2}T)^{\ell-1}\big)\big)

by using (
3.6
), (
3
) and (
3.8
). Finally, by substituting (
3.9
) into (
3.5
) and combining with (
3.1
), we prove Theorem
1.1

6.
Final comments

Levinson
[
13
]
noted that joint extreme values for

\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\zeta(1/2+ijt)
and
\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}1/\zeta(1+ijt)
can also be obtained.
However, the long resonance method employed in this paper is no longer applicable in that setting.
Since
1/\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mu(n)n^{-s}
, “positivity” is lost due to the presence of the Möbius function
\mu(n)
, a property crucial in discarding many terms when establishing lower bounds. The method described in this paper is not applicable when
\sigma=1/2
, as there are infinitely many zeros on the critical line. To overcome this, a convolution formula analogous to that in
[
3
]
would need to be established, along with a computation of a more elaborate resonator
R(t)
. In addition, the maximum of
|\zeta(1/2+it)|
was predicted earlier by Farmer et al.
[
8
]
. This problem is left for future work.

Following the work of Aistleitner et al.
[
2
]
, Dixit and Mahatab
[
5
]
also studied lower bounds for a general family of
L
-functions on the
1
-line. Hence, it is possible to apply our method to obtain analogous results for these
L
-functions.

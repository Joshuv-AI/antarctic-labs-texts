Precision of an autonomous demon exploiting nonthermal resources and information
Juliette Monsel, Matteo Acciai, Didrik Palmqvist, Nicolas Chiabrando, Rafael Sánchez, Janine Splettstoesser

Abstract
Quantum-dot systems serve as nanoscale heat engines exploiting thermal fluctuations to perform a useful task. Here, we investigate a multi-terminal triple-dot system, operating as a refrigerator that extracts heat from a cold electronic contact. In contrast to standard heat engines, this system exploits a nonthermal resource. This has the intriguing consequence that cooling can occur without extracting energy from the resource on average —a seemingly demonic action— while, however, requiring the resource to fluctuate . Using full counting statistics and stochastic trajectories, we analyze the performance of the device in terms of the cooling-power precision, employing performance quantifiers motivated by the thermodynamic and kinetic uncertainty relations. We focus on two regimes with large output power, which are based on two operational principles: exploiting information on one hand and the nonthermal properties of the resource on the other. We show that these regimes significantly differ in precision. In particular, the regime exploiting the nonthermal properties of the resource can have cooling-power fluctuations that are suppressed with respect to the input fluctuations by an order of magnitude. We also substantiate the interpretation of the two different working principles by analyzing cross-correlations between input and output heat currents and information flow.

II
Theoretical approach

In this section, we introduce the model of the studied triple-dot system. The upper part forms a nonthermal resource region capacitively coupled to a working substance, where the resource is used to achieve cooling via energy filtering. While a generic nonthermal resource could serve as input, we here, for simplicity, take a mixture of two thermal reservoirs at different temperatures to emulate the nonthermal resource. We also introduce the observables of interest and show how to compute them using full counting statistics based on an extended master equation approach.

II.1
Model

We consider a setup made of three capacitively coupled quantum dots, as depicted in Fig.
1
.
The lower part of this setting represents the working substance, which will here be operated as a refrigerator; it consists of the lower quantum dot W connected to two electronic contacts. The upper part constitutes the nonthermal resource region, consisting of the upper two dots H and C in contact with two further separate electronic contacts.
Such a system has previously been considered as a model for peculiar thermodynamic operations

;

;

;

For each dot, we consider a single level

, with

, and assume that the intradot Coulomb interaction is larger than all other energy scales of the system. We therefore exclude double occupation

The Hamiltonian describing the three quantum dots is then

\hat{H}=\sum_{i=\text{H},\text{W},\text{C}}\epsilon_{i}\hat{n}_{i}+U_{\text{H}}\hat{n}_{\text{H}}\hat{n}_{\text{W}}+U_{\text{C}}\hat{n}_{\text{C}}\hat{n}_{\text{W}}+U\hat{n}_{\text{H}}\hat{n}_{\text{C}}\ ,

(1)

denotes the electron-number operator in dot

U_{\text{H}},U_{\text{C}},U
are interdot interactions.
Furthermore, we assume that the interaction energy between the two upper dots, H and C, is very large,
U\to\infty
, so that double occupation of the resource dots is excluded. This simplification is convenient to separate the effects of their respective fluctuations on the system dynamics, but the observed features persist even for finite
U
, see Ref.
Whitney2016Jan
.

Each dot of the resource region is tunnel-coupled to a single terminal, that is a large fermionic reservoir, characterized by a temperature
T_{\alpha}
and chemical potential
\mu_{\alpha}
, with
\alpha=\text{C},\text{H}
. We choose to have
T_{\mathrm{C}}<T_{\mathrm{H}}
and therefore call the corresponding terminals the cold and hot resource reservoirs. Note again that the nonthermal resource created by this upper subsystem does not rely on being constructed from thermal reservoirs. However, here we use this scheme for convenience.
The dot W, in the lower, working-substance part of the setup, is tunnel-coupled to two terminals, called left (L) and right (R), characterized by temperatures
T_{\mathrm{L}}
,
T_{\mathrm{R}}
, and chemical potentials
\mu_{\text{L}}
,
\mu_{\text{R}}
, respectively. In the following, we denote the average temperature of the working substance reservoirs by
\bar{T}=(T_{\mathrm{L}}+T_{\mathrm{R}})/2
. Without loss of generality, we set the energy references such that
\mu_{\text{L}}=\mu_{\text{C}}=\mu_{\text{H}}=0
. Furthermore, since we are interested in operating the working substance as a refrigerator, we consider a temperature difference
\delta T=T_{\mathrm{L}}-T_{\mathrm{R}}\geq 0
, aiming at cooling down the right reservoir, and set
\mu_{\text{R}}\equiv 0
.
With these parameters, the four reservoirs are characterized by Fermi functions
f_{\alpha}(E)=1/(1+\text{e}^{(E-\mu_{\alpha})/k_{\mathrm{B}}T_{\alpha}})
.

The coupling between the quantum dots and the electronic reservoirs is characterized by the tunnel-coupling strengths
\Gamma_{\alpha}
. While we assume the tunnel couplings
\Gamma_{\text{C}},\Gamma_{\text{H}}
of the resource region to be constant, those of the working substance depend on the energy at which the electrons tunnel. Since this energy is fully determined by the occupations

of the resource dots H and C, we denote these tunnel rates
\Gamma_{\text{L}/\text{R}}^{hc}
.
In this work, we consider the weak-coupling (sequential-tunneling) regime, i.e.,
\hbar\Gamma_{\alpha}\ll k_{\text{B}}T_{\alpha}
, and, in the following, we use the convention
\hbar,k_{\text{B}}\equiv 1

II.2
Observables of interest

We are interested in the operation of the engine as a refrigerator and aim to analyze the cooling power, the efficiency, and, in particular, the precision. The observables of interest are hence the heat currents

J_{\alpha}^{Q}=J^{E}_{\alpha}-\mu_{\alpha}J^{N}_{\alpha}

(2)

defined by the energy currents,
J^{E}_{\alpha}

out of
the reservoir. In the stationary regime of interest here, for the resource particle currents, we always have

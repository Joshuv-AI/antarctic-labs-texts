On the zero-free region for the chromatic polynomial of graphs with maximum degree Δ \Delta and girth g g
Paula M. S. Fialho, Emanuel Juliano, Aldo Procacci

Abstract
The purpose of the present paper is to provide, for all pairs of integers ( Δ , g ) (\Delta,g) with Δ ≥ 3 \Delta\geq 3 and g ≥ 3 g\geq 3 , a positive number C ⁡ ( Δ , g ) C(\Delta,g) such that chromatic polynomial P 𝔾 ​ ( q ) P_{\mathbb{G}}(q) of a graph with maximum degree Δ \Delta and finite girth g g is free of zero if | q | ≥ C ⁡ ( Δ , g ) |q|\geq C(\Delta,g) . Our bounds enlarge the zero-free region in the complex plane of P 𝔾 ​ ( q ) P_{\mathbb{G}}(q) in comparison to previous bounds. In particular, for small values of Δ \Delta our estimates yield a sensible improvement on the bounds recently obtained by Jenssen, Patel and Regts in [ 8 ] , while they coincide with those of [ 8 ] when Δ → ∞ \Delta\to\infty .



1
Introduction and results

Given a graph
{\mathbb{G}}=({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{E}})
, a coloring of
{\mathbb{V}}
with
q\in\mathbb{N}
colors is a function
\kappa:{\mathbb{V}}\to[q]
. A coloring of
{\mathbb{V}}
is called
proper
if for any edge
\{x,y\}\in{\mathbb{E}}
it holds that

\kappa(x)\neq\kappa(y)
. Letting
{\mathcal{K}}^{*}_{\mathbb{V}}(q)
be the set of all proper colorings of
{\mathbb{V}}
with
q
colors, the quantity
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q):=|{\mathcal{K}}^{*}_{\mathbb{V}}(q)|
, i.e. the number of proper colorings with
q
colors of the graph
\mathbb{G}
, is, as a function of
q
, a polynomial known as the
chromatic polynomial
of
{\mathbb{G}}
.

It is long known (see
[
9
]
,
[
14
]
and references therein) that
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)

coincides with the partition function of the antiferromagnetic
Potts model with
q
states on
\mathbb{G}
at zero temperature, which in turn can be written in terms of a “polymer gas” partition function
(see e.g. Proposition 2.1 in
[
14
]
). Namely,

P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)=q^{|{\mathbb{V}}|}\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)

(1.1)

where
\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
is the grand canonical partition function of a “polymer gas”, in which the “polymers” are subsets
R\subset\mathbb{V}
, with cardinality
|R|\geq 2
, subjected to a non-overlapping constraint and with activities
z(R,q)
that depend on the topological structure
of
\mathbb{G}
. Explicitly,

\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)=1+\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\{R_{1},\dots,R_{k}\}:\,R_{i}\subset{\mathbb{V}}\atop|R_{i}|\geq 2,\;R_{i}\cap R_{j}=\emptyset}\prod_{i=1}^{k}z(R_{i},q)

(1.2)

with

z(R,q)={1\over q^{|R|-1}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}}(-1)^{|E_{g}|}

(1.3)

where
{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}
is the restriction of
{\mathbb{G}}
to
R
(i.e.
{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}=(R,{\mathbb{E}}|_{R})
where
{\mathbb{E}}|_{R}=\{\{x,y\}\subset R:\{x,y\}\in{\mathbb{E}}\}
) and
\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
represents the set of all connected spanning subgraphs of
{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}
.

Therefore to find zero-free regions of
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
when
q\in\mathbb{C}
it is equivalent to find zero-free regions of the partition
function
\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
of the above polymer gas. The latter is a classical problem in statistical mechanics, deeply related to phase transitions.

For a general finite graph
{\mathbb{G}}
,
whose sole information is its maximum degree
\Delta
, Sokal
[
14
]
proved that all the zeros of
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
lie
in the disc
|q|<C_{\rm So}(\Delta)
, where
C_{\rm So}(\Delta)
is an explicitly computable function of
\Delta

(see
[
14
]
Table 1, some values for
C_{\rm So}(\Delta)
). He also proved that
C_{\rm So}(\Delta)\leq 7.963906\Delta
for any
\Delta
. The strategy followed by Sokal to prove his results was to find values of
q\in\mathbb{C}
such that the absolute value of the logarithm of the partition function
\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
defined in (
1.2
) is bounded (and therefore
\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)\neq\emptyset
). To this end, he used the so-called Dobrushin criterion
[
3
]
for the convergence of the cluster expansion of the abstract polymer gas. Later, following the same strategy developed by Sokal, Fernández and Procacci
[
5
]
improved the results of Sokal using their improved version of the Dobrushin criterion given in
[
4
]
, and bounding the activities
z(q,R)
given in (
1.3
) using the so called Penrose identity
[
10
]
(see also ahead). In
[
5
]
the authors provide a function
C_{\rm FP}(\Delta)
(also explicitly computable)
which is, for any
\Delta
, smaller than the Sokal function
C_{\rm So}(\Delta)
and bounded above by
6.9077\Delta
. Moreover,
C_{\rm FP}(\Delta)

can be done even smaller by using some further information about the structure of the graph
{\mathbb{G}}
beyond its maximum degree.
(see Section 2 of
[
5
]
, Table 1).

Very recently, Jenssen, Patel and Regts
[
8
]
, using a representation of
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)

known as Whitney broken circuit Theorem
[
15
]
,
have improved the bound given in
[
5
]
for the zero-free region of the chromatic polynomial. In
[
8
]
the authors show, by an inductive method
a la
Dobrushin inspired by the proof of Proposition 3.1 in
[
1
]
, that for any
graph
{\mathbb{G}}
with maximum degree
\Delta
and girth
g\geq 3
the chromatic polynomial
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)

is free of zero outside the disk
|q|<K_{g}\Delta
,
where

K_{g}=\min_{a\in(0,1)}\left({b_{g}(a)\over(1-a)\ln b_{g}(a)}\right)

(1.4)

with
b_{g}(a)
being the unique solution of the equation

\exp\left\{{(1-a)\ln b\over b}\right\}-1+{b(\ln b)^{g-1}\over 2(1-{\ln b})}=a

(1.5)

in the interval
b\in[1,e)
.
By the above definitions,
K_{g}
is a decreasing and explicitly computable function of
g

(see Figure 1 in
[
8
]
for some values of
K_{g}
for
3\leq g\leq 100
). In particular, via (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), one gets that
K_{3}=5.93148
, which improves the asymptotic bound
C_{\rm FP}(\Delta)\leq 6.9077\Delta
obtained in
[
5
]
, and that
\lim_{g\to\infty}K_{g}=3.85977
.

As
Jenssen Patel and Regts comment (see Section 5 in
[
8
]
), the zero-free region of the chromatic polynomial
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
established in their paper could be improved for graphs with small maximum degree
\Delta
.
The purpose of this work is precisely this. Namely, we provide, for all pairs of integers
(\Delta,g)
with
\Delta\geq 3
and
g\geq 3
, a positive number
C(\Delta,g)

such that the chromatic polynomial
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
of a graph
{\mathbb{G}}
with maximum degree
\Delta
and finite girth
g
is free of zero if
|q|\geq C(\Delta,g)
where the function
C(\Delta,g)
is such that
C(\Delta,g)<K_{g}\Delta
for all
\Delta
.

Our results are summarized through the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1

Given a graph
{\mathbb{G}}=({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{E}})
with maximum degree
\Delta\geq 3
and finite girth
g\geq 3
,
there exists an explicitly computable constant

C(\Delta,g)
such that
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)\not=0
for any
q\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying

|q|\geq C(\Delta,g).

Theorem 1.2

For any fixed
g\geq 3
, the sequence
\left\{{C(\Delta,g)/\Delta}\right\}_{\Delta\geq 3}
is monotonic increasing and

\lim_{\Delta\to\infty}{C(\Delta,g)\over\Delta}\leq K_{g}

where
K_{g}
is the
Jenssen-Patel-Regst
constant defined in (
1.4
).

The analytic procedure that must be followed to explicitly compute the constant
C(\Delta,g)
as a function of
\Delta
and
g
will be given in the next section.

The bounds obtained in Theorem
1.1

enlarge the zero-free region of the chromatic polynomial
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
in comparison to the bounds recently given in
[
8
]
. In particular,
for small values of
\Delta
our estimative yields a sensible improvement on
[
8
]
, while it coincides with that of
[
8
]
when
\Delta\to\infty
.

The strategy used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
follows closely that developed in
[
8
]
. In particular,
as in
[
8
]
, we use the representation of
\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
given by
the Whitney Broken Circuit Theorem (see ahead) and then we revisit and adapt the inductive strategy developed in
[
8
]

to accommodate efficiently the case in which
\Delta
is not large.

2
The construction of the constant
C(\Delta,g)

Given an integer
\Delta\geq 3
, let

\rho_{\Delta}=\left(\Delta-1\over\Delta-2\right)^{\Delta}~~~~{\rm and}~~~~R_{\Delta}={(\Delta-2)^{\Delta-2}\over(\Delta-1)^{\Delta-1}}~.

(2.1)

Define, for any
b\in[1,\rho_{\Delta})
,

x_{\Delta}(b)={b^{2\over\Delta}-b^{1\over\Delta}\over b}

(2.2)

and note that the quantity
x_{\Delta}(b)
is an increasing function of
b\in[1,\rho_{\Delta})
, varying from 0 to
R_{\Delta}
.

Define then, for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Delta\geq 3
and
b\in[1,\rho_{\Delta})
,

f^{d}_{\Delta}(b)=\displaystyle{{1\over(\Delta-1)}~{[(\Delta-1)(1-b^{-{1\over\Delta}})]^{d}\over[1-(\Delta-1)(1-b^{-{1\over\Delta}})]}}~.

(2.3)

Moreover, given integers
\Delta\geq 3
and
g\geq 3
, we define, for
a\in[0,1]
,
b\in[1,\rho_{\Delta})
, the function

K^{g}_{\Delta}(a,b)=\Big[1+{(1-a)}x_{\Delta}(b)\Big]^{\Delta}-1+{f^{g-2}_{\Delta}(b)}{\Delta\choose 2}bx_{\Delta}(b)(1+bx_{\Delta}(b))^{\Delta-1}.

(2.4)

and we set

b_{\Delta}^{g}(a)=\sup\{b\in[1,\rho_{\Delta}):~K^{g}_{\Delta}(a,b)\leq a\},

(2.5)

z_{\Delta}^{g}(a)=(1-a)x_{\Delta}(b_{\Delta}^{g}(a)).

(2.6)

Lemma 2.1

For any fixed
a\in[0,1]
and for any
\Delta\geq 3
, the quantity
b_{\Delta}^{g}(a)
is the unique solution of the equation

K^{g}_{\Delta}(a,b)=a
as
b
varies in the interval
[1,\rho_{\Delta})
, where
\rho_{\Delta}
is defined in (
2.1
). Moreover, for all
\Delta\geq 3
and
all
g\geq 3
we have that
\max_{a\in[0,1]}{z^{g}_{\Delta}(a)}
is a well defined number.

Proof:

Note that, for any fixed
a\in[0,1]
and for any
\Delta\geq 3
, the function
K^{g}_{\Delta}(a,b)
given in (
2.4
)
is a continuous increasing function of
b\in[1,\rho_{\Delta})
such that
K^{g}_{\Delta}(a,1)=0
and
\lim_{b\to\rho_{\Delta}}K^{g}_{\Delta}(a,b)=+\infty
. Therefore,
b_{\Delta}^{g}(a)
is the unique solution of the equation
K^{g}_{\Delta}(a,b)=a
as
b
varies in the interval
[1,\rho_{\Delta})
.
Observe also that function
b_{\Delta}^{g}(a)
in the domain
a\in[0,1]
is defined implicitly via the equation
K_{\Delta}^{g}(a,b)-a=0
with
K_{\Delta}^{g}(a,b)-a
continuous in
a\times b\in[0,1]\times[1,\rho_{\Delta})
. Therefore
b_{\Delta}^{g}(a)
is continuous in the interval
a\in[0,1]
.
Consequently,
z_{\Delta}^{g}(a)
, as product and composition of continuous functions of
a
, is in turn a continuous function of
a
in the interval
a\in[0,1]
and moreover
z_{\Delta}^{g}(0)=z_{\Delta}^{g}(1)=0
. This implies that
\max_{a\in[0,1]}{z^{g}_{\Delta}(a)}
is a well defined number for all
\Delta\geq 3
and all
g\geq 3
.

\Box

3.1
An alternative representation for
\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)

The present subsection, based in
[
6
]
, aims to rewrite function
\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
, defined in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) in a
more convenient manner in terms of a sum over forests. In order to do that, let us first establish some notations and recall some definitions regarding subgraphs, trees and forests of a graph
{\mathbb{G}}
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and given a finite set
X
, let
|X|
denote its cardinality.
Along this paper,
{\mathbb{G}}=({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{E}})
represents a fixed simple graph with vertex set
{\mathbb{V}}
and edge set
{\mathbb{E}}
.
A graph
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}=(\tilde{\mathbb{V}},\tilde{\mathbb{E}})
is a subgraph of
{\mathbb{G}}=({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{E}})
if
\tilde{\mathbb{V}}\subseteq{\mathbb{V}}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\subseteq{\mathbb{E}}
. A subgraph
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}

of
{\mathbb{G}}
is called spanning if
\tilde{\mathbb{V}}={\mathbb{V}}
. A graph
{\mathbb{G}}=({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{E}})

is
connected

if for any pair
B,C
of subsets of
{\mathbb{V}}
such that

B\cup C={\mathbb{V}}
and
B\cap C=\emptyset
, there is at least an edge
e\in{\mathbb{E}}
such
that
e\cap B\neq\emptyset
and
e\cap C\neq\emptyset
.
A connected component of
{\mathbb{G}}
is a maximal connected subgraph of
{\mathbb{G}}
.
A tree is a connected graph with no cycles.
A subset
R\subset{\mathbb{V}}
is said to be connected if
{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}
is connected; we denote by
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{V}}
the set of all connected subsets of
{\mathbb{G}}
with cardinality greater than one, i.e.

\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{V}}=\{R\subseteq{\mathbb{V}}:\;{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}\;\mbox{is connected and}\;|R|\geq 2\}
.

We denote by
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{G}}
the set of (not necessarily spanning) trees which are subgraphs of
{\mathbb{G}}
.
An element
\tau\in\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{G}}
will be regarded as a collection of edges and we concur that the empty set is an element of
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{G}}
. An element
\tau\in\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{G}}
distinct from the empty set is said to be non trivial. The number of edges of a non trivial tree
\tau
will be indicated by
|\tau|
, and the set of vertices of a non trivial
\tau
will be denoted by
V_{\tau}
. A forest in
{\mathbb{G}}
is a subgraph of
{\mathbb{G}}
whose connected components are trees. We denote by
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathbb{G}}
the set of
all forests in
{\mathbb{G}}
and we agree that the empty set belongs to
\mathfrak{F}_{\mathbb{G}}
.
An element
F\in\mathfrak{F}_{\mathbb{G}}
distinct from the empty set (i.e. a non trivial forest) is thus, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a collection
\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{k}\}
of non trivial trees such that

V_{\tau_{i}}\cap V_{\tau_{j}}=\emptyset
for all pairs
\{i,j\}\subset[k]
. A non trivial forest
F
of
{\mathbb{G}}
will
be treated as a collection of edges. We denote by

|F|
the number of edges in
F
and by
V_{F}
the subset formed by all vertices of
{\mathbb{G}}
belonging to the non trivial connected components of
F
. Namely, if
F
is the collection

\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{k}\}
, then
V_{F}=\cup_{i=1}^{k}V_{\tau_{i}}
and
|F|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{|\tau_{i}|}
.

Given
R\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{V}}
, recall that
\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
represents the set of all connected spanning subgraphs of
{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}
, and let us denote by

\mathcal{T}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
the set formed by all spanning trees of
{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}
.

We now rewrite the sum in the r.h.s. of (
1.3
) using a beautiful and long known identity discovered by O. Penrose in 1967
[
10
]
.
This identity is based on the existence of a map from
\mathcal{T}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
to
\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
,
called
partition scheme
(see e.g.
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
7
]
).

Partition scheme in
{\mathbb{G}}
.

Given
R\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{V}}
, a partition scheme in
{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}
is a map
{\mathrm{m}_{R}}:\mathcal{T}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}\to\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}

such that for all
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
, it holds that

\tau\subset{\mathrm{m}_{R}}(\tau)
and

\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}=\biguplus_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}}[\tau,\mathrm{m}_{R}(\tau)]
, where
\biguplus
stands for disjoint union
and
[\tau,\mathrm{m}_{R}(\tau)]=\{g\in\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}:\tau\subseteq g\subseteq{\mathrm{m}_{R}(\tau)}\}
.
A partition scheme in
{\mathbb{G}}
is a family
{\mathrm{\mathbf{m}}}=\{\mathrm{m}_{R}\}_{R\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{V}}}
such that
for any
R\subset\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{V}}
,
\mathrm{m}_{R}
is a partition scheme on
{\cal C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
. For any non trivial
\tau
subtree of
{\mathbb{G}}

such that
V_{\tau}=R
, we will denote shortly

\mathrm{\mathbf{m}}(\tau)=\mathrm{m}_{R}(\tau)
.

Given a partition scheme
{\rm\bf m}
in
{\mathbb{G}}
and given
R\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{V}}
,
the Penrose identity is the following equality

\sum_{g\in\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}}(-1)^{|E_{g}|}=(-1)^{|R|-1}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}\atop{\rm\bf m}(\tau)=\tau}1.

(3.1)

See e.g.
[
4
]
for the proof.
Using (
3.1
) we can rewrite
the activity
z(R,q)
of a polymer
R
, given in (
1.3
), as follows

z(R,q)=\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}\atop{\rm\bf m}(\tau)=\tau}\left(-{1\over q}\right)^{|\tau|},

(3.2)

Inserting (
3.2
) into (
1.2
) we get that

\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
can be rewritten
as

\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)=\sum_{F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\rm\bf m}_{\mathbb{G}}}\left(-{1\over q}\right)^{|F|},

(3.3)

where the symbol
\mathfrak{F}^{{\rm\bf m}}_{\mathbb{G}}
denotes the set of all forests in
{\mathbb{G}}
such that any non trivial tree
\tau
of the forest obeys

{\rm\bf m}(\tau)=\tau
and again in the sum in the r.h.s. of (
3.3
) the empty set contributes with the factor “1”.

In conclusion, recalling (
1.1
), we get that the chromatic polynomial
P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
of a graph
{\mathbb{G}}=({\mathbb{V}},{\mathbb{E}})
can be written as

P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)=q^{|{\mathbb{V}}|}\sum_{F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\rm\bf m}_{\mathbb{G}}}\left(-{1\over q}\right)^{|F|}

(3.4)

where
{\rm\bf m}
is any partition scheme in
{\mathbb{G}}
.

It is remarkable to observe that,
although the set
\mathfrak{F}^{\rm\bf m}
depends strongly on the partition scheme
{\rm\bf m}
used,
the identity (
3.4
) holds independently of
{\rm\bf m}
.
Various partition schemes are available (see e.g.
[
13
]
, Sec. 2.2 and references therein), in particular, in applications on statistical mechanics and combinatorics,
as far as we know, two partition schemes have been used: the first one is the so called Penrose partition scheme, originally presented in
[
10
]
; the second one is the minimal-tree partition scheme (described e.g. in the proof of Lemma 2.2. in
[
13
]
). The Penrose partition scheme was used in
[
4
]
to improve the Dobrushin criterion for the convergence of the cluster expansion on the abstract polymer and in
[
2
]
to improve the Lovász local lemma.
The minimal tree partition scheme was used in
[
11
]
to improve the lower bound of the convergence radius of the Mayer Series on a gas of
classical particles in the continuum interacting via a stable and regular pair potential.

We will use here the representation (
3.3
) of the function
\Xi_{\mathbb{G}}(q)
with the partition scheme
{\rm\bf m}
set to be equal the above mentioned minimal tree partition scheme, which we define here below.

Minimal tree partition scheme in
{\mathbb{G}}
.
Choose a total order
\succ
in the set
{\mathbb{E}}
. For any
R\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{V}}
, let
\mu_{R}:\mathcal{T}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}\to\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
be the map that associates to each
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
the graph
\mu_{R}(\tau)\in\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}

whose edge set is
E_{\mu_{R}(\tau)}=\tau\cup E_{\tau}^{*}
, where
E_{\tau}^{*}
is composed by all edges
\{x,y\}\subset{\mathbb{E}}|_{R}\setminus\tau

such that
\{x,y\}\succ\{z,u\}
for every edge
\{z,u\}\in\tau
belonging to the path from
x
to
y
through
\tau
.
It is easy to show that
\mu_{R}
is a partition scheme in
\mathcal{C}_{{\mathbb{G}}|_{R}}
(see e.g.
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
), and thus

Observe that

in

In graph theory a

in the graph

is a set of edges of

obtained by removing from some cycle in
{\mathbb{G}}
its largest edge (in the chosen order).
Therefore, the condition

in (

In conclusion, denoting by

whose non trivial trees are broken-circuit free, we get, by (

), that

P_{\mathbb{G}}(q)=q^{|{\mathbb{V}}|}\sum_{F\in\mathfrak{F}^{\bm{\mu}}_{\mathbb{G}}}\left(-{1\over q}\right)^{|F|}.

(3.5)

The rewriting (

is known as

[
15

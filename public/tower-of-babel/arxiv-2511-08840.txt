Brauer groups of abelian varieties over fields of finite characteristic
Livia Grammatica, Alexei N. Skorobogatov, Yuan Yang

Abstract
We study the Brauer group of an abelian variety A A over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p p focusing on the p p -primary torsion, the key part of which is a certain quasi-algebraic unipotent group 𝐔 A {\bf U}_{A} . We determine its dimension and obtain a sharp upper bound for its p p -exponent. The isogeny class of 𝐔 A {\bf U}_{A} is classified for abelian varieties A A of dimension at most 3. For principally polarised abelian varieties we compute the dimension of the p p -torsion subgroup of 𝐔 A {\bf U}_{A} in terms of the Ekedahl–Oort type of A A .



Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p\geqslant 0
.
In this paper we are interested in the following question:
what is the Brauer group of an abelian variety
A
over
k
? In particular,
what is the
p
-primary torsion subgroup
{\rm{Br}}(A)[p^{\infty}]
when
p>0
?

Let us start by recalling the structure of the Brauer group of an arbitrary smooth proper variety
X

over
k
.
In
[
GB
, III]
Grothendieck proved that
{\rm{Br}}(X)
is a torsion group
and computed
{\rm{Br}}(X)[\ell^{\infty}]

for primes
\ell\neq p
. The exactness of the Kummer sequence with
coefficients
\mu_{\ell^{n}}
for the étale topology gives an isomorphism

{\rm{Br}}(X)[\ell^{\infty}]\cong({\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell})^{b_{2}-\rho}\oplus{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}(1))[\ell^{\infty}],

where
b_{2}={\rm{dim}}_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}}{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is the second
\ell
-adic Betti number,

\rho={\rm{rk}}\,{\rm NS\,}(X)
is the Picard number, and

{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}(1))[\ell^{\infty}]
is finite, see
[
CTS21
, Prop. 5.2.9]
.
When
X=A
is an abelian variety of dimension
g>0
,
we have
b_{2}=g(2g-1)
and
{\rm H}^{3}_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}(A,{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}(1))[\ell^{\infty}]=0
.
In particular, the abelian group
{\rm{Br}}(A)[\ell^{\infty}]
is an invariant of the isogeny class of
A
.

The
p
-primary torsion subgroup
{\rm{Br}}(X)[p^{\infty}]
in characteristic
p>0

has more interesting structure.
Let
W=W(k)
be the ring of Witt vectors of
k
.
Define
{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))=\varprojlim{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm fppf}(X,\mu_{p^{n}})
.
By Illusie, we have
{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))/{\rm tors}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{r_{i}}
,
where
r_{i}
is the multiplicity of slope 1
of the crystalline cohomology group
{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm cris}(X/W)
, see
[
Ill79
, Thm. II.5.5 (5.5.3)]
.
By Illusie–Raynaud
[
IR83
, Thm. IV.3.3 (b), Cor. IV.3.6]

it is known that
{\rm H}^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{\infty}]
is annihilated by a power of
p
.
The exactness of the Kummer sequence with
coefficients
\mu_{p^{n}}
for the fppf topology gives an isomorphism

{\rm{Br}}(X)[p^{\infty}]\cong({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})^{r_{2}-\rho}\oplus{\rm H}^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{\infty}],

(1)

see
[
P24
, Theorem A.1]
. In contrast with the
\ell
-adic case, the group

{\rm H}^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{\infty}]
can be infinite.
A phenomenon specific to finite characteristic is the existence of a natural commutative
quasi-algebraic group over
k
whose group of
k
-points is
{\rm H}^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{\infty}]
.
Its connected component, which is unipotent, will be denoted by
{\bf U}_{X}
.
This goes back to Milne
[
Mil76
, Cor. 2.7 (a)]
, see
[
Ber81
, Cor. 2.10]
,

[
IR83
, Lemme IV.3.2.2]
,
[
Mil86
, Lemma 1.8]
.
Quasi-algebraic groups were introduced by Serre
[
Ser60
, §1.2]
as equivalence classes
of algebraic groups with a common set of
k
-points, where two groups
G
and
H
are called equivalent if there is a morphism
G\to H^{(p^{n})}
which is a bijection on
k
-points.

When
X=A
is an abelian variety, the attached quasi-algebraic group is connected and so is equal
to
{\bf U}_{A}
, see

[
YY
, Thm. 1.5]
based on
[
IR83
, Thm. II.3.4]
(“survie du cœur”).
In particular, the group of
k
-points
{\bf U}_{A}(k)\cong{\rm H}^{3}(A,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{\infty}]
, the dimension

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})
, and the
p
-exponent
\pexp({\bf U}_{A})

(the least integer
m
such that
p^{m}{\bf U}_{A}=0
)
are all well-defined. By general theory
[
Ser59
, VII.10]
,

{\bf U}_{A}
is isogenous to a product of truncated Witt (quasi-algebraic) groups

\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\bf W}_{n_{i}}
,
for some positive integers
n_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant n_{r}
.
We have

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i},\quad{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])=r,\quad\pexp({\bf U}_{A})=n_{r}.

Note that if
\pexp({\bf U}_{A})=1
, then
{\bf U}_{A}\simeq{\bf G}_{a,k}^{d}
, see

[
Ser59
, VII.2, Prop. 11]
.

The guiding question of this paper is: what sequences
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})
occur for abelian varieties
of dimension
g
over an algebraically closed field
k
of positive characteristic
p
?

Using results of Crew and deep results of Ekedahl,
together with the fact that the groups
{\rm H}^{i}_{\rm cris}(A/W)
are torsion-free
and the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence for
A
degenerates at the first page, one obtains

[
YY
, Theorem 1.1, 1.6]

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})=\frac{g(g-1)}{2}-\sum_{0\leqslant\lambda<1}(1-\lambda)m_{\lambda},

(2)

where
g={\rm{dim}}(A)

and
m_{\lambda}
is the multiplicity of slope
\lambda
in
{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm cris}(A/W)
.
In particular,
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})
depends only on the slopes of
A

and thus is an invariant of the isogeny class of
A
.
The meaning of the terms of (
2
) is the following:

{\rm{dim}}_{k}{\rm H}^{0}(A,\Omega^{2}_{A/k})=g(g-1)/2
is the dimension of the tangent space of the formal Brauer group
\widehat{{\rm{Br}}}(A)
,
the sum is the dimension of the maximal quotient of
\widehat{{\rm{Br}}}(A)
of finite height,
and
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})
is the dimension of the unipotent part of
\widehat{{\rm{Br}}}(A)
,
see Section
3.2
. Thus (
2
)
implies the smoothness of the formal Brauer group of any abelian variety over
k

(Corollary
3.3
). Note that
smoothness of
\widehat{{\rm{Br}}}(A)
is a particular case of a general criterion of smoothness
of Artin–Mazur formal groups obtained by the first named author in
[
Gr25b
]
.

From (
2
) we see that
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})\leqslant g(g-1)/2
with equality if and only if
g=1

or
A
is supersingular (Remark
3.2
).
For
g=2
, it gives
{\bf U}_{A}=0
unless
A
is supersingular, in which case

{\bf U}_{A}\cong{\bf G}_{a,k}
, a fact that goes back to Illusie
whose computation was based on the de Rham–Witt cohomology
[
Ill79
, II.7]
. In general,
it is known that
{\bf U}_{A}=0
when
{\rm H}^{2}(A,W{\mathcal{O}}_{A})
is a finitely generated
W
-module,
for example, when
A
is ordinary (i.e., the
p
-rank is
g
) or almost ordinary
(i.e., the
p
-rank is
g-1
), see
[
Ill83
, Corollary 6.3.16]
,
[
YY
, Theorem 3.7]
.
In fact,
{\bf U}_{A}=0
if and only if
A
is ordinary or almost ordinary, see Remark
3.2
.

Let us now describe the main results of this paper and give its brief outline.

•

Developing the approach of
[
Gr25a
]

based on the Nygaard filtration, we express
{\bf U}_{A}
in terms of the F-crystal

{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm cris}(A/W)
, see Corollary
1.7
.
This gives a quick proof that
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})
depends only on the isogeny class of
A

(Proposition
2.6
).
From this we obtain a proof of formula (
2
) that uses only the classical Dieudonné theory and
so avoids the deep theory of Ekedahl, see Theorem
3.1
. At the end of §
3.2

we give a different, short proof of (
2
) based on the smoothness of the formal Brauer group
of
A
.

•

We interpret
{\rm{Br}}(A)
in terms of homomorphisms of truncated
Dieudonné modules. Namely, for
p\neq 2
there is a natural isomorphism of

{\bf U}_{A}[p^{n}]
and the quasi-algebraic group

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M_{A}^{\vee}/p^{n},M_{A}/p^{n})^{\rm skew}/\left({\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M_{A}^{\vee},M_{A})^{\rm skew}/p^{n}\right),

which is a subquotient of the quasi-algebraic group
{\rm{Hom}}_{W(k)}(M_{A}^{\vee}/p^{n},

M_{A}/p^{n})
, see Corollary
2.5
. Here
M_{A}
is the Dieudonné module of
A
,

\mathbb{E}
is the Dieudonné ring (see §
1.1
) and
‘
{\rm skew}
’ stands for the subgroup of anti-self-dual elements.
This gives a different way to reduce the determination of
{\bf U}_{A}
to
a computation with the Dieudonné module
M_{A}
.

•

We prove that the
p
-exponent of
{\bf U}_{A}
is at most
g-1
, where
g={\rm{dim}}(A)
and
p\neq 2
,
see Theorem
3.8
. We show that the bound is sharp by exhibiting,
for every
g\geqslant 1
, a supergeneral (i.e. supersingular with
a
-number 1)
abelian variety
A

of dimension
g
such that
{\bf U}_{A}
is isogenous to
\prod_{n=1}^{g-1}{\bf W}_{n}
. Combining this with
a result of D’Addezio we obtain that if
A
is an abelian variety over a field
k_{0}

finitely generated over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and
k=\overline{k_{0}}
, then the transcendental Brauer group

{\rm{Im}}[{\rm{Br}}(A)\to{\rm{Br}}(A_{k})]
is a direct sum of a finite group and an abelian group annihilated by

p^{g-1}
(Corollary
3.9
).
Moreover, if
A
is supersingular, then the transcendental Brauer group of
A
is annihilated by

p^{g-1}
, whereas if
A
is ordinary or almost ordinary, then this group is finite.

•

We determine the isogeny class of
{\bf U}_{A}
for arbitrary abelian threefolds (assuming
p\neq 2
):
{\bf U}_{A}\simeq{\bf G}_{a,k}^{n}
, where
0\leqslant n\leqslant 3
,
unless
A
is supergeneral, in which case

U_{A}
is isogenous to
{\bf G}_{a,k}\times{\bf W}_{2}
, see Proposition
3.16
.
In particular, the isogeny class of
{\bf U}_{A}
is
not
an invariant of the isogeny class of
A
.

•

We compute
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])
for principally polarised abelian varieties
A

of arbitrary dimension
g\geqslant 1
in terms of the Ekedahl–Oort type of
A[p]
,
see Theorem
4.1
. From this we deduce that in the principally polarised case we have

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})\leqslant r(r+1)/2

and
\pexp({\bf U}_{A})\leqslant r
, where
r={\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])
, see Corollary
4.7
.

Preliminaries

1.1
Notation

In this paper
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
.
We write
W=W(k)
for the ring of Witt vectors, and
denote by
\sigma\colon W\to W
the canonical lifting of the Frobenius
x\to x^{p}
on
k
.

All group schemes in this paper are commutative.
For a finite flat commutative group
k
-scheme
G
we denote by
G^{\vee}
its Cartier dual.
If
A
is an abelian variety, we write
A^{\vee}
for the dual abelian variety.

The Dieudonné ring
{\mathbb{E}}=W_{\sigma}[F,V]
is the ring of non-commutative polynomials in
F
and
V
with coefficients in
W
subject to the relations

FV=VF=p
,
F\lambda=\lambda^{\sigma}F
,
V\lambda=\lambda^{\sigma^{-1}}V
, where
\lambda\in W
.
An
{\mathbb{E}}
-module
M
, which is free and finitely generated as a
W
-module, will be called
a Dieudonné module. The dual Dieudonné module is defined as
M^{\vee}={\rm{Hom}}_{W}(M,W)

with the action of
{\mathbb{E}}
on
f\in M^{\vee}
given by the rules
Ff(x)=\sigma f(Vx)
and
Vf(x)=\sigma^{-1}f(Fx)
,
for any
x\in M
.

1.2
Quasi-algebraic and pro-algebraic groups

Quasi-algebraic groups were introduced by Serre
[
Ser60
]
as a way to have an abelian category of group schemes where infinitesimal groups and radicial morphisms are ignored. This is useful whenever one is interested in working with
k
-points of algebraic groups, rather than algebraic groups themselves, as in our case. Serre’s construction can be interpreted as restricting a group scheme over
k
to the category of perfect schemes over

Let
\pi\colon X\to{\rm{Spec}}(k)
be a proper and smooth

k

{\rm{Spec}}(k)_{\rm perf}
be the

.
The objects of

(X/k)_{\rm perf}
be the

, where

is a perfect

is étale over

, equipped with the étale topology.
By
[

, 2.7]
we have a commutative diagram of topoi, where

is the big étale site of

,
and

are natural restriction maps:

\scriptstyle{\beta_{X}}

\textstyle{X_{\rm Et}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

By definition, perfect
k
-groups are the group objects in
{\rm{Spec}}(k)_{\rm perf}
.
The
perfection

G^{\rm perf}
of a group

-scheme
G
is defined as the inverse limit

\varprojlim G_{\rm red}^{(p^{-n})}
of iterated Frobenius twists
along the Frobenius morphisms
F\colon G_{\rm red}^{(p^{-(n+1)})}\to G_{\rm red}^{(p^{-n})}

.
The restriction
\alpha_{k}\circ\beta_{k}
of the sheaf on

are exact (

).

Let
{\mathcal{G}}[p^{\infty}]
be the category of perfect commutative affine

annihilated by some power of

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be the category of pro-objects of

.
The objects of
{\mathcal{G}}[p^{\infty}]
(respectively,
{\mathcal{P}}
)
can be described as

(respectively,

)

in the sense of Serre
[
Ser60
, §1, §2]
. Serre shows that
{\mathcal{G}}[p^{\infty}]

and
{\mathcal{P}}
are abelian categories, where subobjects are closed subgroups

[
Ser60
, §1.2, Prop. 5, §2.4, Prop. 7]
,
see also
[
Ber81
, 2.2 (a)]
. An example of an object of
{\mathcal{G}}[p^{\infty}]

is
{\bf W}_{n}=(\mathbb{W}_{n})^{\rm perf}
, where
\mathbb{W}_{n}
is the group

n
.
In particular, we have
{\bf G}_{a,k}:={\bf W}_{1}=({\mathbb{G}}_{a,k})^{\rm perf}
.
An example of an object of

is
{\bf W}=\mathbb{W}^{\rm perf}
, see
[

.

Unipotent quasi-algebraic groups from F-crystals

Let
L
be an F-crystal, i.e. a free finitely generated
W
-module with an injective

\sigma
-linear endomorphism
F
. Define

{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)=\{x\in L\text{ such that }p|F(x)\}.

In particular, we have
pL\subset{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)\subset L
.

The F-crystal
L
has a natural structure of a pro-algebraic group
{\bf L}
such that
{\bf L}(k)=L
,
so that
{\bf L}
is isomorphic to
{\bf W}^{n}
where
n={\rm{rk}}_{W}(L)
.
Then
F
naturally gives rise to a
k
-endomorphism
{\bf L}\to{\bf L}

also denoted by
F
. It is the relative Frobenius

F_{{\bf L}/k}\colon{\bf L}\to{\bf L}
followed by a linear map
{\bf L}\to{\bf L}
.

Thus
{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}({\bf L})
is a closed subgroup of
{\bf L}
, and
F/p-1\colon{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}({\bf L})\to{\bf L}

is a morphism in the category of pro-algebraic
k
-groups
{\mathcal{P}}
.
But
{\mathcal{P}}
is an abelian category, so

{\bf U}_{L}:={\rm{Coker}}[F/p-1\colon{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}({\bf L})\to{\bf L}]

(3)

is a pro-algebraic group.

Proposition 1.1

Let
L
be an F-crystal. Then
{\bf U}_{L}
is a connected unipotent quasi-algebraic group.

Proof.
Let
n
be the rank of the free finitely generated
W
-module
L
.
By a standard argument
[
Ill79
, Lemme II.5.6]
, the cokernel of
F/p-1\colon{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)\to L

is a torsion abelian group.
We claim that it is annihilated by
p^{r}
for some
r\geqslant 1
. From this it follows that

{\bf U}_{L}
is the quotient of the connected unipotent quasi-algebraic group
k
-scheme
{\bf W}_{r}^{n}

by a closed subgroup. This implies the proposition.

It remains to prove the claim. We proceed as in
[
Gr25a
, Lemma 5.1.3]
. Since
L/{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)
is killed by
p
, it is enough to prove
that the cokernel of
F-p\colon L\to L
has finite
p
-exponent. By the Manin–Dieudonné
classification, it is enough to prove this for an F-crystal
M
with a
W
-basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}

such that
Fe_{i}=e_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
and
Fe_{r}=p^{s}e_{1}
, where
r>0
and
s\geqslant 0

are coprime integers. There are three possible cases:

(1) If
r=s=1
, then
F-p=p(F/p-1)
, and
(F/p-1)(\lambda e_{1})=(\sigma(\lambda)-\lambda)e_{1}
is surjective by
[
Ill79
, Lemme II.5.3]
.

(2) If
s>r
, then
p^{r}M\subset(F-p)M
. Indeed, if
a\in p^{r}M
, then the infinite sum

-\sum_{i\geqslant 1}F^{i-1}p^{-i}a
converges to some
x\in M
, and we have
a=Fx-px
.

(3) If
s<r
, then
p^{s}M\subset(F-p)M
. Indeed, if
a\in p^{s}M
, then
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}F^{-i}p^{i}a

converges to some
x\in M
, and we have
a=Fx-px
.
\Box

Definition 1.2

We call
{\bf U}_{L}
defined in
(\ref{def1})

the connected unipotent quasi-algebraic group attached to
the F-crystal
L
.

The
p
-exponent of a unipotent quasi-algebraic group
{\bf U}
is the least
non-negative integer
n
such that
p^{n}{\bf U}=0
.

Corollary 1.3

Define
{\bf H}_{L,n}={\rm{Ker}}[F/p-1\colon{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}({\bf L})/p^{n}\to{\bf L}/p^{n}]
. Then we have an exact sequence
of quasi-algebraic
k
-groups

0\to{\bf L}^{F=p}/p^{n}\to{\bf H}_{L,n}\to{\bf U}_{L}[p^{n}]\to 0.

(4)

In particular, for
n
greater or equal to the
p
-exponent of
{\bf U}_{L}
the quasi-algebraic group

{\bf U}_{L}
is naturally isomorphic to the cokernel of
{\bf L}^{F=p}/p^{n}\to{\bf H}_{L,n}
.

Proof.
We have an exact sequence of pro-algebraic groups

0\to{\bf L}^{F=p}\to{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}({\bf L})\xrightarrow{F/p-1}{\bf L}\to{\bf U}_{L}\to 0.

Breaking it into two short exact sequences and applying the snake lemma
to the diagrams formed by multiplication by
p^{n}
, we obtain (
4
).
\Box

Note that
{\bf L}^{F=p}
is finitely generated as a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-module
[
Ill79
, Lemme II.5.11]
,
so
{\bf L}^{F=p}/p^{n}
is finite. Thus the map
{\bf H}_{L,n}\to{\bf U}_{L}[p^{n}]
is an isogeny
of quasi-algebraic groups.

1.3
Quasi-algebraic groups from flat and crystalline cohomology

The sheaves
W_{n}\Omega^{i}
on
X_{\rm{\acute{e}t}}
can be lifted to sheaves
on
(X/k)_{\rm perf}
, again denoted
W_{n}\Omega^{i}
, see
[
IR83
, p. 192]
.
Formation of
W_{n}\Omega^{i}
commutes with perfect base change
[
Ill79
, Prop. I.1.9.2]
.
From this one deduces that the sheaves
R^{j}\pi_{*}(W_{n}\Omega^{i})

on
{\rm{Spec}}(k)_{\rm perf}
belong to

{\mathcal{G}}[p^{n}]
, that is, are represented by quasi-algebraic groups,
see

2.1

Let

.
The
W
-modules

are F-crystals
with the
\sigma
-linear action of

given

F(x\otimes y)=Fx\otimes Fy
and

A natural isomorphism of F-crystals

\wedge^{2}(M\oplus N)\cong(\wedge^{2}M)\oplus(\wedge^{2}N)\oplus(M\otimes N)

gives a natural isomorphism of attached connected unipotent quasi-algebraic

-groups

Remark 2.1

If
N\subset M
is an inclusion of Dieudonné modules, we write
M\wedge N

for the image of
M\otimes N\subset M\otimes M
in
\wedge^{2}M
.

Lemma 2.2

We have
{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(M)=VM
,
{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(M\otimes N)=VM\otimes N+M\otimes VN
,
and
{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(\wedge^{2}M)=M\wedge VM
. In particular, the canonical map

{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(M\otimes M)\to{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(\wedge^{2}M)
is surjective.

Proof.
Since
F
is injective, we have
Fx=py
if and only if
x=Vy
, giving
the first equality.

Let
m={\rm{rk}}_{W}(M)
and
n={\rm{rk}}_{W}(N)
.
We can reduce modulo
p
and consider
k
-vector spaces
M_{1}=M/p
and

N_{1}=N/p
of dimensions
m
and
n
, respectively. Let

m^{\prime}={\rm{dim}}_{k}(M_{1}[F])
and
n^{\prime}={\rm{dim}}_{k}(N_{1}[F])
. Let
K
be
the kernel of
F
acting on
M_{1}\otimes_{k}N_{1}
. It is clear that

M_{1}[F]\otimes_{k}N_{1}+M_{1}\otimes_{k}N_{1}[F]\subset K.

This subspace has dimension
m^{\prime}n+mn^{\prime}-m^{\prime}n^{\prime}=mn-(m-m^{\prime})(n-n^{\prime})
. The dimension of

F(M_{1}\otimes_{k}N_{1})=FM_{1}\otimes_{k}FN_{1}
is

(m-m^{\prime})(n-n^{\prime})=mn-{\rm{dim}}(K)
, so the displayed inclusion is
an equality. Since
M_{1}[F]=VM_{1}
and
N_{1}[F]=VN_{1}
,
we have
K=VM_{1}\otimes_{k}N_{1}+M_{1}\otimes_{k}VN_{1}
.
The proof for
\wedge^{2}M
is similar.
\Box

Note that from
{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(M)=VM
we deduce
{\bf U}_{M}=0
. Indeed, the map

is surjective on
M
.

Remark 2.3

For any Dieudonné module

Comparing with (

We note that
{\rm{Hom}}_{W}(M,N)
has a natural structure of a pro-algebraic group, which is
isomorphic to

Proposition 2.4

Let
M
and
N
be Dieudonné modules. For every

there is a natural isomorphism of quasi-algebraic groups

{\bf U}_{M^{\vee}\otimes N}[p^{n}]\cong{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M/p^{n},N/p^{n})/\left({\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M,N)/p^{n}\right).

Proof.
Applying Corollary
1.3
with
L=M^{\vee}\otimes N={\rm{Hom}}_{W}(M,N)
we obtain that

{\bf U}_{L}[p^{n}]
is naturally isomorphic to the cokernel of
{\bf L}^{F=p}/p^{n}\to{\bf H}_{L,n}
,
where by definition we have
{\bf H}_{L,n}={\rm{Ker}}[F/p-1\colon{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}({\bf L})/p^{n}\to{\bf L}/p^{n}]
.

The Frobenius
F
acts on

by sending
a homomorphism of
W
-modules

to
FfV
.
We have
f\in L^{F=p}
if and only if
pf=FfV
,
which is equivalent to

Vf=fV
, and this implies
Ff=fF
. We conclude that

Next we note that
{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)
is the set of

such that
p
divides
FfV
.
Thus
H_{L,n}=K_{L,n}/p^{n}{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)

K_{L,n}:=\{f\in L\ \text{such that}\ p|FfV(x)\ \text{and}\ p^{n}|FfV(x)/p-f(x)\ \text{for any}\ x\in M\}.

The conjunction of these two conditions is equivalent to

p^{n+1}|FfV(x)-pf(x)

K_{L,n}=\{f\in L\ \text{such that}\ p^{n+1}\ \text{divides}\ FfV(x)-pf(x)\ \text{for any}\ x\in M\}.

Replacing

by
Fx
we deduce that

Ff\equiv fF\bmod p^{n}
. Similarly, applying
V
to both sides we get

fV\equiv Vf\bmod p^{n}
. Thus reducing

modulo
p^{n}
gives a homomorphism

\theta\colon K_{L,n}\to{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M/p^{n},N/p^{n}).

By Corollary

, it remains to prove that

{\rm{Ker}}(\theta)=p^{n}{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)
.

It is clear that
p^{n}{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)\subset{\rm{Ker}}(\theta)

f\in{\rm{Ker}}(\theta)
.
Then

for some

and we have that

divides

. Hence

so that

. Thus
{\rm{Ker}}(\theta)=p^{n}{{\rm F}^{1}_{N}{}}(L)
.

To prove that
\theta
is surjective, take any

and lift it
to some

. We need to find a

such that

, that is, such that
p^{n+1}|FfV-pf-p^{n}F\phi V
.

Since
Ff-fF\equiv 0\bmod p^{n}
, there is a
\sigma
-linear map

such that
Ff-fF=p^{n}g
. This implies

FfV-pf=p^{n}gV
. Likewise, there is a
\sigma^{-1}
-linear map
h\colon M\to N
such that

fV-Vf=p^{n}h
. This implies
FfV-pf=p^{n}Fh
. The condition on
\phi
can now be restated as
the condition that
p
divides
gV-F\phi V=Fh-F\phi V
.

Since
pM\subset VM\subset M
, we can find

-bases

and

of

for

Ve_{i}=pe^{\prime}_{i}

For

For integers

By Proposition

This is a free

. It is enough to check this
for

and for
M=M_{i}
, where
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Let

. We need to check that

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{N})=(c+d)-\sum_{0\leqslant\lambda<1}(1-\lambda)m_{\lambda},

where
\lambda
is a slope of
N

we obtain that the right hand side equals
2c
, and the same is obtained by applying
Proposition
2.9
and Corollary
2.11
.

Now let

where

. Calculating slopes and their multiplicities, we obtain that the right hand side of
the last displayed formula gives

Proposition
2.9

2.11
. This finishes the proof.
\Box

3.2
Relation to the formal Brauer group

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety over
k
, and let
{\bf U}_{X}
be the connected component
of the quasi-algebraic group over
k
whose group of
k
-points is
{\rm H}^{3}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}(1))[p^{\infty}]

(as discussed in the introduction).

In this section we relate
{\bf U}_{X}
to the formal Brauer group of
X
.
The formal Brauer group
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)
is the functor parameterising deformations of the trivial class in

{\rm{Br}}(X)
. For an Artinian
k
-algebra
R
one defines

\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X):={\rm{Ker}}[{\rm{Br}}(X_{R})\to{\rm{Br}}(X_{R_{\textnormal{red}}})],

(17)

where
X_{R}=X\times_{k}{\rm{Spec}}(R)
. A detailed exposition of the formal Brauer group and its properties can be found in
[
AM77
]
.

Proposition 3.4

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety over
k
. The following properties hold.

(1)
If the Picard scheme
{\bf Pic}_{X/k}
is smooth, then
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)
is representable
by a connected formal group of finite type with tangent space
{\rm H}^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

(2)
If
\widehat{{\rm{Br}}}(X)
is representable, then its Cartier module of typical curves
is isomorphic to
{\rm H}^{2}(X,W\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

(3)
If
\widehat{{\rm{Br}}}(X)
is representable and
{\rm H}_{\rm cris}^{3}(X/W)
is torsion-free, then

\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)
is smooth.

Proof.
Parts (1) and (2) are proved in
[
AM77
]
, see
Proposition II.1.8, Corollaries II.2.4 and II.4.3. Part (3) is a consequence of work of Ekedahl
[
Eke85
]
. For a different proof, see
[
Gr25b
, Cor. 3.17]
.
\Box

Suppose that
{\bf Pic}_{X/k}
is smooth, so that
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)
is representable,
and let
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)_{\rm red}
be the reduced subgroup of
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)
.
Recall
[
Ha
, (28.3.2)]

that a smooth connected formal group of finite type is an extension of a smooth connected formal group of finite height by a smooth unipotent formal group, in a unique way.
Let us denote by
\widehat{U}_{X}
the unipotent part of
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)_{\rm red}
, and by
\widehat{P}_{X}
the maximal finite height quotient of
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)_{\rm red}
.
By construction,
\widehat{U}_{X}
is the reduced formal subgroup of
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)[p^{n}]
for large enough
n
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to{\rm{Spec}}(k)
be the structure morphism.
By a theorem of Bragg and Olsson
[
BO21
, Cor. 1.6]
we know that

R^{2}\pi_{*}\mu_{p^{n},X}
is represented by a group
k
-scheme. Let us denote
by
{\mathcal{U}}_{X,n}
its reduced connected component. The exact sequence

1\to\mu_{p^{n},X}\to\mu_{p^{n+1},X}\to\mu_{p,X}\to 1

and the natural isomorphism
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mu_{p^{n},X}\cong{\bf Pic}_{X/k}[p^{n}]

imply that the induced map
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mu_{p^{n},X}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mu_{p^{n+1},X}

is injective. Define
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}:=\varinjlim{\mathcal{U}}_{X,n}
. The dimension of

{\mathcal{U}}_{X,n}
stabilises, so
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}\cong{\mathcal{U}}_{X,n}
for

n
large enough.

Lemma 3.5

The formal group
\widehat{U}_{X}
is canonically isomorphic to the formal
completion of
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}
at the identity.

Proof.
A more general statement is proved in
[
BO21
, version 1, Prop. 10.7]
.
Recall that the formal completion functor maps a presheaf

\mathcal{F}
over
{\rm{Spec}}(k)_{\rm fppf}
to the functor

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:R\mapsto{\rm{Ker}}[\mathcal{F}(R)\to\mathcal{F}(R_{\textnormal{red}})]

from the category of Artinian
k
-algebras to the category of abelian groups. Since the projection
R\to R_{\textnormal{red}}
has a canonical section, the formal completion functor is exact. Pushing forward the Kummer sequence along
\pi:X\to{\rm{Spec}}(k)
we obtain the short exact sequence of fppf sheaves

0\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,X}/p^{n}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mu_{p^{n},X}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,X}[p^{n}]\to 0,

where
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,X}/p^{n}
is representable by an étale group scheme, by the theory of the Picard scheme
[
CTS21
, p. 121]
. Therefore, taking the formal completion we get an isomorphism

(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mu_{p^{n},X})^{\wedge}\cong(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{G}_{m,X}[p^{n}])^{\wedge}.

(18)

Notice that the right-hand group is by definition
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)[p^{n}]
.
Take
n
large enough so that
\widehat{U}_{X}
is the reduced formal subgroup of
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)[p^{n}]
, and such that
{\mathcal{U}}_{X}={\mathcal{U}}_{X,n}
.
Then (
18
) gives a desired isomorphism
({\mathcal{U}}_{X})^{\wedge}\cong\widehat{U}_{X}
.
\Box

By the Kummer sequence, the quasi-algebraic group
({\mathcal{U}}_{X})^{\rm perf}
attached to

{\mathcal{U}}_{X}
is isogenous to
{\bf U}_{X}
. From Lemma
3.5
we obtain

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{X})={\rm{dim}}({\mathcal{U}}_{X})={\rm{dim}}(\widehat{U}_{X})={\rm{dim}}(\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)_{\rm red})-{\rm{dim}}(\widehat{P}_{X}).

(19)

Let us compute
{\rm{dim}}(\widehat{P}_{X})
. By Proposition
3.4
(2), the
Cartier module of typical curves of
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)_{\rm red}

is isomorphic to
{\rm H}^{2}(X,W\mathcal{O}_{X})
.
It follows that, after inverting
p
, the covariant Dieudonné module of
\widehat{P}_{X}

is isomorphic to
{\rm H}^{2}(X,W\mathcal{O}_{X})[1/p]
, hence it is isogenous to

{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm cris}(X/W)_{[0,1)}
by the theory of the slope spectral sequence. It is then well-known
(see, e.g.
[
AM77
, Proof of Cor. III.3.4]
) that

{\rm{dim}}(\widehat{P}_{X})=\sum_{0\leqslant\lambda<1}m_{\lambda}(1-\lambda),

(20)

where
\lambda
is a slope of
{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm cris}(X/W)
of multiplicity
m_{\lambda}
, see Lemma
3.6
below.

Lemma 3.6

Let
G
be a
p
-divisible group. Let
M(G)
be the covariant Dieudonné module of
G
.
We have

{\rm{dim}}(G)=\sum_{\lambda}m_{\lambda}(1-\lambda),

where
\lambda
is a slope of
M(G)
of multiplicity
m_{\lambda}
.

Proof.
The dimension of a
p
-divisible group is an isogeny invariant, so we may assume that
M(G)
is the simple isoclinic Dieudonné module
M_{c,d}
as in the proof of Proposition
2.9
.
Thus
\lambda=c/(c+d)
and
m_{\lambda}=c+d
. We can easily compute

{\rm{dim}}(G)={\rm{dim}}_{k}(M(G)/VM(G))=d=m_{\lambda}(1-\lambda)
.

\Box

Proposition 3.7

Let
X
be a proper smooth variety over
k
such that the Picard scheme

{\bf Pic}_{X/k}
is smooth. Then the formal Brauer group
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)
is smooth if and only if

h^{0,2}(X)={\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{X})+\sum_{\lambda}m_{\lambda}(1-\lambda),

where
m_{\lambda}
is the multiplicity of the slope
\lambda
in
{\rm H}^{2}_{\rm cris}(X/W)_{[0,1)}
.

Proof.
By Proposition
3.4
(1) the tangent space of
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)

has dimension

. Thus

\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)
is smooth if and only if
{\rm{dim}}(\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)_{\rm red})=h^{0,2}(X)
.
It remains to apply (
19
) and (
20
).
\Box

This gives another proof of Theorem
3.1
assuming smoothness of the formal Brauer group
of abelian varieties.

When
X
is a surface such that
{\bf Pic}_{X/k}
is smooth, then
\widehat{\mathrm{Br}}(X)
is smooth
[
AM77
, p. 190]
.
In this case Proposition

follows from
[
Mil75
, Remark 7.4]
.

3.3
The
p
-exponent of
{\bf U}_{A}
is at most
g-1

In this section we prove the following theorem. The non-supersingular case uses Corollary
2.5

and thus requires the assumption
p\neq 2
.

Theorem 3.8

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g\geqslant 1
over an algebraically closed field
k

of positive characteristic
p\neq 2
. Then
p^{g-1}{\bf U}_{A}=0
.

In Section
3.4
we show that the bound of Theorem
3.8
is best possible.

Corollary 3.9

Let
k_{0}
be a field finitely generated over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, where
p\neq 2
.
Let
k
be an algebraic closure of
k_{0}
.
Let
A
be an abelian variety over
k_{0}
of dimension
g\geqslant 1
.
Then the transcendental Brauer group
{\rm{Im}}[{\rm{Br}}(A)\to{\rm{Br}}(A_{k})]
is a direct sum of
a finite group and an abelian group annihilated by
p^{g-1}
.

Proof.
This follows from Theorem
3.8
and a theorem of D’Addezio

[
D’A24
, Thm. 5.2]
, see also
[
S24
, Thm. 3.2]
.
\Box

Let
M={\rm H}^{1}_{\rm cris}(A/W)
be the contravariant Dieudonné module of
A
.
Recall that
{\bf U}_{A}={\bf U}_{\wedge^{2}M}
.

The non-supersingular case

Let
n\geqslant m\geqslant 1
.
Precomposing a map
M^{\vee}/p^{m}\to M/p^{m}
with the surjective map

M^{\vee}/p^{n}\to M^{\vee}/p^{m}
and composing with the injective map

M/p^{m}\to M/p^{n}
given by multiplication by
p^{n-m}
, we obtain an embedding

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{m},M/p^{m})\hookrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{n},M/p^{n}).

Taking the anti-self-dual parts we get an embedding

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{m},M/p^{m})^{\rm skew}\hookrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{m+1},M/p^{m+1})^{\rm skew}.

By Corollary
2.5
the
p
-exponent of
{\bf U}_{A}

is the least positive integer
m
such that

{\rm{dim}}\,{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{m},M/p^{m})^{\rm skew}={\rm{dim}}\,{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{n},M/p^{n})^{\rm skew}.

Thus
\pexp(A)\leqslant r
, where
r
is the least positive integer such that for all
s\geqslant r
we have

{\rm{dim}}\,{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{r},M/p^{r})^{{\rm skew}}={\rm{dim}}\,{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{s},M/p^{s})^{\rm skew}.

For any Dieudonné modules
N
and
N^{\prime}

there exists an integer
f_{N,N^{\prime}}\geqslant 0
such that the image of
the reduction modulo
p^{m}
map

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(N/p^{n},N^{\prime}/p^{n})\to{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{E}}(N/p^{m},N^{\prime}/p^{m})

is finite if and only if
n\geqslant m+f_{N,N^{\prime}}
, see
[
GV13
, §6.1]
.
From
[
GV13
, §6.1 (i), (ii)]
, see also
[
LNV13
, Lemma 7.8]
,
we obtain
r=f_{M^{\vee},M}
.

The abelian varieties
A
and
A^{\vee}
have the same Newton polygon that we denote by

\nu
. By
[
LNV13
, Corollary 9.2]
we have
f_{M^{\vee},M}\leqslant 2\nu(g)
. If
A

is not supersingular, we have
\nu(g)<g/2
, hence
f_{M^{\vee},M}\leqslant g-1
.
This finishes the proof in the non-supersingular case.

The supersingular case

Let
A
be a supersingular abelian variety over
k
of dimension
g\geqslant 1
.
Let
E
be a supersingular elliptic curve over
k
and let
M_{E}
be its Dieudonné module.
We have
M_{E}\cong{\mathbb{E}}/(F-V){\mathbb{E}}
.
Since
A
is isogenous to
E^{g}
, we have an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{E}}
-modules

M_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong M_{E,{\mathbb{Q}}}^{g}
.
Recall that an abelian variety is called superspecial if it is isomorphic
to
E^{n}
for some
n\geqslant 1
. We call a Dieudonné module superspecial if it is
isomorphic to
M_{E}^{n}
for some
n\geqslant 1
. Recall that the
a
-number of
A
is defined as

a(A):={\rm{dim}}_{k}({\rm{Hom}}_{k}(\alpha_{p},A))={\rm{dim}}_{k}(M/(FM+VM)),

hence the inequality
a(M)\leqslant g
.

Let us recall the basic theory of supersingular Dieudonné modules as
developed in
[
Li89
, §1]
. By
[
Li89
, Lemma 1.3]
there exists a smallest
superspecial Dieudonné module in
M_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong M_{E,{\mathbb{Q}}}^{g}
containing
M
.
Let us call it the
superspecial envelope
of
M
and denote it by
S^{\prime}(M)
, using
the notation of
[
Li89
, §1]
.
The
W
-module
S^{\prime}(M)/M
has finite length.

The
W
-module
S^{\prime}(M)
is the direct sum of
g
copies of
M_{E}=We\oplus Wf
,
where
Fe=Ve=f
and
Ff=Vf=pe
.
Consider the filtration by the images of powers of Frobenius

F^{i}S^{\prime}(M)\subset S^{\prime}(M)
for
i\geqslant 0
, where

F^{0}S^{\prime}(M)=S^{\prime}(M)
.
Note that the same filtration is obtained by using
V
instead of
F
.
Thus each of
F
and
V
induces a (
\sigma
-linear or
\sigma^{-1}
-linear) bijective homomorphism
of
g
-dimensional
k
-vector spaces:

F^{i}S^{\prime}(M)/F^{i+1}S^{\prime}(M)\tilde{\longrightarrow}F^{i+1}S^{\prime}(M)/F^{i+2}S^{\prime}(M).

This implies the injectivity of each of
F
and
V
on the graded factors of
the induced filtration
M^{i}:=M\cap F^{i}S^{\prime}(M)
:

M^{i}/M^{i+1}\hookrightarrow M^{i+1}/M^{i+2}.

Let
n
be the smallest integer such that
F^{n}S^{\prime}(M)\subset M
, which exists because
F
is topologically nilpotent. Equivalently,

n
is the smallest integer such that

M^{n}/M^{n+1}\simeq k^{g}
. (For all
i\geqslant n
we have
M^{i}/M^{i+1}\simeq k^{g}
.)

Write
s_{i}={\rm{dim}}_{k}(M^{i}/M^{i+1})
, so
s_{n}=g
.
Following
[
Li89
, §1]
we claim that if
s_{i}<g
, then

F(M^{i}/M^{i+1})\neq V(M^{i}/M^{i+1}).

In this case
M^{i+1}/M^{i+2}
contains two distinct subspaces of dimension
s_{i}
, hence
we have
s_{i}<s_{i+1}
for
i\leqslant n-1
.

Let us prove the claim. By
[
Li89
, Lemma 1.6]
, there is an element
v\in M

whose
e_{i}
-coefficients reduced modulo
p
are linearly independent over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}
.
(Equivalently, the image of
v
in
M/M^{1}
is not contained in
L\otimes_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}}k

for a
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}
-vector space
L
of dimension at most
g-1
. If this is not the case,
then
M
is contained in
a proper superspecial submodule of
S^{\prime}(M)
, which contradicts the fact that
S^{\prime}(M)

is the superspecial envelope of
M
, see
[
Li89
]
for details.)
If
s_{i}<g
and
F(M^{i}/M^{i+1})=V(M^{i}/M^{i+1})
, then
this is a proper
k
-vector subspace of the
k
-vector space
M^{i+1}/M^{i+2}

of dimension at most
g-1
,
which is invariant under
x\to x^{p^{2}}
, and so it is
L\otimes_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}}k

for some
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}
-vector space
L
. Then
F^{i+1}(v)\in L\otimes_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{2}}}k
,
but this contradicts the definition of
v
.

Write
N=(F,V)M
and consider the induced filtration
N^{i}:=N\cap F^{i}S^{\prime}(M)
.
We have
(F,V)M\subset FS^{\prime}(M)=VS^{\prime}(M)
, so
N=N^{1}
.
Define
t_{i}={\rm{dim}}_{k}(N^{i+1}/N^{i+2})
for
i=0,\ldots,n-1
. The crucial inequality
is
t_{i}\geqslant s_{i}+1
for
i=0,\ldots,n-1
.
This follows from the claim above. Indeed, for these values of
i
we have two injective maps

M^{i}/M^{i+1}\hookrightarrow M^{i+1}/M^{i+2}
(given by
F
and
V
)
whose images are different and contained in

N^{i+1}/N^{i+2}\subset M^{i+1}/M^{i+2}
.

We finally note that
F^{n}S^{\prime}(M)\subset M
implies
F^{n+1}S^{\prime}(M)\subset(F,V)M
,
hence
N^{n+1}=F^{n+1}S^{\prime}(M)=M^{n+1}
.

Since the
a
-number of
A
is
{\rm{dim}}_{k}(M/(FM+VM))
, we obtain

a(A)={\rm length}(M/M^{n+1})-{\rm length}(N/N^{n+1})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}s_{i}-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}t_{i}=s_{n}-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(t_{i}-s_{i})\leqslant g-n.

Let
A^{\prime}_{i}
be an abelian variety with Dieudonné module
M^{i}
, for
i=0,\ldots,n
.
In particular,
A^{\prime}_{0}=A
and
A^{\prime}_{n}\simeq E^{g}
is superspecial. Thus we get
a chain of isogenies

A=A^{\prime}_{0}\to A^{\prime}_{1}\to\ldots\to A^{\prime}_{n}\simeq E^{g},

such that the kernel
K_{i}
of
A^{\prime}_{i}\to A^{\prime}_{i+1}
is
(\alpha_{p})^{s_{i}}
.
It is easy to see that
a(A^{\prime}_{i+1})-a(A^{\prime}_{i})=t_{i}-s_{i}\geqslant 1
. It follows that
n\leqslant g-1
,
and
n=g-1
if and only if
a(A)=1
and
a(A^{\prime}_{i})=i+1
for
i=0,\ldots,g-1
.
In this last case we have
s_{i}=i+1
for
i=0,\ldots,g-1
, so that
K_{i}=\alpha(A^{\prime}_{i})

is the group subscheme of
A^{\prime}_{i}
generated by the images of morphisms
\alpha_{p}\to A^{\prime}_{i}
.

Applying previous considerations to the dual abelian variety, we obtain a chain of isogenies

E^{g}\simeq A_{n}\to A_{n-1}\to\ldots\to A_{0}=A,

whose successive kernels are
\alpha
-groups. Again, we have
n\leqslant g-1
,
and
n=g-1
if and only if
a(A)=1
and
a(A_{i})=i+1
for
i=0,\ldots,g-1
.
In this case, the kernel of
A_{i}\to A_{i-1}
is
(\alpha_{p})^{i}
, so in particular the kernel
of
E^{g}\to A_{g-2}
is
(\alpha_{p})^{g-1}
.

Proof of Theorem

3.8
.
By Proposition
2.7
and the fact that
p{\bf U}_{E^{g}}=0
,
to prove that the
p
-exponent of
{\bf U}_{A}
is at most
g-1
,
we can assume that

, so that the kernel of
E^{g}\to A_{g-2}

is
(\alpha_{p})^{g-1}
.
Theorem

will follow if we show that

{\bf U}_{A_{g-2}}\to{\bf U}_{E^{g}}
is the zero map.

We start with the case

.

Lemma 3.10

Let
E^{2}\to A
be an isogeny with kernel
(\alpha_{p})^{n}
, where

.
Then the induced map

Proof.

There is a commutative diagram with exact rows

\textstyle{\wedge^{2}(M_{E^{2}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\bf U}_{E^{2}}(k)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

4
Ekedahl–Oort types and
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
over an algebraically closed field
k

of characteristic
p\geqslant 3
.
In this section we compute
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])

when
A
is principally polarised in terms of the Ekedahl–Oort type of
A
.

In
[
O01
]
, Ekedahl and Oort stratified the moduli space of
principally polarised abelian varieties of dimension
g
over
k

according to the isomorphism class of
A[p]
.
In total there are
2^{g}
such isomorphism classes; they are numbered by the Ekedahl–Oort types.
An Ekedahl–Oort type is a non-decreasing function

\varphi\colon\{0,1,\ldots,g\}\to\{0,1,\ldots,g\},\quad\varphi(0)=0,

such that

\varphi(i)-\varphi(i-1)
is 0 or 1. Given a subset
P\subset\{1,\ldots,g\}

we define

\varphi_{P}(i)=\varphi_{P}(i-1)+1
if
i\in P
, and
\varphi_{P}(i)=\varphi_{P}(i-1)
otherwise.
It is clear that every Ekedahl–Oort type can be written as
\varphi_{P}
for some subset

P\subset\{1,\ldots,g\}
.
The correspondence between Ekedahl–Oort types and isomorphism classes of
A[p]
is recalled in Theorem
4.3
.
Note that the
a
-number of
A
is
g-|P|
.

Theorem 4.1

Let
A
be a principally polarised abelian variety of dimension
g
over an algebraically closed
field of characteristic
p\geqslant 3
.
If the Ekedahl–Oort type of
A
is
\varphi_{P}
, where
P=\{m_{1}<\ldots<m_{g-a}\}
,
where
a
is the
a
-number of
A
, then

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])=\frac{a(a-1)}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{g-a}(m_{i}-i).

When
P=\emptyset
, the sum is understood to be 0.

Example 4.2

Let
A
be a principally polarised abelian variety.

(1) Suppose that
A
is supersingular.
Then we have
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})=g(g-1)/2
.
There are two extreme cases:

•

A
is superspecial if and only if
a=g
, which is equivalent to
the condition that
\varphi
is the zero function (that is,
P=\emptyset
).
Theorem
4.1
gives
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])=g(g-1)/2
, so that
p{\bf U}_{A}=0
. This is already proved in
[
YY
, Proposition 4.4]
.

•

A
is supergeneral if and only if
a=1
.
Then
P=\{2,\ldots,g\}
and

\varphi(i)=i-1
for
i\geqslant 1
. (This can be deduced from
[
O01
, (9.7)]
using that
the
p
-rank is
0
.) Theorem
4.1
says that
{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])=g-1
.

(2) Suppose that
A
is ordinary. This is equivalent to
a=0
, and thus to
\varphi(i)=i

for all
i=0,1,\ldots,g
.

We now recall the construction of
covariant
self-dual Dieudonné modules
associated to Ekedahl–Oort types
[
O01
, §9]
.
Let
P=\{m_{1}<...<m_{h}\}
be a subset of
\{1,2,...,g\}
of size
h=g-a
, and let

\varphi=\varphi_{P}
.
Extend
\varphi
to a non-decreasing function

\psi\colon\{0,1,\ldots,2g\}\to\{0,1,\ldots,g\},\quad\psi(0)=0,\ \psi(2g)=g,

by setting

\psi(i)=\varphi(2g-i)+i-g
for
i=g+1,...,2g
.
Let
m_{1}<\ldots<m_{g}
be the increasing sequence of all integers

1\leqslant i\leqslant 2g
such that
\psi(i)=\psi(i-1)+1
. Likewise, let

n_{1}>\ldots>n_{g}
be the decreasing sequence of all integers
1\leqslant j\leqslant 2g

such that
\psi(j)=\psi(j-1)
. In particular,

\{1,...,g\}
is the disjoint union of
P=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{h}\}

and its complement
Q=\{n_{g},\ldots,n_{h+1}\}
.

Let
M_{\varphi}
be a vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with basis
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{2g}
.
Let us write
{\cal Z}_{1}:={\rm Span}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{g})
and

{\cal Z}_{2}:={\rm Span}(Z_{g+1},\ldots,Z_{2g})
.
We rename the set of basis vectors

Z_{m_{i}}=X_{i},\quad Z_{n_{i}}=Y_{i},\ \ \text{where}\ \ i=1,\ldots,g,

and write
{\cal X}:={\rm Span}(X_{i})
,
{\cal Y}:={\rm Span}(Y_{j})
.
The
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-vector space
M_{\varphi}
has an alternating bilinear form
\langle x,y\rangle

which is zero on
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
,
and satisfies
\langle X_{i},Y_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
, where
\delta_{ij}
is Kronecker’s delta.
We have
m_{i}+n_{i}=2g+1
for all
i=1,\ldots,g
. Using this one checks that

{\cal Z}_{1}
and
{\cal Z}_{2}
are maximal hyperbolic subspaces, alongside with
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
.

Define
\mathcal{F}\in{\rm{End}}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{\varphi})
as follows:

\mathcal{F}(X_{i})=Z_{i},\quad\mathcal{F}(Y_{i})=0.

We have
\langle\mathcal{F}x,\mathcal{F}y\rangle=0
for all
x,y\in M_{\varphi}
, because

{\rm{Im}}(\mathcal{F})={\cal Z}_{1}
which is maximal hyperbolic.
Define
\mathcal{V}\in{\rm{End}}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}(M_{\varphi})
so that

\langle\mathcal{F}x,y\rangle=\langle x,\mathcal{V}y\rangle

(27)

for all
x,y\in M_{\varphi}
. By the non-degeneracy of the form we have
\mathcal{V}\mathcal{F}=0
.
Using this non-degeneracy
and the fact that
{\rm{Ker}}(\mathcal{F})={\cal Y}
, we see that

{\rm{Im}}(\mathcal{V})={\cal Y}
and
{\rm{Ker}}(\mathcal{V})={\cal Z}_{1}
. We deduce that

\langle\mathcal{V}x,\mathcal{V}y\rangle=0
for all
x,y\in M_{\varphi}
.
This implies
\mathcal{F}\mathcal{V}=0
.

Theorem 4.3

(Oort)

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic
p\neq 2
.
Let

be a principally polarised abelian variety over

of Ekedahl–Oort type
\varphi
.
Then the covariant Dieudonné module

of

is

with
the natural
\sigma
-linear action of
\mathcal{F}
and the natural

\sigma^{-1}
-linear action of
\mathcal{V}
.
The Dieudonné module

is self-dual with respect to the alternating

\langle x,y\rangle
.

Proof.
This is
[

.
\square

Let

be the
(g\times g)
-matrix with
(i,j)
-entry 1 and all other entries 0.
Define

, whereas

is the permutation matrix of the permutation of

defined on

as the unique order-preserving
function

(the inverse of

)
and on

Lemma 4.4

The group of self-dual endomorphisms of the self-dual Dieudonné module

is isomorphic to the additive group of
pairs of
(g\times g)
-matrices
(A,C)
with entries in
k

(28)

where superscript
(p)
means that every entry of the matrix is raised to the power

.

Proof.

We use the basis
\mathcal{B}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{g},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{g}\}
.
In this basis the matrix of the form

is

\left(\begin{matrix}0&I\\
-I&0\end{matrix}\right).

The linear action of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{V}

\mathcal{[}\mathcal{F}]=\left(\begin{matrix}U&0\\

-V^{t}&U^{t}\end{matrix}\right).

Here

Let

be an endomorphism of the self-dual Dieudonné module

We have

if and only if the matrix of

,

. The condition

Since

is invertible, we have

. This implies the lemma.
\Box

.

Lemma 4.6

The following properties hold:

(1)
if there is an edge from
(i_{1},j_{1})
to
(i_{2},j_{2})
, then

i_{1}-j_{1}
and
i_{2}-j_{2}
have the same sign (so the connected components ‘do not cross
the diagonal’);

(2)
every vertex has at most one incoming edge and at most one outgoing edge;

(3)
the connected components of the graph
\mathcal{G}_{\varphi}

are either cycles
(i_{1},j_{1})\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow(i_{r},j_{r})\rightarrow(i_{1},j_{1})

or chains
(i_{1},j_{1})\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow(i_{s},j_{s})
(without repetition of vertices);

(4)
for every ‘diagonal’ vertex
(i,i)
there is exactly one incoming and one outgoing edge;
the edge starting at a diagonal vertex ends in a diagonal vertex;

(5)
the connected components of the set of diagonal vertices are cycles;

(6)
no edge originates at
(i,j)
if and only if

i\leqslant h<j
or
j\leqslant h<i
;

(7)
there are exactly
h(g-h)
chains above (respectively, below) the diagonal;

(8)
no cycle contains a marked vertex;

(9)
every chain below the diagonal ends with a marked vertex;

(10)
every marked vertex above the diagonal is the start point of a chain;

(11)
the number of chains without marked vertices is
\sum_{i=1}^{h}(m_{i}-i)
.

Proof.
For a type (m) edge,
i<j
implies

because the function

is order-preserving. For a type (n) edge,

implies

because the function

is order-inversing. This proves (1).

(3) is a consequence of (2). Property (5) is a consequence of (4). A chain is uniquely

or

(i,j)\in P\times Q

, so

(i,j)
is the start point of a chain. This proves (10).

Let us prove (11). All marked points are either start or end points of chains, so

This means that it starts at a vertex

Corollary 4.7

Let
A
be a principally polarised abelian variety over an algebraically closed
field

of characteristic

. Let

.
Then there exists an abelian variety

such that

. In particular, we have

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A})\leqslant r(r+1)/2\quad\text{and}\quad\pexp({\bf U}_{A})\leqslant r.

Proof.
Let
f
be the
p
-rank of
A
. Since
k
is perfect,
the
p
-divisible group

is isomorphic to the product of
p
-divisible

p
-divisible group
of an abelian variety

of dimension

.

By

,

is the maximal value of

A result of Oort

The main question of this paper is

Question 5.1

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
over
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. What are the possible integers

n_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant n_{r}
such that
{\bf U}_{A}
is isogenous to
\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\bf W}_{n_{i}}
?

We can ask more specific questions about various particular types of abelian varieties.

The reader will notice that our proof of Theorem
3.8
in the non-supersingular case
bounded the dimension of
{\rm{End}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{m},M/p^{m})^{\rm skew}
by the dimension of
the larger quasi-algebraic group
{\rm{End}}_{\mathbb{E}}(M^{\vee}/p^{m},M/p^{m})
. It is thus likely
that in this case the bound
g-1
for the
p
-exponent of
{\bf U}_{A}
is not the best possible.

Question 5.2

Let
A
be a non-supersingular abelian variety of dimension
g\geqslant 2
over
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. Is it true that
p^{g-2}{\bf U}_{A}=0
?

Assuming
p\neq 2
,
this holds for
g\leqslant 3
(Proposition
3.16
) and also for
g=4
in the principally polarised case, as one sees by arguing similarly to Example
4.8
.

In the proof of Proposition
3.12
we used a supersingular abelian variety
A
with
Dieudonné module
M={\mathbb{E}}/(F^{g}-V^{g}){\mathbb{E}}
. We note that the
a
-number of
A

equals
{\rm{dim}}_{k}(M/(FM+VM))=1
, that is,
A
is supergeneral.

Question 5.3

Let
A
be a supersingular abelian variety of dimension
g\geqslant 3
over
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
such that the
a
-number
of
A
is at least
2
(that is,
A
is not supergeneral). Is it true that
p^{g-2}{\bf U}_{A}=0
?

Here we must exclude the case
g=2
because for a supersingular elliptic curve
E
we have

a(E^{2})=2
whereas
{\bf U}_{E^{2}}\cong{\bf G}_{a,k}
.

Finally, one can enquire whether the description of the isogeny class of
{\bf U}_{A}
in Proposition
3.12

remains true for all supergeneral abelian varieties (this is
[
YY25
, Conjecture 5.2.1]
).
In general it would be interesting to understand which numerical invariants of
M
completely determine the isogeny type of
{\bf U}_{A}
.

Question 5.4

Consider the abelian varieties
A
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0

with a fixed Newton polygon and
a
-number
1
. Is the isogeny type of
{\bf U}_{A}

independent of
A
?

For
g\leqslant 3

the answers to Questions
5.3
and

3.16
.
The positive answer to Question
5.4
for supergeneral varieties would be in agreement with Proposition

{\rm{dim}}({\bf U}_{A}[p])=g-1
.

with

-number
1
. The following curious question seems
to have a positive answer for all the examples at our disposal.

Question 5.5

Does the isogeny type
n_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant n_{r}
of
{\bf U}_{A}
always contain
a subsequence
1,2,\ldots,n_{r}-1,n_{r}

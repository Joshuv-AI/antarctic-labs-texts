Representation theory of hereditary artin algebras of finite representation type
Shiping Liu, Gordana Todorov

Abstract
. Let H H be a hereditary artin algebra of finite representation type. We first determine all hammocks in the Auslander-Reiten quiver Γ mod ​ H \mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H} of mod ​ H {\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH , the category of finitely generated left H H -modules. This enables us to obtain an effective method to construct Γ mod ​ H \mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H} by simply viewing the ext-quiver of H H . As easy applications, we compute the numbers of non-isomorphic indecomposable objects in mod ​ H {\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH and the associated cluster category 𝒞 H \mathscr{C}_{H} , as well as the nilpotencies of the radicals of mod ​ H , {\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH\hskip-0.4pt, D b ​ ( mod ​ H ) \hskip-0.5ptD^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}(\hskip-0.5pt{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH\hskip-0.5pt) and 𝒞 H \mathscr{C}_{H} .

Introduction

Since Gabriel’s pivotal work on representations of quivers; see
[
17
]
, the representation theory of hereditary artin algebras has been extensively studied over the past fifty years; see, for example,
[
13
,
14
,
29
,
32
,
35
]
. The representation type of such an algebra is finite precisely when its ext-quiver is of Dynkin type; see
[
2
,
14
,
17
]
. In this case, the information on the module category is encoded in its Auslander-Reiten quiver. In the linearly oriented
\mathbb{A}_{n}
-case, the Auslander-Reiten quiver has a wing shape; see
[
28
, (2.6)]
and
[
32
, (3.3)]
. In all other cases, the Auslander-Reiten quiver has only been roughly described; see
[
2
, (VIII.1.15)]
and
[
28
, (1.13)]
.

The main objective of this paper is to provide an effective method to construct the Auslander-Reiten quiver simply by viewing the ext-quiver of the algebra. This enables us to compute the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable objects in the module category and the associated cluster category, and also the nilpotency of the radicals of the module category, its bounded derived category and the associated cluster category. More details are outlined below.

In this introduction,
H
denotes a hereditary artin algebra with a Dynkin extension quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
; see (
1.6.1
). Let
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
be the category of finitely generated left
H
-modules and
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
be its Auslander-Reiten quiver; see (1.5). It is well-known that every
\tau
-orbit in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
is finite and contains a projective module and an injective module; see
[
29
, (1.8)]
, and also
[
14
, (1.9)]
. Moreover,
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
embeds as a convex valued translation quiver in the repetitive quiver
\mathbb{Z}
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}
, where
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}
is the full valued subquiver of
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
generated by the projective modules; see
[
28
, (1.13)]
, and also
[
2
, (VIII.1.15)]
. Thus, in order to describe the precise shape of
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
, it suffices to determine the injective module and the number of modules in the
\tau
-orbit of any projective module.

For giving a combinatorial criterion for a finite translation quiver to be the Auslander-Reiten quiver of a finite dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field, Brenner introduced the notions of hammocks and hammock functions; see
[
10
]
. We adapt and extend these notions to the artin setting; see (
1.7.2
) and (
4.2.1
). After a thorough study of the valued translation quivers of tree type in Section 2, we describe all the hammocks in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
; see (
4.2.4
). This allows us to determine which injective module lies in the
\tau
-orbit of any given projective module; see, for example, (
4.2.5
), (
4.2.6
) and (
4.2.7
).

To study representations of a valued graph, Dlab and Ringel defined a Coxeter transformations as a product of all reflections in the corresponding rational vector space; see
[
13
, Page 8]
. Alternatively, for any hereditary artin algebra, Auslander and Platzeck defined the Coxeter transformation as an special automorphism of the Grothendieck group of the module category; see (
3.3.1
) and
[
29
, Section 2]
. Since
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
is of Dynkin type, the Coxeter transformation
C_{H}
for
H
is of finite order
|C_{H}|
; see
[
29
, (4.1)]
, called the
Coxeter order
. The Coxeter order has been computed for each Dynkin diagram with a particular orientation; see
[
2
, Pages 289, 290]
. We show that
|C_{H}|
is independent of the orientation of
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
, so it is explicitly given no matter how
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
is oriented; see (
4.4.2
). Further, we shall pair the
\tau
-orbits of projective modules in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
in such a way that the sum of the numbers of modules in the paired
\tau
-orbits is equal to
|C_{H}|
; see (
4.4.1
), while the difference equals the difference of the numbers of arrows in two reduced walks in
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
between the corresponding vertices; see (
4.3.2
). So, the number of modules in any
\tau
-orbit is expressed in terms of
|C_{H}|
and the number of arrows in a specific reduced walk in
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
. This ultimately completes the picture of
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
in terms of
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
; see (
4.5.1
).

Since
H
is of finite representation type, it is interesting to compute the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable
H
-modules. Since the center
\Bbbk
of
H
is a field; see
[
29
, (3.1)]
,
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
is equivalent to the representation category of a
\Bbbk
-linear species of
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
; see
[
13
, Theorem C]
. Hence, this number coincides with the number of positive roots of
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
; see
[
14
,
17
]
, which can be found in
[
12
]
.
However, we directly obtain this number from our results, that is, half of
|C_{H}|
multiplied by the number of non-isomorphic simple
H
-modules; see (
4.6.1
).

A well-known result of Auslander says that an artin algebra
{\mathit{\Lambda}}
is representation-finite if and only if the radical of
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Lambda}}$}
is nilpotent; see
[
2
, (V.7.7)]
and
[
34
, (1.1)]
. In this case, it is interesting to compute the nilpotency of
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Lambda}}$})
, namely the least integer
r
for which
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt^{r}\hskip-1.0pt({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Lambda}}$})=0
. Using preprojective partitions, Zacharia proved that
|C_{H}|-2
is the maximal length of chains of irreducible maps in
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Lambda}}$}
with a non-zero composite; see
[
35
, (4.11)]
, and consequently,
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
is of nilpotency
|C_{H}|-1
. Alternatively, using some nice properties of
\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$};
see (
3.2.1
), we show that the radicals of
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
and
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
have the same nilpotency
|C_{H}|-1
; see (
4.6.4
) and (
4.6.5
).

In order to categorify Fomin and Zelevinsky’s cluster algebra associated with a finite acyclic unvalued quiver
[
16
]
, Buan, Marsh, Reineke, Reiten and Todorov introduced the associated cluster category, which is an orbit category of the bounded derived category of finite dimensional representations of the quiver; see
[
7
]
. The same construction yields the cluster category
\mathscr{C}_{H}
associated with
H
. We show that the radical of
\mathscr{C}_{H}
is also of nilpotency
|C_{H}|-1
, and the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable objects in
\mathscr{C}_{H}
is half of
|C_{H}|+2
multiplied by the number of non-isomorphic simple
H
-modules; see (
4.6.6
), which coincides with the number of cluster variables of the cluster algebra associated with
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
; see
[
15
, (5.9.1)]
. Finally, we want to express our gratitude to Platzeck and Zacharia for some helpful discussions.

1.
Preliminaries

The objective of this section is to lay the foundation for this paper. Besides fixing some terminology and notation, we shall collect and also prove some results in the general context, which are needed later. Throughout this paper,
R
denotes a commutative artinian ring. All algebras are
R
-algebras, and all categories are additive
R
-categories whose morphisms are composed from right to left.

1.1.
Quivers

We start with laying the combinatorial foundation. Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a quiver, where
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices and
Q_{1}
is the set of arrows from a vertex, called the
start point
, to another vertex, called the
end point
. An arrow
\alpha
from
x
to
y
is usually represented graphically as
x\to y
, and we write
x=s(\alpha)
and
y=e(\alpha)
. Given
x\in Q_{0}
, write
x^{+}
for the set of vertices
y
such that there exists an arrow
x\to y
, and
x^{-}
for the set of vertices
z
such that there exists an arrow
z\to x
. We call
x
a
source
or
sink
if
x^{-}
or
x^{+}
is empty, respectively. With each vertex
x\in Q_{0}
, one associates a
trivial path

\varepsilon_{x}
of length 0 with
s(\varepsilon_{x})=e(\varepsilon_{x})=x
.
A
path
of length
r>0
is a sequence
\eta=\alpha_{r}\cdots\alpha_{1}
, where
\alpha_{i}\in Q_{1}
such that
e(\alpha_{i})=s(\alpha_{i+1})
for
1\leq i<r
; and in this case, write
s(\eta)=s(\alpha_{1})
and
e(\eta)=e(\alpha_{r})
. In the sequel, the length of a path
\eta
will be written as
l(\eta)
.
A non-trivial path
\eta
is called an
oriented cycle
if
s(\eta)=e(\eta)
, and an oriented cycle of length
2
is called a
2
-
cycle
. Two paths
\eta,\zeta
are called
parallel
if
s(\eta)=s(\zeta)
and
e(\eta)=e(\zeta)
. A subquiver
Q^{\prime}
of
Q
is called
full
if it contains all arrows
x\to y
in
Q
with
x,y\in Q^{\prime}
, and
convex
if it contains all paths
x\rightsquigarrow y
in
Q
with
x,y\in Q^{\prime}
.

For each arrow
\alpha:x\to y
in
Q
, one introduces its
formal inverse

\alpha^{-1}
with
s(\alpha^{-1})=e(\alpha)
and
e(\alpha^{-1})=s(\alpha)
. The formal inverses of arrows will be called
inverse arrows.

A
trivial walk
of length 0 is a trivial path. A
walk
of length
t(\hskip 0.5pt>0)
is a sequence
w=c_{t}c_{t-1}\cdots c_{1}
, where
c_{i}
is an arrow or an inverse arrow such that
e(c_{i})=s(c_{i+1})
for
1\leq i<t
. In this case, we write
s(w)=s(c_{1})
and
e(w)=e(c_{t})
, and call
w
a
walk from
s(w)
to
e(w)
. Note that
w^{-1}=c_{1}^{-1}\cdots c_{t-1}^{-1}c_{t}^{-1}
is a walk from
y
to
x
, and the number of arrows in
w^{-1}
is the number of inverse arrows in
w
, while the number of inverse arrows in
w^{-1}
is the number of arrows in
w
. A walk in
w
in
Q
is called
reduced
if
w
is trivial or
w=c_{t}c_{t-1}\dots c_{1}
such that
c_{i+1}\neq c_{i}^{-1}
for all
1\leq i<t
. One says that
Q
is a
tree
if, for any
x,y\in Q_{0}
, there exists at most one reduced walk from
x
to
y
. The following notation is important for later purposes.

Notation 1.1.1
.

Let
Q
be a connected tree with
x,y\in Q_{0}
. In case
x=y
, put

\mathfrak{a}^{+}(x,y)=\mathfrak{a}^{-}(x,y)=0
. Otherwise, there exists a unique reduced
w=c_{t}\cdots c_{1}
from
x
to
y
, where each
c_{i}
is an arrow or an inverse arrow in
Q
. Then we denote by
\mathfrak{a}^{+}(x,y)
the number of indices
i
such that
c_{i}
is an arrow; and by
\mathfrak{a}^{-}(x,y)
the number of indices
j
such that
c_{j}
is an inverse arrow.

We define the
opposite quiver

Q^{\rm op}
of
Q
by
(Q^{\rm op})_{0}=Q_{0}
and
(Q^{\rm op})_{1}=\{\alpha^{\rm o}:y\to x\mid\alpha:x\to y\in Q_{1}\}.
The trivial paths in
Q
are identified with the trivial paths in
Q^{\rm op}
. And a non-trivial walk
w
in
Q
from
x
to
y
induces a non-trivial walk
w^{\rm o}
in
Q^{\rm op}
from
x
to
y
so that the number of arrows in
w^{\rm o}
is the number of inverse arrows in
w
, and the number of inverse arrows in
w^{\rm o}
is the number of arrows in
w
.

1.2.
Valued quivers

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
denote the set of positive integers. A
valued quiver
is a pair
(Q,v)
, where
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
is a quiver without multiple arrows and
v
is the
valuation
, that is a map

v:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}:\alpha\mapsto v(\alpha):=(v_{\alpha},v^{\prime}_{\alpha});

see, for example,
[
2
, Page 69]
, and compare
[
19
, Page 287]
. The valuation
v(\alpha)
of an arrow
\alpha
is called
trivial
if
v(\alpha)=(1,1).
In a graphic representation of a valued quiver, we will omit the trivial valuations. In case
Q
has no loop, we will write
v(\alpha)=(v_{xy},v_{xy}^{\prime})
for an arrow
\alpha:x\to y
. And in case
Q
has no
2
-cycle, we will write
v(\alpha)=(v_{xy},v_{yx})
for an arrow
\alpha:x\to y
.
A
full valued subquiver
of a valued quiver
(Q,v)
is a full subquiver of
Q
with the valuation obtained by restricting
v
. In order to define the opposite valued quiver, we write

(a,b)^{\circ}=(b,a)
for any pair
(a,b)\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}^{+}\times\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}^{+}
.

Definition 1.2.1
.

Let
(Q,v)
be a valued quiver. Its
opposite valued quiver

(Q^{\rm op},v^{\rm o})
is defined by

(1)

Q^{\rm op}
is the opposite quiver of
Q;

(2)

v^{\rm o}
is defined by
v^{\circ}(\alpha^{\circ})=v(\alpha)^{\circ},
for any arrow
\alpha
in
Q
.

1.3.
Valued graphs

We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of non-negative integers. The following definition is slightly modified from the one given in
[
2
, Page 241]
; compare
[
14
, Page 1]
.

Definition 1.3.1
.

A
valued graph
is a triplet
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0},\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1},v)
, where

(1)

\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
is a set of vertices
\,;

(2)

\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1}
is a set of edges between vertices, containing no loop or multiple edge
\,;

(3)

v
is the
valuation
, that is a function

v:\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}\times\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}\to\mathbb{N}:(x,y)\mapsto v(x,y):=v_{xy}

such that
v_{xy}\neq 0
if and only if
v_{yx}\neq 0
if and only if there exists an edge between
x
and
y
.

In the sequel, a valued graph
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0},\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1},v)
will be written as
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},v)
, where
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}=(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0},\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1});
or simply as
{\mathit{\Delta}}
. If
e
is an edge between
x
and
y
such that
v_{xy}=v_{yx}=1
, then we say that
e
is
trivially valued
with
trivial valuation

(1,1)
. One says that
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is
simply laced
if every edge in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is trivially valued. In this paper, we shall only consider valued graphs with a planar representation defined as follows.

Notation 1.3.2
.

Let
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},v)
be a planar valued graph such that every vertex is incident to at most one non-trivially valued edge. Consider an edge
e
between vertices
x
and
y
. If
e
is trivially valued, then it is represented by a blank line between
x
and
y
. Otherwise,
e
is represented by a horizontal line
\textstyle{i\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{j\hskip-2.0pt}
labeled with the pair
(v_{xy},v_{yx})
or
\textstyle{\hskip-2.0pty\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{x\hskip-2.0pt}
labeled with the pair
(v_{yx},v_{xy})
. In either case, the pair is called the
valuation
of
e
in the respective representation.

Remark 1.3.3
.

In contrast to the valuation of an arrow in a valued quiver, the valuation of a non-trivially valued edge in a valued graph depends on how the edge is written, namely, which vertex is on the left and which is on the right.

Now, we introduce valued graph isomorphisms, which we cannot find in any existing literature.

Definition 1.3.4
.

Let
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},v)
and
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be valued graphs. We define a
valued graph isomorphism

\varphi:(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},v)\to(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{\prime},v^{\prime})
to be a bijection
\varphi:\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}\to\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}^{\prime}
such that
v^{\prime}_{\varphi(x)\varphi(y)}=v_{xy},
for all
x,y\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
.

Remark 1.3.5
.

Let
\varphi:(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},v)\to(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be a valued graph isomorphism. Then, for
x,y\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
, there exists an edge
e
between
x
and
y
in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
if and only if

there exists an edge
e^{\prime}
between
\varphi(x)
and
\varphi(y)
in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{\prime}
. And if
e
is written as
\textstyle{x\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{y}
with valuation
(a,a^{\prime})
, then
e^{\prime}
can be written as
\textstyle{\varphi(x)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\varphi(y)}
with valuation
(a,a^{\prime})
or as
\textstyle{\varphi(y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\varphi(x)}
with valuation
(a^{\prime},a)
.

Definition 1.3.6
.

Let
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},v)
be a valued graph. The
weight
of

is defined by

{\rm w}(x)\hskip-0.5pt:\hskip 0.5pt=\sum_{y\in\mathit{\Delta}_{0}}\hskip-2.0ptv_{xy}

The following statement follows immediately from the definition a valued graph isomorphism.

Lemma 1.3.7

Let
\varphi:(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},v)\to(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{\prime},v^{\prime})

\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},
we have

.

Definition 1.3.9

Let

The graph
\hskip 1.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.6ptQ\hskip-0.5pt}

\hskip 1.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.6ptQ\hskip-0.5pt}

1.4.
Valued translation quivers

A
valued translation quiver
is a triplet
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$},v,\tau)
, where
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$},v)
is a valued quiver and
\tau
is a bijection, called the
translation
, from a subset
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}^{\prime}
of
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}
to another
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}^{\prime\prime}
such, for any
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}^{\prime}
and any arrow
y\to x
with valuation
(v_{yx},v^{\prime}_{yx})
, that there exists an arrow
\tau x\to y
with valuation
(v_{yx}^{\prime},v_{yx})
. In this case, we shall write
\tau^{-}
for the inverse of
\tau
. The
\tau
-
orbit
of a vertex
x
is the set of vertices
\tau^{s}x
with
s\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
.
A path
x\rightsquigarrow y
in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
is called
sectional
if it does not contain any subpath of the form
\tau a\to b\to a
; and in this case, we call
x
a
sectional predecessor
of
y
, and
y
a
sectional successor
of
x
. Note that a trivial path in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
is sectional. A sectional path is called
strictly sectional
if it meets any
\tau
-orbit in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
at most once. The following easy statement follows immediately from the definition of a valued translation quiver, which will be used frequently.

Lemma 1.4.1
.

Let
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$},v,\tau)
be a valued translation quiver. Let
x\to y
be an arrow in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
with valuation
(a,a^{\prime})
. If
\tau^{s}x
and
\tau^{s}y
are defined, then
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
contains an arrow
\tau^{s}x\to\tau^{s}y
with valuation
(a,a^{\prime})
.

If

is irreducible if and only if

.

{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Lambda}}$}
to paths in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3pt\mathit{\Lambda}}

Lemma 1.6.5
.

Let
{\mathit{\Lambda}}
be an artin algebra. Consider a radical map
f:M\to N
between indecomposable modules in
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Lambda}}$}
. If
{\rm dp}(f)=t

{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Lambda}}$}
such that

Assume that

Let
{\mathit{\Lambda}}
be an artin algebra of directed representation type. Consider a simple module
S
in the Auslander-Reiten quiver
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8pt{\rm mod}\mathit{\Lambda}}
.

(1)

The
hammock
associated with
S
is the full valued translation subquiver
\mathcal{H}_{S}
of
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8pt{\rm mod}\mathit{\Lambda}}
generated by the modules
M
of which
S
is a composition factor.

(2)

The
canonical hammock function
associated with
S
is the function

h_{S}:\mathcal{H}_{S}\to\mathbb{N}:M\mapsto\ell_{S}(M).

The following statement explains in particular the terminology of a hammock.

Proposition 1.7.3
.

Let
{\mathit{\Lambda}}
be an artin algebra of directed representation type. Consider a simple module
S
with projective cover
P
and injective envelope
I
, where
S,P,I\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8pt{\rm mod}\mathit{\Lambda}}
.

(1)

Every module in the hammock
\mathcal{H}_{S}
is a successor of
P
and a predecessor of
I
.

(2)

If
M
is a sectional successor of
P
or a sectional predecessor of
I
in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8pt{\rm mod}\mathit{\Lambda}}
, then
M\in\mathcal{H}_{S}.

Proof.
It is clear that
P,I\in\mathcal{H}_{S}
. Consider a module
M\in\mathcal{H}_{S}
. By Lemma
1.7.1
, we have non-zero maps
f:P\to M
and
g:M\to I
.
Since
{\mathit{\Lambda}}
is representation-finite,
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8pt{\rm mod}\mathit{\Lambda}}
contains paths
P\rightsquigarrow M
and
M\rightsquigarrow I
; see
[
2
, (V.7.8)]
. We claim that
\mathcal{H}_{S}
contains a path
P\rightsquigarrow M
. Since
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
is finite without oriented cycles, we have a maximal length
l_{M}
of paths
P\rightsquigarrow M
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
. We may assume that
l_{M}>0
. Then,
f
factors through the minimal right almost split map for
M
. Thus,
\mathcal{H}_{S}
contains an arrow
N\to M
. Since
l_{N}<l_{M}
, by the induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{H}_{S}
contains a path
P\rightsquigarrow N
, and hence, a path
P\rightsquigarrow M
. Our claim holds. Dually,
\mathcal{H}_{S}
contains a path
M\rightsquigarrow I
. This establishes Statement (1). Next, if
M
is sectional successor of
P
or a sectional predecessor of
I
in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8pt{\rm mod}\mathit{\Lambda}}
, then
\Hom_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(P,M)\neq 0
or
\Hom_{\mathit{\Lambda}}(M,I)\neq 0
; see
[
20
, (13.4)]
and also
[
2
, (VII.2.4)]
. So,

M\in\mathcal{H}_{S}
; see (
1.7.1
). The proof of the proposition is completed.

2.
Valued translation quivers with sections

The notion of sections is useful in describing Auslander-Reiten components without oriented cycles for artin algebras; see
[
24
,
25
]
. The objective of this section is to further investigate valued translation quivers with sections, by studying the repetitive quiver
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
of a valued quiver
{\mathit{\Delta}}
without oriented cycles. The sectional paths in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
are precisely described in terms of the reduced walks in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
; see (
2.1.4
) and (
2.1.6
). In case
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is a tree, a sectional path in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is the only path between its end-points, and any two parallel paths have the same length; see (
2.1.8
). Finally, we shall consider additive functions on stable valued translation quivers with sections. These results will be applied to preprojective and preinjective components of hereditary artin algebras in the next two sections.

2.1.
Repetitive quiver

A valued translation quiver
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$},v,\tau)
is called
stable
if
\tau x
and
\tau^{-}x
are defined for all
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}
. In her classification of self-injective algebras of finite representation type, Riedtmann introduced a canonical way to construct a stable translation quiver by “knitting” repetitively a tree; see
[
31
]
. This has been generalized to “knit” any valued quivers without oriented cycles.

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$},v)
be a valued quiver without oriented cycles. The
repetitive quiver

\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
of
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is a stable valued translation quiver defined as follows:

(1)

The vertex set is

(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})_{0}=\{(s,x)\ |\ s\in\mathbb{Z};\ x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}\}.

(2)

The arrow set is
(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})_{1}=\{(s,x)\xrightarrow{(s,\alpha)}(s,y)\mbox{ and }(s,y)\xrightarrow{(s,\alpha)^{*}}(s+1,x)\mid s\in\mathbb{Z};\,\alpha:x\to y\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1}\}.

(3)

The translation

\tau:(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})_{0}\to(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})_{0}
is defined by
\tau(s,x)=(s-1,x)
, for all
(s,x)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
.

(4)

The valuation
\tilde{v}
is such that
\tilde{v}((s,\alpha))=v(\alpha)
and
\tilde{v}((s,\alpha)^{*})=v(\alpha)^{\circ}
, for all
\alpha\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1}
and
s\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
.

Remark 2.1.2
.

Since
{\mathit{\Delta}}
contains no oriented cycles, it is evident that
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
contains no oriented cycles.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
the set of paths in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
, and by
\mathcal{W}(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
the set of walks in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
.

Proposition 2.1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
be the repetitive quiver of a valued quiver
{\mathit{\Delta}}
without oriented cycles. Setting
\pi(s,\alpha)=\alpha
and
\pi(s,\alpha)^{*}=\alpha^{-1},
we obtain a length-preserving map
\pi:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})\to\mathcal{W}(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
such that
\pi(\eta\cdot\xi)=\pi(\eta)\cdot\pi(\xi)
, for all
\eta,\xi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}).

Proof.
Given a trivial path
\varepsilon_{(s,x)}
with
(s,x)\in(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})_{0}
, we set
\pi(\varepsilon_{(s,x)})=x
. Consider a path
\eta=\delta\gamma
, where
\gamma,\delta
are arrows in
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
. Assume that
\gamma=(s,\alpha)
for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha:x\to y
in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1}
. Then
\delta=(s,\alpha)^{*}
or
\delta=(s,\beta)
for some
\beta:y\to z
in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1}
. This yields a walk
\pi(\delta)\cdot\pi(\gamma)=\alpha^{-1}\alpha
in the first case and
a path
\pi(\delta)\cdot\pi(\gamma)=\beta\alpha
in the second case. In any case, we set
\pi(\eta)=\pi(\delta)\cdot\pi(\gamma)\in\mathcal{W}(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
. Similarly in case
\gamma=(s,\alpha)^{*}
, we set
\pi(\eta)=\pi(\delta)\cdot\pi(\gamma)\in\mathcal{W}(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}).
By induction,
\pi
extends to a map
\pi:\mathcal{P}(\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})\to\mathcal{W}(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
. It is easy to see that
\pi(\eta\cdot\xi)=\pi(\eta)\cdot\pi(\xi)
, for all
\eta,\xi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

In the sequel, the map
\pi:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})\to\mathcal{W}(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
defined above will be called the
covering map,
which can be used to determine sectional paths in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
as shown below.

Lemma 2.1.4
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
be the repetitive quiver of a valued quiver
{\mathit{\Delta}}
without oriented cycles. Then a path
\eta
in
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
is sectional if and only if
\pi(\eta)
is a reduced walk in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
, where
\pi
is the covering map.

Proof.
Let
\eta\in\mathcal{P}(\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
. If
\eta
is not sectional, then it contains a subpath
(s-1,\alpha)^{*}(s,\alpha)
or
(s,\alpha)(s,\alpha)^{*}
, for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{1}
. This yields a subwalk
\alpha^{-1}\alpha
or
\alpha\alpha^{-1}
of
\pi(\eta)
. So,
\pi(\eta)
is not reduced. If
\pi(\eta)
is not reduced, then we similarly show that
\eta
is not sectional. The proof of the lemma is completed.

The following statement says that
\pi
is indeed a covering map, which we do not rigorously define.

Lemma 2.1.5
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
be the repetitive quiver of a valued quiver
{\mathit{\Delta}}
without oriented cycles, with paths
\eta,\xi
such that
\pi(\eta)\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0pt\pi(\xi)
, where
\pi
is the covering map. If
s(\eta)\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0pts(\xi)
or
e(\eta)\hskip-1.0pt=\hskip-1.0pte(\xi)
, then
\eta=\xi
.

Proof.
Consider only the case where
s(\eta)=s(\xi)=(t,x)=:\tilde{x}
, for some
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
. Since
\pi
preserves the length, it is easy to see that the statement holds if
l(\eta)=0
or
1
. Suppose that
l(\eta)\geq 2
. Write
\eta=\eta^{\prime}\tilde{\gamma}
and
\xi=\xi^{\prime}\tilde{\delta}
, for some arrows
\tilde{\gamma}:\tilde{x}\to\tilde{y}
and
\tilde{\delta}:\tilde{x}\to\tilde{z}
, and some paths
\eta^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}
such that
s(\eta^{\prime})=\tilde{y}
and
s(\xi^{\prime})=\tilde{z}
. Then,

\pi(\eta^{\prime})\cdot\pi(\tilde{\gamma})=\pi(\eta)=\pi(\xi)=\pi(\xi^{\prime})\cdot\pi(\tilde{\delta})
. Since
\pi(\tilde{\gamma})
and
\pi(\tilde{\delta})
are arrows or inverse arrows in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
, we have
\pi(\tilde{\gamma})=\pi(\tilde{\delta})
and
\pi(\eta^{\prime})=\pi(\xi^{\prime})
. As seen above,
\tilde{\gamma}=\tilde{\delta}
. Then
s(\eta^{\prime})=s(\xi^{\prime})
. By the induction hypothesis,
\eta^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime}
. Hence,
\eta=\xi
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

We precisely describe the sectional paths in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
in terms of reduced walks in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
as follows.

Lemma 2.1.6
.

Let
{\mathit{\Delta}}
be a valued quiver without oriented cycles, and let
w=c_{t}\cdots c_{2}c_{1}
be a reduced walk in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
, where
c_{i}
is an arrow or an inverse arrow from
x_{i-1}
to
x_{i}
, such that the edge
\textstyle{x_{i-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{x_{i}}

in
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}}
has valuation
(v_{i},v_{i}^{\prime})
, for
i=1,\ldots,t
. Then,
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
contains a sectional path

\textstyle{\hskip-2.0pt(0,x_{0})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(r_{1},x_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(r_{t},x_{t}),}

where
r_{i}
is the number of inverse arrows in the subwalk of
w
from
x_{0}
to
x_{i}
, and
(r_{i-1},x_{i-1})\to(r_{i},x_{i})
has valuation
(v_{i},v_{i}^{\prime})
, for
i=1,\ldots,t.

Proof.
Observe that
r_{0}=0
. We may assume that
t>0
and that
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
contains a desired sectional path
(x_{0},r_{0})\to\cdots\to(r_{t-1},x_{t-1})
.
Suppose first that
{\mathit{\Delta}}
has an arrow
x_{t-1}\to x_{t}
. Then, its valuation is
(v_{t},v_{t}^{\prime})
; see (
1.3.10
) and
r_{t}=r_{t-1}
. By definition,
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
has an arrow
(r_{t-1},x_{t-1})\to(r_{t},x_{t})
with valuation
(v_{t},v_{t}^{\prime})
. Suppose now that
{\mathit{\Delta}}
has an arrow
x_{t-1}\leftarrow x_{t}
. Then, its valuation is
(v_{t}^{\prime},v_{t})
; see (
1.3.10
) and
r_{t}=r_{t-1}+1
. Thus,
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
has an arrow
(r_{t-1},x_{t-1})\to(r_{t},x_{t})
with valuation
(v_{t},v_{t}^{\prime})
. Since
x_{t}\neq x_{t-2}
, we obtain a desired sectional path as stated in the lemma. The proof of the lemma is completed.

The following easy statement is useful for inductive arguments.

Lemma 2.1.7
.

Let
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$},v,\tau)
be a stable valued translation quiver. If
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
contains a non-sectional path

\zeta:x\rightsquigarrow y
, then it contains a path
\xi:x\rightsquigarrow\tau y
with
l(\xi)=l(\zeta)-2
.

Now, we state some nice properties of repetitive quivers of valued trees.

Proposition 2.1.8
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
be the repetitive quiver of a valued tree
{\mathit{\Delta}}
with translation
\tau
.

(1)

If
\eta:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tilde{y}
is a sectional path in
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
, then it is the only path from
\tilde{x}
to
\tilde{y}
.

(2)

Any two parallel paths
\eta:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tilde{y}
and
\zeta:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tilde{y}
in
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
have the same length.

Proof.
Consider the covering map
\pi:\mathcal{P}(\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})\to\mathcal{W}(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$})
. Let
\eta:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tilde{y}
and
\zeta:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tilde{y}
be paths in
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
.

(1) If
\ell(\eta)=0
, then clearly
\xi=\eta
. Assume that
\ell(\eta)>0
. Write
\eta=\tilde{\alpha}\eta^{\prime}
, where
\tilde{\alpha}:\tilde{z}\to\tilde{y}
is an arrow and
\eta^{\prime}:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tilde{z}
is a sectional path with
l(\eta^{\prime})=l(\eta)-1.
Consider the arrow
\tilde{\beta}:\tau\tilde{y}\to\tilde{z}
in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
. If
\xi
is not sectional then, by Lemma
2.1.7
,
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
contains a path
\xi^{\prime}:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tau\tilde{y}
, and a path
\tilde{\beta}\xi^{\prime}:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tau\tilde{y}\to\tilde{z}
. By the induction hypothesis,
\eta^{\prime}=\tilde{\beta}\xi^{\prime}
, and consequently,
\eta=\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{\beta}\xi^{\prime}
, a contradiction to
\eta
being sectional. Thus,
\xi
is sectional. By Lemma
2.1.4
, both
\pi(\eta)
and
\pi(\xi)
are reduced walks in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
from
\pi(\tilde{x})
to
\pi(\tilde{y})
. Since
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is tree,
\pi(\eta)=\pi(\xi)
, and by Proposition
2.1.5
,
\eta=\xi
.

(2) By Statement (1), we may assume that
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are non-sectional. By Lemma
2.1.7
, there exist paths
\xi:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tau\tilde{y}
and
\xi^{\prime}:\tilde{x}\rightsquigarrow\tau\tilde{y}
, where
\tau
is the translation of
\mathbb{Z}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
, such that
l(\xi)=l(\eta)-2
and
l(\xi^{\prime})=l(\eta^{\prime})-2
. By the induction hypothesis,
l(\xi)=l(\xi^{\prime})
. Therefore,
l(\eta)=l(\eta^{\prime})
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

2.2.
Sections

In the existing literature, sections are defined only for connected valued translation quivers; see
[
25
, (2.1)]
. We shall drop this restriction in order to include the Auslander-Reiten quiver of the bounded derived category of a simple artin algebra; see (
4.1.2
). Throughout this subsection,
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
denotes a valued translation quiver with translation
\tau
.

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
be a valued translation quiver with translation
\tau
. A valued subquiver
{\mathit{\Delta}}
of
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
is called a
section
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

{\mathit{\Delta}}
is connected and convex in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
.

(2)

{\mathit{\Delta}}
contains no oriented cycle.

(3)

{\mathit{\Delta}}
meets every
\tau
-orbit in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
exactly once.

The following statement says that
\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{A}_{1}
is essentially the only valued translation quiver which is not connected and contains sections.

Lemma 2.2.2
.

Let
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
be a valued translation quiver with translation
\tau
, containing a non-trivial section
{\mathit{\Delta}}
. Then,

x^{+}
and
x^{-}\hskip-3.0pt
are non-empty for all
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}
. And consequently,
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
is connected.

Proof.
Let
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}
. Then
x=\tau^{s}\hskip-0.5pty
for some
y\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
and
s\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
. Since
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is non-trivial and connected, we may assume that it contains an arrow
y\to z
. Being stable,
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
contains arrows
\tau^{s}\hskip-0.5pty\to\tau^{s}\hskip-0.5ptz
and
\tau^{s+1}\hskip-0.5ptz\to\tau^{s}\hskip-0.5pty
. Thus,
x^{+}
and
x^{-}\hskip-3.0pt
are non-empty. The proof of the lemma is completed.

Let
{\mathit{\Delta}}
be a section in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
. By definition, every vertex in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
is uniquely written as
\tau^{-r}x
with
r\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
and
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}.
We shall say that
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is a
leftmost section
if all vertices in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
are of the form
\tau^{-r}x
with
r\geq 0
and
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0};
and a
rightmost section
if all vertices in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
are of the form
\tau^{r}x
with
r\geq 0
and
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
.

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Let
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
be a valued translation quiver with translation
\tau
, containing a section
{\mathit{\Delta}}
.

(1)

If
x\to y
is an arrow in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
, then
y\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
implies
x
or
\tau^{-}x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$};
and
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
implies
y
or
\tau y\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
.

(2)

There exists a valued translation quiver embedding
\phi:\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}\to\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
,
sending
\tau^{s}x
to
(-s,x)
.

(3)

If
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is leftmost or rightmost, then it is the unique leftmost or rightmost section in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
, respectively.

Proof.
Statements (1) and (2) are quoted from
[
25
, (2.2), (2.3)]
, and Statement (2) follows immediately from the definitions. The proof of the proposition is completed.

In view of Proposition
2.2.3
(2), we will see that valued translation quivers with sections inherits many nice properties of repetitive quivers. We will also need the following notion.

Definition 2.2.4
.

Let
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
be a valued translation quiver with a vertex
x
. A section
{\mathit{\Delta}}
in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
is called an
x
-source section
if every vertex in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is a successor of
x
in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$};
and an
x
-sink section
if every vertex in
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is a predecessor of
x
in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}.

Given a vertex
x
in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
, we denote by

{\rm Suc}(x)
and
{\rm Pred}(x)
the full valued translation subquivers of
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
generated by the successors of
x
and by the predecessors of
x
, respectively.

Lemma 2.2.5
.

Let
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
be a valued translation quiver with a vertex
x
.

(1)

If
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is an
x
-source section in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
, then it is unique in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
and a leftmost section in
{\rm Suc}(x)
.

(2)

If
{\mathit{\Sigma}}
is an
x
-sink section in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
, then it is unique in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
and a rightmost section in
{\rm Pred}(x)
.

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1). Let
{\mathit{\Delta}}
be an
x
-source section in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
. Then,
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}\subseteq{\rm Suc}(x)
. Thus,
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is a section in
{\rm Suc}(x)
. Suppose that
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
contains a path
x\rightsquigarrow y
. Then,
y=\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptz
for some
r\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
and
z\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
. If
r<0
, then
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
contains a path
x\rightsquigarrow y\rightsquigarrow z
. Since
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is convex in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
, both
y
and
\tau^{r}y
belong to
{\mathit{\Delta}}
, a contradiction. Thus,
r\geq 0
. That is,
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is a leftmost section in
{\rm Suc}(x)
, which is unique by Lemma
2.2.3
. Thus,
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is the unique
x
-source section in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

The following statement is important for our investigation in the next section.

Proposition 2.2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
be the repetitive quiver of a connected valued tree
{\mathit{\Delta}}
with a vertex
(r,x)
. Then the sectional successors of
(r,x)
in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
generate an
(r,x)
-source section in
\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$};
and the sectional predecessors of
(r,x)
in
\mathbb{Z}

generate an
(r,x)
-sink section in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}

Proof.
We shall only prove the first part of the statement. Let
{\mathit{\Omega}}
be the full valued subquiver of
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
generated by the sectional successors of
(r,x)
in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
. It is easy to see that every vertex in
{\mathit{\Omega}}
is a successor of
(r,x)
in
{\mathit{\Omega}}
. In particular,
{\mathit{\Omega}}
is connected and contains no oriented cycle. Now, suppose that
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
contains a path
(s,y)\rightsquigarrow(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rightsquigarrow(t,z)
, where
(s,y),(t,z)\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Omega}}$}_{0}
. Then,
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
contains sectional paths
\zeta:(r,x)\rightsquigarrow(s,y)
and
\xi:(r,x)\rightsquigarrow(t,z)
. By Lemma
2.1.8
,

(r,x)\rightsquigarrow(s,y)\rightsquigarrow(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\rightsquigarrow(t,z)
coincides with
\xi
. Thus,
(r,x)\rightsquigarrow(s,y)\rightsquigarrow(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})
is sectional, and
(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Omega}}$}
. So,
{\mathit{\Omega}}
is convex in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
.

Consider finally any
y\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
. Since
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is connected, by Lemma
2.1.6
,
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
has a sectional path
(r,x)\rightsquigarrow(s_{0},y)
with
s_{0}\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
. So,
(s_{0},y)\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Omega}}$}
. Suppose that
(t_{0},y)\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
for another
t_{0}\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
, say
s_{0}\leq t_{0}
. Then,
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
contains a path
(s_{0},y)\rightsquigarrow(t_{0},y)
and a sectional path
(r,x)\rightsquigarrow(t_{0},y)
. By Lemma
2.1.8
, the path
(r,x)\rightsquigarrow(s_{0},y)\rightsquigarrow(t_{0},y)
is sectional. So,
s_{0}=t_{0}.
Therefore,
{\mathit{\Omega}}
meets every
\tau
-orbit in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
exactly once. Thus,
{\mathit{\Omega}}
is a
(r,x)
-source section in
\mathbb{Z}
{\mathit{\Delta}}
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

2.3.
Additive functions

In the study of Auslander-Reiten components of artin algebras, a powerful tool is additive functions on translation quivers; see, for example,
[
10
,
19
,
33
]
. We shall need it to describe hammocks for hereditary artin algebras of finite representation type.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$},\tau,v)
be a valued translation quiver. A function
f:\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}\to\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
is called
additive
on
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
provided, for any
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{0}
with
\tau x
defined, that
f(\tau x)+f(x)=\sum_{y\in x^{-}}v^{\prime}_{yx}f(y),\vskip-1.0pt
where the sum is zero in case
x^{-}
is empty.

Proposition 2.3.2
.

Let
(\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$},\tau,v)
be a stable valued translation quiver with a finite section
{\mathit{\Delta}}
. Given
r_{x}\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
with
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
, there exists a unique additive function
f
on
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
such that
f(x)=r_{x}
for all
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
.

Proof.
It suffices to establish the existence of
f
. We start with setting
f(x)=r_{x}\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
, for all
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}.
Given
s\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
, the vertices
\tau^{s}x
with
x\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
generate a section
\tau^{s}\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
in
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
. We shall first define
f
on
\tau\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
. Being finite without oriented cycles,
{\mathit{\Delta}}
contains a sink vertex
x_{1}
. By Lemma
2.2.3
(1),
x_{1}^{-}\subseteq\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}
. Put
f(\tau x_{1})=\sum_{y\in x_{1}^{-}}v_{x_{1}\hskip-0.6pt,y}^{\prime}f(y)-f(x_{1})
.

Clearly, the vertices in
\{\tau x_{1}\}\cup(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}\backslash\{x_{1}\})
generate a section
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{(1)}
, on which
f
is defined. Suppose that
f
is defined on a section
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{(i)}
generated by the vertices in
\{\tau x_{1},\ldots,\tau x_{i}\}\cup(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}\backslash\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\})
for some
i\geq 1
. If

\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}
, then
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{(i)}=\tau\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
. Otherwise,
{\mathit{\Delta}}
is non-trivial and there exists
x_{i+1}\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}\backslash\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}
such that
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{(i)}
contains no arrow
x_{i+1}\to y
with
y\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}\backslash\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}\}
. Suppose that
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{(i)}
contains an arrow
x_{i+1}\to\tau x_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq i
. Since
x_{j}^{-}\neq\emptyset
; see (
2.2.2
),
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
contains a path
x_{i+1}\to\tau x_{j}\rightsquigarrow x_{j}
, and hence,
\tau x_{j}\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
, absurd. Thus,
x_{i+1}
is a sink in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{(i)}
. By Lemma
2.2.3
(1),
x_{i+1}^{-}\subseteq\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{(i)}
.

Define
f(\tau x_{i+1})=\sum_{y\in x_{i+1}^{-}}v_{x_{i+1}\hskip-0.6pt,y}^{\prime}f(y)-f(x_{i+1}).
Then,
f
is defined on the section
\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}^{(i+1)}
generated by the vertices in
\{\tau x_{1},\ldots,\tau x_{i+1}\}\cup(\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}_{0}\backslash\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{i+1}\})
. By induction, we may define
f
on
\tau\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
. Repeating this process, we may define
f
on
\tau^{s}\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
for all
s>0
. Dually, considering sources in sections, we define
f
on
\tau^{-s}\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}
for all
s>0
. This yields a desired additive function
f
on
{\mathit{\Gamma}}
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

3.
Hereditary artin algebras

The main objective of this section is to study the preprojective and preinjective components of a connected hereditary artin algebra. Although they have already been well studied; see
[
2
, Section VIII.1]
and
[
32
, Pages 80 - 81]
, we shall further study them in terms of the ext-quiver of the algebra. In case the ext-quiver is a tree, a sectional path in the preprojective component is the unique path between the end-points, two parallel paths have the same length,
and every projective module is the source of a source section, which is explicitly described in terms of reduced walks in the ext-quiver;
see (
3.1.5
) and (
3.2.1
). Finally, we shall show that the Coxeter transformation is compatible with the derived Auslander-Reiten translation; see (
3.3.2
).

Throughout this section,
H
stands for a connected hereditary artin algebra with ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
, and
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
for the category of finitely generated left
H
-modules. We simply write
\tau
for the Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau_{\hskip-0.6pt{}_{H}}
of the Auslander-Reiten quiver
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
. And we associate a simple module
S_{i}
, a projective module
P_{i}
and an injective module
I_{i}
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
with each
i\in(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
such that
{\rm top}P_{i}\cong S_{i}\cong{\rm soc}I_{i}
.

3.1.
Preprojective and preinjective components

The following result is probably well-known. We sketch a proof for the first statement and refer to
[
2
, Page 267]
for a proof for the second.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra with ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
. Consider the simple modules
S_{i}
, the projective modules
P_{i}
and the injective modules
I_{i}
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
associated with
i\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
.

(1)

If
i
is a vertex in
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
, then
\End_{H}(P_{i})\cong\End_{H}(I_{i})\cong\End_{H}(S_{i})
and
\ell_{S_{i}}(P_{i})=\ell_{S_{i}}(I_{i})=1
.

(2)

There exists an arrow
i\to j
in
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
if and only if there exists an irreducible map
f:P_{j}\to P_{i}
, or equivalently, an irreducible map
g:I_{j}\to I_{i}
, in
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
.

Proof.
Fix
i\in(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
. Since
I_{i}
is not a direct summand of the injective module
I_{i}/S_{i}
, we have
\Hom_{H}(S_{i},I_{i}/S_{i})=0.
Thus, we deduce from Lemma
1.7.1
that
\ell_{S_{i}}(I_{i})=1
. Since
S_{i}\cong{\rm soc}I_{i}
, every non-zero map in
\End_{H}(S_{i})
induces a non-zero map in
\End_{H}(I_{i})
. Consider a non-zero map
f:I_{i}\to I_{i}
. Since
{\rm Im}(f)
is injective,
f
is invertible. Since
\Hom_{H}(S_{i},I_{i}/S_{i})=0
, we see that
f
is induced from a non-zero map
g:S_{i}\to S_{i}
. Thus,
\End_{H}(I_{i})\cong\End_{H}(S_{i})
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

Since
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
is connected, by Lemma
3.1.1
(2), the projective modules in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{{\rm mod}H}
lie in the same connected component
\mathscr{P}_{H}
, called the
preprojective component
; and the injective modules lie in the same connected component
\mathscr{I}_{H}
, called the
preinjective component
. The following statement is reformulated from Proposition 1.15 in
[
2
, Chapter VIII]
and its dual statement. Since our valuation for
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
is different from that given in
[
2
, Page 225]
, we include a detailed proof for the preinjective component.

Theorem 3.1.2
.

Let
H
be a connected hereditary artin
R
-algebra with
ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
. Consider the projective modules
P_{i}
and the injective modules
I_{i}
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
associated with
i\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
.

(1)

The projective modules
P_{i}
with
i\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
generate a leftmost section
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}
in the preprojective component
\mathscr{P}_{H}
. Moreover, there exists a valued quiver isomorphism
\hbox{$Q\hskip-0.8pt_{H}^{\hskip 0.8pt\rm op}$}\to\hbox{$\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}$},
sending
i
to
P_{i}
.

(2)

The injective modules
I_{i}
with
i\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
generate a rightmost section
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
in the preinjective component
\mathscr{I}_{H}
. Moreover, there exists a valued quiver isomorphism
\hbox{$Q\hskip-0.8pt_{H}^{\hskip 0.8pt\rm op}$}\to\hbox{$\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}$},
sending
i
to
I_{i}
.

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (2). We write
\ell_{R}(V)
for the
R
-length of an
R
-module
V,
and put
k_{M}={\rm End}_{H}(M)/{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt({\rm End}_{H}(M))
for
M\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{{\rm mod}H}
. Let
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
be the full valued subquiver of
\mathscr{I}_{H}
generated by the
I_{i}
with
i\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
. Set
S_{i}={\rm soc}I_{i}
for
i\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
. By Lemma
3.1.1
(2),
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
contains an arrow
i\to j
if and only if
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
contains an arrow
I_{j}\to I_{i}
; and in this case, the valuation for
i\to j
is
(d_{ij},d_{ij}^{\prime})
, where
d_{ij}={\rm dim}_{{\rm End}_{H}(S_{j})}\Ext_{H}^{1}(S_{i},S_{j})
and
d_{ij}^{\prime}={\rm dim}\Ext_{H}^{1}(S_{i},S_{j})_{{\rm End}_{H}(S_{i})}.
Thus,

d_{ij}^{\prime}\cdot\ell_{R}(\End_{H}(S_{i}))=\ell_{R}(\Ext_{H}^{1}(S_{i},S_{j}))=d_{ij}\cdot\ell_{R}(\End_{H}(S_{j})).\vskip-2.0pt

On the other hand, we see from Proposition
1.5.1
that
I_{j}\to I_{i}
has valuation
(d_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}}\hskip-0.5pt,d^{\prime}_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}})
, where
d_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}}={\rm dim}\,_{k_{I_{i}}}{\rm irr}(I_{j},I_{i})
and
d_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}}^{\prime}={\rm dim}\,{\rm irr}(I_{j},I_{i})_{k_{I_{j}}}\hskip-2.0pt.
Since
k_{I_{j}}\cong\End_{H}(S_{j})
and
k_{I_{i}}\cong\End_{H}(S_{i})
by Lemma
3.1.1
(1),
d_{I_{j},I_{i}}^{\prime}\cdot\ell_{R}(\End_{H}(S_{j}))=\ell_{R}({\rm Irr}(I_{j},I_{i}))=d_{I_{j},I_{i}}\cdot\ell_{R}(\End_{H}(S_{i})).

Now since
I_{j}/S_{j}
is injective, by Proposition 1.15(b) in
[
2
, (III.1.15)]
,
d_{ij}^{\prime}
is the multiplicity of
I_{i}
as a direct summand of
I_{j}/S_{j}
. And since the canonical projection
\varphi\hskip-1.0pt:\hskip-1.0ptI_{j}\to I_{j}/S_{j}
is minimal left almost split,
d_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}}
is the multiplicity of
I_{i}
as a direct summand of
I_{j}/S_{j}
; see (
1.5.1
). So,
d_{ij}^{\prime}=d_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}}
. Hence,

\hskip 30.0ptd_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}}^{\prime}\cdot\ell_{R}(\End_{H}(S_{j}))=d_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}}\cdot\ell_{R}(\End_{H}(S_{i}))=d_{ij}^{\prime}\cdot\ell_{R}(\End_{H}(S_{i}))=d_{ij}\cdot\ell_{R}(\End_{H}(S_{j})).\vskip-3.0pt

Thus,
d_{I_{\hskip-0.8ptj\hskip-0.6pt},I_{i}}^{\prime}=d_{ij}
. This proves the second part of Statement (2). In particular,
\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
is connected. Being injective, the
I_{i}
lie in pairwise distinct
\tau
-orbits in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
. Note that the irreducible maps between the modules
I_{i}
are all epimorphisms. Thus, since
\End_{H}(I_{i})
is divisible; see (
3.1.1
),
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
has no oriented cycle. Hence,
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
is a section in
\mathscr{I}_{H}
, which is clearly rightmost. The proof of the theorem is completed.

In the sequel, we shall call the underlying valued graph
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
of
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
the
ext-graph
of
H
. Moreover,
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}
will be called the
projective section
in
\mathscr{P}_{H}
, and
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
called the
injective section
in
\mathscr{I}_{H}
. The following well-known statement is a consequence of Theorem
3.1.2
and well-known; see
[
2
, (V.7.5), (VIII.1.9)]
.

Corollary 3.1.3
.

Let
H
be a connected hereditary artin algebra. Consider modules
M,N
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
with
M
in the preinjective component
\mathscr{I}_{H}
or
N
in the preprojective component
\mathscr{P}_{H}
. Then, every non-zero radical map
f:M\to N
is a sum of composites of irreducible maps between modules in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
.

We shall need the following easy statement.

Lemma 3.1.4
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra. Consider an arrow
M\to N
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
.

(1)

If
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains
\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptM
for some
r\geq 0
, then it contains an arrow
\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptM\to\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptN.

(2)

If
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains
\tau^{-r}\hskip-1.0ptN
for some
r\geq 0
, then it contains an arrow
\tau^{-r}\hskip-1.0ptM\to\tau^{-r}N
.

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1). Assume that

\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptM\hskip-1.0pt\in\hskip-1.0pt\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
for some
r>0
, but
\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptN\hskip-1.0pt\not\in\hskip-1.0pt\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
. Then,
\tau^{s}\hskip-1.0ptN
is projective for some
0\leq s<r
. Since
\tau^{s}M\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
, by Lemma
1.4.1
,
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains an arrow
\tau^{s}\hskip-1.0ptM\to\tau^{s}\hskip-1.0ptN
. Since
H
is hereditary,
\tau^{s}\hskip-1.0ptM
is projective, and hence,
\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptM\not\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
, absurd. Thus,
\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptN\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
. So,
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains an arrow
\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptM\to\tau^{r}\hskip-1.0ptN
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

The following statement describes in terms of
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
the sectional paths in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
, which start with a projective module or end with an injective module.

Lemma 3.1.5
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra with ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
. Consider the projective modules
P_{i}
and the injective modules
I_{i}
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
associated with
i\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
. And let
w=c_{t}\cdots c_{2}c_{1}
be a non-trivial reduced walk in
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
, where
c_{j}
is an arrow or inverse arrow from
i_{j-1}
to
i_{j}
, for
j=1,\ldots,t
.

(1)

The preprojective component
\mathscr{P}_{H}
of
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains a sectional path

\textstyle{P_{i_{0}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{-r_{1}}\!P_{i_{1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{-r_{t}}\!P_{i_{t}},}

where
r_{j}
is the number of arrows in the subwalk of
w
from
i_{0}
to
i_{j}
, for
j=1,\ldots,t
.

(2)

The preinjective component
\mathscr{I}_{H}
of
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains a sectional path

\textstyle{\tau^{s_{0}}\!I_{i_{0}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{s_{1}}\!I_{i_{1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{s_{t-1}}\!I_{i_{t-1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I_{i_{t}},}

where
s_{j}
is the number of arrows in the subwalk of
w
from
i_{j}
to
i_{t}
, for
j=0,1,\ldots,t-1
.

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1). Since
w
is reduced,
the
P_{i_{j}}
with
0\leq j\leq t
lie in pairwise distinct
\tau
-orbits in
\mathscr{P}_{H}
.

Set
r_{0}=0
. Assume that
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains a desired sectional path
\textstyle{\hskip-3.0ptP_{i_{0}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}.}

Assume first that
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
contains an arrow
i_{t-1}\to i_{t}
. Then
r_{t}=r_{t-1}+1
and by Theorem
3.1.2
(1),
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains an arrow
P_{j_{t}}\to P_{j_{t-1}}.
By
Lemma
3.1.4
(2),
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains an arrow

\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t}}\!\to\!\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}
.
Since
r_{t-1}+1=r_{t},

it follows from Lemma
1.6.2
(1) that
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains a sectional path

\textstyle{\hskip-2.0ptP_{i_{0}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{-r_{t}}\!P_{i_{t}}.}

Assume now that
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
contains an arrow
i_{t-1}\leftarrow i_{t}
. Then
r_{t}=r_{t-1}
and
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains an arrow
P_{i_{t-1}}\to P_{i_{t}}
. We claim that
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains an arrow
\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}\!\to\!\tau^{-r_{t}}\!P_{i_{t}}
.
This is the case if
r_{t-1}=0
. Otherwise,
\mathscr{P}_{H}
has an arrow
P_{i_{t}}\to\tau^{-}\hskip-1.2ptP_{i_{t-1}}
.
Since
\tau^{1-r_{t-1}}(\tau^{-}P_{i_{t-1}})=\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}

3.1.4
(1),
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains an arrow
\tau^{1-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t}}\to\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}
. By Lemma
1.6.2
(1),
\tau^{1-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t}}
is not injective. Since

, there exists an arrow
\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}\!\to\!\tau^{-r_{t}}\!P_{i_{t}}
in
\mathscr{P}_{H}
.

This establishes our claim. Hence,
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains a sectional path
\textstyle{P_{i_{0}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{-r_{t-1}}\!P_{i_{t-1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau^{-r_{t}}\!P_{i_{t}}.}
The proof of the lemma is completed.

The following statement is crucial for us to study hammocks in the next section.

Proposition 3.1.6
.

Let
H
be a connected hereditary artin algebra.

(1)

The preprojective component
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains a
P
-source section for every projective module
P
in
\mathscr{P}_{H}
.

(2)

The preinjective component
\mathscr{I}_{H}
contains an
I
-sink section for every injective module
I
in
\mathscr{I}_{H}
.

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1). Consider a module
P
in the projective section
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}
in
\mathscr{P}_{H}
. Let
{\mathit{\Sigma}}
be the full valued subquiver of
\mathscr{P}_{H}
generated by the successors
M
of
P
in
\mathscr{P}_{H}
such that
\tau M
is not a succesor of
P
. Then,
{\mathit{\Sigma}}
is contains no oriented cycle and meets any
\tau
-orbit in
\mathscr{P}_{H}
at most once. Given
P^{\prime}\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}$}
, since the ext-quiver of
H
is connected, we deduce from Lemma
3.1.5
(1) that there exists a minimal
s\geq 0
such that
\tau^{-s}\hskip-1.5ptP^{\prime}
is a successor of
P
in
\mathscr{P}_{H}
. Then
\tau^{-s}\hskip-1.5ptP^{\prime}\in\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}
. Hence,
{\mathit{\Sigma}}
meets every
\tau
-orbit in

exactly once. Finally, suppose that

contains a path
\eta:M\rightsquigarrow L\rightsquigarrow N

\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains a path

We shall say that

Proposition 3.2.1
.

Let

(1)

(2)

(3)

Proof.
(1) Let
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}

embeds in

\mathbb{Z}\hbox{$\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}$}

), we see that Statement (1) follows from Proposition

(2) Given

is a sectional successor

. Suppose that

The following statement will enable us to define extended hammocks in the next section.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Let
H
be a connected hereditary artin algebra of tree type. Consider a sectional path
\textstyle{P=M_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{t-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{t}}
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
, where

is a projective module with top
S
, and
M_{i-1}\to M_{i}
with

(d_{i},d_{i}^{\prime})

\ell_{S}(M_{i})=d_{i}^{\prime}\cdots d_{1}^{\prime},
for

Proof.
By Lemma
3.1.1
(1),
\ell_{S}(M_{0})=1.
Suppose that
t>0
. Consider a minimal right almost split map
f:L\to M_{t}
. Since
P\not\cong M_{t}
,
the
{\rm End}_{H}(P)
-linear map

is an epimorphism. We claim that it is an isomorphism. If
M_{t}
is projective, then
f
is a monomorphism, and so is
f_{*}
. Otherwise, we have an exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom_{H}(P,\tau M_{t})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom_{H}(P,L)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{*}}
\textstyle{\Hom_{H}(P,M_{t})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

Since the path stated in the lemma is sectional, by Lemma
3.2.1
(1),
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains no path from
P
to
\tau M_{t}
. Hence,
\Hom_{H}(P,\tau M_{t})=0
; see (
3.1.3
). This establishes our claim. Therefore,
\ell_{S}(M_{t})=\ell_{S}(L)
; see (

). On the other hand, by Proposition
1.5.1
,

, where
L_{1}\ldots,L_{r}

\mathscr{P}_{H}
different from

. By Lemma
3.2.1
(1),
\mathscr{P}_{H}
contains no path from

to

, and hence,

\Hom_{H}(P,L_{j})=0
and
\ell_{S}(L_{j})=0
, for

. By the induction hypothesis, we see that
\ell_{S}(M_{t})=\ell_{S}(L)=d_{t}^{\prime}\ell_{S}(M_{t-1})=d_{t}^{\prime}\cdots d_{1}^{\prime}.
The proof of the lemma is completed.

4.
Main Results

The objective of this section is to study the module category of a hereditary artin algebra of finite representation type with a connection to the derived category and the associated cluster category. We first determine all the hammocks in the Auslander-Reiten quiver; see (
4.2.4
). This leads to a description of the precise shape of the Auslander-Reiten quiver in terms of the ext-quiver of the algebra; see (
4.5.1
). As applications, we obtain the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable objects in the module category and the associated cluster category; see (
4.6.1
) and (
4.6.6
). Moreover, the radicals of the module category, the bounded derived category and the associated cluster category all have the same nilpotency; see (
4.6.4
), (
4.6.5
), and (
4.6.6
).

It is well-known that a hereditary artin algebra is connected of finite representation type if and only if its ext-graph is a Dynkin diagram; see
[
2
, (VIII.5.4)]
. In the algebraically closed case, this is equivalent to the ext-graph being
\mathbb{A}_{n}(n\geq 1)
,
\mathbb{D}_{n}(n\geq 4)
or
\mathbb{E}_{n}(n=6,7,8)
; see
[
2
, (VIII.5.5)]
.

Throughout this section,
H
stands for a hereditary artin algebbra with a Dynkin ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
, and
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
for the category of finitely generated left
H
-modules. Let
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
and
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8ptD^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
be the Auslander-Reiten quivers of
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
and
D^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
, respectively. We shall simply write
\tau
for the Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau_{\hskip-0.6pt{}_{H}}
of
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
, and
\tau_{\hskip-0.5pt{}_{D}}
for the Auslander-Reiten translation of
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8ptD^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
. With each vertex

, we associate a simple module
S_{i}
, a projective module

and an injective module

4.1.
The canonical embedding

\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
, then

m(i)-m(j)=\mathfrak{a}^{+}(\rho(i),\hskip 0.3pt\rho(j))-\mathfrak{a}^{+}(i,j).\vskip 0.5pt

Proof.
Consider the projective modules
P_{i}
and the injective modules
I_{i}
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
associated with
i\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
. By Proposition
4.1.1
, we have a canonical embedding
\varphi:\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}\to\mathbb{Z}Q^{\rm op}_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
, sending
\tau^{-r}\hskip-1.5ptP_{i}
to
(r,i).
Write
r_{p,q}=\mathfrak{a}^{+}(p,q)
for
p,q\in(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
.
Fix some vertices
i,j\in\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}
. Observe that
r_{i,j}
is the number of inverse arrows in the reduced walk in
Q^{\rm op}_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
from
i
to
j
. By Lemma
2.1.6
(2),
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 0.8ptQ^{\rm op}_{\hskip-1.0ptH}
contains a sectional path from
(0,i)
to
(r_{i,j},j)
, and hence, a sectional path from
(m(j)-r_{i,j},i)
to
(m(j),j)
.

On the other hand, since
r_{\rho(i),\hskip 0.3pt\rho(j)}
is the number of arrows in the reduced walk in
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
from
\rho(i)
to
\rho(j)
, by Lemma
3.1.5
(2),
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains a sectional path from
\tau^{\hskip 0.5ptr_{\rho(i),\hskip 0.3pt\rho(j)}\hskip-1.0pt}I_{\rho(i)}
to
I_{\rho(j)}
. By definition,
I_{\rho(i)}=\tau^{-m(i)}\hskip-1.2ptP_{i}
and
I_{\rho(j)}=\tau^{-m(j)}\hskip-1.2ptP_{j}
. Thus,
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains a sectional path from
\tau^{\hskip 0.5ptr_{\rho(i),\hskip 0.3pt\rho(j)}-m(i)\hskip-1.2pt}P_{i}
to
\tau^{-m(j)}\hskip-1.2ptP_{j},
which induces a sectional path in
\mathbb{Z}Q^{\rm op}_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
from
(m(i)-r_{\rho(i),\hskip 0.3pt\rho(j)},i)
to
(m(j),j)
. By Proposition
2.2.6
,

both
(m(j)-r_{i,j},i)
and
(m(i)-r_{\rho(i),\hskip 0.3pt\rho(j)},i)
belong to the
(m(j),j)
-sink section in
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 0.8ptQ^{\rm op}_{\hskip-1.0ptH}
, and consequently,
m(i)-r_{\rho(i),\hskip 0.3pt\rho(j)}=m(j)-r_{i,j}
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

Next,

we shall show that the pi-permutation of
(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
is of order at most two. For doing this, we shall extend it to an automorphism of the ext-graph
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
in the following statement.

Proposition 4.3.3
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra with a Dynkin ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
. Then the pi-permutation
\rho
of
(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
induces a valued graph automorphism
of
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-1.5pt\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$}\hskip-7.0pt}\hskip 7.0pt,

called the
pi-automorphism
and denoted again by
\rho.

Proof.
Let
\rho
be the pi-permutation of
(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
and
m
the pi-index function on
(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
. Consider the projective modules
P_{i}
and the injective modules
I_{i}
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
associated with
i\in(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
. Let
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}
be the projective section generated by the
P_{i}
, and
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
the injective section generated by the
I_{i}
, in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
. We first construct a valued graph isomorphism
\rho^{*}:\hskip 3.0pt\overline{\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}}_{\hskip-0.5ptH}\to\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}}\hskip-1.5pt_{H},
sending
P_{i}
to
I_{\rho(i)}.

Fix
i,j\in(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
. Suppose that
\hskip 3.0pt\overline{\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}}_{\hskip-0.5ptH}
contains an edge
\textstyle{\hskip-2.0ptP_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P_{j}\hskip-2.0pt}
with valuation
(d,d^{\prime})
.

We claim that
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}}\hskip-1.5pt_{H}
contains an edge

\textstyle{I_{\rho(i)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I_{\rho(j)}}

with valuation
(d,d^{\prime})
. First, assume that
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}
contains an arrow
P_{i}\to P_{j}
, whose valuation is then
(d,d^{\prime})
; see (
1.3.10
). Note that
\tau^{-m(i)}\hskip-1.5ptP_{i}=I_{\rho(i)}
and
\tau^{-m(j)}\hskip-1.5ptP_{j}=I_{\rho(j)}
. By Lemma
3.1.4
(2),
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains an arrow
\tau^{-m(j)}\hskip-1.5ptP_{i}\to I_{\rho(j)}
, whose valuation is
(d,d^{\prime})
; see (
1.4.1
). So,
m(j)\leq m(i)
.

If
m(j)=m(i)
, then
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
contains an arrow
I_{\rho(i)}\to I_{\rho(j)}
with valuation
(d,d^{\prime})
, and hence,
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}}\hskip-1.5pt_{H}
contains an edge
\textstyle{I_{\rho(i)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I_{\rho(j)}}
with valuation
(d,d^{\prime})
. If
m(j)<m(i)
, then
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains an arrow
I_{\rho(j)}\to\tau^{-m(j)-1}\hskip-1.5ptP_{i}
with valuation
(d^{\prime},d)
.
Since
H
is hereditary,
\tau^{-m(j)-1}\hskip-1.5ptP_{i}
is injective, and hence,
\tau^{-m(j)-1}\hskip-1.5ptP_{i}=I_{\rho(i)}
. By definition,
m(i)=m(j)+1
,

and hence,
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
has an arrow
I_{\rho(j)}\to I_{\rho(i)}
with valuation
(d^{\prime},d)
.

So,
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}}\hskip-1.5pt_{H}
contains an edge
\textstyle{I_{\rho(j)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I_{\rho(i)}}
with valuation
(d^{\prime},d)
, that is, an edge
\textstyle{I_{\rho(i)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I_{\rho(j)}}
with valuation
(d,d^{\prime})
.
Next,

assume that
\mathit{\Delta}_{\hskip-0.2ptH}
contains an arrow
P_{j}\to P_{i}
, whose valuation is then
(d^{\prime},d)
; see (
1.3.10
).

As has been shown,
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}}\hskip-1.5pt_{H}
contains an edge
\textstyle{I_{\rho(j)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I_{\rho(i)}}
with valuation
(d^{\prime},d)
, that is, an edge
\textstyle{I_{\rho(i)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I_{\rho(j)}}
with valuation
(d,d^{\prime})
. This establishes our claim.

Suppose, on the other hand, that
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}}\hskip-1.5pt_{H}
contains an edge
\textstyle{I_{\rho(i)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{I_{\rho(j).}}

We shall show that
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}}\hskip-1.0pt_{H}
contains an edge
\textstyle{P_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P_{j}.}

Note that
P_{i}=\tau^{\hskip 0.5ptm(i)}\hskip-0.6ptI_{\rho(i)}
and
P_{j}=\tau^{\hskip 0.5ptm(j)}\hskip-0.6ptI_{\rho(j)}
. Assume first that
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
contains an arrow
I_{\rho(i)}\to I_{\rho(j)}
.

By Lemma
3.1.4
(2),
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains an arrow
P_{i}\to\tau^{m(i)}\!I_{\rho(j)}
.

So,
m(i)\leq m(j)
. If
m(i)=m(j)
, then
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
contains an arrow
P_{i}\to P_{j}
, and hence,
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}}\hskip-1.0pt_{H}
contains an edge
\textstyle{P_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P_{j}.}

If
m(i)<m(j)
, then
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains an arrow
\tau^{\hskip 0.5ptm(i)+1}\hskip-1.2ptI_{\rho(j)}\to P_{i}
. Since
H
is hereditary,
\tau^{\hskip 0.5ptm(i)+1}\!I_{\rho(j)}
is projective.

Then
m(j)=m(i)+1
, and hence,
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
contains an arrow
P_{j}\to P_{i}
. So,
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}}\hskip-1.0pt_{H}
contains an edge
\textstyle{P_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P_{j}.}

In case
\mathit{\Sigma}_{\hskip-0.3ptH}
contains an arrow
I_{\rho(j)}\to I_{\rho(i)}
, by a similar argument,

\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}}\hskip-1.0pt_{H}
contains an edge
\textstyle{P_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P_{j}.}

Thus, we have a valued graph isomorphism
\rho^{*}:\hskip 3.0pt\overline{\hskip-3.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}}_{\hskip-0.5ptH}\to\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}}\hskip-1.5pt_{H},
sending
P_{i}
to
I_{\rho(i)}
.

Finally,

the valued quiver isomorphisms stated in Theorem
3.1.2
induce a valued graph isomorphism
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}^{\hskip-4.0pt\rm op}\to\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Delta}}$}\hskip-0.5pt}_{H}
sending
i
to
P_{i}
, and a valued graph isomorphism
\hskip 2.0pt\overline{\hskip-2.0pt\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}}\hskip-1.5pt_{H}\to\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}^{\hskip-4.0pt\rm op}
sending
I_{i}
to
i
. Composing these two isomorphisms with
\rho^{*},
we obtain a valued graph automorphism
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}^{\hskip-4.0pt\rm op}\to\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}^{\hskip-4.0pt\rm op},
sending
i
to
\rho(i)
. This induces a desired valued graph automorphism
\rho:\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}\to\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$},
sending
i
to
\rho(i)
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

We collect some easy properties of the pi-automorphism in the following statement.

Lemma 4.3.4
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra with a Dynkin ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
, and let
\rho
be the pi-automorphism of
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
.

(1)

If
i
is a vertex in
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
of weight
3
, then
\rho(i)=i
.

(2)

If
\textstyle{i\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{j}
is a non-trivially valued edge in
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
, then
\rho(i)=i
and
\rho(j)=j
.

(3)

If
\textstyle{i\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{j\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{k}
is a reduced walk in
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
, where
j
has only two neighbors
i
and
k
, such that
\rho(i)=i
and
\rho(j)=j
, then
\rho(k)=k
.

Proof.
(1) Being a Dynkin diagram,
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
has at most one vertex of weight
3
; see (
1.3.8
). And by Lemma
1.3.7
,
{\rm w}(i)={\rm w}(\rho(i))
for any vertex
i
in
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
. Thus, if
{\rm w}(i)=3
, then
\rho(i)=i
.

(2) Let
\textstyle{i\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{j}
be an edge in
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
with a non-trivial valuation
(d_{ij},d_{ji})
. Since
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
is a Dynkin digram, this is the only non-trivially valued edge, and moreover,
d_{ij}\neq d_{ji}
; see (
1.3.8
). Since
\rho
is a valuded graph automorphism,
\rho(i),\rho(j)\in\{i,j\}
and
d_{\rho(i)\rho(j)}=d_{ij}
. If
\rho(i)=j
, then
\rho(j)=i
. So,
d_{ji}=d_{\rho(i)\rho(j)}=d_{ij}
, a contradiction. Thus,
\rho(i)=i
and
\rho(j)=j
.

(3) Let

\textstyle{i\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{j\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{k}
be a reduced walk in
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
, where
j
has only two neighbors
i
and
k
. If

\rho(j)=j
, then
\textstyle{j\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\rho(k)}
is an edge in
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
, and hence,
\rho(k)\in\{i,k\}
. If
\rho(i)=i
, then
\rho(k)=k
. The proof of the lemma is completed.

We are ready to determine the order of the pi-automorphism for every Dynkin type. In particular, the pi-automorphism is the identity for all non-simply laced types.

Theorem 4.3.5
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra of Dynkin type, and let
\rho
be the pi-automorphism
\rho
of the ext-graph
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
.

(1)

If
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
is of type
\mathbb{A}_{1}
,
\mathbb{G}_{2}
,
\mathbb{F}_{4}
,
\mathbb{E}_{7},

\mathbb{E}_{8}
,
\mathbb{B}_{n}(n\hskip-1.5pt\geq\hskip-1.5pt2)
,
\mathbb{C}_{n}(n\hskip-1.5pt\geq\hskip-1.5pt3)
or
\mathbb{D}_{n}(n\hskip-1.5pt\geq\hskip-1.5pt4\ even)
, then
\rho
is the identity.

(2)

If
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
is of type
\mathbb{A}_{n}(n\geq 2)
,
\mathbb{D}_{n}(n\geq 5\ odd\hskip 0.6pt)
or
\mathbb{E}_{6}
, then
\rho
is of order
2
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
is a canonical Dynkin diagram as stated in Definition
1.3.8
. The theorem holds trivially in case
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}=\mathbb{A}_{1}
. We shall proceed case by case for all other cases.

1) Suppose that
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}=\mathbb{G}_{2}
or
\mathbb{B}_{2}
. Then it contains only one edge, which is non-trivially valued. By Lemma
4.3.4
(2),
\rho
is the identity.

2) Suppose that
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}=\mathbb{F}_{4}
. By Lemma
4.3.4
(2),
\rho(2)=2
and
\rho(3)=3
. And by Lemma
4.3.4
(3),
\rho(4)=4
. Thus,
\rho(1)=1
. Therefore,
\rho
is the identity.

3) Suppose that
\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}
is
\mathbb{E}_{7}
or
\mathbb{E}_{8}
. Since
3
is the only vertex of weight
3
, by Lemma
4.3.4
(1),
\rho(3)=3
. Thus,
\{\rho(2),\rho(4),\rho(5)\}\subseteq\{2,4,5\}
.
Since
{\rm w}(4)=1
, we have
\rho(4)=4
. If
\rho(2)=5
, then
\rho(1)=6
, a contradiction to Lemma
1.3.7
for
\rho(6)=2
. Thus,
\rho(2)=2
, and consequently,
\rho(1)=1
and
\rho(5)=5
. Since each of the vertices
5,6,7
has at most two neighbors, we deduce from
4.3.4
(3) that
\rho(i)=i
for all vertices
i>5.
So,
\rho
is the identity.

4) Suppose that
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}=\mathbb{B}_{n}
or
\mathbb{C}_{n}
with
n\geq 3
. By Lemma
4.3.4
(2),
\rho(1)=1
and
\rho(2)=2
. Since every vertex
i
with
1<i<n
has exactly two neighbors, we easily deduce from Lemma
4.3.4
(3) that
\rho(i)=i
for all
3\leq i\leq n
. So,
\rho
is the identity.

5) Suppose that
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}=\mathbb{D}_{n}
with
n\geq 4
even. By Lemma
4.2.6
,
I_{1}=\tau^{2-n}\hskip-1.0ptP_{1}
. Using the same argument there, we can show that
I_{2}=\tau^{2-n}\hskip-1.0ptP_{2}
. Thus,
\rho(1)=1
and
\rho(2)=2
. Since
3
is of weight
3
, by Lemma
4.3.4
(1),
\rho(3)=3
. Then,
\rho(4)=4
. Since every vertex
i
with
4\leq i<n
has exactly two neighbors, by Lemma
4.3.4
(3),
\rho(i)=i
for all
4<i\leq n
. So,
\rho
is the identity.

6) Suppose that
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}=\mathbb{A}_{n}
with
n\geq 2.
By Lemma
4.2.5
,
I_{1}=\tau^{-r_{1,n}}P_{n}
with
r_{1,n}\geq 0
. So,
\rho(n)=1
. Since every vertex
i
with
1<i<n
has two neighbors,
\rho(1)=n
. Since
n-1
is the only neighbor of
n
, we have
\rho(2)=n-1.
Similarly,
\rho(i)=n+1-i
for all
1\leq i\leq n.
Hence,
\rho
is of order
2
.

7) Suppose that
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}=\mathbb{D}_{n}
with
n\geq 5
odd. by Lemma
4.3.4
(1),
\rho(3)=3
. Since
\tau^{-s}P_{2}=I_{1}
for some
s\geq 0
; see (
4.2.6
),
\rho(2)=1
. Since the vertex
4
has two neighbors,
\rho(2)=1
, and hence,
\rho(4)=4
. Since every vertex
i
with
3<i<n
has only two neighbors, by Lemma
4.3.4
(3),
\rho(i)=i
for
5\leq i\leq n
. So,
\rho
is of order
2
.

8) Suppose
\hbox{$\hskip 2.5pt\overline{\hskip-1.5ptQ}_{\hskip-0.9ptH\hskip-1.0pt}$}=\mathbb{E}_{6}.
By Lemma
4.3.4
(1),
\rho(3)=3
. Since the vertex
4
has only one neighbors,
\rho(4)=4.
By Lemma
4.2.7
,
\tau^{-t}\hskip-1.2ptP_{6}=I_{1}
for some
t\geq 0
. So,
\rho(6)=1
. Then
\rho(1)=6
. Now,
\rho(2)=5
and
\rho(5)=2
. So
\rho
is of order
2
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

4.4.
Coxeter order

In case
H
is of finite representation type, it is well-known that the Coxeter transformation
C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}
of
K_{0}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
is of finite order; see
[
29
, (4.1)]
. We call this finite order the
Coxeter order
and write as
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|
. The following statement reinforces this fact in particular,.

Proposition 4.4.1
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra with a Dynkin ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
. Consider the Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau_{\hskip-0.5pt{}_{D}}
of
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8ptD^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)},
the pi-permutation
\rho
and the pi-index function
m
for

Now, since

Proposition 4.5.4
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra of Dynkin type. If
P
is a projective module and
I
is an injective module in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
, then
I
is a successor of
P
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
.

Proof.
Let
P
be a projective module and
I
an injective module in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
. By Proposition
3.1.6
,
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains a
P
-source section
{\mathit{\Delta}}
. Then

for some

and

4.6.
Applications

Our first application is to compute the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable modules in
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
. Applying a series of results in
[
29
]
and
[
13
]
, we see that this number was given case by case in
[
13
]
and
[
17
]
, and also by the number of positive roots of Dynkin diagrams; see
[
14
,
17
]
, which was given in
[
12
]
. However, using our previous results, we obtain this number in a direct and easy way.

Theorem 4.6.1
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra with a Dynkin ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
and Coxeter order
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|
. Then, the number of non-isomorphic indecomposable modules in
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
is equal to

\frac{1}{2}n|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|,

where
n
is the number of non-isomorphic simple modules in
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
.

Proof.
Consider the pi-permutation
\rho
and the pi-index function
m
for
(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
P_{i}
be the projective module and
I_{i}
the injective module in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
associated with
i\in(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
. Since
\tau^{-m(i)}=I_{\rho(i)}
, the
\tau
-orbit of
P_{i}
contains exactly
m(i)+1
modules. Let
t
be the number of modules in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
. Since the
P_{i}
generate a section in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
, we see that
t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(m(i)+1)
. And since
\rho
is a permutation,
t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(m(\rho(i)+1)
.

Applying Proposition
4.4.1
yields
2t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(m(i)+m(\rho(i))+2)=n|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|.
The proof of the theorem is completed.

Now, we shall compute the nilpotency of the radical of
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
. This has been essentially done by Zacharia using preprojective partitions; see
[
35
, Section 4]
. However, we shall provide an alternative approach. Since
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
is a Dynkin diagram, by Propositions
2.1.8
and
4.1.1
, any two parallel paths in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
and
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-1.0ptD^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
have the same length. So we may introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.6.2
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra of Dynkin type.

(1)

If
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains a path from
M
to
N
, then we define the
distance
between
M
and
N,
written as
{\rm dist}(M,N),
to be the length of any path from
M
to
N
in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
.

(2)

If
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-1.0ptD^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
contains a path from
M^{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 4.0pt
to
N^{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 4.0pt
, then we define the
distance
between
M^{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 4.0pt
and
N^{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 4.0pt,
written as
{\rm dist}(M^{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 4.0pt,N^{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 4.0pt\hskip-2.0pt)
, to be the length of any path from
M^{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 4.0pt
to
N^{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 4.0pt
in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-1.0ptD^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
.

The following statement allows us to easily determine the depth of any map in
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
.

Lemma 4.6.3
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra of Dynkin type. If
f:M\to N
is a non-zero map with
M,N\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
, then
{\rm dp}(f)={\rm dist}(M,N).

Proof.
Let
f:M\to N
be a non-zero map with
M,N\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
. Since
H
is representation-finite,
f\not\in{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt^{s}(M,N)
for some
s>0
; see
[
2
, (V.7.6)]
. Thus,
{\rm dp}(f)=t<\infty
. If
t=0
, then
M=N
and
{\rm dist}(M,N)=0
. Otherwise, by Lemma
1.6.5
,
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
contains a path from
M
to
N
of length
t
. Hence,
{\rm dist}(M,N)=t.
The proof of the lemma is completed.

We are ready to provide a new proof for Zacharia’s theorem as follows; see
[
35
, (4.11)]
.

Theorem 4.6.4
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra with a Dynkin ext-quiver
Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
and Coxeter order
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|
. Then, the radical of
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
is nilpotent of nilpotency
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|-1
.

Proof.
Given
i\in(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}=\{1,\ldots,n\},
consider the simple module
S_{i}\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
with a projective cover
\pi_{i}:P_{i}\to S_{i}
and an injective envelope
\iota_{i}:S_{i}\to I_{i}
, where
P_{i},I_{i}\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
.
Applying Theorem 2.7 in
[
11
]
followed by Lemma
4.6.3
, we see that the nilpotency of
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
is equal to

\sup\{{\rm dp}(\iota_{1}\pi_{1}),\ldots,{\rm dp}(\iota_{n}\pi_{n})\}+1=\sup\{{\rm dist}(P_{1},I_{1}),\ldots,{\rm dist}P_{n},I_{n})\}+1.\vskip-2.0pt

Thus, it suffices to show that
\text{dist}(P_{i},I_{i})=|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|-2
, for any
i\in(\hbox{$Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}$})_{0}
. In fact,
since
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
is a convex valued translation subquiver of
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-1.0ptD^{b}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
; see (
4.1.2
), we have
\text{dist}(M[0],N[0])=\text{dist}(M,N),
for all
M,N\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
. And since
\tau_{\hskip-0.5pt{}_{D}}^{-}I_{i}[0]=P_{i}[1]
; see
[
5
, (7.2)]
, we deduce

\text{dist}(P_{i}[0],P_{i}[1])=\text{dist}(P_{i}[0],I_{i}[0])+\text{dist}(I_{i}[0],P_{i}[1])=\text{dist}(P_{i},I_{i})+2.\vskip-2.0pt

As a consequence, we obtain

\text{dist}(P_{i}[0],P_{i}[2])=\text{dist}(P_{i}[0],P_{i}[1])+\text{dist}(P_{i}[1],P_{i}[2])=2\hskip 0.5pt\text{dist}(P_{i}[0],P_{i}[1])=2(\text{dist}(P_{i},I_{i})+2).\vskip-2.0pt

On the other hand, by Proposition
4.4.1
,

\text{dist}(P_{i}[0],P_{i}[2])=\text{dist}(P_{i}[0],\tau_{\hskip-0.5pt{}_{D}}^{-|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|}P_{i}[0])=2\hskip 0.5pt|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|.
That is,
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|=\text{dist}(P_{i},I_{i})+2
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

Next, we shall study the radical of the bounded derived category
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
. Being a triangulated category,
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
coincides with its projectively stable category and its injectively stable category; see
[
27
, (2.4)]
. Since
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
has almost split triangles, the Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau_{\hskip-1.0pt{}_{D}}
of
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
induces an auto-equivalence
\tau_{\hskip-1.0pt{}_{D}}:D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)\to D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
; see
[
27
, (4.10)]
.

Theorem 4.6.5
.

Let
H
be a hereditary algebra of Dynkin type with Coxeter order
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|
. Then the radical of
D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
is nilpotent of nilpotency
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|-1
.

Proof.
Write
\mathscr{D}=D^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.6pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
. Since
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
is a convex subcategory in
\mathscr{D}
, by Theorem
4.6.4
,
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt^{|C_{H}|-2}(\mathscr{D})\neq 0.
Suppose that
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt^{r}(\mathscr{D})\neq 0
with
r\geq 0
. Then,
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt^{r}_{\mathscr{D}}(M,N[s])\neq 0
, for some
M,N\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
and
s\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
. Write
M=\tau^{-t}P
for some
t\geq 0
, where
P
is a projective module in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
. Applying the equivalence
\tau_{\hskip-1.0pt{}_{D}}
,

we obtain
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt^{r}_{\mathscr{D}}(P[0],(\tau_{\hskip-1.0pt{}_{D}}N[0])[s])\neq 0
. Since
P
is projective,
(\tau_{\hskip-1.0pt{}_{D}}N[0])[s]=L[0]
for some
L\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}
.

Then,
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt^{r}(P,L)\neq 0
, and by Theorem
4.6.4
,
r\leq|C_{H}|-2.

So,
{\rm rad}\hskip 0.3pt(\mathscr{D})
is nilpotent of nilpotency
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|-1
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

We conclude with the cluster category associated with
H
. Let
\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}(H)
be a skeleton of
D^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)
, containing the complexes in
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-1.5ptD^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
. Then,
\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}(H)
is a
Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt
R
-category, which has almost split sequences. Note that the Auslander-Reiten quiver
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-0.8pt\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}(H)}
of
\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}(H)
coincides with
\hbox{${\mathit{\Gamma}}$}_{\hskip-1.5ptD^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}({\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH)}
, and its
Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau_{\hskip-0.6pt{}_{\mathscr{D}}}
extends to an automorphism of
\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}(H)
. Thus, we have an automorphism
F=\tau_{\hskip-0.6pt{}_{\mathscr{D}}}^{-1}\circ[1]
of
\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}(H)
such, for any indecomposable objects
M^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt},N^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}\in\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}(H)

F^{\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.6pt}(M^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt})\not\cong M^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}
for all
p\neq 0
and
\Hom_{\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}(H)}(M^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt},F^{\hskip 0.5ptp\hskip-0.6pt}(N^{\hskip 0.8pt{\accentset{\hskip 1.0pt\mbox{\large.}}{}}\hskip 1.5pt}))=0
for all but finitely many
p\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}.
Thus, the action of the group
{\mathcal{F}}
generated by
F
on
\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}(H)
is free and locally bounded. As did in
[
7
]
, we define the
cluster category
associated with
H
to be the orbit category

\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-0.5pt}(H)/\mathcal{F}=:\mathscr{C}_{H},

which is Hom-finite and Krull-Schmidt; see
1.5.4
.

Theorem 4.6.6
.

Let
H
be a hereditary artin algebra of Dynkin type with Coxeter order
|C_{H}|
, and let
\mathscr{C}_{H}
be the cluster category associated with
H
.

(1)

The radical of
\mathscr{C}_{H}
is nilpotent of nilpotency
|C_{\hskip-0.5ptH}|-1
.

(2)

The number of non-isomorphich indecomposable objects in
\mathscr{C}_{H}
is equal to

\frac{1}{2}n(|C_{H}|+2),\vskip-1.0pt

where
n
is the number of non-isomorphic simple modules in
{\rm mod}\hskip 0.3ptH
.

Proof.
First, Statement (1) follows immediately from Proposition
1.5.4
(1) and Theorem
4.6.5
. Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
be the projective modules and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}
the injective modules in
\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}
with
{\rm top}P_{i}={\rm soc}I_{i}
. Recall that
F=\tau_{\hskip-0.6pt{}_{\mathscr{D}}}^{-1}\circ[1]
, where
\tau_{\hskip-0.6pt{}_{\mathscr{D}}}
is the Auslander-Reiten translation for
\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}(H)
. Given
p\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
, write
\mathcal{S}^{(p)}=\{M[p]\mid M\in\hbox{$\mathit{\Gamma}_{\hskip 0.8pt{\rm mod}H}$}\}\cup\{P_{1}[p+1],\ldots,P_{n}[p+1]\}
and
\mathcal{T}^{(p)}=F^{p}(\mathcal{S}^{(0)})
. Since
\tau_{\hskip-0.6pt{}_{\mathscr{D}}}^{-}I_{i}=P_{i}[1]
, we obtain
F(I_{i})=P_{i}[2]
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus,
\mathcal{T}^{(1)}=F(\mathcal{S}^{(0)}).
In general,

\mathcal{T}^{(p)}=F^{p}(\mathcal{S}^{(0)})
for all
p\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
. Since
\mathcal{F}=\{F^{p}\mid p\in\hbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\}
, we see that
\mathcal{S}^{(0)}
is a complete set of representatives of the
\mathcal{F}
-orbits of non-isomorphic indecomposable objects in
\mathscr{D}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-0.7pt}(H)
. Now, Statement (2) follows from Proposition
1.5.4
and Theorem
4.6.1
. The proof of the theorem is completed.

Q_{\hskip-0.8ptH}
; see

15

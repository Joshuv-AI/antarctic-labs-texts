EIF3B is associated with m6A-related regulation of E2F7 and promotes stemness in glioblastoma multiforme
Authors: Jiao Meng, Xinyi Jiang, Yaling Hu, Jinyue Tian, Zhenkun Yang, Zhening Pu, Ying Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundE2F transcription factors are key regulators of the cell cycle and play an important role in tumorigenesis. N6-methyladenosine (m6A), the most prevalent internal RNA modification in eukaryotic cells, is also crucial for cancer progression. However, the interplay between E2F family members and m6A modification in glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) remains poorly understood.MethodsWe applied weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) to identify m6A-related gene modules co-expressed with E2F7, a gene that is significantly associated with adverse prognosis and tumor stemness. Through intersection analysis, eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 subunit B (EIF3B) emerged as a hub gene, showing a strong positive correlation with E2F7 in GBM datasets. Functional validation was performed in GBM cells to assess the regulatory relationship between EIF3B and E2F7, including examination of mRNA and protein expression, mRNA stability, and m6A modification levels. GBM stemness was evaluated by tumor sphere formation assays and detection of stemness-associated markers.ResultsFunctional validation revealed that EIF3B knockdown reduced E2F7 mRNA and protein expression, promoted E2F7 mRNA degradation, and decreased its m6A modification, suggesting an association between EIF3B and m6A-related regulation of E2F7 stability. Furthermore, EIF3B knockdown markedly suppressed GBM stemness, as demonstrated by impaired tumor sphere formation and downregulation of stemness-associated markers. Clinically, EIF3B expression increased with glioma grade and correlated with poor prognosis.ConclusionsCollectively, these findings suggest that the EIF3B-E2F7 axis is associated with m6A-related regulation in GBM and may warrant further investigation as a candidate for therapeutic targeting.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-026-16552-8.Keywords: EIF3B, E2F7, m6A modification, Stemness, Glioblastoma multiforme

Introduction

Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) represents the most aggressive form of primary central nervous system tumors [1, 2]. Despite multimodal treatments, including maximal surgical resection and radiotherapy, the median survival remains only 12–15 months [3, 4], with glioma stem cells (GSCs) driving tumor initiation, progression, and therapeutic resistance [5]. Therefore, identifying regulators of GSCs’ stemness is critical.

The E2F family comprises master regulators that couple transcriptional activity with cell cycle progression. Among them, E2F transcription factor 7 (E2F7) is an atypical repressor that plays a crucial role in regulating cell cycle progression across multiple cancers. Studies have further shown that E2F7 regulates DNA damage and repair pathways, contributing to drug resistance in GBM [6]. In nasopharyngeal carcinoma, E2F7 undergoes N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification, and insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 2 (IGF2BP2) stabilizes its mRNA [7]. These observations underscore the clinical relevance of E2F7 regulation in tumor biology and point to a tight link with epigenetic m6A RNA modification.

Eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 subunit B (EIF3B), a critical subunit of the translation initiation factor EIF3, functions as a scaffold protein that ensures translation initiation accuracy and indirectly influences m6A-modified mRNA translation [8]. Elevated levels of EIF3B have been observed in various malignancies, including hepatocellular carcinoma, colorectal cancer, breast cancer, gastric cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, bladder cancer, and prostate cancer [9–11]. However, this has not yet been investigated in GBM. Moreover, although m6A modification is a known regulator of stemness, whether EIF3B interacts with the m6A machinery to regulate GSCs fate remains unexplored.

Interaction analysis of m6A-related genes nominated EIF3B for further study. Experimental validation showed a positive correlation between EIF3B and E2F7 expression in vitro. Functionally, EIF3B knockdown diminished tumor-sphere formation and reduced expression of key stemness markers. Collectively, these results indicate that EIF3B may participate in m6A-related regulation of E2F7 and that the EIF3B-E2F7 axis influences GBM cell stemness and cell-cycle characteristics, making it a potential therapeutic target for GBM.

Materials and methods

Datasets and m6A-related genes

RNA sequencing data and clinical information were sourced from the UCSC Xena database (https://xenabrowser.net/), covering 172 samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)-GBM project (sampleMap/HiSeqV2). The cohort comprised 5 normal and 167 tumor samples. Following previous studies, 35 m6A regulators were identified and classified into writers, erasers, and readers [12, 13], as shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) in GBM

To identify DEGs between GBM and normal brain tissues in the TCGA cohort. Samples were obtained from untreated, surgically resected primary tumors. Each sample met the quality criteria of at least 80% tumor cell nuclei and 20%-30% or less necrotic tissue. All specimens underwent centralized pathological review and molecular quality control to confirm that the extracted DNA and RNA met sequencing standards. The limma package in R Studio (version 3.3.3) was used with |log2FoldChange| > 2 and an adjusted p-value < 0.05. The raw data underwent log transformation following quantile normalization. The list of gene expression profiles was downloaded from the UCSC database (http://www.genome.ucsc.edu).

Weighted Gene Co-Expression Network Analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA was employed to construct a co-expression network of DEGs using scale-free topology criteria. Initially, all DEGs were analyzed using the WGCNA package in RStudio to determine the soft thresholding power. Subsequently, a weighted co-expression network was constructed, clustering DEGs into modules labeled with color. The correlation between each module and GBM or controls was investigated, identifying the module most correlated with GBM as the key module for further enrichment analysis.

Bioinformatics validation of the hub gene

The Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) website was used to explore the overall survival of hub genes in GBM samples. We conducted a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis to identify functional interaction patterns using the GeneMANIA database. The expression correlation between two genes was visualized and analyzed using the cBioPortal database. The predictive ability of hub genes for survival was assessed using Kaplan-Meier analysis in RStudio with the survival package, based on TCGA-GBM data. The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database provided details on the tissue and cellular distribution of human proteins using immunoassay technology.

Other bioinformatics analysis

A gene-pharmaco-interaction network was developed utilizing the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) and Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC), concentrating on FDA-approved drugs that influence EIF3B at the mRNA or protein level.

Cell culture and reagents

The human GBM cell lines U87 and U251 were obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Beijing, China) and authenticated via short tandem repeat assays by GENEWIZ (Suzhou, China). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (Hyclone, Los Angeles, USA) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (P/S; Hyclone) at 37 °C in 5% CO2.

Vector construction and transduction

The small interfering RNAs (siRNA) targeting human EIF3B were synthesized by Saisofi Biotech (Wuxi, China). Transfection of EIF3B siRNA was conducted using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Leicester, UK) following the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA and protein samples were collected 48–72 h post-transfection for further assays. The siRNA sequences employed in this study are detailed in Supplementary Table 2. To construct a stable cell line overexpressing (OE) E2F7, the E2F7-Flag lentivirus was constructed by inserting the cDNA sequence into the lentivirus vector GV640 (Genechem, Shanghai, China) with a Flag-tag.

Reverse-transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific) per the manufacturer’s protocol. cDNA synthesis employed the M-MLV Reverse Transcriptase Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). mRNA expression levels were quantified via RT-qPCR using Real SYBR Mixture (Vazyme) on a LightCycler 480 II (Roche Applied Science, Basel, Switzerland), with GAPDH as the internal control. Primer sequences are detailed in Supplementary Table 3.

Western blot analysis

Cells were harvested and lysed in RIPA buffer (Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, USA). Protein concentration was determined using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (CWBIO, Beijing, China). Western blotting was performed following standard protocols [14]. Briefly, equal amounts of protein were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk and incubated with primary and secondary antibodies. Target bands were visualized using chemiluminescence (Millipore, Billerica, USA). Antibody details are provided in Supplementary Table 4.

Methylated RNA Immunoprecipitation (MeRIP)-qPCR

Following the manufacturer’s guidelines, MeRIP was conducted using the EpiQuik CUT&RUN m6A RNA Enrichment Kit (AD Technology, Zhangjiakou, China). Cells were lysed in lysis buffer, and m6A antibodies (Lot: 2408206, 100 µg/ml) supplied with the kit were conjugated to magnetic beads. The lysate was incubated with the antibody-coated beads at room temperature for two hours. The supernatant was treated with proteinase K, and RNA bound to the beads was extracted with phenol-chloroform-isoamyl alcohol. 10% of the total RNA was used as the Input control to determine the enrichment efficiency. To assess m6A-modified RNA enrichment, we performed real-time quantitative (RT-qPCR). Using the Input group as the internal background reference, we analyzed and compared the IP values for each group. The m6A site on E2F7 was predicted with SRAMP (http://www.cuilab.cn/sramp), and primers were designed from the highest-scoring predicted fragments. Primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 5.

RNA stability assay

The stability of mRNA transcripts was assessed in U87 and U251 cells with EIF3B knockdown or control cells following treatment with the transcriptional inhibitor Actinomycin D (MedChem-Express, New Jersey, USA) for zero, three, and six hours. Total RNA was extracted and analyzed using RT-qPCR. The mRNA half-life was subsequently estimated through linear regression analysis.

3D spheroids assay

The cells were seeded at a density of 400 cells per well in a low-attachment 96-well plate and cultured in neural stem cell (NSC) medium. The NSC medium consisted of DMEM/F-12 (Bio-Channel Biotechnology, Nanjing, China) supplemented with 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco, California, USA), 20 ng/mL epidermal growth factor (EGF, PeproTech, Rocky Hill, USA), 20 ng/mL human basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF, PeproTech), 2% B-27 (Thermo Fisher Scientific), and 1% GlutaMAX (Gibco). The growth of the 3D spheroid cultures was monitored using a microscope equipped with a real-time camera (EVOS® FL Auto Imaging System, Life Technologies, Carlsbad, USA). Photographs of the tumor spheres were captured at the indicated time points, and the sphere diameter was measured.

Cell cycle assay

Cells were cultured for 24 h, synchronized with serum deprivation for another 24 h, and then returned to normal medium for a further 24 h. Subsequently, cells were fixed with 70% ethanol at -20 °C for 24 h. The cells were then suspended in PBS with 100 ng/ml RNaseA and 50 µg/ml propidium iodide (PI), and incubated for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. Cell cycle progression was assessed using a FACS Canto II, and the proportions of cells in G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases were calculated with ModFit LT software. Each experiment was conducted in triplicate.

Molecular docking

To determine whether the four drugs can bind to the EIF3B protein, we conducted a detailed analysis of EIF3B binding sites. The structural data for the human proteins were obtained from the AlphaFold Database (https://alphafold.com), specifically for EIF3B (UniProt ID: P55884). Protein preparation involved using the prepare_receptor4 script from MGLTools 1.4.2 to assign Gasteiger charges. The 3D structures of the drugs were sourced from PubChem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Molecular docking was performed with AutoDock Vina v1.2.5, using the first pocket centroid predicted by CavityPlus as the docking center. A binding free energy below − 6 indicates a strong docking score and a high likelihood of binding, while a score up to -5 suggests lower reliability and reduced binding probability.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were independently replicated at least three times, with data presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Student’s t-test and one-way ANOVA, followed by Student-Newman-Keuls multiple comparison test, were used to assess differences between two independent samples and among multiple groups, respectively. Survival analyses were performed using the Kaplan-Meier method with the log-rank test. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was employed to evaluate gene expression correlations. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Statistical analyses and data visualization were conducted using SPSS 20.0 (IBM, New York, USA) and GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad, California, USA), respectively.

Results

High expression of the E2F family correlates with an adverse prognosis in GBM

The TCGA-GBM cohort (n = 172; Tumor = 167, Normal = 5) was analyzed using transcriptomic data obtained from the UCSC repository. Principal component analysis (PCA) clearly distinguished tumor samples from normal tissues, exhibiting distinct clustering patterns (Fig. 1A). Volcano plot analysis identified 8663 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in GBM, including 4406 upregulated and 4257 downregulated genes (Fig. 1B). The Gene Ontology-Biological Process (GO-BP) analysis revealed that DEGs in GBM patients were significantly enriched in protein modification, RNA metabolism, cell proliferation, and cell cycle regulation (Fig. 1C). The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis further indicated that the DEGs were primarily concentrated in pathways involved in RNA transport, apoptosis, p53 signaling, DNA replication, and ABC transporters, among other critical cellular processes (Fig. 1D). Cell cycle and DNA replication disorders are intrinsically connected to tumorigenesis and drug resistance. The E2Fs, along with cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) and retinoblastoma tumor suppressor protein (RB), are the central transcription factors driving cell cycle progression to ensure the genetic material is properly transmitted as cells go through each cycle of cell division [15]. Our investigation focused on the E2F family, analyzing their expression in the TCGA-GBM database. The analysis showed that all E2F family members, except E2F3, are significantly elevated in GBM (Fig. 1E). The elevated expression of E2F7 exhibits the strongest correlation with poor clinical outcomes in GBM patients (Fig. 1F). Above all, these findings suggest a pivotal role for E2F7 in the pathogenesis of GBM.

High expression of the E2F family correlates with Glioma Stem Cells (GSCs) stemness and Tumor mutational burden (TMB) in GBM

Temozolomide (TMZ) is a primary pharmacological agent for the clinical management of GBM. Utilizing data from the TCGA-GBM database, we analyzed the association between TMZ sensitivity and E2F transcription factor expression in GBM. Our findings revealed significant negative correlations between the expression of E2F2, E2F6, and E2F8 and TMZ sensitivity (Fig. 2A). Additionally, we analyzed the Stem Score in GSCs, which are implicated in tumor propagation, resistance to chemotherapy and radiation therapy, and disease recurrence [4, 16]. We found that E2F expression levels positively correlated with Stem Score (Fig. 2B). TMB is a characteristic and biomarker of gliomas [17], and our analysis indicated that E2F7 expression was most strongly correlated with TMB (Fig. 2C). Collectively, these findings suggest that E2F7 may be of particular significance in GBM research.

WGCNA analysis unveils EIF3B as a m6A-related gene positively correlated with E2F7

Using WGCNA, we constructed a network from the expression matrix of 8663 DEGs and clinical data of 172 GBM samples. A scale-free co-expression network (scale-free R² > 0.8) was achieved with a soft thresholding power of 12, ensuring adequate average connectivity (Fig. 3A). DEGs with similar expression profiles were grouped into modules, merging those with a cut height difference of < 0.25. This resulted in 16 co-expression modules: grey, cyan, blue, turquoise, brown, salmon, lightgreen, black, greenyellow, purple, midnightblue, lightcyan, grey60, royalblue, darkred, and darkgreen (Fig. 3B). The grey module contains unassigned genes. Notably, the turquoise module’s eigengenes showed a strong correlation with GBM tumors and E2F7 (Fig. 3C-D). Within the turquoise module, the correlation between GS and MM was significant (Cor = 0.62, p = 6.9 × 10−⁵) (Fig. 3E). Ultimately, the turquoise module, comprising 965 genes, was identified as the most biologically relevant, showing the strongest correlation with E2F7.

To assess the relationship between the transcription factor E2F7 and m6A regulatory genes in GBM, we analyzed TCGA transcriptomic data. Initial correlation analysis identified 18 m6A-related genes that were positively correlated with E2F7 expression, with a correlation coefficient threshold of > 0.3 (Table 1). Subsequent intersection analysis between turquoise module genes from WGCNA and the primary m6A-associated genes revealed EIF3B as a key m6A-regulated hub gene strongly correlated with E2F7 expression (Fig. 3F). Using the GeneMania database, we constructed a putative PPI network for EIF3B and E2F7, comprising 22 link proteins (Fig. 3G). Analysis via the public database showed a statistically significant positive correlation between E2F7 and EIF3B expression levels across various databases (Fig. 3H). Our findings indicate that the m6A-associated gene EIF3B is strongly positively correlated with E2F7 expression, suggesting their potential functional synergy in GBM pathobiology.

EIF3B is associated with m6A-related regulation of E2F7 expression in GBM

Knockdown (KD) of EIF3B using three distinct siRNAs significantly inhibited its expression in U87 and U251 cells, as confirmed by both mRNA and protein level analyses. Concomitantly, E2F7 expression decreased in KD EIF3B cells compared to the control group (Fig. 3I-J). To assess mRNA stability, cells were treated with ActD to block de novo transcription. RT-qPCR analysis revealed that the decay rate of E2F7 mRNA was accelerated in the KD EIF3B group (Fig. 3K). Furthermore, based on the SRAMP database, three potential m6A modification sites were predicted in the CDS region of E2F7 transcripts (Figure S1). Moreover, MeRIP-qPCR analysis showed decreased m6A enrichment within these putative m6A-enriched regions in the KD EIF3B group compared to the control group (Fig. 3L). Given that RNA m6A modification has been reported to regulate gene expression by affecting mRNA stability or translation [2], these results raise the possibility that EIF3B promotes E2F7 expression through an m6A-dependent mechanism affecting RNA stability.

EIF3B expression correlates with tumor grade and depletion compromises GSC features in GBM

To assess the association between the hub gene EIF3B and clinical severity, we performed an integrated analysis across TCGA via the Gliovis platform. As shown, EIF3B expression was elevated in GBM compared to normal tissues (NT), and higher EIF3B levels correlated with poorer survival outcomes (Fig. 4A-B). Immunohistochemical (IHC) data from the HPA database revealed increased EIF3B expression in higher-grade glioma (HGG) compared to lower-grade glioma (LGG) tissues (Fig. 4C). These findings underscore the research significance of EIF3B in GBM and its potential as a target for further mechanistic studies.

Given the pivotal role of GSCs in GBM pathophysiology and patient outcomes [24, 25], we explored the influence of EIF3B on GBM cell stemness. First, we examined the relationship between EIF3B expression and key stem cell-related genes in GBM patients from TCGA. Our analysis revealed a positive correlation between EIF3B and the mRNA levels of Octamer-binding transcription factor 4 (Oct4), SRY-box transcription factor 2 (Sox2), Nanog homeobox (Nanog), and Nestin (Fig. 4D), and was confirmed by RT-qPCR in U87 and U251 cells. KD EIF3B reduces the expression of stemness-associated genes (Fig. 4E-F). Furthermore, KD EIF3B significantly reduced stemness in U87 and U251 cells under GSC conditions (Fig. 5A, D), suggesting a potential role for EIF3B in maintaining the stem-like phenotype of these GBM cells. Given the importance of the cell cycle in cancer progression, we also evaluated the impact of EIF3B on cell cycle dynamics. KD EIF3B delayed the G0/G1 phase transition and accelerated the G2/M phase transition (Fig. 5B-C and E-F). These data further suggest EIF3B’s involvement in stem cell maintenance and cell cycle regulation in GBM cells.

The EIF3B-E2F7 axis regulates stem-like characteristics of GBM cells

We showed that KD EIF3B significantly reduced stemness in U87 and U251 cells. We next examined whether this effect is mediated through E2F7. To this end, we ectopically expressed E2F7 in KD EIF3B GBM cells. As shown in Fig. 6A-B, KD EIF3B reduced stemness, and overexpression (OE) E2F7 partically restored stemness in U87 and U251 cells. KD EIF3B resulted in a decreased G0/G1 population and a concomitant accumulation of cells in G2/M phase. Remarkably, OE E2F7 in KD EIF3B cells reversed this phenotype, as evidenced by increased G0/G1 fraction and reduced G2/M accumulation (Fig. 6C-E). These findings suggest that EIF3B may regulate cell cycle progression, at least in part, through modulating E2F7 expression.

Exploratory in silico prediction of potential EIF3B targeting compounds

Our comprehensive analysis revealed that EIF3B is an oncogenic gene that promotes stemness in GBM. As an exploratory analysis to generate hypotheses for future investigation, we queried the CTD database for drugs potentially associated with EIF3B (Supplementary Table 6). Cross-referencing with the GDSC database revealed four clinically approved anti-cancer drugs: Doxorubicin (DOX), Temozolomide (TMZ), Sunitinib (SU), and Vincristine (VCR) that may be associated with EIF3B mRNA or protein levels (Fig. 7A). Among these, SU was reported to increase EIF3B mRNA expression, while VCR showed an inverse relationship. To explore the potential binding modes of these drugs with EIF3B, we performed exploratory molecular docking using the predicted EIF3B protein structure from the AlphaFold Database and the primary pocket centroid predicted by CavityPlus (Fig. 7B). Docking simulations conducted with AutoDock Vina predicted notable binding affinities for DOX, TMZ, and SU with EIF3B, whereas VCR showed moderate predicted affinity (Fig. 7C). While these in silico predictions suggest that EIF3B may represent a candidate for further investigation, experimental validation is required to determine whether these drugs directly target EIF3B or exert anti GBM effects through EIF3B-dependent mechanisms. Therefore, these computational findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than conclusive.

Discussion

The presence of glioma stem cells (GSCs) is a critical factor contributing to suboptimal therapeutic efficacy and an unfavourable prognosis in GBM [18]. These cells not only fuel robust tumour initiation and progression but also confer resistance to conventional chemoradiotherapy on tumours [19, 20]. Therefore, identifying molecular targets and therapeutic strategies is essential, particularly as advancements in bioinformatics enable the discovery of additional potential molecular markers. Here, we found that EIF3B regulates E2F7 expression through m6A modification and can regulate stemness-associated phenotypes.

EIF3 is an important multisubunit complex in EIFs and consists of 13 subunits [21]. EIF3B is considered to be the main scaffolding subunit in the EIF3 complex [22]. Some studies have suggested that EIF3B is involved not only in protein translation but also in cell proliferation, invasion, migration, and tumour development [23, 24]. However, the expression of EIF3B and its impact on GBM stemness have not been reported. In this study, we show that EIF3B correlates with pathological grade and survival in human glioma patients. Consistent with previous reports, EIF3B was also shown to promote the proliferation of GBM cells [24, 25]. Our study showed that KD EIF3B reduced stemness-associated phenotypes in GBM cells, as evidenced by decreased expression of the key stemness factors Oct4, Sox2, Nanog, and Nestin, which are essential to tumor stemness [26, 27]. Using WGCNA and correlation-prediction analyses of bioinformatics databases, we found that EIF3B and E2F7 exhibit a positive regulatory relationship, which may be mediated by m6A modification.

The m6A modification is the most prevalent mRNA modification in eukaryotes and has been implicated in diverse biological processes, including transcription, splicing, nuclear export, translation control, and cell fate determination [28–30]. Accumulating evidence underscores the critical role of m6A in regulating the hallmarks of GBM, such as proliferation, migration, stemness, and chemoresistance [31–33]. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that concurrent inhibition of m6A-related genes enhances the efficacy of chemoimmunotherapy by suppressing tumor growth and restoring immune surveillance [34]. Recent studies report that EIF3B functions as an m6A reader [12, 35] and cooperates with core m6A methylation enzymes, including METTL3 and METTL16, to modulate m6A-mediated regulation in tumors [36, 37]. Our study reveals a novel epigenetic axis involving EIF3B and E2F7 in GBM, facilitated by m6A-dependent mRNA stabilization. KD EIF3B not only reduced transcript and protein levels but also decreased E2F7 m6A abundance. Similar to a report that EIF3B positively regulates the expression of E2F1 in gastric cancer [38]. The identification of specific m6A motifs within E2F7 transcripts, along with reduced m6A enrichment following EIF3B depletion, supports a direct regulatory mechanism. This is further confirmed by the accelerated decay of E2F7 mRNA in KD EIF3B cells, underscoring the role of m6A in sustaining oncogene expression. However, we acknowledge that the role of EIF3B in m6A-associated regulation in GBM remains to be fully elucidated, and direct evidence of a functional link is currently lacking.

In rescue experiments, we overexpressed E2F7 on a KD EIF3B background, which partially restored stemness and increased the fraction of cells arrested in G0/G1, indicating that EIF3B may influence stemness and the cell cycle in part through regulation of E2F7. Drug prediction analysis identified EIF3B as a binding partner for several tumor treatment drugs, including TMZ for GBM and commonly used agents DOX, SU, and VCR. SU is a multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor that is currently used for renal cell carcinoma (RCC). Literature suggests that EIF3D contributes to sunitinib resistance in RCC [39], and increased EIF3B expression is linked to poor treatment response and reduced survival in adult acute lymphoblastic leukemia patients [40]. Our data established a functional EIF3B-E2F7 axis in GSC stemness, future studies are required to test whether targeting this axis sensitizes GBM cells to TMZ, whether EIF3B mediates chemoresistance, and whether it is mediated by E2F7. Therefore, the clinical relevance of our findings should be considered preliminary and hypothesis-generating. These findings suggest that EIF3B could serve as a prognostic candidate and warrant further investigation as a therapeutic target in GBM. However, further conclusions need to be verified by specific experimental methods (SPR, CETSA).

Several limitations of this study warrant acknowledgment. First, the limited number of normal samples in TCGA-GBM introduces a potential bias, indicating that future research with larger cohorts of normal brain tissue is necessary to validate our findings. Another limitation is that, although our MeRIP-qPCR data indicate an association between EIF3B expression and m6A enrichment on E2F7 transcripts, EIF3B is not a canonical m6A regulator. We have not demonstrated direct binding between EIF3B and E2F7 mRNA, nor have we shown interaction with known m6A machinery. Consequently, our data are correlative rather than mechanistic. Future investigations utilizing RIP-qPCR, RNA pull-down, and Co-IP assays are essential to elucidate the precise molecular link, if any, between EIF3B and m6A modification.

Conclusions

This study identifies an association between EIF3B and m6A-mediated regulation of E2F7 expression in GBM, and suggests that the EIF3B-E2F7 axis may warrant further investigation as a candidate for therapeutic targeting. Nevertheless, the mechanistic contribution of this axis to GBM stemness remains to be fully elucidated.

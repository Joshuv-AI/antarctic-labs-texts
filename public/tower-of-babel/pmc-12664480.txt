Microstructural integrity of the dual-stream fiber pathways significantly affects language performance of individuals with subacute post-stroke aphasia
Authors: Qiwei Yu, Yan Sun, Tianfen Ye, Kefu Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Language correlates of left-hemispheric fiber tracts have been widely recognized.•The linguistic roles of the left dual-stream pathways remain quite unclear.•Left-hemispheric tract integrity correlates with broad language domain deficits.•Dorsal-ventral pathway interactions explain diverse lesion-symptom mapping.Keywords: Post-stroke aphasia, Dual-stream model, White matter fiber pathway, Diffusion tensor imaging

Introduction

Aphasia is one of the most prevalent and disabling sequelae of stroke. It was reported that approximately 30 % of survivors suffered from language impairments, which significantly affects their activities of daily lives (Saini et al. 2021). To date, understanding the brain mechanisms of post-stroke aphasia (PSA) has driven sustained scientific efforts to explore how the functional state and structural integrity of brain tissues influence the phenotypic manifestations of linguistic deficits (Fridriksson et al., 2018, Han et al., 2024). However, the neuropathological substrates of PSA remain incompletely understood.

In recent decades, advances in neuroscience have proved that physiological language processing predominantly involves the left frontotemporal network (Stockert et al., 2020, Yu et al., 2023). Accumulating evidence from modern neuroimaging techniques has underscored the pivotal roles of functional activation in several left-hemispheric cortical areas, including Broca’s area, Wernicke’s area, and the temporal-parietal junction (TPJ), in language organization (Flinker et al., 2015, Mesulam et al., 2019, Rajimehr et al., 2022, Fedorenko et al., 2024). The dual-stream model, proposed by Hickok and Poeppel (Hickok and Poeppel, 2004, Hickok and Poeppel, 2007), stands as one of the most influential contemporary neuropsychological frameworks for understanding the functional anatomy of language organization. As described, the dorsal stream encompasses frontal lobe areas and the TPJ, while the ventral stream projects predominantly through the temporal lobe. Functionally, the dorsal stream is posited to transform acoustic speech signals to motor plans for articulation (i.e., speech production), whereas the ventral stream subserves the mapping of auditory stimuli to semantic representations (i.e., comprehension processing) (Zhang et al., 2021b, Zhu et al., 2023).

It has been well known that the neural substrates supporting language organization extend beyond the functional recruitment of cortical regions to include the structural connectivity of subcortical white matter fiber pathways. Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) is a powerful tool for in vivo investigation of the microstructural properties of these pathways (Zhu et al. 2023). Consequently, a key application of DTI in PSA is to quantify the integrity of specific subcortical neural tracts and to assess their correlation with behavioral language performance. Numerous studies (Zhu et al., 2023, Han et al., 2024) with DTI technology have demonstrated that the structural integrity of these pathways is critical for language processing. According to the dual-stream model (Hickok and Poeppel, 2004, Hickok and Poeppel, 2007), the language-related fiber pathways could be anatomically classified into the dorsal and ventral streams. Specifically, the dorsal pathway primarily comprises the arcuate fasciculus (AF) and superior longitudinal fasciculus (SLF), and the ventral pathway consists of the inferior frontal-occipital fasciculus (IFOF), uncinate fasciculus (UF), inferior longitudinal fasciculus (ILF), and the middle longitudinal fasciculus (MdLF) (Zhang et al., 2021b, Yu et al., 2023).

While the involvement of subcortical fiber pathways in speech organization has been established, the specific linguistic roles of individual fiber tracts and their mechanistic contributions to language impairments have not been fully elucidated (Zhang et al., 2021b, Zhu et al., 2023, Han et al., 2024). Determining the linguistic functions of the neural tracts is of great significance for clinical practices, such as guiding the preservation of critical neural structures during neurosurgeries; facilitating language assessment in poorly cooperative patients; and assisting in predicting language outcomes after brain injury (Forkel et al., 2014, Zhang et al., 2021b, Yu et al., 2024). Therefore, some studies (Ivanova et al., 2016, Ivanova et al., 2021, Yu et al., 2022, Jang et al., 2023, Yu et al., 2023, Zhu et al., 2023, Osa García et al., 2024) with DTI technology have investigated the correlation between the integrity of white matter fiber pathways and language deficits of patients with aphasia and have yielded inconsistent results. For example, an early study by Breier et al. (2008) involving 20 patients with chronic PSA reported the correlations between the left AF/SLF and comprehension/repetition abilities. Geva et al. (2015) observed in 15 chronic PSA patients that damage to the left AF was associated with impairments in word repetition, object naming, sentence comprehension, as well as homophone and rhyme judgement. Subsequently, Xing et al. (2017) examined the distinct contributions of four left-hemisphere tracts (i.e., the AF, IFOF, ILF, and UF) to auditory word and sentence comprehension in individuals with chronic PSA. Recently, Braun et al. (2022) found that the integrity of the left AF, SLF, and ILF was linked to chronic aphasia severity, while the left ILF was related to naming. However, the majority of these prior DTI studies have been carried out in the chronic phase of stroke (Sebastian and Breining, 2018, Schevenels et al., 2022).

It is noteworthy that in the chronic phase after stroke, substantial autonomous neural reorganization within the language network has already occurred (Vaidya et al. 2019). As a result, observed correlations between structural pathways and language behavior during this stage may reflect compensatory reorganization rather than native neural function (Schevenels et al. 2022). This implies that inferences about the functional organization and localization of language in healthy individuals—if derived from chronic patients—are potentially limited. They are influenced by post-stroke adaptive changes (Stockbridge et al. 2025). The functional differentiation of white matter tracts may also vary across individuals by the chronic stage due to reorganization (Ding and Schnur 2022). In addition, the contribution of lesioned areas or white matter damage to language deficits may be significantly underestimated in chronic studies (Kümmerer et al. 2013). In contrast, studying individuals in the early phase—before substantial restorative or rehabilitative reorganization—facilitates a more precise identification of the neural substrates underlying language deficits, without the confounding effects of long-term plasticity (Klaus et al., 2020, Perez et al., 2021, Zhu et al., 2023). In other words, a critical advantage of early lesion studies is the absence of neural reorganization that could alter the neural representation of language, thereby allowing more focal brain regions to be reliably implicated in specific language tasks (Ochfeld et al., 2010, Kümmerer et al., 2013, Kristinsson et al., 2020).

To date, few studies have examined changes in white matter integrity during the early phases of stroke. For example, Yang et al. (2017) reported a positive correlation between the integrity of the left IFOF and SLF and the severity of acute aphasia after stroke. Kyeong et al. (2019) observed that the integrity of the left SLF contributed to speech, repetition, and naming performance, while the ILF was correlated with comprehension in individuals with subacute aphasia. Cho and Jang (2021) later found that the left AF was significantly associated with naming and repetition. Jang et al. (2023) suggested that the left AF was strongly related to aphasia severity in subacute stroke patients. These findings suggest that microstructural properties of white matter pathways may differentially influence language performance in the early stages after stroke. However, existing studies exhibit several limitations. Most have relatively small sample sizes, which restricts the generalizability of findings. Many also examine only one or a few predetermined white matter tracts within the dual-stream pathways, overlooking the roles of broader neural networks. Furthermore, language function assessments in these studies are often narrow, focusing on only one or a limited set of linguistic subdomains.

Given that stroke lesions often disrupt extensive brain tissue, resulting in diverse and co-occurring language deficits, it is methodologically inappropriate to investigate the linguistic roles of isolated regions by studying the neural correlates of a single or a limited set of language aspects in a stroke cohort (Dick et al., 2014, Ding and Schnur, 2022). Therefore, to gain insights into the linguistic roles of specific neural tracts and to advance our understanding of the neural substrates underlying aphasic deficits, we herein comprehensively analyzed the microstructural properties of both the dorsal and ventral pathways within the dual-stream language model, as well as two additional language-related tracts: the frontal aslant tract (FAT) and the extreme capsule fascicule (EmC), in a cohort of patients with subacute PSA. Furthermore, we evaluated the relationships between the microstructural integrity of these tracts and deficits across multiple language subdomains, aiming to identify specific tract-linguistic correlates.

Materials and methods

Subjects

Participants were enrolled in this study if they met the following inclusion criteria: 1) aged, 18 to 80 years; 2) native Chinese speakers with right-handedness; 3) diagnosis of first-ever left hemispheric hemorrhagic or ischemic stroke; 4) time post onset (TPO) within 90 days; 5) aphasia quotient (AQ) score less than 93.8, as assessed by the Chinese version of the Western Aphasia Battery (WAB) (Yu et al. 2023). The exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) previous history of stroke, right or bi-hemispheric stroke; 2) age over 80 years or TPO exceeding 90 days; 3) history of coexisting psychiatric disorders that could influence language performance; 4) inability to complete MRI scanning or language assessment; and 5) failure to reconstruct any of the right-hemispheric target fiber tracts using DTI tractography.

The protocol of this study has been registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.prg.cn) (no. ChiCTR2200058117) and finally approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital. Informed consent to participate in this research was obtained from one relative of all participants.

Language assessment

The Chinese version of WAB was applied to assess the language ability of all participants. The percentage score of the language subdomains, including spontaneous speech, auditory comprehension, repetition, naming, and fluency, was calculated according to a formula: percentage score = (patient’s score / total score of each subset) × 100 %. In addition, an AQ value of every participant was also obtained to measure the global severity of aphasia (Yu et al. 2023). Generally, a higher percentage score of language subdomains and AQ value indicates retaining relatively good language ability and a mild degree of aphasia.

Image data acquisition and processing

All Image data acquisition was completed with a 3.0 Tesla Siemens MR scanner. Three-dimensional high-resolution T1-weighted images (3D T1WI) of the whole brain tissue were acquired with a magnetization-prepared rapid gradient-echo (MPRAGE) protocol (voxel size of 1 × 1 × 1 mm). The scanning parameters of the 3D T1WI sequence were as follows: repetition time (TR) / echo time (TE) = 2300 ms / 2.98 ms; inverse time (TI) = 900 ms; flip angle = 9°; field of view (FOV) = 256 × 248 mm2; slice thickness = 1.1 mm with no inter-slice gap. The DTI sequence scanning was achieved with diffusion-weighted single-shot echo planar imaging based on the parameters as follows: TR / TE = 7200 ms / 104 ms; FOV = 896 × 896 mm2; flip angle = 90°; b-values = 0 / 1000 s/mm2; non-collinear sensitizing gradient directions = 64; slice thickness/ slice spacing = 2.5 mm / 2.5 mm.

All DICOM data were preprocessed offline by applying the FMRIB Software Library (FSL, 5.0.9 version) software package (http://fsl.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/fsl/fslwiki/FSL) (Yu et al., 2022, Yu et al., 2023). Firstly, all DICOM files were converted into NIfTI format files with the “dcm2niigui” tool; secondly, head motion and eddy currents correction was performed with the “eddy_correct” command; next, skull-stripping and removal of non-brain tissue were accomplished with “BET” function; then, diffusion tensor model was linearly fitted to diffusion-weighted images utilizing the “DTIFIT” tool and generated fractional anisotropy (FA) images, axial diffusivity (AD) images, mean diffusivity (MD) images and radial diffusivity (RD) images. Finally, the preprocessed T1WI, along with the FA, AD, MD, and RD images, were spatially normalized into the standard MNI152 atlas space by using the “FLIRT” registration tool.

The imaging software “MRIcroGL” (https://www.mccauslandcenter.sc.edu/mricrogl/) was used to manually delineate the lesion map of each participant slice-by-slice on the normalized T1WI images and create the lesion overlay maps of all participants. An index “lesion load (i.e., the proportion of the critical region involved by a lesion)” was obtained to reflect the degree of a lesion infiltrating into the cortical language areas for every participant, follows an equation: lesion load = (the volume of the overlap / the total volume of the language areas) × 100 % (Benghanem et al., 2019, Ivanova et al., 2021, Yu et al., 2022, Yu et al., 2023). The masks of the cortical language areas, including the pars triangularis and pars opercularis of the left frontal gyrus (IFG), the posterior divisions of the left superior (pSTG) and middle temporal gyrus (pMTG), the left angular gyrus (AG), and the anterior and posterior divisions of the left supramarginal gyrus (SMG), were extracted from the standard brain atlas maps “HarvardOxford-cort-maxprob-thr25-2 mm.nii.gz” within the FSL software package.

DTI tractography and target fiber tracts

The reconstruction of the whole brain diffusion tensors was accomplished by applying the Diffusion Toolkit software package (version 0.6.4.1, https://trackvis.org/), and fiber tracking was performed employing the TrackVis software (version 0.6.1, https://trackvis.org/) comply with a deterministic fiber-tracking algorithm (i.e., fiber assignment by continuous tracking, FACT algorithm) (https://trackvis.org/faq/2007_ISMRM_diffusion_toolkit.pdf) (Yu et al., 2022, Yu et al., 2023). The fiber reconstruction was finally finished with a tracking threshold of a minimum FA value of 0.20 and a maximum angle of 35° by combining multiple a priori regions of interest (ROIs). Based on anatomical knowledge, the fiber streams passing through the manually-defined waypoint ROIs that define the trajectory of the fascicle were assigned to a specific fiber group (Wakana et al., 2004, Yeatman et al., 2014).

In this work, the dorsal and ventral fiber pathways, including the AF, the second (i.e., SLF_II) and third (i.e., SLF_III) branches of SLF, ILF, UF, IFOF, and the MdLF, as well as two other neural tracts − the FAT and EmC fibers − were selected as the target fiber tracts due to their potential linguistic roles as previously reported (Catani et al., 2012, Kümmerer et al., 2013, La Corte et al., 2021, Janelle et al., 2022).

By applying an approach of manually delineating multiple ROIs combined with the ‘AND/NOT’ function, all the target fiber tracts were reconstructed. The reconstruction protocols referred to the published literature (Makris et al., 2009, Catani et al., 2012, Makris et al., 2013, Fekonja et al., 2019, Barbeau et al., 2020, Kourtidou et al., 2021, Zhong et al., 2022). Four diffusion metrics, including the mean FA value, mean AD value, mean MD value, and mean RD value, were measured to reflect the microstructural properties of the selected fiber tracts.

Statistical analyses

The IBM SPSS software package (version 25.0) was applied to accomplish statistical analyses. Data distribution was evaluated for normality with the Shapiro-Wilk test. The inter-hemispheric difference in diffusion metrics was analyzed with a paired samples t-test (normally distributed variables) or Wilcoxon signed-rank test (non-normally distributed variables). The correlation between diffusion indices and percentage scores of language subdomains was determined with Spearman correlation analyses. Partial correlation analyses between diffusion metrics and language performance were also conducted while considering age, lesion size (i.e., lesion volume), and lesion load as controlled variables. The statistical significance level was set at α = 0.05. A Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons was applied and a corrected significant threshold of p < 0.0125.

Correlational analyses

Then, to examine the associations between DTI metrics and language subdomains assessments, Spearman correlational analyses were conducted. First, correlations between demographic/lesion variables and language subdomains were assessed. Lesion size (i.e., lesion volume) and lesion load were negatively correlated with all language subdomains scores (all *p* < 0.0125, Bonferroni-corrected; Supplementary Table S1). Age was negatively correlated with AQ (ρ= −0.266, p = 0.028), comprehension (ρ = −0.242, p = 0.047), and naming (ρ = −0.276, p = 0.023); however, these associations did not survive Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons (corrected α = 0.0125). No significant correlations were observed between language performance and sex, TPO, or education duration (p > 0.05).

Correlations between the left-hemispheric fiber tracts and language performance

In the left hemisphere, significant positive correlations were observed between mean FA values of several fiber tracts and language performance: the SLF_II with all language subdomains; SLF_III with subdomains except Comprehension; AF with AQ, Repetition, and Naming; MdLF with AQ, Comprehension, Repetition, and Naming; EmC with AQ, Speech, and Fluency; IFOF with Comprehension; and FAT with Fluency (all *p* < 0.0125, Bonferroni-corrected). Notably, several additional correlations were observed: the AF with Speech, Comprehension, and Fluency; IFOF with AQ, Naming, and Fluency; ILF with AQ, Comprehension, and Naming; MdLF with Speech and Fluency; EmC with Repetition and Naming; and FAT with Naming; however, these associations did not survive Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons (corrected α = 0.0125). No significant correlation was found between FA of the UF and any language subdomain (p > 0.05). See Table 3, Fig. 4, and Supplementary Fig. S1.

Correlations between the right-hemispheric fiber tracts and language performance

In the right hemisphere, the mean AD of the ILF remained significantly correlated with Speech (ρ = −0.317, p = 0.008) and AQ (ρ = −0.302, p = 0.012) after Bonferroni correction (corrected α = 0.0125). Several other correlations were observed but did not survive correction. Specifically, the mean FA of the UF correlated with comprehension (ρ = −0.257, p = 0.034); and the mean AD of the ILF was related to Repetition (ρ = −0.255, p = 0.036) and fluency (ρ = −0.261, p = 0.031). For the EmC, mean MD was associated with fluency (ρ = 0.270, p = 0.026), while mean RD correlated with Speech (ρ = 0.253, p = 0.037), Fluency (ρ = 0.258, p = 0.034), and AQ (ρ = 0.250, p = 0.040). The mean MD of the FAT correlated with Speech (ρ = 0.249, p = 0.041), Repetition (ρ = 0.260, p = 0.032), Naming (ρ = 0.242, p = 0.047), Fluency (ρ = 0.244, p = 0.045), and AQ (ρ = 0.280, p = 0.021). No other correlations were found between the remaining fiber tracts and language subdomains scores (p > 0.05).

Partial correlation analyses between the left-hemispheric fiber tracts and language performance

To account for potential confounding effects of age, lesion size, and lesion load, partial correlation analyses were performed between diffusion indices of left-hemispheric fiber tracts and language subdomains scores. For the mean FA value, several significant correlations were observed after Bonferroni correction (corrected α = 0.0125): the SLF_II was associated with AQ and Repetition; the IFOF positively correlated with all language subdomains except Repetition; the ILF correlated with AQ and Comprehension; the EmC was related to AQ, Speech, and Fluency; and the MdLF was associated with Comprehension. Several additional correlations were observed but did not survive multiple comparison correction: the IFOF with Repetition; the ILF with Speech, Naming, and Fluency; the MdLF with Speech and Naming; and the EmC with Comprehension and Naming. No significant correlations were found between language performance and diffusion metrics of the left AF, SLF_III, UF, or FAT (p > 0.05; see Table 4).

For the IFOF, mean AD, MD, and RD were correlated with AQ and Naming; in addition, AD and MD were related to Speech and Fluency, while AD was correlated with Comprehension (all *p* < 0.0125, Bonferroni-corrected; see Supplementary Table S2).

Discussion

To examine the relationship between white matter microstructure and language deficits, this study conducted a comprehensive analysis of all major fiber tracts within the dual-stream language pathways, as well as two additional language-related tracts—the FAT and EmC—in a cohort of patients with subacute PSA. The results revealed significant interhemispheric (i.e., left vs. right) differences in the microstructural integrity of the target tracts. Moreover, the integrity of both the dorsal and ventral fiber pathways was significantly correlated with performance across multiple language subdomains.

Diffusion metrics and white matter microstructural integrity

In this study, mean FA values were significantly lower in all target fiber tracts within the left hemisphere compared to the right hemisphere. As a widely used DTI metric, the FA value serves as a sensitive indicator for axonal integrity and microstructural organization (Alexander et al., 2007, Tae et al., 2018). Generally, reduced FA value reflects impaired fiber tract integrity. Indeed, the FA values of the left hemispheric fiber tracts were reported to decrease in previous studies on left hemispheric stroke. For example, two recent meta-analyses (Zhang et al., 2021b, Zhu et al., 2023) reported lower FA values in the left language-related pathways among aphasic patients relative to healthy controls. Notably, although region-of-interest (ROI)-based fiber tractography remains prevalent, deterministic algorithms are limited in regions containing crossing fibers. Several studies applying alternative methodologies—such as tract-based spatial statistics (TBSS) (Geva et al., 2015, Xing et al., 2017), Automated Fiber Quantification (AFQ) (Zhang et al., 2018, Braun et al., 2022), constrained spherical deconvolution (CSD)-based tractography (Soliman et al. 2023), voxel-based DTI (Rosso et al. 2015), and atlas-based DTI (Ivanova et al. 2016) —have also identified reduced FA values in left-hemispheric pathways. Thus, our finding of lower mean FA in left-sided tracts is consistent with established evidence.

On other diffusion metrics—including AD, RD, and MD values—we observed a trend toward lower AD and higher RD and MD values in the left hemisphere tracts compared to those in the right (Table 2). Few studies have comprehensively examined these three diffusion metrics in populations with PSA (Soliman et al. 2023). Using TBSS/AFQ, Zhang et al. (2018) found increased RD in the left IFOF and ILF in subacute aphasic patients relative to controls. Subsequently, Soliman et al. (2023), applying CSD-based tractography, reported significantly elevated RD in the left AF, ILF, and FAT, along with increased MD in the ILF and IFOF, in subacute aphasic patients. A meta-analysis by Zhu et al. (2023) further identified a trend of higher RD in both dorsal and ventral language pathways among patients with PSA. These collective findings align with our detected increases in RD in left hemisphere pathways. Literature on MD alterations in PSA is scarce. Biophysically, RD reflects water diffusion perpendicular to axonal fibers and its increase could be used as a sensitive biomarker of microstructural damage or demyelination (Winklewski et al., 2018, Paolini et al., 2025); while, MD, which represents the overall magnitude of water displacement, is often associated with pathological processes such as edema and necrosis and elevated MD values may indicate increased freedom of water diffusion resulting from expanded extracellular space due to axonal degeneration or myelin loss (Tae et al., 2018, Sanjari Moghaddam et al., 2021).

AD quantifies water diffusion parallel to axonal direction and is considered a marker of axonal integrity (Alexander et al. 2007). However, findings on AD alterations in PSA have been inconsistent. Zhang et al. (2018) observed decreased AD of the left IFOF in patients with subacute PSA relative to healthy controls, whereas Soliman et al. (2023) reported no significant increase in AD within left-hemisphere tracts in a cohort of subacute aphasia. Conversely, Zhu et al. (2023) found a trend of higher AD in both left dorsal and ventral stream pathways—including the AF, SLF, IFOF, ILF, and UF—in individuals with PSA. These discrepancies may be partly attributable to temporal dynamic AD evolution throughout post-stroke recovery. Evidence indicates that AD often decreases acutely (Xie et al. 2010), apparently normalizes during the subacute phase (Soliman et al. 2023), and increases in chronic stages (Bhasin et al., 2021, Zhu et al., 2023). As noted by Winklewski et al. (2018), the evolving pathology from acute to chronic stages diminishes the interpretability of AD over time, potentially explaining its limited correlation with behavioral outcomes. Moreover, variations in AD—as well as in RD—can be influenced by multiple interdependent biophysical processes, including myelination, axon diameter, membrane permeability, fiber reorganization, and glial responses (Alexander et al., 2007, Jones et al., 2013, Soliman et al., 2023). Thus, AD changes following stroke exhibit considerable heterogeneity, reflecting the complexity of underlying microstructural alterations and temporal dynamics, which limit their standalone interpretability.

Although the diffusion metrics AD, RD, and MD could provide valuable quantitative measures of microstructural integrity, their interpretation remains constrained by a strong dependence on accurate identification of the principal diffusion direction (Wheeler-Kingshott and Cercignani, 2009, Geva et al., 2015, Bain et al., 2019, Lee et al., 2021). In contrast, FA value is a more robust index, exhibiting minimal sensitivity to variations in diffusion-sensitizing parameters and demonstrating high sensitivity to white matter pathology (Zhang et al., 2021b, Zhu et al., 2023). Thus, the FA value is commonly used to reflect microstructural properties such as fiber density, axonal diameter, and myelination in white matter (Blom-Smink et al., 2020, Schurr et al., 2020, Lee et al., 2021). Accordingly, consistent with the majority of previous DTI studies in aphasia, the findings and correlation analyses results in the current study should be interpreted primarily based on FA values.

Alterations in diffusion metrics along white matter pathways are commonly observed after stroke; however, the neurobiological mechanisms underlying these microstructural changes and their relevance to PSA remain poorly understood (Yang et al., 2017, Zhu et al., 2023). The direct effects of stroke lesions on fiber tracts—such as axonal cytotoxic edema and lesion infiltration—typically occur during the acute phase following injury (Grassi et al. 2022). Currently, several potential mechanisms—such as wallerian degeneration (Schulz et al. 2017), neuroinflammatory responses (Nistor-Cseppentö et al., 2022, Shen et al., 2024a), and trans-synaptic degeneration (Shen et al. 2024b) —by which stroke could affect white matter integrity outside the primary lesion site in individuals with the subacute stage have been proposed. However, elucidating these topics extends beyond the scope of this work and warrants further investigation.

Dorsal fiber pathways and language

Although the relationship between the integrity of the left SLF/AF and language impairments secondary to a stroke has been explored, most previous tractography-based studies have treated them as uni-functional entity bundles, which may obscure their distinct functional roles in language processing (Kourtidou et al., 2021, Zhang et al., 2021b, Zong et al., 2023). Anatomically, the SLF is proposed to comprise three to four subdivisions, with only two branches—the SLF_II and SLF_III—directly implicated in language organization (Schurr et al., 2020, Janelle et al., 2022, Zong et al., 2023). The SLF_II originates from the angular gyrus and the anterior intraparietal sulcus and ends in the posterior regions of the superior and middle frontal gyrus, while the SLF_III extends from the supramarginal gyrus and terminates at the ventral premotor and prefrontal areas (Barbeau et al. 2020). As well, the AF can be subdivided into three segments: a long pathway directly connecting Wernicke’s to Broca’s area, along with two indirect pathways—the anterior and posterior segments— each associated with specific linguistic functions (Catani et al., 2005, Yu et al., 2022). To our best of knowledge, few studies with DTI have explicitly investigated the distinct contributions of these subcomponents within the left SLF and AF to language impairment in PSA. Given their differential cortical projections, precise dissection and clear delineation of these distinct tracts are essential for understanding their specific lingustic roles (Barbeau et al. 2020). Therefore, we investigated the associations of the subdivisions of the left SLF (i.e., SLF_II and SLF_III) and the long segment of AF with language deficits following subacute PSA.

So far, the specific linguistic functions of the left SLF_II and SLF_III remain rarely understood. Anatomical evidence indicates strong left-lateralization of SLF_II, suggesting its potential involvement in speech motor planning and/or syntactic processing during language production (Wang et al., 2016, Janelle et al., 2022). SLF_III, on the other hand, has been hypothesized to contribute to articulation and phonological processing (Barbeau et al., 2020, Zong et al., 2023), auditory comprehension (Nakajima et al., 2020, Schurr et al., 2020), repetition (Barbeau et al. 2020), and semantic memory (Malcolm et al., 2010, Zong et al., 2023). Recent work by Shams et al. (2023) demonstrated that SLF_II was significantly associated with naming performance, whereas SLF_III correlated most strongly with verbal repetition and comprehension. Additionally, the long AF may support pragmatic integration and higher-order cognitive function of language and contribute to speech repetition (Sierpowska et al. 2017), novel word learning (López-Barroso et al. 2013), and development of reading ability (Gullick and Booth 2015). Ivanova et al. (2021) reported that the left long segment of the AF (LSAF) correlated with auditory comprehension and naming in individuals with chronic aphasia, a finding extended by Yu et al. (2022), who identified associations between the LSAF and aphasia severity, comprehension, and naming in patients with subacute PSA. Together with our current results, these findings support the multifaceted linguistic functions of the left SLF_II, SLF_III, and AF, likely mediated through their integration within the distributed fronto-parietal-temporal networks (Nozais et al. 2023). Further research is warranted to enhance our knowledge of the distinct functional contributions of these tracts to language processing.

Ventral fiber pathways and language

Among ventral white matter pathways, the IFOF is the longest associative fiber bundle in the human brain, linking the frontal lobe with temporal, parietal, and occipital regions (Zhang et al., 2021b, Zhu et al., 2023, Nogueira et al., 2025). Owing to its extensive cortical terminations, the IFOF is considered multifunctional and has been implicated in a wide range of language subdomains such as comprehension, naming, reading, and global severity of aphasia (Zhang et al., 2021b, Zhu et al., 2023, Nogueira et al., 2025). Additionally, the IFOF can be structurally dissected into two distinct subcomponents: a superficial dorsal subdivision linking the frontal lobe to the superior parietal lobe and posterior occipital gyri, and a deep ventral subdivision connecting posterior occipital and basal temporal regions to the dorsolateral prefrontal and orbitofrontal cortices (Zhang et al., 2021b, Zhu et al., 2023, Nogueira et al., 2025). The left ILF connects the inferior temporal gyrus to the occipital lobe and has been associated with semantic processing, including comprehension and naming (Zhang et al., 2018, Zhang et al., 2021b, Soliman et al., 2023, Zhu et al., 2023). The MdLF, an association fiber pathway projecting from the superior temporal gyrus (STG) to the inferior parietal lobule (i.e., angular gyrus, AG), has been speculated to contribute to phonological and semantic processing—including word production, comprehension, and naming—attention processing, higher level visual and auditory processing (Makris et al., 2013, Makris et al., 2017, Hula et al., 2020, Luo et al., 2020).

Employing diverse analytical approaches, several studies have examined the relationships between ventral language pathways and language subdomains in patients with subacute PSA. For example, using TBSS and AFQ, Zhang et al. (2018) observed that the FA value of the left IFOF significantly correlated with multiple language subdomains, including comprehension, naming, repetition, and aphasia quotient. They also reported that the left ILF showed significant correlations with comprehension and repetition. Subsequently, through fixel-based analysis, Zhang et al. (2021b) reported significant associations between word-level comprehension and naming with the left IFOF. Additionally, using CSD-based tractography, Soliman et al. (2023) identified a significant correlation between the FA value of the left ILF and comprehension. In this study, correlation analyses revealed the involvement of the left IFOF and ILF across multiple language subdomains. Together with previous findings, our results further support the broad linguistic roles of the left IFOF and ILF in subacute PSA. Moreover, we found for the first time in a PSA population that the microstructural integrity of the left MdLF was associated with language performance—including aphasia severity, comprehension, and naming.

It should be noted that no significant linguistic relevance of the left UF was observed in our study, which contrasts with the findings reported by Zhang et al. (2018) and Zhang et al. (2021a). A few studies have reported that the left UF may be involved in some language processes, including lexical and semantic processing (Catani and Bambini, 2014, Yang et al., 2017), verbal fluency and object naming (Hope et al., 2016, Li et al., 2017), speech production (Geller et al. 2019), reading (Zhang et al. 2021b), and syntactic processes (Friederici and Gierhan 2013). These proposed functions are largely inferred from the language functions of its cortical projected regions, such as the orbito-frontal cortex and the superior temporal gyrus (Ivanova et al., 2016, Zhang et al., 2021b). However, the specific linguistic contributions of the left UF remain a subject of ongoing debate (Zhang et al., 2021b, Zhu et al., 2023, Han et al., 2024). According to the literature, several factors may account for the controversy regarding the linguistic roles of the left UF. First, the UF is anatomically positioned in a region less susceptible to spontaneous stroke lesions, which typically affect the basal ganglia. Second, the UF has been proposed to be bilaterally organized and to play a subsidiary role relative to the IFOF (Duffau et al., 2009, Yu et al., 2023). Thus, damage to the left UF may be compensated by the ipsilateral IFOF or the contralateral homolog in cases of left hemispheric injury (Duffau et al., 2009, Yu et al., 2023). In this study, we observed a correlation between the FA of the right UF and comprehension scores, which may reflect such a compensation effect. Additionally, the UF is challenging to accurately measure due to the susceptibility of diffusion sequences to artifacts near sinus regions (Yu et al. 2023). Therefore, elucidating the precise contributions of the UF to language processing may require specialized methods such as intraoperative electrical stimulation or surgical resection studies.

Dorsal and ventral interaction during language processing

While the dual-stream model posits a distinct separation between dorsal (speech production) and ventral (auditory comprehension) pathways (Ivanova et al. 2021), clinical observations often reveal inconsistencies between lesion sites and language deficits, challenging this strict dichotomy (Saur et al., 2008, Ueno et al., 2011, Kümmerer et al., 2013). A growing body of literature provides evidence supporting close interactions between the dorsal and ventral streams during language processing (Rolheiser et al., 2011, Ueno et al., 2011, Kümmerer et al., 2013, Bajada et al., 2015, Yu et al., 2023). Indeed, the mechanisms underlying language suggest that many clinical tests of aphasia—such as those assessing aphasia severity, speech repetition, naming, and grammatical processing—likely recruit broad cortical networks involving multiple processes that depend on connections and interactions between both streams (Fridriksson et al. 2018). For instance, naming requires processes including visual recognition, semantic and lexical retrieval, and motor planning and execution, engaging distributed nodes such as the posterior STG and precentral gyrus within both dorsal and ventral streams (Fridriksson et al., 2018, Yu et al., 2023). Likewise, speech repetition involves speech perception, phonological working memory, and articulatory planning and execution (Friederici and Gierhan 2013), with key hubs including the MTG, STG, and inferior parietal lobule (IPL) (Matchin and Hickok, 2020, Yu et al., 2023). Recently, Hula et al. (2020) found that both dorsal (AF) and ventral (IFOF, UF, MdLF) tracts were associated with semantic processing, while phonological ability correlated with AF and MdLF. Their findings challenge traditional accounts that deny a role for the dorsal AF in semantic processing or ventral pathways in speech production. Similarly, Zhang et al. (2021b) reported that aphasia quotient (AQ) was associated with both the SLF and IFOF, supporting the viewpoint that speech maintenance relies on dorsal–ventral interactions (Cloutman 2013). Consistent with these findings, our results also support functional interactions between the dorsal and ventral streams during language processing. However, the precise nature of these interactions remains unclear and warrants further investigation.

The FAT and EmC, and language

The EmC, another important ventral association pathway connecting the anterior temporal lobe (ATL) with the ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (Saur et al. 2008), has been previously implicated in language comprehension (Makris and Pandya, 2009, Rolheiser et al., 2011, Han et al., 2024). In the present study, we observed significant correlations between the left EmC integrity and multiple language subdomains, including global aphasia severity, spontaneous speech, comprehension, naming, and fluency. This is consistent with the role of the left EmC in mediating interactions between the inferior frontal gyrus (Broca’s area) and semantic-processing regions in the anterior to intermediate temporal cortex (Saur et al., 2008, Kourtidou et al., 2021), which may underlie our findings. Additionally, our results indicated a potential involvement of the left FAT in speech fluency and naming. The FAT is a left-lateralized frontal pathway connecting the inferior (IFG) and superior frontal gyri (SFG), which has been previously linked to speech fluency—including semantic and phonological fluency—as well as motor speech disorders such as stuttering (Kemerdere et al., 2016, Kronfeld-Duenias et al., 2016, Neef et al., 2018). Zhong et al. (2022) recently reported that left FAT damage was related to apraxia of speech and naming in patients with chronic PSA. The FAT has also been hypothesized to be critical for lexical selection and retrieval (Robinson et al., 2010, Satoer et al., 2014), which may explain the observed association between its structural integrity and naming performance.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the relatively small sample size and gender imbalance (with a predominance of male participants) may limit the generalization of our findings; second, while the WAB provides a comprehensive assessment of language faculty, it may be insufficient to detect specific deficits, such as semantic impairment, verbal fluency, naming, and complex syntax abilities. Future studies could incorporate more finely-grained linguistic assessments to better capture these aspects. Third, the inclusion of bilingual individuals with aphasia (e.g., Chinese-English speakers) could provide valuable insights into the linguistic and neural mechanisms underlying cross-linguistic differences and improve the external validity of future studies. Finally, the use of deterministic tractography in DTI presents challenges in accurately quantifying microstructural properties within crossing-fiber regions, which are prevalent in language-related fiber tracts. Advanced diffusion MRI techniques such as high angular resolution diffusion imaging (HARDI), diffusion spectrum magnetic resonance imaging (DSI), and modeling approaches like probabilistic tractography, constrained spherical deconvolution (CSD), or multi-tensor filtering (e.g., Unscented Kalman Filter), may help overcome these limitations by improving fiber reconstruction accuracy. Moreover, the functional relevance of specific subdivisions within language pathways remains incompletely characterized and warrants further detailed investigation.

Conclusions

A key advantage of investigating the correlation between fiber pathway injury and language deficits during the acute/subacute phase of stroke is the absence of confounding effects from long-term neural reorganization, which may alter the neural representation of language. Despite several limitations, the current work enhances our understanding of the linguistic functions of white matter fiber pathways. The effects of impaired microstructural integrity within the dominant hemispheric fiber bundle on language performance are complex in the early stage of stroke. Our findings provide supplementary evidence supporting an interaction between the left dorsal and ventral fiber pathways during language processing. This interaction may partially explain the diversity observed in lesion-symptom mapping in clinical practice, as well as discrepancies in lesion patterns across studies. Therefore, further follow-up studies are warranted to address the limitations of this study. Additionally, elucidating how stroke affects the structural integrity of fiber pathways remote from the primary lesion site would provide an important framework for further understanding of our findings and should be explored in future investigations.

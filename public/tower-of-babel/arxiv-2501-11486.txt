1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group. For an element
x\in G
, the
solubilizer
of
x
in
G
is define as

\Sol_{G}(x):=\{g\in G~|~\langle x,g\rangle\ \mbox{is soluble}\}.

By a result of John G. Thompson
[
17
, Corollary 2]
a finite group
G
is soluble if and only if every two-generated subgroup of
G
is soluble.Therefore,
G
is soluble if and only if
\Sol_{G}(x)=G
for all
x\in G
. By
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
,
x\in R(G)
, the soluble radical of
G
if and only if
\langle x,g\rangle
is soluble for any
g\in G
. So,
\Sol_{G}(x)=G
if and only if
x\in R(G)
.

In 2013, Hai-Reuven
[
8
]
, prove that, for every
x\in G
,
|{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(x)|
divides
|\Sol_{G}(x)|
, where
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(x)
is the centralizer of
x
in
G
. In 2023, Mousavi and et al.
[
14
]
, propose the following conjecture:

Conjecture

(Normalizer-Solubilizer Conjecture)
.

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group. Then for any
x\in G
,

|{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)|\mid|\Sol_{G}(x)|
, where
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
is the normalizer of
x
in
G
.

The authors of
[
1
]
confirm the validity of this conjecture for minimal simple groups and all groups of orders up to
2000000
.
The purpose of this paper is firstly to present an alternative condition that is equivalent to the normalization conjecture, and second is to prove the correctness of the normalizer-solubilizer conjecture when
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
is a Frobenius group with kernel
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(x)
. Additionally, as an application of these results, the paper classifies the finite simple groups
G
for which, for some
x\in G
,
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a maximal subgroup of order
pq
, where
p
and
q
are distinct primes.

All notations used are standard. We denote the normalizer of the subgroup generated by
x
as
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
, the centralizer of the element
x
as
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(x)
, soluble radical of
G
as
R(G)
, the set of Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
as
\Syl_{p}(G)
, where
p
is a prime number and set of all conjugates of
x
by elements of
G
as
x^{G}
. Additionally,
C_{n}
represents a cyclic group of order
n
,
D_{2n}
denotes the dihedral group of order
2n
. In this paper, all finite simple groups are discussed using standard notation.

.

[

Let

.

(vi)

If the elements of

.

[

Finite insoluble groups do not have self-normalizing subgroups of prime order.

Lemma 2.3
.

[

3.
Main Results

In this section, we present our main results, including the compatibility condition for the normalizer-solubilizer conjecture (Theorem
3.4
). Additionally, we discuss the connectedness of the normalizer-solubilizer conjecture in the special case where the normalizer of an element
x
in group
G
is a Frobenius group, and its kernel
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(x)
has prime index (Theorem
3.5
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
x\in G
. Then
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
acts by conjugation on
\Sol_{G}(x)
.

Proof.

Assume that
y\in\Sol_{G}(x)
and
t\in{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
. Then for some
i
coprime to
|x|
,

\langle x,y^{t}\rangle=\langle x^{t^{-1}},y\rangle^{t}=\langle x^{i},y\rangle^{t}=\langle x,y\rangle^{t}.

Hence
\langle x,y^{t}\rangle
is soluble and so
y^{t}\in\Sol_{G}(x)
.
∎

We consider the following notations for this section. Let
G
be a finite group and
x\in G
. We set
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}={\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}={\mathcal{C}}_{G}(x)
. By Lemma
3.1
, for any subgroup
{\mathcal{H}}
of
G
such that
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\leqslant{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
,
{\mathcal{H}}
acts by conjugation on
\Sol_{G}(x)
. The number of orbits resulting from this action is denoted by
\ell_{{\mathcal{H}}}
. So for
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{C}}_{x}
or
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
, we have
\ell_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}}
and
\ell_{N_{x}}
.

As
{\mathcal{H}}\trianglelefteq{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
, then,
{\mathcal{H}}\backslash\{1\}=\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}g_{i}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}
for some
g_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Also for any
g\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we set

n(g,x):=|\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|/|{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)|.

Note that
n(1,x)=|\Sol_{G}(x)|/|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|
. Now, for any
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
, as

{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)={\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)
, then

|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g^{u})|=|({\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g))^{u}|=|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)|.

Also,

|\Sol_{G}(x)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g^{u})|=|\Sol_{G}(x^{u^{-1}})\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)|=|\Sol_{G}(x)\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)|.

The last equality follows from Lemma
2.1
-(ii), since
\langle x\rangle=\langle x^{u}\rangle
. Therefore, for any
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
n(g,x)=n(g^{u},x)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group. By the notation above, for any
x\in G
, we have

|{\mathcal{H}}|\ell_{{\mathcal{H}}}=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}n(g_{i},x).

Proof.

We note that

|{\mathcal{H}}|\ell_{{\mathcal{H}}}=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{H}}}|{\rm Fix}_{Y}(g)|
, where
Y=\Sol_{G}(x)
,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{H}}|\ell_{{\mathcal{H}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in{\mathcal{H}}}|{\rm Fix}_{Y}(g)|

\displaystyle=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+\sum_{1\neq g\in{\mathcal{H}}}|{\rm Fix}_{Y}(g)|

\displaystyle=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+\sum_{1\neq g\in{\mathcal{H}}}|{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)\cap\Sol_{G}(x)|

\displaystyle=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+\sum_{1\neq g\in{\mathcal{H}}}|\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|

\displaystyle=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+\sum_{1\neq g\in{\mathcal{H}}}n(g,x)|{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)|

\displaystyle=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+\sum_{1\neq g\in{\mathcal{H}}}n(g,x)|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}(g)|

\displaystyle=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{g\in g_{i}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}}n(g,x)|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}(g)|

\displaystyle=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}n(g_{i},x)|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}(g_{i})||g_{i}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}|

\displaystyle=|\Sol_{G}(x)|+|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}n(g_{i},x).

∎

By Lemma
3.2
we can provide a formula for calculating the number of orbits resulting from the conjugation action of
{\mathcal{H}}
on
\Sol_{G}(x)
(especially for
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{C}}_{x}
or
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
). We note that, if
G
is soluble, then for any
x\in G
, and any
H\leqslant G
,
\Sol_{H}(x)=H
. Hence, for any
1\neq g\in{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
,

n(g,x)=|\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|/|{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)|=|{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g):{\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)|.

According to Lemma
3.2
, we have:

(1)

\ell_{{\mathcal{H}}}=|G:{\mathcal{H}}|+|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}:{\mathcal{H}}|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g_{i}):{\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g_{i})|,

where
{\mathcal{H}}\backslash\{1\}=\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}g_{i}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}
.

Instead of the solubility assumption, a weaker assumption can be considered. For example, let
G=KH
be a Frobenius group with kernel
K
and complement
H
. Suppose that
1\neq x\in K
. Since
K\leqslant R(G)
, so
\Sol_{G}(x)=G
and, for any
g\in{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\backslash\{1\}
, as
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)\leqslant K
,
\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)={\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)
. Hence, by Lemma
3.2
,
\ell_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}}
is given by Equation (
1
) with substituting
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
for
{\mathcal{H}}
. Additionally, if
K
is abelian, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}={\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)=K\leqslant{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
, so

\ell_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}}=|H|+\ell|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}:K|,

where
\ell+1
is the number of orbits resulting from the action of
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}/C_{x}
on
K
, for
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
acts trivially on
K
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

For any finite group
G
and any
x\in G
,
|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|\mid|\Sol_{G}(x)|
.

(ii)

For any finite group
G
and any
x\in G
,
\frac{|{\mathcal{C}}_{x}|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|}\ell_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}}
is an integer.

Proof.

Assume that (i) holds. Let
G
be a finite group and
x\in G
. Then for any
g\in{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
, by (i),
|{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)|
divides
|\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|
. So
n(g,x)
is an integer (note that
n(1,x)=|\Sol_{G}(x)|/|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|
). Now, by Lemma
3.2
,

\frac{|{\mathcal{C}}_{x}|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|}\ell_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}}=\frac{|\Sol_{G}(x)|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}n(g_{i},x),

where
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\backslash\{1\}=\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}g_{i}^{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}
. Therefore
\frac{|{\mathcal{C}}_{x}|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|}\ell_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}}
is an integer and (ii) holds.

Assume that (ii) holds. We show that for any group
G
and any
x\in G
,
|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|\mid|\Sol_{G}(x)|
. Let
G
be a finite group and
x\in G
. We will use induction on
|G|
. Assume that for any group
H
with an order less than
|G|
and for any
x\in H
,
|{\mathcal{N}}_{H}(\langle x\rangle)|\mid|\Sol_{H}(x)|
(we note that this is true for any group of order less that
60
). We set
{\mathcal{Z}}:=Z(G)
. Suppose that
{\mathcal{Z}}\neq 1
and
\bar{G}=G/{\mathcal{Z}}
. Since
{\mathcal{N}}_{\bar{G}}(\langle\bar{x}\rangle)={\mathcal{N}}_{x}/{\mathcal{Z}}
and
\Sol_{\bar{G}}(\bar{x})=\Sol_{G}(x)/{\mathcal{Z}}
then, by induction, we find that
|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}/{\mathcal{Z}}|\mid|\Sol_{G}(x)/{\mathcal{Z}}|
, which implies that
|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|\mid|\Sol_{G}(x)|
.

Now suppose that,
{\mathcal{Z}}=1
. Then for all
1\neq g\in{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)
is a proper subgroup of
G
contains
x
. By induction,

|{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)|\mid|\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|=|{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)\cap\Sol_{G}(x)|.

Therefore,
n(g,x)
is an integer and by Lemma
3.2
, with
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{C}}_{x}
we have

\frac{|\Sol_{G}(x)|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}n(g_{i},x)-\frac{|{\mathcal{C}}_{x}|}{|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|}\ell_{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}},

is an integer. Therefore (i) holds.
∎

In the following we show that, when
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
is a Frobenius group with kernel
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
for some
x\in G
, then
|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|\mid|\Sol_{G}(x)|
, if
|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}:C_{x}|
is prime. We note that the conjecture always is true for a soluble group
G
, since
\Sol_{G}(x)=G
for any
x\in G
. Therefore, the insoluble case is important.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be a finite insoluble group and
1\neq x\in G
. If
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
is a Frobenius group with kernel
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
such that
|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}/{\mathcal{C}}_{x}|
is a prime number. Then
|{\mathcal{N}}_{x}|\mid|\Sol_{G}(x)|
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
x\not\in R(G)
.
Let the result be considered true for every insoluble group of order less than
|G|
(note that the induction process begins with
A_{5}
, as shown in Table
1
, where
|x|=3
or
5
. In these cases, respectively,
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)\cong S_{3}
or
D_{10}
).

As
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
is a Frobenius group,
Z(G)\leqslant Z({\mathcal{N}}_{x})=1
. Thus for any
1\neq g\in{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)
is a proper subgroup of
G
. First assume that
1\neq g\in{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
. Then
x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)
, so
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)\neq 1
. If
{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)
is soluble, then
\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)={\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)
. Hence
|{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)|\mid|\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|
. Otherwise, since
|{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|\mid\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|
by Lemma
2.1
-(i), we can assume that

{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)={\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)\neq{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)={\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x).

Then
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)
is Frobenius group with kernel
{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)
. Also,

1\neq\frac{{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)}{{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)}=\frac{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}\cong\frac{({\mathcal{N}}_{x}\cap{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)){\mathcal{C}}_{x}}{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}}\hookrightarrow\frac{{\mathcal{N}}_{x}}{{\mathcal{C}}_{x}}

So,
|{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle):{\mathcal{C}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|
is prime and by induction

|{\mathcal{N}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(\langle x\rangle)|\,\mid\,|\Sol_{{\mathcal{C}}_{G}(g)}(x)|
.

Now assume that
g\in{\mathcal{N}}_{x}\backslash{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
. Let
H
be a complement of
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
in
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
. By assumption
H
is cyclic. We set
H=\langle t\rangle
. Then for any

g\in{\mathcal{N}}_{x}\backslash{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
,
g\in H^{u}
for some
u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{x}
, so

is an integer.

Therefore,

Proof.

The first part of Theorem
4.4
is proved in Theorem
3.7
. Therefore, we assume that
\Sol_{G}(x)
is a maximal subgroup of
G
. The simple groups with a maximal subgroup of order
pq
are listed in Table
2
. We need to identify which groups in Table
2
satisfy the theorem conditions.

Let, for some
q
-element

,
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)
be a maximal subgroup of
G
of order
pq
. Assume that
H
is another maximal subgroup of

such that
q\mid|H|
. Then for some

is a proper subgroup of
{\mathcal{N}}_{G}(\langle x\rangle)^{g}

|{\mathcal{N}}_{H}(\langle x^{g}\rangle)|=q
and

is soluble by Lemma
2.2
.

By GAP
[

]
, we can compute the conjugacy class of maximal subgroups of the Mathieu group

and the baby monster group

. The group
M_{23}
just contain one conjugacy class of maximal subgroups isomorphic to

which is normalizer of its Sylow

-subgroup. So, by Lemma
4.3
, for any
23
-element

of

\Sol_{M_{23}}(x)=C_{23}:C_{11}
.

Similarly, the baby monster group

, has only one conjugacy class of maximal subgroups isomorphic to

which is normalizer of its Sylow

-subgroups. So, by Lemma
4.3
, for any
47
-element

of
\mathscr{B}
,
\Sol_{\mathscr{B}}(x)=C_{47}:C_{23}

The Monster group
\mathscr{M}
contains a maximal subgroup that is isomorphic to

. According to
[

,
13
]
,
\mathscr{M}
contains no

-local soluble maximal subgroups. Furthermore, as noted in
[

, for some

-element

\Sol_{G}(x)\cong C_{59}:C_{29}
.

The Suzuki group has a maximal subgroup isomorphic to

, the Suzuki group has another soluble maximal subgroup contains a Sylow

Let

-element (by Lemma

). Assume that

, where

-element

.

Assume that

The universal continuous six functor formalism on light condensed anima
Li He

Abstract
. After the universal property of the six functor formalism Shv ⁡ ( − , Sp ) \mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}) on locally compact Hausdorff spaces given by Zhu, we show that the six functor formalism Shv ⁡ ( − , Sp ) \mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}) on light condensed anima in the sense of Heyer-Mann is initial among all six functor formalisms D D satisfying some mild conditions, and then we present some applications.



1.
Introduction

In mathematics, the six functor formalisms are fundamental to understand topology and geometry.
Recent years, people started to study the six functor formalisms from higher categorical perspective.
For example, Liu-Zheng and Mann (
[
Man22
, Appendix A.5]
) gave a very concise definition of six functor formalisms.

Heyer-Mann in
[
HM24
]
says that a
geometric setup

is a pair
(\mathscr{C},E)
, where
\mathscr{C}
is a category has all finite limits, and
E
is a family of morphisms in
\mathscr{C}
,
which is closed under composition, base change,
and contains all isomorphisms, and is closed under passing to diagonals.

Given a geometric setup
(\mathscr{C},E)
,
there is a
category of correspondences

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)
associated with
(\mathscr{C},E)
, which can be described as follows:

(a)

objects in
\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)

are objects in
\mathscr{C}
.

(b)

morphisms from
Y
to
X
are correspondences

Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\leftarrow}}Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}X
,
where
g
lies in
E
.

One can promote the category
\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)

to a symmetric monoidal category

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)^{\otimes}
whose underlying category is

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)
. See
[
HM24
, Section 2.3]
.

Liu-Zheng and Mann says that a
3-functor formalism

is a lax symmetric monoidal functor

D:\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)^{\otimes}\to{\Pr}^{L,\otimes},

where the symmetric monoidal structure on
\Pr^{L,\otimes}
is given by the Lurie’s tensor product.

Now, given a 3-functor formalism

D:\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)^{\otimes}\to{\Pr}^{L,\otimes},

we are able to obtain the following functors:

(1)

For any map
f:Y\to X
,
the correspondence
Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\leftarrow}}X=X

gives the pullback functor

f^{*}:D(X)\to D(Y)
.

(2)

For any map
g:Y\to X
in
E
,
the correspondence
Y=Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}X

gives the functor
g_{!}:D(Y)\to D(X)
.

(3)

The tensor product functor

-\otimes-:D(X)\otimes D(X)\to D(X)

is given by the composition

D(X)\otimes D(X)\to D(X\times X)\to D(X)
,
where the first functor is given by the lax symmetric monoidal functor
D
, and the second functor is
\Delta^{*}
,
where
\Delta:X\to X\times X
is the diagonal.

Then one can make the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

A
6-functor formalism

is a 3-functor formalism

D:\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)^{\otimes}\to{\Pr}^{L,\otimes}
,
such that the functors

-\otimes A
,
f^{*}
and
f_{!}
admit right adjoints.

A fundamental example of six functor formalisms is the six functor formalism on locally compact Hausdorff spaces.
Given a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
,
we can construnction
the category
\mathrm{Shv}(X;\mathrm{Sp})
of
\mathrm{Sp}
-valued sheaves on
X
.
And we know that the assignment
X\mapsto\mathrm{Shv}(X;\mathrm{Sp})

promotes to a six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{LCH},\mathrm{all})^{\otimes}\to{\Pr}^{L,\otimes},

where
\mathrm{LCH}
is the category of locally compact Hausdorff spaces. See
[
Sch25
, 7.4]
.

In his work
[
Zhu25
]
,
Qingchong Zhu showed that
the six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{LCH})^{\otimes}\to{\Pr}^{L,\otimes}

is initial among all
continuous
six functor formalisms
(See
[
Zhu25
, 4.18]
).
Here, a six functor formalism

D:\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{LCH})^{\otimes}\to{\Pr}^{L,\otimes}

is called continuous, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

For any locally compact Hausdorff space
X
, the category
D(X)
is dualizable.

(2)

D
satisfies canonical descent.
That is, for any locally compact Hausdorff space
X
,
the assignment

\mathrm{Open}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\to{\Pr}^{L};U\mapsto D(U)

is a sheaf.

(3)

D
sasisfies profinite descent.
That is, for a cofiltered limit
X\simeq\lim_{i}X_{i}
of compact Hausdorff spaces
X_{i}
, the induced functor

D(X)\to\lim_{i}D(X_{i})

is an equivalence.

(4)

For any hypercomplete locally compact Hausdorff space
X
, the family

\{x^{*}:D(X)\to D(*)\}_{x\in X}

is jointly conservative.

In other words, if
D
is a six functor formalism on locally compact Hausdorff spaces satisfying conditions (1)-(4),
then there exists uniquely a morphism

\alpha:\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})\to D(-)

between six functor formalisms.

Given a six functor formlism
D
on a geometric setup
(\mathscr{C},E)
, Heyer-Mann gave an extension of
D

so that we get a six functor formalism
on
(\mathrm{HypShv}(\mathscr{C}),E^{\prime})
, where
E^{\prime}
is a family in
\mathrm{HypShv}(\mathscr{C})
. See
[
HM24
, Section 3.4]
.
In particular, one can extend the six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})\to{\Pr}^{L}

on light profinite sets to the six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)\to{\Pr}^{L}

on light condensed anima.

Now, we can state our main result:

Theorem 1.2
.

The six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)\to{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa}

is initial among all six functor formalisms

D:\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)\to{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa}
satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

For any light profinite set
S
,
the
\infty
-category
D(S)
is dualizable,
and the family
\{s^{*}:D(S)\to D(*)\}_{s\in S}

is jointly conservative.

(b)

The functor
D:\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light},\mathrm{op}}\to{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa}

preserves limits.

We denote the unique morphism
by
\alpha:\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})\to D(-)
.

Given a six functor formalism
D:\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)\to{\Pr}^{L}
, Heyer-Mann introduced the concepts of
D
-suave maps
and
D
-prim maps. See
[
HM24
, Section 4.4, Section 4.5]
.
Using these concepts, we are able to show the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a map in
E

and
\alpha:D\simeq\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})\to D^{\prime}
the unique morphism from Theorem
1.2
. We have:

(1)

If
f:Y\to X
is
D
-suave,
then the commutative square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\prime}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\prime}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{X}}

is vertically right adjointable. That is, the canonical map

\alpha_{Y}f^{!}\to f^{!}\alpha_{X}

is an equivalence.

(2)

If
f:Y\to X
is
D
-prim,
then the commutative square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\prime}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{\prime}(Y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{Y}}

is vertically right adjointable. That is, the canonical map

\alpha_{X}f_{*}\to f_{*}\alpha_{Y}

is an equivalence.

With the morphism
\alpha:\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})\to D(-)
in hand,
We can give the following application.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
D:\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)\to{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa}
be a 6-functor formalism
satisfying conditions (a) and (b) in Theorem
1.2
.
For any light condensed anima
X

with projection
f_{X}:X\times\mathbb{R}\to X
,
the pullback functor

f_{X}^{*}:D(X)\to D(X\times\mathbb{R})

is fully faithful.

2.
Six functor formalisms on light profinite sets

In this section, we present the universal property of six functor formalisms on light profinite sets.

We always fix a cardinal
\kappa>\omega
.
And let
\Pr^{L}_{\kappa}

be the subcategory of
\Pr^{L}

spanned by
\kappa
-presentable
\infty
-categories
and colimit-preserving functors which preserves
\kappa
-compact objects.

We start with the definition of light profinite sets and
light condensed anima from

[
Cla25
, Section 4]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

A
light profinite set
is a topological space
S

which can be written as
S=\varprojlim_{i}S_{i}
,
the sequence inverse limit of finite discrete topological spaces
S_{i}
. The full subcategory of
\mathrm{Top}
spanned by light profinite sets is denoted by
\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}}
.

(2)

A
light condensed anima

is a functor
X:\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light},\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{An}
such that:

(a)

X(\sqcup_{i\in I}S_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\prod_{i\in I}X(S_{i})
, where
I
is a finite set and
each
S_{i}
is a light profinite set.

(b)

If
S_{\bullet}\to S
is a hypercover of
S

in
\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}}
, then the canonical map

X(S)\to\lim_{[n]\in\Delta}X(S_{n})

is an equivalence.

The full subcategory of

\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light},\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{An})

spanned by light condensed anima is denoted by

\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}}
.

Notation
.

Given a geometric setup
(\mathscr{C},E)
,
we use
6\mathrm{FF}(\mathscr{C},E)
to denote
the
\infty
-category of all six functor formalisms on
(\mathscr{C},E)
. That is, we have

6\mathrm{FF}(\mathscr{C},E)\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax}}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E)^{\otimes},{\Pr}^{L,\otimes}_{\kappa})\simeq\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},E),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes}).

Remark 2.2
.

Recall from
[
HM24
, 2.3.5]

that every morphism

(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}_{0})\to(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{D}_{0})
of geometric setups
will induce a lax symmetric monoidal functor

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{C}_{0})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Corr}(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{D}_{0})^{\otimes}.

(1)

Since
(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}},\text{all})\to(\mathrm{LCH},\text{all})

is a morphism of geometric setups,
we get a lax symmetric monoidal functor

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{LCH})^{\otimes},

which is indeed symmetric monoidal
by
[
HM24
, 2.3.6]
.

(2)

Since
(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}},\text{all})\to(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)

is a morphism of geometric setups
by
[
HM24
, 3.4.11]
,
we get a lax symmetric monoidal functor

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}},\text{all})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)^{\otimes},

which is indeed symmetric monoidal
by
[
HM24
, 2.3.6]
.

Recall that a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category

\mathscr{C}^{\otimes}
is called
presentably symmetric monoidal
,
if the underlying
\infty
-category
\mathscr{C}

is presentable, and the tensor functor

-\otimes-:\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}

preserves colimits separately in each variable.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Nik16
, 3.8]
)
.

Let
q:\mathscr{C}^{\otimes}\to\mathscr{D}^{\otimes}

be a symmetric monoidal functor between symmetric monoidal

\infty
-categories
\mathscr{C}^{\otimes}
and
\mathscr{D}^{\otimes}
.
For any presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category

\mathscr{E}^{\otimes}
, the functor

q^{*}:\mathrm{Fun}(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{E})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E})^{\otimes}

induced by pre-composition with
q

admits a symmetric monoidal left adjoint

q_{!}:\mathrm{Fun}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{E})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathscr{D},\mathscr{E})^{\otimes}.

Proposition 2.4
.

There is an adjunction
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{LCH}),}

where the right adjoint is given by restriction.
And the left adjoint

6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})\to 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{LCH})

is fully faithful.

Proof.

By Remark
2.2
,
we have the symmetric monoidal functor

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{LCH})^{\otimes}
.
Since
\Pr_{\kappa}^{L,\otimes}

is a presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category,
which is from
[
Aok25
, 2.3]
,
by Proposition
2.3
,
we know that the functor

\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{LCH}),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}}),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes},

admits a symmetric monoidal left adjoint.
Therefore, we have the adjunction
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}}),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{LCH}),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes},}

where the left adjoint is symmetric monoidal,
and the right adjoint is lax symmetric monoidal.
Passing to commutative algebras, we get the adjunction
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}}),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{LCH}),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes}),}

which is exactly the adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{LCH}).}

It’s clear that the left adjoint

6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})\to 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{LCH})

is fully faithful.
∎

The following result is given by

Zhu25
, 4.18]

Theorem 2.5
.

The six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})\to{\Pr}_{\kappa}^{L}

is initial among all six functor formalisms

(a)

For any light profinite set

,
the
\infty

is dualizable,
and the family

(b)

In other words, if we let
6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})^{\mathrm{cont}}

be the full subcategory of

\alpha:\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})\to D(-)
.

Proof.

We will use the notation in
[
Zhu25
, Theorem 4.18]
.
Let
D\in 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})

satisfies the assumptions,
whose image under the functor

6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})\to 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{LCH})

is still denoted by
D
.
Now since
\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})\in\mathrm{CoSys}^{c}

is initial,
there exists a unique morphism

\alpha:\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})\to D(-)
in
\mathrm{CoSys}^{c}
.
In the proof of
[
Zhu25
, Theorem 4.18]
,
Zhu showed that for any proper map

of locally compact Hausdorff spaces, the commutative square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Shv}(Y;\mathrm{Sp})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Shv}(X;\mathrm{Sp})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{Y}}

,

.

Construction 2.8
.

Let

be a light profinite set.
Note that the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Shv}(S;\mathrm{Sp})

is generated under colimits
by objects of the form

,
where

is an open subset of

.
Recall from

, 3.10]

\alpha_{S}:\mathrm{Shv}(S;\mathrm{Sp})\to D(S)

is a colimit-preserving functor,

,

is an open subset of

.

With this construction, one has the following basic observation.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
S
be a light profinite set.
By the construction of the functor

, we have:

(1)

\alpha_{S}1_{\mathrm{Shv}(S;\mathrm{Sp})}\simeq 1_{D(S)}
.

(2)

For any

,
we have
\alpha_{S}(A)\otimes\alpha_{S}(B)\simeq\alpha_{S}(A\otimes B)

and

,

3.
Six functor formalisms on light condensed anima

In this section, we show that the six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)\to{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa}

is initial among all six functor formalisms satisfying some conditions, see Theorem
3.3
.

We recall the extension result of six functor formalisms
given by Heyer-Mann.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
HM24
, 3.4.11]
)
.

There is a geometric class
E
of maps of light condensed anima with the following properties:

(1)

There exists a unique six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)\to{\Pr}^{L}

extending the six functor formalism

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp}):\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})\to\Pr^{L}
.

(2)

If
f:Y\to X
is a map of light condensed anima such that for any map
S\to X
from a light profinite set
S
, the pullback
Y\times_{X}S\to S

lies in
E
, then
f
lies in
E
.

(3)

If
f:Y\to X
is a map of light condensed anima which is
!
-locally on source or target in
E
,
then
f
lies in
E
.

(4)

If
f:X\to S
lies in
E
and
S
is a light profinite set, then
X
admits a

\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})^{!}
-cover by light profinite sets.

The maps in
E
are called
!
-able maps.

Corollary 3.2
.

There is an adjunction
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E),}

where the right adjoint is given by restriction.
And the left adjoint

6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}})\to 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)

is fully faithful.

Proof.

By Remark
2.2
,
we have the symmetric monoidal functor

\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}},\text{all})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)^{\otimes}
.
Since
\Pr_{\kappa}^{L,\otimes}

is a presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category,
which is from
[
Aok25
, 2.3]
,
by Proposition
2.3
,
we know that the functor

\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}}),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes},

admits a symmetric monoidal left adjoint.
Therefore, we have the adjunction
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{ProFin}^{\mathrm{light}}),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Corr}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E),{\Pr}^{L}_{\kappa})^{\otimes},}

where the left adjoint is symmetric monoidal,

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
D\in 6\mathrm{FF}(\mathrm{CondAn}^{\mathrm{light}},E)^{\mathrm{cont}}
.
For any light condensed anima
X

with projection
f_{X}:X\times\mathbb{R}\to X
,
the pullback functor

f_{X}^{*}:D(X)\to D(X\times\mathbb{R})

is fully faithful.

Proof.

The proof here is the same as the proof in
[
Zhu25
, 4.28]
. Consider the square
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times\mathbb{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\mathbb{R}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

By Lemma
4.14
,
we have
f_{!}1_{D(\mathbb{R})}\simeq 1_{D(*)}[-1]
,
thus by base change, we have

f_{X!}1_{D(X\times\mathbb{R})}\simeq f_{X!}p_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}1_{D(\mathbb{R})}\simeq p^{*}f_{!}1_{D(\mathbb{R})}\simeq p^{*}(1_{D(*)}[-1])\simeq 1_{D(X)}[-1].

The map
f:\mathbb{R}\to*
is
\mathrm{Shv}(-;\mathrm{Sp})
-suave,
and hence is
D
-suave, we get

f_{X}^{!}1_{D(X)}\simeq f_{X}^{!}p^{*}1_{D(*)}\simeq p_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}f^{!}1_{D(*)}\simeq p_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}(1_{D(\mathbb{R})}[1])\simeq 1_{D(X\times\mathbb{R})}[1],

where the third equivalence is by Lemma
4.14
.
Thus we have

f_{X}^{!}(-)\simeq f_{X}^{!}1_{D(X)}\otimes f_{X}^{*}(-)\simeq 1_{D(X\times\mathbb{R})}[1]\otimes f_{X}^{*}(-)\simeq f_{X}^{*}(-)[1].

Now, for any

, we have

\displaystyle f_{X*}f_{X}^{*}E

\displaystyle\simeq f_{X*}f_{X}^{!}E[-1]\simeq f_{X*}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(1_{D(X\times\mathbb{R})},f_{X}^{!}E)[-1]

\displaystyle\simeq\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(f_{X!}1_{D(X\times\mathbb{R})},E)[-1]\simeq\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(1_{D(X)}[-1],E)[-1]\simeq E.

Thus
f_{X}^{*}:D(X)\to D(X\times\mathbb{R})

is fully faithful.
∎

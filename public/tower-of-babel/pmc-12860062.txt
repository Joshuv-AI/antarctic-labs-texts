Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the primary cause of non-communicable disease mortality worldwide, with ischemic heart disease representing the most significant epidemiological burden [1]. Recent data indicate that CVDs account for over 17.9 million deaths annually, emphasizing the urgent need for novel therapeutic insights [2].

The paradigm-shifting discovery of ferroptosis in 2012 established an iron-dependent mode of regulated cell death, distinct from classical apoptosis or necrosis through its unique dependence on lipid peroxidation and glutathione depletion [3]. While early research predominantly focused on its roles in oncology and neurodegenerative disorders [4], recent years have witnessed accelerated migration of ferroptosis investigations into cardiovascular pathophysiology [5]. Compelling evidence now implicates ferroptotic cell death in myocardial infarction progression, doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity, and atherosclerotic plaque instability [6].

Despite exponential growth in publications, the research landscape of ferroptosis and cardiovascular diseases remains fragmented and lacks a well-defined intellectual structure. Only a few studies have systematically analyzed the collaborative networks across institutions and countries, visualized the evolution of knowledge with patterns of interdisciplinary convergence, or identified emerging hotspots, such as the associations between various types of cell death and CVDs [7]. Traditional narrative reviews cannot objectively capture these multidimensional dynamics, whereas bibliometric analysis addresses this critical gap by providing quantitative mapping of research trends, collaborative patterns, and knowledge diffusion pathways [8–10].

Currently, several bibliometric analyses of ferroptosis in CVDs have been published [11]. However, previous studies have been limited in their analytical approaches, which has precluded a more in-depth analysis of mechanisms and future research trends. To bridge this gap, we conducted the comprehensive bibliometric analysis of ferroptosis in CVDs using CiteSpace (6.2.R3), VOSviewer (1.6.19), and Microsoft Excel 2021. This study aims to map the scientific collaboration network since 2016, identify burst keywords signaling frontier topics, profile core journals, and high-impact authors, and ultimately translate findings into strategic priorities for future research clinical translation.

Methods

Data collection

The selected time frame (January 2016 to April 2025) encompasses the significant progress and growing attention in the field of ferroptosis research in CVDs, starting from when the association between ferroptosis and CVDs began to attract attention [12] and ending with the most recent data available at the time of our study. From the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and were downloaded on April 22, 2025. The search criteria employed were: [TS=(“cardiovascular disease” OR “heart disease” OR “coronary artery disease”) AND TS=(ferroptosis)], and the time span for publications was from “2016-01-01” to “2025-04-22,” ultimately retrieving 525 records. We filtered out non-valid publications, specifically: early access articles (n = 9), meeting abstracts (n = 3), editorial materials (n = 3), retracted publications (n = 1), book chapters (n = 1), and non-English literature (n = 1). A total of 507 publications were ultimately included for analysis after rigorous screening, and the relevant records were performed and preserved in plain text format (Fig. 1).

Flowchart of the study

Data visualization

The relevant literature records obtained from the WoSCC were exported in plain text (.txt) format for subsequent bibliometric analysis. All raw data underwent systematic preprocessing in Microsoft Excel 2021 (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA, USA), including deduplication, format standardization, and metadata verification. This structured dataset enabled quantitative analysis of publication volume, citation frequency, and disciplinary distribution patterns. The bibliometric analysis was performed utilizing VOSviewer 1.6.19 (Centre for Science and Technology Studies at Leiden University) and CiteSpace 6.2.R3 (developed by Chaomei Chen, Drexel University, USA) to systematically investigate collaboration patterns, knowledge structures, and research frontier.

Visualization parameters

Network layouts were generated using CiteSpace’s force-directed algorithm with modularity optimization (Q > 0.7 indicating significant clustering). Cluster labels were extracted through log-likelihood ratio testing to ensure semantic relevance. All visualizations underwent 3 iterations of parameter tuning to balance interpretability and analytical rigor [13]. Notably, in VOSviewer, we applied “full counting” for normalized data processing. For cluster analysis of authors, institutions, and countries or regions, a minimum threshold of three publications and citations was set. Additionally, the Walktrap method was used in thematic map analysis for more precise topic delineation, while other analyses adhered to default software thresholds. By using these visualization methods, this study provided researchers in related fields with an intuitive and clear understanding of the research landscape.

Discussion

Primary findings

Ferroptosis, as a novel mode of cell death, has garnered substantial attention within the field of CVDs in recent years. The bibliometric analysis revealed the rapid development since 2016. From 2016 to 2025, a total of 507 relevant papers were published, indicating a yearly increase in research activity and influence. The fitting function curve analysis demonstrated that the number of publications peaked in 2024 at 248 papers, signifying that the studies are attracting an increasing number of scholars. Moreover, the average annual citation counts also reached its highest value in 2024, with 9,772 citations, further underscoring the academic significance of this research direction.

Ferroptosis research in CVDs was multidisciplinary, with biochemistry and molecular biology, pharmacology and pharmacy, and cell biology as the primary disciplines, accounting for nearly 70% of published papers. From a national and institutional perspective, China not only held a leading position in this research area but also markedly exceeded the USA, with 1,188 institutions involved. Among these, Southern Medical University topped the list with 21 published articles, which reflected the robust research capacity of China and its international collaborative capabilities.

The journal analysis showed that the International Journal of Molecular Sciences had published the highest number of papers. Citation network analysis revealed that journals focusing on “Molecular, Biology, Genetics” formed the core of the citation network. Co-citation analysis identified the significant role of journals such as Nature. The author analysis revealed that authors like Zhang Jing had the highest number of publications, while authors like Dixon SJ had the highest co-citation counts, indicating their substantial influence in this field.

The keyword analysis demonstrated a shift in research focus over the period from 2016 to 2025, with keywords such as “cell death,” “oxidative stress,” “cardiovascular diseases,” “ferroptosis,” “reactive oxygen species,” and “inflammation.” After 2020, research on “ferroptosis” indicted a significant increase, and since 2022, new burst keywords have emerged, including “cardiovascular diseases,” “myocardial infarction,” “lipid metabolism,” “autophagy,” and “mitochondrial dysfunction.”

Systematic summary of ferroptosis mechanisms in CVDs

Ferroptosis, a form of iron-dependent regulated cell death, has emerged as a significant mechanism in the pathogenesis of CVDs. This process is characterized by the accumulation of lipid peroxidation products and is regulated through a complex interplay of antioxidant systems, iron metabolism, and lipid metabolism. The core regulatory framework of ferroptosis is anchored by the interconnection of these three axes, where any imbalance can trigger cell death.

The antioxidant system, primarily composed of the cystine/glutamate antiporter (System Xc⁻), GSH, and GPX4, plays a crucial role in maintaining redox homeostasis. System Xc⁻, a heterodimeric amino acid transporter, facilitates the uptake of cystine, which is converted to cysteine and subsequently used for GSH synthesis [24, 40]. GPX4 then utilizes GSH to reduce lipid peroxides, thereby preventing oxidative damage. Research findings suggest that interference with the GPX4/GSH/SLC7A11 antioxidant system accelerates lipid peroxide accumulation and raises ROS levels within cells, consequently inducing ferroptosis [41]. Dysfunction of this system, depletion of GSH, or loss of GPX4 activity leads to the accumulation of lipid peroxides and subsequent ferroptosis [42].

Iron metabolism is another critical pathway involved in ferroptosis. Iron overload can lead to the generation of ROS through the Fenton reaction, exacerbating lipid peroxidation. The regulation of iron homeostasis involves proteins such as ferritin, which stores iron, and ferroportin, which exports iron from cells. Disruption of these proteins can lead to iron accumulation and increased susceptibility to ferroptosis. Studies on the impact of high-iron diets on ferroptosis and cardiac damage, as well as the protective role of mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) in managing iron homeostasis, have also been significantly co-cited [43]. These studies highlight the potential therapeutic targets for myocardial infarction by specifically managing iron metabolism. Additionally, the nuclear receptor coactivator 4 (NCOA4) mediates ferritinophagy, a process that degrades ferritin and releases iron, further contributing to ferroptosis [44].

Lipid metabolism also plays a pivotal role in ferroptosis. Polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) are particularly susceptible to peroxidation, and their oxidation products, such as phospholipid hydroperoxides, drive ferroptosis. Key enzymes involved in this process include acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4) and lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase 3 (LPCAT3), which facilitate the incorporation of PUFAs into phospholipids [45]. Lipid peroxidation is further catalyzed by lipoxygenases (LOXs), leading to the accumulation of lipid peroxides and cell death [46].

Beyond these primary pathways, other mechanisms such as the FSP1-COQ10-NAD(P)H axis (FSP1 utilizes NAD(P)H to reduce ubiquinone (CoQ10) to ubiquinol, thereby scavenging lipid peroxidation radicals and inhibiting ferroptosis.) and the GCH1-BH4-DHFR axis (GCH1 catalyzes the synthesis of BH4, which is regenerated via DHFR to capture radicals, protecting cells from ferroptosis.) also contribute to ferroptosis regulation [47, 48]. These pathways provide additional layers of complexity and potential therapeutic targets for modulating ferroptosis in CVDs.

In summary, ferroptosis in CVDs is orchestrated through a delicate balance of antioxidant defenses, iron homeostasis, and lipid metabolism. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial for developing targeted therapies aimed at mitigating cardiovascular damage and improving patient outcomes.

Research frontiers and new topics

The keyword analysis of ferroptosis in CVDs from 2016 to 2025 reveals a significant shift in research focus, with emerging themes and hotspots indicating the evolving nature of the field. Initially, keywords such as “cell death,” “oxidative stress,” “cardiovascular diseases,” “ferroptosis,” “reactive oxygen species,” and “inflammation” dominated the literature, reflecting foundational research into the mechanisms of ferroptosis. After 2020, there was a notable increase in studies focusing on “ferroptosis,” highlighting its growing importance in CVDs research. Since 2022, new burst keywords have emerged, including “cardiovascular diseases,” “myocardial infarction,” “lipid metabolism,” “autophagy,” and “mitochondrial dysfunction,” indicating a shift towards more specific and clinically relevant topics.

These emerging topics underscore the intricate pathophysiology of CVDs and suggest that targeting these mechanisms could offer promising prevention and treatment strategies. For instance, the increasing focus on lipid metabolism and mitochondrial dysfunction highlights the potential for novel therapeutic interventions targeting these pathways [49–51]. Additionally, the emergence of keywords related to myocardial infarction and autophagy indicates a growing interest in understanding the role of ferroptosis in acute and chronic cardiac conditions.

The analysis of burst references further supports these trends, identifying key papers that have significantly impacted the field. These papers cover a range of topics, from the molecular mechanisms of ferroptosis to its role in specific CVDs, providing a foundation for future research. The identification of these emerging trends and hotspots not only enhances our understanding of ferroptosis in CVDs but also facilitates the rapid translation of these findings into clinical practice, thereby accelerating the development of new diagnostic and therapeutic methods. Future work will benefit from employing multi-omics approaches to comprehensively understand the cellular and molecular changes associated with ferroptosis, thereby facilitating the development of effective therapies, with a particular focus on clinical applications and the development of innovative treatments for CVDs.

Limitations

This study shows a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of 507 scholarly papers related to ferroptosis and CVDs in the WoSCC database. Utilizing diverse analytical methods, it explores the research hotspots and future trends in this field, providing a reference for the study of ferroptosis and CVDs. The comprehensive and systematic review of the latest progress in this domain significantly enhances the academic value and practical significance of this research, offering important guidance for the future development of the field of ferroptosis and CVDs.

However, there are some limitations to this study. Firstly, our focus on English-language publications limits the comprehensiveness of the study, excluding high-quality non-English research. Secondly, all data are sourced solely from the WoSCC database, which may not cover all relevant literature from other databases (such as PubMed, Google Scholar, and Scopus), potentially introducing bias. Finally, the manual screening of literature may be subject to the subjective judgment of researchers, lacking a unified standard and objectivity. This may compromise the integrity of the literature collection and the reliability of the analysis results. Despite these limitations, WoSCC was chosen as the primary data source due to its extensive coverage of core articles in biomedical research and its widespread use in bibliometric analyses. The quality of the study data is crucial for the credibility of the generated knowledge maps, further justifying our choice of WoSCC. Similar limitations have also been reported in other bibliometric studies [52–54].

Future directions

The future directions in the study of ferroptosis in CVDs are likely to focus on several key areas that hold significant promise for advancing our understanding and developing novel therapeutic strategies. Future research should aim to further elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying ferroptosis in CVDs, with a particular emphasis on lipid peroxidation, iron metabolism, and oxidative stress. The identification of novel regulatory proteins and pathways will be crucial for developing targeted therapies, as these mechanisms play a central role in the induction and regulation of ferroptosis.

In parallel, the development of reliable biomarkers for ferroptosis is essential for early detection and monitoring of CVDs. Future studies should focus on identifying and validating biomarkers that can accurately reflect the presence and extent of ferroptosis in clinical samples, thereby facilitating the translation of basic research findings into clinical practice. Optimizing detection methods for ferroptosis is another critical area for future research. Current methods are often limited by low sensitivity and specificity, and frequently require expensive techniques, such as costly equipment and specialized reagents. Future efforts should aim to improve detection accuracy and reduce costs, enabling more widespread use of ferroptosis detection in both research and clinical settings.

The therapeutic potential of modulating ferroptosis in CVDs is a promising area for future research. Studies should focus on developing small molecules, genetic probes, and other interventions (such as exercise and nutrition) that can specifically target ferroptosis pathways. Preclinical and clinical trials will be essential for evaluating the efficacy and safety of these potential therapies, paving the way for novel therapeutic approaches. Future research should also expand the scope of disease models to include a broader range of CVDs, such as cardiovascular toxicity, diabetic cardiomyopathy, and heart failure. This will provide a more comprehensive understanding of the role of ferroptosis in various cardiovascular conditions and identify new therapeutic targets.

Finally, fostering global collaboration is essential for advancing the study of ferroptosis in CVDs. Future efforts should focus on building partnerships between institutions and countries to share resources, expertise, and data. This collaborative approach will be crucial for addressing the challenges associated with ferroptosis research and accelerating the development of effective therapies.

Conclusion

The study of ferroptosis in CVDs is a rapidly evolving field with significant translational potential. Our bibliometric analysis has illuminated the progress made in this domain and identified key directions for future research, which are crucial for advancing our understanding of disease mechanisms and developing novel therapies. Future studies should continue to elucidate the molecular mechanisms of ferroptosis to enhance its clinical applicability and accelerate discoveries and innovations in this field through global collaboration and interdisciplinary research.

Abstract
. Let 𝒞 \mathcal{C} be a ( d + 2 ) (d+2) -angulated category. In this paper, we define the notions of cotorsion pairs and weak cotorsion pairs in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , which are generalizations of the classical cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories. As an application, we give a geometric characterization of weak cotorsion pairs in ( d + 2 ) (d+2) -angulated cluster categories of type A A . Moreover, we prove that any mutation of a (weak) cotorsion pair in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is again a (weak) cotorsion pair. When d = 1 d=1 , this result generalizes the work of Zhou and Zhu on classical cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories.

1.
Introduction

The concept of torsion pairs in abelian categories was first introduced by Dickson
[
5
]
, with its triangulated version later explored by Iyama and Yoshino
[
12
]
. Nakaoka
[
23
]
then introduced cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories, aiming to unify the abelian structures that arise from
t
-structures
[
2
]
and from cluster tilting subcategories
[
19
,
20
,
12
]
. In triangulated categories, torsion pairs and cotorsion pairs are closely related, as they can be transformed into each other by shifting the torsion-free components. Therefore, considering torsion pairs and cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories is essentially equivalent.

In
[
8
]
, Geiss, Keller, and Oppermann introduced a higher-dimensional generalization of triangulated categories, known as
(d+2)
-angulated categories. These categories extend the classical notion of triangulated categories, with the latter being a special case when
d=1
.
(d+2)
-angulated categories often arise in the context of
d
-cluster tilting subcategories in triangulated categories.

A significant motivation for studying mutation stems from cluster algebras. Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
7
]
introduced cluster algebras to provide an algebraic and combinatorial framework for understanding the positive and canonical bases of quantum groups. In their work, the mutation of clusters was defined in the context of cluster algebras. To categorize cluster algebras, Buan et al.
[
4
]
introduced cluster categories, which Keller
[
18
]
later showed to be triangulated. In these categories, rigid indecomposable objects correspond to cluster variables, while cluster tilting subcategories correspond to clusters. Similar to the mutation of clusters, the mutation of cluster tilting subcategories involves replacing one indecomposable object with a unique alternative such that the result is again a cluster tilting subcategory. For more details, see
[
4
]
.
As a further generalization, the mutation of cluster tilting subcategories in arbitrary Krull-Schmidt
K
-linear triangulated categories was studied in
[
3
,
12
,
25
]
. Iyama and Yoshino
[
12
]
introduced a broader concept of mutation in triangulated categories, which encompasses the mutation of cluster tilting subcategories as a special case. Zhou and Zhu
[
28
]
later proved that any mutation of a torsion pair remains a torsion pair.
After the introduction of
(d+2)
-angulated categories, Lin
[
21
]
defined mutation pairs in these categories and proved that, given such a mutation pair, the corresponding quotient category naturally inherits a
(d+2)
-angulated structure.

In this paper, we define cotorsion pairs and weak cotorsion pairs in
(d+2)
-angulated categories, generalizing the classical notion of cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories. It is worth noting that the concept of cotorsion pairs in
(d+2)
-angulated categories introduced here is stricter than the torsion class defined by Jørgensen in
[
17
]
. As an application, we provide a geometric characterization of weak cotorsion pairs in
(d+2)
-angulated cluster categories of type
A
. Furthermore, we demonstrate that any mutation of a (weak) cotorsion pair in
(d+2)
-angulated cluster categories remains a (weak) cotorsion pair. When
d=1
, this result extends the work of Zhou and Zhu on classical cotorsion pairs in triangulated categories.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we provide an overview of
(d+2)
-angulated categories, mutation pairs within these categories, and recall the construction of quotient categories from
[
21
]
. In Section 3, we define cotorsion pairs in
(d+2)
-angulated categories and present a geometric characterization of weak cotorsion pairs in
(d+2)
-angulated cluster categories of type
A
. In Section 4, we define mutation of cotorsion pairs, examine their compatibility with cotorsion pairs, and prove that any mutation of a (weak) cotorsion pair in
(d+2)
-angulated cluster categories remains a (weak) cotorsion pair.

3.
Cotorsion pairs in
(d+2)
-angulated categories

In this section, we define cotorsion pairs and weak cotorsion pairs in
(d+2)
-angulated categories. Moreover, we give a geometric characterization of weak cotorsion pairs in
(d+2)
-angulated cluster categories of type
A
.

From now on, we assume that
\mathcal{C}:=(\mathcal{C},\Sigma^{d},\Theta)
is a
(d+2)
-angulated category.

3.1.
Cotorsion pairs

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
.

(1)

The pair
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is called a
torsion

pair
if
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})=0
and for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, there exist two
(d+2)
-angles

X\rightarrow C\rightarrow Y_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow Y_{d}\rightarrow\Sigma^{d}X

and

\Sigma^{-d}Y\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{d}\rightarrow C\rightarrow Y

with
X_{i}\in\mathscr{X}
,
Y_{i}\in\mathscr{Y}
for
i=1,\cdots,d
, and
X\in\mathscr{X}
,
Y\in\mathscr{Y}
.

(2)

The pair
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is called a
cotorsion

pair
if
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{X},\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})=0
and for any
C\in\mathcal{C}
, there exist two
(d+2)
-angles

\Sigma^{-d}X\rightarrow C\rightarrow Y_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow Y_{d}\rightarrow X

and

Y\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{d}\rightarrow C\rightarrow\Sigma^{d}Y

with
X_{i}\in\mathscr{X}
,
Y_{i}\in\mathscr{Y}
for
i=1,\cdots,d
, and
X\in\mathscr{X}
,
Y\in\mathscr{Y}
.

(3)

The pair
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is called a
weak

cotorsion

pair
if
\mathscr{X}
is contravariantly finite,
\mathscr{Y}
is covariantly finite,
\mathscr{Y}=(\Sigma^{-d}\mathscr{X}){{}^{\bot}}
and
\mathscr{X}={{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})
.

Remark 3.2
.

By definition, we know that
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is a cotorsion pair if and only if

(\mathscr{X},\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})
is a torsion pair. Therefore, the concepts of cotorsion pairs and torsion pairs are equivalent in this context. This is consistent with the case in triangulated categories.

We recall the following fact which is needed in the sequel.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
6
, Lemma 3.13]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(d+2)
-angulated category, and

A_{0}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{0}}A_{1}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{1}}A_{2}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\alpha_{d-1}}A_{d}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{d}}A_{d+1}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{d+1}}\Sigma^{d}A_{0}

be a
(d+2)
-angle in
\mathcal{C}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\alpha_{0}
is a section;

(2)

\alpha_{d}
is a retraction;

(3)

\alpha_{d+1}=0
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
be a cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
. Then the following statements hold

(1)

\mathscr{X}
is contravariantly finite;

(2)

\mathscr{Y}
is covariantly finite;

(3)

\mathscr{X}={{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})
;

(4)

\mathscr{Y}=(\Sigma^{-d}\mathscr{X}){{}^{\bot}}
.

Proof.

We prove the statements (1) and (3), (2) and (4) can be proved similarly. Since
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is a cotorsion pair, for arbitrary object
\Sigma^{-d}C\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists a
(d+2)
-angle

\Sigma^{-d}X\xrightarrow{\Sigma^{-d}f}\Sigma^{-d}C\rightarrow Y_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow Y_{d}\rightarrow X

with
X\in\mathscr{X}
and
Y_{i}\in\mathscr{Y}
for
i=1,\cdots,d
. Applying
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\mathscr{X},-)
to the
(d+2)
-angle above, we obtain the exact sequence

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\mathscr{X},X)\xrightarrow{\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\mathscr{X},f)}\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\mathscr{X},C)\rightarrow 0

since
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{X},\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})=0
. Thus
f
is a left
\mathscr{X}
-approximation of
C
and then
\mathscr{X}
is contravariantly finite.

Now we give the proof of (3). Since
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathscr{X},\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})=0
,
\mathscr{X}\subset{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})
. It is enough to show
{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})\subset\mathscr{X}
. For any object
Z\in{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})
, there exists a
(d+2)
-angle

\Sigma^{-d}X\xrightarrow{g}\Sigma^{-d}Z\xrightarrow{h}Y_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow Y_{d}\rightarrow X

with
X\in\mathscr{X}
and
Y_{i}\in\mathscr{Y}
for
i=1,\cdots,d
. Note that

, then

is a retraction by Lemma
3.3
. So

is a direct summand of

, and we obtain that
\mathcal{Z}\in\mathscr{X}
.
∎

This proposition immediately yields the following conclusion.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
. If
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is a cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
, then
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is a weak cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 3.6

When

In this subsection, we give a geometric characterization of weak cotorsion pairs in

-angulated cluster categories of type

[

, Section 6]
. First, we briefly review some definitions and related results.

Iyama introduced higher dimensional analogues of Auslander-Reiten theory and Auslander algebras (see, for instance
[
11
]
), generalizing several classical concepts from the representation theory of finite dimensional algebras. In
[
13
]
, the notion of
d
-representation finiteness was introduced as an ideal framework for studying these concepts. (See also
[
11
,
14
,
9
]
)

In this subsection, we focus on the most well-understood class of
d
-representation finite algebras: the
(d-1)
-th higher Auslander algebras of linearly oriented
A_{n}
(see
[
11
,
13
]
), which are denoted by
A_{n}^{d}
. The case when
d=1
is already well understood. In this case, the algebra
A_{n}^{1}
is the path algebra of linearly ordered
A_{n}
.

We recall a higher dimensional cluster category, denoted
\mathscr{O}_{\Lambda}
, for any
d
-representation finite algebra
\Lambda
, as introduced in
[
24
]
.
The cluster category
\mathscr{O}_{\Lambda}
is constructed as a subcategory of the
2d
-Amiot cluster category
\mathscr{C}_{\Lambda}^{2d}
in
[
1
]
. The
2d
-Amiot cluster categories generalize classical
2d
-cluster categories to non-hereditary algebras
\Lambda
. In particular, the categories
\mathscr{C}_{\Lambda}^{2d}
are
2d
-Calabi-Yau and triangulated. These properties of
\mathscr{C}_{\Lambda}^{2d}
are used to show that
\mathscr{O}_{\Lambda}
is
(d+2)
-angulated, and also satisfies a certain Calabi-Yau property. It is worth noting that for
d=1
, the categories
\mathscr{O}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathscr{C}_{\Lambda}^{2}
, and both coincide with the classical cluster category of the hereditary representation finite algebra
\Lambda
. For all
d
-representation finite algebras
\Lambda
, the category
\mathscr{O}_{\Lambda}
contains only finitely many indecomposable objects, which can be arranged in a
d
-dimensional analogue of an Auslander-Reiten quiver.

For the case
\Lambda=A_{n}^{d}
, we obtain the
(d+2)
-angulated cluster categories of type
A
, denoted by
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
. The following property is easy to verify based on the construction of
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y}
be two subcategories of
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
. Then we have an isomorphism

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}}(\mathscr{X},\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})\cong\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits_{\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}}(\mathscr{Y},\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{X})

We recall the geometric description of
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
based on
[
24
, Section 6]
. For general positive integers
m,r
, index sets are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{m}^{r}

\displaystyle=\{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{r})\in\{1,\ldots,m\}^{r+1}\mid\forall x\in\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}\colon i_{x}+2\leq i_{x+1}\}

{\vphantom{\mathbf{I}_{m}^{r}}}{}^{\circlearrowleft}{\mathbf{I}_{m}^{r}}

\displaystyle=\{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{r})\in\mathbf{I}_{m}^{r}\mid i_{r}+2\leq i_{0}+m\}.

Let
X
and
Y
be increasing

(r+1)
-tuples of real numbers which belong to
{\vphantom{\mathbf{I}_{m}^{r}}}{}^{\circlearrowleft}{\mathbf{I}_{m}^{r}}
. We say that
X=(x_{0},\dots,x_{r})

intertwines

Y=(y_{0},\dots,y_{r})
if
x_{0}<y_{0}<x_{1}<y_{1}\dots<x_{r}<y_{r}
.
We write
X\wr Y
for this relation. A collection of increasing
(d+1)
-tuples is called
non-intertwining
if
no pair of the elements intertwine (in either order).

We call the elements in
{\vphantom{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}}{}^{\circlearrowleft}{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}

diagonals
. There is a bijection between indecomposable objects in
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
and diagonals in
{\vphantom{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}}{}^{\circlearrowleft}{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}
. For convenience, we index the indecomposable objects in
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
by
{\vphantom{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}}{}^{\circlearrowleft}{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}
as in
[
24
]
. For a set of diagonals
\mathfrak{X}
in
{\vphantom{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}}{}^{\circlearrowleft}{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}

\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
by
\mathscr{X}
. We write

for the indecomposable object corresponding to
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{d})\in{\vphantom{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}}^{\circlearrowleft}{\mathbf{I}_{n+2d+1}^{d}}
, sometimes
(i_{0},\ldots,i_{d})
represents an indecomposable object in
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
directly without confusion.

Lemma 3.12
.

[

By Lemma

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y}
be subcategories of
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
, and
\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{Y}
be the corresponding set of diagonals in

(1)

(2)

\mathfrak{X}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathfrak{Y}
and
\mathfrak{Y}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathfrak{X}

(3)

(\mathscr{Y},\mathscr{X})

(4)

\mathfrak{Y}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathfrak{X}
and

.

(5)

\mathfrak{X}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathfrak{Y}
and
\mathfrak{Y}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathfrak{Y}
.

Proof.

Note that the category
\mathscr{O}_{A_{n}^{d}}
contains only finitely many indecomposable objects, so every subcategory of

and

and
\mathfrak{Y}=\mathop{\rm nc}\nolimits\mathfrak{X}
.

“
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
”: It is easy to check by Remark
3.13
.

“
(3)\Rightarrow(4)

is a weak cotorsion pair, we have
\mathscr{Y}={{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{X})
and
\mathscr{X}=(\Sigma^{-d}\mathscr{Y}){{}^{\bot}}
. This means

and

“

“

\mathscr{X}={{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathscr{Y})
and

, so
(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{Y})
is a weak cotorsion pair.

4.
Mutation of cotorsion pairs in

-angulated categories

In this section, we define mutation of cotorsion pairs in

-angulated categories, and show that any mutation of a (weak) cotorsion pair in

4.1.
Mutation of cotorsion pairs

First, we give the definition of mutation of subcategories,
analogous to the mutation pairs defined in Definition

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X

\mathcal{C}

(1)

We call

\mathcal{D}
-

\mathcal{D}
-approximation and
a_{d+1}
is a right
\mathcal{D}
-approximation.

(2)

\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(X)

\mathcal{D}
-

of

(d+2)
-angle

\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(X)\xrightarrow{c_{1}}D_{1}\xrightarrow{c_{2}}D_{2}\xrightarrow{c_{3}}\cdots\xrightarrow{c_{d}}D_{d}\xrightarrow{c_{d+1}}X\xrightarrow{c_{d+2}}\Sigma^{d}X

where

for
i=1,\cdots,d
,
c_{1}
is a left
\mathcal{D}
-approximation and
c_{d+1}
is a right
\mathcal{D}
-approximation.

Definition 4.2
.

Given two subcategories

of
\mathcal{C}
with
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathscr{X}

(1)

\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X}):=\{\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(X)\mid X\in\mathscr{X}\}\cup\mathcal{D}.

(2)

\mathcal{D}
-

of

by

\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X}):=\{\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(X)\mid X\in\mathscr{X}\}\cup\mathcal{D}.

Remark 4.3

It is clear that
\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{D})=\mathcal{D}=\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{D})
. When

, we have

and
\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X})=\Sigma^{-d}\mathscr{X}

We give some definitions which will be used in what follows.

By the definition of cotorsion pair, it is easy to see the core

-rigid subcategory.

In this subsection, we fix a strongly functorially finite

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{D}

-rigid subcategory of
\mathcal{C}

{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathcal{D})=(\Sigma^{-d}\mathcal{D}){{}^{\bot}}

-mutation pair.

Proof.

Since

-angle

where

for
i=1,\cdots,d
,

is a left
\mathcal{D}
-approximation.
By Remark

, we have

By assumption

.
Since

\mathcal{D}
-approximation of

Since

\mathcal{D}

-mutation pair.
∎

The following results are useful in the sequel.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a strongly functorially finite
d
-rigid subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
satisfying
{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathcal{D})=(\Sigma^{-d}\mathcal{D}){{}^{\bot}}

\mathcal{Z}

\mathcal{Z}

(1)

The subfactor category
\mathfrak{U}:=\mathcal{Z}/\mathcal{D}
is a
(d+2)
-angulated category.

(2)

For any

, there exists an isomorphism

\mathfrak{U}(X,T^{d}Y)\cong\mathcal{C}(X,\Sigma^{d}Y).

Proof.

The statement (1) is obvious by Lemma

. We only show the statement (2).

For any

, since
(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Z})
is a
\mathcal{D}
-mutation pair by Lemma

, there exists a
(d+2)
-angle

Y\xrightarrow{f}D_{1}\rightarrow D_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow D_{d}\xrightarrow{g}T^{d}Y\rightarrow\Sigma^{d}Y,

where

for
i=1,\cdots,d
,

,
f
is a left
\mathcal{D}
-approximation and
g
is a right
\mathcal{D}
-approximation.

For any
X\in\mathcal{Z}
, applying

to the above

-angle, we have the following exact sequence:

\mathcal{C}(X,D_{d})\xrightarrow{\mathcal{C}(X,~g)}\mathcal{C}(X,T^{d}Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(X,\Sigma^{d}Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(X,\Sigma^{d}D_{1}).

Since

, we have

. In this case, we get

\mathcal{C}(X,\Sigma^{d}Y)\cong\mathcal{C}(X,T^{d}Y)/\text{Im}(C(X,g)).

It is enough to show

∎

\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X})
be a strongly functorially finite subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
satisfying
{{}^{\bot}}(\Sigma^{d}\mathcal{D})=(\Sigma^{-d}\mathcal{D}){{}^{\bot}}
, which is denoted by
\mathcal{Z}
. Moreover, we assume that
\mathcal{Z}
is extension closed. Then the pairs

(\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X}),\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{Y}))\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{and}\hskip 8.53581pt(\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X}),\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{Y}))

are cotorsion pairs in
\mathcal{C}
and we have

\mathcal{I}(\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X}))=\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X}))\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{and}\hskip 8.53581pt\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X}))=\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X})).

Proof.

By Remark
4.5
, we have that
\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X})
is
d
-rigid, then so is
\mathcal{D}
. So both
\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X})
and
\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{Y})
are well-defined. We denote
\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X})
,
\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{Y})
and
\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X}))
by

\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
,
\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
respectively. As in the previous subsection, we denote by
\mathfrak{U}=\mathcal{Z}/\mathcal{D}
the subfactor
(d+2)
-angulated category. By Theorem
4.11
, we have that
(\overline{\mathscr{X}},\overline{\mathscr{Y}})
is a cotorsion pair in
\mathfrak{U}
and
\mathcal{I}(\overline{\mathscr{X}})=\overline{\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X})}
. Note that its forward 0-mutation
(T^{d}\overline{\mathscr{X}},T^{d}\overline{\mathscr{Y}})
is also a cotorsion pair in
\mathfrak{U}
. By the construction of
(d+2)
-angles in
\mathfrak{U}
, we have
T^{d}\overline{\mathscr{X}}=\overline{\mathscr{X}^{\prime}}
and
T^{d}\overline{\mathscr{Y}}=\overline{\mathscr{Y}^{\prime}}
. Then by Theorem
4.11
, we have that the pair
(\mathscr{X}^{\prime},\mathscr{Y}^{\prime})
is a cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{I}(\overline{\mathscr{X}^{\prime}})=\overline{\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X}^{\prime})}
. This means that
\overline{\mathcal{I}^{\prime}}=T^{d}\overline{\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X})}=T^{d}\mathcal{I}(\overline{\mathscr{X}})=\mathcal{I}(\overline{\mathscr{X}^{\prime}})=\overline{\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X}^{\prime})}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I}(\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X}))=\mu^{-1}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X}))
. The assertion for
\mathcal{I}(\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathscr{X}))=\mu_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{I}(\mathscr{X}))
can be proved similarly.
∎

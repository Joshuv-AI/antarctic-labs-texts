Nonreciprocal Spin-Wave Dynamics in Crescent-Shaped Ferromagnetic Nanowires Preprint: APS/123-QED
Uladzislau Makartsou, Mateusz Gołębiewski, Attila Kákay, Olena Tartakivska, Maciej Krawczyk

Abstract
Recent progress in the study of spin-wave propagation in ferromagnetic waveguides has highlighted the role of nonreciprocity arising from both the interfacial Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction and the chiral nature of dipolar interactions. In this paper, we examine how a nanowire with a crescent-shaped cross-section affects spin-wave propagation along its long axis when a bias magnetic field is applied perpendicular to the axis. Employing micromagnetic simulations supported by analytical modeling, we systematically analyze the effects of geometry and external magnetic field strength on the magnetization dynamics and spin-wave amplitude distribution. The results demonstrate that these factors modify the dispersion relation of spin waves and influence its nonreciprocity, which can vary depending on the mode type. This work advances the fundamental understanding of spin-wave dynamics in curved geometries and offers new perspectives for designing magnonic waveguides with tailored properties.



I
Introduction

Progress in nanoscale fabrication technologies improves the ability to control and manipulate spin waves (SW)
Gubbiotti 2019
;
Fischer et al. 2020
. Such control can be achieved through geometrical factors—including the size, shape, and curvature of the ferromagnet—as well as through the orientation and texture of the magnetization
Donnelly et al. 2021
;
Girardi et al. 2024
. For instance, self-assembly, or two-photon lithography for polymer structuring, combined with eltrodeposition
Gołębiewski et al. 2024
;
Hunt et al. 2020
;
van den Berg et al. 2023
, atomic layer deposition (ALD)
Guo et al. 2023
, or focused electron beam induced deposition (FEBID)
Fernández-Pacheco et al. 2020
,
enables the realization of nearly arbitrary shapes in the sub-micron regime, including complex three-dimensional (3D) nanoarchitectures
Makarov et al. 2022
;
Fernández-Pacheco et al. 2017
. Understanding how the geometric and topological properties of ferromagnetic materials influence SW propagation in 3D sub-micron systems is therefore emerging as a key frontier in magnonics
Gubbiotti et al. 2025
.

Even a single nanowire, acting as an SW waveguide, provides a versatile platform for studying complex magnetization dynamics and exploring potential applications
Stano and Fruchart 2018
;
Heinz et al. 2020
;
Giordano et al. 2023
. When curvature is introduced—either along the wire axis
Tkachenko et al. 2012
or across its cross-section—additional phenomena emerge
Streubel et al. 2016
, including domain-wall pinning, curvature-induced interactions reminiscent of the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya type, and topological effects, thereby opening up new research directions
Sheka 2021
. Among these, nonreciprocity is of particular interest. As a manifestation of broken time-reversal symmetry, nonreciprocity of SWs has been recognized for decades
Camley 1987
, and the ability to control it is crucial for device functionalities such as isolators, diodes, and circulators
Szulc et al. 2020
;
Jamali et al. 2013
, which are essential in wave-based signal processing. However, studies of SW dynamics in this context have so far been limited to thin films and bilayers
Stamps 1994
;
Szulc et al. 2024
;
Teuling et al. 2025
;
Ishibashi et al. 2020
;
Heins et al. 2025
;
Jiang et al. 2025
, and nanowires with highly symmetric cross-sections and regular magnetization textures, such as tubes with circular or hexagonal cross-section, or rolled circular tubes
Otálora et al. 2016
;
Körber et al. 2021
.

Given the multitude of factors that influence SW dynamics, novel behaviors can be expected from complex geometries with noncolinear magnetization. Nanowires with a crescent-shaped (CS) cross-section (see Fig.
1
), including those arranged in diamond-like networks
May et al. 2019
;
Sahoo et al. 2021
;
May et al. 2021
;
Sahoo et al. 2018
, have already proven promising for realizing near-degenerate states in 3D artificial spin-ice structures and for providing reconfigurable platforms for magnonic devices. Following the work on CS links in 3D spin-ice lattices, standalone CS nanowires with a 3D sinusoidal geometry were realized
Askey et al. 2024
. Subsequently, straight nanowires of the similar CS shape were fabricated in the inverse arangement, i.e., by electron-beam lithography, reactive-ion etching, and Py sputtering on pre-patterned Si substrates
Pradhan et al. 2024
. In addition, deposition of magnetic multilayers onto nanospheres, establishes curved geometries in a form of ferromagnetic cups
Albrecht et al. 2005
, the technique which can be extended to cover naorods and form long CS ferromagnetic nanowires. Within this framework, CS nanorods can represent a fundamental building block for more complex magnonic architectures. Despite this potential, the nonreciprocal properties of SWs in CS nanowires have not yet been investigated. The crescent geometry introduces unique effects—such as varying curvature in the cross-section, inhomogeneous magnetization, and edge contributions—that remain absent in nanowires with regular, highly symmetric cross-sections.

In this work, we demonstrate SW nonreciprocity in an infinitely long nanowire with a CS cross-section. The combination of geometry and a bias magnetic field sets an inhomogeneous equilibrium magnetization and edge conditions, on top of curvature-driven contributions, thereby enabling nonreciprocal dispersion
Gołębiewski et al. 2023
. Our analysis extends beyond the fundamental mode, characterized by in-phase oscillations across the entire cross-section, to include edge-localized and quantized modes in the transverse plane. Analyzed modes can be excited and detected using a microstrip antenna, thus providing direct relevance to experimental realization. We show that the sign and magnitude of the nonreciprocity depend not only on the nanowire curvature but also on the mode character, exhibiting strong variation with the wavenumber. The micromagnetic simulation results are supported by an analytical model,
which clarifies how inversion-symmetry breaking and mode profile govern the nonreciprocity of SWs propagating in transversely magnetized nanowires. The reported properties highlight the complexity of SW dynamics in nanowires with a complex cross-section shape and show the potential of CS nanowires as key building blocks for nanoscale magnonics.

III.3
Ellipticity-driven sign reversal of nonreciprocity

Let us assume that the external field
H

\mu_{0}H=B_{\mathrm{ext}}

However, deviations of the magnetization from the

In the SW approximation, we assume that the magnetization vector has the following form

\mathbf{M}=\Big(A\,m_{x}(y,z)\,e^{-i\Omega t+ik_{x}x},\;B\,m_{y}(y,z)\,e^{-i\Omega t+ik_{x}x},\;M_{\text{s}}\Big),

(2)

where
\Omega=2\pi f
is angular frequency, the SW profiles in the transverse cross-section of the nanowire, i.e., in the
(y,z)
plane, are:
m_{x}(y,z)

A change in the sign of the

As the film thickness increases (from 30 µm), the critical point shifts to lower wavenumbers and eventually disappears. Conversely, the second critical point shifts to higher wavenumbers with increasing film thickness and it was correlated with the transition from a dipole-dominating regime to an exchange-dominating regime.

Similar asymmetric hybridization between the PSSW and the fundamental modes was observed in the homogeneous ferromagnetic film but with asymmetric surface anisotropies
Szulc et al. 2024

\delta f
was not the focus of this study, the results clearly indicate a direct corelation between the hybridization of the fundamental mode and the PSSW on nonreciprocity. Recently, the strong asymmetric hybridization between the fundamental magnetostatic mode and first-order PSSW was demonstrated in bilayers composed of two ferromagnetic films in direct contact
Heins et al. 2025
. The change of the sign of the nonreciprocity has also been shown experimentally in the dipole-coupled two
FeGa

Gerevenkov et al. 2023

\delta f
for acoustic and optical mode exchanges.

The situation is more complex in the case of the CS nanowire, studied in this paper. The sign of the nonreciprocity parameter,
\delta f

We associate edge-localized LF mode appearance from strong inhomogeneity of the dipolar field near the edges. This mechanism already reported for in-plane-magnetized rectangles
Jorzick et al. 2002

Guslienko et al. 2003
and circular ferromagnetic dots
Guo et al. 2013

The situation is different for the fundamental mode of the CS (i.e., the HA mode), where the sign of the

IV
Conclusions

We numerically studied the SW propagation in a long CS nanowire magnetized perpendicular to its axis, focusing on the asymmetry of the dispersion relation. Our results show that the type of SW is an important factor influencing nonreciprocity, which is measured as the difference in frequencies of waves with the same wavenumber that propagate in opposite directions. Specifically, the sign of the nonreciprocity (under the assumed convention) is negative for the edge mode and the mode quantized along the CS width, but positive for the mode quantized along the bias field. Interestingly, changing the CS geometry (i.e., changing the curvature of the bottom CS surface or the magnitude of the bias magnetic field) can induce a change in the sign of the nonreciprocity for the lowest frequency mode. We demonstrate that this transition is associated with the change from an edge mode to a bulk mode due to the change in the demagnetizing field at the CS edges, which is introduced by the change in geometry or the rotation of the magnetization.
We also observed a change in the sign of the nonreciprocity for the fundamental mode depending on the wavenumber. We demonstrate that, similarly to the previously studied asymmetric thin films, this effect originates from the hybridization between fundamental SW mode with the higher frequency thickness quantized SW modes. However, in the case of CS, it is multimode hybridization.
These results demonstrate the interesting tunability of nonreciprocal effects in CS nanowires, which can be fabricated with current technologies, and thus underscore their potential as building blocks for reconfigurable magnonic devices.

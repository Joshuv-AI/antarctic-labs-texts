Transverse densities of the energy-momentum tensor and the gravitational form factors of the pion Thanks: Submitted to Acta Physica Polonica B special volume dedicated to Dmitry Diakonov, Victor Petrov, and Maxim Polyakov.
Wojciech Broniowski 1,2 and Enrique Ruiz Arriola 3

Abstract
We present general features of the transverse densities of the stress-energy-momentum tensor Θ μ ​ ν \Theta^{\mu\nu} in the pion. We show positivity of the transverse density of Θ + ⁣ + \Theta^{++} (analogous to the positivity of the transverse density of the electromagnetic current J + J^{+} ) and discuss its consequences in conjunction with analyticity and quark-hadron duality, as well as the connection to π ​ π \pi\pi scattering at low energies. Our analysis takes into account the perturbative QCD effects, dominating at high momenta (or low transverse coordinate b b ), the effects of Chiral Perturbation Theory, dominating at low momenta (high b b ), and meson dominance in the intermediate region. We incorporate constraints form analyticity, leading to sum rules for the spectral densities of the corresponding form factors, which i.a. are relevant for the high-momentum (or the low- b b ) asymptotics. With the obtained high- and low- b b behavior, we deduce that the scalar (trace-anomaly) gravitational transverse density Θ μ μ ​ ( b ) \Theta^{\mu}_{\mu}(b) must change sign, unlike the case of the positive definite J + ​ ( b ) J^{+}(b) or Θ + ⁣ + ​ ( b ) \Theta^{++}(b) . We also discuss the transverse pressure in the pion, which is positive and singular at low b b , and negative at high b b , in harmony with the stability criterion. The results for the form factors for space-like momenta are compared to the recent lattice QCD data.

2.2
The energy momentum tensor

We will use the Hilbert definition of the SEM tensor via the coupling
to gravity, which in the QCD case coincides with the
Belinfante-Rosenfeld definition
[
41
]
(see also
Appendix E of
[
42
]
),

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\Theta^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{i}{4}\bar{\Psi}\left[\gamma^{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu}\!+\!\gamma^{\nu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu}\right]\Psi-F^{\mu\lambda a}F^{\nu}_{\lambda a}+\tfrac{1}{4}g^{\mu\nu}F^{\sigma\lambda a}F_{\sigma\lambda a}+\Theta_{\rm GF-EOM}^{\mu\nu},

(1)

where in the quantized case one has in addition the gauge-fixing and the
equations-of-motion terms. This object is local, symmetric,

\Theta^{\mu\nu}=\Theta^{\nu\mu}
, conserved
\partial_{\mu}\Theta^{\mu\nu}=0
, and from the Lorentz group can be irreducibly
decomposed as a sum of a traceful and traceless parts. It is a highly singular operator which requires
renormalization. The trace can be written as an anomalous
divergence of the dilatation current,
D^{\mu}=x_{\nu}\Theta^{\mu\nu}
,
related unambiguously to the scale invariance breaking
[
41
]
, namely

\displaystyle\Theta\equiv\Theta^{\mu}_{\mu}=\frac{\beta(\alpha)}{4\alpha}{G^{\mu\nu}}^{2}+[1+\gamma_{m}(\alpha)]\sum_{f}m_{f}\bar{q}_{f}q_{f}.

(2)

Here
\beta(\alpha)=\mu^{2}d\alpha/d\mu^{2}=-\alpha[\beta_{0}(\alpha/4\pi)+{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})]<0
is the QCD beta function with
\beta_{0}=(11N_{c}-2N_{F})/3
,

N_{c}
is the number of colors,
N_{F}
is the number of flavors,

\gamma_{m}(\alpha)=2\alpha/\pi+{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})
is the quark mass anomalous dimension, and
f
enumerates active flavors. One has

\alpha(t)=(4\pi/\beta_{0})/\ln\left(-t/\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}\right)
with
\alpha(t)
real for
t=-Q^{2}<0
. We take
\Lambda_{Q}=225
MeV and
N_{F}=3
in our numerical studies presented later on.

2.3
Raman decomposition

The standard tensor decomposition of the SEM tensor matrix element in the pion state of isospin
a,b
is

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt\langle\pi^{a}(p^{\prime})|\Theta^{\mu\nu}(0)|\pi^{b}(p)\rangle=\delta^{ab}\left[2P^{\mu}P^{\nu}A(q^{2})+\frac{1}{2}(q^{\mu}q^{\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}q^{2})D(q^{2})\right],

(3)

where
P=\tfrac{1}{2}(p^{\prime}+p)
,
q=p^{\prime}-p
, and
A
and
D
are the gravitational form factors. For the electromagnetic current,
J_{Q}^{\mu}=\bar{\Psi}\gamma^{\mu}Q\Psi
, one has

\displaystyle\langle\pi^{\pm}(p^{\prime})|J_{Q}^{\mu}(0)|\pi^{\pm}(p)\rangle=\pm 2P^{\mu}F(q^{2}),

(4)

where
F
is the charge form factor, to which we shall make frequent
references in the context of our discussion of GFFs for comparison purposes.

The proper decomposition into a sum of two separately conserved irreducible tensors of a
well-defined total angular momentum,
J^{PC}=0^{++}
(scalar) and
2^{++}
(tensor), has the form
[
7
]

\displaystyle\Theta^{\mu\nu}=\Theta_{S}^{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{T}^{\mu\nu},\;\;\Theta_{S}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{3}Q^{\mu\nu}\Theta,

(5)

\displaystyle\Theta_{T}^{\mu\nu}=\Theta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{3}Q^{\mu\nu}\Theta=2\left[P^{\mu}P^{\nu}-\frac{P^{2}}{3}Q^{\mu\nu}\right]A,

where
Q^{\mu\nu}\equiv g^{\mu\nu}-{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}/{q^{2}}
(for brevity, we drop here the argument of the GFFs, which is
q^{2}
). This
decomposition implements a separate conservation of the scalar and
tensor parts,
q_{\mu}\Theta_{S}^{\mu\nu}=q_{\mu}\Theta_{T}^{\mu\nu}=0
, unlike the and often used naive decomposition
with
\Theta_{T}^{\mu\nu}=\Theta^{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{4}g^{\mu\nu}\Theta
and

\Theta_{S}^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{4}g^{\mu\nu}\Theta
, which violated the separate
conservation property, since, e.g.,
q_{\mu}\Theta_{S}^{\mu\nu}=q^{\nu}\Theta
.
The Raman separation of
the GFFs, when promoted to the operator level in QCD, also has the
feature that the trace of SEM is singular and requires
renormalization, cf. Eq. (
2
). From this point of view,
\Theta

and
A
, carrying good
J^{PC}
quantum numbers, should be regarded as the basic form
factors
[
36
]
, whereas
D
mixes the quantum numbers and is given as a
secondary quantity (albeit naturally appearing in the mechanistic properties
[
2
,
3
]
) by the combination

\displaystyle D=-\frac{2}{3q^{2}}\left[\Theta-\left(2m_{\pi}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,q^{2}\right)A\right].

(6)

3.1
Preliminary

The transverse densities of hadrons in the infinite momentum frame were advocated

[

,
46
,

,

]

as proper objects relating to the probabilistic interpretation of the

[

49

45
]
. Likewise, the transverse

\Theta^{++}
distribution is also positive definite (for any hadronic state), as we will demonstrate below. Moreover, as

3.2
Light-cone kinematics for plane waves

We take the conventions
p^{\pm}=(p^{0}\pm p^{3})/\sqrt{2}=p_{\mp}
, such that
x\cdot p=p^{+}x^{-}+p^{-}x^{+}-p_{\perp}\cdot x_{\perp}
and
d^{4}p=dp^{+}dp^{-}d^{2}p_{\perp}
. Also,

g^{++}=g^{--}=0
and
g^{+-}=g^{-+}=1
.
Assume the momentum transfer has only the
q_{\perp}
component, i.e.

q^{+}=q^{-}=0
.
The momenta of the pions in the chosen frame (IMBF, infinite momentum Breit frame) are therefore

\displaystyle p^{+}={p^{+}}^{\prime}=P^{+},\;\;p^{-}={p^{-}}^{\prime}=\frac{\tfrac{1}{4}q_{\perp}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}{2{P^{+}}},\;\;p_{\perp}=-{p_{\perp}}^{\prime}=-\tfrac{1}{2}q_{\perp}.

(9)

In the light front quantization (see,
e.g.
,
[
48
]
and references therein),
the states on the mass shell are labeled as
|p^{+},p_{\perp}\rangle
. They fulfill the completeness relation

\displaystyle\int\frac{dp^{+}dp^{-}d^{2}p_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{4}}2\pi\theta(p^{+})\,\delta(2p^{+}p^{-}-p_{\perp}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2})|p^{+},p_{\perp}\rangle\langle p^{+},p_{\perp}|=1,

(10)

corresponding to
p^{-}=(p_{\perp}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2})/2p^{+}
and
p^{+}>0
,
and the invariant normalization

\displaystyle\langle p^{+},p_{\perp}|{p^{+}}^{\prime},{p_{\perp}}^{\prime}\rangle=2p^{+}(2\pi)^{3}\delta({p^{+}}-{p^{+}}^{\prime})\delta^{2}(p_{\perp}-{p_{\perp}}^{\prime}),

(11)

which implies the rule to divide with
\sqrt{2p^{+}}
for the initial and
\sqrt{{2p^{+}}^{\prime}}
for the final state.

The matrix element of the
+
component of the electromagnetic current is

2p^{+}F(q_{\perp}^{2})
, and the factor of
2p^{+}
cancels with the one originating
from the normalization (
11
) (here this is similar to the instant form case). One defines the transverse charge density as

\displaystyle F^{+}(b)=\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}F(q_{\perp}^{2}).

(12)

Note that
F^{+}(b)
corresponds to the
+
component of the current. The
\perp
components of the current vanish identically in IMBF.
For the
-
component of the current, we acquire the factor
P^{-}/P^{+}
in the integrand,
which vanishes in IMBF when
P^{+}\to\infty
.

Next, let us look at the
++
component of Eq. (
3
),

\displaystyle\Theta^{++}(b)=\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{2P^{+}(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}\,2{P^{+}}^{2}A(q_{\perp}^{2})=P^{+}A(b),

(13)

which gives a simple interpretation to
A(b)
as the relative distribution of
P^{+}
in the transverse coordinate space.
Obviously,
\int d^{2}b\,\Theta^{++}(b)=P^{+}
.
For the
+-
component we have

\displaystyle\Theta^{+-}(b)=\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{2P^{+}(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}\left[2{P^{+}}P^{-}A(q_{\perp}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}q_{\perp}^{2}D(q_{\perp}^{2})\right],

(14)

where we have used
g^{+-}=1
. With the kinematics (
9
) we get immediately

\displaystyle\Theta^{+-}(b)=\frac{1}{2P^{+}}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}\left[(m_{\pi}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}q_{\perp}^{2})A(q_{\perp}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}q_{\perp}^{2}D(q_{\perp}^{2})\right].

(15)

The
--
component
is strongly suppressed,
\sim 1/P_{+}^{3}
, and involves only
A
in the chosen frame. The transverse components are

\displaystyle\Theta^{ij}(b)=\frac{1}{2P^{+}}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}\frac{1}{2}\left[q_{\perp}^{i}q_{\perp}^{j}-\delta^{ij}q_{\perp}^{2}\right]D(q_{\perp}^{2})=

\displaystyle~~~~~\delta^{ij}p(b)+\left[\frac{b^{i}b^{j}}{b^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{ij}\right]s(b),

(16)

where
p(b)
is the transverse pressure and
s(b)
denotes the transverse shear forces. The trace GFF is

\displaystyle\Theta^{\mu}_{\mu}(b)=2\Theta^{+-}(b)-\Theta^{11}(b)-\Theta^{22}(b)=

\displaystyle~~~~~~~\frac{1}{2P^{+}}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}\left[2(m_{\pi}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}q_{\perp}^{2})A(q_{\perp}^{2})+\frac{3}{2}q_{\perp}^{2}D(q_{\perp}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle~~=\frac{1}{2P^{+}}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}\Theta(q_{\perp}^{2})=\frac{1}{2P^{+}}\Theta(b).

(17)

The normalizations are
\int d^{2}b\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\mu}(b)=m_{\pi}^{2}/P^{+}
and

\int d^{2}b\,\Theta(b)=2m_{\pi}^{2}
.
In the above formulas the normalization factors of
1/(2P^{+})
factor out of the Fourier
transforms, in contrast to the instant form quantization, where
1/(2P^{0})
=
1/(2\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+q_{\perp}^{2}/4})
remains inside the integral
and largely affects the interpretation, in particular for light hadrons.

3.3
Intrinsic properties vs form factors

From a Quantum Mechanical point of view, intrinsic physical properties
are obtained as expectation values of self-adjoint operators in a
normalizable quantum mechanical state
|\psi\rangle
,
\langle A\rangle_{\psi}=\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle
. However, form factors
by themselves are non-diagonal matrix elements between plane waves.
The use of the light-cone (LC) coordinates has been promoted as a way to provide a
proper definition of intrinsic properties related to form factors, fully compatible with the
probabilistic interpretation.
To establish the connection, instead of the plane waves one considers wave packets, which in
LC coordinates and for the pion read

\displaystyle|\phi\rangle=\int\frac{d^{2}p_{\perp}dp^{+}}{(2\pi)^{3}2p^{+}}\tilde{\phi}(p_{\perp},p^{+})|p_{\perp},p^{+}\rangle.

(18)

From here we have the scalar product

\displaystyle\langle\phi|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{2}p_{\perp}dp^{+}}{(2\pi)^{3}2p^{+}}\tilde{\phi}(p_{\perp},p^{+})^{*}\tilde{\psi}(p_{\perp},p^{+})

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}x_{\perp}dx^{-}\phi(x_{\perp},x^{-})^{*}\psi(x_{\perp},x^{-}).

The coordinate and momentum representations are related via the Fourier transform,

\displaystyle\psi(x_{\perp},x^{-})=\int\frac{d^{2}p_{\perp}dp^{+}}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}2p^{+}}}\tilde{\psi}(p_{\perp},p^{+})e^{i(x_{\perp}\cdot p_{\perp}-p^{+}x^{-})}.

(20)

In
[
44
]
wave packets localized sharply around
p_{z}\to\infty
were considered. An equivalent way
is to integrate over the
x^{-}
coordinate in the local operator, and define
the transverse wave packet distribution in the transverse coordinate
b=x_{\perp}
,

\displaystyle n_{\psi}(b)=\int dx^{-}|\psi(b,x^{-})|^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp^{+}}{4\pi p^{+}}\left|\int\frac{d^{2}p_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{ib\cdot p_{\perp}}\tilde{\psi}(p_{\perp},p^{+})\right|^{2}.

(21)

For local operators, we consider the
x^{+}=0
quantization surface.
Using translational invariance,
O(x)=e^{iP\cdot x}O(0)e^{-iP\cdot x}
, and after some straightforward manipulations, one obtains the intuitive formula for the expectation value of the electromagnetic current
J^{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\int dx^{-}J^{+}(b,x^{-})|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}b^{\prime}n_{\psi}(b-b^{\prime})F(b^{\prime}),

(22)

where
F(b)
is the Fourier transform of the charge form factor in the space-like momentum space,

\displaystyle F(b)=\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}F(-q_{\perp}^{2})e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}.

(23)

Next, we define

\displaystyle n^{+}_{\psi}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dx^{-}\psi^{*}(b,x^{-})i\partial^{+}\psi(b,x^{-})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp^{+}}{4\pi p^{+}}p^{+}\left|\int\frac{d^{2}p_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{ib\cdot p_{\perp}}\tilde{\psi}(p_{\perp},p^{+})\right|^{2}

to obtain, for the quark part
\Theta_{q}^{++}
,

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\int dx^{-}\Theta_{q}^{++}(b,x^{-})|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}b^{\prime}n_{\psi}^{+}(b-b^{\prime})A_{q}(b^{\prime}),

(25)

where

\displaystyle A_{q}(b)=\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}A_{q}(-q_{\perp}^{2})e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}.

(26)

Obviously, for a localized wave packet
n_{\psi}(b)\to\delta^{(2)}(b)
and
n^{+}_{\psi}(b)\to p^{+}\delta^{(2)}(b)
, hence one has

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt\langle\psi|\int dx^{-}J^{+}(b,x^{-})|\psi\rangle\to

\displaystyle F(b),\;\;\;\langle\psi|\int dx^{-}\Theta^{++}(b,x^{-})|\psi\rangle=P^{+}A(b),

(27)

in accordance with Eqs. (
12
) and (
13
).

3.4
Positivity

In QCD, the EM current and SEM in LC
coordinates and with the gauge
A^{+}=0
(which is ghost free), one has

\displaystyle J^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}Q\Psi_{+},

\displaystyle\Theta_{q}^{++}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\left(\Psi^{\dagger}_{+}\partial^{+}\Psi_{+}-\partial^{+}\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\Psi_{+}\right),\;\;\;\Theta_{g}^{++}=(\partial^{+}A^{a}_{\perp})^{2},

\displaystyle\Theta^{++}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta_{q}^{++}+\Theta_{g}^{++}.

(28)

Here
Q
is the electric charge,

\Psi_{\pm}={\cal P}_{\pm}\Psi
, where
{\cal P}_{\pm}=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{\pm}
are orthogonal projection operators satisfying
{\cal P}_{+}+{\cal P}_{-}=1
,
{\cal P}_{\pm}^{2}={\cal P}_{\pm}={\cal P}_{\pm}^{\dagger}
and
{\cal P}_{\pm}{\cal P}_{\mp}=0
, and the
A^{a\mu}
is the gluon field.
We note that the gluon component,
\Theta_{g}^{++}
, is manifestly positive definite. Importantly,
the sum of the quark and gluon parts is renormalization group
invariant.

The field expansion for the quark field in the
transverse coordinate space
[
45
]
at
x^{+}=0
is

\displaystyle q_{+}(b,x^{-})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dp^{+}}{4\pi p^{+}}\sum_{\lambda}

\displaystyle[b_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})u_{\lambda,+}(p^{+})e^{-ip^{+}x^{-}}

(29)

\displaystyle+d_{\lambda}^{\dagger}(b,p^{+})v_{\lambda,+}(p^{+})e^{ip^{+}x^{-}}],

with
b_{\lambda}^{\dagger}(b,p^{+})
and
d_{\lambda}^{\dagger}(b,p^{+})
denoting
the particle and antiparticle creation operators with LC helicity
\lambda
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle\int dx^{-}q_{+}^{+}q_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{dp^{+}}{4\pi p^{+}}\left[n(b,p^{+})-\bar{n}_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})\right],

\displaystyle\int dx^{-}q_{+}^{+}i\partial^{+}q_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{dp^{+}}{4\pi p^{+}}\left[p^{+}n_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})-p^{+}\bar{n}_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})\right],

(30)

with
n_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})=b^{\dagger}_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})b_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})

and
\bar{n}_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})=d^{\dagger}_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})d_{\lambda}(b,p^{+})
denoting
the particle and antiparticle number operators, respectively.
Thus, for
\pi^{+}=u\bar{d}
,

\displaystyle\int dx^{-}J^{+}(b,x^{-})\underbrace{\to}_{\pi^{+}}\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{dp^{+}}{4\pi p^{+}}\left[\frac{2}{3}n_{u,\lambda}(b,p^{+})+\frac{1}{3}n_{\bar{d},\lambda}(b,p^{+})\right],

(31)

since generally
q_{+}^{\dagger}q_{+}
is positive for quarks and negative for antiquarks.
Thus (
31
), and consequently
F(b)
, are positive definite.

For
\Theta_{q}^{++}
one also finds positivity,

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\int dx^{-}\frac{i}{2}\left(\Psi^{\dagger}_{+}\partial^{+}\Psi_{+}-\partial^{+}\Psi_{+}^{\dagger}\Psi_{+}\right)=i\int dx^{-}\Psi^{\dagger}_{+}\partial^{+}\Psi_{+}

(32)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\to}_{\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\int\frac{dp^{+}}{4\pi p^{+}}\left[p^{+}n_{u,\lambda}(b,p^{+})+p^{+}n_{\bar{d},\lambda}(b,p^{+})\right]

To summarize this Section,
both
F(b)
and
A(b)
are
intrinsic
properties of the pion
which are
positive definite
. This allows to interpret
F(b)
as the

transverse charge distribution
, whereas
A(b)
is the
transverse
p^{+}

distribution
in the pion. Note that these two distributions do not

interpretation as a whole depend crucially on the LC kinematics and the
gauge
A^{+}=0
.

having important implications for the
\pi\pi

must be attractive

in the corresponding
J^{PC}

In each of the considered channels,

(33)

where
J_{0}(z)
is a Bessel function.

4
Dispersion relations and sum rules

4.1
General properties

All the considered form factors vanish sufficiently fast at large space-like momenta,
hence satisfy the unsubtracted dispersion relations

\displaystyle f(-Q^{2})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\frac{{\rm Im}f(s)}{s+Q^{2}},

(34)

where
Q^{2}=-q^{2}=-t
is the space-like momentum transfer squared.
The relations make sense, as asymptotically
{\rm Im}\,f(s)
tends to zero sufficiently fast (see the following) and the integrals converge.
For
f=F
or
f=A
, one has the conditions

f(0)=1
and
\lim_{Q^{2}\to\infty}Q^{2}f(Q^{2})=0
, which are
equivalent
to the two sum rules

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\frac{{\rm Im}f(s)}{s}=1,

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\,{\rm Im}f(s)=0.

(36)

For the scalar part of the gravitational form factor, since
\Theta(0)=2m_{\pi}^{2}
, we get the mass sum rule

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\frac{{\rm Im}\Theta(s)}{s}=2m_{\pi}^{2}.

(37)

The once-subtracted form of (
34
) is

\displaystyle\Theta(-Q^{2})=2m_{\pi}^{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\frac{Q^{2}}{s}\frac{{\rm Im}\Theta(s)}{s+Q^{2}},

(38)

where one can immediately see that the condition

\lim_{Q^{2}\to\infty}\Theta(-Q^{2})=0
is equivalent to Eq. (
37
). By a miracle of analyticity, the low energy
condition for
\Theta(0)
translates into the asymptotic vanishing of
\Theta(-Q^{2})
.
For
\Theta
, there is no sum rule of the form of (
36
), since the spectral strength decays too slowly,

{\rm Im}\Theta(s)\sim 1/\ln^{3}s
(see Sec.
4.3
).

The slope sum rules,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{df(t)}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\equiv f^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\frac{{\rm Im}f(s)}{s^{2}}

(39)

have significance when comparing to
\chi
PT and the data. In particular,

\displaystyle\Theta^{\prime}(0)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\frac{{\rm Im}\Theta(s)}{s^{2}}=1+{\cal O}(m_{\pi}^{2}/f_{\pi}^{2}),

(40)

where
f_{\pi}=93
MeV (95 MeV for the lattice
m_{\pi}=170
MeV
[
36
]
).
Combining Eqs. (
37
) and (
39
) for
\Theta
we get

\displaystyle 2m_{\pi}^{2}(1\!-\!2\Theta^{\prime}(0))=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}\!\!\!ds(s-4m_{\pi}^{2})\frac{{\rm Im}\,\Theta(s)}{s^{2}},

(41)

The left-hand side is negative because from
\chi
PT it follows that
\Theta^{\prime}(0)=1+{\cal O}(m_{\pi}^{2}/f_{\pi}^{2})>1/2
,
hence there must be a region in
s
where

{\rm Im}\,\Theta(s)
is negative. Since it is positive at the origin, it must change sign.
Sum rule (
36
) immediately yields that the spectral strengths of
F
and
A
also need to change sign.

4.2
Sum rules and modeling

In modeling form factors in
Q^{2}
, one usually distinguishes three
regions indicated in Fig.
1
: low
Q^{2}
dominated by a threshold expansions and/or

\chi
PT, the high
Q^{2}
where pQCD can be applied, and the
intermediate region dominated resonances. The difficulty of modeling
in
Q^{2}
lies in appropriate smooth matching of various regions in a
way that preserves analyticity. This is a serious obstacle, as any
step functions or their smoothed versions would unavoidably lead to
spurious behavior in the complex
t
plane. Moreover, asymptotic
expansions in space-like momenta are obviously not meant
per se

as analytic functions. An alternative, vastly used to avoid these
problems, is to carry out the modeling in the time-like region, which
amounts to assuming proper physically motivated formulas for
{\rm Im}\,f(s)
along the cut. Here also we have three regions: the
low-
s
range, described with
\chi
PT and extending from the
threshold up to
\Lambda_{\chi}^{2}\sim m_{\rho}^{2}
, the high-
s
range
controlled by pQCD and extending from
\Lambda_{p}^{2}
upwards, and the
intermediate range where resonances dominate. Importantly, we can use
physical time-like scattering data to obtain
{\rm Im}\,f(s)
, which
is practical in the elastic channel thanks to Watson’s final state theorem,
or when the number of open channels is not too large (typically
in the pion case one considers only
\pi\pi-K\bar{K}
coupled channels
[
9
]
.)

According to the dispersion relation (
34
), the spectral strength from
a given range in
s
feeds all the values of
Q^{2}
(or in general, complex
t
), such that analyticity
is guaranteed. In particular, as we will see below, resonances and
\chi
PT provide some strength also to the
1/Q^{2}
tails,
which is globally canceled by the contribution from pQCD, in harmony with the asymptotic sum rule.
The role of the constraints following from the dispersive sum rules of
Sec.
4
on modeling of the spectral strengths has been
little considered.
2
2

2

To our knowledge, the only work which
discusses the issue (for the case of the charge form factor of the
pion) is
[
52
]
.
Of a particular relevance are sum
rules (
36
) and (
37
), which relate to the
asymptotic behavior of form factors in the space-like region, and
provide global (i.e., involving all the values of
s
) constraints. From this
perspective, the large mismatch of the experimental or lattice QCD
space-like form factors with the pQCD asymptotics in the available

Q^{2}
range is due to a missing (negative) strength in the corresponding
spectral density at some sufficiently high

s

[
37
]
. We will explore this issue in the
following parts of this paper.

4.3
Asymptotic consistency

The leading pQCD asymptotic expressions in
Q^{2}
are

\displaystyle f_{p}(-Q^{2})\simeq c_{f}\frac{16\pi f_{\pi}^{2}\alpha(-Q^{2})}{Q^{2}}=c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}Q^{2}\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})},

(42)

\displaystyle\Theta_{p}(-Q^{2})\simeq-4\beta_{0}\alpha(-Q^{2})^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}=-\frac{64\pi^{2}f^{2}}{\beta_{0}\ln^{2}(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})},

(43)

where the subscript
p
indicates pQCD,
\alpha(-Q^{2})=\alpha(t)=4\pi/[\beta_{0}\ln(-t/\Lambda^{2})]
,

\beta_{0}=\tfrac{1}{3}(11N_{c}-2N_{f})=9
with 3 active flavors, and
\Lambda=225
MeV. To treat the
F
and
A
channels uniformly, since here they differ by a factor of 3, we ntroduce the constant
c_{f}
, with
c_{F}=1
and
c_{A}=3
.
Analytic continuation to time-like
s
yields, along the upper edge of the cut,

\alpha(s+i\epsilon)={4\pi}/({\beta_{0}(L-i\pi)})
,
with the short-hand notation
L=\ln(s/\Lambda^{2})
.
Then the spectral densities are

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}\,f_{p}(s)=-c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}s(L^{2}+\pi^{2})},

(44)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}\,\Theta_{p}(s)=-\frac{128\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}L}{\beta_{0}(L^{2}+\pi^{2})^{2}}.

(45)

The formulas are valid at
s>\Lambda_{p}
, a large scale where pQCD sets in.

At first glance, there seems to be a sign clash between Eqs. (
44
) and (
42
), since the kernel
1/(s+Q^{2})

in the dispersion relation (
34
) is positive definite. The resolution is linked to the asymptotic sum rule, as we demonstrate below.
Changing the integration variable
into
L=\ln(s/\Lambda^{2})
, we get

\displaystyle f_{p}(-Q^{2})=-c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}Q^{2}}\int_{L_{0}}^{\infty}dL\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}e^{L}/Q^{2}+1}\frac{1}{L^{2}+\pi^{2}},

(46)

where
L_{0}=\ln(\Lambda_{p}^{2}/\Lambda^{2})
.
At large
Q^{2}
the factor
1/(\Lambda^{2}e^{L}/Q^{2}+1)
approaches the step function
\theta[\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})-L]
, hence

\displaystyle f_{p}(-Q^{2})\simeq-c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}Q^{2}}\int_{L_{0}}^{\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}\frac{dL}{L^{2}+\pi^{2}}=

\displaystyle~~~~-c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}Q^{2}}\left[\arctan(\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})/\pi)-\arctan(L_{0}/\pi)\right]=

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}}\left[\frac{1}{Q^{2}\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}\right]+\frac{d_{f}}{Q^{2}}+\dots,

(47)

where in the last line we have expanded for asymptotic
Q^{2}
. The dots indicate sub-leading terms in the large
Q^{2}
expansion.
Note that the first term reproduces, as a check, Eq. (
42
) with the correct sign,
while the second term is negative, with

\displaystyle d_{f}=c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}}\left[-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(L_{0}/\pi)\right]=-c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{2}{f_{\pi}}^{2}}{{\beta_{0}}{L_{0}}}+\dots,

(48)

where for clarity we have expanded for large
\Lambda_{p}/\Lambda
and the dots mean terms sub-leading in
L_{0}
.
The
d_{f}/Q^{2}
term (dominant over the
1/[Q^{2}\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})]
term) is canceled by other contributions to the asymptotics via the sum rule (
36
).

For
\Theta
we get with a similar calculation

\displaystyle\hskip-34.1433pt\Theta_{p}(-Q^{2})\simeq-\frac{128\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}}\int_{\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!dL\frac{L}{(L^{2}+\pi^{2})^{2}}=-\frac{64\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}\ln^{2}(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}+\dots,

(49)

which agrees as a check with Eq. (
43
). Note that in contrast to the previous
f=F,A
case, for
\Theta
there is no “super-leading” term that needs to be canceled, in accordance to the fact that
there is no asymptotic sum rule of the form of Eq. (
36
) for
\Theta
.

4.4
Elastic region and threshold behavior

In the elastic region, above the two-pion and below the four-pion production threshold,
4m_{\pi}^{2}\leq s\leq 16m_{\pi}^{2}
,
Watson’s theorem states that

\displaystyle f^{I}_{J}(s)=|f^{I}_{J}(s)|e^{i\delta^{I}_{J}(s)},

(50)

where
\delta^{I}_{J}(s)
are the corresponding
\pi\pi
elastic scattering phase shifts in the isospin
I
and spin
J
channels.
This in particular implies that

\displaystyle{\rm Im}f^{I}_{J}(s)=|f^{I}_{J}(s)|\sin\delta^{I}_{J}(s),

(51)

which for attractive interactions with
0\leq\delta^{I}_{J}(s)\leq\pi
is positive. Current analyses are consistent with the
elastic regime, to hold in practice in the extended range
4m_{\pi}^{2}\leq s\leq 4m_{K}^{2}
. Phenomenologically, one has resonance saturation for the three
form factors
f=F,\Theta,A
, for Breit-Wigner masses
\sqrt{s}=m_{R}
,
where
\delta^{I}_{J}(m_{R}^{2})=\pi/2
with
m_{R}=m_{\rho},m_{\sigma},m_{f_{2}}
, respectively. Also,
\delta^{I}_{J}(4m_{K}^{2})<\pi
, such that
positivity holds,

\displaystyle{\rm Im}A(s),{\rm Im}F(s),{\rm Im}\Theta(s)\geq 0\,,\qquad 4m_{\pi}^{2}\leq s\leq 4m_{K}^{2}.

(52)

Parametrization of the leading threshold behavior of the
\pi\!-\!\pi
scattering amplitude

t^{I}_{J}(s)=(e^{2i\delta^{I}_{J}(s)}-1)/\rho(s)
, where
\rho(s)=(1-4m_{\pi}^{2}/s)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, in
the isospin
I
and spin
J
channels is

\displaystyle t^{I}_{J}(s)=a^{I}_{J}(\tfrac{1}{4}s-m_{\pi}^{2})^{J}

(53)

(which is real).
Our convention follows
[
53
]
, such that the combinations
a^{I}_{J}m^{2J}
are dimensionless.
Using Watson’s theorem, one obtains for the corresponding form factors the threshold formula to leading order,

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\,f^{I}_{J}(s)=|f^{I}_{J}(4m_{\pi}^{2})|\,a^{I}_{J}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{\pi}^{2}}{s}}(\tfrac{1}{4}s-m_{\pi}^{2})^{J}(1+{\cal O}(s-4m_{\pi}^{2})).

(54)

4.5
\chi
PT

The NLO expressions from
\chi
PT are
[
9
,
10
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}\,F_{\chi}(s)=\frac{1}{96\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}s\left(1-\frac{4m_{\pi}^{2}}{s}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}},

(55)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}\,\Theta_{\chi}(s)=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}}(2m_{\pi}^{2}+s)(2s-m_{\pi}^{2})\left(1-\frac{4m_{\pi}^{2}}{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(56)

whereas
{\rm Im}\,A_{\chi}(s)
is NNLO in chiral counting, hence
numerically tiny, and we do not include it in the forthcoming model
analysis applied to the lattice data. The formulas are assumed to be
valid up to a scale
\Lambda_{\chi}
.

5
Transverse densities from spectral strengths

With the help of the dispersion relation (
34
) plugged into (
33
), one can write the transverse densities
for all channels in the form
[
54
]
,

\displaystyle f(b)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}dsK_{0}(b\sqrt{s})\,{\rm Im}\,f(s),

(57)

where the order of integration over
s
and
q_{\perp}
has been
flipped, which is allowed since the integrals exist. For the cases
of
F(b)
and
A(b)
, the positivity shown in Sec.
3.4
is a non-trivial condition
since, although
K_{0}>0
, the sum rules for their spectral densities imply that

{\rm Im}F(s)
and
{\rm Im}A(s)

cannot
be positive.

Vector meson dominance for the transverse
F(b)
was exploited
in
[
55
]
. A recent and precise analysis considering time-like Babar data up to
s\leq 9{\rm GeV}^{2}
with a modulus-phase dispersion relation
[
56
]

and consideration of sum rules
[
57
]

allows a confident estimate for
b\geq 0.1{\rm fm}

[
58
]
.

5.1
Behavior at
b\to 0

From Eq. (
57
) we can readily obtain the low-
b
behavior of the
transverse densities for the case of
f=F,A
, expanding
K_{0}(b\sqrt{s})
, where the leading term at low
b
is
-\ln b
.
However, this piece cancels from Eq. (
57
) because of the
asymptotic sum rule (
36
), as derived below.
This generic feature is consistent with the asymptotic behavior of
f(Q^{2})
falling off faster than
1/Q^{2}
.

For the transverse charge density, the singular behavior at the origin was first noticed by Gerry Miller
[
59
]
, who considered a Fourier-Bessel
transform of the asymptotic
1/[Q^{2}\ln(Q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})]
tail. Here we repeat this analysis starting from Eq. (
57
), which
for
b\ll\sqrt{s}
we rewrite in the form

\displaystyle f(b)\simeq c_{f}\frac{64\pi^{3}f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}}\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\Lambda_{p}^{2}}^{1/b^{2}}ds\frac{1}{2}\ln(sb^{2})\frac{1}{s(\ln^{2}(s/\Lambda)+\pi^{2})}=

\displaystyle~~~~~~~c_{f}\frac{49\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}{3\beta_{0}}\left[\ln\ln\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\right)+\frac{\ln(b^{2}\Lambda_{p}^{2})}{\ln(\Lambda_{p}^{2}/\Lambda^{2})}\right].

(58)

The first term (positive singularity) is of the shape found in
[
59
]
, whereas the second term (negative and dominant
over the first term) is canceled by other contributions to
the spectral density to satisfy the asymptotic sum rule (
36
), according to the discussion in subsection
4.3
.
Hence

\displaystyle f(b)\simeq c_{f}\frac{49\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}{3\beta_{0}}\ln\ln\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\right).

(59)

For the scalar transverse density, obtaining the low-
b
limit is more subtle, as the
spectral density is not damped with the
1/s
factor, as was the case of
f=F,A
. Scaling the integration variable by introducing

s=S/b^{2}
, we can write

\displaystyle\Theta(b)=-\frac{64\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}b^{2}}\int_{\Lambda_{p}^{2}b^{2}}^{\infty}dSK_{0}(\sqrt{S})\frac{\ln[S/(\Lambda^{2}b^{2})]}{[\ln^{2}(S/\Lambda^{2}b^{2})]+\pi^{2})^{2}}=

\displaystyle~~~~~~~-\frac{128\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}}\frac{1}{b^{2}\ln^{3}\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\right)}+\dots,

(60)

where we have expanded for
b\to\infty
and used
\int_{0}^{\infty}dS\,K_{0}(\sqrt{S})=2
.
The singularity is negative and integrable with
\int\!d^{2}\,b
, as it should.

5.2
Behavior at
b\to\infty

Generally,
\pi\pi
scattering analyses find that the elastic channel practically
extends up to the
K\bar{K}
threshold, thus

\displaystyle f^{I}_{J}(b)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{4m_{K}^{2}}dsK_{0}(b\sqrt{s})|f^{I}_{J}(s)|\sin\delta^{I}_{J}(s)+{\cal O}(e^{-2m_{K}b}),

(61)

such that for
b\gg 1/(2m_{K})\sim 0.2~{\rm fm}
one has
F(b),\Theta(b),A(b)>0
, since the three cases correspond to attractive
interactions with
0\leq\delta^{I}_{J}(s)\leq\pi
. The very high-
b
behavior of the transverse densities
is dictated by the
s
behavior near the threshold
s=4m_{\pi}^{2}
.
Using Eq. (
54
) in Eq. (
57
) we readily obtain the
asymptotic behavior of the transverse densities at
b\to\infty
,

\displaystyle f^{I}_{J}(b)=m_{\pi}^{2}|f^{I}_{J}(s\!=\!4m_{\pi}^{2})|\frac{(2J+1)!!e^{-2bm_{\pi}}[a^{I}_{J}m^{2J}]}{2^{J+1}\pi(bm_{\pi})^{J+2}}

(62)

For the cases of interest, and using the values of the
a^{I}_{J}
coefficients extracted by the Bern
[
60
]

(upper values in the formula below)
and Madrid-Cracow
[
61
]
(lower values) groups, we can write

\displaystyle F(b)=m_{\pi}^{2}|F(s\!=\!4m_{\pi}^{2})|\frac{3e^{-2bm_{\pi}}}{4\pi(bm_{\pi})^{3}}\left(\begin{cases}0.0379(5)\\
0.0377(13)\end{cases}+{\cal O}[(bm_{\pi})^{-2}]\right),

\displaystyle A(b)=m_{\pi}^{2}|A(s\!=\!4m_{\pi}^{2})|\frac{5e^{-2bm_{\pi}}}{8\pi(bm_{\pi})^{4}}\left(\begin{cases}0.00175(3)\\
0.00178(3)\end{cases}+{\cal O}[(bm_{\pi})^{-2}]\right),

\displaystyle\Theta(b)=m_{\pi}^{2}|\Theta(s\!=\!4m_{\pi}^{2})|\frac{e^{-2bm_{\pi}}}{2\pi(bm_{\pi})^{2}}\left(\begin{cases}0.220(5)\\
0.220(8)\end{cases}+{\cal O}[(bm_{\pi})^{-2}]\right).

(63)

Thus, at
b\to\infty
,
the approach of the above transverse densities to 0 is from above, which reflects the attractive nature of the
\pi\!-\!\pi
interactions in the channels of interest,
manifest in the positivity of
a^{I}_{J}
. Combining (
63
) with Eq. (
58
,
60
) we immediately conclude that
\Theta(b)
must
change sign, whereas the limits are consistent with the positive definiteness of
F(b)
and
A(b)
.

Using the NLO
\chi
PT formulas (
56
) we get, to the leading order in
m_{\pi}^{2}
,

\displaystyle F(b)=\frac{m_{\pi}e^{-2m_{\pi}b}}{32\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}b^{3}}+{\cal O}(b^{-5}),

(64)

\displaystyle\Theta(b)=\frac{21m_{\pi}^{4}e^{-2m_{\pi}b}}{32\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}b^{2}}+{\cal O}(b^{-4}),

(65)

where to this order we take
|F(s\!=\!4m_{\pi}^{2})|=1
and
|\Theta(s\!=\!4m_{\pi}^{2})|=6m_{\pi}^{2}
.

Modeling spectral densities with resonances, pQCD, and
\chi

According to what has been said above, a generic model for spectral densities has the form

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\,f(s)={\rm Im}\,f_{\chi}(s)\theta(\Lambda_{\chi}^{2}-s)+{\rm Im}\,f_{R}(s)+{\rm Im}\,f_{p}(s)\theta(s-\Lambda_{p}^{2}),

(66)

with the
\chi

channel
\chi

\sigma

s
from the threshold up to the

[
62

We do not enter these issues here, but just take Eq. (

to estimate the size of various contributions to the sum rules from Sec.

We take

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\chi}=0.6~{\rm GeV},\;\;\;\Lambda_{p}=3~{\rm GeV},

\chi

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}f(s)=\sum_{i}a_{i}M_{i}^{2}\delta(s-M_{i}^{2}),\;\;\;\;\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}\Theta(s)=\sum_{i}b_{i}M_{i}^{4}\delta(s-M_{i}^{2}),

where

In approximations with just one resonance, which work phenomenologically remarkably well in the description of the data in the available

,

, and
\Theta

,
m_{f_{2}}
, and
m_{\sigma}
, respectively, with corresponding

or

6.1
Model with two resonances

Here we consider a model where a second resonance, to be treated as an effective negative strength, is included in
each channel.

With two resonances (+pQCD +
\chi
PT), for
f=F,A
we get from the sum rules (
35
,
36
)

\displaystyle a_{1}+a_{2}+c_{\rm nr}=1,

\displaystyle{M_{1}^{2}}{a_{1}}+{M_{2}^{2}}{a_{2}}+a_{\rm nr}=0,

(69)

where nr indicates the (known) non-resonant (
\chi
PT + pQCD) contributions.
Both sum rules can obviously be satisfied now,
with the solution

\displaystyle a_{1}=\frac{(1-c_{\rm nr})M_{2}^{2}+a_{\rm nr}}{M_{2}^{2}-M_{1}^{2}},

\displaystyle a_{2}=-\frac{(1-c_{\rm nr})M_{1}^{2}+a_{\rm nr}}{M_{2}^{2}-M_{1}^{2}},

(70)

whereby

\displaystyle f(Q^{2})=\frac{(1-c_{\rm nr})M_{1}^{2}M_{2}^{2}-a_{\rm nr}Q^{2}}{(M_{1}^{2}+Q^{2})(M_{2}^{2}+Q^{2})}+f_{\rm nr}(Q^{2}).

(71)

Since
f(Q^{2})=1
, we have
c_{\rm nr}=f_{\rm nr}(0)
. On the other hand, in the limit of
Q^{2}\to\infty

we find
a_{\rm nr}=\lim_{Q^{2}\to\infty}Q^{2}f_{\rm nr}(Q^{2})
(cf. discussion in Sec.
4.3
).
For the case of
\Theta
we take the mass and slope sum rules,

\displaystyle b_{1}M_{1}^{2}+b_{2}M_{2}^{2}+u_{\rm nr}=2m_{\pi}^{2},

\displaystyle{b_{1}}+{b_{2}}+v_{\rm nr}=S,

(72)

where
S=1+{\cal O}(m_{\pi}^{2}/f_{\pi}^{2})
is the desired slope. The solution is

\displaystyle b_{1}=-\frac{M_{2}^{2}(S-v_{\rm nr})+u_{\rm nr}-2m^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}-M_{1}^{2}},

\displaystyle b_{2}=-\frac{M_{2}^{2}(S-v_{\rm nr})+u_{\rm nr}-2m^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}-M_{1}^{2}},

(73)

hence

\displaystyle\hskip-12.80373pt\Theta(-Q^{2})=\frac{(2m^{2}\!-\!u_{\rm nr})M_{1}^{2}M_{2}^{2}+[(2m^{2}\!-\!u_{\rm nr})(M_{1}^{2}+M_{2}^{2})-(S\!-\!v_{\rm nr})M_{1}^{2}M_{2}^{2}]Q^{2}}{(M_{1}^{2}+Q^{2})(M_{2}^{2}+Q^{2})}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~+\Theta_{\rm nr}(-Q^{2}).

(74)

Near the origin

\displaystyle\Theta(-Q^{2})=2m^{2}-u_{\rm nr}+\Theta_{\rm nr}(0)-[S-v_{\rm nr}+\Theta^{\prime}_{\rm nr}(0)]Q^{2}+{\cal O}(Q^{4})

(75)

(the prime indicates the derivative with respect to
t
), hence
u_{\rm nr}=\Theta_{\rm nr}(0)
and

v_{\rm nr}=\Theta^{\prime}_{\rm nr}(0)
. Asymptotically, the resonance contribution falls off as
1/Q^{2}
, hence the
asymptotics of the full
\Theta
originates from the non-resonant (pQCD) part, as discussed earlier.

We remark that the considered model, although schematic in the sense of placing all the needed negative strength in a single narrow resonance placed far away in
s
, is generic. The same features are expected from more involved modeling, where the negative strength is distributed over an extended region in
s
, or in Regge-type modeling with infinitely many resonances. A problem with more realistic modeling is the multitude of model parameters, not possible to fix with the presently available data, so much freedom/overfitting is left.
We expect that the large negative strength in the spectral density is distributed at high
s
, beyond the range of the presently available time-like data.

6.5
Transverse pressure

Finally, we look at one of the mechanistic properties of the pion, namely, the transverse pressure, obtained from the two
gravitational transverse densities.
From Eq. (
16
), by contracting with
\delta_{ij}
(
i,j=1,2
), one readily finds

\displaystyle 2P^{+}p(b)=-\frac{1}{4}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}q_{\perp}^{2}D(-q_{\perp}^{2})=

\displaystyle~~~~-\frac{1}{6}\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}\left[\Theta(-q_{\perp}^{2})-(2m_{\pi}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,q_{\perp}^{2})A(-q_{\perp}^{2})\right],

(76)

where Eq. (
6
) has been used. With the behavior of
\Theta(-q_{\perp}^{2})
and
A(-q_{\perp}^{2})
near 0 we find that

\int d^{2}b\,p(b)=0
. Also,
2P^{+}\int d^{2}b\,p(b)=\tfrac{1}{4}D(0)
, which is the transverse version of the relation given in
[
3
]
.
From Eq. (
76
) and the
b
-representations of the form factors we find

\displaystyle 2P^{+}p(b)=-\frac{1}{6}\Theta(b)+\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}}{3}A(b)-\frac{1}{12}A_{1}(b),

(77)

where

\displaystyle A_{1}(b)=-\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}q_{\perp}^{2}A(-q_{\perp}^{2})=\int\frac{d^{2}q_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{-iq_{\perp}\cdot b}\times

(78)

\displaystyle~~~\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\left[\frac{s}{s+q_{\perp}^{2}}-1\right]{\rm Im}\,A(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}^{\infty}ds\,K_{0}(b\sqrt{s})\,s\,{\rm Im}\,A(s),

where the term with -1 in the square bracket (that would yield a singular
contribution proportional to
\delta^{2}(b)
) cancels thanks to the asymptotic sum

Derivation as for Eq. (
60
) gives at low

\displaystyle A_{1}(b)=-\frac{196\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}{\beta_{0}}\frac{1}{b^{2}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\right)}+\dots,

(79)

which is one power of the log stronger

\Theta(b)
of Eq. (

tends to positive infinity

\displaystyle 2\pi p(b)=\frac{49\pi f_{\pi}^{2}}{3\beta_{0}}\frac{1}{b^{2}\ln^{2}\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\right)}+{\cal O}[1/(b^{2}\ln^{3}b)]

On the other end, at

In this contribution we have reviewed some general features of the
gravitational form factors and the related transverse densities of the
pion. We have used analyticity and the available information from
\pi-\pi
scattering
and pQCD to draw general conclusions on the behavior of these quantities.
In particular, the scalar gravitational transverse density (related to the trace anomaly) must
change sign as a function of the transverse coordinate
b
. On the other hand, the tensor
gravitational transverse density (similarly to the electromagnetic charge case) is positive definite for all values of
b
,
as deduced in the light-front quantization framework in the
A^{+}=0
gauge. This positivity feature allows for a probabilistic interpretation.

The basic properties of the spectral densities, form factors for space-like momenta, and the transverse densities are collected
in Table
2
, with references to the explicit formulas in the text. The signs of the low- and high values of the arguments are indicated.
For the spectral densities, the “low” limit means the behavior right from the
2\pi
production threshold, while “high” means the asymptotic limit.
For the other cases “low” means at zero.

We have also discussed the implications of the recent MIT lattice QCD analysis of the
pion GFFs in the space-like region for
0<Q^{2}<2~{\rm GeV}^{2}
, which roughly maps into
the
b>0.1~{\rm fm}
region, and show that the data can be well described within the meson dominance approach.
While a single resonance per channel suffices,
to satisfy the sum rules following from the short-distance constraints of pQCD (the large-
Q^{2}
behavior), we have added
the needed negative contribution to the spectral densities in the form of a delta function and argued it has to appear at sufficiently high
s
,
at least a few GeV
2
, not to spoil the agreement with the lattice data.

Infinite-time ruin probability of a multivariate renewal risk model with Brownian perturbations
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides

Abstract
. We consider the multivariate risk model with common renewal process among the lines of business, and Brownian perturbations. Assuming that the integrated tail distribution of claims is multivariate subexponential, we establish an asymptotic relation for the infinite-time ruin probability. A more explicit expression is given in case of claim distribution from multivariate regular variation. The results indicate the insensitivity of the asymptotic behavior of the ruin probability with respect to Brownian perturbations. Furthermore, we show that a multivariate distribution with finite expectation, that belongs to the class of multivariate dominatedly varying and long-tailed distributions, possesses integrated tail distribution from the class of multivariate subexponential distributions, which makes easier the checking of conditions in the theorem.



Keywords:
Ruin probability; Integrated tail; Multivariate subexponentiality; Multivariate regular variation; Brownian motion;

Mathematics Subject Classification
: Primary 62P05; Secondary 91G05; 91B05

In this paper, we consider an insurer who operates
d

Let us assume that the claim vectors
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

\displaystyle N(t)=\sup\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\;:\;\tau_{n}\leq t\}\,,

Hereafter, we suppose that

Further, we suppose that the insurer charges premiums from the
d

d

\displaystyle{\bf U}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{1}(t)\\
\vdots\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b_{1}\\

1

\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

2.
Preliminiaries

2.1.
Multivariate heavy-tailed distributions

Before introduction of the multivariate, heavy-tailed distribution classes, we need some notational conventions. At first, all the limit relations hold as
x\to\infty
. For two real numbers
a,\,b
, we denote by
a\wedge b:=\min\{a,\,b\}
. All the vectors are denoted either by Latin bold script or arrowed Greek script and their dimension is equal to
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For two vectors
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{d})
,
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\,\ldots,\,y_{d})
, and a positive quantity
\kappa>0
, we denote as usual
{\bf x}\pm{\bf y}=(x_{1}\pm y_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{d}\pm y_{d})
and
\kappa\,x=(\kappa\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,\kappa\,x_{d})
, with
{\bf x}^{T}
we denote the inverse of
{\bf x}
, while with
{\bf 0}
we denote the origin of the axes. For any set
{\mathbb{B}}
from the vector space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}=(-\infty,\,\infty)^{d}
, we denote by
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}}
its closed hull, by
\partial{\mathbb{B}}
its border and by
{\mathbb{B}}^{c}
its complement. We notice that the set
{\mathbb{B}}
is called increasing, if for any
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{B}}
and
{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}:=[0,\,\infty)^{d}
, it holds
{\bf x}+{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{B}}
. For two positive
d
-variate functions
{\bf f}
,
{\bf g}
, and some set
{\mathbb{B}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, we denote
{\bf f}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})\sim c\,{\bf g}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})
, for some constant
c>0
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{{\bf f}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}{{\bf g}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}=c\,.

For a uni-variate distribution
V
, we denote by
\overline{V}
its tail, namely it holds
\overline{V}(x)=1-V(x)
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we denote by
V^{n*}
the
n
-fold convolution of
V
with itself.

Let us consider now the multivariate, heavy-tailed distribution classes. Initially, we give these classes only for random vectors with distribution
F
, whose support is on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
. For the definition of the multivariate subexponentiality, in
[
29
]
was used the following fundamental set family:

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}=\left\{A\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;A\;{\text{o}pen}\,,\;{\text{i}ncreasing}\,,\;A^{c}\;{\text{c}onvex}\,,\;{\bf 0}\notin\overline{A}\right\}

Let us observe that the
\mathscr{R}
represents a cone with respect to multiplication with positive constants, namely if
A\in\mathscr{R}
and
k>0
, then it holds
k\,A\in\mathscr{R}
. Thus, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, was proved that if
{\bf X}
is a random vector with distribution
F
, then
the random variable

\displaystyle Y_{A}:=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf X}\in u\,A\}

has proper distribution
F_{A}
, whose tail is given by relation

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,A)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{T}\,{\bf X}>x\right)\,,\;x>0

for some index set
I_{A}\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, see in
[
29
, Lemmas 4.3, 4.5]
for more details.

Therefore, for some fixed set
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we say that
F
belongs to class of multivariate subepxonential distributions, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
, that means for any (or, equivalently, for some) integer
n\geq 2
it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{F^{n*}_{A}}(x)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}=n\,.

Along the same line, in
[
19
]
was introduced the distribution class
\mathcal{L}_{A}
, of the multivariate distribution with long tail on
A
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{L}
, namely for any (or, equivalently, for some)
y>0
it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}(x-y)}{\overline{F}_{A}(x)}=1\,.

We denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{B}_{A}
, with
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{L}\}
the classes of multivariate subexponential and multivariate long-tailed distributions, respectively. In the following proposition we reformulate some known results for the classes
\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
\mathcal{L}_{A}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set,
F
and
G
be distributions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
, and

\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{L}\}
. Then we have

(i)

\mathcal{S}_{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{A}
.

(ii)

If
F\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, and for some
c\in(0,\,\infty)
it holds
F(x\,A)\sim c\,G(x\,A)
, then

G\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
.

(iii)

If
F\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, then for any
{\bf a}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
it holds

F(x\,A+{\bf a})\sim F(x\,A)
.

Assertion
(i)
of this proposition is direct consequence of the definitions of

\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
\mathcal{L}_{A}
and the fact that
\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}
, see for example in
[
6
, Lemma 2]
. Further, Assertion
(ii)
is implied by
[
29
, Proposition 4.12(a)]
, for the class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
and by
[
19
, Proposition 2.2(3)]
. Finally, Assertion
(iii)
can be found in
[
19
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Finally we present the class of multivariate, regularly varying distributions, symbolically
MRV
, in its standard version. We recall that for a uni-variate distribution
V
, we say that it belongs to class of multivariate regularly varying distributions, with variation index
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, symbolically
\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, if

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V}(t\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}=t^{-\alpha}

for any
t>0
. For a random vector
{\bf X}
with distribution
F
, we say that
it belongs to (standard)
MRV
, if there exists uni-variate distribution
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, and some Radon measure
\mu
, non-degenerate to zero, such that the limit

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{1}{\overline{V}(x)}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,{\mathbb{B}})=\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

(2.1)

for any Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\in[0,\,\infty]^{d}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, which is such that

\mu(\partial{\mathbb{B}})=0
. In this case, we denote
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
. So, if the class
MRV
represents the union of all
MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
from
[
29
, Proposition 4.14]
in combination with Proposition
2.1
(i) we find that

\displaystyle MRV\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}\,,

(2.2)

and relation (
2.2
) remains intact for the
\mathcal{B}_{A}
, with
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{L}\}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
.

The
MRV
distributions, especially in the standard form, are the most popular
multivariate heavy-tailed distributions and they have found a wide spectrum of applications in probability theory, see in
[
27
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
28
]
anong others. However, the regular variation of the marginal distributions, in presence of
MRV
at claim vectors, restricts the actuarial applications and eventually leads to overpricing of the premiums, when the portfolios follow distribution with moderate heavy tails, which are the most common in insurance practice.

In
[
18
]
can be found a variety of general, distribution examples, that belong in class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
but not in class
MRV
. Thus, the study of risk models with claim vectors distributions (or integrated tail distributions) from class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
, does not offer only mathematical generalization, due to (
2.2
), but have impact to the actuarial practice, since it contains a significant multitude of distributions, that do not belong to
MRV
.

2.2.
Ruin probabilities

In multivariate risk models, the ruin probability can be defined by several ways,
since the sets in which can enter the insurer’s surplus and to provoke economic
instabilities for the company, are of several kinds. We refer the reader to
[
5
]
and
[
25
]
for more discussions about the different definitions of the ruin probabilities. Here, we use the following assumption for the ruin sets, introduced in
[
29
]
.

Assumption 2.1
.

Let
L
be some set, that is open, with
L^{c}
convex,
-L
increasing,
{\bf 0}\in\partial L
and it holds

x\,L=L
, for any
x>0
.

Remark 2.1
.

For any set
L
, as described in Assumption
2.1
, it holds

\displaystyle A:=({\bf b}-L)\in\mathscr{R}\,,

which indicates the immediate relation of the multivariate distribution classes with
the ruin probability. The following two ruin sets
L
, are very useful in actuarial practice

\displaystyle L_{1}:=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}<0\right\}\,,\qquad L_{2}:=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;y_{i}<0\,,\;\exists\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\;\right\}\,.

Hereafter, the set
L
satisfies Assumption
2.1
.
We denote by
{\bf U}_{0}(t)
the surplus in (
1
) for
\vec{\delta}={\bf 0}
,
only in the present subsection. In
[
29
]
, was examined the
ruin probability for
{\bf U}_{0}(t)
, in a renewal risk model, under Assumption
2.1
, and in this case the ruin probability obtains the form

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf b},\,L}^{o}(x):=\mathbf{P}({\bf U}_{0}(t)\in L\,,\;t>0)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}{\bf X}^{(i)}-t\,{\bf p}\in x\,{\bf b}-L\,,\;\exists\;t>0\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}-\theta_{i}\,{\bf p}\in x\,A\,,\;\exists\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf Z}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;\exists\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right)\,,

with
\left\{{\bf Z}^{(i)}:={\bf X}^{(i)}-\theta_{i}\,{\bf p}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
, which is sequence of i.i.d. copies of
{\bf Z}
, since by Assumption
2.1

we have renewal counting process. We find there also the assumption that
\mathbf{E}[{\bf X}]<\overrightarrow{\infty}
, namely
\mathbf{E}\left[X_{j}\right]<\infty
, for any
j=1,\,\ldots,\,d
. To avoid trivial cases, was accepted the assumption of net profit condition

\displaystyle{\bf c}=-\mathbf{E}[{\bf Z}]>{\bf 0}\,,

and for the integrated tail distribution
F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, where

\displaystyle F_{I}(x\,A):=\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x\,A+v\,{\bf c})\,dv\,,

with

\displaystyle\Theta:=\int_{0}^{\infty}F([0,\,\infty)^{d}+v\,{\bf c})\,dv\,,

where
\Theta\in(0,\,\infty)
, since
{\bf 0}<\mathbf{E}[{\bf X}]<\overrightarrow{\infty}
. Hence, under these conditions was found

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf b},\,L}^{o}(x)\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x\,A+v\,{\bf c})\,dv=:H(x)\,.

In this paper, we examine also the case
\vec{\delta}>{\bf 0}
. In risk model (
1
)
the ruin probability is reduced to

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf b},\,L}(x)=\mathbf{P}({\bf U}(t)\in L\,,\;\exists\;t>0)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}{\bf X}^{(i)}-t\,{\bf p}-\vec{\delta}\odot{\bf B}(t)\in x\,{\bf b}-L\right)

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}-\theta_{i}\,{\bf p}-\vec{\delta}\odot{\bf B}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;\exists\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf W}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;\exists\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right)\,,

where
\left\{{\bf B}^{(i)}:={\bf B}-{\bf B}(\tau_{i-1})\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
represents a sequence of i.i.d. copies of
{\bf B}
, since the process
\{{\bf B}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is a Brownian motion (with independent increments) and the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is a renewal one, while these two sources of randomness are independent each other. Further, we denote by
\left\{{\bf W}^{(i)}:={\bf Z}^{(i)}-\vec{\delta}\odot{\bf B}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
, which also represents a sequence of i.i.d. copies of
{\bf W}
. Due to the fact that the multivariate Brownian motion
\{{\bf B}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
has nonnegative expectation of each component, we obtain that to avoid the trivial case
\psi_{{\bf b},\,L}(x)=1
, in relation (
2.3
), it should be satisfied a new net profit condition

\displaystyle{\bf c}^{*}=-\mathbf{E}[{\bf W}]={\bf c}+\vec{\delta}\odot\vec{\mu}>{\bf 0}\,,

(2.4)

where
\vec{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\,\ldots,\,\mu_{d})=(\mathbf{E}[B_{1}],\,\ldots,\,\mathbf{E}[B_{d}])
, with
{\bf B}=(B_{1},\,\ldots,\,B_{d})
. From relation (
2.4
) we can easily see that if a pair
(\mu_{i},\,\delta_{i})
, with
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
has positive components, then the net profit condition, relaxes the relation with
\vec{\delta}={\bf 0}
case, in the sense that it permits to insurer to reduce the premiums a the
i
-line of business.

Finally, we need also the function

\displaystyle F_{I}^{*}(x\,A)=\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{*}}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv\,,

(2.5)

with

\displaystyle\Theta^{*}=\int_{0}^{\infty}F([0,\,\infty)^{d}+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv\in(0,\,\infty)

for the preliminary lemmas of the main result. Let us note that the condition
\mathbf{E}[{\bf X}]<\overrightarrow{\infty}
remains.

3.
Main result

Here we provide the main result together with its proof.

3.1.
Insensitivity of ruin probability

We remind that, in the following theorem the ruin set
L
, satisfies Assumption
2.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A=({\bf b}-L)\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. We consider the risk
model (
1
) under the Assumption
1.1
; if
\mathbf{E}[{\bf X}]<\overrightarrow{\infty}
,

F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, and relation (
2.4
) is true, then we have

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf b},\,L}(x)\sim H(x)\,.

(3.1)

Remark 3.1
.

Theorem
3.1
from one hand side generalizes the
[
29
, Theorem 5.2]
, since it permits also the case
\vec{\delta}>{\bf 0}
, and from the other hand side it shows that the asymptotic behavior of the ruin probability remains unchanged, either with or without the presence of the Brownian motion. This last is referred as ’insensitivity of ruin probability with respect to Brownian perturbations’. Furthermore, if instead of condition
F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, we require
F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
, then relation (
3.1
) is true for all the ruin sets
L
, that satisfy Assumption
2.1
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the uni-variate case, where
A^{*}=(1,\,\infty)
,
b^{*}=1
and
L^{*}=(-\infty,\,0)

relation (
3.1
) is reduced into

\displaystyle\psi_{b^{*},\,L^{*}}(x)\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{F}(x+v\,c)\,dv=\dfrac{1}{c}\int_{x}^{\infty}\overline{F}(v)\,dv\,.

(3.2)

Relation (
3.2
) represents a generalization of
[
30
, Theorem 1]
,
where was considered model (
1
) where the process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is Poisson,
with intensity
\lambda>0
, and the Brownian motion has strictly zero expectation. From (
3.2
)
in case of Poisson process and recalling that
c=\mathbf{E}[Z]
, we reach to the formula

\displaystyle\psi_{b^{*},\,L^{*}}(x)\sim\dfrac{1}{\lambda\,p-\mathbf{E}[X]}\int_{x}^{\infty}\overline{F}(v)\,dv\,,

which coincides with that of
[
30
, Theorem 1]
.

Finally we present a corollary, that is implied by Theorem
3.1
, if we require
that
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha\in(1,\,\infty)
. It is worth to notice that
that if
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha\in(1,\,\infty)
, then we have

F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
, which follows easily from the line of proof in

[

, Proposition 5.4]
. However, we give a more general example in

{\bf X}
has some degenerate components, we suppose that
\mu({\bf x}\;:\;x_{i}>0)>0
, for any

. The following result presents a more explicit asymptotic expression in comparison with (

) and generalizes
[

. The proof of this corollary follows a similar line with that of

Corollary 3.1

Let
L

. We consider risk model (
1
)
under Assumption

and the conditions

, with

,
and (

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf b},\,L}(x)\sim x\,\overline{V}(x)\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu({\bf b}-L+v\,{\bf c})\,dv\,.

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Before the proof of the main result, we need two preliminary lemmas. Let us remind

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. If
F_{I}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, then we find

\displaystyle F_{I}^{*}(x\,A)\sim\dfrac{\Theta}{\Theta^{*}}\,F_{I}(x\,A)\,.

(3.3)

Proof.

From the one hand side, since
\vec{\delta},\,\vec{\mu}\geq{\bf 0}
, and the set
A

is increasing, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{I}^{*}(x\,A)=\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{*}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}+v\,\vec{\delta}\odot\vec{\mu})\,dv

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\Theta}{\Theta^{*}}\,\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c})\,dv=\dfrac{\Theta}{\Theta^{*}}\,F_{I}(x\,A)\,,

that provides the desired upper bound for relation (
3.3
).

For the lower bound, from
[
29
, Lemma 4.3(d)]
we can find
some
u=u(v)\in(0,\,x)
, such that it holds

\displaystyle F_{I}^{*}(x\,A)=\dfrac{1}{\Theta^{*}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}+v\,\vec{\delta}\odot\vec{\mu})\,dv

(3.5)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\Theta}{\Theta^{*}}\,\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in(x+u)\,A+v\,{\bf c})\,dv\sim\dfrac{\Theta}{\Theta^{*}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c})\,dv\,,

where at the next-to-last step we used the fact that
F_{I}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Relation (
3.3
) implies that if
F_{I}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, with
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{L}\}
, then in combination with Proposition
2.1
(ii), we have
F_{I}^{*}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
. Lemma
3.1
is a crucial step in simplification of the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

For next lemma we need the following function

\displaystyle H(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv\,.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. If
F_{I}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, then we have

\displaystyle H^{*}(x)\sim H(x)\,.

(3.6)

Proof.

The upper bound in relation (
3.6
), follows by similar arguments with that in
relation (
3.4
).

For the lower bound in relation (
3.6
), we use the similar line
with that in relation relation (
3.5
) in combination with the fact
that
H\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, which follows by the inclusion
F_{I}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
,
and that the
F_{I}
and
H
are strongly equivalent, see Proposition
2.1
(ii).
∎

We can now present the proof of the main result.

Proof of Theorem
3.1

Following similar line with the proof of
[
29
, Theorem 5.2]
, taking into account Lemma
3.1
, we easily find that

\displaystyle\psi_{{\bf b},\,L}(x)\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf W}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv\,.

(3.7)

Now, we shall show that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf W}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv\sim H^{*}(x)\,.

(3.8)

We notice that through similar steps of relation
[
29
, Eq. (5.7)]
we can verify that

\displaystyle I(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv\sim H^{*}(x)\,.

(3.9)

From the fact that
F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, and through Lemma
3.2
we find

\displaystyle F_{I}(x\,A)\sim\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\,H^{*}(x\,A)\,,

therefore, by Proposition
2.1
(ii) follows that
H^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, and by relation (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle I(x)\in\mathcal{S}_{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{A}\,.

Next, we estimate the upper bound of relation (
3.8
). Let

\displaystyle\overline{M}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq d}|\delta_{j}\,B_{j}|

be a random variable. Since the set
A
is increasing, and through
[
29
, Lemma 4.3(d)]
, for some
u\in(0,\,x)
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf W}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}-\vec{\delta}\odot{\bf B}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}-\overline{M}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*}+y)\,\mathbf{P}(\overline{M}\in dy)\,dv

(3.10)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in(x+u)\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*}+y)\,\mathbf{P}(\overline{M}\in dy)\,dv

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv\sim H^{*}(x)\,,

where at the next-to-last step we used the property of class
\mathcal{L}_{A}
, while at the last step we considered relation (
3.9
). The previous relation renters the desired upper bound for (
3.8
).

Further, we deal with the lower bound of relation (
3.8
). Let

\displaystyle\underline{M}:=\left(\min_{1\leq j\leq d}\delta_{j}\,B_{j}\right)\bigwedge 0

be a non-positive random variable. Following similar symmetric steps with (
3.2
) we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf W}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}-\vec{\delta}\odot{\bf B}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}-\underline{M}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}+y\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,\mathbf{P}(-\underline{M}\in dy)\,dv\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in(x-u)\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,\mathbf{P}(-\underline{M}\in dy)\,dv\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}^{*})\,dv\sim H^{*}(x).

Hence, (
3.8
) is true. Combining (
3.7
) with (
3.8
) and (
3.6
), we get relation (
3.1
).
∎

4.
Integrated tails in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}

In this section we show that if a distribution
F
belongs to a concrete distribution
class, then for the integrated tail distribution the relation
F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
is true.
So, we obtain a useful tool for the control of the condition in Theorem
3.1
.

Let start with the class
\mathcal{D}
, of dominatedly varying distributions.
A uni-variate distribution
V
, is such that
V\in\mathcal{D}
, if for any
(or, equivalently, for some)
b\in(0,\,1)
it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{V}(b\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}<\infty\,.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}\equiv\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
, see
[
12
]
, and further we find

\displaystyle\bigcup_{0<\alpha<\infty}\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}\,,

(4.1)

see in
[
21
, Chapter 2]
for more details. Thus,
in
[
19
]
were introduced the corresponding multivariate versions of
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
. For a set
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we say that
F\in\mathcal{D}_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{D}
, and further that
F\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}
, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
. Let us denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{D}_{A}\,,\qquad(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}\,.

By
[
19
, Proposition 2.1]
and relations (
2.2
) (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle MRV\subsetneq(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}\,.

(4.2)

Following the line of proof of
[
29
, Proposition 5.4]
, we find that
if
F\in MRV
, with index
\alpha>1
(to secure a finite expectation), it implies that

F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
.

The following result shows that if
F\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}
, with

\mathbf{E}[{\bf X}]<\overrightarrow{\infty}
. Then we obtain
F_{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
,
and hence we get weaker sufficient conditions for the property of multivariate subexponentiality
of the integrated tail distribution.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\bf X}
be nonnegative random vector with distribution
F\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}

and
\mathbf{E}[{\bf X}]<\overrightarrow{\infty}
. Then it holds
F_{I}\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
. We can write for the integrated tail distribution

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{I,A}(x)=F_{I}(x\,A)=\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x\,A+v\,{\bf c})\,dv\,.

(4.3)

Hence, it is enough to show that
F_{I,A}\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
.

Let deal first with class
\mathcal{L}
. Let
A^{*}:=A+v\,{\bf c}
, from where, since
A\in\mathscr{R}
, it follows
A^{*}\in\mathscr{R}
. Indeed, by
[
29
, Lemma 4.3]
, we find that there exists a
u\in(0,\,1)
such that it holds
A^{*}\subsetneq(1-u)\,A
, with the right member of this inclusion to belong in
\mathscr{R}
, since
R
is closed with respect to positive scalar multiplication. Let us consider some
y>0
, then we obtain

\displaystyle 1\leq\lim\dfrac{\overline{F}_{I,A}(x-y)}{\overline{F}_{I,A}(x)}=\lim\dfrac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in(x-y)\,A+v\,{\bf c}\right)\,dv}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}\right)\,dv}

\displaystyle=\lim\dfrac{x\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in(x-y)\,\left(A+\dfrac{x}{x-y}\,v\,{\bf c}\right)\,\right)\,dv}{x\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,(A+v\,{\bf c})\,\right)\,dv}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq\lim\dfrac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in(x-y)\,A^{*}\,\right)\,dv}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A^{*}\right)\,dv}=\lim\dfrac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A^{*}\,\right)\,dv}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A^{*}\right)\,dv}=1\,,

where at the third step we made change of variables via
z=x/v
, while at the next-to-last
step we used the fact that
F\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}
. Hence
F_{I,A}\in\mathcal{L}
, thus we obtain
F_{I}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
.

Now, we continue to show that
F_{I,A}\in\mathcal{D}
. Let
b\in(0,\,1)
, then it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F}_{I,A}(b\,x)}{\overline{F}_{I,A}(x)}=\limsup\dfrac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in b\,x\,A+v\,{\bf c}\right)\,dv}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A+v\,{\bf c}\right)\,dv}

\displaystyle=\limsup\dfrac{x\,b\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in b\,x\,A+b\,x\,v\,{\bf c}\right)\,dv}{x\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,(A+v\,{\bf c})\,\right)\,dv}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=b\,\limsup\dfrac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in b\,x\,A^{*}\right)\,dv}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A^{*}\right)\,dv}\leq b\,C\,\limsup\dfrac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A^{*}\,\right)\,dv}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A^{*}\right)\,dv}=b\,C<\infty\,,

where at the second step we made change of variables
z=b\,x/v
on the numerator, and

z=x/v
on the denominator, while at the fourht step the constant
C\in[1,\,\infty)
is implied
by the fact that
F\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathscr{R}}
. Therefore, from relation (
4
) we obtain

F_{I,A}\in\mathcal{D}
, that means
F_{I}\in\mathcal{D}_{A}
. In combination with the previous remarks
we have
F_{I}\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}
, and due to arbitrariness in the choice of
A\in\mathscr{R}
,
it follows that
F_{I}\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

The question about what distributions
F
are such that the integrated tail distribution
F_{I}
are (multivariate) subexponential, has both theoretical and practical interest. In practice it is much easier to check the distribution class of
F
, in comparison with that of
F_{I}
. In case of uni-variate distribution this question was examined by several papers, see
[
9
]
and
[
15
]
for more discussions.

We close this section providing a simple example of distributions that belong to

(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}
, which is inspired by
[
29
, Example 4.17]
, see also
[
18
, Example 4.6]
. Let us notice that in this example
Y_{A}
follows distribution
F_{A}
, as in previous text.

Example 4.1
.

Let
Z
be a random variable with distribution
G
, whose tail is given as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{G}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}>x\;\big|\;\dfrac{{\bf X}}{\|{\bf X}\|}\in(\theta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\theta_{d})\,\right)

with
(\theta_{1},\,\ldots,\,\theta_{d})\in\Delta_{j}
, where
\Delta_{d}
represents a unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
\|\cdot\|
is the
L_{1}
-norm. Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
, some fixed set and we introduce the random variable

\displaystyle W:=\inf\left\{u>0\;:\;u\,\dfrac{{\bf X}}{\|{\bf X}\|}\in A\right\}\,.

If
Z,\,W
are independent, we define the following random variable

\displaystyle Y_{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}Z\,W^{-1}\,,

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}
is the equality in distribution. Let
G\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
. Then,
since the
W
is bounded away from zero, the
W^{-1}
is bounded from above. So from

[
7
, Theorem 2.2(iii), 3.3(ii)]
we obtain

F_{A}\in\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
. Thus, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we find

F\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}
, which implies
F\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}
. ∎

Another example with distribution
F\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}
can be found in

[
29
, Proposition 4.15]
.

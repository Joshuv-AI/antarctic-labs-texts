Calibration and Model Validation

In this section, we describe the efficiency corrections applied to the signal and
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
simulations, as well as the calibration and validation of the
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}

2.1
Efficiency and Multiplicity Corrections

The reconstruction efficiency of the hadronic FEI algorithm is initially estimated from simulation and corrected using two data-driven control samples: inclusive semileptonic decays (
B^{+}\rightarrow X\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
) and hadronic decays (
B^{+}\rightarrow\overline{D}^{0(*)}\pi^{+}
). Correction factors, ranging from 0.6 to 1.1, are derived by comparing yields in data and simulation, and depend on the
B_{\text{tag}}
decay mode.

Additional corrections account for differences in particle identification between data and simulation. These are obtained from control samples including leptons from
{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}
decays and low-multiplicity processes, as well as pions from
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle S}
,
D^{*+}
, and
\Lambda
decays. Corrections depend on the momentum and polar angle of the track.

Simulation accurately models the
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
shape within each
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}
bin but underestimates or overestimates the overall multiplicity distribution. A bin-by-bin reweighting is applied to correct the
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}
spectrum using different control samples.
Correction factors range from 0.8 to 1.2. The reweighting is validated in several control regions, including high
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
, low
M_{\text{bc}}
, low
M_{\mathrm{miss}}^{2}
(leptonic channels), and low
P_{\text{c}and}
(hadronic channels) sidebands.

3
Signal Extraction

To discriminate signal from background, we exploit the
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
and
M_{\mathrm{miss}}^{2}
variables. Signal events are characterized by low
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
and large
M_{\mathrm{miss}}^{2}
values, whereas background events typically exhibit higher
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
and lower
M_{\mathrm{miss}}^{2}
. To capture these behaviors and their correlation, we construct a two-dimensional binned probability density function (PDF) of
E_{\text{ECL}}^{\text{extra}}
and
M_{\mathrm{miss}}^{2}
.

The fit includes five floating parameters: the common branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau})
and the total background yield
n_{b,k}
for each
\tau^{+}
decay mode
k=e^{+},\mu^{+},\pi^{+},\rho^{+}
. The signal yields
n_{s,k}
are not free parameters, but are derived from
\mathcal{B}(B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau})
and fixed quantities as:

n_{s,k}=2n_{B^{+}B^{-}}\times\epsilon_{k}\times\mathcal{B}(B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}),

(2)

where
n_{B^{+}B^{-}}=n_{\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}}f^{+-}

f^{+-}=0.5113^{+0.0073}_{-0.0108}
is the branching fraction
\mathcal{B}(\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}\rightarrow B^{+}B^{-})
, taken from Ref.
[
4
]

\epsilon_{k}
is the probability to reconstruct a
B^{+}\rightarrow\tau^{+}\nu_{\tau}
event in category
k

\tau^{+}
decay modes. It includes the
\tau^{+}

We perform both a simultaneous fit over all four
\tau^{+}

94\pm 31

(1.24\pm 0.41)\times 10^{-4}

4
Systematic Uncertainties

The main sources of systematic uncertainty affecting the measurement are described in the following section.

The largest contribution comes from limited simulation statistics, which result in a 13.3% uncertainty. This is estimated by fluctuating the bin contents of the two-dimensional histogram PDFs 200 times according to Poisson statistics and evaluating the spread of the resulting fitted branching fractions.

Corrections applied to the fit variable PDFs, particularly the
n_{\gamma\mathrm{extra}}
multiplicity distributions, introduce an additional 5.5% uncertainty. This is assessed by generating 100 sets of correction factors, each with Gaussian fluctuations derived from control sample uncertainties, and performing fits using the corresponding PDFs.

Other sources of systematic uncertainty include uncertainties in the branching fractions of decay modes used in the Monte Carlo (4.1%), the efficiency of the tag-side
B^{-}
reconstruction (2.2%), the continuum reweighting procedure (1.9%),
\pi^{0}
reconstruction efficiency (0.9%), continuum normalization (0.7%), and particle identification (0.6%).

The uncertainties that only affect the branching fraction calculation, and not the signal yield, are estimated separately. These are the number of
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
mesons (1.5%), the fraction of
B^{+}B^{-}
pairs (2.1%), and the tracking efficiency (0.2%).

Adding all contributions in quadrature, the total systematic uncertainty on the branching fraction amounts to 15.5%.

The signal significance is evaluated by convolving the likelihood profile with a Gaussian distribution of width equal to the total systematic uncertainty. The test statistic is defined as
-2\log(\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}_{0})
, where
\mathcal{L}
is the likelihood with the signal yield floated and
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is the likelihood with the signal yield fixed to zero. To compute the
p
-value,
10^{6}
pseudo-experiments are generated from the background-only hypothesis, and the fraction of fits yielding a test statistic smaller than that observed in data is used. This procedure results in a signal significance of
3.0\sigma
.

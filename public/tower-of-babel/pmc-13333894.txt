Adaptive long short term memory-PI based control for load frequency regulation in electric vehicle integrated two area power systems
Authors: Sandip Kumar Das, Sarat Chandra Swain, Byamakesh Nayak, Ritesh Dash, Futa Osumanu, Bhabasis Mohapatra, Seif Al Bustanji, Oleksandr Rubanenko
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The growing penetration of electric vehicles (EVs) and renewable energy sources has introduced significant nonlinear and stochastic disturbances in modern power systems, posing major challenges to conventional load frequency control (LFC) strategies. To address these issues, a Long Short-Term Memory-based Proportional Integral (LSTM-PI) controller is proposed to enable adaptive frequency regulation in a two-area EV-integrated system. The LSTM network dynamically tunes the proportional and integral gains in response to temporal variations in frequency and tie-line states. The learning module is trained using an Integral of Time weighted Absolute Error (ITAE) objective. Practical non-linearities such as governor deadband, generation-rate constraints, valve saturation, and EV state-of-charge limits are incorporated into both the training dataset and the online control evaluation. A constrained Model Predictive Control (CMPC-PI) is developed as a modern benchmark to compare the proposed technique under identical sampling rates and actuator limits. The proposed method shows better performance under dynamic conditions. Statistical performance analysis has been carried out through 30 runs of Monte Carlo analysis with a mean of 95% confidence intervals. Stability analysis under time-varying gains is supported by a bounded-gain Lyapunov Framework and a delay-margin analysis that addresses sensing and actuation delays. The result analysis confirms that the LSTM-PI controller provides a scalable intelligent solution for modern EV-integrated smart grid frequency regulation. The proposed intelligent adaptive controller provides a scalable, low-complexity, and stability-assured solution for robust load-frequency regulation in nonlinear, EV-integrated smart power grids.

The integration of renewable energy sources and electric vehicles (EVs) in AC microgrids has become increasingly prevalent in modern power systems. However, this integration introduces complex challenges for the microgrid’s stability and reliability. Specifically, in the context of a two-area load frequency control system, incorporating EVs as power system stabilisers raises questions about the overall stability of the microgrid45. The increasing integration of EVs and renewable sources has changed LFC dynamics, introducing fast, time-varying disturbances and nonlinearity. All these challenges lead to the requirement of adaptive control strategies that can act to rapid system fluctuation.

Frequency deviation in a large interconnected system results from load mismatches between the connected generators in the tie-line system and the electrical load connected to it6. This effect leads to the development of the load-frequency control problem, which warrants further research. Most industrial-grade load frequency controllers are based on PI control and require online tuning using a trial-and-error approach7. Various decentralised, robust, and optimal control methods have been developed in the past decade to tune the LFC problem in designing the load-frequency controller8.

Modern control techniques offer various structures, but power system utilities often favour using online-tuned PI controllers. This preference is likely due to the ease of online tuning and concerns about stability in certain adaptive or variable-structure methods. Today’s power systems incorporate a mix of energy sources, including both traditional and renewable ones, which are often spread across different locations9. The placement of energy generation sources depends on the availability of natural resources, such as fossil fuels, wind, and tidal power. With the deregulation of power systems, electricity is often transmitted over long distances using existing transmission lines, which are operated near their thermal capacity limits10. Operating a power system with overloaded transmission lines can induce continuous frequency oscillations under sudden changes in demand or supply. As a result, recent research has increasingly focused on load frequency control11.

The Load Frequency Control (LFC) problem has been studied using both traditional and modern control methods, including heuristic-based approaches. Due to the complexity of modern power systems, control loops often rely on approximate models. Traditionally, PID controllers are used for LFC issues, with tuning methods like Hit and Trial and the Ziegler-Nichols method. However, if a PID controller is not properly tuned, it can have a poor response, and choosing the wrong integral gain can even destabilize the system. Conventional tuning works well for simpler systems, whereas heuristics-based methods are better suited to complex systems, such as power systems. Therefore, a Model Predictive Control (MPC) approach has been applied to solve the LFC problem in multi-area power systems. The Bacterial Foraging Optimisation Algorithm (BFOA) was used to find the best controller settings to minimise the time-domain objective function. The high computational burden limits the real-time applicability. These limitations motivate the search for a controller capable of combining adaptability and computational efficiency.

In multi-area power systems, a mismatch between generated power and load demand leads to unwanted changes in frequency and tie-line power flows. Load Frequency Control (LFC) is used to maintain a balance between power generation and demand. The primary objectives of LFC are to keep the system frequency and inter-area power exchange close to their planned values, both under normal conditions and during disturbances or sudden changes in demand. Physical constraints, such as generation-rate limits, can significantly affect the performance of LFC systems. PI controllers are simple to design and highly reliable, but they have limitations in handling generation-rate constraints.

This creates a clear research gap in designing an adaptive, memory-enabled and computationally efficient controller that can handle real-time variations in load and renewable penetration while coordinating with EV aggregators. The motivation of this study is to address this gap by developing an LSTM-PI controller. The proposed method dynamically tunes PI parameters to ensure faster frequency restoration, reduced overshoot and improved stability. In another study22, the author proposed a cascade proportional-derivative controller tuned using the Imperialist Competitive Algorithm (ICA) for load-frequency control of an AC multi-microgrid. The system integrates thermal, diesel, microturbine, and EV units. The ICA-based tuning achieved ITAE improvements of 70%, 54.23%, and 47.10% compared to PI, PID, and PIDF controllers, respectively. A fractional order Fuzzy PID (FOFPID) controller optimised via Sunflower Optimisation was applied to a two-area solar wind hydrothermal system augmented by a Hydrogen Aqua Equaliser Fuel Cell unit23. The SFO-SOFPID. The controller reduced ITAE by 84.61% and 56.04% compared to GA and TLBO, respectively. The approach exhibits increased computational overhead due to the five-dimensional parameter space. Similarly, in another study24, the authors introduced an improved Modified Search Algorithm (i-MSA) to tune a fractional-order type-2 Fuzzy PID controller for stand-alone microgrid frequency control under variable solar wind inputs. The computational complexity of i-MSA increases with the parameter dimension, thereby limiting its real-time implementation. A fractional two integral derivative with filtered feedback controller tuned via an improved equilibrium optimisation algorithm was developed for a two-area microgrid under stochastic solar, wind and load conditions25. The controller reduced the peak frequency deviation by 65.73% and settling time by 37% compared to conventional designs.

Despite extensive work on load frequency control the current approach still face three major limitations such as (i) most classical and intelligent controllers is based on fixed gains thereby making them unable to stochastic disturbances introduced by EV (ii) MPC, FOPID and metaheuristic based controller incur high computational cost and doesn’t support the practical constraints like GRC and EV. These gaps motivates the development of an adaptive and sequential framework with lower computational complexity. The proposed LSTM-framework is designed to fill precisely this space in the literature262728.

Based on the literature survey, it is understood that most earlier LFC strategies based on intelligent optimisation and learning algorithms exhibit significant limitations in terms of complexity and scalability. This work extends conventional PI based load frequency control by introducing adaptive gain scheduling within a two area EV-integrated power system. A constraint based real time gain generation framework is developed where governor rate constraints, actuator saturation and EV state of charge limits are taken into consideration for both the training data set and online implementation. This motivates the integration of LSTM with PI controller to create an adaptive architecture for EV-driven LFC. Problem formulation & solution methodology

The increase in power demand, convergence of renewable power integration to the traditional power system to meet the power demand. The reduction in power system dependency also helps the deregulated power DISCOMS to plan more effectively. A large-scale interconnection of EVs results in unbalanced power sharing with the nearby system.

The LFC measures and monitors generation against demand, thereby providing the proper command to the steam turbine valve to open or close, or to operate the penstock valve in the case of a hydropower plant. However, the large-scale interconnection of EVs via V2X has led to frequency deviations. The efficiency of LFC generally depends on the gain parameter tuning.

The considered LFC model incorporates both stochastic disturbances and practical nonlinearities to closely represent real grid conditions. The major disturbances include (i) step and random load demand variations of ±5–10% in each area, (ii) renewable generation intermittency modelled as zero mean Gaussian noise with a variance of 0.02 pu, representing solar irradiance and wind velocity fluctuations, and (iii) sensor to actuator communication delays up to 50ms. The explicit inclusion of these effects ensures that the problem formulation captures the full nonlinear and stochastic nature of a multi-area EV-integrated power system.

LSTM network architecture and training procedure

The proposed LSTM-PI framework adaptively generates time-varying gains \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_p$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$K_i$$\end{document} based on sequential system state information. The classical PI controller depends only on the instantaneous and integral error; the LSTM captures temporal dependencies and dynamic operating conditions. This enables improved disturbance rejection and ITAE minimisation under renewable variability and EV participation. Therefore, the LSTM-PI controller enhances adaptability without altering the fundamental PI control structure.

The training data are obtained from the MATLAB based time line domain simulation of the two area LFC-EV model under randomized operating scenario. The LSTM network architecture consists of two hidden LSTM layers, followed by a fully connected dense layer and a final regression output layer providing tuned PI gains. The tanh activation function was used within the LSTM layers to capture non-linear temporal dependencies, and the ReLu activation was applied in the dense layer to avoid vanishing gradients. The network was trained using the Adam optimiser with a learning rate of 0.001, a batch size of 32 and 150 epochs. The Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and actual deviation was used as the loss function.

To prevent overfitting, early stopping was employed with 20 epochs, and a dropout regularisation was applied in hidden layers. During training, convergence was achieved after 110 epochs, where the validation error stabilised without divergence from the training error. The final trained model consistently achieved an \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2>0.95$$\end{document} across all test cases. The novelty of this work is the development and implementation of a hybrid LSTM-PI controller for two-area LFC with EV integration. The proposed framework with an LSTM model adjusts PI parameters in response to real-time load frequency deviations. This ensures effective utilization of EV stabilizing resources within the microgrid. This is the advantage over the RNN-PI controller, which suffers from over-fitting, and the SVM-PI controller, which is bounded by the static classification boundaries. The proposed method achieves 65% faster settling time, 85% lower overshoot and 50% reduction in IAE compared to the classical PI model.

The LSTM-PI controller does not aim to replace the MPC approach. The CMPC-PI benchmark provides constrained based optimization categorically suitable for supervisory control action. This requires a high volume of computational power because of repeated quadratic optimization. The LSTN-PI framework perform gain adaption through a trained neural network which actually decreases the computational burden. This makes the LSTM-PI controller more suitable in distributed EV integrated Power System343536.

Conclusion

The comparative analysis of the proposed LSTM-PI controller for frequency regulation using Electric Vehicle (EV) aggregation demonstrated its remarkable robustness. The investigation involved a meticulous comparison with traditional PI, SVM-PI, and RNN-PI controllers, using a dataset generated from simulations of classical PI controllers under varying load conditions. The dataset was partitioned into training and testing sets, and the gains for SVM-PI, RNN-PI, and LSTM-PI were evaluated using the cost function, which influences tie-line power flow and, consequently, Area Control Error (ACE).

The interrelationships among proportional gain (Kp), integral gain (Ki), and the cost function were analysed. The classical PI model exhibited a 25% slower response than the LSTM-PI controller under various disturbances, emphasising the superior performance of the proposed LSTM-PI model. Despite the challenges observed in other models, such as overfitting in RNN-PI, the LSTM-PI controller consistently demonstrated its resilience and efficiency. The recent studies on adaptive LFC and EV-assisted frequency regulation typically report settling time in the range of 65–90 sec. and overshoot level of 25–40% under multi-area test conditions. The proposed LSTM-PI controller achieves a settling time of 55 sec. and overshoot of 22%. His comparative improvement confirms that the present work advances beyond existing methods by delivering both faster frequency restoration and stronger stability margins in EV-integrated two-area systems.

The performance metrics further validated the advantages of the LSTM-PI controller. Time-response analysis revealed a lower steady-state error (0.51) than that of the classical PI (0.81) and RNN-PI (0.57) controllers. Again, the LSTM-PI controller exhibited a lower maximum overshoot of 4.7%, confirming its robustness. A dynamic Simulink model, accounting for continuous load variations, examined the impact of the EV aggregator on tie-line power flow. Results in Fig. 3a,b show the LSTM-PI controller’s efficacy in ensuring stable and efficient operation. In conclusion, this comprehensive study validates the superiority of the proposed LSTM-PI controller, positioning it as a promising solution for frequency regulation in power systems with EV aggregators. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically integrate LSTM-based adaptive learning with PI control for EV aggregation in load frequency regulation.

Future scope

The present study establishes the efficacy of the LSTM-PI controller in simulations; however, the next logical extension is to validate it on hardware-in-the-loop and real-time digital simulation. Such validation will test controller performance under stochastic load variations and communication delays, with frequency regulation at 0.01Hz, and will demonstrate a settling time below 200 ms under load surges.

The proposed framework can be further scaled to multi-area interconnected grids with high levels of renewable interconnection and large-scale V2G operations. The future target is to maintain a steady-state error below 2% and an overshoot below 5%, even under 30–40% renewable variability and a 25% EV penetration level.

Another direction may be coupling the LSTM-PI model with a hybrid optimisation framework, such as reinforcement learning or quantum-inspired optimisation. The hybridisation is expected to reduce the ITAE by 30% relative to LSTM-PI, thereby further enhancing adaptability. Similarly, making the model resistant to cyber-physical attacks on false data injection, with the goal of achieving a 95% stability margin, could be another research area.

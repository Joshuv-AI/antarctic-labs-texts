miR-4772-3p serves as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in septic acute kidney injury by targeting CISD2
Authors: Qiang Chen, Gaoshang Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to explore the clinical value and function of miR-4772-3p in septic acute kidney injury (AKI). A total of 99 septic patients without AKI and 93 with AKI were enrolled. RT-qPCR measured miR-4772-3p expression. Diagnostic and predictive value was assessed using the ROC curve and logistic regression, and prognostic utility was evaluated by Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression. An lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced human kidney‑2 cell line (HK-2) cell model was established to assess viability by CCK-8, apoptosis by flow cytometry, inflammatory cytokines (tumor necrosis factor‑α (TNF‑α), interleukin‑6 (IL‑6), interleukin‑1β (IL‑1β)) by ELISA, and oxidative stress (superoxide dismutase (SOD) and malondialdehyde (MDA)). Bioinformatics and dual-luciferase assay identified the targets of miR-4772-3p. miR‑4772‑3p was upregulated in septic AKI, and its level positively correlated with cystatin C (Cys‑C), serum creatinine (Scr), Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score, Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) score, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). miR‑4772‑3p showed high diagnostic value and was an independent risk factor for AKI occurrence. Moreover, higher miR‑4772‑3p expression was linked to elevated mortality and served as an independent prognostic risk factor. In the LPS‑induced HK‑2 model, decreased cell viability, increased apoptosis, elevated pro‑inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β), reduced SOD activity, and increased MDA content were observed. Inhibition of miR‑4772‑3p reversed these injury phenotypes. Mechanistically, CDGSH iron sulfur domain 2 (CISD2) was verified as a direct target gene of miR‑4772‑3p; knockdown of CISD2 could reverse the effects of miR-4772-3p inhibition in pathological regulation. miR‑4772‑3p may serve as a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for septic AKI. Its upregulation may promote sepsis-induced HK-2 cell injury by regulating CISD2, thereby facilitating AKI progression.

Introduction

Sepsis is defined as a life-threatening clinical syndrome resulting from a dysregulated immune response to infection, ultimately leading to impaired organ function [1]. Among its most prevalent and critical complications is acute kidney injury (AKI), which manifests as a sudden deterioration of kidney function over a short period [2]. Septic AKI can occur at any stage of the septic process. Particularly during septic shock, sustained microcirculatory hypoperfusion in the kidneys predisposes the tissue to ischemia, cellular damage, and even necrosis, thereby significantly increasing patient morbidity and mortality [3]. Currently, the pathophysiological mechanisms of septic AKI remain incompletely understood, and the clinical treatment outcomes are not satisfactory [4]. Therefore, exploring reliable diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers and deeply clarifying their molecular mechanisms are crucial for enhancing the clinical diagnosis and treatment of this condition.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are endogenous, single-stranded non-coding RNA molecules that modulate target gene expression and are involved in diverse biological processes, including inflammation, immunity, and tumorigenesis [5]. Certain miRNAs are released into the extracellular space and remain stable in bodily fluids such as blood and urine, and are therefore regarded as promising new diagnostic biomarkers for human diseases [6]. In the context of sepsis-induced kidney diseases, miRNAs have been implicated in the pathogenesis of septic AKI. For instance, miRNA-29a and miRNA-10a-5p serve as predictors for the 28-day mortality in patients with this condition [7]; microRNA-155 demonstrates potential for the early diagnosis and prognostic evaluation of septic AKI [8]; and miR-26a-5p has been reported to alleviate renal injury caused by sepsis [9]. Nevertheless, the specific functions and mechanisms of many miRNAs in septic AKI remain unclear and require further in-depth exploration.

In the field of sepsis, dysregulation of miR-4772-3p has gradually gained attention. For instance, studies have found through serum sample analysis that miR-4772-3p has the potential to distinguish sepsis [10]. Additionally, a screening study on the ceRNA regulatory network related to the prognosis of sepsis, verified by RT-qPCR, pointed to the possible engagement of miR-4772-3p in sepsis regulation [11]. Further evidence suggests that the high levels of miR-4772-3p in the plasma of sepsis patients are associated with organ dysfunction, including lung, liver, and kidney impairment, in sepsis. The results support the potential biomarker role of miR-4772-3p in different organ dysfunctions in sepsis [12]. These findings collectively reveal the important role of miR-4772-3p in sepsis and its complications. Based on this, the present study proposes the hypothesis that miR-4772-3p is abnormally expressed in septic AKI, which may have diagnostic and prognostic value and has regulatory functions on the disease process. To verify this hypothesis, the study analyzed the level and clinical significance of miR-4772-3p using clinical samples, and then employed cell models to simulate the disease state, thereby deeply exploring its specific function and potential molecular mechanism in septic AKI.

Materials and methods

Patient enrollment and sample collection

A total of 192 septic patients were included in this study. Sepsis was diagnosed according to the Third International Consensus Definitions for Sepsis and Septic Shock [13]. Then, patients were categorized into two groups based on the occurrence of AKI (septic AKI, n = 93) and the non-AKI group (n = 99). AKI was defined using the criteria outlined in the Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines [14].

Exclusion criteria: 1) Patients with chronic renal insufficiency or those who have undergone kidney transplantation; 2) Patients who have used nephrotoxic drugs or have other complications; 3) Patients with infectious diseases (excluding sepsis) and autoimmune disorders; 4) Patients with malignant tumors; 5) Pregnant women.

All patients had their venous fasting blood samples collected within 24 h after clinical diagnosis. Serum was separated by centrifugation and preserved at −80 °C for subsequent analysis. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects involved in the study and a written informed consent has been obtained from the patients to publish this paper. The research protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Tongde Hospital of Zhejiang Province (No. 2022K20).

All septic AKI patients were followed for 28 days after diagnosis. The endpoint of follow-up was death attributed to the underlying sepsis or its complications.

Cell culture and treatment

The human kidney-2 proximal tubule epithelial cell line (HK‑2) was sourced from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). Cells were maintained at 37°C with 5% CO₂ in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (A5669201, Sigma-Aldrich, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (SV30010, HyClone, USA).

To establish a cellular model of septic AKI, HK-2 cells were exposed to 10 μg/mL LPS for 24 h [15].

Cell transfection

The miR-4772-3p mimic (mimic-miR) and its control (mimic-NC), the miR-4772-3p inhibitor (inhibitor-miR) and its control (inhibitor-NC) were obtained from Gemma Gene (Shanghai, China). Transfection of cells was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (L3000001, Invitrogen, USA), following the manufacturer’s instructions.

RNA extraction and RT‑qPCR

Total RNA was isolated from the processed samples and cells with TRIzol reagent (15596026CN, Invitrogen, USA). cDNA was then synthesized using a commercial reverse transcription kit (RR037A, Takara Bio, Japan). qPCR was conducted with LightCycler® 480 SYBR Green I Master (4913850001, Roche, Switzerland), using U6 snRNA and GAPDH as endogenous controls. The reaction conditions were as follows: 95 °C for 10 min for initial denaturation, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 60 s. Relative gene expression was determined via the 2−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blot analysis

The total protein of the cells was extracted using the RIPA (Biyuntian, Shanghai) lysis buffer. The obtained protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and then transferred onto the polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane (Biyuntian, Shanghai). The membrane was blocked with 5% skimmed milk and incubated together with specific primary antibodies, including anti-CISD2 antibody (ab32059, 1:1000, Abcam, USA) and anti-β-actin antibody (ab8227, 1:1000, Abcam, USA). Subsequently, the goat anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody (ab205718, 1:10,000, Abcam, USA) was used for incubation. The protein expression levels were quantitatively analyzed by ImageJ software.

Cell viability assay

The Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay (HY-K0301, MedChemExpress, USA) was employed to evaluate cell viability. HK-2 cells were plated in 96-well plates and cultured under standard culture conditions (37 °C, 5% CO2) for 48 h. Subsequently, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well, followed by an additional 1.5-h incubation. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm on a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, USA) to determine viability.

Cell apoptosis assay

Apoptosis was analyzed via flow cytometry after double staining with Annexin V-FITC (640906, BioLegend, USA) and propidium iodide (PI) (421301, BioLegend, USA). HK-2 cells were harvested, rinsed with ice-cold PBS, and resuspended in binding buffer. The cells were then sequentially incubated with 5 μL Annexin V-FITC and 10 μL PI, followed by incubation at room temperature in the dark. Flow cytometric analysis was then performed on the stained samples (Agilent Technologies, USA), and the total apoptosis rate was calculated.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent (ELISA) assay

The concentrations of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α; KHC3014C, Invitrogen, USA), interleukin-6 (IL-6; BMS213-2, Invitrogen, USA), and interleukin-1β (IL-1β; BMS6002-2TEN, Invitrogen, USA) in the cell culture supernatants were quantified using commercially available ELISA kits. All procedures were conducted strictly following the instructions provided with the kits, and the final results were expressed in picograms per milliliter (pg/mL).

Oxidative stress assay

Oxidative stress markers were assessed by determining superoxide dismutase (SOD; 19160-1KT-F, Merck, USA) activity and malondialdehyde (MDA; MAK568-1KT, Merck, USA) content in the cell supernatants using specific assay kits, strictly following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Bioinformatics analysis and dual-luciferase reporter assays

The downstream target genes of miR-4772-3p were predicted through the online websites miRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/), miRDB (https://mirdb.org), and miRTarBase (https://mirtarbase.cuhk.edu.cn/∼miRTarBase/miRTarBase_2025/php/index.php).

To confirm the targeting interaction between miR-4772-3p and CISD2, DNA fragments containing the predicted binding sites of miR-4772-3p on CISD2 (CISD2-WT) 3′UTR sequences and corresponding mutant version (CISD2-MUT) were cloned into the pGL3 reporter vector (Promega, USA). The reporter constructs were co-transfected with the miR-4772-3p mimic or inhibitor, which were co-introduced into HK-2 cells. Following a 48-h transfection period, luciferase activity was assessed with a dual-luciferase reporter assay kit (E1910, Promega, USA), where firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were implemented with SPSS 21.0 and GraphPad Prism 7.0 software. Measurement data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Inter-group comparisons were made using the chi-square test, Student’s t-test, or one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. Correlations between variables were assessed by Pearson correlation analysis. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was constructed to evaluate the diagnostic value of serum miR-4772-3p for AKI. Logistic regression analysis was used to assess the predictive ability of miR-4772-3p for the occurrence of AKI. The survival rate of AKI patients was assessed with the Kaplan-Meier method, and independent risk factors affecting the prognosis of patients were analyzed using the Cox regression model. All in vitro experiments were performed with at least three independent replicates. Differences were regarded as significant if the P value was less than 0.05.

Results

Expression level and clinical significance of miR-4772-3p

Through analysis of the clinical data, septic non-AKI patients and septic AKI patients were matched in terms of baseline characteristics such as age, sex, body mass index (BMI), and white blood cell (WBC) with no statistical differences; nevertheless, they differed significantly in C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), estimated glomerular filtration (eGFR), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), cystatin-C (Cys-C), acute physiology and chronic health evaluation II (APACHE II score), serum creatinine (Scr), and sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score between the two groups (Table 1). Quantification in serum revealed that miR-4772-3p was significantly upregulated in AKI patients (Figure 1(a)). ROC curve analysis indicated that serum miR-4772-3p had good diagnostic value for AKI, with an AUC of 0.864 (95% CI: 0.808–0.919, p < 0.001), and its sensitivity and specificity were 85.1% and 84.0% (Figure 1(b)). Logistic regression analysis further suggested that miR-4772-3p was identified as an independent predictor for AKI occurrence (OR = 2.651, p = 0.002); a higher expression level is associated with an increased risk of developing AKI (Table 2).

Pearson correlation analysis results illustrated that the level of serum miR-4772-3p showed a positive correlation with Cys-C (r = 0.726, p < 0.001; Figure 1(c)), Scr (r = 0.737, p < 0.001; Figure 1(d)), SOFA score (r = 0.707, p < 0.001; Figure 1(e)), APACHE II score (r = 0.721, p < 0.001; Figure 1(f)), and BUN (r = 0.715, p < 0.001; Figure 1(g)), and a negative correlation with eGFR (r = −0.778, p < 0.001; Figure 1(h)).

Additionally, patients were stratified into high- and low-expression groups based on the median level of miR-4772-3p. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis indicated that a decrease in the overall survival rate occurred among AKI patients with high expression (Figure 1(i)). Cox regression analysis further suggested that miR-4772-3p (HR = 4.974, p = 0.004), Cys-C (HR = 3.498, p = 0.022), SOFA score (HR = 3.040, p = 0.032), and APACHE II score (HR = 3.039, p = 0.032) were independent factors associated with the prognosis of AKI patients (Table 3). Further analysis was conducted on the aforementioned independent factors and the combined factors of miR-4772-3p, Scr, and urine output (UO). The HR of combined miR-4772-3p, Scr, and UO was 6.057 (p = 0.002), and the HR of miR-4772-3p was 2.612 (p = 0.043). The combined miR-4772-3p, serum creatinine, and urine output had a higher value in predicting the risk of late death in patients with AKI (Supplementary Table 2).

Inhibition of miR-4772-3p reversed the LPS-induced damage in HK-2 cells

A cell model of septic AKI was constructed by treating with 10 μg/mL LPS for 24 h. In this model, miR-4772-3p expression was significantly upregulated (Figure 2(a)), concomitant with a decrease in cell viability (Figure 2(b)) and an increase in the apoptotic rate (Figure 2(c)). Concurrently, levels of pro-inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) were elevated (Figure 2(d)). The oxidative stress profile was also altered, characterized by an increase in MDA content (Figure 2(e)) and a decrease in SOD activity (Figure 2(f)). Inhibiting the expression of miR-4772-3p could significantly reverse the LPS-induced injury in HK-2 cells.

Inhibition of miR-4772-3p attenuated LPS-induced HK2 cell injury. (a–f) In HK-2 cells induced by LPS, the expression level of miR-4772-3p increased (a), cell viability decreased (b), apoptosis rate rose (c), inflammatory factors increased (d), MDA elevated (e), and SOD decreased (f); inhibiting miR-4772-3p could restore these changes. (***p < 0.001).

miR-4772-3p directly interacted with CISD2

Bioinformatics analysis predicted that ORAI2, ATG14, TXLNG, CISD2, and RBM48 were predicted potential target genes of miR-4772-3p (Figure 3(a)). Among them, CISD2 has shown regulatory potential in related studies on sepsis and HK-2 cells, and in LPS-induced HK-2 cells, inhibition of miR-4772-3p resulted in the most significant upregulation of CISD2 expression, suggesting that CISD2 may directly bind to miR-4772-3p (Figure 3(b)). CISD2 was selected for further experimental verification. The dual luciferase reporter gene assay confirmed that in cells transfected with the wild-type CISD2 reporter vector (CISD2-WT), the miR-4772-3p mimic could inhibit luciferase activity, while its inhibitor enhanced this activity; however, this effect was absent in CISD2-MUT (Figure 3(c)). Clinical sample analysis indicated that the level of CISD2 was reduced in AKI patients (Figure 3(d)), and its level showed a negative correlation with the level of miR-4772-3p (r = −0.740, p < 0.001; Figure 3(e)).

Knockdown of CISD2 reversed the effect of miR-4772-3p inhibition on LPS-induced HK-2 function

In the LPS-induced HK-2 cell model, the expression of CISD2 was downregulated; after inhibition of miR-4772-3p, its expression was partially restored; while upon co-inhibition of CISD2 and miR-4772-3p, CISD2 expression levels were significantly decreased again, and the changes in its mRNA and protein levels were consistent (Figure 4(a)). Functionally, knockdown of CISD2 significantly reversed the multiple effects induced by inhibition of miR-4772-3p, including promoting cell viability (Figure 4(b)), inhibiting apoptosis (Figure 4(c)), reducing inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) levels (Figure 4(d)), decreasing MDA content (Figure 4(e)), and enhancing SOD activity (Figure 4(f)).

Discussion

In the ICU, sepsis is a prevalent and serious medical condition with rapid disease progression. When it affects the kidneys, it can cause AKI and often leads to life-threatening multi-organ dysfunction [16]. Currently, the clinical diagnosis of septic AKI relies mainly on the KDIGO criteria (serum creatinine and urine output). However, these traditional indicators have significant lag and non-specificity, making early identification and intervention challenging [17]. Therefore, it is crucial to shift the diagnostic model of septic AKI from reliance on lagging functional indicators to a precise strategy that integrates early damage markers. Biomarkers have significant value in this context. Previous studies have reported that certain microRNAs, including miRNA-29a and microRNA-155, demonstrate predictive potential in septic AKI [7,8]; however, these investigations were limited by their exclusive reliance on clinical samples and relatively modest sample sizes. Additionally, earlier research has indicated that miR-4772-3p is abnormally elevated in sepsis and shows diagnostic and prognostic potential [10–12]; nevertheless, its specific role in septic AKI remains unclear. This study verified the role of miR-4772-3p through larger clinical samples combined with in vitro cellular assays, providing a more comprehensive validation of its diagnostic and prognostic value. Specifically, miR-4772-3p levels are elevated in septic AKI, implying its potential involvement in the pathological process of septic AKI. Further analysis revealed that miR-4772-3p was positively correlated with Cys-C, Scr, SOFA score, APACHE II score, BUN, and eGFR. Among them, Cys-C, as an early indicator of renal function, can more accurately reflect the decline in glomerular filtration rate [18]; Scr is the core indicator for clinical diagnosis of AKI [19]; SOFA score reflects the severity of AKI in the context of overall organ dysfunction [20]; and APACHE II score comprehensively reflects the severity of the condition and the risk of death [21]; BUN reflects the severity of renal failure and the degree of hypercatabolism [22]; a decrease in eGFR is an independent risk factor for AKI in patients with sepsis [23]. These correlations collectively suggest that the level of miR-4772-3p is closely related to the severity of septic AKI. Moreover, miR-4772-3p can effectively distinguish between septic AKI and non-AKI, and has been confirmed as an independent risk factor for AKI. These findings imply that miR-4772-3p has potential as a biomarker for septic AKI, providing a new research direction for achieving earlier and more precise diagnosis.

When patients with sepsis progress to AKI, their mortality rate significantly increases [18]. Early identification of the adverse prognostic factors for AKI patients helps initiate preventive measures in advance [7]. This study found that the mortality rate of patients with high miR-4772-3p expression was increased. Further Cox risk regression analysis indicated that miR-4772-3p, Cys-C, Scr, SOFA score, and APACHE II score were all independent adverse prognostic factors for AKI. Among them, existing studies have confirmed that Cys-C, SOFA score, and APACHE II score are adverse prognostic factors for AKI [24,25]. UO is a key diagnostic criterion for septic AKI and serves as an independent predictor of poor prognosis, directly reflecting the severity of renal hypoperfusion and tubular injury [19]. Furthermore, the combination of miR-4772-3p with Scr and UO demonstrated a higher predictive ability for mortality risk. Based on the above findings, it could be suggested that miR-4772-3p can also serve as a potential prognostic marker for septic AKI.

The core pathology of septic AKI lies in the uncontrolled systemic inflammatory response triggered by infection, which leads to acute tubular injury and dysfunction of the kidneys [26]. Among them, the imbalance of oxidative stress is the key central link connecting the initiation of inflammation and the ultimate cell functional failure [27]: MDA, as the final product of lipid peroxidation, its elevated level directly reflects severe oxidative damage to the cells [28]; SOD, as the core antioxidant enzyme within the cells, its reduced activity indicates a decline in the local antioxidant defense capacity of the kidneys [29]. To simulate the above pathological process at the cellular level, this study used LPS to induce the HK-2 cell model to establish an in vitro model, which can effectively reproduce the inflammatory injury, apoptosis, and functional disorder of renal tubular epithelial cells in septic AKI. Previous studies have successfully used 10 μg/mL LPS-induced HK-2 cells for 24 h to explore the related molecular mechanisms [30]. In the model of this study, the levels of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) were elevated, cell viability decreased, apoptosis rate increased, SOD decreased, and MDA increased, indicating that the cell model was successfully constructed. Moreover, miR-4772-3p was elevated in the cell model, and inhibiting miR-4772-3p could reverse the above injury phenotypes induced by LPS. The data reveal that inhibition of miR-4772-3p may alleviate inflammation, oxidative stress, and cellular damage in HK-2 cells, which could ameliorate the pathological progression of septic AKI.

miRNAs typically participate in disease regulation by targeting and inhibiting the expression of downstream genes. In this study, bioinformatics analysis predicted that ORAI2, ATG14, TXLNG, CISD2, and RBM48 were potential target genes of miR-4772-3p. Previous literature has shown that CISD2 plays an important role in sepsis: CISD2 exerts a protective effect in sepsis-related myocardial injury and shows good predictive value for 28-day prognosis and myocardial injury [31]; CISD2 shows decreased expression in HK-2 cells exposed to LPS, and its overexpression conversely attenuates the LPS-triggered inflammation and apoptosis [32]. This study verified that there exists a targeting association between miR-4772-3p and CISD2. And it was confirmed that the expression of CISD2 was downregulated in septic AKI, a trend that was in line with the findings reported by Wu et al. [32]. Further, CISD2 was also significantly downregulated in LPS-induced HK-2 cells, while knockdown of CISD2 significantly reversed the protective effect of miR-4772-3p inhibition on LPS-induced HK-2 cells. These evidences reveal that miR-4772-3p may exert regulatory effects in the pathological process of septic AKI by targeting and inhibiting the level of CISD2.

This study investigated the function of miR-4772-3p in septic AKI using HK-2 cells. The primary reason for this choice lies in the fact that renal tubular epithelial cells serve as the principal targets for oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis during AKI. However, the pathological process of septic AKI involves multiple intrinsic renal cells (such as glomerular endothelial cells, podocytes, and mesangial cells) and immune cells, which interact with each other in a complex manner. Therefore, conclusions drawn based on a single cell model may not fully simulate and reflect the complex network of the disease within the body. This is a limitation of this study. Previous research has verified the involvement of PCED1B-AS1 in septic AKI in mouse macrophages [24]. Due to the limitations of exploring specific mechanisms, as well as factors such as the technical conditions of cell co-culture, project funds, and time, this study was unable to explore the function of miR-4772-3p in other relevant cells. Future research will use cell co-culture systems or conditional gene knockout animal models to validate and expand upon the findings of this study. Then, due to its retrospective design, blood samples were collected only within 24 h of AKI diagnosis, with no follow-up sampling at subsequent time points (e.g., 48 h, 72 h). Consequently, the dynamic expression trajectory of miR-4772-3p during disease progression could not be analyzed. Obtaining multi-time-point expression data would help clarify whether this marker reflects early injury events or dynamic changes accompanying disease progression, thereby better assessing its clinical value as a predictive or diagnostic biomarker. Further, although septic patients without AKI were included as controls, non-septic AKI groups (e.g., ischemia-reperfusion, drug-induced, or contrast-induced AKI) were not incorporated. Therefore, whether the elevated expression of miR-4772-3p is specifically associated with sepsis-related AKI remains to be fully elucidated. Future prospective studies with multi-time-point sampling and multi-etiology control groups are warranted to further validate the diagnostic specificity and dynamic expression patterns of miR-4772-3p in septic AKI.

Conclusion

In summary, miR-4772-3p was upregulated in septic AKI patients and has high clinical diagnostic and prognostic value. Functional experiments demonstrated that inhibiting miR-4772-3p can effectively reverse the damage caused by LPS to HK-2 cells. Mechanistically, CISD2 was verified as a downstream target gene of miR-4772-3p; miR-4772-3p may exert its effect in septic AKI by regulating CISD2. These results provide new clues for in-depth analysis of the molecular mechanism of septic AKI and exploration of potential therapeutic targets.

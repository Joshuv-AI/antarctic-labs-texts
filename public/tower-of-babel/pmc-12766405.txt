Protocol for measuring in vivo, ex vivo, and in vitro glucose uptake in brown adipocytes
Authors: Chul-Hong Park, Ji Suk Chang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryHere, we present a protocol for assessing in vivo glucose uptake in brown adipose tissue (BAT) from mice using 2-deoxy-D-[1,2=-3H]glucose. We describe steps for reagent and animal preparation, tracer administration, and tissue processing to quantify radiolabeled glucose uptake and its conversion to glucose-6-phosphate. We then detail procedures for conducting glucose uptake assays in freshly isolated BAT and cultured brown adipocytes, enabling assessment under a range of experimental conditions.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Park et al.1Subject areas: cell-based assays, Health Sciences, Metabolism

Before you begin

Innovation

2-deoxy-D-glucose (2DG) is a non-metabolizable glucose analog that closely resembles glucose structurally but lacks the hydroxyl group at the 2-position, rendering it incapable of progressing beyond the initial phosphorylation step catalyzed by hexokinase in glycolysis.2 This leads to intracellular accumulation of 2-deoxy-D-glucose-6-phosphate (2DG6P), providing a reliable proxy for quantifying glucose transport activity. Leveraging this property, we present a comprehensive protocol for assessing β-adrenergic stimulated glucose uptake in brown adipose tissue using radiolabeled 2-deoxy-D-[1,2-3H]glucose. This protocol details procedures for in vivo tracer administration, tissue harvesting, and quantification of radiolabeled glucose uptake and utilization. In addition, it includes adaptations for ex vivo and in vitro glucose uptake assays in brown adipocytes, enabling versatile application across experimental models and conditions.

Preparation of mice for in vivo and ex vivo glucose uptake assays

Timing: 1 day

1.Randomly assign 8–10 mice to each experimental group and transfer them to new clean cages in groups (2 animals per cage). Refer to troubleshooting 1.

Randomly assign 8–10 mice to each experimental group and transfer them to new clean cages in groups (2 animals per cage). Refer to troubleshooting 1.

Note: Metabolic phenotypes are influenced by multiple factors, including mouse strain, age, sex, diet, and housing conditions such as ambient temperature. To ensure valid comparisons, these variables should be carefully matched across all experimental groups.

2.Remove food while allowing free access to water.

Remove food while allowing free access to water.

Note: Fasting reduces baseline glucose and insulin levels, improving sensitivity to changes. Fasting duration ranging from 5 h to overnight (approximately 12–16 h) are routinely used prior to 2-deoxy-D-[1,2-3H]glucose uptake assays and glucose tolerance tests. However, short-term fasting (5–6 h) is generally preferred to minimize physiological stress and maintain metabolic relevance. The optimal fasting duration may vary depending on the experimental objectives, dietary conditions, and the route of glucose administration. It should be carefully determined to balance physiological relevance, minimize stress, and ensure data consistency.

Note: For the ex vivo glucose uptake assay, fasting is not required.

Preparation of brown adipocytes for in vitro glucose uptake assay

Timing: 11–12 days

4.Seed brown preadipocytes at a density of 3 × 104 cells per well (approximately 30% confluence) in a 12-well plate using pre-warmed culture medium.

Seed brown preadipocytes at a density of 3 × 104 cells per well (approximately 30% confluence) in a 12-well plate using pre-warmed culture medium.

Note: Brown preadipocytes can be isolated from neonatal pups up to 4 days of age. For complete details of the brown preadipocyte isolation and differentiation protocol, please refer to Fasshauer et al.6 and Fasshauer et al.7

5.Allow the cells to grow in a 37°C cell culture incubator for approximately 2 to 2 ½ days or until they reach 90%–100% confluence.

Allow the cells to grow in a 37°C cell culture incubator for approximately 2 to 2 ½ days or until they reach 90%–100% confluence.

CRITICAL: Even distribution is the most critical factor for achieving 100% differentiation efficiency. Refer to troubleshooting 2.

6.Once cells reach 90%–100% confluence, induce differentiation by replacing the culture medium with the induction medium supplemented with 20 nM insulin, 1 nM T3, 5 μM dexamethasone, 0.125 mM indomethacin, and 0.5 mM IBMX. Incubate the cells at 37°C for 2 days.

Once cells reach 90%–100% confluence, induce differentiation by replacing the culture medium with the induction medium supplemented with 20 nM insulin, 1 nM T3, 5 μM dexamethasone, 0.125 mM indomethacin, and 0.5 mM IBMX. Incubate the cells at 37°C for 2 days.

7.After the 2-day induction period, replace the medium with differentiation medium containing 20 nM insulin and 1 nM T3. Incubate the cells at 37°C.

After the 2-day induction period, replace the medium with differentiation medium containing 20 nM insulin and 1 nM T3. Incubate the cells at 37°C.

8.Change the medium every 2 days for an additional 5 days, or until the cells are fully differentiated.

Change the medium every 2 days for an additional 5 days, or until the cells are fully differentiated.

Note: To assess the effect of β-adrenergic activation on glucose uptake in brown adipocytes, treat the cells with the β-adrenergic receptor agonist isoproterenol at a final concentration of 5 μM for 4 h prior to conducting the glucose uptake assay.

Step-by-step method details

In vivo tissue-specific glucose uptake assay

Timing: 5 h

This in vivo glucose uptake protocol is a close adaptation of protocols described in Ferre et al.8 and Moller et al.9 which utilize radiolabeled 2-deoxy-D-[1,2-3H]glucose to assess tissue-specific glucose uptake. This section describes the preparation of the 2-deoxy-D-[1,2-3H]glucose solution, its administration to mice with or without the β3-adrenergic receptor agonist CL316243, and subsequent tissue processing to quantify 2-deoxy-D-[1,2-3H]glucose uptake across various tissues.

1.Preparation of 2-deoxy-D-[1,2-3H]glucose solution and other reagents.a.Prepare 2-deoxy-D-[1,2-3H]glucose ([3H]-2DG) working solution.i.To administer a dose of 0.5 μCi per gram body weight using a [3H]-2DG working solution at 100 μCi/mL, calculate the injection volume (in μL) for each mouse by multiplying its body weight (in grams) by 5 μL.Note: For example, ten mice weighing 25 g each would require a total volume of 1,250 μL (10 × 25 g × 5 μL/g) for the experiment.ii.Add [3H]-2DG (stock radioactivity: 1 mCi/mL) to 20% glucose solution in a volume ratio of 1:10 to generate a final concentration of 100 μCi/mL.Note: Be sure to prepare extra volume beyond the calculated amount to account for any potential loss or error during handling.Note: Dissolving [3H]-2DG in 20% glucose ensures that its uptake occurs through glucose-specific transport mechanisms.Note: The administrated dose of [3H]-2DG can be adjusted based on the specific tissue of interest.CRITICAL: Personal protective equipment (PPE) must be worn while handling radioactive materials. Radioactive experiments must be carried out in the designated area.b.Prepare a solution of the β3-adrenergic receptor agonist CL316243.i.To administer a dose of 1 μg per gram body weight using a CL316243 solution at 0.2 mg/mL, calculate the injection volume (in μL) for each mouse by multiplying its body weight (in grams) by 5 μL.Note: For example, ten mice weighing 25 g each would require a total volume of 1,250 μL (10 × 25 g × 5 μL/g) for the experiment.ii.Dissolve 5 mg of CL316243 in 5 mL water to a final concentration of 1 mg/mL, filter sterilize the stock solution using a 0.22 μm filter, and then aliquot and store in −80°C freezer.iii.Dilute CL316243 stock solution in sterile saline to generate a final concentration of 0.2 mg/mL.Note: Be sure to prepare extra volume beyond the calculated amount to account for any potential loss or error during handling.Note: The β3-adrenergic receptor agonist CL316243 selectively activates β3-adrenergic receptors enriched in brown and white adipose tissue.3,4,5

Expected outcomes

A successful in vivo glucose uptake assay should demonstrate tissue-specific [3H]-2DG uptake that correlates with the metabolic activity of each tissue under the given experimental conditions. An example of this is shown in Figure 1C, where administration of the β3-adrenergic receptor (β3-AR) agonist CL316243 selectively increased [3H]-2DG uptake in brown adipose tissue (BAT). BAT exhibited the highest [3H]-2DG uptake among all tissues examined (Figure 1C). Elevated [3H] signal detected in the heart may, in part, reflect residual circulating [3H]-2DG in the bloodstream rather than true tissue-specific uptake. Furthermore, increasing the tracer dose fivefold resulted in a proportionally elevated uptake in BAT and other tissues (Figure 1D). Notably, the elevated [3H]-2DG uptake in BAT was closely associated with increased accumulation of [3H]-2DG-6-phosphate (Figures 1E–1H), suggesting enhanced glycolytic flux in this tissue under β3AR-stimulated conditions.

Similarly, successful ex vivo and in vitro glucose uptake assays should demonstrate transporter-specific uptake across the plasma membrane. This is illustrated in Figures 2 and 3, which show specific glucose uptake in freshly isolated BAT and cultured brown adipocytes, respectively. Transporter-mediated glucose entry can be quantified by subtracting non-specific uptake −measured in the presence of cytochalasin B− from total uptake (Figures 2B and 3B). Subsequent normalization to tissue weight or protein content should be carried out for accurate comparisons between experimental groups. Consistent with the in vivo findings, β-adrenergic stimulation of brown adipocytes with isoproterenol significantly enhanced [3H]-2DG uptake (Figure 3C), further validating the assay’s sensitivity to metabolic activation.

Quantification and statistical analysis

The data were analyzed and visualized using GraphPad Prism 10 software (Dotmatics, Boston, MA). Differences between two groups were evaluated using the unpaired two-tailed Student’s t test or two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni’s multiple comparisons as post-test. Data were presented as means ± SEM, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05. Significance is considered as ∗∗∗p < 0.001, ∗∗∗∗p < 0.0001.

Limitations

This protocol is optimized to assess both glucose uptake and phosphorylation in brown adipose tissue, which is characterized by inherently high metabolic activity. However, quantifying glucose phosphorylation in other tissues is challenging due to the low levels of detectable [3H]-2DG-6-phosphate and substantial inter-individual variability. These factors can significantly compromise the accuracy and reliability of glucose phosphorylation measurements in non-BAT tissues under the conditions tested. Another limitation of the in vivo glucose uptake assay is the lack of control for non-specific [3H]-2DG binding. In contrast, ex vivo and in vitro glucose uptake assays incorporate cytochalasin B to define and subtract non-specific uptake, thereby enhancing the specificity of the measurement.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

There are large biological variations in tissue-specific glucose uptake between animals (related to Mouse Preparation Step 1).

Potential solution

Ensure that each experimental group includes enough animals to achieve statistical reliability. Also, ensure that animals in each experimental group are matched for age, sex, diet, and housing conditions.

Problem 2

Cells tend to accumulate at the center of the well shortly after plating, especially in the incubator (related to Brown Adipocyte Preparation Step 1).

Potential solution

If this occurs within a few minutes, gently shake the plate back and forth and side to side several times to redistribute the cells evenly. The more cells are seeded, the higher tendency cells are concentrated at the center. Seed the cells at the density of 3 × 104 cells per well (cell growth area per well is 3.8 cm2) in a 12-well plate.

Problem 3

Brown adipocyte differentiation is inconsistent across the wells (related to Brown Adipocyte Preparation Steps 1 and 2).

Potential solution

Seed cells evenly at approximately 30% confluence and allow them to grow until they reach 90%–100% confluence before initiating differentiation. Uniform cell distribution is critical for achieving consistent and complete differentiation efficiency.

Problem 4

There are large technical variations in tissue-specific glucose uptake (related to Method Step 2a).

Potential solution

Administer [3H]-2DG in a sufficiently large volume, ensuring a minimum of 100 μL per animal during intraperitoneal (IP) injection. Alternatively, intravenous (IV) injection may be considered to reduce potential intra-group variability, which can result from inconsistencies in absorption or distribution associated with IP administration.

Problem 5

The amount of [3H]-2DG-6-phosphate in tissue samples is significantly lower than that of [3H]-2DG and varies across different tissues (related to Method Step 3f).

Potential solution

Consider increasing the dose of the [3H]-2DG tracer to enhance detection sensitivity and reduce the impact of individual variability.

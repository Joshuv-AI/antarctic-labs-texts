Neurological immune-related adverse events after ICI treatment: a protocol for an overview of systematic reviews
Authors: Mai Erritzøe-Jervild, Christian Gunge Riberholt, Anders Hougaard, Christian Stenør, Inge Marie Svane, Christina Kruuse, Markus Harboe Olsen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have become an important treatment option for selected cancer patients in recent years. In this overview of systematic reviews, we aim to present a comprehensive summary of the evidence on neurological immune-related adverse events (irAEs) in patients treated with ICIs.Methods and analysisThe overview will follow the preferred reporting items for overview of reviews statement. All systematic reviews reporting on neurological irAEs in adult patients treated with ICIs will be included. We will search PubMed, EMBASE and Cochrane’s Database of Systematic Reviews. Two authors will independently screen studies and extract data, with disagreements resolved by discussion or a third reviewer. Methodological quality will be assessed using AMSTAR-2. We will assess overlap by identifying shared primary studies and summarising their frequency in a table. Data will be presented narratively and in tables; when possible, frequencies of neurological irAEs and weighted means, along with associated distributions, will be reported.Ethics and disseminationAs this study involves secondary analysis of published literature and does not include primary data collection, ethical approval is not required. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251020892.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have gained a great impact as a treatment for selected cancer patients within the past decade.1 2 The treatment boosts the immune system to attack the cancer cells. Treatment with ICIs has improved survival for some cancer types—including malignant melanoma, renal-cell cancer and non-small-cell lung cancer.3 Unfortunately, this treatment can cause immune-related adverse events (irAEs) due to the overactivation of the immune system, which can affect all organs in the body.4 5 Neurological irAEs are rare but represent some of the most severe types of adverse events, with a high mortality rate.6 7 The neurological irAEs can affect both the central and peripheral nervous systems. These include encephalitis, meningitis, vasculitis, neuropathy and myositis.8 9 Neurological irAEs are difficult to treat, and current management is extrapolated from overall autoimmune disease treatment principles.10 11 However, this approach is not always effective, and many suffer from serious disabilities as a consequence of the treatment.12 13

Throughout the years, many case reports and small observational studies have been published focusing on neurological irAEs. These reports have continuously been gathered in reviews—with the focus on one or a few subtypes of neurological irAEs.1416

In this overview of systematic reviews, we aim to provide a complete summary of the body of evidence of neurological irAEs in patients treated with ICIs to identify gaps in the existing evidence where further research is needed.

Method

This protocol and the subsequent review will be based on the preferred reporting items for systematic review and meta-analysis protocols (PRISMA-P) and PRISMA guidelines and checklists;17 18 furthermore, on the preferred reporting items for overview of review statement developed for overviews of systematic reviews19 and the Cochrane Handbook recommendations for Cochrane Overviews of Reviews.20 We have established a review team prior to initiation, including two experts in methodology, two board-certified neurologists—one professor in neurology and one with extensive clinical expertise regarding neurological irAEs—and one board-certified oncologist and professor with expertise regarding ICI treatment. A protocol was uploaded at PROSPERO prior to search initiation (PROSPERO 2025 CRD420251020892, 5 June 2025).

Objectives

The primary purpose of this overview is to compile and summarise existing knowledge on neurological irAEs associated with ICIs. We will address the following research question, formulated using the PICO framework: what are the clinical manifestations and frequencies (Outcome) of neurological irAEs (Intervention) occurring in cancer patients treated with ICIs (Population), regardless of potential comparison group (Comparison), based on systematic reviews (Study type)? (table 1). We will collect and analyse all data regarding neurological irAEs. We will describe their symptomatology, the objective findings and paraclinical results. Furthermore, we will present their management and outcome and report the frequency.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria/study types

We will include all systematic reviews written in English reporting on neurological irAEs in patients with cancer treated with ICIs. This consists of both adverse events in the central and peripheral nervous system and includes the following diagnoses: encephalitis, aseptic meningitis, CNS vasculitis, myelitis, neuropathy, myasthenia syndrome and myositis. We will only include reviews from patients 18 years and above. Primary studies will not be included in this systematic review of reviews.

Systematic reviews addressing adverse events in general without a specific focus on neurological effects will be excluded. Systematic reviews concerning myocarditis that also mention myositis will be screened to determine their eligibility for inclusion. Systematic reviews addressing rheumatologic adverse effects will also undergo full-text screening to ascertain whether they include cases of myositis. Narrative reviews, expert opinions, scoping reviews and other non-systematic evidence syntheses will be excluded.

Searching strategy

We will perform a comprehensive search in PubMed, EMBASE and Cochrane’s Database of Systematic Reviews. Before initiation, we searched PROSPERO for similar studies. We will search the reference lists of the included reviews identified in the initial search. We will limit the search period from the first approval of ICIs—that is, 2011.21 The detailed search strings are presented in the online supplemental material.

Two authors will independently screen the titles and abstracts identified from the search using Covidence (Covidence, Melbourne, Australia). Discrepancies will be solved by discussion and consensus. After the initial screening of titles and abstracts, the full text version will be added for the potentially eligible studies. Then, two independent authors will assess the eligibility of the full text articles. Conflicts will first be investigated by the two screeners, and if agreement cannot be reached, a third party will decide. The process will be reported in a PRISMA flow diagram. The search will be conducted between 1 July 2025 and 31 October 2025.

Data extraction

A pre-designed data extraction sheet will be developed. We will pilot the extraction sheet on at least five articles to assess the usability before full extraction is commenced. Two reviewers will extract data separately. Any discrepancies will first be resolved through discussion between the two reviewers. If consensus cannot be reached, a third senior reviewer will adjudicate the disagreement.

The following will be extracted: title, aim, search strategy used, number of studies included and total number of participants, study types included in review (table 2). Furthermore, we will extract specific data about the neurological irAEs such as type of neurological irAEs, treatment, outcome, number of ICI cycles and paraclinical results.

The included papers will be categorised based on the type of neurological irAEs or if they report on overall neurological irAEs. Furthermore, papers addressing management and outcomes will also be placed in a separate category.

Quality assessment

Each of the included papers will be evaluated for risk of bias using AMSTAR-2.22 AMSTAR-2 is developed and used to assess the quality of the systematic reviews. Conclusions will primarily be based on reviews with moderate to high quality.23 This is to ensure optimal quality of the synthesised evidence. Two reviewers will conduct the risk of bias assessment independently and subsequently compare their findings. As this overview summarises findings from existing systematic reviews rather than primary studies, and given the expected heterogeneity in outcome reporting, formal grading of recommendations assessment, development and evaluation (GRADE) assessment will not be feasible. The strength of evidence will instead be discussed narratively in relation to the methodological quality of the included reviews (AMSTAR-2).

Overlapping primary studies

Since we are conducting an overview of systematic reviews, there is a risk of overlapping data. Thus, whenever multiple reviews provide overlapping information, we will evaluate the degree of overlapping primary studies and present it in a table showing how many times each primary study appears. We will calculate the corrected covered area to quantify the extent of overlap in meta-reviews.24 This will help us detect whether the overlap is broad or limited. When substantial overlap is identified, data from the most recent and methodologically robust review (as judged by AMSTAR-2 ratings) will be prioritised. If two reviews are equivalent in quality, the review with the broader scope or larger sample size will be retained for quantitative synthesis, and overlapping data will be discussed narratively to avoid double-counting.

Data analysis

The results will be summarised for all neurological irAEs and for each of the different neurological irAEs. Whenever possible, we will present a weighted overall frequency of neurological irAEs, and frequencies for each of the different neurological irAEs, for example, encephalitis, meningitis and so forth. Similarly, weighted frequencies for categorical and binomial variables—such as sex or mortality—will be presented using weighted frequencies or distributions. Furthermore, in all of these groups, we will present data about symptomatology, paraclinical results, onset of symptoms, type of ICI, management of neurological irAEs and outcome (table 3). For continuous descriptive outcome-related variables—such as age or paraclinical results—we will present weighted means and distributions. To account for the above-mentioned overlapping primary studies, we will only include unique participants in the weighted frequencies, means and distributions. If we are unsure that participants are truly unique, they will be excluded from the analysis. However, due to the presumed heterogeneity of the reviews, if the abovementioned weighted calculations are not possible, data will be presented qualitatively in narrative and tabular form. If data permit, quantitative synthesis will use inverse-variance weighted random-effects pooling for continuous and proportion outcomes.

Discussion

To our knowledge, although many systematic reviews exist, no overview summarising their results has been conducted. We hope this overview will identify gaps and limitations in the knowledge about neurological irAEs and thereby help direct future research on this topic. It will help clinicians to access evidence quickly instead of scoping through all reviews on the topic.

Knowledge from multiple systematic reviews is essential for a comprehensive understanding of neurological irAEs, particularly in the evolving landscape of immunotherapy. Single systematic reviews may focus on specific cancer types,25 specific ICI26 or specific neurological toxicities,27 resulting in fragmented insight. Comparing and integrating findings across reviews will help us identify broader patterns such as shared symptoms, paraclinical results, common diagnostic challenges and effective management strategies.28 Furthermore, we will also be able to report discrepancies in, for example, reported incidence rates.

The growing use of ICIs will possibly lead to a rise in neurological irAEs. They can mimic other neurological conditions.29 A well-documented body of knowledge will enable clinicians to recognise early signs, helping avoid misdiagnosis and treatment delays. A comprehensive understanding can contribute to the development of evidence-based clinical guidelines.

Strengths and limitations

This overview includes only systematic reviews, excluding narrative and non-systematic reviews to ensure methodological rigour. A major issue in conducting an overview of systematic reviews is that the primary studies included in the reviews may overlap, and caution is warranted to minimise overinterpretation of the results. This can lead to overinterpretation and biased results. We will cross-check the included reviews to detect repeated primary studies.30 This will make the results more transparent and objective. Overlap between included reviews will be assessed and managed using a predefined hierarchy prioritising recency and methodological quality. Furthermore, the methodological rigour might result in missing potentially relevant information from studies not included in systematic reviews, and from non-systematic reviews. Moreover, the study is limited to English-language publications, which may introduce language bias and omit relevant evidence reported in other languages. Finally, the overview does not apply a formal GRADE assessment of certainty, as heterogeneity across included reviews may preclude consistent evaluation.

There is no specific guideline on how to do an overview of reviews. This protocol is outlined in detail to exemplify how to perform an overview so that it may be possible to replicate an overview of systematic reviews in the future. By this, we contribute to the current guidelines on overviews of reviews. A clear protocol will help plan and structure a systematic data collection and analysis. Furthermore, clearly defined methods and objectives reduce bias and allow for a more objective assessment of findings.

Abstract
. Turbulent behavior of the two-parameter family of generalized surface quasigeostrophic equations is examined both rigorously and numerically. We adapt a cascade mechanism argument to derive an energy spectrum that scales as κ 2 ​ β / 3 − 3 \kappa^{2\beta/3-3} where β \beta controls the regularity of the velocity ( β = 1 \beta=1 in the special case of the SQG). Direct numerical simulations indicate that this fits better than κ β / 3 − 3 \kappa^{\beta/3-3} which was derived in [ 44 ] . Guided by earlier work on the 2D Navier–Stokes equations, we prove a certain condition implies a direct cascade of enstrophy, as well as an upper bound on the enstrophy dissipation rate, and sharp bounds on a dissipation wavenumber. The dependence of these rigorous results on the two parameters is demonstrated numerically.

1.
Introduction

We study the turbulent behavior of the generalized surface quasi-geostrophic equation (gSQG) over the domain
\Omega=[0,L]^{2}
. The gSQG equation is given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta+\gamma\Lambda^{\alpha}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=g,\quad u=\nabla^{\perp}\psi\overset{\text{def}}{=}(-\partial_{x_{2}}\psi,\partial_{x_{1}}\psi),\quad\Delta\psi=\kappa_{0}^{-\beta}\Lambda^{\beta}\theta,

where
0\leq\beta<2
,
0<\alpha\leq 2
, and
\kappa_{0}=2\pi/L
. The driving force
g
is time-independent and given; this acts as a large-scale energy source to sustain turbulent behavior. The damping parameter,
\gamma
, is positive, so the fractional laplacian,
\Lambda^{\alpha}
, acts as a small-scale energy sink; recall that
\Lambda\overset{\text{def}}{=}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Lastly, we equip (
1.1
) with periodic boundary conditions and assume that
\theta
,
g
are mean-free over
\Omega
. Note that the factor
\kappa_{0}^{-\beta}
is included in the constitutive law so that
u
retains the dimensions of velocity and the overall dimensional consistency of the equation is maintained. When
\alpha=2
and
\beta=0
, the equation reduces to the vorticity formulation of the Navier–Stokes equations (NSE), while fixing
\beta=1
yields the special case of the surface quasi-geostrophic equation (SQG).

The theories for two-dimensional turbulence by Batchelor, Kraichnan, and Leith

[
1
,
36
,
39
]
are akin to that for 3D by Kolmogorov
[
35
]
in that they
are derived through scaling arguments without direct use of the equations of motion. The main difference is that in 2D there are two invariances, one for energy and one for enstrophy, which enable cascades of both quantities toward larger and smaller scales, respectively. Foias
[
20
]
made explicit use of the Navier-Stokes equations to provide rigorous support for certain elements of these theories. That approach was continued in
[
22
,
18
]
, which contain arguments that are modified here for some of our results for
the gSQG.

The theoretical study of the inviscid gSQG family was introduced to the mathematical community in
[
4
]
, where the weak solution theory, local strong solution theory, and local theory for the corresponding patch problem were initially developed. Since then, several works have subsequently refined the understanding of when the initial value problem corresponding to (
1.1
) and associated modifications are well-posed
[
8
,
5
,
26
,
34
,
47
,
24
,
28
,
7
]
or ill-posed
[
33
,
37
,
27
,
7
,
17
,
9
]
in various contexts, as well as the construction of global solutions
[
42
,
43
,
16
,
25
,
46
,
3
]
. The global issue of whether solutions emanating from smooth initial data blow-up in finite time remains an outstanding open problem. On the other hand, in the presence of fractional dissipation, global regularity has been resolved within a subset of the family
[
45
,
15
,
11
]
, most notably in the presence of so-called
critical dissipation
, where the dissipation power is related to the constitutive law in a particular way
[
32
,
31
,
2
,
14
,
12
,
38
,
41
]
. Generally speaking, the dissipative SQG family whose turbulent behavior we study in this article is known to be locally well-posed for large initial data and globally well-posed for small initial data
[
40
,
6
,
29
]
.

The particular features
of turbulence
considered in this paper are the energy spectrum, a direct cascade of enstrophy, and a 2D analogue of Kolmogorov’s dissipation law. There are two wavenumbers that play critical roles:
\kappa_{\eta}
where the spectrum is expected to start decaying exponentially and
\kappa_{\sigma}
, a Dirichlet quotient which can determine the extent of the cascade range. The relevant physical quantities are rescaled from the NSE case in terms of
\alpha
and
\beta
which also appear in the relations between them.
We first use the Richardson/Kraichnan cascade mechanism to derive a power law for the energy spectrum reflecting this rescaling. That is followed by an estimate which guarantees a pronounced direct cascade of energy from the forcing scale to a fraction of
\kappa_{\sigma}
. We then show rigorously that if the power law holds, other features of turbulence follow. The first is that
\kappa_{\sigma}
is comparable to
\kappa_{\eta}
, up to a factor that scales as
\beta^{-1}
. The second
is that both wave numbers should scale as

G^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
in the Grashof number
G
.

Two results are proved without assuming the spectrum. One is the general bound
G^{\frac{1}{3\alpha}}\lesssim\kappa_{\eta}/\kappa_{0}\lesssim G^{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}
. The other is one side of the dissipation law, namely
\eta\lesssim U^{3}/L^{3}
, where
\eta
is the enstrophy dissipation rate and
U
is a suitably scaled quantity that reduces to the root mean square velocity in the case of the NSE.
We note that while most of the proofs we provide are rescaled adaptations of results for the NSE case in
[
22
,
18
]
. However, the proofs of dissipation law
\eta\lesssim U^{3}/L^{3}
, as well as the tighter bounds on
\kappa_{\eta}
,
\kappa_{\sigma}
involve a more delicate estimate of the nonlinear term involving commutators. Some technical background for this approach is included in the Appendix.

Although there is no known rigorous derivation of the power law for the spectrum, it has been observed in countless numerical studies of the NSE. We present here high-resolution simulations (up to
32,768^{2}
collocation points) to test the rescaled power law for the gSQG. We note where in the
\alpha,\beta
-plane the spectrum for gSQG starts to deviate from the heuristically predicted power law and in turn the extent to which the relations that would follow from that law fail. We consistently find a marked breakdown when we cross a critical line where the gSQG changes from being quasilinear to fully nonlinear.

2.
Mathematical Preliminaries

We identify the domain
\Omega
with the two-dimensional torus:

\displaystyle\Omega=[0,L]^{2}=\mathbb{T}^{2}

and in the analysis retain the factor
\kappa_{0}
to help track the dependence of physical dimensions line-to-line.
We denote the phase space of (
1.1
) by
H
, which we define to be the subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
of real-valued, scalar functions which are mean-zero over
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Within this space-periodic setting, we make use the standard Fourier series framework. Thus,
H
can be characterized as

H:=\left\{\theta=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\hat{\theta}_{k}e^{i{\kappa_{0}}k\cdot x}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):\hat{\theta}_{k}\in\mathbb{C},\ \hat{\theta}_{\vec{0}}=0,\ \hat{\theta}_{-k}=\overline{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\right\},

where
\hat{\theta}_{k}
denotes the Fourier coefficient of
\theta
at wavenumber
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. The scalar product in
H
is simply the
L^{2}
-inner product and we will denote by

(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\theta_{1}(x)\theta_{2}(x)dx.

We will denote the associated norm in
H
by

|\theta|=(\theta,\theta)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\theta^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Parseval’s identity can be read as

{|\theta|}^{2}=4\pi^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|{\hat{\theta}_{k}}|^{2}\text{ as well as }(\theta,\theta^{\prime})=4\pi^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}{\hat{\theta}_{k}}\cdot{\hat{\theta}_{-k}^{\prime}}

for
\theta^{\prime}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}{\hat{\theta}_{k}^{\prime}}e^{ik\cdot x}
.

The fractional laplacian operator
\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is self-adjoint and can be defined spectrally. Its eigenvalues are of the form
{\kappa_{0}}|k|
where
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
; the eigenvalues are denoted and arranged as
0<\lambda_{0}=1\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...
, where they are counted according to their multiplicities. Let
w_{0},w_{1},w_{2},...
be the corresponding normalized eigenvectors, i.e.,
|w_{i}|=1
, for all
j
. Then for each
\theta\in H
, we have

\displaystyle{\theta(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\hat{\theta}_{k}e^{i\kappa_{0}k\cdotp x}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\theta,w_{j})w_{j}(x).}

For
\sigma\geq 0
, the positive powers of
\Lambda
are defined by linearity through

\Lambda^{\sigma}w_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}w_{j},\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ j=0,1,2,...

We define projectors
P_{\kappa}:H\to\text{span}\{w_{j}:\lambda_{j}\leq\kappa\}
by

(2.1)

P_{\kappa}\theta=\sum_{|k|\leq\kappa}\hat{\theta}_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}

with
Q_{\kappa}=I-P_{\kappa}
. In our analysis, it will be useful to consider components of
\theta
within a range in wavenumbers, so we define

\theta_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}=(P_{\kappa^{\prime}}-P_{\kappa})\theta

for
0\leq\kappa<\kappa^{\prime}
, with the convention that
\theta_{\kappa,\infty}=\theta_{\kappa}={Q_{\kappa}\theta}
for all
0\leq\kappa<1
. Note that

\displaystyle\theta_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}=Q_{\kappa}P_{\kappa^{\prime}}\theta=P_{\kappa^{\prime}}Q_{\kappa}\theta.

Lastly, recalling that
u=\nabla^{\perp}\psi
, and is
thus divergence-free, we note that the nonlinear term satisfies orthogonality relations similar to ones known for the NSE. In particular, for
u,\theta,w
sufficiently smooth, one has

(2.2)

\displaystyle(u\cdotp\nabla\theta,w)=-(u\cdotp\nabla w,\theta)

and hence

(2.3)

\displaystyle(u\cdotp\nabla\theta,\theta)=0\;.

Moreover, from the vector identity
\mathbf{v}^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{w}=-\mathbf{w}^{\perp}\cdot\mathbf{v}
and (
2.2
) one has

(2.4)

(u\cdot\nabla\theta,\psi)=(\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdot\nabla\theta,\psi)=-(\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdot\nabla\psi,\theta)=0.

2.1.
Apriori Estimates

Proceeding formally, if we multiply (
1.1
) by
-\psi
(respectively,
\theta
), then integrate over
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and apply (
2.3
), we find that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2}{2}}\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle=(g,-\psi)

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|\theta|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle=(g,\theta)\;.

We define

\frac{1}{L^{2}}|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta|^{2}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\text{2 times the total ``energy" per unit mass}

and

\frac{1}{L^{2}}|\theta|^{2}\overset{\text{def}}{=}{2\ \text{times}\ }\text{the total ``enstrophy" per unit mass}.

Note that in the context of the NSE, when
\beta=0
, these match the conserved quantities of energy,
\frac{1}{L^{2}}|u|^{2}
, and enstrophy,
\frac{1}{L^{2}}|\omega|^{2}
, respectively, (per unit mass) where
\theta
is interpreted as the fluid vorticity
\omega=\nabla^{\perp}\cdotp u
.

The relations (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) are the balance equations for the energy and enstrophy, respectively. Applying the Poincaré, Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities to (
2.6
), we find that

(2.7)

\frac{d}{dt}|\theta|^{2}+\kappa_{0}^{\alpha}\gamma|\theta|^{2}\leq\frac{d}{dt}|\theta|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\leq\frac{|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|^{2}}{\gamma},

so that the Gronwall lemma gives

(2.8)

{\sup_{t\geq t_{*}}|\theta(t)|^{2}}\leq 2\frac{|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}\kappa_{0}^{\alpha}}

for
t_{*}
sufficiently large, depending on
\gamma,\kappa_{0},\alpha,|\theta_{0}|,|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|
.

Arguing similarly, we derive

\frac{d}{dt}|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2}{2}}\theta|^{2}\leq\frac{|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-\alpha-2}{2}}g|^{2}}{\gamma},

from which we deduce

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq t_{*}}|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta(t)|^{2}\leq 2\frac{|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-\alpha-2}{2}}g|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}\kappa_{0}^{\alpha}}

for sufficiently large
t_{*}
, depending on
\gamma,\kappa_{0},\alpha,|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta_{0}|,|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-\alpha-2}{2}}g|
.

The inequalities in (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) can equivalently be expressed in terms of the dimensionless
Grashof numbers

G
,
{G_{*}}
as

|\theta|\leq 2\gamma\kappa_{0}^{\alpha-1}G\quad\text{and}\quad|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta|\leq{2}\gamma\kappa_{0}^{\frac{2\alpha+\beta-4}{2}}G_{*}

where

(2.10)

G\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|}{\gamma^{2}\kappa_{0}^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{2}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad G_{*}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-\alpha-2}{2}}g|}{\gamma^{2}\kappa_{0}^{\frac{3\alpha+\beta-4}{2}}}.

We note that

(2.11)

\left(\frac{\underline{\kappa}}{\kappa_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}G_{*}\leq G\leq\left(\frac{\bar{\kappa}}{\kappa_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2-\beta}{2}}G_{*}

whenever
0<\underline{\kappa}<\bar{\kappa}
and
g
satisfies

(2.12)

g=\sum_{{\underline{\kappa}}<|k|\leq\bar{\kappa}}\hat{g}_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}=g_{\underline{\kappa},\bar{\kappa}}.

In other words, (
2.11
) holds whenever the external driving force
g
has finite spectral support.

2.2.
Some Remarks on the Mathematical Framework

It is common in the physics and engineering literature to assume for turbulent flows that the time averages of physically relevant quantities exist and are independent of the initial condition. In the sequel, we adopt this view and define

\langle\Phi(\theta)\rangle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(S(\tau)\theta_{0})\ d\tau\;.

While it is conceivable that this limit would not exist, it can be replaced with a generalized limit, denoted
\lim
, that is guaranteed to exist by the Hahn-Banach theorem and matches the ordinary limit when it does exist. Moreover, the
\lim
functional can typically be expressed as an integral with respect to an invariant measure supported on the global attractor of the system provided that the global attractor exists. Thus, in contexts where a global attractor theory is available, these technical adjustments make averaging over solutions mathematically rigorous.

In the particular case of the 2D NSE, such a framework exists and a systematic study of the Kraichnan theory from a first principles perspective can indeed be developed. We refer the reader to
[
22
]
for such a study and additional details regarding this rigorous framework. We do not address these concerns in the paper, although we remark that global attractors for certain subsets of the gSQG family of equations have been established, namely the critical and subcritical regimes of dissipative SQG, i.e.,
\beta=1
,
\alpha\in[1,2]
, in
[
30
,
13
]
. To our best knowledge, it remains an open direction to develop the global attractor theory for the gSQG family in general.

We conclude these remarks by emphasizing that, for our purposes, the absence of such a framework does not alter our formal calculations. In fact, we point out that any theory of weak solutions for which global-in-time existence can be guaranteed from arbitrary initial data in
H
and for which the energy balance (
2.5
) and enstrophy balance (
2.6
) hold with equality is sufficient to justify the analysis performed in paper. We will not pursue the development of this solution theory here and instead focus on the consequences for turbulence that emanate from these putative solutions. Lastly, regarding our numerical results, the time-averages that are calculated in our experiments are computed by simply taking
t
large. The inherent error due
to implementing finite time averages can then be estimated in terms of the Grashof number (see
[
21
]
for details in the case of 2D NSE).

3.
Energy spectrum

3.1.
Heuristic derivation

The energy spectrum, which describes how energy varies over different length scales, plays a central role in turbulence. Although it is widely observed in experiments and numerical simulations to satisfy a universal power law, a rigorous derivation, which would have to depend on the nature of the force, is not known even in the case of the NSE. However, there are several heuristic arguments for it. We carry out one adapted from Kraichnan’s 2D interpretation of Richardson’s cascade mechanism
[
36
]
. To start, based on (
2.6
), we define the
total enstrophy dissipation rate per unit mass
as

(3.1)

\eta=\frac{\gamma}{L^{2}}\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle\;.

Let

e_{\kappa}=\text{2 times the average energy per unit mass of the eddies of linear size }\mathit{l}\in\left[\frac{1}{2\kappa},\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)\;.

In terms of the solution of the gSQG equation, the quantity
e_{\kappa}
can be expressed as

(3.2)

e_{\kappa}={\frac{1}{L^{2}}}\big\langle\,|\Lambda^{\tfrac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta_{\kappa,2\kappa}|^{2}\,\big\rangle.

An analogue of the average velocity of eddies of size
l
is defined by

U_{\kappa}={L^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}e_{\kappa}^{1/2}={L^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}}\big\langle\,|\Lambda^{\tfrac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta_{\kappa,2\kappa}|^{2}\,\big\rangle^{1/2}.

Correspondingly, the average time for these eddies to travel a distance of order
l
is given by

t_{\kappa}=\frac{l}{U_{\kappa}}\sim\frac{1}{\kappa U_{\kappa}}\sim\frac{\kappa_{0}^{\beta/2}}{\kappa e_{\kappa}^{1/2}}.

The average enstrophy per unit mass associated with scale
l
is defined as

E_{\kappa}=\kappa^{2-\beta}e_{\kappa},

which yields the enstrophy dissipation rate for eddies of length
l
:

\eta_{\kappa}\sim\frac{E_{\kappa}}{t_{\kappa}}\sim\kappa_{0}^{-\beta/2}\,\kappa^{3-\beta}\,e_{\kappa}^{3/2}.

In particular, this implies that

\displaystyle e_{\kappa}\sim\eta_{\kappa}^{2/3}\,\kappa_{0}^{\beta/3}\,\kappa^{\tfrac{2\beta}{3}-2}

We recall that within the inertial range of wavenumbers, it is expected that the relation
\eta_{\kappa}\approx\eta
holds. Upon assuming that
\eta_{\kappa}\approx\eta
holds, one may deduce

(3.3)

e_{\kappa}\sim\eta^{2/3}\,\kappa_{0}^{\beta/3}\,\kappa^{\tfrac{2\beta}{3}-2}.

With this observation in hand, we will now use (
3.3
) to
define
the “inertial range.” In particular, we define
inertial range of wavenumbers
to be the interval of wavenumbers of amplitude
\kappa
over which (
3.3
) holds. Note that this power law depends explicitly on
\beta
, but it depends on
\alpha
only implicitly through
\eta
.

The time average

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{L^{2}}|\Lambda^{\sigma}(P_{\kappa^{\prime}}-P_{\kappa})\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}S(\tau)\theta_{0}|^{2}d\tau.

can be written as a Riemann sum (as
L\to\infty
, or equivalently
\kappa_{0}\to 0
) for an integral in the wavenumbers

(3.4)

\int_{\kappa}^{\kappa^{\prime}}\chi^{2\sigma}\mathcal{E}(\chi)\ d\chi

of some function
\mathcal{E}
, called the
energy spectrum
of the turbulent flow sustained by the force
g
. The spectrum is related to the average energy per unit mass
e_{\kappa}
through

\int_{\kappa}^{2\kappa}\mathcal{E}(\chi)d\chi\sim e_{\kappa}.

Thus, by (
3.3
),
it is expected to satisfy

(3.5)

\mathcal{E}(\kappa)\sim\eta^{2/3}\kappa^{2\beta/{3}-3}.

in the inertial range.

Let
{\kappa_{\textrm{d}}}
denote the wavenumber cut-off where inertial effects achieve a sustained balance with small-scale viscous effects. While a precise expression for this wavenumber is not known, one may again use dimensional analysis to establish a putative relationship to known quantities. This is done by assuming that
\kappa_{\text{d}}
depends on only
\gamma
and
\eta
though some function
\varphi
:

{\kappa_{\textrm{d}}}=\varphi(\gamma,\eta).

We consider the following rescaling of
\kappa_{d}^{\prime}
:

{\kappa_{\textrm{d}}^{\prime}}=\varphi(\gamma^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}),\quad{\kappa_{\textrm{d}}}=\frac{{\kappa_{\textrm{d}}^{\prime}}}{\xi},\quad\gamma^{\prime}=\frac{\xi^{\alpha}}{\tau}\gamma,\quad\eta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\tau^{3}}\eta.

It follows that

\frac{1}{\xi}\varphi(\gamma^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})=\varphi(\xi^{\alpha}\tau^{-1}\gamma,\frac{1}{\tau^{3}}\eta)\;.

Thus, if we choose
\frac{\xi^{\alpha}}{\tau}=\frac{1}{\gamma}
and
\tau^{3}=\eta
, we obtain

(3.6)

{\kappa_{\textrm{d}}}=\varphi(\gamma,\eta)=\xi\varphi(1,1)\sim\left(\frac{\eta}{\gamma^{3}}\right)^{1/(3\alpha)}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\kappa_{\eta}\;.

In contrast to the energy spectrum power law,
\kappa_{\eta}
depends explicitly on
\alpha
, while the dependence on
\beta
remains implicit through
\eta
.

If we assume that

\mathcal{E}(\kappa)\sim\varphi(\eta,\kappa)

holds in an inertial range of wavenumbers, for some function
\varphi
, a similar dimensional argument results in the spectrum

(3.7)

{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(\kappa)\sim\eta^{2/3}\kappa^{\beta-3}\;.

Yet another dimensional analysis by Pierrehumbert, Held, and Swanson
[
44
]
based on locality in scale results in

(3.8)

{\mathcal{E}_{PHS}}(\kappa)\sim\eta^{2/3}\kappa^{\beta/3-3}\;.

All three energy spectra, (
3.5
), (
3.7
), (
3.8
), are consistent with Kraichnan’s
\kappa^{-3}
spectrum
in the special case
\beta=0
corresponding to the 2D NSE.

N
modes in each direction. For the NSE we use

and for the gSQG

. The force is restricted to

We randomly assign Fourier coefficients to these modes, choosing values from

4.
The Enstrophy Cascade

4.1.
Analytical Support

Let
p_{\kappa}=P_{\kappa}\theta\text{ and }q_{\kappa}=Q_{\kappa}\theta
.
Taking the
H
-inner product of (
1.1
) with
p_{\kappa}
and applying the first relation in (
2.3
),
we obtain

(4.1)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|p_{\kappa}|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}p_{\kappa}|^{2}&=(P_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla p_{\kappa},q_{\kappa})-(Q_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla q_{\kappa},p_{\kappa})+(g,p_{\kappa})\\
&=-L^{2}\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}+(g,p_{\kappa}).\end{split}

While taking the
H
-inner product of (
1.1
) instead with
q_{\kappa}
, we find

(4.2)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|q_{\kappa}|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}q_{\kappa}|^{2}&=(Q_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla q_{\kappa},p_{\kappa})-(P_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla p_{\kappa},q_{\kappa})+(g,q_{\kappa})\\
&=L^{2}\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}+(g,q_{\kappa}),\end{split}

In both of the above energy balances,
\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}
is the quantity defined by

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}\overset{\text{def}}{=}(Q_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla q_{\kappa},p_{\kappa})-(P_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla p_{\kappa},q_{\kappa}),

and it is interpreted to be the
net rate of enstrophy transfer
(or
net enstrophy flux
) at the wavenumber
\kappa
, from the low modes to the high modes.

Similarly, upon taking
H
-inner product of (
1.1
) with
-P_{\kappa}\psi
(respectively
-Q_{\kappa}\psi
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}P_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2}{2}}P_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}=-L^{2}\mathfrak{e}_{\kappa}+(g,P_{\kappa}\Lambda^{\beta-2}\theta)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}Q_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2}{2}}Q_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}=L^{2}\mathfrak{e}_{\kappa}+(g,Q_{\kappa}\Lambda^{\beta-2}\theta)\;,

where
\mathfrak{e}_{\kappa}
is defined by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{e}_{\kappa}\overset{\text{def}}{=}(Q_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla q_{\kappa},\Lambda^{\beta-2}p_{\kappa})-(P_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla p_{\kappa},\Lambda^{\beta-2}q_{\kappa}).

Then
\mathfrak{e}_{\kappa}
is interpreted as the
net rate of energy transfer
(or
net energy flux
) at the wavenumber
\kappa
from low modes to high modes. For additional details in deriving (
4.4
), the reader is referred to Appendix
A
.

The following result states that for wavenumbers smaller than the injection range of the force, both enstrophy and energy are transferred to smaller wavenumbers. The proof is a straightforward adaptation of the analogous result for the NSE which can be found in
[
22
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\kappa\leq{\underline{\kappa}}
, then the time average of net fluxes (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) satisfy

(4.5)

\langle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}(\theta)\rangle=-\frac{\gamma}{L^{2}}\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}P_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}\rangle

and

(4.6)

\langle\mathfrak{e}_{\kappa}(\theta)\rangle=-\frac{\gamma}{L^{2}}\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2}{2}}P_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}\rangle.

Proof.

We take the time average of (
4.1
) to obtain

\frac{1}{2t}(|p_{\kappa}(t)|^{2}-|p_{\kappa}(0)|^{2})+\frac{\gamma}{t}\int_{0}^{t}|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}p_{\kappa}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau=-\frac{L^{2}}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}(\theta(\tau))d\tau.

We take
t\to\infty
and by the boundedness of
{|p_{\kappa}|}
, find

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\gamma}{t}\int_{0}^{t}|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}p_{\kappa}(\tau)|^{2}d\tau=\lim_{t\to\infty}-\frac{L^{2}}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}(\theta(\tau))d\tau

from which (
4.5
) follows.
A similar procedure applied to (
4.2
) yields (
4.6
).
∎

At wavenumbers larger than the injection range, the direction of transfer switches to larger wavenumbers.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
\kappa\geq\bar{\kappa}
, then the time average of net fluxes satisfy

\langle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}(\theta)\rangle=\frac{\gamma}{L^{2}}\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}Q_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}\rangle

and

\langle\mathfrak{e}_{\kappa}(\theta)\rangle=\frac{\gamma}{L^{2}}\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2}{2}}Q_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}\rangle\;.

Proof.

One may argue in a similar fashion to the proof of Proposition
4.1
, except that one instead considers the high-mode energy (respectively enstrophy) balance; we omit the details.
∎

By a
direct cascade
of enstrophy, we mean that the net enstrophy flux,
\langle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}\rangle
, is positive (“direct”) and roughly constant (“cascade”) over some range in
\kappa
. That it is positive above the injection scale determined by
\bar{\kappa}
is guaranteed by Proposition
4.2
. The expectation is that the enstrophy injected at
\bar{\kappa}
should match the total that is dissipated, so that
\langle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}\rangle\approx\eta
over this range. The extent to which this approximation holds then quantifies how pronounced the direct enstrophy cascade is. In contrast, an
inverse cascade
refers to a roughly constant transfer towards smaller wavenumbers from higher wavenumbers. Proposition
4.1
guarantees the correct direction of
energy
transfer below the injection scale determined by
\underline{\kappa}
, namely, that the net energy flux,
\langle\mathfrak{e}_{\kappa}\rangle
, is negative for
\kappa\leq\underline{\kappa}
.
Although Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
indicate that both energy and enstrophy are directed in the same way through scales, a more detailed analysis in the case of the NSE in
[
22
]
shows that the direct cascade of enstrophy is actually more pronounced than that of energy, while the inverse cascade of energy is more pronounced than that of enstrophy. In what follows, we will focus on the
direct cascade of enstrophy
.

Let

(4.7)

\kappa_{\sigma}=\left(\frac{\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle}{\langle|\theta|^{2}\rangle}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

We claim that this wavenumber can serve as an indicator for a strongly pronounced direct enstrophy cascade.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
{\kappa>\bar{\kappa}}
we have

(4.8)

1-\left(\frac{\kappa}{\kappa_{\sigma}}\right)^{\alpha}\leq\frac{\langle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}\rangle}{\eta}\leq 1.

Hence, given
\delta\in(0,1]
, and
\kappa\in(\bar{\kappa},\delta^{1/\alpha}\kappa_{\sigma}]
,

0\leq 1-\frac{\langle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}\rangle}{\eta}\leq\delta.

In particular, this implies that

\langle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}\rangle\approx\eta
over the range
{\bar{\kappa}<\kappa\ll\kappa_{\sigma}}
, whenever
{\kappa_{\sigma}\gg\bar{\kappa}}
. The proof of Proposition

4.3
is another straightforward adaptation of one for the NSE case in
[
22
]
. Since it is brief, we include it for completeness.

Proof.

Note that by the Bernstein inequalities (Lemma
B.1
)

\gamma\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}P_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}\rangle\leq\gamma\kappa^{\alpha}\langle|P_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}\rangle\leq\gamma\kappa^{\alpha}\langle|\theta|^{2}\rangle\leq\gamma\left(\frac{\kappa}{\kappa_{\sigma}}\right)^{\alpha}\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle

and consequently, for
\kappa>\bar{\kappa}
we obtain that

L^{2}\langle\mathfrak{E}_{\kappa}\rangle=\gamma\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}Q_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}\rangle=\gamma\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle-\gamma\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}P_{\kappa}\theta|^{2}\rangle\geq\gamma\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle\left(1-\left(\frac{\kappa}{\kappa_{\sigma}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)\;.

∎

5.
Relating
\kappa_{\sigma}
to
\kappa_{\eta}

5.1.
A rigorous result

If we quantify the similarity in the spectral power law by specifying a prefactor,
c_{\text{Kr}}
, up to which the following holds

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\kappa)\approx c_{Kr}\eta^{\frac{2}{3}}\kappa_{0}^{\frac{\beta}{3}}\kappa^{\frac{2\beta}{3}-3},\qquad\ \bar{\kappa}\leq\kappa\leq\kappa_{\eta}\;,

then we can relate
\kappa_{\sigma}
to
\kappa_{\eta}
. We establish such a relation through Proposition
5.1
below. From the plots of the compensated spectra in Figures
4
and
6
it appears that
c_{\text{Kr}}=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Later, in Section
7
, we will develop results that give lower bounds on
\kappa_{\eta}
in terms of the Grashof number; these lowers bounds subsequently provide lower bounds on
\kappa_{\sigma}
through Proposition
5.1
, thereby ensuring a strong enstrophy cascade.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
\beta>0
,
\kappa_{\eta}\geq 2^{3/\beta}\bar{\kappa}
and
{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}}\in(0,1)
satisfy

(i)

\langle|\theta_{\overline{\kappa},\kappa_{\eta}}|^{2}\rangle\geq(1-\delta_{1})\langle|\theta|^{2}\rangle

(ii)

\left|\frac{\mathcal{E}(\kappa)}{c_{Kr}\eta^{2/3}\kappa_{0}^{\beta/3}\kappa^{2\beta/3-3}}-1\right|\leq\delta_{2}\;,\qquad\bar{\kappa}\leq\kappa\leq\kappa_{\eta}
.

Then

(5.2)

\frac{3}{\beta}c_{Kr}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\bar{\kappa}}\right)^{\beta/3}{\left(\frac{1-\delta_{2}}{2}\right)}\kappa_{\sigma}^{\alpha}\leq\kappa_{\eta}^{\alpha}\leq\frac{3}{\beta}c_{Kr}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\bar{\kappa}}\right)^{\beta/3}\left(\frac{1+\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{1}}\right)\kappa_{\sigma}^{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
E:=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\langle|\theta|^{2}\rangle
. Then

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle{\leq}\frac{1}{(1-\delta_{1})L^{2}}\langle|\theta_{\overline{\kappa},\kappa_{\eta}}|^{2}\rangle={\frac{1}{1-\delta_{1}}\int_{\underline{\kappa}}^{\kappa_{\eta}}\kappa^{2-\beta}\mathcal{E}(\kappa)\ d\kappa}

\displaystyle{\leq}c_{Kr}\eta^{\frac{2}{3}}\kappa_{0}^{\frac{\beta}{3}}\left(\frac{1+\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{1}}\right)\int_{\bar{\kappa}}^{\kappa_{\eta}}\kappa^{-1-\beta/3}\ {d\kappa}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\beta}c_{Kr}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\bar{\kappa}}\right)^{\beta/3}\left(\frac{1+\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{1}}\right)\left[1-\left(\frac{\bar{\kappa}}{\kappa_{\eta}}\right)^{\beta/3}\right]\eta^{2/3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{\beta}c_{Kr}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\bar{\kappa}}\right)^{\beta/3}\left(\frac{1+\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{1}}\right)\eta^{2/3}\;.

Multiplying both sides by
\eta^{1/3}\gamma^{-1}E^{-1}
, then recalling (
3.1
) and (
3.6
), we obtain

\kappa_{\eta}^{\alpha}=\frac{\eta^{1/3}}{\gamma}\leq\frac{{3}}{\beta}c_{Kr}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\bar{\kappa}}\right)^{\beta/3}\left(\frac{1+\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{1}}\right)\frac{\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle}{\langle|\theta|^{2}\rangle}=\frac{3}{\beta}c_{Kr}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\bar{\kappa}}\right)^{\beta/3}\left(\frac{1+\delta_{2}}{1-\delta_{1}}\right)\kappa_{\sigma}^{\alpha}.

Conversely, we have

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\geq\langle|\theta_{\overline{\kappa},\kappa_{\eta}}|^{2}\rangle{\geq}c_{Kr}\eta^{2/3}\kappa_{0}^{\frac{\beta}{3}}(1-\delta_{2})\int_{\bar{\kappa}}^{\kappa_{\eta}}\kappa^{-1-\beta/3}d\kappa

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\beta}c_{Kr}\eta^{2/3}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\bar{\kappa}}\right)^{\beta/3}(1-\delta_{2})\left[1-\left(\frac{\bar{\kappa}}{\kappa_{\eta}}\right)^{\beta/3}\right]

\displaystyle{\geq}\frac{3}{\beta}c_{Kr}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{\bar{\kappa}}\right)^{\beta/3}\left(\frac{1-\delta_{2}}{2}\right)\eta^{2/3}\;.

Similarly, multiplying both sides by
\eta^{1/3}\gamma^{-1}E^{-1}
, we obtain the lower bound in (
5.2
).

∎

The estimates in (
5.2
) differ somewhat from the following analogous result for the NSE that was proved in
[
18
]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Consider the NSE (
\alpha=2,\beta=0
) and suppose that there exists
4\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{\eta}
such that

\displaystyle\langle|P_{\kappa_{1}}\theta|^{2}\rangle\lesssim\langle|Q_{\kappa_{1}}\theta|^{2}\rangle.

If

\mathcal{E}(\kappa)\sim\eta^{2/3}\kappa^{-3},\quad\text{for}\quad\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa\lesssim\kappa_{\eta}\;,\quad

then

(5.3)

\kappa_{\eta}^{2}\sim\kappa_{\sigma}^{2}\ln\frac{\kappa_{\eta}}{\kappa_{1}}\;.

The log term in (
5.3
) effectively replaces in (
5.2
) the factor
(\kappa_{0}/\overline{\kappa})^{\beta/3}/\beta

which blows up as
\beta\to 0
.

6.
dissipation law

Kolmogorov’s dissipation law for the 3D NSE

(6.1)

\epsilon\sim\frac{U_{\text{3D}}^{3}}{L}\;,

where

\epsilon=\frac{\gamma}{L^{3}}\langle|\theta|^{2}\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad U_{\text{3D}}=\kappa_{0}^{3/2}\langle|u|^{2}\rangle^{1/2}

are the
energy dissipation rate
and
root mean square velocity
. The 2D analogue of Kolmogorov’s 3D dissipation law is

(6.2)

\eta\sim\frac{U_{\text{2D}}^{3}}{L^{3}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad U_{\text{2D}}={\frac{1}{L}}\langle|u|^{2}\rangle^{1/2}\;.

If this law were to extend to the gSQG, it would take the form

(6.3)

\eta\sim\frac{U^{3}}{L^{3}}\qquad\text{but with}\qquad U={L^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}}\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle^{1/2}.

6.1.
Analytical support

The upper bound
\epsilon\lesssim{U_{\text{3D}}^{3}}/{L}
was proved in
[
10
]
for shear and channel flow and in
[
20
]
for the periodic NSE. We show that the analogous upper bound for gSQG holds provided that the Grashof number is sufficiently large.

Theorem 6.1
.

There exists
\underline{G}_{1}
, depending only on
\kappa_{0},\bar{\kappa},\alpha,\beta
, such that if
G\gtrsim\underline{G}_{1}
, then

(6.4)

\eta\lesssim\frac{U^{3}}{L^{3}}.

Our proof of Theorem
6.1
involves the use of commutator operators that allow us to obtain more precise estimates on dyadic triad interactions that arise through localizing the nonlinear term in the enstrophy balance. This localization is facilitated by the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, which we provide a brief introduction to
in the appendix. For efficiency of the present goal of proving Theorem
6.1
, we simply state the commutator estimates invoked its proof (Lemma
6.1
and Lemma
6.2
below). We remark that Lemma
6.2
for the setting of
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

We start with upper and lower bounds on

Proposition 7.1
.

Suppose that

. Then

(7.1)

Proof.

Recall

\eta=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\langle(g,\theta)\rangle\leq\frac{1}{L^{2}}|\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|\rangle||\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|=\frac{1}{L\gamma^{1/2}}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|.

In particular

\eta\leq\frac{1}{L^{2}\gamma}|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|^{2}.

The claimed upper bound then follows from (
3.6
) and (
2.10

For the lower bound, observe that since

, we have from (

) that,

\displaystyle\langle(u\cdotp\nabla\theta,\Lambda^{-\alpha}g)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{\kappa}^{3-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{0}^{-\beta}|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{\kappa}^{3-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{0}^{-2-\alpha}|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle

(7.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{\kappa}^{3-\frac{\alpha}{2}}L^{2+\alpha}|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle.

Applying (
7.2
) and (

) to (

\frac{1}{L^{2}}|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|^{2}\leq\frac{\gamma^{1/2}}{L}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|+\frac{C}{L^{2}}\bar{\kappa}^{3-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{0}^{-2-\alpha}|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|\langle|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\theta|^{2}\rangle.

In particular

\displaystyle\frac{|\Lambda^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}g|}{\gamma^{2}\kappa_{0}^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{2}}}\leq\frac{L}{\kappa_{0}^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{2}}}\left(\frac{\eta}{\gamma^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{CL^{2}}{\kappa_{0}^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{2}}}\bar{\kappa}^{3-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\kappa_{0}^{-2-\alpha}\left(\frac{\eta}{\gamma^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle G\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\eta}}{\kappa_{0}}\right)^{\frac{3\alpha}{2}}+C_{2}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\eta}}{\kappa_{0}}\right)^{3\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}G+\left(\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{2G}+C_{2}\right)\left(\frac{\kappa_{\eta}}{\kappa_{0}}\right)^{3\alpha},

for some non-dimensional constants

. It is now clear that if

∎

Appendix A
Energy Flux

To compute the energy flux (
4.4
), recall that the energy balance through frequency
\kappa
is (formally) given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|\Lambda^{\frac{\beta-2}{2}}p_{\kappa}|^{2}+\gamma|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-2}{2}}p_{\kappa}|^{2}=-(u\cdotp\nabla\theta,\Lambda^{\beta-2}p_{\kappa})+(g,\Lambda^{\beta-2}p_{\kappa}),

where
p_{\kappa}=P_{\kappa}\theta
. Upon recalling that
u=\nabla^{\perp}\psi
and
\psi=-\kappa_{0}^{-\beta}\Lambda^{\beta-2}\theta
,
we apply the identities (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) to argue

\displaystyle-(u\cdotp\nabla\theta,\Lambda^{\beta-2}p_{\kappa})=\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdotp\nabla\theta,P_{\kappa}\psi)=-\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(\nabla^{\perp}\psi\cdotp\nabla P_{\kappa}\psi,\theta)=-\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(\nabla^{\perp}Q_{\kappa}\psi\cdotp\nabla P_{\kappa}\psi,\theta).

Thus, for
q_{\kappa}=Q_{\kappa}\theta
, we have

\displaystyle-(u\cdotp\nabla\theta,\Lambda^{\beta-2}p_{\kappa})

\displaystyle=-\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(\nabla^{\perp}Q_{\kappa}\psi\cdotp\nabla P_{\kappa}\psi,p_{\kappa})-\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(Q_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla P_{\kappa}\psi,q_{\kappa})

\displaystyle=\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(\nabla^{\perp}P_{\kappa}\psi\cdotp\nabla Q_{\kappa}\psi,p_{\kappa})+\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(Q_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla q_{\kappa},P_{\kappa}\psi)

\displaystyle=-\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(P_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla p_{\kappa},Q_{\kappa}\psi)+\kappa_{0}^{\beta}(Q_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla q_{\kappa},P_{\kappa}\psi)

\displaystyle=(P_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla p_{\kappa},\Lambda^{\beta-2}q_{\kappa})-(Q_{\kappa}u\cdotp\nabla q_{\kappa},\Lambda^{\beta-2}p_{\kappa}).

which yields (
4.4
).

Appendix B
Littlewood-Paley decomposition

We give a brief introduction to the Littlewood-Paley decomposition of functions. We state the decomposition for
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and point out that it is also valid in the case
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
Let
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
denote the space of Schwartz class functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
denote the space of tempered distributions. We denote by
\hat{f}
or
\mathcal{F}(f)
, the Fourier transform of
f
, defined by

\hat{f}(\xi)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\int e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx,\quad f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Recall that for
f,g
, we have

\displaystyle(f,g)=(\hat{f},\hat{g}).

The fractional laplacian operator,
\Lambda^{\sigma}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\Lambda^{\sigma}f)(\xi)=|\xi|^{\sigma}\mathcal{F}(f),\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{R}.

For
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Fourier-based homogeneous and inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces by

(B.1)

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\hat{f}\in L^{2}_{loc},\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}|\Lambda^{\sigma}f|<\infty\right\},

(B.2)

\displaystyle H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\hat{f}\in L^{2}_{loc},\lVert f\rVert_{H^{\sigma}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}|(I-\Delta)^{\sigma/2})f|<\infty\right\}.

We define

\displaystyle\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\int f(x)x^{\tau}\,dx=0,\quad\absolutevalue{\tau}=0,1,2,\cdots\right\}.

Let
\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{\prime}
denote the topological dual of
\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then,
\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{\prime}
can be identified with the space of tempered distributions modulo the vector space of polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, denoted by
\mathscr{P}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathscr{Q}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cong\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})/\mathscr{P}.

Let us denote by
{\mathcal{B}}(r)
, the open ball centered at the origin of radius
r
and by
{\mathcal{A}}(r_{1},r_{2})
, the open annulus centered at the origin with inner and outer radii
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
. There exist two non-negative radial functions
\chi,\phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\supp\chi\subset{\mathcal{B}}(1)
and
\supp\phi\subset{\mathcal{A}}(2^{-1},2)
such that for
\phi_{j}(\xi)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\phi(2^{-j}\xi)
and
\chi_{j}(\xi)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\chi(2^{-j}\xi)
, the following conditions are satisfied

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi_{j}(\xi)=1,\\
\chi+\sum_{j\geq 0}\phi_{j}\equiv 1,\,\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\},\\
\supp\phi_{i}\cap\supp\phi_{j}=\varnothing,\,\text{if}\,|i-j|\geq 2,\\
\text{and}\quad\supp\phi_{i}\cap\supp\chi=\varnothing.\end{cases}

We denote

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{j}={\mathcal{A}}(2^{j-1},2^{j+1}),\quad{\mathcal{A}}_{\ell,k}={\mathcal{A}}(2^{\ell},2^{k}),\quad{\mathcal{B}}_{j}={\mathcal{B}}(2^{j}).

Note that

(B.3)

\displaystyle\supp\phi_{j}\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{j},\quad\supp\chi_{j}\subset{\mathcal{B}}_{j}.

we denote by
{\triangle_{j}}
and
S_{j}
, the (homogeneous) Littlewood-Paley dyadic blocks defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}({\triangle_{j}}f)=\phi_{j}\mathcal{F}(f),\quad\mathcal{F}(S_{j}f)=\chi_{j}\mathcal{F}(f).

By (
B.3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}({\triangle_{j}}f)|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{j}^{c}}=0,\quad\mathcal{F}(S_{j}f)|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j}^{c}}=0,

For any
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=S_{i}f+\sum_{j\geq i}\triangle_{j}f,\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}.

and for any
f\in\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\triangle_{j}f.

We have the following characterization of the Sobolev seminorms

\displaystyle C^{-1}\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}\leq\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(2^{j\sigma}\lVert\triangle_{j}f\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}},

for some constant
C
depending only on
\sigma
.
We recall the following inequality which quantifies the relation between the dyadic blocks and the fractional Laplacian operator.

Lemma B.1

(Bernstein inequalities)
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. Then

\displaystyle C^{-1}2^{\sigma j}\lVert{\triangle_{j}}f\rVert_{L^{q}}\leq\lVert\Lambda^{\sigma}{\triangle_{j}}f\rVert_{L^{q}}\leq C2^{\sigma j+2j(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\lVert{\triangle_{j}}f\rVert_{L^{p}},

where
C>0
is a constant that depends on
p,q
and
\sigma
.

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.

We define the sum

{\mathcal{L}}_{s,\ell}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}m_{s,\ell}(u,v)\widehat{f_{1}}(u)\widehat{f_{2}}(v-u)\overline{\widehat{f_{3}}(v)},

where

m_{s,\ell}(u,v)\overset{\text{def}}{=}|v|^{-s}v-|v-u|^{-s}(v-u),

and observe that

\left([\Lambda^{-s}\nabla,f_{1}]f_{2},f_{3}\right)=\mathcal{L}_{s,\ell}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}).

Now let

(B.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(u,v)(\tau)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\tau v+(1-\tau)(v-u).

Henceforth, we suppress the dependence of
\mathbf{A}
on
u,v
. Observe that

\displaystyle|m_{s,\ell}(u,v)|

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{{d\tau}}\left(\abs{\mathbf{A}(\tau)}^{-s}\mathbf{A}(\tau)\right)d\tau}

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\int_{0}^{1}\left(-s\abs{\mathbf{A}(\tau)}^{-s-2}(\mathbf{A}(\tau)\cdot u)\mathbf{A}(\tau)+\abs{\mathbf{A}(\tau)}^{-s}u\right)d\tau}

(B.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\absolutevalue{u}\int_{0}^{1}\absolutevalue{\mathbf{A}(\tau)}^{-s}d\tau,

where the fact
s\in(0,1)
is invoked to obtain the last inequality. Since
\text{supp}\ \widehat{f_{2}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
\text{supp}\ \widehat{f_{3}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{j}
, we can assume that
\text{supp}\ \widehat{f_{1}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{i+k+2}
. We consider two cases:

Case 1:
\text{supp}\ \widehat{f_{1}}\subset\mathcal{B}_{i-3}

For
u\in\mathcal{B}_{i-3}
, we have

|\mathbf{A}(\tau)|\geq|v-u|-\tau|u|\geq 2^{i-1}-2^{i-3}=3(2^{i-3})\geq\frac{3}{16}|v-u|.

For
\varphi\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{v-u}{|u|}
and
\vartheta\overset{\text{def}}{=}\frac{u}{|u|}
, we therefore obtain

(B.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\absolutevalue{\mathbf{A}(\tau)}^{-s}d\tau\leq C|v-u|^{-\rho}|u|^{\rho-s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{|\varphi+\tau\vartheta|^{s-\rho}}d\tau\leq C|v-u|^{-\rho}|u|^{\rho-s},

where we invoked Lemma 3.2 in
[
28
]
for the last inequality.

Case 2:
\text{supp}\ \widehat{f_{1}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{i-3,i+j+k}

. Once again invoking Lemma 3.2 in
[
28
]
, we have

(B.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\absolutevalue{\mathbf{A}(\tau)}^{-s}d\tau\leq C|u|^{-s}\leq C_{k}|v-u|^{-\rho}|u|^{\rho-s}.

Using (
B.6
) and (
B.7
) in (
B
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}_{s,\ell}(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})|\leq C\sum_{(u,v)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}|\widehat{\Lambda^{1+\rho-s}f_{1}}(u)||\widehat{\Lambda^{-\rho}f_{2}}(v-u)|{\widehat{f_{3}}(v)}|.

Finally, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality gives us the claimed result.

Batch Sample-wise Stochastic Optimal Control via Stochastic Maximum Principle
Hui Sun, Feng Bao

Abstract
In this work, we study the stochastic optimal control problem (SOC) mainly from the probabilistic view point, i.e. via the Stochastic Maximum principle (SMP) [ 25 ] . We adopt the sample-wise backpropagation scheme proposed in [ 1 ] to solve the SOC problem under the strong convexity assumption. Importantly, in the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) procedure, we use batch samples with higher order scheme in the forward SDE to improve the convergence rate in [ 1 ] from ∼ 𝒪 ⁡ ( N K + 1 N ) \sim\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{N}{K}+\frac{1}{N}}) to ∼ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 K + 1 N 2 ) \sim\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{1}{K}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}}) and note that the main source of uncertainty originates from the scheme for the simulation of Z Z term in the BSDE. In the meantime, we note the SGD procedure uses only the necessary condition of the SMP, while the batch simulation of the approximating solution of BSDEs allows one to obtain a more accurate estimate of the control u u that minimizes the Hamiltonian. We then propose a damped contraction algorithm to solve the SOC problem whose proof of convergence for a special case is attained under some appropriate assumption. We then show numerical results to check the first order convergence rate of the projection algorithm and analyze the convergence behavior of the damped contraction algorithm. We note that comparisons between the numerical results from these two algorithms (projection and contraction) show the projection approach is still favored in terms of stability and efficiency. Lastly, we briefly discuss how to incorporate the proposed scheme in solving practical problems especially when the Randomized Neural Networks are used. We note that in this special case, the error backward propagation can be avoided and parameter update can be achieved via purely algebraic computation (vector algebra) which will potentially improve the efficiency of the whole training procedure. Such idea will require further exploration and we will leave it as our future work. Keywords: Stochastic Maximum Principle, batch gradient descent, contraction with damping, convergence analysis. Neural networks. MSC codes: 60H10, 60H30, 65C20.

In this approach, one will first find the numerical approximation of the variational derivative

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X^{N}_{n}&=b(t_{n},X^{N}_{n},u_{t_{n}})h+\sigma(t_{n},X^{N}_{n},u_{t_{n}})\Delta W_{n}\\

Z^{N}_{t_{n}}&=\mathbb{E}_{t_{n}}[Y^{N}_{t_{n+1}}\Delta W_{t_{n+1}}]/h.\end{cases}

(1.1)

(Y^{N}_{t_{n}},Z^{N}_{t_{n}})

Review of SOC Problem and the SMP.

Consider the following controlled stochastic process

\displaystyle dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t},u_{t})dt+\sigma(t,x_{t},u_{t})dW_{t},\ \ X_{t_{0}}=x_{0}

(2.1)

where we have
X_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},W_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},b(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\sigma(\cdot,\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
, and
\mathcal{U}
is the collection of all controls
u_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|b(t,0,u_{t})|^{2}+|\sigma(t,0,u_{t})|^{2}dt]<\infty.

(2.2)

To ease notations, we sometimes suppress the time dependencies in the coefficients
b,\sigma,f
. We make the following assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

Let
b,\sigma,f,g
be deterministic smooth functions in their variables with the derivatives
\partial_{x}b\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},\partial_{u}b\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k},\partial_{x}\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m\times d},\partial_{u}\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m\times k}
all bounded such that :

i.

The lipschitz condition hold uniformly in
t
:

|\phi(x,u)-\phi(y,v)|\leq C(|x-y|+|u-v|).

where
\phi:=b,b^{\prime}_{x},b^{\prime}_{u},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}_{x},\sigma^{\prime}_{u}
. We also assume that
|b(0,u)|+|\sigma(0,u)|\leq C
for some
C>0
.

ii.

The terminal function
g
is bounded from below and it has at most quadratic growth.

\displaystyle|g(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2}).

(2.3)

Typically, the control
\mathcal{U}(t_{0},x_{0})
contains all the controls evaluated in
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}f(t,X_{t},u_{t})dt]<\infty

(2.4)

and we assume that the set
\mathcal{U}(t_{0},x_{0})
is not empty for all
(t_{0},x_{0})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Abusing notation by still writing the space of admissible controls as
\mathcal{U}
, we state the stochastic optimal control problem as follows:

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathcal{U}}J(u):=\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t_{0}}f(t,X_{t},u_{t})dt+g(X_{T})]

(2.5)

2.1
Stochastic Maximum Principle

The stochastic optimal control can be classically solved by first using the dynamic programming principle and then the related value function can be attained by solving the derived HJB equation.
In this section however, we take the probabilistic approach and solve the stochastic optimal control problem by using the Pontryagin’s Stochastic Maximum principle. Let
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
and define the
Hamiltonian
to be:

\displaystyle H(t,x,y,z,u):=by+\sigma z+f

(2.6)

where we make the following comments on the Hamiltonian

i).

y,z
are the co-variables

ii).

The multiplication is understood to be the following

(a)

by:=b^{T}y

(b)

\sigma z:=tr(\sigma^{T}z)=tr(z^{T}\sigma)

Assuming convex control domain and consider
u^{\epsilon}:=u+\epsilon\beta,\beta:=v-u
and we consider the following SDE

\displaystyle dDX^{u}_{t}=\bigg(b^{\prime}_{x}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})DX^{u}_{t}+b^{\prime}_{u}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})\beta_{t}\bigg)dt+\bigg(\sigma^{\prime}_{x}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})DX^{u}_{t}+\sigma^{\prime}_{u}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})\beta_{t}\bigg)dW_{t}\ DX^{u}_{0}=0

we note that this SDE is motivated by considering the following derivative:

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{X_{t}^{u+\epsilon\beta}-X_{t}^{u}}{\epsilon}.

(2.7)

See Lemma 4.7
[
7
]
for a proof of the well-posedness of the solution of the above SDE.

The covariables introduced are defined to be the adjoint process
(Y_{t},Z_{t})
which is a solution to the following BSDE:

\displaystyle dY_{t}=-\partial_{x}H(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t},u_{t})dt+Z_{t}dW_{t},\ \ Y_{T}=\partial_{x}g(X_{T})

(2.8)

where by definition of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\partial_{x}H(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t},u_{t})=\partial_{x}b^{T}Y_{t}+tr\big(\partial_{x}\sigma^{T}Z_{t})+\partial_{x}f.

(2.9)

Then, by applying the Itô product
Y_{t}^{T}DX^{u}_{t}
and take expectation, the following equation is easily obtained:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\partial_{u}g(X^{u}_{T})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[Y^{T}_{T}DX^{u}_{T}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}Y^{T}_{t}b^{\prime}_{u}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})\beta_{t}-(DX^{u}_{t})^{T}f^{\prime}_{x}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})+tr(Z^{T}_{t}\sigma^{\prime}_{u}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})\beta_{t})dt]

(2.10)

Now we find gradient of
J
in the direction of
\beta
:

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{J(u+\epsilon\beta)-J(u)}{\epsilon}=\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}\big[f^{\prime}_{x}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})DX^{u}_{t}+f^{\prime}_{u}(t,X^{u}_{t},u_{t})\beta_{t}dt+g^{\prime}_{x}(X^{u}_{T})DX^{u}_{T}\big]

(2.11)

By substituting (
2.10
) into the above equation, we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{dJ(u+\epsilon\beta)}{d\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}=\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}\partial_{u}H(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t},u_{t})\cdot\beta_{t}dt]

(2.12)

As such, suppose that
u^{*}
is the optimal control and since the control domain is convex, we have
\forall s\in U
:

\displaystyle\partial_{u}H(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t},u^{*}_{t})\cdot(s-u^{*}_{t})\geq 0,a.e.\textit{ in }t,\ a.s.

(2.13)

And we note that given the control
u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}
,

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{u}(u)|_{t=t_{n}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\partial_{u}H(t_{n},X_{t_{n}},Y_{t_{n}},Z_{t_{n}},u_{t_{n}})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[b^{\prime}_{u}(t_{n},X_{t_{n}},u_{t_{n}})Y_{t_{n}}+\sigma^{\prime}_{u}(t_{n},X_{t_{n}},u_{t_{n}})Z_{t_{n}}+f^{\prime}_{u}(t_{n},X_{t_{n}},u_{t_{n}})]

(2.14)

Furthermore, we note that with some additional assumption on the convexity of the terminal loss function and the Hamiltonian, one can obtain global minimum.

Theorem 2.1
.

With all the above assumptions, assume in addtion that
g
is convex and that for each
t\in[0,T]
, the function
(x,u)\rightarrow H(t,x,Y_{t},Z_{t},u)
is convex. Then if
u^{*}\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}

\displaystyle H(t,X^{u^{*}}_{t},Y^{u^{*}}_{t},Z^{u^{*}}_{t},u_{t}^{*})=\inf_{u\in U}H(t,X^{u^{*}}_{t},Y^{u^{*}}_{t},Z^{u^{*}}_{t},u),a.s

(2.15)

then the control
u
is an optimal control.

For a proof, please refer to
[
7
]
, theorem 4.14.
As such, based on the theorem above, we can write down the following extended Hamiltonian system:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX^{u^{*}}=b(t,X_{t}^{u^{*}},u_{t}^{*})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t}^{u^{*}},u_{t}^{*})dW_{t},\ \ &X^{u^{*}}_{0}=x_{0}\\
dY_{t}^{u^{*}}=-H_{x}(t,X^{u^{*}}_{t},Y^{u^{*}}_{t},Z^{u^{*}}_{t},{u_{t}^{*}})dt+Z^{u^{*}}_{t}dW_{t},\ \ &Z^{u^{*}}_{T}=\partial_{x}g(X_{T}^{u^{*}})\\
H(t,X^{u^{*}}_{t},Y^{u^{*}}_{t},Z^{u^{*}}_{t},u_{t}^{*})=\inf_{u\in U}H(t,X^{u^{*}}_{t},Y^{u^{*}}_{t},Z^{u^{*}}_{t},u)\end{cases}

(2.16)

In the following, we will suppress the dependency on the control
u
for the process
(X^{u}_{t},Y^{u}_{t},Z^{u}_{t})
when its meaning is clear from the context.

The two Algorithm

In this work, we restrict our control to be the space of
deterministic
controls:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{ad}:=\Big\{u:[0,T]\rightarrow U\big|u\in L_{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{k})\Big\}

(3.1)

for some convex set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
. For simplicity, we will work with
d=m=k=1
, higher dimensional generalization is similar.We will work with the whole space
U=\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and so we assume
\lim_{||u||\rightarrow\infty}J(u)\rightarrow\infty
. Also, we let the diffusion term be controlled but it is not a function of the state
X_{t}
.

Then according to the stochastic maximum principle, we are considering
u\in\mathcal{U}_{ad}
for the following

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[H(t,X^{u^{*}}_{t},Y^{u^{*}}_{t},Z^{u^{*}}_{t},u_{t}^{*})]=\inf_{u\in U}\mathbb{E}[H(t,X^{u^{*}}_{t},Y^{u^{*}}_{t},Z^{u^{*}}_{t},u)].

(3.2)

1.

In light of the necessary condition of the SMP, one can consider the projection algorithm since the gradient alone usually provides rich information on the loss functional. Thus, one can propose the following algorithms (see
[
1
,
29
,
14
,
2
]
), for each
k\in\{1,...,K\}

\displaystyle u_{t}^{k+1}=u_{t}^{k}-\eta_{k}\nabla_{u}\mathbb{E}[H(t,X^{u^{k}}_{t},Y^{u^{k}}_{t},Z^{u^{k}}_{t},u^{k}_{t})],\ \ \forall t\in[0,T].

(3.3)

where we use the
k
to denote the
k
th step in the iteration and
{u^{k}}
the control related to the FBSDE system.

2.

On the other hand, to find the optimal control, one can treat (
3.2
) as a fixed point problem and tentatively propose the following solution strategy: for
k=1,..,K
,
K\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

\displaystyle u_{t}^{k+1}=\max_{u\in U}\mathbb{E}[H(t,X^{u^{k}}_{t},Y^{u^{k}}_{t},Z^{u^{k}}_{t},u)]

(3.4)

and expect the solution to converge. However, numerical experiments shows that such simple algorithm in most cases will not take place because the map does not form a contraction. Thus, one can use damping
\rho\in(0,1)
to ensure that the update is not too rapid: for
k=1,..,K
,
K\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

\displaystyle u_{t}^{k+1}=(1-\rho)\max_{u\in U}\mathbb{E}[H(t,X^{u^{k}}_{t},Y^{u^{k}}_{t},Z^{u^{k}}_{t},u)]+\rho u_{t}^{k}.

(3.5)

We note that the existence of such contraction usually imposes more restrictive structure on the terminal cost
g
and the structure of the Hamiltonian. As such, we will assume such property hold to facilitate the analysis.

Based on the structure of the problem (
2.16
), one needs to find the numerical approximation for the gradient
J^{\prime}_{u}
or
H^{\prime}_{u}
and one challenge is finding numerical solution for the BSDE
(Y_{t},Z_{t})
. We first define the discrete control space:

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{N}:=\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{C}^{N},\ \ \ \mathcal{C}^{N}:=\big\{u\big|u:=\sum^{N-1}_{i=1}a_{n}\mathbf{1}_{[t_{n},t_{n+1}),n=0,...,N-1}\big|a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\big\}

(3.6)

that is,
\mathcal{U}^{N}
consists of the piecewise control functions which are admissible.

Classically, the BSDE finds the following discretization scheme, for some
u\in\mathcal{U}^{N}

\displaystyle Y^{N}_{t_{n}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t_{n}}[Y^{N}_{t_{n+1}}]+h\mathbb{E}_{t_{n}}[b^{\prime}_{x}(t_{n},X^{N}_{t_{n}},u_{t_{n}})Y^{N}_{t_{n+1}}+f^{\prime}_{x}(t_{n},X_{n},u_{t_{n}})]

(3.7)

\displaystyle Z^{N}_{t_{n}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t_{n}}[Y^{N}_{t_{n+1}}\Delta W_{n+1}]/h

(3.8)

where
h:=\frac{T}{N}
is the uniform mesh size,
\mathbb{E}_{t_{n}}[\cdot]:=\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}]
and
X^{N}_{t_{n}}
is the solution of the numerical SDE under the Euler scheme

\displaystyle X^{N}_{t_{n+1}}=X^{N}_{t_{n}}+b(t_{n},X^{N}_{t_{n}},u_{t_{n}})h+\sigma(t_{n},u_{t_{n}})\Delta W_{t_{n}}\ \ X_{0}=x_{0}.

(3.9)

Typical methods include regression methods, Monte Carlo, finding values
(Y^{N}_{t_{n}},Z^{N}_{t_{n}})
at fixed spacial locations and obtain function by interpolation, and deep learning
[
13
,
21
,
19
,
15
]
. Then, the key update steps can be summarized as
\forall n\in\{0,...,N-1\}
,
k=0,1,2...,K
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{t_{n}}^{N,k+1}=u_{t_{n}}^{N,k}-\eta_{k}\mathbb{E}[\nabla_{u}H(t_{n},X^{N,u^{k}}_{t_{n}},Y^{N,u^{k}}_{t_{n}},Z^{N,u^{k}}_{t_{n}},u^{k}_{t_{n}})],\ \ \ &\text{Projection Algorithm}\\
u_{t_{n}}^{N,k+1}=(1-\rho)\max_{u\in U}\mathbb{E}[H(t_{n},X^{N,u^{k}}_{t_{n}},Y^{N,u^{k}}_{t_{n}},Z^{N,u^{k}}_{t_{n}},u)]+\rho u_{t_{n}}^{k}.\ \ \ &\text{Damped contraction}\end{cases}

(3.10)

However, it is noted that solving BSDEs is overall a challenging and computationally expensive task. The challenge is even more pronounced under the projection or the current contraction framework as at each iteration
k\in\{1,...,K\}
a new system for FBSDE has to be solved repeated.

On the other hand, we note that the goal of solving the stochastic optimal control problem is to find either the value function or the control instead of solving explicitly the BSDEs. As such, we use (see also
[
1
,
2
]
) the sample-wise approximation of the BSDE which turns out to be an unbiased sample estimation of the classical numerical solution
(Y^{N}_{t_{n}},Z^{N}_{t_{n}})
. More specifically, we have:

\displaystyle X_{n+1}

\displaystyle=X_{n}+b(t_{n},X_{n},u_{n})h+\sigma(t_{n},u_{n})\Delta W_{n}

(3.11)

\displaystyle Y_{n}

\displaystyle=Y_{n+1}+h\big(b_{x}(t_{n},X_{n},u_{n})Y_{n+1}+f^{\prime}_{x}(t_{n},X_{n},u_{n})\big),\ \ Y_{T}=g_{x}(X_{T})

(3.12)

\displaystyle Z_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{Y_{n+1}\Delta W_{n}}{h}

(3.13)

where
\Delta W_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,h)
is a Normal random variable.

For the projection algorithm, we observed that the simulated samples
Z_{n}
typically has high variance with increasing number of temporal discretization
N
. To reduce variance, we simply use independently simulated trajectories of batch size
N
or larger to compute the estimated gradient.
That is, given
j^{\prime}_{u}|_{t=t_{n}}:=\partial H_{u}(t_{n},X^{u}_{n},Y^{u}_{n},Z^{u}_{n},u_{n})=b^{\prime}_{u}(t_{n},X^{u}_{n},u_{n})Y^{u}_{n}+\sigma^{\prime}_{u}(t_{n},X^{u}_{n},u_{n})Z^{u}_{n}+f^{\prime}_{u}(t_{n},X^{u}_{n},u_{n})

we use
M
in dependent samples to estimate the gradient.

\displaystyle\overline{(j^{\prime}_{u})}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum^{M}_{i=1}\partial_{u}H(t_{n},X^{u,i}_{n},Y^{u,i}_{n},Z^{u,i}_{n},u_{n}).

(3.14)

It is expected that such batch estimator will lead to faster convergence. In the meantime, we note that an higher order convergence rate (1st order) is possible given a more accurate sampling for the solution of forward SDE than Euler is used. This is mostly because the temporal semi-discretization of solution of BSDE itself has first order convergence. We will use
\Psi
and

\displaystyle X^{u}_{n+1}=\Psi(X^{u}_{n},u^{l}_{n},\Delta W_{n}),

(3.15)

to denote update for the state under a higher order scheme. We make explicit the choice for the higher order numerical scheme in the appendix.

For the damped contraction algorithm, we still use the Euler discretization for the forward SDE while keeping the batch simulation for the solution of FBSDEs for the estimate of
u
that minimizes the Hamiltonian.

As such, based on the sampling scheme (
3.11
)-(
3.13
), in light of (
3.10
), we propose the Algorithm
1
for finding the optimal control.

,

4
Proof of convergence

In this section, we prove the convergence of the projection algorithm (1st order convergence in
h
) under the strong convexity assumption as well as the convergence of the damped contraction mapping algorithm under stronger assumption.

Assumption 2
.

We make the following strong convexity assumption on the cost functional
J
:
for some constant
\lambda>0
, and any two controls
v,w\in\mathcal{U}
:

\langle J_{u}^{\prime}(v)-J_{u}^{\prime}(w),v-w\rangle\geq\lambda||v-w||_{2}^{2}

(4.1)

Equivalently, one also have the following inequality:

\displaystyle J(v)-J(w)\geq\langle J^{\prime}(w),v-w\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{2}||v-w||^{2}_{2}.

(4.2)

Assumption 3
.

(Assumption for the damped contraction algorithm, a linear quadratic case.)

I.

We assume that the drift takes the form
b(x,u):=Ax+Bu+C
or the form
b(x,u):=A(u+1)x+Bu+C
. We assume the diffusion term takes the form
\sigma(u)=Du+F
. The running cost is assumed to take the form
\frac{1}{2}\int^{T}_{0}|u_{t}|^{2}dt
.

II.

We assume that there is a function
\bar{H}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow U
such that:

\displaystyle\bar{H}(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})=\text{argmin}_{u\in U}H(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t},u).

(4.3)

Based on the first point, the map
\bar{H}
takes the following form for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
:

\displaystyle\bar{H}(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})=\alpha(X_{t})Y_{t}+\beta_{t}Z_{t}+\gamma(X_{t}).

(4.4)

We then further assume that there exists a negative
\mu
such that

\displaystyle\langle\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}[\bar{H}(t,X^{u}_{t},Y^{u}_{t},Z^{u}_{t})]-\mathbb{E}[\bar{H}(t,X^{v}_{t},Y^{v}_{t},Z^{v}_{t})],u_{t}-v_{t}\rangle dt\leq\mu||u-v||_{2}^{2}.

(4.5)

Remark
.

We take
H^{\prime}_{u}=BY+DZ+u
which means
\bar{H}=-BY-DZ
, and so
\alpha=-B,\beta=-D
.
We note that
\mathrm{II}
is intimately related to the strong convexity assumption:

\displaystyle\langle J_{u}^{\prime}(v)-J_{u}^{\prime}(w),v-w\rangle\geq\lambda||v-w||_{2}^{2}

(4.6)

which in the current set up reads as

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle BY^{u}_{t}+DZ^{u}_{t}+u_{t}-(BY^{v}_{t}+DZ^{v}_{t}+v_{t}),u_{t}-v_{t}\rangle dt\Big]\geq\lambda||u-v||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\langle\alpha Y^{u}_{t}+\beta Z^{u}_{t}-(\alpha Y^{v}_{t}+\beta Z^{v}_{t}),u_{t}-v_{t}\rangle dt\Big]\leq-(\lambda-1)||u-v||_{2}^{2}

(4.7)

Hence, if the factor
\lambda
is larger than
1
,
\mu
will take negative values. We also comment it is also viable to assume
\mu
takes a small enough positive value in the argument of Theorem
4.11
.

To proceed with the proof, we first list the following propositions which can be proved based on Assumption
1
. Those results highlights the fact that under strong regularity assumptions on the coefficients of the differential equations, the solution of the SDEs/BSDEs is stable with respect to the controls.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under Assumption
1
, we have that for
u,v\in\mathcal{U}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{u}-X^{v}_{t}|^{2}]\leq||u-v||^{2}.

(4.8)

Proposition 4.2
.

Under the Assumption
1
, Consider the two discrete SDEs with control
u,v\in\mathcal{U}^{N}
.
Let
\Delta X^{u,v}_{n}:=X^{u}_{n}-X^{v}_{n}
, then

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta X^{u,v}_{n}|^{2}]\leq C||u-v||^{2}.

(4.9)

Proposition 4.3
.

Under the Assumption
1
, let
\Delta Y^{u,v}_{n}:=Y^{N,u}_{t_{n}}-Y^{N,v}_{t_{n}}
,
\Delta Z^{u,v}_{n}:=Z^{N,u}_{t_{n}}-Z^{N,v}_{t_{n}}
,
we have the following estimates, for
u,v\in\mathcal{U}^{N}
:

\sup_{0\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{n}|^{2}]+h\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Z^{u,v}_{n}|^{2}]\leq C||u-v||^{2}.

(4.10)

Now we state the proposition related to the damped contraction mapping setting.

Proposition 4.4
.

Under Assumption
1
and Assumption
3
for any control
u,v\in\mathcal{U}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{u}-Y_{t}^{v}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|Z_{t}^{u}-Z_{t}^{v}|^{2}dt]\leq||u-v||^{2}.

(4.11)

Proposition 4.5
.

Let Assumption
1
and Assumption

hold. Let
\bar{H}
be defined as follows and

\displaystyle\bar{H}(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})=\alpha(X_{t})Y_{t}+\beta_{t}Z_{t}+\gamma(X_{t}).

(4.12)

Then we have for some

,

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\big|\mathbb{E}[\bar{H}(t,X^{u}_{t},Y^{u}_{t},Z^{u}_{t})]-\mathbb{E}[\bar{H}(t,X^{v}_{t},Y^{v}_{t},Z^{v}_{t})]\big|^{2}dt\leq C||u-v||^{2}.

(4.13)

The following result is important in understanding the sample-wise approximation of the solution of the FBSDE (decoupled) which essentially says that the conditional expectation of the sample-wise simulated

is the solution of the BSDE under the classical numerical scheme. As such, we are generating unbiased estimates for the classical numerical solution of the BSDE.

.

Given the control

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t_{n}}[Y_{n}]

\displaystyle=Y^{N}_{t_{n}}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{t_{n}}[Z_{n}]

\displaystyle=Z^{N}_{t_{n}}

(4.15)

and so

(4.16)

\displaystyle=Z^{N}_{t_{N-1}}.

(4.17)

(4.18)

\displaystyle=Y^{N}_{t_{N-1}}.

(4.19)

where the last equality is by (

). The case

for the

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\Psi
defined above be a scheme of order
\Theta
, assume that there exists constant

such that
\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[|X^{N}_{t_{n}}-X_{t_{n}}|^{4}]}\leq C\mathbb{E}[|X^{N}_{t_{n}}-X_{t_{n}}|^{2}]

\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t_{n}\leq t\leq t_{n+1}}|Y_{t}-Y^{N}_{t_{n}}|^{2}]+\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|Z_{t}-Z^{N}_{t_{n}}|^{2}dt]\leq Ch^{2}.

(4.27)

provided

Proof.

By definition of
\hat{Y}^{N}_{t_{n}}
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\hat{Y}^{N}_{t_{n+1}}&=\hat{Y}^{N}_{t_{n}}-h\partial_{x}H(t_{n},X_{t_{n}},\hat{Y}^{N}_{t_{n}},\hat{Z}^{N}_{t_{n}},u_{t_{n}})+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\hat{\tilde{Z}}_{s}dW_{s}\\

\end{cases}

We take the difference between the two equations above and denote
\Delta Y^{N}_{t_{n+1}}:=\hat{Y}^{N}_{t_{n+1}}-Y^{N}_{t_{n+1}}
,
\Delta Z^{N}_{t_{n+1}}:=\hat{Z}^{N}_{t_{n+1}}-Z^{N}_{t_{n+1}}

(4.28)

square both sides and take expectation, note the independence between

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Delta Y^{N}_{t_{n}}|^{2}+\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}|\Delta\tilde{Z}_{s}|^{2}ds\Big]

5
Numerical Example

In this section, we demonstrate the convergence of the algorithm by studying a few numerical examples. We show examples for both cases where the diffusion are controlled/uncontrolled. More specifically:

1.

For the Batch projection algorithm with high order in forward SDE, we note that both numerical examples agree with the analysis: a first decay is observed when the number of iterations is taken as
K\sim\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
.

2.

For the damped contraction algorithm, we examine the convergence behavior of the algorithm by choosing different hyperparameter for
K,N,M
. We comment that to obtain exactly the rate of convergence that match the experiment results, a more refined analysis is needed.

More specifically, the
\sim\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\eta^{K}+\frac{N}{M}+\frac{1}{N}})
convergence is harder to examine since our conclusion is based on a more restrictive assumption, and we only know that there exists
\rho
such that
\eta<1
for the contraction to take place. In this regard, we simply pick
\eta\sim\mathcal{O}(1-\rho)
to be a large constant, in this case
0.995
and ensure that the convergence will take place when the batch size/iteration number is large. To study the convergence behavior, we take the following relationship between
K,M,N
: let
N=\lfloor 1.0/(\eta^{K})\rfloor
where
K
is the number of iteration, so that we are trying to roughly look at the order
\sim\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}+\frac{N}{M}+\frac{1}{N}})
. As such, we note that after choosing
K
, by taking
M\sim\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
, one should expect the convergence to take place and the convergence should be of half order. This is especially the case when the diffusion is controlled. Lastly, we remark that the while for the projection approach, it is viable to only use plain SGD (one sample) as optimization procedure, for the designed damped-contraction approach, a reasonable batch size is needed (see examples below).

We note that a higher order scheme is used for SDE simulation for the projection algorithm and we used Method (4) from

5.3
Solving high dimensional HJB using Batch projection algorithm

In this section, we will use the Batch projection algorithm to solve a high dimension HJB equation used in the seminal paper
[
11
]
. The equation of interest is as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\Delta_{x}v-\lambda|D_{x}v|^{2}=0,&(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
v(T,x)=g(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

(5.7)

which originates from the following HJB equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}+\Delta_{x}v+\lambda\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}[|a|^{2}+2a\cdot D_{x}v]=0,&(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
v(T,x)=g(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

(5.8)

This equation is related to the following stochastic optimal control problem:

\displaystyle v(t,x)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t}|\alpha_{s}|^{2}ds+g(X^{t,x,\alpha}_{T})]

(5.9)

and
X_{s}=X_{s}^{t,x,\alpha}
is the controlled process governed by

\displaystyle dX_{s}=2\sqrt{\lambda}\alpha_{s}ds+\sqrt{2}dW_{s},\ \ \ t\leq s\leq T,\ X_{t}=x.

(5.10)

Note that the optimal control of the problem is of a feedback form, i.e
\alpha_{s}=u_{s}(x)
for some
u_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, to find the optimal control, after discretizing the time interval
[0,T]
with uniform mesh of size
\frac{T}{N}
, we approximate
u_{n}(x)
with a a randomized neural network:

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)=A_{n}\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{A}_{n}x+\tilde{b}_{n})+b_{n}

(5.11)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},A_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times\tilde{d}},\tilde{A}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{d}\times d},b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}
with
d_{1}=d=100
.
The SDE (
5.10
) then finds the following discretization:

\displaystyle X_{n+1}=X_{n}+2\sqrt{\lambda}u_{n}(X_{n})\Delta t+\sqrt{2}\Delta W_{n}

(5.12)

We emphasize a few considerations of using the Randomized Neural network structure:

i.)

We use the randomized neural network with only one hidden layer and the only trainable parameters are
(A_{n},b_{n})
for
n=0,...,N-1
.

ii.)

The fixed parameters
(\tilde{A}_{n},\tilde{b}_{n})
are sampled independently from Gaussian distribution with zero mean and preselected variance.

iii.)

Given this special structure, the gradient of the Hamiltonian with respect to both the input variable
x
and the weight vectors
(A_{n},b_{n})
can be computed manually. This avoids the full error backpropagation of the loss under the approach of
[
18
]
. Under our SMP framework, we see that the coefficient is actually linear in the control parameters (trainable weights).

Now, the Hamiltonian is defined accordingly as

\displaystyle H_{n}=2\sqrt{\lambda}Y^{T}_{n}u_{n}(x)+\sqrt{2}tr(IZ_{n})+|u_{n}|^{2}

(5.13)

and
(Y_{n},Z_{n})
are defined via the following system of equations:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Y_{n}&=Y_{n+1}+2\sqrt{\lambda}Y^{T}_{n+1}\Big(A_{n}\cdot\text{diag}(\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{\prime})\cdot\tilde{A}_{n}+2u_{n}(x)^{T}\big(A_{n}\cdot\text{diag}\big(\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{\prime}\big)\cdot\tilde{A}_{n}\big)\Big)\Delta t\\
Z_{n}&=\frac{Y_{n+1}\Delta W^{T}_{n}}{\Delta t}\end{cases}

where we write
\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{\prime}
to represent
\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}:=\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}(\tilde{A}_{n}x+\tilde{b}_{n})
.
The sample-wise gradient
j^{\prime}(A_{n},b_{n})
is then computed as follows:

\displaystyle j^{\prime}(A_{n})

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\lambda}Y_{n}\tilde{\phi}_{n}^{T}+2(A_{n}\cdot\tilde{\phi}_{n}+b)\tilde{\phi}^{T}

\displaystyle j^{\prime}(b_{n})

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\lambda}Y_{n}^{T}+(A_{n}\cdot\tilde{\phi}_{n}+b_{n})^{T}

(5.14)

where we have used the shorthand notation
\tilde{\phi}_{n}
to denote
\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{A}_{n}x+\tilde{b}_{n})
. With the explicit form of the gradient above, one can perform batch gradient decent accordingly (Algorithm
1
).

6
Conclusion and future outlook

In this work, we designed and updated the algorithm proposed in
[
1
]
and improved the convergence rate in
[
1
]
from
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{N}{K}+\frac{1}{N}})
to
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\frac{1}{K}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}})
under the strong convexity assumption. Under some specific setup, we further designed a damped contraction algorithm that leverages both the sufficient and necessary condition of the Stochastic Maximum principle. Our experiments show the two algorithms under the batch sample setup demonstrate improvement in time efficiency over the original SGD approach. In particular, the batch SGD is shown to be more efficient. Our last example then demonstrates that the batch SGD algorithm can be used to solve practical problems of much higher dimensions. Also, when randomized neural networks are used to approximate the feedback control, the whole training process is more interpretable in the sense that the complicated error back propagation can be avoided, and the gradients can be computed algebraically.

We further note that to better improve the efficiency/accuracy of the algorithm, the pathwise realization for
Z_{t}
in the solution
(Y_{t},Z_{t})
of the BSDE needs to be further designed for variance reduction. Also, we will further explore the possible extension of the designed contraction mapping based algorithm to the training of deep neural networks so that the entire neural network traning will be derivative free.

h\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Z^{u,v}_{n}|^{2}]
, we sum up the inequality (
7.19
) on both sides and use (
7.20
), we obtain.

{}h\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Z^{u,v}_{n}|^{2}]\leq Ch\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}|u_{n}-v_{n}|^{2}

(7.21)

Combining (
7.21
) and (
7.20
), we have

\sup_{0\leq n\leq N}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{n}|^{2}]+h\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Z^{u,v}_{n}|^{2}]\leq Ch\sum^{N-1}_{n=0}|u_{n}-v_{n}|^{2}

(7.22)

∎

Proof for
Proposition
4.4
.

Proof.

We take the Itô derivative of the term
|Y^{u}_{t}-Y^{v}_{t}|^{2}
which are defined as follows:

\displaystyle dY^{u}_{t}

\displaystyle=-(b^{\prime}_{x}(u_{t})Y_{t}^{u}+f^{\prime}_{x}(x^{u}_{t}))dt+Z_{t}^{u}dW_{t}

\displaystyle dY^{v}_{t}

\displaystyle=-(b^{\prime}_{x}(v_{t})Y_{t}^{v}+f^{\prime}_{x}(x^{v}_{t}))dt+Z_{t}^{v}dW_{t}

(7.23)

The following results are attained:

\displaystyle d|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2Y^{u,v}_{t}dY^{u,v}_{t}+|\Delta Z^{u,v}_{t}|^{2}dt

(7.24)

Integrating both sides and take expectation, the following equality is attained:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{t}|^{2}+\int^{T}_{t}|\Delta Z^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds]\leq|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{T}|^{2}+2\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t}\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}\Big((\Delta b^{u,v}_{x})_{s}Y^{u}_{s}+(b^{v}_{x})_{s}\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}+\Delta f^{u,v}_{s}\Big)ds]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{T}|^{2}+2\int^{T}_{t}|(\Delta b^{u,v}_{x})_{s}||\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}Y^{u}_{s}|+|(b^{v}_{x})_{s}||\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}+\Delta f^{u,v}_{s}\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}ds]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{T}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[(\sup_{s}|Y^{u}_{s}|)^{2}]\int^{T}_{t}(\Delta b^{u,v}_{x})_{s}ds+\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t}|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds]+C\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t}|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t}|\Delta f^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds]+\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t}|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds]

(7.25)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{T}|^{2}]+C\int^{T}_{t}|u_{s}-v_{s}|^{2}ds+C\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t}|\Delta X^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds]+C\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{t}|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds

(7.26)

Thus, by using the (backward) Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{t}|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{0}|\Delta X^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds]+\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{T}|^{2}]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|\Delta X^{u,v}_{t}|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[|\Delta X^{u,v}_{t}|^{2}]+\int^{T}_{0}|u_{s}-v_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{YZ}||u-v||^{2}_{2}

(7.27)

This naturally also imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\Delta Y^{u,v}_{t}|^{2}+\int^{T}_{t}|\Delta Z^{u,v}_{s}|^{2}ds]\leq C_{YZ}||u-v||^{2}_{2}

(7.28)

for some
C_{YZ}>0
.
∎

Proof for
Proposition
4.5
.

Proof.

Consider the following systems of equations.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}H_{t}^{{}^{\prime},u}:=\mathbb{E}\big[\alpha(X^{u}_{t})Y^{u}_{t}+\beta_{t}Z^{u}_{t}+\gamma(X^{u}_{t})\big]\\
H_{t}^{{}^{\prime},v}:=\mathbb{E}\big[\alpha(X^{v}_{t})Y^{v}_{t}+\beta_{t}Z^{v}_{t}+\gamma(X^{v}_{t})\big]\end{cases}

Take the difference between the two equations, square both sides and integrate from
0
to
T
to obtain:

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}|\mathbb{E}\big[\alpha(X^{u}_{t})Y^{u}_{t}-\alpha(X^{v}_{t})Y^{v}_{t}+\beta_{t}(Z^{u}_{t}-Z^{v}_{t})+\gamma(X^{u}_{t})-\gamma(X^{v}_{t})\big]|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int^{T}_{0}3|\mathbb{E}\big[\alpha(X^{u}_{t})Y^{u}_{t}-\alpha(X^{v}_{t})Y^{u}_{t}+\alpha(X^{v}_{t})Y^{u}_{t}-\alpha(X^{v}_{t})Y^{v}_{t}\big]|^{2}+3C\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}[|Z^{u}_{t}-Z^{v}_{t}|^{2}]dt+3\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}[|\gamma(X^{u}_{t})-\gamma(X^{v}_{t})|^{2}]dt

\displaystyle\leq 6C\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}[|Y^{u}_{t}-Y^{v}_{t}|^{2}]dt+6\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}[|\alpha(X^{u}_{t})-\alpha(X^{v}_{t})|^{2}](\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}[|Y^{u}_{t}|^{2}])dt+C||u-v||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C||u-v||^{2}

(7.29)

where we used the fact that
\alpha,\beta
are both uniformly bounded, and
\alpha
is Lipshitz in its argument. We also used Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.4
.
∎

We list here the explicit form for the higher order scheme used for the simulation of forward SDE which is Method (4) from

\displaystyle X_{n+1}

\displaystyle=X_{n}+b\Delta t+\sigma\Delta W_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\big((\Delta W_{n})^{2}-\Delta t\big)+\sigma b^{\prime}\Delta Q_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\big(bb^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}b^{\prime\prime}\big)\Delta t^{2}

\displaystyle+\big(b\sigma^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\sigma^{\prime\prime}\big)(\Delta W_{n}\Delta t-\Delta Q_{n})+\frac{1}{2}\sigma(\sigma\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(\sigma)^{2})(\frac{1}{3}\Delta W_{n}^{2}-\Delta t)\Delta W_{n}

where
\Delta Q_{n}=\int^{t_{n+1}}_{t_{n}}\int^{s}_{t_{n}}dW_{\tau}ds
is a Gaussian random variable satisfying
\mathbb{E}[\Delta Z_{n}]=0
,
\mathbb{E}[(\Delta Z_{n})^{2}]=\frac{\Delta t^{3}}{3}
and
\mathbb{E}[\Delta Z_{n}\Delta W_{n}]=\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}

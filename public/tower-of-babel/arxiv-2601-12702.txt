Igusa-Todorov properties of recollements of abelian categories
*

Abstract




\bf Abstract

In this paper, we investigate the behavior of Igusa-Todorov properties under recollements of abelian categories. In particular, we study how the Igusa-Todorov distances of the categories involved in a recollement are related.
Applications are given to Artin algebras, especially to Morita context rings.

Keywords:
Recollements of abelian categories; Igusa-Todorov distance;
(m,n)
-Igusa-Todorov algebras; Morita context rings.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
18G80, 18G25, 16E30.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
be an Artin algebra and let
\mathrm{mod}\text{-}A
denote the category of finitely generated right
A
-modules.
Recall that the finitistic dimension
\mathsf{fin.dim}A
of
A
is defined as the supremum of the projective dimension of all finitely generated right
A
-modules of finite projective dimension.
The finitistic dimension conjecture asserts that
\mathsf{fin.dim}A<\infty
for any Artin algebra
A.

There esists a variety of literature on the study of the finitistic dimension of Artin algebras. Recently, Xi in
[
15
,
16
,
17
]

developed various methods to detect finiteness of the finitistic dimension using Igusa-Todorov function introduced in
[
8
]
.
Based on Xi’s work, Wei introduced the concept of Igusa-Todorov algebras, and proved that the finitistic dimension conjecture holds for all Igusa-Todorov algebras in
[
14
]
.
In particular, this class of algebras contains algebras with radical cube zero, monomial algebras, and algebras of representation dimension at most 3 among others.
However, T. Conde
[
4
]
showed that not all Artin algebras are Igusa-Todorov algebras.
In order to measure how far an algebra is from being an Igusa-Todorov algebra, Zhang and Zheng
[
20
]
introduced the concept of the Igusa-Todorov distance of Artin algebras, and studied its behavior under stable equivalences, singular equivalence of Morita type with level and recollements of derived module categories. Moreover, they proved that this distance is not only finite but also can bound the dimension of the singularity category.
On the other hand, Zheng and Huang
[
23
]
gave an upper bound for the dimension of bounded derived categories of
n
-Igusa-Todorov algebras in terms of
n.

To further study the dimension of derived categories, Zheng
[
21
]
introduced
(m,n)
-Igusa-Todorov algebras as a generalization of
n
-Igusa-Todorov algebras, and derived an upper bound for the dimension of bounded derived categories of
(m,n)
-Igusa-Todorov algebras in terms of
m
and
n.
Recently, Ma, Zhang and Liu
[
11
]
also investigated Igusa-Todorov distances and the dimesion of bounded derived categories under the cleft extensions of abelian categories.

Recollements of abelian categories are exact sequences of abelian categories

0\rightarrow\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{\sf i}\mathscr{B}\xrightarrow{\sf e}\mathscr{C}\rightarrow 0

where both the inclusion functor
\mathsf{i}:\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}
and the quotient functor
\mathsf{e}:\mathscr{B}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}
have left and right adjoints.
Recollements have been introduced first in the context of triangulated categories by Beilinson, Bernstein and Deligne
[
2
]
, in their axiomatization of the Grothendieck’s six functors for derived categories of sheaves obtained from the stratification of spaces.
It should be noted that recollements of abelian categories appear quite naturally in various settings and are omnipresent in representation theory, providing a proper framework for investigating the homological connections among these categories.
For example, Psaroudakis
[
12
]
investigated homological properties of recollements of abelian categories, and then gave a series of bounds among the global, finitistic and representation dimensions of abelian categories linked by such recollements of abelian categories; Psaroudakis, Skartsæterhagen and Solberg
[
13
]
studied singularity equivalence, Gorensteinness, and finite generation conditions in recollements of abelian categories; Zhang
[
19
]
investigated the Gorenstein global dimension in recollements of abelian categories.

Inspired by this, the main purpose of this paper is to investigate the behavior of Igusa-Todorov properties under recollements of abelian categories.

Let
m
and
n
be nonnegative integers.
In order to study
(m,n)
-Igusa-Todorov algebras and Igusa-Todorov distances within a more general framework, Ma, Zheng and Liu introduced the concept of
(m,n)
-Igusa-Todorov categories in

be an abelian category with enough projectives.
Recall that abelian category

with

Let

be an Artin algebra, and let

be the category of finitely generated right

We note that

For an Artin algebra

3.
Igusa-Todorov properties

In this section we introduce the notion of the Igusa-Todorov distance of an abelian category, which is a generalization of the Igusa-Todorov distance of an Artin algebra given in
[
22
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an abelian category and
n
a positive integer.
If there is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow P_{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}}P_{n-2}\xrightarrow{d_{n-2}}\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}X\rightarrow 0

in
\mathscr{A}
such that each
P_{i}
is projective for
0\leq i\leq n-1
, then
K
is called an
n
-syzygy object of
X
.

Remark 3.2
.

For
X\in\mathscr{A}
, we know that its
n
-syzygy objects are not unique up to isomorphism, but are unique up to projective summands. Hence, for the sake of brevity, we denote by
\Omega^{n}(X)

, then
\mathscr{B}
is a
(\mathsf{gl.dim}_{\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{B}-t,t)
-Igusa-Todorov category.

(4)

Assume that
\sf q
is exact.
If
n+t+1\geq\mathsf{gl.dim}_{\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{B}
, then
\mathscr{B}
is an
(n+1,t)
-Igusa-Todorov category.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{C}
is an
(n,t)
-Igusa-Todorov category, it follows that for any

there exist an object
W\in\mathscr{C}
and an exact sequence

0\rightarrow W_{n}\rightarrow W_{n-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow W_{0}\rightarrow\Omega^{t}(\mathsf{e}(B))\rightarrow 0

with
W_{i}\in\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(W)
for
0\leq i\leq n
.
Then by Proposition
3.13
, we obtain the following exact sequence

(3.15)

0\rightarrow\Omega^{t+n+2}(\mathsf{iq}(B))\rightarrow\Omega^{t+n}(\mathsf{i}(A))\oplus Q_{n+1}\rightarrow\mathsf{l}(W_{n})\oplus Q_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X\oplus Q_{0}\rightarrow\Omega^{t}(B)\rightarrow 0

for some
A\in\mathscr{A}
, where each
Q_{i}\in\mathsf{Proj}\mathscr{B}
and
X\in\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\mathsf{l}(W)\oplus P)
.
We denote by

the cokernel of
\Omega^{t+n+2}(\mathsf{iq}(B))\rightarrow\Omega^{t+n}(\mathsf{i}(A))\oplus Q_{n+1}.

We know from

\mathsf{pd}_{\mathscr{B}}C\leq\mathsf{max}\{\mathsf{pd}_{\mathscr{B}}\Omega^{t+n+2}(\mathsf{iq}(B))+1,\mathsf{pd}_{\mathscr{B}}\Omega^{t+n}(\mathsf{i}(A))\}.

(1) Assume that
n+t<\mathsf{gl.dim}_{\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{B}<\infty
.
Then

\mathsf{pd}_{\mathscr{B}}C\leq\mathsf{max}\{\mathsf{pd}_{\mathscr{B}}\Omega^{t+n+2}(\mathsf{iq}(B))+1,\mathsf{pd}_{\mathscr{B}}\Omega^{t+n}(\mathsf{i}(A))\}\leq\mathsf{gl.dim}_{\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{B}-(t+n).

\mathsf{gl.dim}_{\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{B}=k
.

which gives the following exact sequence

(3.16)

\scalebox{1}{$0\rightarrow P_{k-(t+n)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow\mathsf{l}(W_{n})\oplus Q_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\mathsf{l}(W_{1})\oplus Q_{1}\rightarrow X\oplus Q_{0}\rightarrow\Omega^{t}(B)\rightarrow 0$}.

\mathsf{Proj}\mathscr{B}=\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(P)

P

is a
(\mathsf{gl.dim}_{\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{B}-t+1,t)

(2) If

\mathop{\rm add}\nolimits(\mathsf{l}(W)\oplus P)

is an
(n+2,t)
-Igusa-Todorov category.

The assertions (3) and (4) can be similarly proved by using Proposition

4.
Applications and examples

In this section, we apply our results to ring theory, building on Examples
2.3
and
2.4
.

For an Artin algebra
\Lambda
, the Igusa-Todorov distance of
\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\Lambda
is denoted by
\mathop{\rm IT.dist}\nolimits(\Lambda)
.
We use
{}_{\Lambda}M

\Lambda

(resp.,

We denote by

Following
[
12

, we denote by
\mathsf{gl.dim}_{\Lambda/\Lambda e\Lambda}\Lambda=\sup\{\mathsf{pd}X_{\Lambda}\mid X\in\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\Lambda/\Lambda e\Lambda\}.

The following corollary summarizes some results from Section 3, applied to the recollement of module categories
(\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\Lambda/\Lambda e\Lambda,\mathrm{mod}\text{-}\Lambda,\mathrm{mod}\text{-}e\Lambda e)
induced by an idempotent

in an Artin algebra
\Lambda
.

-Igusa-Todorov algebra and

\Lambda

is a

Assume that
\Lambda/\Lambda e\Lambda
is an
(m,r)
-Igusa-Todorov algebra and

is an
(n,s)
-Igusa-Todorov algebra. If

\Lambda

\Lambda

\Lambda
-module, then

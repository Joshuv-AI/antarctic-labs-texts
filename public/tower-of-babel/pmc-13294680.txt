Generation of an induced pluripotent stem cell line (NCHi026-A) from a patient with a partial deletion of exon 55 in the DMD gene
Authors: Hui Lin, Stefan Nicolau, Kayla Stevens, Tatyana A. Vetter, Sarah Atkins, Emma C. Frair, Nicolas Wein, Ming-Tao Zhao, Liubov V. Gushchina, Kevin M. Flanigan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is an X-linked genetic disorder disease which is characterized by progressive muscle degeneration or weakness due to the loss of functional dystrophin expression. For use as a cell-based disease model, we generated an induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) line (NCHi026-A) from fibroblasts derived from a skin biopsy of a 13-year-old patient with a nucleotide deletion across the DMD intron 54/exon55 junction (c.8028–501_8078del). The resulting cell line NCHi026-A was free of transgenes, expressed pluripotency-associated stem cell markers, maintained the normal karyotype and could be differentiated into three germ layers in vitro.

Resource utility

NCHi026-A was derived from a patient with a unique mutation in the DMD gene. With an ability to differentiate into any type of cells, iPSCs represent an attractive source for generating myoblasts for cell models of diseases and capturing genetic diversity of DMD patients for clinical or research applications.

Resource details

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a severe, progressive, and muscle-wasting disease caused by mutations in the X-linked DMD gene, which results in the loss of functional dystrophin protein, affecting 1 in 5,200 male births. Early symptoms are typically apparent around 2–3 years old with difficulties in climbing stairs, a waddling gait, and frequent falls. Around 10–12 years, most patients become wheelchair dependent, most require nocturnal ventilation assistance at around 20 years(Duan et al. 2021). Most patients die of cardiac and respiratory illnesses in the third or fourth decade of life(Heydemann and Siemionow 2023). Mouse models exist but only harbour a small subset of mutations, limiting studies relevant to pathogenesis or preclinical therapeutic development. Establishing primary myoblasts require invasive muscle biopsies, whereas skin biopsies are simple and well-tolerated. Furthermore, fibroblast-derived induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs) can be differentiated into any affected cell type, including myoblasts that may potentially recapitulate patient specific pathology and be used in a variety of culture model systems.

The DMD c.8028–501_8078del mutation alters splicing of exon 55, resulting in four different transcripts identified by long-read sequencing of RT-PCR amplicons from patient transdifferentiated myoblasts. Three utilize alternate splice acceptor sites, and the fourth deletes exon 55 entirely (Supplementary Fig. 1). In total, 93% of transcripts are out of frame.

We generated the hiPSC line NCHi026-A by using CytoTune-iPSC 2.0 Sendai virus (SeV) Reprogramming kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, cat. # A16517) (Table 1). On day 15, iPSCs clones had stem cell-like morphologies with a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio. Individual clones were picked and expanded in culture (Fig. 1A). By Sanger sequencing, we confirmed that the clone designated NCHi026-A has the same mutation as its parental cell line F486AB (Fig. 1B). A normal chromosome structure and numbers (46, XY) was confirmed by KaryoStat+ (Fig. 1C). To measure transgene free in NCHi026-A, total RNA was isolated from a 7-day post-transduced cell line F486AB as a positive control; a parental untransduced F486AB cell line as a negative control; and cell line NCHi026-A (passage 5). Compared to the positive control, we didn’t detect any reprogramming transgenes SeV and KOS by RT-PCR in NCHi026-A cells. (Fig. 1D). To demonstrate their undifferentiated hPSC state, we stained NCHi026-A cells with markers including TRA-1–60, OCT3/4 and NANOG. Cells highly expressed TRA-1–60 (92.23%), OCT3/4 (94.47%), ang NANOG (91.38%) (Fig. 1E and Supplementary Table 2). To evaluate its tri-lineage differentiation, iPSCs were differentiated into three germ layers using the Human Pluripotent Stem Cell Functional Identification Kit. We confirmed the expression of ectoderm (OTX2/SOX1), mesoderm (TBX6/HAND1), and endoderm (SOX17/GATA-4) markers by immunofluorescence staining (Fig. 1F). These results indicate that NCHi026-A could differentiate into cell types from all three germ layers. NCHi026-A cells are negative for mycoplasma (Supplementary Fig. 2). These characterizations and validations indicate that we have successfully generated the patient-specific iPSC line NCHi026-A, which could be useful for research studies.

Materials and methods

Reprogramming and cell culture

Fibroblasts derived from skin biopsy were plated onto a 6 well plate so that they were 30–40% confluency on the day of transduction (day 0). Cells were transduced by using three Sendai virus (SeV) reprogramming vectors containing KOS, hc-Myc and hKlf4 with MOI 5: 5: 4, respectively. After 1 day, medium was replaced with cDMEM containing DMEM (Gibco), 20% FBS (R&D Systems) and 1% Antibiotic-Antimycotic (AA, Gibco), and the following days medium was refreshed every other day. On day 7, transduced cells were replated onto a plate coated with 1:300 diluted Matrigel by DMEM/F12 in cDMEM. On day 8, medium was changed to the complete Essential 8 medium (E8, cat. # A1517001) and replaced spent medium daily until emergence of iPSC colonies. Cells were passaged every 3–4 days using 0.5 uM EDTA for dissociation, in the presence of the ROCK inhibitor to promote cell survival, according to a published protocol (Hanley et al. 2024).

Sanger sequencing

DNA was extracted from cell lines F486AB and NCHi026-A. PCR amplification was performed using AccuStart II PCR SuperMix, with the primers listed in Table 2. Initial denaturation was performed at 95 °C for 3 min, followed by 35 cycles of amplification as follows: 95 °C for 30 s, 60 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 60 s. Purified PCR products were analyzed on Applied Biosystems 3730xl DNA analyzer.

RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from 5×106 iPSCs using the TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen, cat. # 15–596–018). cDNA generation was performed with 1 μg of template RNA using the RevertAid RT Transcription Kit (Thermo Scientific, cat. # K1691) per the manufacturer’s instructions.

Immunofluorescence staining and quantification

Cells were seeded on sterile 18 mm coverslips coated with Matrigel in a 12-well plate. Cells were fixed 2 days later using 4% Paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 10 min, followed by gently rinsing with 0.2 M glycine in DPBS for 10 min. Permeabilization was performed with 0.5% Triton X-100 in DPBS for 10 min. Blocking and antibody incubations were performed in DPBS containing 10% normal goat serum. Primary antibodies (Table 2) were incubated overnight at 4 °C, then secondary antibodies diluted in blocking buffer were applied for 1 h. Cells were mounted on coverslips with ProLong Gold Antifade Mountant with DAPI (Invitrogen™, cat. # P36935). Images were acquired on a Nikon Ti2 microscope. Automated image analysis was performed using NIS-Elements AR with the General Analysis 3 (GA3) module (Supplementarty Table 2). DAPI-labeled nuclei were individually segmented using spot detection and the center of each nucleus was marked. Image pixels positive for the pluripotency markers were selected using automated thresholds calculated based on background signal intensities within the image. Cells were considered positive for OCT3/4 or NANOG (markers with nuclear expression) if the center of the nucleus overlapped with marker-positive pixels. Since TRA-1–60 is not a nuclear marker, cells were considered positive if the 8-μm diameter circle around the center of the nucleus overlapped with TRA-1–60-positive pixels. Four fields were captured from each coverslip, and the numbers of total nuclei and positive nuclei were summed to produce the final percent positive value for the sample.

Tri-lineage differentiation

The trilineage differentiation potential of fibroblast-derived iPSCs was assessed using the Human Pluripotent Stem Cell Functional Identification Kit (R&D Systems, cat. # SC027B).

Karyotyping

iPSC486AB (p6) cell pellets were sent to Thermo Fisher Scientific for KaryoStat + with Cell ID Assay Service. Karyotype was evaluated with whole genome array to detect chromosomal abnormalities. Cell ID was confirmed with a 1.1 M SNP correlation plot.

Mycoplasma detection

Mycoplasma contamination was evaluated using the VenorGeM Mycoplasma Detection Kit, PCR-based (Sigma-Aldrich, cat. #MP0025) following manufacturer’s instructions.

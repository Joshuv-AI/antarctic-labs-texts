Intra-Individual Comparative Study of Methotrexate versus Triamcinolone Acetonide Delivered via Microneedling for Recalcitrant Alopecia Areata
Authors: Mofreh Mansour, Refaat R Mohamed, Athar Fathy Abodief Mohamed, Mahmoud A Rageh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAlopecia areata (AA) is a chronic, immune-mediated, non-scarring hair loss disorder. While intralesional corticosteroids remain first-line treatment, some patients with recalcitrant AA respond poorly. Microneedling enhances transdermal drug delivery via microchannel formation. Methotrexate, an established systemic immunomodulator, is effective in resistant AA but has been less extensively studied for topical administration.ObjectivesTo compare the efficacy and safety of methotrexate and triamcinolone acetonide, both delivered via microneedling, in recalcitrant AA.MethodsIn this prospective, intra-individual comparative study, 34 patients with at least two patches of recalcitrant AA received microneedling followed by topical methotrexate (25 mg/mL) on one site and triamcinolone acetonide (5 mg/mL) on a matched site, administered fortnightly for 12 weeks. The primary outcome was the change in Severity of Alopecia Tool (SALT) score. Secondary outcomes included trichoscopic assessment, blinded photographic evaluation, patient satisfaction, and relapse rates at three-month follow-up. Safety was assessed at each visit.ResultsBoth treatment modalities were associated with overall clinical improvement. Methotrexate-treated areas showed significantly greater reductions in SALT scores (P<0.05), higher levels of patient satisfaction, and lower relapse rates at follow-up compared to triamcinolone-treated areas. Both modalities were well tolerated, with only mild, transient adverse effects reported.ConclusionsIn this pilot intra-individual study, microneedling-assisted methotrexate appeared to provide superior short-term clinical outcomes to triamcinolone acetonide with an acceptable safety profile in recalcitrant AA. These findings warrant confirmation in larger, adequately powered controlled trials with 6–12-month follow-up to evaluate durability, relapse, and long-term safety.Keywords: Alopecia areata, Microneedling, Methotrexate, Triamcinolone acetonide

Introduction

Alopecia areata (AA) is a common autoimmune disorder characterized by nonscarring hair loss with unpredictable progression and varied clinical presentations. It affects approximately 2% of the general population and is associated with considerable psychosocial morbidity [1]. While the exact etiology remains unclear, a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on anagen hair follicles is central to its patho genesis [2].

Topical and intralesional corticosteroids are widely used in the management of AA, particularly for localized disease. However, a subset of patients develops recalcitrant or treatment-resistant AA, necessitating alternative therapeutic approaches [3]. Methotrexate, an antimetabolite with immunosuppressive properties, has demonstrated efficacy when administered systemically in severe or extensive AA, but its topical administration remains underexplored [4].

Microneedling is a minimally invasive technique that enhances transdermal drug delivery by creating microchannels through the stratum corneum, thereby increasing drug bio-availability at the follicular level. Its ability to facilitate drug penetration and potentially stimulate local wound-healing responses makes it a promising adjunct in dermatological therapies [5]. Preliminary evidence supports local and device-assisted methotrexate delivery approaches in AA, although robust comparative data are lacking [6].

Objectives

This study aimed to compare the clinical outcomes of microneedling-assisted topical methotrexate with triamcinolone acetonide in patients with recalcitrant AA using a controlled, intra-individual comparative design.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This prospective, intra-individual comparative study included 34 patients with clinically diagnosed recalcitrant multifocal alopecia areata (AA). Recalcitrant AA was defined as a failure to respond to previous treatment modalities with poor or partial response or relapse following discontinuation of therapy [8]. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants or legal guardian in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Given its exploratory nature, the study was conceived as a pilot investigation; no formal a priori power calculation was performed. The sample size of 34 consecutive eligible patients reflected the number of patients presenting during the recruitment period and was considered sufficient to assess feasibility and to generate preliminary estimates of efficacy and safety.

Within each patient, different alopecic patches were randomly assigned to receive either methotrexate or triamcinolone acetonide. Exclusion criteria included age under 12 years, cicatricial alopecia or AA unilocularis, ophiasis, totalis, or universalis subtypes. Patients with an active scalp infection or dermatitis, pregnancy or lactation, systemic immunosuppressive therapy within the two months prior to enrolment, an immunocompromised status, hematological disorders, or other causes of non-AA alopecia were also excluded.

Randomization and Intervention

Patients were not assigned to separate treatment groups; instead, each received both treatment modalities on different scalp areas using an intra-individual design. Randomization of treatment sides was performed using a simple coin toss. One designated area was treated with microneedling followed by topical methotrexate at a concentration of 25 mg/mL, with a maximum total volume of 0.1–0.2 mL (equivalent to 2.5–5 mg). The other area received microneedling followed by topical triamcinolone acetonide at a concentration of 5 mg/mL, with a maximum total volume of 0.5–1 mL.

The concentrations used (methotrexate 25 mg/mL and triamcinolone acetonide 5 mg/mL) were selected within clinically utilized ranges for intralesional and device-assisted regimens in alopecia areata, aiming to balance efficacy with safety and procedural practicality [9–12]. While the pharmacological potency of the two agents is not directly comparable on a milligram-to-milligram basis, our intention was to contrast two clinically relevant, commonly implemented protocols under uniform microneedling delivery conditions rather than to establish pharmacodynamic equivalence.

A topical anesthetic cream was applied under occlusion to the affected areas for 60 minutes before the procedure. Microneedling was performed using a dermapen device (Dr. Pen® A6S Wireless, USA) fitted with a 12-needle disposable tip, at a depth of 1.5–2.5 mm. Needle speed and movement were adjusted according to patient tolerance. The endpoint was the appearance of pinpoint bleeding. Topical treatments were applied before, during, and immediately after microneedling. Treatments were administered fortnightly over a 12-week period, totaling six sessions. Each session was followed by a 30-minute occlusion period.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome was the change in Severity of Alopecia Tool (SALT) score from baseline to week 12 [7]. Secondary outcomes included trichoscopic assessment of disease activity and regrowth features using the DermLite DL4 (Model DL4D3248, 3Gen Inc., San Juan Capistrano, CA, USA), patient satisfaction measured on a five-point Likert scale (0 = not satisfied, 1 = poorly satisfied, 2 = moderately satisfied, 3 = satisfied, 4 = very satisfied), and global photographic assessment by two blinded dermatologists.

Hair regrowth was evaluated using two validated scales:

General regrowth scale: <10% = no response; 11–25% = poor; 26–50% = fair; 51–75% = satisfactory; ≥75% = excellent response.

MacDonald Hull and Norris grading system: Grade 1= vellus hair regrowth; Grade 2 = sparse pigmented terminal hair (<50% SALT improvement); Grade 3 = terminal hair with patchy regrowth (50–75% SALT improvement); Grade 4 = complete terminal regrowth (>75% SALT improvement) [13].

Relapse rates were recorded at the three-month follow-up. Safety was assessed by documenting any adverse effects at each treatment session.

This manuscript was prepared in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). All analyses followed an intra-individual (paired) framework. Continuous paired outcomes (e.g., change in SALT score) were summarized as mean ± standard deviation and compared using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, as appropriate. Categorical paired outcomes (e.g., presence or absence of trichoscopic signs, adverse events) were compared using McNemar’s test, and multi-category paired outcomes (e.g., response categories, regrowth grades) were analyzed using the Stuart-Maxwell test. Two-sided p-values of <0.05 were considered statistically significant. Given the pilot nature of the study, emphasis was placed on estimation of effect sizes and 95 % confidence intervals (CI) in addition to hypothesis testing.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

Thirty-four patients with recalcitrant AA were enrolled. Each patient received both methotrexate- and triamcinolone-treated areas on different scalp sites. The mean age was 26.5 ± 9.76 years (range 12–43 years), with 73.5 % being male. The mean disease duration was 11.91 ± 15.12 months. Nail involvement was observed in 14.7 % of cases, including longitudinal ridging (8.8 %) and nail pitting (5.9 %).

Clinical Efficacy

At the end of the 12-week treatment period, methotrexate-treated areas demonstrated a significantly greater reduction in SALT scores compared with triamcinolone-treated areas (mean reduction 23.6 ± 6.2 vs. 17.9 ± 5.8; P=0.020). Complete hair regrowth (SALT score = 0) was achieved in 85.3 % of methotrexate-treated areas compared with 67.6 % of triamcinolone-treated areas (P=0.012) (Table 1).

Trichoscopic Assessment

Baseline trichoscopic findings were similar in both treatment sites and included empty follicles, exclamation mark hairs, broken hairs, and black dots. Posttreatment, both groups demonstrated a marked reduction in these features. In methotrexate-treated areas, empty follicles decreased from 85.3 % to 11.8 % (−73.5 percentage points), exclamation-mark hairs from 29.4 % to 2.9 % (−26.5 percentage points), and black dots from 17.6 % to 0 % (−17.6 percentage points; all P≤0.031). Similar within-arm improvements were observed in triamcinolone-treated areas (empty follicles 82.4 %→11.8 %, exclamation-mark hairs 20.6 %→0 %, black dots 26.5 %→0 %; all P≤0.016). Regrowing hairs increased from 11.8 % to 41.2 % (+29.4 percentage points) and upright regrowing hairs from 0 % to 38.2 % (+38.2 percentage points) on methotrexate sites, and from 14.7 % to 38.2 % (+23.5 percentage points) and 0 % to 35.3 % (+35.3 percentage points), respectively, on triamcinolone sites. Between-treatment comparisons for trichoscopic markers were not statistically significant (P>0.05), indicating broadly comparable dermoscopic patterns of recovery despite the superior clinical outcomes observed with methotrexate (Table 5).

Safety and Adverse Effects

Adverse effects were mild and transient. Pain was reported in 17.6 % of methotrexate-treated areas and in 2.9 % of triamcinolone-treated areas (P=0.317). Hyperpigmentation occurred in 14.7 % of methotrexate-treated areas, while telangiectasia and atrophy were each reported in 14.7 % of triamcinolone-treated areas (P=0.060). Recurrence occurred in 11.8 % versus 17.6 % of methotrexate-and triamcinolone-treated areas, respectively (P=1.000). Overall, no statistically significant between-treatment difference in adverse events was observed, suggesting comparable safety profiles under microneedling delivery (Table 6).

Discussion

Recalcitrant alopecia areata remains a therapeutic challenge, often requiring the exploration of alternatives to conventional corticosteroids [14]. In this intra-individual pilot study, microneedling-assisted methotrexate was associated with significantly greater reductions in SALT scores, higher rates of complete regrowth, and improved patient satisfaction compared to triamcinolone acetonide.

The enhanced efficacy of methotrexate may relate to its established immunosuppressive effects and the improved dermal penetration achieved via microneedling. This technique facilitates both transfollicular and transepidermal delivery while also inducing local growth factor release and perifollicular angiogenesis, mechanisms that may act synergistically with methotrexate’s immunomodulatory action. In contrast, while triamcinolone remains a cornerstone in AA management, its recognized cutaneous adverse effects, such as telangiectasia, and atrophy, were marginally more common in our study, albeit without statistical significance [15–18].

Our results partially align with those of Hamdino et al. [19], who reported better short-term outcomes with triamcinolone at 12 weeks but greater efficacy with methotrexate at later follow-up. The difference may reflect the more consistent drug delivery depth achieved with microneedling in our study compared to the operator-dependent variability of intralesional injections [9].

Trichoscopic improvement was evident in both groups, with notable reductions in disease activity markers such as black dots, broken hairs, and exclamation mark hairs, alongside increased regrowing hairs [10,20,21]. Although there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups in trichoscopic findings, the superior clinical results with methotrexate suggest that its deeper immunological effects may precede or extend beyond early follicular changes.

Importantly, methotrexate-treated areas required fewer sessions to achieve visible regrowth, suggesting a more efficient treatment trajectory. From a practical perspective, methotrexate offers additional advantages: it is widely available, inexpensive, and does not require refrigeration or specialized preparation. These attributes make it particularly suitable for resource-limited settings or for patients wishing to avoid corticosteroid-associated adverse effects [22].

Recurrence rates in our study were similar between the two modalities (11.8 % vs. 17.6 %), and although not statistically significant, methotrexate-treated patches showed slightly more durable responses. These findings are consistent with those of Hamdino et al. [19], who reported a single relapse in the triamcinolone group and none in the methotrexate group at three-month follow-up. In our cohort, four patients in the methotrexate arm and six in the triamcinolone arm experienced recurrence or new lesion development during follow-up.

In line with Rodríguez-Villa Lario et al. [6], who demonstrated successful outcomes using intralesional methotrexate combined with fractional CO laser in refractory AA, our results affirm the potential of enhancing methotrexate’s efficacy through minimally invasive delivery techniques. Likewise, our findings partially corroborate those of El Mulla et al. [11], who noted marked improvement with microneedling–triamcinolone combinations.

Furthermore, Ageeba et al. [12] reported significant improvements in both clinical and dermoscopic parameters using microneedling with triamcinolone, particularly in terms of patient satisfaction. Our findings reinforce the idea that microneedling facilitates effective steroid delivery while offering a less painful, cost-effective alternative to conventional intralesional injections. However, the steroid-sparing advantage of methotrexate, coupled with similar or superior outcomes, positions it as a compelling candidate in resistant AA management.

A notable strength of this study is its intra-individual design, which minimizes inter-patient variability and strengthens the comparative analysis. Another distinguishing feature is the consistency of drug delivery depth through microneedling, which may mitigate variability inherent to manual injection techniques. This approach is particularly advantageous in widespread or irregularly distributed lesions where intralesional injection is impractical. Furthermore, the use of objective (SALT scores) and semi-objective (trichoscopy and blinded photographic assessment) outcome measures enhances the robustness and reliability of the results.

In summary, our results support the use of microneedling-assisted methotrexate as a safe and effective therapeutic option in recalcitrant alopecia areata. The method provides a well-tolerated, minimally invasive alternative with superior clinical outcomes and comparable trichoscopic improvement. While both treatments were effective, methotrexate demonstrated a clear advantage in several outcome measures, warranting further exploration in controlled trials with larger cohorts and extended observation periods.

This study has several limitations. First, the follow-up period of three months is relatively short and may be insufficient to characterize long-term remission, relapse patterns, and durability of response; future studies should incorporate 6–12-month outcomes. Second, although the photographic assessments were blinded, the treatment itself was open-label, which may have introduced bias into subjective outcomes such as patient satisfaction and possibly clinician-performed trichoscopy. Third, the modest sample size (N=34) limits statistical power and generalizability; accordingly, these findings should be interpreted as pilot data that require confirmation in larger, adequately powered cohorts. Finally, histological confirmation of follicular changes was not undertaken.

Conclusions

This pilot intra-individual study suggests that microneedling-assisted methotrexate may provide superior short-term clinical outcomes to triamcinolone acetonide in recalcitrant alopecia areata, with greater reductions in SALT scores, higher rates of complete regrowth, and improved patient satisfaction, while both modalities were well tolerated with only mild, self-limiting adverse effects. The intra-individual design strengthens the comparative signal and supports methotrexate as a promising steroid-sparing alternative, particularly for patients in whom corticosteroid therapy is ineffective, contraindicated, or poorly tolerated, and its accessibility, low cost, and ease of storage further support its potential utility in resource-limited settings. These findings justify further evaluation of microneedling-assisted methotrexate in larger multicenter randomized controlled trials with at least 6–12 months of follow-up to assess sustained efficacy, safety and relapse rates over time.

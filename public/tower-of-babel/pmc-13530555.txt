Poster 172. Collegiate Athletes Do Not Have Higher Musculoskeletal Health Literacy Than Their Non-Athlete Peers: A Survey of 213 College Students
Authors: Samantha L Watson, Samuel B Perez, Whitney G Currie, Andrew T Pickens, Michael O Sohn, Vehniah K Tjong, Noah Forrest
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Healthcare providers perceive student-athletes to possess advanced knowledge of the musculoskeletal (MSK) system due to their years of intense athletic training and exposure to sports medicine personnel. The objective of this study is to use the Literacy in Musculoskeletal Problems (LiMP) questionnaire-a validated survey measuring MSK health literacy-to determine whether athletes, including Division I (D-I) and club athletes, possess significantly higher MSK health literacy than their non-athlete counterparts.Methods:Eligible participants were recruited from a single university with D-I athletics. Participants were required to be 18 years of age or older and currently enrolled in classes. Demographic information was collected, including year in school, history of orthopaedic injury, participation in an injury prevention program (IPP), and affiliation with a D-I or club sports team, and the LiMP survey questions were asked. Mean LiMP scores were calculated, and unpaired t-tests were used to compare group means.Results:A total of 213 students participated in the survey (100 D-I athletes, 40 club athletes, 73 general population students, 62% female; Table 1). The mean LiMP score for athletes (D-I and club) was 5.42 (SD=0.29) and 5.37 (SD=0.39) for non-athletes; the difference between the two groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.835). When analyzing D-I vs. non-D-I students (including club athletes), the mean LiMP score between groups remained insignificant (5.39 ± 0.35 vs. 5.42 ± 0.31; p=0.912). Notably, the completion of pre-health coursework (p=0.00) and participation in an IPP (p = 0.04) were significantly associated with higher MSK health literacy scores. All other variables, including sex, year in school, history of prior injury, and participation in club sports, did not yield significant differences (Table 2).Conclusions:The difference in mean LiMP scores between athletes and non-athletes was not statistically significant, suggesting that participation in athletics may not correlate with significantly higher MSK health literacy. This relationship held for D-I vs. non-D-I students. Specialized types of MSK knowledge exposure, including pre-health coursework and IPP participation, may appreciably augment MSK health literacy. Our findings underscore the importance of assessing each patient's health literacy and avoiding assumptions based on athletic experience. By understanding the MSK health literacy of individual student-athletes, providers can implement patient-centered care and shared decision-making strategies that improve patient engagement and, ultimately, enhance patient experience and outcomes.

Scaling solutions in three-form cosmology
Vitor da Fonseca, Bruno J. Barros, Tiago Barreiro, Nelson J. Nunes

Abstract
A hybrid three-form model of dark energy is developed in order to identify scaling solutions, a long-sought feature in three-form cosmology. Exploiting Hodge dualities, the theory is formulated in terms of two scalar functions that are associated with the conjugate momentum, and the three-form dual vector in an isotropic background. The resulting Lagrangian yields a stable scaling attractor where the three-form energy density tracks the dominant background fluid. A dynamical mechanism is also identified that naturally drives the system out of this regime toward a late-time accelerated phase distinguishable from a cosmological constant. This constitutes the first realization of scaling behavior within a three-form dark energy framework.

Among possible scenarios, scaling solutions are particularly appealing, as they allow the dark energy density to evolve dynamically and to remain non-negligible in the early universe. In such configurations, the energy scale of the dark component can approach that of radiation or matter, thereby alleviating the coincidence problem. More importantly, these solutions often exhibit attractor behavior, being weakly sensitive to initial conditions and effectively providing a self-tuning mechanism. In this picture, the observed energy composition of the universe today can be viewed as the outcome of an attractor-like evolution. However, an additional process is required to enable an exit from the scaling regime so that dark energy eventually dominates at late times, in agreement with present observations.

II
The hybrid formalism

II.1
First order formulation of a massive three-form effective field theory

Let us start by considering a massive three-form field
A_{\mu\nu\rho}
within a first order Lagrangian formulation,

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{48}f(\Pi^{2})+\frac{1}{6}\Pi^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\nabla_{[\mu}A_{\nu\rho\sigma]}-V(A^{2})\,,

(1)

where
\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is an arbitrary four-form auxiliary field, and the notation for squaring a tensor indicates full contraction of its indices, i.e.,
{A^{2}\equiv A^{\mu\nu\rho}A_{\mu\nu\rho}}
. The function
f(\Pi^{2})
generalizes the kinetic term, while
{V(A^{2})\neq 0}
represents a self-interaction potential. For simplicity, we omit the explicit indices throughout this section.

To recover a second-order theory, the auxiliary field
\Pi
can be integrated out by solving its algebraic equation of motion,

\Pi f^{\prime}(\Pi^{2})=-4\left[\nabla A\right]\,,

(2)

where a prime denotes differentiation with respect to the argument in parentheses. Substituting this relation back into Eq. (
1
) yields a Lagrangian expressed purely in terms of the three-form field and its derivatives,

\mathcal{L}(A,\nabla A)=\left[\frac{1}{48}\bigg(f(\Pi^{2})-2\Pi^{2}f^{\prime}(\Pi^{2})\bigg)-V(A^{2})\right]_{\Pi(\nabla A)}

(3)

We note that in the simple case
{f(\Pi^{2})=-\Pi^{2}}
, Eq. (
2
) identifies the conjugate momentum
\Pi
with the field-strength tensor of the three-form,
\Pi=\mathrm{d}A
. Substituting this relation back into Eq. (
1
) recovers the canonical massive three-form Lagrangian
26
, corresponding to the standard kinetic term.

Conversely, we may derive the dynamical equations for the three-form
A
,

\nabla\cdot\Pi=-12AV^{\prime}(A^{2})\,,

(4)

which allows us to find the relation
{A(\nabla\cdot\Pi)}
and thereby rewrite the original Lagrangian as an effective four-form theory:

\mathcal{L}(\Pi,\nabla\Pi)=\left[\frac{1}{48}f(\Pi^{2})+2A^{2}V^{\prime}(A^{2})-V(A^{2})\right]_{A(\nabla\cdot\Pi)}\,,

(5)

where the mixed term in Eq. (
1
) has been integrated by parts.

II.2
Hodge dualities take the stage

We now introduce the Hodge duals to the fields
\Pi
and
A
,

\displaystyle\left(\star\Pi\right)=\frac{1}{4!}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\Pi^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=:\phi\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\Pi_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=-\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\phi\,,\quad

(6)

\displaystyle\left(\star A\right)_{\mu}=\frac{1}{3!}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}A^{\nu\rho\sigma}=:B_{\mu}\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,A_{\nu\rho\sigma}=\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}B^{\mu}\,,\quad

(7)

which are a scalar and a vector,
\phi
and
B^{\mu}
, respectively. Here
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
is the generalized Levi-Civita symbol. These Hodge duals let us map high-rank antisymmetric tensors to more tractable quantities, simplifying the dynamical analysis. In four spacetime dimensions, the three-form
A
behaves dynamically like a vector field
B^{\mu}
while the four-form
\Pi
behaves like a scalar field
\phi
. Thus, we can square the above relations to find,

\Pi^{2}=-24\phi^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad A^{2}=-6B^{2}\,.

(8)

This allows us to rewrite the initial Lagrangian, Eq. (
1
), in a hybrid representation of a vector and a scalar,

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{48}f(-24\phi^{2})-\phi\nabla\cdot B-V(-6B^{2})\,.

(9)

Again, the three-form
A
is now expressed through the (Hodge) 1-form
B
and the auxiliary four-form
\Pi
through the 0-form (scalar)
\phi
. Performing integration by parts in the middle term of Eq. (
9
), we can write

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{48}f(-24\phi^{2})+B\cdot\nabla\phi-V(-6B^{2})\,.

(10)

Naturally, both Eq. (
9
) and (
10
) share the same equations of motion, which read:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot B

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\phi f^{\prime}(-24\phi^{2})\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\nabla\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-12BV^{\prime}(-6B^{2})\,.

(12)

These are also obtainable by direct dualization of Eqs. (
2
) and (
4
).

The latter Lagrangian, Eq. (
10
), gives the following components for the energy momentum tensor,

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}+g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2B_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi-12V^{\prime}(-6B^{2})B_{\mu}B_{\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}\,,\quad\quad\quad

where
g_{\mu\nu}
is the spacetime metric, which is not yet particularized.

II.3
Isotropic background

Let us now adopt a parametrization compatible with a FLRW isotropic metric with signature
{(-+++)}
. Assuming spatial flatness, the line element is given by
25
;
30
;
41
,

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\eta_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\,,

(14)

for spatial indices
i,j
and cosmic time
t
, where
a
is the dimensionless scale factor that parametrizes the expansion of the universe, and
\eta_{ij}
denotes the three-dimensional Euclidean spatial metric.

The three-form dual vector can be written with the aid of a scalar function
\chi(t)
, dependent only on cosmic time at background level
26
. Thus, its components are

B^{0}=\chi(t)\quad\text{and}\quad B^{i}=0\,,

(15)

and the invariant
B^{2}
reads

B^{2}=B^{\mu}B_{\mu}=B^{0}B_{0}=-\chi^{2}\,,

(16)

in the isotropic background. Additionally, from Eq. (
7
), the non-vanishing components of the three-form are

A_{ijk}=\sqrt{-g}\,\epsilon_{ijk}\chi(t)=a^{3}(t)\chi(t)\,,

(17)

where
g
is the metric determinant and
\epsilon_{ijk}
the standard three-dimensional Levi-Civita. At linear level, this allows us to recast the Lagrangian of the hybrid formalism, Eq. (
10
), as a function of two scalars,
\phi
and
\chi
.

Accordingly, the stress-energy tensor in Eq. (
13
) with the parametrization in Eq. (
15
) reads

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2B_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi-\frac{V_{,\chi}}{\chi}B_{\mu}B_{\nu}

(18)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\Big[-U(\phi)-V(\chi)+B^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\phi\Big]\,,\quad

where we have defined
U
as

U=-\frac{f}{48}\,,

(19)

recalling that the function
f
generalizes the kinetic term of the massive three-form in the original Lagrangian (
1
). If the three-form is treated as a perfect fluid, the temporal and spatial components of the energy-momentum tensor,
T^{0}{}_{0}=-\rho_{A}
and
T^{i}{}_{j}=p_{A}\delta^{i}{}_{j}
, respectively, define the energy density and pressure of the three-form in the FLRW metric as,

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U(\phi)+V(\chi)-\chi V_{,\chi}+\chi\dot{\phi}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle p_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-U(\phi)-V(\chi)+\chi\dot{\phi}\,,

(21)

where an overdot denotes differentiation with respect to cosmic time.

Owing to the first-order nature of the formalism, the dynamics of the scalars are governed by two first-order equations of motion, which are mathematically coupled. Rewriting the equations of motion Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
) in the isotropic background gives

\displaystyle\dot{\chi}+3H\chi

\displaystyle=-U_{,\phi}\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle=V_{,\chi}\,.

(23)

Using Eq. (
23
), the on-shell three-form energy density and pressure are

\displaystyle\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U(\phi)+V(\chi)\,,

(24)

\displaystyle p_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-U(\phi)-V(\chi)+\chi V_{,\chi}\,,

(25)

respectively. Since
U
and
V
play the role of self-interacting terms in the equations of motion Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
), we will simply refer to them as potentials.

Although
\phi
and
\chi
are not directly observable, they fully encode the dynamics of the original three-form field whose mixed Lagrangian reads,

\mathcal{L}=-U(\phi)-V(\chi)+\chi V_{,\chi}\,.

(26)

While the conventional massive canonical three-form (with a standard kinetic term and typical self-interacting potentials) fails to produce scaling solutions
28
;
29
;
20
, we explore whether such cosmologies can emerge from the hybrid model (
26
) within a general relativistic FLRW background.

III
Scaling solutions

III.1
Setting the cosmological background

We embed the effective field theory within standard Einstein gravity by adding an Einstein-Hilbert term to the total action. The resulting field equations yield the Friedmann constraint,

3H^{2}=V(\chi)+U(\phi)+\rho_{\gamma}\,,

(27)

in natural units, where
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble function. The energy density
\rho_{\gamma}
represents that of an additional background fluid with a barotropic index
\gamma
satisfying
0<\gamma<2
:
{\gamma=4/3}
for radiation and
{\gamma=1}
for pressureless matter. The continuity equation for this background component is then

\rho_{\gamma}^{\prime}=-3\gamma\rho_{\gamma}\,,

(28)

where a prime denotes differentiation with respect to the number of
e
-folds,
N\equiv\ln a
. Integrating it gives

\rho_{\gamma}\propto e^{-3\gamma N}\,.

(29)

As for the three-form fluid, the energy conservation reads

\rho_{A}^{\prime}=-3\left(\rho_{A}+p_{A}\right)\,,\quad\quad\rho_{A}+p_{A}=\chi V_{,\chi}\,,

(30)

according to Eqs. (
24
) and (
25
).

Our goal is to identify scaling solutions in cosmological regimes where the universe can be approximately described by radiation or pressureless matter (baryons and dark matter), together with a dark energy component represented by the three-form field. In particular, we are interested in solutions for which the energy densities of the dominant background fluid and dark energy remain of the same order of magnitude over an extended period, thereby alleviating the cosmological coincidence problem
36
.

Denoting the fractional energy densities of the three-form and the dominant background fluid by

\Omega_{A}=\frac{\rho_{A}}{3H^{2}}\,,\quad\quad\Omega_{\gamma}=\frac{\rho_{\gamma}}{3H^{2}}\,,

(31)

respectively, a scaling regime is characterized by a constant, non-zero ratio
\Omega_{A}/\Omega_{\gamma}

49
;
48
. Using Eqs. (
24
) and (
29
), and assuming
{U\propto V}
in the scaling, we may write

U\propto V\propto\rho_{A}\propto\rho_{\gamma}\propto e^{-3\gamma N}\,,

(32)

that is, the potentials dilute at the same rate

\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}=\frac{U^{\prime}}{U}=3\gamma\,,

(33)

during the cosmological expansion, which satisfies

\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}=-\frac{3\gamma}{2}\,.

(34)

This is analogous to the constancy of the kinetic-to-potential energy ratio in the scaling regime of a canonical scalar field
38
.

With Eqs. (
30
) and (
32
), we have

\rho_{A}+p_{A}=\chi V_{,\chi}\propto e^{-3\gamma N}\,,

(35)

and therefore
\chi V_{,\chi}\propto e^{-3\gamma N}
is the general condition that characterizes scaling solutions for the three-form Lagrangian (
26
) derived from first-order formulation.

For the record, we can introduce the adiabatic sound speed of the three-form background, defined by

c_{a}^{2}=\frac{p_{A}^{\prime}}{\rho_{A}^{\prime}}\,.

(36)

Since
\rho_{A}^{\prime}=-3(\rho_{A}+p_{A})=-3\chi V_{,\chi}
, the sound speed reads

\displaystyle c_{a}^{2}=-\frac{(\chi V_{,\chi})^{\prime}}{3\chi V_{,\chi}}-1\,.

(37)

Under the scaling condition in Eq. (
35
), it reduces to

c_{a}^{2}=\gamma-1\,,

(38)

along a scaling trajectory, as expected.

III.2
Finding the general scaling form for
V
and
U

To find the form of the potential
V(\chi)
that realizes a scaling solution from the general condition in Eq. (
35
), we write

\frac{d\ln{(\rho_{A}+p_{A})}}{dN}=\frac{\partial\ln(\chi V_{,\chi})}{\partial\ln\chi}\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}=-3\gamma\,.

(39)

Moreover, since Eq. (
35
) can be written as

\frac{\chi}{\chi^{\prime}}V^{\prime}\propto e^{-3\gamma N}\,,

(40)

and
{V^{\prime}\propto e^{-3\gamma N}}
according to Eq. (
32
),
\chi^{\prime}/\chi
is a constant that we call
\alpha\neq 0
:

\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}=\alpha\,.

(41)

Substituting Eq. (
41
) in Eq. (
39
):

\frac{\partial\ln(\chi V_{,\chi})}{\partial\ln\chi}=-\frac{3\gamma}{\alpha}\,,

(42)

which can be integrated to obtain the following power-law for the potential
V
that produces solutions

V(\chi)=V_{0}\chi^{n}\,,

(43)

where
V_{0}
is a mass scale and
n
is the non-vanishing constant,

n\equiv-\frac{3\gamma}{\alpha}\,.

(44)

Accordingly, Eq. (
41
) reads

\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}=-\frac{3\gamma}{n}\,.

(45)

Now, we seek to determine the corresponding form of
U(\phi)
required for the existence of scaling solutions. To do so, from Eqs. (
39
) and (
23
) we note,

\frac{d\ln(\chi\phi^{\prime}H)}{dN}=\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}+\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}+\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}=-3\gamma\,,

(46)

which leads to

\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}=3\gamma\frac{2-n}{2n}\,,

(47)

where we used Eqs. (
34
) and (
45
).

According to the latter expression, the way
\phi
evolves depends on the value of
n
. For
{n=2}
,
\phi^{\prime}
is constant and
U(\phi)
is an exponential potential, which corresponds to a canonical scalar field. For
n\neq 2
, one finds it is
\phi^{\prime}/\phi
that is constant, in which case
U(\phi)
is a power-law form that achieves scaling. The exponential and power-law cases are presented in the next two sections, respectively.

III.3
Canonical scalar field

By setting
{n=2}
, we can readily identify a trivial scaling solution corresponding to the canonical scalar field with an exponential potential. Following Eq. (
47
),
\phi^{\prime\prime}=0
and therefore

\phi^{\prime}=\lambda\,,

(48)

where
\lambda
is a non-vanishing constant. Therefore, the potential
U(\phi)
must take an exponential form because

\frac{U_{,\phi}}{U}=\frac{U^{\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}U}=-\frac{3\gamma}{\lambda}\,,

(49)

as
{U^{\prime}/U=-3\gamma}
according to Eq. (
33
). We can thus write

U(\phi)=U_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{3\gamma}{\lambda}\phi\right)\,,

(50)

where
U_{0}
is a constant energy scale. Since we are in the case
n=2
, the potential
V(\chi)
in Eq. (
43
) reads

V(\chi)=V_{0}\chi^{2}\,,

(51)

where we can set
V_{0}=1/2
. Then, the equation of motion for
\phi
(
23
) reads

\dot{\phi}=\chi\,.

(52)

Plugging Eqs. (
50
), (
51
) and (
52
) into the hybrid three-form Lagrangian (
26
) we obtain

\mathcal{L}=X-U\,,

(53)

where

X=-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\phi)^{2}\,,

(54)

which corresponds precisely to the Lagrangian of a quintessence field with an exponential potential, whose cosmological scaling behavior is well established
15
.

III.4
Two related power-laws

For
n\neq 2
, the potential
U(\phi)
is determined using

\frac{U^{\prime}}{U}=\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\phi}\frac{\phi U_{,\phi}}{U}\,,

(55)

which leads to

\frac{\phi U_{,\phi}}{U}=\frac{2n}{n-2}\,,

(56)

in accordance with Eqs. (
33
) and (
47
).
Hence, integrating this expression, the potential
U
takes a power-law form,

U(\phi)=U_{0}\phi^{2n/(n-2)}\,.

(57)

As a general result, plugging Eqs. (
43
) and (
57
) into (
26
), we find that the three-form Lagrangian that yields scaling with the dominant background fluid consists of two power-laws whose exponents are related:

\mathcal{L}=V_{0}(n-1)\chi^{n}-U_{0}\phi^{2n/(n-2)}\,,

(58)

with
n\neq 0,1,2
.

For convenience, we can normalize the amplitude
V_{0}
to unity, and introduce the constant mass scale
M
for the potential
U
. This can be done without loss of generality, since a rescaling of the fields can always absorb the original coefficients of the two potentials. The corresponding scaling Lagrangian is

\mathcal{L}=(n-1)\chi^{n}-\left(\frac{\phi}{M}\right)^{2n/(n-2)}\,.

(59)

While it is convenient to work with this hybrid formulation, expressed by the dual variables
(\chi,\phi)
, the theory can be written in the usual three-form framework, by using Eq. (
22
),

\mathcal{L}\propto\chi^{n}-\left(\dot{\chi}+3H\chi\right)^{2n/(n+2)}\,.

(60)

It can equivalently be reformulated in a pure scalar field representation, from Eq. (
23
),

\mathcal{L}\propto\dot{\phi}^{n/(n-1)}-\phi^{2n/(n-2)}\,.

(61)

In Appendix
B
, we demonstrate that the latter Lagrangian is consistent with the class of scaling solutions obtained for general k-essence models
38
;
46
.

It is important to note, however, that although three-form theories can, in principle, be dualized into a scalar field, the procedure often becomes algebraically intractable
24
;
27
;
44
. In contrast, the three-form formulation (
59
), written in terms of the dual variables
(\chi,\phi)
, provides a more transparent and tractable description of the cosmological dynamics, with first-order equations of motion, Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
), that greatly simplify the analysis.

In this context, the following section focuses on a dynamical analysis of the cosmological equations derived from the hybrid Lagrangian that provides for scaling solutions. We aim to identify the critical points in phase space and investigate the existence of cosmological attractors.

IV
Dynamical system analysis

IV.1
Autonomous equations

We turn to the study of the cosmological dynamics of the model admitting scaling solutions, derived from the three-form Lagrangian in Eq. (
58
), where the potentials take the power-law form

V(\chi)=V_{0}|\chi|^{n}\,,\quad\quad\quad U(\phi)=U_{0}\phi^{2n/(n-2)}\,,

(62)

which permit negative
\chi
values. The exponent
n=2
is discarded since it corresponds to a scalar field with an exponential potential whose scaling behavior has already been extensively studied in the literature (e.g. in Ref.
15
).

In addition to the three-form component, we recall that the spatially flat FLRW background also contains a cosmological fluid characterized by an adiabatic index
\gamma
, and the Friedmann equation is given by Eq. (
27
). Dividing it by
H^{2}
naturally motivates the introduction of the dimensionless variables

x^{2}\equiv\frac{V}{3H^{2}}\,,\hskip 14.22636pty^{2}\equiv\frac{U}{3H^{2}}\,,\hskip 14.22636ptz^{2}\equiv\frac{\rho_{\gamma}}{3H^{2}}\,,

(63)

so that the Friedman constraint becomes

1=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}\,,

(64)

which allows us to eliminate the variable
z
. In view of Eq. (
62
), the power-law indices can be expressed as

n=\frac{\chi V_{,\chi}}{V}\,,\quad\quad\quad k\equiv\frac{2n}{n-2}=\frac{\phi U_{,\phi}}{U}\,.

(65)

Furthermore, we introduce the auxiliary dimensionless variable
s
as

s\equiv\frac{\chi\phi}{H}\,,

(66)

which allows us to write the system in an autonomous and tractable form. The corresponding first-order equations governing the cosmological dynamics are then given by

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}n\left(1+k\frac{y^{2}}{s}\right)x-\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}x\,,

(67a)

\displaystyle y^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}n\,k\frac{x^{2}}{s}y-\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}y\,,

(67b)

\displaystyle s^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-3\left(s+k\,y^{2}-n\,x^{2}\right)-\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}s\,,

(67c)

where

\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}=-\frac{3}{2}\left[n\,x^{2}+\gamma\left(1-x^{2}-y^{2}\right)\right]\,.

(68)

The fractional energy density and equation-of-state parameter of the three-form are given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{A}

\displaystyle=x^{2}+y^{2}\,,

(69)

\displaystyle w_{A}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{n\,x^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}}\,.

(70)

The effective equation of state,
w_{\mathrm{eff}}
, describing the combined cosmological fluid (the three-form plus the background component) reads

w_{\mathrm{eff}}=-1+\gamma\left(1-x^{2}-y^{2}\right)+n\,x^{2}\,.

(71)

Consequently, the Raychaudhuri equation is compactly written as

\frac{H^{\prime}}{H}=-\frac{3}{2}\left(1+w_{\mathrm{eff}}\right)\,.

(72)

The dimensionality of the dynamical system can be reduced by noting that the auxiliary variable in Eq. (
66
),
{s=\chi\phi/H}
, reads

s=\sqrt{3}Mx^{2/n}y^{2/k}\,,

(73)

It is worth noting that when the dynamical system lies in the scaling regime, its stability prevents any transition toward three-form domination. V
Scaling exit toward cosmic acceleration

V.1
Cosmological constant solution

The simplest realization of a dark energy component is to add a cosmological constant to the hybrid Lagrangian (
26
).
However, this approach does not exploit the intrinsic dynamics of the three-form field to generate dark energy following the scaling behavior.
A similar limitation arises if we include an exponential potential term, function of
\phi
, to induce a late-time transition from the scaling solution toward cosmic acceleration. The effect of this extra term remains essentially equivalent to that of a cosmological constant.

Another possibility is to consider a Lagrangian density of the form

\mathcal{L}\propto-\left(\phi^{k}+\phi^{-k}\right)+\chi^{n}\,,

(90)

where
k=2n/(n-2)
. This model triggers the exit from the scaling regime at late times because it is a saddle point in the associated dynamical system, which can be straightforwardly adapted from Sec.
IV
. The trajectories are eventually attracted toward a stable three-form–dominated node. Nevertheless, we observe that this exit solution still remains close to a cosmological-constant–like behavior, as the field
\phi
asymptotically vanishes while undergoing damped oscillations.

V.2
Double potential structure

Alternatively, we want to introduce a scenario that enables a transition out of the scaling regime toward the domination of dark energy, which could be distinguished from a cosmological constant. We propose to include additional terms to the scaling three-form Lagrangian while preserving its original structure, thereby avoiding unnecessary parameter proliferation and degeneracies. Specifically, we extend both potentials as

\displaystyle V(\chi)

\displaystyle=V_{0}\chi^{n}+V_{1}\chi^{m}\,,

(91)

\displaystyle U(\phi)

\displaystyle=U_{0}\phi^{2n/(n-2)}+U_{1}\phi^{2m/(m-2)}\,,

(92)

introducing the additional parameter
m
and mass scales
V_{1}
and
U_{1}
. When we build the corresponding working model in Section
V.3
, these additional mass scales help us reproduce today’s cosmology with matter abundance
\Omega_{m}^{0}\simeq 0.3
.

The idea is to construct two dynamical systems of the same form as that analyzed in Sec.
IV
, exploiting the scaling point of the first and the three-form-dominated attractor of the second. This is inspired by quintessence models with exponential potentials, in which a double-exponential structure enables both scaling behavior and cosmic acceleration. Indeed, by combining potentials of different slopes,
\alpha
and
\beta
,

V(Q)\propto e^{\alpha Q}+e^{\beta Q}\,,

(93)

for a scalar field
Q
, it is possible to take advantage of both attractors within a unified dynamical framework
4
. Appropriate values of the first slope realize a period of scaling, while the second evolves the solution towards scalar field domination. Remarkably, the one-parameter scalar-field model first proposed in Ref.
35

V(Q)\propto e^{-\frac{3}{\lambda}Q}+e^{-\lambda Q}\,,

(94)

is fully characterized by a single parameter,
\lambda
, and admits an analytic solution for the dark-energy density (in Eq. (
99
) in the next section) which makes the model particularly convenient for cosmological studies
18
;
21
;
6
;
19
;
23
. It accounts for both early-time scaling and late-time acceleration, yielding a form of dark energy component that is dynamically distinct from a cosmological constant.

In the following section, we construct a viable family of scaling-exit solutions within our three-form framework, which is analog to this one-parameter model. Although the scalar double-exponential picture motivates our approach, the formalism is different and rely on the double-potential structure introduced in Eqs. (
91
) and (
92
). The leading terms
\chi^{n}
and
\phi^{2n/(n-2)}
reproduce the scaling behavior with matter, corresponding to the critical Point
(a)
of the associated dynamical system. At late times, the initially subdominant contributions
\chi^{m}
and
\phi^{2m/(m-2)}
become dominant, driving the system toward the three-form–dominated configuration at the critical Point
(b)
. In this framework, the transition toward dark-energy domination emerges dynamically, without introducing arbitrary potential structures.

VI
Conclusion

Starting from a first-order action for a massive three-form field, we have constructed an original
hybrid
representation in which its dynamics can be recast in terms of two dual scalar degrees of freedom. This formulation provides a tractable framework to identify scaling solutions in three-form dark energy, and has allowed us to derive general conditions that the underlying Lagrangian must satisfy in order for the three-form energy density to scale with the dominant background fluid. Our dynamical-system analysis further shows that these scaling regimes are attractors, leading to robust cosmological solutions that are weakly sensitive to initial conditions. The analysis also shows the existence of three-form-dominated universe configurations that are stable.

In the minimal setup, however, the scaling solution remains stable, preventing the system from naturally evolving toward a late-time three-form–dominated accelerated phase. Achieving a transition from early-time scaling to late-time acceleration therefore requires extending the Lagrangian. Simple extensions that effectively mimic a cosmological constant are of limited interest, as they freeze the field dynamics and reduce the scenario to a nearly non-dynamical dark-energy component. Instead, we have identified a more compelling construction based on a double potential structure that preserves the original functional form of the hybrid Lagrangian.

Overall, under appropriate conditions and fine-tuning, this mechanism provides a viable realization of scaling dark energy in three-form cosmology. In particular, it gives rise to a late-time acceleration phase that remains distinct from a pure cosmological constant, while remaining compatible with standard dark energy phenomenology.

Appendix B
Comparison with scaling k-Lagrangians

B.1
Finding the equivalent k-essence model

Within the context of general k-essence models, the class of Lagrangians admitting scaling solutions is well established
38
;
46
. In this Appendix, we demonstrate the consistency between these solutions and the three-form Lagrangian (
58
) that exhibits scaling behavior, derived in Sec.
III.4
from a first order formulation. This correspondence arises because three-form theories can be dualized into an equivalent scalar-field framework, thereby establishing the connection between the two descriptions of scaling cosmologies.

Our starting point is the three-form hybrid action (
26
),

\mathcal{L}=-U(\phi)-V(\chi)+\chi V_{,\chi}\,,

(133)

which admits a trivial scaling solution for
V=V_{0}\chi^{2}
(
V_{0}=1/2
), corresponding to the canonical scalar field with an exponential potential, as derived in Sec.
III.3
.

We assume the general scaling potential
V(\chi)
in Sec.
III.4
,

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{0}\chi^{n}\,,

(134)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-U(\phi)+V_{0}(n-1)\chi^{n}\,,

(135)

for
n\neq 0
and
n\neq 2
. We aim to determine the corresponding and most general form of the function
U(\phi)
that yields scaling, by using the k-essence models, and to confirm that it coincides with the power law in Eq. (
57
) that we obtained within the three-form framework.

Following Eq. (
12
), let
X
denote the kinetic term,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\phi\right)^{2}\,,

(136)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}V_{0}^{2}}{2}\chi^{2(n-1)}\,,

(137)

which can be inverted (for
n\neq 1
) to yield

\chi=\left(\frac{2X}{n^{2}V_{0}^{2}}\right)^{1/2(n-1)}\,.

(138)

Substituting this relation into the Lagrangian (
135
), we obtain

\mathcal{L}=-U(\phi)+\frac{n-1}{V_{0}^{1/(n-1)}}\left(\frac{2X}{n^{2}}\right)^{n/2(n-1)}\,.

(139)

We now map our three-form theory onto a non-canonical scalar field
\varphi
defined as

\varphi=-\ln\phi\,,

(140)

whose kinetic term,
{\tilde{X}=-(1/2)g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi}
, reads

\tilde{X}=Xe^{2\varphi}\,.

(141)

Defining

\tilde{Y}=\tilde{X}e^{k\varphi}\,,

(142)

for an arbitrary parameter
k
, we can re-write Eq. (
139
) in the form of a k-essence Lagrangian,

\displaystyle P(\varphi,\tilde{X})

\displaystyle=\tilde{X}\bigg[-\frac{U}{\tilde{Y}}e^{k\varphi}+c\,\tilde{Y}^{(2-n)/2(n-1)}

(143)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left({\frac{\varphi}{2}\frac{k(n-2)-2n}{n-1}}\right)\bigg]\,,

where
c
is a constant given by

c=\frac{n-1}{V_{0}^{1/(n-1)}}\left(\frac{2}{n^{2}}\right)^{n/2(n-1)}\,.

(144)

It is known that the general k-essence Lagrangian admits scaling solutions only if it can be written as

P(\varphi,\tilde{X})=\tilde{X}g(\tilde{Y})\,,

(145)

which is satisfied for

g(\tilde{Y})=-\frac{U_{0}}{\tilde{Y}}+c\,\tilde{Y}^{(2-n)/2(n-1)}\,,

(146)

provided the following two conditions hold:

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n-2}\,,

(147)

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{0}e^{-k\varphi}\,.

(148)

Using Eq. (
140
), the potential corresponds to

U(\phi)=U_{0}\phi^{2n/(n-2)}\,,

(149)

which is exactly the power law (
57
) obtained in the hybrid three form formulation. Substituting it in Eq. (
139
) leads to the same Lagrangian (
61
), hence demonstrating the consistency between the three-form and k-essence frameworks in describing scaling cosmologies.

B.2
Scaling density parameter
\Omega_{\varphi}

To further the comparison between the two frameworks, we now derive, as an example, the density parameter
\Omega_{\varphi}
of the k-essence scalar field and verify that it coincides with
\Omega_{A}
, the one obtained in the three-form dynamical system analysis.

Along a scaling solution of the k-essence Lagrangian
P(\varphi,\tilde{X}
) in Eq. (
145
), the density parameter
\Omega_{\varphi}
is given by
47

\Omega_{\varphi}=3\gamma\frac{g(\tilde{Y})+Yg^{\prime}(\tilde{Y})}{k^{2}}\,,

(150)

where
g^{\prime}=dg/d\tilde{Y}

146
), we find

\Omega_{\varphi}=\frac{3}{8n}\frac{(n-2)^{2}}{V_{0}^{1/(n-1)}}\left(\frac{2}{n^{2}}\right)^{n/2(n-1)}\tilde{Y}^{(2-n)/2(n-1)}\,,

(151)

where
\tilde{Y}
remains constant during the scaling regime.

The density parameter can be re-expressed back in terms of the hybrid potentials,
U(\chi)
and
V(\phi)

135
), (
140
), (
141
), (
142

\tilde{Y}=\frac{n^{2}}{2}V_{0}^{2/n}U_{0}^{2(n-1)/n}\left(\frac{U}{V}\right)^{2(1-n)/n}\,.

Substituting this expression in Eq. (

The ratio

(2-\gamma)g(\tilde{Y})-2(\gamma-1)\tilde{Y}g^{\prime}(\tilde{Y})=0\,,

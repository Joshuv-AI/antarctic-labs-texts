Approximation Schemes for k-Subset Sum Ratio and k-way Number Partitioning Ratio
Sotiris Kanellopoulos, Giorgos Mitropoulos, Antonis Antonopoulos, Nikos Leonardos, Aris Pagourtzis, Christos Pergaminelis, Stavros Petsalakis, Kanellos Tsitouras

Abstract
The Subset Sum Ratio problem (SSR) asks, given a multiset A A of n n positive integers, to find two disjoint subsets of A A such that the largest-to-smallest ratio of their sums is minimized. In this paper we study the k k -version of SSR, namely k k - Subset Sum Ratio ( k k -SSR), which asks to minimize the largest-to-smallest ratio of sums of k k disjoint subsets of A A . We develop approximation schemes for k k -SSR running in O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ k / ε k − 1 ) O({n^{2k}}/{\varepsilon^{k-1}}) and O ~ ​ ( n / ε 3 ​ k − 1 ) \widetilde{O}(n/{\varepsilon^{3k-1}}) time, by expanding known techniques for SSR and combining them with novel ideas to address the multi-subset setting. Our second FPTAS employs carefully designed calls to the first one, being much faster than the first one when n ≫ 1 / ε n\gg 1/\varepsilon . To the best of our knowledge, these are the first FPTASs for k k -SSR for fixed k > 2 k>2 . We also study the variant of Multiway Number Partitioning in which the objective is to minimize the largest-to-smallest sum ratio of k k subsets. We extend our first FPTAS for k k -SSR to this problem through additional arguments to comply with the partitioning constraint, also achieving O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ k / ε k − 1 ) O({n^{2k}}/{\varepsilon^{k-1}}) time complexity. This represents an improvement over the best known bound of O ⁡ ( n 4 ​ k 2 + 1 / ε 2 ​ k 2 ) O(n^{4k^{2}+1}/\varepsilon^{2k^{2}}) for this problem, which stems from Nguyen and Rothe’s FPTAS Nguyen and Rothe (2014) for Minimum Envy-Ratio , although that FPTAS concerns a more general problem.

We define an alternative restricted version of

\mathcal{R}\left(S^{j},A_{j}^{\prime}\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT_{L}}\left(A_{j}^{\prime}\right)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\min_{k\leq j\leq n}\mathcal{R}\left(S^{j},A_{j}^{\prime}\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\min_{k\leq j\leq n}\mathrm{OPT_{L}}\left(A_{j}^{\prime}\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\mathrm{OPT}(A).

[by Lemma
30
]

For all
j\in\{k,\ldots,n\}
, the set
A_{j}^{\prime}
contains at most
C
elements, with

\displaystyle C=(k-1)\left(2+\left\lceil\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\ln\frac{2(k-1)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right\rceil\right)<3k+k\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\ln\frac{2k}{\varepsilon^{2}}<\frac{9k}{\varepsilon}\ln\frac{k}{\varepsilon},

assuming
k\geq 2
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. As such, we have to run the given algorithm on
O(n)

k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR_{L}}
instances of size bounded by
(9k/\varepsilon)\ln(k/\varepsilon)
in order to obtain a
(1+\varepsilon)^{2}
-approximation for
\mathrm{OPT}(A)
. For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
it holds that
(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\leq 1+3\varepsilon
. Therefore, by setting the error parameter to
\varepsilon/3
instead of
\varepsilon
in the approximation algorithm for
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR_{L}}
, we obtain a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation for
\mathrm{OPT}(A)
running in time

O\left(nT_{L}\left(\frac{9k}{\varepsilon}\ln\frac{k}{\varepsilon},\ \frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)\right).

∎

We now present the main theorem of this section, which follows by using the FPTAS of Theorem
15
as the
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm for
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR_{L}}
in Lemma
31
.

Theorem 32
.

There is an FPTAS for
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
that runs in
\widetilde{O}(n/{\varepsilon^{3k-1}})
time, where
\widetilde{O}
hides
\mathrm{polylog}(1/\varepsilon)
factors.

Proof.

Let
A
be a
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
instance. Suppose we use our
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
FPTAS running in time
T_{L}(n,\varepsilon)=O({n^{2k}}/{\varepsilon^{k-1}})
(see Theorem
15
) as a subroutine to solve the
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR_{L}}
instances
A_{j}^{\prime}=A\bigl[j-C+1,j\bigr]
,
k\leq j\leq n
. This subroutine serves as a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation for
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR_{L}}
, since for the solution
S^{j}=(S^{j}_{1},\dots,S^{j}_{k})
returned it holds that

\mathcal{R}\left(S^{j},A_{j}^{\prime}\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT}(A_{j}^{\prime})\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{OPT_{L}}(A_{j}^{\prime}).

Note that this subroutine does not take into account the
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR_{L}}
restriction
j\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}S_{i}^{j}
, therefore it might return an invalid
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR_{L}}
solution for
A_{j}^{\prime}
. However, all solutions returned are feasible for the (unrestricted)
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
instance
A
, therefore the solution which yields
\min_{k\leq j\leq n}\mathcal{R}\left(S^{j},A_{j}^{\prime}\right)
is a feasible one. As such, by Lemma
31
we obtain another FPTAS for
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
running in time

O\left(nT_{L}\left(\frac{9k}{\varepsilon}\ln\frac{k}{\varepsilon},\ \frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)\right)=\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{n}{\varepsilon^{3k-1}}\right).

∎

6
Conclusion

In this work we presented the first FPTASs for
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
with constant
k>2
. A natural open question that arises regards the existence of a PTAS for
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
when
k
is a variable; this would require different techniques, since both of our
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
FPTASs contain factors exponential in
k
in their time complexity. Note that such a PTAS already exists for
k\text{-}\mathrm{PART}
by Lipton et al.
Lipton et al. (2004)
.

We remark that our FPTAS in Section
5
is only faster than the one in Section
3
when
n\gg 1/\varepsilon
. This contrasts the current status of the FPTASs for
k=2
, where the state-of-the-art FPTAS by Bringmann
Bringmann (2024)
is the fastest without any tradeoff. This naturally leads to an open question regarding the existence of a stronger density argument that works for
k>2
and is always faster than the DP-based approach.

Another interesting direction for future work would be to extend the methods of Alonistiotis et al.
Alonistiotis et al. (2024)
for
k>2
, possibly leading to an improvement over the approximation schemes we presented in this work.
Even though the FPTASs presented by Alonistiotis et al.
Alonistiotis et al. (2024)
are slower than Bringmann’s FPTASs
Bringmann (2024)
, their techniques might prove useful if generalized to
k>2
, since Bringmann’s density arguments do not seem to generalize well for
k>2
(cf. Subsection
2.1
).

Regarding
k\text{-}\mathrm{PART}
, a meaningful direction involves extending our techniques to the more general
Minimum Envy-Ratio
problem, involving general additive valuations or at least some sub-class such as
ordered
additive valuations. This could potentially achieve a better time complexity than that of Nguyen and Rothe’s FPTAS
Nguyen and Rothe (2014)
for
Minimum Envy-Ratio
, which works for general additive valuations.

Lastly, as stated in
Bringmann (2017)
, it is unlikely for the current state-of-the-art FPTAS for SSR to be optimal. We restate the open problem of determining the optimal constant
c\geq 0
such that SSR can be solved in time
O(n/\varepsilon^{c})
and extend this challenge to
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
. More generally, the development of lower bounds for
k\text{-}\mathrm{SSR}
and
k\text{-}\mathrm{PART}
would be particularly beneficial.

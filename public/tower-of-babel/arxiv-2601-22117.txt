Monochromatic cycle partitions of r r -edge-coloured graphs with high minimum degree
Francesco Di Braccio, Viresh Patel

Abstract
A question posed independently by Letzter and Pokrovskiy asks: how many vertex-disjoint monochromatic cycles are needed to cover the vertex set of an r r -edge-coloured graph, as a function of its minimum (uncoloured) degree? We resolve this problem up to a ( log ⁡ r ) (\log r) -factor. Specifically, we prove that, for any r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 and δ ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ) \delta\in(0,1/2) , any n n -vertex r r -edge-coloured graph G G with δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ( 1 − δ ) ​ n \delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)n can be covered with 𝒪 ⁡ ( r ​ log ⁡ r ⋅ ⌈ r log ⁡ ( 1 / δ ) ⌉ ) \mathcal{O}\left(r\log r\cdot\left\lceil\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\rceil\right) vertex-disjoint monochromatic cycles. We construct graphs that show this is tight up to the ( log ⁡ r ) (\log r) -factor for all values of r r and δ \delta , and along the way disprove a conjecture of Bal and DeBiasio about monochromatic tree covering.

Introduction

Monochromatic partitioning is a classical research area at the intersection of Ramsey theory and the study of spanning structures in graphs. In line with a common trend in Ramsey theory, it concerns
r
-edge-coloured graphs
—that is, graphs
G
equipped with an edge-colouring
\chi:E(G)\to[r]
—and their
monochromatic
subgraphs, whose edges all receive the same colour. Since generic
r
-edge-coloured graphs may not contain a monochromatic subgraph covering all vertices, one instead seeks a cover by a small collection of such subgraphs. Many problems additionally require these subgraphs to be vertex-disjoint, in which case we say they
partition
the graph.

Cycles are one of the most natural and intensively studied subgraph classes in this setting.
2
2

2

Throughout the paper, we consider an empty graph, a single vertex, and a single edge to be (degenerate) cycles.
This line of inquiry has produced several compelling and influential questions. Lehel conjectured that every
2
-edge-colouring of
K_{n}
admits a partition into two monochromatic cycles of different colours. This was first proven asymptotically by Łuczak, Rödl, and Szemerédi
[
ŁRS98
]
and then exactly by Bessy and Thomassé
[
BT10
]
. For general
r\geq 2
, Erdős, Gyárfás, and Pyber
[
EoGP91
]
conjectured that every
r
-edge-coloured
K_{n}
can be partitioned into
r
monochromatic cycles, and proved an upper bound of
\mathcal{O}(r^{2}\log r)
on the number of cycles required. Pokrovskiy
[
Pok14
]
later constructed an example showing that at least
r+1
cycles are sometimes necessary, though the conjecture may still hold with
\mathcal{O}(r)
cycles. Despite substantial work, the best current upper bound is
\mathcal{O}(r\log r)
due to Gyárfás, Sárközy, Ruzsinkó, and Szemerédi
[
GRSS06
]
.

In this paper, we consider analogous problems where the host graph is not assumed to be complete. Specifically, we address the following question: given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, how many monochromatic cycles are needed to partition an
n
-vertex
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
whose minimum (uncoloured) degree is at least
\alpha n
? Interest in this problem started with a conjecture of Schelp
[
Sch12
]
stating that every
2
-edge-coloured graph
G
with minimum degree at least
\frac{3}{4}|V(G)|
admits a partition into two cycles, which was later solved over a series of papers
[
BBG+14
,
DN17
,
Let19
]
. The general version of the problem was posed independently by Letzter
[
Let19
]
, Pokrovskiy
[
Pok23
]
, and later reiterated by Allen, Böttcher, Lang, Skokan, and Stein
[
ABL+24
]
.

To state the problem precisely, let
cp(G)
denote the size of the smallest partition into monochromatic cycles of an
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
, and let
\delta(G)
be its minimum degree (viewed as an uncoloured graph). For
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, define

cp_{r}(\delta):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{G\in\mathcal{G}(n,r,\delta)}cp(G),

where
\mathcal{G}(n,r,\delta)
is the family of
n
-vertex
r
-edge-coloured graphs with
\delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)n
. Thus
cp_{r}(\delta)
measures the number of monochromatic cycles needed to partition any sufficiently large graph of minimum degree at least
(1-\delta)n
, provided this quantity is bounded in terms of
r
and
\delta
alone. For example,
cp_{r}(\delta)
is unbounded for
\delta>1/2
, since any colouring of
K_{\delta n,(1-\delta)n}
requires at least
(2\delta-1)n
monochromatic cycles. On the other hand, a result of Korándi, Lang, Letzter, and Pokrovskiy
[
KLLP21
]
shows that
cp_{r}(\delta)=\mathcal{O}(r^{2})
whenever
\delta<1/2
(and that it is also unbounded for
\delta=1/2
). This leads to the following formulation of the central question.

Problem 1.1
.

Determine
cp_{r}(\delta)
for each
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1/2)
.

Here, we typically allow
\delta
to depend on
r
. This problem is by now relatively well understood for
r=2
. Note that
cp_{r}(\delta)
is increasing in
\delta
since
\delta^{\prime}>\delta
implies
\mathcal{G}(n,r,\delta)\subseteq\mathcal{G}(n,r,\delta^{\prime})
. Resolving Schelp’s conjecture, Letzter
[
Let19
]
proved that
cp_{2}(1/4)=2
, which is optimal as
cp_{2}(\delta)>2
for
\delta>1/4
. Allen, Böttcher, Lang, Skokan, and Stein
[
ABL+24
]
showed that
cp_{2}(\delta)=3
for
\delta<1/3
. Pokrovskiy
[
Pok23
]
conjectured that
cp_{2}(1/3)=3
and that
cp_{2}(\delta)=4
for all
\delta<1/2
, which would settle the problem completely.

For general
r\geq 3
, the picture is much less complete. The abovementioned result of Korándi, Lang, Letzter, and Pokrovskiy shows that
cp_{r}(\delta)=\mathcal{O}(r^{2})
for
\delta

1/2
, and this is tight up to a constant factor. At the opposite end, a straightforward adaptation of the methods yielding the

[

\delta=r^{-\Omega(r)}
. The main gap lies in the intermediate region: how does

\Theta(r^{2})

\mathcal{O}(r\log r)
(or possibly

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist constants

such that for all

kr\left\lceil\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\rceil\;\leq\;cp_{r}(\delta)\;\leq\;Kr\log r\left\lceil\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\rceil.

The theorem resolves

\log

decreases from
\Theta(r)
, when
\delta

1

\delta\leq e^{-r}

is between
\Omega(r^{2})
and
\mathcal{O}(r^{2}\log r)
for constant
\delta

[

performs better) and between
\Omega(r)
and
\mathcal{O}(r\log r)
when
\delta
is close to

.

As a crucial ingredient in the proof of
1.2
, we will also obtain results about the analogue of

The problem of determining

represents a weakening of

[

]
, Bucić, Korandi, and Sudakov
[

proved that every

-edge-coloured graph

\delta(G)\geq(1-2^{-r})n

tc_{r}(2^{-r})=r
, which highlights the jump in difficulty from tree covering to cycle partitioning.

For instance, covering

-edge-coloured

At the opposite extreme, Korándi, Lang, Letzter, and Pokrovskiy

is a constant independent of

. Regarding the intermediate range, Bal and DeBiasio suggested the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.3

(

]
)
.

For all

, if

There exist constants

1.1
Proof overview and organization

We now briefly outline the proofs of our main theorems. We begin by establishing the upper and lower bounds for the monochromatic tree covering problem,
1.4
, which forms the backbone of several subsequent arguments. We show that determining
tc_{r}(\delta)
is equivalent to determining the transversal number of a certain class of
r
-partite multi-
r
-graphs, which we call
\delta
-intersecting
(see
3.3
). This equivalence allows us to derive the lower bound on
tc_{r}(\delta)
from constructions of
\delta
-intersecting hypergraphs with large transversal number, while the upper bounds follow from bounds on how large this transversal number can be. Several of these arguments rely on results of Bucić, Korándi, and Sudakov
[
BKS21
]
, who studied a closely related problem.

Our lower bound on
tc_{r}(\delta)
immediately implies the same lower bound for
cp_{r}(\delta)
. The remainder (and indeed the majority) of the paper is therefore devoted to proving the upper bound on
cp_{r}(\delta)
stated in
1.2
. Our approach combines the connected matching method, a now standard technique for constructing long cycles in dense graphs via the Szemerédi regularity lemma that goes back to Łuczak
[
Łuc99
]
, with the absorption method.

The high-level strategy, similar to that used in
[
KLLP21
,
GRSS06
]
, proceeds as follows. We start by applying the multi-colour version of the Szemerédi regularity lemma to
G
, obtaining a regular partition
\{V_{0},V_{1},\dots,V_{t}\}
of
V(G)
and the associated reduced graph
R
, which is itself
r
-edge-coloured. We then carefully select a small collection of monochromatic components of
R
and show that their union, viewed as an uncoloured graph, contains a spanning subgraph
H
of
V(R)
with special properties. In particular,
H
is the vertex-disjoint union of graphs
F
and
M
where

•

F
covers a significant proportion of
V(R)
, has bounded maximum degree, and satisfies a ‘robust matchability’ property (as defined in
[
KLLP21
]
): after blowing up its vertices to large sets, any of its induced subgraphs obtained by deleting a small number of vertices contains a perfect matching; and

•

M
is a perfect matching of
G-V(F)
.

Using the regularity of the partition, together with the bounded maximum degree of
H
, we can remove a small number of vertices from each cluster of
H=F\cup M
to guarantee that any of its edges (of colour
c
, say) corresponds to a regular pair of clusters in
G
with positive minimum degree in colour
c
.

We combine the discarded vertices from each cluster into a (small) leftover set
L
. We further remove, and add to
L
, a few vertices from each cluster in
M
so that these clusters all have equal size. Let
W
denote the union of all clusters covered by
F
. We then show (in the
absorption step
) that all vertices in
L
can be incorporated into a small number of monochromatic cycles contained in the bipartite edge-coloured graph
G[L,W]
. This is possible because
F
covers a large portion of
V(R)
, ensuring that
W
is large and that every vertex of
L
retains most of its degree in
G[L,W]
.

After handling the vertices in
L
, the remainder of the graph exhibits strong regularity properties, enabling us to cover it using blow-up lemma-style arguments. Since all previously constructed cycles intersect each cluster of
F
in a small number of vertices, the robust matchability of
F
allows us to construct a small family of cycles covering its remaining vertices. The clusters in
M
can be covered with a similar argument, this time exploiting the fact that they contain equal numbers of uncovered vertices. The number of cycles required to complete this step roughly matches the number of monochromatic components used to build
F
and
M
, which is in turn closely tied to
tc_{r}(\delta)
and depends crucially on our bounds from
1.4
.

Although this overall strategy is fairly standard, implementing each step presents substantial difficulties and requires several new ideas. The most demanding part is the absorption step, which is handled via a separate lemma asserting that any edge-coloured bipartite graph
G[A,B]
, where
A
is small relative to
B
and every
v\in A
satisfies
\deg(v,B)\geq(1-\delta)|B|
, contains a small number of vertex-disjoint monochromatic cycles covering
A
. The proof relies on constructing large families of structures we call
connecting hubs
(see
4.4
) and applying our tree covering results (i.e.
1.4
) to suitable auxiliary graphs based on these. We defer a detailed discussion to
Section

4
—see the beginning of that section for a thorough proof sketch.

The other main challenge is the construction of
H=F\cup M
. For
F
, we take a vertex-disjoint union of triangles and graphs we call
barbells
(that is,
7
-vertex graphs formed from two triangles by adding a new vertex adjacent to one vertex in each triangle). Both triangles and barbells readily satisfy the robust matchability property required of
F
. To ensure that
F
and
M
are vertex-disjoint and together span
V(R)
, we proceed as follows:

•

find a large collection of vertex-disjoint triangles in
R
;

•

cover most of the remaining vertices with a matching
M
; and

•

use a small number of triangles from the first step to construct barbells covering all remaining vertices of
R
(finally,
F
is formed from the union of all triangles and barbells thus found).

Throughout, we ensure that the edges added to
F\cup M
lie in only a few monochromatic components. In particular, roughly
\mathcal{O}(tc_{r}(\delta))
components are required for both the first and third step, whereas the second step uses a simple greedy argument requiring at most
\mathcal{O}(r\log r)
components.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
we introduce notation and tools, including the regularity lemma and various results about
sublinear expanders
, which are used in the absorption step.
Section

3
contains the proof of
1.4
. The absorption lemma is proved in
Section

4
. In
Section

5
we establish several lemmas for constructing the graphs
F
and
M
and verifying their required properties. Finally,
Section

6
combines all previous ingredients to prove
1.2
, and
Section

7
discusses some open problems.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

An
r
-edge-coloured graph

G
is a triple
(V(G),E(G),\chi(G))
where
(V(G),E(G))
is a simple graph and
\chi(G):E(G)\to[r]
is a colouring of its edges by elements of
[r]
. Given an
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
and some colour
c\in[r]
, we write
G_{c}
to denote the graph obtained by considering only the edges of colour
c
(which is monochromatic and thus may be viewed as an uncoloured graph). Given
C\subseteq[r]
, we write
G_{C}
to denote the
r
-edge-coloured graph
\bigcup_{c\in C}G_{c}
.

Given a graph
G
, a vertex
v\in V(G)
and a set
S\subseteq V(G)
,
N_{G}(v,S)
is the set of neighbours of
v
in
S
; if
G
is an
r
-edge-coloured graph itself, this refers to the set of neighbours regardless of the colour of the edge connecting them to
G
.
So, for instance, if we wish to refer to the set of vertices in
S
connected to
v
by an edge of colour
c\in[r]
or some colour belonging to
C\subseteq[r]
, we can just write
N_{G_{c}}(v,S)
and
N_{G_{C}}(v,S)
(respectively). Degrees and codegrees are now defined in the obvious way:
\deg_{G}(v,S)
is
|N_{G}(v,S)|
and
\operatorname{codeg}_{G}(v,u,S)
is
|N_{G}(v,S)\cap N_{G}(u,S)|
. Whenever the host graph
G
is clear from context, we will suppress the use of the letter
G
and simply write, for instance,
\deg(v,S)
,
\deg_{c}(v,S)
,
\deg_{C}(v,S)
in place of
\deg_{G}(v,S),\deg_{G_{c}}(v,S),\deg_{G_{C}}(v,S)
. When
S=V(G)
, we simply write
\deg(v)
,
\deg_{c}(v)
,
\deg_{C}(v)
. Finally, the minimum degree
\delta(G)
of an
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
is just
\min_{v\in V(G)}\deg(v)
.

Given an
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
and some
i\in[r]
, the

i
-coloured components
of
G
are simply the components of
G_{i}
. Throughout the paper, we view the components themselves as subgraphs of
G
rather than subsets of
V(G)
. This allows us to talk about, say, certain edges being contained in the union of some components. A
monochromatic component
is any component of
G_{i}
for some
i\in[r]
. A
monochromatic connected matching
is a matching that is also a subgraph of a monochromatic component.

Given a graph
G
and disjoint subsets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, we write
G[A,B]
for the subgraph of
G
whose edges are in
A\times B
. We will also often use
G[A,B]
to directly refer to a bipartite (possibly edge-coloured) graph on vertex classes
A
and
B
. We write
G[A]
for the induced subgraph of
G
on
A
, and
G-A
for
G[V(G)\setminus A]
. Given two graphs
G
and
H
,
G\cap H
is the subgraph on edge set
E(G)\cap E(H)
and
G\setminus H
the one on edge set
E(G)\setminus E(H)
(this definition extends in the obvious way to the case when the graphs are edge-coloured and agree on the colouring of their shared edges). We write
e(G)
for the number of edges in
G
, and
d(G)
is its average degree
2e(G)/|V(G)|
.

We will often make assumptions of the form
a\ll b_{1},\dots,b_{k}
. This means that there exists a positive function
f
for which the relevant result holds provided that
a\leq f(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})
. Moreover, when writing this expression, we always assume that
a,b_{1},\dots,b_{k}
are positive. If we write
1/n
instead of
a
or
b_{i}
, it is implicitly assumed that
n
is an integer. We omit ceilings and floors when they are not crucial to the argument. All logarithms throughout the paper are base
e
.

X

and

(Chernoff’s inequality for the hypergeometric distribution)
.

Let

and

. Then, for any

Given a graph

We will simply say that

We will be using the following degree form of the multicolour regularity lemma. This follows from the multicolour regularity lemma (Theorem 1.18 in

]

R(G)
by retaining exactly one edge between each pair of adjacent vertices. Then,

\delta(R)\geq\left(\frac{\delta(G)}{n}-rd-\varepsilon\right)t.

Next we state some basic facts about regular pairs.

Fact 2.4

(Facts 1.3 and 1.5 in
[
KS96a
]
)
.

Let
d,\varepsilon>0
and
\varepsilon<1/4
. Let
(A,B)
be a
(d,\varepsilon)
-regular pair.

(1)

If
A^{\prime}\subseteq A,B^{\prime}\subseteq B
satisfy
|A^{\prime}|\geq\sqrt{\varepsilon}|A|
and
|B^{\prime}|\geq\sqrt{\varepsilon}|B|
, then
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
is a
(d,\sqrt{\varepsilon})
-regular pair.

(2)

All but at most
\varepsilon|A|
vertices
v\in A
satisfy
\deg(v,B)\geq(d-\varepsilon)|B|
.

The next lemma shows how to connect vertices in the host graph using the connectivity of the reduced graph. Its proof is the same as Lemma 3.4 in
[
KLLP21
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1/m\ll\varepsilon\ll d
and
t\geq 2
. Let
G
be a
t
-partite
n
-vertex graph on vertex classes
V_{1},\dots,V_{t}
with
|V_{i}|=m
for each
i\in[t]
. Suppose that each pair
(V_{i},V_{i+1})
is
\varepsilon
-regular with density at least
d
. Then, for any
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S\cap V_{i}|\leq dm/4
for each
i\in[t]
and
x,y\in V(G)
with
\deg(x,V_{1}),\deg(y,V_{t})\geq dm/2
, there is a path
xv_{1}\dots v_{t}y
in
G
such that
v_{i}\in V_{i}\setminus S
for each
i\in[t]
.

The next lemma is a corollary of the celebrated blow-up lemma
[
KSS97
]
; alternatively, see the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
KLLP21
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
1/m\ll\varepsilon\ll d
. Let
(A,B)
be a
(d,\varepsilon)
-regular pair in a graph
G
with
|A|=|B|=m
. Suppose that
\delta(G[A,B])\geq 5\varepsilon m
. Then, for any
x\in A
and
y\in B
, the graph
G[A,B]
contains a
x
–

path spanning
A\cup B
.

2.4
Sublinear expanders

The proof of our absorption lemma makes use of certain sparse expanding graphs known as
sublinear expanders
. These graphs were introduced by Komlós and Szemerédi
[
KS97
,
KS96b
]
and have found widespread use in recent years—see
[
Let24
]
for a survey. The specific variant and related results that we will be using are also due to
[
KS97
,
KS96b
]
but specifically stated in the form below in
[
Let24
]
.

Definition 2.7

(sublinear expander)
.

For
\varepsilon,t>0
, let
\rho(x)=\rho(x,\varepsilon,t)
be the function defined by, for
x\geq t/2
,

\rho(x):=\frac{\varepsilon}{\log^{2}(15x/t)}.

An
(\varepsilon,t)
-expander
is a graph
G
in which every set of vertices
U
with
t/2\leq|U|\leq|G|/2
satisfies

|N_{G}(U)\setminus U|\geq\rho(|U|)\cdot|U|.

The next theorem lets us find a sublinear expander in an arbitrary graph at the cost of a small loss in its average degree, whereas the one after says that such expanders have good connectivity properties.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
KS97
,
KS96b
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be sufficiently small and let
t>0
. Then every graph
G
has a subgraph
H
which is an
(\varepsilon,t)
-expander, and satisfies
d(H)\geq d(G)/2
and
\delta(H)\geq d(H)/2
.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
KS97
,
KS96b
]
)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
(\varepsilon,t)
-expander. Then, for every
x\geq t/2
and every three sets of vertices
U_{1},U_{2}
and
W
, where
|U_{1}|,|U_{2}|\geq x
and
|W|\leq\rho(x)x/4
, there is a path from
U_{1}
to
U_{2}
in
G-W
of length at most
\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\log^{3}(15n/t)
.

3
Tree covering

In this section, we prove
1.4
, which provides close-to-matching upper and lower bounds on
tc_{r}(\delta)
for each choice of
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. These bounds will serve as key tools in our cycle partitioning results later on.

For the proof of
1.4
, in
Section

3.1
we will show that determining
tc_{r}(\delta)
is essentially equivalent to determining the transversal number of certain multi-hypergraphs we call
\delta
-intersecting
(see
3.3
below). With this equivalence in hand, we will be able to derive our lower bound (in
Section

3.2
) and general upper bound (in
Section

3.3
) mostly from results of
[
BKS21
]
(our upper bound for
\delta\geq 1/2
uses a different, though straightforward, argument).

3.1
Translating to a hypergraph transversal problem

Given a multi-hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, a
transversal
for
\mathcal{H}
is a set
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
intersecting every edge of
\mathcal{H}
. The
transversal number
of
\mathcal{H}
, denoted
\tau(\mathcal{H})
, is the size of the smallest transversal for
\mathcal{H}
. It is well-known that questions about monochromatic tree covering can often be reduced to hypergraph transversal problems (for instance, see
[
BKS21
,
EoGP91
,
GK17
,
Gyá11
]
). This is generally achieved by constructing a multi-hypergraph which encodes the monochromatic component structure of the graph, as in the following definition.

Definition 3.1

(connectivity hypergraph)
.

Let
G
be an
r
-edge-coloured graph and let
T_{1},\dots,T_{\ell}
be its monochromatic components. We define its
connectivity hypergraph

\mathcal{C}(G)
as the multi-
r
-graph on vertex set
\{T_{1},\dots,T_{\ell}\}
and having, for each vertex
v\in V(G)
, an edge
e_{v}=\{T_{i_{1}},\dots,T_{i_{r}}\}
where each
T_{i_{j}}
is the (unique)
j
-coloured component containing
v
.

Note that, under our notion of monochromatic component (see
Section

2.1
), each vertex of
G
is contained in some monochromatic component in each colour in
[r]
, and so indeed
\mathcal{C}(G)
is
r
-uniform. Further observe that, letting
\mathcal{T}_{i}\subseteq\{T_{1},\dots,T_{\ell}\}
be the set of
i
-coloured components for each
i\in[r]
, the hypergraph
\mathcal{C}(G)
is
r
-partite with partition
\{\mathcal{T}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{T}_{r}\}
. Also, we have the following fact.

Fact 3.2
.

Let
r\geq 2
. For each
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
, we have
tc(G)=\tau(\mathcal{C}(G))
.

We will be particularly focusing on hypergraphs which fall under the following definition.

Definition 3.3

(
\delta
-intersecting)
.

Let
\delta\in[0,1]
. A multi-hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
\delta
-intersecting
if every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
intersects at least
(1-\delta)\cdot e(\mathcal{H})
edges of
\mathcal{H}
.

Next, we relate this definition to edge-coloured graphs with high minimum degree.

Fact 3.4
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1]
. If
G
is an
r
-edge-coloured graph with
\delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)|V(G)|-1
, then
\mathcal{C}(G)
is a
\delta
-intersecting.

Proof.

Any given edge
e_{v}\in E(\mathcal{C}(G))
intersects
e_{v^{\prime}}
for each
v^{\prime}\in N_{G}(v)
since
v
and
v^{\prime}
necessarily belong to the same monochromatic component in the colour of
vv^{\prime}
. Also including itself,
e_{v}
intersects at least
(1-\delta)|V(G)|-1+1=(1-\delta)e(\mathcal{C}(G))
edges of
\mathcal{C}(G)
.
∎

The next proposition provides a partial converse to the previous observation.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1]
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
\delta
-intersecting
r
-partite multi-
r
-graph. Then there exists an
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)|V(G)|-1
and
tc(G)=\tau(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

We begin by defining
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
as the
r
-partite multi-
r
-graph obtained from
\mathcal{H}
by substituting each
e_{v}\in E(\mathcal{H})
with
4r
identical copies
e_{v}^{1},\dots,e^{4r}_{v}
copies of
e_{v}
.
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
have the same underlying
r
-graph and so
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})=\tau(\mathcal{H})
. It is also easy to see that each
e_{v}^{i}\in E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
intersects at least

4r\cdot(1-\delta)e(\mathcal{H})=(1-\delta)e(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})

edges of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
, thus implying that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is also
\delta
-intersecting. We will further assume that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
contains no isolated vertices, as otherwise removing them does not affect any of these properties. Crucially, this guarantees that each vertex in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is contained in at least
4r
edges.

To prove the proposition, it will be sufficient by
3.2
to construct an
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
with minimum degree at least
(1-\delta)|V(G)|
satisfying
\mathcal{C}(G)=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. To accomplish this, we begin by constructing a (possibly) sparse
r
-edge-coloured graph
G^{\prime}
also satisfying
\mathcal{C}(G^{\prime})=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and then boost its minimum degree by filling in many of its missing edges.

Recall that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is
r
-partite and let
V_{1}\cup\dots\cup V_{r}
be a partition for it. Let
V_{i}=\{T^{i}_{1},\dots,T^{i}_{\ell_{i}}\}
for each
i\in[r]
. Now we construct
G^{\prime}
by starting with an empty graph
G^{0}
on vertex set
W:=E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
and progressively adding spanning monochromatic linear forests in each colour in
[r]
.

We now describe an iteration of this procedure. Let us suppose that for some
i\in[r]
we have previously obtained an
(i-1)
-edge-coloured graph
G^{i-1}
on
W
with
\Delta(G^{i-1})\leq 2(i-1)
; we will argue that this property is maintained at the end of the next iteration replacing
i-1
with
i
. For each
j\in[\ell_{i}]
, let
E^{i}_{j}\subseteq W
be the set of edges of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
containing
T_{j}^{i}
, and observe that
\{E_{j}^{i}:j\in[\ell_{i}]\}
forms a partition of
W
, as
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is
r
-partite
r
-uniform with
V_{i}
being one of its partition classes. Also, each
T_{j}^{i}
is contained in at least
4r
edges in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and so
|E_{j}^{i}|\geq 4r
. Then, for each
j\in[\ell_{i}]
, we have that the complement of
G^{i-1}[E_{j}^{i}]
has minimum degree at least

|E_{j}^{i}|-\Delta(G^{i-1})\geq|E_{j}^{i}|-2(i-1)\geq|E_{j}^{i}|-2r\geq\frac{|E_{j}^{i}|}{2},

and thus by Dirac’s theorem the complement of
G^{i-1}
contains a path
P^{i}_{j}
whose vertex set is precisely
E_{j}^{i}
. The union
F_{i}:=\bigcup_{j\in[\ell_{i}]}P^{i}_{j}
is a linear forest spanning
W
. Now we let
G^{i}
be obtained from
G^{i-1}
by adding the edges of
F_{i}
and colouring them in colour
i
. We have
\Delta(G^{i})\leq 2i
, as needed for the procedure to continue.

When the procedure terminates, we obtain a graph
G^{\prime}:=G^{r}
in which any two vertices belong to the same
i
-coloured component for any given
i\in[r]
if and only if the corresponding edges in
E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
intersect in
V_{i}
. It is easy to see that this implies
\mathcal{C}(G^{\prime})=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. To finish the construction of
G
, we take every intersecting pair of distinct edges
x,y\in E(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
which are not adjacent as vertices of
G^{\prime}
, and we simply connect them by an edge in a colour
i\in[r]
such that
V_{i}\cap x\cap y\neq\varnothing
. Note that this implies that
T_{j}^{i}\in x\cap y
for some
j\in[\ell_{i}]
and thus
x,y\in E_{j}^{i}
. Thus,
x
and
y
are already connected in
G^{\prime}
by the
i
-coloured path spanning
E_{j}^{i}
and so adding the
i
-coloured edge
xy
does not affect the monochromatic component structure. Once this has been done for each choice of
x
and
y
, we obtain a graph
G
with
\mathcal{C}(G)=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
and

\delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)e(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})-1\geq(1-\delta)|V(G)|-1,

since each
x
intersects itself in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
but cannot be adjacent to itself in
G
.
∎

3.2
Lower bound

Our lower bound construction for
1.4
is obtained by constructing a
\delta
-intersecting hypergraph with high transversal number and applying
Proposition

3.5
to it. We now describe the hypergraph construction we will be using, which was introduced in
[
BKS21
]
for a different, though related, problem.

Given
m\geq 1
and
t\geq r\geq 2
, we define
H_{r,t,m}
to be the following
r
-partite
r
-graph. In each of the
r
parts of its partition, there is a set
U_{i}
with
|U_{i}|=m
of so-called
important
vertices. For every choice of
r-t
important vertices
u_{1},\dots,u_{r-t}
each belonging to a different
U_{i}
, we add an edge containing those important vertices together with
t
new vertices unique to this edge. Each of these new vertices is placed into a distinct part not already covered by the
u_{i}
.

Fact 3.6

(Proposition 6.5 in
[
BKS21
]
)
.

\tau(H_{r,t,m})=(t+1)m
.

We prove that, with the right choice of parameters, this hypergraph is
\delta
-intersecting.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
r\geq 3
and
\delta\in[e^{-r/5},1)
. Let
t:=\lfloor r/3\rfloor
and
m:=\left\lfloor\frac{r}{5\log(1/\delta)}\right\rfloor
. Then
H_{r,t,m}
is
\delta
-intersecting.

Proof.

Note that the lower bound on
\delta
ensures that
m\geq 1
and so
H_{r,t,m}
is a non-empty graph. Let
e\in E(H_{r,t,m})
be an arbitrary edge and let
u_{1},\dots,u_{r-t}
be its important vertices. By relabelling the parts if necessary, we may assume that
u_{i}\in U_{i}
for each
i\in[r-t]
.

Now let us compute the probability that a uniformly chosen random edge
e^{\prime}\in E(H_{r,t,m})
intersects
e
. Note that the uniform distribution on
E(H_{r,t,m})
can be simulated by first picking
r-t
important classes
U_{i_{1}},\dots,U_{i_{r-t}}
uniformly at random, and then choosing vertices
u_{i_{1}}^{\prime}\in U_{i_{1}},\dots,u^{\prime}_{i_{r-t}}\in U_{i_{r-t}}
uniformly and independently of each other. Then
e^{\prime}
is picked to be the unique edge containing
u_{i_{1}}^{\prime},\dots,u^{\prime}_{i_{r-t}}
.

Observe that if we condition on any choice of
U_{i_{1}},\dots,U_{i_{r-t}}
, then we have

|\{1,\dots,r-t\}\cap\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r-t}\}|\geq r-2t\geq\frac{r}{3}.

So, let
j_{1},\dots,j_{\lfloor r/3\rfloor}
belong to this intersection. Then,

\mathbb{P}\left(u^{\prime}_{j_{i}}\neq u_{j_{i}}\mbox{ for each }1\leq i\leq\lfloor r/3\rfloor\right)=\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\right)^{\lfloor r/3\rfloor}\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{r}{5m}\right\}\leq\delta,

where in the first inequality we used
\lfloor r/3\rfloor\geq r/5
since
r\geq 3
. Thus, conditioned on any choice of
U_{i_{1}},\dots,U_{i_{r-t}}
, the probability that
e^{\prime}
is disjoint from
e
is at most
\delta
. But then this remains true without conditioning on this choice. This implies that
e
intersects at least
(1-\delta)\cdot e(H_{r,t,m})

distinct edges of
H_{r,t,m}
. This completes the proof since our choice of
e
was arbitrary.
∎

Now we are ready to prove our lower bound.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. There are arbitrarily large
r
-edge-coloured graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)|V(G)|
and

tc(G)\geq\frac{r}{120}\left\lceil\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\rceil.

Proof.

First note that if
\delta\leq e^{-r/60}
we have
\frac{r}{60\log(1/\delta)}\leq 1
, in which case the statement is trivially true by the following simple construction. We take an
n
-vertex graph
G
with
1/n\ll 1/r,\delta
with
r
distinguished vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
forming an independent set. We make each
v_{i}
complete to
V(G)\setminus\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
in colour
i
, whereas
V(G)\setminus\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
is complete and coloured arbitrarily. We have
tc(G)=r
and
\delta(G)\geq n-r\geq(1-\delta)n
.

From now on, we will assume that
\delta\geq e^{-r/60}
. Our next step is to construct a
\delta^{2}
-intersecting
r
-partite
r
-graph with no cover of size
\frac{r^{2}}{60\log(1/\delta)}\geq\frac{r}{120}\left\lceil\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\rceil
. Let us start by showing that this can be done when
r=2
, as this is handled differently from the case
r\geq 3
. Here we need to construct a bipartite (
2
-)graph, so let us take a matching on
m^{\prime}:=\lfloor(1-\delta^{2})^{-1}\rfloor
edges. Since each edge intersects itself, the proportion of edges that each edge intersects is
\frac{1}{m^{\prime}}\geq 1-\delta^{2},
and so it is
\delta^{2}
-intersecting. Its transversal number is

m^{\prime}=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{1-\delta^{2}}\right\rfloor\geq\frac{1}{2(1-\delta^{2})}.

However, using the fact that
e^{x/2}\leq 1+x
for all
x\in[0,1]
, we obtain

\frac{1-\delta^{2}}{2}\leq\log(1+1-\delta^{2})\leq\log\left(1+\frac{1-\delta^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)=2\log(1/\delta),

where the third inequality used
\delta\leq 1
. Thus,
m^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{8\log(1/\delta)}\geq\frac{r^{2}}{60\log(1/\delta)}
. This handles the case
r=2
.

If
r\geq 3
, we apply
3.7
with
\delta^{2}
in place of
\delta
and
t=t(r)
and
m=m(r,\delta^{2})
defined as in its statement. Note that
\delta\geq e^{-r/60}\geq e^{-r/5}
and so the lemma applies. Then
H_{r,t,m}
is
\delta^{2}
-intersecting. By
3.6
,

\tau(H_{r,t,m})=(t+1)m\geq r/3\cdot\left\lfloor\frac{r}{10\log(1/\delta)}\right\rfloor\geq\frac{r^{2}}{60\log(1/\delta)},

where in the last inequality we used
r/(10\log(1/\delta))\geq 1
as
\delta\geq e^{-r/60}
.

The previous paragraphs yield a
\delta^{2}
-intersecting
r
-partite
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
with
\tau(\mathcal{H})\geq\frac{r^{2}}{60\log(1/\delta)}
for all choices of
r\geq 2
. By
Proposition

3.5
, this can be converted into a graph
G^{\prime}
with
\delta(G^{\prime})\geq(1-\delta^{2})|V(G^{\prime})|-1
and
tc(G^{\prime})=\tau(\mathcal{H})
. Given any
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
1/n\ll 1/\delta
, blow up each vertex of
G^{\prime}
to a set of size
n
. Rather than letting these
n
-sized sets be independent sets, we make them all complete and colour them arbitrarily, thereby obtaining an
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
on
n\cdot|G^{\prime}|
vertices with

\delta(G)\geq n\cdot\left(\left(1-\delta^{2}\right)|V(G^{\prime})|-1\right)+(n-1)=\left(1-\delta^{2}\right)|V(G)|-1\geq(1-\delta)|V(G)|.

It is easy to see that
tc(G)=tc(G^{\prime})=\tau(\mathcal{H})
, which finishes the proof. ∎

3.3
Upper bounds

In this section, we prove the two upper bounds in
1.4
. These are proven separately in two lemmas:
3.9
is a simple bound for when
\delta
is large that is tight up to a constant factor, and
3.11
is our general bound.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in[1/2,1)
. Any
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)|V(G)|
satisfies

tc(G)\leq\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1/\delta)}.

Proof.

Consider
G
’s connectivity hypergraph
\mathcal{C}(G)
and define

X:=\left\{v\in V(\mathcal{C}(G)):\deg_{\mathcal{C}(G)}(v)\geq\frac{1-\delta}{r}e(\mathcal{C}(G))\right\}.

By
3.4
,
\mathcal{C}(G)
is
\delta
-intersecting. By averaging, each edge
e\in E(\mathcal{C}(G))
contains some vertex
x\in X
. Therefore,
X
is a transversal for
\mathcal{C}(G)
.

Recall that
\mathcal{C}(G)
is
r
-partite. If
V_{1},\dots,V_{r}
are the
r
parts of
\mathcal{C}(G)
, note that
|V_{i}\cap X|\leq\frac{r}{1-\delta}
using the degree of each vertex in
X
and the fact that every edge in
\mathcal{C}(G)
contains a unique vertex from
V_{i}
. Hence,

|X|\leq r\cdot\frac{r}{1-\delta}\leq\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1/\delta)}.

To see the last inequality, let
\alpha:=1-\delta
and note

\log(1/\delta)=\log\left(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right)\leq\log\left(1+2\alpha\right)\leq 2\alpha=2(1-\delta),

where we use
\alpha\leq 1/2
in the first inequality. In conclusion,
tc(G)=\tau(\mathcal{C}(G))\leq\frac{2r^{2}}{\log(1/\delta)}
. ∎

Our general upper bound is derived from the following.

Lemma 3.10

(Corollary 5.5 in
[
BKS21
]
)
.

Let
e^{r}\geq k>r\geq 2
and let
m=\frac{4r}{\log k}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-graph in which any
k
edges have a transversal of size at most
r
. Then
\tau(\mathcal{H})\leq 4rm\log m
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1/2)
. Any
r
-edge-coloured graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)|V(G)|
satisfies

tc(G)\leq 100r\cdot\left\lceil\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\cdot\log\left(1+\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right)\right\rceil.

Proof.

First suppose that
\delta\geq r^{-2}
. Then we have

\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\cdot\log\left(1+\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right)\geq\frac{r}{2\log r}\cdot\log\left(\frac{r}{2\log r}\right)\geq\frac{r}{2\log r}\cdot\log(r^{0.1})\geq\frac{r}{20},

applied with

playing the roles of

\delta(G)\geq|V(G)|/2
and so the lemma application yields a tree cover with

monochromatic trees.

Now let us assume that

\mathcal{C}(G)
is
\delta

be the ‘flattened’

by retaining only one copy of each of its edges. Let

that intersect every

is at least

(1-k\delta)e(\mathcal{C}(G))>0.

Since

were arbitrary, this shows that any

have a transversal of size at most

\tau(\mathcal{C}(G))=\tau(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\leq\frac{16r^{2}}{\log k^{\prime}}\cdot\log\left(\frac{4r}{\log k^{\prime}}\right)\leq\max\left\{100r,\frac{16r^{2}}{\log k}\cdot\log\left(\frac{4r}{\log k}\right)\right\}.

(1)

But we have
k\geq 1/(2\delta)\geq\delta^{-1/2}

\frac{16r^{2}}{\log k}\cdot\log\left(\frac{4r}{\log k}\right)\leq\frac{32r^{2}}{\log(1/\delta)}\cdot\log\left(\frac{8r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right).

We also have

as the term inside the floor is positive. This shows that the RHS in

4
Absorption

In this section, we prove our main absorbing lemma, which will play a central role in our main proof by allowing us to cover a small leftover set of vertices with few monochromatic cycles. Roughly speaking, it says that in any edge-coloured bipartite graph
G[A,B]
where
A
is small relative to
B
, and every vertex of
A
has many neighbours in
B
, one can cover
A
with only a few monochromatic cycles.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1/n\ll 1/r,1/K
and
1/K\ll 1
, and let
\delta\in[r^{-7r},1/24]
. Let
G
be an
r
-edge-coloured bipartite graph on vertex classes
A
and
B
with
|B|=n
and
|A|\leq\frac{n}{r^{40r}}
. Suppose that
\deg(v,B)\geq(1-\delta)n
for each
v\in A
. Then
G
contains a collection of at most

Kr\left\lceil\frac{r\log r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\rceil

vertex-disjoint monochromatic cycles covering
A
.

As the proof of this lemma introduces several novel ideas, we now provide a brief sketch. Suppose we are given an
r
-edge-coloured bipartite graph
G[A,B]
as in the statement. At a high level, the proof strategy is to reduce the problem from monochromatic cycle partitioning to tree covering, for which we can use the upper bound from
1.4
. To do so, we start with a cleaning procedure on
G
ensuring that each of its monochromatic components, say
T
, satisfies the following two conditions.

(a)

T
contains a small collection of monochromatic cycles
C_{1},\dots,C_{t}
covering almost all of the vertices of
V(T)\cap A
.

(b)

Any two vertices
v,u\in V(T)
are connected by many paths that are internally vertex-disjoint from
V(C_{i})
,
i\in[t]
.

If these two properties hold with suitable parameters, then
(b)
allows us to ‘stitch together’ the cycles
C_{1},\dots,C_{t}
from
(a)
into a single monochromatic cycle covering almost all of
V(T)\cap A
.

Following this approach, a cover of
A
with few monochromatic trees can be transformed into an almost-cover of
A
by monochromatic cycles. This still falls slightly short of
4.1
, however, which demands that the cycles be vertex-disjoint and cover all of
A
. While the first requirement can be guaranteed through a slight modification of the argument for
(a)
, the second presents a delicate issue. Indeed, even if the leftover is small relative to
A
, it is far from clear that covering it should be any easier than our original problem unless it is
extremely
small.

To address this issue, our proof will need to be exceptionally efficient in controlling the leftover of the almost-cover, specifically ensuring that it contains not just a bounded number of vertices as a function of
r
, but in fact no more than
r^{O(r)}
vertices. This is essentially the largest number of vertices that can be covered by a simple greedy argument with the prescribed number of cycles (see
4.14
below).

Let us outline how we attempt to prove
(a)
and
(b)
in order to achieve such a small leftover. Condition
(b)
is the main obstacle; by contrast,
(a)
is relatively straightforward, since it is generally easy to find a bounded collection of cycles covering
A
when each component has not-too-small minimum degree (which can be guaranteed by deleting a small number of edges). To establish
(b)
, we introduce certain structures that we call
connecting hubs
(see
4.4
below). Roughly speaking, a connecting hub
\mathcal{H}
consists of a large set
S\subseteq B
and a set of colours
C\subseteq[r]
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

for each
c\in C
, any two vertices of
S
are connected by many internally vertex-disjoint
c
-coloured paths; and

(ii)

every vertex
v\in A
either sends few edges to
S
or many of these edges are
C
-coloured.

It is not difficult to show that such a structure exists in
G[A,B]
. Iterating this, we obtain a family of vertex-disjoint hubs
\mathcal{H}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
that together cover almost all of
B
, where each
\mathcal{H}_{i}
consists of a set
S_{i}\subseteq B
together with a colour set
C_{i}\subseteq[r]
. By discarding a few additional edges and vertices, we can further ensure that

•

each
S_{i}
is incident only with
C_{i}
-coloured edges, and for each
v\in A
and
c\in[r]
, the degree
\deg_{c}(v,S_{i})
is either
0
or large; and

•

for each
i,j\in[\ell]
and
c\in[r]
, there are either no or many so-called
c
-links
between
\mathcal{H}_{i}
and
\mathcal{H}_{j}
: that is, vertices in
A
having many
c
-coloured neighbours in both
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
.

After these preparations, if two vertices
u,v\in A
are connected by a
c
-coloured path in
G
, it becomes easy to find many internally vertex-disjoint
c
-coloured
u
–
v
paths, as required by
(b)
. Indeed, any such path necessarily passes through a sequence of hubs
\mathcal{H}_{i_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{H}_{i_{\ell}}
, where consecutive hubs admit many
c
-links. Many alternative paths can therefore be constructed by varying the
c
-link used between successive hubs and by choosing different internal paths within each hub (using property
(i)
).

This is essentially enough to carry out our entire strategy. The hubs can only be guaranteed to satisfy
|S_{i}|\geq n/r^{\mathcal{O}(r)}
, which means that, for technical reasons, for condition
(a)
we cannot do better than
r^{\mathcal{O}(r)}
cycles. Fortunately, the monochromatic connectivity we can guarantee inside the hubs (i.e. property
(i)
) is of order
r^{\Omega(r)}
vertex-disjoint paths, which provides enough room to stitch these cycles into one. All of this comes at the cost of
r^{\mathcal{O}(r)}
discarded vertices of
A
, which we handle separately, as noted earlier.

The remainder of the section is structured as follows. Connecting hubs are introduced in
Section

4.1
, where we also show how to find a single one. In
Section

4.2
, we construct families of hubs with the properties we need, which we refer to as
linked hub families
.
Section

4.3
shows how to cover all but
r^{O(r)}
vertices with monochromatic cycles, whereas
Section

4.4
shows how to cover the rest and proves
4.1
.

4.1

In order to formally introduce connecting hubs, we first require the following definition.

Definition 4.2

(contracted graph)
.

Let
k\geq 1
and let
G
be a bipartite graph on vertex classes
A
and
B
. The
contracted graph

\tilde{G}(A,B,k)
is defined as the graph on vertex set

We record, for later use, the following simple fact about contracted graphs.

Fact 4.3
.

Let

be a bipartite graph on vertex classes

and
B

for each

\tilde{G}(A,B,\frac{|B|}{5k^{2}})
has no independent set of size
2k
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that it contains an independent set

of size

\begin{split}|N_{G}(Y)|&\geq\sum_{v\in Y}\deg_{G}(v,B)-\sum_{vu\in\binom{Y}{2}}\operatorname{codeg}_{G}(v,u,B)\\
&\geq\frac{|Y||B|}{k}-\binom{|Y|}{2}\frac{|B|}{5k^{2}}\\
&\geq 2|B|-4k^{2}\cdot\frac{|B|}{5k^{2}}>|B|,\end{split}

which contradicts the fact that
N_{G}(Y)\subseteq B
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The definition of a connecting hub allows

, in which case the hub exhibits no useful connectivity properties. If this happens, however, note that every

Linked hub families

Two

. An
(r,t,m,\varepsilon)
-linked hub family
for

-connecting hubs

Now we prove that any edge-coloured bipartite graph

Lemma 4.8

Let

(3)

is disjoint from all the routing sets

.

(r,r^{Kr},m,\varepsilon)
-connecting hub

for a set

Each

with

is vertex-disjoint from

and it is also a

-connecting hub for

)
holds.

It remains to find a suitable subset

so that

-linked hub family with respect to

Given a linked hub family

for a set

Let

•

•

•

Our next lemma shows how to lower bound the minimum degree of

as a function of the degrees of vertices in

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
n,m,r,t\geq 2
, let
1/n\leq\delta\leq 1/2
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
G
be an
r
-edge-coloured bipartite graph on vertex classes
A
and
B
with
|B|=n
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\}
be a
(r,t,m,\varepsilon)
-linked hub family for a non-empty set

. Suppose that
\deg(v,\bigcup_{i\in[\ell]}S_{i})\geq(1-\delta)n
for each

, where

is the core of
\mathcal{H}_{i}
for each

. Then

\delta\left(R(\mathcal{F},X)\right)\geq(1-6\delta)|R(X,\mathcal{F})|.

Proof.

Let
Y:=\bigcup_{i\in[\ell]}S_{i}
. Note that, in
R:=R(X,\mathcal{F})
,

forms a monochromatic
(r+1)
-coloured clique. Since
X
is non-empty, some element of

has at least

neighbours in

. This implies

\deg_{R}(v)\geq|Y|-1\geq(1-2\delta)n

for each
v\in Y
(also using

).

For each

, let
Q_{u}:=\{i\in[\ell]:\deg_{G}(u,S_{i})\leq m/2\}
. We can count the degree of

into

in two ways as

(1-\delta)n\leq\deg_{G}(u,Y)\leq\frac{|Q_{u}|m}{2}+(\ell-|Q_{u}|)m.

However, we have

and so this inequality rearranges to

|Q_{u}|\leq\frac{2}{m}\left(|Y|-(1-\delta)n\right)\leq\frac{2\delta n}{m}\leq 4\delta\ell,

This leads to the following corollary, essentially already proven in

Now we prove the main lemma of this subsection.

Introduce a new constant

Covering a very small leftover

Our next lemma shows how to cover a leftover of

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
1/n\ll 1/r,1/K\leq 1/2
and let
\delta\in(0,1/4]
. Let

be an
r
-edge-coloured bipartite graph on vertex classes

and

where
|B|=n
and
|A|\leq r^{Kr}
. Suppose that

for each

.

Proof.

Let
A:=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
for some
m\leq r^{Kr}
. For each
u\in B
, we encode its adjacencies to vertices in
A
(together with their colours) through a tuple
\sigma^{u}\in[r+1]^{m}
by the following rule. For each

, if
uv_{i}
is an edge in
G

is defined as the unique colour of
uv_{i}
in
G
(which is an element of
[r]
); if there is no edge
uv_{i}
in
G
, then we let
\sigma^{u}_{i}:=r+1
.

For each
\textnormal{{x}}\in[r+1]^{m}
, define

B_{\textnormal{{x}}}:=\{v\in B:\sigma^{v}=\textnormal{{x}}\}.

Now we remove from
B
all the vertices belonging to the sets
B_{\textnormal{{x}}}
(for some
\textnormal{{x}}\in[r+1]^{m}
) satisfying
|B_{\textnormal{{x}}}|\leq m
, and call the resulting set
B^{\prime}
. This set has size

|B^{\prime}|\geq|B|-m\cdot(r+1)^{m}\geq\frac{n}{2},

(9)

since
m\leq r^{Kr}
and
1/n\ll 1/K,1/r
. Then for each
u\in B^{\prime}
, we have
|B_{\sigma^{u}}|\geq m
and
B_{\sigma^{u}}\subseteq B^{\prime}
.

The total number of edges between
A
and
B^{\prime}
is at least

|A|(|B^{\prime}|-\delta n)\geq(1-2\delta)|A||B^{\prime}|,

where we used
(
9
)
. By averaging, there is some vertex
u\in B^{\prime}
that is adjacent to at least
(1-2\delta)|A|
vertices

. For each

, the graph

is complete and
i
-coloured. Also,
|B_{\sigma^{u}}|\geq m
as noted earlier. Taking each colour

in order, we can thus easily find an

-coloured cycle

alternating between vertices of
N_{i}(u)
and

and covering

, all the while avoiding previously constructed cycles

. After going through all the colours in

, this yields a collection of vertex-disjoint cycles

covering

vertices of
A
.

Now we iterate the argument just presented, thus obtaining a procedure which finds
r
vertex-disjoint monochromatic cycles at each step and shrinks the leftover by a factor of
2\delta
. Indeed, if
A^{*}\subseteq A
and

are the uncovered sets at any given step

, we still have

|B^{*}|\geq|B^{\prime}|-m\geq|B|-m\cdot(r+1)^{m}-m\geq\frac{n}{2}

since these sets are obtained by removing a collection of vertex-disjoint cycles from

, which implies

. Thus, each vertex of
A^{\prime}
has degree at least
(1-2\delta)|B^{*}|
into

, and we can find a vertex

with

Let

(2\delta)^{\ell}|A|\leq r^{Kr}\cdot\exp\left\{-\frac{\log(1/(2\delta))}{\log(1/\delta)}\cdot 3Kr\log r\right\}\leq r^{Kr}\cdot\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\cdot 3Kr\log r\right\}<1,

where in the second inequality we used

5
Covering the reduced graph

In this section, we will show that, roughly speaking, any
r
-edge-coloured graph with high minimum degree admits a partition into monochromatic matchings contained in the union of a small number of monochromatic components. This result will be applied in our main proof to cover the reduced graph obtained by applying the regularity lemma. These matchings will then be converted to a small number of monochromatic cycles covering almost the entire host graph.

In order for this almost-cover to combine well with the cycles obtained in the absorption step, we further need to cover a large portion of the reduced graph not just by matchings, but also by certain other subgraphs which carry crucial additional properties. Some of these subgraphs will be triangles, whereas others will be of the following form.

Definition 5.1
.

A
barbell
is a graph obtained from two vertex-disjoint triangles
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}
and
b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}
by adding a new vertex
c
together with the edges
ca_{1}
and
cb_{1}
(see
Figure

3
).

Though triangles and barbells do not contain a perfect matching, they exhibit a perhaps even more useful property: if you blow them up in an approximately balanced way, the resulting graph has a perfect matching. This is encoded in the following definition.

Definition 5.2

(perfect
b
-matching)
.

Let
b:V(G)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
be a function on the vertices of a graph
G
. A
perfect
b
-matching
is a non-negative function
\omega:E(G)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
such that
\sum_{w\in N(v)}\omega(wv)=b
for each
v\in V(G)
.

The next two statements establish the abovementioned fact about perfect matchings in blow-ups of triangles and barbells.

Fact 5.3
.

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. Let
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}
be a triangle. Let
b:\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
be a function such that
3n/10\leq b(a_{1})\leq n
,
9n/10\leq b(a_{2}),b(a_{3})\leq n
, and each
b(a_{i})
is even. Then there is a perfect
b
-matching
\omega(\cdot)
of
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}
such that
\omega(a_{i}a_{i+1})\geq n/10
for each
i\in[3]
.

Proof.

For each
i\in[3]
, define

\omega(a_{i}a_{i+1}):=\frac{b(a_{i})+b(a_{i+1})-b(a_{i+2})}{2}\geq\frac{n}{10},

where indices are taken modulo
3
. Observe that
\omega(a_{i}a_{i+1})
is an integer since each
b(v)
is even. Furthermore,

\omega(a_{i}a_{i+1})+\omega(a_{i+2}a_{i})=\frac{b(a_{i})+b(a_{i+1})-b(a_{i+2})}{2}+\frac{b(a_{i+2})+b(a_{i})-b(a_{i+1})}{2}=b(a_{i})

for each
i\in[3]
.∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
n\geq 20
. Let
G
be a barbell. Given any function
b:V(G)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
such that
9n/10\leq b(v)\leq n
and
b(v)
is even for each
v\in V(G)
,
G
has a perfect
b
-matching
\omega(\cdot)
such that
\omega(e)\geq n/10
for each
e\in E(G)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
consists of triangles
a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}
and
b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}

7
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we studied how many monochromatic cycles are required to partition a large
r
-edge-coloured graph as a function of its minimum degree, as captured by the function
cp_{r}(\delta)
. We determined
cp_{r}(\delta)
up to a
(\log r)
-factor for all choices of
r
and
\delta
. Along the way, we also investigated a related but easier problem where, instead of a cycle partition, we seek a cover of the vertex set by (not necessarily vertex-disjoint) monochromatic trees. For this variant, we obtained slightly stronger bounds. Our work leaves open several intriguing questions.

The most natural and significant open problem is to better understand the behaviour of
cp_{r}(\delta)
. We propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 7.1
.

There exists
K>0
such that, for all
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1/2)
,

cp_{r}(\delta)\leq Kr\cdot\left\lceil\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\rceil.

If true,
7.1
would be tight by
3.8
. Moreover, it holds for
\delta=\Omega(1)
by
6.2
, the main result of
[
KLLP21
]
. Notably,
7.1
predicts that
\mathcal{O}(r)
cycles already suffice for
n
-vertex edge-coloured graphs with minimum degree at least
(1-2^{-\Omega(r)})n
. This would constitute a substantial strengthening of the corresponding statement for edge-coloured complete graphs, which remains a well-known open problem.

As a stepping stone towards
7.1
, we also propose the analogous conjecture for tree covering.

Conjecture 7.2
.

There exists
K>0
such that, for all
r\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1)
,

tc_{r}(\delta)\leq Kr\cdot\left\lceil\frac{r}{\log(1/\delta)}\right\rceil.

This bound would also be tight by
3.8
. Furthermore,
1.4
implies that
7.2
holds when
\delta=\Omega(1)
and, in contrast to
7.1
, also when
\delta=2^{-\Omega(r)}
. The remaining gap thus lies in the intermediate range. Note that, in order to prove
7.2
, one may equivalently work with its reformulation in the language of transversals of
\delta
-intersecting multi-
r
-graphs provided by
3.4
and
Proposition

3.5
.

Pokrovskiy
[
Pok14
]
initiated the study of monochromatic cycle partitioning problems in which one allows a leftover set of uncovered vertices whose size is bounded as a function of
r
. In a similar spirit, our methods yield the following result.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
1/n\ll 1/r,1/K
and
1/K\ll 1
. Let
\delta\in(0,1/2)
. Every
n
-vertex
r
-edge-coloured graph with
\delta(G)\geq(1-\delta)n
admits a collection of at most

K(r\log r+tc_{r+1}(\delta^{1/4}))

monochromatic cycles covering all but
r^{Kr}
vertices of
G
.

The proposition follows directly from the proof of
6.1
if, in the absorption step, one replaces the application of
4.1
with
4.13
.

Interestingly, if
7.2
holds, then
Proposition

7.3
is tight up to a constant factor (in the number of cycles used) for all values of
r
and
\delta
satisfying
tc_{r}(\delta)=\Omega(r\log r)
(namely, for
\delta=2^{-\mathcal{O}(r/\log(r))}
). Indeed,
7.2
implies
tc_{r+1}(\delta^{1/4})=\mathcal{O}(tc_{r}(\delta))
, and hence, for these values of
\delta
, the proposition guarantees that
\mathcal{O}(tc_{r}(\delta))
cycles suffice to cover all but a bounded number of vertices. On the other hand, the proof of
3.8
actually shows that, for some
k>0
, there are arbitrarily large
n
-vertex graphs in which any collection of
kr\lceil r/\log(1/\delta)\rceil
monochromatic trees leaves not just a single vertex, but
\Omega(n)
vertices uncovered. Consequently, under these assumptions,
Proposition

7.3
is tight.

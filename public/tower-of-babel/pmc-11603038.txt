Green synthesis of zinc nanoparticles by hydroalcoholic extract of lavender (Lavandula stoechas L.), characterization, and cytotoxic effects on human breast and colon cancer
Authors: Aida Mehdipour, Mahdieh Ameri Shah Reza, Alireza Rasouli, Mohammad Hossein Jafari Baravati, Gholam Ali Jafari, Fatemeh Heidari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractGreen synthesis is the production of metal nanoparticles (MNPs) with biological agents, including plant extracts; it is environmentally friendly. The study aimed to investigate the cytotoxic effects of zinc nanoparticles (ZnNPs) synthesized by the hydroalcoholic extract of lavender (Lavandula stoechas L.) against MCF7 and HT29 with the approach of identifying the ability of these NPs to produce anticancer drugs. ZnNPs are synthesized using the hydroalcoholic extract of the lavender. Nanoparticles (NPs) are evaluated and characterized by the Tyndall effect, UV-Vis, DLS, FT-IR, Zeta-P, Raman spectroscopy, SEM, AFM, and XRD methods. The cytotoxic effects of produced NPs against MCF7 and HT29 and the healthy cell lines MCF10a and HGF were measured using the MTT Assay. Results show the average size of ZnNPs is 40 and 50 nm at a concentration of 3 mM. The highest level of cytotoxicity of NPs occurred after 48 h and was dependent on dose and time. It was determined that lavender is a suitable option for the green synthesis of ZnNPs and can be used as a stable source for the production of MNPs. Also, the synthesized ZnNPs showed cytotoxic effects against the examined cancer cells, while they did not cause toxicity to healthy cells.Keywords: Lavender, Hydroalcoholic extract, Zinc nanoparticles, Green synthesisSubject terms: Biotechnology, Nanobiotechnology

Introduction

The use of nanotechnology and the production of environmentally friendly products are in line with the United Nations’ goals, which include increasing the quality of life and improving the protection of the planet1. Nanotechnology examines the properties of materials and compounds in 1 to 100 nanometers. Materials in nano size have many applications in various industries, including medicine2. One of the therapeutic applications of nanoparticles is antibacterial activity. So far, no antibacterial factor has been found that can fully cope with the increase in antibiotic resistance. That’s why it is very important to find new antibacterial factors. Investigating and producing nanoparticles with new sources and methods can lead to finding new antibacterial factors to reduce the global health threat caused by increasing antibiotic resistance3. In the past years, synthetic compounds synthesized by physical methods have been the primary approach in industries. Still, due to identifying their toxic effects on human health, the approach to using these artifacts for compounds synthesized by natural methods has been changed4. Green Synthesis refers to producing NPs using plants or their metabolites, ultimately reducing the toxicity of produced NPs4.

Plants are the largest and most widely used source for the green synthesis of NPs due to their diverse and wide range of phytochemicals that act as reducing and stabilizing agents for forming NPs. Various phytochemicals found in plants, such as organic acids, amines, amides, aldehydes, ketones, and flavonoids, are responsible for the reduction of metal ions and the formation of NPs, and various proteins found in metabolites and plant extracts are responsible for the stability of synthesized NPs5. Biosynthesis using different species leads to the production of durable NPs with safe performance. In past research, various plant species and microorganisms have succeeded in producing NPs with various abilities6–8.

The structure of NPs synthesized by the green method includes shape, size, and morphology depending on the type of metabolism used. Factors such as chemical interactions, pH, and temperature effects impact the final result. According to the type of organism used, NPs are formed intracellularly or extracellularly9–12.

The green synthesis of NPs resulting from using plant or microbial extracts has various advantages, including compatibility with the environment, simplicity, and low cost of the process. In contrast, the chemical and physical methods of NP synthesis have complex, expensive processes that lead to the production of waste becomes dangerous13. Green Synthesis using plant extracts due to the use of secondary metabolites as reducing and masking agents ultimately leads to the formation and stabilization of NPs, while green synthesis using microbial extracts requires aseptic cultivation and production; the commercialization of these NPs is expensive, and the green synthesis of NPs with algae is also not economical due to its time-consuming nature13. Different parts of plants, including stems, leaves, roots, flowers, seeds, and metabolites, make NPs14–17. One of the advantages of green synthesis of NPs by plants is less energy consumption, no need for high temperature and pressure, production of fewer by-products, low final cost, and the possibility of large-scale production. Synthesized green NPs are not limited in clinical applications due to their high stability and low toxicity compared to non-biological chemical NPs18.

Lavender plants are one of the flowering plants in the mint family (Lamiaceae). It is a sturdy and full-stemmed plant whose height reaches 80–120 cm. The leaves are small, oval-triangular, with deep comb divisions. This plant has compounds such as diterpenes, cyclic alcohols, flavonoids, organic acids, methylcarnosic acid, and saponins. Among other known important chemical compounds of lavender are cineol, linalool, volatile oils, tannin, essential oil, linalyl acetate, camphor, and geraniol. One of the most important members of the lavender plant family is the genus Lavandula, which has more than 35 species worldwide, spread from Key Verde and the Canary Islands to the Mediterranean region, India, and Southwest Asia. Lavandula stoechas essential oil and extract have many biological activities and medicinal properties, including antispasmodic, sedative, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antimicrobial, antifungal, insecticidal, and larvicidal activities19. Phenolic and flavonoid compounds are the main metabolites of lavender extract20–22. The lavender essential oil has 300 chemical components, and its descriptive and analytical composition varies depending on the genotype, growth area, climatic conditions, reproduction, and morphological characteristics; each compound has different uses23. It has been shown that lavender leaf extract is a suitable reducer of metal particles24, and nanoparticles synthesized with the extract have significant antibacterial and antioxidant properties compared to plant extracts25,26.

Metal zinc is an essential trace element found in muscle, bone, skin, and dental tissues, as well as in the hard part. It plays a vital role in numerous metabolic processes, from protein synthesis to immunity building to gene expression. As a structural and regulatory component of more than 300 enzymes in the body and more than 2000 transcription factors, zinc has antioxidant properties that, in addition to physiological functions, can help reduce the damage caused by ultraviolet rays and reduce the risk of cancer. Zinc Oxide Nanoparticles (ZnONPs) are white, odorless powders with a molecular weight of 81.38 g/mol. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) considers it a generally recognized safe substance (GRAS)27. In recent studies, the cytotoxic effects of NPs produced by the green synthesis method have been investigated28–30. One of the common diseases with a high incidence rate is colon cancer. Research on colon cancer and methods to deal with it is very important due to the increase in prevalence and decrease in its rate31. Due to its characteristics of mature intestinal cells, the human colon adenocarcinoma cell line HT29 is used in studies in various fields, including bioavailability and the study of intestinal immune response to various diseases32. The most common malignancy in women is breast cancer. This disease has a great impact on the population and is one of the public health problems, and there is a need for extensive research at the molecular level to deal with it. The MCF-7 cell line is the most common breast cancer cell, which has been studied and characterized by various scientists for more than 40 years, and is the best choice for breast cancer research33.

This research aims to investigate the Indigenous lavender plant’s ability to synthesize ZnNPs, evaluate the structure of the synthesized NPs, and investigate their cytotoxic effects on human breast cancer (MCF7) and human colon cancer (HT29) cell lines. Also, this approach helps to increase information to prove the hypothesis of the therapeutic capability of nanoparticles synthesized by the green method in clinical research.

Method and materials

The plant used

The lavender plant was collected from the pastures and agricultural lands around the city of Qom (Iran) and registered as a reference sample after identification in the herbarium section of the Faculty of Pharmacy of Shahid Beheshti University (Tehran, Iran) with the specific numbers of lavender (SBMU-8303). Also, the name of the identified species of lavender (Lavandula stoechas L.) was determined; it is necessary to mention that Prof Kamali Nejad registered the above strains.

Cell lines

Cell lines, human breast cancer cells (MCF7), human colon cancer cells (HT29), normal human gingival fibroblasts (HGF), and healthy breast cells (MCF10a) were obtained from the National Center for Genetic and Biological Resources of Iran (IBRC) has been prepared and used.

Hydroalcoholic extract

Two hundred grams of dried and ground lavender leaves were poured into a sterile Erlenmeyer flask containing one liter of solvent (distilled water-ethanol) with a ratio of 50/50 and heated for ten days at 25 ⁰C in the dark34. Then, the extract was passed through a filter paper, and the resulting solution was placed in an oven at 45 ⁰C to evaporate the solvent.

Preparation of salt storage solution

Different concentrations of zinc salt were required, and for their preparation, a concentrated solution with a specific purity percentage and standard of one molar was used. To prepare 50 mL, zinc sulfate and zinc nitrate solutions with one molar concentration of 12.05, 12.45, 14.35, 14.85 g of ZnSO4.4H2O and Zn(No3)2.6H2O salt powder (Merck, Germany), was mixed with deionized water to a volume of 50 ml. The prepared solutions were filtered with a 0.22-micron filter and stored at 4 ⁰C.

Synthesis of NPs

Metal powder was prepared in 20 mL of extracts with different concentrations, including (1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, and 5 mM) at 25 ⁰C, pH = 10, and was mixed at 120 rpm for 24 h.

Isolation and purification of NPs

The NPs were separated with an ultracentrifuge device for 10 min at 4 ⁰C and 11,000 rpm. After dispersing them in deionized distilled water, the deposited NPs were centrifuged under the previous conditions to wash their surfaces. This process was repeated three times in total to separate the ZnNPs and thoroughly wash their surfaces.

Characterization of NPs

NPs confirmation and identification analyses have been performed at Shahid Beheshti University (Tehran, Iran). The Tyndall effect test, which verifies the formation of colloidal particles, was determined by irradiating the laser light in the environment containing the extract and nanoparticles before and after the synthesis. Ultraviolet-visible spectroscopy (UV-Vis), (Agilent Cary 100, USA) was used to investigate the synthesis spectrum of NPs. For this analysis, 0.01 g of NPs were dissolved in ethanol and dispersed well under ultrasonic waves for 30 min. Then, their absorption spectrum was measured by the spectrometer of an Optizon 3230 UV device at a wavelength of 200 to 800 nm. In addition, Dynamic Light Diffraction (DLS) (Zetasizer, model 3600, England), Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FT-IR) (Shimadzu IR-spirit, Japan), Zeta Potential Analysis (Zeta-P) (ZETA-check, Particle Metrix, Germany), Raman Spectroscopy, Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) (model VEGA3, TESCAN company, Czech Republic), Atomic Force Microscope (AFM) (Agile8O, Iran), and Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy (XRD) (EXD 8000, Shimadzu Japan) were used to Characterize synthesis NPs8,12,17.

Cell culture

Human colon cancer (HT 29) and breast cancer (MCF7) cell lines, healthy gum fibroblast (HGF) cell lines, and healthy breast cells (MCF10a) were used in this study. These cells were cultured respectively in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium DMEM - Ham’s F12 and DMEM high glucose containing 10% fetal bovine serum, penicillin (100 units/ml) and streptomycin (100 mg/ml). Then, they were placed in an incubator containing 5% CO2 and 95% humidity at 37 °C. Flasks were examined daily by an inverted microscope in terms of cell growth and density, morphology, and contamination control. The complete culture medium containing 10% FBS was changed at the required time.

MTT assay

Investigation of the cytotoxicity effect of ZnNPs (A2) and its control (C2) by colorimetric method, using the MTT staining 3-[4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl]− 2,5-diphenyl tetrazolium bromide) water-soluble salt.

This method is based on the activity of succinate dehydrogenase enzyme in the mitochondria of living cells and purple insoluble formazan crystals. This compound is soluble in DMSO. Since dead cells cannot convert MTT into formazan, the level of formazan created is proportional to the number of living cells. To experiment, HT 29, MCF7, MCF10a, and HGF cells were planted in several 5000 cells in each well of a 96-well plate. Forty-eight hours after seeding the cells, the old culture medium was removed from the wells, and the cells were treated with different concentrations (50, 100, 150, 250, and 300 µg/mL) of NPs and chemotherapy drug cisplatin (1.25, 5 2, 5, 10, 20, 40 µg/mL) were treated. Four wells were considered for Hertimar, and four wells were assigned as controls for the whole culture medium in each experiment. The plates were incubated in an incubator containing 5% CO2, 95% humidity, and 37 °C for 24, 48 and 72 h. After the incubation time, the supernatant of each well was carefully removed and replaced with a complete culture medium containing MTT solution and placed in the incubator for 4 h; then, the supernatant of each well was drained and 200 µl of DMSO solution was replaced with the previous solution to dissolve the formazan crystals. After 15 min, the optical density was read using an ELISA device (USA, Bio Tek, Synergy/HTX) at a wavelength of 570 nm. The amount of absorption is directly proportional to the number of living cells. The results were calculated in terms of the percentage of live cells compared to the control from the following equation:

100 x (average absorption of control cells/average absorption of treated cells) = percentage of living cells.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was done by Excel and GraphPad Prism 8.2.1 software. The drug concentration required to inhibit cell growth by 50% (IC50) was calculated and plotted by PRISM software and by non-linear regression analysis using dose-response curves.

Based on the obtained results, it can be seen that the size of NPs calculated in SEM photography and dynamic light diffraction analysis (DLS) were not equal. The particles in the solution have a surface charge; in any case, they cover the surrounding particles. In addition, ions with the opposite charge can be seen on the surface of the particles. Therefore, the particle has two layers: one on the surface and the other around it. These outer and inner layers move along with the particle when the particle moves in the solution. Therefore, a hypothetical distance between the particle and the fluid medium can be imagined. This distance is called hydrodynamic distance, and it is known as zeta potential. Therefore, according to the zeta potential of the produced ZnNPs, it can be concluded that the electrostatic agent alone could not ensure the stability of the NPs. However, apart from repulsive and attractive forces, other effects are also involved in the stability of NPs. In this regard, we can mention the effect of steric hindrance 1 as one of the most important factors affecting the stability of NPs. This agent is formed by the absorption of polymer materials on the surface of NPs. The mentioned agent formed on the particle acts like a resistant barrier and prevents the particles from coming together and causing coagulation. Unlike electrostatic stability, which is seen in the range of zeta potential greater than + 30 and less than − 30, there is not much repulsive force in this case. When molecules come into contact, the particles are exposed to the attraction force. This is even though the electrostatic repulsion force typically repels them from each other.

Therefore, according to the observations, a combined state resulting from electrostatic repulsion and steric hindrance has been created in the produced ZnNPs. Hence, the difference in the average size of the particles reported in the SEM images compared to the zeta size of each plant extract was related to the difference caused by the hydrodynamic diameter of the NPs. The actual diameter of the NPs has been displayed in the presence of the dynamic diameter. Based on this figure, electron imaging has calculated the actual diameter of NPs, and dynamic light diffraction analysis has reported the diameter of NPs along with the bio-stabilizing factors attached to NPs.

Discussion

In this research, the proof of the ability to produce ZnNPs by the hydroalcoholic extract of the lavender medicinal plant was investigated to find a new way to improve the characteristics of the synthesized NPs in terms of particle size and distribution. The lavender plant was used for the production of NPs due to its high diversity of chemical compounds and the effect of environmental characteristics on these compounds’ diversity and structure. Compounds in plant extracts such as flavonoids, alcohols, organic acids, cytokines, terpenoids, proteins, peptides, vitamins, and saccharides act as reducing agents and convert metal ions into MNPs.

In the first step, after the preparation of hydroalcoholic extract and synthesis of NPs, the effect of time on the amount and size of NPs production was evaluated, and it was shown that the synthesis of NPs depends on time and other conditions. In other words, this is related to the higher amount of secreted proteins and phenolic compounds in the obtained extract, and based on macroscopic observations, out of the five time periods, the final period considered for the synthesis of ZnNPs is more favorable. Is. As a result, based on the macroscopic and microscopic results, at the end of the synthesis process and with equal volume ratios of the extract to the metal salt solution, they could produce ZnNPs. Based on the results, the pH value influences the size and distribution of the particles. Also, increasing or decreasing the concentration of zinc metal salts has changed the size of NPs. These observations are related to the ratio of metal salt concentration to regenerating and stabilizing proteins in the supernatant. In addition, the plant extract showed the highest color intensity at pH = 10. In other words, it can be said that the mentioned pH provides more reducing agents to metal ions to produce more ZnNPs. Also, the performance of the functional groups in proteins has been improved at the mentioned pH for the stability of NPs. Research findings show that pH, temperature, and RPM affect the size, shape, and stability of NPs, and optimizing production by controlling experimental parameters can lead to the production of desirable NPs.

Examining the results of this research shows that the average diameter of ZnNPs produced in lavender extract at a temperature of 25ºC, a pH = 10, a final concentration of 3 mM, and a speed of 120 rpm was equal to 27.62 nm. The zeta potential of NPs has been reported as -27 mV. NPs produced with positive zeta potential can strengthen their interaction with the cell membrane and help increase cell death through various mechanisms, such as electrostatic attraction, penetration into the cell, generation of oxidative stress, disturbance in metabolism, and stimulation of the immune response. This understanding can help design NPs with desirable properties for medical and therapeutic applications. NPs produced with negative zeta potential showed significant cytotoxic effects in this study.

The green synthesis of nanoparticles is a bottom-up method, and the metabolites of plant extracts containing active compounds of terpenoids, alkaloids, phenols, tannins, and vitamins play the main role in it. In this process, these active compounds act as reducers, giving electrons to metal particles, causing the formation of nanoparticles, and by continuing this process, they prevent re-aggregation.

In this study, the formation of NPs was measured using the Tyndall effect, UV-Vis, DLS, FTIR, Zeta-P, Raman spectroscopy, SEM, AFM, and XRD methods, and all the mentioned measurements confirmed the synthesis of ZnNPs. they did.

Also, this research showed that the proteins released by the lavender plant extract could regenerate zinc ions by the extracellular method. In addition, considering the lack of reports that the lavender plant is pathogenic, this plant can be regarded as a suitable option for entering the industry to produce MNPs.

By binding to cell surface receptors, NPs activate signaling pathways that play an important role in cell growth and survival. Binding to the cell surface changes the permeability of the cell membrane and leads to the entry or exit of substances that are effective in signaling and Gene expression. In addition, by affecting the signaling pathway through the production of reactive oxygen species, NPs activate caspases, which ultimately initiate the apoptosis process in the cell. Also, NPs stop the growth of cancer cells by disrupting the cell cycle. In this research, the cytotoxic effect of ZnNPs on the proliferation of human breast cancer cells (MCF7), human colon cancer cells (HT29), normal human gingival fibroblasts (HGF), and healthy breast cells (MCF10a) was evaluated using the MTT method. These cells were treated with six different concentrations of NPs (50, 100, 150, 250, and 300 µg/ml) for 24, 48, and 72 h. The data of the investigation of the cytotoxic effect of the present study show that the synthesized ZnNPs had significant cytotoxic effects in most of the tested concentrations in human breast cancer cells (MCF7) and human colon cancer cells (HT29) and inhibited the growth of cancer cells. Cytotoxic effects of NPs depend on time and concentration. Achieving the optimal dose of drugs for consumption requires extensive research. Adjusting the optimal dosage of drugs to maximize effectiveness and minimize side effects in clinical environments is a complex and multi-stage process that requires attention to various factors, extensive research, careful monitoring, and consideration of patients’ individual characteristics. As a result, the above NPs can find many biomedical applications with therapeutic importance to deal with breast and colon cancer.

The green synthesis method, which uses plant extracts to produce NPs, helps to reduce the final cost of production on a large scale. Also, unlike chemical methods of NPs synthesis, this method does not produce dangerous waste, is environmentally friendly, and can produce NPs with different properties that are useful in specific applications.

In 2019, Fakhari et al. conducted a study to produce ZnONPs by green synthesis method using the aqueous extract of the plant (Laurus nobilis L) and with two salts (zinc acetate and zinc nitrate) as precursors. Synthesized NPs were characterized by UV-Vis, FT-IR, XRD, EDX, and SEM analyses, and the results showed a hexagonal wurtzite structure; SEM analyses showed a spherical shape with a medium size (21.49 and 25.26 nm) for the synthesized NPs. This is demonstrated by zinc acetate and zinc nitrate, respectively. EDX analysis confirmed the high purity of the synthesized NPs36. Patrón-Romero et al. 2020 produced ZnONPs using green synthesis, and after characterizing the NPs with different analyses, they investigated the cytotoxic effects of NPs on two cell lines, which did not have toxic effects28. Salim et al. 2020 synthesized ZnONPs using Deverra tortuosa extract. The anticancer activity of the synthesized NPs in vitro against two cancer cell lines (human colon adenocarcinoma “Caco-2” and human lung adenocarcinoma “A549”) was compared with their activity on human lung fibroblast cell line (WI38) using MTT method was investigated and the synthesized NPs showed significant selective cytotoxicity against the two investigated cancer cell lines29. Umamaheswari et al. 2021 Investigating the ability of green synthesis of ZnONPs with the extract of Raphanus sativus var. Longipinnatus did. The results showed that the synthesized ZnONPs have a cytotoxic effect on the lung cancer cell line (A549)37.

In past research, ZnNPs were synthesized using the green method and characterized by various spectroscopic techniques. Then, the properties created by the synthesized NPs, such as the different properties and cytotoxic effects, were measured. In the current research, all the above steps have been carried out, and the NPs produced by the green synthesis method, in addition to having all the characteristics of NPs, have strong cytotoxic effects. It was also found that the synthesized NPs do not have cytotoxic effects on healthy cells.

Considering the determination of the conditions and amount of compounds for the production of ZNPs by the lavender plant after repetitions of the experiment, it can be said that the production of NPs with the used method is stable and can be implemented on a large scale for use in biological processes. However, the large-scale production of nanoparticles by green synthesis method with plant extracts includes various advantages, including being environmentally friendly, reducing costs, simplicity of the process, high quality of the final product, and remarkable stability. However, large-scale production has remaining problems and obstacles, such as the uncontrollability of the final size and shape of nanoparticles, the very difficult scalability of green synthesis methods, the wide variety of raw materials, and their limited availability at different times. To solve these obstacles, it is necessary to carry out extensive research in the field of large-scale production.

Conclusion

This research showed significant cytotoxic effects on synthesized ZnNPs. This research proves that the native lavender plant can synthesize NPs with therapeutic applications. However, it is necessary to investigate other therapeutic applications, such as antibacterial, drug delivery, and anti-adhesion effects.
